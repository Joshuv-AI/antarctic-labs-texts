Hilbert properties of varieties
Arno Fehm and Ariyan Javanpeykar

Abstract
. This is a survey of results on the Hilbert property of algebraic varieties, and variants of it.



Starting from Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem,
various Hilbert properties of fields and varieties have been
introduced, studied and applied.
This survey aims to give an overview of the various notions
and to document the state of the art of the research on this topic.
On the way we also collect several important open problems.

Where to find what

Section
1
introduces and tells the history of the central notions of this survey:
Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem, Hilbertian fields, the Hilbert property, and the weak Hilbert property.
The remaining two subsections discuss Hilbert properties for integral (instead of rational) points,
and special varieties in the sense of Campana.
Section
2
collects general results on the preservation of various Hilbert properties
under for example base change, morphisms and products.
The remaining sections each discuss one class of concrete varieties, usually over number fields, finitely generated fields or Hilbertian fields:
curves in Section
3
,
algebraic groups in Section
4
,
surfaces in Section
5
,
and some classes of higher-dimensional varieties in Section
6
–
closed subvarieties of abelian varieties, Fano varieties, varieties with nef tangent bundle, Kummer varieties,
and symmetric products.

Notation

Let
K
be a field.
We denote by
\overline{K}
an algebraic closure of
K
.
A
K
-variety
or a
variety over
K
is an integral separated scheme of finite type over
K
.
If
X
is a
K
-variety and
L/K
a field extension, we denote by
X_{L}=X\times_{{\rm Spec}(K)}{\rm Spec}(L)
the base change of
X
to
L
.
If
X
and
Y
are
K
-varieties,
X\sim Y
denotes that
X
and
Y
are birationally equivalent.

Disclaimer

Due to the vast amount of results relating to Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem in the context of algebraic varieties, we are not able to cover all of them.
Rather, we try to structure and compare some of the important results,
while still aiming to at least mention every paper that is directly related to the Hilbert property of varieties in the sense of Serre, or generalizations thereof.
As most of the theorems we state are a summary of the work of many people, we do not attribute theorems by names (with very few exceptions, like Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem) but rather try to explain the various contributions in the “proof” following the theorem.
So apart from precise references, these proofs also contain some historical information, and in some cases hints to stronger and/or more general statements.

1.
Hilbert irreducibility

1.1.
Hilbert’s theorem

Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem is the following result proven in
[
Hil92
, Theorem I]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

For every irreducible
f\in\mathbb{Q}[t,x]\setminus\mathbb{Q}[t]
there exist infinitely many

\tau\in\mathbb{Q}
with
f(\tau,x)
irreducible in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
.

Remark 1.2
.

In fact, Hilbert obtained infinitely many
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}
with
f(\tau,x)
irreducible.
He also deduced several generalizations:
over number fields instead of
\mathbb{Q}
,
for several polynomials instead of one
f
, for polynomials in several variables
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
instead of
t
,
and in several variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
instead of
x
.
We will state some of these variants explicitly later on
(see in particular Theorem
1.10
and Proposition
1.11
).

Example 1.3
.

If
g\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
is such that
g(\tau)
is a perfect square for all sufficiently large
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
g
is itself a square of another polynomial:
Otherwise one could apply Theorem
1.1
in the version for
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}
(Remark
1.2
) to
f(t,x)=x^{2}-g(t)
.

Hilbert’s motivation came from Galois theory:
Using the version of his theorem for several variables
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
instead of
t
,
he deduces the following corollary
[
Hil92
, Theorem IV]
and example:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
F=\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
be the rational function field in
n
variables over
\mathbb{Q}
.
For every
f\in F[x]
with Galois group
G:={\rm Gal}(f/F)
, there exists
\underline{\tau}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}

such that

f(\underline{\tau},x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]

is defined
and

{\rm Gal}(f(\underline{\tau},x)/\mathbb{Q})\cong G
.

Example 1.5
.

Let

f=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x-t_{i})=x^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}s_{i}x^{n-i}

where

s_{i}
is the
i
-th elementary symmetric polynomial in
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
.
Then
f\in\mathbb{Q}(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})[x]
has splitting field
\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

and Galois group (over
F=\mathbb{Q}(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
) the symmetric group
S_{n}
.
Indeed,
F
is precisely the fixed field of
the action of
S_{n}
on

\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

by permuting the
t_{i}
.
It follows also that
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
are algebraically independent,
and so we can apply Corollary
1.4
to
f
to obtain
polynomials over
\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
S_{n}
.

In geometric terms, this argument of Hilbert exploits that the quotient variety
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}/S_{n}
is again a rational variety (i.e., birationally equivalent to
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}
), in fact
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}/S_{n}\cong\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, so that the Galois cover
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}/S_{n}
can be specialized to a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with the same Galois group.
(We will return to this geometric interpretation in Section
1.3
.)
In what became known as the
Noether program
,
Emmy Noether
[
Noe18
]
started the investigation of whether

\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}/G
is rational for all finite groups
G\leq S_{n}
,
which if true would give a strategy to solve the inverse Galois problem:
Every finite group
G
would be the Galois group of a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.
However, counterexamples found by Swan
[
Swa69
]
and Lenstra
[
Len74
]

show that
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}/G
is non-rational already for the cyclic groups
G=\mathbb{Z}/47\mathbb{Z}
respectively
G=\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}
.
(See the references in
[
Len74
]
for more on the history of this problem.)

Remark 1.6
.

Apart from Galois theory, Hilbert’s theorem has found numerous applications in number theory and diophantine geometry.
For example, Néron
[
Nér54
]
used it to specialize abelian varieties from
\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
to
\mathbb{Q}
, which was used to construct elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
of high rank,
see e.g.
[
Ser97
, Chapter 11]
.
See also
[
Sil83
]
for a strengthening in the case
n=1
,
and
[
Col20
,
LS22
,
CS22
]
for recent advances.

Another example application
is the question
asked by H. Friedman
whether there exists a polynomial
f(x,y)\in\mathbb{Q}[x,y]
such that the associated map
\tilde{f}\colon\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}
is injective.
Although this question remains currently unsolved,
[
Poo10
, Theorem 1.1]
uses Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem to prove that such a polynomial exists if
the set of rational points of
a certain specific irreducible hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{Q}}

is not Zariski-dense,
and
[
Bre25
]

gives a proof,
conditional on a weak version of Lang’s conjecture (see Conjecture
1.42
below),
of a higher-dimensional generalization of Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem first explored systematically in
[
CZ18
, Section 3.8.1]
(there called a ‘Hilbert property for fibrations’), which would imply that
\tilde{f}
is never
bijective
.

Remark 1.7
.

Hilbert’s proof of Theorem
1.1
works with Puiseux series expansions (in
t
) of the roots of
f(x)
. See also
[
VGR18
]
for a modern account of Hilbert’s approach and the combinatorics involved.
Hilbert’s proof was simplified by Dörge
[
Dör27
]
, see for example
[
Völ96
]
for a modern exposition.
A non-standard proof was given by Roquette
[
Roq75
]
following an approach to Hilbertian fields (see Section
1.2
) of Gilmore and Robinson.
Most other proofs use reductions modulo different prime numbers, like
Fried’s proof
[
Fri74
]
that uses the Riemann hypothesis for curves,
or the one that follows from Theorem
1.22
below that uses the Lang–Weil estimates.

Several strengthenings exist:
For example,

[
Sch65
]
obtains a whole arithmetic progression
a+b\mathbb{Z}

of
\tau
for which
f(\tau,x)
is irreducible,
and also Fried’s proof mentioned above gives this.
Quantitative versions of Theorem
1.1

give estimates for the number of
\tau
in
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Q}
such that
f(\tau,x)
is irreducible, like
[
Coh81
, Theorem 2.5]
that uses the large sieve inequality, phrased in
[
Ser08
, Theorem 3.4.4]
in terms of thin sets (cf. Section
1.3
).
For more recent developments see for example
[
Zyw10
]
,
and see
[
Zan98
]
for a version uniform across number fields.
Effective versions,
starting with
[
Dèb96
,
SZ95
]
,
give upper bounds on the smallest
\tau\in\mathbb{N}
with
f(\tau,x)
irreducible,
see
[
PS24
,
CD+23
]
for recent results.

1.2.
Hilbertian fields

Lang
[
Lan62
]
coined the term
Hilbertian
for fields
K

over which the statement of Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem holds:

Definition 1.8
.

A field
K
is
Hilbertian

if for every irreducible
f\in K[t,x]
, separable and of positive degree in
x
,
there exist infinitely many
\tau\in K
with
f(\tau,x)\in K[x]
irreducible.

Example 1.9
.

Finite fields and algebraically closed fields are trivially not Hilbertian.
The same holds for
K=\mathbb{R}
(take e.g.
f=x^{2}-t^{2}-1
) and
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(cf.
[
FJ08
, Example 15.5.5]
).
In fact, no local field is Hilbertian.

Theorem 1.10
.

The field
K
is Hilbertian in each of the following cases:

(1)

K
is a number field.

(2)

K
is finitely generated and transcendental over some field
k
.

Proof.

(1) was in fact already proven by Hilbert
[
Hil92
]
.
(2) is a Bertini-type argument, see e.g.
[
FJ08
, Theorem 13.4.2]
.
∎

In particular, all global fields
and more generally all infinite fields that are
finitely generated
(i.e. finitely generated over their prime field) are Hilbertian.
Many more fields are known to be Hilbertian,
in particular various fields of power series, like

{\rm Frac}(\mathbb{C}[\![x,y]\!])
and
{\rm Frac}(\mathbb{Z}[\![t]\!])
, cf.
[
Wei82
,
FP13
]
.
In Section
2.1
we will discuss results regarding when
an algebraic extension of a Hilbertian field is again Hilbertian.
Definition
1.8
is equivalent to several variants of it:

Proposition 1.11
.

For a field
K
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

K
is Hilbertian.

(2)

For every

r,n\in\mathbb{N}
and every

f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in K[t_{1},\dots,t_{n},x]
that are irreducible in
K(\underline{t})[x]

and separable in
x
,
and for every
0\neq g\in K[\underline{t}]

there exists
\underline{\tau}\in K^{n}
with
g(\underline{\tau})\neq 0

such that
f_{1}(\underline{\tau},x),\dots,f_{r}(\underline{\tau},x)\in K[x]
are irreducible.

(3)

For every
r,n\in\mathbb{N}
and every absolutely irreducible
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in K[t_{1},\dots,t_{n},x]
that are monic, separable and of degree at least
2
in
x

there exists
\underline{\tau}\in K^{n}
such that each
f_{i}(\underline{\tau},x)
has no zero in
K
.

(4)

For every absolutely irreducible
f\in K[t,x]
that is separable and of positive degree in
x
, there exist infinitely many
\tau\in K
with
f(\tau,x)\in K[x]
irreducible.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
is
[
FJ08
, Prop. 13.2.2]
,

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
is
[
FJ08
, Lemma 12.1.6]
,

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
is
[
FJ08
, Lemma 13.1.2]
,

(1)\Rightarrow(4)
is trivial
and
(4)\Rightarrow(1)
is
[
Bar08
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

Remark 1.12
.

Hilbertian fields play an important role in Galois theory, model theory and field arithmetic.
For example, it is conjectured that over every Hilbertian field
K
,
the inverse Galois problem has a positive answer,
and, more generally,
that every so-called finite split embedding problem is solvable
[
DD99
]
.
This was proven in
[
Pop96
]
in the case that
K
is
large
,
i.e. every smooth
K
-curve
X
satisfies
|X(K)|\in\{0,\infty\}
.
If a field
K
is
pseudo-algebraically closed
(also called PAC),
i.e. every geometrically integral
K
-variety
X
satisfies
X(K)\neq\emptyset
,
a celebrated theorem of Fried-Völklein and Roquette states that
K
is Hilbertian if and only if the absolute Galois group of
K
is
\omega
-free
[
Jar11
, Theorem 5.10.3]
.
This allows a satisfactory description of the first-order theory of Hilbertian PAC fields
and makes them an object of intense study in model theory, see e.g.
[
Cha02
]
.

.

Extensive sources on Hilbertian fields are

,

[
FJ08

,

[

, and

[

For

of positive characteristic,
there are also variants of Definition

in the literature that are not equivalent to it.

and is the one commonly used nowadays.

and we mention only some of them:
fully Hilbertian fields
[
BP13
]
,

g
-Hilbertian fields
[
FJ08
, Chapter 13.6]
,
RG-Hilbertian fields and R-Hilbertian fields
[
DH99
]
,
real Hilbertian fields
[
FHV94

,
and
\Theta
-Hilbertian fields
[

.
There are also the notions of Hilbertian rings

and integrally Hilbertian rings
[

]

1.3.
The Hilbert property

Motivated by the failure of the Noether program,
Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc and Serre
[
CS87
,
Ser08
]

investigated whether it might be possible to specialize
the cover
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}/G
to a
Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
G

even if
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}/G
is not rational.
This led them to the following definition.
Here,
X
is a
K
-variety (i.e. an integral separated scheme of finite type over
K
),
and a
cover
is a finite surjective generically étale morphism of
K
-varieties.

Definition 1.14
.

A subset
\Sigma\subseteq X(K)
is
thin (in
X
)
if there are
n\in\mathbb{N}

and for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
a
K
-variety
Y_{i}
and a cover
\pi_{i}\colon Y_{i}\to X

with
{\rm deg}(\pi_{i})>1
such that

\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(Y_{i}(K))

is not Zariski-dense in
X
.
The variety
X
has the
Hilbert property (“
X
has HP”)
if
X(K)
is not thin in
X
.

Example 1.15
.

By definition, if
X(K)
is not Zariski-dense in
X
, then
X
does not have HP.
So for example, by Faltings’s theorem, no curve
X
of genus
g_{X}>1
over a number field
K
has HP.

Example 1.16
.

An elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
does not have HP:
By Mordell’s theorem,
2E(\mathbb{Q})
has finite index in
E(\mathbb{Q})
.
Thus if
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\in E(\mathbb{Q})
is a system of coset representatives of
2E(\mathbb{Q})
, then
E(\mathbb{Q})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\pi_{i}(E(\mathbb{Q}))
,
where
\pi_{i}\colon E\rightarrow E
is the cover of degree
4
given by

P\mapsto 2P+P_{i}
.
The same argument works for any nonzero abelian variety
A

over a finitely generated field
K
of characteristic zero,
as
A(K)
is finitely generated by the theorems of Mordell–Weil and Lang–Néron, cf.
[
Ser08
, Exercise 4 on p. 20]
.

Remark 1.17
.

By definition, HP is a birational property,
so if
X
is birationally equivalent to a
K
-variety
X^{\prime}
(written
X\sim X^{\prime}
),
then
X
has HP if and only if
X^{\prime}
does.
In Definition
1.14
one can replace the finite surjective generically étale morphisms
\pi\colon Y_{i}\rightarrow X
by generically finite generically étale dominant rational maps
\pi_{i}\colon Y_{i}\dashrightarrow X
without changing the class of thin sets.
Similarly, one could demand that the
Y_{i}
are geometrically integral, since otherwise
\pi_{i}(Y_{i}(K))
is not Zariski-dense in
X
.

Example 1.18
.

\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
has HP if and only if
K
is Hilbertian, as follows essentially from Proposition
1.11
(3) and Remark
1.17
.
For example,
if
K
is finitely generated and infinite,
then every
K
-variety
X

that is rational (i.e.
X\sim\mathbb{A}_{K}^{n}
for some
n
) has HP (Theorem
1.10
and Remark
1.17
).

Example 1.19
.

If
K
is Hilbertian and PAC (cf. Remark
1.12
),
then every
K
-variety has HP (cf.
[
BFP25
, Prop. 5.1]
).
An example of such a field is
\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr}(\sqrt{-1})
,
where
\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr}
is the field
of totally real algebraic numbers,
which itself is neither Hilbertian nor PAC,
cf.
[
BFP25
, Prop. 5.6]
.

The connection to the Noether program is given by the following observation,
which is the geometric version of the equivalence of (2) and (3) in Proposition
1.11
:

Proposition 1.20
.

A
K
-variety
X
has HP if and only if
for every
K
-variety
Y
and every Galois cover
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X

there exists a Zariski-dense set of
x\in X(K)
such that the fiber
\pi^{-1}(x)
is integral.

Proof.

For
\Rightarrow
,
if
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
is a Galois cover with Galois group
G
,

\pi^{-1}(x)
is integral for every
x\in X(K)
over which
\pi
is unramified
and which does not lie in the image of
(Y/H)(K)
for the finitely many proper subgroups
H
of
G
, cf.
[
Ser08
, Prop. 3.3.1]
.
For
\Leftarrow
, if
\pi_{i}\colon Y_{i}\rightarrow X
are covers with
{\rm deg}(\pi_{i})>1
,
let
Y
be the normalization of
X
in the Galois closure of the compositum of the function fields

K(Y_{i})
(embedded in an algebraic closure of
K(X)
).
Then every
x\in X(K)
with
\pi^{-1}(x)
integral does not lie in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(Y_{i}(K))
.
∎

So if
X=\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}/G
has HP, then there exists
x\in X(K)
for which
\pi^{-1}(x)
is integral, where
\pi\colon\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow X
is as above, and then

\pi^{-1}(x)={\rm Spec}(L)
for a Galois extension
L/K
with
{\rm Gal}(L/K)\cong G
,
similar to Corollary
1.4
.
This raised the questions whether all unirational varieties over
\mathbb{Q}

(i.e. all
X
which admit a dominant rational map
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}\dashrightarrow X
for some
n
)
have HP,
and which varieties over number fields in general have HP.
Regarding the first question, conjecturally
all unirational varieties over number fields have HP:

Definition 1.21
.

A variety
X
over a number field
K
satisfies
weak weak approximation (“
X
satisfies WWA”)
if there is a finite set
S_{0}
of places of
K
such that
for every finite set
S_{1}
of places of
K
with
S_{1}\cap S_{0}=\emptyset
,
the image of the diagonal embedding
X(K)\rightarrow\prod_{v\in S_{1}}X(K_{v})

is dense, where
K_{v}
denotes the completion of
K
at
v
.

For example,
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
satisfies WWA (in fact with
S_{0}=\emptyset
)
by the usual Artin–Whaples weak approximation theorem
[
Lan02
, Theorem XII.1.2]
, and therefore so does every smooth rational
K
-variety, cf.
[
Ser08
, Lemma 3.5.5]
.

Theorem 1.22
.

If a variety
X
over a number field
K
satisfies WWA, then
X
has HP.

Proof.

This is
[
Ser08
, Theorem 3.5.7]
, where it is
deduced from the Lang–Weil estimates
and is attributed to
[
Eke90
]
and
a letter of Colliot-Thélène to Ekedahl from 1987
1
1

1

mistakenly dated 1988 in
[
Ser08
]
. This letter is also the reference given for the following conjecture.
∎

Conjecture 1.23
.

Every smooth unirational variety over a number field satisfies WWA, so in particular it has HP.

Regarding the second question, Corvaja and Zannier observed an important necessary condition,
and they raised the question whether this might possibly be the only one:

Theorem 1.24
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety over a finitely generated field
K
.
If
X
has HP, then
X_{\overline{K}}
is simply connected in the sense that every étale cover
Y\rightarrow X_{\overline{K}}
is trivial.

Proof.

In the case where
K
is a number field,
this is
[
CZ17
, Theorem 1.4]
, which proves and uses a variant of the Chevalley–Weil theorem for number fields.
The same proof goes through for finitely generated
K

by using the corresponding generalization of the Chevalley–Weil theorem in
[
CD+22
, Theorem 3.8]
; see
[
Lug22
, Theorem 3.2]
.
∎

Question 1.25

(
[
CZ17
, Question-Conjecture 1]
)
.

Does every
smooth projective variety
X
over a number field
K

with
X_{\overline{K}}
simply connected
and
X(K)
Zariski-dense in
X
have HP?

Remark 1.26
.

As evidence that the answer to Question
1.25
might be positive, Corvaja and Zannier draw conclusions about Kummer varieties
[
CZ17
, p. 589]
which they then prove in the case of Kummer surfaces
[
CZ17
, Appendix]
,
also cf. Section
5.2
.
However, they also give an example
[
CZ17
, Theorem 1.5]
that shows that the smoothness assumption is necessary,
and they suggest that it might be necessary to
allow for a finite extension of the field
K
for the answer to be positive.

Theorem
1.24
gives another explanation for why
abelian varieties over number fields do not have HP (Example
1.16
).

Remark 1.27

1.4.
The weak Hilbert property

Their observation (Theorem
1.24
) motivated Corvaja and Zannier to introduce the following weakening of the Hilbert property in
[
CZ17
, Section 2.2]
.
In the following,
let
K
always be a field of characteristic zero
and
X
a normal
K
-variety.
A cover is
ramified
if it is not étale.

Definition 1.29
.

A subset
\Sigma\subseteq X(K)
is
strongly thin (in
X
)
if there are
n\in\mathbb{N}

and for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
a normal
K
-variety
Y_{i}
and a ramified cover
\pi_{i}\colon Y_{i}\to X

such that
\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(Y_{i}(K))

is not Zariski-dense in
X
.
The variety
X
has the
weak Hilbert property (“
X
has WHP”)
if
X(K)
is not strongly thin in
X
.

Trivially, if
X
has HP, then
X
has WHP,
and if
X_{\overline{K}}
is simply connected (in the sense of Theorem
1.24
), then the converse holds.
Again, if
X(K)
is not Zariski-dense in
X
, then
X
does not have WHP.

Example 1.30
.

If
K
is a number field and
X=E
is an elliptic curve of positive rank, then

has WHP:
If
\pi_{i}\colon Y_{i}\rightarrow E
is a ramified cover, then

has genus at least
2
by the Riemann–Hurwitz formula,
and therefore
Y_{i}(K)
is finite by Faltings’s theorem.
In fact, this argument shows that no infinite
\Sigma\subseteq X(K)
is strongly thin.
See also Section
3
where we discuss curves in more detail,
and Section
4
for generalizations to abelian varieties.

We have the following analogue of Remark
1.17
:

Proposition 1.31
.

If
X
and
X^{\prime}
are smooth proper geometrically connected
K
-varieties with
X\sim X^{\prime}
,
then

has WHP if and only if
X^{\prime}
does.

Proof.

This is
[
CD+22
, Prop. 3.1]
.
∎

1.5.
Integral Hilbert properties

There are several variants of HP and WHP in the literature, both explicit and implicit, that refer to integral rather than rational points.
We will mention such results only occasionally,
and the reader interested mainly in HP and WHP may safely skip this subsection.
To talk about integral points, we need to work with models of our varieties over
\mathbb{Z}
or whatever the ring of “integers” of
K
should be:

Let
R
be an integral domain with fraction field
K
and let
X
be a quasi-projective variety over
K
. A
model for
X
over
R
is a quasi-projective flat integral
R
-scheme
\mathcal{X}
together with an isomorphism
\mathcal{X}_{K}\cong X
over
K
.
We will usually omit the isomorphism from our notation.
If
\mathcal{X}
is a model for
X
over
R
, then
\mathcal{X}(R)
is naturally a subset of
X(K)
.

Example 1.37
.

Assume
X=\mathrm{Spec}(K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}))\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
is the zero locus of a finite collection of polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}\in R[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
prime in
R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Then
\mathcal{X}=\mathrm{Spec}(R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}))
is a model for
X
over
R
.

If
K
is a number field and
S
a finite set of finite places of
K
,
we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
the ring of
S
-integers of
K
.
In this setting,
if
X
is a variety over
K

and
\mathcal{X}
is a fixed model of
X
over
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
,

[
Coc19
]

defines

to have the
(weak)
S
-integral Hilbert property

(IHP/WIHP) if
\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{K,S})
is not (strongly) thin in
X
.
A more general definition can be found in
[
Lug22
]
,
which would in this situation say that
\mathcal{X}
has the (weak) Hilbert property over
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
.

Remark 1.38
.

If
R
is a regular noetherian integral domain with fraction field
K
and
\mathcal{X}
is a model for
X
over
R
,

[

defines

to have the Hilbert property over

if the set of near-

-integral points on

where a

is

(as defined by Vojta

with complement of codimension at least two and a morphism

extending

.
However, if
\dim R=1
(e.g.,

for

Example 1.39
.

Let
X=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
X^{\prime}=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{0\}
,
with models

\mathcal{X}={\rm Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[t])
respectively

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}={\rm Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}])
over
\mathbb{Z}
.
By Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem (Remark

),
X(\mathbb{Z})
is not thin in
X
,
but

X^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})=\{1,-1\}
is thin in
X^{\prime}
(equivalently, in
X
).
Similarly,
if
K
is a number field and
S
a finite set of finite places of
K
, then
X^{\prime}(\mathcal{O}_{K,S})=\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}
is thin in
X^{\prime}_{K}
and strongly thin in
X_{K}
,
by Dirichlet’s unit theorem (cf. the argument in Example
1.16
).
For example,
every element of

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q},\{2\}}^{\times}=\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2}]^{\times}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{\times}

is in the image of
X^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
under one of
the four ramified covers

given by

,
z\mapsto-z^{2}
,
z\mapsto 2z^{2}
, and
z\mapsto-2z^{2}
.
However,
X^{\prime}(\mathcal{O}_{K,S})
is not always strongly thin in

:

Proposition 1.40
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
S
be a finite set of finite places of
K
. If
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}
is infinite, then
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}
is not strongly thin in
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

This is similar to the proof of Example
1.30
:
If
\pi\colon C\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}\setminus\{0\}
is a ramified cover with

a smooth curve,
let
\overline{C}
be a smooth projective curve containing

of genus

, and let
\overline{\pi}\colon\overline{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
be the unique extension of

.
Applying the Riemann–Hurwitz formula to
\overline{\pi}
, we see that
2g_{\overline{C}}-2+\#(\overline{C}_{\overline{K}}\setminus C_{\overline{K}})>0
,
so Siegel’s theorem (more precisely Mahler’s generalization thereof, cf.
[

, Theorem 1]
), gives that any model of

over
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
has only finitely many
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
-points.
This shows that
if
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}
is infinite, then it is not strongly thin.
In fact,
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}
contains an infinite cyclic subgroup
\Gamma
,
and already
\Gamma
is not strongly thin in
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}\setminus\{0\}

by

[

, Theorem 4]

(cf. Theorem

below),
which does not use Siegel’s theorem.
∎

A positive answer to Question

Definition 1.41

The

has

potential HP
, respectively

,
if

has HP, respectively WHP, for some finite field extension

So in the case where

is a number field,
a positive answer to this weaker version of Question

would offer a precise prediction when

should have potential WHP,
namely if and only if
X(L)
is Zariski-dense in

for some finite extension

.
We now discuss for which

the latter condition is expected to be satisfied.
Results and conjectures in this subsection will only be used to motivate some questions and results later on.

Recall that a projective variety
X
over
K
is of

if there is a resolution of singularities
Y\to X
such that the
canonical divisor
K_{Y}

is big.
Equivalently,
X
is of maximal Kodaira dimension
\kappa(X)={\rm dim}(X)
.
Lang’s conjecture
[
Lan86
, Conjecture 5.7]
predicts that varieties of general type over number fields have few rational points,
and therefore do not have HP:

Conjecture 1.42
.

A projective variety
X
over a number field
K
is of general type
if and only if there is a proper closed subset
\Delta\subsetneq X_{\overline{K}}
such that
X(L)\setminus\Delta
is finite for every finite field extension
L/K
.

In
[
Cam04
, Definition 2.1]
(see also
[
Cam11
, Definition 8.1]
or
[
BJL24
, Definition 1.2]
) Campana introduced the class of
special
varieties
and he conjectured that the arithmetic properties of
special varieties are opposite to those conjectured by Lang for varieties of general type; see
[
Cam11
, Conjecture 13.23]
. Combined with the expected positive answer to the weakened version (see Remark
1.35
) of
Question
1.34
,
extended to finitely generated fields,
this gives:

Conjecture 1.43
.

If
K
is finitely generated and
X
smooth projective,
the following are equivalent.

(1)

X
has potential WHP.

(2)

There is a finite field extension
L/K
such that
X(L)
is Zariski-dense in
X
.

(3)

X
is special.

We will not make use of the precise definition, but for completeness we include a definition for projective varieties: If
X
is projective over a field
K
of characteristic zero, it is
special if and only if there is a proper birational surjective morphism
Y\to X
with
Y
a smooth projective variety over
K
with no Bogomolov sheaves,
where a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
Y
is a
Bogomolov sheaf (for
Y
)
if there is an integer
1\leq p\leq\dim Y
such that there is a nonzero morphism
\mathcal{L}\to\Lambda^{p}\Omega^{1}_{Y}
and the Iitaka dimension
\kappa(\mathcal{L})
of
\mathcal{L}
,
which is always at most
p

[
Bog78
, §12, Theorem 4]
,
is equal to
p
.
This notion is
independent of the base field:
X
is special if and only if
X_{L}
is special, where
L/K
is any field extension
[
BJ25+
]
.

Example 1.44
.

Examples of special varieties
include algebraic groups
[
Bar25
, Lemma 2.11]
, smooth projective (geometrically) rationally connected varieties (e.g., Fano varieties, cf. Section
6.2
)
[

, Corollary 2.28]
, varieties with Kodaira dimension zero (e.g., hyperkähler varieties and Calabi–Yau varieties)
[
Cam04
, Theorem 5.1]

are either Fano varieties or have Kodaira dimension zero, and are thus special.

Campana established several properties of his class of special varieties.
We record some of his results here.

Proposition 1.45
.

Let
X
and
Y
be projective geometrically integral
K
-varieties.

(1)

If
X
is special and

is birationally equivalent to

, then
Y
is special.

(2)

If

is a surjective morphism and

is special, then
Y
is special.

(3)

If
X\to Y
is an étale cover, then

is special if and only if
Y
is special.

(4)

If
X
and

Proof.

For (1), choose proper birational surjective morphisms

and

is special if and only if
Z
is special if and only if

is special by
[
BJL24
, Lemma 2.8(2)]
. For (2) and (3) see
[
BJL24

.
∎

2.
Preservation theorems

In this section we discuss the preservation of Hilbert properties
under various algebraic and geometric constructions.
We always let
K
be a field of characteristic zero.

2.1.
Base change

Already Hilbert observed that his theorem for
\mathbb{Q}

implies the same statement over arbitrary number fields. In fact, any finite extension of a Hilbertian field is again Hilbertian.
Similarly, HP and WHP are preserved under finite field extensions.
We now give some examples of what is known regarding infinite extensions (which includes references for the case of finite extensions).
Let

X
be a normal
K
-variety and let

L/K
be a field extension.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
K
is Hilbertian, then so is its extension
L
,
whenever one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

L/K
is finitely generated.

(2)

L/K
is small, i.e.
L/K
is algebraic and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there are only finitely many intermediate fields
K\subseteq F\subseteq L
with
[F:K]\leq n
.

(3)

There are Galois extensions
M_{1}/K
and
M_{2}/K
such that

L\subseteq M_{1}M_{2}
,
L\not\subseteq M_{1}
,
L\not\subseteq M_{2}
.

(4)

There are
m\in\mathbb{N}
and fields
K=K_{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq K_{m}
with
L\subseteq K_{m}

such that for each
i
,
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
is Galois with Galois group either abelian
or a (possibly infinite) product of finite simple groups.

(5)

L/K
is algebraic and there is

and for each prime number

a Galois representation (i.e. a continuous homomorphism)

\rho_{\ell}:{\rm Gal}(\overline{K}/K)\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
such that

is fixed by
\bigcap_{\ell}{\rm ker}(\rho_{\ell})
.

Proof.

(1) is classical, see e.g.
[
FJ08

(2) is written down in the special case where

is Galois in
[

;
it applies in particular when

is finitely generated as a profinite group;

(2) below.
(3) is
[

it applies in particular when

is a proper finite extension of a Galois extension of

[

(4) is
[

]
,
and the special case where

[
Kuy70
]

(5) builds on (4) and is
[

3.
Curves

We summarize what is known regarding HP and WHP for curves.
Let
K
always be a field of characteristic zero.
By a

over

Proposition 3.1

Let

(Proposition

However, the case where

.

Let

be finitely generated

Since

Then

does not have HP (cf. Example

),

is Zariski-dense in

[

Zariski-dense

[

]

Remark 4.9

Proof.

It suffices to show that

By

5.
Surfaces

Let

be a field of characteristic zero
and

a

over

a smooth projective geometrically integral variety over

of dimension two.
We discuss what is known regarding HP and WHP for

We recall that

under birational equivalence and base change
[

, Proposition 9.1.2]

and

is birationally equivalent to a minimal surface
[

, Theorem 6.3]
,
to which the Enriques–Kodaira classification applies,

,

]
.

5.1.
Surfaces with
\kappa(X)=-\infty

By a theorem of Enriques
[
Băd01
, Theorem 13.2]
,

\kappa(X)=-\infty
if and only if

is
birationally ruled
,
i.e.

for a (smooth projective) curve

.

The
irregularity

of

is a birational invariant
[
Băd01
, Remark 6.4]
and invariant under base change,
so if

,
then

[

, Corollary V.2.5]
.

We start with the most simple observation regarding

:

Proposition 5.1
.

If
K
is Hilbertian,
\kappa(X)=-\infty
and
q(X)=0
, then
X
has potential HP.

Proof.

Since
X_{\overline{K}}\sim\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{K}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{K}}
is rational, there exists
L/K
finite with
X_{L}
rational, and
L
is Hilbertian,
so
X_{L}
has HP (Proposition
1.33
).
∎

Similarly easy, the question whether
X
has HP or WHP
is well-understood if already

is birationally ruled (and not only

):

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
K
be finitely generated and let

be a surface that is birationally ruled over
K
.

(1)

X
has HP if and only if
q(X)=0
and
X(K)
is Zariski-dense in
X
.

(2)

X
has WHP if and only if
q(X)\leq 1
and
X(K)
is Zariski-dense in
X
.

(3)

X
has potential HP if and only if
q(X)=0
.

(4)

X
has potential WHP if and only if
q(X)\leq 1
.

Proof.

Since HP and WHP are birational invariants (of smooth projective varieties, see Remark
1.17
and Proposition
1.31
),
we can assume that
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}\times C
for a curve
C
over
K
of genus

.
By Theorem
2.14
and Theorem
2.5

applied to the projection
X\rightarrow C
,

X
has HP or WHP if and only if
C
has.
Moreover,
X(K)
is Zariski-dense in
X
if and only if
C(K)
is Zariski-dense in
C
.
Therefore, all claims follow from Theorem
3.2
for the curve

.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
K
be finitely generated and let
X
be a surface over
K
with
\kappa(X)=-\infty
. Then

has potential WHP if and only if
q(X)\leq 1
.

Proof.

There is a finite extension

such that
X_{L}
is birationally ruled over

,
and

has potential WHP if and only if
X_{L}
does,
so the claim follows from Proposition

(4).
∎

If

is birationally ruled, but

itself is not,
then (3) and (4) of Proposition

still hold,
as do
\Rightarrow
in (1) and (2),
and we now discuss what can be said regarding
\Leftarrow
in (1) and (2).

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
K
be finitely generated and
X
a surface over
K
with
\kappa(X)=-\infty
and
q(X)=1
.
If
X(K)
is Zariski-dense in
X
, then
X
has WHP.

Proof.

The Albanese variety

of
X
(cf.
[
Băd01
, Definition 5.2, Theorem 5.3]
) is an elliptic curve, since
{\rm dim}(E)=q(X)=1
,
and so the Albanese map

(induced by the choice of a base point in

) is surjective.
If
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\to}}C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\to}}E
is the Stein factorization of

,
then

is again an elliptic curve,
as follows from
g_{C}\geq g_{E}
and

,
and so

and

by the universal property of the Albanese variety.
In particular,

has geometrically connected fibers.
By generic smoothness, almost all fibers of

are also smooth.
So since (a dense open subset of)

is covered by rational curves,
which are contracted by the Albanese map,
almost all fibers of

are (smooth projective) curves of genus zero.
As

is Zariski-dense in

,
the set

of

such that

is infinite
and therefore not strongly thin in

(see Remark

).
Since

has HP for every

),
it follows
from Theorem

(2)
that

has WHP.
∎

If
\kappa(X)=-\infty
and
q(X)=0
, then

X_{\overline{K}}
is rational and so

where

(1)

admits a conic fibration,

(2)

is a del Pezzo surface of degree

is ample and its self-intersection

equals

cf.
[

, Corollary on p. 27]
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let

(2)

Proof.

(1) is
[

[

, Corollary 3.7]

for

[

]

.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

As it is believed that every surface

5.2.
Surfaces with
\kappa(X)=0

By the Enriques–Kodaira classification,
if

then
X_{\overline{K}}\sim X^{\prime}

with a surface

(2)

).

(3)

with

.

We briefly explain how to deduce this from
[

, Section 10]

By

, if

(K^{2})=0
,

.

.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let

be finitely generated and

.
Then

A surface

Theorem 5.8
.

Let

.
In case

This is

∎

Remark 5.9
.

Since all smooth projective varieties with Kodaira dimension zero are special

Remark 5.10

Another class of K3 surfaces (case

We summarize the open problems on the arithmetic of a K3 surface

(1)

Does there exist a finite field extension

(2)

(3)

Remark 5.11
.

Let

be a number field, let

be a smooth plane cubic in

and let

.
Using conic fibrations on

,
[

, Theorem 1.14]
shows that there is a finite field extension

, a finite set of finite places

of

, and a model

for

over

]
.

5.3.
Surfaces with
\kappa(X)=1

If
X
has Kodaira dimension one,
then the canonical divisor
K_{X}
defines an elliptic fibration
f\colon X\to C
where
C
is a curve of genus
q(X)
; see
[
Lie13
, Theorem 6.3]
.
As in the proof of Proposition
5.2
, one can show that if

K
is finitely generated and

X
has potential WHP, then
q(X)\leq 1
.
For every closed point
c
of
C
, the fibre
X_{c}
is divisor on
X
, and so we may write
X_{c}=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{c}}a_{i}F_{i}
, where the
F_{i}
are the distinct irreducible components of
X_{c}
,
and we define
m_{c}:=\mathrm{gcd}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r_{c}})
.
The elliptic fibration
X\to C
has
general type orbifold base
if

2g_{C}-2+\sum_{c\in C\textrm{ closed}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{m_{c}}\Big)>0.

Moreover, a surface
X
of Kodaira dimension one is special if and only if its elliptic fibration does not have a general type orbifold base
[
Cam04
, Lemma 3.34]
.
Therefore, Conjecture
1.43

predicts that if
K
is finitely generated, then
X
has potential WHP if and only if its elliptic fibration does not have a general type orbifold base.
We prove the forward direction of this:

Proposition 5.12
.

Assume that

K
is finitely generated,
\kappa(X)=1

and the canonical elliptic fibration
f\colon X\to C
has general type orbifold base.
Then
X(L)
is not Zariski-dense in
X
for any finitely generated field extension
L/K
.
In particular,
X
does not have potential WHP.

Proof.

Since
f
has general type orbifold base, it follows from the theory of elliptic fibrations
[
Cam04
, Lemma 3.34]
that there is a finite field extension
L/K
, a curve
D
of genus at least two over
L
, an étale cover
X^{\prime}\to X_{L}
from a surface
X^{\prime}
over
L
with
\kappa(X^{\prime})=1
and
q(X^{\prime})\geq 2
, and an elliptic fibration
X^{\prime}\to D
.
Since
D
has genus at least two, it follows from Faltings’s theorem
[
Fal84
]
that
X^{\prime}(M)
is not dense for any finitely generated field extension
M/L
. Therefore, by the Chevalley–Weil theorem
[
CD+22
, Theorem 3.8]
, it follows that
also
X(M)
is not Zariski-dense in
X
for such
M
.
∎

5.4.
Surfaces with
\kappa(X)=2

Finally, if the Kodaira dimension of
X
is two (hence maximal), then
X
is of general type (by definition). In particular,
Lang’s conjecture (Conjecture

) implies that
if

is a number field, then

does not have potential WHP.

6.
Some higher-dimensional varieties

We conclude this survey with some results on varieties of arbitrary dimension (excluding algebraic groups, which are discussed in Section

As before, let

6.1.
Closed subvarieties of abelian varieties

Work of Faltings
[

]

implies the following characterization of subvarieties of abelian varieties with potential WHP.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that
K
is finitely generated,
let
A
be an abelian variety over
K

and let
X\subseteq A
be a closed subvariety
which is geometrically integral.
Then the normalization
X^{\prime}
of

has potential WHP if and only if
X_{\overline{K}}
is the translate of an abelian subvariety of

.

Proof.

If
X
is the translate of an abelian subvariety of
A
, then
X=X^{\prime}
has potential WHP by Theorem
4.2
.
Conversely, assume that
X^{\prime}_{L}
has WHP for a finite extension

of
K
.
Then
X(L)
is dense in

and therefore
X_{L}
is the translate of an abelian subvariety of

by
the special case of the Mordell–Lang conjecture proved in

[
Fal94
]
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

If
X
as in Theorem
6.1
has positive dimension, then it does not have potential HP.
Indeed, otherwise by
Theorem
6.1

there exists a finite extension
L/K

such that

has HP and can be endowed with the structure of an abelian variety, contradicting Example
1.16
.

6.2.
Fano varieties

A smooth projective geometrically integral
K
-variety
X

is a
Fano variety
if
the anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is ample.
A Fano variety
X
is special (as it is geometrically rationally connected, cf. Example
1.44
), and
X_{\overline{K}}
is simply connected
[
Deb01
, Corollary 4.29]
.
Thus,
if
K
is finitely generated, then
Conjecture
1.43
predicts that
X
has potential HP.
This is currently not known, unless
\dim X\leq 2
(in which case
X
is geometrically rational, cf. Section
5.1
),
and for the special case
of del Pezzo varieties of degree at least three (see
[
Str21
, Definition 4.1]
for the definition used here):

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth del Pezzo variety over
K
of degree
d\geq 3
and dimension
n\geq 2
with
X(K)\neq\emptyset
. Then
X
has HP in each of the following cases:

(1)

K
is Hilbertian and
d\geq 4
.

(2)

K
is Hilbertian,
d=3
, and there exists a line on
X
(under its anticanonical embedding).

(3)

K
is a number field and

.

Proof.

(1) is
[
Str21
, Theorem 1.5(a)]
.
(2) is
[
Str21
, Theorem 1.5(b)]
.
(3) is
[
Dem21
, Theorem 3.1]
,
as
X
is a smooth cubic hypersurface in this case.
Indeed,
the fundamental class
A_{X}
is very ample and
\dim|A_{X}|=d+n-2=n+1

[
KP23
, Proposition 2.2(i,iv)]
,
hence
the embedding

associated to

realizes

as a smooth hypersurface of degree

.
The special case

was obtained by a similar argument in
[
Man86
, Theorem 46.1]
, as Colliot-Thélène pointed out to us,
and also follows from
[
SD01
]
, cf. Theorem
5.5
.
∎

6.3.
Smooth projective varieties with nef tangent bundle

Let
K
be finitely generated and

X
smooth and projective.
Specialness is a “positivity” condition on the tangent bundle
T_{X}
of
X
. For example, if
T_{X}
is ample, trivial, or nef, then
X
is special. In the two extreme cases that
T_{X}
is trivial or ample, the situation is well-understood:

(1)

If
T_{X}
is ample, then
X
has potential HP. Indeed, by Mori’s celebrated proof of Hartshorne’s conjecture
[
Mor79
]
, we have that
X_{\overline{K}}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\overline{K}}
, so that it follows from Theorem
1.10
that
X
has potential HP.

(2)

If
T_{X}
is trivial, then
X_{\overline{K}}
has the structure of an abelian variety. In particular,
X
has potential WHP by Theorem
4.2
.

If
T_{X}
is nef, then the geometry of
X
is not fully understood yet,
but
[
DPS94
]
shows that
the Albanese map
X\to\mathrm{Alb}(X)
is smooth proper surjective and its fibres are Fano varieties with nef tangent bundle.

Theorem 6.4
.

Assume that Fano varieties with nef tangent bundle are homogeneous spaces
(the Campana–Peternell conjecture
[
CP91
, p. 170]
).
If
K
is a number field and
X
is a smooth projective variety over
K
with
T_{X}
nef, then
X
has potential WHP.

Proof.

This is
[
Jav22
, Theorem 1.8]
, which uses
Theorem
2.14
(2) and Theorem
4.6
(2).
∎

6.4.
Kummer varieties

If
A
is an abelian variety, then the Kummer variety
\mathrm{Kum}(A)
associated to
A
is the quotient of
A
by the natural involution on
A
(given by multiplication with
-1
).
Such varieties are special (Example

1.45
(2)) and simply connected, and should therefore have potential HP if

.
In this direction we have the following result:

Theorem 6.5
.

If
K

H

K

,
and
\mathrm{Alb}^{1}(H)

\mathrm{Kum}(\mathrm{Alb}^{1}(H))

Proof.

This is

6.5.
Symmetric products

Let
X
be smooth and projective.
Similar to
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}/G
in Section
1.1
,
the
n
-th symmetric power of
X
is the quotient variety
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(X)=X^{n}/S_{n}
, which is
a normal projective variety of dimension
n\cdot\dim(X)
.

Example 6.6
.

If
X
is rational over
K
of positive dimension and has HP,
then
K
is Hilbertian (Proposition
1.33
)
and
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(X)
is rational
[
Mat68
]
,
and thus
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(X)
has HP.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
K
be finitely generated,

C
a curve of genus
g_{C}\geq 2
over
K
,
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(1)

{\rm Sym}^{n}(C)
has potential WHP if and only if
n\geq g_{C}
.

(2)

\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K})
has potential WHP for every
n\geq g_{C}
.

Proof.

(1) is
[
BJL24
, Lemma 6.3]
.
(2) is
[
BJL24
, Theorem B]
,
whose proof uses Theorem
2.14
, Theorem
4.4
and (1).
∎

Remark 6.8
.

Note that
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K})
for
n\geq g_{C}

is special, see
[
CCR22
, Theorem 14]
,
so Theorem
6.7
confirmed a case of Conjecture
1.43
.
In general, Conjecture
1.43

suggests that
if
K
is finitely generated and

X
has potential WHP, then
by Proposition
1.45
also
any resolution of singularities of
{\rm Sym}^{n}(X)
has potential WHP.
This, however, is weaker than the statement that

{\rm Sym}^{n}(X)
itself has potential WHP.
Moreover, it does not apply to

X=C
or

X=C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
as in Theorem
6.7
,
cf. Theorem
3.2
and Proposition

Incidence and predictors of stroke among hypertension patients in Awi Zone public hospitals, Northwest Ethiopia: Retrospective follow-up study
Authors: Animut Takele Telayneh, Negese Getie Eneyew, Habtamu Temesgen, Muluken Teshome
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStroke is a medical emergency neurologic dysfunction that occurs when blood flow to the brain is interrupted, due to a blockage or bleeding lasting ≥ 24 h, resulting in brain cell death and severe complications. It is the second most common cause of death, next to coronary heart disease. There are limited findings on the incidence of stroke among hypertension patients in Ethiopia.ObjectivesTo assess the incidence and predictors of stroke among hypertensive patients at Awi Zone public hospitals, Northwest Ethiopia.MethodsA hospital-based retrospective follow-up study was conducted among adult hypertensive patients in Awi public hospitals between April 1, 2016 and March 30, 2023. A computer-generated simple random sampling method was used to select 510 study participants. Data were entered using EpiData version 4.6 and Stata version 17 for coding, cleaning, and analysis. In bi-variable analysis, variables with a p-value < 0.25 were selected for multivariable Cox proportional regression analysis. Finally, in multivariable analysis, variables with a p-value < 0.05, and 95% CI with their AHR were used to declare them as predictors.ResultsThe incidence rate of stroke was 5.53 per 100 person-years [95% CI: 4.60, 6.65]. Rural residence [AHR: 1.88 (95% CI: 1.13, 3.12)], family history of stroke [AHR: 2.52 (95% CI: 1.33, 4.77)], Atrial fibrillation [AHR: 3.10 (95% CI: 1.62, 5.90)], total cholesterol ≥ 200 mg/dl [AHR: 4.42 (95% CI: 2.71, 7.23)], overweight [AHR: 1.82 (95% CI: 1.16, 2.87)], and low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol ≥ 100 mg/dl [AHR: 1.77 (95% CI: 1.11, 2.83)] were statistically significant.ConclusionsIn this study, the incidence rate of stroke was 5.5 per 100 person-years. Rural residence, overweight, atrial fibrillation, high levels of cholesterol and low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol, and a positive family history of stroke were identified as predictors of stroke. To lower the incidence of stroke among hypertension patients, patients should adopt healthy lifestyles such as weight loss and healthy eating, and healthcare professionals should pay special attention to patients presenting with known risk factors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-06071-6.Keywords: Incidence of stroke, Hypertension, Predictor, Retrospective follow-up, Ethiopia

Introduction

Stroke is a medical emergency neurologic dysfunction caused by interruption in blood supply to the brain or bleeding in the brain and/or in the space below the layers covering, persisting ≥ 24 h or until death [1–4]. Ischemic stroke is an acute focal neurological dysfunction caused by focal brain infarction. Hemorrhagic stroke is an acute focal or global neurological dysfunction caused by intracerebral or subarachnoid hemorrhage [1, 2]. Ischemic stroke accounts for about 80% of all strokes worldwide, while hemorrhagic stroke accounts for 10% to 20% [5–7]. Stroke is the second leading cause of death and the third leading cause of death and disability combined globally [1, 8]. The incidence, death, and disability after stroke increased from 1990 to 2021, and an estimated 11.9 million stroke incidence cases occurred in 2021, whereas the burden is increasing dramatically in developing countries [8].

Hypertension is diagnosed as a systolic blood pressure of ≥ 140 mmHg, and/or a diastolic blood pressure of ≥ 90 mmHg on both days of a visit, on different days, or taking hypertension medication [9, 10]. The prevalence of hypertension in Ethiopia ranges from 7.5% to 41.9% with significant geographical variations. Even though, there is plentiful and integrated hypertension control and prevention measures implemented in the healthcare facilities but still it is the major cardiovascular morbidity and mortality [11–14]. Stroke is the most common complication among hypertension patients [9, 15, 16]. High blood pressure exerts an extra strain on the blood vessels, which causes a stroke due to a clot ischemic stroke, or a burst of blood vessel hemorrhagic stroke [9, 17]. Plaques that are released increase the risk of blocking the blood vessels in the brain, resulting in a stroke [18].

A study in China showed that the incidence rate of stroke among hypertensive patients was much higher than the incidence rate in the general population [19–21]. A similar study reported in Nigeria and China, the overall incidence rate of stroke among hypertension patients was 260.6 and 1236.30 per 100,000 person-years, respectively [22, 23]. A study in Korea revealed that hypertension increased the risk of ischemic stroke compared to non-hypertensive people, with an incidence rate of 2.97 per 100 person-years [24]. Whereas the overall incidence rate of stroke among hypertensive patients was 14.19 per 1000 person-years reported in Ghana [25].

Stroke was the primary cause of death and the third most common reason for admission of patients in an intensive care unit in Ethiopia. It is currently one of the major public health problems in Ethiopia, accounting for 7% of deaths [26–28]. Hypertension remains the most important modifiable risk factor for stroke in Africa, including Ethiopia [9, 16, 29, 30]. The incidence and mortality of stroke are increasing in low-income countries as a result of inadequate healthcare systems and changes in social, economic, demographic, and epidemiological patterns [31–33]. Stroke care includes regular monitoring of risk factors, implementation of prevention strategies, improving acute care, and rehabilitation services [30, 34]. Approximately 80% of strokes may be prevented when necessary precautions and actions are taken into account in the risk factors [16, 35].

Increased age, sex, education, residence, cigarette smoking, alcohol use, salty diet, low social support, diabetes mellitus, cholesterol level, family history, atrial fibrillation, blood pressure control level, hematocrit level, heart diseases, chronic rheumatic disease, and anti-hypertension medication adherence all increased the risk of stroke [24, 25, 36–45]. The Ethiopian government attempted to reduce chronic illness risk factors by establishing central triage, chronic care centers, and follow-up services, yet the stroke burden is still increasing. It is also an overlooked growing public health problem in Ethiopia, resulting in lost productivity, premature death, and long-term disability [46, 47]. There is a paucity of evidence in Ethiopia on the incidence of stroke among hypertension patients, but the problem is increasing pragmatically, entailing scientific evidence. This study was conducted in Awi Zone, which have limited access to higher levels of healthcare facilities for better diagnosis and quality of care, are uneducated, and rural residents, which urges further investigation in the study area. The objective of this study was to determine the incidence and predictors of stroke among hypertension patients in Awi Zone public hospitals, Ethiopia. This is helpful for clinicians, programmers, planners, and policymakers in designing innovative interventions for patient management, preventing this growing public health problem, and promoting the well-being and control of comorbid complications in hypertension patients.

Methods

Study design, area, and population

An institution-based retrospective follow-up study was conducted. The healthcare system in Ethiopia is structured as a three-tiered system, including primary, secondary, and tertiary levels. The primary healthcare level consists of health posts, health centers, and a primary hospital that cover 100,000 people, called primary healthcare units, located at the district (Woreda level). The secondary levels are general hospitals serving 1-1.5 million people, and the tertiary levels are specialized hospital that covers 3.5-5 million people [48, 49]. There is an uneven distribution of healthcare resources, specifically in rural areas, which poses a challenge to equitable access to healthcare services [49]. Public hospitals are pivotal in the Ethiopian healthcare system by providing essential health services to the population, such as delivering primary healthcare, managing complex health conditions, and ensuring equitable access to health services [50]. Private health facilities fill the gaps of public health facilities by serving the community that was not served in public facilities. The study used records from April 1, 2016 to March 30, 2023 from Awi Zone public hospitals, including Injibara General Hospital, Chagni, Jawi, Dangila, and Agew Gimijabet primary hospitals. The Awi Zone is bordered by the Benishangul-Gumuz region to the west, the North Gondar Zone to the north, and the West Gojjam Zone to the east and south. It is located 447 km from Ethiopia’s capital, Addis Ababa, and 118 km from Bahir Dar, a city in the Amhara National Regional State. According to the annual report of the Awi Zonal Health Department of 2022, there are five public hospitals and 49 health centers serving a total population of 1, 080, 000 [51]. In these hospitals, hypertension was diagnosed on two visits with systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg. Whereas blood tests, clinical examinations, and brain computed tomography (CT) scans are used to diagnose stroke patients. Chemotherapies, complications management, and rehabilitation services are available treatments. The source population included all hypertension patients who received treatment follow-ups at the Awi Zone public hospitals. Hypertension patients with a prior history of stroke before enrollment in the study, as well as pregnant mothers with pregnancy-induced hypertension, were excluded.

Sample size determination and sampling techniques

The sample size was calculated using Stata version 17 software. A previous study conducted in Bahir Dar used the presence of comorbidities as the predictor of stroke [52]. These are the assumptions used: 0.05 precision level, 0.8 power, 0.5 probability of event, 1.88 hazard rate, and 0.66 withdrawal rate. The calculated sample size was 464, including a 10% dropout rate, resulting in a final sample size of 510. The study was conducted in the Awi Zone public hospitals. All hypertension patients registered in chronic care between April 1, 2016 and March 30, 2022 in the five hospitals were included; proportional allocation method was used to draw a number of study participants to each hospital. A computer-generated simple random sampling technique was used to select the study participants (Fig. 1).

Operational definition

Event: Hypertension patients had developed a stroke during the follow-up period.

Censored: Hypertension patients who were lost to follow-up, did not know when the event of interest occurred, and died without developing the outcome of interest during the follow-up period.

Stroke: Is a neurologic dysfunction caused by an interruption in blood supply to the brain or bleeding in the brain, persisting ≥24 hours, resulting in brain cell death and severe complications [1, 2].

Hypertension: Is diagnosed as a systolic blood pressure of ≥140 mmHg, and/or a diastolic blood pressure of ≥90 mmHg on both days of a visit, on different days. It is classified as grade I 140-159/90-99mm Hg, grade II 160-179/100-109 mm Hg, and grade III ≥180/110 mm Hg [9, 10].

Uncontrolled and controlled blood pressure: Systolic blood pressure: controlled (<140 mmHg) and uncontrolled (≥140 mmHg), and/or diastolic blood pressure: controlled (<90 mmHg) and uncontrolled (≥ 90 mmHg) [42, 44].

Alcohol drinker: It refers to those participants who consume alcohol on average ≥2 drinks/day for males and ≥1 drink for females recently [52-54].

Diabetes mellitus: Diagnosed as a random blood sugar level of ≥200 mg/dL or fasting venous plasma glucose levels of ≥126 mg/dL or HbA1C ≥6.5% conﬁrmed by repeat tests on different days [9, 10].

Smoking: Men smoked an average of two sticks of cigarettes per day, while women smoked one stick. A former smoker is someone who has not smoked for more than one year, whereas a current smoker is someone who has started smoking within the last year [37, 55].

Adherence to anti-hypertensive medications: Good (missed <20% of the doses last month) and poor (missed ≥20% of the doses last month). Medication adherence, also known as compliance, is “the act of conforming to the recommendations made by the provider with respect to timing, dosage, and frequency of medication taking.” It is described as “the extent to which a patient acts in accordance with the prescribed interval and dose of a dosing regimen” and recorded in the chart [5, 42].

High salt in diet: The act of reducing salt intake to less than 5 gm of salt per day [43, 56].

Blood test profiles: Total cholesterol level: Is normal if it is <200 mg/dl and abnormal if it is ≥200 mg/dl. Whereas low-density lipoprotein cholesterol level (LDL-C) is normal if it is <100mg/dl and abnormal if it is ≥100 mg/dl, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol level (HDL-C) is abnormal if it is <45 mg/dl and normal if it is ≥45 mg/dl, and/or serum triglyceride level ≥150 mg/dl [5, 57].

Recurrent stroke: It was defined as a new neurological deficit presenting after a period of clinical stability, lasting ≥24 hours, and with attributable new ischemic or hemorrhagic lesions [58].

Social support: Support obtained from family and non-family members. Social support from family and friends included contacts with family members and friends, children's frequent visits, siblings' frequent visits, sick care, money received (whether participants received money from children), and money given (whether participants gave money to their children) [59, 60].

Body mass index (BMI): Can be classified as normal between 18.5 kg/m2 and 24.9 kg/m2, underweight <18.5 kg/m2, and overweight ≥25 kgm2 [61].

Data collection tool, procedure, and quality assurance

A paper-based data extraction checklist was used to collect the data. The data collection checklist was prepared using hypertension patient charts, intake forms, follow-up forms, treatment registrations, and similar literature [5, 9, 52, 57, 62, 63]. The data abstraction checklist includes socio-demographic factors, patient behaviors, comorbidities and physical measurements, and treatment-related factors. Five BSc health professionals and two masters of public health supervisors were recruited. The lists of enrolled patients were obtained from follow-up logbook charts using medical record numbers for each hospital, and then randomly selected charts were reviewed. The quality of the data was assured starting with careful designing of a data extraction checklist, a one-day training for data collectors and supervisors, and close follow-up and supervision were carried out during the entire data collection period.

Data processing and analysis

Data were entered using EpiData version 4.6 and then exported to Stata version 17 for cleaning, coding, and analysis. The descriptive statistics were presented using mean, standard deviation (SD), frequency tables, and graphs. A Cox proportional hazards regression model was used to determine the association between dependent and predictor variables. In bi-variable Cox proportional regression analysis, variables with a p-value ≤ 0.25 were included in the multivariable analysis. The Cox proportional hazard assumption was checked using a log-log plot graphical test, and a global test (p = 0.3006). The final model was checked, using the Cox-Snell residual goodness of fit test (Supplemental Fig. 1). The interaction between independent variables was checked by Multicollinearity. Finally, in multivariable Cox proportional regression analysis, variables with a p-value < 0.05, and 95% confidence interval (CI) corresponding adjusted hazard ratio (AHR) were used to declare statistical significance for the incidence of stroke among hypertension patients.

Incidence of stroke among hypertensive patients

In this study, the stroke incidence rate was 5.53 per 100 person-years (95% CI: 4.60, 6.65) with a total follow-up of 2,044 person-years. The stroke incidence rate was 2.55 per 100 person-years (95% CI: 1.48, 4.39), 7.89 per 100 person-years (95% CI: 5.33, 11.67), and 45.24 per 100 person-years (95% CI: 28.85, 70.92) at the end of the first, fourth, and seventh years of follow-up, respectively (Fig. 2). During the 7-year follow-up period, about 113 (22.2%) of hypertension patients developed a stroke (Fig. 3). Moreover, the proportion of stroke among uncontrolled diastolic and systolic blood pressure was 39/79 (49.4%) and 52/127 (41%), respectively (Fig. 4).

Predictors of stroke among hypertensive patients

In the multivariable Cox proportional hazard regression model, residence, family history of stroke, atrial fibrillation, overweight, total cholesterol, and low-density lipoprotien cholesterol levels were identified as significant predictors of stroke. Hypertension patients from rural areas were at 1.88 times the hazard of stroke compared with those from urban areas [AHR: 1.88 (95% CI: 1.13, 3.12)]. Hypertension patients with a family history of stroke were 2.52 times more likely to develop a stroke compared to those patients without a family history [AHR: 2.52 (95% CI: 1.33, 4.77)]. Patients with a history of atrial fibrillation were 3.10 times more likely to develop stroke compared to patients with hypertension who have no history of atrial fibrillation [AHR: 3.10 (95% CI: 1.62, 5.90)]. Similarly, hypertensive patients with baseline total cholesterol levels ≥ 200 mg/dl were 4.42 times more likely to develop stroke compared to those with baseline total cholesterol levels < 200 mg/dl [AHR: 4.42 (95% CI: 2.71, 7.23)]. Moreover, participants who were overweight were 1.82 times more likely to develop stroke compared to those with normal body weight [AHR: 1.82 (95% CI: 1.16, 2.87)]. Finally, participants with baseline LDL-C levels ≥ 100 mg/dl were 1.77 times more likely to develop stroke compared to those with baseline LDL-C levels ≤ 100 mg/dl [AHR: 1.77 (95% CI: 1.11, 2.83)] (Table 4 and Figs. 5, 6, 7 and 8).

Discussion

This study investigated the incidence of stroke and predictors among hypertensive patients who had treatment follow-ups in Awi Zone public hospitals. In this study, the incidence rate of stroke was 5.53 per 100 person-years (95% CI: 4.60, 6.65) with a total follow-up of 2,044 person-years. This finding is higher than that of 1 per 100 person-years in Bahir Dar [52], 31.4 per 1000 person-years in Gondar [64], 14.19 per 1000 person-years in Ghana [25], and 1.06 per 1000 person-years in America [65]. The variation may be due to differences in study period, setting, and sample size. This finding was conducted in multicenter settings with lower-level healthcare facilities, 72% illiteracy, and the majority of participants being rural residents, which may increase the incidence of stroke compared to previous findings in Ethiopia. The previous studies conducted in Bahir Dar and Gondar were done at single health facilities with a relatively high standardized level of care and educated participants. Whereas previous studies in Ghana and America used large sample sizes and technologically advanced settings, to improve literacy, knowledge, and awareness regarding stroke prevention and control, although measurement disparities may have contributed to stroke incidence.

Hypertensive patients from rural areas were at 1.88 times the hazard of stroke compared with those from urban areas. This finding is consistent with the study conducted in Metu, South Wollo, and Oromia Special Zones of Ethiopia and Canada [65–67]. The possible reason for this could be due to illiteracy, poor healthcare access, including transportation problems, lower socioeconomic status resulting in food insecurity and lack of self-care, and lack of early screening and diagnosis among rural residents [65, 68]. Hypertensive patients with a family history of stroke were at 2.52 times the hazard of stroke compared with those without a family history. This finding is supported by the studies conducted in South Wollo and Oromia Special Zones of Ethiopia, China, and Korea [21, 36, 38, 39, 67]. This is due to a genetic or hereditary predisposition inherited from the ancestors [30, 55].

Similarly, patients with atrial fibrillation had more than threefold the hazard of stroke compared to their counterparts. This finding was consistent with the studies conducted in Ethiopia, Thailand, and Sweden [30, 69–71]. Atrial fibrillation is an irregular heartbeat that causes the heartbeat faster than normal, which makes the heart overloaded and improper functioning. The lower cavity cannot fill or pump enough blood into the lungs and body. This could include blood clots in the heart that are transferred into the brain, causing a stroke [69, 70].

In addition, patients with a baseline total cholesterol level of ≥ 200 mg/dl were 4.42 times more likely to develop stroke compared to those with a TC level of < 200 mg/dl. This result is supported by previous studies conducted in Tigray, Thailand, Pakistan, and China [40, 45, 70, 72, 73]. Increased dyslipidemia in the form of total cholesterol levels, LDL-C, and decreased HDL-C are contributing factors for atherosclerosis and cardiovascular disease, including stroke [70, 74]. This is also because too much amount of TC influences inflammatory atherogenic lipid molecules, increasing the risk of stroke [75].

Moreover, patients who were overweight were 1.82 times more likely to develop stroke compared to their counterparts. This study is similar to studies conducted in China, Pakistan, and Japan [21, 39, 45, 61]. Obesity and overweight are important public health concerns that can lead to a variety of serious medical complications. Obesity can raise the risk of stroke due to the inflammation generated by excess fatty tissue. This will complicate blood flow and increase the risk of blockage [45, 73]. Finally, hypertensive patients with baseline low-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels ≥ 100 mg/dl were 1.77 times more likely to develop stroke compared to those with low-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels < 100 mg/dl. This finding is supported by previous studies in Ethiopia, Pakistan, and China [36, 40, 45, 73, 76]. Higher levels of LDL-C were associated with an increased risk of stroke due to increasing atrial fragility [70, 74]. LDL-C stimulates platelet activation and tissue factor appearance, leading to reduced clotting function; at excess levels, it might cause blood vessel rupture, resulting in a stroke [18, 75].

In general, this study showed that modifiable factors like elevated cholesterol, obesity, or atrial fibrillation are the most important predictors of stroke among hypertension patients. However, clinically relevant variables such as diabetes, BP control, and adherence were not statistically significant predictors. This may be due to the complex nature of stroke diagnosis, due to the secondary data source there is a lack of high-quality documented evidence, model prediction limitations, and/or other unknown confounding factors.

Strengths and limitations of the study

The study was conducted in a multicenter setting, which increased the trustworthiness and generalizability of the evidence. In this study, only baseline clinical information was included in the analysis, which may mask or alter the association. Important variables like income, physical activity, and previous history of hypertension follow-up other than this study hospital are not included in the study. Furthermore, due to the retrospective nature of this study, health service-related factors and private health facilities that are capable of diagnosing and managing stroke patients in the zone are not included, which may be considered a limitation.

Conclusions

In this study, the incidence rate of stroke among hypertensive patients was relatively high compared to previous evidence. Rural residence, a family history of stroke, atrial fibrillation, overweight, and high TC and LDL-C levels were identified as predictors of stroke among hypertension patients. Encouraging weight loss and a healthy diet are critical for hypertension patients. During treatment follow-ups, healthcare providers should pay special attention to hypertension patients who have presented with identified modifiable risk factors such as elevated cholesterol, obesity, or atrial fibrillation. Using baseline clinical information may confound the results of the study. Further prospective studies are required to control these current public health problems, addressing important variables not included in this study.

Ethical consideration

Ethical clearance was obtained from Debre Markos University, College of Health Sciences Ethical Review Committee (HSC/RCS/161/11/12). The ethical committee, due to the study conducted using patient chart review, waived the need for patient consent. The permission was received from the hospital-responsible bodies for each hospital. There is no personal identifiers were included, anonymity was handled using coding, and the data were solely used for this study’s purpose to maintain confidentiality.

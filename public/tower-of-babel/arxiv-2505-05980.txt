Siegel-Radon transforms of transverse dynamical systems
Michael Björklund, Tobias Hartnick

Abstract
. We extend Helgason’s classical definition of a generalized Radon transform, defined for a pair of homogeneous spaces of an lcsc group G G , to a broader setting in which one of the spaces is replaced by a possibly non-homogeneous dynamical system over G G together with a suitable cross section. This general framework encompasses many examples studied in the literature, including Siegel (or � \Theta -) transforms and Marklof–Strombergsson transforms in the geometry of numbers, Siegel–Veech transforms for translation surfaces, and Zak transforms in time-frequency analysis. Our main applications concern dynamical systems ( X , μ ) (X,\mu) in which the cross section is induced from a separated cross section. We establish criteria for the boundedness, integrability, and square-integrability of the associated Siegel–Radon transforms, and show how these transforms can be used to embed induced G G -representations into L p ​ ( X , μ ) L^{p}(X,\mu) for appropriate values of p p . These results apply in particular to hulls of approximate lattices and certain “thinnings” thereof, including arbitrary positive density subsets in the amenable case. In the special case of cut-and-project sets, we derive explicit formulas for the dual transforms, and in the special case of the Heisenberg group we provide isometric embedding of Schrödinger representations into the L 2 L^{2} -space of the hulls of positive density subsets of approximate lattices in the Heisenberg group by means of aperiodic Zak transforms.

Remark 1.17
.

The explicit form of the hull system of a regular cut-and-project set has several applications. Firstly, in many case we can compute the
H
-transverse measure and the Siegel constant explicitly in terms of the underlying lattice and window, see Theorem

Example 1.18

(Thinned cut-and-project sets)

Example 1.19

(Positive density sets)

We show in Theorem

By construction the projection

3.
Twisted Siegel-Radon transforms

In this section we introduce twisted Siegel-Radon transforms in the generality of integrable transverse
(G,H)
-systems and establish Theorem
B
and Theorem
C
.

3.1.
Eigencharacters

§ 3.1
.

We denote by
\widehat{H}
the set of all (unitary) characters
\xi:H\to\mathbb{T}
. Let
\xi\in\widehat{H}
and let
Y
be a standard Borel
H
-space and
\sigma
be a finite
H
-invariant measure on
\sigma
. In our applications,
Y
will be an integrable
(G,H)
-cross section and
\sigma
will be an
H
-transverse measure.

We say that
\xi
is an
eigencharacter
of
(Y,\sigma)
if there exists an
H
-invariant
\sigma
-conull set
Y_{0}\subset Y
and a nonzero bounded Borel function
\psi_{\xi}:Y_{0}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

(3.1)

\psi_{\xi}(h^{-1}.y)=\xi(h)\psi_{\xi}(y)\quad\text{for all }h\in H,\;y\in Y_{0}.

The function
\psi_{\xi}
is then called an
eigenfunction
with domain
Y_{0}
for
\xi
. If
Y_{0}=Y
, then we say that
\psi_{\xi}
is a
strict eigenfunction
. Note that every eigenfunction can be extended by
0
to a strict eigenfunction.

3.2.
Induced representations

§ 3.2
.

We will consider two different models of the induced representation of a character
\xi\in\widehat{H}
. Concerning the first model, we observe that if a Borel function
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies

(3.2)

f(hg)=\xi(h)f(g)\quad\text{for all }h\in H,g\in G

for some
\xi\in\widehat{H}
, then
|f|
descends to a function on
H\backslash G
. We may thus define

{}^{0}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\xi)=\{f\in C(G)\mid f(hg)=\xi(h)f(g)\;\text{for all }h\in H,g\in G\;\text{and}\;|f|\in C_{c}(H\backslash G)\},

and obtain an action of
G
on this space by
\pi_{\xi}(g)f(x)=f(xg)
. Given
f_{1},f_{2}\in{}^{0}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\xi)
the function
f_{1}(g)\overline{f_{2}(g)}
descends to a function on
H\backslash G
, and we define

\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\xi)}:=\intop\nolimits_{H\backslash G}f_{1}(g)\overline{f_{2}(g)}\;\mathrm{d}m_{H\backslash G}(Hg).

The corresponding completion
\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\xi)
is then a Hilbert space and
\pi_{\xi}
extends to a unitary
G
-representation on
\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\xi)
, which is our first model for the induced representation. For the trivial character we have
{}^{0}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\mathbf{1})\cong C_{c}(H\backslash G)
and
\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\mathbf{1})\cong L^{2}(H\backslash G)
.

§ 3.3
.

Our second model of the induced representation depends on the choice of a locally bounded Borel section
s:H\backslash G\to G
of
\pi
with
s(H)=e
. We fix such a choice once and for all and employ the following notations. Given
g\in G
we write

(3.3)

g=h(g)\ell(g),\quad\text{where}\quad\ell(g):=s(\pi(g))\quad\textrm{and}\quad h(g):=g\ell(g)^{-1}\in H.

This provides a bijection
G\to H\times\ell(G)
,
g\mapsto(h(g),\ell(g))
with inverse given by multiplication. We now define a cocycle
\alpha=\alpha_{s}
by

\alpha:G\times H\backslash G\to G,\quad\alpha(g,Hx)=s(Hx)gs(Hxg)^{-1}=\ell(x)g\ell(xg)^{-1}.

This satisfies the cocycle and normalization identities

(3.4)

\alpha(g_{1}g_{2},Hx)=\alpha(g_{1},Hx)\alpha(g_{2},Hxg_{1}),\quad\alpha(e,Hx)=e\quad\textrm{and}\quad\alpha(g,H)=h(g),

hence we may define a
G
- action
\pi_{s,\xi}
on
C_{c}(H\backslash G)
by

(3.5)

\pi_{s,\xi}(g)\varphi(Hx):=\xi(\alpha(g,Hx))\varphi(Hxg),

which in turn extends to a unitary representation on
L^{2}(H\backslash G)
. Moreover, the map

(3.6)

i:{}^{0}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\xi)\to C_{c}(H\backslash G),\quad i(\varphi_{o})(Hx):=\varphi_{o}(s(Hx))=\varphi_{o}(\ell(x))

is a bijective intertwiner between
\pi_{\xi}
and
\pi_{s,\xi}
with inverse given by

i^{-1}(\varphi)(g)=\xi(h(g))\varphi(Hg)=\xi(\alpha(g,H))\varphi(Hg)=\pi_{s,\xi}(g)\varphi(H).

which extends to a unitary equivalence
i:(\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\xi),\pi_{\xi})\to(L^{2}(H\backslash G),\pi_{s,\xi})
. Note that

(3.7)

g\in\ell(G)=s(H\backslash G)\implies h(g)=e\implies i^{-1}(\varphi)(g)=\varphi(Hg).

3.3.
Twisted Siegel-Radon transforms

In this subsection we establish Part (i) of Theorem
C
and discuss the integrability properties of Siegel-Radon transforms mentioned in §
1.6
.

§ 3.4
.

From now on,
(X,Y,\mu)
denotes a
p
-integrable
(G,H)
-transverse system for some
p\geq 1
with
H
-transverse measure
\sigma
. Integrability implies that
Y_{x}
is locally finite for
\mu
-almost every

, hence for all

and
\mu
-almost every

, the sum

Sf(x):=\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}f(Hg)

is finite. Moreover,

\intop\nolimits_{X}\left|Sf(x)\right|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\intop\nolimits|Y_{x}\cap\mathrm{supp}(f)|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty,

hence we obtain a well-defined

(3.8)

S:C_{c}(H\backslash G)\to L^{p}(X,\mu),\quad Sf(x):=\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}f(Hg).

§ 3.5
.

If
\xi
is an eigencharacter of
(Y,\sigma)
, then we can define a twisted version of (
3.8
). For this we fix an eigenfunction
\psi_{\xi}
with domain
Y_{0}
. Denote by

the set of all

such that
Y_{x}
is locally finite and coincides with

and

g.x\in Y_{0}
,

is defined. Moreover, if

f(hg)\psi_{\xi}(hg.x)=\xi(h)f(g)\xi(h^{-1})\psi_{x}(g.x)=f(g)\psi_{\xi}(g.x),

hence the function

is well-defined. We may thus define

S_{\psi_{\xi}}f:X_{0}\to\mathbb{C},\quad x\mapsto\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}f(g)\psi_{\xi}(g.x).

Lemma 3.6
.

The subset
X_{0}\subset X
is a
\mu
-conull set and
S_{\psi_{\xi}}f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
.

Proof.

Since

be locally finite is a
\mu
-conull condition. To see that

is conull it remains to show that for
\mu
-almost every

we have

. For this let

; this is an
H
-invariant
\sigma
-nullset and we have to show that
N_{x}=Y_{x}\setminus(Y_{0})_{x}=\emptyset
for
\mu
-almost all

. Since

is
\sigma
-compact, it suffices to show that for every compact subset

we have

for
\mu
-almost every

. We thus fix a compact subset
K\subset H\backslash G
; by
[
38
]
there then exists a non-negative function
\rho\in C_{c}(G)
such that

\widetilde{\rho}(Hg)=\intop\nolimits_{H}\rho(hg)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h)=1\quad\text{for all }Hg\in K.

By (
2.2
) we then have (using that

is an
H
-invariant null set)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sigma(N)\cdot m_{G}(\rho)=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{Y}\rho\otimes\chi_{N}(g,y)\;\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\;\mathrm{d}m_{G}(g)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{X}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Yx}\intop\nolimits_{H}\rho(hg)\chi_{N}(hg.x)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h)\right)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\intop\nolimits_{X}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}\widetilde{\rho}(g)\chi_{N}(g.x)\right)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

Restricting the sum to

then yields

0=\intop\nolimits_{X}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\cap K\in Y_{x}}\widetilde{\rho}(g)\chi_{N}(g.x)\right)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\intop\nolimits_{X}|N_{x}\cap K|\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

This proves that

is conull, and we deduce that

\intop\nolimits_{X}\left|S_{\psi_{\xi}}f(x)\right|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\intop\nolimits_{X_{0}}\left|S_{\psi_{\xi}}f(x)\right|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\|\psi_{\xi}\|_{\infty}\intop\nolimits_{X_{0}}|Y_{x}\cap\mathrm{supp}(f)|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty.\qed

Definition 3.7
.

The map
S_{\psi_{\xi}}:{}^{0}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\xi)\to L^{p}(X,\mu)
is called the
\psi_{\xi}
-twisted Siegel-Radon transform
of the

-integrable
(G,H)
-transverse system

.

Since

is

and the integrability claims made in §

Remark 3.8

It follows from Lemma

3.4.
Twisted orbital integrals and twisted Siegel duality

We now conclude the proof of Theorem
C
by establishing Part (ii) and (iii) of the theorem.

§ 3.9
.

Let
(X,Y,\mu)
be an integrable
(G,H)
-transverse system with
H
-transverse measure
\sigma
. We denote by
\widetilde{X}
the associated intersection space and by
\widetilde{\mu}
the associated intersection measure (see Example
2.11
).
We fix an
H
-eigencharacter
\xi
of
Y
with eigenfunction
\psi_{\xi}
.

Definition 3.10
.

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(X)
the
\psi_{\xi}
-twisted orbital integral
of
\varphi
along
Y
is defined as

S_{\psi_{\xi}}^{*}\varphi(g):=\intop\nolimits_{Y}\varphi(g^{-1}.y)\overline{\psi_{\xi}(y)}\;\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(G).

§ 3.11
.

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(X)
, then for all
g,g^{\prime}\in G
and
h\in H
we have

S^{*}_{\psi_{\xi}}(\varphi)(gg^{\prime})=S^{*}_{\psi_{\xi}}(g^{\prime}.\varphi)(g)\quad\textrm{and}\quad S^{*}_{\psi_{\xi}}\varphi(hg)=\xi(h)S^{*}_{\psi_{\xi}}\varphi(g).

In particular,
|S^{*}_{\psi_{\xi}}\varphi|
descends to a bounded function on
H\backslash G
, and for every
f\in{}^{0}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\xi)
the product
f\cdot\overline{S^{*}_{\psi_{\xi}}\varphi}
descends to an integrable function on
H\backslash G
, hence we obtain a well-defined pairing

\langle f,S_{\psi_{\xi}}^{*}\varphi\rangle_{H\backslash G}:=\intop\nolimits_{H\backslash G}f(g)\overline{S^{*}\psi_{\xi}(g)}\;\mathrm{d}m_{G}(Hg).

We also define

\langle S_{\psi_{\xi}}f,\varphi\rangle_{X}:=\intop\nolimits_{X}S_{\psi_{\xi}}f\cdot\overline{\varphi}\;\mathrm{d}\mu.

Theorem 3.12

(Twisted Siegel duality)
.

For all
f\in{}^{0}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\xi)
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(X)
we have

\langle S_{\psi_{\xi}}f,\varphi\rangle_{X}=\langle f,S_{\psi_{\xi}}^{*}\varphi\rangle_{H\backslash G}.

Proof.

By Remark
3.8
we assume that
\psi_{\xi}
is strict. We then abbreviate

F_{f,\varphi}(g,y)=f(g)\overline{\varphi(g^{-1}.y)}\psi_{\xi}(y),

so that by definition

\langle f,S_{\psi_{\xi}}^{*}\varphi\rangle_{H\backslash G}=\intop\nolimits_{H\backslash G}\intop\nolimits_{Y}F_{f,\varphi}(g,y)\;\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H\backslash G}(Hg).

Since
F_{f,\varphi}(hg,h.y)=F_{f,\varphi}(g,y)
for all
h\in H
and
(g,y)\in G\times Y
, Corollary
2.5
yields

\langle f,S_{\psi_{\xi}}^{*}\varphi\rangle_{H\backslash G}=\intop\nolimits_{X}\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}F_{f,\varphi}(g,g.x)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\intop\nolimits_{X}\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}f(g)\overline{\varphi(x)}\psi_{\xi}(g.x)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\langle S_{\psi_{\xi}}f,\varphi\rangle_{X}.\qed

For the following reformulation, recall that
s:H\backslash G\to G
denotes a locally bounded Borel section of
\pi:G\to H\backslash G
.

Corollary 3.13

(Intersection formula)
.

For every
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(X)
and
f\in{}^{0}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\xi)
we have

(3.9)

\langle S_{\psi_{\xi}}f,\varphi\rangle_{X}=\langle f,S_{\psi_{\xi}}^{*}\varphi\rangle_{H\backslash G}=\intop\nolimits_{\widetilde{X}}f(s(Hg))\psi_{\xi}(s(Hg).x)\overline{\varphi(x)}\;\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}(Hg,x).

Moreover, if
\xi
is a non-trivial character, then

(3.10)

\intop\nolimits_{X}S_{\psi_{\xi}}f(x)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\langle S_{\psi_{\xi}}f,1\rangle_{X}=0.

Proof.

The first equality in (
3.9
) is just Siegel duality. For the second equality we note that by definition of
\widetilde{\mu}
the integral on the right hand side is given by

\intop\nolimits_{H\backslash G}\left(\intop\nolimits_{Y}f(s(Hg))\psi_{\xi}(s(Hg).g^{-1}y)\overline{\varphi(g^{-1}y)}\;\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\right)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H\backslash G}(Hg).

Recall that
\ell(g)=s(Hg)
. By (
3.3
) we have

f(\ell(g))\psi_{\xi}(\ell(g).g^{-1}y)=\xi(h(g))^{-1}f(g)\psi_{\xi}(\ell(g).g^{-1}y)=f(g)\psi_{\xi}(h(g)\ell(g)g^{-1}.y)=f(g)\psi_{\xi}(y),

ans hence the inner integral equals

\intop\nolimits_{Y}f(g)\psi_{\xi}(y)\overline{\varphi(g^{-1}y)}\;\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=f(g)\overline{S^{*}\varphi(g)},

which proves (
3.9
). If
\varphi\equiv 1
, then for all
h\in H
the inner integral satisfies

I_{\xi}(f):=\intop\nolimits_{Y}f(g)\psi_{\xi}(y)\;\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=\intop\nolimits_{Y}f(g)\psi_{\xi}(h^{-1}.y)\;\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=\xi(h)I_{\xi}(f),

which for nontrivial
\xi
forces
I_{\xi}(f)=0
and proves (
3.10
).
∎

At this point we have established Theorem
C
and all the claims made in §
1.6
.

3.5.
Untwisted Radon-Siegel transform and Siegel formula

§ 3.14

. It follows from (
4.3
) that assigns which every
(G,Z)
-finite
G
-invariant measure on
X
its transverse measure is injective.

Secondly, if
\mathcal{O}_{G}
denotes the
G
-orbit equivalence relation on
X
, then
\nu
is
\mathcal{O}_{G}|_{Z}
-invariant. Conversely, for every
\mathcal{O}_{G}|_{Z}
-invariant finite Borel measure
\nu
on
Z
there exists a unique Borel measure
\mu
on
X
which is
G
-invariant,
(G,Z)
-finite (but not necessarily finite) and satisfies
(
4.3

of
\nu
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
Z\subset\widetilde{Z}
be separated cross sections for
X
and
\mu\in\mathrm{Prob}(X)^{G}
. If
\nu_{Z}
and
\nu_{\widetilde{Z}}
denote the corresponding transverse measures on
Z
and
\widetilde{Z}
respectively, then

\mu_{Z}=\mu_{\widetilde{Z}}|_{Z}.

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{Z}
is separated there exists an identity neighbourhood
U\subset G
such that

\mu_{Z}(B)=\frac{\mu(U.B)}{m_{G}(U)}=\mu_{\widetilde{Z}}(B)\quad\text{for any Borel subset $B\subset Z$.}\qed

4.3.
Couplings and thinnings of separated transverse systems

§ 4.7
.

Let
(X_{1},Z_{1},\mu_{1})
and
(X_{2},Z_{2},\mu_{2})
be two
G
-transverse systems. A probability measure
\mu\in\mathrm{Prob}(X_{1}\times X_{2})
is called a
coupling
if it pushes forward to
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
respectively under the canonical projections. A coupling is called
monotone
if it gives full measure to the Borel subset

\mathcal{M}=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in X_{1}\times X_{2}\,:\,(Z_{1})_{x_{1}}\subseteq(Z_{2})_{x_{2}}\}\subset X_{1}\times X_{2}

is a Borel subset, and a
monotone joining
if it is moreover
G
-invariant. We say that
(X_{1},Z_{1},\mu_{1})
is a
thinning
of
(X_{2},Z_{2},\mu_{2})
if such a monotone joining exists.

It follows from (
4.1
) that every monotone coupling of two separated
G
-transverse systems
(X_{1},Z_{1},\mu_{1})
and
(X_{2},Z_{2},\mu_{2})
is also a monotone coupling of the induced systems
(X_{1},Y_{1},\mu_{1})
and
(X_{2},Y_{2},\mu_{2})
. In particular, if
(X_{1},Z_{1},\mu_{1})
is a thinning of
(X_{2},Z_{2},\mu_{2})
, then
(X_{1},Y_{1},\mu_{1})
is a thinning of
(X_{2},Y_{2},\mu_{2})
. The following lemma provides a useful way to construct thinnings.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose there exists a Borel map
p:X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
such that

p_{*}\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad Z_{1}\subseteq p^{-1}(Z_{2}).

Then
(X_{1},Z_{1},\mu_{1})
is a thinning of
(X_{2},Z_{2},\mu_{2})
, and if the latter is separated, then so is the former.

Proof.

Define
\eta\in\Prob(X_{1}\times X_{2})^{G}
by

\eta(f)=\intop\nolimits_{X_{1}}f(x_{1},p(x_{1}))\,d\mu_{1}(x_{2}),\quad f\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(X_{1}\times X_{2}).

Since
p_{*}\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
, we deduce that
\eta
is a joining of
(X_{1},\mu_{1})
and
(X_{2},\mu_{2})
.
Note that

(Z_{1})_{x_{1}}\subseteq(p^{-1}(Z_{2}))_{x_{1}}=(Z_{2})_{p(x_{1})}\quad\textrm{for all $x_{1}\in X_{1}$},

and thus
(x_{1},p(x_{1}))\in\mathcal{M}
for all
x_{1}\in X_{1}
. In particular, the joining
\eta
is monotone, and
\Lambda(Z_{1})\subset\Lambda(Z_{2})
, hence
Z_{1}
is separated, hence
Z_{1}
is separated if
Z_{2}
is.

∎

Lemma
4.8
will be particularly useful for systems arisings from cut-and-project schemes, see Subsection
7.6
below, but it also provides a method to construct thinnings for general systems which we now describe.

Construction 4.9
.

We can construct a thinning of any
G
-ergodic separated
G
-transverse system
(X_{2},Z_{2},\mu_{2})
using an arbitrary auxiliary weakly mixing separated
G
-transverse system
(X_{3},Z_{3},\mu_{3})
. For this we choose a symmetric identity neighbourhood
V\subset G
and define a new system
(X_{1},Z_{1},\mu_{1})
as follows: We define subsets of
X_{2}\times X_{3}
by

Z_{1}=Z_{2}\times V^{2}.Z_{3}\quad\textrm{and}\quad X_{1}:=G.Z_{1}.

To define the measure
\mu_{1}
we use the following observation.

Lemma 4.10
.

In the situation of Construction
4.9
we have
\mu_{2}\otimes\mu_{3}(X_{1})=1
.

Proof.

Since
(X_{2},\mu_{2})
is ergodic and
(X_{3},\mu_{3})
is weakly mixing, the product system
(X_{2}\times X_{3},\mu_{2}\otimes\mu_{3})
is ergodic, hence it suffices to show that
(\mu_{2}\otimes\mu_{3})(X_{1})>0
. This in turn readily follows
from the inclusion

G.(Z_{2}\times V^{2}.Z_{3})\supseteq V.Z_{2}\times V.Z_{3},

and the fact that
\mu_{i}(V.Z_{i})>0
for
i=2,3
.
∎

In view of the lemma we can now define
\mu_{1}:=(\mu_{2}\otimes\mu_{3})|_{X_{1}}
.

Corollary 4.11
.

(X_{1},Z_{1},\mu_{1})
is a separated
G
-transverse system and a thinning of
(X_{2},Z_{2},\mu_{2})
.

Proof.

By construction,
Z_{1}
is a
G
-cross section of
X_{1}
, and by Lemma
4.10
the measure
\mu_{1}
is a probability measure. We now consider the map
p:X_{2}\to X_{1}
given by
p(x_{2},x_{2}):=x_{2}
. By construction we have
p_{*}\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
and
Z_{1}\subseteq p^{-1}(Z_{2})
so that Lemma
4.8
applies.
∎

We call
(X_{1},Z_{1},\mu)
the
product thinning
of
(X_{2},Z_{2},\mu)
constructed from
(X_{3},Z_{3},\mu)
and
V
.

4.4.
Partitions of unity

Throughout this subsection let
(X,Z)
be an integrable transverse
G
-system.

Definition 4.12
.

A Borel function
\rho:G\times Z\to[0,1]
is called a
partition of unity
for
(X,Z)
if
T\rho(x)=1
holds for all
x\in X
.

As a special case of (
2.8
) we observe:

Corollary 4.13
.

If
(X,Z)
admits a partition of unity
\rho
, then for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(X)
we have

(4.4)

\intop\nolimits_{X}\varphi\;\mathrm{d}\mu=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{Z}\varphi(g.z)\rho(g,z)\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{G}(g).\qed

Proposition 4.14
.

If
Z\subset X
is separated, then
(X,Z)
admits a partition of unity.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}=(C_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be as in §
4.4
and define

\rho_{\mathcal{C}}:G\times Z\to\{0,1\},\quad(g,z)\mapsto\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\infty}\chi_{C_{k}}(g,z).

We claim that this defines a partition of unity, i.e. that

(4.5)

\sumop\displaylimits_{g\in Z_{x}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\infty}\chi_{C_{k}}(g^{-1},g.x)=1\quad\text{for all }x\in X.

For the proof of (
4.5
) we fix some
x\in X
. By assumption there exist a unique
k(x)
such that
x\in a_{X}(C_{k(x)})
. By injectivity there then exists a unique pair
(h(x),z(x))\in C_{k(x)}
such that
x=h(x).z(x)
. If we set
g(x):=h(x)^{-1}
, then
g(x).x=z(x)
and hence
g(x)\in Z_{x}
. Moreover,

\chi_{C_{k(x)}}(g(x)^{-1},g(x).x)=\chi_{C_{k(x)}}(h(x),z(x))=1,

i.e. one of the summmands in (
4.5
) equals
1
. We have to show that all other summands are
0
. For this assume that
\chi_{C_{k}}(g^{-1},g.x)=1
for some
g\in Z_{x}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If we set
h:=g^{-1}
and
z:=g.x
, then
(h,z)\in C_{k}
and
x=h.z\in a_{X}(C_{k})
, which implies
k=k(x)
. Moreover, since
h.z=x=h(x).z(x)
, injectivity of
a_{X}|_{C_{k(x)}}
implies that
h=h(x)
and hence
g=g(x)
. This shows that
\chi_{C_{k}}(g^{-1},g.x)=0
unless
k=k(x)
and
g=g(x)
.
∎

5.
Integrability of induced cross sections

§ 5.1
.

Throughout this section let
(X,Z,\mu)
be a separated
G
-transverse system with transverse measure
\nu
. We will be concerned with conditions which guarantee that the induced
(G,H)
-transverse system
(X,Y,\mu)
is integrable. We first provide an abstract characterization of integrability, which is however hard to apply in praxis. We then establish Theorem
D
, which provides a more tractable sufficient conditions for integrability which is motivated from our applications to aperiodic order.

5.1.
An abstract integrability criterion

§ 5.2
.

By §
4.5
,
Z
is integrable, and if
\mathcal{O}_{G}
and
\mathcal{O}_{H}
denote the orbit equivalence relation of
G
and
H
on
X
, then the transverse measure
\nu
is
\mathcal{O}_{G}|_{Z}
-invariant, hence in particular
\mathcal{O}_{H}|_{Z}
-invariant. It thus induces an
H
-invariant
(H,Z)
-finite Borel measure
\tau
on
Y
, which will in general not be finite.

Theorem 5.3
.

The induced cross section
Y
is integrable if and only if
\tau(Y)<\infty
, and in this case its
H
-transverse measure
\sigma
is given by
\sigma=\tau
.

The remainder of this subsection is devoted to the proof of Theorem
5.3
.

§ 5.4
.

Denote by
\widetilde{X}\subset H\backslash G\times X
the intersection space of
H\backslash G
and
(X,Y)
and let
\widetilde{Z}\subset\widetilde{Y}\subset\widetilde{X}
as in §
4.1
. By Remark
4.3
,
\widetilde{Z}\subset\widetilde{X}
is separated, which has two consequences: By §
4.5
it is integrable and by Proposition
4.14
it admits a partition of unity
\rho:G\times\widetilde{Z}\to[0,1]
. We now fix such a partition once and for all.

Since the measure
\tau
is
(H,Z)
-finite and
H
-invariant, the associated intersection measure
\widetilde{\mu}_{\tau}
given by (
2.9
) is
G
-invariant and
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Z})
-finite. It thus admits a transverse measure
\widetilde{\nu}
, which is a finite measure on
\widetilde{Z}
, which we will identify explicitly in Lemma
5.7
below. We need:

Lemma 5.5
.

For all
x\in X
we have

Z_{x}=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}
.

Proof.

By (
4.1
) we have

Z_{x}=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}Z_{x}\cap Hg
. If
Hg\in Y_{x}
, then
(Hg,x)=g^{-1}.(H,g.x)\in G.(\{H\}\times Y)=\widetilde{X}
, so that
\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}
is well-defined.
For all
a\in G
we then have

a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}\iff ag^{-1}\in H\enskip\textrm{and}\enskip a\in Z_{x}\iff a\in Z_{x}\cap Hg.\qed

Corollary 5.6
.

For every bounded measurable function
f:\widetilde{X}\to[0,\infty)
we have

(5.1)

\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}f(Hg,x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in Z_{x}}f(Ha,x)\rho(a^{-1},(H,a.x)).

Proof.

Note that if
a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}
, then
Ha=Hg
, hence by Lemma
5.5
the sum on the right-hand side of (
5.1
) equals

\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}}f(Ha,x)\rho(a^{-1},(H,a.x))=\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}f(Hg,x)\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}}\rho(a^{-1},(H,a.x)).

On the other hand, for every fixed
Hg\in Y_{x}
we have

\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}}\rho(a^{-1},(H,a.x))=\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}}\rho(a^{-1},a.(Hg,x))=1.\qed

Lemma 5.7
.

The transverse measure of the
G
-transverse system
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Z},\widetilde{\mu}_{\tau})
is given by
\widetilde{\nu}=\delta_{H}\otimes\nu
.

Proof.

We have to show that for every
f\in\mathcal{L}_{c}^{\infty}(G\times\widetilde{Z})
,

I_{f}:=\intop\nolimits_{\widetilde{X}}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}}f(a^{-1},a.(Hg,x))\Big)\;\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}_{\tau}(Hg,x)=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{Z}f(g,(H,z))\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{G}(g).

Now let
a,g\in G
and
y\in Y
. If we set
h:=ag^{-1}
, then we have
a=hg
and

a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,g^{-1}.y)}\iff h\in Z_{y}\cap H\implies(a^{-1},a.(Hg,g^{-1}y))=(g^{-1}h^{-1},(H,h.y)).

Using (
2.9
) we thus obtain

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{H\backslash G}\intop\nolimits_{Y}\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,g^{-1}.y)}}f(a^{-1},a.(H,g^{-1}.y))\;\mathrm{d}\tau(y)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H\backslash G}(Hg)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{H\backslash G}\intop\nolimits_{Y}\sumop\displaylimits_{h\in Z_{y}\cap H}f(g^{-1}h^{-1},(H,h.y))\;\mathrm{d}\tau(y)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H\backslash G}(Hg).

If we abbreviate
f_{g}(h,z):=f(g^{-1}h,(H,z))
and apply (
2.8
) (with
\varphi\equiv 1
) to the
H
-cross section
Z
of
Y
, then we obtain

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{H\backslash G}\intop\nolimits_{Y}\sumop\displaylimits_{h\in Z_{y}\cap H}f_{g}(h^{-1},h.y)\;\mathrm{d}\tau(y)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H\backslash G}(Hg)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{H\backslash G}\intop\nolimits_{H}\intop\nolimits_{Z}\ f_{g}(h,z)\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H\backslash G}(Hg)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{H\backslash G}\intop\nolimits_{H}\intop\nolimits_{Z}\ f(g^{-1}h,(H,z))\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H\backslash G}(Hg)

Using unimodularity of
H
and
G
and (
2.5
) we obtain

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{H\backslash G}\intop\nolimits_{H}\intop\nolimits_{Z}\ f(g^{-1}h^{-1},(H,z))\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H\backslash G}(Hg)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{Z}f(g^{-1},(H,z))\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{G}(g)=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{Z}f(g,(H,z))\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{G}(g).\qed

Corollary 5.8
.

For every bounded measurable function
f:\widetilde{X}\to[0,\infty)
we have

(5.2)

\intop\nolimits_{\widetilde{X}}f\;\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}_{\tau}=\intop\nolimits_{X}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}f(Hg,x)\Big)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\;\in[0,\infty].

Proof.

Given
f
as in the corollary we then set

F(g,z):=f(Hg^{-1},g.z)\rho(g,(H,z)),\quad\text{so that}\quad F(a^{-1},a.x)=f(Ha,x)\rho(a^{-1},(H,a.x)).

Using (
5.1
) and (
2.8
) (with
\varphi\equiv 1
) we then have

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle:=\intop\nolimits_{X}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}f(Hg,x)\Big)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\;=\;\intop\nolimits_{X}\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in Z_{x}}f(Ha,x)\rho(a^{-1},(H,a.x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\intop\nolimits_{X}\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in Z_{x}}F(a^{-1},a.x)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{Z}F(g,z)\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{G}(g)=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{Z}f(Hg^{-1},g.z)\rho(g,(H,z))\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{G}(g)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{\widetilde{Z}}f(g.(H,z))\rho(g,(Hx,z))\;\mathrm{d}(\delta_{H}\otimes\nu)(Hx,z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{G}(g).

Since
\delta_{H}\otimes\nu
is the transverse measure of
\widetilde{\mu}_{\tau}
, the corollary follows from (
4.4
).
∎

Corollary 5.9
.

The cross section
Y
is integrable if and only if
\tau(Y)<\infty
. If this is the case, then
for every bounded Borel subset
B\subset H\backslash G
we have

(5.3)

\intop\nolimits_{X}|Y_{x}\cap B|\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\tau(Y)\cdot m_{H\backslash G}(B).

Proof.

Given a bounded set

we apply (
5.2
) to the function
f_{B}(Hg,x):=\chi_{B}(Hg)
. In view of (
2.9
) the left-hand side of (

) then becomes

\intop\nolimits_{\widetilde{X}}f_{B}\;\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}_{\tau}=\intop\nolimits_{H\backslash G}\Big(\intop\nolimits_{Y}\chi_{B}({Hg})\,d\tau(y)\Big)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H\backslash G}(Hg)=\tau(Y)\cdot m_{H\backslash G}(B),

and the right hand side becomes

\intop\nolimits_{X}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}\chi_{B}(Hg)\Big)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\intop\nolimits_{X}|Y_{x}\cap Hg|\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

This establishes (
5.3
), and hence

is integrable if and only if
\tau(Y)<\infty
.
∎

Proposition 5.10
.

If
Y
is integrable, then the
H
-transverse measure
\sigma
is given by
\sigma=\tau
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:H\times Z\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a Borel function such that

for some compact set

. We need to show
that

I:=\intop\nolimits_{Y}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{h\in Z_{y}\cap H}\varphi(h^{-1},h.y)\Big)\;\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\overset{!}{=}\intop\nolimits_{Y}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{h\in Z_{y}\cap H}\varphi(h^{-1},h.y)\Big)\;\mathrm{d}\tau(y)=\intop\nolimits_{H}\intop\nolimits_{Z}\varphi(h,z)\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h).

For this we fix
u\in C_{c}(G)^{+}
with
m_{G}(u)=1
and
\varphi:H\times Z\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
as above, and define

f(g,y)=u(g)\sumop\displaylimits_{h\in Z_{y}\cap H}\varphi(h^{-1},h.y).

Then, by (
2.2
) we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=m_{G}(u)\cdot I=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{Y}f(g,y)\;\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\;\mathrm{d}m_{G}(g)=\intop\nolimits_{X}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}\intop\nolimits_{H}f(g^{-1}h^{-1},hg.x)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h)\Big)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{X}\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}\intop\nolimits_{H}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{h^{\prime}\in Z_{hg.x}\cap H}\check{u}(hg)\varphi((h^{\prime})^{-1},h^{\prime}hg.x)\Big)\,dm_{H}(h)\,d\mu(x)

For

,

and

we then have

\displaystyle h^{\prime}\in Z_{hg.x}\cap H\iff h^{\prime}hg\in Z_{x}\cap Hg=\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)},

and thus using the substitution
a:=h^{\prime}hg
(i.e.
h^{\prime}=a(hg)^{-1}
) we can rewrite the inner sum as

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{h^{\prime}\in Z_{hg.x}\cap H}\check{u}(hg)\varphi((h^{\prime})^{-1},h^{\prime}hg.x)

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}}\check{u}(hg)\varphi(a(hg)^{-1},a.x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}}\check{u}(hg)\varphi(ag^{-1}h^{-1},a.x).

If
a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}
, then
a.x\in Z
and
ga^{-1}\in H
, which implies
\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h)=\mathrm{d}m_{H}(hga^{-1})
. If we define

\psi:G\times\widetilde{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\quad\psi(a,(H,z))=\intop\nolimits_{H}\check{u}(ha^{-1})\varphi(h^{-1},z)\,dm_{H}(h),

and recall from Lemma

that

we thus obtain

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{X}\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}}\intop\nolimits_{H}\check{u}((hga^{-1})a)\varphi((hga^{-1})^{-1},a.x)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h)\Big)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{X}\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}}\intop\nolimits_{H}\check{u}(ha)\varphi(h^{-1},a.x)\,dm_{H}(h)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{X}\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}}\psi(a^{-1},(H,a.x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\intop\nolimits_{X}\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in Y_{x}}\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in\widetilde{Z}_{(Hg,x)}}\psi(a^{-1},a.(Hg,x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{X}\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in Z_{x}}\psi(a^{-1},a.(Ha,x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\intop\nolimits_{X}\sumop\displaylimits_{a\in Z_{x}}\psi(a^{-1},(H,a.x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

Applying (
2.8
) (with
\varphi\equiv 1
) and using
m_{G}(u)=1
and unimodularity of

and

we obtain

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{Z}\psi(g,(H,z))\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{G}(g)=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{Z}\intop\nolimits_{H}u(g^{-1}h^{-1})\varphi(h^{-1},z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h)\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)dm_{G}(g)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{G}\intop\nolimits_{Z}\intop\nolimits_{H}u(g)\varphi(h,z)\;\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h)\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)dm_{G}(g)=\intop\nolimits_{H}\intop\nolimits_{Z}\varphi(h,z)\;\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\,dm_{H}(h).\qed

This completes the proof of Theorem
5.3
.

5.2.
The cocompact case

§ 5.11
.

We recall that a
G
-cross section
Z\subset X
is called
cocompact
if there exists a compact subset
D\subset G
such that
D.Z=X
. We are going to provide an integrability criterion for systems induced from cocompact separated cross sections for which the return time set
\Lambda(Z)
is contained in an approximate lattice.

If
(X,Z,\mu)
is a separated
G
-transverse system, then it follows from (
4.1
) that the induced system
(X,Y,\mu)
is
p
-integrable if and only if the function
f_{K}(x):=|\pi(Z_{x})\cap\pi(K)|
is in
L^{p}(X,\mu)
for every compact subset
K\subset G
. In favourable cases, these functions can be estimated using the following combinatorial lemma.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
A,B\subset G
. Suppose there is a set
C\subset H
such that
(A\cap H)C=H
.
Then,

|\pi(A)\cap\pi(B)|\leq|A^{-1}A\cap BC|.

Proof.

If
Hg\in\pi(A)\cap\pi(B)
, then there are
h_{A},h_{B}\in H
,
a\in A
and
b\in B
such that

g=h_{A}a=h_{B}b.

In particular,
b=h_{B}^{-1}h_{A}a
. Since
(A\cap H)C=H
, and thus
C^{-1}(A\cap H)^{-1}=H
, we can find
a_{H}\in A\cap H
and
c\in C
such that
h_{B}^{-1}h_{A}=c^{-1}a_{H}^{-1}
. Hence,
b=c^{-1}a_{H}^{-1}a
, or equivalently,

a_{H}^{-1}a=cb\in(A\cap H)^{-1}A\cap CB\subset A^{-1}A\cap CB.

Note that
Hg=Ha\in\pi(A^{-1}A\cap CB)
. Since
Hg\in\pi(A)\cap\pi(B)
is arbitrary, we now see that

|\pi(A)\cap\pi(B)|\leq|\pi(A^{-1}A\cap BC)|\leq|A^{-1}A\cap BC|,

which finishes the proof.
∎

§ 5.13
.

We recall that
(H,\Lambda)
is called a

if

is a uniform approximate lattice (i.e. a discrete relatively dense approximate subgroup of

Proposition 5.14
.

Let

-cross section, then

.

Remark 5.15
.

The following proof of Proposition

actually works under slightly weaker assumptions on

is symmetric, that

Proof of Proposition

.

We have to show that the function

At this point we have proved Theorem

5.3.
The non-cocompact case

We now discuss the proof of Theorem
D
in the non-cocompact case. In fact, we prove the result under the following slightly weaker assumptions.

Proposition 5.16
.

Let
Z\subset X
be a separated
G
-cross section. Assume that
\Lambda(Z)
is countable and contained in a subset
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset G
with the following properties:

(1)

\Lambda^{\prime}
is countable and
(\Lambda^{\prime})^{3}
does not accumulate at
e\in G
;

(2)

there exists a Borel subset
D\subset H
with
m_{H}(D)<\infty
such that
H=D(\Lambda^{\prime}\cap H)
.

Then the induced
(G,H)
-cross section

is integrable with respect to every
\mu\in\mathrm{Prob}(X)^{G}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.3
we have to show that
\tau(Y)<\infty
, where
\tau
is the unique
(H,Z)
-finite
H
-invariant measure on
Y
with transverse measure
\nu
. For this we abbreviate
\Lambda:=\Lambda(Z)
and choose
D
as in (2). Furthermore we set
\Lambda^{\prime}_{H}:=\Lambda^{\prime}\cap H
and
Z^{\prime}:=\Lambda^{\prime}_{H}.Z\subset X
. We observe that
H.Z^{\prime}=H.Z=Y
, i.e.
Z^{\prime}\subset Y
is a cross section.

Claim 1:
The cross section
Z^{\prime}\subset X
is separated.

If
z^{\prime}\in Z^{\prime}
and
g\in Z^{\prime}_{z^{\prime}}
, then there exists
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda^{\prime}\cap H
and
z_{1},z_{2}\in Z
such that

z^{\prime}=\lambda_{1}.z_{1}
and
g.z^{\prime}=g\lambda_{1}.z=\lambda_{2}.z_{2}
, and thus

\lambda_{2}^{-1}g\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda
, whence

g\in\lambda_{2}\Lambda\lambda_{1}^{-1}\subset(\Lambda^{\prime})^{3}.

Since
g\in\Lambda(Z^{\prime})
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\Lambda(Z^{\prime})\subset(\Lambda^{\prime})^{3}
. By assumption, we can find an open identity neighbourhood
V
in
G
such that
(\Lambda^{\prime})^{3}\cap V=\{e\}
, and thus
\Lambda(Z^{\prime})\cap V=\{e\}
, proving that
Z^{\prime}
is separated.

We denote by
\nu^{\prime}
the transverse measure of
(X,Z^{\prime},\mu)
. Since
\mu
is finite, so is
\nu^{\prime}
. Since
\nu^{\prime}
is invariant under
\mathcal{O}_{G}|_{Z^{\prime}}
, it is invariant under
\mathcal{O}_{H}|_{Z^{\prime}}
and hence induces a measure
\tau^{\prime}
on
Y
.

Claim 2:
The measure
\tau^{\prime}
is finite.

We consider the function
\rho:Y\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
given by

\rho(y):=\sumop\displaylimits_{h\in Z^{\prime}_{y}}\chi_{D^{-1}}(h).

Since
\nu^{\prime}
is the transverse measure of
\tau^{\prime}
and

is unimodular we have

\intop\nolimits_{Y}\rho\;\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}(y)\;=\;\intop\nolimits_{Y}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{h\in Z^{\prime}_{y}}\chi_{D}(h^{-1})\Big)\,d\tau^{\prime}(y)\;=\;\intop\nolimits_{H}\intop\nolimits_{Z^{\prime}}\chi_{D}(h)\;\mathrm{d}\nu^{\prime}(Z^{\prime})\;\mathrm{d}m_{H}(h)\;=\;m_{H}(D)\nu^{\prime}(Z^{\prime}),

which is finite since
\nu^{\prime}
is finite. It thus suffices to show that
\rho(y)\geq 1
for all
y\in Y
. Since
\rho
takes values in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
it in fact suffices to show that
\rho(y)\neq 0
for all
y\in Y
. Otherwise we can find some
y\in Y
with
Z^{\prime}_{y}\cap D^{-1}=\emptyset
.
Since
Z^{\prime}_{y}=\Lambda_{H}Z_{y}
this would imply that

\emptyset=\Lambda^{\prime}_{H}Z_{y}\cap D^{-1}\iff\emptyset=Z_{y}\cap(D\Lambda^{\prime}_{H})^{-1}=Z_{y}\cap H.

On the other hand, since
Y=H.Z
we have

for some

,

and hence
h^{-1}\in Z_{y}\cap H
, contradiction.

Claim 3:

\tau=\tau^{\prime}
, hence
\tau
is finite.

As explained in §
4.5
, it suffices to show that the corresponding transverse measure
\tau_{Z}
and
\tau^{\prime}_{Z}
on

coincide. By construction we have
\tau_{Z}=\nu
and
\tau^{\prime}_{Z^{\prime}}=\nu^{\prime}
, and by
Lemma

the latter implies that
\tau^{\prime}_{Z}=\nu^{\prime}|_{Z}
. We are thus left with showing that
\nu^{\prime}|_{Z}=\nu
.

For this we fix an open symmetric identity neighbourhood
V_{o}\subset V
such that
V_{o}^{2}\subset V
. Then for any Borel set
B\subset Z\subset Z^{\prime}
the set
V_{o}\times B
is injective, hence by (

) we have

m_{G}(V_{o})\nu^{\prime}(B)=\mu(V_{o}.B)=m_{G}(V_{o})\nu(B).

Since
m_{G}(V_{o})>0
, the claim follows.
∎

This finishes the proof of Theorem
D
.

5.4.
Higher integrability via monotone couplings

§ 5.17
.

It is often of great interest to establish that the image of the Siegel transform consists of functions which are not just integrable, but even square-integrable. Beyond the cocompact case, this is difficult problem, since square-integrability can fail even in the lattice case. For some of our examples below, square-integrability problem can be reduced to the lattice case using the following trivial observation.

Proposition 5.18
.

If
(X_{1},Y_{1},\mu_{1})
admits a monotone coupling to
(X_{2},\mu_{2},Y_{2})
and
(X_{2},\mu_{2},Y_{2})
is
p
-integrable for some
p\in[1,\infty]
, then so is
(X_{1},Y_{1},\mu_{1})
.

Proof.

If
X:=X_{1}\times X_{2}
and
\mu\in\mathrm{Prob}(X)
is a monotone coupling, then for every compact subset
L\subset H\backslash G
we have

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{X_{1}}|L\cap(Y_{1})_{x_{1}}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(x_{1})=\intop\nolimits_{X}|L\cap(Y_{1})_{x_{1}}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}{\mu}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{X}|L\cap(Y_{2})_{x_{2}}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}{\mu}(x_{1},x_{2})=\intop\nolimits_{X_{2}}|L\cap(Y_{2})_{x_{2}}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}(x_{2})<\infty.\qed

Classical examples

6.1.
Lattices

In this subsection we briefly explain how the case of lattices, as outlined in §
1.7
and §

in the introduction, fits into our general framework.

§ 6.1
.

If
\Gamma<G
is a lattice, then
X(\Gamma):=\Gamma\backslash G
admits a unique
G
-invariant probability measure
\mu
and
Z(\Gamma):=\{\Gamma e\}\subset X(\Gamma)
is a
G
-cross section with return time set
\Gamma
, hence separated. The induced
(G,H)
-cross section is
Y(\Gamma):=\Gamma_{H}\backslash Y
, where
\Gamma_{H}=\Gamma\cap H
. If
\Gamma
is
H
-compatible, then

is integrable by Theorem
D
(applied with
\Lambda:=\Gamma
), and if
\sigma_{0}
denotes the unique
H
-invariant probability measure on
Y(\Gamma)
, then the
H
-transverse measure is given by
\sigma=\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)^{-1}\cdot\sigma_{0}
. In particular, the Siegel constant is given by
\sigma(Y)=\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)^{-1}
.

§ 6.2
.

We observe that
Z(\Gamma)_{\Gamma a}=\{g\in G\mid\Gamma ag^{-1}=\Gamma\}=\Gamma a
. It thus follows from (
4.1
) that
Y(\Gamma)_{\Gamma a}=\pi(\Gamma a)
. This in turn implies that the Siegel-Radon transform associated with the system

is given by

Sf(\Gamma a)=\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in\pi(\Gamma a)}f(Hg).

As mentioned in the introduction, this is precisely the Helgason-Radon transform associated with the homogeneous double fibration
H\backslash G\leftarrow{\Gamma_{H}}\backslash G\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash G
, which identifies with the intersection space.

§ 6.3
.

To describe eigencharacters of
Y(\Gamma)
observe that if
\xi\in\widehat{H}
vanishes on
\Gamma_{H}
, then it descends to a function
\psi_{\xi}
on
Y(\Gamma)
. For all
y=\Gamma_{H}a\in Y(\Gamma)
and
h\in H
we then have

\psi_{\xi}(h^{-1}.y)=\xi(ah)=\xi(h)\xi(a)=\xi(h)\psi_{\xi}(y),

whence
\xi
is an eigencharacter of
Y(\Gamma)
with eigenfunction
\psi_{\xi}
.

Remark 6.4
.

For uniform lattices
\Gamma
which intersect

implies that the associated Siegel transform takes values in
L^{\infty}(X(\Gamma),\mu)
, whereas in the non-uniform case we can only guarantee that it takes values in

. While it does indeed take values in

in many classical examples, there are examples of pairs
(H,\Gamma)
(with

a non-uniform lattice) for which the (untwisted) Siegel-Radon transform does not take values in

(cf.
[
15

6.2.
Siegel-Veech transform

§ 6.5
.

If we consider Example
1.8
for
n=2
and identify
\mathbb{R}^{2}\cong\mathbb{C}
, then every
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{2}
corresponds to a pair
(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda,dz)
of a Riemann surface (in fact, a torus) with a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
1
-form, normalized to volume
1
. More generally, given
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})^{\top}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
we can consider pairs
(\Sigma,\omega)
, where
\Sigma
is a Riemann surface (necessarily of genus
g=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}+2)
) and
\omega
is a holomorphic
1
-form on
\Sigma
with
n
zeros of orders
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
respectively, subject to the volume normalization
\frac{i}{2}\intop\nolimits_{\Sigma}\omega\wedge\overline{\omega}=1
. Such pairs are called
(normalized) translation surfaces
of type
\alpha
; see
[
3
]
for references.

Every translation surface
(\Sigma,\omega)
carries a canonical metric, which is flat outside the marked points and has a cone point singularity of angle
2\pi(\alpha_{i}+1)
at the
i
th marked point; a geodesic for this metric which connects two marked points is called a
saddle connection
, and the collection of all such geodesics is denoted
\mathcal{SC}(\Sigma,\omega)
. Every saddle connection
\gamma
defines a
holonomy vector

\mathrm{hol}(\gamma)=\intop\nolimits_{\gamma}\omega\in\mathbb{C}
, and we denote the collection of these holonomy vectors by
\Lambda(\Sigma,\omega)
.

§ 6.6
.

The moduli space of (normalized) translation surfaces of a fixed genus
g
decomposes into strata
X(\alpha)
consisting of (marked) translation surfaces of type
\alpha
, and these strata admit a (non-transitive) action of
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
which preserves an invariant probability measure
\mu_{\alpha}
called the
Masur-Veech measure
. Moreover, the map

\Lambda:X(\alpha)\to\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad(\Sigma,\omega)\mapsto\Lambda(\Sigma,\omega)

is
G
-equivariant, and its image is contained in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\cong H\backslash G
with
H
as in Example
1.8
. Since
G
acts transitively on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, the closed subset

Y(\alpha):=\{(\Sigma,\omega)\in X(\alpha)\mid(1,0)^{\top}\in\Lambda(\Sigma,\omega)\}\subset X(\alpha)

is a
(G,H)
-cross section. Moreover, by equivariance of
\Lambda
, for every
(\Sigma,\omega)\in X(\alpha)
we have

Y(\alpha)_{(\Sigma,\omega)}=\{g^{-1}.e_{1}\in G\mid e_{1}\in\Lambda(g.(\Sigma,\omega))\}=\{g^{-1}.e_{1}\in G\mid g^{-1}.e_{1}\in\Lambda(\Sigma,\omega)\}=\Lambda(\Sigma,\omega).

Masur
[
36
]
has proved that there exist constant
0\leq c_{1}<c_{2}<\infty
(depending on
\alpha
) such that for
\mu_{\alpha}
-almost all
(\Sigma,\omega)\in X(\alpha)
we have

(6.1)

c_{1}R^{2}\leq|\Lambda(\Sigma,\omega)\cap B_{R}(0)|\leq c_{2}R^{2};

this implies that the systemm
(X(\alpha),Y(\alpha),\mu_{\alpha})
is integrable, and we denote by
\sigma_{\alpha}
the
H
-transverse measure on
Y(\alpha)
. The associated Siegel-Radon transform

S:C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})\to L^{1}(X(\alpha),\mu_{\alpha}),\quad(\Sigma,\omega)\mapsto\sumop\displaylimits_{x\in\Lambda(X,\omega)}f(x)

is known as the
Siegel-Veech transform
; in the case of tori without marked points we recover the classical Siegel transform, and it was shown recently
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
that, as in the classical case, the Siegel-Veech transform takes values in
L^{2}(X(\alpha),\mu_{\alpha})
.

The Siegel-Veech transform can be used to count holonomy vectors, i.e. to refine (
6.1
). For instance, this leads to a proof of the Eskin-Masur formula
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{|\Lambda(\Sigma,\omega)\cap B_{R}(0)|}{\pi R^{2}}=\sigma_{\alpha}(Y(\alpha))\text{ for $\mu_{\alpha}$-almost all }(\Sigma,\omega).

where the Siegel constant
c(\alpha):=\nu_{\alpha}(Y(\alpha))
is known as the
Siegel-Veech constant
of the stratum. Studying the asymptotics of these constants for large genus is a major problem in the theory of translation surfaces.

§ 6.7
.

Already in the theory of Siegel-Veech transforms the flexibility in choosing a
(G,H)
-cross section
Y
becomes useful. For instance, assume that
\alpha=(1,2,3)^{\top}
and that we want to estimate the size of the set
\Lambda_{(1\to 3)}(\Sigma,\omega)
of holonomy vectors of saddle connections which connect a marked point of order
1
to a marked point of order
3
. This amounts to studying the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-equivariant map
\Lambda_{(1\to 3)}:X(\alpha)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
.

In general, an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-equivariant map
\Lambda_{*}:X(\alpha)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
with
\Lambda_{*}(\Sigma,\omega)\subset\Lambda(\Sigma,\omega)
is known as a
configuration
. Every configuration gives rise to a corresponding
(G,H)
-cross section

Y_{*}(\alpha)=\{(\Sigma,\omega)\in X(\alpha)\mid e_{1}\in\Lambda_{*}(\Sigma,\omega)\}\subset Y(\alpha),

and one can show (similarly to the proof of Lemma
4.6
) that the corresponding transverse measure is just
\nu_{\alpha}|_{Y_{*}(\alpha)}
. The associated Siegel transform is then given by

S_{*}f(\Sigma,\omega)=\sumop\displaylimits_{x\in\Lambda_{*}(\Sigma,\omega)}f(x),

and

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{|\Lambda_{*}(\Sigma,\omega)\cap B_{R}(0)|}{\pi R^{2}}=\nu_{\alpha}(Y_{*}(\alpha))\text{ for $\mu_{\alpha}$-almost all }(\Sigma,\omega).

These generalized Siegel-Veech transforms have recently attracted a lot of attention in the study of the spectral theory of translation surfaces
[
2
]
.

6.3.

§ 6.8
.

Let
m,n
be positive integers and denote by
\mathbb{R}_{m}
the space of

by

and denote by

H\backslash G\to\mathbb{R}_{m}\setminus\{0\},\quad Hg\mapsto v_{o}g.

The group

also acts on
\mathbb{R}_{m+n}\cong\mathbb{R}_{m}\times\mathbb{R}_{n}
via
g.(x,y)=(xg^{-1},y)
and hence on the space
\mathscr{L}_{m+n}
of all unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}_{m+n}
. We define a

-invariant Borel subset
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}_{m+n}\subset\mathscr{L}_{m+n}
by

\mathscr{L}^{\prime}_{m+n}=\{\Delta\in\mathscr{L}_{m+n}\,:\,\textrm{
$\pr_{\mathbb{R}_{n}}(\Delta)<\mathbb{R}_{n}$ is dense}\}.

We now fix
\Delta_{o}\in\mathscr{L}^{\prime}_{m+n}
and denote by

the orbit closure of
\Delta_{o}
in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{m+n}
. By
[
34
, Subsection 1.4]

L_{\Delta_{o}}<\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathbb{R})
such that
X=L_{\Delta_{o}}.\Delta_{o}
and the stabilizer of
\Delta_{o}
in
L_{\Delta_{o}}
is a lattice, hence
X
carries a unique
L_{\Delta_{o}}

\mu_{\Delta_{o}}
. We now choose a window
W\subset\mathbb{R}_{n}
, i.e. a bounded Borel subset with non-empty interior. We then define a
(G,H)
-cross section

by

Theorem 6.9

(Marklof-Strömbergsson,
[

)
.

For every
\Delta_{o}\in\mathscr{L}^{\prime}_{m+n}
and every window

the

-transverse system
(X(\Delta_{o}),Y(W),\mu_{\Delta_{o}})
is integrable.

One can show that the system is even square-integrable, see
[
40

§ 6.10
.

We now proceed to identify the Siegel-Radon transform associated with the system
(X(\Delta_{o}),Y(W),\mu_{\Delta_{o}})
. Given
\Delta\in X(\Delta_{o})
we define the
associated cut-and-project set

P_{W}(\Delta)\subset\mathbb{R}_{m}
as

P_{W}(\Delta):=\mathrm{pr}_{\mathbb{R}_{m}}(\Delta\cap(\mathbb{R}_{m}\times W))

Since
\Delta
projects densely to
\mathbb{R}_{n}
and

, this is a Delone set (i.e. a relatively dense and uniformly discrete subset) in
\mathbb{R}_{m}
. On the other hand,

Under the identification (

) this corresponds precisely to the set

, hence the Siegel transform of

S:C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{m}\setminus\{0\})\to L^{1}(X(\Delta_{o}),\mu_{\Delta_{o}}),\quad Sf(\Delta):=\sumop\displaylimits_{v\in P_{W}(\Delta)}f(v).

This is precisely the Siegel transform for quasicrystals studied by Marklof and Strömbergsson in
[

, Theorem 1.5]
.

7.1.
Measured sets and induced Hull systems

§ 7.1
.

The space

P_{o}P_{o}^{-1}
.
The following proposition summarizes basic properties of the hull; here we equip

with the inductive limit topology and its dual

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
P_{o}\subset G
be a closed subset.

(i)

X(P_{o})
is an lcsc space and
G
acts on

by homeomorphisms.

(ii)

X(P_{o})
is a homogeneous
G
-space if and only if
P_{o}=\Gamma g
for some subgroup
\Gamma\subset G
and some
g\in G
, in which case
X(P_{o})=\Gamma\backslash G
.

(iii)

X(P_{o})
is compact if and only if

for some compact

. (i.e.

is
relatively dense
).

(iv)

For all
P\in X(P_{o})
we have
PP^{-1}\subset{\overline{P_{o}P_{o}^{-1}}}
.

(v)

If

is uniformly discrete, then every

is uniformly discrete and the embedding

\delta:X(P_{o})\hookrightarrow C_{c}(G)^{*},\quad P\mapsto\delta_{P}:=\sumop\displaylimits_{x\in P}\delta_{x}

is a homeomorphism onto its image.

§ 7.3
.

Assume that
P_{o}\in\mathcal{C}(G)
is non-empty and let
Z(P_{o}):=\{Q\in X(P_{o})\mid e\in Q\}
. By definition of the Chabauty topology,

is compact, hence Borel and
p.P=Pp^{-1}\in Z
for all

. This shows that

is a cross section for

, called the
canonical cross section
. Here, the word canonical refers to the fact that

(7.1)

Z(P_{o})_{P}=P\quad\text{for all }P\in X(P_{o}).

Proposition 7.4
.

The canonical cross section
Z(P_{o})
has the following properties:

(i)

P_{o}P_{o}^{-1}\subset\Lambda(Z(P_{o}))\subset\overline{P_{o}P_{o}^{-1}}
. In particular, if
P_{o}P_{o}^{-1}
is discrete, then
\Lambda(Z(P_{o}))=P_{o}P_{o}^{-1}
.

(ii)

Z(P_{o})
is separated if

is uniformly discrete.

(iii)

Z(P_{o})
is cocompact if
P_{o}
is there exists a compact subset
L\subset G
such that
G=LP_{o}
.

Proof.

(i) We may assume that
e\in P_{o}
, hence
P_{o}P_{o}^{-1}\subset\Lambda(Z(P_{o}))
. If
Q\in Z(P_{o})
, then

Z(P_{o})_{Q}=Q=Qe^{-1}\subset QQ^{-1}\subset\overline{P_{o}P_{o}^{-1}}\implies P_{o}P_{o}^{-1}\subset\Lambda(Z(P_{o}))\subset\overline{P_{o}P_{o}^{-1}}.

(ii) follows from (i).

(iii) If
L\subset G
is compact with

, then
P_{0}\in\{P\in X(P_{o})\mid LP=G\}
, and the latter is closed and
G
-invariant, hence coincides with

. For every

we thus find

with

and hence

. This implies

and hence

, which shows that

.
∎

Definition 7.5
.

We say that a uniformly discrete subset

is

if
\mathrm{Prob}(X({P_{o}}))^{G}\neq\emptyset
.

Corollary 7.6
.

If
P_{o}\subset G
is a measured uniformly discrete subset and
\mu_{P_{o}}\in\mathrm{Prob}(X({P_{o}}))^{G}
, then
(X({P_{o}}),Z({P_{o}}),\mu_{P_{o}})
is a separated
G
-transverse system. ∎

§ 7.7
.

In the situation of Corollary
7.6
we define

Y(P_{o}):=\{P\in X(P_{o})\mid P\cap H\neq\emptyset\}=H.Z(P_{o})

so that
(X(P_{o}),Y(P_{o}),\mu_{P_{o}})
is the induced
(G,H)
-transverse system of
(X({P_{o}}),Z({P_{o}}),\mu_{P_{o}})
. We refer to such a system as an

.

Remark 7.8
.

Every relatively dense and uniformly discrete subset
P_{o}
of an amenable group is measured since in this case
X(P_{o})
is compact and hence admits a
G
-invariant probability measure. We will see in Theorem
7.27
below that it actually suffices to assume positive density of
P_{o}
, but this requires an argument.

If
G
is non-amenable (or
P_{o}
is not relatively dense) then deciding whether a uniformly discrete subset
P_{o}\subset G
is measured is more difficult. For example, unlike lattices, approximate lattices themselves need not be measured. See
[
24
,
10
]
for examples of uniform approximate lattices in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
which are not measured.

§ 7.9
.

Let
(X,Z,\mu)
be a separated
G
-transverse system with induced
(G,H)
-system
(X,Y,\mu)
. As explained in
[
11
, Sec. 3.2]
, the map
\mathrm{can}:X\to\mathcal{C}(G)
,
x\mapsto Z_{x}
is Borel and its image consists of uniformly discrete subset of
G
. Now assume that
\mu
ist ergodic and set
\mu^{\prime}:=\mathrm{can}_{*}\mu
; then
\mu^{\prime}(\emptyset)=0
for almost every
x
the set
P_{o}=Z_{x}
has a dense orbit in
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
, hence we can consider as a probability measure on
X(P_{o})
. The map
\mathrm{can}
thus induces (up to nullsets) factor maps

(X,Z,\mu)\to(X(P_{o}),Z(P_{o}),\mu^{\prime})\quad\textrm{and}\quad(X,Y,\mu)\to(X(P_{o}),Y(P_{o}),\mu^{\prime})

which preserves
p
-integrability for all

. To summarize, almost every hitting time set of a separated transverse
G
-system is measured, every
(G,H)
-transverse system which is induced from a separated system factors over an induced hull system.

7.2.
Integrability of induced hull systems

From Theorem
D
we can now deduce the following (cf. Theorem
E
from the introduction).

Theorem 7.10
.

Let
(H,\Lambda)
be a compatible pair and
P_{o}\subset\Lambda
be a measured subset of
G
. Then for every
\mu_{P_{o}}\in\mathrm{Prob}(X({P_{o}}))^{G}
the induced system
(X(P_{o}),Y(P_{o}),\mu_{P_{o}})
is integrable with Siegel-Radon transform

S:C_{c}(H\backslash G)\to L^{1}(X(P_{o}),\mu_{P_{o}}),\quad Sf(P)=\sumop\displaylimits_{Hg\in\pi(P)}f(Hg).

If
(H,\Lambda)
is uniform, then

takes values in
L^{\infty}(X(P_{o}),\mu_{P_{o}})
.

Proof.

Since
P_{o}\subset\Lambda
we have
P_{o}P_{o}^{-1}\subset\Lambda\Lambda^{-1}=\Lambda^{2}
, and the latter is again an approximate lattice, in particular uniformly discrete. We deduce with Proposition
7.4
.(i) that
\Lambda(Z(P_{o}))\subset\Lambda^{2}
. Since
(H,\Lambda)
is compatible pair, so is
(H,\Lambda^{2})
, and hence Theorem
D
yields the desired integrability. It then follows from (
7.1
) and (
4.1
) that
Y(P_{o})_{P}=\pi(P)
for all
P\in X(P_{o})
, resulting in the explicit formula for

.
∎

Note that Theorem
7.10
applies in particular if
P_{0}=\Lambda
is itself a measured approximate lattice which intersects
H
in an approximate lattice.

7.3.
Cut-and-project sets as measured approximate lattices

We now explain how cut-and-project systems give rise to measured approximate lattices; see
[

,
8
]
and in particular
[

Construction 7.11
.

Given a cut-and-project system
\mathcal{S}=(G_{1},G_{2},\Gamma)
in the sense of Example
1.16
we denote by
\mu_{\mathcal{S}}
the unique
(G_{1}\times G_{2})
-invariant probability measure on the space

X(\mathcal{S}):=\Gamma\backslash(G_{1}\times G_{2}).

Every window
W\subset G_{2}
then defines a separated
G_{1}
cross section

, and we denote by

Y(W):=H_{1}.X(W)=\Gamma\backslash(H_{1}\otimes W)

the induced
(G,H)
-cross section. The key observation is now that the return time sets of

are precisely the cut-and-project sets arising from
(\mathcal{S},W)
(cf.
[
21
,
44
]
). We thus deduce from §
7.9
that for almost all choices of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
the cut-and-project set

is measured, and that we can choose
\mu_{P_{o}}\in\mathrm{Prob}(X(P_{o}))^{G}
to obtain almost everywhere defined canonical factor maps

(7.2)

(X(\mathcal{S}),Z(W),\mu_{\mathcal{S}})\to(X(P_{o}),Z(P_{o}),\mu_{P_{o}})\quad\textrm{and}\quad(X(\mathcal{S}),Y(W),\mu_{\mathcal{S}})\to(X(P_{o}),Y(P_{o}),\mu_{P_{o}}).

It will usually be more convenient to work with
(X(\mathcal{S}),Y(W),\mu_{\mathcal{S}})
than with
(X(P_{o}),Y(P_{o}),\mu_{P_{o}})
directly. In general, there might be other invariant probability measure on
X(P_{o})
, but if
P_{o}
happens to be regular in the sense of
[
12
]
, then
\mu_{\mathcal{S}}
is actually the
unique

G
-invariant probability measure on

and the canonical factor map is an isomorphism up to nullsets (see
[
12
, Thm. 1.1]
and
[
41
]
for the original proof in the abelian case).

§ 7.12
.

For later use, let us point out that the measure
\mu_{\mathcal{S}}
satisfies a very strong uniqueness property. To define this, let
p\in\mathrm{Prob}(G_{1})
be a regular probability measure, i.e.
p
is symmetric, absolutely continuous with respect to Haar measure and its support generates
G_{1}
. A probability measure
\mu
on a
G_{1}
-space is then called
p
-stationary
if
p\ast\mu=\mu
. By
[
12
, Lemma 3.7]
the measure
\mu_{\mathcal{S}}
is the unique
p
-stationary probability measure for any regular probability measure
p
on
G_{1}
.

Remark 7.13
.

Following
[
24
]
we say that an approximate lattice
\Lambda
is
laminar
if it is a relatively dense subset in a cut-and-project set. All approximate lattice in abelian
[
37
]
, amenable
[
32
]
and semisimple
[
24
]
groups are laminar, hence finite index subsets of
measured
approximate lattices. However, there exist examples of approximate lattices in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
which are not commensurable to any measured approximate lattices
[
24
]
.

§ 7.14
.

Throughout this subsection,

and

and

(L3)

Then

is a cut-and-project scheme and we choose a bounded Jordan-measurable window

with non-empty interior. We are going to abbreviate

Example 7.15

(Arithmetic cut-and-project sets)
.

To underscore that our present assumptions are fairly mild, let us provide a generic example satisfying these assumptions: For this consider the Galois-involution on
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb Z[\sqrt{2}]
given by
(a+b\sqrt{2})^{*}:=a-b\sqrt{2}

Replacing

§ 7.16
.

Denote by

the closed
(H_{1}\times H_{2})
-orbit of

in

we have

We now fix once and for all a compactly supported symmetric continuous function
\rho_{o}:H_{1}\to[0,1]
with normalization

. By Remark

, the measure
\sigma_{o}
is the unique

-stationary probability measure on

-convergence

\frac{1}{n}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}(\rho_{o}^{*k}\otimes\delta_{e})*\delta_{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\sigma_{o}.

Construction 7.17
.

Since
\sigma_{o}
is
H_{1}\times H_{2}
-invariant, we have a well-defined map

Theorem 7.18
.

Let
\sigma\in M_{\mathrm{fin}}(Y)^{H_{1}}
denote the
H_{1}
-transverse measure of
\mu
.

(i)

If

.

(ii)

§ 7.19
.

Let
\nu

§ 7.20

Proof of Theorem

.

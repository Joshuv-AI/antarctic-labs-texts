“Metric-affine-like” generalization of YM (mal-YM): main idea and results
W. Wachowski

Abstract
For the first time, we build a generalization of the U ⁡ ( n ) U(n) Yang-Mills theory obtained by abandoning the condition of covariant constancy of the Hermitian form in the fibers: ∇ a g α ​ β ′ ≠ 0 \nabla_{a}g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}\neq 0 . So this theory is a simpler analogue of the well-known metric-affine gravity with ∇ a g b ​ c ≠ 0 \nabla_{a}g_{bc}\neq 0 . In our case the connection ∇ a \nabla_{a} and the Hermitian form g α ​ β ′ g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}} are two independent variables, and the total curvature and the total potential are no longer anti-Hermitian matrices: in addition to the usual 𝑭 a ​ b \bm{F}_{ab} and 𝑨 a \bm{A}_{a} they also obtain new Hermitian parts 𝑮 a ​ b \bm{G}_{ab} and 𝑩 a \bm{B}_{a} . It is shown that the Hermitian form g α ​ β ′ g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}} is a Higgs field breaking the general G ​ L ​ ( n , ℂ ) GL(n,\mathbb{C}) gauge symmetry to U ⁡ ( n ) U(n) , and its perturbations are Goldstone bosons which can be eliminated from the theory by redefining other fields. The result is a theory consisting of two non-trivially interacting gauge fields, one of which can be made massive while the other remains massless. Letting the mass of the second gauge field tend to infinity allows one to restore the usual YM.



I
Motivation

The deep kinship between the Yang-Mills theory (YM), which describes strong and electro-weak interactions, and the Einstein theory of gravity (EG) is commonplace today. This kinship is based on the fact that at the center of the formalism of both theories is the same geometric object—the covariant derivative or connection. In case of YM, it has the form

\nabla_{a}\bm{\varphi}=(\partial_{a}-ie\bm{A}_{a})\bm{\varphi},

(1)

where
\partial_{a}
is the coordinate derivative,
\bm{\varphi}\cong\varphi^{\alpha}
is the gauge-charged field,
\alpha

S_{\mathrm{HE}}=M_{P}^{2}\int d^{d}x\,\sqrt{g}\,R

It seems quite natural that in order to bring both theories even closer to each other, it is necessary to consider
the connection and the metric as independent variables

However, our attention is now drawn to a completely different circumstance, which has always remained in shadows before. It is surprising that, as far as we know, no one has previously attempted to construct and study an analogous (and even much simpler)
“metric-affine-like” generalization of the Yang-Mills theory (mal-YM)
. In this letter we show how such generalization can be constructed and point out some interesting results that can be obtained this way.

II
The key observation

To construct the aforementioned generalization of YM, we must first ask the following simple question: if in MAG the partner of the affine connection
\Gamma_{ac}{}^{b}
is the spacetime metric
g_{ab}
, then what structure must be the partner of the potential
\bm{A}_{a}
in mal-YM? Perhaps the whole point is simply that for YM such a partner does not exist, and therefore the generalization we seek is impossible?

No, this is not so. As is well explained, for example, in the book by Penrose and Rindler (
Penrose and Rindler 1984
, pp. 343-344), any specific symmetry in YM is equivalent to the presence of a certain additional structure in its internal color space (i.e. in the fibers of the corresponding vector bundle over spacetime). Unfortunately, the notation that is convenient for standard YM, and therefore has become widespread in it, leaves these structures in shadows. This is probably the reason why a very simple generalization under consideration has not been discovered earlier. To build it, we need to bring the structure in fibers to the foreground, and for this we have to use a rather unusual Penrose notation
Penrose and Rindler 1984
.

Since now we want, first of all, to show our general idea, for the sake of simplicity and clarity we will consider here and everywhere below one specific case—a theory with
U(n)
symmetry. Generalizations to other symmetry groups are straightforward. Also, for simplicity, we will assume that spacetime is flat everywhere below.

So, what additional structure in fibers corresponds to
U(n)
symmetry? In this case, the
n
-dimensional internal color space
V
(a typical fiber of the bundle) will be complex. Note that the operation of complex conjugation cannot map the color space
V
into itself, since otherwise it would contain real and imaginary vectors, but all vectors in the color space (on which the fundamental representation of the corresponding group acts) are equivalent. Therefore, complex conjugation will antilinearly map the color space into
another space

\bar{V}
, complex conjugate to it. Such complex conjugate spaces
V
and
\bar{V}
always occur in pairs. We will (following Penrose) denote the indices associated with them by unprimed and primed Greek indices
4
4

4

The undotted and dotted indices, standardly used to denote Weyl 2-spinors, have the same nature.
. Since unprimed and primed indices belong to different spaces
V
and
\bar{V}
, they cannot be contracted with each other. However, complex conjugation turns primed indices into unprimed ones and vice versa:

\overline{H_{\alpha\ldots\beta^{\prime}\ldots}^{\gamma\ldots\delta^{\prime}\ldots}}=\bar{H}_{\alpha^{\prime}\ldots\beta\ldots}^{\gamma^{\prime}\ldots\delta\ldots}

(8)

Now, suppose we are given a
U(n)
-charged scalar
\bm{\varphi}\cong\varphi^{\alpha}
. How can we construct a covariant real Lagrangian density for it? Obviously, this requires not only the complex conjugate scalar
\bar{\varphi}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
, but also a form in the fibers
g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}
, which is Hermitian and non-degenerate

\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}=g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}},\qquad g_{\alpha\gamma^{\prime}}g^{\beta\gamma^{\prime}}=\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}.

(9)

Then, using it, the desired Lagrangian density can be written as

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}=-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}\nabla_{a}\varphi^{\alpha}\nabla^{a}\bar{\varphi}^{\beta^{\prime}}-P\left(|\varphi|^{2}\right),

(10)

where
|\varphi|^{2}=g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}\varphi^{\alpha}\bar{\varphi}^{\beta^{\prime}}
, and
P\left(|\varphi|^{2}\right)
is some lower-bounded potential which describes self-interaction of the field
\bm{\varphi}
.

We claim that this Hermitian form in fibers
g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}
is the very analogue of the spacetime metric
g_{ab}
, which will play the role of potential partner in mal-YM. Indeed,
g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}
is quite similar in its properties to
g_{ab}
: Hermiticity is an analogue of the symmetry of the metric, and non-degeneracy allows raising and lowering color indices in the usual way. In this case, when changing the position, the unprimed index becomes primed and vice versa, for example,
\bar{\varphi}_{\alpha}=g_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\bar{\varphi}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
. Thus, the Hermitian form
g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}
establishes a (non-canonical) isomorphism
\bar{V}\cong V^{*}
between the complex conjugate and the dual spaces.

Moreover, the analogy between YM and EG can be carried much further: in fact, the standard YM also has an implicit assumption that the Hermitian form in the fibers is covariantly constant

\nabla_{a}g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}=0,

(11)

which is completely analogous to the condition of covariant constancy of the spacetime metric (
5
) in EG. Accordingly,
the “metric-affine-like” generazization of YM can be obtained by dropping this condition
.

In the remainder of this letter we briefly, without any detailed explanations, list the main consequences of this generalization. All the statements made below will be substantiated in detail in a separate article
Wachowski
.

III
Hermitian and anti-Hermitian parts and gauge transformations

Let us agree to call “matrices” linear maps of the internal color space
V\to V
, i.e. tensors with two unprimed indices—one upper and one lower
\bm{M}\cong M_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, so that the product of matrices is simply a contraction over one of the indices
\bm{M}\bm{N}\cong M^{\alpha}_{\gamma}N^{\gamma}_{\beta}
. Let us note the following: the complex conjugate object
\bar{M}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime}}
is no longer a “matrix” in this sense—it is a map of the complex conjugate space
\bar{V}\to\bar{V}
, it has primed indices, and therefore cannot be multiplied by “matrices”. However, we can define the operation of
Hermitian conjugation
, which maps matrices to matrices, but contains not only complex conjugation, but also “transposition,” i.e. contractions with
g_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
and
g^{\beta\beta^{\prime}}
:

\bm{M}^{\dagger}\cong\bar{M}_{\alpha}^{\beta}=g_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}g^{\beta\beta^{\prime}}\bar{M}_{\beta^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}.

(12)

Although it is not explicitly stated in standard algebra courses, Hermitian conjugation always requires the specification of some Hermitian form
g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}
.

Let us be given two different connections
\nabla_{a}
and
\tilde{\nabla}_{a}
. We define the
total
curvature in the bundle
\mathcal{F}_{ab}[\nabla]\cong\mathscr{F}_{ab}{}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and the
total
connection potential
\mathcal{A}_{a}[\tilde{\nabla}-\nabla]\cong\mathscr{A}_{a}{}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
by the relations
5
5

5

\mathcal{A}_{a}
thus defined is in fact not a usual potential, but a difference or variation of potentials. Accordingly,
\bm{A}_{a}
below differs from the potential in the formulas (
1
) and (
3
).

\displaystyle[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}]\bm{\varphi}=\mathcal{F}_{ab}\bm{\varphi},

(13)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}-\nabla_{a})\bm{\varphi}=\mathcal{A}_{a}\bm{\varphi},

(14)

where
\bm{\varphi}\cong\varphi^{\alpha}
. Then the action of
[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}]
and
(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}-\nabla_{a})
on other gauge-charged (i.e. carrying color indices) fields is obtained simply by the Leibniz rule.

Using the Hermitian conjugation (
12
), we can split
\mathcal{A}_{a}
and
\mathcal{F}_{ab}
into Hermitian and anti-Hermitian parts

\mathcal{A}_{a}=\tilde{e}\bm{B}_{a}-ie\bm{A}_{a},\qquad\mathcal{F}_{ab}=\tilde{e}\bm{G}_{ab}-ie\bm{F}_{ab},

(15)

where
e
and
\tilde{e}
are two positive coupling constants, and

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\tilde{e}}(\mathcal{A}_{a}+\mathcal{A}_{a}^{\dagger}),

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2e}(\mathcal{A}_{a}-\mathcal{A}_{a}^{\dagger}),

(16)

\displaystyle\bm{G}_{ab}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\tilde{e}}(\mathcal{F}_{ab}+\mathcal{F}_{ab}^{\dagger}),

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{ab}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2e}(\mathcal{F}_{ab}-\mathcal{F}_{ab}^{\dagger}).

(17)

As we mentioned at the very beginning, in YM only anti-Hermitian components
\bm{F}_{ab}
and
\bm{A}_{a}
are present. Since this statement always appears at the very beginning and is not substantiated in any way, it may seem that this is some kind of axiom. But why should we adhere to it? In fact, this is not an axiom at all, but a very simple consequence of a deeper condition of covariant constancy of the structure in fibers (
11
). However, if we abandon this condition, it will no longer be the case.

Let us introduce the following quantity, which is a measure of the condition (
11
) violation and which we will therefore call
the YM-deviation vector
:

\bm{N}_{a}\cong N_{a\alpha}{}^{\beta}=-\frac{1}{2\tilde{e}}g^{\beta\beta^{\prime}}\nabla_{a}g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}.

(18)

It can be shown that the Hermitian part of the total curvature
\bm{G}_{ab}
is completely determined by the YM-deviation vector
\bm{N}_{a}
:

\bm{G}_{ab}=\nabla_{a}\bm{N}_{b}-\nabla_{b}\bm{N}_{a}-2\tilde{e}[\bm{N}_{a},\bm{N}_{b}].

(19)

If the condition (
11
) is satisfied, then the YM-deviation vector vanishes
\bm{N}_{a}=0
, so
\bm{G}_{ab}=0
, and the total curvature in the bundle is reduced to the usual strength tensor
\mathcal{F}_{ab}=-ie\bm{F}_{ab}
. Thus, we restore the standard YM. Another way to formulate this is as follows:
if a connection respects the Hermitian form in fibers, then its total curvature will be anti-Hermitian
.

(Note that this situation is an (even somewhat simpler) analogy to what happens in MAG. There, if we abandon the condition of covariant constancy of the metric (
5
), the spacetime curvature ceases to be anti-symmetric with respect to the second pair of indices, but its symmetric part
R_{ab(cd)}
is completely determined by the non-metricity
\nabla_{a}g_{cd}
and vanishes together with it, which returns us to standard EG.)

Let
\bm{u}\cong u_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and
\bm{U}\cong U_{\alpha}^{\beta}
be two arbitrary mutually inverse complex matrices:

\bm{u}\bm{U}=\bm{U}\bm{u}=\mathbf{1}.

(20)

Consider general linear transformations of the color space together with the corresponding change of connection

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{a}[\tilde{\nabla}-\nabla]=\bm{U}\nabla_{a}\bm{u},

(21)

\displaystyle H_{\alpha\ldots}^{\beta\ldots}\mapsto\tilde{H}_{\alpha\ldots}^{\beta\ldots}=U_{\gamma}^{\beta}\cdots\;H_{\delta\ldots}^{\gamma\ldots}\;u_{\alpha}^{\delta}\cdots.

(22)

Since such transformations obviously preserve any index contractions, they will represent
GL(n,\mathbb{C})
gauge symmetry of any geometrically defined theory.

However, introducing the Hermitian form
g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}
breaks this symmetry to
U(1)
. Indeed, the requirement that
g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}
must be invariant under transformations (
21
) singles out a narrower class of
unitary transformations
for which
\bm{U}=\bm{u}^{\dagger}
. If we consider infinitesimal transformations
\bm{u}=\mathbf{1}+\bm{\epsilon}
and split their parameter into Hermitian and anti-Hermitian parts

\bm{\epsilon}=\tilde{e}\bm{\beta}-ie\bm{\alpha},\quad\bm{\beta}=\frac{1}{2\tilde{e}}(\bm{\epsilon}+\bm{\epsilon}^{\dagger}),\quad\bm{\alpha}=\frac{i}{2e}(\bm{\epsilon}-\bm{\epsilon}^{\dagger}),

(23)

then we have the following transformation of the Hermitian form:

\tilde{g}_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}=(\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}+h_{\alpha}^{\beta})g_{\beta\beta^{\prime}},\quad\text{where}\quad\bm{h}=2\tilde{e}\bm{\beta}.

(24)

Thus,
\bm{\alpha}
corresponds to usual unitary transformations preserving the Hermitian form
g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}
, and
\bm{\beta}
corresponds to non-unitary transformations that change it.

Therefore,
g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}
is a Higgs field, and its variation
\bm{h}
is a Goldstone boson. However, it is well known that due to broken gauge symmetry, Goldstone bosons can be completely eliminated from the theory by redefining other fields. This is the case in mal-YM: according to (
24
), for any transformation of the Hermitian form
\bm{h}
we can choose a non-unitary transformation that will reproduce it exactly. Subtracting this gauge transformation from the changes in connection and charged matter fields, when they are present, we can completely eliminate
\bm{h}
from the variation of the action. Thus, a perturbation of the Hermitian form
\bm{h}
is not a physical field carrying degrees of freedom.

This also manifests itself in the Noether identities, which relate the sources of classical fields in theories with gauge symmetry. We define anti-Hermitian
\bm{J}_{a}
and Hermitian
\bm{\Lambda}_{a}
gauge currents as variations of the action of the theory
S[\nabla,g]
with respect to the anti-Hermitian
\bm{A}_{a}
and Hermitian
\bm{B}_{a}
potentials, respectively, and we also define the Hermitian matrix
\bm{E}
, which is the source of the field
\bm{h}

\bm{J}_{a}=\frac{\delta S}{\delta\bm{A}^{a}},\quad\bm{\Lambda}_{a}=-\frac{\delta S}{\delta\bm{B}^{a}},\quad\bm{E}=-2\frac{\delta S}{\delta\bm{h}}.

(25)

Then in pure mal-YM without matter fields, the Noether identities following from the symmetry under unitary
\bm{\alpha}
and non-unutary
\bm{\beta}
gauge transformations, respectively, will be of the form:

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}\bm{J}^{a}-\tilde{e}[\bm{N}_{a},\bm{J}^{a}]+ie[\bm{N}_{a},\bm{\Lambda}^{a}]=0,

(26)

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}\bm{\Lambda}^{a}-\tilde{e}[\bm{N}_{a},\bm{\Lambda}^{a}]-i\frac{\tilde{e}^{2}}{e}[\bm{N}_{a},\bm{J}^{a}]=\tilde{e}\bm{E}.

(27)

The first of these relations is a modification of the conservation law of the Yang-Mills current, which curiously ceases to be preserved in mal-YM. And the second relation means that the equation obtained by varying the action with respect to
\bm{h}
is not an independent EoM, but only a differential consequence of other EoMs.

IV
Action and equations of motion

Summarizing the above, now in our theory in addition to the usual Yang-Mills field
\bm{F}_{ab}
,
\bm{A}_{a}
there appears a second dynamical gauge field
\bm{G}_{ab}
,
\bm{B}_{a}
. The dynamics of these two fields and their interactions with each other are determined by the form of the action.

We will consider the theory with the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{malYM}}=-\frac{1}{4}\tr(\bm{F}_{ab}\bm{F}^{ab}+\bm{G}_{ab}\bm{G}^{ab}+2M^{2}\bm{N}_{a}\bm{N}^{a}).

(28)

Here the coefficient before the first two terms is chosen so that in the quadratic part of the action in perturbations the terms
\nabla_{a}\bm{A}_{b}\nabla^{a}\bm{A}^{b}
and
\nabla_{a}\bm{B}_{b}\nabla^{a}\bm{B}^{b}
have the correct coefficient
-1/2
. The introduction of the third mass term is motivated by the natural desire to be able to restore the condition (
11
) and, consequently, the standard YM in limit
M\to\infty
. Of course, we could also introduce other terms, for example,
\tr(\bm{G}_{ab}\bm{F}^{ab})
,
\tr([\bm{N}_{a},\bm{N}_{b}]\bm{F}^{ab})
, etc., but we do not do this solely for the sake of simplicity, in order not to clutter the presentation.

The Lagrangian (
28
) leads to the following classical equations on the background fields:

\displaystyle\nabla^{b}\bm{F}_{ab}-\tilde{e}[\bm{N}^{b},\bm{F}_{ab}]-ie[\bm{N}^{b},\bm{G}_{ab}]=\bm{J}_{a}^{\mathrm{ext}},

(29)

\displaystyle\nabla^{b}\bm{G}_{ab}-\tilde{e}[\bm{N}^{b},\bm{G}_{ab}]+i\frac{\tilde{e}^{2}}{e}[\bm{N}^{b},\bm{F}_{ab}]+M^{2}\bm{N}_{a}=-\bm{\Lambda}_{a}^{\mathrm{ext}},

(30)

where on the right-hand side we have added external anti-Hermitian
\bm{J}_{a}^{\mathrm{ext}}
and Hermitian
\bm{\Lambda}_{a}^{\mathrm{ext}}
gauge currents that can be generated by the matter fields if they are present in the theory. For example, for a gauge-charged scalar field with Lagrangian (
10
) they will have the form:

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{a}\cong J_{a\alpha}{}^{\beta}=\frac{ie}{2}g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}\left(\varphi^{\beta}\nabla_{a}\bar{\varphi}^{\beta^{\prime}}-\bar{\varphi}^{\beta^{\prime}}\nabla_{a}\varphi^{\beta}\right),

(31)

\displaystyle\bm{\Lambda}_{a}\cong\Lambda_{a\alpha}{}^{\beta}=\frac{\tilde{e}}{2}g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}\nabla_{a}(\bar{\varphi}^{\beta^{\prime}}\varphi^{\beta}).

(32)

Finally, we also present equations for small perturbations
\bm{A}_{a}
and
\bm{B}_{a}
, propagating on a trivial background
\bm{N}_{a}=0
,
\bm{F}_{ab}=\bm{G}_{ab}=0
:

\displaystyle(\delta_{a}^{b}\Box+\nabla^{b}\nabla_{a})\bm{A}_{b}=0,

(33)

\displaystyle(\delta_{a}^{b}\Box+\nabla^{b}\nabla_{a}+M^{2}\delta_{a}^{b})\bm{B}_{b}=0,

(34)

where
\Box=-g^{ab}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}
is the d’Alembertian. It is easy to see that the first equation is the usual equation for a massless gauge field (with the degenerate wave operator, which requires a subsequent gauge fixing procedure), and the second equation is the Proca equation for a vector field of mass
M
, equivalent to the equation
(\Box+M^{2})\bm{B}_{a}=0
with the additional condition
\nabla^{a}\bm{B}_{a}=0
.

It can be noted that if we look for solutions of the equations (
29
)-(
30
) without external sources, for which
\bm{N}_{a}=0
(and hence also
\bm{G}_{ab}=0
), then the remaining field
\bm{F}_{ab}
will satisfy the usual Yang-Mills equation
\nabla^{b}\bm{F}_{ab}=0
. Thus,
any classical solution of pure YM is at the same time a solution of pure mal-YM
. However, it apparently follows from the equation (
34
) that, at least in the vicinity of the trivial background, mal-YM has solutions that are not borrowed from YM.

It also follows from the equation (
30
) that when the mass of the second field tends to infinity,
M\to\infty
, we obtain
\bm{N}_{a}=0
, i.e. we restore the covariant constancy condition (
11
) and return to the standard YM.

V
Potential significance of mal-YM

To summarize, we have constructed what seems to be a very natural generalization of YM, in which in addition to the usual massless Yang-Mills gauge fields
\bm{A}_{a}
and
\bm{F}_{ab}
, there appear some new non-trivially interacting fields
\bm{B}_{a}
and
\bm{G}_{ab}
, which can be made massive. Letting their mass tend to infinity
M\to\infty
restores the standard YM.

Here we have considered this theory exclusively at the classical level. Although at first glance it seems that it is renormalizable and unitary, this, as well as the absence of other pathologies, requires a separate careful check. If it turns out that such a theory for some reason cannot be considered physical, this will already be an interesting result, since it will allow us to understand the nature of the covariant constancy condition (
11
), which underlies the usual YM.

However, if mal-YM turns out to be a physically admissible theory, then such a new generalization with additional parameters has great phenomenological interest. Indeed, the fact that we do not observe the fields
\bm{B}_{a}
and
\bm{G}_{ab}
can always be explained by the too large mass
M
. However, this immediately leads us to the question of what is the lower bound to this mass compatible with the data of accelerator experiments and observational cosmology? Further, can the new theory help explain some phenomena? And can it be that some of the periodically raising (and usually dissolving) deviations from the Standard Model are better described by the new theory at some values of its parameters
e
,
\tilde{e}
and
M
?

In addition to these applied questions, it seems to us that mal-YM also has a more abstract theoretical significance, allowing us to take a new look at the metric-affine gravity (MAG). Typically the standard Hilbert-Einstein action (
7
) is considered. However, our considerations of the mal-YM above also hint that one should probably consider an action quadratic in the curvatures, which is renormalizable and at the same time, in MAG, will not lead to equations with higher derivatives.

An European consensus on [123I]Ioflupane acquisition and reconstruction using 3D CZT SPECT/CT
Authors: Diego Cecchin, Alessandra Zorz, Renaud Lhommel, Anthime Flaus, Perrine Tylski, Gregory Mathoux, Neil Hartman, Eric Guedj, Francesca Serani, Laetitia Imbert, Antoine Verger
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThe introduction of hybrid SPECT/CT systems with 3D multi-head gantries and CZT crystal detectors offers significant clinical potential, especially for brain imaging. These advancements allow for reduced activity/time requirements, improved spatial resolution, contrast, and the ability to study early perfusion phases and enable 4D acquisitions. Approximately 200 systems have been installed worldwide, prompting individual centers to optimize acquisition and reconstruction protocols. A European effort was attempted in this paper to separately harmonize protocols for each system, facilitating multicentric clinical studies and the development of a standardized database.Materials and methodsTo harmonize the protocols for StarGuide (GE HealthCare) system, four online meetings were held involving nuclear medicine experts from seven European centers. These meetings focused on standardizing acquisition and reconstruction parameters based on experts’ opinion and phantom testing. To harmonize the protocols for Veriton (Spectrum Dynamics Medical) results of a prospective study (VERIDAT) were used. Specific acquisition and reconstruction parameters have been detailed for both systems.ResultsSignificant heterogeneity was found in the initial acquisition/reconstruction protocols among the participating centers. After discussions and phantom testing, standard parameters were established for both systems. For the StarGuide, a scan time of 20 min with an energy window of 159 keV (−6.5%/+5%) and relatively wide striatum focus were suggested. For the Veriton, 15 min scan time with a symmetrical energy window (± 7.5%) and adjustable focus were recommended. Reconstruction algorithms for the Veriton used OSEM with kernel inter-iteration filter (factor of 0.125) and a median post-filter (3 × 3 × 3 voxels), while the StarGuide employed a BSREM Q.Clear algorithm with RDP mode. Both systems achieved a central SPECT spatial resolution of around 4 mm.ConclusionThis initiative to standardize acquisition and reconstruction parameters for the new CZT SPECT systems (StarGuide and Veriton) represents an important step towards improving multicenter research and clinical data consistency. The proposed parameters are a preliminary foundation for further optimization and multicentric studies, allowing for more reliable quantitative and qualitative assessments. This work will also aid in updating clinical guidelines and creating a harmonized database for CZT SPECT systems.

Introduction

The introduction of a new technology, undoubtedly, is challenging, both in terms of potential clinical implications and in optimizing acquisition protocols, reconstruction parameters, and dedicated quality control (for example, tailoring NEMA NU-1 standards to these types of systems). The recent market introduction of hybrid devices (Single Photon Emission Computed Tomography/ Computed Tomography SPECT/CT) equipped with 3D multi-head gantries (Nr. 12 heads) and Cadmium Zinc Telluride (CZT) crystal detectors opens new and important clinical prospects, particularly in brain imaging [1]. Among the key advancements are a significant activity/time reduction [2], an increase in contrast, energy resolution and SPECT spatial resolution, the possibility of studying early perfusion phases [3] of receptor tracers (early imaging after radiopharmaceutical injection) and the potential for 4D acquisitions [4]. This advances possibly paves the way of incorporating these devices in large, multicenter drug trials of neuronal synuclein disease [5].

Within a few months (up to March 2025), more than 225 systems of this type have been installed worldwide (> 125 StarGuide - GE HealthCare and > 100 Veriton - Spectrum Dynamics Medical), leading each user/center to independently seek the best protocol to optimize the basic acquisition and reconstruction parameters provided by the manufacturer.

Methods

In this context, at the second European General Electric StarGuide user meeting (September 10, 2023, Vienna), a group of users with an interest in neurology was formed. Among its various objectives, the group aimed to harmonize acquisition and reconstruction protocols to accelerate their implementation and facilitate the collection of homogeneous multicentric data for clinical studies and database creation (a necessity given the numerous differences with traditional scanners and the relative impossibility of using an “as-is” historical database). The aim of this work was not to harmonize the two existing 3D CZT systems (StarGuide and Veriton) but to select, for each platform, the best acquisition and reconstruction parameters based on the experience of expert pilot centers. In fact, the full harmonization of the two systems would have required an excessive economic and human effort in terms of exchange of phantoms and/or testing subjects, detailed understanding of the reconstruction methods (some of which are not yet fully disclosed) and post-processing analysis. The experts therefore agreed to aim at harmonizing the acquisition and reconstruction of the two systems separately with the less prestigious but still very important goal of defining, based on expert consensus, the best possible parameters to date.

To harmonize data provided by the StarGuide (GE HealthCare) system, four online meetings coordinated by prof. Cecchin D., were held, attended by nuclear medicine and physics experts from seven centers (University-Hospital of Padova – Italy; Cliniques Universitaires Saint-Luc – Belgium; Hospices Civils de Lyon – France; Hôpitaux Universitaires de Genève – Switzerland; Swansea-Singleton Hospital – UK; CHU Timone, Marseille – France).

Another group, led by Prof. Verger A., also coordinated an European effort to harmonize the acquisition and reconstruction protocols for the Veriton scanner (Spectrum Dynamics Medical) which were the results of a prospective study (VERIDAT, VERIton and DATscan, NCT03980418). This study, performed in 92 patients referred for suspicion of Parkinson Disease (PD) and Lewy Body Dementia (LBD), aimed to compare 123I-FP-CIT SPECT imaging obtained from a 360°-CZT camera (VERITON-200, Spectrum Dynamics Medical) with different focus configurations (striatum and brain) and from a conventional Anger camera [6]. Data from this study were used to select optimized parameters for the Veriton system.

About the StarGuide, in the absence of a multicenter trial to date, each group independently performed a system performance evaluation, initially utilizing the NEMA NU 1-2018 standards. Specifically, the assessment included at least: energy resolution, flood field uniformity, count rate performance in air, system spatial resolution (with and without scatter), system planar sensitivity, SPECT spatial resolution (with and without scatter), system volume sensitivity, detector-detector (D-D) variations, and tomographic contrast.

Based on the results obtained from the NEMA acceptance testing, each group subsequently conducted in-house qualitative and semi-quantitative evaluations using Hoffman 3D Brain phantom and Jaszczak phantom with cold rod inserts, hot and cold spheres. Furthermore, most groups also employed a Striatal phantom (RSD) equipped with a brain shell containing five separately fillable compartments, inserted into a realistic head to simulate attenuation. The raw data from the phantom acquisitions, particularly those using the Striatal phantom, were extensively reconstructed by varying the available parameters: iterations, subsets, and the gamma (0.2 to 2) and beta (0.01 to 0.08) parameters of the Q.Clear (RDP) reconstruction (in addition to numerous other post-filtering trials).

Each group then independently tested their respective optimized parameters on some patients and compared the result with the values provided by the vendor.

Given the lack of an absolute gold standard to serve as a reference for the optimal combination of reconstruction and post-processing (including post-filtering), each group arrived at its own clinical conclusions. While partially concordant, these were also influenced by local requirements (such as the decision regarding CT acquisition). Consequently, an expert consensus was deemed necessary at this stage.

A specific query form regarding the derived acquisition parameters (scan duration, energy window, focus/non focus acquisition, attenuation method, scatter window and head extender) and reconstruction (reconstruction algorithm, gamma and beta parameters, scatter correction method, reconstruction iterations and subsets, post-filtering and voxel size) was filled in by the involved centers.

Discussion

Several points must be noted and discussed regarding these tables. Starting with the acquisition parameters, the activity to be administered to the patient—110–185 MBq—derives from the drug’s RCP. Since each vial of the drug is single-dose, it is not possible to extract a dose from multiple vials, nor is it possible to go below 110 MBq without engaging in off-label use of the drug. Therefore, the group of experts deemed it obvious to adhere, at this stage, to the package insert guidelines, although it is reasonable to consider whether, with these new ultra-sensitive devices, it would be important to review the package insert recommendations to allow for lower activities. In this line, it should be noted that the VERIDAT (Veriton-200, Spectrum Dynamics Medical) study was performed with a single injection of 130 MBq while [6] a single injection of 145 MBq was used for the planar camera equipped with a CZT detector [7].

Regarding the StarGuide SPECT scan time (which for the StarGuide survey equipment varied between 15 and 32 min), a scan time of 20 min was considered safe to obtain a large number of counts, given the administered activity. It is likely possible to reduce the scan time (even significantly), but this requires a multicenter study (approved now by the Ethics Committee of Padua in October 2025 and ongoing) with blinded data analysis at 20 min and shorter durations (likely 10 and 15 min) to confirm the results and quantify potential time reductions. This has been validated through the prospective study VERIDAT for the Veriton (Spectrum Dynamics) with 15 min of acquisitions.

It is worth noting that the technological differences between the two systems (StarGuide and Veriton) likely do not warrant the approximate 5-minute time difference adopted in the acquisition protocol defined by the expert panel. We reiterate that this discrepancy is due to the fact that, since results from the multicenter StarGuide study validating a potential time reduction are not yet available, a precautionary decision was made to acquire a substantially high number of counts (resulting in an increased acquisition time).

As for the “focus mode,” which allows an increase in counts (or rather an increase in the signal-to-noise ratio) in the target area, it was unanimously decided to use it, but with a relatively wide focus to avoid the risk of truncation. The energy window, given the exceptional energy resolution of the CZT systems, was narrowed compared to traditional equipment, maintaining a slightly asymmetrical “shoulder” broader on the left (159 keV − 6.5% +5%). For the Veriton, a symmetrical energy window (± 7.5%) has been used in the VERIDAT study (Table 4). The fact that one system (Veriton) employs a symmetric window while the other (StarGuide) uses an asymmetric one is essentially based on the initial parameters recommended by the respective vendors.

The acquisition of the CT component for attenuation correction (which can also be performed with non-CT-based methods, such as the Chang method) or for morphology assessment is not clinically mandatory and largely depends on the availability of recent CT or MRI scans. In the context of creating a shared database, it would however be advisable to acquire the CT component of the SPECT/CT scan to create two distinct databases (one corrected for attenuation and one uncorrected).

Although scatter is always present in a SPECT scan, it was not deemed mandatory (at least for the StarGuide system) to correct for it, as the energy window is so narrow that the scatter contribution is minimal. For the Veriton system, as in the VERIDAT study, a symmetrical energy window was applied (to be aligned with conventional camera for comparison), an additional scatter correction can be proposed to increase the contrast ratio.

Additionally, to harmonize the procedure, it was decided to always use the “head extender” and to start image acquisition precisely 3 h after tracer injection.

The reconstruction parameters (Tables 3, 4 and 5) chosen by the expert group reflect the dual objective of obtaining images as close as possible (Fig. 1) to the patient’s actual anatomy (with minimal smoothing) while ensuring they can be easily interpreted without being affected by excessive noise (Figs. 2 and 3). The final voxel size is 2.46 mm³, for both systems corresponding to the intrinsic spatial resolution.

For both the Veriton and the StarGuide 360°-CZT cameras, the 12 high-resolution swiveling detectors are positioned close to the head around a 360° configuration and a total of more than 2000 projections are, at present, recorded using the proposed acquisition protocol. The reconstruction parameters were optimized according to the convergence for the striatum/occipital contrast, a clinically meaningful ratio, from a brain phantom dedicated to dopaminergic imaging (RSD striatal phantom). A kernel inter-iteration filter (factor of 0.125) and a median post-filter (3 × 3 × 3 voxels) was then applied for the Veriton system using an OSEM method. For the StarGuide System a Block Sequential Regularized Expectation Maximization (BSREM) algorithm named Q.Clear have been selected using RDP mode (gamma 1/ Beta 0.04). Further reconstruction parameters were fixed at 8 subsets and 15 iterations for Veriton and 15 subset and 40 iterations for StarGuide using striatum focus. For the Veriton system using brain focus 8 subsets and 10 iterations have been selected. With these reconstruction parameters, the central SPECT spatial resolution of the 360° camera (full-width at half maximum, FWHM), of a punctual source placed at the central sites of fields-of-view was fixed at 4.2 mm [6] for the Veriton system and 4 mm for the StarGuide system [8].

Although a direct comparison of the two systems would be of interest, it remains however, well beyond the scope of the present study.

Aware that the optimization process will be iterative, and that the data provided in this paper are far from definitive, we nonetheless believe that this European initiative to define common basic acquisition and reconstruction parameters could lay the foundation for more robust multicenter research, also based on more reliable quantitative (in addition to qualitative) data and could provide data on new CZT systems to update current clinical international recommendations/guidelines [9, 10] and help to constitute harmonized normal database on these new CZT SPECT systems.

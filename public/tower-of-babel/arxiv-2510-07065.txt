Parameterized Complexity of s-Club Cluster Edge Deletion: When Is the Diameter Bound Necessary?
Ajinkya Gaikwad

Abstract
We study the parameterized and kernelization complexity of the s s -Club Cluster Edge Deletion problem, a natural distance-bounded generalization of Cluster Edge Deletion . Given a graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) and integers k , s k,s , the goal is to delete at most k k edges so that every connected component in the resulting graph has diameter at most s s . This problem captures a broad class of distance-constrained graph modification problems that interpolate between clustering and connectivity control. We first establish that for s = 2 s=2 the problem is NP-hard already on split graphs, closing the complexity gap between the polynomially solvable case s = 1 s=1 and the trivial case s = 3 s=3 . For this setting we design a cubic-vertex kernel parameterized by k k , thereby obtaining the first polynomial kernel for 2-Club Cluster Edge Deletion on split graphs. On the structural side, we settle an open question of Montecchiani, Ortali, Piselli, and Tappini ( Inf. Comput. , 2023) by proving that the problem is W[1]-hard when parameterized by pathwidth (and hence by treewidth ), showing that the diameter bound s s is indispensable for fixed-parameter tractability. In contrast, we identify several width and density parameters for which the dependence on s s is unnecessary: the problem is fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by treedepth , neighborhood diversity , or the cluster vertex deletion number . This generalizes previous FPT results known only for s = 1 s=1 . We also complement these results by proving that no polynomial kernel exists when parameterized by the vertex cover number , even for s = 2 s=2 . Finally, we present an FPT bicriteria approximation scheme that, for graphs excluding long induced cycles, runs in time f ⁡ ( k , 1 / ϵ ) ⋅ n 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) f(k,1/\epsilon)\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)} and produces a solution of size at most k k whose components have diameter at most ( 1 + ϵ ) ​ s (1+\epsilon)s . In addition, we design an exact FPT algorithm for s s -Club Cluster Edge Deletion on interval graphs parameterized solely by k k , and a polynomial-time algorithm for its restriction to unit interval graphs. We also initiate the study of the directed variant, s s -Club Cluster Arc Deletion , and show that it is W[1]-hard when parameterized by k k , even on directed acyclic graphs. Overall, our results delineate the precise role of the diameter bound s s in the parameterized tractability landscape of distance-bounded clustering problems.

Introduction

Graph modification problems lie at the heart of algorithmic graph theory.
They ask whether a given graph can be transformed into one satisfying a desired structural property
by performing a limited number of edge or vertex edits.
This framework has proved remarkably robust: by varying the target property,
one captures many classical problems in clustering, network design, and computational biology.
Among these, perhaps the most fundamental is the
clique-based
clustering model,
which seeks to delete or add a few edges so that the resulting graph is a disjoint union of cliques.
This gives rise to the well-studied
Cluster Edge Deletion
problem.

A natural generalization replaces cliques by
s
-clubs
:
vertex sets whose induced subgraphs have diameter at most
s
.
An
s
-club can be viewed as a relaxed clique that allows distances up to
s
,
thus tolerating missing edges while maintaining dense local structure.
The corresponding modification problem, called
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
,
asks whether at most
k
edges can be deleted from a graph
G=(V,E)

so that every connected component of the resulting graph has diameter at most
s
.
For
s=1
this coincides with
Cluster Edge Deletion
,
while larger values of
s
model progressively looser notions of cluster cohesion.

Other
s
-club modification problems.

The
s
-club concept has inspired a wide family of related graph modification problems,
each differing in the allowed operations but sharing the common goal of enforcing
s
-club structure.
Examples include
s
-Club Cluster Vertex Deletion
,

s
-Club Cluster Edge Addition
, and
s
-Club Cluster Editing
.
The
vertex-deletion
variant was recently investigated by
Chakrabortty, Chandran, and Pillai
[
4
]
,
who proved that for every
s\geq 2
the problem is NP-hard even on bounded-degree planar bipartite graphs
and APX-hard on bounded-degree bipartite graphs,
while being polynomial-time solvable on trapezoid graphs.
The
editing
and
addition
variants were explored by
Figiel, Himmel, Nichterlein, and Niedermeier
[
5
]

and by Bazgan, Heggernes, Nichterlein, and Pontoizeau
[
1
]
, respectively.
Figiel et al. showed that
2-Club Cluster Editing
is W[2]-hard with respect to the number of edge modifications,
and that
2-Club Cluster Vertex Deletion
is fixed-parameter tractable
but admits no polynomial kernel under standard complexity assumptions.
Bazgan et al. proved that the
s
-Club Edge Addition
problem is W[2]-hard
with respect to the number of edges to add, even when restricted to split graphs,
and that minimizing the number of edges retained to maintain diameter
2
or
3
is NP-complete.
Together, these studies demonstrate that permitting additions or combined editing
operations significantly increases computational hardness compared to the pure deletion setting.

Previous work.

The case
s=1
, corresponding to the classical
Cluster Edge Deletion
problem,
has been extensively investigated in both classical and parameterized complexity.
It remains NP-hard even on highly restricted graph classes such as planar graphs
[
9
]
,

C_{4}
-free graphs of maximum degree four
[
12
]
, and various hereditary graph classes.
In particular, Gao et al.
[
8
]
proved that
Cluster Deletion
is NP-hard on

(C_{5},P_{5},\text{bull},\text{fork},\text{co-gem},\text{4-pan},\text{co-4-pan})
-free graphs
as well as on
(2K_{2},3K_{1})
-free graphs.

From a classical viewpoint, the problem is polynomial-time solvable on split graphs for
s=1
,
and the case
s=3
is trivially easy since every connected split graph has diameter at most three.
The complexity of the intermediate case
s=2
, however, was open. On the positive side, the problem for
s=1
is polynomial-time solvable on interval graphs
[
13
]
.

A long line of research has focused on the parameterized complexity of the problem.
When parameterized by the number of edge deletions
k
,

Cluster Edge Deletion
admits a fixed-parameter algorithm
running in time
\mathcal{O}^{*}(1.404^{k})

[
20
]
,
which currently represents the best known bound for this parameterization.

Beyond the solution-size parameter, Italiano et al.
[
10
]
investigated the problem under
structural measures such as
twin cover
and
neighborhood diversity
,
for which they designed fixed-parameter algorithms, thereby identifying broader classes of graphs
on which
Cluster Edge Deletion
remains tractable.
Additionally, Komusiewicz and Uhlmann
[
11
]
established an FPT algorithm
for the problem parameterized by the
cluster vertex deletion number
.
For
s=2
, Abu-Khzam et al. provided an FPT with a running time
\mathcal{O}^{*}(2.692^{k})
.

However, once
s>1
, the overall complexity landscape remains far less understood, leaving a number of natural questions about the role of
s
in determining tractability.
A recent result of Montecchiani et al.
[
17
]

established that the problem is fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by
s+\mathrm{tw}(G)
,
where
\mathrm{tw}(G)
denotes the treewidth of the input graph,
and explicitly asked whether dependence on
s
is necessary—that is,
whether
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
remains FPT under treewidth alone.

Our results.

We revisit
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
through the lens of parameterized and structural complexity,
with the goal of delineating the precise boundary of its tractability.
Our contributions unify and extend several existing lines of research.

•

Classical complexity and kernelization.

We prove that
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is NP-hard on split graphs already for
s=2
,
completing the complexity landscape on this class since the cases
s=1
and
s=3
are polynomial-time solvable.
For the same setting we design a cubic vertex kernel parameterized by solution size,
giving the first polynomial kernel for
2-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
on split graphs.

•

Parameterized intractability.

We settle an open problem posed by Montecchiani, Ortali, Piselli, and Tappini
(
Information and Computation
, 2023) by proving that the problem is W[1]-hard
when parameterized by
pathwidth
—and consequently by
treewidth
.
In fact, our reduction yields a stronger result: the problem remains W[1]-hard
even when parameterized by the combined parameter
\pw(G)+d
,
where d denotes the maximum number of s-clubs allowed in the modified graph.
This firmly establishes that the diameter bound s is essential for fixed-parameter tractability.

•

Structural parameterizations.

We identify several graph parameters for which the dependence on
s
is unnecessary:
the problem is fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by

treedepth
,
neighborhood diversity
, or
cluster vertex deletion number
.
These results extend to all
s\geq 1
the algorithms of Italiano, Konstantinidis, and Papadopoulos
(
Algorithmica
, 2023) for the case
s=1
,
and generalize the FPT algorithm of Komusiewicz and Uhlmann
(
SOFSEM
, 2011) for the cluster vertex deletion parameter.

•

Solution-size parameterization.

While
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is known to be FPT under the combined parameter
s+k
,
the case of parameter
k
alone remains unresolved.
We make progress by designing an FPT bicriteria approximation scheme that,
for graphs excluding long induced cycles, runs in time
f(k,\varepsilon)\,n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}

and outputs a deletion set of size at most
k

yielding components of diameter at most
(1+\varepsilon)s
.
In addition, we present an exact FPT algorithm on
interval graphs
parameterized solely by
k
,
running in time
f(k)\,n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
,
and show that the problem admits a
polynomial-time
solution on
unit interval graphs
for any
s
.

•

Directed generalization.

We introduce and analyze
s
-Club Cluster Arc Deletion
on directed graphs.
Given a digraph
D=(V,A)
, the goal is to delete at most
k
arcs so that in
D-F

every weakly connected component
C
satisfies:
for all
u,v\in V(C)
, if
u
is reachable from
v
, then there exists a directed path from
v
to
u

of length at most
s
.
This directed formulation extends the undirected notion by bounding asymmetric reachability distances.
We show that this variant is W[1]-hard when parameterized by the solution size
k
,
even when restricted to directed acyclic graphs (DAGs).

Preliminaries

2.1
Basic Graph Notation

We consider finite, simple, undirected, and directed graphs.
For a graph
G
, its vertex and edge sets are denoted by
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively.
An undirected edge between vertices
u
and
v
is written as
\{u,v\}
, while a directed edge (or
arc
) from
u
to
v
is written as
(u,v)
.

For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, the
open neighborhood
of
v
is

N_{G}(v):=\{u\in V(G)\mid\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
,
and its
closed neighborhood
is
N_{G}[v]:=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}
.
The
degree
of
v
is
\deg_{G}(v):=|N_{G}(v)|
.

For a directed graph
D=(V,A)
, the
out-neighborhood
and
in-neighborhood
of a vertex
v
are defined as

N_{D}^{+}(v):=\{u\mid(v,u)\in A\}
and
N_{D}^{-}(v):=\{u\mid(u,v)\in A\}
.
The corresponding
outdegree
and
indegree
are

\deg_{D}^{+}(v):=|N_{D}^{+}(v)|
and
\deg_{D}^{-}(v):=|N_{D}^{-}(v)|
, respectively.

A
path
in an undirected or directed graph is a sequence of distinct vertices

v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{\ell}
such that consecutive vertices are adjacent (or properly oriented in the directed case).
The
length
of a path is
\ell-1
.
For two vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, the
distance

\dist_{G}(u,v)
is the length of a shortest path between
u
and
v
,
and is defined to be
\infty
if no path exists.
The
diameter
of a graph
G
is the maximum distance between any two vertices in the same connected component, that is,

\diam(G):=\max_{u,v\in V(G)}\dist_{G}(u,v).

For a vertex subset
S\subseteq V(G)
, we write
G[S]
to denote the subgraph of
G
induced by
S
.

An
orientation
of an undirected graph
G=(V,E)
is a directed graph
\Lambda=(V,A)
obtained by replacing each edge
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
with exactly one of the arcs
(u,v)
or
(v,u)
.
Given an edge-weight function
w:E(G)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, the
weighted outdegree
of a vertex
u
under the orientation
\Lambda
is defined as

w_{\mathrm{out}}(u):=\sum_{(u,v)\in A(\Lambda)}w(\{u,v\}),

which coincides with the usual outdegree when
w(e)=1
for all
e\in E(G)
.
All logarithms are taken to base 2 unless stated otherwise.
We use standard asymptotic notation, and
\mathcal{O}^{*}(\cdot)
suppresses polynomial factors in
n
.

Definition 1

(Interval graph)
.

A graph
G=(V,E)
is an
interval graph
if there exists a family of closed intervals

\mathcal{I}=\{I_{v}=[\ell_{v},r_{v}]\mid v\in V\}
on the real line such that
two distinct vertices
u,v\in V
are adjacent in
G

if and only if the corresponding intervals intersect, that is,

uv\in E\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad I_{u}\cap I_{v}\neq\emptyset.

Equivalently, interval graphs are precisely the intersection graphs of intervals on a line.

Definition 2

(Unit interval graph)
.

An
unit interval graph
(also known as a
proper interval graph
)
is an interval graph that admits an interval representation
in which all intervals have the same length.
Formally, there exists a representation

\mathcal{I}=\{I_{v}=[x_{v},x_{v}+1]\mid v\in V\}

such that
uv\in E
if and only if
I_{u}\cap I_{v}\neq\emptyset
.

2.2
Structural Graph Parameters

We now review the concept of a tree decomposition introduced by Robertson and Seymour
[
19
]
.
Treewidth is a measure of how “tree-like” the graph is.

Definition 3

(Robertson and Seymour
[
19
]
)
.

A tree decomposition of a
graph
G=(V,E)
is a tree
T
together with a
collection of subsets
X_{t}
(called
bags
) of
V
labeled by the nodes
t
of
T
such that

\bigcup_{t\in T}X_{t}=V
and (1) and (2) below hold:

1.

For every edge
(u,v)\in E(G)
, there is some
t
such that
\{u,v\}\subseteq X_{t}
.

2.

(Interpolation Property) If
t
is a node on the unique path in
T
from
t_{1}
to
t_{2}
, then

X_{t_{1}}\cap X_{t_{2}}\subseteq X_{t}
.

Definition 4

(Robertson and Seymour
[
19
]
)
.

The width of a tree decomposition is
the maximum value of
|X_{t}|-1
taken over all the nodes
t
of the tree
T
of the decomposition.
The
treewidth

{\tw}(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimum width among all possible tree decompositions of
G
.

Definition 5
.

If the tree
T
of a tree decomposition is a path, then we say that the tree decomposition
is a
path decomposition
. The
pathwidth

{\pw}(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimum width among all possible path decompositions of
G
.

A rooted forest is a disjoint union of rooted trees. Given a rooted forest
F
, its
transitive closure
is a graph
H
in which
V(H)
contains all the nodes of the rooted forest, and
E(H)
contain an edge between two vertices whenever those two vertices form an ancestor-descendant pair in the forest
F
.

Definition 6
.

The
treedepth
of a graph
G
is the minimum height of a rooted forest
F
whose transitive closure contains the graph
G
as a subgraph. It is denoted by
{\tt td}(G)
.

Definition 7
.

A set
S\subseteq V(G)
is a
vertex cover
of
G=(V,E)
if each edge in
E
has at least one endpoint in
S
. The
size
of a smallest vertex cover of
G
is the
vertex cover number
of
G
.

Ganian
[
7
]
introduced
a new parameter called twin-cover and showed that it is capable of solving
a wide range of hard problems.
According to Ganian
[
7
]
, “The twin-cover is a direct generalization of vertex cover towards richer graph classes,
including dense graph classes (which have unbounded treewidth).”
Here we relax the definition of vertex cover so that not all edges need to be covered.

Definition 8

(Ganian
[
7
]
)
.

An edge
(u,v)\in E(G)
is a
twin edge
of
G
if
N[u]=N[v]
.

Definition 9

(Ganian
[
7
]
)
.

A set
X\subseteq V(G)
is a
twin-cover
of
G
if every edge in
G
is either twin edge or incident to a vertex in
X
. We then say that
G
has
twin-cover number

k
if
k
is the minimum possible size
of a twin-cover of
G
.

Two different vertices
u
,
v
are called
true twins
if
N[u]=N[v]
.
Likewise,
u
,
v
are called
false twins
if
N(u)=N(v)
.
In general,
u
,
v
are called
twins
if they are either true twins or false twins.
If they are twins, we say that they have the same
neighbourhood type
.

Definition 10

(Lampis
[
15
]
)
.

A graph
G=(V,E)

has
neighbourhood diversity
at most
d
, if there exists a partition of
V

into at most
d

sets (we call these sets type classes) such that all the vertices in each set have the same neighbourhood type.

Definition 11
.

A
feedback vertex set
of a graph
G
is a set of vertices whose removal turns
G
into a forest. The minimum size of a feedback vertex set in
G
is the
feedback vertex set number
of
G
, denoted by
{\fvs}(G)
.

Definition 12
.

The
cluster vertex deletion number
of a graph is the minimum number of its vertices whose deletion results in a disjoint union of complete graphs.

3
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
on Split Graphs

It is known that
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
admits a polynomial-time algorithm on split graphs when
s=1
.
Moreover, since every connected component of a split graph has diameter at most 3,
the problem is also polynomial-time solvable on split graphs when
s=3
.
In contrast, we show that
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
becomes NP-hard on split graphs already for
s=2
.
In particular, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 13
.

s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is NP-hard on split graphs already for
s=2
.

Proof.

We give a polynomial-time reduction from the classical
Clique
problem, which is NP-hard even when restricted to
r
-regular graphs.

Reduction construction.

Let
I=(G=(V,E),k)
be an instance of
Clique
, where
G
is
r
-regular.
We construct an equivalent instance
I^{\prime}=(G^{\prime},s,k^{\prime})
of
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
with
s=2
.

Step 1: Subdivision and clique formation.

For each edge
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
, introduce a new vertex
e_{uv}
adjacent to both
u
and
v
,
and delete the edge
\{u,v\}
.
Let
C:=\{e_{uv}:\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
denote the set of all subdivision vertices.
Next, make
C
a clique by adding all edges between every pair of vertices in
C
.

Step 2: Adding a universal auxiliary clique.

Introduce a new clique
C^{\prime}
of size
k^{\prime}+1
(to be defined later) and make every vertex of
C
adjacent to every vertex of
C^{\prime}
.
Thus,
C\cup C^{\prime}
induces a clique in
G^{\prime}
, while the original vertices
V(G)
form an independent set.

Step 3: Parameter setting.

We set

k^{\prime}:=r(n-k)
,
where
n=|V(G)|
.
This completes the construction of
G^{\prime}
.
Clearly,
G^{\prime}
is a split graph, as its vertex set can be partitioned into the clique
C\cup C^{\prime}
and the independent set
V(G)
.
The construction can be performed in polynomial time.

(If) direction.

Assume that
G
contains a clique
R\subseteq V(G)
of size
k
.
We construct an edge-deletion set
F\subseteq E(G^{\prime})
as follows:
for each vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus R
, delete all edges incident to
v
in
G^{\prime}
.
Since each such vertex
v
has degree
r
(adjacent to
r
subdivision vertices in
C
),
the number of deleted edges is exactly

|F|=r(n-k)=k^{\prime}
.
We now show that every connected component of
G^{\prime}-F
has diameter at most 2.
Each deleted vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus R
becomes isolated (diameter 0).
All other vertices lie in a single connected component

S:=R\cup C\cup C^{\prime}
.
Observe that:

•

any two vertices of
C\cup C^{\prime}
are adjacent (distance 1);

•

for any distinct
x,y\in R
, there exists a subdivision vertex
e_{xy}\in C
adjacent to both, since
\{x,y\}\in E(G)
;
hence,
\dist_{S}(x,y)=2
;

•

for
x\in R
and
y\in C\cup C^{\prime}
, either
x
is adjacent to
y
(if
y=e_{x,z}
) or there exists
y^{\prime}\in C
such that
x
–
y^{\prime}
–
y
is a path of length 2.

Therefore,
\diam(S)\leq 2
, and thus all connected components of
G^{\prime}-F
have diameter at most 2.
Hence,
I^{\prime}
is a yes-instance.

(Only if) direction.

Conversely, suppose that
I^{\prime}=(G^{\prime},2,k^{\prime})
is a yes-instance; that is,
there exists
F\subseteq E(G^{\prime})
with
|F|\leq k^{\prime}
such that every connected component of
G^{\prime}-F
has diameter at most 2.
First observe that all vertices in
C\cup C^{\prime}
must lie in the same connected component,
since between any two such vertices there exist at least
k^{\prime}+1
internally edge-disjoint paths,
and deleting at most
k^{\prime}
edges cannot disconnect them.
Let this component be
S
.
Let
R:=S\cap V(G)
denote the subset of original vertices lying in
S
.
We claim that
R
induces a clique of size at least
k
in
G
.
Indeed, if two distinct vertices
x,y\in R
were
not
adjacent in
G
,
then in
G^{\prime}
their shortest connecting path would have length 3
(
x
–
e_{xz}
–
z
–
e_{zy}
–
y
for some common neighbor
z
),
contradicting the assumption that every component has diameter at most 2.
Hence,
R
induces a clique in
G
.
Finally, if
|R|\leq k-1
, then at least
n-(k-1)
vertices of
V(G)\setminus R
must be isolated,
requiring the deletion of all their incident
r
edges,
for a total of at least
r(n-(k-1))=k^{\prime}+r>k^{\prime}
edge deletions,
contradicting
|F|\leq k^{\prime}
.
Thus
|R|\geq k
, and
G
contains a clique of size at least
k
.

For a vertex set

Reduction Rule 5

To further understand the structural limits of tractability, we also consider a natural
refinement of the problem where, in addition to bounding the diameter of each component,
we also restrict their total number.
This parameter, denoted by

s
-clubs allowed
in the resulting graph after at most
k
edge deletions.
Intuitively, the parameter

captures a form of global clustering constraint:
while the classical version asks only for every component to be an
s
-club,
the
d

s
-clubs together form
a limited number of cohesive groups.
This variant naturally generalizes the notion of distance-based clustering
and provides a finer control over the decomposition structure of the solution.

-Club

-Cluster Edge Deletion

such that
the graph

can be partitioned into at most

each having diameter at most

?

Proof.

We modify the reduction used in the proof of Theorem

All other parts of the construction (spine vertices, reinforced paths, and inter-gadget connections)

satisfies

which is bounded by a function of the vertex cover size of the source instance.

Pathwidth bound.

The pathwidth of the constructed graph
G^{\prime}
remains bounded by the same function
as before.
In the original construction, each gadget could be placed in the path decomposition
within a bounded number of consecutive bags.
The identification of the endpoints

x_{0}^{b_{s}}
and
x_{0}^{*b_{s}}
into two common vertices
x_{0}^{I}
and
x_{0}^{*I}

does not increase the width: removing
x_{0}^{I}
again separates all gadgets

\{H_{b_{s}}\}
exactly as in the original argument.
Thus,
\pw(G^{\prime})
remains bounded by a function of
\pw(G)
.

Preservation of correctness.

The correctness arguments of Theorem
16
continue to hold
verbatim.
In particular:

1.

All vertices of each
X^{u}
lie in a single component
C_{u}
.

2.

Distinct spines corresponding to vertices in the vertex cover
C

induce distinct components, and all vertices belonging to the independent-set
gadgets jointly form one additional component
C_{I}
.

3.

For each edge
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
, the set
F
must contain
either the two unique edges incident with
y^{u}_{v}
whose other
endpoints lie in
X^{u}
, or the two unique edges incident with

y^{v}_{u}
whose other endpoints lie in
X^{v}
.
If neither pair were deleted, the reinforced path between
y^{u}_{v}

and
y^{v}_{u}
would remain, merging
C_{u}
and
C_{v}
into a component
of diameter greater than
s
, contradicting feasibility.

Since
|F|\leq 2|E(G)|
, exactly one such pair is deleted for each

\{u,v\}\in E(G)
, defining a unique orientation of
G
consistent with
the solution to the source
MMO
instance.
The modified identification does not affect this argument, because the
gadgets
H_{b_{s}}
never connect to each other directly, and the reinforced
connections remain confined to pairs
\{H_{u},H_{v}\}
for
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
.

Conclusion.

The reduction remains parameter-preserving and computable in polynomial time,
and the correctness of the orientation–deletion correspondence holds unchanged.
Therefore,
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is W[1]-hard when parameterized
by
\pw+d
.
∎

5
FPT under Treedepth, Cluster Vertex Deletion Number, and Neighbourhood Diversity

In this section, we establish fixed-parameter tractability of the
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
problem under three structural parameters of the input graph: treedepth, cluster-vertex-deletion number, and neighbourhood diversity.

5.1
FPT under Treedepth

Theorem 18
.

s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
can be solved in time
\mathcal{O}\!\left(2^{\,2^{\,\mathcal{O}(k^{3})}}\cdot n^{2}\right)
,
and is therefore fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by the treedepth
k
of the input graph.

Proof.

It was shown in
[
17
]
that

s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
admits a dynamic programming algorithm
running in time

\mathcal{O}\!\left(2^{2^{\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{tw}(G)^{2}\log s)}}\cdot n^{2}\right)
,
parameterized by
s+\operatorname{tw}(G)
, where
\operatorname{tw}(G)

is the treewidth of
G
.

Let
k=\operatorname{td}(G)
denote the treedepth of
G
.
By a result of Nešetřil and Ossona de Mendez
[
18
, Chapter 6]
,
every connected graph of treedepth at most
k
has diameter at most
2^{k}-1
.
Moreover,
\operatorname{td}(G)\leq\operatorname{tw}(G)+1
holds for all graphs.
We analyze two cases:

Case 1:
s\geq 2^{k}
.

Then every connected component of
G
already has diameter at most
s
,
so
G
itself is an
s
-club cluster, and the empty set is an optimal solution.
Hence the instance can be solved trivially in polynomial time.

Case 2:
s<2^{k}
.

We invoke the above dynamic programming algorithm with parameter

s+\operatorname{tw}(G)\leq 2^{k}+(k-1)
.
Substituting these bounds into the running time expression yields:

\mathcal{O}\!\left(2^{\,2^{\,\mathcal{O}\!\bigl(k^{2}\log(2^{k}-1)\bigr)}}\cdot n^{2}\right)=\mathcal{O}\!\left(2^{\,2^{\,\mathcal{O}(k^{3})}}\cdot n^{2}\right).

This is an FPT running time depending only on
k
.
Hence,
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is fixed-parameter tractable
when parameterized by the treedepth of the input graph.
∎

5.2
FPT under Cluster Vertex Deletion

Having established tractability under treedepth, we now turn our attention
to the parameter
cluster vertex deletion number
.
Recall that a
cluster vertex deletion set
is a set of vertices whose removal
turns the graph into a cluster graph (a disjoint union of cliques).
Before presenting our FPT result for
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion

parameterized by cluster vertex deletion number, we first establish a
useful structural lemma concerning the distribution of twin classes
across the connected components of a solution.

Lemma 19

(Twin consolidation)
.

Let
s\geq 2
and let
F
be an optimal
s
-club cluster edge-deletion set of a
graph
G
. Let
T
be a twin class in
G
, i.e., a set of vertices such that
for all
x,y\in T
either
N[x]=N[y]
(true twins) or
N(x)=N(y)
(false twins).
Then there exists another optimal solution
F^{\prime}
such that
in
G-F^{\prime}
all but at most one connected component contain at most one vertex of
T
.

Proof.

Let
F
be any optimal solution.
Let the components of
G-F
be
C_{1},\dots,C_{\ell}
.
Fix
t\in T
and set
\gamma_{i}:=|N_{G}(t)\cap V(C_{i})|
for each
i
.
Choose
i^{\star}
maximizing
\gamma_{i}
.

Removing redundant twins.

Suppose
C
is a component containing two twins
t,t^{\prime}\in T
.
Since
N(t)=N(t^{\prime})
(for false twins) or
N[t]=N[t^{\prime}]
(for true twins),
any shortest path in
C
that uses
t^{\prime}
can be rerouted through
t

without increasing its length.
Hence deleting
t^{\prime}
does not increase distances, and

\diam(C-\{t^{\prime}\})\leq\diam(C)\leq s
.
Iterating, we keep only one vertex of
T
in each component.

Adding twins elsewhere.

Let
C
be a component with a surviving twin
t\in T
, and insert a new
copy
t^{\prime}
with
N(t^{\prime})=N(t)
(or
N[t^{\prime}]=N[t]
in the true-twin case).
For every
x\in V(C)
we have

\dist_{C\cup\{t^{\prime}\}}(t^{\prime},x)=\dist_{C}(t,x)
,
and for true twins also
\dist(t,t^{\prime})=1
.
For false twins, if
N_{C}(t)\neq\emptyset
, then
t
and
t^{\prime}
share a common
neighbor in
C
, giving
\dist(t,t^{\prime})=2
; if
N_{C}(t)=\emptyset
, the new vertex

t^{\prime}
is isolated and forms its own singleton component.
Hence, in all cases (for
s\geq 2
when
T
is a false-twin class),

\diam(C\cup\{t^{\prime}\})=\diam(C)\leq s
, so re-inserting deleted twins into a component
never increases its diameter.

Choice of target component.

By Step (1) we may collect all but one twin from each component into a set

R\subseteq T
. By Step (2) we may reinsert
R
into any component already
containing a twin without increasing its diameter.
If a vertex
t^{\prime}\in R
is placed in
C_{j}
, then
F
must delete at least

\deg_{G}(t)-\gamma_{j}
edges.
Placing all of
R
into
C_{i^{\star}}
instead costs at most

\deg_{G}(t)-\gamma_{i^{\star}}
per vertex.
Since
\gamma_{i^{\star}}\geq\gamma_{j}
for all
j
, this does not increase the
number of deletions. Hence
|F^{\prime}|\leq|F|
.
Therefore
F^{\prime}
is also optimal, and all but one connected component contain at most one
vertex of
T
.

Remark.

The assumption
s\geq 2
is essential for false twins.
If
s=1
, adding a false twin
t^{\prime}
to a component already containing its twin
t

creates a pair at distance 2 (via a common neighbor), thus violating the
1-club property.
For true twins, however, the distance between any two members of
T
is 1,
so the lemma also holds for
s\geq 1

∎

Now, we show that
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is FPT when parameterized by the cluster vertex deletion number of the input graph.

Theorem 20
.

s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is FPT when
parameterized by the cluster vertex deletion number of the input graph.

Proof.

Let
X\subseteq V(G)
be a cluster vertex deletion set of size
k
, so that
G-X
is a disjoint union of cliques.

Bounding the Diameter.

It is known that if
\operatorname{cvd}(G)\leq k
, then every connected
component of
G
has diameter at most
3k+1
.
In particular, if
s>3k+1
, the instance is trivially a yes-instance,
since no edge deletions are required. Hence we may assume
s\leq 3k+1
,
so that
s
is bounded in terms of
k
.

Graph Transformation via Twin Consolidation.

We now describe how to transform the input instance
(G,s,k)

into an equivalent
weighted
instance
(G^{\prime},s,k)
on which the
dynamic programming algorithm of
[
17
]
can be
applied.
For every clique
C
in
G-X
,
partition the vertices of
C
into at most
2^{k}
twin classes
according to their neighborhoods in
X
.
Since the vertices within each class are true twins, deleting any subset
of them affects only the multiplicity of edges to vertices outside the
class.

By Lemma
19
, there exists an optimal edge-deletion set

F\subseteq E(G)
such that in
G-F
all but at most one connected component
contain at most one vertex from each true twin class.
Therefore, for each true twin class
T
, the presence of more than
k+1

vertices is redundant, as deleting or keeping additional equivalent vertices
does not change the existence or size of an optimal solution.
Hence, if
|T|>k+1
, we remove all but
k
vertices of
T
and
replace the remaining
|T|-k
surplus vertices by a single
representative vertex.
Each edge incident to this representative vertex is assigned a weight equal
to the number of vertices it replaces, ensuring that the total deletion cost
correctly reflects the original multiplicity of identical edges.
This operation preserves all adjacencies and pairwise distances between
distinct twin classes and produces an equivalent instance for computing an
optimal
s
-club cluster edge-deletion set.

For each twin class
T\subseteq C
:

•

If
|T|\leq k+1
, we keep all vertices of
T
unchanged.

•

If
|T|=k+\delta
for some
\delta>0
,
we remove the extra
\delta
vertices from
T

and replace them by a single representative vertex
t^{\prime}
.
For every vertex
v\in N_{G}(T)
,
we introduce an edge
\{t^{\prime},v\}
of
weight

w(\{t^{\prime},v\})=\delta
,
while all other edge weights are set to 1.
The neighborhood of
t^{\prime}
is exactly
N_{G}(T)
.

We repeat this for each clique
C
of
G-X
.
The resulting graph
G^{\prime}
has at most
2^{k}(k+1)
vertices in each
clique of
G^{\prime}-X
, and each edge of
G^{\prime}
has a positive integer weight.
It is easy to see that
G^{\prime}
can be decomposed along a path
decomposition of width at most
k+(k+1)2^{k}
,
obtained by taking
X
together with one modified clique per bag.

Equivalence of Instances..

We claim that
(G,s,k)
is a yes-instance if and only if

(G^{\prime},s,k)
is a yes-instance.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose
F\subseteq E(G)
is an optimal edge deletion
set of size at most
k
such that
G-F
is an
s
-club cluster graph.
For each deleted edge
\{u,v\}
in
G
, we delete the corresponding
edge in
G^{\prime}
.
Since edges connecting consolidated vertices in
G^{\prime}
represent multiple
identical edges in
G
,
the total deletion cost in
G^{\prime}
equals the number of such edges in
G
,
and thus
\sum_{e\in F}w(e)\leq k
.
Moreover, since consolidation preserves adjacency relations and
distances among distinct twin classes, the resulting
G^{\prime}-F^{\prime}
remains
an
s
-club cluster graph.

(
\Leftarrow
) Conversely, let
F^{\prime}
be an optimal solution in
G^{\prime}

of total cost at most
k
.
For each weighted edge
e=\{u,v\}
with
w(e)=\delta
that is deleted,
we delete
\delta
parallel edges (i.e., edges incident to the
corresponding
\delta
twins) in
G
.
By expanding all consolidated vertices, we recover an edge deletion set

F\subseteq E(G)
of size
|F|\leq k

such that
G-F
is an
s
-club cluster graph.
Therefore, the two instances are equivalent.

Applying the Weighted Dynamic Program..

We now argue that the dynamic programming algorithm of
[
17
]

can be applied directly to
G^{\prime}
.
Recall that, for every bag
X_{i}
of the nice tree decomposition, the algorithm stores a set of
solutions
, each represented by a tuple

S^{l}_{i}=\langle\partial\mathcal{C}^{l}_{i},D^{l}_{i},H^{l}_{i},Q^{l}_{i},\mu^{l}_{i}\rangle.

Here:

•

\partial\mathcal{C}^{l}_{i}
is the family of boundaries of the potential
s
-clubs intersecting
X_{i}
;

•

D^{l}_{i}
is the set of distance tables
D(\partial C)
that record, for each pair
a,b\in\partial C
, the truncated distance
d_{G[C]}(a,b)
capped at
s
;

•

H^{l}_{i}
stores, for each equivalence class of vertices in
\mathrm{int}(C)
, their distances to vertices in
\partial C
;

•

Q^{l}_{i}
is the set of pending
requests

R_{wz}
over
\partial C
, each encoding potential paths of length at most
s-2
that could make a pair of interior vertices
w,z
satisfy the distance constraint;

•

finally,
\mu^{l}_{i}
is the
edge-counter
representing the number of edges deleted in the partial solution corresponding to the subgraph
G_{i}
.

In the weighted setting, these components remain unchanged,
except that
\mu^{l}_{i}
is reinterpreted as the
total edge weight

of deleted edges, namely,

\mu^{l}_{i}=\sum_{e\in E_{i}\setminus P^{l}_{i}(E_{i})}w(e).

Whenever a deleted edge
e
appears in a transition,
the algorithm updates

\mu^{l}_{i}\leftarrow\mu^{l}_{i}+w(e)

instead of incrementing by one.
Since the recurrence relations and feasibility conditions depend only on
the structural components

(\partial\mathcal{C}^{l}_{i},D^{l}_{i},H^{l}_{i},Q^{l}_{i})
,
the correctness proof of Lemma 2 in
[
17
]

remains valid verbatim.

Therefore, the dynamic program computes an optimal weighted solution on
G^{\prime}
,
which by the above equivalence corresponds to an optimal unweighted solution
on
G
.

We now analyze the running time more precisely.
The dynamic programming algorithm of
[
17
]
runs in

\mathcal{O}\!\bigl(2^{\,2^{\,\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{tw}(G^{\prime})^{2}\log s)}}\cdot n^{2}\bigr)

time, where
\mathrm{tw}(G^{\prime})
is the treewidth of the input graph
and
s
is the cluster diameter bound.
For our transformed graph
G^{\prime}
, we have

\mathrm{tw}(G^{\prime})\leq k+(k+1)2^{k}\quad\text{and}\quad s\leq 3k+1
.
Since
k+(k+1)2^{k}\leq(k+2)2^{k}
,
we can further simplify the exponent to obtain

(k+(k+1)2^{k})^{2}\log(3k+1)=\mathcal{O}\!\bigl(4^{k}\,k^{2}\log k\bigr)
,
and hence the total running time is

T(k,n)=\mathcal{O}\!\left(2^{\,2^{\,\mathcal{O}\!\left(4^{k}\,k^{2}\log k\right)}}\cdot n^{2}\right)
.

Thus,
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is fixed-parameter tractable when
parameterized by the cluster vertex deletion number
k
,
with the above double-exponential dependence in
k
and polynomial dependence in
n
.
∎

5.3
Algorithm under Neighbourhood Diversity Parameterization

In this section, we show that
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by the neighbourhood diversity of the input graph.
For the special case
s=1
, corresponding to
Cluster Edge Deletion
, the problem is known to admit an algorithm running in
2^{2^{\mathcal{O}(\nd)}}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time
[
10
]
.
We therefore focus on the case
s\geq 2
.
Our approach is fundamentally different: rather than using the linear-integer programming formulation employed for the
s=1
case, we rely on the structural characterization provided by the twin-consolidation lemma to bound the number of relevant component partitions.
Nevertheless, the resulting algorithm achieves the same asymptotic dependence on the parameter as in the
s=1
case, up to constant factors.

Theorem 21
.

Let
G
be a graph with neighborhood diversity
\nd(G)=k
and let
s\geq 2
.
Then
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
can be solved in

2^{\,k(2^{k}+1)}\cdot\mathcal{O}(nm+n^{2})

time. In particular, the problem is fixed-parameter tractable when
parameterized by
k=\nd(G)
.

Proof.

We exploit the structure induced by the neighborhood-diversity partition of
G
.
The proof proceeds by bounding the number of distinct connected-component
“types” that can appear in an optimal solution and by enumerating all feasible
partitions consistent with these types.

Neighborhood-diversity structure.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}\}
be a neighborhood-diversity partition of
V(G)
:
for each
i
, the vertices in
T_{i}
are pairwise twins (they share the same open
neighborhood outside
T_{i}
), and for each
i\neq j
, the bipartite graph
between
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
is either complete or empty.
Each
T_{i}
is therefore either a clique (true-twin class) or an independent set
(false-twin class).
Let the
type graph

H
be the graph on vertex set
[k]=\{1,\dots,k\}

where
\{i,j\}\in E(H)
if and only if
G
is complete between
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
.

We now reason about how the structure of
H
constrains the components that may
appear in an optimal clustering after edge deletions.

Footprints and equivalence of components.

Given an edge-deletion set
F\subseteq E(G)
producing the clustering
G-F
,
each connected component
C
of
G-F
determines its
footprint

\mathrm{foot}(C):=\{\,i\in[k]\;|\;T_{i}\cap V(C)\neq\emptyset\,\}
.
Two components
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are called
equivalent
if

\mathrm{foot}(C_{1})=\mathrm{foot}(C_{2})
.

We next show that whenever two components share the same footprint, it is safe
to merge them without violating the
s
-club property.

Merging equivalent components.

Assume
C_{1},C_{2}
are equivalent and that for every
i\in\mathrm{foot}(C_{1})

either
T_{i}
is a clique or
\mathrm{foot}(C_{1})
contains a neighbor of
i
in
H
.
Let
F^{\prime}:=F\setminus E_{G}(C_{1},C_{2})
, that is, we restore all edges between

V(C_{1})
and
V(C_{2})
while keeping all other deletions unchanged.
Then the two components merge into a single component

C:=G-(F^{\prime})
induced on
V(C_{1})\cup V(C_{2})
.

We claim that
\diam(C)\leq s
provided
\diam(C_{1})\leq s
and
\diam(C_{2})\leq s
.
Let
u\in T_{i_{u}}\cap V(C)
and
v\in T_{i_{v}}\cap V(C)
, and let

S:=\mathrm{foot}(C_{1})=\mathrm{foot}(C_{2})
.
Consider in
H[S]
a shortest
i_{u}
–
i_{v}
path

i_{u}=\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\ell}=i_{v}

(with
\ell=0
if
i_{u}=i_{v}
).
Since
G
is complete between
T_{\sigma_{t}}
and
T_{\sigma_{t+1}}
for every
t
,
any choice of vertices
x_{t}\in T_{\sigma_{t}}
yields a valid path

x_{0}x_{1}\cdots x_{\ell}
in
G
.
Both
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
contain at least one vertex from each
T_{\sigma_{t}}
,
so such a path lies entirely within
V(C)
.
Hence
\dist_{C}(u,v)\leq\ell=\dist_{H[S]}(i_{u},i_{v})
.
Finally, if
i_{u}=i_{v}
and
T_{i_{u}}
is a false-twin class, the assumption on

S
ensures that
i_{u}
has a neighbor in
H[S]
, yielding

\dist_{C}(u,v)\leq 2
.
In all cases,
\dist_{C}(u,v)\leq\diam(H[S])\leq s
, and thus merging preserves
the
s
-club property.

Having established that equivalent components can be merged safely,
we now use this to limit the number of distinct components that may appear.

Bounding the number of components.

The merging argument implies that in an optimal solution we may assume there
is at most one component per footprint
S\subseteq[k]
,
except possibly for
degenerate
footprints
S=\{i\}

where
T_{i}
is a false-twin class with no neighbor in
H
.
Such components are isolated singletons, trivially forming
s
-clubs for
s\geq 2
.
Consequently, the number of distinct footprints realized by components of

G-F
is at most
2^{k}
, and for each non-degenerate footprint there is at most
one corresponding component.

To further restrict the structure of these components, we next apply the
twin-consolidation lemma.

Twin consolidation within footprints.

By Lemma
19
, we may further assume that in
G-F

all but at most one component contain at most one vertex of each class
T_{i}
;
a single designated component (if any) may contain the remaining vertices of

T_{i}
without violating the
s
-club property.

With this structural restriction in place, we can now enumerate all admissible
ways of distributing the twin classes among the possible component footprints.

Enumerating admissible partitions.

Let
C\leq 2^{k}
denote the number of non-degenerate footprints to be realized.
For a fixed class
T_{i}
, we distribute its vertices among these
C
components
as follows:
(i) choose the designated component (or none), and
(ii) for each remaining component, decide whether it receives a single
representative of
T_{i}
or not.
Thus the number of admissible distributions per class is at most

C\cdot 2^{C}
, and the total number of global distributions (over
k
classes)
is bounded by

\bigl(C\cdot 2^{C}\bigr)^{k}\;\leq\;\bigl(2^{k}\cdot 2^{2^{k}}\bigr)^{k}\;=\;2^{\,k(2^{k}+1)}
.

We now explain how each enumerated partition corresponds to a concrete
edge-deletion solution.

From a partition to a solution and verification.

For each guessed distribution, we instantiate the components by choosing
arbitrary representatives in each
T_{i}
(since all are twins),
delete all edges whose endpoints lie in different components,
and verify that every component has diameter at most
s
.
This verification uses BFS from each vertex within its component and takes

\mathcal{O}(nm)
time overall, plus
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
bookkeeping for
component distances, for a total of
\mathcal{O}(nm+n^{2})
per trial.

For the decision version with deletion budget
\kappa
, we count crossing edges
and accept iff this number is at most
\kappa
.
For the optimization version, we retain the partition yielding the smallest
number of crossing edges among all valid configurations.

Finally, we analyze the runtime and summarize the correctness argument.

Running time and correctness.

The enumeration contributes a factor of
2^{\,k(2^{k}+k)}
, and each partition
is verified in
\mathcal{O}(nm+n^{2})
time.
Correctness follows from:
(i) the merging argument, ensuring at most one component per non-degenerate footprint;
(ii) the twin-consolidation lemma, bounding how many copies of a class appear per component;
and (iii) the fact that the minimum deletion set realizing a fixed partition is
precisely the set of crossing edges.
Therefore, the algorithm runs in

2^{\,k(2^{k}+1)}\cdot\mathcal{O}(nm+n^{2})
time and establishes fixed-parameter
tractability for the parameter
\nd(G)=k
.
∎

6
No Polynomial Kernelization

In this section, we investigate the kernelization complexity of

s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
.
While the problem admits several fixed-parameter algorithms under structural parameters
(such as vertex cover number, cluster vertex deletion number, and treedepth),
it is natural to ask whether these algorithms can be accompanied by efficient
kernelization procedures.
We show that this is unlikely even for restricted cases of the problem.

Specifically, we establish that

s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
does not admit a polynomial kernel
when parameterized by the vertex cover number of the input graph,
even when
s=2
.
Our proof proceeds via a polynomial parameter transformation
from the classical
Red–Blue Dominating Set
problem.

Definition 22

(
Red–Blue Dominating Set)
.

Given a bipartite graph
G=(R\cup B,E)
with red vertices
R

7
Parameterized by
k

The current algorithmic landscape shows that
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion

is fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by the combined parameter
s+k
based on simple branching technique.
However, the complexity of the problem under the single parameter
k

(the solution size) remains unresolved.
This gap naturally motivates the exploration of relaxed formulations that
permit limited deviation from the strict diameter constraint.
In particular, we ask whether one can obtain an FPT algorithm parameterized by
k

if a small multiplicative violation of the diameter bound is allowed.
We answer this affirmatively by presenting an FPT bicriteria approximation scheme:
when the input graph excludes long induced cycles and we permit a

(1+\epsilon)
-approximation of the diameter, the problem becomes fixed-parameter tractable with respect to
k
.

A natural question is whether one could obtain an FPT approximation algorithm
parameterized only by the solution size
k
while still enforcing the exact
diameter bound
s
.
However, such an algorithm is unlikely to exist.
The key obstacle is that
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is not

monotone
under edge deletions:
a feasible solution
F\subseteq E(G)
that yields an
s
-club clustering
may become infeasible if additional edges are deleted,
since these deletions can increase the diameter of some components beyond
s
.
This means that the family of feasible solutions is not closed under inclusion,
and hence the problem lacks the hereditary property
that most known FPT-approximation techniques rely on.
Without such structure, an FPT approximation parameterized solely by
k
and preserving the exact diameter bound
s
seems highly unlikely.

.

Let

be a graph with no induced cycle of length at least

.

.

Case 1:
s\leq\tfrac{k(t-3)}{\epsilon}
.

In this case, we invoke the known FPT algorithm for
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion

parameterized by
s+k

[
17
]
.
This algorithm runs in time
(s+1)^{k}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
and solves the problem exactly.
If
I
is a YES-instance, it returns an optimal deletion set
F
of size at most
k
.
If no such set exists, it correctly outputs “NO”.
Hence, in this regime, the algorithm provides an exact decision and solution.

Case 2:
s>\tfrac{k(t-3)}{\epsilon}
.

Here, we construct a reduction to the
Edge Multicut
problem.
Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, a set of terminal pairs

\mathcal{T}=\{(s_{1},t_{1}),\ldots,(s_{r},t_{r})\}\subseteq V\times V
,
and an integer
k
,
Edge Multicut
asks whether there exists

F\subseteq E
with
|F|\leq k
such that each terminal pair

(s_{i},t_{i})\in\mathcal{T}
lies in distinct connected components of
G-F
.

From our instance
I=(G,s,k)
, we define

\mathcal{T}:=\{\,(u,v)\in V(G)\times V(G)\;:\;\dist_{G}(u,v)>s\,\}.

That is, every vertex pair originally at distance more than
s
becomes a terminal pair.
Observe that if
F
is a feasible deletion set for
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
,
then
(u,v)
must be separated in
G-F
for every
(u,v)\in\mathcal{T}
, since edge deletions can only increase distances.
Thus, every feasible solution for
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
of size at most
k

is also a feasible solution for
Edge Multicut
on
(G,\mathcal{T},k)
.

We now apply the FPT algorithm for
Edge Multicut

[
3
]
,
which runs in time
k^{\mathcal{O}(k^{3})}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
.
If this multicut instance is infeasible, then so is the original instance (since
any
s
-club clustering would induce a feasible multicut).
Hence, in this case, the algorithm safely outputs “NO.”
If the multicut instance is feasible, we obtain a set
F
with
|F|\leq k

that separates all pairs in
\mathcal{T}
.

Bounding the diameter.

By construction,
F
separates all pairs that were at distance greater than
s
in
G
.
Therefore, for any
u,v
that remain in the same component of
G-F
, we have

\dist_{G}(u,v)\leq s
.
In graphs that exclude induced cycles of length at least
t
,
Lemma
26
implies that deleting up to
k
edges
can increase the distance between any two vertices by at most
k(t-3)
.
Hence, in every component of
G-F
, we have

\dist_{G-F}(u,v)\leq s+k(t-3)
.
Since
s>\tfrac{k(t-3)}{\epsilon}
, we obtain

\frac{s+k(t-3)}{s}\leq 1+\frac{k(t-3)}{s}\leq 1+\epsilon,

which shows that each component of
G-F
has diameter at most
(1+\epsilon)\cdot s
.

Conclusion.

If the multicut solver declares infeasibility, the input instance
I
is guaranteed to be a NO-instance for the exact
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
problem.
Otherwise, we obtain a deletion set
F
with
|F|\leq k
such that every component of
G-F

has diameter at most
(1+\epsilon)\cdot s
.
Thus, the algorithm either outputs “NO” (soundly) or returns a bicriteria

(k,(1+\epsilon)s)
-feasible solution.
The total running time is

\max\!\left\{\Bigl(\tfrac{k(t-3)}{\epsilon}+1\Bigr)^{k}\!\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)},\;k^{\mathcal{O}(k^{3})}\!\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}\right\}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

8
FPT on Interval Graphs

A central theme in our investigation is to understand
when the diameter bound
s
can be dropped as a parameter
without losing fixed-parameter tractability.
While on general graphs an FPT algorithm parameterized solely by the solution size
k
appears out of reach, our bicriteria approximation result offers a promising direction:
for graphs excluding induced cycles of length greater than a fixed constant
t
,

s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
admits an
FPT bicriteria approximation scheme
parameterized by
k
.
In particular, when
t=3
—that is, when the input graph is
chordal
—the result immediately yields an FPT bicriteria approximation algorithm for the chordal case.

This naturally motivates the question:
can we move beyond approximation and obtain an
exact
FPT algorithm parameterized only by
k
for a meaningful graph class where
s
can be unbounded?
Chordal graphs represent a natural candidate due to their tree decompositions in which every bag induces a clique, a property that frequently enables dynamic-programming formulations.
As a step toward this goal, we focus on a special subclass of chordal graphs that admit a
path decomposition
where each bag forms a clique.
It is well known that this subclass coincides exactly with the class of
interval graphs
.
We show that on interval graphs,
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is indeed
fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized solely by
k
, even when
s
is unbounded.

For
s=1
, the problem is known to be solvable in polynomial time on interval graphs,
but for
s\geq 2
its classical complexity remains open.
Our result thus provides the first FPT algorithm under the single parameter
k
for this class,
highlighting that the parameter
s
can be safely dropped in the presence of strong structural restrictions such as clique-based path decompositions.
More broadly, our findings suggest a potential avenue for generalization from
path decompositions
to
tree decompositions
whose bags induce cliques—that is, from interval graphs to chordal graphs—where the problem is known to be NP-hard even for
s=1
.
Understanding whether such an extension is possible remains an intriguing open direction for future work.

8.1
Preprocessing the Path Decomposition

Let
G=(V,E)
be an interval graph.
It is well known that every interval graph admits a
clique path decomposition

\mathcal{P}=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{\ell})
where each bag
B_{i}
is a maximal clique of
G
,
and for every vertex
v\in V
, the set of bags containing
v
forms a contiguous subsequence of
\mathcal{P}
.
We can assume without loss of generality that the decomposition satisfies the following structural property.

Lemma 28
.

Given a clique path decomposition
\mathcal{P}=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{\ell})
of an interval graph
G
,
one can in linear time construct an equivalent path decomposition

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=(B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}})
such that:

[(i)]

1.

the sequence alternates between
introduce
and
forget
bags,

2.

each vertex of
G
is introduced and forgotten exactly once, and

3.

last bag in the sequence is empty.

Proof.

Traverse the original sequence
(B_{1},\ldots,B_{\ell})
from left to right.
If two consecutive bags
B_{i},B_{i+1}
are both introduce bags (that is,
B_{i+1}\supseteq B_{i}
),
replace them by a single introduce bag
B_{i}\cup B_{i+1}
.
Likewise, if two consecutive bags are both forget bags (
B_{i+1}\subseteq B_{i}
),
replace them by a single forget bag
B_{i+1}
.
Repeat until introduce and forget bags alternate.
Each vertex is still introduced in the first bag containing it and forgotten in the last bag containing it,
so the resulting decomposition is equivalent and alternates as required. Also we can forget all the vertices in the last bag and add one final empty bag.
∎

Henceforth we work with such an alternating path decomposition,
where
B_{1}
is an introduce bag and the types of bags alternate between introduce and forget.

8.2
Dynamic Programming Records: Well-Posedness

We now formalize the DP state that we maintain at each bag of the alternating
decomposition and prove that the stored information is sufficient and consistent.

Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=(B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}})
be the alternating path
decomposition guaranteed by Lemma
28
. For
i\in[\ell^{\prime}]
define

V_{i}:=\bigcup_{j\leq i}B^{\prime}_{j}
and let
G_{i}:=G[V_{i}]
. A DP state at position
i

encodes a set of partial components of some deletion
F_{i}\subseteq E(G_{i})
,
together with boundary/diameter data needed to extend across future bags.
Because each
B^{\prime}_{i}
induces a clique, for any partial component the distances
from the already
forgotten
part (the
core
) to vertices in the

current boundary
differ by at most one; we use this to store compressed
diameter data.

Definition 29

(DP record at bag
B^{\prime}_{i}
)
.

A record (state) at position
i
is a tuple

\Sigma_{i}=(\Pi_{i},\mathbf{D}_{i},\mathbf{p}_{i},b_{i}),

with the following components:

(Partition).

\Pi_{i}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{\lambda}\}
is a partition of
B^{\prime}_{i}
into nonempty
parts. Each
S_{r}
represents the intersection of
B^{\prime}_{i}
with a distinct
connected component
K_{r}
of the partial solution
G_{i}-F_{i}
(for some

F_{i}\subseteq E(G_{i})
implicit in
\Sigma_{i}
). All edges of
E(G[B^{\prime}_{i}])
with
endpoints in different parts are counted as deleted at step
i
.

(Diameter profiles).

For each
r\in[\lambda]
, let
K_{r}
be the partial component represented by

S_{r}
, and define

C_{r}:=V(K_{r})\setminus B^{\prime}_{i}
and
B_{r}:=K_{r}\cap B^{\prime}_{i}=S_{r}
.
We store

\mathbf{D}_{i}(r)=\bigl(d_{i}(r),\,j_{i}(r),\,\sigma_{i}(r),\,\Delta_{i}(r)\bigr),

where:

•

\sigma_{i}(r)=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p_{r}})
lists the vertices of
B_{r}

in nondecreasing order of their
introduction index
in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
;
here
p_{r}:=|B_{r}|
.

•

d_{i}(r):=\min\{\dist_{G_{i}-F_{i}}(x,y)\mid x\in C_{r},\ y\in B_{r}\}
is the
current
minimum
core–to–boundary distance in
K_{r}

(with the convention
d_{i}(r)=0
if
C_{r}=\emptyset
).

•

j_{i}(r)\in\{0,1,\dots,p_{r}\}
is the unique index such that

\dist_{G_{i}-F_{i}}(C_{r},v_{t})=\begin{cases}d_{i}(r),&\text{for }t\leq j_{i}(r),\\
d_{i}(r)+1,&\text{for }t>j_{i}(r).\end{cases}

This is well-defined because
G[B^{\prime}_{i}]
is a clique, implying that
the distances from
C_{r}
to vertices of
B_{r}
differ by at most one.
Ordering by introduction ensures a contiguous block of vertices at
distance
d_{i}(r)
followed by those at distance
d_{i}(r){+}1
.

•

\Delta_{i}(r):=\diam\!\bigl((G_{i}-F_{i})\bigl[K_{r}\cap V_{i}\bigr]\bigr)

denotes the
current diameter
of the portion of
K_{r}
that has
appeared up to step
i
, restricted to the subgraph induced by all
vertices of
K_{r}
that have been introduced so far.
The value
\Delta_{i}(r)
is
nondecreasing
with
i
since future
operations can only add vertices or edges and never shorten already
realized distances among vertices of
K_{r}
.

In particular, when we reach the first bag
B^{\prime}_{t}
such that

B_{r}=\emptyset
(i.e., when the last vertex of
K_{r}
is forgotten),

\Delta_{t}(r)
equals the true diameter of the completed component
K_{r}
:

\Delta_{t}(r)=\diam\!\bigl((G_{t}-F_{t})[K_{r}]\bigr).

If
\Delta_{t}(r)>s
, the corresponding state is infeasible and is discarded.

(Persistence counters).

\mathbf{p}_{i}=(p_{i}(1),\ldots,p_{i}(\lambda))
with
p_{i}(r)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}

records, for each
K_{r}
, how many consecutive bags of the decomposition
(including
B^{\prime}_{i}
) have contained a nonempty intersection with
K_{r}
.
Formally, if
t_{r}
is the index of the introduce bag where
K_{r}
first
appeared, then
p_{i}(r)=i-t_{r}+1
. We use these counters only to detect

simultaneous long persistence
: we declare
\Sigma_{i}

invalid
if there exist distinct
r\neq r^{\prime}
with

p_{i}(r)\ \geq\ 2k+2\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_{i}(r^{\prime})\ \geq\ 2k+2,

since sustaining two (or more) components side by side for
2k{+}2
bags in
clique-bags would force deletion of at least
k{+}1
crossing edges.

(Budget).

b_{i}\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
is the total number of deleted
crossing
edges
among all vertices that have appeared in
B^{\prime}_{1},\dots,B^{\prime}_{i}
, i.e., edges of

E(G)
whose endpoints lie in different parts of
\Pi_{j}
at the moment the
later endpoint is first present in
B^{\prime}_{j}
(
j\leq i
). A state is infeasible if

b_{i}>k
.

Proposition 30

(Well-definedness and sufficiency of DP records)
.

Let
\Sigma_{i}
be a valid DP state at bag
B^{\prime}_{i}
, and let
K_{r}
denote the
partial component represented by
S_{r}\in\Pi_{i}
, with core
C_{r}
and boundary

B_{r}=S_{r}
. Then the quantities

(d_{i}(r),\,j_{i}(r),\,\sigma_{i}(r),\,\Delta_{i}(r),\,p_{i}(r))

are well-defined and jointly sufficient to capture both the structural and
distance properties of
K_{r}
at bag
B^{\prime}_{i}
. In particular:

[(a)]

1.

(Distance gap
\leq 1
)

For every
x\in C_{r}
and all
u,v\in B_{r}
,

\bigl|\dist_{G_{i}-F_{i}}(x,u)-\dist_{G_{i}-F_{i}}(x,v)\bigr|\leq 1.

Hence, the distances from the core to boundary vertices differ by at
most one.

2.

(Correctness of
(d_{i}(r),j_{i}(r),\sigma_{i}(r))
)

Let
\sigma_{i}(r)=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p_{r}})
list the vertices of
B_{r}
in
nondecreasing order of their introduction indices in the alternating
path decomposition (breaking ties arbitrarily among vertices introduced
in the same bag). Define

d_{i}(r):=\min_{y\in B_{r}}\dist_{G_{i}-F_{i}}(C_{r},y).

By (a), every value
\dist_{G_{i}-F_{i}}(C_{r},y)
lies in

\{d_{i}(r),\,d_{i}(r){+}1\}
. Because each bag
B^{\prime}_{i}
induces a clique,
introducing a (possibly multi-vertex) batch into the boundary can
increase the distance to the core for those new vertices by at most 1,
and can never decrease the distance of vertices introduced earlier.
Consequently, the sequence

\bigl(\dist_{G_{i}-F_{i}}(C_{r},v_{t})\bigr)_{t=1}^{p_{r}}
is nondecreasing
and takes only these two consecutive values.
Therefore, there exists a unique index
j_{i}(r)\in[0,p_{r}]
such that

\dist_{G_{i}-F_{i}}(C_{r},v_{t})=\begin{cases}d_{i}(r),&t\leq j_{i}(r),\\[2.0pt]
d_{i}(r)+1,&t>j_{i}(r),\end{cases}

implying that
\mathbf{D}_{i}(r)
uniquely determines the multiset

\{\dist_{G_{i}-F_{i}}(C_{r},v)\mid v\in B_{r}\}
.

3.

(Recovering the component diameter).

At step
i
,
\Delta_{i}(r)
records the exact diameter of the subgraph
of
K_{r}
induced by all vertices introduced so far:

\operatorname{Diam}_{i}(K_{r})=\Delta_{i}(r)=\diam\!\bigl((G_{i}-F_{i})[K_{r}\cap V_{i}]\bigr).

Since
V_{i}
increases monotonically and subsequent operations do not
shorten already realized distances,
\Delta_{i}(r)
is nondecreasing in
i
.
While
B_{r}
remains nonempty, the algorithm continuously monitors

\Delta_{i}(r)
; if at any step
\Delta_{i}(r)>s
, the corresponding state

\Sigma_{i}
is immediately discarded as infeasible.

When the final vertex of
K_{r}
is forgotten—so that
B_{r}=\emptyset
—we
have
K_{r}\subseteq V_{i}
, yielding

\operatorname{Diam}_{i}(K_{r})=\diam\!\bigl((G_{i}-F_{i})[K_{r}]\bigr)=\Delta_{i}(r),

which represents the true final diameter of the completed component.

4.

(Bound on simultaneous persistence).

Suppose two distinct partial components
K_{r}
and
K_{r^{\prime}}
persist
simultaneously in every bag of an interval

[B^{\prime}_{t},B^{\prime}_{t+2k+1}]
, each having nonempty intersection with these

2k{+}2
consecutive clique-bags.
Then any feasible deletion set
F
consistent with this configuration
must contain at least
k{+}1
edges, contradicting the global budget.
Hence, if
p_{i}(r)\geq 2k{+}2
and
p_{i}(r^{\prime})\geq 2k{+}2
for two distinct
indices
r\neq r^{\prime}
, the state
\Sigma_{i}
is invalid and must be
discarded.

Proof.

Let
H:=G_{i}-F_{i}
. Because
B^{\prime}_{i}
induces a clique in
G
and only cross-part
edges are deleted,
H[B_{r}]
remains a clique.

(a) Fix
x\in C_{r}
and
u,v\in B_{r}
.
If
v
lies on a shortest
x
–
u
path, then

\dist_{H}(x,v)\leq\dist_{H}(x,u)
, and since
u,v
are adjacent in
H
,

\dist_{H}(x,u)\leq\dist_{H}(x,v)+1
.
Otherwise, concatenating a shortest
x
–
v
path with edge
vu

yields an
x
–
u
walk of length
\dist_{H}(x,v)+1
, implying

\dist_{H}(x,u)\leq\dist_{H}(x,v)+1
.
By symmetry, the absolute difference is at most 1.

(b) Let
d:=\min_{y\in B_{r}}\dist_{H}(C_{r},y)
.
By part (a), the distances from
C_{r}
to vertices of
B_{r}
all lie in

\{d,d{+}1\}
.
Ordering
B_{r}
by introduction index (breaking ties within the same bag),
and using that each bag
B^{\prime}_{i}
is a clique, it follows that the distances to
the core form a nondecreasing sequence that changes value at most once.
Thus, there exists a unique index
j\in[0,p_{r}]
such that
the first
j
boundary vertices have distance
d
to
C_{r}

and the remaining have distance
d{+}1
, establishing the correctness of

(d_{i}(r),j_{i}(r),\sigma_{i}(r))
.

(c) Since
H[B_{r}]
is a clique, any two boundary vertices are at distance 1.
Every vertex of
C_{r}
connects to some boundary vertex via paths entirely
contained within
H[K_{r}\cap V_{i}]
.
By definition,
\Delta_{i}(r)=\diam(H[K_{r}\cap V_{i}])

therefore equals the current diameter of the portion of
K_{r}
that has been
introduced so far.
Because
V_{i}
expands monotonically and later steps cannot reduce any existing
distance,
\Delta_{i}(r)
is nondecreasing in
i
.
Whenever
B_{r}\neq\emptyset
, the algorithm verifies that

\Delta_{i}(r)\leq s
; otherwise, the state
\Sigma_{i}
is discarded.
Finally, when the last vertex of
K_{r}
is forgotten (
B_{r}=\emptyset
), we have

K_{r}\subseteq V_{i}
, and thus
\Delta_{i}(r)=\diam(H[K_{r}])
, the final diameter of
the completed component.

(d) For the persistence condition, suppose that two components
K_{r}
and

K_{r^{\prime}}
coexist in each of the
2k{+}2
consecutive clique-bags

B^{\prime}_{t},\dots,B^{\prime}_{t+2k+1}
.
Since each such bag is a clique, every vertex of
K_{r}
is adjacent in
G
to
every vertex of
K_{r^{\prime}}
whenever both appear in the same bag.
To keep these components disconnected throughout this interval, all such
inter-part edges must be deleted.
Each introduce step among the
2k{+}2
bags introduces at least one new vertex,
creating at least one new cross-edge that must also be deleted.
Hence, a minimum of
k{+}1
distinct edges must be removed in total,
exceeding the allowed budget
k
.
Therefore, any state where two components persist simultaneously for more than

2k{+}1
bags cannot correspond to a valid solution and is declared invalid.
∎

8.3
DP on the extended path decomposition.

Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=(B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{m})
be the alternating path decomposition
from Lemma
28
. For each
i\in[m]
, write

V_{i}:=\bigcup_{j\leq i}B^{\prime}_{j}
and
G_{i}:=G[V_{i}]
. The DP maintains a table

\mathcal{T}_{i}
of feasible records
\Sigma_{i}=(\Pi_{i},\mathbf{D}_{i},\mathbf{p}_{i},b_{i})
,
as specified in Definition
29
. All feasibility tests on
diameter profiles use Proposition
30
.

Initialization (first bag).

Let
B^{\prime}_{1}
be the first bag. Since
G[B^{\prime}_{1}]
is a clique, any partition

\Pi_{1}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{\lambda}\}
of
B^{\prime}_{1}
into nonempty parts deletes exactly
the edges whose endpoints lie in different parts. The number of deleted
(crossing) edges is

b_{1}(\Pi_{1})\;=\;\bigl|\{\,uv\in E(G[B^{\prime}_{1}])\mid u,v\ \text{in different parts of }\Pi_{1}\,\}\bigr|.

A partition
\Pi_{1}
is
feasible at initialization
iff
b_{1}(\Pi_{1})\leq k

and (for every part) the initial diameter bound is respected.

Case 1:
|B^{\prime}_{1}|>k{+}1
.

Every nontrivial split has
b_{1}(\Pi_{1})\geq k{+}1
, hence is infeasible. The only
admissible state is the single-part partition:

\Pi_{1}^{\bullet}\;=\;\{\,B^{\prime}_{1}\,\},\qquad b_{1}=0.

For this unique state, define the diameter record for the sole part
S_{1}=B^{\prime}_{1}
:

\mathbf{D}_{1}(1)=(d_{1}(1),\,j_{1}(1),\,\sigma_{1}(1),\,\Delta_{1}(1)),\quad d_{1}(1)=0,\ j_{1}(1)=|B^{\prime}_{1}|,

where
\sigma_{1}(1)
is any ordering of
B^{\prime}_{1}
by introduction index.
Since no edges inside a part are deleted,
G[B^{\prime}_{1}]
remains a clique; thus

\Delta_{1}(1)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }|B^{\prime}_{1}|\geq 2,\\
0,&\text{if }|B^{\prime}_{1}|=1.\end{cases}

We also set the persistence vector
\mathbf{p}_{1}=(p_{1}(1))
with
p_{1}(1)=1
.
This state is kept only if
\Delta_{1}(1)\leq s
.
Hence the initialization table is the singleton

\mathcal{T}_{1}\;=\;\Bigl\{\,\Sigma_{1}^{\bullet}:=\bigl(\Pi_{1}^{\bullet},\ \mathbf{D}_{1},\ (p_{1}(1)=1),\ b_{1}=0\bigr)\,\Bigr\}.

Case 2:
|B^{\prime}_{1}|\leq k{+}1
.

Here multiple parts are allowed provided the budget constraint holds.
Let

\mathfrak{P}_{1}\;:=\;\Bigl\{\,\Pi_{1}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{\lambda}\}\text{ a partition of }B^{\prime}_{1}\ \Big|\ b_{1}(\Pi_{1})\leq k\Bigr\}.

For each
\Pi_{1}\in\mathfrak{P}_{1}
we create one state

\Sigma_{1}^{\Pi_{1}}\;=\;\bigl(\Pi_{1},\ \mathbf{D}_{1}^{\Pi_{1}},\ \mathbf{p}_{1}^{\Pi_{1}},\ b_{1}(\Pi_{1})\bigr),

where, for every part
S_{r}\in\Pi_{1}
,

\mathbf{D}_{1}^{\Pi_{1}}(r)=(d_{1}(r),\,j_{1}(r),\,\sigma_{1}(r),\,\Delta_{1}(r)),\qquad d_{1}(r)=0,\quad j_{1}(r)=|S_{r}|,

\sigma_{1}(r)
is any ordering of
S_{r}
by introduction index, and

\Delta_{1}(r)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }|S_{r}|\geq 2,\\
0,&\text{if }|S_{r}|=1.\end{cases}

We set the persistence counters
\mathbf{p}_{1}^{\Pi_{1}}=(p_{1}(1),\dots,p_{1}(\lambda))

with
p_{1}(r)=1
for all
r
.
A state
\Sigma_{1}^{\Pi_{1}}
is retained iff all parts satisfy
\Delta_{1}(r)\leq s
.
Thus,

\mathcal{T}_{1}\;=\;\Bigl\{\,\Sigma_{1}^{\Pi_{1}}\ \Big|\ \Pi_{1}\in\mathfrak{P}_{1}\text{ and }\forall r:\ \Delta_{1}(r)\leq s\Bigr\}.

In both cases, the initialization explicitly enumerates
all
admissible
states
\Sigma_{1}
with their exact budgets, diameter profiles, and persistence
counters, consistent with the global edge-deletion budget
k
and the
diameter bound
s
.

Transition at an introduce bag.

Let
B^{\prime}_{i}

(i\geq 2)
be an introduce bag in the extended path decomposition

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, and let
X_{i}:=B^{\prime}_{i}\setminus B^{\prime}_{i-1}
denote the vertex (or
batch of vertices) newly introduced at this step. For every feasible state

\Sigma_{i-1}=(\Pi_{i-1},\mathbf{D}_{i-1},\mathbf{p}_{i-1},b_{i-1})

stored in
\mathcal{T}_{i-1}
, we generate successor states

\Sigma_{i}=(\Pi_{i},\mathbf{D}_{i},\mathbf{p}_{i},b_{i})
as follows.

(1) Extending the partition and updating budget.

Each newly introduced vertex

•

join an existing part

, or

•

.

(2) Updating diameter profiles.

For each part

We compute the new profile

as follows.

[(i)]

1.

Updating the boundary ordering.

•

.

):

•

Since

•

Let
\sigma_{i-1}(r^{\prime})=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{p_{i-1}})
denote the previous

For a newly created singleton part

(3) Updating persistence counters.

(5) State insertion.

After these checks, every surviving candidate

Let

(2) Updating diameter profiles.

For each surviving part

•

The new boundary is

If the connected region

For every surviving component

Because

(5) State insertion.

Each surviving candidate

The extended path decomposition

of

The dynamic program thus returns

\textsc{YES}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists\,\Sigma_{m}\in\mathcal{T}_{m}\text{ such that }b_{m}\leq k.

If no such record remains, all partial solutions violate either the

8.4
Running time analysis

We prove that the dynamic program runs in
f(k)\cdot s^{O(1)}\cdot n^{O(1)}
time (hence FPT in
k
), with only a polynomial dependence on
s
.
The key combinatorial fact is that, although we store
actual
distance/diameter values up to
s
, at any bag at most one active component can
attain a value of order
s
; all others are bounded purely as a function of
k
.

Lemma 31

(Capping lemma)
.

Fix
i\in[m]
and let
\Sigma_{i}=(\Pi_{i},\mathbf{D}_{i},\mathbf{p}_{i},b_{i})
be a feasible DP state at bag
B^{\prime}_{i}
.
Let
T:=k+1
. Then at most one part
r\in[\lambda]
satisfies
d_{i}(r)>T
or
\Delta_{i}(r)>T
.

Proof.

In our extended path decomposition, bags
alternate
between introduce and forget.
By the DP transitions:

•

d_{i}(r)
can increase only at a
forget
step, by at most
1
;
it does not increase at introduce steps.

•

\Delta_{i}(r)
can increase only at an
introduce
step, by at most
1
;
it does not increase at forget steps.

Let
p_{i}(r)
be the number of consecutive bags (up to
i
) in which
K_{r}
is present.
Among these
p_{i}(r)
bags, at most
\lfloor p_{i}(r)/2\rfloor
are forget steps and at most
\lceil p_{i}(r)/2\rceil
are introduce steps.

If
d_{i}(r)>k+1
, then since
d
increases only on forget steps by at most
1
, we must have

\lfloor p_{i}(r)/2\rfloor\ \geq\ k+2\quad\Rightarrow\quad p_{i}(r)\ \geq\ 2k+4.

Similarly, if
\Delta_{i}(r)>k+1
, then since
\Delta
increases only on introduce steps by at most
1
,

\lceil p_{i}(r)/2\rceil\ \geq\ k+2\quad\Rightarrow\quad p_{i}(r)\ \geq\ 2k+3.

Hence any part with
d_{i}(r)>T
or
\Delta_{i}(r)>T
must satisfy
p_{i}(r)\geq 2k+3
.

Now suppose for contradiction that there exist two distinct parts
r\neq r^{\prime}
with the fact that

\max\{d_{i}(r),\Delta_{i}(r)\}>T
and
\max\{d_{i}(r^{\prime}),\Delta_{i}(r^{\prime})\}>T
.
From the previous paragraph,
p_{i}(r)\geq 2k+3
and
p_{i}(r^{\prime})\geq 2k+3
.
But our DP declares a state
invalid
if there exist distinct
r\neq r^{\prime}
with

p_{i}(r)\geq 2k+2
and
p_{i}(r^{\prime})\geq 2k+2
(Definition
29
, Persistence counters).
This contradicts the feasibility of
\Sigma_{i}
.
Therefore at most one part can have
d_{i}(r)>T
or
\Delta_{i}(r)>T
.
∎

Corollary 32

(Bounded annotation domain except one)
.

In every feasible DP state
\Sigma_{i}
there is
at most one
part
r^{\star}
whose pair

(d_{i}(r^{\star}),\Delta_{i}(r^{\star}))
may range up to
s
; for all other parts
r\neq r^{\star}

we have
d_{i}(r),\Delta_{i}(r)\leq T=k+1
.

Theorem 33
.

The dynamic program computes a correct solution in time

f(k)\cdot s^{O(1)}\cdot n^{O(1)}\qquad\text{and space}\qquad s^{O(1)}\cdot k^{O(k)},

for some computable function
f(k)=k^{O(k)}
. In particular, the algorithm is fixed-parameter tractable parameterized by
k
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=(B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{m})
be the extended path decomposition constructed in Lemma
28
, and note that
m=O(n)
.
For each
i\in[m]
, denote
t:=|B^{\prime}_{i}|
.
We analyze the number of feasible states stored in
\mathcal{T}_{i}
and the cost of transitions between consecutive bags.

Case distinction by bag size.

If
t\geq k{+}2
, the budget constraint immediately forces the unique partition
\{B^{\prime}_{i}\}
, since splitting the bag into two or more parts would require deletion of at least
k{+}1
inter-part edges.
Hence at such a bag there exists at most one feasible state, and all updates to its parameters can be performed in
k^{O(1)}
time.
Therefore, the analysis below only concerns bags with
t\leq k{+}1
.

Counting admissible partitions.

For a small bag
B^{\prime}_{i}
with
t\leq k{+}1
, the number of possible set partitions of
B^{\prime}_{i}
is the
t
-th Bell number
B_{t}\leq t^{t}\leq(k{+}1)^{k{+}1}=k^{O(k)}
.
Enforcing the budget constraint
b_{i}\leq k
can only reduce this number.
Thus the number of admissible partitions
\Pi_{i}
is bounded by
k^{O(k)}
.

Counting diameter-profile annotations.

Fix an admissible partition
\Pi_{i}=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{\lambda}\}
, where
\lambda\leq t\leq k{+}1
.
For each part
S_{r}
, the annotation
\mathbf{D}_{i}(r)=(d_{i}(r),j_{i}(r),\sigma_{i}(r),\Delta_{i}(r))
is determined as follows:

\sigma_{i}(r)
is canonical (ordered by introduction index) and hence unique;

j_{i}(r)
can take at most
|S_{r}|{+}1\leq k{+}2
values;
and the pair
(d_{i}(r),\Delta_{i}(r))
ranges over
\{0,\ldots,s\}^{2}
.
By Corollary
32
, however, all but one part
r^{\star}
satisfy
d_{i}(r),\Delta_{i}(r)\leq k{+}1
, since only one component may persist long enough to reach diameter values of order
s
.
Consequently, for a fixed partition
\Pi_{i}
,

\#\mathrm{profiles}(\Pi_{i})\;\leq\;(k{+}2)^{\lambda}\cdot(k{+}2)^{2(\lambda-1)}\cdot(s{+}1)^{2}\;=\;s^{O(1)}\cdot k^{O(k)}.

Counting persistence vectors.

Each persistence counter
p_{i}(r)
takes values in
\{1,\ldots,2k{+}2\}
, and two simultaneous counters
\geq 2k{+}2
are forbidden by Definition
29
.
Therefore, across
\lambda\leq k{+}1
parts there are at most
(2k{+}2)^{\lambda}\leq(2k{+}2)^{k{+}1}=k^{O(k)}
possible assignments of
\mathbf{p}_{i}
.

Counting budget values..

The cumulative deletion counter
b_{i}
ranges over
\{0,\ldots,k\}
, which contributes an additional factor of
(k{+}1)
.

Bounding the number of states.

Multiplying the above factors yields

|\mathcal{T}_{i}|\;\leq\;\underbrace{k^{O(k)}}_{\text{partitions}}\cdot\underbrace{s^{O(1)}k^{O(k)}}_{\text{profiles}}\cdot\underbrace{k^{O(k)}}_{\text{persistence}}\cdot\underbrace{(k{+}1)}_{\text{budget}}\;=\;s^{O(1)}\cdot k^{O(k)}.

If
t\geq k{+}2
, we have
|\mathcal{T}_{i}|\leq 1
, and hence the same upper bound holds for all
i
.

Bounding the transition cost.

From each predecessor state at bag
B^{\prime}_{i-1}
we may generate successor states at
B^{\prime}_{i}
.
At an
introduce
bag, each newly added vertex can either form a singleton part or join an existing part; this branching is subsumed by the
k^{O(k)}
partitions already counted above.
All numeric updates of
(d_{i},j_{i},\Delta_{i})
, diameter checks, and persistence updates require only
k^{O(1)}
time.
At a
forget
bag, boundary vertices are removed and the updates of
(d_{i},j_{i})
take
O(k)
time, while
\Delta_{i}
remains unchanged.
Thus the total cost per predecessor state is
k^{O(1)}
.

Combining all bounds.

Since the decomposition contains
m=O(n)
bags and at each bag we maintain at most
s^{O(1)}k^{O(k)}
states, the total running time satisfies

\mathrm{Time}\;\leq\;m\cdot s^{O(1)}k^{O(k)}\cdot k^{O(1)}\;=\;f(k)\cdot s^{O(1)}\cdot n^{O(1)}.

The total space is bounded by the number of states per bag,

s^{O(1)}\cdot k^{O(k)}
.

Conclusion.

Both the running time and the space depend polynomially on
n
and
s
, and
exponentially only on the parameter
k
.
Hence the algorithm is fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by
k
.
∎

9
On Unit Interval Graphs

Having established hardness on split graphs, we next identify a natural class of graphs—unit interval graphs—on which the problem becomes polynomial-time solvable.

Theorem 34
.

For every fixed integer
s\geq 1
, the problem
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
can be solved in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
on unit interval graphs.

Proof.

We first establish a structural lemma showing that no optimal solution needs to split a maximal clique. Then we exploit the canonical clique path of a unit interval graph to obtain a dynamic‐programming algorithm.

Lemma 1 (No Clique Splitting).

Let
G
be a unit interval graph with canonical clique path

C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{m}
, where every vertex of
G
appears in a contiguous subsequence of cliques and consecutive cliques intersect.
Let
F\subseteq E(G)
be an edge-deletion set such that every connected component of
G-F
has diameter at most
s
.
Then there exists a deletion set
F^{\star}
with
|F^{\star}|\leq|F|
such that
(i) each maximal clique
C_{i}
is wholly contained in one component of
G-F^{\star}
,
and (ii) every component of
G-F^{\star}
has diameter
\leq s
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a minimum-size feasible deletion set that minimizes
the number of split cliques.
Assume, for contradiction, that some
C_{p}
is split by
F
:
there exist components
A
and
B
of
G-F

with
A\cap C_{p}\neq\emptyset
and
B\cap C_{p}\neq\emptyset
.
Thus
F
contains all edges between
A\cap C_{p}
and
B\cap C_{p}
.

Each component of
G-F
spans at most
s
consecutive cliques
of the canonical path.
Hence there exist index intervals
[L_{A},R_{A}]
and
[L_{B},R_{B}]

of length
\leq s
such that
A
and
B
are contained in

\bigcup_{t\in[L_{A},R_{A}]}C_{t}
and
\bigcup_{t\in[L_{B},R_{B}]}C_{t}
,
respectively, and both contain
C_{p}
.
Define two windows:

W_{L}=[p-s+1,p]\cap[1,m]\qquad\text{and}\qquad W_{R}=[p,p+s-1]\cap[1,m].

Construct
F^{\star}
by assigning
C_{p}
wholly to one side, say to the right:

F^{\star}=\bigl(F\setminus\tbinom{C_{p}}{2}\bigr)\cup\bigl\{\,uv\in E(G)\mid u\in C_{p},\ v\in C_{t},\ t<p\text{ or }t>p+s-1\,\bigr\}.

That is, we restore all internal edges of
C_{p}
and cut all edges
from
C_{p}
to cliques outside the right window.

Feasibility.

All vertices of
C_{p}
now have neighbors only in

\bigcup_{t\in W_{R}}C_{t}
, a union of at most
s
consecutive cliques.
By the distance property of unit interval graphs,
this subgraph has diameter at most
s
.
Hence every connected component of
G-F^{\star}
has diameter
\leq s
.

Size.

F^{\star}
deletes no more edges than
F
:
we restore all edges internal to
C_{p}

and add only edges from
C_{p}
to outside the chosen window.
By symmetry, we can pick the cheaper of the two assignments
(to
W_{L}
or
W_{R}
), ensuring
|F^{\star}|\leq|F|
.

Finally, since all edges within
C_{p}
are present in
G-F^{\star}
,
the clique
C_{p}
is now contained entirely within one component,
and no new clique becomes split.
Thus
F^{\star}
has strictly fewer split cliques than
F
,
contradicting the minimal choice of
F
.
Therefore no optimal solution splits a maximal clique.
∎

Dynamic‐Programming Formulation.

By Lemma 1, every component of an optimal solution consists of a contiguous block
of at most
s
consecutive maximal cliques of the canonical clique path.
Hence an optimal solution corresponds exactly to a
partition

of the index set
\{1,\dots,p\}
into consecutive blocks

[b_{1},e_{1}],[b_{2},e_{2}],\dots,[b_{r},e_{r}]

such that
b_{1}=1
,
e_{r}=m
, and
b_{t+1}=e_{t}+1
.
For any boundary between two consecutive blocks, we must delete every edge having
endpoints in the two adjacent blocks.
Let
\mathrm{cost}(i,j)
denote the number of edges having one endpoint in

\bigcup_{t\leq i}C_{t}
and the other in
\bigcup_{t>i}C_{t}
when we place a cut between

C_{i}
and
C_{i+1}
.
These costs can be computed in
O(n^{2})
preprocessing by scanning vertex intervals.
Define the DP table

\mathrm{DP}[t]
= minimum number of deletions needed so that

C_{1},\dots,C_{t}
are partitioned into valid
s
-club components.

The recurrence is

\mathrm{DP}[t]=\min_{1\leq j\leq s}\bigl(\mathrm{DP}[t-j]+\mathrm{cost}(t-j,t-j+1)\bigr)
,
with base case
\mathrm{DP}[0]=0
and
\mathrm{cost}(0,1)=0
.
Intuitively, we end the last component at
C_{t}
, whose length may be at most
s
,
and pay the deletion cost for the cut between
C_{t-j}
and
C_{t-j+1}
.
Because there are
p=O(n)
cliques in the canonical path and
s
is fixed,
the DP runs in
O(sn)
time once the cut costs are known.

Finally, reconstructing the partition from the DP yields the edge-deletion set
that achieves the minimum number of deletions.
Thus
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is solvable in polynomial time on unit interval graphs.
∎

10
s
-Club Cluster Arc Deletion (Directed Variant)

In this section, we study the parameterized complexity of the directed variant of the

s
-Club Cluster edge Deletion
problem when parameterized by the

cutset size
, that is, by the number
k
of arcs allowed to be deleted.
This parameterization corresponds to the natural measure of solution size
in edge (or arc) modification problems.

We work with finite simple digraphs
D=(V,A)
. For
u,v\in V
, let

\dist_{D}(u,v)
be the length of a shortest directed
u\!\to\!v
path
(
+\infty
if no such path exists). A (weakly) connected subgraph

H
of
D
is
s
-reach-bounded
if for all ordered pairs
(u,v)\in V(H)\times V(H)
,
whenever
v
is reachable from
u
in
H
we have
\dist_{H}(u,v)\leq s
.
Equivalently,
H
has directed eccentricity at most
s
from every vertex
to all vertices it can reach.

s
-Club Cluster Arc Deletion

Input:
A digraph
D=(V,A)
and integers
k,s\geq 1
.

Question:
Does there exist a set
F\subseteq A
with
|F|\leq k
such that
every
weakly
connected component
C
of
D-F
is
s
-reach-bounded; i.e.,
for all
u,v\in V(C)
, if
v
is reachable from
u
in
C
then
\dist_{C}(u,v)\leq s
?

The above definition imposes a one-sided distance constraint: we only require that whenever a vertex
v
is reachable from a vertex
u
in a component
C
, the directed distance
\dist_{C}(u,v)
is at most
s
; mutual reachability is not necessary. A stricter formulation sometimes considered in the literature would require each component of
D-F
to be strongly connected with directed diameter at most
s
. Our definition follows the “reachable implies bounded distance” interpretation, which generalizes naturally from the undirected case. Finally, when
D
is obtained by replacing every undirected edge
\{u,v\}
in a graph
G
by both arcs
(u,v)
and
(v,u)
, this directed variant coincides with the undirected
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
problem.

We show that this problem is computationally intractable even under
structural restrictions. In particular, we prove that

s
-Club Cluster Arc Deletion
is

\mathrm{W[1]}
-hard when parameterized by
k
,
even when the input digraph is acyclic. In particular, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 35
.

s
-Club Cluster Arc Deletion
is
\mathrm{W[1]}
-hard when
parameterized by the cutset size
k
,
even when restricted to directed acyclic graphs (DAGs).

Proof.

We give a parameterized reduction from the
Edge Multicut
problem,
which is known to be
\mathrm{W[1]}
-hard when parameterized by the size of the cutset
even on directed acyclic graphs
[
14
]
.
Let the input instance be
(G=(V,A),\mathcal{T},k)
, where

\mathcal{T}\subseteq V\times V
is a collection of ordered terminal pairs
and
k
is the allowed number of arc deletions.

Topological ordering of terminals.

Let
T=\{t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{p}\}
denote the set of all distinct terminals appearing in
\mathcal{T}
.
Since
G
is acyclic, we fix a topological ordering of
V(G)
and assume, without loss of generality,
that the terminals are indexed according to this order; that is, for any
1\leq i<j\leq p
,
there is no directed path from
t_{j}
to
t_{i}
in
G
.
All arcs added in our construction will respect this ordering, preserving acyclicity.

Construction of
D^{\prime}
.

We construct a digraph
D^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},A^{\prime})
and an integer
s

such that
(G,\mathcal{T},k)
is a yes-instance of

Edge Multicut
if and only if
(D^{\prime},k^{\prime},s)
is a yes-instance of

s
-Club Cluster Arc Deletion
.

For each terminal
t_{i}\in T
, we introduce two new vertices

t_{i}^{\mathrm{in}}
and
t_{i}^{\mathrm{out}}
.
We then perform the following steps:

•

Add a directed path of length
\ell
from
t_{i}^{\mathrm{in}}
to
t_{i}
, and a directed path of length
\ell
from
t_{i}
to
t_{i}^{\mathrm{out}}
.

•

Each arc
(v_{r},v_{r+1})
on these paths is replaced by
k+1
internally vertex-disjoint paths of length two from
v_{r}
to
v_{r+1}
(a
reinforced step
).
This ensures that deleting at most
k
arcs cannot disconnect the endpoints of the step.

•

For every ordered pair
(t_{i},t_{j})\notin\mathcal{T}
with
i<j
,
add all arcs from every vertex of
L_{i}
to every vertex of
R_{j}
,
except the single arc
(t_{i},t_{j})
.
(Thus, the terminals themselves are never directly connected by a new arc.)
For each
(t_{i},t_{j})\in\mathcal{T}
, add no such arcs.

•

Retain all original arcs of
G
.

Finally, we set
\ell:=2m
where
m=|A(G)|
, and define
s:=4\ell
and
k^{\prime}:=k
.
All arcs thus point from smaller to larger terminal indices, or stay within one gadget
in the forward direction
t_{i}^{\mathrm{in}}\to t_{i}\to t_{i}^{\mathrm{out}}
.

It is straightforward to verify that
D^{\prime}
remains acyclic:
every new arc is directed from a vertex associated with
t_{i}

towards vertices associated with
t_{j}
for some
j\geq i
,
and all reinforced or cross-connection arcs respect this ordering.
Hence no directed cycle is introduced by the construction.

Forward direction (
\Rightarrow
).

Assume there exists a multicut
F\subseteq A(G)
with
|F|\leq k

such that for every
(t_{i},t_{j})\in\mathcal{T}
,
there is no directed path from
t_{i}
to
t_{j}
in
G-F
.
We claim that deleting the same set of arcs
F
from
D^{\prime}

yields a digraph in which every weakly connected component is
s
-reach-bounded
with
s=4\ell
.

That is, for all vertices
u,v
in the same weakly connected component of
D^{\prime}-F
,
if
v
is reachable from
u
in
D^{\prime}-F
, then
\mathrm{dist}_{D^{\prime}-F}(u,v)\leq s
.

We consider cases depending on the position of
u
and
v
.

Case A.

u
and
v
lie within the same terminal gadget.

Each gadget corresponding to
t_{i}
consists of two reinforced chains

t_{i}^{\mathrm{in}}\!\to t_{i}
and
t_{i}\!\to t_{i}^{\mathrm{out}}

and all arcs
L_{i}\to R_{i}
.
Each reinforced step contributes length 2, and there are at most
\ell
such steps per chain.
Hence the maximum directed distance inside a gadget is at most
2\ell+1
,
which is smaller than
s
.
Thus, whenever
v
is reachable from
u
within the same gadget,

\mathrm{dist}_{D^{\prime}-F}(u,v)\leq 2\ell\leq s
.

Case B.

u
and
v
lie in gadgets
i
and
j
with
(t_{i},t_{j})\notin\mathcal{T}
.

By construction, we added all arcs from
L_{i}\setminus\{t_{i}\}
to
R_{j}\setminus\{t_{j}\}
and did not add the arc
(t_{i},t_{j})
. We analyze by the position of
u
inside gadget
i
.

B.1.
u\in L_{i}\setminus\{t_{i}\}
.

Then
u
has a direct outgoing arc to every
y\in R_{j}\setminus\{t_{j}\}
.
If
v\in R_{j}\setminus\{t_{j}\}
, we can pick
y=v
and obtain

\dist_{D^{\prime}-F}(u,v)=1\leq s
.

If
v\in L_{j}
, then
u
does not reach
v
(there are no arcs
R_{j}\to L_{j}
by construction), so the “reachable
\Rightarrow
distance
\leq s
” condition holds vacuously.

B.2.
u\in R_{i}\setminus\{t_{i}\}
.

No vertex of
R_{i}\setminus\{t_{i}\}
has arcs to vertices of other gadgets, so
u
cannot reach any vertex in gadget
j
. Thus the condition holds vacuously.

B.3.
u=t_{i}
.

There is no arc
(t_{i},\cdot)
to any vertex of
R_{j}
from the inter-gadget shortcut layer, but if
t_{i}
reaches
t_{j}
in the original DAG, then there exists a
t_{i}\!\to\!t_{j}
path of length at most
m
. From
t_{j}
one reaches any
y\in R_{j}
within at most
2\ell
steps (along the reinforced
t_{j}\rightsquigarrow t_{j}^{\mathrm{out}}
path). Hence, whenever
t_{i}
reaches any
v\in R_{j}
, we have

\dist_{D^{\prime}-F}(t_{i},v)\ \leq\ m+2\ell\ \leq\ 5m\ \leq\ 8m\ =\ 4\ell\ =\ s,

since we set
\ell=2m
and
s=4\ell
.
If
v\in L_{j}
, then
t_{i}
does not reach
v
(there are no arcs
R_{j}\to L_{j}
), so the condition holds vacuously.

Case C.

u
and
v
lie in gadgets
i
and
j
with
(t_{i},t_{j})\in\mathcal{T}
.

For request pairs, no inter-gadget arcs
L_{i}\to R_{j}
were added.
Any directed path from gadget
i
to gadget
j
would have to correspond
to a
t_{i}\!\to t_{j}
path in
G
.
However, by the assumption that
F
is a valid multicut in
G
,
all such paths are destroyed by deleting
F
.
Therefore, no vertex of gadget
i
can reach any vertex of gadget
j
in
D^{\prime}-F
,
and the
s
-reach-bounded property holds vacuously.

In all cases, if
v
is reachable from
u
in
D^{\prime}-F
,
then
\mathrm{dist}_{D^{\prime}-F}(u,v)\leq s
.
Hence, every weakly connected component of
D^{\prime}-F

is
s
-reach-bounded with
s=4\ell
,
as required.

Backward direction.

Assume that
F\subseteq A(D^{\prime})
is a feasible solution for

s
-Club Cluster Arc Deletion
on
D^{\prime}
with
|F|\leq k
,
and let
F_{G}:=F\cap A(G)
denote the corresponding set of arcs removed from the original DAG
D
.
Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a terminal pair

(t_{i},t_{j})\in\mathcal{T}
such that a directed path from
t_{i}
to
t_{j}

remains in
G-F_{G}
.

In
D^{\prime}-F
, the following hold:

1.

The reinforced path from
t_{i}^{\mathrm{in}}
to
t_{i}
has length exactly
\ell

and remains intact, since every replaced arc was substituted by
k+1

internally vertex-disjoint directed 2-paths, and
|F|\leq k
.

2.

Similarly, the path from
t_{j}
to
t_{j}^{\mathrm{out}}
also has length
\ell

and is unaffected by the deletion of
F
.

3.

No shortcut arcs were added from
L_{i}
to
R_{j}
for
(t_{i},t_{j})\in\mathcal{T}
,
so any path from
t_{i}^{\mathrm{in}}
to
t_{j}^{\mathrm{out}}
must pass through

t_{i}
, traverse a copy of the original
t_{i}\!\to t_{j}
path in
G-F_{G}
,
and then proceed through the reinforced segment leading to
t_{j}^{\mathrm{out}}
.

Hence, concatenating these parts yields a directed path in
D^{\prime}-F
as

t_{i}^{\mathrm{in}}\;\leadsto\;t_{i}\;\leadsto\;t_{j}\;\leadsto\;t_{j}^{\mathrm{out}}
,
whose total length is at least

2\ell\;+\;1\;+\;+\;2\ell\;=\;4\ell+1
.
The first and last
2\ell
correspond to the reinforced segments to and from the terminals,
while the middle part contributes at least one original arc of
G
.
Since
s=4\ell
, we have

\mathrm{dist}_{D^{\prime}-F}\bigl(t_{i}^{\mathrm{in}},t_{j}^{\mathrm{out}}\bigr)\geq 4\ell+1>s
.
Therefore, vertices
t_{i}^{\mathrm{in}}
and
t_{j}^{\mathrm{out}}
lie in the same weakly connected component of
D^{\prime}-F

and violate the
s
-reach-bounded property,
contradicting the assumption that
F
is a valid solution.
It follows that no terminal pair
(t_{i},t_{j})\in\mathcal{T}
remains reachable in
G-F_{G}
,
and thus
F_{G}
is a valid multicut for
(G,\mathcal{T},k)
.
∎

11
Conclusion and Open Problems

This work presented a systematic study of the parameterized and structural complexity of the
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
problem,
a natural generalization of
Cluster Edge Deletion
in which cliques are replaced by
s
-clubs.
Our investigation was guided by a central question:

when is the diameter bound
s
essential for achieving tractability?

The results reported here delineate the fine boundary between fixed-parameter tractability and hardness,
showing how the interplay between structural parameters and distance constraints governs computational feasibility.

Classical complexity and kernelization.

We first examined the classical and kernelization aspects of the problem.
We proved that
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
is NP-hard on split graphs already for
s=2
,
thus completing the complexity landscape for this graph class:
the cases
s=1
and
s=3
are polynomial-time solvable.
For the same setting, we designed a cubic vertex kernel parameterized by solution size,
giving the first polynomial kernel for
2
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
on split graphs.
We also established a polynomial-time algorithm for
s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
on unit interval graphs,
further expanding the classes of graphs on which the problem is tractable.

Parameterized complexity.

From the viewpoint of parameterized complexity,
we established that the problem is fixed-parameter tractable
when parameterized by neighborhood diversity, twin cover, and cluster vertex deletion number.
These results extend to all
s\geq 1
the FPT algorithms previously known only for the case
s=1
,
thus unifying and generalizing several earlier approaches.
On the hardness side, we proved that the problem is W[1]-hard when parameterized by pathwidth—and consequently by treewidth—
thereby resolving an open question posed by Montecchiani et al. and demonstrating that the dependence on
s
in their FPT algorithm is indeed unavoidable. .
In fact, our construction yields a stronger result:

s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
remains W[1]-hard even when parameterized by the combined parameter

\pw(G)+d
, where
d
denotes the maximum number of
s
-clubs allowed in the resulting graph.

Bicriteria approximation and the role of the diameter bound.

To further probe the influence of the diameter constraint,
we studied the single-parameter regime where only the solution size
k
is bounded.
While the existence of an exact FPT algorithm parameterized solely by
k
remains open,
we made progress by designing a bicriteria fixed-parameter approximation algorithm.
For graphs excluding long induced cycles, our algorithm runs in time

f(k,1/\varepsilon)\,n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}

and outputs a solution of size at most
k
whose connected components have diameter at most
(1+\varepsilon)s
.
This shows that a small relaxation of the diameter bound can restore fixed-parameter tractability,
highlighting the subtle and central role played by distance constraints in defining computational hardness.

Future directions.

Together, these contributions nearly complete the parameterized landscape of

s
-Club Cluster Edge Deletion
under structural measures,
while opening several compelling directions for future research.
The most immediate challenge is to determine whether the problem admits an
exact
FPT algorithm when parameterized solely by
k
.
Other promising directions include exploring parameterizations by feedback vertex set, modular width, or feedback edge set,
and investigating the existence of polynomial kernels under the combined parameter
(s+k)
—
a question that remains open
[
16
]
.

In summary, our results pinpoint when and why the diameter bound
s
becomes crucial for tractability.
By linking local distance constraints with global structural graph properties,
this work advances the theoretical understanding of how bounded-diameter clustering problems transition
from efficiently solvable to intractable regimes.

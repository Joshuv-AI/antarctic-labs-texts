An alternative approach for the mean-field behaviour of spread-out Bernoulli percolation in dimensions d > 6 d>6
Hugo Duminil-Copin, Romain Panis

Abstract
This article proposes a new way of deriving mean-field exponents for sufficiently spread-out Bernoulli percolation in dimensions d > 6 d>6 . We obtain up-to-constant estimates for the full-space and half-space two-point functions in the critical and near-critical regimes. In a companion paper, we apply a similar analysis to the study of the weakly self-avoiding walk model in dimensions d > 4 d>4 [ DCP ] .



We dedicate this article to Geoffrey Grimmett on the occasion of his seventieth birthday. Geoffrey’s contributions to percolation theory have been profoundly influential for our generation of probabilists. His renowned manuscript was the bedside book for the authors during their PhD theses.

1
Introduction

1.1
Motivation

Grasping the (near-)critical behaviour of lattice models is one of the key challenges in statistical mechanics. A possible approach involves determining the models’
critical exponents
. Performing this task is typically very difficult as it involves the unique characteristics of the models and the geometry of the graphs on which they are constructed.

A significant observation was made for models defined on the hypercubic lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
: beyond the
upper-critical dimension

d_{c}
, the influence of geometry disappears, and the critical exponents simplify, matching those found on a Cayley tree (or
Bethe lattice
) or on the complete graph. The regime
d>d_{c}
forms the
mean-field
regime of a model.

Prominent techniques such as the
lace expansion

[
BS85
]
and the
rigourous renormalisation group method

[
BBS14
,
BBS15a
,
BBS15b
,
BBS19
]
have been developed to analyse the mean-field regime. These approaches have a predominantly
perturbative
nature, which necessitates to identify a small parameter within the model. It has been established, using lace expansion, that in several contexts
[
HS90a
,
Sak07
,
Har08
,
Sak15
,
FvdH17
,
Sak22
]
this small parameter can be taken to be
\tfrac{1}{d-d_{c}}
, meaning that mean-field behaviour was recovered in these setups in dimensions
d\gg d_{c}
.

In the example of the nearest-neighbour (meaning that bonds are pairs of vertices separated by unit Euclidean distance) Bernoulli percolation, mean-field behaviour was established in dimensions
d>10

[
HS90a
,
Har08
,
FvdH17
]
. This leaves a gap to fill in order to reach the expected upper critical dimension of the model
d_{c}=6
.
It is however possible to provide rigorous arguments to identify
d_{c}
by introducing an additional perturbative parameter in the model. In the
spread-out
Bernoulli percolation model, the bonds are pairs of vertices separated by distance between
1
and
L
, where
L
is taken to be sufficiently large. According to the
universality
conjecture, the critical exponents of these two models should match. This makes spread-out Bernoulli percolation a natural testing ground to develop the analysis of the mean-field regime of Bernoulli percolation.

Lace expansion was successfully applied to study various spread-out models in statistical mechanics, including Bernoulli percolation
[
HS90a
,
HHS03
]
, lattice trees and animals
[
HS90b
]
, the Ising model
[
Sak07
,
Sak22
]
, and even some long-range versions of the aforementioned examples
[
CS15
,
CS19
]
. Much more information on the lace expansion approach can be found in
[
Sla06
]
.

In this paper, we provide an alternative argument to obtain mean-field bounds on the two-point function of sufficiently spread-out Bernoulli percolation in dimensions
d>6
. This technique extends to a number of other (spread-out) models after relevant modifications. In a companion paper
[
DCP
]
, we provide a treatment of the weakly self-avoiding walk model. However, the strategy developed there does not apply
mutatis mutandis
to the setup of spread-out Bernoulli percolation, and the presence of long finite range interactions requires additional care.

Notations.

Consider the hypercubic lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Set
{\bf e}_{j}
to be the unit vector with
j
-th coordinate equal to 1. Write
x_{j}
for the
j
-th coordinate of
x
and denote its
\ell^{\infty}
norm by
|x|:=\max\{|x_{j}|:1\leq j\leq d\}
. Set
\Lambda_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|x|\leq n\}
and for
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\Lambda_{n}(x):=\Lambda_{n}+x
. Also, set
\mathbb{H}_{n}:=-n{\bf e}_{1}+\mathbb{H}
, where
\mathbb{H}:=\mathbb{Z}_{+}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}=\{0,1,\ldots\}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
. For a set
S
of vertices, introduce
S^{*}:=S\setminus\{0\}
. Let
\partial S
be the boundary of the set
S
given by the vertices in
S
with one neighbour outside
S
. Finally, define the set of
generalised blocks
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\mathcal{B}:=\Big\{\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{d}[a_{i},b_{i}]\Big)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\text{ such that }-\infty\leq a_{i}\leq 0\leq b_{i}\leq\infty\text{ for }1\leq i\leq d\Big\}.

(1.1)

If
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
x,y\in\Lambda
, we denote by
\{x\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}y\}
the event that
x
and
y
are connected by a path of open edges in
\Lambda
.
If
A
and
B
are two percolation events, we write
A\circ B
for the event of
disjoint
occurrence of
A
and
B
, i.e. the event that there exist two disjoint sets
I
and
J
of edges such that the configuration restricted to
I
(resp.
J
) is sufficient to decide that
A
(resp.
B
) occurs.

1.2
Definitions and statement of the results

Let
L\geq 1
. Since
L
is fixed for the whole article, we omit it from the notations. We consider the Bernoulli percolation measure
\mathbb{P}_{\beta}
such that for every
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

p_{uv,\beta}:=\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[uv\text{ is open}]=1-\exp(-\beta J_{uv})=1-\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[uv\text{ is closed}],

(1.2)

where
J_{uv}=c_{L}\mathds{1}_{1\leq|u-v|\leq L}
, and
c_{L}
is a normalization constant which guarantees that
|J|:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}J_{0,x}=1
(i.e.
c_{L}=|\Lambda_{L}^{*}|^{-1}
). We also let
p_{\beta}:=1-e^{-\beta c_{L}}
. Much more general choices can be made for
J
(see e.g.
[
HS90a
,
HS02
,
HHS03
]
) but we restrict our attention to the above for simplicity.

We are interested in the model’s two-point function which is, for
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the probability

\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[x\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}y]
.
When
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we simply write
\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y]=\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[x\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}^{d}\>}y]
.
It is well known that Bernoulli percolation undergoes a phase transition for the existence of an infinite cluster at some parameter
\beta_{c}\in(0,\infty)
. Moreover, for
\beta<\beta_{c}
,
\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y]
decays exponentially fast in
|x-y|
, see
[
AN84
,
Men86
,
AB87
,
DCT16
]
. It is convenient to measure the rate of exponential decay in terms of the the
sharp length

L_{\beta}
defined below (see also
[
DCT16
,
Pan
,
DCP25
]
for a study of this quantity in the context of the Ising model). For
\beta\geq 0
and
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, let

\varphi_{\beta}(S):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in S\\
z\notin S\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y]p_{yz,\beta}.

(1.3)

The sharp length
L_{\beta}
is defined as follows:

L_{\beta}:=\inf\{k\geq 1:\varphi_{\beta}(\Lambda_{k})\leq 1/e^{2}\}.

(1.4)

Exponential decay of the two-point function yields that
L_{\beta}
is finite for
\beta<\beta_{c}
. It is infinite for
\beta=\beta_{c}
; see
[
Sim80
]
.

We now turn to our first main result, which provides uniform upper bounds on the critical full-space and half-space two-point functions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d>6
.
There exist
C,L_{0}>0
such that for every
L\geq L_{0}
and every
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
for which
L_{\beta}\geq L
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{L^{d}}\left(\frac{L}{L\vee|x|}\right)^{d-2}\exp(-|x|/L_{\beta})

\displaystyle\forall x\in(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{*},

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{L^{d}}\left(\frac{L}{L\vee|x_{1}|}\right)^{d-1}\exp(-|x_{1}|/L_{\beta})

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{H}^{*}.

(1.6)

Using the lace expansion, it is possible to obtain exact asymptotics of the critical full-space two-point function
[
HHS03
,
LS
]
: if
d>6
, there exist
D,L_{0}>0
such that if
L\geq L_{0}
,

\mathbb{P}_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x]=\frac{D}{L^{2}|x|_{2}^{d-2}}(1+o(1)),

(1.7)

where
|\cdot|_{2}
is the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
o(1)
tends to
0
as
|x|_{2}
tends to infinity. However, the best uniform (in
x
) upper bound that this method yield is the following: if
d>6
, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C>0
such that for all
L
large enough and
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
,

\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x]\leq\frac{C}{L^{2-\epsilon}|x|^{d-2}}\qquad\forall x\in(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{*}.

(1.8)

Hence, Theorem
1.1
is an improvement of the bound obtained using the lace expansion. Half-space up-to-constants critical estimates have been obtained in
[
CH20
]
using the upper and lower bounds on
\mathbb{P}_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x]
induced by (
1.7
).
Near-critical estimates have also been derived in
[
CHS23
,
HMS23
]
. In these two papers,
L_{\beta}
is replaced by
(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1/2}
, but we will in fact prove that
L_{\beta}\asymp(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1/2}
(where
\asymp
means that the ratio of the quantities is bounded away from
0
and
\infty
by two constants that are independent of
\beta
), see Corollary
1.4
.

Let us also mention that in the long-range setup, sharp estimates on the full-space two-point function have recently been derived using non-perturbative arguments, see
[
Hut22
,
Hut
]
.

A direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
is the finiteness at criticality of the so-called
triangle diagram
, which plays a central role in the study of the mean-field regime of Bernoulli percolation, see
[
AN84
,
HS90a
,
BA91
,
Sla06
]
. In particular, its finiteness implies that various critical exponents exist and take their mean-field value.

Corollary 1.2

(Finiteness of the triangle diagram)
.

Let
d>6
. There exists
L_{0}=L_{0}(d)>0
such that for every
L\geq L_{0}
,

\nabla(\beta_{c}):=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x]\mathbb{P}_{\beta_{c}}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y]\mathbb{P}_{\beta_{c}}[y\xleftrightarrow{}0]<\infty.

(1.9)

The second main result of this paper provides lower bounds matching the upper bound of Theorem
1.1
. We introduce,

\beta_{0}:=\inf\{\beta\geq 0:\varphi_{\beta}(\{0\})=1\}.

(1.10)

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d>6
. There exist
c,L_{0}
such that for every
L\geq L_{0}
, every
\beta_{0}\leq\beta\leq\beta_{c}
, and every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
with
|x|\leq cL_{\beta}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{L^{d}}\left(\frac{L}{L\vee|x|}\right)^{d-2},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{L^{d}}\left(\frac{L}{L\vee|x_{1}|}\right)^{d-1},

\displaystyle\text{provided that }x_{1}=|x|.

(1.12)

We now describe how to recover the mean-field behaviour of the
susceptibility
defined by

\chi(\beta):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x],

(1.13)

and the
correlation length

\xi_{\beta}
defined by

\xi_{\beta}^{-1}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{}n\mathbf{e}_{1}].

(1.14)

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
d>6
. There exist
c,C,L_{0}
such that for every
L\geq L_{0}
and every
\beta_{0}\leq\beta<\beta_{c}
for which
L_{\beta}\geq L
,

\displaystyle c(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\chi(\beta)\leq C(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1},

(1.15)

\displaystyle cL(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\beta}\leq CL(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1/2}.

(1.16)

Moreover,

cL\frac{(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1/2}}{\log(1/(\beta_{c}-\beta))}\leq\xi_{\beta}\leq CL(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1/2}.

(1.17)

Proof.

Let
d>6
. Let
L_{0}
be given by Corollary
1.2
. It is classical (see
[
AN84
]
) that
\nabla(\beta_{c})<\infty
implies the existence of
c,C>0
such that, for
\beta<\beta_{c}
,

c(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1}\leq\chi(\beta)\leq C(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1}.

(1.18)

Combining Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, we additionally obtain that
\chi(\beta)\asymp(L_{\beta}/L)^{2}
when
\beta_{0}\leq\beta<\beta_{c}
and
L_{\beta}\geq L
. This gives (
1.15
)–(
1.16
).

It remains to observe to prove (
1.17
). We use the same proof as in
[
DCP25
, Theorem 1.6]
: combining (
1.5
) and (
1.14
) readily implies
\xi_{\beta}\leq L_{\beta}
for
\beta<\beta_{c}
; using
[
Gri99
, Proposition 6.47]
implies that
\varphi_{\beta}(\Lambda_{n})\leq C_{1}n^{d-1}e^{-n/\xi_{\beta}}
(where
C_{1}=C_{1}(d)>0
) for every
\beta<\beta_{c}
, and every
n\geq 1
. This gives that
L_{\beta}\leq C_{2}\xi_{\beta}\log\xi_{\beta}
(where
C_{2}=C_{2}(d)>0
) and the result.
∎

1.3
Strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.1

Recall the van den Berg–Kesten (BK) inequality (see
[
Gri99
, Section 2.3]
) stating that for two increasing events (i.e. events that are stable by the action of opening edges)
A
and
B
,

\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[A\circ B]\leq\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[A]\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[B].

(BK)

The strategy of the proof is similar to that in
[
DCP
]
, though fundamental new difficulties arise due to the presence of non-nearest neighbour edges. The starting point is the following inequality, which is a classical consequence of the BK inequality.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\beta>0
,
o\in S\subset\Lambda
, and
x\in\Lambda
. Then,

\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[o\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\leq\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}x]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in S\\
z\in\Lambda\setminus S\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y]p_{yz,\beta}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x].

(1.19)

A similar statement in the context of the Ising model is often referred to as the Simon–Lieb inequality, see
[
Sim80
,
Lie04
]
. For completeness, we include the proof immediately.

Proof.

Considering an open self-avoiding path from
o
to
x
gives

\{o\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x\}\setminus\{o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}x\}\subset\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in S\\
z\in\Lambda\setminus S\end{subarray}}\{o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y\}\circ\{yz\textup{ is open}\}\circ\{z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x\},

(1.20)

which implies the result by applying (
BK
).
∎

For
\beta>0
and
k\geq 0
, define

\psi_{\beta}(\mathbb{H}_{k}):=\sum_{x\in(\partial\mathbb{H}_{k})^{*}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}_{k}\>}x].

(1.21)

We will use a bootstrap argument (following the original idea from
[
Sla87
]
) and prove that an
a priori
estimate on the half-space two-point function can be improved for sufficiently large
L
.
The idea will be to observe that the Simon–Lieb type inequality (
1.19
) can be coupled with a reversed version of the inequality— see Lemma
2.1
below— to provide a good control on
\psi_{\beta}(\mathbb{H}_{k})
, which can be interpreted as an
\ell^{1}
estimate on the half-space two-point function at distance
k
. The point-wise, or
\ell^{\infty}
, half-space bound will follow from a
regularity
estimate which allows to compare two-point functions for points that are close. Finally, we will deduce the full-space estimate from the half-space one (see Lemma
2.3
). The improvement of the a priori estimates will be permitted by classical random walk computations— gathered in Appendix
A
— involving the spread-out random walk defined as follows.

Definition 1.6
.

Define the random walk
(X_{k}^{x})_{k\geq 0}
started at
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and of law
\mathbb{P}^{\rm RW}_{x}
given by the step distribution:

\mathbb{P}^{\rm RW}_{x}[X_{1}^{x}=y]:=J_{xy}.

(1.22)

To implement the scheme described in the previous paragraph, we introduce the following parameter
\beta^{*}
.

Definition 1.7
.

Fix
d\geq 2
,
\mathbf{C}>1
, and
L\geq 1
. We define
\beta^{*}=\beta^{*}(\mathbf{C},L)
to be the largest real number in
[0,2\wedge\beta_{c}]
such that for every
\beta<\beta^{*}
,

\displaystyle\psi_{\beta}(\mathbb{H}_{n})

\displaystyle<\frac{\mathbf{C}}{L}

\displaystyle\forall n\geq 0,

(
\ell^{1}_{\beta}
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x]

\displaystyle<\frac{\mathbf{C}}{L^{d}}\left(\frac{L}{L\vee|x_{1}|}\right)^{d-1}

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{H}^{*}.

(
\ell^{\infty}_{\beta}
)

It might be surprising to require
\beta^{*}\leq 2
. However, we will see that for
d>6
, one has
\beta_{c}=1+O(L^{-d})
as
L
goes to infinity, so this bound is a posteriori harmless. Let us mention that the lace expansion allows to obtain an estimate of the form
\beta_{c}=1+C_{1}L^{-d}+O(L^{-(d+1)})
for an explicit
C_{1}>0
, see
[
HS05
]
.

It could also be, a priori, that
\beta^{*}(\mathbf{C},L)=0
. However, we will see in the lemma below that if
\mathbf{C}
exceeds a large enough constant one may compare
\beta^{*}(\mathbf{C},L)
to
\beta_{0}
defined by

\beta_{0}:=\min\{\beta\geq 0:\varphi_{\beta}(\{0\})=1\},

(1.23)

and obtain that
\beta^{*}(\mathbf{C},L)\geq 1
.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
d>2
. The following properties hold:

(i)

for every
L\geq 1
, one has
1\leq\beta_{0}\leq 2
, and if
\beta\geq\beta_{0}
,

|\Lambda_{L}^{*}|p_{\beta}\geq 1;

(1.24)

(ii)

for every
\mathbf{C}>0
, every
L\geq 1
, and every
\beta\leq\beta^{*}(\mathbf{C},L)\vee\beta_{0}
,

|\Lambda_{L}|p_{\beta}\leq 4;

(1.25)

(iii)

there exists
\mathbf{C}_{\rm RW}\geq 64e^{2}
such that, for every
\mathbf{C}>\mathbf{C}_{\rm RW}
, and every
L\geq 1
,
\beta^{*}(\mathbf{C},L)\geq\beta_{0}
.

Proof.

Let us first prove
(i)
. Using that
|J|=1
and the inequality
1-e^{-x}\leq x
for every
x\geq 0
, we get

1=\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(\{0\})=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1-e^{-\beta_{0}J_{0x}})\leq\beta_{0}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}J_{0x}=\beta_{0}|J|=\beta_{0}.

(1.26)

Moreover, one has that for
x\geq 0
,
1-e^{-x}\geq x-\tfrac{x^{2}}{2}
. Hence,

\varphi_{2}(\{0\})\geq|\Lambda_{L}^{*}|(2c_{L}-2c_{L}^{2})=2-2c_{L}>1,

(1.27)

which implies that
\beta_{0}\leq 2
. The second part of the statement follows from the observation that, if
\beta\geq\beta_{0}
,
|\Lambda_{L}^{*}|p_{\beta}=\varphi_{\beta}(\{0\})\geq\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(\{0\})=1
.

We now turn to the proof of
(ii)
. Note that by definition,
{\beta^{*}(\mathbf{C},L)\leq 2}
. Hence, using
(i)
we obtain that
\beta^{*}(\mathbf{C},L)\vee\beta_{0}\leq 2
. Thus, one has
|\Lambda_{L}^{*}|p_{\beta}=c_{L}^{-1}p_{\beta}\leq\beta\leq 2
when
\beta\leq\beta^{*}(\mathbf{C},L)\vee\beta_{0}
. As a result,

|\Lambda_{L}|p_{\beta}=\frac{|\Lambda_{L}|}{|\Lambda_{L}^{*}|}|\Lambda_{L}^{*}|p_{\beta}\leq 4,

(1.28)

which concludes the proof.

Finally, we prove
(iii)
. Iterating (
1.19
) with
S
a singleton and
\Lambda=\mathbb{H}
gives for
x\neq 0
,

\mathbb{P}_{\beta_{0}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x]\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}^{\rm RW}\Big[\sum_{\ell<\tau}\mathds{1}_{X_{\ell}=x}\Big],

(1.29)

where
\tau
is the exit time of
\mathbb{H}
, that is
\tau:=\inf\{\ell\geq 1:X_{\ell}\notin\mathbb{H}\}
(and more generally,
\tau_{k}
is the exit time of
\mathbb{H}_{k}
).

Using a classical random walk estimate proved in Proposition
A.1
, we obtain the existence of
C_{\rm RW}=C_{\rm RW}(d)>0
such that: for every
L\geq 1
and every
x\in\mathbb{H}^{*}
,

\mathbb{E}_{0}^{\rm RW}\Big[\sum_{\ell<\tau}\mathds{1}_{X_{\ell}=x}\Big]\leq\frac{C_{\rm RW}}{L^{d}}\left(\frac{L}{L\vee|x_{1}|}\right)^{d-1}.

(1.30)

The two previously displayed equations imply that

\mathbb{P}_{\beta_{0}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbb{H}\>}x]\leq\frac{C_{\rm RW}}{L^{d}}\left(\frac{L}{L\vee|x_{1}|}\right)^{d-1}.

(1.31)

Now, let
k\geq 0
. Markov’s property gives that

\varphi^{\rm RW}(\mathbb{H}_{k}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{H}_{k}\\
z\notin\mathbb{H}_{k}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}_{0}^{\rm RW}\Big[\sum_{\ell<\tau_{k}}\mathds{1}_{X_{\ell}=y}\Big]J_{yz}=\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\rm RW}[\tau_{k}<\infty]\leq 1.

(1.32)

Since
p_{\beta,xy}\leq\beta J_{xy}
, we have by (
1.29
) that
\beta_{0}\varphi^{\rm RW}(\mathbb{H}_{k})\geq\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(\mathbb{H}_{k})
. It is possible to compare
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(\mathbb{H}_{k})
to
\psi_{\beta_{0}}(\mathbb{H}_{k})
to obtain that
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(\mathbb{H}_{k})\tfrac{32e^{2}}{L}\geq\psi_{\beta_{0}}(\mathbb{H}_{k})
. The proof of this technical result is postponed to Lemma
3.2
. Collecting all the above and using
(i)
, we obtain

\psi_{\beta_{0}}(\mathbb{H}_{k})\leq\beta_{0}\frac{32e^{2}}{L}\leq\frac{64e^{2}}{L}\leq\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\rm RW}}{L}

(1.33)

if we set
\mathbf{C}_{\rm RW}:=C_{\rm RW}\vee 64e^{2}
. Hence, from (
1.29
)–(
1.33
) and the monotonicity of the two-point function in
\beta
, we have obtained that for every
\mathbf{C}>\mathbf{C}_{\rm RW}
and every
L\geq 1
, one has
\beta^{*}(\mathbf{C},L)\geq\beta_{0}
.

∎

Organisation.

In Lemma
2.3
, we prove that we can estimate the full-space two-point function at
\beta
under the hypothesis that
\beta\leq\beta^{*}
. Thus, our main goal is to show that
\beta^{*}
is in fact equal to
\beta_{c}
provided that
\mathbf{C}
and
L
are large enough. The proof goes in three steps:

•

First, we show that we can improve the
\ell^{1}
bound. We do so by relating
\psi_{\beta}(\mathbb{H}_{n})
to
\varphi_{\beta}(\mathbb{H}_{n})
(see Lemma
3.2
) and then obtaining a bound on
\varphi_{\beta}(\mathbb{H}_{n})
when
\beta<\beta^{*}
that becomes better and better when
L
is chosen larger and larger.

•

Second, we control the gradient of the two-point function (see Proposition
3.6
) and use this estimate to turn the improved
\ell^{1}
bound into an improved
\ell^{\infty}
bound.

•

Third, we show that these improvements imply that
\beta^{*}
cannot be strictly smaller than
\beta_{c}
(we will also derive that
\beta_{c}<2
), since otherwise the improved estimates would remain true for
\beta
slightly larger than
\beta^{*}
, which would contradict the definition of
\beta^{*}
.

Organisation of the paper.

The three steps are implemented in Section
3
. Before this, we describe the reversed Simon–Lieb type inequality in
Section
2
, and explain how one may bound an “error” term appearing in this reversed inequality. The proof of the lower bounds of Theorem
1.3
builds on the results of Section
3
, and is performed in Section
4
. Again, it follows the strategy used in
[
DCP
]
, and relies on a key Harnack-type estimate.

2.1
The inequality

A crucial role will be played by the following inequality, which can be viewed as a
reversed
Simon–Lieb inequality.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
0<\beta<\beta_{c}
,
o\in S\subset\Lambda
, and
x\in\Lambda
. Then,

\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[o\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\geq\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}x]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in S\\
z\in\Lambda\setminus S\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y]p_{yz,\beta}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-E_{\beta}(S,\Lambda,o,x),

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle E_{\beta}(S,\Lambda,o,x):=\sum_{u,v\in S}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in S\\
z\in\Lambda\setminus S\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}u]\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y]p_{yz,\beta}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}v]\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}v]\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

(2.2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in S\\
v\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,s\in S\\
z,t\in\Lambda\setminus S\\
yz\neq st\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}u]\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y]\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}s]p_{yz,\beta}p_{st,\beta}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}v]\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[t\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}v]\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x].

(2.3)

Before diving into the proof of this lemma, let us recall an elementary yet important
tree-bound inequality
from
[
AN84
, Proposition 4.1]
: for every set
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and every
o,a,b\in S
,

\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}a,b]\leq\sum_{u\in S}\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}u]\mathbb{P}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}a]\mathbb{P}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}b].

(2.4)

This bound directly follows from the BK inequality and the observation that for
o
to be connected to both
a
and
b
in
S
, there must exist
u\in S
such that the following event occurs

\{o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}u\}\circ\{u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}a\}\circ\{u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}b\}.

(2.5)

Proof.

We fix
\beta
and drop it from notation (e.g. we write
p_{xy}
instead of
p_{xy,\beta}
). Let

\mathcal{N}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in S\\
z\in\Lambda\setminus S\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}\{o\xleftrightarrow{S}y,\>yz\textup{ is open},\>z\xleftrightarrow{\mathcal{C}^{c}\>}x\},

(2.6)

where
\mathcal{C}
is the (vertex) cluster of
o
in
S
. Clearly,

\{\mathcal{N}\geq 1\}=\{o\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x\}\setminus\{o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}x\}.

Since for
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathds{1}_{t\geq 1}\geq 2t-t^{2}
, we find

\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}x]=\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{N}\geq 1]\geq 2\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}]-(\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}]).

(2.7)

Lower bound on
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}]
.

Write

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in S\\
z\in\Lambda\setminus S\end{subarray}}\sum_{C\ni y}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{C}=C]p_{yz}\mathbb{P}[z\xleftrightarrow{C^{c}\>}x].

(2.8)

Using
[
AN84
, Proposition 5.2]
, we find that

\mathbb{P}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}[z\xleftrightarrow{C^{c}\>}x]\leq\sum_{v\in C}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{A}(z,v)]\mathbb{P}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x],

(2.9)

where
\mathcal{A}(z,v)
is the event that
z
and
v
are connected by a path whose only element in
C
is the last vertex. Combining (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) with the fact that
C\subset S
yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in S\\
z\in\Lambda\setminus S\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y]p_{yz}\mathbb{P}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}]\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in S\\
z\in\Lambda\setminus S\end{subarray}}\sum_{v\in S}\mathbb{P}[\{o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y,v\}\circ\{z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}v\}]p_{yz}\mathbb{P}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x].

(2.10)

Using (
BK
) and (
2.4
) yields,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\{o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y,v\}\circ\{z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}v\}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y,v]\mathbb{P}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}v]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in S}\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}u]\mathbb{P}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}v]\mathbb{P}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y]\mathbb{P}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}v].

(2.11)

Altogether, we obtained,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in S\\
z\in\Lambda\setminus S\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}u]p_{yz}\mathbb{P}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

\displaystyle-\sum_{u,v\in S}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in S\\
z\in\Lambda\setminus S\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}u]\mathbb{P}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y]\mathbb{P}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}v]p_{yz}\mathbb{P}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}v]\mathbb{P}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x].

(2.12)

Upper bound on
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}]
.

Notice that,

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,s\in S\\
z,t\in\Lambda\setminus S\\
yz\neq st\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{S}y,\>yz\textup{ is open},\>z\xleftrightarrow{\mathcal{C}^{c}\>}x,o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}s,\>st\textup{ is open},\>t\xleftrightarrow{\mathcal{C}^{c}\>}x].

(2.13)

Using the BK inequality once again,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,s\in S\\
z,t\in\Lambda\setminus S\\
yz\neq st\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y,s]p_{yz}p_{st}\mathbb{P}[z,t\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in S\\
v\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,s\in S\\
z,t\in\Lambda\setminus S\\
yz\neq st\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}[o\xleftrightarrow{S\>}u]\mathbb{P}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}y]\mathbb{P}[u\xleftrightarrow{S\>}s]p_{yz}p_{st}\mathbb{P}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}v]\mathbb{P}[t\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}v]\mathbb{P}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

(2.14)

where in the second line we used (
2.4
) twice.

Conclusion.

The proof follows by combining (
2.7
), (
2.12
), and (
2.14
). ∎

1.3
. We follow the strategy of
[
DCP
]
. By definition, when
n\leq L_{\beta}-1
, one has
\varphi_{\beta}(\Lambda_{n})\geq e^{-2}
. The first step will be to turn this inequality into a bound of the form
\psi_{\beta}(\mathbb{H}_{n})\geq c/L
, see Lemma
4.5
. This bound can be viewed as an averaged lower bound on the half-space two-point function. In order to turn it into a point-wise lower bound, we will use an improved regularity estimate (compared to Proposition
3.6
)— see Proposition
4.4
— which can be viewed as a Harnack-type inequality. The proof of this result follows the exact same lines as in
[
DCP
]
. The lower bound on the full-space two-point function will follow from the half-space lower bound together with an application of Lemma
2.1
.

In this section, we fix
d>6
, and let
C
and
L_{0}
be given by Proposition
3.9
.

4.1
Preliminaries

We begin the section by stating a minor generalisation of Proposition
3.6
, which will be useful in the proof of the Harnack-type inequality.

Proposition 4.1

(Regularity estimate at mesoscopic scales)
.

For every
\eta>0
, there exist
\delta=\delta(\eta,d)\in(0,1/2)
, and
L_{5}=L_{5}(\eta,d)\geq L_{0}
such that for every
L\geq L_{5}
, every
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
, every
n\geq\delta^{-1}L
, every
\Lambda\supset\Lambda_{3n}
, every
X\subset\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_{3n}
, and every
u,v\in\Lambda_{\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}
,

\left|\sum_{x\in X}\Big(\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big)\right|\leq\eta\max_{w\in\Lambda_{3n}}\sum_{x\in X}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\\
+\delta^{-1}\max_{w\in\Lambda_{3n}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{B}\\
S+w\subset\Lambda_{3n}\end{subarray}}\sum_{x\in X}E_{\beta}(S+w,\Lambda,w,x).

(4.1)

Moreover, if
u,v\in\Lambda_{n}
are such that
|u-v|\leq 2L
, one has

\left|\sum_{x\in X}\Big(\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big)\right|\leq\eta\max_{w\in\Lambda_{3n}}\sum_{x\in X}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\\
+\delta^{-1}\max_{w\in\Lambda_{3n}}\sum_{x\in X}E_{\beta}(\{w\},\Lambda,w,x).

(4.2)

The proof follows the same strategy as Proposition
3.6
except that we keep track of the form of the term on the right-hand side of (
4.1
).

Proof.

Fix
\eta>0
. We let
\delta=\delta(\eta,d)>0
to be chosen small enough later. Also, let
L\geq L_{0}
and
n\geq 1
to be taken large enough, and fix
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
. For simplicity, below, we omit integer rounding.

We prove the result for
X=\{x\}
, but the general case follows similarly. Set
\varphi:=\varphi_{\beta}(\{0\})
. Let
T\geq 1
to be fixed,
n\geq 6TL
, and first assume that
u,v\in\Lambda_{n}
with
|u-v|\leq 2L
. Using a minor generalisation of Lemma
3.8
(with
\mathbb{H}_{n}
replaced by
\Lambda
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

\displaystyle\leq\varphi^{T}\mathbb{E}_{u}^{\rm RW}\Big[\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[X_{T}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big],

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

\displaystyle\geq\varphi^{T}\mathbb{E}_{v}^{\rm RW}\Big[\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[X_{T}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big]-\Big(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\varphi^{t}\Big)\max_{w\in\Lambda_{n+TL}}E_{\beta}(\{w\},\Lambda,w,x).

(4.4)

As in the proof of Proposition
3.6
, Proposition
A.3
implies that there exists
T=T(\eta/4,d)
large enough such that the random walks
X_{T}^{u}
and
X_{T}^{v}
can be coupled to coincide with probability larger than
1-\eta/4
. This observation yields

\mathbb{E}_{u}^{\rm RW}\Big[\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[X_{T}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big]-\mathbb{E}_{v}^{\rm RW}\Big[\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[X_{T}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big]\leq\frac{\eta}{4}\max\Big\{\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]:w\in\Lambda_{n+TL}\Big\}.

(4.5)

Furthermore, since
\varphi\leq 1+\tfrac{C}{L^{d}}
, we may choose
L_{5}=L_{5}(\eta,T,C,d)\geq L_{0}
large enough such that for
L\geq L_{5}
, one has
\varphi^{T}\leq 2
. As a result, we obtained, for every
n\geq 6TL
, and every
u,v\in\Lambda_{n}
with
|u-v|\leq 2L
,

\Big|\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big|\leq\frac{\eta}{4}\max\Big\{\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]:w\in\Lambda_{n+TL}\Big\}\\
+2T\max_{w\in\Lambda_{n+TL}}E_{\beta}(\{w\},\Lambda,w,x).

(4.6)

This gives (
4.2
) as soon as
\delta^{-1}\geq 6T
.

We now assume that
n\geq\delta^{-1}L
, and let
u,v\in\Lambda_{\delta n}
. We prove the result when
u
and
v
differ by one coordinate only and assume that the difference of the coordinate is even as the latter assumption can be relaxed by using the previous estimate when
u
and
v
are at distance 1 (which is less than
2L
), and the former by summing increments over coordinates. Again, by rotating and translating, we may therefore consider
u=k\mathbf{e_{1}}
and
v=-k\mathbf{e}_{1}
with
k\leq\delta n
. Recall that for
\ell\geq 1
,
\Lambda_{\ell}^{+}=\{x\in\Lambda_{\ell}:x_{1}>0\}
, and that
B^{+}:=\Lambda_{n/3}^{+}
and
B^{-}:=-\Lambda_{n/3}^{+}
. By Lemmata
1.5
and
2.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in B^{+}\\
z\notin B^{+}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{B^{+}\>}y]p_{yz,\beta}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x],

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in B^{-}\\
z\notin B^{-}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{B^{-}\>}y]p_{yz,\beta}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-E_{\beta}(B^{-},\Lambda,v,x).

(4.8)

Now, when
z\in H^{\prime}(L)=\{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|v_{2}|\leq L\}
, we may associate every pair
(y,z)
in the sum in (
4.7
) with the pair
(y^{\prime},z^{\prime})
symmetric with respect to the hyperplane
\{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:u_{2}=0\}
in the sum in (
4.8
). Then,
z
and
z^{\prime}
are within a distance
2L
of each other. Hence, if
L\geq L_{5}
, for such pairs
(y,z)
and
(y^{\prime},z^{\prime})
, the difference
\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[z\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[z^{\prime}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]
can be bounded thanks to (
4.6
).

Plugging this estimate in the difference of (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), and then invoking Lemma
3.5
with
k=6\delta n\>(\geq L)
and
n/3
(to the cost of potentially increasing
L
again and decreasing
\delta
so that
6\delta n\leq n/6
), gives

\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\leq\left(\frac{\eta}{2}+4(36\delta)^{c}\right)\max\Big\{\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]:w\in\Lambda_{n+TL}\Big\}\\
+4T\max_{w\in\Lambda_{n+TL}}E_{\beta}(\{w\},\Lambda,w,x)+E_{\beta}(B^{-},\Lambda,v,x),

(4.9)

where we used Proposition
3.9
to argue that
\varphi(B^{+})\leq 1+\frac{C}{L^{d}}\leq 2
. We now choose
\delta
small enough so that
4(36\delta)^{c}\leq\eta/2
and
\delta^{-1}\geq 6T
. Plugging this choice in (
4.9
) gives, for every
u,v\in\Lambda_{\delta n}
,

\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\leq\eta\max\Big\{\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]:w\in\Lambda_{3n}\Big\}\\
+\delta^{-1}\max_{w\in\Lambda_{3n}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{B}\\
S+w\subset\Lambda_{3n}\end{subarray}}E_{\beta}(S+w,\Lambda,w,x),

(4.10)

and concludes the proof.
∎

We conclude these preliminaries with an easy estimate, whose proof is postponed to Appendix
D
. The first part of the statement can be viewed as an
\ell^{1}
version of Lemma
2.5
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists
D_{1}>0
such that, for every
L\geq L_{0}
, and every
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
,

\max_{w\in\mathbb{H}_{n}}\sum_{x\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{n}}{E}_{\beta}(\{w\},\mathbb{H}_{n},w,x)\leq\frac{D_{1}}{L^{d}},\qquad\forall n\geq 0,

(4.11)

and

\max_{x\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{n}}\max_{w\in\mathbb{H}_{n}\setminus\{x\}}E_{\beta}(\{w\},\mathbb{H}_{n},w,x)\leq\frac{D_{1}}{L^{2d}}.

(4.12)

4.2
A Harnack-type estimate

As explained in the beginning of the section, we will need to turn averaged estimates into point-wise ones. We therefore show another regularity estimate which can be viewed as a Harnack-type estimate.

It will be convenient to replace
L_{\beta}
by the quantity
L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)
defined as follows: for
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
,

L_{\beta}(\varepsilon):=\inf\{n\geq 1:\varphi_{\beta}(\Lambda_{n})\leq 1-\varepsilon\}.

(4.13)

In particular, one has
L_{\beta}=L_{\beta}(1-e^{-2})
. In fact,
L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)
only differs from
L_{\beta}
by a constant, as stated in the next lemma. This result already appeared in the context of the WSAW model in our companion paper, see
[
DCP
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
d>6
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1-e^{-2})
. Recall that
L_{0}
is given by Proposition
3.9
. There exists
C_{0}=C_{0}(\varepsilon,L_{0},d)>0
such that, for every
L\geq L_{0}
, and every
\beta<\beta_{c}
,

L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)\leq L_{\beta}\leq C_{0}(L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)\vee L).

(4.14)

Proof.

Let
L\geq L_{0}
and
\beta<\beta_{c}
. Observe that the map
\varepsilon\mapsto L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)
is increasing. Combined with the fact that
L_{\beta}=L_{\beta}(1-e^{-2})
, this gives the first inequality in (
4.14
).

We turn to the second one. Let
k\geq 1
to be chosen large enough. Assume by contradiction that
L_{\beta}>k(L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)+L)
. We iterate Lemma
1.5

k
times with
S
being translates of
\Lambda_{L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)}
and
\Lambda=\Lambda_{L_{\beta}-1}
to obtain that: for every
x\in\Lambda
and
y\notin\Lambda
,

\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]p_{xy,\beta}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}\in\Lambda_{L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)}\\
v_{1}\notin\Lambda_{L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda_{L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)}\>}u_{1}]p_{u_{1}v_{1},\beta}\\
\ldots\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{k}\in\Lambda_{L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)}(v_{k-1})\\
v_{k}\notin\Lambda_{L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)}(v_{k-1})\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v_{k-1}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda_{L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)(v_{k-1})}\>}u_{k}]p_{u_{k}v_{k},\beta}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v_{k}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]p_{xy,\beta}.

(4.15)

Summing the above displayed equation over
x\in\Lambda
and
y\notin\Lambda
gives

\varphi_{\beta}(\Lambda_{L_{\beta}-1})\leq\varphi_{\beta}(\Lambda_{L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)})^{k}\max\{\varphi_{\beta}(\Lambda_{L_{\beta}-1}(x)):x\in\Lambda_{L_{\beta}-1}\}\leq(1-\varepsilon)^{k}\Big(1+\frac{C}{L_{0}^{d}}\Big),

(4.16)

where
C
is given by Proposition
3.9
. Now, choose
k=k(\varepsilon,L_{0},d)
large enough so that
(1-\varepsilon)^{k}(1+C/L_{0}^{d})<e^{-2}
. Since
\varphi_{\beta}(\Lambda_{L_{\beta}-1})\geq e^{-2}
, this choice contradicts the above displayed equation. As a consequence,

L_{\beta}\leq k(L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)+L)\leq 2k(L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)\vee L).

(4.17)

Setting
C_{0}:=2k
concludes the proof.
∎

We now state the main technical estimate of this section.

Proposition 4.4

(Regularity estimate at macroscopic scales)
.

Let
\alpha>0
. There exists
C_{\rm RW}=C_{\rm RW}(\alpha)>0
, and for every
\eta>0
, there exist
A=A(\alpha,\eta)>0
,
L_{6}\geq L_{0}
, and
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\eta,\alpha)>0
such that the following holds. For every
L\geq L_{6}
, every
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, every
n\leq 6L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)
with
n\geq AL
, every
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
, every
\Lambda\supset\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}
, and every
x\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}
,

\displaystyle\max_{w\in\Lambda_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm RW}\min_{w\in\Lambda_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]+\eta\max_{w\in\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

\displaystyle\quad+A\max_{w\in\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{B}\\
S+w\subset\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}\end{subarray}}{E}_{\beta}(S+w,\Lambda,w,x).

(4.18)

The idea of the proof is to introduce a well-chosen rescaled random walk which we now define. Let
\beta>0
and
L\geq 1
. If
m\geq 1
, consider the random walk distribution
(Y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
(started at
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) defined by the following step distribution
\mu=\mu_{m,L,\beta}
: if
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\mathbb{P}^{{\rm RW},m}_{u}[Y_{1}=v]=\mu(v-u):=\frac{\mathds{1}_{v\notin\Lambda_{m-1}(u)}}{\varphi_{\beta}(\Lambda_{m-1})}\sum_{w\in\Lambda_{m-1}(u)}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda_{m-1}(u)\>}w]p_{wv,\beta}.

(4.19)

In particular, when
m=1
,
\mathbb{P}^{{\rm RW},m}_{u}=\mathbb{P}^{\rm RW}_{u}
. Below, we will restrict to the case
m\geq L
. This case corresponds to the situation where
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{m}
, as described in Appendix
A.2
. It will be important for the argument that follows to use estimates involving the above random walk which are uniform in
m
and
L
. These estimates follow from classical random walk analysis, and can be found in the Appendix
A.2
.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Let
\alpha,\eta>0
. Set
M:=\lfloor\tfrac{\alpha}{6}n\rfloor
, and let
C_{\rm RW}
and
N_{1}
be given by Proposition
A.7
applied to
\tfrac{\alpha}{6}
and
\tfrac{\eta}{4}
.
Let
\delta
and
L_{5}
be given by Proposition
4.1
with
\frac{\eta}{4C_{\rm RW}}
and let
L\geq L_{5}
. To the cost of diminishing
\delta
, we additionally assume that

\delta\leq(N_{1}\alpha)^{-1}.

(4.20)

Recall the random walk distribution
\mathbb{P}^{{\rm RW},m}_{u}
introduced above. Let
\tau
be the hitting time of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\Lambda_{n+M}
. If
m=\lfloor(\delta\alpha/36)^{10/9}n\rfloor
, then
n/m\geq N_{1}
by (
4.20
). We further assume that
n\geq(\delta\alpha/36)^{-10/9}(L+1)
so that
m\geq L
and Proposition
A.7
applies. Using this latter result, we get, for every
u,v\in\Lambda_{n}
,

\mathbb{E}^{{\rm RW},m}_{u}\Big[\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[X_{\tau}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big]\leq C_{\rm RW}\mathbb{E}^{{\rm RW},m}_{v}\Big[\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[X_{\tau}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big]+\frac{\eta}{4}\max\{\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]:w\in\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}\}\\
+2C_{\rm RW}\max\Big\{|\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w^{\prime}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]|:w,w^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{\lfloor\delta M\rfloor}(z),\>z\in\partial\Lambda_{n+M}\Big\}

(4.21)

where we used that, for this choice of
M,m
and for
n
large enough, one has:

\displaystyle 3m(n/m)^{1/10}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta\alpha}{12}n\leq\frac{\delta\alpha}{6}n-\delta-1\leq\lfloor\delta M\rfloor,

(4.22)

\displaystyle n+M+2m

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha)n.

(4.23)

Combining (
4.21
) with Corollary
A.6
(applied to
\frac{\eta}{8C_{\rm RW}}
and
A\approx(\alpha\delta)^{-10/9}
) provides
T=T(\eta/(8C_{\rm RW}),\alpha,d)>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(T)
such that
(1+\varepsilon_{0})^{T}\leq 2
, and for every
\varphi\in[1-\varepsilon_{0},1+\varepsilon_{0}]
,

\mathbb{E}_{u}^{{\rm RW},m}\Big[\varphi^{\tau\wedge T}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[X_{\tau\wedge T}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big]\leq C_{\rm RW}\mathbb{E}_{v}^{{\rm RW},m}\Big[\varphi^{\tau\wedge T}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[X_{\tau\wedge T}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big]\\
+\frac{\eta}{2}\max\{\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]:w\in\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}\}\\
+2C_{\rm RW}\max\Big\{|\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w^{\prime}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]|:w,w^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{\lfloor\delta M\rfloor}(z),\>z\in\partial\Lambda_{n+M}\Big\}.

(4.24)

Thanks to Proposition
A.5
, for every
u\in\Lambda_{n}
,

\mathbb{E}_{u}^{{\rm RW},m}[\tau]\leq C_{1}(1+\alpha)^{2}\delta^{-20/9},

(4.25)

for some
C_{1}=C_{1}(d)>0
.
We are now in a position to prove the desired result.

Let
L\geq L_{5}
and
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. By choosing
L
large enough we may additionally assume that
C/L^{d}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
where we recall that
C
is given by Proposition
3.9
. Fix
u,v\in\Lambda_{n}
. By definition of
m
and (
4.20
), we observe that
6m<n
. Since we also have
n\leq 6L_{\beta}(\varepsilon)
, Proposition
3.9
gives

1-\varepsilon\leq\varphi_{\beta}(\Lambda_{m-1})\leq 1+\frac{C}{L^{d}}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{0}.

(4.26)

We now set
\varphi:=\varphi_{\beta}(\Lambda_{m-1})
. Iterating the two bounds of Lemmata
1.5
and
2.1
with
S
being translates of
\Lambda_{m-1}
, and until time
\tau\wedge T
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{u}^{{\rm RW},m}\Big[\varphi^{\tau\wedge T}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[Y_{\tau\wedge T}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big],

(4.27)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{v}^{{\rm RW},m}\Big[\varphi^{\tau\wedge T}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[Y_{\tau\wedge T}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big]-\mathbb{E}_{v}^{{\rm RW},m}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\tau\wedge T-1}\varphi^{t}{E}_{\beta}(\Lambda_{m-1}(Y_{t}),\Lambda,Y_{t},x)\right].

(4.28)

Using that
(1+\varepsilon_{0})^{T}\leq 2
and (
4.25
), we get

\mathbb{E}_{v}^{{\rm RW},m}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\tau\wedge T-1}\varphi^{t}{E}_{\beta}(\Lambda_{m-1}(Y_{t}),\Lambda,Y_{t},x)\right]\\
\leq 2C_{1}(1+\alpha)^{2}\delta^{-20/9}\max_{w\in\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{B}\\
S+w\subset\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}\end{subarray}}{E}_{\beta}(S+w,\Lambda,w,x).

(4.29)

Combining (
4.27
) and (
4.24
) gives

\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\leq C_{\rm RW}\mathbb{E}_{v}^{{\rm RW},m}\Big[\varphi^{\tau\wedge T}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[Y_{\tau\wedge T}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\Big]+\frac{\eta}{2}\max\{\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]:w\in\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}\}\\
+2C_{\rm RW}\max\Big\{|\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w^{\prime}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]|:w,w^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{\lfloor\delta M\rfloor}(z),\>z\in\partial\Lambda_{n+M}\Big\}.

(4.30)

Assume that
M\geq\delta^{-1}L
, which occurs as soon as
n\geq 6\alpha^{-1}\delta^{-1}(L+1)
. By Proposition
4.1
(recall that it is applied to
\tfrac{\eta}{4C_{\rm RW}}
),

2C_{\rm RW}\max\Big\{|\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]-\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w^{\prime}\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]|:w,w^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{\lfloor\delta M\rfloor}(z),\>z\in\partial\Lambda_{n+M}\Big\}\\
\leq\frac{\eta}{2}\max\{\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]:w\in\Lambda_{n+3M}\}+2C_{\rm RW}\delta^{-1}\max_{w\in\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{B}\\
S+w\subset\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}\end{subarray}}{E}_{\beta}(S+w,\Lambda,w,x).

(4.31)

Set
B:=2C_{\rm RW}(C_{1}(1+\alpha)^{2}\delta^{-20/9}+\delta^{-1})
.
Plugging (
4.28
), (
4.29
), and (
4.31
) in (
4.30
) gives that, for every
n\geq\Big((\delta\alpha/36)^{-10/9}\vee 6\alpha^{-1}\delta^{-1}\Big)(L+1)
,

\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]\leq C_{\rm RW}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[v\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]+\eta\max\{\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[w\xleftrightarrow{\Lambda\>}x]:w\in\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}\}\\
+B\max_{w\in\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{B}\\
S+w\subset\Lambda_{(1+\alpha)n}\end{subarray}}{E}_{\beta}(S+w,\Lambda,w,x),

(4.32)

where we used that
n+3M\leq(1+\alpha)n
. Since
u
and
v
are arbitrary in
\Lambda_{n}
, the proof follows readily from setting
A:=B\vee(\delta\alpha/36)^{-10/9}\vee 6\alpha^{-1}\delta^{-1}
.
∎

4.3
Lower bound on
\psi_{\beta}(\mathbb{H}_{n})

We now turn to our basic estimate for this section. It is a lower bound corresponding to the upper bound on
\psi_{\beta}(\mathbb{H}_{n})
obtained in the previous section. Recall that
\beta_{0}
is such that
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(\{0\})=1
. Introduce for
n,k\geq 1

\psi^{[k]}_{\beta}(\mathbb{H}_{n}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\partial\mathbb{H}_{n}\\

(S_{k}^{u_{i}})_{k\geq 0}

\mathsf{P}_{u_{i}}[S_{1}^{u_{i}}=x+u_{i}]=c_{L}(2L+1)^{d-1}\mathds{1}_{1\leq|x|\leq L}+c_{L}\Big((2L+1)^{d-1}-1\Big)\mathds{1}_{x=0},\qquad(x\in\mathbb{Z}).

(A.16)

Thus, it suffices to find a coupling
(V^{u_{1}},V^{v_{1}})\sim\mathsf{P}
of
(S^{u_{1}},S^{v_{1}})
for which, for every
T
large enough
|V^{u}_{T}-V^{v}_{T}|\leq\kappa L
with high probability. The general case then follows by constructing the coupling coordinate by coordinate. Such a coupling can be obtained through the following procedure.

Below, we assume that
L
is large enough so that
\kappa L\geq 2
. The case of the small values of
L
can be treated using
[
LL10
, Lemma 2.4.3]
. Without loss of generality, we also assume that
v_{1}=0
and
2L\geq x:=u_{1}>\kappa L
.

The idea is to find a coupling
(V^{x},V^{0})
of
(S^{x},S^{0})
such that the difference process
V^{x}-V^{0}
is a random walk that makes jumps at a distance at most
\kappa L
, and stops evolving as soon as it enters
[-\kappa L,\kappa L]
. This classical step is done using the
Ornstein coupling
(see for instance
[
Hol12
, (3.3)]
). We describe it fully for sake of completeness. We let
(\omega_{i})_{i\geq 0}
and
(\omega_{i}^{\prime})_{i\geq 0}
be two independent sequences of i.i.d. random variables of law
S_{1}^{0}
. We construct the pair
(V^{x},V^{0})
inductively as follows:

1.

Set
(V^{x}_{0},V^{0}_{0})=(x,0)
.

2.

Let
k\geq 0
and assume that
(V^{x}_{i},V^{0}_{i})_{i\leq k}
is constructed and that
(V^{x}_{i})_{i\leq k}
(resp.
(V^{0}_{i})_{i\leq k}
) has the same law as
(S^{x}_{i})_{i\leq k}
(resp.
(S^{0}_{i})_{i\leq k}
).

(a)

If
|V^{x}_{k}-V^{0}_{k}|\leq\kappa L
, set
V^{x}_{k+1}-V^{x}_{k}=V^{0}_{k+1}-V^{0}_{k}=\omega_{k+1}
.

(b)

If
|V^{x}_{k}-V^{0}_{k}|>\kappa L
, set
V^{x}_{k+1}-V^{x}_{k}=\omega_{k+1}
and
V^{0}_{k+1}-V^{0}_{k}=\omega^{\prime\prime}_{k+1}
, where

\omega^{\prime\prime}_{k+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\omega_{k+1}^{\prime}&\mbox{if }|\omega_{k+1}-\omega_{k+1}^{\prime}|\leq\kappa L,\\
\omega_{k+1}&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(A.17)

We denote by
\mathsf{P}
the law of the pair
(V^{x},V^{0})
then obtained. One can check (see
[
Hol12
]
) that
(V^{x}_{i})_{i\geq 0}
(resp.
(V^{0}_{i})_{i\geq 0}
) has the same law as
(S^{x}_{i})_{i\geq 0}
(resp.
(S^{0}_{i})_{i\geq 0}
). The difference process
\tilde{S}:=V^{x}-V^{0}
is a random walk started at
x
and stopped at
\tilde{\tau}:=\inf\{k\geq 1:\tilde{S}_{k}<\kappa L\}
with increments
\tilde{\xi}
of law
(\omega_{1}-\omega_{1}^{\prime})\mathds{1}_{|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|\leq\kappa L}
. By definition, one has, for each
T\geq 1
,

\mathsf{P}[|V^{x}_{T}-V^{0}_{T}|>\kappa L]\leq\mathsf{P}[\tilde{\tau}>T].

(A.18)

Using (
A.16
), one may check that the renormalised walk
\tilde{S}/(tL)
satisfies the assumptions of
[
LL10
, Theorem 5.1.7]
for a well chosen
t>0
. It then follows that there exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(d,t)>0
such that for every
T\geq 1
,

\mathsf{P}[\tilde{\tau}>T]\leq\frac{C_{2}\cdot(x/(tL))}{\sqrt{T}}\leq\frac{2C_{2}t^{-1}}{\sqrt{T}}.

(A.19)

The result follows from combining the two previously displayed equations and choosing
T
large enough.
∎

A.2

We now turn to a series of estimates holding uniformly among a class of finite-range random walks on

introduced in (

Let us introduce some notations. For every
m\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{P}_{m}
denote the set of measures on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that satisfy the following assumptions: for every

and

be the law of the random walk

on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
started at

with step distribution
\mu
. Observe that for

m^{2}\leq\sigma(\mu,m)^{2}:=\mathbb{E}_{\mu,0}[|X_{1}|_{2}^{2}]\leq 4dm^{2},

(A.20)

where we recall that
|\cdot|_{2}
denotes the standard Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. When clear from context, we omit

from the notation.

Remark A.4
.

If
m\geq L
, we observe that the step distribution of (

\mathcal{P}_{m}
. Moreover, for every

, the step distribution of

considered in the proof of

.

Our first estimate concerns the expected exit time of a large box
\Lambda_{n}
. For
n\geq 1
, set
\tau_{n}:=\inf\{k\geq 0:X_{k}\notin\Lambda_{n}\}
.

Proposition A.5
.

Let
d\geq 1
. For every
n,m\geq 1
with
n\geq m
, every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{m}
, and every
u\in\Lambda_{n}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu,u}[\tau_{n}]\leq 9d\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{2}.

(A.21)

Proof.

Let
n,m\geq 1
with
n\geq m
. Fix
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{m}
and drop it from the notation. Also, let
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})\in\Lambda_{n}
. Let
X^{(i)}
be the projection of
X
onto the
i
-th coordinate. Note that if
X\sim\mathbb{P}_{0}
, the process
X^{(i)}
is a random walk
S
on
\mathbb{Z}
, whose law does not depend on
i
by symmetries of
\mu
. We denote by
\mathbf{P}_{x}
the law of
S
started at
x\in\mathbb{Z}
and observe that, by symmetry of
\mathbb{P}_{0}
, one has
\mathbf{E}_{0}[|S_{1}|^{2}]\geq\tfrac{1}{d}m^{2}
. Introduce
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{n}:=\inf\{k\geq 1:|S_{k}|\geq n+1\}
. Noticing that
\tau_{n}=\inf_{1\leq i\leq d}\inf\{k\geq 1:|X_{k}^{(i)}|\geq n+1\}
, we get

\mathbb{E}_{u}[\tau_{n}]\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\mathbf{E}_{u_{i}}[\boldsymbol{\tau}_{n}].

(A.22)

Following a classical argument, we observe that
(S_{k}^{2}-\mathbf{E}_{0}[|S_{1}|^{2}]k)_{k\geq 0}
is a martingale, and use the stopping time theorem to argue that
\mathbf{E}_{u_{i}}[S^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{n}}-\mathbf{E}_{0}[|S_{1}|^{2}]\boldsymbol{\tau}_{n}]=u_{i}^{2}
, which gives

\mathbf{E}_{u_{i}}[\boldsymbol{\tau}_{n}]\leq(n+2m)^{2}\mathbf{E}_{0}[|S_{1}|^{2}]^{-1}\leq 9d\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{2}.

(A.23)

Plugging (
A.23
) in (
A.22
) yields the result.
∎

We will also need the following result.

Corollary A.6
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
A,\eta>0
. Let
n,m\geq 1
with
(n/m)\leq A
. There exist
T,\varepsilon>0
which depend on
(A,\eta,d)
such that the following holds. For every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{m}
, every
\varphi\in[1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]
, every

, and every

,

\Big|\mathbb{E}_{\mu,u}[\varphi^{\tau_{n}\wedge T}f(X_{\tau_{n}\wedge T})]-\mathbb{E}_{\mu,u}[f(X_{\tau_{n}})]\Big|\leq\eta\max\{f(w):w\in\Lambda_{n+2m}\}.

(A.24)

Proof.

Let
n,m\geq 1
with
(n/m)\leq A
. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{m}
and drop it from the notation.
Let
T\geq 1
to be fixed and set
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(T)>0
such that
(1+\varepsilon)^{T}\leq 1+\eta/4
and
(1-\varepsilon)^{T}\geq 1-\eta/4
. Let

\Big|\mathbb{E}_{u}[\varphi^{\tau_{n}\wedge T}f(X_{\tau_{n}\wedge T})]-\mathbb{E}_{u}[f(X_{\tau_{n}})]\Big|\leq 2\max\{f(w):w\in\Lambda_{n+2m}\}\cdot\Big(\frac{\eta}{4}+\mathbb{P}_{u}[\tau_{n}>T]\Big).

(A.25)

Using Markov’s inequality and Proposition

, we find

\mathbb{P}_{u}[\tau_{n}>T]\leq\frac{9dA^{2}}{T}.

(A.26)

Now, we choose

large enough so that

. Combining (

) and (

) with such

\Big|\mathbb{E}_{u}[\varphi^{\tau_{n}\wedge T}f(X_{\tau_{n}\wedge T})]-\mathbb{E}_{u}[f(X_{\tau_{n}})]\Big|\leq\eta\max\{f(w):w\in\Lambda_{n+2m}\}.

(A.27)

This concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition A.7

(Uniform Harnack-type estimate)

Proof.

We fix

when

Appendix B
Appendix: a convolution estimate

Proposition B.1
.

Let
d>4
and
L\geq 1
. Let
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Assume that there exists
\mathbf{C}>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

f(x)\leq\mathds{1}_{x=0}+\frac{\mathbf{C}}{L^{d}}\left(\frac{L}{L\vee|x|}\right)^{d-2}.

(B.1)

Then, there exists
A=A(\mathbf{C},d)>0
(independent of
L
) such that, for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

(f*f)(x):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(y)f(x-y)\leq A\Big(\mathds{1}_{x=0}+\mathds{1}_{x\neq 0}\frac{1}{L^{4}}{\left(\frac{1}{L\vee|x|}\right)^{d-4}}\Big).

(B.2)

Proof.

Below, the constants
C_{i}
depend on
\mathbf{C}
and
d
only. The case
x=0
follows from the hypothesis that
d>4
. We turn to the case
x\neq 0
, which we split into two sub-cases
|x|\leq 2L
and
|x|>2L
. Note that for
|x|\leq 2L
, it is sufficient to obtain a bound by
C_{0}/L^{d}
since one has

\frac{1}{L^{d}}\leq\frac{2^{d-4}}{L^{4}}\left(\frac{1}{L\vee|x|}\right)^{d-4}.

(B.3)

Case
|x|\leq 2L
.

We write

\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{4L}\setminus\{0,x\}}f(y)f(x-y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:assumption convolution estimate}}}{{\leq}}|\Lambda_{4L}|\frac{\mathbf{C}^{2}}{L^{2d}}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{L^{d}}.

(B.4)

Moreover,

\sum_{y\notin\Lambda_{4L}}f(y)f(x-y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:assumption convolution estimate}}}{{\leq}}\frac{C_{2}}{L^{4}}\sum_{|u|\geq 2L}\frac{1}{|u|^{2d-4}}\leq\frac{C_{3}}{L^{d}}.

(B.5)

The proof in this case follows from the two last displayed equations and the fact that
f(0)f(x)\leq\tfrac{C_{4}}{L^{d}}
.

Case
|x|>2L
.

We start by noticing that

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{|x|/2}\cup\Lambda_{|x|/2}(x)}f(y)f(x-y)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phantom{\eqref{eq:assumption convolution estimate}}}}{{\leq}}{2\Big(\max_{u\in\Lambda_{|x|/2}}f(x+u)\Big)\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{|x|/2}}f(y)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:assumption convolution estimate}}}{{\leq}}\frac{C_{5}}{L^{2}|x|^{d-2}}\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{|x|/2}}f(y)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:assumption convolution estimate}}}{{\leq}}\frac{C_{6}}{L^{2}|x|^{d-2}}\frac{|x|^{2}}{L^{2}}=\frac{C_{6}}{L^{4}|x|^{d-4}}.

(B.6)

Then,

\sum_{y\notin\Lambda_{|x|/2}\cup\Lambda_{|x|/2}(x)}f(y)f(x-y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:assumption convolution estimate}}}{{\leq}}\frac{C_{7}}{L^{4}}\sum_{|u|\geq|x|/2}\frac{1}{|u|^{2d-4}}\leq\frac{C_{8}}{L^{4}|x|^{d-4}}.

(B.7)

The proof follows readily.
∎

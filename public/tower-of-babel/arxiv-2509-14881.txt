Normalized Indexing for Ramification Subgroups
Stephen DeBacker, David Schwein, Cheng-Chiang Tsai

Abstract
. This expository note introduces a normalization of the indexing of the lower and upper numbering ramification subgroups of local class field theory. We then look at how this normalization interacts with base change for Langlands parameters.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11S15, 22E50

Introduction

Suppose
F
is a nonarchimedean local field, and
\bar{F}
is a fixed separable closure of
F
.
In the standard treatment of ramification groups, the valuation is chosen so that the lower numbering subgroups are indexed by integers. In this expository note we fix the unique valuation
\operatorname{val}:\bar{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}
for which
\operatorname{val}(F^{\times})=\mathbb{Z}
, and we use
\operatorname{val}
to define a new indexing of the lower and upper numbering ramification groups. This new indexing seems to be better suited to base change.

Notation

The residue field,
k_{F}
, of
F
has characteristic
p
.

We will assume everywhere, except in
Appendix

B
,
that finite separable extensions of
F
are contained in
\bar{F}
.
For any finite separable extension
E/F
we denote by
R_{E}
the ring of integers of
E
and by
W_{E}
the Weil group of
E
.

Suppose
F\leq E\leq L
is a tower of (finite) separable extensions with
L/E
Galois. We let
\operatorname{I}(L/E)
denote the inertia subgroup of
\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)
. Thus,
L^{\operatorname{I}(L/E)}
is the maximal unramified subextension of
L/E
.

1.
Ramification groups

1.1.
Lower numbering

Definition 1
.

Suppose
L/E
is finite Galois and
\varpi_{L}
is a uniformizer for
L
.

•

For
\sigma\in\operatorname{I}(L/E)

set

0pt_{L/E}(\sigma):=\operatorname{val}\left(\dfrac{\sigma(\varpi_{L})-\varpi_{L}}{\varpi_{L}}\right).

•

For
r\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
we set

\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{r}:=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{I}(L/E)\,:\,0pt_{L/E}(\sigma)\geq r\}.

Remark 2
.

Since
\sigma
lies in the inertia subgroup, the definition of
0pt_{L/E}
is independent of the choice of uniformizer.
Indeed, any other uniformizer is of the form
c\varpi_{L}
for
c\in R_{L}^{\times}
, and the claim follows from the identity

\sigma(c\varpi_{L})-c\varpi_{L}=c(\sigma(\varpi_{L})-\varpi_{L})+\sigma(\varpi_{L})(\sigma(c)-c).

One often makes similar sorts of arguments in proving basic properties of the depth function.

The definition of the ramification groups in lower numbering goes back at least to Hilbert
[
Hil97
, §44]
.

Lemma 3
.

The function
0pt_{L/E}
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

0pt_{L/E}(\sigma^{-1})=0pt_{L/E}(\sigma)
.

(2)

0pt_{L/E}(\sigma\tau)\geq\min\bigl(0pt_{L/E}(\sigma),0pt_{L/E}(\tau)\bigr)
, with equality if
\sigma
and
\tau
have different depths.

(3)

If
K/E
is finite Galois with
E\leq K\leq L
, then
0pt_{L/E}(\tau)=0pt_{L/K}(\tau)
for all
\tau\in\operatorname{I}(L/K)
.

Proof.

The first part follows from the fact that automorphisms preserve valuations, the second part follows from the identity

\sigma\tau(\varpi_{L})-\varpi_{L}=\sigma(\tau(\varpi_{L})-\varpi_{L})+(\sigma(\varpi_{L})-\varpi_{L}),

and the third part is clear.
∎

We define

\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{r+}:=\bigcup_{s>r}\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{s}

and say that
t

jump
in the (lower numbering) filtration if
\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{t}\neq\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{t+}
.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose
L/E
is finite Galois.

(1)

If
t
is a jump in the lower numbering filtration then
t\in\frac{1}{e(L/F)}\cdot\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{0}=\operatorname{I}(L/E)
and
\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{r}=\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{1/e(L/F)}
for
0<r\leq 1/e(L/F)
.

(3)

\bigcap_{r\geq 0}\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{r}
is trivial.

(4)

If
K/E
is finite Galois with
E\leq K\leq L
, then

\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{r}\cap\operatorname{I}(L/K)=\operatorname{I}(L/K)_{r}
.

(5)

If
t\geq s\geq 0
, then
\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{t}\trianglelefteq\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{s}
.

Proof.

The first four parts follow directly from the definitions while the last part follows from
Lemma

.
∎

Given

\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{s:t}:=\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{s}/\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{t}

Definition 10
.

For
\sigma\in\operatorname{I}(L/E)
we let
0pt_{L/E}^{L/K}(\sigma)
denote the maximal depth attained by an element of the coset
\sigma\operatorname{I}(L/K)
. That is,

0pt_{L/E}^{L/K}(\sigma)=\max\{0pt_{L/E}(\sigma\tau)\,|\,\tau\in\operatorname{I}(L/K)\}.

Lemma 11
.

If
\sigma\in\operatorname{I}(L/E)
,
then

0pt_{K/E}(\bar{\sigma})=\sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{I}(L/K)}\min(0pt_{L/E}(\tau),0pt^{L/K}_{L/E}(\sigma)).

Remark 12
.

Here and elsewhere it is useful to remember that for all
\tau\in\operatorname{I}(L/K)
we have
0pt_{L/E}(\tau)=0pt_{L/K}(\tau)
.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that

0pt_{L/E}(\sigma)=0pt_{L/E}^{L/K}(\sigma)
.
Suppose
\tau\in\operatorname{I}(L/K)
.
Either
0pt_{L/E}(\sigma\tau)=0pt_{L/E}^{L/K}(\sigma)

or
0pt_{L/E}(\sigma\tau)<0pt_{L/E}^{L/K}(\sigma)
. In the latter case
0pt_{L/E}(\sigma\tau)=0pt_{L/E}(\tau)
, so

0pt_{L/E}(\sigma\tau)=\min(0pt_{L/E}(\tau),0pt_{L/E}^{L/K}(\sigma)).

The result now follows from Lemma
9
.
∎

Lemma 13
.

Suppose
r\geq 0
. We have
\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{r}\operatorname{I}(L/K)/\operatorname{I}(L/K)=\operatorname{I}(K/E)_{s}
where

s=\sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{I}(L/K)}\min(0pt_{L/E}(\tau),r).

Proof.

Suppose
\sigma\in\operatorname{I}(L/E)
.

We have
\sigma\in\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{r}\operatorname{I}(L/K)
if and only if
0pt_{L/E}^{L/K}(\sigma)\geq r
. Since
0pt_{L/E}(1)=\infty
, the function

x\mapsto\sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{I}(L/K)}\min(0pt_{L/E}(\tau),x)

is strictly increasing. Thus,
0pt_{L/E}^{L/K}(\sigma)\geq r
if and only if

\begin{split}0pt_{K/E}(\bar{\sigma})&=\sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{I}(L/K)}\min(0pt_{L/E}(\tau),0pt^{L/K}_{L/E}(\sigma))\\
&\geq\sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{I}(L/K)}\min(0pt_{L/E}(\tau),r)=s.\end{split}

Since
\bar{\sigma}\in\operatorname{I}(K/E)_{s}
if and only if

0pt_{K/E}(\bar{\sigma})\geq s
, the lemma is proved.
∎

Definition 14
.

For

we define a function

\varphi_{L/E}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{I}(L/E)}\min(0pt_{L/E}(\sigma),x)

.

The function

Lemma 21

If

\varphi_{L/E}=\varphi_{K/E}\circ\varphi_{L/K},\qquad\psi_{L/E}=\psi_{L/K}\circ\psi_{K/E}.

1.3.3.
Example: quaternionic Galois groups

Let
Q=\{\pm 1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}
be the quaternion group with the usual relations.

Our first example of a quaternionic extension is taken from
[
Ser79
, IV.3 Exc. 2]
. Serre shows
[
Ser60
, §4]
that there exist fields
F
,
E
, and
L
such that

(1)

L
is a totally ramified Galois extension of
E
,

(2)

\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)=Q
, and

(3)

\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{4/e(L/F)}
is trivial.

To ease notation, we assume
F=E
.
Since
L/E
is totally ramified, we have
\operatorname{I}(L/E)=\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)
. Thus,
e(L/F)=e(L/E)=8
.

Since
\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{0:0^{+}}
injects into
k_{L}
, it is a cyclic group.
As
Q
is not cyclic, we must therefore have that
\operatorname{I}(L/F)_{0+}
is nontrivial. Since every subgroup and quotient group of
Q
is a two group, we conclude that the characteristic of
k_{F}=k_{E}
is two. This forces
\operatorname{I}(L/F)_{0:0^{+}}\leq k_{F}^{\times}
to have an odd number of elements, hence it must be trivial.
Since the commutator subgroup of
Q
is
Z=\{\pm 1\}
, there are two more jumps. The next jump can’t be at
2/8
or
3/8
: if it were, then we must have

which implies
-1\in\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{4/8+}\leq\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{4/8}=1
, a contradiction. Therefore, the next jump must happen at
1/8
. If the other jump happens at
t/8
, then by Lemma
5
we have that
2
must divide
8\cdot(t/8-1/8)
. Thus, the final jump happens at
3/8
. We therefore have
\operatorname{I}(L/E)_{r}
is
Q
for
0\leq r\leq 1/8
, it is
Z
for
1/8<r\leq 3/8
, and it is

1.5.
Upper numbering for the absolute inertia subgroup

Suppose
E/F
is an extension.
Set

I(E)=\{K\leq\bar{F}\colon\text{$K/E$ is finite and Galois}\}.

If

This defines an inverse system, and the inverse limit is the inertia subgroup

\operatorname{I}_{E}=\operatorname{I}(\bar{F}/E)=\lim_{\longleftarrow}\operatorname{I}(K/E)=\lim_{\longleftarrow}\operatorname{I}(\bar{F}/E)/\operatorname{I}(\bar{F}/K).

This is, by definition, a profinite group. Equivalently,
\operatorname{I}_{E}
is a compact totally disconnected topological group
[
CF67
, II.1.4, Theorem 1]

\{\operatorname{I}(\bar{F}/K)\,|\,K\in I(E)\}

\operatorname{I}_{E}\times\operatorname{I}_{E}
is the preimage of the closed set
\{e\}
under the map
(x,y)\mapsto xy^{-1}
.

Note that the upper numbering filtration is defined so that for all

,

s_{KL}[\operatorname{I}(L/E)^{t}]=\operatorname{I}(K/E)^{t}\text{ and }s_{KL}[\operatorname{I}(L/E)^{t+}]=\operatorname{I}(K/E)^{t+}.

.

These are closed subgroups of

CF67

\Lambda_{K}:=\Lambda_{F}\circ\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}:K\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Since
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}:K\rightarrow F
is surjective, we have that
\Lambda_{K}
is also a nontrivial continuous additive character.
Define

0pt(\Lambda_{K}):=\max\{\operatorname{val}(x)\,|\,x\in K,\;\Lambda_{K}(x)\not=1\}.

Corollary 42
.

0pt(\Lambda_{L})=0pt(\Lambda_{E})+c_{L/E}
.
∎

To finish, and to set up a connection with the norm map
that we develop in the next section,
we explain the relationship between
c_{L/E}

and the Herbrand function.

Lemma 43

(
[
Ser79
, IV.2 Proposition 4]
)
.

c_{L/E}=\sum_{1\neq\sigma\in\operatorname{I}(L/E)}0pt_{L/E}(\sigma)
.
∎

Corollary 44
.

If
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfies
s\geq\ell(L/E)
, then
\varphi_{L/E}(s)=s+c_{L/E}
.

Proof.

Using the definition of
\varphi_{E/F}
(see Definition
14
),
we see that

\begin{split}\varphi_{L/E}(s)-s&=-s+\varphi_{L/E}(s)\\
&=-s+\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{I}(L/E)}\min(0pt_{L/E}(\sigma),s)\\
&=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{I}(L/E)\setminus\{1\}}0pt_{L/E}(\sigma).\qed\end{split}

Remark 45
.

We conclude from Corollary
44
that
c_{L/E}\geq 0
with equality if and only if
L/E
is tamely ramified.

1.7.
Some results about norm maps

If
L/E
is a finite Galois extension, then for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\operatorname{Tr}_{L/E}[L_{\geq s}]=E_{\geq(s+c_{L/E})}.

Since this is true for all
s
, we have a surjection, also denoted
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/E}
,

\operatorname{Tr}_{L/E}\colon L_{=s}\twoheadrightarrow E_{=(s+c_{L/E})}.

There are similar, but more subtle, results for the norm map
\operatorname{Nm}_{L/F}\colon L^{\times}\rightarrow F^{\times}
. For example, thanks to
[
Ser79
, V.2 Proposition 2 and V.6 Proposition 8]
, for all
s\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
we have

\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}[L^{\times}_{\geq s}]=\operatorname{Nm}_{L^{u}/E}[\operatorname{Nm}_{L/L^{u}}[L^{\times}_{\geq s}]]\subseteq\operatorname{Nm}_{L^{u}/E}[L^{u\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/L^{u}}(s)}]=E^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/L^{u}}(s)}.

Here
L^{u}/E
denotes the maximal unramified subextension of
E
in
L
.
Since
\varphi_{L^{u}/E}(t)=t
for all
t\geq 0
, we conclude that
\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}[L^{\times}_{\geq s}]\subseteq E^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/E}(s)}
. Since this is true for all
s\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we have a homomorphism, also denoted
\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}
,

\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}\colon L^{\times}_{=s}\longrightarrow E^{\times}_{=\varphi_{L/E}(s)}.

Lemma 46
.

Suppose
s\geq 0
.

(1)

The homomorphism

\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}\colon L^{\times}_{\geq s}\longrightarrow E^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/E}(s)}

is surjective if and only if either
L
is unramified and
s\geq 0
or
\operatorname{I}(L/E)
is nontrivial and
s>\ell(L/E)
.

(2)

Suppose
s>\ell(L/E)
. If
x\in L_{\geq s}
, then
\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}(1+x)=1+\operatorname{Tr}_{L/E}(x)
modulo
E^{\times}_{>\varphi_{L/E}(s)}
. Since we know from Corollary
44
that
s>\ell(L/E)
implies
\varphi_{L/E}(s)=s+c_{L/E}
, we have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{=s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{=(s+c_{L/E})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{\times}_{=s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\times}_{=(s+c_{L/E})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Tr}_{L/E}}

\scriptstyle\sim

\scriptstyle\sim

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}}

in which the vertical maps are the
x\mapsto 1+x
isomorphisms.

Proof.

Suppose first that
L/E
is unramified. In this case the statements in the Lemma follow from
[
Ser79
, V.2 Propositions 1 and 3]
and their proofs.

Now suppose that
L/E
is totally ramified and cyclic of prime order. Since

\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}\colon L^{\times}_{=\ell(L/E)}\rightarrow E^{\times}_{=\varphi_{L/E}(\ell(L/E))}

is neither injective nor surjective
[
Ser79
, V.3 Proposition 5(iii)]
, it follows that if
s\leq\ell(L/E)
, then
\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}[L^{\times}_{\geq s}]\subsetneq E^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/E}(s)}
. On the other hand, for
s>\ell(L/E)
,
the proof of
[
Ser79
, V.3 Proposition 5(iv)]
shows that Claim
2
of the Lemma holds. It follows that for all
s>\ell(L/E)
we have
\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}[L^{\times}_{\geq s}]=E^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/E}(s)}
.

Suppose now that
L/E
is a totally ramified Galois extension. We shall proceed by induction on
|\operatorname{I}(L/E)|
. If
\operatorname{I}(L/E)
is trivial, then
E=L
and there is nothing to prove. If
\operatorname{I}(L/E)
is not trivial, then since
\operatorname{I}(L/E)
is solvable there is a subextension
K/E
of
L/E
such that
\operatorname{I}(K/E)=\operatorname{I}(L/E)/\operatorname{I}(L/K)
is cyclic of prime order. By induction we know that for all
r>0

(1)

\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}[L^{\times}_{\geq r}]\subseteq K^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/K}(r)}
with equality if and only if
r>\ell(L/K)
and

(2)

if
r>\ell(L/K)
and
x\in L_{\geq r}
, then
\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}(1+x)=1+\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(x)
modulo
K^{\times}_{>\varphi_{L/K}(r)}
.

Since
K/E
is cyclic of prime order, we know that for all
t>0

(1)

\operatorname{Nm}_{K/E}[K^{\times}_{\geq t}]\subseteq E^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{K/E}(t)}
with equality if and only if
t>\ell(K/E)
and

(2)

if
t>\ell(K/E)
and
y\in K_{\geq t}
, then
\operatorname{Nm}_{K/E}(1+y)=1+\operatorname{Tr}_{K/E}(y)
modulo
E^{\times}_{>\varphi_{K/E}(t)}
.

For all
s>0
we have

\begin{split}\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}[L^{\times}_{\geq s}]&=\operatorname{Nm}_{K/E}[\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}[L^{\times}_{\geq s}]]\\
&\subseteq\operatorname{Nm}_{K/E}[K^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/K}(s)}]\\
&\subseteq E^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{K/E}(\varphi_{L/K}(s))}=E^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/E}(s)}\end{split}

with equality if and only if both
s>\ell(L/K)
and
\varphi_{L/K}(s)>\ell(K/E)
. But, from Corollary
20
we know that
s>\ell(L/K)
and
\varphi_{L/K}(s)>\ell(K/E)
if and only if
s>\ell(L/E)
. Thus, Claim
1
holds for totally ramified Galois extensions. Finally, if
r>\ell(L/E)
, then for all
x\in L_{\geq r}
we have

\begin{split}\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}(1+x)&=\operatorname{Nm}_{K/E}(\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}(1+x))\\
&=\operatorname{Nm}_{K/E}(1+\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(x)+z)\qquad\hphantom{+w}\text{ for some $z\in K_{>\varphi_{L/K}(r)}$}\\
&=1+\operatorname{Tr}_{K/E}(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(x)+z)+w\qquad\text{ for some $w\in E_{>\varphi_{K/E}(\varphi_{L/K}(r))}$}\\
&\equiv 1+\operatorname{Tr}_{L/E}(x)\mod{E^{\times}_{>\varphi_{L/E}(r)}},\end{split}

and so Claim
2
holds for totally ramified Galois extensions.

Finally, suppose
L/E
is any finite Galois extension. The case when
L/E
is unramified is handled above, so we may assume
\operatorname{I}(L/E)
is nontrivial. We let
K=L^{\operatorname{I}(L/E)}
be the maximal unramified extension of
E
inside
L
. Since
L/K
is totally ramified with Galois group
\operatorname{I}(L/K)=\operatorname{I}(L/E)
and
\varphi_{L/E}=\varphi_{L/K}
, from our results above we have that for all
s>0

\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}[L^{\times}_{\geq s}]=\operatorname{Nm}_{K/E}[\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}[L^{\times}_{\geq s}]]\subseteq\operatorname{Nm}_{K/E}[K^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/K}(s)}]=E^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/K}(s)}=E^{\times}_{\geq\varphi_{L/E}(s)}

with equality if and only if
s>\ell(L/K)=\ell(L/E)
. For
r>\ell(L/E)
our results above similarly yield
\operatorname{Nm}_{L/E}(1+x)\equiv 1+\operatorname{Tr}_{L/E}(x)\mod{E^{\times}_{>\varphi_{L/E}(r)}}
.
∎

Remark 49

(Norm map for non-Galois extensions)

Lemma 50
.

Suppose
r\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. We have

\operatorname{I}_{E}^{r}\cap\operatorname{I}_{L}^{0}=\operatorname{I}_{L}^{\psi_{L/E}(r)}\text{ and }\operatorname{I}_{E}^{r+}\cap\operatorname{I}_{L}^{0}=\operatorname{I}_{L}^{\psi_{L/E}(r)+}.

Moreover, if
\operatorname{I}_{E}^{r}\leq\operatorname{I}^{0}_{L}
, then for all
s\geq r
we have

(1)

\operatorname{I}_{L}^{\psi_{L/E}(s)}=\operatorname{I}_{E}^{s}
and

(2)

\psi_{L/E}(s)-s=\psi_{L/E}(r)-r
.

Proof.

Suppose
M/E
is a finite Galois extension such that
L\leq M
.

For the first statement, it will be enough to show that
\operatorname{I}(M/L)^{\psi_{L/E}(s)}=\operatorname{I}(M/E)^{s}\cap\operatorname{I}(M/L)
for all
s\geq r
. Suppose
s\geq r
. We have

\begin{split}\operatorname{I}(M/E)^{s}\cap\operatorname{I}(M/L)&=\operatorname{I}(M/E)_{\psi_{M/E}(s)}\cap\operatorname{I}(M/L)=\operatorname{I}(M/L)_{\psi_{M/E}(s)}\\
&=\operatorname{I}(M/L)^{\varphi_{M/L}(\psi_{M/E}(s))}=\operatorname{I}(M/L)^{\psi_{L/E}(s)}.\end{split}

We now assume
\operatorname{I}_{E}^{r}\leq\operatorname{I}_{L}^{0}
and turn to the remaining statements. From the computations above we have

\operatorname{I}(M/E)^{s}=\operatorname{I}(M/E)^{s}\cap\operatorname{I}(M/L)=\operatorname{I}(M/L)^{\psi_{L/E}(s)}.

For the second statement, note that
for all
t\geq\psi_{M/E}(r)
we have

\operatorname{I}(M/E)_{t}=\operatorname{I}(M/E)^{\varphi_{M/E}(t)}=\operatorname{I}(M/L)^{\psi_{L/E}(\varphi_{M/E}(t))}=\operatorname{I}(M/L)^{\varphi_{M/L}(t)}=\operatorname{I}(M/L)_{t}.

Thus

\begin{split}\psi_{L/E}(s)-s&=\varphi_{M/L}(\psi_{M/E}(s))-\varphi_{M/E}(\psi_{M/E}(s))\\
&=\varphi_{M/L}(\psi_{M/E}(r))+\int_{\psi_{M/E}(r)}^{\psi_{M/E}(s)}\big|\operatorname{I}(M/L)_{t}\big|\,dt\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt-\Bigr(\varphi_{M/E}(\psi_{M/E}(r))+\int_{\psi_{M/E}(r)}^{\psi_{M/E}(s)}\big|\operatorname{I}(M/E)_{t}\big|\,dt\Bigl)\\
&=\varphi_{M/L}(\psi_{M/E}(r))-\varphi_{M/E}(\psi_{M/E}(r))\\
&=\psi_{L/E}(r)-r.\qed\end{split}

2.1.

Suppose

2.4.
The Local Langlands Correspondence and depth for induced tori

Let
E/F
be a finite Galois extension.

Set

, the Weil restriction of scalars of

[

, V.1 Exc. 11]

Appendix B
Non-Galois extensions

Here we briefly explain how most of the basic definitions
of ramification theory can be extended to arbitrary finite
separable extensions, not necessarily Galois.
We leave it as an exercise to the reader
to fully work out the generalization in this setting.
The appendix to
[
Del84
]
has many steps in this direction as well.

Let
E/F
be a finite separable extension
and let
\bar{E}
be a separable closure of
E
.
Let
L/E
be a finite separable extension
which, in this
appendix
,
we do not necessarily assume is contained in
\bar{E}
.
As before, let
\operatorname{val}
be the unique valuation

\operatorname{val}\colon\bar{E}\to\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}
such that
\operatorname{val}(F^{\times})=\mathbb{Z}
.

When
L/E
is not Galois, we can simply define the Herbrand functions
by reducing to the Galois case:
choose a finite Galois extension
\tilde{L}/E
containing
L
and set

\varphi_{L/E}:=\varphi_{\tilde{L}/E}\circ\varphi^{-1}_{\tilde{L}/L}.

Alternatively, and perhaps more conceptually,
it is possible to extend our basic definitions
to non-Galois extensions directly,
rather than first passing through the Galois case, as follows.

Much of Galois theory can be extended to separable,
non-Galois extensions by replacing the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)

with the
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{E}/E)
-set

\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg}}(L,\bar{E})
of
E
-algebra embeddings
L\to\bar{E}
.
This is the perspective of Grothendieck’s Galois theory
[
SGA1
]
–
see
[
Len06
]
for a textbook treatment –
whose simplest manifestation is the basic fact that

\big|{\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg}}}(L,\bar{E})\big|=[L:E].

Moreover, among the discrete
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{E}/E)
-sets,
the transitive sets are precisely those isomorphic to
\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg}}(L,\bar{E})

for some finite separable extension
L/E
.
This relationship connects ideas from Galois theory
to the category of discrete
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{E}/E)
-sets,
a connection that we will extend to ramification theory.

Restricting this analogy to
\operatorname{I}(L/E)
rather than
\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)
is possible,
but slightly awkward.
To do so, we fix an embedding
\iota\colon k_{L}\to k_{\bar{E}}

and define
\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg},\iota}(L,\bar{E})

to be the subset of
\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg}}(L,\bar{E})

of maps inducing
\iota
on residue fields.
Writing
\operatorname{I}_{E}
for the inertia subgroup of
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{E}/E)
,
the natural action of
\operatorname{I}_{E}
on
\bar{E}
makes

\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg},\iota}(L,\bar{E})
into a discrete transitive
\operatorname{I}_{E}
-set.
The action of
\operatorname{Aut}(L/E)
on
L

does not induce an action of
\operatorname{Aut}(L/E)
on
\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg},\iota}(L,\bar{E})
,
simply because if
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(L/E)

induces the automorphism
\bar{\sigma}\in\operatorname{Gal}(k_{L}/k_{E})
,
then
\sigma
will map
\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg},\iota}(L,\bar{E})

to
\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg},\iota\circ\bar{\sigma}}(L,\bar{E})
.
However, the inertia subgroup

\operatorname{Aut}_{0}(L/E):=\ker\bigl(\operatorname{Aut}(L/E)\to\operatorname{Gal}(k_{L}/k_{E})\bigr)

does act on
\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg},\iota\circ\bar{\sigma}}(L,\bar{E})
,
since it preserves
\iota
,
and in fact,
\operatorname{Aut}_{0}(L/E)
is the group of

\operatorname{I}_{E}
-equivariant automorphisms of

\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg},\iota\circ\bar{\sigma}}(L,\bar{E})
.

Given
\lambda,\mu\in\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg},\iota}(L,\bar{E})
, define

0pt_{L/E}(\lambda,\mu):=\operatorname{val}\left(\frac{\lambda(\varpi_{L})-\mu(\varpi_{L})}{\lambda(\varpi_{L})}\right).

The non-inertial version of this definition is due to Weil

[
Wei95
, page 148]
.
To compare this notation with its previous meaning,
suppose
L/E
is Galois with
L\subseteq\bar{E}
.
If we write
\lambda^{-1}\mu
for the unique element of

\operatorname{I}(L/E)
such that
\lambda\circ(\lambda^{-1}\mu)=\mu
, then

0pt_{L/E}(\lambda,\mu)=0pt_{L/E}(\lambda^{-1}\mu)
.

The function
0pt_{L/E}
satisfies the following properties for all
\lambda,\mu,\tau\in\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg},\iota}(L,\bar{E})
,
generalizing
Lemma

3
:

(0)

0pt_{L/E}(\lambda,\mu)=\infty
if and only if
\lambda=\mu
.

(1)

0pt_{L/E}(\lambda,\mu)=0pt_{L/E}(\mu,\lambda)
.

(2)

0pt_{L/E}(\lambda,\mu)\geq\min\bigl(0pt_{L/E}(\lambda,\tau),0pt_{L/E}(\tau,\mu)\bigr)

It follows that the formula

(1)

d(\lambda,\mu):=p^{-0pt_{L/E}(\lambda,\mu)}

defines an ultra metric on
\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg}}(L,\bar{E})
.

This construction extends to any discrete
\operatorname{I}_{E}
-set
X
as follows.
The function
0pt_{L/E}
is
\operatorname{I}_{E}\times\operatorname{Aut}_{0}(L/E)
-invariant
in the sense that for every
\sigma\in\operatorname{I}_{E}
and
\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}_{0}(L/E)
,

(2)

0pt_{L/E}(\lambda,\mu)=0pt_{L/E}(\sigma\circ\lambda\circ\alpha,\sigma\circ\mu\circ\alpha).

When
\operatorname{I}_{E}
acts transitively on
X
, there is some
L/E

for which there exists a
\operatorname{I}_{E}
-equivariant isomorphism

i\colon X\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg},\iota}(L,\bar{E})
.
We can use this isomorphism to define a depth function on
X
by

0pt_{X}(x,y):=0pt_{L/E}(i(x),i(y)).

Using (
2
),
we see that this definition is independent of the choice of
i
.
For a general discrete
X
, we decompose
X
uniquely as a disjoint union

X=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}X_{i}
of homogeneous
\operatorname{I}_{E}
-sets
X_{i}
,
choose a negative constant such as
-1
,
and define a depth function on
X
as follows,
where
x_{i}\in X_{i}
and
y_{j}\in X_{j}
:

0pt_{X}(x_{i},y_{j}):=\begin{cases}0pt_{X_{i}}(x_{i},y_{j})&\textnormal{if }i=j\\
-1&\textnormal{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

Mimicking (
1
), for
x,y\in X
we set
d(x,y):=p^{-0pt_{X}(x,y)}
.
In this way we have an
\operatorname{I}_{E}\times\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{I}_{E}}(X)
-invariant
metric on any discrete
\operatorname{I}_{E}
-set
X
.

One advantage of extending ramification theory to non-Galois extensions
is that it gives a new interpretation of the depth function as a distribution on
\operatorname{I}_{E}
, see
[
Wei95
, page 152]
.

Lemma 76

(Weil)
.

The following assignment on basic open sets of
\operatorname{I}_{E}

extends to a distribution on
\operatorname{I}_{E}
:

H(1_{\sigma\operatorname{I}_{L}})=\begin{cases}-c_{L/E}&\textnormal{if $\sigma\in\operatorname{I}_{L}$}\\
0pt_{\operatorname{I}_{E}/\operatorname{I}_{L}}(\sigma\operatorname{I}_{L},\operatorname{I}_{L})&\textnormal{if $\sigma\notin\operatorname{I}_{L}$.}\end{cases}

Here
L
ranges over finite subextensions of
\bar{E}/E
and
\sigma\operatorname{I}_{L}\in\operatorname{I}_{E}/\operatorname{I}_{L}
.

In other words, to spell out the definitions above in this particular case,
the tautological embedding
\textnormal{taut}\colon L\hookrightarrow\bar{E}

induces an identification between

\operatorname{I}_{E}/\operatorname{I}_{L}
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{E\textnormal{-alg},\iota}(L,\bar{E})
,
and if
\sigma\operatorname{I}_{L}
corresponds to
\mu\colon L\to\bar{E}
,
then
0pt_{\operatorname{I}_{E}/\operatorname{I}_{L}}(\sigma\operatorname{I}_{L},\operatorname{I}_{L})=0pt_{L/E}(\mu,\textnormal{taut})
.

Proof.

Let
L/K/E
be a tower of finite separable extensions contained in
\bar{E}

and suppose the coset
\tau\operatorname{I}_{K}\in\operatorname{I}_{E}/\operatorname{I}_{K}
decomposes into
\operatorname{I}_{L}
-cosets as

\tau\operatorname{I}_{K}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}\operatorname{I}_{L}
.
We need to show that

H(1_{\tau\operatorname{I}_{K}})=\sum_{i\in I}H(1_{\sigma_{i}\operatorname{I}_{L}}).

If
\tau\notin\operatorname{I}_{K}
, then this claim amounts to
a generalization of
Lemma

9
,

0pt_{\operatorname{I}_{E}/\operatorname{I}_{K}}(\tau\operatorname{I}_{K},\operatorname{I}_{K})=\sum_{i\in I}0pt_{\operatorname{I}_{E}/\operatorname{I}_{L}}(\sigma_{i}\operatorname{I}_{L},\operatorname{I}_{L}),

which is
[
Wei95
, (11), page 152]
up to our renormalization.
If
\tau\in\operatorname{I}_{K}
, then this claim amounts to the equation

-c_{K/E}=-c_{L/E}+\sum_{i\in I,\,\sigma_{i}\notin\operatorname{I}_{L}}0pt_{\operatorname{I}_{E}/\operatorname{I}_{L}}(\sigma_{i}\operatorname{I}_{L},\operatorname{I}_{L}),

or, by the additivity of
c
(see
Remark

39
),
the equation

\sum_{i\in I,\,\sigma_{i}\notin\operatorname{I}_{L}}0pt_{\operatorname{I}_{E}/\operatorname{I}_{L}}(\sigma_{i}\operatorname{I}_{L},\operatorname{I}_{L})=c_{L/K}.

This generalization of
Lemma

43
follows from
[
Wei95
, (9), page 149]
.
∎

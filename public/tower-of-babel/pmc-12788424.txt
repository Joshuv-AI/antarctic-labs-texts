Teeth Preservation Esthetically through Unison of Periodontology, Endodontics, and Prosthodontics: An Original Research Study
Authors: Reddi Narendra, Thandra Nagasruthi, Gujjalapudi Mahalakshmi, Amarendhar Reddy Tummala, Swati Solanki, Shreya C. Patil, Sovesh Das
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Tooth preservation remains the cornerstone of modern dentistry, especially in esthetically critical regions. Multidisciplinary approaches combining periodontology, endodontics, and prosthodontics have demonstrated potential in preserving natural dentition with optimal esthetic outcomes.Methods:A prospective clinical study was conducted on 60 patients (aged between 25 and 55 years) presenting with anterior teeth exhibiting periodontal pockets (>5 mm), endodontic involvement, and structural damage. Patients were randomly assigned into two groups: Group A (multidisciplinary treatment) and Group B (conventional extraction and implant-supported prosthesis). Esthetic outcomes were assessed using the Pink Esthetic Score (PES) and White Esthetic Score (WES), while patient satisfaction was recorded using a 10-point visual analogue scale (VAS). Measurements were taken at baseline, six months, and 12 months.Results:Group A demonstrated significant improvement in PES (mean increase from 5.6 ± 1.2 to 8.4 ± 0.9, P < 0.001) and WES (6.1 ± 1.4 to 9.2 ± 1.1, P < 0.001) compared to Group B (PES: 6.0 ± 1.0 to 7.2 ± 1.3; WES: 6.3 ± 1.2 to 7.5 ± 1.0). Patient satisfaction was also higher in Group A at 12 months (VAS: 9.1 ± 0.6 vs. 7.5 ± 1.1, P < 0.01).Conclusion:The interdisciplinary approach demonstrated superior esthetic results and patient-reported satisfaction in anterior tooth preservation. Collaborative treatment planning among specialists in periodontology, endodontics, and prosthodontics is essential for successful esthetic rehabilitation.

INTRODUCTION

Esthetic rehabilitation in dentistry is increasingly patient-driven, particularly for anterior teeth, where function and appearance are equally prioritized. While dental implants offer predictable outcomes, the preservation of natural teeth remains the most conservative and biologically advantageous option.[1] With advancements in clinical protocols, teeth once considered non-salvageable can now be restored with interdisciplinary strategies combining periodontology, endodontics, and prosthodontics.[2]

Periodontal therapy contributes to soft and hard tissue stability, endodontic treatment addresses pulpal pathologies, and prosthodontic rehabilitation restores form and function. This synergistic approach emphasizes minimal invasiveness and long-term success.[3] Several clinical reports support the integrated model for complex dental cases; however, comprehensive research evaluating the esthetic benefits and patient satisfaction remains limited.[4]

Recent studies have suggested that interdisciplinary collaboration improves clinical outcomes, yet the esthetic impact of such strategies, particularly in the anterior maxillary region, lacks robust quantitative data.[5] This gap underscores the need for clinical investigations focused on esthetic indices, functional longevity, and patient-centered outcomes.

Procedures

Group A received scaling and root planing, followed by periodontal flap surgery with bone grafting (DFDBA) and membrane placement. Root canal therapy was completed, and after six weeks of healing, crowns were fabricated using lithium disilicate ceramics.

Group B underwent atraumatic extraction and immediate implant placement with guided bone regeneration. Restoration was completed after three months using screw-retained ceramic crowns.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS v25.0. Paired and unpaired t-tests were applied. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

This study demonstrates the clinical efficacy of integrating periodontology, endodontics, and prosthodontics in preserving anterior teeth with improved esthetic and patient-reported outcomes. Group A patients displayed significantly better PES and WES scores compared to Group B, supporting the advantages of natural tooth retention in esthetic zones.[6]

Our findings align with those of Avila-Ortiz et al.,[7] who emphasized the importance of interdisciplinary planning for predictable esthetic results. Moreover, Chen et al.[8] noted that regenerated periodontal tissues and minimally invasive endodontics enhance long-term outcomes. The significant reduction in PD and CAL observed in Group A further supports periodontal regeneration as a critical step in multidisciplinary rehabilitation.[9]

While implant therapy offers reliable outcomes, it may fall short in soft tissue esthetics, especially in high-smile line cases.[10] Preserving the periodontium and papillae, as observed in this study, provides superior pink esthetics compared to peri-implant soft tissue responses.[11]

Patient satisfaction was higher in the preservation group, likely due to a more natural look and avoidance of surgery. Similar findings were reported by Tsukiyama et al.,[12] highlighting the psychological benefits of maintaining one’s own dentition.

Despite the promising results, this study had limitations, including a relatively short follow-up duration and a focus on anterior teeth. Further multicenter randomized controlled trials are warranted to validate long-term outcomes.

CONCLUSION

The combined interdisciplinary approach of periodontology, endodontics, and prosthodontics offers superior esthetic and functional results compared to conventional implant therapy for compromised anterior teeth. Preserving the natural dentition, when feasible, leads to higher patient satisfaction and improved soft tissue esthetics, reinforcing the value of collaborative dental care.

ClusterTalk: Corpus Exploration Framework using Multi-Dimensional Exploratory Search
Ashish Chouhan, Saifeldin Mandour, Michael Gertz

Abstract
Exploratory search of large text corpora is essential in domains like biomedical research, where large amounts of research literature are continuously generated. This paper presents ClusterTalk 1 1 1 The demo video and source code are available at: https://github.com/achouhan93/ClusterTalk , a framework for corpus exploration using multi-dimensional exploratory search. Our system integrates document clustering with faceted search, allowing users to interactively refine their exploration and ask corpus and document-level queries. Compared to traditional one-dimensional search approaches like keyword search or clustering, this system improves the discoverability of information by encouraging a deeper interaction with the corpus. We demonstrate the functionality of the ClusterTalk framework based on four million PubMed abstracts for the four-year time frame.

ClusterTalk Frontend.

Figure
1
shows the
ClusterTalk
interface designed to provide users with an interactive experience for exploring the corpus and obtaining answers to their queries.

Users are presented with a visualization of the topic clusters derived from the abstracts by reducing the embedding dimensionality to two dimensions. This visualization helps to understand the thematic organization of the corpus at a glance. The
ClusterTalk
interface offers faceted search capabilities, allowing users to filter and refine their exploration based on multiple criteria. Document filtering is based on publication date ranges (temporal analysis) and keyword search within abstracts and titles, enabling users to locate documents containing specific terms of interest. Users are able to select one or more clusters to explore a particular topic of interest within the corpus.

After refining the document selection, users can pose document-level questions about the selected documents. For instance, they might ask, “Which treatment options are available for cancer?”. Corpus-level questions, i.e., about the entire corpus or for selected cluster(s) questions like “Which topics are covered in the Cancer Treatment cluster?” are also supported.

3
Discussion

In this paper, we presented an exploratory search system that enhances corpus exploration by combining document clustering with faceted search and answering questions to enable users to navigate corpora more efficiently and interactively. For instance, medical researchers can use
ClusterTalk
to discover and explore emerging trends in specific areas for example, lung cancer, by navigating the ‘Cancer Treatment’ cluster and filtering about publication dates and keywords. Additionally, the question answering feature enables users to obtain specific information from selected documents, making the research work more accessible.

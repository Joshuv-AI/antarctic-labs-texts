Insertion Correcting Capability for Quantum Deletion-Correcting Codes Preprint: APS/123-QED
Ken Nakamura, Takayuki Nozaki

Abstract
This paper proves that any quantum t t -deletion-correcting codes also correct a total of t t insertion and deletion errors under a certain condition. Here, this condition is that a set of quantum states is defined as a quantum error-correcting code if the error spheres of its states are disjoint, as classical coding theory. In addition, this paper proposes the quantum indel distance and describes insertion and deletion errors correcting capability of quantum codes by this distance.



I
Introduction

In classical coding theory,
a classical insertion error adds a new symbol to a sequence
and
a classical deletion error loses a symbol of a sequence.
A set of sequences
after
classical deletion/insertion errors to a sequence
\bm{c}

is called
a
deletion/insertion sphere centered at
\bm{c}
.

Classical deletion/insertion-correcting codes
are defined by
the sets of sequences
such that
deletion/insertion spheres centered at their elements
are disjoint.
It is known that
a code
C
corrects
t
classical deletion errors
if and only if

C
corrects a total of
t
classical deletion and insertion errors

8
.

In quantum coding theory,
deletion and insertion errors are defined as
classical coding theory;
namely,
an insertion error adds a new qudit to a multi-qudit system
and
a deletion error loses a qudit of a multi-qudit system.
In previous research,
the quantum deletion-correcting codes

1
;
11
;
3

correct insertion errors

2
;
12
;
14
.
In other words,
these quantum deletion-correcting codes
are also
quantum insertion-correcting codes.
However,
it is an open problem
10
;
4

that
for a quantum code
\mathcal{C}
,
the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{C}
corrects

t
deletion errors.

(ii)

\mathcal{C}
corrects
a total of
t
deletion and insertion errors.

To solve this problem,
Shibayama et al.
focused on the Knill-Laflamme (KL) condition.
They
showed that
KL condition for
t
deletion errors and
that for
t

separable insertion errors

are
equivalent
16
.
Here,
a separable insertion error is
an insertion error where
there is no entanglement
between the original multi-qudit system and the inserted qudit.
It is known that
insertion errors
are limited to
separable insertion errors
if
the original multi-qudit system is pure
15
.
Furthermore,
a quantum code
\mathcal{C}
satisfies
the KL condition for deletion/insertion errors
if and only if
there is
a recovery operator of
\mathcal{C}

17
.
In other words,
Shibayama et al.
16
;
17
proved that
a quantum code
\mathcal{C}
corrects

t
deletion errors
if and only if

\mathcal{C}
corrects a total of
t
deletion and separable insertion errors.
In particular,
a quantum code
\mathcal{C}
corrects
single-deletion errors
if and only if

\mathcal{C}
corrects single-insertion errors.

There
are
two issues
in
16
;
17
.
A first issue
is
to limit insertion errors to separable insertion errors.
In the case of
composite errors consisting of deletions and insertions,
any insertion error is not necessarily limited to
separable insertion error
since deletion/insertion errors
may change to mixed states from pure states.
Hence,
the discussion in
17

was limited to single-deletion/insertion errors
and
this result is not applied to composite errors.
Furthermore,
separable insertion errors
are
strong assumptions
since
deletion and insertion errors are non-commutative
4
,
deletion and separable insertion errors are not.
A second issue is that
the KL condition for deletion/insertion errors in
16
;
17

is not
suitable
to discuss
the deletion/insertion correcting capability.
More precisely,
this KL condition
also requires
the orthogonality of the basis after deletion/insertion errors
for distinct error positions.
Indeed,
there exist
a quantum code
5

such that
the basis after single-deletion error
is
orthogonal
but
distinct codewords
change
to
the same state
by this error;
namely,
this code
satisfies
the orthogonality of the basis after single-deletion
but
cannot correct this error.
From the above,
the condition for the deletion/insertion correcting capability
also requires that
distinct codewords
do not change
to
the same state
by deletion/insertion errors.

To solve the open problem and these issues,
we focus on the deletion/insertion sphere.
Here,
the deletion/insertion sphere
is defined as classical coding theory;
namely,
the deletion/insertion sphere centered at a quantum state
\rho

is
a set of quantum states
after
deletion/insertion errors to
\rho
.

The main theorem (Theorem
4
) in this paper
is that
any quantum
t
-deletion-correcting codes
also correct a total of
t
insertion and deletion errors,
where
a quantum deletion/insertion-correcting code
is defined
by
a set of quantum states
such that
the deletion/insertion spheres of its states are disjoint,
similar to classical coding theory.
The main theorem is derived

3
) that
any composite errors consisting of
t
-insertion and
s
-deletion errors
are in
an error where
t
-insertion errors occur after
s
-deletion errors.

In addition,
this paper explicitly provides
quantum states after insertion error to mixed states.
The previous research
15

explicitly provided
quantum states after insertion error to pure states.
This result is not applied
in cases of composite errors consisting of insertions and deletions
since
deletion/insertion errors
may change
to mixed states
from pure states.
By generalizing the result in
15
,
this paper explicitly provides
quantum states after insertion error to mixed states.
From this,
we can calculate
quantum states after insertion error
and
the set of its quantum state.
By using this result,
this paper gives

(i)

an example of the main theorem and Theorem
3
,

(ii)

a set of quantum states
such that
the set corrects single-insertion
but
not a single-deletion.

This

In other words,

From the main theorem and Theorem
3
,
this paper
defines

and
characterizes the insertion and deletion errors correcting capability of quantum codes
by this distance.
The Hamming distance
6
,
the indel distance
8
, and
the distance of a stabilizer code
13

bit-flip errors,

and

To characterize insertion and deletion errors correcting capability

this paper
defines

By this distance,

proves that

\mathcal{X}

t

V
Deletion Correcting Codes Correct Insertion Errors

This paper employs the following definition of the error correcting capability
similar to classical coding theory.

Definition 1
.

For
\mathcal{X}\subset S(\mathbb{C}^{l\otimes n})
and a quantum error
\mathcal{E}
,
we say that
\mathcal{X}
corrects
\mathcal{E}

if
\mathcal{X}
satisfies

\displaystyle\forall\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{X}~\rho_{1}\neq\rho_{2}\Rightarrow\mathcal{E}(\rho_{1})\cap\mathcal{E}(\rho_{2})=\emptyset.

V.1
Properties of Deletion and Insertion Errors

This section derives the properties of deletion and insertion errors.
From the definition of
\mathcal{I}_{Q}(\rho_{2})
,
we get

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}(\rho_{2})

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{D}_{Q}(\rho_{1})=\rho_{2}.

(9)

The following lemma is
an extension of Eq. (
9
).

Lemma 7
.

Any
\rho_{1}\in S(\mathbb{C}^{l\otimes(m+s)}),\rho_{2}\in S(\mathbb{C}^{l\otimes m})

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\in\mathcal{I}^{s}(\rho_{2})\iff\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{1})\ni\rho_{2}.

Proof.

From Eq. (
9
),

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\in\mathcal{I}^{s}(\rho_{2})\iff

\displaystyle\exists Q\in\tbinom{[\hskip-1.42262pt[m+s]\hskip-1.42262pt]}{s}~\rho_{1}\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}(\rho_{2})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\exists Q\in\tbinom{[\hskip-1.42262pt[m+s]\hskip-1.42262pt]}{s}~\mathcal{D}_{Q}(\rho_{1})=\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{1})\ni\rho_{2}

holds.
∎

The following lemma is an extension of Lemma
7
.

Lemma 8
.

Any
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}\subset S(\mathbb{C}^{l\otimes\mathbb{N}})
satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}\cap\mathcal{I}^{s}(\mathcal{X}_{2})\neq\emptyset\iff\mathcal{D}^{s}(\mathcal{X}_{1})\cap\mathcal{X}_{2}\neq\emptyset.

Proof.

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}\cap\mathcal{I}^{s}(\mathcal{X}_{2})\neq\emptyset\iff

\displaystyle\exists\rho_{1}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}~\rho_{1}\in\mathcal{I}^{s}(\mathcal{X}_{2})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\exists\rho_{1}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}~\exists\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{X}_{2}~\rho_{1}\in\mathcal{I}^{s}(\rho_{2})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\exists\rho_{1}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}~\exists\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{X}_{2}~\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{1})\ni\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\exists\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{X}_{2}~\mathcal{D}^{s}(\mathcal{X}_{1})\ni\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{s}(\mathcal{X}_{1})\cap\mathcal{X}_{2}\neq\emptyset

holds.

.

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{D}^{s}(\mathcal{X}_{1})\cap\mathcal{D}^{t}(\mathcal{X}_{2})\neq\emptyset,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(t+1)}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(s-1)}(\rho_{1})\cap\mathcal{I}^{(t+1)}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(s-1)}(\rho_{2})=\emptyset.

Proof.

From Lemmas
8
and
9
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{t}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{1})\cap\mathcal{I}^{t}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{2})=\emptyset

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{1})\cap\mathcal{D}^{t}\circ\mathcal{I}^{t}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{2})=\emptyset

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\notin\mathcal{I}^{s}\circ\mathcal{D}^{t}\circ\mathcal{I}^{t}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{2})

(10)

holds.
From Lemma

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(s-1)}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(t+1)}\circ\mathcal{I}^{(t+1)}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(s-1)}(\rho_{2})

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{I}^{s}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(t+1)}\circ\mathcal{I}^{t}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(s-1)}(\rho_{2})

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{I}^{s}\circ\mathcal{D}^{t}\circ\mathcal{I}^{t}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{2}).

Hence,
we have

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\notin\mathcal{I}^{s}\circ\mathcal{D}^{t}\circ\mathcal{I}^{t}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{2})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\rho_{1}\notin\mathcal{I}^{(s-1)}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(t+1)}\circ\mathcal{I}^{(t+1)}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(s-1)}(\rho_{2}).

(11)

By a similar way to Eq. (
10
),

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\notin\mathcal{I}^{(s-1)}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(t+1)}\circ\mathcal{I}^{(t+1)}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(s-1)}(\rho_{2})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(t+1)}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(s-1)}(\rho_{1})\cap\mathcal{I}^{(t+1)}\circ\mathcal{D}^{(s-1)}(\rho_{2})=\emptyset

(12)

holds.
Combining
Eqs. (
10
), (
11
), and (
12
),
we get the lemma.
∎

Since deletion and insertion errors are non-commutative
4
,
the converse of Theorem

does not necessarily hold.

Example 4

VI
Quantum Indel Distance

This section defines the quantum indel distance and
describes
insertion and deletion errors correcting capability of quantum codes
by this distance.

VI.1
Definition and Examples

The indel distance for
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{l}^{n}
and
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{Z}_{l}^{m}
is
defined as the sum of the number of classical deletions and insertions
required to transform
\bm{x}
into
\bm{y}

8
.
Similar to the indel distance,
we define
the quantum indel distance for
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in S(\mathbb{C}^{l\otimes\mathbb{N}})
as
the sum of the number of deletion and insertion errors
required to transform

\rho_{1}
into
\rho_{2}
.
The quantum indel distance is mathematically defined as follows:

Definition 2
.

The quantum indel distance
d(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
of
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in S(\mathbb{C}^{l\otimes\mathbb{N}})

is defined as follows.

\displaystyle d(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})

\displaystyle:=\min_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{N}^{2},(s,t)\text{-error~}\mathcal{E}}\{s+t\mid\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{E}(\rho_{1})\}

\displaystyle=\min_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\{s+t\mid\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{I}^{t}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{1})\}

\displaystyle=\min_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\{s+t\mid\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{1})\cap\mathcal{D}^{t}(\rho_{2})\neq\emptyset\}

The first equality
follows
from Theorem
3

and
the second equality
follows
from Lemma
9
.

Example 5

\displaystyle\rho_{1}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\ket{00}\bra{00}+\tfrac{1}{2}\ket{11}\bra{11},

\displaystyle\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\{1\}}(\rho_{1})=\mathcal{D}_{\{2\}}(\rho_{2})=\tfrac{1}{2}\ket{0}\bra{0}+\tfrac{1}{2}\ket{1}\bra{1}.

Definition 3
.

The minimum distance
d_{\text{min}}(\mathcal{X})

\mathcal{X}\subset S(\mathbb{C}^{l\otimes\mathbb{N}})

is defined as follows:

\displaystyle d_{\text{min}}(\mathcal{X}):=\min_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{X}:\rho_{1}\neq\rho_{2}}d(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})

Now,

VI.2
Properties

Theorem 5
.

The quantum indel distance is a metric on
S(\mathbb{C}^{l\otimes\mathbb{N}})
.

Proof.

Any
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in S(\mathbb{C}^{l\otimes\mathbb{N}})
satisfy

\displaystyle d(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})=0

\displaystyle\iff\emptyset\neq\mathcal{D}^{0}(\rho_{1})\cap\mathcal{D}^{0}(\rho_{2})=\{\rho_{1}\}\cap\{\rho_{2}\}

\displaystyle\iff\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}.

The non-negativity and the symmetry are trivial.
Now,
we will prove the triangle inequality.
For
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3}\in S(\mathbb{C}^{l\otimes\mathbb{N}})
,
define

\displaystyle(s_{1},t_{1})

\displaystyle:=\underset{(s,t)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}{\operatorname{argmin}}~\{s+t\mid\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{I}^{t}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{1})\},

\displaystyle(s_{2},t_{2})

\displaystyle:=\underset{(s,t)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}{\operatorname{argmin}}~\{s+t\mid\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{I}^{t}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s}(\rho_{3})\}.

From
\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{I}^{t_{1}}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s_{1}}(\rho_{1})
and

\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{I}^{t_{2}}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s_{2}}(\rho_{3})
,
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{t_{1}}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s_{1}}(\rho_{1})\cap\mathcal{I}^{t_{2}}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s_{2}}(\rho_{3})\neq\emptyset.

From Lemma
9
and Theorem
3
,
we have

\displaystyle\rho_{1}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{I}^{s_{1}}\circ\mathcal{D}^{t_{1}}\circ\mathcal{I}^{t_{2}}\circ\mathcal{D}^{s_{2}}(\rho_{3})\subset\mathcal{I}^{s_{1}+t_{2}}\circ\mathcal{D}^{t_{1}+s_{2}}(\rho_{3}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle d(\rho_{1},\rho_{3})

\displaystyle\leq s_{1}+t_{2}+t_{1}+s_{2}

\displaystyle=d(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})+d(\rho_{2},\rho_{3}).

holds.
∎

.

Any
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in S(\mathbb{C}^{l\otimes n})
satisfy

\displaystyle d(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\geq 2t+1\iff\mathcal{D}^{t}(\rho_{1})\cap\mathcal{D}^{t}(\rho_{2})=\emptyset

Noting that

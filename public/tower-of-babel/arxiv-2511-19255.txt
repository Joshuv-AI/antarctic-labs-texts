Magnetic susceptibility of hot hadronic medium and quark degrees of freedom near the QCD cross-over point
Rupam Samanta, Wojciech Broniowski

Abstract
We argue that the lattice QCD results for the temperature-dependent magnetic susceptibility of the medium below the cross-over temperature are difficult to reconcile with the available hadronic models. In particular, in the widely used Hadron Resonance Gas model, reproducing well numerous other features of the medium, one observes a substantially too strong diamagnetism compared to the lattice simulations. One thus needs a significant source of paramagnetism below the QCD cross-over temperature, possible to achieve with sufficiently light fermions. We thus consider a quark-meson model, where the temperature-dependent quark masses are fixed from the baryon-baryon and baryon-strangeness susceptibility data from the lattice at zero magnetic field. We show that in such a framework one can describe the magnetic susceptibility, with the vacuum contributions duly incorporated. In the hadronic picture, we also evaluate the contribution of the pion–vector-meson loops to the magnetic susceptibility evaluated via the photon polarization, showing it is small and paramagnetic.

With the above successes, HRG became a standard benchmark for the evaluation of thermodynamic quantities in the hadronic phase.
Moreover, this is to be linked with the success in the description of the particle yields (see
Andronic et al. 2018

in ultra-relativistic nuclear

Consequently, a conviction followed that below

we deal with just hadrons, and above with quarks and gluons. Certainly, the cross-over
nature of the transition makes the division smooth, with hadrons and partons overlapping near
T_{c}
,
but in practice HRG could be applied up to

with a stunning accuracy.

One of the goals of this paper is to point out that there is a notable difficulty in the application of HRG
to describe the lattice data for
\chi_{B}
in the vicinity of
T_{c}
.
A comparison study was already done in the lattice papers
Bali et al. 2014
;
Bali et al. 2020
and most
recently in

, but the significance of the vivid mismatch

T_{c}
has not been accentuated, while the agreement between HRG and the lattice at lower

We try to address this mismatch in the range of

where HRG is expected to be valid, stressing that to be able to describe the lattice data,
a suitable model, including the vacuum effects

;
Kamikado and Kanazawa 2015
,
needs to possess sufficient paramagnetism. It also implies that the paramagnetic degrees of freedom in that region must be sufficiently light,
not to be damped thermally. Natural candidates here are quarks of constituent masses of a few hundred MeV,
appearing in the system from the melt-down of baryons (see, e.g.,
McLerran et al. 2009
;
McLerran and Reddy 2019
;
Marczenko et al. 2023

The methodology applied in this paper is as follows: we use a non-interacting quark-meson model below

to describe

\chi_{\cal{BB}}

Specifically, we use the lattice
data for
\chi_{\cal{BB}}
and
\chi_{\cal{BS}}
(at
B=0
)

quark masses. For these susceptibilities, as we carefully discuss in Sec.
III.2
, in the independent particle approach only the thermal
part of the free energy contributes, because the vacuum part does not explicitly depend on the chemical potentials.

With thus obtained temperature-dependent quark masses, and with the pions and kaons present, we compute the subtracted
magnetic susceptibility
\chi_{B}(T)-\chi_{B}(0)
, as provided by the lattice simulations
Bali et al. 2020
;
Brandt et al. 2024
.
The vacuum contributions, which here are relevant, are incorporated. For the quark case, the vacuum part requires a regulator in the spirit of the low-energy effective non-perturbative models

;
Broniowski et al. 2022

which eliminates the contribution of the hard modes. Additionally, we investigate the relevant role of the anomalous magnetic
moments of the constituent quarks.

The result of our study is that it is possible to adjust in a natural way the model parameters
(the low-energy cut-off, the quark anomalous magnetic moments) such
that the quark-meson model, together with the vacuum terms, complies with the data for
\chi_{B}
and simultaneously with
\chi_{\cal{BB}}
and
\chi_{\cal{BS}}

The article is organized in the following way. First, in Sec.

In Sec.

For completeness, within HRG we analyze the contribution to

III
Thermodynamics in a magnetic field

III.1
Basic definitions

Thermodynamical properties of a system treated via the grand canonical ensemble can be obtained from the
the grand thermodynamical potential density
\Omega

Landau and Lifshitz 1980
;
Pathria and Beale 2011
, which in the presence of a magnetic field is

\displaystyle\Omega=U-TS-B{\cal M}-\mu N,

(1)

where
U
is the internal energy,
S
- the entropy,
{\cal M}
- magnetization, and
\mu N=\mu_{\cal B}{\cal B}+\mu_{S}{\cal S}+...
involves the combination of chemical potentials and the corresponding conserved charges.
1
1

1

To distinguish the baryon number from the magnetic field, we use

{\cal B}
for the former, and
B
for the latter.

In the thermodynamic limit of a large volume,
\Omega
is
related to the system pressure,
P=-\Omega/V
.

The conserved charge susceptibilities used in this work are defined as

\displaystyle\chi_{\cal BB}=\left.\frac{1}{T^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial\mu_{\cal B}^{2}}\right|_{\mu_{\cal B,S}=0},\;\;\;\chi_{{\cal B}S}=\left.\frac{1}{T^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial\mu_{\cal B}\partial\mu_{\cal S}}\right|_{\mu_{\cal B,S}=0}.

(2)

They are evaluated at vanishing chemical potentials, as in lattice QCD.

According to Eq. (
1
), the magnetization density of the system is obtained as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial B},

(3)

while the magnetic susceptibility is given by the second derivative of the pressure at
B=0
,

\displaystyle\chi_{B}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{M}}{\partial B}\bigg|_{B=0}=\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial B^{2}}\bigg|_{B=0}.

(4)

Generically, the pressure can be decomposed into the thermal and vacuum parts,
P=P^{\rm th}+P^{\rm vac}
.
In the non-interacting particle picture, such as HRG or the quark-meson model studied later, at
B=0
the thermal part for
an individual particle species (for brevity, we omit indexing of the particle species below) has the form

\displaystyle P^{\rm th}=-\eta T\sum_{s_{z}}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\log[1-\eta f(E_{p},T,\mu)],

(5)

where
f(E_{p},T,\mu)
represents the occupation number of a stable hadron or a resonance with a given statistics,

\displaystyle f(E_{p},T,\mu)=\frac{1}{\exp(\frac{E_{p}-\mu}{T})+\eta}.

(6)

Here
E_{p}=\sqrt{m^{2}+p^{2}}
denotes the energy,
s_{z}
is the spin projection, and
\eta=+1
or
-1
depending whether the particle is a fermion or a boson.
The corresponding vacuum part can be formally written as
Endrödi 2013

\displaystyle P^{\rm vac}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\eta\sum_{s_{z}}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}E_{p},

(7)

which is infinite, thus requires regularization, to be discussed later and detailed in Appendix
B
.

In the presence of a magnetic field
\vec{B}=B\hat{z}
, for charged particles the corresponding formulas involve a sum
over the Landau levels
l

Landau and Lifshitz 1980
;
Pathria and Beale 2011

\displaystyle P^{\rm th}=-\eta T\frac{B|Q|}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{s_{z}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp_{z}\log[1-\eta f(p_{z},B)],

\displaystyle P^{\rm vac}=-\eta\frac{B|Q|}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{s_{z}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp_{z}E(p_{z},B).

(8)

For the neutral particles, equations of the form (
5
,
7
) hold, but
E
may
explicitly depend on
B
if the particle has an anomalous magnetic moment (cf. Sec.
IV.1
).

III.2
Self-consistency

From the above expressions, it is apparent that in the independent particle picture,
the decomposition of the pressure into the thermal and vacuum parts involves functions of the following
arguments:

\displaystyle P=P^{\rm th}(T,\mu,B;\{m\})+P^{\rm vac}(B;\{m\}),

(9)

where
\{m\}
denotes the collection of masses of particles.
Since the energy spectrum of particles and antiparticles is the same due to the charge conjugation symmetry,
we have the feature

\displaystyle P^{\rm th}(T,\mu,B;\{m\})=P^{\rm th}(T,-\mu,B;\{m\}),

(10)

i.e., the symmetry in
\mu
.
Note that the vacuum term does not explicitly depend on the thermal parameters.

The masses may depend on the thermal parameters and
B
, namely
\{m\}=\{m(T,\mu,B)\}
, bringing additional implicit dependence in
P
.
In various effective models,
P
is treated as an effective potential for the determination of masses (or other quantities).
The self-consistency condition is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial P}{\partial m}=0.

(11)

Note that thus obtained mass must be a symmetric function of
\mu
, as implied by the property (
10
). In particular,

\displaystyle\partial m/\partial\mu\mid_{\mu=0}=0.

(12)

It is now instructive to evaluate various quantities needed in this work.
The baryon density is

\displaystyle{\cal B}=\frac{dP}{d\mu_{\cal B}}=\frac{\partial P}{\partial\mu_{\cal B}}+\frac{\partial P}{\partial m}\frac{\partial m}{\partial\mu_{\cal B}}=\frac{\partial P^{\rm th}}{\partial\mu_{\cal B}},

(13)

where (
11
) was used. For the susceptibility,

\displaystyle\hskip-25.60747pt\chi_{\cal BB}=\frac{1}{T^{2}}\left.\frac{d^{2}P}{d\mu_{\cal B}^{2}}\right|_{\mu_{\cal B}=0}=\frac{1}{T^{2}}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial\mu_{\cal B}^{2}}+\frac{\partial P}{\partial m}\frac{\partial^{2}m}{\partial\mu_{\cal B}^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+2\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial m\partial\mu_{\cal B}}\frac{\partial m}{\partial\mu_{\cal B}}+\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial m^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial m}{\partial\mu_{\cal B}}\right)^{2}\right]_{\mu_{\cal B}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{2}}\left.\frac{\partial^{2}P^{\rm th}}{\partial\mu_{\cal B}^{2}}\right|_{\mu_{\cal B}=0},

(14)

where we have used conditions (
11
) and (
12
). An analogous equality follows for
\chi_{{\cal B}S}
:

\displaystyle\chi_{{\cal B}S}=\frac{1}{T^{2}}\left.\frac{\partial^{2}P^{\rm th}}{\partial\mu_{\cal B}\partial\mu_{S}}\right|_{\mu_{\cal B,S}=0}.

(15)

The meaning of Eqs. (
13
-
15
), despite the simplicity of the derivation, is sound, as (in the independent particle approach)
it allows one for a “naive” evaluation of these quantities just from the thermal part. The vacuum term manifests itself only indirectly through the self-consistency
conditions, in general leading to
T
,
\mu
, or
B
-dependent particle masses.

For the magnetization and the magnetic susceptibility, derived using Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
),
the situation is entirely different, since both the thermal and the vacuum parts depend
explicitly on the magnetic field.
2
2

2

The explicit dependence of
P^{\rm th}
and
P^{\rm vac}
on
B
originates
simply from the explicit dependence of the energy spectrum on
B
, as
discussed in detail in Sec.
IV.1
.
Thus also the vacuum
contributes directly to
{\cal M}
and
\chi_{B}
and needs to be considered
Endrödi 2013
;
Kamikado and Kanazawa 2015
.
This makes the modeling of these magnetic features much more demanding, as it is prerequisite to model the vacuum part of
P
.

IV.1

In HRG, one deals with a non-interacting system of stable hadrons and resonances, which incorporate in a simplified way the interactions between the stable

;

The energy spectra of individual hadrons in a magnetic field depend on their spin. The relevant formulas were reviewed in

In particular, for spin-0 charged states (e.g.,

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38109ptE\equiv\epsilon_{l,p_{z}}=\sqrt{M^{2}+p_{z}^{2}+B|Q|(2l+1)},\;

(16)

where

denotes the particle electric charge and

For spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\epsilon_{l,p_{z}}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left(\sqrt{M^{2}+B\left[|Q|(2l\!+\!1\!)-\!2Qs_{z}\right]}-2\mu_{M}B\kappa s_{z}\right)^{2}\!+\!p_{z}^{2}},

(17)

for states of non-zero charge

(such as the proton), and

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\epsilon_{p,p_{z}}=\sqrt{\left(\sqrt{M^{2}+p^{2}-p_{z}^{2}}-2\mu_{M}B\kappa s_{z}\right)^{2}+p_{z}^{2}}

(18)

for neutral states (such as the neutron). Here

is the natural magneton of the particle. Our

\displaystyle g=2(Q+\kappa).

(19)

For higher spin states we adopt the same prescription as in
Samanta and Broniowski 2025
, where the

in case of spin-1 or spin-

\displaystyle\epsilon_{l,p_{z}}=\sqrt{M^{2}+p_{z}^{2}+B[|Q|(2l\!+\!1\!)-\!2Qs_{z}]}-2B\mu_{M}\kappa s_{z},

and for particle with spin larger than

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38109pt\epsilon_{l,p_{z}}=\sqrt{M^{2}+p_{z}^{2}+B|Q|(2l\!+\!1\!)}-gB\mu_{M}s_{z}.

(21)

With the above spectra one can obtain the pressure for individual hadron species (

), which then

IV.3
Magnetic susceptibility with anomalous magnetic moments

In HRG, one considers only the thermal part of the magnetic susceptibility.

A
).

For the diamagnetic component one gets

\displaystyle\chi_{B}^{\rm dia}=-\frac{Q^{2}(2s+1)}{24\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\;\frac{f(E_{p})}{E_{p}},

For spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692pt\chi_{B;s=\frac{1}{2}}^{\rm para}=\frac{s(s+1)(2s+1)}{6\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\times

\displaystyle~~~\left[Q^{2}+2Q\;\kappa+\left(1+\frac{p^{2}}{m^{2}}\right)\;\kappa^{2}\right]\;\frac{f(E_{p})}{E_{p}}.

The cases corresponding to spectra of Eqs. (

In the limit of a large mass

\displaystyle\chi_{B;m\gg T}^{\rm para}=\frac{s(s+1)(2s+1)g^{2}}{24\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!dp\frac{f(E_{p})}{E_{p}},

Quark degrees of freedom

V.1
Motivation

In the previous sections we have argued that within HRG it is not possible to achieve agreement with the lattice results
for
\chi_{B}(T)
in the fiducial temperature range.
A general conclusion is that one needs states with

and of sufficiently low mass, such that at

their paramagnetism may overcome the diamagnetism from the pions and kaons.
Obvious candidates here are
the (constituent) quarks, which as spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}

Moreover, the inclusion of quarks allows one to model the needed vacuum term, advocated in Sec.
III.2

As seen from the lattice data, we also need
to retain the diamagnetism at low values of

, where
\chi_{B}(T)
is negative. For that reason we shall keep the pions and kaons
as additional degrees of freedom, in the spirit of the quark-meson models (see
Schaefer and Wambach 2005
and references therein).
Thus, we consider an independent particle model with constituent quarks of three colors and flavors, pions, and kaons.
Recently, quark meson models at non-zero

have been in similar tasks in
Kamikado and Kanazawa 2015
;
Mao 2025
;
Mei et al. 2024

VI
Pion–vector-meson loops

In the last part of this paper we return to a purely hadronic description to explore yet another contribution to the paramagnetism of the
medium, which originates from the pion–vector-meson loops in the photon polarization.
The possibility of couplings between photons, pions, and vector mesons involving the Levi-Civita tensor
\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
is well known,
and such “anomalous” effects are expected to be generically small. Yet, it is worthwhile to examine the issue in a great detail to have a complete picture.

In analogy to Eq. (
28
)
Weldon 1982
;
Fujimoto and Yamada 1988
;
Endrödi 2013
;
Endrődi and Markó 2022
,

\displaystyle\chi_{B}=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{q\rightarrow 0}\lim_{q_{0}\rightarrow 0}\frac{\partial^{2}\text{Re}[\Pi_{s}]}{\partial q^{2}}\ ,

(34)

To pick up the thermal contribution, one
calculates the vacuum polarization tensor of the photon using the loop diagrams, where one of the propagators in the loop
corresponds to the thermal part. For the case where the two propagators in the loop correspond to the same particle, such as for instance the pion,
one obtains the formula of Eq. (
23
) as a check.

The loops, however, can mix different states. In particular, there is the above-mentioned coupling between a
photon, pion, and a vector meson (cf. Fig.
10
). A detailed calculation of these
diagrams is provided in Appendix
C.2
. The magnetic susceptibility in this case becomes,

\displaystyle\chi_{B}=-\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}\Delta}\left(\frac{g_{\gamma\pi V}^{2}}{m_{V}^{2}}\right)\left[I_{\pi}-I_{V}\right],

(35)

where
V=\rho,\omega
,
\Delta=m_{V}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}
,
g_{\gamma\pi V}
is the coupling constant, and
I_{a}
is given by,

\displaystyle I_{a}=\int p^{2}dp\frac{f(E_{a})}{E_{a}}\left(p^{2}-3E_{a}\right),\;\;\;a=\pi,V.

(36)

Here
E_{a}=\sqrt{p^{2}+m_{a}^{2}}
represents the energy of meson
a
.
The values of the coupling constants have been determined from the vector meson electroproduction data and have the values
Oh et al. 2000
;
Gokalp and Yilmaz 2002

\displaystyle g_{\gamma\pi\rho}=0.54,\;\;\;g_{\gamma\pi\omega}=1.82.

(37)

In Eq. (
35
),
I_{\pi}
dominates over
I_{V}
due to the small mass of the pion compared to the masses of
\rho
or
\omega
.

VII
Conclusions

Here are the main results of this paper:

•

Within HRG, it is impossible to describe simultaneously the lattice data for the conserved charge susceptibilities,
\chi_{\cal BB}
and
\chi_{{\cal B}S}
, and the magnetic susceptibility,
\chi_{B}
.
In the fiducial range of temperatures where HRG should apply, there is a general need for paramagnetism provided by some relatively light states (such as
constituent quarks). Pions are a necessary ingredient to account for the diamagnetic contribution at low temperatures.

•

We have explored a framework of non-interacting quarks and mesons (pions and kaons) to
simultaneously

describe the lattice data for the conserved charge multiplicities and for
\chi_{B}
in the vicinity of
T_{c}
. In this picture,
fitting the lattice results for
\chi_{\cal BB}
and
\chi_{{\cal B}S}

yields the effective temperature-dependent quark masses above the typical constituent values, but , interestingly, very
close to the quasi-particle description
Mykhaylova et al. 2019
.

•

The vacuum contribution to the pressure does not directly affect the conserved charge susceptibilities, but it does contribute to
\chi_{B}
.
Our modeling incorporates the vacuum contribution from quark loops, treated with a low-energy cut-off, and from meson loops.

•

We have accounted for the anomalous magnetic moments of the quarks, which improve the results.

•

The contribution of the hadron magnetic polarizabilities to the magnetic susceptibility is negligible.

•

In the hadronic picture, the contribution to the magnetic susceptibility arising from the pion–vector-meson loops in the photon vacuum is paramagnetic and small.

Vacuum contribution to

from quark loops

We consider non-zero anomalous magnetic moment of quarks.
In presence of
\kappa
, the photon vacuum polarization tensor is given by

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\Pi^{\mu\nu}=ie^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\Tr[\Gamma^{\mu}S_{F}(p+q)\Gamma^{\nu}S_{F}(p)]\;,\;

(48)

where
\Gamma^{\mu}=Q\gamma^{\mu}+\frac{\kappa}{2M}\sigma^{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}
, with
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{i}{4}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]
.
p=(p_{0},\vec{p})
and
q=(q_{0},\vec{q})
are the loop momentum and the external momentum respectively.
S_{F}(p)
denotes the vacuum quark propagator

\displaystyle S_{F}(p)=\frac{\not{p}+m}{p^{2}-m^{2}+i\epsilon}.

(49)

Using Eq. (
28
) yields

\displaystyle\chi_{B}^{\rm vac}=R_{0}+R_{1}\kappa+R_{2}\kappa^{2},

(50)

where

\displaystyle R_{0}=\frac{4}{3}Q^{2}\pi^{2}\left[(q^{2}+2m^{2})B_{0}(q^{2},m^{2},m^{2})-2A_{0}(m^{2})\right],

\displaystyle R_{1}=4\pi^{2}Q\;q^{2}B_{0}(q^{2},m^{2},m^{2}),

(51)

\displaystyle R_{2}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\frac{q^{2}}{M^{2}}\left[(q^{2}+8m^{2})B_{0}(q^{2},m^{2},m^{2})-2A_{0}(m^{2})\right].

Here
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
are the one-loop Passarino-Veltman (PaVe) functions
Passarino and Veltman 1979
,

\displaystyle i\pi^{2}A_{0}(m^{2})=\int d^{4}p\;\frac{1}{p^{2}-m^{2}+i\epsilon},

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692pti\pi^{2}B_{0}(q^{2},m_{1}^{2},m_{2}^{2})=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}p\;\bigg[\frac{1}{(p+q)^{2}-m_{1}^{2}+i\epsilon}\frac{1}{p^{2}-m_{2}^{2}+i\epsilon}\bigg].

(52)

Since these integrals are divergent, one needs to use regularization. In an effective model, regularization
has a meaning of suppressing the high-momentum contributions in the loop integration.
Here we use Pauli-Villars regularization of
Ruiz Arriola 2002
;
Broniowski et al. 2022
using two subtractions, amounting to the replacement of

F(\{m^{2}\})
with

\displaystyle\hskip-34.1433ptF^{\Lambda}(\{m^{2}\})=F(\{m^{2}\})-F(\{m^{2}+\Lambda^{2}\})

\displaystyle+\Lambda^{2}\frac{dF(\{M^{2}+\Lambda^{2}\})}{d\Lambda^{2}},

(53)

where
F
denotes a PaVe function. We explicitly obtain

\displaystyle A_{0}=-\frac{m^{2}\log(\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m^{2}})+\Lambda^{2}}{16\pi^{4}}

(54)

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692ptB_{0}=\frac{\log(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m^{2}}+1)-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}+m^{2}}}{16\pi^{4}}+\frac{\Lambda^{4}q^{2}}{96\pi^{4}m^{2}(\Lambda^{2}+m^{2})^{2}}.

After collecting the terms in Eq. (
50

V.3

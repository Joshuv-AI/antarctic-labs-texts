Abstract
. In classical coding theory, error-correcting codes are designed to protect against errors occurring at individual symbol positions in a codeword. However, in practical storage and communication systems, errors often affect multiple adjacent symbols rather than single symbols independently. To address this, symbol-pair read channels were introduced [ 1 ] , and later generalized to b b -symbol read channels [ 16 ] to better model such error patterns. b b -Symbol read channels generalize symbol-pair read channels to account for clustered errors in modern storage and communication systems. By developing bounds and efficient codes, researchers improve data reliability in applications such as storage devices, wireless networks, and DNA-based storage. Given integers q q , n n , d d , and b ≥ 2 b\geq 2 , let A b ​ ( n , d , q ) A_{b}(n,d,q) denote the largest possible code size for which there exists a q q -ary code of length n n with minimum b b -symbol distance at least d d . In [ 4 ] , various upper and lower bounds on A b ​ ( n , d , q ) A_{b}(n,d,q) are given for b = 2 b=2 . In this paper, we generalize some of these bounds to the b b -symbol read channels for b > 2 b>2 and present several new bounds on A b ​ ( n , d , q ) A_{b}(n,d,q) . In particular, we establish the linear programming bound, a recurrence relation on A b ​ ( n , d , q ) A_{b}(n,d,q) , the Johnson bound (even), the restricted Johnson bound, the Gilbert-Varshamov-type bound, and the Elias bound for the metric of symbols b b , b ≥ 2 b\geq 2 . Furthermore, we provide examples demonstrating that the Gilbert-Varshamov bound we establish offers a stronger lower bound than the one presented in [ 14 ] . Additionally, we introduce an alternative approach to deriving the Sphere-packing and Plotkin bounds.

On the lower end, the Gilbert-Varshamov Bound guarantees the existence of codes with a certain minimum size, and the Griesmer Bound helps in understanding the length of linear codes. \rho(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-m},x_{n-m+1},\dots,x_{n})=(x_{n-m+1},\dots,x_{n})

\rho^{\prime}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-m},x_{n-m+1},\dots,x_{n})=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-m}).

Let

Take

Then

is a code of length

We claim that the minimum

-symbol distance of

that is

In order to show this let
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-m})
and
(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-m})
be distinct codewords in
C^{\prime\prime}.
Then there are
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-m},x_{n-m+1},\dots,x_{n})

\rho^{\prime}(x)=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-m})

Also, since

therefore

By triangle inequality, we get

Consequently, we have

(3.1)

d-w_{b}((z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n}))\geq d-2r

where
z_{i}=x_{i}-y_{i}.
From now onwards, we write

and
z_{i}=x_{i}-y_{i}
for all

Also, since
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
therefore
d\leq d_{b}(C^{\prime}).
Observe that

\displaystyle d\leq

\displaystyle d_{b}(x,y)=w_{b}(z)=w_{H}((z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{b}),\dots,(z_{n-m},z_{n-m+1},\dots,z_{n-m+b-1}),(z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n-m+b})

\displaystyle\dots,(z_{n},z_{1},\dots,z_{b-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{H}((z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{b}),\dots,(z_{n-m-b+1},z_{n-m-b+2},\dots,z_{n-m}),(z_{n-m-b+2},z_{n-m-b+2},\dots,z_{n-m},z_{n-m+1}),

\displaystyle\dots,(z_{n-m},z_{n-m+1},\dots,z_{n-m+b-1}),(z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n-m+b}),

\displaystyle(z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n-m+b+1}),\dots,(z_{n-b+1},z_{n-b+2},\dots,z_{n}),(z_{n-b+2},\dots,z_{n},z_{1}),

\displaystyle\dots,(z_{n-1},z_{n},z_{1},\dots,z_{b-2}),(z_{n},z_{1},\dots,z_{b-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{H}(S_{1},S_{3},S_{2},S_{4}),

\displaystyle S_{1}=

\displaystyle(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{b}),\dots,(z_{n-m-b+1},z_{n-m-b+2},\dots,z_{n-m}),

\displaystyle S_{2}=

\displaystyle(z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n-m+b}),(z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n-m+b+1}),\dots,(z_{n-b+1},z_{n-b+2},\dots,z_{n}),

\displaystyle S_{3}=

\displaystyle(z_{n-m-b+2},z_{n-m-b+2},\dots,z_{n-m},z_{n-m+1}),\dots,(z_{n-m},z_{n-m+1},\dots,z_{n-m+b-1}),\text{ and }

\displaystyle S_{4}=

\displaystyle(z_{n-b+2},\dots,z_{n},z_{1}),\dots,(z_{n-1},z_{n},z_{1},\dots,z_{b-2}),(z_{n},z_{1},\dots,z_{b-1}).

\displaystyle\text{Let }S_{5}=

\displaystyle S_{6}=

\displaystyle(z_{n-b+2},z_{n-b+3},\dots,z_{n},z_{n-m+1}),\dots,(z_{n},z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n-m+b-1}).

and
w_{H}(S_{2},S_{6})=w_{b}(z_{n-m+1},\dots,z_{n}).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle d\leq

\displaystyle w_{H}(S_{1},S_{3},S_{2},S_{4})+w_{H}(S_{5})+w_{H}(S_{6})-w_{H}(S_{5})-w_{H}(S_{6})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{H}(S_{1})+w_{H}(S_{3})+w_{H}(S_{2})+w_{H}(S_{4})+w_{H}(S_{5})+w_{H}(S_{6})-w_{H}(S_{5})-w_{H}(S_{6})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{H}(S_{1},S_{5})+w_{H}(S_{2},S_{6})+w_{H}(S_{3})+w_{H}(S_{4})-w_{H}(S_{5})-w_{H}(S_{6})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{b}(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n-m})+w_{b}(z_{n-m+1},\dots,z_{n})+w_{H}(S_{3})+w_{H}(S_{4})-w_{H}(S_{5})-w_{H}(S_{6})

\displaystyle d-

\displaystyle w_{b}(z_{n-m+1},\dots,z_{n})\leq w_{b}(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n-m})+w_{H}(S_{3})+w_{H}(S_{4})-w_{H}(S_{5})-w_{H}(S_{6})

Using Eq.
3.1
, we get

d-2r\leq w_{b}(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n-m})+w_{H}(S_{3})+w_{H}(S_{4})-w_{H}(S_{5})-w_{H}(S_{6}).

In Lemma
3.2
, take
l=n-m,A_{1}=S_{3},A_{2}=S_{4},A_{3}=S_{5}
and
A_{4}=S_{6}.
Then

w_{H}(S_{3})+w_{H}(S_{4})-w_{H}(S_{5})-w_{H}(S_{6})\leq b-1.

We have

equivalently,

d-2r-b+1\leq w_{b}(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n-m}).

Hence,
d_{b}(C^{\prime\prime})\geq d-2r-b+1.
Thus
|C^{\prime\prime}|\leq A_{b}(n-m,d-2r-b+1,q)
.

Next, we claim that
|C^{\prime}|=|C^{\prime\prime}|
that is
\rho^{\prime}
is one-one map on
C^{\prime}
To see this, let
x,y\in C^{\prime}=C\cap\rho^{-1}(B_{b}(m,r))
be distinct codewords. This implies

(3.2)

0<d_{b}(\rho(x),\rho(y))\leq 2r,\text{ and }d_{b}(x,y)\geq d_{b}(C)=d.

Now, let
\rho^{\prime}(x)=\rho^{\prime}(y)
that is
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-m})=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-m})
implies that
x_{i}-y_{i}=z_{i}=0
for

Observe that

\displaystyle d_{b}(x,y)=

\displaystyle w_{b}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{H}((z_{1},\dots,z_{b}),\dots,(z_{n-m-b+1},\dots,z_{n-m}),(z_{n-m-b+2},\dots,z_{n-m},z_{n-m+1}),

\displaystyle(z_{n-m-b+3},\dots,z_{n-m},z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2}),\dots,(z_{n-m},\dots,z_{n-m+b-1}),

\displaystyle(z_{n-m+1},\dots,z_{n-m+b}),(z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n-m+b+1}),\dots,(z_{n},z_{1},\dots,z_{b-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{H}((0,0,\dots,0,z_{n-m+1}),(0,0,\dots,0,z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2}),\dots,(0,z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n-m+b-1})

\displaystyle(z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n-m+b}),\dots,(z_{n-b+1},\dots,z_{n}),(z_{n-b+2},\dots,z_{n},0),\dots(z_{n-1},z_{n},0,\dots,0),

\displaystyle(z_{n},0,\dots,0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{H}(A,B,C)=w_{H}(A)+w_{H}(B)+w_{H}(C),

where

\displaystyle A=(0,0,\dots,0,z_{n-m+1}),(0,0,\dots,0,z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2}),\dots,(0,z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n-m+b-1}),

\displaystyle B=(z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n-m+b}),\dots,(z_{n-b+1},\dots,z_{n}),\text{ and}

\displaystyle C=(z_{n-b+2},\dots,z_{n},0),(z_{n-b+3},\dots,z_{n},0,0),\dots,(z_{n},0,\dots,0).

Let

\displaystyle D=(z_{n-b+2},\dots,z_{n},z_{n-m+1}),(z_{n-b+3},\dots,z_{n},z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2}),\dots,(z_{n},z_{n-m+1},\dots,z_{n-m+b-1}).

Then

Thus, we have

\displaystyle d_{b}(x,y)=

\displaystyle w_{H}(A,B,C)=w_{H}(A)+w_{H}(B)+w_{H}(C)+w_{H}(D)-w_{H}(D)

\displaystyle d_{b}(x,y)=

\displaystyle w_{H}(B,D)+w_{H}(A)+w_{H}(C)-w_{H}(D)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{b}(z_{n-m+1},z_{n-m+2},\dots,z_{n})+w_{H}(A)+w_{H}(C)-w_{H}(D)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{b}(\rho(x),\rho(y))+w_{H}(A)+w_{H}(C)-w_{H}(D).

In Lemma
3.1
, take
l=n-m,B_{1}=A,B_{2}=C,B_{3}=D
and
z=(0,0,\dots,z_{n-m+1},\dots,z_{n})
then

w_{H}(A)+w_{H}(C)-w_{H}(D)\leq b-1.
So, we get

d\leq d_{b}(x,y)\leq d_{b}(\rho(x),\rho(y))+(b-1)\leq 2r+b-1\ \text{(Using Eq. \ref{eq2})}

which is a contradiction since
d\geq 2r+b.

Hence
\rho^{\prime}(x)\neq\rho^{\prime}(y)
that is
\rho^{\prime}
is one-one consequently,
|C^{\prime}|=|C^{\prime\prime}|.

Our next claim is that
|C^{\prime}|\geq\frac{MB_{b}(m,r)}{q^{m}}.
To show; let
\bm{c_{1}},\bm{c_{2}},\dots,\bm{c_{M}}
be images of codewords of
C
under the map
\rho.
(need not be distinct) Then
|C^{\prime}|
is the number of
\bm{c}_{i}
’s that belong to
B_{b}(m,r)
(since cardinality does not depend on the choice of the center). Choose
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
as the center of a
b
-ball of radius
r
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
such that the number of
\bm{c}_{i}
’s that belongs to
B_{b}(m,r,a)
is as large as possible. Now, replace
B_{b}(m,r)
by
B_{b}(m,r,a)
that is
|C^{\prime}|
is the number of
\bm{c}_{i}
’s that belong to
B_{b}(m,r,a).
For any
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m},
consider characteristic function

\chi_{u}(\bm{c_{i}})=\begin{Bmatrix}1\text{ if }\bm{c_{i}}\in B_{b}(m,r,u),\\
0\text{ if }\bm{c_{i}}\notin B_{b}(m,r,u),\end{Bmatrix}.

The number of
\bm{c}_{i}
’s that belong to the ball

can be expressed as
\sum_{i=1}^{M}\chi_{u}(\bm{c}_{i}).
Finally, note that the number
of
\bm{c}_{i}
’s that belong to

is larger than or equal to the average overall
b
-balls (this is because

\bm{c}i
’s belonging to

), that is,

\displaystyle|C^{\prime}|\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q^{m}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\chi_{u}(\bm{c_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q^{m}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}}\chi_{u}(\bm{c_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{MB_{b}(m,r)}{q^{m}},

where the last equality holds because for any fixed

Therefore
\frac{MB_{b}(m,r)}{q^{m}}\leq|C^{\prime}|=|C^{\prime\prime}|\leq A_{b}(n-m,d-2r-b+1,q)

4.
Bounds on code size for
b
-symbol distance

In this section, we present several bounds on code size for
b
-symbol distance. First, we establish Johnson bound for even minimum
b
-symbol distance. The proof follows a similar approach to that of the Johnson bound (
[
9
, Theorem 2.3.8]
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n
and
d
be positive integers such that
d=2t+2
for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

A_{b}(n,d,q)\leq\frac{q^{n}}{B_{b}(n,t)+\frac{S_{b}(n,t+1)}{A_{b}(n,d,t+1,q)}}.

Proof.

Let
C
be a code of size
M=A_{b}(n,d,q)
with
d_{b}(C)\geq d
. For
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, define

d_{b}(C,y)=\min_{c\in C,\ c\neq y}d_{b}(c,y).

Denote
\Im=\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}|\ d_{b}(C,y)=t+1\}
. Since
d=2t+2
,
\bigcup_{c\in C}B_{b}(n,t,c)\cup\Im
is a disjoint union. Thus, we have

(4.1)

MB_{b}(n,t)+|\Im|\leq q^{n}.

Let
\aleph=\{(c,y)\in C\times\Im|\ d_{b}(c,y)=t+1\}
. For
y\in\Im
, let
C_{y}=\{c-y|\ c\in C,d_{b}(c,y)=t+1\}
. Observe that
wt_{b}(c-y)=t+1
and
d_{b}(c-y,c^{\prime}-y)=d_{b}(c,c^{\prime})\geq d
for
c,c^{\prime}\in C
with
d_{b}(c,y)=t+1=d_{b}(c^{\prime}-y)
. Therefore,
C_{y}
is a constant
b
-symbol weight code of length
n
with constant
b
-symbol weight
t+1
and
d_{b}(C_{y})\geq d
. Hence, we have

|C_{y}|\leq A_{b}(n,d,t+1,q).

Consequently,

(4.2)

|\aleph|\leq|\Im|\cdot A_{b}(n,d,t+1,q).

To obtain a lower bound on
|\aleph|
, let
\aleph_{c}=\{y\in\Im|\ (c,y)\in\aleph\}
for
c\in C
. We have

(4.3)

|\aleph|=\sum_{c\in C}|\aleph_{c}|.

As the cardinality of
b
-symbol sphere is independent of center, for a fix
c\in C
, we have

S_{b}(n,t+1)=|S_{b}(n,t+1,c)|=|\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}|\ d_{b}(c,y)=t+1\}|.

For an arbitrary
c^{\prime}\in C
distinct from
c
and
y\in S_{b}(n,t+1,c)
(
d_{b}(c,y)=t+1
implies
d_{b}(C,y)\leq t+1
), by triangle inequality

d\leq d_{b}(c^{\prime},c)\leq d_{b}(c^{\prime},y)+d_{b}(c,y)=d_{b}(c^{\prime},y)+t+1.

That is,
d_{b}(c^{\prime},y)\geq d-t-1=t+1
for any
c^{\prime}\in C
distinct from
c
. So,
d_{b}(C,y)=t+1
. Hence
y\in\Im
. Thus, if
y\in S_{b}(n,t+1,c)
then
y\in\aleph_{c}
. Therefore,

(4.4)

|\aleph_{c}|=S_{b}(n,t+1).

Combining Eqs.
4.1
,
4.2
,
4.3
and
4.4
, we have

MB_{b}(n,t)+\frac{MS_{b}(n,t+1)}{A_{b}(n,d,t+1,q)}\leq q^{n}.

Equivalently,

A_{b}(n,d,q)\leq\frac{q^{n}}{B_{b}(n,t)+\frac{S_{b}(n,t+1)}{A_{b}(n,d,t+1,q)}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

A similar bound for
b=2
has been obtained for binary and
q
-ary codes in
[
8
]
and
[
4
]
, respectively.

Remark 4.3
.

Corollary
4.5
below gives an upper bound for the value of
A_{b}(n,d,t+1,q)
and thus gives a closed-form formula in Theorem
4.1
.

Now, we explore that any code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in
b
-symbol distance can be viewed as a code over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
in Hamming distance such that
b
-symbol distance and Hamming distance of the codes are same. Using this correspondence, we derive several bounds on the code size for
b
-symbol distance.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
be an extension field of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
b
, then there exists an element
\gamma
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
such that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\gamma)
. Consequently, there exists a monic irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
b
such that
f(\gamma)=0
. Therefore, any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
, can be uniquely written as
a_{1}+a_{2}\gamma+\dots+a_{b}\gamma^{b-1},
where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q},
for
1\leq i\leq b.
For
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},
define
\delta(x):=x^{\prime}=(x_{1}+x_{2}\gamma+\dots+x_{b}\gamma^{b-1},\ x_{2}+x_{3}\gamma+\dots+x_{b+1}\gamma^{b-1},\ \dots,\ x_{n}+x_{1}\gamma+\dots+x_{b-1}\gamma^{b-1}).
Clearly,
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}^{n}.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any
(n,M)
code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with minimum
b
-symbol distance
d
,

C^{\prime}:=\{\delta(c)~:~c\in C\}

is an
(n,M)
code over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
with minimum Hamming distance
d
. If
c\in C
has
b
-symbol weight
w
then Hamming weight of
\delta(c)\in C^{\prime}
is
w
.

Proof.

For
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},
define
\delta(x):=x^{\prime}=(x_{1}+x_{2}\gamma+\dots+x_{b}\gamma^{b-1},\ x_{2}+x_{3}\gamma+\dots+x_{b+1}\gamma^{b-1},\ \dots,\ x_{n}+x_{1}\gamma+\dots+x_{b-1}\gamma^{b-1}).
Clearly,
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}^{n}.
Then
C^{\prime}=\{\delta(c)|c\in C\}
is an
(n,M)
code over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}.
Also,
wt_{b}(c)=wt_{H}(\delta(c)).
Hence, the minimum Hamming distance of
C^{\prime}
is
d.

∎

Corollary 4.5

(Restricted Johnson Bound for
b
-symbol distance)
.

Let
n
,
d
and
w
be positive integers. Then

A_{b}(n,d,w,q)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{d-2w+\frac{q^{b}w^{2}}{(q^{b}-1)n}}\right\rfloor

whenever the denominator is positive.

Proof.

Let
C
be a code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
|C|=A_{b}(n,d,w,q).
From Proposition
4.4
,
C^{\prime}
is a constant Hamming weight code over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
of length
n
and minimum Hamming distance at least
d
whose all codewords have Hamming weight
w.
Using Restricted Johnson Bound for Hamming distance
[
9
, Theorem 2.3.4]
, we have

A_{b}(n,d,w,q)=|C|=|C^{\prime}|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{d-2w+\frac{q^{b}w^{2}}{(q^{b}-1)n}}\right\rfloor.

whenever
\left(d-2w+\frac{q^{b}w^{2}}{(q^{b}-1)n}\right)
is positive.
∎

Corollary 4.6

(Plotkin bound for
b
-symbol distance)
.

Let
q
be a prime power, and
r,n
be positive integers satisfying
2\leq d\leq n
and
rn<d,
where
r=1-q^{-b}.
Then

A_{b}(n,d,q)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{d-rn}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Let
C
be a code such that
A_{b}(n,d,q)=|C|.
By Proposition
4.4
,
C^{\prime}
is a code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{b}}
with minimum Hamming distance at least
d.
By Plotkin bound for Hamming distance
[
9
, Theorem 2.2.1]
, we have

A_{b}(n,d,q)=|C|=|C^{\prime}|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{d-rn}\right\rfloor.

∎

Remark 4.7
.

In

]

Example 4.8

For

q=2,
we have

A_{3}(7,7,2)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{d-rn}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{7}{7-\frac{7}{8}\cdot 7}\right\rfloor=8.

Consider a code
C
with length
7
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
given by

C:=\{1111111,0100011,1010001,1101000,0110100,0011010,1000110,0001101\}.

It is easy to see
|C|=8
and
d_{b}(C)=7
. Thus, the bound in Corollary
4.6
is attained.

Corollary 4.9

(Sphere packing type bound for
b
-symbol distance)
.

Let
n
and
d
be positive integer with
1\leq d\leq n
. Let
t=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor
. Then

A_{b}(n,d,q)\leq\frac{q^{bn}}{\sum_{i=0}^{t}{{n}\choose{i}}(q^{b}-1)^{i}}.

Proof.

The proof will follow using Proposition
4.4
and Sphere packing bound
[
9
, Theorem 1.12.1]
for the Hamming distance.
∎

Corollary 4.10

(Gilbert-Varshamov type bound for
b
-symbol distance)
.

Let
n
and
d
be positive integer with
1\leq d\leq n
. Then
A_{b}(n,d,q)\geq\frac{q^{bn}}{\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}{{n}\choose{i}}(q^{b}-1)^{i}}
.

Proof.

The proof will follow using Proposition
4.4
, and Gilbert bound
[
9
, Theorem 2.8.1]
for the Hamming distance.
∎

Example 4.11
.

Let

,

, we have

Thus,

Corollary 4.12

(Elias bound for
b
-symbol distance)
.

Let
r=1-q^{-b}
. Let
w\leq rn
and
w^{2}-2rnw+rnd>0
. Then

A_{b}(n,d,q)\leq\frac{rnd}{w^{2}-2rnw+rnd}\cdot\frac{q^{bn}}{\sum_{i=0}^{w}{{n}\choose{i}}(q^{b}-1)^{i}}.

Proof.

The proof will follow using Proposition
4.4
, and Elias bound
[

for the Hamming distance.
∎

5.
b
-symbol linear programming bound

Linear programming bound is the most powerful bound that gives an upper bound on code size. This bound, as indicated by its name, is derived by solving a linear optimization problem. In
[
8
]
, the authors derived linear programming bound specially for symbol-pair codes (
b=2
). In this section, we establish linear programming bound for
b
-symbol codes for any arbitrary
b\geq 2
. Our approach draws from techniques used in the Hamming distance. Notably, we demonstrate that when
b=1
, our result aligns with the classical linear programming bound for Hamming distance.

Let
C_{s}
be a simplex code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with parameters
\left[\frac{q^{b}-1}{q-1},b,d_{H}=q^{b-1}\right]
and generator matrix
G_{s}
. Define a map
\Phi:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}\to C_{s}
as
\Phi(x)=x\cdot G_{s}
. It is easy to see that
\Phi
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear bijective map.

Let
C
be an
(n,M,d_{b})
code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
c=(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1})\in C
, denote
\overline{c}_{i}=(c_{i},c_{i+1},\dots,c_{i+b-1})
(indices taken modulo
n
). Then
\pi(c)=(\overline{c}_{0},\overline{c}_{1},\dots,\overline{c}_{n-1})
. We concatenate code
C
with
C_{s}
as

(
\ast
)

C_{H}=\{\Phi(\pi(c))=(\Phi(\overline{c}_{0}),\Phi(\overline{c}_{1}),\dots,\Phi(\overline{c}_{n-1})):\ c=(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1})\in C\}.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
C
is a code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with parameters
(n,M,d_{b})
then
C_{H}
is a code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with parameters
\left(\frac{(q^{b}-1)n}{q-1},M,d_{H}=q^{b-1}d_{b}\right)
.

Proof.

Since
\Phi
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear bijection,
C_{H}
is a code of length
\frac{(q^{b}-1)n}{q-1}
and cardinality
M
. Let
c\in C
with
b
-symbol weight
wt_{b}(c)
. Then the Hamming weight of
(\Phi(\overline{c}_{0}),\Phi(\overline{c}_{1}),\dots,\Phi(\overline{c}_{n-1}))
is
q^{b-1}wt_{b}(c)
, since the simplex code
C_{s}
is a constant weight code with constant weight
q^{b-1}
. Thus,
C_{H}
is a
\left(\frac{(q^{b}-1)n}{q-1},M,d_{H}=q^{b-1}d_{b}\right)
code.
∎

Let
W_{C}^{b}(x)
and
W_{C_{H}}^{H}(x)
be the weight enumerator of
C
with respect to the
b
-symbol distance and of
C_{H}
with respect to the Hamming distance, respectively.

Lemma 5.2
.

[
11
]

Let
C
and
C_{H}
be the codes as the above. Then

W_{C}^{b}(x)=1+A_{1}^{b}x+A_{2}^{b}x^{2}+\dots+A_{n}^{b}x^{n}

if and only if

W_{C_{H}}^{H}(x)=1+A_{1}^{b}x^{q^{b-1}}+A_{2}^{b}x^{2q^{b-1}}+\dots+A_{n}^{b}x^{nq^{b-1}}

Let

B_{i}^{b}(C)=\frac{1}{M}|\{(u,v)\in C^{2}:\ d_{b}(u,v)=i\}

and

B_{i}^{H}(C_{H})=\frac{1}{M}|\{(x,y)\in C_{H}^{2}:\ d_{H}(x,y)=i\}

be distance distribution of
C
with respect to
b
-symbol distance and of
C_{H}
with respect to the Hamming distance, respectively.

Lemma 5.3
.

(i)

B_{j}^{H}(C_{H})=\begin{cases}B_{i}^{b}(C)\ \ \ \ \text{ if }\ \ j=iq^{b-1}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n,\\
0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

(ii)

\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}^{b}(C)=M=\sum_{i=0}^{N}B_{i}^{H}(C_{H})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}B_{jq^{b-1}}^{H}(C_{H})
.

Proof.

(i)
Let
u,v\in C
. Then
\Phi(\pi(u)),\Phi(\pi(v))\in C_{H}
and

\begin{split}d_{H}(\Phi(\pi(u)),\Phi(\pi(v)))&=w_{H}(\Phi(\pi(u))-\Phi(\pi(v)))\\
&=w_{H}(\Phi(\pi(u)-\pi(v)))\ \ \ \text{ (since $\Phi$ is linear)}\\
&=q^{b-1}w_{H}(\pi(u)-\pi(v))\\
&=q^{b-1}d_{b}(u,v).\end{split}

(ii)
It will follow from the fact that
\Phi
is a bijection.
∎

For any
q
, Krawtchouck polynomial
K^{q,n}_{k}(x)
of degree
k
,
0\leq k\leq n
is defined as

K^{n,q}_{k}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}(q-1)^{k-j}\binom{x}{j}\binom{n-x}{k-j}.

The following lemma is well-known in the Hamming distance (for instance, see
[
9
, Lemma 2.6.2]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\xi
be
q
-th root of unity in complex numbers. Let
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{N}
with
w_{H}(u)=iq^{b-1}
. Then

(
\ast\ast
)

K^{N,q}_{k}(iq^{b-1})=\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{N},\ w_{H}(v)=k}\xi^{u\cdot v}.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
C
be a code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with parameters
(n,M,d_{b})
and
B_{i}^{b}(C)
,
0\leq i\leq n
be the distance distribution of
C
with respect to
b
-symbol distance. Then

\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}^{b}(C)K_{k}^{N,q}(iq^{b-1})\geq 0\text{ for all integers }0\leq k\leq n,\text{ where }N=\frac{(q^{b}-1)n}{q-1}

Proof.

Let
C_{H}
be the concatenated code. By Lemma
5.3
, we have

\begin{split}M\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}^{b}(C)K_{k}^{N,q}(iq^{b-1})=&M\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{iq^{b-1}}^{H}(C_{H})K_{k}^{N,q}(iq^{b-1})\\
=&\sum_{i=0}^{n}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\in C_{H}^{2},\\
w_{H}(x-y)=iq^{b-1}\end{subarray}}\ \ \sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{N},\ w_{H}(v)=k}\xi^{(x-y)\cdot v}\text{ (by Lemma \ref{rootunity})}\\
=&\sum_{(x,y)\in C_{H}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{N},\\
w_{H}(v)=k\end{subarray}}\xi^{x\cdot v-y\cdot v}\\
=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{N},\\
w_{H}(v)=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{x\in C_{H}}\xi^{x\cdot v}\sum_{x\in C_{H}}\xi^{-x\cdot v}\\
=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{N},\\
w_{H}(v)=k\end{subarray}}|\sum_{x\in C_{H}}\xi^{x\cdot v}|^{2}\geq 0.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

(Linear programming bound for
b
-symbol distance)
For given integers
q
,
n
,
d_{b}
with
1\leq d_{b}\leq n
, we have

A_{b}(n,d,q)\leq\max\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}^{b}

subject to the constraints

(1)

B_{0}^{b}=1
, and
B_{i}^{b}=0
for
1\leq i\leq d_{b}-1
.

(2)

B_{i}^{b}\geq 0
for
d_{b}\leq i\leq n
.

(3)

\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}^{b}K_{k}^{N,q}(iq^{b-1})\geq 0
for all integers
0\leq k\leq n
, where
N=\frac{(q^{b}-1)n}{q-1}
.

Corollary 5.7
.

(Linear programming bound for the Hamming distance)
For given integers
q
,
n
,
d_{H}
(
b=1
) with
1\leq d_{H}\leq n
, we have

A_{H}(n,d,q)\leq\max\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}

subject to the constraints

(1)

B_{0}=1
, and
B_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq d_{H}-1
.

(2)

B_{i}\geq 0
for
d_{H}\leq i\leq n
.

(3)

\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}K_{k}^{n,q}(i)\geq 0
for all integers
0\leq k\leq n
.

To illustrate the above linear programming bound, we provide an explicit example.

Example 5.8
.

Let
b=2,q=2,n=11
and
d_{b}=10
. Then
N=33
and
B_{0}^{b}=1
and
B_{i}^{b}=0
for
1\leq i\leq 9
. Thus linear programming problem is

(5.1)

\text{maximize}\ \ z=1+B_{10}^{b}+B_{11}^{b}

subject to constraints

\begin{split}B_{10}^{b},B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
K_{k}^{33}(0)+K_{k}^{33}(10\cdot 2)B_{10}^{b}+K_{k}^{33}(11\cdot 2)B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\ \ \ \text{ for }0\leq k\leq 11\\
i.e.,K_{k}^{33}(0)+K_{k}^{33}(20)B_{10}^{b}+K_{k}^{33}(22)B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\ \ \ \text{ for }0\leq k\leq 11\end{split}

By evaluating values of
K_{k}^{33}(0),K_{k}^{33}(20),K_{k}^{33}(22)
for
0\leq k\leq 11
, we have

\begin{split}1+B_{10}^{b}+B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
33-7B_{10}^{b}-11B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
528+8B_{10}^{b}+44B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
5456+56B_{10}^{b}-44B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
40920-160B_{10}^{b}-220B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
237336-112B_{10}^{b}+748B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
1107568+904B_{10}^{b}-308B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
4272048-456B_{10}^{b}-2508B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
13884156-2652B_{10}^{b}+4488B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
38567100+3380B_{10}^{b}+1760B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
92561040+4264B_{10}^{b}-13156B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
193536720-10088B_{10}^{b}+9316B_{11}^{b}&\geq 0\\
\end{split}

Upon solving this linear programming problem, we obtain
z\cong 5.71428
. Therefore, by LP bound,
A_{2}(11,10,2)\leq 5
. In comparison, sphere-packing bound (in
[
1
]
) provides
A_{2}(11,10,2)\leq 26
, while Johnson bound (in
[
8
]
) gives
A_{2}(11,10,2)\leq 16
. Also, an exhaustive computer search confirms that the exact value of
A_{2}(11,10,2)=4
.

Conclusion

Codes in the Hamming distance are effective for communication channels that process one symbol at a time, such as binary symmetric channels, where errors typically affect individual symbols. These channels can correct errors by measuring the Hamming distance between codewords, which counts the number of positions where two codewords differ. However, there are channels that read multiple symbols simultaneously, such as in high-density data storage systems, where physical limitations or resolution constraints lead to errors affecting multiple symbols at once. In these cases, traditional Hamming distance codes are insufficient, as they are designed to handle errors in single symbols. To address this issue, the concept of
b
-symbol distance (with
b\geq 1
) has been introduced. When
b=1
, this approach coincides with the traditional Hamming distance. By using b-symbol read channels, we can detect and correct errors that affect multiple symbols in these more complex channels, providing a more accurate error correction strategy than the traditional Hamming distance.

Establishing bounds on code size is a central challenge in coding theory. While numerous bounds have been derived for the Hamming distance, relatively few have been known for the
b
-symbol distance. In this paper, we extend the analysis of bounds for
b
-symbol distance and present several new upper and lower bounds. These include the linear programming bound, a recurrence relation on code size, the even Johnson bound, the restricted Johnson bound, the Gilbert-Varshamov-type bound, and the Elias bound for the
b
-symbol read channels with
b\geq 2
. Moreover, we provide examples that illustrate how the Gilbert-Varshamov bound we derive offers a stronger lower bound compared to the one presented in
[
14
]
(see Example
4.11
). We believe there is potential for further improvement in the sphere-packing and Gilbert-Varshamov bounds, and we identify this as a future direction. For practical applications, the next step is to construct codes that achieve these established bounds, ensuring both the reliability and efficiency of these codes. We encourage researchers to explore the construction of codes that meet the bounds presented in this paper.

Abstract
This work is concerned with a distributed integrated sensing and edge artificial intelligence (ISEA) system, where distributed devices perform target sensing and then offload locally estimated features to the access point (AP) for collaborative inference. The inference performance of this system is naturally determined by two levels: sensing and communication. We explore how priors can be exploited to enhance inference performance at both levels. At the sensing level, a Bayesian estimator under a Gaussian mixture (GM) prior is proposed, which yields the posterior by weighting class-conditional posterior with responsibilities. At the communication level, given a discriminative prior, optimal transceiver designs in terms of closed-form power allocation are derived for both time-division multiplexing (TDM) and frequency-division multiplexing (FDM) settings, revealing threshold-based and dual-decomposition structures. Experimental results verify that Bayesian estimator outperforms maximum likelihood (ML) at low SNR and discriminant-aware transceiver designs yield additional inference gains.



I
Introduction

To support latency-sensitive applications such as smart factories, digital twins, and the low-altitude economy, the sixth generation (6G) of wireless networks is expected to extend its functional scope by integrating sensing capabilities, thereby giving rise to the paradigm of integrated sensing and communication (ISAC)
[
21
]
. In this fashion, massive sensory data needs to be processed in real time, which naturally involves three tightly coupled processes, namely sensing, communication, and computation, as illustrated in Fig.
1
.
From the computational perspective, intelligent computation is evolving toward an edge artificial intelligence (AI) assisted offloading architecture, which supports heterogeneous computing
[
25
]
, specialized learning algorithms
[
10
]
, and joint environmental perception with edge inference
[
33
]
. This architecture is referred to as integrated sensing and edge AI (ISEA)
[
23
]
.

An efficient ISEA system relies on two key aspects: sensing-level enhancement and communication-level optimization
[
23
]
. The former aims to improve data quality from the sensing source through joint optimization of sensing error
[
32
]
and multi-view sensing aggregation
[
7
]
. The latter focuses on the cooperative multi-user communication, giving rise to a rich set of relevant techniques such as interference cancellation
[
27
]
, time-slot and power allocation
[
11
]
, and over-the-air computation (AirComp)
[
28
,
5
,
22
]
. A key design principle for both aspects is to enhance the inference performance by maximizing task-relevant semantic information, thereby shifting the focus from intrinsic observation to task-oriented design
[
23
]
.

In view of this shift, both sensing and communication designs should incorporate task-relevant priors by modeling the parameter of interest as a random variable rather than a deterministic constant, as in classical maximum likelihood (ML) methods
[
17
]
. From the estimation-theoretic perspective, it corresponds to Bayesian estimation
[
17
]
. From the information-theoretic perspective, it can be interpreted as task-aware optimization given priors, such as maximizing semantic mutual information
[
4
]
or minimizing Bayes risk
[
8
]
.

:
The considered ISEA system consists of a single common target and one AP. Multiple devices first transmit probing signals for target sensing and then offload locally extracted features to the AP by AirComp.

Within a Bayesian framework, existing ISEA studies adopt a Gaussian mixture (GM) prior at the sensing level and employ AirComp for multi-access aggregation at the communication level
[
7
,
24
,
32
]
. Specifically,
[
32
]
considered the sensing process with a GM prior and proposed a task-oriented AirComp framework for ISEA, where the Mahalanobis distance (MD) between two classes is adopted as a discriminant proxy for inference performance. This scheme was further proved to preserve the superior scaling law for max-value aggregation
[
24
]
. From an analytical perspective,
[
7
]
refined the multi-access process and compared AirComp with orthogonal multiple access (OMA). Their analysis demonstrates that AirComp substantially outperforms OMA and achieves superior asymptotic scaling behavior, particularly when the number of receive antennas at the access point (AP) is limited
[
7
]
.

ISEA remains in its infancy, and most existing studies do not properly exploit the prior information. While a GM prior is assumed, it is rarely incorporated explicitly into estimator design. Instead, these studies typically rely on the maximum likelihood (ML) estimator
[
7
,
24
,
32
,
5
,
27
,
11
]
. However, properly exploiting priors at the sensing stage is crucial, since the optimal estimator is inherently task-dependent, e.g., the Bayes-optimal estimator is the MMSE estimator under a squared error loss
[
17
]
. Furthermore, how to exploit priors at the communication level remains underexplored, due to the lack of a unified framework to characterize the relationship between task performance and AirComp error. Even with the introduction of task-aware proxies (e.g., MD in
[
32
]
), the performance gap between proxy-optimal and AirComp-optimal designs remains unclear.

Motivated by the aforementioned perspectives, this paper investigates a distributed ISEA system with the objective of maximizing inference performance, as illustrated in Fig.
1
. Here, inference refers to determining the category given a specific sensing sample and represents a task-oriented classification process. The inference performance of this system is naturally
determined by two levels: sensing and communication. We
explore how priors can be exploited to enhance inference performance at both levels. The key contributions of this work are summarized as follows.

•

We establish a unified framework that jointly exploits the prior information at both the sensing and communication levels. At the sensing level, we develop a responsibility-weighted Bayesian (RWB) estimator, which formulates the global posterior mean by weighting class-conditional posteriors with their associated responsibilities. At the communication level, we derive closed-form power allocation solutions for both TDM and FDM settings given discriminative priors, revealing threshold-based structures and a dual-decomposition interpretation.

•

We provide theoretical insights at both sensing and communication levels. At the sensing level, the RWB estimator is interpreted using variational Bayes and conditional entropy. At the communication level, we characterize the relationship between AirComp aggregation error and classification performance, thereby providing a formal justification for two performance proxies, termed the computation-optimal and decision-optimal proxies. Furthermore, we explicitly establish the analytical connection between these two proxies.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
, the system model is presented. Section
III
introduces the RWB estimator and provides a theoretical analysis from the perspectives of variational Bayes and conditional entropy. In Section
IV
, we establish two performance proxies and subsequently derive the optimal transceiver designs for both TDM and FDM settings. Finally, Section
V
presents the numerical results, and Section
VI
concludes the paper.

In this section, we investigate the exploitation of prior information at the sensing level, aiming to estimate the truth vector

III-A

Maximum Likelihood Estimation

The classical ML estimation treats the truth vector
\bm{x}
as a deterministic but unknown parameter
[
17
]
. Based on the observations in (

\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\mathsf{ML}}=\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},

(11)

where
\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\mathsf{ML}}=[\hat{x}_{k,1}^{\mathsf{ML}},\dots,\hat{x}_{k,M}^{\mathsf{ML}}]^{\top}
. The element-wise MSE is

\mathrm{MSE}^{\mathsf{ML}}_{k}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[|\hat{x}_{k,m}^{\mathsf{ML}}-x_{m}|^{2}\right]=\sigma_{k}^{2},\ \forall m.

(12)

III-B

Bayesian Estimation

Different from ML estimation, Bayesian estimation treats
\bm{x}
as a random vector and aims at estimating its particular realization by MAP or minimum mean squared error (MMSE). Specifically, MAP estimation is

\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\mathsf{MAP}}=\arg\max_{\bm{x}}p(\bm{x}|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k})\overset{(a)}{=}\arg\max_{\bm{x}}p(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}|\bm{x})p({\bm{x}}),

(13)

while MMSE estimation is

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\mathsf{MMSE}}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}-\hat{\bm{x}}\|^{2}|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\int\|\bm{x}-\hat{\bm{x}}\|^{2}p(\bm{x}|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k})d{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}\arg\min_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\int\|\bm{x}-\hat{\bm{x}}\|^{2}p(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}|\bm{x})p({\bm{x}})d{\bm{x}},

(14)

where
(a)
and
(b)
follow from Bayes’ theorem:
p(\bm{x}|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k})\propto p({\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}}|\bm{x})\times p({\bm{x}})
, the likelihood function
p({\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}}|\bm{x})
in (
13
) and (
14
) is determined by sensing model in (
2
) and
p({\bm{x}})
is the prior distribution given in (
4
). For a specific class
\ell
, both
p({\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}}|\bm{x})
and
p({\bm{x}})
are Gaussian. Within this Gaussian model, the MAP estimator coincides with the MMSE estimator, i.e.,
\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\mathsf{MAP}}=\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\mathsf{MMSE}}

[
17
, p. 358]
. Hence, we focus on MMSE estimator below. Since the multiplication of two Gaussians is still a Gaussian, we have

p(\bm{x}|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\mu}_{\ell},\bm{\Sigma})=\mathcal{N}(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell},\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}),

(15)

where

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}_{\ell}+\bm{\Sigma}(\bm{\Sigma}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M\times M})^{-1}(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}-\bm{\mu}_{\ell}),

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}-\bm{\Sigma}(\bm{\Sigma}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M\times M})^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}.

In terms of each element, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{\ell,m}=\frac{\sigma_{m}^{2}\tilde{x}_{k,m}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\mu_{\ell,m}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}},\quad\bar{\sigma}_{m}^{2}=\frac{\sigma_{m}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}}.

(16)

Then, we can obtain the MMSE estimator as

\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\mathsf{MMSE}}=\frac{\sigma_{m}^{2}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}+\frac{\sigma_{k}^{2}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}}\bm{\mu}_{\ell},

(17)

The element-wise MSE is

\mathrm{MSE}^{\mathsf{MMSE}}_{k}=\mathbb{E}\left[|\hat{x}_{k,m}^{\mathsf{MMSE}}-x_{m}|^{2}\right]=\frac{\sigma_{m}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}},\forall m.

(18)

By comparing (
12

\mathrm{MSE}^{\mathsf{MMSE}}_{k}\leq\mathrm{MSE}^{\mathsf{ML}}_{k}
and equality holds only when the prior is non-informative (e.g.,

).

In practice, the optimal
\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathsf{MMSE}}^{(k)}
relies on
\bm{\mu}_{\ell}
as (
17
). However, exploiting a well-calibrated prior for the class means is challenging, see the following Remark.

Remark 1
.

Since the true class varies across observations, the class-conditional prior in (
4
) can be invoked in (
17

\bm{\mu}_{\ell}
is known.
\bm{\mu}_{\ell}

Definition 1

III-C
3
Variational Analysis of the RWB Estimator

To gain further insight, we analyse the RWB estimator from the perspective of variational Bayes
[
2
, Ch. 10]
. Let
\bm{\eta}\triangleq\{\{\bm{\mu}_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L},\bm{\Sigma},\{\pi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}\}
denotes all model parameters and let
\bm{\omega}\triangleq\{\bm{v},\bm{x}\}
collects all latent variables. Our goal is to estimate the truth vector
\bm{x}
from noisy observations
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}
given prior
\bm{\eta}
, which can be formulated as

\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\mathsf{RWB}}=\arg\min_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}-\hat{\bm{x}}\|^{2}|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\eta}]=\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{x}\mid\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\eta}\right].

(26)

When
\bm{\eta}
is unknown and must be learned, we typically maximize the marginal likelihood as

p(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}\mid\bm{\eta})=\int p(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\omega}\mid\bm{\eta})d\bm{\omega},

(27)

which is often intractable due to the high-dimensional integration over
\bm{\omega}
. A variational distribution
q(\bm{\omega})
is introduced to approximate
p(\bm{\omega}\mid\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\eta})
in variational Bayes
[
2
, Ch. 10]
, and the approximation is quantified by the KL divergence as
\mathsf{KL}\left(q(\bm{\omega})||p(\bm{\omega}\mid\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\eta})\right)
. Then, rewriting (
27
) as log likelihood with
q(\bm{\omega})
yields

\displaystyle\log p(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}\mid\bm{\eta})

\displaystyle=\log\int q(\bm{\omega})\frac{p(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\omega}\mid\bm{\eta})}{q(\bm{\omega})}d\bm{\omega}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\geq}\!\!\!\int\!\!q(\bm{\omega})\log\frac{p(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\omega}\mid\bm{\eta})}{q(\bm{\omega})}d\bm{\omega}\triangleq\mathcal{Q}(q,\bm{\eta}),

(28)

where
(a)
follows from Jensen’s inequality, equality holds only when the optimal variational distribution equals the true posterior, i.e.,

\mathsf{KL}\left(q(\bm{\omega})||p(\bm{\omega}\mid\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\eta})\right)=0
and
\mathcal{Q}(q,\bm{\eta})
denotes a functional with respect to
q(\bm{\omega})
. The exact decomposition of (
28
) can be derived as

\log p(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}\mid\bm{\eta})=\mathcal{Q}(q,\bm{\eta})+\mathsf{KL}\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}q(\bm{\omega})||p(\bm{\omega}\mid\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\eta})\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}.

(29)

By comparing (
29
) and (
26
), we observe that when
\bm{\eta}
is fixed,
the posterior responsibility is obtained exactly as

\theta_{\ell}=q(v_{\ell}{=}1)=p\left(v_{\ell}{=}1\mid\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\eta}\right).

(30)

Substituting
\theta_{\ell}
and
p(\bm{x}\mid\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{\eta})
into (
26
) yields the RWB estimator.

III-D

Aggregation Analysis

Given the specific estimator designs, we now investigate their impact on the aggregation gain. Since the receiver is only interested in the specific computation as (
5
), we analyze the aggregation performance in terms of the entropy of the aggregated random variable under different estimators. For simplicity, we consider noise-free aggregation without fading or noise and focus on the aggregation of a single feature dimension, i.e.,

y_{m}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\hat{x}_{k,m},

and omit the subscript
m
in the following for notation simplicity.

When each device adopts the ML estimator in (
12
), the fundamental limit of the achievable aggregation gain is characterized by the conditional entropy
[
9
, Ch. 8]

\displaystyle H\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}x\mid y^{\mathsf{ML}}\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}2\pi e\hbox{$\left[\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{K^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sigma_{k}^{2}}\hbox{$\left]\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}^{-1}\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}

(31)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\geq}\frac{1}{2}\log\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}2\pi e\hbox{$\left[\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}^{2}}\hbox{$\left]\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}^{-1}\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}.

where
y^{\mathsf{ML}}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\hat{{x}}_{k}^{\mathsf{ML}}
,
(a)
follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and equality holds only when all devices have identical sensing noise variances, i.e.,
\sigma_{1}^{2}=\cdots=\sigma_{K}^{2}
. Further, if each device adopts the MMSE estimator in (
24
), the conditional entropy is

\displaystyle H(x\mid y^{\mathsf{MMSE}})\!=\!\frac{1}{2}\log\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}2\pi e\hbox{$\left[\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varrho_{k}\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varrho_{k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}}\hbox{$\left]\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}^{-1}\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$},

(32)

where
y^{\mathsf{MMSE}}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\hat{x}_{k}^{\mathsf{MMSE}}
and
\varrho_{k}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}}
. By comparing (
31
) and (
32
), we can obtain the following insights:

•

Two conditional entropies are structurally equivalent and only differ in the effective noise variance term, i.e.,
\frac{K^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sigma_{k}^{2}}

and
\frac{(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varrho_{k})^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varrho_{k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}}
.

•

When the prior becomes non-informative (i.e.,
{\sigma}^{2}\to\infty
, which implies
\varrho_{k}\to 1
), we have

H\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}x\mid y^{\mathsf{MMSE}}\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}\approx H\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}x\mid y^{\mathsf{ML}}\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}.

•

When the prior is informative, we have
H\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}x\mid y^{\mathsf{MMSE}}\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}\leq H\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}x\mid y^{\mathsf{ML}}\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}
, which follows Chebyshev’s sum inequality and equality holds only when all devices have identical sensing noise variances
[
15
, p. 43]
.

Based on the above analysis, we can conclude that exploiting the prior information at the sensing level reduces the conditional entropy of the aggregated signal and reverts to the ML under the non-informative prior regime. The above discussion assumes a noise-free AirComp aggregation. In the following, we further consider noisy aggregation, and investigate how to exploit priors at the communication level.

IV
Prior-Aided AirComp Transceiver Design

In this section, we study how to exploit the prior knowledge at the communication level. Specifically, two proxies, namely the computation-optimal and decision-optimal proxies, are introduced to quantify the impact of AirComp on classification performance, both based on prior-driven structures.
Second, building on the two proxies, we further derive the optimal transceiver designs for AirComp under both TDM and FDM settings.

IV-A

Decision-Optimal Proxy

The decision-optimal proxy is developed from the perspective of Bayesian hypothesis testing, which establishes a connection between MD and classification accuracy.

Theorem 2
.

The MAP estimator achieves the minimum error probability in classifying

\operatorname{P}_{e}\doteq\exp\left(-\kappa K\operatorname{G}_{\operatorname{min}}\right),

is the cardinality of the set

H(\ell|{\bm{x}})\doteq P_{e}\doteq\exp\left(-KC\right),

(35)

denotes the minimum Chernoff information over all the possible class pairs, achieved by optimizing the parameter

\mathsf{D}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cdot\parallel\cdot)

∎

Definition 2

\displaystyle\operatorname{G}_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\!\!\left({\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\!-\!{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)^{\top}\!{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}\!\left({\bm{\mu}}_{\ell}\!-\!{\bm{\mu}}_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)\!\!\overset{(b)}{=}\!\!\sum_{m=1}^{M}\!\!\operatorname{G}_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\!(x_{m}),

u_{k}\triangleq\frac{|h_{k}|\sqrt{P_{k}}}{\nu_{k}}

u_{1}\leq\cdots u_{k}\leq\cdots u_{K}
. Then,
(\mathrm{P}3)
can be equivalently reformulated as

(\mathrm{P}4)\!\!\quad\max_{{c_{k}}}F(\mathbf{c})\!=\!\frac{\Delta^{\prime}(\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{k})^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{k}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{2}},\quad\!\!\!\text{s.t.}0\leq c_{k}\leq u_{k},\ \forall k.

(\mathrm{P}4)
is a fractional programming problem
[
3
]
. Define
\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{k}=S_{1}
.
Since the denominator of
F(\mathbf{c})
is a convex function,
(\mathrm{P}4)
can be readily verified to be equivalent to

(\mathrm{P}5)\ \min_{\{c_{k}\}}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{k}^{2},\qquad\text{s.t.}\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{k}=S_{1},\\
0\leq c_{k}\leq u_{k},\ \forall k.

(\mathrm{P}5)
is a quadratic programming problem
[
3
]
, whose Lagrangian can be expressed as

\!\!\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{P}5}\!=\!\!\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{k}^{2}\!-\!\!\lambda\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\!c_{k}-\!\!S_{1}\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}\!\!+\!\!\sum_{k=1}^{K}\!\alpha_{k}(c_{k}-u_{k})\!-\!\sum_{k=1}^{K}\beta_{k}c_{k},

where
\lambda
,
\alpha_{k}
, and
\beta_{k}
denote the Lagrange multipliers.
According to the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions
[
3
]
, the optimal solution satisfies

c_{k}^{\star}=\min\{u_{k},\tau\},\quad\tau=\lambda/2\geq 0,

which exhibits a threshold-based structure, where weaker links (i.e., smaller
u_{k}
) saturate first. Without loss of generality, we assume that the first
j
links are saturated, while the remaining
(K-j)
links share a common capping threshold
\tau
, i.e.,

c_{k}=\begin{cases}u_{k},&1\leq k\leq j,\\[6.0pt]
\tau,&j<k\leq K.\end{cases}

(41)

By substituting (
41
) into
F(\mathbf{c})
, we have
F(\tau)=\frac{\Delta^{\prime}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{j}u_{k}+(K-j)\tau\right)^{2}}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{j}u_{k}^{2}\right)+(K-j)\tau^{2}+\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{2}}.

Checking the first derivative of
F(\tau)
yields the stationary point

\tau_{j}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{j}u_{k}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{j}u_{k}},j\geq 1.

If
\tau_{j}\in(u_{j},u_{j+1})
, it serves as the unique maximizer of
F(\tau)
within this segment. Otherwise, the segment-wise optimum occurs at the boundary, i.e.,
\tau=u_{j}
or
\tau=u_{j+1}
.
By comparing all segments, the global threshold
\tau^{\star}
can be obtained.
Accordingly, the optimal
c_{k}^{\star}
values are determined, and the corresponding transmit amplitudes
|b_{k}^{\star}|
can be obtained as (
40
).

Remark 4
.

By comparing (
40
) and (
38
), we observe that both proxies yield the similar optimal solution structure. The resulting MSE and MD performances are also identical, as illustrated in Fig.
4
. The underlying reason is that, in the TDM scheme, the temporal degree of freedom (DoF) do not provide additional gain, since all slots experience the same quasi-static channel realization. Despite known discriminative prior
\Delta_{m}
, the lack of slot heterogeneity makes it impossible to allocate resources across slots according to discriminative features. In this regard, irrespective of the chosen proxy, the resulting solution exhibits the same structural form.

We notice that the discriminative prior is heterogeneous across feature dimensions. To better utilize this prior, it is beneficial for the channel DoF to support corresponding multiplexing, enabling non-uniform resource allocation that aligns with feature importance. We now extend this analysis to the FDM case.

VI
Conclusion

This paper investigates a distributed ISEA system under a Bayesian framework, focusing on incorporating task-relevant priors to maximize inference performance. At the sensing level, an RWB estimator with a GM prior is designed. By weighting class-conditional posterior means with responsibilities, RWB effectively denoises features and outperforms ML at low SNR. At the communication level, two theoretical proxies are introduced: the computation-optimal and decision-optimal proxies. Optimal transceiver designs in terms of closed-form power allocation are derived for both TDM and FDM settings, revealing threshold-based and dual-decomposition structures. Results show that the discriminant-aware allocation yields additional inference gains.

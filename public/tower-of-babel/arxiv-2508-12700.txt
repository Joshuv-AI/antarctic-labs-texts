Abstract
. We study the insulated conductivity problem with inclusions embedded in a bounded domain in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . It was known that in the setting of strictly convex inclusions, the gradient of solutions may blow up as the distance between inclusions approaches 0. The optimal blow-up rate was proved in [ 10 ] and was achieved in the presence of a uniform background gradient field. In this paper, we demonstrate that when the inclusions are partially flat, the gradient of solutions does not blow up under any uniform background fields.

1.
Introduction and Main Results

When two conducting or insulating inclusions are closely located in a composite, the gradient of the solution to the conductivity problem may become arbitrarily large in the narrow region in between them as the distance between the inclusions tends to zero. This phenomenon is known as field concentration, a central topic in the theory of composite materials. The study of field concentration phenomenon has attracted a lot of attention from various authors in recent decades (see, e.g.
[

,

,

[

Let us first describe the mathematical setup of the conductivity problem. Let
n\geq 2
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
contain two disjoint open sets
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
with
\text{dist}(\mathcal{D}_{1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{2},\partial\Omega)>1
. Let
\widetilde{\Omega}:=\Omega\setminus\overline{(\mathcal{D}_{1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{2})}
and
\varepsilon=\text{dist}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
. The conductivity problem can be modeled by the following elliptic equation:

\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}\Big((k\,\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{2}}+\chi_{\widetilde{\Omega}})(x)\,D{u}\Big)=0&\mbox{in}~\Omega,\\
u=\varphi(x)&\mbox{on}~\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
k
represents the conductivity of the inclusions
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
,

represents the voltage potential, and
-Du
stands for the electric field. For the case when

is bounded away from

\infty

[
7
,
17
,
16
]
that
Du
is always uniformly bounded independent of
\varepsilon

degenerates to either
\infty

, the gradient of solutions may blow up when

.

In the case when
k=\infty
, Eq.(

) reduces to the following perfect conductivity problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=0&\mbox{in}~\widetilde{\Omega},\\
u=U_{j}&\mbox{on}~\partial\mathcal{D}_{i},\,i=1,2,\\
\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{i}}\partial_{\nu}u\,d\sigma=0&i=1,2,\\
u=\varphi&\mbox{on}~\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where

is some constant determined by the third line.
When
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are strictly convex sets, the optimal

was shown in
[
2
,
3
,
20

21
,
5
,
6
]
to be

\begin{cases}\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega})}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}(\partial\Omega)}&\mbox{when}~n=2,\\
\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega})}\leq C|\varepsilon\ln\varepsilon|^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}(\partial\Omega)}&\mbox{when}~n=3,\\
\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega})}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}(\partial\Omega)}&\mbox{when}~n\geq 4.\end{cases}

However, when

\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
have partially flat boundary near their closest points, it was proved in
[
8
]
that

remains uniformly bounded independent of
\varepsilon
.

In the case when
k=0
, Eq.(
1.1
) becomes the following insulated conductivity problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=0&\mbox{in}~\widetilde{\Omega},\\
\partial_{\nu}u=0&\mbox{on}~\partial\mathcal{D}_{i},\,i=1,2,\\
u=\varphi&\mbox{on}~\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

When
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are strictly convex sets in

[
10
,
9

15

, and is related to the principal curvatures of the inclusions.

Remark 1.2
.

Any

\varphi=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N(k)}{f}_{k,i}(r,x_{n})Y_{k,i}(\xi).

In (

A physically significant example of boundary data is the uniform background field

1.1.
Difficulty and our strategy

We briefly explain the main difficulty of the problem in comparison to the setting of strictly convex insulators. An important ingredient in
[
10
]
is the estimate (
2.1
) with
r_{0}=0
. The authors used the orthogonality of the spherical harmonics to obtain an optimal control of
\operatorname{osc}_{\Omega_{x,\eta}}u
that appeared on the right-hand side of (
2.1
). That argument is not applicable to the case when
r_{0}>0
, due to the fact that
|x^{\prime}|
and
\eta:=\frac{1}{4}(\varepsilon+(|x^{\prime}|-r_{0})_{+}^{2})^{1/2}
are not always comparable when
r_{0}>0
. Therefore, we rely on more refined and delicate estimates for each mode in the spherical harmonics decomposition (
2.12
).

By the linearity of equation (
1.2
), it suffices to prove the uniform boundedness of
Du
in Theorem
1.1
for
\varphi
consisting of a single mode. A key ingredient is Lemma
2.3
, which shows that if the boundary data
\varphi
contains only a single mode, then so does the solution
u
. We treat separately the cases when
u
contains a single nonzero mode and when
u
contains a single zero mode, and prove Theorem
1.1
in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively.

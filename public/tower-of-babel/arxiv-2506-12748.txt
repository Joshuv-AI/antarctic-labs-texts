Operators of Dirac’s theory with mass and axial chemical potential
Ion I. Cotăescu

Abstract
The Dirac equation with mass and axial chemical potential is solved analytically obtaining the mode spinors and corresponding projection operators giving the spectral representations of the principal conserved operators. In this framework, the odd partner of the Pryce spin operator is defined for the first time showing how these operators may be combined for defining the particle and antiparticle spin and polarization operators of Dirac’s theory of massive fermions either in the free case or in the presence of the axial chemical potential. The quantization procedure is applied in both these cases obtaining two distinct operator algebras in which the particle and antiparticle spin and polarization operators take canonical forms. In this approach statistical operators with independent particle and antiparticle vortical chemical potentials may be constructed.

Introduction

For understanding the rotation effects in quark-gluon plasma one releases on the helicity operator of Dirac’s theory

or even on the projection of the spin operator on a given direction (see for instance

[

[
6
,
7
]
. We consider this discrepancy as an opportunity because this could be the starting point to an approach in which the particle and antiparticle polarization operators may be defined independently. The principal goal of this paper is to write down the conserved operators of Dirac’s theory, including those related to the spin and polarization effects, either in the free case or in the presence of chemical potentials. As in the relativistic quantum mechanics (RQM) one cannot interpret the antiparticle terms, we finally construct the corresponding one-particle operators of the quantum field theory (QFT) we need for writing down suitable statistical operators.

In the case of the Dirac free fields we derived already the even polarization operator that can be combined with the helicity one for obtaining the particle and antiparticle polarization operators
[
6
]
. As this operator is related to the Pryce(e) spin operator, we may ask which is the odd spin-type operator corresponding to the helicity one. Solving this problem with the help of the spectral representations we find a new odd vector-operator whose components do not satisfy a
su(2)
algebra but combined with the components of the Pryce(e) operator form a larger
o(4)
algebra. This can be split in two independent
su(2)
algebras generated by the components of the particle and antiparticle spin operators we define here for the first time.

For extending these results to the theory with chemical potentials we start with the Dirac equation of massive fermions having only the vector and axial chemical potentials which may be seen as a new equation as long as the axial term does not commute with the mass term of the Hamiltonian operator. Solving this equation we obtain new mode spinors depending on the axial chemical potential and the corresponding projection operators we need for constructing the spectral representations of the conserved operators. We observe that in the presence of the axial chemical potential the Pryce(e) spin and its associated odd spin-type operator are conserved in spite of the fact that the axial term breaks the spin symmetry defined in Ref.
[
6
]
. Combining these operators we define the particle and antiparticle spin and polarization operators giving their spectral representations. We obtain thus a coherent and flexible approach in which the vortical effects can be studied separately for particles and antiparticles. The principal benefits of this approach are the mode spinors of Dirac’s theory with mass and axial chemical potential as well as the form of the projection operators which depend only on this chemical potential. Based on these results, the conserved one-particle operators of QFT are derived shoving how the field operators of the theory of free field are related to those derived in Dirac’s theory with mass and axial potential.

We start in the next section revisiting the mode spinors of the momentum basis of the Dirac free field, focusing on the projection operators of this basis. The next section is devoted to the spin and polarization operators. Here we define the odd spin-type operator shoving that its components and those of the Pryce(e) spin operator form a
so(4)
algebra that can be split in two independent
su(2)
algebras of particle and antiparticle spin operators. Moreover, the particle and antiparticle polarization operators are defined combining our polarization operator derived in Ref.
[
6
]
with the helicity one. This section ends with ta brief inspection of the momentum-helicity basis and its projection and polarization operators. Section 4 is devoted to the Dirac equation with axial chemical potential which is analytically solved obtaining new mode spinors and corresponding projection operators we use for constructing the new spin and polarization operators in the presence of the axial chemical potential. In the next section we perform the quantization obtaining the operator algebras of the systems without or with axial chemical potential. We show that these systems are different, their field operators being related through a non-trivial Bogolyubov transformation. In the last section we comment on how the statistical operators may be constructed in the theory with mass and axial chemical potential.

In three Appendices we present successively, the algebra of Dirac’s matrices and the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})

3
Even and odd spin and polarization operators

The basis of polarization spinors can be changed at any time,
\xi\to\hat{r}\xi
, by applying a rotation
\hat{r}\in SU(2)

{T}^{s}:\hat{r}\to{T}^{s}_{\hat{r}}

SU(2)

\left({T}^{s}_{\hat{r}(\theta)}\psi_{\xi}\right)(x)=\psi_{\hat{r}(\theta)\xi}(x)\,,

\hat{r}(\theta)

A.4

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\left[u({\bf p},\sigma)\Sigma_{i\,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf n})u^{+}({\bf p},\sigma^{\prime})\right.

(38)

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 39.83385pt-v(-{\bf p},\sigma)\Sigma^{*}_{i,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf n})v^{+}(-{\bf p},\sigma^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle{s}_{i}({\bf p})\,\hat{\Pi}_{+}({\bf p})+{s}_{i}(-{\bf p})\,\hat{\Pi}_{-}({\bf p})\,,

\displaystyle{W}_{s}U_{{\bf p},\sigma}=\sigma\,U_{{\bf p},\sigma}\,,\quad~~~

\displaystyle~~~{W}_{s}V_{{\bf p},\sigma}=-\sigma\,V_{{\bf p},\sigma}\,,

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}s_{i}-\frac{p^{i}\gamma^{0}{\bf p}\cdot{\bf s}}{E(E+m)}+\frac{p^{i}}{2E}\gamma^{5}\,.

\displaystyle\hat{W}_{s}({\bf p})\hat{N}({\bf p})=\sum_{\sigma}\sigma\left(\Pi^{(+)}_{\sigma}+\Pi^{(-)}_{\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle\hat{W}_{s}^{pa}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{W}_{s}({\bf p})+\hat{W}_{s}^{\flat}({\bf p})\right)=w({\bf p})\hat{\Pi}^{(+)}({\bf p})\,,

(58)

\displaystyle\hat{W}_{s}^{ap}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{W}_{s}({\bf p})-\hat{W}_{s}^{\flat}({\bf p})\right)=w(-{\bf p})\hat{\Pi}^{(-)}({\bf p})\,,

guarantee that these are Hermitian conserved and translation invariant operators commuting between themselves. Note that in the case of common polarization, when

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{p}(E_{p}+m)}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2\sigma p\eta_{\sigma}({\bf p})\\
-(E_{p}+m)\eta_{\sigma}({\bf p})\end{array}\right)\,,

may be substituted in Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
) obtaining the mode spinors
U_{h\,{\bf p},\sigma}
and
V_{h\,{\bf p},\sigma}
which constitute the momentum-helicity basis
{\mathfrak{B}}_{h}
. When we use this basis we denote the free field as
\psi_{h}
.

The advantage of the basis
{\mathfrak{B}}_{h}
is that the coressponding projection operators take more comprehensive forms having the Fourier transforms

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}^{(\pm)}_{h\,\sigma}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1\pm\frac{m}{E}\gamma^{0}\right)\left(\frac{1}{4}+\sigma\frac{{\bf p}\cdot{\bf s}}{p}\right)

(67)

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4E}\left(\gamma^{0}{\bf\gamma}\cdot{\bf p}+2\sigma\,p\,\gamma^{5}\right)\,.

The Fourier transforms of the polarization operators can be written now as

\displaystyle W_{h}~\Rightarrow~\hat{W}_{h}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}\sigma\left(\hat{\Pi}_{\sigma}^{(+)}({\bf p})-\hat{\Pi}^{(-)}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\right)

(68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m\gamma^{0}}{E}\frac{{\bf s}\cdot{\bf p}}{p}+\frac{p}{2E}\gamma^{5}=\frac{1}{p}\,{\bf p}\cdot{\hat{\bf S}^{\,\flat}}({\bf p})\,,

\displaystyle W_{h}^{\flat}~\Rightarrow~\hat{W}^{\flat}_{h}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}\sigma\left(\hat{\Pi}_{\sigma}^{(+)}({\bf p})+\hat{\Pi}^{(-)}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\right)

(69)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\bf s}\cdot{\bf p}}{p}=\frac{1}{p}\,{\bf p}\cdot{\hat{\bf S}}({\bf p})=\hat{h}({\bf p})\,,

where
\hat{h}({\bf p})
is the Fourier transform of the helicity operator
W^{\flat}_{h}=h

[
2
,
3
]
. The even polarization operator (
68
), we present here for the first time, is related to the chiral one as in the massless limit we have
\lim_{m\to 0}W_{h}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{5}
. Therefore the particle and antiparticle polarization operators of the massless fermions,

\lim_{m\to 0}W_{h}^{pa/ap}=\frac{1}{4}\,\gamma^{5}\pm\frac{1}{2}\,h\,,

(70)

combine the helical and chiral contributions.

4
Dirac’s theory with mass and axial chemical potential

The Dirac equation of massive fermions with vector (V), axial (A) and helical (H) chemical potentials considered until now
[
3
]

\left(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m+\mu_{V}\gamma^{0}+\mu_{A}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{5}+\mu_{H}\gamma^{0}h\right)\psi(x)=0\,,

(71)

involves the helical term
\mu_{H}\gamma^{0}h
given by the odd operator
h
. As mentioned before, one of our goals is to generalize this term adding the contribution of an even polarization operator. However, for deriving this operator as in the case of the free fields we need to know the mode spinors and the projector operators of the momentum basis.

Therefore, we start solving the Dirac equation with
\mu_{H}=0
that can be put in Hamiltonian form

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\psi_{A}=H_{A}\psi_{A}\,,\quad H_{A}=-i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}\partial_{i}+m\gamma^{0}-\mu_{A}\gamma^{5}\,,

(72)

after performing the substitution
\psi(x)=\psi_{A}(x)e^{i\mu_{V}t}
. The general solutions of this equation,
\psi_{A}\in{\cal F}_{A}\sim{\cal F}
, may be expanded in momentum representation,

\displaystyle\psi_{A}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{A}^{+}(x)+\psi_{A}^{-}(x)

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma}\left[U_{A,{\bf p},\sigma}(x)\hat{\alpha}_{\sigma}({\bf p})+V_{A,{\bf p},\sigma}(x)\hat{\beta}^{*}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\right],

assuming that the mode spinors of negative frequencies are related to the positive frequency ones through the charge conjugation,
V_{A,{\bf p},\sigma}={C}U^{*}_{A,{\bf p},\sigma}
, just as in the free case. Furthermore, after a few manipulation we find the mode spinors,

\displaystyle U_{A,{\bf p},\sigma}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{A}({\bf p},\sigma)\frac{e^{-iE_{p\sigma}t+i{\bf x}\cdot{\bf p}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,,

(74)

\displaystyle V_{A,{\bf p},\sigma}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{A}({\bf p},\sigma)\frac{e^{iE_{p\sigma}t-i{\bf x}\cdot{\bf p}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,,

(75)

expressed in terms of new spinors in momentum representation,

\displaystyle u_{A}({\bf p},\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{p\sigma}(E_{p\sigma}+m)}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(E_{p\sigma}+m)\xi_{\sigma}({\bf p})\\
(2\sigma p-\mu_{A})\xi_{\sigma}({\bf p})\end{array}\right)\,,

\displaystyle v_{A}({\bf p},\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{p\sigma}(E_{p\sigma}+m)}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(2\sigma p-\mu_{A})\eta_{\sigma}({\bf p})\\
-(E_{p\sigma}+m)\eta_{\sigma}({\bf p})\end{array}\right)\,.

corresponding to the energies
[
3
]

E_{p\sigma}=\sqrt{m^{2}+(2\sigma p-\mu_{A})^{2}}=E_{p}-\frac{2\sigma p}{E_{p}}\mu_{A}+{\cal O}(\mu_{A}^{2})\,,

(82)

which depend explicitly on
\mu_{A}
and polarization. The spinors
u_{A}
and
v_{A}
are normalized satisfying similar relations as (
19
) and (
20
) and, consequently, the mode spinors
U_{A}
and
V_{A}
satisfy similar orthonormalization and completeness relations as in the free case, i. e. (
21
), (
22
) and (
23
). Therefore, the set
{\mathfrak{B}}_{A}=\{U_{A,{\bf p},\sigma},\,V_{A,{\bf p},\sigma}\}
represents a new ”rotated” basis in
{\cal F}={\cal F}_{A}^{+}\oplus{\cal F}_{A}^{-}
, giving a new splitting of
{\cal F}
in subspaces of spinors of positive and negative frequencies,
{\cal F}_{A}^{\pm}\not={\cal F}^{\pm}
. More specific, the transformation between the basis
{\mathfrak{B}}_{A}
and that of the free case,
{\mathfrak{B}}_{h}
, has non-vanishing particle-antiparticle matrix elements,
\langle U_{A,{\bf p},\sigma},V_{h\,{\bf p},\sigma}\rangle\propto\mu_{A}
as we shall see later.

The above solutions can be obtained only if the polarization spinors are those of the helicity basis such that there are no longer spin degrees of freedom and the spin symmetry is broken. Nevertheless, conserved even and odd spin operators which commute with the Hamiltonian (
72
) can be constructed generalizing the definition (
38
) while the conserved polarization operators can be defined using spectral representations in terms of projection operators. As in the previous case, the new projection operators
\Pi^{(\pm)}_{A\,\sigma}
may be defined giving their Fourier transforms,

\displaystyle\Pi^{(+)}_{A\,\sigma}~~~\Rightarrow~~~\hat{\Pi}^{(+)}_{A\,\sigma}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{A}({\bf p},\sigma)u_{A}({\bf p},\sigma)^{+}\,,

(83)

\displaystyle\Pi^{(-)}_{A\,\sigma}~~~\Rightarrow~~~\hat{\Pi}^{(-)}_{A\,\sigma}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{A}(-{\bf p},\sigma)v_{A}(-{\bf p},\sigma)^{+}\,,

(84)

that, after a little calculation, can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}^{(\pm)}_{A\,\sigma}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1\pm\frac{m\gamma^{0}}{E_{p\sigma}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{4}+\sigma\frac{{\bf s}\cdot{\bf p}}{p}\right)\pm\left(1-2\sigma\frac{\mu_{A}}{p}\right)\frac{\gamma^{0}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot{\bf p}+2\sigma p\gamma^{5}}{4E_{p\sigma}}\,.

(85)

These projection operators have complicated Fourier transforms but that can be manipulated using algebraic codes on computer. We may verify thus that these operators form a complete system of orthogonal projection operators. Moreover, we may define the operators of number of particles (pa) and antiparticles (ap),

N_{A}^{pa}=\sum_{\sigma}\Pi_{A\,\sigma}^{(+)}=\Pi_{A}^{(+)}\,,\quad N_{A}^{ap}=-\sum_{\sigma}\Pi^{(-)}_{A\,\sigma}=-\Pi_{A}^{(-)}\,,

(86)

and that of the total number of particles
N_{A}=N_{A}^{pa}+N_{A}^{ap}
whose Fourier transform

\hat{N}_{A}({\bf p})=\sum_{\sigma}\left(\hat{\Pi}_{A\,\sigma}^{(+)}({\bf p})-\hat{\Pi}^{(-)}_{A\,\sigma}({\bf p})\right)\,,

(87)

is no longer proportional with that of the Hamiltonian operator (
72
) which now can be represented as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{A}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot{\bf p}+m\gamma^{0}-\mu_{A}\gamma^{5}

(88)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}E_{p\sigma}\left(\hat{\Pi}_{A\,\sigma}^{(+)}({\bf p})-\hat{\Pi}^{(-)}_{A\,\sigma}({\bf p})\right)\,,

since for
\mu_{A}\not=0
the energy (
82
) depends on polarization.

The particle and antiparticle conserved spin operators,
{\bf S}^{pa/ap}_{A}
, may be defined directly assuming that the Fourier transforms of their components are

\displaystyle{\hat{S}}^{pa}_{A\,i}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\left[u_{A}({\bf p},\sigma)\Sigma_{i\,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf p})u_{A}^{+}({\bf p},\sigma^{\prime})\right]\,,

(89)

\displaystyle{\hat{S}}^{ap}_{A\,i}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\left[v_{A}(-{\bf p},\sigma)\Sigma_{i\,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}(-{\bf p})v_{A}^{+}(-{\bf p},\sigma^{\prime})\right]\,,

(90)

where the matrix elements

\Sigma_{i\,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf p})=\xi^{+}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\sigma_{i}\,\xi_{\sigma}({\bf p})

(91)

depend now on the Pauli spinors of the helicity basis. These operators have very complicated and less relevant forms but can be manipulated exploiting only the orthogonality of the spinors
u_{A}
and
v_{A}
. We verify thus that the total spin operator
{\bf S}_{A}={\bf S}^{pa}_{A}+{\bf S}_{A}^{ap}
acts on the field
\psi_{A}
just as in Eq. (
37
) but without generating a spin symmetry. Moreover, we find that they are conserved and translation invariant forming two independent
su(2)
algebras.

The new even and odd conserved polarization operators,
W_{A}
and respectively
W_{A}^{\flat}=W_{A}N_{A}
, defined by the spectral representations

\displaystyle W_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}\sigma\left(\Pi_{A\,\sigma}^{(+)}-\Pi^{(-)}_{A\,\sigma}\right)\,,

(92)

\displaystyle W_{A}^{\flat}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}\sigma\left(\Pi_{A\,\sigma}^{(+)}+\Pi^{(-)}_{A\,\sigma}\right)\,,

(93)

have the Fourier transforms,

\displaystyle\hat{W}_{A}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}\sigma\left(\hat{\Pi}_{A\,\sigma}^{(+)}({\bf p})-\hat{\Pi}^{(-)}_{A\,\sigma}({\bf p})\right)

(94)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}2\sigma\left[\frac{m\gamma^{0}}{E_{p\sigma}}\left(\frac{1}{4}+\sigma\frac{{\bf s}\cdot{\bf p}}{p}\right)+\left(1-2\sigma\frac{\mu_{A}}{p}\right)\frac{\gamma^{0}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot{\bf p}+2\sigma p\gamma^{5}}{4E_{p\sigma}}\right]\,,

\displaystyle\hat{W}^{\flat}_{A}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}\sigma\left(\hat{\Pi}_{A\,\sigma}^{(+)}({\bf p})+\hat{\Pi}^{(-)}_{A\,\sigma}({\bf p})\right)=\frac{{\bf s}\cdot{\bf p}}{p}=\hat{h}({\bf p})\,.

(95)

We observe that the odd polarization operator,
W^{\flat}_{A}=h
, is not affected by the presence of chemical potentials while the even one,
W_{A}
, depends on
\mu_{A}
. With these commuting operators we define the operators of particle and antiparticle polarization,

W_{A}^{pa}=\frac{1}{2}\left(W_{A}+h\right)\,,\quad W_{A}^{ap}=\frac{1}{2}\left(W_{A}-h\right)\,,

(96)

which are conserved and translation invariant commuting with
H_{A}
and
P^{i}
. They act on the mode spinors (
4
) and (
4
) as

\displaystyle{W}_{A}^{pa}U_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma}=\sigma\,U_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma}\,,~~~~~~\quad

\displaystyle{W}_{A}^{pa}V_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma}=0\,,

(97)

\displaystyle{W}_{A}^{ap}U_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma}=0\,,\hskip 39.83385pt\quad

\displaystyle{W}_{A}^{ap}V_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma}=-\sigma\,V_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma}\,,

(98)

pointing out independently the particle and antiparticle polarizations. In ultra-relativistic regime, when the momentum is extremely large, we have
p>\mu_{A}
and we can take
m=0
. In this limit we find again

\lim_{m\to 0}W_{A}^{pa/ap}=\frac{1}{4}\,\gamma^{5}\pm\frac{1}{2}\,h\,,

(99)

as in the free case.

As a general conclusion we may say that the theory at the level of RQM is difficult involving complicated Fourier transforms as, for example, in Eqs. (
85
) and (
94
). Bearing in mind that similar difficulties of Pryce’s theory of spin operator were solved at the level of QFT
[
6
]
, we shall use all the above results for performing the quantization.

5
Field and one-particle operators of QFT

Here we discuss two systems, the Dirac free field with
\mu_{A}=0
having the mode spinors of the basis
{\mathfrak{B}}
and the Dirac fields with axial chemical potential
\mu_{A}\not=0
whose mode spinors form the basis
{\mathfrak{B}}_{A}
. The first system was revisited recently
[
6
,
7
]
applying
the Bogolyubov method of quantization
[
14
]
which consists in replacing the wave spinors with field operators
(\alpha,\alpha^{*})\to({\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{a}}^{{\dagger}})
and
(\beta,\beta^{*})\to({\mathfrak{b}},{\mathfrak{b}}^{{\dagger}})
in the expansion (
3
) for obtaining the field operator

\displaystyle\Psi(x)=\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma}\left[U_{{\bf p},\sigma}(x){\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})+V_{{\bf p},\sigma}(x){\mathfrak{b}}^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\right]\,.

(100)

The field operators
{\mathfrak{a}}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}
satisfy canonical anti-commutation rules
[
6
,
7
]
and define the vacuum state
|0\rangle
as
{\mathfrak{a}}|0\rangle={\mathfrak{b}}|0\rangle=0
. Moreover, through quantization the expectation value of any conserved operator
X
of RQM becomes the one-particle operator,

X~\Rightarrow~\mathsf{X}=:\langle\Psi,X\Psi\rangle_{D}:

calculated respecting the normal ordering of the operator products
[
11
]
that changes the sign of the antiparticle term. In this manner we obtained a large algebra of one-particle operators among them we mention the charge operator
\mathsf{Q}=\mathsf{N}^{pa}-\mathsf{N}^{ap}
and that of the total number of particles
\mathsf{N}=\mathsf{N}^{pa}+\mathsf{N}^{ap}
, formed by the particle and antiparticle number operators

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{pa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\psi,\Pi^{(+)}\psi\rangle_{D}:=\int d^{3}p\,\sum_{\sigma}{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\vec{p}}){\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\vec{p}})\,,

(101)

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{ap}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:-\langle\psi,\Pi^{(-)}\psi\rangle_{D}:=\int d^{3}p\,\sum_{\sigma}{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\vec{p}}){\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\vec{p}})\,.

(102)

The set of energy and momentum operators,

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\psi,H_{D}\psi\rangle_{D}:

(103)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}p\,E(p)\sum_{\sigma}\left[{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\vec{p}}){\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\vec{p}})+{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\vec{p}}){\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\vec{p}})\right]\,,

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\psi,P^{i}\psi\rangle_{D}:

(104)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}p\,p^{i}\sum_{\sigma}\left[{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\vec{p}}){\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\vec{p}})+{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\vec{p}}){\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\vec{p}})\right]\,.

must be completed now with the new particle and antiparticle spin and polarization operators we defined here,

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{i}^{pa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi,S_{i}^{pa}\Psi\rangle_{D}:

(105)

\displaystyle~~~~~~=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}{\Sigma}_{i\,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf p}){\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p}){\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\,,

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{i}^{ap}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi,S^{ap}_{i}\Psi\rangle_{D}:

(106)

\displaystyle~~~~~~=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}{\Sigma}_{i\,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf p}){\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p}){\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\,.

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}^{pa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi,W^{pa}\Psi\rangle_{D}:=\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma}{\sigma}{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p}){\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\,,

(107)

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}^{ap}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi,W^{ap}\Psi_{A}\rangle_{D}:=\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma}{\sigma}{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p}){\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\,,

(108)

where the matrices
\Sigma_{i}({\bf p})
defined by Eq. (
91
) are given in the Appendix B.

For the system with
\mu_{a}\not=0
, when we have the set of the new mode spinors
{\mathfrak{B}}_{A}
, we may apply the same method of quantization but considering new field operators
\hat{a}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{b}}
and a new corresponding vacuum state
|0A\rangle
satisfying
\hat{\mathfrak{a}}|0A\rangle=\hat{\mathfrak{b}}|0A\rangle=0
. Then the Dirac field (
73
) becomes the new field operator

\displaystyle\Psi_{A}(x)=\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma}\left[U_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma}(x)\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})+V_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma}(x)\hat{\mathfrak{b}}^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\right]\,,

(109)

acting on the same Fock state as
\Psi
. In this case the conserved one-particle operators may be derived using obvious identities as

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi_{A},\Pi^{(+)}_{A\,\sigma}\Psi_{A}\rangle_{D}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}p\,\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\,,

(110)

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi_{A},\Pi^{(-)}_{A\,\sigma}\Psi_{A}\rangle_{D}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int d^{3}p\,\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\,,

(111)

for deriving the charge operator

\displaystyle\mathsf{Q}_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi_{A},\Psi_{A}\rangle_{D}:

(112)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma}\left[\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})-\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\right]\,,

the operator of number of particles,

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi_{A},N_{A}\Psi_{A}\rangle_{D}:

(113)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma}\left[\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})+\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\right]\,,

as well as the Hamiltonian and momentum operators

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi_{A},H_{A}\Psi_{A}\rangle_{D}:

(114)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma}E_{p\sigma}\left[\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})+\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\right]\,,

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{A}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi_{A},-i\partial_{i}\Psi_{A}\rangle_{D}:

(115)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}pp^{i}\sum_{\sigma}\left[\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})+\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\right]\,,

The particle and antiparticle spin and polarization operators we derive here for the first time lead to the one particle operators,

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{A\,i}^{pa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi_{A},S_{A\,i}^{pa}\Psi_{A}\rangle_{D}:

(116)

\displaystyle~~~~~~=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}{\Sigma}_{i\,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\,,

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{A\,i}^{ap}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi_{A},S^{ap}_{A\,i}\Psi_{A}\rangle_{D}:

(117)

\displaystyle~~~~~~=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}{\Sigma}_{i\,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\,.

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}_{A}^{pa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi_{A},W^{pa}_{A}\Psi_{A}\rangle_{D}:=\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma}{\sigma}\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\,,

(118)

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}_{A}^{ap}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\langle\Psi_{A},W^{ap}_{A}\Psi_{A}\rangle_{D}:=\int d^{3}p\sum_{\sigma}{\sigma}\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}({\bf p})\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})\,.

(119)

which have very similar expressions as those of the free theory. However, the crucial difference is that now the field operators
\hat{\mathfrak{a}}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{b}}
are different from those of the free field,
{\mathfrak{a}}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}
.

We can verify that this is true assuming that at the instant
t_{0}
the field
\psi_{A}(x)
satisfies the initial condition

\left.\Psi_{A}(t,{\bf x})\right|_{t=t_{0}}=\left.\Psi_{h}(t,{\bf x})\right|_{t=t_{0}}\,,

(120)

where
\Psi_{h}
is the quantum Dirac free field expanded in the basis
{\mathfrak{B}}_{h}
. Using the inversion formulas (
24
) we may write the field operators of the Dirac field with axial chemical potential as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle U_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma},\Psi_{h}\rangle_{D}|_{t=t_{0}}

(121)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\left[\langle U_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma},U_{h\,{\bf p}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle_{D}|_{t=t_{0}}{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf p}^{\prime})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 39.83385pt+\langle U_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma},V_{h\,{\bf p}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle_{D}|_{t=t_{0}}{\mathfrak{b}}^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf p}^{\prime})\right]\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{h},V_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma}\rangle_{D}|_{t=t_{0}}

(122)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\left[\langle U_{h\,{\bf p}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}},V_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma}\rangle_{D}|_{t=t_{0}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf p}^{\prime})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 39.83385pt+\langle V_{h\,{\bf p}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}},V_{A\,{\bf p},\sigma}\rangle_{D}|_{t=t_{0}}{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}({\bf p}^{\prime})\right]\,,

obtaining a non-trivial Bogolyubov transformation

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A({\bf p},\sigma){\mathfrak{a}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})+B({\bf p},\sigma){\mathfrak{b}}^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(-{\bf p})\,,

(123)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A({\bf p},\sigma){\mathfrak{b}}_{\sigma}({\bf p})+B({\bf p},\sigma){\mathfrak{a}}^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(-{\bf p})\,,

(124)

whose coefficients, derived in the Appendix C, satisfy the conditions
A(-{\bf p},\sigma)=A({\bf p},\sigma)
,
B(-{\bf p},\sigma)=-B({\bf p},\sigma)
and

A({\bf p},\sigma)^{2}+|B({\bf p},\sigma)|^{2}=1\,,

(125)

which guarantees that the field operators satisfy canonical anticommutation rules. We remind the reader that
|B({\bf p},\sigma)|^{2}
gives the probability of measuring free particles in the vacuum state of the system with axial chemical potential,
|0A\rangle
.

On the other hand, the initial condition (
120
) allows us to relate the field operators
\Psi_{h}
and
\Psi_{A}
at any time
t
, taking into account that these fields evolve as

\displaystyle\Psi_{h}(t,{\bf x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\mathsf{H}(t-t_{0})}\Psi_{h}(t_{0},{\bf x})e^{-i\mathsf{H}(t-t_{0})}\,,

(126)

\displaystyle\Psi_{A}(t,{\bf x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\mathsf{H}_{A}(t-t_{0})}\Psi_{A}(t_{0},{\bf x})e^{-i\mathsf{H}_{A}(t-t_{0})}\,.

(127)

Hereby we obtain the unitary transformation

\Psi_{A}(t,{\bf x})=\mathsf{U}(t-t_{0})\Psi_{h}(t,{\bf x})\mathsf{U}^{{\dagger}}(t-t_{0})\,,

(128)

whose unitary operator

\mathsf{U}(t)=e^{i\mathsf{H}_{A}t}e^{-i\mathsf{H}t}

assures the equivalence between the time evolution pictures governed by the Hamiltonian operators
\mathsf{H}
and the
\mathsf{H}_{A}
.

6
Concluding remarks

We studied here systematically the Dirac theory with chemical potentials focusing on the new conserved operators that can be constructed using spectral representations. For applying this method we solved first the Dirac equation with axial chemical potential obtaining new mode spinors and corresponding projection operators. As in the case of the free field, we focused on the spin and polarization operators showing how the helicity and our even polarization operator

After quantization, we obtained the one-particle operators of Dirac’s QFT with mass and axial chemical potential we need for writing down the statistical operator

Appendx A: Dirac representation

We use here the Dirac
\gamma
-matrices in the standard representation with diagonal
\gamma^{0}
. These satisfy
\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}
giving rise to the generators

s^{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{4}\left[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\right]=\overline{s^{\mu\nu}}
of the Dirac reducible representation
\rho_{D}=(\frac{1}{2},0)\oplus(0,\frac{1}{2})
of the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
group in the four-dimensional space
{\cal V}_{D}={\cal V}_{P}\oplus{\cal V}_{P}
of Dirac spinors. All these matrices, including the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
generators, are Dirac self-adjoint such that the transformations

\lambda(\omega)=\exp\left(-\frac{i}{2}\omega^{\alpha\beta}s_{\alpha\beta}\right)\in\rho_{D}[SL(2,\mathbb{C})]\,,

(A.1)

having real-valued parameters,
\omega^{\alpha\beta}=-\omega^{\beta\alpha}
, leave invariant the Hermitian form
\overline{\Psi_{A}}\Psi_{A}
as
\overline{\lambda(\omega)}=\lambda^{-1}(\omega)=\lambda(-\omega)
.

We denote by

r={\rm diag}(\hat{r},\hat{r})\in{\rho_{D}}\left[SU(2)\right]

(A.2)

the transformations we call here simply rotations for which we use the generators

s_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}s^{jk}={\rm diag}(\hat{s}_{i},\hat{s}_{i})=-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{i}\,,\quad\hat{s}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{i}\,,

(A.3)

expressed in terms of Pauli matrices
\sigma_{i}
, and Cayley-Klein parameters
\theta^{i}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}\omega^{jk}
such that

\displaystyle r(\theta)={\rm diag}(\hat{r}(\theta),\hat{r}(\theta))\,,\quad~~\hat{r}(\theta)=e^{-i\theta^{i}\hat{s}_{i}}=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\theta^{i}\sigma_{i}}\,.

(A.4)

Similarly, we chose the parameters
\tau^{i}=\omega^{0i}
and the generators

s_{i0}=s^{0i}=\frac{i}{2}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}\,,

(A.5)

for the Lorentz boosts,

\displaystyle l(\tau)=e^{-i\tau^{i}s_{0i}}\,.

(A.6)

Of a special interest are the Wigner boosts with parameters

\displaystyle l_{{\bf p}}=\frac{E_{p}+m+\gamma^{0}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\cdot{\bf p}}{\sqrt{2m(E_{p}+m)}}=l_{{\bf p}}^{+}\,,\quad l^{-1}_{\bf p}=l_{-{\bf p}}=\bar{l}_{\bf p}\,,

Waves drive the rise and fall of 2D flows in rotating turbulence Preprint: APS/123-QED
Sébastien Gomé, Anna Frishman

Abstract
Turbulence follows a few well-known organizational principles, rooted in conservation laws. One such principle states that a system conserving two sign-definite invariants self-organizes into large-scale structures. Ordinary three-dimensional turbulence does not fall within this paradigm. However, when subject to rotation, 3D turbulence is profoundly altered: rotation produces 3D inertial waves, while also sustaining emergent two-dimensional structures and favoring domain-scale flows called condensates. This interplay raises a fundamental question: why and when are 2D flows sustained even when only 3D waves are excited? Using extensive numerical simulations of the rotating 3D Navier–Stokes equations together with a quasi-linear wave–kinetic theory, we show that near-resonant interactions between 3D waves and a large-scale 2D flow impose an additional conservation law: inertial waves must conserve their helicity separately for each helicity sign. This emergent sign-definite invariant constrains the waves to transfer their energy to large-scale 2D motions. However, as rotation increases, resonance conditions become more restrictive and the energy transfer from 3D to 2D progressively vanishes, leading to a transition from condensate-dominated turbulence to pure inertial-wave turbulence for the 3D modes. We derive analytical expressions for this 3D–2D energy transfer as a function of rotation, Reynolds number and domain geometry, and verify them numerically. Together, these results establish a mechanism underlying two-dimensionalization in rotating turbulence, and, more broadly, illustrate how wave–mean flow interactions can drive large-scale self-organization.



Rotating turbulent flows abound in nature, from industrial to geophysical and even astrophysical flows.
Yet, they remain challenging to understand, as they exhibit signatures of two-dimensional, three-dimensional and wave turbulence.
A guiding principle for understanding turbulence is that out-of-equilibrium fluxes arise through inviscid conservation laws,
determining how the various degrees of freedom interact.

A remarkable manifestation is provided by
systems which conserve two sign-definite, scale-related, quantities,
causing turbulence to self-organize into
large-scale structures.
The archetype system following this principle is the two-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations (2DNSE), in which both energy and enstrophy (the vorticity squared) are conserved and flow in opposite directions:
energy cascades upscale and enstrophy downscale
(
Fjørtoft 1953
;
Kraichnan 1967
)
.
In a finite domain and with low dissipation, energy is ultimately concentrated on the lowest modes and forms a condensate: a strong large-scale coherent flow fueled by small-scale eddies.

Three-dimensional fluids, in contrast, conserve energy and sign-indefinite helicity (the scalar product between velocity and vorticity
(
Moffatt 1969
)
)
and are organized very differently:
both energy and helicity are transferred to small scales
(
Chen et al. 2003
;
Ditlevsen and Giuliani 2001
)
.
However, when a 3D fluid is rotated, its phenomenology fundamentally changes, while its invariants remain the same.
Rotation produces 3D inertial waves
(
Yarom et al. 2013
)
, each with an energy and a helicity of a given sign, while also tending to homogenize the flow along
the rotation
axis, generating two-dimensional motions
(
Godeferd and Moisy 2015
)
.
Once energized, 2D modes seem to exhibit an inverse energy cascade, similarly to 2DNSE, which progressively generates
larger-scale flows
(
Smith et al. 1996
;
Smith and Waleffe 1999
;
Smith and Lee 2005
;
Chen et al. 2005
;
Mininni and Pouquet 2010
;
Yarom et al. 2013
;
Deusebio et al. 2014
;
Campagne et al. 2015
;
Biferale et al. 2016
;
Buzzicotti et al. 2018a
;
Alexakis and Biferale 2018
;
van Kan and Alexakis 2020
;
Lam et al. 2023
;
Shaltiel et al. 2024
)
.
With low dissipation, this leads to the generation of large-scale condensates in these rotating 3D flows
(
Kolvin et al. 2009
;
Rubio et al. 2014
;
Guervilly et al. 2014
;
Le Reun et al. 2017
;
Di Leoni et al. 2020
;
Aguirre Guzmán et al. 2020
;
Kolokolov et al. 2020
;
de Wit et al. 2022
;
Shaltiel et al. 2024
)
.

How and why two-dimensionalization occurs in rotating turbulence
remains unsettled.
Generally, for strong rotation,
nonlinear interactions should be restricted to resonances,
but such 3D-2D interactions have a strictly vanishing coupling
(
Waleffe 1992
;
Cambon et al. 1997
;
Bordes et al. 2012
)
.
This causes 3D and 2D modes to decouple in the limit of infinite rotation, as established mathematically in

(
Babin et al. 1997
;
Babin et al. 1999
)
, which stabilizes 2D motions when those are directly excited
(
Gallet 2015
;
Billant 2021
)
.

However, most experiments and numerical simulations do not reach this asymptotic limit, and energy is observed to be transferred from 3D to 2D modes
(
Smith et al. 1996
;
Smith and Waleffe 1999
;
Smith and Lee 2005
;
Chen et al. 2005
;
Bourouiba and Bartello 2007
;
Mininni and Pouquet 2010
;
Yarom et al. 2013
;
Deusebio et al. 2014
;
Campagne et al. 2015
;
Biferale et al. 2016
;
Buzzicotti et al. 2018a
;
Le Reun et al. 2017
;
Alexakis and Biferale 2018
;
van Kan and Alexakis 2020
;
Lam et al. 2023
;
Shaltiel et al. 2024
)
. This possibly occurs via near-resonant triads
(
Waleffe 1992
;
Smith and Waleffe 1999
;
Smith and Lee 2005
;
Alexakis 2015
;
di Leoni and Mininni 2016
;
Le Reun et al. 2020
)
or four-wave interactions
(
Brunet et al. 2020
)
,
but it is unclear why energy is transferred directionally

from
3D
to
2D.
To date, no mechanism for such a transfer based on the flow’s global inviscid invariants has been established.

Here we focus on two-dimensional condensates in rotating 3D turbulence, and identify such a mechanism in this context.
Large-scale steady condensates
alter the nature of turbulence.
They favor non-locality and a different hierarchy of correlations.
Contrary to homogeneous and isotropic turbulence, the dynamics is dominated by mean-flow-turbulence interactions.
Quasi-Linear (QL) theory (inherited from Rapid Distortion Theory
(
Batchelor and Proudman 1954
)
)
provides a self-consistent perturbative statistical framework for this kind of inhomogeneous turbulence
(
Marston and Tobias 2023
)
.
It has led to analytical predictions of both mean-flow profiles and turbulent correlations in 2DNSE
(
Laurie et al. 2014
;
Kolokolov and Lebedev 2016
;
Frishman 2017
;
Frishman and Herbert 2018
;
Doludenko et al. 2021
)
, and has also been applied to
2D geophysical and plasma systems
(
Diamond et al. 2005
;
Srinivasan and Young 2012
;
Parker and Krommes 2013
;
Marston et al. 2016
;
Woillez and Bouchet 2017
;
Svirsky et al. 2023
)

We investigate the formation of condensates in rotating 3D turbulence using direct numerical simulations (§
I
) and a quasi-linear wave–kinetic theory (§
II
&
III
).
We show that near-resonant interactions enforce a conservation of helicity by sign, causing inertial waves to energize the large-scale 2D flow (§
IV
).
As rotation increases, resonances become increasingly stringent and the coupling vanishes, marking the transition from a condensate-dominated turbulence to an asymptotic turbulence of inertial waves for the 3D modes.
We derive scaling predictions for the 3D–2D energy transfer and confirm them numerically, thereby explaining the origin and evolution of two-dimensionalization (§
V
).

I
Numerical set-up and observations

We consider an incompressible rotating 3D fluid governed by the Navier-Stokes equations (3DNSE)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{u}+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}

\displaystyle=-2\Omega\mathbf{e}_{z}\times\bm{u}-\nabla p+\nu\nabla^{2}\bm{u}+\nu_{h}\nabla^{2h}\bm{u}+\bm{f},

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\rm 2D}+\epsilon_{\rm 3D}=\epsilon,~~~\epsilon_{\rm 2D}\ll\epsilon_{\rm 3D}.

(3)

Nondimensional Reynolds and Rossby numbers defined at the forcing scale

We simulate Eq. (

The inertial range of the forward energy cascade is controlled by an eighth-order hyperviscosity

In the absence of strong bulk dissipation (e.g. Ekman friction

. This is a type of resonance condition for the condensate-wave system, similar to that in wave turbulence, where wave interactions are restricted to triadic resonances in the limit of fast rotation. As a result, interactions between two 3D waves with wavenumbers
\bm{p},\bm{q}
are possible only if

\displaystyle 2p_{z}\Big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega};

(15)

\displaystyle 2p_{z}\Big(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\Big)

\displaystyle\underset{k\ll p}{\simeq}\frac{2\omega_{\bm{p}}^{s}}{\Omega}\lesssim\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega};

(16)

We will establish these conditions in more details and discuss their consequences in §
III
.

Rotation is therefore seen to select only the interactions obeying (
15
)-(
16
) out of the full set of wave-condensate interactions in the 3DNSE, depending only on the value of the rescaled condensate amplitude
\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}
. This implies that, in the limit of fast rotation, the energy transfer
T_{\rm 2D}
is a function of
\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}
only. Hence Eq. (
7
) can be recast in the form

\displaystyle\frac{{{U^{\prime}}}^{2}}{\Omega^{2}}=4Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}~\frac{T_{\rm 2D}}{\epsilon}\Big[\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\Big],

(17)

where

Ro_{\epsilon}\equiv\frac{1}{2\Omega}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{\nu}}=Ro\times Re^{1/2}

(18)

is a large-scale Rossby number emerging when rescaling Eq. (
7
) by
\Omega
, see also
Kolokolov et al. 2020
;
Parfenyev et al. 2021
.

Equation (
17
) describes a self-tuning of the rescaled condensate amplitude,
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
, such that the condensate exactly dissipates the energy input from the waves.
It also implies that
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
is a function of the single parameter
Ro_{\epsilon}
.
This is confirmed in our DNS data, for which the rescaled condensate amplitude
(\int\bm{U}^{2}dy/L_{y})^{1/2}/(L_{y}\Omega)\approx U^{\prime}/\Omega
collapses as a function of the control parameter
Ro_{\epsilon}
for all
Ro_{\epsilon}\lesssim 1
and high
Re
, see Fig.
3
(a).

To obtain the condensate amplitude as a function of
Ro_{\epsilon}
, i.e. solve equation (
17
) for
U^{\prime}/\Omega
, we need to consider the interactions selected by a given value of
U^{\prime}/\Omega
in Eq. (
15
)-(
16
). In particular, for heterochiral-wave interactions, condition (
16
) reads

\displaystyle\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s,-s}\underset{k\ll p}{\simeq}2\omega_{\bm{p}}^{s}=\frac{4\Omega p_{z}}{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim{U^{\prime}}.

(19)

At sufficiently-large rotation,
when
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega\lesssim l_{f}/L_{z}
, no waves
\bm{p}

satisfy condition (
19
) and heterochiral interactions therefore decorrelate.
Thus, for fast enough rotation, we expect that only homochiral-wave interactions would be present.

To verify this in our DNS, we decompose the energy flux to the condensate into contributions from homochiral and heterochiral interactions, by measuring
\Pi^{s_{1},s_{2}}=-\sum_{|\bm{k}|<5}\sum_{\bm{k}\in 2D,\bm{p},\bm{q}\in 3D}\langle\bm{u}_{\bm{k}}\cdot(\bm{u}_{\bm{p}}^{s_{1}}\times{\bm{\omega}_{\bm{q}}^{{s_{2}}})}\rangle\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}
. Here
\Pi^{s,s}
is the transfer between 3D waves with the same chirality and 2D modes (the latter not decomposed into the two helical modes).
Similarly,
\Pi^{s,-s}
is the energy transfer involving waves of opposite chiralities.

We find that
\Pi^{s,-s}=0
below
Ro_{\epsilon}\simeq l_{f}/(2L_{z})=0.1
, see Fig.
3
(a) (we will explain later how this condition on
Ro_{\epsilon}
arises).
In the following, we will focus on the regime
Ro_{\epsilon}\lesssim l_{f}/(2L_{z})
,
where condensate-wave interactions do not couple waves of opposite helicity signs.
The range
Ro_{\epsilon}\gtrsim l_{f}/(2L_{z})
will be analyzed in a forthcoming publication
(
Gomé and Frishman 2025
)
.

III
Kinetic theory for the mean-wave system

III.1
Kinetic equation and near-resonances

The remaining homochiral interactions require additional treatment: 3D waves and 2D modes are expected to decouple in the infinite rotation limit, making a kinetic theory for the mean-wave system subtle. Here we develop such a theory, showing how the constraint (
15
) arises.
Assuming
{U^{\prime}}\ll\Omega
, Eq. (
12
)
can be treated following the procedural steps of wave-kinetic theory
(
Zakharov et al. 2012
;
Galtier 2003
;
Newell and Rumpf 2011
;
Buckmaster et al. 2021
;
Newell and Nazarenko 2025
;
Nazarenko 2011
)
, here applied to an inhomogeneous system.
We focus on correlators
at an arbitrary time
t=0
,

\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{\bm{q}}^{s}\rangle(t=0)=2\delta_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}~e_{\bm{p}}^{s}(0)+\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle(0)\delta_{\bm{k}+\bm{p}+\bm{q}}
,
with
e_{\bm{p}}^{s}\equiv|a_{\bm{p}}^{s}|^{2}/2
the energy of a 3D mode
\bm{p}
and
\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle
an inhomogeneous correlator associated to the single condensate wavenumber
\bm{k}
.
We consider the wave dynamics on a time scale
t=o(1/{U^{\prime}})
which is slow compared to the characteristic wave period
1/\Omega
, and
obtain
a kinetic equation
describing the energy evolution from
t=0
(see Appendix
A
):

\displaystyle\frac{e_{\bm{p}}^{s}(t)-e_{\bm{p}}^{s}(0)}{t}=\epsilon\chi_{\bm{p}}^{s}

\displaystyle~+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t}}{i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t}U_{\bm{k}}^{*}V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}^{ss}\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{\bm{q}}^{s}\rangle^{*}\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}+O\Big(\frac{{U^{\prime}}^{2}}{\Omega^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+(\bm{k}\to-\bm{k})+c.c.

(20)

Because the oscillating factor

\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t}}{i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t}

in Eq. (
20
) decays with
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t
,
mean-wave interactions are restricted to triads such that
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t\ll 1
for
t=o(1/{U^{\prime}})
, with two possibilities:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{Exact resonances: }\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}=0\\
\text{Near-resonances: }\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}\neq 0\text{ and }\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t\ll 1.\end{cases}

Exact resonances are the only contribution to Eq. (
20
) in the asymptotic limit
\Omega t\to\infty
(meaning the limit
Ro\to 0
taken before any other).
However, system (
5
)-(
6
)
obeys the Greenspan property
(
Greenspan 1969b
;
Waleffe 1993
)
:
the coupling to the 2D mode in Eq. (
11
), proportional to

\displaystyle ik_{y}C_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}

\displaystyle=\frac{ik_{y}}{4pp_{\perp}qq_{\perp}}\Big[(p_{y}-q_{y})p_{x}(p_{z}^{2}-pq)

\displaystyle-is(p-q)p_{z}(p_{x}^{2}+p_{y}q_{y}))\Big],

(21)

is null when
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}=0
, i.e when either
(k_{y}=0,q_{y}=-p_{y})
or
(k_{y}=-2p_{y},q_{y}=p_{y})
.
This is a consequence of the triad being constrained by the resonant condition and the conservation of energy and helicity

(
Greenspan 1969b
;
Waleffe 1993
;
Shavit et al. 2025
)
.
Therefore, exactly-resonant triads do not transfer energy to the 2D flow.

However, 3D modes which satisfy the near-resonance condition rather than exact one,
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t\ll 1
and in particular
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}/U^{\prime}\ll 1
,
can
exchange energy with 2D modes.
As the condensate occupies the lowest, box-scale, mode
k_{y}=\pm 2\pi/L_{y}
, it couples with waves such that
q_{y}\simeq-p_{y}
, hence the corresponding homochiral interaction is naturally near-resonant (i.e close to the exactly-resonant triads
k_{y}=0,\bm{p}=-\bm{q}
). Indeed, the beating parameter

\displaystyle\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}=\frac{2\pi}{L_{y}p}\frac{2p_{y}p_{z}\Omega}{p^{2}}+O\Big(\frac{1}{(L_{y}p)^{2}}\Big)

(22)

can be much smaller than
\Omega
(in absolute value), suppressed by a factor of
1/(pL_{y})
for such triads. Note that as these interactions are never exactly resonant, the transfer coefficient Eq. (
21
) is always finite, proportional to
2\pi/L_{y}
.

The oscillating factor can then be modeled as

\displaystyle\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t}}{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t}\simeq\Theta\left(1-\frac{|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}|}{{U^{\prime}}}\right)\equiv\mathbb{1}_{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}<{U^{\prime}}},

(23)

using a
Heaviside filter equal to one
when
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}|/{U^{\prime}}<1
and zero otherwise.
The cutoff at
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}|/U^{\prime}=1
approximates the decay of the oscillating factor
with
|\omega^{ss}_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}|t=o(|\omega^{ss}_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}|/{U^{\prime}})
(see Appendix §
A.1
).
The geometry of a given triad enters the condition (
23
) through the geometric factor
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}|/\Omega
, which determines how close to resonance the triad is. For
\Omega/{U^{\prime}}
large but finite, triads
with a sufficiently small geometric factor are near-resonant (
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}|<{U^{\prime}}
) and
give a secular contribution to (
20
) (see §
A.2
).
However, any such triad becomes off-resonant if
\Omega/{U^{\prime}}
is too large (
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}|\geq{U^{\prime}}
), since
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}|/\Omega>0
. Such triads will therefore not contribute to (
20
) and will decouple from the 2D flow.
Note that while it is formally more accurate to approximate the oscillating factor as a smooth function of
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
, and thereby capture a
smooth transition from near- to off-resonances (See §
A.1
), in most of this paper we use a sharp Heaviside filter for simplicity.

With our modeling of the oscillating factor in (
23
), we finally obtain a kinetic equation at
O({U^{\prime}}/\Omega)
(see the discussion of the order in Appendix
A.2
),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}e_{\bm{p}}^{s}=

\displaystyle\epsilon\chi_{\bm{p}}^{s}+\frac{1}{2}U_{\bm{k}}^{*}V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}^{ss}\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{\bm{q}}^{s}\rangle^{*}\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}~\mathbb{1}_{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}<{U^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle+(\bm{k}\to-\bm{k})+c.c.,

(24)

describing the evolution of the wave energy of mode
\bm{p}
due to a near-resonant coupling with the condensate mode
\bm{k}
, where most of the 2D energy is concentrated.

Similarly to classical wave-turbulence theory, here the order of limits
l_{f}/L\to 0
and
\Omega t\to\infty
matters. Taking the infinite box limit first, followed by
\Omega t\to\infty
, leads to
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}|/\Omega\to 0
in (
22
), so that the 2D condensate and 3D waves remain coupled. On the contrary, in the opposite order of limits, near-resonances disappear and the 2D mode decouples from the 3D waves. The former limit is the classical weak wave-turbulence limit
(
Nazarenko 2011
;
Galtier 2003
)
, and can also be considered in the absence of a condensate, for a homogeneous 2D flow. As interactions are dominated by near-resonances in this limit, we expect 2D and 3D modes to remain coupled,
as they do here with a condensate.
However, within the standard weak wave-turbulence derivation
(
Nazarenko 2011
;
Galtier 2003
)
, the transfer coefficient between 3D and 2D modes entering the kinetic equation is evaluated on the resonant manifold,
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}=0
, where it vanishes, resulting in decoupling instead. This implies that wave-turbulence theory may need to be refined to properly capture the coupling between waves and 2D modes in this limit, as we discuss in
A.3
.

III.2
Kinetic equation in the limit of scale separation
l_{f}/L_{y}\ll 1

In the limit
l_{f}/L_{y}\ll 1
, where there is scale separation between the 2D flow and the 3D waves, the kinetic equation Eq. (
24
) can be greatly simplified.
In this limit, nearly-resonant interactions such that
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}<{U^{\prime}}
(evaluated at the forcing scale
k_{f}
) obey the energy balance

\displaystyle\partial_{t}e_{\bm{p}}^{s}=\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigg[p_{x}\partial_{p_{y}}+\frac{p_{x}p_{y}}{p^{2}}\Bigg]\Phi_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{s}+\epsilon\chi_{\bm{p}}^{s},

(25)

\displaystyle\text{with }\Phi_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{s}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle+\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle\Big)+c.c,

obtained from Eq. (
24
) by expanding the coupling coefficient
V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}^{ss}
at the lowest order in
l_{f}/L_{y}
(see Appendix
B
and
(
Kolokolov et al. 2020
)
for a similar treatment).
The correlator

\Phi_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{s}

is responsible for
the energy transfer between the 3D waves and the 2D condensate,
as it determines the Reynolds stress correlator between modes
\bm{p}
and
\bm{p}\pm\bm{k}
with
\bm{k}=2\pi/L_{y}~\mathbf{e}_{y}
:

\displaystyle\langle uv\rangle_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{ss}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\langle u_{\bm{p}}^{s}v_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle+\langle u_{\bm{p}}^{s}v_{-\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle}{2}+c.c

\displaystyle=-\frac{p_{x}p_{y}}{p^{2}}\Phi_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{s}+O\Big(\frac{1}{Lk_{f}}\Big).

(26)

The correlator
\langle uv\rangle_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{ss}
contributes to the total Reynolds stress

\langle uv\rangle=\left(\sum_{\bm{p},s}\langle uv\rangle_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{ss}\right)\cos(k_{y}y)
and enters the mean-flow energy balance:

\displaystyle\nu{U^{\prime}}^{2}=T_{\rm 3D-2D}+T_{\rm 2D-2D},~~\text{with}~~

(27)

\displaystyle T_{\rm 3D-2D}=\mathcal{V}\int{\rm d}\bm{p}\sum_{s=\pm 1}\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{2}}\langle uv\rangle_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{ss}~\mathbb{1}_{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}<{U^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{{U^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{p_{z}\neq 0}{\rm d}\bm{p}\sum_{s=\pm 1}\frac{p_{x}p_{y}}{p^{2}}~\Phi_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{s}~\mathbb{1}_{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}<{U^{\prime}}}+O\Big(\frac{1}{Lk_{f}}\Big),

(28)

where
T_{\rm 3D-2D}
is the total energy transfer between the 3D waves and the 2D condensate, and

T_{\rm 2D-2D}
is the energy input from 2D modes
p_{z}=0
, which must be treated separately (Appendix
C
).

In steady state, Eq. (
25
) takes the form

\displaystyle\partial_{p_{y}}\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}(\bm{p})=-\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{2}}\langle uv\rangle_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{ss}+\epsilon\chi_{\bm{p}}^{s},

(29)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}(\bm{p})\equiv-({U^{\prime}}/\sqrt{2})p_{x}\Phi_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{s},

where
\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}(\bm{p})

is the spectral energy flux in the
p_{y}
direction, due to shearing of the waves by the mean flow, and
-\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{2}}\langle uv\rangle_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{ss}

is the energy transfer between the condensate and wave modes
\bm{p}
,
which enters the mean-flow energy balance Eq. (
28
).

Equation (
29
) reflects the exact relationship between the spectral wave energy flux and the energy transfer to the condensate:
outside of the forcing scale, the energization of the condensate is determined by the dependence of the energy flux
\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}
on
p_{y}
.
A wave
\bm{p}
can lose (gain) energy to (from) the condensate, in which case
{U^{\prime}}\langle uv\rangle_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{ss}>0
(
{U^{\prime}}\langle uv\rangle_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{ss}<0
), and
\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}(\bm{p})
decreases (increases) with
p_{y}
. A vanishing transfer term naturally implies a constant energy flux
\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}(\bm{p})
.

Note that Eq. (
25
)
does not apply to
modes excited at
k_{f}
and that do no resonate with the condensate (i.e such that
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}\delta_{p-k_{f}}>{U^{\prime}}
). These modes rather interact with other 3D modes via wave-wave interactions.
By writing our QL energy balance (
25
) as a closed system, we have ignored the scenario by which some modes
p>k_{f}
, energized from off-resonant modes at
k_{f}
by 3D-3D interactions,
become nearly-resonant with the condensate.
Instead, we consider that for each
(p_{x},p_{z})
, if the mode
p_{y}
at the forcing scale (
p_{y}^{2}=k_{f}^{2}-p_{x}^{2}-p_{z}^{2}
) is off-resonant,
larger wavenumbers
p_{y}^{2}>k_{f}^{2}-p_{x}^{2}-p_{z}^{2}
also remain so,
and
do not exchange energy with the condensate.
3
3

3

This assumption is difficult to assess.
It could be that waves with very large
p
always reenergize the 2D manifold (and then eventually the condensate) via near resonances, and that decoupling never fully occurs.
However, wave-wave interactions or viscous effects might dominate over such a 2D-3D interaction if
p
is very large, preventing this scenario.
In the DNS, we find complete decoupling for large enough rotations, suggesting that this scenario does not occur.

IV
Emergent conservation law and energy transfer to 2D

Before analyzing the kinetic equation (
25
) in detail,
we first discuss
why energy is transferred directionally
from
the 3D waves
to
the large-scale 2D flow.
We show that this is due to
(i)
the emergent conservation of the waves’ single-sign helicity
H^{s}\equiv\sum_{\bm{p}}spe_{\bm{p}}^{s}
, which implies that
(ii)
energy is transferred from the 3D waves to the large-scale 2D flow.
Note that the argument does not require the presence of a condensate and applies for any scale-separated 2D-wave interaction in steady state.

Wave interactions mediated by a
2D flow are restricted to be between same-helicity-sign waves at large rotation (see §
II
).
Given such a restriction,
energy (resp. helicity) injected into a sector
s
is only transferred to 2D or to the
s
-waves, and
the balance of wave energy (resp. helicity) in the inertial range
can be written separately for each sign
s
:

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{s}

\displaystyle{}+{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{p},\,p\neq k_{f}}T_{\rm 3D}^{s,\bm{p}}

(30)

\displaystyle sk_{f}\epsilon^{s}

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\mathcal{H}^{s}}_{\mathclap{3+3\leftrightarrow 2}}

\displaystyle{}+{}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{\bm{p},\,p\neq k_{f}}spT_{\rm 3D}^{s,\bm{p}}}_{\mathclap{3+2\leftrightarrow 3}}

(31)

with
\epsilon^{s}
the energy injection rate into modes of helicity sign
s
,

T_{3D}^{s,\bm{p}}
the energy transfer to a 3D mode
(\bm{p},s)
mediated by the 2D flow, and

\mathcal{E}^{s}
the energy transfer between the 2D flow and the
s-
waves.

\mathcal{H}^{s}
is the helicity transfer rate between the
s
-waves and the 2D flow.
(Note that
T_{\rm 3D}^{s,\bm{p}}
and
\mathcal{E}^{s}
generalize, respectively, the terms
\partial_{p_{y}}\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}
in Eq. (
29
) and
\int d\bm{p}{U^{\prime}}\langle uv\rangle_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{ss}/\sqrt{2}
in Eq. (
28
), derived in the presence of a condensate.)
The 2D flow is assumed to be of characteristic wavenumber
k
, and
\mathcal{H}^{s}
can be further decomposed into the contribution from each chirality of the 2D flow:

\mathcal{H}^{s}=k(\mathcal{E}^{s,+}-\mathcal{E}^{s,-})
.

The only way for waves of opposite helicities to exchange helicity is via
\mathcal{H}^{s}
.
However, when the 2D flow occupies a scale much larger than the energy injection scale,
k/k_{f}\ll 1
,
this 2D-3D helicity exchange is negligible:
\mathcal{H}^{s}\lesssim k\epsilon^{s}\ll k_{f}\epsilon^{s}
,
assuming that
the energy transfer to each 2D chirality is of the order of
\epsilon^{s}
(
|\mathcal{E}^{s,\pm}|/\epsilon^{s}\leq O(1)
).
Therefore, from (
31
),

\displaystyle k_{f}\epsilon^{s}\simeq\sum_{\bm{p},p\neq k_{f}}spT_{\rm 3D}^{s,\bm{p}},

(32)

and the waves of chirality
s
that interact with the large-scale 2D flow conserve their single-sign helicity
H^{s}\equiv\sum_{\bm{p}}spe_{\bm{p}}^{s}

at the leading order in

k/k_{f}
. This establishes
(i)
.

Due to this additional conservation of a sign-definite quantity
\mathcal{H}^{s}
,
following Fjørtoft
(
Fjørtoft 1953
)
, we expect the energy of the waves to be blocked from reaching arbitrarily small scales (large
p
).
Indeed, assuming that all modes at arbitrarily large
p
have the same sign of transfer
T_{\rm 3D}^{s,\bm{p}}
,
and that
|T_{\rm 3D}^{s,\bm{p}}|
remains bounded,
we have that
T_{\rm 3D}^{s,\bm{p}}\to 0
as
p\to\infty
, otherwise the sum in equation (
32
) does not converge and the single-sign helicity balance cannot be satisfied.

The wave energy can then either be transferred to large-scale 3D modes
p\ll k_{f}
, or to the large-scale 2D mode
k\ll k_{f}
.
However, the former scenario is not expected due to the advection of the waves by the large-scale 2D flow, which tends to shear them and generally
transfer the wave energy to small scales. This generates a positive forward flux, which however is vanishingly small at small scales due to the constraint of conservation of
H^{s}
.
Thus, energy is dominantly transferred from the waves to the large-scale 2D flow,
\mathcal{E}^{s}\sim\epsilon^{s}
, showing
(ii)
.
This explains how large-scale 2D flows can be irreversibly energized by 3D inertial waves at large rotation.

We now derive these results for our condensate-wave system, starting from Eq. (
25
).
When multiplying Eq. (
25
) by
sp
, we obtain a conservation law for the wave helicity
spe_{\bm{p}}^{s}
, for each pair
(p_{x},p_{z})
that obeys the near-resonant condition
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}<{U^{\prime}}

at the forcing scale
p=k_{f}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(sp~e_{\bm{p}}^{s})+\partial_{p_{y}}(sp~\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s})=sp~\epsilon\chi_{\bm{p}}^{s},

(33)

with
sp\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}
a helicity flux in the
p_{y}
direction due to the advection by the mean flow.

The advection of the waves by the mean shear flow
\bm{U}=U(y)\mathbf{e}_{x}
causes the motion of energy along characteristics in the variable
p_{y}
, and hence generates an energy flux
\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}
to small scales
|p_{y}|\to\infty
.
However, the downscale energy flux
\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}

decays with
p
as a consequence of the conservation of single-sign helicity.
Indeed, integrating Eq. (
33
) in steady state with consistent boundary conditions at
|p_{y}|\to\infty
(See Appendix
D
),
we obtain that all the injected helicity is transferred to small scales via a finite helicity flux, so that

\displaystyle|\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}(\bm{p})|

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\chi^{s}_{p_{x},p_{z}}\frac{k_{f}}{p},&\text{ for }p>k_{f}\text{ and }{U^{\prime}}p_{x}p_{y}<0\\
&0,&\text{ for }p>k_{f}\text{ and }{U^{\prime}}p_{x}p_{y}>0\end{aligned}\right.

(34)

with

\chi^{s}_{p_{x},p_{z}}\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\chi^{s}_{\bm{q}}~dq_{y}

the energy injection rate in each
(p_{x},p_{z})
line, assuming that the near-resonant condition

\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}<{U^{\prime}}

is satisfied at the forcing shell.
Because
\Pi_{\rm adv}^{s}(\bm{p})
decreases with
p_{y}
at small scales, the energy balance
Eq. (
29
) implies that
waves lose energy to the condensate, i.e. that the energy
transfer
{U^{\prime}}\langle uv\rangle_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}=-{U^{\prime}}\frac{p_{x}p_{y}}{2p^{2}}\Phi_{\bm{p}}^{s}>0
when
p>k_{f}
.
Now, we can integrate Eq. (
29
) to obtain the energy transfer to the condensate from each resonant
(p_{x},p_{z}\neq 0)
line:

\displaystyle\int\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{2}}\langle uv\rangle_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{ss}dp_{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp_{y}~\epsilon\chi_{\bm{p}}^{s}-\Pi_{{\rm adv}}^{s}(\bm{p})\Big|_{p_{y}=-\infty}^{p_{y}=\infty}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\chi^{s}_{p_{x},p_{z}},

(35)

using Eq. (
34
) when
|p_{y}|\to\infty
.
Equation (
35
) shows that all the energy from such a line is transferred to the condensate.

The conservation of the wave helicity
H^{s}

in the background of a large-scale mean flow can also be understood as the conservation of wave action
\sum_{\bm{p}}e_{\bm{p}}^{s}/\omega_{\bm{p}}^{s}

(
Andrews and McIntyre 1978
)
, applied to each wave species
s
separately.
Indeed, for each
p_{z}\neq 0
, wave action is proportional to helicity:

\sum_{p_{y}}e_{\bm{p}}^{s}/\omega_{\bm{p}}^{s}=(\sum_{p_{y}}spe_{\bm{p}}^{s})/p_{z}
. The directional transfer of energy to the mean flow can then be understood as being due to the motion of energy along characteristics in
p_{y}
-space, towards large
p
. This decreases the wave frequency
\omega_{\bm{p}}^{s}
, and hence requires a decrease in wave energy
e_{\bm{p}}^{s}
. A similar phenomenon occurs in plasma
or Rossby-wave systems
(
Diamond et al. 2005
)
and in streaming of acoustic waves
(
Lighthill and Lighthill 2001
)
.

V
Solution for the condensate as
\Omega\to\infty
and 2D-3D decoupling

We now explicitly compute how much energy is transferred from the 3D waves to the large-scale 2D flow as a function of control parameters
Ro_{\epsilon}
and
l_{f}/L_{i}
(
i=x,y,z
), and thus determine the condensate amplitude
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
, using our QL theory.
The strategy goes as follows: we first determine the total energy transfer from the 3D waves to the condensate for a given mean-shear rate
{U^{\prime}}
,
T_{\rm 3D-2D}[{U^{\prime}}/\Omega]
.
Next, we use the mean-flow energy balance (
17
) to close the system and determine the condensate amplitude
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
, and thereby the energy transfer to the 2D flow,
T_{\rm 2D}
, as a function of
Ro_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{2\Omega}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{\nu}}
and
l_{f}/L_{i}
(
i=x,y,z
).

V.1
Energy transfer as a function of
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega

For a given mean-shear rate
{U^{\prime}}
, the total transfer to the condensate is obtained by summing Eq. (
35
) over all near-resonant lines
(p_{x},p_{z}\neq 0)
, which gives

\displaystyle T_{\rm 3D-2D}\left[\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\right]=\epsilon\mathcal{V}\int_{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}<{U^{\prime}},p_{z}\neq 0}{\rm d}\bm{p}~\chi_{\bm{p}},

(36)

where integration is restricted to
near-resonant
waves excited in the forcing shell.
The explicit dependence of
T_{\rm 3D-2D}
on
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
is found by
computing the number of contributing near-resonant modes,
i.e such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{p_{y}p_{z}}{k_{f}^{2}}<\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\frac{k_{f}}{2k_{y}},\qquad p_{y}\geq k_{y}\\
\frac{p_{z}}{k_{f}}<\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\frac{k_{f}^{2}}{k_{y}^{2}},\qquad p_{y}=0,\end{cases}

(37)

at the forcing scale, corresponding to condition (
23
) using the approximation
p_{y}\gg k_{y}
up to
p_{y}=k_{y}
.

At low rotation, provided that all interactions are with homochiral waves (
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega<l_{f}/L_{z}
),
all the excited waves transfer their energy to the condensate:

\displaystyle T_{\rm 3D-2D}=\epsilon_{\rm 3D},\qquad\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}<\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}<\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}},

(38)

where
\epsilon_{\rm 3D}
is the energy injection rate into the 3D modes.
However, below
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega=l_{f}/L_{y}
, waves start to progressively decouple from the 2D flow as rotation is increased (i.e do not obey condition (
37
)),
with fewer and fewer modes contributing, being restricted to low
p_{z}
and
p_{y}
.
Consequently, the energy transfer
T_{\rm 3D-2D}[{U^{\prime}}/\Omega]
, computed explicitly in Eq. (
138
) (Appendix
D.2
), deviates from

\epsilon_{\rm 3D}
and decreases with rotation, scaling as

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}\sim\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}},\qquad\text{for }\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}<\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}},

(39)

with a regime-dependent prefactor including logarithmic corrections in
U^{\prime}/\Omega
.
The latter produce a steeper scaling in
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
and cause
\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}
to vanish at the finite value
\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}=\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}})^{2}
, as

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\left(\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}-\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\left(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right)^{2}\right),\quad\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\gtrsim\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\left(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right)^{2}.

(40)

Below this threshold, even waves with the lowest values of
p_{z}
and
p_{y}
,

p_{z}\sim 2\pi/L_{z}
and
p_{y}=0
,
are completely
decoupled from the 2D flow.

The remaining missing piece to determine the total energy transfer is
T_{\rm 2D-2D}
, the energy input from 2D modes
p_{z}=0
due to 2D-2D interactions.
The above framework does not apply to such modes, which are not affected by rotation.
In particular, 2D modes do not conserve single-sign helicity but instead horizontal enstrophy
(
Biferale et al. 2017
)
.
It can be shown that half the energy injected into the 2D modes is transferred to the condensate, because of the conservation of horizontal enstrophy,

\displaystyle T_{\rm 2D-2D}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\rm 2D}.

(41)

The other half is transferred to small 3D scales due to the vertical velocity component
behaving as a passive scalar, hence transferring its energy to small scales (Appendix
C
).

V.2
Closed solution with isotropic forcing

For isotropic forcing, like in our DNS, we obtain a piecewise function
\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}=f(Ro_{\epsilon})
by solving (
17
) (see Eq. (
)),
which, at low rotation, reads

\displaystyle\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 3D}}{\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 2D}}{2\epsilon}\right)Ro_{\epsilon}\Longleftrightarrow\nu{U^{\prime}}^{2}=\epsilon_{\rm 3D}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\rm 2D},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{ for }\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{y}}<Ro_{\epsilon}<\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{z}}.

(42)

This solution is similar to condensates in 2DNSE, where all the injected energy reaches the domain scale.
In contrast, at large rotation, the solution deviates from the 2D-like scaling (
42
) and an approximate asymptotic solution when
Ro_{\epsilon}\lesssim\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\left(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
can be obtained for our aspect ratio:

\displaystyle\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi}Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\ln\left(\frac{2L_{z}}{l_{f}}\right),\qquad Ro_{\epsilon}>Ro_{\epsilon}^{*}

(43a)

\displaystyle\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}

\displaystyle=2Ro_{\epsilon}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 2D}}{2\epsilon}},\qquad\qquad Ro_{\epsilon}<Ro_{\epsilon}^{*},

(43b)

where
Ro_{\epsilon}^{*}=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 2D}}{2\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\ln(2L_{z}/l_{f})}
, with
\epsilon_{\rm 2D}=\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{z}}\ll\epsilon_{\rm 3D}
for our isotropic forcing,
is a crossover
Ro_{\epsilon}
below which

T_{\rm 2D-2D}=\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 2D}}{2}
dominates over
T_{\rm 3D-2D}
.
From solution (
43
),
the amplitude of the 2D flow saturates to a finite value
{U^{\prime}}\sim(\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 2D}}{2\nu})^{1/2}
when
Ro_{\epsilon}\ll Ro_{\epsilon}^{*}
.
Meanwhile, the transfer
T_{\rm 3D-2D}
, written explicitly in Eq. (
167
), vanishes at a critical
Ro_{\epsilon}^{c}
as

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\left(\frac{l_{f}}{4L_{z}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Ro_{\epsilon}-Ro_{\epsilon}^{c}\right),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{ with }Ro_{\epsilon}^{c}=\left(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(44)

Below
Ro_{\epsilon}^{c}
, 3D modes completely decouple from the 2D flow. We therefore predict a second-order transition for the energy transfer when the 2D manifold is forced.

We compare the condensate amplitude, Eqs. (
42
)-(
43
), to our DNS results in Fig.
3
(a),
and the corresponding energy transfer
T_{\rm 3D-2D}(Ro_{\epsilon})
, Eq. (
167
), in Fig.

(black solid lines).
The energy balance equation (
17
), closed with
T_{\rm 3D-2D}({U^{\prime}}/\Omega)
from (
138
), can also be solved directly via a numerical root-finding procedure, leading to the red lines in Figs.
3
(a) (inset) and
4
, which our asymptotic solutions approximate.

The agreement between our DNS data points for
Ro_{\epsilon}<l_{f}/(2L_{y})\simeq 0.05
and the QL solutions
is excellent and does not require any fitting parameter:
below
Ro_{\epsilon}=l_{f}/(2L_{y})\simeq 0.05
,
the condensate progressively disappears with increasing rotation, following solution (
167
) (black line in Fig.
3
), while
T_{\rm 3D-2D}
decays to 0, in a manner close to, but steeper than
Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}
(Fig.
4
).
As
Ro_{\epsilon}
decreases,
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
eventually approaches a linear scaling
\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\propto Ro_{\epsilon}
for
T_{\rm 3D-2D}\to 0
. When
T_{\rm 3D-2D}=0
(white points in Fig.

), the condensate amplitude is set only by the
2D-2D interactions.

Note that since
\epsilon_{\rm 2D}\ll\epsilon
,
the value of
T_{\rm 2D-2D}
in the DNS can be reduced and even vanish due to viscous effects at the forcing scale. This is why the condensate disappears in the lowest-
Re
cases in the DNS, replaced by a state of inertial waves.
Observing the linear scaling (
43b
) when
Ro_{\epsilon}\to 0
therefore requires very large
Re
and small

, and it is difficult to completely confirm it from the DNS.
However, the point
Ro_{\epsilon}\simeq 0.01
, where
T_{\rm 3D-2D}=0
while
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega\neq 0
, is consistent with such a regime.
Note also that our prediction overshoots the DNS data points in the regime
Ro_{\epsilon}\sim l_{f}/(2L_{y})
.
This could be due to our sharp treatment of near-resonances, which overestimates the contribution of near-resonances
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t\sim 1
(see Appendix
A
), or of other viscous or non-linear effects neglected in the theory.

V.3
Other forcing scenarios

When forcing only the 3D waves (
\epsilon_{\rm 2D}=0
),
the notable difference with the isotropic case in §
V.2
is that there are no remaining 2D-2D interactions in the limit
Ro_{\epsilon}\to 0
.
Then, for
Ro_{\epsilon}\ll\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{y}}
the solution essentially takes the same form as in (
43a
)
(see (
162
) for the full result),
but vanishes below a critical
Ro_{\epsilon}^{c}
. The condensate amplitude and the 3D-2D energy transfer then exhibit a jump near the transition:

\displaystyle\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2}{\pi}Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\left(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{Ro_{\epsilon}^{c2}}{Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}}}\right)

(45)

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\right)^{2}Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}\left(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{Ro_{\epsilon}^{c2}}{Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}}}\right)^{2},

(46)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{ with }Ro_{\epsilon}^{c}=\sqrt{\pi}\left(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right)^{3/2}\left(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\right)^{1/2}.

Therefore, in the absence of forcing in the 2D manifold, we predict a complete disappearance of the 2D flow below a finite
Ro_{\epsilon}^{c}
,
and an associated first-order transition occurring at
Ro_{\epsilon}^{c}
(Appendix
D.3.1
).
This discontinuous transition is expected to be generic, independent of the particular 3D forcing and large-scale dissipation mechanism.
Since the energy transfer in (
40
) vanishes at a finite
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
, the solution of Eq. (
17
) for
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
with
\epsilon_{\rm 2D}=0
switches at this point from a finite value to a zero condensate amplitude.

In contrast, when forcing only the 2D manifold (
\epsilon_{\rm 3D}=0,\epsilon=\epsilon_{\rm 2D}
),
T_{\rm 2D}=\epsilon/2
: Eq. (
17
) therefore leads to

\displaystyle\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}=Ro_{\epsilon}\Longleftrightarrow\nu{U^{\prime}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon,\text{ for }Ro_{\epsilon}<\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{z}},

(47)

a rotation-independent scaling similar to that of condensates in 2DNSE.
This regime was observed in numerical simulations of the 2D-forced rotating 3DNSE when
Ro\to 0

(
Seshasayanan and Alexakis 2018
)
.
We expect this regime to be realized below
Ro\sim(l_{f}/2L_{z})Re^{-1/2}
.

V.4
Remarks on lower rotations

At large-enough rotation (
Ro_{\epsilon}\lesssim l_{f}/(2L_{z}))
,
we have shown that
only condensate-wave interactions
involving homochiral waves
survive, and that these waves transfer all their energy to the condensate: rotation has turned sign-definite an otherwise sign-indefinite invariant, thereby fundamentally altering the energy distribution through scales
(
Biferale et al. 2012
;
Shavit et al. 2024
)
.
However, at lower rotation, (
Ro_{\epsilon}\gtrsim l_{f}/(2L_{z})
) interactions are not limited to be homochiral, and waves of opposite helicity interact through the condensate (Fig.
3
a).
In this case, waves can also extract energy from the 2D flow.
We dedicate a forthcoming publication to this higher-
Ro
regime
(
Gomé and Frishman 2025
)
.

As the sustainment of 2D condensates in rotating 3D flows requires some homochiral wave-condensate interactions, it relies on the existence of inertial waves (or, more loosely, rotation-dominated modes) at the forcing scale.
This is why the condensate is lost when
Ro\gtrsim 0.5
, above which the forcing scale is not constrained by rotation (see Fig.
1
).

V.5
Order of limits

We have established that 3D-2D decoupling occurs progressively, starting from a fully-coupled regime at intermediate rotation (
\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{y}}<Ro_{\epsilon}
), to complete decoupling below a critical
Ro_{\epsilon}^{c}\propto\left(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Crucially, the limits between
L_{y},L_{z}\to\infty
and
Ro_{\epsilon}\to 0
do not commute.

Taking the infinite box limit in the horizontal direction followed by
Ro_{\epsilon}\to 0
(the weak wave turbulence limit in this direction), 3D and 2D modes remain fully coupled. For our
y
dependent condensate, this corresponds to
L_{y}/l_{f}\to\infty
first, followed by
Ro_{\epsilon}\to 0
, resulting in a vanishing 2D wavenumber
\bm{k}\to 0
which makes the corresponding 2D-3D triads always near-resonant. As a result,
the scaling
\nu{U^{\prime}}^{2}\simeq\epsilon
(
42
), similar to 2DNSE, is realized in this limit.
This is consistent with the previous derivation in
(
Kolokolov et al. 2020
)
, corresponding to this order of limits.

In the vertical direction, when taking
L_{z}/l_{f}\to\infty
before
Ro_{\epsilon}\to 0
,
3D and 2D modes never completely decouple, as
3D modes
p_{z}\sim 2\pi/L_{z}
are always nearly-resonant with the 2D flow.
This behavior is consistent with the numerical stability analysis in
(
Billant 2021
)
and with the convergence rate between rotating 3DNSE and 2DNSE derived in
(
Babin et al. 1997
)
.
In the other direction, when
L_{z}/l_{f}
is decreased (for
Ro_{\epsilon}<l_{f}/2L_{y}
),
there are fewer resonant modes
p_{z}\sim 2\pi/L_{z}
, so decoupling should occur for larger
Ro_{\epsilon}\sim(l_{f}/L_{z})^{1/2}
.

Finally, our treatment of near-resonances and the broadening around them, via the Heaviside approximation of the oscillating factor in (
23
), is rough by nature.
It implies, in particular, that near-resonances completely decorrelate below a finite value of the frequency broadening
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
.
Thus, our solution predicts a complete 3D-2D decoupling below a
finite

Ro_{\epsilon}=Ro_{\epsilon}^{c}
, which seems to be confirmed by the DNS.
A smoother treatment of the oscillating factor, as with a Lorentzian function of
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
, would not produce a complete decoupling at any finite
Ro_{\epsilon}
(see Appendix
E
).

VI
Conclusion and discussions

This work identifies a nonlinear mechanism by which 3D inertial waves transfer energy to large-scale two-dimensional structures in rotating turbulence.
Near-resonant, scale-separated, 2D–3D interactions conserve the helicity of sufficiently-fast waves, separately by sign.
This
imposes an upscale flux of energy into large 2D scales. This is in sharp contrast with 3D–3D interactions, whether dominated by waves
(
Galtier 2003
)
or by eddies
(
Chen et al. 2003
;
Alexakis 2017
)
, which mix helicity signs, so that both energy and sign-indefinite helicity cascade to small scales.
We have shown that this mechanism is responsible for sustaining
large-scale 2D condensates in numerical simulations of rotating 3D turbulence.

As rotation is increased, near-resonances become increasingly stringent and progressively suppress the 2D-3D coupling, until 3D waves and 2D motions fully decouple in the limit
Ro\ll l_{f}/L\ll 1
, i.e taking the limit of fast rotation before the infinite-box limit.
When 3D modes are excited, this leads to a regime of pure inertial-wave turbulence for the 3D modes.
3D-2D decoupling is a fundamental property of the rotating 3D Navier-Stokes equations
(
Babin et al. 1999
;
Chen et al. 2005
)

for which we provide compelling numerical evidence.
We have derived analytical expressions for the condensate amplitude and 3D-2D energy transfer, confirmed in simulations, which clarify the dependence of this phase transition on rotation rate and domain geometry.

How our results extend to rotating turbulence without a condensate – for instance, in the presence of strong large-scale dissipation – remains to be clarified.
In that case, statistically homogeneous and isotropic 2D flows generically emerge, even in systems forced in 3D
(
Smith and Waleffe 1999
;
Yarom et al. 2013
;
Deusebio et al. 2014
;
Godeferd and Moisy 2015
;
Brunet et al. 2020
)
.
Once the energy reaches 2D modes,
it is transferred to large 2D scales via a classical inverse cascade within the 2D manifold,
see e.g.
(
Mininni and Pouquet 2010
;
Yarom et al. 2013
;
Campagne et al. 2014
)
:
2D–2D interactions are indeed expected to dominate over 3D-2D interactions, as the latter operate on slower near-resonant time scales or even decouple
(
Alexakis and Biferale 2018
)
.

Thus, a key remaining question is how and why energy is transferred from 3D to 2D, powering this inverse cascade.
Our work suggests a possible scenario,
through near-resonant triads irreversibly transferring energy to 2D modes when the scales of 2D and 3D modes are well separated.
The extent to which this scenario is realized in statistically homogeneous flows requires further investigation,
but there is some numerical evidence of non-local 3D-to-2D transfers that
seem to support this picture
(
Bourouiba et al. 2012
)
.
It also remains to be seen whether a complete 3D-2D decoupling, similar to the one we report, can be observed in laboratory flows.

Our helicity-based mechanism may explain the generation of 2D flows in other types of rotating systems, like rotating convection
(
Guervilly et al. 2014
;
Favier et al. 2014
;
Rubio et al. 2014
;
Guervilly and Hughes 2017
;
van Kan et al. 2025
)
or rotating–stratified flows
(
Bartello 1995
;
Smith and Waleffe 2002
;
Alexakis et al. 2024
)
.
The helicity-by-sign constraint uncovered here is part of a wider class of emergent adiabatic invariants in nonlinear systems, where resonant interactions give rise to effective conservation laws, that then govern large-scale self-organization
(
Nazarenko 2011
)
. It is also an example from a broader category of wave systems where zero-frequency modes are spontaneously generated and dominate, even though their resonant coupling with the waves vanishes.
Examples are found in planetary jets
(
Vallis and Maltrud 1993
)
, plasma turbulence
(
Diamond et al. 2005
)
and stratified flows
(
Caulfield 2021
;
Smith and Waleffe 2002
;
Labarre and Shavit 2024
)
.
Our near-resonant mean-wave framework opens new avenues for the understanding of this phenomenon.

Appendix A
Derivation of the kinetic equation

In this section, we derive the dynamical
equation for the cumulant
e_{\bm{p}}^{s}(t)=\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{\bm{p}}^{s*}\rangle/2

in an expansion in
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
. Here,
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes an ensemble average over forcing realizations.
Due to the presence of the mean flow, inhomogeneous correlators will be non-zero, and we will consider

\displaystyle\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{\bm{q}}^{s}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\delta_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}e_{\bm{p}}^{s}+\Phi^{s}_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}\delta_{\bm{k}+\bm{p}+\bm{q}}

(48)

where
\delta_{f(k)}
is the discrete delta function:

\delta_{f(k)}=\begin{cases}1\qquad f(k)=0\\
0\qquad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(49)

and
\Phi^{s}_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}
is the (inhomogeneous) correlator between modes with wavenumbers
\bm{p}
and
\bm{q}=-\bm{p}-\bm{k}
.

We consider an isotropic white in time forcing, limited to a shell around a wave number
k_{f}
:

\displaystyle\langle f_{\bm{p}}^{s}(t)f_{\bm{q}}^{s^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\rangle=2\epsilon\delta(t-t^{\prime})\delta_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\chi_{\bm{p}}^{s}.

(50)

where
\sum_{p}\chi^{s}_{\bm{p}}=1/2
and
\chi_{\bm{p}}\propto\mathbb{1}_{k_{f}^{2}-\Delta\leq p^{2}\leq k_{f}^{2}+\Delta}
,
\Delta k=\left(\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{2\pi}{L_{y}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{2\pi}{L_{z}}\right)^{2}

, i.e. a shell around
k_{f}^{2}
is assumed to be forced such that the total injected energy is equal to
\epsilon
. Note that we assume zero total helicity injection.
In the continuum limit,
\sum_{p}\chi^{s}_{\bm{p}}\to\mathcal{V}\int dp\chi^{s}_{p}
where
\mathcal{V}=(L_{x}L_{y}L_{z})/(2\pi)^{3}
so that the noise correlator becomes
\chi_{\bm{p}}^{s}=\chi_{\bm{p}}/2=\delta(p-k_{f})/(8\pi k_{f}^{2}\mathcal{V})
, giving that

\mathcal{V}\int d\bm{p}\chi_{\bm{p}}=1
, and the forcing injects total energy at a rate
\epsilon
.

A.1
Energy equation

We begin with the equations for the mean flow and fluctuations (waves) assuming the quasi-linear approximation, a time-independent two-dimensional mean flow
U_{\bm{k}}
, and considering scales where the dissipation is negligible for the waves. We shall work with a finite space (and discrete wavenumbers for the waves) here, and will comment about the continuum limit at the end. Recall that we are working in the interaction picture (working with the amplitudes of inertial waves,
a_{p}^{s}
) so that the equations are non-autonomous.
We take the arbitrary initial time to be
t=0
. The equations then read:

\displaystyle\nu k_{y}^{2}U_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=ik_{y}\sum_{\bm{p},\bm{q},s,\tilde{s}}\Big[C_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s*}a_{\bm{q}}^{\tilde{s}*}~e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}\rangle\Big]\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}

(51)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}a_{\bm{p}}^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{q},\bm{k},\tilde{s}}\Big[V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}U_{\bm{k}}^{*}~a_{\bm{q}}^{\tilde{s}*}e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}\Big]\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}+\bm{f}_{\bm{p}}^{s}e^{-i\omega^{s}_{\bm{p}}t}

(52)

with

\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}=\omega^{s}_{\bm{p}}+\omega^{\tilde{s}}_{\bm{q}}=2\Omega p_{z}\left(\frac{s}{p}-\frac{\tilde{s}}{q}\right)

(53)

where we have used that
U_{k}
is two dimensional so that
k_{z}=0
, implying that
p_{z}=-q_{z}
.

Next we write the energy balance (equivalent of the kinetic equation) for the waves
e_{\bm{p}}^{s}=\frac{1}{2}\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{\bm{p}}^{s*}\rangle
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}e^{s}_{\bm{p}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle a^{s*}_{\bm{p}}\partial_{t}a^{s}_{\bm{p}}\rangle+\langle\partial_{t}a^{s*}_{\bm{p}}a^{s}_{\bm{p}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{q},\bm{k},\tilde{s}}\Big[V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}U_{\bm{k}}^{*}~\langle a^{s*}_{\bm{p}}a_{\bm{q}}^{\tilde{s}*}\rangle e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}\Big]\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\langle a^{s*}_{\bm{p}}\bm{f}_{\bm{p}}^{s}\rangle e^{-i\omega^{s}_{\bm{p}}t}+c.c

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{q},\bm{k},\tilde{s}}\Big[V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}U_{\bm{k}}^{*}~\langle a^{s*}_{\bm{p}}a_{\bm{q}}^{\tilde{s}*}\rangle(t)e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}\Big]\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}+c.c+\epsilon\chi^{s}_{\bm{p}}

(54)

where
c.c
denotes complex conjugate and we have used that
\langle a^{s*}_{\bm{p}}\bm{f}_{\bm{p}}^{s}\rangle=\epsilon\chi^{s}_{\bm{p}}e^{i\omega^{s}_{\bm{p}}t}
, since the forcing is white in time (we assume the Stratonovich convention).

We assume that wave-mean flow interactions occur on a typical time scale of the order of
1/{U^{\prime}}
, so that over a much shorter time scale, of order
t\ll 1/U^{\prime}
, the wave amplitude correlators can be assumed constant.
Thus, performing a partial time average up to time
t\ll 1/{U^{\prime}}
(the same procedure should be applied to the equation for the mean flow, Eq. (
51
), consistently giving contributions from the same triads),

\displaystyle\frac{e^{s}_{\bm{p}}(t)-e^{s}_{\bm{p}}(0)}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{q},\bm{k},\tilde{s}}\Big[V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}U_{\bm{k}}^{*}~\langle a^{s*}_{\bm{p}}a_{\bm{q}}^{\tilde{s}*}\rangle\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}\Big]\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}+c.c+\epsilon\chi^{s}_{\bm{p}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{q},\bm{k},\tilde{s}}\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}}{i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}\Big[V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}U_{\bm{k}}^{*}~\langle a^{s*}_{\bm{p}}a_{\bm{q}}^{\tilde{s}*}\rangle\Big]\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}+c.c+\epsilon\chi^{s}_{\bm{p}}

(55)

The influence of rotation on the dynamics comes in through the oscillating factor,
\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}}{i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}}
, whose real part is shown in Fig.
5
as a function of
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t
.
In particular

\displaystyle\Delta(t)\equiv\Re\left(\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}}{i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}\right)\approx\begin{cases}1\qquad\qquad\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t\ll 1\\
0\qquad\qquad\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t\gg 1\end{cases}.

(56)

Indeed, if
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}
is too large compared to the inverse of the evolution time scale
1/t
, the wave-mean-flow interaction term does not contribute secularly, as the oscillating factor evolves as
\simeq\frac{1}{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}t}
. Triads such that
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t\gg 1
are therefore off-resonant and do not couple with the 2D flow.
In contrast, for resonant or near-resonant triads, such that
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t
is sufficiently close to zero, the oscillating factor approaches its Taylor expansion and the interaction contributes secularly to the waves energy dynamics over the slow time scale
t
.
Note that
\Im\left(\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}t}}}{i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}\right)=\frac{\cos(\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t)-1}{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}
decays like
1/\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t
for
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t\gg 1
and is at most
O(\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t)
for
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t\ll 1
, hence we neglect its contribution.
There is also a lower bound for the time
t
we need to consider here: the oscillations of the waves will be felt by the system (meaning that some interactions will average out to zero) only for times much larger than
1/\max(\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}})=1/4\Omega
, which occurs in interactions between opposite-helicity waves,
s=-\tilde{s}
. Hence, such an expansion makes sense for
1/4\Omega\ll t\ll 1/U^{\prime}
, requiring a sufficient time-scale separation. Thus, the kinetic equation that we derive is an expansion in
U^{\prime}/4\Omega
(the ratio between the wave and non-linear time scale), where we keep the leading order term
O(U^{\prime}/4\Omega)
.

We now approximate the oscillating factor as

\displaystyle\Delta(t)\approx\begin{cases}1\qquad\qquad\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t<1\\
0\qquad\qquad\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t>1\end{cases},

(57)

as visualized in Fig.
5
(red line).
By doing so, we extend the secular contribution of near-resonances up to
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t=1
,
and deem off-resonant all values of
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t
larger than 1.
Next, given that
0<t<1/{U^{\prime}}
, we further simplify the oscillating factor by estimating it at the upper bound of
t
:

\displaystyle\Delta(t)\approx\Theta\Big(1-\frac{|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}|}{{U^{\prime}}}\Big)

(58)

where
\Theta
denotes the Heaviside function.
Making this approximation amounts to the following:

-

When
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}|/{U^{\prime}}\ll 1
, all times
t<\frac{1}{{U^{\prime}}}\ll\frac{1}{|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}|}
produce a secular growth, which is consistent with the approximate behavior of
\Delta(t)
for such times.

-

For modes for which
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}|/{U^{\prime}}\lesssim 1
, and if the time-scale separation between
1/\Omega
and
1/U^{\prime}
is not very large, the time for partial-time averaging cannot be chosen such that
t\ll 1/U^{\prime}
, and modes that produce a secular growth then do not contribute fully (as
\Delta(t)<1
for
t\lesssim 1/\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}
), so that approximating their contribution at one will be an overestimate;

-

For modes for which
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}|/{U^{\prime}}\gg 1
the interaction term is set to zero. This is consistent since there is sufficient time scale separation to take the partial-time average over times
\frac{1}{|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}|}\ll t\ll\frac{1}{{U^{\prime}}}
, for which such terms indeed average out to zero.

-

For modes such that
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}|/{U^{\prime}}\gtrsim 1
, we have that
t<1/U^{\prime}\lesssim|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}|
, so such modes should have non-zero interactions in principle, though they are here set to zero.

Another option, more refined than the Heaviside approximation in (

is to use a Lorentzian function to model the oscillating factor,

\displaystyle\Re\left(\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}}{i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}\right)\simeq\frac{1}{\left(1+Q\left(\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}}

(59)

with

a tunable quality factor. See the blue line in Fig.

with

.
Like the original oscillating factor, this function decays as
\frac{1}{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t}
when
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t\gg 1
, and is
1
when
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t=0
but is less than one for
|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t|>0
. Outside this range, it can be thought to model the evolution of the averaged oscillating factor,
\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\Delta(t)
with
t
.

Next, we evaluate (
59
) at

:

\displaystyle\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}}{i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}\simeq\frac{1}{\left(1+Q\left(\frac{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}}{{U^{\prime}}}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}}

(60)

By doing so, for modes for which
\frac{1}{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}}<t<\frac{1}{{U^{\prime}}}
we set the oscillating factor to a value between 0 and 1, instead of setting it at
0
as in (
58
). Therefore, unlike the Heaviside filter, the Lorentzian filter models an infinitesimal contribution of near-resonances as
\Omega\to\infty
, which never fully decouple. On the other hand, for modes for which
t<1/\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}
the oscillating factor is taken to be less than 1 unless there is significant time scale separation
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}/U^{\prime}\ll 1
.

Alltogether, due to the contribution of near-resonant triads modeled by our choice of filter, the energy equation reduces at
O({U^{\prime}}/\Omega)
to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}e_{\bm{p}}^{s}=\epsilon\chi_{\bm{p}}^{s}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{q},\bm{k},\tilde{s}}U_{\bm{k}}^{*}V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s*}a_{\bm{q}}^{\tilde{s}*}\rangle F_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}+c.c

(61)

where
F_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}
is either the Heaviside filter (
58
) or the Lorentzian filter (
60
).
In most of this paper, we use the Heaviside filter (
58
), and discuss the use of the Lorentzian filter (
60
) in §
E
. Note that this equation is the quasi-linear version of the Navier-Stokes equation with only part of the interactions included, based on the quasi resonant condition
\frac{|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}|}{{U^{\prime}}}<1
.

Both filters (
58
)-(
60
)
select exact resonances
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}=0
when time-scale separation between the non-linear time
1/U^{\prime}
and the wave period is infinite
\frac{1}{\Omega}\ll\frac{1}{{U^{\prime}}}
so that the resonant condition
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}/U^{\prime}<1
becomes extremely stringent.
They therefore reproduce the behavior of the oscillating factor in this limit:

\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}}{i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t}=\delta_{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}}
when
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}t\to\infty
.

Unlike quasi-resonances, exact resonances
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{s\tilde{s}}=0
exhibit the
anomalous property of zero 2D-3D coupling,
ik_{y}C_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}=0
for our
x
-invariant 2D flow
U_{\bm{k}}
. For such a 2D flow, there are two exactly-resonant poles:

k_{y}=0,q_{y}=-p_{y}
(however,
U_{0}=0
by Galilean invariance)
and
k_{y}=-2p_{y},q_{y}=p_{y}
.
Any triad involving 3D modes in the vicinity of these resonant poles (i.e such that either
q_{y}\simeq-p_{y},k_{y}\ll p_{y}
or that
q_{y}\simeq p_{y},k_{y}\simeq-2p_{y}
) is nearly-resonant.
When the 2D manifold has condensed due to the inverse energy cascade, low 2D modes
k_{y}\ll p
are favored, and the interactions are naturally nearly-resonant, as triads lie in the vicinity of the first resonant pole (
q_{y}\simeq-p_{y},k_{y}\ll p_{y}
).
Furthermore, we can neglect the quasi-resonances around the second pole (
q_{y}\simeq p_{y},k_{y}\simeq-2p_{y}
), as they involve a small-scale 2D mode which is assumed to be of negligible amplitude compared to the largest scale mode.
By neglecting these quasi-resonant triads, we also neglect any catalytic effect, by which a 2D mode energized by quasi-resonant waves acts as a catalyzer for energy transfer between other 3D waves via exact resonances.

A.2
Continuum limit

In some of the following we will take the infinite box limit
Lk_{f}\gg 1
, corresponding to the continuum limit for the wavenumbers. In this limit, the sums in
\bm{k}
in Eq. (
54
) turn into integrals, in which case the main contribution comes from infinitely many discrete quasi-resonant triads
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}\gg\Delta\omega_{\bm{p}}
(the frequency step due to the discreteness of the spectral grid). In this limit
we can use the asymptotic result (Riemann-Lebesque lemma):

\displaystyle\Re\left(\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t}}{i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}}\right)\underset{\Omega t\to\infty}{\sim}2\pi\delta(\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}),

(62)

that is, the contribution from near resonances around each exact resonance is weighted correctly in order to conserve the integral over them (since
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{1-e^{-ixt}}{ix}=2\pi
). However, this contribution is not secular. Alternatively,

\displaystyle\Re\left(\frac{1-e^{-i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t}}{i\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t}\right)\underset{\Omega t\to\infty}{\sim}\delta_{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}},

(63)

where
\delta_{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}}
is the discrete delta function, which gives zero contribution upon integration in
\bm{k}
.
Thus, terms at order
O({U^{\prime}}/\Omega)
, considered here, are not secular, and in the traditional wave-turbulence approach, one needs to go to the next order in the non-linearity parameter.

For the condensate, however, the energy is concentrated at a few lowest modes.
Hence, in the continuous limit
Lk_{f}\to\infty
, we have
U_{\bm{k}}=\delta(\bm{k}-\bm{k}_{0})
with a given wavenumber
\bm{k}_{0}
(typically the lowest one), which contains all the mean-flow energy. Then, one does obtain a secular contribution of the individual condensate mode
\bm{k}_{0}

at
O({U^{\prime}}/\Omega)
even in the continuum limit, in which mode
\bm{k}_{0}
interacts with a continuum of 3D waves
\bm{p}
via near-resonances.

In the absence of a condensate, given a 2D flow with a continuous spectrum, one does need to go to the next order to obtain a secular growth.
Then, the classical kinetic equation

(
Zakharov et al. 2012
;
Newell and Rumpf 2011
;
Galtier 2003
;
Galtier 2023
)
is eventually of the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}e_{\bm{p}}={U^{\prime}}^{2}\int\mathcal{T}_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}\delta(\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}),

(64)

where
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{q}}=|U_{\bm{k}}|^{2}/(4U^{\prime 2})\Big(\Re(V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}V_{\bm{q}\bm{k}\bm{p}}^{*})e_{\bm{p}}+V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}V_{\bm{p}\bm{k}\bm{q}}^{*}e_{\bm{q}}\Big)

in the homogeneous case
\Phi_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{ss}=0

(see
(
Galtier 2003
)
).

A.3
Near resonances and 2D-3D decoupling in wave turbulence

In the previous section, we explained why the order of our expansion is one order less in the non-linearity compared to usual wave turbulence theory. However, since 2D modes decouple from 3D modes for exactly resonant interactions, the classical weak-wave turbulence framework cannot be straightforwardly applied to describe 2D-3D interactions, with or without a condensate. Instead, one must consider near-resonant interactions more explicitly, taking into account the finite width around the exact resonance.
In such near-resonant interactions, the coupling coefficient between the 2D flow and the waves is non-zero, being of the order of the deviation of the frequency sum from exact resonance
Shavit et al. 2024
.

In a system of finite size
L
,
the minimal frequency broadening that allows for 2D-3D coupling depends on the discreteness of the system, as
\omega^{ss}_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}\propto 1/L
.
Therefore, when
\Omega t\to\infty
is taken first, only exact resonances contribute, and
decoupling necessarily occurs.
This is the case in our example with a 2D condensate, where dealing with near-resonances and decoupling is particularly simple, as the 2D flow is dominated by a few discrete low modes, associated to a well-defined interaction time scale
1/{U^{\prime}}
.
However, decoupling is also expected for any 2D spectrum
U_{\bm{k}}
(for example, without a condensate).
Taking the limit
\Omega\to\infty
, before the infinite box limit produces a transition from near-resonances contributing to only exact-resonances
contributing. This corresponds to a transition from continuous to discrete wave turbulence, in the parlance of wave turbulence theory
(
Nazarenko 2011
;
L’vov and Nazarenko 2010
)
.

When the infinite box limit
L\to\infty
is taken first, however, the occurrence of decoupling is more intricate, as there is a large number of near-resonances
\omega^{ss}_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}t\ll 1
, which in principle enable 2D-3D coupling.
However, in its current formulation, (continuous) weak wave turbulence predicts a kinetic equation
(
Galtier 2003
)
restricted to the resonant manifold
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}=0
, on which the energy transfer to 2D exactly vanishes.
Our procedure gives a hint as to how
this paradox can be resolved: one needs to broaden the delta function enforcing the resonant condition in the kinetic equation to include all near resonances which are allowed by the time scale separation between the non-linearity and the wave frequency (i.e. the frequency broadening), similarly to what is done in
L’vov et al. 1997
. This width will in turn determine the magnitude of the interaction coefficient between the 2D and 3D modes. Note that this discussion focuses on capturing interactions between
exactly
zero frequency modes and waves, through
near
resonances, rather than interactions between
approximate
zero modes (slow modes
\omega_{\bm{p}}\to 0
) and waves through
exact
resonances, as done in
Shavit et al. 2025
.

An important question is if such a regularized kinetic theory could provide relevant predictions for what 2D scale is energized and how much energy is transferred to the 2D manifold in the absence of a condensate.
The fraction of energy reaching the 2D manifold is key in dictating how much energy feeds a wave-turbulent cascade due to 3D-3D interactions,
for which kinetic theories provide relevant estimates of energy spectra and fluxes
(
Shaltiel et al. 2024
)
.

Thus, using equation (
61

\displaystyle\Big[p_{x}\partial_{p_{y}}+\frac{p_{x}p_{y}}{p^{2}}\Big]\Phi_{\bm{p}\bm{k}}^{s}

Reynolds stress

The wave energy equation (

17

The total energy transfer due to homochiral waves is

\displaystyle T_{\rm 2D}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{p},s}U^{\prime}_{-\bm{k}}\langle u_{\bm{p}}^{s}v_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle+U^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\langle u_{\bm{p}}^{s}v_{-\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\left.+\langle u_{\bm{p}}^{s}v_{-\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle+\langle u_{-\bm{p}}^{s}v_{\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle u_{\bm{p}}^{s}v^{s}_{-\bm{p}-\bm{k}}\rangle+\langle u_{\bm{p}}^{s}v_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle\Big)+c.c

\displaystyle=\Re\left[\langle u_{\bm{p}}^{s}v^{s}_{-\bm{p}-\bm{k}}\rangle\right]+\Re\left[\langle u_{\bm{p}}^{s}v_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\Re\left[h_{\bm{p},x}^{-s}h_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k},y}^{-s}\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle+h_{\bm{p},x}^{-s}h_{-\bm{p}-\bm{k},y}^{-s}\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle\right]

where in the last line we used that

together with equation

and that
the imaginary part of the correlator is subleading:

\displaystyle 2\Im\left[\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle+\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle+\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle-c.c

\displaystyle=\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle-\langle a_{\bm{p}+\bm{k}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}}^{s}\rangle+\langle a_{\bm{p}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}\rangle-\langle a_{\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}}^{s}\rangle

\displaystyle=-k_{y}\partial_{p_{y}}\langle a_{\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}}^{s}\rangle+k_{y}\partial_{p_{y}}\langle a_{\bm{p}-\bm{k}}^{s}a_{-\bm{p}}^{s}\rangle

using a Taylor expansion in each correlator.

The real space Reynolds stress reads

L_{i}=\infty
(
i=x,y,z
).
We therefore truncate the modes at the first nonzero Fourier modes
p_{i}=2\pi/L_{i}
to account for this discreneness, and
handle modes
p_{i}>2\pi/L_{i}
continously.
This is a
semi-continuous
approximation, corresponding to a regularization of the
L_{i}k_{f}\to\infty
limit.
We will consider the different limits
L_{i}k_{f}\to\infty
in a later stage.

This stepwise treatment of near-resonances via Heaviside filter
\mathbb{1}_{|\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}|<{U^{\prime}}}
corresponds to a condition on the values of
p_{y}
and
p_{z}
within the forcing shell,

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}}{{U^{\prime}}}<1\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{p_{y}p_{z}}{k_{f}^{2}}\leq\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\frac{k_{f}}{2k_{y}}\text{ for }|p_{y}|>k_{y}

(106)

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}}{{U^{\prime}}}<1\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{p_{z}}{k_{f}}<\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\frac{k_{f}^{2}}{k_{y}^{2}}\text{ for }p_{y}=0,

(107)

where we have used the approximated value of
\omega_{\bm{p}\bm{q}}^{ss}
when
p_{y}\gg k_{y}
in (
106
), and
when
p_{y}\ll k_{y}
in (
107
).
In the following we consider the case
l_{f}/L_{y},l_{f}/L_{z}\ll 1
, and do not treat the case of a thin layer compared to the forcing scale
l_{f}/L_{z}\geq 1
.

Because the resonant condition in (
107
) differs in plane
p_{y}=0
and in modes
p_{y}\neq 0
, we need to consider the integration of the forcing ring
p_{y}=0
and of the bulk
p_{y}\neq 0
separately.

D.2.1
Contribution from modes
p_{y}\neq 0
,
p_{z}\neq 0

We introduce non-dimensional polar coordinates

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{z}=p_{z}/k_{f},\qquad\tilde{p}_{y}=\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}\sin(\phi),

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{x}=\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}\cos(\phi)

(108)

where we consider only
p_{y},p_{z},p_{x}>0
, the other quadrants giving the same result by symmetry. We let
p_{x}\to 0
continuously but cutoff a strip of width
k_{y}/k_{f}
(
l_{f}/L_{z}
) around
p_{y}=0
(
p_{z}=0
) from the integration, giving a limit on the smallest angle to use in the
\phi
integration and on the smallest possible
\tilde{p}_{z}
:

\displaystyle\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{z}}<\tilde{p}_{z}<\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right)^{2}}\approx 1,

\displaystyle\phi^{-}<\phi<\frac{\pi}{2}\qquad\phi^{-}=\arcsin\left[\frac{k_{y}}{k_{f}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}}\right].

(109)

When all 3D modes are resonant (i.e obey condition (
106
)), their contribution is

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}^{\rm sphere}}{\epsilon}\approx\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}}^{1}\int_{\phi^{-}}^{\pi/2}d\tilde{p}_{z}d\phi\approx 1-\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}-\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\qquad(p_{y}\neq 0)

(110)

where we only keep terms up to
O(l_{f}/L_{z}),O(l_{f}/L_{y})
(and in the following contributions of higher order from the integral should be discarded).
We are also assuming that all modes in the sphere are forced with the same weight.

Nearly-resonant modes
p_{y}\neq 0
satisfy

|\sin(\phi)|\tilde{p}_{z}\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}<\beta,

(111)

with

\displaystyle\beta\equiv\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{2\Omega}\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}},

(112)

which results in a requirement for the angular integration,

\frac{k_{y}}{k_{f}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}}<|\sin(\phi)|<\frac{\beta}{\tilde{p}_{z}\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}},

(113)

taking into consideration the
p_{y}-
cutoff in the lower bound.
This leads to a range of angles
[\phi^{-}~\phi^{+}]
defined for each
\tilde{p}_{z}
as

\displaystyle\phi^{+}=\begin{cases}\arcsin\left[\beta\frac{1}{\tilde{p}_{z}\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}}\right],\qquad\frac{\beta}{\tilde{p}_{z}\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}}<1\\
\pi/2\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad{\rm otherwise}\end{cases}

(114)

and

\displaystyle\phi^{-}=\arcsin\left[\frac{k_{y}}{k_{f}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}}\right]

(115)

(we consider
0<\phi<\pi/2
and multiply the result by 4 to get the contribution from the other quadrants).

Resonant modes exist, i.e.
\phi^{+}>\phi^{-}
, only for

\tilde{p}_{z}<\beta\frac{k_{f}}{k_{y}}

(116)

which restricts the
\tilde{p}_{z}
we can consider in this class. Outside this class modes still need to satisfy

\tilde{p}_{z}<\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{k_{y}}{k_{f}}\right)^{2}}

(117)

for
\tilde{p}_{y}
to lie below the cutoff
l_{f}/L_{y}
.

The resonant condition is restrictive, i.e.
\phi^{+}<\pi/2
, for
\tilde{p}_{z}
in the range

\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\beta^{2}}}{2}}<\tilde{p}_{z}<\sqrt{\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\beta^{2}}}{2}}

(118)

For
\tilde{p}_{z}
outside this range, and satisfying
\tilde{p}_{z}<\beta k_{f}/k_{y}
, all modes up to
\phi=\pi/2
contribute.

In particular, we see that the transition to decoupling,
T_{\rm 2D}<\epsilon
, occurs when
\beta=1/2
, for
\tilde{p}_{z}=1/\sqrt{2}
(the point where
\tilde{p}_{z}\tilde{p}_{y}
is maximized), where the full sphere is resonant.

Now assuming
\beta\leq 1/2
, we define

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{z}^{-}=\max\left(\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\beta^{2}}}{2}},\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\right)

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{z}^{+}=\min\left(\sqrt{\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\beta^{2}}}{2}},\beta\frac{k_{f}}{k_{y}},\right),

(119)

The contribution from modes
p_{y}\neq 0,p_{z}\neq 0
is finally

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}^{\rm sphere}}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}}^{p_{z}^{-}}d\tilde{p}_{z}\int_{\phi^{-}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\phi+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{p_{z}^{-}}^{p_{z}^{+}}d\tilde{p}_{z}\int_{\phi^{-}}^{\phi^{+}}d\phi

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{p_{z}^{+}}^{\min\left(\beta\frac{k_{f}}{k_{y}},\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{k_{y}}{k_{f}}\right)^{2}}\right)}d\tilde{p}_{z}\int_{\phi^{-}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\phi,

(120)

where we consider that each integral has increasing bounds, and is otherwise null.

D.2.2
Contribution from modes
p_{y}=0

In addition, we need to consider separately the contribution from the ring at
\tilde{p}_{y}=0
.
The contribution from the full ring at
p_{y}=0
is a fraction of energy equal to
\frac{2\pi k_{f}\Delta k}{(2\pi/L_{x})(2\pi/L_{z})}\frac{(2\pi/L_{x})(2\pi/L_{z})(2\pi/L_{y})}{4\pi k_{f}^{2}\Delta k}=k_{y}/2k_{f}
where
\Delta k
is the width of the shell.
Introducing polar coordinates
\tilde{p}_{z}=\sin\theta
,
\tilde{p}_{x}=\cos\theta
,
the contribution from the ring is written as

\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}^{ring}}{\epsilon}=4\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{y}}\int\frac{d\theta}{2\pi},

(121)

(we count 4 times the sector
\theta\in[0~\frac{\pi}{2}]
),
where the bounds of the
\theta-
integration
vary depending on the resonant condition (
107
), which reads

\tilde{p}_{z}=\sin\theta<\frac{U^{\prime}}{\Omega}\frac{L_{y}^{2}}{l_{f}^{2}}\equiv 2\beta\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}.

(122)

The integration on the ring should therefore be performed over

\displaystyle\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}<\sin(\theta)<2\beta\frac{k_{f}}{k_{y}}

(123)

and we can define

\displaystyle\theta^{-}\approx\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\qquad\theta^{+}=\begin{cases}\pi/2\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad 2\beta\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}>1\\
\arcsin\left(2\beta\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\right)\qquad 2\beta\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}<1\end{cases}

(124)

so that

\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}^{\rm ring}}{\epsilon}=\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\int_{\theta^{-}}^{\theta^{+}}\frac{d\theta}{\pi}=\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\frac{(\theta^{+}-\theta^{-})}{\pi}

(125)

The ring does not contribute when
\theta^{+}<\theta^{-}
, implying the restriction

\beta\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}

(126)

For
\beta<1/2(l_{f}/L_{z})(l_{f}/L_{y})
, using the Heaviside filter, the 3D modes inside the forcing ring in the plane
p_{y}=0
completely decouple from the condensate.

D.2.3
Regimes of decoupling

The total energy contribution from resonant 3D modes is finally given by

\begin{split}\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}=\tilde{\epsilon}\left(\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}^{\rm sphere}}{\epsilon}+\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}^{\rm ring}}{\epsilon}\right)\end{split}

(127)

with

\tilde{\epsilon}\equiv\frac{1-\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{z}}}{1-\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{y}}-\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}},

(128)

a factor correcting the weight of each point in the truncated sphere and in the ring.
Such a rescaling is necessary to make the continuous integration compatible with the energy injection rate in the discrete setup.
If all 3D modes contribute,

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}=1-\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{z}}\equiv\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 3D}}{\epsilon},

(129)

the fraction of the energy injected in 3D modes.
Note that when writing (
127
), we use the same weight for modes in the ring and in the truncated sphere, which is only an approximation to what happens in the discrete setup with anisotropic dimensions
L_{y}=2L_{x}
.
This can result in errors in
O(l_{f}/L_{y},l_{f}/L_{z})
, which will only slighly alter the leading-order behavior of
\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}
.

From (
127
), (
120
) and (
125
), we obtain explicitly:

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\tilde{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=\frac{l_{f}}{\pi L_{y}}(\theta^{+}-\theta^{-})+\left(p_{z}^{-}-\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{p_{z}^{-}}^{p_{z}^{+}}d\tilde{p}_{z}\phi^{+}-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{p_{z}^{-}}^{\min\left(\beta\frac{k_{f}}{k_{y}},\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{k_{y}}{k_{f}}\right)^{2}}\right)}\phi^{-}d\tilde{p}_{z}

\displaystyle-\left(p_{z}^{+}-\min\left(\beta\frac{k_{f}}{k_{y}},\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{k_{y}}{k_{f}}\right)^{2}}\right)\right)

(130)

with
\beta=\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{2\Omega}
.
The energy transfer
\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}
from Eq. (
130
) is shown as a function of
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
in Fig.
6
(b).
The energy transfer decreases with decreased
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega=\beta\frac{l_{f}}{2L_{y}}
, due to a combined effect of the bounds in the integral being more and more restricted, and the integrand
\phi^{+}
decreasing with decreasing
\beta
.

In the following, we consider some asymptotic limits in order to simplify Eq. (
130
).

1.

Beginning of decoupling:
l_{f}/L_{y},l_{f}/L_{z}\ll\beta\ll 1/2
.

Here we can use the asymptotic form
\tilde{p}_{z}^{-}\approx\beta
and
\tilde{p}_{z}^{+}\approx 1-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}
, and to leading order

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\tilde{\epsilon}}=\beta-\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}

(131)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\beta}^{1-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}}\arcsin\left[\frac{\beta}{\tilde{p}_{z}\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}}\right]d\tilde{p}_{z}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\beta^{2}-\frac{l_{f}^{2}}{L_{y}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\beta}^{\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right)^{2}}}\arcsin\left[\frac{k_{y}}{k_{f}\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}}\right]d\tilde{p}_{z}.

(132)

Outside the regions where
\tilde{p}_{z}\sim 1
and
\tilde{p}_{z}\sim\beta
, the argument of the arcsin is small and one can take a linear approximation:

\int\phi^{+}d\tilde{p}_{z}\approx\int\beta\frac{1}{\tilde{p}_{z}\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}}d\tilde{p}_{z}=-\beta\tanh^{-1}\left(\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}\right)

(133)

and

\int\phi^{-}d\tilde{p}_{z}\approx\int\frac{k_{y}}{k_{f}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}}d\tilde{p}_{z}=\frac{k_{y}}{k_{f}}\sin^{-1}(\tilde{p}_{z}).

(134)

We then obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}=\beta-\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}--\beta\,\frac{2}{\pi}\left[\tanh^{-1}(\beta)-\tanh^{-1}\!\big(\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}\big)\right],

(135)

where we have used that

\left[\tanh^{-1}(\sqrt{1-p_{z}^{2}})\right]_{a}^{b}=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\log\Big(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}}{1+\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}}\Big)\Big]_{a}^{b}\approx\log(\frac{b}{a})

when
b,a\ll 1
and

\approx\log(2/a)
when
b\sim 1
and
a\ll 1
.

2.

Decoupling of bulk modes (
p_{y},p_{z}\gg l_{f}/L_{z},l_{f}/L_{y}
)

For
l_{f}/L_{y}\ll\beta\ll l_{f}/L_{z}
(if
L_{z}<L_{y}
)
we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\tilde{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{l_{f}/L_{z}}^{1-\beta^{2}}\arcsin\left[\frac{\beta}{\tilde{p}_{z}\sqrt{1-\tilde{p}_{z}^{2}}}\right]d\tilde{p}_{z}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{2}{\pi}\beta\ln(2L_{z}/l_{f})

(136)

Here, the lower boundaries of integration have changed (i.e. the smallest possible
p_{z}
are now restricted by the resonance condition, hence
p_{z}^{-}=l_{f}/L_{z}
), and we have neglected terms of the order
O(l_{f}/L_{y})
.
Note that when
L_{z}>L_{y}
, for
l_{f}/L_{z}\ll\beta\ll l_{f}/L_{y}
we get

\begin{split}\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\tilde{\epsilon}}\approx&\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\beta}^{\beta L_{y}/l_{f}}d\tilde{p}_{z}\phi^{+}\\
&\approx\frac{2}{\pi}\beta\ln(L_{y}/l_{f})\end{split}

3.

Energy transfer due to small
p_{z}
and
p_{y}
.

In the range
(l_{f}/L_{y})(l_{f}/L_{z})\ll\beta\ll l_{f}/L_{y},l_{f}/L_{z}
we now have
\tilde{p}_{z}^{+}=\beta L_{y}/l_{f}
, and
\tilde{p}_{z}^{-}=l_{f}/L_{z}
:

\begin{split}\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\tilde{\epsilon}}&=\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\int_{l_{f}/L_{z}}^{2\beta L_{y}/l_{f}}\frac{d\theta}{\pi}+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{l_{f}/L_{z}}^{\beta\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}}d\tilde{p}_{z}\phi^{+}\\
&\approx\frac{2}{\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{1}{2}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right)+\frac{2}{\pi}\beta\ln\left(\beta\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\frac{L_{z}}{l_{f}}\right)\end{split}

4.

Energy transfer from
p_{y}=0
only.

Finally, for
\frac{1}{2}(l_{f}/L_{y})(l_{f}/L_{z})<\beta<(l_{f}/L_{y})(l_{f}/L_{z})
, the tiny region where most of the contribution comes from are modes with
p_{y}=0
and they do not decouple fully,

\begin{split}\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}\approx\frac{2}{\pi}\beta-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\end{split}

(137)

For even lower
\beta<\frac{1}{2}(l_{f}/L_{y})(l_{f}/L_{z})
, there is no energy transfer from the 3D waves to the 2D condensate using the Heaviside approximation of the oscillating factor.

All in all, we obtain the following asymptotic results:

\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\tilde{\epsilon}}=\begin{cases}1,\qquad\beta\geq\frac{1}{2},\\
\frac{2}{\pi}\beta\ln\left(\frac{2}{\beta}\right)+\beta-\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}},\quad\max\left\{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}},\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right\}<\beta<\frac{1}{2}\\
\\
\frac{2}{\pi}\beta\ln\left(\min\left(\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}},\frac{2L_{z}}{l_{f}}\right)\right),\\
\quad\quad\min\left\{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}},\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right\}<\beta<\max\left\{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}},\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right\}\\
\\
\frac{2}{\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{1}{2}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right)+\frac{2}{\pi}\beta\ln\left(\beta\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\frac{L_{z}}{l_{f}}\right),\\
\quad\quad\quad\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}<\beta<\min\left\{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}},\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right\}\\
\\
\frac{2}{\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{1}{2}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right),\quad\frac{1}{2}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}<\beta<\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\\
\\
0,\qquad\qquad\beta<\frac{1}{2}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\end{cases}

(138)

with
\beta=\frac{U^{\prime}}{2\Omega}\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}
.
We have included linear terms in
O(\beta)
in Eq. (
138
), so as to obtain better predictions for our DNS case where
Lk_{f}=10
.
Note, however, that the asymptotic scalings are valid within each range when
\beta
is sufficiently far from the bounds of the range.
Thus, when using inequalities in (
138
), we
habe derived a good leading-order approximation of the exact function, but where small discontinuities appear at the regime bounds.

The scalings in (
138
) are shown in Fig.
6
(b) as a dashed red line, for the parameters corresponding to our DNS (
L_{y}/l_{f}=10,L_{z}/l_{f}=5
),
and are a very good approximation of the integration of the exact formula (
130
).

D.2.4
Including 2D-2D interactions

We now need to include the fraction of energy injected into the 2D manifold, which is not affected by the resonant condition, and never decouples if forced.
As established in §
C
,
T_{\rm 2D-2D}
is half the energy injected in the 2D manifold.
With our isotropic forcing,

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 2D-2D}}{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{k_{f}}{2L_{z}}

(139)

The final asymptotic expression for the total energy transfer is therefore

\frac{T_{\rm 2D}}{\epsilon}=\begin{cases}\frac{T_{\rm 2D-2D}}{\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 3D}}{\epsilon},\qquad\beta\geq\frac{1}{2}\\
\\
\frac{T_{\rm 2D-2D}}{\epsilon}+\tilde{\epsilon}\left(\beta-\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}+\frac{2}{\pi}\beta\ln\left(\frac{2}{\beta}\right)\right),\\
\qquad\qquad\max\{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}},\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\}<\beta<\frac{1}{2}\\
\\
\frac{T_{\rm 2D-2D}}{\epsilon}+\tilde{\epsilon}\frac{2}{\pi}\beta\ln\left(2\frac{L_{z}}{l_{f}}\right),\\
\quad\quad\min\{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}},\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\}<\beta<\max\{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}},\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\}\\
\\
\frac{T_{\rm 2D-2D}}{\epsilon}+\tilde{\epsilon}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{1}{2}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right)\right)+\frac{2}{\pi}\beta\ln\left(\beta\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\frac{L_{z}}{l_{f}}\right),\\
\quad\quad\quad\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}<\beta<\min\{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}},\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\}\\
\\
\frac{T_{\rm 2D-2D}}{\epsilon}+\tilde{\epsilon}\frac{2}{\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{1}{2}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\right),\quad\frac{1}{2}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}<\beta<\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\\
\\
\frac{T_{\rm 2D}}{\epsilon},\qquad\qquad\beta<\frac{1}{2}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}\end{cases}

(140)

D.3
Closure of the energy balance for the mean flow

Here we derive the expression of both the condensate
amplitude
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
and the energy transfer
T_{\rm 3D-2D}
as a function of the control parameters
Ro_{\epsilon}
and
l_{f}/L_{i}
(
i=x,y,z
).
We determine
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
from (
140
) by closing with
the mean-flow energy balance

\left(\frac{U^{\prime}}{\Omega}\right)^{2}=4Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}\frac{T_{\rm 2D}}{\epsilon}

(141)

which in terms of variable
\beta
reads

\beta^{2}=Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}\left(\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\right)^{2}\frac{T_{\rm 2D}}{\epsilon}\left(\beta\right).

(142)

With the piecewise function
T_{\rm 2D}(\beta)
in (
140
), we can solve numerically the algebraic equation (
142
)
via a root-finding procedure.
The resulting solution
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
is shown as a function of
Ro_{\epsilon}
as a red line in the inset of Fig.
3
.
In the following, we instead proceed analytically by extracting the leading-order terms in (
140
) and closing
with (
142
) separately in each range in
\beta
.

D.3.1
Closure in absence of energy injection into the 2D manifold

It is simpler to first consider the case where
\epsilon_{\rm 2D}=0
,
for which we need to close the mean-flow equation (
142
) with (
138
).
We also assume
L_{z}<L_{y}
for shortness.

1.

For
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
,
T_{\rm 2D}=\epsilon_{\rm 3D}
and the solution is
\beta=Ro_{\epsilon}L_{y}/l_{f}
, which is valid for
Ro_{\epsilon}>l_{f}/(2L_{y})
. So, in this regime we obtain the result

\frac{U^{\prime}}{\Omega}=2\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 3D}}{\epsilon}Ro_{\epsilon},

(143)

which is a rotation-independent similar to the scaling of condensates in 2DNSE.

2.

For
l_{f}/L_{z}\ll\beta\ll\frac{1}{2}
, consistent with
Ro_{\epsilon}\ll l_{f}/L_{y}
, we have the balance

\frac{\beta}{\ln(1/\beta)}=\frac{2}{\pi}Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}\left(\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\right)^{2}\equiv\alpha

(144)

using Eq. (
140
) (second row) at the leading order in
\beta
.
Since
\alpha\ll 1
, solving this equation perturbatively gives

\beta=\alpha\ln(1/\alpha)=-\frac{2}{\pi}Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}\left(\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\right)^{2}\ln\left(\frac{2}{\pi}Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}\left(\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\right)^{2}\right)

(145)

with corrections of order
\alpha\ln(-\ln\alpha)
. From the definition of
\beta
and the condition that
1/2\gg\beta\gg l_{f}/L_{y},l_{f}/L_{z}
we then obtain the solution

\displaystyle\frac{U^{\prime}}{\Omega}=-\frac{8}{\pi}Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\ln\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}Ro_{\epsilon}\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}\max\left(\sqrt{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}},\sqrt{\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}}\right)\ll Ro_{\epsilon}\ll\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}

(146)

3.

For
\frac{l_{f}}{L_{y}}<\beta<\frac{l_{f}}{L_{z}}
, the closure equation is trivial and we obtain at leading order

Appendix E
Discussions on the Lorentzian filter

We can compare our usage of a Heaviside form for the oscillating factor (
58
) with a Lorentzian form (
60
), which is continuous in the time-scale ratio
{U^{\prime}}/\Omega
, hence better weights the contribution of the oscillating factor over the time window
t\in[0~1/{U^{\prime}}]
.
We consider here a discrete set of waves and compute

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}\left[\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{p}}\chi_{\bm{p}}~F_{k_{f}}\Big(\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\Big),

(169)

with the Lorentzian filter

\displaystyle F_{k_{f}}=\frac{1}{\left(1+\left(\frac{\Omega}{{U^{\prime}}}Q\frac{4\pi p_{z}\sqrt{k_{f}^{2}-p_{x}^{2}-p_{z}^{2}}}{k_{f}^{3}}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}},

(170)

where parameter
Q
is set to
Q=8
.

The numerical value of (
169
) is shown as a blue line in Fig.
6
(b).
At the leading order, it follows a linear scaling
\sim\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}
(up to prefactors depending on the domain geometry), hence vanishes exactly at
\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}=0
, contrary to the computation with the Heaviside filter.
The mean flow balance

\displaystyle\left(\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\right)^{2}=4Ro_{\epsilon}^{2}\left(\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 2D}}{2\epsilon}\right)

(171)

is then solved numerically with varying parameter
Ro_{\epsilon}
, yielding the numerical estimate for
T_{\rm 3D-2D}(Ro_{\epsilon})
shown in Fig.
7
(blue line).

(a)

Figure 7:
Results obtained with two approximation of the oscillating factor (
57
): Heaviside (red), and Lorentzian (blue). Both are obtained numerically from closing (
141
) with either (
138
) or (
169
).
The Lorentzian approximation takes into account infinitesimally-small near-resonances when
Ro_{\epsilon}\to 0
, hence predicts that
T_{\rm 3D-2D}
vanishes only at
Ro_{\epsilon}=0
. In contrast, the prediction of
T_{\rm 3D-2D}
from the Heaviside approximation vanishes at a finite
Ro_{\epsilon}
, which better corresponds the observations of the DNS of rotating 3DNSE.

With our isotropic forcing, which excites surviving 2D-2D interactions when
Ro_{\epsilon}\to 0
, the solution when
Ro_{\epsilon}\to 0
therefore scales as

\displaystyle\frac{{U^{\prime}}}{\Omega}\sim 2Ro_{\epsilon}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 2D}}{2\epsilon}},~~~~\frac{T_{\rm 3D-2D}}{\epsilon}\sim 2Ro_{\epsilon}\frac{L_{y}}{l_{f}}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\rm 2D}}{2\epsilon}}.

(172)

Therefore, with the inclusion of infinitesimally-small near-resonances via the tails of the Lorentzian filter, there is always a small 3D-2D coupling until
Ro_{\epsilon}=0
exactly, at which 2D and 3D modes completely decouple.
Because the DNS data points deviate from the Lorentzian prediction when
Ro_{\epsilon}\to 0
, this suggests that such off-resonant tails are negligible in the nonlinear dynamics.

Meanwhile, the Lorentzian filter better approximates the beginning of the decoupling regime (
Ro_{\epsilon}\sim l_{f}/L_{y}
), and, in particular, the fact that
T_{\rm 3D-2D}/\epsilon
is never 1, even when rotation is low (but
Ro_{\epsilon}\lesssim l_{f}/L_{z}
for waves to be still homochiral).
This is due to the fact that the Lorentzian filter better weights the time window
[0~1/{U^{\prime}}]
where the oscillating factor
\Delta(t)
is not exactly one, but decays with time
t
.
When rotation is low, time-scale separation is limited and it matters to weight this time window appropriately, as achieved with the Lorentzian filter.

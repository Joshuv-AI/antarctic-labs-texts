Blockchain and digital twin integration for predictive and secure pandemic alerting
Authors: Padmavathi V, Kanimozhi R
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Due to the COVID-19 pandemic, there is a necessity to implement the latest technologies that will help provide safe, real-time, and predictive medical services. The current pandemic alert apps, including BlueDot, Aarogya Setu, and the JHU Dashboard, are based on centralized reporting and manual updates, which reduce their scalability, security, and predictions. In an effort to address these gaps, this paper proposes a novel concept that integrates blockchain, artificial intelligence (AI), and digital twins to decentralize pandemic alerting and monitoring. Within the proposed COVID-DT model, blockchain enables the tamper-free, decentralized sharing of data across healthcare stakeholders. BiLSTM networks improve predictive accuracy over time, and DTs generate dynamic digital imprints for use in ongoing monitoring and simulation of outbreaks. An implementation was designed based on Raspberry Pi edge devices, IoT sensors, and Hyperledger Fabric, and simulated in MATLAB. Findings show predictive accuracy of 97.7, lower latency time of 4.3 min using 12 worker nodes, consistent message delivery (approximately 80%), and a cost of communication of less than 700 bytes with an error of 10 percent. These results demonstrate the scalability, low power, and cybersecurity of this model. The COVID-DT framework provides a safe, efficient, and interoperable backbone for the management of future pandemics, surpassing current centralized systems.

Introduction

The healthcare industry is shifting towards what is known as healthcare 4.0, which involves building a learning healthcare system to support data-driven decisions and converting care into an autonomous process. This is achieved by utilizing big data, digital twins, machine learning (ML), and the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT). Digital twins are virtual representations of real-world objects, such as machinery and people with associated gadgets. Combining blockchain technology with digital twin technologies is the optimal approach for constructing medical cyber-physical systems (MCPS) that incorporate the safe interconnection of multiple medical devices and systems1,2. A secure, decentralized collaboration system is also required for successful device interconnections to send timely signals to the decision-makers in MCPSs. The COVID-19 pandemic highlights the need to utilize sophisticated technologies like blockchain, digital twins, and AI to enable and improve remote work during lockdowns and prevent further coronavirus epidemics.

Industry 4.0 is a digital system that combines the Industrial Internet of Things, Machine Learning, Big Data, and Edge Computing. This convergence results in an autonomous and decentralized system. Decentralized manufacturing, which utilizes digitalization in smart manufacturing, employs an effective form of collaboration for information exchange, analytics, and real-time group decision-making3. Digital Twin technology is a key component of manufacturing digitalization, which represents real-world systems like production processes in a virtual environment. A distributed production system has many DTs at different levels, including the machine level, where DTs in a flat network represent distinct objects4.

A digital twin is a virtual replica of a physical object that must have congruent virtual and physical characteristics for its intended use. In healthcare, a patient-centric DT refers to a virtual patient in a digital environment representing the patient’s physical state with important data. However, to provide a complete picture of a patient’s health, all relevant information about the patient’s care, hospital, and medications must be included. It is crucial to consider the complexity of the human body and the various external factors that can affect it, including the environment, age, and social interactions. Furthermore, the factors contributing to illness or comorbidity can vary greatly among individuals5. Thus, the DT can evaluate a patient’s current status accurately and even forecast future risks by analyzing real-time data and gathering relevant information from birth to death6. The primary goal of a patient-centered digital healthcare tool is to provide all the necessary information for remotely monitoring and caring for a patient in various situations, including specific periods.

The integration of DT and blockchain technology has been observed in several industrial areas. ManuChain, a suggested iterative bi-level paradigm, incorporates blockchain into decentralized manufacturing7. It utilizes blockchain’s decentralized properties to establish a lower level for fine-grained self-organization intelligence and an upper level for coarse-grained holistic optimization intelligence. Another blockchain-based architecture based on DTs, called Makerchain, coordinates updating data tags to increase the trustworthiness of manufacturing service transactions among makers.

A consensus-oriented transaction logic for MBCoT is introduced in a blockchain-based manufacturing architecture to ensure autonomous manufacturing processes8. ExplorerChain is another blockchain-based protocol created to serve as a resource for academics looking to use blockchain technology in healthcare/genomics. It incorporates online machine learning, transaction metadata, and the Proof-of-Information-Timed algorithm. Additionally, the authors describe in detail how they have utilized DLT to create a protocol that ensures value transfer across DTs in economic systems9. Furthermore, a method for secure DT data exchange based on DLT is offered to track events and provenance data and promote transparency for all stakeholders.

Blockchain is a distributed ledger that enables all nodes on the network to access a single ledger containing all block data. Replication is used in this process to achieve immutability and non-repudiation. Blockchain eliminates the need for a third party and a central authority, resulting in technical advancements. Dispersed transactions between members of different organizations are made possible by blockchain without requiring a centralized system10. Once a block has been verified and added to the chain, it cannot be deleted or changed. However, obtaining the same state for all nodes in a distributed system becomes challenging. Consensus, a technique developed by blockchain, relies on earlier rule agreements and the idea of majority rule to ensure consistency across various nodes. Therefore, blockchain primarily relies on consensus methods to guarantee consistency across nodes.

As far as we know, none of the existing pandemic alert systems integrates blockchain, AI, and digital twins in one system. The existing applications like BlueDot, Aarogya Setu, and JHU Dashboard rely largely on centralized data aggregation and do not have secure interoperability or predictive modeling. Blockchain supports decentralized and trusted work among multiple healthcare stakeholders; BiLSTM enhances the accuracy of temporal prediction compared to traditional models; and digital twins are utilized to generate dynamic images for monitoring patients and populations. This synergy offers an alert system on pandemic in real-time, secured and scalable, which is a clear distinction between our work and the solutions that exist today.

Related works and contributions

This section explores the current approaches to developing digital twins and integrating them with blockchain technology11. Currently, the creation of DTs often relies on outdated practices and centralized authority, which raises concerns about the reliability of the resulting systems. The need for more literature on the topic and interoperability among collaborators and partners are challenges that must be addressed. Additionally, real-time communication transparency is a crucial feature that must be incorporated into the development of DTs. One proposed solution is the manufacturing blockchain of things (MBCoT) architecture, which offers a decentralized, traceable, and secure production configuration. The data- and knowledge-driven digital twin manufacturing cell is a reference model for decentralized production, with MBCoT incorporating a fault-tolerant protocol to support autonomous manufacturing. ExplorerChain, developed by Kuo, is another example that can serve as a reference for academics interested in applying blockchain technology in the healthcare/genomic industries, incorporating online machine learning, transaction metadata, and the Proof-of-Information-Timed algorithm12. Developing a protocol can also facilitate the exchange of values between digital twins in economic systems. In addition, the literature further describes a digital twin reference model for preventive maintenance separated into edge, fog, and cloud layers and a DLT-based architecture for secure digital twin data sharing to track events and provenance data, which improves transparency for all parties involved. The previous works mentioned in the article have all discussed collaboration within innovative industries. Still, they have yet to focus on how blockchain technology can facilitate cooperation between digital twins to improve management in distributed smart manufacturing13,14. This study addresses this issue by proposing and implementing a decentralized blockchain-based solution that provides a secure, robust, and reliable method for tracking and tracing the various stages of creating digital twins.

A blockchain is a chain of data structures that links data blocks chronologically and cryptographically for immutability and unforgeability. Each data block has a block header and a block body. The block header contains information such as the block generation’s version, timestamp, and hash value of the previous block. In contrast, the block body has a copy of the transaction list in the interim. As a result, blockchain can be considered a distributed ledger. Because of its immutability and unforgeability, blockchain can hold valuable digital assets (such as money, copyrights, contracts, and notarization). It can record and transfer the values of these assets over time. Blockchain technology has the potential to provide significant value.

In recent years, blockchain integrated with AI and digital twins has gained momentum in healthcare. Several studies combine blockchain with federated learning to secure medical data while enabling collaborative training across hospitals. For example, a blockchain-driven customized FL framework for the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) improves both privacy and scalability15 , while a blockchain-enabled FL framework ensures privacy-preserved electronic health record (EHR) sharing16. These works demonstrate how blockchain strengthens trust in distributed health data learning environments. In parallel, blockchain-secured digital twins have emerged to safeguard patient DT updates through smart contracts and permissioned ledgers. Such models support integrity verification, fine-grained access control, and interoperability with healthcare systems, highlighting blockchain’s role in maintaining trustworthy DTs17. Finally, several recent surveys and integrative studies emphasize the convergence of blockchain, AI, and DTs in healthcare. These works underline trends toward privacy-preserving federated models, explainable AI integration, and interoperable DT ecosystems18. Collectively, they frame the healthcare industry’s transition toward secure, data-driven, and decentralized infrastructures.

The proposed framework for pandemic alerting using blockchain-based collaborative digital twins

The proposed architecture presents a reliable and efficient platform for Blockchain-based AI-enabled applications at the edge. This technology ensures continuous AI prediction by removing single points of failure and delivering processed data and results for improved decision-making to governmental agencies and companies19. Using a secure, decentralized, and open blockchain platform to authenticate and safeguard all AI data, including inputs and outcomes, ensures data integrity. Compared to other relevant research, the suggested architecture offers a platform that provides AI data integrity through validation and transparency. This resilient and redundant AI engine can be deployed without compromising its accuracy by utilizing edge devices and IoT end devices that require minimal power20.

BiLSTM model architecture and training.

We present a Bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network designed to be used with epidemic time-series and medical imaging data to increase the accuracy of predictions in the COVID-DT framework. The model takes as input several features, including epidemiological data, chest X-ray images, CT scan images, and patient metadata. The preprocessing stage involved the normalization of the medical images, the resizing of medical images (224 × 224 pixels), and data augmentation (rotation, flipping, scaling) to enhance generalization. Min–max scaling was used to normalize the numerical data (case counts and patient metadata). The BiLSTM structure has a 128-unit stacked BiLSTM layer at both its base and top layers with tanh-activation functions to evaluate forward and backward temporal correlation. To minimize overfitting, a dropout rate = 0.3 was used between BiLSTM layers. The learned features are subsequently merged into a fully connected dense layer of 64 units with ReLU activation, and a final softmax output layer is used to classify the cases into COVID vs. non-COVID.

AI-based options for combating coronavirus

In this section, we discuss how AI can be applied to combat the COVID-19 outbreak, as shown in Fig. 3. AI has the potential to aid in five key areas: estimating the epidemic, detecting the virus, analyzing data related to the virus, developing vaccines/drugs, and forecasting future outbreaks similar to COVID-1922,23. Various AI-based approaches have been proposed for COVID-19 detection. One such process involves using AI to measure a person’s facial temperature to detect potential COVID-19 symptoms. AI’s face recognition capabilities can generate solutions in response to the outbreak, as demonstrated in a recent study that utilized real-world datasets and AI to advance masked face identification. The researchers trained a reliable masked face detection model using deep learning and applied it to a masked face recognition challenge to identify if a person is wearing a mask.

Another study employed deep learning to classify six clinically significant respiratory patterns associated with COVID-19 to identify potential carriers through their respiratory features. Remembering people’s unexpected abnormal respiratory patterns discreetly and remotely is crucial for COVID-19 diagnosis. In addition, image analysis-based approaches have been employed to detect the virus. The researchers analyzed chest X-ray radiographs using various convolutional neural network-based models to identify individuals with COVID-19 pneumonia. According to simulations, the ResNet50 model achieved the highest accuracy (98%) among the three suggested models for COVID-19 classification.

Latency of the system

The system’s performance was evaluated in three areas: latency, accuracy, and power consumption. In terms of latency, the system’s total latency was determined for each test while mining using different numbers of worker nodes (WNs). It was observed that increasing the number of WNs participating in the block mining process decreased the average latency. The latency was reduced from over 40 min when using only one WN to around 4.3 min when 12 WNs were employed to mine one block. The latency could have been further reduced if more WNs were available.

Accuracy of AI

Regarding accuracy, the AI algorithm was trained on 75% of the dataset using a Raspberry Pi, resulting in a prediction accuracy of 97.7%. The algorithm was then tested on a standalone Raspberry Pi device without using the blockchain network, accessing 15% of the dataset, and the prediction accuracy was 95%. When the blockchain network solution was installed using a single AN, the system was tested thrice using 5% of the unused dataset. The prediction accuracy of 95% was consistent with the standalone system, indicating that incorporating blockchain for better data security did not affect the accuracy of the AI engine.

Powercost

In doing so, we conducted an extensive analysis at various stages of operation using the Raspberry Pi. These stages are the following: (1) the connection and idle state, (2) exchange of data with the blockchain network, (3) the mining process, and (4) the AI model prediction procedures.

The analysis reveals that power consumption is minimal when the device is in idle and connected states. The energy consumption increases moderately in the mining and AI processing procedures, yet neither is extraordinarily large; thus, it is acceptable for the embedded IoT device presentations28. These features, combined with an optimization algorithm, blockchain consensus, and a lightweight AI architecture, facilitate an energy-efficient design system.

Moreover, we tested the system’s applicability to diverse use scenarios where edge devices requiring batteries are implemented in remote settings. Result and discussion

The following results directly validate the contributions outlined in Section “Introduction”, including blockchain-driven security, BiLSTM predictive accuracy, and the scalability of the digital twin framework.

Simulation evaluation

The simulation experiment tests the efficacy of the suggested method in maximizing performance at the minimum cost. A COVID-2019 epidemic digital twin system has been developed on a MATLAB network simulation platfor 29. This system also incorporates hardware and software architecture to achieve its objectives.

To make the simulation more faithful to actual operation restrictions, we have subsequently extended and augmented the MATLAB simulation through a snippet of a proof-of-concept implementation on 16 Raspberry Pi edge devices, interlinked by a local area network and serviced by a fog server. Each of the Raspberry Pi machines has the Raspbian operating system installed, as well as being configured to support containerized PoW modules to participate in the blockchain and lightweight AI models to run. The fog server is a machine equipped with an Intel Core i7 processor and 16 GB of RAM, which orchestrates the functions of data combination and blockchain verification30. Scalability beyond current configuration

While our proof-of-concept implementation was demonstrated on Raspberry Pi devices with up to 12–16 Worker Nodes (WNs), the framework is designed to scale further. The reduction in latency from ~ 40 min (1 WN) to 4.3 min (12 WNs) suggests a near-linear improvement trend with additional nodes, up to the point where network congestion or consensus overhead becomes significant.

Beyond Raspberry Pi clusters the architecture supports heterogeneous edge and fog devices (e.g., GPU-enabled edge servers, cloud-fog hybrids), which provide higher throughput and lower latency at scale. In practical deployments, hundreds or thousands of WNs could be distributed across hospitals and health agencies, with blockchain ensuring secure interoperability.

Consensus scalability While Proof-of-Work (PoW) was adopted for prototype testing, it may become inefficient at large scale. Alternatives such as Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT), Proof-of-Authority (PoA), or Delegated Proof-of-Stake (DPoS) can reduce energy cost and improve throughput while maintaining resilience.

Energy and communication costs As demonstrated in our IoT analysis, a 600mAh-powered device can sustain more than two months of operation with 3000 WNs and even longer with 5000 WNs due to workload distribution. This indicates feasibility for large-scale, battery-powered deployments in both urban and rural settings.

A key requirement for the adoption of healthcare data systems is interoperability with widely accepted standards such as HL7 FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources) and DICOM (Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine). Our COVID-DT framework integrates with these standards in the following ways:HL7 FHIR Integration The framework uses RESTful APIs to map blockchain transactions and digital twin states into FHIR-compliant resources (e.g., Patient, Observation, ImagingStudy). Smart contracts enforce schema validation to ensure that only FHIR-compatible records are committed to the blockchain. This enables the secure exchange of patient demographics, laboratory results, and epidemiological updates between hospitals and public health authorities.DICOM Support for Imaging Data Chest X-rays and CT scans remain stored in existing PACS or imaging repositories in DICOM format. The blockchain maintains hashed metadata and secure pointers referencing these images. This approach ensures data integrity and provenance without duplicating large image files, while keeping interoperability with standard DICOM workflows intact.Workflow Integration When a new clinical or imaging record is processed:The record is validated against HL7 FHIR or DICOM schemas.Cryptographic hash and metadata are stored on the blockchain.The record is linked to the patient’s digital twin instance.Authorized users (clinicians, hospitals, agencies) retrieve or update the record through permissioned blockchain channels.

Conclusions

This research work describes a new COVID-DT model that combines blockchain, AI (BiLSTM), and digital twins to enable safe, decentralized, and predictive pandemic alerts. The proposed system offers various advantages, including data privacy, remote testing, automatic communication tracing, and continuous patient monitoring. The BiLSTM-based model provides better predictive performance and reduced latency than CNN and LSTM baselines, and could be used in practice during pandemic response. In addition to predictive performance, the framework also implements cybersecurity resilience, where blockchain consensus, immutability, and permission access control are deployed to guarantee tamper-proof and privacy-assuring data transfer. Scalability analysis shows that the design is not restricted to Raspberry Pi prototypes, but instead, many thousands of heterogeneous nodes and other consensus protocols can be supported in large deployments.

Notably, the system also supports compatibility with HL7 FHIR and DICOM standards, thereby enabling seamless integration with pre-existing healthcare IT systems and imaging processes. The COVID-DT approach promotes technological innovation, fairness, accountability, and patient trust by integrating ethical protection and governance frameworks. The analysis of the current literature proves the increase in the significance of blockchain-AI-DT convergence in healthcare. We develop our work based on these innovations and combine security, predictive intelligence, and digital twin simulation into one end-to-end system. However, there are still obstacles related to scalability, interoperability, and standardization, in addition to the need to ensure adherence to international privacy standards (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA).

Future research will focus on privacy-enhancing technologies, federated learning, and the creation of FHIR compliance APIs with ontology mapping to enhance interoperability in international healthcare systems. By following the research directions, the COVID-DT framework can become a practical tool to monitor a pandemic and other healthcare emergencies in the real world.

Ethical and governance considerations

Deployment of blockchain–AI–DT systems in healthcare requires careful attention to ethics and governance. First, data privacy and informed consent must be ensured, particularly as sensitive health records and imaging data are exchanged across institutions. Our proposed framework addresses this by using permissioned blockchain channels and role-based access, in line with global regulations such as GDPR and HIPAA.

Second, ethical use of AI is essential. Predictive models must be transparent and explainable to clinicians to avoid misinterpretation, and bias in training data should be continuously monitored to ensure fairness across diverse populations.

Third, governance mechanisms are needed to oversee network operation. A multi-stakeholder governance board (healthcare providers, government agencies, regulatory bodies) can define policies for data ownership, access control, and security audits. Smart contracts provide an immutable log of all transactions, strengthening accountability and compliance.

Ultimately, striking a balance between innovation and responsibility is crucial. While our COVID-DT framework offers strong technological capabilities for pandemic monitoring, it must operate under governance structures that safeguard patient trust, equity, and legal compliance.

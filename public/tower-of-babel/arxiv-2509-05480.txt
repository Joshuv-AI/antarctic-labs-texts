Rigidity of Kobayashi isometries of a class of 2-dimensional Lempert manifolds Thanks: Supported by the Preludium bis grant no. 2021/43/O/ST1/02111 of the National Science Centre, Poland.
Anand Chavan

Abstract
. In this article, we study the Kobayashi isometries of 2-dimensional complex manifolds having a finite Carathéodory universal set. In particular, we prove that the Kobayashi isometries of these complex manifolds are (anti)holomorphic.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and Results

In the theory of invariant distances and metrics it is an important problem to know, for which complex manifolds the isometry map with respect to their holomorphically invariant distances/metrics, is holomorphic. Trivial example of the unit disc shows that the isometry map from the unit disc to itself with respect to the (Poincaré)invariant metric is indeed (anti)holomorphic whereas on the bidisc we can find an isometry map from the bidisc to itself with respect to Kobayashi metric which is not (anti)holomorphic.

The problem of (anti)holomorphic rigidity of the isometry map between complex manifolds is widely studied. It was first considered in
[
11
]
for the product of
n
-unit Hilbert balls over the Carathéodory metric and subsequently in
[
18
,
19
]
for the Cartesian product of the unit Euclidean ball with respect to the Carathéodory metric or distance.

In the case of the Kobayashi metric in
[
6
]
, the (anti)holomorphicity of the Kobayashi isometry was proved between strongly convex bounded domains of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
in complex Euclidean space. Additionally, the following conjecture was formulated, which remains open to our knowledge (see also
[
16
,
12
,
9
,
13
]
). Although not explicitly formulated in the conjecture, one should consider
M,M^{\prime}
to be equidimensional.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be Kobayashi hyperbolic complex manifolds. Let
f:(M,K_{M})\to(M^{\prime},K_{M^{\prime}})
be a Kobayashi isometry. If
M
and
M^{\prime}
are not biholomorphic to a product of complex manifolds, then
f
is holomorphic or antiholomorphic.

The most comprehensive result concerning the Kobayashi metric in complex Euclidean bounded domains was established by Edigarian
[
4
]
, inspired by
[
2
]
, who proved that the Kobayashi isometry between bounded strictly convex domains are (anti)holomorphic. Recent works
[
3
,
5
]
, proved the (anti)holomorphicity of Kobayashi isometry for the domain diamond and the symmetrized bidisc. The domain diamond is not strictly convex, and the symmetrized bidisc is not biholomorphic to a convex domain.

1.2.
Lempert Theory, Carathéodory sets and Carathéodory universal sets.

For a necessary definition, see Section
2
. Lempert theory is an effective tool in understanding the isometry problem
[
4
,
3
,
5
]
. Lempert’s theorem

[
14
]
states that for a convex or strongly linearly convex domain the Carathéodory and Kobayashi distance or metric are equal, viz., there is a unique holomorphically invariant metric or distance on these domains with the distance/metric decreasing property.

In
[
10
]
while studying norm preserving extension problem on subsets of tridisc the authors considered Carathéodory sets
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
,

\mathcal{M_{\alpha}}:=\{(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in{\mathbb{D}}^{3}:\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+\alpha_{3}z_{3}=\overline{\alpha_{1}}z_{2}z_{3}+\overline{\alpha_{2}}z_{1}z_{3}+\overline{\alpha_{3}}z_{1}z_{2}\}

where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{3}

and discovered that the special domain
D_{a,b}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
(a domain in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
biholomorphic to
\mathcal{M}_{(a,b,1)}
, where
|a|,|b|,1
forms sides of triangle) though not linearly convex admits the equality of these two distances or metrics, adding
D_{a,b}
to the list of domains where the conclusion of Lempert theorem holds. Moreover, the Carathéodory universal set of
D_{a,b}
consists of 3 elements, namely
F_{1}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1}
,
F_{2}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{2}
and
F_{3}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{az_{1}+bz_{2}-z_{1}z_{2}}{\overline{b}z_{2}+\overline{a}z_{1}-1}
. The other known example of finite number of elements in Carathéodory universal set was for bidisc which has 2 elements, the two coordinate projection function.

In
[
1
]
, domains were characterized up to biholomorphism by the cardinality of their Carathéodory universal set. In dimension one, a Lempert domain with a Carathéodory universal set consisting of a single element is biholomorphic to the unit disc, and in this case the Kobayashi isometries are (anti)holomorphically rigid. In dimension two, a Lempert domain whose Carathéodory universal set consists of two elements is biholomorphic to the bidisc, which does not exhibit the (anti)holomorphic rigidity of Kobayashi isometries.

In this article, we prove that a two-dimensional Lempert manifold whose Carathéodory universal set is finite but contains more than two elements exhibits the (anti)holomorphic rigidity phenomenon, in sharp contrast to the bidisc. The following is the main theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
M,N
be 2-dimensional Lempert manifolds such that their Carathéodory universal set is finite but has at least three elements. Let
F:M\to N
be a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
-smooth Kobayashi isometry. Then
F
is (anti)holomorphic.

As a consequence, we have the (anti)holomorphic rigidity of Kobayashi isometry for the domain
D_{a,b}
whose Carathéodory universal set consists of 3 elements.

Corollary 3
.

Let
f:D_{a,b}\to D_{c,d}
be a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
-smooth Kobayashi isometry where
(a,b,1),(c,d,1)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
and satisfies the triangle inequality. Then
f
is (anti)holomorphic.

Above
D_{a,b}=\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{2}:|az_{1}+bz_{2}-z_{1}z_{2}|<|\overline{b}z_{1}+\overline{a}z_{2}-1|\}
. For
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
satisfying the triangle inequality, we mean that
|\alpha_{1}|,|\alpha_{2}|,|\alpha_{3}|
forms the sides of a triangle.

The plan of the paper is to introduce necessary notions in further sections and to develop the proof of this main theorem. The core idea of the proof is to show that the (real)Fréchet derivative of the isometry map at a point is (anti)
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear and further extends this (anti)
{\mathbb{C}}
-linearity for all points of the domain using Rado’s theorem argument due to Edigarian
[
4
]
.

Remark 4
.

Regarding Theorem 2, at present we know of only one nontrivial example of a two-dimensional manifold whose Carathéodory universal set consists of three elements. We expect, however, that further two-dimensional manifolds with a finite Carathéodory universal set containing more than two elements do exist, and in such cases Theorem 2 would apply in a much broader range of situations.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will introduce necessary definitions, give some important results that are true in much more generality, and develop necessary tools to prove the main results.

2.1.
Invariant distance, indicatrix, affine maps

We will recall the definition of the Kobayashi and Carathéodory invariant (pseudo)distance and metric.

Let
M
be a complex manifold, consider
z,w\in M
and
(p;X)\in M\times T_{p}M
.

The Carathéodory (pseudo)distance
is defined as follows

c_{M}(z,w):=\sup\{\rho(F(z),F(w)):F\in\mathcal{O}(M,\mathbb{D})\}

where
\rho
is the Poincaré distance on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
.

The Lempert function
is defined as

l_{M}(z,w):=\inf\{\rho(\sigma,\zeta):f(\sigma)=z\text{ and }f(\zeta)=w\text{ where }f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},M)\}.

The Kobayashi (pseudo)distance
k_{M}
is the largest (pseudo)distance not greater than the Lempert function. We have inequalities
c_{M}\leq k_{M}\leq l_{M}
.

The Kobayashi (pseudo)metric
is defined as

\varkappa_{M}(p;X):=\inf\{|\alpha|:\alpha f^{\prime}(0)=X\text{ for }f\in\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{D},M})\text{ and }f(0)=p\}.

In addition to the above, we define
the Carathoédory (pseudo)metric
as

\gamma_{M}(p;X):=\sup\{|F^{\prime}(p)X|:F\in\mathcal{O}(M,\mathbb{D})\text{ and }F(p)=0\}.

Recall that
\gamma_{M}\leq\varkappa_{M}
.

We call the complex manifold
M

Lempert manifold

[
14
]
if
M
is taut and the conclusion of the Lempert Theorem holds on
M
, i.e. if we have the equalities

l_{M}\equiv k_{M}\equiv c_{M},\;\varkappa_{M}\equiv\gamma_{M}.

Kobayashi
Indicatrix
of a manifold
M
at a point
p
is defined as:

I_{M}(p):=\{X\in T_{p}M:\varkappa_{D}(p;X)<1\}

It is important to note that for Lempert manifolds the Kobayashi indicatrix is a convex and balanced, moreover
\varkappa_{M}(p;X)
is a norm on
T_{p}M
for
p\in M

[
8
]
.

A
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
smooth map

where
M,N
are complex manifolds of dimension
m,n
respectively, is a
Kobayashi isometry
if

\varkappa_{M}(z;X)=\varkappa_{N}(F(z);F^{\prime}(z)X)

for all
z\in M
and
X\in T_{p}M
.

When we identify tangent space with complex Euclidean space of appropriate dimensions,
F^{\prime}(z)
denotes the real Fréchet derivative, that is,

is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear map,

An
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear isomorphism from

Lemma 5
.

If
A:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear map such that

then
A(z)=\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\omega z

Proof.

A

{\mathbb{R}}

. As it maps boundary to boundary, we have

∎

Consider a Lempert manifold

4.
Conclusion and Remarks

The holomorphic rigidity of Kobayashi isometry in the case of a special 2-dimensional Lempert manifold was shown to be true. However, the methods used here do not need to make the case for the holomorphic rigidity of Kobayashi isometry on general domains in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. The result in this article extended the phenomenon of rigidity previously known as observed in strictly convex domains
[
4
]
. The domain Diamond, Symmetrized bidisc,
D_{a,b}
are Lempert domains, and considering Conjecture 1 to be true at least in case of equidimensional domains, this poses a natural question on the holomorphic rigidity of Kobayashi isometry on Lempert domains which are not biholomorphic to product domains.

4.1.

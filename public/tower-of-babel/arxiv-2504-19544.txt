On Weight Enumeration and Structure Characterization of Polar Codes via Group Actions
Vlad-Florin Drăgoi ‡ , and Mohammad Rowshan †

Abstract
In this article, we provide a complete characterization of codewords in polar codes with weights less than twice the minimum distance, using the group action of the lower triangular affine (LTA) group. We derive a closed-form formula for the enumeration of such codewords. Furthermore, we introduce an enhanced partial order based on weight contributions, offering refined tools for code design. Our results extend previous work on Type II codewords to a full description of Type I codewords and offer new insights into the algebraic structure underlying decreasing monomial codes, including polar and Reed–Muller codes.

The information-theoretic approach used for constructing polar codes results in poor weight distribution. Weight distribution of decreasing monomial codes

Decreasing monomial codes – permutation groups

Decreasing monomial codes
[
2
]
are a large family of binary linear codes that includes well-known error-correcting codes, such as polar codes
[
10
]
and Reed–Muller codes. A decreasing monomial code is a linear code that admits as basis the evaluation of a set of monomials in
m
variables, say
\mathcal{I}
, on all the elements in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}.
These monomials satisfy an order relation
\preceq
, which translates in part the reliability relations between the
2^{m}
synthetic channels of polar codes. This algebraic description provided key insights into the structure of polar codes. Let us now recall key historical milestones relevant to our work. In
[
2
]
properties such as duality, permutation group, and minimum weight codewords were revealed. Shortened and punctured decreasing monomial codes were studied from a cryptographic point of view in
[
1
,
4

[
8
,
14
]
. In
[
14
]
the authors demonstrated that these are the only affine automorphisms of polar codes. In
[
9
]
it was shown that in the asymptotic regime the permutation group of polar codes converges to
{\rm LTA}(m,2).
However, decreasing monomial codes admit other permutations than affine transformations
[
17
]
. One of the most notable practical contributions of the permutation group was in parallelized decoding
[
7
,

,

]
.

Weight enumeration

Recently, a strong focus has been directed towards weight enumeration for polar codes. Although algorithmic methods are among the most widespread solutions
[
16
,
26
,
29
,
6
,
19
,
27
]
, they suffer from several drawbacks, the most significant being their high complexity (weight enumeration is possible only for codes with lengths up to
n=128
). That is why closed formulae were proposed. The formation of minimum weight codes in terms of rows of the generator matrix of polar codes was discussed in
[
21
]
. In
[
2
]
based on the action of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
a formula for the number of minimum weight codewords was proposed. This was further extended to
1.5\w_{\min}
-weight codewords in
[
5
]
. The main difficulty between
\w_{\min}
and the higher weights is in characterization of Minkowski sums of orbits. Indeed, a minimum weight codeword lies inside one orbit
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f
where
f
is a maximum degree monomial. However, higher weights are formed by summing several minimum weight codewords, making the characterization and counting more challenging. The result on
1.5\w_{\min}
was further extended to weight up to
2\w_{\min}
, for Type II codewords in
[
22
]
. In parallel, Type II and Type I codewords were enumerated in
[
28
]
, based on the extension of Kasami and Tokura’s theorem for Reed–Muller codes
[
12
]
. Such results are in line with older contributions on the special case of Reed-Muller codes
[
12
,
13
]
.

I-B

Contributions

In this study, we extend the findings presented in
[
5
]
and
[
22
]
to characterize codewords of Type I. We introduce a new subgroup of the
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
in order to characterize special cases of sums of orbits
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f+{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot g
where
f,g
are maximum degree monomials with
\deg(\gcd(f,g))>r-\mu.
Since our formulae are based on the action of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
they are different from
[
28
]
. We provide numerical examples that align with and go beyond known examples, such as polar, Reed–Muller and RMxPolar codes. In addition, we provide arguments on how to use our characterization to improve recent partial orders based on weight contribution
[
22
]
. Let us detail our contributions.

Starting point

To completely characterize Type I codewords we build upon a recent perspective codewords in terms of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
action. Given a decreasing monomial code
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
with
r=\max_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f)
, we start from
[
22
]
(Theorem 2) which states that any such codeword
\operatorname{ev}(P)
(
P
being a polynomial in the span of the monomial basis
P\in\mathrm{span}(I)
) is of the form
P=y_{1}\dots y_{r-\mu}(y_{r-\mu+1}\dots y_{r}+y_{r+1}\dots y_{r+\mu})
with
P\in{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f+{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot g
with
f,g\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
having
\deg(f)=\deg(g)=r.
and
y_{i}
being independent linear forms. The weight of
\operatorname{ev}(P)
equals
\w_{\mu}=2^{m+1-r}-2^{m+1-r-\mu}
and the parameter
\mu
satisfies
m\geq r+\mu,r\geq\mu\geq 3.

A crucial tool in our proofs is the independence condition for linear forms. Already mentioned in
[
5
,
22
]
, it states that any finite product of linear independent forms
l_{i}
can be rewritten such that maximum variables in the new linear forms
l_{i}^{*}
are distinct. A direct consequence of this property is that any Type I codeword should have at least
r+\mu
distinct variables in
y_{1}\dots,y_{r+\mu}.
This observation plays a central role throughout our work.

Another useful tool is the number of free variables taken into consideration when applying
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
on a monomial
f=x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{r}}
with
i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}.
More precisely, we will use the fact that the number of free variables taken together with
x_{i}\in\operatorname{ind}(f)
is
\lambda_{f}(x_{i})=|J_{f}(i)|,
where
J_{f}(i)=\{j\in[0,i)\mid j\notin\operatorname{ind}(f)\}.
This was previously used in
[
2
]
for enumerating minimum weight codewords, where
\lambda_{f}=(i_{r}-(r-1),\dots,i_{1}-0)
and
|\lambda_{f}(f)|=\sum_{i\in\operatorname{ind}(f)}\lambda_{f}(x_{i})
, (or simply
\lambda_{f}
) was defined. We shall employ these objects several times in our paper.

f,g
that define the orbits for
P={\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f+{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot g.
We will denote by
\mathcal{I}_{r}
the set of monomials in
\mathcal{I}
with degree
r.
We demonstrate that a pair
(f,g)
can define a valid Type I generating codewords if the following conditions are met

•

both have maximum degree, i.e.,
\deg(f)=\deg(g)=r

•

they share at least

h=\gcd(f,g)

\deg(h)\geq r-\mu.

(f,g)
is a valid pair

if
f

Then, we need to check whether there are

x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{l}}
such that a shift on each variable
x_{i_{1}-\epsilon_{1}}\dots x_{i_{l}-\epsilon_{l}}

if

f

h

Also we have degree

Type I codewords - Minkowski sums of orbits

Characterizing the Minkowski sum of orbits
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f+{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot g
was a complex and challenging task for all three cases. In
[
5
]
the authors introduced the notion of collisions in order to determine the cardinality of the Minkowski sum. The difficulty comes from the fact that all the structure that
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f
inherits from the group properties cannot be transferred to the Minkowski sum. Therefore, each case had to be considered apart. Another difficulty consists in breaking down the elements inside a Minkowski sum into clusters with respect to the Hamming weight weight. Take for example
f=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3},g=x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}
provided in Figure
1
. We see that in
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f+{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot g
we have at least two different weights, i.e.,
1.5\w_{\min}
for example the evaluation of
x_{1}(x_{2}x_{3}+x_{4}x_{5})
, and
1.75\w_{\min}
for example the evaluation of
x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}+(x_{1}+x_{0})x_{4}x_{5}
. And there is also,
2\w_{\min}
, the evaluation of
x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}+(x_{1}+1)x_{2}x_{3}.

For
Case A1.
we showed that there are no collisions and thus, the cardinality of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot h\left({\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot f/h+{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{g}\cdot g/h\right)
is the product of the cardinality of each orbit. We have built our proof on a more general properties. One of these properties state that if the degree of
\gcd(f,g)
is strictly smaller than
r-2
then the cardinality of the Minkwoski sum is the sum of the cardinality of the two orbits.

Regarding
Case A2.
the situation becomes more complex. Suppose we have
f,g\in\mathcal{I}_{r}
with
h\in\mathcal{I}_{r-\mu}
such that
\gcd(f,g)=hh^{*}
with
\deg(h^{*})\geq 1.
The
\w_{\mu}
-weight codewords are evaluation of polynomials that are composed of
r+\mu
independent linear forms lying inside the orbit
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\left({\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}+{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{g}\cdot\frac{g}{h}\right).
While
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\times{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}
is a product of
r
independent linear forms, the remaining part might not provide the extra
\mu
independent linear forms, due to the existence of
h^{*}
on both
f
and
g.
Hence, the group action on
h^{*}
should be restricted to only those matrices
\mathbf{B}
that produce independent linear forms with the preexisting forms coming from the action on
f.
Here we introduce a new sub-group
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}^{g}
that acts on
f/h
group that is formed by the matrices with the aforementioned condition. In other words, if on
g
we allow the complete group action on the
h^{*}
, then on
f
we need to split the action into two distinct parts, a free part on
\frac{f}{\gcd(f,g)}
and a restricted part on
h^{*}.

We also determine a closed formula for the cardinality of this new orbit. We do connect our formula with the preexisting Young diagrams for counting the elements in
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f
(see
[
2
,
3
]
). With this new group action the cardinality of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot h\left({\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot f/h+{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{g}\cdot g/h\right)
equals
\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\right|\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}\right|\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{g}^{{f}{}}\cdot\frac{g}{h}\right|.

The
Case B1.
is similar to the previous case. However, since
f\notin\mathcal{I}
we need to take into account the monomials od degree
r
produced by
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f
that are not in
\mathcal{I}.
Hence, we introduce a new subgroup of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
, denoted
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}^{f,\mathcal{I}}
that formally describes all the independent variables one can consider in each
x_{i}
from
f
, such that all the monomials in
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}^{f,\mathcal{I}}\cdot f/h+{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot f/h
belong to
\mathcal{I}.
The set
J_{f}^{\mathcal{I}}(i)=\{j\in[0,i-1)\mid j\not\in\operatorname{ind}(f),x_{j}f/x_{i}\in\mathcal{I}\}
will be required for this new group action. We further provide a closed formula for the cardinality of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}^{f,\mathcal{I}}.

II-A

Basic Concepts in Coding Theory and Notations

We denote by
\mathbb{F}_{2}
the finite field with two elements and by
+
the addition operator in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. The symmetric group of order
N
is denoted by
\mathrm{S}_{N}.

Also, subsets of consecutive integers are denoted by
[\ell,u]\triangleq\{\ell,\ell+1,\ldots,u\}
.
The binary expansion of an integer
i
is denoted by
\bin(i)=(i_{0},i_{1},\dots,i_{m-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, where
i_{m-1}
is the most significant bit, i.e.,
i=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}i_{j}2^{j}.
We will use an order relation on the set
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
by considering the decreasing index order on
[0,2^{m}-1]
. For example the elements in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}
will be ordered as follows
\{(1,1),(0,1),(1,0),(0,0)\}
and it corresponds to
\{3,2,1,0\}.

The
support
of a vector
\bm{c}=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{N-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
is defined by
\supp(\bm{c})\triangleq\{i\in[0,N-1]\mid c_{i}\neq 0\}
.
The cardinality of a set is denoted by
|\cdot|.
and the set difference by
\backslash
. The complement of a support is
\supp(\bm{c})^{c}=[0,N-1]\setminus\supp(\bm{c}).
The Hamming
weight
of
\bm{c}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
is
\w(\bm{c})\triangleq|\supp(\bm{c})|
.
Given two vectors
\bm{c}=(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{N-1})
and
\bm{c}^{\prime}=(c^{\prime}_{0},c^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,c^{\prime}_{N-1})
,
the Hamming
distance
between
\bm{c}
and
\bm{c}^{\prime}
is defined by
\dist(\bm{c},\bm{c}^{\prime})=|\{i\in[0,N-1]\mid c_{i}\neq c^{\prime}_{i}\}|
.

A
K
-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
is called a linear
(N,K)

code
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
(
N
is the length,
K
the dimension and
R\triangleq K/N
is the code rate).
\mathcal{C}
is said to be a linear
(N,K,d)
code if
\mathcal{C}
is a linear
(N,K)
code and
its minimum distance,

d_{\mathrm{min}}=\dist(\mathcal{C})\triangleq\min_{\bm{c},\bm{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C},\bm{c}\neq\bm{c}^{\prime}}\dist(\bm{c},\bm{c}^{\prime})

is
d.
The Hamming weight induces the Hamming distance and vice-versa
[
15
, Section 3.3]
, hence

\w_{\min}\triangleq\min_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{C},\bm{c}\neq 0}\w(\bm{c})=\dist(\mathcal{C}).

The vectors in
\mathcal{C}
are called
codewords
and can be collected with respect to their weight
\w
in
W_{\w}(\mathcal{C})=\{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{C}\mid\w(\bm{c})=\w\}.

Moreover, for any
(N,K)
one can define the
weight enumerator polynomial

W(\mathcal{C};x)=\sum_{\w=0}^{N}|W_{\w}(\mathcal{C})|x^{\w}.

A
generator matrix

\bm{G}
of an
(N,K)
code
\mathcal{C}
is a
K\times N
matrix in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{K\times N}
whose rows are
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-linearly independent codewords of
\mathcal{C}
. Then
\mathcal{C}=\{\bm{v}\bm{G}\colon\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{K}\}
.

II-B

Multivariate monomials and polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{2}

Let
m
be a fixed integer, which will represents the number of different variables
\mathbf{x}\triangleq(x_{0},\dots,x_{m-1})
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{0},\dots{},x_{m-1}]
be the set of polynomials in
m
variables. Since we are dealing with binary codes, we will identify
x_{i}
with
x_{i}^{2}
(using the Frobenius endomorphism) and consider the ring
{\mathbf{R}}_{m}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{0},\dots{},x_{m-1}]/(x_{0}^{2}-x_{0},\dots,x_{m-1}^{2}-x_{m-1})
.

A monomial in
{\mathbf{R}}_{m}
can be defined as

\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{i}}=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}x_{j}^{i_{j}}=x_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots x_{m-1}^{i_{m-1}},

where
\mathbf{i}=\bin(i)=(i_{0},\dots,i_{m-1})
with
i_{j}\in\{0,1\}.
Let

\mathcal{M}_{m}\triangleq\left\{\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{i}}\mid\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\right\}
denote the set of all monomials in
{\mathbf{R}}_{m}.

We have thus defined a one-to-one mapping between any integer
i\in[0,2^{m}-1]
and a monomial
\mathbf{x}^{{\bin(i)}}.

Since for
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
we have used the decreasing index order we shall still with this convention for monomials as well

\begin{array}[h]{ccccc}[0,2^{m}-1]&\to&\mathcal{M}_{m}\\
i&\mapsto&\mathbf{x}^{{\bin(2^{m}-1-i)}}\end{array}

For example, for
m=2
we have
\mathcal{M}_{m}=\{x_{0}x_{1},x_{1},x_{0},\bm{1}\}.

Further we will define the
support of monomial

f=x_{l_{1}}\dots x_{l_{s}}
by
\operatorname{ind}(f)=\{l_{1},\dots,l_{s}\}
. The
multiplicative complement
of a monomial
f
is
\check{f}=\frac{x_{0}\dots x_{m-1}}{f}.
In other words, we have
\operatorname{ind}(f)\cup\operatorname{ind}(\check{f})=[0,m-1]
and
\operatorname{ind}(f)\cap\operatorname{ind}(\check{f})=\emptyset.
Also, the degree of a monomial is
\deg(f)=|\operatorname{ind}(f)|.
The degree induces a ranking on any monomial set
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{m}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{I}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{m}\mathcal{I}_{j}
, where
\mathcal{I}_{j}=\{f\in\mathcal{I}\mid\deg(f)=j\}.
We denote the indicator function
\mathbb{I}_{f=g}
, which equals
0
whenever
f\neq g
and
1
for
f=g.

II-C

Decreasing Monomial Codes

Next we will define the evaluation function that associates to a polynomial
g\in{\mathbf{R}}_{m}
the binary vector denoted by

\operatorname{ev}(g)
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2^{m}}.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
ordered w.r.t. the decreasing index order. For
g\in{\mathbf{R}}_{m}
define the evaluation function

\begin{array}[h]{ccccc}{\mathbf{R}}_{m}&\to&\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2^{m}}\\
g&\mapsto&\operatorname{ev}(g)=\big(g(\bm{i})\big)_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}\end{array}

The function
\operatorname{ev}(\cdot)
defines a vector space isomorphism between the vector space
({\mathbf{R}}_{m},+,\cdot)
and
(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2^{m}},+,\cdot).
We can now define monomial/polynomial codes.

Definition 2

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq{\mathbf{R}}_{m}
be a finite set of polynomials in
m
variables.
The linear code defined by
\mathcal{I}
is the vector subspace
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2^{m}}

generated by
\{\operatorname{ev}(f)\penalty\ |\penalty\ f\in\mathcal{I}\}
.

•

When
\mathcal{I}\subseteq{\mathbf{R}}_{m}
we say that
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
is a
polynomial code
.

•

When
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{m}
we say that
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
is a
monomial code
.

Now let us see how to construct monomial codes. For that we shall define the Kronecker product matrix

\bm{G}_{2^{m}}\triangleq\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\otimes\cdots\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}}_{m\;\text{ times}}.

In
[
3
]
it was demonstrated that
\bm{G}_{2^{m}}
is the monomial evaluation basis for the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2^{m}}
, fact that relies on the following diagram

\begin{array}[h]{ccccc}[0,2^{m}-1]&\to&\mathcal{M}_{m}&\to&\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2^{m}}\\
i&\mapsto&g\triangleq\mathbf{x}^{{\bin(2^{m}-1-i)}}&\mapsto&\bm{G}_{2^{m}}[i]=\operatorname{ev}(g)\end{array}

We shall further continue with the definition of Decreasing monomial codes. Let “
|
” denote divisibility between monomials, i.e.,
f|g
iff
\operatorname{ind}(f)\subseteq\operatorname{ind}(g).
Also, the greatest common divisor of two monomials is
\gcd(f,g)=h
with
\operatorname{ind}(h)=\operatorname{ind}(f)\cap\operatorname{ind}(g).

Definition 3

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
m
be a positive integer and
f,g\in\mathcal{M}_{m}.
Then
f\preceq_{w}g
if and only if
f|g.
When
\deg(f)=\deg(g)=s
we say that
f\preceq_{sh}g
if
\forall\;1\leq\ell\leq s\;\text{ we have }\;i_{\ell}\leq j_{\ell}
, where
f=x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{s}}
,
g=x_{j_{1}}\dots x_{j_{s}}
.
Define
f\preceq g\quad\text{iff}\quad\exists g^{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{m}\;\text{s.t.}\;f\preceq_{sh}g^{*}\preceq_{w}g
.

Here we will need an order relation stricter than
\preceq_{sh}.

Definition 4
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
with
f=x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{s}}
,
g=x_{j_{1}}\dots x_{j_{s}}.
We define

f\prec_{sh}g\Leftrightarrow\forall\;1\leq\ell\leq s\;\text{ we have }\;i_{\ell}<j_{\ell}.

Notice that
f\prec_{sh}g\Rightarrow f\preceq_{sh}g
, while the converse is not valid. For example
x_{0}x_{2}\preceq_{sh}x_{0}x_{3}
but
x_{0}x_{2}\not\prec_{sh}x_{0}x_{3}.
There are particular cases when the converse is also true.

Lemma 1
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{M}_{m}.
The following holds

•

if
\gcd(f,g)=1
then
f\preceq_{sh}g\Leftrightarrow f\prec_{sh}g.

•

if
h=\gcd(f,g)\neq 1
then
f\preceq_{sh}g\Leftrightarrow f/h\prec_{sh}g/h.

The proof of this lemma is rather straightforward from the definition of
\prec_{sh}
and
\preceq_{sh}.

Notice that we have
x_{0}\prec_{sh}x_{1}\prec_{sh}\dots\prec_{sh}x_{m-1}.
Understanding decreasing sets is crucial since they determine the structure of decreasing monomial codes. We can now recall the definition of monomial decreasing sets.

Definition 5

(
[
3
,
2
]
)
.

A set
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{m}
is
decreasing
if and only if (
f\in\mathcal{I}
and
g\preceq f
) implies
g\in\mathcal{I}
.

A decreasing closed interval with respect to
\preceq
is
[f,g]_{\preceq}=\{h\in\mathcal{M}_{m}\mid f\preceq h\preceq g\}.

Any monomial code
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
with
\mathcal{I}
decreasing is called decreasing monomial code. Both Polar and Reed-Muller codes are decreasing monomial codes
[
2
]
. For
\mathcal{R}(r,m)
we have

\mathcal{R}(r,m)=\mathcal{C}\left([1,x_{m-r}\cdots{}x_{m-1}]_{\preceq}\right).

(1)

II-D

Permutation group of Decreasing Monomial Codes

The set of applications that leave a code
\mathcal{C}
globally invariant, forms the
automorphism group
of the code
\mathcal{C}
, which is denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{C})
. In the case of binary codes
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathrm{S}_{N}
, thus being all permutations.

Decreasing monomial codes admit as subgroup of permutations the lower triangular affine group,
{\rm LTA}(m,2).
An affine transformation over
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
is represented by a pair
(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})
where
\mathbf{B}=(b_{i,j})
is an invertible matrix lying in the general linear group
{\rm GL}(m,2)
and
\bm{\varepsilon}
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. Lower triangular affine transformation are defined by lower triangular binary matrices
\mathbf{B}\in{\rm GL}(m,2)
with
b_{i,i}=1
and
b_{i,j}=0
whenever
j>i
. More on this group action on decreasing monomial codes can be found in
[
3
,
5
]
.

Let us recall how
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
acts on a monomial, say
g=\prod_{i\in\operatorname{ind}(g)}x_{i}.
This action is denoted by
(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})\cdot g
and it replaces each variable
x_{i}
of
g
by a ”new”variable
y_{i}

y_{i}=x_{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}b_{i,j}x_{j}+\varepsilon_{i}.

Note that
y_{i}
is a
linear form
, i.e., a polynomial of degree 1. Also, the maximum variable of this linear form is
x_{i}
, as others are smaller than
x_{i}
w.r.t. the order relation
\preceq.

Moving forward, we can recall the notion of orbits.

Definition 6

(
[
2
,
3
]
)
.

The orbit of a monomial
f
under the action of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
is defined as the set of polynomials

{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f=\{(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})\cdot f\mid(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})\in{\rm LTA}(m,2)\}.

Since
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
acts as a permutation on
\operatorname{ev}(f)
, all the elements in
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f
have the same Hamming weight.

II-E

Minimum Weight Codewords

In
[
2
]
the authors demonstrated that any decreasing monomial code
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
with
r=\max_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f)
has minimum distance
\w_{\min}\triangleq 2^{m-r}
and any minimum weight codeword of
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
lie in one of the orbits
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f
where
f\in\mathcal{I}_{r}.
The main challenge was to determine the stabilizer subgroup for each coset leader. Then, a particular subgroup of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
came into play, subgroup that will be useful for our case as well.

Definition 7

(
[
2
,
3
]
)
.

For any
g\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
define
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{g}
as the subgroup
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
where

\varepsilon_{i}=0\text{ if }i\in\operatorname{ind}(\check{g})\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ b_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0\text{ if }i\in\operatorname{ind}(\check{g})\\
0\text{ if }j\in\operatorname{ind}(g).\end{array}\right.

This subgroup spans the whole orbit as provided by the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(
[
2
,
3
]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}_{m}.
Then we have

{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f={\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot f.

(2)

Moreover, there are no polynomials in
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot f
that are fixed by more than one group element (the identity).

Estimating the size of an orbit boils down to counting the number of
\varepsilon_{i}
(translations) and
b_{i,j}
(linear mapping) from
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}.
For translations we have
2^{\deg(f)}
choices, while for linear mapping we have
2^{|\lambda_{f}|}
choices
[
2
]
. To be more precise, for
f=x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{r}}
with
i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}
the number of free variables taken together with
x_{i}\in\operatorname{ind}(f)
is
\lambda_{f}(x_{i})=|J_{f}(i)|,
where
J_{f}(i)=\{j\in[0,i)\mid j\notin\operatorname{ind}(f)\}.
One can associate a partition to
f
, of length
\deg(f)
defined by
\lambda_{f}=(i_{r}-(r-1),\dots,i_{1}-0).
Hence, the number of free variables on all
x_{i}
in the set
\operatorname{ind}(f)
equals
|\lambda_{f}(f)|=\sum_{i\in\operatorname{ind}(f)}\lambda_{f}(x_{i})
, (or simply
\lambda_{f}
).

In
[
5
]
the definition of
\lambda_{f}
was extended to any monomial
g=x_{j_{0}}\dots x_{j_{l-1}}
satisfying
g|f
, i.e.,
\lambda_{f}(g)
is the partition of length
l
defined by
\lambda_{f}(g)=(\lambda_{f}(x_{i}))_{i\in\operatorname{ind}(g)}
, which yields
|\lambda_{f}(g)|=\sum_{i\in\operatorname{ind}(g)}\lambda_{f}(x_{i}).
In other words
\lambda_{f}(g)
is the restriction of
\lambda_{f}(f)
to the indices from
\operatorname{ind}(g).

Finally, we get the well known formula from
[
2
]

\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot f\right|=2^{\deg(f)+|\lambda_{f}|}.

(3)

The collection of all orbits
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot f
for
f\in\mathcal{I}_{r}
gives the complete characterization of minimum weight codewords of any decreasing monomial code
[
2
]
.

In our article, any code
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
will implicitly be decreasing monomial, and the parameters
r
always denote the maximum degree of monomials in
\mathcal{I}
, i.e.,
r=\max_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f).

and review existing results on higher weights codewords for decreasing monomial codes.

IV
Characterization of Type I codewords

We shall proceed step-by-step and firstly, have a deeper look at the existence conditions for Type I codewords. Our next result, details one of the implications, namely, if such a codeword exists then it has to belong to a particular Minkowski sum of orbits, that we characterize. This first part deals with the conditions on the monomials defining the orbits.

IV-A

Conditions on monomials

Before formally stating our result, we first explain the key conditions. The whole idea behind it is that we require
r+\mu
independent linear forms which means that we need at least
r+\mu
distinct variables to obtain independence. Also, there are
r-\mu
common factors between the two terms
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f
and
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot g.
When
f
and
g
share
r-\mu
common variable (
h=\gcd(f,g)
) things are rather straightforward, the remaining parts
f/h,g/h
are co-prime and thus
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
acts trivially on both parts.
If
f,g
share more that
r-\mu
common variables then we need to check which of those common variables are big enough to fetch using the action of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
enough variables from
\widecheck{fg}.
In other words, we can set a product of
r-\mu
variables in
\gcd(f,g)
(denoted
h
), and verify whether we can shift backwards the variables in
h^{*}=\gcd(f,g)/h
(subtract a strictly positive integer from each index of the variables in
h
). If the shifted monomial divides
\widecheck{fg}
, in other words these variables are free to be considered in
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
, then such combination is valid.

The last case to consider is when
f\notin\mathcal{I}.
Then, the two monomials should be equal and we should eliminate all monomials smaller than
f
that are not in
\mathcal{I}
using the action of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
on
f.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
be a decreasing monomial code and
r=\max_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f)
. Then any codeword
\operatorname{ev}(P)
of Type I satisfying
\w(\operatorname{ev}(P))=\w_{\mu}
with
m-r\geq\mu,r\geq\mu\geq 3
is such that

•

A.

\exists f,g\in\mathcal{I}_{r},h\in\mathcal{I}_{r-\mu}
and
h\preceq_{w}\gcd(f,g)
with

P\in{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\cdot\left({\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}+{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{g}\cdot\frac{g}{h}\right),

where

–

A.1
either
h=\gcd(f,g)

–

A.2
or
hh^{*}=\gcd(f,g),h^{*}\neq 1
, and
\exists h_{s}^{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
satisfying
h_{s}^{*}\preceq_{w}\widecheck{fg}
and
h_{s}^{*}\prec_{sh}h^{*}
,

•

B.1

\exists f\not\in\mathcal{I}_{r}
,
\deg(f)=r,h\in\mathcal{I}_{r-\mu},h\preceq_{w}f
with

P\in{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\cdot\left({\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}+{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}\right)

and the following conditions are satisfied

IV-B

Orbit characterization

In order to characterize the Minkowski sum or product of two orbits we need to define the concept of collisions.

Definition 8
.

Collision: Let
P,P^{*}\in{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f
and
Q,Q^{*}\in{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot g.
we say that

•

(P,Q),(P^{*},Q^{*})
produces a collision for addition if
P+Q=P^{*}+Q^{*}
with
P\neq P^{*},Q\neq Q^{*}.

•

(P,Q),(P^{*},Q^{*})
produces a collision for multiplication if
PQ=PQ^{*}
or
PQ=P^{*}Q^{*}
or
PQ=P^{*}Q
with
P\neq P^{*},Q\neq Q^{*}.

Example 3
.

Let
f=x_{0}x_{1}
and
g=x_{0}x_{2}.
A collision for addition is
x_{0}x_{1}+x_{0}x_{2}=x_{0}(x_{1}+1)+x_{0}(x_{2}+1).
A collision for multiplication is
\left((x_{0}+1)x_{1}\right)\left(x_{0}(x_{2}+1)\right)=\left((x_{0}+1)(x_{1}+1)\right)\left(x_{0}x_{2}\right).

The sub-case
A.2

We shall informally explain what are the ingredients for this case. Suppose we have two monomials
f.g\in\mathcal{I}_{r}
with
h\in\mathcal{I}_{r-\mu}
such that
\gcd(f,g)=hh^{*}
with
\deg(h^{*})\geq 1.
The
\w_{\mu}
-weight codewords are evaluation of polynomials that are composed of
r+\mu
independent linear forms lying inside the orbit
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\left({\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}+{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{g}\cdot\frac{g}{h}\right).
This means that we have to characterize exactly those polynomials that satisfying
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\times{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}\times{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{g}\cdot\frac{g}{h}
equals a product of
r+\mu
independent linear forms. While
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\times{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}
is a product of
r
independent linear forms, the remaining part might not provide the extra
\mu
independent linear forms, due to the existence of
h^{*}
on both
f
and
g.
Hence, the group action on
h^{*}
should be restricted to only those matrices
\mathbf{B}
that produce independent linear forms with the preexisting forms coming from the action on
f.
In other words, if on
f
we allow the complete group action on the
h^{*}
, then on
g
we need to split the action into two distinct parts, a free part on
\frac{g}{\gcd(f,g)}
and a restricted part on
h^{*}.

Before we continue let us recall that for each variable
i\in\operatorname{ind}(f)
the number of free variables from the group action is
J_{f}(i)=\{j\in[0,i)\mid j\notin\operatorname{ind}(f)\}.
We thus have
(|J_{f}({i_{l}})|,\dots,|J_{f}({i_{1}})|)=\lambda_{f}(h)
for any divisor
h\preceq_{w}f
with
\operatorname{ind}(h)=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{l}\}.
Also, one can easily observe that the inclusion
J_{f}(i_{1})\subseteq\dots J_{f}(i_{l})
holds.

Definition 9
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{M}_{m},h\preceq_{w}f
and
h^{*}=\gcd(f/h,g)
with
\deg(h^{*})=l
and
\operatorname{ind}(h^{*})=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{l}\}.
Let
(\mathbf{B},\varepsilon)\in{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}
be such that
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{B}_{\operatorname{ind}(h^{*}),J_{fg}(i_{l})})=\deg(h^{*})
. The orbit
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}^{g}\cdot\frac{f}{h}
is the collection of all polynomials of the form
(\mathbf{B},\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{f}{h}
where
\mathbf{B}
satisfies the rank restrictions.

This new group action can be explained as follows. Split the orbit
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}
into two separate actions, i.e.,
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{hh^{*}}
and
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot h^{*}.
This is always possible since the two monomials on which we act
\frac{f}{hh^{*}},h^{*}
do not share any common variables. Because
h^{*}
is the common factor with
g
simply add a new restriction on this part of the group action.

The orbit is well-defined even for the case
h=1.

For the trivial case when
(f,g)
or
(\frac{f}{h},g)
are co-prime we have
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}^{g}\cdot\frac{f}{h}={\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\frac{f}{h}.

Let us give a formula for the cardinality of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}^{g}\cdot f.

Proposition 2
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{M}_{m},h\preceq_{w}f
and
h^{*}=\gcd(f/h,g)
with
\operatorname{ind}(h^{*})=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{l}\}.
Then as long as
\left|J_{fg}(i_{j})\right|>j-1
for
j\in\{1,\dots,l\}
we have

\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}^{g}\cdot\frac{f}{h}\right|=\frac{2^{\deg(\frac{f}{h})+|\lambda_{f}\frac{f}{h}|}}{2^{|\lambda_{fg}h^{*}|}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\deg(h^{*})}\left(2^{|J_{fg}({i_{j}})|}-2^{j-1}\right).

(6)

Since we can split
|\lambda_{f}\frac{f}{h}|=|\lambda_{f}\frac{f}{hh^{*}}||\lambda_{f}h^{*}|
we deduce that the first factor in (
6
) can be rewritten as

2^{\deg(\frac{f}{h})+|\lambda_{f}\frac{f}{hh^{*}}|+|\lambda_{f}h^{*}|-|\lambda_{fg}h^{*}|}.
Notice that

|\lambda_{f}h^{*}|-|\lambda_{fg}h^{*}|=|\lambda_{\widecheck{\frac{g}{\gcd{(f,g)}}}}h^{*}|.

Remark 3
.

Notice that for any
h^{*}\neq 1
we have

\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\deg(h^{*})}\left(2^{|J_{fg}({i_{j}})|}-2^{j-1}\right)}{2^{|\lambda_{fg}h^{*}}|}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\deg(h^{*})}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{|J_{fg}({i_{j}})|-(j-1)}}\right)<1.

Thus, naturally we get

\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}^{g}\cdot\frac{f}{h}\right|\leq 2^{\deg(f/h)+|\lambda_{f}\frac{f}{h}|}=\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}\right|,

with equality when
\gcd(f/h,g)=1.

Remark 4
.

When the group
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}
acts on
f
itself instead of

\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}^{g}\cdot{f}\right|=\frac{2^{\deg({f})+|\lambda_{f}{f}|}}{2^{|\lambda_{fg}\gcd{(f,g)}|}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\deg(\gcd{(f,g)})}\left(2^{|J_{fg}({i_{j}})|}-2^{j-1}\right)

(7)

Also, if
g=f

\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}^{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}\right|=2^{\deg(\frac{f}{h})}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\deg(\frac{f}{h})}\left(2^{|J_{f}({i_{j}})|}-2^{j-1}\right)

with the particular case

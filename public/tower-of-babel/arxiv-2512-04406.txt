A Dual Riemannian ADMM Algorithm for Low-Rank SDPs with Unit Diagonal Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Jie Wang, Liangbing Hu, Bican Xia

Abstract
This paper proposes a dual Riemannian alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) for solving low-rank semidefinite programs with unit diagonal constraints. We recast the ADMM subproblem as a Riemannian optimization problem over the oblique manifold by performing the Burer-Monteiro factorization. Global convergence of the algorithm is established assuming that the subproblem is solved to certain optimality. Numerical experiments demonstrate the excellent performance of the algorithm. It outperforms, by a significant margin, a few advanced SDP solvers ( MOSEK , COPT , SDPNAL+ , ManiSDP ) in terms of accuracy, efficiency, and scalability on second-order SDP relaxations of dense and sparse binary quadratic programs.

Introduction

In this paper, we aim to solve the following semidefinite program (SDP) with unit diagonal constraints:

\begin{cases}\inf\limits_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}&b^{\intercal}y\\
\,\,\,\rm{s.t.}&S={\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y)-C\succeq 0,\\
&\diag(S)=1,\end{cases}

(DSDP)

where
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
is the adjoint operator of a linear operator
{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathbb{S}}_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
(
{\mathbb{S}}_{n}
denotes the set of
n\times n
symmetric matrices).
We make the following assumptions on (
DSDP
):

Assumption 1
.

(
DSDP
) admits a low-rank optimal solution
S^{\star}
.

Assumption 2
.

The linear operator
{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
is invertible.

(
DSDP
) arises as relaxations of a wide range of real or complex or noncommutative polynomial optimization problems, including binary quadratic programs (BQP)
[
32
]
, the generalized orthogonal Procrustes problem
[
20
]
, group synchronization
[
19
,
21
,
23
]
, phase recovery
[
31
]
, multiple-input multiple-output detection
[
22
]
, designing unimodular codes
[
27
]
, inference in graphical models
[
10
]
, synchronization and community detection problems
[

]
, the classical Ising problem
[
9
]
, and the ground-state problem of quantum spin systems
[
33
]
.

Despite being convex, solving general SDPs to high accuracy is a challenging problem and nowadays is still an active research area.
For small/medium-scale SDPs, interior-point methods are usually accurate, efficient, and robust
[
3
,
30
]
. Nevertheless, interior-point methods are not applicable to large-scale SDPs due to their huge memory occupation. First-order methods, e.g., alternating direction methods of multipliers (ADMM)
[
11
,
35
,
39
]
, are more memory-friendly, but converge much slowly and typically only return a solution of low or medium accuracy. There are also first- and second-order hybrid algorithms based on augmented Lagrangian methods (ALM) that achieve a better balance between accuracy and scalability
[
37
,
38
]
. On the other hand, SDPs from practice frequently possess certain structures, e.g., low-rank optimal solutions, data sparsity, and unit diagonal constraints. Those structures could be exploited to design more efficient and scalable algorithms
[
8
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
18
,
24
,
29
,
34
,
36
]

The structure of low-rank optimal solutions is usually exploited via the Burer-Monteiro factorization
[
6
,
7
]
. A series of works have extended the approach to SDPs with distinguished constraints that define manifolds
[
16
,
32
,
34
]
.
Particularly in
[
32
]
, the first two authors proposed a Riemannian ALM algorithm for solving low-rank SDPs in the primal form which has superior performance on a variety of non-degenerate and degenerate SDPs with low-rank optimal solutions, including those from the moment-sum-of-squares (SOS) hierarchy of polynomial optimization problems. This work follows the research stream along
[
16
,
32
,
34
]
and
presents an efficient algorithm for solving (
DSDP
) in the dual form that can be viewed as a dual version of the algorithm given in
[
32
]
. The motivation to consider the dual version
stems from the fact that in many cases the rank of the linear operator
{\mathcal{A}}
is much smaller when taking the dual SDP rather than the primal SDP. One important such case comes from the moment-SOS hierarchy of polynomial optimization problems: the SOS problem typically has significantly less linear constraints than the moment problem when relaxation orders are greater than one. Our contributions are summarized as follows.

\bullet
We propose a dual Riemannian ADMM algorithm for solving low-rank SDPs with unit diagonal constraints. First, we apply the ADMM framework to (
DSDP
). Then, to exploit the low-rank property, we perform the Burer-Monteiro factorization
S=YY^{\intercal}
(
Y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times p},p\ll n
) to the ADMM subproblem. Due to the presence of unit diagonal constraints, we may recast the ADMM subproblem as a Riemannian optimization problem over the oblique manifold. To circumvent the non-convexity introduced by the Burer-Monteiro factorization, we employ a similar strategy as in
[
32
]
to escape from saddle points. Global convergence of the proposed algorithm is established assuming that the subproblem is solved to certain optimality.

\bullet
Assumption (
2
) implies a closed-form expression of
y
in terms of
S
:
y=({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*})^{-1}{\mathcal{A}}(S+C)
. As a preprocessing step, we propose to eliminate
y
from the objective of (
DSDP
) using
y=({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*})^{-1}{\mathcal{A}}(S+C)
. We empirically observe that it is more efficient to solve this modified version of (
DSDP
) with our algorithm.

\bullet
Numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed algorithm outperforms, by a significant margin, several advanced SDP solvers (
MOSEK
,
COPT
,
SDPNAL+
) in terms of accuracy, efficiency, and scalability on second-order SDP relaxations of dense and sparse BQPs. Besides, it typically runs several times faster than the recent low-rank SDP solver
ManiSDP

[
32
]
. The experiments also indicate an intriguing feature of our algorithm — the residue diving phenomenon, that is, the maximal KKT residue sharply decreases to far less than
10^{-8}
at some ADMM iteration. Moreover, it is worth mentioning that our algorithm typically returns a solution of extremely high accuracy in a few tens of ADMM iterations. In comparison, traditional ADMM-based algorithms often require more than ten thousand iterations to arrive at a solution of high accuracy for the same type of SDPs
[
17
]
.

3
The dual ADMM approach

In this section, we introduce the dual ADMM framework for solving (
DSDP
).
Let us define

{\mathcal{M}}\coloneqq\{S\in{\mathbb{S}}_{n}^{+}\mid\diag(S)=1\}=\{S\in{\mathbb{S}}_{n}^{+}\mid{\mathcal{B}}(S)=1\}.

(1)

Throughout the paper, we define the constant matrix
D
as

D\coloneqq{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\left(({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*})^{-1}b\right).

(2)

From
S={\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y)-C
together with Assumption
2
, we have

y=({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*})^{-1}{\mathcal{A}}(S+C)
, and consequently,

b^{\intercal}y=b^{\intercal}({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*})^{-1}{\mathcal{A}}(S+C)=\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\left(({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*})^{-1}b\right),S+C\right\rangle=\langle D,S+C\rangle.

Therefore, we may reformulate (
DSDP
) as

\begin{cases}\inf\limits_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},S\in{\mathcal{M}}}&\langle D,S+C\rangle\\
\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\rm{s.t.}&S={\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y)-C.\end{cases}

(DSDP’)

Remark 3
.

From now on, we work on the reformulation (
DSDP’
) instead of (
DSDP
) as the former empirically exhibits better performance with the algorithm that will be developed in this paper.

We now employ the ADMM framework to solve (
DSDP’
).
The augmented Lagrangian function associated with (
DSDP’
) is defined by

L_{\sigma}(S,y,\widetilde{X})=\langle D,S+C\rangle-\langle\widetilde{X},{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y)-S-C\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y)-S-C\|^{2}.

(3)

At the
k
-th ADMM iteration, one needs to solve the subproblems:

\displaystyle S^{k+1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\argmin\limits_{S\in{\mathcal{M}}}L_{\sigma_{k}}\left(S,y^{k},\widetilde{X}^{k}\right),

(4a)

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\argmin\limits_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}L_{\sigma_{k}}\left(S^{k+1},y,\widetilde{X}^{k}\right).

(4b)

Then,
\widetilde{X}^{k}
is updated as

\widetilde{X}^{k+1}\coloneqq\widetilde{X}^{k}-\sigma_{k}\left({\mathcal{A}}^{*}\left(y^{k+1}\right)-S^{k+1}-C\right).

(5)

Lemma 4
.

For any
k\geq 0
,
{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{X}^{k+1})=0
. Moreover, for any
k\geq 1
, the subproblem (
4b
) has a closed-form solution
y^{k+1}=({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*})^{-1}{\mathcal{A}}(S^{k+1}+C)
.

Proof.

The first-order optimality condition of (
4b
) reads as

-{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{X}^{k})+\sigma_{k}{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y^{k+1})-S^{k+1}-C)=0,

(6)

from which we obtain

{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{X}^{k+1})={\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{X}^{k}-\sigma_{k}({\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y^{k+1})-S^{k+1}-C))=0.

(7)

For
k\geq 1
, plugging
{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{X}^{k})=0
into (
6
) yields

({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*})(y^{k+1})={\mathcal{A}}(S^{k+1}-C),

(8)

which gives
y^{k+1}=({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*})^{-1}{\mathcal{A}}(S^{k+1}+C)
.

Let
\Phi_{k}(S)\coloneqq L_{\sigma_{k}}(S,y^{k},\widetilde{X}^{k})
.
The following lemma characterizes the optimality conditions of (
4a
).

Lemma 5
.

An element
S\in{\mathcal{M}}
is a minimizer of (
4a
) if and only if

\displaystyle X\coloneqq\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)S)

\displaystyle\succeq 0,

(9a)

\displaystyle XS

\displaystyle=0,

(9b)

where
\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)=\widetilde{X}^{k}-\sigma_{k}({\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y^{k})-S-C)+D
.

Proof.

Since (
4a
) is convex,
S\in{\mathcal{M}}
is a minimizer if and only if the KKT conditions hold.
The Lagrangian function of (
4a
) is

\Phi_{k}(S)-z^{\intercal}({\mathcal{B}}(S)-1)-\langle X,S\rangle,

(10)

where
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{n}^{+}
are the dual variables.
The KKT conditions of (
4a
) thus read as

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\in{\mathcal{M}},

(11a)

\displaystyle\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)-{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(z)-X

\displaystyle=0,

(11b)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\succeq 0,

(11c)

\displaystyle XS

\displaystyle=0.

(11d)

To get the closed-form expression of

, plugging

yields

\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)S=\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(z)S,

(12)

which gives

by matching the diagonal elements of both sides.

According to Lemma

is updated as

X^{k+1}\coloneqq\widetilde{X}^{k+1}+D-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}\left(\left(\widetilde{X}^{k+1}+D\right)S^{k+1}\right).

(13)

4
Exploiting the manifold structure

By Assumption
1
, (
DSDP
) admits a low-rank optimal solution. To take into account the low-rank property, we apply the Burer-Monteiro factorization to
\Phi_{k}(S)
and define
\Psi_{k}(Y)=\Phi_{k}(YY^{\intercal})
for
Y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times p}
(
p\ll n
). Doing so, the convex subproblem (
4a
) becomes the non-convex factorized subproblem:

\min_{Y\in{\mathcal{N}}_{p}}\Psi_{k}(Y)=L_{\sigma_{k}}(YY^{\intercal},y^{k},\widetilde{X}^{k}),

(Sub-
k
)

where

{\mathcal{N}}_{p}\coloneqq\{Y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times p}\mid YY^{\intercal}\in{\mathcal{M}}\}=\{Y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times p}\mid\|Y(i,:)\|=1,\,i=1,\ldots,n\},

(14)

is the oblique manifold (
Y(i,:)
stands for the
i
-th row of
Y
).
We call
p
the
factorization size
.
Thus, it remains to solve (
Sub-

k

) on the manifold
{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
, which could be done by off-the-shelf efficient Riemannian optimization methods. Here, we choose the Riemannian trust-region method to solve (
Sub-

k

) due to its superior performance, as shown in
[
32
]
. We refer the reader to
[
1
]
for details on the Riemannian trust-region method.

We next calculate the Riemannian gradient and Riemannian Hessian of (
Sub-

k

) that are necessary to perform optimization on the manifold
{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
via the Riemannian trust-region method. First of all, we note that at a point
Y\in{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
, the tangent space of
{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
is given by

T_{Y}{\mathcal{N}}_{p}=\{U\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times p}\mid\diag(UY^{\intercal})=0\},

(15)

and the normal space to
{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
is given by

N_{Y}{\mathcal{N}}_{p}=\{\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(u)Y\mid u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\}.

(16)

Lemma 6
(
[
32
]
, Lemma 4.1).

Let
Y
be a point on
{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
. The orthogonal projector
P_{Y}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times p}\rightarrow T_{Y}{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
is given by

P_{Y}(U)=U-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(UY^{\intercal})Y,\quad\text{ for }U\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times p}.

(17)

Proposition 7
.

Consider the non-convex subproblem (
Sub-

k

). Let
S=YY^{\intercal}
and
X=\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)S)
. Then the
Riemannian gradient
at
Y
is given by

\hbox{\rm{grad}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)=2XY.

(18)

For
U\in T_{Y}{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
, let

\widetilde{H}=\nabla^{2}\Psi_{k}(Y)[U]=2\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)U+2\sigma_{k}\left(Z-{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\left(({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*})^{-1}{\mathcal{A}}(Z)\right)\right)Y,

where
Z\coloneqq YU^{\intercal}+UY^{\intercal}
.
Then the
Riemannian Hessian
is given by

\hbox{\rm{Hess}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)[U]=\widetilde{H}-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(\widetilde{H}Y^{\intercal})Y-2\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)S)U.

(19)

Proof.

By (3) of
[
2
]
,
\hbox{\rm{grad}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)=P_{Y}(\nabla\Psi_{k}(Y))=P_{Y}(2\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)Y)
. By Lemma
6
, there exists
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that

\hbox{\rm{grad}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)=2\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)Y-2\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(z)Y.

Moreover, since
\hbox{\rm{grad}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)\in T_{Y}{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
, we have

\diag\left(\hbox{\rm{grad}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)Y^{\intercal}\right)=2\diag\left((\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(z))S\right)=0,

(20)

which gives
z=\diag(\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)S)
.

By (10) of
[
2
]
, we have

\hbox{\rm{Hess}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)[U]=P_{Y}(\nabla^{2}\Psi_{k}(Y)[U])+\mathfrak{A}_{Y}(U,P_{Y}^{\perp}(\nabla\Psi_{k}(Y))),

(21)

where
\mathfrak{A}_{Y}
is the Weingarten map at
Y
and
P_{Y}^{\perp}=I-P_{Y}
is the orthogonal projector at
Y
to
N_{Y}{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
. Let
D_{Y}(\cdot)[U]
be the directional derivative at
Y
along
U
.
Then,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{Y}(U,P_{Y}^{\perp}(\nabla\Psi_{k}(Y)))

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{A}_{Y}(U,2\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(z)Y)

\displaystyle=-P_{Y}(D_{Y}(2\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(z)Y)[U])

\displaystyle=-2P_{Y}(\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(z)U)-2P_{Y}(\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(D_{Y}(z)[U])Y)

\displaystyle=-2\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(z)U,

where we have used the fact that
P_{Y}(\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(z)U)=\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(z)U
and
P_{Y}(\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(u)Y)=0
for any
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. (
19
) then follows.

The global optimality of (
Sub-

k

.

Proposition 8
.

Let
Y\in{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
,
S=YY^{\intercal}
, and
X=\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)S)
. Then a stationary point

of the non-convex factorized subproblem (

k

) is a global minimizer if and only if

.

Proof.

First, notice that
Y
is a global minimizer of (
Sub-

k

) if and only if
S
is a minimizer of the convex subproblem (
4a
). The condition that
Y
is a stationary point means
\hbox{\rm{grad}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)=2XY=0
implying
XS=0
. The conclusion then follows from Lemma
5
.

We then obtain the following theorem as an immediate corollary of Proposition
8
.

Theorem 9
.

A stationary point
Y\in{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
of the non-convex factorized subproblem (
Sub-

k

) provides a minimizer
S=YY^{\intercal}
of (
DSDP’
) if and only if
\mathcal{A}^{*}(y^{k})=S+C
and
X=\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)S)\succeq 0
.

As the subproblem (
Sub-

k

) is non-convex, it is very important to escape from saddle points in order to solve (
Sub-

k

) to certain optimality. We shall see that a second-order descent direction
U
is readily available whenever
X=\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)S)\nsucceq 0
.

Theorem 10
.

Suppose
X=\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)S)\nsucceq 0
. Let
\delta\in{\mathbb{N}}
be a positive number and let
V\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times\delta}
be a matrix whose columns consist of eigenvectors corresponding to negative eigenvalues of
X
. Then
U\coloneqq[0_{n\times p},V]
is a second-order descent direction of (
Sub-

k

) with
p\coloneqq p+\delta
at the point
Y\coloneqq[Y,0_{n\times\delta}]
, namely,
U
satisfies

\langle U,\hbox{\rm{grad}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)\rangle=0,\quad\langle U,\hbox{\rm{Hess}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)[U]\rangle<0.

(22)

Proof.

By construction, we have
YU^{\intercal}=0
which implies
U\in T_{Y}{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
. Therefore,

\langle U,\hbox{\rm{grad}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)\rangle=2\hbox{\rm{Tr}}(U^{\intercal}XY)=2\hbox{\rm{Tr}}(XYU^{\intercal})=0.

On the other hand, by (
19
) and Lemma
6
, we have
\widetilde{H}=2\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)U
and

\displaystyle\hbox{\rm{Hess}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)[U]

\displaystyle=\widetilde{H}-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(\widetilde{H}Y^{\intercal})Y-2\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)S)U

\displaystyle=2\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)U-2\hbox{\rm{Diag}}(\nabla\Phi_{k}(S)S)U

\displaystyle=2XU.

Thus,

\displaystyle\langle U,\hbox{\rm{Hess}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y)[U]\rangle=2\hbox{\rm{Tr}}(U^{\intercal}XU)=2\hbox{\rm{Tr}}(V^{\intercal}XV)<0.

Remark 11
.

In analogy with Theorem 4.8 of
[
32
]
, one can show that
a rank deficient second-order critical point of (
Sub-

k

) is a global minimizer. Therefore, by escaping from saddle points we necessarily reach a global minimizer of (
Sub-

k

).

5
The
ManiDSDP
algorithm

Our dual Riemannian ADMM algorithm for solving (
DSDP’
) is presented in Algorithm
2
.

Algorithm 2

ManiDSDP

0:

{\mathcal{A}},b,C,\sigma_{0}>0
,
\sigma_{\max}>\sigma_{\min}>0
,
p_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\gamma>1,\tau_{2}\geq\tau_{1}>0

1:

k\leftarrow 0
,
y^{0}\leftarrow 0
,
p\leftarrow p_{0}
,
Y^{0}\leftarrow 0_{n\times p}
,
U\leftarrow 0_{n\times p}
;

2:

while
The stopping criterion is not fulfilled
do

3:

Solve the ADMM subproblem (
Sub-

k

) inexactly using the Riemannian trust-region method with
U
as a descent direction, obtaining an approximate minimizer
Y^{k+1}
;

4:

S^{k+1}\leftarrow Y^{k+1}(Y^{k+1})^{\intercal}
;

5:

y^{k+1}\leftarrow({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}^{*})^{-1}{\mathcal{A}}(S^{k+1}+C)
;

6:

\widetilde{X}^{k+1}\leftarrow\widetilde{X}^{k}-\sigma_{k}\left({\mathcal{A}}^{*}\left(y^{k+1}\right)-S^{k+1}-C\right)
;

7:

z^{k+1}\leftarrow\diag\left(\left(\widetilde{X}^{k+1}+D\right)S^{k+1}\right)
;

8:

X^{k+1}\leftarrow\widetilde{X}^{k+1}+D-\hbox{\rm{Diag}}\left(z^{k+1}\right)
;

9:

Compute a descent direction
U
from
X^{k+1}
according to Theorem
10
;

10:

Update
p
following the strategy described in
[
32
, Section 5.1]
;

11:

Update
\sigma_{k}
according to the rules (
23
);

12:

k\leftarrow k+1
;

13:

end

while

14:

return

(S^{k},y^{k},X^{k},z^{k})
;

We make a few remarks on Algorithm
2
.

•

The initial factorization size
p_{0}
can be set to
2
. However, we have found that sometimes choosing
p_{0}\sim O(\lceil\log(m)\rceil)
would bring some speed-up; see
[
26
]
.

•

At Step 9, one could perform a partial eigenvalue decomposition when the full eigenvalue decomposition is too expensive.

•

To minimize the cost of solving the ADMM subproblem (
Sub-

k

), we follow the strategy described in
[
32
, Section 5.1]
to dynamically adjust the factorization size
p
;

•

The following self-adaptive strategy is adopted to update the penalty factor
\sigma
:

\sigma_{k+1}=\begin{cases}\max\,\{\sigma_{k}/\gamma,\sigma_{\min}\},&\text{ if }\|R^{k+1}\|/(1+\left\lVert b\right\rVert)<\tau_{1}\|\hbox{\rm{grad}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y^{k+1})\|,\\
\min\,\{\gamma\sigma_{k},\sigma_{\max}\},&\text{ if }\|R^{k+1}\|/(1+\left\lVert b\right\rVert)>\tau_{2}\|\hbox{\rm{grad}}\,\Psi_{k}(Y^{k+1})\|,\\
\sigma_{k},&\text{ otherwise,}\\
\end{cases}

(23)

where
R^{k+1}=\mathcal{A}^{*}(y^{k+1})-S^{k+1}-C
and
\gamma>1,\tau_{2}\geq\tau_{1}>0,\sigma_{\max}>\sigma_{\min}>0
are constants.

We now establish the global convergence of
ManiDSDP
assuming that the subproblem (
Sub-

k

) is solved to certain optimality.

Theorem 12
.

Let
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}},\{\tau_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
satisfy
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty
and
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\tau_{k}<\infty
. Suppose that we use the following stopping criterion for the subproblem (
Sub-

k

):

\|X^{k+1}Y^{k+1}\|\leq\varepsilon_{k}\text{ and }\lambda_{\min}(X^{k+1})\geq-\tau_{k}.

(24)

Let
(\hat{S},\hat{y},\hat{X},\hat{z})
be a limit point of
\{(S^{k},y^{k},X^{k},z^{k})\}_{k\geq 1}
. Then
(\hat{S},\hat{y},\hat{X},\hat{z})
is a KKT point of (
DSDP
).

Let us begin by proving two lemmas.

Lemma 13
.

Suppose that
Y^{k+1}
is an approximate minimizer of the subproblem (
Sub-

k

) so that the criterion (
24
) is fulfilled.
Let
S^{k+1}\coloneqq Y^{k+1}(Y^{k+1})^{\intercal}
and
(S^{\star},y^{\star},X^{\star},z^{\star})
be a KKT point of (
DSDP’
). Let
\widetilde{X}^{\star}=X^{\star}-D+{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(z^{\star})
and
R^{k+1}=\mathcal{A}^{*}(y^{k+1})-S^{k+1}-C
. Then

\langle\widetilde{X}^{k}-\widetilde{X}^{\star},R^{k+1}\rangle\geq\sigma_{k}\|R^{k+1}\|^{2}-n\tau_{k}-\sqrt{n}\varepsilon_{k}.

(25)

Proof.

By the stopping criterion (
24
), we have

\langle X^{k+1},S^{\star}-S^{k+1}\rangle=\langle X^{k+1},S^{\star}\rangle-\langle X^{k+1}Y^{k+1},Y^{k+1}\rangle\geq-n\tau_{k}-\sqrt{n}\varepsilon_{k}.

(26)

From the definitions
S^{\star}={\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y^{\star})-C
and
S^{k+1}={\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y^{k+1})-R^{k+1}-C
, we obtain

S^{\star}-S^{k+1}={\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y^{\star}-y^{k+1})+R^{k+1}.

(27)

It follows that

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{X}^{k+1},S^{\star}-S^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{X}^{k}-\sigma_{k}R^{k+1},{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y^{\star}-y^{k+1})+R^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{X}^{k+1}),y^{\star}-y^{k+1}\rangle+\langle\widetilde{X}^{k},R^{k+1}\rangle-\sigma_{k}\|R^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{X}^{k},R^{k+1}\rangle-\sigma_{k}\|R^{k+1}\|^{2}.

(28)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\langle D,S^{\star}-S^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle D,{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y^{\star}-y^{k+1})+R^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathcal{A}}(D),y^{\star}-y^{k+1}\rangle+\langle D,R^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathcal{A}}(X^{\star}+{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(z^{\star})),y^{\star}-y^{k+1}\rangle+\langle D,R^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle X^{\star}+{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(z^{\star}),{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y^{\star})-{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(y^{k+1})\rangle+\langle D,R^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle X^{\star}+{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(z^{\star}),(S^{\star}+C)-(S^{k+1}+C+R^{k+1})\rangle+\langle D,R^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle X^{\star},S^{\star}\rangle-\langle X^{\star},S^{k+1}\rangle-\langle X^{\star}+{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(z^{\star}),R^{k+1}\rangle+\langle D,R^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle D-X^{\star}-{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(z^{\star}),R^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\widetilde{X}^{\star},R^{k+1}\rangle,

(29)

where we have used
{\mathcal{A}}(D)=b={\mathcal{A}}(X^{\star}+{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(z^{\star}))
,
\langle X^{\star},S^{\star}\rangle=0
, and
\langle X^{\star},S^{k+1}\rangle\geq 0
.
Besides,

\langle-{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(z^{k+1}),S^{\star}-S^{k+1}\rangle=\langle-z^{k+1},{\mathcal{B}}(S^{\star})-{\mathcal{B}}(S^{k+1})\rangle=0.

(30)

Substituting
\widetilde{X}^{k+1}+D-{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(z^{k+1})
for
X^{k+1}
in (
26
) and using (
28
)–(
30
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle X^{k+1},S^{\star}-S^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{X}^{k+1}+D-{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(z^{k+1}),S^{\star}-S^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle\widetilde{X}^{k}-\widetilde{X}^{\star},R^{k+1}\rangle-\sigma_{k}\|R^{k+1}\|^{2}.

(31)

Thus, combining (
26
) with (
5
) yields (
25
).

Lemma 14
.

It holds that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|R^{k}\|=0
.

Proof.

Let
(S^{\star},y^{\star},X^{\star},z^{\star})
be a KKT point of (
DSDP’
). For all
k\geq 1
, by invoking Lemma
13
, we have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{X}^{k+1}-\widetilde{X}^{\star}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{X}^{k}-\widetilde{X}^{\star}\|^{2}-2\sigma_{k}\langle\widetilde{X}^{k}-\widetilde{X}^{\star},R^{k+1}\rangle+\sigma_{k}^{2}\|R^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{X}^{k}-\widetilde{X}^{\star}\|^{2}-\sigma_{k}^{2}\|R^{k+1}\|^{2}+2\sigma_{k}\left(n\tau_{k}+\sqrt{n}\varepsilon_{k}\right).

For an arbitrary
N\geq 1
, summing the above inequality for
k=1,\ldots,N
yields

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{X}^{N+1}-\widetilde{X}^{\star}\|^{2}\leq\|\widetilde{X}^{1}-\widetilde{X}^{\star}\|^{2}-\sigma_{\min}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|R^{k+1}\|^{2}+2\sigma_{\max}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(n\tau_{k}+\sqrt{n}\varepsilon_{k}\right).

It follows

\sigma_{\min}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|R^{k+1}\|^{2}\leq\|\widetilde{X}^{1}-\widetilde{X}^{\star}\|^{2}+2\sigma_{\max}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(n\tau_{k}+\sqrt{n}\varepsilon_{k}\right).

(32)

Now, because the right-hand side of (
32
) is bounded and
N
is arbitrary, we see that
\|R^{k+1}\|
must converge to
0
.

Proof of Theorem
12
.
Because
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{k}=0
, we have
\hat{X}\succeq 0
. Moreover, noting
\langle X^{k},S^{k}\rangle=|\langle X^{k}Y^{k},Y^{k}\rangle|\leq\varepsilon_{k-1}\cdot\|Y^{k}\|
, since
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varepsilon_{k}=0
, we have
\langle\hat{X},\hat{S}\rangle=0
. By Lemma
14
,
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(\hat{y})-\hat{S}-C=0
. In addition, by Lemma
4
,
{\mathcal{A}}(\hat{X}+{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(\hat{z}))={\mathcal{A}}(\hat{\widetilde{X}}+D)=b
where
\hat{\widetilde{X}}\coloneqq\hat{X}+{\mathcal{B}}^{*}(\hat{z})-D
. Therefore,
(\hat{S},\hat{y},\hat{X},\hat{z})
is a KKT point of (
DSDP
).

Remark 15
.

Algorithm
2
can be extended to handle SDPs with multi-blocks which often arise as relaxations of sparse polynomial optimization problems.

The Stopping Criterion.
To measure the feasibility and optimality of an approximate solution

\begin{cases}\inf\limits_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{(q-2)t+2}}&\sum_{k=1}^{t}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{\intercal}Q_{k}{\mathbf{x}}_{k}+\mathbf{c}_{k}^{\intercal}{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\right)\\

7
Conclusions and future work

In this paper, we have presented a dual Riemannian ADMM algorithm for solving low-rank SDPs with unit diagonal constraints.
Global convergence of the algorithm is established assuming that the subproblem is solved to certain optimality. Numerical experiments demonstrate the superior performance of the algorithm.
There are several directions for future research:

•

We have empirically observed that our algorithm typically takes a few tens of ADMM iterations to converge to a solution of extremely high accuracy. This super fast convergence rate in comparison with traditional ADMM-based algorithms remains mysterious to us and should be carefully analyzed in future work. In particular, it is important to figure out which role the low-rank factorization plays for the acceleration, which will deepen our understanding of the algorithm.

•

An intriguing feature of our algorithm is the residue diving phenomenon as illustrated in Section
6.5
. A clear theoretical explanation of this phenomenon is lacking and will be pursued in future work.

•

Finally, in view of the strong performance, it would be very interesting to extend the algorithm to handle more types of SDPs.

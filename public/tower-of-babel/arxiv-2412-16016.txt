Abstract
. Let E E be an elliptic curve over a quartic field K K . By the Mordell-Weil theorem, E ⁡ ( K ) E(K) is a finitely generated group. We determine all the possibilities for the torsion group E ​ ( K ) tors E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}} where K K ranges over all quartic fields K K and E E ranges over all elliptic curves over K K . We show that there are no sporadic torsion groups, or in other words, that all torsion groups either do not appear or they appear for infinitely many non-isomorphic elliptic curves E E . Proving this requires showing that numerous modular curves X 1 ​ ( m , n ) X_{1}(m,n) have no non-cuspidal degree 4 4 points. We deal with almost all the curves using one of 3 methods: a method for the rank 0 cases requiring no computation ( Proposition 5.1 ); the Hecke sieve, a local method requiring computer-assisted computations; and the global method, an argument for the positive rank cases also requiring no computation. We deal with the handful of remaining cases using ad hoc methods.

1.
Introduction

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
K
. The set
E(K)
of
K
-rational points on
E
has, by the Mordell-Weil theorem, the structure of a finitely generated abelian group. Hence it is isomorphic to
E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, where
E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}
is the
torsion subgroup
of
E(K)
, and
r
is the
rank
of
E
over
K
. The question of what are the possible values of
E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}
for
E
running through all elliptic curves over all number fields
K
of degree
d
has received considerable attention. The first major result was Mazur’s torsion theorem
[
26
]
, which, building on work of Kubert
[
24
]
(and others), completed the classification of possible torsion groups of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}.
For quadratic fields, the classification was completed by Kamienny
[
18
]
, building on work of Kenku and Momose
[
22
]
. The last degree
d
for which all the possible torsion groups of elliptic curves over degree
d
number fields are known is
d=3
. The result was proved by Derickx, Etropolski, van Hoeij, Morrow and Zureick-Brown in
[
7
]
, building on previous work of Jeon, Kim and Schweizer
[
17
]
, and Bruin and Najman
[
3
]
.

The approach in all the mentioned papers is to study degree
d
points on the modular curves
X_{1}(m,n)
. When
m>4
or
n>4
these are fine moduli spaces of (generalized) elliptic curves of triples
(E,P,Q)
with
P
and
Q
being points on
E
generating a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
.

In this paper, we complete the classification of possible torsion groups of elliptic curves over quartic fields. Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
K
varies over all quartic number fields and
E
varies over
all elliptic curves over
K
, the groups that appear as

E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}
are exactly the following

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle n=1-18,20,21,22,24,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2n\mathbb{Z},\quad\quad

\displaystyle n=1-9,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/3n\mathbb{Z},\quad

\displaystyle n=1-3,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/4n\mathbb{Z},\quad

\displaystyle n=1,2,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z},\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}.\quad

One additional reason that the classification of possible torsion groups over quartic fields might be particularly interesting is that it seems it might be the last degree where there are no non-cuspidal
sporadic points
on
X_{1}(m,n)
. Recall that a sporadic point on a curve
X
is a point of degree
d
such that there are only finitely many points of degree
\leq d
on
X
. There are no non-cuspidal sporadic points on
X_{1}(m,n)
of degree
1
and
2
, while there exist sporadic cubic points on only one such curve,
X_{1}(21)
, and all of them correspond to a single elliptic curve (see
[
27
]
). Jeon, Kim and Park
[
16
]
proved that all the groups in
Theorem

1.1
appear infinitely often (see
Theorem

3.1
), so it follows that there are no sporadic points of degree
4
on
X_{1}(m,n)
. Moreover, from van Hoeij’s results
[
32
]
one can easily see that there exist sporadic points of degree
5\leq d\leq 13
. See
Section

A.1
for more details. Similarly, Clark, Genao, Pollack and Saia
[
5
]
showed that
X_{1}(n)
has sporadic points (corresponding to CM elliptic curves) for all
n>720
. These results show that there is an abundance of sporadic points on
X_{1}(n)
, and it might well be possible that
d=4
is the largest degree such that, for all
n
,
X_{1}(n)
has no degree
d
sporadic points.

We use several tools to prove
Theorem

1.1
. To deal with the cases where
\rk J_{1}(m,n)(\mathbb{Q})=0,
we first prove
Proposition

5.1
which allows us to eliminate all the cases where the hypothetical
E/K
with
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\subseteq E(K)
has bad reduction at all places above some rational prime
p
, e.g. when
Y_{1}(m,n)(\mathbb{F}_{p^{d}})=\emptyset
for all
1\leq d\leq 4.
To deal with the case where
E
has good reduction at a prime over
p
, we develop the
Hecke sieve
to show that all points in
Y_{1}(m,n)^{(4)}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
cannot be reductions of points in
Y_{1}(m,n)^{(4)}(\mathbb{Q})
by studying the actions of Hecke operators. We give more details at the beginning of
Section

5
.

To deal with the
\rk J_{1}(m,n)(\mathbb{Q})>0
cases we use the
global method
(see
Proposition

4.4
) and its variants to deal with almost all the remaining cases. The idea of this method is that, under some mild assumptions which are satisfied in all our cases, from a hypothetical point of degree
4
on
Y_{1}(m,n)
one could construct a map to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree smaller than known gonality bounds, which is clearly impossible.

A notable advantage of
Proposition

5.1
and the global method is their minimal reliance on computer calculations, especially in comparison to related works (e.g.
[
7
]
). These methods significantly reduce the number of cases requiring computational effort. For instance, we handle
X_{1}(45)
using
Proposition

5.1
and
X_{1}(121)
through the global method, whereas previous approaches demanded extensive computations to address these cases (see
[
7
]
).

Another important advantage of our work is that we do not use formal immersions, which have been used extensively in the classification of torsion groups over number fields of degree
d=1,2,3.
The formal immersion criterion (without additional explicit computations) can only deal with elliptic curves that have bad reduction at all primes in the number field
K
above some fixed rational prime
p
. So once the order
N
of the point one wants to rule out is smaller than the Hasse bound at
2
(in the best case), i.e. smaller than
(2^{d/2}+1)^{2}
, then the formal immersion approach runs into difficulties. On the other hand, we expect the lower bound for
N
that the global method can deal with is quasi-linear in the level (more about this will be said in upcoming work by Derickx and Stoll). All this means that our methods are much more suitable to tackling the same question over higher degree number fields. As a demonstration, we apply our methods to higher degree number fields in
Section

7
. Specializing to quintic number fields, we eliminate more than half of the torsion groups one would need to eliminate to obtain a complete classification. We do this by ”pure thought”, i.e., by just checking the LMFDB and performing calculations that can easily be done by hand.

In
Section

2.2
we work out a version of the Eichler-Shimura relation for
X_{1}(2,2n)
. This relation is already well-known for
X_{1}(n)
. Doing this was essential to be able to apply the Hecke sieve directly on
X_{1}(2,20)
and
X_{1}(2,24)
to deal with the non-cyclic cases
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/20\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/24\mathbb{Z}
.

To be able to use the Hecke sieve, we implemented the action of Hecke operators on non-cuspidal points on modular curves in
magma

[
2
]
.

Overview

For a finite abelian group
T
we will say it is a

if there exists an elliptic curve
E
over a quartic field

such that

.

. This is the list of all abelian groups

•

G
occurs only finitely often as a quartic torsion group,

•

H\subsetneq G

H

•

if
G
contains a subgroup isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{2}
, then
\varphi(n)|4.

•

|G|
is divisible only by primes

. The third bullet point is necessary because of the Weil pairing, while the third follows from

2.
Conventions and notation

Let
m\mid n
. We define
X_{1}(m,n)
to be the moduli space parametrizing triples
(E,P,Q)
, where
E
is a generalized elliptic curve, and
P
and
Q
are points generating a subgroup of
E[n]
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. It is a smooth projective geometrically irreducible curve over
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m},\frac{1}{n}]
. It is a fine moduli space if
n>4
.

Cusps of the modular curve
X_{1}(m,n)
correspond to
Néron

k
-gons, where
m\mid k\mid n
together with a level structure; for the definition see
[
6
, Sections 1.3 and 2.2.1]
. Over an algebraically closed field
K
a Néron
k
-gon is a group scheme isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
. If
n
is invertible in
K
then the level structure is an injective homomorphism
\phi:\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}(K)\times\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
such that
\phi
is surjective on the second coordinate.

2.1.
Analytic and algebraic ranks of modular abelian varieties

If
A
is an abelian variety over a number field
K
then we use
\rk A(K)
to denote the rank of the Mordell-Weil group
A(K)
. The integer
\rk A(K)
is also called the algebraic rank of
A
. Similarly we use
\rk_{an}A(K)
to denote the analytic rank of
A
which is defined to be the order of vanishing of
L(A,s)
at
s=1
. The BSD conjecture predicts that
\rk A(K)=\rk_{an}A(K)
. We use
S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(n))^{new}
to denote the set of all cuspidal newforms of weight
k
for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{1}(n)
. If
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(n))^{new}
then we denote by
A_{f}
the isogeny factor of
J_{1}(n)
corresponding to
f
. This
A_{f}
is a simple abelian variety over
\mathbb{Q}
but might not be simple over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Note that for two newforms
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(n))^{new}
and
f^{\prime}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(n^{\prime}))^{new}
we have
A_{f}=A_{f^{\prime}}
if and only if
n=n^{\prime}
and
f
and
f^{\prime}
are Galois conjugates. It is known that every simple isogeny factor of
J_{1}(n)
is isogenous to an abelian variety
A_{f}
where
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(n^{\prime}))
and
n^{\prime}|n
. If
f
is a newform in
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(n))^{new}
then we use
L(f,s)
to denote its
L
-function and define
\rk_{an}f
as the order of vanishing of
L(f,s)
at
s=1
. If
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(n))^{new}
then we use
K_{f}\subset\mathbb{C}
to denote the subfield generated by its coefficients. The field
K_{f}
is known to be a number field isomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}\otimes\End A_{f}
. There is the following relation between
L
-functions:

L(A_{f},s)=\prod_{\sigma:K_{f}\to\mathbb{C}}L(\sigma(f),s).

Assuming the Birch-Swinnerton–Dyer conjecture, the order of vanishing of
L(\sigma(f),s)
is the same for all choices of
\sigma\in\Hom_{\mathbb{Q}}(K_{f},\mathbb{C})
. In particular
\rk_{an}A_{f}(\mathbb{Q})=[K_{f}:\mathbb{Q}]\rk_{an}f
.

A part of the BSD conjecture is known to be true for abelian varieties of the form
A_{f}
due to the following celebrated result
[
19
, Corollary 14.3]
(we specialize by taking
\chi
to be the trivial character):

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
\mathbb{Q}
such that there is a surjective
homomorphism
J_{1}(n)\rightarrow A
for some
n\geq 1
. Assume
L(A,1)\neq 0
. Then
A(\mathbb{Q})
is finite.

This result of Kato generalizes earlier results by Kolyvagin and Logachev for abelian varieties attached to newforms in
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(n))
.

We use
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
to denote the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
inside
\mathbb{C}
. Since the coefficients of newforms all lie in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
we get an action of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
on
S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(n))^{new}
. We use
S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(n))^{new}_{\Gal}:=S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(n))^{new}/\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
to denote the set of all Galois orbits of newforms under this action. Since the isogeny class of
A_{f}
only depends on the Galois conjugacy class of a newform, it also makes sense to define
A_{[f]}
when
[f]\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(n))^{new}_{\Gal}
is a Galois orbit. In doing so, we get an isogeny decomposition

J_{1}(n)\sim\bigoplus_{n^{\prime}|n}\bigoplus_{f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(n^{\prime}))^{new}_{\Gal}}A_{[f]}^{\sigma_{0}(n/n^{\prime})},

where
\sigma_{0}(n/n^{\prime})
denotes the sum of positive divisors of
n/n^{\prime}
.

Example 2.2
.

In this example we explain how to use the LMFDB to explicitly find a large rank 0 quotient of
J_{1}(N)
. The LMFDB contains an upper bound on the analytic rank of all weight 2 modular forms of level
\leq 1000
. In this article we often need the existence of a large isogeny factor
J_{1}(N)
of algebraic rank 0 for
N<1000
. Such isogeny factors are easy to find using the data in the LMFDB. The
LMFDB data
on the modular forms of weight
2
and level dividing
65
shows that the Galois orbit of newforms with LMFDB label
\hrefhttps://www.lmfdb.org/ModularForm/GL2/Q/holomorphic/65/2/a/a\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(65))^{new}
is the only orbit for which the analytic rank could potentially be positive. In particular, it follows that every isogeny factor of
J_{1}(65)/J_{0}(65)
has analytic and hence algebraic rank 0. Implying that
J_{1}(65)/J_{0}(65)(\mathbb{Q})
is a finite abelian torsion group. In the digital form of this article the words
LMFDB data
will always be a hyperlink that brings one to the LMFDB page containing the data that is needed for the argument. Similarly, LMFDB labels of newforms like
65.2.a.a
will also directly link to the LMFDB page on that newform.

2.2.
Hecke operators

Let
q\nmid n
be a prime, and
T_{q}
be a Hecke operator on
X_{1}(n)
defined as in
[
12
, Section 3.3]
:

(2.1)

T_{q}(E,P)=\sum_{G\subseteq E[q]\text{ of order $q$}}(E/G,P\hskip-7.11317pt\mod G).

Recall that
T_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q}}
acts on
J_{1}(n)_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
as
\Frob_{q}+\langle q\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q},*}\Ver_{q}
, where Verschiebung
\Ver_{q}
is the dual of the Frobenius, see
[
12
, (10.2.3)]
. This is the Eichler-Shimura relation.

For
q\nmid 2n
a prime, we define the action of
T_{q}
on
X_{1}(2,2n)
similarly as in (
2.1
):

(2.2)

T_{q}(E,P,Q)=\sum_{G\subseteq E[q]\text{ of order $q$}}(E/G,P\hskip-7.11317pt\mod G,Q\hskip-7.11317pt\mod G).

We will need to consider
T_{q}
as an operator on
X_{1}(2,2n)
. There is a degree 2 morphism

X_{1}(4n)\rightarrow X_{1}(2,2n)=X_{1}(4n)/\langle 2n+1\rangle,

(E,P)\mapsto(E/\langle 2nP\rangle,Q,P\text{ mod}\langle 2nP\rangle),

\text{where }Q\text{ generates }E[2]/\langle 2nP\rangle,
which is compatible with
T_{q}
,
\Frob_{q}
and
\Ver_{q}
. It follows that
T_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q}}
acts on
J_{1}(2,2n)_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
and also satisfies the Eichler-Shimura relation (as does
J_{1}(n)_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
)

(2.3)

T_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q}}=\Frob_{q}+\langle q\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q},*}\Ver_{q},

where
\langle q\rangle
acts on
X_{1}(2,2n)
as

\langle q\rangle(E,P,Q)=(E,qP,qQ)=(E,P,qQ).

The following two propositions are not used in eliminating any of the groups in the paper, but we believe that they are interesting in their own right, and might be useful in future work.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
m=1
or
2
and
m|n
. Let
q\nmid n
be a prime,
k>0
an integer, and let
\Delta(n):=(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}/\langle-1\rangle
denote the group of diamond operators. Then there exists a monic polynomial
\Psi_{k}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[\Delta(n)][x]
of degree
k
such that

\Psi_{k}(T_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q}})=\Frob_{q}^{k}+(\langle q\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q},*}\Ver_{q})^{k}.

Proof.

Let
a:=\Frob_{q}
and
b:=\langle q\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q},*}\Ver_{q}
. Then the elementary symmetric polynomials on
a
and
b
are
a+b=T_{q}
and
ab=q\langle q\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q},*}
. Now

T_{q}^{k}-a^{k}-b^{k}=ab\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\binom{k}{i}a^{i-1}b^{k-i-1}.

Now
\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\binom{k}{i}a^{i-1}b^{k-i-1}
is a symmetric polynomial of degree
k-2
and can therefore be expressed in terms of
T_{q}
and
q\langle q\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q},*}
, where the power of
T_{q}
occurring is at most
k-2
. In particular, one can write
\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\binom{k}{i}a^{i-1}b^{k-i-1}=\Psi^{\prime}_{k}(T_{q})
for some polynomial
\Psi^{\prime}_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[\Delta(n)][x]
of degree at most
k-2
. So
\Psi_{k}:=x^{k}-q\langle q\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q},*}\Psi^{\prime}_{k}
satisfies the proposition.
∎

Let

5.
The Hecke sieve for the rank 0 cases

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
K
of degree
d
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
be a suitable prime. Our main approach is to split the problem up into cases depending on the reduction of
E
above the different primes of
K
above
p
. The argument exposition is tailored to the case in which
\rk J_{1}(m,n)(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m}))=0
, but it could be generalized to
\rk J_{1}(m,n)(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m}))>0
. We first prove
Proposition

5.1
, which is a modification of
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, and which allows us to rule out the cases where
E
has bad reduction at all primes above
p
; which is automatically satisfied if
Y_{1}(m,n)(\mathbb{F}_{p^{d}})=\emptyset
for
1\leq d\leq 4.

To deal with the case in which
E
has good reduction at least one prime of
K
over
p
we use a method that we call the
Hecke sieve
(
Propositions

5.4
and
5.6
). The Hecke sieve works by studying the point
x^{(d)}\in X_{1}(m,n)^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m}))
corresponding to
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow E(K)
and using Hecke operators to rule out the different possibilities for
x^{(d)}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
that have at least one non-cuspidal place in their support. The Hecke sieve has some similarities to the Mordell-Weil sieve, but has the advantage that one does not need to calculate any generators of the Mordell-Weil group
J_{1}(m,n)(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m}))
.

.

m\mid n
be positive integers such that

,

be a prime not dividing

, and

an integer. Suppose

.

If
p=2

or

Proposition 5.4

(Hecke Sieve)
.

Let
m=1
or
2
and
m|n
,
p
a prime not dividing
n
,
q
a prime with
q\nmid 2pn
and
1\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
be integers. Assume
\rk J_{1}(m,n)(\mathbb{Q})=0
. Suppose that

i)

D\in Y_{1}(m,n)^{(d^{\prime})}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is a non-cuspidal effective divisor of degree
d^{\prime}
such that

[A_{q}(D)]\neq 0,

and

ii)

C\in X_{1}(m,n)^{(d-d^{\prime})}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is a cuspidal effective divisor of degree

, such that
[A_{q}(C)]=0
,

where
A_{q}=T_{q}-\langle q\rangle q-1
as in

. Then a point

We have that

by

. This is obviously a contradiction since

.
∎

for

(corresponding to the newform

) and

for

(corresponding to the newform

(respectively

\chi
be a character in the character orbits

(respectively

). If we choose
a
in the kernel of

then

is finite, and we can apply
Proposition

4.4
as well. Choosing

with

of order coprime to

suffices to ensure

.

We will use

in all cases, and will have

in all cases, apart from

of the paper

\displaystyle Q_{1}(2,\,_{>\,}\!2)

\displaystyle:=(b,b).

Theorem B.1
.

The curve
E_{1}(2,\,_{>\,}\!2)
is an elliptic curve over
Y_{1}(2,\,_{>\,}\!2)
. The points
P_{1}(2,\,_{>\,}\!2)
and
Q_{1}(2,\,_{>\,}\!2)
on
E_{1}(2,\,_{>\,}\!2)
have order
2
respectively
>2
in all fibers.
Furthermore, the triple

(E_{1}(2,\,_{>\,}\!2),P_{1}(2,\,_{>\,}\!2),Q_{1}(2,\,_{>\,}\!2))

over
Y_{1}(2,\,_{>\,}\!2)
is universal in the sense that it represents the moduli problem
[\Gamma_{1}(2,\,_{>\,}\!2)]
.

Proof.

We start with
E/S
written in Weierstrass form

(B.1)

y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6},

together with two points
P,Q\in E(S)
of order
2
in all fibers and order
>2
in all fibers, respectively. This equation can be mapped via an isomorphism
f
to another equation in
Weierstrass form, where the isomorphism
f
is represented by a quadruple
(u,r,s,t)
, such that
f
maps
(x,y)
to

((x-r)u^{2},\;(y-s(x-r)-t)u^{3}),

see
[
29
, Proposition VII.1.3]
. The effect of this isomorphism on the coefficients
a_{i}
is

(B.2)

\begin{split}a_{1}^{\prime}&=(a_{1}+2s)u,\\
a_{2}^{\prime}&=(a_{2}-a_{1}s+3r-s^{2})u^{2},\\
a_{3}^{\prime}&=(a_{3}+a_{1}r+2t)u^{3},\\
a_{4}^{\prime}&=(a_{4}+2a_{2}r-a_{1}(rs+t)-a_{3}s+3r^{2}-2st)u^{4},\\
a_{6}^{\prime}&=(a_{6}-a_{1}rt+a_{2}r^{2}-a_{3}t+a_{4}r+r^{3}-t^{2})u^{6}.\end{split}

We move the point
P
to
(0,0)
by selecting an appropriate
r
(to move
x(P)
to
0
) and
t
(to move
y(P)
to
0
, after
x
has been moved to
0
). Now our equation is of the form

y^{2}+a_{1}^{\prime}xy=x^{2}+a_{2}^{\prime}x^{2}+a_{4}^{\prime}x.

Notice that
a_{6}^{\prime}=0
since
(0,0)
is a point on the curve and
a_{3}^{\prime}=0
since
(0,0)
is of order 2. We can move
a_{1}^{\prime}
to
0
by selecting an appropriate
s
in the quadruple corresponding to the isomorphism by (
B.2
). Now the point
Q
is of the form
(v,w)
for some
v,w\in\mathcal{O}_{S}(S)
since it is of order
>2
in all fibers. We can scale the coordinates of
Q
by
(v,w)\mapsto(vu^{2},wu^{3})
. Hence, we can choose
u=w/v
and letting
b:=v^{3}/w^{2}
, we get that
Q
is now of the form
(b,b)
.
Note here that
w,v,u\in\mathcal{O}_{S}(S)^{\times}
, since otherwise
Q
would be a point of order
2
in the fiber where one of
w,v,u
vanishes.
Let

y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{2}^{\prime\prime}x^{2}+a_{4}^{\prime\prime}x

be the equation we get after this transformation.
Let
c=a_{2}^{\prime\prime}
. Since
Q=(b,b)
lies on this equation we get that

b^{2}=b^{3}+cb^{2}+ba_{4}^{\prime\prime},

so
a_{4}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{b^{2}-b^{3}-cb^{2}}{b}=(1-b-c)b
, as claimed.
∎

Remark B.2
.

Jain gives in
[
15
]
a (two-variable) family of elliptic curves that is birational to
E_{1}(2,_{>}2).
. His family is given in terms of variables
t
and
q
, and to get from
E_{1}(2,_{>}2)
(given in terms of
b
and
c
) to his family and vice versa, one uses the following transformations:

\displaystyle t:=(-2b-c+1)/(c-1);

\displaystyle q:=b+1/2c+1/2-2b/(2b+c-1);

\displaystyle b:=(t+1)(qt+1)/t^{2};

\displaystyle c:=(t^{2}-2qt-2)/t^{2}.

Torsion of α \alpha -connections on the density manifold
Nihat Ay, Lorenz J. Schwachhöfer

Abstract
We study the torsion of the α \alpha -connections defined on the density manifold in terms of a regular Riemannian metric. In the case of the Fisher-Rao metric our results confirm the fact that all α \alpha -connections are torsion free. For the α \alpha -connections obtained by the Otto metric, we show that, except for α = − 1 \alpha=-1 , they are not torsion free.



1
Introduction

Classical information-geometric structures are agnostic to the geometry of the sample space
M
which is given, for instance, in terms of a Riemannian metric

{\rm g}
. To address this problem, many works within information geometry have tried to incorporate Wasserstein geometry
[
5
]
. In this context, the so-called Otto metric has been studied as an alternative to the Fisher-Rao metric
[
7
]
.
This line of research has been recently extended by the definition of an exponential connection as the dual of the mixture connection with respect to the Otto metric
[
2
]
. It was conjectured that the new exponential connection is torsion free, which would directly imply counterparts of important results within the classical theory, such as the generalised Pythagorean theorem in terms of a canonical contrast function.

In the present article, we disprove this conjecture by deriving an explicit formula for the torsion of the new exponential connection. In fact, we study the torsion of all
\alpha
-connections associated with some regular Riemannian metric on the density manifold. For
\alpha=0
, we do not recover the Levi-Civita connection whenever the connection for
\alpha=1
is not torsion free.

2
The affine structure of
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)

We consider a finite-dimensional compact connected
C^{\infty}
-manifold
M
without boundary. A
smooth measure on
M

is a measure which is induced by some smooth Riemannian metric on
M
. By compactness of
M
, such a measure is finite, and we are particularly interested in the space
P_{+}^{\infty}(M)
of smooth probability measures.

Picking some
\mu\in P_{+}^{\infty}(M)
, we may identify the
vector space
S^{\infty}(M)
of smooth signed measures on
M
with the space
C^{\infty}(M
) of arbitrarily often differentiable real valued functions on
M
. This is done by the linear isomorphism

\imath_{\mu}:C^{\infty}(M)\longrightarrow S^{\infty}(M),\qquad f\longmapsto f\;\mu,

(1)

whose inverse is given by the Radon-Nikodym derivative w.r.t.
\mu
. We also consider the codimension one subspaces

\displaystyle{S}^{\infty}_{0}(M)\;

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\;\left\{\nu\in S^{\infty}(M)\;:\;\int_{M}\nu=0\right\},

(2)

\displaystyle C^{\infty}_{0}(M,\mu)\;

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\;\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(M)\;:\;\int_{M}f\;d\mu=0\right\},

so that obviously,
\imath_{\mu}
from (
1
) restricts to an isomorphism of
C^{\infty}_{0}(M,\mu)
and
S^{\infty}_{0}(M)
. Morover,
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
is the image of the intersection of the affine hyperplane
1+C^{\infty}_{0}(M,\mu)
with the convex open set of positive functions.

As
C^{\infty}(M)
has a canonical Fréchet space structure
[
6
]
, it follows that
\imath_{\mu}
induces a Fréchet space structure on
S^{\infty}(M)
and
S^{\infty}_{0}(M)
, and
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
has the structure of an open convex set in an affine hyperplane. It is straightforward to verify that these Fréchet structures are independent of the choice of
\mu
. Therefore, there is a canonical identification

T_{\mu}P^{\infty}_{+}(M)\cong S^{\infty}_{0}(M),\qquad TP^{\infty}_{+}(M)\cong P^{\infty}_{+}(M)\times S^{\infty}_{0}(M),

(3)

and the canonical flat connection on the vector space
S^{\infty}(M)
restricts to a canonical flat affine connection
\nabla^{({\rm m})}
on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
, called the
mixture connection
. It is characterized by the fact that the constant vector fields
(A_{\mu}):=(\mu,\nu_{0})
are
\nabla^{({\rm m})}
-parallel for each
\nu_{0}\in S^{\infty}_{0}(M)
. In particular, the
\nabla^{({\rm m})}
-geodesics are the straight line segments, and due to the convexity, any two elements in
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
can be joined by a unique
\nabla^{({\rm m})}
-geodesic.

3
Regular metrics on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)

A
Riemannian metric on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)

is a collection of inner products
{\mathcal{G}}=({\mathcal{G}}_{\mu})_{\mu\in P^{\infty}_{+}(M)}
on each
T_{\mu}P^{\infty}_{+}(M)\cong S^{\infty}_{0}(M)
which depends smoothly on
\mu
, in the sense that smooth vector fields
A,B
on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
(i.e., smooth families
A=(A_{\mu})_{\mu\in P^{\infty}_{+}(M)}
,
B=(B_{\mu})_{\mu\in P^{\infty}_{+}(M)}\in S^{\infty}_{0}(M)
) yield a smooth function on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)

\mu\longmapsto{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(A_{\mu},B_{\mu}).

Example: The Fisher-Rao metric

Given
\mu\in P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
, we use (
1
) to identify
T_{\mu}P^{\infty}_{+}(M)\cong S^{\infty}_{0}(M)\cong C^{\infty}_{0}(M,\mu)\subset L^{2}(M,\mu)
, where the latter is the Hilbert space of square integrable functions. The
Fisher-Rao metric on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)

is then the restricton of the inner product on
L^{2}(M,\mu)
to
C^{\infty}_{0}(M,\mu)
under this identification. That is,

\llangle A,B\rrangle^{\rm FR}_{\mu}:=\int_{M}\frac{dA_{\mu}}{d\mu}\;\frac{dB_{\mu}}{d\mu}\;d\mu.

(4)

The Fisher-Rao metric has very natural properties; e.g. it is invariant under diffeomorphisms of
M
and is in fact (up to constant rescaling) the only Riemannian metric on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
with this property
[
4
]
.

However, there are other important choices of such metrics, for instance the
Otto metric
which we shall discuss in more detail in section
5
. For the moment, we shall stay fairly general in our discussion, but always keep the Otto metric in mind as our main application.

Definition 1

A
regular Riemannian metric on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)

is a metric of the form

{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(A,B):=\llangle\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(A_{\mu}),B_{\mu}\rrangle^{\rm FR}_{\mu},

(5)

where
\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}:S^{\infty}_{0}(M)\to S^{\infty}_{0}(M)
is an automorphism of Fréchet spaces.

Evidently, this is only well defined if for each
\nu\in S^{\infty}_{0}(M)
the expression
(\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu})(\nu)
depends smoothly on
\mu
, and if

\llangle\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(\nu^{1}),\nu^{2}\rrangle^{\rm FR}_{\mu}=\llangle\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(\nu^{2}),\nu^{1}\rrangle^{\rm FR}_{\mu},\qquad\llangle\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(\nu),\nu\rrangle^{\rm FR}_{\mu}>0,\;\nu\neq 0.

(6)

We also shall use the notation for the Gâteaux derivative

\partial_{\nu^{1}}\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(\nu^{2}):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu+t\nu^{1}}(\nu^{2})\in S^{\infty}_{0}(M).

(7)

4
The
\alpha
-connections of a regular metric on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)

For a Riemannian metric
{\mathcal{G}}
on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
its
covariant mixture derivative
\nabla^{({\rm m})}{\mathcal{G}}
is defined as the tensor

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A;B,C)\;

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\;-\left(\nabla^{({\rm m})}_{A}{\mathcal{G}}\right)(B,C)

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;-A\left({\mathcal{G}}(B,C)\right)+{\mathcal{G}}\left(\nabla^{({\rm m})}_{A}B,C\right)+{\mathcal{G}}\left(B,\nabla^{({\rm m})}_{A}C\right).

It is straightforward to verify that this expression is tensorial in all entries, so that
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A;B,C)_{\mu}
only depends on
A_{\mu},B_{\mu}
and
C_{\mu}
. Furthermore, it is obviously symmetric in the last two entries.

Lemma 1

We have the identity

{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A;B,C)={\mathcal{G}}\left({\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A,B),C\right),

(9)

where

{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A,B)_{\mu}=(\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu})^{-1}\left(\frac{d{A_{\mu}}}{d\mu}\;\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(B_{\mu})-\partial_{A_{\mu}}\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(B_{\mu})\right),

(10)

using the notation (
7
).

Proof

Let
\mu\in P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
and
a,b,c\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M,\mu)
, so that the constant vector fields
A:\equiv a\mu
,
B:\equiv b\mu
and
C:\equiv c\mu
on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
are
\nabla^{({\rm m})}
-parallel, i.e.,
\nabla^{({\rm m})}_{A}B=\nabla^{({\rm m})}_{A}C=0
. Then (
8
) implies

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A;B,C)\;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;-A\llangle B,C\rrangle^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}=-\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\llangle\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{(1+ta)\mu}(B),C\rrangle^{\rm FR}_{(1+ta)\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;-\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\int_{M}\frac{d\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{(1+ta)\mu}(B)}{d((1+ta)\mu)}\frac{dC}{d((1+ta)\mu)}\;d((1+ta)\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\int_{M}\left(a\;\frac{d\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(B)}{d\mu}-\frac{d(\partial_{a\mu}\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(B))}{d\mu}\right)\frac{dC}{d\mu}\;d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left\llangle\left(\frac{dA_{\mu}}{d\mu}\;\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(B_{\mu})-\partial_{A_{\mu}}\Phi^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu}(B_{\mu})\right),C_{\mu}\right\rrangle_{\mu}^{\rm FR},

which together with (
5
) implies (
10
).

Definition 2

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a regular metric on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
, given by (
5
) for some map
\phi_{\mu}^{\mathcal{G}}:S^{\infty}_{0}(M)\to S^{\infty}_{0}(M)
. Then for
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
we define the
\alpha
-connection of
\mathcal{G}

by

\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},\alpha)}_{A}B:=\nabla^{({\rm m})}_{A}B-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A,B).

(11)

Observe that according to (
11
) we have
{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A,B)=\nabla^{({\rm m})}_{A}B-\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},1)}_{A}B
, whence (
11
) is equivalent to

\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},\alpha)}_{A}B\;=\;\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\;\nabla^{({\rm m})}_{A}B+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\;\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},1)}_{A}B.

We now collect the following properties of these
\alpha
-connections.

Theorem 4.1

1.

Equation (
11
) defines indeed a connection of
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
.

2.

The torsion of
\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},\alpha)}
is given by

{\rm Tor}^{({\mathcal{G}},\alpha)}(A,B)\;=\;\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\;{\rm Tor}^{({\mathcal{G}},1)}(A,B)\;=\;\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\;\big({\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}(B,A)-{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A,B)\big).

(12)

That is, apart from
\nabla^{(-1)}=\nabla^{({\rm m})}
, either all or none of the
\nabla^{(\alpha)}
,
\alpha\neq-1
, have torsion.

3.

\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},\alpha)}
and
\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},-\alpha)}
are conjugate w.r.t.
{\mathcal{G}}
, i.e., we have the identity

A\;{\mathcal{G}}(B,C)={\mathcal{G}}\left(\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},\alpha)}_{A}B,C\right)+{\mathcal{G}}\left(B,\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},-\alpha)}_{A}C\right)

for all vector fields
A,B,C
on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
. In particular,
\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},0)}
is compatible with
{\mathcal{G}}
, and the connection
\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},1)}
has vanishing curvature.

4.

The Levi-Civita connection of
{\mathcal{G}}
is given as

\nabla^{LC}_{A}B=\nabla^{({\rm m})}_{A}B-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A,B)-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}(B,A)+\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A,B),

(13)

where
{\mathcal{D}}^{\mathcal{G}}
is the symmetric tensor determined by the identity

{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{G}}}(A,B),C)\;=\;{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}(C;A,B).

(14)

Proof

Since
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}
is tensorial, (
9
) immediately implies that
{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}
is tensorial in both entries as well. From this, it is straightforward to verify the connection properties for
\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},\alpha)}
.

The remaining statements follow by straightforward calculations, cf.
[
1
, ch. 3]
. In particular,
\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},1)}
is conjugate to
\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},-1)}=\nabla^{({\rm m})}
w.r.t.
{\mathcal{G}}
and
\nabla^{({\rm m})}
is flat. Being conjugate to a flat connection,
\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},1)}
has vanishing curvature as well.

Corollary 1

For a regular metric
{\mathcal{G}}
on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
the following are equivalent.

1.

\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},\alpha)}
is torsion free for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

2.

\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},0)}
coincides with the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{LC}
of
{\mathcal{G}}
.

3.

\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},0)}
and
\nabla^{LC}
have the same geodesics.

Proof

As
\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},0)}
is compatible with
{\mathcal{G}}
, it coincides with
\nabla^{LC}
iff
\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},0)}
is torsion free, which by (
12
) is equivalent to the first statement. So the first two statements are equivalent, and second statement obviously implies the third. For the converse, let
\gamma_{t}
be a common geodesic of
\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},0)}
and
\nabla^{LC}
. Then

0=(\nabla^{LC}_{\dot{\gamma}_{t}}\dot{\gamma}_{t})-(\nabla^{({\mathcal{G}},0)}_{\dot{\gamma}_{t}}\dot{\gamma}_{t})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{LC})}}{{=}}-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}(\dot{\gamma}_{t},\dot{\gamma}_{t})+\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{\mathcal{G}}(\dot{\gamma}_{t},\dot{\gamma}_{t}).

Thus for any tangent vector
C
,

0={\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}(\dot{\gamma}_{t},\dot{\gamma}_{t}),C)-{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{D}}^{\mathcal{G}}(\dot{\gamma}_{t},\dot{\gamma}_{t}),C)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{Tor-alpha}),(\ref{DG-tensor})}}{{=}}{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}(\dot{\gamma}_{t};\dot{\gamma}_{t},C)-{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}(C;\dot{\gamma}_{t},\dot{\gamma}_{t}).

Since this holds for
all
geodesics and hence for
all tangent vectors
\dot{\gamma}_{t}
, polarization implies

0={\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}(A;B,C)+{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}(B;A,C)-2{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}(C;A,B).

From this, standard tensor calculations imply that
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathcal{G}}
is totally symmetric and hence,
{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}
is symmetric by (
9
), so that (
12
) implies the first statement.

Example: The Fisher-Rao metric

According to (
5
) the Fisher-Rao metric is a regular metric with
\Phi^{\rm FR}_{\mu}={\mathbf{1}}
for all
\mu
. Thus, it follows that
\partial_{A}\Phi^{\rm FR}_{\mu}=0
, whence letting
A_{\mu}:=a\mu
,
B_{\mu}:=b\mu
and
C_{\mu}:=c\mu
we have according to (
10
)

{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm FR}_{\mu}(a\mu,b\mu)=\frac{dA_{\mu}}{d\mu}\;B_{\mu}=ab\;\mu={\mathcal{K}}^{\rm FR}_{\mu}(b\mu,a\mu),

(15)

so that by (
8
),

{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm FR}_{\mu}(a\mu,b\mu,c\mu)=\llangle ab\;\mu,c\;\mu\rrangle_{\mu}^{\rm FR}\;=\;\int_{M}abc\;{d\mu}.

Thus,
{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm FR}
coincides with the classical
Amari-Chentsov tensor
on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
.

That is, the
\alpha
-connections
\nabla^{({\rm FR},\alpha)}
from (
11
) for the Fisher-Rao metric coincide with the standard
\alpha
-connections defined e.g. in
[
1
, ch. 3]
or
[
3
]
. They are torsion free as
{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm FR}
is symmetric by (
15
). In particular,
\nabla^{({\rm FR},0)}=\nabla^{\rm LC}
is the Levi-Civita-Connection of the Fisher-Rao metric, and
\nabla^{({\rm FR},1)}=:\nabla^{({\rm e})}
is the
exponential connection
, i.e., the flat connection conjugate to
\nabla^{({\rm m})}

[
1
]
,
[
3
]
.

Thus, the
\alpha
-connections from (
11
) may be regarded as a canonical generalization of the
\alpha
-connections of the Fisher-Rao metric to arbitrary regular metrics.

5
The Otto metric

We shall now present another important regular Riemannian metric on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
, called the
Otto metric
, as it was first introduced by F. Otto in
[
7
]
. The dual connection of
\nabla^{({\rm m})}
w.r.t. this metric was considered by the first named author in
[
2
]
, where it was denoted by
\nabla^{({\rm e}_{1})}
.

Let
(M,{\rm g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary. We shall use the notation
{\rm g}(\xi,\zeta)=:\langle\xi,\zeta\rangle
for
\xi,\zeta\in T_{x}M
. Let
{\mathcal{T}}(M)
denote the Lie algebra of vector fields on
M
. Given
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the
gradient vector field of
f
(w.r.t.
{\rm g}
)
is the vector field
{\rm grad}(f)\in{\mathcal{T}}(M)
determined by the property

\langle{\rm grad}\,f,X\rangle=X(f)\qquad\text{for any vector field $X\in\mathcal{T}(M)$}.

(16)

In order to construct the Otto metric, we need some properties of the
divergence of vector fields
, and the
Laplace operator
, of which we only collect the most important facts; for a more comprehensive explanation, cf.
[
2
]
.

Proposition 1

Let
(M,{\rm g})
be as above and
\mu\in P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
.

1.

For a vector field
X\in{\mathcal{T}}(X)
there is a unique function
{\rm div}_{\mu}(X)\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M,\mu)
, called the divergence of
X
w.r.t.
\mu
, satisfying

\int_{M}X(f)\;d\mu=-\int_{M}f\;{\rm div}_{\mu}(X)\;d\mu\qquad\text{for all $f\in C^{\infty}(M)$}.

(17)

2.

For
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
define the
\mu
-Laplacian of
f
as

\Delta_{\mu}f:={\rm div}_{\mu}({\rm grad}f).

(18)

Then
\Delta_{\mu}f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M,\mu)
and
\ker\Delta_{\mu}
consists of the constant functions.

3.

The restriction
\Delta_{\mu}:C^{\infty}_{0}(M,\mu)\to C^{\infty}_{0}(M,\mu)
is an isomorphism of Fréchet spaces.

It follows that

-\int_{M}(\Delta_{\mu}f)\;g\;d\mu=\int_{M}({\rm grad}f)(g)\;d\mu=\int_{M}\langle{\rm grad}f,{\rm grad}g\rangle\;d\mu.

(19)

With this notation, we define the
Otto metric on
P^{\infty}_{+}(M)

by

\llangle a\mu,b\mu\rrangle^{\rm O}_{\mu}:=\int_{M}\left\langle{\rm grad}(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}a),{\rm grad}\left(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}b\right)\right\rangle\;d\mu.

(20)

We assert that this is a regular metric in the sense of Definition
1
. Bilinearity, symmetry are nonnegativity are immediate. Also, note that
\llangle\nu,\nu\rrangle_{\mu}^{\rm O}=0
iff
\nu=c\mu
for some constant
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
. But then,
\nu\in S^{\infty}_{0}(M)
implies
c=0
, whence
\nu=0
. That is, the Otto metric defines an inner product on each tangent space.

Moreover, comparing (
19
) and (
20
), we can rewrite

\llangle a\mu,b\mu\rrangle_{\mu}^{\rm O}=-\int_{M}(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}a)\;b\;d\mu=\left\llangle-(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}a)\;\mu,b\mu\right\rrangle_{\mu}^{\rm FR}.

(21)

Thus, the Otto metric can be written in the form (
5
) with

\Phi^{\rm O}_{\mu}(a\mu):=-(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}a)\;\mu\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\Phi^{\rm O}_{\mu}=-\imath_{\mu}\circ\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}\circ\imath_{\mu}^{-1}

(22)

with
\imath_{\mu}
from (
1
), so that
\Phi^{\rm O}_{\mu}
is the composition of Fréchet space isomorphisms, showing its regularity. Then the tensor fields
{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm O}
from (
10
),
{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm O}
from (
9
), the torsion of
\nabla^{({\rm O},\alpha)}
and the Levi-Civita connection of
\llangle\cdot,\cdot\rrangle^{\rm O}
are as follows.

Theorem 5.1

Let
(M,{\rm g})
as before and
\mu\in P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
. Then for
a\mu,b\mu\in S^{\infty}_{0}(M)\cong T_{\mu}P^{\infty}_{+}(M)
and
A=a\mu,B=b\mu
we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm O}_{\mu}(a\mu,b\mu)\;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm FR}_{\mu}(a\mu,b\mu)+\langle{\rm grad}\;(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}b),{\rm grad}\;a\rangle\;\mu.

(23)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm O}_{\mu}(a\mu,b\mu,c\mu)\;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;-\left\llangle a\mu,\;\Delta_{\mu}\langle{\rm grad}(\Delta^{-1}_{\mu}b),{\rm grad}(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}c)\rangle\;\mu\right\rrangle^{\rm O}.

(24)

\displaystyle{\rm Tor}^{({\rm O},\alpha)}_{\mu}(a\mu,b\mu)\;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\Big(\langle{\rm grad}\;(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}a),{\rm grad}\;b\rangle

(25)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\langle{\rm grad}\;(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}b),{\rm grad}\;a\rangle\Big)\;\mu.

\displaystyle\nabla^{LC}_{A}B\;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\nabla^{({\rm m})}_{A}B-\frac{1}{2}\Big(2ab\;+\;\Delta_{\mu}\langle{\rm grad}(\Delta^{-1}_{\mu}a),{\rm grad}(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}b)\rangle

(26)

\displaystyle\;\;\langle{\rm grad}\;(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}a),{\rm grad}\;b\rangle\;+\;\langle{\rm grad}\;a,{\rm grad}\;(\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}b)\rangle\Big)\;\mu.

Proof

For the proof, we shall use the identity
[
2
, Proposition 1, (8)]

{\rm div}_{\mu}(X)={\rm div}_{\mu_{\rm g}}(X)+\left\langle X,{\rm grad}\;\left(\ln\frac{d\mu}{d\mu_{\rm g}}\right)\right\rangle

(27)

which – substituting
X={\rm grad}\;h
for any
h\in C^{\infty}(M)
– yields

\Delta_{\mu}h=\Delta_{\mu_{\rm g}}h+\left\langle{\rm grad}\;h,{\rm grad}\;\left(\ln\frac{d\mu}{d\mu_{\rm g}}\right)\right\rangle.

(28)

Let
\mu:=\rho\mu_{\rm g}
and
\mu_{t}:=(1+ta)\mu=(1+ta)\rho\mu_{\rm g}=:\rho_{t}\mu_{\rm g}
. Furthermore, let
h_{t}:=-\displaystyle{\Delta_{\mu_{t}}^{-1}\frac{d(b\mu)}{d\mu_{t}}}
, so that
\Phi^{\rm O}_{\mu_{t}}(b\mu)=h_{t}\mu_{t}
and
h_{0}=-\Delta_{\mu}^{-1}b
. Then by (
10
),

\displaystyle\Phi^{\rm O}_{\mu}{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm O}_{\mu}(a\mu,b\mu)\;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;ah_{0}\;\mu-\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}(h_{t}\mu_{t})=-\left(\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}h_{t}\right)\;\mu.

(29)

Now

\displaystyle-\frac{b}{1+ta}\;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{d(b\mu)}{d\mu_{t}}\;=\;\Delta_{\mu_{t}}h_{t}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{Delta-mu})}}{{=}}\;\Delta_{\mu_{\rm g}}h_{t}+\left\langle{\rm grad}\;h_{t},{\rm grad}\;(\ln\rho_{t})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mu_{\rm g}}h_{t}+\left\langle{\rm grad}\;h_{t},{\rm grad}\;(\ln\rho)\right\rangle+\left\langle{\rm grad}\;h_{t},{\rm grad}\;(\ln(1+ta))\right\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{Delta-mu})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;\Delta_{\mu}h_{t}+\langle{\rm grad}\;h_{t},{\rm grad}\;(\ln(1+ta))\rangle.

Evaluating the derivative
\frac{d}{dt}
of this equation at
t=0
yields

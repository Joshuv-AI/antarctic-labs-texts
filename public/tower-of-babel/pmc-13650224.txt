Effectiveness and Tolerability of Low-Dose Micronized Isotretinoin in Patients with Severe Recalcitrant Nodular Facial Acne in India: a Real-World Retrospective Study Using Electronic Medical Records

ABSTRACT
IntroductionSevere recalcitrant nodular facial acne is a chronic inflammation with considerable psychosocial impact. Conventional oral isotretinoin is effective, but optimal absorption necessitates dietary compliance. Micronized formulations improve bioavailability and allow flexible dosing. This study evaluated the effectiveness and tolerability of low-dose micronized isotretinoin.MethodsThis real-world, retrospective, multicenter, observational study analyzed aggregated and anonymized electronic medical records (EMRs) collected over approximately 12 months from patients aged ≥ 12 years with severe recalcitrant nodular facial acne who were treated with low-dose micronized isotretinoin for ≥ 8 weeks. The primary endpoint was improvement in acne severity [in terms of Investigator’s Global Assessment (IGA) score] from baseline to 8 ± 2 weeks. Secondary endpoints included improvement at the end of treatment, investigator-graded response, and incidence of adverse events (AEs; serious AEs [SAEs]). Exploratory endpoints assessed patient-graded response and change in nodular/total lesion count.ResultsAmong 305 patients (86.89% adults; 13.11% adolescents), 59.67% had an IGA score of 3 and 40.33% had an IGA score of 4 at baseline. Post-treatment, the overall IGA score reduced significantly from 3.40 ± 0.49 at baseline to 2.11 ± 0.79 at 8 ± 2 weeks (37.94% reduction; p < 0.001), 1.30 ± 0.82 at 12 ± 2 weeks (61.76% reduction; p < 0.001), and 0.53 ± 0.73 at 16 ± 2 weeks (84.41% reduction; p < 0.001). By 16 ± 2 weeks, 91.81% patients were graded ‘clear’/‘almost clear’ (IGA score ≤ 1), 67.87% had ‘excellent’ investigator-graded response, and 65.25% had ‘very good’ self-graded response. After 16 ± 2 weeks, significant reductions were observed in nodular lesion counts (from 7.06 ± 9.22 to 0.38 ± 1.29; 94.62% reduction; p < 0.001) and total lesions (from 32.25 ± 36.95 to 2.46 ± 4.63; 92.37% reduction; p < 0.001). Six (1.97%) patients experienced AEs; no SAE was observed.ConclusionIn this real-world EMR-based study, low-dose micronized isotretinoin was found to be clinically effective and well tolerated in patients with severe recalcitrant nodular facial acne, achieving rapid and sustained improvements in lesion counts and acne severity.Trial registrationClinical Trials Registry-India, identifier CTRI/2024/07/070813.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s13555-026-01841-8.

Introduction

Acne vulgaris is a chronic inflammation of the pilosebaceous unit that often leads to scarring and significant psychological distress [1, 2]. According to the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021, the age-standardized prevalence among individuals aged 10–24 years was 9790.5 per 100,000 [3]. A more recent global epidemiological study involving over 50,000 individuals aged ≥ 16 years reported an overall acne prevalence of 20.5%, peaking at 28.3% in the 16–24 age group [4]. In India, a prospective observational study of 24,056 adults aged > 25 years reported a prevalence of 0.74%, with a predominance in females and a high incidence of inflammatory lesions and facial scarring [5].

Severe recalcitrant nodular acne is a highly inflammatory form of acne characterized by numerous comedones, papules, pustules, and nodulocystic lesions. It also has a greater likelihood of scarring. Though topical therapies are the mainstay treatments for mild-to-moderate acne, systemic therapies are required for moderate-to-severe forms of the condition [6]. Oral isotretinoin (13-cis-retinoic acid), a retinoid derivative of vitamin A, is currently the only therapy for severe recalcitrant nodular acne approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (US FDA). It is a nonhormonal, nonantimicrobial agent that targets all four major pathogenic mechanisms of acne: excess sebum production, follicular hyperkeratinization, bacterial colonization, and inflammation. Isotretinoin reduces sebaceous gland activity, alters the cutaneous microbiome, promotes keratinocyte shedding, and mitigates inflammation [7, 8]. Despite its proven efficacy, conventional isotretinoin’s lipophilic nature limits its aqueous solubility, making gastrointestinal absorption highly dependent on concurrent intake of high-fat meals. Absorption may decrease by up to 60% in the absence of such dietary intake, potentially compromising therapeutic outcomes [9, 10]. Since long-term remission correlates with cumulative systemic exposure, inconsistent dietary habits, especially among adolescents and busy adults, can hinder treatment success.

To address these limitations, newer formulations such as lidose and micronized isotretinoin have been developed. These offer improved bioavailability and allow flexible dosing without strict dietary requirements, thereby improving patient compliance. Among these, micronized isotretinoin demonstrates superior absorption due to reduced particle size, which increases the dissolution rate and systemic availability. Additionally, lower doses of micronized isotretinoin may reduce adverse effects, ultimately leading to better compliance [11–13].

While clinical trials have established, the safety and efficacy of lidose-isotretinoin in the treatment of severe recalcitrant nodular acne [14–16], clinical evidence supporting the efficacy of micronized isotretinoin has primarily focused on moderate-to-severe acne only in a regulated trial setting [13, 17–22]. Data specifically evaluating its use in severe (and moderate) recalcitrant nodular acne remain limited in real-world settings. Given the pharmacokinetic advantages of micronized formulations and the need for effective, well-tolerated therapies in this population, our study aimed to assess the effectiveness and tolerability of low-dose micronized isotretinoin in adolescent and adult patients with severe recalcitrant nodular facial acne in a real-world clinical setting.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

This was a real-world, retrospective, multicentric, observational study using aggregated and anonymized data from electronic medical records (EMRs) retrieved from MedSygn®, a practice management software database adhering to the data privacy policy of India. Data were retrieved on patients treated with low-dose micronized isotretinoin for severe recalcitrant nodular facial acne by dermatologists in routine clinical practice. EMR data were collected for approximately 12 months from 11 centers situated across 8 cities (Bangalore, Lucknow, Hyderabad, Chennai, Mumbai, Kolkata, Guntur, and Delhi) in India. Available data were retrieved for up to 20 weeks after treatment initiation.

The data collection was done in accordance with the protocol and applicable ethical and regulatory guidelines, including the Declaration of Helsinki, Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)-National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants (2017), Indian Good Clinical Practice (GCP), and International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) GCP. The study protocol was approved by the Royal Pune Independent Ethics Committee (IEC; reference no. RPIEC210824 dated August 20, 2024). An informed consent form (ICF) waiver was applied and granted by the EC in accordance with the ICMR 2017 Guidelines for Biomedical Research because the study involved procurement of retrospective patient treatment data in anonymized format. The study was registered with the Clinical Trials Registry-India (CTRI/2024/07/070813) and was conducted in compliance with all applicable regulatory and ethical guidelines.

Patients and Treatment

The study included EMRs of male and female patients aged ≥ 12 years diagnosed with severe recalcitrant nodular facial acne and who had completed at least 8 weeks of therapy with low-dose micronized isotretinoin (Sotret® NF available as 8 mg/16 mg/24 mg capsules) prescribed by the treating dermatologists. Pregnant and lactating females, patients who had been administered conventional or micronized isotretinoin in the past, and those with known hypersensitivity to isotretinoin were excluded. As per the EMRs, patients were prescribed micronized isotretinoin at 0.4–0.8 mg/kg/day for 15–20 weeks.

Study Endpoints

The primary endpoint was improvement in acne severity based on Investigator’s Global Assessment (IGA) scale (0: clear; 1: almost clear; 2: mild; 3: moderate; 4: severe) from baseline to 8 ± 2 weeks of treatment. Secondary endpoints included the improvement in acne severity based on IGA scale and response to treatment as assessed and graded by investigator (excellent: > 75% clearing; good: 50–75% clearing; fair: 25–50% clearing; poor: < 25% clearing) from baseline to end of treatment in patients stratified by treatment duration and the proportion of patients with adverse events (AEs) and serious AEs (SAEs). Exploratory endpoints included change in nodular lesion count and total lesion count and response to treatment graded by the patient (1: very good, 2: good, 3: average, 4: poor) from baseline to end of treatment.

Sample Size and Statistical Analysis

A sample size of approximately 300 patients was calculated to detect a 26% difference in IGA score from baseline to week 8 of treatment [15], with a 95% confidence interval (CI) and ~5% margin of error, accounting for 10% incomplete medical records.

Statistical methods were based on the ICH E9 document ‘Statistical Principles for Clinical Trials;’ all analyses were performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS; version 29.0.2.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA; Bonferroni adjusted pairwise based on estimated marginal means) was used to compare IGA scores and lesion counts (nodular and total) across data accrual points. Missing data were handled using last observation carried forward (LOCF). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline Demographic and Clinical Characteristics of Patients

Between April 2023 and February 2024, 315 EMRs were retrieved, of which 10 did not fulfill the study eligibility criteria. Of the 305 EMRs included for analysis (Fig. 1), 195 (63.93%) were of females and 110 (36.07%) were of males. Stratified by age, 86.89% were adults (age ≥ 18 years) and 13.11% were adolescents (age: 12–17 years). Overall, 49.84% patients were in the 18–24-year age group. The mean age was 24.15 ± 7.07 years, and the mean weight was 62.20 ± 12.23 kg. Prior to treatment initiation, 59.67% of patients had an IGA score of 3 and 40.33% had an IGA score of 4. Prescription Pattern of Micronized Isotretinoin

The prescribing patterns of micronized isotretinoin showed that most patients (60.33%) initially received 16 mg dose, followed by 8 mg (30.16%) and 24 mg (9.51%). Subsequently, the proportion of patients on an 8-mg dose increased, while the use of 24 mg remained comparatively low and comparable at all visit points (Table S1). Regarding administration frequency, nearly all prescriptions were for once daily dosing at baseline and 8 ± 2 weeks. This regimen mostly shifted toward alternate-day dosing (from 0% at baseline to 11.48% at 16 ± 2 weeks) (Table S2).

Tolerability Evaluation: Incidence of AEs and SAEs

Eight mild AEs (dryness, headache, itching, mild acne, new lesions, and mild pain) were reported by 6 of 305 (1.97%) patients. No SAEs were reported in EMRs.

Discussion

Low-dose micronized isotretinoin represents a novel formulation of an established therapeutic agent for severe recalcitrant nodular acne. Increased bioavailability aided by reduced particle size and superior absorption, non-dependency on strict dietary requirements, and effective outcome with lower doses have established it as a better treatment option. However, there are limited data proving the effectiveness of low-dose micronized isotretinoin in the treatment of severe recalcitrant nodular acne, especially in real-world settings.

To strengthen the existing evidence supporting the effectiveness and tolerability of low-dose micronized isotretinoin, a retrospective evaluation was conducted using EMR data from 305 patients diagnosed with severe recalcitrant nodular facial acne who received low-dose micronized isotretinoin. The study cohort predominantly comprised patients of both genders and individuals aged 24 years or younger, consistent with demographic trends reported in the literature [3–5]. Initial treatment typically involved a 16-mg daily dose, followed by a reduction to 8 mg, likely reflecting dose de-escalation in response to clinical improvement.

In the present study, mean IGA score decreased by approximately 38% at 8 ± 2 weeks and > 80% by 16 ± 2 weeks, with > 90% of patients achieving an IGA score of 0–1 (“clear”/“almost clear”) at the end of treatment. Similar findings have been reported across several clinical trials and real-world studies involving micronized isotretinoin. Del Rosso and Kircik (2024) [23] showed that once-daily micronized isotretinoin administered without food led to > 95% of patients achieving an IGA of score 0 or 1 after 20 weeks, with minimal mucocutaneous side effects. Earlier, Strauss et al. (2001) [17] compared standard and micronized isotretinoin and reported that the latter achieved equivalent efficacy at lower doses with reduced incidence of hypertriglyceridemia and mucocutaneous dryness. Reports by Faghihi et al. (2017) and Lee et al. (2011) also presented comparable efficacy of low- and conventional-dose isotretinoin in reducing the acne lesion count and severity [21, 22]. In the present study, only eight mild AEs were reported, reaffirming the effectiveness and tolerability of micronized isotretinoin at reduced daily doses (0.4—0.8 mg/kg/day) and without dietary restrictions. However, it should be mentioned here that in real-world settings, under-reporting of AEs is often a major limitation. Nevertheless, the current findings, together with the available literature, strengthen the clinical rationale for adopting lower-dose isotretinoin regimens, especially in populations where tolerability and adherence are critical considerations. In the Indian setting, where dietary variability and adherence challenges are common, the availability of a food-independent isotretinoin formulation may lead to improved compliance and sustained therapeutic outcomes.

In our study, both investigators and patients reported high satisfaction with low-dose micronized isotretinoin therapy. Investigator assessments showed that the proportion of patients with an “excellent” response increased steadily throughout treatment, reaching over two-thirds by the end of follow-up. Likewise, patient ratings improved in parallel, with nearly two-thirds of patients grading their response as “very good” at the final assessment. These findings reflect meaningful improvements not only in clinical acne severity but also in patients' perceptions of outcomes and well-being. These observations were similar to those of other studies—Del Rosso et al. [16] reported high clinical clearance rates and low relapse rates with once-daily micronized isotretinoin, with both investigators and patients expressing high satisfaction and mild side effects. Zaenglein et al. [24] similarly reported improved quality of life and strong agreement between patient and investigator satisfaction in a phase IV study of micronized isotretinoin. Amichai et al. [12] showed that low or intermittent dosing led to high satisfaction among both patients and physicians, highlighting meaningful improvements with fewer side effects. Collectively, these studies reinforce that low-dose isotretinoin regimens provide marked benefits and satisfaction for both clinicians and patients.

One of the main strengths of the current study is its large, multicentric design and real-world data collection from EMRs, providing insights into the effectiveness and tolerability of low-dose micronized isotretinoin across diverse patient populations in India. The study included both clinical parameters and patient- and investigator-reported outcomes, allowing for a comprehensive evaluation of treatment effectiveness, satisfaction, and tolerability. Utilization of multiple endpoints, robust statistical methods, and stratified analysis by severity and treatment duration further strengthen the reliability and relevance of the findings. Incorporation of adequate patient data ensured sufficient statistical power to detect meaningful differences in outcomes, and multicentric inclusion enhanced the generalizability of the results. However, several limitations, inherent to real-world EMR-based studies should be acknowledged. The retrospective nature of the study introduces potential for selection and documentation bias, lack of data on adherence limits the use of an important covariate, and possible under-reporting of AEs may affect the conclusions. The single-arm observational design limits the scope of comparison of the study medication with other forms of isotretinoin or other anti-acne medications. Also, the definitions for exploratory endpoints, such as nodular/total lesion counts, might differ among dermatologists. A prospective design would help to validate optimal dosing protocols, examine predictors of response and relapse, and support economic evaluations such as cost-effectiveness and healthcare resource utilization to confirm treatment efficacy. These factors will contribute to building clinical guidelines and personalized management strategies for severe recalcitrant nodular facial acne, strengthening the evidence base for micronized isotretinoin and improving outcomes in routine practice.

Conclusion

This real-world, retrospective, multicenter, EMR-based study demonstrated that low-dose micronized isotretinoin was effective and well tolerated for the treatment of severe recalcitrant nodular facial acne with IGA scores of 3–4 in routine clinical practice. Most patients experienced significant reductions in IGA scores upon treatment, reflected by statistically significant reductions in both nodular and total lesion counts, and high satisfaction was reported by both patients and doctors.

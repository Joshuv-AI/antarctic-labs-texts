Illuminating dark matter admixed in neutron stars with simultaneous mass–radius constraints
Nai-Bo Zhang 1 , Bao-An Li 2 , Jia-Yu Zhang 1 , Wei-Na Shen 1 , and Hui Zhang 1

Abstract
We investigate how simultaneous mass and radius measurements of massive neutron stars (NSs) can help constrain properties of dark matter (DM) possibly admixed in them. Within a fermionic DM model that interacts only through gravitation, along with a well-constrained nuclear matter equation of state, we show that the simultaneous mass and radius measurement of PSRJ0740+6620 reduces the uncertainty of DM central energy density by more than 50% compared to the results obtained from using the two observables independently, while other DM parameters remain unconstrained. Additionally, we find that the DM fraction f D f_{D} should be smaller than 2% when constrained by the observed NS maximum mass alone, and it could be even smaller than 0.3% with the simultaneous measurement of mass and radius, supporting the conclusion that only a small amount of DM exists in DM admixed neutron stars (DANS).

Alternatively, DM properties can be constrained indirectly. Neutron stars (NSs), densest visible objects in the universe, provide an ideal environment to study the fundamental physics of supradense matter, including the effects of DM. As DM particles pass through a NS, their interactions with the dense core can lead to the capture of DM, forming a dark matter admixed neutron star (DANS). The presence of DM may modify various NS properties and influence the gravitational wave signatures produced during binary NS mergers. These effects can potentially be identified through NS observations (see a recent review in ref.

II
Theoretical framework

The EOS for both high-density NM and DM remains highly uncertain. For instance, refs.
Grippa 2025
;
Burgio 2021
summarize the possible EOSs for both NM and DM. Among the various DM candidates, non-annihilating WIMPs are considered the most generous and popular choice. In the present work, we choose the fermionic DM model. We use the same meta-model EOS for NM as in refs.
Zhang 2025
. Once the EOSs are determined, the properties of DANS can be obtained by solving the traditional Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkov (TOV) equations or the two fluid TOV equations. The effects of DM on the properties of DANS can be analyzed either through Bayesian methods
Rutherford 2025
;
Liu 2025
, which can handle statistically many parameters and provide insights into their interrelations, or by selecting representative parameter sets to model DANS directly
Xiang 2014
;
Routaray 2023
;
Grippa 2024
. The Bayesian analysis approach allows for a probabilistic view of the parameter space, while the use of representative parameter sets offers a direct connection between the observational data and specific model parameters.

II.2
The EOS of DM

As mentioned earlier, numerous models and candidates have been proposed to explain the nature of DM. In this work, we treat the DM particles as fermions that primarily interact through gravity. Both vector and scalar fields are considered, with a neutral scalar meson coupling to the DM particles as
g_{s}\bar{\psi}_{\mathrm{D}}\psi_{\mathrm{D}}\phi
, and a neutral vector meson coupling to the conserved DM current as
g_{v}\overline{\psi}_{\mathrm{D}}\gamma_{\mu}\psi_{\mathrm{D}}V^{\mu}

g_{i}
represents the coupling constants between DM and the mediator. Thus, similar to the potential for baryons, one can define an effective Yukawa potential for DM with the exchange of bosons

:

V_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)=\frac{g_{v}^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{e^{-m_{v}r}}{r}-\frac{g_{s}^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{e^{-m_{s}r}}{r}.

(3)

The above potential is governed by the coupling constants
g_{i}
and the masses of the mediators
m_{i}
. With appropriate coupling constants and masses, this potential can be attractive at large distances and repulsive at short distances.

The full Lagrangian, including both attractive and repulsive interactions, is given by ref.
Xiang 2014

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}_{D}\left[\gamma_{\mu}\left(i\partial^{\mu}-g_{\mu}V^{\mu}\right)-\left(m_{D}-g_{s}\phi\right)\right]\psi_{D}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi-m_{s}^{2}\phi^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{4}D_{\mu\nu}D^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m_{v}^{2}V_{\mu}V^{\mu},

where
D_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}V_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}V_{\mu}
is the strength tensor for the vector meson.

Within the relativistic mean field approximation, the vector and scalar field operators are replaced by their ground state expectation values:
\langle\phi(x)\rangle=\phi_{0}

\displaystyle p_{D}

II.3
Two fluid TOV equaitons

Once the EOSs for NM and DM are prepared, the properties of DANS are determined by solving the two-fluid TOV equations, given by
Ciarcellut 2011
:

\frac{{\rm d}P_{1}(r)}{{\rm d}r}=-\left(P_{1}(r)+\varepsilon_{1}(r)\right)\frac{4\pi r^{3}\left(P_{1}(r)+P_{2}(r)\right)+m(r)}{r(r-2m(r))},

(7)

\frac{{\rm d}P_{2}(r)}{{\rm d}r}=-\left(P_{2}(r)+\varepsilon_{2}(r)\right)\frac{4\pi r^{3}\left(P_{1}(r)+P_{2}(r)\right)+m(r)}{r(r-2m(r))},

(8)

\frac{{\rm d}m(r)}{{\rm d}r}=4\pi\left(\varepsilon_{1}(r)+\varepsilon_{2}(r)\right)r^{2},

(9)

where
r
is the radial coordinate from the center of the star, and
P_{i}
and
\varepsilon_{i}
represent the pressure and energy density of NM or DM, respectively. The total pressure and energy density are given by
p(r)=p_{D}(r)+p_{N}(r)
and
\varepsilon(r)=\varepsilon_{D}(r)+\varepsilon_{N}(r)
.

To solve the above equations, initial conditions must be specified, such as the central densities of NM and DM. However, there are no reliable constraints on the central density of DM as of now. Therefore, we set the ratio of the energy densities between NM and DM, i.e.,
\varepsilon_{D}^{c}/\varepsilon_{N}^{c}
, as a parameter. This method is similar to those used in ref.
Kumar 2025
and other studies.

In short, four parameters describing DM can vary in this model:
m_{D}
,
C_{DS}
,
C_{DV}
, and
\varepsilon_{D}^{c}/\varepsilon_{N}^{c}
. For a slected
m_{D}
value, by constructing a three-dimensional (3D) parameter space of
C_{DS}-C_{DV}-\varepsilon_{D}^{c}/\varepsilon_{N}^{c}
, we can constrain these parameters simultaneously based on current observations of NSs, particularly the simultaneous observation of the mass and radius of PSRJ0740+6620. As we shall demonstrate below, the advantage of this 3D parameter space is that it provides a direct relationship between the parameters and observational data, enabling clearer insights into the effects of DM on the properties of DANS.

In the present work, we are interested in how the simultaneous measurement of mass and radius of PSR J0740+6620 can help constrain the parameters
C_{DS}
,

This is because the NM EOS used in the present work already describes well the current astrophysical observations within their uncertain ranges without considering DM. Though the DM EOS exhibits a broad distribution at high densities, the amount of DM in DANS is determined jointly by the distributions and fraction of DM in it. Namely, it is easy for the DM to satisfy the observational constraints within our model framework. We found that the lower limit

IV
Conclusions

In this work, we used a fermionic DM model, which describes DM as interacting solely through gravity, alongside a well-constrained, parameterized EOS for NM. We compare the constraints on DM properties within the DANS by considering both the observed NS maximum mass (
M_{\rm max}=2.08
M
⊙
) and the simultaneous measurement of mass and radius (
R_{2.08}=12.2
km) for PSRJ0740+6620, in the 3D parameter space of
C_{DS}-C_{DV}-\varepsilon_{D}^{c}/\varepsilon_{N}^{c}
. The NM EOS selected in this study satisfies all relevant observational constraints, including the maximum NS mass as well as the masses and radii of several NSs observed by NICER and GW170817 by LIGO/VIRGO.

Our results indicate that the simultaneous measurement of mass and radius can reduce the uncertainty in
\varepsilon_{D}^{c}/\varepsilon_{N}^{c}
by more than 50%. However, the parameters
C_{DS}
and
C_{DV}
remain unconstrained, even with the potential for tighter constraints from future measurements. To better constrain these parameters, new kinds of observation will be essential. Although the central energy density of DM can be much larger than that of NM, the DM fraction
f_{D}
in the star should remain below 2% with the
M_{\rm max}=2.08
M
⊙
constraint, and could be as low as 0.3% with the
R_{2.08}=12.2
km constraint. This suggests that only a small amount of DM is present in the DANS.

The NM EOS we selected is consistent with all existing astrophysical constraints, making it relatively easy for DANS to satisfy these conditions, as the needed changes from DM EOS to the NM EOS are minimal. Essentially, there is a degeneracy between the EOS of NM and that of DM because the TOV equations and composition-blind. Namely, different combinations of NM and DM can lead to the same pressure and energy density necessary to describe the observations. Naturally, other choices of NM EOS could lead to more stringent constraints on DM properties. The inclusion of new particles, such as hyperons and mesons, or the consideration of a phase transition from NM to quark matter could also modify the NM EOS. Additionally, we focused on the lower limit
R_{2.08}=12.2
km constraint derived from the simultaneous measurement of mass and radius. We did not discuss the corresponding upper limit of
R_{2.08}=16.3
km or the analysis from Ref.
Riley 2021
, as these are satisfied by almost all EOS parameter combinations within our model framework. Therefore, tighter constraints on the masses and especially radii of massive NSs are crucial to deepen our understanding of DM properties in DANS.

Spectral Generator Neural Operator for Stable Long-Horizon PDE Rollouts
Jiayi Li, Zhaonan Wang, Flora D. Salim

Abstract
Neural operators provide fast PDE surrogates and often generalize across parameters and resolutions. However, in the short train long test setting, autoregressive rollouts can become unstable. This typically happens for two reasons: one step errors accumulate over time, and high frequency components feed back and grow. We introduce the Spectral Generator Neural Operator (SGNO), a residual time stepper that targets both effects. For the linear part, SGNO uses an exponential time differencing update in Fourier space with a learned diagonal generator. We constrain the real part of this generator to be nonpositive, so iterating the step does not amplify the linear dynamics. For nonlinear dynamics, SGNO adds a gated forcing term with channel mixing within each Fourier mode, which keeps the nonlinear update controlled. To further limit high frequency feedback, SGNO applies spectral truncation and an optional smooth mask on the forcing pathway. We derive a one step amplification bound and a finite horizon rollout error bound. The bound separates generator approximation error from nonlinear mismatch and gives sufficient conditions under which the latent L 2 L^{2} norm does not grow across rollout steps. On APEBench spanning 1D, 2D, and 3D PDE families, SGNO achieves lower long horizon error and longer stable rollout lengths than strong neural operator baselines. Ablations confirm the roles of the generator constraint, gating, and filtering.The code is available at https://github.com/lijy32123-cloud/SGNO .

Introduction

Many scientific and engineering problems require long-horizon forecasting for time-dependent partial differential equations (PDEs), including turbulent flows, reaction-diffusion PDEs, and climate-related dynamics.
Neural operators offer fast surrogate models that map input fields to output fields
(
17
;
13
;
11
)
and often generalize across PDE parameters and spatial resolutions.
However, training data typically covers only short time windows, while evaluation often requires long autoregressive rollouts.
This train-test mismatch makes stability and temporal extrapolation a major bottleneck.

Long rollouts are difficult because errors feed back into future predictions and accumulate.
First, the model applies the same one-step update many times, so even small per-step amplification can compound and cause drift.
Second, nonlinear interactions and discretization effects can excite unresolved high-frequency modes; these modes then feed back and destabilize the rollout.
Therefore, reliable long-range prediction requires controlling stepwise amplification and limiting high-frequency feedback, without sacrificing generalization.

We propose the Spectral Generator Neural Operator (SGNO) to address these failure modes.
SGNO is a residual time stepper that splits the dynamics into a linear component and a nonlinear residual in a latent feature space.
For the linear part, SGNO uses an exponential time differencing (ETD) update in Fourier space with a learned diagonal generator.
We parameterize the generator so its real part is always nonpositive, which prevents the linear ETD factor from increasing the magnitude of Fourier modes when the step is iterated in autoregressive rollouts.
For the nonlinear part, SGNO injects a
\phi_{1}
-weighted forcing term produced by a generator network with gating and channel mixing within each Fourier mode.
To further reduce high-frequency feedback, SGNO uses spectral truncation and an optional smooth mask on the forcing pathway.

We support these design choices with theory and experiments aligned with the architecture.
We derive a one-step amplification bound and sufficient conditions under which the linear backbone does not amplify the latent feature norm.
We also derive a finite-horizon rollout error bound that separates generator approximation error from nonlinear mismatch.
Following the APEBench protocol
(
10
)
, we evaluate seven tasks spanning linear dynamics, nonlinear dynamics, and reaction-diffusion dynamics under short training and long-rollout evaluation.
Across tasks, SGNO reduces long-horizon error and extends stable rollout length relative to strong neural operator baselines, and ablations isolate the roles of the generator constraint, gating, and filtering.

Contributions.

We propose SGNO, a residual neural operator for stable long horizon autoregressive rollouts of semilinear PDE surrogates.
SGNO advances latent features with an explicit ETD update in the Fourier domain, where a learned diagonal generator is parameterized to have nonpositive real part and thus prevents linear amplification under iteration.
Nonlinear effects are injected through a
\phi_{1}
weighted forcing pathway, and we apply filtering on this pathway to suppress high frequency feedback during rollouts.
We provide architecture aligned analysis, including a one step gain bound, a sufficient stability condition for iterated rollouts, and a finite horizon error recursion that separates generator approximation error from nonlinear forcing mismatch.
We evaluate SGNO on seven APEBench tasks in the short train long test regime, and we include mechanism-oriented ablations that probe the two residual pathways controlled by
\alpha_{g}
and
\alpha_{w}
.

Related Work

Neural operators.

DeepONet
(
17
)
and the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO)
(
13
)
are widely used neural operator baselines, with broader reviews in
(
11
)
.
Many follow ups modify the spectral backbone to improve mixing across modes, for example AFNO
(
4
)
and multiwavelet operators
(
5
)
.
Other lines increase expressivity through attention or encoder decoder backbones, such as the Galerkin Transformer
(
2
)
and UNO
(
20
)
.
These architectures are typically optimized for one step prediction accuracy and parameter generalization, while stability over long autoregressive rollouts is not enforced by design.

Rollout stability for autoregressive surrogates.

Several works analyze why autoregressive rollouts drift or diverge when a learned one step map is iterated.

1
relate robust rollouts to notions from numerical stability and propose training procedures that improve stability in practice.

18
identify operations that increase gain during rollouts and propose gain control principles, including frequency domain normalization and adaptive filtering.
A complementary approach reduces train test mismatch by training on model generated states, for example recurrent training for neural operators
(
22
)
.
Other directions aim at long time structure, such as Markov style operator learning for dissipative chaos
(
14
)
and objectives that regularize invariant measures
(
21
)
.

Stable steppers and exponential integrator inspired operators.

Exponential integrators and ETD methods are classical steppers for semilinear stiff PDEs
(
3
;
9
;
6
)
.
They separate a linear propagator from a nonlinear forcing term and use
\phi
functions to integrate the forcing in a stable way.
In data driven dynamics, stabilized neural ODE models also exploit a linear nonlinear split to reduce high wavenumber growth and improve long time robustness
(
15
)
.
In operator learning, integrating factor style updates and implicit iteration have been explored, including IFNO
(
23
)
, iUFNO
(
12
)
, IAFNO
(
7
)
, and SSNO
(
24
)
.
These methods stabilize rollouts either by using an integrating factor based on a chosen linear part, or by adding implicit or iterative correction at each step.

Inference time correction and diffusion based refinement.

Another line improves long horizon behavior by adding correction at inference time.
PDE Refiner
(
16
)
refines predictions through iterative denoising, which improves stability but increases inference cost.
Diffusion conditioned on neural operator outputs can further target missing high frequency content
(
19
)
, and recent work combines diffusion with adaptive Fourier operators for 3D turbulence forecasting
(
8
)
.
These approaches typically trade additional inference iterations or sampling for improved rollout quality.

Positioning

SGNO is a stepper-level design that combines gain-control analyses with exponential-integrator stepping, but it makes two concrete changes.
First, instead of fixing the linear part as in standard integrating-factor updates, SGNO learns a diagonal Fourier generator and constrains its real part to be nonpositive, so the linear channel is nonexpansive under repeated application.
Second, rather than filtering the full state or adding iterative correction, SGNO filters only the nonlinear forcing pathway integrated by the ETD
\phi_{1}
weighting, which targets high-frequency feedback at the injection point.
Our theory follows the same decomposition and gives a one-step gain bound and a finite-horizon error recursion that separates generator approximation error from nonlinear forcing mismatch.

Problem Setup

We consider a class of semilinear PDEs on the periodic domain
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
d\in\{1,2,3\}
.
The state
u(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}u(t,x)+\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{phys}}(u(t,\cdot))(x),

(1)

\displaystyle u(0,\cdot)

\displaystyle=u_{0}.

Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
denote the Fourier transform and its inverse.
We retain frequencies in
K\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and use the spectral projector
P_{K}
to truncate modes outside
K
.

To match the benchmark protocol, we work at a fixed spatial resolution and time step
\Delta t
.
We denote the data step size by
\Delta t_{\mathrm{data}}:=\Delta t
.
Let
P_{h}
be a reference numerical one-step map at step
\Delta t
acting on the discrete state

u_{h}^{[t]}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, so that

u_{h}^{[t+1]}=P_{h}\!\left(u_{h}^{[t]}\right),\qquad t=0,1,\ldots

(2)

We learn a neural stepper
f_{\theta}\approx P_{h}
and evaluate autoregressive rollouts

\hat{u}_{h}^{[t+1]}=f_{\theta}\!\left(\hat{u}_{h}^{[t]}\right),\qquad\hat{u}_{h}^{[0]}=u_{h}^{[0]}.

(3)

In all experiments we set history length
m=1
, so we omit history notation and write
f_{\theta}(u_{h}^{[t]})
.

Training objective.

In our experiments we use one step teacher forcing with MSE.
Given training pairs
(u_{h}^{[t]},u_{h}^{[t+1]})
from the trajectories, we optimize

\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T_{\mathrm{train}}-1}\ell\!\left(f_{\theta}(u_{h}^{[t]}),\,u_{h}^{[t+1]}\right)\right].

(4)

Evaluation metric.

For
M
test trajectories and
N
degrees of freedom, define the mean nRMSE at time
t
as

L_{\mathrm{nRMSE}}^{[t]}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\hat{u}_{j,i}^{[t]}-u_{j,i}^{[t]}\right)^{2}}}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(u_{j,i}^{[t]}\right)^{2}}+\varepsilon}.

(5)

In all experiments we set
\varepsilon=10^{-12}
for numerical stability.

Aggregate metric.

We summarize rollout error by

\mathrm{GMean100}=\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{100}\sum_{t=1}^{100}\log L_{\mathrm{nRMSE}}^{[t]}\right),

(6)

computed from a
T_{\mathrm{eval}}=200
step rollout using the first 100 steps.

4
Method: Spectral Generator Neural Operator

We learn a neural one step operator
f_{\theta}
for stable long-horizon autoregressive rollouts.
SGNO advances latent features with a ETD update with a constrained diagonal spectral generator and injects nonlinear effects through a scaled residual forcing term followed by a lightweight pointwise correction.

4.1
Architecture overview

Let
u_{n}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{C_{u}}
be the state on the periodic domain
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
let
m
be the history length, and let
x\in[0,1]^{d}
be the spatial coordinate.
In all experiments we set
m=1
.
Given the history window
u_{n-m+1:n}
, we lift it to a latent feature field

v^{(0)}=\mathrm{Lift}_{\theta}\!\big([u_{n-m+1:n};x]\big)\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{C}).

(7)

We then apply
L
shared spectral time advance blocks with internal step size
\delta t
:

v^{(\ell+1)}=\Psi_{\theta}\!\left(v^{(\ell)};\delta t\right),\qquad\ell=0,1,\ldots,L-1.

(8)

We set

\delta t=\Delta t_{\mathrm{data}}/L,

(9)

so that the composition of
L
blocks corresponds to one data step.
Finally, we project the features back to the physical space to obtain the next state:

\Delta u_{n+1}=\mathrm{Proj}_{\theta}\!\left(v^{(L)}\right),\qquad u_{n+1}=u_{n}+\Delta u_{n+1}.

(10)

Here
u_{n}
denotes the last state in the history window
u_{n-m+1:n}
.
This defines the learned one step operator

f_{\theta}(u_{n-m+1:n})=u_{n}+\mathrm{Proj}_{\theta}\!\left((\Psi_{\theta})^{L}\!\left(\mathrm{Lift}_{\theta}([u_{n-m+1:n};x])\right)\right).

(11)

Pointwise components.

\mathrm{Lift}_{\theta}
and
\mathrm{Proj}_{\theta}
are pointwise networks shared over
x
.

G_{\theta}
and
W_{\theta}
are pointwise residual networks acting on
v
.
In our implementation, these are realized as
1\times 1
convolutions or two layer MLPs applied independently at each spatial location.

Figure 1
:

(a) Overall architecture of SGNO.
The input state is lifted to a latent feature space, propagated through stacked time advance blocks, and projected back to obtain the next state.
(b) Time advance block.
Features are propagated by a stabilized spectral ETD operator in the Fourier domain, with a nonlinear residual injected through a
\phi_{1}
weighted forcing term, followed by a pointwise correction.

4.2
Spectral ETD time advance block

Each block models semilinear feature dynamics

\partial_{t}v=L_{\theta}v+G_{\theta}(v),

(12)

where
L_{\theta}
is a learned stiff linear kernel and
G_{\theta}
is a nonlinear residual network.

Mode set and truncation.

Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
denote the discrete Fourier transform on the spatial grid and its inverse.
We retain a low frequency index set
K\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and apply truncation by setting
\hat{f}(k)=0
for
k\notin K
.
For real valued fields we use real FFTs and keep the corresponding nonredundant half spectrum.

Stabilized diagonal spectral generator.

For each retained mode
k\in K
, we parameterize a diagonal generator

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\theta}(k)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}\!\big(\lambda_{\theta,1}(k),\ldots,\lambda_{\theta,C}(k)\big),

(13)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\theta,c}(k)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\theta,c}(k)+\mathrm{i}\,\beta_{\theta,c}(k).

and enforce a nonpositive real part by

\alpha_{\theta,c}(k)=-\mathrm{softplus}\!\big(\eta_{\theta,c}(k)\big)\leq 0.

(14)

The imaginary part
\beta_{\theta,c}(k)
is learned without sign constraints.

ETD update with residual injection.

Let
\phi_{1}(z)=(e^{z}-1)/z
with
\phi_{1}(0)=1
.
Given
v
, we form a gated residual
g=\alpha_{g}\,G_{\theta}(v)
and mix channels per frequency with
M_{\theta}(k)\in\mathbb{C}^{C\times C}
.
For retained modes
k\in K
, the block performs an ETD Euler update in the Fourier domain
(
3
;
9
;
6
)

\displaystyle\widehat{v}^{\mathrm{etd}}(k)

\displaystyle=\exp\!\big(\delta t\,\Lambda_{\theta}(k)\big)\,\hat{v}(k)

(15)

\displaystyle+\delta t\,\phi_{1}\!\big(\delta t\,\Lambda_{\theta}(k)\big)\,F(k)\,M_{\theta}(k)\,\hat{g}(k),

and sets
\widehat{v}^{\mathrm{etd}}(k)=0
for
k\notin K
.
Here
F(k)\in[0,1]
is an optional spectral mask applied only to the forcing term to suppress high-frequency feedback.
Transforming back with
v^{\mathrm{etd}}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\widehat{v}^{\mathrm{etd}})
, we apply a pointwise correction

v_{\mathrm{out}}=\sigma\!\big(v^{\mathrm{etd}}+\alpha_{w}\,W_{\theta}(v)\big),

(16)

where
\sigma
is a pointwise nonlinearity and is set to identity in the final block.
The scalars
\alpha_{g}\geq 0
and
\alpha_{w}\geq 0
are explicit stability knobs.

4.3
Implementation notes

SGNO uses real FFTs in 1D, 2D, and 3D and truncates to the lowest retained modes in
K
.
We optionally pad in the physical domain before FFT and crop back after inverse FFT.
When masking is enabled, we use a smooth filter of the form

F(k)=\exp\!\Big(-\gamma\big(\|k\|_{\infty}/\|k_{\max}\|_{\infty}\big)^{p}\Big)\in(0,1],

(17)

with order
p
and strength
\gamma
, and set
F(k)=1
when masking is disabled.
In all experiments we use history length
m=1
and train with a one step MSE loss without multi step unrolling.

5
Theory: Stability and Error Propagation

We summarize a stability bound for the SGNO time advance block
\Psi_{\theta}
defined in Section
4
and its
L
fold composition over one data step.
The full proofs are deferred to the appendix.

5.1
Assumptions

We analyze the SGNO time advance block
\Psi_{\theta}(\cdot;\delta t)
as a map on feature fields in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{C})
.
The analysis is local, meaning that all regularity constants are required only on the subset of feature space visited by training and evaluation rollouts.
Let
R>0
and define the bounded trajectory set

\mathcal{V}_{R}\coloneqq\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{C}):\ \|v\|_{L^{2}}\leq R\right\}.

(18)

All Lipschitz constants below are understood to hold for
v,w\in\mathcal{V}_{R}
.
In practice,
R
can be taken as an empirical upper bound on
\|v\|_{L^{2}}
observed on training and validation rollouts.

Assumption 5.1

(Spectral bound)
.

There exists
\omega\leq 0
such that for all retained modes
k\in K
and channels
c\in[C]
,

\Re\!\big(\lambda_{\theta,c}(k)\big)\leq\omega.

(19)

Practical enforcement of the spectral bound.

In SGNO we parameterize the diagonal generator real part as

\Re(\lambda_{\theta,c}(k))=-\mathrm{softplus}(\eta_{\theta,c}(k))
,
so Assumption
5.1
holds by construction with
\omega=0
.
If a strict contraction margin is desired, one can use

\Re(\lambda_{\theta,c}(k))=-\mathrm{softplus}(\eta_{\theta,c}(k))-\epsilon_{\lambda}

with
\epsilon_{\lambda}\geq 0
, which yields
\omega=-\epsilon_{\lambda}
.

Assumption 5.2

(Residual regularity and bounded mixing)
.

On
\mathcal{V}_{R}
, the pointwise components
\mathrm{Lift}_{\theta}
,
\mathrm{Proj}_{\theta}
,
G_{\theta}
, and
W_{\theta}
are Lipschitz:

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Lift}_{\theta}(v)-\mathrm{Lift}_{\theta}(w)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\mathrm{lift}}\|v-w\|_{L^{2}},

(20)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Proj}_{\theta}(v)-\mathrm{Proj}_{\theta}(w)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\mathrm{proj}}\|v-w\|_{L^{2}}.

\displaystyle\|G_{\theta}(v)-G_{\theta}(w)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{G}\|v-w\|_{L^{2}},

(21)

\displaystyle\|W_{\theta}(v)-W_{\theta}(w)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{W}\|v-w\|_{L^{2}}.

The pointwise nonlinearity
\sigma
is Lipschitz with constant
L_{\sigma}
on the relevant range.
Moreover, the frequency wise mixing matrices are uniformly bounded:

\sup_{k\in K}\|M_{\theta}(k)\|_{2}\leq M.

(22)

How the Lipschitz and mixing constants can be controlled.

Since
\mathrm{Lift}_{\theta}
,
\mathrm{Proj}_{\theta}
,
G_{\theta}
, and
W_{\theta}
act pointwise in space, their
L^{2}
Lipschitz constants coincide with the Euclidean Lipschitz constants of the underlying map
\mathbb{R}^{C}\to\mathbb{R}^{C}
.
For the implemented
1\times 1
convolutions or two layer MLPs, these constants admit standard operator norm upper bounds, for example if
h(x)=A_{2}\rho(A_{1}x)
with
\rho
Lipschitz
L_{\rho}
, then
\mathrm{Lip}(h)\leq\|A_{2}\|_{2}\,L_{\rho}\,\|A_{1}\|_{2}
.
Accordingly,
L_{G}
and
L_{W}
can be kept bounded by constraining per layer operator norms via spectral normalization or explicit weight renormalization.
For the frequency mixing, one can enforce
\|M_{\theta}(k)\|_{2}\leq M
by normalizing each matrix as

M_{\theta}(k)=\bar{M}_{\theta}(k)/\max\{1,\|\bar{M}_{\theta}(k)\|_{2}\}
,
optionally multiplied by a learned scalar scale with a prescribed upper bound.
Finally, the explicit scalars
\alpha_{g}
and
\alpha_{w}
provide additional gain control because they enter the amplification factor
q(\delta t)
multiplicatively.

Assumption 5.3

(Nonexpansive truncation and masking)
.

Fourier truncation to
K
and the optional mask
F(k)\in[0,1]
do not increase the
L^{2}
norm.

FFT normalization for nonexpansiveness.

We assume the discrete Fourier transform is scaled so that Parseval holds, hence the truncation operator
P_{K}
is an orthogonal projector in Fourier space and is nonexpansive in
L^{2}
.
Likewise, the mask is an entrywise multiplier with coefficients in
[0,1]
, hence it is nonexpansive.
If a non orthonormal FFT normalization is used in code, the induced constant scaling can be absorbed into the Lipschitz constants above.

5.2
One block amplification bound

Theorem 5.4

(One step amplification)
.

Under Assumptions
5.1
–
5.3
, the block map
\Psi_{\theta}(\cdot;\delta t)
satisfies

\|\Psi_{\theta}(v;\delta t)-\Psi_{\theta}(w;\delta t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq q(\delta t)\,\|v-w\|_{L^{2}},

(23)

where

q(\delta t)=L_{\sigma}\Big(e^{\omega\,\delta t}+\delta t\,\phi_{1}(\omega\,\delta t)\,\alpha_{g}\,M\,L_{G}+\alpha_{w}\,L_{W}\Big),

(24)

and
L_{\sigma}
is the Lipschitz constant of
\sigma
.

Corollary 5.5

(Nonexpansiveness and gain control)
.

If
q(\delta t)\leq 1
, then the block is nonexpansive in
L^{2}
.
For a fixed data step
\Delta t_{\mathrm{data}}
, increasing
L
reduces
\delta t=\Delta t_{\mathrm{data}}/L
and yields the one step gain bound

\|(\Psi_{\theta})^{L}(v)-(\Psi_{\theta})^{L}(w)\|_{L^{2}}\leq q(\Delta t_{\mathrm{data}}/L)^{L}\,\|v-w\|_{L^{2}}.

(25)

Decreasing
\alpha_{g}
or
\alpha_{w}
directly reduces
q(\delta t)
.

5.3
Finite horizon rollout error

Let
P_{h}
be the reference one step solver at step size
\Delta t_{\mathrm{data}}
and let
f_{\theta}
be the learned one step operator.
Here
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the discrete Euclidean norm on the spatial grid.
Define the modeling defect at state
u
as
e(u)=\|P_{h}(u)-f_{\theta}(u)\|_{2}
.
For the reference trajectory
\{u_{n}\}
and the SGNO rollout
\{\hat{u}_{n}\}
, the error
E_{n}=\|u_{n}-\hat{u}_{n}\|_{2}
satisfies

E_{n+1}\leq q_{\mathrm{data}}E_{n}+e(u_{n}).

(26)

q_{\mathrm{data}}\coloneqq 1+L_{\mathrm{proj}}\,L_{\mathrm{lift}}\;q(\Delta t_{\mathrm{data}}/L)^{L}.

(27)

In particular,
e(u)
can be decomposed into a generator approximation term, a nonlinear mismatch term, and truncation or masking effects, with details provided in the appendix.

Experiments

6.1
Benchmarks and evaluation protocol

We follow the APEBench scenario definitions and the official train/test splits.
Each task contains 50 training trajectories of length
T_{\mathrm{train}}=51
and 30 test trajectories of length
T_{\mathrm{test}}=201
.
At test time we initialize rollouts from the first test frame and run autoregressive rollouts for
T_{\mathrm{eval}}=200
steps.

We use a fixed, dimension-specific default configuration without task-specific hyperparameter tuning.
Within each spatial dimension (
d\in\{1,2,3\}
), we keep the same model and optimizer hyperparameters across all tasks, and only the scenario-defined
\Delta t
varies.
The full default configurations for 1D, 2D, and 3D are provided in Appendix
B
.

We measure per-trajectory nRMSE as

\mathrm{nRMSE}(i,t)=\|\hat{u}(i,t)-u(i,t)\|_{2}/(\|u(i,t)\|_{2}+\varepsilon)
with
\varepsilon=10^{-12}
.
We use
\mathrm{GMean100}
, the geometric mean of the mean nRMSE over the first 100 steps of a 200-step rollout, defined in Section
3
.
Following the APEBench reporting protocol, we compute metrics for each training seed and report the median across seeds for all methods, using 50 seeds for 1D tasks and 20 seeds for 2D and 3D tasks
(
10
)
.

Task suite.

We evaluate on seven representative PDE scenarios spanning three dynamical regimes.

Linear
includes 1D Dispersion (wave dispersion), 2D Anisotropic Diffusion (diffusive smoothing), and 3D Unbalanced Advection (transport dominated dynamics).

Nonlinear
includes 1D KdV (nonlinear dispersive waves), 1D Kuramoto–Sivashinsky (chaotic pattern formation), and 2D Kolmogorov Flow (2D fluid dynamics with forcing).

Reaction–diffusion
is represented by 3D Swift–Hohenberg, which couples diffusion with nonlinear local reactions and is known to generate complex spatial patterns.
Within each dimension, we use the same default hyperparameter configuration across all tasks, and only the scenario-defined
\Delta t
varies.

Long-horizon stability evidence

7
Conclusion

We introduced the Spectral Generator Neural Operator (SGNO) for stable long-horizon autoregressive PDE rollouts under the short train, long test regime.
SGNO advances latent features with a spectrally stabilized ETD propagator and injects nonlinear effects through a
\phi_{1}
weighted forcing term with controllable scaling, while spectral truncation and masking mitigate high-frequency feedback.
We provided a compact stability analysis via a one step amplification bound and a finite horizon error recursion that connects rollout stability to explicit architectural knobs.
Empirically, on seven APEBench tasks spanning linear, nonlinear, and reaction–diffusion dynamics, SGNO achieves the best
\mathrm{GMean100}
on six tasks, the runner-up result on 3D Swift–Hohenberg, and exhibits markedly improved long-horizon behavior on representative rollouts.

Limitations.

First, our experiments focus on a seven task subset of APEBench at fixed resolutions and time steps; evaluating the full suite and stress testing changes in
\Delta t
, resolution, and PDE parameters would better characterize robustness.
Second, our stability analysis is local in feature space and provides sufficient conditions that depend on Lipschitz constants of the pointwise networks and operator norms of the frequency mixing; while the diagonal generator is spectrally stabilized by construction, we do not in the main experiments explicitly enforce or report bounds for the nonlinear components, so the analysis should be interpreted as design guidance rather than an end to end certificate, and incorporating practical controls such as spectral normalization, mixing normalization, or norm penalties is a straightforward extension.
Third, SGNO currently uses a first order ETD Euler residual injection and our ablations are limited; in strongly stiff regimes higher order exponential integrators or integrating factor Runge Kutta variants and more systematic sweeps over truncation, masking, depth
L
, and generator parameterizations remain to be explored.

Future work.

A natural next step is to extend evaluation to the full APEBench suite and complementary benchmarks, and to test robustness under changes in resolution, time step, and PDE parameters.
On the modeling side, richer generator parameterizations and adaptive spectral masking may improve performance on more chaotic regimes while preserving stability.
On the theory side, tightening the bound to better reflect practical masking and truncation, and connecting feature space nonexpansiveness to physical state error and invariant statistics, are promising directions.

Proof of the one step amplification bound

We restate the block map in operator form.
Given input
v

g(v)=\alpha_{g}G_{\theta}(v)
.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}
be the Fourier domain mixing and masking operator acting on a field
h

\widehat{\mathcal{M}_{\theta}h}(k)=F(k)\,M_{\theta}(k)\,\hat{h}(k),\qquad k\in K,

(30)

\widehat{\mathcal{M}_{\theta}h}(k)=0

Assume
\sup_{k\in K}\|M_{\theta}(k)\|_{2}\leq M

Define the linear ETD propagator and the

(S_{\delta t}v)\widehat{\ }(k)=\exp(\delta t\,\Lambda_{\theta}(k))\,\hat{v}(k),\qquad(\Phi_{\delta t}v)\widehat{\ }(k)=\phi_{1}(\delta t\,\Lambda_{\theta}(k))\,\hat{v}(k),\qquad k\in K,

(31)

and zero outside

The block update is

v^{\mathrm{etd}}=S_{\delta t}v+\delta t\,\Phi_{\delta t}\,\mathcal{M}_{\theta}g(v),\qquad\Psi_{\theta}(v;\delta t)=\sigma\!\big(v^{\mathrm{etd}}+\alpha_{w}W_{\theta}(v)\big).

Lemma A.1 (Norm bounds for

If
\Re(\lambda_{\theta,c}(k))\leq\omega

\|S_{\delta t}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq e^{\omega\delta t},\qquad\|\Phi_{\delta t}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq\phi_{1}(\omega\delta t).

Proof.

For each diagonal entry with

Let
P_{h}
be the reference one step map at step size

and let

be the learned one step map.
For

f_{\theta}(u)=u+\mathrm{Proj}_{\theta}\!\left(\big(\Psi_{\theta}(\cdot;\delta t)\big)^{L}(\mathrm{Lift}_{\theta}(u))\right),\qquad\delta t=\Delta t_{\mathrm{data}}/L.

Assume
\mathrm{Lift}_{\theta}
and
\mathrm{Proj}_{\theta}
are Lipschitz with constants
L_{\mathrm{lift}}
and
L_{\mathrm{proj}}
on the relevant sets.
Define

.

\displaystyle E_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\|P_{h}(u_{n})-f_{\theta}(\hat{u}_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|P_{h}(u_{n})-f_{\theta}(u_{n})\|_{L^{2}}+\|f_{\theta}(u_{n})-f_{\theta}(\hat{u}_{n})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq e(u_{n})+\mathrm{Lip}(f_{\theta})\,E_{n}.

(43)

By composition,

\mathrm{Lip}(f_{\theta})\leq 1+L_{\mathrm{proj}}\;q(\delta t)^{L}\;L_{\mathrm{lift}}.

(44)

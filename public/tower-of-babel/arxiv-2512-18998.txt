Global well-posedness for the generalized intermediate NLS with a nonvanishing condition at infinity
Takafumi Akahori, Rana Badreddine, Slim Ibrahim, Nobu Kishimoto

Abstract
. The Intermediate Nonlinear Schrödinger equation models quasi-harmonic internal waves in two-fluid layer system, and admits dark solitons, that is, solutions with nonvanishing boundary conditions at spatial infinity. These solutions fall outside existing well-posedness theories. We establish local and global well-posedness in a Zhidkov-type space naturally suited to such non-trivial boundary conditions, and extend these results to a generalized defocusing equation. This appears to be the first well-posedness result for the equation in a functional setting adapted to its dark soliton structure.



1.
Introduction

We study the intermediate nonlinear Schrödinger equation,

(INLS)

i\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u=u(i-T_{\delta})\partial_{x}|u|^{2}\qquad\mbox{in}~\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},

where
u
is a complex-valued function,
\delta>0
is a parameter called the total fluid depth, and
T_{\delta}
is a singular integral operator defined by

T_{\delta}(f)(x):=\frac{1}{2\delta}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}\coth{\Big(\frac{\pi(x-y)}{2\delta}\Big)}f(y)\,dy.

The equation (
INLS
) was introduced by Pelinovsky in
[
39
]
to describe the long-term dynamics of the envelope of weakly nonlinear internal waves in a stratified fluid of finite depth.

The operator

can be written as a Fourier multiplier form

\widehat{T_{\delta}(f)}(\xi)=-i\coth(\delta\xi)\hat{f}(\xi),

where

denotes the Fourier transformation (see Section

).
The asymptotics of this symbol reveal that (
INLS
) interpolates between two fundamental models.
In the deep-water limit

converges to
-i\,\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)

H(f)(x):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}\frac{f(y)}{\pi(x-y)}\,dy,\qquad\widehat{H(f)}(\xi)=-i\,\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi).

A notable property of (

(1.2)

{\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}}:=\{u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\colon\partial_{x}u\in H^{k-1}(\mathbb{R}),~|u|^{2}-\rho^{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}.

(1.3)

d^{k}_{\rho}(f,g):=\|\partial_{x}(f-g)\|_{H^{k-1}}+\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\big\||f|^{2}-|g|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}.

To measure the size of functions in
\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}

(1.4)

\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(u):=\|\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\big\||u|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}.

Our argument does not rely on the complete integrability of (

Thus, we can deal with the following generalized version of (

(gINLS)

i\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u=u(i\alpha-\beta T_{\delta})\partial_{x}|u|^{2}\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}$,}

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}

Note that (

\beta=\pm|\alpha|\neq 0

Our first main result establishes local well-posedness in
\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}

Theorem 1.1

(Local well-posedness in
\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
)
.

Let
\delta>0
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary.
For
M_{0}>0
, there exists
T_{*}\in(0,1]
satisfying
T_{*}\geq c_{\alpha,\beta}(1+\delta^{-1})^{-1}(1+M_{0}^{4})^{-1}
such that the following holds.

For any
\rho>0
and
\phi\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
with
\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)\leq M_{0}^{2}
, there exists a unique solution
u\in C([0,T_{*}];\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho})
to (
gINLS
) on
[0,T_{*}]
with initial condition
u(0)=\phi
.
Moreover, the mapping
\phi\mapsto u
from
\{f\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}:\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(f)\leq M_{0}^{2}\}
into
C([0,T_{*}];\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho})
is continuous.
Furthermore, if in addition
\phi\in\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}
for some integer
k\geq 3
, then
u\in C([0,T_{*}];\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho})
.

Remark 1.1
.

Notice that the
L^{2}
-rescaling

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}u(\lambda^{-1}x,\lambda^{-2}t)

maps a solution

INLS

INLS
), but with rescaled depth

This formally indicates that

is the scaling-critical space.
Consequently, one may expect well-posedness for initial data in
\mathcal{Z}^{0}_{\rho}
, a problem that remains widely open, particularly in the non-decaying data setting.

The proof relies on a modified energy method
[

,
13

enabling us to control the derivative present in the nonlinearity of (

\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
.
We remark that in

for the defocusing Calogero-Moser DNLS equation (

For the intermediate model (
INLS
), the analysis is more subtle because of the singular nature of the operator
T_{\delta}
.
Although (
INLS
) is completely integrable for solutions in Sobolev spaces with vanishing boundary conditions, admits an inverse scattering transform and several Lax formulations
[
37
,
9
]
, these integrable structures do not provide well-defined conserved quantities in a setting with nonvanishing boundary conditions such as
\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}
.
Indeed, if we consider the conservation laws at the
H^{n/2}
level, derived via the Lax operator given in
[
9
]
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}\mathcal{L}_{u}^{n}u\,dx\quad(n=0,1,2,\dots),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{u}:\ f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\mapsto-i\partial_{x}f+u\frac{1+iT_{\delta}}{2}(\overline{u}f)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

then, after expansion and reduction of the number of
T_{\delta}
operators using Cotlar’s identity (see
[
9
, Eq. (2.12)]
), the first five conservation laws read

\displaystyle E_{0}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle E_{1/2}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{\Im[\overline{u}\partial_{x}u]+\frac{1}{2}|u|^{4}\Big\}\,dx,

\displaystyle E_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{|\partial_{x}u|^{2}+|u|^{2}\Im[\overline{u}\partial_{x}u]+\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}T_{\delta}\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})+\frac{1}{3}|u|^{6}\Big\}\,dx,

\displaystyle E_{3/2}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{\begin{aligned} &\Im[(\partial_{x}\overline{u})\partial_{x}^{2}u]+\frac{1}{2}[\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})]^{2}+\frac{3}{2}|u|^{2}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\Im[\overline{u}\partial_{x}u]T_{\delta}\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})\\
&+|u|^{4}\Big(\Im[\overline{u}\partial_{x}u]+\frac{3}{4}T_{\delta}\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})\Big)+\frac{1}{4}|u|^{8}\Big\}\,dx,\end{aligned}

\displaystyle E_{2}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{\begin{aligned} &|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{2}+\Big(2|\partial_{x}u|^{2}-3\partial_{x}^{2}(|u|^{2})\Big)\Im[\overline{u}\partial_{x}u]\\
&+\Big(2|\partial_{x}u|^{2}-\frac{3}{4}\partial_{x}^{2}(|u|^{2})\Big)T_{\delta}\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})+\Im[\overline{u}\partial_{x}u]T_{\delta}\partial_{x}(\Im[\overline{u}\partial_{x}u])\\
&+2|u|^{4}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}+|u|^{2}\Big(\frac{3}{2}[\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})]^{2}+\frac{1}{2}[T_{\delta}\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})]^{2}\Big)\\
&+\Im[\overline{u}\partial_{x}u]\Big(2|u|^{2}T_{\delta}\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})+T_{\delta}\partial_{x}(|u|^{4})\Big)\\
&+|u|^{6}\Big(\Im[\overline{u}\partial_{x}u]+\frac{2}{3}T_{\delta}\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})\Big)+\frac{1}{4}|u|^{4}T_{\delta}\partial_{x}(|u|^{4})+\frac{1}{5}|u|^{10}\Big\}\,dx.\end{aligned}

However, these integrals
fail to be well-defined for

u\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
, even after substituting
|u|^{2(n+1)}-\rho^{2(n+1)}
for the last term
|u|^{2(n+1)}
in
E_{n/2}(u)
.
To overcome this, we construct conservation laws via the modified energy method, which allows us to establish global well-posedness for (
gINLS
).
Our motivation for considering the more general model (
gINLS
), rather than (
INLS
), is to emphasize that the argument does not rely on the complete integrability of (
INLS
).

Theorem 1.2

(Global well-posedness and a priori bound)
.

Let
\delta>0
,
\rho>0
.
For general
\alpha\neq 0
and
\beta\geq 0
, the Cauchy problem associated with (
gINLS
) is globally well-posed in
\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
.
Moreover, for the integrable case (
INLS
), the following uniform bound holds:

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u(t))<\infty.

In the general defocusing case
\alpha\neq 0
,
\beta\geq 0
, we still have a uniform bound for
\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(u(t))
but only an exponential bound for
\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
; see Theorem
4.1
in Section

.
The global theory is built on two new conservation laws compatible with nonvanishing boundary conditions, which allow us to control
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2},\|\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{1}}^{2},
and
\||u|^{2}-\rho^{2}\|_{L^{2}}
in time.

2.1.
Notation

We use
\mathcal{F}
(or
\widehat{\,\cdot\,}
) and

to denote the Fourier and its inverse transformations:

\mathcal{F}[f](\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\,dx,\qquad\mathcal{F}^{-1}[g](x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}g(\xi)\,d\xi.

We use
U(t)
to denote the free Schrödinger operator given by

U(t):=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}e^{it|\xi|^{2}}\mathcal{F}.

Our convention for the
L^{2}
-inner product
\left(f,g\right)_{L^{2}}
is

\left(f,g\right)_{L^{2}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{g}(x)\,dx.

For the frequency localization, let
\varphi
be a smooth, nonnegative radial function supported on
|\xi|\leq 2
with
\varphi(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 1
.
For each nonnegative integer
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, define the (inhomogeneous) Littlewood-Paley projection
P_{\leq j}
as the Fourier multiplier with symbol
\varphi(\xi/2^{j})
, and set
P_{\geq j+1}:=I-P_{\leq j}
,
P_{j}:=P_{\leq j}-P_{\leq j-1}
(
j\geq 1
) and
P_{0}:=P_{\leq 0}
.

We use
\mathcal{N}(u)
to denote the nonlinear term of (
gINLS
), namely

\mathcal{N}(u):=iu(\alpha+i\beta T_{\delta})\partial_{x}|u|^{2},

and also define the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\delta}
as

\mathcal{L}_{\delta}:=(T_{\delta}-H)\partial_{x}.

We may write the nonlinear term as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(u)

\displaystyle=-\beta u\mathcal{L}_{\delta}|u|^{2}+iu(\alpha+i\beta H)\partial_{x}|u|^{2}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(u)+\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(u).

We have already defined the space
\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}
, the distance function

2.2.
Properties of
T_{\delta}

Having an unbounded symbol (
1.1
), the operator
T_{\delta}
itself is not bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
nor on
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
.
However, since the Fourier symbol
r_{\delta}(\xi)=(\coth(\delta\xi)-\mathrm{sgn}(\xi))\xi
of
\mathcal{L}_{\delta}
satisfies
|r_{\delta}(\xi)|\lesssim\delta^{-1}
, by Plancherel’s theorem we see that
\mathcal{L}_{\delta}
is bounded on
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and satisfies

(2.2)

\|\mathcal{L}_{\delta}f\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\delta^{-1}\|f\|_{H^{s}}.

Combining (
2.2
) with the boundedness of
H
, we also see that
T_{\delta}\partial_{x}
is bounded from
H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R})
to
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
and satisfies

(2.3)

\|T_{\delta}\partial_{x}f\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}f\|_{H^{s}}+\delta^{-1}\|f\|_{H^{s}}.

For
u\in\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}
, we notice that
\partial_{x}|u|^{2}=\partial_{x}(|u|^{2}-\rho^{2})
gives

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|T_{\delta}\partial_{x}|u|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{-1}\big\||u|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{\delta}|u|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{-1}\big\||u|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}.

We also remark that the operator
T_{\delta}\partial_{x}
is positive, symmetric, and satisfies
\overline{T_{\delta}\partial_{x}f}=T_{\delta}\partial_{x}\overline{f}
:

(2.5)

\left(T_{\delta}\partial_{x}f,f\right)_{L^{2}}\geq 0,\quad\left(T_{\delta}\partial_{x}f,g\right)_{L^{2}}=\left(f,T_{\delta}\partial_{x}g\right)_{L^{2}},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}fT_{\delta}\partial_{x}g\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}gT_{\delta}\partial_{x}f\,dx,

which follows from the fact that the Fourier symbol
\coth(\delta\xi)\xi
of
T_{\delta}\partial_{x}
is non-negative and even.
The same is true for the operator
H\partial_{x}
.

2.3.
Properties of the space
\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}
and control of Littlewood-Paley operators

We begin this subsection with a basic estimate in
\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho}
, which will be used in Section
4
:

(2.6)

\rho\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\rho+\big\||f|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\qquad(f\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho}).

The first inequality is clear and follows from the definition of
\rho
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
The second inequality is obtained by Gagliardo-Nirenberg and Young’s inequalities as follows:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{2}+\big\||f|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\rho^{2}+C\big\||f|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+C\big\||f|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}.

Next, from Sobolev’s embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
we see that for any integer
k\geq 1
,
f\in\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}
and
g\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R})
, it holds
f+g\in\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}
and

(2.7)

\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(f+g)\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(f)+\|g\|_{H^{k}}^{2}.

In a similar manner (but using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality instead of Sobolev’s embedding), for
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho}
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we can show that

(2.8)

\displaystyle d^{1}_{\rho}(f_{1}+g_{1},f_{2}+g_{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\|g_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big)d^{1}_{\rho}(f_{1},f_{2})

\displaystyle+\big(1+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|g_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|g_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}(g_{1}-g_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

Below, we collect some estimates for frequency localized functions.
They are all easily proven using Bernstein’s inequality.
For any
\ell\geq 0
and
k,j\geq 1
, we can show that
P_{\leq\ell}:\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}\to\mathcal{Z}^{k+j}_{\rho}
with the estimates

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(P_{\leq\ell}f)&\leq\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(f)+\big\||P_{\leq\ell}f|^{2}-|f|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(f)+2\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{\geq\ell+1}f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(f)+2^{1-\ell}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(f)+2^{1-\ell}\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(f)\leq 3\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(f),\end{aligned}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{k+j}_{\rho}(P_{\leq\ell}f)\lesssim 2^{j\ell}\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(f)+2^{-\ell}\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(f)\lesssim 2^{j\ell}\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(f).

We also see that
P_{\leq\ell}f\to f
in
\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}
as
\ell\to\infty
, thanks to the estimate

(2.11)

\displaystyle d^{k}_{\rho}(P_{\leq\ell}f,f)

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{\geq\ell+1}\partial_{x}f\|_{H^{k-1}}+\|P_{\geq\ell+1}f\|_{L^{\infty}}+\big\||P_{\leq\ell}f|^{2}-|f|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|P_{\geq\ell+1}\partial_{x}f\|_{H^{k-1}}+2^{-\frac{1}{2}\ell}\|P_{\geq\ell+1}\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}+2^{-\ell}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{\geq\ell+1}\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}.

Note that if
f\in C([0,T];\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho})
, then the above convergence is uniform in
t
.
Using the identity

(2.12)

\displaystyle|P_{\leq\ell}f|^{2}-|P_{\leq\ell}g|^{2}

\displaystyle=|f|^{2}-|g|^{2}-\Big(P_{\geq\ell+1}f\cdot\overline{P_{\leq\ell}f}+f\cdot\overline{P_{\geq\ell+1}f}\Big)+\Big(P_{\geq\ell+1}g\cdot\overline{P_{\leq\ell}g}+g\cdot\overline{P_{\geq\ell+1}g}\Big)

\displaystyle=|f|^{2}-|g|^{2}-P_{\geq\ell+1}(f-g)\cdot\overline{P_{\leq\ell}f}-P_{\geq\ell+1}g\cdot\overline{P_{\leq\ell}(f-g)}-(f-g)\overline{P_{\geq\ell+1}f}-g\overline{P_{\geq\ell+1}(f-g)},

we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d^{k}_{\rho}(P_{\leq\ell}f,P_{\leq\ell}g)&\leq d^{k}_{\rho}(f,g)\begin{aligned} &+\|P_{\geq\ell+1}(f-g)\|_{L^{2}}\|P_{\leq\ell}f\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|P_{\geq\ell+1}g\|_{L^{2}}\|P_{\leq\ell}(f-g)\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&+\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{\geq\ell+1}f\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{\geq\ell+1}(f-g)\|_{L^{2}}\end{aligned}\\
&\leq d^{k}_{\rho}(f,g)+2^{1-\ell}\Big(\big[\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(f)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\big[\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(g)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)d^{1}_{\rho}(f,g),\end{aligned}

and

(2.14)

\displaystyle d^{k+j}_{\rho}(P_{\leq\ell}f,P_{\leq\ell}g)\lesssim 2^{j\ell}d^{k}_{\rho}(f,g)+2^{-\ell}\Big(\big[\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(f)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\big[\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(g)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)d^{1}_{\rho}(f,g).

Finally, for different
\ell,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
f,g\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
, we use (
2.11
) with
k=1
to see that

(2.15)

d^{1}_{\rho}(P_{\leq\ell}f,P_{\leq m}g)\lesssim d^{1}_{\rho}(f,g)+2^{-\ell}\Big(\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(f)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(f)\Big)+2^{-m}\Big(\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(g)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(g)\Big).

2.4.
Estimates for the free Schrödinger operator

The action of
U(t)
in the Zhidkov-type spaces has been well-studied; see, e.g.,
[
18
,
11
]
.

A standard Fourier analysis with the pointwise bound
|(e^{it\xi^{2}}-1)/\xi|\lesssim|t|^{1/2}
reveals that

(2.16)

\|U(t)\phi-\phi\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}\phi\|_{L^{2}}.

Using Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we see that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\|U(t)\phi-\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{x}\phi\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|U(t)\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+|t|^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{x}\phi\|_{L^{2}},

and by (
2.16
) and (
2.17
),

(2.18)

\displaystyle\big\||U(t)\phi|^{2}-|\phi|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+|t|^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{x}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\Big)|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}\phi\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\big\||U(t)\phi|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\||\phi|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}+\Big(\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+|t|^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{x}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\Big)|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}\phi\|_{L^{2}}.

These estimates with the unitarity of
U(t)
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
imply that

(2.19)

\phi\in\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad U(t)\phi\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}),\quad\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(U(t)\phi)\lesssim(1+|t|^{\frac{3}{4}})\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(\phi).

Finally, we see the continuity of the mapping
\phi\mapsto U(t)\phi
by showing the difference estimate

(2.20)

d^{k}_{\rho}(U(t)\phi,U(t)\psi)\lesssim(1+|t|^{\frac{3}{4}})\Big(1+\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(\phi)+\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(\psi)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}d^{k}_{\rho}(\phi,\psi)

for any
k\geq 1
and
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}
.
To prove (
2.20
), it is clearly sufficient to estimate the non-Sobolev part of
d^{1}_{\rho}
.
From (
2.17
), we have

\|U(t)\phi-U(t)\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\phi-\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}+|t|^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{x}(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+|t|^{\frac{1}{4}})d^{1}_{\rho}(\phi,\psi).

Using the decomposition similar to (
2.12
)
and (
2.16
), (
2.17
), we have

\displaystyle\big\||U(t)\phi|^{2}-|U(t)\psi|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\||\phi|^{2}-|\psi|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}\begin{aligned} &+\big\|U(t)(\phi-\psi)-(\phi-\psi)\big\|_{L^{2}}\|U(t)\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|U(t)\psi-\psi\|_{L^{2}}\|U(t)(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&+\|\phi-\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|U(t)\phi-\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|U(t)(\phi-\psi)-(\phi-\psi)\big\|_{L^{2}}\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+|t|^{\frac{3}{4}})\Big(1+\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(\phi)+\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(\psi)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}d^{1}_{\rho}(\phi,\psi),

which shows (
2.20
).

2.5.
Nonlinear estimates and an integration by parts lemma

We give some nonlinear estimates in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\rho>0
.

\mathrm{(i)}

For
f\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho}
,

\|\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(f)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\delta^{-1}\big[\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(f)\big]^{\frac{3}{2}}.

For any integer
k\geq 1
and
f\in\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\rho}
,

\|\partial_{x}^{k}\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(f)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{k}\delta^{-1}\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(f)\|\partial_{x}f\|_{H^{k-1}}.

\mathrm{(ii)}

For any integer
k\geq 0
and
f\in\mathcal{Z}^{k+1}_{\rho}
,

\|\partial_{x}^{k}\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(f)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{k}\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(f)\|\partial_{x}f\|_{H^{k}}.

\mathrm{(iii)}

For
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho}
,

\delta\;\|\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(f_{1})-\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(f_{2})\|_{H^{1}}+\|\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(f_{1})-\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(f_{2})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\Big(1+\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(f_{1})+\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(f_{2})\Big)d^{1}_{\rho}(f_{1},f_{2}).

Proof.

We use the boundedness of
\mathcal{L}_{\delta}
((
2.2
), (
2.4
)) and of
H
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for estimates of
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
and those of
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
, respectively.
In the case of large
k
, we use appropriate interpolation inequalities to bound
\|\partial_{x}^{j}f\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\partial_{x}^{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}}
for
1\leq j\leq k-1
in terms of
\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\|_{L^{2}}
.
A detailed verification is left to the reader.
∎

We conclude this section with the following lemma, which describes typical integration-by-parts procedure in the energy method.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, and
g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. The following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\big|\Re\big(f(\overline{f}\partial_{x}g),g\big)_{L^{2}}\big|+\big|\Re\big(f(f\partial_{x}\overline{g}),g\big)_{L^{2}}\big|+\big|\Re\big(f(iH)(\overline{f}\partial_{x}g),g\big)_{L^{2}}\big|\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Remark 2.1
.

We do not have a similar estimate for the term
\Re\big(f(iH)(f\partial_{x}\overline{g}),g\big)_{L^{2}}
, which suggests that the standard energy method is not sufficient to control this type of interactions.
We will use the modified energy argument to handle this case.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

The first two terms are easily treated by integration by parts (one can estimate the second term without taking the real part).
For the term involving
H
, an integration by parts yields

\Re\big(f(iH)(\overline{f}\partial_{x}g),g\big)_{L^{2}}=\Re\big(iH\partial_{x}(\overline{f}g),\overline{f}g\big)_{L^{2}}-\Re\big(f(iH)(g\partial_{x}\overline{f}),g\big)_{L^{2}}.

Observe that the first term on the right-hand side vanishes by the positivity of
H\partial_{x}
, then we obtain the desired bound with no derivative on
g
.
∎

3.
Local well-posedness

In this section, we prove our main local well-posedness result, Theorem
1.1
.
Throughout this section, we fix
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and do not indicate the dependence of constants on these parameters.

We employ a compactness argument.
For
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, consider the mollified equation for (
gINLS
):

(3.1)

i\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u=P_{\leq\ell}\mathcal{N}(P_{\leq\ell}u).

The associated integral equation is given by

(3.2)

u=U(\cdot)u(0)+\Phi^{(\ell)}[u],\qquad\Phi^{(\ell)}[u](t):=-i\int_{0}^{t}U(t-s)P_{\leq\ell}\mathcal{N}(P_{\leq\ell}u(s))\,ds.

The proof proceeds in four main steps.
First, in Proposition
3.1
, we establish the existence of a unique local solution
u^{(\ell)}\in C([0,T_{\ell});\mathcal{Z}^{\infty}_{\rho})
to the mollified problem (
3.1
) with mollified initial condition
u^{(\ell)}(0)=P_{\leq\ell}\phi
.
Second, in Proposition
3.2
, we derive uniform-in-
\ell
existence time
T_{0}>0
and
\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
a priori bound for the sequence
\{u^{(\ell)}\}_{\ell\geq 0}
, valid for all
t\in[0,T_{0}]
.
Third, in Proposition
3.3
, we prove that
\{u^{(\ell)}\}_{\ell\geq 0}
is Cauchy in
C([0,T_{*}];\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho})
for some possibly shorter time
T_{*}\leq T_{0}
.
Finally, we pass to the limit
\ell\to\infty
and prove uniqueness and continuity of the flow map in
\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
, completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Fix
\rho>0
, and let
\phi\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho}
.
For any
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the initial value problem (
3.1
) with initial condition
u(0)=P_{\leq\ell}\phi
admits a unique maximal-lifespan solution
u^{(\ell)}\in C([0,T_{\ell}(\phi));\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho})
which belongs to
C([0,T_{\ell}(\phi));\mathcal{Z}^{\infty}_{\rho})
.
Moreover, if
T_{\ell}(\phi)<\infty
, then
\lim\limits_{t\to T_{\ell}(\phi)}\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))=\infty
.

Remark 3.1
.

From the proof below and the estimate
\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(P_{\leq\ell}\phi)\leq 3\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(\phi)
from (
2.9
), we see that
T_{\ell}(\phi)\gtrsim\big[(\delta^{-1}+2^{\ell})(1+[\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(\phi)]^{2})\big]^{-1}
.
This does not imply a uniform-in-
\ell
lower bound, however.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

It suffices to construct a unique local solution
u^{(\ell)}\in C([0,T];\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho})
to the initial value problem of (
3.1
) with initial data
\phi
satisfying
\mathrm{supp}\,(\widehat{\phi})\subset\{|\xi|\leq 2^{\ell+1}\}
and
\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(\phi)\leq M_{0}^{2}
, for some
T>0
depending on
M_{0}
and
\ell
.
Indeed, the solution
u^{(\ell)}
then satisfies
\widehat{u^{(\ell)}}(\xi,t)=0
outside
\{|\xi|\leq 2^{\ell+1}\}
for all
t\geq 0
, from which the regularity of
u^{(\ell)}
follows.

Recall from (
2.7
) and (
2.19
) that
\|\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(U(t)\phi+\Phi^{(\ell)}(u))\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq C_{*}\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(\phi)+C\|\Phi^{(\ell)}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1;H^{1})}^{2}
for some
C_{*},C>0
.
For
0<T\leq 1
, define the complete metric space
(X_{T},d_{T})
by

X_{T}:=\big\{u\in C([0,T];\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho}):\|\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\leq 2C_{*}M_{0}^{2}\big\},\qquad d_{T}(u,v):=\|d^{1}_{\rho}(u,v)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}},

and we shall show that the mapping
\mathcal{S}^{(\ell)}_{\phi}:u\mapsto U(\cdot)\phi+\Phi^{(\ell)}[u]
is a contraction on
X_{T}
.

By Bernstein’s inequality, Lemma
2.1
, and (
2.9
), for
u\in X_{T}
we obtain

(3.3)

\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[u]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}\lesssim T\big(\|\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(P_{\leq\ell}u)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}+2^{\ell}\|\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(P_{\leq\ell}u)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}\big)\lesssim T(\delta^{-1}+2^{\ell})M_{0}^{3},

and similarly but using (
2.13
) instead of (
2.9
), for
u,v\in X_{T}

(3.4)

\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[u]-\Phi^{(\ell)}[v]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}\lesssim T(\delta^{-1}+2^{\ell})(1+M_{0}^{3})d_{T}(u,v).

By (
2.7
) and (
3.3
), we have
\|\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(\mathcal{S}^{(\ell)}_{\phi}[u])\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\leq 2C_{*}M_{0}^{2}
if
T>0
is chosen as
T(\delta^{-1}+2^{\ell})M_{0}^{2}\ll 1
.
Moreover, using (
2.8
), (
2.20
) and (
2.16
), we can show that the mapping
t\mapsto\mathcal{S}^{(\ell)}_{\phi}[u](t)
is continuous in
\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho}
, and hence
\mathcal{S}^{(\ell)}_{\phi}
maps
X_{T}
into itself.
Furthermore, (
2.8
) with (
2.17
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) imply that

\displaystyle d_{T}(\mathcal{S}^{(\ell)}_{\phi}[u],\mathcal{S}^{(\ell)}_{\phi}[v])

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\|U(t)\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}+\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[u]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}+\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[v]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}\big)\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[u]-\Phi^{(\ell)}[v]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+M_{0}+T(\delta^{-1}+2^{\ell})M_{0}^{3}\big)T(\delta^{-1}+2^{\ell})(1+M_{0}^{3})d_{T}(u,v).

Therefore,
\mathcal{S}^{(\ell)}_{\phi}
is a contraction on
X_{T}
if
T
is chosen as
T(\delta^{-1}+2^{\ell})(1+M_{0}^{4})\ll 1
, and the contraction mapping theorem shows that there exists a unique solution
u^{(\ell)}\in X_{T}
to the initial value problem.
The uniqueness in the whole space
C([0,T];\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho})
can be shown by a standard argument; we have thus proven the proposition.
∎

Proposition 3.2

(Uniform
\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
estimate)
.

Let
\rho>0
,
M_{0}>0
, and
\phi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\rho}^{2}
be such that
\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)\leq M_{0}^{2}
.
For
\ell\geq 0
, let
u^{(\ell)}\in C([0,T_{\ell}(\phi));\mathcal{Z}_{\rho}^{\infty})
be the unique solution to (
3.1
) with
u^{(\ell)}(0)=P_{\leq\ell}\phi
given in Proposition
3.1
.
Then, there exists
T_{0}\gtrsim\big[(1+\delta^{-1})(1+M_{0}^{4})\big]^{-1}
such that for all
\ell
, we have
T_{\ell}(\phi)>T_{0}
and

(3.5)

\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi).

Moreover, for any integer
k\geq 3
we have

(3.6)

\|\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}}\lesssim_{k}\|\partial_{x}^{k}P_{\leq\ell}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi).

Proof.

To establish the uniform bound (
3.5
) in
\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
, the principal difficulty arises from a derivative loss in the highest-order energy estimate.
To overcome this, we employ a
modified energy method
: we design a frequency-localized correction term that neutralizes the problematic nonlinear interactions in the nonlinearity of (
3.1
).
In the following proof, we put

\zeta_{\ell}:=P_{\leq\ell}u^{(\ell)}.

Let
\ell\geq 0
, and take arbitrary
T\in(0,1]
with
T<T_{\ell}(\phi)
.
We begin by controlling the lower-order part
\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})
using the integral equation (
3.2
) and (
2.7
) with
k=1
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(U(t)P_{\leq\ell}\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}+\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[u^{(\ell)}]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(U(t)P_{\leq\ell}\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell}(s))\|_{H^{1}}\,ds\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(\phi)+T^{2}(1+\delta^{-1})^{2}\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}^{3},

where we have used estimates in Sections
2.3
,
2.4
, and Lemma
2.1
.

Now, we move on to the energy estimate for the highest-order component.
Note that the following formal computations are justified since
u^{(\ell)}
is a smooth solution.
Using the equation (
3.1
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\Re\big(\partial_{x}^{2}\partial_{t}u^{(\ell)}(t),\partial_{x}^{2}u^{(\ell)}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}=2\Re\big({-}i\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell}(t)),\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\Re\big({-}i\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\zeta_{\ell}(t)),\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}+2\Re\big(\partial_{x}^{2}\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)(\alpha+i\beta H)\partial_{x}|\zeta_{\ell}(t)|^{2}\big),\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}.

The first term is harmless and can be estimated directly by using Lemma
2.1
,

\Big|\big({-}i\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\zeta_{\ell}(t)),\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}\Big|\lesssim\delta^{-1}\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u(t))\big]^{2}.

For the second term, after distributing the differentials we easily estimate in the case where no function has three differentials; that is,

\displaystyle\Big|2\Re\big(\partial_{x}^{2}\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)(\alpha+i\beta H)\partial_{x}|\zeta_{\ell}(t)|^{2}\big),\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}-2\Re\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)(\alpha+i\beta H)\big(\partial_{x}^{3}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\,\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}+\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u(t))\big]^{2}.

Even for the remaining case, a standard integration by parts argument with Lemma
2.2
can be applied except for one term; that is,

\displaystyle\Big|2\Re\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)(\alpha+i\beta H)\big(\partial_{x}^{3}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\,\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}+\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}-2\beta\Re\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)(iH)\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u(t))\big]^{2}.

To summarize, we have obtained

(3.8)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\beta\Re\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)(iH)\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}+C(1+\delta^{-1})\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u(t))\big]^{2}.

The first term on the right-hand side contains a derivative loss that cannot be handled by the standard energy method, and we introduce a time-dependent corrector to compensate with this term.
For a frequency threshold
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
to be chosen sufficiently large later, define

(3.9)

\mathcal{I}_{2}(t):=\beta\Re\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)H\partial_{x}\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}\overline{P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}.

This corrector is designed so that its time derivative cancels the above problematic term in the evolution of
\|\partial_{x}^{2}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
Expanding the product in (
3.9
), we obtain the bound

(3.10)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\zeta_{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-n_{0}}\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))\|\partial_{x}^{2}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Differentiating
\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)
and substituting the equation (
3.1
), and using the symmetry property (
2.5
) for
H\partial_{x}
, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=2\beta\Re\big(H\partial_{x}\big(\partial_{t}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\cdot\partial_{x}\overline{P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+2\beta\Re\big(H\partial_{x}\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\cdot\partial_{x}\overline{P_{\geq n_{0}}\partial_{t}\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\beta\Re\big(iH\partial_{x}\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}+\mathcal{R}(t),

where the remainder term
\mathcal{R}(t)
collects all terms having at most two differentials on each function,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(t)

\displaystyle=-2\beta\Re\big({-}iH\big(\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\cdot\partial_{x}\overline{P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}\big(\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\beta\Re\big({-}iH\partial_{x}\big(P_{\leq\ell}^{2}\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell}(t))\cdot\partial_{x}\overline{P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\cdot\partial_{x}\overline{P_{\geq n_{0}}P_{\leq\ell}^{2}\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell}(t))}\big),\partial_{x}\big(\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)\big)_{L^{2}},

where for the first and the last terms we have applied an integration by parts.
Using Lemma
2.1
, we can control these terms as

|\mathcal{R}(t)|\lesssim\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))\big]^{2}+(1+\delta^{-1})\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))\big]^{3}.

Moreover, the main term in
\partial_{t}\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)
compensates with the first term in (
3.8
), that is,

\displaystyle\Big|2\beta\Re\big(iH\partial_{x}\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}+2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\cdot\partial_{x}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}\Big|

\displaystyle\quad+\Big|2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}^{2}P_{\geq n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}-2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+2^{n_{0}})\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))\big]^{2},

where we have applied integration by parts several times and used Bernstein’s inequality as

\|\partial_{x}^{3}P_{\leq n_{0}-1}\zeta_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{n_{0}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u^{(\ell)}\|_{L^{2}}.

To summarize, we have obtained

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=-2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}^{2}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C(1+2^{n_{0}})\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))\big]^{2}+C(1+\delta^{-1})\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))\big]^{3}.

Combining (
3.8
) and (
3.11
), we obtain the modified energy inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\big(\|\partial_{x}^{2}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)\big)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\delta^{-1}+2^{n_{0}})\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))\big]^{2}+C(1+\delta^{-1})\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))\big]^{3}.

Then, integrating it from
0
to
t
, and incorporating the bound (
3.10
) of
\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)
, we see that

(3.12)

\|\partial_{x}^{2}u^{(\ell)}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\partial_{x}^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\Big\{\big(2^{-n_{0}}+T(1+\delta^{-1}+2^{n_{0}})\big)\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}^{2}+T(1+\delta^{-1})\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}^{3}\Big\}.

Putting (
3.7
) and (
3.12
) together, we obtain

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)+\Big\{2^{-n_{0}}\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}+T(1+\delta^{-1}+2^{n_{0}})\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}

\displaystyle+\big[T(1+\delta^{-1})+T^{2}(1+\delta^{-1})^{2}\big]\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}.

We now choose
n_{0}
as
2^{n_{0}}=C(1+M_{0}^{2})
with a large constant
C>0
so that
2^{-n_{0}}\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)\leq 2^{-n_{0}}M_{0}^{2}\ll 1
, and choose
T_{0}
sufficiently small so that
T_{0}(1+\delta^{-1})\big((1+2^{n_{0}})M_{0}^{2}+M_{0}^{4}\big)\ll 1
.
Then, (
3.13
) and a standard continuity argument shows that

\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi),\qquad 0<T<\min\{T_{0},T_{\ell}(\phi)\}.

By the blow-up criterion in Proposition
3.1
, we see that
T_{\ell}(\phi)>T_{0}
and that (
3.5
) holds.

The a priori bound in high regularity (
3.6
) can be derived by a similar modified energy argument; we only outline the proof.
It suffices to estimate the highest component
\|\partial_{x}^{k}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
.
Arguing similarly to the proof of (
3.5
), by Lemmas
2.1
,
2.2
and interpolation inequalities, we deduce that

(3.14)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{k}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\beta\Re\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)(iH)\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{k+1}\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}^{k}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}+C_{k}(1+\delta^{-1})\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u(t))\|\partial_{x}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}.

Define the corrector as

\mathcal{I}_{k}(t):=\beta\Re\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)H\partial_{x}\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{k-1}\overline{P_{\geq n_{1}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}^{k-1}P_{\geq n_{1}}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

for
n_{1}\gg 1
to be chosen later. Then we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{k}(t)|\lesssim_{k}2^{-n_{1}}\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))\|\partial_{x}^{k}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{k}(t)&=-2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)\partial_{x}^{k+1}\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\big),\overline{\zeta_{\ell}(t)}\partial_{x}^{k}\zeta_{\ell}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}\\
&\quad+C_{k}\Big\{(1+2^{n_{1}})\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))+(1+\delta^{-1})\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))\big]^{2}\Big\}\|\partial_{x}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}.\end{aligned}

in analogy with (
3.10
) and (
3.11
).

Now, we invoke the
\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
bound (
3.5
). First, choose
n_{1}
as
2^{n_{1}}=C(1+M_{0}^{2})
with
C\gg_{k}1
, so that

|\mathcal{I}_{k}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad t\in[0,T_{0}].

Next, we have the modified energy inequality

\frac{d}{dt}\big(\|\partial_{x}^{k}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{k}(t)\big)\lesssim_{k}(1+\delta^{-1})\Big(\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)+\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)\big]^{2}\Big)\|\partial_{x}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}.

Noticing that
\|\partial_{x}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))+\|\partial_{x}^{k}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)+\|\partial_{x}^{k}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, we see that the function

X_{k}(t):=\|\partial_{x}^{k}u^{(\ell)}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{k}(t)+\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)

satisfies the differential inequality

X_{k}^{\prime}(t)\leq C_{k}(1+\delta^{-1})\Big(\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)+\big[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)\big]^{2}\Big)X_{k}(t),\qquad t\in[0,T_{0}].

Integrating it and recalling the definition of
T_{0}
, we obtain

\|X_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}}\leq X_{k}(0)e^{C_{k}T_{0}(1+\delta^{-1})\big(\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)+[\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)]^{2}\big)}\leq Ce^{C_{k}}\Big(\|\partial_{x}^{k}P_{\leq\ell}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\phi)\Big).

Since
\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)}(t))\lesssim X_{k}(t)
for
t\in[0,T_{0}]
by (
3.5
), this yields claim (
3.6
).
∎

Proposition 3.3

(
\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{\rho}
difference estimate)
.

Let
\delta>0
,
\rho>0
,
M_{0}>0
, and let
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\rho}^{2}
satisfy
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}^{2}(\phi)\leq M_{0}^{2}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}^{2}(\psi)\leq M_{0}^{2}
.
For
\ell,m\geq 0
, let
u^{(\ell)},v^{(m)}\in C([0,T_{0}];\mathcal{Z}^{\infty}_{\rho})
be the solutions to (
3.1
) with the mollification parameters
\ell,m
and the initial data
P_{\leq\ell}\phi
and
P_{\leq m}\psi
, respectively, where
T_{0}=T_{0}(\delta,M_{0})\in(0,1]
is the constant given in Proposition
3.2
.
Then, there exists
T_{*}\in(0,T_{0}]
with
T_{*}\gtrsim\big[(1+\delta^{-1})(1+M_{0}^{4})\big]^{-1}
such that we have

(3.15)

\|d_{\rho}^{1}(u^{(\ell)},v^{(m)})\|_{L_{T_{*}}^{\infty}}\lesssim(1+M_{0})d_{\rho}^{1}(\phi,\psi)+(1+M_{0}^{3})(2^{-\frac{1}{2}\ell}+2^{-\frac{1}{2}m}).

In particular, if
\phi=\psi
, then

(3.16)

\|d_{\rho}^{1}(u^{(\ell)},u^{(m)})\|_{L_{T_{*}}^{\infty}}\lesssim(1+M_{0}^{3})(2^{-\frac{1}{2}\ell}+2^{-\frac{1}{2}m}).

Proof.

In the following, we write

\zeta_{\ell}:=P_{\leq\ell}u^{(\ell)},\quad\eta_{m}:=P_{\leq m}v^{(m)},\quad\omega_{\ell,m}:=\zeta_{\ell}-\eta_{m},\quad\mu_{\ell,m}:=\frac{\zeta_{\ell}+\eta_{m}}{2}.

Take an arbitrary
T\in(0,T_{0}]
, and recall
T(1+\delta^{-1})(1+M_{0}^{4})\ll 1
by the definition of
T_{0}
.
We see from Proposition
3.2
and (
2.9
) that

(3.17)

\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\zeta_{\ell})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}+\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(\eta_{m})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}+\|\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(v^{(m)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\lesssim M_{0}^{2}.

These bounds and Lemma
2.1
imply that

\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[u^{(\ell)}]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}+\|\Phi^{(m)}[v^{(m)}]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}\lesssim T(1+\delta^{-1})M_{0}^{3}\lesssim 1.

Moreover, we have the following difference estimates:
by (
2.20
) and (
2.15
),

\displaystyle\|d^{1}_{\rho}(U(t)P_{\leq\ell}\phi,U(t)P_{\leq m}\psi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+M_{0})d^{1}_{\rho}(P_{\leq\ell}\phi,P_{\leq m}\psi)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+M_{0})d^{1}_{\rho}(\phi,\psi)+(2^{-\ell}+2^{-m})(1+M_{0}^{3}),

and by Bernstein’s inequality, Lemma
2.1
, and (
2.15
),

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[u^{(\ell)}]-\Phi^{(m)}[v^{(m)}]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T\Big(2^{-\ell}\|\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}+2^{-m}\|\mathcal{N}(\eta_{m})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}+\|\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell})-\mathcal{N}(\eta_{m})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{-\ell}+2^{-m})T(1+\delta^{-1})(1+M_{0}^{3})+T(1+\delta^{-1})(1+M_{0}^{2})\|d^{1}_{\rho}(\zeta_{\ell},\eta_{m})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{-\ell}+2^{-m})T(1+\delta^{-1})(1+M_{0}^{4})+T(1+\delta^{-1})(1+M_{0}^{2})\|d^{1}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)},v^{(m)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}.

Combining the above estimates and (
2.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|d^{1}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)},v^{(m)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[u^{(\ell)}]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}})\|d^{1}_{\rho}(U(t)P_{\leq\ell}\phi,U(t)P_{\leq m}\psi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}+\|\partial_{x}(\Phi^{(\ell)}[u^{(\ell)}]-\Phi^{(m)}[v^{(m)}])\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\big(1+\|U(t)P_{\leq m}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}+\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[u^{(\ell)}]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}+\|\Phi^{(m)}[v^{(m)}]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}\big)\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[u^{(\ell)}]-\Phi^{(m)}[v^{(m)}]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|d^{1}_{\rho}(U(t)P_{\leq\ell}\phi,U(t)P_{\leq m}\psi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{(\ell)}-v^{(m)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}+(1+M_{0})\|\Phi^{(\ell)}[u^{(\ell)}]-\Phi^{(m)}[v^{(m)}]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+M_{0})d^{1}_{\rho}(\phi,\psi)+(2^{-\ell}+2^{-m})(1+M_{0}^{3})+\|\partial_{x}(u^{(\ell)}-v^{(m)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+(2^{-\ell}+2^{-m})T(1+\delta^{-1})(1+M_{0}^{5})+T(1+\delta^{-1})(1+M_{0}^{3})\|d^{1}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)},v^{(m)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}},

where at the second inequality we have used the equation as
\Phi^{(\ell)}[u^{(\ell)}]=u^{(\ell)}-U(t)P_{\leq\ell}\phi
.
Imposing further smallness
T(1+\delta^{-1})(1+M_{0}^{4})\ll 1
if necessary, we can absorb the last term into the left-hand side and obtain the control of lower order terms,

(3.18)

\|d^{1}_{\rho}(u^{(\ell)},v^{(m)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\lesssim(1+M_{0})d^{1}_{\rho}(\phi,\psi)+(2^{-\ell}+2^{-m})(1+M_{0}^{3})+\|\partial_{x}(u^{(\ell)}-v^{(m)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}.

We move on to the energy estimate for the highest order term.
Using the equation (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\big\|\partial_{x}\big(u^{(\ell)}(t)-v^{(m)}(t)\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\Re\big({-}i\partial_{x}\big(P_{\leq\ell}\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell}(t))-P_{\leq m}\mathcal{N}(\eta_{m}(t))\big),\partial_{x}\big(u^{(\ell)}(t)-v^{(m)}(t)\big)\big)_{L^{2}}

We first rearrange the frequency projections by using Bernstein’s inequality, integration by parts, Lemma
2.1
and the uniform
\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\rho}
bound (
3.17
), as

\frac{d}{dt}\big\|\partial_{x}\big(u^{(\ell)}(t)-v^{(m)}(t)\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=2\Re\big({-}i\partial_{x}\big(\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell}(t))-\mathcal{N}(\eta_{m}(t))\big),\partial_{x}\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)-\eta_{m}(t)\big)\big)_{L^{2}}+\mathcal{R}_{1}(t),

with

|\mathcal{R}_{1}(t)|\lesssim(2^{-\ell}+2^{-m})(1+\delta^{-1})M_{0}^{4}.

Next, we expand the difference of the nonlinearity as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell})-\mathcal{N}(\eta_{m})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\zeta_{\ell})-\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\eta_{m})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{i}{2}\omega_{\ell,m}(\alpha+i\beta H)\partial_{x}(|\zeta_{\ell}|^{2}+|\eta_{m}|^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+i\mu_{\ell,m}(\alpha+i\beta H)\partial_{x}(\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}}\omega_{\ell,m}+\mu_{\ell,m}\overline{\omega_{\ell,m}}),

and hence

\displaystyle 2\Re\big({-}i\partial_{x}\big(\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell}(t))-\mathcal{N}(\eta_{m}(t))\big),\partial_{x}\big(\zeta_{\ell}(t)-\eta_{m}(t)\big)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\Re\big({-}i\partial_{x}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\zeta_{\ell}(t))-\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\eta_{m}(t))\big),\partial_{x}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\Re\big(\partial_{x}\big(\omega_{\ell,m}(t)(\alpha+i\beta H)\partial_{x}(|\zeta_{\ell}(t)|^{2}+|\eta_{m}(t)|^{2})\big),\partial_{x}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\Re\big(\partial_{x}\big(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)(\alpha+i\beta H)\partial_{x}(\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}(t)}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)+\mu_{\ell,m}(t)\overline{\omega_{\ell,m}(t)})\big),\partial_{x}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}.

The contribution of the
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
part is easily estimated with Lemma
2.1
, by

\|\partial_{x}(\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\zeta_{\ell})-\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\eta_{m}))\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}\omega_{\ell,m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}\lesssim\delta^{-1}(1+M_{0}^{2})\|d^{1}_{\rho}(\zeta_{\ell},\eta_{m})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}^{2}.

For the other terms, integration by parts argument with Lemma
2.2
shows that the only problematic interaction is

2\beta\Re\big(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)(iH)(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{\omega_{\ell,m}(t)})),\partial_{x}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)_{L^{2}},

while the others can be bounded by
M_{0}^{2}\|d^{1}_{\rho}(\zeta_{\ell},\eta_{m})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}^{2}
.
So far, we have proven that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\big\|\partial_{x}\big(u^{(\ell)}(t)-v^{(m)}(t)\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\beta\Re\big(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)(iH)(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{\omega_{\ell,m}(t)}),\partial_{x}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C(2^{-\ell}+2^{-m})(1+\delta^{-1})M_{0}^{4}+C(1+\delta^{-1})(1+M_{0}^{2})\|d^{1}_{\rho}(\zeta_{\ell},\eta_{m})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}^{2}.

A corrector to cancel the problematic interaction in this case is given by

\mathcal{J}(t):=\beta\Re\big(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)H\partial_{x}\big(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)\overline{P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)}\big),P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

with a large integer
n_{2}
to be chosen later.
Similar to the proof of Proposition
3.2
, we see that

(3.20)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{J}(t)

\displaystyle=-2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}\big(\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}(t)}P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)\big)_{L^{2}}+\mathcal{R}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=-2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{\omega_{\ell,m}(t)}\big),\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}(t)}\partial_{x}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}+\mathcal{R}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{R}_{3}(t),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle:=-2\beta\Re\big({-}iH\big(\partial_{x}^{2}\mu_{\ell,m}(t)\overline{P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}\big(\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}(t)}P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\;\quad+\beta\Re\big({-}iH\partial_{x}\big((P_{\leq\ell}^{2}\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell}(t))+P_{\leq m}^{2}\mathcal{N}(\eta_{m}(t)))\overline{P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)}\big),\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}(t)}P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\;\quad-2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)\overline{P_{\geq n_{2}}(P_{\leq\ell}^{2}\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{\ell}(t))-P_{\leq m}^{2}\mathcal{N}(\eta_{m}(t))}\big),\partial_{x}\big(\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}(t)}P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)\big)_{L^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{3}(t)

\displaystyle:=2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{P_{\leq n_{2}-1}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}\big(\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}(t)}P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\;\quad+2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\partial_{x}\mu_{\ell,m}(t)\cdot\partial_{x}\overline{P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}(t)}\cdot P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)-\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}(t)}\partial_{x}P_{\leq n_{2}-1}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\;\quad+2\beta\Re\big(iH\big(\mu_{\ell,m}(t)\partial_{x}\overline{P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)}\big),\partial_{x}\big(\partial_{x}\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}(t)}\cdot P_{\geq n_{2}}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)-\overline{\mu_{\ell,m}(t)}\partial_{x}P_{\leq n_{2}-1}\omega_{\ell,m}(t)\big)\big)_{L^{2}}.

Invoking the estimates obtained so far, we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{2}(t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{-\ell}+2^{-m})(1+\delta^{-1})M_{0}^{6}+(1+\delta^{-1})(M_{0}^{2}+M_{0}^{4})\|d^{1}_{\rho}(\zeta_{\ell},\eta_{m})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}^{2},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{3}(t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+2^{n_{2}})M_{0}^{2}\|d^{1}_{\rho}(\zeta_{\ell},\eta_{m})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}^{2}.

Putting (
3.19
)–(
3.21
) together, we obtain the estimate

(3.22)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\big\|\partial_{x}\big(u^{(\ell)}(t)-v^{(m)}(t)\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{J}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{-\ell}+2^{-m})(1+\delta^{-1})(M_{0}^{4}+M_{0}^{6})

\displaystyle+(1+\delta^{-1})(1+2^{n_{2}}M_{0}^{2}+M_{0}^{4})\|d^{1}_{\rho}(\zeta_{\ell},\eta_{m})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}^{2}.

Integrating (
3.22
) from
0
to
t
, together with the estimate

\|\mathcal{J}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\lesssim 2^{-n_{2}}M_{0}^{2}\|d^{1}_{\rho}(\zeta_{\ell},\eta_{m})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}^{2},

yields

\displaystyle\left|F_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{e^{i\pi\varepsilon}e^{\pi\xi}-e^{-i\pi\varepsilon}e^{-\pi\xi}}-\frac{1}{e^{i\pi\varepsilon}e^{-\pi\xi}-e^{-i\pi\varepsilon}e^{\pi\xi}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{(e^{i\pi\varepsilon}+e^{-i\pi\varepsilon})(e^{-\pi\xi}-e^{\pi\xi})}{e^{2i\pi\varepsilon}+e^{-2i\pi\varepsilon}-(e^{2\pi\xi}+e^{-2\pi\xi})}\right|=\left|\frac{2\cos{(\pi\varepsilon)}(e^{-\pi\xi}-e^{\pi\xi})}{2\cos{(2\pi\varepsilon)}-(e^{2\pi\xi}+e^{-2\pi\xi})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2|e^{-\pi\xi}-e^{\pi\xi}|}{e^{2\pi\xi}+e^{-2\pi\xi}-2}=\frac{2|e^{-\pi\xi}-e^{\pi\xi}|}{|e^{\pi\xi}-e^{-\pi\xi}|^{2}}=\frac{2}{|e^{\pi\xi}-e^{-\pi\xi}|}.

By (
A.6
), we see that
F_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
for
|\xi|>r
; for instance, we have

\left|F_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\right|\leq\frac{2}{e^{\pi r}-e^{-\pi r}}.

Thus, Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem shows that

(A.7)

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{|\xi|>r}F_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\phi(\xi)\,d\xi=\int_{|\xi|>r}\operatorname{cosech}{(\pi\xi)}\phi(\xi)\,d\xi.

Furthermore, by (
A.6
), we see that

(A.8)

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left|\int_{-r}^{r}F_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\left\{\phi(\xi)-\phi(0)\right\}d\xi\right|\leq\int_{-r}^{r}\frac{2e^{\pi\xi}|\xi|}{|e^{2\pi\xi}-1|}\left|\frac{\phi(\xi)-\phi(0)}{\xi}\right|d\xi\lesssim|r|\|\partial_{\xi}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}},

where the implicit constant is independent of
\varepsilon
and
r
.
Putting (
A.5
), (
A.7
) and (
A.8
) together, we see that

(A.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\phi(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\bigg\{\int_{|\xi|>r}F_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\phi(\xi)\,d\xi+\int_{-r}^{r}F_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\left\{\phi(\xi)-\phi(0)\right\}d\xi\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\int_{|\xi|>r}\operatorname{cosech}{(\pi\xi)}\phi(\xi)\,d\xi=\langle\textrm{p.v.}\operatorname{cosech}{(\pi\xi)},\phi\rangle.

Thus, we have proven (
A.4
) and the lemma.
∎

Now, we are in a position to prove (
1.1
).

Proof of (
1.1
).

It is sufficient to prove that

(A.10)

\mathcal{F}[\textrm{p.v.}\coth{(\cdot)}](\xi)=-i\pi\,\textrm{p.v.}\coth{\Big(\frac{\pi\xi}{2}\Big)}.

Indeed, (
A.10
) together with basic properties of Fourier transformations show that for any Schwartz function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[T_{\delta}f](\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\delta}\mathcal{F}\left[\textrm{p.v.}\coth{\left(\frac{\pi\cdot}{2\delta}\right)}*f\right](\xi)=\frac{1}{2\delta}\mathcal{F}\left[\textrm{p.v.}\coth{\left(\frac{\pi\cdot}{2\delta}\right)}\right](\xi)\mathcal{F}[f](\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathcal{F}\left[\textrm{p.v.}\coth{(\cdot)}\right]\Big(\frac{2\delta\xi}{\pi}\Big)\mathcal{F}[f](\xi)=-i\,\textrm{p.v.}\coth{\left(\delta\xi\right)}\mathcal{F}[f](\xi).

Note that

\frac{1}{e^{x}+1}-\frac{1}{e^{-x}+1}=\frac{e^{-\frac{x}{2}}-e^{\frac{x}{2}}}{e^{\frac{x}{2}}+e^{-\frac{x}{2}}}=-\tanh{\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}.

Hence, by Lemma
A.1
, we see that

(A.11)

\mathcal{F}\left[\tanh{\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}\right]\left(\xi\right)=-\mathcal{F}\left[\frac{1}{e^{x}+1}-\frac{1}{e^{-x}+1}\right]\left(\xi\right)=-2i\pi\,\textrm{p.v.}\operatorname{cosech}{\left(\pi\xi\right)}.

Furthermore, by (
A.11
), and
\mathcal{F}^{2}[f](x)=2\pi f(-x)
, we see that

(A.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[\textrm{p.v.}\operatorname{cosech}{\left(x\right)}\right](\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\mathcal{F}\left[-2i\pi\,\textrm{p.v.}\operatorname{cosech}{\left(\pi x\right)}\right]\left(\pi\xi\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\mathcal{F}^{2}\left[\tanh{\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}\right]\left(\pi\xi\right)=i\pi\tanh{\Big(\frac{-\pi\xi}{2}\Big)}=-i\pi\tanh{\Big(\frac{\pi\xi}{2}\Big)}.

Note that

(A.13)

\displaystyle\coth{(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{x}+e^{-x}}{e^{x}-e^{-x}}=\frac{(e^{\frac{x}{2}}-e^{-\frac{x}{2}})^{2}+2}{e^{x}-e^{-x}}=\frac{e^{\frac{x}{2}}-e^{-\frac{x}{2}}}{e^{\frac{x}{2}}+e^{-\frac{x}{2}}}+\frac{2}{e^{x}-e^{-x}}

\displaystyle=\tanh{\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}+\operatorname{cosech}{(x)}.

Moreover, we see that

(A.14)

\operatorname{cosech}{\left(x\right)}+\coth{(x)}=\frac{2+e^{x}+e^{-x}}{e^{x}-e^{-x}}=\frac{(e^{\frac{x}{2}}+e^{-\frac{x}{2}})^{2}}{e^{x}-e^{-x}}=\coth{\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}.

Using (
A.13
) twice,
(
A.11
), (
A.12
), and
(
A.14
), we see that

(A.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[\textrm{p.v.}\coth{(x)}\right](\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}\left[\tanh{\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}\right](\xi)+\mathcal{F}\left[\textrm{p.v.}\operatorname{cosech}{(x)}\right](\xi)

\displaystyle=-2i\pi\,\textrm{p.v.}\operatorname{cosech}{\left(\pi\xi\right)}-i\pi\tanh{\Big(\frac{\pi\xi}{2}\Big)}

\displaystyle=-i\pi\,\textrm{p.v.}\operatorname{cosech}{\left(\pi\xi\right)}-i\pi\,\textrm{p.v.}\coth{(\pi\xi)}

\displaystyle=-i\pi\,\textrm{p.v.}\coth{\Big(\frac{\pi\xi}{2}\Big)}.

Thus, we have proven (
A.10
); thus (
1.1
) is true.
∎

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
3.4
and (
3.27
)

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

(i) The first inequality follows immediately from the definition of
c_{j}[f]
.
We also see that
c_{j\pm 1}[f]\leq 2^{\varepsilon}c_{j}[f]
, which implies the second inequality.
For the last one, we easily see
\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\rho}(f)\lesssim\|\mathbf{c}[f]\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}
, while the converse is shown by the (discrete) Young inequality.

(ii) Using the inequality
\big|\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}-\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\big|\leq\|\phi-\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}
, we have

|c_{j}[f]-c_{j}[g]|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-\varepsilon|j-k|}\big\|P_{k}\partial_{x}(f-g)\big\|_{H^{1}}+2^{-\varepsilon j}\Big(\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\big\||f|^{2}-|g|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

The claim follows by Young’s inequality as before.

(iii) By Bernstein’s inequality, (i), and that
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we see that

\|P_{\leq j}\partial_{x}f\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{j}2^{k}\|P_{k}\partial_{x}f\|_{H^{1}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{j}2^{k}2^{\varepsilon(j-k)}c_{j}[f]\lesssim 2^{j}c_{j}[f].

Similarly,

\displaystyle\|P_{\leq j}f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\big\||P_{\leq j}f|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{\leq j}f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\big\||f|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}+\big(\|P_{\leq j}f\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|P_{\geq j+1}f\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\big\||f|^{2}-\rho^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(2^{\varepsilon j}c_{j}[f]\big)^{2}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}\big(2^{-2k}2^{\varepsilon(k-j)}c_{j}[f]\big)^{2}\lesssim 2^{2j}c_{j}[f]^{2}.

The claim follows from these estimates.
Thus, we have completed the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Proof of (
3.27
).

By Bernstein’s inequality, the
\mathcal{Z}^{3}_{\rho}
bound from (
3.24
), and Lemma
3.4
(iii), we see that

\displaystyle\|P_{k}\partial_{x}^{2}(u^{j}-u^{j-1})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k}\big(\|\partial_{x}u^{j}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}H^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}u^{j-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}H^{2}}\big)\lesssim 2^{-k}\big(\mathcal{E}^{3}_{\rho}(P_{\leq j}\phi)+\mathcal{E}^{3}_{\rho}(P_{\leq j-1}\phi)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k+j}c_{j}[\phi].

This gives (
3.27
) if
j\leq k
.
When
k<j
, we use the difference bound (
3.26
) instead, to obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{k}\partial_{x}^{2}(u^{j}-u^{j-1})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k}\|\partial_{x}(u^{j}-u^{j-1})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}\lesssim_{M_{0}}2^{k}d^{1}_{\rho}(P_{\leq j}\phi,P_{\leq j-1}\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k}\big(\|P_{j}\partial_{x}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|P_{j}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{j}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k}\big(2^{-j}+2^{-\frac{3}{2}j}+M_{0}2^{-2j}\big)\|P_{j}\partial_{x}\phi\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim_{M_{0}}2^{k-j}c_{j}[\phi],

as desired.
∎

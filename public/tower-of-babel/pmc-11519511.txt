Nanostructures/TiN layer/Al2O3 layer/TiN substrate configuration-based high-performance refractory metasurface solar absorber
Authors: Pei Zeng, Yuting Zhou, Chonghao Zhang, Jingtong Yao, Meiyan Pan, Yifei Fu, Hao Chen, Guanying Chen, Qian Zhao, Xun Guan, Mengjie Zheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Metasurface solar absorber serves as a kind of important component for green energy devices to convert solar electromagnetic waves into thermal energy. In this work, we design a new solar light absorber configuration that incorporates the titanium nitride substrate, aluminum oxide layer, titanium nitride layer, and the topmost refractory nanostructures. The metasurface absorber based on this configuration can achieve an average spectral absorption of over 91% and a total solar radiation absorption of 91.5% at ultra-wide wavelengths of 300–2500 nm. It is discovered that the excellent performance of the proposed metasurface absorber is attributed to the synergistic effects of surface plasmonic effect and Fabry–Pérot (FP) cavity resonance by comprehensive analysis of the simulated field distributions. Furthermore, the effect of geometrical parameter of the proposed configuration on absorber performance is studied, indicating the proposed configuration possesses a large fabrication tolerance. Moreover, the proposed configuration is not sensitive to the polarization direction and the angle of incident light. It is also found that the use of other refractory metal materials and other shapes as the topmost absorbent nanostructures also have good results with this configuration. This work can offer a universal platform for constructing and guiding the design of refractory metasurface solar absorbers.

Introduction

Most of the global energy is derived from fossil fuels1,2, and the excessive consumption of conventional fossil fuels will lead to the unacceptable energy crisis, with negative effects on the environment, economy, and society2–4. In contrast, solar energy is an attractive green renewable energy, which has advantages in an almost unlimited capacity, completely clean, and wide distribution. Making full use of solar energy can effectively mitigate the energy global crisis. Photothermal conversion devices are the key to effectively utilize solar energy, which is the most common and direct method to collect solar energy5–9. The most important component of the photothermal conversion system is the solar light absorber. Recently, plasmonic absorbers have obtained significant attention due to their peculiar property to trap electromagnetic wave in the specific bands10–16. Particularly, solar absorbers based on metasurface have been widely studied for excellent absorption in the microwave range since 200817. Numerous plasmon solar absorbers based on metal–insulator-metal (MIM) metasurface configurations can realize single-wavelength absorption or multi-band absorption13,18–20. However, their absorption bandwidth is normally narrow. The radiation wavelength range of sunlight on the earth’s surface spans from about 300 nm to 2500 nm. In general, the absorption band of an ideal absorber should include or even exceed this range. Moreover, the absorptivity of the absorber should be not sensitive to the angle of incident light due to the change of the sunlight angle. A direct way to realize wideband absorption is to pile up multiple functional layers in the absorber configuration, achieving several absorption peaks in the absorption spectrum to cover each other’s absorption valleys, thus expanding the spectral range of high absorption21,22. However, the absorptivity of this kind of configuration is also limited by the finite number of mixed layers in the configuration which is subject to interactions with adjacent functional layers. Additionally, the fabrication process for such multilayer configuration is usually complex and labor-intensive23. Noble metals have been applied because of their strong coupling effect and plasmon resonance behaviors24. However, further widespread application of most noble metals is still limited by the disadvantages of narrow absorption bandwidth, low reserves, and expensive cost. Therefore, extensively used solar absorbers with a wide absorption bandwidth are highly desired.

To achieve solar absorbers with a wide spectrum and high absorption, many researchers have explored solar energy absorbers based on titanium and its compounds5,14,25–28. Titanium is a high-temperature metal with the characteristics of low mass density, excellent corrosion resistance and physicochemical stability, which can be used in spacecraft, aircraft, and various mechanical parts. Besides, titanium and its compounds can produce strong plasma behavior25. Titanium and its compounds possess higher work temperatures compared with traditional noble metals such as gold29–32, silver33–36, and copper37,38. Moreover, the reserves of titanium on the earth are much higher than those commonly used noble metals. These above-mentioned properties make titanium and its compounds a promising material for solar absorbers. For example, Feng et al. designed a broadband, wide-angle, polarization-insensitive TiN-based solar absorber that achieves an average absorption of 91.3% at the wavelength range of 300 to 2500 nm39. But the wide-angle stability of this absorber needs to be further improved. Zhang et al. only used titanium nitride to create a high-performance metamaterial solar absorber, achieving near-perfect absorption40. However, the manufacturing process of this absorber is too complicated, involving several lithography and etching processes. Piao et al. proposed a metamaterial perfect absorber based on a triplelayer structure composed of titanium nitride (TiN) periodic nanoarray, dielectric film, and TiN bottom layer, achieving near-perfect absorption41. However, the trapezoidal unit cell of this absorber poses fabrication challenges, and can not be manufactured in industrial production. Kim et al. used a quantum computing-assisted optimization method to design an absorber with a high absorption efficiency (> 98%) in the visible/near-infrared wavelength range (400–1300 nm) where solar irradiance is high42. However, the absorption bandwidth of this absorber is limited. Huo et al. used the stacks with patterned TiN layer to achieve an average absorbance of 95% in the visible-to-near-infrared regime from 400 to 2000 nm43. However, the absorption bandwidth of this absorber still needs to be further broadened. Despite all the above-mentioned high-performance absorbers, practicable easy-to-manufacture high-temperature solar absorbers with a high average absorption rate and ultra-wide absorption band are highly desired and worth further development.

In this work, we introduce an innovative solar metasurface absorber configuration consisting of a titanium nitride (TiN) substrate, an aluminum oxide (Al2O3) layer, a thin titanium nitride (TiN) layer, and top refractory nanostructures to achieve wide-spectrum high absorption of solar radiation. Due to the mutual effect between the designed configuration and the selected materials, the proposed titanium nitride-based absorber can generate surface plasmon resonance and Fabry–Pérot (FP) cavity resonance, leading to the incident photons being strongly absorbed or scattered on the absorber surface. The energy of the incident electromagnetic wave (sunlight) is also localized in a sub-wavelength range, resulting in an obvious electromagnetic field enhancement effect. This phenomenon can greatly enhance the coupling interaction between the incident light and matter, and reduce the reflected light. The average absorption of the designed absorber can reach over 91% in the ultra-wide spectrum wavelength range (300 nm-2500 nm). The high performance of the constructed absorber is analyzed by the simulated field distributions. The effect of geometric parameters of the absorbers on the absorption performance is investigated, demonstrating large fabrication tolerance of our configuration. Moreover, the average absorption rate of the proposed absorber is extremely insensitive to polarized light and incident angle, which is still more than 85% at an incident angle of 75° under TE-polarization. The total solar absorption (AM1.5 data) of the constructed absorber is calculated to be 91.5%. Furthermore, our proposed configuration is proven to have certain versatility for other refractory metal materials and other shapes. This work can provide a good general platform to construct excellent refractory solar absorbers and provide a useful reference for the design of metasurface absorbers, which have great potential for various applications, such as solar thermal energy harvesting, thermoelectrics and imaging.

Configurations and theory

Our proposed configuration is constituted by a TiN substrate, an Al2O3 layer as dielectric, a thin TiN layer, and topmost refractory nanostructures. TiN and Al2O3 are chosen due to their high melting point. The top refractory nanostructure takes TiN nano-cuboid as an example. The metasurface absorber based on the above-proposed configuration is displayed in Fig. 1. The geometrical parameters L, T1, T2, and P represent the side length of the TiN cuboid, the thickness of the TiN cuboid, the thickness of thin TiN layer and the period of the TiN cuboid, respectively. The default parameters are as follows, the side length L = 250 nm, the thickness of cylinder T1 = 200 nm, the thickness of thin TiN layer T2 = 5 nm, and the period P = 500 nm. The thickness of Al2O3 layer T3 is fixed at 200 nm and the thickness of the downmost TiN substrate is set as 800 nm to avoid sunlight transmittance. The proposed absorber is calculated and numerically simulated using the FDTD method. Results and discussion

Simulated results

To show the advantages of the proposed configuration, as shown in Fig. 2, the absorptivity among TiN layer/Al2O3 layer/TiN substrate configuration (Configuration 1), TiN nano-cuboid /Al2O3 layer/TiN substrate configuration (Configuration 2) and TiN nano-cuboid / TiN layer/Al2O3 layer/TiN substrate configuration (Our absorber) are compared. The simulated average absorptivity of the three displayed configurations is 91% (green curve), 81% (red curve), and 74% (blue curve) in the same wavelength range, respectively. Configuration 1 presents two peaks with a spectral absorption of above 0.99 at 527 nm and 1325 nm wavelengths, which is mainly due to the intrinsic absorption effect of TiN material and Fabry–Pérot cavity effect. This configuration has an obvious absorption valley at 805 nm with a low absorption of only 0.30 which is attributed to the intrinsic loss effect of TiN material. Configuration 2 achieves high absorption effect in the visible band, but a relatively low absorption capacity in the near-infrared band. Compared to Configuration 1 and Configuration 2, the proposed configuration fully integrates the resulting absorption effect of these two configurations and enhances the absorptivity of the proposed metasurface absorber. It can effectively eliminate the absorption valley in the visible light waveband and improve the absorption performance in the near-infrared wavelengths.Fig. 2The simulated absorptive spectrum of the three different configurations of absorbers: TiN layer/Al2O3 layer/TiN substrate (Configuration 1), TiN nano-cuboid/Al2O3 layer/TiN substrate configuration (Configuration 2), and TiN nano-cuboid/TiN layer/Al2O3 layer/TiN substrate (Our absorber).

Mechanism analysis

In order to understand the fundamental physical mechanism of the proposed metasurface absorber, the electric field (|E|) distributions of three different configurations are calculated. Figure 3a,b gives the electric field distributions of Configuration 1 at the wavelengths of 805 nm, and 1325 nm. The strongest electric field region of Configuration 1 at 805 nm is located in the air, indicating that this type of configuration has a strong reflection at the wavelength of 805 nm. When the wavelength is 1325 nm, the enhanced electric field is mainly concentrated inside this absorber, which is ascribed to the Fabry–Pérot resonance. Figure 3c,d shows electric field distributions of Configuration 2 at the wavelengths of 815 nm, and 1488 nm. The strongest electric field is mainly located at the TiN/air interface at 815 nm, showing that the absorption peak at this point can be attributed to the comprehensive effect of the intrinsic characteristics of TiN material and the light-excited surface plasma resonances. When the wavelength is 1488 nm, the electric field at the TiN/air interface is significantly weakened, which is consistent with the absorption valley. Figure 3e,f displays the simulated electric field distribution results of our configuration at wavelengths of 868 nm, and 1655 nm. It should be noted that the physical phenomenon of the proposed configuration at the wavelength of 868 nm is similar to Configuration 2 at the wavelength of 815 nm. At the wavelength is 1655 nm, the excited strong electric field is mainly located at the TiN/air interface, which is mainly due to the light-excited surface plasma resonances. The enhanced absorption performance of our configuration results from the synergistic effects of surface plasmon resonance and Fabry–Pérot resonances, effectively integrating and amplifying the absorption characteristics observed in the other two configurations.Fig. 3The simulated electric field distributions. To further investigate the outstanding performance of our designed metasurface absorber, numerical calculations of the magnetic field (|H|) distribution and current density (J) at the resonance peak are presented in Fig. 4. At the wavelength of 868 nm, the incident light penetrates the thin titanium nitride and aluminum oxide layers and is reflected by the thick-enough bottommost titanium nitride substrate. Hence, the TiN layer/Al2O3 layer/TiN substrate at this peak can be regarded as a typical Fabry–Pérot cavity, which results in part of the magnetic field energy localizing at the Al2O3/TiN substrate interface. Titanium nitride is well known for exhibiting surface plasmon resonance in the near-infrared window45. It can be distinctly observed in Fig. 4a that the strong magnetic field is excited at the corners of TiN nano-cube due to the surface plasmon resonance of titanium nitride at the wavelength of 868 nm. Similar phenomena also appear in Fig. 4b, surface plasmon resonance and Fabry–Pérot resonance are also excited at the same positions. Additionally, a remarkable observation at the wavelength of 1655 nm is that a lossy Fabry–Pérot cavity with a rather low Q factor is formed in the Al2O3/TiN substrate interface, which further broadens the bandwidth of resonance peak. It indicates that Fabry–Pérot resonance plays a decisive role at this wavelength. As shown in Fig. 4c,d, the current is mainly distributed in the middle titanium nitride layer, which indicates the origins of energy loss that leads to the broadband absorption6,13. In summary, the excellent performance of our absorber originates from the combined and switching coupling effect between surface plasmon resonance and Fabry–Pérot resonance.Fig. 4The simulated magnetic field distributions and current density of our absorber. Geometric parameters investigation

The effect of geometric parameters on the absorption performance is further studied by scanning structure parameters using FDTD simulation. Figure 6a shows the simulated colormap of the absorption spectrum with different side lengths of the top TiN cuboid. It can be seen that the proposed absorber is not deeply dependent on the side length. The metasurface absorber can keep high absorptivity in the solar spectrum with side lengths ranging from 200 to 300 nm. Figure 6b shows the influence of the side length L on the spectral absorption of the proposed configuration. When side length increases, the absorption slightly increases and the absorption peak shows a red-shift trend. In general, the summing spectral absorption performance does not present an obvious fluctuation with the side length. As shown in Fig. 6c, the effect of the thickness of the top TiN cuboid T1 also has a similar phenomenon on the overall absorption properties. It can be seen from Fig. 6d that increasing the thickness of the TiN cuboid results in a decrease in absorption in the visible region and a redshift in the near-infrared absorption peak. Conversely, Fig. 6e shows that as the period P increases, absorption rate in the visible region gradually increases, while the absorption peak blueshifts and the average absorption rate do not change significantly in the near-infrared region. The influence of TiN layer thickness T2 is given in Fig. 6f. Without the middle TiN layer, the absorption performance drops sharply in the near-infrared region as shown by the pink curve, which also fully illustrates the advantage of our proposed configuration. With the TiN layer, the absorption performance weakens gradually with increased TiN thickness and the absorption peak blueshifts in the near-infrared region. Overall, the absorption performance of our configuration is not obviously affected by the fluctuation of geometric parameters. Hence, the proposed configuration possesses a large fabrication tolerance, significantly benefiting the practical manufacture and application of this kind of absorber.Fig. 6The effect of geometric parameters on the absorption performance of our proposed metasurface configuration. Polarization sensitivity and angle sensitivity

In practical application, sunlight radiation is mainly divided into TM and TE polarization, apart from an uncertainty in the incident angle. Hence, it is necessary to investigate the influence of light polarization and incident angle on the absorption performance of the metasurface configuration. Figure 7a shows a schematic diagram of the incidence angle of the sunlight. Figure 7b shows the spectral absorption of our absorber versus the polarization from TE to TM for normal incidence. It can be seen that the proposed metasurface absorber is not sensitive to the light polarization under the wavelength range from 300 to 2500 nm. This is due to the axis symmetry of the design and the isotropy of the used material. Next, the incident angle dependence of the proposed absorber under TE-polarization and TM-polarization are studied, as shown in Fig. 7c,d. The results show that the proposed absorber is also insensitive to the incident angle and still demonstrates a strong absorptivity for large-angle deflection incident sunlight. To intuitively demonstrate the extremely insensitivity property of the proposed absorber to incident angles, the relationship curves of average absorption at different incident angles under TE-polarization and TM-polarization are given in Fig. 7e,f. It can be seen that the average absorptivity can be maintained above 90% when the incident angle is less than 71° under TE-polarization and the average absorptivity can be maintained above 90% when the incident angle is less than 67° under TM-polarization. It should be noted that even when the incident angle is up to 75°, the average absorption rate can still exceed 85% under TE-polarization. It indicates the proposed absorber configuration can is well suited for large-angle incidence, which facilitates the practical application of sunlight absorber.Fig. 7(a) A schematic diagram of the incidence angle of the sunlight. Versatility of the absorber configuration

To further demonstrate the versatility of our proposed absorber configuration, we test whether the proposed configuration is applicable to other similar shapes and other refractory metals, with the simulated results given in Fig. 10. In Fig. 10a, the average absorptivity of the top refractory nanostructures using titanium nitride, titanium, vanadium, and tungsten materials are 91.18%, 89.61%, 88.29%, and 87.95%, respectively. Most high-temperature metal materials have certain broad-spectrum absorption performances of light due to the large imaginary part of the dielectric constant and the large absorption loss of light of these refractory metals. We change the shape of the top TiN nanostructures on the proposed configuration and obtain their absorption spectrum, as shown in Fig. 10b. All the curves show a good absorption effect on sunlight and the average absorption of the top TiN nanostructures using the shapes of cuboid, tri-prism, and cylinder are 91.18%, 90.20%, and 89.76%, respectively. However, using different shapes on the topmost TiN nanostructure will also have different effects. For example, when in tri-prism shape, the absorber has the best absorption performance in the visible band; when using cuboid shape, the absorber has the best absorption performance in the near-infrared band. This suggests that a specific shape on the top nanostructures can be designed and applied according to the required main working band. Conclusion

In this study, we demonstrate a metasurface absorber based on refractory nanostructures/TiN layer/Al2O3 layer/TiN substrate configuration for solar energy harvesting by FDTD method. The simulation carried out from 300 to 2500 nm, proves that the proposed metasurface absorber possesses a wide bandwidth of sunlight absorption, an average spectral absorption of over 91%, and a total solar radiation absorption of 91.5%. The field analysis indicates that the high performance of the metasurface absorber originates from the integrated effect of surface plasmon behavior and Fabry–Pérot cavity resonance. After that, the influences of various parameters of the proposed absorber on the absorption capacity are discussed. It is verified that the proposed absorber has a large fabrication tolerance and an excellent absorption property unrelated to light polarization and incident angles, making it advantageous in practical applications. The proposed absorber configuration also shows exceptional versatility for other materials and shapes of top nanostructures of the absorber. This work sheds lights on new paths to design high-temperature solar absorbers for thermal energy harvesting and manipulation and represent a substantial opportunity for real-life applications for refractory metasurface absorbers.

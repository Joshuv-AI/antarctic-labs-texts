Anisotropic spin-1/2 XXZ chains with uniform gamma interaction
Authors: S. Mahdavifar, D. C. Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In this study, we delve into the intricate ground state phase diagram of anisotropic spin-1/2 XXZ chains under the uniform Gamma interaction. Employing the robust infinite time evolving block decimation (iTEBD) technique and the Lanczos technique, we meticulously obtain the model’s ground state properties. Complementing our numerical analysis, we derive analytical approximations through a mean-field framework, transposed into the fermionic representation. Our investigation rigorously scrutinizes the energy spectrum, distinguishing between gapped and gapless domains at absolute zero. Furthermore, we thoroughly examine various order parameters including magnetization, staggered magnetization, chirality, and spin nematicity, which demarcate the diverse phases within the ground state phase diagram, each characterized by its unique long-range order.

Background

The study of low-dimensional quantum magnets, particularly spin-1/2 chains, has garnered significant attention due to the intricate interplay of quantum fluctuations and interactions that give rise to a rich tapestry of ground state phases1,2. The ground state phase diagram serves as a fundamental map that delineates the various quantum phases and the transitions between them, encapsulating the essence of the system’s quantum mechanical nature.

The symbol \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J>0$$\end{document} represents the antiferromagnetic exchange coupling and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta$$\end{document} denotes the anisotropy parameter. The variable N represents the size of the system, which corresponds to the number of spins. Studies of the ground state phase diagram of the XXZ chain model span decades and have been thoroughly explored in numerous outstanding books and papers3–7. It is well-established that at zero temperature, the ground state of the XXZ chain model exhibits three distinct phases. Specifically, for the limiting case where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta < -1$$\end{document}, the ground state is ferromagnetically aligned. Consequently, the system undergoes a first-order transition to the gapless Luttinger liquid (LL) phase at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta _c = -1$$\end{document}. Finally, through a continuous quantum phase transition at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta _c = 1$$\end{document}, the system transitions into the antiferromagnetic Néel phase.

The ground state phase diagram of spin-1/2 XXZ chains is a cornerstone in the field of quantum magnetism, offering profound insights into the behavior of one-dimensional quantum systems. These diagrams are pivotal for understanding the various magnetic phases and the transitions between them, which are influenced by the interplay of anisotropy and magnetic interactions within the chain. The spin-1/2 XXZ chain, a quintessential model in the study of low-dimensional quantum systems, is particularly celebrated for its rich phase structure and integrability, which allows for exact solutions and rigorous analysis. It serves as a fundamental model for exploring a wide array of physical phenomena, from quantum phase transitions to topological states of matter.

Introduced in 2006, the Kitaev model is a pivotal quantum spin model that has been referenced in key scientific literature8. This model is defined by a two-dimensional honeycomb lattice of spin-1/2 particles, where the Ising interactions between spins are dictated by the orientation of the lattice bonds. Notably, the Kitaev model applies to real-world materials, such as honeycomb iridates-compounds known for their honeycomb structure and significant spin-orbit coupling9 as well as other materials like \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha -RuC_{l3}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Na_{2}Co_{2}TeO_{6}$$\end{document}10,11. Honeycomb iridates have been recognized for their ability to exhibit quantum phase transitions and develop new magnetic phases through the Gamma interaction (GI)12. The GI, an extension of the Kitaev model, involves bond-dependent coupling that includes the product of two distinct spin components on adjacent sites, with varying signs or strengths depending on the bond.

The Hamiltonian of uniform GI is written as2\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} {\mathscr {H}}_{GI} = \Gamma \sum \limits _{n = 1}^N (S _n^x S _{n + 1}^y+\alpha S _n^y S _{n + 1}^x), \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where the amplitudes of off-diagonal exchange are characterized by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Gamma$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} symbolize the relative coefficient between different terms of off-diagonal exchange couplings. A multitude of studies have focused on the exploration of the Gamma interaction (GI) within spin-1/2 chain models, as documented in seminal works13–16. The Gamma model’s practical application involves a quantum simulator that harnesses the potential of trapped atoms and photons, leading to the derivation of an effective Hamiltonian13. This Hamiltonian emerges from a dual-atom strategy, where one atom type encodes the spin variable and the other mediates photon interactions. Confined within an optical lattice and linked to a cavity mode serving as a photonic reservoir, these atoms give rise to an effective Hamiltonian that mirrors the XY-Gamma model with additional terms. Adjusting the cavity’s attributes frequency, decay rate, and driving field allows for the tailoring of the Hamiltonian. The effective Hamiltonian is versatile, displaying various regimes such as weak-coupling, strong-coupling, and resonance, determined by the cavity-to-atomic frequency ratio. It encompasses diverse interactions like Heisenberg, Kitaev, and Gamma couplings, dependent on the cavity’s detuning and the driving field’s frequency. The phase diagram for the spin-1/2 XY model with GI, delineated in its ground state, identifies three distinct regions: gapless, gapped, and critical. The gapless phase is marked by an incommensurate spiral order, characterized by vector-chiral correlations, whereas the gapped phase is noted for its topological order, defined by the string order parameter. Additional research15 has shed light on the bond-alternating spin-1/2 K-\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Gamma$$\end{document} chain’s ground phase diagram, unveiling two predominant phases: the even-Haldane and the odd-Haldane. While the even-Haldane phase lacks topological traits, the odd-Haldane phase is topologically robust with symmetry protection. Near the antiferromagnetic Kitaev limit, two gapped phases emerge, distinguishable by their distinct non-local string correlators.

In this paper, we focus on the ground state phase diagram of the spin-1/2 Heisenberg XXZ chain model with uniform GI. The GI breaks some symmetries of the system, such as the time-reversal symmetry, the inversion symmetry, and the U(1) symmetry of the XXZ Heisenberg model. The time-reversal symmetry is the symmetry under the reversal of the direction of time. The inversion symmetry is the symmetry under the inversion of the spatial coordinates. The GI can induce spin chirality, which is a measure of the twist of the spins around the chain. The spin chirality breaks the time-reversal symmetry, as it changes sign when the time direction is reversed. In the context of anisotropic Spin-1/2 XXZ chains with a uniform Gamma interaction, understanding the ground state phase diagram is not merely an academic pursuit; it is pivotal for unraveling the emergent properties of the system, which may have profound implications for quantum computing and information processing. As such, the quest to obtain and comprehend the ground state phase diagram of this model is not only a step towards decoding the mysteries of quantum magnetism but also a stride forward in harnessing the potential of quantum phenomena for technological advancements.

In our study, we utilized the numerical infinite Time Evolving Block Decimation (iTEBD) and Lanczos methods to achieve the diagonalization of the Hamiltonian. Furthermore, we employed an analytical mean-field approach within a fermionic framework, which also proved effective in diagonalizing the Hamiltonian. Our findings indicate that the analytical mean-field predictions hold within the gapless phase of the pure XXZ model. For other phases, we applied our numerical algorithms, which allowed us to construct a detailed ground state phase diagram. This was accomplished by meticulously calculating various physical quantities such as the energy gap, magnetization, staggered magnetization, spin structure factor, as well as chiral and spin-nematic order parameters. These calculations enabled us to delineate the different phases and transitions of the system with high precision. The comprehensive analysis of these parameters sheds light on the intricate behaviors and properties of the system under study, providing a deeper understanding of its quantum phase transitions.

Therefore, to address the aforementioned eventualities in further detail, we organized this study as follows: In the next section, we introduce the model and employ the mean-field fermionization approach to derive the system’s spectrum. In Section III, we present our findings regarding the ground state phases and critical points. Finally, in Section IV, we provide our conclusions and a summary of the results.

Results

Within the framework of quantum many-body systems, order parameters serve as pivotal indicators that demarcate distinct ground state domains through their manifestation of order. These parameters assume a null value within disordered phases, signifying symmetry, and acquire a finite value in ordered phases, indicating broken symmetry. The role of order parameters extends to elucidating the essence of quantum phase transitions, driven by quantum fluctuations at absolute zero temperature. They provide insights into the character and classification of such transitions, which may unfold progressively as continuous transitions or occur abruptly in the case of discontinuous transitions.

The concept of chiral order parameter emerges as a quantifier of the helical configuration of spin alignments along a chain. In the context of spin-1/2 chain models, chiral order can arise from a variety of sources including competitive interactions, spin-orbit coupling, three-spin, or the influence of external fields. Such chiral fluctuations are instrumental in driving quantum phase transitions across different chiral states or between chiral and non-chiral states. Notably, a chiral phase is characterized by the breaking of time-reversal and parity symmetries without the onset of magnetic order. The implications of chiral phases and ordering in spin-1/2 chains are profound, underpinning the understanding of quantum magnetism, spin-liquid states, and topological matter. The presence of a chiral phase is discerned through the chiral order parameter, articulated as:15\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \chi =\frac{1}{N} \sum _{n=1}^{N} \langle S _{n}^x S _{n+1}^y- S_{n}^y S _{n+1}^x \rangle , \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\langle ... \rangle$$\end{document} signifies the expectation value in the ground state. Essentially, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\chi$$\end{document} quantifies the degree of non-coplanarity between adjacent spins.

The quantum spin-nematic phase is a unique state of matter characterized by spins in a chain aligning in two distinct directions, diverging from the typical single direction. This phenomenon disrupts the system’s rotational symmetry without affecting time-reversal symmetry. Such a phase is observable in spin-1/2 chains under specific interactions, for instance, the biquadratic XY model coupled with rhombic single-ion anisotropy25. Additionally, GI can induce a nematic phase in spin-1/2 chains under certain conditions, like proximity to the antiferromagnetic Kitaev region with a non-zero GI26.

The spin-nematic order parameter quantifies the extent of spin quadrupolar order within a system, where spins are oriented along two perpendicular axes instead of a singular axis. Typically, this order parameter is represented by a second-rank, traceless, symmetric tensor, defined as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Q_{\mu \nu } = \langle S_\mu S_\nu + S_\nu S_\mu \rangle$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_\mu$$\end{document} (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu = x, y, z$$\end{document}) denotes the components of the spin operator. It is essential to recognize that magnetization and staggered magnetization are pivotal observables that reflect the intricate quantum interactions within a spin-1/2 chain system. These response functions demonstrate the collective behavior of spins, which is instrumental in minimizing the system’s energy across various regimes. Their significance in determining the ground state phase diagram of spin-1/2 chains stems from their capacity to unveil the multitude of quantum phases the system may display. By scrutinizing the variations in these observables as control parameters change, one can pinpoint distinct phases, each characterized by its unique long-range order. This analysis is key to understanding the complex quantum states and phase transitions within spin systems.

Quantum phase transitions between two gapped phases in spin systems are significant for understanding critical phenomena in low-dimensional systems, which often exhibit unique behaviors not seen in higher dimensions. They are often observed in different quantum many-body models28–34. These transitions can reveal new types of orders, characterized by non-local order parameters and robustness against local perturbations, making them crucial for condensed matter physics. They are also important for quantum information science, as gapped phases in one-dimensional systems can be used for robust quantum computation due to their inherent stability. Additionally, understanding these transitions can lead to the discovery of new materials with unique electronic and magnetic properties. These transitions serve as a testing ground for theoretical models and numerical methods, refining our understanding of quantum many-body systems and the interplay between different types of interactions.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our comprehensive study of anisotropic spin-1/2 XXZ chains with a uniform Gamma interaction has yielded significant insights into the complex behaviors of quantum systems. The application of the iTEBD and numerical Lanczos techniques has allowed us to numerically diagonalize the Hamiltonian with high precision, revealing a rich tapestry of ground state properties. Our analytical approximations, facilitated by a mean-field approach in the fermionic perspective, have complemented the numerical findings and enhanced our understanding of the energy landscape at zero temperature. The meticulous analysis of order parameters has enabled us to map out a detailed phase diagram, showcasing the intricate interplay between gapped and gapless phases, as well as various forms of long-range order (see Fig. 6 and Fig. s1 to Fig. s3 in Supplementary Materials). This work not only advances our knowledge of quantum phase transitions but also sets the stage for future explorations that may uncover even more exotic phases and contribute to the development of new quantum technologies.

In addition, the impetus for this research is driven by the desire to decode the complex nature of quantum materials. Such explorations are crucial, as they lay the groundwork for transformative advancements in areas like quantum computing and the pursuit of high-temperature superconductors. Delving into the ground state phase diagram allows for the discovery of novel quantum states and unexpected phases, presenting challenges to conventional theoretical frameworks. The knowledge derived from these explorations aids in the creation of innovative materials designed with specific functionalities, potentially leading to a paradigm shift across various technological sectors, including energy solutions and computational systems. Additionally, the nuanced quantum interactions present in these materials offer a valuable testbed for pushing the boundaries of both computational and analytical methodologies, fostering progress in numerical simulations and theoretical constructs.

Melatonin alleviates oxidative stress-induced injury to nucleus pulposus-derived mesenchymal stem cells through activating PI3K/Akt pathway
Authors: Ze-Nan Huang, Ze-Yu Wang, Xiao-Fei Cheng, Zhao-Zhang Huang, Yan-Ling Han, Ya-Zhou Cui, Bo Liu, Wei Tian
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe changes in the microenvironment of degenerative intervertebral discs cause oxidative stress injury and excessive apoptosis of intervertebral disc endogenous stem cells. The purpose of this study was to explore the possible mechanism of the protective effect of melatonin on oxidative stress injury in NPMSCs induced by H2O2.MethodsThe Cell Counting Kit-8 assay was used to evaluate the cytotoxicity of hydrogen peroxide and the protective effects of melatonin. ROS content was detected by 2′7′-dichlorofluorescin diacetate (DCFH-DA). Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) was detected by the JC-1assay. Transferase mediated d-UTP Nick end labeling (TUNEL) and Annexin V/PI double staining were used to determine the apoptosis rate. Additionally, apoptosis-associated proteins and PI3K/Akt signaling pathway-related proteins were evaluated by immunofluorescence, immunoblotting and PCR. ECMs were evaluated by RT‒PCR and immunofluorescence. In vivo, X-ray, Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and Histological analyses were used to evaluate the protective effect of melatonin.ResultsMelatonin had an obvious protective effect on NPMSCs treated with 0–10 μM melatonin for 24 h. In addition, melatonin also had obvious protective effects on mitochondrial dysfunction, decreased membrane potential and cell senescence induced by H2O2. More importantly, melatonin could significantly reduce the apoptosis of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells induced by H2O2 by regulating the expression of apoptosis-related proteins and decreasing the rate of apoptosis. After treatment with melatonin, the PI3K/Akt pathway was significantly activated in nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells, while the protective effect was significantly weakened after PI3K-IN-1 treatment. In vivo, the results of X-ray, MRI and histological analyses showed that therapy with melatonin could partially reduce the degree of intervertebral disc degeneration.ConclusionOur research demonstrated that melatonin can effectively alleviate the excessive apoptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells induced by oxidative stress via the PI3K/Akt pathway, which provides a novel idea for the therapy of intervertebral disc degeneration.The translational potential of this articleThis study indicates that melatonin can effectively alleviate the excessive apoptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction of NPMSCs through activating the PI3K/Akt pathway. Melatonin might serve as a promising candidate for the prevention and treatment of Intervertebral disc degeneration disease (IVDD) in the future.

Introduction

Intervertebral disc degenerative disease is one of the most common causes of low back pain in middle-aged and elderly individuals and causes a serious economic burden to families and society [1,2]. According to an article published in the Lancet, the current prevalence of low back pain is 11.9 %, with the highest proportion in middle-aged and elderly people (42 %) [3]. Unfortunately, at present, either conservative treatment or surgical treatment, such as posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF) and transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF), can only relieve the symptoms and fail to restore the structure and function of the intervertebral disc itself [4]. Therefore, these long-term effects are not ideal. In recent years, mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), such as adipose-derived MSCs, bone marrow-derived MSCs, umbilical cord-derived MSCs, have been widely used in the treatment of intervertebral disc degenerative diseases [[5], [6], [7], [8], [9]]. In 2007, Risbud isolated and cultured mesenchymal stem cells from the nucleus pulposus for the first time, which provides the possibility for endogenous repair of intervertebral disc degenerative diseases [10]. Our previous studies also confirmed the existence of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells in both normal and degenerative nucleus pulposus tissues. Compared with exogenous stem cells, nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells can better adapt to the hypoxia and hyperosmotic microenvironment in the degenerative intervertebral disc [11,12]. However, pathological factors including inflammation, oxidative stress and annulus fibrosus rupture may cause the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) [[13], [14], [15]], and the accumulation of ROS in the disc will lead to the senescence and apoptosis of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells, and eventually to irreversible intervertebral disc degeneration.

Melatonin (N-acetyl-5-methoxytryptamine) is a multifunctional molecule. It is synthesized in the pineal gland and released into cerebrospinal fluid and circulation, where it shows a circadian rhythm, with a baseline level at day and a peak at night. Melatonin is also produced by several other organs and tissues, including bone marrow, the ovaries, skin, lymphocytes placenta and testicles. More importantly, melatonin can participate in the proliferation, senescence, apoptosis, inflammation and oxidative stress of NPMSCs [16]. Melatonin can enhance the ability of NPMSCs to resist degeneration of the extracellular matrix by activating the autophagy pathway [17]. In addition, it has been reported that the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/protein kinase B (PI3K/Akt) pathway is closely related to the apoptosis of NPMSCs [[18], [19], [20]].

Therefore, the purpose of this study was to explore whether melatonin can protect NPMSCs from oxidative damage induced by H2O2 by activating the PI3K/Akt pathway, which provides a new idea for the clinical treatment of intervertebral disc degenerative diseases.

Materials and methods

Isolation and culture of NPMSCs

16 SD rats, weighing 250g–350g, aged 2.5–3.5 months, were purchased from the Laboratory Animal Shandong Medical Biotechnology Research Center. All procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of Shandong Medical Biotechnology Research Center (2021110015). The separation method of NP organization is the same as that described in our previous study [11,[21], [22], [23]]. In short, after euthanasia, NP tissue of the caudal vertebrae of SD rats was collected under sterile conditions and placed in a 10 cm dish containing 1 % penicillin-streptomycin phosphate buffered saline (PBS) (Gibco, United States). After centrifuging 1000 rpm/5min, remove the supernatant and resuscitate with 0.5 % type II collagenase (American Gibco), then incubate for 6 h. After 1000 rpm/5min centrifugation, the cells were re-suspended in F12 complete culture medium (Cyagen,USA) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS; HyClone, USA) and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin, and cultured at 37 °C and 5%CO2. The culture medium is updated every three days. When the adherent cells reached 80 % fusion, they were digested and passaged at 1:3. These cells are observed and photographed under a microscope (Olympus, Japan). The P3 cells were collected for subsequent experiments.

Multilineage differentiation ability of NPMSCs

In order to identify the multi-directional differentiation potential of isolated and cultured cells, we induced them to differentiate into osteogenesis, cartilage and adipogenesis. According to the instructions of the manufacturer's kit (Cyagen Biosciences, China), we inoculated NPMSC into a 6-well plate and induced the cells to differentiate when they reached 70 % fusion. After washing the cells with PBS for 28 days, 24 days and 21 days respectively, 4 % polymethylene was fixed for 20 min, and then stained with Alizarin red (Sigma, USA), Alizarin blue (Sigma, USA) and Oil red O (Sigma, USA), respectively. finally, the stained area was observed by fluorescence microscope.

Surface marker identification of NPMSCs

In addition to the ability of multiple differentiation, according to the criteria for the definition of mesenchymal stem cells proposed by the International Society of Cell Therapy [[24], [25]]. Cell surface markers CD105 PE (BD Pharmingen TM, catalog no. 560839), CD90 FITC (Exbio, catalog no. 1F-652-T100), CD73 PE (BD Pharmingen TM, catalog no.550257), CD45 PE (BD Pharmingen TM, catalog no.341071) and CD11b/c (Biosciences, catalog no. 559868) are also used to label cells. When the cells reached 90 % fusion rate, the cells were digested by trypsin. After 1000 rpm/5min centrifugation and resuspension with PBS, analyze the labeled cells according to the standard procedure by flow cytometry (FACSCalibur, BD, LSR II, Becton Dickinson USA).

Application of H2O2 and Melatonin to NPMSCs

In order to explore the protective effect of melatonin on hydrogen peroxide injury, we studied the dose and time effects of different doses of hydrogen peroxide and melatonin to evaluate the effective protective dose and time of melatonin. According to the experimental results (see results for details), in subsequent experiments, NPMSC was pretreated with 1 μM melatonin for 24 h, followed by exposure to 200 μM mH2O2 for 6h. In order to further verify that the protective effect of melatonin is through the PI3K/Akt pathway, PI3K-IN-1 as PI3K selective inhibitors were treated for 2 h before NPMSC was treated with hydrogen peroxide. To sum up, we divided the experiment into four groups, namely: (A) control group; (B) H2O2 group（200 μM/6h）; (C) MT group (1 μM melatonin/24h + 200 μM H2O2/6h); (D) PI3K-IN-1 group (1 μM melatonin/24h + 25 μM PI3K-IN-1/2h + 200 μM H2O2/6h).

Cell viability assay

In order to detect the cytotoxicity of melatonin on nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells, we purchased a cell counting kit-8（CCK-8, Beyotime, China）. According to the protocol, NPMSC was inoculated in a 96-well plate with a density of 1 × 104 and cultured in cell culture incubator at 37 °C. After the cells were attached, 10 μl CCK-8 solution was added to each well for 24 h, and then incubated in the incubator for 2 h, the optical density (OD) value was measured at 450 nm with a Bio-Rad mini-plate reader (Bio–Rad, Hercules, United States). Calculate the cell survival rate: cell viability (100 % of control) = [(Ae-Ab)/(Ac-Ab)] × 100 %, where Ae, Ab, and Ac represent the A450 of the treatment, blank, and control groups, respectively.

5-Ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU) incorporation assay

In order to further verify the effect of melatonin on the proliferation of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells, we purchased an EDU cell proliferation detection kit (Beyotime, China). According to the manufacturer's instructions, NPMSC was inoculated into a 6-well plate with a density of 5 × 104 per well. The cells were cultured at 37 °C. After the cells were attached, 500 μl EDU was added to each well and incubated for another 2 h. Then the cell was fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde for 20min. After 0.5%TritonX-100 was used to permeate for 10min, the cell was incubated with click reaction mixture for another 30min. After that, it was washed with PBS for 3 times and stained with Hoechst33342 in the darkroom. Finally, the number of EDU positive nuclei was observed by fluorescence microscope and analyzed by ImageJ software (NIH, USA).

Senescence β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining

In order to verify the effect of melatonin on the senescence of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells, we purchased SA- β-Gal staining kit (Beyotime, China). According to the manufacturer's instructions, NPMSC was inoculated in a 6-well plate with a density of 5 × 104 per well and cultured in cell culture incubator at 37 °C. After treatment in each group, wash the cells with PBS and fix the cells for 15min with the fixed solution in the kit at room temperature. Then, wash the cells with PBS 3 times and incubate the cells with working solution in the kit overnight, and the cell staining was observed under microscope the next day.

Annexin V-FITC/PI staining

In order to assess the effect of melatonin on apoptosis of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells, we purchased AnnexinV-FITC/PI apoptosis detection kit (KeyGen, China) to analyze apoptosis of NPMSC. According to the manufacturer's instructions, after washing with PBS, each group cells were digested with trypsin without-EDTA, then resuscitated with 100 μl 1X binding buffer, and then incubated 15min with 5 μl Annexin-V and 5 μl PI in the dark condition. The positive rates of Annexin and PI were analyzed by flow cytometry (BD Company, USA).

TUNEL staining

To further evaluate the effect of melatonin on apoptosis of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells, terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase mediated d-UTP Nick end labeling (TUNEL) (Beyotime, China，catalog no. C1088) was used to detect the level of DNA damage. The cells in each group were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde at room temperature for 15 min, then permeated with 0.1%TritonX-100 for 5min, the cells were incubated with TUNEL reagent after being washed with pbs for three times, and then the cells were incubated with DAPI stain for 15 min, and finally the TUNEL-positive cells were observed by fluorescence microscope.

Protein stability by cycloheximide (CHX) chase assay

P3 NPMSCs were seeded on 6-well plates at density of 2 × 105 cells/well and incubated for one day. Then, the cells were treated with CHX for 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 h. After treatment each group of cells was harvested and lysed with RIPA lysis buffer. Western blot analysis was used to evaluate bcl-2 protein levels. Finally, the protein bands were analysed with ImageJ software and the bcl-2 protein half-life was calculated.

JC-1 assay for mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP)

In order to detect the effect of melatonin on the mitochondrial function of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells, we purchased iodide 5,5′,6,6′-tetrachloro-1,1′,3,3′-tetraethylbenzimidazolcarbocyanine (JC-1) detection kit (KeyGen, China). In the cells with normal mitochondria, JC-1 aggregates in the mitochondrial matrix to form a polymer, which emits intense red fluorescence (polarized). In damaged mitochondria, JC-1 exists in the cytoplasm in the form of a monomer and produces green fluorescence (depolarization) due to the decrease or loss of MMP. the NPMSCs in each group were observed using a fluorescence microscope. The ratio of green to red fluorescence intensity was calculated using ImageJ software.

ROS assay

In order to verify the effect of melatonin on ROS production under oxidative stress, ROS fluorescence probe dihydroethidium (DHE) kit (KeyGen, China) was used to detect ROS production in NPMSCs. According to the manufacturer's instructions, nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells were inoculated in 6-well plates with a density of 5 × 104/well. After different treatments, wash the cells for three times with PBS and incubate with 10 μM MDHE at 37 °C for 30min. Then the cells were observed by fluorescence microscope and analyzed by ImageJ software.

Immunofluorescence assay

The P3 NPMSCs from different groups were collected and fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde for 20 min. The cells were washed twice with PBS containing 0.5 % Triton X-100 for 20 min. Then the cells were incubated with 10 % bovine serum albumin for 1 h at room temperature, rinsed with PBS and incubated with primary antibodies, including PI3K (Cell Signaling Technologies #4257, USA); p-PI3K (Cell Signaling Technologies #4228, USA) p53 (10442-1-AP) and p21(10355-1-AP), Proteintech, USA, Bax (A0207), Caspase-3 (A0214) and Bcl-2 (A11025), ABclonal, USA; 1:100, p-Akt (bs-0876R), Bioss, China; Akt (Cell Signaling Technologies #4691, USA), at 4 °C overnight. The plates were washed and incubated with secondary antibody (1:400) (Bioss, China, catalog no. bs-0293M-FITC) for 1 h at room temperature. The cell nucleus was labeled by DAPI for 10 min and observed under fluorescence microscope.

Western blot assay

The collected cell proteins were treated with Total Extraction Sample Kit according to the manufacturer's instruction. Then, BCA protein assay kit (Beyotime, China, catalog no. P0010) was used to measure the protein concentration. Equal aliquots of the obtained protein were loaded and separated by sodium dodecylsulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE). 5 % Non-Fat milk was used to block the membranes for 2 h at room temperature after electrotransfered onto PVDF membranes (Millipore, USA, catalog no. IPVH 20200), and then followed by incubation with primary antibodies overnight at 4 C. The membranes were then exposed to the secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature. The bands were observed and photoed by a computer imaging system. The expression level was semi-quantified by Image J software (NIH, USA).

Total RNA from NPMSC were obtained using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, US, catalog no.15596-026) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The expression level was evaluated by the 2﹣ΔΔCt method. β-Actin was used to normalize the expression of the target gene. IVDD model induction

Eighteen SD rats were randomly divided into three groups (n = 6 per group): control group (no operation), IVDD group (punctured and treated with DMSO), and MT group (punctured and treated with melatonin). The IVDD model was established as reported previously [26]. Briefly, the rat was placed in a prone position after anesthesia with an intraperitoneal injection of 1 % pentobarbital sodium at 0.1 mg/kg, and a percutaneous needle puncture was performed with a 21G needle in the coccygeal intervertebral disc (Co 6–7). The needle was carefully inserted in the middle of the disc, perpendicular to the skin and parallel to the endplate, rotated 180°, and held for 5 s. After removal of the needle, the wound was covered with gauze and the rats underwent standard postoperative procedures. The melatonin was dissolved in DMSO (1 μM) and further diluted in saline immediately before intraperitoneal administration. Two weeks of puncture, the MT group received 100 ng/kg/day melatonin [27]. Meanwhile, the control group and IVDD group received an equal amount of saline supplemented with the required volume of DMSO.

X-ray and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) analysis

X-ray and MRI scans were performed before and 5 weeks after puncture. Disc height index (DHI) was evaluated by X-ray [28]. The degree of intervertebral disc degeneration was evaluated by using a 7.0-T MRI scanning system (Philips InteraAchieva7.0 MR, Netherlands) and using sagittal T2-weighted images to calculate Pfirmann grades based on signal strength [29].

Histologic analysis

Five weeks after IVDD model establishment, all rats were killed by excessive euthanasia. IVD samples were collected and fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde, and then decalcified with 10 % ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid solution. After paraffin embedding, the specimen was cut into 5-μm slices, and the histological images of hematoxylin-eosin (HE) and safranin-O/fast green (S–O) were analyzed according to the histological grading criteria reported by Norcross et al. [30].

Statistical analysis

Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS 19.0, and GraphPad Prism 8 was used to make statistical graphs. All measurements were performed in triplicate, and the results are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD). The Kolmogorov–Smirnova test or Shapiro–Wilk test is used to check whether the data conformed to a normal distribution. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to analyze the data from multiple groups. The student's t-test was applied to analyze the differences between the two groups. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Identification of NPMSCs

After isolation, NPMSCs aggregated to form a ring shape, and then differentiated and proliferated. The cells were spindle-shaped after passage (Fig. 2(A)). Similar to our previous studies [10,30], NPMSCs can induce osteogenesis, chondrogenesis and adipogenic differentiation in vitro (Fig. 2(B)), and their multilineage differentiation ability has been confirmed. In addition, the related antigens CD105 (97.5 ± 0.12), CD90 （98.9 ± 0.06）and CD73（98.7 ± 0.13）marked on the surface of MSCs were highly expressed in NPMSCs, while CD45（0.83 ± 0.07） and CD11b/c（2.45 ± 0.09）were expressed at low levels (Fig. 2(C)). Ideal incubation concentrations of H2O2 and melatonin

The effects of hydrogen peroxide and melatonin on the viability of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells were evaluated by the CCK-8 test. The results showed that an appropriate concentration of H2O2 could induce oxidative stress injury in nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells, and cell viability was properly inhibited when cells were treated with 200 μM hydrogen peroxide for 6 h. In the concentration range of 0.01–1 μM, culture of melatonin and NPMSCS for 0, 12, 24 and 48h had no significant effect on cell viability, but cell viability was inhibited at concentration of 10–20 μM (Fig. 3(A)). When NPMSCs were pre-treated with melatonin for 24 h h before H2O2 treatment, the viability of those cells (61.65 ± 2.92 %) was significantly higher than that of cells in 200 μM H2O2 group (44.64 ± 1.93 %) (Fig. 3(B)). Melatonin enhanced the proliferation of NPMSCs

The effect of melatonin on the proliferation of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells was evaluated by an EDU staining kit. As shown in Fig. 3, the number of EDU-labelled cells in the hydrogen peroxide group was significantly lower than that in the control group (P < 0.05), while the number of EDU-labelled cells increased significantly after treatment with melatonin (P < 0.05), but was still lower than that in the normal group (P < 0.05). When PI3K-IN-1 was used to inhibit the PI3K/Akt pathway, the therapeutic effect of melatonin was significantly weakened (P < 0.05).

Melatonin resisted NPMSC senescence

We evaluated the antiaging effect of melatonin on NPMSCs in two ways. First, cell senescence was detected by SA-β-Gal staining. According to the manufacturer's instructions, ageing cells are dyed blue. Our results showed that the percentage of blue-stained nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells treated with hydrogen peroxide was significantly higher than that of the control group (P < 0.05). However, after melatonin treatment, the percentage of blue-stained cells was significantly lower than that of the hydrogen peroxide group (P < 0.05), but still higher than that of the control group (P < 0.05). Second, matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) are markers of mitochondrial dysfunction and early apoptosis. In normal mitochondria, the membrane potential is polar, JC-1 is rapidly absorbed into the nucleus to form a polymer, and the fluorescence is red; when the mitochondria are dysfunctional, the mitochondrial membrane is depolarized, JC-1 is released from the mitochondria, the red intensity is weakened, and there is green fluorescence in the cytoplasm because of JC-1 monomers. Our results showed that the polarized mitochondrial membrane in the control group exhibited orange-red fluorescence. After treatment with hydrogen peroxide, the red fluorescence intensity was significantly weakened and the green fluorescence was enhanced (P < 0.05). Compared with the hydrogen peroxide group, the melatonin group was still orange-red as a whole. However, when treated with PI3K-IN-1, the protective effect of melatonin was weakened (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7(C) and (D)). These results suggest that melatonin can reduce the production of ROS induced by hydrogen peroxide and maintain mitochondrial homeostasis, but PI3K-IN-1 weakens these effects, which indicates that PI3K plays an important role in this process.

Discussion

Intervertebral disc degeneration disease (IVDD) has become one of the main causes of back pain in the past 20 years and low back pain (LBP) results in a loss of labor in middle-aged and elderly people worldwide that cannot be ignored [[1], [2], [3]]. Therefore, the high cost of treatment poses a serious economic burden on families and society. This has also made the treatment of intervertebral disc degeneration one of the research hotspots in recent years. With the development of tissue engineering, treatments based on mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) have gradually shown advantages. Previous studies have shown that degenerative intervertebral discs are in a microenvironment characterized by hypoxia, hyperosmosis and lack of nutrition, which leads to many problems for exogenous stem cell transplantation [[31], [32], [33], [34]], such as the risk of the cells being stranded in the transplantation area or even tumorigenesis. Therefore, the long-term effect is not very satisfactory.

Fortunately, a kind of mesenchymal stem cell, nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells, was isolated and cultured from the nucleus pulposus for the first time by Risbud in 2007 [10]. Our previous studies also confirmed the existence of NPMSCs in both normal and degenerative nucleus pulposus [11,12,[21], [22], [23]]. In this study, NPMSCs were isolated and cultured from the nucleus pulposus of SD rats, which conformed to the three characteristics of mesenchymal stem cells defined by ISCT: 1. adherent growth 2. potential for multidirectional differentiation, and 3. positive molecular antigen labelling on the cell surface. Compared with exogenous stem cells, nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells can better adapt to the hypoxic microenvironment of the intervertebral disc, and can better differentiate into nucleus pulposus cells, thus delaying the process of intervertebral disc degeneration. However, with the aggravation of intervertebral disc degeneration, inflammation, oxidative stress and other pathological factors lead to a decrease in nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells, which may lead to the failure of endogenous intervertebral disc repair [35,36]. Therefore, maintaining the number and vitality of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells in the process of intervertebral disc degeneration is particularly important.

Melatonin, N-acetyl-5-methoxy tryptamine (Fig. 1), is one of the hormones secreted by the brain pineal gland. Compared with other exogenous drugs, it has many advantages, such as the existence of its own metabolic pathway, which does not cause accumulation in the body, and its half-life is short [37,38]. In addition, melatonin has been reported to have a wide range of protective effects, such as anti-inflammation, antioxidation, circadian rhythm regulation, anti-ageing, and regulation of central nervous system excitation [39,40]. Thus, the present study evaluated the effect of melatonin on H2O2-induced oxidative damage in NPMSCs for the first time and explored its possible mechanism.

Although melatonin has been reported to have protective effects such as anti-inflammation and antioxidation, the concentration of melatonin is also controversial. It has been found that 0.01–1 μM melatonin has a positive effect on the proliferation of nucleus pulposus cells [17]. However, a concentration of 10–20 μM had a dose-dependent inhibitory effect. It has also been reported that melatonin concentrations that are too high can cause hypothermia, release too much prolactin and cause infertility, reduce male libido and cause other side effects [17,40]. This study found that melatonin in the concentration range of 0.01–1 μM had no significant effect on cell viability, but 10–20 μM had a negative effect on cell viability. What is more intriguing is that the protective effect of 1 μM melatonin on oxidative stress injury induced by hydrogen peroxide was most obvious. Therefore, 1 μM melatonin was used to treat nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells in subsequent experiments.

Cell senescence is characterized by the weakening of cell proliferation and differentiation and the decline of physiological function. EdU (5-ethynyl-2-deoxyuridine) is a thymidine analogue. As a probe for DNA synthesis, it mainly reflects the ability of cell proliferation. Our results show that hydrogen peroxide can significantly reduce the proliferation and differentiation of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells induced by oxidative stress, while melatonin can significantly reverse this trend but not return to the normal level. Similarly, there was a similar trend in cell apoptosis. In addition, some studies have shown that melatonin inhibits apoptosis of NP cells by regulating the nuclear factor-κ B (NF--κ B) signaling pathway [37]. BAX and bcl-2 are two main proteins that reflect the sensitivity of cells to apoptosis. Bcl-2 can inhibit the activity of caspase, thus inhibiting apoptosis, while Bax can promote the expression of caspase, thus promoting apoptosis. In this study, we found that melatonin could significantly inhibit the upregulated expression of P53, P21, BAX and caspase-3 induced by hydrogen peroxide, and restore the downregulated expression of Bcl-2 protein. The above results suggest that melatonin has an obvious therapeutic effect on the senescence of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells induced by hydrogen peroxide.

Collagen II and aggrecan degradation are hallmarks of IVDD, and MMP-13 can regulate their metabolism. Qi et al. found that oxidative stress could inhibit the expression of collagen II and aggrecan, which play a critical role in high glucose-induced damage [41]. As shown in our research, hydrogen peroxide upregulated the expression of MMP-13 but negatively regulated the expression of aggrecan and collagen II. Pretreatment with melatonin reversed this phenomenon. The results of the present study showed that melatonin treatment promotes ECM expressions by reducing the expression of MMP-13 increased by oxidative stress injury.

ROS accumulation is closely related to the occurrence and development of IVDD. ROS are an important intermediate in the pathogenesis of IVDD. This is because aggregated ROS can disrupt the dynamic balance of mitochondria by releasing cytochrome c and increasing the changes in mitochondrial permeability transition pores [42,43]. Similar to previous studies, our study also found that after nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells were cocultured with hydrogen peroxide for 6 h, cell proliferation and mitochondrial membrane function were inhibited but ROS production and apoptosis increased. The above results further confirmed that oxidative stress induced by hydrogen peroxide is harmful to the maintenance of the physiological function of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells. Ma et al. found that melatonin treatment of nucleus pulposus cells for 24 hours could significantly reduce the secretion of GSH and SOD [44]. In our study, melatonin significantly inhibited ROS aggregation induced by hydrogen peroxide and increased the ratio of JC-1 polymers to monomers. According to our results, melatonin plays a positive role in antioxidative stress injury by regulating the function of mitochondria in nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells.

The PI3K/Akt signaling pathway is the central pathway that regulates metabolism, angiogenesis, cell survival and proliferation in response to various extracellular signals.

Under physiological and pathological conditions, the activated PI3K/Akt pathway regulates the biological process of cells by interacting with downstream proteins. Recently, several studies have reported on the key regulatory functions of PI3K PI3K/Akt signaling in bone formation and remodeling. According to Tan et al. higher expression of PI3K and Akt in the Nb group at weeks 4 and 8 suggesting a role in endochondral ossification of the callus via histological staining [45]. Liao et al. [46] have reported that PI3K/AKT/ERK pathway plays a crucial role in the ECFC-Exos-mediated regulation of canine MDO angiogenesis and bone remodeling. In addition, PI3K-Akt signaling also works in vascular regeneration. It was reported by Li that Cerebrospinal fluid-derived extracellular vesicles could enhance vascular regeneration by activating the PI3K/AKT pathway, hence improving motor function recovery after spinal cord injury [47].More importantly, many studies have shown that PI3K/Akt is closely related to apoptosis or senescence changes in MSCs and NP cells [48,43]. For example, Cao et al. [49] found that Platelet-rich plasma pretreatment can protect anterior cruciate ligament fibroblasts by increasing cell viability, proliferation and migration and reducing apoptosis. It means that the activation of PI3K/Akt could maintain the balance between synthesis and metabolism of cells to avoid degeneration. This study found that melatonin can partially alleviate the inhibition of the PI3K/Akt pathway induced by hydrogen peroxide, thus improving the mitochondrial membrane and reducing the production of ROS. In addition, the PI3K-IN-1, as a PI3K inhibitor, has been shown to block PI3k/Akt signaling pathway at 25 μM. However, the positive effect of melatonin on NPMSCs was weakened when the PI3K/Akt pathway was inhibited by PI3K-IN-1. Therefore, as shown in Fig. 10, our results indicate that melatonin may reduce H2O2-induced apoptosis of NPMSCs by activating the PI3K/Akt pathway.

In vivo, to further evaluate the protective effect of melatonin on degenerative intervertebral discs, we applied melatonin to the IVDD model of SD rats. The results show that melatonin can partially alleviate the height loss of intervertebral discs and delay the process of intervertebral disc degeneration. The results of immunohistochemical staining (HE and S–O) confirmed that melatonin could decrease the loss of nucleus pulposus, which further confirms that an appropriate amount of melatonin can delay the process of intervertebral disc degeneration.

There are also some shortcomings in this study. First, melatonin can participate in the metabolism of nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells through a variety of signaling pathways, such as inhibiting the assembly of NLRP3 inflammatory bodies and nuclear factor kappa B. Therefore, other protective mechanisms of melatonin on nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells need to be further studied. Second, our isolated and cultured nucleus pulposus mesenchymal stem cells cannot be cultured in a hypoxic and hyperosmotic microenvironment simulating that of degenerative intervertebral discs, which needs to be improved in the future.

Conclusions

In summary, this is the first study concerning the protective effect of melatonin on NPMSCs against oxidative damage induced by hydrogen peroxide. Melatonin can not only activate PI3K/Akt pathway to reduce NPMSC apoptosis and protect mitochondrial function in vitro, but also retard the procession of IVDD in vivo (Fig. 11).

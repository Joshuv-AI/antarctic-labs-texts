Delay and Memory-Type Null Controllability for Heat Equations in Finite Dimensions
Dev Prakash Jha, Raju K. George

Abstract
We study null controllability for linear heat-type systems in finite dimensions that incorporate both memory and time-delay effects. A strengthened notion of controllability, referred to as delay and memory-type null controllability, is introduced, which requires the state, the memory functional, and the delayed history to vanish at the terminal time. Using a duality approach, we establish an augmented observability inequality for the adjoint system and show its equivalence to controllability. In the finite-dimensional setting, this leads to sharp necessary and sufficient algebraic rank conditions extending the classical Kalman criterion to systems with memory and delay.

Introduction

The study of controllability in evolution equations has a rich mathematical history, originating with finite-dimensional linear systems
[
12
]
and extending to infinite-dimensional spaces through the pioneering works of Lions
[
14
]
, Russell
[
18
]
, and Zuazua
[
24
]
, among others. In finite-dimensional settings, controllability is characterized by algebraic rank conditions on matrices defining system dynamics. However, the infinite-dimensional case requires more sophisticated analytical tools, particularly for systems incorporating memory and delay effects.

The study of systems with either memory or delay is well-established. However, many modern engineering and physical systems exhibit both phenomena simultaneously, where they are inextricably linked. For example, in the control of flexible aerospace structures, the material’s viscoelasticity introduces memory effects, while the digital feedback control loop has inherent processing and actuation delays. Similarly, in networked control systems and adaptive identification problems
[
7
]
, physical plants have their own internal dynamics with memory, while communication channels impose significant delays. Analyzing these effects in isolation fails to capture the complex interactive dynamics that can lead to instability or performance degradation. Therefore, developing a control theory for systems with coexisting memory and delay is not merely a theoretical extension but a crucial step towards designing robust controllers for a wide range of real-world applications.

Evolution equations with memory effects have gained considerable attention due to their physical relevance. The seminal work of Gurtin
[
9
]
introduced a modification of Fourier’s law to address the nonphysical instantaneous heat propagation predicted by the classical heat equation, leading to models with memory kernels. Similarly, the Cattaneo equation
[
4
]
incorporates finite propagation speed, resulting in systems that more accurately represent physical phenomena.

The primary focus of this study is the controllability of a general class of evolution equations that feature both memory and delay terms. We formulate the problem based on the following system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} y_{t}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\{\mathfrak{a}^{ij}(x)\left[\mathfrak{a}y_{x_{i}}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{b}(t-s,x)y_{x_{i}}(s,x)\,ds\right]\right\}_{x_{j}}&=u\chi_{\varpi}(x)\quad\text{in }Q,\\
y&=0\quad\text{on }\Xi,\\
y(0)&=y_{0}\quad\text{in }\Theta,\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

In this formulation,
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain with a smooth boundary
\partial\Theta
, while
Q=(0,T)\times\Theta
and
\Xi=(0,T)\times\partial\Theta
define the spatio-temporal domain and its lateral boundary, respectively. The function
\mathfrak{b}(\cdot,\cdot)
acts as a smooth memory kernel, the matrix
(\mathfrak{a}^{ij}(x))
is uniformly positive definite, and
\mathfrak{a}\in\{0,1\}
serves as a parameter. The control action
u\chi_{\varpi}
is confined to a subset
\varpi\subset\Theta
.

If the memory term is omitted by setting
\mathfrak{b}\equiv 0
and we choose
\mathfrak{a}=1
, then (
1
) reduces to the canonical heat equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} y_{t}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left[\mathfrak{a}^{ij}(x)y_{x_{i}}\right]_{x_{j}}&=u\chi_{\varpi}(x)\quad\text{in }Q,\\
y&=0\quad\text{on }\Xi,\\
y(0)&=y_{0}\quad\text{in }\Theta.\end{aligned}\right.

(2)

The null controllability for the classical heat equation (
2
) is a cornerstone result in control theory. It has been established that for any positive final time
T
and any nonempty open subset
\varpi\subset\Theta
, a control
u\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\varpi)
exists that steers any initial condition
y_{0}\in L^{2}(\Theta)
to the zero state at time
T
(
[
6
]
). The physical basis for this powerful result is the infinite speed of propagation inherent to parabolic equations.

However, the inclusion of memory effects fundamentally alters the controllability landscape. Even when
y(T)=0
, the system cannot be considered fully controlled unless the accumulated memory also vanishes:

\int_{0}^{T}\mathfrak{b}(T-s,x)y_{x_{i}}(s,x)\,ds\equiv 0.

(3)

Without this condition, the memory term continues to influence the system’s future evolution, preventing true null controllability.

Recent investigations have revealed that classical null controllability may fail for systems with nontrivial constant memory kernels when the control region remains fixed
[
8
,
10
,
17
]
. This foundational challenge has spurred a variety of recent investigations. For instance, Lü et al.
[
16
]
established null controllability for wave equations with memory, while Wang et al. focused on flow decomposition
[
19
]
and observability for heat equations with time-dependent analytic memory
[
20
]
. Research has also explored alternative control objectives like time-optimal control
[
23
]
, the weaker notion of approximate controllability
[
13
,
22
,
2
]
, and extension to degenerate coupled systems
[
1
]
. These studies underscore the richness of the problem, and the complete characterization of controllability for memory systems remains largely open.

The analysis becomes considerably more intricate when time-delay effects are incorporated alongside memory. Similar complexity arises in wave equations with time-varying delay
[
15
]
, semilinear parabolic equations with nonlocal time delays
[
3
]
, and stochastic semidiscrete parabolic equations
[
21
]
. We examine the system within the abstract structure given by:

\left\{\begin{aligned} y_{t}(t)&=\mathfrak{A}y(t)+\mathfrak{A_{1}}y(t-h)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{M}(t-s)y(s)\,ds+\mathfrak{B}(t)u(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
y(\theta)&=\phi(\theta),\quad\theta\in[-h,0].\end{aligned}\right.

(4)

Here, the state vector is
y(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The system dynamics are defined by the matrices
\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{A_{1}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
\phi(\theta)\in C([-h,0],R^{n})
and the memory kernel
\mathfrak{M}(\cdot)\in L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
. The control input
u(t)
has values in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and the control operator is
\mathfrak{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
. The transpose of a matrix
\mathfrak{N}
is denoted by
\mathfrak{N}^{T}
.

The necessity for a more stringent definition of controllability becomes evident when considering the interaction between the delay and the state’s history in the abstract system (
4
). Even if a control steers the state to zero at the terminal time,
y(T)=0
, and simultaneously nullifies the memory term, the system may not remain at rest for
t>T
. To see this, let us analyze the dynamics of (
4
) at a time
t=T+\varepsilon
for an infinitesimally small
\varepsilon>0
. Suppose that
y(T)=0
and the memory integral vanishes, but the state was not identically zero on the preceding delay interval, i.e.,
y(t)\neq 0
for some
t\in[T-h,T)
. The evolution of the system is governed by:

y_{t}(T+\varepsilon)=\mathfrak{A}y(T+\varepsilon)+\mathfrak{A_{1}}y(T+\varepsilon-h)+\int_{0}^{T+\varepsilon}\mathfrak{M}(T+\varepsilon-s)y(s)\,ds+\mathfrak{B}(T+\varepsilon)u(T+\varepsilon).

While the term
\mathfrak{A}y(T+\varepsilon)
,
u(T+\varepsilon)
and the memory integral are initially close to zero, the delay term
\mathfrak{A_{1}}y(T+\varepsilon-h)
depends on the history of the state over the interval
[T-h,T)
. Since
y
was non-zero in this interval, the term
\mathfrak{A_{1}}y(T+\varepsilon-h)
can act as an active source, reintroducing energy into the system and driving the state away from zero.

This reveals a critical insight: to guarantee that
y(t)=0
for all
t>T
, it is not sufficient to control the state and memory to zero only at the instant
T
. One must also enforce the condition that the state is nullified over the entire delay interval preceding the final time:

y(t)=0\quad\text{for all }t\in[T-h,T).

This additional requirement is essential to neutralize the "lingering effect" of the delay, thereby ensuring a true and stable state of repose. It is this crucial observation that motivates the stronger notion of controllability that we introduce.

We formalize our controllability concept as follows:

Definition 1.1

(Delay and Memory-Type Null Controllability)
.

[
11
]

Let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}(\cdot)\in L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
be a given memory kernel. The system (
4
) is defined as being
delay and memory-type null controllable
with respect to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}(\cdot)
if for every initial condition
\phi\in Y
, there exists a control function
u(\cdot)\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
such that the corresponding solution satisfies the following three conditions:

\begin{cases}(a)\quad y(T)=0,\\[4.0pt]
(b)\quad\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T-h}\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}(T-s)y(s)\,ds=0,\\[4.0pt]
(c)\quad y(t)=0\quad\text{for all }t\in[T-h,T).\end{cases}

(5)

This definition generalizes existing controllability notions: classical null controllability corresponds to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}\equiv 0
and
\mathfrak{A_{1}}=0
, while memory-type controllability
[
5
]
corresponds to
\mathfrak{A_{1}}=0(\text{that is, }h=0)
.

The controllability analysis is transformed via duality into an observability problem for the adjoint system. Although observability estimates are well-developed for classical parabolic systems, the introduction of memory and delay terms presents significant analytical challenges, forming the core technical contribution of this work.

Main contributions.
This paper makes three significant contributions to the theory of controllability for evolution equations with delay and memory:

First
, we introduce a comprehensive definition of controllability for systems with simultaneous delay and memory effects: delay and memory-type null controllability. While Chaves-Silva et al.
[
5
]
developed memory-type null controllability for systems without delay, our work represents the first systematic treatment of the combined delay-memory case. We establish that achieving null controllability requires not only
y(T)=0
and the vanishing of memory integrals, but also the additional condition
y(t)=0
for
t\in[T-h,T)
to prevent reactivation through the delay term.

Second
, we establish the unified abstract framework for this new problem. A key theoretical result is the proof of an exact duality, which states that delay and memory-type null controllability is equivalent to a specific observability inequality for the adjoint system. This inequality is stronger than classical ones as it requires the observation to control the adjoint state’s past, present, and "future" terminal data. For finite-dimensional systems, this framework is then used to derive sharp necessary and sufficient conditions for observability.

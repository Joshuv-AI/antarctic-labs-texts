A Hofer-like Metric on the Space of Anosov Flows
Stéphane Tchuiaga

Abstract
This paper develops a family of Hofer-like metrics ( d An , V d_{\mathrm{An},V} ) on the space of Anosov vector fields An ⁡ ( M ) \mathrm{An}(M) , providing dynamically relevant distances based on the cost of deformation paths using 𝒞 k \mathcal{C}^{k} or Sobolev H k H^{k} norms. We establish fundamental properties, including completeness for V = 𝒞 r ​ ( r ≥ 1 ) V=\mathcal{C}^{r}(r\geq 1) or H k ​ ( k > dim ( M ) / 2 + 1 ) H^{k}(k>\dim(M)/2+1) , and naturality under diffeomorphisms. We show the utility of these metrics by proving quantitative stability results: proximity in d An , V d_{\mathrm{An},V} implies controlled variation of essential dynamical invariants, including topological entropy, Lyapunov exponents, SRB measures, thermodynamic pressure, spectral gaps (mixing rates), and zeta functions. Sufficient regularity ensures local Lipschitz continuity and Fréchet differentiability, connecting the metric structure to linear response formulas, particularly for pressure, exponents, and the spectral gap. While Sobolev metrics yield locally flat geometry with straight-line geodesics, the framework is broadly applicable. We explore implications for the moduli space of Anosov flows, including stability of invariants and the framework for local slice theorems. Furthermore, we introduce Topological Anosov Flows , defined via simultaneous uniform flow convergence and the d An d_{\mathrm{An}} -Cauchy condition on generators, alongside the related class of Finite Energy Anosov Flows requiring only d An d_{\mathrm{An}} -bounded generators. These new classes aim to capture essential hyperbolic features in non-smooth settings. Overall, the proposed metrics offer several geometric perspectives for analyzing the stability, classification, and possible extensions of Anosov dynamics.

Anosov flows, Hofer metric, Finsler geometry, infinite-dimensional manifolds, dynamical systems, stability, topological entropy, Lyapunov exponents, SRB measures, topological pressure, thermodynamic formalism, spectral gap, Ruelle zeta function, metric geometry, moduli space, topological Anosov flows.

Introduction

Anosov flows stand as paradigms of robustly chaotic dynamical systems, characterized by the uniform splitting of the tangent bundle into contracting, expanding, and neutral directions along flow lines
[
2
]
. The set
\mathrm{An}(M)
of all
C^{r}
(
r\geq 1
) Anosov vector fields on a given compact manifold
M
forms an open subset within the Banach space
C^{r}(TM)
, inheriting the structure of an infinite-dimensional manifold
[
22
]
. While the standard
C^{r}
topology provides a notion of proximity, it primarily measures the difference between vector fields at the endpoints of a deformation, rather than the intrinsic "effort" required to continuously deform one Anosov system into another while maintaining the Anosov property throughout.

In the realm of symplectic geometry, the Hofer type metrics furnish powerful intrinsic distances on the identity component of the group of symplectomorphisms
[
6
,
13
,
24
]
. It measures the minimal total
L^{\infty}
-variation of the generators along paths, revealing connections to symplectic capacities and rigidity phenomena. Motivated by this successful framework, we propose and investigate an analogous family of metrics on the space
\mathrm{An}(M)
.

We consider smooth paths
X_{s}:[0,1]\to\mathrm{An}(M)
connecting two Anosov vector fields
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
. The instantaneous change along such a path is given by the velocity vector field
Y_{s}=dX_{s}/ds
, which resides in the tangent space
T_{X_{s}}\mathrm{An}(M)\approx C^{r}(TM)
(or a suitable subspace thereof). Choosing a norm
\|\cdot\|_{V}
on this tangent space (typically the
C^{k}
norm for
k\geq 0
or a Sobolev
H^{k}
norm), we define the length of the path as
L_{V}(X_{s})=\int_{0}^{1}\|Y_{s}\|_{V}ds
. The corresponding Hofer-like distance is then defined as the infimum of these lengths over all admissible paths:

d_{\mathrm{An},V}(X_{0},X_{1})=\inf\{L_{V}(X_{s})\mid X_{s}\in C^{1}([0,1],\mathrm{An}(M)),X(0)=X_{0},X(1)=X_{1}\}.

The simplest case uses the
C^{0}
norm, denoted
d_{\mathrm{An}}=d_{\mathrm{An},C^{0}}
. This construction yields a family of genuine metrics on the path components of
\mathrm{An}(M)
. A crucial observation, derived directly from the definition, is the inequality

\|X_{1}-X_{0}\|_{V}\leq d_{\mathrm{An},V}(X_{0},X_{1}).

(1)

This ensures that convergence in the Hofer-like metric
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
implies convergence in the standard
\|\cdot\|_{V}
norm topology, providing a bridge to influence the wealth of existing stability results in dynamical systems theory which rely on standard
C^{k}
or
H^{k}
convergence. Metrics based on stronger norms
V
naturally induce finer topologies on
\mathrm{An}(M)
.

The primary contributions of this paper are: (1) establishing fundamental metric properties like completeness (Proposition
5.5
) and naturality under diffeomorphisms (Proposition
5.2
); (2) demonstrating the dynamical significance of these metrics by establishing quantitative relationships between distance in
(\mathrm{An}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},V})
and the variation of key dynamical invariants; (3) exploring the implications for the moduli space of Anosov flows; and (4) introducing the concept of Topological Anosov Flows. We prove a series of stability results, summarized as follows:

•

A basic
C^{0}
-rigidity property holds (Proposition
5.7
).

•

Continuity results: Topological entropy and Lyapunov exponents are continuous with respect to
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}
(Theorem
7.1
, Proposition
7.6
). SRB measures (weak*), measure-theoretic entropy, topological pressure, and Ruelle zeta functions are continuous with respect to
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}
for
k\geq 2
(Theorems
7.8
,
7.10
).

•

Local Geodesics: For the Hilbert-Riemannian metric
d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}
(
k
large), minimizing geodesics exist locally and are straight lines in
H^{k}(TM)
(Theorem
7.3
).

•

Quantitative Stability (Lipschitz): Under sufficient regularity (
k\geq 2
), topological pressure, spectral gap (mixing rate), and SRB dimension exhibit local Lipschitz continuity with respect to
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}
(Theorems
7.8
,
7.12
). These Lipschitz bounds extend to the moduli space for conjugacy invariants (Theorem
7.25
). Remarks suggest similar bounds hold for entropy and exponents.

•

Differentiability: Pressure, Lyapunov exponents, and spectral gap are Fréchet differentiable functions on
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
(
k
sufficiently large) equipped with its natural Hilbert/Banach manifold structure (compatible with
d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}
/
d_{\mathrm{An},C^{k}}
), with derivatives given by linear response formulas (Theorems
7.14
,
7.16
,
7.18
).

These findings underscore that the
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
metrics provide a dynamically relevant way to measure distances within the space of Anosov flows, quantitatively controlling the variation of statistical, spectral, and topological invariants. They pave the way for applying geometric and variational techniques to study deformations of chaotic systems.

2
Basic notions

Let
M
be a compact, smooth manifold without boundary. Let
\mathcal{C}^{r}(TM)
denote the Banach space of
\mathcal{C}^{r}
vector fields on
M
, where
r\geq 1
. We are interested in the subset
\mathrm{An}(M)\subset\mathcal{C}^{r}(TM)
consisting of vector fields
X
whose generated flows
\varphi^{t}_{X}
are Anosov flows.

Definition 2.1

(Anosov Flow)
.

A flow
\varphi^{t}
on
M
, generated by a vector field
X\in\mathcal{C}^{r}(TM)
, is
Anosov
if there exists a continuous,
\mathrm{d}\varphi^{t}
-invariant splitting of the tangent bundle
TM=E^{s}\oplus E^{u}\oplus E^{c}
, where
E^{c}=\mathbb{R}X
is the 1-dimensional bundle tangent to the flow direction, and there exist constants
C>0
,
\lambda>0
, and an auxiliary Riemannian metric
g
on
M
such that for all
t>0
:

•

\left\|\mathrm{d}\varphi^{t}(v)\right\|_{g}\leq Ce^{-\lambda t}\left\|v\right\|_{g}
for all
v\in E^{s}
.

•

\left\|\mathrm{d}\varphi^{-t}(v)\right\|_{g}\leq Ce^{-\lambda t}\left\|v\right\|_{g}
for all
v\in E^{u}
.

Here
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g}
denotes the norm on tangent vectors induced by
g
.

A fundamental result (structural stability) states that
\mathrm{An}(M)
is an
open
subset of
\mathcal{C}^{r}(TM)
for
r\geq 1

[
2
,
22
]
. As an open set in a Banach space,
\mathrm{An}(M)
is an infinite-dimensional manifold.

3
Analogy with the Hofer Metric

The Hofer metric, introduced in
[
13
]
, is defined on the group
\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)
of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms of a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
. A path
\phi_{t}\in\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)
(
t\in[0,1]
) starting at the identity is generated by a time-dependent Hamiltonian function
H_{t}
. The length of the path is given by

\mathrm{Length}(\phi_{t})=\int_{0}^{1}\left\|H_{t}\right\|_{\text{Hofer}}\mathrm{d}t,

where
\left\|H_{t}\right\|_{\text{Hofer}}=\max H_{t}-\min H_{t}
(or sometimes
\left\|H_{t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
). The Hofer distance
d_{H}(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
is the infimum of path lengths over all Hamiltonian paths connecting
\phi_{0}
to
\phi_{1}

[
13
,
24
]
. Our construction aims to mimic this structure for Anosov vector fields.

4
Construction of the Metric on
\mathrm{An}(M)

We define a metric on
\mathrm{An}(M)
by defining the length of paths within this space. Fix an auxiliary Riemannian metric
g
on
M
.

Definition 4.1

(Paths in
\mathrm{An}(M)
)
.

An admissible path connecting
X_{0},X_{1}\in\mathrm{An}(M)
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
(or piecewise
\mathcal{C}^{1}
) map
X:[0,1]\to\mathrm{An}(M)
, denoted
X_{s}
, such that
X(0)=X_{0}
and
X(1)=X_{1}
.

Since
\mathrm{An}(M)
is open, such paths exist between any two points in the same path component.

Definition 4.2

(Velocity and
\mathcal{C}^{0}
Norm)
.

The velocity of the path
X_{s}
at parameter
s
is the vector field
Y_{s}=\frac{\mathrm{d}X_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}\in\mathcal{C}^{r}(TM)
(viewed as a tangent vector to the path in
\mathcal{C}^{r}(TM)
). We equip the space of velocity vectors with the
\mathcal{C}^{0}
norm (supremum norm) induced by the metric
g
:

\|Y_{s}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\coloneqq\sup_{x\in M}\left|Y_{s}(x)\right|_{g}=\sup_{x\in M}\sqrt{g_{x}(Y_{s}(x),Y_{s}(x))}.

Definition 4.3

(Length of a Path)
.

The length of a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
path
X_{s}
in
\mathrm{An}(M)
with respect to the
\mathcal{C}^{0}
norm is defined as the integral of the
\mathcal{C}^{0}
norm of its velocity:

\mathrm{Length}_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}(X_{s})\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\|\frac{\mathrm{d}X_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\mathrm{d}s.

Definition 4.4

(Hofer-like Metric
d_{\mathrm{An}}
)
.

Let
X_{0},X_{1}
be two Anosov vector fields in the same path component of
\mathrm{An}(M)
. The Hofer-like distance
d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1})
, based on the
\mathcal{C}^{0}
norm, is defined as the infimum of the lengths of all admissible (
\mathcal{C}^{1}
) paths connecting
X_{0}
to
X_{1}
:

d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1})\coloneqq\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\|\frac{\mathrm{d}X_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\mathrm{d}s\;\middle|\;X_{s}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,1],\mathrm{An}(M)),X(0)=X_{0},X(1)=X_{1}\right\}.

If
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are in different path components, we set
d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1})=\infty
.

5
Properties

•

Well-defined:
The infimum exists since lengths are non-negative.

•

Metric Axioms:

d_{\mathrm{An}}
defines a metric on each path component of
\mathrm{An}(M)
.

–

d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1})\geq 0
, and
d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{0})=0
(via the constant path).

–

d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1})=0\implies X_{0}=X_{1}
. This follows from the inequality

\|X_{1}-X_{0}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\leq d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1}),

(2)

which can be derived from
\|X_{1}-X_{0}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}=\|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}X_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathrm{d}s\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|\frac{\mathrm{d}X_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\mathrm{d}s
.

–

Symmetry:
d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1})=d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{1},X_{0})
by path reversal (
X^{\prime}_{s}=X_{1-s}
).

–

Triangle Inequality:
d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{2})\leq d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1})+d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{1},X_{2})
by path concatenation.

•

Finsler Structure:
The metric
d_{\mathrm{An}}
is the integrated distance associated with a Finsler structure on the infinite-dimensional manifold
\mathrm{An}(M)
. The Finsler norm at a point
X\in\mathrm{An}(M)
applied to a tangent vector
Y\in T_{X}\mathrm{An}(M)\approx\mathcal{C}^{r}(TM)
is given by
F_{X}(Y)=\|Y\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}
.

•

Dependence on
g
:
While the specific value of
d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1})
depends on the choice of the auxiliary Riemannian metric
g
, the induced topology is independent of this choice since
M
is compact (all Riemannian metrics on a compact manifold induce equivalent
\mathcal{C}^{0}
norms on vector fields).

Remark 5.1

(Non-Degeneracy in Specific Settings)
.

While the general non-degeneracy property
d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1})=0\implies X_{0}=X_{1},
follows readily from inequality (
2
), it is noteworthy that deeper results can sometimes be obtained in specific geometric contexts using specialized techniques. For instance, a recent preprint by Smith & Zhang
[
32
]
reportedly establishes the non-degeneracy of
d_{\mathrm{An}}
for contact Anosov flows on 3-manifolds, highlighting unique properties related to harmonic structures associated with contact forms. Such results provide valuable confirmation of the metric properties in important subclasses of Anosov flows and highlight thepossible for richer interactions between the metric structure and the underlying geometry.

5.1
Invariance Properties of the Hofer-like Metric

We investigate how the Hofer-like metrics
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
behave under coordinate changes on the underlying manifold
M
. Naturally, the value of the metric depends on the choice of auxiliary Riemannian metric
g
used to define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V,g}
on vector fields. The following proposition shows that diffeomorphisms of
M
act as isometries between the spaces
(\mathrm{An}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},V,g})
equipped with appropriately transformed metrics.

Proposition 5.2

(Diffeomorphism Pushforward Induces Isometry Between Metric Spaces)
.

Let
M
be a compact smooth manifold. For any auxiliary Riemannian metric
g
on
M
, let
f:M\to M
be a smooth (
C^{k}
,
k\geq r+1
) diffeomorphism, and let
f^{*}g
denote the pullback metric. Let
V
represent a type of norm on
C^{r}(TM)
(such as
C^{l}
for
0\leq l\leq r
, or Sobolev
H^{l}
for
l\leq r
) whose definition depends naturally on the Riemannian metric, denoted
\|\cdot\|_{V,g}
or
\|\cdot\|_{V,f^{*}g}
. Assume this dependence is such that the pushforward map
f_{*}:C^{r}(TM)\to C^{r}(TM)
, defined by

(f_{*}Y)(y)=Df_{f^{-1}(y)}\bigl(Y(f^{-1}(y))\bigr),

satisfies the isometry property relating the norms based on
g
and
f^{*}g
:

\|f_{*}Y\|_{V,f^{*}g}=\|Y\|_{V,g}\quad\text{for all }Y\in C^{r}(TM).

(3)

Let
d_{\mathrm{An},V,g}
and
d_{\mathrm{An},V,f^{*}g}
be the Hofer-like metrics on
\mathrm{An}(M)
defined using the norms
\|\cdot\|_{V,g}
and
\|\cdot\|_{V,f^{*}g}
, respectively. Then, for any
X,Z\in\mathrm{An}(M)
, the pushforward
f_{*}
induces an isometry:

d_{\mathrm{An},V,f^{*}g}(f_{*}X,f_{*}Z)=d_{\mathrm{An},V,g}(X,Z).

Proof.

The proof relies on demonstrating that the pushforward map
f_{*}
establishes a length-preserving bijection between admissible paths connecting
X
and
Z
(measured using
\|\cdot\|_{V,g}
) and admissible paths connecting
f_{*}X
and
f_{*}Z
(measured using
\|\cdot\|_{V,f^{*}g}
).

1. Pushforward of Paths:
Let
X_{s}:[0,1]\to\mathrm{An}(M)
be any admissible
C^{1}
path such that
X(0)=X
and
X(1)=Z
. Define the pushed-forward path
X^{\prime}_{s}:[0,1]\to C^{r}(TM)
by
X^{\prime}_{s}=f_{*}X_{s}
.
Since
f:M\to M
is a diffeomorphism and
X_{s}
takes values in
\mathrm{An}(M)
, the pushforward
X^{\prime}_{s}=f_{*}X_{s}
also takes values in
\mathrm{An}(M)
(as the Anosov property is invariant under smooth coordinate changes). Furthermore, since
f_{*}:C^{r}(TM)\to C^{r}(TM)
is a continuous linear map (for
f\in C^{r+1}
) and
X_{s}
is
C^{1}
in
s
, the path
X^{\prime}_{s}
is also
C^{1}
in
s
. The endpoints are
X^{\prime}_{0}=f_{*}X_{0}=f_{*}X
and
X^{\prime}_{1}=f_{*}X_{1}=f_{*}Z
. Thus,
X^{\prime}_{s}
is an admissible
C^{1}
path in
\mathrm{An}(M)
connecting
f_{*}X
to
f_{*}Z
.
Conversely, applying
(f^{-1})_{*}
shows that any path from
f_{*}X
to
f_{*}Z
is the pushforward of a path from
X
to
Z
. Hence,
f_{*}
establishes a bijection between these sets of paths.

2. Velocity Transformation:
Let
Y_{s}=\frac{\mathrm{d}X_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}
be the velocity vector field of the path
X_{s}
. The velocity of the pushed-forward path
X^{\prime}_{s}=f_{*}X_{s}
is given by
Y^{\prime}_{s}=\frac{\mathrm{d}X^{\prime}_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}
. Since
f_{*}
is a continuous linear map, it commutes with differentiation with respect to the parameter
s
:

Y^{\prime}_{s}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}(f_{*}X_{s})=f_{*}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}X_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right)=f_{*}Y_{s}.

3. Length Preservation:
We compute the length of the path
X^{\prime}_{s}
using the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V,f^{*}g}
associated with the target metric space:

L_{V,f^{*}g}(X^{\prime}_{s})=\int_{0}^{1}\|Y^{\prime}_{s}\|_{V,f^{*}g}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{1}\|f_{*}Y_{s}\|_{V,f^{*}g}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Using the assumed isometry property (
3
), we have
\|f_{*}Y_{s}\|_{V,f^{*}g}=\|Y_{s}\|_{V,g}
. Substituting this into the integral gives:

L_{V,f^{*}g}(X^{\prime}_{s})=\int_{0}^{1}\|Y_{s}\|_{V,g}\,\mathrm{d}s.

The right-hand side is precisely the length
L_{V,g}(X_{s})
of the original path
X_{s}
, measured using the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V,g}
. Thus, we have shown that
L_{V,f^{*}g}(f_{*}X_{s})=L_{V,g}(X_{s})
.

4. Infimum Preservation:
We have established a bijection
X_{s}\mapsto f_{*}X_{s}
between the set of admissible paths from
X
to
Z
and the set of admissible paths from
f_{*}X
to
f_{*}Z
. This bijection preserves the lengths when measured with respect to the corresponding norms:
L_{V,f^{*}g}(f_{*}X_{s})=L_{V,g}(X_{s})
. Therefore, the set of possible path lengths for paths
X\to Z
(using
\|\cdot\|_{V,g}
) is identical to the set of possible path lengths for paths
f_{*}X\to f_{*}Z
(using
\|\cdot\|_{V,f^{*}g}
). Taking the infimum over these identical sets of values yields:

\inf\{L_{V,f^{*}g}(X^{\prime}_{s})\mid X^{\prime}_{s}:f_{*}X\to f_{*}Z\}=\inf\{L_{V,g}(X_{s})\mid X_{s}:X\to Z\}.

By definition of the Hofer-like metrics, this is exactly:

d_{\mathrm{An},V,f^{*}g}(f_{*}X,f_{*}Z)=d_{\mathrm{An},V,g}(X,Z).

∎

Remark 5.3
.

This proposition highlights that while the specific value of
d_{\mathrm{An},V,g}(X,Z)
depends on the choice of
g
, the underlying geometric structure is natural with respect to diffeomorphisms. The map
f_{*}
provides an isometry relating the geometry defined by
g
to the geometry defined by
f^{*}g
. Standard norms like
C^{l}
and
H^{l}
(defined using the metric
g
) satisfy the crucial property (
3
).

Corollary 5.4

(Invariance under Isometries)
.

If the diffeomorphism
f:M\to M
is an isometry of the auxiliary metric
g
(i.e.,
f^{*}g=g
), then the pushforward
f_{*}
is an isometry of the metric space
(\mathrm{An}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},V,g})
:

d_{\mathrm{An},V,g}(f_{*}X,f_{*}Z)=d_{\mathrm{An},V,g}(X,Z).

Proof.

If
f^{*}g=g
, then the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V,f^{*}g}
is identical to
\|\cdot\|_{V,g}
. Proposition
5.2
then directly yields the result.
∎

5.2
Completeness

Proposition 5.5

(Completeness in the
C^{r}
and Sobolev
H^{k}
Settings)
.

Let
M
be a compact smooth manifold.

(1)

(
C^{r}
Setting):
Let
r\geq 1
. Define
\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)=\{X\in C^{r}(TM):X\text{ generates an Anosov flow}\}
, which is an open subset of the Banach space
C^{r}(TM)
. Equip
\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
with the Hofer-like metric

d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(X,Y)=\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{X}_{s}\|_{C^{r}}\,ds\,\middle|\,X_{s}\in C^{1}([0,1],\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)),\;X_{0}=X,\;X_{1}=Y\right\}.

Then, each path component of the metric space
(\mathrm{An}^{r}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}})
is complete.

(2)

(Sobolev
H^{k}
Setting):
Let
k>\dim(M)/2+1
. Consider the Hilbert space
H^{k}(TM)
and define
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)=\mathrm{An}(M)\cap H^{k}(TM)
. By Sobolev embedding
H^{k}(TM)\hookrightarrow C^{1}(TM)
and the openness of
\mathrm{An}(M)
in the
C^{1}
topology,
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
is an open subset of
H^{k}(TM)
. Equip
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
with the Hofer-like metric

d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}(X,Y)=\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{X}_{s}\|_{H^{k}}\,ds\,\middle|\,X_{s}\in C^{1}([0,1],\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)),\;X_{0}=X,\;X_{1}=Y\right\}.

Then, each path component of the metric space
(\mathrm{An}^{k}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}})
is complete.

Proof.

We prove both cases simultaneously, letting
V
denote either the
C^{r}
norm (
r\geq 1
) or the
H^{k}
norm (
k>\dim(M)/2+1
), and letting
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
denote the corresponding space (
\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
or
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
). The underlying space
B_{V}=C^{r}(TM)
or
H^{k}(TM)
is a complete Banach (or Hilbert) space.

1. Cauchy in
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
implies Cauchy in Norm
\|\cdot\|_{V}
:

We have the fundamental inequality,
\|X-Y\|_{V}\leq d_{\mathrm{An},V}(X,Y)
for any
X,Y\in\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
. If
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
(\mathrm{An}^{V}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},V})
, then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
N
such that for
n,m\geq N
,
d_{\mathrm{An},V}(X_{n},X_{m})<\epsilon
. This implies,
\|X_{n}-X_{m}\|_{V}<\epsilon
. Thus,
\{X_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in the complete space
B_{V}
.

2. Existence and Location of the Limit:

Since
B_{V}
is complete, the Cauchy sequence
\{X_{n}\}
converges in norm to a limit
X\in B_{V}
. That is,
\|X_{n}-X\|_{V}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
As established in the proposition statement (based on the openness of
\mathrm{An}(M)
in
C^{1}
and Sobolev embedding when applicable), the set
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
is open in
B_{V}
. Since
X_{n}\in\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
for all
n
and
X_{n}\to X
in the topology of
B_{V}
, the limit
X
must belong to
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
.

3. Convergence in the
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
Metric:

We need to show that
d_{\mathrm{An},V}(X_{n},X)\to 0
. Since
X_{n}\to X
in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V}
and
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
is open in
B_{V}
, for
n
sufficiently large, the norm distance
\|X_{n}-X\|_{V}
is small. For such
n
, consider the straight-line path
\gamma_{n}(s)=X+s(X_{n}-X)
for
s\in[0,1]
. Since
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
is an open subset of a vector space
B_{V}
, it contains line segments starting at
X
provided they are short enough. Thus, for
n
large enough,
\gamma_{n}(s)\in\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
for all
s\in[0,1]
, making
\gamma_{n}
an admissible path connecting
X
to
X_{n}
.
The length of this path is

L_{V}(\gamma_{n})=\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\frac{\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right\|_{V}\,ds=\int_{0}^{1}\|X_{n}-X\|_{V}\,ds=\|X_{n}-X\|_{V}.

By the definition of
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
as an infimum, we have
d_{\mathrm{An},V}(X,X_{n})\leq L_{V}(\gamma_{n})=\|X_{n}-X\|_{V}
.
Since
\|X_{n}-X\|_{V}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, we conclude that
d_{\mathrm{An},V}(X_{n},X)\to 0
.

Therefore, every
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
-Cauchy sequence
\{X_{n}\}
in
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
converges to a limit
X\in\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
with respect to the
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
metric. This proves that
(\mathrm{An}^{V}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},V})
is complete on each path component.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The completeness result highlights a crucial difference compared to the metric
d_{\mathrm{An}}=d_{\mathrm{An},C^{0}}
. The openness of the Anosov property in the
C^{r}
(
r\geq 1
) and relevant
H^{k}
topologies ensures that limits of Cauchy sequences remain within the space
\mathrm{An}(M)
, a property not guaranteed by
C^{0}
convergence alone. This completeness provides an analytic foundation for studying geodesic paths or performing variational analysis within these spaces using the Hofer-like metrics.

5.3
Anosov Flows Approximations

The
d_{\mathrm{An}}
metric convergence interacts with the standard convergence of flows.

Proposition 5.7

(Consistency of Limits under
d_{\mathrm{An}}
Convergence)
.

Let
\phi_{i}=(\varphi^{t}_{X_{i}})
be a sequence of flows generated by Anosov vector fields
X_{i}\in\mathrm{An}(M)
. Let
\psi=(\varphi^{t}_{Y})
be a flow generated by an Anosov vector field
Y\in\mathrm{An}(M)
. Let
\theta=(\theta_{t})
be a continuous flow generated by a vector field
X\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(TM)
. Assume that:

(i)

\phi_{i}\to\theta
uniformly on compact time intervals as maps. (Meaning:
\forall T>0,\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}d_{C^{0}}(\varphi^{t}_{X_{i}},\theta_{t})\to 0
as
i\to\infty
).

(ii)

d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{i},Y)\to 0
as
i\to\infty
(using the
d_{\mathrm{An}}=d_{\mathrm{An},C^{0}}
metric).

Then the flow
\theta
is precisely the Anosov flow
\psi
(i.e.,
X=Y
and
\theta=\psi
). In particular,
\theta
is an Anosov flow.

Proof.

1.
C^{0}
Convergence of Generators:
Assumption (b),
d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{i},Y)\to 0
, implies convergence of the generators in the
C^{0}
norm via inequality (
2
):
\|X_{i}-Y\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\leq d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{i},Y)
. Thus,
\lim_{i\to\infty}\|X_{i}-Y\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}=0
.

2.
C^{0}
Convergence of Flows from Generators:
By the standard theorem on continuous dependence of ODE solutions on parameters (specifically, the generating vector field in the
C^{0}
topology), the convergence
X_{i}\to Y
in
C^{0}(TM)
implies that the corresponding flows converge uniformly on compact time intervals in the
C^{0}
topology of maps on
M
. That is, for any
T>0
:

\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}d_{C^{0}}(\varphi^{t}_{X_{i}},\varphi^{t}_{Y})\right)=0.

Using the notation
\phi_{i}=(\varphi^{t}_{X_{i}})
and
\psi=(\varphi^{t}_{Y})
, this means
\phi_{i}\to\psi
uniformly on compact time intervals.

3. Identifying the Limit:
We have two limits for the sequence
\{\phi_{i}\}
in the space of continuous flows (equipped with the topology of uniform convergence on compact time intervals, which is Hausdorff):

•

\phi_{i}\to\theta
(from Assumption (a)).

•

\phi_{i}\to\psi
(from Step 2).

By the uniqueness of limits, we must have
\theta_{t}=\psi_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This implies that the flows
\theta
and
\psi
are identical.

4. Conclusion:
Since
\theta=\psi
, and
\psi
is given as an Anosov flow (generated by
Y\in\mathrm{An}(M)
), it follows that
\theta
is also an Anosov flow. Furthermore, their generators must be equal:
X=Y
.
∎

Remark 5.8

(Implications)
.

This proposition demonstrates a basic consistency property: if a sequence of Anosov flows converges uniformly to some continuous flow
\theta
, and simultaneously their generators converge in the
d_{\mathrm{An}}
metric to the generator of an Anosov flow
\psi
, then the limit flow
\theta
must be equal to
\psi
. This highlights consistency between metric convergence of generators (
d_{\mathrm{An}}
) and uniform convergence of the flows. Note this does not imply that
C^{0}
convergence of generators preserves the Anosov property; it relies on the target
Y
being Anosov.

Lemma 5.9

(Anosov Path Approximation by Boundary Flat Paths)
.

Let
\{X_{s}\}_{s\in[0,1]}
be a
C^{1}
path in
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
(where
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
is
\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
or
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
as in Prop.
5.5
) equipped with norm
\|\cdot\|_{V}
, connecting
X_{0}
to
X_{1}
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists another
C^{1}
path
\{Y_{s}\}_{s\in[0,1]}
in
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
satisfying:

(i)

Boundary Flat Velocity:
There exists
\delta>0
such that
\dot{Y}_{s}=0
for all
s\in[0,\delta]\cup[1-\delta,1]
.

(ii)

Endpoint Agreement:

Y_{0}=X_{0}
and
Y_{1}=X_{1}
.

(iii)

Controlled Length:

L_{V}(Y)<L_{V}(X)+\epsilon
.

(iv)

C^{0}
-Closeness:

\max_{s\in[0,1]}\|X_{s}-Y_{s}\|_{C^{0}}<\epsilon.

Proof.

The proof uses a standard reparameterization technique.

Step 1. Choice of the Reparameterization.

Let
\chi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be a smooth non-decreasing function satisfying:

•

\chi(0)=0
,
\chi(1)=1
.

•

\chi^{\prime}(s)=0
for
s\in[0,\delta]\cup[1-\delta,1]
for some
\delta>0
.

•

\chi
is arbitrarily
C^{1}
-close to the identity map
id(s)=s
. Specifically, for any
\eta>0
, we can choose
\chi
such that
\|\chi-id\|_{C^{0}}<\eta
and
\|\chi^{\prime}-1\|_{L^{\infty}}<\eta
. (This requires
\delta
to be small).

Such functions can be constructed using standard bump functions.

Define the new path
\{Y_{s}\}
by
Y_{s}=X_{\chi(s)}
for
s\in[0,1]
. Since
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
is open in the Banach space
B_{V}
, and
X_{s}
is continuous, if
\eta=\|\chi-id\|_{C^{0}}
is small enough, the path
Y_{s}
remains in
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
. As
X_{s}
and
\chi
are
C^{1}
,
Y_{s}
is
C^{1}
.

Step 2. Verification of Properties.

(i)

Boundary Flat Velocity:

\dot{Y}_{s}=\dot{X}_{\chi(s)}\chi^{\prime}(s)
. Since
\chi^{\prime}(s)=0
on
[0,\delta]\cup[1-\delta,1]
,
\dot{Y}_{s}=0
there.

(ii)

Endpoint Agreement:

Y_{0}=X_{\chi(0)}=X_{0}
and
Y_{1}=X_{\chi(1)}=X_{1}
.

(iii)

Controlled Length:
Let
f(s)=\|\dot{X}_{s}\|_{V}
.
f
is continuous on
[0,1]
, hence bounded (
M_{1}
) and uniformly continuous (
\omega_{f}
).

\displaystyle L_{V}(Y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{Y}_{s}\|_{V}ds=\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{X}_{\chi(s)}\chi^{\prime}(s)\|_{V}ds=\int_{0}^{1}\chi^{\prime}(s)\|\dot{X}_{\chi(s)}\|_{V}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\chi^{\prime}(s)(\|\dot{X}_{s}\|_{V}+\|\dot{X}_{\chi(s)}-\dot{X}_{s}\|_{V})ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\chi^{\prime}(s)(f(s)+\omega_{f}(|\chi(s)-s|))ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}(1+\eta)(f(s)+\omega_{f}(\eta))ds\quad(\text{since }|\chi^{\prime}(s)-1|<\eta,\chi^{\prime}(s)\leq 1+\eta)

\displaystyle=(1+\eta)\int_{0}^{1}f(s)ds+(1+\eta)\omega_{f}(\eta)

\displaystyle=(1+\eta)L_{V}(X)+(1+\eta)\omega_{f}(\eta).

Since
\eta
can be made arbitrarily small and
\omega_{f}(\eta)\to 0
as
\eta\to 0
, we can choose
\eta
small enough such that
(1+\eta)L_{V}(X)+(1+\eta)\omega_{f}(\eta)<L_{V}(X)+\epsilon
.

(iv)

C^{0}
-Closeness:
Since
s\mapsto X_{s}
is continuous from
[0,1]
to
C^{0}(TM)
, it is uniformly continuous. Let
\omega_{X,C^{0}}
be its modulus of continuity.

\|X_{s}-Y_{s}\|_{C^{0}}=\|X_{s}-X_{\chi(s)}\|_{C^{0}}\leq\omega_{X,C^{0}}(|s-\chi(s)|)\leq\omega_{X,C^{0}}(\eta).

Choosing
\eta
small enough ensures
\omega_{X,C^{0}}(\eta)<\epsilon
.

Thus, the reparameterized path
\{Y_{s}\}
satisfies all desired properties.
∎

Proposition 5.10

(Anosov
L^{1}/L^{\infty}
Path Approximation)
.

Let
X_{s}:[0,1]\to\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
be a
C^{1}
path with norm
\|\cdot\|_{V}
, connecting
X_{0}
to
X_{1}
. Assume
L_{V}(X)=\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{X}_{s}\|_{V}ds>0
.
Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists another
C^{1}
path
Y_{s}:[0,1]\to\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
satisfying:

(i)

Endpoint Agreement:

Y_{0}=X_{0}
and
Y_{1}=X_{1}
.

(ii)

Controlled
L^{\infty}
Velocity Norm:

L^{\infty}_{V}(Y)=\esssup_{s\in[0,1]}\|\dot{Y}_{s}\|_{V}<L_{V}(X)+\epsilon
.

(iii)

C^{0}
-Closeness of Path:

\max_{s\in[0,1]}\|X_{s}-Y_{s}\|_{V}<\epsilon
.

Proof.

Step 1: Arc-Length Reparameterization (Ideal Path).

Define the arc-length function
l(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\|\dot{X}_{u}\|_{V}du
. Since
L_{V}(X)>0
,
l(s)
is strictly increasing if
\|\dot{X}_{s}\|_{V}>0
a.e. If
\|\dot{X}_{s}\|_{V}=0
on a set of positive measure, we can reparameterize
X_{s}
to remove these constant segments without changing the endpoints or the length
L_{V}(X)
, so assume
\|\dot{X}_{s}\|_{V}>0
for almost all
s
. Let
L=L_{V}(X)
. The function
l:[0,1]\to[0,L]
is
C^{1}
and absolutely continuous. Let
\chi:[0,L]\to[0,1]
be its inverse (or a suitable selection if not strictly monotone, though we assume strict monotonicity after reparameterization).
\chi
is absolutely continuous. Define the ideal path
Z_{u}=X_{\chi(u)}
for
u\in[0,L]
. This path traverses the same geometric curve as
X_{s}
but parameterized by arc length. Its velocity is
\dot{Z}_{u}=\dot{X}_{\chi(u)}\chi^{\prime}(u)
. Since
\chi^{\prime}(u)=1/l^{\prime}(\chi(u))=1/\|\dot{X}_{\chi(u)}\|_{V}
, we have
\|\dot{Z}_{u}\|_{V}=\|\dot{X}_{\chi(u)}\|_{V}\cdot(1/\|\dot{X}_{\chi(u)}\|_{V})=1
for almost every
u
.

Step 2: Linear Rescaling.

Define
Y^{ideal}_{s}=Z_{sL}=X_{\chi(sL)}
for
s\in[0,1]
. This path connects
X_{0}
to
X_{1}
. Its velocity is
\dot{Y}^{ideal}_{s}=\dot{Z}_{sL}\cdot L
. Therefore,
\|\dot{Y}^{ideal}_{s}\|_{V}=\|\dot{Z}_{sL}\|_{V}\cdot L=1\cdot L=L_{V}(X)
. This path has constant speed
L_{V}(X)
.

Step 3: Smoothing (Approximation).

The inverse function
\chi
and thus
Y^{ideal}_{s}
might only be absolutely continuous or
C^{1}
if
s\mapsto\|\dot{X}_{s}\|_{V}
is bounded away from zero. To get a
C^{1}
path
Y_{s}
satisfying the conditions, we approximate
Y^{ideal}_{s}
.
Let
\phi_{\delta}
be a standard mollifier. Define the smoothed path
Y_{s}=(Y^{ideal}*\phi_{\delta})(s)
, extended appropriately near the boundaries (e.g., using reflection or constant extension). For
\delta
sufficiently small:

(i)

Endpoint Agreement:
By adjusting the smoothing near the boundary (e.g., using partitions of unity or modifying
\phi_{\delta}
), we can ensure
Y_{0}=X_{0}
and
Y_{1}=X_{1}
. The path
Y_{s}
will remain in
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
for small enough
\delta
since
\mathrm{An}^{V}(M)
is open and
Y^{ideal}
stays within it.

(ii)

Controlled
L^{\infty}
Velocity Norm:

\dot{Y}_{s}=(\dot{Y}^{ideal}*\phi_{\delta})(s)
. Since
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{V}
is a norm,

\|\dot{Y}_{s}\|_{V}=\left\|\int\dot{Y}^{ideal}_{s-u}\phi_{\delta}(u)du\right\|_{V}\leq\int\|\dot{Y}^{ideal}_{s-u}\|_{V}\phi_{\delta}(u)du=\int L_{V}(X)\phi_{\delta}(u)du=L_{V}(X).

This gives
L^{\infty}_{V}(Y)\leq L_{V}(X)
. We want
L^{\infty}_{V}(Y)<L_{V}(X)+\epsilon
. This is automatically satisfied if
\epsilon>0
. If we need a strict upper bound slightly perturbed from
L_{V}(X)
, we could start with a path slightly shorter than the original
X_{s}
(using Lemma
5.9
) and then apply this process. However,
L^{\infty}_{V}(Y)\leq L_{V}(X)
suffices for the statement.

(iii)

V
-Closeness of Path:
We need to show
\max_{s\in[0,1]}\|X_{s}-Y_{s}\|_{V}<\epsilon
. We know
Y_{s}\approx Y^{ideal}_{s}=X_{\chi(sL)}
. The map
s\mapsto X_{s}
is uniformly continuous from
[0,1]
to
(B_{V},\|\cdot\|_{V})
. Let its modulus be
\omega_{X,V}
. The reparameterization function
\psi(s)=\chi(sL)
maps
[0,1]
to
[0,1]
. If
s\mapsto\|\dot{X}_{s}\|_{V}
is roughly constant, then
l(s)\approx Ls
,
\chi(u)\approx u/L
, and
\psi(s)\approx s
. In general,
\psi(s)
is close to
s
.

\|X_{s}-Y^{ideal}_{s}\|_{V}=\|X_{s}-X_{\psi(s)}\|_{V}\leq\omega_{X,V}(|s-\psi(s)|).

Also, by standard properties of mollifiers,
\|Y-Y^{ideal}\|_{V}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
.
Thus,

\|X_{s}-Y_{s}\|_{V}\leq\|X_{s}-Y^{ideal}_{s}\|_{V}+\|Y^{ideal}_{s}-Y_{s}\|_{V}\leq\omega_{X,V}(\|id-\psi\|_{C^{0}})+\|Y^{ideal}-Y\|_{V}.

By choosing the mollification parameter
\delta
small enough, we can make
\|Y^{ideal}-Y\|_{V}<\epsilon/2
. Since
\psi
is continuous and maps
[0,1]
to
[0,1]
,
\|id-\psi\|_{C^{0}}
is controlled. And
\omega_{X,V}(\eta)\to 0
as
\eta\to 0
. Thus, we can ensure
\|X_{s}-Y_{s}\|_{V}<\epsilon
.

This completes the proof sketch.
∎

6
Discussion of Metric Variants

The metric
d_{\mathrm{An}}
provides a natural distance measure on the space of Anosov vector fields, capturing the minimal "total
\mathcal{C}^{0}
change" required to deform one Anosov system into another while staying within the class of Anosov systems.

Remark 6.1

(Alternative Norms and Stronger Topologies)
.

The choice of the
\mathcal{C}^{0}
norm on the velocity vector field
Y_{s}=\frac{\mathrm{d}X_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}
is just one possibility. One can define a family of Hofer-like metrics by using other norms. Let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{V}
be a norm on (a suitable subspace of)
\mathcal{C}^{r}(TM)
. Define the length
L_{V}(X_{s})=\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\frac{\mathrm{d}X_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right\|_{V}\mathrm{d}s
and the distance
d_{\mathrm{An},V}(X_{0},X_{1})=\inf L_{V}(X_{s})
over admissible paths. Examples include:

•

\mathcal{C}^{k}
Norms (
k\geq 1
):
Using a norm like
\left\|Y\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sup_{x\in M}\left|\grad^{j}Y(x)\right|_{g}
, where
\grad
is a covariant derivative associated with
g
. This leads to a distance
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}
. Since
\|Y\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\leq C_{k}\left\|Y\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}
for some constant
C_{k}
on the compact manifold
M
, we have
d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1})\leq C_{k}d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}(X_{0},X_{1})
. Thus, the metric
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}
induces a
stronger
(finer) topology than
d_{\mathrm{An}}=d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{0}}
. Convergence in this metric implies that the derivatives up to order
k
of the deforming vector fields remain close in the supremum norm along the path. The fundamental inequality relating norm difference to distance holds:

\left\|X_{1}-X_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}\leq d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}(X_{0},X_{1}).

(4)

•

Sobolev Norms (
H^{k}=W^{k,2}
):
Using norms like

\left\|Y\right\|_{H^{k}}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\int_{M}\left|\grad^{j}Y(x)\right|_{g}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{g}.

This leads to a distance
d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}
. By the Sobolev embedding theorem on compact manifolds, for
k>\dim(M)/2+l
,
H^{k}(TM)
embeds continuously into
\mathcal{C}^{l}(TM)

[
1
]
. In particular, for
k>\dim(M)/2
, we have
H^{k}(TM)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{0}(TM)
, implying
\|Y\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\leq C\left\|Y\right\|_{H^{k}}
for some constant
C
. This gives

d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{0},X_{1})\leq C\,d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}(X_{0},X_{1}).

Hence, for sufficiently large
k
, the metric
d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}
also induces a
stronger
topology than
d_{\mathrm{An}}
. This metric measures closeness in an
L^{2}
-average sense for derivatives up to order
k
. The norm vs distance inequality holds:

\left\|X_{1}-X_{0}\right\|_{H^{k}}\leq d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}(X_{0},X_{1}).

(5)

Metrics based on these stronger norms make finer distinctions between Anosov vector fields. The choice of norm may affect properties like completeness (addressed in Proposition
5.5
) or the existence and regularity of minimizing geodesics (addressed in Theorem
7.3
). The
L^{2}
-based Sobolev norms are often advantageous for variational methods due to the Hilbert space structure they induce
[
12
,
14
]
.

Remark 6.2

(Relation to Dynamics - Preliminary)
.

A primary motivation is to understand how proximity in these metrics relates to similarity in dynamical behavior (entropy, exponents, mixing, etc.). This is explored further in the next section.

Remark 6.3

(Volume-Preserving)
.

If the manifold
M
admits volume-preserving Anosov flows, one could restrict the construction to the space
\mathrm{An}_{\mu}(M)
of Anosov vector fields preserving a given volume form
\mu
. Admissible paths
X_{s}
must then lie entirely within
\mathrm{An}_{\mu}(M)
. The velocity vectors
Y_{s}=\frac{\mathrm{d}X_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}
would need to satisfy the condition ensuring that
X_{s}+\epsilon Y_{s}
remains (infinitesimally) volume-preserving, which typically means
Y_{s}
must be divergence-free, i.e.,
\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(Y_{s})=0
. The distance would be the infimum over such divergence-free velocity paths.

7
Dynamical Significance and Stability Results

A central motivation for introducing metrics like
d_{\mathrm{An}}
and its variants (
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}},d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}
) on the space
\mathrm{An}(M)
of Anosov vector fields is to understand the interplay between the geometry of this space and the dynamical properties of the flows it parameterizes. Establishing continuity or stability results for key dynamical invariants with respect to these metrics would underscore their relevance. It is important to note that many desirable continuity properties require convergence in a metric stronger than the basic
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-based
d_{\mathrm{An}}
. The following subsections establish several such results.

7.1
Continuity of Topological Entropy

Theorem 7.1

(Entropy Continuity under
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}
Convergence)
.

Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{An}(M)
be a sequence of Anosov vector fields converging to
X\in\mathrm{An}(M)
in the
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}
metric. That is,
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}(X_{n},X)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Then, the topological entropies satisfy:

\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{\mathrm{top}}(\varphi_{X_{n}})=h_{\mathrm{top}}(\varphi_{X}),

where
\varphi_{Y}
denotes the flow generated by vector field
Y
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in two main steps: relating
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}
convergence to standard
\mathcal{C}^{1}
norm convergence, and then invoking the known continuity of topological entropy in the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
topology for Anosov flows.

Step 1:
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}
convergence implies
\mathcal{C}^{1}
norm convergence.

From inequality (
4
) (with
k=1
):
\left\|X_{n}-X\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}\leq d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}(X_{n},X)
. The assumption
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}(X_{n},X)\to 0
, therefore directly implies
\left\|X_{n}-X\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}\to 0
. This establishes
X_{n}\to X
in the standard
\mathcal{C}^{1}
topology on
\mathcal{C}^{1}(TM)
.

Step 2: Continuity of
h_{\mathrm{top}}
in the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
topology.

It is a standard result that the topological entropy map
h_{\mathrm{top}}:\mathrm{An}(M)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(TM)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is continuous when the domain is equipped with the subspace topology inherited from
\mathcal{C}^{1}(TM)

[
16
, Chapter 20]
. This follows from structural stability and the continuous dependence of periodic orbit data or properties of equilibrium states in the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
topology
[
8
]
.

Conclusion:

From Step 1,
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}(X_{n},X)\to 0
implies
X_{n}\to X
in
\mathcal{C}^{1}
. Since
X\in\mathrm{An}(M)
(which is open in
\mathcal{C}^{1}(TM)
),
X_{n}\in\mathrm{An}(M)
for large
n
. By the continuity result (Step 2),
h_{\mathrm{top}}(\varphi_{X_{n}})\to h_{\mathrm{top}}(\varphi_{X})
.
∎

Remark 7.2

(Implications)
.

This theorem provides a direct link between the metric geometry of
\mathrm{An}(M)
(when measured by
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}
) and a key measure of dynamical complexity. It confirms that small deformations in this space correspond to small changes in topological entropy, reinforcing the dynamical relevance of the metric.

7.2
Existence and Properties of Minimizing Geodesics

Theorem 7.3

(Local Geodesic Existence for
d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}
)
.

Assume that for
k>\dim(M)/2+1
, the space of Anosov vector fields
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)=\mathrm{An}(M)\cap H^{k}(TM)
is an open subset of the Hilbert space
H=H^{k}(TM)
. Equip
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
with the Riemannian metric
g_{X}(\cdot,\cdot)=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{k}}
induced by the standard
H^{k}
inner product on each tangent space
T_{X}\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)\approx H
.
For any
X\in\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
X
(in the
H^{k}
topology) such that for any
Y\in U
:

(i)

There exists a unique path
\gamma_{XY}:[0,1]\to\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
of minimal
H^{k}
-length connecting
X
to
Y
within
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
.

(ii)

This path is the straight line segment in the ambient Hilbert space
H
, given by
\gamma_{XY}(s)=X+s(Y-X)
.

(iii)

The path
\gamma_{XY}
depends smoothly on
Y\in U
.

The length of this minimizing geodesic is
d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}(X,Y)=\left\|Y-X\right\|_{H^{k}}
.

Proof.

1. Manifold Structure:
By assumption,
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
is an open subset of the Hilbert space
H=H^{k}(TM)
. Thus,
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
is a Hilbert manifold with tangent spaces
T_{X}\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)\cong H
.

2. Riemannian Metric:
The metric
g_{X}(V,W)=\langle V,W\rangle_{H^{k}}
defines a flat Riemannian metric on
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
because it is induced directly from the constant inner product on the ambient Hilbert space
H
.

3. Geodesics in Flat Metric:
In a flat Riemannian manifold (locally isometric to the model Hilbert space), geodesics are straight lines in the ambient space. That is, paths of the form
\gamma(s)=X+sV
for a tangent vector
V
.

4. Local Existence within
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
:
Since
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
is open in
H
, for any
X\in\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
, there exists
\delta>0
such that the open ball
B_{H}(X,\delta)\subset\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
. Let
U=B_{H}(X,\delta/2)
. For any
Y\in U
, the straight line path
\gamma_{XY}(s)=X+s(Y-X)
connects
X
to
Y
. For any
s\in[0,1]
, the distance from a point on the path to
X
is

\left\|\gamma_{XY}(s)-X\right\|_{H^{k}}=\left\|s(Y-X)\right\|_{H^{k}}=s\left\|Y-X\right\|_{H^{k}}\leq\left\|Y-X\right\|_{H^{k}}<\delta/2.

Thus, the entire path
\gamma_{XY}([0,1])
lies within
B_{H}(X,\delta/2)\subset B_{H}(X,\delta)\subset\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
.

5. Minimizing Property and Uniqueness:
The length of the straight line path
\gamma_{XY}
is

L_{H^{k}}(\gamma_{XY})=\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\gamma^{\prime}_{XY}(s)\right\|_{H^{k}}ds=\int_{0}^{1}\left\|Y-X\right\|_{H^{k}}ds=\left\|Y-X\right\|_{H^{k}}.

For any other
\mathcal{C}^{1}
path
\tilde{\gamma}:[0,1]\to\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
connecting
X
to
Y
, its length is

L_{H^{k}}(\tilde{\gamma})=\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)\right\|_{H^{k}}ds\geq\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)ds\right\|_{H^{k}}=\left\|\tilde{\gamma}(1)-\tilde{\gamma}(0)\right\|_{H^{k}}=\left\|Y-X\right\|_{H^{k}}.

Equality holds if and only if the velocity
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)
is parallel to
Y-X
and points in the same direction for all
s
, and its norm is constant, which implies
\tilde{\gamma}
is a monotonic reparameterization of the straight line path
\gamma_{XY}
. Thus,
\gamma_{XY}
is the unique minimizing path (up to reparameterization) in
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
for
Y\in U
, and
d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}(X,Y)=\left\|Y-X\right\|_{H^{k}}
.

6. Smooth Dependence:
The formula
\gamma_{XY}(s)=X+s(Y-X)
shows that the path depends affinely (hence smoothly) on
Y\in U
.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

This theorem shows that when using the
H^{k}
norm (for
k
large enough), the space
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
locally inherits the flat geometry of the ambient Hilbert space
H^{k}(TM)
. Geodesics are simply straight lines, and the Hofer-like distance
d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}
locally coincides with the standard
H^{k}
norm distance. This simplifies the local geometry significantly compared to the possibly curved Finsler geometry induced by other norms like
C^{0}
.

Remark 7.5

(Implications)
.

This result provides a concrete local geometric structure on
(\mathrm{An}^{k}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}})
. The most efficient path (locally) between two nearby Anosov vector fields in
H^{k}
is simply the linear interpolation between them. This simplifies local analysis and possibly allows for easier application of variational methods in this Hilbert setting.

7.3
Stability of Lyapunov Exponents

Proposition 7.6

(Lyapunov Exponent Stability under
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}
Convergence)
.

Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{An}(M)

in the

\varphi_{X}

-invariant measures

, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{i}(\mu_{n})=\lambda_{i}(\mu)
for all

.

Proof.

C^{1}
Convergence:

implies
X_{n}\to X
in
\mathcal{C}^{1}
by inequality (
4
) (with
k=1
).

2. Continuity of Anosov Splitting:
The stable/unstable bundles
E^{s/u/c}_{X_{n}}
depend continuously on
X_{n}
in the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
topology
[
16
, Thm. 18.2.3]
.

3. Convergence of Derivative Cocycles:
The derivative maps
\mathrm{d}\varphi_{X_{n}}^{t}
converge to
\mathrm{d}\varphi_{X}^{t}
uniformly on compact time intervals in
t
and uniformly over
M
because
X_{n}\to X
in
\mathcal{C}^{1}
.

4. Existence and Convergence of Measures:
Given an ergodic
\varphi_{X}
-invariant measure
\mu
, Sigmund’s specification theorem results imply the existence of a sequence of ergodic
\varphi_{X_{n}}
-invariant measures
\mu_{n}
such that
\mu_{n}\to\mu
in the weak* topology as
X_{n}\to X
in
\mathcal{C}^{1}

[
29
]
.

5. Continuity of Lyapunov Exponents:
By Oseledets’ Multiplicative Ergodic Theorem
[
21
]
, Lyapunov exponents exist
\mu
-a.e. Mañé proved that the Lyapunov exponents
\lambda_{i}(Y,\nu)
are continuous functions of
(Y,\nu)
on the space
(\mathrm{An}(M)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(TM))\times\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{erg}}(M)

,
[
20
]
.

6. Conclusion:
Since
X_{n}\to X
in
\mathcal{C}^{1}
and we found

weak*, the continuity result from Step 5 implies

.
∎

(Implications)
.

The
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{1}}

7.4
Continuity of SRB Measures and Thermodynamic Quantities

Theorem 7.8

(SRB Measure and Pressure Continuity)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Assume
\{X_{n}\}\subset\mathrm{An}(M)\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(TM)
converges to
X\in\mathrm{An}(M)\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(TM)
in the
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{2}}
metric (or
d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}
for

). Assume
X
admits a unique SRB measure
\mu
. Then for
n
sufficiently large,
X_{n}
admits a unique SRB measure
\mu_{n}
. Furthermore:

(i)

\mu_{n}\to\mu
in the weak* topology as
n\to\infty
.

(ii)

The measure-theoretic entropies converge:
h_{\mu_{n}}(\varphi_{X_{n}})\to h_{\mu}(\varphi_{X})
.

(iii)

Let
\{f_{n}\}\subset C^{\alpha}(M)
be a sequence of Hölderpossibles converging to
f\in C^{\alpha}(M)
in the
C^{\alpha}
norm. Then the topological pressure
P(X_{n},f_{n})
converges to
P(X,f)
.

(iv)

The map
Y\mapsto P(Y,f)
is locally Lipschitz on
\mathrm{An}(M)\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(TM)
with respect to the
\mathcal{C}^{2}
norm (and thus w.r.t.

).

C^{1+\alpha}
Convergence (Implied):

d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{2}}(X_{n},X)\to 0\implies X_{n}\to X
in
\mathcal{C}^{2}
by (
4
). Since
C^{2}(TM)\hookrightarrow C^{1,\alpha}(TM)
for any
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we have
X_{n}\to X
in
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
. (The Sobolev case
k>\dim(M)/2+1+\alpha
implies
H^{k}\hookrightarrow C^{1,\alpha}
by Sobolev embedding
[
1
]
). This regularity is sufficient for the transfer operator theory used below.

2. SRB Existence/Uniqueness/Stability:
For
C^{2}
(or
C^{1+\alpha}
) Anosov flows, the existence and uniqueness of the SRB measure
\mu
is a classical result
[
31
,
25
,
9
]
. The stability of the SRB measure under
C^{1}
(or slightly stronger) perturbations ensures that
\mu_{n}
exists and is unique for
X_{n}
sufficiently close to
X

[
34
]
.

3. Part (i) Weak* Convergence:
The map
Y\mapsto\mu_{Y}
assigning the SRB measure to a
C^{2}
Anosov flow

is known to be continuous with respect to the
C^{2}
topology on vector fields and the weak* topology on measures
[
11
,
10
]
. Since
X_{n}\to X
in
C^{2}
, it follows that
\mu_{n}\to\mu
weak*.

4. Part (ii) Entropy Convergence:
By Pesin’s entropy formula,
h_{\nu}(\varphi_{Y})=\int\sum_{\lambda_{i}(x,Y)>0}\lambda_{i}(x,Y)d\nu(x)
for the SRB measure
\nu
. From Proposition
7.6
, the Lyapunov exponents
\lambda_{i}(x,Y)
depend continuously on
(Y,\nu)
. Combined with the weak* convergence

, this yields
h_{\mu_{n}}(\varphi_{X_{n}})\to h_{\mu}(\varphi_{X})
.

5. Part (iii) Pressure Convergence:
Topological pressure
P(Y,g)
can be defined via the spectral radius of a transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{Y,g}
acting on a suitable Banach space (e.g.,
C^{\alpha}(M)
)
[
3
]
. The map

\mathcal{B}(C^{\alpha}(M))

[

(Implications)
.

This theorem connects the metric geometry (using

7.5
Convergence of Periodic Orbit Data and Zeta Functions

Theorem 7.10

(Periodic Orbits and Zeta Functions)
.

Let
r\geq 1
. Let
\{X_{n}\}\subset\mathrm{An}(M)\cap\mathcal{C}^{r}(TM)
converge to
X\in\mathrm{An}(M)\cap\mathcal{C}^{r}(TM)
in the
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{r}}
metric. Then:

(i)

For any periodic orbit
\gamma
of
\varphi_{X}
with period
T
, there exists
N
such that for all
n\geq N
,
\varphi_{X_{n}}
has a unique periodic orbit
\gamma_{n}
near
\gamma
. The period
T_{n}
converges to
T
.

(ii)

Let
P_{\gamma}=\mathrm{d}\varphi_{X}^{T}|_{\gamma(0)}
and
P_{\gamma_{n}}=\mathrm{d}\varphi_{X_{n}}^{T_{n}}|_{\gamma_{n}(0)}
be the linearized Poincaré return maps (restricted to directions transversal to the flow). Then the eigenvalues of
P_{\gamma_{n}}
converge to the eigenvalues of
P_{\gamma}
.

(iii)

If
X_{n}\to X
in
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(TM)
for some
\alpha>0
(which is implied if
r\geq 2
and convergence is in
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{r}}
), then the Ruelle zeta function
\zeta_{X_{n}}(s)
converges to
\zeta_{X}(s)
uniformly on compact subsets of some right half-plane
\{\Realpart(s)>c\}
where
\zeta_{X}(s)
is analytic and non-zero.

Proof.

1.
C^{r}
Convergence:

d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{r}}(X_{n},X)\to 0\implies X_{n}\to X
in
\mathcal{C}^{r}
by inequality (
4
).

2. Parts (i) and (ii): Orbit Persistence/Convergence:
A periodic orbit
\gamma
for
X
at
x_{0}=\gamma(0)
with period
T
satisfies
\varphi_{X}^{T}(x_{0})=x_{0}
. The existence and uniqueness of a nearby orbit
\gamma_{n}
for
X_{n}
follows from applying the Implicit Function Theorem to the map
(Y,y,\tau)\mapsto\varphi_{Y}^{\tau}(y)-y
near
(X,x_{0},T)
, using the hyperbolicity of
\gamma
(which ensures the derivative w.r.t.
y
transversal to the flow is invertible)
[
22
]
. The theorem guarantees that the solution
(y_{n},T_{n})
exists, is unique locally, and depends continuously (in fact,
C^{r-1}
) on
X_{n}
(in the
C^{r}
topology). Thus
T_{n}\to T
, and
\gamma_{n}\to\gamma
. The linearized Poincaré map
P_{\gamma_{n}}
depends continuously on
(X_{n},T_{n},y_{n})
, so its eigenvalues converge to those of
P_{\gamma}
.

3. Part (iii): Zeta Function Convergence (requires
C^{1+\alpha}
):
Ruelle zeta functions for
C^{1+\alpha}
Anosov flows can be expressed via Fredholm determinants of transfer operators, e.g.,
\zeta_{Y}(s)^{-1}=\det(\Id-\mathcal{L}_{Y}(s))
for a suitable operator
\mathcal{L}_{Y}(s)
acting on an appropriate Banach space
[
23
,
3
]
. The map
Y\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{Y}(s)
is continuous (often analytic) from
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}(TM)
to the space of trace class operators (or operators with suitable decay properties), uniformly on compact sets of
s
in a half-plane
[
3
,
19
]
. The convergence
X_{n}\to X
in
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
(implied by
\mathcal{C}^{r}
convergence for
r\geq 2
) implies
\mathcal{L}_{X_{n}}(s)\to\mathcal{L}_{X}(s)
in trace norm (or relevant operator norm), uniformly on compacts in
s
. The Fredholm determinant is continuous with respect to the trace norm
[
30
]
. Hence
\det(\Id-\mathcal{L}_{X_{n}}(s))\to\det(\Id-\mathcal{L}_{X}(s))
uniformly on compacts where the operators are defined and the determinant is non-zero. This implies
\zeta_{X_{n}}(s)\to\zeta_{X}(s)
uniformly on compact subsets away from the poles/zeros.
∎

Remark 7.11

(Implications)
.

This theorem shows that metrics based on sufficiently strong norms (like
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{r}}
,
r\geq 1
) respect the underlying periodic orbit structure of Anosov flows. Furthermore, convergence in metrics implying
C^{1+\alpha}
regularity (like
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{2}}
) ensures the convergence of the Ruelle zeta function, linking the metric geometry to important analytic objects encoding spectral and periodic orbit information.

7.6
Quantitative Stability of Mixing Rates and SRB Dimension

We strengthen the continuity results to Lipschitz stability by combining known regularity properties of spectral data and dimension formulas with respect to stronger Banach norms, with the Hofer-like metric definition.

Theorem 7.12

(Lipschitz Stability of Mixing Rate and Dimension)
.

Let
k\geq 2
. Let
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)=\mathrm{An}(M)\cap C^{k}(TM)
be equipped with the metric
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}
. Assume for
X\in\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
,
\varphi_{X}^{t}
admits a unique SRB measure
\mu_{X}
, a transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{X}
(on a suitable space like
C^{\alpha}(M)
) with spectral gap
\delta_{X}>0
below the leading eigenvalue
\lambda_{X}=1
(for normalized operator), and an SRB dimension
D_{X}=\dim_{\mathrm{SRB}}(\mu_{X})
characterized by
P_{X}(-D_{X}\psi_{X})=0
where
\psi_{X}
relates to the unstable Jacobian.
Then, for any
X_{0}\in\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
, there exist a neighborhood
U
of
X_{0}
in
(\mathrm{An}^{k}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}})
and constants
L_{1},L_{2}>0
such that for all
X,Y\in U
:

(i)

|\delta_{X}-\delta_{Y}|\leq L_{1}\cdot d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}(X,Y)
.

(ii)

|D_{X}-D_{Y}|\leq L_{2}\cdot d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}(X,Y)
.

Proof.

The proof demonstrates local Lipschitz continuity of
\delta_{X}
and
D_{X}
with respect to the
\mathcal{C}^{k}
norm, which implies the result via
\left\|X-Y\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}\leq d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}(X,Y)
(inequality (
4
)).

1. Setup and Preliminaries:
We work on
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
with the metric
d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}
(
k\geq 2
). Convergence in this metric implies
\mathcal{C}^{k}
norm convergence. Assume existence of
\mu_{X}
,
\delta_{X}
, and
D_{X}
characterized as stated. Regularity
k\geq 2
ensures sufficient smoothness for transfer operators and pressure functions
[
3
,
16
]
.

2. Mixing Rate Stability (Spectral Gap
\delta_{X}
):

The transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{X}
depends continuously (often smoothly or Lipschitzly) on
X\in C^{k}(TM)
(
k\geq 2
) when viewed as an operator on a suitable Banach space
\mathcal{B}
(e.g., anisotropic Sobolev/Hölder spaces)
[
5
,
7
]
. Spectral perturbation theory
[
15
,
18
]
for hyperbolic systems implies that the spectral gap
\delta_{X}
(distance from the eigenvalue 1 to the rest of the spectrum, assuming normalization) also depends locally Lipschitz continuously on
X
in the
\mathcal{C}^{k}
norm (possibly requiring
k
large enough for the specific operator construction): there exists
L_{1}^{\prime}>0
such that
|\delta_{X}-\delta_{Y}|\leq L_{1}^{\prime}\left\|X-Y\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}
for
X,Y
in a
C^{k}
-neighborhood. Combining with inequality (
4
):

|\delta_{X}-\delta_{Y}|\leq L_{1}^{\prime}\left\|X-Y\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}\leq L_{1}^{\prime}d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}(X,Y).

Setting
L_{1}=L_{1}^{\prime}
proves part (i).

3. Fractal Dimension Stability (
D_{X}
):

D_{X}
is defined implicitly by
F(Y,D)=P_{Y}(-D\psi_{Y})=0
. Thepossible
\psi_{Y}\approx-\log|\det(d\varphi_{Y}|E^{u})|
depends smoothly (
C^{k-1}
) on
Y\in C^{k}
. The pressure function
(Y,f)\mapsto P_{Y}(f)
is known to be at least
C^{1}
w.r.t.
Y\in C^{k}
(
k\geq 2
) and
f\in C^{\alpha}

[
17
,
27
]
. Thus

is
C^{1}
in

(in
C^{k}
norm) and
D
. The partial derivative
\frac{\partial F}{\partial D}|_{(X,D_{X})}
is related to the variance of
\psi_{X}
and is typically non-zero for hyperbolic systems (it is negative). By the Implicit Function Theorem, the solution
D_{Y}
is a
C^{1}
function of
Y
in the
C^{k}
topology locally.
C^{1}
dependence implies local Lipschitz continuity: there exists

such that
|D_{X}-D_{Y}|\leq L_{2}^{\prime}\left\|X-Y\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}
. Combining with inequality (
4
):

|D_{X}-D_{Y}|\leq L_{2}^{\prime}\left\|X-Y\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}\leq L_{2}^{\prime}d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{k}}(X,Y).

Setting

proves part (ii).

4. Conclusion:
Both estimates hold simultaneously in a neighborhood

by taking the intersection of the neighborhoods required for each part.
∎

Remark 7.13

(Implications)

Theorem 7.14

(Differentiability of Topological Pressure)
.

Let

. Let

(X,\varphi)

\dot{X}_{0}

Proof.

The proof utilizes the transfer operator framework and analytic perturbation theory.

\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
for

andpossible

[
26

23
]

The map

C^{k}(TM)
(
k\geq 2
) to the space of bounded operators
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{B})
(or suitable subspace like trace class)
[
3
,
27
]

\{X_{t}\}

\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)

3. Differentiability of the Leading Eigenvalue:

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{B})
(the space of bounded operators on a suitable Banach space
\mathcal{B}
) is
C^{2}
(twice Fréchet differentiable with continuous second derivative). This often holds if
k
is sufficiently large, e.g.,
k>\dim(M)/2+2
and
f
is smooth enough.

Then:

(1)

The topological pressure function along the geodesic,

p(t)\coloneqq P(\gamma(t),f),
is
C^{2}
as a function of
t\in[0,1]
.

(2)

The second derivative of the pressure along the geodesic is given by the Hessian of the pressure functional
P(\cdot,f)
evaluated on the direction vector
Y-X
:

\frac{d^{2}p}{dt^{2}}(t)=D^{2}P(\gamma(t))[Y-X,Y-X].

(3)

The sign of this second derivative
\frac{d^{2}p}{dt^{2}}(t)
determines the local convexity or concavity of the pressure function
p(t)
at parameter
t
. Due to the non-linear dependence of the dynamics (and thus the transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma(t),f}
) on the interpolation parameter
t
, the sign of
D^{2}P(\gamma(t))[Y-X,Y-X]
is generally not constant along the geodesic. Therefore, the pressure function
t\mapsto p(t)
is neither convex nor concave over the entire interval
[0,1]
.

Proof Sketch.

1.
C^{2}
Differentiability of Pressure Function
P(Z,f)
:
By Theorem
7.14
, the map
Z\mapsto P(Z,f)
is
C^{1}
on
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
for
k\geq 2
. Under the stronger assumption that
Z\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{Z,f}
is
C^{2}
, analytic perturbation theory (
[
15
]
) implies that the leading eigenvalue
\lambda(Z,f)
is a
C^{2}
function of
Z
. Since
P(Z,f)=\log\lambda(Z,f)
, the pressure map
Z\mapsto P(Z,f)
is also
C^{2}
on
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
.

2. Regularity along the Geodesic:
The path
\gamma(t)=X+t(Y-X)
is an affine (hence
C^{\infty}
) path in the Hilbert space
H^{k}(TM)
. Since
P(\cdot,f)
is a
C^{2}
function on the Hilbert manifold
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)
, the composition
p(t)=P(\gamma(t),f)
is a
C^{2}
function from
[0,1]
to
\mathbb{R}
.

3. Second Derivative Formula:
Applying the chain rule twice to the composition
p(t)=P(\gamma(t),f)
:

•

\frac{dp}{dt}(t)=DP(\gamma(t))[\gamma^{\prime}(t)]=DP(\gamma(t))[Y-X]
.

•

\frac{d^{2}p}{dt^{2}}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}(DP(\gamma(t))[Y-X])=D^{2}P(\gamma(t))[Y-X,\gamma^{\prime}(t)]=D^{2}P(\gamma(t))[Y-X,Y-X]
.

This confirms formula (2).

4. Non-Convexity:
The sign of
\frac{d^{2}p}{dt^{2}}(t)
depends on the Hessian
D^{2}P
evaluated at
\gamma(t)
in the direction
Y-X
. As established, the relationship between the vector field
\gamma(t)
and the resulting dynamics/spectral data is highly non-linear. There is no general principle guaranteeing that
D^{2}P(Z)[V,V]
maintains a constant sign (non-negative for convexity, non-positive for concavity) for all points
Z
along a linear segment and a fixed direction
V=Y-X
. Therefore, convexity or concavity of
p(t)
over the whole interval
[0,1]
is not expected.
∎

Remark 7.23

(Interpretation)
.

This proposition clarifies that while the pressure varies smoothly (
C^{2}
) along linear interpolations (geodesics in
H^{k}
), it generally does not do so in a convex or concave manner. The second derivative, related to the Hessian
D^{2}P
, provides a measure of the local curvature of the pressure function along the geodesic path. Its sign can change, reflecting the complex interplay between the linear structure of the vector field space
H^{k}(TM)
and the non-linear nature of the thermodynamic formalism. This Hessian
D^{2}P
itself could be considered a geometric invariant associated with the stability of the leading eigenvalue under second-order perturbations, distinct from the Hessian
D^{2}\delta
related to the spectral gap.

7.8
Stability and Structure on the Moduli Space of Anosov Flows

We consider the space of dynamically distinct Anosov flows by quotienting by the action of the diffeomorphism group. Let
G=\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
be the group of
C^{r}
diffeomorphisms of
M
, acting on
\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
by pushforward:
(f,X)\mapsto f_{*}X
, where
(f_{*}X)(y)=Df_{f^{-1}(y)}(X(f^{-1}(y)))
. This action corresponds to
C^{r}
conjugacy of the generated flows.

Definition 7.24

(Moduli Space and Quotient Metric)
.

The
moduli space
of
C^{r}
Anosov flows is the orbit space
\mathcal{M}_{r}=\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)/G
. We denote the equivalence class of
X\in\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
by
[X]
. We equip
\mathcal{M}_{r}
with the quotient metric
d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}}
induced by
d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}
:

d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}}([X],[Y])=\inf_{X^{\prime}\in[X],\,Y^{\prime}\in[Y]}\left\{d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\right\}.

While the completeness of the moduli space
(\mathcal{M}_{r},d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}})
is a subtle issue (requiring stronger assumptions on the group action or metric structure than assumed here), we can establish the stability of dynamically relevant invariants on this space.

Theorem 7.25

(Quantitative Stability of Conjugacy Invariants on the Moduli Space)
.

Let
r\geq 2
. Let
I:\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
be a dynamical invariant (e.g., topological entropy, Lyapunov spectrum components, SRB measure entropy) satisfying:

(1)

Conjugacy Invariance:

I(f_{*}X)=I(X)
for all
X\in\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
and
f\in G=\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
. This allows
I
to descend to a well-defined function
\bar{I}:\mathcal{M}_{r}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\bar{I}([X])=I(X)
.

(2)

Local Lipschitz Continuity on
\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
:
For every
X_{0}\in\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
X_{0}
in
(\mathrm{An}^{r}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}})
and a constant
L>0
such that for all
X,Y\in U
,

|I(X)-I(Y)|\leq L\cdot d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(X,Y).

(This holds for many standard invariants when
r\geq 2
, as established in Theorems
7.8
,
7.12
, etc., using inequality (
4
)).

Then, the induced invariant
\bar{I}:\mathcal{M}_{r}\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz continuous with respect to the quotient metric
d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}}
. That is, for every
[X_{0}]\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
[X_{0}]
in
(\mathcal{M}_{r},d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}})
and a constant
L^{\prime}>0
(in fact,
L^{\prime}=L
) such that for all
[X],[Y]\in\mathcal{U}
,

|\bar{I}([X])-\bar{I}([Y])|\leq L^{\prime}\cdot d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}}([X],[Y]).

Proof.

Let
[X_{0}]\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
. By assumption (2), the invariant
I
is locally Lipschitz with constant
L
in some neighborhood
U
of
X_{0}
defined by
d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(X^{\prime},X_{0})<\delta
for some
\delta>0
.
Consider the neighborhood
\mathcal{U}=\{[Y]\in\mathcal{M}_{r}\mid d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}}([Y],[X_{0}])<\delta/2\}
. Let
[X],[Y]\in\mathcal{U}
.

By definition of the quotient metric
d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}}
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist representatives
X^{\prime}\in[X]
and
Y^{\prime}\in[Y]
such that

d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})<d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}}([X],[Y])+\epsilon.

We also need to ensure
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
are close enough to
X_{0}
to use the local Lipschitz property of
I
. Since
[X]\in\mathcal{U}
, we have
d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}}([X],[X_{0}])<\delta/2
. By definition of the quotient metric, this implies
\inf_{f\in G}d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(f_{*}X,X_{0})<\delta/2
. We can thus choose the representative
X^{\prime}
such that
d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(X^{\prime},X_{0})<\delta/2
. Similarly, we can choose the representative
Y^{\prime}\in[Y]
such that
d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(Y^{\prime},X_{0})<\delta/2
. By the triangle inequality for
d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}
,

d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\leq d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(X^{\prime},X_{0})+d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(X_{0},Y^{\prime})<\delta/2+\delta/2=\delta.

So, both
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
lie within the
\delta
-ball around
X_{0}
where
I
is Lipschitz with constant
L
.

Now we estimate the difference of the invariant
\bar{I}
:

\displaystyle|\bar{I}([X])-\bar{I}([Y])|

\displaystyle=|I(X^{\prime})-I(Y^{\prime})|\quad(\text{by conjugacy invariance (1)})

\displaystyle\leq L\cdot d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\quad(\text{by local Lipschitz property (2) since $X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}\in U$})

\displaystyle<L\cdot(d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}}([X],[Y])+\epsilon).

Since this holds for arbitrary
\epsilon>0
, we conclude that

|\bar{I}([X])-\bar{I}([Y])|\leq L\cdot d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}}([X],[Y]).

This demonstrates that
\bar{I}
is locally Lipschitz on
(\mathcal{M}_{r},d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}})
with constant
L^{\prime}=L
.
∎

Remark 7.26
.

This theorem confirms that the dynamically relevant distances captured by
d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}
on the space of Anosov flows descend to provide quantitative control over the variation of conjugacy invariants on the moduli space, provided sufficient regularity (
r\geq 2
). The local Lipschitz constant for the invariant on the moduli space is bounded by its local Lipschitz constant on the space of flows itself.

Local Structure of the Moduli Space via Slice Theorems

Theorem
7.25
demonstrates that conjugacy invariants behave well with respect to the quotient metric
d_{\mathcal{M}_{r}}
on the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{r}=\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)/\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
. A deeper geometric understanding of
\mathcal{M}_{r}
, including questions of completeness or the existence of geodesics, requires analyzing its local structure. In the context of group actions on manifolds, the standard tool for this is a slice theorem. We outline the structure of such a theorem and its demanding analytical prerequisites in our setting.

Let
G_{0}
be a suitable subgroup of
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
(e.g., identity component
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{0}(M)
, volume-preserving diffeomorphisms
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{\mu}(M)
, or isometries
\mathrm{Isom}(M,g_{0})
) acting on
\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
via pushforward
\Psi(f,X)=f_{*}X
. For the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)/G_{0}
to possess a well-behaved local structure akin to a manifold modelled on a Banach space, a slice theorem is generally needed.

Proposition 7.27

(Conditional Local Slice Theorem)
.

Let
r\geq 1
and let
G_{0}
be a Banach Lie subgroup of
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
acting on the Banach manifold
\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
. Let
X_{0}\in\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
. Assume the following conditions hold:

(i)

Smooth Action:
The action map
\Psi:G_{0}\times\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)\to\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
is at least
C^{1}
.

(ii)

Complemented Splitting of Tangent Space:
Let
V_{X_{0}}=T_{X_{0}}(G_{0}\cdot X_{0})
be the tangent space to the orbit through
X_{0}
. Assume
V_{X_{0}}
is a closed subspace of
T_{X_{0}}\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)\approx C^{r}(TM)
and admits a closed complementary subspace
H_{X_{0}}
such that
T_{X_{0}}\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)=V_{X_{0}}\oplus H_{X_{0}}
.

Then, there exists a local
C^{1}
slice for the
G_{0}
action through
X_{0}
. That is, there is a neighborhood
U_{X_{0}}
of
X_{0}
in
\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
and a
C^{1}
submanifold
S\subset U_{X_{0}}
containing
X_{0}
with
T_{X_{0}}S=H_{X_{0}}
such that:

•

The map
\Phi:G_{0}\times S\to\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
,
\Phi(f,Y)=f_{*}Y
, restricts to a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism from a neighborhood of
(e,X_{0})
onto
U_{X_{0}}
.

•

Every orbit
G_{0}\cdot X
for
X\in U_{X_{0}}
intersects
S
locally in exactly one point.

Proof Sketch.

The proof relies on the Inverse Function Theorem for Banach manifolds applied to the map
\Phi(f,h)=f_{*}(X_{0}+h)
defined locally near
(e,0)
on
G_{0}\times H_{X_{0}}
, where
h\in H_{X_{0}}
is viewed as a tangent vector representing displacement from
X_{0}
. The derivative
D\Phi(e,0)
maps
(\xi,h^{\prime})\in T_{e}G_{0}\times H_{X_{0}}
to
\alpha_{X_{0}}(\xi)+h^{\prime}\in V_{X_{0}}\oplus H_{X_{0}}
, where
\alpha_{X_{0}}
is the infinitesimal action (Lie derivative essentially). Assumption (ii) ensures this derivative is an isomorphism between the product tangent space and
T_{X_{0}}\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
. Application of the IFT establishes
\Phi
as a local diffeomorphism, which yields the slice properties. The slice
S
can be parameterized locally by
X_{0}+H_{X_{0}}
.
∎

Remark 7.28

(Analytical Challenges and Strategies)
.

Verifying the hypotheses of Proposition
7.27
is a major analytical task:

•

Smoothness of Action:
Requires careful estimates in function spaces, often demanding higher regularity (
r
large). The pushforward action involves derivatives of
f
, requiring
f
to be smoother than
X
.

•

Splitting Condition:
Establishing the closedness of the orbit tangent space
V_{X_{0}}
and constructing a continuous projection onto it (to define
H_{X_{0}}
) is typically the main bottleneck. Strategies include:

–

Restricting
G_{0}
to subgroups with simpler structure (e.g., finite-dimensional Lie groups like
\mathrm{Isom}(M,g_{0})
where
V_{X_{0}}
is finite-dimensional).

–

Using a Hilbert space structure (e.g., working with
\mathrm{An}^{k}(M)\subset H^{k}(TM)
and defining
H_{X_{0}}
via orthogonal complements using the
H^{k}
inner product), though compatibility with the group action remains crucial (the action might not preserve
H^{k}
).

–

Employing advanced analytical tools like Hodge decomposition (for volume-preserving groups acting on divergence-free fields) or Nash-Moser techniques if loss of derivatives occurs during the construction.

Despite these challenges, the slice theorem framework provides the standard route to understanding the local geometry of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)/G_{0}
. If established, it shows that
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is locally modelled on the Banach space
H_{X_{0}}
(the space of "transverse" deformations), providing a foundation for studying completeness (using Proposition
5.5
), geodesics, and the behavior of invariants on the quotient space.

Proposition 7.29

(Rigidity of Strict Entropy Maximizers)
.

Let
r\geq 2
such that the topological entropy functional
h_{\mathrm{top}}:\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
is at least
C^{2}
. Let
G_{0}=\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{0}(M)
be the group of
C^{r}
diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity, acting on
\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
by pushforward.
Suppose
X_{*}\in\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
satisfies:

(1)

X_{*}
is a local maximizer of
h_{\mathrm{top}}
within its path component in
\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)
, hence
Dh_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})=0
.

(2)

X_{*}
is a
strict
local maximizer modulo
G_{0}
-conjugacy. Assume this is guaranteed by a non-degeneracy condition on the second derivative (Hessian): Let
T_{X_{*}}\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)=V_{X_{*}}\oplus H_{X_{*}}
be a continuous splitting where
V_{X_{*}}=T_{X_{*}}(G_{0}\cdot X_{*})
is the tangent space to the orbit. Assume the Hessian
D^{2}h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})
is negative definite when restricted to the complementary subspace
H_{X_{*}}
.

Then, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
X_{*}
in
(\mathrm{An}^{r}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}})
such that if
X\in U
satisfies
h_{\mathrm{top}}(\varphi_{X})=h_{\mathrm{top}}(\varphi_{X_{*}})
, then
X
must be
C^{r}
-conjugate to
X_{*}
via some
f\in G_{0}
close to the identity. Equivalently, the set of flows locally maximizing topological entropy is, up to
G_{0}
-conjugacy, isolated within
U
.

Proof.

The proof combines the variational properties of
h_{\mathrm{top}}
near a non-degenerate maximum with the local structure possibly provided by a slice theorem (or arguments mirroring its logic).

1. Variational Analysis near
X_{*}
:
Since
X_{*}
is a local maximum,
Dh_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})=0
. By Taylor’s theorem for Banach manifolds, for
X=X_{*}+Y
with
\|Y\|_{C^{r}}
small, we have

h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)\approx h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})+Dh_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})[Y]+\frac{1}{2}D^{2}h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})[Y,Y]=h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})+\frac{1}{2}D^{2}h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})[Y,Y].

If
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})
, then we must have (approximately, or exactly in the limit as
Y\to 0
)
D^{2}h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})[Y,Y]\leq 0
.

2. Non-degeneracy Implication:
We assume the existence of a splitting
T_{X_{*}}\mathrm{An}^{r}(M)=V_{X_{*}}\oplus H_{X_{*}}
where
V_{X_{*}}
represents infinitesimal variations due to conjugacy (tangent to the orbit, where
D^{2}h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})
might be zero) and
H_{X_{*}}
represents dynamically distinct variations. The assumption (2) states that the Hessian
D^{2}h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})
is negative definite on
H_{X_{*}}
. Let
Y=Y_{V}+Y_{H}
be the decomposition according to the splitting. Then
D^{2}h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})[Y,Y]\approx D^{2}h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})[Y_{H},Y_{H}]
(assuming Hessians involving
V_{X_{*}}
vanish or are handled appropriately). If
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})
, then
D^{2}h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})[Y,Y]\approx 0
, which by negative definiteness on
H_{X_{*}}
implies
Y_{H}\approx 0
. Thus, the perturbation
Y
must predominantly lie in
V_{X_{*}}
, meaning
X=X_{*}+Y
is approximately infinitesimally conjugate to
X_{*}
.

3. From Infinitesimal to Actual Conjugacy (using Slice Idea):
Assume a local slice
S
through
X_{*}
exists, parameterized by
H_{X_{*}}
. Any
X
near
X_{*}
can be written uniquely as
X=f_{*}(X_{*}+h)
for some
f\in G_{0}
near identity and
h\in H_{X_{*}}
small. Since entropy is conjugacy invariant,
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*}+h)
. The function
h\mapsto h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*}+h)
has a strict local maximum at
h=0
by assumption (2). If
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})
, then
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*}+h)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})
. By the strict maximum property, this requires
h=0
. Therefore,
X=f_{*}X_{*}
for some
f\in G_{0}
close to the identity.

4. Regularity of Conjugacy:
Standard structural stability results for Anosov flows ensure that if
X
is
C^{r}
-close to
X_{*}
, the conjugacy
f
such that
X=f_{*}X_{*}
is itself
C^{r}
(possibly requiring slightly higher initial regularity
r
) and close to the identity.
∎

Remark 7.30
.

This proposition suggests a strong form of rigidity for Anosov flows that uniquely maximize topological entropy (modulo conjugacy). The crucial assumption is the non-degeneracy of the maximum, typically related to the Hessian
D^{2}h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{*})
restricted to directions transverse to the conjugacy orbit. Verifying this non-degeneracy condition and the required splitting of the tangent space would involve significant analysis, possibly using linear response theory for second derivatives or detailed knowledge of the specific system
X_{*}
. If these conditions hold, the proposition implies that entropy maximizers are dynamically isolated points in the relevant moduli space.

8
Topological and Finite Energy Anosov Flows

Building on the metric structure developed, particularly the
d_{\mathrm{An}}=d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{0}}
metric, we propose definitions for continuous flows that aim to retain key hyperbolic characteristics topologically, even if they lack smoothness. These definitions are motivated by the idea of controlled approximation by genuine smooth Anosov flows, potentially linking to results like Proposition
5.7
(consistency of limits under
d_{\mathrm{An}}
convergence).

Definition 8.1

(Topological Anosov Flow)
.

A continuous flow (a continuous group action of
\mathbb{R}
)
\theta=(\theta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}:M\to M
on a compact manifold
M
is called a
topological Anosov flow
if there exists a sequence of flows
\phi_{i}=(\varphi^{t}_{X_{i}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, generated by smooth Anosov vector fields
X_{i}\in\mathrm{An}(M)
, such that:

(i)

Uniform Flow Convergence:
For every
T>0
,

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}d_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\bigl(\varphi^{t}_{X_{i}},\theta_{t}\bigr)\to 0\quad\text{as }i\to\infty,

where
d_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}
denotes the uniform (i.e.,
C^{0}
) distance between maps on
M
.

(ii)

Generator Sequence is Cauchy in
d_{\mathrm{An}}
Metric:
The sequence of generators
\{X_{i}\}
is a Cauchy sequence with respect to the
d_{\mathrm{An}}=d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{0}}
metric:

\lim_{i,j\to\infty}d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{i},X_{j})=0.

We denote by
\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)
the set of all topological Anosov flows on
M
.

Definition 8.2

(Finite Energy Anosov Flow)
.

A continuous flow (a continuous group action of
\mathbb{R}
)
\theta=(\theta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}:M\to M
on a compact manifold
M
is called a
finite energy Anosov flow
if there exists a sequence of flows
\phi_{i}=(\varphi^{t}_{X_{i}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, generated by smooth Anosov vector fields
X_{i}\in\mathrm{An}(M)
, such that:

(i)

Uniform Flow Convergence:
For every
T>0
,

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}d_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\bigl(\varphi^{t}_{X_{i}},\theta_{t}\bigr)\to 0\quad\text{as }i\to\infty,

where
d_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}
denotes the uniform (i.e.,
C^{0}
) distance between maps on
M
.

(ii)

Generator Sequence is Bounded in
d_{\mathrm{An}}
Metric:
There exists a positive constant
C
such that the sequence of generators
\{X_{i}\}
is bounded with respect to the
d_{\mathrm{An}}=d_{\mathrm{An},\mathcal{C}^{0}}
metric relative to the zero vector field (or any fixed reference field):

d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{i},0)\leq C,

for all
i
.

We denote by
\mathcal{FEA}\mathcal{F}(M)
the set of all finite energy Anosov flows on
M
.

Remark 8.3
.

Since a Cauchy sequence in a metric space is always bounded, it follows directly from the definitions that every topological Anosov flow is also a finite energy Anosov flow:

\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{FEA}\mathcal{F}(M).

Whether this inclusion is strict is an open question. The "finite energy" condition is weaker than the Cauchy condition.

8.1
Motivation and Features

These definitions aim to capture the essence of Anosov dynamics in settings where smoothness might be lost, particularly for Definition
8.1
.

•

Approximation by Smooth Systems:
The flow
\theta
is defined as a limit of genuine smooth Anosov flows
\phi_{i}
.

•

Uniform Convergence (i):
Ensures that the orbit structure of
\phi_{i}
closely approximates that of
\theta
over any finite time horizon.

•

Metric Control on Generators (ii):

–

For
topological Anosov flows
, the condition that
\{X_{i}\}
is
d_{\mathrm{An}}
-Cauchy prevents the generators from varying wildly. Since
\|X_{i}-X_{j}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\leq d_{\mathrm{An}}(X_{i},X_{j})
(assuming this property holds for
d_{\mathrm{An},C^{0}}
), this implies
\{X_{i}\}
is also Cauchy in the
C^{0}
norm. By completeness of
C^{0}(TM)
,
X_{i}
converges in
C^{0}
to some vector field
X\in C^{0}(TM)
. By continuous dependence of solutions to ODEs on the vector field (in the
C^{0}
topology), the flow
\varphi^{t}_{X}
generated by
X
must be the limit flow
\theta
. Thus, a topological Anosov flow
\theta
possesses a
C^{0}
generator
X
which is the
C^{0}
limit of smooth Anosov generators
X_{i}
forming a
d_{\mathrm{An}}
-Cauchy sequence. The
d_{\mathrm{An}}
-Cauchy condition is stronger than just
C^{0}
convergence and imposes a constraint related to the "total deformation effort" among the approximants.

–

For
finite energy Anosov flows
, the generators are merely required to remain within a bounded "distance" from a reference point (e.g., the zero field) in the
d_{\mathrm{An}}
metric. This still implies
C^{0}
boundedness of the generators
X_{i}
. Under suitable conditions (e.g., Arzela-Ascoli if equicontinuity holds), this might allow extracting a
C^{0}
converging subsequence, but the limit flow might not satisfy the stronger properties expected from the Cauchy condition.

8.2
Properties and Conjectures

The definition of topological Anosov flows (Definition
8.1
) raises several crucial questions about their stability, structure, and relationship to classical hyperbolic dynamics. Exploring these questions is essential to understanding the scope and utility of this concept. We outline some key conjectures and possible results, primarily focusing on the more structured class
\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)
.

1. Stability

Conjecture 1

(Closedness under Uniform Limits)
.

The set
\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)
of topological Anosov flows is closed under uniform convergence of flows on compact time intervals. That is, if
\{\theta_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)
is a sequence such that for every
T>0
,
\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}d_{C^{0}}(\theta_{n}(t,\cdot),\theta(t,\cdot))\to 0
for some continuous flow
\theta
, then
\theta\in\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)
.

2. Relation to Smooth Anosov Flows

Conjecture 2

(Characterization of Smoothness)
.

A topological Anosov flow
\theta\in\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)
is topologically conjugate to a smooth Anosov flow generated by some
Y\in\mathrm{An}(M)
if and only if its
C^{0}
-generator
X\in C^{0}(TM)
(the
C^{0}
limit of the approximating
X_{i}
) belongs to the closure of
\mathrm{An}(M)
in the
d_{\mathrm{An},C^{1}}
-metric topology (or a similarly strong topology related to smoothness).

3. Inherited Dynamical Properties

Conjecture 3

(Density of Periodic Orbits)
.

Every topological Anosov flow
\theta\in\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)
possesses a dense set of periodic orbits.

Conjecture 4

(Positive Topological Entropy)
.

Every topological Anosov flow
\theta\in\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)
has positive topological entropy,
h_{\mathrm{top}}(\theta)>0
.

4. Role of the
d_{\mathrm{An}}
-Cauchy Condition

It is expected that the
d_{\mathrm{An}}
-Cauchy condition (ii) in Definition
8.1
is significantly stronger than merely requiring the generators
\{X_{i}\}
to be
C^{0}
-Cauchy.

Conjecture 5

(Structural Implications of
d_{\mathrm{An}}
-Cauchy)
.

The requirement that the approximating generators
\{X_{i}\}
form a Cauchy sequence in the
d_{\mathrm{An}}=d_{\mathrm{An},C^{0}}
metric ensures that the limit flow
\theta
inherits strong structural or stability properties (beyond those guaranteed by just
C^{0}
generator convergence), such as certain shadowing properties, existence of continuous invariant foliations (or laminations), or the existence of physical/SRB-like invariant measures reflecting the hyperbolicity of the approximants.

5. Geometric Properties of the Space

Defining a suitable topology on
\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)
itself is a prerequisite for studying its geometric properties. A natural candidate topology might involve simultaneous uniform convergence of flows and a condition related to the
d_{\mathrm{An}}
-convergence of generators (perhaps defining a metric on
\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)
based on the
d_{\mathrm{An}}
distance between approximating sequences).

Conjecture 6

(Completeness)
.

The space
\mathcal{TA}\mathcal{F}(M)
, endowed with a suitable topology reflecting both uniform flow convergence and the
d_{\mathrm{An}}
-Cauchy condition on generators, forms a complete metric space (or is complete in a relevant sense).

6. Connections to Other Hyperbolic Definitions

A fundamental question is how Definition
8.1
relates to classical intrinsic definitions of topological hyperbolicity, which are often based on the existence of continuous invariant splittings with exponential behaviour or local product structure.

Conjecture 7

(Equivalence with Classical Definitions)
.

A continuous flow
\theta
is a topological Anosov flow according to Definition
8.1
if and only if it satisfies standard criteria for topological hyperbolicity, such as possessing a continuous invariant splitting
TM=E^{s}_{\theta}\oplus E^{u}_{\theta}\oplus E^{c}_{\theta}
(where
E^{c}_{\theta}
is the one-dimensional bundle tangent to the flow orbits) such that for some continuous Riemannian metric, vectors in
E^{s}_{\theta}
are uniformly contracted exponentially fast by the forward flow
\theta_{t}
(
t>0
) and vectors in
E^{u}_{\theta}
are uniformly contracted exponentially fast by the backward flow
\theta_{-t}
(
t>0
).

These conjectures outline a research program aimed at validating Definition
8.1
and understanding its place within the broader theory of hyperbolic dynamics. Similar questions could be posed for the larger class
\mathcal{FEA}\mathcal{F}(M)
, likely with different answers.

9
Conclusion and Future Directions

In this work, we introduced a family of Hofer-like metrics
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
on the space of Anosov vector fields
\mathrm{An}(M)
, motivated by the Hofer metric in symplectic geometry. We established their basic properties, including the crucial inequality
\|\cdot\|_{V}\leq d_{\mathrm{An},V}
, invariance properties under diffeomorphisms, and completeness when using
C^{r}(r\geq 1)
or suitable Sobolev
H^{k}
norms for
V
.

The core contribution lies in demonstrating the dynamical significance of these metrics. We showed that proximity in
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
(for appropriate
V
, typically
C^{1}
or stronger) implies quantitative control over key dynamical invariants. Specifically, we established continuity results for topological entropy, Lyapunov exponents, SRB measures, pressure, and zeta functions. Furthermore, under sufficient regularity (
C^{2}
or
H^{k}
with
k
large), we demonstrated local Lipschitz continuity for pressure, spectral gap, and SRB dimension, and Fréchet differentiability for pressure, Lyapunov exponents, and the spectral gap, with derivatives given by linear response formulas. These results highlight that the
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
metrics provide a dynamically relevant geometric structure on
\mathrm{An}(M)
. The
H^{k}
metric variant offers the advantage of a locally flat geometry with straight-line geodesics. We also explored the implications for the moduli space of Anosov flows, showing stability of invariants and outlining the slice theorem framework for analyzing its local structure.

Finally, we proposed a definition for
Topological Anosov Flows
based on simultaneous uniform convergence of flows and
d_{\mathrm{An}}
-Cauchy convergence of the generating
C^{0}
vector fields. This definition offers a possibly new way to extend hyperbolic concepts to non-smooth settings, leveraging the metric geometry of
\mathrm{An}(M)
.

Future Research Directions

Several promising avenues for future research emerge:

•

Global Geometry and Geodesics:
Investigate the existence and properties of globally minimizing geodesics for
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
. Are path components always connected by minimizing paths? What is the diameter of
(\mathrm{An}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},V})
? (Finite diameter seems unlikely for
d_{\mathrm{An}}
). Does the
H^{k}
metric admit global geodesics?

•

Curvature Analogues:
Explore the second variation of path length or the Hessian of dynamical invariants (like pressure or spectral gap, see Theorem
7.18
, Proposition
7.20
, Proposition
7.22
) as notions of curvature on
(\mathrm{An}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},V})
. Does negative curvature relate to divergence of nearby deformation paths?

•

Completeness of
d_{\mathrm{An}}
:
Is the space
(\mathrm{An}(M),d_{\mathrm{An}})
complete? Proposition
5.5
showed completeness for
d_{\mathrm{An},C^{r}}
(
r\geq 1
) and
d_{\mathrm{An},H^{k}}
. The case
V=C^{0}
is more subtle as
C^{0}
limits of Anosov flows need not be Anosov.

•

Distance to Boundary:
Can
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
be used to quantify the distance from an Anosov flow
X
to the boundary
\partial\mathrm{An}(M)
within
C^{r}(TM)
? Does
\inf_{Y\notin\mathrm{An}(M)}d_{\mathrm{An},V}(X,Y)
relate to stability margins?

•

Isometries and Symmetries:
Characterize the isometry group of
(\mathrm{An}(M),d_{\mathrm{An},V})
. Are there non-trivial isometries beyond reparameterizations or diffeomorphisms acting via pushforward (Proposition
5.2
)?

•

Topological Anosov Flows (Definition
8.1
):

–

Explore the properties of flows satisfying this definition. Do they exhibit shadowing, expansivity, or possess canonical invariant measures? (Address Conjectures
3
,
4
,
5
)

–

How does this definition compare to other existing definitions of topological hyperbolicity? (Address Conjecture
7
)

–

Can concrete examples be constructed, especially non-smooth ones?

–

Investigate the stability and completeness of the space
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{An},\mathrm{top}}(M)
. (Address Conjectures
1
,
6
)

–

Explore the relationship between smoothness of the flow and properties of the generator sequence (Address Conjecture
2
).

•

Connections to Other Geometries:
Relate
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
to other geometric structures on spaces of dynamical systems, such as Optimal Transport-based metrics or metrics arising from thermodynamic formalism.

•

Extensions:
Can similar metrics be defined and studied for partially hyperbolic systems, or for discrete-time Anosov diffeomorphisms?

These directions highlight thepossible for the Hofer-like metrics
d_{\mathrm{An},V}
to serve as a valuable tool in the geometric analysis of dynamical systems, offering new perspectives on stability, classification, and the boundary between regular and chaotic behavior.

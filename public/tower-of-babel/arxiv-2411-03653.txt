RoCK blocks of double covers of symmetric groups and generalized Schur algebras Thanks: The author was supported by the NSF grant DMS-2346684.
Alexander Kleshchev

Abstract
. We study blocks of the double covers of symmetric and alternating groups. The main result is a ‘local’ description, up to Morita equivalence, of arbitrary defect RoCK blocks of these groups in terms of generalized Schur superalgebras corresponding to an explicit Brauer tree superalgebra. In view of the recent results on Broué’s Conjecture for these groups, our result provides a ‘local’ description of an arbitrary block of the double covers of symmetric and alternating groups up to derived equivalence.

20C30, 20C20

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Double covers and generalized Schur algebras

Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
be a double cover of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
be the double cover of the alternating group
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
. In this paper, we are concerned with the blocks of
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
. Our main result is a ‘local’ description, up to Morita superequvalence, of arbitrary defect RoCK blocks of these groups of
\bar{p}
-weight
d
in terms of generalized Schur superalgebras
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)
corresponding to an explicit Brauer tree superalgebra
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}
.
The analogous results for the symmetric groups were obtained in
[
10
]
. In view of the recent results
[
7
,
6
]
on
Broué’s Conjecture for
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
, our result provides a ‘local’ description of an arbitrary block of these groups up to derived equivalence.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be an algebraically closed field of odd characteristic
p
.
We have a canonical central element
z\in\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
and a central idempotent
e_{z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(1-z)/2\in\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}.
This yields an ideal decomposition
\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}=\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}e_{z}\oplus\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(1-e_{z}).

So the blocks of
\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
split into blocks of
\mathcal{T}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}e_{z}
and the blocks of
\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(1-e_{z})
.
Note that
\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(1-e_{z})\cong\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. A ‘local’ description of the blocks of
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
in terms of certain generalized Schur algebras was conjecture by Turner
[
28
]
and established in
[
10
]
. So in this paper we concentrate on the blocks of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
sometimes referred to as the
spin blocks of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

The algebra
\mathcal{T}_{n}
comes with a natural
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading
\mathcal{T}_{n}=(\mathcal{T}_{n})_{\bar{0}}\oplus(\mathcal{T}_{n})_{\bar{1}}
with
(\mathcal{T}_{n})_{\bar{0}}=\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{A}_{n}e_{z}
, and the blocks of
(\mathcal{T}_{n})_{\bar{0}}
are the
spin blocks of the alternating group
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
. As
\mathcal{T}_{n}
is a superalgebra, we can also consider the
superblocks
of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
, i.e. indecomposable superideals of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
. It is well-known that the superblocks are indecomposable as usual ideals (i.e. superblocks are blocks) unless we deal with trivial defect blocks. Trivial defect blocks are simple algebras and are completely elementary, thus working with superblocks vs. blocks does not create any serious problems.

Superblocks of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
are labeled by the pairs
(\rho,d)
, where
\rho
is a
\bar{p}
-core partition and
d
is a non-negative integer such that
|\rho|+dp=n
. We denote by
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
the block of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
corresponding to such a pair
(\rho,d)
. The integer
d
is then referred to as the
\bar{p}
-weight
of
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
. Denoting by
h(\rho)
the number of non-zero parts of the partition
\rho
, the
parity
of
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
is defined as

\operatorname{par}(\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|\rho|-h(\rho)+d\text{ }(\text{\rm mod }2)\,.
The even (resp. odd) parity blocks are the ones whose irreducible supermodules have type
\tt M
(resp. type
\tt Q
), see
[
20
, §12.2, Theorem 22.3.1]
.

The case
d=0
corresponds to the defect zero situation, so we often assume that
d>0
. The even part
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}
is then a spin block of
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
. The defect group of
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
(which is the same as the defect group of
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}
) depends only on
d
and is abelian if and only if
d<p
.

The following was conjectured by Schaps and Kessar, cf.
[
1
]
and proved in
[
7
,
6
]
:

Theorem (Ebert-Lauda-Vera and Brundan-Kleshchev).

Let
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
be
\bar{p}
-cores and
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(i)

\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho^{\prime},d^{\prime}}
are derived equivalent if and only if
d=d^{\prime}
and
\operatorname{par}(\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d})=\operatorname{par}(\mathcal{B}^{\rho^{\prime},d^{\prime}})
.

(ii)

\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho^{\prime},d^{\prime}}_{\bar{0}}
are derived equivalent if and only if
d=d^{\prime}
and
\operatorname{par}(\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d})=\operatorname{par}(\mathcal{B}^{\rho^{\prime},d^{\prime}})
.

(iii)

\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho^{\prime},d^{\prime}}_{\bar{0}}
are derived equivalent if and only if
d=d^{\prime}
and
\operatorname{par}(\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d})\neq\operatorname{par}(\mathcal{B}^{\rho^{\prime},d^{\prime}})
.

In
[
24
]
, we have defined the notion of a
RoCK spin block
. To be more precise, for every
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define
d
-Rouquier
\bar{p}
-cores
; then, if
\rho
is a
d
-Rouquier
\bar{p}
-core, the spin blocks
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}
(of symmetric and alternating groups, respectively) are called
RoCK
. Importantly, for every
d
and every
{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2
there exists a
d
-Rouquier
\bar{p}
-core
\rho
with
\operatorname{par}(\rho)={\varepsilon}
. So, in view of the theorem above,
every spin block of a symmetric or alternating group is derived equivalent to a RoCK spin block. Therefore, to get a ‘local’ description of an arbitrary spin block of a symmetric or alternating group up to derived equivalence, it suffices to get a ‘local’ description of an arbitrary RoCK spin block.

Let
A_{\ell}
be the (graded) Brauer tree (super)algebra defined in (
10.18
). Denote by
\mathcal{C}_{1}
the
rank
1
Clifford superalgebra
, defined in Example
3.21
. For superalgebras
A
and
B
, we always denote by
A\otimes B
their
tensor product as superalgebras
, and by
A\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
the
wreath superproduct
defined in (
3.5
). In
[
24
, Proposition 5.4.10]
, the following Morita (super)equivalences were established for the (non-trivial) abelian defect case
d<p
:

Theorem (Kleshchev-Livesey).

Let
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
be a RoCK spin block with
d<p
.

(i)

If
\operatorname{par}(\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d})
is even, then
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}\sim_{\mathop{\rm sMor}\nolimits}A_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}\sim_{{\mathrm{Mor}}}(A_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d})_{\bar{0}}
.

(ii)

If
\operatorname{par}(\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d})
is odd, then
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}\sim_{\mathop{\rm sMor}\nolimits}(A_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d})\otimes\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}\sim_{{\mathrm{Mor}}}A_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
.

As explained in
[
24
]
, this can be considered a local description of an
abelian defect
RoCK block.

The case of an arbitrary defect
d
is much more subtle. First of all we need a
regrading

{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}
of the graded superalgebra
A_{\ell}
, defined in §
4.1
. The wreath superproduct
A_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
is isomorphic to a regrading of the wreath superproduct
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, see §
11.8
, therefore
A_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}\sim_{\mathop{\rm sMor}\nolimits}{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. So in the abelian defect case, treated in the previous theorem, we could use either
A_{\ell}
or
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}
. However, for the arbitrary defect case the distinction between
A_{\ell}
and
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}
becomes crucial.

In
[
25
]
, certain
generalized Schur algebras

T^{A}(n,d)
were studied. These generalize Turner’s construction
[
28
]
studied in
[
9
]
. Let
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)
be the
generalized Schur (graded super)algebra corresponding to
A={\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}
, see §
5.5
. One can think of it informally as an ‘upgrade’ of the wreath superproduct
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
; in fact, in general
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
is an idempotent truncation of
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(d,d)
, and
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(d,d)\sim_{\mathop{\rm sMor}\nolimits}{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
if and only if
d<p
. Note also that for a fixed
d
, the graded superalgebras
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)
are graded Morita superequivalent for all
n\geq d
, so it will suffice to consider only
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(d,d)
. Our first main result is (see Theorem
11.26
):

Theorem A.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
be a RoCK spin block.

(i)

If
\operatorname{par}(\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d})
is even, then
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}\sim_{\mathop{\rm sMor}\nolimits}T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(d,d)
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}\sim_{{\mathrm{Mor}}}T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(d,d)_{\bar{0}}
.

(ii)

If
\operatorname{par}(\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d})
is odd, then
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}\sim_{\mathop{\rm sMor}\nolimits}T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(d,d)\otimes\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}\sim_{{\mathrm{Mor}}}T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(d,d)
.

To prove Theorem A, we relate the blocks of
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
to cyclotomic quiver Hecke superalgebras following the work of Kang-Kashiwara-Tsuchioka
[
17
]
and then study RoCK blocks in the setting of cyclotomic quiver Hecke superalgebras.

1.2.
Cyclotomic quiver Hecke superalgebras

Let
\ell=(p-1)/2
and
R_{\theta}
be the
quiver Hecke superalgebra
of Lie type
{\tt A}_{\ell}^{\hskip-0.85358pt{}^{(2)}}
corresponding to an element
\theta
of the non-negative
part of the root lattice of Lie type
{\tt A}_{\ell}^{\hskip-0.85358pt{}^{(2)}}
. (In the main body of the paper we work in a more general situation, see §
7.1
, in particular a ground field of characteristic
0
is allowed and
\ell
does not have to be of the form
\ell=(p-1)/2
for a prime
p
.)

Let
H_{\theta}
be the
cyclotomic quotient
of
R_{\theta}
corresponding to the fundamental dominant weight
\Lambda_{0}
, see §
9.1
. We have
H_{\theta}\neq 0
if and only if
\theta\in\{\Lambda_{0}-w\Lambda_{0}+d\delta\mid w\in W,\,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
, where
W
is the Weyl group and
\delta
is the null root, see §
6.2
. Any
\theta\in\{\Lambda_{0}-w\Lambda_{0}+d\delta\mid w\in W,\,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
can be written in the from
\theta=\rho(\theta)+d(\theta)\delta
for unique
\rho(\theta)\in\{\Lambda_{0}-w\Lambda_{0}\mid w\in W\}
and unique
d(\theta)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

We call
H_{\theta}
a
RoCK block
(of cyclotomic quiver Hecke superalgebras) if
(\theta\mid\alpha_{0}^{\vee})\geq 2d(\theta)
and
(\theta\mid\alpha_{i}^{\vee})\geq d(\theta)-1
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
, see §
10
. This Lie-theoretic condition is equivalent to the combinatorial condition considered in
[
24
]
.
Theorem A is deduced from the following theorem (see Corollary
11.23
):

Theorem B.

Let
H_{\theta}
be a RoCK block and
d=d(\theta)
.
If
n\geq d
then the graded superalgebras
H_{\theta}
and
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)
are graded Morita superequivalent.

Let
H_{\theta}
be a RoCK block with
\rho=\rho(\theta)
and
d=d(\theta)
. To prove Theorem B, we construct an explicit idempotent
e\in H_{\theta}
such that the idempotent truncation
X_{\rho,d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=eH_{\theta}e
is (graded) Morita (super)equivalent to
H_{\theta}
. The construction of the idempotent
e
involves crucially the so-called Gelfant-Graev idempotents, see §
8.1
.

A subtle point is that we then need to consider an explicit regrading
{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}
of
X_{\rho,d}
. Thus
{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}=X_{\rho,d}
as algebras, but the degree and parity of elements is changed. It is easy to see that such algebras

X_{\rho,d}
and
{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}

are graded Morita superequivalent, see §
3.4
.

We use Gelfand-Graev idempotents

\bar{\mathsf{f}}^{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}
corresponding to
J
-multicompositions
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Uplambda^{J}(n,d)
to
construct a projective generator

\Gamma_{\rho,d,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Uplambda^{J}(n,d)}{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}\bar{\mathsf{f}}^{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}.

Theorem B then follows from the following (see Theorem
11.19
):

Theorem C.

Let
H_{\theta}
be a RoCK block with
\rho=\rho(\theta)
and
d=d(\theta)
. If
n\geq d
then there is an isomorphism of graded superalgebras
\operatorname{End}_{{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}}(\Gamma_{\rho,d,n})^{\mathrm{sop}}\cong T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)
.

To prove Theorem C, we need to work ‘integrally’, i.e. over an appropriate DVR
\Bbbk
. Working over
\Bbbk
, we proceed in the following steps:

(1) For a special Gelfand-Graev idempotent
\bar{\mathsf{f}}_{\omega_{d}}
we have an explicit graded superalgebra isomorphism

\Xi_{\rho,d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\sim}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{\mathsf{f}}_{\omega_{d}}{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}\bar{\mathsf{f}}_{\omega_{d}},

see Theorem
10.17
.

(2) For
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Uplambda^{J}(n,d)
, the idempotent truncation
\bar{\mathsf{f}}^{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}\bar{\mathsf{f}}_{\omega_{d}}
is naturally a graded right
\bar{\mathsf{f}}_{\omega_{d}}{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}\bar{\mathsf{f}}_{\omega_{d}}
-supermodule. In view of the isomorphism in (1), it can be considered as a graded right supermodule over the wreath superproduct
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. In Theorem
11.7
, we identify
\bar{\mathsf{f}}^{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}\bar{\mathsf{f}}_{\omega_{d}}
with a certain ‘permutation module’
M_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}
over
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
.

(3) In view of (2), we get an explicit homomorphism (see (
11.14
)):

\Upphi_{\rho,d,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathsf{E}}_{\rho,d,n}=\bigoplus_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$},\text{\boldmath$\mu$}\in\Uplambda^{J}(n,d)}\bar{\mathsf{f}}^{\text{\boldmath$\mu$}}{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}\bar{\mathsf{f}}^{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\to S^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{End}_{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\bigg(\bigoplus_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Uplambda^{J}(n,d)}M_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\bigg).

(4) We use the faithfulness of the

{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}
-module
{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}\bar{f}_{\omega_{d}}
to prove that
\Upphi_{\rho,d,n}
is injective,
see Corollary
10.11
and Lemma
11.16
.

(5) We use the identification in (2), we show that the image
\Upphi_{\rho,d,n}({\mathsf{E}}_{\rho,d,n})
contains certain explicit endomorphisms of the form
{\mathtt{i}}^{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}({\mathsf{x}})
for
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Uplambda^{J}(n-1,d-1)
and
{\mathsf{x}}\in{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}
, see Lemma
11.18
.

(6) We use the theory of
imaginary cuspidal representations
of the cyclotomic quiver Hecke algebras developed in
[
21
]
, especially Theorem
8.28
, to show that
\Upphi_{\rho,d,n}({\mathsf{E}}_{\rho,d,n})
contains the degree zero component
S^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)^{0}
, see Lemma
11.17
.

(7) We use the fact established in Corollary
5.18
that, under the assumption
d\leq n
, the
\Bbbk
-subalgebra
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)\subseteq S^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)
is generated by the degree zero component
S^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)^{0}
and the set
\{{\mathtt{i}}^{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}({\mathsf{x}})\mid{\mathsf{x}}\in{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell},\,\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Uplambda^{J}(n-1,d-1)\}
. Together with (5) and (6), this implies that
\Upphi_{\rho,d,n}({\mathsf{E}}_{\rho,d,n})
contains
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)
.

(8) To show that the image
\Upphi_{\rho,d,n}({\mathsf{E}}_{\rho,d,n})
is exactly
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)
and not larger, we bring in a symmetricity argument. First, we show that the algebra
\Upphi_{\rho,d,n}({\mathsf{E}}_{\rho,d,n})\cong{\mathsf{E}}_{\rho,d,n}
is symmetric (extending scalars to a field), see Lemma
11.12
. Then we apply the main result of
[
23
]
showing that
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)
is a
maximal
symmetric subalgebra of
S^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
General conventions

We denote
\mathbb{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
\mathbb{N}_{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
\mathbb{Z}/2=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=\{{\bar{0}},{\bar{1}}\}
.

We will work over a principal ideal domain
\Bbbk
such that
2
is invertible in
\Bbbk
. Additional assumptions will eventually be made on
\Bbbk
when necessary.

Throughout the paper we fix

\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and denote

I\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{0,1,\dots,\ell\}\quad\text{and}\quad J\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{0,1,\dots,\ell-1\}.

(2.1)

We eventually identify
I
with the set of vertices of a certain Dynkin diagram, see §
6.1
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
and a set
S
, we write elements of
S^{d}
as
\text{\boldmath$s$}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})=s_{1}\cdots s_{d}
, with
s_{1},\dots,s_{d}\in S
, and refer to them as
words with entries in
S
.
We use the notation
s^{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=s\cdots s\in S^{d}
for
s\in S
.
Given words
\text{\boldmath$s$}\in S^{d}
and
\text{\boldmath$t$}\in S^{c}
, we have the concatenation word
\text{\boldmath$s$}\text{\boldmath$t$}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=s_{1}\cdots s_{d}t_{1}\cdots t_{c}\in S^{d+c}
.

2.2.
Partitions and compositions

A
composition
is an infinite sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
of non-negative integers which are eventually zero. Any composition
\lambda
has finite sum
|\lambda|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dots
, and we say that
\lambda
is a composition of
|\lambda|
. We write
\Uplambda
for the set of all compositions, and for each
d\in\mathbb{N}
we write
\Uplambda(d)
for the set of all compositions of
d
. When writing compositions, we may collect consecutive equal parts together with a superscript, and omit an infinite tail of
0
’s. For example, for
d\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, we have the composition

\omega_{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(1^{d})\in\Uplambda(d).

(2.2)

For
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

\Uplambda(n,d)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)\in\Uplambda(d)\mid\lambda_{k}=0\ \text{for all $k>n$}\}.

If
\lambda\in\Uplambda(n,d)
we always write
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
.

Let
\Uplambda^{J}(d)
denote the set of all
J
-multicompositions
of
d
. So the elements of
\Uplambda^{J}(d)
are the tuples

\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}=(\lambda^{(0)},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell-1)})
of compositions satisfying
\sum_{j\in J}|\lambda^{(j)}|=d
. We set

\Uplambda^{J}(n,d)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Uplambda^{J}(d)\mid\lambda^{(j)}_{k}=0\ \text{for all $j\in J$ and $k>n$}\}.

We have a bijection

\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Uplambda^{J}(n,d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\sim}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Uplambda(n\ell,d),\ \text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\mapsto(\lambda^{(0)}_{1},\lambda^{(1)}_{1},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell-1)}_{1},\dots,\lambda^{(0)}_{n},\lambda^{(1)}_{n},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell-1)}_{n}).

(2.3)

A
partition
is a composition whose parts are weakly decreasing. We write
{\mathcal{P}}(d)
for the set of partitions of
d
.
We set

\displaystyle\Uplambda_{+}(n,d)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Uplambda(n,d)\cap{\mathcal{P}}(d).

Let
{\mathcal{P}}^{\ell}(d)
denote the set of all
\ell
-multipartitions
of
d
. We identify
{\mathcal{P}}^{\ell}(d)
with the set
{\mathcal{P}}^{J}(d)
of all
J
-multipartitions of
d
, i.e. the set of the
\ell
-tuples
(\lambda^{(0)},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell-1)})
of partitions satisfying
\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}|\lambda^{(i)}|=d
.

Define the sets of
colored compositions

\Uplambda^{\operatorname{col}}(n,d)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(\lambda,\text{\boldmath$j$})\mid\lambda\in\Uplambda(n,d),\ \text{\boldmath$j$}\in J^{n}\},\quad\Uplambda^{\operatorname{col}}(d)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Uplambda^{\operatorname{col}}(n,d).

Let
(\lambda,\text{\boldmath$j$})\in\Uplambda^{\operatorname{col}}(n,d)
. For each
j\in J
, set

|\lambda,\text{\boldmath$j$}|_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq n\\
j_{r}=j\end{subarray}}\lambda_{r}.

(2.4)

We denote

\text{\boldmath$k$}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0\,1\,\cdots\,(\ell-1)\in J^{\ell}.

Then

\text{\boldmath$k$}^{n}=0\,1\,\cdots\,(\ell-1)\ 0\,1\,\cdots\,(\ell-1)\ \cdots\ 0\,1\,\cdots\,(\ell-1)\in J^{n\ell},

We now have the injective map

\upgamma^{J}_{n,d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Uplambda^{J}(n,d)\to\Uplambda^{\operatorname{col}}(n\ell,d),\ \text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\mapsto(\alpha(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}),\text{\boldmath$k$}^{n}).

(2.5)

We often consider multicompositions in
\Uplambda^{J}(n,d)
as colored compositions in
\Uplambda^{\operatorname{col}}(n\ell,d)
via this injection.

A composition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
is called
essential
if
\lambda_{k}=0
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
implies that
\lambda_{l}=0
for all
l\geq k
. We denote by
{\mathcal{C}}(d)
the set of all essential compositions of
d
. We also have the set
{\mathcal{C}}^{\operatorname{col}}(d)
of
essential colored composition of
d
, i.e. those colored compositions
(\lambda,\text{\boldmath$j$})
of
d
with
\lambda
essential.

2.3.
Symmetric group

We denote by
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
the symmetric group on
\{1,\dots,d\}
(with
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
interpreted as the trivial group).
Let

s_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(r,r+1)\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
for
r=1,\dots,d-1
be the elementary transpositions in
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
.
We denote by
{\mathtt{l}}(w)
the corresponding
length
of
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
and set
\mathtt{sgn}(w)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-1)^{{\mathtt{l}}(w)}
. We denote by
w_{d}
the longest element of
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
.

For any
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\Uplambda(n,d)
, we have the
standard parabolic subgroup

\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{n}}\leq\mathfrak{S}_{d}.

Using the bijection (
2.3
), for any

\mathfrak{S}_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})}.

(2.6)

For any set
S
, the group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
acts on
S^{d}
on the left by place permutations:
g\cdot\text{\boldmath$s$}=s_{g^{-1}(1)}\cdots s_{g^{-1}(d)}
for
g\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
and
\text{\boldmath$s$}=s_{1}\cdots s_{d}\in S^{d}
.

3.
Graded superalgebras

3.1.
Graded superspaces and superalgebras

By a graded superspace we understand a
\Bbbk
-module with
\Bbbk
-module decomposition
V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z},\,{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2}V_{n,{\varepsilon}}
.
We refer to the elements of
V_{n,{\varepsilon}}
as elements of
bidegree

(n,{\varepsilon})
. We also refer to
n
as
degree
and
{\varepsilon}
as
parity
, and write for
v\in V_{n,{\varepsilon}}
:

\operatorname{bideg}(v)=(n,{\varepsilon}),\ \deg(v)=n,\ |v|={\varepsilon}.

We use the notation
V_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n,{\varepsilon}}
and

V^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=V_{n,{\bar{0}}}\oplus V_{n,{\bar{1}}},\quad V^{>n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{m>n,\,{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2}V_{m,{\varepsilon}}.

(3.1)

A
graded subsuperspace

W\subseteq V
is a
\Bbbk
-submodule such that
W=\sum_{n,{\varepsilon}}(W\cap V_{n,{\varepsilon}})
.

Let
V,W
be graded superspaces. The tensor product
V\otimes W
is considered as a graded superspace via
\operatorname{bideg}(v\otimes w)=(\deg(v)+\deg(w),|v|+|w|)
for all homogeneous
v\in V
and
w\in W
.
For
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}/2
, a bidegree
(m,\delta)
homogeneous linear map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V\to W
is a
\Bbbk
-linear map satisfying
f(V_{n,{\varepsilon}})\subseteq W_{n+m,{\varepsilon}+\delta}
for all
n,{\varepsilon}
. We denote the
\Bbbk
-module of all bidegree
(m,\delta)
homogeneous linear maps from
V
to
W
by
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(V,W)_{m,\delta}
, and set

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(V,W)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z},\,\delta\in\mathbb{Z}/2}\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(V,W)_{m,\delta}.

Let
V
be a graded superspace and
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
acts on
V^{\otimes d}
via

{}^{g}(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{d})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-1)^{[g;v_{1},\dots,v_{d}]}v_{g^{-1}(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{g^{-1}(d)},

(3.2)

where

[g;v_{1},\dots,v_{d}]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq d,\,g^{-1}(a)>g^{-1}(b)}|v_{a}||v_{b}|.

A graded (
\Bbbk
-)superalgebra is a graded superspace
A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z},\,{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2}A_{n,{\varepsilon}}
which is a unital algebra such that
A_{n,{\varepsilon}}A_{m,\delta}\subseteq A_{n+m,{\varepsilon}+\delta}
.
The
opposite superalgebra

A^{\mathrm{sop}}
is the same as
A
as a
\Bbbk
-module but has multiplication
a*b=(-1)^{|a||b|}ba
.

Let
A,B
be graded superalgebras. An
isomorphism

f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to B
is an algebra isomorphism which is homogeneous of bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
. We write
A\cong B
if
A
and
B
are isomorphic in this sense. The tensor product
A\otimes B
is considered as a graded superalgebra via
(a\otimes b)(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})=(-1)^{|b||a^{\prime}|}aa^{\prime}\otimes bb^{\prime}

for all homogeneous
a,a^{\prime}\in A
and
b,b^{\prime}\in B
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
V
be a graded superspace which is finite rank free as a
\Bbbk
-module. Then
\operatorname{End}_{\Bbbk}(V)
is a graded superalgebra.
Since
\Bbbk
is a PID, every
V_{n,{\varepsilon}}
is a free
\Bbbk
-module of finite rank. Choosing a homogeneous basis of
V
we see that
\operatorname{End}_{\Bbbk}(V)
is isomorphic to a matrix algebra over
\Bbbk
with appropriate bi-degrees assigned to the matrix units.
We often refer to the graded superalgebra of the form
\operatorname{End}_{\Bbbk}(V)
as a
graded matrix superalgebra
.

Let
A
be a graded superalgebra and
B\subseteq A
be a (unital) graded subsuperalgebra. The

supercentralizer

Z_{A}(B)
of
B
in
A
is the
graded subsuperalgebra of
A
defined as the linear span of all (homogeneous)
a\in A
such that
ba=(-1)^{|b||a|}ab
for all (homogeneous)
b\in B
.
We will need the following analogue of
[
8
, Proposition 4.10]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A
be a graded superalgebra and
B
a unital graded subsuperalgebra isomorphic to a graded matrix superalgebra. Then we have an isomorphism of graded superalgebras

B\otimes Z_{A}(B)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\sim}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A,\ b\otimes z\mapsto bz.

Proof.

By definition, we may assume that
B=\operatorname{End}_{\Bbbk}(V)
for some finite rank free graded
\Bbbk
-superspace
V
. Let
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
be a homogeneous
\Bbbk
-basis of
V
, and
E_{i,j}\in B
be the element given by
E_{i,j}(v_{k})=\delta_{j,k}v_{i}
.
Let
C\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E_{1,1}AE_{1,1}
. Note that for any
c\in C
, the element

{\varphi}(c)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{|E_{i,1}||c|}E_{i,1}cE_{1,i}
super-commutes with every
E_{j,k}
, so
{\varphi}(c)\in Z_{A}(B)
. It follows that the maps
{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C\to Z_{A}(B)
and
Z_{A}(B)\to C,\ z\mapsto zE_{1,1}
are
mutually inverse isomorphisms of graded superalgebras. Moreover, for all
i,j
, we have mutually inverse bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
isomorphisms of superspaces
C\to E_{i,i}AE_{j,j},\ c\mapsto E_{i,1}cE_{1,j}
and
E_{i,i}AE_{j,j}\to C,\ a\mapsto E_{1,i}aE_{j,1}
. Note that for all homogeneous
c\in C
and all
i,j
we have
E_{i,j}{\varphi}(c)=(-1)^{|E_{j,1}||c|}E_{i,1}cE_{1,j}
, so
E_{i,j}Z_{B}(A)=E_{i,i}AE_{j,j}
. It follows that the graded superalgebra homomorphism

B\otimes Z_{A}(B)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\sim}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A,\ b\otimes z\mapsto bz

is an isomorphism.
∎

We always consider the group algebra
\Bbbk\mathfrak{S}_{d}
of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
as a graded superalgebra in the trivial way, i.e. concentrated in bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
.
Given a graded superalgebra
A
, we consider the
wreath superproduct

W_{d}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=A\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d},

(3.5)

where
A\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}=A^{\otimes d}\otimes\Bbbk\mathfrak{S}_{d}
as graded superspaces. To define the algebra structure, we identify
A^{\otimes d}
and
\Bbbk\mathfrak{S}_{d}
with subspaces of
A^{\otimes d}\otimes\Bbbk\mathfrak{S}_{d}
in the obvious way, and postulate that
A^{\otimes d}
,
\Bbbk\mathfrak{S}_{d}
are subalgebras of
A\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, and that, recalling (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle w\,(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{d})={}^{w}(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{d})\,w\qquad(w\in\mathfrak{S}_{d},\ a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\in A).

Let
A
be a superalgebra and
d\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
For
1\leq t\leq d
,
1\leq r<s\leq d
, we have maps

\displaystyle\iota^{(d)}_{t}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to A^{\otimes d},\ a\mapsto 1_{A}^{\otimes(t-1)}\otimes a\otimes 1_{A}^{\otimes(d-t)},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\iota^{(d)}_{r,s}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\otimes A\to A^{\otimes d},\ a\otimes b\mapsto 1_{A}^{\otimes(r-1)}\otimes a\otimes 1_{A}^{\otimes(s-r-1)}\otimes b\otimes 1_{A}^{\otimes(d-s)}.

(3.7)

For
x\in A
and
y\in A\otimes A
, we sometimes denote

x_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\iota^{(d)}_{r}(x)\in A^{\otimes d},\quad y_{r,s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\iota^{(d)}_{r,s}(y)\in A^{\otimes d}.

(3.8)

3.2.
Graded supermodules

Let
A
be a superalgebra.
A
graded
A
-supermodule

V
is a (left)
A
-module which is also a graded superspace such that
A_{n,{\varepsilon}}V_{m,\delta}\subseteq V_{n+m,{\varepsilon}+\delta}
for all
n,m,{\varepsilon},\delta
.
A graded subsupermodule is then an
A
-invariant graded subsuperspace of
V
.
A graded
A
-supermodule
V
is
irreducible
if it has exactly two graded subsupermodules:
0
and
V
.

Let
V,W
be graded
A
-supermodules. A

bidegree
(m,\delta)
homogeneous graded
A
-supermodule homomorphism from
V
to
W
is a bidegree
(m,\delta)
homogeneous linear map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V\to W
satisfying
f(av)=(-1)^{\delta|a|}af(v)
for all (homogeneous)
a\in A,v\in V
. We denote by
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W)_{m,\delta}
the
\Bbbk
-module of all bidegree
(m,\delta)
homogeneous graded
A
-supermodule homomorphism from
V
to
W
, and set

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z},\,\delta\in\mathbb{Z}/2}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W)_{m,\delta}.

We refer to the elements of
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W)
as

graded
A
-supermodule homomorphisms from
V
to
W
.

We denote by

A\!\operatorname{-mod}
the category of all

finitely generated
graded
A
-supermodules and all graded
A
-supermodule homomorphisms.
An isomorphism in
A\!\operatorname{-mod}
will be denoted
\cong
.
On the other hand, a bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
homogeneous isomorphism in
A\!\operatorname{-mod}
will be denoted
\simeq
.
We denote by
{\mathrm{Irr}}(A)
the set of the isomorphism classes of the graded irreducible
A
-supermodules (for the isomorphism
\cong
).

In fact,
A\!\operatorname{-mod}
is a graded
({\mathpzc{Q}},\Uppi)
-supercategory in the sense of
[
3
, Definition 6.4]
, with
\Uppi
the parity change functor and
{\mathpzc{Q}}
the degree shift functor. To be more precise, if
M=\bigoplus_{n,{\varepsilon}}M_{n,{\varepsilon}}\in A\!\operatorname{-mod}
, we have
(\Uppi M)_{n,{\varepsilon}}=M_{n,{\varepsilon}-{\bar{1}}}
with the new action
a\cdot v=(-1)^{|a|}av
, and
({\mathpzc{Q}}M)_{n,{\varepsilon}}=M_{n-1,{\varepsilon}}
(with the same action).

Let
A,B
be graded superalgebras. Given a graded
A
-supermodule
V
and a graded
B
-supermodule
W
, we have the graded
(A\otimes B)
-supermodule
V\boxtimes W
with the action

(a\otimes b)(v\otimes w)=(-1)^{|b||m|}(av\otimes bw)\qquad(a\in A,\,b\in B,\,v\in V,\,w\in W).

A graded
(A,B)
-bisupermodule

V
is an
(A,B)
-bimodule which is also a graded superspace such that
A_{n,{\varepsilon}}V_{m,\delta}\subseteq V_{n+m,{\varepsilon}+\delta}
and
V_{m,\delta}B_{n,{\varepsilon}}\subseteq V_{n+m,{\varepsilon}+\delta}
for all
n,m,{\varepsilon},\delta
.
A homomorphism of graded
(A,B)
-bisupermodules is defined similarly to a homomorphism of graded
A
-supermodules. In particular, a bidegree
(m,\delta)
homogeneous homomorphism of graded
(A,B)
-bisupermodules
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V\to W
satisfies
f(avb)=(-1)^{\delta|a|}af(v)b
for all homogeneous
a\in A,v\in V,b\in B
.
Just like for graded supermodules, we use the notation
\cong
to denote an isomorphism of graded bisupermodules, and
\simeq
to denote a bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
homogeneous isomorphism of graded bisupermodules.

Example 3.9
.

Given a graded
A
-supermodule
M
, we have the graded superalgebra
\operatorname{End}_{A}(M)^{\mathrm{sop}}
, and
M
is naturally a graded
(A,\operatorname{End}_{A}(M)^{\mathrm{sop}})
-bisupermodule with
f\in\operatorname{End}_{A}(M)^{\mathrm{sop}}=\operatorname{End}_{A}(M)
acting via

m\cdot f=(-1)^{|f||m|}f(m)
.

3.3.
Graded Morita superequivalence

Let
A
and
B
be graded superalgebras. A
graded Morita superequivalence
between
A
and
B
is a Morita equivalence between
A
and
B
induced by a graded
(A,B)
-bisupermodule
M
and a graded
(B,A)
-bisupermodule
N
, i.e.
M\otimes_{B}N\simeq A
and
N\otimes_{A}M\simeq B

(both bimodule isomorphisms homogeneous of bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
).
We write
A\sim_{\mathop{\rm gsMor}\nolimits}B
for graded Morita superequivalence.

Example 3.10
.

Let
A
be a graded superalgebra and
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
be (not necessarily distinct) bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
idempotents of
A
.
We have a graded superalgebra
\bigoplus_{1\leq i,j\leq n}e_{i}Ae_{j}
with multiplication inherited from
A
. In fact, setting

P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}Ae_{i},

there the isomorphism of graded superalgebras

\bigoplus_{1\leq i,j\leq n}e_{i}Ae_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\sim}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{End}_{A}(P)^{\mathrm{sop}},\ a\mapsto{\varphi}_{a},

(3.11)

where for (a homogeneous)
a\in e_{i}Ae_{j}
, the endomorphism
{\varphi}_{a}
sends the summand
Ae_{k}
of
P
to
0
if
k\neq i
and the summand
Ae_{i}
to the summand
Ae_{j}
as follows:

{\varphi}_{a}(be_{i})=(-1)^{|a||b|}be_{i}a

(for a homogeneous
b\in A
). If
P
is a projective generator for
A
(equivalently, if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}Ae_{i}A=A
), then
A
is graded Morita superequivalent to
\operatorname{End}_{A}(P)^{\mathrm{sop}}
, with
M
being
P
considered as a graded
(A,\operatorname{End}_{A}(P)^{\mathrm{sop}})
-superbimodule as in Example
3.9
.

Let the graded
\Bbbk
-superalgebra
A
be finitely generated as a
\Bbbk
-module,
e\in A
be a bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
idempotent, and
\mathbb{F}=\Bbbk/\mathfrak{m}
be the quotient field of
\Bbbk
by a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Denote
A_{\mathbb{F}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}\otimes_{\Bbbk}A
and
e_{\mathbb{F}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1_{\mathbb{F}}\otimes e\in A_{\mathbb{F}}
.
We have the functors

\begin{split}Ae\otimes_{eAe}-\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}eAe\!\operatorname{-mod}\to A\!\operatorname{-mod},\\
eA\otimes_{A}-\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\!\operatorname{-mod}\to eAe\!\operatorname{-mod}.\end{split}

(3.12)

and

\begin{split}A_{\mathbb{F}}e_{\mathbb{F}}\otimes_{e_{\mathbb{F}}A_{\mathbb{F}}e_{\mathbb{F}}}-\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}e_{\mathbb{F}}A_{\mathbb{F}}e_{\mathbb{F}}\!\operatorname{-mod}\to A_{\mathbb{F}}\!\operatorname{-mod},\\
e_{\mathbb{F}}A_{\mathbb{F}}\otimes_{A_{\mathbb{F}}}-\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A_{\mathbb{F}}\!\operatorname{-mod}\to e_{\mathbb{F}}A_{\mathbb{F}}e_{\mathbb{F}}\!\operatorname{-mod}.\end{split}

(3.13)

The following lemma can be found in
[
21
, Lemma 2.2.20]
, see also
[

, Lemma 8.26]
.

Lemma 3.14
.

If for all fields
\mathbb{F}\in{\mathscr{F}}=\{\Bbbk/\mathfrak{m}\mid\text{$\mathfrak{m}$ is a maximal ideal of $\Bbbk$}\}
, the functors (
3.13
) are mutually quasi-inverse equivalences, then so are the functors (
3.12
).

3.4.
Regrading

An easy special case of a graded Morita superequivalence comes from a ‘regrading’ of a graded superalgebra in the following sense. Suppose that
A
is a graded superalgebra and we are given an orthogonal idempotent decomposition
1_{A}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}e_{r}
with the idempotents
e_{r}
of bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
. Then as graded superspaces we have
A=\bigoplus_{1\leq r,s\leq n}e_{s}Ae_{r}
. Given
grading supershift parameters

t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{and}\quad{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}/2,

(3.15)

we can consider the new graded superalgebra

{\mathsf{A}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{1\leq r,s\leq n}{\mathpzc{Q}}^{t_{r}-t_{s}}\Uppi^{{\varepsilon}_{r}-{\varepsilon}_{s}}e_{s}Ae_{r},

(3.16)

which equals
A
as an algebra but has different
\mathbb{Z}
- and
\mathbb{Z}/2
-gradings. To distinguish between the elements of
A
and
{\mathsf{A}}
, we denote by
{\mathsf{a}}
the element of
{\mathsf{A}}
corresponding to
a\in A
. In particular, we have the idempotents
{\mathsf{e}}_{1},\dots,{\mathsf{e}}_{n}\in{\mathsf{A}}
.
We have the graded
({\mathsf{A}},A)
-bisupermodule

X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{r=1}^{n}{\mathpzc{Q}}^{-t_{r}}\Uppi^{-{\varepsilon}_{r}}e_{r}A=\bigoplus_{s=1}^{n}{\mathpzc{Q}}^{-t_{s}}\Uppi^{-{\varepsilon}_{s}}{\mathsf{A}}{\mathsf{e}}_{s},

and the graded
(A,{\mathsf{A}})
-bisupermodule

Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{s=1}^{n}{\mathpzc{Q}}^{t_{s}}\Uppi^{{\varepsilon}_{s}}Ae_{s}=\bigoplus_{r=1}^{n}{\mathpzc{Q}}^{t_{r}}\Uppi^{{\varepsilon}_{r}}{\mathsf{e}}_{r}{\mathsf{A}},

inducing a graded Morita superequivalence between
A
and
{\mathsf{A}}
. Under this graded Morita superequivalence, a graded
A
-supermodule
V
, corresponds to the graded
{\mathsf{A}}
-supermodule

{\mathsf{V}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=X\otimes_{A}V\simeq\bigoplus_{r=1}^{n}{\mathpzc{Q}}^{-t_{r}}\Uppi^{-{\varepsilon}_{r}}e_{r}V.

(3.17)

3.5.
Superalgebras and supermodules

A
superalgebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded algebra. In order to apply the theory developed in the previous subsections, we identify superalgebras with graded superalgebras concentrated in
\mathbb{Z}
-graded degree
0
, and similarly for (bi)supermodules.
On the other hand, we consider graded superalgebras as superalgebras by forgetting
\mathbb{Z}
-grading.

In particular,
we get the notion of
Morita superequivalence
of superalgebras
A
and
B
induced by
an
(A,B)
-bisupermodule
M
and a
(B,A)
-bisupermodule
N
, i.e.
M\otimes_{B}N\simeq A
and
N\otimes_{A}M\simeq B
. We write
A\sim_{\mathop{\rm sMor}\nolimits}B
for Morita superequivalence.

Suppose that
A
is a superalgebra which is free of finite rank as a
\Bbbk
-module. An element
{\mathsf{t}}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(A,\Bbbk)
is called a
symmetrizing form
if
{\mathsf{t}}(A_{{\bar{1}}})=0
and the bilinear form
(a,b)_{\mathsf{t}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\mathsf{t}}(ab)
is a perfect symmetric pairing.
If
A
is equipped with a symmetrizing form, we say
A
is a
symmetric superalgebra.
If
A,B
are symmetric superalgebras, then
A\otimes B
is also a symmetric superalgebra with symmetrizing form

a\otimes b\mapsto{\mathsf{t}}_{A}(a){\mathsf{t}}_{B}(b),

where
{\mathsf{t}}_{A}
and
{\mathsf{t}}_{B}
are the symmetrizing forms of
A
and
B
respectively.

Lemma 3.18
.

If
A
is a symmetric superalgebra then so is
A^{\mathrm{sop}}
.

Proof.

Denoting the product in
A^{\mathrm{sop}}
by
*
, the lemma follows from the computation
{\mathsf{t}}(a*b)={\mathsf{t}}((-1)^{|a||b|}ba)={\mathsf{t}}((-1)^{|a||b|}ab)={\mathsf{t}}(b*a)
.
∎

Lemma 3.19
.

Suppose that a graded superalgebra
{\mathsf{A}}
is obtained from a graded superalgebra
A
by regrading, as in (
3.16
). If
A
is a symmetric superalgebra then so is
{\mathsf{A}}
.

Proof.

Identify
A
and
{\mathsf{A}}
as sets. We claim that if
{\mathsf{t}}
is a symmetrizing form on
A
then it is also a symmetrizing form on
{\mathsf{A}}
. The only thing that is not clear is that

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
A
be a
\Bbbk
-superalgebra which is free of finite rank as a
\Bbbk
-module. Then
A
is a symmetric superalgebra if and only if
A\otimes\mathcal{C}_{n}
is a symmetric superalgebra.

Proof.

The ‘only-if’ direction follows from the fact that
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is a symmetric superalgebra and tensor product of symmetric superalgebras is a symmetric superalgebra.

To prove the converse, since
\mathcal{C}_{n}\cong\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\otimes n}
, it suffices to establish that
A
is a symmetric superalgebra if so is
A\otimes\mathcal{C}_{1}
.
So suppose that
A\otimes\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a symmetric superalgebra with symmetrizing form
{\mathsf{t}}
and the corresponding symmetric perfect pairing
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathsf{t}}
. By definition,
{\mathsf{t}}((A\otimes\mathcal{C}_{1})_{\bar{1}})=0
, so
{\mathsf{t}}(A_{\bar{1}}\otimes 1)={\mathsf{t}}(A_{\bar{0}}\otimes\mathpzc{c}_{1})=0
. We claim that, moreover,
{\mathsf{t}}(A_{\bar{1}}\otimes\mathpzc{c}_{1})=0
. Indeed, for
a\in A_{\bar{1}}
, we have
a\otimes\mathpzc{c}_{1}=(a\otimes 1)(1\otimes\mathpzc{c}_{1})=-(1\otimes\mathpzc{c}_{1})(a\otimes 1)
, hence
{\mathsf{t}}(a\otimes\mathpzc{c}_{1})={\mathsf{t}}((a\otimes 1)(1\otimes\mathpzc{c}_{1}))={\mathsf{t}}((1\otimes\mathpzc{c}_{1})(a\otimes 1))=-{\mathsf{t}}(a\otimes\mathpzc{c}_{1})
which now must be zero by our running assumption that
2
is invertible in
\Bbbk
. It follows that the restriction of
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathsf{t}}
to
A_{\bar{0}}\otimes 1
is a perfect symmetric pairing,
so we can define the symmetrizing form on
A
via
a\mapsto{\mathsf{t}}(a\otimes 1)
.
∎

Using (

[

]
.

, and by

using the notation (

.

Let

9.5.
Correspondence of irreducible modules and formal characters

The material of this subsection will only be used in §
11.7
.
In this subsection we assume that
\Bbbk
is an algebraically closed field of odd characteristic
2\ell+1
.

Let
\theta=\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}\alpha_{i}\in Q_{+}
and
D
be an irreducible
\mathcal{T}_{\theta}
-supermodule. By
[
20
, Theorem 22.3.1]
, we have that
D
is of type
\mathtt{M}
if
n-m_{0}
is even and of type
\mathtt{Q}
if
n-m_{0}
is odd. Let
U_{n}
be the Clifford supermodule, i.e. the only irreducible
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-supermodule up to isomorphism, see
[
20
, Example 12.2.4]
. We have that
U_{n}
is of type
\mathtt{M}
if
n
is even and of type
\mathtt{Q}
if
n
is odd. Moreover,
\dim U_{n}=2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}
. By
[
20
, Lemma 12.2.13]
, we have a bijection

{\mathrm{Irr}}(\mathcal{T}_{\theta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\sim}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathrm{Irr}}(\mathcal{TC}_{\theta}),\ D\mapsto D\circledast U_{n},

with
D\circledast U_{n}
of type
\mathtt{M}
if and only if
D
and
U_{n}
have the same type, i.e. if and only if
n-m_{0}
and
n
have the same parity, i.e. if and only if
m_{0}
is even. Moreover,

\dim(e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\otimes 1_{\mathcal{C}_{n}})(D\circledast U_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2^{\lceil n/2\rceil-1}\dim e(\text{\boldmath$i$})D&\hbox{if $n$ and $n-m_{0}$ are odd,}\\
2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}\dim e(\text{\boldmath$i$})D&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Applying the Morita superequivalence and the isomorphism
\mathcal{TC}_{\theta}\sim_{\mathop{\rm sMor}\nolimits}\mathpzc{e}\mathcal{TC}_{\theta}\mathpzc{e}\cong H_{\theta}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{m_{0}}
of Theorem
9.19
, we now obtain a bijection

{\mathrm{Irr}}(\mathcal{T}_{\theta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\sim}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathrm{Irr}}(H_{\theta}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{m_{0}}),\ D\mapsto\mathpzc{e}(D\circledast U_{n}),

with
\mathpzc{e}(D\circledast U_{n})
of type
\mathtt{M}
if and only if
m_{0}
is even, and

\dim(1_{\text{\boldmath$i$}}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{C}_{m_{0}}})\mathpzc{e}(D\circledast U_{n})=2^{m_{0}-n}\dim(e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\otimes 1)(D\circledast U_{n}).

Finally, taking into account that all irreducible
H_{\theta}
-supermodules are of type
\mathtt{M}
by
[
14
, Proposition 6.15]
,
[
18
, §4.2]
,
[
19
, Theorem 8.6]
,
we have a bijection

{\mathrm{Irr}}(H_{\theta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\sim}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathrm{Irr}}(H_{\theta}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{m_{0}}),\ L\mapsto L\boxtimes U_{m_{0}}

with

\dim(1_{\text{\boldmath$i$}}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{C}_{m_{0}}})(L\boxtimes U_{m_{0}})=2^{\lceil m_{0}/2\rceil}\dim 1_{\text{\boldmath$i$}}L.

Composing this bijection with the inverse of previous one, we get a bijection

\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathrm{Irr}}(H_{\theta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\sim}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathrm{Irr}}(\mathcal{T}_{\theta})

with

\dim e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\beta(L)=2^{x(\theta)}\dim 1_{\text{\boldmath$i$}}L

where

x(\theta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lceil m_{0}/2\rceil+n-m_{0}-\lceil n/2\rceil+1&\hbox{if $n$ and $n-m_{0}$ are odd,}\\
\lceil m_{0}/2\rceil+n-m_{0}-\lceil n/2\rceil&\hbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

which is easily seen to be
\lceil(n-m_{0})/2\rceil
.

We have proved

Theorem 9.23
.

Let
\theta\in Q_{+}
.
There is a bijection
\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathrm{Irr}}(H_{\theta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{}_{\sim}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathrm{Irr}}(\mathcal{T}_{\theta})
such that for any
L\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(H_{\theta})
we have
\dim e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\beta(L)=2^{\lceil(n-m_{0})/2\rceil}\dim 1_{\text{\boldmath$i$}}L
.

Theorem 11.20
.

If

then the graded
\Bbbk
-superalgebras

and

are graded Morita superequivalent.

Proof.

By Lemma
9.14
, there exists a bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
idempotent
{\varepsilon}\in H_{\theta}
such that

. We have further idempotent truncation

). Thus

for the bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})

The regrading
{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}
of

defined in (

, see §
3.4
. Moreover,
{\mathsf{X}}_{\rho,d}
is graded Morita superequivalent to
{\mathsf{E}}_{\rho,d,n}
, see Lemma

. Finally, by Theorem
11.19
, we have the isomorphism of graded superalgebras
{\mathsf{E}}_{\rho,d,n}\cong T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(n,d)
. So it suffices to prove that

is graded Morita superequivalent to
H_{\theta}
, or by Lemma
3.14
that

is graded Morita superequivalent to
H_{\theta,\mathbb{F}}
.

It is well-known, see for example
[
24
, Corollary 2.2.15]
, that

is graded Morita superequivalent to

if

. By the first paragraph, the algebras

11.8.
Remarks on
A_{\ell}
vs.
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}

Throughout this subsection we work over
{\mathbb{L}}=\mathbb{F}
or
\mathbb{K}
(and drop
{\mathbb{L}}
from all the indices).
Let
H_{\theta}
be a RoCK block (of cyclotomic quiver Hecke superalgebras) with
d=d(\theta)
.

Let
A_{\ell}
be the regrading of
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}
defined in (
10.18
). (Note that, annoyingly,
A_{\ell}
was denoted
{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}
in
[
24
]
). It is a somewhat non-trivial fact which follows from
[
21
, Proposition 3.2.28]
that the wreath superproduct
W_{d}^{A_{\ell}}
is isomorphic to a regrading of the wreath superproduct
W_{d}^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}
.

One of the main results of
[
24
]
is that
the graded
{\mathbb{L}}
-superalgebras
H_{\theta}
and
W_{d}^{A_{\ell}}
are graded Morita superequivalent as long as
{\mathbb{L}}=\mathbb{K}
or
{\mathbb{L}}=\mathbb{F}
and
p>d
. Since a graded superalgebra is graded Morita superequivalent to its regrading by §
3.4
, this result also follows immediately from Corollary
11.25
and the isomorphism mentioned in the previous paragraph.

It has been conjectured in
[
24
, Conjecture 3]
that in general
H_{\theta}
and
T^{A_{\ell}}(d,d)
are graded Morita superequivalent. In Corollary
11.23
, we prove instead that
H_{\theta}
and
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(d,d)
are graded Morita superequivalent. In view of this result, Conjecture 3 of
[
24
]
is in fact
false
, since
T^{A_{\ell}}(d,d)
and
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(d,d)
are
not
in general graded Morita superequivalent (and not even Morita equivalent). The last statement follows from the fact that the irreducible modules of
T^{A_{\ell}}(d,d)
and
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(d,d)
have the same dimensions (being inflations of the irreducible modules over the common quotient algebra appearing in the right hand side of (
5.20
)), while the dimensions of the algebras
T^{A_{\ell}}(d,d)
and
T^{{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}}(d,d)
are in general different, so these algebras in general have different Cartan invariants.

Thus, while for the case where
p>d
and
p=0
(‘abelian defect case’, see
[
24
]
) one can get away without regrading, for the general case regrading is crucial.

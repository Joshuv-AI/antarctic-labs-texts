NSI-IBP: A General Numerical Singular Integral Method via Integration by Parts
Shaolin Liao *

Abstract
A general framework of Numerical Singular Integrals (NSI) method based on the Integration By Parts (IBP) has been developed for integrals involving singular and nearly singular integrands, or NSI-IBP. Through a general integration by parts formula and by choosing some analytically integrable function to approximate the original integrand, various well-known integration by parts methods can be derived. Rigorous mathematical derivations have been performed to transform the original singular or nearly singular integrals into non-singular integrals that can be computed efficiently, along with the boundary values added. What’s more important, the NSI-IBP method works well even when the exact form of the singular integrand is not known. Criteria on how to choose the appropriate function with a known analytical integral that closely approximates the original integrand have been outlined and explained. Numerical recipe has been presented to apply the proposed NSI-IBP. Numerical experiments have been carried out on various singular integrals such as the power-law decaying integrand, the logarithmic function, and their hybrid products. It can be shown that various relative accuracy up to 10 − 15 10^{-15} can be achieved, even the exact singular function is not known. Finally, the nearly singular integrals involving the scalar Green’s function have been evaluated for both electrostatics applications and Computational Electromagnetics (CEM) applications.

III
Integration by Part Method

The original integral
I_{0}
can be computed through the integration by part method as follows,

\displaystyle I_{0}=\left.p(x)h(x)\right|_{a}^{b}-\int_{a}^{b}h(x)p^{\prime}(x)dx,

(13)

\displaystyle h(x)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{q(x)}dx.

(14)

Simple Integration by Part Method

One might try to transform the original integral of Eq. (

\displaystyle f_{\gamma}(x)=x^{\gamma}f(x),

\displaystyle h_{\gamma}(x)\equiv\int\frac{1}{x^{\gamma}}dx=\left\{\begin{matrix}\frac{x^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma},\ \ \gamma\neq 1,\\

Some possible
q(x)
that satisfies Eq. (
51
) are given below.

(a)

Power-law decay:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q(x)}=x^{-\gamma}\rightarrow\beta=\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma},\ \ c=\gamma.

(52)

(b)

Exponential decay:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q(x)}=\exp(-\lambda x)\rightarrow\beta=-1,\ \ c=\lambda.

(53)

(3)

I_{+}=I_{0}
: to achieve
I_{+}=I_{0}
, the following condition should be met,

\displaystyle\frac{du(x)}{dx}-u(x)\frac{1}{q(x)}\frac{dq(x)}{dx}=1,

(54)

where
u(x)\equiv h(x)\tilde{q}(x)
and Eq. (
46
) reduces to

\displaystyle I_{0}=\left.u(x)\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\right|_{a}^{b}-\int_{a}^{b}\frac{u(x)}{q(x)}\frac{dp(x)}{dx}dx.

(55)

Eq. (
54
) is the ordinary differential equation of order one that can be readily solved as follows,

\displaystyle v(x)=\exp\left\{-\int\frac{1}{q(x)}\frac{dq(x)}{dx}dx\right\}=\frac{1}{q(x)},

(56)

\displaystyle u(x)=\frac{1}{v(x)}\int v(x)dx=h(x)q(x),

(57)

from which Eq. (
55
) reduces to

\displaystyle I_{0}=\left.h(x)p(x)\right|_{a}^{b}-\int_{a}^{b}h(x)\frac{dp(x)}{dx}dx.

(58)

which is actually the same as that obtained through integration by part method given in Eq. (
13
).

VI
Discussion

It can be seen that the integral of Eq. (
30
) in Section
V
can lead to various of integration by part methods by choosing different
\tilde{q}(x)
, which are summarized as follow.

1.

\tilde{q}(x)=q(x)
: when
q(x)
is known and the indefinite integral of its inverse is known, it is equivalent to the integration by part method shown in Eq. (
25
).

\displaystyle I_{0}=\left.{p}(x){h}_{q}(x)\right|_{a}^{b}-I^{\prime},

(59)

with

\displaystyle I^{\prime}\equiv\int_{a}^{b}{h}_{q}(x)p^{\prime}(x)dx;

(60)

\displaystyle h_{q}(x)=\int\frac{1}{q(x)}dx.

2.

\tilde{q}(x)
: other
\tilde{q}(x)
close to
q(x)
can be used if the integral of its inverse
\tilde{h}(x)
is known and the following integration by part can be used,

\displaystyle I_{0}=\left.\tilde{p}(x)\tilde{h}_{q}(x)\right|_{a}^{b}-\tilde{I}^{\prime},

(61)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{I}^{\prime}=\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{h}_{q}(x)\left[\tilde{q}(x)f(x)\right]^{\prime}dx

(62)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{q}(x)=\int\frac{1}{\tilde{q}(x)}dx.

3.

\tilde{q}(x)=x^{\gamma}
: it is equivalent to the integration by part method shown in Eq. (
16
). In particular, when
\gamma=0
and
h_{\gamma}(x)=x
, the following is obtained,

\displaystyle I_{0}=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left\{\left.xf(x)\right|_{a}^{b}-I_{\gamma}^{\prime}\right\},

(63)

with

\displaystyle I_{\gamma}^{\prime}\equiv\int_{a}^{b}x^{1-\gamma}\left[x^{\gamma}f(x)\right]^{\prime}dx.

(64)

In particular, when
\gamma=0
,

\displaystyle I_{0}=\left.xf(x)\right|_{a}^{b}-I_{0}^{\prime},

(65)

with

\displaystyle I_{0}^{\prime}\equiv\int_{a}^{b}xf^{\prime}(x)dx.

(66)

In practice, which one of the above integration by part is used depends on the following criteria.

(a)

The analytical formula of
q(x)
or
\tilde{q}(x)
and integrals of its inverse
h_{q}(x)
or
\tilde{h}_{q}
are known.

(b)

The integral
I^{\prime}
,
\tilde{I}^{\prime}
or
I_{\gamma}^{\prime}
should be non-singular and can be numerically computed efficiently.

VI-A

Error Analysis

Now let’s perform the error analysis of Eq. (
43
). Without loss of generality, assuming that
p^{\prime}(x)=0
and the following is obtained,

\displaystyle I_{0}=\tilde{I}_{b}-\left.o(x)\tilde{h}(x)\tilde{q}(x)f(x)\right|_{a}^{b}-I_{o}.

with

\displaystyle I_{o}\equiv\int_{a}^{b}f_{o}(x)dx,

(67)

\displaystyle f_{o}(x)\equiv o(x)\left[\tilde{h}(x)\tilde{q}(x)f(x)\right]^{\prime},\ \ o(x)=-\ln\left(\frac{\tilde{q}(x)}{q(x)}\right).

It can be seen that
o(x)\sim 0
when
\tilde{q}(x)\sim q(x)
. Let’s assume
q(x)\sim x^{\gamma}
and
\tilde{q}(x)\sim x^{\tilde{\gamma}}
, from which the following is obtained,

\displaystyle o(x)=-(\tilde{\gamma}-\gamma)\ln(x)=-\delta_{\gamma}\ln(x),

(68)

where
\delta_{\gamma}\equiv\tilde{\gamma}-\gamma
and
I_{o}
of Eq. (
67
) becomes

\displaystyle I_{o}=\int_{a}^{b}o(x)\left[\tilde{h}(x)\tilde{q}(x)f(x)\right]^{\prime}dx

(69)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\gamma}\frac{1-\gamma}{1-\tilde{\gamma}}\int_{a}^{b}(-\ln(x))f(x)dx

\displaystyle\delta_{\gamma}\frac{(1-\gamma)}{1-\tilde{\gamma}}p(0)\int_{a}^{b}\frac{-\ln(x)}{x^{\gamma}}dx=c\mathcal{O}(\delta_{\gamma}),

with

\displaystyle c=\frac{p(0)}{(1-\tilde{\gamma})(1-\gamma)}\left.\left\{[1-(1-\gamma)\ln(x)]x^{1-\gamma}\right\}\right|_{a}^{b}.

(70)

Compared to the original integral
I_{0}
, the following relative accuracy is obtained,

\displaystyle\epsilon=\frac{I_{o}}{I_{0}}=\frac{\delta\gamma}{(1-\tilde{\gamma})}\frac{\left.\left\{[1-(1-\gamma)\ln(x)]x^{1-\gamma}\right\}\right|_{a}^{b}}{\left.x^{1-\gamma}\right|_{a}^{b}}

(71)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\gamma}{(1-\tilde{\gamma})}\frac{[1-(1-\gamma)\ln(b)]b^{1-\gamma}}{b^{1-\gamma}-a^{1-\gamma}},

where
a\geq 0
has been assumed.

In particular, when
b\leq 1
, Eq. (
71
) reduces to

\displaystyle\epsilon\leq\frac{\delta\gamma}{(1-\tilde{\gamma})}\frac{1}{b^{1-\gamma}-a^{1-\gamma}}\leq\frac{\delta\gamma}{(1-\tilde{\gamma})}\sim\mathcal{O}(\delta\gamma).

VI-B

The NSI-IBP Algorithm

Now let’s present the numerical singular integral algorithm via the integration by part methods.

For a given singular or nearly singular integral, the following numerical recipe can be used.

1.

If the analytical formulas of
q(x)
and the integral of its inverse
h(x)
is known, then the integration by part method of Eq. (
59
) can be used. Otherwise, go to the next step.

2.

Find an approximate function
\tilde{q}(x)
that can closely fits
q(x)
. Also, the analytical formula of the integral of its inverse
\tilde{h}(x)
should be known. Then the integration by part method of Eq. (
61
) can be used. Otherwise, go to the next step.

3.

Find
\tilde{q}(x)=x^{\gamma}
so that the original integrand
f(x)
can be effectively suppressed at its singularity location,
i
.
e
.,
x_{k}^{\gamma}f(x_{k})=0
. Then, Eq. (
63
) can be used. If the integrand of
I_{\gamma}^{\prime}
is non singular and
I_{\gamma}^{\prime}
can be computed numerically, the algorithm stops. Otherwise, go to the next step.

4.

Choose
\gamma=0
and
\tilde{q}(x)=1
, so that Eq. (
65
) can be used.

5.

Repeat Step 4) until
I_{0}^{\prime}
in Eq. (
66
) can be computed numerically, for example, when
\gamma\leq 1
and is known.

IX
Conclusion

In this paper, a general framework to compute the singular or nearly singular integrals via the integration by part, or NSI-IBP method, has been presented. A general integration by part formula has been shown to be able to recover all well-known IBP methods. The NSI-IBP method doesn’t even need to know the exact forms of the singular or nearly singular integrands. By choosing appropriate singular or nearly singular function with known analytical integral, the singular and nearly singular integral can be transformed to non-singular integral, plus the boundary values. To make the NSI-IBP method more computation friendly, appropriate singular or nearly singular function with known analytical integral has to be chosen. The thumb of rule is to find the approximate function with known analytical integral that best fits the singular integrand. Numerical experiments have been carried out on various singular integrals such as the power-law decaying function, the logarithmic function and their hybrid products, which shows that the relative accuracy can be as good as
10^{-15}
when the exact singular function is known and still works well even when the exact singular function is not known. What’s more, nearly singular integrals are numerically evaluated for both the electrostatic applications, as well as Computational Electromagnetics (CEM) involving the singular scalar Green’s function on the RWG triangle pair, which shows that the NSI-IBP method is very promising in many important applications involving singular integrands, even its exact form is not known. Finally, although 1D scenario is used to present the method, higher-dimensional singular integrals can be dealt with the NSI-IBP method because they can be reduced to many 1D integrals.

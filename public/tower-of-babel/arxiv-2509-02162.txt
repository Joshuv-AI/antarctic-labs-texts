Abstract
. The symmetric decreasing rearrangement of functions on ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} features in several seminal inequalities, such as the Pólya-Szegő inequality. The latter was shown by the authors [ 6 ] to hold for all smoothing rearrangements, a class that includes the more general ( k , n ) (k,n) -Steiner rearrangement, as well as others introduced by Brock and by Solynin. The theory of rearrangements and their associated set maps is developed, with an emphasis on approximation, particularly by polarizations. The Pólya-Szegő inequality holds with equality for polarizations, so is proved relatively easily for rearrangements that can be suitably approximated by them. One goal here is to show that the Brock rearrangements cannot be approximated in such a way. It turns out that under mild conditions, each set map associated with a rearrangement has in turn an associated contraction map from ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} to ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} . With this new analytical tool, several general results on the approximation of rearrangements are also proved.

Introduction

Steiner symmetrization, introduced by Jacob Steiner around 1836 in the course of his work on the isoperimetric inequality, is the forerunner of many similar processes. Schwarz symmetrization followed some fifty years later, and shortly afterwards, in 1899 according to Talenti in his remarkable survey
[
23

on rearrangements, Carlo Somigliana employed decreasing rearrangements of functions in hydrostatics. Nowadays there are many different types of symmetrization of sets and rearrangements of functions. They all share the general idea of replacing a set or a function by one which has more desirable or tractable properties, while preserving some important quantity. There are an astounding number of applications to diverse areas, both in mathematics and beyond. For more information and hints to the literature, we refer to the introductions to our previous papers

The focus here is on rearrangements. Suppose that

T:X\to{{\mathcal{V}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, where the first four properties are assumed to hold for all
f,g\in X
:

1.

(
Equimeasurable
)
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\{x:Tf(x)>t\})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\{x:f(x)>t\})

for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

2.

(
Monotonic
)
f\leq g
, essentially, implies
Tf\leq Tg
, essentially.

3.

(
L^{p}
-contracting
)
\|Tf-Tg\|_{p}\leq\|f-g\|_{p}
when
f-g\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

4.

(
Modulus of continuity reducing
) If
d>0
, we define the
modulus of continuity
of
f\in X
by

\omega_{d}(f)=\esssup_{\|x-y\|\leq d}|f(x)-f(y)|

and say that
T

reduces the modulus of continuity
if
\omega_{d}(Tf)\leq\omega_{d}(f)
for all
d>0
and
f\in X
.

5.

(
Smoothing
)  We say that
T
is
smoothing
if
{\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{L}^{n}
and the induced map
\diamondsuit_{T}
is smoothing, i.e.,

(\diamondsuit_{T}^{*}A)+dB^{n}\subset\diamondsuit_{T}^{*}(A+dB^{n})=\diamondsuit_{T}(A+dB^{n}),

essentially, for each
d>0
and
A\in\mathcal{B}^{n}
.

The map
T
is called a
rearrangement
if it is equimeasurable and monotonic.

Our approach to set maps and rearrangements of functions, including our definition of the term “smoothing,” differs from that in the groundbreaking works of Brock and Solynin
[
10
]
, Van Schaftingen
[
24
]
, and Van Schaftingen and Willhem
[
27
]
. A comparison of these differences in
[
5
, Appendix]
and
[
6
, Section 3]
shows that ultimately these approaches are equivalent.

Each rearrangement
T:{\mathcal{V}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to{\mathcal{V}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is
L^{p}
-contracting for
1\leq p<\infty
; see
[
6
, Proposition 3.7 and Appendix A]
for a proof in our setting. For the convenience of the reader, we now state, in a slightly different form, two results proved in
[
5
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X\subset{{\mathcal{V}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, let
{\mathcal{E}}=\{A\in{\mathcal{L}}^{n}:1_{A}\in X\}
, and let

be equimeasurable. The induced map
\diamondsuit_{T}:{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathcal{L}^{n}
given by (
3.3
) is measure preserving and satisfies

T1_{A}=1_{\diamondsuit_{T}A},

(3.4)

essentially, for all

.

Proof.

The result is stated for

in

, Lemma 4.5]
, but its proof extends to the slightly more general setting here.
∎

The following proposition is a special case of
[
5
, Lemma 4.8]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
T:{{\mathcal{V}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to{{\mathcal{V}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a rearrangement.

(i)
The map
\diamondsuit_{T}:\mathcal{L}^{n}\to\mathcal{L}^{n}
defined by (
3.3
) is monotonic.

(ii)
If
f\in{{\mathcal{V}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, then

\{x:Tf(x)\geq t\}=\diamondsuit_{T}\{x:f(x)\geq t\}\quad{\text{and}}\quad\{x:Tf(x)>t\}=\diamondsuit_{T}\{x:f(x)>t\},

essentially, for

. Moreover,

is essentially determined by

, since

Tf(x)=\max\left\{\sup\{t\in{\mathbb{Q}},\,t>\essinf f:x\in\diamondsuit_{T}\{z:f(z)\geq t\}\},\essinf f\right\},

(3.5)

essentially.

The following proposition addresses the extendability of a set map defined on the compact sets. We do not require the outer regularity assumed in earlier similar extensions, such as that of Brock and Solynin
[
10
, Section 3]
. In contrast,
[
5
, Example 6.8]
shows that the polarization set map
\diamondsuit_{P_{H}}
has more than one essentially different extension to from
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{n}
to
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\diamondsuit:{\mathcal{C}}^{n}\to\mathcal{L}^{n}
be monotonic and measure preserving. Then there is an essentially unique monotonic and measure-preserving map
\overline{\diamondsuit}:\mathcal{L}^{n}\to\mathcal{L}^{n}
such that
\overline{\diamondsuit}=\diamondsuit
on
{\mathcal{C}}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
. Since
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
is inner regular, there is an increasing sequence
(C_{k})
of sets in
{\mathcal{C}}^{n}
such that
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(A\setminus C_{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Define
\overline{\diamondsuit}A=\cup_{k}\diamondsuit C_{k}
. The continuity of
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
from below yields
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left(\overline{\diamondsuit}A\right)=\lim_{k}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(C_{k})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(A)
, so
\overline{\diamondsuit}
preserves measure and in particular
\overline{\diamondsuit}A\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
.

We claim that the definition of
\overline{\diamondsuit}
is essentially independent of the sequence
(C_{k})
. To see this, let
(C_{k}\!^{\prime})
be another sequence with the same properties, and let
\overline{\diamondsuit}\,^{\prime}A=\cup_{k}\diamondsuit C_{k}\!^{\prime}
. Let
D_{k}=C_{k}\cap C_{k}\!^{\prime}
. Then

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(A)-{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(D_{k})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(A\setminus D_{k})\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(A\setminus C_{k})+{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(A\setminus C_{k}\!^{\prime})\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. The monotonicity of
\diamondsuit
implies that
\diamondsuit D_{k}\subset\diamondsuit C_{k}
, essentially, so using the measure-preserving property of
\diamondsuit
, we obtain

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\diamondsuit C_{k}\setminus\diamondsuit D_{k})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\diamondsuit C_{k})-{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\diamondsuit D_{k})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(C_{k})-{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(D_{k})\to{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(A)-{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(A)=0,

as
k\to\infty
. Moreover,

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left(\overline{\diamondsuit}A\setminus\diamondsuit C_{k}\right)={\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left(\overline{\diamondsuit}A\right)-{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\diamondsuit C_{k})\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. Therefore

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left(\overline{\diamondsuit}A\setminus\overline{\diamondsuit}\,^{\prime}A\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left(\overline{\diamondsuit}A\setminus\diamondsuit D_{k}\right)\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left(\overline{\diamondsuit}A\setminus\diamondsuit C_{k}\right)+{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\diamondsuit C_{k}\setminus\diamondsuit D_{k})\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. Thus
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left(\overline{\diamondsuit}A\setminus\overline{\diamondsuit}\,^{\prime}A\right)=0
, and similarly
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left(\overline{\diamondsuit}\,^{\prime}A\setminus\overline{\diamondsuit}A\right)=0
, proving the claim.

It is easy to see that
\overline{\diamondsuit}
is monotonic.
∎

We end this section with some examples of smoothing set maps and rearrangements.

Example 3.4
.

Let
H
be a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Each of the following set maps can be defined as a monotonic, measure preserving, and smoothing map from
\mathcal{L}^{n}
to
\mathcal{L}^{n}
and gives rise to a smoothing rearrangement from
{\mathcal{V}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to
{\mathcal{V}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

(i) The reflection of a set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
in
H
is
R_{H}A=A^{\dagger}=\{x^{\dagger}:x\in A\}
, where
x^{\dagger}=2(x|H)-x
the reflection of
x
in
H
. The reflection
R_{H}f=f^{\dagger}
in
H
of a function
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is defined by
R_{H}f(x)=f^{\dagger}(x)=f(x^{\dagger})
.

(ii) When
H
is oriented, polarization of sets and functions was defined above; see (
1.2
) and (
1.1
). For background and references, see
[
5
, p. 3]
. Proofs of the smoothing property (recall that this is equivalent to reducing the modulus of continuity of functions) are given in
[
1
, Theorem 1.37]
and
[
10
, Lemma 5.1]
.

(iii) If
A\in{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
, the Steiner symmetral of
A
with respect to
H
is the set
S_{H}A
such that for each line
G
orthogonal to
H
and meeting
A
, the set
G\cap S_{H}A
is a (possibly degenerate) closed line segment with midpoint in
H
and length
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(G\cap A)
, if
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(G\cap A)<\infty
, and
G\cap S_{H}A=\emptyset
if
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(G\cap A)=\infty
or if
G\cap A
is not
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
-measurable. If instead
H
is an
(n-k)
-dimensional subspace in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the Schwarz or
(k,n)
-Steiner symmetral of
A
with respect to
H
is obtained in the same way, where
G
is now a
k
-dimensional plane orthogonal to
H
,
G\cap S_{H}A
is a (possibly degenerate)
k
-dimensional ball with center in
H
, and
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
is replaced by
{\mathcal{H}}^{k}
. For the definitions of the corresponding rearrangements of functions in
{{\mathcal{V}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we refer to
[
1
, Definition 6.2]
. The smoothing property is proved in
[
1
, Theorem 6.10]
and
[
10
, Corollary 6.1]
.

(iv) If
H
is a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, what we call Solynin set maps
\diamondsuit_{So_{H}}
and rearrangements
So_{H}
are
far-reaching extensions of a process discovered by McNabb
[
15
]
, in which a convex body is transformed continuously into its Steiner symmetral. In this special case, the action of
\diamondsuit_{So_{H}}
on a convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
can be visualized as follows. If
H
is horizontal and oriented with the
x_{n}
-axis, then any chord of
K
orthogonal to
H
is left unchanged if its midpoint lies above or on
H
, while if it lies below
H
, it is translated orthogonal to
H
so that its midpoint belongs to
H
. If
H
is imagined to be moving up vertically through
K
, then
\diamondsuit_{So_{H}}K
changes continuously from
K
itself to the Steiner symmetral
S_{H}K
. The first extension to non-convex sets was carried out by Solynin
[
21
]
, who called it continuous symmetrization. It achieved its full generalization in Solynin’s paper
[
22
]
, where it is analysed in detail and applied to integral inequalities. Solynin set maps and rearrangements with respect to
H
act fiber-wise on lines orthogonal to
H
, so it suffices to define them when
n=1
. In this case, if
H=\{t\}
, with the usual orientation of
{\mathbb{R}}
, and
A\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}
, then

\diamondsuit_{So_{\{t\}}A}=[t-r_{A},t+r_{A}]\cup(A\cap[t,\infty)),

(3.6)

where
r_{A}\geq 0
is such that
{{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}(So_{\{t\}}A)={{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}(A)
. Note that when
A\subset(t,\infty)
, so that
r_{A}=0
, we have
\diamondsuit_{So_{\{t\}}A}=\{t\}\cup A\neq A
, but in this case
\diamondsuit_{So_{\{t\}}A}=A
, essentially. Moreover, (
3.6
) essentially agrees with the description above when
A
is a convex body. Up to translation, the set
A
tranforms continuously into its Steiner symmetral
S_{\{o\}}A
with respect to
\{o\}
as
t
goes from
-\infty
to
\infty
. Compare
[
22
, (4.5) and (4.6)]
, which lead to the definitions

[
22
, Definitions 4.3 and 4.4]
. The smoothing property is proved in
[
22
, Lemma 4.4]
. An extension analogous to
(k,n)
-Steiner symmetrization is studied in
[
22
, Section 9]
.

(v) For our purposes, Brock set maps and rearrangements of functions depend on a parameter
0\leq b\leq 1
. For convex bodies, the set maps were introduced by Pólya and Szegő
[
17
, Note B]
(in collaboration with M. Shiffman), who used the term continuous symmetrization also adopted by Solynin. In this special case, if
H
is a horizontal hyperplane at height
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a convex body, its image under the Brock set map
\diamondsuit_{B_{H}}
with parameter
b
is obtained by translating orthogonal to
H
each vertical chord of
K
with midpoint at height
t
so that its midpoint is at height
(1-b)t_{0}+bt
. Thus
b=1
corresponds to the identity map and
b=0
to Steiner symmetrization with respect to
H
. The extension of
\diamondsuit_{B_{H}}
to a map on
\mathcal{L}^{n}
, leading to the definition of the Brock rearrangement
B_{H}
on functions in
{{\mathcal{V}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, requires considerable ingenuity. It is described and fully analysed by Brock
[
8
, Sections 2 and 3]
and
[
9
, Section 2]
(where the parametrization is different), and the smoothing property is proved in
[
9
, Remark 2.3]
. The extension itself was anticipated by Rogers
[
18
]
, who used it in his remarkable proof of the Brascamp-Lieb-Rogers inequality. The crucial step is to define
\diamondsuit_{B_{H}}A
when
n=1
and
A
is a finite union of line segments; see Step 1 in
[
9
, pp. 165–166]
and
[
18
, pp. 106–107]
.
∎

4.
Contractions associated with set maps

The following lemma was proved in
[
5
, Lemma 6.2]
when
t_{0}=0
and
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{n}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
. We shall need the slight generalization presented here in the proof of Theorem
7.5
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
H=u^{\perp}+t_{0}u
,
u\in S^{n-1}
,
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let
{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
be a class that contains all balls in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and suppose that
\diamondsuit:{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
is monotonic, respects
H
-cylinders, and maps balls to balls. Then there is a contraction (i.e., a Lipschitz function with Lipschitz constant
1
)
\varphi_{\diamondsuit}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\diamondsuit B(x+tu,r)=B\left(x+\varphi_{\diamondsuit}(t)u,r\right),

(4.1)

essentially, for
x\in u^{\perp}
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
r>0
.

Proof.

It is a simple matter to modify the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 6.2]
so that it applies when
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Apart from this, the only adjustment required is in the penultimate sentence, where
K
can be replaced by the ball
B_{1}
.
∎

The following result generalizes and extends Lemma
4.1
to maps not necessarily respecting
H
-cylinders.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
be a class that contains all balls in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and let
\diamondsuit:{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathcal{L}^{n}
be monotonic, measure preserving, and map balls to balls.

(i)
There are contractions
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\diamondsuit B(x,r)=B(\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x),r)

(4.2)

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r>0
. We have

\|\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x)-\psi_{\diamondsuit,r^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})\|\leq\max\{\|x-x^{\prime}\|,|r-r^{\prime}|\}.

(4.3)

for
x,x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r,r^{\prime}>0
.

(ii)
Let
H=u^{\perp}+t_{0}u
,
u\in S^{n-1}
,
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, be a hyperplane. If, in addition,
\diamondsuit
respects

H
-cylinders, then
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}
is independent of
r>0
, and

\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x)=x|u^{\perp}+\varphi_{\diamondsuit}(\langle x,u\rangle)u\quad{\text{and}}~~\quad\varphi_{\diamondsuit}(t)=\langle\psi_{\diamondsuit}(tu),u\rangle

(4.4)

for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where
\varphi_{\diamondsuit}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is the contraction associated with
\diamondsuit
from (
4.1
).

Proof.

(i) As
\diamondsuit
preserves measure and maps balls to balls, for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r>0
, there is a
z_{x,r}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\diamondsuit B(x,r)=B(z_{x,r},r)
. Define
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x)=z_{x,r}
.

Let
x,x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r,r^{\prime}>0
be given. If neither of the balls
B(x,r)
and
B(x^{\prime},r^{\prime})
is contained in the other, the smallest ball
B
that contains both has diameter
d=\|x-x^{\prime}\|+r+r^{\prime}
. Moreover,
\|x-x^{\prime}\|>|r-r^{\prime}|
.
Monotonicity and the measure-preserving property imply that

\diamondsuit B
is a ball of radius
d
that contains
\diamondsuit B(x,r)=B(\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x),r)
and
\diamondsuit B(x^{\prime},r^{\prime})=B(\psi_{\diamondsuit,r^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}),r^{\prime})
, so

\|\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x)-\psi_{\diamondsuit,r^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})\|+r+r^{\prime}\leq d=\|x-x^{\prime}\|+r+r^{\prime}.

If
B(x,r)
is contained in
B(x^{\prime},r^{\prime})
, say, the smallest ball
B
containing them both is
B(x^{\prime},r^{\prime})
, and
\|x-x^{\prime}\|\leq r^{\prime}-r
. Then
B(\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x),r)\subset B(\psi_{\diamondsuit,r^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}),r^{\prime})
and hence

\|\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x)-\psi_{\diamondsuit,r^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})\|\leq r^{\prime}-r
. Combining these two cases gives (
4.3
). Letting
r=r^{\prime}
in (
4.3
), we see that
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}
is a contraction.

(ii) If
\diamondsuit
also respects
H
-cylinders, then Lemma
4.1
gives

\displaystyle\diamondsuit B(y+tu,r)=B\big(y+\varphi_{\diamondsuit}(t)u,r\big)

for all
y\in u^{\perp}
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
r>0
. With (
4.2
) and
x=y+tu
, we obtain

B(\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x),r)=\diamondsuit B(x,r)=B\big(x|u^{\perp}+\varphi_{\diamondsuit}(\langle x,u\rangle)u,r\big)

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Hence,

\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x)=x|u^{\perp}+\varphi_{\diamondsuit}(\langle x,u\rangle)u

is independent of
r>0
and (
4.4
) holds.
∎

Without additional assumptions, the contractions
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}
from (

, as the following example shows.

Example 4.3
.

Let

be fixed and define
\diamondsuit:\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n}\to\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n}

\diamondsuit K=B(ru,r),

where

. ∎

The next lemma gives some conditions guaranteeing that

.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{n}\subset{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
, let
\diamondsuit:{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathcal{L}^{n}
be monotonic, measure preserving, and map balls to balls, and let
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be the associated contraction from (
4.2
).

(i)
If
\diamondsuit
is smoothing, then
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}
is independent of
r>0
.

(ii)
Let
H=u^{\perp}+t_{0}u
,
u\in S^{n-1}
,
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, be a hyperplane. If there is an
r_{0}>0
such that the restriction of
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r_{0}}
to

is the identity on
H
, then
\diamondsuit\big|_{\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n}}
respects
H
-cylinders and hence
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}
is independent of
r>0
.

Proof.

(i) Suppose that
\diamondsuit
is monotonic, measure preserving, and smoothing. (The assumption that
\diamondsuit
maps balls to balls is superfluous here, since it is guaranteed by
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
.) If
0<r<d
, the smoothing property and (

) yield

\mathrm{int}B(\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x),d)=(\diamondsuit^{*}B(x,r))+(d-r)B^{n}\subset\diamondsuit^{*}B(x,d)=\mathrm{int}B(\psi_{\diamondsuit,d}(x),d)

and hence
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x)=\psi_{\diamondsuit,d}(x)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Consequently,
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}
does not depend on
r
.

(ii) Assume that
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r_{0}}
acts as the identity on
H
for some
r_{0}>0
. We claim that (
4.2
) holds with
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}=\psi_{\diamondsuit,r_{0}}
for all
x\in H
and
r>r_{0}
.
Indeed, for
r=r_{0}+\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon>0
, the ball
B(x,r)
,
x\in H
, contains the set

D=\bigcup_{z\in x+(\varepsilon S^{n-1}\cap H)}B(z,r_{0}),

so monotonicity implies that
the ball
\diamondsuit B(x,r)=B(\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x),r)
contains

\bigcup_{z\in x+(\varepsilon S^{n-1}\cap H)}\diamondsuit B(z,r_{0})=D.

It follows that
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}(x)=x=\psi_{\diamondsuit,r_{0}}(x)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n}
, and for
x\in H
, let
r_{K,x}=\min\{r\geq r_{0}:K\subset B(x,r)\}
. Note that if
y\not\in(K|u^{\perp})+{\mathrm{span}}\,\{u\}
, we can choose an

and an

such that

and hence
y\not\in B(x,r_{K,x})
. Therefore

K\subset L=\bigcap_{x\in H}B(x,r_{K,x})\subset(K|u^{\perp})+{\mathrm{span}}\,\{u\},

where
L\in\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n}
, and monotonicity shows that
\diamondsuit
respects
H
-cylinders. Then Lemma
4.2
(ii) implies that
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}
is independent of
r>0
.
∎

When
\psi_{\diamondsuit,r}
is independent of
r>0
, we shall henceforth write
\psi_{\diamondsuit}
instead. In the following example, we give explicit formulas for the contractions associated with the set maps from Example

. In the formulas, we use the first equation in (

) and the fact that
x=x|u^{\perp}+\langle x,u\rangle u
for

and
u\in S^{n-1}
.

Example 4.5
.

Let
H=u^{\perp}+t_{0}u
,
u\in S^{n-1}
,

A

Lemma 4.7
.

Let

be a body in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, and let

,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be contractions. The following are equivalent.

(i)

(\psi_{k})
converges pointwise on
C
.

(ii)

(\psi_{k})
converges uniformly on
C
.

(iii)

(\psi_{k})
converges in
L^{1}
on
C
.

Proof.

Suppose that (i) holds, i.e.,
(\psi_{k})
converges pointwise to a function
\psi
on
C
. It is easy to see that
\psi
is a contraction on
C
. Let
r>0
and
\varepsilon>0
be given and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in C
be such that the open balls
{\mathrm{int}}\,B(x_{i},r)
,
i=1,\dots,m
, cover
C
. Let
x\in C
and choose
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
such that
x\in B(x_{i},r)
. Since
\psi
and
\psi_{k}
are contractions, we have
\|\psi(x)-\psi(x_{i})\|\leq\varepsilon
and
\|\psi_{k}(x)-\psi_{k}(x_{i})\|\leq\varepsilon
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\psi_{k}(x)-\psi(x)\|\leq\|\psi_{k}(x)-\psi_{k}(x_{i})\|+\|\psi_{k}(x_{i})-\psi(x_{i})\|+\|\psi(x_{i})-\psi(x)\|\leq 3\varepsilon,

for all
k\geq k_{0}
, where
k_{0}
can be chosen independently of
i
and
x
. This implies (ii).

If (ii) holds, then

\|\psi_{k}-\psi\|_{C,1}=\int_{C}\|\psi_{k}(x)-\psi(x)\|\,dx\leq\|\psi_{k}-\psi\|_{C,\infty}\,{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(C)\to 0

as
k\to\infty
, proving (iii).

Finally, suppose that (iii) holds. If (i) is false, there is an
x\in C
and an
\varepsilon>0
such that
\|\psi_{k_{j}}(x)-\psi(x)\|>\varepsilon
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It is well known (see, for example,
[
14
, Theorem 2.7]
) that since
\|\psi_{k_{j}}-\psi\|_{C,1}\to 0
as

, there is a subsequence
(\psi_{k_{j_{m}}})
of
(\psi_{k_{j}})
that converges to
\psi
almost everywhere on
C
. Since
C
is a body, we can choose
y\in C
with
\|x-y\|\leq\varepsilon/3
such that
\psi_{k_{j_{m}}}(y)\to\psi(y)
as
m\to\infty
. Since
\psi
and
\psi_{k_{j_{m}}}
are contractions, we have
\|\psi(x)-\psi(y)\|\leq\varepsilon/3
and
\|\psi_{k_{j_{m}}}(x)-\psi_{k_{j_{m}}}(y)\|\leq\varepsilon/3
for all

. Then

\displaystyle\|\psi_{k_{j_{m}}}(x)-\psi(x)\|\leq\|\psi_{k_{j_{m}}}(x)-\psi_{k_{j_{m}}}(y)\|+\|\psi_{k_{j_{m}}}(y)-\psi(y)\|+\|\psi(y)-\psi(x)\|<\varepsilon

for sufficiently large

The main interest here is in approximation by polarizations and we begin the discussion with a couple of simple remarks. Firstly, (weak) sequential approximability by polarizations is the same as (weak) sequential approximability by finite compositions of polarizations, so we only use the former simpler terms. However, with the other forms of approximation, the finite compositions of polarizations, and not just the polarizations, is the appropriate class to consider. For example, if
{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}^{n}
and
\diamondsuit:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
is weakly approximable by polarizations, then for each
A\in{{\mathcal{E}}}
, either
\diamondsuit A=A
, essentially, or
\diamondsuit A=P_{H_{A}}A
, essentially, for some hyperplane
H_{A}
depending on
A
. We omit the easy proof. It follows that if
\diamondsuit
is approximable by polarizations, then
\diamondsuit
is essentially either the identity or a polarization itself.

Van Schaftingen
[
26
, Theorem 1 and Section 4.3]
proved that Steiner and Schwarz rearrangements are sequentially approximable in
L^{p}
on
L^{p}_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by polarizations. By Theorem

, the same is true for the associated set maps on
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
. In contrast, Theorem
5.2
and the following lemma show that Brock and Solynin rearrangements are not sequentially approximable in

on

by polarizations.

\diamondsuit_{k}\circ\dots\circ\diamondsuit_{2}

.

Since translations and Brock set maps with

of

, this remains true with
{\mathcal{B}}^{n}
replaced by
\mathcal{L}^{n}
.

Solynin
[
22
]
proved that the Solynin rearrangement
So_{H}
is weakly approximable in
L^{p}
on
L^{p}_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by finite compositions of polarizations. By Theorem
5.2
, the same is true for the associated set map on
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
. In Theorem
8.4
below, we show that “weakly approximable” can be replaced by “approximable” in these results. We do not know if it can be replaced by “weakly sequentially approximable”; see Problem
9.5
.

We do not know if a map
\diamondsuit:{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathcal{L}^{n}
which is approximable by finite compositions of polarizations is also weakly sequentially approximable by polarizations, or if the converse is true; see Problem
9.3
.

6.
Approximation by polarizations with respect to parallel hyperplanes

Recall that if
{\mathcal{E}}\subset\mathcal{L}^{n}
, then
\mathcal{J}({\mathcal{E}})
denotes the family of monotonic and measure-preserving maps from
{\mathcal{E}}
to
\mathcal{L}^{n}
that map balls to balls, and if
H
is a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then
\mathcal{J}_{H}({\mathcal{E}})\subset\mathcal{J}({\mathcal{E}})
is the subfamily that respect

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
, let
H=u^{\perp}+t_{0}u
,
u\in S^{n-1}
,
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let
\diamondsuit_{k}\in\mathcal{J}_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n})
,
k=0,1,\dots
, and let
\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{k}}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
k=0,1,\dots
, be their associated contractions from (
4.1
).

(i)
If
\diamondsuit_{k}\to\diamondsuit_{0}
as
k\to\infty
, then
\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{k}}\to\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{0}}
pointwise as
k\to\infty
.

(ii)
If
\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{k}}\to\varphi
pointwise as
k\to\infty
, there is an essentially unique
\diamondsuit\in\mathcal{J}_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n})
, with associated contraction
\varphi
from (
4.1
), such that
\diamondsuit_{k}\to\diamondsuit
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

To show (i), let
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and use Lemma
4.1
and the
L^{1}
-convergence of
\diamondsuit_{k}
to
\diamondsuit
to conclude that

B(\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{k}}(t)u,1)=\diamondsuit_{k}B(tu,1)\to\diamondsuit B(tu,1)=B(\varphi_{\diamondsuit}(t)u,1),

as
k\to\infty
, implying that
\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{k}}\to\varphi_{\diamondsuit}
pointwise as
k\to\infty
.

To prove (ii), assume that
\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{k}}\to\varphi
pointwise as
k\to\infty
. In view of Lemma
4.7
, this convergence holds uniformly on bounded intervals. It is easy to see that
\varphi
is a contraction. The map
\diamondsuit
defined via (
4.7
) has all the required properties apart from the claimed convergence.

To prove this, let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n}
, let
\varepsilon>0
, and let
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be an interval such that
\{t_{x}:x\in K|H\}\subset I
, where
t_{x}
is the midpoint of
K\cap(H^{\perp}+x)
for each
x\in H
. The uniform convergence of
\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{k}}
to
\varphi
on
I
yields a
k_{0}
such that
|\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{k}}(t)-\varphi(t)|\leq\varepsilon
for all

and

. It is evident from (
4.7
) applied to
\diamondsuit
and to
\diamondsuit_{k}
,
k\geq k_{0}
, that

\diamondsuit_{k}K\subset(\diamondsuit K)+\varepsilon[-u,u]~~\quad{\text{and}}~~\quad\diamondsuit K\subset(\diamondsuit_{k}K)+\varepsilon[-u,u]

essentially, for
k\geq k_{0}
. These inclusions and Fubini’s theorem imply that
\diamondsuit_{k}K\to\diamondsuit K
as
k\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
H=e_{n}^{\perp}
, let
s_{1}<s_{2}
, and let
K
be any convex body in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that

[s_{1},s_{2}]\subset\{t_{x}:x\in K|H\},

(6.1)

where
x+t_{x}e_{n}
is the midpoint of the line segment
K\cap(H^{\perp}+x)
. Let
\diamondsuit\in\mathcal{J}_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n})
and suppose that there are
\diamondsuit_{K,k}\in\mathcal{J}_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n})
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that

\|1_{\diamondsuit_{K,k}K}(x)-1_{\diamondsuit K}(x)\|_{1}\to 0

(6.2)

as
k\to\infty
. Then the associated contractions
\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{K,k}}
from (
4.1
) converge uniformly to
\varphi_{\diamondsuit}
on
[s_{1},s_{2}]
.

Proof.

Suppose that
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{n}
satisfies (
6.1
) and that there are
\diamondsuit_{K,k}\in\mathcal{J}_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n})
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, satisfying (
6.2
). Then

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|1_{\diamondsuit_{K,k}K}(x)-1_{\diamondsuit K}(x)|\,dx\to 0

and hence
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left((\diamondsuit_{K,k}K)\,\triangle\,\diamondsuit K\right)\to 0
as

. By Fubini’s theorem,

{\mathcal{H}}^{1}\left(((\diamondsuit_{K,k}K)\cap(H^{\perp}+x))\,\triangle\,((\diamondsuit K)\cap(H^{\perp}+x))\right)\to 0,

(6.3)

as
k\to\infty
, for
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}
-almost all

. By (
4.7
), we have

(\diamondsuit_{K,k}K)\cap(H^{\perp}+x)=K\cap(H^{\perp}+x)+(\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{K,k}}(t_{x})-t_{x})e_{n}

(6.4)

for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

(\diamondsuit K)\cap(H^{\perp}+x)=K\cap(H^{\perp}+x)+(\varphi_{\diamondsuit}(t_{x})-t_{x})e_{n},

(6.5)

up to sets of
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
-measure zero, for
{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}
-almost all

. From (
6.1
), (
6.3
), (
6.4
), and (
6.5
), we obtain
\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{K,k}}(s)\to\varphi_{\diamondsuit}(s)
for
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
-almost all

. Since
\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{K,k}}
,

, and

are contractions, the convergence holds for all

∎

An example of a convex body satisfying (

) (with equality) is the parallelepiped

for which

.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
H
be a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and let
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{J}_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n})
. A map
\diamondsuit\in\mathcal{J}_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n})
is approximable on
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{n}
by maps in
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
if and only if it is weakly approximable on
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{n}
by maps in
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\diamondsuit\in\mathcal{J}_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n})
is weakly approximable on
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{n}
by maps in
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
. Let
K_{m}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma
6.2
with
s_{1}=-m
and
s_{2}=m
. Then there are
\diamondsuit_{K_{m},k}\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that (
6.2
) holds with
K
replaced by
K_{m}
. By Lemma
6.2
,
\varphi_{K_{m},k}\to\varphi
uniformly on
[m,m]
as
k\to\infty
. Hence, there is a
k_{m}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
|\varphi_{K_{m},k}(x)-\varphi(x)|\leq 1/m
for all
x\in[-m,m]
and
k\geq k_{m}
. It follows that the maps
\varphi_{m}=\varphi_{K_{m},k_{m}}\to\varphi
pointwise on
{\mathbb{R}}
as
m\to\infty
. Lemma
6.1
(ii) now implies that
\diamondsuit_{m}=\diamondsuit_{K_{m},k_{m}}\to\diamondsuit
as
m\to\infty
, so
\diamondsuit
is approximable on
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{n}
by maps in
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
. The converse is obvious.
∎

We shall write
I(a,b)
for the open interval in
{\mathbb{R}}
with endpoints
a
and
b
, where
a\neq b
, and
I(a,a)=\emptyset
. Let
\mathcal{I}
be the class of contractions
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that whenever
a<b
and
\varphi((a,b))\subset I(\varphi(a),\varphi(b))
, the restriction
\varphi|_{(a,b)}
is an affine function on
(a,b)
with slope
\pm 1
. More formally,
\varphi\in\mathcal{I}
if whenever
a<b
and

\varphi(s)\in I(\varphi(a),\varphi(b))\ \ \ \forall s\in(a,b),

(6.6)

there is a
\delta\in\{-1,1\}
such that

\varphi(s)=\delta(s-a)+\varphi(a)\ \ \ \forall s\in(a,b).

Since
\varphi
is a contraction, the existence of
\delta\in\{-1,1\}
such that the last displayed formula holds is equivalent to

|\varphi(b)-\varphi(a)|=b-a.

A contraction
\varphi
may be piecewise linear, with each piece having slope
\pm 1
, and yet not belong to
\mathcal{I}
. For example, suppose that
\varphi(t)=t
, if
t<0
,
\varphi(t)=-t
, if
0\leq t\leq 1
, and
\varphi(t)=t-2
, if
t>1
. Then

-2=\varphi(-2)<\varphi(s)<\varphi(3)=1

for
-2<s<3
, but
\varphi
is not affine on
(-2,3)
.

A class of real-valued functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
is called
bi-reflection invariant
if it contains
-\varphi
and
s\mapsto\varphi(-s)
whenever
\varphi
is in this class. It is called

bi-translation invariant
if it contains
\varphi(\cdot+s_{0})
and
\varphi(\cdot)+s_{0}
for all
s_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
whenever
\varphi
is in this class.

Lemma 6.4
.

The family
\mathcal{I}
has the following properties.

(i)

{\rm{Id}}\in\mathcal{I}
,
(t\mapsto|t|)\in\mathcal{I}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{I}
is bi-reflection invariant.

(iii)

\mathcal{I}
is bi-translation invariant.

(iv)

\mathcal{I}
is closed under composition.

(v)

\mathcal{I}
is closed with respect to pointwise convergence.

Proof.

(i), (ii) and (iii) are obvious.

To show (iv), let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{I}
and let
\varphi=\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}
. Suppose that
a<b
are such that (
6.6
) holds. For brevity, we define
\tilde{a}=\varphi_{1}(a)
and
\tilde{b}=\varphi_{1}(b)
. If
r\in I(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
, then by the intermediate value theorem applied to
\varphi_{1}
, there is an
s\in(a,b)
such that
\varphi_{1}(s)=r
and hence
\varphi_{2}(r)=\varphi_{2}(\varphi_{1}(s))\in I(\varphi_{2}(\tilde{a}),\varphi_{2}(\tilde{b}))
. Since
\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{I}
, this shows that
\varphi_{2}(s)=\delta s+c

for all
s
in the closure of
I(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
, where
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\delta\in\{-1,1\}
.

Suppose that there is an
s_{1}\in(a,b)
such that
\varphi_{1}(s_{1})\not\in I(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
. Then by the intermediate value theorem again, there is an
s_{2}\in(a,b)
with
\varphi_{1}(s_{2})\in\{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\}
. We only consider the case when
\varphi_{1}(s_{2})=\tilde{a}
, as the case
\varphi_{1}(s_{2})=\tilde{b}
is treated in a very similar way. We obtain

\varphi(s_{2})=\delta\varphi_{1}(s_{2})+c=\delta\varphi_{1}(a)+c=\varphi(a)\not\in I(\varphi(a),\varphi(b)),

contradicting (
6.6
). Thus (
6.6
) holds with
\varphi
replaced by
\varphi_{1}
. Since
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{I}
, its restriction to
(a,b)
must be an affine function with slope
\pm 1
. The composition of two such functions is again an affine function with slope
\pm 1
and (iv) follows.

To prove (v), let
(\varphi_{k})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{I}
with pointwise limit
\varphi
. Clearly
\varphi
is a contraction. Consider
a<b
such that (
6.6
) holds. We may assume without loss of generality that
\varphi(a)<\varphi(b)
. For
\varepsilon\in(0,(\varphi(b)-\varphi(a))/4)
, let
I_{\varepsilon}=(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})
, where
a<a_{\varepsilon}<b_{\varepsilon}<b
are such that
\phi(a_{\varepsilon})=\phi(a)+2\varepsilon
,
\phi(b_{\varepsilon})=\phi(b)-2\varepsilon
, and
\phi(s)\in(\phi(a)+2\varepsilon,\phi(b)-2\varepsilon)
for all
s\in I_{\varepsilon}
. By Lemma
4.7
, there is a
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

|\varphi_{k}(s)-\varphi(s)|\leq\varepsilon

for all
k\geq k_{0}
and
s\in(a,b)
. Suppose that
k\geq k_{0}
. Then for
s\in I_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\varphi_{k}(s)-\varphi_{k}(a)\geq(\varphi(s)-\varepsilon)-(\varphi(a)+\varepsilon)=\varphi(s)-(\varphi(a)+2\varepsilon)>0

and similarly
\varphi_{k}(b)-\varphi_{k}(s)>0
, so
\varphi_{k}:I_{\varepsilon}\to(\varphi_{k}(a),\varphi_{k}(b))
. Let

a_{k}=\max\{s\in[a,a_{\varepsilon}]:\varphi_{k}(s)=\varphi_{k}(a)\}~~\quad{\text{and}}~~\quad b_{k}=\min\{s\in[b_{\varepsilon},b]:\varphi_{k}(s)=\varphi_{k}(b)\}).

Then
a_{k}\in(a,a_{\varepsilon})
,
b_{k}\in(b_{\varepsilon},b)
, and

\varphi_{k}:(a_{k},b_{k})\to(\varphi_{k}(a_{k}),\varphi_{k}(b_{k}))
. Since
\varphi_{k}\in\mathcal{I}
,
\varphi_{k}
is affine with slope
1
on
(a_{k},b_{k})
and hence also on the smaller interval
I_{\varepsilon}
.
Therefore
\varphi
is also affine with slope
1
on
I_{\varepsilon}
. As
a_{\varepsilon}\to a
and
b_{\varepsilon}\to b
as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
\varphi
is affine with slope
1
on
[a,b]
, so
\varphi\in\mathcal{I}
.
∎

Recall the formula for
\varphi_{P_{H}}
for a polarization
P_{H}
, given in Example
4.5
(ii). This and parts (i)–(iv) of the previous lemma immediately yield the following result.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
H
be a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. If
\diamondsuit
is a finite composition of polarizations with respect to hyperplanes parallel to
H
, then
\varphi_{\diamondsuit}\in\mathcal{I}
.

The family
\mathcal{I}
is not closed under taking maxima and minima. For example, if
f_{1}(s)=\max\{0,s\}
and
f_{2}(s)=-\max\{0,-s\}+1
, then
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{I}
but
\max\{f_{1},f_{2}\}\not\in\mathcal{I}
since it is constant on
(0,1)
and strictly increasing on
(-1,0)\cup(1,\infty)
.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
H
be a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and let
\diamondsuit\in\mathcal{J}_{H}(\mathcal{K}_{n}^{n})
. If
\varphi_{\diamondsuit}\not\in\mathcal{I}
, then
\diamondsuit
cannot be weakly approximated on
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{n}
by finite compositions of polarizations with respect to hyperplanes parallel to
H
.

Proof.

If
\diamondsuit
can be weakly approximated on
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{n}
by finite compositions of polarizations with respect to hyperplanes parallel to
H
, then by Lemma
6.3
, there are such maps
\diamondsuit_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
\diamondsuit_{k}\to\diamondsuit
as
k\to\infty
. By Lemma
6.1
(i),
\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{k}}\to\varphi_{\diamondsuit}
pointwise as
k\to\infty
. The associated contractions
\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{k}}
from (
4.1
) belong to
\mathcal{I}
by Corollary
6.5
, so by Lemma
6.4
(v),
\varphi_{\diamondsuit}\in\mathcal{I}
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a contraction such that there exists a point where
\varphi^{\prime}
exists and is different from 0, 1, and -1. Then
\varphi\not\in\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\varphi^{\prime}(s_{0})
exists and is different from 0, 1, and -1.
Since
\varphi
is a contraction, we have
-1<\varphi^{\prime}(s_{0})<0
or
0<\varphi^{\prime}(s_{0})<1
. We shall assume the latter holds, since the former can be dealt with similarly. Then there is an
a>0
satisfying

a<\min\{\varphi^{\prime}(s_{0}),1-\varphi^{\prime}(s_{0})\}

and
r_{1}<s_{0}<r_{2}
such that on
[r_{1},r_{2}]
, the graph of
\varphi
lies between the lines
t=f_{\pm}(s)
through
(s_{0},\varphi(s_{0}))
with slopes
\varphi^{\prime}(s_{0})\pm a
. Let

s_{1}=\sup\{r\in[r_{1},r_{2}]:\varphi(r)=\min\{\varphi(s):r_{1}\leq s\leq r_{2}\}\}

and

s_{2}=\inf\{r\in[r_{1},r_{2}]:\varphi(r)=\max\{\varphi(s):r_{1}\leq s\leq r_{2}\}\}.

Then
\varphi(s_{1})<\varphi(s)<\varphi(s_{2})
for all
s\in(s_{1},s_{2})
. We also have

f_{+}(s_{1})\leq\varphi(s_{1})\leq f_{-}(s_{1})~~\quad{\text{and}}~~\quad f_{-}(s_{2})\leq\varphi(s_{2})\leq f_{+}(s_{2}),

so

0<\varphi^{\prime}(s_{0})-a\leq\frac{f_{-}(s_{2})-f_{-}(s_{1})}{s_{2}-s_{1}}\leq\frac{\varphi(s_{2})-\varphi(s_{1})}{s_{2}-s_{1}}\leq\frac{f_{+}(s_{2})-f_{+}(s_{1})}{s_{2}-s_{1}}=\varphi^{\prime}(s_{0})+a<1.

This shows that
\varphi
is not affine with slope 1 on
(s_{1},s_{2})
.
∎

.

If

, the Brock set map

with parameter

cannot be weakly approximated on

Since
0<\varphi_{\diamondsuit_{B_{H}}}^{\prime}(s)=b<1
, this follows immediately from Theorem
6.6
and Lemma

.
∎

7.
Approximation by arbitrary polarizations

In the previous section, we focused on contractions
\varphi
associated with maps that respect
H
-cylinders. Here we consider the contractions
\psi
introduced in Lemmas
4.2
and
4.4
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a contraction that can be approximated (pointwise) on
B^{n}
by finite compositions of contractions associated with polarizations. If
\psi|_{B^{n}}
is injective, then
{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(\psi(B^{n}))=\kappa_{n}={{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(B^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
P_{0}
be a polarization with respect to an oriented hyperplane
H
and with associated contraction
\psi_{0}
. We shall use the fact that if
B=B(x,r)
is a ball, then by (
4.5
), we have
\psi_{0}(B)=B\cap H^{+}
if
x\in H^{+}
and
\psi_{0}(B)=(R_{H}B)\cap H^{+}
if
x\in H^{-}
, where
R_{H}
denotes reflection in
H
.

Since
\psi
is a contraction,
{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(\psi(B^{n}))\leq\kappa_{n}
. Suppose that
{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(\psi(B^{n}))<(1-\delta)^{n}\kappa_{n}
for some
\delta>0
. We have to show that
\psi
is not injective on
B^{n}
. Let

\psi_{k}=\psi_{k,m_{k}}\circ\dots\circ\psi_{k,1},

k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be finite compositions of contractions
\psi_{k,i}
associated with polarizations
P_{k,i}
taken with respect to oriented hyperplanes
H_{k,i}
,
i=1,\dots,m_{k}
, with the property that
\psi_{k}\to\psi
pointwise as
k\to\infty
.
For each
i=1,\dots,m_{k}
, let
x_{k,i}=(\psi_{k,i}\circ\dots\circ\psi_{k,1})(o)
and
x_{k,0}=o
for each
k
. Note that

(\psi_{k,i}\circ\dots\circ\psi_{k,1})(B^{n})\subset B(x_{k,i},1),

as all mappings are contractions. If
H_{k,i}
,
i=1,\dots,m_{k}
, does not meet
{\mathrm{int}}\,B(x_{k,i-1},1-\delta)
, then
\psi_{{k,i}}({B(x_{k,i-1},1-\delta)})={B(x_{k,i},1-\delta)}
, regardless of the orientation of
H_{k,i}
. If this is the case for each
i=1,\dots,m_{k}
, then
{B(x_{k,m_{k}},1-\delta)}\subset\psi_{k}(B^{n})
, so
{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(\psi_{k}(B^{n}))\geq(1-\delta)^{{n}}\kappa_{n}
. If moreover this occurs for arbitrarily large
k
, then there is a subsequence
(\psi_{k_{j}})
of
(\psi_{k})
such that
{B(x_{k_{j},m_{k_{j}}},1-\delta)}\subset\psi_{k_{j}}(B^{n})
for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. This would imply that
\psi(B^{n})
contains a ball of radius
(1-\delta)
and hence that
{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(\psi(B^{n}))\geq(1-\delta)^{{n}}\kappa_{n}
, a contradiction.

Thus there is an
N_{1}
such that for each
k\geq N_{1}
, there is an oriented hyperplane
H_{k,i_{k}}
, where
1\leq i_{k}\leq m_{k}
and
i_{k}
is as small as possible, which meets
{\mathrm{int}}\,{B(x_{k,i_{k}-1},1-\delta)}
. Assume from now on that
k\geq N_{1}
. We claim that there are
y_{k}
,
z_{k}\in B^{n}
(possibly for
k
in a subsequence) such that

\psi_{k}(y_{k})=\psi_{k}(z_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|y_{k}-z_{k}\|>0.

(7.1)

In order to prove this claim, we assume initially that

o\in H_{k,i}^{+}\quad\text{for $k\geq N_{1}$ and $i=1,\dots,i_{k}$.}

(7.2)

This implies that

Lemma 7.4

Choose
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that

\varepsilon_{1}<\min\left\{1,\frac{{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(D_{1})}{2},\frac{5\kappa_{n-1}}{n},\frac{n\kappa_{n}}{m^{\prime}},\frac{(\kappa_{n-1}\varepsilon)^{2}}{50n^{2}\rho_{c}}\right\}.

(7.12)

(The list of quantities in the minimum is for convenience; in fact, one can show that the second quantity is less than the third.) By assumption, there is a map
\heartsuit\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
such that

\|1_{\diamondsuit K}-1_{\heartsuit K}\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{1}
.

The circumball
B_{c}=B(o,\rho_{c})
of
K
has its center at the origin, so
\psi_{\diamondsuit}(o)=o
gives
\diamondsuit B_{c}=B_{c}
. Since
\heartsuit K\subset\heartsuit B_{c}=B(\psi_{\heartsuit}(o),\rho_{c})
we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left(D_{j}\setminus B(\psi_{\heartsuit}(o),\rho_{c})\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left((\diamondsuit D_{j})\setminus\heartsuit B_{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}((\diamondsuit K)\setminus\heartsuit K)\leq\|1_{\diamondsuit K}-1_{\heartsuit K}\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{1}<{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(D_{j})/2

for
j=1,\dots,2(n-1)
. It follows that
B(\psi_{\heartsuit}(o),\rho_{c})
contains the centers of all the balls in
K_{0}
, that is, the points
\pm(\rho_{c}-(n-1)^{-1/2})e_{j}
, for
j=1,\dots,n-1
. Thus each vector
\psi_{\heartsuit}(o)\pm(\rho_{c}-(n-1)^{-1/2})e_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n-1
, has Euclidean norm less than or equal to
\rho_{c}
. By considering their projections on the
j
th coordinate axis, we see that

|\langle\psi_{\heartsuit}(o),e_{j}\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}}

for
j=1,\dots,n-1
. From this and
\psi_{\heartsuit}(o)|H=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\langle\psi_{\heartsuit}(o),e_{j}\rangle e_{j}
, we obtain

\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(o)|H\|\leq 1.

(7.13)

Choose
i\in\{1,\ldots,m^{\prime}\}
. The ball
\heartsuit B_{i}
must meet the interior of at least one component of
\diamondsuit K
, as otherwise, we would have the contradiction

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(B_{i})={{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(\heartsuit B_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left((\heartsuit B_{i})\setminus\diamondsuit K\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left((\heartsuit K)\setminus\diamondsuit K\right)\leq\|1_{\diamondsuit K}-1_{\heartsuit K}\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{1}<{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(D_{1})/2\leq{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(B_{i})/2.

We claim that
\diamondsuit B_{i}
is the unique component of
\diamondsuit K
that meets
\heartsuit B_{i}
. To see this, first recall that
B_{i}
is contained in the cylinder
C_{i}=\rho_{i}B^{n}+{\mathrm{span}}\,\{e_{n}\}
and that
|\langle x_{i},e_{n}\rangle|\leq 1
. This gives
B_{i}\subset B(o,\rho_{i}+1)
, since

\|x_{i}\|+r_{i}\leq\sqrt{(\rho_{i}-r_{i})^{2}+1}+r_{i}\leq(\rho_{i}-r_{i})+1+r_{i}=\rho_{i}+1.

Using (
7.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\heartsuit B_{i}

\displaystyle\subset\heartsuit B\left(o,\rho_{i}+1\right)=B\left(\psi_{\heartsuit}(o),\rho_{i}+1\right)\subset(\rho_{i}+2)B^{n}+{\mathrm{span}}\,\{e_{n}\}=C_{i}+2B^{n}.

By the construction of
K
, the only components of
K
contained in the cylinder
C_{i}+2B^{n}
are
B_{1},\ldots,B_{i}
, and since
\diamondsuit
respects
H
-cylinders, the only components of
\diamondsuit K
contained in
C_{i}+2B^{n}
are
\diamondsuit B_{1},\ldots,\diamondsuit B_{i}
.

Because
\heartsuit B_{i}
has radius at most 2, by (
7.11
) it meets the interior of exactly one component of
(C_{i}+2B^{n})\cap\diamondsuit K
. Let
\diamondsuit B^{\prime}
be this component, and suppose that
B^{\prime}\neq B_{i}
. Then our construction ensures that the radius
r
of
B^{\prime}
satisfies
r_{i}-r\geq 1/m^{\prime}
. Thus, using
r_{i},r\geq 1
and
(\heartsuit B_{i})\cap\diamondsuit K=(\heartsuit B_{i})\cap\diamondsuit B^{\prime}
, we find that

\displaystyle\frac{n\kappa_{n}}{m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{n}(r_{i}-r)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}r_{i}^{k}r^{n-1-k}=\kappa_{n}(r_{i}^{n}-r^{n})={\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\heartsuit B_{i})-{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\diamondsuit B^{\prime})\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left((\heartsuit B_{i})\setminus\diamondsuit B^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{H}}^{n}((\heartsuit B_{i})\setminus\diamondsuit K)\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\left((\heartsuit K)\setminus\diamondsuit K\right)\leq\|1_{\diamondsuit K}-1_{\heartsuit K}\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{1},

contradicting the choice (
7.12
) of
\varepsilon_{1}
. Therefore,
(\diamondsuit B_{i})\cap\heartsuit B_{i}\neq\emptyset
, as claimed.

Since
\diamondsuit B_{i}
meets
\heartsuit B_{i}
, Lemma
7.3
with
x=x_{i}
and

implies that

\left\|\psi_{\diamondsuit}(x_{i})-\psi_{\heartsuit}(x_{i})\right\|\leq\frac{n}{2r_{i}^{n-1}\kappa_{n-1}}\|1_{\diamondsuit B_{i}}-1_{\heartsuit B_{i}}\|_{1}\leq\frac{n}{2\kappa_{n-1}}\|1_{\diamondsuit K}-1_{\heartsuit K}\|_{1}\leq\frac{n\varepsilon_{1}}{2\kappa_{n-1}}.

(7.14)

For
i=1,\dots,m^{\prime}/2
, the points
x=x_{2i-1}
and
x^{\prime}=x_{2i}
belong to the line
\ell_{i}=R_{2i-1}\cup R_{2i}
, which is parallel to
H
. Since
\diamondsuit
respects
H
-cylinders, (
4.4
) implies that
\|\psi_{\diamondsuit}(x)-\psi_{\diamondsuit}(x^{\prime})\|=\|x-x^{\prime}\|
, so
\psi_{\diamondsuit}
is affine when restricted to
[x,x^{\prime}]
. The last equality and (

) yield

\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(x)-\psi_{\heartsuit}(x^{\prime})\|\geq\|x-x^{\prime}\|-\frac{n\varepsilon_{1}}{\kappa_{n-1}}.

(7.15)

Let
z=(1-t)x+tx^{\prime}
, where
0<t<1
. Then (
7.15
) allows us to apply Lemma
7.4
with
\varepsilon=n\varepsilon_{1}/\kappa_{n-1}
and
\psi=\psi_{\heartsuit}
, and (
7.14
), to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(z)-\psi_{\diamondsuit}(z)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(z)-((1-t)\psi_{\diamondsuit}(x)+t\psi_{\diamondsuit}(x^{\prime}))\right\|

(7.16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(z)-((1-t)\psi_{\heartsuit}(x)+t\psi_{\heartsuit}(x^{\prime}))\right\|+

\displaystyle+(1-t)\left\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(x)-\psi_{\diamondsuit}(x)\right\|+t\left\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(x^{\prime})-\psi_{\diamondsuit}(x^{\prime})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{n\varepsilon_{1}}{\kappa_{n-1}}\left(2L+\frac{n\varepsilon_{1}}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)}+\frac{n\varepsilon_{1}}{2\kappa_{n-1}}.

By (
7.10
), we have
\|x-x^{\prime}\|\geq 6
, and the choice (
7.12
) of
\varepsilon_{1}
gives
n\varepsilon_{1}/\kappa_{n-1}\leq 5
. These together imply the second inequality in the following chain:

\varepsilon_{1}\leq 1\leq\|x-x^{\prime}\|-\frac{n\varepsilon_{1}}{\kappa_{n-1}}\leq L=\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(x)-\psi_{\heartsuit}(x^{\prime})\|\leq\|x-x^{\prime}\|\leq 2\rho_{c}.

The other relations follow from the choice (
7.12
) of
\varepsilon_{1}
, (
7.15
), and the fact that
\psi_{\heartsuit}
is a contraction. It is straightforward to check that for integer
n\geq 2
, we have
n/\kappa_{n-1}\geq 3/\pi
, the value when
n=3
or 4, and hence
2\leq(3n)/\kappa_{n-1}
. All these estimates, (
7.16
), and the choice (
7.12
) of
\varepsilon_{1}
yield

\left\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(z)-\psi_{\diamondsuit}(z)\right\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{n\varepsilon_{1}}{\kappa_{n-1}}\left(\frac{3nL}{\kappa_{n-1}}+\frac{nL}{\kappa_{n-1}}\right)}+\frac{n\sqrt{L\varepsilon_{1}}}{2\kappa_{n-1}}=\frac{5n\sqrt{L\varepsilon_{1}}}{2\kappa_{n-1}}\leq\frac{5n\sqrt{2\rho_{c}\varepsilon_{1}}}{2\kappa_{n-1}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(7.17)

By construction,
[-1,1]^{n}\cap\ell_{i}\subset[x,x^{\prime}]
, so (
7.17
) holds for all
z\in[-1,1]^{n}\cap\cup_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}/2}\ell_{i}
. Now for any
w\in B^{n}
, there is an
i\in\{1,\dots,m^{\prime}/2\}
and a
z\in[-1,1]^{n}\cap\ell_{i}
with
\|w-z\|\leq\sqrt{n-1}/(2m)\leq\varepsilon/4
. Since
\psi_{\heartsuit}
and
\psi_{\diamondsuit}
are contractions, (
7.17
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(w)-\psi_{\diamondsuit}(w)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(w)-\psi_{\heartsuit}(z)\right\|+\left\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(z)-\psi_{\diamondsuit}(z)\right\|+\left\|\psi_{\diamondsuit}(z)-\psi_{\diamondsuit}(w)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\psi_{\heartsuit}(z)-\psi_{\diamondsuit}(z)\right\|+2\left\|w-z\right\|\leq\varepsilon,

as required.
∎

Corollary 7.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{n}\subset{\mathcal{E}}\subset\mathcal{L}^{n}
. If
H
is a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the Brock set map
\diamondsuit_{B_{H}}
with parameter
0<b<1
cannot be weakly approximated on
{\mathcal{E}}
by finite compositions of polarizations.

Proof.

Consider the class of finite compositions of polarizations (not necessarily with respect to parallel hyperplanes). The associated contractions from (
4.2
) of polarizations are independent of
r>0
and the associated contractions of their compositions inherit this property.

If
\diamondsuit_{B_{H}}
could be weakly approximated on
{\mathcal{E}}
by finite compositions of polarizations, it could a fortiori be weakly approximated on
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{n}
by their restrictions to
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{n}
. Theorem
7.5
would then yield a sequence
(\diamondsuit_{k})
of finite compositions of polarizations such that the associated contractions
\psi_{\diamondsuit_{k}}
converge uniformly on
B^{n}
to the contraction
\psi_{\diamondsuit_{B_{H}}}
associated with
\diamondsuit_{B_{H}}
. Since
\psi_{\diamondsuit_{B_{H}}}
is injective, Theorem
7.1
would imply that
{{\mathcal{H}}}^{n}(\psi_{\diamondsuit_{B_{H}}}(B^{n}))=\kappa_{n}
, a contradiction, as
b<1
.
∎

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
X\subset{\mathcal{V}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
contain all nonnegative
C^{\infty}
functions with compact support, and let
1\leq p<\infty
. If
H
is a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the Brock rearrangement
B_{H}
with parameter
0<b<1
is not weakly approximable in
L^{p}
on
X
by finite compositions of polarizations.

Proof.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and suppose that
B_{H}
is weakly approximable in
L^{p}
on
X
by finite compositions of polarizations. As
X
contains all nonnegative
C^{\infty}
functions with compact support, the usual approximation argument based on mollifiers shows that
{\mathrm{cl}}\,_{p}X=L_{+}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. By Lemma
5.1
(with
T=B_{H}
and
{\mathcal{W}}
the class of finite compositions of polarizations),
B_{H}
is weakly approximable in
L^{p}
on
L_{+}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by finite compositions of polarizations. Then Theorem
5.2
implies that
\diamondsuit_{B_{H}}
is weakly approximable on
\mathcal{C}^{n}
by finite compositions of polarizations. Since
{\mathcal{F}}^{n}_{n}\subset\mathcal{C}^{n}
, this contradicts Corollary
7.6
.
∎

Theorem 8.4
.

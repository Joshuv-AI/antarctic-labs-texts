Abstract




Further results on
r
-Euler-Mahonian statistics

Abstract.

As natural generalizations of the descent number (
\mathsf{des}
) and the major index (
\mathsf{maj}
), Rawlings introduced the notions of the
r
-descent number (
r\mathsf{des}
) and the
r
-major index (
r\mathsf{maj}
) for a given positive integer
r
. A pair
(\mathsf{st}_{1},\mathsf{st}_{2})
of permutation statistics is said to be
r
-Euler-Mahonian if
(\mathrm{st_{1}},\mathrm{st_{2}})
and

(r\mathsf{des},r\mathsf{maj})
are equidistributed over the set
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of all permutations of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. The main objective of this paper is to confirm a recent conjecture posed by Liu which asserts that
(g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell},g\mathsf{den}_{\ell})
is
(g+\ell-1)
-Euler-Mahonian for all positive integers
g
and
\ell
, where
g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}
denotes the
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance number and
g\mathsf{den}_{\ell}
denotes the
g
-gap
\ell
-level Denert’s statistic. This is accomplished via a bijective proof of the equidistribution of
(g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell},g\mathsf{den}_{\ell})
and
(r\mathsf{des},r\mathsf{maj})
where
r=g+\ell-1
.
Setting
g=\ell=1
, our result recovers the equidistribution of
(\mathsf{des},\mathsf{maj})
and
(\mathsf{exc},\mathsf{den})
, which was first conjectured by Denert and proved by Foata and Zeilberger.
Our second main result is concerned with the analogous result for
(g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell},g\mathsf{den}_{g+\ell})
which states that
(g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell},g\mathsf{den}_{g+\ell})
is
(g+\ell-1)
-Euler-Mahonian for all positive integers
g
and
\ell
.

Keywords
:
r
-major index,
r
-descent number,
g
-gap
\ell
-level Denert’s statistic,
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance number,
r
-Euler-Mahonian statistic.

Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
denote the set of permutations of
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. A permutation
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is usually written in one-line notation as
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\cdots\pi_{n}
. Given a permutation
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, a pair
(i,j)
is called an
inversion
of
\pi
if
i<j
and
\pi_{i}>\pi_{j}
. Let
\mathsf{inv}(\pi)
denote the number of inversions of
\pi
.
Define the
descent set
of
\pi
as

\mathsf{Des}(\pi)=\{i\mid\pi_{i}>\pi_{i+1},\,\,1\leq i\leq n-1\}.

The
descent number
of
\pi
, denoted by
\mathsf{des}(\pi)
, is defined to be the cardinality of the set
\mathsf{Des}(\pi)
.
The
major index
of
\pi
is defined as

\mathsf{maj}(\pi)=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathsf{Des}(\pi)}i.

The well-known MacMahon’s equidistribution theorem
[
12
]
states that

\sum\limits_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}q^{\mathrm{inv(\pi)}}=\sum\limits_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}q^{\mathrm{maj(\pi)}}=[n]_{q}[n-1]_{q}\ldots[1]_{q},

(1.1)

where
[k]_{q}:=1+q+q^{2}+\ldots+q^{k-1}
.
Any permutation statistic equidistributed with
\mathsf{inv}
is said to be
Mahonian
.
Various interesting extensions of MacMahon’s equidistribution theorem have been obtained for other combinatorial objects (see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
11
,
15
,
16
]
).

Let
r\geq 1
.
Rawlings
[
13
]
introduced the notions of
r
-descent number
and
r
-major
index
, which are natural generalizations of the descent number and the major index.
Let
r\geq 1
. Given a permutation
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\ldots\pi_{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, define the
r
-descent set
and the
r
-inversion set
of
\pi
to be

r\mathsf{Des}(\pi)=\{i\in[n-1]\mid\pi_{i}\geq\pi_{i+1}+r\},

r\mathsf{Inv}(\pi)=\{(i,j)\in\mathsf{Inv}(\pi)\mid\pi_{i}<\pi_{j}+r\},

respectively, where
\mathsf{Inv}(\pi)=\{(i,j)\mid\pi_{i}>\pi_{j},\,\,i<j\}
.
The
r
-descent number
of
\pi
, denoted by
r\mathsf{des}(\pi)
, is defined to be the cardinality of the set
r\mathsf{Des}(\pi)
.
The
r
-major
index
of
\pi
is defined to be

r\mathsf{maj}(\pi)=\sum\limits_{i\in r\mathsf{Des}(\pi)}i+|r\mathsf{Inv}(\pi)|.

Clearly, the statistic
r\mathsf{maj}
interpolates between
\mathsf{maj}
and
\mathsf{inv}
since
r\mathsf{maj}
reduces to
\mathsf{maj}
when
r=1
, and reduces to
\mathsf{inv}
when
r\geq n
.
Rawlings
[
13
]
presented a bijective proof of the equidistribution of
r\mathsf{maj}
and
\mathsf{inv}
over
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, thereby proving that
r\mathsf{maj}
is Mahonian. A pair
(\mathsf{st}_{1},\mathsf{st}_{2})
of permutation statistics is said to be
r
-Euler-Mahonian
if

\sum\limits_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{\mathsf{st}_{1}(\pi)}q^{\mathsf{st}_{2}(\pi)}=\sum\limits_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{r\mathsf{des}(\pi)}q^{r\mathsf{maj}(\pi)}.

Given a permutation
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\ldots\pi_{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
an index
i
,
1\leq i<n
, is called an
excedance place

of
\pi
if
\pi_{i}>i
. The
excedance number
of
\pi
, denoted by
\mathsf{exc}(\pi)
, is defined to be the number of excedance places of
\pi
.
Denert
[
4
]
introduced a new permutation statistic
\mathsf{den}
and conjectured that
(\mathsf{exc},\mathsf{den})
has the same distribution as
(\mathsf{des},\mathsf{maj})
over
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, that is,

\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{\mathsf{des}(\pi)}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\pi)}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{\mathsf{exc}(\pi)}q^{\mathsf{den}(\pi)}.

(1.2)

This conjecture was first proved by Foata and Zeilberger
[
5
]
and a bijective proof was provided by Han
[
6
]
.

Motivated by finding extensions of (
1.2
) for
r
-Euler-Mahonian statistics,
Liu
[
9
]
introduced the notions of
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance number
and
g
-gap
\ell
-level Denert’s statistic
. Fix integers
g,\ell\geq 1
. Given a permutation
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, an index
i\in[n-1]
is called a
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance place
and
\pi_{i}
is called a
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance letter
if
\pi_{i}\geq i+g
and
\pi_{i}\geq\ell
. For example, if we let
\pi=836295417
, then the
2
-gap
3
-level excedance places of
\pi
are given by
1,3,5
and the
2
-gap
3
-level excedance letters of
\pi
are given by
6,8,9
.
Let
g\mathsf{Excp}_{\ell}(\pi)
and
g\mathsf{Nexcp}_{\ell}(\pi)
denote the set of
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance places of
\pi
and the set of non-
g
-gap-
\ell
-level excedance places of
\pi
, respectively. Let
g\mathsf{Excl}_{\ell}(\pi)
denote the set of
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance letters of
\pi
.
Assume that
g\mathsf{Excp}_{\ell}(\pi)=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}
with
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}
and
g\mathrm{Nexcp}_{\ell}(\pi)=\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n-k}\}

with
j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{n-k}
. Define

g\mathsf{Exc}_{\ell}(\pi)=\pi_{i_{1}}\pi_{i_{2}}\ldots\pi_{i_{k}},

and

g\mathsf{Nexc}_{\ell}(\pi)=\pi_{j_{1}}\pi_{j_{2}}\ldots\pi_{j_{n-k}}.

The
g
-gap
\ell
-level Denert’s statistic
of
\pi
, denoted by
g\mathsf{den}_{\ell}(\pi)
, is defined as

g\mathsf{den}_{\ell}(\pi)=\sum\limits_{i\in g\mathsf{Excp}_{\ell}(\pi)}(i+g-1)+{\mathsf{inv}}(g\mathsf{Exc}_{\ell}(\pi))+{\mathsf{inv}}(g\mathsf{Nexc}_{\ell}(\pi)).

For example, if we let
\pi=836295417
, then
2\mathsf{Excp}_{3}(\pi)=\{1,3,5\}
,
2\mathsf{Excl}_{3}(\pi)=\{6,8,9\}
,
2\mathrm{Nexcp}_{3}(\pi)=\{2,4,6,7,8,9\}
,

2\mathsf{Exc}_{3}(\pi)=869
, and
2\mathsf{Nexc}_{3}(\pi)=325417
. Therefore, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}2\mathsf{den}_{3}(\pi)&=\sum\limits_{i\in 2\mathsf{Excp}_{3}(\pi)}(i+g-1)+{\mathsf{inv}}(2\mathsf{Exc}_{3}(\pi))+{\mathsf{inv}}(2\mathsf{Nexc}_{3}(\pi))\\
&=2+4+6+{\mathsf{inv}}(869)+{\mathsf{inv}}(325417)\\
&=19.\end{array}

When
g=\ell=1
, the statistic
g\mathsf{den}_{\ell}

\mathsf{den}
introduced by Denert
[

]
.
The
g
-gap
1

is called the
g

of
\pi
, denoted by
g\mathsf{den}(\pi)
. Similarly, The
1
-gap
\ell

\ell
-level Denert’s statistic
of

.

The
g
-gap

of
\pi
, denoted by

, is defined to be the number of
g

\pi_{\ell}

g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}(\pi)=|\{i\mid\pi_{i}\geq i+g,i\geq\ell\}|.

For example, if we let
\pi=836295417
, then we have

2\mathsf{exc}_{3}(\pi)=2
.
The
g
-gap
1
-level excedance number of
\pi
is called the
g
-gap excedance number
of
\pi
, denoted by
g\mathsf{exc}(\pi)
. Similarly, the
1
-gap
\ell
-level excedance number of
\pi
is called the
\ell
-level excedance number
of
\pi
, denoted by
\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}(\pi)
.

Rawlings’s and Liu’s insertion methods

Let
r\geq 1
.
In order to prove the equidistribution of
r\mathsf{maj}
and
\mathsf{inv}
,
Rawlings
[
13
]
introduced an extension of
Carlitz’s insertion method
[
3
]
. Here, we give a brief discription of this insertion method.

Given
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}
, let

S(\pi)=\{\pi_{i}\mid\pi_{i-1}\geq\pi_{i}+r\}\cup\{\pi_{i}\mid\pi_{i}>n-r\}.

It is easily seen that
|S(\pi)|=r\mathsf{des}(\pi)+r-1
. The
r
-maj-labeling
of
\pi
is obtained as follows.

•

Label the space after
\pi_{n-1}
by
0
.

•

Label the spaces before those letters of
\pi
that are belonging to
S(\pi)
from right to left with
1,2,\ldots,r\mathsf{des}(\pi)+r-1
.

•

Label the remaining spaces from left to right with
r\mathsf{des}(\pi)+r,\ldots,n-1
.

Take
\pi=836295417
and
r=3
for example. Clearly, we have
S(\pi)=\{1,2,3,5,8,9\}
, and thus the
3
-maj-labeling of
\pi
is given by

\begin{array}[]{llllllllll}{}_{6}8&{}_{5}3&{}_{7}6&{}_{4}2&{}_{3}9&{}_{2}5&{}_{8}4&{}_{1}1&{}_{9}7&{}_{0},\end{array}

where the labels of the spaces are written as subscripts.
Define

\phi^{\mathsf{maj}}_{r,n}:\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}\times\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{n}

by mapping the pair
(\pi,c)
to the permutation in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
obtained by inserting
n
at the space in
\pi
which is labeled by
c
in the
r
-
\mathsf{maj}
-labeling of
\pi
. Continuing with our running example, we have

\phi^{\mathsf{maj}}_{3,10}(836295417,4)=836(10)295417
.

Rawlings
[
13
]
proved that the map
\phi^{\mathsf{maj}}_{r,n}
verifies the following celebrated properties.

Lemma 2.1
(Rawlings
[
13
]
)

The map
\phi^{\mathsf{maj}}_{r,n}:\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}\times\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is a bijection such that for
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}
and
0\leq c\leq n-1
, we have

r\mathsf{des}(\phi^{\mathsf{maj}}_{r,n}(\pi,c))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r\mathsf{des}(\pi)&\,\mathrm{if}\,\,0\leq c\leq r\mathsf{des}(\pi)+r-1,\\
r\mathsf{des}(\pi)+1&\,\mathrm{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and

r\mathsf{maj}(\phi^{\mathsf{maj}}_{r,n}(\pi,c))=r\mathsf{maj}(\pi)+c.

Now we proceed to give a description of Liu’s insertion method
[
9
]
, which was employed to prove that the pair
(g\mathsf{exc},g\mathsf{den})
is
g
-Euler-Mahonian for all
g\geq 1
.

A
g
-gap
1
-level excedance letter is called a
g
-gap excedance letter.
Given
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}
, assume that
g\mathsf{exc}(\pi)=s
and that
e_{1}<e_{2}<\cdots<e_{s}
are the
g
-gap excedance letters of
\pi
. The
g
-gap-den-labeling of the index sequence
of
\pi
is obtained by labelling the spaces of the index sequence
12\cdots(n-1)
of
\pi
as follows.

•

Label the rightmost
g
spaces by
0,1,\ldots,g-1
.

•

Label the space before the index
e_{i}-(g-1)
with
g+s-i
for all
1\leq i\leq s
.

•

Label the remaining spaces from left to right with
g+s,\ldots,n-1
.

The
insertion letters
of the index sequence of
\pi
are obtained by labeling the space to the left of the index
e_{1}-(g-1)
with
e_{1}
, labeling the space to the left of the index
e_{2}-(g-1)
and to the right of the index
e_{1}-(g-1)
with
e_{2}
,
\ldots
, and labeling the space to the right of the index
e_{s}-(g-1)
with
n
.

For example, we let
\pi=836295417
. Then the
3
-gap excedance letters are given by
e_{1}=6
,
e_{2}=8
and
e_{3}=9
and thus the
3
-gap-den-labeling and the insertion letters of the index sequence of
\pi
are given by

\begin{array}[]{lllllllllll}&{}_{6}1&{}_{7}2&{}_{8}{3}&{}_{5}4&{}_{9}5&{}_{4}{\mathbb{6}}&{}_{3}7&{}_{2}{\mathbb{8}}&{}_{1}{\mathbb{9}}&{}_{0}\\
&\uparrow&\uparrow&\uparrow&\uparrow&\uparrow&\uparrow&\uparrow&\uparrow&\uparrow&\uparrow\\
&6&6&6&6&8&8&9&10&10&10,\end{array}

where the labels of the spaces in the
3
-gap-den-labeling are written as subscripts and the insertion letters of the spaces are written on the bottom row.

Define

\phi^{\mathsf{den}}_{g,n}:\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}\times\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{n}

by mapping the pair
(\pi,c)
to the permutation in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
according to the following rules.

•

If
c=0
, then let
\phi^{\mathsf{den}}_{g,n}(\pi,c)
be the permutation obtained from
\pi
by inserting
n
immediately after
\pi_{n-1}
.

•

For
c\geq 1
, assume that the space labeled by
c
in the
g
-gap-den-labeling of the index sequence of
\pi
is immediately before the index
x
, and assume that the insertion letter for this space is
e_{y}
, where
1\leq y\leq g\mathsf{exc}(\pi)+1
and
e_{g\mathsf{exc}(\pi)+1}=n
. Define
\pi^{\prime}=\pi^{\prime}_{1}\pi^{\prime}_{2}\ldots\pi^{\prime}_{n-1}
from
\pi
by replacing
e_{j}
with
e_{j+1}
for all
y\leq j\leq g\mathsf{exc}(\pi)
. Then define
\phi^{\mathsf{den}}_{g,n}(\pi,c)
to be the permutation obtained from
\pi^{\prime}
by inserting the letter
e_{y}
immediately before the letter
\pi^{\prime}_{x}
.

Take
\pi=836295417
,
g=3
and
c=4
for example. Clearly, the space labeled by
4
in the
3
-gap-den-labeling of the index sequence of

is immediately before the index

, and the insertion letter for this space is

. Then we get a permutation
\pi^{\prime}=9362(10)5417
from
\pi
by replacing replacing
e_{2}=8
with
e_{3}=9
and replacing
e_{3}=9
with
e_{4}=10
. Finally, we get
\phi^{\mathsf{den}}_{3,10}(\pi,4)=9362(10)85417
from
\pi^{\prime}
by inserting

immediately before
\pi^{\prime}_{x}=\pi^{\prime}_{6}=5
.

[

proved that the map

Lemma 2.2
(Liu
[
9
]
)

The map
\phi^{\mathsf{den}}_{g,n}:\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}\times\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is a bijection such that
for
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}
and
0\leq c\leq n-1
, we have

g\mathsf{exc}(\phi^{\mathsf{den}}_{g,n}(\pi))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g\mathsf{exc}(\pi)&\,\mathrm{if}\,\,0\leq c\leq g\mathsf{exc}(\pi)+g-1,\\

and

g\mathsf{den}(\phi^{\mathsf{den}}_{g,n}(\pi))=g\mathsf{den}(\pi)+c.

and
2.2
, Liu
[

provided a bijective proof of the equidistribution of
(g\mathsf{exc},g\mathsf{den})
and
(g\mathsf{des},g\mathsf{maj})
.

3
Proof of Conjecture
1.2

This section is devoted to the proof of Conjecture
1.2
. Our proof is based on introducing a nontrivial extension of Liu’s insertion method
[
9
]
.

Let

n\geq g+\ell
and
g,\ell\geq 1
.
Given
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}
, assume that
g\mathsf{Excl}_{\ell}(\pi)=\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{s}\}
with
e_{1}<e_{2}<\cdots<e_{s}
.
Define

\mathcal{A}(\pi)=\{i\mid\pi_{i}\in g\mathsf{Excl}_{\ell}(\pi),\,\,\pi_{i}\geq g+\ell,\,\,i<\ell\},

\mathcal{B}(\pi)=\{i\mid\pi_{i}\notin g\mathsf{Excl}_{\ell}(\pi),\,\,i<\ell\},

and

\mathcal{C}(\pi)=\{i\mid\pi_{i}\in g\mathsf{Excl}_{\ell}(\pi),\,\,\pi_{i}<g+\ell,\,\,i<\ell\}.

It is apparent that

|\mathcal{A}(\pi)|+|\mathcal{C}(\pi)|+g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}(\pi)=s
and

Expanding Solutions to Free Boundary 3D Spherically Symmetric Compressible Navier-Stokes-Poisson Equations near the Lane-Emden Stars
Han Cao

Abstract
We consider the gravitational Navier-Stokes-Poisson equations with the equation of state P ⁡ ( ρ ) = K ​ ρ γ P(\rho)=K\rho^{\gamma} , where γ ∈ ( 6 5 , 4 3 ] \gamma\in(\frac{6}{5},\frac{4}{3}] , which models the viscous polytropic gaseous stars. We prove the existence of global weak solutions to the equations with constant viscosity and radially symmetric initial data. For γ = 4 3 \gamma=\frac{4}{3} , we require the initial data having mass less than the mass of the Lane-Emden stars; for γ ∈ ( 6 5 , 4 3 ) \gamma\in(\frac{6}{5},\frac{4}{3}) , we require that the initial data belong to an invariant set where initial initial data can be taken near the Lane-Emden stars. For γ ∈ ( 6 5 , 4 3 ) \gamma\in(\frac{6}{5},\frac{4}{3}) , we show that the invariant set contains some initial data that are not allowed in previous literature. We also prove the support of any strong solution expands to infinity for the Navier-Stokes-Poisson equations with constant viscosity and a class of density-dependent viscosity, which indicates the strong instability of Lane-Emden solutions for the Navier-Stokes-Poisson equations.

Introduction

The evolution of viscous gaseous stars can be described by the Navier-Stokes-Poisson equations:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}(\rho\boldsymbol{u})=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\boldsymbol{u})+\mathrm{div}(\rho\boldsymbol{u}\otimes\boldsymbol{u})+\nabla p=\mathrm{div}T-\rho\nabla\varPhi,\\
\Delta\varPhi=4\pi\rho,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\rho(\boldsymbol{x},t)\geq 0
is the density,
\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the velocity.
p
is the pressure satisfying

p=p(\rho)=K\rho^{\gamma}\text{ with }\gamma\in(1,2),

(1.2)

which is the equation of state for a polytropic gas, where
K>0
is a constant and
\gamma
is the adiabatic exponent. For convenience, we set
K=1
.
\varPhi
is the potential function of the self-gravitational force and the stress tensor
T
is given by

T=\varepsilon\left(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{T}-\frac{2}{3}(\mathrm{div}\boldsymbol{u})I_{3\times 3}\right)+\eta(\mathrm{div}\boldsymbol{u})I_{3\times 3},

(1.3)

where
\varepsilon\geq 0
is the shear viscosity and
\eta\geq 0
is the bulk viscosity. Viscosity coefficients can be either constant or density-dependent, i.e.
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\rho)\geq 0,\ \eta=\eta(\rho)\geq 0
. One example is
\varepsilon(\rho)=\varepsilon_{0}\rho^{\alpha},\ \eta(\rho)=\eta_{0}\rho^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>0

[
9
,
13
,
33
]
. On the other hand, if
\varepsilon=0,\ \eta=0
, then (
1.1
) models the inviscid self-gravitating gaseous stars, called the Euler-Poisson system. Our results mainly focus on the constant viscosity model with at least one of
\varepsilon
and
\eta
positive.

One type of special solution is the Lane-Emden solution, that is, the non-rotating steady solution of (
1.1
). We denote
(\rho_{\mu}(r),0)
to be such a radially symmetric solution with central density
\rho_{\mu}(0)=\mu
. Then
\rho_{\mu}
satisfies:

\begin{cases}\nabla\rho_{\mu}^{\gamma}=-\rho_{\mu}\nabla\varPhi_{\mu},\\
\Delta\varPhi_{\mu}=4\pi\rho_{\mu}.\end{cases}

(1.4)

By rewriting (
1.4
), we obtain the following ODE

\partial_{r}\rho_{\mu}^{\gamma}=-\frac{4\pi\rho_{\mu}}{r^{2}}\int_{0}^{r}\rho_{\mu}s^{2}ds.

(1.5)

The studies of the equation (
1.5

\gamma

[

[

\gamma\searrow 1

1.5

\gamma\in(\frac{6}{5},2)
, for given finite total mass, there exists at least one compactly supported solution to (
1.5

\gamma\in(\frac{4}{3},2)
. When
\gamma=\frac{6}{5}
, there exist a unique solution with explicit expression, which has infinite support. When
\gamma\in(1,\frac{6}{5})
, there are no solutions with finite mass
[
25
]
.

When
\gamma\in(\frac{6}{5},2)
, we denote the radius of the support of the Lane-Emden solution by

, the total mass and the total energy by

By scaling, we can see that if

We call the case

The following theorem shows the algebraic expanding rate of the free boundary, which illustrates the strong instability of the Lane-Emden solutions for Navier-Stokes-Poisson equations. Theorem 2.3

(Algebraic expanding rate of the free boundary)

Remark 2.3

If we consider the following radially symmetric 3D Navier-Stokes-Poisson equations

The rest part is arranged as follows. In section

Basic energy estimate

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T>0
,
\gamma\in(\frac{6}{5},\frac{4}{3}]
, and
(\rho,u,a)
is a strong solution to problem (
4.1
)-(
4.4
) with initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfying
\rho_{0}\in L^{1}([\xi,a_{0});r^{2}dr)\cap L^{\gamma}([\xi,a_{0});r^{2}dr)
,
\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\in L^{2}([\xi,a_{0});r^{2}dr)
and (
2.21
). Let

E(\rho(t),u(t))=4\pi\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{2}r^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}r^{2}dr-4\pi\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}4\pi\rho r\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)dr,

(4.14)

then for any
t\in[0,T]
, there holds:

E(\rho(t),u(t))+4\pi\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\xi}^{a(s)}(\eta+\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon)(\partial_{r}u+\frac{2u}{r})^{2}r^{2}drds=E(\rho_{0},u_{0}).

(4.15)

For the gravitational energy term, it can also be written as follows

4\pi\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}4\pi\rho r\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)dr=\frac{1}{2a(t)}\left(4\pi\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho r^{2}dr\right)^{2}+\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\left(4\pi\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)^{2}dr.

(4.16)

Proof.

Multiply the momentum equation in (
4.1
) by
ur^{2}
and integrate it over
[\xi,a(t))
to get

\displaystyle\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{r}u)ur^{2}dr+

\displaystyle\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\partial_{r}\rho^{\gamma}\cdot ur^{2}dr

(4.17)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}4\pi\rho u\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}dsdr+\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}ur^{2}\partial_{r}\left(\frac{\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}(r^{2}u)\right)dr.

For the first term on the left-hand side of (
4.17
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{r}u)ur^{2}dr

(4.18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{2}r^{2}dr-\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{3}r^{2}|_{r=a(t)}-\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{2}u^{2}r^{2}\partial_{t}\rho dr+\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho u^{2}\partial_{r}u\cdot r^{2}dr.

Using the mass equation in (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{2}u^{2}r^{2}\partial_{t}\rho dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{r}\rho\cdot u^{3}r^{2}dr+\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\rho\cdot(r^{2}\partial_{r}u+2ru)dr=I+II.

(4.19)

By integration by part,

\displaystyle II=

\displaystyle-\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{r}\rho\cdot u^{3}r^{2}+\rho u^{2}\partial_{r}u\cdot r^{2}dr+\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{3}r^{2}|_{r=a(t)}.

(4.20)

As a result,

\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{r}u)ur^{2}dr=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{2}r^{2}dr.

(4.21)

For the second term on the left-hand side of (
4.17
), integrating by part and using the mass equation in (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\partial_{r}\rho^{\gamma}\cdot ur^{2}dr=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}r^{2}dr+\rho^{\gamma}ur^{2}|_{r=a(t)}.

(4.22)

For the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.17
), by using the mass equation, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}-4\pi\rho u\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)dr=

\displaystyle\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}2\pi(r\partial_{t}\rho+r\partial_{r}(\rho u))\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)dr

(4.23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}2\pi\rho r\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)dr-\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}2\pi\rho u\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)dr,

which gives

\displaystyle\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}-4\pi\rho u\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)dr=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}4\pi\rho r\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)dr.

(4.24)

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.17
), by integration by part,

\displaystyle\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}ur^{2}

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\left(\frac{\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}(r^{2}u)\right)dr

(4.25)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}(\eta+\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon)(\partial_{r}u+\frac{2u}{r})^{2}r^{2}dr+(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta)(\partial_{r}u+\frac{2u}{r})ur^{2}|_{r=a(t)}.

Combining (
4.21
), (
4.22
), (
4.24
), (
4.25
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(4\pi\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{2}r^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}r^{2}dr-4\pi\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}4\pi\rho r\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)dr\right)

(4.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}(\eta+\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon)(\partial_{r}u+\frac{2u}{r})^{2}r^{2}dr,

which gives (
4.15
).

Also,

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho r\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)dr=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{1}{2r}d\left[\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)^{2}\right]

(4.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{a(t)}\left(\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho s^{2}ds\right)^{2}+\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\frac{2\pi}{r^{2}}\left(\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho s^{2}ds\right)^{2}dr,

which gives (
4.16
).

What’s more,

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}(\eta+\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon)(\partial_{r}u+\frac{2u}{r})^{2}r^{2}drdt

(4.28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}(\eta+\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon)(r^{2}(\partial_{r}u)^{2}+2u^{2})drdt+8\pi\int_{0}^{T}(\eta+\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon)a(t)|a^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt\leq E_{0},

which gives
\int_{0}^{T}a(t)|a^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt\leq C
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

When written in 3D Cartesian coordinates, the energy reads:

E(\rho(t),\boldsymbol{u}(t))=\int_{B(0;a(t))\backslash B(0;\xi)}\frac{1}{2}\rho|\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}d\boldsymbol{x}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{8\pi}|\nabla\varPhi|^{2}d\boldsymbol{x},

(4.29)

and the energy identity is

E(\rho(t),\boldsymbol{u}(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B(0;a(s))\backslash B(0;\xi)}(\eta+\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon)|div\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}d\boldsymbol{x}ds=E(\rho_{0},\boldsymbol{u}_{0}).

(4.30)

When written in Lagrangian coordinates, the energy becomes

E(\rho(\tau),u(\tau))=\int_{0}^{M}\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma-1}-\frac{x}{r}dx,

(4.31)

and the energy identity is

\displaystyle E(\rho(\tau),u(\tau))+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{M}(\eta+\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon)\frac{1}{\rho}(4\pi\rho r^{2}\partial_{x}u+\frac{2u}{r})^{2}dxds=E(\rho_{0},u_{0}).

(4.32)

In this subsection, we establish several further estimates in Eulerian coordinates, which are necessary for the precompactness of the approximate solutions near the origin.

In the following lemma, we prove a higher space-time integrability for
\rho
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
T>0
,
\gamma\in(\frac{6}{5},\frac{4}{3}]
and
(\rho,u,a)
be any strong solution to the problem (
4.1
)-(
4.4
) with initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfying
\rho_{0}\in L^{1}([\xi,a_{0});r^{2}dr)\cap L^{\gamma}([\xi,a_{0});r^{2}dr)
,
\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\in L^{2}([\xi,a_{0});r^{2}dr)
and (
2.21
). Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(E_{0},M,T)
depending on
E_{0},\ M
and
T
, but independent of
\xi
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho^{2\gamma}r^{8}drdt\leq C(E_{0},M,T).

(4.33)

Proof.

We rewrite the momentum equation in (
4.1
):

\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\frac{2}{r}\rho u^{2}+\partial_{r}(\rho u^{2}+\rho^{\gamma})=-\frac{4\pi\rho}{r^{2}}\int_{0}^{r}\rho(s,t)s^{2}ds+\partial_{r}\left(\frac{\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}(r^{2}u)\right).

Change the variable
r
into
y
, multiply the resulting equation by
y^{3}
and integrate it from
r
to
a(t)
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{a(t)}\partial_{t}(\rho u)y^{3}dy+\int_{r}^{a(t)}2\rho u^{2}y^{2}dy+\int_{r}^{a(t)}y^{3}\partial_{y}(\rho u^{2}+\rho^{\gamma})dy

(4.34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{r}^{a(t)}4\pi\rho y\int_{\xi}^{y}\rho(s,t)s^{2}dsdy+\int_{r}^{a(t)}y^{3}\partial_{y}\left(\frac{\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta}{y^{2}}\partial_{y}(y^{2}u)\right)dy.

We integrate the terms
\int_{r}^{a(t)}y^{3}\partial_{y}(\rho u^{2}+\rho^{\gamma})dy
and
\int_{r}^{a(t)}y^{3}\partial_{y}\left(\frac{\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta}{y^{2}}\partial_{y}(y^{2}u)\right)dy
by parts and multiply the resulting equation by
\rho^{\gamma}
to obtain

\displaystyle\rho^{2\gamma}r^{3}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\rho^{\gamma}\left(\partial_{r}u+\frac{2u}{r}\right)r^{3}-\rho^{\gamma+1}u^{2}r^{3}+\rho^{\gamma}\partial_{t}\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy

(4.35)

\displaystyle+\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}2\rho u^{2}y^{2}dy-3\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}(\rho u^{2}+\rho^{\gamma})y^{2}dy

\displaystyle+3\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\left(\partial_{y}u+\frac{2u}{y}\right)y^{2}dy+\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}4\pi\rho y\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho(s,t)s^{2}dsdy.

Using the mass conservation
\partial_{t}\rho^{\gamma}=-\partial_{r}(\rho^{\gamma}u)-(\gamma-1)\rho^{\gamma}\partial_{r}u-\gamma\rho^{\gamma}\frac{2u}{r}=0
, we can rewrite the following term in (
4.35
) as

\displaystyle\rho^{\gamma}\partial_{t}\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy\right)-\partial_{t}\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy

(4.36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy\right)+\partial_{r}\left(\rho^{\gamma}u\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy\right)+\rho^{\gamma+1}u^{2}r^{3}

\displaystyle+(\gamma-1)\rho^{\gamma}\partial_{r}u\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy+\gamma\rho^{\gamma}\frac{2u}{r}\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy,

then (
4.35
) reads as

\displaystyle\rho^{2\gamma}r^{3}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\rho^{\gamma}\left(\partial_{r}u+\frac{2u}{r}\right)r^{3}+\partial_{t}\left(\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy\right)+\partial_{r}\left(\rho^{\gamma}u\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy\right)

(4.37)

\displaystyle+(\gamma-1)\rho^{\gamma}\partial_{r}u\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy+\gamma\rho^{\gamma}\frac{2u}{r}\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy

\displaystyle+\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}2\rho u^{2}y^{2}dy-3\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}(\rho u^{2}+\rho^{\gamma})y^{2}dy+\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}4\pi\rho y\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho(s,t)s^{2}dsdy

\displaystyle+3\rho^{\gamma}\int_{r}^{a(t)}\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\left(\partial_{y}u+\frac{2u}{y}\right)y^{2}dy.

Multiply (
4.37
) by
r^{5}
and integrate it over
[\xi,a(t))\times[0,T]
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho^{2\gamma}r^{8}drdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\{\text{r.h.s. of (\ref{rho2gamma})}\}r^{5}drdt=\sum_{i=1}^{9}I_{i}.

(4.38)

We estimate each term on the right hand side of (
4.38
). The controls of
I_{1},\ I_{4}\text{ and }I_{5}
are similar and can be estimated together as

\displaystyle|I_{1}+I_{4}+I_{5}|

(4.39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\rho^{\gamma}\left(\partial_{r}u+\frac{2u}{r}\right)r^{8}+\left((\gamma-1)\rho^{\gamma}\partial_{r}u+\gamma\rho^{\gamma}\cdot\frac{2u}{r}\right)r^{5}\left(\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy\right)drdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho^{2\gamma}r^{8}drdt+\delta^{-1}C.

For
I_{3}
, we integrate by parts with respect to
r
and estimate similarly as in (
4.39
):

\displaystyle|I_{3}|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\partial_{r}\left(\rho^{\gamma}u\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy\right)r^{5}drdt\right|

(4.40)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}u\left(\int_{r}^{a(t)}\rho uy^{3}dy\right)5r^{4}drdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho^{2\gamma}r^{8}drdt+\delta^{-1}C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}u^{2}\left(\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}(\rho+\rho u^{2})y^{3}dy\right)drdt

\displaystyle\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho^{2\gamma}r^{8}drdt+\delta^{-1}C.

For
I_{6},\ I_{7}\text{ and }I_{8}
,

\displaystyle|I_{6}+I_{7}+I_{8}|

(4.41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}r^{5}\left(\int_{r}^{a(t)}\left[2\rho u^{2}y^{2}-3(\rho u^{2}+\rho^{\gamma})y^{2}+\int_{r}^{a(t)}4\pi\rho y\int_{\xi}^{r}\rho(s,t)s^{2}ds\right]dy\right)drdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}y^{5}drdt\leq C,

where we use the basic energy estimate to control the integral in the interior layer. For
I_{9}
, we apply Hölder inequality to the interior layer to get

\displaystyle|I_{9}|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}r^{5}\left(\int_{r}^{a(t)}\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\left(\partial_{y}u+\frac{2u}{y}\right)3y^{2}dy\right)drdt\right|

,

\displaystyle|I_{2}|=

The following lemma provides a pointwise upper bound and lower bound for the density along the particle path.

.

T>0
,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+u\partial_{r})\left(\int_{a(t)}^{r}\rho udy+\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\log\rho\right)

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{a(t)}\frac{2}{y}\rho u^{2}dy-\rho^{\gamma}+\rho^{\gamma}(a(t),t)-\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\left(\partial_{r}u+\frac{2u}{r}\right)(a(t),t)

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{a(t)}\frac{2}{y}\rho u^{2}dy-\rho^{\gamma}+\int_{r}^{a(t)}\frac{4\pi\rho}{y^{2}}\int_{\xi}^{y}\rho(s,t)s^{2}dsdy,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{a(t)}^{r}\rho udy+\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\log\rho\right)+\rho^{\gamma}

(4.54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{a(t)}\frac{2}{y}\rho u^{2}dy+\int_{r}^{a(t)}\frac{4\pi\rho}{y^{2}}\int_{\xi}^{y}\rho(s,t)s^{2}dsdy.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\log\frac{\rho(r(t),t)}{\rho(r(0))}+\int_{0}^{t}\rho^{\gamma}(r(s),s)ds

(4.55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{r(s)}^{a(s)}\frac{2}{y}\rho u^{2}dyds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{r(\tau)}^{a(\tau)}\frac{4\pi\rho}{y^{2}}\int_{\xi}^{y}\rho(s,\tau)s^{2}dsdyd\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{r(t)}^{a(t)}\rho udy-\int_{r_{0}}^{a_{0}}\rho udy.

Making use of Lemma
4.3
, we have the following controls

\int_{r(t)}^{a(t)}\rho udy\leq\frac{1}{r^{2}(x,t)}\left(\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho u^{2}y^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\xi}^{a(t)}\rho y^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq CE_{0}^{\frac{2}{3(\gamma-1)}+\frac{1}{2}}M^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{-\frac{2\gamma}{3(\gamma-1)}},

(4.56)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{r(s)}^{a(t)}\frac{2}{y}\rho u^{2}dyds\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{r^{3}(x,s)}\int_{r(s)}^{a(t)}\rho u^{2}y^{2}dyds

(4.57)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4E_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{r^{3}(x,s)}ds\leq C_{\gamma}TE_{0}^{1+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}x^{-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{r(\tau)}^{a(\tau)}\frac{4\pi\rho}{y^{2}}\int_{\xi}^{y}\rho(s,\tau)s^{2}dsdyd\tau

(4.58)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{r^{3}(x,\tau)}\int_{r(\tau)}^{a(\tau)}4\pi\rho y\int_{\xi}^{y}\rho(s,\tau)s^{2}dsdyd\tau\leq C_{\gamma}TE_{0}^{1+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}x^{-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}},

which yield

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\log\frac{\rho(r(t),t)}{\rho(r(0))}\leq C_{1}(x,E_{0},M,T),

(4.59)

so

\rho(r(t),t)\leq\rho(r(0))\exp\left(\frac{C(x,E_{0},M,T)}{\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta}\right).

(4.60)

on the other hand,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta\right)\log\frac{\rho(r(t),t)}{\rho(r(0))}\geq-C_{1}(x,E_{0},M,T)-\int_{0}^{t}\rho^{\gamma}(r(s),s)ds

(4.61)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-C_{1}(x,E_{0},M,T)-T\exp\left(\frac{\gamma C(x,E_{0},M,T)}{\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta}\right)\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\gamma}=:-\tilde{C}(x,E_{0},M,T),

which gives

\rho(r(t),t)\geq\rho(r(0))\exp\left(\frac{\tilde{C}(x,E_{0},M,T)}{\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon+\eta}\right).

(4.62)

∎

4.1.3

In this subsection, we prove several further weighted
L^{2}
estimates for temporal and spatial derivatives of
\rho
and

away from the origin in Lagrangian coordinates, which are crucial to the convergence of the free boundary.

We introduce the following cutoff functions. Let
0<x_{0}<x_{1}<x_{2}<M
. Let
\phi,\ \zeta\in C^{\infty}[0,M]
such that

0\leq\phi(x)\leq 1,\ \phi(x)=1\text{ for }x\in[x_{1},M],\ \phi(x)=0\text{ for }x\in[0,x_{0}+\frac{x_{1}-x_{0}}{2}],

0\leq\zeta(x)\leq 1,\ \zeta(x)=1\text{ for }x\in[x_{2},M],\ \zeta(x)=0\text{ for }x\in[0,x_{1}+\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{2}],

0\leq\zeta^{\prime}(x)\leq\frac{C}{x_{2}-x_{1}}.

Let
\varphi=\phi^{2},\ \psi=\zeta^{2}
. Then

|\varphi^{\prime}|=2\phi|\phi^{\prime}|\leq C\sqrt{\varphi},\ |\psi^{\prime}|=2\zeta|\zeta^{\prime}|\leq C\sqrt{\psi}.

(4.63)

We recall the effective viscous flux

\sigma=\rho^{\gamma}-(\eta+\frac{4}{3}\varepsilon)(4\pi\rho r^{2}\partial_{x}u+\frac{2u}{r}),

(4.64)

which is an important quantity in the estimates of derivatives of
\rho
and

∎

4.2.2
Convergence near the origin

As for the region near the origin, the solutions have lower regularities. We establish the compactness argument by adapting the idea from
[
21

. The difference is that the spatial domain changes in different time

Since

) and Lemma

\displaystyle\rho^{\xi_{j}}\rightharpoonup\rho_{in}\text{ weak* in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}([0,r_{in}(t)),r^{2}dr)),

\displaystyle\rho^{\xi_{j}}\rightharpoonup\rho_{in}\text{ weakly in }L^{2\gamma}(0,T;L^{2\gamma}_{loc}((0,r_{in}(t)),r^{2}dr)),

\displaystyle u^{\xi_{j}}\rightharpoonup u_{in}\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}([0,r_{in}(t)),r^{2}dr)).

By lower semicontinuity,

\displaystyle\rho_{in}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}([0,r_{in}(t)),r^{2}dr))\cap L^{2\gamma}(0,T;L^{2\gamma}_{loc}((0,r_{in}(t)),r^{2}dr)),

(4.132)

\displaystyle u_{in}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}([0,r_{in}(t)),r^{2}dr)),

\displaystyle\frac{u_{in}}{r}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}([0,r_{in}(t)),r^{2}dr)).

We need to show that
(\rho_{in},u_{in})
satisfies equations (
2.4
) in the weak sense on the set
\Omega_{in}:=\{(r,t):0\leq t\leq T,\ 0\leq r\leq r_{in}(t)\}
. We denote the weak limit of

by

as

In the proofs of the following several lemmas, we will apply a generalized version of the Aubin-Lions lemma, that is, Lemma

in the Appendix. For any function

By the fact that

and

satisfies the conditions in Lemma

The main difficulty to prove

indeed converges to a solution on

is the pressure term. Convexity only implies

for

for

for

Appendix B
Derivation of (
3.7
)

In this section, we briefly discuss the approach in
[
24
]
and derive (
3.7
).

We apply Hölder inequality to the gravitational term:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\varPhi|^{2}d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\varPhi d\boldsymbol{x}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{p}(B(0;a(t)))}\|\varPhi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

(B.1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(B(0;a(t)))}^{\theta}\|\rho\|^{1-\theta}_{L^{\gamma}(B(0;a(t)))}\|\varPhi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

where
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{\gamma}+\frac{1-\theta}{1}
. By Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality,

\|\varPhi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})^{3}}\leq A_{\gamma}\|\rho\|_{L^{\gamma}(B(0;a(t)))},

(B.2)

where
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{2}{3}
. So

\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\varPhi|^{2}d\boldsymbol{x}\leq\frac{1}{2}A_{\gamma}\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(B(0;a(t)))}^{1+\theta}\|\rho\|^{1-\theta}_{L^{\gamma}(B(0;a(t)))},

(B.3)

where
1+\theta=\frac{\gamma}{3(\gamma-1)}
,
1-\theta=\frac{5\gamma-6}{3(\gamma-1)}
. Since
\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\varPhi|^{2}d\boldsymbol{x}=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\rho(\boldsymbol{x})\rho(\boldsymbol{y})}{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{y}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\int_{B(0;a(t))}\rho^{\gamma}d\boldsymbol{x}-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\rho(\boldsymbol{x})\rho(\boldsymbol{y})}{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{y}

(B.4)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\int_{B(0;a(t))}\rho^{\gamma}d\boldsymbol{x}-BM^{\frac{5\gamma-6}{3(\gamma-1)}}\left(\int_{B(0;a(t))}\rho^{\gamma}d\boldsymbol{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{3(\gamma-1)}}

for some positive constant
B
.

Now recall

\tilde{E_{0}}=4\pi\int_{0}^{a_{0}}\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}u_{0}^{2}r^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho_{0}^{\gamma}r^{2}dr,

consider the function

f(s)=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}s-BM^{\frac{5\gamma-6}{3(\gamma-1)}}s^{\frac{1}{3(\gamma-1)}}\ \ (s>0),

(B.5)

we can easily find the maximum of the function is attained at
s^{*}=(\frac{B}{3})^{-\frac{3(\gamma-1)}{4-3\gamma}}M^{-\frac{5\gamma-6}{4-3\gamma}}
,

f(s^{*})=\frac{4-3\gamma}{\gamma-1}(\frac{B}{3})^{-\frac{3(\gamma-1)}{4-3\gamma}}M^{-\frac{5\gamma-6}{4-3\gamma}},

(B.6)

and
f(s)
is strictly increasing in
(0,s^{*})
. Recall that in
[
24
]
, the critical mass is defined as follows

M<M_{c}=\left(\frac{4-3\gamma}{\gamma-1}(\frac{B}{3})^{-\frac{3(\gamma-1)}{4-3\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{4-3\gamma}{5\gamma-6}}(\frac{\tilde{E_{0}}}{4\pi})^{-\frac{4-3\gamma}{5\gamma-6}}.

By direct computation,

s^{*}=(\frac{B}{3})^{-\frac{3(\gamma-1)}{4-3\gamma}}M^{-\frac{5\gamma-6}{4-3\gamma}}>(\frac{B}{3})^{-\frac{3(\gamma-1)}{4-3\gamma}}M^{-\frac{5\gamma-6}{4-3\gamma}}_{c}=\frac{\gamma-1}{4-3\gamma}\frac{\tilde{E_{0}}}{4\pi}>2(\gamma-1)\frac{\tilde{E_{0}}}{4\pi},

(B.7)

and

f(s^{*})=\frac{4-3\gamma}{\gamma-1}(\frac{B}{3})^{-\frac{3(\gamma-1)}{4-3\gamma}}M^{-\frac{5\gamma-6}{4-3\gamma}}>\frac{1}{4\pi}\tilde{E_{0}}.

(B.8)

On the other hand, it follows from the basic energy estimate that

f\left(\int_{0}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}r^{2}dr\right)\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}\tilde{E_{0}}.

(B.9)

Since
4\pi\int_{0}^{a_{0}}\rho_{0}^{\gamma}r^{2}dr\leq(\gamma-1)\tilde{E_{0}}<4\pi s^{*}

for

. The following shows how the gravitational term is controlled

\displaystyle 3\int_{0}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}r^{2}dr-4\pi\int_{0}^{a(t)}\rho r\int_{0}^{r}\rho s^{2}dsdr

\displaystyle\geq

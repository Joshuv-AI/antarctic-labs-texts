Mean-field backward stochastic Volterra integral equations: well-posedness and related particle systems
Tao Hao, Ying Hu, Jiaqiang Wen

Abstract




Abstract:
This paper studies the mean-field backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (mean-field BSVIEs) and associated particle systems.
We establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions to mean-field BSVIEs when the generator
g
is of linear growth or quadratic growth with respect to
Z
, respectively.
Moreover, the propagation of chaos is analyzed for the corresponding particle systems under two conditions. When
g
is of linear growth in
Z
, the convergence rate is proven to be of order
{\cal Q}(N)
.
When
g
is of quadratic growth in
Z
and is independent of the law of
Z
, we not only establish the convergence of the particle systems but also derive a convergence rate of order
{\cal O}(N^{-\frac{1}{2\lambda}})
, where
\lambda>1
.

Key words:
Backward stochastic Volterra integral equation; mean-field; particle system; quadratic growth; convergence rate

AMS subject classifications.
60H10; 60H30

1
Introduction

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space, where a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion
W
is defined, and the completed augmented natural filtration is denoted by
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}
. In 1990, Pardoux and Peng
[
22
]
first established the existence and uniqueness of solutions to nonlinear backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs, for short) under Lipschitz assumptions.
Since then, this theory has found extensive applications in stochastic optimal control, partial differential equations, and financial mathematics. One important application of BSDEs in economics is their use in modeling stochastic differential utility (SDU, for short), as illustrated below:

Y(t)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\xi+\int_{t}^{T}h(s,Y(s))ds\Big|\mathscr{F}_{t}\Big],

where
\xi
is a square-integrable random variable,
h:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an appropriate function, and
\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\mathscr{F}_{t}]
represents the conditional expectation under the probability
\mathbb{P}
.
This SDU can be interpreted as the solution to a particular BSDE with terminal value
\xi
and generator
h
(see Duffie and Epstein
[
5
]
). Moreover, El Karoui, Peng, and Quenez
[
6
]
extended their work by introducing the concept of a general SDU. Nonetheless, the standard expected utility function (EUF, for short) is typically expressed as

Y(t)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\xi e^{\gamma(T-t)}+\int_{t}^{T}u(c(s))e^{-\gamma(s-t)}ds\Big|\mathscr{F}_{t}\Big],

where
u(\cdot)
is a utility function,
c(\cdot)
is a consumption process, and
\gamma
is the discount rate.
The standard EUF, which includes a consumption process, a discount rate, and a utility function, emphasizes that both the terminal value
\overline{\psi}(t)\equiv\xi e^{\gamma(T-t)}
and the generator
\overline{g}(t,s)\equiv u(c(s))e^{-\gamma(s-t)}
are time-dependent and have memory effects, i.e., a feature not naturally captured by classical BSDEs. To better describe this phenomenon, many researchers have turned it to be backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (BSVIEs, for short) of the following form:

Y(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,Y(s),Z(t,s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

For this class of equations, Lin
[
21
]
established the existence and uniqueness of adapted solutions under Lipschitz conditions,
Yong
[
32
]
introduced a class of general BSVIEs and proved their well-posedness.
Readers may refer to
[
11
,
12
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
for new developments of BSVIEs and their applications.
Note that although the Lipschitz condition facilitates mathematical derivations, it often restricts the range of interesting applications, such as risk-sensitive problems.
To address this limitation, Peng
[
23
]
proposed relaxing the Lipschitz assumption on the generator
g
when studying the existence and/or uniqueness of adapted solutions to BSDEs. This suggestion has inspired numerous research efforts in the field.
For instance, Lepeltier and San Martin
[
19
]
studied the case where the generator
g
is continuous in
(Y,Z)
and has linear growth.
Kobylanski
[
18
]
, Briand and Hu
[
3
,
4
]
, as well as Bahlali, Eddahbi, and Ouknine
[
1
]
,
worked on one-dimensional BSDEs with quadratic growth (QBSDEs).
Hu and Tang
[
15
]
, Xing and Zitkovic
[
31
]
, and Fan, Hu, and Tang
[
8
]
focused on multi-dimensional QBSDEs.
For BSVIEs, similar efforts have been made to relax the Lipschitz assumption on the generator

g
. For instance, Shi and Wang
[
24
]
investigated general BSVIEs under non-Lipschitz conditions. Moreover, Wang, Sun, and Yong
[
28
]
extended Lin’s work
[
21
]
from linear growth to quadratic growth and established the well-posedness of both local and global solutions for BSVIEs with quadratic growth.

However, to the best of our knowledge, no fundamental results have yet been established for general mean-field BSVIEs (i.e., the generator
g
depends on the distribution of the solution
(Y,Z))
, let alone for related particle systems. Note that the work of Shi, Wang, and Yong
[
25
]
dealt with classical mean-field BSVIEs, in which the generator
g
depends on the expectation of the solution
(Y,Z)
; meanwhile, Jiang, Li, and Wei
[
16
]
discussed a special case within the general setting.

In this paper, motivated by the wide applications of particle systems, we study general mean-field BSVIEs. Specifically, we focus on analyzing the convergence rate of particle systems associated with the following BSVIEs:
For
i=1,\cdots,N
,

\displaystyle Y^{N,i}(t)

\displaystyle=\psi^{i}(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,Y^{N,i}(s),Z^{N,i,i}(t,s),\mu^{N}(s),\nu^{N}(t,s))ds

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}Z^{N,i,j}(t,s)dW^{j}(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

where
\{W^{j};1\leqslant j\leqslant N\}
are
N
independent
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motions on the complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
, and

\mu^{N}(s)\triangleq\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{Y^{N,i}(s)}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\nu^{N}(t,s)\triangleq\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{Z^{N,i,i}(t,s)}.

(1.2)

The equation above is a multi-dimensional BSVIE. As the system size becomes very large, i.e., as
N\rightarrow\infty
, based on the theory of convergence under the Wasserstein distance for the empirical measure (as discussed in Fournier and Guillin
[
10
]
), the particle system described in Eq. (
1.1
) will converge in structure to the following mean-field BSVIE:

Y(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,Y(s),Z(t,s),\mathbb{P}_{Y(s)},\mathbb{P}_{Z(t,s)})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

(1.3)

The process
\psi(\cdot)
and the mapping
g
are referred to as the free term and the generator, respectively. We denote equation (
1.3
) as a general mean-field BSVIE. The term “general” here indicates that the generator

g
depends on the distributions of
Y(s)
and that of
Z(t,s)
, rather than merely their expectations. This convergence implies that various analyses related to the particle system in equation (
1.1
), such as error estimates of solutions and asymptotic Nash equilibrium, can be effectively addressed using the framework of equation (
1.3
).

The main goal of this paper is to study the convergence and its rate for the particle system described by (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).
Before studying our main results, we first establish the well-posedness of BSVIE (
1.3
). To obtain more general results, we consider both linear growth and quadratic growth cases.
For the linear growth case, we initially prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution to BSVIE (
1.3
) by parameterizing the time variable
t
and applying the contraction mapping principle (see
Theorem 3.2
). Subsequently, we derive a convergence rate of order
{\cal Q}(N)
by combining the
t
-parameterization method with convergence theory in the Wasserstein distance for empirical measures (see
Theorem 4.2
).

Note that when
g
depends on the law of
Z(\cdot,\cdot)
, we need to work within the space

L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
1<p<2
, because we only have that
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}|\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)|^{2}dsdt<\infty

(see (
4.11
)). However, when
g
is independent of the law of
Z(\cdot,\cdot)
, we can work in the space

L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p>1
(see
4.4
).
Furthermore, even for general mean-field BSDEs with Lipschitz conditions, our result concerning the convergence rate is new. To the best of our knowledge, all previous works assume that the generator is independent of the law of
Z(\cdot)
.

For the quadratic growth case, we first establish the existence and uniqueness of global solutions to BSVIE (
1.3
) using BMO martingale techniques, considering the generator
g
under both bounded and unbounded conditions with respect to the distribution
\mathbb{P}_{Z(t,s)}
(see
Theorem 3.5
and
Theorem 3.8
). Next, under the extra assumption that
g
is independent of the law of
Z(\cdot,\cdot)
,
we prove the convergence for the particle system, using BMO techniques and Girsanov’s theorem (see
Theorem 4.6
).
Finally, based on these convergence results, we derive a convergence rate of order
{\cal O}(N^{-\frac{1}{2\lambda}})
with
\lambda>1
for the particle system (see
Theorem 4.7
).
In summary, the main innovations of this work include:
(i) Providing, for the first time, the convergence and precise convergence rates of particle systems for mean-field BSVIEs.
(ii) Allowing the generator
g
to depend on the distributions of
Y(t)
and
Z(t,s)
rather than merely on their expectations.
(iii) Allowing the generator
g
to be of quadratic growth in
Z(t,s)
.
(iv) Establishing new existence and uniqueness results when the generator
g
is of linear growth in
Z(t,s)
.
(v)
2
and
3
here are weaker than the condition (A2) of Wang, Sun, and Yong
[
28
]
, as we relax the boundedness requirements of the generator
g
in both
(t,s)
and its dependence on distributions.

This paper is organized as follows.
Section 2
introduces some notations and spaces.
Section 3
presents the solvability of general mean-field BSVIEs.
Section 4
addresses the convergence and the convergence rate of associated particle systems.
Finally, several supporting results are presented in
Section 5
.

This section introduces some useful spaces and properties of BMO martingales.
We begin with the introduction of the upper triangle domains. For
0\leqslant a<b\leqslant T
, denote

\displaystyle\Delta[a,b]

\displaystyle=\Big\{(t,s)\in[a,b]\Big|a\leqslant t\leqslant s\leqslant b\Big\}.

In what follows, we denote
\delta_{0}
the Dirac measure at
0
and
\mathscr{T}[a,b]
the set of all
\mathbb{F}
-stopping times
\tau
valued in
[a,b]
.
For almost all
t\in[0,T]
and for stopping time
\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]
,
by
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}^{t}_{\tau}[\cdot]:=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}^{t}[\cdot|\mathscr{F}_{\tau}]

2.1
Useful spaces

Let us introduce spaces of random variables and stochastic processes. To avoid repetition,
all processes
(t,\omega)\mapsto f(t,\omega)
are assumed to be at least
\mathscr{B}[a,b]\otimes\mathscr{F}_{b}
-measurable without further mention, where
\mathscr{B}[a,b]
is the Borel-field on
[a,b].
For

and
r>0

\displaystyle L^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{b}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})=

\displaystyle\ \Big\{\xi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\Big|\xi\ \text{is}\ \mathscr{F}_{b}\text{-measurable},\|\xi\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{b}}(\Omega)}:=\mathbb{E}[|\xi|^{p}]<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle L^{q,p}_{\mathscr{F}_{b}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n})=

\displaystyle\ \Big\{f:\Omega\times[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{a}^{b}|f(s)|^{q}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle L^{\infty,p}_{\mathscr{F}_{b}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n})=

\displaystyle\ \Big\{f:\Omega\times[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big(\mathop{\rm esssup}\limits_{t\in[a,b]}|f(t)|^{p}\Big)<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle L^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{b}}(\Omega;C([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n}))=

\displaystyle\ \Big\{f:\Omega\times[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\Big|t\mapsto f(t,\omega)\ \text{is\ continuous},\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup\limits_{t\in[a,b]}|f(t)|^{p}\Big)<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle L^{\infty}_{\mathscr{F}_{b}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n})\triangleq

\displaystyle\ L^{\infty,\infty}_{\mathscr{F}_{b}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Big\{f:\Omega\times[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\Big|\mathop{\rm esssup}\limits_{(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[a,b]}|f(t)|<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{r}_{\mathscr{F}_{b}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})=

\displaystyle\ \Big\{\xi\in L^{1}_{\mathscr{F}_{b}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big|\ \mathbb{E}[\exp(r|\xi|)]<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{r}_{\mathscr{F}_{b}}(\Omega;L^{p}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n}))=

\displaystyle\ \Big\{f:\Omega\times[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\Big|\big(\int_{a}^{b}|f(s)|^{p}ds\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\in{\cal E}^{r}_{\mathscr{F}_{b}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big\}.

In addition, the subset of

\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes,

-progressively measurable
version of the above other spaces can be understood in the same way. In analogy with

we set

\displaystyle L^{q,p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Big\{

\displaystyle f(\cdot,\cdot):\Omega\times\Delta[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ f(\cdot,\cdot)\ \text{is}\ \mathscr{B}(\Delta[a,b])\otimes\mathscr{F}_{b}\text{-measurable\ such }\text{that}

\displaystyle s\mapsto f(t,s)\ \text{is}\ \mathbb{F}\text{-progressively\ measurable\ for\ all}\ t\in[a,b],\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\|f(\cdot,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L^{q,p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[a,b])}=\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{a}^{b}\int_{t}^{b}|f(t,s)|^{q}dsdt\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}<\infty\Big\}.

The space
L^{q,p}_{\mathbb{F}}([a,b]\times[b,c];\mathbb{R}^{n})

Similarly, one can understand

and so on.

2.2
BMO martingale

For
0\leqslant a<b\leqslant T
, let
M=\{M(t),\mathscr{F}_{t};a\leqslant t\leqslant b\}
be a uniformly integrable martingale with
M(0)=0
, and we set, for
q\geqslant 2
,

\|M\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P},q}[a,b]}\triangleq\sup_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[a,b]}\bigg\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(\langle M\rangle(b)-\langle M\rangle(\tau)\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\infty}.

We write
\|M\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P},2}[a,b]}
as
\|M\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]}
for short.
If
\|M\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]}<\infty
, then
M
is called a
BMO martingale
on
[a,b].

The space of all BMO martingales on
[a,b]
is denoted by
\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]
.
Note that
\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]
is a Banach space under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]}.

We now recall the following properties of BMO martingales, which can be found in He, Wang, Yan
[
14
]

and Kazamaki
[
17
]
.

\bullet

Denote by
{\cal E}(M)
the Doléans–Dade exponential of a continuous local martingale
M
, i.e.,

{\cal E}(M)_{a}^{b}=\exp\{M_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\langle M\rangle_{t}\}
, for any
t\in[a,b]
.
If
M\in\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]
, then
{\cal E}(M)
is a uniformly integrable martingale.

\bullet

The energy inequality: If
M\in\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]
, then for any positive integer
n
, one has

\mathbb{E}[(\langle M\rangle(b))^{n}]\leqslant n!\|M\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]}^{2n}.

\bullet

For
L>0
, there exists two positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending on
L
such that, for any
BMO martingale
M
on
[a,b]
and any one-dimensional BMO martingale
N
such that
\|N\|_{{}_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]}}\leqslant L
, we have

c_{1}\|M\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]}\leqslant\|\widetilde{M}\|_{\text{BMO}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}[a,b]}\leqslant c_{2}\|M\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]},

(2.1)

where
\widetilde{M}\triangleq M-\langle M,N\rangle
and
d\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\triangleq{\cal E}(N)_{a}^{b}d\mathbb{P}
.

\bullet

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
M
be a one-dimensional continuous BMO martingale.
If
\|M\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]}<\Phi(p)
, then
{\cal E}(M)
satisfies the reverse Hölder’s inequality:

\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[{\cal E}(M)_{\tau}^{\infty}\right]^{p}\leqslant c_{p},

(2.2)

for any stopping time
\tau
, with a positive constant
c_{p}
depending only on
p
.

Next, we introduce some spaces associated with BMO martingales. Let
0\leqslant a<b<c\leqslant T
, denote

\displaystyle{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n})=

\displaystyle\ \Big\{f:\Omega\times[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\big|f(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle\quad\|f(\cdot)\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}[a,b]}\triangleq\sup\limits_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[a,b]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{b}|f(s)|^{2}ds\Big]\Big\|_{\infty}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n})=

\displaystyle\ \Big\{f:\Omega\times\Delta[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\big|f(\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle\quad\|f(\cdot,\cdot)\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[a,b])}\triangleq\mathop{\rm esssup}\limits_{t\in[a,b]}\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}[t,b]}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([a,b]\times[b,c];\mathbb{R}^{n})=

\displaystyle\ \Big\{f:\Omega\times[a,b]\times[b,c]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\big|f(\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([a,b]\times[b,c];\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle\quad\|f(\cdot,\cdot)\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([a,b]\times[b,c])}\triangleq\mathop{\rm esssup}\limits_{t\in[a,b]}\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}[b,c]}<\infty\Big\}.

Remark 2.1
.

For
0\leqslant a\leqslant t\leqslant b\leqslant T
,
the process
\int_{a}^{\cdot}f(r)dW(r)
(denoted by
f\cdot W
) belongs to
\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]
if and only if
f\in{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, i.e.,

\|f\cdot W\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[a,b]}\equiv\|f(\cdot)\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}[a,b]}.

(2.3)

The process
\int_{t}^{\cdot}f(t,r)dW(r)
belongs to
\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[t,b]
for almost all
t\in[a,b)

if and only if
f(\cdot,\cdot)\in{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, i.e.,

\|f\cdot W\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Delta[a,b])}\triangleq\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[a,b]}\|f(t)\cdot W\|_{\text{BMO}_{\mathbb{P}}[t,b]}=\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[a,b]}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([t,b])}\equiv\|f(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[a,b])}.

Definition 2.2
.

A pair of processes
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}[0,T]\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})

(resp.,
L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}[0,T]\times{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
)
is called an
adapted solution
of the general mean-field BSVIE (
1.3
) in the linear growth case (resp., quadratic growth case) if it satisfies the equation in the usual Itô’s sense for Lebesgue almost all
t\in[0,T]
.

Finally, we recall the Wasserstein distance to end this section.
For
q\geqslant 1
, let
\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the set of all probability measures
\mu
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
with finite
q
-th moment, i.e.,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{q}\mu(dx)<\infty
. Here
\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the Borel
\sigma
-field over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In addition, the set
\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is endowed with the following
q
-Wasserstein metric: for
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{q}(\mu,\nu)\triangleq\inf\bigg\{\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{q}\rho(dxdy)\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Bigm|\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),\ \rho(\cdot\times\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mu(\cdot),\ \rho(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\cdot)=\nu(\cdot)\bigg\}.

Note that for
\xi,\eta\in{\cal P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

Solvability of Global Solutions

In this section, we would like to prove the global solvability of BSVIE (

Before presenting the main results, we first introduce the

-parameterization approach for mean-field BSVIEs.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any given
(y(\cdot),z(\cdot,\cdot))
and for almost all

,
if the following mean-field BSDE

{\cal Y}(t,s)=\psi(t)+\int_{s}^{T}g(t,r,y(r),{\cal Z}(t,r),\mathbb{P}_{y(r)},\mathbb{P}_{{\cal Z}(t,r)})dr-\int_{s}^{T}{\cal Z}(t,r)dW(r),\quad s\in[t,T]

({\cal Y}(t,\cdot),{\cal Z}(t,\cdot))

then the following mean-field BSVIE

Y(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,y(s),Z(t,s),\mathbb{P}_{y(s)},\mathbb{P}_{Z(t,s)})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T]

(3.2)

also admits a unique solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle Y(t)=\mathcal{Y}(t,t)\quad\text{and}\quad Z(t,s)=\mathcal{Z}(t,s)\quad\text{for}\ (t,s)\in\Delta[0,T].

Proof.

For the existence, for any fixed
t\in[0,T]
, we
set

Y(t)\triangleq{\cal Y}(t,t)\quad\hbox{and}\quad Z(t,s)\triangleq{\cal Z}(t,s),\quad(t,s)\in\Delta[0,T].

Obviously,
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
is a solution of BSVIE (
3.2
).

Regarding uniqueness, suppose
(\overline{Y}(\cdot),\overline{Z}(\cdot,\cdot))
is another adapted solution of BSVIE (
3.2
). By
(\overline{{\cal Y}}(t,\cdot),\overline{{\cal Z}}(t,\cdot))
we denote the unique adapted solution of the following mean-field BSDE (parameterized by
t\in[0,T]
):

\overline{\mathcal{Y}}(t,s)=\psi(t)+\int_{s}^{T}g(t,r,y(r),\overline{Z}(t,r),\mathbb{P}_{y(r)},\mathbb{P}_{\overline{Z}(t,r)})dr-\int_{s}^{T}\overline{{\cal Z}}(t,r)dW(r),\quad s\in[t,T].

(3.3)

Setting
s=t
and taking the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\cdot](=\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\mathscr{F}_{t}])
on both sides of BSDE (
3.3
), and then comparing the resulting expression with the BSVIE satisfied by
(\overline{Y}(\cdot),\overline{Z}(\cdot,\cdot))
, i.e.,

\overline{Y}(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,y(s),\overline{Z}(t,s),\mathbb{P}_{y(s)},\mathbb{P}_{\overline{Z}(t,s)})ds-\int_{t}^{T}\overline{Z}(t,s)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

we find that for almost all

\overline{Y}(t)=\overline{{\cal Y}}(t,t)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\overline{Z}(t,s)=\overline{{\cal Z}}(t,s),\quad s\in[t,T].

Substituting (
3.4
) into (

) yields that

\overline{\mathcal{Y}}(t,s)=\psi(t)+\int_{s}^{T}g(t,r,y(r),\overline{{\cal Z}}(t,r),\mathbb{P}_{y(r)},\mathbb{P}_{\overline{{\cal Z}}(t,r)})dr-\int_{s}^{T}\overline{{\cal Z}}(t,r)dW(r),\quad s\in[t,T].

Now, comparing the above equation with (

Therefore, one has

This completes the proof.
∎

is defined in (
3.17
) with
\delta
replaced by
\gamma_{0}
.
In addition, similar to the relation (
5.12
) in Appendix, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{r}\int_{r}^{T}\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{4}|{\cal Z}(t,u)|^{2}du\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}|{\cal Y}(t,r)|+\varepsilon_{0}(K_{1}+K_{3})+L_{1}T+\varepsilon_{0}(\beta+\beta_{0})\int_{r}^{T}\|Y(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}[u,T]}du.

(3.19)

Now, by taking the expectation on both sides of (
3.19
) firstly and then inserting it into (
3.18
), we have

\displaystyle|{\cal Y}(t,r)|

\displaystyle\leqslant(K_{1}+K_{3})(\varepsilon_{0}+1)+L_{1}T+L_{2,\gamma_{0}}T+\varepsilon_{0}\mathbb{E}|{\cal Y}(t,r)|

\displaystyle+\int_{r}^{T}(\beta+\beta_{0})(1+\varepsilon_{0})\|Y(\cdot)\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}[u,T]}du.

By taking the expectation on both sides of the above inequality and letting
\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}|{\cal Y}(t,r)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{3}{2}(K_{1}+K_{3})+L_{1}T+L_{2,\gamma_{0}}T+\frac{3}{2}(\beta+\beta_{0})\int_{r}^{T}\|Y(\cdot)\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}[u,T]}du.

Combining the above two inequalities provides

\displaystyle|{\cal Y}(t,r)|

\displaystyle\leqslant 3(K_{1}+K_{3})+2L_{1}T+2L_{2,\gamma_{0}}T+3(\beta+\beta_{0})\int_{r}^{T}\|Y(\cdot)\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}[u,T]}du.

(3.20)

In particular, when
r=t
, we deduce by
3.1
that

\displaystyle|Y(t)|

\displaystyle\leqslant 3(K_{1}+K_{3})+2L_{1}T+2L_{2,\gamma_{0}}T+3(\beta+\beta_{0})\int_{t}^{T}\|Y(\cdot)\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}[u,T]}du.

(3.21)

In order to further obtain the estimate of
Y(\cdot)
, we consider the following ordinary differential equation (ODE)

\alpha(t)=L_{7}+\int_{t}^{T}L_{7}\alpha(u)du,\quad t\in[0,T],

whose solution is expressly given by
\alpha(t)=L_{7}e^{L_{7}(T-t)},\ t\in[0,T].
Note that
\alpha(\cdot)
is a continuous, non-increasing function, and

\|\psi(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}_{{\cal F}_{T}}[0,T]}\leqslant L_{7}=\alpha(T)\leqslant\alpha(0).

Then, from (
3.21
), we have

\|Y(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}[t,T]}\leqslant\alpha(t)\leqslant\alpha(0),\ \forall t\in[T-\kappa_{0},T].

(3.22)

Inserting (
3.22
) into (
3.20
), we deduce

\displaystyle|{\cal Y}(t,r)|

\displaystyle\leqslant 3(K_{1}+K_{3})+2L_{1}T+2L_{2,\gamma_{0}}T+3(\beta+\beta_{0})T\alpha(0)\triangleq\widetilde{M}.

Set

M_{1}\triangleq\max\{\alpha(0),\widetilde{M}\}.

Thence, we have that for any
(t,r)\in\Delta[T-\kappa_{0},T]
,

\|Y(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}[t,T]}\leqslant M_{1}\quad\hbox{and}\quad|{\cal Y}(t,r)|\leqslant M_{1}.

(3.23)

On the other hand,
by letting
\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
, then (
3.19
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{r}\int_{r}^{T}\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}}{8}|{\cal Z}(t,u)|^{2}du

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}|{\cal Y}(t,r)|+\frac{K_{1}+K_{3}}{2}+L_{1}T+\frac{\beta+\beta_{0}}{2}\int_{r}^{T}\|Y(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}[u,T]}du

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\Big(M_{1}+K_{1}+K_{3}+2L_{1}T+(\beta+\beta_{0})TM_{1}\Big).

Note that (
3.24
) still holds if we replace
\gamma
by stopping time
\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]
.
Finally, by combining (
3.23
), (
3.24
) and recalling
the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[T-{\kappa_{0}},T])}
, we obtain

\|Z(\cdot,\cdot)\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[T-\kappa_{0},T])}\leqslant\frac{4}{\widetilde{\gamma}}\Big(M_{1}+K_{1}+K_{3}+2L_{1}T+(\beta+\beta_{0})TM_{1}\Big)\triangleq M_{2}.

Taking
T-\kappa_{0}
as the terminal time and
\psi^{T-\kappa_{\varrho}}(t)={\cal Y}(t,T-\kappa_{0})
as the terminal value,
making a similar analysis as the proof of
Theorem 3.5
,
it is easy to prove the well-posedness and boundedness of global solutions to BSVIE (
1.3
) on the interval
[0,T]
. This completes the proof.
∎

4
Particle Systems

This section focuses on the convergence and convergence rate of the particle system in Eq. (
1.1
) associated with Eq. (
1.3
).
Let
{\psi^{i}(\cdot);1\leqslant i\leqslant N}
be
N
independent copies of the free term
\psi(\cdot)
, and let
\mathbb{F}^{N}={\mathscr{F}^{N}_{t}},{t\geqslant 0}
denote the natural filtration of
W^{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant N
, augmented by all
\mathbb{P}
-null sets. Here,
W^{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant N
are
N
independent
d
-dimensional Brownian motions, as introduced earlier.

In the following, we use the function:
\delta_{ij}=1
, if
i=j
; or else, it equals
0
.
Moreover, we always assume that
s\mapsto g(t,s,y,z,\mu,\nu)
is
\mathbb{F}^{N}
-progressively measurable for
all
(t,y,z,\mu,\nu)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Additionally, let
(Y^{N,i}(\cdot),Z^{N,i,j}(\cdot,\cdot))
be the adapted solutions to the
particle system (
1.1
), and let
(\overline{Y}^{i}(\cdot),\overline{Z}^{i}(\cdot,\cdot))
be the solution to
the following mean-field BSVIE:

\displaystyle\overline{Y}^{i}(t)=\psi^{i}(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,\overline{Y}^{i}(s),\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s),\overline{\mu}(s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)dW^{i}(s),

(4.1)

where

\displaystyle\overline{\mu}(s)\triangleq\mathbb{P}_{\overline{Y}^{i}(s)}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\overline{\nu}(t,s)\triangleq\mathbb{P}_{\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)}.

(4.2)

Next, following the approach analogous to that in
Section 3
, we study the convergence and convergence rate of the particle system
(
1.1
) by dividing the analysis into two cases: the linear growth case and the quadratic growth case.

4.1
Linear growth case

In this subsection, we study the convergence rate of the particle system (
1.1
) in the case where
g
is of linear growth in
(y,z,\mu,\nu)
.
First, we give the convergence of the particle system (
1.1
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
1
be in force and
g
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
(\mu,\nu)
in 1-Wasserstein distance.
Let
(Y^{N,i}(\cdot),Z^{N,i,j}(\cdot,\cdot))_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}
,
(\overline{Y}^{i}(\cdot),\overline{Z}^{i}(\cdot,\cdot))
be the solutions of Eq. (
1.1
) and Eq. (
4.1
), respectively. Then, for
1<p<2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{N,i}(t)-\overline{Y}^{i}(t)|^{p}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|Z^{N,i,j}(t,s)-\delta_{ij}\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}dt\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\mu^{N}(t),\overline{\mu}(t))dt+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(t,r),\overline{\nu}(t,r))drdt.

Proof.

Thanks to Lin
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
, the multi-dimensional BSVIE (
1.1
) has a unique solution

(\mathbf{Y}^{N},\mathbf{Z}^{N})=(Y^{N,i},Z^{N,i,j})_{i,j=1,\cdots,N}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];(\mathbb{R}^{N\times d})^{N}).
By
(\mathbb{Y}^{N,i}(\cdot,\cdot),\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,i}(\cdot,\cdot))
,
we denote the unique solution to the following BSDE (parameterized by
t
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}^{N,i}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\psi^{i}(t)+\int_{s}^{T}g(t,r,Y^{N,i}(r),\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,i}(t,r),\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{Y^{N,i}}(r),\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,i}}(t,r))dr

(4.3)

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,j}(t,r)dW^{j}(r),\quad s\in[t,T].

It follows from the uniqueness of BSVIEs that

Y^{N,i}(t)=\mathbb{Y}^{N,i}(t,t)\quad\hbox{and}\quad Z^{N,i,j}(t,s)=\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,j}(t,s),\quad(t,s)\in\Delta[0,T].

(4.4)

Additionally,
3.1
provides that

\displaystyle\overline{Y}^{i}(t)=\overline{\mathbb{Y}}^{i}(t,t)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)=\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{i}(t,s),\quad(t,s)\in\Delta[0,T],

where for almost all
t\in[0,T]
, the pair

(\overline{\mathbb{Y}}^{i}(t,\cdot),\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{i}(t,\cdot))
solves the following equation

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{Y}}^{i}(t,s)=\psi^{i}(t)+\int_{s}^{T}g(t,r,\overline{Y}^{i}(r),\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{i}(t,r),\overline{\mu}(r),\mathbb{P}_{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{i}(t,r)})dr-\int_{s}^{T}\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{i}(t,r)dW^{i}(r),\quad s\in[t,T].

Next, for simplicity of presentation, we denote by
\Delta(\cdot)
the corresponding differences of solutions.
Then, according to Briand et al.
[
2
]
, it follows that for
1<p<2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[|\Delta\mathbb{Y}^{N,i}(t,s)|^{p}+\Big(\int_{s}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,j}(t,r)|^{2}dr\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{s}^{T}(g(t,r,Y^{N,i}(r),\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{i}(t,r),\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{Y^{N,i}}(r),\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,i}}(t,r))

\displaystyle-g(t,r,\overline{Y}^{i}(r),\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{i}(t,r),\overline{\mu}(r),\mathbb{P}_{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{i}(t,r)}))dr\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\Big(\mathbb{E}\int^{T}_{s}|\Delta Y^{N,i}(r)|^{p}dr+\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{T}{\cal W}_{1}^{p}(\mu^{N}(r),\overline{\mu}(r))dr\Big)

\displaystyle+C_{1}(T-s)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{T}{\cal W}_{1}^{p}(\nu^{N}(t,r),\overline{\nu}(t,r))dr,

where
C_{1}
depends on
L,p
and is independent of
T
.
Thanks to the definition of the
p
-Wasserstein metric, we have that for any
r>1
and
\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2}\in{\cal P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

{\cal W}_{1}^{r}(\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2})\leqslant{\cal W}_{r}^{r}(\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2}).

Taking
s=t
, it follows from Gronwall inequality that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y^{N,i}(t)|^{p}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\mu^{N}(r),\overline{\mu}(r))dr+C_{1}(T-t)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(t,r),\overline{\nu}(t,r))dr,

(4.5)

and

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta Z^{N,i,j}(t,r)|^{2}dr\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\mu^{N}(r),\overline{\mu}(r))dr+C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(s,r),\overline{\nu}(s,r))drds

(4.6)

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}(T-t)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(t,r),\overline{\nu}(t,r))dr.

Integrating from
0
to
T
on both sides of (
4.5
) and (
4.1
), we get the desired result.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
1
be in force and
g
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
(\mu,\nu)
in 1-Wasserstein distance.
Let
(Y^{N,i}(\cdot),Z^{N,i,j}(\cdot,\cdot))_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant N}
,
(\overline{Y}^{i}(\cdot),\overline{Z}^{i}(\cdot,\cdot))
be the unique solutions of Eq. (
1.1
) and Eq. (
4.1
), respectively.
Then, for any
1<p<2
, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
L
and
p
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{N,i}(t)-\overline{Y}^{i}(t)|^{p}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|Z^{N,i,j}(t,s)-\delta_{ij}\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}dt\Big]\leqslant C{\cal Q}(N),

where

{\cal Q}(N)=\begin{cases}N^{-(2-p)/2}&\text{as}\quad d=1,2,3,\\
N^{-\min\{p/d,(2-p)/2\}}&\text{as}\quad d\geqslant 4,\end{cases}

(4.7)

and
d
is the dimension of Brownian motion.

Proof.

For
i=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,N
,
let
(\widetilde{Y}^{N,i}(\cdot),\widetilde{Z}^{N,i}(\cdot,\cdot))
be i.i.d. copies of
(\overline{Y}^{i}(\cdot),\overline{Z}^{i}(\cdot,\cdot))

such that

\widetilde{Y}^{N,i}(t)=\psi^{i}(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,\widetilde{Y}^{N,i}(s),\widetilde{Z}^{N,i}(t,s),\bar{\mu}(s),\bar{\nu}(t,s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{Z}^{N,i}(t,s)dW^{i}(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

(4.8)

where
\overline{\mu}(\cdot)
is given in (
4.2
).
Then

\displaystyle Y^{N,i}(t)-\widetilde{Y}^{N,i}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}\Big(g(t,s,Y^{N,i}(s),Z^{N,i,i}(t,s),\mu^{N}(s),\nu^{N}(t,s))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-g(t,s,\widetilde{Y}^{N,i}(s),\widetilde{Z}^{N,i}(t,s),\overline{\mu}(s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))\Big)ds

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{t}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(Z^{N,i,j}(t,s)-\delta_{ij}\widetilde{Z}^{N,i}(t,s))dW^{j}(s).

Similar to (
4.5
) and (
4.1
), we have that for
1<p<2
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\bigg[|Y^{N,i}(t)-\widetilde{Y}^{N,i}(t)|^{p}+\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|Z^{N,i,j}(t,s)-\delta_{ij}\widetilde{Z}^{N,i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\mu^{N}(r),\overline{\mu}(r))dr+C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(s,r),\overline{\nu}(s,r))drds

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}(T-t)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))ds,

where
C_{1}
is a constant depending only on
L
and
p
, independent of
T
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\mu^{N}(t),\widetilde{\mu}^{N}(t))\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Big|Y^{N,i}(t)-\widetilde{Y}^{N,i}(t)\Big|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\mu^{N}(r),\overline{\mu}(r))dr+C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(s,r),\overline{\nu}(s,r))drds

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}(T-t)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))ds,

and

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\widetilde{\nu}^{N}(t,s))ds\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Big|Z^{N,i,i}(t,s)-\widetilde{Z}^{N,i}(t,s)\Big|^{p}ds\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\Big|Z^{N,i,i}(t,s)-\widetilde{Z}^{N,i}(t,s)\Big|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\mu^{N}(r),\overline{\mu}(r))dr+C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(s,r),\overline{\nu}(s,r))drds

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}(T-t)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))ds.

Here

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}^{N}(t)\triangleq\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\widetilde{Y}^{N,i}(t)}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\widetilde{\nu}^{N}(t,s)\triangleq\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\widetilde{Z}^{N,i}(t,s)}.

(4.9)

Then, it follows from the triangle inequality that

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\mu^{N}(t),\overline{\mu}(t))+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\mu^{N}(t),\widetilde{\mu}^{N}(t))+\mathbb{E}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\widetilde{\mu}^{N}(t),\overline{\mu}(t))

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\widetilde{\nu}^{N}(t,s))ds+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\widetilde{\nu}^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\widetilde{\mu}^{N}(t),\overline{\mu}(t))+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\widetilde{\nu}^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))ds

\displaystyle\quad+C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\mu^{N}(r),\overline{\mu}(r))dr+C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(s,r),\overline{\nu}(s,r))drds

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}(T-t)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}_{p}^{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))ds.

Taking a proper
\varepsilon^{*}
such that
C_{1}(\varepsilon^{*})^{p-1}=\frac{1}{2}
. Then, for any
t\in[T-\varepsilon^{*},T]
, it is easy to see that

C_{1}(T-t)^{p-1}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.
Thereby, Gronwall’s inequality provides

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\mu^{N}(t),\overline{\mu}(t))+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))ds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigg\{\mathbb{E}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\widetilde{\mu}^{N}(t),\overline{\mu}(t))+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\widetilde{\nu}^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))ds\bigg\}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\mu^{N}(t),\overline{\mu}(t))dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))dsdt

\displaystyle\leqslant C\Big(\mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\widetilde{\mu}^{N}(t),\overline{\mu}(t))dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\widetilde{\nu}^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))dsdt\Big).

According to Fournier and Guillin
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\widetilde{\mu}^{N}(t),\overline{\mu}(t))dt\leqslant{\cal Q}(N)\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}\{\mathbb{E}|\overline{Y}^{i}(t)|^{2}\}^{\frac{p}{2}}dt\leqslant{\cal Q}(N)\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}(1+\mathbb{E}|\overline{Y}^{i}(t)|^{2})dt<\infty,

(4.10)

and

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))dsdt

\displaystyle\leqslant{\cal Q}(N)\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}\{\mathbb{E}|\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)|^{2}\}^{\frac{p}{2}}dsdt\leqslant{\cal Q}(N)\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}(1+\mathbb{E}|\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)|^{2})dsdt<\infty.

(4.11)

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\mu^{N}(t),\overline{\mu}(t))+\mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))dsdt\leqslant C{\cal Q}(N).

Next, let us consider the time terminal
[T-2\varepsilon^{*},T-\varepsilon^{*}]
. On the one hand, making a similar analyses, one gets

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{T-2\varepsilon^{*}}^{T-\varepsilon^{*}}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\mu^{N}(t),\overline{\mu}(t))dt\leqslant C{\cal Q}(N).

On the other hand, we have, for
t\in[T-2\varepsilon^{*},T-\varepsilon^{*}]
,

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\int_{T-2\varepsilon^{*}}^{T-\varepsilon^{*}}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal W}^{p}_{p}(\nu^{N}(t,s),\overline{\nu}(t,s))dsdt\leqslant{\cal Q}(N)\int_{T-2\varepsilon^{*}}^{T-\varepsilon^{*}}\int_{t}^{T}\{\mathbb{E}|\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)|^{2}\}^{\frac{p}{2}}dsdt

\displaystyle\leqslant{\cal Q}(N)\mathbb{E}\int_{T-2\varepsilon^{*}}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}(1+|\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)|^{2})dsdt\leqslant C{\cal Q}(N).

By repeating the above process a finite number of times, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{N,i}(t)-\overline{Y}^{i}(t)|^{p}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|Z^{N,i,j}(t,s)-\delta_{ij}\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}dt\Big]\leqslant C{\cal Q}(N).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

From the proof of
Theorem 4.2
, we know that when
g
depends on the law of
Z(\cdot,\cdot)
, we should work within the space

L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
1<p<2
, because we only have that
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}|\overline{Z}^{i}(t,s)|^{2}dsdt<\infty
(see (
4.11
)).

If the generator
g
is independent of the law of
Z(\cdot,\cdot)
, we have a better convergence rate. More precisely, it is not necessary to restrict
p
to the interval
(1,2)
. Indeed, for any
p>1
, we have the following result concerning the convergence rate.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
1
hold. Assume that
g
is independent of the law of
Z(\cdot,\cdot)
and
g
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
\mu
in 1-Wasserstein distance. Furthermore, suppose that for
p>1
, there exists a
q>p
such that
the free term
\psi(\cdot)\in L^{q}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}([0,T])
and
g(t,s,0,0,\delta_{0},\delta_{0})\in L^{q}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[0,T])
.
Then, there exists a positive constant
C
depending on the Lipschitz constant of
g
and
p

\forall(y^{N}(\cdot),z^{N}(\cdot,\cdot))\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}([T-\varepsilon,T];\mathbb{R}^{N})\times{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[T-\varepsilon,T];(\mathbb{R}^{N\times d})^{N}),

if
\forall(y^{N}(\cdot),z^{N}(\cdot,\cdot))\in\mathscr{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\widehat{R}_{1},\widehat{R}_{2}),
then we have

\Upsilon(y(\cdot),z(\cdot,\cdot))\in\mathscr{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\widehat{R}_{1},\widehat{R}_{2}),

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(R_{1},R_{2}):=\Big\{

\displaystyle(P(\cdot),Q(\cdot,\cdot))\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}([T-\varepsilon,T];\mathbb{R}^{N})\times{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[T-\varepsilon,T];(\mathbb{R}^{N\times d})^{N})\Big|

(5.26)

\displaystyle\|P(\cdot)\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}([T-\varepsilon,T];\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leqslant R_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\|Q(\cdot,\cdot)\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[T-\varepsilon,T];(\mathbb{R}^{N\times d})^{N})}\leqslant R_{2}\Big\},

endowed with the norm

\|(P(\cdot),Q(\cdot,\cdot))\|_{\mathscr{B}_{N,\varepsilon}}\triangleq\sqrt{\|P(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}([T-\varepsilon,T];\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|Q(\cdot,\cdot)\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[T-\varepsilon,T];(\mathbb{R}^{N\times d})^{N})}}.

Similar to
3.4
, one can show
\Upsilon
is contractive. Hence, Eq. (
1.1
)
has a local solution. Based on the method used in the proof of
Theorem 3.5
to extend the local solution to a global one, we can obtain that Eq. (
1.1
) admits a unique global adapted solution.

Next, let us show that the bounds of
||Y^{N,i}(\cdot)||_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}[0,T]}
and

||Z^{N,i,j}(\cdot,\cdot)||_{\overline{{\cal Z}}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[0,T])}
are independent of
N
.

Let
(\mathbb{Y}^{N,i}(t,\cdot),\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,j}(t,\cdot))
be the unique adapted solution to Eq. (
4.3
). Recall that

Y^{N,i}(t)=\mathbb{Y}^{N,i}(t,t),\quad Z^{N,i,j}(t,s)=\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,j}(t,s),\quad(t,s)\in\Delta[0,T]

(see (
4.4
)).
Set
\widehat{\theta}=\beta^{2}+\beta_{0}^{2}+1
. Making a similar deduction as (
3.6
), one has

\displaystyle|\mathbb{Y}^{N,i}(t,s)|^{2}\leqslant e^{\theta T}[(K_{1})^{2}+K_{2}]+e^{\theta T}\int_{s}^{T}\|Y^{N,i}(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}[r,T]}dr+e^{\theta T}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|Y^{N,i}(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}[r,T]}dr.

(5.27)

Taking
s=t
, we have from Gronwall’s inequality that

||Y^{N,i}(\cdot)||_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}[0,T]}\leqslant\overline{C},

where
\overline{C}
depends only on
\beta,\beta_{0},K_{1},K_{2},T
, independent of
N
.
Substituting the above inequality into (
5.27
) yields

|\mathbb{Y}^{N,i}(t,s)|\leqslant C_{0},\ s\in[t,T].

Recall the definition of the function
\Xi
(see (
3.9
)).
Applying Itô’s formula to
\Xi(\mathbb{Y}^{N,i}(t,\cdot))
provides, for any stopping time
\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[\Xi(\mathbb{Y}^{N,i}(t,\tau))]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\bigg[\int_{\tau}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,j}(t,s)|^{2}ds\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[\Xi(\psi^{i}(t))]+\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\bigg[\int_{\tau}^{T}|\Xi^{\prime}(\mathbb{Y}^{N,i}(t,s))|

\displaystyle\cdot\Big(\ell(t,s)+\beta|Y^{N,i}(s)|+\beta_{0}{\cal W}_{2}(\mu^{N}(s),\delta_{0})\Big)ds\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\Xi(K_{1})+\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\bigg[\int_{\tau}^{T}|\Xi^{\prime}(C_{0})|(\ell(t,s)+(\beta+\beta_{0})\overline{C}])ds\bigg].

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\bigg[\int_{\tau}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,j}(t,s)|^{2}ds\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\Xi(K_{1})+|\Xi^{\prime}(C_{0})|[K_{2}+(\beta+\beta_{0})\overline{C}T]\triangleq\overline{\overline{C}}.

Notice that
Z^{N,i,j}(t,s)=\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,j}(t,s),\ (t,s)\in\Delta[0,T]
, we arrive at

||Z^{N,i,j}(\cdot,\cdot)||^{2}_{\overline{{\cal Z}}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta[0,T])}\leqslant\overline{\overline{C}},

where
\overline{\overline{C}}
is independent of
N
.
∎

6
Conclusions

This paper studies the well-posedness of mean-field BSVIEs and the convergence rate of related particle systems. When the generator is of linear growth in the variables
(y,z,\mu,\nu)
, the convergence rate is
{\cal Q}(N)
. However, for generators with quadratic growth in
z
, the convergence rate becomes
{\cal O}(N^{-\frac{1}{2\lambda}})
for some
\lambda>1
when
g
is independent of the law of
Z(\cdot,\cdot)
. An extra constant
\lambda
appears in the denominator of the exponent for the quadratic growth case. This phenomenon arises primarily from applying Girsanov’s theorem to eliminate the effect of the quadratic term. Whether the convergence rate for the quadratic case can achieve
{\cal O}(N^{-\frac{1}{2}})
is an interesting topic, which we leave for future research.

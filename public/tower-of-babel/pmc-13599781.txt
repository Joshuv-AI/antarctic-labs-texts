Apatinib monotherapy for pretreated advanced squamous cell carcinoma of the penis: a phase 2 trial
Authors: Juan Zhou, Wenfeng Li, Mingjuan Sun, Sheng Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTreatment options for patients with advanced penile squamous cell carcinoma who have disease progression on systemic chemotherapy are scarce. Antiangiogenic drugs have shown antitumor effects in case series. This phase 2 trial evaluated the activity and safety of apatinib (a Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF) receptor inhibitor) in such patients.MethodsThis investigator-initiated, single-arm, phase 2 trial was registered at the University Hospital Medical Information Network Clinical Trials Registry (UMIN000021849) and performed at a tertiary cancer center in Shanghai, China. Eligible patients had histologically confirmed penile squamous cell carcinoma, were not candidates for surgery with curative intent, had measurable disease, had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2, pretreated with one or more kinds of systemic treatment. Patients were prescribed oral apatinib 500 mg daily with the option of dose reduction to manage adverse events. The primary endpoint was the investigator-assessed overall response rate (ORR) assessed in all patients who received at least one dose of study drug.ResultsBetween 2016 and 2023, we enrolled 23 patients, all of whom received at least one dose of study treatment. Of the 23 patients, 16 had an ECOG performance status of 2. Only 6 patients had human papillomavirus-positive tumors. Five patients had a partial response (ORR = 22% [95% CI, 10%-42%]). The median progression-free survival was 3.8 months, and median overall survival was 7.2 months. Treatment-related adverse events of grade 3 or worse occurred in 7 (30%) of 23 patients. The most common grade 3 or 4 treatment-related adverse events were hand–foot syndrome (13%), hypertension (9%), and increase in serum alanine aminotransferase (9%).ConclusionApatinib in pretreated advanced penile cancer showed limited efficacy with a manageable safety profile.ClinicalTrials.gov IdentifierUMIN000021849.Keywords: apatinib, penile cancer, chemotherapy

Trial information

This phase 2 trial (registered at the University Hospital Medical Information Network Clinical Trials Registry: UMIN000021849) was a single-center, single-arm, investigator-initiated phase 2 study that enrolled patients with unresectable advanced penile cancer to receive apatinib treatment as later-line therapy. This trial was performed at Shanghai Cancer Center, Fudan University, China. This study was conducted in compliance with Good Clinical Practice Guidelines and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was required before any screening procedures. The trial was approved by the ethics committee of Fudan University Affiliated Shanghai Cancer Center.

The enrollment began in October 2016 but was interrupted due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The trial was restarted in 2022, and other patients were included up to December 2023. In total, 30 patients were screened for eligibility, of whom 24 patients met the eligibility criteria. One patient withdrew consent before starting treatment and underwent surgery. Thus, 23 participants received at least one dose of apatinib and were included in this analysis (Figure 1). The trial was closed and analyzed in July 2024. At that time, all patients had progressed. Only one patient was still alive at 8 months after starting apatinib treatment.

Drug information

The dose of 500 mg per day was chosen but could be reduced to 250 mg/day, if necessary. Treatment for 3 weeks is regarded as one cycle. The median number of apatinib cycles was 3.8 (IQR 0.4-15.9). 21 patients initiated apatinib with 500 mg daily, and the dose was reduced in 5 of them (22%) due to treatment-related adverse events. The remaining 2 patients were given apatinib 250 mg daily according to the judgment of treating physicians. The median follow-up time was 14.0 months (95% CI, 12.8-15.2).

Patient characteristics

Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age was 58 years (IQR 29-78), and 61% of the men received apatinib as second-line therapy, while 39% received apatinib after at least 2 lines of systemic therapy. Sixty-one percent of the men had received TIP (T: Paclitaxel; I: Ifosfamide; P: Cisplatin) chemotherapy and 39% other types of chemotherapy. All patients had lymph node metastases; other metastatic sites included the lung, bronchus, abdominal wall, muscle, and scrotal wall. Seventy percent of patients had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 2, and only 30% had ECOG status 0 or 1. Only 6 patients (26%, 95% CI, 13%-47%) were positive for hrHPV (High-Risk Human Papillomavirus).

Primary assessment method

At the data cutoff of July 2024, 5 men (22%, 95% CI, 10%-42%) had a confirmed partial response with a median duration of response of 4.6 months (95% CI, 3.0-6.0). The median progression-free survival (PFS) duration was 3.8 months (95% CI, 1.7-5.3 months; Figure 2A), and the median OS duration was 7.2 months (95% CI, 6.1-8.2 months; Figure 2B). There was no evident difference in PFS between patients with hrHPV-negative and positive disease. There was also no evident difference in PFS and OS between patients who had response to prior TIP chemotherapy and patients who had no response. Serious adverse events

Toxicity is summarized in Table 2. Any grade of treatment-related adverse event was reported in 21 patients (91%), and grades 3 and 4 adverse events were reported in 7 patients (30%). The most common treatment-related adverse events were hand–foot syndrome (39%), hypertension (35%), and decreased platelet count (22%). The most common grade 3 or 4 treatment-related adverse events were hand–foot syndrome (13%), hypertension (9%), and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) increase (9%). The toxicity spectrum in this study was consistent with previous clinical trials of apatinib.1

Additional details of study design

Eligibility criteria

The study recruited men with advanced, histologically documented, squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the penis who had progressed after at least one kind of systemic chemotherapy. Advanced was defined as distant metastases or loco-regional advanced penile cancer (large or inoperable primary tumor [T4], palpable node >3 cm in diameter or fixed nodes, and extranodal extension or pelvic node involvement [N2/N3]). Participants were required to have measurable disease and ECOG performance status of 0-2 with adequate organ function. Exclusion criteria included uncontrolled hemorrhage, abnormal coagulation, a high risk of bleeding, poorly controlled hypertension, and 24-hour urinary protein >1.0 g. The other eligibility criteria are provided in the study protocol (online only).

Study endpoints

The primary endpoint was ORR, with PFS and OS as secondary endpoints. Due to a COVID-19 outbreak, there was a trial pause, which restarted in October 2022, and 13 more patients were included up to December 2023.

hrHPV status

DNA was isolated from 3 to 5 formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissues of 10 mm thickness using the Yaneng DNA Tissue kit (Shenzhen, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. After isolation, the hrHPV status was determined using the Yaneng Screen kit according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The presence of the following 17 hrHPV genotypes was tested: hrHPV 16, 18, 31, 33, 35, 39, 45, 51, 52, 53, 56, 58, 59, 66, 68, 73, 82.

Tumor marker collection

During treatment and follow-up, serum SCC antigen was measured and collected in patients with penile cancer. The SCC level was determined before treatment, at every tumor assessment, and at every follow-up using electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (Roche, Shanghai, China). The upper limit of the normal reference values of SCC was 1.5 ng/ml.

Statistical analysis

Sample size calculation

We recalculate the sample size by assuming a null hypothesis of ORR <5% vs the alternative hypothesis ORR of >20% using a binomial exact test. A sample size of 22 would provide 81% power with an overall 2-sided alpha level of 0.1.Ultimately, 24 patients were finally included. SPSS 29.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) and Prism 10 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) were used for statistical analysis. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize patient characteristics, treatment administration, and antitumor activity. The OS and PFS were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method and Cox proportional hazards models. The median follow-up time was analyzed using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. Two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Biomarker analyses

In this study, exploratory biomarker analysis was conducted. The serum SCC level was increased in 11 patients (48%) before initiation of treatment (range: 2.3-66 ng/ml). The SCC level decreased by more than 30% on treatment in 7 patients, including 2 patients with PR. There was no evident difference in PFS or OS between patients who had normal baseline SCC and who had increased baseline SCC. However, patients with increased baseline SCC that decreased during apatinib treatment had longer PFS (5.3 vs 2.1 months) than others in the study cohort, although their OS was similar.

Discussion

This prospective clinical trial was designed to investigate the efficacy of VEGF blockade for penile cancer, using the drug apatinib. Overall, 5 (22%) of patients had a partial response with a median duration of response of 4.6 months. The median overall survival was only 7.2 months. Most (70%) of the enrolled men had an ECOG performance status of 2, and the general condition of the men was poorer than in some previous studies of men with penile cancer. The ECOG score is a prognostic factor for OS of various cancer types. The poor ECOG score in our trial might contribute to the limited response and OS of these patients. Many patients with a low performance status were able to receive apatinib, but our trial suggests that it offers limited symptomatic disease control.

Our trial was open for accrual for more than 7 years. This reflects the inherent difficulty in enrollment of a rare cancer in trials, and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic further complicated trial recruitment. However, the treatment paradigm for metastatic penile cancer has not changed, so the long duration of this trial should not affect the validity of its results.

The combination of PD1 and VEGF inhibitors has been shown to be synergistic in some cancer types. In a small phase 2 trial in the United States with a PD1 inhibitor and cabozantinib,2,3 treatment of 9 patients with advanced penile cancer led to a substantial response rate of 44%. Based on these results, we are currently recruiting penile cancer patients through a collaboration with other national cancer centers in a phase 2 trial to test the efficacy of a PD1 inhibitor and apatinib (Chinese Clinical Trial number registration: 2400086422).

hrHPV has been shown to be associated with an inflamed tumor microenvironment and has been implicated as a potential prognostic stratification factor in penile cancer studies.4 It has been reported that localized hrHPV-positive penile cancers have better outcomes than hrHPV-negative penile cancer,5 while there was no influence of hrHPV status on response to chemo-radiotherapy in loco-regional penile cancer.6 In our study, the status of hrHPV was not associated with survival, although small numbers of patients limit confidence in this conclusion. Recently, single-cell RNA sequencing by Elst et al. described the cell composition of the penile cancer microenvironment and demonstrated that TP53 mutations are key determinants of aggressive phenotypes, irrespective of human papillomavirus status.7,8 Further studies are needed to explore whether these findings may be used as prognostic factors in penile cancer.

Data suggest that SCC, a tumor antigen, is not a sensitive marker of penile cancer burden, although it may have prognostic significance for disease-free survival in men with penile cancer treated with surgery.9 In a recent study, a decrease in SCC was associated with tumor response after chemotherapy.10 Our results are similar; SCC decrease was associated with tumor response and/or stabilization of disease. However, an association of SCC decrease with survival was not observed.

One limitation of this study is that the enrollment was exclusively in China, and its applicability to other areas remains unknown. Genomic profiling of different penile cancer populations remains unknown.

In conclusion, the VEGF inhibitor apatinib has shown limited efficacy in the treatment of metastatic penile cancer albeit with manageable toxicity. Further studies are ongoing to evaluate apatinib in combination with other agents such as immunotherapy.

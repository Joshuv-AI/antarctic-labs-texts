Ricci Flow on ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} -bundles over a Product of Kähler-Einstein Manifolds
Frederick Tsz-Ho Fong, Hung Tran

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Ricci flow on ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} -bundles over a product of Kähler-Einstein manifolds whose initial metric g 0 g_{0} is constructed by the ansatz used in works by M. Wang et. al. We prove that the ansatz is preserved along the Ricci flow. Furthermore, in the Kähler case, we proved that Type I finite-time singularity must occur.



1.
Introduction

The Ricci flow is one of the central topics in geometric analysis and is one of the most extensively studied geometric flows. It was introduced by Hamilton in
[
Ham82
]
, and has been used to resolve the long-standing Poincaré Conjecture by Perelman
[
Per02
,
Per03b
,
Per03a
]
. One important component in many works on the Ricci flow is to study the blow-up or convergence limit of the flow. To this end, it is then essential to understand how fast the curvature blows up as the flow approaches to the maximal existence time. Following Hamilton’s classification in
[
Ham95b
]
, when the Ricci flow approaches a finite-time singularity at
T<\infty
, we say that the singularity is of Type I if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
t\in[0,T)
we have

\left|\textup{Rm}(g(t))\right|_{g(t)}\leq\frac{C}{T-t}.

Otherwise, we say that the singularity is of Type II.

Type I rescaling is a more desirable because the blow-up factor is essentially the inverse linear expression
\frac{1}{T-t_{i}}
. The rescaled limit
g_{i}(t):=\frac{1}{T-t_{i}}g\big(t_{i}+(T-t_{i})t\big)
would then converge in Cheeger-Gromov sense to a srhinking gradient Ricci soliton as shown in
[
EMT11
]
. It is therefore of strong interest in knowing under what conditions the Ricci flow would encounter Type I singularity.

In the Kähler case, it has recently been proved by Conlon-Hallgren-Ma in
[
CHM25
]
that any Ricci flow on compact Kähler surfaces
X
(i.e.
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X=2
) with finite-time singularity
T<\infty
and
\inf_{[0,T)}\textup{Vol}(X,g(t))>0
must also encounter Type I singularity, and the blow-up limit is the gradient shrinking Kähler-Ricci soliton on the
\mathbb{C}
-bundle
\mathcal{O}(-1)
over
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
constructed by Feldman-Ilmanen-Knopf in
[
FIK03
]
. In higher complex dimensions, it was proved by Li-Tian-Zhu in
[
LTZ24
]
that Type II singularity could occur on some Fano compactification of a semi-simple complex Lie group if it admits no Käher-Einstein metrics.

In contrast, it appears that if the manifold admits some sort of good symmetry, then it is more likely that Type I singularity occurs. In
[
Fon14
]
, the first-named author proved that the Kähler-Ricci flow on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-bundle
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus L)
over a Kähler-Einstein manifold
\Sigma
would encounter Type I singularity if the initial metric satisfies the Calabi’s
U(n)
-symmetry and the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-fibers of the bundle collapse as
t\to T
. The rescaled Ricci flow would converge in pointed Cheeger-Gromov’s sense to the direct product
\mathbb{C}^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Sigma}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. On a
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-bundle
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-k))
over
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
where the initial Kähler metric has Calabi’s
U(n)
-symmetry, Song proved in
[
Son15
]
that Type I singularity must occur when the exceptional divisor contracts to a point as
t\to T
. Subsequently, Guo and Song also proved in
[
GS16
]
that the Type I parabolic blow-up limit along the exceptional divisor is the unique
U(n)
-complete shrinking Kähler-Ricci soliton on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
blown-up at a point constructed in
[
FIK03
]
.

Recently, Jian-Song-Tian
[
JST23
]
generalized the above-mentioned works by studying the Kähler-Ricci flow on higher rank projective bundle
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{Z}\oplus L^{\oplus m})
,
m\geq 1
, over a Kähler-Einstein manifolds
Z^{n}
. Assuming Calabi’s
U(m+n)
-symmetry, they proved that Type I singularity must occur, and the blow-up limit near the singularity is the shrinking Kähler-Ricci soliton with Calabi symmetry on the total space of the vector bundle
L^{\oplus m}
over
Z^{n}
. In addtion, there have been several recent developments in precise understanding of Ricci flows on manifolds with different types of symmetry:
[
App23
]
(
U(2)
-metric on
T\mathbb{S}^{2}
),
[
DG20
]
(
SU(2)
-metric on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
),
[
Wu25
]
(
SO(n)
-metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
),
[
IKMS25
]
(multiple-warped product), and the aforementioned
[
CHM25
]
(compact Kähler surfaces with non-collapsed finite time singularities).

It is not a coincidence that many of the above-mentioned works study the Kähler-Ricci flow on underlying manifolds which admit some bundles or fibration structures, as these underlying manifolds appear frequently in the context of minimal model program proposed by Song-Tian in
[
ST17
]
. In fact, in complex dimension 3, it was shown by Tosatti-Zhang in
[
TZ15
]
that if the Kähler-Ricci flow encounters finite-time singularity, the underlying manifold must admit a Fano fibration. Apart from the above-mentioned references, there are other works investigating the finite-time singularity of a Kähler-Ricci flow, sharing similar contexts as in this paper, such as
[
SW11
,
SW13b
,
SW13a
,
SY12
]
and the references therein.

To enrich the perspective of the singularity analysis of Ricci flow with symmetry, this article studies the Ricci flow on a particular family of metrics, with the ansatz used by Wang-Wang
[
WW98
]
and Dancer-Wang
[
DW11
]
among others. Their works were about the construction of Einstein metrics and Ricci solitons on manifolds foliated by equidistant hypersurfaces that are circle bundles over products of Kähler-Einstein manifolds. We will recall fundamental components of the ansatz in detail in Section
2.1
. Roughly speaking, this ansatz is built upon a submersion of a Riemannian manifold
\pi:P\to N
onto a
product
of Kähler-Einstein manifolds
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{r}
with circle fibers, such that there exists a contact
1
-form
\eta
on
P
. One then defines a metric
g
on the product
[0,s_{0}]\times P
with metric of the form given by (
2.1
) and some closing conditions described in Subsection
2.2
. This ansatz generalizes the Calabi’s
U(n)
-symmetry whose base manifold is one single homogeneous Kähler-Einstein manifold.

Working on the above-mentioned ansatz, we study the Ricci flow and its singularity analysis, and obtain the following:

Main Result.

Let
M\to N
be a
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-bundle over
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{r}
which is a product of compact Kähler-Einstein manifolds
(N_{i},J_{i},g_{i})
such that the initial metric
g_{0}
is constructed by the ansatz described in Section
2.1
. Then, the ansatz is preserved under the Ricci flow (Proposition
3.1
). Furthermore, in the Kähler case, the Ricci flow solution must encounter Type I finite-time singularity (Theorem
4.5
).

Our results generalize some former works such as
[
Fon14
,
Son15
,
GS16
,
Max14
]
by keeping the fibers to be
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
but allowing the base manifold to a
product
of Kähler-Einstein manifolds, whereas these former work required the base manifold to be
one
Kähler-Einstein manifold. Such a generalization is also different from the recent work
[
JST23
]
which studies the
higher-rank
bundles over
one
Kähler-Einstein manifold.

It would also be interesting, as in the above-mentioned former works, to identify the blow-up singularity model by studying its pointed Cheeger-Gromov limit. If the singularity is caused by the collapsing of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-fibers, then by the same argument as in
[
Fon14
]
one can prove that the blow-up model is the product
\mathbb{CP}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
where
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N
. However, if the finite-time singularity is caused by the contraction of some (but not all) of the factor
N_{i}
’s, it is not straight-forward to find out its blow-up limit. We will outline the possibilities and the detail will be discussed in forthcoming work.

2.
An Ansatz with Equidistant Hypersurfaces

In this section, we give a definition and describe properties of a general ansatz with Calabi’s symmetry as its special case. This particular setting was considered in
[
WW98
,
DW11
]
, among others, to construct Einstein metrics and Ricci solitons.

2.1.
The ansatz

The ansatz (c.f.
[
DW11
, Section 4]
) used in
[
WW98
]
and
[
DW11
]
is described as follows. Let

\big(N_{i}^{2n_{i}},J_{N_{i}},g_{N_{i}},\omega_{N_{i}}\big),\quad 1\leq i\leq r

be compact Kähler-Einstein manifolds of real dimension
2n_{i}
with
\textup{Ric}(g_{N_{i}})=k_{i}g_{N_{i}}
for constant
k_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. Here
J_{N_{i}}
is the complex structure of
N_{i}^{2n_{i}}
, and
\omega_{N_{i}}
is the Kähler form defined by

\omega_{N_{i}}(X,Y)=g_{N_{i}}(J_{N_{i}}X,Y)

for any smooth vector fields
X,Y
. We denote the product manifold of these
N_{i}
’s by

N:=N_{1}^{2n_{1}}\times\cdots\times N_{r}^{2n_{r}}.

Next we consider another Riemannian manifold
P
of real dimension

\dim P=1+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\dim N_{i}

such that there exists a submersion
\pi:P\to N
with circle fibers, and there exists an
1
-form
\eta
on
P
satisfying

d\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{N_{i}}

for integers
q_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
. We let
\zeta
in the kernel of
\pi_{*}
be the dual vector field of
\eta
such that
\eta(\zeta)=1
. A particular case is given by the Hopf fibration of an odd-dimensional sphere over the complex projective space.

Now consider a smooth manifold
M
with a dense subset
M_{0}
which is diffeomorphic to
I\times P
where
I
is an open interval. For each
s\in I
, we consider a metric
g_{s}
on
\{s\}\times P
of the form

g_{s}=H^{2}(s)\eta\otimes\eta+\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}^{2}(s)\pi_{i}^{*}g_{N_{i}}

where
\pi_{i}:P\to N_{i}
is the composition of
\pi:P\to N
composed with the
i
-th canonical projection map
N\to N_{i}
. The metric on
M_{0}
is in the form of

(2.1)

g=ds^{2}+g_{s}=ds^{2}+H^{2}(s)\eta\otimes\eta+\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}^{2}(s)\pi_{i}^{*}g_{N_{i}}

The complete metric on
M
is obtained by smooth compactification at end points of
I
for which the closing condition will be discussed later.

Let’s examine this construction in more details. Denote
E:=\ker(\eta:TP\to\mathbb{R})
, then
TM_{0}
can then be decomposed into

TM=\textup{span}\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right\}\oplus\textup{span}\left\{\zeta\right\}\oplus E.

Note that
\zeta
may not be an integrable vector field. With some abuse of notations, we still denote the following submersion map from
M_{0}=I\times P\to N
by
\pi
, and similarly for each
\pi_{i}
, then the vertical and horizontal distributions with respect to
\pi:M_{0}\to N
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle:=\ker\pi_{*}=\textup{span}\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial s},\zeta\right\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{V}^{\perp_{g}}=E.

Moreover, each
F_{i}
is the conformal factor of the submersion
\pi_{i}
to the fixed metric
(N_{i},g_{i})
while
H
is associated with the length of the circle fiber. By continuous extension to
M
, they can be considered as smooth non-negative functions from
M
to
\mathbb{R}
independent of coordinates.

We can define an almost complex structure
J
on
TM_{0}
by, for each
X\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle J\frac{\partial}{\partial s}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{H(s)}\zeta,\text{ and }J\zeta=H(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}

\displaystyle JX

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}J_{N_{i}}\big((\pi_{i})_{*}(X)\big).

It is immediate that
g
is Hermitian with respect to
J
and the Kähler form
\omega
of
(M_{0},J,g)
is defined by

\omega(\cdot,\cdot)=g(J\cdot,\cdot).

By observing that
\eta(X)=0
for any
X\in\mathcal{H}
and that
\pi_{*}(\zeta)=0
, we obtain

\omega=H\eta\wedge ds+\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}^{2}(s)\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{N_{i}}.

Henceforth, by the fact that
H
depends only on
s
so that
dH\wedge ds=0
, we have

\displaystyle d\omega

\displaystyle=dH\wedge\eta\wedge ds+Hd\eta\wedge ds+\sum_{i=1}^{r}dF_{i}^{2}\wedge\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{N_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}H(s)q_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{N_{i}}\wedge ds+\frac{d}{ds}F_{i}^{2}(s)ds\wedge\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{N_{i}}.

Therefore,
M_{0}
is Kähler with respect to
J
iff

(2.2)

q_{i}H(s)=\frac{dF_{i}^{2}(s)}{ds}\;\;\forall i=1,\cdots,r.

2.2.
Closing conditions

Suppose that
I
has the left end-point
s_{0}
where

\lim_{s\rightarrow s_{0}^{+}}H(s)=0<\lim_{s\rightarrow s_{0}^{+}}F_{i}(s).

The completeness of the metric imposes certain conditions on
H
and each
F_{i}
approaching this point. Let
\theta
be a (local)
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-fiber coordinate of submersion
\pi:P\to N
, and write
\eta
locally as
\eta=d\theta+\alpha
for some
1
-form
\alpha
independent of
\theta
. By expressing the metric using polar coordinates
x=r\cos\theta
,
y=r\sin\theta
, where
r(s)
is a function of
s
obtained by solving the ODE:

\frac{1}{r}dr=\frac{1}{H(s)}ds,\quad\text{and}\quad r(s_{0})=0.

Then, the metric can be written as

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=\frac{H(s)^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\theta+\alpha)\otimes(d\theta+\alpha)\right)+\sum_{j}F_{j}(s)^{2}\pi_{j}^{*}g_{N_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{H(s)^{2}}{x^{2}+y^{2}}\left(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+(x\,dy-y\,dx)\cdot\alpha+(x^{2}+y^{2})\alpha\otimes\alpha\right)+\sum_{j}F_{j}(s)^{2}\pi_{j}^{*}g_{N_{j}}.

Let
\bar{H}(r)
and
\bar{F}_{j}(r)
be functions such that

\bar{H}(r(s))=H(s)\text{ and }\bar{F}_{j}(r(s))=F_{j}(s),

then in order to extend the metric of
I\times P
to the left end-point
s_{0}\in I
, we require that there exist smooth functions
\phi
and
\psi_{j}
of
r
defined near
r=0
and with
\phi(0)>0
and
\psi_{j}(0)>0
such that

\frac{\bar{H}(r)}{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}=\phi(x^{2}+y^{2})=\phi(r^{2}),\text{ and }\bar{F}_{j}(r)=\psi_{j}(x^{2}+y^{2})

such that
\phi(0),\psi_{j}(0)>0
. By considering Taylor expansion, the above is equivalent to the conditions that

H^{\prime}(s_{0})=1,\text{ and }H^{(2k)}(s_{0})=F_{i}^{(2k+1)}(s_{0})=0\text{ for any $k\geq 0$.}

The condition is similar if
s_{1}
is the right end-point of
I
, except that the first condition is replaced by
H^{\prime}(s_{1})=-1
.

2.3.
Special case: Calabi symmetry

When the base manifold
N
consists of only one factor, i.e.
r=1
, then for each
s\in I
, the submersion
\pi:(P,g_{s})\to(N,F(s)^{2}g_{N})
is a Riemannian submersion, and the submersion
\pi:(I\times P,g)\to(N,g_{N})
is a conformal submersion.

One important special case of the ansatz (
2.1
) is given by metrics under Calabi’s ansatz. It can be obtained by taking
P=\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}:=L^{2n-1}(k)
, commonly called a lens space, with the round metric, and
N=\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
with the Fubini-study metric
g_{\textup{FS}}
. The submersion
\pi:\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}\to\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
is then the canonical projection. Take
I=(0,\infty)
, then
I\times P
is biholomorphic to
(\mathbb{C}^{n}\backslash\{0\})/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. A metric
g
defined on
(\mathbb{C}^{n}\backslash\{0\})/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
is said to have
Calabi symmetry
under the global coordinates
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
if it can be written in the form

g_{i\bar{j}}=g\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}\right)=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial z_{i}\partial\bar{z}_{j}}

where
u
depends only
\|z\|^{2}=\sum_{i}\left|z_{i}\right|^{2}
. It is typical that we let
\rho=\log\|z\|^{2}
, then one can directly compute that
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\rho=\pi^{*}\omega_{\textup{FS}}
. By the chain rule, one can check that

g_{i\bar{j}}=u_{\rho\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z_{i}}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}+u_{\rho}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial z_{i}\partial\bar{z}_{j}}.

By letting

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{k\sqrt{-1}}{2}\left(\bar{\partial}\rho-\partial\rho\right)=\frac{k\sqrt{-1}}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\bar{z}_{i}}d\bar{z}^{i}-\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z_{i}}dz^{i}\right),

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{k}\left(z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}-\bar{z}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}_{i}}\right),

then we have
\eta(\zeta)=1
since
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z_{i}}=\frac{\bar{z}_{i}}{\left\|z\right\|^{2}}
, and
d\eta=k\pi^{*}\omega_{\textup{FS}}
by
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\rho=\pi^{*}\omega_{\textup{FS}}
. One can then check that

g=u_{\rho\rho}\left(d\rho\otimes d\rho+\frac{1}{k^{2}}\eta\otimes\eta\right)+u_{\rho}\pi^{*}g_{\textup{FS}}.

By the change of variables
s=\psi(\rho)
via the ODE

\frac{d\psi}{d\rho}=\sqrt{u_{\rho\rho}}

one can rewrite
g
in the form of

g=ds^{2}+\frac{1}{k^{2}}u_{\rho\rho}\circ\psi^{-1}(s)\eta\otimes\eta+u_{\rho}\circ\psi^{-1}(s)\pi^{*}g_{\textup{FS}},

corresponding to ansatz (
2.1
) with

H(s)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{k^{2}}u_{\rho\rho}\circ\psi^{-1}(s)}\quad\text{ and }\quad F(s)=\sqrt{u_{\rho}\circ\psi^{-1}(s)}.

It is easy to verify that

\frac{d}{ds}F(s)^{2}=u_{\rho\rho}\frac{d\rho}{ds}=\sqrt{u_{\rho\rho}\circ\psi^{-1}(s)}=kH(s),

which is exactly the Kähler condition.

2.4.
O’Neill Tensors

A submersion
\pi:M\to N
is often associated the following two important tensors introduced by O’Neill
[
O’N66
]
. For any
X,Y\in TM
,

\displaystyle A_{X}Y

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{V}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}X}\mathcal{H}Y+\mathcal{H}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}X}\mathcal{V}Y

\displaystyle T_{X}Y

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{H}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}X}\mathcal{V}Y+\mathcal{V}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}X}\mathcal{H}Y

where
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
denotes the horizontal and vertical projections respectively. One can check that under the ansatz (
2.1
), we have
T\equiv 0
, i.e. the fibers of
\pi
are totally geodesic. For the
A
tensor, we collect the following whose proofs are given in the Appendix for completeness. Let
\sharp
denote the vector dual to the one-form with respect to
g
.

.

For the ansatz (
2.1
), the
A
-tensor is given by

\displaystyle A_{X}Y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{V}[X,Y]-\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}^{2}\pi_{i}^{*}g_{N_{i}}(X,Y)\mathcal{V}\nabla\log F_{i}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}^{2}\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{N_{i}}(X,Y)\mathcal{V}(J\nabla\log F_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}^{2}\pi_{i}^{*}g_{N_{i}}(X,Y)\mathcal{V}\nabla\log F_{i}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{V}\left(d\omega(X,Y,J(\cdot))\right)^{\sharp}

\displaystyle A_{X}V

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{H}\left(g\big(V,A_{X}(\cdot)\big)^{\sharp}\right)

\displaystyle A_{U}X

\displaystyle=A_{U}V=0

for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
and
U,V\in\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

Corollary 2.2
.

There exists a constant such that

\left|A\right|_{g}^{2}\leq C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|\nabla\log F_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}+\left|d\omega\right|_{g}^{2}\right).

2.5.
Computation of Curvature

Next, we will compute the curvature tensors via Gauss, Codazzi, and Riccati equations and the second fundamental form. Regard each
P_{s}:=\{s\}\times P
as a hypersurface of
M
, we consider the shape operator
L_{s}
, as in
[
DW11
, Remark 2.18]
, defined by

L_{s}(X):=\nabla_{X}\nu\;\;\text{for any }X\in TP_{s}

where
\nu:=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
and
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection on
M
. We will denote it simply by
L
if it is clear from the context. For each
s
,
L_{s}
can be diagonalized with eigenvalues
\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}
(multiplicity one) and
\frac{F_{i}^{\prime}}{F_{i}}
(multiplicity
2n_{i}
). We denote

L^{\prime}_{s}=\nabla_{\nu}L_{s}.

Consequently, the eigenvalues of
L_{s}
and
L^{\prime}_{s}
are given by

\displaystyle L_{s}

\displaystyle:\frac{H^{\prime}}{H},\underbrace{\frac{F_{i}^{\prime}}{F_{i}}}_{\text{multiplicity $2n_{i}$}}

\displaystyle L^{\prime}_{s}

\displaystyle:\frac{H^{\prime\prime}}{H}-\left(\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}\right)^{2},\underbrace{\frac{F_{i}^{\prime\prime}}{F_{i}}-\left(\frac{F_{i}^{\prime}}{F_{i}}\right)^{2}}_{\text{multiplicity $2n_{i}$}}

Then, for the trace
tr
is taken with respect to
g_{s}
, we have

\displaystyle\textup{tr}{L_{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}+\sum_{i}2n_{i}\frac{F^{\prime}_{i}}{F_{i}},

\displaystyle\textup{tr}{L^{2}_{s}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i}2n_{i}\left(\frac{F^{\prime}_{i}}{F_{i}}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\textup{tr}{L^{\prime}_{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{\prime\prime}}{H}-\left(\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i}2n_{i}\left(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}_{i}}{F_{i}}-\left(\frac{F^{\prime}_{i}}{F_{i}}\right)^{2}\right).

By the Gauss, Codazzi, and Riccati equations, the Ricci curvature
Ric
of
(M,g)
is totally determined by that of
(P,g_{s})
and the shape operator
L_{s}
. Precisely, for tangential vectors
X
and
Y
in
TP_{s}
, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Ric}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\textup{Ric}}_{s}(X,Y)-\textup{tr}(L_{s})g_{s}(LX,Y)-g_{s}({L^{\prime}}(X),Y),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\textup{Ric}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{X}\textup{tr}(L_{s})-g_{s}(\delta L,X),

\displaystyle\textup{Ric}(\nu,\nu)

\displaystyle=-\text{tr}({L^{\prime}})-\text{tr}(L^{2}).

Here
\widetilde{\textup{Ric}}_{s}
denotes the Ricci curvature of
(P,g_{s})
, which we may abbreviate by
\widetilde{\textup{Ric}}
. By observing that the eigenvalues of
L_{s}
depends only on
s
, we have
\textup{Ric}(u,\nu)=0
for any

3.
Reduction of Ricci flow to ODEs

Proposition

To this end, we consider an evolving family of metrics of the form:

\displaystyle ds

\displaystyle=a(\sigma,t)\,d\sigma,

\displaystyle\sigma(s_{0})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle H(s,t)

\displaystyle=h(\sigma(s),t),

\displaystyle F_{i}(s,t)

\displaystyle=f_{i}(\sigma(s),t)

\displaystyle\textup{Ric}(X_{i},Y_{i})

\displaystyle\dot{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{\sigma\sigma}}{ah}-\frac{h_{\sigma}a_{\sigma}}{a^{2}h}+\sum_{i}2n_{i}\left(\frac{(f_{i})_{\sigma\sigma}}{af_{i}}-\frac{(f_{i})_{\sigma}a_{\sigma}}{a^{2}f_{i}}\right)

Denote

4.
Type I Singularity

According to Hamilton’s classification
[

]
, a Ricci flow solution

which encounters finite-time singularity at

can be classified into the two types:

•

Type I singularity: if
\displaystyle{\lim_{t\to T^{-}}\sup_{M}(T-t)\left|\textup{Rm}\right|<\infty}

•

Type II singularity: if
\displaystyle{\lim_{t\to T^{-}}\sup_{M}(T-t)\left|\textup{Rm}\right|=\infty}

One goal of this section is to establish that the singularity of the Kähler-Ricci flow under ansatz (
2.1
) must encounter Type I singularity.

4.1.

Lemma 4.1

For any

\displaystyle h(X,Y)

\displaystyle=g(\nabla_{X}Y,\nu)=g(A_{X}Y,\nu)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{1}{2}H^{2}d\eta(\tilde{Z},\tilde{W})d\eta(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})+\frac{1}{4}H^{2}d\eta(\tilde{X},J\tilde{Z})d\eta(\tilde{Y},J\tilde{W})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{1}{4}H^{2}d\eta(\tilde{Y},J\tilde{Z})d\eta(\tilde{X},J\tilde{W}).

In particular,

\displaystyle\textup{Rm}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Y},\tilde{X})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}F_{i}^{2}\pi_{i}^{*}\textup{Rm}_{N_{i}}(X,Y,Y,X)+\frac{3}{4}H^{2}d\eta(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}H^{2}d\eta(\tilde{X},J\tilde{Y})^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{1}{4}H^{2}d\eta(\tilde{Y},J\tilde{Y})d\eta(\tilde{X},J\tilde{X})

For any
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
, one can write
X=\sum_{i}X_{i}
and
Y=\sum_{i}Y_{i}
where
\pi_{*}X_{i},\pi_{*}Y_{i}\in TN_{i}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i}F_{i}^{2}\pi_{i}^{*}\textup{Rm}_{N_{i}}(X,Y,Y,X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}F_{i}^{2}\pi_{i}^{*}\textup{Rm}_{N_{i}}(X_{i},Y_{i},Y_{i},X_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{F_{i}^{2}}\pi_{i}^{*}\textup{Rm}_{N_{i}}(F_{i}X_{i},F_{i}Y_{i},F_{i}Y_{i},F_{i}X_{i}).

Observing that

\left|d\eta(X,Y)\right|\leq\sum_{i}\left|q_{i}\pi^{*}_{i}g_{N_{i}}(JX,Y)\right|\leq\sum_{i}\left|q_{i}\right|\left|JX_{i}\right|_{g_{N_{i}}}\left|Y_{i}\right|_{g_{N_{i}}}\leq\sum_{i}\frac{\left|q_{i}\right|}{F_{i}^{2}}\left|X\right|_{g}\left|Y\right|_{g},

we conclude that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\textup{Rm}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Y},\tilde{X})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{F_{i}^{2}}\textup{Rm}_{N_{i}}(F_{i}X_{i},F_{i}Y_{i},F_{i}Y_{i},F_{i}X_{i})+\sum_{i}O\left(\frac{H^{2}}{F_{i}^{4}}\left|X\right|_{g}^{2}\left|Y\right|_{g}^{2}\right).

Recall that by the Kähler condition
qH=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}F_{i}^{2}
, we have

O\left(\frac{H^{2}}{F_{i}^{4}}\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{F_{i}^{4}}(F_{i}F_{i}^{\prime})^{2}\right)=O\left(\left|\nabla\log F_{i}\right|^{2}\right).

4.2.
Li-Yau’s gradient estimates

One key ingredient in the proof of Type I singularity is the use of Li-Yau’s gradient estimates for heat equations. Given that
u
is a positive solution satisfying the heat-type equation (
3.1
), the Li-Yau’s estimates proves that
\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u}
is uniformly bounded. For convenience, we include the proof here:

Proposition 4.3

(Li-Yau
[
LY86
]
)
.

Suppose
u:M\times[0,T)\to(0,\infty)
is a positive solution to the heat-type equation (
3.1
) so that (
3.2
) holds , then there exists
C>0
such that

(4.2)

\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u}\leq C.

Proof.

By straight-forward computations, we get

(4.3)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u}=-\frac{C\left|\nabla\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u}+\frac{2\langle\nabla\left|\nabla u\right|^{2},\nabla u\rangle}{u^{2}}+\frac{2k\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{2\left|\nabla u\right|^{4}}{u^{3}}

Consider the second term of the RHS:

\displaystyle\frac{2\langle\nabla\left|\nabla u\right|^{2},\nabla u\rangle}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\left\langle\nabla\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u},\nabla u\right\rangle}{u}+\frac{2\left|\nabla u\right|^{4}}{u^{3}}

\displaystyle\frac{2\langle\nabla\left|\nabla u\right|^{2},\nabla u\rangle}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle=(2-\varepsilon)\frac{\langle\nabla\left|\nabla u\right|^{2},\nabla u\rangle}{u^{2}}+\varepsilon\frac{\langle\nabla\left|\nabla u\right|^{2},\nabla u\rangle}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle=(2-\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\left\langle\nabla\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u},\nabla u\right\rangle}{u}+\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{4}}{u^{3}}\right)+\varepsilon\frac{\langle\nabla\left|\nabla u\right|^{2},\nabla u\rangle}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(2-\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\left\langle\nabla\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u},\nabla u\right\rangle}{u}+\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{4}}{u^{3}}\right)+2\varepsilon\frac{\left|\nabla\nabla u\right|\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(2-\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\left\langle\nabla\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u},\nabla u\right\rangle}{u}+\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{4}}{u^{3}}\right)+\varepsilon\left(\frac{2\left|\nabla\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u}+\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{4}}{2u^{3}}\right)

where
\varepsilon>0
is to be chosen. Substituting it back to (
4.3
), we get

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u}\leq(-C+2\varepsilon)\frac{\left|\nabla\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u}+(2-\varepsilon)\frac{\left\langle\nabla\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u},\nabla u\right\rangle}{u}+\frac{2k\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{4}}{u^{3}}.

Choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small so that
-C+\varepsilon<-\frac{C}{2}
and
-2+\varepsilon<-1
, then we have

(4.4)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u}\leq-\frac{C}{2}\frac{\left|\nabla\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u}+(2-\varepsilon)\frac{\left\langle\nabla\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u},\nabla u\right\rangle}{u}+\frac{2k\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{4}}{u^{3}}.

If
k\leq 0
, then the maximum principle directly shows (
4.2
). Suppose
k>0
, then by letting
Q=\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}{u}
, (
4.4
) can be written as

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)Q\leq(2-\varepsilon)\frac{\langle\nabla Q,\nabla u\rangle}{u}+\frac{2k}{u}Q-\frac{\varepsilon}{2u}Q^{2}.

This implies
Q_{\max}(t):=\sup_{M\times\{t\}}Q
is decreasing if
Q_{\max}(t)>\frac{4k}{\varepsilon}
. This also proves
Q_{\max}(t)
is uniformly bounded from above, and hence (
4.2
) holds.
∎

4.3.
Schwarz’s estimates

In this subsection we are going to derive a linear lower bound for each of
F_{j}
’s, and prove that if
T<\infty
is the extinction time of the Kähler-Ricci flow under the ansatz (
2.1

which is commonly called the Schwarz’s type estimates. Under the ansatz (

for any

in the Kähler case. It is interesting to observe that when restricted to the boundary
\partial M_{0}

Under the change of variables by the relation:

\frac{1}{H(s)}ds=\frac{1}{r}dr,\quad r(s_{0})=0,

where

is the left-end point of

\bar{H}(r(s))=H(s)

\bar{H}(r)
and
\bar{F}_{j}(r)

\phi(r)

Using the Kähler condition
q_{j}H(s)=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}F_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle\Delta F_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}\partial_{s}F_{j}^{2}+\left(\frac{H_{s}}{H}+\sum_{i}2n_{i}\frac{(F_{i})_{s}}{F_{i}}\right)\partial_{s}F_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}(q_{j}H)+\left(\frac{H_{s}}{H}+\sum_{i}\frac{n_{i}q_{i}H}{F_{i}^{2}}\right)q_{j}H

\displaystyle=2q_{j}H_{s}+\sum_{i}\frac{n_{i}q_{i}q_{j}H^{2}}{F_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=2q_{j}(r\phi(r^{2}))_{r}\frac{dr}{ds}+\sum_{i}\frac{n_{i}q_{i}q_{j}r\phi^{2}(r^{2})}{\psi_{i}^{2}(r^{2})}

\displaystyle=2q_{j}\cdot\frac{\phi(r^{2})+2r^{2}\phi^{\prime}(r^{2})}{\phi(r^{2})}+\sum_{i}\frac{n_{i}q_{i}q_{j}r^{2}\phi^{2}(r^{2})}{\psi_{i}^{2}(r^{2})}

Therefore, when

. Similarly, we have
\Delta F_{j}^{2}=-2q_{j}
at the right end-point of
I
.

This shows when restricted on the boundary
\partial M_{0}
, we have

\frac{\partial f_{j}^{2}}{\partial t}=\pm 2q_{j}-2k_{j}\implies f_{j}(\sigma,t)|_{\partial M_{0}}=f_{j}(\sigma,0)|_{\partial M_{0}}-2(\mp q_{j}+k_{j})t.

Moreover, by the Kähler condition we have
q_{j}H=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}F_{j}^{2}
and
H>0
except that the end-points of
I
. As
q_{j}\not=0
,
F_{j}^{2}
is then either strictly increasing or strictly decreasing on

F_{j}^{2}
is bounded above and below by a pair of linear functions
A-Bt
of

with
A>0
and
B\in\mathbb{R}
. If any of these linear functions goes to

as

, then the flow will extinct. Therefore these linear functions are either bounded away from
0
on
[0,T)
, or is of the form
\frac{T-t}{C}
for some constant
C>0
. In either case, we can conclude that

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists
C>0
such that

(4.5)

f_{j}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{C}(T-t).

4.4.
Type I singularity

In this subsection, we prove the main result of this article that Type I singularity must occur on the Kähler-Ricci flow on manifolds constructed by the ansatz defined in Section
2.1
. Our results further extend and generalize former works
[
Fon14
,
Son15
,
GS16
]
in a sense that these former works require Calabi’s
U(n)
-symmetry (a special case of the ansatz (
2.1
)) so that one can analyze and estimate the Kähler potential. These works also require the base manifold of the submersion
\pi:P\to N
to be
one
single Kähler-Einstein manifold. Recently, Jian-Song-Tian
[
JST23
]
further generalized the aforesaid-said result to
higher rank
bundles over
one
Kähler-Einstein manifold, proving that the Kähler-Ricci flow with Calabi’s symmetry on these bundles must also encounter Type I singularity. In the following result, we give a generalization in a different direction: that the fibers are still
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
but we allow the base manifold to be a
product
of Kähler-Einstein manifolds. We also adopt a different approach in analyzing the curvature, by making use of the O’Neill’s tensor to reveal the structure of the curvature.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(M^{2n},J,g_{0})
be a compact Kähler manifold with
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}=2n
such that there exists a holomorphic submersion
M\to N
onto the product
N
of Kähler-Einstein manifolds
\{(N_{i},g_{N_{i}},J_{N_{i}})_{i=1}^{r}
, and there exists an open dense subset
M_{0}
which is biholomorphic to
I\times P
for some open interval
I
and Riemannian manifold
P^{2n-1}
such that on
I\times P
the initial metric
g_{0}
is constructed using the ansatz described in Section
2.1
. In particular, we have

g_{0}=ds^{2}+H(s)^{2}\eta\otimes\eta+\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}(s)^{2}\pi_{i}^{*}g_{N_{i}}.

Then, this ansatz is preserved along the Kähler-Ricci flow

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(t)=-2\textup{Ric}(g(t)),\quad g(0)=g_{0}.

Suppose the solution to flow
g(t)
encounters finite-time singularity at
T<\infty
, then the singularity is of Type I, i.e. there exists
C>0
such that

\left|\textup{Rm}\right|_{g(t)}\leq\frac{C}{T-t}.

Proof.

The first part follows from Proposition
3.1
. Now we prove the main result concerning the Type I singularity formation. Let
T<\infty
be the extinction time of the flow. For any sequence
t_{i}\to T
, we consider the scaling factor
K_{i}:=\sup_{M}\left|\textup{Rm}\right|_{g(t_{i})}=\left|\textup{Rm}\right|(x_{i},t_{i})
where
x_{i}\in M
, and let

g_{i}(t):=K_{i}g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t),\quad t\in[-K_{i}t_{i},K_{i}(T-t_{i})).

From Corollary
2.2
, we have

\left|A\right|_{g(t)}^{2}\leq C\left|\nabla\log F_{j}\right|_{g(t)}^{2}=\frac{C\left|\nabla F_{j}\right|^{2}_{g(t)}}{F_{j}^{2}}

for each
j
, so after rescaling by
K_{i}
, we get

\left|A\right|_{g_{i}(t)}^{2}=\frac{1}{K_{i}}\left|A\right|_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}^{2}\leq\frac{C\left|\nabla F_{j}\right|^{2}_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}}{K_{i}F_{j}^{2}(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}\leq\frac{C\left|\nabla F_{j}\right|^{2}_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}}{K_{i}(T-t_{i}-K_{i}^{-1}t)}.

The last inequality follows from (
4.5
). From (
4.2
), by taking
u=F_{j}^{2}
, we get
\left|\nabla F_{j}\right|\leq C
, and hence we conclude that

(4.6)

\left|A\right|_{g_{i}(t)}^{2}\leq\frac{C}{K_{i}(T-t_{i}-K_{i}^{-1}t)}.

Therefore, if we assumed that the singularity is of Type II, i.e.
\sup_{M\times[0,T)}(T-t)\left|\textup{Rm}\right|_{g(t)}=\infty
, then there exists a sequence
t_{i}\to T
such that
K_{i}(T-t_{i})\to\infty
, and hence for any fixed
t
we have

\left|A\right|_{g_{i}(t)}^{2}\to 0.

This shows the rescaled sequence
(M,g_{i}(t),x_{i})
converges (after passing to subsequences) in pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a steady Ricci soliton
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),x_{\infty})
with
\left|A\right|_{g_{\infty}(t)}\equiv 0
. Together with
\left|T\right|_{g_{\infty}(t)}\equiv 0
, the metric
g_{\infty}(t)
splits isometrically into a product manifold
(\Sigma\times N^{\prime},g_{1}(t)\oplus g_{2}(t))
, where
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Sigma=1
.

When
\{X,Y\}
are orthonormal horizontal frame, then from (
4.1
) we have:

\textup{Rm}_{g}(X,Y,Y,X)=\sum_{j}\frac{1}{F_{j}^{2}}\textup{Rm}_{N_{i}}(F_{j}X_{j},F_{j}Y_{j},F_{j}Y_{j},F_{j}X_{j})+\sum_{j}O\left(\frac{H^{2}}{F_{j}^{4}}\left|X\right|_{g}^{2}\left|Y\right|_{g}^{2}\right)

where
X_{i}=d\pi_{i}(X)
and
Y_{i}=d\pi_{i}(Y)
. For the Kähler case, we have

q_{j}H=2F_{j}F_{j}^{\prime}=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}F_{j}^{2}\implies\frac{H^{2}}{F_{j}^{4}}=\frac{1}{q_{j}^{2}}\left|\nabla\log F_{j}^{2}\right|^{2}=\frac{4}{q_{j}^{2}}\frac{\left|\nabla F_{j}\right|^{2}}{F_{j}^{2}}.

Now consider a time-dependent orthonormal (with respect to
g(t)
) horizontal frame
\{X(t),Y(t)\}
. Express them as

\displaystyle X(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}X_{j}(t),

\displaystyle Y(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}Y_{j}(t)

where
X_{j}(t)=d\pi_{j}(X(t))
and
Y_{j}(t)=d\pi_{j}(Y(t))
.

Now consider the rescaled frame which is orthonormal with respect to the rescaled metric
g_{i}(t)
:

\displaystyle X^{(i)}(t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{X(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}{\left|X(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t))\right|_{g_{i}(t)}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K_{i}}}\frac{X}{\left|X\right|_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}}

\displaystyle Y^{(i)}(t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{Y(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}{\left|Y(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t))\right|_{g_{i}(t)}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K_{i}}}\frac{Y}{\left|Y\right|_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}}.

Here we abbreviate
X(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)
by simply
X
and
Y(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)
by simply
Y
.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Rm}_{g_{i}(t)}\big(X^{(i)}(t),Y^{(i)}(t),Y^{(i)}(t),X^{(i)}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=K_{i}\textup{Rm}_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}\big(X^{(i)}(t),Y^{(i)}(t),Y^{(i)}(t),X^{(i)}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K_{i}}\textup{Rm}_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}\left(\frac{X}{\left|X\right|_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}},\frac{Y}{\left|Y\right|_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}},\frac{Y}{\left|Y\right|_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}},\frac{X}{\left|X\right|_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K_{i}}\frac{1}{\left|X\right|^{2}_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}}\frac{1}{\left|Y\right|^{2}_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}}\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\left\{\sum_{j}\frac{1}{F_{j}^{2}}\textup{Rm}_{N_{j}}(F_{j}d\pi_{j}(X),F_{j}d\pi_{j}(Y),F_{j}d\pi_{j}(Y),F_{j}d\pi_{j}(X))\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\left.+\sum_{j}O\left(\frac{1}{q_{j}^{2}}\frac{\left|\nabla F_{j}\right|^{2}}{F_{j}^{2}}\left|X\right|^{2}_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}\left|Y\right|^{2}_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}\right)\right\}.

Here we abbreviate
F_{j}(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)
by simply
F_{j}
.

For each
j
, there exists a dimensional constant
C_{j}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\textup{Rm}_{N_{j}}(F_{j}d\pi_{j}(X),F_{j}d\pi_{j}(Y),F_{j}d\pi_{j}(Y),F_{j}d\pi_{j}(X)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{j}F_{j}^{4}\left|d\pi_{j}(X)\right|_{g_{N_{j}}}^{2}\left|d\pi_{j}(Y)\right|_{g_{N_{j}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=C_{j}\left|X\right|_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}^{2}\left|Y\right|_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}^{2}

by the Riemannian submersion property.

Furthermore, (
4.2
) implies
\left|\nabla F_{j}\right|\leq C
. Combining all these estimates, we get:

\left|\textup{Rm}_{g_{i}(t)}\big(X^{(i)}(t),Y^{(i)}(t),Y^{(i)}(t),X^{(i)}(t)\big)\right|\leq O\left(\frac{1}{K_{i}F_{j}^{2}(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}\right).

By (
4.5
), we have

F_{j}^{2}(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)\geq\frac{T-(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}{C}\implies K_{i}F_{j}^{2}(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)\geq\frac{K_{i}(T-t_{i})-t}{C}.

As we assume Type II singularity, we have
K_{i}(T-t_{i})-t\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
. We conclude that
\textup{Rm}_{g_{\infty}(t)}(X,Y,Y,X)=0
for any horizontal vectors
X,Y\in TN^{\prime}
, show the
(N^{\prime},g_{2}(t))
-factor is flat.

By the split structure of
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))=(\Sigma\times N^{\prime},g_{1}(t)\oplus g_{2}(t))
, and the fact that
(N^{\prime},g_{2}(t))
is flat and
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\Sigma=2
, the absolute-value of the scalar curvature
\left|R_{g_{\infty}(t)}\right|
then equals
\left|\textup{Rm}\right|_{g_{\infty}(t)}
(up to a constant factor). Also, the limit metric
g_{\infty}(t)
is defined on
(-\infty,\infty)
since
g_{i}(t)
is defined on
[-K_{i}t_{i},K_{i}(T-t_{i}))
and we have
K_{i}(T-t_{i})\to\infty
. By standard parabolic maximum principle, any ancient solution to the Ricci flow must have either positive, or identically zero scalar curvature. The later case is ruled out since
\left|\textup{Rm}\right|_{g_{\infty}(t)}
at
x_{\infty}
is not zero. This shows
(\Sigma,g_{1}(t))
must have positive scalar curvature, and is a steady Ricci soliton. However, it shows
(\Sigma,g_{1}(t))
must be a cigar soliton by the standard classification of two-dimensional steady Ricci soliton, which is impossible as a limit metric by the local non-collapsing theorem due to Perelman. This concludes that the flow
(M,g(t))
must encounter Type I singularity in the case
\inf_{M\times[0,T)}u(x,t)>0
.
∎

5.
Discussion on Blow-up Models of Singularity

To further deepen our understanding of the singularity formation, it would be interesting, as in former works
[
Fon14
,
Son15
,
GS16
,
JST23
]
, to study the pointed Cheeger-Gromov limit of the rescaled sequence

g_{i}(t):=K_{i}g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t).

Knowing that the singularity is of Type I, we can assume without loss of generality that

K_{i}=\frac{1}{T-t_{i}}.

Perelman’s local non-collapsing theorem
[
Per02
]
and Hamilton-Cheeger-Gromov’s compactness
[
Ham95a
]
show that there exist diffeomorphisms
\Phi_{i}
’s such that
(M,K_{i}\Phi_{i}^{*}g_{i}(t))
converges (after passing to subsequences) to a limit manifold and Ricci flow solution
(M,g_{\infty}(t))
.

In the current literature, the singularity formation for
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-bundles over Kähler-Einstein manifolds with Calabi’s symmetry can be divided into two major cases, namely (i) collapsing of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-fibers, or (ii) contraction of the zero/infinity-sections. Using the form of metrics in this article, i.e.

g(t)=ds^{2}+H(s,t)^{2}\eta\otimes\eta+F(s,t)^{2}\pi^{*}g_{N},

the fiber-collapsing case corresponds to the situation that
\displaystyle{\lim_{t\to T}\sup H(\cdot,t)=0}
and
\displaystyle{\inf F(\cdot,\cdot)>0}
, whereas the contracting of zero/infinity-sections case corresponds to
\displaystyle{\lim_{t\to T}\inf F(\cdot,t)=0}
and
\displaystyle{\inf H(\cdot,\cdot)>0}
.

Now consider
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-bundles over a
product
of Kähler-Einstein manifolds
(N:=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{r},\oplus_{k}\pi_{k}^{*}g_{N_{k}})
with metrics constructed by the ansatz (
2.1
):

g(t)=ds^{2}+H(s,t)^{2}\eta\otimes\eta+\sum_{k=1}^{r}F_{k}(s,t)^{2}\pi_{k}^{*}g_{N_{k}},\quad s\in[s_{0},s_{1}],t\in[0,T).

We expect that the singularity formation in the Kähler case will be divided into three major cases:

(i)

collapsing of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-fibers, where
H
converges to
0
as
t\to T
while each
F_{k}
is bounded away from
0
,

(ii)

contraction of the entire zero/infinity section, where
H
is bounded away from
0
as
t\to T
, while each
F_{k}(s_{0},t)\to 0
(if each
q_{k}>0
), or each
F_{k}(s_{1},t)\to 0
(if each
q_{k}<0
),

(iii)

contraction of
some
but not all
g_{N}
-components of the zero/infinite-section, which will be discussed in more detail below.

Given that we have proved that the singularity is of Type I, the singularity model of case (i) is the direct product
\mathbb{CP}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N}
as in the case of
[
Fon14
]
. The proof is similar to that in
[
Fon14
]
(see also the conformal submersion adoptation in
[
Ngu23
]
). The key idea is that we assume in (i) that each
F_{k}
is bounded away from
0
, so by Corollary
2.2
, we have

\left|A\right|_{g(t)}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k}\left|\nabla\log F_{k}\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{k}O\left(\left|\nabla F_{k}^{2}\right|^{2}\right).

As each
F_{k}^{2}
satisfies (
3.2
), so each
\left|\nabla F_{k}^{2}\right|
is uniformly bounded. These shows
\left|A\right|_{g(t)}^{2}
is uniformly bounded and hence
\left|A\right|_{g_{i}(t)}^{2}=\frac{1}{K_{i}^{2}}\left|A\right|^{2}_{g(t_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}t)}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
. This shows the limit model
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))
splits into a product of
(\Sigma_{1}^{2}\times\Sigma_{2}^{2\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N},g_{1}(t)\oplus g_{2}(t))
. Similar to
[
Fon14
]
, we can then identify that
(\Sigma_{2},g_{2}(t))
is flat by the expression of Riemann curvature (
4.1
) which shows, under the fiber-collapsing assumption,
\left|\textup{Rm}_{g(t)}(X,Y,Y,X)\right|\leq C\left|X\right|^{2}\left|Y\right|^{2}
for any horizontal vectors
X
and
Y
before rescaling, and hence after rescaling the horizontal component of
\textup{Rm}_{g_{\infty(t)}}
would vanish.

For the volume non-collapsing cases (ii) and (iii), we expect that case (ii), where all of
F_{k}(s_{0},t)
’s converge to
0
as
t\to T
, would be similar to the contracting divisor case in
[
GS16
,
JST23
]
where the whole zero section contracts to a point. By adopting the proofs in
[
GS16
,
JST23
]
, it should be possible to show that the blow-up limit should be a shrinking Ricci soliton on the total space of a line bundle over
N
constructed by Dancer-Wang in
[
DW11
]
. However, case (iii), where some but not all of
F_{k}(s_{0},t)
’s converge to
0
while others remain positive at
t=T
, seems to be more subtle and the current literature does not seem to give any suggestion on what kind of blow-up limits will be obtained. One possibility is that it would be a shrinking Ricci soliton on the total space of a line bundle over a product of some compact Kähler-Einstein manifolds and a flat space, or on the product of a flat space and the total space of a line bundle over a product of compact Kähler-Einstein manifolds. It would be an interesting direction for further research.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

First for a submersion
\pi
and the fact that
\pi_{*}V=0
, we have

\pi_{*}[X,V]=[\pi_{*}X,\pi_{*}V]=0.

Therefore,
[X,V]\in\ker\pi_{*}=\mathcal{V}
. Then, by the Koszul’s formula, we have

\displaystyle 2g(\nabla_{X}Y,V)

\displaystyle=X\big(g(Y,V)\big)+Y\big(g(X,V)\big)-V\big(g(X,Y)\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+g([X,Y],V)-g([X,V],Y)-g([Y,V],X)

\displaystyle=-V\left(\sum_{i}F_{i}^{2}\pi_{i}^{*}g_{N_{i}}(X,Y)\right)+g([X,Y],V)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}g(\nabla F_{i}^{2},V)\pi_{i}^{*}g_{N_{i}}(X,Y)+g([X,Y],V).

Since the above holds for all
V\in\mathcal{V}
, we have

(6.1)

2\mathcal{V}(\nabla_{X}Y)=-\sum_{i}\mathcal{V}(\nabla F_{i}^{2})\pi_{i}^{*}g_{N_{i}}(X,Y)+\mathcal{V}[X,Y].

This proves the first equality since
A_{X}Y=\mathcal{V}(\nabla_{X}Y)
for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
. For the second equality, we relate
\mathcal{V}[X,Y]
with
d\omega
and consider

\displaystyle d\omega(X,Y,JV)

\displaystyle=X\big(\omega(Y,JV)\big)-Y\big(\omega(X,JV)\big)+JV\big(\omega(X,Y)\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\omega\big([X,Y],JV\big)+\omega\big([X,JV],Y\big)-\omega\big([Y,JV],X\big)

\displaystyle=X\big(g(JY,JV)\big)-Y\big(g(JX,JV)\big)+JV\big(g(JX,Y)\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-g\big(J[X,Y],JV\big)+g\big(J[X,JV],Y\big)-g\big(J[Y,JV],X\big)

\displaystyle=JV\big(g(JX,Y)\big)-g\big(J[X,Y],JV\big)

\displaystyle=JV\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}^{2}\pi_{i}^{*}g_{N_{i}}(JX,Y)\right)-g\big([X,Y],V\big).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle g\big([X,Y],V\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}g\big(\nabla F_{i}^{2},JV\big)\pi_{i}^{*}g_{N_{i}}(JX,Y)\big)-d\omega(X,Y,JV)

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{i=1}^{r}g\big(J\nabla\log F_{i},V)F_{i}^{2}\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{N_{i}}(X,Y)-d\omega(X,Y,JV),

and hence

(6.2)

\mathcal{V}[X,Y]=-2\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}^{2}\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{N_{i}}(X,Y)\mathcal{V}J\nabla\log F_{i}-\mathcal{V}\left(d\omega(X,Y,J(\cdot))\right)^{\sharp}.

It completes the second equality.

Next, we take any
Y\in\mathcal{H}
and consider

\displaystyle g\big(\nabla_{X}V,Y\big)

\displaystyle=X\big(g(V,Y)\big)-g(V,\nabla_{X}Y)

\displaystyle=-g(V,A_{X}Y).

Hence, we have
\mathcal{H}\nabla_{X}V=-g\big(V,A_{X}(\cdot)\big)^{\sharp}
. The last equality is trivial.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2.2
.

It follows from the fact that if
\{e_{i}\}
is an orthonormal (with respect to
g
) frame of
\mathcal{H}
, then

\displaystyle\left|F_{k}^{2}\pi_{k}^{*}\omega_{N_{k}}(e_{i},e_{j})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|F_{k}^{2}\pi_{k}^{*}g_{N_{k}}(Je_{i},e_{j})\right|

\displaystyle\leq F_{k}^{2}\left|d\pi_{k}(Je_{i})\right|_{g_{N_{k}}}\left|d\pi_{k}(e_{j})\right|_{g_{N_{k}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left|d\pi_{k}(Je_{i})\right|_{g}\left|d\pi_{k}(e_{j})\right|_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\left|Je_{i}\right|_{g}\left|e_{j}\right|_{g}=1.

Similarly, we have

\left|F_{k}^{2}\pi_{k}^{*}\omega_{N_{k}}(e_{i},e_{j})\right|\leq 1.

The corollary then follows from Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

As the tensor
\tilde{A}
are related to the tensor
A
by
\tilde{A}=\textup{proj}_{TP_{s}}(A)
, and that
\nabla\log F_{i}\in\text{span}\{\nu\}
, by Lemma
2.1
we have

\tilde{A}_{X}Y=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}[X,Y].

Taking the exterior derivative of
\eta^{*}
, we get:

\displaystyle d\eta^{\ast}(X_{i},Y)

\displaystyle=(\tilde{\nabla}_{X_{i}}\eta^{\ast})Y-(\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}\eta^{\ast})X_{i}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\nabla}_{X_{i}}(\eta^{\ast}(Y))-\eta^{\ast}(\tilde{\nabla}_{X_{i}}Y)-\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}(\eta^{\ast}(X_{i}))+\eta^{\ast}(\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}X_{i})

\displaystyle=-\eta^{\ast}([X_{i},Y])=-g_{s}(\zeta^{\ast},[X_{i},Y])=-2g_{s}(\tilde{A}_{X_{i}}Y,\zeta^{*}).

On the other hand, as
d\eta=\sum_{i}q_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{N_{i}}
and
dH(X_{i})=dH(Y)=0
, we have

\displaystyle d\eta^{\ast}(X_{i},Y)

\displaystyle=(dH\wedge\eta+Hd\eta)(X_{i},Y)=Hq_{i}\omega_{N_{i}}(X_{i},Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{i}H}{F_{i}^{2}}g(JX_{i},Y)

completing the proof of the first equality.

The second equality follows from the fact that
g_{s}(\tilde{A}_{X}\zeta^{\ast},Y)=-g_{s}(\zeta^{\ast},\tilde{A}_{X}Y)
.

∎

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

From
[
BG08
, Cor. 2.5.17]
, we have

K_{s}(X_{i},Z_{i})=K_{N_{i}}(X_{i},Z_{i})-3\left|\tilde{A}_{X_{i}}Z_{i}\right|_{g_{s}}^{2}.

Note that
\tilde{A}_{X_{i}}Z_{i}
has only
\zeta^{*}
-component, we have

\left|\tilde{A}_{X_{i}}Z_{i}\right|^{2}_{g_{s}}=g(\tilde{A}_{X_{i}}Z_{i},\zeta^{*})^{2}=\frac{q_{i}^{2}H^{2}}{4F_{i}^{4}}g_{s}(J_{N_{i}}X_{i},Z_{i})^{2}

from Lemma
2.3
, completing the proof of the first result.

Similarly, using the fact that
T=0
under the ansatz (
2.1
), Corollary 2.5.17 in
[
BG08
]
shows

K_{s}(X_{i},\zeta^{*})=\left|\tilde{A}_{X_{i}}\zeta^{*}\right|^{2}=\left|\frac{q_{i}H}{2F_{i}^{2}}JX_{i}\right|^{2}=\frac{q_{i}^{2}H^{2}}{4F_{i}^{4}}\left|X_{i}\right|^{2}_{g_{s}}

proving the second result.

From
[
BG08
, Theorem 2.5.18]
and noting that
T=0
under ansatz (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textup{Ric}}(X_{i},Y_{i})

\displaystyle=\textup{Ric}_{N_{i}}(X_{i},Y_{i})-2g_{s}(\tilde{A}_{X_{i}},\tilde{A}_{Y_{i}})

\displaystyle=k_{i}g_{N_{i}}(X_{i},Y_{i})-2g_{s}(\tilde{A}_{X_{i}},\tilde{A}_{Y_{i}})

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{i}}{F_{i}^{2}}g_{s}(X_{i},Y_{i})-2g_{s}(\tilde{A}_{X_{i}},\tilde{A}_{Y_{i}}).

Here we have used the fact that
(N_{i},g_{N_{i}})
is a Kähler-Einstein manifold. From Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle g_{s}(\tilde{A}_{X_{i}},\tilde{A}_{Y_{i}})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q_{i}H}{2F_{i}^{2}}\right)^{2}g_{s}(JX_{i},JY_{i})=\frac{q_{i}^{2}H^{2}}{4F_{i}^{4}}g_{s}(X_{i},Y_{i})

completing the proof of the third equation.

Finally, from
[
BG08
, Theorem 2.5.18]
again and observing that
T=0
and fibers of
\pi:P_{s}\to N
is 1-dimensional (hence its Ricci curvature vanishes), we have

\widetilde{\textup{Ric}}(\zeta^{*},\zeta^{*})=g_{s}(\tilde{A}\zeta^{*},\tilde{A}\zeta^{*})

Suppose
\{e_{a},Je_{a}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
{\mathcal{H}}
such that each
e_{a}
is in exactly one of
TN_{i}
, then we have

\displaystyle g_{s}(\tilde{A}\zeta^{*},\tilde{A}\zeta^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\left(\left|\tilde{A}_{e_{a}}\zeta^{*}\right|_{g_{s}}^{2}+\left|\tilde{A}_{Je_{a}}\zeta^{*}\right|_{g_{s}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sum_{e_{a}\in TN_{i}}\left(\left|\tilde{A}_{e_{a}}\zeta^{*}\right|_{g_{s}}^{2}+\left|\tilde{A}_{Je_{a}}\zeta^{*}\right|_{g_{s}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}2n_{i}\left(\frac{q_{i}H}{2F_{i}^{2}}\right)^{2}

proving the last equality.
∎

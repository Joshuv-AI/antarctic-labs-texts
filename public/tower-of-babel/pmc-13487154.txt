A data-driven analysis and forecasting of Leishmaniasis-COVID-19 co-infection model using ensemble Kalman filter
Authors: Muhammad Said, Mussa A Stephano, Il Hyo Jung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCo-infections involving viruses and parasites pose a major concern for global health, particularly in areas where both infections are endemic. In this paper, we present a co-infection epidemic model that considers the transmission dynamics of COVID-19 and leishmaniasis among human and vector populations. The model is developed based on considering the specific latency periods of each infection, treatment, recovery, reinfection, and cross-infection process. The Ensemble Kalman Filter technique is applied to estimate the key model’s parameters using real data, incorporating temporal variation and uncertainty in disease transmission and progression. The model is first divided into COVID-19-only and leishmaniasis-only sub-models to validate the dynamics of each disease over 12 months. For the COVID-19 sub-model, several important estimated parameters include transmission rate , disease mortality , and treatment rate , indicating a relatively slow response to disease treatments and moderate disease severity. On the other hand, the Leishmaniasis model had a slightly lower transmission rate , and a considerably higher treatment rate , implying more efficient case management of the vector-borne disease. The complete model for co-infection, estimated from three groups of six months of data, exhibits synergistic effects through interaction parameters and that reflect a higher likelihood of acquiring another disease while already infected with the first. The model predictions were highly consistent with observed data, and the estimated parameters show significant temporal variability. Projections over a three-month horizon demonstrated strong predictive performance. These results underscore the need for an integrated surveillance and control framework for co-endemic disease management.Keywords: COVID-19, Leishmaniasis, Co-infection model, EnKF, Forecasting, Parameter estimation, Real data analysisSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Co-infections by different pathogens are an increasing area of concern, especially in areas where endemic diseases coincide with global pandemics1. Leishmaniasis, caused by protozoan parasites of the genus Leishmania and transmitted by sand-flies, is a neglected tropical disease that affects millions worldwide2. COVID-19, caused by the novel coronavirus SARS-CoV-2, has taken a deep toll on global health systems3,4. While both diseases operate through different biological mechanisms, their geographical overlap in high-disease-burden areas increases the possibility of co-infection, raising important questions about combined effects on the immune system and disease severity in co-infected hosts5.

The biological interaction of Leishmaniasis and COVID-19 co-infection is complex and largely modulated by their contrasting effects on the immune system. Leishmaniasis is characterised by parasitic invasion of macrophages, leading to disruption of immune regulation and resultant chronic inflammation6. COVID-19 causes a hyperactive immune response in severe cases, often leading to a cytokine storm. Co-infection may amplify such immune disturbances, enhancing tissue damage and disease severity. Moreover, immunosuppression due to visceral Leishmaniasis may impede effective immune responses against SARS-CoV-2 and complicate treatment outcomes7.

The co-infection context is further complicated by overlapping biological and environmental risk factors. Conditions prevalent in Leishmaniasis-endemic regions, including malnutrition, poverty, and weakened immunity, may increase vulnerability to severe COVID-198. Furthermore, the sand-fly vector of Leishmaniasis is most common in tropical and subtropical climates, the same regions where COVID-19 has had considerable impact. Understanding the biological consequences of this co-infection is important for informing therapeutic interventions and mitigating the combined health burden9.

The potential impact of co-infection on healthcare systems is substantial. As illustrated in Fig. 1, Leishmaniasis–COVID-19 co-infection involves overlapping transmission pathways, immune impairment, and compounded disease manifestations that can hinder diagnosis, delay treatment, and elevate mortality risk. Co-infected individuals are therefore likely to require more elaborate management approaches, given the interaction of immune responses and the distinct therapies required by each disease10. This adds to the resource burden in areas already constrained by Leishmaniasis management needs. Knowledge of such dynamics is essential for informing public health policies on resource optimisation and integrated disease management11.

Transmission pathways and health effects of Leishmaniasis–COVID-19 co-infection. Sandfly-mediated transmission of Leishmaniasis involves humans and animal reservoirs, resulting in cutaneous and visceral disease manifestations. When combined with COVID-19, overlapping symptoms and immune impairment can hinder diagnosis, delay treatment, and elevate disease burden and fatality risk.

Mathematical modelling plays a fundamental role in understanding, predicting, and controlling the spread of infectious diseases, providing a rigorous quantitative framework for evaluating intervention strategies such as vaccination, treatment, and vector control12–19. In particular, compartmental models incorporating stability analysis, sensitivity evaluation, and optimal control have proven indispensable tools for characterising transmission dynamics across a wide range of pathogens, including dengue, Mpox, hepatitis, and emerging respiratory viruses12–19 Building on this broader methodological foundation, various mathematical models have been developed specifically to study the dynamics of co-infections, offering important insights into how multiple pathogens interact within a shared host population20–25. Teklu and Mekonnen20 developed and analysed a mathematical model of HIV/AIDS and pneumonia co-infection, examining treatment possibilities at each stage of the disease and deriving effective treatment strategies. Said et al.21 performed identifiability analysis of an HIV–Ebola co-infection model using the maximum likelihood estimation method. Amoah-Mensah et al.22 investigated the stability of a mathematical model describing co-infection dynamics between Zika virus and malaria in a malaria-endemic region, advancing our understanding of disease relationships and contributing to preventive strategy development. Said et al.23 formulated and analysed a compartmental leptospirosis–COVID-19 co-infection model fitted to real data, establishing a methodological precedent for the present work. Slater et al.24 utilised a mathematical model to investigate the interaction between malaria and lymphatic filariasis, shedding light on the population-level dynamics of parasitic co-infections. Ringa et al.25 introduced a mathematical model to study the dynamics of HIV and COVID-19 co-infection, analysing transmission dynamics and optimal control strategies.

Despite this growing body of literature, no prior study has developed a compartmental model that jointly captures the transmission dynamics of COVID-19 and Leishmaniasis with explicit human-vector coupling. Furthermore, existing co-infection models rarely employ data assimilation frameworks such as the Ensemble Kalman Filter (EnKF) to estimate time-varying parameters from real epidemiological data. The present work addresses this gap by: (i) formulating a novel ODE-based co-infection model that integrates bidirectional disease interactions, treatment dynamics, and an explicit sandfly vector population; (ii) applying the EnKF to dynamically estimate model parameters from real-world COVID-19 and Leishmaniasis case data; and (iii) performing short-term forecasting with quantified uncertainty. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first data-driven co-infection modelling study combining these two pathogens.

In this work, we present a nonlinear compartmental model based on ordinary differential equations to study the dynamics of COVID-19 and Leishmaniasis co-infection, with explicit modelling of human and sandfly vector populations. A data assimilation framework using the EnKF is applied to estimate time-evolving model parameters from epidemiological data reports. The model is applied to validate single-disease dynamics, quantify co-infection interactions, and make short-term forecasts.

Ensemble Kalman filter for parameter estimation

The Ensemble Kalman Filter (EnKF) is a sequential data assimilation technique that combines model predictions with observed data to estimate both the state and parameters of nonlinear systems26,27. Unlike the traditional Kalman Filter, which assumes linear dynamics, or the Extended Kalman Filter, which uses linear approximations, the EnKF employs an ensemble of state realisations to compute error statistics, making it particularly suitable for nonlinear and high-dimensional epidemiological models28–30.

Discussion

The simulation results offer important insights into the transmission dynamics of COVID-19 and Leishmaniasis, both individually and as a co-infection system. The application of the EnKF enabled dynamic estimation of key epidemiological parameters while accounting for uncertainty in real data. Individual sub-model validation over 12 months provided a solid foundation for the co-infection analysis. The strong agreement between observations and predictions confirms the model structure and estimation framework.

In the COVID-19 sub-model, the estimated is consistent with community-level SARS-CoV-2 spread. The relatively slow treatment initiation rate () may reflect diagnostic delays or healthcare access barriers. In contrast, the The leishmaniasis sub-model showed a higher treatment rate (), suggesting a more efficient clinical response to this disease in the modelled setting. The lower mortality among Leishmaniasis-treated individuals () highlight effective antileishmanial therapy. These differences reflect fundamentally distinct transmission mechanisms and disease management realities: COVID-19 requires rapid response to prevent large-scale community outbreaks, while Leishmaniasis control relies more heavily on vector control and sustained treatment access.

The co-infection analysis revealed that the simultaneous presence of both diseases significantly alters transmission and clinical dynamics. The elevated in the co-infection model may reflect heightened susceptibility due to immunosuppression, while the slightly lower may result from under-reporting or interaction-induced suppression of Leishmaniasis spread. The high values of and confirm a strong bidirectional synergy. The higher co-infection mortality rate () compared to single-disease rates underscores the compounded clinical risk.

These findings carry several important implications for public health practice. First, the high values of the interaction parameters () indicate that in co-endemic settings, the presence of either disease substantially elevates the risk of the other. This calls for integrated co-infection surveillance systems capable of simultaneously tracking and reporting both diseases and flagging at-risk individuals. Second, resource allocation strategies in Leishmaniasis-endemic regions must account for the potential for COVID-19 co-infection, which may require additional clinical resources (intensive care, combined diagnostic protocols). Third, the higher mortality in co-infected individuals argues for prioritising this group in triage and treatment protocols. Fourth, the success of the EnKF framework in dynamically tracking parameter changes demonstrates its value for real-time epidemic monitoring; health authorities could use such adaptive models to update disease projections as new surveillance data becomes available. Finally, the differing treatment dynamics between the two diseases (faster uptake for Leishmaniasis, slower for COVID-19) suggest that different intervention priorities are needed: COVID-19 management should emphasise early detection and rapid clinical referral, while Leishmaniasis control should maintain strong vector control programmes and sustained access to antileishmanial therapies.

Conclusion

This study presents a comprehensive modelling framework to analyse the transmission dynamics of COVID-19 and Leishmaniasis, both individually and as a co-infection system, using the Ensemble Kalman Filter for dynamic parameter estimation. Sub-model simulations provided a clear epidemiological characterisation of each disease, while the co-infection model revealed critical interaction effects that alter transmission rates and clinical outcomes. The quantitative agreement between model projections and observed data confirms both the model structure and the capacity of the EnKF to infer time-varying disease dynamics from real-world data.

Significant differences in transmission mechanisms, mortality rates, and treatment dynamics were identified between the two diseases. COVID-19’s direct human-to-human transmission and low treatment uptake necessitate rapid response interventions, while Leishmaniasis’ vector-borne nature and favourable treatment outcomes underscore the importance of persistent vector control and treatment access. Under co-infection, the bidirectional interaction parameters and indicate significant mutual amplification of transmission risk and mortality, requiring comprehensive clinical management.

The findings underscore the importance of integrated disease surveillance in co-endemic regions, and the successful application of the EnKF demonstrates the value of adaptive data assimilation for real-time epidemic monitoring. These insights contribute to a more nuanced understanding of multi-pathogen epidemics and can inform holistic public health responses and resource allocation policies.

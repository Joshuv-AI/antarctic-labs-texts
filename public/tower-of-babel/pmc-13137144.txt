The UFSP2-C1orf27 complex positions nascent GPCRs to the ufmylation system for ER export control
Authors: Xin Xu, Yinquan Fang, Lifen Qiu, Wei Huang, Honglin Li, Guangyu Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe ufmylation system is implicated in multiple cellular processes and human diseases; our recent study has identified its function in protein trafficking, but detailed mechanisms remain elusive. Here, we reveal cooperative actions of ufmylation protease UFSP2 and endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-anchored protein C1orf27 on ER-to-Golgi transport of some G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs). UFSP2 interacts with C1orf27 for ER recruitment, and its activation is dispensable for recruitment but essential for ER export of GPCRs. Structural analysis suggests that UFSP2 and C1orf27 are diverged from a C. elegans ODR8-like protein. Similar to their C. elegans homologs, UFSP2, C1orf27, and cargo GPCRs form a multi-protein complex, and GPCR interaction with C1orf27 is required for their ER export and forward delivery. Collectively, these data demonstrate that the C1orf27-UFSP2 complex interacts with its cargo GPCRs, directs them to ufmylation regulation, and controls their ER export, providing important insights into the trafficking function of the ufmylation system and GPCR maturation processing.Subject areas: Biochemistry, Molecular biology, Cell biology

Introduction

Ufmylation is a ubiquitination-like post-translational modification in which ubiquitin-fold modifier 1 (UFM1) is conjugated to the targeted proteins by a three-enzyme cascade involving the E1 UFM1 activation enzyme 5 (UBA5), the E2 UFM1-conjugating enzyme 1 (UFC1), and the E3 UFM1-specific ligase 1 (UFL1).1,2,3 UFM1-specific proteases 1 and 2 (UFSP1 and UFSP2) are responsible for the cleavage of UFM1 from the ufmylated proteins as well as for the generation of free mature UFM1 from its precursors to be used for protein ufmylation.4,5 Recent studies have shown that ufmylation is implicated in some human diseases, such as hematopoietic diseases,6 neuronal diseases,7,8,9 and cancers.10,11 and regulates multiple cellular processes, such as cell death,12 survival,13 development,14 senescence,15 and immune responses.16 Most cellular functions of the ufmylation system are associated with the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), including ER stress and homeostasis,17 ER-phagy,18,19,20 and ribosome- and translocation-associated quality control.21,22,23,24,25 ER-localized proteins, such as C1orf27 and UFM1-binding protein 1 (UFBP1), have been demonstrated to directly interact with ufmylation components, anchoring the ufmylation system at the ER.26,27,28

G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) constitute the largest superfamily of cell surface signaling proteins and modulate a wide variety of cell functions under physiological and pathological conditions. The human genome encodes about 800 GPCRs which, based on their amino acid sequences, can be divided into classes A (rhodopsin), B (secretin and adhesion), C (glutamate), and F (Frizzled) subfamilies. Family A contains about 90% of GPCRs, including a large group of olfactory receptors (ORs) that are expressed in the cilia and synapses of olfactory neurons and are responsible for the detection of odor molecules. It has been known that, after synthesis in the ER, GPCR targeting to the cell surface dictates the cellular response to hormones and drugs, and its dysregulation is directly associated with the pathogenesis of human diseases.29 In the past decades, a number of regulatory proteins that directly interact with a specific GPCR or a subset of GPCRs, as well as structural determinants embedded within the receptors, have been identified to control GPCR biosynthetic transport from the ER to the functional destinations en route through the Golgi apparatus.30,31,32,33,34,35

Human C1orf27 is an ER-anchored type II membrane protein with physiological functions being poorly studied. Odorant response abnormal 4 (ODR4) is the C. elegans homolog of C1orf27. The ODR4 gene was identified in chemotaxis-defective screens more than thirty years ago36 and its protein product was found to be expressed exclusively on intracellular membranes in specialized chemosensory neurons, where it enhanced the surface delivery of some ORs, such as ODR10.37,38 Like ODR4, ODR8, the only UFSP in C. elegans, was shown to regulate the subcellular localization of ORs in chemosensory neurons.37,38 We have recently demonstrated that the depletion of C1orf27 and several core components of the ufmylation cascade, including UFSP2, each markedly inhibits the transport of nascent GPCRs, specifically from the ER to the Golgi, in mammalian cells,39,40 revealing important functions for C1orf27 and the ufmylation system in controlling the ER export of GPCRs. However, the underlying mechanisms are still not well elucidated. Here we demonstrate that UFSP2 and C1orf27 cooperatively regulate the ER-Golgi traffic of some GPCRs, which is mediated through direct interaction, forming a multi-protein complex at the ER, which directs nascent GPCRs to be regulated by the ufmylation system for ER export.

Results

Cooperative action of UFSP2 and C1orf27 on the ER-to-Golgi transport of a subset of GPCRs

As a recent study has demonstrated that, similar to UFSP2, C1orf27 is important for protein deufmylation in cell,26 to study how the ufmylation system regulated GPCR export from the ER, we compared the effects of depleting UFSP2 and C1orf27 individually and in combination on the ER-to-Golgi transport of five GPCRs [α2A-adrenergic receptor (AR), α2B-AR, β2-AR, CXCR4 and muscarinic acetylcholine receptor type 3 (M3R) ] and two non-GPCR plasma membrane proteins [vesicular stomatitis viral glycoprotein (VSVG) and epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)] in the retention using the selective hooks (RUSH) assays. UFSP2 KO cells were generated using the CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing technology, and C1orf27 knockdown was achieved by the transient expression of siRNA (Figure 1A). Individual cargoes were conjugated with enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP) and a streptavidin (Str) binding peptide (SBP) at the N-terminus (NT), and Str fused to the ER retention signal KDEL (Str-KDEL) was used as a hook (Figure 1B). Previous studies have shown that before biotin induction, these cargo molecules are retained in the ER. After biotin induction, they export from the ER to the juxtanuclear region, where they are extensively colocalized with the Golgi markers. Therefore, in this study, cargoes that were highly concentrated in the juxtanuclear region after biotin induction were considered to be transported to the Golgi without using Golgi markers.39,40,41,42,43

The ER-Golgi transport of α2A-AR, α2B-AR, and β2-AR was significantly inhibited in UFSP2 KO cells and in C1orf27 siRNA-transfected cells as compared with that in control cells (control KO cells transfected with control siRNA) (Figures 1C and 1D). Interestingly, as compared to individual UFSP2 KO or C1orf27 knockdown, simultaneous depletion of UFSP2 and C1orf27 did not exaggerate the inhibitory effects, and the magnitudes of inhibition by depleting UFSP2 and C1orf27 individually and in combination were virtually the same at either time point (Figures 1C and 1D). In marked contrast, combinational depletion of UFSP2 and C1orf27 did not disrupt the ER-to-Golgi transport of CXCR4, M3R, VSVG, and EGFR (Figures 1E and 1F).

Live cell RUSH assays were used to further characterize the effects of UFSP2 and C1orf27 on the export kinetics of GPCRs using α2A-AR as a representative. In control cells, α2A-AR was clearly transported to the Golgi at 15 min, and the strongest Golgi expression was found at 30 min after biotin incubation (Figures 2A and 2B). The export curves of α2A-AR observed in UFSP2 KO cells, C1orf27 siRNA-transfected cells, and C1orf27 siRNA-transfected UFSP2 KO cells were quite similar, which were shifted to the right as compared to that in control cells (Figure 2B and Videos S1, S2, S3, and S4). These data suggest that UFSP2 and C1orf27 cooperatively regulate GPCR transport from the ER to the Golgi.

UFSP2 and C1orf27 are structurally related proteins and form a complex

To elucidate the mechanism underlying the cooperative action of UFSP2 and C1orf27, we analyzed their interaction and structures. Earlier studies have shown that UFSP2 and C1orf27 interact to form a complex.26,37 Consistently, they formed a complex in HEK293 cells in co-immunoprecipitation (IP) assays (Figure 3A). We then used bioluminescence resonance energy transfer (BRET) assays and in situ proximity ligation assays (PLAs) to determine if the UFSP2-C1orf27 interaction could occur spatially at the ER, at endogenous expression levels, and in live cells. In BRET assays, strong net BRET was detected in cells expressing UFSP2 and C1orf27, while almost no BRET was seen in cells expressing C1orf27 and Rab43, which was used as a negative control (Figure 3B). In PLAs, the cells were transfected with GFP or GFP-C1orf27 and stained with antibodies against UFSP2 and GFP. PLA signals were markedly higher in cells expressing GFP-C1orf27 than in cells expressing GFP alone (Figures 3C and 3D). To study endogenous C1orf27 and UFSP2 in PLAs. The cells were stained with antibodies against C1orf27 and UFSP2 individually or in combination. PLA signals were much greater in cells stained with two antibodies together than in cells stained with individual antibodies (Figures 3E and 3F).

The crystal structures of UFSP2 and its C. elegans analog ODR8 have been revealed.28,44 UFSP2 is composed of a C-terminal catalytic domain and a new N-terminal domain, which displays a 6-stranded β-sheet and five helices.28 The structure of ODR8 is similar to that of UFSP2, except it has an MPN domain in the N-terminal extension.44 As predicted by AlphaFold3,45 the overall structure of C1orf27 and ODR4 is remarkably similar and composed of two domains. Surprisingly, the structure of their N-terminal domains resembles to that of the N-terminal extension domain of ODR8, showing a 4-stranded antiparallel β-sheet flanked by three helices and a pair of parallel β-strands on one side and two small helices on the opposite side, indicative of an MPN domain. The structure of their C-terminal domains is similar to that of the N-terminal domains of ODR8 and UFSP2 (Figure 3G). These data suggest that C1orf27/ODR4 and UFSP2 might be diverged from an ODR8-like protein containing the catalytic, MPN, and new domains, and that C1orf27 and UFSP2 share some structural features (Figure 3G). As predicted by AlphaFold3, the interaction between UFSP2 and C1orf27 is mediated through the N-terminal E122-S138 residues of UFSP2 (EMSTSLAAVTPIIERES) and the C-terminal Q259-C275 residues of C1orf27 (QLLLNSDHRSTATVQIC) (Figure 3G).

UFSP2 inactivation does not affect its ER recruitment via C1orf27 but inhibits GPCR export from the ER

Recent studies have demonstrated an important function of the C1orf27-UFSP2 interaction to recruit UFSP2 to the ER membrane, which in turn affects the function of the ufmylation system.26 UFBP1 was also shown to interact with UFSP2 and enhance its localization at the ER.28 We first compared the abilities of these two proteins to induce UFSP2 recruitment to the ER using protein-tyrosine phosphatase 1B (PTP1B) as an ER marker in BRET assays (Figure 4A). As expected, GFP-C1orf27 was extensively colocalized with the ER marker dsRed2-ER (Figure S1A). The expression of C1orf27 significantly enhanced the BRET between UFSP2-Rluc8 and Venus-PTP1B (Figure 4A). Imaging analysis showed that UFSP2 was mainly expressed in the cytoplasm in GFP-expressing cells, while it was strongly colocalized with C1orf27 in C1orf27-expressing cells (Figure 4B). However, enhanced expression of UFBP1 affected neither BRET between C1orf27 and UFSP2 (Figure 4A) nor the subcellular distribution of UFSP2 (Figure S1B). These data suggest that C1orf27 is mainly responsible for UFSP2 recruitment to the ER.

We next studied the relationship between UFSP2 recruitment to the ER and its deufmylation activity. UFSP2 and its three catalytically inactive mutants, C302S, C302A, and H428A,28,46 were similarly expressed in the cytoplasm in GFP-expressing cells and strongly colocalized with C1orf27 in C1orf27-expressing cells (Figure 4B). These data suggest that UFSP2 recruitment to the ER via interaction with C1orf27 is independent of its activation.

We then measured the effect of transient expression of UFSP2 inactive mutants on the ER-Golgi transport of α2B-AR. The expression of UFSP2 inactive mutants each markedly inhibited the ER-Golgi transport of α2B-AR at 15 min after biotin induction in RUSH assays (Figures 4C and 4D). These data show that the enzymatic activity of UFSP2 is needed for GPCR export from the ER.

The cargo GPCRs, C1orf27 and UFSP2, form ternary complexes

To further study the mechanism underlying the function of C1orf27 and UFSP2 in GPCR export from the ER, we measured their interaction with the cargo GPCRs, particularly α2A-AR and α2B-AR, in co-IP assays. Consistent with our previous studies,39,40 α2A-AR tagged with HA was found to form a complex with C1orf27 (Figure 5A), but not UFSP2 (Figure 5B), after IP with HA antibodies in HEK293 cells. However, α2A-AR and UFSP2 were found to form a complex when C1orf27 was overexpressed (Figure 5C). Similar to α2A-AR, α2B-AR pulled down C1orf27 (Figure 5D), but not UFSP2 (Figure 5E). α2B-AR also pulled down UFSP2 when C1orf27 was co-expressed (Figure 5F). Furthermore, in modified live cell BRET assays in which α2B-AR was tagged with Rluc8 and anchored at the ER via the RUSH system, coexpression of C1orf27 markedly enhanced net BRET between α2B-AR-Rluc8 and Venus-UFSP2 (Figure 5G). These data demonstrate that α2A-AR and α2B-AR interactions with UFSP2 are mediated through C1orf27 and suggest that the cargo GPCRs, C1orf27 and UFSP2, can form ternary complexes in which C1orf27 interacts with GPCRs and UFSP2.

We then determined if α2B-AR interaction with C1orf27 could alter the C1orf27-UFSP2 interaction as well as UFSP2 recruitment to the ER. In BRET assays, the expression of ER-anchored RUSH α2B-AR had no effect on BRET between C1orf27 and UFSP2 (Figure 6B). The expression of HA-tagged RUSH α2B-AR also did not alter the ER recruitment of UFSP2 by C1orf27 (Figure 6C). These data suggest that C1orf27 interacts with GPCRs and UFSP2 are mutually inclusive.

Identification of the C1orf27-binding domain of α2B-AR and effects of removing the domain on the trafficking and signaling of the receptor

To identify the C1orf27-binding domain of α2B-AR, we focused on the third intracellular loop (ICL3) and C-terminus (CT), which consist of 165 and 23 residues, respectively. Both the ICL3 and the CT have been demonstrated to mediate α2B-AR interaction with a number of regulatory proteins involved in receptor signal regulation and trafficking.47,48 In glutathione S-transferase (GST) fusion protein pulldown assays, the α2B-AR ICL3, but not its CT, strongly interacted with C1orf27 (Figure 7A). The progressive deletion strategy was used to search for the ICL3 subdomain binding C1orf27, and the shortest fragment found was N327-E369, which, when further split, abolished the interaction with C1orf27 (Figures 7B and 7C). To confirm this domain indeed mediated α2B-AR interaction with C1orf27, we measured C1orf27 interactions with α2B-AR and its mutant Δ43, in which the fragment N327-E369 with 43 residues was truncated. In contrast to its WT counterpart, the mutant Δ43 was unable to form a complex with C1orf27 in co-IP assays (Figure 7D). Structural prediction by AlphaFold3 suggests that the fragment N327-E369 partially forms a helix, which binds to the interface between the N- and C-terminal domains of C1orf27 (Figure S2).

We then sought to figure out the functional importance of the α2B-AR-C1orf27 interaction by comparing α2B-AR and its mutant Δ43 in the ER-Golgi transport, steady state expression at the cell surface, and signaling using ERK1/2 activation as a readout. As measured in RUSH assays, the ER-Golgi transport of the mutant Δ43 was significantly less than that of WT α2B-AR at both 15 and 30 min after biotin induction (Figures 7E and 7F). After transient expression for 24 h, α2B-AR expression at the cell surface was measured by radioligand binding of intact live cells. The cell surface expression of the mutant Δ43 was reduced by more than 60% when compared to WT α2B-AR (Figure 7G). ERK1/2 activation in response to stimulation with UK14304, an α2-AR agonist, was much lower in cells expressing the mutant Δ43 than in cells expressing α2B-AR (Figures 7H and 7I). These data strongly suggest that α2B-AR interaction with C1orf27 is important for its export from the ER to the cell surface.

Discussion

One important finding presented in this paper is the cooperative action of C1orf27 and UFSP2 in the ER-Golgi transport of GPCRs. We found that the inhibitory effects of simultaneous depletion of C1orf27 and UFSP2 on the ER-to-Golgi transport of three GPCRs, including α2A-AR, α2B-AR, and β2-AR, were virtually identical to those produced by their depletion individually. These data suggest that the trafficking functions of C1orf27 and UFSP2 depend on each other, and their actions on GPCR transport are cooperative rather than additive. As C1orf27 and UFSP2 interact to form a complex, as discussed later in discussion, this complex acts as one unit to direct GPCR export from the ER to the Golgi.

Previous studies have demonstrated that C1orf27 interacts with UFSP226,37 and α2A-AR39 in mammalian cells and that ODR4 binds ODR8 and the OR ODR10 in C. elegans.37,38 Consistent with these studies, we have shown here that C1orf27 interacts with UFSP2 and the cargo α2B-AR. Together, these data imply that GPCR export from the ER in humans and nematodes is under control by parallel systems in which the trio of human proteins GPCR, C1orf27, and UFSP2 (Figure 8A) and their C. elegans analogs ODR10, ODR4, and ODR8 (Figure 8B) form ternary complexes. It is interesting to note that, based on the crystal structures and AlphaFold prediction, C1orf27, ODR4, and UFSP2 share some structural features, suggesting that they might have originated from an ODR8-like, three domain-containing protein (Figures 8A and 8B).

There are several important points about the formation, functions, and mechanisms of the ternary complex in GPCR anterograde trafficking from the ER. (1) The C1orf27-UFSP2 interaction not only recruits UFSP2 to the ER, which partially contributes to the ER localization of the ufmylation system,26,37 but also regulates GPCR export from the ER, which is mediated through interaction with the cargo receptor to form a ternary complex (Figure 8C). (2) Our data showing that UFSP2 inactive mutants are normally recruited to the ER but inhibit GPCR export suggest that, such as WT UFSP2, its inactive mutants form ternary complexes with C1orf27 and GPCRs, but these complexes are inactive, unable to couple the cargo GPCRs to the ufmylation regulation, thus functioning as dominant-negative mutants (Figure 8D). (3) Recent studies have shown that, similar to UFSP2, C1orf27 depletion markedly affects protein ufmylation,26 indicating that it is an essential component of the ufmylation system. As demonstrated here, the depletion of UFSP2 and C1orf27 produces similar inhibitory effects on GPCR transport. These data suggest the possibility that the role played by C1orf27 in GPCR transport may be attributable to its function in protein ufmylation/deufmylation. (4) Our data showing that the deletion of the C1orf27-binding domain N327-E369 markedly inhibits the ER-Golgi transport, surface expression, and signaling of α2B-AR strongly support the function of the ternary complex in directing GPCR export from the ER.

It is worth mentioning that, in addition to the complex GPCR-C1orf27-UFSP2, nascent GPCRs were shown to interact with UFBP1 and UFL1, forming another ternary complex GPCR-UFBP1-UFL1 at the ER membrane.40 Similar to UFSP2 recruitment to the ER via interaction with C1orf27, UFL1 recruitment to the ER is mediated through interaction with UFBP1.27 As such, two two-protein complexes, C1orf27-UFSP2 and UFBP1-UFL1, anchor the ufmylation system at the ER compartment, and both complexes are able to interact with nascent GPCRs to form ternary complexes to control GPCR export from the ER, indicating the complex regulation of GPCR anterograde trafficking at the ER by the protein ufmylation pathway. In addition, many GPCRs, including α2-ARs, are known to undergo homodimerization in the ER, which also regulates their ER export.49 These data suggest the possibility that C1orf27, UFSP2, and GPCRs may form larger oligomers to control receptor export from the ER.

In summary, our studies have shown the cooperative action of UFSP2 and C1orf27 in GPCR export from the ER, which is likely mediated through protein-protein interactions to form the ternary complex GPCR-C1orf27-UFSP2 at the ER, which links nascent GPCRs to the ufmylation cascade and directs their export. Overall, our data provide important insights into the functions of the ufmylation system in protein trafficking and GPCR maturation processing.

Limitations of the study

Although structural modeling suggests that specific domains of UFSP2 and C1orf27 are involved in the formation of C1orf27-UFSP2 and α2B-AR-C1orf27-UFSP2 complexes, whether these domains indeed mediate these protein-protein interactions has not been verified experimentally. Key UFM1 substrates whose ufmylation and deufmylation directly affect GPCR trafficking are not studied here. In addition, this study uses only HeLa and HEK293 cell lines, without the consideration of sex or gender variables, which may limit the generalizability of the study’s findings.

Experimental model and study participant details

Cell culture

HeLa (Cat# CRM-CCL-2) and HEK293 cells (Cat# CRL-1573) were purchased from ATCC. CRISPR control and UFSP2 KO HeLa and HEK293 cells were generated as described previously.40 The cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 units/ml penicillin and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. The cells were regularly tested for mycoplasma contamination and used within passages 10–15.

Method details

Plasmid constructions

GFP- and Venus-tagged C1orf27 and 3HA-tagged α2B-AR were generated as described.30,39,40 3HA-tagged α2B-AR mutant Δ43 and C1orf27 in the pcDAN3.1 vector, UFSP2 and Rab43 in the pRluc8-N1 vector, Myc-tagged C1orf27 in the pCMV-Myc vector, and DsRed-tagged UFSP2 in the pDsRed-Monomer-C1 vector were generated by PCR using primers. RUSH plasmids expressing GFP-tagged α2A-AR, α2B-AR, β2-AR, CXCR4, M3R, VSVG and EGFR were generated as described previously.40,42 To generate the RUSH plasmids Str-KDEL_SBP-3HA-α2B-AR, Str-KDEL_SBP-α2B-AR-Rluc8 and Str-KDEL_SBP-EGFP-α2B-AR Δ43, 3HA-α2B-AR and α2B-AR Δ43 were amplified by PCR using primers and then subcloned into Str-KDEL_SBP-EGFP-Ecadherin after release of EGFP-Ecadherin or Ecadherin. GST fusion protein constructs coding the intracellular domains of α2B-AR were generated in the pGEX-4T-1 vector as described previously.33 Venus-PTP1B was kindly provided by Dr. Nevin Lambert (Augusta University). The QuikChange site-directed mutagenesis kit (Agilent) was used to generate mutations.

Cell transfection

For biochemical studies, transient transfection of cells was carried out by using Lipofectamine 2000 as described previously.32 For imaging studies, cells were transfected by using linear polyethyleneimine (PEI) as described.42

Generation of UFSP2 knockout cells by CRISPR-Cas9

UFSP2 KO HEK293 and HeLa cells were generated by using the lentiCRISPR v2 vector (Addgene plasmid #52961) and targeting the sgRNA sequence 5-AATAAGAGGAGGCCTTGATT-3 as described previously.40 UFSP2 KO was determined by immunoblotting.

siRNA-mediated depletion

siRNA targeting human C1orf27 and negative control med GC siRNA were purchased from Invitrogen and siRNA-mediated depletion was carried out as described previously.39 C1orf27 knockdown was confirmed by immunoblotting. All experiments were performed at 48 h after siRNA transfection.

Fluorescence microscopy

To measure protein transport in fixed cells, cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min before imaging. To measure cargo transport in live cells, images were captured at an interval of 30 s. All images were captured using LAS X software with a 63× objective on a Leica Stellaris 5 confocal microscope equipped with an Okolab UNO stage top incubator. The fluorescence intensities were quantified by using NIH ImageJ.

RUSH transport assays

RUSH assays were essentially carried out as described.39,40 Briefly, after transfection with RUSH plasmids for 20 h, the ER-Golgi transport was induced with biotin at 40 μM plus cycloheximide (CHX) at 400 μg/mL for different time periods as indicated in figure legends. Cargo expression in the Golgi apparatus and the whole cell was quantified by measuring the fluorescence intensities. The Golgi/total expression ratio of cargo molecules was defined as the ER-to-Golgi transport. In live cell RUSH assays, the Golgi/total ratio at each time point was subtracted from the ratio at time 0 and then normalized to the highest ratio which was defined as 100%.

Co-IP

Co-IP assays were performed as described.30 Briefly, after transfection for 24 h, the cells were lysed with 500 μL of buffer [50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.4), 150 mM NaCl, 1% Nonidet P-40, 0.5% sodium deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS and 1% protease inhibitors]. After centrifugation, the supernatants were incubated with 2 μg of antibodies overnight at 4°C, followed by incubation with 20 μL protein G dynabeads (Invitrogen) for 1 h at 4°C. Immunoprecipitated proteins were detected by immunoblotting.

GST fusion protein pulldown assays

GST fusion protein pulldown assays were carried out using MagneGST pull-down system as described essentially,40 using HEK293 cell homogenates in a total volume of 400 μL binding buffer [20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.4), 140 mM NaCl, 1% Nonidet P-40 and 10% glycerol] overnight at 4°C. The bound proteins were detected by Western blotting.

BRET assays

The live cell-based BRET assays were used to measure the proximity of two proteins and possible protein-protein interactions as described.40

PLAs

PLAs were performed by using Duolink in situ red starter mouse/rabbit kits (Sigma-Aldrich). Briefly, HeLa cells were grown on coverslips in 12-well plates and transfected with 500 ng of GFP or GFP-C1orf27 for 24 h. After fixation and permeabilization with 0.1% saponin for 20 min, the cells were blocked with blocking solution for 1 h and stained with mouse host GFP antibodies (1:200) and rabbit host UFSP2 antibodies (1:100) overnight at 4°C. To study the proximity of endogenous C1orf27 and UFSP2, cells were stained with rabbit C1orf27 antibodies plus mouse IgG, mouse UFSP2 antibodies plus rabbit IgG or rabbit C1orf27 plus mouse UFSP2. The cells were then incubated with secondary antibodies conjugated with plus and minus PLA probes for 1 h and ligation-ligase solution for 30 min, followed by incubation with amplification-polymerase solution for 2 h at 37°C. PLA puncta were counted and a total of randomly selected 50 cells were quantified in each group.

Radioligand binding of intact live cells

α2B-AR expression at the cell surface was quantified by ligand binding in intact live cells using [3H]-RX821002 as described previously.33 In brief, after transfection with α2B-AR or its mutants for 24 h, the cells were incubated with the radioligand at 20 nM. Non-specific binding was measured in the presence of rauwolscine. All binding assays were performed in duplicate.

Measurement of ERK1/2 activation

HEK293 cells were cultured on 12-well plates and transfected with α2B-AR or its mutants for 24 h. After starvation for 12 h, the cells were stimulated with UK14304 at a concentration of 1 μM for 5 min. ERK1/2 activation was determined by immunoblotting using phospho-specific antibodies.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Details regarding the quantification of ER-Golgi transport in fixed and live cells, cell surface receptor, PLA signals are provided in the method details section. All data were calculated and presented as mean ± SE. The exact value of n is given in each figure legend. Statistical differences were analyzed by using one way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s multiple comparisons posttest for more than two groups or by unpaired Student’s t test for 2 groups. Significance levels are ∗p < 0.05, ∗∗p < 0.01 and ∗∗∗p < 0.001.

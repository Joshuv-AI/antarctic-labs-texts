Brauer-Siegel theorem for families of number fields over almost S n S_{n} fields
Anup B. Dixit

Abstract
. The classical Brauer-Siegel conjecture describes the asymptotic behavior of the product of the class number and the regulator in families of number fields. All known cases of the conjecture rely on reducing the problem, via group-theoretic methods, to Siegel’s theorem for quadratic fields over ℚ \mathbb{Q} or over a fixed base field. In this paper, we establish a new form of descent for the Brauer–Siegel conjecture. We show that if the conjecture holds for a family of almost S n S_{n} -fields, it necessarily holds for all quadratic extensions over that family, under mild conditions. This result may be viewed as an analogue of Siegel’s theorem in which the base field is allowed to vary. In addition, we also establish the generalized Brauer-Siegel conjecture as formulated by Tsfasman-Vlăduţ for asymptotically good towers of number fields over a family of almost S n S_{n} -fields.

1.
Introduction

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field. Denote by
n_{K}=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
its degree, by
d_{K}:=|disc(K/\mathbb{Q})|
its absolute discriminant, by
h_{K}
its class number, by
R_{K}
its regulator and by
\widetilde{K}
its Galois closure over
\mathbb{Q}
. A classical problem in number theory, dating back to Gauss, is to understand how
h_{K}
varies on varying
K
. If one uses analytic methods, it is more feasible to study the distribution of
h_{K}R_{K}
, which appears in the class number formula. Writing
\rho_{K}
for the residue of the Dedekind zeta-function
\zeta_{K}(s)
at
s=1
, the class number formula states that

\rho_{K}=\frac{2^{r_{1}}(2\pi)^{r_{2}}h_{K}R_{K}}{\mu_{K}\,\log\sqrt{d_{K}}},

where
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are the number of real and complex embeddings of
K
respectively and
\mu_{K}
denote the number of roots of unity in
K
. Consequently, the asymptotic behaviour of
h_{K}R_{K}
in a family is governed by that of
\rho_{K}
, which is amenable to analytic techniques. In the case of a family of quadratic fields
K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}
, Siegel
[
8
]
proved that

(1)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\log h_{K_{i}}R_{K_{i}}}{\log\sqrt{d_{K_{i}}}}=1.

Thus,
\log h_{K}R_{K}
grows asymptotically like
\log\sqrt{d_{K}}
over a family of quadratic fields. In particular, there are only finitely many quadratic fields with bounded
h_{K}R_{K}
, even though it is conjectured that
h_{K}=1
for infinitely many real quadratic fields. This is famously known as the class number one problem. In the rest of the article, following the notation in
[
10
]
, we denote
g_{K}:=\log\sqrt{d_{K}}
.

Siegel’s theorem was extended by Brauer in
[
1
]
, who showed that for any family of number fields
\{K_{i}\}
with
K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}
Galois for all
i
, if
d_{K_{i}}^{1/n_{K_{i}}}\to\infty
, then (
1
) holds. This was further extended to families of almost normal fields by Stark
[
9
]
and Zykin
[
12
]
. Here, we call a number field
L
almost normal if there is a tower

\mathbb{Q}=L_{1}\subsetneq L_{2}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq L_{t}=L,

such that
L_{i+1}/L_{i}
is Galois for all
i
. For families of number fields
K
with solvable Galois closure, (
1
) was proved by the author in
[
3
]
building on the work of V. K. Murty
[
7
]
. More recently Wong
[
11
]
proved (
1
) for suitable combinations of the above conditions.

In all the above cases, a crucial input is the existence of a zero-free region for
\zeta_{K}(s)
in a neighbourhood of
s=1
. More precisely, Stark
[
9
]
showed that if
K/\mathbb{Q}
is a number field for which
\zeta_{K}(s)
has a real zero
\beta
satisfying

1-\frac{1}{4f(n_{K})\log d_{K}}\leq\beta<1,

then there exists a quadratic subfield
N\subset K
such that
\zeta_{N}(\beta)=0
. This reduction to quadratic case is the key mechanism underlying existing proofs. Stark further showed that one may take
f(n_{K})=1
when
K/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois, and
f(n_{K})=4
when
K/\mathbb{Q}
is almost normal. For number fields whose Galois closure over
\mathbb{Q}
is solvable, V. K. Murty
[
7
]
showed that one can take

f(n_{K})=cn_{K}^{e(n_{K})}\delta(n_{K}),

where
e(n_{K})=\max_{p^{a}\|n_{K}}a
and
\delta(n_{K})\ll n_{K}^{4}
is an explicitly described function. These estimates are sufficiently strong to permit a reduction of such families to that of quadratic fields, where Siegel’s theorem is applicable.

For a general extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
, however, the only unconditional bound due to Stark
[
9
]
allows one to take
f(n_{K})=n_{K}!
, and grows too rapidly to yield (
1
). However, under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis,
\zeta_{K}(s)
has no zeros in the region
1/2<\Re(s)<1
and hence we expect
(
1
)
to hold in general. This leads to the Brauer-Siegel conjecture, which we refer to as
(BS)
and state below.

Conjecture 1.1

(BS)
.

Let
\{K_{i}\}
be a family of number fields satisfying
d_{K_{i}}^{1/n_{K_{i}}}\to\infty
. Then,

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\log h_{K_{i}}R_{K_{i}}}{g_{K_{i}}}=1.

Applying the class number formula, this is equivalent to

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\log\rho_{K_{i}}}{g_{K_{i}}}=0,

where
\rho_{K_{i}}
denotes the residue of
\zeta_{K}(s)
at
s=1
.

Recent years have seen substantial interest in various aspects of this conjecture, such as its effective versions, see for instance
[
2
]
. In this work, we focus on establishing a reduction-type result in this setting. More precisely, in all the cases where Conjecture
1.1
is currently known, Siegel’s theorem for quadratic fields plays an indispensable role. This leads to the question of whether the Brauer-Siegel property is preserved under quadratic extensions of number fields.

Remark.
One may be tempted to extend the above result to families of almost normal fields over almost

For a number field

and a prime power

, denote by

the number of ideals in

with norm

\phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{K})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{r_{1}(K_{i})}{g_{K_{i}}},\,\phi_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{r_{2}(K_{i})}{g_{K_{i}}}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\phi_{q}(\mathcal{K})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{N_{q}(K_{i})}{g_{K_{i}}},

for all prime powers

is as follows.

Conjecture 1.5

(GBS)
.

For any asymptotically exact family
\mathcal{K}=\{K_{i}\}

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\log h_{K_{i}}R_{K_{i}}}{g_{K_{i}}}=1+\sum_{q}\phi_{q}\log\frac{q}{q-1}-\phi_{\mathbb{R}}\log 2-\phi_{\mathbb{C}}\log 2\pi.

Using the class number formula, the above statement is equivalent to

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\log\rho_{K_{i}}}{g_{K_{i}}}=\sum_{q}\phi_{q}\log\frac{q}{q-1}.

For an asymptotically exact family, if
\lim_{i}\frac{n_{K_{i}}}{g_{K_{i}}}>0

\{K_{i}\}
over
K
, where the root discriminant remains a constant is an example of an asymptotically good family. Note that
\lim_{i}\frac{n_{K_{i}}}{g_{K_{i}}}=0
is equivalent to the classical case that the root discriminant
d_{K_{i}}^{1/n_{K_{i}}}\to\infty
. For asymptotically good families, we obtain the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{L_{i}\}
be an asymptotically good family satisfying GBS. For each
i
, suppose
L_{i}
is an almost
S_{n_{i}}
-field, but not an
S_{n_{i}}
-field. Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\}
be an asymptotically good tower of number fields satisfying
M_{i}\cap\widetilde{L_{i}}=L_{i}
. Then, GBS holds for
\mathcal{M}
if for all
i
, either

(1)

M_{i}/L_{i}
is almost normal      or

(2)

M_{i}/L_{i}
has solvable Galois closure.

The limitation of the above result is that it does not include family of
S_{n}
-fields, i.e., we force
m<n-1
. On the other hand, unlike the case of Theorem
1.4
, we no longer require condition (

) and hence have the result in more generality.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some facts and lemmata towards the proof of our main theorem. We first introduce some basic group theoretic constructions. Our method is inspired from that in Hoffstein and Jochnowitz
[
4
]
, where they establish a zero free region for
\zeta_{K}/\zeta_{k}(s)
, where
K
is a CM-field over a totally real
S_{n}
-field
k
. We largely maintain the notation in their paper.

Let
S_{m}
denote the symmetric group of
m
elements. It is well known that the irreducible complex characters of
S_{m}
are in bijection with the partitions of
m
. Let
\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\cdots,\chi_{m}
denote the irreducible characters of
S_{m}
corresponding to the partitions

[m],[m-1,1],[m-2,1,1],\ldots,[1,1,\ldots,1]

respectively. These partitions are commonly referred to as hooks. The character
\chi_{1}
is the trivial character, while
\chi_{m}
is the sign character, which is the unique quadratic non-trivial character of
S_{m}
, for
m\geq 5
.

For
0\leq i\leq m
, let
\psi_{i}
denote the character of the subgroup
S_{m-i}\times S_{i}\subset S_{m}
, that is trivial on the factor
S_{m-i}
and equal to the alternating character on
S_{i}
. Let
\psi_{i}^{*}
denote the character of
S_{m}
induced from
\psi_{i}
. By an application of Littlewood-Richardson rule, one obtains the decomposition

(3)

\psi_{i}^{*}=\chi_{i}\oplus\chi_{i+1}.

Next, let
\theta
denote the unique non-trivial character of the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Define the character
\psi_{i}^{\prime}:=\psi_{i}\otimes\theta
on
S_{m}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Similarly, define
\chi_{i}^{\prime}:=\chi_{i}\otimes\theta
on
S_{m}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Then, from (
3
), one can deduce that

(\psi_{i}^{\prime})^{*}=\chi_{i}^{\prime}\oplus\chi_{i+1}^{\prime}.

We also recall some known results on the exceptional zeros of Dedekind zeta-functions. For a number field
K/\mathbb{Q}
, Stark
[
9
]
showed that
\zeta_{K}(s)
has at most one zero in the region

1-\frac{1}{4\log d_{K}}\leq\Re(s)<1\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,|\Im(s)|\leq\frac{1}{4\log d_{K}}.

If such a zero exists, it is necessarily real and simple. Here and in the rest of the paper,

is chosen with the principal branch.

L(s,\chi_{2}^{\prime})
,
L(s,\chi_{3}^{\prime})
,
\ldots,L(s,\chi_{r}^{\prime})
at
s=\beta
. But, by Lemma
3.1
,
L(s,\chi_{r}^{\prime})
can neither have a zero or pole at
\beta
. This leads to a contradiction. Hence, there exists a
j\geq 1
for which
L(\beta,\psi_{j}^{\prime})=0
.
∎

For a quadratic extension
N/L
, we now establish a zero free region for
\zeta_{N}/\zeta_{L}(s)
, which is at the heart of the proof of the main theorems.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
L
be an almost
S_{n}
-field and
Gal(\widetilde{L}/L)\cong S_{m}
. Let
N/L
be a quadratic extension satisfying
N\cap\widetilde{L}=L
. Then, any real zero
\beta
of
\zeta_{N}/\zeta_{L}(s)
satisfies

1-\beta\geq\frac{1}{4^{m+2}m\log d_{N}}.

Proof.

Suppose
\beta
is a real zero of
\zeta_{N}/\zeta_{L}(s)
satisfying
\beta\in[1-(4^{m+2}m\log d_{N})^{-1},1]
. By Lemma
3.2
, there exists
L_{1}
such that
L\subseteq L_{1}\subseteq\widetilde{L}=\widetilde{K}
and
Gal(\widetilde{L}/L)\cong S_{m-j}\times S_{j}
, satisfying
\zeta_{NL_{1}}/\zeta_{L_{1}}(\beta)=0
. Without loss of generality, assume
m-j<j
. Next, define
L_{1}^{*}
to be the fixed field of
S_{j}
in
\widetilde{L}/L
. Thus, we have
Gal(L_{1}^{*}/L_{1})\cong S_{m-j}
. We apply the Lemma
3.2
again and obtain that there exists
L_{2}
such that
L_{1}\subseteq L_{2}\subseteq L_{1}^{*}
with
Gal(L_{1}^{*}/L_{2})\cong S_{m-j-k}\times S_{k}
and
\zeta_{NL_{2}}/\zeta_{L_{2}}(\beta)=0
. Again WLOG,
m-j-k<k
. We continue this process until we reach the group of the type
S_{a}\times S_{1}
. In the final step, we have the extension
L_{g}\subseteq L_{g+1}\subseteq L_{g}^{*}
such that
Gal(L_{g}^{*}/L_{g+1})\cong S_{a}\times S_{1}
.

Now, we also have
Gal(\widetilde{K}/L_{g+1})\cong H\times S_{1}
where
H\subseteq S_{n-2}
. Hence,
L_{g+1}
contains
L
and a conjugate of
L
, say
L^{\prime}
. Write
L=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
and
L^{\prime}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha^{\prime})
such that
N=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\alpha})
, where
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are conjugates. Call
N^{\prime}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}})
. Then
N
and
N^{\prime}
are conjugates. Moreover,
N_{g+1}=NL_{g+1}=L_{g+1}(\sqrt{\alpha})
and
N_{g+1}^{\prime}=NL_{g+1}^{\prime}=L_{g+1}(\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}})
are also conjugates. Applying Lemma
2.5
and (
4
), we deduce that
\zeta_{N_{g+1}}(s)
does not have any zero in the region

\bigg[1-\frac{1}{4\log d_{N_{g+1}N_{g+1}^{\prime}}},1\bigg]\supset\bigg[1-\frac{1}{16\log d_{N_{g+1}}},1\bigg].

Write
t=[N_{g+1}:N]
. Applying
[
9
, Lemma 7]
, we have
d_{\widetilde{N}}\mid d_{N}^{m.m!}
and hence
d_{N_{g+1}}\leq d_{\tilde{N}}^{1/[\widetilde{N}:N_{g+1}]}\leq d_{N}^{mt}
. Therefore, we have

(7)

1-\beta>\frac{1}{16mt\log d_{N}}.

To get a bound on
[L_{g+1}:L]
, note that
[L_{1}:L]=\frac{m!}{(m-j)!j!}\leq 2^{m}
. Since
m-j<j
, in the second step, we have
[L_{2}:L_{1}]\leq\frac{(m-j)!}{(m-j-k)!k!}\leq 2^{m-j}\leq 2^{m/2}
. Similarly, in the third step, we have
[L_{3}:L_{2}]\leq 2^{m/4}
and so on. Hence,

[L_{g+1}:L]\leq 2^{m+m/2+m/4+\cdots}\leq 4^{m}.

Using this in (
7
), we deduce
1-\beta>(4^{m+2}m\log d_{N})^{-1}
, which contradicts our assumption.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For the family
\{N_{i}\}
, we would like to show that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\log\rho_{N_{i}}}{\log\sqrt{d_{N_{i}}}}=0.

It is known that (see for instance
[
6
, Chapter XVI, Lemma 1]
)

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{\log\rho_{N_{i}}}{\log\sqrt{d_{N_{i}}}}\leq 0.

Therefore, it suffices to prove that

\liminf_{i}\frac{\log\rho_{N_{i}}}{\log d_{N_{i}}}\geq 0.

Let
N/L
be a quadratic extension and let
L
be an almost
S_{n}
-field. Suppose
\beta_{0}
is the possible real zero of
\zeta_{N}(s)
in the region
[1-(4\log d_{N})^{-1},1]
. By Stark’s Lemma
2.3
,

\rho_{N}\geq c(1-\beta),

where
\beta=\max(1-(4\log d_{N})^{-1},\beta_{0})
. Suppose
\beta_{0}
is not a zero of
\zeta_{L}(s)
. Then, by Theorem
3.3
, we deduce

\rho_{N}\geq c\,4^{m}m\log d_{N}.

In this case,

\frac{\log\rho_{N}}{\log d_{N}}\gg\frac{m}{\log d_{N}}+\frac{\log\log d_{N}}{\log d_{N}}.

Since
d_{N_{i}}^{1/n_{N_{i}}}\to\infty
and
m\leq n_{L_{i}}
, the RHS above tends to
0
in the family and we have that (BS) holds. Now, suppose
\zeta_{L}(\beta_{0})=0
. Then, we can write

\rho_{N}=\rho_{L}\,L(1,\chi),

where
L(s,\chi)=\zeta_{N}/\zeta_{L}(s)
. Since
\beta_{0}
is not a zero of
L(s,\chi)
and
\zeta_{N}(s)
has at most one zero in
[1-(4\log d_{N})^{-1},1]
, we have this region as a zero free region for
L(s,\chi)
. Applying Lemma
2.4
with
\sigma_{1}=2
, we obtain

L(1,\chi)>c_{1}\,\frac{1}{4\log d_{N}}\,\frac{1}{d_{L}^{-1/2}}\,c_{2}^{n_{L}},

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are absolute constants. Hence,

\frac{\log L(1,\chi)}{\log d_{N}}\gg\frac{\log d_{L}}{\log d_{N}}+\frac{\log\log d_{N}}{\log d_{N}}+\frac{n_{L}}{\log d_{N}}.

Since
\log d_{L_{i}}/\log d_{N_{i}}\to 0
and
n_{N_{i}}/{\log d_{N_{i}}}\to 0
, we deduce that
\liminf\frac{\log L(1,\chi)}{\log d_{N_{i}}}=0
. Therefore,

\liminf_{i}\frac{\log\rho_{N_{i}}}{\log\sqrt{d_{N_{i}}}}=\liminf_{i}\frac{\log\rho_{L_{i}}}{\log\sqrt{d_{N_{i}}}}.

By assumption, (BS) holds for
\{L_{i}\}
, and hence
\lim_{i}\log\rho_{L_{i}}/\log d_{L_{i}}=0
. Since
\log d_{N_{i}}\geq\log d_{L_{i}}
, we deduce from above that
\liminf_{i}\frac{\log\rho_{N_{i}}}{\log\sqrt{d_{N_{i}}}}=0
and (BS) holds for
\{N_{i}\}
.

∎

4.
Asymptotically good families

Let
L
be an almost
S_{n}
-field with
Gal(\widetilde{L}/L)\cong S_{m}
. Let
M/L
be an extension satisfying
M\cap\widetilde{L}=L
. Suppose
M/L
is either almost normal or has solvable Galois closure. Let

\beta_{0}:=\max\bigg(1-\frac{1}{32g_{M}},1-\frac{c}{n^{e(n)}\delta(n)g_{M}}\bigg),

where the second term is as in (
5
). Recall that
g_{M}:=\log\sqrt{d_{M}}
. By Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
, we deduce that if
\zeta_{M}(s)
has a zero
\beta
in the region
[1-\beta_{0},1]
, then there exists a field
N
such that
L\subseteq N\subseteq M
,
\zeta_{N}(\beta)=0
and
[N:L]\leq 2
. If
[N:L]=2
, by Theorem
3.3
, we have that
\zeta_{N}/\zeta_{L}(s)
does not vanish on the interval
[1-\frac{1}{4^{m+2}\log d_{M}},1]
. Hence,
\zeta_{M}/\zeta_{L}(s)
does not vanish in the region
[1-\beta_{1},1]
, where

\beta_{1}:=\max\left(\beta_{0},1-\frac{1}{4^{m+2}\log d_{M}}\right).

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Write

\zeta_{M}(s)=\frac{\rho_{M}}{(s-1)}F_{M}(s).

Taking
\log
on both sides and dividing by
g_{M}
, we get for
s=1+\theta_{M}

\frac{\log\zeta_{M}(1+\theta_{M})}{g_{M}}=\frac{\log\rho_{M}}{g_{M}}+\frac{\log F_{M}(1+\theta_{M})}{g_{M}}-\frac{\log\theta_{M}}{g_{M}}.

In
[
10
]
, it is shown that for any asymptotically exact family of number fields,

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{\log\rho_{M_{i}}}{g_{M_{i}}}\leq\sum_{q}\phi_{q}(\mathcal{M})\log\frac{q}{q-1}.

In order to prove the Theorem
1.6
, it suffices to show that for the asymptotically good tower
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\}
of number fields, there is a choice of
\theta_{M_{i}}\to 0
, such that

(8)

\liminf_{i\to\infty}\frac{\zeta_{M_{i}}(1+\theta_{M_{i}})}{g_{L_{i}}}\geq\sum_{q}\phi_{q}(\mathcal{M})\log\frac{q}{q-1},

(9)

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{\log F_{M_{i}}(1+\theta_{M_{i}})}{g_{M_{i}}}\leq 0,

and

(10)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\log\theta_{M_{i}}}{g_{M_{i}}}=0.

We first prove (
9
) and make our choice of
\theta_{L_{i}}
’s. Write

Z_{M}(s):=\frac{d}{ds}\log F_{M}(s).

Using Mellin transform of the Chebyshev step function, we have

(11)

\frac{Z_{M}(s)}{s}=\int_{1}^{\infty}(G_{M}(x)-x)\,x^{-s-1}\,dx-\frac{1}{s},

for
\Re(s)>1
, where

G_{M}(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,m>1\\
q^{m}\leq x\end{subarray}}N_{q}(M)\log q.

The unconditional Lagarias-Odlyzko
[
5
]
estimate for
G_{M}(x)
gives

|G_{M}(x)-x|\leq C_{4}\,x\exp\left(-C_{5}\sqrt{\frac{\log x}{n}}\right)+\frac{x^{\beta}}{\beta}

for
\log x\geq C_{6}\,n_{M}\,g_{M}^{2}
, where
C_{4}
,
C_{5}
,
C_{6}
are positive absolute constants. Here,
\beta=\max(\beta^{\prime},1-(4\log d_{M})^{-1})
, where
\beta^{\prime}
is the possible real exceptional zero of
\zeta_{M}(s)
in
[1-(4\log d_{M})^{-1},1]
.
For an asymptotically good family
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\}
, we can find positive constants
C_{0}
and
C_{00}
depending on
\mathcal{M}
such that

C_{0}\,n_{M_{i}}\leq g_{M_{i}}\leq C_{00}\,n_{M_{i}}.

First assume that
\zeta_{M}(s)
does not vanish in the interval
[1-\beta_{1},1]
. Then,

|G_{M}(x)-x|\leq C_{4}\,x\exp\left(-C_{5}\sqrt{\frac{\log x}{n_{M}}}\right)+\frac{x^{\beta_{1}}}{\beta_{1}}.

For all
x
satisfying
\log x>\max(4^{m+2}g_{M},\,\,n^{e(n)}\delta(n)g_{M})
, we have

\frac{x^{\beta_{1}}}{\beta_{1}}\ll x\exp\left(-C_{5}^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{\log x}{n_{M}}}\right).

Setting
s=1+\theta
in (
11
), we have

\left|\frac{Z_{M}(1+\theta)}{(1+\theta)}\right|=\left|\int_{1}^{\infty}(G_{M}(x)-x)\,x^{-2-\theta}\,dx\right|+O(1)

(12)

=\left|\int_{1}^{I}(G(x)-x)x^{-2-\theta}dx+\int_{I}^{\infty}(G(x)-x)x^{-2-\theta}dx\right|+O(1).

In case of the first integral, we use the following bound on
G_{M}(x)
,

G_{M}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,m\geq 1\\
q^{m}\leq x\end{subarray}}N_{q}(M)\log q\leq n_{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,m\geq 1\\
q^{m}\leq x\end{subarray}}\log q\ll g_{M}\,x\log x.

Therefore, for some constant
C_{11}>0
, we have

|G_{M}(x)-x|\leq C_{11}\,g_{M}\,x\log x.

Thus the integral

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{I}(G(x)-x)x^{-2-\theta}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{11}g_{M}\int_{1}^{I}x^{-1-\theta}\log xdx

\displaystyle\leq C_{11}g_{M}\,I\left(1-\exp\left(-\theta\left(I\right)\right)\right)\ll Ig_{M}.

We now bound the second integral in (
12
). By the Lagarias-Odlyzko estimate (
11
), we have

(13)

\int_{I}^{\infty}(G(x)-x)x^{-2-\theta}dx\leq C_{4}\int_{I}^{\infty}\exp\left(-C_{5}^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{\log x}{g_{M}}}\right)x^{-1-\theta}dx.

Using change of variables

x=y^{g_{M}\log y},

we get the right hand side of (
13
) as

(14)

2C_{4}\int_{\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log I}{g_{M}}}\right)}^{\infty}\,\,g_{M}\,y^{-C_{5}^{\prime}-1-\theta g_{M}\log y}\log y\,dy.

For large
g_{M}
and any fixed
\epsilon>0
, we bound
\log y\leq y^{\epsilon}
to get (
14
) to be

(15)

\leq 2C_{4}\int_{\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log I}{g_{M}}}\right)}^{\infty}\,\,g_{M}\,y^{-C_{5}^{\prime}-1+\epsilon-\theta g_{M}\log y}dy.

We further know that in the above interval,

\log y\geq\sqrt{\frac{\log I}{g_{M}}}.

Hence, (
15
) is

(16)

\leq 2C_{4}\int_{\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log I}{g_{M}}}\right)}^{\infty}g_{M}\,y^{-C_{5}^{\prime}-1+\epsilon-\theta\alpha(g_{M})}dy,

where

\alpha(g_{M})=\sqrt{\frac{\log I}{g_{M}}}.

Evaluating the integral (
16
), we have

\frac{2C_{4}\,g_{M}}{C_{5}^{\prime}-\epsilon+\theta\alpha(g_{M})}\exp\left(-(C_{5}^{\prime}-\epsilon+\theta\alpha(g_{M}))\sqrt{\frac{\log I}{g_{M}}}\right).

We now choose
I=\max\left(4^{m+2}\log d_{M},\,\,n_{M}^{e(n_{M})}\delta(n_{M})\,g_{M}\right)
. Here
\delta(n_{M})\ll g_{M}^{4}
and
e(n_{M})\ll\log n_{M}\ll\log g_{M}
. Choosing
\theta_{M}
as

\theta_{M}:=(Ig_{M})^{-1},

we get

\log F_{M}(1+\theta_{M})=\int_{0}^{\theta_{M}}Z_{M}(1+\theta)d\theta\ll 1.

Therefore, (
9
) holds. Furthermore, we also have

n_{M}^{e(n_{M})}\delta(n_{M})\,g_{M}\ll g_{M}^{C\log g_{M}},

for some absolute constant
C
and since
m\leq n-2
, we have
n_{M}\geq m(m+1)
and hence

4^{m+2}\ll 4^{m}\ll 4^{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\ll 4^{C_{5}\sqrt{g_{M}}}.

Therefore,

\frac{\log\theta_{M_{i}}}{g_{M_{i}}}=O\left(\frac{\sqrt{g_{M}}}{g_{M}}\right)+O\left(\frac{(\log g_{M})^{2}}{g_{M}}\right)

and hence
\lim_{i}\log\theta_{M_{i}}/g_{M_{i}}=0
. Hence, we also get (
10
). For (
8
), we use the fact that
\{M_{i}\}
is a tower of number fields. Note that

\frac{\zeta_{M_{i}}(1+\theta)}{g_{M_{i}}}=\sum_{q}\frac{N_{q}(M_{i})}{g_{M_{i}}}\log\frac{1}{1-q^{-1-\theta}}

=\sum_{p}\frac{N_{p}(M_{i})}{g_{M_{i}}}\log\frac{1}{1-p^{-1-\theta}}+\sum_{q=p^{k},k>1}\frac{N_{q}(M_{i})}{g_{M_{i}}}\log\frac{1}{1-q^{-1-\theta}}

In a tower, we know that
\phi_{p}(\mathcal{M})\leq\frac{N_{p}(M_{i})}{g_{M_{i}}}
. Therefore,

\sum_{p}\frac{N_{p}(M_{i})}{g_{M_{i}}}\log\frac{1}{1-p^{-1-\theta}}\geq\sum_{p}\phi_{p}\log\frac{1}{1-p^{-1-\theta}},

for any
\theta>0
. We also have

\sum_{q=p^{k},k>1}\frac{N_{q}(M_{i})}{g_{M_{i}}}\log\frac{1}{1-q^{-1-\theta}}\to\sum_{q=p^{k},k>1}\phi_{q}\log\frac{1}{1-q^{-1-\theta}}

uniformly for
\theta>-\delta
, for some
\delta>0
. Hence, we get

\liminf_{i\to\infty}\zeta_{M_{i}}(1+\theta_{M_{i}})\geq\sum_{q}\phi_{q}\log\frac{q}{q-1}.

Suppose
\zeta_{M}(s)
admits a zero in the region
[1-\beta_{1},1]
. Then, we have that
\zeta_{L}(\beta_{1})=0
and the Artin
L
-function
\zeta_{M}/\zeta_{L}(s)
is non-vanishing in
[1-\beta_{1},1]
. We can again apply Lagarias-Odlyzko’s estimate as above to deduce
\log\left(\frac{F_{M}}{F_{L}}(1+\theta_{M})\right)\ll 1
and hence

\log F_{M}(1+\theta_{M})\ll\log F_{L}(1+\theta_{M}).

By our assumption, GBS holds for
\{L_{i}\}
and hence

\frac{\log F_{L_{i}}(1+\theta_{M_{i}})-\log\theta_{M_{i}}}{g_{L_{i}}}\to 0.

Since
g_{M_{i}}\geq g_{L_{i}}
, we deduce

\frac{\log F_{M_{i}}(1+\theta_{M_{i}})-\log\theta_{M_{i}}}{g_{M_{i}}}\to 0.

Therefore, GBS holds for
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\}
.
∎

5.
Brauer-Siegel for
S_{n}
fields under Artin’s conjecture

In
[
9
, Theorem 4]
, Stark showed that Conjecture
1.1
(BS) holds under the assumption of Artin’s holomorphy conjecture, which asserts that Artin
L
-functions are analytic on
s\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}
.
In this section, we explicitly capture the Artin
L
-functions, whose holomorphy near
s=1
shall imply (BS) for a family of
S_{n}
-fields.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be an
S_{n}
-field. Write

\zeta_{K}(s)=L(s,\text{Ind}^{S_{n}}_{S_{n-1}}1)=\zeta(s)L(s,\rho_{(n-1)}),

where
\rho_{(n-k)}
is the representation of
S_{n}
corresponding to the partition
[n-k,1,1,\ldots,1]
. Denote by
L\subset\widetilde{K}
, the fixed field of
S_{n-4}\times S_{2}\times S_{2}
. Then,

\zeta_{L}(s)=L(s,\text{Ind}^{S_{n}}_{S_{n-4}\times S_{2}\times S_{2}}1).

We compute the decomposition of this representation. Since
S_{n-4}\times S_{2}\times S_{2}
corresponds to the partition
[n-4,2,2]
, the representation
\text{Ind}^{S_{n}}_{S_{n-4}\times S_{2}\times S_{2}}
is a Young permutation module, whose irreducible components are precisely the Specht modules
S^{\mu}
such that
\mu
dominates
[n-4,2,2]
. The multiplicity of each of this irreducible representation is given by the Kostka number, which can be calculated by the number of Young tableaux with the given content
n-4
one’s,
2
two’s and
2
three’s. Using this, we have the decomposition of the form

\text{Ind}^{S_{n}}_{S_{n-4}\times S_{2}\times S_{2}}1=1\oplus 2\rho_{(n-1)}\oplus\rho_{(n-2)}\oplus\rho_{(n-3)}.

Therefore,

\zeta_{L}(s)=\zeta(s)L^{2}(s,\rho_{(n-1)})L(s,\rho_{(n-2)})L(s,\rho_{(n-3)}).

Any real zero
\beta
of
\zeta_{K}(s)
with
1/2\leq\beta\leq 1
must be zero of
L(s,\rho_{(n-1)})
, since
\zeta(s)
does not vanish in this region. Suppose Artin’s conjecture holds true for
L(s,\rho_{(n-2)})
and
L(s,\rho_{(n-3)})
. Then, they are entire and do not have pole at
\beta
. Thus,
\beta
is a double zero of
\zeta_{L}(s)
. By Stark’s theorem, there is at most one zero of
\zeta_{L}(s)
in the region
(1-(4\log d_{L})^{-1},1)
, which is simple. Thus, we conclude that

1-\beta>\frac{1}{4\log d_{L}}.

But

d_{L}=d_{K}^{n}N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(d_{L/K})\leq d_{K}^{n}\prod_{p|d_{K}}(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\,\,\,p)^{[L:K]}\leq d_{K}^{n+n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)/4}\leq d_{K}^{n^{4}/4}.

Thus

(17)

1-\beta>\frac{1}{4\log d_{L}}\geq\frac{1}{4n^{4}\log d_{K}}.

Hence, we deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{K}=\{K_{i}\}
be a family of
S_{n_{i}}
-fields satisfying
d_{K_{i}}^{1/n_{K_{i}}}\to\infty
. Under the assumption of Artin holomorphy conjecture for
L(s,\rho_{n_{i}-2})
and
L(s,\rho_{n_{i}-3})
for all
i
’s, the Conjecture
1.1
holds for
\mathcal{K}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

\liminf_{i}\frac{\log\rho_{K_{i}}}{\log d_{K_{i}}}\geq 0.

By Lemma
2.3

\rho_{K_{i}}\geq c(1-\beta_{i}),

where
\beta_{i}=\max(\beta_{i}^{\prime},(4\log d_{K_{i}})^{-1})
and
\beta_{i}^{\prime}
denotes the possible real zero of
\zeta_{K_{i}}(s)
in the region
[1-(4\log d_{K})^{-1},1)
. By (
17
), we get

\log\rho_{K_{i}}\geq\log c+\log(4n_{i}^{4}\log d_{K_{i}})\gg\log(\log^{4}d_{K_{i}}),

which is sufficient to establish Conjecture
1.1
.
∎

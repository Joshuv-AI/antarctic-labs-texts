Bundle Fragments into a Whole: Mining More Complete Clusters via Submodular Selection of Interesting webpages for Web Topic Detection Journal: X
Junbiao Pang, Anjing Hu, Qingming Huang

Abstract
Organizing interesting webpages into hot topics is one of key steps to understand the trends of multimodal web data. A state-of-the-art solution is firstly to organize webpages into a large volume of multi-granularity topic candidates; hot topics are further identified by estimating their interestingness. However, these topic candidates contain a large number of fragments of hot topics due to both the inefficient feature representations and the unsupervised topic generation. This paper proposes a bundling-refining approach to mine more complete hot topics from fragments. Concretely, the bundling step organizes the fragment topics into coarse topics; next, the refining step proposes a submodular-based method to refine coarse topics in a scalable approach. The propose unconventional method is simple, yet powerful by leveraging submodular optimization, our approach outperforms the traditional ranking methods which involve the careful design and complex steps. Extensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed approach surpasses the state-of-the-art method ( i.e. , latent Poisson deconvolution 29 ) 20% accuracy and 10% one on two public data sets, respectively.

With the boom of web technologies, recently it is convenient for people to share data on social media websites which successfully facilitates both the generation and the propagation of User Generated Content (UGC)

Hot topics provide an easy way to abstract the public concerns, social trends and urgent events.
However, the unprecedented explosion of UGC data has made it difficult for web users to quickly access hot web topics

Discovering hot web topics is like looking for a needle in a haystack. Because only a small number of the interesting webpages could evolve into the hot topics eventually. To handle this challenge, the Detection-By-Ranking (DBR) approach
26
is proposed. Firstly, a huge number of the multi-granularity topic candidates is generated, attempting to hit all hot topics in a brute-force approach; secondly, the interestingness of the topics is estimated to discover hot topics. However, due to the lack of the supervised information, the multi-granularity approach inevitably generates the fragments of hot topics

Because Poisson Deconvolution (PD)

A naive idea attempts to generate complete topics in presence of noises via advantaged clustering methods,

, low-rank models

In this paper, we seek a
fragment-to-whole
and
coarse-to-fine
method, based on four motivations. Firstly, bundling the fragment topics into a whole would produce complete yet coarse topics; this avoids the drawback of the NMS
11

8
. Secondly, refining a topic into the accurate, coherent and interpretable

Therefore, we propose the Bundling-Refining (BR) method as follows:

1.

Bundling:
This step agglomerates topics into a coarse one (more details will be discussed in Section
3.2
). This step produces a over-complete topics from the fragments, and reduces the number of false detected topics at same time.

2.

Refining:
This step models the interestingness of webpages, and then removes uninteresting webpages in a scalable approach. Concretely, a over-complete topic is firstly represented as a graph, where the interestingness of a webpage is modeled as the stationary distribution.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first to mine a whole topic from fragments for topic detection on web. The proposed method is computationally simple, and yet exceptionally powerful. Simply by greedily selecting a set of webpages, without complex training process, we find a novel clustering method that exceeds or meets the traditional approach

Related Work

Web Topic Detection.
Webpages are the typical heterogeneous data. As a result, many literatures have considered topics as clusterings from the multi-modal data

34

31

In the former case, topic detection extends the single-modality based methods into multi-modal data. For example, multi-modal LDA

et al.

5

The DBR method
29

Classical topic models have been proposed to infer hidden themes for document analysis, including Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)
3

10
and various variations. These topic models generally work well on long and structured documents
7
. Moreover, these models have assumed that each webpage must belong to a topic. For instance, a subset of twitters was firstly retrieved by a set of predefined keywords, and then LDA was used to organize these twitters into topics

Recently,
38

37

Clustering In a Sea of Noise.
The ideal approach for hot topics detection from web was to cluster in a sea of noise

There has been a trend of introducing noise-resistant clustering techniques into web topic detection. For instance, Li et al

In fact, web topics are not equivalent to conventional clusters or the zero-shot topic classification

3.1
Ranking Multi-granularity Topic Candidates

Following the approach in
28
, we convert the matrix
W
into the affinity one
\hat{W}
by Gaussian kernel,
i.e.
,
\hat{W}_{ij}=\exp\left(-\|W_{ij}\|_{2}^{2}/\sigma^{2}\right)
where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the
\ell_{2}
norm, and
\sigma^{2}
denotes the deviation. Once these affinity matrices are constructed, we build the visual and the textual
k
-Nearest Neighbor Graphs (
k
-N
2
Gs),
where the
k
nearest neighbors of each webpage truncate the affinity matrix
\hat{W}
into the spare one
A
as follows
:

\displaystyle G^{vis}

\displaystyle=(V,E^{vis},A^{vis}),

(1)

\displaystyle G^{txt}

\displaystyle=(V,E^{txt},A^{txt}),

(2)

where vertex
V
denotes webpages, edge
E^{vis}
(
E^{txt}
) represents whether two webpages are connected by the visual (textual) modality or not, and matrix
A^{vis}
(
A^{txt}
) describes the closeness of two webpages determined by the visual (textual) features. Subsequently, a mixed graph is obtained as follows:

G=\left(V,E,A\right),

(3)

where
E=E^{vis}\cup E^{txt}
, and
A=(A^{vis}+A^{txt})/{2}
.

E=E^{vis}\cup E^{txt}
means that either the individual modality or the multi-modalities could be used to model the similarity between two webpages.

By Similarity Cascade (SC)
26
, the multi-granularity topics
C_{k}
(
k=1,\ldots,K
) are generated from the mixed graph
G
. A topic
C_{k}
is formally represented as follows:

C_{k}=c^{\top}_{k}{\circ}c_{k},

(4)

in which the indicator vector
c_{k}\in\{0,1\}^{1\times N}
, where
1
or
0
means that whether the topic
C_{k}
contains the webpage
\mathbf{x}_{i}
or not. The operation
\circ
means that the diagonal of the matrix
c^{\top}_{k}c_{k}
is set to zero. The weight of a topic
\mu_{k}
is estimated as follows:

\begin{split}w_{ij}&\sim\text{Poisson}(a_{ij})\\
s.t.:&\ \ w_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mu_{k}C_{k_{ij}}.\end{split}

(5)

The interestingness of a topic is estimated as
i_{k}=\mu_{k}\cdot|C_{k}|
, where
|C_{k}|
is the number of webpages in a topic
C_{k}
. Therefore, we obtain the interestingness order of topics
\mathcal{L}=i_{1}\prec\ldots i_{k}\ldots\prec i_{K}
. For more details about the PD-based ranking, please refer to
26
.

3.2
Bundling Fragments into a Coarse Topic

We assume that every topic is a fragment of some hot topic. That is, each topic should at least be bundled into a coarse one. Given the order list
\mathcal{L}
and the bundling window
W
, Jaccard distance between the
k
-th
topic and the subsequent one
j
-th
(
j\in\{k{+}1,\ldots,k{+}W\}

J_{kj}=\frac{|C_{k}\bigcap C_{j}|}{|C_{k}\bigcup C_{j}|},

(6)

where
\bigcap
and
\bigcup
respectively represent the intersection operation and the union operation, and
|\cdot|
denotes the number of webpages in a set. If
J_{kj}
is large than a predefined threshold
\tau
, we bundle the
j
-th topic and the
k
-th one into a coarse topic,
i.e.
,
\widehat{C}_{k}=\bigcup C_{i}
(
i\in\{k,\ldots,k{+}W\}
). Next, we replace the topic
C_{k}
with the bundled one
\widehat{C}_{k}
, as illustrated in Fig.
1
.

Bundling fragment topics into a coarse one. In this example, the size of the bundling window is 3.

Theoretically, the bundling process has a time complexity of
O(K{\cdot}W)
(
W{\ll}K
). In practice,
W
is frequently smaller than 50. Therefore, the bundling process is quite efficient. However, the uninteresting webpages in the fragments are inevitably merged into the coarse topics. In this paper, the interestingness of webpages is estimated in Section

3.3
Modeling Interestingness by Stationary Distribution

An interesting webpage is defined as the likelihood of that people are attracted. If the correlations among webpages are encoded as a graph, the stationary distribution measures the interestingness of a webpage
23
. However, the structure information between webpages (
e.g.
, link) is unavailable. We convert a coarse topic into a graph with the similarities among webpages.

The reconstructed similarity between webpages is computed as follows:

\widehat{S}_{ij}=\sum_{C_{k}\in\widehat{C}}\mu_{k}C_{k_{ij}},

(7)

where
\widehat{S}_{ij}
(
\widehat{S}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\widehat{C}|\times|\widehat{C}|}
) denotes the point-wise similarities between the webpages from the coarse topic
\widehat{C}
. Rather than using the original similarity
A
,
\widehat{S}
is considered as a refined similarity
28
.

Consequently, a graph
\widehat{G}=(\widehat{V},\widehat{E},\widehat{S})
is constructed, where node
\widehat{V}
represents the webpages in the coarse topic
\widehat{C}
, and edge
\widehat{E}
denotes the connectivity between these webpages,
i.e.
, if
\widehat{S}_{ij}>0
,
\widehat{E}_{ij}=1
; otherwise,
\widehat{E}_{ij}=0
.

Assuming a random walker travelling on the given graph
\widehat{G}
, the probability of a transition from node
i
to node
j
is
P_{ij}=\widehat{S}_{ij}/d_{i}
, where
d_{i}=\sum_{j}\widehat{S}_{ij}
is the out-degree of the node
i
.
P_{ij}
is the probability of the node
i
being visited by the random walker at the
0
-th step.

Aiming at computing the stationary distribution
\pi
, random jumps
14
are introduced into
P_{ij}
as follows:

\pi_{j}=\alpha\sum_{i\in\widehat{V}}\pi_{i}P_{ij}+(1-\alpha)\frac{1}{|\widehat{C}|},

(8)

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
denotes the decay factor that respects the probability with which a walker follows the connected edges, and
1{-}\alpha
is the probability of jumping to a random node. (
8
) is PageRank
23
algorithm, where
\alpha
is usually recommended to range from 0.8 to 0.95
21
. In this paper, we set
\alpha=0.9
in (
8
).

By defining the new transition matrix as
\widehat{P}\leftarrow\alpha P+(1-\alpha)\mathbf{e}\mathbf{e}^{\top}/|\widehat{C}|

(where the vector
\mathbf{e}
is a column of ones), the stationary distribution can be efficiently solved by the power method
21
with the time complexity
O(T\cdot nz(P))
, where
T
is number of iterations in the power method, and
nz(P)
is number of non-zero elements in
P
.
In our experiment, the size of coarse topics
\widehat{C}
range from 20 to 93. Therefore, the computation cost of (
8
) is very small.

3.4
Refining a Coarse Topic by Submodular Selection

To remove uninteresting webpages from a coarse topic, we establish two criteria: 1)
Interestingness:
a hot topic consists of the interesting webpages. 2)
Similarity:
the interesting webpages in a hot topic should be more similar than that of the uninteresting webpages. By following the above criteria, an immediate solution is to select the interesting and similar webpages as follows:

\begin{split}\arg&\max_{I}\sum_{i,j}(I_{i}\pi_{i})\widehat{S}_{ij}(I_{j}\pi_{j})\\
s.t.:&I\mathbf{e}^{\top}=k,\end{split}

(9)

where
I\in\{0,1\}^{1\times|\widehat{C}|}
is the indicator vector,
\widehat{S}
is the similarity matrix, and
k
is the number of the selected webpages. However, the optimization of (
9
) is equal to quadratic integer programming which is NP-hard problem.

In order to approximate (
9
), our approach selects a subset of webpages
\mathcal{P}
as follows:

\arg\max_{\mathcal{P}}g(\mathcal{P})=\lambda\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}}\pi_{i}-\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{P}}\pi_{i}D_{ij}\pi_{j},

(10)

where
i
is the index of the selected webpage
\mathbf{p}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
,
D_{ij}
is the dissimilarity between two webpages, and
\lambda>0
is a parameter that trades off between the interestingness and the dissimilarity.

In (
10
), the first term means that the selected webpages should have the high interestingness scores; while, the second term requires that the selected webpages should have small dissimilarity values between each other. Therefore, the definition of the dissimilarity
D_{ij}
should follow the following property:

Definition 1
.

[Property of
D_{ij}
in (
10
)]
Given three webpages
\mathbf{x}_{i}
,
\mathbf{x}_{j}
, and
\mathbf{x}_{k}
, as well as the corresponding dissimilarity values
D_{ij}
and
D_{kj}
between these webpages, if
\mathbf{x}_{i}
is more similar to
\mathbf{x}_{j}
than that of between
\mathbf{x}_{k}
and
\mathbf{x}_{j}
, then dissimilarity values should satisfy that
D_{ij}<D_{kj}
.

By the definition of the dissimilarity
D
in (
10
), the goodness function
g(\mathcal{P})
approximates the objective function (
9
). In this work, considering that
\widehat{S}_{ij}\in(0,1)
, the dissimilarity
D
is converted from
\widehat{S}
by Gaussian kernel,
i.e.
,
D_{ij}=\text{exp}(-\widehat{S}^{2}_{ij}/\sigma)
, where
\sigma
is the bandwidth to control the decay speed of the similarity
\widehat{S}
. In this paper, we set
\sigma=10
.

3.4.1
Submodular Selection of (
10
)

We present the so-called diminishing return property of the goodness function (
10
) in Proposition
1
. By Proposition
1
, if we add more webpages into an existing subset
\mathcal{P}
, the goodness of (
10
) is non-decreasing.

Proposition 1
.

[Diminishing of
g(\mathcal{P})
in (
10
)]
The goodness function in (
10
) has the following properties:

1.

(
P1: Submodularity.
) for any
\lambda>0
, the goodness
g(\mathcal{P})
is submodular with respect to
\mathcal{P}
;

2.

(
P2: Monotonicity.
) for any
\lambda\geq 2
and
\sum_{ij}D_{ij}=1
, the goodness
g(\mathcal{P})
is monotonically non-decreasing with respect to
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

We firstly prove (P1). For any
\mathcal{P}_{1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}
and a webpage
\mathbf{x}\notin\mathcal{P}_{2}
, we have

\begin{split}g(\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathbf{x}){-}g(\mathcal{P}_{1})&=\lambda\sum_{i\in(\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathbf{x})}\pi_{i}-\sum_{(i,j)\in(\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathbf{x})}\pi_{i}D_{ij}\pi_{j}\\
&-\left(\lambda\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{i}-\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{i}D_{ij}\pi_{j}\right)\\
&=\lambda\pi_{\mathbf{x}}-\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{i}D_{i\mathbf{x}}\pi_{\mathbf{x}}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{\mathbf{x}}D_{\mathbf{x}j}\pi_{j}\right)\end{split}

(11)

Following (
11
), we have
g\left(\mathcal{P}_{2}\cup\mathbf{x}\right)-g\left(\mathcal{P}_{2}\right)=\lambda\pi_{\mathbf{x}}-\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{2}}\pi_{i}D_{i\mathbf{x}}\pi_{\mathbf{x}}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}_{2}}\pi_{\mathbf{x}}D_{\mathbf{x}j}\pi_{j}\right)
.
Therefore, we have

\begin{split}&\left(g(\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathbf{x})-g(\mathcal{P}_{1})\right)-\left(g(\mathcal{P}_{2}\cup\mathbf{x})-g(\mathcal{P}_{2})\right)\\
&=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{2}/\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{i}D_{i\mathbf{x}}\pi_{\mathbf{x}}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}_{2}/\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{\mathbf{x}}D_{\mathbf{x}j}\pi_{j}\geq 0,\end{split}

which completes the proof of (P1).

Next, we prove (P2). Given any
\mathcal{P}_{1}\cap\mathcal{P}_{2}=\varnothing
, where
\varnothing
means an empty set, we have

\begin{split}&g(\mathcal{P}_{2}\cup\mathcal{P}_{1})-g(\mathcal{P}_{2})=\lambda\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{i}-\left(\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{i}D_{ij}\pi_{j}\right.\\
&+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{1},j\in\mathcal{P}_{2}}\pi_{i}D_{ij}\pi_{j}+\left.\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{2},j\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{i}D_{ij}\pi_{j}\right)\\
&=\lambda\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{i}-\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{1},j\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}}\pi_{i}D_{ij}\pi_{j}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{2},j\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{i}D_{ij}\pi_{j}\right)\end{split}

(12)

with the constraint
\lambda\geq 2
and the inequality
\sum a_{i}b_{i}\leq\sum a_{i}\sum b_{i},(a_{i}\geq 0,b_{i}\geq 0)
, (
12
) is relaxed as follows:

\geq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{i}\left(1-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}}D_{ij}\pi_{j}\right)+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\left(1-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{2}}\pi_{i}D_{ij}\right)\pi_{j}

with the constraints
\sum_{ij}D_{ij}=1
,
\sum_{i}\pi_{i}=1
, the above inequality is:

\geq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\pi_{i}\left(1-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}}\pi_{j}\right)+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\left(1-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{2}}\pi_{i}\right)\pi_{j}\geq 0.

which completes the proof of (P2).
∎

Based on the Proposition
1
, a subset of
k
webpages is greedily selected by the following discrete derivative of
g(\mathcal{P})

\begin{split}\triangle(\textbf{p}|\mathcal{P})&=g(\mathcal{P}\cup\{\textbf{p}\})-g(\mathcal{P})\\

(13)

In this paper,

is assigned as
2
. Alg.

3.4.2
Determining the number of the selected webpages

Considering that the size of different topics is very diverse, we present a heuristic approach based on the relative change of the goodness value in (
13
) as follows:

\triangle_{g}^{t}=\frac{g^{t}-g^{t+1}}{g^{t}},

(14)

where
g^{t}
is the maximal increase of the goodness value,
g^{t}=\max_{i}\{\triangle(\mathbf{p}_{i}|\mathcal{P})\}
at the
t
-th selection. That is,
g^{t}
measures the incremented goodness value when a webpage is added into a topic.
When the first noise webpage is being added into a topic at the
t{+}1
-th iteration, the value of
\triangle_{g}^{t}
would be significantly increased. Because the goodness value
g^{t}
is larger than that of
g^{t+1}
. Remark
1
proofs that
\triangle_{g}^{t}
is bounded. To give a vivid illustration of Alg.
1
, Fig.
2
use a 4-nodes toy graph to demonstrate how to greedily select the nodes by
\triangle_{g}^{t}
at the
t
-th iteration.

Remark 1
.

The indicator
\triangle_{g}^{t}
in (
14
) is bounded,
i.e.
,
0\leq\triangle_{g}^{t}\leq 1
.

Conventionally, we would select the webpages which have been selected before the maximal value
\hat{\triangle}_{g}^{t}
, that is,
\hat{\triangle}_{g}^{t}=\max_{t}\{\triangle_{g}^{t}\}
,(
t=1,\ldots,|\hat{C}|
). In this paper, we empirically use a margin
m
(
m>0
) to increase the robustness of a topics. That is, the value
\triangle_{g}^{t}
of a selected node should be larger than
\hat{\triangle}_{g}^{t}+m
. Because the similarity between two webpages are easily corrupted by the sparse and noise textual feature. In this paper, we set
m=0.1
.

In summary, modeling interestingness utilizes pagerank (
8
) to assign a high score to an interesting webpage; besides, these unimportant or error webpages introduced in the bundling step (
6
) would be refined by submodular selection. Therefore, the error webpages are tended to be removed and barely influence the topic ranking results.
As Fig.
2
illustrated, by selecting the maximal value points, we would select two nodes with the interestingness scores with 0.7 and 0.6 as a refined topic.

Time Complexity:
Given a list of topic candidates
\mathcal{C}=C_{0}\prec,\ldots,\prec C_{K}
, the proposed BR method is summarized in Alg.
2
. The time complexity of Alg.
2
consists of both the bundling process and the refining one.

Concretely,
the bundling process
leads to a complexity of
O(K{\cdot}W)
(
W{\ll}K
); while, the time cost of
the refining process
is
O(K(T\cdot nz(P)+|\widehat{C}|\cdot M))
. Usually, the number size of an unrefined topic is larger than

(

,

). Therefore, the time complexity of the proposed BR is
O(K(T\cdot nz(P)+|\widehat{C}|\cdot M+W))
.
In practice,

is smaller than 20, the maximum number of a coarse topic

(
|\widehat{C}|>M
) is also smaller than 50, and the matrix

is very sparse. Therefore, the proposed BR is quite efficient.

Input:

The dissimilarity matrix
D\in\mathbb{R}^{|\widehat{C}|\times|\widehat{C}|}
, the weight
\lambda\geq 2
, and the score vector
\mathbf{\triangle_{g}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\widehat{C}|\times 1}

Top-10

For top-10

the averaged F1 scores of the top 10 detected topics
are averaged to measure the performance:

F_{1}=\frac{2\times Precision\times Recall}{Precision+Recall},

(15)

where
D
is a detected topic,
G
is a ground truth topic, and
|\cdot|

Precision=\frac{|D\cap G|}{|D|}
is the precision,
Recall=\frac{|D\cap G|}{|G|}
is the recall. However, top-10
F_{1}
versus NDT does not consider the influence of the number of detected topics. Therefore, accuracy versus FPPT is further proposed to handle this problem
26

For accuracy versus FPPT, if a topic is correctly detected, how many falsely detected topics are generated by a detection system, where accuracy is defined as follows:

\text{Accuracy}=\frac{\#\text{Successful}}{\#\text{Groundtruth}}.

(16)

A topic candidate

r=\frac{|D\cap G|}{|D\cup G|}
is larger than a threshold. Following the previous work
26
, NIR with a threshold of 0.5 is used in our experiments.

In summary, the top-10 F1 focuses on measuring the partial performance of the top ranked topics. While the accuracy measures the overall performance of the detected results.

4.2
Methods in Comparison Study

Our experimental goal is to compare the proposed approach with four state-of-the-art methods:

1.

Event-Clustering Based Method (ECBM)
5

Different from the PD-based method
26

5

Note that this approach involves many engineering details and the tuning of the hyper-parameters. We implemented this method by ourselves and reported the best tuned results.

2.

Multi-Modality Graph (MMG)
41

This baseline belongs to the similarity-graph based method. Zhang
et al.

41
utilize the NDKs of video clips and the textual information to build the similarity graph
30

3.

Maximal Cliques with Poisson Deconvolution (MCPD)
26
.
Rather than using NMFR to generate topics, MCPD leverages Maximal Cliques (MCs) to generate topics. This comparison demonstrates the generalization ability of the proposed BR method across different topic patterns. Because a good generalization ability guarantees the success of the combination of the BR method and the other topic patterns,
e.g.

35

4.

Latent Poisson Deconvolution (LPD)
29

This method achieves the state-of-the-art performances on both

5
Conclusion

In this paper, BR is proposed to boost performances of the DBR approach. BR consists of the
Bundling
and the
Refining
steps. The
Bundling
step puts fragment topics into a whole yet coarse topic; while, the
Refining
step refines a coarse topic into a fine one. Considering than the
Refining
step is equivalent to strip noise webpages, without the complex training process, the proposed submodular selection adaptively refines coarse topics into more accurate, compact and coherent ones. Experiments show that the proposed method meets or surpasses the state-of-the-art methods in terms of both effectiveness and efficiency.

The promising results of this paper motivate a further examination of the components of the proposed BR. A high-efficient method to generate topic candidates should be investigated. For instance, an incremental method to generate topic candidates on a graph
9
may remedy the drawback of the off-line clustering method here.

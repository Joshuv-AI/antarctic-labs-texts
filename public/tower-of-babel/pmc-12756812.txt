Structures and Chemical Bonding of B5O7 +/0/– with a Hexagonal B3O3 Ring†
Authors: Shu-Juan Gao, Chong Hu, Feng Li, Ling Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This work aims to illustrate the influence of a single electron on the bonding properties and geometric structure of a benzene-like cluster, using B5O7 +/0/– as an exemplar. The inherent electron deficiency of boron dictates not only the rich structural variety of boron oxide clusters but also their unique electronic characteristics and unconventional bonding schemes. The discovery of cyclic boron oxide clusters has facilitated the study of their unique structures and bonding properties. We report a study of the bonding properties of B5O7 +/0/– clusters. The B5O7 + clusters exhibit a GM structure consisting of a boroxol core and two terminal OBO groups. The neutral B5O7 and anionic B5O7 – clusters are similar in structure, consisting of a boroxol core and three terminal ligands: a terminal boronyl group and an oxygen group and a terminal OBO group. In contrast to the B5O7 + and B5O7, a single electron completely reverses the potential energy surface of this system. All three structures have the heteroatomic ring B3O3 as their key structural. The terminal OBO ligand also has a double 3c–4e high valent π-bond.

Introduction

Owing to its inherent electron deficiency, elemental boron forms clusters with remarkable structural and electronic properties, often involving nonclassical chemical bonding. − This field has matured into a rich research paradigm, largely defined by the continual discovery of novel boron clusters. These species have established boron oxides as a cornerstone of main-group chemistry, celebrated for their nonclassical bonding and architectural complexity. Research on boron oxide clusters has predominantly focused on boron-rich systems, leaving their oxygen-rich counterparts receiving considerably less attention. −

The pioneering work of Zhai, Wang, and co-workers in 2007 significantly advanced the study of boron oxide clusters. By integrating anion photoelectron spectroscopy (PES) with theoretical calculations, they successfully elucidated the properties of the simplest prototypes, BO– and BO2–, which served as the basic unit, with the boronyl group (BO) appearing to dominate their structure. − As for the B n O n system, a wide range of BO, B2O2, B3O3, B4O4, B5O5, B6O6 and B8O8 clusters have recently been studied. − Among these clusters, rhombic, pentagonal or hexagonal B–O rings have been found to be the key structural units, for example the D3h B6O6 cluster has been computationally identified in which the hexagonal B3O3 ring, there are three B–O groups on the periphery. In addition, boron oxide clusters exhibit novel chemical bonding, 4c–4e bonds (o-bonds) in the B2O2 unit and double 3c–4e high valent bonds (ω-bonds) in the OBO ligands. , An increasingly profound understanding of bonding within boron-based nanoclusters plays an indispensable role in designing and analyzing the electronic structure, adsorption properties, and sensing capabilities of boron-based nanomaterials. −

We report a computational investigation of the structural, electronic, and bonding of B5O7 +/0/– clusters. The results show that the B5O7 + cluster have a GM structure consisting of a boroxol oxide core and two terminal OBO groups. The neutral B5O7 and anionic B5O7 – clusters have a similar structure consisting of a boroxol core and three terminal ligands: a terminal boronyl group and an oxygen and a terminal OBO unit. In contrast to the B5O7 + and B5O7 cluster, a single electron completely reverses the potential energy surface of this system. Conclusive bonding analyses reveal that the GM B5O7 +/0/– clusters exhibit a 6π-aromatic system and are stabilized by sophisticated double 3c–4e π hypervalent bonds.

Methods

Global structure searches of the clusters were calculated at the PBE0/6-31g level using the Coalescence Kick (CK) algorithm, supplemented by artificial structure construction. − More than 8000 standing points (4000 single and 4000 triplet states) were explored on the potential energy surface of the B5O7 + cluster, more than 4000 standing points were explored on the potential energy surface of the B5O7 cluster. The most stable isomers identified from the initial screening were further treated at the benchmark PBE0/6–311+G­(d) level, where geometry optimization and subsequent frequency analysis validated all low-energy structures as genuine minima on the potential energy surface. The computational consistency was verified by reoptimizing the low-energy structures at the B3LYP/6–311+G­(d) level, which yielded congruent geometric and energetic data. Furthermore, the relative energies of the five lowest-lying isomers were benchmarked using CCSD­(T) single-point calculations performed on the geometries obtained from both PBE0 and B3LYP methods. − The computational data demonstrate excellent agreement across all four levels of theory. For clarity and coherence, the PBE0 level has been selected as the benchmark for the detailed presentation and discussion in this work.

Chemical bonding was analyzed using multiple approaches: Natural Bond Orbital (NBO) analysis (version 6.0) provided Weinberg Bond Indices (WBIs) and natural atomic charges, while canonical molecular orbital (CMO) analysis and adaptive natural density partitioning (AdNDP) offered further electronic structure insights, the linear combination of spin–orbitals is obtained from Occupancy/Coefficients/Hybrids Bond orbital. The π aromaticity of the system was probed by calculating iso-chemical shielding surfaces (ICSSs) and electron localization functions (ELFs). , All quantum chemical calculations were conducted at the PBE0/6–311G level. The subsequent analyses, including ICSS, orbital composition, and AdNDP, were performed utilizing the Multiwfn program. The Gaussian 09 package was used for all electronic structure calculations; subsequent visualization and analysis employed GaussView, CYLview, and VMD. −

Results

Global-Minimum Structure

A systematic global search for the B5O7 +/0/– clusters was first conducted at the PBE0/6–31G level of theory, employing the Coalescence Kick (CK) algorithm supplemented by artificial structure construction. This search located over 8000 stationary on the B5O7 + potential energy surface. The low-energy candidate isomers identified from this screening were subsequently reoptimized at the PBE0/6–311+G­(d) level. To verify methodological consistency, comparative geometry optimizations were further performed at the B3LYP/6–311+G­(d) level. The reliability of the density functional results was confirmed by benchmarking against single-point energy calculations at the CCSD­(T) level. The excellent agreement in relative energies across all four theoretical levels allowed for the conclusive identification of the global minimum (GM) structures, as depicted in Figure . Energetic analysis reveals significant stability of the GM structures: GM B5O7 + is more stable than its closest competitor by 0.17 eV at the PBE0 level, a value refined to 0.30 eV at the CCSD­(T) level. Similarly, GM B5O7 demonstrates stabilities of 0.35 and 0.42 eV at the PBE0 and CCSD­(T) levels, respectively. Cartesian coordinates for all GM structures and a comprehensive presentation of the geometries and relative energies for the first 20 low-lying isomers are provided in the Supporting Information (Table S1 and Figures S1, S2).

The GM B5O7 + cluster has quasi-planar C s symmetry as shown in Figure a. It consists of an internal B3O3 hexagonal ring and two terminal OBO ligand units. The GM B5O7 and B5O7 – clusters are structurally similar and are composed of a boroxol core and three terminal ligands: a terminal boronyl group, an oxygen unit and a terminal OBO unit. Since GM B5O7 and B5O7 – clusters structures are similar, we will only discuss B5O7 here.

Bond Distances, Wiberg Bond Indices, and Natural Atomic Charges

The upper bond-length limits, derived from standard covalent radii, are 1.70 Å for B–B and 1.48 Å for B–O bonds, consistently with the referenced typical distances for single (B–B: 1.62 Å, B–O: 1.38 Å), double (BB: 1.51 Å, BO: 1.28 Å), and triple (BO: 1.21 Å) bonds. In the B5O7 + cluster, the calculated distances of B–O bonds within the B3O3 ring are slightly inhomogeneous: 1.27–1.48 Å (Figure a). The structural distortion of the B3O3 ring, caused by the binding of two terminal ligands, accounts for the observed inhomogeneity. Overall, the backbone bonds in the B3O3 ring are approximately σ single bond. The OBO ligands have inconsistent B–O distances: 1.20 versus 1.35 Å. Their average value corresponds to a double bond. The B–O bonds linking the B3O3 ring to the OBO ligand are 1.31 Å and can be described as a σ bond. In the B5O7 cluster, the B–O bonds within the B3O3 ring are almost unanimously calculated to be 1.37–1.38 Å (Figure b). The backbone bonds in the B3O3 ring of the cluster are standard σ single bonds. The OBO ligands have inconsistent B–O distances: 1.21 versus 1.32 Å. Their average value corresponds to a double bond. The B–O bond linking the B3O3 ring to the OBO ligand is 1.36 Å and can be described as a σ single bond. The B–B linkage in the cluster (1.69 Å) is described as a σ single bond, although the bond appears to be weak, The B–O distances confirm distinct bonding environments: a triple-bonded boronyl group (BO, 1.21 Å) in the terminal BO ligand, and a double bond (BO, 1.36 Å) in the upper BO unit. The GM B5O7 – cluster (Figure c) is geometrically similar to the GM B5O7 cluster.

The NBO analyses have provided pairs of Wiberg Bond Indices (WBIs) which support the structural assignments of the corresponding bonds described above (Figure ). In the B5O7 + cluster, the B–O bonds within the B3O3 ring exhibit WBIs ranging from 0.67 to 1.19, which is consistent with single-bond character. For the two terminal OBO ligands, the WBIs range from 0.80 to 1.97. The average value of these indices is 1.39, which is significantly higher than that expected for a single bond, thereby confirming their double-bond character. The B–O bonds connecting the B3O3 ring to the OBO ligands possess WBIs of 1.00, fully consistent with a single-bond description. In the neutral B5O7 cluster, the B–O bonds within the B3O3 ring display WBIs between 0.85 and 0.92 (Figure b), indicating that the skeletal bonds of this ring are standard single bonds. The terminal OBO ligands show WBIs ranging from 0.90 to 1.86, with an average value of 1.38 that is markedly higher than the single-bond level, again confirming double-bond character. The connecting B–O bond between the ring and an OBO ligand has a WBI of 0.85, which can be described as a single bond. Furthermore, the sole B–B bond in this cluster has a bond order of 0.98, defined as a single bond, while the BO unit exhibits a WBI of 1.87, which is boronyl (BO) group.

Figure presents the computed natural atomic charge distributions for the B5O7 +/0/– clusters. As illustrated in Figure a, the B5O7 + cluster carries an overall positive charge. Within this cation, a significant intramolecular charge transfer is observed, resulting in a negative charge of −0.35 |e| localized on the OBO ligand and a compensating positive charge of +1.69 |e| on the hexagonal B3O3 core. The natural atomic charge distribution for the neutral B5O7 cluster is displayed in Figure b. The cluster is electrically neutral overall, with charges distributed as follows: the OBO ligand bears a negative charge of −0.23 |e|, the hexagonal B3O3 core carries a positive charge of +0.37 |e|, and the boronyl (BO) ligand remains nearly neutral with a net charge of +0.02 |e|. Notably, the upper oxygen atom possesses a substantial negative charge of −0.64 |e|. Structurally, the global minimum (GM) of the B5O7 – cluster (Figure c) closely resembles that of the neutral B5O7 cluster, with its corresponding anionic charge distribution detailed in the figure.

Discussion

Despite differing by only a single electron, the B5O7 + and B5O7 clusters exhibit distinctly different GM configurations. To elucidate the unique core-ligand architecture in these two GM species, a detailed analysis of chemical bonding is essential. Canonical molecular orbital (CMO) analysis provides the foundational framework for such an investigation. As illustrated in Figure , the B5O7 + cluster possesses 56 valence electrons, which occupy a set of CMOs that can be categorized into four groups based on the character of their constituent atomic orbitals (AOs).

Pictures of occupied canonical molecular orbitals (CMOs) of GM B5O7 + cluster. (a) Five lone pairs for the O atom. (b) Twelve σ CMOs for Lewis-type B–O σ bonds. (c) Eight CMOs for both in-plane and out-of-plane π bonds relative to terminal OBO units. (d) Three delocalized π CMOs over the B3O3 ring.

Subset (a) shows five lone pairs O sites except O10O9 (see Figure for atomic labeling), specifically HOMO-21 and HOMO-22 are two O 2S lone pairs on the terminal OBO ligand, and HOMO-23/HOMO-24/HOMO-25 are bonding/nonbonding/antibonding combinations typical of triple-symmetric systems, which can be recombined along the B3O3 ring into three O lone pairs.

Subset (b) shows the 12 B–O bonds in B5O7 + cluster. Notwithstanding their delocalized nature, these CMOs can be qualitatively characterized as follows. The first set of six CMOs corresponds roughly to three B–O bonds close to the two OBO ligands. The following set of six CMOs represents a delocalized σ framework for the hexagonal ring, with electron density arising from O 2p and B 2p atomic orbitals oriented radially and tangentially. These CMOs correspond directly to the six skeletal B–O σ bonds.

The subset of (c) has 8 CMOs, which are localized π bonds associated with the two OBO ligands. Specifically, in B5O7 + cluster, HOMO-9, HOMO-10 is a fully π-bonded CMO (Figure c). It contains in-face 2p y AO contributions from sites O10, B5 and O12, as well as in-face 2p y AO contributions from sites O7, B4 and O9. Atomic labeling is shown in Figure . The second in-face π CMOs are HOMO-1 and HOMO-3. HOMO-1 and HOMO-3 show destructive bonding between the two O centers in the two-sided OBO unit. In contrast to HOMO-9, HOMO-10 as a three-center π-bonded CMO, HOMO-1 and HOMO-3 should mainly be classified as unbonded, especially between the two O sites. Similarly, the combination of the remaining four orbitals is along the 3c–4e π bonds of the OBO ligands in the B5O7 + cluster. The bonding within the OBO ligand is composed of a quadruple 3c–4e π bond and two polar B–O σ bonds (supported by an O 2p x lone pair). This configuration results in each boron atom forming a σ bond and sharing both in-plane and out-of-plane 3c–4e π bonds with the oxygen atoms. Consequently, each B–O bond has a formal order of 2, and the boron center a total order of 4. The 3c–4e π bonding framework accounts for 8 electron pairs.

A π-framework is established across the hexagonal ring by the three canonical molecular orbitals in subset (d), displaying a spatial arrangement reminiscent of the π-system in benzene. As a result, B5O7 + can be regarded as the sixth representative of the “inorganic benzene” series. While this π-hexamer constitutes a six-center structure, the contributions from constituent centers show greater disparity compared to the uniform electron distribution in benzene. The system contains six π electrons, conforming to the Hückel aromaticity criterion (4n + 2).

The canonical molecular orbital (CMO) distribution of the GM B5O7 cluster closely resembles that of GM B5O7 –. As illustrated in Figure S3, the occupied CMOs of both clusters can be categorized into five groups based on their AOs. Subset (a) corresponds to seven lone pairs located at all oxygen atomic sites except the O9 site. Subset (b) represents 11 B–O σ single bonds. The remaining 11 electron pairs are allocated to eight 3c–4e OBO π bonds and three delocalized π bonds within the hexagonal ring. Subset (c) consists of four CMOs, corresponding to π bond associated with the OBO unit and the boronyl unit. Subset (d) contains only one CMO responsible for the formation of the B–B σ bond. Within subset (d), the three CMOs collectively construct the delocalized π-framework of the hexagonal ring.

For a better and more intuitive understanding of the chemical binding, AdNDP analysis can be performed. The AdNDP bonding scheme of B5O7 + cluster is reasonable (Figure ). As shown in Figure a, the O lone pairs in the B5O7 + cluster display near-ideal occupancy numbers (ONs) of 1.81–1.98 |e|. Meanwhile, the ONs for all backbone B–B and B–O σ bonds are 1.99 |e|. All of the above elements are strictly Lewis-type, consuming a total of 17 electron pairs. The 11 pairs of electrons are shared and π-hexametrized between the four 3c–4e OBO π bonds. In the B5O7 cluster, the O lone pairs are recovered in Figure S4a with an occupancy number (ONs) of 1.82–1.98 |e|, which is close to ideal. The ONs for all backbone B–O and B–B σ bonds are 1.99 |e|. The ONs for the B–O π bonds associated with the terminal O and boron ligands are 1.82–1.99 |e|, including the π bonds which occupy a value of 0.92 due to the fact that this cluster system is an open shell layer. The remaining seven pairs of electrons in the B5O7 cluster are shared between the 3c–4e OBO π-bonds (four pairs) and the π-hexamers (three pairs). To facilitate a clearer understanding of the spatial distribution of the 3c–4e π bond around the B3O3 ring, we have provided Lewis-type structures for the B5O7 +/0/– clusters in the Supporting Information (Figure S5). These structures allow one to more intuitively visualize the 6π delocalized bonding and the dual three-center four-electron “ω-π hyperbonds”.

In order to study the π-aromaticity in B5O7 + cluster, we performed ICSS calculations. Specifically, ICSSzz(1) data were obtained 1 Å below the B3O3 ring (Figure ), which allows a rough distinction of π-aromaticity. It is apparent that cationic B5O7 + cluster exhibit significantly higher ICSS values compared to neutral B5O7 and anionic B5O7 – clusters, consistent with their greater aromaticity (as confirmed by ELF, as detailed below). Furthermore, Figure b,c reveal that B5O7 and B5O7 – clusters share similar response values, which confirms their comparable delocalization characteristics. To validate the ICSS analysis results, we provide the variation in magnetic shielding values for the B3O3 cluster with the ring plane oriented vertically upward, to facilitate direct comparison of aromaticity between these B5O7 +/0/– (Figure S6).

Iso-chemical shielding surfaces (ICSSs) of (a) GM B5O7 + (C s , 1A), (b) GM B5O7 (C s , 2A), and (c) B5O7 – (C s , 1A) cluster. ICSS zz (1) at 1.0 Å below B3O3 ring. For ICSSs, a positive value indicates aromaticity, and vice versa.

Alternatively, we can use the ELF data to compare the π-aromaticity of a number of species as described above (Figure ). The π divergence values of B5O7 + cluster and B5O7 cluster are 0.58 and 0.54 for ELFπ. This trend is in perfect agreement with the ICSS data. Not surprisingly, the ELFπ values are on the small side. We suggest that the inhomogeneity of the ligand linkage and the resulting structural distortion of the B3O3 ring in B5O7 +/0 cluster are the main reasons for their weak 6π aromaticity.

We calculate the charge density difference (CDD) diagrams for mentioned B5O7 +/0/– clusters, which may correctly describe the interaction between the adsorbate and adsorbent at the interface region. As shown in the Figure S7 (Supporting Information), we have calculated the charge density difference (CDD) diagrams for the B5O7 +/0/– clusters. It can be observed that the reaction site for cluster B5O7 + is at B3, while the reaction site for cluster B5O7 – is at O12.

Comparing B4O4 –/0, B6O6 –/0, and B8O8 –/0, with the B5O7 +/0/– clusters discussed in this work (Figure S8, Supporting Information), similarities and differences in bonding are observed: all four types of boron oxide clusters feature delocalized bonding in their core structural unitsthe rhombic B2O2 ring, the B3O3 hexagon, and the fused heteronuclear ringswhich is facilitated by the involvement of 2p electrons from oxygen atoms. These delocalized bonds significantly enhance structural stability. The differences lie in the electron compensation mechanisms: the rhombic B2O2 unit in B4O4 is compensated by a (4c–4e) “o–π bond”; the five-membered ring in B8O8 –/0 is compensated by a (5c–4e) π bond (effectively 2π); whereas both B5O7 +/0/– and B6O6 –/0 are compensated by (6c–6e) π bonds.

This suggests that the formation of rings in boron–oxygen heterocyclic clusters can be governed by several key structural motifs, such as the four-center four-electron (4c–4e) “o–π bond,” the dual three-center four-electron (3c–4e) “ω–π hyperbond” associated with linear BO2 units, the B3O3 hexagonal ring with 6π delocalized bonding, as well as boronyl (BO) structural units.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that through global structural searches and quantum chemical calculations on the B5O7 +/0/– boron oxide clusters, we can systematically reveal their structural evolution, electronic characteristics, and bonding mechanisms. All the studied clusters contain a B3O3 heterocyclic hexagonal core, but their ligand termination patterns and overall configurations vary significantly with changes in charge state. In the neutral B5O7 cluster, the three unsaturated boron sites are terminated by a boronyl group, an OBO ligand, and an oxygen ligand, respectively, whereas the global minimum structure of the B5O7 + cation consists of a B3O3 ring and two terminal OBO units. Bonding analysis further reveals that π-hexamer aromatic structures analogous to benzene are present in B5O7 +/0/–, qualifying them as new members of the “inorganic benzene” family. In addition, the double 3c–4e hypervalent π bonds formed in the terminal OBO ligands are key factors stabilizing these clusters. Notably, changes in the electronic configuration in this system can lead to a complete reconstruction of the potential energy surface, highlighting the sensitive regulatory role of charge state on structural preference. This research shows that by rationally tuning the electronic configuration of clusters, targeted design and expansion of boron-based nanostructures can be achieved, offering a new perspective for exploring structure-bonding relationships.

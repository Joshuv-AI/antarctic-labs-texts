Sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab for advanced triple-negative breast cancer with PD-L1-positive: a cost-effectiveness analysis
Authors: Binliang Liu, Tao Wu, Fang Xu, Dongmei Sun, Yu Tang, Can Tian, Liping Liu, Zheyu Hu, Quchang Ouyang, Ning Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTherapeutic options for patients with poor-prognosis invasive triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) remain lacking. Although novel treatment strategies have demonstrated improved efficacy, they are frequently associated with substantial financial costs. Consequently, rigorous economic evaluation is essential to inform clinical and policy decisions.MethodsA three-state Markov model with a lifetime horizon was developed using clinical efficacy data from the ASCENT-04/KEYNOTE-D19 trial, along with treatment-related cost inputs. Lifetime costs and health outcomes in cases of advanced TNBC for patients undergoing treatment with either sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab or chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab were estimated with the developed model. Model uncertainty and structural stability were examined via probabilistic and deterministic sensitivity analyses. Total costs, quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs), incremental net health benefit (INHB), incremental net monetary benefit (INMB), and incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) were examined at a willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold of $150,000/QALY in the USA.ResultsThe estimated lifetime cost was $1,831,187 for sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab and $574,011 for chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab. Corresponding health outcomes were 5.28 QALYs and 4.74 QALYs, respectively. The resulting ICER was $2,323,487 per QALY gained. The INHB and INMB were -7.84 QALYs and -$1,176,175, respectively. The most influential variables in the model included the utility value assigned to progression-free survival, patient body weight, and the cost-effectivness of sacituzumab govitecan. Sensitivity analysis demonstrated that the model findings were robust.ConclusionsAt current market prices, sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab showed limited cost-effectiveness as a first-line option relative to chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab for treating advanced TNBC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13561-026-00824-x.

Introduction

Breast cancer is ranked second in cancer diagnoses and fourth in terms of glocal cancer-associated mortality [1]. In 2022, ~ 2,296,840 new cases were reported globally, with 666,103 associated deaths [1]. Approximately 15–20% of patients initially present with triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) when the disease has already metastasized [2]., associated with aggressive clinical behavior, distinct molecular features, and five-year survival rates below 15% [3]. Approximately 40% of TNBC are positive for programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) [4]. The NCCN guidelines, based on the results of the KEYNOTE 355 investigation, recommend pembrolizumab together with chemotherapy as first-line treatment for those with a PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) ≥ 10 [4, 5]. Although this regimen improves clinical outcomes, the median progression-free survival (PFS) benefit over chemotherapy alone is limited to 4.1 months, and approximately 50% of patients experience disease recurrence despite treatment [4]. Furthermore, nearly half of patients with metastatic TNBC do not proceed to second-line therapy following first-line treatment failure [6, 7]. These limitations underscore the need for more effective and accessible first-line therapeutic strategies.

Antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) are frequently utilized for cancer treatment, with indications extending from later-line settings to earlier lines of therapy [8]. Increasingly, ADCs are being combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Sacituzumab govitecan is a trophoblast cell surface antigen 2 (Trop-2)-targeting ADC. It consists of humanized monoclonal anti-Trop-2 conjugated to SN-38, an inhibitor of topoisomerase I, through a water-soluble linker [9]. The recent phase III trial, ASCENT-04/KEYNOTE-D19 (NCT05116189), demonstrated that sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab significantly improved PFS compared with chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab in advanced TNBC (median PFS 11.2 vs. 7.8 months; HR 0.65; 95% CI 0.51–0.84; P < 0.001) [10]. This suggests that this combined regimen might redefine the typical first-line paradigm for treating advanced TNBC with PD-L1-positive population.

However, highly effective innovative therapies are frequently tethered to substantial economic burden. In the context of healthcare reform and the increasing emphasis on value-based care, economic evaluation has become critical for guiding reimbursement and clinical decision-making. Assessing whether the clinical benefits of novel regimens justify their costs is particularly relevant in TNBC, where treatment options are limited and resource allocation decisions carry significant implications. Here, an important knowledge gap is addressed by evaluating the cost-effectiveness of sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab compared with chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab as first-line regimen for treating advanced TNBC with PD-L1 positivity. The findings are intended to inform clinicians, policymakers, and stakeholders involved in oncology care planning.

Methods

Data on clinical safety and efficacy were obtained from the ASCENT-04/KEYNOTE-D19 trial, supplemented by published literature for economic parameters. This investigation of cost-effectiveness was conducted per 2022 CHEERS guidelines [10, 11] (Supplementary Table S1).

Population and treatments

The modeled cohort (n = 445) reflected the patient population enrolled in the ASCENT-04/KEYNOTE-D19 trial: individuals with new TNBC diagnoses, where the tumor had advanced locally or metastasized, was unsuited for resection, had not been treated with systemic agents, and showed PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 [10]. Of these, 221 patients received sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab and 222 received chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab. Tumor assessment was performed every 12 weeks. Upon documentation of progressive disease (PD), second-line treatment with doxorubicin plus vinorelbine was administered in 9 patients (4.1%) from the sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab group and 26 patients (11.7%) from the chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab group, consistent with NCCN guidelines and relevant randomized controlled trials [5, 12]. Remaining patients received best supportive care. Additional treatment details are provided in Supplementary Table S2. Patients were assumed to enter the end-of-life phase one year prior to treatment-related death. Model overview and transitions

A decision-analytic Markov model with mutually exclusive PFS, PD, and death was constructed to simulate disease progression (Supplementary Figure S1). On model entry, patients were classified as PFS but could shift to the PD or death states with each cycle. Similarly, PD pateints could stay as PD or move to death, which represented an absorbing state. The duration of the cycle was 12 weeks, consistent with tumor evaluation intervals in the clinical trial. The Markov cycle length was set at 12 weeks with the lifetime horizon [10], and the model was run with a lifetime horizon [10]. Annual discounts of 3% were applied to both health outcomes and costs [13] (Table 1). The primary outcomes were the overall costs, life-years (LYs), QALYs, incremental net health benefit (INHB), incremental net monetary benefit (INMB), and ICERs, evaluated using a WTP threshold of $150,000 per QALY in the USA [13]. ICER refers to the incremental cost required to achieve an incremental effect, and it makes the difference between different treatments by comparing the ratio of the cost difference to the effective output. ICER = ∆C/∆E (∆C is the incremental cost, ∆E is the incremental effect). The calculated ICER results should also be correlated with the patient’s WTP to determine whether the intervention is cost-effective. TreeAge Pro 2011 was utilized for model implementation, and R v 4.5.2 was used for analysis.

Reconstructed pseudo-individual patient data (IPD) were derived from the KEYNOTE-D19 trial curves via the Guyot et al. (2012) method [17]. Raw coordinates were first extracted from the KM curves using GetData Graph Digitizer (v2.26), and then combined with the reported 'number at risk' and total events to mathematically recalculate individual survival times and censoring events by R R v 4.5.2 with the ‘KMSubtraction’, ‘survival’, and ‘survminer’ packages [17, 18]. These data were used to estimate transition probabilities and extrapolate long-term survival. Five parametric survival distributions (Weibull, Exponential, Log-normal, Log-logistic, Gompertz) were evaluated using MATLAB v R2020b [13]. Models were selected according to Akaike and Bayesian criteria, visual fit, and clinical plausibility. The Weibull distribution demonstrated superior overall performance and was therefore chosen for the base-case analysis (Supplementary Figure S2 and Table S3-4) [13]. The probabilities of time-dependent transitions for each cycle were calculated using the Weibull function: 1-exp(λtγ-λ[t−1]γ) for the Weibull model, where t denotes the cycle number, and λ (scale) and γ (shape) represent the Weibull parameters [13] (Table 1).

Utilities and costs estimates

The utility was used to reflect patients’ quality-of-life (QoL) weights in the natural history of the disease from 0 (death) to 1 (total health). These utilities were applied to LYs to derive QALYs, with appropriate discounting. As there was no information on health-related quality of life parameters in the ASCENT-04/KEYNOTE-D19 trial, utility values were estimated from earlier studies [14, 15]. The mean utility values were 0.875 for PFS and 0.573 for PD. To account for treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), disutility adjustments were incorporated. For grade 3 or higher TRAEs occurring at an incidence ≥ 5%, a mean disutility of 0.141 was applied [13, 15] (Table 1). Drug acquisition costs were from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services and relevant literature [13, 15, 16]. This analysis was performed from the perspective of U.S. public payers and therefore included only direct medical costs (Table 1). These costs comprised drug acquisition, imaging and surveillance, management of TRAEs, administration expenses, and best supportive and end-of-life care. All cost inputs were adjusted to 2025 dollars using the appropriate inflation index.

Sensitivity and subgroup analyses

To evaluate parameter uncertainty and assess model robustness, multiple sensitivity analyses were conducted, including one-way, two-way, and probabilistic sensitivity analyses. Unless otherwise specified, all parameters were varied within ± 20% of their base-case values, consistent with standard modeling practice. In the one-way analysis, individual parameters were varied across their predefined ranges to determine their influence on the ICER. The results are presented in a tornado diagram [13]. The two-way analysis assessed the combined impact of sacituzumab govitecan and pembrolizumab costs, as these variables were identified in the one-way analysis as having substantial influence on cost-effectiveness outcomes [19]. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA) was entailed 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations. Model parameters were selected from appropriate probability distributions according to parameter type, following recommended methodological standards [13]. Curves to assess acceptability of cost-effectiveness and scatterplots for changes in cost-effectiveness were generated to illustrate the odds of cost-effectiveness across varying WTP thresholds.

Subgroup cost-effectiveness analyses were also performed across eight clinically relevant patient subgroups defined by age, ECOG performance status, disease presentation, previous immunotherapy with PD-1 or PD-L1 antibodies, chemotherapy regimen prior to randomization, history of liver metastases, history of brain metastases, and menopausal status. Because subgroup-specific Kaplan–Meier survival curves were unavailable, subgroup modeling incorporated hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals for PFS derived from subgroup analyses, while all other model parameters were the same as those utilized for the overall population [13].

Results

Base-case results

In the base-case scenario, the projected life expectancy for patients receiving sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab was 7.88 life-years (94.56 months), compared with 7.28 life-years (87.36 months) for those treated with chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab. After adjusting for quality of life and applying a 3% annual discount rate, total effectiveness was estimated at 5.28 QALYs for sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab and 4.74 QALYs for chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab. Corresponding lifetime costs were $1,831,187 and $574,011, respectively. The resulting ICER for sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab versus chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab was $2,323,487 per QALY gained. The INMB and INHB were -$1,176,175 and −7.84 QALYs, respectively (Table 2). Sensitivity and subgroup analyses

The one-way analysis identified three parameters with the greatest influence on the ICER, including the utility value assigned to PFS, patient weight, and the cost of sacituzumab govitecan (Fig. 1A). The ICER decreased most substantially when the PFS utility was increased to its upper bound ($1,853,180/QALY), or when body weight ($1,898,441/QALY) or sacituzumab govitecan cost ($1,904,108/QALY) were reduced to their lower bounds. In contrast, variations in other parameters produced comparatively modest changes, with ICER values remaining below $2,639,336/QALY across their respective ranges. Discussion

The combination of ICIs with ADCs has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy across multiple solid tumors, including aggressive and treatment-resistant malignancies [10, 20–23]. This dual-targeted approach represents a potential shift away from the long-established paradigm of ICIs combined with chemotherapy in TNBC, offering a novel first-line alternative. Despite encouraging clinical outcomes, these regimens are associated with substantial financial costs. As healthcare systems worldwide increasingly emphasize value-based care and efficient resource allocation, assessing both clinical benefit and economic sustainability has become essential. The present study addresses this need by providing a comprehensive cost-effectiveness evaluation of sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab for newly diagnosed advanced TNBC, thereby contributing important economic evidence to complement existing clinical data.

Prior economic studies in TNBC have primarily evaluated ADC monotherapy or ICIs combined with chemotherapy. Based on findings from the ASCENT-03 trial, multiple analyses demonstrated that sacituzumab govitecan monotherapy for advanced or metastatic TNBC produced ICERs exceeding commonly accepted WTP thresholds in both the United States and China [15, 24, 25]. Similarly, economic analyses derived from the KEYNOTE-355 trial indicated that pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy yielded ICERs of $44,157/QALY in the United States and $319,506.90/QALY in China [26, 27]. These findings suggest that while immunotherapy combinations may be economically acceptable in certain settings, incorporating high-cost ADCs can markedly increase financial burden. To date, however, no economic evaluation has examined combinations of ADCs and ICIs as first-line therapy for advanced TNBC. This study is therefore the first to assess the economic implications of this dual-target strategy. Our lifetime Markov model demonstrated that sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab provides additional survival gains compared with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, with an estimated life expectancy of 7.88 years. After adjusting for quality-of-life effects, the ICER was $2,323,487 per QALY, markedly higher than the predefined USA WTP threshold of $150,000/QALY. Thus, under current pricing conditions, the combination regimen is not cost-effective.

In one-way sensitivity analysis, the utility allocated to the PFS state exerted the greatest influence on ICER estimates. Nevertheless, even when varied across its full range, the ICER substantially exceeded the WTP threshold. Patient body weight was the second most influential parameter. Because sacituzumab govitecan dosing is weight-based, lower body weight reduced overall treatment cost and improved cost-effectiveness estimates. This observation raises potential equity concerns, as heavier patients may be disproportionately disadvantaged when high-cost, weight-based therapies are evaluated under conventional cost-effectiveness thresholds. Drug pricing was also a key driver of model outcomes. Two-way sensitivity analysis indicated that cost-effectiveness at a WTP of $150,000/QALY could be achieved only if the price of sacituzumab govitecan ($2/mg) were reduced by more than 73% and pembrolizumab ($44/mg) by more than 10%. Achieving such reductions would require coordinated negotiations among payers, healthcare institutions, and pharmaceutical manufacturers. Furthermore, current single-vial dispensing practices may result in drug wastage. In real-world settings, strategies such as optimized weight-based dosing or shared vial utilization across multiple patients could potentially reduce excess costs.

Given the heterogeneity of TNBC, subgroup analyses were conducted to explore whether certain patient populations might derive greater economic benefit. Across all predefined subgroups, results were directionally consistent with the base-case findings. Notably, cost-effectiveness was inversely correlated with the hazard ratio (HR) for PFS: subgroups with lower HRs versus pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy demonstrated relatively more favorable economic outcomes, whereas those with higher HRs showed less favorable results. This pattern aligns with prior subgroup evaluations [28]. From a strictly clinical perspective, patients previously treated with PD-1-or PD-L1-targeted immunotherapy, those presenting with metastatic disease at diagnosis, and older individuals may experience greater survival gains. Subgroup stratification may therefore assist clinicians in identifying patients most likely to benefit when clinical efficacy is prioritized over cost considerations. Biomarker refinement remains critical for improving therapeutic precision. PD-L1 expression is an established predictor of response to ICIs across multiple tumor types and warrants further stratified investigation in TNBC [29]. In addition, the normalized membrane ratio of Trop-2 (Trop-2 NMR) has shown potential as a predictive biomarker for Trop-2-targeted ADC therapy [30, 31]. Expanding and integrating biomarker-driven selection strategies may help identify those wit hthe greatest chance of benefitting from ADC plus ICI combinations, thereby enhancing clinical value and potentially improving economic efficiency.

This study offers important strengths. First, it is based on the most recent phase III clinical evidence and represents the first comprehensive economic evaluation comparing sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in advanced TNBC. As such, it addresses an important economic evidence gap in randomized trial data. Second, a detailed exploration of influential cost drivers provides actionable insights relevant to pricing strategy and clinical implementation. Third, subgroup-specific analyses offer practical guidance for personalized treatment decision-making.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The model relies on clinical trial data, and overall survival outcomes remain immature, requiring longer follow-up. Although parametric modeling techniques were used to extrapolate long-term survival, these projections may not fully reflect real-world outcomes. In addition, some model parameters were derived from previously published economic evaluations, which could introduce bias. However, extensive sensitivity analyses confirmed model stability within plausible ranges. Finally, subgroup analyses were based on PFS hazard ratios rather than subgroup-specific survival curves, which limits the ability to capture the full extent of clinical heterogeneity.

Conclusion

This economic evaluation indicates that, at the WTP threshold of $150,000/QALY in the USA, first-line sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab is not a cost-effective alternative to pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy for individuals with advanced TNBC under current pricing conditions. Nevertheless, in scenarios where therapeutic options are limited and clinical benefit is prioritized, the combination regimen may serve as a valuable alternative for selected patients. These findings provide important evidence to inform clinical decisions and reimbursement policy formulation.

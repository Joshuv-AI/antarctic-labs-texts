Magnetic field-free braiding and nontrivial fusion of Majorana bound states in high-temperature planar Josephson junctions
Pankaj Sharma, and Narayan Mohanta Department of Physics, Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee, Roorkee 247667, India

Abstract
Demonstration of non-Abelian statistics of zero-energy Majorana bound states (MBS) is crucial for long-sought-after decoherence-free topological quantum computing. The ability to move the MBS on a two-dimensional platform such as a planar Josephson junction is practically constrained by a fixed direction of applied magnetic field. In addition, the detrimental effects of the magnetic field on proximity-induced superconductivity in semiconductor-superconductor heterostructures is an outstanding problem for the realization of topological superconductivity. Here we show that these problems can be solved in a planar Josephson junction coupled to a skyrmion crystal, which generates the MBS without the need of any external magnetic field, phase biasing, and Rashba spin-orbit coupling. Using a high-temperature superconductor having d d -wave pairing symmetry, we confirm that our planar junction can support the MBS at high temperatures. We propose protocols for performing non-trivial fusion, exchange and non-Abelian braiding of multiple MBS in our field -free platforms. The proposed geometries and MBS movement protocols open a path towards successful experimental detection of the MBS via confirmation of their non-Abelian statistics.

Although there is no phase biasing requirement to realize topological superconductivity in our skyrmion-based PJJ platform, the phase difference between the superconducting leads can be used as an additional tunable knob to control the existence of the MBS. In addition, the skyrmion size can provide another parameter that can control the existence of the MBS
sup
. Hence, the proposed platform has many advantages over 1D hybrid nanowires and the conventional PJJ with external in-plane magnetic field.

To conclude, this work establishes the suitability of engineering robust topological superconductivity by leveraging a skyrmion crystal that generates a synthetic Rashba-type spin-orbit coupling and also eliminates the need for the harmful magnetic fields. Having verified that both
s
-wave and
d
-wave pairing symmetries can generate the desired topological superconductivity in the 2DEG by proximity effect to the skyrmion crystal, both conventional BCS and high-
T_{c}
superconductors can be used reliably, even at elevated operating temperatures. We have shown that using the PJJ with a skyrmion-crystal, any device architectures can be realized with ease of fabrication, permitting more complex logical operations needed for topological quantum computing.

Structure theory of set addition with two operations
Semchankau A.S. and Shkredov I.D

Abstract




Annotation.

We take the first step toward a structure theory that includes both operations of a ring
\mathcal{R}
. More precisely, we prove a series of inverse results for the structure of sets
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that, under certain conditions on integers
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}
, one has

|A^{r_{1}}+\dots+A^{r_{k}}|\ll\sqrt[k]{p^{k-1}|A|}
.

1.
Introduction

Given an abelian group
{\mathbf{G}}
and two sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
, define
the
sumset
of
A
and
B
as

(1)

A+B:=\{a+b~:~a\in{A},b\in{B}\}.

In a similar way we define the
difference sets
and
higher sumsets
, e.g.,
A-A
,
A+A-A
, and so on.
The
doubling constant
of a finite set
A
is

(2)

\mathcal{D}[A]:=\frac{|A+A|}{|A|},

an important additive–combinatorial characteristic of
A
.

The study of the structure of sumsets is a fundamental problem in classical additive combinatorics
[
17
]
, and this relatively new branch of mathematics provides us with a number of excellent results.
For example, Freiman’s theory on the structure of the family of sets having small doubling — i.e., sets for which the ratio (
2
) is “bounded”
\mathcal{D}[A]\leqslant K
, where
K
is a fixed or slowly growing parameter — seems especially complete (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
,
[
17
, Section 5]
and recent results in
[
5
]
).

Further, given a ring
\mathcal{R}
and two sets
A,B\subseteq\mathcal{R}
, one can similarly define the
product set

AB
and higher products/sumsets, involving both operations of the ring such as
AA+A
or
A(A+A)
.
The interaction of these additive and multiplicative structures on the ring
\mathcal{R}
is the subject of arithmetic combinatorics, or, in other words, the
sum–product phenomenon
.
This area typically studies the behavior of

\max\{|A+A|,|AA|\}

under various assumptions on
A
, see
[
17
]
.
More generally, given two polynomials
f,g\in\mathcal{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

one can consider the sets

(3)

\left\{\frac{f(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})}{g(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})}~:~a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in A\right\}.

In this work we take a first step towards a structure theory that simultaneously involves
both
operations
+
and
\times
, which is the novelty of the paper.

1.1.
Main conjecture

In this paper we limit ourselves to considering the field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Let
p
be a prime number and let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Denote by

A^{*}:=A^{-1}=\{1/a\bmod p:a\in A,a\neq 0\}

its inverse set. In
[
15
]
, we proved the lower bound

(4)

|A+A^{*}|\geqslant(1-o(1))\min\{2\sqrt{p|A|},p\},

valid whenever
|A|\gg p(\log p)^{-1/2+o(1)}
.
The archetypal
question of the present work is the corresponding
inverse problem
for a simple rational function (
3
):
given that

(5)

|A+A^{*}|\leqslant K\sqrt{p|A|},

for a fixed parameter
K\geqslant 2
, what structural information can we deduce about
A
?

To construct examples of sets
A
such that the cardinality of
A+A^{*}
obeys (
5
)
consider well–structured sets

P,Q\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
P
and
Q^{*}
behave “independently”:

|P+Q|\leqslant K\sqrt{|P||Q|},\qquad|P\cap Q^{*}|\approx\frac{1}{p}|P||Q|.

This situation arises when, for instance,
P
and
Q
are sufficiently large arithmetic progressions:

|P\cap Q^{*}|=\frac{1}{p}|P||Q|+O\left(\sqrt{p}(\log p)^{2}\right)=\frac{1+o(1)}{p}|P||Q|,\qquad\text{provided that }|P|,|Q|\gg p^{3/4}\log p.

Now set
A:=P\cap Q^{*}
. Then
A+A^{*}\subseteq P+Q
, and hence

|A+A^{*}|\leqslant K\sqrt{|P||Q|}\approx K\sqrt{p|A|}.

Finally, if we add a constant number
C
of arbitrary elements to
A
to form
A_{1}
, then

|A_{1}+A_{1}^{*}|\leqslant|A+A^{*}|+2C|A|+C^{2}\sim(K+2C\sqrt{\alpha}+o(1))\sqrt{p|A|},

hence the modified constant
K_{1}:=|A_{1}+A_{1}^{*}|/\sqrt{p|A_{1}|}
remains close to
K
provided
C\ll K/\sqrt{\alpha}
, where
\alpha=|A|/p
.

This heuristic suggests the following:

Conjecture 1
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a set of size at least
p^{1-c}
for some absolute constant
c>0
, and suppose that

|A+A^{*}|\leqslant K\sqrt{p|A|}.

Then there exist sets
P,Q\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that, after removing
O_{K,\alpha}(1)
elements from
A
, the following holds:

•

A\subseteq P\cap Q^{*}
;

•

|P\cap Q^{*}|=\tfrac{1+o(1)}{p}|P||Q|
;

•

|P+Q|\leqslant(1+o(1))K\sqrt{p|A|}
.

The first two conditions imply

p|A|\leqslant(1+o(1))|P||Q|.

Combined with the third condition, this gives

|P+Q|\leqslant(1+o(1))K\sqrt{|P||Q|},

and sets
P
and
Q
can be further characterized using Freiman’s Theorem
[
4
]
.

1.2.
Main results

We verify several variants of this conjecture for dense sets
A
.
The methods we develop for this conjecture turn out to be applicable in a more general setting, where the exponent configuration
(1,-1)
in (
5
) is replaced by a tuple
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
satisfying certain arithmetic conditions. Some definitions are therefore to be introduced.

Let
r
be an exponent (non–zero integer), and
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Define
A^{r}:=\{a^{r}:a\in A\}
(note that this notation is not standard, and should not be confused with the
r
-fold product set or the set of
r
-tuples). Since we work in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, an exponent
r
might be treated as an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{p-1}
.

Definition 1
.

Exponent
r
is called
generic
if
(r,p-1)=O(1)
, and
coprime
if
(r,p-1)=1
. More generally, a tuple
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
of distinct exponents satisfying
(r_{i}-r_{j},p-1)<p^{1-\delta}
for some
\delta>0
is
generic
or
coprime
if all individual
r_{i}
’s are generic or coprime, respectively.

Define
\sqrt[r]{A}:=\{x:x^{r}\in A\}
. We will work only with generic
r
, implying that always
|\sqrt[r]{A}|\leqslant(r,p-1)|A|\ll|A|
.

The following result generalizes the lower bound (
4
).

Theorem 1

(Lower Bound)
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a set with
|A|\gg p
, and let
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
be a generic tuple. Then

\big|A^{r_{1}}+\cdots+A^{r_{k}}\big|\geqslant(1-o(1))\min\big(kp^{\frac{k-1}{k}}|A|^{1/k},p\big).

The following theorem confirms the conjecture for almost all, or ‘99%’ elements of
A
, that is, after removing only
o(p)
of them.

Theorem 2

(
99\%
Characterization of
A
)
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a set with
|A|\gg p
, and let
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
be a generic tuple. If

|A^{r_{1}}+\cdots+A^{r_{k}}|\leqslant Kp^{\frac{k-1}{k}}|A|^{1/k},

then there exist sets
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that,
after removing
o(p)
elements from
A
, the following holds:

•

A\subseteq\sqrt[r_{1}]{P_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{P_{k}}
;

•

\big|\sqrt[r_{1}]{P_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{P_{k}}\big|=\frac{1+o(1)}{p^{k-1}}|P_{1}|\cdots|P_{k}|
;

•

|P_{1}+\cdots+P_{k}|\leqslant(1+o(1))Kp^{\frac{k-1}{k}}|A|^{1/k}
.

In fact, the sets
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
obtained in Theorem
2
(and in Theorem
3
below) are quite specific and possess a good additive structure. More precisely, they are well approximated by sets of small Wiener norm; see the proofs and Section
3
.

We can also obtain a structural characterization for the
entire
set
A
,
after removing only
O(p/|A|)
elements, although with a suboptimal dependence of
f(K)
on
K
, see Theorem
3
below. We also have to make additional assumptions on the exponents.

Definition 2
.

A generic tuple
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
is called
bounded

if there exists a scaling parameter
L=O(1)
, coprime to
p-1
,
such that all residues
r_{i}L\bmod(p-1)
have order
O(1)
.
In other words, there is an absolute constant
C>0
for which

|r_{i}L\bmod(p-1)|\leqslant C,\qquad i=1,\ldots,k,

where residues modulo
p-1
are identified with the interval

[-\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{p-1}{2}]
.

Theorem 3

(
100\%
Characterization of
A
)
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a set of size
|A|=\alpha p
with
\alpha\gg 1
,
and let tuple
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
be coprime and bounded such that

|A^{r_{1}}+\cdots+A^{r_{k}}|\leqslant Kp^{\frac{k-1}{k}}|A|^{1/k}.

Then there exist sets
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that,
after removing
O(1/\alpha)
elements from
A
, the following holds:

•

A\subseteq\sqrt[r_{1}]{P_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{P_{k}}
;

•

\big|\sqrt[r_{1}]{P_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{P_{k}}\big|=\frac{1+o(1)}{p^{k-1}}|P_{1}|\cdots|P_{k}|
;

•

|P_{1}+\cdots+P_{k}|\leqslant f(K)p^{\frac{k-1}{k}}|A|^{1/k}
,

where
f(K)\ll K^{O(1)}
.

Although both the density
\alpha
and the parameter
K
are assumed to be ‘constants’, they have different scales — one may think the case
K=10
and
\alpha=10^{-100}
.

The main ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1
is the observation that algebraic transformations
\sqrt[r]{W}
of sets with small Wiener norm intersect in a manner similar to
independent
sets.
The main ingredient in Theorem
2
is Green’s Arithmetic Regularity Lemma.
Finally, the main ingredient in the proof of Theorem
3
is that, under additional assumptions, the intersections

W\cap\sqrt[r_{1}]{W_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{W_{k}}

behave similarly to
pseudorandom
subsets of
W
.

Organization of the paper

•

Section
2
recalls the basic properties of the Fourier transform, the Wiener norm, exponential sums, sumsets, and proves various auxiliary propositions.

•

Section
3
develops combinatorial tools based on the Wiener norm. We introduce the
algebraic intersection property
and establish it for sets, approximated by sets of small Wiener norm: their algebraic transformations intersect as independent sets.
We also recall the concept of
wrappers
from
[
15
]
,
which serve as a source of such sets and help to extend the algebraic intersection property to sets of popular differences.

•

Sections
4
and
5
contain the proofs of Theorems
1
and
2
, respectively. These proofs are relatively short and use only a subset of tools introduced earlier.

•

Section
6
defines an additive distance
\rho(f,g)
between functions and shows that intersection of algebraic transformations of sets with algebraic intersection property not only has ‘expected’ cardinality, but also exhibits strong pseudorandom properties.

•

Section
7
studies sumsets of the form
Y+T
, where
T
is a dense subset of intersections such as
P\cap Q^{*}
.
We first show that
|Y+T|\gg|P|
, and then derive a Ruzsa-type structural description of sets
Y
for which
|Y+T|\ll|P|
. The first result heavily relies on the previous section.

•

Section
8
concludes with the proof of the

100\%
Characterization Theorem

3
.
This argument builds on the
99\%
case and relies on the results of the preceding section.

•

Appendix
9
includes some further facts on the
additive distance introduced in Subsection
6.1
and other metrics.

2.
Definitions and Preliminary Results

2.1.
Notation

It is always assumed, unless stated otherwise, that all sets are subsets of the abelian group
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the field of residues modulo a (very) large prime
p
, with the usual addition and multiplication.
We write
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
for
\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
and
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
for the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Throughout the work,
o(1)
means hidden dependency on
p
. Both
o(1)
and
O(1)
may also have hidden dependence on the parameter
k
(the number of sets in the sumset under consideration) and, depending on the context, other constants. To highlight the dependency, those constants can be added as indices, e.g.
O_{X}(1)
.
By definition, the terms
o(\ldots)
and
O(\ldots)
may be negative or positive. In case we want to highlight that the quantity of interest is actually
negative
, we may write
-o(\ldots)
or
-O(\ldots)
.

The signs
\ll
and
\gg
are the usual Vinogradov symbols. In case we want to highlight the dependence of underlying constant
c
on a parameter
X
, we may write
\ll_{X}
and
\gg_{X}
. We write
f\asymp g
if
f\gg g\gg f
.
There might be several scenarios for using these signs:

•

“If
|A|\gg p
, then
\ldots
” might be restated as “for any constant
c>0
, if
|A|\geqslant cp
, and
p
is sufficiently large, meaning
p>p(c)
, then
\ldots
”

•

“If condition
\mathbf{X}
holds, then
A\gg B
” might be restated as “there exists an absolute constant
c>0
(depending on a situation, either sufficiently small or sufficiently large), such that condition
\mathbf{X}
implies
A\geqslant cB
”.

The particular scenario should always be clear from the context.

All logarithms are to base
e
. Denote
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix/p}
.
The complement of the set
A
is denoted by
A^{c}
.
The symbol
A\sqcup B
denotes the disjoint union of sets
A
and
B
.

2.2.
Fourier Transform

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a finite abelian group and
\widehat{{\mathbf{G}}}
its dual group. By definition,
\widehat{{\mathbf{G}}}
consists of
additive characters
, i.e. functions
\xi:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\xi(a+b)=\xi(a)\xi(b)
,
\forall a,b\in{\mathbf{G}}
.
In the context of this work,
{\mathbf{G}}
is always
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and, conveniently,
\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}
. All additive characters take the form
\xi_{a}(x)=e(ax)
for an arbitrary
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
(with
a=0
corresponding to the
trivial
additive character
\xi_{0}(x)\equiv 1
), and therefore one might identify
\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
by
a\leftrightarrow\xi_{a}(x)
.

Having any function
f:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\xi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
define the
Fourier transform
of
f
at
\xi
by the formula

(6)

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}f(x)\overline{\xi(x)}.

Because of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\cong\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
we can, with abuse of notation, write
\widehat{f}(a):=\widehat{f}(\xi_{a})
.
The
Parseval identity
is

(7)

p\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}|f(x)|^{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\big|\widehat{f}(\xi)\big|^{2},

and the identity

(8)

f(x)=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\xi(x).

is called the
inverse
formula.
Given two functions
f,g:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
, we define two operators:
convolution
and
correlation
:

(f*g)(x):=\sum_{a+b=x}f(a)g(b)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad(f\circ g)(x):=\sum_{b-a=x}\overline{f(a)}g(b)\,.

Fourier transform of the convolution and correlation might be written as follows:

(9)

\widehat{f*g}=\widehat{f}\widehat{g}\quad\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\widehat{f\circ g}=\overline{\widehat{f}}\,\widehat{g}\,.

We can define Fourier transform of a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
via identifying
set
A
with its characteristic function
A:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\{0,1\}
.

2.3.
Wiener Norm

The Wiener norm of a function
f:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
is defined as

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\omega}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|.

One can verify the following inequality for any two functions
f,g:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
:

(10)

\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{\omega}\leqslant\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\omega}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\omega}

In this work, we will be interested in Wiener norms of
sets
— for a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\omega}
denotes the Wiener norm of its characteristic function. Given sets
P,Q
, the following properties of Wiener norm are well-known:

•

If
P
is nonempty, then
1\leqslant\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{\omega}\leqslant\sqrt{|P|}
,

•

If
P
is an arithmetic progression, then
\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{\omega}\ll\log{p}
,

•

\left\lVert P^{c}\right\rVert_{\omega}=\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{\omega}+O(1)
,

•

\left\lVert P\cap Q\right\rVert_{\omega}\leqslant\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{\omega}\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\omega}
,

•

If
P
and
Q
are disjoint, then
\left\lVert P\sqcup Q\right\rVert_{\omega}\leqslant\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{\omega}+\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\omega}
.

2.4.
Bounds on exponential sums

At various points in the proof, we would like to verify that certain subsets of structured sets behave pseudorandomly in a strong quantitative sense. The following results will assist us with these.

First, we need the following variation of the classical Weil bound (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
10
]
).

Theorem (Weil).

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
, and let
\psi
be a nontrivial additive character on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
be a rational function.
Suppose that
f(x)
does not coincide with a function of the form
h(x)^{p}-h(x)+c
, where
h(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Then

(11)

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\mathcal{P}}\psi(f(x))\ll_{f}\sqrt{q},

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set of poles of
f
.

The second result is a famous theorem of Bombieri
[
1
, Theorem 6]
. We present it in a simplified form (the definition of a pole on a curve can be found in
[
1
, Chapter 6, page 93]
).

Theorem (Bombieri).

Let
p
be a prime number,
n\geqslant 2
be a positive integer,
\mathcal{C}\subset\overline{\mathbb{F}}^{n}_{p}
be an irreducible curve of degree
D
,
f\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be a rational function,
f=f_{1}/f_{2}
, where
f_{1},f_{2}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
have degrees
d_{1},d_{2}
, and
\psi
be a nontrivial additive character on
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Suppose that
f
is non–constant on
\mathcal{C}
. Then

(12)

\left|\sum_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{P}}\psi(f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))\right|\ll_{d_{1},d_{2},D}\sqrt{p},

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set of poles of
f
.

Since the classical curves below (
13
), (
14
) are absolutely irreducible, we obtain the following:

Corollary 1
.

Let
p
be a prime number and
g\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
be a polynomial of degree
s
and
\mathcal{C}
be the curve

(13)

\mathcal{C}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}~:~y^{t}=g(x)\}\,,

where
(s,t)=1

or

(14)

\mathcal{C}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}~:~ax^{s}+by^{t}=c\}\,,

where all
s,t,a,b,c
are non–zero.
Further let
\psi
be a nontrivial additive character on
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
suppose that
f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}(x,y)
be a rational function,
f=f_{1}/f_{2}
, where
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x,y]
have degrees
d_{1},d_{2}
such that
f
is non–constant on
\mathcal{C}
.
Then

(15)

\left|\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{P}}\psi(f(x,y))\right|\ll_{d_{1},d_{2},s,t}\sqrt{p}\,,

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set of poles of
f
.

Proof.

Any curve of the form (
13
) is absolutely irreducible; see, e.g.,
[
16
, Chapter I]
.
If we have a Fermat’s curve of the form (
14
), then for
G(x,y)=ax^{s}+by^{t}-c
it is easy to see that the system
0=G(x,y)=\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}(x,y)=\frac{\partial G}{\partial y}(x,y)
has no solution for
s,t\geqslant 1
. For negative
s,t
use the projectivization. Thus, our curve is non-singular and, therefore, absolutely irreducible.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Let the tuples
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}),(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
be generic and bounded.
Let

h(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x^{r_{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x-s)^{d_{j}},

where
s\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
is some fixed element.
Let
\psi
be a nontrivial additive character on
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Then, assuming
h
is not a constant,

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\psi(h(x))\ll\sqrt{p}.

Proof.

Let
L
be a scaling parameter, such that all
r_{i}^{\prime}:=r_{i}L,d_{j}^{\prime}:=d_{j}L
are small modulo
p-1
. Since it is coprime to
p-1
, the map
x\mapsto x^{L}
is a bijection of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Hence

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\psi(h(x))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y,z):\\
y-z=s\end{subarray}}\psi\left(\sum_{i}y^{r_{i}}+\sum_{j}z^{d_{j}}\right)=\\
=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,b):\\
a^{L}-b^{L}=s\end{subarray}}\psi\left(\sum_{i}a^{Lr_{i}}+\sum_{j}b^{Ld_{j}}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,b):\\
a^{L}-b^{L}=s\end{subarray}}\psi\left(\sum_{i}a^{r_{i}^{\prime}}+\sum_{j}b^{d_{j}^{\prime}}\right)

By Corollary
1
the curve
\mathcal{C}=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}~:~a^{L}-b^{L}=s\}
is absolutely irreducible,
L=O(1)
, and therefore by Bombieri bound (
12
),

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\psi(h(x))\ll\sqrt{p}.

∎

Another deep and useful result is due to Bourgain (
[
2
]
; see also
[
11
]
).

Theorem (Bourgain).

Let
p
be a prime,
k
a positive integer, and
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Then there exists

Sumsets

We use representation function notations like

or

and so on, which count the number of ways

can be expressed as a sum

or

|A|=r_{A-A}(0)

Given two sets

, the sumset of
A
and

is defined in (
1

Now if
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

for the set of

having at least
\varepsilon p
representations as a sum
a+b
,
a\in A
,
b\in B
.
Given positive integer

and set

we write

For arbitrary abelian group the Plünnecke–Ruzsa inequality (see, e.g.,
[

]

[

]
) holds, stating

|nA-mA|\leqslant\left(\frac{|A+A|}{|A|}\right)^{n+m}\cdot|A|,

where
n,m
are any positive integers.
Further, if
|A+B|\leqslant K|A|
for some sets

, then for any
n

(18)

Another useful set of inequalities is

|A||B-C|\leqslant|A-B||A-C|,\ \ \ |A||B+C|\leqslant|A+B||A+C|,

where
A,B,C
are arbitrary sets.

Now, let
K
be a parameter, and let

be any positive real values. We call them

\max_{i}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\ll K^{O(1)}\min_{i}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\,.

be a collection of sets. We say that they are

Proposition 1
.

Let

be sets of

2.6.
Set unions/intersections

When dealing with a union of sets, the following inequality is often useful:

\left|S_{1}\cup\ldots\cup S_{n}\right|\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}|S_{i}|-\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}|S_{i}\cap S_{j}|.

Under the additional hypothesis that all set sizes are at least
k
, and all set intersections are at most
l
, this results in the inequality
|S|\geqslant nk-\binom{n}{2}l
, which has the disadvantage that it does not improve with growth of
n
beyond some threshold value. The following standard proposition helps in this situation.

Proposition 2
.

Let
S_{1},S_{2},\dots,S_{n}
be finite sets, and let
k\geqslant l
be positive integers, such that the following holds:

•

|S_{i}|\geqslant k
for all
i
,

•

|S_{i}\cap S_{j}|\leqslant l
for all
i\neq j
.

Let
S:=S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup\dots\cup S_{n}
. Then

|S|\geqslant\frac{nk^{2}}{k+nl}.

Proof.

Let
m=\sum_{i\leqslant n}\sum_{s\in S}S_{i}(s)\geqslant nk
.
Observe that

m^{2}=\bigg(\sum_{s\in S}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}(s)\big)\bigg)^{2}\leqslant|S|\sum_{s\in S}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}(s)\big)^{2}=|S|\sum_{s\in S}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}S_{i}(s)S_{j}(s)=\\
=|S|\sum_{i,j\leqslant n}|S_{i}\cap S_{j}|\leqslant|S|\big(m+(n^{2}-n)l\big),

which implies

|S|\geqslant\frac{m^{2}}{m+(n^{2}-n)l}\geqslant\frac{(nk)^{2}}{nk+(n^{2}-n)l}\geqslant\frac{nk^{2}}{k+nl}.

∎

This has the following simple corollaries.

Corollary 3
.

If
n\gg k/l
, then
|S|\gg k^{2}/l
.

Corollary 4
.

If all sets
S_{i}
are supported in the ground set
G
and
l|G|<k^{2}
, then

n\leqslant\frac{|G|/k}{1-\frac{l|G|}{k^{2}}}.

We will often deal with intersections of sets.
These sets can be approximated by other sets that are more suitable in some respects.
The following facts will assist us in maintaining good bounds on intersections of those approximations; the proof is a routine exercise.

Proposition 3
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k},U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
be arbitrary sets. Then

•

\left|(T_{1}\cap\ldots\cap T_{k})\ \triangle\ (U_{1}\cap\ldots\cap U_{k})\right|\leqslant|T_{1}\ \triangle\ U_{1}|+\ldots+|T_{k}\ \triangle\ U_{k}|
,

•

\left|(T_{1}\cup\ldots\cup T_{k})\ \triangle\ (U_{1}\cup\ldots\cup U_{k})\right|\leqslant|T_{1}\ \triangle\ U_{1}|+\ldots+|T_{k}\ \triangle\ U_{k}|
,

The following proposition will be helpful when dealing with intersections of sets, having algebraic-independence property:

Proposition 4
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k},U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
be arbitrary sets, supported on ground set
G
, such that
\max_{i}|T_{i}\ \triangle\ U_{i}|\leqslant\xi|G|
. Then

\frac{1}{|G|^{k-1}}|T_{1}|\ldots|T_{k}|=\frac{1}{|G|^{k-1}}|U_{1}|\ldots|U_{k}|+O(\xi|G|).

Proof.

Define
t_{i}=|T_{i}|/|G|,u_{i}=|U_{i}|/|G|
. Clearly,
0\leqslant t_{i},u_{i}\leqslant 1
, and
|t_{i}-u_{i}|\leqslant\xi
. The statement now is equivalent to
t_{1}\ldots t_{k}=u_{1}\ldots u_{k}+O_{k}(\xi)
, which follows from

t_{1}\ldots t_{k}-u_{1}\ldots u_{k}=t_{2}\ldots t_{k}(t_{1}-u_{1})+u_{1}t_{3}\ldots t_{k}(t_{2}-u_{2})+\ldots+u_{1}\ldots u_{k-1}(t_{k}-u_{k}).

∎

3.
Combinatorial properties of the Wiener norm

In this section, we develop combinatorial tools for working with intersections of sets.
In many of our arguments, we will require the following
algebraic intersection property
(AIP), namely, the AIP takes place for some sets
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
if for generic exponents
r_{1},\ldots,r_{k}
one has

\bigl|\sqrt[r_{1}]{P_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{P_{k}}\bigr|=\frac{1+o(1)}{p^{k-1}}|P_{1}|\cdots|P_{k}|.

The AIP holds for a broad class of sets, which includes, in particular, sets with bounded Wiener norm.

Lemma 1
.

Let
W_{1},\ldots,W_{k}
be sets with Wiener norms bounded by
M
. Let tuple
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
be generic. Then

\bigl|\sqrt[r_{1}]{W_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{W_{k}}\bigr|=\frac{1}{p^{\,k-1}}\,|W_{1}|\cdots|W_{k}|+O\left(M^{k}p^{1-\varepsilon}\right),

where
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(k)>0
.

Proof.

Identifying sets
W_{i}
’s with their indicator functions, we write

(19)

\bigl|\sqrt[r_{1}]{W_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{W_{k}}\bigr|=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}W_{1}(x^{r_{1}})\cdots W_{k}(x^{r_{k}}).

Using the Fourier inversion formula we can write

W_{i}(x^{r}_{i})=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{u_{i}}\widehat{W}_{i}(u_{i})e(u_{i}x^{r_{i}})

for all
i=1,\ldots,k
. Plugging it in we arrive at

\bigl|\sqrt[r_{1}]{W_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{W_{k}}\bigr|=\frac{1}{p^{k}}\sum_{x}\sum_{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}}\widehat{W}_{1}(u_{1})\ldots\widehat{W}_{k}(u_{k})e(u_{1}x^{r_{1}}+\ldots+u_{k}x^{r_{k}})=\\
=\frac{1}{p^{k}}\sum_{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}}\widehat{W}_{1}(u_{1})\ldots\widehat{W}_{k}(u_{k})\sum_{x}e(u_{1}x^{r_{1}}+\ldots+u_{k}x^{r_{k}}).

The tuple
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})=(0,\ldots,0)
contributes the main term
\frac{1}{p^{k-1}}|W_{1}|\ldots|W_{k}|
.
By Bourgain’s bound (
16
), the last sum has order
O(p^{1-\varepsilon})
,
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(k)
if
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})\neq(0,\ldots,0)
. Thus,

\bigl|\text{error term}|\ll\frac{1}{p^{k}}\sum_{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}}|\widehat{W}_{1}(u_{1})|\ldots|\widehat{W}_{k}(u_{k})|\times p^{1-\varepsilon}\leqslant M^{k}p^{1-\varepsilon}.

∎

Remark 1
.

If tuple
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
is also bounded, then the error term is
O(\sqrt{p})
, which is guaranteed by Bombieri’s exponential bound.

Since finite intersections and complements of sets with bounded Wiener norm also have bounded Wiener norms, we can generate a lot of sets satisfying AIP. In practice, we would also like to have this property for sets, which can be
approximated
by sets with small Wiener norms.

Definition 3
.

We call a set
P

approximable
if
|P|\gg p
and there exists a set
W
such that

•

|W\ \triangle\ P|=o(p)
(small symmetric difference),

•

\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{\omega}=p^{o(1)}
(bounded Wiener norm).

We denote the family of such sets by
\mathcal{F}
.

Remark 2
.

This definition is rather informal, as it does not specify the dependencies quantitatively. A possible solution would be to define the
levels

\mathcal{F}(c,\varepsilon,M)
consisting of sets
P,|P|\geqslant cp
which might be approximated by sets
W
such that
|W\ \triangle\ P|\leqslant\varepsilon p
and
\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{\omega}\leqslant M
. This would be a well-defined notion, making the bounds in Proposition
6
below explicit. However, in the context of this work, we have no interest in the particular expression of those dependencies, and we intentionally keep this definition informal for the sake of being concise.

The family
\mathcal{F}
provides a much broader source of sets with AIP:

Proposition 5
.

Any collection of sets
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
from the family
\mathcal{F}
has AIP.

Proof.

Let
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
be an arbitrary generic tuple, and let
W_{1},\ldots,W_{k}
be sets approximating
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
. Then, for all
i
, the symmetric difference
\sqrt[r]{P_{i}}\triangle\sqrt[r]{W_{i}}
is a subset of
\sqrt[r]{P_{i}\triangle W_{i}}
, and therefore its size does not execeed
(r,p-1)|P_{i}\triangle W_{i}|=o(p)
. Thus,

\bigl|\sqrt[r_{1}]{P_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{P_{k}}\bigr|\overset{\raisebox{1.80835pt}{$\scriptstyle\text{Proposition~\ref{proposition:set-triangles}}$}}{=}\bigl|\sqrt[r_{1}]{W_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{W_{k}}\bigr|+o(p)=\\
\overset{\raisebox{1.80835pt}{$\scriptstyle\text{Lemma~\ref{lemma:aip}}$}}{=}\frac{1}{p^{k-1}}|W_{1}|\ldots|W_{k}|+o(p)\overset{\raisebox{1.80835pt}{$\scriptstyle\text{Proposition~\ref{proposition:prods-and-diffs}}$}}{=}\frac{1}{p^{k-1}}|P_{1}|\ldots|P_{k}|+o(p)=\\
\overset{\raisebox{1.80835pt}{$\scriptstyle|P_{i}|\gg p$}}{=}\frac{1+o(1)}{p^{k-1}}|P_{1}|\ldots|P_{k}|.

∎

There is a certain liberty one can take when working with this family:

Proposition 6
.

The family
\mathcal{F}
has the following properties:

(1)

If
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\in\mathcal{F}
, then
P_{1}\cup\ldots\cup P_{k}\in\mathcal{F}
,

(2)

If
P\in\mathcal{F}
and
|X|\ll 1
, then
X+P\in\mathcal{F}
,

(3)

Let
A,|A|=\alpha p
be an arbitrary dense set, and let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
There exists
D\in\mathcal{F}
such that

A-_{\varepsilon\alpha^{2}}A\subseteq D\subseteq A-A.

Again, these inclusions are stated rather informally; see Remark
2
above.

The proof of Proposition
6
is given below, and for the proof of the last property (3), we will need to recall the results of our previous work.

Namely, following
[
15
]
, we introduce special subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
called
wrappers
, each associated with two parameters:
granularity

\varepsilon>0
and
dimension

d\geqslant 1
.
A wrapper
W
with granularity
\varepsilon
and dimension

satisfies

\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{\omega}\leqslant\left(\frac{C\log p}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d},

where
C>0
is an absolute constant.

The following result, taken from
[
15
]
, shows that level sets of convolutions can be efficiently approximated by wrappers.

Supplementary Lemma.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
and
\delta,\xi>0
.
Let
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
|A|\geqslant\alpha p
and
|B|\geqslant\beta p
, and let
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Define

X:=\Bigl\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:\tfrac{1}{p}(A*B)(x)\in[\eta_{1},\eta_{2}]\Bigr\},\qquad X^{+}:=\Bigl\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:\tfrac{1}{p}(A*B)(x)\in[\eta_{1}-\delta,\,\eta_{2}+\delta]\Bigr\}.

Then there exist

•

a wrapper
W
of granularity
\varepsilon
and dimension
d
where

\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta,\delta)\gg\min\Bigl(\tfrac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha\beta}},\,1\Bigr),\ \ d=d(\alpha,\beta,\delta,\xi)\ll\max\Bigl(\tfrac{\alpha\beta}{\delta^{2}},\,1\Bigr)\log\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\xi}\Bigr),

•

a set
Y\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
|Y|\leqslant\xi p
,

such that

X\setminus Y\subseteq W,\qquad W\setminus Y\subseteq X^{+}.

Now we are ready to prove Proposition
6
:

Proof.

We will prove the properties one by one.

(1)

Let
W_{1},\ldots,W_{k}
be the corresponding approximations. Let
P:=P_{1}\cup\ldots\cup P_{k}
and
W:=W_{1}\cup\ldots\cup W_{k}
. We claim that
W
approximates
P
. Indeed,

|P\ \triangle\ W|\overset{\raisebox{1.80835pt}{$\scriptstyle\text{Proposition~\ref{proposition:set-triangles}}$}}{\leqslant}\sum_{i}|P_{i}\ \triangle\ W_{i}|=o(p),

and

W\overset{\raisebox{1.80835pt}{$\scriptstyle\text{Venn Diagram}$}}{=}\bigsqcup_{\{1,\ldots,k\}=I\sqcup J}\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}W_{i}\setminus\bigcap_{j\in J}W_{j}\right),

implying that
\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{\omega}\leqslant(Cp^{o(1)})^{k}=p^{o(1)}
for some absolute
C>0
, since intersections, complements, and disjoint unions of sets with a bounded Wiener norm also have a bounded Wiener norm.

(2)

Let
W
be the set approximating
P
.
Here we claim that
X+P
is approximated by
X+W
.
Application of the second part of Proposition
3
with

T_{x}:=x+P,\qquad U_{x}:=x+W,\qquad x\in X,

yields

|(X+P)\ \triangle\ (X+W)|\leqslant|X||P\ \triangle\ W|=o(p).

Similarly to the previous point, we can write

X+W=\bigsqcup_{X=Y\sqcup Z}\left(\bigcap_{y\in Y}(y+W)\setminus\bigcap_{z\in Z}(z+W)\right),

and conclude that
X+W
also has a bounded Wiener norm.

(3)

Denote the set
\{x:(A\circ A)(x)\in[2\varepsilon\alpha^{2}p,p]\}
by
D
, so that

A-_{\varepsilon\alpha^{2}}A\subseteq D\subseteq A-A.

Let us choose a reasonably small parameter
\xi\in(0,1)
. Application of the Supplementary Lemma to the correlation
A\circ A
with parameters

\eta_{1}=\varepsilon\alpha^{2},\qquad\delta=\varepsilon\alpha^{2}/2,\qquad\eta_{2}=1,\qquad\varepsilon=\varepsilon,\qquad\xi=\xi

gives a wrapper
W
such that

\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{\omega}\leqslant(c\log p)^{d},\qquad|D\ \triangle\ W|\leqslant\xi p,

where

c=c(\alpha,\varepsilon)\ll\frac{1}{\varepsilon\alpha},\qquad d=d(\alpha,\varepsilon,\xi)\ll\frac{\log(1/\xi)}{(\varepsilon\alpha)^{2}}\,.

By choosing

to be sufficiently small (but not too small) we can guarantee that

and

, so
W
approximates
D
.

∎

k\geqslant 3

Let
B

A^{r_{1}}+\cdots+A^{r_{k}}

If

W_{1},\ldots,W_{k},W_{B}

6.1.

Definition 4

Let

We write

\rho^{2}(f,g)=\|f\circ f-g\circ g\|_{\infty}\quad\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\quad\rho^{2}_{*}(f,g)=\max_{x\neq 0}|(f\circ f)(x)-(g\circ g)(x)|\,.

We put
\rho(f)

\rho(f,0)=\|f\circ f\|^{1/2}_{\infty}

\rho_{*}(f)

Let us make
some simple remarks concerning the definition above and compare
\rho(f,g)

Other relations are discussed in the appendix.
First of all, it is easy to see that
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
,
\rho_{*}(\cdot,\cdot)
are pseudometrics (for example, one has
\rho(f,g)=\rho(f+c,g+c)
for any
c
and arbitrary functions
f,g
with zero mean).
Let us remark that
\|f\|_{2}\leqslant\rho(f)
.
Also, if
\delta:=N^{-1}\sum_{x}f(x)
, then

\rho(f,\delta)=\rho(f-\delta)
.
Finally, there is a simple relation between
the quantity
\rho(f)

and Fourier transform.
Indeed,
using
(
6
)
one has

(21)

|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}=|\sum_{x}(f\circ f)(x)\overline{\xi(x)}|\leqslant\rho^{2}(f)N

and hence
\|\widehat{f}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\rho(f)\sqrt{N}
.

6.2.
The family
\mathcal{W}

In this subsection, we study the family of sets
\mathcal{W}
(not to be confused with the family in Proposition
1
), which will play a central role in the next section. In general terms, the elements of this family can be viewed as intersections of polynomial images of arithmetic progressions.

Definition 5
.

Let
p
be a prime number,
k
a positive integer, and
M\geqslant 1
a real number.
A function
f:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
belongs to the family
\mathcal{W}(k,M)
if it has the form

f(x)=f_{S_{0},\dots,S_{k},r_{1},\ldots,r_{k}}(x)=S_{0}(x)\prod_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}(x^{r_{i}}),

where the sets
S_{i}
satisfy
\left\lVert S_{i}\right\rVert_{\omega}\leqslant M
for all
i=0,1,\dots,k
, and the tuple
(1,r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
is coprime. Again, sets
S_{i}
’s are identified with their characteristic functions.
We write
S_{j}(f)=S_{j}
, further

\delta_{i}(f):=p^{-1}\sum_{x}S_{i}(x),\quad i=1,\ldots,k\,,

and set
\delta_{f}:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{i}(f)
.

Note that by the multiplicative property of the Wiener norm (
10
), we have

\mathcal{W}(n,M)\cdot\mathcal{W}(m,M)\subseteq\mathcal{W}(n+m,M^{2}).

We now state the main lemma regarding the family
\mathcal{W}(k,M)
.
It shows that any function
f\in\mathcal{W}(k,M)
is close, in the
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
-distance, to its
mean component

\delta_{f}\cdot S_{0}(f)
.

Lemma 2
.

Let

f=f_{S_{0},\ldots,S_{n},r_{1},\ldots,r_{k}}\in\mathcal{W}(k,M),

where the tuple
(1,r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
is coprime and bounded.
Then

(22)

\rho_{*}(f,\delta_{f}S_{0}(f))\ll M^{2k+2}\sqrt{p}.

Proof.

Since all
r_{i}
are coprime to
p-1
, we conclude that they are odd, and therefore at least
2
-separated. It is an easy exercise then, that for all
s\neq 0
the polynomials
1,x,x^{r_{1}},\ldots,x^{r_{k}},(x-s)^{r_{1}},\ldots,(x-s)^{r_{k}}
are linearly independent in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
.

We write

f(x)=\prod_{i=0}^{k}S_{i}(x^{r_{i}})

where
r_{0}=1
.
By the inverse formula (
8
),

S_{i}(x^{r_{i}})=p^{-1}\sum_{u_{i}}\widehat{S}_{i}(u_{i})e(u_{i}x^{r_{i}}),

hence

f(x)=p^{-(k+1)}\sum_{u_{0},\dots,u_{n}}\Bigl(\prod_{i=0}^{k}\widehat{S}_{i}(u_{i})\Bigr)e\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}u_{i}x^{r_{i}}\right).

Substituting these expressions into the formula of
(f\circ f)(s)
for any
s\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
gives

(f\circ f)(s)=\sum_{x-y=s}f(x)f(y)=\\
=p^{-(2k+2)}\sum_{x-y=s}\sum_{u_{0},\dots,u_{k}}\sum_{v_{0},\dots,v_{k}}\prod_{i=0}^{k}\widehat{S}_{i}(u_{i})\prod_{j=0}^{k}\widehat{S}_{j}(v_{j})\times e\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}u_{i}x^{r_{i}}+\sum_{j=0}^{k}v_{j}y^{d_{j}}\right)=\\
=p^{-(2k+2)}\sum_{u_{0},\dots,u_{k}}\sum_{v_{0},\dots,v_{k}}\prod_{i=0}^{k}\widehat{S}_{i}(u_{i})\prod_{j=0}^{k}\widehat{S}_{j}(v_{j})\times\sum_{x}e\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}u_{i}x^{r_{i}}+\sum_{j=0}^{k}v_{j}(x-s)^{r_{j}}\right).

The main term
\sigma_{*}(s)
arises when
u_{i}=v_{i}=0
for
i\geqslant 1
.
In this case, the inner sum over
x
becomes

e(-v_{0}s)\sum_{x}e\Bigl((u_{0}+v_{0})x\Bigr)

and it is nonzero only when
u_{0}=-v_{0}
.
Thus, using (
6
),

\sigma_{*}(s)=p^{-(2k+1)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(\sum_{z}S_{i}(z)\Bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Bigl(\sum_{w}S_{j}(w)\Bigr)\sum_{u}\widehat{S}_{0}(u)\widehat{S}_{0}(-u)e(us)=\\
=\delta_{f}^{2}\cdot(S_{0}\circ S_{0})(s)=\delta_{f}^{2}\cdot\bigl(S_{0}(f)\circ S_{0}(f)\bigr)(s).

Let
\mathcal{E}(s):=(f\circ f)(s)-\sigma_{*}(s)
denote the error term. Then, expanding as before, we obtain

\mathcal{E}(s)=p^{-(2k+2)}\sum_{u_{0},\dots,u_{k}}\sum_{v_{0},\dots,v_{k}}\prod_{i=0}^{k}\widehat{S}_{i}(u_{i})\prod_{j=0}^{k}\widehat{S}_{j}(v_{j})\times\sum_{x}e\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}u_{i}x^{r_{i}}+\sum_{j=0}^{k}v_{j}(x-s)^{r_{j}}\right).

where not all
u_{i},v_{j}
vanish for
i,j\geqslant 1
.
By Corollary
2
, each exponential sum has order
O(\sqrt{p})
. Hence

\mathcal{E}(s)\ll p^{-(2k+2)}\sum_{u_{0},\dots,u_{k}}\sum_{v_{0},\dots,v_{k}}\prod_{j=0}^{k}|\widehat{U}_{j}(u_{j})|\prod_{i=0}^{k}|\widehat{V}_{i}(v_{i})|\cdot\sqrt{p}\ll M^{2k+2}\sqrt{p}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3
.

This proof can be easily altered to approximate functions

f\circ g,\quad f*g,

by

\delta_{f}\delta_{g}\cdot S_{0}(f)\circ S_{0}(g),\quad\delta_{f}\delta_{g}\cdot S_{0}(f)*S_{0}(g),

respectively.

At the end of this subsection, consider another family of sets which can be approximated by its subsets in the sense of distance
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Remark 4
.

Consider the family
\mathcal{R}
of functions
A_{S}(x)=S(x)R(x)
, where
R
is the set of quadratic residues and
\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{\omega}\leqslant M
.
Using similar computations as in the proof of Lemma
2
and the Weil bound, one can show

\|A_{S}*A_{T}-\delta_{A_{S}}\delta_{A_{T}}\cdot S*T\|_{\infty}\ll M^{2}\sqrt{p}\,.

However, the family
\mathcal{R}
is not invariant under additive shifts, and a complete analogue of the Lemma
2
for the products (consider the set
A_{S}(x)A_{T}(x+1)
, say) requires an analogue of the Weil estimate over some surfaces.

7.
Sumsets with pseudorandom sets

To motivate the main results of this section, let us imagine that we work with a sumset
A+A^{*}
.

From the proof of the
99% Theorem
, we know that there exist sets
W_{1},W_{2}
with Wiener norms of order
p^{o(1)}
, and subsets

P\subseteq W_{1}
,
Q\subseteq W_{2},A_{0}\subseteq A
of relative densities
1-o(1)
such that

•

|P+Q|\leqslant(1+o(1))K\sqrt{p|A|}
;

•

|P\cap Q^{*}|=\frac{1+o(1)}{p}|P||Q|
;

•

A_{0}\subseteq P\cap Q^{*}
.

Let
\kappa_{1}=|P|/p
,
\kappa_{2}=|Q|/p
, and density of
A_{0}
in
P\cap Q^{*}
be
\eta
.
The last two bulletpoints imply

\alpha\leqslant(1+o(1))\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2},

and the first one gives

|P+Q|\leqslant(1+o(1))K\sqrt{\eta}\sqrt{|P||Q|}.

Since
|P+Q|\geqslant\max(|P|,|Q|)
, it follows that

\eta\gg 1/K^{2},\qquad\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\asymp_{K}\sqrt{\alpha}.

From these relations and the inequality
|A+A^{*}|\leqslant K\sqrt{p|A|}
we deduce

|A+A_{0}^{*}|\ll_{K}|P|.

Intuitively, the set
P\cap Q^{*}
should behave similarly to a random subset of
P
, and
A_{0}
should behave similarly to a random subset of some
P_{0}\subset P
of density
\eta
. Hence, one might expect the approximation

(23)

A^{*}+A_{0}\approx A^{*}+P_{0},

to hold. If (
23
) were true, then

|A^{*}+P_{0}|\approx|A^{*}+A_{0}|\ll_{K}|P|\ll_{K}|P_{0}|

would hold, and by the Ruzsa Covering Lemma one obtains

A^{*}\subseteq X_{P}+(P_{0}-P_{0}),\qquad|X_{P}|\ll_{K}1.

Applying the same argument to
A+A_{0}^{*}
yields

A\subseteq X_{Q}+(Q_{0}-Q_{0}),

and therefore

A\subseteq(X_{Q}+(Q_{0}-Q_{0}))\cap(X_{P}+(P_{0}-P_{0}))^{*},

an intersection of precisely the form we expect.

Our goal
now
is to
arrive to
a similar conclusion
without
assuming (
23
).
We will proceed as follows:

•

Although we cannot directly obtain the approximation
A^{*}+A_{0}\approx A^{*}+P_{0}
, we can deduce its consequence
|A^{*}+A_{0}|\gg_{K}|P|
.
This is based on pseudorandom properties of the set
P\cap Q^{*}\subset P
.

•

The main idea is to treat
P
as if it were an additive subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and to consider a “quotient” of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
modulo
P
and get almost-disjoint “cosets”.
Using the previous result, we show that

|A^{*}+A_{0}|\gg_{K}|P|\cdot\#\{\text{cosets}\},

which contradicts the upper bound
K^{O(1)}|P|
, if
A
has large intersections with many such cosets.
This part of the argument is purely combinatorial.

These ideas generalize to arbitrary bounded coprime exponent configurations at a small additional cost.
That is, we will prove the following lemma:

Lemma 3
.

Let
W,W_{1},\ldots,W_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be sets with Wiener norms
p^{o(1)}

and densities
\omega,\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}
, respectively.
Set
\omega_{\times}:=\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{k}
.
Let tuple
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
be coprime and bounded.

Let
P\subseteq W
and
P_{i}\subseteq W_{i}

(i=1,\ldots,k)
be subsets of relative density at least
1-\varepsilon
,
where
\varepsilon\ll\omega\omega_{\times}
.
Define

R:=P\cap\sqrt[r_{1}]{P_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{P_{k}},

and let
T\subseteq R
be a subset of relative density
\eta
.
Let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be arbitrary.

(a)

If
|Y|\gg\eta/\omega_{\times}
, then

|Y+T|\gg\eta^{2}|P|.

(b)

Assume in addition that
|P-P|\leqslant L|P|
.
If

|Y+T|\leqslant K|P|,

then there exist sets
X,E\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that

Y\subseteq\bigl(X+(P-_{\kappa^{2}/2}P)\bigr)\sqcup E,\qquad|X|\ll\frac{KL^{7}}{\eta^{2}},\qquad|E|\ll\frac{1}{\omega\omega_{\times}},

where
\kappa:=|P|/p
.

Remark 5
.

In applications,
\eta
has order
K^{-O(1)}
and is ‘small’, while
\omega_{\times}
has order
\alpha^{O(1)}
and is ‘very small’.

Remark 6
.

Although we will only apply this result for
dense
sets
P_{i},W_{i}
, it is worth noting that it works for sets with cardinalities as small as
\Omega(p^{1-c}),c>0
.

7.1.
Proof of Lemma

(a)

The main idea is that

.

2
. Define

.

Assume

Then for any
\lambda\neq 0

Remark 7
.

Here we really need that exponents

, which is evident from the following example with

W:=\{x:\omega_{1}p+10\leqslant x\leqslant(\omega_{1}+\omega)p+10\},\quad W_{1}:=\{x:0\leqslant x\leqslant\omega_{1}p\}.

It is clear from the definition that sets
W+W
and

are disjoint (provided

r_{W-W}(1)=|W|-1\approx\omega p
. On the other hand,
r_{I-I}(1)=0
, where
I:=W\cap\sqrt{W_{1}}
and this is far from
\omega_{1}^{2}r_{W-W}(1)
. Indeed, if there was
x\in I
such that the element
y=x+1
is also in
I
, we would obtain
y^{2}-x^{2}=(y-x)(y+x)=y+x\in W+W
. On the other hand,
y^{2}-x^{2}\in W_{1}-W_{1}
, which is a contradiction.

Proof.

Treat
I
as its indicator function. Since
I\in\mathcal{W}(k,M)
,
Lemma
2
gives

\bigl|r_{I-I}(\lambda)-\omega_{\times}^{2}r_{W-W}(\lambda)\bigr|\ll M^{2k+2}\sqrt{p},

as required.
∎

Proposition 8
.

For any
\lambda\neq 0
one has

r_{R-R}(\lambda)\leqslant\omega_{\times}^{2}r_{P-P}(\lambda)+O\!\left(\varepsilon\omega\omega_{\times}^{2}p\right).

Proof.

Since
R\subseteq I
, we have
r_{R-R}(\lambda)\leqslant r_{I-I}(\lambda)
.
By Proposition

r_{I-I}(\lambda)=\omega_{\times}^{2}r_{W-W}(\lambda)+O\!\left(M^{2k+2}\sqrt{p}\right).

Moreover, since
P\subseteq W
and
|W\setminus P|\leqslant\varepsilon|W|

r_{W-W}(\lambda)\leqslant r_{P-P}(\lambda)+2|W\setminus P|\leqslant r_{P-P}(\lambda)+2\varepsilon|W|=r_{P-P}(\lambda)+O(\varepsilon\omega p).

Combining these estimates and using

yields

r_{R-R}(\lambda)\leqslant\omega_{\times}^{2}r_{P-P}(\lambda)+O\!\left(\varepsilon\omega\omega_{\times}^{2}p\right),

as claimed.
∎

We also record the size of
R
(and hence of

).
On the one hand, by the algebraic intersection property,

|R|\leqslant|I|=\bigl|W\cap\sqrt[r_{1}]{W_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{W_{k}}\bigr|=(1+o(1))\omega\omega_{\times}p.

On the other hand, since
|W_{i}\setminus P_{i}|\leqslant\varepsilon|W_{i}|
for all

,

|R|\geqslant|I|-|W\setminus P|-|W_{1}\setminus P_{1}|-\ldots-|W_{k}\setminus P_{k}|=|I|-O(\varepsilon p),

so
|R|\sim\omega\omega_{\times}p\sim\omega_{\times}|P|
and
|T|\sim\eta|R|\sim\eta\omega_{\times}|P|
.

(a).

For distinct

7.2.
Proof of Lemma
3
(b)

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a set with small doubling, i.e.
|P-P|\leqslant K|P|
.
It is well known (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Section 2.4]
) that the entire group
\mathbb{F}_{p}

can be covered by a bounded number of translates of
P-P
via the following greedy algorithm:

(1)

Initialize
X:=\varnothing
.

(2)

At each step, if there exists
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that

x+P
is disjoint from all
x^{\prime}+P
with
x^{\prime}\in X
, add
x
to
X
.

(3)

When no such
x
exists, conclude that

\mathbb{F}_{p}=\bigcup_{x\in X}(x+P-P).

Clearly,

|X|\leqslant\frac{p}{|P|}\leqslant\frac{pK}{|P-P|}.

Unfortunately, even if
P
has small doubling, the difference set
P-P

does not necessarily possess the algebraic intersection property required in our arguments.
However, as shown in Section
3
, there exists a subset

D\subseteq P-P
that
does
have this property and, moreover,

A simple modification of the above algorithm then yields a covering by a bounded number

In this modified version, we allow the translates

Since the trivial bound

.

.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}

\kappa p
, and let

Then there exists a set

\mathbb{F}_{p}=\bigcup_{x\in X}\bigl(x+P-_{\varepsilon\kappa^{2}}P\bigr),\qquad|X|\leqslant\frac{1}{\kappa(1-\varepsilon)}.

Proof.

The modified algorithm proceeds as follows:

(1)

Initialize
X:=\varnothing
.

(2)

At each step, if there exists
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that

|(x+P)\cap(x^{\prime}+P)|\leqslant\varepsilon\kappa^{2}p\quad\text{for all }x^{\prime}\in X,

then add
x
to
X
.

(3)

When no such
x
exists, we claim that

\mathbb{F}_{p}=\bigcup_{x\in X}(x+P-_{\varepsilon\kappa^{2}}P).

To verify the claim, let
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
be arbitrary.
Since
x\notin X

\varepsilon\kappa^{2}p

have size

, and their pairwise intersections

\varepsilon\kappa^{2}p

As a byproduct, we obtain

|P-_{\varepsilon\kappa^{2}}P|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)|P|.

[

]
),

|P-_{\varepsilon\kappa^{2}}P|\gg|P|\qquad\text{for all }\varepsilon\in(0,1).

(b)
.

P
.

X

8.
Proof of the
100\%
Theorem

Let
A_{0},W_{i},P_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
be as in the proof of
Theorem
2
.
In particular, recall that

\frac{|A_{0}|}{|A|}\geqslant 1-o(1),\qquad\frac{|P_{i}|}{|W_{i}|}\geqslant 1-o(1),\qquad A_{0}^{r_{i}}\subseteq P_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,k.

Let
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}
denote the densities of

We recall that

(1+o(1))\alpha\leqslant\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{k}\leqslant\left(\frac{\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{k}}{k}\right)^{k}\leqslant(1+o(1))(K/k)^{k}\alpha.

In particular,
\omega_{i}\sim K^{O(1)}\alpha^{1/k}

.
Thus the densities
\omega_{i}
, and hence the cardinalities
|P_{i}|
, are

K
-comparable.

Moreover, by Proposition
1
,
each set
P_{i}
has doubling constant

L_{i}\ll K^{O(1)}.

The relative density
\eta
of
A_{0}
inside the algebraic intersection of
the sets
P_{i}
satisfies

\eta:=\frac{|A_{0}|}{|\sqrt[r_{1}]{P_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{k}]{P_{k}}|}\geqslant(1-o(1))\frac{k^{k}}{K^{k}}.

Let
A_{1}:=A\setminus A_{0}
.
Fix an index
i
, and choose an index
j=j(i)
with
j\neq i
.
From the bound

|A^{r_{1}}+\cdots+A^{r_{k}}|\leqslant K\alpha^{1/k}p

we deduce

|A_{1}^{r_{i}}+A_{0}^{r_{j}}|\leqslant K_{i}|P_{j}|,\qquad K_{i}:=\frac{K\alpha^{1/k}p}{|P_{j}|}\sim\frac{K\alpha^{1/k}}{\omega_{j}}\ll K^{O(1)}.

Since the tuple
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
is coprime and bounded,
so is the tuple

\bigl(r_{j}/r_{1},\ \ldots,\ 1,\ \ldots,\ r_{j}/r_{k}\bigr).

Because of this and the inclusion

A_{0}^{r_{j}}\subseteq P_{j}\cap\sqrt[r_{j}/r_{1}]{P_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt[r_{j}/r_{k}]{P_{k}}

we may apply Lemma
3
(b)
with the following choices:

Y:=A_{1}^{r_{i}},\quad T:=A_{0}^{r_{j}},\quad P:=P_{j},\quad\eta:=\eta,\quad L:=L_{j},\quad K:=K_{i}.

This yields a set
X_{i}
such that, after removing

O(1/(\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{k}))\ll O(1/\alpha)
elements from
A_{1}
, we have

A_{1}^{r_{i}}\subseteq X_{i}+\bigl(P_{j}-_{\kappa_{j}^{2}/2}P_{j}\bigr),\qquad|X_{i}|\ll\frac{K_{i}L_{j}^{7}}{\eta^{2}}\ll K^{O(1)}.

By Proposition
6
(3), there exists a set

D_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
such that

P_{j}-_{\kappa_{j}^{2}/2}P_{j}\subseteq D_{i}\subseteq P_{j}-P_{j}.

Moreover, by (
24
) we have
|D_{i}|\gg|P_{j}|
, so the
cardinalities of the sets
D_{i}
are
K
-comparable.
By Proposition
6
(2), the set

X_{i}+D_{i}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}
.
Recall also that
A_{0}^{r_{i}}\subseteq P_{i}
, and define

P_{i}^{\prime}:=(X_{i}+D_{i})\cup P_{i}.

Thus, after removing
O(1/\alpha)
elements from
A
, we obtain

A^{r_{i}}\subseteq P_{i}^{\prime},

and the sets
P_{i}^{\prime}
belong to family
\mathcal{F}
by
Proposition
6
(1), and therefore have algebraic intersection property, by Proposition
5
.

Finally, Proposition
1
implies that the
sets
P_{i}^{\prime}
are
K
-compatible, and their cardinalities are

K
-comparable to
\alpha^{1/k}p
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
3
by taking

P_{i}:=P_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
.

Appendix

In this section we discuss further connections between additive distance introduced in Subsection
6.1
and other metrics.

First of all, let us remark that
for any
s>1
the quantity
\rho(f)
controls the higher energies
[
14
]

(25)

\mathsf{E}_{s}(f):=\sum_{x}(f\circ f)^{s}(x)\leqslant\rho^{2s-2}(f)\|f\|^{2}_{1}\,,

and, similarly, if
B\subseteq A
,
then for the entropies of
A
and
B
(see
[
13
,
6
]
)

H(A):=\frac{1}{|A|^{2}}\sum_{x}(A\circ A)(x)\log\left(\frac{|A|^{2}}{(A\circ A)(x)}\right)=2\log|A|-\frac{1}{|A|^{2}}\sum_{x}(A\circ A)(x)\log(A\circ A)(x)\,,

we have

(26)

|H(A)-H(B)|\ll\frac{\sqrt{\rho(A,B)}|B-B|\log|B|}{|B|^{2}}

and thus the entropies of
A
and
B
are close in terms of
\rho(A,B)
.

Remark 8
.

Thanks to formula (
26
) we know that entropies of sets with small distance
\rho
are close.
What can be said about the sumsets?
It is easy to see that the smallness of the distance
\rho
does not
give us
too much.
Indeed, let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a random set,
|A|=\delta{\mathbf{G}}
, and

\|(A\circ A)(x)-\delta^{2}N\cdot\delta_{0}(x)\|_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon=C\delta^{2}N
and
C>1
is a large constant.
Then one can delete
O(\delta^{-1})
points from
A
such that we still have
\|(A\circ A)(x)-\delta^{2}N\cdot\delta_{0}(x)\|_{\infty}\ll\varepsilon
,
and such that
A-A
misses these
O(\sqrt{N}\varepsilon^{-1/2})
points.

Secondly, if
\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
, then
using formula (
21
), it is easy to see that
\rho(f)\leqslant\sqrt{N}
and hence the condition
\rho(f)=o(\sqrt{N})
guaranties that all Fourier coefficients of the function
f
can be estimated nontrivially.
Further, if the support of
f
is small, then
it is easy to construct a function with small quantity
\rho
but large Fourier coefficients, take, e.g.,
f(x)=A(x)-|A|/|H|\cdot H(x)
, where
A\subseteq H
is a random set and
H\leqslant\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, say.
Thus, estimate (
21
) is useful only for functions with large support.

In another direction, take any random set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|A|\gg N
and let
f=f_{A}(x)+c\delta_{0}(x)
,
c\sim\sqrt{|A|}
. Then the Fourier transform of
f
is small, namely,
\|\widehat{f}\|_{\infty}\ll|A|^{1/2+o(1)}
but
\rho^{2}(f)\gg c^{2}\gg|A|
is close to its maximal value.
Thus there are functions with small Fourier coefficients but large value
\rho(f)
.
Consider another example.

Example 1
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a perfect difference set (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
).
Then
\|A\circ A-E\|_{\infty}=0
, where
E=1-\delta_{0}(0)
but
\|A*A-E\|_{\infty}=1
.
Thus, it is important to take the convolution
\circ
in (
20
) instead of
*
.
Similarly, if
B=-A
, then
\|A\circ A-B\circ B\|_{\infty}=0
but
\rho^{2}(A-B)=\max_{x}(2-(A*A)(x)-(A*A)(-x))\geqslant 1
.
So, the functions
A\circ A
,
B\circ B
can be close in the sense of
L_{\infty}
, but at the same time quite far from each other in the sense of the pseudometric
\rho
.

Finally, as we have seen above (consult formula (
21
)) if
\rho(f)
is small, then
f
has small Fourier coefficients.
In the usual way
[
7
]
, one can say that a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
is
\varepsilon
–uniform in the sense of
\rho
if
\rho(f_{A})=\rho(f,\delta)\leqslant\varepsilon
.
It is easy to see that
\rho
–uniformity does not imply the higher–order
U^{k}
–uniformity, see
[
7
]
. Indeed, consider the Gowers example
[
7
]
, namely, let
A(x)=P(x^{2})
, where
P\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
is an arithmetic progression,
|P|\gg p
.
Then it is known (see
[
7
]
) that
A(x)
is not
U^{3}
–uniform.
On the other hand, using calculations of Lemma
2
, one can show that
\rho(f_{A})\ll p^{1/4+o(1)}
and hence
A
is

\rho
–uniform.
Nevertheless, Lemma
2
provides us examples of sets that are
U^{k}
–uniform for all
k
, e.g.,
the set
A(x):=P(x)P(x^{-1})
is
\rho
–uniform inside
P
and, moreover,
all
set
A_{s_{1},\dots,s_{l}}:=A\cap(A+s_{1})\cap\dots\cap(A+s_{l})
are
\rho
–uniform inside
P
.

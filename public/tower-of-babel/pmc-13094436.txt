Antibody–drug conjugate sequencing in HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer: Real-world outcomes of trastuzumab deruxtecan with and without prior T-DM1 exposure

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOptimal sequencing of trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) and trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) in HER2 positive metastatic breast cancer (mBC) remains uncertain, and real-world evidence on the impact of prior T-DM1 exposure on subsequent T-DXd outcomes is limited.MethodsWe conducted a multicenter, retrospective cohort study across 21 oncology centers in Türkiye including consecutive adults with HER2 positive mBC who received at least one cycle of T-DXd between 2020 and 2025. Patients were classified as T-DM1 pretreated or T-DM1 naive at T-DXd initiation. The primary endpoint was progression free survival (PFS); secondary endpoints included objective response rate (ORR), duration of response (DOR), and overall survival (OS). To address confounding by indication, we applied stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) based on prespecified clinical covariates and assessed covariate balance using standardized mean differences.ResultsAmong 218 patients treated with T-DXd, 137 (62.8%) had received prior T-DM1 and 81 (37.2%) were T-DM1 naive. The ORR was 71.9%, including 15.2% complete and 56.7% partial responses. Median DOR in the overall cohort was 20.96 months (95% CI, 15.71–26.22). In the IPTW-weighted analysis, prior T-DM1 exposure was associated with significantly shorter PFS: 12.1 months (95% CI, 10.3–20.5) versus 26.0 months (95% CI, 18.9 - not reached) in T-DM1 naive patients (IPTW-weighted log-rank p = 0.006). Prior T-DM1 remained independently associated with higher progression risk (HR 1.99, 95% CI 1.09 - 3.62; p = 0.02). Median OS was 18.9 months (95% CI, 17.2–not reached) in T-DM1 pretreated patients and not reached in T-DM1 naive patients; the IPTW-weighted OS hazard ratio did not reach statistical significance (HR 1.80, 95% CI 0.86–3.79; p = 0.12).ConclusionsTrastuzumab deruxtecan demonstrated substantial real-world activity after prior T-DM1 exposure, but PFS was significantly shorter compared with T-DM1 naive patients, even after rigorous adjustment for measured confounding. These findings highlight the clinical relevance of antibody drug conjugate sequencing and support prospective studies to define the optimal positioning of T-DXd in HER2 positive mBC treatment algorithms.

Introduction

Antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) have reshaped the treatment landscape of HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer (mBC) [1,2]. Building on the longstanding efficacy of HER2-directed antibodies and TKIs, newer ADCs deliver cytotoxic payloads directly to HER2-expressing cells, aiming to maximize antitumor activity while limiting off-target toxicity [3,4]. Trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) combines trastuzumab with the microtubule inhibitor DM1, whereas trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) links trastuzumab to a topoisomerase-I inhibitor payload (DXd) with a higher drug to antibody ratio and a membrane permeable bystander effect features that may confer activity in the setting of intra tumoral HER2 heterogeneity [5,6].

Despite the rapid uptake of T-DXd across lines of therapy, optimal sequencing after prior ADC exposure remains uncertain [[7], [8], [9]]. In clinical practice, a sizeable proportion of patients encounter T-DXd after T-DM1, raising biologically plausible concerns about cross-resistance, altered drug delivery, and selection of treatment-refractory clones [10]. Conversely, mechanistic differences between DM1 and DXd payloads and the bystander effect unique to T-DXd, could mitigate or even overcome resistance accrued under T-DM1 [11]. Real-world evidence directly comparing outcomes of T-DXd in T-DM1 pretreated versus T-DM1 naive populations is limited and heterogeneous, leaving clinicians without clear guidance.

We conducted a large, multicenter, retrospective cohort study across 21 oncology centers in Türkiye to evaluate whether prior T-DM1 exposure influences the effectiveness of T-DXd in HER2-positive mBC. The primary objective was to compare progression-free survival (PFS) between T-DM1 pretreated and T-DM1 naive patients receiving T-DXd in routine practice. We further prespecified multivariable adjustment and sensitivity analyses (including propensity-based methods and subgroup assessments) to address confounding by indication and center-level heterogeneity, aiming to provide robust, practice-informing evidence on ADC sequencing in advanced HER2-positive disease.

Methods

Study design and oversight

This multicenter, retrospective cohort study was conducted across 21 medical oncology centers in Türkiye. The coordinating center prespecified the analytic plan, harmonized variable definitions, and performed centralized data cleaning prior to database lock. The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki (2013) and received approval from the coordinating center's Institutional Review Board (primary approval number provided) and local ethics committees where required. Given the retrospective design and the use of de-identified data, informed consent was waived at all participating sites.

Patient population

Consecutive adult patients (≥18 years) with histologically confirmed HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer (IHC 3+ or IHC 2+/ISH+) who received at least one cycle of trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) between 2020 and 2025 in routine practice were included. Eligible patients were required to have a documented T-DXd start date, available progression or last follow-up information, and baseline clinicopathologic data. Exclusion criteria included: (i) unverified HER2 status; (ii) progression or death prior to T-DXd initiation; (iii) active second primary malignancy; or (iv) irreconcilable data inconsistencies following query resolution.

Exposure definition

The primary exposure was prior T-DM1 use (yes/no), defined as receipt of ≥1 cycle of trastuzumab emtansine before T-DXd. Patients were classified a priori as T-DM1–pretreated or T-DM1–naive at the time of T-DXd initiation.

Data collection and variable harmonization

Data were abstracted from electronic medical records using a standardized case-report form specifying operational definitions and acceptable value ranges. The coordinating center performed format checks, range checks, and logic checks to ensure chronological consistency between diagnosis, systemic therapies, and outcomes. Variables collected included demographics (age, menopausal status, comorbidities), tumor characteristics (hormone-receptor status, grade, visceral involvement, number and sites of metastases, de novo vs recurrent disease), HER2 testing method, prior systemic therapies (trastuzumab, pertuzumab, T-DM1, chemotherapy, endocrine therapy), T-DXd line and dose, and dates for T-DXd initiation, progression, death, and last assessment. HER2 determinations were recorded as reported by each center; no central pathology review was performed.

Outcomes and assessments

The primary outcome was progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the interval from T-DXd initiation to radiographic or clinical progression, or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. Patients without an event were censored at the date of last disease assessment. Median follow-up was calculated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. Progression evaluations followed center-specific practices and, where available, adhered to RECIST v1.1 criteria.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were summarized as mean (SD), median (IQR), or n (%). Between-group comparisons were performed using χ2 or Fisher's exact tests for categorical variables and t-tests or Wilcoxon rank-sum tests for continuous variables.

Because of anticipated baseline imbalances between patients with and without prior T-DM1 exposure, we applied propensity score–based inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) to reduce confounding. Propensity scores were estimated using a multivariable logistic regression model with prior T-DM1 exposure as the dependent variable and the following prespecified clinically relevant covariates as predictors: age (<65 vs ≥ 65), ECOG performance status, comorbidity status, menopausal status, hormone-receptor status, HER2 IHC score (2+ vs 3+), de novo versus recurrent metastasis, visceral involvement, and number of metastatic sites (1 vs ≥ 2). Stabilized IPTW weights were calculated using the marginal probability of exposure (treated) or non-exposure (untreated) divided by the individual propensity score or its complement. Extreme weights were not observed (range 0.53–1.97).

Covariate balance before and after weighting was assessed using absolute standardized mean differences (SMD), with SMD <0.10 denoting acceptable balance. All survival analyses incorporated stabilized IPTW weights. PFS was estimated using IPTW-weighted Kaplan–Meier curves and compared using IPTW-weighted log-rank tests. The primary estimate of the association between prior T-DM1 exposure and progression risk was obtained from an IPTW-weighted Cox proportional hazards model including only the exposure variable, in line with causal-inference methodology. Robust (sandwich) variance estimators were applied. Analyses were conducted in R version X.X using the survival, survey, and cobalt packages.

Data integrity and risk of bias

Selection bias was mitigated through the inclusion of consecutive eligible patients at each site. Information bias was minimized with standardized CRFs, predefined variable dictionaries, and centralized quality checks. Measured confounding was addressed through IPTW and balance diagnostics. No formal sample-size calculation was possible due to the retrospective design; precision was assessed through 95% confidence intervals.

Results

A total of 218 patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer were included in the analysis. The median age at T-DXd initiation was 43.0 years (IQR 37.0–54.1). More than half of the cohort was premenopausal (57.3%), and the majority had good performance status, with ECOG 0 in 67.9% and ECOG 1 in 31.2% of patients. HER2 status was predominantly high-level positive, with 74.8% of patients having IHC 3+ disease and 25.2% having IHC 2+/ISH-positive tumors. T-DXd was administered a median of three prior lines of systemic therapy (median 3; IQR 2–4), and was used as third-line treatment in 29.4% of patients and as second- or fourth-line therapy in 48.1%. Overall, 62.8% of patients (n = 137) had previously received T-DM1, whereas 37.2% (n = 81) were T-DM1-naive. Baseline clinical characteristics of the study population are summarized in Table 1. Among 217 evaluable patients, the objective response rate (ORR; CR + PR) was 71.9%, including a complete response in 15.2% and a partial response in 56.7% of patients (see Table 3). The median duration of response (DOR) among all patients treated with T-DXd was 20.96 months (95% CI, 15.71–26.22). When stratified by prior exposure to T-DM1, patients without previous T-DM1 treatment showed a numerically longer duration of response; however, the median could not be estimated due to heavy right-censoring. In contrast, patients who had received prior T-DM1 had a median DOR of 20.47 months (95% CI, 16.35–24.59). The difference in DOR distributions between the two groups was statistically significant (log-rank p = 0.038).

A total of 78 progression events (35.8%) occurred during a median follow-up of 8.0 months (IQR, 4.7 - 15.0). In the unweighted cohort, patients who had received prior T-DM1 demonstrated shorter progression-free survival compared with T-DM1 naive patients (log-rank p = 0.006).

After applying stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW), covariate balance between exposure groups was achieved (all post-weighting standardized mean differences <0.10). In the IPTW-weighted analysis, patients with prior T-DM1 exposure continued to demonstrate significantly shorter progression-free survival, with a median PFS of 12.1 months (95% CI, 10.3–20.5) compared with 26.0 months (95% CI, 18.9–NR) in T-DM1–naive patients (IPTW-weighted log-rank p = 0.006; Fig. 1).Fig. 1IPTW weighted Kaplan Meier curves for progression free survival according to prior T-DM1 exposure.Fig. 1

IPTW weighted Kaplan Meier curves for progression free survival according to prior T-DM1 exposure.

In the IPTW-weighted Cox proportional hazards model which, in accordance with causal-inference principles, included only the exposure variable prior T-DM1 use remained independently associated with a significantly higher risk of progression (HR 1.99, 95% CI 1.09–3.62, p = 0.02). Model discrimination was modest (C-index 0.561), which is expected given the heterogeneity of real-world data and the limited adjustment inherent to causal weighting models.

Overall survival (OS) was also evaluated according to prior T-DM1 exposure. In the unweighted analysis, OS curves diverged significantly (log-rank p = 0.03). In the IPTW-weighted analysis (Fig. 2), the median OS for patients with prior T-DM1 exposure was 18.9 months (95% CI, 17.2–NR), whereas the median OS for T-DM1 naive patients was not reached within the available follow-up. In the IPTW-weighted Cox proportional hazards model, prior T-DM1 exposure was not significantly associated with OS (HR 1.80, 95% CI 0.86–3.79, p = 0.12), and model discrimination was modest (C-index 0.56), consistent with the limited number of death events.Fig. 2IPTW-weighted Kaplan–Meier curves for overall survival by prior T-DM1 status.Fig. 2

Discussion

In this large multicenter real world cohort, prior exposure to T-DM1 emerged as a meaningful determinant of treatment outcomes with trastuzumab deruxtecan. Patients who had previously received T-DM1 experienced substantially shorter progression free survival after T-DXd and although overall survival differences did not reach statistical significance after weighting, the direction and magnitude of the observed effect suggest a clinically relevant impact of ADC sequencing. Taken together, these findings underscore the growing recognition that the order in which antibody drug conjugates are deployed may influence subsequent therapeutic benefit, reinforcing the need for clearer guidance on sequencing strategies in HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer.

In this context, the progression-free survival we observed after T-DXd in T-DM1–pretreated patients appears shorter than the approximately 16–18 months reported in the pivotal DESTINY-Breast01 and DESTINY-Breast02 trials, in which T-DXd was evaluated after trastuzumab emtansine in more selected populations [12,13]. Moreover, the updated DESTINY-Breast03 analysis in the second-line setting showed a median PFS of 29.0 months with T-DXd versus 7.2 months with T-DM1, underscoring the impressive efficacy of early ADC sequencing under controlled trial conditions [14]. By contrast, in our real-world cohort, prior T-DM1 exposure was associated with a median PFS of 12.1 months compared with 26.0 months in T-DM1–naive patients, despite a high objective response rate and durable responses in both groups. Several factors likely account for this apparent erosion of PFS relative to clinical trials: patients in our study were more heavily pretreated, T-DXd was frequently used beyond the strict second-line setting, visceral disease burden was substantial, progression assessments relied on heterogeneous, center-specific practices rather than blinded central review, and real-world recipients of T-DXd are likely to have more comorbidities and slightly less favorable performance status than the highly selected patients enrolled in pivotal trials. Consistent with this interpretation, a recent French nationwide target-trial emulation reported a median time to treatment discontinuation of 14.1 months for second-line T-DXd versus 6.5 months for T-DM1 shorter absolute durations than in DESTINY-Breast03, but with a very similar relative benefit attributed to the less selective nature of real-world populations and differences between PFS and treatment-discontinuation endpoints [15]. Taken together, these data indicate that the shorter PFS observed in our cohort is most likely driven by the complexity of real-world patient characteristics and treatment conditions rather than by any true reduction in the intrinsic effectiveness of T-DXd after prior T-DM1 exposure.

Although both T-DM1 and T-DXd target HER2, they differ markedly in payload chemistry and drug delivery: T-DM1 delivers the microtubule inhibitor DM1, whereas T-DXd carries the topoisomerase-I inhibitor DXd with a substantially higher drug-to-antibody ratio and a membrane-permeable payload capable of bystander killing [16,17]. These features theoretically allow T-DXd to overcome resistance driven by HER2 heterogeneity or partial antigen loss a phenomenon frequently observed after exposure to HER2-directed therapies [6,18]. However, prior treatment with T-DM1 may still generate cross-resistance through several non-overlapping pathways, including increased tolerance to ADC-mediated cytotoxic payloads, reduced HER2 internalization, alterations in intracellular trafficking, selection of refractory tumor subclones, and further amplification of intratumoral HER2 heterogeneity [5,19]. Together, these mechanisms provide a biologically plausible explanation for the attenuated but not abolished efficacy of T-DXd observed in T-DM1 pretreated patients in real-world settings.

The markedly longer PFS observed in T-DM1 naive patients likely reflects the biological consequences of first versus subsequent exposure to HER2-directed antibody drug conjugates [8]. Initial ADC treatment may exert a strong selective pressure on tumor clones, eliminating highly HER2-dependent populations while enriching for cells with altered internalization, trafficking, or reduced payload susceptibility [[20], [21], [22]]. In this context, administering T-DXd as the first ADC may allow its higher drug-to antibody ratio and membrane permeable DXd payload to act on a less resistant cellular landscape, thereby maximizing its bystander effect driven cytotoxicity [23,24]. Conversely, initiating T-DXd after T-DM1 may expose the drug to a tumor already shaped by prior ADC-mediated selection, resulting in attenuated but still substantial benefit [25]. These observations, supported by emerging mechanistic models and early sequencing analyses, suggest that the relative positioning of ADCs within the treatment continuum may meaningfully influence therapeutic outcomes and warrant prospective investigation into optimal sequencing strategies.

Our study provides one of the largest real-world datasets evaluating trastuzumab deruxtecan after prior T-DM1 exposure, drawing on harmonized clinical information from 21 oncology centers. The multicenter design enhances external validity by capturing a wide spectrum of patient characteristics, treatment practices, and disease biology that are often underrepresented in clinical trials. Additionally, the use of stabilized IPTW substantially reduced measured confounding and enabled a more balanced comparison between exposure groups, thereby strengthening the credibility of the observed sequencing effect. Given the limited and heterogeneous evidence currently available on T-DM1→T-DXd sequencing, our findings address a key knowledge gap and provide practice-informing insights that complement both trial data and emerging real-world analyses.

This study has several limitations inherent to its retrospective, multicenter design. Although standardized case report forms and centralized data harmonization were used, residual selection or information bias cannot be fully excluded. Radiologic assessments were performed according to local practices without blinded central review, introducing potential heterogeneity in progression ascertainment. The follow up period was relatively short, limiting the maturity of overall survival analyses and reducing the precision of late event estimates. In addition, HER2 determinations were based on local pathology reports without centralized reassessment, which may introduce misclassification in the context of known HER2 heterogeneity. Finally, molecular and genomic correlates such as ERBB2 mutations, HER2 spatial heterogeneity, or biomarkers of ADC trafficking were not available, preventing deeper mechanistic exploration. Nevertheless, none of these limitations are likely to account for the magnitude or consistency of the observed sequencing effect, which remained robust across weighted analyses.

Conclusion

In this multicenter real-world analysis, trastuzumab deruxtecan demonstrated clinically meaningful activity after prior T-DM1 exposure, despite the biologically more resistant disease context shaped by earlier ADC treatment. The sequencing effect observed remained robust after adjustment for measured confounding using stabilized IPTW. These findings bridge an important evidence gap in the optimal positioning of HER2-directed ADCs and provide practice-relevant insights that complement both clinical trial outcomes and emerging real-world data. Prospective studies specifically designed to evaluate ADC sequencing are warranted to refine treatment algorithms and guide personalized therapy for patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer.

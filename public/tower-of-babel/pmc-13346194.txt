Synergistic attenuation of Aeromonas dhakensis virulence by dual deletion of PhoBR and KdpE two-component systems and its protective immunity in zebrafish
Authors: Xuesong Li, Yukun Wu, Mengqi Wang, Roushan Li, Haibo Gao, Chenlu Yang, Aodi Yi, Xueqian Wang, Shuai Ye, Guiying Guo, Lixia Fan, Nuo Yang, Jifeng Zeng, Jiping Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAeromonas dhakensis, an emerging opportunistic pathogen prevalent in tropical regions, poses significant threats to both public health and aquaculture industries. This study elucidates the synergistic effects of dual deletion of the two-component systems PhoBR and KdpDE on virulence attenuation. Using homologous recombination and seamless cloning techniques, we constructed a ΔphoBRΔkdpE double mutant based on our laboratory's existing ΔphoBR mutant. Notably, phenotypic characterization revealed that the double mutant maintained comparable growth kinetics to wild-type, ΔphoBR mutant, and complemented strains under high-potassium conditions. However, under potassium-limiting conditions, ΔphoBRΔkdpE exhibited significantly impaired growth, compromised antioxidant capacity, diminished biofilm formation, reduced extracellular protease activity, and attenuated cell adhesion compared to controls. Furthermore, the mutant demonstrated enhanced susceptibility to antimicrobial peptides and marked virulence attenuation in zebrafish models, as evidenced by a substantially higher LD50 (6.53 × 108 CFU/mL) compared to ΔphoBR (1.15 × 108 CFU/mL) and wild-type strains (2.91 × 107 CFU/mL). Intriguingly, immunization trials showed moderate protective efficacy (43.3% survival) for the double mutant, though not statistically different from ΔphoBR (50% survival). This study provides the first evidence that ΔphoBRΔkdpE mutant strain reduces virulence while maintaining partial immunogenicity, laying crucial groundwork for developing crocodile-targeted live attenuated vaccines against A. dhakensis.Key points • PhoBR/KdpE deletion attenuates A. dhakensis virulence via K+ uptake and virulence factors. • ΔphoBRΔkdpE shows enhanced stress sensitivity but retains immunogenicity in zebrafish. • ΔphoBRΔkdpE is a promising live vaccine candidate against A. dhakensis in aquaculture.

Introduction

Aeromonas dhakensis is the most prevalent species of Aeromonas in Malaysia, Taiwan, Japan, and Australia (Chen et al. 2016; Kitagawa et al. 2020) and is also the most virulent strain of Aeromonas that causes human diseases, presenting with symptoms including wound infections, respiratory infections, hepatobiliary infections, urinary tract infections, gastroenteritis, and septicaemia (Beaz-Hidalgo et al. 2013). Studies have demonstrated that A. dhakensis exhibits high virulence in various animal models and clinical infections (Morinaga et al. 2013; Chen et al. 2014). It is a predominant strain associated with soft tissue infections and bacteraemia (Wu et al. 2015), particularly in patients with cirrhosis or malignant tumours (Chen et al. 2016; Shin et al. 2013). Notably, bacteraemia caused by A. dhakensis is more lethal than that induced by other Aeromonas species (Chen et al. 2014; Morinaga et al. 2013). Therefore, understanding the pathogenicity and detection strategies of A. dhakensis is of great significance.

The two-component system PhoBR, consisting of the phosphate sensor kinase PhoR and the response regulator PhoB, is a key regulatory system involved in phosphate metabolism. The PhoBR two-component signal transduction system plays a pivotal role in multiple metabolic pathways of bacteria and is also crucial for the expression of bacterial virulence factors. Studies have shown that phosphate regulators primarily enhance acid tolerance (Gajiwala and Burley 2000; Suziedė et al. 1999), toxin production (Pratt et al. 2010), and biofilm formation (Flemming and Wingender 2010) to regulate the virulence of pathogenic bacteria. Research on attenuated vaccines targeting the PhoBR two-component signal transduction system has become an important approach for bacterial prevention and control. Deletion or constitutive expression of the phoBR gene leads to varying degrees of virulence attenuation in deletion strains. For example, Bertrand et al. (2010) found abnormal expression of incomplete type I pili in phoBR-deficient strains of avian pathogenic Escherichia coli, which reduced resistance to H2O2 and rabbit serum and exhibited lower toxicity compared to the wild-type strain (Bertrand et al. 2010). Asensio et al. studied Mycobacterium tuberculosis Mt130 and found that the virulence of the phoPR gene deletion strain was significantly attenuated (Asensio et al. 2006). With further research, a vaccine based on the M. tuberculosis phoP deletion strain was developed, and experiments confirmed that it did not cause adverse reactions in immunodeficient mice and provided immunological protection to mice challenged with lethal M. tuberculosis (Aguilar et al. 2007).

The two-component signal transduction system KdpDE comprises the sensor kinase KdpD and the response regulator KdpE, which regulates the high-affinity K transporter KdpFABC. KdpDE not only modulates the expression of the downstream K transporter KdpFABC under low K concentrations but also is critical for bacterial survival and virulence expression. For instance, Parish et al. found that in M. tuberculosis, deletion of kdpDE led to enhanced virulence, as mice infected with the kdpDE-deficient strain of M. tuberculosis succumbed more rapidly than those infected with the wild-type strain (Parish et al. 2003). When Staphylococcus aureus enters the high-K environment of the host from the external environment, the transcriptional level of kdpDE is upregulated, thereby increasing the expression of exotoxin genes (Xue et al. 2011). In contrast, the kdpD-deficient strain of Salmonella typhimurium constructed by Alegado et al. (2011) markedly reduced the survival time in nematode intestines and the survival rate within macrophages. Other studies have shown that the absence of KdpDE in Vibrio alginolyticus also results in decreased bacterial virulence and survival ability in animal hosts. These findings suggest that the two-component signal transduction system KdpDE exerts distinct effects on the virulence of different bacterial species.

In previous studies on A. dhakensis conducted in our laboratory, compared to the wild-type strain, the ΔphoB deletion strain, ΔphoBR deletion strain, and ΔkdpE deletion strain exhibited significantly reduced virulence, extracellular protease activity, and biofilm-forming ability (Wang, 2019, 2020). Transcriptomic analysis of differential gene expression between A. dhakensis strain C160501 (wild-type) and its ΔphoBR deletion mutant revealed that KdpDE was the most highly expressed two-component signal transduction system in the phoBR deletion strain (Chen et al. 2023). The aim of this study was to construct a ΔphoBRΔkdpE double deletion mutant derived from the A. dhakensis phoBR deletion strain, characterize the phenotypic traits of this double deletion mutant to clarify its virulence characteristics, and further assess the efficacy of this mutant as a candidate vaccine for the prevention of A. dhakensis infections.

Materials and methods

Culture media

All culture media used in this study were prepared following standard microbiological and cell culture protocols and sterilized by autoclaving at 121 °C for 20 min unless otherwise specified, with the selection of each medium determined by experimental objectives, the growth characteristics of Aeromonas dhakensis and the viability requirements of EPC cells. Tryptic Soy Agar (TSA) was used as a general non-selective medium, with its agar concentration adjusted to 0.3% (semi-solid) for swarming motility assay to reduce physical resistance for bacterial movement and 1.5% (solid) for routine bacterial culture; Luria–Bertani (LB) medium, a rich general medium, was prepared as liquid for bacterial activation and biofilm formation assay, and solid (1.5% agar) for colony screening, counting, H₂O₂ sensitivity assay and antimicrobial peptide sensitivity assay, due to its high nutrient content supporting rapid logarithmic growth of A. dhakensis. M9 minimal medium, a defined synthetic medium, was used as liquid (no agar) for growth curve assay and semi-solid (0.3% agar) for potassium-modified swarming motility assay, with its potassium ion concentration artificially adjusted to 10 µM (low K⁺) and 10 mM (high K⁺) to simulate potassium-limiting/replete conditions without interference from other ions. The potassium basic medium referred to in this study is a potassium ion-modified basal medium based on M9 minimal medium, with the same core composition as M9 minimal medium and only the KCl concentration adjusted to the experimental required low (10 µM) or high (10 mM) potassium level. MOPS medium (3-(N-Morpholino) propanesulfonic acid medium), a buffered defined medium, was used as semi-solid (0.3% agar) to assist M9 medium in swarming motility assay for its excellent pH buffering capacity that maintains a stable extracellular environment under potassium ion concentration changes. M199 medium, a cell culture-specific medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin, was used for EPC cell culture and bacterial cell adhesion assay, with the antibiotic-free version applied in adhesion assay to avoid antibiotic interference with bacterial adhesion, serving as the gold standard medium for fish EPC cell culture in this study.

Wild-type A. dhakensis of crocodile origin was isolated from the blood of a septic Siamese crocodile (Crocodylus siamensis) at a crocodile farm in Wenchang, Hainan Province, China in 2016, and has been preserved in our laboratory to date (Chen et al. 2023; Pu et al. 2019). The large intestine suicide plasmid pRE112, the wide host shuttle plasmid pBBR1MCS-2 and E. coli WM3064 were stored in our laboratory. The strains and plasmids used in this study

Zebrafish and ethics statement

The model animal zebrafish used in this study were purchased from a local farm in Haikou, Hainan Province, China, with basically uniform body sizes. They were raised in a flow tank at 28–29 ℃ according to the Wester method (Westertleld 1989) for one week. After the survival rate of zebrafish was stable, follow-up experiments were conducted. Before the experiment, fasting for 24 h was performed for intestinal emptying.

The experimental protocol was approved by the Animal Management and Ethics Committee of Hainan University (HNUAUCC-2023-00166), and adhered to the recommendations of the Animal Ethics Procedures and Guidelines of the People’s Republic of China.

Construction of mutant and complementary strains

All PCR amplifications in this study were performed using PrimeSTAR® HS DNA Polymerase (TaKaRa, Japan) with a 50 µL reaction system, and the annealing temperature of each PCR reaction was set according to the Tm values of the specific primers listed in Table S1. The general PCR cycling program was as follows: pre-denaturation at 98 °C for 3 min; 30 cycles of denaturation at 98 °C for 10 s, annealing at primer-specific Tm for 15 s, and extension at 72 °C (1 kb/min); final extension at 72 °C for 5 min, and hold at 4 °C. Colony PCR verification was conducted using Taq DNA Polymerase (TransGen, China) with the same cycling program for rapid and efficient positive clone identification.

The upstream and downstream homologous arms of the kdpE gene were amplified by PCR using kdpE-up-F/R and kdpE-down-F/R primers (Table S1), with XbaⅠ and SacⅠ restriction enzyme sites pre-designed in the 5' ends of the upstream and downstream primers, respectively. The amplified homologous arm fragments and the suicide plasmid pRE112 were double-digested with XbaⅠ and SacⅠ restriction endonucleases (TaKaRa, Japan) at 37 °C for 4 h, and the digested products were recovered by gel extraction. The purified target fragments and linearized pRE112 were ligated with T4 DNA Ligase (TaKaRa, Japan) at 16 °C for 12 h to generate the knockout plasmid pRE112-ΔkdpE. The plasmid was then introduced into the ΔphoBR strain of A. dhakensis via electroporation under the following conditions: voltage of 2.5 kV, 0.2 cm gap electroporation cuvette (Bio-Rad, USA), and pulse settings of 25 µF capacitance and 200 Ω resistance. A stable mutant was obtained by double-crossover homologous recombination and selected from an LB plate containing 20% sucrose and 50 µg/mL chloramphenicol, then confirmed by colony PCR using the validation primers F0/R0 (Table S1)—the F0/R0 primers were specifically designed for the kdpE gene coding region and its upstream/downstream flanking non-coding sequences of A. dhakensis C160501, with the forward primer F0 targeting the conserved non-coding region upstream of kdpE and the reverse primer R0 targeting the conserved non-coding region downstream of kdpE. The amplification fragment size of the positive ΔphoBRΔkdpE double mutant is significantly shorter than that of the ΔphoBR single mutant (lacking the 876 bp intact kdpE coding region), which can directly and effectively verify the successful knockout of the kdpE gene.

To construct the complemented strain, the intact kdpE gene (including its native promoter) was amplified with kdpE-F/R primers (Table S1) under the aforementioned high-fidelity PCR conditions. The broad-host-range plasmid pBBR1MCS-2 was digested with XbaⅠ and SacⅠ for linearization, and the amplified kdpE fragment was fused with the linearized pBBR1MCS-2 to replace the lacZα gene via ClonExpress® One Step Cloning Kit (Vazyme, China) (seamless cloning) at 37 °C for 30 min, generating the complement plasmid pBBR1MCS-2Δ(lacZα)-kdpE. This plasmid was then transformed into the ΔphoBRΔkdpE double mutant via electroporation under the same conditions (2.5 kV voltage, 0.2 cm cuvette, 25 µF capacitance, 200 Ω resistance), yielding the complemented strain ΔphoBRΔkdpE + CkdpE, with positive clones verified by colony PCR with pBBR-F/R and 2kdpE-F/R primers (Table S1). Meanwhile, the empty pBBR1MCS-2 plasmid was separately transformed into the ΔphoBRΔkdpE mutant under the same electroporation conditions to generate the empty vector control strain ΔphoBRΔkdpE + pBBR1MCS-2, with positive clones screened and verified by colony PCR using pBBR-F/R primers (Table S1).

Growth curve assay

Wild-type strain, ΔphoBR deletion strain, ΔphoBRΔkdpE deletion strain, ΔphoBRΔkdpE + CkdpE complemented strain and ΔphoBRΔkdpE + pBBR1MCS-2 empty plasmid strain were streaked onto LB solid agar medium and incubated at 37 ℃ for 18 h for single-colony activation. Single colonies were inoculated into LB liquid medium and cultured at 37 ℃ with shaking at 200 r/min. When the OD600 value of the bacterial culture reached approximately 0.02, the culture was inoculated into basic potassium medium with different potassium concentrations and incubated in a constant-temperature shaking incubator at 37 ℃. A 9-mL aliquot of bacterial culture was taken every two hours and divided into three groups for OD600 measurement, with a total incubation time of 18 h.

Swarming motility assay

The wild-type, mutant and complementary strains cultured overnight were diluted to OD600 of 1.0, then inoculated (3 µL) on 0.3% semi-solid TSA medium and 0.3% semi-solid potassium utilization base medium with different concentrations, and placed for half an hour until the plates were completely solidified, and incubated at 30 °C for 14 h. The average value was obtained from three replicates. Swarming motility was assessed by examining a circular turbidity that formed from the center to the periphery of the plate (Selvaraj et al. 2020). Semi-solid medium with 0.3% agar was selected because the low agar concentration reduces physical resistance and allows bacterial swarming movement, while TSA/M9-MOPS medium was used to distinguish the effect of potassium ions from general nutrient conditions on bacterial motility. All phenotypic assays were carried out in triplicate.

Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) sensitivity assay

The bacterial cells were activated separately, and when the OD600 value was cultured to about 1.0, the 100 µL bacterial solution was uniformly coated on the LB solid plate (1.5% agar), and the sterile paper impregnated in different concentrations of H2O2 (0.5 mol/L, 1 mol/L, 2 mol/L) solution in front of the medium was pasted above the medium respectively. After 12 h of inverted culture at 37 ℃, the diameter of the antibacterial zone was measured. Solid LB medium was selected for this assay because its rich nutrients ensure uniform bacterial lawn formation, which is the basis for accurate measurement of the antibacterial zone diameter.

Quantitative analysis of biofilm

Using crystal violet method, the bacterial solution with OD600 value of 0.4–0.6 was diluted 40 times, and 200 µL bacterial solution was taken and placed on 96-well plate, which was cultured at 37 ℃ for 24 h. After rinsed with PBS buffer, 100 µL formaldehyde solution was added and fixed for 20 min, then dumped and dried, crystal violet was stained for 6 min, PBS buffer was cleaned and dried, 100 µL 95% ethanol solution was added to each well, and the OD590 value was determined and repeated for three groups after being left at room temperature for half an hour.

Extracellular protease (ECPase) activity assay

Wild strains, ΔphoBR deletion strain, ΔphoBRΔkdpE deletion strain, ΔphoBRΔkdpE + CkdpE complemented strain, and ΔphoBRΔkdpE + pBBR1MCS-2 deletion strain were selected, and the bacterial solution was adjusted to OD600 value of 0.2, and 3 µL was lightly applied to the protease activity detection medium. The hydrolytic rings were measured directly in a constant temperature incubator at 37 ℃ for 48 h, and three sets of repeats were performed.

Antimicrobial peptide sensitivity assay

Wild strains, ΔphoBR deletion strain, ΔphoBRΔkdpE deletion strain, ΔphoBRΔkdpE + CkdpE complement strain, and ΔphoBRΔkdpE + pBBR1MCS-2 deletion strain were all adjusted to an OD600 value of 0.5, then uniformly diluted to 1 × 105 CFU/mL. The concentration of antimicrobial peptide cOT1 (Crocodylus siamensis antimicrobial peptide) (Chen et al. 2023; Pata et al. 2011) was diluted with ddH2O to 50 µg/mL, 100 µg/mL and 200 µg/mL, respectively. The bacterial solution and different concentrations of antimicrobial peptides were mixed and added to 96-well plates, and the results were repeated for three times. After incubation at 30 ℃ for 1 h, the bacterial solution was diluted 1000 times with ddH2O, LB solid medium was coated, and colony count was performed after overnight culture at 37 ℃. The formula for calculating bacterial survival rate was = the number of sample colonies/the number of untreated bacterial colonies.

Cell adhesion experiment

The adhesion of wild strain, ΔphoBR deletion strain, ΔphoBRΔkdpE deletion strain, ΔphoBRΔkdpE + CkdpE complemented strain, and ΔphoBRΔkdpE + pBBR1MCS-2 deletion strain were detected. The EPC cells (Epithelioma Papulosum Cyprini cells, carp epithelial cells) were cultured in M199 cell medium, counted and adjusted in concentration after passage, and inoculated into 24-well cell plates. The EPC cells were cultured overnight into monolayer, then the medium was discarded, and the unadherent cells (suspension cells and dead detached cells after adherence) were washed with M199 medium. Bacteria were diluted to 1 × 108 CFU/mL using M199 medium and inoculated into 24-well cell plates at a ratio of 10:1 (bacteria: cells) for three repetitions, with equal amount of PBS solution as the negative control. After 1 h in the cell incubator at 25 ℃, the cells were cleaned with PBS solution for 3 times, and the cells were lysed with 1% Triton X-100. After being treated at 25 ℃ for 45 min at room temperature, the lysate was diluted to an appropriate concentration and coated for counting. The adhesion was evaluated by the adhesion index. Adhesion index = total number of bacteria/number of cells attached to the cell surface.

Adhesion rate = (Number of adherent bacteria/Total initial number of bacteria in cell wells) × 100%

Zebrafish virulence test

1. Virulence assay

Take wild strain, ΔphoBR deletion strain, ΔphoBRΔkdpE deletion strain, ΔphoBRΔkdpE + CkdpE complemented strain and ΔphoBRΔkdpE + pBBR1MCS-2 deletion strain 2 mL, respectively. After centrifugation, the suspensions were diluted with PBS solution to 1 × 108 CFU/mL, 50 zebrafish in each experimental group were injected with 10 µL bacterial solution, and 10 µL PBS solution was injected into each fish in the control group.

2. Determination of median lethal dose (LD50)

When bacterial cells were cultured to the mid-logarithmic phase, they were washed three times with PBS and serially diluted. A total of 260 zebrafish were selected in the experiment, 10 in each group were randomly injected with 10 µL bacterial solution in the abdomen and 10 µL PBS solution in the control group. The number of deaths in each group within 96 h was recorded until no more deaths occurred. Bliss method (Finney 1985) was used to calculate the median lethal dose of wild strains and each missing strain.

Zebrafish immune efficacy test

Bacterial strains were cultured to the logarithmic phase in LB liquid medium, centrifuged at 8000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C; the bacterial pellet was washed three times with sterile PBS, washed with PBS solution and diluted to 1 × 107 CFU/mL, 50 zebrafish in each group, 5 experimental groups and 1 control group, with 10 µL bacterial solution in the experimental group and 10 µL PBS solution in the control group.On day 15 post-immunization, 30 zebrafish per group were intraperitoneally injected with 10 µL of wild strain C160501 (1.09 × 10⁸ CFU/mL), and mortality was recorded until stabilized. Then the immune protection rate of the deletion strain was measured. The bacteria of the dead fish were isolated and identified, and the death was confirmed to be caused by A. dhakensis.

Statistical analyses

SPSS software, ver. 24.0(IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), was used for statistical analysis in one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Data were presented as the mean ± SE, and used G*Power 3.1 software to calculate the required sample size (α = 0.05, power = 0.8). Significant differences between groups were considered at a p value < 0.05, as determined by Student’s t-test.

Results

Construction of ΔphoBRΔkdpE mutant and complemented strain

The suicide plasmid pRE112 used in this study contains the selective marker gene sacB, which is used as a negative screening marker gene to facilitate the screening of positive clones after homologous recombination (Table 1). Based on ΔphoBR deletion strain of A. dhakensis previously constructed in our laboratory, ΔphoBRΔkdpE deletion strain of A. dhakensis was successfully constructed using suicide plasmid pRE112. Colony PCR was performed using PRE112-F/R, a primer designed with pRE112 plasmid sequence, to obtain a colony containing the recombinant plasmid. The size is 1,076 bp, the results are consistent with the correct results (Figure S1). The lacZα gene of pBBR1MCS-2 plasmid was replaced by kdpE gene in a seamless cloning method, and the ΔphoBRΔkdpE + CkdpE complemented strain was successfully constructed. Colony PCR verification was performed using validation primer pBBR-F/R and a redesigned validation primer 2kdpE-F/R at both ends of kdpE. The sizes are 1286 bp and 2173 bp respectively (Figure S2). At the same time, ΔphoBRΔkdpE deletion strain with empty plasmid pBBR1MCS-2 was constructed and screened using validation primer pBBR-F/R with a size of 969 bp (Figure S3). DNA sequencing confirmed the correct constructs and genetic stability analysis confirmed that the amplified fragment was correct and that no sequence changes occurred even after 30 successive passes (Figure S4).

Phenotype analysis of mutant ΔphoBRΔkdpE

Growth curve analysis

The growth curves of ΔphoBRΔkdpE deficient strains at different K+ concentrations were studied. The results showed that the growth of ΔphoBRΔkdpE deficient strains and ΔphoBRΔkdpE + pBBR1MCS-2 deficient strains was restricted under low potassium environment, and the growth state was far lower than that of wild strains and ΔphoBR deficient strains. The growth state was basically consistent with that of wild strain and ΔphoBR deficient strain (Fig. 1A). H2O2 resistance

The diameter of inhibition zone was measured under different concentration of H2O2 (0.5 mol/L, 1 mol/L, 2 mol/L). Moreover, the diameter of inhibition zone of the repaired strain after kdpE gene was similar to that of the ΔphoBR deletion strain. Discussion

In our laboratory, transcriptomic analysis of A. dhakensis C160501 and its ΔphoBR mutant revealed that the two-component system (TCS) KdpDE was the most highly upregulated TCS in the ΔphoBR strain (Chen et al. 2023). Since PhoBR is a well-characterized TCS regulating virulence and phosphate metabolism (Gardner and McCleary 2019), we hypothesized that KdpDE may contribute to A. dhakensis pathogenicity. Given that KdpE (a transcription factor) likely plays a more critical role than KdpD in target gene regulation, we constructed a kdpE knockout in the ΔphoBR background and compared phenotypic traits among wild-type, ΔphoBR, ΔkdpE, and complemented strains.

For genetic manipulation, we employed the suicide plasmid pRE112, carrying sacB (a sucrose-sensitive counterselection marker from Bacillus subtilis) to facilitate homologous recombination screening. Additionally, we used E. coli WM3064 (an Δasd auxotroph requiring 2,6-diaminopimelic acid, DAP) as a donor strain. After conjugation with the ΔphoBR mutant, transformants were selected via chloramphenicol resistance, while DAP-free media prevented WM3064 survival, significantly improving screening efficiency.

To confirm the genetic stability of the ΔphoBRΔkdpE double deletion mutant, we performed colony PCR after 30 generations of serial passaging, which verified the stable inheritance of the deletion. Growth curve analysis under varying potassium concentrations revealed that ΔphoBRΔkdpE mutants and empty vector (pBBR1MCS-2)-complemented strains exhibited growth comparable to wild-type (WT), ΔphoBR single mutants, and complemented strains under potassium-replete conditions. However, under potassium limitation, both ΔphoBRΔkdpE mutants and vector-control strains showed significantly impaired growth compared to WT and ΔphoBR strains, while kdpE-complemented strains restored growth capacity. This phenotype aligns with prior observations in E. coli kdpDE mutants (Schramke et al. 2017).The KdpDE two-component system is known to sense extracellular potassium flux and osmotic stress (Xue et al. 2011; Jung et al. 2000), coordinating adaptive gene expression. Thus, kdpE deletion in ΔphoBRΔkdpE likely disrupts potassium homeostasis in A. dhakensis, compromising environmental adaptation.

Hemolytic activity and swarming motility on standard TSA plates were unaffected by kdpE deletion. However, under low potassium, ΔphoBRΔkdpE mutants and vector-control strains exhibited markedly reduced swarming compared to WT and ΔphoBR strains, consistent with our lab’s earlier ΔkdpE constructs (Chen et al. 2023). Metal ion concentrations are established modulators of bacterial motility and biofilm formation via gene regulatory networks (Patriquin et al. 2008), mirroring findings in avian pathogenic E. coli (Xue et al. 2020). In the latter, ΔkdpE downregulates flagellar genes (fliR, fliP) while upregulating quorum-sensing regulators (yjjQ, bglG). Whether this mechanism operates in A. dhakensis requires further investigation.

The ΔphoBRΔkdpE double mutant exhibited significantly reduced biofilm production compared to the wild-type (WT) and ΔphoBR single mutant, a phenotype consistent with previous observations in ΔkdpE strains (Chen et al. 2023). Notably, A. dhakensis is known to form more robust biofilms and exhibit higher virulence than A. hydrophila (Chen et al. 2014, 2023).In Aeromonas spp., biofilm regulation involves quorum sensing (QS) and the secondary messenger cyclic di-AMP (c-di-AMP) (Talagrand-Reboul et al. 2017; Kozlova et al. 2011), though a direct role of KdpDE in this process remains undocumented. Intriguingly, in Staphylococcus aureus, KdpE has been shown to modulate capsular polysaccharide synthesis by binding to the cap promoter via the LuxS/AI-2 QS system (Zhao et al. 2010). Whether a similar regulatory mechanism exists in A. dhakensis warrants further investigation.

The KdpDE two-component system has been demonstrated to confer resistance to macrophage-derived stresses, including antimicrobial compounds, oxidative stress (H2O2), and nitric oxide (NO), while promoting bacterial persistence within phagocytes (Freeman et al. 2013). Notably, H2O2 exerts its bactericidal effects by disrupting metalloenzyme activity and DNA integrity (Imlay 2013). Our oxidative stress assays revealed that the ΔphoBRΔkdpE double mutant exhibited significantly greater sensitivity to H2O2 at various concentrations compared to both wild-type (WT) and ΔphoBR single mutant strains. Complementation with kdpE restored H2O2 resistance to levels comparable to the ΔphoBR strain, confirming the essential role of KdpDE in A. dhakensis survival under oxidative stress.

Furthermore, we observed marked differences in protease activity between the ΔphoBRΔkdpE mutant and control strains (WT and ΔphoBR). Genetic complementation of kdpE restored protease production to levels similar to the ΔphoBR strain. These findings suggest that KdpE may facilitate evasion of host immune defenses through protease-mediated mechanisms, consistent with previous reports establishing extracellular proteases as crucial virulence factors in Aeromonas pathogenesis (Sakai 1985).

Crocodile serum contains abundant antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) that provide robust defense against pathogenic bacteria. Our susceptibility assays with the crocodile AMP cOT1 demonstrated a concentration-dependent bactericidal effect across all tested strains. Notably, at 100 µg/mL cOT1, wild-type (WT) C160501 exhibited a survival rate of 11.93%, while ΔphoBRΔkdpE mutants (both with and without empty vector pBBR1MCS-2) showed near-complete lethality (0.01% survival). This enhanced sensitivity was consistently observed at lower concentrations (25 µg/mL), where ΔphoBRΔkdpE strains displayed significantly reduced survival compared to both WT and ΔphoBR single mutants. Genetic complementation with kdpE restored bacterial resistance to levels comparable to ΔphoBR strains.

AMPs typically target cell membranes or critical intracellular components as part of innate immune defenses. The observed phenotype may be explained by the dual role of KdpE in: (1) potentially upregulating biofilm formation, which can physically shield bacteria from AMPs, and (2) modulating interactions between AMPs and biofilm matrix polymers. This hypothesis aligns with the established protective function of biofilms against antimicrobial agents, though the specific regulatory mechanism warrants further investigation.

Bacterial adhesion represents a critical initial step in pathogenesis, involving specific interactions between microbial adhesins and host cell surface receptors that enable colonization, proliferation, and subsequent toxin production (Dong et al. 2017). Recent advances in understanding A. dhakensis virulence mechanisms have been facilitated by in vitro cellular models (Sheng, 2022). Using carp epithelioma (EPC) cells, we quantitatively compared the adhesive capacity of wild-type (WT), mutant, and complemented A. dhakensis strains. Strikingly, the ΔphoBRΔkdpE double mutant exhibited a 9.47-fold reduction in adhesion compared to WT and a 2.35-fold reduction relative to the ΔphoBR single mutant. Genetic complementation restored adhesion to WT levels, strongly implicating KdpE in mediating host cell attachment. While these findings suggest KdpE's regulatory role in adhesion, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying this phenotype require further elucidation.

Our comprehensive phenotypic analyses consistently demonstrated virulence attenuation in the ΔphoBRΔkdpE mutant of A. dhakensis. To further evaluate its potential as a live attenuated vaccine candidate, we conducted systematic virulence assessments using zebrafish as a model organism.

The challenge experiments revealed significant differences in pathogenicity: wild-type strains exhibited 60% mortality, ΔphoBR mutants showed 40% mortality, while ΔphoBRΔkdpE double mutants demonstrated only 27% cumulative mortality. Quantitative analysis of median lethal dose (LD50) indicated that the ΔphoBRΔkdpE mutant was 22.44-fold less virulent than wild-type and 5.68-fold less virulent than the ΔphoBR single mutant, confirming substantial virulence attenuation. These findings align with previous reports in Vibrio alginolyticus, where kdpDE deletion similarly reduced virulence (Zhang, 2015). However, this contrasts with Mycobacterium tuberculosis, where kdpDE deletion enhanced murine pathogenicity (Parish et al. 2003). This dichotomy in KdpDE-mediated virulence regulation likely reflects pathogen-specific evolutionary adaptations to distinct ecological niches.

The genus Aeromonas poses significant threats to global aquaculture, causing both acute hemorrhagic septicemia and chronic infections that result in substantial economic losses (Chen et al. 2016). Among these pathogens, A. dhakensis has emerged as a particularly virulent species with broad environmental distribution in tropical regions, demonstrating high infectivity rates in both aquatic species and humans. Current disease management in aquaculture remains heavily dependent on antibiotic prophylaxis, which raises critical concerns regarding food safety, environmental contamination, and the alarming rise of antimicrobial resistance. These challenges have intensified the need for alternative disease control strategies, particularly vaccine development. Live attenuated vaccines, generated through targeted mutation or deletion of virulence-associated genes, offer a promising solution by stimulating robust host immune responses and antibody production (Wang et al., 2025). In this context, we systematically evaluated the attenuation potential of a ΔphoBRΔkdpE double mutant through comprehensive phenotypic analyses following kdpE gene deletion.

Immunoprotective capacity serves as the most critical indicator for assessing vaccine candidate viability. To evaluate the ΔphoBRΔkdpE attenuated strain, we conducted immunization-challenge trials using zebrafish as a model host. The results demonstrated that vaccination with the ΔphoBRΔkdpE strain conferred moderate protection, with vaccinated groups showing 56.7% cumulative mortality following challenge, corresponding to a relative percent survival (RPS) of 43.3%. Notably, this protection level did not differ significantly from that provided by the ΔphoBR single mutant.We propose that kdpE could regulate virulence related genes, so after the deletion of kdpE gene on the basis of ΔphoBR deletion strain, the strain was less virulent and retained less cell surface antigen. After infecting the body, the host could not make a timely immune response to produce enough antibodies, so it did not achieve better immune effect.

Conclusion

In summary, A. dhakensis has strong pathogenicity, but the current research on its pathogenicity is limited, and in-depth research on the pathogenic mechanism of A. dhakensis is of great significance for its prevention and control. The deletion strain ΔphoBRΔkdpE constructed in this study has certain attenuated toxicity, and the immune protection effect is not significantly different from that of ΔphoBR deletion strain, which provides a certain reference for the study of pathogenic mechanism of A. dhakensis and the development of attenuated live vaccine.

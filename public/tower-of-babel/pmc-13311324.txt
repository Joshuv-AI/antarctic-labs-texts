Association between osteoporosis and arthritis: Results from the NHANES and Mendelian randomization study
Authors: Xingyu Wang, Hao Lv, Long Liang, Ting Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study seeks to investigate associations of osteoporosis (OP) with arthritis and to assess causality using Mendelian randomization (MR).MethodsFirstly, an observational study was conducted using the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES). The relationship between OP and arthritis was analyzed by weighted multivariate logistic regression. Furthermore, a Mendelian randomization analysis was performed to examine the causal effect of arthritis on OP. Genetic data on OP and arthritis were derived from independent genome-wide association studies (GWAS). Arthritis includes osteoarthritis and inflammatory arthritis. The inverse variance weighting method was used to estimate causal effects. Sensitivity analyses were performed to further validate the robustness of the results.ResultsIn this cross-sectional analysis, a positive association was found between OP and arthritis (OR = 1.758; 95% CI, 1.316-2.347; P < 0.01). These results did not change on multiple imputations (OR = 1.469, 95% CI, 1.044-2.068; P = 0.031 < 0.05). In MR analysis, it was found that OP was causally associated with rheumatoid arthritis (OR = 0.854; 95% CI, 0.736-0.992; P = 0.038 < 0.05), seropositive rheumatoid arthritis (OR = 0.998; 95% CI, 0.998-0.999; P < 0.001), ankylosing spondylitis (OR = 1.003; 95% CI, 1.000-1.005; P = 0.026 < 0.05), systemic lupus erythematosus (OR = 1.001; 95% CI, 1.000-1.001; P = 0.002 < 0.01), and gout (OR = 1.001; 95% CI, 1.000-1.001; P = 0.038 < 0.05) based on the IVW method. There was no causal association between OP and osteoarthritis, knee osteoarthritis, hip osteoarthritis, Spondyloarthritis, and seronegative rheumatoid arthritis. None of the analyses revealed any horizontal pleiotropy or heterogeneity.ConclusionIn this cross-sectional study, a significant association is found between OP and arthritis. Additionally, the investigation using MR revealed evidence supporting a causal relationship between OP and certain types of arthritis.

Background

Osteoporosis (OP) is a skeletal disease that increases fracture risk, with hip and spine fractures being the most common [[1], [2], [3]]. Globally, OP affects approximately 18.3% of women and 6.8% of men over 50, leading to substantial morbidity and healthcare costs [4]. Arthritis is another common chronic disease in the general population. The most common is osteoarthritis which is non-inflammatory arthritis, inflammatory arthritis mainly includes rheumatoid arthritis (RA), ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), and gout [5]. Epidemiological studies have shown that the prevalence of arthritis rises with age, affecting nearly 50% of individuals over 65, and its coexistence with osteoporosis may exacerbate disability and reduce quality of life [6]. Although an abundance of literature has examined the relationship between OP and arthritis [[7], [8], [9], [10]], few have investigated the causal relationship between OP and arthritis systematically and stratified. OP and arthritis may share common pathophysiological pathways, such as aging, degeneration, inflammation, and glucocorticoids [7,11,12]. However, the relationship between OP and arthritis showed a wide range of variations. Therefore, understanding the relationship between OP and arthritis would pave the way for more holistic management of both conditions.

The National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) is a representative multi-level and multi-dimensional project, its continuity is due to the organization's annual survey of a sample of the American people [13,14]. Thus, NHANES has offered a nationally representative and high-quality sample to investigate the relationship between OP and arthritis.

The genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have greatly aided in the identification of single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) linked to common complex diseases [15]. Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis uses genetic variation as an instrumental variable (IV) to investigate the relationship between exposure factors and disease outcomes [16].

This research aims to demonstrate the relationship between OP and arthritis. Therefore, an observational study was performed via NHANES and two-sample MR analyses to investigate the causal association between OP and arthritis.

Materials and methods

Data and study population

NHANES data were collected and screened for the cross-sectional study. Participants signed the informed consent form. Data were combined from the 2013-2014, and 2017-2018 waves of NHANES. The reasons and criteria for selecting data and participants are referred to in a paper (Lv Hao et al., 2022) [17] (The raw data and final data can be found in Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). To ensure accuracy, statistical analysis was performed on data from different years before combining them. Statistically, there were no significant differences between the two waves of data (the detailed analysis results can be found in Supplementary Table 3). The analysis included 3893 participants out of 6883 eligible participants, excluding those with missing data and participants under 50 years of age.

Data for MR analysis were taken from a GWAS database (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac. uk/). Data associated with osteoporosis (OP) were taken from the GWAS database that collected 462933 samples (7547 OP samples and 455386 control samples) saved in the UK Biobank. There are four sources of OA data in this study, including total OA, knee OA, hip OA, and spondyloarthritis (SpA). The GWAS data of total OA with a total of 38472 OA patients and 424461 control samples. Knee OA with 403124 participants (24955 cases and 378169 controls), hip OA with 393873 samples (15704 cases and 378169 controls), SpA with 201581 samples (3037 cases and 198544 controls). Overall RA with 361194 participants (1605 cases and 359589 controls), seronegative RA with 174771 participants (1937 cases and 172834 controls), seropositive RA with 215661 participants (1297 cases and 214364 controls), ankylosing spondylitis with 213879 participants (1637 cases and 212242 controls), psoriatic arthritis with 22647 participants (9069 cases and 13578 controls), SLE with 177128 participants (538 cases and 176590 controls), and gout with 217938 participants (1699 cases and 216239controls). All data were obtained from the FinnGen Biobank Analysis Consortium database, UK Biobank, EMBL's European Bioinformatics Institute, and IEU OpenGWAS project (The GWAS information for OP and arthritis can be found in Supplementary Table 4).

OP, arthritis diagnosis and bone mineral density (BMD) measurement of the cross-sectional study

The outcome variable of this study was whether the participants were diagnosed with OP. The diagnosis of osteoporosis is determined if any of the following three conditions are met: a BMD test diagnosis, being told previously that one has OP/brittle bones by a doctor, or being advised by a doctor or other health care professional to take prescribed medication for OP. The auxiliary examination diagnosis of OP was based on T-scores calculated from the BMD of the neck of femur and lumbar vertebra with dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA). The femur and lumbar spine scans were analyzed with the APEX [18]. BMD was calculated as T-scores via a method. T-scores were divided into ≥ −1, −1 to −2.5, and ≤ −2.5, representing normal, osteopenia, and OP [1,19]. The diagnosis of arthritis is being told previously that one has arthritis by a doctor.

Covariate ascertainment of the cross-sectional study

Concomitant variables of this cross-sectional study included sex, age, race, education, federal poverty level (FPL), body mass index (BMI), smoking, drinking, diabetes, and hypertension. Age was classified as 50-60, 60-70, and 70-80. Covariates about sex, race, education, age, FPL, smoking, and drinking were obtained from structured questionnaires. The race was classified as White race, Black race, Mexican, and Others. Education was categorized into high school and below, college (and equivalent educational attainment), and above college. FPL was classified as < 200% FPL and ≥200% FPL. BMI was defined as the underweight range when the index was less than 18.5. Healthy weight was in the range of 18.5 to 25, overweight range: 25 - 30. It was in the obesity range if the BMI was over 30. Obesity was further divided into Classes 1-3, BMI was 30 - 35, 35 - 40, and ≥40 respectively [20]. Smoking less than 100 cigarettes in life was described as no, the definition of yes was smoking more than 100 cigarettes in life. The diagnosis of diabetes was the doctor told that you have diabetes, or glycohemoglobin Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c(%)) ≥ 6.5, or fasting glucose ≥7.0 mmol/l, or random blood glucose≥11.1 mmol/l, or 2-h Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) blood glucose ≥11.1 mmol/l or use of diabetes medication or insulin.

Statistical analysis

All NHANES data were weighted due to the complex probability clustering design. Continuous variables are expressed as mean (95% confidence intervals (CI)) and categorical variables as count (percentage). The relationship between OP and arthritis was analyzed via weighted logistical regression. The confounding factors were added to the multivariate logistic model to adjust the relationship analysis. Odds ratios (OR) with 95% CI and P values were calculated. Statistical analysis of all data was performed by R Studio (https://ropensci.org/blog/2021/11/16/how-to-cite-r-and-r-packages/). A 2-tailed P < 0.05 was regarded as significant.

MR analyses were carried out through the inverse-variance-weighted (IVW) method, which is the best-unbiased estimate in the absence of pleiotropy (Bowden et al., 2015) [21]. This method consists of a meta-analysis of the Wald ratio of each SNP between the exposure and the effect outcome to estimate the causal effect of arthritis on OP. In addition, we also estimated the effects using the weighted median and the MR-Egger methods. The software used to analyze the data was R version (3.6.2) with the package “TwoSampleMR.” All genome-wide significant SNPs were filtered out with a strict threshold of P < 5 × 10−8 and were then clumped with a cutoff of r2 = 0.001 and kb = 10,000 to avoid linkage disequilibrium (LD).

Sensitivity and pleiotropy analysis

Our MR analysis was based on three assumptions [22]. The MR-Egger regression approaches (MR- PRESSO) were performed to verify the second assumption (pleiotropic test). The heterogeneity between SNPs was evaluated by Cochran's Q test. In order to testify whether the results of MR analysis could be biased or determined by a single SNP, Leave-one-out analysis was conducted by removing one SNP at a time and recalculating the IVW outcomes for the remaining (All visualized results can be found in Supplementary File 1).

Discussion

This cross-sectional study concluded that OP was associated with arthritis. The relationship remained the same even after other factors were added (demographic factors and smoking, drinking, diabetes, hypertension). Furthermore, the MR results support an association between OP and RA, seropositive RA, SLE, gout, and ankylosing spondylitis.

A few studies have confirmed that OP was associated with arthritis (OA and inflammatory arthritis). Bone mineral density (BMD) has been proven to be positively correlated with the risk of OA [24]. However, a two-sample MR analysis found that OP can reduce the incidence of OA (including knee OA and hip OA) [25]. Data are contradictory regarding the relationship between OA and OP for the reason that swapping outcomes and exposure factors may lead to changes in the study results. A review study suggested that RA may further cause OP through autoimmune inflammation leading to up-regulation of osteoclasts [26]. The effect of seropositive RA on OP was more obvious, as the disease progresses, seropositive RA patients are more likely to decrease bone mineral density and bone damage than seronegative RA patients [27]. Rim Sghiri et al. [28] confirmed the association between IgA RF and BMD preservation among patients with RA. A large retrospective matched cohort study finally found a higher incidence and earlier development of OP in patients with ankylosing spondylitis [29]. A recent study of the relationship between psoriatic arthritis and OP showed that psoriatic arthritis is an independent risk factor for decreased BMD and is more likely to lead to fractures and falls [30]. A Meta-Analysis of the relationship between SLE and OP showed that low BMD, osteopenia, and OP appeared to be prevalent in patients with SLE [31]. A prospective clinical study confirmed this conclusion [32]. A longitudinal Follow-Up study showed that gout may result in a slightly elevated likelihood of developing OP [33].

From a clinical perspective, these findings have several important implications. Firstly, patients with certain types of arthritis, particularly RA, SLE, gout, and ankylosing spondylitis, may benefit from routine BMD screening to enable early detection and management of OP. Current treatment approaches for OP include bisphosphonates (e.g., alendronate, zoledronic acid), denosumab, and teriparatide, while arthritis management involves disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) such as methotrexate and biologic agents like TNF inhibitors [34,35]. Secondly, the causal relationships identified suggest that aggressive control of inflammation in RA and SLE could potentially reduce OP risk, highlighting the need for integrated care strategies. Thirdly, clinicians should be aware that seropositive RA confers a higher risk of bone loss compared with seronegative RA, warranting closer monitoring. These insights underscore the importance of multidisciplinary management involving rheumatologists and endocrinologists to optimize outcomes for patients with both conditions.

To the authors' knowledge, there have been observational studies or MR analyses investigating the relationship between OP and arthritis, but these studies are confined to a specific type of arthritis or BMD at one site, and It is rare for systematic and combined studies to be available like this one. NHANES data are acquired and screened according to a standardized unified procedure, ensuring the accuracy and consistency of the results. The results were reliable because they were based on a large number of community samples and a weighted analysis of the data. Comparing regression analysis to mechanism research, it is more intuitive and comprehensive to include some important confounding factors.

Nevertheless, this research still has some limitations. Due to the fact that this was a cross-sectional survey based on NHANES, there is no way to determine the causal relationship between the dependent and independent variables. However, this deficiency is precisely the advantage of MR analysis. Thus, the major strength of this study was the use of MR analysis combined with observational study design in NHANES. Based on the consistency of findings between the MR analysis and the observational study, the results were more reliable.

In the following studies, the mutual causal relationship between arthritis and OP can be verified by bidirectional two-sample MR analysis. To further strengthen and verify these results, a large-scale cohort study and mechanism research are required to complete the validation. Secondly, NHANES data sources are measured or collected only once, which increases the possibility of data bias. Therefore, the database can be repeated multiple times in the following study.

Conclusion

In this cross-sectional study, a significant association was found between OP and arthritis. Additionally, the investigation using MR reveals evidence supporting a causal relationship between OP and certain types of arthritis (RA, seropositive RA, SLE, gout, and ankylosing spondylitis).

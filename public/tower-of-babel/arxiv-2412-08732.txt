Exploring superconformal Yang-Mills theories through matrix Bessel kernels
Zoltan Bajnok a , Bercel Boldis a,b and Gregory P. Korchemsky c

Abstract
A broad class of observables in four-dimensional 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 and 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 superconformal Yang-Mills theories can be exactly computed in the planar limit for arbitrary ’t Hooft coupling as Fredholm determinants of integrable Bessel operators. These observables admit a unifying description through a one-parameter generating function, which possesses a determinant representation involving a matrix generalization of the Bessel operator. We analyze this generating function over a wide range of parameter values and finite ’t Hooft coupling. We demonstrate that it has a well-behaved weak-coupling expansion with a finite radius of convergence. In contrast, the strong-coupling expansion exhibits factorially growing coefficients, necessitating the inclusion of non-perturbative corrections that are exponentially suppressed at strong coupling. We compute these non-perturbative corrections and observe a striking resemblance between the resulting trans-series expansion of the generating function and the partition function of a strongly coupled theory expanded in powers of a mass gap.

Introduction and summary

Computing observables in four-dimensional superconformal Yang-Mills theories in the planar limit for finite ’t Hooft coupling
\lambda=g_{\rm YM}^{2}N

In recent years, a significant progress has been achieved in computing various observables specified below.
A common feature of these observables is that they exhibit well-behaved weak coupling expansions with finite radii of convergence, but their strong coupling expansions involve factorially growing coefficients, necessitating the inclusion of non-perturbative corrections that are exponentially suppressed at strong coupling,
\lambda\gg 1

The method of differential equations is effective for computing the function

For our purposes it is convenient to rewrite (

\Lambda_{+}^{2}

Given that the observable in (

Using the technique described above, we can compute an arbitrary

7.2

We demonstrate below that the analytical structure of the Borel transform of the perturbative function carries information on the non-perturbative corrections and the various sectors in (

Appendix A
Derivation of the differential equations

In this appendix, we derive the differential equations (
53
) – (
55
). The derivation relies on the properties of the Bessel kernel (
35
), which are summarized below. Our analysis closely follows the approach outlined in
[
14
]
.

Properties of the Bessel kernel

The Bessel kernel
K_{\ell}(x,y)=K_{\ell}(y,x)
is defined in (
35
). It satisfies the following relations

\displaystyle{\left({x\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}+1}\right)}K_{\ell}(x,y)=\frac{1}{4}J_{\ell}(\sqrt{x})J_{\ell}(\sqrt{y})\,,

\displaystyle(x-y)K_{\ell}(x,y)=J_{\ell}(\sqrt{x})y\partial_{y}J_{\ell}(\sqrt{y})-x\partial_{x}J_{\ell}(\sqrt{x})J_{\ell}(\sqrt{y})

\displaystyle\phantom{(x-y)K_{\ell}(x,y)}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\sqrt{x}J_{\ell+1}(\sqrt{x})J_{\ell}(\sqrt{y})-\sqrt{y}J_{\ell+1}(\sqrt{y})J_{\ell}(\sqrt{x})\right]

\displaystyle\phantom{(x-y)K_{\ell}(x,y)}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\sqrt{y}J_{\ell}(\sqrt{x})J_{\ell-1}(\sqrt{y})-\sqrt{x}J_{\ell}(\sqrt{y})J_{\ell-1}(\sqrt{x})\right].

(195)

The first relation follows from the integral representation (
35
). The second is a direct consequence of the Christoffel-Darboux formula applied to the Bessel function and its differential equation.

The Bessel function
J_{\ell}(x)
has infinitely many zeros, denoted by
\mu_{a}
(where
a=1,2,\dots
). Setting
x=\mu_{a}^{2}
in the second relation of (
195
) yields

\displaystyle K_{\ell}(\mu_{a}^{2},y)={\mu_{a}J_{\ell+1}(\mu_{a})J_{\ell}(\sqrt{y})\over 2(\mu_{a}^{2}-y)}\,,\qquad\qquad K_{\ell+1}(\mu_{a}^{2},y)={J_{\ell+1}(\mu_{a})\sqrt{y}J_{\ell}(\sqrt{y})\over 2(\mu_{a}^{2}-y)}\,.

(196)

In addition, for
y=\mu_{b}^{2}
and
J_{\ell}(\mu_{b})=0
we find

\displaystyle K_{\ell}(\mu_{a}^{2},\mu_{b}^{2})=K_{\ell+1}(\mu_{a}^{2},\mu_{b}^{2})=\frac{1}{4}J^{2}_{\ell+1}(\mu_{a})\delta_{ab}\,.

(197)

The orthogonality condition (
17
) implies that the Bessel kernel (
35
) possesses the properties of a projector

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\,K_{\ell}(x,y)K_{\ell}(y,z)=K_{\ell}(x,z)\,.

(198)

Furthermore, the normalized Bessel functions (
16
) satisfy a recurrence equation

\displaystyle{\psi_{n}(x)\over\sqrt{x}}={1\over 2\sqrt{n}}\left[{\psi_{n+1}(x)\over\sqrt{n+1}}+{\psi_{n-1}(x)\over\sqrt{n-1}}\right],

(199)

which leads to

\displaystyle\int{dy\over\sqrt{y}}\,K_{\ell}(x,y)K_{\ell+1}(y,z)={1\over\sqrt{z}}K_{\ell}(x,z)-{\psi_{\ell+1}(x)\psi_{\ell}(z)\over 2\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}}\,,

\displaystyle\int{dy\over\sqrt{y}}\,K_{\ell+1}(x,y)K_{\ell}(y,z)={1\over\sqrt{z}}K_{\ell+1}(x,z)\,.

(200)

Appendix B
Matrix generalization of the first Szegő theorem

At strong coupling, the leading asymptotic behaviour of the Fredholm determinants of the truncated Bessel operators (
37
)
is given by

\displaystyle F_{\ell}=-{2g\over\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z\partial_{z}\log(1+\chi(z))+O(g^{0})\,.

(220)

In mathematical literature this relation is known as the first Szegő theorem
[
40
]
.
In this appendix, we follow
[
16
]
and generalize the relation (
220
) to the determinant (
46
) involving matrix generalization of the Bessel operators.

We start with the first relation in (
202
) and rewrite it as

\displaystyle g\partial_{g}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}{}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\left(\gamma(x)U\right)g\partial_{g}\chi\Big(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{2g}\Big),

(221)

where the notation was introduced for a
2\times 2
matrix

\displaystyle\gamma(x){}

\displaystyle=\langle{x|{1\over 1+\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}\bm{\mathcal{X}}}\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}|x}\rangle

\displaystyle={\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}(x,x)-\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\,\chi\Big(\frac{\sqrt{y}}{2g}\Big){\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}(x,y)U{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}(y,x)+\dots\,.

(222)

Here in the second relation we expanded the resolvent in powers of the operators and replaced the product of operators by a convolution of their integral kernels (
2.4
) and (
2.4
).

At strong coupling, the dominant contribution to the integrals in (
221
) and (
222
) comes from
x
and
y
being of order

. This allows us to replace the kernel
{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}(x,y)
by its leading behaviour at large

and

. Replacing the Bessel functions in (

) and (

) with their asymptotic behaviour at infinity we find

\displaystyle K_{\ell}(x,y)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi(xy)^{1/4}}\left[\frac{\sin\left(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y}\right)}{\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y}}-(-1)^{\ell}\frac{\cos\left(\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y}\right)}{\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y}}\right]\,.

The second term inside the brackets is a rapidly oscillating function and its contribution to (

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}(x,y)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi(xy)^{1/4}}\frac{\sin\left(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y}\right)}{\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y}}\times\mathbf{1}\,.

Appendix G
Generalized Borel transformation

In this appendix, we outline various techniques for investigating the analytic properties of asymptotic series (for a review, see e.g.
[
57

58
]
).

The coefficient functions (

\displaystyle f(g)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{f_{k}}{g^{k+1}},

(296)

grow factorially at large orders in

\displaystyle f_{k}=\frac{1}{\pi}\bigg[c_{0}\frac{\Gamma(k+\lambda)}{(4\pi A)^{k+\lambda}}+c_{1}\frac{\Gamma(k+\lambda-1)}{(4\pi A)^{k+\lambda-1}}+c_{2}\frac{\Gamma(k+\lambda-2)}{(4\pi A)^{k+\lambda-2}}+\dots\bigg],

We outline below a method to determine the coefficients in (

Borel singularities.

To regularize the series (

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{p}(s)=\sum_{k\geq 0}f_{k}{s^{k+p}\over\Gamma(k+p+1)}\,,

where

167

The function (

Substituting (

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{p}(s){}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}\,\Gamma(\lambda-p-n)(4\pi A-s)^{p+n-\lambda}+\dots\,,

By slightly deforming the integration contour in (

\displaystyle S^{+}[f](g)-S^{-}[f](g){}

We conclude that the factorial growth of the expansion coefficients (

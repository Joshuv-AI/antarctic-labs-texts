Cone structures and path geometries with constant torsion
Wojciech Kryński

Abstract
. We study three-dimensional path geometries with nontrivial torsion of maximal rank. We introduce the notion of constant torsion and show that such path geometries are in one-to-one correspondence with certain cone structures modeled on homogeneous ruled surfaces. We also describe these cone structures in terms of solutions to new integrable systems admitting dispersionless, non-isospectral Lax pairs.



1.
Introduction

There is a well-established theory relating 4-dimensional conformal metrics of split signature to 3-dimensional torsion-free path geometries
[
9
]
, which can be seen as a manifestation of twistor theory in the real category
[
1
]
. In this paper, we study path geometries with non-vanishing torsion, which turn out to be naturally associated with certain types of cone structures. These structures generalize conformal metrics by replacing the usual field of light cones with a field of more general, non-quadratic cones.
Building on our earlier results
[
13
,
14
]
, we characterize and provide a complete classification of path geometries with constant torsion in terms of the associated cone structures. Our main result is as follows:

Theorem 1
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between integrable isotypic cone structures in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
modeled on projective surfaces defined by equations

(1)

\ln(z)=2\arctan\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)

or

(2)

\ln\left(\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{1+z^{2}}\right)=2\left(\arctan\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)-\arctan(z)\right)

and 3-dimensional path geometries whose torsion
T^{X}
is of rank-2, satisfies
\nabla T^{X}\simeq T^{X}
and
\hat{\mathbb{S}}^{X}(T^{X})=0
and has two real roots, or two imaginary roots, respectively.

The surfaces (
1
) and (
2
) are homogeneous, ruled projective surfaces given in
[
2
, formulas (17) and (20)]
(see also
[
3
, surfaces (2) and (3)]
).

The terminology used in the formulation of the theorem will be explained in the next section. For now, let us simply note that an isotypic cone structure refers to a cone structure in which the cone at each point of the manifold is projectively equivalent to a fixed one (in our case, the one given by (
1
) or (
2
), respectively). Moreover, the integrability of a cone structure generalizes the notion of (anti-)self-duality for conformal metrics (see
[
4
]
). Indeed, it is well known that a conformal metric of split signature is self-dual if and only if the
\alpha
-planes can be integrated to yield
\alpha
-submanifolds. A similar notion can be introduced for general cone structures modeled on ruled surfaces. The operators
\nabla
and
\hat{\mathbb{S}}^{X}
are certain differential operators canonically associated with systems of pairs of second-order ordinary differential equations (ODEs), originally introduced in
[
13
]
, whose definitions are recalled in Section
2
. Note that, locally a path geometry is just a system of second order ODEs given up to point transformations, see e.g.
[
9
]
.

Let us point out that the torsion of a system is defined only up to multiplication by a function. Strictly speaking, it can be regarded as a section of
S^{2}(\mathcal{X}^{*})\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})
, where
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{V}
are certain natural vector bundles of ranks
1
and
2
, respectively, defined over the space of
1
-jets, and where
S^{2}(\mathcal{X})
denotes the symmetric tensor product. Given a section
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{X})
, we denote by
T^{X}
the corresponding section of
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})
. This assignment satisfies the property
T^{fX}=f^{2}T^{X}
. Therefore,
a priori
, it is far from obvious what it means for the torsion to be constant. However, there exists a distinguished class of sections of
\mathcal{X}
, called later
projective vector fields
, on which the Möbius group acts transitively. The conditions
\nabla T^{X}\simeq T^{X}
and
\hat{\mathbb{S}}^{X}(T^{X})=0
together imply – as will be explained later – that there exists a projective vector field

for which

is constant. In this sense, we shall say that the torsion is constant.

Theorem
1
completes the classification of 3-dimensional path geometries (or the pairs of ODEs) with constant torsion, in the above sense, in terms of the associated cone structures. Indeed, the case of torsion of rank

, where the so-called Cayley structures are introduced. These are the second simplest examples of cone structures after conformal structures. The torsion-free case, on the other hand, is a classical result due to Grossman

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Cone structures.

We shall restrict here to the cone structures on 4-dimensional manifolds. Given a manifold
M
,
\dim M=4
, we shall assume that there is an immersed surface
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
in the projective tangent space
P(T_{x}M)
, for each
x\in M
. It follows that the corresponding preimage
\mathcal{C}_{x}\subset T_{x}M
,
\mathcal{C}_{x}=q^{-1}(\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{x})
, where
q\colon TM\to P(TM)
is the canonical quotient map, is a 3-dimensional cone in the tangent space. We shall assume that
\mathcal{C}_{x}
depends smoothly on
x
.

Remark
.

If all
\mathcal{C}_{x}
are quadratic cones, then the they uniquely determine a class of conformaly equivalent metrics on
M
of Lorentzian or split signature. Indeed, one can treat the cones as sets of null directions of the metrics. By analogy, more general cone structures in dimension 4 are sometimes referred to as the causal structures (c.f.
[
13
,
15
]
).

Later we shall need the following two definitions.

Definition
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{V}
be a cone in a vector space
V=\mathbb{R}^{4}
and let
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{V}
be the corresponding surface in
\mathbb{P}(V)=\mathbb{P}^{3}
. A cone structure
\mathcal{C}
on a manifold
M
is
isotypic
, modeled on
\mathcal{C}_{V}
(or on
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{V}
), if for any
x\in M
,
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
is projectively equivalent to
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{V}
.

Definition
.

A cone structure
\mathcal{C}
on a manifold
M
is
ruled
if all surfaces
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\subset P(TM)
,
x\in M
are ruled.

If a cone structure is ruled then
\mathcal{C}_{x}
is a union of 2-dimensional subspaces of
T_{x}M
, we shall refer to them as
\alpha
-planes by analogy to the conformal geometry of split signature, where the quadratic cone has two rulings by so-called
\alpha
- and
\beta
-planes (c.f.
[
4
]
). We shall denote by
N_{\mathcal{C}}
the space of
\alpha
-planes defined by a given ruling of
\mathcal{C}
. It follows that
N_{\mathcal{C}}
is a
5
-dimensional manifold. It is a fibered bundle over
M
, with a canonical projection denoted
\pi\colon N_{\mathcal{C}}\to M
. The bundle
N_{\mathcal{C}}
possesses additional rich geometric structure defined by tautologically defined distributions. Namely,

\mathcal{X}(p)=\pi_{*}^{-1}(0),\qquad\mathcal{D}(p)=\pi_{*}^{-1}(p)

where
p\in N_{\mathcal{C}}
is an
\alpha
-plane. In other words,
\mathcal{X}
is a rank-1 distribution tangent to fibers of
\pi
and
\mathcal{D}
is the tautological rank-3 distribution, defined as the lift of
\alpha
-planes. Notice that
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{D}
.

Definition
.

A cone structure is
non-degenerate
if the pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})
is
regular
, i.e.
[\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}]=TN_{\mathcal{C}}
, where
[\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}]
is defined as a distribution spanned by Lie brackets of all sections of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{D}
.

One has the following result which can be deduced directly from
[
18
]
specified to systems of second order equations.

Proposition 1
.

Given a regular pair of distributions
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})
on a 5-dimensional manifold
N
, such that
\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{X}=1
,
\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{D}=3
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{D}
there are local coordinates
t,x^{1},x^{2},p^{1},p^{2}
such that

\mathcal{X}=\operatorname{span}\{X_{F}\},\quad\mathcal{V}=\operatorname{span}\{X_{F},\partial_{p^{1}},\partial_{p^{2}}\}

where

X_{F}=\partial_{t}+p^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}+p^{2}\partial_{p^{2}}+F^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}+F^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}

for some functions
F^{1},F^{2}\colon N\to\mathbb{R}
if and only if
\mathcal{D}
possesses an integrable co-rank 1 sub-distribution
\mathcal{V}
such that
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Indeed, if the pair is regular and
\mathcal{D}
possesses an integrable co-rank 1 sub-distribution then necessarily
\mathcal{D}
satisfies assumptions of of
[
18
]
and consequently it is locally equivalent to the canonical distribution on the space of 1-jets. Additionally, since
\mathcal{X}
is not contained in the integrable sub-distribution (since
[\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}]=TN
) it has to be of the form
X_{F}
for some choice of functions
F^{1}
and
F^{2}
.
∎

The coordinates of Theorem establish local equivalence of
N
with the space
J^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
of jets of functions
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In this context the pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})
, or equivalently
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
, encodes a system of second order ODEs. Indeed,
\mathcal{X}
is spanned by a total derivative vector field and
\mathcal{V}
is tangent to a fiber of a natural projection
J^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})\to J^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Definition
.

A ruled cone structure
\mathcal{C}
is
integrable
if the associated pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
on
N_{\mathcal{C}}
is regular and
\mathcal{D}
possesses integrable sub-distribution
\mathcal{V}
.

Remark
.

In
[
14
]
, the pairs
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})
satisfying conditions given in Proposition
1
are called regular pairs of
equation type
. In particular, any integrable cone structure gives rise to a regular pair of equation type.

Remark
.

Notice that the existence of the integrable sub-distribution
\mathcal{V}
in
\mathcal{D}
on
N
implies that
N
is equipped with a 2-dimensional foliation. In the case of cone structures the leaves of the foliation are transverse to the fibers of the bundle
N_{\mathcal{C}}\to M
and the projection of each leaf is, by the construction, tangent to
\alpha
-planes. We get 3-dimensional space
T
of sub-manifolds in
M
tangent to all
\alpha
-planes, which are called
\alpha
-manifolds by the analogy, again, to the conformal case. This leads to the following double fibration, twistorial-like, picture

M\longleftarrow N_{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow T

where the twistor space
T
is the space of leaves of
\mathcal{V}
, or the space of
\alpha
-manifolds in
M
. Conversely, any such a family of
\alpha
-manifolds gives rise to a foliation of
N_{\mathcal{C}}
. By Proposition
1
these are exactly the ruled cone structures of equation type and
T
can be locally identified with the space
J^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
of
0
-jests.

2.2.
Systems of ODEs.

We shall concentrate on systems of two second-order ODEs, given in the form

\ddot{x}^{i}=F^{i}(t,x,\dot{x}),\quad i=1,2.

Systems of this form are encoded by pairs
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
described in Proposition
1
. Explicitly,

\mathcal{X}=\operatorname{span}\{X_{F}\},\quad\mathcal{V}=\operatorname{span}\{\partial_{p^{1}},\partial_{p^{2}}\}

where, as before,
X_{F}=\partial_{t}+p^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}+p^{2}\partial_{p^{2}}+F^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}+F^{2}\partial_{x^{2}}
.
The general equivalence problem for systems of second-order ODEs was solved in
[
6
]
, where a Cartan connection is assigned to the system. The invariants are of two kinds: torsion and curvature. In coordinates, they are given by the following formulas

(8)

T^{i}_{j}=F^{i}_{j}-\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}\delta^{i}_{j}F^{k}_{k}},\qquad\qquad R^{i}_{jkl}=F^{i}_{jkl}-\textstyle{\frac{3}{4}}F^{r}_{r(jk}\delta^{i}_{l)}

where
1\leq i,j,k,l\leq 2
and

(9)

F^{i}_{j}=\textstyle{-\partial_{x^{j}}F^{i}+\frac{1}{2}X_{F}(\partial_{p^{j}}F^{i})-\frac{1}{4}\partial_{p^{k}}F^{i}\partial_{p^{j}}F^{k},}\quad F^{i}_{jkl}=\partial_{p^{j}}\partial_{p^{k}}\partial_{p^{l}}F^{i}.

It is important to stress that both
T^{i}_{j}
and
R^{i}_{jkl}
are defined only up to multiplication by a function. In fact,
T=(T^{i}_{j})
is a section of
S^{2}(\mathcal{X}^{*})\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})
, where
S^{2}
denotes the bundle of symmetric 2-tensors. We will denote by
T^{X}
the evaluation of
T
on a section
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{X})
.

Remark
.

The torsion
T^{X}
can be interpreted in terms of sections of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{V}
. Namely, we say that a section
X
of
\mathcal{X}
is
projective
, and a frame
(V_{1},V_{2})
of
\mathcal{V}
is
normal
(cf.
[
14
]
), if

(10)

[X,[X,V_{i}]]=T_{i}^{j}V_{j}\mod\mathcal{X},

for certain coefficients
T^{i}_{j}
such that
T^{1}_{1}+T^{2}_{2}=0
. It turns out that these coefficients are the components of the torsion in the basis
(V_{1},V_{2})
, i.e.
T^{i}_{j}
in (
10
) are linear combinations of
T^{i}_{j}
in (
8
). As a matter of fact, formula (
10
) can be taken as an equivalent definition of the torsion. If
X
and
fX
are two projective vector fields, then the transformation rule for the corresponding torsion coefficients is
T^{fX}\mapsto f^{2}T^{X}
, which justifies the aforementioned interpretation of
T
as a section of
S^{2}(\mathcal{X}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})
. Projective vector fields are only defined up to the action of Möbius transformations

t\mapsto\frac{at+b}{ct+d}

which act on the parameterizations of projective vector fields. Consequently, the projective vector fields form a 2-parameter family of vector fields along an integral curve of
\mathcal{X}
. Indeed, it is directly verified (see e.g.
[
14
]
for details) that two vector fields
X
and
fX
are projective if and only if

(11)

fX^{2}(f)-2X(f)^{2}=0.

Moreover, when rewritten in terms of parameterizations of integral curves of
X
and
fX
, equation (
11
) is equivalent to the vanishing of the Schwarzian derivative of a natural parameter of
fX
with respect to a natural parameter of
X
.

Later, in Theorem
1
, we shall deal with systems with nonvanishing torsion. We use two differential operators,
\nabla
and
\hat{\mathbb{S}}
, whose definitions are given in
[
13
]
(see also
[
14
]
for a more detailed exposition). The first is an operator

\nabla\colon\Gamma(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}))\to\mathcal{X}^{*}\otimes\Gamma(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})),

which can be interpreted as a partial connection on the bundle of endomorphisms of the vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
, acting in the direction of
\mathcal{X}
. If a normal frame is fixed and
A\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}))
is represented by a matrix
A^{i}_{j}
in this frame, then
\nabla_{X}A
is represented by the matrix
X(A^{i}_{j})
. Later on, we shall apply
\nabla
to the torsion. Although the torsion is only defined up to multiplication by a positive function, the condition
\nabla T^{X}=\phi T^{X}
is well defined, where
\phi
is a smooth function.

The second is a second-order operator which, for any projective vector field
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{X})
, is a mapping

\hat{\mathbb{S}}^{X}\colon\Gamma(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}))\to\Gamma(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})),

defined as

\hat{\mathbb{S}}^{X}(A)=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{2}_{X}A\otimes A+A\otimes\nabla^{2}_{X}A)-\frac{5}{4}\nabla_{X}A\otimes\nabla_{X}A.

It is proven in
[
13
]
(see also
[
14
]
for a more general exposition) that
\hat{\mathbb{S}}^{X}(T^{X})
is a well defined section of
S^{6}(\mathcal{X}^{*})\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})
.

Proposition 2
.

If
\nabla_{X}T^{X}=\phi T^{X}
for some function
\phi
and
\hat{\mathbb{S}}^{X}(T^{X})=0
then there exists a normal frame of
\mathcal{V}
and a projective vector field of
\mathcal{X}
such that the corresponding torsion matrix is constant.

Proof.

Let
X
be a projective vector field and let
(V_{1},V_{2})
be a normal basis, so that equation (
10
) holds. Then, for any section
A\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}))
with the matrix
(A^{i}_{j})
in the basis
(V_{1},V_{1})
we have
\nabla_{X}(A)
is a section of
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})
with the matrix
X(A^{i}_{j})
in the same basis
(V_{1},V_{2})
. Consequently,
\nabla_{X}T^{X}=\phi T^{X}
implies that
X(T^{i}_{j})=\phi T^{i}_{j}
, where
T^{i}_{j}
are torsion coefficients of
T^{X}
in the basis
(V_{1},V_{2})
. It follows that in the basis
(V_{1},V_{2})
,
T^{X}=(T^{i}_{j})
is constant up to a multiplication by some function, i.e.

T^{X}=\psi\tilde{T}

where
\tilde{T}
satisfies
X(\tilde{T})=0
in the basis of
(V_{1},V_{2})
. Consequently,
\nabla_{X}\tilde{T}=0
and we get that

\hat{\mathbb{S}}^{X}(T^{X})=\hat{\mathbb{S}}^{X}(\psi\tilde{T}^{X})=\left(\psi X^{2}(\psi)-\frac{5}{4}X(\psi)^{2}\right)\tilde{T}\otimes\tilde{T}.

On the other hand, by assumption we have
\hat{\mathbb{S}}^{X}(T^{X})=0
. Hence, we get
\psi X^{2}(\psi)-\frac{5}{4}X(\psi)^{2}=0
. This is equivalent to equation (
11
) with
f=\sqrt{\psi^{-1}}
, as can be directly verified. Furthermore, the equation is equivalent to the fact that parameterizations of
X
and
fX
are related by a Möbius transformation, which means that
fX
is also a projective vector field. Finally, the transformation rule
T^{fX}=f^{2}T^{X}=f^{2}\psi\tilde{T}=\tilde{T}
proves that
T^{fX}
is constant.
∎

3.
Results

We shall assume that a cone structure
\mathcal{C}
on a manifold
M
is modeled on the surface defined either by (
1
) or (

). The cones in the tangent bundle are equivalently described by the following equations

(12)

\arctan\left(\frac{x^{3}}{x^{1}}\right)=\operatorname{arctanh}\left(\frac{x^{4}}{x^{2}}\right),

or, respectively,

(13)

\ln\left(\frac{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}{(x^{3})^{2}+(x^{4})^{2}}\right)=2\arctan\left(\frac{x^{1}x^{4}+x^{2}x^{3}}{x^{2}x^{4}-x^{1}x^{3}}\right)

where
(x^{1},\ldots,x^{4})
are linear coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Indeed, in the first case we assign
(x:y:z:1)=(x^{2}:x^{1}:x^{4}:x^{3})
to get equivalence of (
1
) and (

Neumann scalar determinants
on constant curvature disks

Abstract




Working in the
\zeta
-function regularisation scheme, we find certain infinite series representations of the logarithms of massive scalar determinants,
\det(\Delta+m^{2})
for arbitrary
m^{2}
, on finite round disks of constant curvature (
R=\frac{2\eta}{L^{2}},\eta=0,\pm 1
) with Neumann boundary conditions. The derivation of these representations relies on a relation between the Neumann determinants on the disks and the corresponding Dirichlet determinants via the determinants of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps on the boundaries of the disks. We corroborate the results in an appendix by computing the Neumann determinants in an alternative way. In the cases of disks with nonzero curvatures, we show that the infinite series representations reduce to exact expressions for some specific values of
m^{2}
, viz.
m^{2}=-\frac{\eta}{L^{2}}q(q+1)
with
q\in\mathbb{N}
. Our analysis uses and extends the results obtained in
[
1
]
for similar Dirichlet determinants on constant curvature disks.

1
Introduction

The evaluation of functional determinants of differential operators such as Laplacians (and their generalisations) defined on different manifolds has a long and rich history. Such determinants show up in a variety of physical problems including problems in quantum field theory and quantum gravity
[
2
,
3
]
. For instance, in case of a free massive scalar field on a closed
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, the partition function is given by

Z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det(\Delta+m^{2})}},

(1.1)

where
\Delta
is the positive Laplacian for the background metric and
m
is the mass of the scalar field. Computing such a determinant requires working in some regularisation scheme. The
\zeta
-function regularisation
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
is a convenient scheme for doing such computations and it has been employed extensively in the literature (see, for example
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
). When the manifold has boundaries, one needs to impose some boundary conditions on the field. For Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions, the partition function is given by the same expression as in (
1.1
) and the determinant in this expression can still be evaluated in the
\zeta
-function regularisation scheme, but its value depends on the specific boundary conditions.

Such determinants on manifolds with boundaries have been explored for spaces with negative curvature in the infinite volume limit, particularly in the context of holography (see, for example,
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
and their references). At finite volume, they are, in general, difficult to evaluate. However, in two dimensions, when
m^{2}=0
, the problem becomes remarkably simple as the determinant is related to a conformal invariant
[
15
]
. There are also several results for massive determinants on two dimensional Riemannian surfaces with geodesic boundaries (see, for instance,
[
16
,
17
]
).
When the boundary of the surface is not a geodesic and the mass is nonzero, evaluating the determinants is typically challenging. Nonetheless, for sufficiently simple geometries one can make progress.

A particular instance of such simple geometries is a round disk
\mathscr{D}
with constant bulk curvature which is described by the metric

\ ds_{\eta}^{2}=e^{2\sigma_{\eta}}(dr^{2}+r^{2}d\theta^{2}),\ r\in[0,1],\ \theta\sim\theta+2\pi,

(1.2)

where
\eta
can take the values
0
,
+1
or
-1
, and accordingly,

\begin{split}&e^{2\sigma_{0}}=\Big(\frac{\ell_{0}}{2\pi}\Big)^{2},\\
&e^{2\sigma_{+}}=\frac{4r_{0}^{2}L^{2}}{(1+r_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}}\ \text{with}\ r_{0}\in(0,\infty),\\
&e^{2\sigma_{-}}=\frac{4r_{0}^{2}L^{2}}{(1-r_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}}\ \text{with}\ r_{0}\in(0,1).\end{split}

(1.3)

Here
\ell_{0}
and
L
are positive quantities with the dimension of length. The Neumann determinants on these disks will be the objects of our study in this work. We provide a few geometrical properties of these disks below that will be useful for the analysis done later in the paper. The length of the boundary (
\partial\mathscr{D}
) of the disk is given by

\begin{split}&\ell_{\eta}=\begin{cases}\ell_{0}\ \ \ \ \text{for}\ \eta=0,\\
\frac{4\pi r_{0}L}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\ \text{for}\ \eta=\pm 1,\end{cases}\end{split}

(1.4)

while the area of the disk is given by

\begin{split}&A_{\eta}=\begin{cases}\frac{\ell_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\ \ \ \ \ \text{for}\ \eta=0,\\
\frac{4\pi r_{0}^{2}L^{2}}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\ \text{for}\ \eta=\pm 1.\end{cases}\end{split}

(1.5)

The constant bulk curvature scalar of the disk is
R_{\eta}=2\eta/L^{2}
, while the extrinsic curvature at the boundary
\partial\mathscr{D}
is

k_{\eta}=\begin{cases}\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}}\ \ \ \ \text{for}\ \eta=0,\\
\frac{1-\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}L}\ \text{for}\ \eta=\pm 1.\end{cases}

(1.6)

We will denote the positive Laplacian corresponding to the disk by
\Delta_{\eta}
, and it is given by

\Delta_{\eta}=-e^{-2\sigma_{\eta}}\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\Big).

(1.7)

For the above constant curvature disks, the Dirichlet determinant
{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})
was evaluated in
[
1
]
using
\zeta
-function regularisation. For general values of the mass, the logarithm of this determinant was expressed as a convergent infinite series. Furthermore, for some special values of
m^{2}
, viz,
m^{2}=-\frac{\eta}{L^{2}}q(q+1)
with
q\in\mathbb{N}
, exact expressions were obtained in terms of certain elementary functions and the Euler Gamma function. These expressions were found to be useful in the analysis of Liouville and Jackiw-Teitelboim gravities
[
18
]
.

In this paper we will extend the above results by computing the Neumann determinants of
(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})
on the constant curvature round disks defined by the metrics given in (
1.2
). To do such a computation, one may follow the strategy discussed in
[
1
]
while adapting it to the altered boundary condition, i.e. Neumann instead of Dirichlet. This would involve exploiting the rotational symmetry of the geometry to first find the determinants of Sturm-Liouville operators corresponding to each Fourier mode (for the angular coordinate
\theta
) of the scalar field, and then obtaining the determinant of
(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})
from these Sturm-Liouville determinants.
We will present this procedure in an appendix. However, in the main text of this paper we will follow a different approach that is somewhat shorter. This approach relies on a relation between the Neumann and Dirichlet determinants which involves the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map on the boundary of the disk. Such relations were recently derived in
[
19
]
for more general setups (in arbitrary dimensions and for general metrics). We will show that in the case of the constant curvature disks this relation is particulary simple. Given this relation and the fact that the Dirichlet determinants have already been evaluated in
[
1
]
, the computation of the Neumann determinants would require just the evaluation of the determinants of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps. Performing this computation, we will derive an infinite series representation of
\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})
for general values of
m^{2}
, and verify that this matches with the result obtained by following the strategy laid out in
[
1
]
. In case of the curved disks
(\eta=\pm 1)
, we will then obtain exact expressions of the Neumann determinants for
m^{2}=-\frac{\eta}{L^{2}}q(q+1),\ q\in\mathbb{N}
, that are analogous to those obtained for the Dirichlet determinants in
[
1
]
.

In an appendix we will also consider an example where the determinant with arbitrary mass can be evaluated exactly. This example corresponds to the positive curvature disk that is a hemisphere. In this case the boundary is a geodesic, and hence we can make a connection with the results in existing literature (in particular,
[
17
]
). We will derive a very simple expression for the Neumann determinant (for arbitrary
m^{2}
) on the hemisphere in terms of the Barnes
G
-function that is analogous to the expression of the Dirichlet determinant on the hemisphere evaluated in
[
1
]
. We will also show that this determinant vanishes for the special values of
m^{2}
that we mentioned above, i.e. for
m^{2}=-\frac{1}{L^{2}}q(q+1),\ q\in\mathbb{N}
.

The organisation of the paper is as follows:

In section
2
we introduce the aforementioned relation between the Neumann and the Dirichlet determinants via the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map. In section
3
we evaluate the determinants of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps for the constant curvature disks. In section
4
, we put together everything and use the Dirichlet determinants derived in
[
1
]
to obtain the infinite series representations of the logarithms of the Neumann determinants for arbitrary masses. In section
5
we derive the aforementioned exact expressions for the Neumann determinants on the disks with nonzero curvature for the special values of the mass, i.e.
m^{2}=-\frac{\eta}{L^{2}}q(q+1),\ q\in\mathbb{N}
. In appendix
A
, for the sake of completeness, we review some arguments for the positivity of the Laplacian with Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions, as well as the positivity of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map. In appendix
B
, we derive the behaviour of the lowest eigenvalue of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map as
m^{2}\rightarrow 0
which is required for the discussion in section
2
. In appendix
C
we verify the infinite series representations obtained in section
4
through an alternative derivation of the Neumann determinants. In appendix
D
, we find an exact expression of the Neumann determinant on a hemisphere for arbitrary mass and show that it vanishes when
m^{2}=-\frac{1}{L^{2}}q(q+1)
with
q\in\mathbb{N}
.

2
Relation between the Neumann and Dirichlet determinants via the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map

In this section we will introduce the result derived in
[
19
]
that relates the difference between the Neumann determinant and the Dirichlet determinant to the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for manifolds with a boundary in arbitrary dimensions. We will then show that this relation is particularly simple for the manifolds of our interest, viz. two-dimensional disks of constant curvature.

2.1
The relation for general values of the mass

Consider a
d
-dimensional smooth compact Riemannian manifold
\mathscr{M}
with a connected boundary
\partial\mathscr{M}
. Let us denote the metric on
\mathscr{M}
by
g_{\mu\nu}
and the induced metric on
\partial\mathscr{M}
by
h_{ab}
. The positive Laplacian corresponding to
g_{\mu\nu}
is
\Delta=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu})
. The eigenvalues of
\Delta
would depend on the boundary condition. Accordingly, we denote the eigenvalues for Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions by
\lambda_{i}^{(\text{D})}
and
\lambda_{i}^{(\text{N})}
respectively, with the index
i
running over all non-negative integers. These eigenvalues are ordered such that
\lambda_{i}^{(\text{D})}\leq\lambda_{i+1}^{(\text{D})}
and
\lambda_{i}^{(\text{N})}\leq\lambda_{i+1}^{(\text{N})}
. Note that there can be degeneracies in the eigenvalues and in that case, the same value will be assigned to multipe
\lambda_{i+1}^{(\text{D}/\text{N})}
’s.

Using these eigenvalues, one can define the following zeta functions:

\zeta^{(\text{D})}(m^{2};s)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{i}^{(\text{D})}+m^{2})^{s}},\ \zeta^{(\text{N})}(m^{2};s)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{i}^{(\text{N})}+m^{2})^{s}}.

(2.1)

These series representations of the above
\zeta
-functions are valid only when the real part of
s
is sufficiently large, but the functions themselves can be meromorphically extended to the rest of the complex plane, with these extended functions being regular at
s=0
. We will implicitly assume this to be true also for all the other
\zeta
-functions that will be introduced later in this paper.

The
\zeta
-regularised Dirichlet and Neumann determinants of
(\Delta+m^{2})
are defined in terms of the above
\zeta
-functions by the following relations:

\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta+m^{2})=-\partial_{s}\zeta^{(\text{D})}(m^{2};s)|_{s=0},\ \ln{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta+m^{2})=-\partial_{s}\zeta^{(\text{N})}(m^{2};s)|_{s=0}.

(2.2)

Now, let us introduce the notion of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map which is a pseudo-differential operator acting on functions at the boundary. Suppose
f
is a smooth function defined on
\partial\mathscr{M}
. We can define a smooth extension of this function to the bulk, which we denote by
H_{m^{2}}[f]
, that satisfies the following conditions:

(\Delta+m^{2})H_{m^{2}}[f]=0,\ H_{m^{2}}[f]|_{\partial\mathscr{M}}=f.

(2.3)

The Dirichlet-to-Neumann map, denoted by
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
, is defined via the following action on
f
:

\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}[f]=\partial_{n}H_{m^{2}}[f]|_{\partial\mathscr{M}},

(2.4)

where
\partial_{n}
is the derivative along the outward-pointing unit normal at the boundary. Suppose the eigenvalues of this operator are
v_{i}^{(m^{2})}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, which we again choose to be arranged in an ascending order. We can define a
\zeta
-function with these eigenvalues as follows:

\zeta^{(\text{DN})}(m^{2};s)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(v_{i}^{(m^{2})})^{s}}.

(2.5)

The
\zeta
-regularised determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
is then defined by

\ln{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})=-\partial_{s}\zeta^{(\text{DN})}(m^{2};s)|_{s=0}.

(2.6)

Now, the relation between the determinants defined in (
2.2
) and (
2.6
) that was proven in
[
19
]
is as follows:

\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta+m^{2})-\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta+m^{2})=\sum_{j=0}^{\nu}(m^{2})^{j}b_{j}+\ln{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}),

(2.7)

where
\nu=[\frac{d-1}{2}]
.
1
1

1

[x]
stands for the greatest integer that is less than or equal to
x
.
Here the coefficients
b_{j}
are independent of
m^{2}
, and depend only on the geometry of the space. They are determined by the coefficients in the expansions of the Dirichlet and Neumann heat kernels corresponding to the bulk Laplacian
\Delta
as well as the coefficients in the large
m
-expansion of the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
[
20
]
. The physical significance of relations of the form (
2.7
) will be discussed in
[
21
]
.

Note that when
d=2
, which is the case of our interest, we have
\nu=0
. Thus, for two-dimensional manifolds with connected boundaries, the relation (
2.7
) reduces to

\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta+m^{2})-\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta+m^{2})=b_{0}+\ln{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}).

(2.8)

As we mentioned earlier,
b_{0}
is independent of the mass. So, it can be evaluated by comparing the determinants in (
2.8
) as
m^{2}\rightarrow 0
. .This massless limit was also considered in
[
22
]
and a general form of the quantity
b_{0}
was found for arbitrary two-dimensional surfaces with a boundary. In what follows, we will present a derivation of
b_{0}
for the constant curvature disks and verify that our result matches with that of
[
22
]
.

2.2
The massless limit

To consider the massless limit, let us first note that for the Neumann boundary condition, the lowest eigenvalue of
\Delta_{\eta}
is zero
2
2

2

The other eigenvalues are positive-definite as shown in appendix
A
.
, and the corresponding eigenfunction is the constant function on the disk
\mathscr{D}
. Therefore, in this case, the lowest eigenvalue of the operator
(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})
is
m^{2}
, and it goes to zero as
m^{2}\rightarrow 0
. Similarly, as argued in appendix
B
, the lowest eigenvalue of
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
goes to zero when
m^{2}\rightarrow 0
and for small values of
m^{2}
, it is given by

v_{0}^{(m^{2})}=\frac{m^{2}A_{\eta}}{\ell_{\eta}}+O(m^{4}).

(2.9)

The vanishing of these eigenvalues in the
m^{2}\rightarrow 0
limit means that
\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})
and
\ln{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})
diverge in this limit. However, one can regularise these divergences by removing the contributions of
\ln(\lambda_{0}^{(\text{N})}+m^{2})=\ln(m^{2})
and
\ln v_{0}^{(m^{2})}\approx\ln\Big(\frac{m^{2}A_{\eta}}{\ell_{\eta}}\Big)
as shown below:

\begin{split}&\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})=\lim_{m^{2}\rightarrow 0}\Big[\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})-\ln(m^{2})\Big],\\
&\ln{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})=\lim_{m^{2}\rightarrow 0}\Big[\ln{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})-\ln\Big(\frac{m^{2}A_{\eta}}{\ell_{\eta}}\Big)\Big].\end{split}

(2.10)

This is actually equivalent to removing the lowest eigenvalues while defining the Neumann
\zeta
-function for the Laplacian (see (
2.1
)) and the
\zeta
-function for the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map (see (
2.5
)) in the massless case. The quantities in (
2.10
) are then given by the derivatives of these
\zeta
-functions just as shown in (
2.2
) and (
2.6
).

Let us note that the difference of
\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})
and
\ln{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})
has a well-defined limit as
m^{2}\rightarrow 0
which is given by

\lim_{m^{2}\rightarrow 0}\Big[\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})-\ln{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})\Big]=\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})-\ln{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})-\ln\Big(\frac{A_{\eta}}{\ell_{\eta}}\Big).

(2.11)

Moreover, the logarithm of Dirichlet determinant,
\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})
, has a well-defined limit as
m^{2}\rightarrow 0
because the Dirichlet boundary condition does not allow for a nonzero constant mode and consequently there is no zero eigenvalue of the Laplacian.

Combining all of the above observations, we can take the limit
m^{2}\rightarrow 0
in equation (
2.8
) to obtain the following expression for
b_{0}
:

b_{0}=\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})-\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta_{\eta})-\ln{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})-\ln\Big(\frac{A_{\eta}}{\ell_{\eta}}\Big).

(2.12)

To evaluate this quantity for the different values of
\eta
, let us note that each of these constant curvature disks is related by a Weyl transformation to a flat disk of unit radius as is manifest from the form of the metrics given in (
1.2
). We will indicate all quantities related to this flat disk of unit radius with a subscript
\delta
. Now, in
[
15
]
it was shown that there are two Weyl-invariant quantities which can be constructed out of the massless determinants
{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta)
and
{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta)
. In our case, these two Weyl-invariants imply

\begin{split}&\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})-\ln(A_{\eta})+\frac{S_{L}[\sigma_{\eta}]}{12\pi}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\eta}\ k_{\eta}\\
&=\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\delta})-\ln(A_{\delta})-\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\delta}\ k_{\delta},\\
\end{split}

(2.13)

and

\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta_{\eta})+\frac{S_{L}[\sigma_{\eta}]}{12\pi}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\eta}\ k_{\eta}=\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta_{\delta})+\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\delta}\ k_{\delta},

(2.14)

where
S_{L}[\sigma_{\eta}]
is the Liouville action defined as

S_{L}[\sigma_{\eta}]=\int_{\mathscr{D}}d^{2}x\ \partial_{\mu}\sigma_{\eta}\partial_{\mu}\sigma_{\eta}+2\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\delta}\ \sigma_{\eta}=2\pi\int_{0}^{1}dr\ r(\partial_{r}\sigma_{\eta})^{2}+4\pi\sigma_{\eta}|_{r=1}.

(2.15)

Here,
ds_{\eta}
and
ds_{\delta}
are the measures for the arc-length at the boundary
\partial\mathscr{D}
for the respective metrics so that
\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\eta}=\ell_{\eta}
and
\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\delta}=2\pi
.
k_{\eta}
is the extrinsic curvature at the boundary of the constant curvature disk. Its value is given in (
1.7
). Similarly,
k_{\delta}=1
is the extrinsic curvature at the boundary of the flat disk with unit radius.
A_{\delta}=\pi
is the area of the flat disk with unit radius.

Combining the relations in (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), we get

\begin{split}\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})-\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta_{\eta})-\ln A_{\eta}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\eta}\ k_{\eta}\\
=\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\delta})-\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta_{\delta})-\ln A_{\delta}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\delta}\ k_{\delta}.\end{split}

(2.16)

The values of
{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\delta})
and
{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta_{\delta})
were computed in
[
15
]
, and they are given by

\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\delta})=-\frac{1}{6}\ln(2)+\frac{1}{2}\ln(\pi)-2\zeta_{\text{R}}^{\prime}(-1)+\frac{7}{12},

(2.17)

\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta_{\delta})=-\frac{1}{6}\ln(2)-\frac{1}{2}\ln(\pi)-2\zeta_{\text{R}}^{\prime}(-1)-\frac{5}{12},

(2.18)

where
\zeta_{\text{R}}^{\prime}(-1)
denotes the derivative of the Riemann zeta function evaluated at
-1
. Combining these expressions and subtracting the values of
\ln A_{\delta}
and
\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\delta}\ k_{\delta}
, we get

\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\delta})-\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta_{\delta})-\ln A_{\delta}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\delta}\ k_{\delta}=0.

(2.19)

Hence, from (
2.16
), we find that

\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})-\ln{\det}_{\text{D}}(\Delta_{\eta})-\ln A_{\eta}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\eta}\ k_{\eta}.

(2.20)

Finally, we also need the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
\mathscr{O}_{0}
to complete the computation of
b_{0}
given in (
2.12
). For this determinant, we use the general result proven in
[
16
]
that in case of 2-dimensional surfaces with a connected boundary, the ratio of the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map (
{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})
) with the length of the boundary is a Weyl-invariant. It was also shown in
[
23
,
16
]
that for the conformal class of the flat disk, this Weyl-invariant is just
1
. Hence, for the constant curvature disks of our interest, we have

\ln{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})=\ln\ell_{\eta}.

(2.21)

We will verify this explicitly in section
3
.

Using the equations (
2.20
) and (
2.21
), we get the following value of
b_{0}
from (
2.12
):

b_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}}ds_{\eta}\ k_{\eta}=\frac{k_{\eta}\ell_{\eta}}{2\pi}=\begin{cases}1\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for}\ \eta=0,\\
1-\frac{\eta A_{\eta}}{2\pi L^{2}}\ \text{for}\ \eta=\pm 1.\end{cases}

(2.22)

We note that this is exactly the value of
b_{0}
that can be obtained from Proposition 2.2 in
[
22
]
.

3
Evaluation of the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map

Having introduced the relation (
2.8
) and computed the quantity
b_{0}
for the constant curvature disks, let us now evaluate the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
.

3.1
Flat case

Let us first consider the flat case, i.e.
\eta=0
. To find the eigenvalues of the operator
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
in this case, we need to find (real) solutions to the Steklov problem,

(\Delta_{0}+m^{2})\Phi=0,\ \partial_{n}\Phi|_{\partial\mathscr{D}}=\varv\Phi|_{\partial\mathscr{D}},

(3.1)

for some
\varv\geq 0
. The normal derivative at the boundary and the Laplacian are given in the polar coordinates introduced in (
1.2
) by

\partial_{n}=e^{-\sigma_{0}}\partial_{r}\Big|_{\partial\mathscr{D}}=\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}}\partial_{r}\Big|_{\partial\mathscr{D}},\ \Delta_{0}=-e^{-2\sigma_{0}}\Big[\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\Big]=-\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\ell_{0}^{2}}\Big[\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\Big].

(3.2)

To find the solutions to the above problem, let us decompose the field
\Phi
into its Fourier modes:

\Phi(r,\theta)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi_{j}(r)e^{ij\theta}.

(3.3)

Since we are looking for real solutions, we demand that
\Phi_{j}(r)^{*}=\Phi_{-j}(r)
. Moreover, let
\varphi(\theta)=\Phi(1,\theta)
be the boundary value of
\Phi
. The Fourier decomposition of this boundary field is
\varphi

\varphi(\theta)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}e^{ij\theta},

(3.4)

where
\varphi_{j}=\Phi_{j}(1)
. Then the Steklov problem posed in (
3.1
) reduces to seeking solutions to the equation

\Phi_{j}^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{1}{r}\Phi_{j}^{\prime}(r)-\frac{j^{2}}{r^{2}}\Phi_{j}(r)=\frac{m^{2}\ell_{0}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\Phi_{j}(r),

(3.5)

with the boundary condition

\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}}\Phi_{j}^{\prime}(1)=\varv\varphi_{j},\ \text{for all}\ j\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.6)

The solution to the differential equation (
3.5
) that is regular at
r=0
is

\Phi_{j}(r)=c_{j}J_{j}\Big(\frac{im\ell_{0}}{2\pi}r\Big),

(3.7)

for some
c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
with the reality of
\Phi
requiring
c_{j}^{*}=c_{-j}
when
m^{2}>0
. Here
J_{j}
is the Bessel function of order
j
. The boundary condition (
3.6
) can be satisfied by this solution only if

c_{j}\propto(\delta_{j,p}+\delta_{j,-p})\ \text{or}\ c_{j}\propto i(\delta_{j,p}-\delta_{j,-p})

(3.8)

for some
p\in\mathbb{Z}
with the proportionality constant being a real number. Accordingly, the independent real solutions to the Steklov problem can be enumerated as follows:

\Phi^{(p)}(r,\theta)=\begin{cases}i^{p}J_{p}\Big(\frac{im\ell_{0}}{2\pi}r\Big)\cos(p\theta)\ \text{when}\ p\geq 0,\\
i^{p}J_{p}\Big(\frac{im\ell_{0}}{2\pi}r\Big)\sin(p\theta)\ \text{when}\ p<0,\end{cases}\\

(3.9)

with
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. The corresponding values of
\varv
are given by

\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})=\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}}\frac{\frac{d}{dr}[J_{p}(\frac{im\ell_{0}}{2\pi}r)]|_{r=1}}{J_{p}(\frac{im\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}=\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}}\Big[-p+\frac{im\ell_{0}}{2\pi}\frac{J_{p-1}(\frac{im\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}{J_{p}(\frac{im\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}\Big].

(3.10)

One can check that
\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})
is symmetric under
p\rightarrow-p
by using the identities
J_{-n}(z)=(-1)^{n}J_{n}(z)
and
J_{n}(z)=\frac{2(n-1)}{z}J_{n-1}(z)-J_{n-2}(z)
that are valid for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, we can express
\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})
as

\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})=\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}}\Big[-|p|+\frac{im\ell_{0}}{2\pi}\frac{J_{|p|-1}(\frac{im\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}{J_{|p|}(\frac{im\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}\Big].

(3.11)

These are the eigenvalues of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
. They can be re-arranged in an ascending order to express them as
v_{k}^{(m^{2})}
with
k\geq 0
, the notation we had introduced earlier. However, from now on we will stick to the notation
\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})
with
p\in\mathbb{Z}
as it is more convenient for the present discussion.

Let us note here that massless limits of these eigenvalues are readily obtained as follows:

\varv^{(p)}(0)=\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}}\Big[-|p|+\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\Big\{ix\frac{J_{|p|-1}(ix)}{J_{|p|}(ix)}\Big\}\Big]=\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}}\Big[-|p|+2|p|\Big]=\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}}|p|.

(3.12)

From the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
and
\mathscr{O}_{0}
given above, one can obtain the following infinite series representation of the logarithm of the ratio of the determinants of these operators:

\ln\Big(\frac{{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})}{{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})}\Big)=\ln\Big(\varv^{(0)}(m^{2})\Big)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\ln\Big(\frac{\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})}{\varv^{(p)}(0)}\Big).

(3.13)

The convergence of this series can be checked by using the asymptotic expansion of the Bessel function at large order which is

\begin{split}J_{\nu}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\Big[\nu+\nu\ln(\frac{z}{2})-(\nu+\frac{1}{2})\ln\nu\Big]\Big[1-\frac{3z^{2}+1}{12\nu}+O(\frac{1}{\nu^{2}})\Big].\end{split}

(3.14)

The above asymptotic expansion of the Bessel function yields
\frac{\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})}{\varv^{(p)}(0)}=1+O(1/p^{2})
, and hence,
\ln\Big(\frac{\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})}{\varv^{(p)}(0)}\Big)=O(1/p^{2})
as
p\rightarrow\infty
. This ensures the convergence of the series in (
3.13
).
Substituting the values of
\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})
and
\varv^{(p)}(0)
in (
3.13
), and then using the relation
I_{\alpha}(x)=i^{-\alpha}J_{\alpha}(ix)
where
I_{\alpha}
is a modified Bessel function of the first kind, we get

\ln\Big(\frac{{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})}{{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})}\Big)=\ln\Big(m\frac{I_{-1}(\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}{I_{0}(\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}\Big)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\ln\Big(\frac{\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi}I_{p-1}(\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi})-pI_{p}(\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}{pI_{p}(\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}\Big).

(3.15)

Furthermore, from (
2.21
) we know that
\ln{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})=\ln\ell_{0}
. We can now check this explicitly by considering the following
\zeta
-function:

{\zeta^{(\text{DN})}}^{\prime}(0;s)=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z},p\neq 0}(\varv^{(p)}(0))^{-s}=2(\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}})^{-s}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}p^{-s}=2(\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}})^{-s}\zeta_{\text{R}}(s),

(3.16)

where
\zeta_{\text{R}}
denotes the Riemann zeta function. This yields the expected result for the determinant
{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})
as shown below:

\begin{split}\ln{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})&=-\partial_{s}{\zeta^{(\text{DN})}}^{\prime}(0;s)|_{s=0}=-2\partial_{s}\Big[(\frac{2\pi}{\ell_{0}})^{-s}\zeta_{\text{R}}(s)\Big]\Big|_{s=0}\\
&=-2\Big[\ln(\frac{\ell_{0}}{2\pi})\zeta_{\text{R}}(0)+\zeta_{\text{R}}^{\prime}(0)\Big]=-2\Big[-\frac{1}{2}\ln(\frac{\ell_{0}}{2\pi})-\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi)\Big]=\ln(\ell_{0}).\end{split}

(3.17)

Here we have used the fact that
\zeta_{\text{R}}(0)=-\frac{1}{2}
and
\zeta_{\text{R}}^{\prime}(0)=-\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi)
.

Combining the results obtained in (
3.15
) and (
3.17
), we get the following expression of the determinant of the Dirchlet-to-Neumann map:

\ln{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})=\ln(2\pi)+\ln\Big(\frac{\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi}I_{-1}(\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}{I_{0}(\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}\Big)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\ln\Big(\frac{\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi}I_{p-1}(\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi})-pI_{p}(\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}{pI_{p}(\frac{m\ell_{0}}{2\pi})}\Big).

(3.18)

3.2
Curved cases

Now, let us turn our attention to the curved cases, i.e.
\eta=\pm 1
. Evaluating the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map in these cases is analogous to the same for the flat case. The Steklov problem involves finding real solutions to

(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})\Phi=0,\ \partial_{n}\Phi|_{\partial\mathscr{D}}=\varv\Phi|_{\partial\mathscr{D}},

(3.19)

for some
\varv\geq 0
. The normal derivative at the boundary and the Laplacian are

\begin{split}&\partial_{n}=e^{-\sigma_{\eta}}\partial_{r}\Big|_{\partial\mathscr{D}}=\frac{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}L}\partial_{r}\Big|_{\partial\mathscr{D}},\\
&\Delta_{\eta}=-e^{-2\sigma_{\eta}}\Big[\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\Big]=-\frac{(1+\eta r_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}}{4r_{0}^{2}L^{2}}\Big[\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\Big].\end{split}

(3.20)

To find the solutions to the Steklov problem, one can again decompose the bulk field and its boundary value as shown in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). Then the Steklov problem reduces to finding solutions to the ordinary differential equations

\Phi_{j}^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{1}{r}\Phi_{j}^{\prime}(r)-\frac{j^{2}}{r^{2}}\Phi_{j}(r)=\frac{4r_{0}^{2}m^{2}L^{2}}{(1+\eta r_{0}^{2}r^{2})^{2}}\Phi_{j}(r),

(3.21)

with the boundary condition

\frac{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}L}\Phi_{j}^{\prime}(1)=\varv\varphi_{j},\ \ \text{for all}\ \ j\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.22)

The solution to (
3.21
) that is regular at
r=0
is

\Phi_{j}(r)=c_{j}f_{j}^{(\eta)}(r;-m^{2}L^{2})

(3.23)

where

f_{j}^{(\eta)}(r;\lambda)=r^{|j|}F\Big(\frac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{1+4\eta\lambda}),\frac{1}{2}(1-\sqrt{1+4\eta\lambda}),|j|+1,\frac{\eta r_{0}^{2}r^{2}}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}r^{2}}\Big),

(3.24)

with
F
representing the Gaussian hypergeometric function
\ {}_{2}F_{1}
. Just as in the flat case, the boundary condition (
3.22
) can be satisfied for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
only if the coefficients
\{c_{j}\}
are of the forms given in (
3.8
). Accordingly, the independent real solutions to the above Steklov problem are

\Phi^{(p)}(r,\theta)=\begin{cases}f_{p}^{(\eta)}(r;-m^{2}L^{2})\cos(p\theta)\ \text{when}\ p\geq 0,\\
f_{p}^{(\eta)}(r;-m^{2}L^{2})\sin(p\theta)\ \text{when}\ p<0,\end{cases}\\

(3.25)

with
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. The corresponding values of
\varv
are given by

\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})=\frac{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}L}\frac{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2})}{f_{p}^{(\eta)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})},

(3.26)

where

\begin{split}&g_{p}^{(\eta)}(\lambda)=\frac{\partial}{\partial r}[f_{p}^{(\eta)}(r;\lambda)]|_{r=1}\\
&=|p|F\Big(\frac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{1+4\eta\lambda}),\frac{1}{2}(1-\sqrt{1+4\eta\lambda}),|p|+1,\frac{\eta r_{0}^{2}}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\Big)\\
&\quad-\frac{2\lambda r_{0}^{2}}{(|p|+1)(1+\eta r_{0}^{2})^{2}}F\Big(\frac{1}{2}(3+\sqrt{1+4\eta\lambda}),\frac{1}{2}(3-\sqrt{1+4\eta\lambda}),|p|+2,\frac{\eta r_{0}^{2}}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\Big).\end{split}

(3.27)

The quantities
\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})
with
p\in\mathbb{Z}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
. The massless limits of these eigenvalues are given by

\varv^{(p)}(0)=\frac{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}L}\frac{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(0)}{f_{p}^{(\eta)}(1;0)}=\frac{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}L}|p|.

(3.28)

From these values of
\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})
and
\varv^{(p)}(0)
, we can again obtain an infinite series representation of the logarithm of the ratio of the determinants of
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
and
\mathscr{O}_{0}
which is of the following form:

\ln\Big(\frac{{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})}{{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})}\Big)=\ln\Big(\varv^{(0)}(m^{2})\Big)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\ln\Big(\frac{\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})}{\varv^{(p)}(0)}\Big).

(3.29)

The convergence of this series can be checked by using the following asymptotic form of
F(a,b,c+\lambda,z)
in the large
\lambda
regime
[
24
]
:

F(a,b,c+\lambda,z)=\frac{\Gamma(c+\lambda)}{\Gamma(c+\lambda-b)}\lambda^{-b}\Big[1+O(1/\lambda)\Big].

(3.30)

This gives us the following asymptotic form of
\frac{\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})}{\varv^{(p)}(0)}
in the large
p
regime:

\begin{split}\frac{\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})}{\varv^{(p)}(0)}&=1+\frac{2m^{2}L^{2}r_{0}^{2}}{(1+\eta r_{0}^{2})^{2}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}+O(1/p^{3}).\end{split}

(3.31)

Hence, in this regime
\ln\Big(\frac{\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})}{\varv^{(p)}(0)}\Big)=O(1/p^{2})
which ensures the convergence of the series in (
3.29
).

Substituting
\varv^{(p)}(m^{2})
and
\varv^{(p)}(0)
in (
3.29
) by their values given in (
3.26
) and (
3.28
) respectively, we get

\ln\Big(\frac{{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})}{{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})}\Big)=\ln\Big(\frac{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}L}\Big)+\ln\Big(\frac{g_{0}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2})}{f_{0}^{(\eta)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}\Big)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\ln\Big(\frac{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2})}{pf_{p}^{(\eta)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}\Big).

(3.32)

Now, from (
2.21
), we have that

\ln{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})=\ln\ell_{\eta}=\ln\Big(\frac{4\pi r_{0}L}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\Big).

(3.33)

As in the flat case, we can check this explicitly by defining the
\zeta
-function,

{\zeta^{(\text{DN})}}^{\prime}(0;s)=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z},p\neq 0}(\varv^{(p)}(0))^{-s}=2\Big(\frac{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}L}\Big)^{-s}\zeta_{\text{R}}(s).

(3.34)

This yields the expression of
\ln{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})
in (
3.33
) as shown below:

\begin{split}\ln{\det}^{\prime}(\mathscr{O}_{0})&=-\partial_{s}{\zeta^{(\text{DN})}}^{\prime}(0;s)|_{s=0}=-2\partial_{s}\Big[\Big(\frac{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}L}\Big)^{-s}\zeta_{\text{R}}(s)\Big]\Big|_{s=0}\\
&=-2\Big[\ln\Big(\frac{2r_{0}L}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\Big)\zeta_{\text{R}}(0)+\zeta_{\text{R}}^{\prime}(0)\Big]=-2\Big[-\frac{1}{2}\ln\Big(\frac{2r_{0}L}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi)\Big]\\
&=\ln\Big(\frac{4\pi r_{0}L}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\Big).\end{split}

(3.35)

Combining the expressions in (
3.32
) and (
3.33
), we get

\ln{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})=\ln(2\pi)+\ln\Big(\frac{g_{0}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2})}{f_{0}^{(\eta)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}\Big)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\ln\Big(\frac{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2})}{pf_{p}^{(\eta)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}\Big).

(3.36)

5
The special case of
m^{2}=-\frac{\eta}{L^{2}}q(q+1)
with
q\in\mathbb{N}

In (
4.4
) we obtained infinite series representations of the logarithms of the Neumann determinants on the curved disks corresponding to
\eta=\pm 1
. We will now show that these quantities reduce to some exact expressions for the special cases when
m^{2}=-\frac{\eta}{L^{2}}q(q+1)
with
q\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that for the positive curvature disk, i.e. for
\eta=1
, these values of
m^{2}
are negative. Although our infinite series representations of the logarithms of the Neumann determinants were derived for positive

Having given the above clarification, let us now go on to derive the exact expressions of the Neumann determinants for the special values of the masses mentioned above. To derive these expressions, it is convenient to define

&\quad+\ln\Big[F(1+q,-q,n+1,z_{\eta})-2z_{\eta}(1-z_{\eta})\frac{q(q+1)}{n(n+1)}\ F(2+q,1-q,n+2,z_{\eta})\Big],\end{split}

for

Appendix A
Positivity of the Laplacian and the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map

In this appendix we will show that
\Delta
, for both Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions, is a positive operator. We will also show that the Dirichlet-to Neumann map
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
is a positive operator as well when
m^{2}\geq 0
. These results are well-known but we include their proofs for the sake of clarity and completeness. In what follows we will consider these operators for a smooth
d
-dimensional compact manifold
\mathscr{M}
with a connected boundary
\partial\mathscr{M}
. We will denote the metric on
\mathscr{M}
by
g
and the induced metric on
\partial\mathscr{M}
by
h
.

To show the positivity of
\Delta
, let us define the following inner product on the space of smooth functions satisfying Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions

\langle\psi_{1}|\psi_{2}\rangle=\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\sqrt{g}\psi_{1}\psi_{2}.

(A.1)

Note that for either Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions,
\Delta
is a self-adjoint operator with respect to this inner product as shown below:

\langle\psi_{1}|\Delta|\psi_{2}\rangle=\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\sqrt{g}\psi_{1}\Delta\psi_{2}=\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\sqrt{g}\psi_{2}\Delta\psi_{1}+\oint_{\partial\mathscr{M}}d^{d-1}y\ \sqrt{h}(\psi_{2}\partial_{n}\psi_{1}-\psi_{1}\partial_{n}\psi_{2}).

(A.2)

The last term in the above equation, involving the boundary integral, vanishes both for Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions. So, we get

\langle\psi_{1}|\Delta|\psi_{2}\rangle=\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\sqrt{g}\psi_{2}\Delta\psi_{1}=\langle\psi_{2}|\Delta|\psi_{1}\rangle,

(A.3)

and hence,
\Delta
is a self-adjoint operator. Now, the positivity of
\Delta
can be shown as follows:

\begin{split}\langle\psi|\Delta|\psi\rangle&=-\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\ \psi\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\psi)\\
&=-\oint_{\partial\mathscr{M}}d^{d-1}y\ \sqrt{h}\psi\partial_{n}\psi+\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\ \sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\psi\partial_{\nu}\psi\\
&=\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\ \sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\psi\partial_{\nu}\psi\geq 0.\end{split}

(A.4)

Here, we have used the fact the boundary integral vanishes for both Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions. Moreover, the positivity of the quantity in the last line follows from the positive eigenvalues of the metric.

Similarly, in order to show the positivity of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map, we can first define the following inner product on the space of smooth functions on the boundary:

\langle f_{1}|f_{2}\rangle=\oint_{\partial\mathscr{M}}dy\ \sqrt{h}f_{1}f_{2}.

(A.5)

To show that
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
is a self-adjoint operator with respect to this inner product, consider the following identities:

\begin{split}\langle f_{1}|\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}|f_{2}\rangle&=\oint_{\partial\mathscr{M}}d^{d-1}y\ \sqrt{h}f_{1}\partial_{n}H_{m^{2}}[f_{2}]=\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\ \partial_{\mu}\Big[H_{m^{2}}[f_{1}]\sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}H_{m^{2}}[f_{2}]\Big]\\
&=-\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\ \sqrt{g}H_{m^{2}}[f_{1}]\Delta H_{m^{2}}[f_{2}]+\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\ \sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}H_{m^{2}}[f_{1}]\partial_{\nu}H_{m^{2}}[f_{2}]\\
&=m^{2}\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\ \sqrt{g}H_{m^{2}}[f_{1}]H_{m^{2}}[f_{2}]+\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\ \sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}H_{m^{2}}[f_{1}]\partial_{\nu}H_{m^{2}}[f_{2}].\end{split}

(A.6)

Here we have used the fact that
\Delta H_{m^{2}}[f_{2}]=-m^{2}H_{m^{2}}[f_{2}]
and
H_{m^{2}}[f_{1}]|_{\partial\mathscr{M}}=f_{1}
. Note that the last line of (
A.6
) is symmetric under the exchange of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
. Therefore, we have

\langle f_{1}|\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}|f_{2}\rangle=\langle f_{2}|\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}|f_{1}\rangle,

(A.7)

and hence,
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
is a self-adjoint operator. The positivity of the operator
\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}
for
m^{2}\geq 0
also immediately follows from (
A.6
) by setting
f_{1}=f_{2}=f
as shown below:

\begin{split}\langle f|\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}}|f\rangle&=m^{2}\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\ \sqrt{g}\Big(H_{m^{2}}[f]\Big)^{2}+\int_{\mathscr{M}}d^{d}x\ \sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}H_{m^{2}}[f]\partial_{\nu}H_{m^{2}}[f]\geq 0.\end{split}

(A.8)

Appendix C
An alternative derivation of the Neumann determinant

In this appendix we will evaluate the Neumann determinant

To evaluate these determinants, we consider the following equation satisfied the eigenfunctions of the Laplacian

\Delta_{\eta}\Phi=\lambda\Phi,

\Phi^{(p)}(\lambda;r,\theta)=\begin{cases}f_{p}^{(\eta)}(r;\lambda L^{2})\cos\theta,\ \text{for}\ p\geq 0,\\
f_{p}^{(\eta)}(r;\lambda L^{2})\sin\theta\ \text{for}\ p<0.\end{cases}

\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(m^{2};s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}+m^{2})^{s}}

-function as folows:

\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(L_{0}^{(\eta)})=-\partial_{s}{\zeta_{0}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)|_{s=0}.

(C.10)

Having introduced the determinants of
(L_{p}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})
both for nonzero mass and zero mass, let us next look at the ratios
\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)})}
for
p\neq 0
and
\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{0}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(L_{0}^{(\eta)})}
. The logarithms of these ratios are given by

\begin{split}&\ln\Big[\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)})}\Big]=\sum_{k\geq 0}\ln\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big)\ \ \text{for}\ \ p\neq 0,\\
&\ln\Big[\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{0}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(L_{0}^{(\eta)})}\Big]=\ln m^{2}+\sum_{k>0}\ln\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{0,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big).\end{split}

(C.11)

The convergence of the above series can be checked by noting that Weyl’s law
[
25
,
26
]
in one dimension gives the following asymptotic form for the eigenvalues
\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
:

\lambda^{(\eta;\text{N})}_{p,k}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\Bigl(\frac{\pi k}{a_{\eta}}\Bigr)^{2},

(C.12)

where

a_{\eta}=\int_{0}^{1}\!e^{-\sigma_{\eta}(r)}\,{\rm d}r=\begin{cases}2\mathop{\text{arctanh}}\nolimits r_{0}&\text{if $\eta=-1$},\\
2\arctan r_{0}&\text{if $\eta=+1$}.\end{cases}

(C.13)

This leads to
\ln\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big)=O(\frac{1}{k^{2}})
in the large
k
regime, which ensures that the series in (
C.11
) are convergent.

Having established the expressions for the ratios of the massive and massless Sturm-Liouville determinants given in (
C.11
), we will prove in the next subsection that these ratios have certain specific values (see proposition
C.1
). Then in subsection
C.2
we will explain how the Neumann determinant
{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})
can be computed from these ratios.

C.1
Ratio of the massive and massless Sturm-Liouville determinants

Proposition C.1
.

For the constant curvature round disks with nonzero curvature, i.e. for
\eta=\pm 1
, the ratios of the massive and massless Sturm-Liouville determinants corresponding to Neumann boundary condition are given by

\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)})}=\prod_{k\geq 0}\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big)=\frac{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2})}{|p|}\ \text{for}\ p\neq 0,

(C.14)

\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{0}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(L_{0}^{(\eta)})}=m^{2}\prod_{k>0}\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{0,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big)=\frac{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}^{2}L^{2}}g_{0}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2}).

(C.15)

Proof.

\bf{p\neq 0}
:

To prove (
C.14
) for
p\neq 0
, let us first note that the function
g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)
given in (
3.27
) is an entire function in the complex plane. Now, we can define the following function
h_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)
which is also entire:

e^{h_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)}=\frac{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)}{\prod_{k\geq 0}\Big(1-z/(\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}L^{2})\Big)}.

(C.16)

The derivative of the function
h_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)
is given by

\partial_{z}h_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)=\frac{\partial_{z}g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)}{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)}-\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{z-\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}L^{2}}.

(C.17)

We will now show that this derivative vanishes everywhere in the complex plane. For this, consider a circular contour
\mathcal{C}_{n}
which is centred at the origin of the complex plane and whose radius is
R_{n}=\frac{L^{2}}{2}\Big(\lambda_{p,n-1}^{(\eta;\text{N})}+\lambda_{p,n}^{(\eta;\text{N})}\Big)
. Since the eigenvalues
\lambda_{p,n}^{(\eta;\text{N})}
go to infinity as
n\rightarrow\infty
, the radius
R_{n}
also goes to infinity in the same limit. For any point
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we can take
n
to be sufficiently large so that the circle encloses this point, and then use Cauchy’s residue theorem to get

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{z^{\prime}-z}\frac{\partial_{z^{\prime}}g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z^{\prime})}{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z^{\prime})}=\frac{\partial_{z}g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)}{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)}-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{z-\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}L^{2}}.

(C.18)

Therefore,

\partial_{z}h_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{z^{\prime}-z}\frac{\partial_{z^{\prime}}g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z^{\prime})}{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z^{\prime})}.

(C.19)

From the asymptotic behaviour of the hypergeometric functions derived in
[
27
]
, one can check that as
n\rightarrow\infty
,
\Big|\frac{1}{z^{\prime}-z}\frac{\partial_{z^{\prime}}g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z^{\prime})}{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z^{\prime})}\Big|\sim O(|z^{\prime}|^{-\frac{3}{2}})
along
\mathcal{C}_{N}
. This means that the above contour integral vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and hence we find that
h_{p}^{(\eta)}
is a constant function in the entire complex plane, i.e.

\partial_{z}h_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)=0\ \text{for all}\ z\in\mathbb{C}.

(C.20)

Now, as
z\rightarrow 0
, we have

e^{h_{p}^{(\eta)}(0)}=\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)=|p|.

(C.21)

Then by using the fact that
h_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)
is a constant function in the complex plane, we get

g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z).=|p|\prod_{k\geq 0}\Big(1-z/(\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}L^{2})\Big),

(C.22)

for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Setting
z=-m^{2}L^{2}
,we have

g_{p}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2})=|p|\prod_{k\geq 0}\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big).

(C.23)

This immediately implies that

\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)})}=\prod_{k\geq 0}\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big)=\frac{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2})}{|p|}.

(C.24)

\bf{p=0}
:

To prove (
C.15
), let us introduce the entire function
h_{0}^{(\eta)}(z)
as follows:

e^{h_{0}^{(\eta)}(z)}=\frac{g_{0}^{(\eta)}(z)}{z\prod_{k>0}\Big(1-z/(\lambda_{0,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}L^{2})\Big)}.

(C.25)

The derivative of this function is given by

\partial_{z}h_{0}^{(\eta)}(z)=\frac{\partial_{z}g_{0}^{(\eta)}(z)}{g_{0}^{(\eta)}(z)}-\frac{1}{z}-\sum_{k>0}\frac{1}{z-\lambda_{0,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}L^{2}}=\frac{\partial_{z}g_{0}^{(z)}(z)}{g_{0}^{(\eta)}(z)}-\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{z-\lambda_{0,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}L^{2}},

(C.26)

with the second equality following from the fact that
\lambda_{0,0}^{(\eta;\text{N})}=0
. Just as in
p\neq 0
case, this derivative vanishes everywhere in the complex plane, and hence
h_{0}^{(\eta)}(z)
is a constant function in the complex plane. Evaluating the value of this constant function at
z=0
, we get

e^{h_{0}^{(\eta)}(0)}=\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{g_{0}^{(\eta)}(z)}{z}=-\frac{2r_{0}^{2}}{(1+\eta r_{0}^{2})^{2}}F\Big(2,1,2,\frac{\eta r_{0}^{2}}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\Big)=-\frac{2r_{0}^{2}}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}.

(C.27)

Substituting
e^{h_{0}^{(\eta)}(z)}
by this constant value, we have

g_{0}^{(\eta)}(z)=-\frac{2r_{0}^{2}}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}z\prod_{k>0}\Big(1-z/(\lambda_{0,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}L^{2})\Big).

(C.28)

Finally, setting
z=-m^{2}L^{2}
, we get

\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{0}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(L_{0}^{(\eta)})}=m^{2}\prod_{k>0}\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{0,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big)=\frac{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}^{2}L^{2}}g_{0}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2}).

(C.29)

This completes the proof of Proposition
C.1
.

∎

C.2
Evaluation of the Neumann determinant
{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})

To relate the Neumann determinant
{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})
to the Sturm-Liouville determinants, let us first rearrange the all eigenvalues of the Laplacian (i.e. all the
\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}
’s) in ascending order as described in section
2
, and label them as
\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then we can introduce the following Neumann
\zeta
-function:

\zeta^{(\eta,\text{N})}(m^{2};s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})}+m^{2})^{s}}=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\zeta_{p}^{(\eta,\text{N})}(m^{2};s).

(C.30)

The
\zeta
-regularised Neumann determinant of
(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})
is defined by

\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})=-\partial_{s}\zeta^{(\eta,\text{N})}(m^{2};s)|_{s=0}=-\partial_{s}\Big[\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\zeta_{p}^{(\eta,\text{N})}(m^{2};s)\Big]\Big|_{s=0}.

(C.31)

As earlier, in the massless case, one has to remove the zero eigenvalue of
\Delta_{\eta}
while defining the zeta function and the determinant. Accordingly, we define

{\zeta^{(\eta,\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})})^{s}}={\zeta_{0}^{(\eta,\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z},p\neq 0}\zeta_{p}^{(\eta,\text{N})}(0;s),

(C.32)

and

\ln{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})=-\partial_{s}{\zeta^{(\eta,\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)|_{s=0}=-\partial_{s}\Big[{\zeta_{0}^{(\eta,\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z},p\neq 0}\zeta_{p}^{(\eta,\text{N})}(0;s)\Big]\Big|_{s=0}.

(C.33)

Note that from the above forms of the massive and massless Neumann determinants, we get

\begin{split}\ln\Big[\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})}\Big]=-\partial_{s}\Big[&\zeta_{0}^{(\eta,\text{N})}(m^{2};s)-{\zeta_{0}^{(\eta,\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)\\
&+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z},p\neq 0}\Big(\zeta_{p}^{(\eta,\text{N})}(m^{2};s)-\zeta_{p}^{(\eta,\text{N})}(0;s)\Big)\Big]|_{s=0}.\end{split}

(C.34)

This may suggest a naive expression for
\ln\Big[\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2}}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})}\Big]
of the following form:

\begin{split}\Bigg(\ln\Big[\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})}\Big]\Bigg)_{\text{naive}}&=\ln\Big[\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{0}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(L_{0}^{(\eta)})}\Big]+\sum_{p\neq 0}\ln\Big[\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)})}\Big]\\
&=\ln(m^{2})+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\ln\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{0,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big)+\sum_{p\neq 0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\ln\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big)\\
&=\ln(m^{2})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\ln\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big).\end{split}

(C.35)

However, Weyl’s law in two dimensions
[
25
,
26
]
gives the following asymptotic form for
\lambda^{(\eta;\text{N})}_{n}
:

\lambda^{(\eta;\text{N})}_{n}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\frac{4\pi n}{A_{\eta}}\,\raise 2.0pt\hbox{,}

(C.36)

where
A_{\eta}
is the area of the disk. From this, we find that

\begin{split}&\ln\Big(1+\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}\Big)\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\frac{m^{2}A_{\eta}}{4\pi n},\end{split}

(C.37)

which implies that the series in the last line of (
C.35
) diverges. This divergence arose because in going from (
C.34
) and (
C.35
) we interchanged the order of taking the limit
s=0
and the performing the infinite sum over
p
. However, these two operations do not commute. Another way to see this is to note that the series expansion

\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}(m^{2};s)-{\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)=\frac{1}{(m^{2})^{s}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})}+m^{2})^{s}}-\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})})^{s}}\Big]

(C.38)

is valid only when
\text{Re}(s)>0
or
s=0
. This can be verified by looking at the following asymptotic form of the summand which is obtained from the Weyl’s law given in (
C.36
):

\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})}+m^{2})^{s}}-\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})})^{s}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}-\frac{sm^{2}(A_{\eta})^{s+1}}{(4\pi)^{s+1}n^{s+1}}.

(C.39)

Naively it may seem that one can use the above series representation to evaluate the derivative of the LHS of (
C.38
) at
s=0
. However, taking such a derivative of the series in (
C.38
) at
s=0
, results in terms which have the asymptotic form
-\frac{m^{2}A^{\eta}}{4\pi n}
. This leads to the divergence in (
C.35
) that we had noted earlier.

To avoid this problem, let us remove the would-be divergent piece by considering the function
\Big(\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}(m^{2};s)-{\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)+sm^{2}{\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s+1)\Big)
which has the following series representation

\begin{split}&\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}(m^{2};s)-{\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)+sm^{2}{\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s+1)\\
&=\frac{1}{(m^{2})^{s}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})}+m^{2})^{s}}-\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})})^{s}}+\frac{sm^{2}}{(\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{N})})^{s+1}}\Big]\\
&=\frac{1}{(m^{2})^{s}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{1}{(\lambda_{0,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}+m^{2})^{s}}-\frac{1}{(\lambda_{0,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})})^{s}}+\frac{sm^{2}}{(\lambda_{0,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})})^{s+1}}\Big]\\
&\quad+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z},p\neq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{1}{(\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}+m^{2})^{s}}-\frac{1}{(\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})})^{s}}+\frac{sm^{2}}{(\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})})^{s+1}}\Big]\end{split}

(C.40)

that is valid in the domain
\text{Re}(s)>-1
. Now, we can safely calculate the derivative at
s=0
which gives

\begin{split}&\ln\Big[\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})}\Big]=-\partial_{s}\Big[\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}(m^{2};s)-{\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)\Big]_{|s=0}\\
&=m^{2}C^{(\eta;\text{N})}-\Big[\partial_{s}\Big(\zeta_{0}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(m^{2};s)-{\zeta_{0}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)\Big)|_{s=0}+m^{2}{\zeta_{0}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;1)\Big]\\
&\quad-\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z},p\neq 0}\Big[\partial_{s}\Big(\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(m^{2};s)-\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(0;s)\Big)|_{s=0}+m^{2}\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(0;1)\Big]\\
&=m^{2}C^{(\eta;\text{N})}+\Big[\ln\Big(\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{0}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(L_{0}^{(\eta)})}\Big)-m^{2}{\zeta_{0}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;1)\Big]\\
&\quad+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z},p\neq 0}\Big[\ln\Big(\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}(L_{p}^{(\eta)})}\Big)-m^{2}\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(0;1)\Big],\end{split}

(C.41)

where

\begin{split}&C^{(\eta;\text{N})}=\partial_{s}\Big(s{\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s+1)\Big)_{|s=0}.\end{split}

(C.42)

The quantity
C^{(\eta;\text{N})}
gives the finite piece of
{\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)
at
s=1
after removing the simple pole at this point. Using the Weyl’s law (
C.36
), one can find that the residue of
{\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)
at this pole is
\frac{A_{\eta}}{4\pi}
. So, an equivalent expression for
C^{(\eta;\text{N})}
is

\begin{split}&C^{(\eta;\text{N})}=\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}\Big({\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)-\frac{1}{s-1}\frac{A_{\eta}}{4\pi}\Big).\end{split}

(C.43)

The equation (
C.41
) shows how the ratio of the massive and the massless Neumann determinants is obtained from the ratios of the massive and the massless Sturm-Liouville determinants that we had derived in the previous subsection. Substituting these ratios of the massive and the massless Sturm-Liouville determinants by their values given in proposition
C.1
, we get

\begin{split}\ln\Big[\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2})}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})}\Big]&=m^{2}C^{(\eta;\text{N})}+\Big[\ln\Big(\frac{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}{2r_{0}^{2}L^{2}}g_{0}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2})\Big)-m^{2}{\zeta_{0}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;1)\Big]\\
&\quad+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z},p\neq 0}\Big[\ln\Big(\frac{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(-m^{2}L^{2})}{|p|}\Big)-m^{2}\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(0;1)\Big].\end{split}

(C.44)

The only things that remain to be evaluated to completely determine the above infinite series are the quantities
{\zeta_{0}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;1)
,
\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(0;1)
for
p\neq 0
, and
C^{(\eta;\text{N})}
. The quantities
{\zeta_{0}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;1)
and
\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(0;1)
for
p\neq 0
can be evaluated from certain relations that appeared in the proof of proposition
C.1
as we will explain shortly. The quantity
C^{(\eta;\text{N})}
can be evaluated by considering the Neumann Green’s functions of the scalar field in a manner analogous to that explained in
[
1
]
for the Dirichlet boundary conditions. However, let us present an alternative representation of
\ln\Big[\frac{{\det}_{\text{N}}(\Delta_{\eta}+m^{2}}{{\det}_{\text{N}}^{\prime}(\Delta_{\eta})}\Big]
that would allow us to bypass the actual computation of
C^{(\eta;\text{N})}
and corroborate the result obtained in section
4
. For this, let us note that in case of Dirichlet boundary condition, one can define
\zeta
-functions from the eigenvalues of the Laplacian and the Sturm-Liouville operators in a manner analogous to those defined for the Neumann boundary conditions in this paper
5
5

5

These
\zeta
-functions have been discussed in detail in
[
1
]
.
. These
\zeta
-functions are as follows:

\begin{split}&\zeta_{p}^{(\eta,\text{D})}(0;s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{D})})^{s}}\ \ \text{for}\ \ p\in\mathbb{Z},\\
&\zeta^{(\eta,\text{D})}(0;s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{D})})^{s}}=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\zeta_{p}^{(\eta,\text{D})}(0;s).\end{split}

(C.45)

Here
\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{D})}
are the eigenvalues of the Sturm-Liouville operator
L_{p}^{(\eta)}
, and
\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{D})}
are the eigenvalues of
\Delta_{\eta}
, each of them being now defined for Dirichlet boundary condition. The difference with the Neumann case is that all these eigenvalues are strictly positive because the Dirichlet boundary condition does not allow for a nonzero constant mode on the disk. The main point to note here is that the asymptotic form of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}^{(\eta;\text{D})}
following from Weyl’s law
[
25
,
26
]
is exactly the same as in the Neumann case (given in (
C.36
)) at leading order, i.e.,

\lambda^{(\eta;\text{D})}_{n}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\frac{4\pi n}{A_{\eta}}.

(C.46)

This leads to
\zeta^{(\eta,\text{D})}(0;s)
having the same residue as
{\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)
at the pole
s=1
. Consequently, one can define the following quantity analogous to
C^{(\eta;\text{N})}
(see (
C.43
)) which gives the finite piece of
\zeta^{(\eta;\text{D})}(0;s)
at
s=1
:

\begin{split}&C^{(\eta;\text{D})}=\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}\Big(\zeta^{(\eta;\text{D})}(0;s)-\frac{1}{s-1}\frac{A_{\eta}}{4\pi}\Big).\end{split}

(C.47)

From this we can conclude that the difference between
C^{(\eta;\text{N})}
and
C^{(\eta;\text{D})}
should be a finite quantity that is given by

\begin{split}C^{(\eta;\text{N})}-C^{(\eta;\text{D})}&=\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}\Big({\zeta^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;s)-\zeta^{(\eta;\text{D})}(0;s)\Big)\\
&={\zeta_{0}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}^{\prime}(0;1)-\zeta_{0}^{(\eta;\text{D})}(0;1)+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z},p\neq 0}\Big[\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(0;1)-\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{D})}(0;1)\Big].\end{split}

(C.48)

To check that the infinite series in the above equation is indeed convergent, let us note that
\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{D})}(0;1)
was evaluated in
[
1
]
and it is given by
6
6

6

In
[
1
]
,
\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{D})}(0;1)
was computed after setting
L=1
. To obtain the expression in (
C.49
), one needs to restore the
L
-dependence by dimensional analysis. Such an analysis gives
\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{D})}(0;1)=L^{2}\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{D})}(0;1)|_{L=1}
. See the discussion just below (
C.59
).

\begin{split}\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{D})}(0;1)&=\frac{A_{\eta}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{|p|+1}F\Big(1,1,|p|+2,\frac{\eta r_{0}^{2}}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\Big)\\
&=\frac{L^{2}r_{0}^{2}}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\frac{1}{|p|+1}F\Big(1,1,|p|+2,\frac{\eta r_{0}^{2}}{1+\eta r_{0}^{2}}\Big).\end{split}

(C.49)

To derive the form of
\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(0;1)
for
p\neq 0
, note that from (
C.22
), we have

\ln g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)=\ln(|p|)+\sum_{k\geq 0}\ln\Big(1-\frac{z}{\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}L^{2}}\Big).

(C.50)

for
z\in\mathbb{C}
. This implies that

-\partial_{z}\ln g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)|_{z=0}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}L^{2}-z}\Big|_{z=0}=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p,k}^{(\eta;\text{N})}}=\frac{\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(0;1)}{L^{2}},

(C.51)

from which we get

\zeta_{p}^{(\eta;\text{N})}(0;1)=-\frac{L^{2}}{g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)}\partial_{z}g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)|_{z=0}=-\frac{L^{2}}{|p|}\partial_{z}g_{p}^{(\eta)}(z)|_{z=0}.

(C.52)

Here we have used the fact that

Appendix D
An example: Neumann determinant on a hemisphere

In this appendix we will discuss a particular case of the positive curvature disk, viz. the case where the disk corresponds to a hemisphere. We will show that the infinite series representation for the Neumann determinant with an arbitrary mass can be exactly computed in this case and the result can be expressed in terms of the Barnes
G
-function. The similar computation for Dirichlet boundary condition was done in
[
1
]
. As we have discussed earlier, there would be additional contributions to the logarithm of the Neumann determinant from the integral over the extrinsic curvature at the boundary and the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map. We will compute the contribution of the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map from the general result derived in section
3
. This calculation of the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for the hemisphere was also done in
[
17
]
. We will show that our result matches exactly with theirs. We will then add the contribution of the logarithm of this determinant to the logarithm of the Dirichlet determinant obtained in
[
1
]
to extract the result for the logarithm of the Neumann determinant.

Having outlined the strategy, let us proceed with the computation of the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for the hemisphere. In case of the hemisphere with radius
L
, we have
\eta=1
and
r_{0}=1
. Hence, the parameter
\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{1+r_{0}^{2}}
that enters into the hypergeometric functions in the definitions of
f_{p}^{(+)}(1;z)
and
g_{p}^{(+)}(z)
as an argument (see (
3.24
) and (
3.27
)) is
\frac{1}{2}
. In this case, the hypergeometric functions simplify due to the general identities given below:

\begin{split}&F\Big(a,1-a,c,\frac{1}{2}\Big)=\frac{2^{1-c}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(\frac{a+c}{2})\Gamma(\frac{c-a+1}{2})},\\
&F\Big(a,3-a,c,\frac{1}{2}\Big)=\frac{2^{3-c}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(c)}{(a-1)(a-2)}\Big[\frac{c-2}{\Gamma(\frac{a+c-2}{2})\Gamma(\frac{c-a+1}{2})}-\frac{2}{\Gamma(\frac{a+c-3}{2})\Gamma(\frac{c-a}{2})}\Big].\end{split}

(D.1)

Using these identities, we get that
f_{p}^{(+)}(1;z)
and
g_{p}^{(+)}(z)
defined in (
3.24
) and (
3.27
) reduce to the following functions:

(D.2)

\begin{split}g_{p}^{(+)}(z)&=p\Big[\frac{2^{-(p-1)}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(p)}{\Gamma(\frac{2p+1+\sqrt{1+4z}}{4})\Gamma(\frac{2p+1-\sqrt{1+4z}}{4})}\Big],\ \text{for}\ p>0.\end{split}

(D.3)

and

g_{0}^{(+)}(z)=\sqrt{\pi}\Big[\frac{2}{\Gamma(\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4z}}{4})\Gamma(\frac{1-\sqrt{1+4z}}{4})}\Big].

(D.4)

Notice that from the above expressions, we get

g_{p}^{(+)}(z)=pf_{p-1}^{(+)}(1;z)\ \text{for}\ p>0.

(D.5)

Substituting this in the expression of the logarithm of the determinant of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map given in (
3.36

\begin{split}\ln{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})&=\ln(2\pi)+\ln\Big(\frac{g_{0}^{(+)}(-m^{2}L^{2})}{f_{0}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}\Big)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\ln\Big(\frac{f_{p-1}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}{f_{p}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}\Big)\\
&=\ln(2\pi)+\ln\Big(\frac{g_{0}^{(+)}(-m^{2}L^{2})}{f_{0}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}\Big)+2\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{p=1}^{N}\ln\Big(\frac{f_{p-1}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}{f_{p}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}\Big).\end{split}

(D.6)

Here, in the second line we have replaced the infinite sum by the limit of a finite sum. We can now use the fact that

\begin{split}\sum_{p=1}^{N}\ln\Big(\frac{f_{p-1}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}{f_{p}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})}\Big)&=\sum_{p=1}^{N}\Big[\ln\Big(f_{p-1}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})\Big)-\ln\Big(f_{p}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})\Big)\Big]\\
&=\ln\Big(f_{0}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})\Big)-\ln\Big(f_{N}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})\Big),\end{split}

to obtain

\begin{split}\ln{\det}(\mathscr{O}_{m^{2}})&=\ln(2\pi)+\ln\Big(g_{0}^{(+)}(-m^{2}L^{2})f_{0}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})\Big)\\
&\quad-2\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\ln\Big(f_{N}^{(+)}(1;-m^{2}L^{2})\Big).\end{split}

Abstract
. We show that if X X is a smooth Fano manifold which caries a Kähler Ricci soliton, then the canonical cone of the product of X X with a complex projective space of sufficiently large dimension is a Calabi–Yau cone, i.e. admits a Ricci-flal Kähler cone metric. This can be seen as an asymptotic version of a conjecture by Mabuchi and Nikagawa. This result is obtained by the openness of the set of weight functions v v over the momentum polytope of a given smooth Fano manifold, for which a v v -soliton exists. We discuss other ramifications of this approach, including a Licherowicz type obstruction to the existence of a Kähler Ricci soliton and a Fujita type volume bound for the existence of a v v -soliton.

1.
Introduction

A number of different notions of
special
Kähler metrics have emerged in the last 20 years or so, in connection with Calabi’s seminal program
[
15
]
of finding a canonical representative of a given de Rham class of Kähler metrics on a smooth compact Kähler manifold
X
. Perhaps the most studied notion of all, introduced by Calabi himself, is that of
constant scalar curvature
Kähler (cscK) metrics.

In the case of a smooth Fano variety
X
endowed with its anti-canonical de Rham Kähler class
2\pi c_{1}(X)
, a cscK metric in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
is necessarily a Kähler–Einstein metric with scalar curvature equal to
2n
. The existence problem for such Kähler metrics is understood in terms of the Yau–Tian–Donaldson (YTD) conjecture
[
62
,
57
,
27
]
which states that
X
admits a Kähler-Einstein metric in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
if and only if the anticanonical polarization
(X,K^{-1}_{X})
is K-polystable. There are, by now, many different proofs of this conjecture
[
58
,
20
,
65
,
10
,
46
]
, following the initial work of Chen–Donaldson–Sun
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
(who proved K-polystability implies existence) and Tian and Berman (who proved existence implies K-stablity)
[
57
,
7
]
.

Beyond the study of Kähler-Einstein metrics on
(X,2\pi c_{1}(X))
, other notions of canonical Kähler metrics have been considered. These allow to treat cases where a Kähler-Einstein metric do not exist due to the classical obstructions in terms of the automorphisms of
X

[
52
,
48
,
30
]
. Tian-Zhu
[
59
,
60
]
initiated a systematic study of the so-called
Kähler-Ricci solitons
(KRS) on
(X,2\pi c_{1}(X))
, whereas Martelli–Sparks–Yau
[
51
]
developed the theory of Calabi–Yau cone structures (or, equivalently, Sasaki–Einstein structures) defined on the affine cone
K_{X}^{\times}
associated to
X
. These works prompted separated investigations of the corresponding existence theories, and the formulation and proofs of appropriate modifications of the YTD conjecture in each case, see respectively
[
24
,
22
]
.

More recently, there have been developments providing a framework to treat the existence problems mentioned above all together. The unifying geometric object is that of a
v
-soliton
Kähler metric
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
(called
g
-soliton in
[
37
]
), which was introduced in a foundational work by Mabuchi
[
49
]
, followed by further comprehensive studies by Berman–Witt Nyström
[
11
]
and Han–Li
[
37
]
. A
v
-soliton is defined in terms of a fixed maximal compact torus
{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X)
with associated canonical polytope
\mathrm{P}_{X}\subset\left({\rm Lie}({\mathbb{T}})\right)^{*}
, and a positive smooth function
v(x)>0
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
, via the equation

{\rm Ric}(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log v(\mu_{\omega}).

In the above formula,
\omega
is a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
,
{\rm Ric}(\omega)\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
is its Ricci form and
\mu_{\omega}:X\to\mathrm{P}_{X}
is the canonically normalized
{\mathbb{T}}
-momentum map. Thus, Kähler–Einstein metrics correspond to
1
-solitons, KRS metrics to
e^{\ell}
-solitons
[
59
]
, and Calabi–Yau cone structures on
K_{X}^{\times}
to
\ell^{-(n+2)}
-solitons
[
3
]
, where
\ell(x)
is a suitably defined (and in general different for each case) affine-linear function on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
. As an outcome, the work
[
37
]
gives a YTD type correspondence for the existence of a
v
-soliton on
(X,{\mathbb{T}},2\pi c_{1}(X))
, expressed in terms of a suitable notion of
uniform
v
-weighted Ding stability
of
(X,{\mathbb{T}},2\pi c_{1}(X)))
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-equivariant test-configurations.

Despite the above remarkable progress, it remains a very challenging problem to use effectively the YTD correspondence in order to produce examples of
v
-solitons. A specific ramification in this general direction is the following

Problem 1.1
.

Suppose
(X,{\mathbb{T}},2\pi c_{1}(X))
is a smooth Fano manifold. Describe the set
\mathcal{S}(X)
of positive smooth functions
v
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
, such that
(X,{\mathbb{T}},2\pi c_{1}(X))
admits a
v
-soliton.

By an idea going back to A. Futaki
[
30
]
, it turns out that
\mathcal{S}(X)\subset\mathcal{F}(X)
where
\mathcal{F}(X)
is the subset

\mathcal{F}(X):=\left\{v\in C^{\infty}_{>0}(\mathrm{P}_{X})\,|\,\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}\langle\zeta,x\rangle v(x)d\mu_{\rm DH}=0,\,\,\forall\zeta\in{\rm Lie}({\mathbb{T}})\right\}.

In the above formula,
d\mu_{\rm DH}
denotes the Duistermaat–Heckman measure
[
28
]
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
induced by
2\pi c_{1}(X)
. Indeed,
\mathcal{F}(X)
is identified with the set of weight functions
v
for which the corresponding
v
-Futaki invariant
{\rm Fut}_{v}:{\rm Lie}({\mathbb{T}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
on
X
vanishes. Clearly,
\mathcal{F}(X)
is a relatively closed convex cone in
C^{\infty}_{>0}(\mathrm{P}_{X})
(in the relative
C^{0}(\mathrm{P}_{X})
-topology).

Problem
1.1
is particularly interesting when
\mathcal{S}(X)
is a non-empty subset of
\mathcal{F}(X)
. We know in this case that the usual Calabi–Lichnerowicz–Matsushima obstruction vanishes, i.e. the connected component of the identity
\mathrm{Aut}_{\circ}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
of the group of complex automorphisms of
X
commuting with
{\mathbb{T}}
is reductive (see. e.g.
[
44
]
for a general statement). In
[
37
]
, it is shown that if
X
is a smooth
toric
Fano variety, one has the equality
\mathcal{S}(X)=\mathcal{F}(X)
. Similar
weight insensitive
highly symmetric Fano examples were recently found by L. Wang
[
63
]
and T. Delcroix
[
25
]
; these works also demonstrate examples satisfying
\mathcal{S}(X)\subsetneq\mathcal{F}(X)
.

Despite the above progress, a general understanding of
\mathcal{S}(X)
remains an open problem. While we do not attempt in this work to obtain any deep structure result for
\mathcal{S}(X)
, we underline a basic property which provides a strong openness result à la LeBrun–Simanca
[
43
]
. To state it, we introduce the space of weights

\mathcal{D}(X):=\left\{v\in C^{\infty}_{>0}(\mathrm{P}_{X})\,\Big|\,{\bf D}_{v}\,\textrm{is}\,{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathbb{C}}-\textrm{coercive}\right\},

where
{\bf D}_{v}
denotes the
v
-weighted Ding functional (see Definitions
2.3
and
2.4
).

Theorem 1.1
.

\mathcal{D}(X)
is an open convex cone in
\mathcal{F}(X)
with respect to the relative
C^{0}(\mathrm{P}_{X})
-topology.

By
[
37
, Theorem 1.7]
,
\mathcal{S}(X)=\mathcal{D}(X)
. Thus, Theorem
1.1
yields an effective estimate for the radius of an open ball in
\mathcal{S}(X)
around a given
v_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(X)
, expressed in terms of the coercivity slope of the
v_{0}
-weighed Ding functional, see Corollary
3.1
. Our main geometric applications below use the fact that
\mathcal{S}(X)
is relatively open in
\mathcal{F}(X)
; to obtain these results, instead of Theorem
1.1
and
[
37
]
, one can merely use a LeBrun–Simanca
[
43
]
type perturbation argument as in
[
41
]
, which yields the relative opennes of finite dimensional smooth families of weights in
\mathcal{S}(X)
. We present such a perturbation result adapted to our context in Appendix
A
.

Remark 1.1
.

In a somewhat similar vein, on a given Kähler cone
Y
endowed with a maximal compact torus
\hat{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(Y)
, the authors study in
[
13
]
the cone
\hat{\mathfrak{t}}^{\rm ext}\subset{\rm Lie}(\hat{\mathbb{T}})
of Sasaki–Reeb vector fields
\hat{\xi}
such that
Y
admits an extremal Sasaki metric polarized by
\hat{\xi}
. Translated to the weighted Kähler terminology (see
[
1
,
2
,
41
]
), this is equivalent to study on a given quasi-regular quotient
(X,L,{\mathbb{T}})
(endowed with a momentum polytope
\mathrm{P}_{L}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
) the set
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\rm ext}
of positive affine-linear functions
\ell
on
\mathrm{P}_{L}
such that
X
admits a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
(\ell^{-n-1},\ell_{\rm ext}\ell^{-n-3})
-cscK metric in
2\pi c_{1}(L)
. Here,
\ell
is arbitrary positive affine linear function on
\mathrm{P}_{L}
and
\ell_{\rm ext}
is uniquely determined by
\ell
. In this setup, it is shown in
[
13
]
that
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\rm ext}
is not convex in general, which contrasts with Theorem
1.1
.

We now discuss geometric applications of Theorem
1.1
. To this end, we consider the Sasaki geometry corresponding to the canonical bundle
K_{X}
of a Fano manifold
X
. It was shown in
[
3
]
that the existence of a Sasaki–Einstein structure (or equivalently, the existence of a Ricci-flat Kähler cone structure on the affine cone
K_{X}^{\times}
) is equivalent to the existence of a
v
-soliton in
(X,2\pi_{1}c_{1}(X))
for a weight function
v=\ell^{-(n+2)}
where
\ell(x)
is a positive affine-linear function on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
.

Thus, Problem
1.1
incorporates as a special case answering the following Conjecture due to Mabuchi and Nakagawa:

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
50
]
If the smooth Fano manifold
X
admits a KRS, then its canonical cone
Y:=K_{X}^{\times}
admits a Calabi–Yau cone structure.

We note that the recent work
[
39
]
shows that the above conjecture generally fails for Fano orbifolds.

Using Theorem
1.1
, we make the following observation related to Conjecture
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
X
is a smooth Fano manifold which admits a Kähler–Ricci soliton. Then, there exists a non-negative integer
k_{0}
, such that for any
k\geq k_{0}
, the canonical cone
K^{\times}_{Z}
of the Fano manifold

Z:=X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{k}_{{\mathbb{C}}}

admits a Ricci-flat Kähler cone metric.

Theorem
1.2
is obtained along the following lines: we construct a sequence of positive affine-linear functions
\ell_{N}(x)
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
, satisfying that: (1)
(\ell_{N}(x))^{-N}\in{\mathcal{F}}(X)
, and (2)
(\ell_{N}(x))^{-N}
converges in
C^{0}(\mathrm{P}_{X})
to the weight
v=e^{\langle\tau,x\rangle}
corresponding to the KRS. By Theorem
1.1
and
[
37
, Theorem 1.7]
(or equivalently by Corollary
A.1
), for
N>>1
there exists an
(\ell_{N}(x))^{-N}
-soliton on X, and hence an
(\ell_{N})^{-({\rm dim}(Z)+2)}
-soliton on
Z=X\times{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{N-{\rm dim}(X)-2}
. The latter in turn defines a Calabi–Yau cone structure on
K^{\times}_{Y}
by
[
3
, Prop.2]
(see also Remark
4.4
below).

Our proof only shows that a sufficiently large
k_{0}
as above exists, but it does not yield a quantitative bound of
k_{0}
. Of course, Mabuchi–Nakagawa conjecture predicts that
k_{0}=0
. We also note that the
k
-dimensional complex projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{k}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
in Theorem
1.2
can be replaced with any other
k
-dimensional Kähler–Einstein Fano manifold.

We next extend the geometric setup by considering, more generally, Sasaki structures on the unitary bundle
N
in
K_{X}
, which are transversal Kähler–Ricci solitons in the sense of Futaki–Ono–Wang
[
32
]
. Equivalently, such Sasaki structures correspond to cone Kähler metrics on the canonical cone
K_{X}^{\times}
, polarized by vector fields in the Lie algebra of
\hat{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
, which give rise to transversal Kähler–Ricci solitons. We show in Corollary
4.1
below that transversal KRS Sasaki structures on
N
correspond to
v
-solitons on
X
with
v=\ell_{1}^{-(n+2)}e^{\ell_{2}/\ell_{1}}
for affine-linear functions
\ell_{1}>0,\ell_{2}
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
. The relative openness of
\mathcal{S}(X)
and the fact that
\ell_{1}
ans
\ell_{2}
are determined by the corresponding Sasaki–Reeb vector field (see Lemma
4.2
below) allow us to recover a result of D. Petrecca:

Theorem 1.3
.

[
55
]
Let
X
be a smooth Fano manifold with canonical cone
K_{X}^{\times}
and maximal compact torus
\hat{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(K_{X}^{\times})
. Suppose
K_{X}^{\times}
admits a compatible cone Kähler metric, polarized by a Sasaki–Reeb vector field
\hat{\xi}_{0}\in{\rm Lie}(\hat{\mathbb{T}})
, which is a transversal Kähler Ricci soliton. Then, for any Sasaki–Reeb vector field
\hat{\xi}\in{\rm Lie}(\hat{\mathbb{T}})
which is sufficiently close to
\hat{\xi}_{0}
,
K_{X}^{\times}
admits a compatible cone Kähler metric polarized by
\hat{\xi}
, which is transversal Kähler–Ricci soliton.

We finally obtain some apriori constraints for the weight functions
v\in\mathcal{S}(X)
.

First, we give a uniform upper bound for the weight

v(x)=(\langle\xi,x\rangle+1)^{-(n+2)}\exp\left(\frac{\langle\tau_{\xi},x\rangle}{1+\langle\xi,x\rangle}\right)
corresponding to a Sasaki transversal Kähler–Ricci soliton.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano manifold admitting a KRS with soliton vector field
\tau
, invariant under a maximal torus
{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X)
. Then, on the canonical momentum polytope
\mathrm{P}_{X}
,

\langle\tau,x\rangle<n.

More generally, if
\hat{\xi}\in{\rm Lie}(\hat{\mathbb{T}})
is a Sasaki–Reeb polarization of the cone
Y=K_{X}^{\times}
, normalized by the identity

{\mathcal{L}}_{-J\hat{\xi}}\Omega=\Omega
where
\Omega
is the Liouville holomorphic volume form of
K_{X}
, and if, moreover,
(Y,\hat{\xi})
admits a compatible Kähler cone metric which is transversal KRS, then the projected vector field
\xi\in{\rm Lie}({\mathbb{T}})
and the corresponding soliton vector field
\tau_{\xi}\in{\rm Lie}({\mathbb{T}})
satisfy on
\mathrm{P}_{X}

\langle\tau_{\xi},x\rangle<n\left(\langle\xi,x\rangle+1\right).

Second, we derive a weighted version of Fujita’a inequality
[
29
]
:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano manifold admitting a
v
-soliton
\omega
. Suppose without loss of generality that
v
is normalized so that

\int_{X}v(\mu_{\omega})\omega^{[n]}=\int_{X}\omega^{[n]}.

Then the first Chern number of
X
satisfies

c_{1}^{n}(X)\leq\left(\frac{n+1}{\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{P}_{X}\end{subarray}}v}\right)^{n}.

The above inequality was established in the Kähler–Einstein case (i.e. with
v\equiv 1
) in a series of works by Berman-Berndtsson
[
8
,
9
]
under some additional conditions, and by Fujita
[
29
]
in general. The latter proof uses the resolution of the YTD conjecture and a computation of the Donaldson–Futaki invariant of a certain family of test configurations. Our approach here is to adapt these computations to the weighted soliton case, using the (weighted) YTD correspondence and the notions of weighted beta invariant and weighted volume from Han-Li
[
37
,
38
]
. Notice that Theorem
1.5
yields an apriori constraint for
v\in\mathcal{S}(X)
on a given Fano manifold
X
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sect.2, we review the theory of KRS solitons and their generalization, the
v
-solitons. In Sect.3, we establish Theorem
1.1
as an application of the results in
[
37
]
. In Sect.4, we introduce the point of view of Sasaki geometry and the corresponding Kähler cones. We recall here the definition of transversal KRS soliton on the canonical cone
K_{X}^{\times}
of a smooth Fano variety, and show in Corollary
4.1
that these structures give rise to special
v
-solitons on
X
. With this observation, we apply the openness Theorem
1.1
to derive the proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. In Sect. 5, we prove Theorem
1.4
and show how it can be seen as a variant of the so-called
Lichnerowicz obstruction

[
34
]
which is a necessary condition for the existence of Calabi–Yau cone metrics on
K_{X}^{\times}
. The final Sect. 5, we prove Theorem
1.5
. In the Appendix
A
, we recast in the setup of
v
-solitons studied in this paper a (weaker) openness result à la LeBrun–Simanca from
[
41
]
, which gives an alternative tool for obtaining our main geometric applications, Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
above. In the final Appendix
B
, we gather some well-known curvature identities for gradient Ricci solitons and use them to observe, by a simple application of the maximum principle, that the transversal scalar curvature of a compact Sasaki transversal KRS is positive. This is a key ingredient for our proof of Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Fano manifolds: notation and normalization

In what follows,
X
will denote a smooth compact complex manifold of complex dimension
n
, for which the anti-canonical bundle
K^{-1}_{X}
is ample. Such an
X
is called a
smooth Fano variety
. The Fano condition implies that
X
is projective, and that the de Rham class
\alpha=2\pi c_{1}(X)=2\pi c_{1}(K_{X}^{-1})
contains Kähler metrics.

Any Kähler metric
\omega\in\alpha
is de Rham cohomologous with the corresponding Ricci form
{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
, and thus we can write in this case

{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}h_{\omega},

for a smooth function
h_{\omega}
which (by the maximum principle) is unique up to an additive constant. Such a function will be referred to as a
Ricci potential
of
\omega
;
we can further fix the additive constant
by requiring that

(2.1)

\int_{X}e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega}}\omega^{[n]}=\int_{X}\omega^{[n]}=:\mathit{vol}(X),

where
\omega^{[n]}:=\omega^{n}/n!
stands for the Riemannian volume form of the Kähler metric
\omega
. We shall then refer to this uniquely defined Ricci potential
\mathring{h}_{\omega}
as the
normalized Ricci potential
of
\omega
.
In these terms, the
Kähler–Einstein
condition

(2.2)

{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)=\omega

is equivalent to
\mathring{h}_{\omega}=0
.

We shall next fix once for all a maximal compact real torus
{\mathbb{T}}
inside the connected component of identity
\mathrm{Aut}_{\circ}(X)
of the group of complex automorphisms of
X
. The corresponding complex torus will be denoted by
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
. There is a canonical lift (still denoted by
{\mathbb{T}}
) of the action of
{\mathbb{T}}
on
X
to an action on the canonical bundle
K_{X}
. The latter bundle has a further
{{\mathbb{S}}}^{1}
-extension of the lifted
{\mathbb{T}}
action, given by fibre-wise multiplications with complex numbers
e^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. We denote by
\hat{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
the resulting
({\rm dim}({\mathbb{T}})+1)
-dimensional torus acting on
K_{X}
.
We shall respectively denote by
{\mathfrak{t}}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{t}}
the Lie algebras of
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\hat{{\mathbb{T}}}
.

We consider the space

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metrics
\omega
on
X
, belonging to
\alpha
; by a standard averaging argument,
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\neq\emptyset
. Introducing a base-point
\omega_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
, we will identify
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
with the Fréchet space
{\mathcal{H}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)/{\mathbb{R}}
, where

\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X):=\left\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}\,\,|\,\,\omega_{\varphi}:=\omega_{0}+dd^{c}\varphi>0\right\}

is the space of smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler potentials with respect to
\omega_{0}
.

For each
\omega\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)
, we let
H_{\omega}
denote the Hermitian metric on
K_{X}
whose Chern curvature is

R^{H_{\omega}}=-i\omega
, and by
\nabla^{H_{\omega}}
the
H_{\omega}
-Chern connection on
K_{X}
. A basic fact in the theory is that any lift of the
{\mathbb{T}}
action on
K_{X}
gives rise to a
{\mathbb{T}}
-momentum map
\mu_{\omega}
for
(X,{\mathbb{T}},\omega)
, defined as follows: for any
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
and any smooth section
s\in C^{\infty}(X,-K_{X})
,

(2.3)

{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}s=\nabla^{H_{\omega}}_{\xi}s-i\mu_{\omega}^{\xi}s,

where
\mu_{\omega}^{\xi}
satisfies

\omega(\xi,\cdot)=-d\mu_{\omega}^{\xi}

see
[
33
, Proposition 8.7.2]
.
Therefore, the canonical lift of
{\mathbb{T}}
to
-K_{X}
gives rise to a
canonically normalized
momentum map
\mu_{\omega}:X\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
whose image
\mathrm{P}_{X}
is a compact convex polytope
[
4
,
35
]
; one can further show (see e.g.
[
11
,
41
]
) that
\mu_{\omega}(\mathrm{P}_{X})
is independent of the choice of
\omega\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
. In this paper, we shall refer to
\mathrm{P}_{X}
as the
canonical polytope
of
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
.

Remark 2.1
.

In general, a
{\mathbb{T}}
-momentum map
\mu_{\omega}:X\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
is defined only up to a translation with an element of
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
; the fact that in the Fano case there is a canonical normalization for
\mu_{\omega}
follows from the existence of a canonical lift of the
{\mathbb{T}}
-action on
X
to
K_{X}
.
An alternative way to define the canonical normalization for
\mu_{\omega}
(see e.g.
[
3
,
59
]
) is to require that for any
\zeta\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, the function
\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}:=\langle\mu_{\omega},\zeta\rangle
satisfies

(2.4)

\int_{X}\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}e^{h_{\omega}}\omega^{[n]}=0,

where
h_{\omega}
is any Ricci potential of
\omega
.

Once we have suitably normalized
\mathrm{P}_{X}
, we can define the
Duistermaat-Heckman

measure
d\mu_{\rm DH}
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
as the push-forward via
\mu_{\omega}
of the Riemannian measure of
(X,\omega)
: for any continuous function
f
on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
, we let

(2.5)

\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}f(x)d\mu_{\rm DM}:=\int_{X}f(\mu_{\omega})\omega^{[n]}.

The fact that the LHS is independent of the choice of
\omega\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
follows for instance from the
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant Moser lemma (see e.g.
[
31
]
.)

2.2.
Kähler–Ricci solitons

Following
[
59
]
, a
Kähler Ricci soliton
(KRS for short) is a Kähler metric
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
which satisfies

(2.6)

{\rm Ric}({\omega})-\omega=-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{L}}_{J\tau}\omega,

where
\tau
is a Killing vector field for the Kähler structure
\omega
. In the case
\tau=0
, (
2.6
) reduces to the Kähler–Einstein condition (
2.2
). Tian–Zhu
[
59
]
have extended the Matsushima’s theorem
[
52
]
to the case of a KRS, which in turn yields that any Kähler metric satisfying (
2.6
) must be invariant by the action of a maximal torus in
\mathrm{Aut}_{\circ}(X)
, containing the flow of
\tau
. Up to a pull-back by an element of
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(X)
, we can and will assume that a KRS on
X
belongs to
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
and
\tau\in{\mathfrak{t}}
. Thus, similarly to the Kähler–Einstein case, the KRS condition can be rewritten as

(2.7)

{h}_{\omega}=\mu_{\omega}^{\tau}

or, equivalently,

(2.8)

{\rm Ric}({\omega})-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\mu_{\omega}^{\tau},\qquad\tau\in{\mathfrak{t}}.

By Remark
2.1
, (
2.7
) and (
2.5
), if
X
admits a KRS in
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
, then for any
\zeta\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, we have

\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}\langle\zeta,x\rangle e^{\langle\tau,x\rangle}d\mu_{\rm DH}=0.

The above condition means that
\tau
is a critical point of the function
F:{\mathfrak{t}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
:

F(\zeta):=\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}e^{\langle\zeta,x\rangle}d\mu_{\rm DH}.

Tian–Zhu
[
59
]
further show that
F
admits a unique critical point, independent of the existence of a KRS on
X
. We shall refer to this
\tau\in{\mathfrak{t}}
as the
KRS vector field
of
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
and to the positive smooth function
v(x):=e^{\langle\tau,x\rangle}
on
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
as the
KRS weight function
.

2.3.
v
-solitons

The notion of KRS extends to the following more general geometric situation, studied by Berman–Witt Nyström in
[
11
]
and, more recently, by Han–Li in
[
37
]
. We follow the notation of
[
3
, Sect.2]
.

Definition 2.1

(
v
-soliton)
.

In the setup as above, let
v(x)
be a given positive function defined on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
. A Kähler metric
\omega\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
is called a
v
-soliton
if it satisfies

(2.9)

{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log v(\mu_{\omega}).

Clearly, Kähler–Einstein metrics are
1
-solitons whereas KRS are
v=e^{\langle\tau,x\rangle}
-solitons.
Notice that if
\omega
is a
v
-soliton it is also a
\lambda v
-soliton for any
\lambda>0
. To read off this ambiguity, we shall sometimes consider
normalized
weight functions
\mathring{v}:=\frac{v}{\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}vd\mu_{\rm DH}}
, i.e.

(2.10)

\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}\mathring{v}(x)d\mu_{\rm DH}=1.

We also notice that for any
v
-soliton,
h_{\omega}=\log(v(\mu_{\omega}))
,
the linear function

(2.11)

{\rm Fut}_{v}:{\mathfrak{t}}\to{\mathbb{R}},\qquad{\rm Fut}_{v}(\zeta):=\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}\langle\zeta,x\rangle v(x)d\mu_{\rm DH}

identically vanishes by Remark
2.1
.

Definition 2.2

(
v
-Futaki invariant)
.

The linear function defined by (
2.11
) is called the
v
-Futaki invariant
of
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
.

We next define a functional
{\bf I}_{v}
on the
space
\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)
of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler potentials (see
[
41
,
37
]
:

d_{\varphi}{\bf I}_{v}(\dot{\varphi})=\int_{X}v(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})\dot{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]},\qquad{\bf I}_{v}(0)=0.

Following
[
37
]
, we introduce

Definition 2.3
.

[
v
-Ding functional] The
v
-Ding functional is the map

{\bf D}_{v}:{\mathcal{H}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by

{\bf D}_{v}(\varphi):=-\left(\frac{{\bf I}_{v}(\varphi)}{\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\int_{X}e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega_{0}}-2\varphi}\frac{\omega_{0}^{[n]}}{\mathit{vol}(X)}\right),

where we have set
\mathit{vol}_{v}(X):=\int_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}v(x)d\mu_{\rm DH}=\int_{X}v(\mu_{\omega})\omega^{[n]}
and
\mathring{h}_{\omega_{0}}
stands for the normalized Ricci potential of the base point
\omega_{0}
, see (
2.1
).

Notice that
{\bf D}_{v}
does not change if we add a constant to
\varphi
, so it actually descends to a functional, denoted
{\bf D}_{v}(\omega_{\varphi})
, on the space
\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)/{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\alpha}(X)
.

It is not hard to see that the differential of
{\bf D}_{v}
is given by

(2.12)

(d_{\omega_{\varphi}}{\bf D}_{v})(\dot{\varphi})=\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}\left(\frac{e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}}}{\mathit{vol}(X)}-\frac{v(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})}{\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)}\right)\omega^{[n]}_{\varphi},

so that the critical points of
{\bf D}_{v}
are precisely the Kähler metrics
\omega_{\varphi}
for which

e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega_{\varphi}}}=\mathit{vol}(X)\mathring{v}(\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}})

i.e. the
v
-solitons.

Another consequence of the formula (
2.12
) is the following

Lemma 2.1
.

The
v
-Ding functional is
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant, i.e. satisfies

{\bf D}_{v}(\sigma^{*}(\omega))={\bf D}_{v}(\omega)\qquad\forall\sigma\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}},

iff the
v
-Futaki invariant
{\rm Fut}_{v}\equiv 0
.

Proof.

{\bf D}_{v}
is clearly
{\mathbb{T}}
invariant. For any
\zeta\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, we consider the flow of
-J\zeta
, say
\sigma_{t}\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
, and take the derivative at
t=0
of
{\bf D}_{v}(\sigma_{t}^{*}(\omega))
. By (
2.12
)

\frac{d}{dt}_{|_{t=0}}{\bf D}_{v}(\sigma_{t}^{*}(\omega))=\int_{X}\mu_{\omega}^{\zeta}\left(\frac{e^{\mathring{h}_{\omega}}}{\mathit{vol}(X)}-\frac{v(\mu_{\omega})}{\mathit{vol}_{v}(X)}\right)\omega^{[n]}=-{\rm Fut}_{v}(\zeta),

where we have used
(
2.4
) for the canonically normalized momentum map
\mu_{\omega}
. The claim follows from the above by a standard argument.
∎

We end-up this section with stating one of the main results of
[
37
]
, which gives an analytic criterion for the existence of a
v
-soliton on
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
in terms of
{\bf D}_{v}
. To state it, we recall the definition
[
5
]
of the Aubin functional
{\bf J}:\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)\to{\mathbb{R}}
:

{\bf J}(\varphi):=\int_{X}\varphi\omega_{0}^{[n]}-{\bf I}_{1}(\varphi),

which descends to
{\mathcal{H}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(X)/{\mathbb{R}}
, and has the property that
{\bf J}(\omega_{\varphi})\geq 0
with
{\bf J}(\omega_{\phi})=0
iff
\omega_{\varphi}=\omega_{0}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that
{\bf D}_{v}
is coercive relative to
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
if it is
{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathbb{C}}
-invariant and there are positive constants
\Lambda,C
such that

(2.13)

{\bf D}_{v}(\omega)\geq\Lambda\inf_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}}{\bf J}(\sigma^{*}(\omega))-C.

The constant
\Lambda>0
is called the slope of coercivity of
{\bf D}_{v}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
37
]
Let
X
be Fano manifold,
{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(X)
a maximal compact torus with canonical momentum polytope
\mathrm{P}_{X}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
. Let
v>0
be a positive smooth function on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
. Then
X
admits a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
v
-soliton in
2\pi c_{1}(X)
if and only if the
v
-weighted Ding functional
{\bf D}_{v}
is coercive with respect to the complex torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
.

Versions of the above theorem have been known for KRS by the works of Cao–Tian–Zhu (see
[
16
, Theorems 0.1 and 0.2]
) and Darvas–Rubinstein (see
[
23
, Theorem 2.11]
).

3.
A quantitative openness result: Proof of Theorem
1.1

We have the following elementary

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
v_{1},v_{2}
are positive weights with respective normalizations
\mathring{v}_{1}
and
\mathring{v}_{2}
such that

\inf_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}\left({\mathring{v}}_{1}-{\mathring{v}}_{2}\right)=-\lambda_{0},\qquad\lambda_{0}>0.

Then, there exists a constant
C_{v_{1},v_{2}}
such that for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)

\left({\bf D}_{v_{1}}(\omega_{\varphi})-{\bf D}_{v_{2}}(\omega_{\varphi})\right)\geq-\lambda_{0}{\bf J}(\omega_{\varphi})+C_{v_{1},v_{2}}.

Proof.

Using that
{\bf D}_{v}={\bf D}_{\mathring{v}}
, we have

\begin{split}\left({\bf D}_{v_{1}}(\omega_{\varphi})-{\bf D}_{v_{2}}(\omega_{\varphi})\right)&=-\left({\bf I}_{\mathring{v}_{1}}(\varphi)-{\bf I}_{\mathring{v}_{2}}(\varphi)\right)\\
&={\bf J}_{\mathring{v}_{1}}(\varphi)-{\bf J}_{\mathring{v}_{2}}(\varphi)-\int_{X}\varphi\mathring{v}_{1}(\mu_{\omega_{0}})\omega_{0}^{[n]}+\int_{X}\varphi\mathring{v}_{2}(\mu_{\omega_{0}})\omega_{0}^{[n]},\end{split}

where
{\bf J}_{v}
denotes the
v
-weighted Aubin–Mabuchi functional defined by

{\bf J}_{v}(\varphi):=\int_{X}\varphi\,v(\mu_{\omega_{0}})\omega_{0}^{[n]}-{\bf I}_{v}(\varphi).

It is easy to check from the above formula that
{\bf J}_{v}(\varphi)={\bf J}_{v}(\omega_{\varphi})
is a functional defined on the space of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metrics
\omega_{\varphi}\in\alpha
, which is
linear
in
v
. Another key property established in
[
37
]
is that if
v>0
on
\mathrm{P}
, then

{\bf J}_{v}(\omega_{\varphi})\geq 0.

The above is actually true, by continuity in
v
(see
[
3
, Lemma 6.5]
), even if we merely assume
v\geq 0
on
P
. We thus have, by the assumption in the proposition,

{\bf J}_{\mathring{v}_{1}}(\varphi)-{\bf J}_{\mathring{v}_{2}}(\varphi)+\lambda_{0}{\bf J}(\varphi)={\bf J}_{\mathring{v}_{1}-{\mathring{v}_{2}}+\lambda_{0}}(\varphi)\geq 0,

which leads to the inequality

\left({\bf D}_{v_{1}}(\omega_{\varphi})-{\bf D}_{v_{2}}(\omega_{\varphi})\right)\geq-\lambda_{0}{\bf J}(\omega_{\varphi})+\int_{X}\varphi\mathring{v}_{2}(\mu_{\omega_{0}})\omega_{0}^{[n]}-\int_{X}\varphi\mathring{v}_{1}(\mu_{\omega_{0}})\omega_{0}^{[n]}.

Proposition
3.1
then follows from Lemma
3.1
below.
∎

Lemma 3.1
.

[
37
, Lemma 13]
Let
\mathring{v}
be a normalized positive weigh-function on
\mathrm{P}_{X}
. Then, there exists a uniform positive constant
C_{\mathring{v}}>0
, such that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)
,

\sup_{X}\varphi-C_{\mathring{v}}\leq\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathring{v}(\mu_{\omega_{0}})\omega_{0}^{[n]}\leq\sup_{X}\varphi.

Proof.

The RHS of the inequality is obvious. To obtain the LHS we observe that the inequality is invariant under translations of
\varphi
with a constant, so we can assume without loss that
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
. In this case, we need to prove

\int_{X}\varphi\mathring{v}(\mu_{\omega_{0}})\omega_{0}^{[n]}\geq-C_{\mathring{v}}.

The above is established for instance in
[
23
, (27)]
for
\mathring{v}=\mathring{1}=1/{\rm Vol}(X)
. For a general weight function
\mathring{v}>0
, we let

\sup_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}\mathring{v}=\lambda_{\mathring{v}}>0.

Using
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
we then have

\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathring{v}(\mu_{\omega_{0}})\omega_{0}^{[n]}\geq\lambda_{\mathring{v}}\int_{X}\varphi\omega_{0}^{[n]}\geq-C_{\mathring{1}}\lambda_{\mathring{v}}=:-C_{\mathring{v}}.

∎

Applying Theorem
2.1
and the previous observation we get the following quantitative openness result.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\Lambda_{0}>0
be such that
{\bf D}_{v_{0}}
is coercive with respect to
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
of slope
\Lambda_{0}
. Then for all
v\in\mathcal{F}(X)
such that

0\leq-\inf_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}(\mathring{v}-\mathring{v}_{0})=\lambda_{0}<\Lambda_{0}

{\bf D}_{v}
is coercive relative to
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
of slope
(\Lambda-\lambda_{0})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
v_{0}>0
be a weight such that
{\bf D}_{v_{0}}
is coercive relative to
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
of slope
\Lambda_{0}>0
.
Suppose
v>0
is a weight such that

\big\|v-v_{0}\big\|_{C^{0}(\mathrm{P}_{X})}=\varepsilon.

For
\varepsilon
small enough, the above inequality yields

\inf_{\mathrm{P}_{X}}(\mathring{v}-\mathring{v}_{0})=-\lambda_{0},\qquad 0<\lambda_{0}<\Lambda_{0}.

We also assume that
{{\rm Fut}}_{v}\equiv 0
. By Lemma
2.1
, we know that the latter condition is equivalent to
{\bf D}_{v}
being
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant. We let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)
and denote by
\omega^{*}_{\varphi}
a Kähler metric in the
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-orbit of
\omega_{\varphi}
, such that

\inf_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}}{\bf J}(\sigma^{*}\omega_{\varphi})={\bf J}(\omega^{*}_{\varphi}).

Such a metric exists by
[
37
, Lemma 29]
.
By the argument in the proof of Lemma
3.1
above and the
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariance of
{\bf D}_{v}
and
{\bf D}_{v_{0}}
, we have

\begin{split}\left({\bf D}_{v}-{\bf D}_{v_{0}}\right)(\omega_{\varphi})&=\left({\bf D}_{v}-{\bf D}_{v_{0}}\right)(\omega^{*}_{\varphi})\\
&\geq-\lambda_{0}{\bf J}(\omega^{*}_{\varphi})-C_{v}=-\lambda_{0}\inf_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}}{\bf J}(\sigma^{*}\omega_{\varphi})-C_{v}.\end{split}

As
{\bf D}_{v_{0}}
is
\Lambda_{0}
-coercive relative to
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
and
\Lambda_{0}>\lambda_{0}
, we conclude that

{\bf D}_{v}
is also coercive relative to
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
.

The convexity of
\mathcal{D}(X)
follows from the fact that the subspace of normalized weight functions is linearly convex. ∎

4.
Sasaki geometry and transversal Kähler–Ricci solitons.

In this section, we recall a notion of special Sasaki geometry introduced in
[
32
]
. We use the point of view of
[
2
]
, which will allow us to recast the possibly irregular transversal Kähler geometry of a Sasaki manifold in terms of the Kähler geometry of a given regular or quasi-regular quotient.

4.1.
Sasaki structures

We consider the following general set up:
(N,{\amathscr D}_{0},J_{0})
is a compact
(2n+1)
-dimensional strictly pseudo-convex CR manifold invariant under the action of a compact torus
\hat{\mathbb{T}}
whose Lie algebra is denoted by
\hat{\mathfrak{t}}
. We say that
\hat{\xi}\in\hat{\mathfrak{t}}
is a
Sasaki–Reeb vector field
if
\hat{\xi}
is transversal to
{\amathscr D}_{0}
and the corresponding contact
1
-form
\eta_{0}^{\hat{\xi}}
, which vanishes on
{\amathscr D}
and is equal to
1
when evaluated at
\hat{\xi}
, defines a transversal Kähler form
d\eta_{0}^{\hat{\xi}}
on
({\amathscr D}_{0},J_{0})
, i.e.
(d\eta_{0}^{\hat{\xi}})_{|_{{\amathscr D}_{0}}}>0
. We denote by
\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_{+}(N)\subset\hat{\mathfrak{t}}
the
Sasaki–Reeb
cone of Sasaki–Reeb vector fields, and assume that
\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_{+}(N)
is non-empty. For any
\hat{\xi}\in\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_{+}(N)
, the data
(\hat{\xi},\eta_{0}^{\hat{\xi}},{\amathscr D}_{0},J_{0})
is referred to as a
Sasaki structure
on
N
.

Example 4.1

(regular Sasaki structures)
.

A basic example of Sasaki manifolds are the so-called
regular Sasaki
structures, described as follows. Let
X
be a smooth compact complex manifold
X
polarized by a line bundle
L
. Consider the unitary
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
-bundle
N_{\omega_{0}}\subset L^{-1}
with respect to the Hermitian metric
H_{\omega_{0}}
on
L^{-1}
whose curvature is
-i\omega_{0}
. Here,
\omega_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{2\pi c_{1}(L)}(X)
, where
{\mathbb{T}}
is a maximal torus in the group of reduced automorphisms of
X
, corresponding to a maximal torus
\hat{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X,L)
. Clearly,
\hat{\mathbb{T}}
acts on
N_{\omega_{0}}
preserving the induced CR structures
({\amathscr D}_{0},J_{0})
. If
\hat{\chi}\in\hat{\mathfrak{t}}
denotes the generator of the
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
-action on the fibres, then
\hat{\chi}\in\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_{+}(N)
. The corresponding
1
-form
\eta_{0}^{\hat{\chi}}
is the unique connection
1
-form on
N_{\omega_{0}}
with curvature
d\eta_{0}^{\hat{\chi}}=\pi^{*}\omega_{0}
whereas
{\amathscr D}_{0}
becomes the horizontal distribution of
\eta_{0}^{\hat{\chi}}
. The transversal Kähler structure in this case is just the pull-back of
\omega_{0}
to
({\amathscr D}_{0},J_{0})
. Note that
X=N_{\omega_{0}}/{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{\hat{\chi}}
, which allows us to recover the Kähler structure
\omega_{0}
on
X
from the corresponding Sasaki structure
(\hat{\chi},\eta_{0}^{\hat{\chi}},{\amathscr D}_{0},J_{0})
on
N_{\omega_{0}}
. This correspondence also applies to periodic Sasaki-Reeb vector fields
\hat{\xi}\in\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_{+}(N)
, in which case
(N,\hat{\xi},{\amathscr D}_{0},J_{0})
is the (smooth) total space of an
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
-orbibundle over the Kähler orbifold
(X:=N/{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{\hat{\xi}},\omega_{0})
. This situation is referred to in the literature as a
quasi-regular
Sasaki structure.

We now introduce the variation spaces of Sasaki structures: For
\hat{\xi}\in\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_{+}(N)
on
(N,{\amathscr D}_{0},J_{0})
and
\eta^{\hat{\xi}}_{0}
the associated contact form, we let

\Xi_{\hat{\xi},\eta_{0}^{\hat{\xi}},J^{\hat{\xi}}}^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}(N):=\left\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(N)^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}\,\,|\,\,\eta_{\varphi}^{\hat{\xi}}:=\eta_{0}^{\hat{\xi}}+d^{c}_{\hat{\xi}}\varphi\,\,\mathrm{satisfies}\,\,d\eta_{\varphi}^{\hat{\xi}}>0\mbox{ on }{\amathscr D}_{\varphi}:=\ker(\eta_{\varphi}^{\hat{\xi}})\right\},

where
J^{\hat{\xi}}\in{\rm End}(TN)
extends
J_{0}\in{\rm End}({\amathscr D}_{0})
by letting
J^{\hat{\xi}}(\hat{\xi})=0
,

d^{c}_{\hat{\xi}}\varphi:=-d\varphi\circ J^{\hat{\xi}}
, and the positivity of
d\eta^{\hat{\xi}}_{\varphi}
on
{\amathscr D}_{\varphi}
is defined with respect to
J^{\hat{\xi}}_{|_{{\amathscr D}_{\varphi}}}
. We refer to
[
2
, §2]
for the basic properties of this space. In particular, for any
\varphi\in\Xi_{\hat{\xi},\eta_{0}^{\hat{\xi}},J^{\hat{\xi}}}^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}(N)
,
(\hat{\xi},\eta_{\varphi}^{\hat{\xi}},{\amathscr D}_{\varphi},J^{\hat{\xi}}_{|_{{\amathscr D}_{\varphi}}})
is a Sasaki structure and the space
\Xi_{\hat{\xi},\eta_{0}^{\hat{\xi}},J^{\hat{\xi}}}^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}(N)
is called a
slice of
(\hat{\xi},J^{\hat{\xi}})
-compatible
\hat{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Sasaki potentials
. We shall use through this paper the following identification established in
[
2
,
40
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any Sasaki–Reeb vector fields
\hat{\chi},\hat{\xi}\in\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_{+}(N)
, there exists a natural bijection

\Theta_{\hat{\chi},\hat{\xi}}:\Xi_{\hat{\xi},\eta_{0}^{\hat{\xi}},J^{\hat{\xi}}}^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}(N)\cong\Xi_{\hat{\chi},\eta_{0}^{\hat{\chi}},J^{\hat{\chi}}}^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}(N).

Remark 4.1
.

For a (quasi) regular Sasaki structure
(N,\hat{\chi},\eta_{0}^{\hat{\chi}},{\amathscr D}_{0},J_{0})
(see Example
4.1
), we have

\Xi_{\hat{\chi},\eta_{0}^{\hat{\chi}},J^{\hat{\chi}}}^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}(N)\equiv\mathcal{H}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X),

which, together with Proposition
4.1
, allows one to study
(\hat{\xi},J^{\hat{\xi}})
-compatible
\hat{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Sasaki structures on
N
via the induced Kähler geometry of
(M,{\mathbb{T}},\alpha=2\pi c_{1}(L))
.

4.2.
Kähler cones vs Sasaki structures

A smooth complex cone
[
21
,
2
]

(Y,J,\hat{\xi})
is a non-compact
(n+1)
-dimensional complex manifold endowed with a free holomorphic
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
-action (sometimes denoted by
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}_{-J\hat{\xi}}
) generated by the flow of a real holomorphic vector field
-J\hat{\xi}
. We shall further assume that
N=Y/{\mathbb{R}}_{-J\hat{\xi}}^{+}
is a compact
(2n+1)
-dimensional manifold
N
. A typical example is
Y=L^{\times}
, where
L
is a holomorphic line bundle over
X
and
L^{\times}
denotes the total space of
L
with its zero section removed. In this case, we can take
\hat{\chi}
be the generator of the natural fiberwise
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
-action on
L
, so that
L^{\times}/{\mathbb{R}}_{-J\hat{\chi}}^{+}=N
as in Example
4.1
.

We shall further fix a maximal compact torus
\hat{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(Y)
and consider free
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}_{-J\hat{\xi}}
-actions for
\hat{\xi}\in\hat{\mathfrak{t}}
, where
\hat{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm Lie}(\hat{\mathbb{T}})
. For any such
\hat{\xi}\in\hat{\mathfrak{t}}
, we consider the space of functions

{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\xi}}^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}(Y):=\left\{r\in C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{>0})^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}\,|\,{\mathcal{L}}_{-J\hat{\xi}}r=r,\,\,\hat{\omega}_{r}:={\frac{1}{4}}dd^{c}r^{2}>0\right\},

and assume that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\xi}^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}(Y)\neq\emptyset
for at least some
\hat{\xi}\in\hat{\mathfrak{t}}
;
clearly, for such a
\hat{\xi}
, any
r\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\xi}}^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}(Y)
gives rise to a Kähler cone metric
\hat{\omega}_{r}
, i.e. satisfying
{\mathcal{L}}_{-J\hat{\xi}}\hat{\omega}_{r}=2\hat{\omega}_{r}
with respect to the
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
-action generated by

admits a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant KRS with soliton vector field

.

give rise to certain holomorphic functions on the corresponding cone

should also lead to an a priori bound on the eigenvalues (also called

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a smooth polarized complex cone satisfying the conditions of Proposition
5.1
with respect to a maximal torus of automorphisms
\hat{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(Y)
. If

{\mathcal{L}}_{\hat{\xi}}\varphi=i\kappa\varphi
and

for some

and

(n-\lambda)>0.

Proof.

Let
r\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\xi}}^{\hat{\mathbb{T}}}(Y)
be the potential of a compatible transversal KRS cone metric on
(Y,\hat{\xi})
, with soliton vector field
\tau=\tau_{\hat{\xi}}\in{\mathfrak{t}}
. As in the proof of Proposition
5.1
, we shall work on the link
N:=r^{-1}(1)
, with the induced transversal KRS Sasaki structure
({\amathscr D},J,\hat{\xi},\eta^{\hat{\xi}})
. We still denote by
\omega:=d\eta^{\hat{\xi}}
the transversal Kähler structure and let

f:=\eta^{\hat{\xi}}(\tau_{\hat{\xi}})=\mu_{\omega}^{\tau}
be the transversal Killing potential of
\tau_{\hat{\xi}}

We will first prove that, when restricted on
N
,
\varphi
satisfies the inequality

(5.3)

\Delta_{\omega,f}(e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2})\leq 2\kappa(n-\lambda)e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2},

where
\Delta_{\omega,f}\psi:=\Delta_{\omega}\psi+\langle d\psi,df\rangle_{\omega}
is the twisted
\hat{\xi}
-basic Laplacian (recall that
\Delta_{\omega}
denotes the
\hat{\xi}
-basic Laplacian).

As
\varphi
is holomorphic, the assumptions equivalently read as

{\mathcal{L}}_{-J\hat{\xi}}\varphi=\kappa\varphi,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{-J\tau_{\hat{\xi}}}\varphi=\kappa\lambda\varphi,

and hence

(5.4)

{\mathcal{L}}_{\hat{\xi}}|\varphi|^{2}=0,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{-J\hat{\xi}}|\varphi|^{2}=2\kappa|\varphi|^{2},\qquad\mathcal{L}_{-J\tau_{\hat{\xi}}}|\varphi|^{2}=2\kappa\lambda|\varphi|^{2}.

At any point of
N
, we can write (see e.g.
[
1
, Lemma 1]
)

\tau_{\hat{\xi}}=f\hat{\xi}-\omega^{-1}(df),\qquad J\tau_{\hat{\xi}}=fJ\hat{\xi}-g_{\omega}^{-1}(df).

We get from the above

(5.6)

\langle d|\varphi|^{2},df\rangle_{\omega}=2\kappa(\lambda-f)|\varphi|^{2},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\omega}
stands for the inner product induced by the transversal Kähler structure
\omega
on
\hat{\xi}
-basic tensors. Together with (
5.2
), the latter yields

\left\langle d(e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2}),df\right\rangle_{\omega}=2\kappa e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2}\left(\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\omega}f\right).

A direct computation using (
5.6
) shows that

\Delta_{\omega}(e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2})=e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2}\left(-\kappa\Delta_{\omega}f-\kappa^{2}||df||^{2}_{\omega}+4\kappa^{2}\lambda-4\kappa^{2}f\right)+e^{-\kappa f}\Delta_{\omega}|\varphi|^{2},

so that, combining with (
5.2
), we obtain

(5.7)

\Delta_{\omega,f}(e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2})=e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2}\left(-\kappa^{2}||df||^{2}_{\omega}+2\kappa(2\kappa-1)\lambda-4\kappa^{2}f\right)+e^{-\kappa f}\Delta_{\omega}|\varphi|^{2}.

We next develop the term
\Delta_{\omega}|\varphi|^{2}
. To this end, recall that any holomorphic function
\varphi
on
Y
satisfies (on the open dense subset where
\varphi\neq 0
)

(5.8)

dd^{c}|\varphi|^{2}=2id\varphi\wedge d\bar{\varphi}=\frac{1}{|\varphi|^{2}}\left(d|\varphi|^{2}\wedge d^{c}|\varphi|^{2}\right).

Furthermore, if
\iota:N\hookrightarrow Y
denotes the inclusion and

Y

d^{c}_{\hat{\xi}}(\iota^{*}\psi)=\iota^{*}(d^{c}\psi)+(\mathcal{L}_{J\hat{\xi}}\psi)\eta^{\hat{\xi}},\qquad dd^{c}_{\xi}\psi=\iota^{*}(dd^{c}\psi)-d\left(({\mathcal{L}}_{-J\hat{\xi}}\psi)\eta^{\hat{\xi}}\right).

We use the previous two identities and (

) to compute

\Delta_{\omega}|\varphi|^{2}=-{\mathrmsl tr}_{\omega}dd^{c}_{\hat{\xi}}|\varphi|^{2}=-{\mathrmsl tr}_{\omega}(2id\varphi\wedge d\bar{\varphi})+2n\kappa|\varphi|^{2}.

Substituting back in (

) and regrouping the terms yields

\begin{split}\Delta_{\omega,f}(e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2})=&2\kappa e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2}\left(n-\lambda\right)\\
&+2\kappa^{2}e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2}\left(-\frac{||df||^{2}_{\omega}}{2}+2\lambda-2f\right)\\
&-e^{-\kappa f}{\mathrmsl tr}_{\omega}(2id\varphi\wedge d\bar{\varphi})\end{split}

Working outside the zero locus of
\varphi
for a moment, we compute

||d(e^{-\frac{\kappa f}{2}}|\varphi|)||^{2}_{\omega}=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}|\varphi|^{2}e^{-\kappa f}(||df||^{2}_{\omega})-\kappa|\varphi|e^{-\kappa f}\langle df,d|\varphi|\rangle_{\omega}+e^{-\kappa f}||d|\varphi|||^{2}_{\omega}.

Using (

\begin{split}\Delta_{\omega,f}(e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2})=&2\kappa e^{-\kappa f}|\varphi|^{2}\left(n-\lambda\right)\\

m_{x}
. We can thus set

\mathit{Vol}_{v}((-b^{*}K_{X}-x[D])):=\frac{1}{m_{x}^{n}}\left(\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{n!}{k^{n}}\sum_{\alpha\in\hat{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}}v\left(\frac{\alpha}{m_{x}k}\right)\dim\left(H^{0}(\hat{X},km_{x}(-b^{*}K_{X}-x[D]))\right)_{\alpha}\right),

which is independent of
m_{x}
. The above definition yields the monotonicity property

(6.2)

\mathit{Vol}_{v_{1}}(-b^{*}K_{X}-x[D])\leq\mathit{Vol}_{v_{2}}(-b^{*}K_{X}-x[D])\qquad\forall\,v_{1}\leq v_{2}\,\textrm{on}\,\mathrm{P}_{X},

which we shall use below.

The weighted
\beta
invariant of
D\subset\hat{X}
is then introduced by

(6.3)

\beta_{v}(D):=A_{X}(D)\mathit{Vol}_{v}(-K_{X})-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathit{Vol}_{v}(b^{*}(-K_{X})-x[D])dx,

where
A_{X}(D)
stands for the log discrepancy of the prime divisor
D
, see
[
26
, Definition 2.5]
. In the case when
D\subset\hat{X}
is the exceptional divisor as above,
A_{X}(D)=n
(see e.g.
[
29
]
).

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

From
[
37
, Theorems 1.7 & 5.18]
it follows that under the hypothesis of Theorem
1.5
, we have
\beta_{v}(D)\geq 0
, i.e.

\frac{1}{n}A_{X}(D)\mathit{Vol}_{v}(-K_{X})=\mathit{Vol}_{v}(-K_{X})\geq\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathit{Vol}_{v}(-b^{*}K_{X}-x[D]).

Let
m_{v}:=\underset{\mu\in\mathrm{P}_{X}}{\min}v(\mu)>0
. By (
6.2
) we have

\mathit{Vol}_{v}(-b^{*}K_{X}-x[D])\geq m_{v}\mathit{Vol}(-b^{*}K_{X}-x[D])

whereas by
[
29
, Theorem 2.3]

\mathit{Vol}(b^{*}K_{X}-x[D])\geq\mathit{Vol}(-K_{X})-x^{n}.

The above inequalities give

\begin{split}\mathit{Vol}_{v}(-K_{X})\geq&\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathit{Vol}_{v}(-b^{*}K_{X}-x[D])dx\\
\geq&\frac{m_{v}}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathit{Vol}(-b^{*}K_{X}-x[D])dx\\
\geq&\frac{m_{v}}{n}\int_{0}^{(\mathit{Vol}(-K_{X}))^{1/n}}\mathit{Vol}(-b^{*}K_{X}-x[D])dx\\
\geq&\frac{m_{v}}{n}\int_{0}^{(\mathit{Vol}(-K_{X}))^{1/n}}\left(\mathit{Vol}(-K_{X})-x^{n}\right)dx\\
=&\frac{m_{v}}{n}\left((\mathit{Vol}(-K_{X}))^{1/n}\mathit{Vol}(-K_{X})-\frac{1}{n+1}(\mathit{Vol}(-K_{X}))^{(n+1)/n}\right)\\
=&\frac{m_{v}}{n+1}(\mathit{Vol}(-K_{X}))^{(n+1)/n}\end{split}

Under the normalization
\mathit{Vol}_{v}(-K_{X})=\mathit{Vol}(-K_{X})
for the weight
v
, we obtain

\mathit{Vol}(-K_{X})\leq\left(\frac{n+1}{m_{v}}\right)^{n}.

The claim follows by (
6.1
).∎

Remark 6.1
.

More generally, let
(X,L,{\mathbb{T}})
be a polarized projective manifold and
{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X,L)
a lift of the action of a real torus in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, with corresponding momentum polytope
\mathrm{P}_{L}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
. For any two weight functions
v>0
,
w
on
\mathrm{P}_{L}
, and a
{\mathbb{T}}
-stable prime divisor
D
of
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
, one can associate the
(v,w)
-weighted
\beta
-invariant of
D
by the formula

(6.4)

\beta_{v,w}(D):=A_{X}(D)\mathit{Vol}_{v}(L)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathit{Vol}_{w}(L-x[D])dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathit{Vol}^{\prime}_{v}(L-x[D])\cdot[K_{X}]dx,

where, as in
[
26
]
,
\mathit{Vol}^{\prime}_{v}(L-x[D])\cdot[K_{X}]dx
stands for the (formal) derivative of
\mathit{Vol}_{v}
in the direction of the canonical bundle
K_{X}
. The above formula extends the “unweighted” situation with
v=1,w=2n\left(\frac{c_{1}(L)\cdot L^{n-1}}{L^{n}}\right)
studied in
[
26
]
. In general, in (
6.4
), we assume the differentiability of the extension of
\mathit{Vol}_{v}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
-line bundles in each direction, a fact established in the case
v=1
in
[
12
]
but which is open in general.

Similarly to
[
26
, Corollary 3.11]
, using integration by parts, one can rewrite (
6.4
) as

\beta_{v,w}(D)=A_{X}(D)\mathit{Vol}_{v}(L)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathit{Vol}_{w-(\tilde{v}+v)}(L-x[D])dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathit{Vol}^{\prime}_{v}(L-x[D])\cdot[L+K_{X}]dx.

When
X
is Fano,
L=-K_{X}
and
w=\tilde{v}:=2(n+d\log v)v
,
the above reduces to (
6.3
), showing the consistency of (
6.4
). It would be interesting to show that, similarly to the unweighted case
[
26
]
, the value
\beta_{v,w}(D)
is related to the
(v,w)
-weighted Donaldson-Futaki invariant
[
41
]
of a certain test configuration.

Appendix A
Smooth deformations of weighted cscK metrics

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold endowed with a Kähler class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
and
{\mathbb{T}}\subset{\rm Aut}_{r}(X)
a maximal torus with momentum polytope
\mathrm{P}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
. Let
v,w\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{P}),\,v>0
be a pair of weight functions on
\mathrm{P}
. Recall that
(v,w)
-weighted Futaki invariant (see
[
41
]
) vanishes if for some (and hence any)
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric
\omega\in\alpha
and for any affine-linear function
\ell(x)
on
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
,

{\rm Fut}_{v,w}(\ell):=\int_{X}\left({\rm Scal}_{v}(\omega)-w(\mu_{\omega_{\omega}})\right)\,\ell(\mu_{\omega})\omega^{[n]}=0,

where
{\rm Scal}_{v}(\omega)
stands for the
v
-scalar curvature of
\omega
given by

{\rm Scal}_{v}(\omega):=v(\mu_{\omega}){\rm Scal}(\omega)+2\Delta_{\omega}v(\mu_{\omega})+\big\langle g_{\omega},\mu_{\omega}^{*}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}(v)\right)\big\rangle,

with
{\rm Scal}(\omega)
being the usual scalar curvature of the Riemannian metric
g_{\omega}
associated to
\omega
,
\Delta_{\omega}
standing for the Laplace operator of
g_{\omega}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denoting the contraction between the smooth
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
-valued function
g_{\omega}
on
X
(the restriction of the Riemannian metric
g_{\omega}
to
{\mathfrak{t}}\subset C^{\infty}(X,TX)
) and the smooth
{\mathfrak{t}}\otimes{\mathfrak{t}}
-valued function
{\mu_{\omega}}^{*}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}(v)\right)
on
X
(given by the pull-back by
\mu_{\omega}
of
{\rm Hess}(v)\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{P},{\mathfrak{t}}\otimes{\mathfrak{t}})
).

The following result is a slight modification of
[
41
, Theorem B2]
(see also
[
36
, Theorem 6.1.2]
for the special case when
w
is fixed), where the weight functions
(v_{t},w_{t})
are supposed to be positive on
\mathrm{P}
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\{(v_{t},w_{t}),\,\,v_{t},w_{t}\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{P}),\,v_{t}>0,\,\,t\in U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}
be a finite dimensional smooth family of weight functions on
\mathrm{P}
, parametrized by a neighbourhood
U
of
0
and satisfying
{\rm Fut}_{v_{t},w_{t}}\equiv 0
for all
t\in U
. Suppose
\omega_{0}
is a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric in
\alpha
such that

{\rm Scal}_{v_{0}}(\omega_{0})=w_{0}(\mu_{\omega_{0}}).

Then, there exists
\epsilon>0
and a differentiable family of smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metrics
\omega_{t}\in\alpha
, such that for
||t||<\epsilon

{\rm Scal}_{v_{t}}(\omega_{t})=w_{t}(\mu_{\omega_{t}}),

where
\mu_{\omega_{t}}:X\to\mathrm{P}
is the
\omega_{t}
-momentum map of
{\mathbb{T}}
normalized by
\mu_{\omega_{t}}(X)=\mathrm{P}
.

Proof.

The proof is a standard adaptation of the arguments in
[
43
]
, using the computations in
[
41
]
.

Consider the Fréchet space
C^{\infty}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
of smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant functions on
X
and denote by
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)
the subset of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
\omega_{0}
-relative Kähler potentials; for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)
, we let
\omega_{\varphi}:=\omega_{0}+dd^{c}\varphi>0
be the corresponding Kähler structure on
X
,
\mu_{\varphi}:=\mu_{\omega_{0}}+d^{c}\varphi
be the normalized
\omega_{\varphi}
momentum map, and we let

\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{\varphi}}:=\{\ell(\mu_{\varphi}),\,\ell\in{\rm Aff}(\mathrm{P})\}

denote the space of all
\omega_{\varphi}
-Hamiltonians of elements of
{\rm Lie}({\mathbb{T}})
. We denote by

\Pi_{\omega_{\varphi}}:C^{\infty}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}\to\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{\varphi}}

the orthogonal projection with respect to the global
L^{2}
product defined by
\omega_{\varphi}^{[n]}
and let

C^{\infty}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}:=(1-\Pi_{\omega_{0}})(C^{\infty}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}})

be the space of smooth
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant functions on
X
, which are
L^{2}
-orthogonal to the finite dimensional space
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
.

We consider the map
\Psi:U\times\left(\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)\cap C^{\infty}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}\right)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times C^{\infty}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
defined by

\Psi(t,\varphi):=\Big(t,(1-\Pi_{\omega_{0}})\left({\rm Scal}_{v_{t}}(\omega_{\varphi})-w_{t}(\mu_{\varphi})\right)\Big).

By the computations in
[
41
]
and using that
{\rm Scal}_{v_{0}}(\omega_{0})=w_{0}(\mu_{\omega_{0}})
, the differential
(T_{(0,0)}\Psi)
of
\Psi
at
(0,0)
in the direction of
\varphi
is given by

(T_{(0,0)}\Psi)(0,\dot{\varphi})=\left(0,-2\left((1-\Pi_{\omega_{0}}\right)\left(\delta_{\omega_{0}}\delta_{\omega_{0}}(v_{0}(\mu_{\omega_{0}})(Dd\dot{\varphi})^{-}\right)\right),

where
\delta_{\omega_{0}}T:=-\sum_{i=1}^{2n}(D_{e_{i}}T)(e_{i},\cdot)

denotes the codifferential of a
(p,0)
-tensor
T
with respect to
\omega_{0}
,
D
stands for the Levi-Civita connection of
\omega_{0}
and
(Dd\dot{\varphi})^{-}
is the
(2,0)+(0,2)
part of the Hessian of
\dot{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
.

Notice that the weighted Lichnerowicz operator
{\mathbb{L}}_{\omega_{0}}(\dot{\varphi}):=\delta_{\omega_{0}}\delta_{\omega_{0}}\Big(v_{0}(\mu_{\omega_{0}})(Dd\dot{\varphi})^{-}\Big)
is a linear self-adjoint operator with respect to the
L^{2}
inner product of
\omega_{0}
, whose kernel in
C^{\infty}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
consists of all
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Killing potentials of
\omega_{0}
. As
{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}_{r}(X)
is a maximal torus, we conclude that the kernel of
{\mathbb{L}}_{\omega_{0}}
is
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
and its image is in
C^{\infty}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
. Thus, by standard linear elliptic theory,
{\mathbb{L}}_{\omega_{0}}
(and hence
\dot{\varphi}\to(T_{0,0}\Psi)(0,\dot{\varphi})
) is an isomorphism when restricted to
C^{\infty}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
, and hence also when viewed as a linear map between the Banach spaces
W^{k+4}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
and
W^{k}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
, where for any positive integer
k
,
W^{k}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
denotes the completion in
L^{2}(X,\omega_{0})
of
C^{\infty}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
with respect to the Sobolev norm involving derivatives up to order
k
, and
W^{k}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}:=(1-\Pi_{\omega_{0}})(W^{k}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}})
. By Sobolev’s embedding theorem, for
k
large enough, we have
W^{k+4}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}\subset C^{4}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
. As
{\rm Scal}_{v_{t}}(\omega_{\varphi})
is a 4th order quasilinear operator in
\varphi
,
\Psi(t,\varphi)
admits an extension (still denoted by
\Psi
) to a
C^{1}
map
\Psi:U\times\left(\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)\cap W^{k+4}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}\right)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times W^{k}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
, where the open subspace
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)\cap W^{k+4}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
is of
C^{2}
-Kähler metrics in
\alpha
. Applying the implicit function theorem to
\Psi
and using bootstraping elliptic regularity for the map
\varphi\to{\rm Scal}_{v}(\omega_{\varphi})
, we conclude that we can find a differentaible path of potentials
\varphi_{t}\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}_{\omega_{0}}(X)\cap C^{\infty}_{\perp}(X)^{{\mathbb{T}}},\,|t|<\epsilon_{0}
with
\varphi_{0}\equiv 0
, such that

{\rm Scal}_{v_{t}}(\omega_{\varphi_{t}})-w_{t}(\mu_{\varphi_{t}})=\Pi_{\omega_{0}}\left({\rm Scal}_{v_{t}}(\omega_{\varphi_{t}})-w_{t}(\mu_{\varphi_{t}})\right).

Now, by the assumption
{\rm Fut}_{v_{t},w_{t}}=0
,
\Pi_{\omega_{\varphi_{t}}}\left({\rm Scal}_{v_{t}}(\omega_{\varphi_{t}})-w_{t}(\mu_{\varphi_{t}})\right)=0
. Thus, for
||t||<\epsilon
small enough, one also has
\Pi_{\omega_{0}}({\rm Scal}_{v_{t}}(\omega_{\varphi_{t}})-w_{t}(\mu_{\varphi_{t}})=0
, which finishes the proof.
∎

The above deformation result applies in particular in the case of a
v_{0}
-soliton and a smooth family weights
v_{t}\in\mathcal{F}(X)
.

Corollary A.1
.

Let
(X,{\mathbb{T}})
be a smooth Fano manifold with canonically normalized polytope
\mathrm{P}_{X}
and
\{v_{t}\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{P}_{X}),\,t\in U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}
be a finite dimensional smooth family of positive weight functions, parameterized by a neighbourhood
0\in U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
, which satisfy
{\rm Fut}_{v_{t}}\equiv 0
for all
t\in U
. Suppose
v_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(X)
, i.e.
X
admits a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
v_{0}
-soliton. Then there exits
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
||t||<\varepsilon
,
v_{t}\in\mathcal{S}(X)
.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Prop.1]
, a
v
-soliton
\omega
on
(X,{\mathbb{T}},2\pi c_{1}(X))
can be characterized by the property

{\rm Scal}_{v}(\omega)=\tilde{v}(\mu_{\omega}),

where
\tilde{v}(x):=2(n+\langle d\log v,x\rangle)v(x)
. Furthermore, by
[
3
, Lemma B1]
we have

{\rm Fut}_{v}\equiv 0\,\Leftrightarrow\,{\rm Fut}_{v,\tilde{v}}\equiv 0.

The claim then follows from Lemma
A.1
.
∎

Appendix B
Curvature identities for
v
-solitons

Lemma B.1
.

[
54
]
Suppose
(g,J,\omega)
is a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric which is a KRS, i.e. satisfies

(B.1)

{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}f,

where
f
is the potential of a real Killing and holomorphic vector field
\tau=Jgrad_{g}f
.
Then the scalar curvature
{\rm Scal}(\omega)
satisfies the following identity

(B.2)

\frac{1}{2}\Delta{\rm Scal}(\omega)-\langle d{\rm Scal}(\omega),df\rangle_{g}+{\rm Scal}(\omega)=||{\mathrm{Ric}}_{g}||^{2}.

Proof.

This is the forth formula in
[
54
, Lemma 2.5]
(which generally holds for any gradient Ricci soliton). We reproduce its proof in our Kähler situation for convenience of the Reader.

We denote by
{\mathrm{Ric}}_{g}
the (symmetric) Ricci tensor of
g
, so that the (Kähler) Ricci form is
{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)(\cdot,\cdot)=\langle{\mathrm{Ric}}_{g}J\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
As
\tau:=J\mathrm{grad}_{g}f
is a Killing vector field, we have

\nabla d^{c}f=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}f,

and, by Bochner’s identity,

\Delta(d^{c}f)=2{\mathrm{Ric}}_{g}(d^{c}f)
or, equivalently,

(B.3)

-d\left(\Delta f\right)=2{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)(\tau).

Taking trace with respect to
\omega
in the soliton equation (
B.1
) yields

{\rm Scal}(\omega)-2n=-\Delta f,

so that we have

(B.4)

d{\rm Scal}(\omega)=-\Delta df=2{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)(\tau),\qquad\Delta{\rm Scal}(\omega)=-\Delta\Delta f.

Applying the covariant derivative
\nabla_{X}
in (
B.1
) yields

(B.5)

\nabla_{X}\left({\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{X}(dd^{c}f).

Taking interior product with
\xi
in (
B.5
) and using (
B.4
) and (
B.1
) again gives

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{X}dd^{c}f)(\tau)&=\left(\nabla_{X}{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)\right)(\tau)=\nabla_{X}({\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)(\tau))-{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)(\nabla_{X}\tau)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{X}d{\rm Scal}(\omega)-{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega)({\mathrm{Ric}}_{g}(JX)-JX)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{X}d{\rm Scal}(\omega)+{\mathrm{Ric}}_{g}({\mathrm{Ric}}_{g}(X)-X).\end{split}

Taking trace gives

\frac{1}{2}(\Delta d^{c}f)(\tau)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta{\rm Scal}(\omega)+||{\mathrm{Ric}}_{g}||^{2}-{\rm Scal}(\omega).

By (
B.4
) we have
\frac{1}{2}(\Delta d^{c}f)(\tau)=-\frac{1}{2}\langle d{\rm Scal},df\rangle
, so we finally get (
B.2
). ∎

As an immediate corollary, we get the following fact, which we presume is well-known to experts, but which we were not able to trace in the literature.

Corollary B.1
.

Suppose
(N,{\amathscr D},J,\hat{\xi},\eta_{\hat{\xi}})
is a compact Sasaki manifold which is a transversal KRS. Then the transversal scalar curvature is everywhere positive.

Proof.

We denote by
{\rm Scal}(\omega)
the transversal scalar curvature and consider a local
\hat{\xi}
-quotient
(U,J,\omega,g)
around a point of global minimum of
{\rm Scal}(\omega)
on
N
. Notice that
(U,J)
is a (open) complex manifold and
\omega
descends to define a KRS on
U
. By Lemma
B.1
, the smooth function
{\rm Scal}(\omega)
satisfies on
U
the identity (
B.2
).
As
{\rm Scal}(\omega)
achieves its minimum in the interior of
U
, we get by the strong maximum principle
{\rm Scal}(\omega)>0
on
U
, and hence on
N
.
∎

On the breakdown point of transport-based quantiles
Marco Avella-Medina, Alberto González-Sanz

Abstract
Recent work has used optimal transport ideas to generalize the notion of (center-outward) quantiles to dimension d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . We study the robustness properties of these transport-based quantiles by deriving their breakdown point, roughly, the smallest amount of contamination required to make these quantiles take arbitrarily aberrant values. We prove that the transport median defined in Chernozhukov et al. (2017) and Hallin et al. (2021) has breakdown point of 1 / 2 1/2 . Moreover, a point in the transport depth contour of order τ ∈ [ 0 , 1 / 2 ] \tau\in[0,1/2] has breakdown point of τ \tau . This shows that the multivariate transport depth shares the same breakdown properties as its univariate counterpart. Our proof relies on a general argument connecting the breakdown point of transport maps evaluated at a point to the Tukey depth of that point in the reference measure.



Keywords
: Center-outward quantiles, contamination model, multivariate medians, optimal transport, robustness, Tukey depth.

Introduction

Recently,
[
9
,
27
]
proposed a new notion of multivariate quantiles based on mass transportation. They naturally lead to multivariate definitions of ranks and signs that are distribution free and can be used to construct statistically efficient nonparametric tests
[
27
,
12
,
61
,
13
]
. The idea behind transport quantiles is to preserve two fundamental properties of the quantile function
Q_{X}
of a univariate random variable

[0,1]
forward to
P
; and it is monotone, i.e., it is the derivative of a convex function. The transport map provides a natural multivariate analogue construction. Given a random vector
X\sim P
and a
reference measure

\mu

\mathbf{Q}_{\pm}
pushing
\mu
forward to
P
. The existence and uniqueness of
\mathbf{Q}_{\pm}
is guaranteed by McCann’s Theorem
[
47
]
.

The univariate empirical median is one the most fundamental examples of a robust statistic. It estimates consistently the population median and it is highly robust in the presence of outliers. In particular, the minimum fraction of outlying sample values required for the median to take arbitrarily large values is
1/2
. In other words, its breakdown point is
1/2
. In this paper we show that the breakdown point of the multivariate transport-based median is also
1/2
. More generally, we show that the transport-based depth contour of order
\tau\in[0,1/2]
has breakdown point
\tau
for natural choices of reference measures. This general result is analogous to the breakdown point of the empirical univariate quantile of order
q=k/n\in(0,1)
which is exactly

\min(k,n+1-k)/n
. Note that the breakdown points that we find are dimension independent and they are attained by computationally efficient estimators, unlike many classical high breakdown methods in the robust statistics literature
[
45
,
11
,
18
,
63
,
66
]
. Anecdotally, this high breakdown point result for the transport-based median came as a surprise to us, as our initial intuition was that the breakdown point was low and we were trying to prove it.

The proof of the breakdown point of the transport-based quantile contours relies on an unexpected relation between the optimal transport problem and the Tukey depth. We show that the breakdown point of transport maps evaluated at a point of the support of the reference measure is equal to the Tukey depth of that point in the reference measure. Therefore in order to maximize the breakdown point of transport quantiles, one has to choose reference measures
\mu
that attain the maximal Tukey depth of
1/2
. This is the case of natural choices of
\mu
; c.f.
1.4
and
3.11
.

Our breakdown point results cover both the finite sample breakdown point of transport-based quantiles as well as the asymptotic breakdown point
[

,
30
]

1.1
Breakdown point of the optimal transport map

It turns out that studying the finite sample breakdown point of empirical transport-based quantiles, is equivalent to answering the following question, which itself is of independent interest:

Question 1.1
.

Given a sample
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
, what is the minimum fraction of bad sample values required to make the optimal transport map from
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
to
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
evaluated at a particular
u_{i}
arbitrarily aberrant?

Before delving into details, let us recall the definition of the optimal transport problem for discrete measures. We refer to
[
64
]
for an in-depth exposition on the topic. Let
X^{(n)}=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}
and

u^{(n)}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
be two finite sequence points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We call
X^{(n)}
and
u^{(n)}

target
and
reference
samples.

We denote by
\Gamma(u^{(n)},X^{(n)})
the set of all of bijective mappings
T:u^{(n)}\to X^{(n)}
. An
empirical optimal transport map

\hat{T}=\hat{T}_{X^{(n)}}
is any solution of the (quadratic) optimal transport problem

\min_{T\in\Gamma(u^{(n)},X^{(n)})}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T(u_{i})-u_{i}\|^{2}.

(1)

Answering Question
1.1
is equivalent to determining the
finite sample breakdown point
of the estimator
\hat{T}(u_{i})
, i.e.,

{\rm BP}(\hat{T}(u_{i}))=\frac{1}{n}\min\left\{\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}:\ \sup_{Z^{(n)}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\ell,n}}\|\hat{T}(u_{i})-\hat{T}_{Z^{(n)}}(u_{i})\|=\infty\right\},

where
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell,n}
is the set of all sets
Z^{(n)}=\{Z_{1},\dots Z_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, sharing at least
n-\ell
elements with
X^{(n)}
. Since the transport problem (
1

[
28
,
34
,
44
]
. In particular, one could expect the finite sample breakdown point to be
1/n
. We will see that this is not at all the case .

The breakdown point is one of the simplest and most popular tools for quantifying robustness in statistics. The finite sample definition given above was introduced by
[

30

The median and more generally quantiles are fundamental statistics. However, the definitions of median and quantiles, based on the canonical order of the real line, do not naturally extend to multivariate data. In higher dimensions, statistical depth theory offers an alternative. The literature provides various notions of depth, such as Tukey depth, spatial depth, integrated depth, or lens depth (see
[
50
]
and references therein). The most prominent example is undoubtedly the Tukey (or halfspace) depth

It takes values in the interval

are regarded as more extreme. The breakdown point of the Tukey median is
1/3
for halfspace symmetric distributions

1

1.2
Breakdown points of transport-based medians and contours.

The
empirical transport-based quantile function

\mathbf{Q}_{\pm}^{(n)}
of a sample
X^{(n)}

Note that these definitions depend on the choice of

The transport-based depth

\mathbf{F}_{\pm}^{(n)}(X_{i})
with respect to

{\rm D}_{\pm}^{(n)}(X_{i})={\rm TD}(\mathbf{F}_{\pm}^{(n)}(X_{i});u^{(n)}).

So far, the depth function is defined on

. In order to extend its definition to the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

[
27
]
proposed to extend

\mathbf{F}_{\pm}^{(n)}
by considering a continuous cyclically monotone interpolation, i.e., by choosing a convex and differentiable function
\psi_{n}=\psi_{n}(\cdot;X^{(n)}):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\nabla\psi_{n}(X_{i})=\mathbf{F}_{\pm}^{(n)}(X_{i})
for all

. Equipped with this extension one can define the
empirical transport-based depth function
of a point

as

{\rm D}_{\pm}^{(n)}(x)={\rm D}_{\pm}^{(n)}(x;X^{(n)}):={\rm TD}(\nabla\psi_{n}(x);u^{(n)})

as well as the empirical

{\cal C}_{\pm}^{(n)}(\tau)=\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{(n)}(\tau;X^{(n)}):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ {\rm D}_{\pm}^{(n)}(y)=\tau\},\quad\tau\in[0,1/2].

An
empirical transport-based median

m_{\pm}^{(n)}
of
X^{(n)}
is a deepest point in the sense

m_{\pm}^{(n)}\in\argmax_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\rm D}_{\pm}^{(n)}(x).

Our main result

provides asymptotically sharp estimates of the

{\rm BP}({\cal C}_{\pm}^{(n)}(\tau)):=\frac{1}{n}\min\left\{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}:\ \sup_{Z^{(n)}\in\mathcal{Q}_{k,n}}d_{H}(\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{(n)}(\tau;X^{(n)}),\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{(n)}(\tau;Z^{(n)}))=\infty\right\}.

Here

denotes the

Hausdorff distance
, which is defined as:

d_{H}(A,B)=\max\left\{\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\|a-b\|,\sup_{b\in B}\inf_{a\in A}\|b-a\|\right\},\quad A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Note that, as depth contours are compact sets

Let

The choice of

Remark 1.6

Part 2 of

1.3
Related work

There is a rich literature focusing on deriving the breakdown point of robust multivariate estimators of location and designing high breakdown point methods. It has been well established that while univariate M-estimators defined by a bounded score function have a high breakdown point, many natural multivariate generalizations of them can have a dimension dependent breakdown
[
45
,
19
,
33
,
63
,
43
,

]
.
Because the definition of the univariate median relies on the canonical ordering of the real line, it can be generalized in multiple natural ways to multivariate settings starting with
d=2
. As a result, numerous definitions of multivariate medians have been studied in the literature. Prominent examples include the geometric/spatial median
[

,
7
,
48
,
49
]
, the Tukey median
[

,
18
,
8
]
, projection depth-based medians
[

,
53
,
17
]
, among others. The coordinatewise median and the geometric median
[

,
7
,
48
,
49
]
can be computed efficiently and are known to have high breakdown point of
1/2

[
41
]
. However they do not correspond to natural notions of depth as discussed in
[
67
,
9
]
. Perhaps the most popular depth-based median is the Tukey median and is known to have a breakdown point of
1/3

[
18
]
. Some other depth-based medians attain the maximal breakdown point of
1/2

[

,
66
]
. However, we note that computing the Tukey median and high breakdown point projection-based estimators is NP hard
[

]
which significantly reduces their applicability for

Furthermore, transport-based quantiles can also naturally be used to define multivariate ranks. Such rank statistics are distribution free and have been shown to be useful for constructing statistically efficient nonparametric tests

,

Another popular notion of multivariate quantiles are the spatial or geometric quantiles

As noted in

, the robustness of optimal transport in the sense of robust statistics measures such as breakdown point and influence functions were unexplored. Our results and the parallel work of

The influence function approach is commonly referred to as infinitesimal robustness since it aims to quantify the impact of small amounts of contamination in the statistical functional of interest by finding a Gâteaux derivative at a fixed perturbation

1.4
Organization of the paper

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: In

In particular, we introduce the general population optimal transport problem, the canonical extension of transport maps, transport-based depth contours and an appropriate notion of breakdown point.
In

. Finally,

2.2
Extension of monotone transport maps

McCann’s Theorem is one of the fundamental results in the measure transportation literature. It states that given two probability measures
\mu
and
P
, with
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{a.c.}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a unique a.s. defined gradient
\nabla\varphi=\nabla\varphi_{\mu\to P}
of a lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) convex function
\varphi
pushing
\mu
forward to
P
(see
[
47
]
). Moreover, if
\int\|u\|^{2}d\mu(u)
and
\int\|x\|^{2}dP(x)
are finite,
\nabla\varphi
is the unique solution of the so-called Monge problem

\min_{T_{\#}\mu=P}\int\|T(u)-u\|^{2}d\mu(u),

where
(T_{\#}\mu)(\cdot)=\mu(T^{-1}(\cdot))
denotes the push-forward measure. We call
\nabla\varphi

optimal transport map
even if the second order moments of
\mu
and
P
are not finite.
The
canonical extension
of the transport map
\nabla\varphi
is the subdifferential (see
[
27
,
16
,
60
,
21
,
15
]
), that is

\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni u\mapsto\partial\varphi(u)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ \varphi(z)\geq\varphi(u)+\langle x,z-u\rangle\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

It holds that
\{\nabla\varphi(u)\}=\partial\varphi(u)
for
\mu
-a.e.
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which shows that
\partial\varphi
is indeed an extension of
\nabla\varphi
. However,
\partial\varphi
is no longer taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, but in
2^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. That is,
\partial\varphi
is a set-valued map.
A celebrated theorem by Rockafellar (see
[
55
]
) states that subdifferentials of convex functions are
maximal cyclically monotone
, i.e., for any choice of points
u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
(for arbitrary
m\geq 1
) and

3

3

We define the breakdown point of the depth contour

We start with a straightforward observation, which follows directly from (Fatou’s version of) the continuity of the probability measure (see e.g.,

Lemma 4.2
.

Set
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\pi/2)
and
\{v_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
\theta_{k}\to 0
and

where

We will prove that
{\rm BP}(\partial\varphi(u),P)=\mathrm{TD}(u;\mu)
by showing matching upper and lower bounds separately. Recall that for a probability measure
\nu

\nabla\varphi_{\mu\to\nu}
is the unique gradient of a convex function pushing
\mu

\nu

2.2
ensures that a point with (strictly) positive Tukey depth has always non-empty subdifferential. The following lemma shows the upper bound.

Lemma 4.3

\{\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon,k}(u)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

being any maximal cyclical monotone extension of
\nabla\varphi_{\mu\to P+\varepsilon(Q_{k}-P)}

\inf_{y\in\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon,k}(u)}\|y\|\to+\infty.

Note first that as

implies that

for all

and any maximal cyclical monotone extension of

.
We will show that for any sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, with

x_{k}\in\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon,k}(u)

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it must hold that
\|x_{k}\|\to\infty.

To prove the claim we argue by contradiction. To do so, we can assume, after taking subsequences, that

(P+\varepsilon(Q_{k}-P))(\mathcal{C}_{x_{k},e_{k},\theta_{k}})\geq\varepsilon Q_{k}(\mathcal{C}_{x_{k},e_{k},\theta_{k}})=\varepsilon{\geq}{\rm TD}(u;\mu)+\alpha

\mu(\mathcal{A}_{u,e_{k},\theta_{k}}){\geq}{\rm TD}(u;\mu)+\alpha.

We now turn to the lower bound and argue again by contradiction. Assume that
\alpha={\rm TD}(u;\mu)-\varepsilon>0

\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{P}^{a.c.}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

4.2
Empirical optimal transport: proof of
Theorem 3.1

For two
n\times n
real matrices
A=(A_{ij})
and
B=(B_{ij})
, where
i,j\in[n]
and
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
, let
\langle A,B\rangle_{F}=\sum_{i,j\in[n]}A_{ij}B_{ij}
denote their Frobenius inner product.
An optimal matching between the dataset
X^{(n)}
and the reference points
u^{(n)}
is any
n\times n
real matrix
\gamma=(\gamma_{ij})
solving

\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi_{n}}\langle c,\pi\rangle.

where
c=(c_{ij})
is a symmetric
n\times n
cost matrix with elements
c_{ij}=(\|u_{i}-X_{j}\|^{2})
, and
\Pi_{n}
denotes the
Birkhoff polytope
of doubly stochastic matrices
[
4
]
defined as

\Pi_{n}=\left\{\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}:\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{ij}=1,\;\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pi_{ij}=1,\;\pi_{ij}\geq 0\right\}.

A celebrated theorem due to Birkhoff states that the vertices of
\Pi_{n}
is the set of permutation matrices. This implies that an optimal matching can be identified with an
optimal permutation

\hat{\sigma}
on the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
. Therefore, an empirical optimal transport map

\hat{T}=\hat{T}_{X^{(n)}}
can be described as
\hat{T}_{n}(u_{i})=X_{\hat{\sigma}(i)}
, where
\hat{\sigma}
is an optimal permutation, which
in general might not be unique. However, it is well-known (see e.g. Exercise 2.21 in
[
64
]
) that
\hat{T}
is monotone, i.e.,

\langle u_{i}-u_{j},\hat{T}(u_{i})-\hat{T}(u_{j})\rangle\geq 0\quad\text{ for all }i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}

(12)

for any optimal transport map

\hat{T}_{n}
. The monotonicity property (
12
) is the key ingredient necessary to apply
4.1
in the proof of
Theorem 3.1
. We show at the same time
Theorem 3.1
and
3.7
. We denote as
\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{X^{(n)}}
any maximal cyclical monotone interpolation in the sense of
[
27
]
. That is,
\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{X^{(n)}}
is the subdifferential of a convex function such that
\hat{T}_{X^{(n)}}(u_{i})\in\hat{\mathbb{T}}_{X^{(n)}}(u_{i})
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
The claimed finite sample breakdown point results follow from the upper and lower bounds that we establish in the following two lemmas. Their proofs follow along the lines of those of given for
4.3
and
4.4
.

Lemma 4.5

Fix

(13)

Proof of

.

Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence such that

\lim_{n}|{\rm TD}(x_{n};\mu_{n})-{\rm TD}(x_{n};\mu)|=\lim_{n}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|{\rm TD}(x;\mu_{n})-{\rm TD}(x;\mu)|.

converges to

, there exists a compact convex set

.
We define, for
\varepsilon>0
, the continuous and bounded functions
\Theta_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{S}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}

We note that the functions
\Theta_{\varepsilon}
and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
are Lipschitz with constant
L_{\varepsilon}
depending exclusively on
\varepsilon
.
Then if
\mu_{n}
converges in distribution to
\mu
, it follows that

\sup_{x,y}\left|\int\Theta_{\varepsilon}(x,y,s)d(\mu_{n}-\mu)(s)\right|\to 0\quad{\rm and}\quad\sup_{x,y}\left|\int\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x,y,s)d(\mu_{n}-\mu)(s)\right|\to 0.

(15)

Then

\displaystyle{\rm TD}(x_{n};\mu_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{v}\int\Theta_{\varepsilon}(x_{n},y,s)d\mu_{n}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{y}\int\Theta_{\varepsilon}(x_{n},y,s)d\mu(s)-\sup_{x,y}\left|\int\Theta_{\varepsilon}(x,y,s)d(\mu_{n}-\mu)(s)\right|

so that, by taking limits, using (
15
) and the Lipschitz continuity of
\Theta_{\varepsilon}
,

\liminf_{n}{\rm TD}(x_{n};\mu)\geq\inf_{y}\int\Theta_{\varepsilon}(x_{*},y,s)d\mu(s).

Since, for every
\varepsilon>0

,

\liminf_{n}{\rm TD}(x_{n};\mu_{n})\geq{\rm TD}(x_{*};\mu)-\sup_{y}\mu(\{s:\langle s-x_{*},y\rangle\in[-\varepsilon,0]\}).

(16)

We claim that

L:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y}\mu(\{s:\langle s-x_{*},y\rangle\in[-\varepsilon,0]\})=0.

(17)

Let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}
and
\{y_{n}\}
be such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and

\liminf_{n}\mu(\{s:\langle s-x_{*},y_{n}\rangle\in[-\varepsilon_{n},0]\})=L.

By compactness of the unit sphere, we can assume (after taking subsequences) that
y_{n}\to y_{*}
as

. Fix
\delta>0
and let

and

,

\mu(\{s:\langle s-x_{*},y_{n}\rangle\in[-\varepsilon,0]\})\leq\delta.

\liminf_{n}{\rm TD}(x_{n};\mu_{n})\geq{\rm TD}(x_{*};\mu),

.

4.3.2

The univariate result stated below has been well-known by the community working on transport-based quantiles and is stated below for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 4.7

Proof.

For
\nu\in\mathcal{P}^{a.c.}(\mathbb{R})

m_{\nu}=\{x_{*}\in\mathbb{R}:\nu(\{z\in\mathbb{R}:z\leq x_{*}\})=\nu(\{z\in\mathbb{R}:z>x_{*}\})=1/2\}.

Let
\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}
be any maximal cyclical monotone extension of the unique monotone map
{T}_{\mu\to P}
pushing
\mu
forward to
P
.
Our goal is to show that

m_{\pm}(P)=\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}(m_{\mu})=m_{P}

(18)

Note that by definition
m_{\pm}(P)=\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}(m_{\mu})
. Let
u^{*}\in m_{\mu}
be a median of the reference measure
\mu
.
Then using the monotonicity of
{T}_{\mu\to P}
, for every
x_{*}\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}(u_{*})
, if
\pm u<\pm u_{*}
then
\pm x\leq\pm x_{*}
for all
x\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}(u)
. As a consequence, for every
x_{*}\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}(u_{*})
,

P(\{z\in\mathbb{R}:z<x_{*}\})=\mu(\{u\in\mathbb{R}:{T}_{\mu\to P}(u)<x_{*}\})\leq\mu(\{u\in\mathbb{R}:u\leq u_{*}\})=\frac{1}{2}

and

P(\{z\in\mathbb{R}:z>x_{*}\})=\mu(\{u\in\mathbb{R}:{T}_{\mu\to P}(u)>x_{*}\})\leq\mu(\{u\in\mathbb{R}:u\geq u_{*}\})=\frac{1}{2},

so that
x_{*}\in m_{P}
. Now let
\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}^{-1}
be the inverse of
\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}
in the set-valued sense, i.e.,
\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}^{-1}(x)=\{u:x\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}(u)\}
. Note that the transport map
T_{P\to\mu}
from
P
to
\mu
is a Borel measurable selection of
\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}^{-1}
(see
[
47
, Remark 16]
and
[
54
, Corollary 23.5.1.]
). Since
\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}^{-1}
is also monotone, we get that if
x_{*}
is a median of
P
then any
u_{*}\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu\to P}^{-1}(x_{*})
is a median of
\mu
. Therefore, (
18
) holds and the proof is concluded.
∎

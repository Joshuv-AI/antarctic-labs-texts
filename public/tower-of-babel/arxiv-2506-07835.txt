Global weak solutions to a compressible Navier–Stokes/Cahn–Hilliard system with singular entropy of mixing
Danica Basarić & Andrea Giorgini

Abstract
. We study a Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard system modeling the evolution of a compressible binary mixture of viscous fluids undergoing phase separation. The novelty of this work is a free energy potential including the physically relevant Flory-Huggins (logarithmic) entropy, as opposed to previous studies in the literature, which only consider regular potentials with polynomial growth. Our main result establishes the existence of global-in-time weak solutions in three-dimensional bounded domains for arbitrarily large initial data. The core contribution is the derivation of new estimates for the chemical potential and the Flory-Huggins entropy arising from a density-dependent Cahn-Hilliard equation under minimal assumptions: non-negative γ \gamma -integrable density with γ > 3 2 \gamma>\frac{3}{2} . In addition, we prove that the phase variable, which represents the difference of the mass concentrations, takes value within the physical interval [ − 1 , 1 ] [-1,1] almost everywhere on the set where the density is positive.



1.
Introduction

We investigate the compressible Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard system

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho\textbf{{u}})

\displaystyle=0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho\textbf{{u}})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho\textbf{{u}}\otimes\textbf{{u}})+\nabla_{x}p

\displaystyle={\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}-{\rm div}_{x}\left(\nabla_{x}c\otimes\nabla_{x}c-\frac{|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}}{2}\mathbb{I}\right),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho c)+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho c\textbf{{u}})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{x}\mu,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\varrho\,\mu

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{x}c+\varrho\frac{\partial f(\varrho,c)}{\partial c}.

System (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) describes the motion of a mixture of two viscous and compressible fluids incorporating phase separation mechanisms. The state variables (unknowns) in (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) are the density
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
of the mixture, the velocity
\textbf{{u}}=\textbf{{u}}(t,x)
of the fluid mixture and the phase variable
c=c(t,x)
, representing the mass concentration difference of the two components, and the chemical potential
\mu=\mu(t,x)
. More precisely,
c=c_{1}-c_{2}
, where the mass concentrations are defined by

(1.5)

c_{i}=\frac{m_{i}}{m},\quad i=1,2\quad\text{with}\quad m=m_{1}+m_{2},

where
m_{i}
denotes the mass of the fluid
i
in the representative volume
V
.

The pressure
p=p(\varrho,c)
is related to the free energy density
f=f(\varrho,c)
through the constitutive law

(1.6)

p(\varrho,c)=\varrho^{2}\frac{\partial f(\varrho,c)}{\partial\varrho},

and the viscous stress tensor
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{S}(c,\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}})
satisfies Newton’s rheological law,

(1.7)

\mathbb{S}(c,\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}})=\eta(c)\left(\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}+\nabla_{x}^{\top}\textbf{{u}}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}\,\mathbb{I}\right)+\lambda(c){\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}\,\mathbb{I}.

The functions
\eta=\eta(c)
and
\lambda=\lambda(c)
are the shear and bulk viscosities, respectively. We assume that
\eta,\,\lambda
are continuously differentiable functions such that

(1.8)

0<\underline{\eta}\leq\eta(c)\leq\overline{\eta},\quad 0\leq\lambda(c)\leq\overline{\lambda}\quad\mbox{for all }c\in\mathbbm{R}.

The free energy density is given by

f(\varrho,c)=f_{\rm e}(\varrho)+f_{\rm mix}(c),

where
f_{\rm e}(\varrho)
and
f_{\rm mix}(c)
represent the potential and mixing contributions, respectively. In particular, we consider

\displaystyle f_{\rm e}(\varrho)=\int_{1}^{\varrho}\frac{p_{\rm e}(z)}{z^{2}}\ \textup{d}z,\quad

\displaystyle p_{\rm e}(\varrho)=\varrho^{\gamma},\ \gamma>1,

and

\displaystyle f_{\rm mix}(c)=F(c)

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}c^{2},

where
F=F(c)
denotes the Flory-Huggins (also known as Boltzmann-Gibbs) entropy of mixing

(1.9)

\displaystyle F(c)=\frac{\theta}{2}\bigg((1+c)\ln(1+c)+(1-c)\ln(1-c)\bigg),\quad\mbox{for any }c\in[-1,1].

The parameters
\theta
and
\theta_{0}
satisfy the thermodynamical conditions
0<\theta<\theta_{0}
.

We consider the system (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) in
(0,T)\times\Omega
, where the positive time
T
can be arbitrarily chosen, and
\Omega\subset\mathbbm{R}^{3}
denotes a bounded domain of class
C^{2}
. We close the system with the boundary conditions

(1.10)

\textbf{{u}}=\textbf{0},\quad\nabla_{x}c\cdot\textbf{n}=0,\quad\nabla_{x}\mu\cdot\textbf{n}=0,\quad\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,

and the initial conditions

(1.11)

\varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\quad(\varrho\textbf{{u}})(0,\cdot)=\textbf{{m}}_{0},\quad c(0,\cdot)=c_{0},\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Two main properties of system (
1.1
)–(
1.4
), subject to (
1.10
)–(
1.11
), are
the mass conservation relations and the energy balance. More precisely, sufficient smooth solutions to (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) and (
1.10
)–(
1.11
) satisfy

(1.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho(t,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}\ \textup{d}x,\quad\int_{\Omega}(\varrho c)(t,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}c_{0}\ \textup{d}x,

for all
t\geq 0
, and

(1.13)

E(\varrho(t),\textbf{{u}}(t),c(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(c,\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}):\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}\mu|^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}s=E(\varrho_{0},\textbf{{u}}_{0},c_{0}),

for all
t\geq 0
, where the total energy of the system is

E(\varrho,\textbf{{u}},c):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\textbf{{u}}|^{2}+\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}+\varrho F(c)-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}\varrho c^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}\right)\ \textup{d}x.

1.1.
Background, literature review and aim of this work

Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard systems arise within the framework of Diffuse Interface methods, also referred to as the Phase Field approach, to model multi-phase flows undergoing phase separation. In this formulation, the interface between the two constituents (e.g. fluids) is described as a diffuse layer of finite thickness. The order parameter
c
is allowed to smoothly vary between the pure phases
+1
and
-1
at the interface, with the interface identified as level sets of
c
. The evolution of the state variables, including the density and the velocity, are derived by combining the continuum theory of mixtures and an energy-based variational energy framework, which capture the multi-scale nature of the phenomena. Due to its ability to handle topological changes and singular interfacial behaviors, the Diffuse Interface approach has become a widely used and versatile tool in computational fluid dynamics. We refer the reader to
[
7
,
32
]
and the references therein for a broad overview.

The first Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard model, also known as Model H, was introduced by Hohenberg and Halperin
[
35
]
, and later derived within the continuum mechanics setting by Gurtin, Polignone and Viñals
[
33
]
. Since then, Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard systems have been the subject of extensive theoretical and computational research, especially in the case of incompressible or quasi-incompressible mixtures. Since Model H relies on the assumption of constant density for the whole mixture, several generalizations have been developed in
[
6
,
9
,
12
,
14
,
17
,
34
,
42
,
43
,
44
]
to account for mixtures of two incompressible fluids with unmatched constant densities. We refer in particular to
[
17
]
for a comparative analysis of these models.

The progresses in modeling have been complemented by a comprehensive mathematical theory addressing well-posedness, regularity properties of solutions, long-time convergence to stationary states, existence of the global attractor, as well as nonlocal-to-local limits. Without claiming for completeness, we mention
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
10
,
11
,

,

,

,

,

, where the Flory-Huggins potential (

) has been mostly considered. In addition, a non-homogeneous incompressible Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard model was studied by the second author and Temam in
[
31
]
, where the existence of global-in-time weak solutions is demonstrated under the assumption of strictly positive and bounded density.

In contrast, considerably less is known concerning compressible Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard systems. A first compressible model was introduced by Lowengrub and Truskinovsky in
[
42
]
(see also
[

]
for recent extensions to

f_{\rm mix}(\rho,c)=H(c)\ln(\rho)+G(c),

with the following growth conditions on
H
and

:

-\underline{H}\leq H^{\prime}(c),H(c)\leq\overline{H},\quad\underline{G}_{1}c-\underline{G}_{2}\leq G^{\prime}(c)\leq\overline{G}(1+c)\quad\forall\,c\in\mathbbm{R},

where

More recently, Elbar and Poulain

proved the existence of global-in-time weak solutions to system (

)–(

\kappa(\varrho,c)\textbf{{u}}

\gamma>6\quad\text{and}\quad f_{\rm mix}(c)=\frac{1}{4}(c^{2}-1)^{2}.

On the other hand, Kotschote and Zacher in
[

established local-in-time existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to the model proposed in

Despite the above-mentioned contributions, a significant gap remains unsolved between the analysis of incompressible and compressible Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard systems. All existing results for the compressible case deal with regular mixing potentials with polynomial growth (cf. Remark 1.1 in
[

]
), which are not suitable to enforce the physical constraint

We conclude this literature review by mentioning some works related to the compressible Navier-Stokes/Allen-Cahn system with Flory-Huggins potential. In

-norm of the chemical potential
\mu

Finally, we also recall the work of Kotschote

37

[

The aim of this paper is to demonstrate the first result on the existence of global-in-time weak solutions to system (
1.1
)–(
1.4
), subject to (
1.10
)–(
1.11
), under the thermodynamically relevant assumptions (
1.6
)–(
1.9
) with
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
. Our approach is based on a compactness method. Specifically, we first approximate the Flory-Huggins potential
F
by a sequence of potentials
F_{\varepsilon}
satisfying the structural assumptions of
[
4
]
. This allows us to construct a sequence of approximating global weak solutions
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon})
to a regularized problem replacing
F
with
F_{\varepsilon}
(cf. (
3.3
)–(
3.6
) and (
3.7
)).
Next, the core of the proof consists in deriving uniform bounds for the approximating solutions
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon})
.
We initially deduce the mass conservation relations (
4.14
)–(
4.15
) and the energy estimates (
4.16
)–(
4.20
), which are a direct consequence of the energy inequality for the approximating solutions. However, these bounds are insufficient to get compactness for the pressure
p(\varrho_{\varepsilon})
, the chemical potential
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and the nonlinear term
F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})
. To overcome this issue, we develop a new set of estimates for the density-dependent Cahn-Hilliard equation under minimal conditions on the density, namely non-negative
\gamma
-integrable density with
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
.
Roughly speaking, we gain uniform estimates on
\mu_{\varepsilon}
,
\varrho_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})
and
c_{\varepsilon}
by exploiting the control of
\nabla_{x}\mu_{\varepsilon}
available from the energy balance. More precisely, our key step is to derive the uniform estimate

(1.15)

\left\|\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C,

where the constant
M_{r}
is suitably chosen such that
\varrho_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})
has zero total mass. Then, since the product
F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})c_{\varepsilon}
is comparable to
|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|
away from
c_{\varepsilon}\approx 0
, we exploit the above bound to infer the control

(1.16)

\left\|\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|\ \textup{d}x\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C,

which, in turn, allows us to obtain a uniform estimate of
\mu_{\varepsilon}
in the full
W^{1,2}
-norm, namely

(1.17)

\|\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))}\leq C.

In addition, by exploiting the relation (
1.4
), we conclude the uniform bounds

(1.18)

\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{6\gamma}{\gamma+3}}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3}))}\leq C\quad\mbox{with}\quad\frac{3\gamma}{\gamma+3}>1.

The estimates (
1.15
)–(
1.18
) significantly improve the analysis in
[
31
]
, as they do not require strict positivity or boundedness of the density, and a broader class of initial data is admitted (cf. Remark
1.2
).
The latter estimate in (
1.18
) is then crucially used in Subsection
4.4
to obtain the fundamental estimate

(1.19)

\|p(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{q(\gamma)}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\leq C\quad\mbox{with}\quad q(\gamma):=\min\left\{\frac{5}{3}-\frac{1}{\gamma},\ \frac{3}{2}\right\}.

Next, building on the above uniform estimates, we apply the Lions-Feireisl theory for the compressible Navier-Stokes equations to pass to the limit in the continuity equation (
1.1
) and in the momentum equations (
1.2
). Regarding the density-dependent Cahn-Hilliard equation (
1.3
)–(
1.4
), we recover the strong convergence for
c_{\varepsilon}
by adapting a compactness argument originally developed in
[
4
]

Notably, the combination of (

enables us to show that the limit functions
\varrho
and

satisfy the
physical
condition

-1<c<1\quad\mbox{a.e. in }\{{\rho>0\}}\subset\Omega\times(0,T),

which is essential for the consistency of the model.

1.2.
The main result

We are now ready to state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T>0
be chosen arbitrarily, let
\Omega\subset\mathbbm{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain of class
C^{2}
, and let

\gamma>\frac{3}{2}.

Assume that the initial conditions
(\varrho_{0},\textbf{{m}}_{0},c_{0})
satisfy

(1.20)

\displaystyle\varrho_{0}\geq 0\ \ \text{a.e. in }\Omega,\quad-1\leq c_{0}\leq 1\ \ \text{a.e. in }\Omega,

(1.21)

\displaystyle E_{0}:=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\textbf{{m}}_{0}|^{2}}{\varrho_{0}}+\varrho_{0}f(\varrho_{0},c_{0})+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c_{0}|^{2}\right)\textup{d}x<\infty,

and the total and relative masses

M:=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}\ \textup{d}x>0,\quad M_{c}:=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}c_{0}\ \textup{d}x

comply with the condition

(1.22)

M_{r}:=\frac{M_{c}}{M}\in(-1,1).

Then, the Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard system (
1.1
)–(
1.10
) admits a weak solution
(\varrho,\textbf{{u}},c)
with chemical potential
\mu
in the following sense:

(i)

Regularity class
: the weak solution
(\varrho,\textbf{{u}},c)
satisfies

\displaystyle\varrho\geq 0

\displaystyle\mbox{a.e. in }(0,T)\times\Omega,

\displaystyle-1<c<1

\displaystyle\mbox{a.e. in }\{\varrho>0\}\subset(0,T)\times\Omega,

where

\{\varrho>0\}:=\{(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega\ :\ \varrho(t,x)>0\},

and

\displaystyle(\varrho,\varrho\textbf{{u}},\varrho c)

\displaystyle\in C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\gamma}(\Omega)\times L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})\times L^{\frac{6\gamma}{6+\gamma}}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle(\textbf{{u}},c)

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3}))\times L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{2,\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)).

as well as

\varrho F^{\prime}(c)=\begin{cases}\varrho F^{\prime}(c)&\mbox{if }\varrho>0\\
0&\mbox{if }\varrho=0\end{cases}\quad\text{with}\quad\rho F^{\prime}(c)\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega)).

(ii)

Weak formulation of the renormalized continuity equation
: given the functions
b
and
B
such that

(1.23)

b\in(L^{\infty}\cap C)([0,\infty)),\quad B(\varrho):=B(1)+\int_{1}^{\varrho}\frac{b(z)}{z^{2}}\ \textup{d}z,

the integral identity

(1.24)

\left[\int_{\Omega}\big(\varrho B(\varrho)\varphi\big)(t,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho B(\varrho)\left(\partial_{t}\varphi+\textbf{{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right)-b(\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}\,\varphi\right]\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

holds for any
\tau\in[0,T]
and any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
, with
\varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0}
.

(iii)

Weak formulation of the balance of momentum
: the integral identity

(1.25)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}(\varrho\textbf{{u}}\cdot\bm{\varphi})(t,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\big[\varrho\textbf{{u}}\cdot\partial_{t}\bm{\varphi}+(\varrho\textbf{{u}}\otimes\textbf{{u}}):\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}\big]\,\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[p(\varrho)\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{S}(c,\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}})\Big]:\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left((\nabla_{x}c\otimes\nabla_{x}c):\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}-\frac{|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}}{2}{\rm div}_{x}\bm{\varphi}\right)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

holds for any
\tau\in[0,T]
and any
\bm{\varphi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})
, with
(\varrho\textbf{{u}})(0,\cdot)=\textbf{{m}}_{0}
.

(iv)

Weak formulation of the equation of the concentration
: the integral identity

(1.26)

\left[\int_{\Omega}(\varrho c\varphi)(t,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho c\,(\partial_{t}\varphi+\textbf{{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi)-\nabla_{x}\mu\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right]\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

holds for any
\tau\in[0,T]
and any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
, with
(\varrho c)(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0}c_{0}
.

(v)

Weak formulation of the equation of the chemical potential:
the integral identity

(1.27)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\mu\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\varrho F^{\prime}(c)\varphi-\theta_{0}\varrho c\varphi+\nabla_{x}c\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

holds for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
.

(vi)

Energy inequality
: setting, for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
,

E(\varrho(\tau),\textbf{{u}}(\tau),c(\tau)):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\textbf{{u}}|^{2}+\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}+\varrho F(c)-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}\varrho c^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x,

where

\varrho F(c)=\begin{cases}\varrho F(c)&\mbox{if }\varrho>0\\
0&\mbox{if }\varrho=0,\end{cases}

the integral inequality

(1.28)

E(\varrho(\tau),\textbf{{u}}(\tau),c(\tau))+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(c,\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}):\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}\mu|^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\leq E_{0}

holds for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
.

Remark 1.2
.

We point out that assumption (
1.22
), namely

\frac{M_{c}}{M}=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}c_{0}\ \textup{d}x}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}\ \textup{d}x}\in(-1,1),

is physically grounded in the context of phase separation for a two-phase mixture.
Indeed, when the initial concentration exhibits a diffuse interface between the pure phases, the set

A:=\Big\{x\in\Omega\ :\ -1<c_{0}(x)<1\Big\}\cap\Big\{x\in\Omega\ :\ \varrho_{0}(x)>0\Big\}

has positive measure, i.e.
|A|>0
.
Then, due to hypothesis (
1.20
), we have

|M_{c}|\leq\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}|c_{0}|\ \textup{d}x=\int_{A}\varrho_{0}|c_{0}|\ \textup{d}x+\int_{A^{c}}\varrho_{0}|c_{0}|\ \textup{d}x<\int_{A}\varrho_{0}\ \textup{d}x+\int_{A^{c}}\varrho_{0}\ \textup{d}x=M.

On the other hand, the case
M_{c}=M
is equivalent to

\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}(1-c_{0})\ \textup{d}x=0,

which implies that
c_{0}=1
in the set
\{\varrho_{0}>0\}
. Similarly, if
M_{c}=-M
, then
c_{0}=-1
in the set
\{\varrho_{0}>0\}
. In both cases, only one fluid is initially present, and thus no phase separation should occur. Furthermore, assumption (
1.22
) significantly broadens the class of admissible initial conditions compared to the assumption

\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}c_{0}\ \textup{d}x}{\varrho_{\ast}|\Omega|}\in(-1,1),\quad\text{where}\quad 0<\varrho_{\ast}\leq\varrho_{0}(x)\leq\varrho^{\ast}\ \text{a.e. in }\Omega,

for some
\varrho_{\ast}
,
\varrho^{\ast}\in\mathbbm{R}
, as
required in
[
31
]

Remark 1.3
.

Further remarks are in order:

(1)

1.1
remains valid for any function
F:[-1,1]\to\mathbbm{R}
in the class

C([-1,1])\cap C^{2}(-1,1)
such that

\lim_{s\to\pm 1}F^{\prime}(s)=\pm\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad F^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq\alpha>0,\ \forall\,s\in(-1,1).

2.
Functional setting

Let
X
be a real Banach space. We denote by
X^{*}
its dual space. For
T>0
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(I;X)

denotes the set of all strongly measurable
f:I\to X
that are
p

-integrable/essentially bounded. The set of continuous functions
f:[0,T]\to X
is denoted by
C([0,T];X)
, endowed with the supremum norm. The space
C_{\rm weak}([0,T];X)
consists of all functions
f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;X)
such that the map
t\in[0,T]\mapsto\langle\phi,f(t)\rangle_{X^{*}\times X}
is continuous for all
\phi\in X^{*}
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbbm{R}^{3}
. The Sobolev spaces of functions
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}
and of vector fields
\mathbf{u}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}^{3}

are denoted by
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
and
W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})
, respectively, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. The notations
\|\cdot\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})}
represent their norms.
We recall the classical embedding results connecting the Sobolev and Lebesgue spaces.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbbm{R}^{3}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and let
k\geq 1
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
be fixed. If
kp<3
, then the continuous embedding

(2.1)

W^{k,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega),

holds for any
1\leq q\leq p^{*}
, where
p^{*}:=\frac{3p}{3-kp}
. Moreover, the embedding is compact whenever
q\neq p^{*}
.

We will mainly use the aforementioned lemma for
k=1
and
p=2
, i.e.

(2.2)

W^{1,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for any }1\leq q\leq 6,

with its dual version

(2.3)

L^{q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow(W^{1,2}(\Omega))^{*}\quad\mbox{for any }q\geq\frac{6}{5};

notice that the embedding in (
2.3
) is compact if
q\neq\frac{6}{5}
.

Next, we recall the definition of total mass (spatial average) for an integrable function

\langle f\rangle_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}f\ \textup{d}x,\quad\forall\,f\in L^{1}(\Omega).

By the classical Poincaré inequality in bounded Lipschitz domains
\Omega\subset\mathbbm{R}^{3}
, there exists a positive constant
C=C(\Omega)
such that

(2.4)

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|\langle f\rangle_{\Omega}|\right)\!,\quad\forall\,f\in H^{1}(\Omega).

We will also make use of the following variant of the Poincaré inequality from
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbbm{R}^{3}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Assume that
\rho
is a non-negative function such that

0<M=\int_{\Omega}\rho\ \textup{d}x,\quad\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\gamma}\ \textup{d}x\leq R,\quad\text{with}\quad\gamma>\frac{6}{5}.

Then, there exists a constant
C=C(\gamma,M,R)
such that

\left\|f-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho f\ \textup{d}x\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for any
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

Lastly, we recall the definition and properties of the Bogovskii operator (see, e.g.,
[
29
, Theorems III.3.3 and III.3.4]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbbm{R}^{3}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. There exists a linear operator

\mathcal{B}:L_{0}^{q}(\Omega)\rightarrow W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3}),\quad 1<q<\infty,

such that, for any
f\in L_{0}^{q}(\Omega)=\{f\in L^{q}(\Omega):\ \int_{\Omega}f\ \textup{d}x=0\}
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}\mathcal{B}(f)

\displaystyle=f\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(f)\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}.

If, in addition,
f={\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{g}}
with
\textbf{{g}}\in L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})
,
1<r<\infty
, and
\textbf{{g}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, then

(2.7)

\|\mathcal{B}(f)\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})}\leq C\|\textbf{{g}}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})}.

3.
Approximation scheme

In this section, we define the approximation scheme employed to prove Theorem
1.1
. More precisely, we approximate the Flory-Huggins potential
F
by a sequence of functions
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}
defined in the whole
\mathbbm{R}
, which exhibit (at most) quadratic growth at infinity in terms of
c
. Next, we exploit the result proved in
[
4
]
, which guarantees the existence of weak solutions to system (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) and (
1.10
)–(
1.11
), with
F
replaced by
F_{\varepsilon}
.

3.1.
Regularization of the potential energy

Following
[
31
]
, for any
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small we consider the following approximations of the logarithmic potential
F
,

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(c):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}\frac{1}{k!}F^{(k)}(1-\varepsilon)\big[c-(1-\varepsilon)\big]^{k}&\mbox{for }c>1-\varepsilon\\[8.0pt]
F(c)&\mbox{for }-1+\varepsilon\leq c\leq 1-\varepsilon\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}\frac{1}{k!}F^{(k)}(-1+\varepsilon)\big[c-(-1+\varepsilon)\big]^{k}&\mbox{for }c<-1+\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Clearly,
F_{\varepsilon}\in C^{2}(\mathbbm{R})
is an even function for any
\varepsilon>0
; additionally,
F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
converges uniformly to
F^{\prime}
in every compact set of
(-1,1)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Moreover, denoting

f_{\rm mix,\varepsilon}(c):=F_{\varepsilon}(c)-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}c^{2},

the function

G_{\varepsilon}(c):=\frac{\partial f_{\rm mix,\varepsilon}(c)}{\partial c}=F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c)-\theta_{0}c

is such that

(3.1)

\underline{G}_{\varepsilon,1}c-\underline{G}_{\varepsilon,2}\leq G_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c)\leq\overline{G}_{\varepsilon}(1+c),

for some positive constants
\underline{G}_{\varepsilon,1},\underline{G}_{\varepsilon,2},\overline{G}_{\varepsilon}
.

3.2.
Existence of approximating solutions.

For any fixed
\varepsilon
, the existence of weak solutions for any finite energy initial data is guaranteed by
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
; more precisely, the following result holds.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
T>0
be chosen arbitrarily large and let
\Omega\subset\mathbbm{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain of class
C^{2}
. Moreover, let

\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
,
and let the triple of functions
(\varrho_{0},\textbf{{m}}_{0},c_{0})
satisfy conditions (
1.20
)–(
1.21
).
Then, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
the Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard system (
1.1
)–(
1.10
) with
f(\varrho,c)
replaced with

f_{\varepsilon}(\varrho,c)=f_{\rm e}(\varrho)+f_{\rm mix,\varepsilon}(c)

admits a weak solution
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon})
with chemical potential
\mu_{\varepsilon}
in the sense that the following holds:

(i)

Regularity class
: we have

\varrho_{\varepsilon}\geq 0\ \ \mbox{a.e. in }(0,T)\times\Omega,

and

(3.2a)

\displaystyle(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\varrho_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon},\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\in C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\gamma}(\Omega)\times L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})\times L^{\frac{6\gamma}{6+\gamma}}(\Omega)),

(3.2b)

\displaystyle(\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3}))\times L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)),

(3.2c)

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)).

(ii)

Weak formulation of the renormalized continuity equation
: given the functions
b
and
B
satisfying (
1.23
), the integral identity

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\big(\varrho_{\varepsilon}B(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\varphi\big)(t,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}B(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\left(\partial_{t}\varphi+\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right)\right]\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,b(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\,{\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

holds for any
\tau\in[0,T]
and any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
, with
\varrho_{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0}
.

(iii)

Weak formulation of the balance of momentum
: the integral identity

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\bm{\varphi})(t,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\big[\varrho_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\partial_{t}\bm{\varphi}+(\varrho_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\otimes\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}\big]\,\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[p(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{S}(c_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon})\Big]:\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left((\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}\otimes\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}-\frac{|\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}{\rm div}_{x}\bm{\varphi}\right)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

holds for any
\tau\in[0,T]
and any
\bm{\varphi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})
, with
(\varrho_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon})(0,\cdot)=\textbf{{m}}_{0}
.

(iv)

Weak formulation of the equation of the concentration
: the integral identity

(3.5)

\left[\int_{\Omega}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\varphi)(t,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\,(\partial_{t}\varphi+\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi)-\nabla_{x}\mu_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right]\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

holds for any
\tau\in[0,T]
and any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
, with
(\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon})(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0}c_{0}
.

(v)

Weak formulation of the equation of the chemical potential:
the integral identity

(3.6)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})\varphi-\theta_{0}\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\varphi+\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

holds for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
.

(vi)

Energy inequality
: defining, for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
,

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}(\tau),\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}(\tau),c_{\varepsilon}(\tau))

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varrho_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}(\varrho_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x

\displaystyle E_{0,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\textbf{{m}}_{0}|^{2}}{\varrho_{0}}+\varrho_{0}f_{\varepsilon}(\varrho_{0},c_{0})+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c_{0}|^{2}\right)\textup{d}x,

the integral inequality

(3.7)

E_{\varepsilon}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}(\tau),\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}(\tau),c_{\varepsilon}(\tau))+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(c_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}\mu_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\leq E_{0,\varepsilon}

holds for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
.

Remark 3.2
.

The validity of (
3.6
) can be extended to a larger class of test functions by a density argument.
In view of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), it is enough to take
\varphi
in (
3.6
) such that

\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)).

In particular, for any
h>0
, any
\tau\in(0,T)
and any
\phi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, we can take

\varphi(t,x)=\mathbbm{1}_{[\tau,\tau+h]}(t)\phi(x)

as test function in (
3.6
), obtaining the identity

\frac{1}{h}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+h}\int_{\Omega}\left(\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}\phi-\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})\phi-\theta_{0}\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\phi-\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\right)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t=0.

By Lebesgue Differentation Theorem, the limit
h\to 0
exists for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
and therefore we conclude that the integral identity

(3.8)

\int_{\Omega}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon})(\tau,\cdot)\phi\ \textup{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\left(\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})-\theta_{0}\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\right)(\tau,\cdot)\phi\ \textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\ \textup{d}x

holds for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
and any
\phi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let us consider the family
\{(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon},c_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon>0}
of weak solutions with correspondent chemical potential
\mu_{\varepsilon}
, whose existence was stated in Theorem
3.1
. We are now going to prove the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, passing to suitable subsequences as the case may be, the following convergences hold:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varepsilon}\to\varrho

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\gamma}(\Omega)),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\to\varrho\textbf{{u}}

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})),

(4.3)

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\to\varrho c

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\frac{6\gamma}{6+\gamma}}(\Omega)),

(4.4)

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}c

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)),

(4.5)

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon}\to c

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\textbf{{u}}

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})),

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\mu

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)),

(4.8)

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\varrho\mu

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{6\gamma}{\gamma+6}}(\Omega)),

(4.9)

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\varrho c\textbf{{u}}

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{3\gamma}{\gamma+3}}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})),

(4.10)

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\otimes\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\varrho\textbf{{u}}\otimes\textbf{{u}}

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{6\gamma}{4\gamma+3}}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3\times 3}))

(4.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}(c_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup\mathbb{S}(c,\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}})

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3\times 3}),

(4.12)

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup\varrho F^{\prime}(c)\mathbbm{1}_{\{\varrho>0\}}

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega)),

(4.13)

\displaystyle p(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup p(\varrho)

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{in }L^{q}((0,T)\times\Omega),\quad q(\gamma):=\min\left\{\frac{5}{3}-\frac{1}{\gamma},\ \frac{3}{2}\right\}.

In order to achieve the aforementioned convergences, we need to establish some uniform bounds independent of
\varepsilon
; the rest of the section will be devoted to this purpose. We will make use of the notation
C
to denote a positive constant which may depend on the parameters of the system and
T
, but is independent of
\varepsilon
.

4.1.
Mass conservation

First of all, taking
b(\varrho)\equiv 0
,
B(\varrho)\equiv 1
in (
3.3
), and choosing
\varphi(t,x)=\psi(t)
with
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,T)
as test function in (
3.3
) and (
3.5
), we can deduce that the identities

(4.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}\ \textup{d}x=M,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon})(\tau,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}c_{0}\ \textup{d}x=M_{c}

hold for any
\tau\in[0,T]
in light of (
3.2a
).

4.2.
Energy estimates

From hypothesis (
1.20
) and the fact that
F_{\varepsilon}(c)\leq F(c)
for any
c\in[-1,1]
, we can deduce that
E_{0,\varepsilon}\leq E_{0}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. Therefore, condition (
1.21
) and the energy inequality (
3.7
) lead to the following uniform bounds:

(4.16)

\displaystyle\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C,

(4.17)

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C,

(4.18)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C,

(4.19)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3\times 3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C.

Notice that we have used condition (
1.8
) and the Korn inequality to deduce estimate (
4.19
). The latter, combined with the standard Poincaré inequality, leads to the uniform bound

(4.21)

\|\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

Owing to (
4.14
), (
4.15
), (
4.16
) and (
4.18
), an application of Lemma
2.2
yields

(4.22)

\|c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))}\leq C.

Moreover, from the estimates (
4.16
) and (
4.17
), we deduce that

(4.23)

\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p_{1}}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3}))}\leq C\quad\mbox{with}\quad p_{1}:=\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}>\frac{6}{5}.

Consequently, from (
4.21
) and the Sobolev embedding (
2.2
), we get

(4.24)

\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\otimes\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{p_{2}}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3\times 3}))}\leq C\quad\mbox{with}\quad p_{2}:=\frac{6\gamma}{4\gamma+3}>1.

Similarly, from estimates (
4.16
) and (
4.22
), we get

(4.25)

\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p_{3}}(\Omega))}\leq C\quad\mbox{with}\quad p_{3}:=\frac{6\gamma}{\gamma+6}>\frac{6}{5},

which leads, along with (
4.21
), to

(4.26)

\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{p_{4}}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3}))}\leq C\quad\mbox{with}\quad p_{4}:=\frac{3\gamma}{\gamma+3}>1.

4.3.
Estimates of chemical and free energy potentials

(i)

First of all, from the conservation of the total (
4.14
) and relative (
4.15
) masses, and from our definition (
1.22
) of
M_{r}
, the identity

(4.27)

\int_{\Omega}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon})(\tau,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x-M_{r}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x=M_{c}-M_{r}\,M=0

holds for any
\tau\in[0,T]
. Therefore, since
F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
is an increasing function, we have

(4.28)

\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\left[F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})-F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(M_{r})\right](c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x\geq 0

a.e. on
(0,T)
. Moreover, in view of Remark
3.2
and (
4.22
), for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
we can choose
\phi=c_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)-M_{r}
as test function in (
3.8
), obtaining that the integral identity

(4.29)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ \textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x+\theta_{0}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x

holds a.e. on
(0,T)
. Using once again (
4.27
), we can write

\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\big(\mu_{\varepsilon}-\langle\mu_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\Omega}\big)(\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r}\varrho_{\varepsilon})\ \textup{d}x,

where
\langle\mu_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\Omega}
denotes the spatial average value of
\mu_{\varepsilon}
. From the Sobolev embedding (
2.2
) and the Poincaré inequality (
2.4
), we infer that

\|\mu_{\varepsilon}-\langle\mu_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\Omega}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|\mu_{\varepsilon}-\langle\mu_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\Omega}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla_{x}\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Consequently, going back to (
4.29
), from estimates (
4.22
), (
4.25
) and applying the Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega)}\left(\|\mu_{\varepsilon}-\langle\mu_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\Omega}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}+\theta_{0}\|c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\nabla_{x}\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})}+1\right).

Integrating over
(0,T)
the previous inequality squared, and exploiting (
4.20
), we end up with

(4.30)

\left\|\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla_{x}\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})}^{2}\ \textup{d}t+C\leq C.

(ii)

Due to condition (
1.22
), we can fix
K_{0}>0
such that

|M_{r}|<K_{0}<1

and, for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
, consider the sets

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ |c_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)|<K_{0}\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ c_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\geq K_{0}\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ c_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\leq-K_{0}\}.

Notice that

–

if
x\in\Omega_{1}(\tau)
,

c_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)-M_{r}\geq K_{0}-M_{r}>0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{c_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)-M_{r}}{K_{0}-M_{r}}\geq 1;

–

if
x\in\Omega_{2}(\tau)
,

c_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)-M_{r}\leq-K_{0}-M_{r}<0\quad\Rightarrow\quad-\frac{c_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)-M_{r}}{K_{0}+M_{r}}\geq 1.

We now write, for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
,

\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|\ \textup{d}x=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{j}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|\ \textup{d}x.

Noticing that
F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}=F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s)
is an odd and strictly increasing function on
\mathbbm{R}
, such that
F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
if and only if
s\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{0}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|\ \textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\max_{s\in[-K_{0},K_{0}]}\{|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s)|\}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\ \textup{d}x\leq M\,F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(K_{0})\leq M\,F^{\prime}(K_{0}),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{1}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|\ \textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{1}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})\ \textup{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{1}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r}}{K_{0}-M_{r}}\ \textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K_{0}-M_{r}}\int_{\Omega_{1}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{2}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|\ \textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{2}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\big(-F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})\big)\ \textup{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{2}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\big(-F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})\big)\left(-\frac{c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r}}{K_{0}+M_{r}}\right)\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K_{0}+M_{r}}\int_{\Omega_{2}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x.

Here we have used that
|F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(s)|\leq|F^{\prime}(s)|
for any
\in(-1,1)
.
Therefore, for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
, recalling also (
4.28
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|\ \textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K_{0}-|M_{r}|}\int_{\Omega_{1}(\tau)\cup\Omega_{2}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\,\textup{d}x+M\,F^{\prime}(K_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K_{0}-|M_{r}|}\left|\int_{\Omega_{1}(\tau)\cup\Omega_{2}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\,\textup{d}x\right|+M\,F^{\prime}(K_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K_{0}-|M_{r}|}\left|\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x-\int_{\Omega_{0}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x\right|+M\,F^{\prime}(K_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K_{0}-|M_{r}|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x+\frac{2}{K_{0}-|M_{r}|}\int_{\Omega_{0}(\tau)}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|\ \textup{d}x+M\,F^{\prime}(K_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K_{0}-|M_{r}|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-M_{r})\ \textup{d}x+\left(\frac{2}{K_{0}-|M_{r}|}+1\right)M\,F^{\prime}(K_{0}).

Integrating over
(0,T)
the previous inequality squared, from (
4.30
), we obtain

(4.31)

\left\|\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|\ \textup{d}x\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\leq C\left\|\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})(c_{\varepsilon}-K)\ \textup{d}x\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+C\leq C.

(iii)

We take
\phi=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}
as test function in (
3.8
), obtaining that the integral identity

\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}\ \textup{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})\ \textup{d}x-\theta_{0}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\ \textup{d}x

holds a.e. in

Integrating over
(0,T)
the previous inequality squared, we deduce from (

\left\|\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}\ \textup{d}x\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\leq\left\|\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon}))|\ \textup{d}x\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+C\leq C.

As consequence of Lemma
2.2
, we conclude that

(4.32)

\|\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))}\leq C.

(iv)

In view of Remark
3.2
, we can take
\varphi=F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))
as test function in (

), obtaining the identity

\quad\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\textup{d}x\textup{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})\big(\mu_{\varepsilon}+\theta_{0}c_{\varepsilon}\big)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

From the convexity of
F_{\varepsilon}
, i.e.
F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0
for any

, and Young inequality, we get

(4.33)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\textup{d}x\textup{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t+\frac{\theta_{0}^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

Exploiting the uniform estimates (
4.16
), (
4.32
) and the Sobolev embedding (

Owing (

).
Hence, it is straightforward to conclude that

(4.35)

\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C.

(v)

In light of (

), (

), (

), (

Hence, from the integral identity (

Then, by the Sobolev embedding (

) with

and

Since, by interpolation,

we infer from (

4.4.
Pressure estimate

Since from the energy inequality (
3.7
), we can only deduce that

\|p(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C.

Hence, we need some supplementary pressure estimates, which can be achieved by choosing a suitable test function in the weak formulation of the balance of momentum (

[

]

Let us consider the functions

\bm{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\psi(t)\bm{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t,x),\quad\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T),\quad\bm{\phi}_{\varepsilon}:=\mathcal{B}\left[b(\varrho_{\varepsilon})-\langle b(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\rangle_{\Omega}\right],

We now take

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon,1}

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon,2}

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon,6}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}\otimes\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[b(\varrho_{\varepsilon})-\langle b(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\rangle_{\Omega}\right]\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

(i)

From (

.

From (
2.6

\displaystyle|I_{\varepsilon,4}|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\times\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[b(\varrho_{\varepsilon})-\langle b(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\rangle_{\Omega}\right]\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{p_{4}}{p_{4}-1}}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3\times 3}))}\leq\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{3\gamma\nu}{2\gamma-3}}(\Omega))}^{\nu}.

) and (

), respectively. Exploiting once again the compact Sobolev embedding (
2.3
), we get from (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and (
4.6
), convergences (
4.9
) and (
4.10
). In addition, from (
4.7
) and (
4.45
), we derive (
4.8
).

4.6.
Strong convergence of the concentrations

The next goal is to show that

(4.46)

\|\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})}\to\|\nabla_{x}c\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})},

which, along with (
4.4
), will imply the strong convergence (
4.5
) of the concentrations.

The idea of the proof is essentially the same as in
[
4
, Section 2.6]
but with some simplifications; we report it for the sake of completeness.

•

From identity (
3.6
), we have that

(4.47)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}c_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}\,\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
, where, due to convergences (
4.1
), (
4.4
), (
4.7
) and estimate (
4.35
),

(4.48)

f_{\varepsilon}:=\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}(\mu_{\varepsilon}-F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})+\theta_{0}c_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup f\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega).

Thus, passing to the limit in (
4.47
), we deduce that the identity

(4.49)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}c\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\overline{\sqrt{\varrho}f}\,\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
, where

\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\overline{\sqrt{\varrho}f}\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{p_{1}}(\Omega))\quad\mbox{with}\quad p_{1}=\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}>\frac{6}{5}.

•

By a density argument, the class of test functions in (
4.47
) and (
4.49
) can be enlarged
to
L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))
. Therefore, taking
\varphi=c_{\varepsilon}
and
\varphi=c
in (
4.47
) and (
4.49
), respectively, (
4.46
) will follow as long as we manage to prove that

(4.50)

\overline{\sqrt{\varrho}fc}=\overline{\sqrt{\varrho}f}\,c\quad\mbox{a.e. in }(0,T)\times\Omega,

where, from (
4.1
), (
4.4
) and (
4.48
),

\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\overline{\sqrt{\varrho}fc}\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{p_{2}}(\Omega))\quad\mbox{with}\quad p_{2}=\frac{6\gamma}{4\gamma+3}>1.

•

From convergences (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and the compact Sobolev embedding (
2.3
), it follows that

\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}^{2}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\varrho c^{2}\quad\mbox{in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p_{4}}(\Omega))\quad\mbox{with}\quad p_{4}=\frac{3\gamma}{\gamma+3}>1.

Then, using additionally convergences (
4.1
) and (
4.3
), we deduce that

\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}(c_{\varepsilon}-c)\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}^{2}-2\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}c+\varrho_{\varepsilon}c^{2})\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Therefore, for any
\varphi\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\overline{\sqrt{\varrho}fc}-\overline{\sqrt{\varrho}f}\,c\right)\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon}-c)\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

where, from (
4.48
),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon}-c)\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}(c_{\varepsilon}-c)\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varphi)\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}(c_{\varepsilon}-c)\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, leading to (
4.50
).

From (
4.5
), we have in particular that

(4.51)

c_{\varepsilon}\to c\quad\mbox{a.e. in }(0,T)\times\Omega.

and thus, from hypothesis (
1.8
) and convergence (
4.6
), we get (
4.11
).

4.7.
Strong convergence of the densities

So far, from estimate (
4.41
), passing to a suitable subsequence as the case may be, we can only deduce that

(4.52)

p(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup\overline{p(\varrho)}\quad\mbox{in }L^{q}((0,T)\times\Omega),

with the exponent
q=q(\gamma)
defined as in (
4.41
). Therefore, in order to conclude that
\overline{p(\varrho)}=p(\varrho)
, we need that

(4.53)

\varrho_{\varepsilon}\to\varrho\quad\mbox{a.e. }(0,T)\times\Omega;

to this end, it is sufficient to show

(4.54)

\varrho_{\varepsilon}\to\varrho\quad\mbox{in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega).

The proof of (
4.54
) is definitely non-trivial but nowadays well-understood. The idea was first developed by Lions in
[
41
]
, and later adapted by Feireisl in
[
20
]
to handle the case of non-constant viscosity coefficients; we report the key ingredients in Appendix
A
.

4.8.
Boundedness of the concentration

Next, we prove some properties of the limit concentration
c
. By definition of the approximating potential
F_{\varepsilon}
, we have

F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(s)=F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(1-\varepsilon)+F^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(1-\varepsilon)(s-(1-\varepsilon))\geq F^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(1-\varepsilon)(s-1),\quad\forall\,s>1.

Since
\rho_{\varepsilon}
is non-negative, we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\{{c_{\varepsilon}}>1\}}\rho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(1-\varepsilon)|^{2}(c_{\varepsilon}-1)^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{{c_{\varepsilon}}>1\}}\rho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t.

In light of (
4.35
), we deduce that

\int_{\{{c_{\varepsilon}}>1\}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon}-1)^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\leq\frac{C}{|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(1-\varepsilon)|^{2}}.

Repeating the same argument for
s<1
, it is easily seen that

\int_{\{{c_{\varepsilon}}<-1\}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon}+1)^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\leq\frac{C}{|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(-1+\varepsilon)|^{2}}.

Thus, by the symmetry of
F_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|c_{\varepsilon}|-1)_{+}^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\leq\frac{C}{|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(1-\varepsilon)|^{2}}=\frac{C}{|F^{\prime\prime}(1-\varepsilon)|^{2}}.

From the a.e. convergence of the concentrations (
4.51
) and densities (
4.53
), an application of the Fatou lemma entails that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho(|c|-1)_{+}^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|c_{\varepsilon}|-1)_{+}^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{C}{|F^{\prime\prime}(1-\varepsilon)|^{2}}=0.

Thus, we conclude that

(4.55)

\Big|\Big\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T):\rho(x,t)>0,|c(x,t)|>1\Big\}\Big|=0.

In other words,

-1\leq c\leq 1\quad\mbox{a.e. in }\{{\rho>0\}}\subset(0,T)\times\Omega.

Furthermore, exploiting once again the pointwise convergences (
4.51
) and (
4.53
), it is not difficult to show that

\rho_{\varepsilon}|F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\to\rho|\widetilde{F^{\prime}}(c)|^{2}\quad\mbox{a.e. in }\{{\rho>0\}}\subset(0,T)\times\Omega,

where

\widetilde{F^{\prime}}(s):=\begin{cases}+\infty,\quad&\text{if}\quad s=1,\\
F^{\prime}(s),\quad&\text{if}\quad s\in(-1,1),\\
-\infty,\quad&\text{if}\quad s=-1.\end{cases}

Now, by the Fatou lemma and (
4.35
), we derive that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\widetilde{F^{\prime}}(c)|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\rho>0\}}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\rho>0\}}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\leq C.

As a consequence, we obtain that

(4.56)

-1<c<1\quad\mbox{a.e. in }\{{\rho>0\}}\subset\Omega\times(0,T),

which in turn implies that
\rho F^{\prime}(c)
is well-defined on the set
\{\rho>0\}
.

Owing to the above convergence results, we are now in the position to characterize the weak limit of
\rho_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})
. To this end, we first recall that

(4.57)

\rho_{\varepsilon}\to 0\quad\text{in}\quad L^{1}(\{{\rho=0\}}).

Now, for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
, we have

(4.58)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t=\int_{\{{\rho=0\}}}\rho_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t+\int_{\{{\rho>0\}}}\rho_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t.

By (
4.1
), (
4.35
) and (
4.57
), we observe that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\{{\rho=0\}}}\rho_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\{{\rho=0\}}}\sqrt{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{\rho_{\varepsilon}}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\sqrt{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\{{\rho=0\}})}\|\sqrt{\rho_{\varepsilon}}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\{{\rho=0\}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0,\quad\text{for}\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

On the other hand, since

\rho_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\to\rho F^{\prime}(c)\quad\mbox{a.e. in }\{{\rho>0\}}\subset(0,T)\times\Omega,

and, from estimates (
4.16
) and (
4.35
),

(4.59)

\|\rho_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{p_{1}}(\Omega))}\leq C\quad\mbox{with}\quad p_{1}=\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1},

we have that

(4.60)

\rho_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\rho>0\}}\rightharpoonup\rho F^{\prime}(c)\mathbbm{1}_{\{\rho>0\}}\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega)).

In conclusion, going back to (
4.58
), we deduce that

(4.61)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho F^{\prime}(c)\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t,

where the function
\rho F^{\prime}(c)
must be interpreted as

\rho F^{\prime}(c)=\begin{cases}\rho F^{\prime}(c)\quad&\text{if}\quad\rho>0\\
0\quad&\text{if}\quad\rho=0.\end{cases}

In particular, we get convergence (
4.12
).

4.9.
Passage to the limit

Thanks to the necessary convergences listed in Lemma
4.1
, we are now ready to let
\varepsilon\to 0
in identities (
3.3
)–(
3.6
), obtaining that the weak formulations (
1.24
)–(
1.27
) hold true for the limit functions
(\varrho,\textbf{{u}},c)
; we point out, in particular, that the validity of the renormalized continuity equation (
3.3
) was achieved in Section
A.3
, when proving the strong convergence of the densities.

In order to conclude the proof Theorem
1.1
, it remains to show that the energy inequality (
1.28
) holds true for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
. To this end, we first observe that, due to the convexity of
F_{\varepsilon}=F_{\varepsilon}(s)
,

F_{\varepsilon}(s_{0})\geq F_{\varepsilon}(s)+F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s)(s_{0}-s)\quad\mbox{for any }s,s_{0}\in\mathbbm{R}.

Hence, choosing
s=c_{\varepsilon}
and
s_{0}=0
, we obtain that
F_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\leq c_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})
, which implies, in view of estimates (
4.16
), (
4.22
) and (
4.35
) that

\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{p_{2}}(\Omega))}\leq C\quad\mbox{with}\quad p_{2}=\frac{6\gamma}{4\gamma+3}.

Proceeding similarly as in Section
4.8
, from the boundedness of the concentrations (
4.56
) and the pointwise convergences (
4.51
) and (
4.53
), we have

\varrho_{\varepsilon}F(c_{\varepsilon})\to\varrho F(c)\quad\mbox{a.e. on }\{\varrho>0\}\subset(0,T)\times\Omega.

On the other hand, from the strong convergence (
4.57
) of the densities on the set
\{\varrho=0\}
, for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\{\varrho=0\}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}(c_{\varepsilon})\varphi\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(\varphi)\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{3}(\{\varrho=0\})}\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\|c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{1}(\{\varrho=0\})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

We can therefore conclude that

\varrho_{\varepsilon}F(c_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup\varrho F(c)\mathbbm{1}_{\{\varrho>0\}}\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{6\gamma}{4\gamma+3}}(\Omega)).

Furthermore, in light of the strong convergences (
4.5
), (
4.54
), it is not difficult to prove that

\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}^{2}\to\varrho c^{2}\quad\mbox{in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega);

indeed, using additionally estimates (
4.16
), (
4.22
), the Sobolev embedding (
2.2
) and interpolation, we have, for some
\omega\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}^{2}-\varrho c^{2}\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|c_{\varepsilon}+c|\,|c_{\varepsilon}-c|\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\varrho_{\varepsilon}-\varrho|\,c^{2}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}\|c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))}\|c_{\varepsilon}-c\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle+\|c\|^{2}_{L^{6}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}-\varrho\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|c_{\varepsilon}-c\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))}+\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}-\varrho\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{\omega}\right)\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

Hence, exploiting the lower semicontinuity of the norms with respect to the weak or weak-
*
convergence, we are allowed to pass to the limit in the energy inequality (
3.7
); notice that it is first necessary to integrate
E_{\varepsilon}=E_{\varepsilon}(t)
over
(\tau,\tau+h)
to deduce

(4.62)

\int_{\tau}^{\tau+h}E(t)\ \textup{d}t\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+h}E_{\varepsilon}(t)\ \textup{d}t

for any
h>0
, where

E(t):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\textbf{{u}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho^{\gamma}+\varrho F(c)\mathbbm{1}_{\{\varrho>0\}}-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}\varrho c^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}\right)(t,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x.

Next, it is enough to divide the left-hand side of (
4.62
) by
h
and let
h\to 0
to recover that the energy inequality holds for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
.

Appendix A
Strong convergence of the densities

In this section we report the main steps in order to prove the strong convergence (
4.54
) of the densities.

A.1.
Step 1

Introducing the “truncation” function, defined for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
as

T_{k}(\varrho):=\min\{\varrho,k\},

the first step consists in showing that

(A.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\Big(p(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\,\xi\,T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})-\mathbb{S}(c_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}\big[\xi\,T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\big]\Big)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\Big(\overline{p(\varrho)}\,\xi\,\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-\mathbb{S}(c,\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}):\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}\big[\xi\,\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\big]\Big)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t,

for any
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T)
and any
\phi,\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Here, the symbol
\Delta_{x}^{-1}
denotes the inverse of the Laplace operator considered on the whole space
\mathbbm{R}^{3}
, while

(A.2)

T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\to\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\quad\mbox{in }C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega))\ \mbox{for any finite }p>1.

Due to the convergences (
4.1
)–(
4.11
) along with (
4.52
), we can pass to the limit in (
3.4
), obtaining that the integral identity

(A.3)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}(\varrho\textbf{{u}}\cdot\bm{\varphi})(t,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\big[\varrho\textbf{{u}}\cdot\partial_{t}\bm{\varphi}+(\varrho\textbf{{u}}\otimes\textbf{{u}}):\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}\big]\,\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\overline{p(\varrho)}\mathbb{I}-\mathbb{S}(c,\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}})\Big]:\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left((\nabla_{x}c\otimes\nabla_{x}c):\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}-\frac{|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}}{2}{\rm div}_{x}\bm{\varphi}\right)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

holds for any for any
\tau\in[0,T]
and any
\bm{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})
. Hence, we can take the quantities

\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\psi(t)\phi(x)\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\left[\xi(x)T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})(t,x)\right],

\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\psi(t)\phi(x)\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\left[\xi(x)\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}(t,x)\right],

with
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,T)
and
\phi,\,\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
fixed, as test functions in (
3.4
) and (
A.3
), respectively. Identity (
A.1
) will then follow by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, observing that
T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})
satisfies the renormalized continuity equation in the sense of distributions and exploiting the boundedness of the singular operator

(A.4)

\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}:L^{p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,p}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for any }p>1.

The main tool needed in this passage is a slightly modified version of the “Div–Curl Lemma” contained in
[
19
, Corollary 6.1, (ii)]
to deduce that

\textbf{{v}}_{\varepsilon}(t)\rightharpoonup\textbf{{v}}(t)\quad\mbox{in }L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbbm{R}^{3})\quad\mbox{for any }1\leq r<\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1},

where the quantities

\displaystyle\textbf{{v}}_{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle:=\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}\left[\xi\,T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})(t,\cdot)\right]\phi\,(\varrho_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon})(t,\cdot)-\xi\,T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})(t,\cdot)\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}{\rm div}_{x}\left[\phi\,(\varrho_{\varepsilon}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon})(t,\cdot)\right],

\displaystyle\textbf{{v}}(t)

\displaystyle:=\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}\left[\xi\,\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}(t,\cdot)\right]\phi\,(\varrho\textbf{{u}})(t,\cdot)-\xi\,\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}(t,\cdot)\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}{\rm div}_{x}\left[\phi\,(\varrho\textbf{{u}})(t,\cdot)\right],

are defined for any
t\in[0,T]
– we point out that all the functions involved are continuous in time.

A.2.
Step 2

For a fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the next step is to show that

(A.5)

\overline{p(\varrho)\,T_{k}(\varrho)}-\overline{p(\varrho)}\,\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}=\left(\lambda(c)+\frac{4}{3}\mu(c)\right)\left(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}}-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}{\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}\right).

We observe that, for any vector
v
,

\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}:[\nabla_{x}\textbf{{v}}]=\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\mathcal{F}\left(\partial_{x_{k}}v_{j}\right)\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(i\xi_{j}\mathcal{F}\left(v_{j}\right)\right)={\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{v}},

and therefore we can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\mathbb{S}(c_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}\big[\xi\,T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\big]\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\Omega}\xi\,\Big(\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}:\big[\phi\,\mu(c_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\big]-\phi\,\mu(c_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}:[\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}]\Big)\,T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\Omega}\xi\,\phi\left(\lambda(c_{\varepsilon})+\frac{4}{3}\mu(c_{\varepsilon})\right)T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\,{\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

By interpolation we have

L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2p_{4}}(\Omega))\hookrightarrow L^{2p_{2}}(0,T;W^{1,2p_{2}}(\Omega))\quad\mbox{with}\quad p_{2}=\frac{6\gamma}{4\gamma+3}>\frac{6}{5},

and therefore, proceeding as for (
4.37
), we can apply a variant of the “Commutator Lemma”, see
[
20
, Lemma 4.2]
, to deduce that

\left\|\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}:\big[\phi\,\mu(c_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\big]-\phi\,\mu(c_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}:[\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}]\right\|_{L^{q}(0,T;W^{\alpha,q}(\Omega))}\leq C

for some
q>1
and
0<\alpha<1
. Hence, from (
4.6
), (
4.51
) and (
A.2
) with
p
large enough so that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<1
, we deduce

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\Omega}\xi\,\Big(\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}:\big[\phi\,\mu(c_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}\big]-\phi\,\mu(c_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}:[\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}]\Big)\,T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\Omega}\xi\,\Big(\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}:\big[\phi\,\mu(c)\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}\big]-\phi\,\mu(c)\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}:[\nabla_{x}\textbf{{u}}]\Big)\,\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t.

Going back to (
A.1
), we finally get (
A.5
).

A.3.
Step 3

We now need to show that the limits
(\varrho,\textbf{{u}})
of (
4.1
), (
4.6
) constitute a renormalized solution, in the sense that the integral identity (
1.24
) holds for any given functions
b
and
B
satisfying (
1.23
). In view of the regularizing procedure by DiPerna and Lions
[
15
]
, see also

[
19
, Corollary 4.1]
, the latter is satisfied if the density
\varrho
is at least square-integrable, which, due to estimate (
4.40
), holds true for
\gamma\geq\frac{9}{5}
. To handle the case
\frac{3}{2}<\gamma<\frac{9}{5}
, as consequence of
[
19
, Proposition 6.3]
it is enough to show that the
oscillation defect measure
is finite:

(A.6)

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})-T_{k}(\varrho)|^{\gamma+1}\textup{d}x\textup{d}t\right)<\infty.

From the convexity of
p=p(\varrho)
, identity (
A.5
), Young inequality and hypothesis (
1.8
), we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})-T_{k}(\varrho)|^{\gamma+1}\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(p(\varrho_{\varepsilon})T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})-\overline{p(\varrho)}\,\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\right)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\lambda(c)+\frac{4}{3}\mu(c)\right)T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\left({\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}\right)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma+1}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})-T_{k}(\varrho)|^{\gamma+1}\textup{d}x\textup{d}t+\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|{\rm div}_{x}(\textbf{{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\textbf{{u}})|^{1+\frac{1}{\gamma}}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t,

and hence, (
A.6
) follows from (
4.6
).

A.4.
Step 4

Finally, we choose

b_{k}(\varrho)\equiv T_{k}(\varrho):=\min\{\varrho,k\},\quad B_{k}(\varrho)\equiv L_{k}(\varrho):=\int_{1}^{\varrho}\frac{\min\{z,k\}}{z^{2}}\ \textup{d}z,

and
\varphi\equiv 1
in both (
1.24
) and (
3.3
). By using (
A.5
), we find that the identity

(A.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\overline{\varrho L_{k}(\varrho)}-\varrho L_{k}(\varrho)\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\lambda(c)+\frac{4}{3}\mu(c)\right)^{-1}\left(\overline{p(\varrho)\,T_{k}(\varrho)}-\overline{p(\varrho)}\,\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\right)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-T_{k}(\varrho)\right){\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t=0

holds for any
\tau\in[0,T]
. The last integral vanishes for
k\to+\infty
; indeed, from (
A.6
), Hölder inequality and interpolation, we have that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-T_{k}(\varrho)\right){\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\|{\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{\omega}\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{\gamma+1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{1-\omega}\|{\rm div}_{x}\textbf{{u}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)},

for some
\omega\in(0,1)
; from the weak lower semi-continuity of the norm, we deduce

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})-\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}+\|\varrho-T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\int_{\{\varrho_{\varepsilon}\geq k\}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t+\int_{\{\varrho\geq k\}}\varrho\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\leq k\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left(\int_{\left\{\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{k}\geq 1\right\}}\left(\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{k}\right)^{\gamma}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t+\int_{\left\{\frac{\varrho}{k}\geq 1\right\}}\left(\frac{\varrho}{k}\right)^{\gamma}\ \textup{d}x\textup{d}t\right)\leq 2\,k^{1-\gamma}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{\gamma},

where the last term on the right-hand side vanishes for
k\to+\infty
.

Moreover, from hypothesis (
1.8
), there exist constants
\underline{V},\,\overline{V}
such that

0<\underline{V}\leq\left(\lambda(c)+\frac{4}{3}\mu(c)\right)^{-1}\leq\overline{V},

while, from the fact that the function
p=p(\varrho)
is non-decreasing in
[0,\infty)
, we have that

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\overline{p(\varrho)\,T_{k}(\varrho)}-\overline{p(\varrho)}\,\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\right)\textup{d}x\textup{d}t\geq 0.

Thus, we obtain, for any
\tau\in[0,T]
,

\int_{\Omega}\left(\overline{\varrho\ln(\varrho)}-\varrho\ln(\varrho)\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x\leq C\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(\overline{\varrho L_{k}(\varrho)}-\varrho L_{k}(\varrho)\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \textup{d}x\leq 0;

on the other side,
\varrho\ln(\varrho)\leq\overline{\varrho\ln(\varrho)}
due to the convexity of the function
\varrho\mapsto\varrho\ln(\varrho)
and therefore we get that

\overline{\varrho\ln(\varrho)}=\varrho\ln(\varrho)\quad\mbox{a.e. on }(0,T)\times\Omega,

leading to (
4.54
).

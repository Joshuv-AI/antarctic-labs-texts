Abstract
. It is shown that every linear strong Birkhoff-James isomorphism between unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebras is a ∗ * -isomorphism followed by a unitary multiplication. Moreover, as a partial extension of this result to the non-unital case, the form of (possibly nonlinear) strong Birkhoff-James isomorphisms between compact C ∗ C^{*} -algebras are determined. A nonlinear characterization of compact C ∗ C^{*} -algebras in terms of strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality is also given.

1.
Introduction

An
\mathscr{S}
-preserver is a mapping that preserves a certain structure
\mathscr{S}
of mathematical objects. As basic examples, a linear isomorphism is a linear-structure preserver and a homeomorphism is a topological-structure preserver. In most cases, preservers can be formulated as the class of (iso)morphisms in some concrete category, and the class of objects are chosen between those of Banach spaces and Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules depending on structures under consideration. It is important to obtain detailed descriptions of various preservers as barometers for amounts of information contained in the corresponding structures. In this direction, the finer the structure
\mathscr{S}
is, the more restrictive
\mathscr{S}
-preservers are. A problem that asks for the form of
\mathscr{S}
-preservers is called a preserver problem of the structure
\mathscr{S}
.

The prototype of the study on preserver problems dates back to 1897, the Frobenius theorem: A linear determinant preserver on a matrix algebra
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is either of the forms
A\mapsto QAP
or
A\mapsto QA^{T}P
, where
P
and
Q
are regular matrices such that
\det(PQ)=1
. Roughly speaking, up to transpose, the matrix equivalence is controlled by the structure of determinant to some extent. If, further, a linear mapping preserves eigenvalues of matrices with multiplicity, then
Q
can be chosen
P^{-1}
; see
[
49
, Theorem 3]
. Here, it is worth noting that the structure of eigenvalues with multiplicity contains more information than that of determinant, since
\det(A)
is the product of the eigenvalues of
A
with multiplicity. Up to now, there are many interesting formulations and results on preserver problems concerning algebraic structures in matrix algebras, or more generally, in
C^{*}
-algebras. We refer to Hou and Petek-Šemrl
[
31
,
57
]
for the structure of preservers of rank-one operators, which is one of the important reduction techniques in preserver problems. Of similarly importance is reduction to singularity preservers
[
22
]
or invertibility preservers; following the idea by Brešar-Šemrl
[
19
]
of using projections as an attempt towards their classification, Aupetit
[
11
]
succeeded to show that unital linear bijections which preserve the invertibility on a (general) von-Neumann algebra, are Jordan maps.
Another powerful technique in invertible linear preserver problems on matrices is using overgroups. One can often easily find the natural candidates which preserve
\mathscr{S}
and it remains to check in the list which of their overgroups still preserves
\mathscr{S}
; for more on this see Đoković, Li, and Platonov
[
23
,
24
,
58
]
. We refer also to Li-Pierce
[
47
]
for a brief overview of general techniques. A different approach is using Hermitian operators (in a sense of Vidav-Palmer); this was used by Sourour
[
56
]
to determine that
X\mapsto UXV^{\ast}
and
X\mapsto UX^{t}V^{\ast}
(
U,V
unitary) are all the isometries of minimal normed ideals of
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})
, the algebra of bounded operators on a separable Hilbert space. One can also successfully use ring-theoretical techniques in solving (linear) preserver problems; we mention here the theory of functional identities. This was pioneered by Brešar and its early success culminated in a solution
[
17
]
of Herstein conjectures that classify commutativity preserving maps on prime rings essentially as Jordan maps. Later it was fruitfully used by Alaminos, Brešar, and Villena in investigating Lie/Jordan derivations on von-Neumann algebras
[
1
]
.
General (unital)
C^{*}
-algebras lack projections or minimal elements, but one can exploit other properties in solving preserver problems. We mention here Hatori and Molnár’s
[
30
]
classification of isometries of Thompson metric
\|\ln(a^{-1/2}ba^{-1/2})\|
on a positive cone, from which one can further determine the structure of positive homogeneous preservers isomorphisms of Löwner order (we refer to
[
53
, Theorem 13]
for a precise statement). By reducing to order-monotone maps, preservers of various means on a positive cone of a general
C^{*}
-algebra were classified in
[
25
]
; see also
[
52
]
. The books by Brešar et al.
[
18
]
and by Molnár
[
51
]
as well as the surveys by Bourhim and Mashreghi
[
16
]
and by Li and Tsing
[
48
]
exhibit plenty of interesting preserver problems on algebraic structures and well-developed techniques thereon.

Another important direction of preserver problems is formulated in the normed space settings by using norm-metrics. The simplest (but often highly nontrivial) one is a problem of determining the form of isometric isomorphisms between certain normed spaces. For example, the Banach-Stone theorem states that if
T\colon C_{0}(K)\to C_{0}(L)
is an isometric isomorphism, then there exists a unimodular continuous function
u
on
L
and a homeomorphism
\varphi\colon K\to L
such that
Tf=u(f\circ\varphi^{-1})
for each
f\in C_{0}(K)
. In particular, the mapping
f\mapsto f\circ\varphi^{-1}
preserves multiplication (and complex conjugation in the complex case). This means that the Banach space structure of
C_{0}(K)
controls its whole structure as a Banach (or a
C^{*}
-) algebra. Moreover, the Banach-Stone theorem has the following generalization: Let
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
be
C^{*}
-algebras, and let
T\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}
be an isometric isomorphism. Then there exists a unitary element
u\in\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{B})
and a Jordan
*
-isomorphism
J\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}
such that
Tx=uJx
for each
x\in\mathfrak{A}
, where
\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{B})
is the multiplier algebra of
\mathfrak{B}
; see Kadison
[
35
]
for the unital case, and Paterson and Sinclair
[
55
]
for the non-unital case. Therefore, the Banach space structure of a
C^{*}
-algebra can still control its Jordan
*
-algebraic structure even if the algebra is non-commutative. Meanwhile, it is well-known as the Mazur-Ulam theorem that every (possibly nonlinear) surjective isometry between normed spaces is affine over the real numbers. Namely, as an affine mapping is a translation of a real-linear mapping, normed spaces are isometrically isomorphic as metric spaces if and only if they are isometrically isomorphic as real normed spaces. Hence, the (nonlinear) metric structure of a normed space solely determines its structure as a real normed space. The combination of the Kadison-Paterson-Sinclair theorem and the Mazur-Ulam theorem suggests a classification of
C^{*}
-algebras based on substructures of the metric structure.

Potential candidates are geometric structures determined by the shape of the unit ball of a normed space. The present paper focuses on the orthogonal structure with respect to (a strengthened version of) the Birkhoff-James (shortly, BJ) orthogonality which is a generalized orthogonality relation in normed spaces introduced by Birkhoff
[
12
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a normed space over
\mathbb{K}
, and let
x,y\in X
. Then,
x
is said to be
Birkhoff-James orthogonal
to
y
, denoted by
x\perp_{BJ}y
if
\|x+\lambda y\|\geq\|x\|
for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
.

Basic properties of the BJ orthogonality were mainly discovered by James
[
33
,
34
]
. In common with the usual inner product orthogonality
\perp
, it is known that
\perp_{BJ}
is non-degenerate and homogeneous, that is,
x\perp_{BJ}x
implies
x=0
, and
x\perp_{BJ}y
implies
\alpha x\perp_{BJ}\beta y
for all scalars
\alpha
and
\beta
. Meanwhile, one of the biggest difference between the usual orthogonality and BJ orthogonality is found in symmetry. Namely, if
\dim X\geq 3
, and if
x\perp_{BJ}y
implies
y\perp_{BJ}x
, then
X
is an inner product space. This result was shown independently by Day
[
21
]
and James
[
33
]
, originally for real normed spaces, based on a characterization of inner product spaces given by Kakutani
[
39
]
. It can be also shown for complex normed spaces with the help of Bohnenblust
[
15
]
; see also
[
63
, Section 4]
. The readers interested in the BJ orthogonality are referred to comprehensive surveys
[
3
,
4
]
.

A linear preserver problem of the BJ orthogonality relation was considered by Koldobsky
[
41
]
for the real case and Blanco and Turnšek
[
14
]
for the general case, who came to the best conclusion. To be precise, if a linear mapping between normed spaces preserves the BJ orthogonality in one direction, then it is a scalar multiple of an isometry. This indicates that the BJ orthogonality structure contains much information. Indeed, it was shown in
[
9
]
that every (possibly nonlinear) bijection between smooth normed spaces preserving the BJ orthogonality in both direction is essentially induced by an isometric isomorphism except for the two-dimensional case; see also
[
32
,
65
]
. Moreover, it turned out that the commutativity of a
C^{*}
-algebra can be characterized in terms of the BJ orthogonality, and that a bijection between abelian
C^{*}
-algebras preserving the BJ orthogonality in both direction induces a
*
-isomorphism; see
[
64
,
66
]
. Here, it should be emphasized that
C^{*}
-algebras are far from smooth except for
\mathbb{C}
. In this direction, recently, the study on nonlinear preserver problems on the BJ orthogonality for non-commutative
C^{*}
-algebras has also begun in
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
.

The Kadison-Paterson-Sinclair theorem reveals that the Jordan
*
-algebraic structure of a
C^{*}
-algebra obeys its Banach space structure, and the Koldobsky-Blanco-Turnšek theorem ensures that the combination of linear and BJ orthogonal structures controls the whole Banach space structure. This simultaneously explains the robustness and limit of the BJ orthogonal structure of a
C^{*}
-algebra. In particular, to obtain the complete information of full
C^{*}
-algebraic structure, the BJ orthogonal structure is not enough. To overcome this difficulty, in fact, we already have an idea of fitting the BJ orthogonality to the algebraic structure of
C^{*}
-algebras. It is actually given for Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules in full generality.

For Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules (refer to Lance’s standard textbook book
[
46
]
) one can accommodate the definition of BJ orthogonality to better fit the module structure. Instead of requiring that in a one-dimensional space
\mathbb{C}y
the origin comes closest to a given vector
x
, we require that this holds for a
C^{*}
-submodule generated by
y
. This was done in
[
5
]
and is known henceforth as
strong Birkhoff-James
(shortly, strong BJ)
orthogonality
. We will give its formal definition only for
C^{*}
-algebras considered naturally as
C^{*}
-modules over themselves, since this is the prime interest of our study.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}
. Then,
x
is said to be
strongly Birkhoff-James orthogonal
to
y
, denoted by
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
if
\|x+yz\|\geq\|x\|
for each
z\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Remark 1.3
.

It is obvious that
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
if and only if
x\perp_{BJ}yz
for each
z\in\mathfrak{A}
. Considering an approximate unit for
\mathfrak{A}
, if necessary, one can show that
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
implies
x\perp_{BJ}y
(see
[
5
, implications in (2.1)]
).

Remark 1.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\subseteq\mathfrak{B}
be two
C^{\ast}
-algebras and
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}
. Then,
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
in
\mathfrak{A}
if and only if
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
in
\mathfrak{B}
(see
[
40
, Lemma 1.1]
or
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
for an even stronger result).

As in the case of BJ orthogonality, the strong BJ orthogonality is non-degenerate and homogeneous, but not symmetric in general. Indeed, the strong BJ orthogonality is symmetric in a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
if and only if
\dim\mathfrak{A}\leq 1
; see
[
7
, Corollary 2.7]
. Linear preserver problems on the strong BJ orthogonality were first studied by Arambasić and Rajić
[
6
]
. It turned out that every linear bijection on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
preserving the strong BJ orthogonality in both directions is a scalar multiple of a
*
-automorphism followed by a unitary element from the left. Later, in
[
42
, Theorem 5.7]
, this result was generalized to linear bijection between von Neumann algebras preserving the strong BJ orthogonality in both directions, which is currently the best in the existing results on the general linear case. In the context of nonlinear preservers, it is known that a bijection between
C_{0}(K)
and
C_{0}(L)
preserving the strong BJ orthogonality in both directions induces a homeomorphism between
K
and
L
; see
[
62
, Theorems 5.2 and 5.5]
. Moreover, in
[
8
, Corollary 3.14]
, it was shown that two compact
C^{*}
-algebras are
*
-isomorphic if (and only if) they admit a bijection preserving the strong BJ orthogonality in both directions. This is stated as a nonlinear classification result and does not contain any explicit description of preservers. In any (known) case, the strong BJ orthogonality structure distinguishes the full
C^{*}
-algebraic structure.

The present paper is devoted to preservers of strong BJ orthogonality between
C^{*}
-algebras which we assign the following term.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
be
C^{*}
-algebras, and let
\Phi:\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}
. Then
\Phi
is called a
strong BJ isomorphism
if it is bijective and
x\perp_{BJ}^{s}y
is equivalent to
\Phi(x)\perp_{BJ}^{s}\Phi(y)
.

The main purpose of the present paper is twofold. One is to describe all linear strong BJ isomorphisms between unital
C^{*}
-algebras, which drastically improves the corresponding result for von Neumann algebras. As our method requires the existence of the unit for at least one of the
C^{*}
-algebras under consideration, the general (non-unital) case remains open. Another purpose is to partially complement the general case by considering compact
C^{*}
-algebras. We begin by giving a complete classification of their nonlinear strong BJ isomorphisms. We further characterize which
C^{*}
-algebras are compact
C^{*}
-algebras completely in terms of the strong BJ orthogonality structure.
This also substantially improves the existing results. Moreover, based on the nonlinear theory, all linear strong BJ isomorphisms between compact
C^{*}
-algebras are determined. A fundamental observation, valid in both the unital and compact settings, is that every linear isometry which is also a strong BJ isomorphism arises from a
*
-isomorphism. This essentially follows from the decomposition theorem for Jordan
*
-isomorphisms between the (enveloping) von Neumann algebras into the direct sum of
*
-isomorphisms and
*
-anti-isomorphisms; see Kadison
[
35
]
and Størmer
[
61
]
. Consequently, the main problem in the classification of linear strong BJ isomorphisms reduces to determining whether they are isometric. In the concluding remarks, a detailed description of linear strong BJ isomorphism on a compact
C^{*}
-algebra from the viewpoint of operator-theory is given.

2.
Preliminaries

We follow the standard terminology in the theory of
C^{*}
-algebras; see, for example,
[
13
,
37
,
38
,
50
,
59
]
. Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. Then the symbols
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A})
and
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
stand for the set of all states and pure states of
\mathfrak{A}
. The multiplicative unit for a unital
C^{*}
-algebra is denoted by
\mathbf{1}
. For notational convenience, the symbol
\mathbf{1}
will also be employed in non-unital algebras, in situations where its occurrence harmlessly abbreviates expressions that do not presuppose the existence of a unit, such as such as
x(\mathbf{1}-y)^{2}x=x^{2}-2xyx+xy^{2}x
or
e^{x}-\mathbf{1}=\sum_{1}^{\infty}x^{n}/n!
, etc. If
\mathfrak{A}
is non-unital, then the spectrum of an element of
\mathfrak{A}
is considered in the unitization
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
of
\mathfrak{A}
. It is known that each element of
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A})
(or
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
) extends uniquely to an element of
\mathcal{S}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}})
(or
\mathcal{P}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}})
). As usual, we always consider a non-unital
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
as a closed two-sided ideal of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
, and a (pure) state of
\mathfrak{A}
as a restriction of that of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
. Regarding this convention, the only problem occurs in functional calculus of a self-adjoint element
x\in\mathfrak{A}
, because the element
f(x)
corresponding to
f\in C(\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(x))
belongs to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
in general. However, we know that
f(x)\in\mathfrak{A}
whenever
f(0)=0
; for such an
f
can be approximated by polynomials without constant terms. Let
|x|=(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
for each
x\in\mathfrak{A}
. Since
0^{1/2}=0
, we obtain
|x|\in\mathfrak{A}
regardless of whether
\mathfrak{A}
is unital or not.

For a (complex) Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
denote the
C^{*}
-algebras of all bounded (linear) operators and compact operators on
\mathcal{H}
. It is well-known that
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
is a closed two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. A
C^{*}
-subalgebra
\mathfrak{A}
of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is called a
concrete

C^{*}
-algebra; in which case,
\mathfrak{A}
is said to act on
\mathcal{H}
. An element of a concrete
C^{*}
-algebra is often denoted by upper case letters as it is a bounded linear operator. By the Gelfand-Naimark theorem, every
C^{*}
-algebra is
*
-isomorphic to a concrete
C^{*}
-algebra.

A concrete
C^{*}
-algebra closed under the weak-operator topology is called a
von Neumann algebra
. Recall that a net
(A_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
converges to
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
with respect to the weak-operator topology if and only if
\lim_{\lambda}\langle A_{\lambda}\xi,\zeta\rangle=\langle A\xi,\zeta\rangle
for each
\xi,\zeta\in\mathcal{H}
; in which case, for any
B,C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the net
(BA_{\lambda}C)_{\lambda}
also converges to
BAC
with respect to the weak-operator topology. Let
X
be a subset of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then the
commutant
of
X
is defined by
X^{\prime}=\{A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):\text{$AB=BA$ for each $B\in X$}\}
. If
X
is self-adjoint, that is, if
A\in X
implies
A^{*}\in X
, then
X^{\prime}
is a von Neumann algebra acting on
\mathcal{H}
.

A
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
is said to be
compact
if it is
*
-isomorphic to a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. The definition adopted here is based on that of “
C^{*}
-algebras of compact operators” discussed in
[
10
, Section 1.4]
, although the term “compact” comes from
[
2
, Definition 3.1]
. These two definitions are formally different from, but for
C^{*}
-algebras the same as, each other. This fact follows from the combination of
[
2
, Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.3 and Lemma 3.4 (i)]
and the structure theorem for compact
C^{*}
-algebras found in
[
2
, Theorem 8.2]
and
[
10
, Theorem 1.4.5]
.

2.1.
Approximate invertibility

Approximately right invertible elements were introduced in
[
27
]
and play a major role in the context of nonunital
C^{*}
-algebra (in unital
C^{*}
-algebra, they are equivalent to right invertible elements; see Proposition 2.7 and the subsequent paragraph of
[
27
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and let
a\in A
. We say that
a
is approximately right invertible if there is a net
\{b_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that
ab_{i}
is approximate unit for
A
.

Characterization of approximately right invertible elements was given in
[
40
, Proposition 2.2]
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
40
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a (possibly nonunital)
C^{*}
-algebra. The following are equivalent for
a\in\mathfrak{A}
:

(i)

a
is approximately right invertible.

(ii)

a
does not belong to any right modular ideal.

(iii)

For any pure state
\rho
we have
\rho(aa^{*})>0
.

We already mentioned that on unital
C^{*}
-algebras approximate and usual right invertibility coincide. However, this does not translate to unitizations. Consider, e.g., a nonunital abelian
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}=C_{0}(X)
of all complex-valued continuous functions on
X=[0,1)
that vanish at
1
. Here, pure states are point evaluations so, by the above classification,
f\in C_{0}(X)
is approximately right invertible if and only if it has no zeros. However,
f
, embedded in unitization
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}\simeq C([0,1])
, cannot be right invertible, because
f
must vanish at
1
. Similarly, on
\mathfrak{A}:=\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, the
C^{\ast}
-algebra of all compact operators on some Hilbert space, pure states are vector states so
a\in\mathfrak{A}
is approximately right invertible if and only if its range is dense. In particular, the classical Volterra operator
(Vf)(x)=\int_{0}^{x}f(t)\,dt
is approximately right invertible, but clearly not right invertible in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

2.2.
Norm-classification of strong BJ orthogonality

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}
. Then,
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
if and only if
\|\|y\|^{2}x-yy^{*}x\|=\|x\|\|y\|^{2}
.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. We note first that, in unitization
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
of
\mathfrak{A}
,
0\leq\mathbf{1}-\|y\|^{-2}yy^{*}\leq\mathbf{1}
which, in
\mathfrak{A}
, gives

(1)

x^{*}(\mathbf{1}-\|y\|^{-2}yy^{*})^{2}x\leq x^{*}(\mathbf{1}-\|y\|^{-2}yy^{*})x\leq x^{*}x\leq\|x\|^{2}\mathbf{1},

and then implies that
\|(\mathbf{1}-\|y\|^{-2}yy^{*})x\|\leq\|x\|
or equivalently,

(2)

\|\|y\|^{2}x-yy^{*}x\|\leq\|x\|\|y\|^{2}.

If
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
, then
x\perp_{BJ}y(y^{*}x)
or

\|x-\|y\|^{-2}yy^{*}x\|\geq\|x\|
. Hence, by (
2
),
\|\|y\|^{2}x-yy^{*}x\|=\|x\|\|y\|^{2}
.

Conversely, suppose that
\|\|y\|^{2}x-yy^{*}x\|=\|x\|\|y\|^{2}
. Let
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A})
be such that

\rho(x^{*}(\mathbf{1}-\|y\|^{-2}yy^{*})^{2}x)=\|x^{*}(\mathbf{1}-\|y\|^{-2}yy^{*})^{2}x\|=\|x\|^{2}.

By (
1
) it follows that
\rho(x^{*}(\mathbf{1}-\|y\|^{-2}yy^{*})x)=\rho(x^{*}x)=\|x\|^{2}
. In particular, we obtain

\rho(x^{*}yy^{*}x)=\|y\|\rho(x^{*}x)-\|y\|\rho(x^{*}(\mathbf{1}-\|y\|^{-1}yy^{*})x)=0.

Combining this with
[
5
, Theorem 2.5]
, we infer that
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra,
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A})
, and
x\in\mathfrak{A}
,
x\geq 0
. If
\rho(x)=\|x\|
, then
\rho(yx)=\rho(xy)=\rho(xyx)=\|x\|\rho(y)
for each
y\in\mathfrak{A}
. Meanwhile, if
\rho(x)=0
, then
\rho(y)=\rho((\mathbf{1}-x)y(\mathbf{1}-x))
for each
y\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Proof.

We may assume
\|x\|=1
. Consider a unique extension of
\rho
to the state of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
. If
\rho(x)=1
, then since
0\leq x^{2}\leq x\leq\mathbf{1}
,
1=\rho(x)\leq\rho(x^{2})^{1/2}\leq 1
, which implies that
\rho((\mathbf{1}-x)^{2})=0
. Hence,

|\rho((\mathbf{1}-x)y)|=|\rho(y^{*}(\mathbf{1}-x))|\leq\rho((\mathbf{1}-x)^{2})^{1/2}\rho(y^{*}y)^{1/2}=0

for each
y\in\mathfrak{A}
. This proves
\rho(y)=\rho(xy)=\rho(yx)
and

\rho(y)=\rho(xyx)
for each
y\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Now, suppose that
\rho(x)=0
. In this case, we have
0\leq\mathbf{1}-x
and
\|\mathbf{1}-x\|=\rho(\mathbf{1}-x)=1
. Therefore, the preceding paragraph shows
\rho(y)=\rho((\mathbf{1}-x)y(\mathbf{1}-x))
for each
y\in\mathfrak{A}
.
∎

2.3.
Reduction to positive cones

The lemma below is known, see
[
40
, Lemma 1.2]
or, independently,
[
5
, Theorem 2.5]
. However, since it is crucial for us we present its short proof for the sake of convenience.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
.

(ii)

|x^{*}|\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
.

(iii)

x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}|y^{*}|
.

(iv)

|x^{*}|\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}|y^{*}|
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv). Considering the universal representation of
\mathfrak{A}
, we may assume that
\mathfrak{A}
acts on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. In this case,
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
allows us to use all operators on
\mathcal{H}
as right multiplier in
\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}
. Namely,
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
in
\mathfrak{A}
if and only if
x\perp_{BJ}yz
for each
z\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Now, by polar decomposition, we recall that
x=|x^{*}|u
,
xu^{*}=|x^{*}|
,
y=|y^{*}|v
and
yv^{*}=|y^{*}|
for some partial isometries
u,v\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

Suppose that
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
. Then, we have

\||x^{*}|+|y^{*}|z\|\geq\|x+yv^{*}zu\|\geq\|x\|

for each
z\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, that is,
|x^{*}|\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}|y^{*}|
. Conversely, if
|x^{*}|\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}|y^{*}|
, then

\|x+yz\|\geq\||x^{*}|+|y^{*}|vzu^{*}\|\geq\|x\|

for each
z\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Therefore,
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
.
∎

2.4.
Right symmetry

Recall that
a\in\mathfrak{A}
is right-symmetric if
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}a
implies
a\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}x
. The corresponding notion of left-symmetricity also exists, see
[
29
]
or
[
54
]
, but will not be needed here. We characterize right symmetric elements via (pure) states, approximate invertibility, and ortho-graphs. Recall
[
40
, Definition 1.3]
that ortho-graph (of a mutual strong BJ orthogonality relation),
\Gamma_{\mkern-7.0mu\perp\mkern-10.0mu\perp}(\mathfrak{A})
, of a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
is an undirected graph whose vertices are the all points of a projective space of
\mathbb{P}\mathfrak{A}:=\{[x]=\mathbb{C}x;\;\;x\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}\}
, with vertices
[x]
and
[y]
connected by an undirected edge if their representatives are mutually strong BJ orthogonal, i.e.,
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
and
y\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}x
. A vertex
[x]
is isolated it no other vertex connects to it.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. The following are equivalent for
x\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
.

(i)

x
is right symmetric for
\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}
.

(ii)

y\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}x
implies
y=0
.

(iii)

\rho(xx^{*})>0
for each
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
.

(iv)

x
is approximately right invertible.

(v)

x
is an isolated point in the ortho-graph
\Gamma_{\mkern-7.0mu\perp\mkern-10.0mu\perp}(\mathfrak{A})
.

Proof.

\neg
(ii)
\Rightarrow

\neg
(i). Suppose that
y\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}x
for some
y\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. It may be assumed that
\|x\|=\|y\|=1
. By Lemma
2.3
, we have
\|y-xx^{*}y\|=1
. Since
\|(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})\|=\|(y-xx^{*}y)(y-xx^{*}y)^{*}\|=1
, there exists a state
\rho
of
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\rho((\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*}))=1
. Extending
\rho
to the unitization of
\mathfrak{A}
if necessary, we obtain

1=\rho((\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*}))\leq\rho((\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})^{2})\leq\rho(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})=1-\rho(xx^{*}).

It follows that
\rho(xx^{*})=0
, which together with

0\leq\rho(|x^{*}|)\leq\rho(xx^{*})^{1/2}\rho(\mathbf{1})^{1/2}=0

implies that
\rho(|x^{*}|)=0
. Therefore, the identity

\rho((\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|)yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|))=\rho(yy^{*})=\rho((\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*}))=1

holds. Now, let
z=|x^{*}|+(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|)yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|)
. Since
\rho(z)=1
and

0\leq z=|x^{*}|+(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|)yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|)\leq|x^{*}|+(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|)^{2}\leq\mathbf{1},

it follows that
\|z\|=1
.
Combining with
|\rho(xx^{*}z)|\leq\rho((xx^{*})^{*}(xx^{*}))^{1/2}\rho(z^{*}z)^{1/2}\leq\rho(xx^{*})^{1/2}\rho(z^{*}z)^{1/2}=0

we get
\|z+\lambda xx^{*}z\|\geq\rho(z+\lambda xx^{*}z)=1=\|z\|
for every complex
\lambda
, so
z\perp_{BJ}xx^{*}z
(by definition of BJ orthogonality)
which together with
[
5
, Theorem 2.5]
ensures
z\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}x
.

We claim that
x\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}z
. Indeed, if
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}z
, then
|x^{*}|\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}|z^{*}|
by Lemma
2.5
. We know that
|z^{*}|=z
,

\displaystyle|x^{*}|-2^{-1}z=2^{-1}(|x^{*}|-(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|)yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|))

and

\displaystyle-\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle\leq-(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|)yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|)

\displaystyle\leq|x^{*}|-(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|)yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-|x^{*}|)

\displaystyle\leq|x^{*}|\leq\mathbf{1}.

However, this means that
\||x^{*}|-2^{-1}z\|\leq 2^{-1}<1=\||x^{*}|\|
, that is,
|x^{*}|\not\perp_{BJ}z
. Thus,
x\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}z
, and
x
is not right symmetric for
\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Trivial observation.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Again it may be assumed that
\|x\|=1
. Suppose that
\rho(xx^{*})>0
for each pure state
\rho
of
\mathfrak{A}
. Let
y\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, there exists a pure state
\rho
of
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\rho((\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*}))=\|(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})y\|^{2}
. Recall that
\rho
extends uniquely to a pure state of the unitization of
\mathfrak{A}
.
It follows from
\rho(xx^{*})>0
that

\rho((\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})yy^{*}(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*}))\leq\|y\|^{2}\rho((\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})^{2})\leq\|y\|^{2}\rho(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})<\|y\|^{2},

which together with Lemma
2.3
implies that
y\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}x
.

\neg
(iii)
\Rightarrow

\neg
(ii). We assume that
\rho(xx^{*})=0
for some pure state
\rho
of
\mathfrak{A}
. Then, the GNS representation
\pi_{\rho}\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
with respect to
\rho
is irreducible. By the Kadison transitivity theorem, there exists a self-adjoint element
h\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
\pi_{\rho}(h)\xi_{\rho}=\xi_{\rho}
, where
\xi_{\rho}
is a unit vector in
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
such that
\rho(y)=\langle\pi_{\rho}(y)\xi_{\rho},\xi_{\rho}\rangle
for each
y\in\mathfrak{A}
. Set
k=|\sin(2^{-1}h\pi)|\in\mathfrak{A}
. It follows that
\pi_{\rho}(k)\xi_{\rho}=\xi_{\rho}
and
\|k\|=\rho(k)=1
. Now, we note that

0\leq(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})k(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})\leq(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})^{2}\leq\mathbf{1}-xx^{*}\leq\mathbf{1}

and

1=\rho(k)=\rho((\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})k(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})).

Therefore,
\|(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})k^{1/2}\|=\|(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})k(\mathbf{1}-xx^{*})\|^{1/2}=1
. Combining this with Lemma
2.3
, we infer that
k^{1/2}\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}x
.

(iv)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii). This is Proposition
2.2
.

(iv)
\Leftrightarrow
(v). See Propositions 2.5 and 2.6 in
[
40
]
.
∎

Since in unital
C^{*}
-algebras, approximate right invertibility coincides with the usual right invertibility (see
[
27
, Proposition 2.7]
), we immediately get the next corollary. However we decided to present a direct proof for convenience.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. The following are equivalent for
x\in A\setminus\{0\}
.

(i)

x
is right symmetric for the strong Birkhoff–James orthogonality
\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}
.

(ii)

x
is right invertible.

(iii)

xx^{\ast}
is invertible.

Proof.

Suppose that
x
is not right invertible. In this case,
xx^{*}
is not invertible; for otherwise,
x^{*}(xx^{*})^{-1}
is the right inverse for
x
. By
0\leq xx^{*}\leq\|xx^{*}\|\mathbf{1}
and the spectral mapping theorem we infer that
\|\mathbf{1}-\|xx^{*}\|^{-1}xx^{*}\|=1
, which together with Lemma
2.3
implies that
\mathbf{1}\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}x
. Meanwhile, we have
x\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}\mathbf{1}
by
x\neq 0
. Therefore,
x
is not right symmetric for
\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}
.

If
x
is right invertible with its right inverse
x^{\prime}
, then
y\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}x
implies that
y\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}xx^{\prime}y
. Hence,
y=0
and
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
. This shows
x
is right symmetric for
\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}
.

Lastly, if
xx^{\prime}=\mathbf{1}
for some
x^{\prime}
, then
\mathbf{1}=(xx^{\prime})(xx^{\prime})^{*}=xx^{\prime}(x^{\prime})^{*}x^{*}\leq\|x^{\prime}\|^{2}xx^{*}
, which ensures that
xx^{*}
is invertible. The converse is also true as shown in the begining of the proof. Hence,
x
is right invertible if and only if
xx^{*}
is invertible.
∎

3.
Preservers

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
be
C^{*}
-algebras, and let
T\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}
. Then,
T
is called a
strong BJ isomorphism
if it is bijective and
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
if and only if
Tx\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}Ty
.

Clearly, every linear
\ast
-isomorphism preserves the norm as well as the multiplication, so it is a strong BJ isomorphism. With usual BJ orthogonality it suffices to assume less: namely, linear isometries are automatically BJ isomorphisms; they, however, might not preserve strong BJ orthogonality. Below we classify which of isometries are strong BJ isomorphism. We will rely on a general description of linear isometries on
C^{*}
-algebras: they are composed by a multiplication with a unitary element from a multiplier algebra (which we briefly review next) and a Jordan
*
-isomorphism
(see
[
55
]
or
[
60
]
).

Given a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathfrak{B}
, represented nondegenerately on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
(i.e.,
\mathfrak{B}\xi=0
implies
\xi=0
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
), define

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B}):=\{X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):X\mathfrak{B}\subseteq\mathfrak{B}\hbox{ and }\mathfrak{B}X\subseteq\mathfrak{B}\}.

This is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra, contained in the von Neumann envelope
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime\prime}
and is (
\ast
-isometrically isomorphic to) a
multiplier algebra
of
\mathfrak{B}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{B})=\mathfrak{B}
if and only if
\mathfrak{B}
is unital. We refer to
[
60
]
and references therein for more information.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
be
C^{*}
-algebras, and let
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}
be an isometry between them. Then,
\Phi
is a strong BJ isomorphism if and only if there exist unitary elements
u\in\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{A})
and
v\in\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{B})
and a
*
-isomorphism
\Psi\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}
such that
\Phi(x)=\Psi(ux)=v\Psi(x)
for each
x\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Proof.

We only prove the nontrivial implication. Consider
\mathfrak{B}
as embedded into suitable
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. It is well-known (see
[
55
]
but see also
[
60
]
) that there exists a unitary
v\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
(actually,
v\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathfrak{B})
, the multiplier algebra of
\mathfrak{B}
) such that
\Phi(x)=vJ(x)
for some Jordan
\ast
-isomorphism
J\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}
. Then also
v^{\ast}\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathfrak{B})
and hence
y\mapsto v^{\ast}y
is a linear isometry on
\mathfrak{B}
. It is easily seen, from the definition of a strong BJ orthogonality, that this map is a strong BJ isomorphism. Thus, a Jordan
\ast
-isomorphism
J=v^{\ast}\Phi
is a composite of two strong BJ isomorphisms. Now, it was proven by Kadison
[
36
, Theorem 2.6]

and independently by Størmer in
[
61
, Theorem 3.3]
that
\mathfrak{B}\subseteq\mathfrak{B}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{B}_{2}
is (perhaps properly) contained in a direct sum of two
C^{*}
-algebras so that

J=J_{1}\oplus J_{2},

with
J_{1}\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}_{1}
being multiplicative and
J_{2}\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}_{2}
anti-multiplicative (more precisely, there exists a central projection
P\in\mathfrak{B}^{-}
, the weak-closure of
\mathfrak{B}
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, such that
J_{1}(x)=PJ(x)P
is a compression of
J
, and
J_{2}(x)=QJ(x)Q
,
Q=(I-P)
).

Assume
\mathfrak{B}_{2}
is not abelian. It is a classical result, due to Kaplansky, that then there exists a normalized
b=b_{1}\oplus b_{2}\in\mathfrak{B}\subseteq\mathfrak{B}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{B}_{2}
such that
b_{2}^{2}=0
(see, for example,
[
13
, II.6.4.14]
). Choose
a\in\mathfrak{A}
with
b=\Phi(a)=J_{1}(a)\oplus J_{2}(a)
. Then,

\displaystyle 0\oplus b_{2}b_{2}^{*}b_{2}

\displaystyle=0\oplus J_{2}(aa^{*}a)=0\oplus(J_{2}(aa^{*}a)-J_{2}(a^{*})b_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=0\oplus(J_{2}(aa^{*}a)-J_{2}(a^{*})J_{2}(a)J_{2}(a))

\displaystyle=(J_{1}(aa^{*}a)-J_{1}(aa^{*})J_{1}(a))\oplus(J_{2}(aa^{*}a)-J_{2}(aa^{*})J_{2}(a))

\displaystyle=\Phi(aa^{*}a)-\Phi(aa^{*})\Phi(a)\in\mathfrak{B}.

In particular,

y:=0\oplus b_{2}b_{2}^{*}b_{2}\in\mathfrak{B}.

It is nonzero by
\|b^{*}y\|=\|0\oplus b_{2}^{*}b_{2}b_{2}^{*}b_{2}\|=\|(b_{2}^{*}b_{2})^{2}\|=\|b_{2}\|^{4}\neq 0
, however
y^{2}=0
.

Now,
(y^{*}y)^{*}y=y^{*}y^{2}=0
, so
y\perp y^{*}y
. However,
y\in(0\oplus\mathfrak{B}_{2})\cap\mathfrak{B}
, so if
y=\Phi(x)=0\oplus J_{2}(x)
, then

\Phi(xx^{\ast})=J_{1}(x)J_{1}(x)^{\ast}\oplus J_{2}(x)^{\ast}J_{2}(x)=0\oplus J_{2}(x)^{\ast}J_{2}(x)=y^{*}y,

and
x\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}xx^{\ast}
, because
|x^{*}|\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}|x^{*}|^{2}
(see Lemma
2.5
and
2.3
). This is a contradiction. Thus,
\mathfrak{B}_{2}
is abelian, and then
J_{2}
, hence also
J=J_{1}\oplus J_{2}
, is multiplicative, so a
\ast
-isomorphism.

This gives the first part since
\Phi(x)=v\Psi(x)
for a
\ast
-isomorphism
\Psi=J
; the second part can be proven along the same lines starting with
\Phi^{-1}
.
∎

3.1.
The unital and linear case

The following is an extension of
[
42
, Theorem 5.4]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. The following are equivalent for
x\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
.

(i)

x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
whenever
y
is not right symmetric for
\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{A}
is unital and
\|x\|^{-1}x
is a coisometry.

Proof.

It may be assumed that
\|x\|=1
. Suppose that
y\in\mathfrak{A}
is not right symmetric for
\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}
, in which case,
y
is not right invertible by Corollary
2.7
. As in the proof of Corollary
2.7
, the spectral mapping theorem gives
\|\mathbf{1}-\|yy^{*}\|^{-1}yy^{*}\|=1
which, by Lemma
2.3
, implies that

\mathbf{1}\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y.

If
x
is a coisometry, then
\mathbf{1}=xx^{*}\in\mathfrak{A}
, so also
|x^{*}|=\mathbf{1}\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
and hence
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
, by Lemma
2.5
.

Conversely, suppose that
x
is not a coisometry. Then, passing to unitization if needed,
|x^{*}|\neq\mathbf{1}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(|x^{*}|)\subset[0,1]
contains an element
t_{0}\in[0,1)
. Let
f
be a continuous function on
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(|x^{*}|)
such that
0\leq f\leq\mathbf{1}
,
f(t_{0})=0
and
f(1)=1
. We note that
the evaluation functional
\delta_{t_{0}}
at
t_{0}
corresponds to the restriction of a pure state
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
to the
C^{*}
-subalgebra generated by
|x^{*}|
. Hence,
y=|x^{*}|f(|x^{*}|)
is not right symmetric for
\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}
by
\rho(y)=0
and Lemma
2.6
. However, we obtain
|x^{*}|\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
by

\||x^{*}|-y\|=\max_{t\in\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(|x^{*}|)}|t-tf(t)|<1=\|\,|x^{*}|\,\|,

which implies that
x\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
.
∎

We can characterize unital
C^{*}
-algebras among all
C^{*}
-algebras by using strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. Then,
\mathfrak{A}
is unital if and only if there exists an element
x\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
such that
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
whenever

is not right symmetric for
\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}
.

Proof.

In the light of Lemma
3.3
, the latter condition means the existence of a coisometry in
\mathfrak{A}
. Combining this with the fact that
\mathfrak{A}
is unital if and only if it contains a coisometry, we obtain the proposition.
∎

We need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
u\in\mathfrak{A}
be a coisometry. Then, there exists a complex number
\lambda
such that
|\lambda|=1
and
u-\lambda\mathbf{1}
is not right invertible.

Proof.

Since
u
is a coisometry,
u^{*}
is an isometry, which implies that
\|(u^{*})^{n}\|=1
for each
n
. It follows that the spectral radius of
u^{*}
is
\lim_{n}\|(u^{*})^{n}\|^{1/n}=1
. Let
\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(u^{*})
be such that
|\lambda|=1
. From now on, by GNS, we consider
\mathfrak{A}
as embedded into
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. We know that
\partial\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(u^{*})
is contained in the approximate point spectrum of
u^{*}
; see, for example,
[
20

. Hence,
\inf\{\|(u^{*}-\lambda\mathbf{1})(\xi):\xi\in\mathcal{H},\|\xi\|=1\}=0
, which implies that
(u-\overline{\lambda}\mathbf{1})^{*}
is not left invertible, or equivalently, that
u-\overline{\lambda}\mathbf{1}
is not right invertible.
∎

The following is the main theorem in the unital and linear case.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
be
C^{*}
-algebras, and let
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}
be a linear strong BJ isomorphism. Suppose that
\mathfrak{A}
is unital. Then,
\mathfrak{B}
is also unital and
\Phi
is a scalar multiple of a
\ast
-isomorphism.

Proof.

Since automorphism
\Phi
preserves the right symmetry it turns out from Proposition
3.4
that
\mathfrak{B}
is also unital. In this case, by Corollary
2.7
and Lemma
3.3

\Phi
preserves right invertible elements and scalar multiples of coisometries. Set
\kappa=\|\Phi\mathbf{1}\|>0
.

Let
u
be a coisometry in
\mathfrak{A}
. Then, by Lemma
3.5
,
u-\lambda\mathbf{1}
is not right invertible for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|=1
. By Lemma
3.3
, it follows that
u\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}u-\lambda\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{1}\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}u-\lambda\mathbf{1}
, which implies that
\Phi u\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}\Phi u-\lambda\Phi\mathbf{1}
and
\Phi\mathbf{1}\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}\Phi u-\lambda\Phi\mathbf{1}
. In particular, we obtain

\|\Phi u\|\leq\|\Phi u-(\Phi u-\lambda\Phi\mathbf{1})\|=\|\Phi\mathbf{1}\|

and

\|\Phi\mathbf{1}\|\leq\|\Phi\mathbf{1}+\lambda^{-1}(\Phi u-\lambda\Phi\mathbf{1})\|=\|\Phi u\|,

that is,
\|\Phi u\|=\|\Phi\mathbf{1}\|=\kappa
. If
v\in\mathfrak{B}
is a coisometry, then
\|\Phi^{-1}v\|^{-1}\Phi^{-1}v
is a coisometry in
\mathfrak{A}
and
\kappa=\|\Phi(\|\Phi^{-1}v\|^{-1}\Phi^{-1}v)\|=\|\Phi^{-1}v\|^{-1}
, that is,
\kappa v=\Phi(\|\Phi^{-1}v\|^{-1}\Phi^{-1}v)
. From this, setting
\Psi=\kappa^{-1}\Phi
yields a linear strong BJ isomorphism
that preserves coisometries in both directions. In particular,
\|\Psi(u)\|=1
for each unitary
u
.
Now, by the Russo-Dye theorem
[
28
]
, each element of the open unit ball
B_{\mathfrak{A}}
is a convex combination of unitary elements of
\mathfrak{A}
. Combining this with
\|\Psi(u)\|=1
for each unitary
u\in\mathfrak{A}
, we get
\|\Psi(r\tfrac{x}{\|x\|})\|\leq 1
for every nonzero
x\in\mathfrak{A}
and
r\in(0,1)
. Equivalently,
\|\Psi(x)\|\leq\|x\|
. Applying the same arguments to linear strong BJ isomorphism
\Psi^{-1}
gives that
\Psi
is an isometric isomorphism. The rest follows from Theorem
3.2
.
∎

3.2.
Compact operators

For each unit vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, let
E_{\xi}
be the rank-one projection onto
\mathbb{C}\xi
.

Remark 3.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a compact
C^{*}
-algebra. Then, it is
*
-isomorphic to a
C^{*}
-algebra of the form
(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}))_{c_{0}(\Lambda)}
, which can be viewed as a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, where
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
(see, e.g.,
[
10
, Theorem 1.4.5]
). Let
P_{\lambda}
be the projection from
\mathcal{H}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
and let
\delta_{\lambda\mu}
denote the Kronecker
\delta
. Then, we have
P_{\mu}(A_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=(\delta_{\lambda\mu}A_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=(A_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}P_{\mu}
for each
(A_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\mathfrak{A}
and each
\mu\in\Lambda
. It follows that
\mathfrak{A}\subset\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Conversely, if
A\in\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, then
AP_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}AP_{\lambda}
which can be viewed as an element of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})
. Moreover,
we have
A=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}AP_{\lambda}
in the norm topology — this easily follows for rank-one
A
, then for finite-ranks and they are norm-dense in
\mathfrak{A}
.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{A}\supset\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, that is,
\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Consequently, if
\mathfrak{A}
is a compact
C^{*}
-algebra, then we may assume the following:

(i)

\mathfrak{A}
acts on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.

(ii)

There exists an orthogonal family of projections
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}P_{\lambda}=I
in the strong-operator topology and

\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}).

In this case, we note that
\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})P_{\lambda}\subset\mathfrak{A}
for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Given
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
we let

M_{A}:=\{\xi\in\mathcal{H}:\|A\xi\|=\|A\|\cdot\|\xi\|\}.

Note that for a compact operator
A
this set is always nonempty.
Clearly, if
A=0
, then
M_{A}=\mathcal{H}
. However, if
\|A\|=1
, then
\xi\in M_{A}
means that

\|\xi\|^{2}=\|A\xi\|^{2}=\langle|A|^{2}\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\langle|A|\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\|\xi\|^{2}.

Hence,
\langle|A|\xi,\xi\rangle=\|\xi\|^{2}=\|A\|\|\xi\|^{2}
, which together with the equality condition for the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that
|A|\xi=\xi
. Since
M_{\gamma A}=M_{A}
whenever
\gamma\neq 0
, it follows that

M_{A}=\{\xi\in\mathcal{H}:|A|\xi=\|A\|\xi\}=\ker(\|A\|\mathbf{1}-|A|)=\ker(\|A\|^{2}\mathbf{1}-A^{*}A).

In particular,
M_{A}
is a (possibly trivial) closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
.

Our next aim is to describe the pure states of a general compact
C^{*}
-algebra and their connection to strong BJ orthogonality (see Lemma
3.9
). Given a normalized vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, we let
\omega_{\xi}
be the induced vector state on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
; it is given by
a\mapsto\langle a\xi,\xi\rangle\in\mathbb{C}
. In
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
these are the only pure states. In general
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
of (not necessarily all) compact operators we show first that
\xi
must belong to a single block-component of
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an orthogonal family of projections with sum
I
, and let
\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Then,

\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})=\{\omega_{\xi}:\xi\in\mathcal{H},~\|\xi\|=1,~\text{$P_{\lambda}\xi=\xi$ for some $\lambda\in\Lambda$}\}.

Proof.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
. Since
\mathfrak{A}
is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, each pure state of
\mathfrak{A}
extends to a pure state of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
be a unit vector such that
\rho=\omega_{\xi}
, and let

\Lambda_{\xi}=\{\lambda\in\Lambda:P_{\lambda}\xi\neq 0\}.

It follows from
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}P_{\lambda}=I
that
\Lambda_{\xi}\neq\emptyset
. To show that
\Lambda_{\xi}
is a singleton, suppose to the contrary that
\Lambda_{\xi}
contains two distinct elements
\lambda,\mu
. In this case,
0\neq P_{\lambda}\xi\neq\xi
and
0<\|P_{\lambda}\xi\|<1
by
\|P_{\lambda}\xi\|^{2}+\|(I-P_{\lambda})\xi\|^{2}=1
. Set
\zeta=\|P_{\lambda}\xi\|^{-1}P_{\lambda}\xi
and
\eta=\|(I-P_{\lambda})\xi\|^{-1}(I-P_{\lambda})\xi
. Since
\mathfrak{A}\subset\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\|P_{\lambda}\xi\|^{2}\omega_{\zeta}+\|(I-P_{\lambda})\xi\|^{2}\omega_{\eta})(A)

\displaystyle=\langle AP_{\lambda}\xi,P_{\lambda}\xi\rangle+\langle A(I-P_{\lambda})\xi,(I-P_{\lambda})\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle P_{\lambda}A\xi,\xi\rangle+\langle(I-P_{\lambda})A\xi,\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\omega_{\xi}(A)

for each
A\in\mathfrak{A}
, which implies that
\|P_{\lambda}\xi\|^{2}\omega_{\zeta}=\|(I-P_{\lambda})\xi\|^{2}\omega_{\eta}
. However, this is impossible by
E_{\zeta}=P_{\lambda}E_{\zeta}P_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{A}
, where
E_{\zeta}
is a rank-one projection onto
\mathbb{C}\zeta
.
Hence,
\Lambda_{\xi}
is a singleton
\{\lambda\}
, and then
P_{\lambda}\xi=\xi
.

Conversely, we assume that
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
,
\|\xi\|=1
and
P_{\lambda}\xi=\xi
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Then,
E_{\xi}=P_{\lambda}E_{\xi}P_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{A}
. Suppose that
\rho,\tau\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A})
,
t\in(0,1)
and
\omega_{\xi}=(1-t)\rho+t\tau
. It follows from
\omega_{\xi}(E_{\xi})=1
that
\rho(E_{\xi})=\tau(E_{\xi})=1
, which, by Lemma
2.4
,
that
\rho(A)=\rho(E_{\xi}AE_{\xi})
and
\tau(A)=\tau(E_{\xi}AE_{\xi})
for each
A\in\mathfrak{A}
. Since
E_{\xi}AE_{\xi}=\omega_{\xi}(A)E_{\xi}
, we have
\omega_{\xi}=\rho=\tau
. Therefore,
\omega_{\xi}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
.
∎

Given a nonzero
x
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
, let

\mathcal{P}_{x}=\{\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A}):\rho(|x|)=\|x\|\}.

Notice that
\mathcal{P}_{x}=\mathcal{P}_{|x|}
. It was proven in
[
5
, Theorem 2.5]
that
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
if and only if there exists a state
\rho
such that
\rho(x^{\ast}x)=\|x\|^{2}
and
\rho(x^{\ast}yy^{\ast}x)=0
. We can now show that, to describe strong BJ orthogonality on compact
C^{*}
-algebras, pure states suffice. Remark that such a description is not possible with the usual BJ. For example, in
\mathfrak{A}=\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\oplus\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, where
\mathcal{H}
is two-dimensional, we have
(\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{1})\perp((-\mathbf{1})\oplus\mathbf{1})
, because there is a state
\phi=1/2(\omega_{\xi\oplus 0}+\omega_{0\oplus\xi})
with
\phi(\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{1})=1
and
\phi((-\mathbf{1})\oplus\mathbf{1})=0
; however, there does not exist a pure state
\rho
with
\rho((-\mathbf{1})\oplus\mathbf{1})=0
(compare with
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
).

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}
. Suppose that
x\neq 0
. Then,
x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y
if and only if there exists a
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{|x^{*}|}
such that
\rho(|y^{*}|)=0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x\perp_{BJ}^{s}y
. It may be assumed that
\mathfrak{A}
is unital (see Remark
1.4
) and
\|x\|=\|y\|=1
.
In view of Lemma
2.5
and Remark
1.3
,
|x^{*}|\perp_{BJ}|y^{*}|
, and then, by James’s result
[
34
, Theorem 2.1]
, there exists a (supporting) functional
\rho\in\mathfrak{A}^{*}
such that
\|\rho\|=\rho(|x^{*}|)=1
and
\rho(|y^{*}|)=0
. Define the functional
\rho^{*}
on
\mathfrak{A}
by
\rho^{*}(z)=\overline{\rho(z^{*})}
. It follows that
\rho^{*}\in\mathfrak{A}^{*}
,
\|\rho^{*}\|=\rho^{*}(|x^{*}|)=1
and
\rho^{*}(|y^{*}|)=0
. Replacing
\rho
with
2^{-1}(\rho+\rho^{*})
if necessary, we may assume that
\rho^{*}=\rho
. In this case, there exists a unique pair of positive functionals
(\rho^{+},\rho^{-})
on
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\rho=\rho^{+}-\rho-
and
\|\rho\|=\|\rho^{+}\|+\|\rho^{-}\|
; see, for example,
[
37
, Theorem 4.3.6]
. Since

1=\rho(|x^{*}|)=\rho^{+}(|x^{*}|)-\rho^{-}(|x^{*}|)\leq\rho^{+}(|x^{*}|)\leq\|\rho^{+}\|\leq\|\rho\|=1,

we obtain
\|\rho^{+}\|=\|\rho\|=1
and
\rho^{-}=0
. Hence,
\rho=\rho^{+}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A})
. Now, let
F=\{\tau\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A}):\tau(|x^{*}|)=1,~\tau(|y^{*}|)=0\}
. Then,
F
is a nonempty weakly
∗
compact face of
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A})
. By the Krein-Milman theorem, we have an extreme point
\tau
of
F
, which has the desired property.

Conversely, suppose that
\rho(|y^{*}|)=0
for some
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{|x^{*}|}
. (In fact,
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A})
is sufficient.) It follows from
|y^{*}|^{2}=|y^{*}|^{1/2}|y^{*}||y^{*}|^{1/2}\leq\|y\|\cdot|y^{*}|
that
\rho(|y^{*}|^{2})=0
. Then, by Lemma
2.4

|\rho(|x^{*}||y^{*}|\cdot z)|=\|x\||\rho(|y^{*}|z)|\leq\|x\|\rho(|z|^{2})^{1/2}\rho(|y^{*}|^{2})^{1/2}=0

for each
z\in\mathfrak{A}
. Thus,
|x^{*}|\perp_{BJ}^{s}|y^{*}|
and so
x\perp_{BJ}^{s}y
.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. Define the incoming and outgoing neighborhood of
x\in\mathfrak{A}
by

L_{x}^{s}=\{y\in\mathfrak{A}:y\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}x\}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad R_{x}^{s}=\{y\in\mathfrak{A}:x\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}y\}.

Define an equivalence relation on
\mathfrak{A}
by declaring that
x\sim y
if
L_{x}^{s}=L_{y}^{s}
and
R_{x}^{s}=R_{y}^{s}
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. If
\mathcal{P}_{x^{*}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{y^{*}}
, then
R_{x}^{s}\subset R_{y}^{s}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
z\in R_{x}^{s}
. Then, there exists a
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{|x^{*}|}=\mathcal{P}_{x^{*}}
such that
\rho(|z^{*}|)=0
. Since
\mathcal{P}_{x^{*}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{y^{\ast}}
, it follows that
y\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}z
. Hence,
R_{x}^{s}\subset R_{y}^{s}
.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an orthogonal family of projections with sum
I
, and let
\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Suppose that
A,B\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

R_{A}^{s}\subset R_{B}^{s}
.

(ii)

M_{|A^{*}|}\subset M_{|B^{*}|}
.

(iii)

\mathcal{P}_{|A^{*}|}\subset\mathcal{P}_{|B^{*}|}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5

R_{A}^{s}=R_{|A^{*}|}^{s}
and
R_{B}^{s}=R_{|B^{*}|}^{s}
, so it may be assumed that
A=|A^{*}|\geq 0
and
B=|B^{*}|\geq 0
. We may also assume that
\|A\|=\|B\|=1
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).
Suppose that
M_{A}\not\subset M_{B}
. Let
\xi
be a unit vector in
M_{A}\setminus M_{B}
, and let
\Lambda_{\xi}=\{\lambda\in\Lambda:P_{\lambda}\xi\neq 0\}
. It follows from

\xi=A\xi=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}AP_{\lambda}\xi=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\xi}}AP_{\lambda}\xi

that

1=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\xi}}\|AP_{\lambda}\xi\|^{2}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\xi}}\|P_{\lambda}\xi\|^{2}\leq\|\xi\|^{2}=1,

which implies that
\|AP_{\lambda}\xi\|=\|P_{\lambda}\xi\|
for each
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\xi}
, that is,
P_{\lambda}\xi\in M_{A}
for each
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\xi}
. Meanwhile, since
\xi\not\in M_{B}
, we obtain

1>\|B\xi\|^{2}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|BP_{\lambda}\xi\|^{2}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\xi}}\|BP_{\lambda}\xi\|^{2}.

From this, we infer that
\|BP_{\lambda}\xi\|<\|P_{\lambda}\xi\|
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\xi}
; otherwise,
BP_{\lambda}\xi=P_{\lambda}\xi
for each
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\xi}

and

B\xi=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}BP_{\lambda}\xi=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\xi}}BP_{\lambda}\xi=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\xi}}P_{\lambda}\xi=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}P_{\lambda}\xi=\xi,

which contradicts
\xi\not\in M_{B}
. Hence,
P_{\lambda_{0}}\xi\in M_{A}\setminus M_{B}
for some
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{\xi}
. Replacing
\xi
with
\|P_{\lambda_{0}}\xi\|^{-1}P_{\lambda_{0}}\xi
if necessary, we may assume that
P_{\lambda_{0}}\xi=\xi
.

Now, set
\xi=\zeta_{\mu_{0}}
, and let
(\zeta_{\mu})_{\mu\in M}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}
such that
\zeta_{\mu}\in\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}P_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
for each
\mu\in M
. Then, the net of projections
(E_{J})_{J}
directed by all finite subsets
J
of
M
with respect to inclusion relation forms an (increasing) approximate unit for
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, where
E_{J}
is the projection onto
\langle\{\zeta_{\mu}:\mu\in J\}\rangle
. Here, we note that
E_{J}=\sum_{\mu\in J}E_{\zeta_{\mu}}\in\mathfrak{A}
for each finite subset
J
of
M
. Let
J
be a finite subset of
M
such that
\mu_{0}\in J
and
\|B-E_{J}B\|<1-\|B\xi\|
,
where
\|B\xi\|^{2}=\langle B^{2}\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\langle B\xi,\xi\rangle<1
by
\xi\not\in M_{B}
. Since
\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle=1=\|A\|
,
E_{\xi}=E_{\{\mu_{0}\}}\leq E_{J}
and
\langle(E_{J}-E_{\xi})\xi,\xi\rangle=0
, it follows by Lemma
3.9
that
A\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}E_{J}-E_{\xi}
. Meanwhile, we note that
E_{\xi}B^{2}E_{\xi}=\omega_{\xi}(B^{2})E_{\xi}=\|B\xi\|^{2}E_{\xi}
, which implies that

\|E_{\xi}B\|=\|BE_{\xi}\|=\|E_{\xi}B^{2}E_{\xi}\|^{1/2}=\|B\xi\|.

Therefore,
B\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}E_{J}-E_{\xi}
by

\|B-(E_{J}-E_{\xi})B\|\leq\|B-E_{J}B\|+\|E_{\xi}B\|<1=\|B\|.

Hence,
E_{J}-E_{\xi}\in R_{A}^{s}\setminus R_{B}^{s}
which proves (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii). Let
\omega_{\xi}\in\mathcal{P}_{A}
. Then,
\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle=1
, which implies that
A\xi=\xi
. Hence,
\xi\in M_{A}\subset M_{B}
, in which case,
B\xi=\xi
and
\omega_{\xi}(B)=1
. This shows that
\omega_{\xi}\in\mathcal{P}_{B}
. Thus, (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii).

The implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) follows from Corollary
3.10
.
∎

Given a nonzero
x\in\mathfrak{A}
, let

\mathcal{N}_{x}=\{\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A}):\rho(|x|)=0\}.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. If
\mathcal{N}_{|x^{*}|}\subset\mathcal{N}_{|y^{*}|}
, then
L_{x}^{s}\subset L_{y}^{s}
.

Proof.

Let
z\in L_{x}^{s}\setminus\{0\}
. Then,
\rho(|x^{*}|)=0
for some
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{|z^{*}|}
. Since
\rho\in\mathcal{N}_{|x^{*}|}\subset\mathcal{N}_{|y^{*}|}
, it follows that
z\in L_{y}^{s}
.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an orthogonal family of projections with sum
I
, and let
\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Suppose that
A,B\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

L_{A}^{s}\subset L_{B}^{s}
.

(ii)

\ker|A^{*}|\subset\ker|B^{*}|
.

(iii)

\mathcal{N}_{|A^{*}|}\subset\mathcal{N}_{|B^{*}|}
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.11
, it may be assumed that
A=|A^{*}|\geq 0
,
B=|B^{*}|\geq 0
and
\|A\|=\|B\|=1
. Suppose that
\ker A\not\subset\ker B
. Let
\xi
be a unit vector in
\mathcal{H}
such that
\xi\in\ker A\setminus\ker B
. Since
A\xi=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}AP_{\lambda}\xi
and
B\xi=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}BP_{\lambda}\xi
, we infer that
AP_{\lambda}\xi=0
for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
but
BP_{\lambda_{0}}\neq 0
for some
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda
. Replacing
\xi
with
\|P_{\lambda_{0}}\xi\|^{-1}P_{\lambda_{0}}\xi
if necessary, we may assume that
P_{\lambda_{0}}\xi=\xi
, in which case,
E_{\xi}\in\mathfrak{A}
. By Lemma
3.9
,

E_{\xi}\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}A
. Meanwhile, if
\omega_{\zeta}\in\mathcal{P}_{E_{\xi}}
, then
\langle E_{\xi}\zeta,\zeta\rangle=1
, which implies that
\zeta=E_{\xi}\zeta=\langle\zeta,\xi\rangle\xi
and
|\langle\zeta,\xi\rangle|=1
. Hence,

\omega_{\zeta}(B)=\omega_{\xi}(B)\geq\omega_{\xi}(B^{2})=\|B\xi\|^{2}>0

by
\xi\not\in\ker B
. This means
E_{\xi}\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}B
, that is,
E_{\xi}\in L_{A}^{s}\setminus L_{B}^{s}
. Therefore, (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).

Next, suppose that
\ker A\subset\ker B
. Let
\xi
be a unit vector in
\mathcal{H}
such that
\omega_{\xi}\in\mathcal{N}_{A}
. Then, we obtain
\|A^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}=\omega_{\xi}(A)=0
and
A\xi=0
, which implies that
\xi\in\ker A\subset\ker B
. It follows that
\omega_{\xi}(B)=0
. Thus,
\mathcal{N}_{A}\subset\mathcal{N}_{B}
, and (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii).

The implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) follows from Lemma
3.12
.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an orthogonal family of projections with sum
I
, and let

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an orthogonal family of projections with sum
I
, and let
\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Suppose that
A\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. Then,
\|A\|^{-1}|A^{*}|
is a projection if and only if
B\in\mathfrak{A}
and
R_{A}^{s}=R_{B}^{s}
imply
L_{A}^{s}\supset L_{B}^{s}
.

Proof.

It may be assumed that
A=|A^{\ast}|\geq 0
and
\|A\|=1
. Suppose that
A
is a projection. In this case, we have
\overline{A(\mathcal{H})}=A(\mathcal{H})=M_{A}
. Let
B\in\mathfrak{A}
be such that
R_{A}^{s}=R_{B}^{s}
. Then, we obtain
B\neq 0
and, by Lemma
3.11
,
\overline{A(\mathcal{H})}=M_{A}=M_{|B^{*}|}\subset\overline{|B^{*}|(\mathcal{H})}
, which implies that
L_{A}^{s}\supset L_{B}^{s}
.

Conversely, suppose that
B\in\mathfrak{A}
and
R_{A}^{s}=R_{B}^{s}
imply
L_{A}^{s}\supset L_{B}^{s}
. We infer that
M_{A}
is finite-dimensional by
A\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
E
be the projection onto
M_{A}
. We note that
E=\chi_{\{1\}}(A)\in\mathfrak{A}
and, from
M_{A}=E(\mathcal{H})=M_{E}
and Lemma
3.11
, that
R_{A}^{s}=R_{E}^{s}
. Hence, by the assumptions,
L_{A}^{s}\supset L_{E}^{s}
which gives
M_{A}\subset A(\mathcal{H})\subset\overline{A(\mathcal{H})}\subset E(\mathcal{H})=M_{A}
. Therefore,
M_{A}=A(\mathcal{H})
and
A(A\xi)=A\xi
for each
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. Combining this with the assumption that

is a projection.
∎

For each pair
\xi,\zeta\in\mathcal{H}
, let
\xi\otimes\zeta
be a rank-one operator defined by
(\xi\otimes\zeta)\eta=\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle\xi
for each
\eta\in\mathcal{H}

(\xi\otimes\zeta)^{*}=\zeta\otimes\xi

(\xi_{1}\otimes\zeta_{1})(\xi_{2}\otimes\zeta_{2})=\langle\zeta_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle(\xi_{1}\otimes\zeta_{2})=\xi_{1}\otimes((\zeta_{2}\otimes\zeta_{1})\xi_{2}).

Remark 3.16
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an orthogonal family of projections with sum
I
, and let
\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Suppose that
\xi,\zeta\in\mathcal{H}
. Then,
\xi\otimes\zeta\in\mathfrak{A}
if and only if
\xi,\zeta\in P_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda
. To show this, suppose that
\xi\otimes\zeta\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. Let
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
be such that
P_{\lambda}\xi\neq 0
and
P_{\mu}\zeta\neq 0
. Since
\xi\otimes\zeta\in\mathfrak{A}
and

0\neq P_{\lambda}\xi\otimes P_{\mu}\zeta=P_{\lambda}(\xi\otimes\zeta)P_{\mu}=P_{\lambda}P_{\mu}(\xi\otimes\zeta),

it follows that
\lambda=\mu
. Hence,
P_{\lambda}\xi=\xi
and
P_{\lambda}\zeta=\zeta
for a unique
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Conversely, if
\xi,\zeta\in P_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then
\xi\otimes\zeta=P_{\lambda}(\xi\otimes\zeta)P_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})\subseteq\mathfrak{A}
.

Remark 3.17
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an orthogonal family of projections with sum
I
, and let
\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Suppose that
E\in\mathfrak{A}
is a projection. Then, there exists an orthogonal family of rank-one projections
E_{\xi_{1}},\ldots,E_{\xi_{n}}\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
E=\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{\xi_{n}}
. To show this, we note that
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits E=n\in\mathbb{N}
by
E\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
\Lambda_{E}=\{\lambda\in\Lambda:EP_{\lambda}\neq 0\}
, and let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{E}
. Since
EP_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}E
, it follows that
EP_{\lambda}
is a nonzero projection. Moreover, if
\{\xi_{\lambda,1},\ldots,\xi_{\lambda,n_{\lambda}}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
P_{\lambda}E(\mathcal{H})
, then

EP_{\lambda}=PE_{\lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\lambda}}E_{\xi_{\lambda,j}}

and
E_{\xi_{\lambda,j}}=\xi_{\lambda,j}\otimes\xi_{\lambda,j}\in\mathfrak{A}
by
\xi_{\lambda,j}\in P_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
. Combining this with the finiteness of
\Lambda_{E}
, we obtain

E=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{E}}EP_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{E}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\lambda}}E_{\xi_{\lambda,j}}

as desired.

C^{*}
-algebras in terms on strong BJ orthogonality (it equals the maximal
n
for which one can find the ascending sequence of length
n
, given in the statement of lemma).

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an orthogonal family of projections with sum
I
, and let
\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Suppose that
A\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
and
n\geq 1
. Then,
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits A\geq n
if and only if there exist
A_{0},\ldots,A_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
, with
A_{0}=A
, such that
L_{A_{0}}^{s}\subsetneq L_{A_{1}}^{s}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq L_{A_{n-1}}^{s}
.

Proof.

Since
\overline{A(\mathcal{H})}=\overline{|A^{*}|(\mathcal{H})}
and
L_{A}^{s}=L_{|A^{*}|}^{s}
, it may be assumed that
A\geq 0
. Suppose that
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits A\geq n
. Considering the functional calculus for
A
, we infer that there exist a non-increasing sequence of nonnegative numbers
(\lambda_{m})_{m}
and an orthogonal sequence of rank-one projections
(E_{m})_{m}
in
\mathfrak{A}
such that
A=\sum_{m}\lambda_{m}E_{m}
, where the series converges in the norm topology. We note that
\lambda_{n}>0
by
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits A\geq n
. Let
A_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-k}E_{j}\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. Then, we have

\overline{A(\mathcal{H})}\supsetneq\overline{A_{1}(\mathcal{H})}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq\overline{A_{n-1}(\mathcal{H})},

which, by Remark
3.14
, implies that
L_{A}^{s}\subsetneq L_{A_{1}}^{s}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq L_{A_{n-1}}^{s}
.

Conversely, if
L_{A}^{s}\subsetneq L_{A_{1}}^{s}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq L_{A_{n-1}}^{s}
for
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
, then

\overline{A(\mathcal{H})}\supsetneq\overline{A_{1}(\mathcal{H})}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq\overline{A_{n-1}(\mathcal{H})}\neq\{0\}.

Therefore,
\dim\overline{A(\mathcal{H})}\geq n
, which ensures
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits A\geq n
.
∎

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an orthogonal family of projections with sum
I
, let
\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, and

\Lambda_{A}=\{\lambda\in\Lambda:AP_{\lambda}\neq 0\}

for each
A\in\mathfrak{A}
. Suppose that
A,B\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. Then,
\Lambda_{A}\cap\Lambda_{B}\neq\emptyset
if and only if there exists a rank-one operator
C\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
C\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}A
and
C\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}B
.

Proof.

It may be assumed that
A\geq 0
and
B\geq 0
. Suppose that
\Lambda_{A}\cap\Lambda_{B}\neq\emptyset
. Let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{A}\cap\Lambda_{B}
, and let
\xi
be a unit vector in
P_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
such that
A\xi\neq 0
. If
B\xi\neq 0
, then we have
E_{\xi}\in\mathfrak{A}
,
E_{\xi}\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}A
and
E_{\xi}\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}B
by
\mathcal{P}_{E_{\xi}}=\{\omega_{\xi}\}
,
\omega_{\xi}(A)=\|A^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}>0
and
\omega_{\xi}(B)=\|B^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}>0
(c.f., Lemma
3.9
). If
B\xi=0
, then
BE_{\xi}=0
and
BP_{\lambda}(I-E_{\xi})=B(P_{\lambda}-E_{\xi})=BP_{\lambda}\neq 0
. In this case, let
\zeta
be a unit vector in
P_{\lambda}(I-E_{\xi})(\mathcal{H})=(I-E_{\xi})P_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
such that
B\zeta\neq 0
. Replacing
\zeta
with
-\zeta
if necessary, we may assume that
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\langle A\xi,\zeta\rangle\geq 0
. Set
\eta=2^{-1/2}(\xi+\zeta)
. Since

\omega_{\eta}(A)=\langle A\eta,\eta\rangle=2^{-1}(\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\langle A\xi,\zeta\rangle+\langle A\zeta,\zeta\rangle)>0

and

\omega_{\eta}(B)=\langle B\eta,\eta\rangle=2^{-1}\langle B\zeta,\zeta\rangle>0,

it follows that
E_{\eta}\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}A
and
E_{\eta}\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}B
.

For the converse, suppose that
\Lambda_{A}\cap\Lambda_{B}=\emptyset
, and that
C
is a rank-one element of
\mathfrak{A}
. It may be assumed that
\|C\|=1
. Let
\xi,\zeta
be unit vectors in
\mathcal{H}
such that
C=\xi\otimes\zeta
. Recall that
P_{\lambda}\xi=\xi
and
P_{\lambda}\zeta=\zeta
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda
. If
C\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}A
, then
|C^{*}|\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}A
and
\omega_{\xi}(A)\neq 0
by
|C^{*}|=E_{\xi}
, which implies that
AP_{\lambda}\xi=A\xi\neq 0
. Hence,
\lambda\in\Lambda_{A}
. Combining this with
\Lambda_{A}\cap\Lambda_{B}=\emptyset
, we obtain
BP_{\lambda}=0
and
B\xi=BP_{\lambda}\xi=0
, that is,
|C^{*}|\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}B
. This means that there is no rank-one operator
C\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
C\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}A
and
C\not\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}B
.
∎

3.3.
Characterizing compact
C^{*}
-algebras

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. Then,
\mathcal{C}
is called an
R
-chain
if
R_{x}^{s}\subsetneq R_{y}^{s}
or
R_{x}^{s}\supsetneq R_{y}^{s}
whenever
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
and
x\neq y
. Define the set
\mathfrak{F}_{x}
by

\mathfrak{F}_{x}=\{\mathcal{C}\subset\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}:\text{$\mathcal{C}$ is an $R$-chain with $R_{y}^{s}\subset R_{x}^{s}$ for each $y\in\mathcal{C}$}\}.

It is important to know if
\mathfrak{F}_{x}
contains an infinite set or not.

Lemma 3.20
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, let
x\in\mathfrak{A}
be positive, and let
(x_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of positive elements of
\mathfrak{A}
. Suppose that
\|x\|=\|x_{n}\|
and
xx_{n}=x_{n}
for each
n
, and that
x_{m}x_{n}=0
whenever
m\neq n
. Then,
\{y_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\in\mathfrak{F}_{x}
, where
y_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}
.

Proof.

It may be assumed that
\|x\|=1
. We note that
\|y_{n}\|=1
for each
n
.
Let
z\in R_{y_{n}}^{s}
. It follows from
xy_{n}=y_{n}
that

1=\|y_{n}\|\leq\|y_{n}+zwy_{n}\|=\|xy_{n}+zwy_{n}\|\leq\|x+zw\|

for each
w\in\mathfrak{A}
, which implies that
z\in R_{x}^{s}
. Hence,
R_{y_{n}}^{s}\subset R_{x}^{s}
for each
n
. Meanwhile, since
z\in R_{y_{n}}^{s}
, Lemma
3.9
generates a pure state
\rho
of
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\rho(y_{n})=1
and
\rho(|z^{*}|)=0
. It follows from Lemma
2.4
that
\rho(y_{n}^{2})=\|y_{n}\|\rho(y_{n})=1
. and
z\in R_{y_{n}^{2}}^{s}
again by Lemma
3.9
. Combining this with
y_{n}^{2}=y_{n+1}y_{n}
, we have

1=\|y_{n}^{2}\|\leq\|y_{n}^{2}+zwy_{n}\|=\|y_{n+1}y_{n}+zwy_{n}\|\leq\|y_{n+1}+zw\|

for each
w\in\mathfrak{A}
, that is,
z\in R_{y_{n+1}}^{s}
. Finally, we note that

\|y_{n+1}+y_{n}w\|\geq\|x_{n+1}(y_{n+1}+y_{n}w)\|=\|x_{n+1}^{2}\|=1=\|y_{n+1}\|

for each
w\in\mathfrak{A}
. This shows that
y_{n}\in R_{y_{n+1}}^{s}\setminus R_{y_{n}}^{s}
. Therefore,
R_{y_{n}}^{s}\subsetneq R_{y_{n+1}}^{s}
for each
n
, and
\{y_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\in\mathfrak{F}_{x}
.
∎

Theorem 3.21
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. Then,
\mathfrak{A}
is a compact
C^{*}
-algebra if and only if it satisfies the following conditions for each
x\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
:

(i)

There exists a nonzero projection
e\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
e\mathfrak{A}e=\mathbb{C}e
and
R_{e}^{s}\subset R_{x}^{s}
.

(ii)

Every element of
\mathfrak{F}_{x}
is a finite set.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{A}
is a compact
C^{*}
-algebra. In this case, it may be assumed that
\mathfrak{A}
acts on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and there exists an orthogonal family of projections
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
with sum
I
such that
\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
A\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
, and let
\lambda\in\Lambda
be such that
\|AP_{\lambda}\|=\|A\|
. Since
|A^{*}|P_{\lambda}
can be viewed as a compact operator on
P_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
, we have a unit vector
\xi\in P_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
such that
|A^{*}|\xi=\||A^{*}|P_{\lambda}\|\xi=\|A\|\xi
. It follows from
E_{\xi}=\xi\otimes\xi\in\mathfrak{A}
and
M_{E_{\xi}}=\mathbb{C}\xi\subset M_{|A^{*}|}
that
R_{E_{\xi}}^{s}\subset R_{|A^{*}|}^{s}=R_{A}^{s}
by Lemma
3.11
. Also,
E_{\xi}
is a rank-one projection and hence
E_{\xi}\mathfrak{A}E_{\xi}=\mathbb{C}E_{\xi}
.

Next, let
\mathcal{C}
be an element of
\mathfrak{F}_{A}
. By Lemma
3.11
, we have
M_{|B^{*}|}\subset M_{|A^{*}|}
for each
B\in\mathcal{C}
, while
M_{|A^{*}|}
is finite-dimensional. Set
\dim M_{|A^{*}|}=n
. We claim that
\mathcal{C}
contains at most
n
elements. Indeed, if
\mathcal{C}
contains mutually distinct elements
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n+1}
, then
\dim M_{|B_{i}^{*}|}=\dim M_{|B_{j}^{*}|}
for some
i,j
with
i\neq j
. However, in this case, we have neither
R_{B_{i}}^{s}\subsetneq R_{B_{j}}^{s}
nor
R_{B_{i}}^{s}\supsetneq R_{B_{j}}^{s}
, which contradicts the choice of
\mathcal{C}
. This proves the claim.

Conversely, we assume that
\mathfrak{A}
satisfies the conditions (i) and (ii). It may be assumed that
\mathfrak{A}
acts on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let
A\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
. By (i), we have
R_{E}^{s}\subset R_{A}^{s}=R_{|A^{*}|}^{s}
for some nonzero projection
E\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
E\mathfrak{A}E=\mathbb{C}E
. Then, it follows from
[
5
, Theorem 2.5(b)]
that
(I-E)A\in R_{E}^{s}\subset R_{A}^{s}
, which implies that

\|A\|\geq\|EA\|=\|A-(I-E)A\|=\lim_{\lambda}\|A-(I-E)AU_{\lambda}\|\geq\|A\|.

where
(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{A}
is an approximate identity.
Hence,
\|EA(EA)^{*}\|=\|EA\|^{2}=\|A\|^{2}
, so
EAA^{*}E=\|A\|^{2}E
and
\|A^{*}\xi\|=\||A^{*}|\xi\|=\|A\|=\|A^{*}\|
whenever
\xi\in E(\mathcal{H})
and
\|\xi\|=1
. In particular, we obtain

|A^{*}|E=\|A\|E
.

Let
A\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
be positive and
\|A\|=1
. By the preceding paragraph, there exists a nonzero projection
E\in\mathfrak{A}
such that

(3)

E\mathfrak{A}E=\mathbb{C}E\quad\hbox{ and }\quad AE=E.

We claim that there exists a finite family of pairwise orthogonal nonzero projections
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
E_{j}\mathfrak{A}E_{j}=\mathbb{C}E_{j}
and
AE_{j}=E_{j}
for each
j
and
\|(I-\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j})A\|<\|A\|
. To show this, suppose to the contrary that there is no such a family. Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}
be orthogonal family of projections such that
E_{j}\mathfrak{A}E_{j}=\mathbb{C}E_{j}
and
AE_{j}=E_{j}
for each
j
. Since
\|(I-\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j})A\|=\|A\|=1
and
AE_{j}=E_{j}=E_{j}A
, it follows that

\left(I-\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j}\right)A=A^{1/2}\left(I-\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j}\right)A^{1/2}\geq 0,

which, by condition (i) and identity (
3
), ensures the existence of a nonzero projection
E_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
E_{n+1}\mathfrak{A}E_{n+1}=\mathbb{C}E_{n+1}
and
(I-\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j})AE_{n+1}=E_{n+1}
. In particular, we derive

E_{i}E_{n+1}=E_{i}\left(I-\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j}\right)AE_{n+1}=0

for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, that is,
\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{n},E_{n+1}\}
is an orthogonal family of projections. As such,
E_{n+1}A=E_{n+1}\cdot(I-\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j})A=((I-\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j})A\cdot E_{n+1})^{*}=E_{n+1}
Therefore, by an induction, we can construct an orthogonal sequence of nonzero projections
(E_{n})_{n}
such that
E_{n}\mathfrak{A}E_{n}=\mathbb{C}E_{n}
and
AE_{n}=E_{n}
for each
n
. However, then
\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\in\mathfrak{F}_{A}
by Lemma
3.20
, which contradicts (ii). Thus, we must have a finite orthogonal family of nonzero projections
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
E_{j}\mathfrak{A}E_{j}=\mathbb{C}E_{j}
and
AE_{j}=E_{j}
for each
j
and
\|(I-\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j})A\|<\|A\|
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
be the abelian
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{A}
generated by
\{A,E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\}
. It may be assumed that
\mathfrak{A}_{0}=C_{0}(K)
for some locally compact Hausdorff space

. Let
f
and

be the elements of

corresponding to

and

. Since

, by Urysohn’s lemma, we infer that

is the characteristic function for a singleton

is not isolated in

. However, then, we have

. Hence,

is isolated in
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(f)
.

We have proved the following for each positive nonzero element
A\in\mathfrak{A}
:

(a)

There exists a finite orthogonal family of nonzero projections
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
E_{j}\mathfrak{A}E_{j}=\mathbb{C}E_{j}
and
AE_{j}=\|A\|E_{j}
for each
j
and
\|(I-\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j})A\|<\|A\|
.

(b)

\|A\|
is isolated in
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(A)
.

Let
A
be a nonzero positive element of
\mathfrak{A}
. Suppose that there exists a nonzero accumulation point

. Then, there exists a strictly monotone sequence
(t_{n})_{n}\subset(0,\|A\|)
that converges to

. If
(t_{n})_{n}
is strictly increasing, then we choose an element
f_{n}\in C(\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(A))
such that

,

\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits f_{n}\subset\left(\frac{t_{n-1}+t_{n}}{2},\frac{t_{n}+t_{n+1}}{2}\right),

where

. We note that
f_{n}(0)=0
for each
n
and
f_{m}f_{n}=0
whenever

. If

is strictly decreasing, the similar construction applies. We can also construct a function

,

and

. However, Lemma

prevents this construction because
\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{n}(A):n\in\mathbb{N}\}\in\mathfrak{F}_{f(A)}
which contradicts (ii). Therefore,

does not contain any nonzero accumulation point. From this,

coincides with the closed linear span of its projections.

Finally, let
\pi_{\rm ra}=\sum_{\lambda}\oplus\pi_{\lambda}\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{B}(\bigoplus_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})
(here, following
[

is a bijection on
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Now, let
A\in C(M,N)
. Then,
M=M_{|A^{*}|}
,
N=\ker|A^{*}|
and
\Phi(A)=f_{M,N}(A)\in C(M,N)
. It follows that
M_{|\Phi(A)^{*}|}=M_{|A^{*}|}
and

\ker|\Phi(A)^{*}|=\ker|A^{*}|.

Thus, by Theorem
3.27
,
\Phi
is preserves strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality in both directions. Conversely, if
\dim\mathcal{H}\geq 3
, then, as follows from Remark
3.28
, every strong BJ isomorphhism
\Phi\colon\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
is of the above form when composed with a suitable unitary or anti-unitary
U
. We remark that, in particular, such
\Phi
are typically not linear at all in general.

3.6.
The linear case

Lemma 3.31
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an orthogonal family of projections with sum
I
, let
\mathfrak{A}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Suppose a linear
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}
is a strong BJ isomorphism such that
\Phi(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}}\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}}\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda}
for each

\Lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda
. Then, there exists a positive number
\kappa
such that
\|\Phi(A)\|=\kappa
whenever
A\in\mathfrak{A}
is a rank-one operator with
\|A\|=1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Lambda
is not a singleton. Let
A
and
B
be rank-one operators with
\|A\|=\|B\|=1
. In this case,
|A^{*}|
and
|B^{*}|
are rank-one projections. If
\Lambda_{A}\neq\Lambda_{B}
, then
AB^{*}=|A^{*}||B^{*}|=0
, which implies that
|(A+B)^{*}|=|A^{*}|+|B^{*}|
is a rank-two projection. By Lemmas
3.15
and
3.18
,
|(\Phi(A)+\Phi(B))^{*}|
is a scalar multiple of a rank-two projection. Meanwhile,
|\Phi(A)^{*}|
and
|\Phi(B)^{*}|
are also rank-one by Lemma
3.18
, and
\Lambda_{\Phi(A)}\neq\Lambda_{\Phi(B)}
by the assumption
\Phi(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}}\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}}\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda}
.
Hence,
|(\Phi(A)+\Phi(B))^{*}|=|\Phi(A)^{*}|+|\Phi(B)^{*}|
and
|\Phi(A)^{*}||\Phi(B)^{*}|=0
, which ensures that
\|\Phi(A)\|=\|\Phi(B)\|
.

Now, fix an index
\lambda_{0}
and a rank-one operator
A_{0}
with
\|A_{0}\|=1
and
\Lambda_{A_{0}}=\{\lambda_{0}\}
. Let
A
be a rank-one operator with
\|A\|=1
. If
\Lambda_{A}\neq\{\lambda_{0}\}
, then
\|\Phi(A)\|=\|\Phi(A_{0})\|
by the preceding paragraph. Suppose that
\Lambda_{A}=\{\lambda_{0}\}
. Since
\Lambda
contains an element
\lambda
other than
\lambda_{0}
, there exists a rank-one projection
E\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
\Lambda_{E}=\{\lambda\}\neq\{\lambda_{0}\}
. Again by the preceding paragraph, we derive
\|A\|=\|E\|=\|A_{0}\|
.

Next, suppose that
\Lambda
is a singleton, that is,
\mathfrak{A}=\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. If
\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty
, then
\mathfrak{A}=\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a (finite-dimensional) von Neumann algebra and the result follows from
[
42
, Theorem 5.7]
. In the sequel we assume
\dim\mathcal{H}=\infty
.
Take a pair of unit vectors
\xi,\zeta\in\mathcal{H}
and let
E_{\xi},E_{\zeta}
be the associated rank-one projections. Suppose that
\xi\perp\zeta
. Then,
E_{\xi}E_{\zeta}=0
, so

E_{\xi}\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}E_{\zeta}
and
|E_{\xi}+E_{\zeta}|=|E_{\xi}|+|E_{\zeta}|
. By Lemmas
3.15
,
3.18
, and equivalence (
4
),
\Phi(E_{\xi})
and
\Phi(E_{\zeta})
are scalar multiples of orthogonal rank-one projections and their sum,
\Phi(E_{\xi}+E_{\zeta})=\Phi(E_{\xi})+\Phi(E_{\zeta})
is also a (rank-two) projection. As above we deduce that
\|\Phi(E_{\xi})\|=\|\Phi(E_{\zeta})\|
.

Now, we omit the assumption that
\xi\perp\zeta
. Since
\dim\mathcal{H}\geq 3
, there exists a unit vector
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\eta\perp\xi
and
\eta\perp\zeta
. From the preceding paragraph, it follows that
\|\Phi(E_{\xi})\|=\|\Phi(E_{\eta})\|=\|\Phi(E_{\zeta})\|
. Set
\kappa=\|\Phi(E_{\xi_{0}})\|
for an arbitrary unit
\xi_{0}
. This does not depend on the choice of
\xi_{0}
.

Next, we consider an arbitrary rank-one operator
A=\xi\otimes\zeta
, where
\xi,\zeta
are unit vectors. Let
\eta
be a unit vector in
\mathcal{H}
such that
\xi\perp\eta
and
\zeta\perp\eta
. Since

\displaystyle(A+E_{\eta})(A+E_{\eta})^{*}

\displaystyle=(\xi\otimes\zeta+\eta\otimes\eta)(\zeta\otimes\xi+\eta\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle=\xi\otimes\xi+\langle\zeta,\eta\rangle(\xi\otimes\eta)+\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle(\eta\otimes\xi)+\eta\otimes\eta

\displaystyle=E_{\xi}+E_{\eta},

it follows that
|\Phi(A+E_{\eta})^{*}|
is a scalar multiple of a rank-two projection. Write
\Phi(A)=\xi_{1}\otimes\zeta_{1}
and
\Phi(E_{\eta})=\xi_{2}\otimes\zeta_{2}
. Since
\Phi(A)\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}\Phi(E_{\eta})
by
A\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}E_{\eta}
, we have
|\Phi(A)^{*}|\perp_{BJ}|\Phi(E_{\eta})^{*}|
, that is,
\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{1}\perp_{BJ}\xi_{2}\otimes\xi_{2}
and
\xi_{1}\perp\xi_{2}
. Then, as in the first paragraph, we can show that
\|\xi_{1}\|=\|\xi_{2}\|
. Thus,
\|\Phi(A)\|=\|\xi_{1}\|=\|\xi_{2}\|=\|\Phi(E_{\eta})\|=\kappa
.
∎

Lemma 3.32
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
be compact
C^{*}
-algebras, and let
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}
be a linear strong BJ isomorphism.
Then,
\Phi
is a scalar multiple of an isometry.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.24
, we may assume that
\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{B}=\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
and
\Phi(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}}\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}}\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda}
for each
\lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda
, where
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is an orthogonal family of projections with sum
I
. In this case, Lemma
3.31
generates a positive number
\kappa
such that
\|\Phi(A)\|=\kappa
whenever
A\in\mathfrak{A}
is a rank-one operator with
\|A\|=1
. Let
A
be an arbitrary norm-one element of
\mathfrak{A}
, and let
A=|A^{*}|U
be the polar decomposition for
A
.
The double commutant theorem ensures that
U\in\{P_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}^{\prime}
.
Since
\|A\|=\sup_{\lambda}\|AP_{\lambda}\|
and
\sum_{\lambda}AP_{\lambda}
converges to (a compact operator)
A
in the norm topology, we can find a
\lambda\in\Lambda
such that
\|AP_{\lambda}\|=\|A\|=1
.
Let
\xi
be a unit vector in
M_{|A^{*}P_{\lambda}|}=M_{|A^{*}|P_{\lambda}}\subset P_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})\cap|A^{*}|(\mathcal{H})
. We note that
U
is a partial isometry from
\overline{|A|(\mathcal{H})}
onto
\overline{A(\mathcal{H})}=\overline{|A^{*}|(\mathcal{H})}
. Set
E
be the rank-one projection onto
\mathbb{C}\xi
. We note that
E=\xi\otimes\xi\in\mathfrak{A}
and
EU\in\mathfrak{A}
. This is because
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
is an ideal of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Moreover, we get
\|EU\|=1
and
EU\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}A-EU
. Indeed, since
UU^{\ast}
is a projection onto
\overline{|A^{*}|(\mathcal{H})}\ni\xi
we have
(EU)(EU)^{*}=EUU^{*}E=E
and, since
AU^{*}=|A^{*}|
, further

(A-EU)(A-EU)^{*}=(|A^{*}|-E)^{2}.

From this and
(|A^{*}|-E)\xi=\xi-\xi=0
, we obtain
|(EU)^{*}|\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}|(A-EU)^{*}|
and
EU\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}A-EU
. Meanwhile, it follows from
M_{|(EU)^{*}|}=E(\mathcal{H})\subset M_{|A^{*}|}
that
R_{EU}^{s}\subset R_{A}^{s}
by Lemma
3.11
.

Now, we know that
\Phi(EU)
is rank-one,
\Phi(EU)\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}\Phi(A-EU)
and
R_{\Phi(EU)}\subset R_{\Phi(A)}
. Set
\Phi(EU)=\zeta\otimes\eta
, where
\|\zeta\|=1
. Since
|\Phi(EU)^{*}|=\zeta\otimes\zeta
, again by Lemma
3.11
, we obtain
\mathbb{C}\zeta=M_{|\Phi(EU)^{*}|}\subset M_{|\Phi(A)^{*}|}
. Since
|\Phi(EU)^{*}|\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}|\Phi(A-EU)^{*}|
, it follows that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\omega_{\zeta}(\Phi(A-EU)\Phi(A-EU)^{*})

\displaystyle=\omega_{\zeta}((\Phi(A)-\zeta\otimes\eta)(\Phi(A)^{*}-\eta\otimes\zeta))

\displaystyle=\omega_{\zeta}(\Phi(A)\Phi(A)^{*}-\Phi(A)(\eta\otimes\zeta)-(\zeta\otimes\eta)\Phi(A)^{*}+\|\eta\|^{2}\zeta\otimes\zeta)

\displaystyle=\|\Phi(A)^{*}\zeta\|^{2}-\langle\eta,\Phi(A)^{*}\zeta\rangle-\langle\Phi(A)^{*}\zeta,\eta\rangle+\|\eta\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi(A)^{*}\zeta-\eta,\Phi(A)^{*}\zeta-\eta\rangle.

Therefore,
\Phi(A^{*})\zeta=\eta
and

\|\Phi(A)^{*}\|^{2}=\langle|\Phi(A)^{*}|\zeta,|\Phi(A)^{*}|\zeta\rangle=\|\Phi(A)^{*}\zeta\|^{2}=\|\eta\|^{2}

by
\zeta\in M_{|\Phi(A)^{*}|}
, which implies that

\|\Phi(A)\|=\|\Phi(A)^{*}\|=\|\eta\|=\|\Phi(EU)\|=\kappa,

where
\kappa
is the value introduced in Lemma
3.31
. This proves that
\kappa^{-1}\Phi
is an isometry.
∎

4.
Concluding Remarks

In this section we will translate previous results into operator-theoretic language. We will also improve them in case a compact
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
contains blocks of small size. We always view a compact
C^{\ast}
-algebra as a
c_{0}
-direct sum

\mathfrak{A}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})

and embedded into
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
where
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
. Recall that every
A\in\mathfrak{A}
has a singular value decomposition (SVD for short)

A=\sum\sigma_{i}(A)\xi_{i}\otimes\zeta_{i};\qquad\|A\|=\sigma_{1}(A)\geq\sigma_{2}(A)\geq\dots\geq 0,

where
(\xi_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
and
(\zeta_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
are two orthonormal bases of
\mathcal{H}
and where
\sigma_{i}(A)
converge to
0
; moreover, each pair
(\xi_{i},\zeta_{i})
belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
for some index
\lambda
(c.f. Remark
3.16
). Also,
M_{A}=\mathrm{span}\{\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{k}\}
where
\sigma_{1}(A)=\dots=\sigma_{k}(A)>\sigma_{k+1}(A)
and
|A^{\ast}|=\sum\sigma_{i}(A)\xi_{i}\otimes\xi_{i}
, hence
M_{|A^{\ast}|}=AM_{A}
.

We start with characterization of strong BJ orthogonality between compact operators.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathfrak{A}=\bigoplus\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})
. Then,
A\mathrel{\perp_{BJ}^{s}}B
if and only if there exists an index
\lambda
and a unit vector
\zeta\in\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
such that
\|A\zeta\|=\|A\|
and
B^{\ast}A\zeta=0
.

Proof.

\|A\zeta\|=\|A\|
if and only if
\xi:=A\zeta\in AM_{A}=M_{|A^{\ast}|}
or, equivalently,
\omega_{\zeta}(|A^{\ast}|)=\langle|A^{\ast}|\xi,\xi\rangle=\|A\|
. The rest follows from Lemmas
3.8
and
3.9
.
∎

Next is a restatement (and extension to all Hilbert spaces) of Theorem
3.27
and Remark
3.28
. Let
R(A):=A(\mathcal{H})
be the range of operator
A
. We say
\Phi\colon\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
has property
{\mathcal{P}}
if

BM_{B}=AM_{A}\ \text{and}\ \overline{R(B)}=\overline{R(A)},\ \text{where}\ B=\Phi(A).

Observe that this is equivalent to the two equalities in (
5
). We also let
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}):=\{[x]:x\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\{0\}\}
be the projective space over
\mathcal{H}
, and declare projective points
[x],[y]
orthogonal if
\langle x,y\rangle=0
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Phi\colon\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
be a strong BJ isomorphism.

(i)

If
\dim\mathcal{H}\geq 3
, then there exists a unitary or anti-unitary operator
U\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
such that
U^{-1}\circ\Phi
has property
\mathcal{P}
.

(ii)

If
\dim\mathcal{H}=2
, then there exist a bijection
\phi\colon\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})
which maps orthogonal projective points onto orthogonal projective points such that

BM_{B}=\phi(AM_{A})\quad\hbox{ and }\quad R(B)=\phi(R(A)),\ \text{where}\ B=\Phi(A).

(iii)

If
\dim\mathcal{H}=1
, then any bijection which fixes
0
is a strong BJ isomorphism on
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
.

Sketch of the proof.

The arguments from the proof of Theorem
3.27
work regardless of the dimension of
\mathcal{H}
and show that
\Phi
induces a bijective orthogonality-preserving map
\phi
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})
(where, with a slight abuse, we identified rank-one projection
\xi\otimes\xi\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
with its range,
\mathbb{C}\xi\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})
). If
\dim\mathcal{H}\leq 2
, Uhlhorn’s theorem is no longer available and we simply replace
M_{|\Phi(E)^{\ast}|}=UM_{E}
and
\ker|\Phi(E)^{\ast}|=U(\ker E)
,
E=\xi\otimes\xi
with an equivalent statement
\Phi(E)M_{\Phi(E)}=\phi(EM_{E})
and
R(\Phi(E))=\phi(R(E))
. Following Example
3.30
we partition
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
into disjoint union of sets
C(M,N)
, and define a bijection
\Psi\colon\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
by

\Psi(0)=0
and mapping
C(M,N)
bijectively onto
C(\phi(M),\phi(N))
(with convention
\phi(0)=0
and
\phi(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{H}
). This
\Psi
is a strong BJ isomorphism, and a strong BJ isomorphism
\Phi^{\prime}:=\Psi^{-1}\circ\Phi
will satisfy
\|\Phi^{\prime}(E)\|^{-1}|\Phi^{\prime}(E)^{\ast}|=|E|
for every rank-one projection
E
. The rest follows arguments of the proof of Theorem
3.27
with
U=I
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

(a) Alternative way to obtain (possibly nonbijective) maps satisfying property
{\mathcal{P}}
is with the help of SVD: Choose any operator written in its SVD,

A=\sum\sigma_{i}(A)\xi_{i}\otimes\zeta_{i};\qquad\sigma_{1}(A)=\dots=\sigma_{k}(A)>\sigma_{k+1}(A)\geq\dots\geq 0;

select unitaries
U_{A},V_{A}
which depend on
A
, a nonzero scalar
\gamma_{A}
, and select a
c_{0}
sequence
\tau_{k+1}(A),\tau_{k+2}(A),\ldots<\sigma_{1}(A)
, which also depends on
A
. Assume in addition that
AM_{A}=\mathrm{span}\,\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\}
and
R(A)\ominus AM_{A}=\mathrm{span}\,\{\xi_{k+1},\xi_{k+2},\dots\}
are both invariant for
U_{A}
. Then, the map

A\mapsto\gamma_{A}U_{A}\Bigl(\sigma_{1}(A)\sum_{1}^{k}\xi_{i}\otimes\zeta_{i}+\sum_{k+1}^{\infty}\tau_{i}(A)\xi_{i}\otimes\zeta_{i}\Bigr)V_{A}^{\ast}

is the most general form of those maps. It can more shortly be written as

A\mapsto\gamma_{A}U_{A}P_{A}AV_{A}^{\ast},

where

such that

\tau_{k+i}(A)<\tau_{k}(A)=\dots=\tau_{1}(A)=\sigma_{1}(A)
. We warn that
\sum\tau_{i}(A)\xi_{i}\otimes\zeta_{i}
might not be a SVD, since we allow also nonmonotonic sequences for
\tau_{i}(A)
.

(b) We can also list all the strong BJ isomorphism in the case of two-dimensional space
\mathcal{H}
. The orthogonality preserving bijections
\phi\colon\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})
are simply the permutations of the partition

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})=\bigcup\{[(\alpha,\beta)],[(-\bar{\beta},\bar{\alpha})]\},\qquad(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}.

Given any such orthogonality preserving bijection
\phi\colon\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})
, let
\varphi_{\phi}
be a homogeneous map from
\mathcal{H}
onto itself, such that
\|\varphi_{\phi}(\xi)\|=\|\xi\|
(i.e., it maps a unit ball of
\mathcal{H}
onto itself) and
[\varphi_{\phi}(\xi/\|\xi\|)]=\phi([\xi])
. Define
\Phi_{\phi}(0)=0
and extend it to
a strong BJ isomorphism (it maps
C(M,N)
onto
C(\phi(M),\phi(N))
; see Example
3.30
) on
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, via

(6)

\Phi_{\phi}\colon A=\sum\sigma_{i}(A)\xi_{i}\otimes\zeta_{i}\mapsto\sum\sigma_{i}(A)\varphi_{\phi}(\xi_{i})\otimes\zeta_{i}.

We may further compose
\Phi_{\phi}
with a map
A\mapsto\gamma_{A}U_{A}P_{A}AV_{A}^{\ast}
as in part (a); one checks that this composition is (an arbitrary) bijection on the set of scalar multiples of unitaries, and maps nonzero
A=\sum_{1}^{2}\sigma_{1}(A)\xi_{i}\otimes\zeta_{i}
, written in its SVD, with
\sigma_{2}(A)<\sigma_{1}(A)
into

\Phi_{\phi}(A)=\gamma_{A}\bigl(\sigma_{1}(A)\varphi_{\phi}(\xi_{1})\otimes\zeta_{1}+\tau_{2}(A)\varphi_{\phi}(\xi_{2})\otimes\zeta_{2}\bigr)V_{A},\quad\sigma_{1}(A)>\sigma_{2}(A)

for some unitary
V_{A}
, a nonzero scalar
\gamma_{A}
and
\tau_{2}(A)<\sigma_{1}(A)
.
These are all the maps from (ii) of Theorem
5
.

We call a map
\Phi_{\phi}
from (
6

induced by orthogonality-preserving bijection
\varphi
; observe that
\|\Phi_{\phi}(A)\|=\|A\|
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}=\bigoplus\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})
be a compact
C^{*}
-algebra and
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}
a strong BJ isomorphism. Then,
\Phi
is composed of

(i)

A permutation of blocks which respects their dimensions.

(ii)

A map which is a multiplication by unitaries on some blocks and multiplication by anti-unitaries on other blocks.

(iii)

A map which is identity on all blocks with
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\neq 2
and is induced by some orthogonality-preserving bijections
\phi_{\lambda}\colon\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})
on blocks with
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}=2
.

(iv)

A map with property
{\mathcal{P}}
which maps norm-achieving blocks to norm-achieving blocks.

Sketch of the proof.

Following the proof (and notations) of Theorem
3.24
there exists a permutation of indices
\varphi\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
such that
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\varphi(\lambda)}
and
\Lambda_{\Phi(A)}=\varphi(\Lambda_{A})
(c.f. Lemma
3.19
for notation
\Lambda_{A}
) for nonzero
A\in\mathfrak{A}
. We extend
\varphi
to a linear
\ast
-isomorphism on
\mathfrak{A}
by

\varphi\colon A=\bigoplus A_{\lambda}\mapsto\bigoplus A_{\varphi(\lambda)};

being a
\ast
-isomorphism it preserves strong BJ orthogonality in both directions. Then,
\Phi^{\prime}:=\varphi^{-1}\circ\Phi
is a strong BJ isomorphism and
\Phi^{\prime}(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}}\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}}\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda}
for each
\Lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda
.

The restriction of
\Phi^{\prime}
to a sum of nonempty collection of blocks in
\mathfrak{A}
remains to be a strong BJ isomorphism by Lemma
3.26
; its structure on individual blocks
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})
is given in Theorem
4.2
and Remark
4.3
and takes the form (ii)-(iv), restricted to
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})
. The maps in (ii)–(iii) do not change the norm of individual blocks and are strong BJ isomorphisms (again see Example
3.30
); by composing
\Phi^{\prime}
with their inverses we obtain a strong BJ isomorphism
\Phi^{\prime\prime}
which has property
{\mathcal{P}}
when restricted to individual blocks and does not change the norm of blocks. Hence, by applying Lemmas
3.11
and
3.13
on each norm-attaining block of
B\in\mathfrak{A}
and on
B
, it has globally the property
{\mathcal{P}}
and takes the form (iv).
∎

Linear strong BJ isomorphisms have a simpler structure.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}=\bigoplus\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})
be a compact
C^{*}
-algebra and
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}
be a linear strong BJ isomorphism. Then, there exists a scalar
\alpha\neq 0
such that
\frac{1}{\alpha}\Phi
is composed of

(i)

A permutation of blocks which respects their dimensions.

(ii)

A map
X\mapsto UXV^{\ast}
for some unitaries
U=\bigoplus U_{\lambda}
and
V=\bigoplus V_{\lambda}
with
U_{\lambda},V_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})
.

Sketch of the proof.

By Corollary
3.33
we can assume
\Phi
is an isometry.
As in the proof of Theorem
4.4
we compose
\Phi
by a suitable map of the form (i) to obtain a strong BJ isomorphism
\Phi^{\prime}
with the property
\Phi^{\prime}(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}}\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}}\mathfrak{A}P_{\lambda}
for each
\Lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda
. By Corollary
3.33
this map is a multiplication of a unitary and a
\ast
-isomorphism, moreover, it preserves rank-one by Lemma
3.18
. Hence, its restriction on individual block
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})\subseteq\mathfrak{A}

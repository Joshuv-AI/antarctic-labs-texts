Divisibility of Character Values of Representations of Coxeter Groups
Jyotirmoy Ganguly, Rohit Joshi

Abstract
. Let d d be a positive integer. We study the proportion of irreducible characters of infinite families of irreducible Coxeter groups whose values evaluated on a fixed element g g are divisible by d d . For Coxeter groups of types A n , B n A_{n},B_{n} and D n D_{n} , the proportion tends to 1 1 as n n approaches infinity. ]
. For the case of the groups of type

Symmetric Groups

The symmetric group

is the Weyl group of type

. The divisibility of the character values of

[

]
. In this section we present a review of the paper.

Consider a chain of symmetric groups

S_{k}
S_{k+1}
S_{k+2}
\cdots
S_{n}
\cdots
j_{1}
j_{2}
j_{3}
j_{n}
j_{n+1}

For an element

which fixes

The main result of the article provides the asymptotic nature of the proportion of the irreducible characters of

Theorem 2.1
.

[

For any positive integers

\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{C}(S_{n},S_{k}),g,d)=1.

In particular, for any integer

approaches infinity.

Let

denote the degree of irreducible representation of

corresponding to partition
\lambda
.
In order to prove the main theorem, the authors focus on the divisibility properties of

.
For each prime number

, let

denote the
q
-adic valuation of an integer
m
, in other words,

is the largest power of
q
that divides

. Another key factor in their proof is the divisibility property of the degree of the irreducible representations of
S_{n}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
GPS19
, Theorem
A
]

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\{\lambda\vdash n\mid\upsilon_{q}(\mathfrak{f}_{\lambda})\leq h+(q-1)\log n\}}{p(n)}=0.

Here

is a positive integer.
The proof of the above mentioned result is based on the theory of
q
-core towers. This construction originated in the seminal paper
[
Mac71
]
of Macdonald, and was developed further by Olsson in
[
Ols93
]
. The proof of the main theorem can be found in
[

, Section
3
]
.

Hyperoctahedral Groups

For this and the next section we follow the definitions and results from

It is the Weyl group of types

4.
Demi-Hyperoctahedral Group

The Demi-Hyperoctahedral group
\mathbb{D}_{n}
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
defined as follows:

\mathbb{D}_{n}=\{\theta\in\mathbb{B}_{n}\mid\#\{i\mid\theta(i)<0,1\leq i\leq n\}\,\,\text{is even}\}.

It is the Weyl group of type
D_{n}
. To study the asymptotic nature of the divisibility of the character values of
\mathbb{D}_{n}
, we follow a similar method.
Fix a positive integer
k
. For the chain of groups
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{D}_{n},\mathbb{D}_{k})
:
\mathbb{D}_{k}
\mathbb{D}_{k+1}
\mathbb{D}_{k+2}
\cdots
\mathbb{D}_{n}
\cdots
j_{k+1}
j_{k+2}
j_{k+3}
j_{n}
j_{n+1}

The map
j_{k}:\mathbb{D}_{k-1}\to\mathbb{D}_{k}
is defined as the restriction of the map
j_{k}:\mathbb{B}_{k-1}\to\mathbb{B}_{k}
. Let
\theta
be an element of
\mathbb{D}_{k}
with cycle type
(\lambda,\mu)
.
Then
\iota_{n}(\theta)=g((\lambda,1^{n-k}),\mu)
. In fact, the conjugacy class of
g((\lambda,1^{n-k}),\mu)
in
\mathbb{B}_{n}
remains a single conjugacy class when restricted to
\mathbb{D}_{n}
. This is evident from the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

The conjugacy class
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}(\lambda,\mu)
in
\mathbb{B}_{n}
splits into a union of two conjugacy classes
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}^{+}(\lambda,\mu)\cup\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}^{-}(\lambda,\mu)
of
\mathbb{D}_{n}
if and only if
\mu=0
and all parts of
\lambda
are even.

The irreducible representations of
\mathbb{D}_{n}
are of two kinds:

•

Let
(\alpha,\beta)\vDash n
with
\alpha\neq\beta
. Then the irreducible representation
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}
remains irreducible when restricted to
\mathbb{D}_{n}
. In this case, we write
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}^{0}
to denote the restricted representation. Moreover,
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}^{0}
is isomorphic to
\pi_{\beta,\alpha}^{0}
. We denote the corresponding character value as
\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}=\chi^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}=\chi^{0}_{\beta,\alpha}
.

•

Let
n>0
be even and
\alpha
be any partition of
n/2
. Then the irreducible representation
\pi_{\alpha,\alpha}
of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
when restricted to
\mathbb{D}_{n}
, is a sum of two non-isomorphic representations of
\mathbb{D}_{n}
, which are denoted by
\pi_{\alpha,\alpha}^{+}
and
\pi_{\alpha,\alpha}^{-}
.

Therefore for
n>0
, we have

\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{D}_{n})=\{\pi^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}\mid\alpha\neq\beta\}\coprod\{\pi^{\pm}_{\alpha,\alpha}\mid\alpha\vdash n/2\}.

Therefore for
n
odd,
|\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{D}_{n})|=\frac{1}{2}p_{2}(n)
. For
n>0
and
n
even,

|\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{D}_{n})|=\frac{1}{2}(p_{2}(n)-p(n/2))+2p(n/2)=\frac{1}{2}(p_{2}(n)+3p(n/2)).

Lemma 4.2
.

For
n
even, we have

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\dfrac{\#\{\pi^{\pm}_{\alpha,\alpha}\mid\alpha\vdash n/2\}}{\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{D}_{n})}=0.

Proof.

For
n
even, we obtain an expression for
p_{2}(n)
as follows:

\displaystyle p_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{n}p(r)p(n-r)

\displaystyle=\underset{0\leq r\leq n,r\neq n/2}{\sum}p(r)p(n-r)+(p(n/2))^{2}.

This gives

p_{2}(n)>(p(n/2))^{2}.

(4.1)

Using the above inequality we compute

\dfrac{\#\{\pi_{\alpha,\alpha}^{\pm}(\theta)\mid\alpha\vdash n/2\}}{|\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{D}_{n})|}=\dfrac{4p(n/2)}{\Bip(n)+3p(n/2)}<\dfrac{4p(n/2)}{(p(n/2))^{2}}=\dfrac{4}{p(n/2)}.

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

For any integer
d
, we have

\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{D}_{n},\mathbb{D}_{k}),g,d)=1.

Proof.

For
n
odd, the set of irreducible characters are

\{\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}\mid(\alpha,\beta)\vDash n,\alpha\neq\beta\}.

Therefore, in this case

\#\{\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{D}_{n})\mid\chi_{\pi}(\iota_{n}(g))\,\text{is divisible by}\,\,d\}=\frac{1}{2}\#\{\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\mid\chi_{\pi}(\iota_{n}(g))\,\text{is divisible by}\,\,d\}.

(4.2)

So the result follows from Theorem
1.1
.

For
n
even, the set of irreducible characters are

\{\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}\mid(\alpha,\beta)\vDash n,\alpha\neq\beta\}\sqcup\{\chi_{\alpha,\alpha}^{\pm}\mid\alpha\vdash n/2\}.

Lemma
4.2
shows that in the computation of
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{D}_{n}),g,d)
we can ignore the subset
\{\pi^{\pm}_{\alpha,\alpha}\mid\alpha\vdash n/2\}
of
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{D}_{n})
. Towards computing the limit we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\#\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{D}_{n})\mid\chi_{\varphi}(\theta)\,\,\text{is not divisible by $d$}\}}{|\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{D}_{n})|}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\#\{(\alpha,\beta)\vDash n\mid\alpha\neq\beta,\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta)\,\,\text{is not divisible by $d$}\}}{\frac{1}{2}\left(p_{2}(n)+3p(n/2)\right)}

\displaystyle<\dfrac{2\#\{(\alpha,\beta)\vDash n\mid\alpha\neq\beta,\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta)\,\,\text{is not divisible by $d$}\}}{p_{2}(n)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\#\{(\alpha,\beta)\vDash n\mid\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{B}_{n}),\chi_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta)\,\,\text{is not divisible by $d$}\}}{p_{2}(n)}.

As
n
approaches infinity, we obtain

\displaystyle 1-\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{D}_{n},\mathbb{D}_{k}),\theta,d)<1-\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{B}_{k}),\theta,d).

Theorem
1.1
asserts that
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{D}_{n},\mathbb{D}_{k}),g,d)=1
.
∎

5.2.
Divisibility of Character Values

Let
\mathcal{O}
denote the ring of integers in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
. Note that the character values of
D_{m}
may not be rational integers. But being a sum of roots of unity they take values in the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
. In this case, we say a positive integer
d
divides the character value
\chi(g)
if
\chi(g)/d\in\mathcal{O}
, where
g\in D_{m}
.

Take
\mathscr{S}_{n}=\{1\leq k<n/2\mid\gcd(k,n)=1\}
. From
[
Gur16
]
we get that the minimal polynomial of

\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}
is

\widehat{\Psi}_{n}(x)=\prod_{k\in\mathscr{S}_{n}}(x-\cos(2\pi k/n)).

(5.1)

The roots of
\widehat{\Psi}_{n}(x)
are Galois conjugate to each other. Therefore
\cos(2\pi k/n)
is Galois conjugate to
\cos(2\pi/n)
, whenever
\gcd(k,n)=1
.
Let
\zeta_{n}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}}
be the primitive root of unity. We write
\alpha_{k}(n)=\zeta_{n}^{k}+\zeta_{n}^{-k}
. Observe that
\chi_{\sigma_{k}}(r)=\alpha_{k}(m)
, where
r\in D_{m}
. Next we present a proof of an elementary result for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have
\alpha_{1}(n)\in\mathbb{Q}
if and only if
n\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
.

Proof.

Note that
\alpha_{1}(n)
is an algebraic integer. Therefore
\alpha_{1}(n)\in\mathbb{Q}
implies
\alpha_{1}(n)\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover,
\alpha_{1}=2\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}
. Thus
\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
if and only if
\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}
is an integral multiple of
1/2
. This is true if and only if
n\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For a positive integer
d\geq 2
, if
\alpha_{1}(n)\notin\mathbb{Q}
then
\alpha_{1}(n)/d\notin\mathcal{O}
.

Proof.

We start with the assumption that
\alpha_{1}(n)\notin\mathbb{Q}
. From
[
Was12
, Proposition
2.16
]
we know that
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{1}(n)]
is the ring of integers for
\mathbb{Q}[\alpha_{1}(n)]
. Let
e
be the degree of the minimal polynomial of
\alpha_{1}(n)
. Observe that
\alpha_{1}(n)\in\mathcal{O}
. Therefore, if
j>e
by an inductive process one has
\alpha_{1}(n)^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{e}c_{i}(\alpha_{1}(n))^{i}
, where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
If
\alpha_{1}(n)/d\in\mathcal{O}
then

\alpha_{1}/d=\sum_{i=1}^{b}a_{i}(\alpha_{1}(n))^{i},

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and and
b\leq e
. So
\alpha_{1}
satisfies the polynomial
f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{b}a_{i}x^{i}-x/d
. Therefore we must have
b=e
. But
f(x)
can’t be transformed into a monic and integral polynomial simultaneously by multiplying it with a rational number.
Therefore
\alpha_{1}(n)/d
is not an algebraic integer.
∎

For a positive integer
d\geq 2
, we have

\alpha_{k}(n)/d=\frac{2}{d}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi k}{n}\right)=\frac{2}{d}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi k^{\prime}}{n^{\prime}}\right),

where
k^{\prime}=k/\gcd(n,k)
and
n^{\prime}=n/\gcd(n,k)
. Since
\gcd(k^{\prime},n^{\prime})=1
,
\alpha_{k}(n)/d
is Galois conjugate to
\alpha_{1}(n^{\prime})/d
. Moreover, we use the fact that Galois conjugate of an algebraic integer is an algebraic integer. In particular, we have
\alpha_{k}(n)/d\in\mathcal{O}
if and only if
\alpha_{1}(n^{\prime})/d\in\mathcal{O}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
k
and
n
be positive integers. Then TFAE:

(1)

\alpha_{k}(n)
is divisible by
2

(2)

k/n
is an integral multiple of
1/4
.

(3)

\alpha_{k}(n)
takes values from the set
\{2,0,-2\}
.

Proof.

Observe that
\alpha_{k}(n)/2\in\mathcal{O}
if and only if
\alpha_{1}(n^{\prime})/2\in\mathcal{O}
. If
\alpha_{1}(n^{\prime})/2\in\mathcal{O}
, Lemma
5.2
implies
\alpha_{1}(n^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Q}
. Further, Lemma
5.1
shows that the possible values of
n^{\prime}
are
\{1,2,3,4,6\}
. Evaluating
\alpha_{1}(n^{\prime})/2
for these values one obtains
\alpha_{1}(n^{\prime})/2\in\mathcal{O}
if and only if

. An equivalent condition is that
k
is an integral multiple of
n/4
. Elementary calculation shows that
\alpha_{k}(n)
takes the values
\{2,0,-2\}
when
k
is an integral multiple of
n/4
.

∎

Let
k,d
and

\alpha_{k}(n)

∎

5.3.
Results on Dihedral Groups

We now prove the results related to Dihedral groups.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

First we consider the case when
m
is even.
In that case,
|\mathrm{Irr}(D_{m})|=m/2+3
. For a two dimensional representation
\sigma_{k}\in\mathrm{Irr}({D}_{m})
, we have
\chi_{\sigma_{k}}(r^{l})=2\cos(2\pi lk/m)
. The required ratio
\cos(2\pi lk/m)\in\mathcal{O}
when
lk/m
is an integral multiple of
1/4
(see Proposition
5.3
). So we need
k=v\frac{m}{4l}
where
v\in\mathbb{Z}
. We have
m
divides
4lk
if and only if
\frac{m}{\gcd(m,4l)}
divides
\frac{4lk}{\gcd(m,4l)}
. Since
\frac{m}{\gcd(m,4l)}
and
\frac{4l}{\gcd(m,4l)}

are coprime, we have
m
divides
4lk
if and only if
\frac{m}{\gcd(m,4l)}
divides
k
.

For
D_{m}
one has

\displaystyle\#\{k\mid\chi_{\sigma_{k}}(r^{l})\,\text{is divisible by}\,\,2\}

\displaystyle=|\{k\mid\dfrac{m}{\gcd(m,4l)}\,\,\text{divides}\,\,k,1\leq k\leq m/2-1\}|

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{m/2-1}{m/\gcd(m,4l)}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{(m-2)\gcd(m,4l)}{2m}\right\rfloor.

So we compute

\displaystyle\#\{k\mid\chi_{\sigma_{k}}(\iota_{n}(r^{l}))\,\text{is divisible by}\,\,2\}

\displaystyle=|\{k\mid\dfrac{m_{n}}{\gcd(m_{n},\frac{4m_{n}l}{m})}\mid k,1\leq k\leq\frac{m_{n}}{2}-1\}|

\displaystyle=|\{k\mid\dfrac{m_{n}}{\frac{m_{n}}{m}\gcd(m,4l)}\mid k,1\leq k\leq\frac{m_{n}}{2}-1\}|

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{(m_{n}-2)\gcd(m,4l)}{2m}\right\rfloor.

We have

\frac{\#\{\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(D_{m_{n}})\mid\chi_{\pi}(\iota_{n}(r^{l}))\,\text{is divisible by}\,\,2\}}{|\mathrm{Irr}(D_{m_{n}})|}=\dfrac{1}{(m_{n})/2+3}\left\lfloor\frac{(m_{n}-2)\gcd(m,4l)}{2m}\right\rfloor

(5.2)

From the property of the floor function we get the inequality

\frac{(m_{n}-2)\gcd(m,4l)}{2m((m_{n})/2+3)}-1<\dfrac{1}{(m_{n})/2+3}\left\lfloor\frac{(m_{n}-2)\gcd(m,4l)}{2m}\right\rfloor\leq\frac{(m_{n}-2)\gcd(m,4l)}{2m((m_{n})/2+3)}.

One computes

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{(m_{n}-2)\gcd(m,4l)}{2m((m_{n})/2+3)}=\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\left\{\frac{\gcd(m,4l)}{m+\frac{6m}{m_{n}}}-\frac{2\gcd(m,4l)}{m(m_{n}+6)}\right\}=\dfrac{\gcd(m,4l)}{m}.

Taking limit of
n
to infinity we obtain the desired result. The proof for the case when
m
is odd follows similarly.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

We have

\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{C}(D_{m_{n}},D_{m}),g,2)=1

if and only if
g
is a reflection or
g\in H
, where

H=\begin{cases}\{e\},&\text{$m$ is odd},\\
Z(D_{m}),&m\equiv 2\pmod{4},\\
\langle r^{m/4}\rangle,&m\equiv 0\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
g
is a reflection, then
g\in s\langle r\rangle
. In that case
\chi_{\pi}(\iota_{n}(g))=0
unless

. For the one dimensional representations
\pi

\chi_{\pi}(\iota_{n}(g))/2\notin\mathcal{O}
. Note that the number of one dimensional representations is at most
4
for any
m_{n}
. Therefore they don’t affect the asymptotic behavior of the ratio.

If
g\in\langle r\rangle
, then
\frac{\gcd(m,4l)}{m}=1
if and only if
m
divides
4l
. This gives
4l\in\{m,2m,3m,4m\}
as

. The theorem follows from this.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

For

\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{C}(D_{m_{n}},D_{m}),r^{l},d)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\mathrm{gcd}(m,4l)}{2m},&\text{if $\upsilon_{2}(m)\geq 2,\,\,\upsilon_{2}(l)\leq\upsilon_{2}(m)-2$},\\

From Proposition

\chi_{\sigma_{k}}(r^{l})=\frac{2}{d}\cos(\frac{2\pi lk}{m})\in\mathcal{O}
if and only if

. Note that an integer integral multiple of

is an integral multiple of

\frac{m}{\gcd(m,4l)}

Observe that if
\upsilon_{2}(m)<2

\frac{2}{d}\cos(\frac{2\pi lk}{m})\notin\mathcal{O}

\begin{split}\#\{k\mid\chi_{\sigma_{k}}(r^{l})\,\text{is divisible by}\,\,d\}&=|\{k\mid\text{$k$ is an odd multiple of }\,\dfrac{m}{\gcd(m,4l)},1\leq k\leq m/2-1\}|\\

\dfrac{(m-2)\gcd(m,4l)}{4m}-1/2<\left\lfloor\dfrac{(m-2)\gcd(m,4l)+2m}{4m}\right\rfloor\leq\dfrac{(m-2)\gcd(m,4l)}{4m}+1/2.

\dfrac{(m_{n}-2)\gcd(m_{n},4\frac{m_{n}}{m}l)}{4m_{n}}-1/2<\#\{k\mid\chi_{\sigma_{k}}(\iota_{n}(r^{l}))\,\text{is divisible by}\,\,d\}\leq\dfrac{(m_{n}-2)\gcd(m_{n},4\frac{m_{n}}{m}l)}{4m_{n}}+1/2.

\displaystyle\dfrac{(m_{n}-2)\gcd(m,4l)-2m}{4m(m_{n}/2+3)}<\frac{\#\{k\mid\chi_{\sigma_{k}}(\iota_{n}(r^{l}))\,\text{is divisible by}\,\,d\}}{|\mathrm{Irr}(D_{m_{n}})|}\leq\dfrac{(m_{n}-2)\gcd(m,4l)+2m}{4m(m_{n}/2+3)}.

The desired result emerges as

∎

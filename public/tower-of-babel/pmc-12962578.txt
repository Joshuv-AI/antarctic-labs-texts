Current Practices and Evolving Trends in Implant-based Breast Reconstruction in China: A Nationwide Cross-sectional Survey
Authors: Qi Zhang, Bingqiu Xiu, Shuang Hao, Xuliren Wang, Zhibo Shao, Benlong Yang, Jiajian Chen, Xiaoyan Huang, Zhi-Ming Shao, Jiong Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Implant-based breast reconstruction (IBBR) is widely used globally and in China, but regional disparities and concerns about complications persist. This nationwide survey evaluates current IBBR practices and trends.Methods:A cross-sectional survey was conducted in 215 hospitals in 2022, collecting data on surgical volume, reconstruction types, mesh usage, and complications. A total of 198 hospitals provided complete responses, with 122 offering IBBR.Results:IBBR hospitals had higher surgical volumes, greater bed capacity, more breast specialists, and higher reconstruction rates. In these hospitals, IBBR accounted for a median of 93.5% of reconstructions, with a median annual volume of 24.5 (interquartile range [IQR]: 9–59.3). High-volume hospitals (≥100 IBBR/y) performed 69.4% of cases. TiLOOP Bra was available in 76.2% of hospitals, with 45.9% using it in more than 50% of cases. Prepectoral reconstruction comprised a median of 30% of IBBR cases. Most IBBR procedures used a single mesh (median 95%, IQR: 60%–100%), whereas 2-mesh usage was rare. Direct-to-implant reconstruction with mesh was the most common immediate approach (54.1%), increasing from 23.2% in 2017. Radiation therapy (62.3%), capsular contracture (62.3%), and infection (61.5%) were the leading concerns. Estimated median complication rates were 5% (IQR: 0%–10%) for contracture, 3% (IQR: 1%–10%) for infection, and 2% (IQR: 0%–5%) for implant loss.Conclusions:IBBR adoption is increasing in China, particularly with mesh-assisted techniques such as TiLOOP Bra. However, disparities in access and concerns over complications highlight the need for standardized care and further research.

Takeaways

Question: What are the current nationwide practices, usage patterns, and concerns surrounding implant-based breast reconstruction (IBBR) in China?

Findings: A 2022 cross-sectional survey of 198 hospitals (122 offering IBBR) showed that IBBR is more common in high-volume, resource-rich centers. Direct-to-implant reconstruction with mesh, especially TiLOOP Bra, has become the leading technique, rising markedly since 2017. However, concerns regarding radiation, capsular contracture, and infection remain prevalent.

Meaning: IBBR is increasingly adopted across China, but variations in practice and ongoing complication concerns underscore the need for standardized protocols and further clinical research.

INTRODUCTION

Breast reconstruction after mastectomy plays a crucial role in breast cancer management, offering significant psychological and quality-of-life benefits. Among the available approaches, implant-based breast reconstruction (IBBR) has become the most widely used method globally, due to its surgical simplicity, shorter recovery time, and favorable aesthetic outcomes. According to the American Society of Plastic Surgeons, 78% of the 151,641 breast reconstruction procedures reported in the United States in 2022 were implant-based.1,2 Similarly, a nationwide study from China found that 65.7% of postmastectomy reconstructions used implants.3 Population-based data from the United Kingdom also suggest a preference for implant-based reconstruction, particularly in immediate reconstruction settings.4

IBBR can be categorized by timing into immediate reconstruction (performed during mastectomy) and delayed reconstruction (done after cancer treatment). Each approach is chosen based on patient preference, cancer stage, and the need for adjuvant therapy.5 Immediate IBBR is often favored for its psychological benefits and aesthetic outcomes. However, delayed reconstruction is typically recommended for patients requiring postmastectomy radiotherapy, as irradiation increases complications (eg, capsular contracture, implant failure) in implant-based approaches.6–8 Autologous reconstruction (eg, deep inferior epigastric perforator flap) is preferred in irradiated patients due to better tolerance of radiated tissues.9,10 Although 2-stage expander/implant reconstruction may be considered in select cases, its success depends on careful patient selection and adjunct techniques (eg, fat grafting, mesh support).11,12

The introduction of materials such as acellular dermal matrix (ADM) and synthetic meshes (eg, TiLOOP Bra) has revolutionized IBBR by providing additional support and enhancing cosmetic results.13 These meshes enable more natural implant positioning and reduce complications such as capsular contracture.14 In recent years, their use in prepectoral reconstructions has increased, offering surgeons a more flexible and efficient approach.15,16

Despite the growing use of IBBR in China, factors such as regional economic differences, varying hospital surgery volumes, limited specialized training, and concerns about complications and patient satisfaction continue to affect its growth.17 A nationwide cross-sectional survey is needed to assess current practices, challenges, and regional disparities in IBBR adoption. This study aimed to provide an updated perspective on IBBR trends by analyzing data from 2022, building on a previous survey from 2017.17,18 By highlighting these insights, the research seeks to guide future clinical studies and strategies to standardize IBBR practices across China, ultimately improving access and outcomes for breast cancer patients.

METHODS

Questionnaire

A standardized questionnaire was developed to investigate current surgical practices for breast cancer treatment in hospitals in China by a committee led by Professor Jiong Wu from Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center, with the authorization of the Chinese Anti-Cancer Association, Committee of Breast Cancer Society (CACA-CBCS), and Chinese Society of Breast Surgery (CSBrS). This development was based on a comprehensive review of the literature, guidelines, and clinical expertise.

The 2017 questionnaire consisted of 4 sections, as previously described.18 In 2022, the questionnaire was expanded to include 7 distinct sections, building upon the original 4 from 2017: (1) sociodemographic and geographic factors, including the number of surgeons and breast procedures performed during the year; (2) the type and timing of breast reconstruction in new breast cancer cases; (3) details on clinical practices related to breast reconstruction procedures; and (4) the use of revision surgery, such as nipple–areola reconstruction, fat grafting, and breast symmetry–correction procedures. The 2022 version also introduced new questions on lymphedema, endoscopic surgery, and barriers to the development of breast reconstruction surgery, resulting in a total of 7 sections. Participants and Survey Methods

The 2022 survey encompassed hospitals across mainland China performing a minimum of 200 breast cancer procedures annually, representing 31 of the country’s 32 provincial-level administrative regions. Consistent with the 2017 methodology, Tibet Autonomous Region remained excluded due to no local hospitals meeting the 200-case threshold. This standardized selection criterion ensured that all participating institutions possessed adequate surgical volume for meaningful reconstruction practice analysis.18 For the 2022 survey, a purposive stratified sampling approach was implemented to achieve nationwide representation while accounting for institutional expertise. The sampling framework prioritized 2 key dimensions: (1) geographic distribution: at least 1 hospital was selected from each of the provincial-level administrative regions, with additional centers from high-population regions (eg, Shandong, Zhejiang) to reflect demographic variation. (2) Annual surgical volume stratification: hospitals were categorized into predefined volume tiers (200–300, 300–400, and >400 cases/y), with mandatory verification through hospital information system audits. CACA-CBCS/CSBrS–affiliated centers were oversampled to ensure inclusion of institutions with specialized reconstruction experience.

Regarding survey administration, survey respondents were specifically required to be breast surgeons with more than 5 years of experience in oncological and reconstructive surgery. Data collection was conducted through a standardized online platform with real-time completeness checks. Quality control measures included automated logic checks and manual review by the research committee.

This rigorous dual-stratification method generated 215 eligible centers, of which 198 (92% response rate) had completed the survey by September 2024. To ensure data reliability, department directors of selected hospitals were required to appoint designated specialists and provide institutional endorsement for all submitted responses.

Statistical Analysis

Questionnaires that were completed in full and contained no logical errors were deemed valid. Categorical data were summarized as counts and percentages. Continuous data were summarized by median, interquartile range (IQR), and range. Two-tailed Mann-Whitney tests were used for analysis. Statistical Analysis Software SPSS 19.0 and GraphPad Prism 10.0 were used for all analyses.

RESULTS

Current Status of IBBR in Mainland China

Among the 215 hospitals surveyed, 198 provided comprehensive responses. These hospitals had an average of 66.8 beds, with a median of 52 beds (IQR: 42.0–80.0), and a median of 10.0 breast specialists (IQR: 8.0–15.0). In 2022, the average annual surgical volume was 625.8 cases per hospital, with a median of 400.0 cases (IQR: 270.8–700.0). The median number of reconstructive procedures was 25.0 (IQR: 4.8–52.0). The average breast reconstruction rate was 9.2%, with a median of 5.0% (IQR: 2.5%–11.8%), whereas the average rate for breast-conserving procedures was 26.1%, with a median of 22.0% (IQR: 17.5%–30%).

Of the 198 hospitals that responded to the survey, 122 had regularly implemented IBBR, whereas 76 had not. Among the latter, 13 hospitals reported offering only autologous breast reconstruction, whereas 63 had not established any form of breast reconstruction program. When comparing these groups, no significant differences were observed in key factors such as per capita GDP and breast-conserving surgery rates (Table 1). However, hospitals that routinely performed IBBR had a significantly higher annual volume of reconstructive procedures, bed counts, annual surgical volume, number of breast surgery specialists, and reconstruction rates.

Growing Dominance of IBBR With Uneven National Distribution

Among the 122 hospitals that regularly implemented IBBR, 100 (80.0%) reported that IBBR made up more than 50% of all postmastectomy reconstructions, with a median rate of 93.5% (IQR 67.7%–100%). In 69 hospitals (56.6%), IBBR accounted for more than 80% of all reconstruction procedures, showing an increase compared with data from a 2017 survey. However, the use of combined autologous flap and implant reconstruction remains low. In 63.1% (77 of 122) of the hospitals, the proportion of such combined techniques was less than 10% (Table 2).

Trends and Preferences in Mesh-assisted IBBR

The use of mesh-assisted IBBR is summarized in Table 3. Of the 122 hospitals, 93 (76.2%) had TiLOOP Bra in reconstructions available, whereas 30 (24.6%) could get access to ADM. The use of TiLOOP Bra has increased compared with 2017 (P < 0.01); 60.2% (56 of 93) of hospitals now use TiLOOP in more than 50% of their IBBR cases, up from 17.0% in 2017. Meanwhile, ADM remains less commonly used in China. About half of the hospitals using ADM (15 of 30, 30.0%) applied it in less than 20% of their IBBR cases. Only 26.6% (7 of 30) and 23.3% (5 of 30) used ADM in 20%–50% and more than 50% of their IBBR cases, respectively, with no significant change from 2017 (Table 3).

Regarding implant placement and mesh use, prepectoral reconstructions made up 30.0% of total IBBR cases (IQR: 10.0%–60.0%). In all IBBR cases, a single mesh was used for implant coverage in a median of 95% (IQR: 60%–100%) of procedures, whereas the use of 2 meshes was much less common, with a median rate of 0% (IQR: 0%–10%) (Fig. 1B).

Trends in Immediate and Delayed Breast Reconstruction Approaches

Among the 122 hospitals performing IBBR, the median proportion of 1-stage reconstructions was 70.7% (IQR: 45.5%–89.3%), compared with 17.6% (IQR: 0.0%–37.7%) for 2-stage procedures. The most common method for immediate breast reconstruction was 1-stage implant with mesh, used by 54.1% (66 of 122) of hospitals. This was followed by 1-stage implant without mesh (22.1%, 27 of 122) and 2-stage procedures (13.1%, 16 of 122). A smaller number of hospitals (8.2%, 10 of 122) used autologous flap or latissimus dorsi flap combined with implants. Compared with 2017, the use of 2-stage expander/implant reconstruction (23.2% versus 13.1%) and direct-to-implant techniques without mesh (36.8% versus 22.1%) declined significantly, whereas single-stage implant-based reconstruction with mesh (direct-to-implant + mesh) surged from 23.2% to 54.1% (χ² = 19.2, P < 0.01), indicating a pronounced shift in clinical practice toward mesh-augmented immediate reconstruction.

For delayed reconstruction, the preferred methods have remained relatively unchanged since 2017 (χ2 = 5.09, P = 0.28). The 2-stage procedure remains the most common, used by 46.7% (57 of 122) of hospitals, followed by autologous flap reconstruction (25.4%, 31 of 122), 1-stage implant with mesh (11.5%,14 of 122), and latissimus dorsi flap with implant (9.0%, 11 of 122) (Table 4).

Clinicians’ Concerns Regarding IBBR and Common Complications

The survey also explored clinicians’ reasons for choosing IBBR and their concerns about the procedure. Easy to learn and practice was the most cited reason (79.5%), followed by conformity to the patient breast shape (77.0%), patient preference (75.4%), and shorter operation times (69.7%). Compared with 2017, conformity to the patient’s breast shape has increased the most (Fig. 2A).

However, several concerns about IBBR remain. The most common issues were the impact of radiation therapy (62.3%) and capsular contracture (62.3%). Other significant concerns included the infection (61.5%) and asymmetry, with low patient satisfaction (50.8%). Compared with 2017, concerns about radiotherapy and asymmetry with low patient satisfaction have decreased, whereas worries about capsular contracture and infection have increased (Fig. 2B) Regarding the real-world complications associated with IBBR, the most frequently reported complications were Baker grade 3–4 capsular contracture in 5% of cases (IQR: 0%–10.0%), infections requiring debridement in 3% of cases (IQR: 1.0%–10.0%), expander or implant rupture in 0% of cases (IQR: 0%–1.0%), and implant loss in 2% of cases (IQR: 0%–5.0%) (Fig. 3).

DISCUSSION

This study provided valuable insights into the current landscape of IBBR in China. Although IBBR is increasingly preferred due to its simplicity and aesthetic outcomes,19 our data show significant disparities in its adoption. In 2022, only 14.8% of hospitals performed more than 100 IBBR cases annually, yet these accounted for 62.7% of all procedures, indicating centralization in a few high-volume centers. Geographic and economic factors likely contribute to this imbalance, as high-volume centers were concentrated in wealthier provinces such as Beijing, Shanghai, and Jiangsu. In contrast, hospitals in less developed regions often lack specialized teams or access to reconstruction materials. Furthermore, limited reconstructive training in general and breast surgery programs may hinder broader adoption. These findings underscore the need to expand surgical education and resource allocation, especially in underserved areas, to improve equitable access to breast reconstruction.

Our study also found that prepectoral reconstruction with mesh is gaining popularity, mainly due to its ease of use, improved aesthetic outcomes, and reduced need for muscle dissection compared with subpectoral approaches.20 Moreover, a meta-analysis by Xu et al21 demonstrated that prepectoral reconstruction significantly reduces the risk of capsular contracture (odds ratio: 0.40, 95% confidence interval: 0.27–0.58, P < 0.001) and postoperative infection (odds ratio: 0.58, 95% confidence interval: 0.36–0.95, P = 0.03) compared with subpectoral reconstruction, supporting its safety in single-stage implant-based procedures. Although these early results are promising, long-term studies are still required to fully evaluate the impact on postoperative complications, including capsular contracture and implant failure.22

TiLOOP Bra is a titanium-coated, lightweight polypropylene mesh specifically designed for breast reconstruction. The titanium coating enhances biocompatibility and reduces inflammatory reactions compared with uncoated polypropylene meshes. TiLOOP Bra is currently the only synthetic mesh explicitly approved by China’s National Medical Products Administration for IBBR, making it the most widely used and clinically validated option in China. The increasing use of TiLOOP Bra mesh in IBBR is another notable trend, with 76.2% of hospitals offering mesh-assisted IBBR choosing TiLOOP Bra. This shift is driven by factors such as differences in cost, availability, and complication rates among various mesh materials. Research suggests that TiLOOP Bra may offer a more favorable safety profile compared with other meshes, significantly reducing risks of seroma, implant loss, and wound complications while maintaining high patient satisfaction.23 A meta-analysis further confirmed that patients in the TiLOOP Bra group had significantly lower rates of secondary surgery, hospitalization, and complications such as wound dehiscence compared with those using ADM.24 In contrast, ADM products are not yet widely available or affordable in many Chinese hospitals, and their use remains limited despite recognized benefits. Additionally, the high cost of ADM has been a limiting factor in its broader adoption. Studies have highlighted that the economic burden of ADM, especially in regions where healthcare costs are a concern, may contribute to its decreasing use in favor of more cost-effective options such as TiLOOP Bra.25,26 Taken together, the trend toward TiLOOP-based IBBR in China seems to be shaped not only by clinical evidence but also by accessibility, cost considerations, and regulatory approvals.

The choice of reconstruction methods is evolving, with a notable increase in the use of immediate, 1-stage reconstruction with mesh. This shift reflects a trend toward less invasive and more efficient techniques. Studies show a growing preference for combining implants with mesh in immediate reconstructions as mesh technology advances.27–29 The popularity of mesh in 1-stage procedures is likely due to its ease of use and positive surgical outcomes, making it a favored option for both surgeons and patients.18 However, this survey did not specifically examine the influence of radiotherapy, a crucial factor in reconstruction decisions.25,30 Further research into how radiotherapy affects these choices could provide a more complete understanding of this trend.

Clinicians continue to favor IBBR for its simplicity, as evidenced by its shorter learning curve and more predictable aesthetic outcomes.20,29 However, despite its growing adoption, several clinical concerns remain prominent and seem to be increasing over time. Compared with 2017, the 2022 survey revealed heightened concern about implant lifespan (from 25.3% to 35.3%) and infection-related failure (from 56.8% to 64.7%). This trend may reflect greater awareness of long-term outcomes as more institutions gain experience with IBBR, as well as rising patient expectations regarding implant durability and safety. The continued prominence of radiotherapy as a concern—despite our study not collecting data on actual radiation use—emphasizes clinicians’ perception of its complicating effects on implant outcomes. These findings underscore the importance of high-quality prospective studies to guide decision-making around IBBR timing (immediate versus delayed), placement plane (prepectoral versus subpectoral), and patient selection in irradiated contexts. Addressing these challenges will be crucial to optimizing outcomes and reducing complication-related revisions.

This survey found lower complication rates for IBBR compared with those typically reported in the literature. Many prospective and retrospective studies have shown that severe complication rates for IBBR usually range from 10% to 30%.31,32 For instance, the Mastectomy Reconstruction Outcomes Consortium study reported reoperation rates of 15.5%–18.8%, reconstruction failure rates of 7.1%, and infection rates of 10.4%–15.2%.33 In China, a recent retrospective study found a 5.4% overall complication rate in the TiLOOP Bra group, compared with 17.7% in the Surgisis mesh group.34 This discrepancy may stem from several methodological limitations inherent to our survey-based approach, including potential underreporting due to retrospective data collection, variability in complication definitions across centers, the concentration of cases in high-volume centers (62.7% from 18 hospitals), and limited follow-up duration missing late complications. Although these limitations caution against direct comparisons with prospective studies, our findings provide valuable real-world insights that highlight the need for standardized national registries with prospective data collection, unified outcome measures, and independent verification to better assess reconstruction outcomes across China’s diverse healthcare settings.

CONCLUSIONS

This study provided an overview of the current practice and trends of IBBR in China. The growing prevalence of IBBR, especially in hospitals with high volumes of reconstructive procedures, and the increased use of mesh-assisted techniques, such as TiLOOP Bra, reflect a shift toward more efficient and aesthetically favorable options. However, the uneven availability of IBBR across hospitals and ongoing concerns about complications highlight the need for efforts to standardize care, enhance training, and ensure equitable access to reconstructive surgery. Further research, including prospective clinical trials, is crucial to better understand the long-term outcomes of IBBR and to refine strategies for minimizing complications, particularly in the context of adjuvant therapies such as radiotherapy.

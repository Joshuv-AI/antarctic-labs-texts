2.
Approximation in seminorm

Definition 2.1
.

A subset
W
of the closed unit ball
B_{[1]}
of a Banach space
B
will be called a
norming set for a subspace

\mathcal{L}
of
B^{*}
, if for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}
we have

\|\varphi\|=\sup\{|\langle\varphi,u\rangle|:\,u\in W\},

where
\langle\varphi,u\rangle
denotes the value of a functional
\varphi
on
u
.

Annihilator

A^{\perp}
of a subset
A
of
B
is the set
A^{\perp}:=\{\phi\in B^{*}:\langle\phi,x\rangle=0,\forall x\in A\}
and for
E\subset B^{*}
-we consider its
pre-annihilator
:
{}^{\perp}E:=\{x\in B:\,\langle\phi,x\rangle=0,\forall\phi\in E\}
.

For
\mathcal{L}\subset B^{*}
we define the following seminorm
p
on
B
by the formula

(2.1)

p(x):=\sup\{|\langle a,x\rangle|:\,a\in\mathcal{L},\|a\|\leq 1\},\quad x\in B.

We shall use following consequence of Hahn-Banach theorem (cf.
[
5
, §20.8.(5)]
):

Lemma 2.2
.

If a set
W\subset B_{[1]}
is norming for
B^{*}
, then its norm-closed absolutely convex hull
\overline{\text{aco}}(W)
contains
B_{[1]}
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a w* -closed subspace of
B^{*}
and let
p
be the seminorm defined in (
2.1
). If a set
W\subset B_{[1]}
is norming for
\mathcal{L}
, then
\overline{\text{aco}(W)}^{p}
, the closure of
{\text{aco}}(W)
in the (not necessarily Hausdorff) topology given by semi-norm
p
contains
\{x\in B:\,p(x)\leq 1\}
.

Proof.

The pre-annihilator
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{L}=\ker(p)
defines equivalence
\sim
such that
(B/_{\sim})^{*}
is isometrically isomorphic with
\mathcal{L}
and the canonical image
W/_{\sim}:=\{a+{}^{\perp}\mathcal{L}:a\in W\}
is norming for
\mathcal{L}
with respect to the quotient norm
\|a+{}^{\perp}\mathcal{L}\|_{\sim}=p(a)
yield by
p
. The latter equality results from
(
2.1
)
, since the canonical embedding of

in

is an isometry. Then we may apply Lemma

and it remains to note that the closure with respect to

{\text{aco}}(W)
is the pre-image under canonical surjection of the corresponding closure in
B/_{\sim}
of
{\text{aco}}(W/_{\sim})

3.
Weak-star topologies

Let
X
be the spectrum of a uniform algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
. On
M(X)
we have two weak-* topologies:
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}=\sigma(M(X),\mathcal{A})
and
\sigma_{C}=\sigma(M(X),C(X))
.

From the definitions of integral we obtain the following fact:

Lemma 3.1
.

Any measure on a compact space
X
is a
\sigma_{C}
-limit of linear combinations of delta-measures
\delta_{x}
, where
x\in X
.

Denote by
T_{\mathcal{A}}\subset C(X)^{*}
the set of finite absolutely convex combinations of elements of
X=\operatorname{Sp}(\mathcal{A})
treated as
\delta_{x}
-measures. Let
T_{C}
be the
\sigma_{C}
- closure of
T_{\mathcal{A}}
.

We have equivalence relation modulo annihilator
{\mathcal{A}}^{\perp}
and the related canonical surjection
\Pi:M(X)\to M(X)/_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\perp}}
.
Let

\widetilde{\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}}
denote the quotient topology of
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}
and
let
\widetilde{\sigma_{C}}
be the quotient topology obtained from
\sigma_{C}
.
In this section our first aim is to compare these two topologies on
T_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Algebra
\mathcal{A}
separates the points of
T_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\Pi
is injective on
T_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proof.

Let us consider a finite set of points of
X
involved with two different convex combinations:
\mu=\sum\alpha_{n}\delta_{x_{n}}
and
\nu=\sum\beta_{n}\delta_{x_{n}}
, where
\sum|\alpha_{n}|\leq 1,\,\,\sum|\beta_{n}|\leq 1
. Without loss of generality assume that
\alpha_{1}\neq\beta_{1}
. Then for any
n>1
we can find a function
f_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}
vanishing at
x_{n}
and equal
1
at
x_{1}
. Then the (finite) product of such functions separates
\mu
from
\nu
. Hence
\Pi|_{T_{\mathcal{A}}}:T_{\mathcal{A}}\to\Pi(T_{\mathcal{A}})
is injective.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

We have
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}=\sigma_{C}
on
T_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\widetilde{\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}}=\widetilde{\sigma_{C}}
on
\Pi(T_{\mathcal{A}})
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{A}}^{\perp}
is
\sigma(M(X),\mathcal{A})
-closed, topology
\widetilde{\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}}
is Hausdorff on
\Pi(T_{C})
. By Banach-Alaoglu theorem,
T_{C}
is compact in
\sigma_{C}
, as a closed, norm - bounded set. Hence

\Pi(T_{C})
is compact in
\widetilde{\sigma_{C}}
-topology, which is stronger than
\widetilde{\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}}
, hence equal to
\widetilde{\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}}
on
\Pi(T_{C})
. By Lemma
3.2

\Pi
is injective on
T_{\mathcal{A}}
, which allows us to identify
T_{\mathcal{A}}
with
\Pi(T_{\mathcal{A}})
and to identify
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}
with
\widetilde{\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}}
,
\sigma_{C}
with
\widetilde{\sigma_{C}}
on
T_{\mathcal{A}}
.
∎

Denoting by
\overline{F}^{\sigma}
the
\sigma_{C}
– closure in
T_{\mathcal{A}}
of a set
F
we have

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that we have
E\subset\operatorname{Sp}(\mathcal{A})
and
x_{0}\in\operatorname{Sp}(\mathcal{A})
.
If
x_{0}
is in the
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}
-closure
\overline{\text{aco}(E)}^{\sigma_{\!\!\mathcal{A}}}
of the absolutely convex hull
\text{aco}(E)
of
E
, then
x_{0}\in\overline{E}^{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Assume the contrary: that
x_{0}\notin\overline{E}^{\sigma}
. Since
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}
is equal
\sigma_{C}
on
T_{\mathcal{A}}\supset\text{aco}(E)\cup\{x_{0}\}
, we would have
x_{0}\in\overline{\text{aco}(E)}^{\sigma_{\!\!\mathcal{A}}}\cap T_{\mathcal{A}}=\overline{\text{aco}(E)}^{\sigma}\cap T_{\mathcal{A}}
, hence
x_{0}\in\overline{\text{aco}(E)}^{\sigma}\setminus\overline{E}^{\sigma}
. Let
f\in C(X)
be equal 0 in
E
, while
f(x_{0})=1.
Then
f=0
on
\text{aco}(E)
. Indeed, if
\phi_{j}\in E
and for
\alpha_{j}
we have
\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\alpha_{j}|\leq 1
, then
\langle\sum\alpha_{j}\phi_{j},f\rangle=0
. By continuity,
f
vanishes also on
\overline{\text{aco}(E)}^{\sigma}
in contradiction with
x_{0}\in\overline{\text{aco}(E)}^{\sigma}
.
∎

4.
The spectrum of
A^{**}

Let us denote by
X
the spectrum of
A
and on
\operatorname{Sp}(C(X)^{**})
let us introduce the equivalence relation:
z_{1}\sim z_{2}
if
f(z_{1})=f(z_{2})
for any
f\in A^{**}
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}
the quotient space:

\mathcal{S}:=\operatorname{Sp}(C(X)^{**})/_{\sim}.

Hence
\mathcal{S}
may be treated as a subset of
\operatorname{Sp}(A^{**}))
.

In the next proof we use two w-* -type topologies on
M:=M(\operatorname{Sp}(A^{**}))
, namely:

\tau_{1}:=\sigma(M,A^{**})\quad\text{and }\tau_{2}:=\sigma(M,C(\operatorname{Sp}(A^{**}))).

Theorem 4.1
.

The spectrum of
A^{**}
is equal to
\mathcal{S}
, i.e. to a quotient space of the hyper-Stonean envelope of the spectrum of
A
.

Proof.

Algebra
A^{**}
is isometrically isomorphic to a subalgebra of
C(\mathcal{S})
. Hence
A^{****}
can be identified isometrically with a subalgebra of
C(\mathcal{S})^{**}=M(\mathcal{S})^{*}
.

Treated as functionals on
M(\mathcal{S})
, elements
\varphi
of
A^{****}
have their norms equal to
\sup\{|\varphi(u)|:\,u\in\mathcal{W}_{[1]}\}
, where
\mathcal{W}_{[1]}
is the unit ball of
M(\mathcal{S})
.

We apply Corollary
2.3
with
B=M(\operatorname{Sp}(A^{**}))
,
\mathcal{L}=A^{****}
, the related semi-norm
p
and
W=\mathcal{W}_{[1]}
. Since
\tau_{1}
is weaker than the topology of
p
,
\mathcal{W}_{[1]}
is absolutely convex, we obtain the inclusions

(4.1)

\overline{\mathcal{W}_{[1]}}^{\tau_{1}}\supset\{\mu\in M(\operatorname{Sp}(A^{**})):\,p(\mu)\leq 1\}\supset\{\delta_{x}:\,x\in\operatorname{Sp}(A^{**})\}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have
\mathcal{W}_{[1]}\subset\overline{\text{aco}(\mathcal{S})}^{\tau_{2}}\subset\overline{\text{aco}(\mathcal{S})}^{\tau_{1}}
, since
\tau_{2}
is stronger than
\tau_{1}
. The last set is included in
\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{\tau_{1}}
, as follows from Lemma
3.4
applied for
\mathcal{A}=A^{**},\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}=\tau_{1},\,E=\mathcal{S}
.

Hence also
\overline{\mathcal{W}_{[1]}}^{\tau_{1}}
is contained in
\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{\tau_{1}}
and by (
4.1
) we obtain

\mathcal{S}\subset\operatorname{Sp}(A^{**})\subset\overline{\mathcal{W}_{[1]}}^{\sigma_{\!\!\mathcal{A}}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{\tau_{1}}=\mathcal{S}.

∎

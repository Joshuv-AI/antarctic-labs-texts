Talenti comparison results for solutions to p p -Laplace equation on multiply connected domains
Luca Barbato, Francesco Salerno*

Abstract
In the last years comparison results of Talenti type for Elliptic Problems have been widely investigated. In this paper we obtain a comparison result for solutions to the p p -Laplace equation in multiply connected domains with Robin boundary condition on the exterior boundary and non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on the interior one, generalizing the results obtained in [ 2 , 7 ] to this type of domains. This will be a generalization to Robin boundary condition of the results obtained in [ 11 , 12 ] , with an improvement of the L 2 L^{2} comparison in the case p = 2 p=2 . As a consequence, we obtain a Bossel-Daners and Saint-Venant type inequalities for multiply connected domains. Keywords: p p -Laplace equation, multiply connected domains, Robin boundary conditions, Talenti comparison results. MSC 2020: 35J92, 35B51, 35P30

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded set. In his seminal paper
[
31
]
, Talenti investigated the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

together with the associated symmetrized problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=f^{\sharp}&\text{in }\Omega^{\sharp},\\
v=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp},\end{cases}

where
\Omega^{\sharp}
denotes the ball centered at the origin with the same Lebesgue measure as
\Omega
,
f\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\Omega)
if
n>2
(or
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
p>1
if
n=2
), and
f^{\sharp}
is the Schwarz rearrangement of
f
.
The cornerstone of Talenti’s theory is the pointwise comparison

u^{\sharp}(x)\leq v(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega^{\sharp},

which has had a profound impact on the development of symmetrization techniques in elliptic partial differential equations.

Since then, Talenti-type comparison principles have been extended in several directions. On the one hand, different boundary conditions have been considered, including Neumann conditions
[
13
]
and Robin conditions
[
2
]
. On the other hand, a wide variety of operators have been investigated: the
p
-Laplace operator with Robin boundary conditions
[
7
]
, the Monge–Ampère operator in dimension two
[
32
]
, Hessian operators in arbitrary dimensions
[
34
]
, first-order Hamilton–Jacobi equations
[
18
]
, and anisotropic operators
[
15
,
30
]
. More recently, significant attention has been devoted to the stability of Talenti-type inequalities; we refer to
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
21
,
24
]
and the references therein.

The extension of these comparison results to
multiply connected domains
presents substantial analytical difficulties and remains a challenging problem. While comparisons for the torsional rigidity associated with degenerate linear elliptic operators
[
11
]
and for the
p
-Laplace operator
[
9
,
12
]
have been obtained, the presence of holes in the domain deeply affects the symmetrization procedure. Indeed, unlike the simply connected case—where level sets of the rearranged solution are naturally spherical—multiply connected geometries give rise to plateau regions, where the gradient vanishes. These regions prevent a direct application of classical Schwarz symmetrization techniques and require new ideas.

In this paper, we address these issues by considering a domain with a finite number of holes, endowed with Robin boundary conditions on the exterior boundary and non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on the interior boundaries. Within this framework, we establish a Talenti-type inequality for the
p
-Laplace operator.
The main novelty of our approach lies in the treatment of mixed, non-homogeneous boundary conditions through the introduction of suitably extended functions that are constant inside the holes. This strategy allows us to overcome the analytical difficulties caused by zero-gradient regions and to recover sharp comparison results in Lorentz spaces. The setting is the following.

Definition 2.1

Definition 2.2

Let

The

It is easily checked that

\displaystyle{{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}={\left\|u^{*}\right\|}_{L^{p}(0,{\left|\Omega\right|})}=\lVert{u^{\sharp}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\sharp})}}.

An important property of the decreasing rearrangement is the Hardy- Littlewood inequality, that is

\int_{\Omega}{\left|h(x)g(x)\right|}\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}h^{*}(s)g^{*}(s)\,ds,

whenever the product

, one has

\int_{{\left|u\right|}>t}{\left|h(x)\right|}\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}h^{*}(s)\,ds.

3
Existence and properties of solutions

In this section, we establish the well-posedness of the associated variational problem. Our aim is to minimize the following functional:

\mathcal{F}(w)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega_{0}}{\left|\nabla w\right|}^{p}\,dx+\frac{\beta}{p}\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}{\left|w\right|}^{p}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\Omega_{0}}fw\,dx,

(7)

over the space

\mathcal{X}=\{u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{0})\,:\text{ $\nabla u=0$ on $\Omega_{i}$ $\forall\,i=1,\dots,m$}\},

(8)

where
\Omega_{i}
,
i=0,1,\dots,m
, satisfy conditions (i),(ii) and (iii).

This approach is motivated by the fact that, given the structure of the functional, a minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathcal{X}
is a weak solution to (
1
). In order to apply the Direct Method, the coercivity and w.l.s.c. of the functional (
7
) are classical results and can be inferred from standard arguments (such as the convexity of the gradient and boundary terms, which are for instance treated in
[
7
, §2]
), in our case the validity depends on the admissible space
\mathcal{X}
being closed with respect to the weak topology.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega_{0}\subset\R^{n}
be connected. Then the class
\mathcal{X}
, defined in (
8
), is closed with respect to the weak topology in
W^{1,p}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{X}
is a vector space, it suffices to prove the closure with respect to the strong convergence in
W^{1,p}
. Let
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{X}
be such that
u_{j}\xrightarrow{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{0})}u
, i.e.,

\|u_{j}-u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{0})}\rightarrow 0,\quad\|\nabla u_{j}-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{0})}\rightarrow 0.

Note that
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{0})
since it is a closed space. We have to prove that
\nabla u=0
on
\Omega_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,m
. So, for a fixed
i=1,\dots,m
, since
u_{j}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\Omega_{i}\subset\Omega_{0}
we have that

0\leftarrow\|\nabla u_{j}-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{0})}\geq\|\nabla u_{j}-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{i})}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{i})}

and this concludes the proof.
∎

By combining the coercivity and weak lower semicontinuity with the weak closure of
\mathcal{X}
(proven in Prop.
3.1
), we have the existence of at least one minimizer
u\in\mathcal{X}
for the functional. Furthermore, the uniqueness of the minimizer (and thus of the weak solution to the PDE) is ensured, as the functional
\mathcal{F}
is the sum of strictly convex terms (the
p
-powers of the gradient and the trace, for
p>1
) and a linear term.

(Implicit definition of

)
.

Let

where
\tilde{c}_{i}
are the constant values that

assumes on

. Finally, we choose the solution

to be such that

\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\Omega_{i}}f\,dx\quad\forall\,i=1,\dots,m.

so that we have the following variational characterization of the solution as

\int_{\Omega_{0}}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}uv\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\Omega_{0}}fv\,dx\quad\forall\,v\in\mathcal{X}.

In the same way, we implicitly define the constant

We remark that if we take the modulus of

4
Proof of the main results

We start this section by proving some useful lemmas. The first result is a dedicated version of the well-known
Gronwall Lemma
(see
[
19
]
for the original version of this result) which we prove to make the work self-contained.

Lemma 4.1

(Gronwall)
.

Let
\xi:[\tau_{0},+\infty)\rightarrow\R
be a continuous and differentiable function satisfying, for some nonnegative constant
C
, the following differential inequality

\tau\xi^{\prime}(\tau)\leq(q-1)\xi(\tau)+C

(11)

for all
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
. Then

\begin{split}\xi(\tau)&\leq\left(\xi(\tau_{0})+\frac{C}{q-1}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{q-1}-\frac{C}{q-1},\\
\xi^{\prime}(\tau)&\leq\left(\frac{(q-1)\xi(\tau_{0})+C}{\tau_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{q-2},\end{split}

for all
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
.

Proof.

Dividing by
\tau^{q}
in (
11
) we obtain

\frac{\xi^{\prime}(\tau)}{\tau^{q-1}}-(q-1)\frac{\xi(\tau)}{\tau^{q}}=\left(\frac{\xi(\tau)}{\tau^{q-1}}\right)^{\prime}\leq\frac{C}{\tau^{q}},

and, integrating from
\tau_{0}
to
\tau
,

\xi(\tau)\leq\left(\xi(\tau_{0})+\frac{C}{q-1}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{q-1}-\frac{C}{q-1},

which proves the first statement. Using the hypothesis and the inequality just proved, we find

\begin{split}\tau\xi^{\prime}(\tau)&\leq(q-1)\xi(\tau)+C\leq(q-1)\left[\left(\xi(\tau_{0})+\frac{C}{q-1}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{q-1}-\frac{C}{q-1}\right]+C\\
&=(q-1)\left(\xi(\tau_{0})+\frac{C}{q-1}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{q-1}=(\xi(\tau_{0})(q-1)+C)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{q-1}.\end{split}

Then, dividing both members by
\tau
, we find

\xi^{\prime}(\tau)\leq\left(\frac{(q-1)\xi(\tau_{0})+C}{\tau_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{q-2}.

∎

thus dividing (

on the other hand, by the isoperimetric inequality, we find

Lemma 4.3
.

For all

we have

\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{p-1}\left(\int_{\partial V_{\tau}\cap\partial\Omega_{0}^{\sharp}}\frac{1}{\tilde{v}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)\,d\tau=\frac{1}{p\beta}\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{*}(s)\,ds,

Proof.

Let us consider the quantity

\tau^{p-1}\left(\int_{\partial U_{\tau}^{ext}}\frac{1}{\tilde{u}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right),

and integrate in
(0,+\infty)
by Fubini’s theorem and the equation (

)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tau^{p-1}\left(\int_{\partial U_{\tau}^{ext}}\frac{1}{\tilde{u}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)\,d\tau=\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\left(\int_{0}^{\tilde{u}(x)}\frac{\tau^{p-1}}{\tilde{u}(x)}\,d\tau\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\tilde{u}^{p-1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=-\frac{1}{p\beta}\int_{\Omega_{0}}\mathrm{div}({\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{p\beta}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}({\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u)\,dx=\frac{1}{p\beta}\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}f^{\ast}(s)\,ds,

and the same equality holds with

.

Given that
\tilde{u}
is positive we have for all

\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{p-1}\left(\int_{\partial U_{\tau}^{ext}}\frac{1}{\tilde{u}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)\,d\tau\leq\frac{1}{p\beta}\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{*}(s)\,ds,

on the other hand, by symmetry,

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
g:\R\to\R
be a positive increasing function. Let
n>1
be an integer and
p\geq n
. Then the function

h(l)=l^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}\int_{0}^{l}g(s)\,ds,

is non-decreasing.

Proof.

For simplicity set

G(l)=\int_{0}^{l}g(s)\,ds,\qquad\alpha=\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}.

Then we compute the first derivative of
h
as

h^{\prime}(l)=l^{-\alpha-1}\left(lg(l)-\alpha G(l)\right).

Since
g
is increasing, we have
g(s)\leq g(l)
for every
s\leq l
, hence

G(l)=\int_{0}^{l}g(s)\,ds\leq lg(l).

Moreover, the condition
p\geq n
yields

\alpha=\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}\leq 1.

Combining these two inequalities gives

\alpha G(l)\leq lg(l),

so the bracket in the expression for
h^{\prime}
is nonnegative. Therefore
h^{\prime}(l)\geq 0
for all
l>0
, and the function
h
is non-decreasing.
∎

and integrating by parts both sides in the last inequality we obtain

\begin{split}\gamma_{n}\tau^{p-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mu(t)\,dt&+\tau^{p-1}G(\mu(\tau))\\
&\leq(p-1)\left[\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-2}\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s)^{\frac{1}{s}}\,ds\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-2}G(\mu(t))\,dt\right]+C.\end{split}

Then, if we set

\xi_{\mu}(\tau)=\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-2}\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s)^{\frac{1}{s}}\,ds\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-2}G(\mu(t))\,dt,

we are under the hypothesis of Gronwall’s Lemma and then, choosing
\tau_{0}=v_{m}
, we get

\gamma_{n}\tau^{p-2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mu(t)^{\frac{1}{k}}\,dt+\tau^{p-2}G(\mu(\tau))\leq\left(\frac{(p-1)\xi_{\mu}(v_{m})+C}{v_{m}}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{v_{m}}\right)^{p-2}.

We remark that the last inequality reads as an equality if we replace
\mu
with
\phi
. As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
we have that
\xi_{\mu}(v_{m})\leq\xi_{\phi}(v_{m})
and then from the definition of
\xi_{\mu},\,\xi_{\phi}
we find

\tau^{p-2}\left(\gamma_{n}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mu(t)^{\frac{1}{k}}\,dt+G(\mu(\tau))\right)\leq\tau^{p-2}\left(\gamma_{n}\int_{0}^{\tau}\phi(t)^{\frac{1}{k}}\,dt+G(\phi(\tau))\right),

that is, as
\tau\rightarrow+\infty
, and dividing by
\tau^{p-2}

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mu(t)^{\frac{1}{k}}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\phi(t)^{\frac{1}{k}}\,dt.

Then the comparison in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{k,1}}
is proved.

Now we have to show that

\|u\|_{L^{pk,p}(\Omega_{0})}\leq\|v\|_{L^{pk,p}(\Omega_{0}^{\sharp})},\quad\forall\,0<k\leq\frac{n(p-1)}{n(p-1)-p}.

It is enough to prove that

\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{p-1}\mu(t)^{\frac{1}{k}}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{p-1}\phi(t)^{\frac{1}{k}}\,dt.

Passing to the limit as
\tau\rightarrow+\infty
and integrating by parts the first term on the right-hand side in (
23
) we have

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\gamma_{n}t^{p-1}\mu(t)^{\frac{1}{k}}\,dt\leq(p-1)\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{p-2}G(\mu(t))\,dt+C,

where the last inequality holds as an equality with
\phi
instead of
\mu
. Hence, we need to show that

\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{p-2}G(\mu(t))\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{p-2}G(\phi(t))\,dt.

Multiplying (
22
) by
t^{p-1}G(\mu(t))\mu(t)^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}
and integrating on
[0,\tau]
,
\tau\geq v_{m}
, we find

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-1}G(\mu(t))\,dt&\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}-\mu^{\prime}(t)t^{p-1}G(\mu(t))\mu(t)^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}\,dt\\
&+\frac{1}{\beta^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-1}G(\mu(t))\mu(t)^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{ext}}\frac{1}{\tilde{u}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,dt.\end{split}

(24)

From Lemma
4.4
we know that
G(l)l^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}
is increasing, then when we compose it with the decreasing function
\mu(\cdot)
we find a decreasing function, this gives us the following

G(\mu(t))\mu(t)^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}\leq G(|\Omega_{0}|)|\Omega_{0}|^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}\quad\forall\,t\in[0,\tau]

and we can apply Lemma
4.3
in (
24
) finding

\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-1}G(\mu(t))\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}-\mu^{\prime}(t)t^{p-1}G(\mu(t))\mu(t)^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}\,dt+C_{2},

where

C_{2}=\frac{G({\left|\Omega_{0}\right|}){\left|\Omega_{0}\right|}^{\frac{-n(p-1)+p}{n(p-1)}}{\left|\Omega\right|}}{p\beta^{\frac{p}{p-1}}},

and the previous inequality reads as equality if we replace
\mu
with
\phi
. Then, integrating by parts and rearranging the terms, we find

\begin{split}\tau\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-2}G(\mu(t))\,dt&+\tau\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-2}\frac{d}{dt}G_{2}(\mu(t))\,dt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{n}r^{p-2}G(\mu(r))\,dr\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}r^{p-2}\frac{d}{dr}G_{2}(\mu(r))\,dr\,dt,\end{split}

where

G_{2}(l)=\int_{0}^{l}G(w)w^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}\,dw.

Then applying Lemma
4.1
with
q=2
and
\tau_{0}=v_{m}
, we get

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-2}G(\mu(t))\,dt&+\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-2}\frac{d}{dt}G_{2}(\mu(t))\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{v_{m}}\left\{\int_{0}^{v_{m}}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{n}r^{p-2}G(\mu(r))\,dr\,dt+\int_{0}^{v_{m}}\int_{0}^{t}r^{p-2}\frac{d}{dr}G_{2}(\mu(r))\,dr\,dt+C_{2}\right\}.\end{split}

(25)

We observe that the previous inequality holds with equality sign if
\mu
is replaced by
\phi
. Then, since
G(\cdot)
is an increasing function we have
G(\mu(r))\leq G(\phi(r))
for all
r\in[0,v_{m}]
, since
\mu(r)\leq\phi(r)
for such
r
. On the other hand, we have that
G_{2}(\mu(\cdot))
is decreasing, then, since
\phi(r)\equiv{\left|\Omega_{0}\right|}
for all
r\leq v_{m}
, we find

\frac{d}{dr}G_{2}(\mu(r))<0=\frac{d}{dr}G_{2}(\phi(r))\quad\forall\,r\leq v_{m}.

Then from (
25
) we get

\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-2}G(\mu(t))\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-2}\frac{d}{dt}G_{2}(\mu(t))\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-2}G(\phi(t))\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-2}\frac{d}{dt}G_{2}(\phi(t))\,dt,

that is

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-2}G(\phi(t))\,dt&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-2}G(\mu(t))\,dt\\
&\geq\tau^{p-2}[G_{2}(\mu(\tau))-G_{2}(\phi(\tau))]+(p-2)\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-3}[G_{2}(\phi(t))-G_{2}(\mu(t))]\,dt.\end{split}

If
p=2
, then sending
\tau\rightarrow+\infty
the claim is proved (proceeding as in (
19
)). Otherwise we have to prove

\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-3}G_{2}(\phi(t))\,dt\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-3}G_{2}(\mu(t))\,dt.

We proceed as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Define

G_{j}(l)=\int_{0}^{l}G_{j-1}(w)w^{-\frac{n(p-1)-p}{n(p-1)}},\quad j=3,\dots,p.

Then, multiplying in (
22
) by
t^{p-1}G_{m-1}(\mu(t))\mu(t)^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}
and integrating on
[0,\tau]
,
\tau\geq v_{m}
, we find

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-1}G_{m-1}(\mu(t))\,dt&\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}-\mu^{\prime}(t)t^{p-1}G_{m-1}(\mu(t))\mu(t)^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}\,dt\\
&+\frac{1}{\beta^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-1}G_{m-1}(\mu(t))\mu(t)^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{ext}}\frac{1}{\tilde{u}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,dt.\end{split}

Then, since
G_{m-1}(l)l^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}
is increasing, applying Lemma
4.3
, we get

\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-1}G_{m-1}(\mu(t))\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}-\mu^{\prime}(t)t^{p-1}G_{m-1}(\mu(t))\mu(t)^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-1}}\,dt+C_{m-1},

where

C_{m-1}=\frac{G_{m-1}({\left|\Omega_{0}\right|}){\left|\Omega_{0}\right|}^{\frac{-n(p-1)+p}{n(p-1)}}{\left|\Omega\right|}}{p\beta^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}.

Then, integrating by parts and rearranging the terms, we obtain

\begin{split}&\tau^{m-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-m}G_{m-1}(\mu(t))\,dt+\tau^{m-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-m}\frac{d}{dt}G_{m}(\mu(t))\,dt\\
&\leq(m-1)\left\{\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{m-2}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{n}r^{p-m}G_{m-1}(\mu(r))\,dr\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{m-2}\int_{0}^{t}r^{p-m}\frac{d}{dr}G_{m}(\mu(r))\,dr\,dt\right\}+C_{m-1}.\end{split}

Then, applying Lemma
4.1
with
q=m
, we find

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-m}G_{m-1}(\mu(t))\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-m}\frac{d}{dt}G_{m}(\mu(t))\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{m-1}{v_{m}}\left[\int_{0}^{v_{m}}t^{m-2}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{n}r^{p-m}G_{m-1}(\mu(r))\,dr\,dt+\int_{0}^{v_{m}}t^{m-2}\int_{0}^{t}r^{p-m}\frac{d}{dr}G_{m}(\mu(r))\,dr\,dt\right]\left(\frac{\tau}{v_{m}}\right)^{m-2}\\
&+\frac{C_{m-1}}{v_{m}}\left(\frac{\tau}{v_{m}}\right)^{m-2},\end{split}

and, again, the previous inequality reads as an equality with
\phi
instead of
\mu
. Then, since
G_{m-1}(\mu(r))\leq G_{m-1}(\phi(r))
for all
r\leq v_{m}
and in this range
\frac{dG_{m}(\mu(r))}{dr}<0=\frac{dG_{m}(\phi(r))}{dr}
, we find

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-m}G_{m-1}(\mu(t))\,dt&+\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-m}\frac{d}{dt}G_{m}(\mu(t))\,dt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-m}G_{m-1}(\phi(t))\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-m}\frac{d}{dt}G_{m}(\phi(t))\,dt,\end{split}

that is

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-m}G_{m-1}(\phi(t))\,dt&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{n}t^{p-m}G_{m-1}(\mu(t))\,dt\\
&\geq\tau^{p-m}[G_{m}(\mu(\tau))-G_{m}(\phi(\tau))]+(p-m)\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{p-m-1}[G_{m}(\phi(t))-G_{m}(\mu(t))]\,dt.\end{split}

For
m=p
, we can conclude sending
\tau\rightarrow+\infty
(proceeding as in (
19
)).
∎

5
Optimization of the p-Torsion and Robin eigenvalue

The results proved in §
4
allow us to address some optimization problems. In this section, we will prove that, among all the multiply connected sets with prescribed measures of the exterior domain and the holes, the annulus maximizes the p-torsional rigidity, for all
p>1
, and minimizes the Robin eigenvalue, where
p\geq n
.
We define the p-torsion functional as

T_{p}(\Omega,\beta)=\max\left\{\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega_{0}}\varphi\,dx\right)^{p}}{\int_{\Omega_{0}}{\left|\nabla\varphi\right|}^{p}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\varphi^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}:\,\varphi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{0}),\nabla\varphi\equiv 0\text{ on }\Omega_{i}\,\forall i=1,\dots m\right\}

and we recall that the maximizer of such a functional solves the following problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}w=1&\text{ in }\Omega\\
{\left|\nabla w\right|}^{p-2}\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}+\beta{\left|w\right|}^{p-2}w=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{0}\\
w=c_{i}&\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{i}.\end{cases}

(26)

Let
w
be the solution to (
26
) and
\tilde{w}
be its constant extension, then the
p
-torsional rigidity is defined analogously

T_{p}(\Omega,\beta)=\|\tilde{w}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{0})}.

Then, as a consequence of Theorem
1.3
we have directly the following optimization result.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
p>1
, then we have

T_{p}(\Omega,\beta)\leq T_{p}(A_{\Omega},\beta).

Proof.

Applying Theorem
1.3
to the solution of (
26
) the conclusion follows from the comparison with
k=1
.
∎

Likewise, the first eigenvalue of the Robin p-laplacian is defined through the following Rayleigh quotient

\lambda_{p}(\Omega,\beta)=\min\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega_{0}}{\left|\nabla\varphi\right|}^{p}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\varphi^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\int_{\Omega_{0}}\varphi^{p}\,dx}:\,\varphi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{0}),\nabla\varphi\equiv 0\text{ on }\Omega_{i}\,\forall i=1,\dots m\right\},

or as the smallest real number such that the following problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda_{p}(\Omega,\beta){\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u\quad\text{ in }\Omega\\
{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{0}\\
u=c_{i}\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{i},\end{cases}

(27)

where
c_{i}
are implicitly defined as in Remark
3.1
, has a solution. As observed in Lemma
3.2
we have that the solution
u\geq 0
, furthermore due to Harnack inequality we can infer that
u>0
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The first eigenvalue
\lambda_{p}(\Omega,\beta)
is simple.

Proof.

Let
u,\,v
be two eigenfunctions related to the first eigenvalue, without loss of generality we assume
u\geq v
, and define

\varphi_{1}=\frac{u^{p}-v^{p}}{u^{p-1}},\qquad\varphi_{2}=\frac{v^{p}-u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}.

We remark that
\varphi_{1},\,\varphi_{2}
are still admissible in the class
\mathcal{X}
defined in (
8
) and then we can use them as test in (
10
) obtaining

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi_{1}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}u^{p-1}\varphi_{1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{p}u^{p-1}\varphi_{1}\,dx+\lambda_{p}\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\varphi_{1}\,dx,\\
&\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla v\right|}^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi_{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}v^{p-1}\varphi_{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{p}v^{p-1}\varphi_{2}\,dx+\lambda_{p}\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\varphi_{2}\,dx.\end{split}

We remark that the terms on
\Omega_{i}
vanish by the definition of
\varphi
, then by summing the two equations

\begin{split}0&=\int_{\Omega}\left\{\left[1+(p-1)\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{p}\right]{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p}+\left[1+(p-1)\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p}\right]{\left|\nabla v\right|}^{p}\right\}\,dx\\
&-p\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{p-1}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}+\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p-1}{\left|\nabla v\right|}^{p-2}\right]\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}(u^{p}-v^{p})({\left|\nabla\log u\right|}^{p}-{\left|\nabla\log v\right|}^{p})\,dx\\
&-p\int_{\Omega}\left[v^{p}{\left|\nabla\log u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla\log u\cdot(\nabla\log v-\nabla\log u)+u^{p}{\left|\nabla\log v\right|}^{p-2}\nabla\log v\cdot(\nabla\log u-\nabla\log v)\right]\,dx.\end{split}

(28)

We recall the following inequality (for the proof see
[
23
]
) that holds for
p\geq 2
and for all
w_{1},w_{2}\in\R^{n}

{\left|w_{2}\right|}^{p}-{\left|w_{1}\right|}^{p}\geq p{\left|w_{1}\right|}^{p-2}w_{1}\cdot(w_{2}-w_{1})+\frac{{\left|w_{2}-w_{1}\right|}^{p}}{2^{p-1}-1}.

(29)

Applying twice (
29
), first with
w_{2}=\nabla\log u
,
w_{1}=\nabla\log v
, second with
w_{1}=\nabla\log u
,
w_{2}=\nabla\log v
, and multiplying respectively by
u^{p}
and
v^{p}
we obtain

\begin{split}u^{p}\left({\left|\nabla\log u\right|}^{p}-{\left|\nabla\log v\right|}^{p}\right)&\geq u^{p}p{\left|\nabla\log v\right|}^{p-2}\nabla\log v\cdot(\nabla\log u-\nabla\log v)\\
&+\frac{u^{p}}{2^{p-1}-1}{\left|\nabla\log u-\nabla\log v\right|}^{p},\\
v^{p}\left({\left|\nabla\log v\right|}^{p}-{\left|\nabla\log u\right|}^{p}\right)&\geq v^{p}p{\left|\nabla\log u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla\log u\cdot(\nabla\log v-\nabla\log u)\\
&+\frac{v^{p}}{2^{p-1}-1}{\left|\nabla\log v-\nabla\log u\right|}^{p}.\end{split}

Then, summing the two previous inequalities and combining it with (
28
), we find

\begin{split}0&=\int_{\Omega}(u^{p}-v^{p})\left({\left|\nabla\log u\right|}^{p}-{\left|\nabla\log v\right|}^{p}\right)\,dx-p\int_{\Omega}v^{p}{\left|\nabla\log u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla\log u\cdot(\nabla\log v-\nabla\log u)\,dx\\
&-p\int_{\Omega}u^{p}{\left|\nabla\log v\right|}^{p-2}\nabla\log v\cdot(\nabla\log u-\nabla\log v)\,dx\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2^{p-1}-1}\int_{\Omega}(u^{p}+v^{p}){\left|\nabla\log u-\nabla\log v\right|}^{p}\,dx\geq 0,\end{split}

that implies

\frac{1}{2^{p-1}-1}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{v^{p}}+\frac{1}{u^{p}}\right){\left|v\nabla u-u\nabla v\right|}^{p}\,dx=0.

Hence, we obtain that
v\nabla u=u\nabla v
a.e., therefore there exists a constant
K
for which
u=Kv
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
p\geq n
be an integer, then we have

\lambda_{p}(\Omega,\beta)\geq\lambda_{p}(A_{\Omega},\beta).

Proof.

Let us consider the problem (
27
) and the symmetrized one in the spirit of Theorem
1.1

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}z=\lambda_{p}(\Omega,\beta){\left|u^{\sharp}\right|}^{p-2}u^{\sharp}\quad\text{ in }A_{\Omega}\\
{\left|\nabla z\right|}^{p-2}\frac{\partial z}{\partial\nu}+\beta{\left|z\right|}^{p-2}z=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{0}^{\sharp}\\
z=\overline{c}\quad\text{ on }\partial S^{\sharp}.\end{cases}

(30)

If we multiply by
z
in (
30
), integrating on
A_{\Omega}
, we find

\int_{A_{\Omega}}{\left|\nabla z\right|}^{p}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}^{\sharp}}z^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=-\int_{A_{\Omega}}z\Delta_{p}z\,dx=\lambda_{p}(\Omega,\beta)\int_{A_{\Omega}}{\left|u^{\sharp}\right|}^{p-2}u^{\sharp}z\,dx.

(31)

Then by property of symmetrization and Corollary
1.2

\int_{\Omega_{0}}{\left|\tilde{u}\right|}^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{0}^{\sharp}}{\left|\tilde{u}^{\sharp}\right|}^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega_{0}^{\sharp}}{\left|\tilde{z}\right|}^{p}\,dx,

that implies, by Hölder inequality

\int_{A_{\Omega}}{\left|u^{\sharp}\right|}^{p-2}u^{\sharp}z\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}_{0}}{\left|\tilde{u}^{\sharp}\right|}^{p-2}\tilde{u}^{\sharp}\tilde{z}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}_{0}}\tilde{z}^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}_{0}}{\left|\tilde{u}^{\sharp}\right|}^{p}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\leq\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}_{0}}\tilde{z}^{p}\,dx

(32)

Therefore, combining (
31
) and (
32
), we can conclude as follows

\lambda_{p}(\Omega,\beta)=\frac{\int_{\Omega_{0}^{\sharp}}{\left|\nabla z\right|}^{p}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}^{\sharp}}z^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\int_{A_{\Omega}}{\left|u^{\sharp}\right|}^{p-2}u^{\sharp}z\,dx}\geq\frac{\int_{\Omega_{0}^{\sharp}}{\left|\nabla z\right|}^{p}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}^{\sharp}}z^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\int_{\Omega_{0}^{\sharp}}z^{p}\,dx}\geq\lambda_{p}(A_{\Omega},\beta).

∎

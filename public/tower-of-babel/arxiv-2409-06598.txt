Rovibrational Spectroscopy of Diatomic Molecules in a Modified Morse Potential using Nikiforov-Uvarov Functional Analysis
Raghav Sharma, Pragati Ashdhir, Amit Tanwar

Abstract
The radial time-independent Schrödinger equation is solved for the diatomic molecules: H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , LiH, HCl, CO, VH, CrH, CuLi, TiC, NiC, and ScN using the recently developed Nikiforov-Uvarov Functional Analysis (NUFA) method. A modified rotating Morse potential is considered and the Pekeris approximation is used to accommodate the centrifugal term. Accurate energy eigenvalues and eigenfunction solutions are obtained for vibrational ( n \mathit{n} ) and rotational ( ℓ \ell ) states. For H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , LiH, HCl, and CO, excellent agreement is observed between present values and literature, provided that the Pekeris approximation remains valid. For other molecules, a collection of low and high-lying states not found in literature are reported. The NUFA method is a simple, general, and accurate approach that may be applied to other interatomic potentials.



I
Introduction

Accurate calculation of energy eigenvalues of diatomic molecules in various rotational and vibrational states is of immense spectroscopic importance in understanding molecular behavior. Many analytical, semi-analytical, and numerical methods have been developed to solve the Schrödinger equation for this purpose. These include supersymmetry (SUSY)
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, analytical transfer matrix
[
4
]
, the shifted
[
5
]
and modified shifted 1/N expansion (MSE)
[
6
]
, the asymptotic iteration method (AIM)
[
7
,
8
]
, the variational method
[
9
]
, the exact quantization rule
[
10
]
, the Nikiforov-Uvarov (NU) method
[
11
]
, the parametric NU method
[
12
]
, the functional analysis approach (FAA)
[
13
]
, and the tridiagonal J-matrix representation
[
14
]
among many others. Recently,
Ikot et al. [15]
,
Ikot et al. [16]
combined the NU, parametric NU, and the FAA methods to formulate a new, simpler method called Nikiforov-Uvarov Functional Analysis (NUFA). They have used it on a variety of potentials, including the Yukawa, Tietz, Deng-Fan, Wood-Saxon, Hellmann, and Mobius potentials.

The choice of a potential function is paramount, as it must accurately model the inter-atomic interactions in a diatomic molecule. The Morse potential
[
17
]
is an important model which has been celebrated for its wide usage across molecular spectroscopy, chemical physics, and others fields
[
18
]
. This potential is applicable to diatomic as well as polyatomic molecules
[
6
,
19
]
. Since the time-independent Schrödinger equation (
eq.

12
) can be solved exactly for diatomic molecules in this potential in the non-rotational/purely vibrational (
\ell=0
) case
[
17
,
14
,
20
,
4
]
, analytical solutions for such
\mathit{n}
states are available. For rotational states, an effective potential must be considered which is the sum of the Morse potential and an
\ell
-dependent centrifugal term; such a model is sometimes called the
rotating Morse potential

[
4
,
6
,
14
]
. This work studies a slightly modified
[
20
]
version of the Morse potential, as shown in
eq.

13b
.

Analytical or semi-analytical solutions may omit the computational cost of numerical methods, but they can be difficult to find for rotational states. The rotating (centrifugal) term must be accommodated using a suitable approximation. Several such approximations have been formulated, including the Greene-Aldrich
[
21
]
, the improved Greene-Aldrich
[
22
]
, the Pekeris
[
23
]
approximation, and variations thereof
[
24
]
. The Pekeris approximation (
eq.

16
) is based on the expansion of the centrifugal term in terms of exponentials whose powers depend on the inter-nuclear distance parameter,
r_{e}

[
14
,
4
]
. This makes it a convenient model for working with the exponential-type Morse potential.

As a newly formulated method, NUFA is being applied to a range of potential models using various approximation schemes. Using NUFA with an improved Greene-Aldrich approximation,
Ettah [25]
has obtained the energy spectra for the Deng-Fan-Eckart (DFEP) potential.
Inyang et al. [26]
have investigated bound state energy eigenvalues for the Eckart-Hellmann (EHP) potential. The HSKP potential model was also studied for several molecules
[
27
]
.
Chen et al. [28]
reported diatomic molecular energy spectra and thermodynamic properties of a newly proposed deformed exponential-type potential (IDEP), employing both the Greene-Aldrich and Pekeris-type approximations. More recently,
Reggab [29]
studied the energy spectra of CO, NO,
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and CH in a modified Kratzer plus Yukawa potential.

The present work investigates the rotational and vibrational states of diatomic molecules which follow the a modified Morse potential using NUFA, which have not been reported in literature to the best of the authors’ knowledge. The purpose of this work is to investigate the accuracy and limitations of the NUFA method, as well as derive formulae for energy eigenvalues of the rotating modified Morse potential, where the Pekeris approximation is used to handle the centrifugal term. It is found that accurate rovibrational energy spectra can be obtained provided that the approximation of the effective potential remains valid.
This article is organized as follows.
Section

II
presents an overview of the mathematical method used in this work, setting up the necessary general forms and equations. In
section

III
, it is applied to the rotating modified Morse potential. The expressions for energy eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of diatomic molecules are derived. These expressions only depend on molecular spectroscopic constants. They are used in
section

IV
to calculate the energy eigenvalues of four well-studied diatomic molecules:
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, LiH, HCl, and CO, as well as six less reported molecules: VH, CrH, CuLi, TiC, NiC, and ScN for various vibrational (
\mathit{n}
) and rotational (
\ell
) states. Where available, comparisons are made with existing literature and values obtained using the finite difference method to validate the accuracy of the present approach. Finally,
section

V
offers some concluding remarks.

II
The NUFA Method—An Overview

The Nikiforov-Uvarov Functional Analysis method is used to solve second-order differential equations of the following type
[
11
,
15
]
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi^{\prime\prime}(z)&+\frac{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}z)}{z(1-\alpha_{3}z)}\psi^{\prime}(z)\\
&+\frac{1}{z^{2}(1-\alpha_{3}z)^{2}}\left(-\xi_{1}z^{2}+\xi_{2}z-\xi_{3}\right)\psi(z)=0\end{split}

(1)

The method given in
[
16
]
which involves converting
eq.

1
to a hypergeometric equation fails for the rotating Morse potential since the parameter
\alpha_{3}
becomes 0, making some intermediate equations unsolvable.
Ikot et al. [15]
, discussing the case for
\alpha_{3}\rightarrow 0
, use a
confluent hypergeometric
equation to arrive at the solutions. This approach, which is used in the present work, is elaborated as follows. Assuming an ansatz of the form

\psi(z)=e^{-\lambda z}z^{\nu}f(z)

(2)

and substituting
eq.

2
in
eq.

1
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&zf^{\prime\prime}(z)+\left(2\nu+\alpha_{1}-(2\lambda+\alpha_{2})z\right)f^{\prime}(z)\\
&-\left(2\lambda\nu+\alpha_{1}\lambda+\alpha_{2}\nu-\xi_{2}\right)f(z)\\
&+\left(\frac{\nu(\nu-1)+\alpha_{1}\nu-\xi_{3}}{z}+(\lambda^{2}+\alpha_{2}\lambda-\xi_{1})z\right)f(z)=0\end{split}

(3)

Changing variables as
y=(2\lambda+\alpha_{2})z
in
eq.

3
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&yf^{\prime\prime}(y)+\left(2\nu+\alpha_{1}-y\right)f^{\prime}(y)\\
&-\frac{2\lambda\nu+\lambda\alpha_{1}+\nu\alpha_{2}-\xi_{2}}{2\lambda+\alpha_{2}}f(y)\\
&+\left(\frac{\nu(\nu-1)+\alpha_{1}\nu-\xi_{3}}{y}+\frac{(\lambda^{2}+\lambda\alpha_{2}-\xi_{1})}{(2\lambda+\alpha_{2})^{2}}y\right)f(y)=0\end{split}

(4)

Equation

4
can be reduced to a confluent hypergeometric equation of the form

yf^{\prime\prime}(y)+(c-y)f^{\prime}(y)-af(y)=0

(5)

if the two terms in
y
and
\frac{1}{y}
vanish, i.e.,

\displaystyle\nu(\nu-1)+\alpha_{1}\nu-\xi_{3}=0

(6a)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}+\lambda\alpha_{2}-\xi_{1}=0

(6b)

Equation

4
can then be written as:

\begin{split}yf^{\prime\prime}(y)&+\left(2\nu+\alpha_{1}-y\right)f^{\prime}(y)\\
&-\frac{2\lambda\nu+\lambda\alpha_{1}+\nu\alpha_{2}-\xi_{2}}{2\lambda+\alpha_{2}}f(y)=0\end{split}

(7)

Solving
eq.

6
for
\lambda
and
\nu
:

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{-\alpha_{2}\pm\sqrt{\alpha_{2}^{2}+4\xi_{1}}}{2}

(8a)

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=\frac{-(\alpha_{1}-1)\pm\sqrt{(\alpha_{1}-1)^{2}+4\xi_{3}}}{2}

(8b)

From
eq.

7
, the energy eigenvalues are defined as:

\frac{2\lambda\nu+\lambda\alpha_{1}+\nu\alpha_{2}-\xi_{2}}{2\lambda+\alpha_{2}}=-n

(9)

which can be expressed as the NUFA
energy equation
, with
\mathit{n}
as the vibrational quantum number:

(2\lambda\nu+\alpha_{1}\lambda+\alpha_{2}\nu-\xi_{2})+\mathit{n}(2\lambda+\alpha_{2})=0

(10)

Using
eqs.

2
,
5
,
7
and
9
, the eigenfunctions are given by:

\psi(z)=\mathcal{N}e^{-\lambda z}z^{\nu}{}_{1}F_{1}\left(-\mathit{n},(\alpha_{1}+2\nu),(\alpha_{2}+2\lambda)z\right)

(11)

where
{}_{1}F_{1}
is the confluent hypergeometric function of the first kind.
\mathcal{N}
is the normalization constant.

III
Algebra

The radial time-independent Schrödinger equation
[
30
]
is given by:

\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}(E_{\mathit{n}\ell}-V_{\text{eff}})\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}(r)=0

(12)

where the subscripts
\mathit{n}\ell
denote the quantum state. The eigenfunction
\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}(r)
is related to the radial wavefunction
R_{\mathit{n}\ell}(r)
as
\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}(r)=rR_{\mathit{n}\ell}(r)
. The effective potential is the sum of the potential function
V(r)
, here a modified Morse potential
[
20
]
, and a centrifugal (rotating) term which depends on the rotational quantum number,
\ell
.

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff}}(r)

\displaystyle=V(r)+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}

(13a)

\displaystyle V(r)

\displaystyle=D_{e}\left(e^{-2\alpha\frac{(r-r_{e})}{r_{e}}}-2e^{-\alpha\frac{(r-r_{e})}{r_{e}}}\right)

(13b)

Given the exponential nature of
eq.

13b
, it is convenient to introduce a change of variables as:

z=e^{-\alpha\frac{(r-r_{e})}{r_{e}}}

(14)

from which it follows that:

\displaystyle V(z)

\displaystyle=D_{e}z^{2}-2D_{e}z

(15a)

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}^{\prime\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{r_{e}^{2}}\left(z^{2}\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}^{\prime\prime}(z)+z\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}^{\prime}(z)\right)

(15b)

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{2}\ell(\ell+1)}{2\mu r_{e}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{ln(z)}{\alpha}\right)^{-2}

(15c)

The
Pekeris approximation

[
23
]
involves expanding the left-hand side of
eq.

15c
about the point
r=r_{e}
, or, equivalently, the right-hand side about
z=1
. Expanding up to the quadratic term:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(1-\frac{ln(z)}{\alpha}\right)^{-2}\approx z^{2}\left(\frac{3}{\alpha^{2}}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)&+z\left(-\frac{6}{\alpha^{2}}+\frac{4}{\alpha}\right)\\
&+\left(\frac{3}{\alpha^{2}}-\frac{3}{\alpha}+1\right)\end{split}

(16)

Using
eqs.

15a
,
15c
and
16
,
eq.

13a
can be expressed as:

V_{\text{eff}}(z)=X_{1}z^{2}+X_{2}z+X_{3}

(17)

where the
X
-parameters are defined as follows:

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=D_{e}+\frac{\hbar^{2}\ell(\ell+1)}{2\mu r_{e}^{2}}\left(\frac{3}{\alpha^{2}}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)

(18a)

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=-2D_{e}+\frac{\hbar^{2}\ell(\ell+1)}{2\mu r_{e}^{2}}\left(-\frac{6}{\alpha^{2}}+\frac{4}{\alpha}\right)

(18b)

\displaystyle X_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{2}\ell(\ell+1)}{2\mu r_{e}^{2}}\left(\frac{3}{\alpha^{2}}-\frac{3}{\alpha}+1\right)

(18c)

Substituting
eqs.

17
and
15b
in
eq.

12
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}z^{2}\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}^{\prime\prime}(z)&+z\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}^{\prime}(z)\\
&+\beta(E_{\mathit{n}\ell}-X_{1}z^{2}-X_{2}z-X_{3})\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}(z)=0\end{split}

(19)

where
\beta=(2\mu r_{e}^{2})/(\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2})
is a parameter defined for clarity. As
z\neq 0,

eq.

19
can be rearranged at once into:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}^{\prime\prime}(z)+\frac{1}{z}\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}^{\prime}(z)\\
&+\frac{1}{z^{2}}\left(-\beta X_{1}z^{2}+(-\beta X_{2})z-\beta(X_{3}-E_{\mathit{n}\ell})\right)\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}(z)=0\end{split}

(20)

which resembles
eq.

1
, the form required for applying NUFA. Comparing the two equations, the necessary parameters are obtained:

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}

\displaystyle=0

(21a)

\displaystyle\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=\beta X_{1}

\displaystyle\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=-\beta X_{2}

\displaystyle\xi_{3}

\displaystyle=\beta(X_{3}-E_{\mathit{n}\ell})

(21b)

III.1
Energy Eigenvalues

Substituting
eq.

21
in
eq.

8
:

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\beta X_{1}}

(22a)

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\beta\left(X_{3}-E_{\mathit{n}\ell}\right)}

(22b)

where only the positive square roots are considered so that
eq.

2
is normalizable.
Equation

10
can be solved for
\nu
:

\nu=\frac{1}{2\lambda+\alpha_{2}}\left(-\mathit{n}(2\lambda+\alpha_{2})-\alpha_{1}\lambda+\xi_{2}\right)

(23)

From
eqs.

21
and
23
:

\nu=-\left(\mathit{n}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\xi_{2}}{2\lambda}\right)=-\left(\mathit{n}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{X_{2}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{X_{1}}}\right)

(24)

Using
eqs.

22b
and
24
to solve for
E_{\mathit{n}\ell}
:

\beta\left(X_{3}-E_{\mathit{n}\ell}\right)=\left(\mathit{n}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{X_{2}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{X_{1}}}\right)^{2}

(25)

Finally,

\boxed{E_{\mathit{n}\ell}=X_{3}-\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\mathit{n}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{X_{2}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{X_{1}}}\right)^{2}}

(26)

Equation

26
contains terms that only depend on molecular constants
D_{e}
,
r_{e}
,
\mu
,
\alpha
and the quantum numbers
(\mathit{n},\ell)
. It can be used to find the energy eigenvalues of diatomic molecules for any
\mathit{n}
and
\ell
-states.

The s-Wave Solution

The case
\ell=0
is highlighted for its simplicity. From
eq.

18
,

X_{1}=D_{e}\qquad X_{2}=-2D_{e}\qquad X_{3}=0\\

(27)

which gives, from
eq.

26
:

E_{\mathit{n}0}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\mathit{n}+\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\beta D_{e}}\right)^{2}

III.2
Eigenfunctions

From
eqs.

11
,
21
,
22
and
14
, the general expression for the eigenfunctions is obtained as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}(r)=\mathcal{N}\exp\left(-\sqrt{\beta X_{1}}\ e^{-\alpha\frac{(r-r_{e})}{r_{e}}}\right)\\
&\times\exp\left(-\alpha\frac{(r-r_{e})}{r_{e}}\sqrt{\beta\left(X_{3}-E_{\mathit{n}\ell}\right)}\right)\\
&\times{}_{1}F_{1}\left(-\mathit{n},\ 1+2\sqrt{\beta\left(X_{3}-E_{\mathit{n}\ell}\right)},\ 2\sqrt{\beta X_{1}}\ e^{-\alpha\frac{(r-r_{e})}{r_{e}}}\right)\end{split}

(29)

The non-normalized probability density
|\psi_{\mathit{n}\ell}(r)|^{2}
is numerically integrated over a sufficiently large range of
r
in Mathematica
[
31
]
. The normalization constant
\mathcal{N}
is the inverse square root of the integral.

Before discussing high-lying states, some important observations must be made about the validity of the Pekeris approximation. By construction, the approximated effective potential (
eq.

17
) keeps the same form as the original potential (
eq.

13a
) at
\ell=0

[
36
]

r=r_{e}
, and deviates further from
\ell(\ell+1)/r^{2}
as
\ell
increases or the distance from
r=r_{e}
increases. The effect of the centrifugal term is to move the particle away from
r=0
, which shifts the wavefunction to the right at larger values of
\ell
. An increase in
\mathit{n}
causes the wavefunction to be spread out of a larger range of
r
, so at high-lying states, it is important that the effective potential is approximated accurately.

Conclusion

The recently developed Nikiforov-Uvarov Functional Analysis (NUFA) method has been used to conduct a spectroscopic study of diatomic molecules in a rotating modified Morse potential. Using the Pekeris-type scheme to approximate the centrifugal term, concise expressions for energy eigenvalues and eigenfunctions have been obtained. Thorough comparisons with existing literature, where available, have been made. Provided that the approximation remains valid, NUFA offers a simple algebraic method to obtain accurate results for low and high rotational-vibrational states. In future work, more accurate approximations of the effective potential may be combined with NUFA. Other potentials can also be considered. The present work can serve as a useful reference of energy states for one of the important potential models in molecular physics.

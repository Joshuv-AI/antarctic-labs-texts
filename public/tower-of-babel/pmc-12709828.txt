The global prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Zahra Cheraghi, Bita Azmi-Naei, Parvin Cheraghi, Amin Doosti-Irani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDespite available treatments, uncontrolled hypertension (systolic/diastolic blood pressure ≥ 140/90 mmHg despite medication) remains prevalent, contributing significantly to mortality. However, global estimates of its prevalence vary widely. We aimed to determine the pooled global prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension among hypertensive patients.MethodsWe performed a systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA guidelines. PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus were searched using a predesigned search strategy up to August 2024. The cross-sectional studies which reported the prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension prevalence in adults were included. Two reviewers independently screened studies, extracted data, and assessed bias risk using the JBI checklist. The heterogeneity was evaluated using I² statistics. The inverse variance method was used in meta-analysis, and the random-effects model at a 95% confidence interval (CI) was used to the report of the results.ResultsOut of 3,500 identified references, 200 studies met the eligibility criteria and were included in this review. The global pooled prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension was 54.6% (95% CI: 52.1–57.1; I² = 99.97%). In the WHO African region, the Americas, South-East Asia, European region, Eastern Mediterranean, and the Western Pacific Region the pooled prevalence was 59.4% (95% CI: 54.4, 64.5; I²=97.11%); 49.7% (95% CI: 42.4, 56.9; I² = 99.98%); 44.2% (95% CI: 35.6, 52.7; I² = 99.86%); 53.7% (95% CI: 49.8, 57.7; I² = 99.89%); 60.0% (95% CI: 54.1, 65.8; I² = 99.47%); and 58.8% (95% CI: 51.5, 66.0; I² = 99.96%) respectively.ConclusionBased on the results of this systematic review and meta-analysis, the global prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension was high. Based on the WHO regions, the highest and lowest prevalence was related to Eastern Mediterranean and South-East Asia respectively.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-25553-4.Keywords: Uncontrolled hypertension, Prevalence, Meta-analysis, Blood pressure

Introduction

Globally, hypertension is a primary contributor to death and disability and ranks among the most significant global risk factors for mortality and morbidity [1]. A prevalent and serious chronic condition, hypertension increases the risk of mortality from cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, and other complications [2]. It is a leading cause of premature death worldwide, with an estimated 1.28 billion adults aged 30–79 affected globally [2].

Hypertension is a modifiable risk factor for many chronic diseases. Poor control and inadequate treatment of hypertension can increase the risk of complications such as stroke, coronary heart disease, and all-cause and cardiovascular disease mortality [3]. Uncontrolled hypertension is defined as systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg in individuals diagnosed with hypertension who are receiving antihypertensive treatment [4]. Despite the availability of effective treatments, a significant proportion of hypertensive patients remain uncontrolled [5]. Current evidence indicates that uncontrolled hypertension significantly increases the risk of all-cause mortality, heart disease-specific mortality, cardiovascular disease (CVD) specific mortality, and cerebrovascular disease-specific mortality compared to normotensive individuals [6–9].

In 2019, the global control rates for hypertension were approximately 23% in women and 18% in men. The highest treatment and control rates were reported in high-income countries, while the lowest were observed in low-income countries [10]. Globally, high blood pressure contributed to over half of all cardiovascular deaths and 62% of deaths due to kidney disease in 2019 [1].

Despite numerous studies estimating the prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension, there is controversy in the results of published studies globally, with reported prevalences varying widely [11–27]. In addition, several meta-analyses have been published that are country- or region-specific. For example, based on a meta-analysis in Sub-Saharan Africa, the prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension was 50.29% [28]. Two meta-analyses in Ethiopia showed prevalences of 48.00% [29] and 51.00% [29]. This discrepancy underscores the need for a comprehensive, up-to-date synthesis of available evidence to clarify the true burden of uncontrolled hypertension across different regions and populations. Our study addresses this gap by conducting a rigorous systematic review and meta-analysis, incorporating a broad range of geographically diverse data sources. By providing a consolidated and precise estimate, this work aims to not only enhance the understanding of the global epidemiology of uncontrolled hypertension but also to inform public health strategies and resource allocation to better manage this critical cardiovascular risk factor. Therefore, we conducted this comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis to determine the global prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension.

Methods

This study is a part of a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis regarding the prevalence and associated factors of uncontrolled hypertension. This systematic review was reported based on the PRISMA statement (additional file 1) [30]. The proposal of this study was approved by the ethics committee of Hamadan University of Medical Sciences (IR.UMSHA.REC.1401.587).

Eligibility criteria

The cross-sectional studies conducted to estimate the prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension among people lining with hypertension were included in this study. In this review there was no restriction on the location, time, and language of the studies. The studies that conducted on hypertensive patients with chronic kidney diseases, pregnant women, HIV positive patients, patient with multiple sclerosis, and history of stroke were excluded. These studies were excluded because they involved different populations, and hypertension management in patients with comorbidities may not be comparable to that in patients without them. Uncontrolled hypertension was defined as systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg in people with hypertension who receiving anti-hypertensive treatment [4].

Identifying the relevant studies

The international databases including PubMed, Web of Sciences, and Scopus were searched up to August 2024. The following keywords were used to search of the databases: (Uncontrolled hypertension [Text word] OR Uncontrolled high blood pressure [Text word] OR poor control of hypertension [Text word]) AND (Prevalence [MeSH terms]) AND Cross-Sectional Studies [MeSH terms]).

Data extraction and assessing the risk of bias

In this review two authors (A.DI and BA), independently were responsible for screening the titles and abstracts of the obtained references. In the next step, the full texts of the selected studies were evaluated by the mentioned authors independently based on the eligibility criteria. Any disagreements between the authors were resolved through discussion.

The extracted data from the included studies were as follow: the name of the first author, year of publication, country of study, study population, mean/median age of participants, gender, sample size (people living hypertension), and number of hypertensive patients with uncontrolled hypertension.

Two authors (A.DI and B.A) were responsible for evaluating the risk of bias. The JBI critical appraisal checklist for analytic cross-sectional and prevalence studies was used for assessing the risk of bias [31]. We could not find the full text of 14 included studies, so we did not assess the risk of bias in these studies.

Statistical analysis

The statistical heterogeneity among the results if included studies was assessed using the Chi-square test. The Tau-square test, and the I2 statistic was used to assess the between-study variance and quantifying the heterogeneity respectively [32].

The prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension, was calculated by dividing the number of hypertensive patients with uncontrolled hypertension by the number of hypertensive patients for each included study. In the next step, the standard error was calculated for each prevalence. The inverse variance method to was used to obtain the pooled prevalence. We performed the sensitivity analysis when the heterogeneity was high (I2 ≥ 50%) using the MetaPlot tool. MetaPlot is a graphical tool used to assess the influence of individual studies on heterogeneity in meta-analyses. It presents a two-way plot with study codes on the x-axis and the corresponding I² statistic (calculated by sequentially excluding each study) on the y-axis. This leave-one-out sensitivity analysis systematically evaluates how omitting a single study affects overall heterogeneity (I² and χ² values), identifying which studies disproportionately contribute to inconsistency. The process iteratively removes the study causing the largest I² reduction, recalculates heterogeneity for the remaining (n-1) studies, and repeats until optimal homogeneity is achieved. This method provides a data-driven approach to refine meta-analytic models by selectively excluding outliers that skew heterogeneity estimates [33, 34]. The random-effects model was used to report the results. The pooled prevalence was reported at 95% confidence revel. Stata 17.0 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA) were used for data analysis.

Results

Characteristics of included studies

From an initial pool of 3,500 identified references, 200 studies met the eligibility criteria and were included in this systematic review and meta-analysis. In the WHO African Region, we identified 31 studies comprising 11,731 hypertensive patients, of whom 7,221 had uncontrolled hypertension (Supplementary Table 1). The pooled prevalence was 59.4% (95% CI: 54.4–64.5; I²=97.11%), indicating high heterogeneity across studies (Supplementary Fig. 1). In the WHO Region of the Americas, we identified 34 studies encompassing 1,219,133 hypertensive patients, including 306,927 with uncontrolled hypertension (Supplementary Table 1). The pooled prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension was 49.7% (95% CI: 42.4–56.9; I² = 99.98%), demonstrating considerable heterogeneity across studies (Supplementary Fig. 2). In the WHO South-East Asia Region, 20 studies met the eligibility criteria, comprising 109,645 hypertensive patients, of whom 30,179 had uncontrolled hypertension (Supplementary Table 1). The pooled prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension in this region was 44.2% (95% CI: 35.6–52.7; I² = 99.86%), indicating substantial heterogeneity across studies (Supplementary Fig. 3). The highest number of included studies pertained to the WHO European Region. This region accounted for 57 studies, involving 580,056 hypertensive patients, of whom 247,841 had uncontrolled hypertension (Supplementary Table 1). The pooled prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension was 53.7% (95% CI: 49.8–57.7; I² = 99.89%) (Supplementary Fig. 4). In the WHO Eastern Mediterranean Region, we identified 33 studies involving 53,818 hypertensive patients, of whom 30,825 had uncontrolled hypertension (Supplementary Table 1). The pooled prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension was 60.0% (95% CI: 54.1–65.8; I² = 99.47%) (Supplementary Fig. 5). In the Western Pacific Region, 25 studies met the eligibility criteria, encompassing 674,061 hypertensive patients, including 305,576 with uncontrolled hypertension. The pooled prevalence was 58.8% (95% CI: 51.5–66.0; I² = 99.96%) (Supplementary Fig. 6).

Given the high heterogeneity observed in this meta-analysis, we conducted sensitivity analyses for each WHO region. While these analyses resulted in a slight reduction in heterogeneity, levels remained high (Table 1).

Based on subgroup analysis by WHO region and study quality, there were 118 low-risk, 21 intermediate-risk, 47 high-risk, and 14 unknown-risk studies. The corresponding pooled prevalence estimates were: 54.4% (95% CI: 51.1–57.7), 51.9% (43.6–60.3), 58.0% (53.3–62.8), and 48.6% (39.4–57.8), respectively (Fig. 2).

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis estimated the global and regional prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension among hypertensive patients. Overall, the findings reveal a high global prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension at 54.6%, indicating that more than half of hypertensive patients worldwide do not achieve adequate blood pressure control. A potential reason for the high prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension is the high frequency of modifiable risk factors in this population, such as salt intake, smoking, physical inactivity, alcohol consumption, obesity, and poor treatment adherence [26, 35–38].

The results of a pooled analysis by the NCD Risk Factor Collaboration showed that in 2019, the hypertension control rates among females and males were 23% and 18%, respectively [10]. This finding indicates a high prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension (nearly 80%), which is greater than our estimate of 54.6%. The discrepancy may be due to differences in study design; our meta-analysis of cross-sectional studies differs from the aforementioned study. Nonetheless, the results of both studies indicate that the prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension is high in this patient population.

The prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension varied across WHO regions but not statistically significant. The Eastern Mediterranean region had the highest pooled prevalence at 60.0%, followed closely by the African region at 59.4% and the Western Pacific region at 58.8%. This high prevalence may be due to factors such as the distribution of risk factors for uncontrolled hypertension, socioeconomic disparities, and cultural factors that influence treatment adherence. In contrast, the Americas region and the South-East Asia region reported relatively lower prevalences of 49.7% and 44.2%, respectively. These differences may reflect variations in healthcare infrastructure, public health policies, and patient awareness across regions. The European region, with the largest number of included studies, reported a pooled prevalence of 53.7%, aligning closely with the global average. This suggests that even in regions with relatively advanced healthcare systems, uncontrolled hypertension remains a significant public health issue. Our finding emphasizes the persistent challenge of managing hypertension effectively, despite advancements in medical treatments and public health interventions.

A key factor contributing to the high prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension may be patient unawareness of their condition. Approximately half of hypertensive patients remain undiagnosed and consequently receive no antihypertensive treatment [39]. This lack of awareness likely stems from the asymptomatic nature of hypertension in most cases, making blood pressure measurement the only reliable detection method [40].

Furthermore, several systemic factors may explain regional variations in uncontrolled hypertension rates, including: disparities in healthcare access, variations in health literacy levels, differences in socioeconomic status, unequal health insurance coverage, and inequitable distribution of healthcare services [41–43]. Difference in the distribution of another risk factors of uncontrolled hypertension such as smoking, alcohol consumption, obesity and overweight, physical inactivity, marriage status of patients, high sodium intake, unhealthy diet, and comorbidities may be another reason of disparity in the prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension among regions of WHO [41, 44, 45].

The high heterogeneity observed across studies (I² >97% in all regions) highlights the diversity in study designs, sample size of included studies, populations, and methodologies [46]. Sensitivity analysis reduced heterogeneity only marginally, indicating that other factors, such as differences in diagnostic criteria, treatment protocols, and patient demographics, may contribute to the variability [46]. Unfortunately, we could not conduct subgroup analysis based on the mentioned factors. However, we conducted subgroup analysis based on the WHO regions and quality of studies, but we not reached to lower heterogeneity.

Subgroup analysis based on study quality revealed that the pooled prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension was highest in studies with a high risk of bias (58.0%) and lowest in studies with an unknown risk of bias (48.6%). This suggests that methodological quality may influence prevalence estimates, emphasizing the need for rigorous study designs and transparent reporting in future research.

Overall, the high prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension globally highlights the urgent need for targeted interventions to improve blood pressure control. Public health strategies should focus on enhancing patient education, promoting adherence to antihypertensive medications, and addressing barriers to healthcare access, particularly in regions with the highest prevalences. Therefore, targeted regional interventions is recommended, for example in high-prevalence regions (Africa, Eastern Mediterranean, Western Pacific, strengthen community-based screening programs using mobile health units or pharmacists to improve early detection, and implement culturally tailored education campaigns (e.g., via local religious leaders or social media) to address low awareness and salt-heavy diets are recommended. In lower-prevalence regions (Americas, South-East Asia), focus on treatment adherence program may be useful.

Policy makers should plan for universal access to affordable antihypertensives: Advocate for national essential medicine lists to include long-acting, low-cost generics (e.g., ARBs/ACE inhibitors). Performance-based financing such as incentivizes for primary health care providers for achieving BP control targets. Subsidize BP medications for low-income groups and integrate hypertension management into universal health coverage (UHC) schemes is another recommendation. Food policy interventions such as collaborate with governments to enforce salt reduction policies is recommended.

This study has several limitations. First, the high heterogeneity across the included studies limits the generalizability of our findings. Second, the full text of 44 articles was unavailable. Of these, we incorporated data from 14 studies based on their abstracts; however, we could not assess their methodological quality. The remaining 30 articles were excluded due to an inability to extract relevant data. Third, variations in the definition of uncontrolled hypertension across studies may have affected the accuracy of our pooled prevalence estimate.

Future research should focus on conducting large-scale, standardized studies to better understand the determinants of uncontrolled hypertension and evaluate the effectiveness of interventions. Additionally, exploring the role of socioeconomic, cultural, and behavioral factors in hypertension management could provide valuable insights for developing targeted strategies.

Conclusion

Based on the results of this systematic review and meta-analysis, the global prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension was high. Based on the WHO regions, the highest and lowest prevalence was related to Eastern Mediterranean and South-East Asia respectively. Our results highlighting gaps in treatment effectiveness and the need for targeted interventions. Findings underscore the urgency of improving hypertension management worldwide to reduce preventable mortality.

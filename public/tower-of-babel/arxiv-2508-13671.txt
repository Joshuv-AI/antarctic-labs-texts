Propagation of Singularities for the Damped Stochastic Klein-Gordon Equation
Hongyi Chen, Cheuk Yin Lee

Abstract
For the 1 + 1 1+1 dimensional damped stochastic Klein-Gordon equation, we show that random singularities associated with the law of the iterated logarithm exist and propogate in the same way as the stochastic wave equation. This provides evidence for possible connections to microlocal analysis, ie. the exact regularity and singularities described in this paper should admit wavefront set type descriptions whose propagation is determined by the highest order terms of the linear operator. Despite the results being exactly the same as those of the wave equation, our proofs are significantly different than the proofs for the wave equation. Miraculously, proving our results for the critically damped equation implies them for the general equation, which significantly simplifies the problem. Even after this simplification, many important parts of the proof are significantly different than (and we think are more intuitive from the PDE viewpoint compared to) existing proofs for the wave equation.

Introduction

We study the regularity of the one-dimensional damped stochastic Klein-Gordon equation

(\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\Box}+a\partial_{t}+m^{2})u(t,x)=\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},

(1)

interpreted in the sense of
[
Wal86
]
subject to initial conditions

Here

are known from the physics literature as the damping constant and mass,
\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\Box}=\partial_{tt}-\partial_{xx}
is the d’Alembertian in one space dimension, and

is Gaussian space-time white noise defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
.
Let
\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
denote the filtration generated by the noise
\dot{W}
.
Our main results are the following Theorems.

Theorem 1.1

(Law of the iterated logarithm)
.

For any fixed
t_{0}\geq 0
and

, there exists a constant

depending on
(t_{0},x_{0})
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{|u(t_{0}+h,x_{0}+h)-u(t_{0},x_{0})|}{\sqrt{h\log\log(1/h)}}=K_{1}\quad\text{a.s.}

(2)

Theorem 1.2

(Modulus of continuity)
.

Fix
w_{0}\geq 0
and
0<a_{1}<a_{2}
, and consider the line segment

\displaystyle I=\left\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}:\tfrac{t-x}{\sqrt{2}}=w_{0},\tfrac{t+x}{\sqrt{2}}\in[a_{1},a_{2}]\right\}.

(3)

Then, there exist constants

(w_{0},a_{1},a_{2})

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\to 0^{+}}\sup_{(t,x)\in J}\frac{|u(t+h,x+h)-u(t,x)|}{\sqrt{h\log(1/h)}}=K_{J}\quad\text{a.s.}

(4)

Theorem
1.1
states that at a fixed space-time point, the increments (along a characteristic direction) are locally of order

, while Theorem
1.2
asserts that the uniform modulus of continuity for the increments are of a larger order, at a logarithmic level.
Therefore, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

Theorem
1.4
below justifies the existence of these random singularities and shows that they propagate along the other characteristic direction.

One of the ingredients for proving Theorem
1.4
is the following simultaneous upper bound for the law of the iterated logarithm.
For notational convenience, we introduce a change of coordinates via a rotation by
-45^{\circ}

\displaystyle(w,z)=\left(\tfrac{t-x}{\sqrt{2}},\tfrac{t+x}{\sqrt{2}}\right),\qquad(t,x)=\left(\tfrac{w+z}{\sqrt{2}},\tfrac{-w+z}{\sqrt{2}}\right).

(5)

The
w
and

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
N>0
and
0<a_{1}<a_{2}
. Then, there exists a constant
0<K_{2}<\infty
such that for all fixed
z_{0}\in[a_{1},a_{2}]

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\limsup_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{|u\big(\frac{w+z_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+h,\frac{-w+z_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+h\big)-u\big(\frac{w+z_{0}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-w+z_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)|}{\sqrt{h\log\log(1/h)}}\leq K_{2}\text{ for all $w\in[0,N]$}\right\}=1.

(6)

Theorem 1.4

(Existence and propagation of singularities)
.

Fix
w_{0}\geq 0
,
0<z_{0}<z_{0}^{\prime}
. Let
t_{0}=w_{0}/\sqrt{2}
. Then:

(i)

There exists an
\mathscr{F}_{t_{0}}
-measurable random variable
Z=Z(\omega)
such that
z_{0}\leq Z\leq z_{0}^{\prime}
a.s. and

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{|u\big(\frac{w_{0}+Z}{\sqrt{2}}+h,\frac{-w_{0}+Z}{\sqrt{2}}+h\big)-u\big(\frac{w_{0}+Z}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-w_{0}+Z}{\sqrt{2}}\big)|}{\sqrt{h\log\log(1/h)}}=+\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

(7)

(ii)

If
Z
is any such random variable, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\limsup_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{|u\big(\frac{w+Z}{\sqrt{2}}+h,\frac{-w+Z}{\sqrt{2}}+h\big)-u\big(\frac{w+Z}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-w+Z}{\sqrt{2}}\big)|}{\sqrt{h\log\log(1/h)}}=+\infty\text{ for all $w>w_{0}$}\right\}=1.

(8)

In general one can define
\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\Box}=b^{2}\partial_{tt}-c^{2}\partial_{xx}

1.1
Literature review and motivation

The (Khinchin) law of the iterated logarithm (LIL, for short) was first stated by Khinchin in 1924
[
Khi24
]
as a description of the fluctuations of a symmetric random walk. Since Brownian motion is the limit of scaled random walks, the LIL for Brownian motion
[
Khi33
]
can be interpreted as both regularity and large time fluctuation statements. We will concentrate on the regularity interpretation, for which we note that it has since been generalized to stochastic differential equations and Brownian sheets
[
Zim72
,
OP73
]
. Importantly, local versions of the LIL with a different power of
h
in the denominator of (
2
) has been proven for fractional Brownian Motion and fractional Brownian sheets
[
Ore72
,
AX05
]
, which can be seen as a reflection of their regularity being higher or lower than Brownian motion. All of these have also been generalized to Gaussian processes, Gaussian random fields, and stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) driven by appropriate objects
[
MR06
,
MWX13
,
LX22
,
HL25
]
.

Another regularity property of Brownian motions, Levy’s modulus of continuity (MC)
[
Lév37
]
, is apparently at odds with the LIL since it divides by a larger denominator compared to the statement of LIL. This has also been generalized to Gaussian processes and SPDEs
[
MR06
,
MWX13
,
LX19
,
LX23
,
HL25
]
. Notably, another way the LIL and MC are different is that the LIL fixes a point in parameter space while MC allows variation of the base point in parameter space. This indicates the existence of random points at which local increments are exceptionally large and fail to satisfy LIL. We will call such points LIL-singularities.
Exceptional increments were first studied for the Brownian motion by Orey and Taylor
[
OT74
]
who showed that the set of LIL-singularities is dense, uncountable, and has Hausdorff dimension 1, and also studied different types of singularities.
Similar results are known for Brownian sheets, general Gaussian processes, and SPDEs
[
Wal82
,
Wal86
,
KS00
,
KPX00
,
HK17
]
.
In particular, Walsh
[
Wal82
]
studied the LIL-singularities of a 2-parameter Brownian sheet and proved that singularities along an axis direction propagate perpendicularly along the other axis direction.
Walsh modified his argument to show that LIL-singularities of stochastic wave equation propagate along characteristic directions
[
Wal86
]
.
These results have been extended to semi-fractional Brownian sheets
[
BM08
]
and more general stochastic wave equations
[
CN88
,
LX22
]
.

Interpreting LIL singularities as local statements about behavior near a point hints at possible connections to microlocal analysis
[
Sat70
,
Hör71
,
Hör73
,
Vas11
,
Hin25
]
, which encompasses the sharpest propagation of singularities literature for deterministic equations. In microlocal analysis, singularities are characterized using wavefront sets. Informally, a point
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
(viewed as the cotangent bundle of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) is an element of the (smooth) wavefront set of some Schwartz distribution
\phi
if
\phi
is not smooth in the direction
\xi
at
x
. The celebrated paper
[
Hör78
]
proved that for solutions of evolution equations governed by some linear operator
P
, wavefront sets of initial conditions and forcing terms evolve according to the Hamiltonian flow of
P
, which is entirely determined by its highest order terms. The simplest application of this result is that equations of the form
(\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\Box}+P_{L})u=0
, where
P_{L}
is an operator of order
<2
, propagate singularities in the same way as
\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\Box}u=0
. Notably, it also applies to operators with variable coefficients. With physical motivations, the definition of the wavefront set and the result of
[
Hör78
]
has been extended to Sobolev norms with arbitrary (including negative) regularity
[
SL82
,
Den82
]
, and most recently, to Besov norms
[
DRS22
]
. For concrete examples, the
H^{s}
-Sobolev wavefront set (here
H^{s},s\in\mathbb{R}
is the standard
L^{2}-
based Sobolev space) of
\delta_{x}
is empty if
s<-\frac{n}{2}
and is equal to the nonzero cotangent space at
x
if
s\geq-\frac{n}{2}
, and the
H^{s}
-Sobolev wavefront set of
\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{D}}
for a smooth domain
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is empty for
s<\frac{1}{2}
and is equal to the nonzero normal bundle of
\partial_{D}
if
s\geq\frac{1}{2}

[
Hin25
, Example 7.37]
. Due to the diffeomorphism invariance of wavefront sets, all of this theory can be extended to smooth manifolds, which has been used in mathematical general relativity for both stability and singularity generation
[
HV18
,
Hin21
,
HHV21
]
.

We conjecture that as regularity statements, an order
\beta
modulus of continuity(
h^{\beta}\log\frac{1}{h}
in the denominator of Theorem
1.2
) or law of iterated logarithm(
h^{\beta}\log\log\frac{1}{h}
in the denominator of Theorem
1.1
) holding at some point
x
for some distribution
\phi
can be characterized as the cotangent space at
x
not belonging to a ”
\beta
-MC wavefront set” or ”
\beta
-LIL wavefront set”, while
\beta
-LIL singularities would correspond to the existence of a random element of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, which we can interpret as a random ”
\beta
-LIL wavefront set”. The basis for our conjecture is the well-known correspondence between small scale and high frequency behavior, which is the intuition behind the Sobolev and Besov wavefront set definitions. Furthermore, we also believe that once the definition of a
\beta
-MC/LIL wavefront set has been written down, it would be valid for
\beta
negative, which would allow for investigation of these properties beyond random fields. We also conjecture that
[
Hör78
]
can be extended to these MC/LIL-wavefront sets(the LIL extension would be more interesting considering Theorem
1.4
). Affirmative answers to our conjectures would not only greatly extend all current results about MC, LIL, and LIL-type singularities, but also can be relevant for many other areas of continuous probability. For instance, it would allow for more precise regularity descriptions and even speak of pointwise properties of many objects of active study such as white noise, Gaussian free fields, LQG measures, and solutions of renormalized SPDEs, all of which are known to have negative regularity.

The current paper can be seen as evidence for the propagation part of the conjectures mentioned earlier. Specifically, we choose to analyze (
1
) and aim to show that for the relevant properties we know are true for
a,m=0

[
Wal86
,
CN88
,
LX22
]
would hold for general
a,m\in\mathbb{R}
. This would thus prove the propagation part of the above conjecture in the case of space-time white noise forcing and the highest order part of the operator being the wave operator
\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\Box}
. For readers interested in the physical motivation for the Klein-Gordon equation as well as the addition of the damping term and the noisy forcing term, we refer to
[
BMS05
,
Son+20
]
and the references therein. A further consequence of extending
[
Hör78
]
in the above conjectures is that since the characteristic set of the heat operator
\partial_{t}-(\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\bigtriangleup}+m^{2})
is empty, the Edwards-Wilkinson equation

(\partial_{t}-\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\bigtriangleup}-m^{2})v(t,x)=\dot{W}(t,x)

(9)

will not propagate its singularities, which are known to exist in
1+1
dimensions
[
HL25
]
. Our main results provide heuristics for this in
1+1
dimensions using the Smolchowski-
Kramers approximation

(\mu^{2}\partial_{tt}+\partial_{t}-\partial_{xx}-m^{2})v_{\mu}(t,x)=\dot{W}(t,x),\quad\mu\in(0,1]

(10)

where one will see (
9
) as
\mu\downarrow 0
(we refer interested readers to
[
CF06
,
CS16
,
CX23
]
for the stability of this approximation and related topics). For
\mu>0
, one can easily adopt the contents of this paper to see that Theorems
1.1
–
1.4
all apply to
v_{\mu}
with appropriately tilted characteristic lines. The effect of tilting the characteristic lines as
\mu
gets smaller is that for any singularity of
v_{\mu}
at some point
(t_{0},x_{0})
, the propagated singularity of part (ii) of Theorem
1.4
is at
(t_{0}+\varepsilon,x_{0}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})
at time
t_{0}+\varepsilon
, which notably goes to infinity as

. This is consistent with the PDE heuristic that hyperbolic operators have finite speed of propagation while parabolic operators have infinite speed of propagation, so
\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\Box}_{\mu}:=\mu^{2}\partial_{tt}-\partial_{xx}
indexes a family of hyperbolic operators whose speed of propagation goes to infinity as

.

1.2
Paper organization and notation

The rest of the paper will be organized as follows. In section 2 we state our Fourier transform convention and prove pointwise and increment second moment bounds for our solution
u
. In section 3, we show that proving any of our main theorems in the case of the critically damped equation actually implies the same theorem for all damped equations. The rest of the paper is then dedicated to proving the main theorems for the critically damped equations. Notably, the proofs of Theorem
1.3
and the propagation part of Theorem
1.4
are significantly different compared to existing proofs of analogous results for the wave equation, despite the simplification of section 3. In fact, one can see directly in the propagation proof that damping does not erase the singularity.

We list some notation we will employ for the rest of the paper.

•

Sets:
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,\infty)
,
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
,
\mathbb{N}_{+}=\{1,2,\dots\}

•

a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}
;
\mathbb{1}_{A}
denotes indicator function of a set
A

•

f(x)\propto g(x)
means that there exists
0<C<\infty
such that

for all

, and

means that there exists

such that

for all

. In one calculation, we may write these in succession without mentioning that the constants change from line to line, and we will only note dependence of the constants on certain parameters if we deem it relevant to do so.

Preliminaries

For notational clarity, we choose a Fourier transform convention and state some basic theorems in it.

Definition 2.1
.

For

, define its
Fourier transform
by

\mathcal{F}[f](\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}dx.

The following are standard results.

1.

Translation:
\mathcal{F}[f(\cdot-a)](\xi)=e^{-ia\xi}\hat{f}(\xi)
.

2.

Differentiation:
\mathcal{F}\left[\derivative[k]{f}{x}\right]=(-i\xi)^{k}\hat{f}(\xi).

3.

Parseval-Plancherel identity:
\braket{f,g}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\braket{\hat{f},\hat{g}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
.

4.

Convolution: define
f*g(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x-y)g(y)dy
. Then
\mathcal{F}[f*g](\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\xi).

The solution to (

The existence and regularity of

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{G}(t,\xi)=&\,\frac{e^{-at/2}\sin\left(t\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-\frac{a^{2}}{4}}\right)}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-\frac{a^{2}}{4}}}\mathbb{1}_{\set{\xi^{2}>\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}}}\\
&+\frac{e^{-at/2}\sinh{\left(t\sqrt{m^2-\frac{a^2}{4}-\xi^2}\right)}}{\sqrt{m^{2}-\frac{a^{2}}{4}-\xi^{2}}}\mathbb{1}_{\set{\xi^{2}<\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}}}.\end{split}

Observe that the small frequency regime exists if and only if

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[e^{-as}\frac{\sin^{2}\left(s\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-\frac{a^{2}}{4}}\right)}{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-\frac{a^{2}}{4}}\mathbb{1}_{\set{\xi^{2}>\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}}}\right.

\displaystyle\,I_{1}+I_{2},

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|\xi|<K}e^{-as}\frac{\sinh^{2}{\left(s\sqrt{K^{2}-\xi^{2}}\right)}}{K^{2}-\xi^{2}}d\xi ds,\quad I_{2}:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|\xi|>K}e^{-as}\frac{\sin^{2}(s\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}})}{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}.

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle+\Bigg[\sinh((t-\tau)\sqrt{K^2-\xi^2})\absolutevalue{e^{-\frac{a}{2}(t-\tau)}-e^{-\frac{a}{2}(s-\tau)}}\Bigg]^{2}\frac{d\xi d\tau}{K^{2}-\xi^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\absolutevalue{\xi}<K}\Bigg[\sqrt{K^{2}-\xi^{2}}(t-s)\cosh((t-s))\sqrt{K^{2}-\xi^{2}})+\sinh((t-\tau)\sqrt{K^2-\xi^2})(t-s)\frac{a}{2}\Bigg]^{2}\frac{d\xi d\tau}{K^{2}-\xi^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,s(t-s)^{2}\int_{\absolutevalue{\xi}<K}\frac{[\sqrt{K^{2}-\xi^{2}}\cosh((t-s))\sqrt{K^{2}-\xi^{2}})+\sinh((t-s)\sqrt{K^2-\xi^2})\frac{a}{2}]^{2}}{K^{2}-\xi^{2}}d\xi.

The third inequality used the mean value theorem and the fact that both
\exp
and
\cosh
are increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The singularities of the integrand at
\xi=0,\pm K
are integrable by an elementary calculus argument.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle J_{2}\propto

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\int_{K<|\xi|<L}\left[e^{-\frac{a(t-\tau)}{2}}\frac{\sin\left((t-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{K^{2}-\xi^{2}}}-e^{-\frac{a(s-\tau)}{2}}\frac{\sin\left((s-\tau)\sqrt{K^{2}-\xi^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}}\right]^{2}d\xi d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2\int_{0}^{s}\int_{K<|\xi|<L}\Bigg[e^{-\frac{a}{2}(s-\tau)}\absolutevalue{\sin((t-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^2-K^2})-\sin((s-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^2-K^2})}\Bigg]^{2}

\displaystyle+\Bigg[\sin((t-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^2-K^2})\absolutevalue{e^{-\frac{a}{2}(t-\tau)}-e^{-\frac{a}{2}(s-\tau)}}\Bigg]^{2}\frac{d\xi d\tau}{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\int_{K<|\xi|<L}\Bigg[\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}(t-s)+\sin((t-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^2-K^2})(t-s)\frac{a}{2}\Bigg]^{2}\frac{d\xi d\tau}{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,s(t-s)^{2}\int_{K<\absolutevalue{\xi}<L}\frac{[\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}(t-s)+\sin((t-s)\sqrt{\xi^2-K^2})\frac{a}{2}]^{2}}{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}d\xi.

Again the singularity at
K
is integrable by elementary arguments.

For
J_{3}
, we split it into two parts as we did above.
For the first term, use the trigonometric identity
\sin(A)-\sin(B)=2\cos(\frac{A+B}{2})\sin(\frac{A-B}{2})
and bound the
\cos
factor by 1.
For the second term, bound
\sin
by 1 and apply mean value theorem to the difference of the exponential functions.
This yields the following:

\displaystyle J_{3}\propto

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\absolutevalue{\xi}>L}\left[e^{-\frac{a(t-\tau)}{2}}\frac{\sin\left((t-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}}-e^{-\frac{a(s-\tau)}{2}}\frac{\sin\left((s-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}}\right]^{2}d\xi d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2\int_{0}^{s}\int_{|\xi|>L}\Bigg[e^{-\frac{a}{2}(s-\tau)}\absolutevalue{\sin((t-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^2-K^2})-\sin((s-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^2-K^2})}\Bigg]^{2}

\displaystyle+\Bigg[\sin((t-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^2-K^2})\absolutevalue{e^{-\frac{a}{2}(t-\tau)}-e^{-\frac{a}{2}(s-\tau)}}\Bigg]^{2}\frac{d\xi d\tau}{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2\int_{0}^{s}\int_{|\xi|>L}\Bigg[4\sin^{2}((t-s)\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}})+a^{2}(t-s)^{2}\sin^{2}((t-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}})\Bigg]\frac{d\xi d\tau}{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\int_{|\xi|>L}\frac{\sin^{2}((t-s)\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}})}{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}d\xi d\tau+(t-s)^{2}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{|\xi|>L}\frac{\sin^{2}((t-\tau)\sqrt{\xi^{2}-K^{2}})}{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}d\xi d\tau=:S_{1}+S_{2}.

Since
L>K
, we have
S_{2}\lesssim s(t-s)^{2}
.
For
S_{1}
, we use the change of variable
\xi\mapsto\xi/(t-s)
, giving us

\displaystyle S_{1}=

\displaystyle\,s(t-s)\int_{(t-s)L}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^{2}(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\xi^{2}-(t-s)^{2}K^{2}})}{\xi^{2}-(t-s)^{2}K^{2}}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,s(t-s)\int_{(t-s)L}^{\infty}\frac{1\wedge(\xi^{2}-(t-s)^{2}K^{2})}{\xi^{2}-(t-s)^{2}K^{2}}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,s(t-s)\left(\int_{(t-s)L}^{2TL}1d\xi+\int_{2TL}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}/2}d\xi\right)\propto s(t-s).

This finishes the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\mathbb{E}[|u(t,x)-u(t,y)|^{2}]\lesssim t|x-y|,

where the implicit constant depends only on
T
,
a
,
m
.

Proof.

We can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\absolutevalue{u(t,x)-u(t,y)}^{2}]=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[G(s,x-z)-G(s,y-z)]^{2}dzds

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{e^{-ix\xi}-e^{iy\xi}}^{2}\hat{G}(s,\xi)^{2}d\xi ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(4\wedge(|x-y|^{2}|\xi|^{2})\right)\hat{G}(s,\xi)^{2}d\xi ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1/|x-y|}|x-y|^{2}|\xi|^{2}\hat{G}(s,\xi)^{2}d\xi ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1/|x-y|}^{\infty}|\hat{G}(s,\xi)|^{2}d\xi ds.

The bound
|\hat{G}(s,\xi)|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}
for all
\xi>0
gives

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1/|x-y|}^{\infty}|\hat{G}(s,\xi)|^{2}d\xi ds\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1/|x-y|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}d\xi ds\leq t|x-y|.

If
|x-y|>1
, then
|\xi|<1/|x-y|
implies
|\xi|<1
. This together with
|\hat{G}(s,\xi)|\lesssim 1
for
|\xi|<1
gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1/|x-y|}|x-y|^{2}|\xi|^{2}\hat{G}(s,\xi)^{2}d\xi ds\lesssim|x-y|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1/|x-y|}d\xi ds=t|x-y|.

Finally, if
|x-y|\leq 1
, we use
\int_{0}^{1/|x-y|}=\int_{0}^{1}+\int_{1}^{1/|x-y|}.

The first estimate and
|\xi|<1
then gives

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|x-y|^{2}|\xi|^{2}\hat{G}(s,\xi)^{2}d\xi ds\lesssim|x-y|^{2}t^{2}.

For
|\xi|>1
, we again use
|\hat{G}(s,\xi)|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}
, so

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{1/|x-y|}|x-y|^{2}|\xi|^{2}\hat{G}(s,\xi)^{2}d\xi ds\lesssim t(|x-y|^{2}-|x-y|)\lesssim t|x-y|.

This finishes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

Combining Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
, and applying the Kolmogorov continuity theorem, we obtain:

Proposition 2.5
.

For any
T>0
, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|u(t,x)-u(s,y)|^{2}]\leq C(|t-s|+|x-y|)

(13)

uniformly for all
s,t\in[0,T]
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
Hence, the sample function
(t,x)\mapsto u(t,x)
is a.s. continuous on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
and locally Hölder of order
1/2-
in both
t
and
x
.

3
Reduction to the critical damping case

We now explain how the proofs of the main theorems can be reduced to the critical damping case
m^{2}=a^{2}/4
. Note that this simplification immediately implies all of the main theorems if
a=0
by
[
Wal86
,
CN88
,
LX22
]
, so for all future sections we will assume
a>0
to avoid triviality.
First, re-write equation (
1
) as:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\Box}+a\partial_{t}+a^{2}/4)u(t,x)=\dot{W}(t,x)+b(u(t,x)),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=\partial_{t}u(0,x)=0,\end{cases}

(14)

where
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the function defined by

\displaystyle b(x)=(a^{2}/4-m^{2})x.

Since
b
is globally Lipschitz and grows linearly, standard existence and uniqueness theory implies that the solution
u
to (
1
) is also the unique mild solution to (
14
) which solves the integral equation

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t-s,x-y)W(ds,dy)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t-s,x-y)b(u(s,y))dyds,\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},

(15)

where
\Gamma
is the fundamental solution to
\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\Box}+a\partial_{t}+a^{2}/4
.
Notice that in this case, we have by (
12
)

\hat{\Gamma}(t,\xi)=\frac{e^{-at/2}\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\quad\text{and hence}\quad\Gamma(t,x)=\frac{e^{-at/2}}{2}\mathbb{1}_{\{|x|<t\}}.

(16)

As mentioned in the preliminaries, this special case is referred to as critical damping and significantly simplifies our problem. To show that the simplification is valid, we write (
15
) as

\displaystyle u(t,x)=u_{C}(t,x)+u_{L}(t,x),\text{ where }

(17)

\displaystyle u_{C}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t-s,x-y)W(ds,dy),

(18)

\displaystyle u_{L}(t,x)=(a^{2}/4-m^{2})\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t-s,x-y)u(s,y)dyds.

(19)

In particular,
u_{C}
is the mild solution to (
1
) with
m^{2}=a^{2}/4
.
In the following lemma, we show that
u_{L}
is a.s. locally Lipschitz (along characteristic direction).

Lemma 3.1
.

For any fixed compact set
F\subset[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in[0,1]}\sup_{(t,x)\in F}\frac{|u_{L}(t+h,x+h)-u_{L}(t,x)|}{h}\leq C\sup_{(s,y)\in\mathscr{C}(F)}|u(s,y)|<+\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

(20)

where
\mathscr{C}(F)=\bigcup_{h\in[0,1]}\bigcup_{(t,x)\in F}\{(s,y)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}:|x+h-y|\leq t+h-s\}
.

Proof.

We first apply absolute value to the integrand to see that

\displaystyle|u_{L}(t+h,x+h)-u_{L}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq|\tfrac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\Gamma(t+h-s,x+h-y)\mathbb{1}_{\{|x+h-y|\leq t+h-s\}}-\Gamma(t-s,x-y)\mathbb{1}_{\{|x-y|\leq t-s\}}\right||u(s,y)|dyds

\displaystyle\leq|\tfrac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}|\sup_{(s,y)\in\mathscr{C}(F)}|u(s,y)|\,(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}),

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{x-(t-s)}^{x+(t-s)}|\Gamma(t+h-s,x+h-y)-\Gamma(t-s,x-y)|dyds,

\displaystyle I_{2}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{x+(t-s)}^{x+h+(t+h-s)}\Gamma(t+h-s,x+h-y)dyds,

\displaystyle I_{3}=\int_{t}^{t+h}\int_{x+h-(t+h-s)}^{x+h+(t+h-s)}\Gamma(t+h-s,x+h-y)dyds.

Then, we may use (
16
) to estimate these three terms easily:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{x-(t-s)}^{x+(t-s)}(e^{-a(t-s)/2}-e^{-a(t+h-s)})dyds=(1-e^{-ah/2})\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)e^{-a(t-s)/2}ds\lesssim h,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{x+(t-s)}^{x+(t-s)+2h}e^{-a(t+h-s)/2}dyds\leq h\int_{0}^{t}e^{-a(t-s)/2}ds\leq h,

\displaystyle I_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{t+h}\int_{x-(t-s)}^{x+(t-s)+2h}e^{-a(t+h-s)}dyds\leq h^{2}.

Finally, we finish the proof using the property that
u
is a.s. bounded on the compact set
\mathscr{C}(F)
, due to the a.s. continuity of
u
(see Proposition
2.5
).
∎

y_{+}-y_{-}=(y_{+}+i\frac{a}{2})-(y_{-}+i\frac{a}{2})
, we have by the substitution
\tilde{y}_{\pm}=y_{\pm}+i\frac{a}{2}

|F(t,x,\tau,\xi)|=\frac{|\tilde{\phi}_{t}(\tilde{y}_{+})-\tilde{\phi}_{t}(\tilde{y}_{-})|}{|\tilde{y}_{+}-\tilde{y}_{-}|},\text{ where }\tilde{\phi}_{t}(\tilde{y})=\frac{e^{it\tilde{y}}-1}{\tilde{y}}.

Note here that
\tilde{\phi}_{t}
is a map
\mathbb{C}\supset\mathbb{R}+i\frac{a}{2}\to\mathbb{C}
, so we need not worry about
\tilde{y}=0
. However, it is easy to show using the elementary arguments that

\sup_{(t,\tilde{y})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}+i\frac{a}{2}}\max(\tilde{\phi}_{t}(\tilde{y}),\tilde{\phi}_{t}^{\prime}(\tilde{y}))\lesssim\frac{1}{1+|\tilde{y}|}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+|y|}.

Thus, the elementary arguments involving the mean value theorem in the proof of
[
DMX15
, Lemma 9.4(b)]
) give us

\frac{|\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{y}_{+})-\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{y}_{-})|}{2|y_{+}-y_{-}|}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+2|y_{+}-y_{-}|}\left[\frac{1}{1+|y_{+}|}+\frac{1}{1+|y_{-}|}\right],

which completes the proof of part (a).

For part (b), we write

\displaystyle|F(t,x,\tau,\xi)-F(t^{\prime},x^{\prime},\tau,\xi)|=

\displaystyle|F(t,x,\tau,\xi)-F(t^{\prime},x,\tau,\xi)+F(t^{\prime},x,\tau,\xi)-F(t^{\prime},x^{\prime},\tau,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|F(t,x,\tau,\xi)-F(t^{\prime},x,\tau,\xi)|+|F(t^{\prime},x,\tau,\xi)-F(t^{\prime},x^{\prime},\tau,\xi)|

from which we will apply the mean value theorem in the first and second variables of
F
, which will conclude the proof. For the space increment, we compute

|\partial_{x}F|=|i\xi F|\leq\left[\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2}|\tau+|\xi||}+\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2}|\tau-|\xi||}\right]

Where the inequality is an application of part (a). Then the mean value theorem gives us

|F(t^{\prime},x,\tau,\xi)-F(t^{\prime},x^{\prime},\tau,\xi)|\leq|x-x^{\prime}|\left[\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2}|\tau+|\xi||}+\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2}|\tau-|\xi||}\right].

(25)

For the time increment, we use
\partial_{t}f(t,z)=e^{tz}
to compute

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F=

\displaystyle-i\tau F+\frac{e^{-it\tau+ix\xi}}{2i|\xi|}[\partial_{t}f(t,z_{+})-\partial_{t}f(t,z_{-})]=-i\tau F+\frac{e^{-it\tau+ix\xi}}{2i|\xi|}[e^{tz_{+}}-e^{tz_{-}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-it\tau+ix\xi}}{2i|\xi|}\left[-i\tau(f(t,z_{+})-f(t,z_{-}))+e^{tz_{+}}-e^{tz_{-}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-it\tau+ix\xi}}{2i|\xi|}[-i\tau(f(t,z_{+})-f(t,z_{-}))+z_{+}f(t,z_{+})-1-(z_{-}f(t,z_{+})-1)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-it\tau+ix\xi}}{2i|\xi|}[(z_{+}-i\tau)f(t,z_{+})-(z_{-}-i\tau)f(t,z_{-})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-it\tau+ix\xi}}{2i|\xi|}[(z_{+}-i\tau)f(t,z_{+})-(z_{-}-i\tau)f(t,z_{+})+(z_{-}-i\tau)f(t,z_{+})-(z_{-}-i\tau)f(t,z_{-})]

The fact
|\xi|=|z_{+}-z_{-}|
gives us

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}F|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|z_{+}-z_{-}||f(t,z_{+})|}{2|z_{+}-z_{-}|}+|z_{-}-i\tau|\frac{|f(t,z_{+})-f(t,z_{-})|}{2|z_{+}-z_{-}|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|f(t,z_{+})|+C\frac{|f(t,z_{+})-f(t,z_{-})|}{2|z_{+}-z_{-}|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{|(1-e^{-\frac{at}{2}})+e^{i(\tau+|\xi|)}|}{|i(\tau+|\xi|)+\frac{a}{2}|}+C\frac{|f(t,z_{+})-f(t,z_{-})|}{2|z_{+}-z_{-}|}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2}|\tau+|\xi||}+\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2}|\tau-|\xi||}.

For the last inequality above, the second term is again an application of part (a), and the argument for the first term is elementary. This gives us

|F(t,x,\tau,\xi)-F(t^{\prime},x,\tau,\xi)|\lesssim|t-t^{\prime}|\left[\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2}|\tau+|\xi||}+\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2}|\tau-|\xi||}\right],

which finishes the proof.

Lemma 5.1
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(u(t,x)-u(t-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\sqrt{2}},x+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\sqrt{2}})-u(t-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},x-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}})+u(t-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},x+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}})\right)^{2}\right]\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}

,

and
0<\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}<t
.

Proof.

By Itô-Walsh isometry, the left hand side of the desired inequality is

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\Gamma(t,x)-\Gamma(t-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\sqrt{2}},x+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\sqrt{2}})-\Gamma(t-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},x-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}})+\Gamma(t-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},x+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}})\right)^{2}dyds.

For this proof, we will employ the simpler notations

In the following, we define some subsets of the open half plane
\set{y\in\mathbb{R},s>0}
given
x\in\mathbb{R},0<\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}<t
from the statement of the lemma.

\displaystyle R:=\set{}{x-y|<t-s},\quad R_{1}:=\left(R\setminus\Set{\left}{x-\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}-y}\right)\cap\Set{\left}{x+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}-y}

\displaystyle R_{12}:=R\cap\Set{\left}{x+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}-y}\cap\Set{\left}{x-\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}-y},\quad\tilde{R}_{12}:=R\setminus(R_{1}\cup R_{2}\cup R_{12}).

We now list some useful observations about the above sets.

1.

R
is a disjoint union of
R_{1},R_{2},R_{12}
, and
\tilde{R}_{12}
. It is the support of
\Gamma
and contains the support of
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
, and
\Gamma_{12}
.

2.

\tilde{R}_{12}
has area
\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}
and only
\Gamma
is nonzero on it.

3.

R_{1}
has area smaller than
\varepsilon_{2}T
and only
G
and
\Gamma_{1}
are nonzero on it. Similarly,
R_{2}
has area smaller than
\varepsilon_{1}T
and only
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{2}
are nonzero on it.

4.

has area no larger than
\frac{1}{2}(t-\varepsilon_{2}-\varepsilon_{2})^{2}<T^{2}
and
\Gamma,\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
and
\Gamma_{12}
are all nonzero on it.

\tilde{I}_{12}+I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{12}

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{12}:=

\displaystyle\iint_{\tilde{R}_{12}}\Gamma^{2}dyds,\quad I_{1}:=\iint_{R_{1}}(\Gamma-\Gamma_{1})^{2}dyds,

\displaystyle I_{2}:=

\displaystyle\iint_{R_{2}}(\Gamma-\Gamma_{2})^{2}dyds,\text{ and }I_{12}:=\iint_{R_{12}}(\Gamma-\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}+\Gamma_{12})^{2}dyds.

We will now show that all the integrals above are
\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}

For
\tilde{I}_{12}

Hölder with the boundedness of the integrand and the area of
\tilde{R}_{12}
gives the result.

For

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{2}T\max_{s\in[0,t-\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}]}\left|e^{-\frac{a}{2}(t-s)}-e^{-\frac{a}{2}(t-\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}-s)}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{2}\left|1-e^{-\frac{a\varepsilon_{1}}{2\sqrt{2}}}\right|\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}.

A similar argument works for

. This leaves us with

\displaystyle I_{12}

\displaystyle\leq T^{2}\max_{s\in[0,t-\frac{\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}]}\left|e^{-\frac{a}{2}(t-s)}+e^{-\frac{a}{2}(t-\frac{\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}-s)}-(e^{-\frac{a}{2}(t-\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}-s)}+e^{-\frac{a}{2}(t-\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}-s)})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|1+e^{\frac{a}{2}(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}})}-(e^{\frac{a}{2}(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\sqrt{2}})}+e^{\frac{a}{2}(\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{2}})})\right|.

If one Taylor expands the exponential function, one can easily see that the term inside the last absolute value is

.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{x-(t-s)}^{x+(t-s)}(e^{-a(t+h-s)/2}-e^{-a(t-s)/2})W(ds,dy)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{x+(t-s)}^{x+(t-s)+2h}e^{-a(t+h-s)/2}W(ds,dy)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-at/2}(e^{-ah/2}-1)}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{-\sqrt{2}w+s}^{\sqrt{2}z-s}e^{as/2}W(ds,dy)+\frac{e^{-a(t+h)/2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\sqrt{2}z-s}^{\sqrt{2}z-s+2h}e^{as/2}W(ds,dy)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\frac{a(w+z)}{2\sqrt{2}}}(e^{-ah/2}-1)}{2}X(w,z)+\frac{e^{-\frac{a(w+z)}{2\sqrt{2}}}e^{-ah/2}}{2}(Y(z+\sqrt{2}h)-Y(z)),

(30)

where

\displaystyle X(w,z)=\int_{-\sqrt{2}w+s}^{\sqrt{2}z-s}e^{as/2}W(ds,dy),\qquad Y(z)=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{-s}^{\sqrt{2}z-s}e^{as/2}W(ds,dy)

(31)

We first establish some basic properties for the processes
X
and
Y
.

Lemma 6.1
.

X=\{X(w,z)\}_{(w,z)\in[w_{0},w_{0}^{\prime}]\times[z_{0},z_{0}^{\prime}]}
is a centered Gaussian random field with

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(X(w,z)-X(w^{\prime},z^{\prime}))\lesssim|w-w^{\prime}|+|z-z^{\prime}|

(32)

uniformly for all
(w,z),(w^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in[w_{0},w_{0}^{\prime}]\times[z_{0},z_{0}^{\prime}]
.
Hence
X
is a.s. continuous on
[w_{0},w_{0}^{\prime}]\times[z_{0},z_{0}^{\prime}]
.

Proof.

Consider
w>w^{\prime}
in
[w_{0},w_{0}^{\prime}]
and
z,z^{\prime}
in
[z_{0},z_{0}^{\prime}]
.
Assuming that
z>z^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(X(w,z)-X(w^{\prime},z^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq 2\operatorname{Var}(X(w,z)-X(w,z^{\prime}))+2\operatorname{Var}(X(w,z^{\prime})-X(w^{\prime},z^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\sqrt{2}z^{\prime}-s}^{\sqrt{2}z-s}e^{as}dyds+2\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{-\sqrt{2}w+s}^{-\sqrt{2}w^{\prime}+s}e^{as}dyds

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{2}(z-z^{\prime})\int_{0}^{t_{0}}e^{as}ds+2\sqrt{2}(w-w^{\prime})\int_{0}^{t_{0}}e^{as}ds\lesssim|z-z^{\prime}|+|w-w^{\prime}|.

The case of
z<z^{\prime}
is similar.
This proves (
32
).
The continuity of
X
follows from a standard application of the Kolmogorov continuity theorem.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

\{C_{0}^{-1}Y(z)\}_{z\geq 0}
is a standard Brownian motion, where
C_{0}=\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}e^{as}ds
.

Proof.

For any
0\leq z<z^{\prime}
, by Itô-Walsh isometry,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(Y(z^{\prime})-Y(z))=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\sqrt{2}z-s}^{\sqrt{2}z^{\prime}-s}e^{as}dyds=\sqrt{2}(z^{\prime}-z)\int_{0}^{t_{0}}e^{as}ds=C_{0}(z^{\prime}-z).

By the Kolmogorov continuity theorem,
Y
is a.s. continuous.
This shows that
B(z):=C_{0}^{-1}Y(z)
is a centered, continuous Gaussian process with
B(0)=0
and
\operatorname{Var}(B(z^{\prime})-B(z))=|z^{\prime}-z|
, and hence is a standard Brownian motion.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.
As before, in order to simplify notations, we write

\displaystyle\ell(w,z)=\limsup_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{|u\big(\frac{w+z}{\sqrt{2}}+h,\frac{-w+z}{\sqrt{2}}+h\big)-u\big(\frac{w+z}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-w+z}{\sqrt{2}}\big)|}{\sqrt{h\log\log(1/h)}},

\displaystyle\ell_{i}(w,z)=\limsup_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{|u_{i}\big(\frac{w+z}{\sqrt{2}}+h,\frac{-w+z}{\sqrt{2}}+h\big)-u_{i}\big(\frac{w+z}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-w+z}{\sqrt{2}}\big)|}{\sqrt{h\log\log(1/h)}},\quad i=1,2.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Fix an integer
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, and take
w_{0}^{\prime}=w_{0}+N
.
The processes
X
and
Y
defined in (
31
) are both
\mathscr{F}_{t_{0}}
-measurable.
By Lemma
6.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{w\in[w_{0},w_{0}+N]}\sup_{z\in[z_{0},z_{0}^{\prime}]}|X(w,z)|<+\infty\right\}=1.

Since
|1-e^{-ah/2}|\lesssim h
for
h>0
small, the preceding implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\limsup_{h\to 0^{+}}\sup_{(w,z)\in[w_{0},w_{0}+N]\times[z_{0},z_{0}^{\prime}]}\frac{e^{-\frac{a(w+z)}{2\sqrt{2}}}|e^{-ah/2}-1||X(w,z)|}{\sqrt{h\log\log(1/h)}}=0\right\}=1.

(33)

According to Lemma
6.2
,
C_{0}^{-1}Y(z)
is a standard Brownian motion.
Hence, Lévy’s modulus of continuity theorem implies that

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z\in[z_{0},z_{0}^{\prime}]}\frac{|Y(z+\sqrt{2}h)-Y(z)|}{\sqrt{h\log(1/h)}}=C_{0}2^{3/4}\quad\text{a.s.}

(34)

With (

), we may use Meyer’s section theorem and an argument with nested intervals as in
[

]
to obtain an

-measurable random variable

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\limsup_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{|Y(Z+\sqrt{2}h)-Y(Z)|}{\sqrt{h\log\log(1/h)}}=+\infty\right\}=1.

By (

In particular, the above holds for

.
Hence, we may apply (

) and (

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\ell_{1}(w_{0},Z)=+\infty\right\}=1.

(37)

On the other hand, observe that

\displaystyle\{u_{2}(t_{0}+t,x)\}_{(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}}\text{ has the same law as }\{u(t,x)\}_{(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}}.

(39)

Thanks to (

) and (

\displaystyle\ell(w_{0},Z)\geq\ell_{1}(w_{0},Z)-\ell_{2}(w_{0},Z)\geq+\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

Uniform-in-time propagation of chaos for the Cucker–Smale model
N. J. Gerber a,, U. Vaes b,

Abstract
This paper presents an elementary proof of quantitative uniform-in-time propagation of chaos for the Cucker–Smale model under sufficiently strong interaction. The idea is to combine existing finite-time propagation of chaos estimates with existing uniform-in-time stability estimates for the interacting particle system, in order to obtain a uniform-in-time propagation of chaos estimate with an explicit rate of convergence in the number of particles. This is achieved via a method that is similar in spirit to the classical “stability + consistency implies convergence” approach in numerical analysis.

Literature review

Local-in-time propagation of chaos for the Cucker–Smale dynamics

Even for a finite time interval,

The first result in this direction was obtained in

),
based on an approximation of the initial distribution
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{0}
by an appropriate empirical measure over a lattice of size

Shortly after,

[
4
]

[
4
]

proved a quantitative local-in-time propagation of chaos estimate,

.
The technique presented there is a rather general strategy,

[

which enables to prove quantitative propagation of chaos estimates for a wide class of models with only locally Lispchitz interactions,
at the cost of very strong moment bounds on the solution to the nonlinear Markov process associated with the interacting particle system.
The authors of
[

also prove global existence and uniqueness results for the interacting particle system (

The local-in-time mean-field limit for the Cucker–Smale model is revisited later in

where the authors use an analytic rather than probabilistic coupling argument

In the aforementioned references, the communication rate

For a proof of propagation of chaos for singular communication rates

In this reference, the authors prove a quantitative propagation of chaos estimate on finite time intervals,

Recent developments on uniform-in-time propagation of chaos for various models

To conclude this section,
let us mention a few of the milestones among the flurry of advances concerning uniform-in-time mean-field limits in recent years.
We refer to
[
9
, Section 3]
for a more comprehensive overview.
In the already mentioned
[
37
]
,
uniform-in-time propagation of chaos is proved for a gradient system in a convex potential via a synchronous coupling approach,
and this result is used to prove exponentially fast convergence of the associated nonlinear process. The idea is extended to a class of kinetic dynamics, which however does not include the Cucker–Smale model, in
[
5
]
.
Further extensions and generalizations were obtained in
[
38
,
23
]
.

In
[
16
]
, a new technique based on reflection couplings is employed to relax convexity assumptions in uniform-in-time propagation-of-chaos results for gradient systems,
and this technique is later adapted to the kinetic setting in
[
22
]
.
Let us also mention the work of Lacker
[
33
]
,
later adapted in
[
34
]
to the uniform-in-time setting,
where a novel approach based on the BBGKY hierarchy is deployed to obtain for a class of interacting particle systems that an appropriate distance
between the marginal law on the first
k
particles of the interacting system on one hand,
and the
k
-fold product of the mean-field law on the other hand,
scales as
\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)^{2}
,
where previous arguments would only give a scaling as
\frac{k}{N}
.

Let us finally mention the recent preprint
[
45
]
,
where general conditions are presented under which uniform-in-time convergence can be deduced from local-in-time consistency estimates,
in a variety of settings including numerical discretizations of stochastic differential equations (SDEs), multiscale methods and mean-field systems.

Preliminaries

Before stating our main results,
we introduce some useful notation.
In order to prove mean-field limits which are uniform-in-time, we have to translate the empirical measure of the system (
1.1
) as well as the mean-field law
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}
from (
1.3
) such that their barycenters coincides with the origin.
The reason is the following behavior of the models (
1.1
) and (
1.3
)
[
25
, Section 2]
:

\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}X^{j}_{t}=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}X^{j}_{0}+\left(\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}V^{j}_{0}\right)t\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{E}\bigl[\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}_{t}\bigr]=\mathbf{E}\bigl[\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}_{0}\bigr]+\mathbf{E}\bigl[\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}_{0}\bigr]t\qquad\text{for all $t\geqslant 0$.}

(2.1)

Therefore, if for instance (
1.1
) is started at two different initial configurations, then the two systems will drift away from each other unless their initial average velocities are the same.
To circumvent this difficulty, we introduce the recentering operator
\mathcal{R}\colon\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{m})\to\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{m})
for
m\in\mathbf{N}^{+}

to be the pushforward of
\mu
under the translation map
z\mapsto z-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{m}}w\,\mu(\mathrm{d}w)
, that is,

\mathcal{R}\mu(A)=\mu\left(A+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{m}}z\,\mu(\mathrm{d}z)\right)\qquad\text{for all Borel set $A\subset\mathbf{R}^{m}$.}

(2.2)

We also need a recentering operator
\mathcal{R}^{J}
for the joint law of the particles (
1.1
).
The operator
\mathcal{R}^{J}:\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{2dJ})\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{2dJ}):
is defined as the pushforward under the map
R^{J}\colon\mathbf{R}^{2dJ}\to\mathbf{R}^{2dJ}
given by

R^{J}(\mathbf{Z})=\left(Z^{1}-\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}Z^{j},\dotsc,Z^{J}-\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}Z^{j}\right).

(2.3)

This operator is different from the recentering operator (
2.2
),
as
R^{J}
acts at the level of configurations in
\mathbf{R}^{2dJ}
,
but the two operators are related.
To make this link explicit,
consider the empirical measure map
m\colon\real^{2dJ}\to\mathcal{P}(\real^{2d})
which,
to a vector
\mathbf{Z}\in\real^{2dJ}
, associates the empirical measure

m(\mathbf{Z})\coloneq\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\delta_{Z^{j}}.

Then for any probability measure
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{2dJ})
,
it follows from the equality
m\circ R^{J}=\mathcal{R}\circ m
that
m_{\sharp}\bigl(\mathcal{R}^{J}(\rho)\bigr)=\mathcal{R}_{\sharp}\bigl(m_{\sharp}(\rho)\bigr)
.

Next, we recall two different notions of propagation of chaos considered in this paper
(see
[
8
]
for a more thorough discussion of different versions of propagation of chaos).

•

Empirical chaos
refers to the property that the empirical measure associated with the solution to the interacting particle system (
1.1
),
a random probability measure on
2d
,
converges to the deterministic mean-field law
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}
in the limit as
J\to\infty
.

•

Infinite dimensional Wasserstein chaos
refers to the property that,
in the limit
J\to\infty
,
the probability distribution
\rho^{J}_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\real^{2dJ})
becomes closer (in an appropriately normalized Wasserstein distance)
to the product measure
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}^{\otimes J}\in\mathcal{P}(\real^{2dJ})
,
where
\rho^{J}_{t}
is the law of the solution to the interacting particle system (
1.1
) at time
t
.

The two notions of chaos are related but not equivalent in general,
with infinite dimensional Wasserstein-2 chaos being the stronger property;
see for example
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
.
In this paper we shall first prove empirical chaos uniformly in time,
and only then focus on infinite dimensional Wasserstein-2 chaos uniformly in time,
the reason being that empirical chaos is particularly simple to prove for (
1.1
) given existing results in the literature.

Having introduced the recentering operators and recalled different notion of chaos,
we are now in a position to state our main results.

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[A_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{1}{J^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}v^{j}_{t}\cdot\biggl(H\Bigl(X^{j}_{t}-X^{k}_{t},V^{j}_{t}-V^{k}_{t}\Bigr)-H\Bigl({\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{j}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{k}_{t},{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{j}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{k}_{t}\Bigr)\biggr)\right]

(6.5)

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{1}{2J^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}\left(v^{j}_{t}-v^{k}_{t}\right)\cdot\biggl(H\Bigl(X^{j}_{t}-X^{k}_{t},V^{j}_{t}-V^{k}_{t}\Bigr)-H\Bigl({\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{j}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{k}_{t},{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{j}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{k}_{t}\Bigr)\biggr)\right].

(6.6)

Adding and subtracting a middle term
H\bigl({\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{j}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{k}_{t},V^{j}_{t}-V^{k}_{t}\bigr)
within the bracket,
and using the definition of

,
we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[A_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{1}{2J^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}\left(v^{j}_{t}-v^{k}_{t}\right)\cdot\left(V^{j}_{t}-V^{k}_{t}\right)\left(\Psi\Bigl(X^{j}_{t}-X^{k}_{t}\Bigr)-\Psi\Bigl({\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{j}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{k}_{t}\Bigr)\right)\right]

(6.7)

\displaystyle\qquad-\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{1}{2J^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}\Psi\Bigl({\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{j}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{k}_{t}\Bigr)\left\lvert v^{j}_{t}-v^{k}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}\right].

The second term is negative.
To bound the first term,
note that by Lipschitz continuity of
\psi
and by the reverse triangle inequality,
it holds that

\left\lvert\Psi\Bigl(X^{j}_{t}-X^{k}_{t}\Bigr)-\Psi\Bigl({\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{j}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{k}_{t}\Bigr)\right\rvert=\left\lvert\psi\Bigl(\left\lvert X^{j}_{t}-X^{k}_{t}\right\rvert\Bigr)-\psi\Bigl(\left\lvert{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{j}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{k}_{t}\right\rvert\Bigr)\right\rvert\leqslant L_{\psi}\left\lvert x^{j}_{t}-x^{k}_{t}\right\rvert.

(6.9)

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[A_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{L_{\psi}}{2J^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}\left\lvert v^{j}_{t}-v^{k}_{t}\right\rvert\times\left\lvert V^{j}_{t}-V^{k}_{t}\right\rvert\times\left\lvert x^{j}_{t}-x^{k}_{t}\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{L_{\psi}\mathcal{D}_{V}(t)}{2J^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}\left\lvert v^{j}_{t}-v^{k}_{t}\right\rvert\times\left\lvert x^{j}_{t}-x^{k}_{t}\right\rvert\right]\leqslant L_{\psi}{\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{V}}(0)\left(\mathbf{E}\Bigl\lvert x^{1}_{t}\Bigr\rvert^{2}+\mathbf{E}\Bigl\lvert v^{1}_{t}\Bigr\rvert^{2}\right).

(6.10)

Here we introduced
\mathcal{D}_{V}(t):=\max_{j,k\in}\bigl\lvert V^{j}_{t}-V^{k}_{t}\bigr\rvert

and used that
\mathcal{D}_{V}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{D}_{V}(0)\leqslant\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{V}(0)
for all

.
The inequality
\mathcal{D}_{V}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{D}_{V}(0)
is a standard property of the Cucker–Smale dynamics;
see for example
[
39
, Theorem 3.4]
.
We also used Young’s inequality together with the elementary bound
(a+b)^{2}\leqslant 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
.

Step 2. Bound of

We follow the classical approach,
but keep track of the constant prefactors.
By Young’s inequality, it holds that

2\mathbf{E}[B_{t}]\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left\lvert v^{1}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}+\mathbf{E}\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}H\Bigl({\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{1}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{k}_{t},{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{k}_{t}\Bigr)-H\star\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}\Bigl({\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{1}_{t},{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}\Bigr)\biggr\rvert^{2}.

the summands are uncorrelated with expectation 0,

\displaystyle 2\mathbf{E}[B_{t}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left\lvert v^{1}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{J}\mathbf{E}\biggl\lvert H\Bigl({\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{1}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{2}_{t},{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{2}_{t}\Bigr)-H\star\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}\Bigl({\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}}^{1}_{t},{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}\Bigr)\biggr\rvert^{2}.

(6.12)

Writing the expectation in terms of the mean-field law,
and then using the property of the mean as the best

approximation by a constant,
we deduce that

\displaystyle 2\mathbf{E}[B_{t}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left\lvert v^{1}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{J}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\biggr\lvert H\Bigl(x-y,v-w\Bigr)-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}H\Bigl(x-Y,v-W\Bigr)\,\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}(\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}W)\biggr\rvert^{2}\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}(\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}w)\,\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v).

(6.13)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left\lvert v^{1}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{J}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\biggr\lvert H\Bigl(x-y,v-w\Bigr)\biggr\rvert^{2}\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}(\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}w)\,\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v).

(6.14)

Since
\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant 1
,
we finally obtain

\displaystyle 2\mathbf{E}[B_{t}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left\lvert v^{1}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{J}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\lvert v-w\rvert^{2}\,\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}(\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}w)\,\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}_{t}(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left\lvert v^{1}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{2}{J}\mathbf{E}\left\lvert{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}-\mathbf{E}\left[{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}\right]\right\rvert^{2},

(6.15)

where we used that

\mathbf{E}\left\lvert{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}-{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{2}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}=2\mathbf{E}\left\lvert{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}-\mathbf{E}\left[{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}\right]\right\rvert^{2}.

(6.16)

Step 3. Grönwall’s inequality

Combining (
6.1
) with (

) and (
6.15
),
we deduce that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbf{E}\Bigl[\lvert x^{j}_{t}\rvert^{2}+\lvert v^{j}_{t}\rvert^{2}\Bigr]\leqslant\left({\frac{1}{2}}+KL_{\psi}{\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{V}}(0)+\frac{K}{2}\right)\left(\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert x^{1}_{t}\bigr\rvert^{2}+\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert v^{1}_{t}\bigr\rvert^{2}\right)+{\frac{K}{J}}\mathbf{E}\Bigl\lvert{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}-\mathbf{E}\left[{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}\right]\Bigr\rvert^{2}.

(6.17)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbf{E}\left[\left\lvert{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}-\mathbf{E}\left[{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}\right]\right\rvert^{2}\right]

where we used again the symmetry

to deduce (

Thus,
\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}-\mathbf{E}\bigl[{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{t}\bigr]\bigr\rvert^{2}\leqslant\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{0}-\mathbf{E}\left[{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{V}}^{1}_{0}\right]\bigr\rvert^{2}

Proof.

Theorem

Proof of

3.2
.

We largely follow the proof given in

, Theorem 1.2]
.
The first step is to obtain differential inequalities of the form

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}_{V}(t)

(6.30)

For the first term on the right-hand side,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{K}{2J^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}\Psi\bigl(X^{j}_{t}-X^{k}_{t}\bigr)\left\lvert V^{k}_{t}-\widetilde{V}^{k}_{t}-V^{j}_{t}+\widetilde{V}^{j}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}

(6.31)

(6.33)

(6.34)

For the second term,
the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequalities,
together with Lipschitz continuity of
\psi
and the reverse triangle inequality,
give

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{K}{J^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}\left\lvert\Psi\bigl(X^{j}_{t}-X^{k}_{t}\bigr)-\Psi\bigl(\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{k}_{t}\bigr)\right\rvert\cdot\Bigl\lvert V^{j}_{t}-\widetilde{V}^{j}_{t}\Bigr\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\widetilde{V}^{k}_{t}-\widetilde{V}^{j}_{t}\right\rvert

(6.35)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\frac{K}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Bigl\lvert V^{j}_{t}-\widetilde{V}^{j}_{t}\Bigr\rvert^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{K}{J^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}\left\lvert\Psi\bigl(X^{j}_{t}-X^{k}_{t}\bigr)-\Psi\bigl(\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{k}_{t}\bigr)\right\rvert^{2}\cdot\left\lvert\widetilde{V}^{k}_{t}-\widetilde{V}^{j}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}}

(6.36)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{2K\mathcal{L}_{V}(t)}\sqrt{\frac{KL_{\psi}^{2}}{J^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}\left\lvert X^{k}_{t}-X^{j}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{k}_{t}+\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{V}}(t)^{2}}

(6.37)

\displaystyle\leqslant{\sqrt{8}}KL_{\psi}\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{V}}(t)\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{V}(t)}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}(t)},

(6.38)

where again we used that

\frac{1}{J^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}\left\lvert X^{k}_{t}-X^{j}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{k}_{t}+\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}=\frac{2}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lvert X^{j}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t}-\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Bigl(X^{j}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t}\Bigr)\right\rvert^{2}\leqslant\frac{2}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lvert X^{j}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}.

(6.39)

It follows from these inequalities that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}}\leqslant\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{V}},\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{V}}\leqslant-K\psi\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{X}(t)\bigr)\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{V}}+\sqrt{2}KL_{\psi}\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{V}}(t)\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}}.

(6.40)

The bound (
6.25
) and a bootstrapping argument based on Grönwall’s lemma,
given in
[
24
, Lemma 3.1]
,
could then be employed to prove (
3.5
).
However, a sharper estimate can be obtained more directly.
To explain how, let us introduce
\alpha:=K\psi(x_{\infty}),

and

\gamma:=\sqrt{2}KL_{\psi}\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{V}}(0).

By (
6.40
),
it holds that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\alpha\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{V}}\right)\leqslant\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\left(\alpha\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{V}}\right)\e^{-\alpha t},

(6.41)

which implies, via a Grönwall inequality, that

\left(\alpha\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}(t)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{V}(t)}\right)\leqslant\exp\left(\frac{\gamma}{\alpha^{2}}\right)\left(\alpha\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}(0)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{V}(0)}\right).

(6.42)

Thus, since
\sqrt{a+b}\leqslant\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\sqrt{a+b}
for any
a,b\in\mathbf{R}^{+}
,
it follows that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}(t)+\mathcal{L}_{V}(t)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}(t)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{V}(t)}

(6.43)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{\alpha}\right\}\left(\alpha\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}(t)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{V}(t)}\right)

(6.44)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{\alpha}\right\}\exp\left(\frac{\gamma}{\alpha^{2}}\right)\left(\alpha\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}(0)}+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{V}(0)}\right)

(6.45)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{2}\max\left\{\alpha,\frac{1}{\alpha}\right\}\exp\left(\frac{\gamma}{\alpha^{2}}\right)\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{X}(0)+\mathcal{L}_{V}(0)}\,.

(6.46)

In order to give an expression of the function
C_{\rm Stab}
as explicit as possible,
define
\mathcal{I}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\psi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
.
From (
6.26
) we have that
\mathcal{I}(x_{\infty})=\mathcal{I}\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{X}(0)\bigr)+\frac{\mathcal{D}_{V}(0)}{K}
.
Therefore, inequality (
3.5
) holds with constant

\displaystyle C_{\rm Stab}\left(\mathcal{D}_{X}(0),\mathcal{D}_{V}(0),\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{V}(0)\right)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{2}\max\Bigl\{K,\frac{1}{\alpha}\Bigr\}\exp\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{2}KL_{\psi}\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{V}}(0)}{\alpha^{2}}\biggr),

(6.47)

where
\alpha=K\psi\Bigl(\mathcal{I}^{-1}\bigl(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{D}_{X}(0))+\frac{\mathcal{D}_{V}(0)}{K}\bigr)\Bigr)
is a non-increasing function of
\mathcal{D}_{X}(0)
and
\mathcal{D}_{V}(0)
.
∎

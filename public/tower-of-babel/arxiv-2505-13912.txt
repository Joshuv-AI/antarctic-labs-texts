Superconnections and Bott-Chern valued Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck for complex orbifolds, I
Qiaochu Ma, Xiang Tang, Hsian-Hua Tseng, Zhaoting Wei

Abstract
. We use flat antiholomorphic superconnections to study orbifold Chern character following the method introduced by Bismut, Shen, and Wei on general complex orbifolds. We show the uniqueness of orbifold Chern character by proving a Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck theorem for orbifold embeddings.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Riemann-Roch, Index Theory, and the Orbifold Setting

The Riemann-Roch theorem originated as a formula relating meromorphic functions and divisors on a compact Riemann surface. Hirzebruch extended it to holomorphic vector bundles on smooth projective varieties. In its analytic form, for a holomorphic vector bundle
E
on a compact complex manifold
X
, the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch formula is

(1.1)

\chi(X,E)=\int_{X}\operatorname{ch}(E)\operatorname{Td}(TX),

where
\chi(X,E)
is the holomorphic Euler characteristic

[
35
]
.

Grothendieck’s reformulation shifted the emphasis from a numerical identity on a single space to functoriality under proper morphisms. If
f\colon X\to Y
is proper and
\mathcal{F}
is a coherent sheaf on
X
, then

(1.2)

\operatorname{ch}(f_{!}\mathcal{F})\operatorname{Td}(TY)=f_{*}\bigl(\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal{F})\operatorname{Td}(TX)\bigr).

Thus, after the correction given by the Todd class, the Chern character is compatible with direct image in
K
-theory and pushforward in cohomology. Another valuable point is that
(
1.2
) is an identity of characteristic classes on
Y
, not just an equality of numbers. Therefore it is compatible with composition and may be applied before a further pushforward to a point
[
14
]
.

For a closed embedding
i\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
, (
1.2
) can be rewritten as

(1.3)

\operatorname{ch}(i_{*}\mathcal{F})=i_{*}\left(\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal{F})\operatorname{Td}(N_{X/Y})^{-1}\right),

where
N_{X/Y}=i^{*}TY/TX
is the normal bundle. This is the form most relevant to the present paper: it recovers the characteristic class of a sheaf on
Y
supported on
X
from data intrinsic to
X
and its embedding into
Y
. The goal of this paper is to establish an analytic orbifold analogue of this class-valued embedding formula, rather than only a formula obtained after integration over the target.

Index theory supplies the analytic counterpart of the numerical Riemann-Roch theorem. For a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle
E
on a compact complex manifold, its Dolbeault complex is elliptic and

\operatorname{ind}(\bar{\partial}_{E})=\chi(X,E).

The Atiyah-Singer index theorem generalized the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem (
1.1
) to general elliptic operators on closed manifolds
[
10
]
. The heat-equation method of Atiyah-Bott-Patodi gives a local analytic derivation of the index density
[
5
]
, while
Atiyah-Patodi-Singer theory extends the theory to manifolds with boundary through the eta invariant and its boundary correction
[
7
]
. These results identify analytic and topological indices, but the final invariant is still a number after pushforward to a point. Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch, by contrast, governs characteristic classes under direct images between spaces. The theorem proved here is of this class-valued kind, although its characteristic classes are constructed by
analytic and differential-geometric methods.

For coherent analytic sheaves, constructing such classes is more subtle than for vector bundles. A coherent sheaf on a compact complex manifold may not have a finite global resolution by holomorphic vector bundles, and its direct image under an embedding may fail to admit such a resolution even when the original sheaf does. O’Brian-Toledo-Tong developed Chern characters and Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch formulas for coherent analytic sheaves in Hodge cohomology
[
43
,
44
]
, and Grivaux constructed characteristic classes in rational Deligne cohomology
[
32
]
.
Bismut-Shen-Wei subsequently used flat antiholomorphic superconnections to construct Chern characters of coherent analytic sheaves taking values in Bott-Chern cohomology and to prove Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck for proper holomorphic maps between compact complex manifolds
[
16
]
.

Bott-Chern cohomology is the natural target for the present construction because the problem is complex analytic and the spaces under consideration are not assumed to be Kähler. Hermitian characteristic forms are
d
-closed forms of pure type, and their variation under a change of metric is
\partial\bar{\partial}
-exact. They therefore define intrinsic Bott-Chern classes. This target preserves the type decomposition, removes dependence on auxiliary metrics, and admits a current realization compatible with pushforward under closed embeddings. When the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma holds, these classes recover the corresponding Dolbeault and de Rham information; in the potentially non-Kähler setting, they may retain strictly finer complex-geometric
information
[
23
,
15
,
19
,
16
]
.

We now turn from manifolds to orbifolds. Equivariant index theory provides the natural bridge. If a compact group
G
acts on a compact manifold
M
and
D
is a
G
-equivariant elliptic operator, its equivariant index is the virtual representation

\operatorname{Ind}_{G}(D)=[\ker D]-[\operatorname{coker}D]\in R(G).

Equivalently, it determines the virtual character

\operatorname{Ind}_{g}(D)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(g|_{\ker D}\bigr)-\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(g|_{\operatorname{coker}D}\bigr),\qquad g\in G.

Segal’s equivariant
K
-theory provides the natural home for the equivariant symbol and index, with
K_{G}(\mathrm{pt})=R(G)

[
48
]
. The fixed-point formulas of Atiyah-Bott and Atiyah-Segal express
\operatorname{Ind}_{g}(D)
in terms of characteristic classes on the
g
-fixed point submanifold
M^{g}
and the action of
g
on its normal bundle
[
6
,
8
]
. The Atiyah-Segal completion theorem gives a complementary relation between equivariant
K
-theory and the
K
-theory of the Borel construction
[
9
]
.

The equivariant Chern character carries the same refinement at the level of characteristic classes. On the
g
-fixed locus, it records both the curvature of the bundle and the action of
g
on its fibers. Evaluation at different group elements provides representation-theoretic information invisible to the ordinary Chern character. Fixed loci, stabilizer-weighted traces, and eigenvalue-dependent normal factors are therefore intrinsic features of equivariant index theory.

Orbifolds, introduced by Satake as
V
-manifolds, provide the intrinsic setting in which local finite-group actions and stabilizer representations vary from chart to chart
[
47
]
. They are locally modeled on finite quotients but need not admit a presentation as a single global quotient. For
X=[M/G]
with
G
finite, an elliptic operator on
X
may be studied as a
G
-equivariant operator on
M
, and its orbifold
index is

\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathrm{orb}}(D)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\operatorname{Ind}_{g}(D).

Kawasaki extended this construction to general compact orbifolds and proved orbifold Riemann-Roch and index theorems
[
36
,
37
]
. His formula expresses the holomorphic Euler characteristic as a sum of characteristic-class integrals over the fixed sectors, now understood as the components of the inertia orbifold.

Kawasaki’s theorems explain why orbifold characteristic classes must retain stabilizer traces and why moving normal directions contribute sectorwise correction factors. They also motivate the passage from
X
to its inertia orbifold
IX
. This passage records the representation theory of orbifold stabilizers and should be distinguished from the use of Bott-Chern cohomology, which is dictated by the complex-analytic and potentially non-Kähler nature of the problem.

Kawasaki’s conclusion is numerical after integration over the inertia orbifold. The present paper instead constructs a class

\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}(\mathcal{F})\in H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{(=)}(IX,\mathbb{C})
,
the orbifold Bott-Chern cohomology, before integration and proves its functoriality under direct image. The two features of the target have distinct origins: the inertia orbifold records stabilizer characters, while Bott-Chern cohomology retains the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-sensitive information carried by characteristic forms of pure type.

Class-valued orbifold Riemann-Roch theorems have also been developed in algebraic and analytic geometry. Toën proved an algebraic Riemann-Roch theorem for Deligne-Mumford stacks using the inertia stack; see also Edidin’s account
[
50
,
28
]
. In the analytic setting, Ma defined a Bott-Chern-valued orbifold Chern character for holomorphic orbifold vector bundles and proved an embedding formula for stabilizer-preserving embeddings under the assumption that the direct image admits a finite locally free resolution
[
38
]
.

1.2.
Context and purpose

The purpose of this paper is to construct a Morita-invariant orbifold Chern character for coherent analytic complexes on a compact complex orbifold and to prove its Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck formula for orbifold embeddings. The construction converts coherent analytic information into differential forms that can be pulled back, multiplied, and pushed forward. It therefore determines the characteristic class of a direct image before final integration and retains information that a numerical index forgets.

Three distinct difficulties determine the form of the construction. First, the absence of finite global locally free resolutions is addressed by using flat antiholomorphic superconnections. These provide finite-rank smooth representatives for coherent complexes and encode, through their higher antiholomorphic-form components, the homotopy-coherent data that a global holomorphic resolution may fail to capture
[
46
,
22
,
16
]
.

Second, the possible failure of the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma requires a cohomology theory that preserves pure type and metric transgression. At the level of forms,

H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{p,q}(X)=\frac{\{\alpha\in\Omega^{p,q}(X):d\alpha=0\}}{\partial\bar{\partial}\Omega^{p-1,q-1}(X)}.

Its pushforward under embeddings can be constructed by realizing it as a current. The example in Section
10
shows concretely that a characteristic form may vanish in Dolbeault cohomology while remaining nonzero in Bott-Chern cohomology; a Dolbeault-valued formula would therefore lose part of the complex-analytic direct-image information
[
55
]
.

Third, orbifold stabilizers carry representation-theoretic information that ordinary cohomology on the underlying space cannot detect. If
X=[U/G]
, the natural receptacle is the inertia orbifold

I[U/G]=\bigsqcup_{(g)\subset G}[U^{g}/Z_{G}(g)],

whose points are pairs
(x,g)
with
g
in the stabilizer of
x
. On the
g
-sector, the Chern form contains the trace of
g
on the bundle. An orbifold embedding may also enlarge stabilizers without changing the local underlying space, in which case the direct image includes induction of representations. This phenomenon is encoded by pushforward on the inertia orbifold. Describing orbifolds by proper étale groupoids and proving Morita invariance ensures that the resulting class depends on the orbifold, not on a chosen presentation
[
40
,
42
,
1
]
.

These points mark the precise difference from index theory. Equivariant and Kawasaki-type index theorems integrate sectorwise characteristic forms to obtain an integer or a virtual character. We construct the sectorwise Bott-Chern class first and prove its functoriality under direct image. The result combines the fixed-sector geometry of orbifold index theory, the class-valued functoriality of Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch, and the finer complex-analytic information detected by Bott-Chern cohomology.

1.3.
Main results

For a compact complex orbifold
X
, let
K(X)
be the Grothendieck group of coherent analytic sheaves and let
IX
be its inertia orbifold. We construct a homomorphism

(1.4)

\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}\colon K(X)\longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{(=)}(IX,\mathbb{C}),\qquad H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{(=)}(IX,\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{p}H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{p,p}(IX,\mathbb{C}).

It is invariant under Morita equivalence and agrees, on holomorphic orbifold vector bundles, with Ma’s orbifold Chern character
[
38
]
. We then prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
i_{X,Y}\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
be an embedding of compact complex orbifold groupoids and let
\mathcal{F}\in D^{b}_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)
. Then

(1.5)

\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}(i_{X,Y,*}\mathcal{F})=Ii_{X,Y,*}\!\left(\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}(\mathcal{F})\text{Td}_{\text{BC}}(N_{X/Y})^{-1}\right)

in
H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{(=)}(IY,\mathbb{C})
. Here
Ii_{X,Y}\colon IX\to IY
is the induced map, and the Todd factor is defined sectorwise using the stabilizer eigenbundle decomposition of the normal bundle.

The formula (
1.5
) applies even when
i_{X,Y,*}\mathcal{F}
has no finite locally free resolution. Its source-side expression simultaneously records the complex normal geometry and the induction of stabilizer representations. It extends Ma’s vector-bundle construction to coherent analytic complexes, removes the finite-resolution hypothesis on the direct image, and includes embeddings that change isotropy.

Theorem 1.2
.

The homomorphism (
1.4
) is the unique group homomorphism that agrees with Ma’s character on holomorphic orbifold vector bundles, is functorial under pullback, and satisfies (
1.5
) for orbifold embeddings.

Theorem
1.2
shows that the superconnection construction is not an arbitrary extension from vector bundles to coherent sheaves: normalization, pullback, and direct-image compatibility determine it uniquely. Theorem
1.1
is therefore a structural characterization as well as a computational formula.

The present paper combines the fixed-sector geometry of Kawasaki and Ma with the superconnection approach of Bismut-Shen-Wei. It is analytic rather than algebraic: its classes are represented by differential forms and currents of pure type, without imposing an algebraic presentation or passing to a Chow theory. It is intrinsic to orbifolds rather than tied to one global group action: all constructions are Morita invariant. It also treats coherent complexes without assuming finite global locally free resolutions and permits stabilizer groups to change under embeddings.

After the first version of this work appeared, Xu proved an equivariant Bott-Chern Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck theorem for proper maps of compact complex manifolds with finite group actions
[
58
]
. Xu treats a broader class of maps in the global-quotient setting. Our Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck theorem is restricted to embeddings, but applies to general compact complex orbifolds and includes isotropy-changing embeddings. The two results therefore address complementary aspects of equivariant and orbifold Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck theory.

1.4.
Construction and proof strategy

Let
B(X)
be the dg-category of flat antiholomorphic superconnections on
X
. We construct it from a groupoid presentation, prove Morita invariance, and establish an equivalence of the associated homotopy category
\underline{B}(X)
:

(1.6)

D^{b}_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)\simeq\underline{B}(X).

If
(E^{\bullet},A_{E}^{\prime\prime})
represents a coherent complex and a generalized Hermitian metric determines the adjoint
A_{E}^{\prime}
, then
A_{E}^{2}=[A_{E}^{\prime\prime},A_{E}^{\prime}]
. Writing
\beta\colon IX\to X
for the natural map and
\tau
for the tautological stabilizer automorphism, the Chern form is, up to the conventional normalization,

\text{Tr}_{s}\!\left[\tau\exp(-\beta^{*}A_{E}^{2})\right].

The Bianchi identity gives closedness, variation of the generalized metric is
\partial\bar{\partial}
-exact, and the supertrace gives additivity under mapping cones. The resulting class therefore descends to the derived category and defines (
1.4
).

For Theorem
1.1
, every orbifold embedding is factored as an iso-spatial embedding, which changes stabilizers without changing the local complex space, followed by a stabilizer-preserving embedding. This factorization separates the genuinely orbifold issue from the normal geometry. In the iso-spatial case, there is no normal-bundle correction: locally, the formula is the character identity for induction along an inclusion of finite stabilizer groups. In the stabilizer-preserving case, deformation to the normal cone reduces the problem to the zero section of the normal bundle, where the Koszul complex yields the inverse Bott-Chern Todd class. Combining the two cases gives the formula for an arbitrary embedding.

The uniqueness Theorem
1.2
is proved by Hironaka’s flattening result and dévissage in
G
-theory. After suitable blow-ups, one separates a locally free part and reduces the remaining contribution to coherent sheaves with lower-dimensional support. This explains why the embedding formula, rather than a particular superconnection representative, characterizes the final class.

1.5.
Outlook and organization

Theorem
1.1
is a first step toward establishing the Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck formula for general proper holomorphic maps of complex orbifolds. A map
f\colon X\to Y
factors through its graph

X\hookrightarrow X\times Y\longrightarrow Y.

The present paper treats the graph embedding, while the projection requires a families theorem for orbifold Dolbeault operators. Such a theorem would combine the superconnection model for coherent complexes with the analytic variation of a family and with sectorwise isotropy data. The construction developed here isolates the characteristic class and the embedding mechanism that any such extension must recover. It also provides a basis for twisted versions involving flat
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-gerbes.

Section
2
develops groupoids and orbifold embeddings. Sections
3
and
4

treat Bott–Chern cohomology, inertia groupoids, and coherent orbifold sheaves. Sections
5
–
8
construct flat antiholomorphic superconnections, generalized metrics, curvature, and the Chern character. Section
9
proves Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, and Section
10
gives the non-Kähler example. The appendix contains results about coherent sheaves.

A review of groupoids and orbifolds

This section consists of two parts. In the first part, we review the related material for groupoids and orbifolds; in the second part, we introduce the groupoid presentation of an orbifold embedding, which is crucial for our development of an orbifold Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck theorem.

2.1.
Lie groupoids

We briefly review the groupoid approach to orbifolds, mostly following

Definition 2.1
.

A groupoid is a (small) category in which each morphism is invertible. Alternatively,
a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
consists of a set
G_{0}
of objects and a set
G_{1}
of arrows. There are maps
s
and

, the inverse map

, and the composition map
m\colon G_{1}\times_{G_{0}}G_{1}\to G_{1}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A homomorphism
\phi:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
is by definition a functor. In more detail, a homomorphism consists of two maps (both) denoted by
\phi:H_{0}\to G_{0}
and
\phi:H_{1}\to G_{1}
, which together commute with all structure maps.

We need to consider groupoids in the category of smooth manifolds.

Definition 2.3
.

A Lie groupoid is a groupoid
\mathcal{G}

and

Definition 2.4

A homomorphism

\phi\colon H_{0}\to G_{0}

Definition 2.5

2.2.
Complex groupoids

We consider groupoids in the category of complex manifolds.

Definition 2.16
.

A Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}=(G_{0},G_{1})

A

\mathcal{G}

Proof.

This is a special case of the linearization theorem

.
∎

For compact complex orbifolds (as in Definition

For a compact complex orbifold, we can choose a representing orbifold groupoid

2.3.
Generalized morphisms

We introduce the notion of generalized morphisms between groupoids.

Definition 2.23
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
be two Lie groupoids. A generalized morphism from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
is a triple
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
where
Z
is a manifold endowed with a left
\mathcal{G}
-action and a free and proper right
\mathcal{H}
-action which commute and
\rho,\sigma
are maps,

G_{0}\overset{\rho}{\leftarrow}Z\overset{\sigma}{\rightarrow}H_{0},

such that
\rho
induces a diffeomorphism
Z/\mathcal{H}\simeq G_{0}
.

Remark 2.24
.

We can define a groupoid
\mathcal{Z}=(Z_{0},Z_{1})
from a generalized morphism
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
where
Z_{0}=Z
and

Z_{1}=\{(z,g,h)\in Z\times G_{1}\times H_{1}|s(g)=\rho(z),~t(h)=\sigma(z)\}.

We define
s(z,g,h)=z
and
t(z,g,h)=gzh
. In addition, we define

(z_{1},g_{1},h_{1})\cdot(z_{2},g_{2},h_{2})=(z_{2},g_{1}g_{2},h_{2}h_{1}),\quad\text{when }z_{1}=g_{2}z_{2}h_{2}.

We have natural groupoid homomorphisms
\phi_{\rho}\colon\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{G}
and
\phi_{\sigma}\colon\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{H}
. We define

\phi_{\rho}(z)=\rho(z)\text{ and }\phi_{\sigma}(z)=\sigma(z)\quad\text{on }Z_{0};\qquad\phi_{\rho}(z,g,h)=g\text{ and }\phi_{\sigma}(z,g,h)=h^{-1}\quad\text{on }Z_{1}.

It is easy to check that
\phi_{\rho}
and
\phi_{\sigma}
are groupoid homomorphisms and
\phi_{\rho}
is a Morita equivalence morphism. If
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
is a principal bibundle, then
\phi_{\sigma}
is also a Morita equivalence morphism.

Remark 2.25
.

Locally, an orbifold groupoid is a transformation groupoid associated with a finite group
G
action on a manifold
X
. In this way, a generalized morphism may be represented as a groupoid homomorphism
G\ltimes X\to H\ltimes Y
defined by a map
\underline{f}\colon X\to Y
equivariant with respect to the group homomorphism
\bar{f}:G\to H
.

We next introduce the notion of proper morphisms between complex orbifold groupoids following
[
54
, Section 7]
. We begin with some basic constructions.

For a generalized morphism
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
, we consider the quotient
\mathcal{G}\backslash Z
. Let
\tilde{\rho}
be the map

\tilde{\rho}\colon\mathcal{G}\backslash Z\to\mathcal{G}\backslash G_{0}

induced by
\rho\colon Z\to G_{0}
. We have the following commutative diagram,

(2.1)

\begin{CD}G_{0}@<{\rho}<{}<Z\\
@V{}V{\pi_{\mathcal{G}}}V@V{}V{\pi_{\mathcal{G}\backslash Z}}V\\
\mathcal{G}\backslash G_{0}@<{\tilde{\rho}}<{}<\mathcal{G}\backslash Z\end{CD}

Meanwhile, since
\sigma
satisfies
\sigma(gz)=\sigma(z)
, the map
\sigma\colon Z\to H_{0}
induces a map

(2.2)

\tilde{\sigma}\colon\mathcal{G}\backslash Z\to H_{0}.

Furthermore,
\sigma
and
\tilde{\sigma}
are both
\mathcal{H}
-invariant maps, hence they induce maps

\theta\colon Z/\mathcal{H}\cong G_{0}\to H_{0}/\mathcal{H},\quad\tilde{\theta}\colon\mathcal{G}\backslash Z/\mathcal{H}\to H_{0}/\mathcal{H}

which make the following diagram commute:

(2.3)

\begin{CD}Z@>{\sigma}>{}>H_{0}\\
@V{}V{\pi_{\mathcal{H}\backslash Z}}V@V{}V{\pi_{\mathcal{H}}}V\\
G_{0}\cong Z/\mathcal{H}@>{\theta}>{}>H_{0}/\mathcal{H}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{\text{id}}V\\
\mathcal{G}\backslash Z/\mathcal{H}@>{\tilde{\theta}}>{}>H_{0}/\mathcal{H}.\end{CD}

We know that
\rho
induces a diffeomorphism
Z/\mathcal{H}\simeq G_{0}
, hence
\mathcal{G}\backslash Z/\mathcal{H}\simeq\mathcal{G}\backslash G_{0}
and the map
Z/\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}\backslash Z/\mathcal{H}
agrees with the quotient map
G_{0}\to\mathcal{G}\backslash G_{0}
.

In summary, we have the following commutative diagram,

(2.4)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}\backslash Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}\backslash G_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{0}/\mathcal{H}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mathcal{G}\backslash Z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\rho}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\sigma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mathcal{H}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\theta}}

The following definitions are taken from
[
54
, Introduction]
.

Definition 2.26
.

Let
Z
be a space with a
\mathcal{G}
-action. A subspace
Y
of
Z
is called
\mathcal{G}
-compact if
Y
is
\mathcal{G}
-invariant and

is compact.

Definition 2.27
.

A generalized morphism
(Z,\rho,\sigma)\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
between Lie groupoids is called

if the action of
\mathcal{G}
on

is proper.

Remark 2.28
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a groupoid morphism. By
[
54
, Proposition 7.4]
, the associated generalized groupoid morphism is locally proper if and only if
(f,r,s)\colon G_{1}\to H_{1}\times G_{0}\times G_{0}
is proper.

Proposition 2.29
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a proper Lie groupoid, then any generalized morphism
(Z,\rho,\sigma)\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
is locally proper.

Proof.

The map
\phi\colon G_{1}\times_{G_{0}}Z\to Z\times Z
defined by
\phi(g,z)=(gz,z)
fits in the following diagram

(2.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{1}\times_{G_{0}}Z}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{0}\times G_{0},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(t,s)}

which is commutative. For any compact subset

,

is a compact subset of

. Hence
(\rho,\rho)(K\times K)
is a compact subset in

Definition 2.30
.

A generalized morphism
(Z,\rho,\sigma)\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
between Lie groupoids is called

if it is locally proper and, in addition, for any compact subset

in

,

is
\mathcal{G}
-compact.

Remark 2.31
.

(Z,\rho,\sigma)
is proper if and only if it is locally proper and the map
\tilde{\sigma}
in (

) is proper.

2.4.
Orbifold embeddings

A morphism
f\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
between Lie groupoids is called an
embedding
if each
f_{i}\colon G_{i}\to H_{i}
,
i=0,1
, is an embedding of manifolds. The definition of embedding of orbifolds, which we develop in Definition
2.32
below, is more involved.

Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
be complex orbifold groupoids and
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
be a generalized morphism from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
be the pullback of
\mathcal{G}
to
Z
under
\rho\colon Z\to G_{0}
. More explicitly, we have
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}=(\widetilde{G}_{0},\widetilde{G}_{1})
where

(2.6)

\widetilde{G}_{0}=Z\,\,\text{and }\widetilde{G}_{1}=\{(z_{1},g,z_{2})\in Z\times G_{1}\times Z|\rho(z_{1})=t(g),~\rho(z_{2})=s(g)\}.

The source and target maps of
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
are the obvious projections to
Z
. Similarly, we can define
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
to be the pullback of
\mathcal{H}
to
Z
under
\sigma\colon Z\to H_{0}
.
More explicitly, we have
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}=(\widetilde{H}_{0},\widetilde{H}_{1})
where
\widetilde{H}_{0}=Z
and

(2.7)

\widetilde{H}_{1}=\{(z_{1},h,z_{2})\in Z\times H_{1}\times Z|\sigma(z_{1})=t(h),~\sigma(z_{2})=s(h)\}.

We can define a morphism
\phi\colon\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\to\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
as follows:
\phi_{0}\colon\widetilde{G}_{0}\to\widetilde{H}_{0}
is the identity map on
Z
. As for
\phi_{1}\colon\widetilde{G}_{1}\to\widetilde{H}_{1}
, consider an element
(z_{1},g,z_{2})\in\widetilde{G}_{1}
. Notice that we have

\rho(gz_{2})=t(g)=\rho(z_{1})\in G_{0}.

Since the
\mathcal{H}
-action on
Z
is free and
\rho
induces an isomorphism
Z/\mathcal{H}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}G_{0}
, there exists a unique
h\in H_{1}
such that

gz_{2}=z_{1}h.

We define
\phi_{1}(z_{1},g,z_{2})
to be

(2.8)

\phi_{1}(z_{1},g,z_{2}):=(z_{1},h,z_{2}).

Definition 2.32
.

A generalized morphism
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
is an
embedding
if it satisfies:

(1)

The map
\sigma\colon Z\to H_{0}
is a local embedding.

(2)

The image
\sigma(Z)
is a closed subset of
H_{0}
with locally finite resolution, i.e. for any
x
in the image of
\sigma\colon Z\to H_{0}
, there is a neighborhood
U
of
x
, and there are
z_{1},...,z_{k}\in Z
with neighborhoods
V_{1},...,V_{k}
such that for all
i
,
\sigma|_{V_{i}}:V_{i}\to\sigma(V_{i})
is a diffeomorphism, and
\sigma(Z)\cap U=\bigcup_{i}\sigma(V_{i})
.

(3)

The morphism
\phi\colon\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\to\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is a
saturated embedding
of groupoids.

Here, “saturated” means that for any
h\in\widetilde{H}_{1}
, there exists a
g\in\widetilde{G}_{1}
such that
s(h)=s(\phi(g))
and
t(h)=t(\phi(g))
.

We explain Condition (2) in Definition
2.32
with the following example.

Example 2.33
.

Consider the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
action on
\mathbb{C}
by
z\mapsto-z
. The associated groupoid is the transformation groupoid
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\ltimes\mathbb{C}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{C}
. Similarly, we consider the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
action on
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
by

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\ni(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}):(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(\gamma_{1}(z_{1}),\gamma_{2}(z_{2})).

The associated groupoid is
\mathcal{H}=(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})\ltimes(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C})\rightrightarrows\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
. We consider the morphism
f:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
by

f_{0}(z)=(z,z),\ f_{1}(\gamma,z)=(\gamma,\gamma,z,z),\quad\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{2},z\in\mathbb{C}.

It is not hard to see that
f
represents the diagonal embedding

\Delta:[\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}]\hookrightarrow[\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}]\times[\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}].

The bibundle
C
is of the form
C=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with the map
\sigma:C\to\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
by

\sigma(z,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=(\gamma_{1}(z),\gamma_{2}(z)),\quad z\in\mathbb{C},\ (\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

We observe that the map
\sigma
is an embedding on each component of
C=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. And the image of
\sigma
is the union of the two axes
\{(z,0)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\,|\,z\in\mathbb{C}\}\cup\{(0,z)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\,|\,z\in\mathbb{C}\}
, which is a closed subset of
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
, but not a submanifold of
H_{0}
.

We can choose
U
to be the whole manifold
H_{0}=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
, and
V_{1}=\mathbb{C}\times\{e\}\times\{e\}
, and
V_{2}=\mathbb{C}\times\{\gamma\}\times\{e\}
, where
e
is the identity element of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\gamma
is the nontrivial element. It is straightforward to check that the restrictions of
\sigma
on
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are embeddings, and

\sigma(V_{1})\cup\sigma(V_{2})=\sigma(C)\cap U=\sigma(C).

This shows that
\sigma(C)
has the locally finite resolution property as in Definition
2.32
(2).

For our purposes, we need to distinguish the following two types of groupoid embeddings.

Definition 2.34
.

A generalized morphism
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
is a
stabilizer-preserving
embedding if it is an embedding in the sense of Definition
2.32
and, in addition, the morphism
\phi\colon\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\to\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is an isomorphism of groupoids.

Remark 2.35
.

The notion of stabilizer-preserving morphism is also considered in a different context in
[
24
, Definition 5.2]
.

Definition 2.36
.

A generalized morphism
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
is an
iso-spatial
embedding if it is an embedding in the sense of Definition
2.32
and, in addition, the map
\sigma\colon Z\to H_{0}
is a surjective submersion.

Remark 2.37
.

Locally, the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is given by a manifold
X
with an action of a finite group
G
. A similar description is valid for the groupoid
\mathcal{H}
. When we restrict to the local case, an iso-spatial embedding is given by an inclusion of the finite group
\bar{f}\colon G\hookrightarrow H
and the identity map on the base manifold
X
; and a stabilizer-preserving embedding is given by the identity map of the finite group and an embedding of complex manifolds
\underline{f}\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
.

We have the following factorization result for embeddings.

Proposition 2.38
.

Any groupoid embedding can be written as the composition of an iso-spatial embedding followed by a stabilizer-preserving embedding.

Proof.

Let
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
be an embedding from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
. We consider the groupoid
\mathcal{H}|_{\text{Im}\sigma}
. Since
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
is an embedding, the generalized morphism
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}|_{\text{Im}\sigma}
is an iso-spatial embedding, and the morphism
\sigma\colon\mathcal{H}|_{\text{Im}\sigma}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is a stabilizer-preserving embedding. It is easy to check that their composition is exactly
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
.
∎

2.5.
Inertia groupoids

Following
[
42
, Section 6.4]
, we can define the inertia groupoid of a complex orbifold groupoid
\mathcal{G}=(G_{0},G_{1})
. We consider

(2.9)

B_{\mathcal{G}}=\{g\in G_{1}|s(g)=t(g)\}.

It is easy to see that
B_{\mathcal{G}}
is a disjoint union of complex manifolds whose dimensions may vary with different components even when
G_{0}
is connected. We also note that
\mathcal{G}
acts on the space
B_{\mathcal{G}}
by conjugation. Then the
inertia groupoid

I\mathcal{G}
is defined to be the semi-direct product
B_{\mathcal{G}}\ltimes\mathcal{G}
. Namely,

(2.10)

(I\mathcal{G})_{0}=B_{\mathcal{G}},

and for
g_{1}
and
g_{2}\in B_{\mathcal{G}}
, an arrow
g_{1}\to g_{2}
consists of
g\in G_{1}
such that
gg_{1}g^{-1}=g_{2}
. In other words

(2.11)

(I\mathcal{G})_{1}=\{(g_{1},g_{2},g)|g_{1},g_{2}\in B_{\mathcal{G}}\text{ and }gg_{1}g^{-1}=g_{2}\},

and
s(g_{1},g_{2},g)=g_{1}
,
t(g_{1},g_{2},g)=g_{2}
. It is clear that
I\mathcal{G}
is also a proper, étale complex Lie groupoid.

Definition 2.39
.

There is a proper holomorphic groupoid map
\beta_{\mathcal{G}}\colon I\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
which maps
g\in(I\mathcal{G})_{0}
to
s(g)=t(g)\in G_{0}
and maps
(g_{1},g_{2},g)\in(I\mathcal{G})_{1}
to
g\in G_{1}
.

Definition 2.40
.

On
I\mathcal{G}
we have the tautological section
\tau_{\mathcal{G}}
with value in
G_{1}
given by

(2.12)

\tau_{\mathcal{G}}(g)=(g,g,g)\text{, for }g\in(I\mathcal{G})_{0}.

Remark 2.41
.

Locally an orbifold groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is a finite group
G
acting on a complex manifold
X
. In this case, the inertia groupoid
I\mathcal{G}
is

(2.13)

G\ltimes\coprod_{g\in G}X^{g},

where
X^{g}
is the fixed point set of
g
, and the
G
-action is given as follows: for any
g^{\prime}\in G
,

(2.14)

g^{\prime}\colon X^{g}\to X^{g^{\prime}g(g^{\prime})^{-1}},\quad x\mapsto g^{\prime}x.

It is easy to see that (
2.13
) is Morita equivalent to

(2.15)

\coprod_{(g)\in\text{Conj}(G)}Z_{G}(g)\ltimes X^{g},

where
\text{Conj}(G)
is the set of conjugacy classes of
G
and
Z_{G}(g)
is the centralizer of
g
in
G
.

From the viewpoint of (
2.15
), the map
\beta_{\mathcal{G}}
is the natural map

Z_{G}(g)\ltimes X^{g}\to G\ltimes X,

which are inclusions on both
Z_{G}(g)
and
X^{g}
. The map
\tau_{\mathcal{G}}
is given on each component by

(2.16)

\tau_{\mathcal{G}}(x)=(g,x)\text{, for }x\in X^{g}.

Next, we consider induced (generalized) morphisms between inertia groupoids.

Definition 2.42
.

Let
(Z,\rho,\sigma)\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a generalized morphism. We define a generalized morphism

(IZ,I\rho,I\sigma)\colon I\mathcal{G}\to I\mathcal{H}

as follows: let

(2.17)

IZ:=\{(g,z,h)\in G_{1}\times Z\times H_{1}|gzh=z\}.

This implies

(2.18)

s(g)=t(g)=\rho(z),\text{ and }s(h)=t(h)=\sigma(z),

hence we define
I\rho\colon IZ\to(I\mathcal{G})_{0}
and
I\sigma\colon IZ\to(I\mathcal{H})_{0}
as

(2.19)

I\rho(g,z,h):=g\text{, and }I\sigma(g,z,h):=h.

The groupoid
I\mathcal{G}
acts on
IZ
from the left by

(2.20)

(g_{1},g_{2},g)\cdot(g_{1},z,h):=(g_{2},gz,h)=(gg_{1}g^{-1},gz,h),

and
I\mathcal{H}
acts on
IZ
from the right by

(2.21)

(g,z,h_{2})\cdot(h_{1},h_{2},h):=(g,zh,h_{1})=(g,zh,h^{-1}h_{2}h).

Remark 2.43
.

Since the
\mathcal{H}
-action on
Z
is free, the
h
component of
IZ
as in (
2.17
) is uniquely determined by
(g,z)
and the condition
gzh=z
. Hence
IZ
is isomorphic to

(2.22)

\{(g,z)\in G_{1}\times Z|s(g)=t(g)=\rho(z)\}.

Remark 2.44
.

In the local case, let
f\colon G\ltimes X\to H\ltimes Y
be a morphism which consists of
\underline{f}\colon X\to Y
and
\bar{f}\colon G\to H
. Then the induced morphism

If\colon\coprod_{(g)\in\text{Conj}(G)}Z_{G}(g)\ltimes X^{g}\to\coprod_{(h)\in\text{Conj}(H)}Z_{H}(h)\ltimes Y^{h}

consists of
\overline{If}=\bar{f}\colon Z_{G}(g)\to Z_{H}(\bar{f}(g))
and
\underline{If}
is given on each component by
\underline{f}|_{X^{g}}\colon X^{g}\to Y^{\bar{f}(g)}
.

Let
(Z,\rho,\sigma)\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a Morita equivalence. It is easy to see that

(IZ,I\rho,I\sigma)\colon I\mathcal{G}\to I\mathcal{H}

is also a Morita equivalence. In other words, the inertia groupoid is invariant under Morita equivalence as in Definition
2.6
, and hence we can consider the inertia orbifold of a complex orbifold. It is not hard to check the following property (See Proposition
A.6
).

Proposition 2.45
.

Let
(Z,\rho,\sigma)\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a generalized morphism between complex orbifold groupoids. If
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
is a stabilizer-preserving embedding, then
(IZ,I\rho,I\sigma)
is also a stabilizer-preserving embedding.

is a bigraded algebra with the ordinary wedge product.
It is clear that the operators

Remark 3.1
.

In the local case,

Definition 3.2
.

Write

for the de Rham differential. The Bott-Chern cohomology group

is defined as

We can similarly define the real vector space

and

Remark 3.3
.

For a general complex groupoid, we need to use the Bott-Shulman double complexes to define its Bott-Chern cohomology. In the case of a proper étale groupoid, Definition

is sufficient.

3.3.
Functorial properties of Bott-Chern cohomology

Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
be complex orbifold groupoids of dimensions

\sigma^{*}\colon\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet}_{\mathcal{H}}(H_{0},\mathbb{C})\to\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet}_{\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}}(Z,\mathbb{C}),\quad\rho^{*}\colon\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet}_{\mathcal{G}}(G_{0},\mathbb{C})\to\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet}_{\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}}(Z,\mathbb{C}).

Moreover
\rho^{*}\colon\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet}_{\mathcal{G}}(G_{0},\mathbb{C})\to\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet}_{\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}}(Z,\mathbb{C})

preserves the corresponding real vector spaces.

By duality, we can define the pushforward map
f_{*}=\sigma_{*}\circ(\rho_{*})^{-1}
which maps
\mathcal{D}^{p,q}_{\mathcal{G}}(G_{0},\mathbb{C})
to
\mathcal{D}^{p-n+n^{\prime},q-n+n^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{H}}(H_{0},\mathbb{C})
.
Moreover, by Definition

we have a generalized morphism

between inertia groupoids, hence a morphism

(3.4)

If_{*}\colon H_{\text{BC}}^{\bullet,\bullet}(I\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C})\to H_{\text{BC}}^{\bullet-n+n^{\prime},\bullet-n+n^{\prime}}(I\mathcal{H},\mathbb{C}).

Proposition 3.4
.

The Bott-Chern cohomology is independent of the choice of the groupoid representation. In particular, if two complex orbifold groupoids
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
are Morita equivalent, then we have

(3.5)

H_{\text{BC}}^{p,q}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C})\cong H_{\text{BC}}^{p,q}(\mathcal{H},\mathbb{C}).

Proof.

By Definition
2.6

as in Definition

∎

3.4.
Pushforward map

In this subsection we consider the case that

is an iso-spatial embedding. We give an explicit description of the pushforward map

at the level of differential forms when we restrict to the local case.

As in Remark

and the identity map on the complex manifold

consists of the inclusion of finite groups

together with the identity map

. For each

, we define

(3.8)

If_{*}(\omega)\colon=\frac{1}{|Z_{G}(g)|}\sum_{h\in Z_{H}(g)}h^{*}\omega,

where
Z_{G}(g)
and
Z_{H}(g)
are the centralizers of
g
in
G
and
H
, respectively.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
f\colon G\ltimes X\to H\ltimes X
be an iso-spatial embedding and

be the induced morphism between inertia groupoids as in (
3.7
). Then the pushforward map
If_{*}
in (
3.8
) gives the pushforward map
If_{*}
in (
3.4
).

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{Z_{G}(g)}(X^{g},\mathbb{C})
, we can consider
\omega
as a
Z_{G}(g)
-invariant current on
X^{g}
by

(3.9)

\omega(\theta):=\frac{1}{|Z_{G}(g)|}\int_{X^{g}}\omega\wedge\theta,\quad\text{where }\theta\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{Z_{G}(g)}(X^{g},\mathbb{C})\text{ is a testing form}.

It is clear that for any form
\theta\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{Z_{H}(g)}(X^{g},\mathbb{C})
, we have
If^{*}(\theta)=\theta
considered as a form in
\Omega^{\bullet}_{Z_{G}(g)}(X^{g},\mathbb{C})

(3.10)

\omega(If^{*}(\theta))=\frac{1}{|Z_{G}(g)|}\int_{X^{g}}\omega\wedge\theta.

On the other hand, by (

(3.11)

If_{*}(\omega)(\theta)=\frac{1}{|Z_{H}(g)|}\int_{X^{g}}If_{*}(\omega)\wedge\theta=\frac{1}{|Z_{H}(g)||Z_{G}(g)|}\sum_{h\in Z_{H}(g)}\int_{X^{g}}h^{*}\omega\wedge\theta.

Since

-invariant, we have

(3.12)

h^{*}\omega\wedge\theta=h^{*}(\omega\wedge\theta),\text{ for any }h\in Z_{H}(g),

hence

(3.13)

If_{*}(\omega)(\theta)=\frac{1}{|Z_{H}(g)||Z_{G}(g)|}\sum_{h\in Z_{H}(g)}\int_{X^{g}}h^{*}(\omega\wedge\theta)=\frac{|Z_{H}(g)|}{|Z_{H}(g)||Z_{G}(g)|}\int_{X^{g}}\omega\wedge\theta,

which is exactly (

in (

∎

4.
Coherent sheaves on complex orbifolds

In this section, we discuss the notion of coherent sheaves on complex orbifolds from the viewpoint of groupoids. We follow the construction in
[
41
, Section 2.1]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{0},G_{1})
be a complex orbifold groupoid. A right
\mathcal{G}
-sheaf is a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
G_{0}
together with a right action of
\mathcal{G}
. In more detail, each
g\colon x\to y
in
G_{1}
gives a map

\mathcal{F}_{x}\to\mathcal{F}_{y},~a\mapsto a\cdot g,

where
\mathcal{F}_{x}
is the stalk of
\mathcal{F}
at
x
. It satisfies the conditions that
(a\cdot g_{1})\cdot g_{2}=a\cdot g_{1}g_{2}
and
a\cdot 1_{x}=a
. If
\mathcal{F}
admits additional structures, say vector space, algebra, etc., then we require the
\mathcal{G}
-action to preserve such structures. We define left
\mathcal{G}
-sheaves in the same way.

The following definitions can be found in
[
40
, Section 3]
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{0},G_{1})
be a complex orbifold groupoid. We define the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}
on
\mathcal{G}
as the sheaf of germs of holomorphic functions on
G_{0}
. This sheaf carries natural left and right
\mathcal{G}
-actions.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{0},G_{1})
be a complex orbifold groupoid. A sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}
modules
\mathcal{F}
is a sheaf on
G_{0}
equipped with a left
\mathcal{G}
action and an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}
action which are compatible. Compatibility means that

(4.1)

g\cdot(fs)=(g\cdot f)(g\cdot s),

where
g\in G_{1}
,
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}
, and
s\in\mathcal{F}
.

We denote the category of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}
modules by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}\text{-Mod}
.

The following result is essential in the construction of derived functors.

Proposition 4.4
.

The category
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}\text{-Mod}
is an abelian category with enough injectives.

Proof.

The claim is local, so we reduce to the case that
\mathcal{G}
is the action groupoid of a finite group acting on a complex manifold. The claim then follows from
[
33
, Propositions 5.1.1 and 5.1.2]
.
∎

Let
f\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a holomorphic morphism between complex orbifold groupoids. We can define the pull back functor

f^{*}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{H}}\text{-Mod}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}\text{-Mod}

as in
[
41
, Section 3.1]
. In more detail, for a sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{H}}\text{-Mod}
, the map
f_{0}\colon G_{0}\to H_{0}
gives a sheaf
f^{-1}\mathcal{F}
on
G_{0}
, which has a natural left
\mathcal{G}
-action: for each
g\colon x\to y
in
G_{1}
, the action of
g
on
a\in(f^{-1}\mathcal{F})_{x}
is simply given by

g\cdot a:=f(g)\cdot a.

Then we define
f^{*}\mathcal{F}
as

(4.2)

f^{*}\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{H}}}f^{-1}\mathcal{F},\quad\text{with }\mathcal{G}\text{-action }\quad g(s\otimes a):=(g\cdot s)\otimes(f(g)\cdot a).

It is clear that
f^{*}\mathcal{F}
is a sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}
modules. We define pullbacks of generalized morphisms in the same way as in Corollary
5.8
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a complex orbifold groupoid. A sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}
-modules is called a
coherent
sheaf if it satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

\mathcal{F}
is finite type, i.e. for every
x\in G_{0}
there exists an open neighborhood
(U,G)
of
x
(Definition
2.20
) and a
\mathcal{G}
-sheaf
\mathcal{M}
, a finite rank free sheaf on
G_{0}
, such that there exists a
\mathcal{G}|_{U}
-equivariant surjective map
\mathcal{M}|_{U}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{F}|_{U}
;

(2)

For every
(U,G)
and any
\mathcal{G}|_{U}
-equivariant map
\phi\colon\mathcal{M}|_{U}\to\mathcal{F}|_{U}
, the kernel of
\phi
is also finite type.

We denote the category of coherent sheaves on
\mathcal{G}
by
\text{coh}(\mathcal{G})
.

Remark 4.6
.

Condition (1) in Definition
4.5
has the flavor of global resolutions of sheaves. Here we discuss the role of such a condition in the context of Riemann-Roch theorems in algebraic geometry.

The Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch theorem for Deligne-Mumford stacks proved by Toën
[
50
]
is in the setting closest to this paper’s. Toen’s theorem requires a few assumptions on the Deligne-Mumford stacks, including the property that every coherent sheaf is the quotient of a locally free sheaf, see
[
50
, HYPOTHÉSE 4.9 (2)]
. This property is called the resolution property and is a property of the stack. In
[
52
]
, Totaro proved that (under minor assumptions) an algebraic stack has the resolution property if and only if it is a quotient stack of a quasi-affine scheme by a general linear group.

In view of the above, the resolution property plays a prominent role for Riemann-Roch theorem for Deligne-Mumford stacks and it is equivalent to some global geometric property of the stacks. For this reason, Condition (1) in Definition
4.5
, which we view as analogous to the resolution property in algebraic geometry, is a reasonable requirement to impose when considering Riemann-Roch for orbifolds.

The following result provides the main tool for our study of coherent sheaves on complex orbifolds.

.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a compact complex orbifold groupoid. For a bounded complex of sheaves

of

-, left

-vector bundles

and a quasi-isomorphism

The proof of this result is similar to
[

for

.

Let

We know

is finite and

. We use induction on

such that

∎

5.
Antiholomorphic flat superconnection on complex orbifolds

Here we introduce the theory of antiholomorphic flat superconnections on complex orbifolds.

A superconnection is a way of packaging several kinds of geometric and analytic information into a single object. Introduced by Quillen on a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector bundle
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
, it extends an ordinary connection by allowing differential-form components of different degrees. Its curvature
\mathbb{A}^{2}
therefore incorporates not only the usual curvature of the bundle, but also the grading and any algebraic or operator-theoretic data built into the superconnection. The associated form

\operatorname{Ch}(\mathbb{A})=\operatorname{Str}\!\left(e^{-\mathbb{A}^{2}}\right)

is closed and represents the Chern character of the corresponding virtual bundle
[
46
]
. In this sense, a superconnection does not measure a single numerical quantity. Rather, it converts geometric and spectral information into a differential form representing a topological characteristic class. For a family of Dirac operators, Bismut’s superconnection makes this principle particularly effective: after introducing a scaling parameter, its small-time limit recovers the local curvature density in the family index theorem, while its large-time behavior reflects the Chern character of the analytic index bundle. The transgression formula shows that the cohomology class is independent of the scaling parameter, thereby connecting the local geometric expression with the global analytic index
[
17
]
,
[
13
]
.

In complex geometry, Block
[
21
]
introduced the corresponding notion of an antiholomorphically flat superconnection. On a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded smooth vector bundle
E^{\bullet}
, this is an operator
A^{\prime\prime}
acting on
\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X;E^{\bullet})
, satisfying the graded
\bar{\partial}
-Leibniz rule and the flatness condition

(A^{\prime\prime})^{2}=0.

Thus,
A^{\prime\prime}
can be regarded as a derived version of the flat
\bar{\partial}
-connection that defines an ordinary holomorphic vector bundle. The higher-degree components of
A^{\prime\prime}
encode not merely one holomorphic bundle, but the differential and homotopy coherent data needed to describe a complex of sheaves. Block proved that the resulting differential graded category of cohesive modules recovers the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on a compact complex manifold
[
22
]
. Bismut, Shen, and Wei
[
16
]
build on this description by equipping an antiholomorphic superconnection with a generalized Hermitian metric and constructing the associated curvature and Chern character forms. In this setting, the flat superconnection
A^{\prime\prime}
records the derived holomorphic structure of the coherent sheaf, while the curvature obtained after adjoining its metric-dependent adjoint produces a differential form representative of the sheaf’s Chern character in Bott-Chern cohomology. Their Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck theorem then describes the behavior of this Bott-Chern Chern character under holomorphic direct image, with the Todd class accounting for the relative tangent geometry. From this viewpoint, their result is a complex-geometric counterpart of the families index theorem: it identifies the characteristic class obtained from the analytic superconnection construction with the class predicted by the geometry of holomorphic pushforward.

5.1.
Basic constructions

Our presentation of this section follows that of

.

Proof.

This is true because the

Let

Proof of Lemma

The claim is standard and we prove bit y induction on the amplitude of

[
49

For

Definition 7.1
.

X

\mathcal{G}=(G_{0},G_{1})
be as above. Let

be a
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant graded complex vector bundle on

(1)

\mathcal{G}
-equivariant;

(2)

h

(3)

8.1.
Supertrace and Chern character

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E^{\bullet},A^{E^{\bullet}\prime\prime})
be a flat antiholomorphic superconnection on
\mathcal{G}=(G_{0},G_{1})
equipped with a generalized metric
h
. In Definition

\beta_{\mathcal{G}}\colon I\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
mapping the inertia groupoid
I\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{E}
via

\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}\mathcal{E}=(\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}E^{\bullet},\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet}\prime\prime}).

In addition, we can also pull back the metric

\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}h

\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}\mathcal{E}

(8.1)

\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet}}=\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet}\prime}+\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet}\prime\prime}.

We can therefore form the curvature

\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet},2}=[\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet}\prime\prime},\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet}\prime}],

0
with respect to
\deg_{-}

On

\tau_{\mathcal{G}}
with value in
G_{1}
as in Definition
2.40

For any

be the point

\beta^{*}E^{\bullet}_{g}=E^{\bullet}_{x}

We have a

Let

Definition 8.1
.

We set

(8.4)

\text{ch}(A^{E^{\bullet}\prime\prime},h)=\varphi\text{Tr}_{\text{s}}[\tau_{\mathcal{G}}\exp(-\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet},2})]\in\Omega^{(=)}_{I\mathcal{G}}((I\mathcal{G})_{0},\mathbb{C}).

For
g\in(I\mathcal{G})_{0}

be the eigenvalues of the

\tau_{\mathcal{G}}(g)
action on
\beta_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}E^{\bullet}_{g}

\beta_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}E^{\bullet}_{g}

(8.5)

\beta_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}E^{\bullet}_{g}=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{q}\beta_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}E^{\bullet}_{g,j},

\beta_{\mathcal{G},b}^{*}A^{E^{\bullet}\prime\prime}=\sum_{j}\beta_{\mathcal{G},b}^{*}A^{E^{\bullet}\prime\prime}_{j},\ \beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet}\prime}=\sum_{j}\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet}\prime}_{j},\ \text{and}\ \beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet},2}=\sum_{j}\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet},2}_{j}.

\text{ch}(A^{E^{\bullet}\prime\prime},h)=\sum_{j}\varphi\text{Tr}_{\text{s}}[e^{i\theta_{j}}\exp(-\beta^{*}_{\mathcal{G},b}A^{E^{\bullet},2}_{j})].

Remark 8.2

In the local case, we have

and
[

Proof.

Since

∎

9.
Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck Theorem for embeddings

The goal of this section is to prove a Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck theorem for embeddings between complex orbifolds, namely Theorem
1.1
, which we reproduce here:

Theorem

(=Theorem
1.1
)
.

Let
i_{X,Y}\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
be an embedding of compact complex orbifold groupoids.
Let
\mathcal{F}\in D^{b}_{\text{coh}}(X)
and
i_{X,Y,*}\mathcal{F}\in D^{b}_{\text{coh}}(Y)
be its direct image. We have

\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}(i_{X,Y,*}\mathcal{F})=Ii_{X,Y,*}\left(\frac{\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}(\mathcal{F})}{\text{Td}_{\text{BC}}(N_{X/Y})}\right)\text{ in }H_{\text{BC}}^{(=)}(IY,\mathbb{C}),

where
Ii_{X,Y}
is the induced morphism between inertia groupoids.

In Proposition
2.38
, we show that an orbifold embedding can be decomposed into a composition of an iso-spatial embedding followed by a stabilizer-preserving embedding. This splits our proof into the corresponding two cases.

9.1.
Iso-spatial case

Recall that (
4.3
) defines the pushforward of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules under a generalized morphism. We define the pushforward of sheaves of
C^{\infty}
-modules in the same way.

The following proposition is important in subsequent constructions.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
f=(Z,\rho,\sigma)\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be an iso-spatial embedding of complex orbifold groupoids. For any finite-dimensional
C^{\infty}
-vector bundle
E
on
\mathcal{G}
, its pushforward
f_{*}(E)
is a
C^{\infty}
-vector bundle on
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof of Proposition
9.1
.

By (
4.3
), we know
f_{*}(E)=\sigma_{*}\rho^{*}(E)
. Recall the definition of
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
in Section
2.4
.
Since
f=(Z,\rho,\sigma)
is iso-spatial (in the sense of Definition
2.36
),
\sigma\colon Z\to H_{0}
is a submersion. So
\sigma_{*}
is simply taking
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
-invariant sections. Note that
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
only acts on
Z
while
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
acts on
Z
as well as on the fibers of
E
.

Since
\rho\colon Z\to G_{0}
and
\sigma\colon Z\to H_{0}
are local diffeomorphisms, for a point
x\in H_{0}
, we can pick a neighborhood
U
of
x
in
H_{0}
such that
\sigma^{-1}(U)
and
\rho(\sigma^{-1}(U))
are finite disjoint unions of open subsets which are diffeomorphic to
U
. More precisely, we know
\sigma^{-1}\{x\}
is a discrete subset of
Z
and
\rho(\sigma^{-1}\{x\})
is a discrete subset of
G_{0}
. Furthermore, we have diffeomorphisms

(9.1)

\sigma^{-1}(U)\cong U\times\sigma^{-1}\{x\},\quad\text{and }\rho(\sigma^{-1}(U))\cong U\times\rho(\sigma^{-1}\{x\}).

Since the map
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\hookrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is saturated, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{G}
acts transitively on
\rho(\sigma^{-1}\{x\})
. Pick a point
\tilde{x}\in\rho(\sigma^{-1}\{x\})\subset G_{0}
, we have the following result.

Lemma 9.2
.

We have the following bijection between sets:

(9.2)

\rho^{-1}(\tilde{x})\cap\sigma^{-1}(x)\cong\mathcal{H}_{x},

hence a diffeomorphism

(9.3)

\rho^{-1}(\tilde{U})\cap\sigma^{-1}(U)\cong U\times\mathcal{H}_{x},

where
\mathcal{H}_{x}
is the isotropy group of
x
.

Proof of Lemma
9.2
.

(
9.3
) follows from (
9.2
) so it suffices to prove (
9.2
).

We pick and fix a
z\in\rho^{-1}(\tilde{x})\cap\sigma^{-1}(x)
. Since the
\mathcal{H}
-action on
Z
is free, it is clear that
z\mathcal{H}_{x}
, which is a subset of
\rho^{-1}(\tilde{x})\cap\sigma^{-1}(x)
, is bijective to
\mathcal{H}_{x}
.

Moreover, since
Z/\mathcal{H}\cong G_{0}
, for any
z^{\prime}\in\rho^{-1}(\tilde{x})\cap\sigma^{-1}(x)
, there exists
h\in H_{1}
such that
z^{\prime}h=z
. Since
\sigma(z^{\prime})=\sigma(z)=x
, we get
xh=x
, i.e.
h\in\mathcal{H}_{x}
. Therefore, we know that
z\mathcal{H}_{x}
is the whole set
\rho^{-1}(\tilde{x})\cap\sigma^{-1}(x)
, hence the latter is bijective to
\mathcal{H}_{x}
. This completes the proof of (
9.2
).
∎

By definition, we know that

(9.4)

\Gamma(U,\sigma_{*}\rho^{*}(E))=\Gamma(\sigma^{-1}(U),\rho^{*}(E))^{\mathcal{G}}.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{x}}
be the isotropy group at
\tilde{x}\in G_{0}
. Since the map
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\hookrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is saturated,
\mathcal{G}
acts transitively on
\rho(\sigma^{-1}\{x\})
. Hence we have

(9.5)

9.6.
Uniqueness of orbifold Chern character

Here we present a proof of Theorem
1.2
, which we reproduce below:

Theorem

(=Theorem
1.2
)
.

The orbifold Chern character
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}\colon K(X)\to H_{\text{BC}}^{(=)}(IX,\mathbb{C})
in (
1.4
) is the unique group homomorphism satisfying

(
\star 1
)

For holomorphic vector bundles
E
on complex orbifolds, our definition of
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}(E)
agrees with the one in
[
38
, Section 1.2]
.

(
\star 2
)

\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}
is functorial under pullbacks.

(
\star 3
)

\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}
satisfies the Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck formula for orbifold embeddings, Equation (
1.5
).

Proof.

Certainly
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}
defined in this paper satisfies the conditions (
\star 1
)-(
\star 3
). To show that any such group homomorphism must coincide with
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}
, we may adopt the argument in the proof of
[
32
, Theorem 8]
. This requires two general results:

(A)

For a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, there exists a bimeromorphic map
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to X
which is the composition of a sequence of blow-ups along smooth centers, such that the pullback
\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}
admits a locally free quotient of maximal rank.

As indicated in
[
32
, Proposition 7]
, this is an immediate consequence of Hironaka’s flattening result
[
34
]
. To make this work for orbifolds, we simply observe that Hironaka’s flattening result is valid for complex orbifolds, because the desired property (flatness) and the construction (blow-ups along smooth centers) commute with étale base changes.

(B)

There exists an isomorphism between
G
-theory
G(D)
of coherent sheaves on a smooth divisor
D\subset\tilde{X}
and the
G
-theory
G_{D}(\tilde{X})
of coherent sheaves on
\tilde{X}
supported on
D
.

By the argument of Proposition 7.2 in the arXiv version of
[
32
]
, this follows from the dévissage theorem (see e.g.
[
56
, Devissage Theorem 6.3]
) applied to abelian categories of coherent sheaves on orbifolds.

With these in hand, we proceed to prove the Theorem. Suppose
\text{ch}^{\prime}:K(-)\to H_{\text{BC}}^{(=)}(-,\mathbb{C})
is another group homomorphism satisfying (
\star 1
)-(
\star 3
). We prove
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}=\text{ch}^{\prime}
by induction on the dimension of the orbifold
X
. The base case, i.e. the
0
-dimensional case, is obvious because (
\star 1
) fixes the Chern character in terms of representation theory of the stabilizer groups.

Suppose the dimension of
X
is positive. Given a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, we would like to show that
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}(\mathcal{F})=\text{ch}^{\prime}(\mathcal{F})
. By (A), there exists a bimeromorphic map
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to X
which is the composition of a sequence of blow-ups along smooth centers, such that the pullback
\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}
admits a locally free quotient
\mathcal{E}
of maximal rank. Furthermore, Hironaka’s result allows us to assume that the support of the kernel of the quotient map
\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}
and the exceptional locus of
\pi
are both contained in a simple normal crossing divisor
D\subset\tilde{X}
. Therefore, the difference
\pi^{!}[\mathcal{F}]-[\mathcal{E}]
is in the
G
-theory
G_{D}(\tilde{X})
of coherent sheaves on
\tilde{X}
supported on
D
.

Let
D_{i}\subset D
be the irreducible components of
D
. By assumption,
D_{i}
are smooth of dimension one less than that of
\tilde{X}
. The natural map
\oplus_{i}G_{D_{i}}(\tilde{X})\to G_{D}(\tilde{X})
is surjective. By (B),
G_{D_{i}}(\tilde{X})=G(D_{i})
.

By induction,
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}=\text{ch}^{\prime}
on complex orbifolds of dimensions less than that of
\tilde{X}
. Therefore
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}
and
\text{ch}^{\prime}
define the same Todd class on
D_{i}
. By (
\star 3
),
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}
and
\text{ch}^{\prime}
are equal on
G_{D_{i}}(\tilde{X})
. By (
\star 1
),
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}(\mathcal{E})=\text{ch}^{\prime}(\mathcal{E})
. Therefore,
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}(\pi^{!}[\mathcal{F}])=\text{ch}^{\prime}(\pi^{!}[\mathcal{F}])
. By (
\star 2
), it follows that
\pi^{*}(\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}(\mathcal{F})-\text{ch}^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}))=0
. Since
\pi
is a composition of blow-ups along smooth centers, the pull-back map
\pi^{*}
on cohomology is injective. Thus
\text{ch}_{\text{BC}}(\mathcal{F})=\text{ch}^{\prime}(\mathcal{F})
.
∎

A.4.
Graph generalized morphisms

For a generalized morphism
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
, we can define its
graph
generalized morphism from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}
as the triple

(\text{Gr}(Z),\text{Gr}(\rho),\text{Gr}(\sigma)),

where

(A.8)

\text{Gr}(Z):=\{(g,z)\in G_{1}\times Z|s(g)=\rho(z)\}.

The map
\text{Gr}(\rho)\colon\text{Gr}(Z)\to G_{0}
is defined to be

\text{Gr}(\rho)(g,z):=t(g),

and the map
\text{Gr}(\sigma)\colon\text{Gr}(Z)\to G_{0}\times H_{0}
is defined to be

\text{Gr}(\sigma)(g,z):=(s(g),\sigma(z)).

The left
\mathcal{G}
-action on
\text{Gr}(Z)
is defined to be

(A.9)

\tilde{g}(g,z):=(\tilde{g}g,z),

and the right
\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}
-action on
\text{Gr}(Z)
is defined to be

(A.10)

(g,z)(\tilde{g},h):=(g\tilde{g},\tilde{g}^{-1}zh).

It is easy to check that
(\text{Gr}(Z),\text{Gr}(\rho),\text{Gr}(\sigma))
is indeed a generalized morphism from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}
.

Moreover, we consider the natural projection
\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
. It is easy to see that the associated comma generalized morphism is given by the triple
(G_{0}\times H_{1},\text{pr}_{1}\times t,s\circ\text{pr}_{2})
. It is easy to check that the composition of
(\text{Gr}(Z),\text{Gr}(\rho),\text{Gr}(\sigma))
with
(G_{0}\times H_{1},\text{pr}_{1}\times t,s\circ\text{pr}_{2})
is canonically isomorphic to
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
. This justifies the term “graph generalized morphism”.

Proposition A.10
.

For a generalized morphism
(Z,\rho,\sigma)
from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
, the graph generalized morphism
(\text{Gr}(Z),\text{Gr}(\rho),\text{Gr}(\sigma))
is an embedding from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

The proof consists of the following lemmas.

First, we show that the image of
\text{Gr}(\sigma)\colon\text{Gr}(Z)\to G_{0}\times H_{0}
is a closed subset.

Lemma A.11
.

The map
\text{Gr}(\sigma)
is an immersion.

Proof of Lemma
A.11
.

Let
(g,z)
be a point in
\text{Gr}(Z)
. We know
s(g)=\rho(z)
. Notice that
\rho\colon Z\to G_{0}
is a surjective submersion and
\mathcal{H}
acts on
Z
properly and freely. Since
\mathcal{H}
is a proper étale groupoid, we know that
\rho
is a local diffeomorphism.

Let
U
be a neighborhood of
s(g)
,
V
be a neighborhood of
g
, and
W
be a neighborhood of
z
, such that
s\colon V\to U
and
\rho\colon W\to U
are diffeomorphisms. Therefore,
V\times_{G_{0}}W\cong U
is a neighborhood of
(g,z)
in
\text{Gr}(Z)
, and the map

\text{Gr}(\sigma)\colon V\times_{G_{0}}W\to G_{0}\times H_{0}

is given by

(g^{\prime},w)\mapsto(g^{\prime},\sigma\circ\rho^{-1}\circ s(g^{\prime})).

On

,
\text{Gr}(\sigma)
is the graph of the map
\sigma\circ\rho^{-1}\circ s\colon V\to W
.
Hence
\text{Gr}(\sigma)
is an immersion.
∎

Lemma A.12
.

The map
\text{Gr}(\sigma)
is a closed map.

Proof of Lemma
A.12
.

Let

\{(\tilde{g}_{k},z_{k})\}\in\text{Gr}(Z)=G_{1}\times_{G_{0}}Z

\text{Gr}(\sigma)(\tilde{g},z)=(g,h)

Recall that

We denote

by

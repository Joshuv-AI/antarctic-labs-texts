Complete quasinormal modes of Type-D black holes
Changkai Chen, Jiliang Jing, Zhoujian Cao, Mengjie Wang

Abstract
Quasinormal mode (QNM) spectra of black holes exhibit two open problems [Conf. Proc. C 0405132 , 145 (2004); CQG 26 , 163001 (2009)]: (i) the discontinuity in highly damped QNMs between Schwarzschild and Kerr solutions as a → 0 a\to 0 , and (ii) the unexplained spectral proximity between QNMs and algebraically special (AS) frequencies, particularly the anomalous multiplet splitting for Kerr ℓ = 2 \ell=2 , m ≥ 0 m\geq 0 modes. We develop a novel method to compute complete QNM spectra for Type-D black holes, solving both problems and establishing a mathematical framework for boundary value problems of dissipative systems. Using analytic continuation of radial eigenvalue equations, our method eliminates the dependence on auxiliary parameters in the connection formulas for confluent Heun solutions. This breakthrough overcomes the long-standing challenge of calculating QNMs that cross or lie on the negative imaginary axis (NIA). For Schwarzschild and Kerr spacetimes ( 0 ≤ n ≤ 41 0\leq{n}\leq 41 , 2 ≤ ℓ ≤ 16 2\leq\ell\leq 16 ), we present complete spectra validated through scattering amplitudes with errors < 10 − 10 <10^{-10} . The results provide definitive solutions to both open problems: (i) The inability of conventional methods to compute QNMs crossing or residing on the NIA leads to apparent discontinuities in Kerr spectra as a → 0 a\to 0 . (ii) When a QNM exactly coincides with the AS frequency, an additional QNM (unconventional mode) appears nearby. For the Kerr case with ℓ = 2 \ell=2 , overtone sequences from both unconventional and AS modes exhibit precisely 2 ​ ℓ + 1 2\ell+1 branches, without multiplets or supersymmetry breaking at AS frequencies. Our method confirms Leung’s conjecture of high- ℓ \ell unconventional mode deviations from the NIA through the first ℓ = 3 \ell=3 calculation. Moreover, this paradigm surpasses the state-of-the-art Cook’s continued fraction and isomonodromic methods in computational efficiency.

II.4.2
Branch-Cut for QNMs and HeunC Function

For QNM frequencies in the complex plane, there exists a branch cut line known as the NIA
Berti and Cardoso 2006
;
Ching et al. 1995
;
Jensen and Candelas 1986
.
In this subsubsection, we develop a method inspired by Fiziev’s epsilon method
Fiziev and Staicova 2010
;
Fiziev and Staicova 2011
to handle the branch cut in QNM spectra.
However, prior to this development, it is necessary to classify the two types of roots in QNM spectra.
Due to the fundamental symmetry of the ATE (
11
), type-D BHs exhibit two distinct sets of complex frequencies for arbitrary BH parameters (rotation or charge).
For instance, previous literature
Berti et al. 2009
;
Cook and Zalutskiy 2016b
often classified Kerr QNMs into two groups based on the sign of their real parts: positive-frequency (
\mathrm{Re}(\omega)>0
) and negative-frequency (
\mathrm{Re}(\omega)<0
) modes.
However, we disagree with this classification, since it is possible for a QNM sequence crossing the NIA to contain both
\mathrm{Re}(\omega)>0
and
\mathrm{Re}(\omega)<0
modes.
Therefore, we suggest using Class-I solutions
\omega_{\text{I}}
and Class-II solutions
\omega_{\text{II}}
to represent the complex frequencies, rather than the positive-frequency
\omega_{+}
and negative-frequency
\omega_{-}
notation.
Furthermore,
\omega^{*}_{\text{I}}
indicates that its corresponding QNMs are predominantly distributed in the first quadrant. Similarly,
\omega^{*}_{\text{II}}
indicates that the associated QNMs are predominantly distributed in the second quadrant.

Next, we provide a mathematically rigorous reformulation of the epsilon method
Fiziev and Staicova 2010
;
Fiziev and Staicova 2011
.
As a multivalued function, the confluent Heun function

has branch cut

in its

-domain.
Using the Möbius transformation (
47
), we can derive the branch cut of
r
is the negative real axis
{\mathscr{B}^{\,\,r}_{1,\infty}}=(-\infty,0)

Subsequently, we observe that the phase difference between the two branch cuts

and

is exactly

, which precisely satisfies the phase constraint condition (

Remarkably, this condition (

and
\omega

Therefore, we can introduce a small quantity (

V.1.4
Extreme Kerr QNMs

The QNM spectrum of Kerr BHs exhibits accumulation points (degeneracies) in the extremal spin limit, which coincide with the critical frequency for superradiance. Specifically,

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 1}\omega_{\mathrm{I}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to 1}m\Omega=\frac{m}{2},\quad m>0,

(117a)

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 1}\omega_{\mathrm{II}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to 1}m\Omega=\frac{m}{2},\quad m<0,

(117b)

where
\Omega=\tfrac{a}{2Mr_{+}}
denotes the horizon’s angular velocity.
This limiting behavior was first predicted by Detweiler
Detweiler 1980
.
Berti and Cardoso
Berti et al. 2003
;
Cardoso 2004
subsequently extended and refined his analysis, ultimately deriving
eq.

117
.
As shown in
eq.

117
, the imaginary part of the QNM frequencies vanishes in the limit
a\to 1
, leaving only the real component. This implies that QNMs at extremal spin lose their damping behavior, retaining only oscillations. Yang et al.
Yang et al. 2013a
;
Yang et al. 2013b
termed these modes zero-damping modes (ZDMs), in contrast to damped modes (DMs) where the real part remains finite. Using matched asymptotic expansions, Yang et al. derived an approximate formula for frequencies near the accumulation points. Subsequently, Cook provided more accurate fitting expressions based on high-precision QNM spectra in near-extremal Kerr
Cook and Zalutskiy 2014
. Using the MST method, Casals et al.
Casals and Longo Micchi 2019
computed both the QNMs and superradiant amplification factors for near-extremal and extremal spins, while demonstrating the formation of additional superradiant branch cuts.

VI
CONCLUSION

In this work, we develop an analytic continuation method to compute the complete QNM spectrum of Type-D BHs.
The Teukolsky equation for Type-D BHs admits exact solutions in terms of confluent Heun functions.
Therefore, the angular eigenvalue equation is constructed by the Wronskian containing the confluent Heun function, while the radial eigenvalue equation involves the connection coefficients of the confluent Heun function.
Existing analytic calculations of connection coefficients necessitate auxiliary parameters like the renormalized angular momentum
\nu
, incurring significant computational burden.
Our method circumvents these parameters (like
\nu
) through analytic continuation, enabling efficient solution of QNM frequencies.
Crucially, conventional numerical methods fail when the QNMs cross or lie on the NIA.
Our method resolves these cases, successfully obtaining these QNMs.
To verify the accuracy of the QNM spectrum, we derive analytic expressions for the scattering amplitudes of Type-D BHs, based on prior work
Chen and Jing 2023
;
Chen and Jing 2024
. These scattering amplitudes provide crucial insights into the properties of complex frequencies.
Our method is equally applicable to the calculation and verification of TTM spectra.
We have numerically computed the complete QNM spectra for both Schwarzschild and Kerr BHs, covering the parameter ranges
s\in\{-2,-1,0\}
,
0\leq n\leq 41
, and
2\leq\ell\leq 16
. Additionally, the QNM spectrum for massive scalar perturbations in near-extremal Kerr spacetime has been computed.
Our extensive numerical simulations demonstrate that our method achieves high-precision solutions with superior computational efficiency.
Compared to existing approaches, our method shows better performance than Cook’s modified CFM, which is currently considered the state of the art for solving Kerr QNMs.
Compared with the isomonodromic method of the same type
Cavalcante et al. 2024a
;
Cavalcante et al. 2024b
, our method exhibits significantly greater computational efficiency.
This enhanced computational performance enables the rapid identification of exceptional points in parameter space.

For

Previous incomplete spectra suggested artificial discontinuities because most numerical methods cannot cover the entire complex plane.

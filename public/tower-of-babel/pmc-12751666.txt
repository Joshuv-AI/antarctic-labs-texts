Identification of candidate gene controlling TSSL via BSA-seq and fine mapping and germplasm innovation in rice
Authors: Xiaojing Dang, Wanlin Wang, Jinbo Liu, Chunyu Jing, Changmin Hu, Jiahui Li, Jiaming Shen, Erbao Liu, Jianhua Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractStigma exsertion ratio (SER) of rice male sterile line directly affects the hybrid rice F1 seed production, while SER was directly affected by the total of stigma and style length (TSSL). To improve the F1 seed production, it is necessary to identify the genes or quantitative trait loci (QTLs) controlling TSSL. In this study, we used the 7001 S with short TSSL, Zitai S with long TSSL and F2 to preliminarily map the QTL controlling TSSL to a 2.71 Mb interval using BSA-seq method. Based on the known SSR marker and newly designed InDel markers, the target region was further narrowed down to a 58.2 kb interval between TSL25 and TSL128 on chromosome 3. Gene knockout experiment was used to confirm Os03g0407400 (GS3) as a candidate gene controlling TSSL. Furthermore, according to the sequence alignment difference in the coding region of GS3 between 7001 S and Zitai S, the marker M1 was developed and revealed to be closely linked to TSSL. Combining fertility identification and molecular marker-assisted selection, a fertility-stable near-isogenic line (NIL) of C815S with long TSSL was created. It was found through small-scale F1 seed production combination trials that the outcrossing seed setting ratio of combination NIL-C815S × R919 was 39.31%, and the yield of the F1 seeds harvested from the female parents in the unit area was 237.50 g, which were both higher than that of the combination C815S × R919. These results laid a foundation for further research on the TSSL trait in rice and enriched its molecular regulatory network.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-025-12378-z.Keywords: BSA-seq, Candidate gene, Molecular marker-assisted selection, Stigma exsertion ratio, Germplasm innovation

Introduction

More than 65% of China’s population relies on rice as their main food. In the face of a continuously growing population and millions of arable land lost every year, the only choice for ensuring food security is to significantly increase the yield per unit area [1, 2]. The hybrid rice, which was developed in the 1970 s, has increased the rice yield by 20% compared to conventional rice at the same maturity period, and its commercialization has significantly improved rice production capacity [3]. The annual planting area of hybrid rice in our country is about 16.7 million hectares, and the annual seed production area is as high as 180,000 hectares [4]. Although the average hybrid F1 seed production has increased from 0.27 t hectares− 2 in 1976 to 2.7 t hectares− 2 now, with few cases of very high seed production of 4.0 t hectares− 2 [5], the cost of seed production has been increasing continuously with the acceleration of urbanization and the shortage of labor. Increasing the F1 seed production is a decisive factor in reducing the cost of hybrid rice and increasing the benefits of planting. Research shows that the low outcrossing rate of sterile line is the main factor of hindering the increase in F1 seed production [6]. The stigma exsertion in the sterile line is the key factor to limit the outcrossing rate in hybrid F1 seed production [7]. Dai et al. [8] found that the stigma exsertion ratio (SER) is significantly positively correlated with the hybrid F1 seed production. Tian et al. [9] found that the average seed setting rate of rice spikelets with exserted stigmas after closed florets was 64.0%, which was much higher than that of the rice spikelets with non-exserted stigmas (15.7%). Yang [10] found that under natural conditions, for every 1% point increase in the exserted stigma of the sterile line, the seed setting rate could increase by 0.74–0.92% points, and the hybrid F1 seed production per hectare could increase by 47–68 kg.

The total of stigma and style length (TSSL) is one of the important stigma related traits to affect SER in rice. The TSSL is significantly or extremely positively correlated with the SER [11–14]. The pace of research progress related to TSSL is seriously lagging behind compared to other traits in rice. The current research on stigma related traits mainly focuses on the SER. Approximately 162 quantitative trait loci (QTLs) controlling SER distributing on 12 rice chromosomes had been detected by different populations, such as F2 populations [15–19], recombinant inbred lines (RILs) population [20–22], backcross inbred lines (BILs) population [23], doubled haploid lines (DHLs) population [24], and natural population [14, 25]. Among these QTLs, qSE7 [15], qSER-7 [17] and qSE11 [20] were finely mapped into 28.4 kb, 322.9 kb and 350.7 kb intervals, respectively. qSE1 and qSE9 were fine-mapped to intervals 246.5 kb and 341.4 kb, respectively [26]. Only OsSPL5 was cloned and confirmed that it can promote rice outcrossing efficiency by G-protein pathway [27].

Zhou et al. [14] confirmed that three major grain shape genes GS3, GW5 and GW2 had pleiotropic effects on SER. Similarly, the alleles gs3, gw5, GW7 and gw8 in constructed single segment substitution lines had pleiotropic effects on grain shape and SER [28]. Knocking out the grain shape genes GS3, GW8, and GS9 can improve SER and increase the outcrossing ratio [29]. These facts indicated that grain shape regulating genes have pleiotropic effects on SER.

However, fewer studies have focused on the TSSL. A total of 39 QTLs controlling TSSL on all 12 chromosomes except chromosome 8 using F2 population [30], RILs population [21], chromosome segment substitute lines (CSSLs) population [31], and natural population [32–34]. Among these QTLs, only qTSSL3.1 was finely mapped into a 19.8 kb interval on the short arm of chromosome 3 [31]. These results not only indicate that the current research on TSSL is limited and superficial, but also suggest that this may also explain why there are no reports of using related research results assist in the selection and breeding of sterile lines with long TSSL and high SER.

The bulked segregant analysis (BSA) is a commonly used method for gene mapping based on extreme phenotypes. By combining next-generation sequencing with BSA, the BSA-seq method can quickly locate the QTLs or genes associated with the target trait without constructing a genetic map [35–37]. In recent years, with the development of sequencing technologies, BSA-seq has been widely used in the study of important agronomic traits in rice. Guo et al. [38] used F2 populations to perform BSA-seq analysis on cold tolerance in rice, identifying three candidate gene intervals on chromosomes 6 and 9, respectively. Wang et al. [39] used the BSA-seq method to detect five QTLs controlling rice storability locating on chromosomes 1 and 2. One candidate gene was identified controlling cold tolerance at the early seedling stage from Dongxiang wild rice by QTL mapping, BSA-seq and RNA-seq [35]. Wu et al. [36] identified COLD11 and OsCTS11 via BSA-seq and fine mapping at the rice seedling stage. One novel QTL was detected for Meloidogyne graminicola resistance in rice by integrated transcriptome and BSA-seq analysis [37].

This study used the japonica type photothermal sensitive nuclear male sterile line 7001 S with short TSSL and low SER, which was independently selected by the research group [40], as the female parent, and the indica temperature sensitive nuclear male sterile line Zitai S with long TSSL and high SER as the male parent to construct F2 segregating populations. In the F2 population, individuals with small value of TSSL and individuals with large value of TSSL were selected to construct the mixed pools, respectively. The BSA-seq method was used to map TSSL. Combining the published SSR marker in Gramene database, qTSSL3 was identified in the interval of RM6931-RM2346 (2.71 Mb) on chromosome 3. Furthermore, by the constructed secondary F2 population and increasing marker density, qTSSL3 was fine-mapped to a 58.2 kb interval on chromosome 3, in which region there are five candidate genes. By gene knockout experiment, Os03g0407400 (GS3) was verified as a candidate gene. Targeting the sequence differences between 7001 S and Zitai S of the candidate gene Os03g0407400, we developed M1 marker. Then the near-isogenic line (NIL) of C815S was created based on the crosses and backcross with molecualr marker-assisted selection. And the outcrossing seed setting rate and yield per unit area of NIL-C815S was significantly higher than that of C815S in hybrid F1 seed production condition. These results provide theoretical basis and material foundation for the creation of rice temperature-sensitive genic male sterile lines with long TSSL and high SER.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

The test materials include 7001 S, Zitai S, F1, F2 and secondary F2 populations. 7001 S is a japonica type rice photothermal sensitive nuclear male sterile line with colorless short TSSL and low SER, while Zitai S is an indica type rice temperature sensitive nuclear male sterile line with purple long TSSL and high SER.

Construction of the research population and field planting

The process of constructing the research population used in this study was shown in Fig. S1. The F1 seeds were derived from a cross of 7001 S as a female parent and Zitai S as a male parent at the Lingshui experimental station of the Rice Research Institute of Anhui Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Hainan, China (18°49’’N, 110°3’E) in April of 2019 and planted at the Lujiang experimnetal station of the Rice Research Institute of Anhui Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Hefei, China (31°30’N, 117°18’E) in 2019 summer. Meanwhile, the true F1 was backcrossed with 7001 S to obtain BC1F1 and self-crossed to obtain F2. The individuals of BC3F1 were identified using the molecular markers. The individuals of BC3F1 containing the chromosome segment of Zitai S and with high background recovery rate were selected to backcross with 7001 S. BC5F1 individuals with high background recovery rate were selfed to generate BC5F2. In the BC5F2 population, the individuals containing one chromosome segment of Zitai S was the NIL of 7001 S. The secondary F2 population was derived from a cross of 7001 S and the NIL of 7001 S containing the target fragment, which was used for further fine mapping.

The experimental materials planted in the Lujiang experimental station were sown on May 12 and transplanted on June 17. The experimental materials planted in the Lingshui experimental station were sown on November 12 and transplanted on December 15. The space between the plants and rows was 16.7 cm × 26.7 cm. Each row planted nine plants with conventional field management.

Phenotype identification

At full bloom, ten open spikelets were collected from the main stem panicle (the highest panicle). The sampling time every day is from 10:00 to 14:00. Eight intact pistils were taken out the spikelets and placed under a stereomicroscope (MZ11, Guangzhou Mingmei Photoelectric Technology Co., Ltd.) equipped with a camera to photograph. The TSSL was measured on the image using the image processing system (MicroShot v1.2, Guangzhou Mingmei Photoelectric Technology Co., Ltd.). The mean value of 16 TSSL values was calculated and used as the TSSL phenotypic value for each plant.

For grain shape traits, the seeds were harvested from five developed plants and dried under natural condition. Fully filled seeds were selected for trait investigation. Ten randomly chosen grains from each test material were used to measure the grain length (GL), grain width (GW), and grain thickness (GT) using an electronic digital Vernier caliper. The average of 10 values was used as the phenotypic value for corresponding trait of the test material. The 1,000-grain weight (TGW) was calculated based on 1,000 grains with three repetitions.

To calculate the SER, SER included three categories, the single stigma exsertion ratio (SSER), the dual stigma exsertion ratio (DSER) and the total stigma exsertion ratio (TSER). After anthesis, 10 main panicles from 10 individuals of each test material were used to calculate the number of spikelets with SSER, DSER and TSER. SER is calculated using the following formulas: (1) SSER=(Number of single exsertion spikelets per panicle/Number of spikelets per panicle) × 100%; (2) DSER=(Number of double exsertion spikelets per panicle/Number of spikelets per panicle) × 100%; (3) TSER = SSER + DSER.

Mixed pool sequencing

Based on the TSSL phenotypes of two parents and F2 plants, two parental pools and two extreme phenotype bulk pools were constructed, respectively. For 7001S, the pool was mixture 10 plants of 7001S and named as R001. For Zitai S, the pool was mixture 10 plants of Zitai S and named as R002. Among the F2 plants, 30 plants with TSSL phenotype less than that of 7001S and 30 plants with TSSL phenotype higher than that of Zitai S were selected to form two offspring bulk pools and named R004 and R005, respectively. DNA was mixed in equal amounts to form two parental pools and two offspring pools, and sent to Beijing Biomarker Technologies Co., Ltd. for sequencing. After the sample inspection is qualified, the DNA is randomly fragmented into 350 bp fragments by ultrasonic crushing. The DNA fragments are then to end repair, A-tailing at the 3’ end, ligation of sequencing adapters, purification, and PCR amplification to complete the construction of the library. The library was sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq after passing quality inspection.

Sequencing data processing

The raw sequencing reads need to be filtered and screened. The BWA software [41] is used to align the high-quality clean reads with the reference genome Nipponbare. The size and distribution of the inserts fragments were examined using Picard [42] software. The GATK software [43] was used to identify SNP variants. The SnpEff software [44] annotates small InDel sites. The detected SNPs and InDel sites were filtered to remove low-quality sites, and finally, association analysis was conducted using the euclidean distance (ED) algorithm [45] and the SNP/InDel-index algorithm [46].

ED algorithm is a method that uses sequencing data to find markers with significant differences between pools and thereby assesses the regions associated with traits. The calculation formula of the ED method is shown below. The larger the ED value, the greater the difference between the small value pool and the large value pool for that marker.

Here, Amut represents the frequency of the A base in the mutant mix pool, and Awt represents the frequency of the A base in the wild-type mix pool; Cmut represents the frequency of the C base in the mutant mix pool, and Cwt represents the frequency of the C base in the wild-type mix pool; Gmut represents the frequency of the G base in the mutant mix pool, and Gwt represents the frequency of the G base in the wild-type mix pool; Tmut represents the frequency of the T base in the mutant mix pool, and Twt represents the frequency of the T base in the wild-type mix pool. In the analysis, SNPs with genotype differences between the R004 bulk and R005 bulk were identified, and the depth of base at these sites in the two bulks was counted. The ED value at each site was then calculated. To eliminate the background effect, the original ED to the power of 5 was used as the correlation value in this experiment to achieve the purpose [45]. Then, the DISTANCE method was used to fit the ED values.

The SNP-index is a marker association analysis method based on the difference in genotype frequencies between mix pools, mainly to find significant differences in genotype frequencies between mix pools. The Δ(SNP-index) was used to statistic. The greater the association between the SNP and the trait, the closer the Δ(SNP-index) is to 1. The formula for calculating Δ(SNP-index) is as follows:

SNP-index (Mut) =ρx/(ρX + ρx)

SNP-index (Mut) =ρx/(ρX + ρx)

SNP-index (WT) =ρx/(ρX + ρx)

SNP-index (WT) =ρx/(ρX + ρx)

Δ(SNP-index) = SNP-index (Mut)-SNP-index (WT)

Δ(SNP-index) = SNP-index (Mut)-SNP-index (WT)

In the formula, Mut is the mutation pool of the offspring, i.e., the large value pool R005. WT is the wild pool of the offspring, i.e., the small value pool R004. ρX and ρx are the number of reads corresponding to the alleles of the wild-type parent 7001 S and the mutant parent Zitai S appearing in each pool, respectively. The difference between each site in the large value pool and the small value pool can be observed through the Δ(SNP-index). To eliminate false positive sites, the markers position on the genome can be used to fit the Δ(SNP-index) values for the markers on the same chromosome. This study fitted the Δ(SNP-index) value using the distance method. And the above situation also applies to InDel. Then the regions above the threshold were selected as trait-related regions.

Fine mapping

DNA extraction from parental and secondary F2 population plants was carried out using the CTAB method [47]. For the initial positioning interval, we searched for all published SSR primers to marker encryption. Meanwhile, according to the sequence alignment results of 7001 S and Zitai S, we also designed primers and selected high-quality primers with clear bands and polymorphism. Then, using the Nipponbare genome sequence as a reference sequence, the markers with better polymorphism were anchored on BAC/PAC clones to determine the physical positions and corresponding orders of the markers. Finally, the recombinant analysis was conducted by combining the phenotypic data of each plant with the marker genotypes at each locus, and the QTL position was determined by the recombinants number at each locus. The 12 µL PCR reaction system is as follows: 2 µL template DNA (50 ng/µL), 6 µL Taq 2× PCR Master Mix, 0.5µL of each primer (10 µmol/L), and sterile ddH2O to 12 µL. PCR amplification procedure: pre-denaturation at 94℃ for 4 min, denaturation 94℃ for 30 s, annealing at 55℃ for 45 s, and extension at 72℃ for 45 s, 35 cycles. The amplified products were detected by 8% PAGE and the bands were counted. Finally, the exchange rate was calculated based on the band results.

Candidate region functional annotation and candidate gene screening

Sequence alignment was conducted using BLAST software with the NR database [48], GO database [49], KEGG database [50], and COG database [51], in combination with the annotations of the gene coding regions, to determine the candidate genes.

Real-time quantitative fluorescence PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from pistils at stage 8 of inflorescence development [52] sampled from 7001 S and NIL-7001 S using the RNA Easy Fast Plant Tissue Kit (Tiangen Biotech (Beijing) Co. Ltd, Beijing, China). cDNA was synthesized using 1 µg RNA by the HiScript II 1 st Strand cDNA synthesis Kit (Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd, Nanjing, China). RT-qPCR with 20 µl reaction volume was carried out using 0.4 µl cDNA, 0.4 µM of each gene-specific primer and the SYBR Green SuperReal Premix Plus Kit (Tiangen Biotech (Beijing) Co. Ltd, Beijing, China) in the Roche Applied Science LightCycler™ 480 (Roche diagnostics Ltd., Germany). The 2−ΔΔCT method [53] was used to calculate the relative gene expression level. The OsActin gene was used as an internal control. All primers are shown in Table S1.

Plasmid construction and rice transformation

To construct a plasmid vector expressing Cas9 of Os03g0407400, we designed and synthesized a 20-bp guide RNA (gRNA) using forward and reverse oligonucleotides. A knockout plasmid GS3Cas was created by incorporating the gRNA into the CRISPR/Cas9 vector as described [54]. The GS3Cas vector was introduced into rice calli via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation. The knockout was carried out in Zitai S. The primers used are listed in Table S1.

Pollen fertility observation

The mature pollen grains of variety R919, C815S, and NIL-C815S were stained by a 1% I2-KI water solution. Then they were examined under a light microscope (Olympus BH-2) under 100 × magnification.

Hybrid F1 seed production potential evaluation for the TSSL3 alleles in the paddy field

In order to evaluate the potential of the GS3 gene in the hybrid F1 rice seed production, an actual F1 seed production experiment was performed using C815S and NIL-C815S as the female parents and variety R919 as the common male parent in the paddy fields of Lujiang experimental station, Hefei. C815S is an indica type two-line nuclear sterile line used in commercial hybrid F1 seed production in Eastern China, and NIL-C815S is a near isogenic line of C815S obtained in this study. R919 is a commonly used restore line. The male and female parents were grown in a ratio of 1:6:1, i.e. 2 lines of R919 plants were planted around 6 lines of female plants. For each combination, 30 m2 of land was utilized for planting. Before artificial pollination at the flowering time, the pollen fertility of C815S and NIL-C815S were observed under a light microscope to ensure that the pollen of the female parents was sterile. The artificial supplementary pollination was carried out at twice per day during pollen dispersal. Thirty days after artificial supplementary pollination, the seeds from the female plants individually were harvested. According to the rice grains weight harvested from the female plants per unit area, the potential value of the GS3 for hybrid rice seed production was evaluated.

Results

Genetic analysis of TSSL trait

The TSSL of Zitai S was 2.942 ± 0.04 mm, which was significantly higher than that of 7001 S (1.642 ± 0.02 mm) (Fig. 1A and B, Table S2). The TSSL of F1 derived from 7001 S and Zitai S was 2.260 ± 0.04 mm (Table S2). The SER of Zitai S was 60.71 ± 2.22%, which was significantly higher than that of 7001 S (17.21 ± 1.31%) (Fig. 1C). The SER of F1 was 45.89 ± 2.01%, which was intermediate between the two parents. The plant morphology of 7001 S, F1 and Zitai S was shown in Fig. S2. Figure 1D-E displayed the rice grain morphology and brown rice morphology of 7001 S, F1, Zitai S, respectively. It was also found that there was significant difference in GL, GW and GT but not in TGW between 7001 S and Zitai S (Fig. 1F-I). The GL and GW of F1 were intermediate between 7001 S and Zitai S (Fig. 1F and G). The GT of F1 was significantly different from that of 7001 S but not significantly different from that of Zitai S (Fig. 1H). There were no significant differences in TGW among F1, 7001 S, and Zitai S (Fig. 1I). It has been shown that GL is significantly positively correlated with TSSL and SER [14, 34].

In this study, it can be confirmed that the longer the GL, the longer the TSSL, and the higher the SER. Meanwhile, these results indicated that the long TSSL allele is semi-dominant. In the F2 population, the genotype of each plant was identified based on the TSSL phenotype values (less than 1.65 mm as homozygous 7001 S genotype, 1.66–2.92 mm as heterozygous genotype, and greater than 2.93 mm as homozygous Zitai S genotype). It was found that the segregation ratio of TSSL was 1:2:1, i.e. 151 for homozygous 7001 S genotype: 307 for heterozygous genotype: 143 for homozygous Zitai S genotype (χ2 = 3.68 < χ20.05, 2 = 5.99), indicating that TSSL in this population is controlled by a single locus.

The raw sequencing reads were filtered. And a total of 62.91 G clean data was obtained with over 80% of the bases having a quality value greater than 30. The average sequencing depth per sample was 42.00 ×. The average genome coverage was 96.56%, and the average alignment efficiency to the reference genome was over 97.60% (Fig. 2A), indicating that the sequencing was normal and could be used for subsequent related studies.

Before conducting the association analysis, SNPs were filtered, i.e. first, SNP sites with multiple genotypes were filtered out; second, SNP sites with read support less than 4 were filtered out; third, SNP sites with identical genotypes between pools and SNP sites where the recessive gene was not from the recessive parent were filtered out. Finally, 1,529,248 high-quality and reliable SNP sites were obtained (Fig. 2B). Based on SNP site analysis, the ED algorithm was used (Fig. 3A). The median + 3SD of all SNP site fitting values was taken as the correlation threshold, resulting in 0.45. A total of one region was obtained based on the correlation threshold, in which there is a total length of 9.85 Mb including 1,640 genes (Fig. 3A and B). Among these genes, 1,086 genes had non-synonymous SNP sites. Using the Δ(SNP-index) algorithm, a total of three regions were obtained with a confidence of 0.99, covering a total of 19.24 Mb and containing 3,451 genes (Fig. 3C and D). Among these genes, 1,883 genes had non-synonymous mutations. The intersection of the two methods resulted in one candidate region associated with TSSL, with a total length of 9.85 Mb.

In summary, the overlapping regions from the SNP and InDel association analysis results were taken as the candidate regions for initial localization, resulting in a total of one trait-related candidate region with a total length of 9.46 Mb including 1,571 genes. Among these genes, there are 1,043 nonsynonymous mutations and 336 frame shift mutations.

Next, based on the Gramene database (https://archive.gramene.org/), there are 15 pairs of SSR markers found that have been published on chromosome 3 and show polymorphism between 7001 S and Zitai S (Table S3). The genotypes of 284 F2 individuals were investigated using the 15 pairs of SSR markers. By linkage analysis, qTSSL3 was located into a 2.71 Mb region between RM6931 and RM2346. The LOD value was 18.19 and the confidence interval was RM3280-RM5488 (Fig. 5A). This locus explained 27.30% of the TSSL phenotypic variation with an additive allele from Zitai S. The additive effect value was 0.04 mm. By integrating the above preliminary mapping results, qTSSL3 was finally located within the 2.71 Mb region between RM6931 and RM2346.

Fine mapping

Based on the sequencing data of BSA-seq, a total of 292 pairs primers were designed in the initial mapping interval of 2.71 Mb. Twenty-eight high-quality primers with clean bands and polymorphism were screened out by using the parents and 284 plants selected from F2 population and the primer information were listed in Table S4. qTSSL3 was mapped between the SSR markers RM6931 and RM2346 based on 284 plants randomly selected from the SSSL20/7001S F2 population (Fig. 5B). The 3,521 secondary F2 plants including the above 284 plants were identified using the two flanking markers of the confidence region obtained from the linkage analysis. Marker genotype analysis based on the RM6931 site found that there were 382 plants underwent recombination between the qTSSL3 site. There were 261 plants underwent recombination between the qTSSL3 site and RM2346 (Fig. 5B). Further marker encryption analysis found that 271, 189, 56, and seven recombination plants were identified at markers TSL43, TSL67, TSL112, and TSL25, respectively. Meanwhile, there were 13 and 72 recombination plants identified at markers TSL128 and TSL34, respectively (Fig. 5B). The genotypes and phenotypes of some of the recombination plants used for fine mapping were showed in Fig. S3. Therefore, qTSSL3 was mapped into the marker interval TSL25-TSL128, which spans a physical distance 58.2 kb and resides on the BAC clone clusters OSJNBa0031I04 and OSJNBa0034E08.

Candidate gene screening and expression analysis

Based on the BSA-seq sequencing data and the rice annotated gene database (http://rapdb.dna.affrc.go.jp/), a total of five annotated genes were found in the 58.2 kb region from TSL25 to TSL128 (Table S5). Among them, gene Os03g0406900, co-localizing with the known gene OsMac4, encoded a MAP kinase-activated that can enhance the translational efficiency of downstream genes [55]. Os03g0407400 encodes a heterotrimeric G-protein γ subunit and co-localized with gene GS3 [29, 56–58]. Then, by comparing the variants of 4,726 rice varieties published on RiceVarMap 2.0 (http://ricevarmap.ncpgr.cn/ncpgr.cn) with the variants of the five candidate genes, variants in four genes were excluded, and the candidate gene was inferred to be Os03g0407400 [59] (Fig. S4−8; Table S6). The previously reported gene GS3 was related to increase grain length and grain weight [14] and style length [34]. GS3 consisted of five exons and encoded 232 amino acids. The sequence alignment results showed that the base C was substituted to base A at position 197 of the cDNA, which resulted the amino acid cysteine encoded was changed to stop gained at the amino acid position 55 to affect its function (Fig. S7).

In order to further clarify the expression patterns of the candidate genes, the young panicles of 7001 S and NIL-7001 S at the stage 8 of young panicle differentiation were selected for RT-qPCR. The experimental results showed that the expression levels of Os03g0406900, Os03g0407000, Os03g0407100, and Os03g0407900 were not significantly different between 7001 S and NIL-7001 S (Fig. 6). However, the expression level of Os03g0407400 was significantly different between 7001 S and NIL-7001 S (Fig. 6). These results indicated that Os03g0407400 was a candidate gene for regulating TSSL.

Functional verification of Os03g0407400

To determine whether Os03g0407400 is the gene that causes changes in TSSL, we conducted genetic modification verification. The Os03g0407400 gene was knocked out using the CRISPR/Cas9 system, and Zitai S was transformed by the Agrobacterium-mediated method to obtain transgenic positive plants. Two independent mutations were constructed in Zitai S, including a 5-bp deletion and a 2-bp insertion in the target region (Fig. 7A). The phenotypic investigation showed that the TSSL of the progeny of Os03g0407400ZT knock-out plants was significantly shorter than that of the wild type (Fig. 7B and C). The SER of the two mutants were also significantly lower than that of the wild type (Fig. 7D). Consequently, it is further proven that the change in TSSL was caused by editing Os03g0407400.

Long TSSL two-line sterile materials created

Based on the sequence alignment differences in the coding region of the Os03g0407400 gene between 7001 S and Zitai S, an InDel marker M1 was developed (Forward sequence: CTGCAGAGAGGTTGACGA; Reverse sequence: TCACAAGCAGGGGGGGCAG). Meanwhile, the crosses and backcrosses were carried out using Zitai S as the donor parent and C815S, which is widely used in production, as the recipient parent. By combining fertility identification and molecular marker-assisted selection, a fertility-stable two-line sterile line material NIL-C815S with long TSSL was created (Fig. S9). The construction process of NIL-C815S was showed in Supplementary Fig. S10 with the number of plants used for crosses/backcrosses and the background recovery rate of each separate generation. Compared with C815S in fertile condition, NIL-C815S had a lower plant height and more tillers (Fig. S11). The TSSL of NIL-C815S (2.121 ± 0.04 mm) was significantly longer than that of C815S (1.934 ± 0.03 mm) (Fig. 8A and B). Figure 8C showed that the SER of NIL-C815S (58.32%) was also significantly higher than that of C815S (45.53%). The GL of NIL-C815S was 9.86 ± 0.28 mm, which was significantly greater than that of C815S (8.91 ± 0.27 mm) (Fig. 8D and E). There were no significant differences in GW between NIL-C815S and C815S (Fig. 8F and G). Figure 8H showed the morphology of brown rice of NIL-C815S and C815S. There were significant differences in GT and TGW between NIL-C815S and C815S (Fig. 8I and J).

To further evaluate the potential value of the GS3 in hybrid rice seed production, we performed a field experiment using the two combinations, C815S × R919 and NIL-C815S × R919. The pollen of plants in C815S and NIL-C815S were completely sterile and could not be stained by a 1% solution of I2-KI. And the pollen of plant in R919 was fully fertile and could be stained (Fig. 8K). So the F1 seeds were harvested by artificial supplementary pollination at the flowering stage. The outcrossing seed setting rate of NIL-C815S × R919 combination was 39.31%, which is significantly (P < 0.05) higher than that of the C815S × R919 combination (33.05%) (Fig. 8L). The yield of the F1 seeds harvested from the female parents in the unit area for the combination of NIL-C815S × R919 was 237.50 g, which is significantly (P < 0.05) higher than that of the combination of C815S × R919 (218.75 g) in the same area of land (Fig. 8M). Meanwhile, there was no significance difference in the number of spikelets per panicle, but significance difference in filled grain number per panicle between C815S and NIL-C815S in hybrid F1 seed production condition (Supplementary Fig. S12). These results indicate that the GS3 could significantly (P < 0.05) increase the F1 hybrid seeds production via enhancing the outcrossing seed setting rate of the male sterile lines.

Discussion

Compared with the homologous cloning and QTL mapping based on constructing genetic linkage maps, BSA-seq method only requires a small number of extreme individuals to build a bulk. There are differences in the target trait-related sites between the mixed pools, while the other sites tend to be consistent. These make it simple, efficient, rapid in mapping genes and free from the need to construct the genetic linkage map [60]. The detection results also provide information on the mutation sites within the interval, which offers references and convenience for molecular marker design during fine mapping. Chen et al. [61] used SSR markers combined with BSA-seq method to conduct linkage analysis in F2 populations obtained by crossing St.1 with three susceptible varieties, C101PKT, CO39, and AS20-1, respectively. And a major gene resistance to rice blast Pi37(t) was identified in the RM543-FPSM1 interval on chromosome 1. The interval size was 374 kb. Luo et al. [62] used the BSA-seq method to evaluate cold tolerance in rice and identified a total of 58 candidate genes, of which five genes showed significant differences in expression level. Wu et al. [63] conducted a BSA analysis on rice plant height using a CSSL population composed of 285 lines. The results showed that candidate intervals of 3.21 Mb and 1.31 Mb were associated on chromosomes 7 and 10, respectively. Wei et al. [64] conducted a BSA-seq analysis on the heading date of rice using the constructed F4 population. The results showed that there were 17 common associated loci of SNP and InDel obtained on chromosomes 6, 7, 8, and 9.

Compared with traditional QTL mapping, BSA-seq has the advantages of sequencing only two extreme phenotype individuals and not relying on genetic maps. In this study, we performed BSA-seq on two extreme phenotype individuals and only one locus was identified. We speculate that the difficulty in precise mapping all the associated sites controlling TSSL may be due to the fact that TSSL is a complex quantitative trait and the depth of BSA-seq is lower.

A total of 22 QTLs for SER [12, 14, 16, 18, 22–24, 30, 65–69] and 12 QTLs for TSSL [21, 30–32, 34] have been identified on chromosome 3, which revealed that there might be a gene cluster controlling stigma-related traits on chromosome 3. In this study, we utilized SNP and InDel markers with different algorithms and finally obtained a consistent QTL qTSSL3. After comparing the qTSSL3 with the gene/QTLs reported previously, it was overlapped with the known gene/QTLs GS3/SYL3 [56, 58, 70]. It is easy to understand this point. GS3 can significantly regulate the GL, which is significantly positively correlated with the style length (SYL). SYL is as a part of TSSL. Therefore, the longer the SYL, the longer the TSSL. The qTSSL3 was fine mapped into a 58.2 kb interval on chromosome 3 by map-based cloning. There were five candidate genes in this interval. After gene annotation, sequence alignment and RT-qPCR analysis, Os03g0407400 (GS3) was finally identified as the candidate gene. The QTL controlling SER on chromosome 3 reported by Miyata et al. [12], Feng et al. [66] and Yamamoto et al. [71] were all near GS3. Takano-Kai et al. [13] confirmed that GS3 can increase stigma length and SER by transgenic test. GS3 not only affects grain shape, but also the stigma length and SER. In the present study, the TSSL of the mutants were significantly decreased compared to that of the wild type by the knockout verification test (Fig. 7). This result provides strong evidence that Os03g0407400 is a candidate gene controlling qTSSL3. Since the GS3 locus had a significant effect on multiple traits and these effects were statistically associated, we considered this locus to be pleiotropic. These results laid the foundation for further cloning and functional analysis of qTSSL3.

There have been many reports on the functional study of GS3 [29, 57, 72, 73]. So this study no longer explores its function but rather its breeding utilization value in hybrid F1 seed production. Based on the sequence alignment differences in the coding region of the GS3 gene between 7001 S and Zitai S, the primer M1 was designed in this study. Combining fertility identification and molecular marker-assisted selection, the two-line sterile line C815S was improved using Zitai S as the donor parent and the fertility-stable NIL-C815S with long TSSL was created. The GL and TSSL of NIL-C815S were significantly larger than those of C815S, but there was no significant difference in GW between them. Next, the small-scale F1 seed production combination trials were carried out. The results showed that the outcrossing seed setting rate of combination NIL-C815S×R919 was 39.31%, and the yield of the F1 seeds harvested from the female parents in the unit area was 237.50 g, which were both higher than that of the combination C815S×R919. These results confirmed that GS3 could regulate TSSL.

Increasing the TSSL of the male sterile line can improve the SER to increase the outcrossing seed setting rate of the sterile line, which provides an effective method for increasing the hybrid rice F1 seed production. However, the reality is that traditional breeding methods have the disadvantages of low selection efficiency and long breeding cycle. Although the new sterile lines were developed with significantly improving SER, their potential value had not been fully mined. Therefore, using the molecular marker-assisted selection can provide theoretical guidance for breeding new sterile line with long TSSL and high SER.

The results of this study will lay a theoretical foundation for further research on TSSL, cloning of their controlling genes, and perfection of their molecular networks. Meanwhile, it provides a material basis for molecular breeding to create rice male sterile lines with long TSSL through gene knockout or site-directed mutagenesis method.

Conclusion

The candidate gene GS3 was identified in the 58.2 kb region via BSA-seq and fine mapping to control TSSL and confirmed by gene knockout test, which can be applied to elite germplasm innovation in rice.

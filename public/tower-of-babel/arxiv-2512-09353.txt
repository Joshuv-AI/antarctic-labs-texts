Bimetric MOND as a framework for variable- G G theories – local systems and cosmology
Mordehai Milgrom

Abstract
Bimetric MOND (BIMOND) is used as a platform for variable- G G theories that have MOND-specific idiosyncrasies. E.g., MOND premises dictate return to standard dynamics in the high-acceleration limit, predicting the standard value of G G for high-acceleration systems. This automatically ensures compliance of such theories with all the constraints on inconstancy of G G that emerge from the study of high-acceleration systems: geophysics, solar system, pulsars, supernovae, stellar evolution, emission of gravitational waves, etc. In MOND, constraints deduced from such phenomena have no bearing on possible G G variability in cosmology. My guiding motivation is to see if such theories may account for some roles of dark matter in cosmology; e.g., in accounting for the expansion history of the Universe in the matter-dominated era, by having a G e ≈ 2 ​ π ​ G G_{e}\approx 2\pi G govern the later stages of the expansion, instead of invoking matter density ≈ 2 π × \approx 2\pi\times baryon density. Introduction

MOND
milgrom83

,a_{0}

This means, formally, that a relation deduced with MOND, which typically contains
a_{0}
, should tend rapidly, as
a_{0}\rightarrow 0

a_{0}

If indeed fundamental, this coincidence tells us that either the cosmological state and evolution of the Universe at large strongly enters local dynamics, or that cosmology and local dynamics are affected by the same agent whose characteristic acceleration enters both cosmology and galactic dynamics. Various ways in which this can happen are sketched in Ref.

If we were convinced that the coincidence is fundamental – which is yet to be established – one would necessarily require from a fundamental MOND theory that it accounts for this coincidence.

But even if it is not fundamental, the mere numerical coincidence has some important implications for how MOND enters relativistic phenomena

Putting aside, then, the important question of what replaces (in MOND) the role of dark matter in CMB fluctuations, and structure formation, we ask, as a limited question, whether MOND can account, not only for the role of dark energy, but also for that of dark matter, in dictating the observed expansion history of the Universe. A possible pointer towards effecting this end is the fact that in this context, the densities of (NR) baryons,

A further motivation for such attempts comes from the fact – which is still a mystery – that the two densities are so near each other,
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle DM}\approx 5\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle b}

At present, the suggested class of theories remains an interesting framework for VGTs that aligns with MOND, from which they borrow heavily.

Our general observations above concerning MOND theories, apply, of course, to the BIMOND-based VGTs I shall discuss here. To recapitulate this important point:
In these theories, the return to standard gravity at high accelerations – a tenet of MOND – ensures compliance with the known constraints on

As regards low-acceleration, local systems, such as virialized galactic systems, I will require that BIMOND itself (without the VGT extension) accounts for galactic dynamics, and I shall, indeed, describe versions of the proposed VGMOND in which the variable-

I end this introduction by a general comment on relativistic MOND theories. Inasmuch as they tend quickly to GR for high-acceleration systems, such theories are not required to account for relativistic objects whose dynamics depart from GR, except for cosmology, which is a unique, and uncontrollable system.
I think then that we may be more forgiving to such theories of some issues that might only appear in applications to local systems (such as Ostrogradky’s instabilities, or the appearance of ghosts (see Ref.
dambrosio20

And if such theories turn out not to be “fundamental” – which known working theory is? – they can at least serve as useful heuristic tools. In the present discussion, they can limelight the idiosyncracies of MOND based VGTs.

S_{1}\equiv{\bar{g}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}C^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\lambda}C^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu\gamma},~~~S_{2}\equiv\bar{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}C_{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma},~~~S_{3}\equiv{\bar{g}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\bar{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu}\bar{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle\nu},~~~S_{4}\equiv{\bar{g}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}C_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu}C_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu},~~~S_{5}\equiv\bar{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\lambda}\bar{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\beta\mu}\bar{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma\nu}C^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\beta\gamma}C^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},

(10)

where

\bar{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}\equiv{\bar{g}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}C^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and
C_{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}\equiv C^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma\alpha}
are the two traces of
C^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}

II.1.1
Lensing constraints on the scalars

As discussed in Ref.
milgrom22
, the requirement of “correct” gravitational lensing limits the choice of scalar arguments that we can employ. By “correct” lensing, I mean that the NR metric
g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
can be brought (by an appropriate choice of gauge) to the form it has in GR,
g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-2\phi\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
; so that there is only one potential that governs the geodesics of both slow particles and photons.
This seems to hold in nature, at least approximately, when comparing the gravitational potentials deduced for a given body (e.g., a galaxy or a galaxy cluster) from lensing, and from slow-particle dynamics.

Expanding the two metrics around a common Minkowski background, we write to first order in the departures (the weak-field limit)

g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-2\phi\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},~~~~\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-2\hat{\phi}\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}.

(11)

Defining
\phi\equiv(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle 00}-g_{\scriptscriptstyle 00})/2
and
\hat{\phi}\equiv(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle 00}-\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle 00})/2
, we have
h_{\scriptscriptstyle 00}=\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle 00}=0
.
Also, the differences and sums of the potentials

\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}=\phi-\hat{\phi},~~~h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}~~~\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle+}=\phi+\hat{\phi},~~~h^{\scriptscriptstyle+}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}.

(12)

In the NR limit, we deal with static sources, and the energy-momentum tensors of matter are thus
T^{\scriptscriptstyle M}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\rho\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu 0}\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu 0}
, and similarly for the hatted one.

The BIMOND field equations imply that the mixed space-time potentials vanish (also follows from the time-reversal symmetry of the static sources). The sum and difference field equations involve, respectively, the sum and difference potentials, which thus decouple. The former is equivalent to the GR equations, with all the gauge freedom. So, as in GR, we can work in a gauge where
h^{\scriptscriptstyle+}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=0
.
To ensure correct lensing, it is thus necessary and sufficient that the NR field equations imply that
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}=0
.

By examining the field equation that results from variation of the NR action with respect to
h_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
and
\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
, is was shown in Ref.
milgrom22
that a sufficient condition for this is that the scalar arguments of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
do not contain mixed
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}-h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
terms.

The NR expressions of the independent scalars in Eq. (
10
) are (ignoring the mixed space-time components of the potentials):

\bar{S}_{1}=-2(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+\frac{1}{4}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle kj,i}(2h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ki,j}-h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle kj,i})-2\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}),

(13)

\bar{S}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i})-2\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}),

(14)

\bar{S}_{3}=(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i})(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle il,l}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}),

(15)

\bar{S}_{4}=4(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+\frac{1}{4}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}-2\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i},

(16)

\bar{S}_{5}=10(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+\frac{1}{4}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle kj,i}(3h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle kj,i}-2h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ki,j})+2\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}-\frac{3}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}).

(17)

(
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}=\sum_{\scriptscriptstyle k=1}^{\scriptscriptstyle 3}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle kk}
is the spatial trace of
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
).

Because there are two forms of mixed terms,
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}
and
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}
, eliminating them leaves us with a three-parameter class of scalars – call them “good scalar of the first type” – whose general form is
milgrom22

S_{q,u,v}=qS_{q}+uS_{u}+vS_{v};~~~~~S_{q}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(3S_{1}-2S_{2}-S_{3}-S_{4}+S_{5}),~~~S_{u}\equiv\frac{1}{4}(S_{1}-S_{4}+S_{5}),~~~S_{v}\equiv S_{3},

(18)

whose NR limit is

\bar{S}_{q,u,v}=u(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+\frac{u}{8}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,k}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,k}+\frac{q}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,k}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,j}-vh^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}+\frac{1}{4}(v-\frac{u}{4})h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}+(v-\frac{q}{2})h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,j}.

(19)

In Ref.
milgrom22
, I stated, mistakenly, that these are the only scalars compatible with correct lensing; i.e., that it is also necessary to employ only such scalars in the arguments of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
.
This, however is not the case: Reexamining the relevant field equations, I noticed that the condition for “correct” lensing, i.e.,
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}=0
, is satisfied even if
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
depends on scalars,
\tilde{S}
, that do include mixed,
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}-h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
terms, but do not include
(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}
terms – provided
\partial\tilde{\mathcal{M}}(\tilde{S},S_{1},...)/\partial\tilde{S}|_{\tilde{S}=0}=0
. The general form of such scalars is

\tilde{S}=\alpha S_{2}+\beta S_{4}+\gamma S_{5}+(2\beta+5\gamma)S_{1}+\varepsilon S_{3};

(20)

call them “good scalars of the second type”.

There is some overlap between the two types of scalars: those in Eq. (

\alpha=-2\gamma
and
\beta=-\gamma
are also of the first type, as they contain only terms with
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}

u=0

Since the scalars of the second type vanish everywhere in the NR limit (where
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}=0
), even if
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}

III
BIMOND framework for effective variable-
G
theories

To obtain a variable-
G
class of theories, we modify the original BIMOND gravitational action (
2
), taking instead,

I_{\scriptscriptstyle G}^{\scriptscriptstyle B}=-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x~(1+\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G})[|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}R+|\hat{g}|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}\hat{R}+(|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}+|\hat{g}|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2})\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{\scriptscriptstyle-2}\tilde{\mathcal{M}}],

(21)

where
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}=\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}(\mathcal{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle G}_{1},\mathcal{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle G}_{2},...)
, like
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
, is a dimensionless function of the variables
\mathcal{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle G}_{k}=\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{2}S^{\scriptscriptstyle G}_{k}
, where
S^{\scriptscriptstyle G}_{k}
are “good” scalars from among those discussed in Sec.
II.1.1
.

\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
is to encapsulate the inconstancy of the effective gravitational constant
G_{e}=G/(1+\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G})
.

It is convenient to absorb
(1+\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G})
into
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
, in the third term in the gravitational action, since they depend on the same type of variables; so, define

\bar{\mathcal{M}}\equiv\tilde{\mathcal{M}}(1+\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G})
, and work with the equivalent form :

I_{\scriptscriptstyle G}^{\scriptscriptstyle B}=I^{\scriptscriptstyle B}(\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow\bar{\mathcal{M}})-\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x~\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}(|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}R+|\hat{g}|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}\hat{R}),

(22)

where
I^{\scriptscriptstyle B}(\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow\bar{\mathcal{M}})
is the BIMOND action (
2
), with
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
replaced by
\bar{\mathcal{M}}
.
This equivalent form of the action is useful in separating the effect of MOND, encapsulated in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
– attained when
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}=0
, and those of “
G
variability”, encapsulated in
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
.
It thus allows us, when considering the resulting field equations, to build on the BIMOND field equations described in Ref.
milgrom22
,
and consider only the extra terms due to the added terms in the action (
22
).

The above action defines a higher-order theory, since
R
and
\hat{R}
depend on second derivatives of the respective metrics.
General relativity is not a higher-order theory, because the dependence on second derivatives in
R
is such that

|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}R=|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}\mathcal{R}+q^{\scriptscriptstyle\nu}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu},

(23)

where

q^{\scriptscriptstyle\nu}=|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}(g^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\lambda}-g^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\alpha}\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\nu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\alpha}),

(24)

and

\mathcal{R}=g^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu},~~~~~~~\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}\equiv(\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda\gamma}-\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\lambda}\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu\gamma})

(25)

depends only on first derivatives of the metric.
6
6

6

The definition of
\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}
here differs by a factor 2 from that in Ref.
milgrom19a
.

So the higher-derivative part in
R

However, in our case, it enters as

which is genuinely higher order.

III.1
Field equation

Varying the integral in Eq. (

-\delta_{g}\int d^{4}x~|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}R=\int d^{4}x~|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}\delta g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\left[\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}G^{\mu\nu}+{\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}}_{,\kappa}\left(g^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\kappa}\frac{\delta\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\lambda}}{\delta g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}}-g^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\kappa}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\beta}}{\delta g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}}\right)-R\frac{\delta\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}}{\delta g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}}\right],

\delta(|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}R)=|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}G^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+V^{\nu}_{,\nu},~~~~~~~~~~~V^{\nu}\equiv|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}(g^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\delta\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\lambda}-g^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\beta}\delta\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\nu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\beta}),

6

\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}

Degenerate, higher-derivative theories, with a function of a scalar field and its first derivative appearing as a prefactor of the Ricci scalar (instead of

Then, in the gravitational Lagrangian (

(30)

If indeed it would dominate, such a term would greatly depart from the NR limit of BIMOND itself. However, for high-acceleration systems, the MOND tenets require that
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}\lll 1

\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}

This can be done, as I show in Sec.

I shall not further expand expressions (

In the examples I give below, in Sec.

\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}

\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}=\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}^{0}

IV
Einstein-Palatini formulation

The Einstein-Palatini formulation of GR starts from the Einstein-Hilbert action
\propto\int g^{1/2}g^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}
, where the curvature is derived from a connection
{{}^{*}\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
, symmetric in
\mu\nu
. In the standard, “metric”, formulation of GR, the connection is taken as the Levi-Civita connection,
\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
, derived from the metric – thus making the metric the only gravitational DoF. In the Einstein-Palatini formulation of GR,
{{}^{*}\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
, as it appears in the expression for the curvature scalar, is taken as an independent gravitational DoF.
7
7

7

A connection appears also in the matter action, via covariant derivatives; there, it is standardly taken as the Levi-Civita, metric connection.
Then, the field equations gotten by varying the action over
{{}^{*}\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
tell us that for solutions of the theory we do have
{{}^{*}\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
. Then, variation over the metric gives the standard Einstein equations for the metric, resulting in a theory equivalent to GR.

However, when adopting the Einstein-Palatini formulations to modifications of GR [such as to
f(R)
theories (e.g.,
defelice10
)], one gets a different theory than is gotten by the standard adoption of the connection as the Levi-Civita one.

We thus consider, in the present context, Einstein-Palatini formulations based on the action (
22
). A possible advantage of such a (different) theory is that only first derivatives of the DoFs now appear in the Lagrangian density, possibly avoiding the difficulties that may beset the higher-order, metric formulation of Sec.
III.1
.

In our gravitational action, connections appear in the construction of
R
and
\hat{R}
, and in the arguments of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
. We then have several options for formulating a theory in this vein. The option I shall further follow here involves taking the connections in the arguments of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
to still be understood as the Levi-Civita expressions in terms of the metrics (they involve only first derivatives of the metrics), but view the Ricci tensors,
R_{\mu\nu}
and
\hat{R}_{\mu\nu}
as constructed from independent-DoF connections
{{}^{*}\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and
{{}^{*}\hat{\Gamma}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
.
As in the treatment of GR, here too, I take the connections appearing in the matter actions to be the Levi-Civita ones, so matter is still coupled minimally to the metrics. This ensures that the theory tends to GR in the high-acceleration limit, where
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}\rightarrow 0
and
\bar{\mathcal{M}},~\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{M}}(\infty)=\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Thus,
{{}^{*}\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and
{{}^{*}\hat{\Gamma}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
appear only in the second term in the action (
22
).
Defining
\mathfrak{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=(1+\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G})g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
, and
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=(1+\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G})\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
, the Einstein-Hilbert part of the gravitational action can be written as

-\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\int[\mathfrak{g}^{1/2}\mathfrak{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}({{}^{*}\Gamma})+{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}^{1/2}{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\hat{R}_{\mu\nu}({{}^{*}\hat{\Gamma}})].

(31)

IV.1
Field equation in the Einstein-Palatini formulation

Equation (
31

{{}^{*}\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and
{{}^{*}\hat{\Gamma}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}

{{}^{*}\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}

{{}^{*}\hat{\Gamma}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}

The relative-accelerations derived from two sets of connections are different, and we have

{{}^{*}C}^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-C^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{2(1+\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G})}(g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}g^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda\alpha}-\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\hat{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda\alpha}){\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}}_{,\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2(1+\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G})}(\mathfrak{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\mathfrak{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda\alpha}-\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\hat{\mathfrak{g}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda\alpha}){\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}}_{,\alpha}.

\mathfrak{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}

and

V
Observational constraints on
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
and some examples

The full field equations in either formulation of this class of theories are quite complicated.
To boot, there is a large variety of possible theories within the class, according to the choice of scalar variables in both
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
, and the dependence of these functions on them. I shall consider in some more detail only the most simple, but potentially interesting and relevant circumstances where
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
can be taken as nearly constant,
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}=\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}^{0}
, to a good enough approximation. The two formulations then coincide, since, in both, the connections that go into the curvature tensors are the Levi-Civita connections of the metrics [see Eqs. (
32
-
33
)].
The two formulations then give BIMOND – still with its variety of choices of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
– with an effective
G_{e}\approx G/(1+\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}^{0})
.

Before offering specific examples, I discuss what we require from such theories to achieve in the subcosmological realm, and the resulting constraints on the behaviour of
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
.

V.0.1
Nonrelativistic limit

While other options might be interesting to explore, we take the safe assumption, or requirement, that
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
vanishes rapidly in the NR limit, for both low- and high-acceleration systems.
As already mentioned, this would leave pure BIMOND as governing the dynamics in such systems, retaining its successful performance in galactic phenomenology, including gravitational lensing. We would like to achieve such a behavior of
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
by making it depend in an appropriate way on (“good”) scalar variables of the first and second type.

For this we need to know how these variables behave in the low- and high-acceleration limits. In the post-Newtonian expansion, if we write the MOND potential that governs the dynamics as
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}=\epsilon c^{2}
, then
\epsilon
is first order in
(V/c)^{2}
, where
V
is the typical virial speed in the system (and I am reinstating
c
in this section for clarity). For a NR system of mass
M
, and characteristic size
\ell
,
V^{2}\sim MG/\ell
in the high-acceleration regime, and
V^{2}\sim(MGa_{0})^{1/2}
in the MOND regime. The
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
potentials vanish to this order, but, supposedly, they do pick up a post-Newtonian contributions of order
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}/c^{2}=O(\epsilon^{2})
(or higher, this needs to be checked). This is sufficient to ensure ‘‘correct lensing’’ by NR systems
8
8

8

VI
Cosmology

Our present picture of cosmology within GR has not emerged as a prediction of the theory – general relativity can, of course, accommodate many other consistent cosmological models. This picture has been built layer upon layer with the benefit of major inputs beyond the adoption of GR: Inputs from physics – as our view of its laws have evolved; inputs from observations - as these have unfolded – e.g., on the realization that galaxies are “island universes”, on the expansion of the universe, etc.
The material contents of the universe, with their properties and interactions, are all put in by hand. This includes components – dark matter and dark energy – that we do not even know exist from independent evidence, and whose presence, amounts, distributions, exact nature, and interactions are all free for one to invoke as long as they help square GR with the observations. Symmetries – the cosmological principle – are indicated by observations, but then assumed generally. Initial conditions, and whether, and how they follow from inflation, in any of its many versions, are still moot.

The situation in BIMOND and VGMOND is even more involved. We have no information on all the above aspects of the twin sector: For example, on the possible existence of matter in the twin sector, on its amounts, and on its physical properties. Likewise, we are ignorant of the initial conditions of this sector. To these is added the fact that BIMOND and VGMOND are not a single theory, but classes of theories, with different possible choices of the dependence of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
on the scalar variables.

Similarly to how we have painstakingly proceeded with GR cosmology, the approach one can take here is to see if there is some version of the theory, and some reasonable set of inputs concerning the material contents, and the cosmological initial conditions in the two sectors, that can account for the observed cosmology without introducing dark matter and “material” dark energy. This would take much work to explore within BIMOND/VGMOND.

In this spirit, references

clifton10

\theta_{\scriptscriptstyle,\lambda}\tilde{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
, etc.) we have for the five basic scalars (contracted with the reference metric)

S_{1}=-2\tilde{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu};~~~~~S_{2}=8\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,0}^{\scriptscriptstyle 2};~~~~~S_{3}=-16\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,0}^{\scriptscriptstyle 2};~~~~~S_{4}=4\tilde{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu};~~~~~S_{5}=10\tilde{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu},

(45)

where,
\varphi\equiv\theta-\hat{\theta}
,
\tilde{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu}=-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,0}^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+(\vec{\nabla}\varphi)^{2}
, and
\nabla\varphi
is the space gradient of
\varphi
with respect to the proper distances
dl=adx
.
Then, we find for the good scalars

S_{q,u,v}=-16v\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,0}^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+u\tilde{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu}.

(46)

We see in this example that scalars of the second type, characterized by
u=0
, and which disappear from the NR limit, can still be important in cosmology due to the time dependence of the metric difference. On the other hand, scalars of the first type (
u\not=0
), but with
v=0
, which
fully contribute in NR systems, may vanish in this example, if
\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu}
is lightlike.
So, we may envisage, for example, a VGMOND version where the interaction depends on two scalars
X
and
Y
, with
Y
having
u\not=0
, and
X
having
u=0
, with the dependence of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
on them being such that the dependence on
X
enters cosmology, and that on
Y
determines the NR limit.

VII
Discussion

I have proposed, and demonstrated with the specific example of BIMOND, that MOND offers a framework for variable-
G
theories that can naturally comply, without fine tuning, with all the constraints from subcosmological systems on
G
inconstancy, and yet exhibit variable-
G
effects in cosmology.

The added, variable-
G
aspects of such a theory are not needed, and perhaps could stand in the way, in applications to galactic dynamics. I have thus concentrated on versions of the theory that do not predict any such effects in galactic systems.
In such systems we do have good constraints from observations on the type of scalar variables that can appear, and on the dependence of the interaction term on them
milgrom22
.
In contradistinction, in cosmology, where the main motivation for introducing variable
G
lies, we are hardly constrained, since all variables can contribute and take up different values according to circumstances. I am thus not able to offer specific promising scenarios for cosmology in this framework, beyond laying the grounds for the study of such theories.

Extensions in a similar vein can be constructed for other relativistic MOND theories, such as RMOND/AeST
sz21
, and the Khronon-Tensor theory described in Ref.
blanchet25
, by multiplying the Ricci scalar in their Einstein-Hilbert action by a function of the gradient of the scalar degree of freedom of the theory.
However, these two theories account for the effect on the expansion history that are conventionally attributed to dark matter in their own way, and do not requite variable-
G
effects for this purpose.

There are important remaining question regarding the proposed class of BIMOND-based VGMOND theories that require elaboration.
In particular, it is not clear that a consistent cosmology can be found within this framework that is consistent with all observations and that does away with the need for dark matter.

The possible effects on gravitational-wave propagation constitute another issue that needs to be addressed. Such effects depend strongly on the choice of variables, and the dependence of
\mathcal{M}_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
on them.
A possibly relevant finding is that, as was shown in Ref.
milgrom14b
, and alluded to above, below Eq. (
41
), there are “good” scalar variables that vanish for any packet of plane gravitational waves that satisfy the harmonic gauge.

The possible presence of ghosts, which looms over BIMOND itself, and the added question of Ostrogradsky instabilities in the affine and in the Einstein-Palatini formulations of VGMOND has to be investigated in detail. We need to check if such unwanted feature exist, and if they do, how deleterious they are, and whether there are ways to exorcise them.

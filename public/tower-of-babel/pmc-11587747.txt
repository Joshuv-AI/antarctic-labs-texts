D-dimer for efficacy prediction in COVID-19 patients treated with paxlovid
Authors: Daishi Li, Qingrong Wu, Wenrui Lin, Yanli Xie, Furong Zeng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPaxlovid is one of the most effective antiviral therapies for COVID-19 patients, but no studies have explored the efficacy predictors of this drug.MethodsTo investigate whether D-dimer could be used as a predictor of paxlovid response. Our study included 394 patients diagnosed with COVID-19 who were treated with paxlovid at Xiangya Hospital from Dec 5, 2022, to Jan 31, 2023. We analyzed the composite outcome and all-cause mortality and compared the clinical and demographic data of patients with normal and abnormal D-dimer levels.ResultsWe found that 324 patients (82.2%) with D-dimer levels were regularly compared with 70 patients (17.8%). Compared with patients with normal D-dimer levels, those with elevated D-dimer levels exhibited significantly reduced albumin levels, along with elevated levels of white blood cells, platelets, neutrophils, blood urea nitrogen, and procalcitonin. Kaplan–Meier survival curves showed that patients displaying increased D-dimer levels demonstrated a significantly higher incidence of composite disease progression within 28 days (p = 0.002) and all-cause death (p < 0.001). The multivariable adjusted Cox proportional hazard regression model also achieved consistent results in composite outcome (hazard ratio [HR] 2.21, 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.21–4.02, p = 0.009) and all-cause death (HR 8.06, 95% CI 2.74–23.71, p < 0.001).ConclusionOur findings suggested that the reduced efficacy of paxlovid could be predicted by elevated D-dimer levels in COVID-19 patients.Keywords: COVID-19, Paxlovid, D-dimer, Efficacy prediction, Composite disease progression outcome

Introduction

Millions of people worldwide have died from the COVID-19 pandemic in the past three years [1]. Several treatments have been authorized to treat patients with COVID-19 [2], among which paxlovid is regarded as one of the most effective antiviral therapies [3–6]. However, there is no clear predictive biomarker for the therapeutic effects of paxlovid. D-dimer is generated via the fibrinolytic system’s systematic degradation of thrombi and serves as a valuable biomarker in the routine diagnosis of venous thromboembolism (VTE) owing to its accessibility and reliable quantification in whole blood or plasma [7]. D-dimer was also studied to determine the optimal anticoagulation duration in VTE patients, to aid in the diagnosis and monitoring of disseminated intravascular coagulation [7]. Moreover, in many clinical scenarios, including inflammation, congestive cardiac failure, pneumonia, septicemia, terminal cancer, surgical procedures, pregnancy, and advanced age, an increase in D-dimer levels is often observed [7, 8]. D-dimer level has been also validated as a prognostic biomarker for COVID-19 severity and mortality [9, 10]. Consistently, our previous studies have suggested that D-dimer acts as an independent predictor of mortality in COVID-19 [11]. Despite its importance, there is a paucity of research examining the correlation between D-dimer levels and the efficacy of paxlovid in patients with COVID-19.

In this study, we assessed the predictive value of D-dimer for the effectiveness of paxlovid in COVID-19 patients. To our knowledge, this is the first study to explore the association between D-dimer levels and the efficacy of paxlovid in COVID-19 patients. Our research findings emphasize the significance of measuring and monitoring D-dimer levels, assisting clinicians in taking prompt medical actions, and accurately predicting the prognosis of COVID-19 patients treated with paxlovid.

Methods

Research programmes and population

This single-center retrospective study included 2,118 hospitalized patients with COVID-19 between Dec 05, 2022, and Jan 31, 2023, in Xiangya Hospital. All participants who (1) were hospitalized with COVID-19 and (2) obtained paxlovid treatment were included. Patients who (1) were younger than 18 years; (2) received oxygen therapy upon admission; (3) received other antiviral agents but not paxlovid; and (4) had no D-dimer measurement on the date of admission were excluded. The study underwent evaluation and received approval from the institutional review board at Xiangya Hospital, Central South University (202002024). Due to the retrospective design of the study, the need for informed consent was waived.

Data collection

We gathered data on demographic attributes (age and sex) and clinical information (symptoms, comorbidities, laboratory results, disease severity, treatment modalities, and prognosis). Severe cases were characterized by a respiratory rate of ≥ 30 breaths/min, oxygen saturation ≤ 93%, PaO2/FiO2 ≤ 300 mmHg, or lung infiltrates exceeding 50%. The patients were categorized based on normal (≤ 0.5 mg/L) or high D-dimer(>0.05 mg/L) levels. The main outcome measured was a composite outcome of disease progression, such as noninvasive respiratory support, initiation of endotracheal intubation, admission to the intensive care unit, and all-cause mortality. The secondary outcome focused on each individual event of disease progression [12]. Clinical outcomes (composite disease progression outcome) were tracked until Jan 31, 2023, marking the conclusion of the follow-up period.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM) and R version 4.2.1. Continuous variables are presented as either mean or median (interquartile range [IQR]) and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables are expressed as proportions and compared using the χ2 test and Fisher’s exact test. Kaplan-Meier analysis, along with a log-rank test, was used to assess the impact of D-dimer levels on the outcomes. The multivariate Cox regression model was used to calculate the hazard ratio (HR) with a 95% confidence interval (CI) to assess the relationship between D-dimer levels and outcomes and adjusted for potential confounders. The significance level for the statistical tests was set at 0.05, using a two-tailed analysis.

Results

Among 2118 patients with COVID-19 in Xiangya Hospital, 394 COVID-19 patients treated with paxlovid were enrolled in the study, including 324 patients (82.2%) with normal D-dimer levels and 70 patients (17.8%) with elevated D-dimer levels (Fig. 1). There were no statistical differences in age, sex, symptoms, or comorbidities between the two groups.

Flow diagram of the study

However, compared with those with normal values, patients displaying elevated D-dimer levels exhibited increased levels of white blood cells (6.2 109/L [4.1–6.8] vs. 5.2*109/L [3.8–7.3], p = 0.019), platelet (216*109/L [121–282] vs. 176*109/L [127–239], p = 0.040), neutrophil (0.9*109/L [0.6–1.2] vs. 4.3*109/L [2.8–7.5], p = 0.006), blood urea nitrogen (5.9 mmol/L [4.6-8.0] vs. 8.1 mmol/L [5.9–13.5], p = 0.005), albumin (33.9 g/L [31.6–37.0] vs. 30.3 g/L [27.2–33.4], p<0.001) and procalcitonin (0.051 ng/L [0.050–0.108] vs. 0.096 ng/L [0.050–0.384], p<0.004) (Table 1). In addition, patients with elevated D-dimer levels had a higher proportion of patients who needed high-flow oxygen therapy (10/70 [14.3%] vs. 11/324[3.4%], p = 0.001) and were more likely to worsen or die (3/70 [4.3%] vs. 3/324[0.9%]; 7/70 [10%] vs. 7/324 [2.2%], p = 0.001) (Table 2).

Kaplan-Meier curves further showed that patients with elevated D-dimer levels were at a greater risk of progression (p = 0.002) and death (p < 0.001) than patients with normal D-dimer levels (Fig. 2A-B). After adjusting for age, sex, vaccine, time from symptom onset to treatment exposure within 5 days, severity at admission, systemic steroids, antibiotics, and comorbidities, the multivariable adjusted Cox proportional hazard regression model also indicated a notably heightened risk of the composite outcome (HR 2.21, 95% CI 1.21–4.02, p = 0.009) and all-cause death (HR 8.06, 95% CI 2.74–23.71, p < 0.001) in patients with elevated D-dimer levels (Fig. 2C).

Discussion

Paxlovid is currently one of the most effective antiviral drugs for COVID-19 [2, 3, 5, 6]. Therefore, it is urgent and important to identify predictive biomarkers for its efficacy. In this retrospective study conducted at a single center, we identified a correlation indicating that elevated D-dimer levels may diminish the effectiveness of paxlovid in COVID-19 patients. We also found that patients with elevated D-dimer levels demonstrated notably reduced levels of albumin and higher levels of white blood cells, platelets, neutrophils, blood urea nitrogen, and procalcitonin. Hypoalbuminemia is associated with disease severity and mortality across numerous clinical settings [13]. Inflammatory levels reflected by high levels of white blood cells, platelets, neutrophils, and procalcitonin have been associated with severe infections [14]. Blood urea nitrogen, a well-established biomarker for assessing kidney function, is associated with mortality in COVID-19 patients [15, 16]. These findings additionally imply an association between elevated D-dimer levels and unfavorable prognosis among COVID-19 patients undergoing paxlovid treatment.

To further clarify whether D-dimer is an independent prognostic biomarker for COVID-19 patients treated with paxlovid, we performed multivariable adjusted Cox proportional hazard regression and found that a higher D-dimer level was independently associated with an increased composite outcome and all-cause death. D-dimer serves as an indicator of deep vein thrombosis in patients with COVID-19 [17]. Increased D-dimer levels, indicative of thrombotic complications, could potentially contribute to adverse outcomes in COVID-19 patients receiving paxlovid treatment. Additional research is required to better understand the underlying mechanisms of this association.

Our study has several limitations. Similar to all retrospective cohort studies, numerous confounding factors may have introduced bias into the observed effectiveness despite the consecutive collection of data and adjustment for a substantial number of confounders. Furthermore, our study predominantly targeted hospitalized adult COVID-19 patients in China who were treated with paxlovid and did not initially require oxygen therapy upon admission. Whether our findings remain consistent across other subgroups of COVID-19 patients or across different countries warrants further investigation. Finally, we did not elucidate the underlying mechanism linking D-dimer levels with the prognosis of COVID-19 patients undergoing paxlovid treatment.

Conclusions

Elevated D-dimer levels were prevalent and could decrease the effectiveness of paxlovid in COVID-19 patients. Monitoring and assessing D-dimer levels could aid clinicians in promptly taking medical interventions and forecasting the prognosis of COVID-19 patients receiving paxlovid treatment.

Morse index stability for p p -Yang-Mills connections
Mario Gauvrit, Paul Laurain, Tristan Rivière

Abstract
Abstract : We establish the lower semi continuity of the Morse index and the upper continuity of the Morse Index plus nullity of sequences of critical points of the Sacks-Uhlenbeck type relaxation of the Yang-Mills Energy in 4 dimension. The result is known not to be true in general for the “cousin problem” of hamonic maps from surfaces into arbitrary manifolds. This result is stressing the more stable behaviour of Yang-Mills Fields compare to harmonic maps as observed in other contexts such as the flow. The Morse Index control at the limit of critical points to Sacks Uhlenbeck relaxations of Yang-Mills Lagrangian is a central result in the implementation of minmax operation on this Lagrangian.



Introduction

The calculus of variations of Yang-Mills energy for connections of principal
G-
bundles over
4-
dimensional Riemannian manifolds where
G

in the early 80’s in relation with important differential topology/geometry questions. While the main focus was concentrated on special absolute minimizing solutions called Self-Dual/Anti-self-dual solutions or

, the construction of non zero Morse index Yang-Mills fields was also a subject of great interest in particular in relation with the so called Atiyah-Jones conjecture. The conjecture is about to know whether the homotopy type of the Moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
of instantons of topological charge

bundle with second Chern class
n

SU(2)-
connections
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
modulo gauge group action with second Chern class
n
. More specifically, a question is about to know whether, the low degree homotopy groups of the two spaces coincide or not.
This question is intimately related to the existence of non zero Morse index Yang-Mills Fields. This general consideration can be sketched as follows : assuming a non zero
k-
homotopy class within the Moduli space of Instantons
\alpha\in\pi_{k}({\mathcal{M}}_{n})
would “trivialize” (would be mapped to zero)
for the canonical inclusion map
\iota

\iota_{\ast}\alpha=0
in
\pi_{k}({\mathcal{A}}_{n})
, one would easily produce a
min-max operation
whose underlying admissible space would be made of any continuous map from the
k+1

B^{k+1}
into
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
whose boundary
\partial B^{k+1}
is mapped into the space of instantons (which happen to realise the minimal Yang-Mills energy
8\pi^{2}|n|
of the given
SU(2)
bundle) and the restriction to the boundary would realise the class
\alpha\neq 0
in
\pi_{k}({\mathcal{M}}_{n})
. Because the minimal energy
8\pi^{2}\,|n|
is characterising the membership to the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
, the “width” of such a min-max operation would be non trivial (strictly larger than
8\pi^{2}\,|n|
) otherwise one would contradicts the non triviality of
\alpha
in
\pi_{k}({\mathcal{M}}_{n})
. It is then “natural” to expect this width to be realised by a non-zero Morse index
k+1
Yang-Mills Fields. In order to produce a rigorous proof of this statement major analytical challenges have to be solved. The main reason is that, due in particular to its conformal invariance, the Yang-Mills energy cannot fulfil the necessary requirements for implementing Palais-Smale deformation theory in infinite dimensional space as such. Numerous publications have been devoted to this difficulty that Yang-Mills energy is sharing with several famous Lagrangians such as the Dirichlet energy of maps from a surface into closed Riemannian manifolds whose critical points are called “harmonic maps”. The main motivation behind the present work is to bring a new piece of understanding of how the absence of Palais-Smale property for Yang-Mills energy can be “overcome” while implementing general min-max operations in particular. Before describing our main result we conclude this preamble by mentioning that there has been numerous contributions in the direction of Atiyah Jones conjecture and that we will omit to report on since this is not the object of the present paper.

In a pioneered work in concentration compactness theory (see
[
25
]

{\mathfrak{M}}:=W^{1,p}(S^{2},N^{n})

to

and hopefully converge to critical points to the Dirichlet energy itself. In order to pass to the limit a delicate analysis has to be performed. The central result in this analysis is the so called

This permits, modulo extraction of a subsequence, to pass to the limit away from finitely many points in the domain while at these points so called “bubbles” (i.e. concentrated critical points) can be formed at the limit. These bubbles are connected to each other by regions which are called “neck regions”.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\alpha\in[0;1)
, we have, for all
u,v\in\mathbf{R}
,

\frac{1}{2}|u-v|^{2\alpha+2}\leq\langle(1+|u|^{2})^{\alpha}u-(1+|v|^{2})^{\alpha}v,u-v\rangle.

In particular, if
2\leq p<3
we have

\frac{1}{2}|u-v|^{p}\leq\langle(1+|u|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}u-(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}v,u-v\rangle

and

\frac{1}{2}|u-v|^{\frac{p}{2}+1}\leq\langle(1+|u|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{4}}u-(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{4}}v,u-v\rangle.

Proof.

\displaystyle\langle(1+|u|^{2})^{\alpha}u-(1+|v|^{2})^{\alpha}v,u-v\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+|u|^{2})^{\alpha}|u|^{2}+(1+|v|^{2})^{\alpha}|v|^{2}

\displaystyle-\left((1+|u|^{2})^{\alpha}+(1+|v|^{2})^{\alpha}\right)\langle u,v\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+|u|^{2})^{\alpha}|u|^{2}+(1+|v|^{2})^{\alpha}|v|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left((1+|u|^{2})^{\alpha}+(1+|v|^{2})^{\alpha}\right)\frac{|v-u|^{2}-|u|^{2}-|v|^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((1+|u|^{2})^{\alpha}-(1+|v|^{2})^{\alpha}\right)\left(\frac{|u|^{2}-|v|^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\left((1+|u|^{2})^{\alpha}+(1+|v|^{2})^{\alpha}\right)\frac{|v-u|^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left((1+|u|^{2})^{\alpha}+(1+|v|^{2})^{\alpha}\right)\frac{|v-u|^{2}}{2},

the inequality follows from the fact that the first term of previous line is non-negative. Finally, we use the following inequality, if
0\leq\beta\leq 1
,

(a+b)^{\beta}\leq a^{\beta}+b^{\beta}\text{ for all }a,b\geq 0,

(32)

to deduce that

\displaystyle|v-u|^{2\alpha+2}

\displaystyle=|v-u|^{2}|v-u|^{2\alpha}\leq|v-u|^{2}(|v|+|u|)^{2\alpha}\leq|v-u|^{2}((|v|^{2})^{\alpha}+(|u|^{2})^{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq|v-u|^{2}\left((1+|v|^{2})^{\alpha}+(1+|u|^{2})^{\alpha}\right),

which achieves the proof.
∎

To prove (
32
), it suffices to assume
a\geq b
by symmetry and
b\not=0
then to prove that

(k+1)^{\beta}\leq k^{\beta}+1\text{ for all }k\geq 1,

which can easily be done by function analysis.
Notice that, if
\beta\geq 1
, we can use the following convexity inequality to obtain a similar result.

(a+b)^{\beta}\leq 2^{\beta-1}(a^{\beta}+b^{\beta})\text{ for all }a,b\geq 0.

Let
f(x)=x(1+x^{2})^{\alpha}
since
|f^{\prime}(x)|=|(1+x^{2})^{\alpha}+2\alpha(1+x^{2})^{\alpha-1}x^{2}|\leq 2(1+x^{2})^{\alpha}
on
\mathbf{R}_{+}
as soon as
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
then
f
is Lipschitz. Let
2\leq p\leq 3
, since
0\leq\frac{p-2}{4}\leq\frac{1}{4}
, we have

|V(a)-V(b)|=|\sqrt{\rho(a)}a-\sqrt{\rho(b)}b|\leq 2\max(\sqrt{\rho(a)},\sqrt{\rho(b)})|a-b|,

(33)

where
V(x)=x(1+x^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{4}}
.

We similarly get, for
2\leq p\leq 3
and

, that

H(a-b)\leq\sqrt{2}(H(a)+b^{p}),

(34)

where
H(x)=(1+x^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}
.

Lemma A.2

(Böchner formula for general connections)
.

There exists
C>0
, such that for any connection
A
with
F
as curvature must satisfy

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|F|^{2}\leq|\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}F|^{2}-\mathrm{d}^{*}\omega-|\nabla_{A}F|^{2}+C|F|^{3}

(35)

where
\omega
is the
1
-form defined by duality via the equation

\langle\omega,\alpha\rangle=\langle\alpha\wedge\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}F,F\rangle=-\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}\star F,\alpha\wedge\star F\rangle

Proof.

We make use of the following Böchner identity :

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|F|^{2}=\langle\nabla_{A}^{\ast}\nabla_{A}F,F\rangle-|\nabla_{A}F|^{2}

Indeed, consider an arbitrary point
q
and chose an orthonormal frame
(e_{i})_{i}
such that
\nabla e_{i}=0
at
q
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|F|^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\Tr(\nabla^{2}|F|^{2})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\nabla^{2}_{e_{i},e_{i}}|F|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}(\nabla_{e_{i}}\nabla|F|^{2})(e_{i})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}(\nabla_{e_{i}}\langle F,\nabla_{A}F\rangle)(e_{i})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}|(\nabla_{A})_{e_{i}}F|^{2}+\langle F,(\nabla_{A}^{2})_{e_{i},e_{i}}F\rangle

\displaystyle=-|\nabla_{A}F|^{2}-\langle F,\Tr(\nabla^{2}_{A}F)\rangle

\displaystyle=-|\nabla_{A}F|^{2}+\langle F,\nabla_{A}^{*}\nabla_{A}F\rangle.

From the Weitzenböck formula (see
[
3
, Theorem 3.10]
), we get

\nabla_{A}^{\ast}\nabla_{A}F=(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}\mathrm{d}_{A}+\mathrm{d}_{A}\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*})F+Q(F)

where
Q
is quadratic. Hence

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|F|^{2}\leq\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}\mathrm{d}_{A}^{\ast}F,F\rangle+C|F|^{3}-|\nabla_{A}F|^{2}

(36)

and for all test functions
\eta
,

\displaystyle\int\eta\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}\mathrm{d}_{A}^{\ast}F,F\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}(\eta\mathrm{d}^{*}_{A}F),F\rangle-\int\langle\mathrm{d}\eta\wedge\mathrm{d}^{*}_{A}F,F\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\langle\eta\mathrm{d}^{*}_{A}F,\mathrm{d}^{*}_{A}F\rangle-\int\langle\mathrm{d}\eta,\omega\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\eta(|\mathrm{d}^{*}_{A}F|^{2}-\mathrm{d}^{*}\omega)

which achieves the proof.
∎

Lemma A.3

(Kato-Yau inequality for
p
-Yang-Mills connections)
.

Let
A
be a smooth
p
-Yang-Mills connection with curvature
F
and

. Then

\mu(p)|\mathrm{d}|F_{A}||^{2}\leq|\nabla_{A}F_{A}|^{2}

where

\mu(p)=\max\left(2-p/2+\frac{1}{2(p-1)},1\right)

Proof.

We write
\nabla=\nabla_{A}
to simplify notations. Recall that for any
2
-form
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{A}\Omega(X_{0},X_{1},X_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{X_{0}}(\Omega(X_{1},X_{2}))-\nabla_{X_{1}}(\Omega(X_{0},X_{2}))+\nabla_{X_{2}}(\Omega(X_{0},X_{1}))

\displaystyle-\Omega([X_{0},X_{1}],X_{2})+\Omega([X_{0},X_{2}],X_{1})-\Omega([X_{1},X_{2}],X_{0})

Fix a point

and consider coordinates
(x^{i})_{1\leq i\leq 4}
around

such that at

,
(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})_{1\leq i\leq 4}
is orthonormal and

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{1}}|F|\right|=|\mathrm{d}|F||.

From the usual Kato inequality

|\mathrm{d}|F||\leq|\nabla_{1}F|.

For every
2
-form
\Omega
,
\mathrm{d}_{A}\Omega=0
if and only if for all
(i,j,k)\in\{(1,2,3),(1,2,4),(1,3,4),(2,3,4)\}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{A}\Omega\left(\partial_{i},\partial_{j},\partial_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\partial_{i}}(\Omega(\partial_{j},\partial_{k}))+\nabla_{\partial_{j}}(\Omega(\partial_{k},\partial_{i}))+\nabla_{\partial_{k}}(\Omega(\partial_{i},\partial_{j}))

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}\Omega_{jk}+\nabla_{j}\Omega_{ki}+\nabla_{k}\Omega_{ij}.

We write

. The Bianchi identity

reads

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\nabla_{1}F_{23}+\nabla_{2}F_{31}+\nabla_{3}F_{12}=0\\
\nabla_{1}F_{24}+\nabla_{2}F_{41}+\nabla_{4}F_{12}=0\\
\nabla_{1}F_{34}+\nabla_{3}F_{41}+\nabla_{4}F_{13}=0\\
\nabla_{2}F_{34}+\nabla_{3}F_{42}+\nabla_{4}F_{23}=0\end{array}\right.

(37)

The

as long as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\nabla_{1}F_{41}+\nabla_{2}F_{24}+\nabla_{3}F_{34}=(p-2)G_{123}\\

\nabla_{1}F_{12}+\nabla_{3}F_{32}+\nabla_{4}F_{42}=(p-2)G_{134}\\
\nabla_{2}F_{12}+\nabla_{3}F_{13}+\nabla_{4}F_{41}=(p-2)G_{234}\end{array}\right.

(38)

In any vector space

Lemma A.6
.

Let
(M^{n},h)
a Riemannian manifold. If
X
is a vector field (identified with the
1
-form
\langle X,\cdot\rangle
) and
\omega
is a (vector-valued) differential
k
-form on
M
then

|\iota_{X}\omega|^{2}+|X\wedge\omega|^{2}=|X|^{2}|\omega|^{2}

and in particular

|X\wedge\omega|^{2}\leq|X|^{2}|\omega|^{2},

and the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of the map
X\mapsto X\wedge\omega
is
(n-k)|\omega|^{2}
.

Proof.

Consider a (local) orthonormal frame
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
such that
X=|X|e_{1}
, and denote by
(e^{1},\dots,e^{n})
the associated coframe. When
I=\{i_{1},\dots i_{k}\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
with
i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}
we write
e^{I}=e^{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e^{i_{k}}
. Recall that
(e^{I})_{|I|=k}
is an orthonormal basis of
k
-forms. If
\omega
is a
k
-form, write

\omega=\sum_{|I|=k}\omega_{I}e^{I}.

\displaystyle|\iota_{e_{1}}\omega|^{2}+|e^{1}\wedge\omega|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{|I|=k}\omega_{I}\iota_{e_{1}}e^{I}\right|^{2}+\left|\sum_{|I|=k}\omega_{I}e^{1}\wedge e^{I}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{|I|=k,1\in I}\omega_{I}\iota_{e_{1}}e^{I}\right|^{2}+\left|\sum_{|I|=k}\omega_{I}e^{1}\wedge e^{I}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{|I|=k,1\in I}\omega_{I}\iota_{e_{1}}(e^{1}\wedge e^{I\backslash\{1\}})\right|^{2}+\left|\sum_{|I|=k,1\not\in I}\omega_{I}e^{1}\wedge e^{I}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{|I|=k,1\in I}\omega_{I}e^{I\backslash\{1\}}\right|^{2}+\left|\sum_{|I|=k,1\not\in I}\omega_{I}e^{\{1\}\cup I}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|I|=k,1\in I}|\omega_{I}|^{2}+\sum_{|I|=k,1\not\in I}|\omega_{I}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|I|=k}|\omega_{I}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\omega|^{2}

By homogeneity, we obtain :

|\iota_{X}\omega|^{2}+|X\wedge\omega|^{2}=|\omega|^{2}|X|^{2}

and in particular

|X\wedge\omega|^{2}\leq|\omega|^{2}|X|^{2}.

\omega^{\prime}

A.3.3

The goal of this section is the proof of the following proposition.

Proposition A.24

Proof.

A.3.4

Proposition A.25

(85)

85

so using Young inequality and (
89

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}}(W(F),\mathrm{d}\varphi)\right|

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}}\eta^{2}f_{i,h}|D_{i,h}F|^{2}

\displaystyle+C(p-1)^{2}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}}f_{i,h}\eta^{2}|A|^{2}|D_{i,h}A|^{2}+C\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}}\eta^{2}|W(F)||D_{i,h}A|^{2}.

A.3.5
Smoothness of
A

Proposition A.26
.

If
p-2
is small enough,
A
is smooth.

Proof.

With the regularity information obtained up to this point, the
p
-Yang-Mills equations rewrites

\mathrm{d}^{\ast}F=\star[A,\star F]+\frac{p-2}{2}\star\frac{\mathrm{d}|F|^{2}\wedge\star F}{1+|F|^{2}}.

This is a second-order non-linear PDE for
A
and since
\mathrm{d}^{*}A=0
, the highest order term is

\mathbb{L}_{p}A:=\Delta A-(p-2)\star\frac{\langle\nabla\mathrm{d}A,F\rangle\wedge\star F}{1+|F|^{2}}.

(90)

This is, for
p-2
small enough, an elliptic operator with Hölder continuous coefficients (depending only on
F
) and form the equation, we get that
\mathbb{L}_{p}A
is Hölder continuous. From standard Schauder regularity theory, this gives that
A
is in
\mathcal{C}^{2,\sigma}
for some
\sigma\in(0,1)
. This improves not only the regularity of
\mathbb{L}_{p}A
but also the one of the coefficients of
\mathbb{L}_{p}
. Bootstrapping this argument, we get that
A
is smooth.
∎

A.3.6
Pointwise estimate for
F

Theorem A.27
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0},C>0
such that for all
R>0
and every smooth
connection
A
on
\mathrm{B}_{R}
satisfying the
p
-Yang-Mills equation
(
4
) and the small energy condition

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}}|F|^{2}<\varepsilon_{0},

then

\sup_{\mathrm{B}_{R/2}}|F|\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\sqrt{\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}}|F|^{2}}.

Proof.

Recall that
\phi=|F|^{2}
satisfies the following estimate

\mathcal{L}_{p}\phi\leq C\phi(1+\phi^{1/2})

where
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is defined in (
90
). Define for
\sigma\in[0,R]
:
f(\sigma)=(R-\sigma)^{4}\max_{\overline{\text{B}}_{\sigma}}\phi
.

f
is a continuous non-negative function vanishing at
\sigma=R
so there
exists
\sigma_{0}\in[0,R[
such that
f
is maximal at
\sigma=\sigma_{0}
. Now since
\phi
is continuous, there exists
x\in\overline{\text{B}}_{\sigma_{0}}
such that
\phi_{0}:=\max_{\overline{\text{B}}_{\sigma_{0}}}\phi=\phi(x_{0})
. Introduce
\rho_{0}=\min((R-\sigma_{0})/2,1)
. By definition of
\sigma_{0}

\max_{\overline{\text{B}}_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})}\phi\leq\max_{\overline{\text{B}}_{\rho_{0}+\sigma_{0}}}\phi\leq\left(\frac{R-\sigma_{0}}{R-(\sigma_{0}+\rho_{0})}\right)^{4}\max_{\overline{\text{B}}_{\sigma_{0}}}\phi=16\phi_{0}.

(91)

Define now
r_{0}=\rho_{0}\phi_{0}^{1/4}
. Let’s prove by contradiction that,
if
\delta_{0}
is small enough then
r_{0}<1
. Assume that
r_{0}\geq 1

and consider
v=|\alpha^{\ast}F|^{2}
where
\alpha(y)=x_{0}+\phi_{0}^{-1/4}y
(defined on
\text{B}_{r_{0}}\supset\text{B}_{1}
). We have the
following expression for
v
:

v(y)=\frac{1}{\phi_{0}}|F|^{2}\left(x_{0}+\frac{y}{\phi_{0}^{1/4}}\right)=\frac{1}{\phi_{0}}\phi\left(x_{0}+\frac{y}{\phi_{0}^{1/4}}\right),

so, from (
91
),
v
satisfies

\displaystyle v(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\sup_{\overline{\text{B}}_{r_{0}}}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\overline{\text{B}}_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})}\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}}\leq 16.

Since
r_{0}\geq 1
and
\rho_{0}\leq 1
then
\phi_{0}\geq 1
.
Consider
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
defined as

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{p}v=\partial_{\alpha}((\delta^{\alpha\beta}+(p-2)\mathcal{A}^{\alpha\beta}\circ\alpha)\partial_{\beta}v)

Then, in
\text{B}_{r_{0}}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{p}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi_{0}}\times\frac{1}{\phi_{0}^{1/2}}(\mathcal{L}_{p}\phi)(x_{0}+\phi_{0}^{-1/4}y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\phi_{0}^{3/2}}(\phi+\phi^{3/2})(x_{0}+\phi_{0}^{-1/4}y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{1}{\phi_{0}^{1/2}}v+\left(\frac{\phi(x_{0}+\phi_{0}^{-1/4}y)}{\phi_{0}}\right)^{1/2}v\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(v+\sqrt{16}v\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cv.

This proves that
v
is a subsolution for the operator

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{p}-C
. We can apply Möser-Harnack inequality (see for example
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
) to get an estimate of the form

v(0)\leq C\int_{\text{B}_{1}}v

where
C>0
is a constant (independent of
p
). Recall that
v(0)=1
and by conformal
invariance of the Yang-Mills energy

\int_{\text{B}_{1}}v=\int_{\text{B}_{1}}|\alpha^{\ast}F|^{2}=\int_{\text{B}_{\phi_{0}^{-1/4}(x_{0})}}|F|^{2}=\int_{\text{B}_{\rho_{0}/r_{0}(x_{0})}}|F|^{2}\leq\int_{\text{B}_{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}}|F|^{2}\leq\int_{\text{B}_{R}}|F|^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{0},

so
1\leq C\varepsilon_{0}
. This becomes a contradiction by choosing

\varepsilon_{0}
small enough.

We have proved that
r_{0}<1

i.e.

\phi_{0}<\rho_{0}^{-4}
.
Consider now
w=|\beta^{\ast}F|^{2}
where
\beta(y)=x_{0}+\rho_{0}y
.
We have the following expression for
w
:

w(y)=\rho_{0}^{4}|F|^{2}(x_{0}+\rho_{0}y)=\rho_{0}^{4}\phi(x_{0}+\rho_{0}y),

so, using (
91
) as before,
w
satisfies

\displaystyle w(0)

\displaystyle=\rho_{0}^{4}\phi_{0}=r_{0}^{4}

\displaystyle\sup_{\overline{\text{B}}_{1}}w

\displaystyle=\sup_{\overline{\text{B}}_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})}\rho_{0}^{4}\phi=\rho_{0}^{4}\phi_{0}\sup_{\overline{\text{B}}_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})}\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}}\leq 16r_{0}^{4}<16.

Define this time the operator
\check{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
as

\check{\mathcal{L}}_{p}w=\partial_{\alpha}((\delta^{\alpha\beta}+(p-2)\mathcal{A}^{\alpha\beta}\circ\beta)\partial_{\beta}w)

Then

\displaystyle\check{\mathcal{L}}_{p}w

\displaystyle=\rho_{0}^{4}\times\rho_{0}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{p}\phi)(x_{0}+\rho_{0}y)

\displaystyle\leq C\rho_{0}^{6}(\phi+\phi^{3/2})(x_{0}+\rho_{0}y)

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho_{0}^{2}w+(\rho_{0}^{4}\phi(x_{0}+\rho_{0}y))^{1/2}w)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(w+\sqrt{16}w\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cw,

i.e.

w
is a subsolution for the operator
\check{\mathcal{L}}_{p}-C
. Apply again Möser-Harnack inequality to obtain

r_{0}^{4}=w(0)\leq C\int_{\text{B}_{1}}w.

Using once more the conformal invariance of the energy,

\int_{\text{B}_{1}}w=\int_{\text{B}_{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}}|F|^{2}\leq\int_{\text{B}_{R}}|F|^{2}.

We deduce

f(\sigma_{0})=(R-\sigma_{0})^{4}\phi_{0}=16r_{0}^{4}\leq C\int_{\text{B}_{R}}|F|^{2}

and therefore for all
\sigma\in[0,R)
,

(R-\sigma)^{4}\sup_{\overline{\text{B}}_{\sigma}}|F|^{2}\leq C\int_{\text{B}_{R}}|F|^{2}.

Taking
\sigma=R/2
, we finally obtain

\sup_{\overline{\text{B}}_{R/2}}|F|\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\sqrt{\int_{\text{B}_{R}}|F|^{2}}.

∎

Simple proofs of certain results on generalized Fekete-Szegő functional in the class 𝓢 \boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}
Teodor Bulboacă, Milutin Obradović, Nikola Tuneski

Abstract
. In this paper we give simple proofs for the main results concerning generalized Fekete-Szegő functional of type | a 3 ​ ( f ) − λ ​ a 2 ​ ( f ) 2 | − μ ​ | a 2 ​ ( f ) | \left|a_{3}(f)-\lambda a_{2}(f)^{2}\right|-\mu|a_{2}(f)| , where λ ∈ ℂ \lambda\in\mathbb{C} , μ > 0 \mu>0 and a n ​ ( f ) a_{n}(f) is n n -th coefficient of the power series expansion of f ∈ 𝒮 f\in\mathcal{S} . In addition, we studied this functional separately for the class 𝒦 \mathcal{K} of convex functions and we emphasize that all the results of the paper are sharp (i.e. the best possible). The advantages of the present study are that the techniques used in the proofs are more easier and use known results regarding the univalent functions, and those that it give the best possible results not only for the entire class of univalent normalized functions 𝒮 \mathcal{S} but also for its subclass of convex functions 𝒦 \mathcal{K} .

1.
Introduction and definitions

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the class of functions
f
analytic in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
and normalized such that
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
, that is

(1.1)

f(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k},\;z\in\mathbb{D},

and by
\mathcal{S}
we denote the subclass of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of univalent functions in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
.

In
[
7
]
the authors considered the
generalized Fekete-Szegő functional
for the class
\mathcal{S}
of type

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f):=\left|a_{3}(f)-\lambda a_{2}(f)^{2}\right|-\mu|a_{2}(f)|,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\mu>0
, and the coefficients
a_{2}(f)=a_{2}
and
a_{3}(f)=a_{3}
are given by (
1.1
). Therefore,

(1.2)

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)=\left|a_{3}-\lambda a_{2}^{2}\right|-\mu|a_{2}|,\;\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\,\mu>0.

We note that for
\mu=0
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
we have the well-known
Fekete-Szegő inequality

[
3
]
, that is

(1.3)

F_{\lambda,0}(f)=\left|a_{3}-\lambda a_{2}^{2}\right|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1+2\exp\left(-\frac{2\lambda}{1-\lambda}\right),&\text{if}&0\leq\lambda<1,\\
1,&\text{if}&\lambda=1.\end{array}\right.

The above inequality is sharp for all
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, in the sense that there exists a function
f_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{S}
such the equality holds in (
1.3
).

We emphasize that the
Koebe function

k(z)=z/(1-z)^{2}
is not the extremal function for the inequality (
1.3
) for
0<\lambda<1
, but it is the extremal function whenever
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
.

The function
\phi:\mathbb{D}\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is called a
Löwner chain
(see
[
8
]
,
[
9
, p. 157]
) if
\phi(z,t)=\mathrm{e}^{it}+c_{2}(t)z^{2}+\dots
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
, is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
for all
t\geq 0
and
\phi(z,s)\prec\phi(z,t)
for all
0\leq s\leq t
, where the symbol “
\prec
” stands for the subordination. This notion is connected with the well-known
Löwner-Kufarev differential equation
introduced and studied first in
[
8
]
, then by
[
6
]
and further by many authors.

Also, a
slit mapping
is a function that maps a domain conformally onto the complex plane minus a set of Jordan curves, while a
single-slit mapping
is a slit mapping that’s range is the complement of a single Jordan curve.

Two important results for the Löwner chains in connection with the univalent functions and the single-slit mappings are given by the following properties:

A.
If
f\in\mathcal{S}
, then there exists a Löwner chain
\phi(z,t)
such that
f(z)=\phi(z,0)
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
(see
[
9
, Therorem 6.1., p. 159]
);

B.
To each
f\in\mathcal{S}
there exists a sequence of single-slit mappings
\left(\psi_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\psi_{n}\to f
(
n\to\infty
) uniformly on each compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
2
, Theorem 3.2., p. 80]
), that is the density of the slit mappings in the class
\mathcal{S}
in the space of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
with respect to the topology of uniformly convergence on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
.

The main results of
[
7
]
are obtained by using the above results of the theory of Löwner chains, and are given in two next theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
7
, Theorem 1.1.]

For all
f\in\mathcal{S}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
\mu>0
, and
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)
is defined by (
1.2
) we have

(1.4)

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1,&\text{if}&|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{\mu}{2},\\
4|1-\lambda|-2\mu+1,&\text{if}&|1-\lambda|>\frac{\mu}{2}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, for each
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
the following implications hold:

(i)

If
|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{\mu}{2}
and
\lambda\neq 1+\frac{\mu}{2}
, then
f\in\mathcal{S}
and the equality in (
1.4
) holds if and only if there exists
|\zeta|=1
such that

(1.5)

f(z)=\frac{z}{1+\zeta^{2}z^{2}},\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

(ii)

If
\lambda=1+\frac{\mu}{2}
, then
f\in\mathcal{S}
and the equality in (
1.4
) holds if and only if
f
is of the form (
1.5
) or
f
is a rotation of the Koebe function
k
;

(iii)

If
|1-\lambda|>\frac{\mu}{2}
, then
f\in\mathcal{S}
and the equality in (
1.4
) holds if and only if
\lambda>1+\frac{\mu}{2}
and
f
is a rotation of the Koebe function
k
.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
7
, Theorem 1.2.]

For all
f\in\mathcal{S}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}
,
\mu>0
, and
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)
is defined by (
1.2
) we have

(1.6)

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{|1-\lambda|}},&\text{if}&|1-\lambda|>\frac{\mu^{2}}{4},\\
-2\mu,&\text{if}&|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{\mu^{2}}{4}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, for each
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
the following implications hold:

(i)

If
|1-\lambda|>\frac{\mu^{2}}{4}
, then
f\in\mathcal{S}
and the equality in (
1.6
) holds if and only if
\lambda\leq\frac{3}{4}
and
\lambda<1-\frac{\mu^{2}}{4}
, and there exists
|\zeta|=1
such that

(1.7)

f(z)=\frac{z}{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{|1-\lambda|}}\zeta z+\zeta^{2}z^{2}},\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

(ii)

If
|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{\mu^{2}}{4}
, then
f\in\mathcal{S}
and the equality in (
1.6
) holds if and only if
\lambda=\frac{3}{4}
and

, and

is a rotation of the Koebe function

.

The main aim of this paper is to give simple proofs of the two previous theorems, and additionally, in the last section there are given sharp estimates of
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)
for the class of convex functions.

Simple proofs of Theorem

For our proofs we will mainly use the next lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
1
]
,
[
4

For every function
f\in\mathcal{S}
given by (

) we have

(2.1)

\left|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}\right|\leq 1,

and the equality in (

(2.2)

We would like to emphasize that the range of
\mathbb{D}
by the above function

was determined in
[

and it’s connected with the slit mappings, while the fact that the equality in (

) holds if and only if

As an immediately consequence of the above result, if

and the equality holds for the function

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Using Lemma
2.1
and (
1.2
) we obtain

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)

\displaystyle=\left|\left(a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}\right)+(1-\lambda)a_{2}^{2}\right|-\mu|a_{2}|

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq\left|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}\right|+|1-\lambda||a_{2}|^{2}-\mu|a_{2}|

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq|1-\lambda||a_{2}|^{2}-\mu|a_{2}|+1=:\varphi\left(|a_{2}|\right),

where (see also
[
1
]
)

\varphi(t)=|1-\lambda|t^{2}-\mu t+1,\;t:=|a_{2}|\in[0,2].

If
\lambda=1
, then
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)=\left|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}\right|-\mu|a_{2}|
which, due to the inequality (
2.1
) of Lemma
2.1
and
\mu>0
implies
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\leq 1-\mu|a_{2}|
. It is easy to verify that for the univalent function
f
given by (
1.5
) we have

f(z)=z-\zeta^{2}z^{3}+\dots,\;z\in\mathbb{D},

thus for this function we obtain equality in the estimate.

On the other hand, if
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)=1
then it follows that
|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}|=1
and
|a_{2}|=0
, which according to the Lemma
2.1
is satisfied if and only if the function
f
has the form (
2.2
) with
a_{2}=0
, that is
f
is given by (
1.5
).

If
\lambda\neq 1
, then we have

(2.5)

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\leq\max\left\{\varphi(0);\varphi(2)\right\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1,&\text{if}&|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{\mu}{2},\\
4|1-\lambda|-2\mu+1,&\text{if}&|1-\lambda|\geq\frac{\mu}{2}.\end{array}\right.

It is easy to check that the estimates (
1.4
), that are in fact (
2.5
), are reached (i.e. equalities sign hold) for the functions given in
(i)
,
(ii)
and
(iii)
of Theorem
1.1
. Next we will show that those are the only such functions.

Let assume that equality holds in the inequality (
2.5
). This means that equalities hold in each of the inequalities (
2.3
) and (
2.4
).

(a)
For the case
|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{\mu}{2}
we have the equality
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)=1
whenever
1=\varphi(0)\geq\varphi(2)
, while
\varphi(0)=1
if and only if
|a_{2}|=0
, hence the function
f
given by (
1.5
) satisfies the equality in (
2.5
).

(b)
In the case
|1-\lambda|\geq\frac{\mu}{2}
we get
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\leq 4|1-\lambda|-2\mu+1
. Since
\varphi(t)\leq\varphi(2)
for all
t\in[0,2]
, the equality in (
2.4
) holds if and only if
|a_{2}|=2
. According to the well-known Bieberbach’s result
[
1
]
(see also
[
4
, Theorem 1, p. 33]
) we have
|a_{2}|=2
if and only if
f
is a rotation of the Koebe function, that is

(2.6)

k_{\theta}(z)=\frac{z}{\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{i\theta}z\right)^{2}}=z+2\mathrm{e}^{i\theta}z^{2}+3\mathrm{e}^{2i\theta}z+\dots,\;z\in\mathbb{D},\;\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

Therefore, the condition that
f=k_{\theta}
is a necessary one to have
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)=4|1-\lambda|-2\mu+1
. It remains to prove that this is also a sufficient condition for having the previous equality.

Thus, if
f=k_{\theta}
, then
a_{2}=2\mathrm{e}^{i\theta}
and
a_{3}=3\mathrm{e}^{2i\theta}
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, that implies
\left|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}\right|=1
, hence we get equality in (
2.4
). In addition, the inequality (
2.3
) will be equivalent to

\left|-\mathrm{e}^{2i\theta}+4(1-\lambda)\mathrm{e}^{2i\theta}\right|-2\mu\leq 4|1-\lambda|-2\mu+1

or

(2.7)

\left|4(\lambda-1)+1\right|\leq 4|\lambda-1|+1.

It is obvious that for all

we have
\left|z_{1}+z_{2}\right|=|z_{1}|+|z_{2}|
if and only if there exists a number

such that

. Denoting

and

, it follows that we obtain equality in (
2.7
) if and only if there exists

for which

, that’s equivalent to

.

From the above reasons we conclude that if
|1-\lambda|\geq\frac{\mu}{2}
, then
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)=4|1-\lambda|-2\mu+1
if and only if
f=k_{\theta}
and
\lambda>1
. We mention that is this last inequality we considered a strictly one because we already assumed that
\lambda\neq 1
. Using the fact that
\lambda>1
, that is
\lambda
should be a real number, we finally obtain that if
\lambda\geq 1+\frac{\mu}{2}
, then
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)=4\lambda-3-2\mu
if and only if
f=k_{\theta}
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Since
|a_{2}|\leq 2
for all

, then

. From the definition formula (

[

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)

\displaystyle=\left|a_{3}-\lambda a_{2}^{2}\right|-\mu|a_{2}|\leq\left|a_{3}-\lambda a_{2}^{2}\right|-\mu|a_{2}|\cdot\frac{1}{2}|a_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\left|a_{3}-\left(\lambda+\frac{\mu}{2}\right)a_{2}^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1+2\exp\left(-\frac{2(\lambda+\mu/2)}{1-(\lambda+\mu/2)}\right),&\text{if}&0\leq\lambda+\frac{\mu}{2}<1,\\

We emphasize that the estimate (

Indeed, having in mind that the assumption

which after substitution

,

(

, becomes

\frac{2e^{2t}}{e^{2}}-t>0,\;t\in[1,+\infty).

According to the first derivative test it’s easy to check that the above inequality holds that proves the required conclusion.

we have

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\geq-2\mu,

while if
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
|1-\lambda|\geq\frac{\mu^{2}}{4}
, then

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\geq-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{|1-\lambda|}}.

Comparing the above two lower bounds for
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)
, since

\max\left\{-2\mu;-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{|1-\lambda|}}\right\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{|1-\lambda|}},&\text{if}&|1-\lambda|\geq\frac{1}{4},\\
-2\mu,&\text{if}&|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{1}{4},\end{array}\right.

we conclude that for all
f\in\mathcal{S}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, and
\mu>0
, the next inequality holds

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{|1-\lambda|}},&\text{if}&|1-\lambda|\geq\max\left\{\frac{\mu^{2}}{4};\frac{1}{4}\right\}\\
-2\mu,&&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and gives a more comprehensive estimation for
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)
than (
2.8
).

3.
The corresponding estimations for the class
\mathcal{K}

It’s well-known that an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
is called to be a
convex function
if it is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
and the range
f(\mathbb{D})
is a convex domain (see
[
10
]
,
[
9
, p. 44]
). We denote by
\mathcal{K}
the class of convex and normalized functions
f
by
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
, hence it’s evident that
\mathcal{K}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
, and the analytic equivalent characterization of the class of convex functions
f
first stated in
[
10
]
is
f^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
and
\operatorname{Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)>0
in
\mathbb{D}
(see, also
[
4
, Theorem 1, p. 111]
,
[
9
, Theorem 2.7., p. 44]
). More information about these classes can be found in
[
9
,
2
]
, etc.

To obtain the similar results for the class
\mathcal{K}
we need the next lemma given by Trimble in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all functions
f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\dots
from
\mathcal{K}
,

(3.1)

\left|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}\right|\leq\frac{1}{3}\left(1-|a_{2}|^{2}\right).

The inequality is sharp with extremal function

(3.2)

f_{\alpha}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\left(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{1}{1-t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=z+\alpha z^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\left(2\alpha^{2}+1\right)z^{3}+\dots,\;z\in\mathbb{D},

and
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
.

Note that the range of
\alpha
for the extremal function exploits the fact that if
f\in\mathcal{K}
then
|a_{n}|\leq 1
,
n=2,3,\ldots
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For all
f\in\mathcal{K}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
\mu>0
and
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)
is defined by (
1.2
), we have:

(
i
)

If
|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{1}{3}
, then
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\leq\frac{1}{3}
. The equality holds if and only if
f
has the form given in (
3.2
) for
\alpha=0
, i.e. for the function
f_{0}(z)=z+\frac{1}{3}z^{3}+\dots
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

(
ii
)

If
|1-\lambda|>\frac{1}{3}
, then

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{1}{3},&\text{if}&\frac{1}{3}<|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{1}{3}+\mu,\\
|1-\lambda|-\mu,&\text{if}&\frac{1}{3}+\mu\geq|1-\lambda|.\end{array}\right.

Both of these estimations are sharp, since the equality holds in the first case for
a_{2}=0
and for
a_{2}=1
in the second case, i.e. for the functions
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
defined by (
3.2
), respectively.

Proof.

Similarly as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, form the triangle’s inequality and using the inequality (
3.1
) of Lemma
3.1
we have

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)

\displaystyle=\left|\left(a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}\right)+(1-\lambda)a_{2}^{2}\right|-\mu|a_{2}|\leq\left|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}\right|+|1-\lambda||a_{2}|^{2}-\mu|a_{2}|

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\left(1-|a_{2}|^{2}\right)+|1-\lambda||a_{2}|^{2}-\mu|a_{2}|

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\left(|1-\lambda|-\frac{1}{3}\right)|a_{2}|^{2}-\mu|a_{2}|+\frac{1}{3}=:\psi_{1}\left(|a_{2}|\right),

where

(3.5)

\psi_{1}(t)=\left(|1-\lambda|-\frac{1}{3}\right)t^{2}-\mu t+\frac{1}{3},\;0\leq t\leq 1,

and we used that
|a_{2}|\leq 1
for
f\in\mathcal{K}
(see
[
8
]
,
[
4
, Theorem 7, p. 117]
).

If
|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{1}{3}
, since
|1-\lambda|-\frac{1}{3}\leq 0
and
-\mu<0
, from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) it follows that
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\leq\psi_{1}\left(|a_{2}|\right)\leq\psi_{1}(0)=\frac{1}{3}
. The last of the previous inequalities becomes equality if and only if
|a_{2}|=0
, while in view of Lemma
3.1
the inequality (
3.3
) becomes equality if and only if the
f=f_{\alpha}
defined by (
3.2
), therefore

f(z)=f_{0}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{1-t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=z+\frac{1}{3}z^{3}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{2k+1},\;z\in\mathbb{D},

hence
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)=\frac{1}{3}
if and only if
f=f_{0}
.

If
|1-\lambda|>\frac{1}{3}
, then since
\psi_{1}(0)=\frac{1}{3}
and
\psi_{1}(1)=|1-\lambda|-\mu
, we have

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{3};|1-\lambda|-\mu\right\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{1}{3},&\text{if}&\frac{1}{3}<|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{1}{3}+\mu,\\
|1-\lambda|-\mu,&\text{if}&\frac{1}{3}+\mu\geq|1-\lambda|.\end{array}\right.

Using the above power series expansion of
f_{0}
and the fact that

f_{1}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}z^{k},\;z\in\mathbb{D},

it follows that the equality holds if the above two cases for the functions
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
defined (
3.2
), respectively.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)
is defined by (
1.2
),
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, and
\mu>\frac{2}{3}
. Then, for every function
f\in\mathcal{K}
we have

(3.6)

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{4\left(|1-\lambda|+\frac{1}{3}\right)},&\text{if}&|1-\lambda|\geq-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{\mu}{2},\\
|1-\lambda|-\mu,&\text{if}&|1-\lambda|\leq-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{\mu}{2}.\end{array}\right.

Both of these results are sharp, as follows:

(i)
The equality holds in the first case for the function
f_{t_{0}}
defined by (
3.2
) for
\alpha=t_{0}
where
t_{0}=\frac{\mu}{2\left(|1-\lambda|+\frac{1}{3}\right)}
, under the conditions

\frac{1}{3}-\frac{3}{4}\mu^{2}\leq 1-\lambda\leq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{2}\mu
,
\mu>\frac{2}{3}
.

(ii)
The equality holds in the second case for the function
f_{1}
defined by (
3.2
) for
\alpha=1
or for its rotations.

Proof.

In the beginning, let note that for every
f\in\mathcal{K}
, since
|a_{2}|\leq 1
we get

F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)=\left|a_{3}-\lambda a_{2}^{2}\right|-\mu|a_{2}|\geq-\mu,

that is
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)\geq-\mu
, which is equivalent to
\left|a_{3}-\lambda a_{2}^{2}\right|\geq\mu\left(|a_{2}|-1\right)
. For
\lambda=1
the equality is obtained if
f(z)=\frac{z}{1-z}
and its rotations.

For
\lambda\neq 1
, we can obtain better estimates as the next consideration shows. Using Lemma
3.1
and the triangle’s inequality we have

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)

\displaystyle=\left|\left(a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}\right)+(1-\lambda)a_{2}^{2}\right|-\mu|a_{2}|\geq|1-\lambda||a_{2}|^{2}-\left|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}\right|-\mu|a_{2}|

\displaystyle\geq|1-\lambda||a_{2}|^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\left(1-|a_{2}|^{2}\right)-\mu|a_{2}|=\left(|1-\lambda|+\frac{1}{3}\right)|a_{2}|^{2}-\mu|a_{2}|-\frac{1}{3}

\displaystyle=:\psi_{2}\left(|a_{2}|\right),

where

(3.7)

\psi_{2}(t)=\left(|1-\lambda|+\frac{1}{3}\right)t^{2}-\mu t-\frac{1}{3},\;0\leq t\leq 1.

From (
3.7
) we have
\psi_{2}(0)=-\frac{1}{3}
,
\psi_{2}(1)=|1-\lambda|-\mu
, and if

0\leq t_{0}=\frac{\mu}{2\left(|1-\lambda|+\frac{1}{3}\right)}\leq 1

then the function
\psi_{2}
has its minimum equal to

\psi_{2}\left(t_{0}\right)=-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{4\left(|1-\lambda|+\frac{1}{3}\right)}.

We note that
0\leq t_{0}\leq 1
if and only if
|1-\lambda|\geq\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{1}{3}
, with
\mu>\frac{2}{3}
because
\mu=\frac{2}{3}
implies the excluded case
\lambda=1
.

In case
|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{1}{3}
,
\mu>\frac{2}{3}
, we have

\min\left\{\psi_{2}(t):t\in[0,1]\right\}=\min\left\{\psi_{2}(0);\psi_{2}(1)\right\}=|1-\lambda|-\mu.

For the second case it is evident that the equality holds for the function
f_{1}
and it’s rotations. Like for the first case, the equality
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)=\psi_{2}\left(t_{0}\right)
could be possible if and only if we have equality in (
3.1
), i.e. if
f
has the form
f_{t_{0}}
defined by (
3.2
) for
\alpha=t_{0}
and
t_{0}=\frac{\mu}{2\left(|1-\lambda|+\frac{1}{3}\right)}
. The condition
F_{\lambda,\mu}(f)=\psi_{2}\left(t_{0}\right)
is equivalent to

\left|\frac{1}{3}\left(2t_{0}^{2}+1\right)-\lambda t_{0}^{2}\right|-\mu t_{0}=\left(|1-\lambda|+\frac{1}{3}\right)t_{0}^{2}-\mu t_{0}-\frac{1}{3},

where
|1-\lambda|\geq\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{1}{3}
,
\mu>\frac{2}{3}
, or

(3.8)

\left|\left((1-\lambda)-\frac{1}{3}\right)t_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\right|=\left(|1-\lambda|+\frac{1}{3}\right)t_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}.

From the condition
|1-\lambda|\geq\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{1}{3}
,
\mu>\frac{2}{3}
, let consider the case
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, hence
1-\lambda\leq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\mu}{2}<0
. Under this assumption the left hand side of the equality (
3.8
) becomes

L=\left|\left(-|1-\lambda|-\frac{1}{3}\right)t_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\right|=\left|\left(|1-\lambda|+\frac{1}{3}\right)t_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right|,

hence the equality (
3.8
) is valid if and only if
\left(|1-\lambda|+\frac{1}{3}\right)t_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\geq 0
, i.e.

|1-\lambda|\leq\frac{3}{4}\mu^{2}-\frac{1}{3}
, or since
1-\lambda<0
, whenever

1-\lambda\geq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{3}{4}\mu^{2},\;\mu>\frac{2}{3},

and the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

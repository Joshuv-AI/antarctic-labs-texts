Power quality enhancement using fractional order type-2 fuzzy SHAF optimized with hPSOFA algorithm
Authors: Alok Kumar Mishra, Jeevan Jyoti Mahakud, Sushanta Kumar Kamilla, Lalit Mohan Satapathy, Amaresh Gantayet, Satish Choudhury, Akshaya Kumar Patra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractReactive Power & Harmonic compensation (RPHC) represents a crucial area of investigation within the domains of power engineering. While passive filters have traditionally been favored for mitigating harmonics, their size becomes impractical when addressing multiple harmonics. In contrast, active power filters offer enhanced performance and address the limitations of passive filters, albeit at a higher cost. This study introduces a Shunt Hybrid Active Filter (SHAF) designed for RPHC. In our approach, the Kalman Filter (KF) is utilized for estimation of reference current per-unit (PU) quantity and the current reference crest value, which are derived from a Type-2 Fuzzy Fractional Order PID Controller (T2FFOPIDC). The optimal parameters for the T2FFOPIDC are determined using an innovative hybrid Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) Firefly Algorithm (hPSOFA), with a focus on minimizing the ITAE. The suggested system is validated, taking balanced as well as unbalanced nonlinear loads, and the outcomes are contrasted with those obtained from an SHAF system based on the Type-1 Fuzzy Fractional Order PID Controller (T1FFOPIDC), which is also optimized using hPSOFA. The compensation strategy employed in the SHAF differs from the conventional (id–iq) or (p–q) techniques, as it necessitates the current from the source side. Various performance metrics, including Reactive Power (Q), THD, and PFS, are assessed to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed controller. Case studies reveal that the hPSOFA-T2FFOPIDC-based SHAF demonstrates superior RPHC compared to the hPSOFA-T1FFOPIDC-based SHAF across various operational scenarios. Furthermore, the performance of the proposed hPSOFA-T2FFOPIDC-SHAF has been experimentally validated using dSPACE.Keywords: THD, PFS, hPSOFA, SHAF, T2FFOPIDC, RPHCSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The use of power electronics interfacing for various load types is increasingly prevalent in meeting consumer demands. A significant drawback of equipment based on power electronics is their tendency to draw nonlinear current from the supply, which is characterized by multiple harmonics1. The drawn nonlinear current is non-sinusoidal and does not align in phase with the supply voltage, necessitating the utility to provide a substantial amount of reactive power, thereby impacting equipment located near the Shared Connection Point (SCP)2.

Typically, Passive Filters (PFs) are employed for harmonic mitigation3. However, researchers have developed Active Filters (AFs), which are power electronic devices designed to overcome issues such as resonance, large size, and fixed compensation associated with PFs4. Nevertheless, the cost of AFs tends to increase with their rating, leading to the adoption of a more economical solution: a Shunt Passive Filter (SPF) combined with a low-rated Shunt Active Filter (SAF), referred to as SHAF, within a 3-wire 3Ø system5.

The controlling procedure of the SAF is critically important for precise RPHC, as any discrepancies can lead to inadequate compensation. Bhattacharya et al. have explored various methods for current reference extraction in both time and frequency domains for SAF in their work6. Panda et al. implemented a Unified Power Quality Conditioner (UPQC) aimed at enhancing power quality through SRF-based power angle control, as detailed in7. Betka et al.8 presented a grid-connected PV system with a Fuzzy-predictive controller-based SAF. Laid et al.9 introduced a technique for generating switching pulses for SAF based on HCC, SPWM, and SVPWM. Pradhan et al. have proposed a dual P-Q theory-controlled DVR for power quality (PQ) improvement in10. Furthermore, the literature discusses a variety of control methods, including ADALINE, ANN, WT, adaptive filtering, and PHC for estimation of the reference current of the SAF11.

A new method of controlling is proposed for the estimation of current references, eliminating the need for reactive VA requirements12,13. Accurate evaluation of phase, amplitude, and frequency is essential for signal construction; this idea is utilized to develop the PU reference value for the SAF from an imprecise power signal. Panda et al.14 have explored a PSO-based KF for enhancing PQ in SAF applications. Additionally, Gooi et al.15 have implemented a Robust Extended Kalman filter for harmonic estimation. In comparison to further estimation techniques like LSE and OCF, the KF is recognized for its robustness in distorted environments; thus, it is employed in this study to estimate the PU reference current16,17.

The crest of reference current is typically determined using CPIDC, as it is cost-effective and can be adjusted quickly and efficiently. However, CPIDC may not yield accurate results in complex systems. In such instances, a T1FLC integrated with PID control is employed, as it enhances recital by continuously upgrading the parameters of the CPIDC18. A significant advantage of utilizing a T1FLC-based CPIDC is that it does not necessitate the formulation of a mathematical model for selecting its gain factor, membership functions, or rule base for inputs and outputs. Radzi et al.19 have introduced a mixed approach combining T1FLC Proportional and crisp integral control. Cuma et al.20 have implemented a SAF with hierarchical neuro-fuzzy control. Das et al.21 have proposed a T1FLC-PID-based SHAF aimed at improving PQ. However, the effectiveness of T1FLC is contingent upon the careful selection of appropriate parameters and expertise in controller design.

To address the limitations associated with T1FLC, a Type-2 Fuzzy Logic Controller (T2FLC) has been developed, offering additional flexibility to manage uncertainties and nonlinearities, thereby enhancing the system’s PQ. Rathina Prabha et al.23 have suggested a 3-level SAF utilizing an Interval T2FLC to improve PQ. Furthermore, the integer order controller remains prevalent in the control domain. Due to the growing interest in fractional calculus, the Fractional Order PID controller has been recognized as more effective than CPIDC in various applications24. In this study, a novel T2FFOPIDC is employed for peak reference current calculation and regulation of the inverter’s DC link voltage.

Typically, various empirical rules are employed for the selection of fuzzy parameters, which may not necessarily yield the most suitable values. An inappropriate choice of gain factor can result in a decline in the controller’s performance. In contemporary practice, evolutionary algorithms and swarm intelligence techniques are widely utilized to determine optimal controller parameters. For instance, Hassan et al.25 introduced a controller optimized using GA. Panda et al.26 demonstrated a SAF for harmonic mitigation, optimized through PSO for a CPIDC. Neema et al.27 presented a controller optimized using a firefly algorithm. Additionally, Gupta et al.28 proposed a CSA aimed at enhancing power system stabilization. However, it is important to note that these algorithms may become ensnared in local minima, potentially failing to achieve the most wanted finest values during challenging and transitory working conditions24–28.

Hybrid mixtures of different optimization methods have been suggested across various applications to improve the performance of controllers. Patnaik et al.29 introduced a SAF based on PSO and Bacterial Foraging Optimization (BFO). In30, Elattar et al. have developed a GA-BFO method. Krishna et al.31 presented a hybrid optimization technique that integrates PSO and firefly algorithms. To enhance transient stability, the authors employed a hybrid Particle Swarm Optimization Firefly Algorithm (hPSOFA) to optimize the T2FFOPID controller as detailed in32. Consequently, hPSOFA is utilized in this context to adjust the scaling factors of the controllers (T1FFOPIDC and T2FFOPIDC), yielding improved results regarding THD and IPF.

Numerous methods for generating gate pulses for Voltage Source Converters (VSCs) are documented in the literature. Patra et al.33 introduced a gate pulse generation technique based on Hysteresis Current Control (HCC). To address the limitations of HCC, Bose et al.34 proposed an Adaptive HCC approach. Additionally, Mallick et al.3 introduced an AFHCC for a SHAF, which minimizes switching losses by maintaining a constant modulation frequency42–44. In the current study, AFHCC is employed for VSC’s switching pulse production.

Consequently, this research utilizes the hPSOFA-T2FFOPID or hPSOFA-T1FFOPIDC controller to optimize the extraction of maximum reference current and regulate the DC link voltage of the VSC. Various simulation cases are conducted to evaluate the compensatory capabilities of both the hPSOFA-T2FFOPIDC-SHAF and the hPSOFA-T1FFOPIDC-SHAF systems. Modeling of SHAF

SAF represents an excellent option for addressing harmonic issues; however, the cost associated with active filtering can be prohibitive. Consequently, passive filtering remains widely employed in various commercial applications for harmonic mitigation even today.

Passive components, including resistors, inductors, and capacitors, are used to create the SPF, rendering it a more economical solution. The SPF facilitates the passage of tuned harmonics through a low-impedance path, allowing harmonic currents to flow between the SPF and load rather than being sourced from the supply. The SAF functions by injecting a harmonic current of equal magnitude but opposite polarity at the SCP to counteract the harmonic current produced by nonlinear loads. A comprehensive design procedure for the SPF is detailed in our preceding publication3, with Fig. 1 providing a circuit diagram of the SPF.

Circuit diagram of SPF.

In this context, the SHAF is developed by integrating a cost-effective, low-rated SPF with an SAF, thereby eliminating the drawbacks associated with both passive and active filters while retaining their advantages. This study introduces an SHAF that merges an SPF with a VSC of reduced capacity. For the design of the SPF, a requirement of 2KVAR reactive power is established, which is allocated as 1000VAR for the 5th harmonic single-tuned filter (STF) and 500VAR each for the 7th STF, 11th–13th double-tuned filters. AFHCC

HCC is widely recognized as a reliable, straightforward, and accurate method for generating gate pulses for VSC. However, a significant drawback of HCC is its variable gating frequency, which leads to audio frequency noise and complicates the design of connecting filters ()33,39. This limitation can be addressed through the implementation of Adaptive HCC (AHCC). Nevertheless, the elevated-frequency gating associated with AHCC results in increased power losses. To mitigate this issue, AFHCC has been introduced. The block diagram of AFHCC is illustrated in Fig. 7. Firefly algorithm (FA)

Fireflies’ flashing behavior serves as an inspiration for FA40,41. These insects are distinguished by their bioluminescent flashing patterns. The modeling of fireflies is based on the following principles: (i) fireflies are considered to be of a single gender, (ii) the attraction among fireflies is independent of gender, and (iii) the attractiveness of each firefly is determined by its brightness level. To proceed with the process, fireflies will select a random path in the absence of brightness from any of their counterparts.

Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)

PSO is recognized as a population-based approach that mimics the behaviors of fish schooling and bird flocking24. In this method, a collection of particles, referred to as a swarm, is engaged in a perpetual search for food. This objective is accomplished by consistently adjusting the velocity and position of each particle within a multidimensional search space. Consequently, to identify the optimal solution, the velocity and position of each particle are continually updated.

Hybrid PSOFA technique (hPSOFA)

This method facilitates earlier convergence. Experimental result analysis

A hardware setup has been established using dSPACE to experimentally validate the proposed hPSOFA-T2FFOPIDC-SHAF system. The experimental parameters are detailed in Table 2. A Fluke PQ analyzer is employed to measure the experimental results. As indicated in the analysis of the simulation results (Fig. 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14), the SHAF system ensures that a sinusoidal & balanced SC is drawn from the supply regardless of whether the load is balanced or unbalanced. Therefore, for the experimental validation of the hPSOFA-T2FFOPIDC-SHAF system, an unbalanced nonlinear load has been utilized. Figures 16a,b illustrate the 3Ø input voltage and the 3Ø unbalanced nonlinear current, respectively, without any filtering. Due to the unbalanced nature of the load, the SC drawn from the input varies across the 3Ø, measuring: Ph-a—2.6 A, Ph-b—2.6 A and Ph-c—2 A. Additionally, the percentage total harmonic distortion (%THD) of the source current differs among the phases, recorded as: Ph-a—20.8%, Ph-b—20.8% and Ph-c—27.9%. Figure 16c,d presents: harmonic analysis for Ph-b and the performance parameter obtained under unbalanced nonlinear loading. A review of Fig. 16 confirms, the load is highly nonlinear and unbalanced, characterized by harmonics of lower order, as the LC and SC remain identical without filtering. To improve the performance of the system, hPSOFA-T2FFOPIDC-SHAF has been incorporated, resulting in the extraction of almost a sinusoidal as well as balanced current from the input. Conclusion

The proposed hPSOFA-T2FFOPIDC-SHAF has been designed, modeled, and simulated using MATLAB/Simulink to facilitate RPHC. The harmonic compensation capabilities of various SHAFs, which utilize T1FFOPID and T2FFOPID controllers, are examined under different loading conditions. Key performance metrics, including %THD, PFS, reactive power compensation, and the settling time of the dc-link voltage, along with the regulation of capacitor voltage, are analyzed to evaluate the robustness of SHAFs based on T2FFOPID and T1FFOPID under both balanced and unbalanced load scenarios. The analysis indicates that the hPSOFA-T2FFOPID-based SHAF demonstrates superior RPHC in comparison to the T1FFOPID-based SHAF across various operating conditions. In future research, a Fuzzy sliding mode control-based SHAF will be developed to enhance power quality through the application of new optimization techniques.

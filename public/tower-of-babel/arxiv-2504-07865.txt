Equidistribution in 2-Nilpotent Polish Groups and triple restricted sumsets
Ethan Ackelsberg, Asgar Jamneshan

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to establish a Ratner-type equidistribution theorem for orbits on homogeneous spaces associated with 2 2 -nilpotent locally compact Polish groups under the action of a countable discrete abelian group. We apply this result to establish the existence of triple restricted sumsets in subsets of positive density in arbitrary countable discrete abelian groups, subject to a necessary finiteness condition.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11B13, 37A15; Secondary 11B30, 22F30.

1.
Introduction

This paper consists of two parts. In the first part, we establish a Ratner-type equidistribution theorem for orbits on a homogeneous space of a
2
-nilpotent locally compact Polish group under the action of a countable discrete abelian group by translations. This result builds on a recent structure theorem
[
18
]
for a certain class of measure-preserving systems over countable discrete abelian groups, known as Conze–Lesigne systems. In the
2
-nilpotent case, it generalizes equidistribution results for linear orbits on nilmanifolds under
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-actions.

In the second part, building on a recent ergodic-theoretic approach to infinite sumsets in sets of positive density in the integers
[
21
]
, we use our equidistribution theorem to establish the existence of triple restricted sumsets in sets of positive density in an arbitrary countable discrete abelian group, under a necessary finiteness assumption on the group. This advances a question and a conjecture from
[
19
]
and extends a recent result on double restricted sumsets in sets of positive density in a countable discrete abelian group from
[
8
]
.

Accordingly, both the remainder of the introduction and the main body of the paper are structured in two corresponding parts. In what follows, we provide a more technical overview of each part.

(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
can be built as a tower of
group extensions

G/\Lambda\to G/G_{2}\Lambda\to\{\cdot\}

so that
(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
is topologically
distal
.
It then follows by a general property of distal systems that each orbit closure
\mathcal{O}(x)
for
x\in G/\Lambda
is
minimal
.
This first step is carried out in Section
2
, where the relevant terms from topological dynamics are more fully explained.

Second, in Section
3
, we prove that the minimal components
\mathcal{O}(x)
,
x\in G/\Lambda
, are
uniquely ergodic
.
For this, we adapt an argument of Lesigne
[
26
]
by decomposing functions on
G/\Lambda
according to the eigenspaces of the action of
G_{2}/\Lambda_{2}
on
G/\Lambda
and then applying a version of the Wiener–Wintner theorem to prove convergence of certain ergodic averages.

Combining the first two steps, we produce a description of the ergodic decomposition of
(G/\Lambda,\Sigma_{G/\Lambda},\mu_{G/\Lambda},T_{G/\Lambda})
in Section
4
.

These initial steps closely follow existing methods developed for describing orbits in nilmanifolds.
The remainder of the proof, however, departs substantially from previous methods.
This is out of necessity.
In the nilmanifold case, the standard approach is to reduce to the case that
G
is generated by the connected component of the identity element
G^{0}
together with the group
\phi(\Gamma)
.
This limited form of connectedness is then used in conjunction with finite-dimensionality of the Lie group
G
in order to describe orbit closures through an inductive argument.
For example, in
[
24
]
, connectedness (in the form
G=\langle G^{0},\phi(\Gamma)\rangle
) is used to show that
T_{G/\Lambda}
is ergodic if and only if
T_{G/[G,G]\Lambda}
is ergodic.
(The connectedness assumption cannot be dropped in this statement, as illustrated by
[
27
, Exemple 1]
.)
This then enables an induction argument that goes roughly as follows (see
[
24
, Theorem 2.21]
).
Assume
T_{G/\Lambda}
is not ergodic, and assume
x_{0}=\Lambda\in G/\Lambda
.
Then the group rotation
T_{G/[G,G]\Lambda}
is not ergodic, so the orbit of the point
[G,G]x_{0}
in
G/[G,G]\Lambda
is a proper subgroup
Z\leq G/[G,G]\Lambda
.
The preimage
G^{\prime}
of
Z
under the factor map
G\to G/[G,G]\Lambda
is then a closed subgroup of
G
with
\phi(\Gamma)\leq G^{\prime}
.
Crucially,
G^{\prime}
is a Lie group of dimension strictly less than the dimension of
G
, so after finitely many iterations, the resulting subnilmanifold
G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}
must be ergodic.
For the setting of translational systems, arguments relying on connectedness and finite-dimensionality meet a serious obstruction: the group
G
may be a totally disconnected group.
For example, a natural analogue of the Heisenberg nilmanifold when the acting group
\Gamma
is a countable vector space over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the space
G/\Lambda
with

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&x&z\\
0&1&y\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right):x,y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{p}((t^{-1}))\right\}

and

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&a&c\\
0&1&b\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right):a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]\right\},

which is topologically a Cantor set. Moreover, if
\Gamma
is not finitely generated, there are
counterexamples that show that finite-dimensionality can fail;
see the example presented after
[
36
, Conjecture 2.14]
.

In order to complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we therefore use a new method.
As a pre-processing step, we reduce to the case that
G
has a more convenient form.
Rather than the assumption
G=\langle G^{0},\phi(\Gamma)\rangle
referenced above, we reduce to the case that
G
contains a compact abelian Lie group
L
satisfying
[G,G]\leq L\leq Z(G)
and
L\cap\Lambda=\{1\}
.
This reduction is justified in Section
5
.

We finish with an ergodic theoretic argument in Section
6
.
The main idea is to represent
(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
as a skew-product system on the space
G/L\Lambda\times L
.
Projecting onto the first component
G/L\Lambda
produces a group rotation, in which we can show that the orbit closure of a point
xL
is a coset
Z_{x}
of a closed subgroup
Z_{0}\leq G/L\Lambda
.
General results in the theory of group extensions then guarantee the existence of a closed subgroup (the
Mackey group
)
L^{\prime}\leq L
such that the orbit closure
\mathcal{O}(x)
can be represented by a skew-product on
Z_{x}\times L^{\prime}
.
We then use arguments similar to those appearing in
[
18
]
to relate the skew-product on
Z_{x}\times L^{\prime}
to a translational system
H/(H\cap x\Lambda x^{-1})\cong Hx
for some closed subgroup
H\leq G
.

2.
Nilpotent translational systems are distal

The first main step in the proof of Theorem
1.5
is to show that nilpotent translational systems fall into the larger class of
distal
systems.
In this section, we review the definition and properties of distal systems and prove that nilpotent translational systems are indeed distal.

Let
(X,T_{X})
be a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system, and fix a metric
d
on
X
.
The system
(X,T_{X})
is
distal
if for every pair
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y
, one has
\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}d(T^{\gamma}_{X}x,T^{\gamma}_{X}y)>0
.
We note that the property of being distal does not depend on the choice of metric
d
.

2.1.
Isometric systems and isometric extensions

The simplest nontrivial examples of distal systems are provided by
isometric systems
, that is, topological dynamical
\Gamma
-systems
(X,T_{X})
, where
T_{X}
acts by isometries with respect to some metric
5
5

5

In any other compatible metric, the action of
T_{X}
will be uniformly equicontinuous, so such systems are also sometimes referred to as
equicontinuous systems
.
.
The relative notion of an isometric extension gives a tool for constructing many other distal systems from a given one.

Definition 2.1

(Isometric extension)
.

Let
\pi\colon(X,T_{X})\to(Y,T_{Y})
be an extension of topological dynamical
\Gamma
-systems.
We say that
(X,T_{X})
is an
isometric extension
of
(Y,T_{Y})
if for each
y\in Y
, there is a metric
d_{y}
on the fiber
\pi^{-1}(\{y\})
such that:

(1)

For any
y\in Y
, any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\pi^{-1}(\{y\})
, and any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, one has

d_{T^{\gamma}_{Y}y}\left(T_{X}^{\gamma}x_{1},T_{X}^{\gamma}x_{2}\right)=d_{y}(x_{1},x_{2}).

(2)

The function
d:\bigcup_{y\in Y}(\pi^{-1}(\{y\})\times\pi^{-1}(\{y\}))\to[0,\infty)
formed by gluing the metrics
d_{y}
is a continuous function on
\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in X\times X:\pi(x_{1})=\pi(x_{2})\}
.

(3)

For any
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y
, the metric spaces
(\pi^{-1}(\{y_{1}\}),d_{y_{1}})
and
(\pi^{-1}(\{y_{2}\}),d_{y_{2}})
are isometric.

As promised, isometric extensions preserve distality:

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
\pi:(X,T_{X})\to(Y,T_{Y})
is an isometric extension.
If
(Y,T_{Y})
is distal, then so is
(X,T_{X})
.

Proof.

This is a standard fact about distal systems; for convenience and completeness, we include a short proof here.
Let
d_{Y}
be a metric on
Y
, and let
(d_{y})_{y\in Y}
be a family of metrics as in Definition
2.1
.
Let
d_{X}
be a metric on
X
.
Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
with
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
.
We want to show
\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}d_{X}\left(T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{1},T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{2}\right)>0
.

If
\pi(x_{1})=\pi(x_{2})=y
, then
d\left(T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{1},T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{2}\right)=d(x_{1},x_{2})
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, where
d
is as in (2) in Definition
2.1
.
Since
d
is continuous on the compact set
K=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in X\times X:\pi(x_{1})=\pi(x_{2})\}
, it is uniformly continuous.
Hence, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
(z_{1},z_{2}),(z^{\prime}_{1},z^{\prime}_{2})\in K
and
d_{X}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{1})+d_{X}(z_{2}^{\prime},z_{2})<\delta
, then
|d(z^{\prime}_{1},z^{\prime}_{2})-d(z_{1},z_{2})|<d(x_{1},x_{2})
.
It follows that
d_{X}\left(T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{1},T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{2}\right)\geq\delta
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
: if not, then taking
(z_{1},z_{2})=(T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{1},T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{2})
and
(z^{\prime}_{1},z^{\prime}_{2})=(T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{1},T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{1})
leads to a contradiction.

Now suppose
\pi(x_{1})\neq\pi(x_{2})
.
Let
y_{1}=\pi(x_{1})
and
y_{2}=\pi(x_{2})
.
Since
(Y,T_{Y})
is distal, we have
\varepsilon=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}d_{Y}\left(T^{\gamma}_{Y}y_{1},T^{\gamma}_{Y}y_{2}\right)>0
.
The map
\pi
is (uniformly) continuous, so let
\delta>0
such that if
z_{1},z_{2}\in X
and
d_{X}(z_{1},z_{2})<\delta
, then
d_{Y}(\pi(z_{1}),\pi(z_{2}))<\varepsilon
.
We then have
d_{X}\left(T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{1},T^{\gamma}_{X}x_{2}\right)\geq\delta
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
∎

2.2.
Rotational systems and group extensions

Note that every rotational
\Gamma
-system is isometric, since every compact abelian groups admits a translation-invariant metric. Moreover, rotational
\Gamma
-system enjoy many additional convenient properties.
In order to formulate these properties, we recall some terminology from topological dynamics.
Given a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system
(X,T_{X})
, a point
x\in X
is
transitive
if the orbit of
x
is dense in
X
, that is,
\overline{\{T_{X}^{\gamma}x:\gamma\in\Gamma\}}=X
.
The system
(X,T_{X})
is
transitive
if it has a transitive point.
We say that a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system
(X,T_{X})
is
minimal
if every point
x\in X
is transitive.
Finally, a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system
(X,T_{X})
is
uniquely ergodic
if there is a unique
T_{X}
-invariant Borel probability measure on
X
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
Z
be a compact abelian group, let
\psi:\Gamma\to Z
be a group homomorphism, and let
(Z,\Sigma_{Z},\mu_{Z},T_{Z})
be the corresponding rotational
\Gamma
-system.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

(\psi(\gamma))_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is dense in
Z
;

(ii)

(\psi(\gamma))_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is well-distributed in
Z
with respect to the Haar measure
\mu_{Z}
;

(iii)

for any
\chi\in\hat{Z}\setminus\{1\}
, there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\chi(\psi(\gamma))\neq 1
;

(iv)

for any
\chi\in\hat{Z}\setminus\{1\}
and any Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
in
\Gamma
, one has

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}\chi(\psi(\gamma))=0.

(v)

(Z,T_{Z})
is minimal;

(vi)

(Z,T_{Z})
is uniquely ergodic;

(vii)

(Z,\Sigma_{Z},\mu_{Z},T_{Z})
is ergodic.

Proof.

These equivalences are well known in the
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
case, with proofs of most of the implications appearing, for example, in
[
37
, §2.6, §2.9]
.
For completenss, we include a short proof in the general case.

First, since the Haar measure
\mu_{Z}
has full support, being well-distributed with respect to
\mu_{Z}
is a stronger property than being dense in
Z
, so (ii)
\implies
(i).
Next, by the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, linear combinations of characters
\chi\in\hat{Z}
are uniformly dense in the space
C(Z)
of continuous functions on
Z
, so (iv)
\implies
(ii).

We now check the implication (i)
\implies
(iii).
Suppose (i) holds.
Let
\chi\in\hat{Z}
and assume
\chi(\psi(\gamma))=1
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
We will show
\chi=1
.
Indeed, since
\chi
is continuous, it is determined by its values on the dense set
\{\psi(\gamma):\gamma\in\Gamma\}
, so
\chi=1
.

Now suppose (iii) holds.
We will prove (iv).
Fix a nontrival character
\chi\in\hat{Z}\setminus\{1\}
and a Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
in
\Gamma
.
We will show that every subsequence of
a_{N}=\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}\chi(\psi(\gamma))
has a further subsequence converging to 0, whence
\lim_{N\to\infty}a_{N}=0
.
Let
N_{1}<N_{2}<\ldots
.
The sequence
(a_{N_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded, so it has a convergent subsequence
(a_{N_{j_{k}}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
By (iii), let
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
such that
\chi(\psi(\gamma_{0}))\neq 1
.
Then applying the asymptotic invariance property of the Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{N_{j_{k}}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N_{j_{k}}}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N_{j_{k}}}}\chi(\psi(\gamma))\\
=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N_{j_{k}}}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N_{j_{k}}}}\chi(\psi(\gamma+\gamma_{0}))\\
=\chi(\psi(\gamma_{0}))\cdot\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{N_{j_{k}}}.

That is,
(1-\chi(\psi(\gamma_{0}))\cdot\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{N_{j_{k}}}=0
, so
\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{N_{j_{k}}}=0
as claimed.

We have thus far established the equivalence between properties (i)–(iv).
We now proceed to show the equivalence with the dynamical properties (v)–(vii).

The orbit of the point
0\in Z
under
T_{Z}
is
(\psi(\gamma))_{\gamma\in Z}
, so (v)
\implies
(i).
For the converse, assuming (i) holds, we can write the orbit of an arbitrary point
z\in Z
under
T_{Z}
as
(z+\psi(\gamma))_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, which is a shift of a dense set and hence dense, since the topology on
Z
is shift-invariant.
Therefore,
(Z,T_{Z})
is minimal.

The Haar measure
\mu_{Z}
is always an invariant measure for
(Z,T_{Z})
, so (vi)
\implies
(vii).
Moreover, since
\mu_{Z}
has full support, (vii)
\implies
(v).

Let us check the remaining implication (i)
\implies
(vi).
Assume (i) holds, and let
\mu
be a
T_{Z}
-invariant Borel probability measure on
Z
.
Let
t\in Z
.
By (i), there is a sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\Gamma
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi(\gamma_{n})=t
.
Hence, by
T_{Z}
-invariance of
\mu
, we have

\int_{Z}f(z)~d\mu(z)=\int_{Z}f(z+\psi(\gamma_{n}))~d\mu(z)

for every continuous function
f\in C(Z)
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Taking a limit as
n\to\infty
,

\int_{Z}f(z)~d\mu(z)=\int_{Z}f(z+t)~d\mu(z)

for every
f\in C(Z)
.
Thus,
\mu
is a shift-invariant probability measure on
Z
, so
\mu=\mu_{Z}
.
∎

In general, as the following proposition shows, isometric systems decompose as unions of rotational systems, so the behavior of isometric systems is well-understood.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(X,T_{X})
be an isometric topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system.
Then
X
decomposes as a disjoint union of minimal systems
X=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}X_{i}
.
Moreover, each minimal system
(X_{i},T_{X_{i}})
is isomorphic to a rotational
\Gamma
-system.

Proof.

This follows from combining
[
37
, Proposition 2.6.7, Proposition 2.6.9]
. These propositions are proved in
[
37
]
for
\mathbb{Z}
-systems, but the proofs extend to cover the general case of
\Gamma
-systems for an arbitrary countable discrete abelian group
\Gamma
.
∎

Definition 2.5

(Group extension)
.

Let
(X,T_{X})
be a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system.
Suppose

,

Proposition 2.6
.

Every free group extension is an isometric extension.

Proof.

Let
(X,T_{X})
,
K
, and
(Y,T_{Y})
be as in Definition
2.5
, and assume the action of
K
on

Fix a
K

.
Then since the action of

Semi-simplicity

We have the following generalization of Proposition

.

(Semi-simplicity of distal systems)

2.4.
Distality of translational systems

We can establish the following description of translational systems.

Proposition 2.9
.

A translational
\Gamma
-system
(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
is distal.

Proof.

Let
G_{\bullet}
be a filtration (of degree
d\in\mathbb{N}
) that is compatible with
\Lambda
.
We claim that
G_{i}\Lambda
is a closed subgroup of
G
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
To see this, suppose
(g_{i,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
G_{i}
,
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
\Lambda
, and
\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{i,n}\lambda_{n}=g\in G
.
Since
G_{i}/\Lambda_{i}
is compact, there exists a subsequence
(g_{i,n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
g_{i,n_{k}}\Lambda_{i}
converges to some point
g_{i}\Lambda_{i}
in
G_{i}/\Lambda_{i}
.
Hence, there exists a sequence
(\lambda_{i,k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\Lambda_{i}
so that
g_{i}=\lim_{k\to\infty}g_{i,n_{k}}\lambda_{i,k}
.
Let
\lambda=g_{i}^{-1}g
.
By continuity of the group operations on
G
, we have

\lambda=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{i,k}^{-1}g_{i,n_{k}}^{-1}g_{i,n_{k}}\lambda_{n_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{i,k}^{-1}\lambda_{n_{k}}.

But
\Lambda
is a closed subgroup of
G
and
\lambda_{i,k}^{-1}\lambda_{n_{k}}\in\Lambda
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, so
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
g=g_{i}\lambda\in G_{i}\Lambda
.

We now let
Y_{i}=G_{i}\backslash X=G/G_{i}\Lambda
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
Then
(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
is obtained as a tower
G/\Lambda=Y_{d+1}\to Y_{d}\to\dots\to Y_{2}\to Y_{1}=\{\cdot\}
.
By Proposition
2.2
, it suffices to show that each of the extensions
\pi_{i}:(Y_{i+1},T_{Y_{i+1}})\to(Y_{i},T_{Y_{i}})
is isometric.
We claim
(Y_{i+1},T_{Y_{i+1}})
is a free extension of
(Y_{i},T_{Y_{i}})
by the compact abelian group
G_{i}/G_{i+1}\Lambda_{i}
so that
\pi_{i}
is an isometric extension by Proposition
2.6
.
Indeed,
K_{i}=G_{i}/G_{i+1}\Lambda_{i}
acts on
(Y_{i+1},T_{Y_{i+1}})
by
(gG_{i+1}\Lambda_{i})\cdot y=gy
.
This action is well defined and free: if
g_{1},g_{2}\in G_{i}
and
y=hG_{i+1}\Lambda\in Y_{i+1}
, then

g_{1}y=g_{2}y\iff g_{1}hG_{i+1}\Lambda=g_{2}hG_{i+1}\Lambda\\
\iff hg_{1}\underbrace{[g_{1}^{-1},h^{-1}]}_{\in G_{i+1}}G_{i+1}\Lambda=hg_{2}\underbrace{[g_{2}^{-1},h^{-1}]}_{\in G_{i+1}}G_{i+1}\Lambda\\
\iff g_{1}G_{i+1}\Lambda=g_{2}G_{i+1}\Lambda.

Moreover,
K_{i}\backslash Y_{i+1}=G_{i}\backslash Y_{i+1}=Y_{i}
.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
be a translational
\Gamma
-system.
Then for any
x\in G/\Lambda
, the orbit closure
\mathcal{O}(x)
is minimal.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.9
and Theorem
2.8
,
G/\Lambda
decomposes as a disjoint union of minimal systems
G/\Lambda=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}X_{i}
.
Taking
i\in I
such that
x\in X_{i}
, we have that
\mathcal{O}(x)=X_{i}
is minimal.
∎

3.
Minimal components of translational systems are uniquely ergodic

The next step in the proof of Theorem
1.5
is to show that each of the minimal components of
(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
is uniquely ergodic.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
be a degree
2
translational
\Gamma
-system.
For any
x\in G/\Lambda
, the orbit closure
(\mathcal{O}(x),T_{\mathcal{O}(x)})
is uniquely ergodic.

3.1.
Unique ergodicity

Let us first review some basic properties of uniquely ergodic systems.

Proposition 3.2
.

A topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system
(X,T_{X})
is uniquely ergodic with unique invariant measure
\mu_{X}
if and only if for every continuous function
f\in C(X)
, every
x\in X
, and every Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
in
\Gamma
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f(T^{\gamma}_{X}x)=\int_{X}f~d\mu_{X}.

Proof.

This is a standard fact, but we include a short proof for completeness.

First, assume that
(X,T_{X})
is uniquely ergodic with unique invariant measure
\mu_{X}
.
Let
x\in X
, and let
(\Phi_{N})
be a Følner sequence in
\Gamma
.
We want to show that

(3.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}\delta_{T_{X}^{\gamma}x}=\mu_{X},

where the limit is taken in the weak* topology.
We will establish this convergence by showing that every subsequence of
\mu_{N}=\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}\delta_{T_{X}^{\gamma}x}
has a further subsequence converging to
\mu_{X}
.
Let
(N_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a strictly increasing sequence in
\mathbb{N}
.
By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem, the sequence of probability measures
(\mu_{N_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
has a convergent subsequence
(\mu_{N_{j_{k}}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
The limit
\mu=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{N_{j_{k}}}
is
T_{X}
-invariant by the asymptotic invariance property of
(\Phi_{N})
, so by unique ergodicity,
\mu=\mu_{X}
.
This proves (
3.1
).

Conversely, suppose that for every continuous function
f\in C(X)
, every
x\in X
, and every Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
in
\Gamma
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f(T^{\gamma}_{X}x)=\int_{X}f~d\mu_{X}.

Let
\mu
be a
T_{X}
-invariant Borel probability measure on
X
.
Then by
T_{X}
-invariance, we have

\int_{X}f~d\mu=\int_{X}f(T_{X}^{\gamma}x)~d\mu(x)

for every
f\in C(X)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
Averaging over
\gamma\in\Phi_{N}
for some Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
and taking a limit as
N\to\infty
, we have

\int_{X}f~d\mu=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}\int_{X}f(T_{X}^{\gamma}x)~d\mu(x)\\
=\int_{X}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f(T_{X}^{\gamma}x)~d\mu(x)=\int_{X}f~d\mu_{X},

where we have moved the limit inside the integral using the bounded convergence theorem.
Thus,
\mu=\mu_{X}
, so
(X,T_{X})
is uniquely ergodic with unique invariant measure
\mu_{X}
.
∎

For minimal systems, a simpler criterion was given by Oxtoby (see
[
31
, Proposition 5.4]
) in the case of
\mathbb{Z}
-actions.
Essentially the same argument works for minimal actions of general countable discrete abelian groups.
For completeness, we record the proof in full generality here.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix a Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
in
\Gamma
.
A minimal topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system
(X,T_{X})
is uniquely ergodic if and only if

\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f\left(T^{\gamma}_{X}x\right)

converges for every
f\in C(X)
and every
x\in X
.

Proof.

If
(X,T_{X})
is uniquely ergodic with unique invariant measure
\mu_{X}
, then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f\left(T^{\gamma}_{X}x\right)=\int_{X}{f~d\mu_{X}}

for every
f\in C(X)
and every
x\in X
by Proposition
3.2
.

For the converse, fix a continuous function
f\in C(X)
.
For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, put
M_{N}f(x)=\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f\left(T^{\gamma}_{X}x\right)
, and let
Mf=\lim_{N\to\infty}M_{N}f
, where the limit is taken pointwise.
We claim
Mf
is a constant function.

Suppose for contradiction that
Mf
is not constant.
Since
(\Phi_{N})
is a Følner sequence,
Mf
is constant on each orbit.
Then since
(X,T_{X})
is minimal (so that every orbit is dense) it follows that
Mf
is everywhere discontinuous.
On the other hand,
Mf
is a pointwise limit of continuous functions
M_{N}f
, so
Mf
is discontinuous only on a meager set by the Baire–Osgood theorem; see, e.g.,
[
32
, Theorem 7.3]
.
This is a contradiction, so
Mf
must be constant.

We can thus define a measure
\mu_{X}
on
X
by
\int_{X}{f~d\mu_{X}}=Mf
.
Since every point
x\in X
is generic for
\mu_{X}
along
(\Phi_{N})
, we conclude that
(X,T_{X})
is uniquely ergodic with
\mu_{X}
as the unique invariant measure.
∎

3.2.
A Wiener–Wintner theorem for abelian groups

The final ingredient in the proof of Theorem
3.1
is the following generalization of the classical Wiener–Wintner theorem (see
[
30
, p. 121]
or
[
39
, Corollary 4.1]
, for a proof), where we denote by
\hat{\Gamma}
the Pontryagin dual of the group
\Gamma
.

Theorem 3.4

(Wiener–Wintner theorem for abelian groups)
.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X},T_{X})
be a measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system, and let
(\Phi_{N})
be a tempered
6
6

6

A Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
is
tempered
if for some
C>0
and all
N

\left|\bigcup_{M<N}\Phi_{M}^{-1}\Phi_{N}\right|\leq C|\Phi_{N}|.

Tempered Følner sequences are important for establishing pointwise ergodic theorems for amenable groups and they always exist, for instance as subsequences of any given Følner sequence; see
[
28
]
.
Følner sequence in
\Gamma
.
There is a set
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
with
\mu_{X}(X^{\prime})=1
such that for every
\lambda\in\hat{\Gamma}
, every
f\in C(X)
, and every
x\in X^{\prime}
, the sequence

\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}\lambda(\gamma)f\left(T^{\gamma}_{X}x\right)

converges.

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

By Corollary
2.10
and Lemma
3.3
, it suffices to show that there exists a Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
in
\Gamma
such that for every
f\in C(G/\Lambda)
and every
x\in G/\Lambda
, the sequence

\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f\left(T^{\gamma}_{G/\Lambda}x\right)

converges.
We will use an argument due to Lesigne
[
26
]
to establish this claim.

Fix a tempered Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
.
By the Stone–Weierstraß  theorem, finite sums of functions
g
satisfying
g(lx)=\chi(l)g(x)
for
l\in G_{2}/\Lambda_{2}
and
x\in G/\Lambda
for some
\chi
in the Pontryagin dual
\widehat{G_{2}/\Lambda_{2}}
of
G_{2}/\Lambda_{2}
are dense in
C(G/\Lambda)
. Therefore, by linearity and continuity, it suffices to consider
f\in C(G/\Lambda)
such that
f(lx)=\chi(l)f(x)
for
l\in G_{2}/\Lambda_{2}
and
x\in G/\Lambda
.

By Theorem
3.4
, let
x_{0}\in G/\Lambda
be such that the averages

\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}\lambda(\gamma)h(T^{\gamma}_{G/\Lambda}x_{0})

converge for every
\lambda\in\hat{\Gamma}
and every
h\in C(G/\Lambda)
.
Write
x_{0}=g_{0}\Lambda
with
g_{0}\in G
.
Then for any
x=g\Lambda\in G/\Lambda
, we have

\displaystyle f\left(T^{\gamma}_{G/\Lambda}x\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(\phi(\gamma)g\Lambda\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(\phi(\gamma)gg_{0}^{-1}g_{0}\Lambda\right)

\displaystyle=f\left([\phi(\gamma),gg_{0}^{-1}]gg_{0}^{-1}\phi(\gamma)g_{0}\Lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\chi\left([\phi(\gamma),gg_{0}^{-1}]\right)f\left(gg_{0}^{-1}\phi(\gamma)x_{0}\right).

Taking
h(z)=f\left(gg_{0}^{-1}z\right)
and
\lambda(\gamma)=\chi\left([\phi(\gamma),gg_{0}^{-1}]\right)
(which defines an element of
\hat{\Gamma}
, since the commutator map
[\cdot,\cdot]
is bilinear in a
2
-nilpotent group), we then have
f(T^{\gamma}_{G/\Lambda}x)=\lambda(\gamma)h(T^{\gamma}_{G/\Lambda}x_{0})
, so

\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f(T^{\gamma}_{G/\Lambda}x)=\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}\lambda(\gamma)h(T^{\gamma}_{G/\Lambda}x_{0})

converges.

4.
Ergodic decomposition of translational systems

Combining the results of the previous sections, we have the following description of the ergodic measures for the translational
\Gamma
-system
(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
be a translational
\Gamma
-system of degree 2.
The space
G/\Lambda
decomposes as a disjoint union of orbit closures
G/\Lambda=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}(x_{i})
, and each of the systems
(\mathcal{O}(x_{i}),T_{\mathcal{O}(x_{i})})
is minimal and uniquely ergodic.
In particular, if
\mu
is an ergodic measure for
(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
, then
\mu=\mu_{x_{i}}
for some
i\in I
, where
\mu_{x_{i}}
is the unique
T_{\mathcal{O}(x_{i})}
-invariant Borel probability measure on
\mathcal{O}(x_{i})
.

Before giving the proof, we state an immediate corollary:

Corollary 4.2
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
4.1
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

(G/\Lambda,\Sigma_{G/\Lambda},\mu_{G/\Lambda},T_{G/\Lambda})
is ergodic.

(ii)

(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
is minimal.

(iii)

(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
is uniquely ergodic.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

The decomposition into disjoint minimal orbit closures comes from Corollary
2.10
.
That each orbit closure is uniquely ergodic follows from Theorem
3.1
.
For the final claim, suppose
\mu
is an ergodic measure for
(G/\Lambda,T_{G/\Lambda})
.
Then by Lindenstrauss’s pointwise ergodic theorem
[
28
]
, there is a Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
in
\Gamma
and a point
x\in G/\Lambda
such that for every
f\in C(G/\Lambda)
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f(T^{\gamma}_{G/\Lambda}x)=\int_{G/\Lambda}f~d\mu.

But by Corollary
2.10
and Proposition
3.2
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f(T^{\gamma}_{G/\Lambda}x)=\int_{G/\Lambda}f~d\mu_{x}.

Hence,
\mu=\mu_{x}
.
∎

5.
Reduction of Theorem
1.5
to a special case

The goal of this section is to reduce the general case of Theorem
1.5
to the special case in which there exists a compact abelian Lie group
L
such that
[G,G]\subseteq L\subseteq Z(G)
and
L\cap\Lambda=\{1\}
.
7
7

7

Such a reduction can always be achieved in the structure theorem (Theorem
1.4
), see
[
18

.

We first set up some notation.
Let
\phi:\Gamma\to G
be the group homomorphism such that
T_{G/\Lambda}^{\gamma}x=\phi(\gamma)x
.
Let
G=G_{0}=G_{1}\geq G_{2}\geq G_{3}=\{1\}
be a filtration of
G
compatible with
\Lambda
.
Note that being a filtration in this case means that
[G,G]\subseteq G_{2}\subseteq Z(G)
.

Reduction 1
.

We may assume without loss of generality that
G
acts faithfully on
G/\Lambda
.

Proof.

Assume that Theorem
1.5
holds under the additional assumption that
G
acts faithfully.
We will deduce the general case.

Let
N=\{g\in G:gx=x~\text{for every}~x\in G/\Lambda\}
.
It is easy to see that
N
is a closed normal subgroup of
G
.
Moreover, by considering the point
x_{0}=\Lambda\in G/\Lambda
, we have
N\leq\Lambda
.
Let

\Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda/N

Define

Fix a point

Let

By assumption, since

We now observe a consequence of Reduction
1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume
G
acts faithfully on
G/\Lambda
.
Then
G_{2}\cap\Lambda=\{1\}

G_{2}

Proof.

Let
\Lambda_{2}=G_{2}\cap\Lambda

\lambda x=\lambda g\Lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{=}}g\lambda\Lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2)}}{{=}}g\Lambda=x,

.

\Lambda_{2}=\{1\}
.
Now, since

We now want to reduce to the case that

is a Lie group.
The key group theoretic fact enabling this reduction is that every compact abelian group is an inverse limit of compact abelian Lie groups.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

be a compact abelian group.
There exists a sequence of closed subgroups

Since

be a sequence of finitely generated subgroups with

.
Then define
K_{n}=A_{n}^{\perp}=\{k\in K:\chi(k)=1~\text{for every}~\chi\in A_{n}\}
.
By construction,
K=K_{1}\geq K_{2}\geq\ldots
and

.
Moreover,

is a Lie group.
∎

Reduction 2
.

7.

Definition 7.1

Let

A tuple

We denote the set of all

Given a point

EP_{k}(x_{0})=\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}):(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1})\in EP_{k}\right\}.

The following lemma shows that Erdős progressions in a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system lead to restricted sumset configurations in corresponding subsets of
\Gamma
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
(X,T_{X})
be a topological
\Gamma
-system, and let
x_{0}\in X
.
Suppose
U_{1},\dots,U_{k-1}\subseteq X
are open sets and
EP_{k}(x_{0})\cap(U_{1}\times\dots\times U_{k-1})\neq\emptyset
.
Then letting
A_{j}=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:T_{X}^{\gamma}x_{0}\in U_{j}\}
, there exists an infinite set
B\subseteq\Gamma
such that

(7.1)

\begin{split}B&\subseteq A_{1}\\
B\oplus B&\subseteq A_{2}\\
\vdots\\
B^{\oplus(k-1)}&\subseteq A_{k-1}.\end{split}

Proof.

The
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
case is shown in
[
22
, Lemma 2.2]
and the same strategy works for a general abelian group
\Gamma
.
We include the details below for completeness.

Let
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1})\in EP_{k}(x_{0})\cap(U_{1}\times\dots\times U_{k-1})
.
By the definition of an Erdős progression, let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of distinct elements such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(T_{X}^{\gamma_{n}}x_{0},\dots,T_{X}^{\gamma_{n}}x_{k-2}\right)=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}).

Refining to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume
T_{X}^{\gamma_{n}}x_{i}\in U_{i+1}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k-2\}
.

We construct a sequence
b_{m}=\gamma_{n_{m}}
inductively to have the property

(7.2)

x_{i}\in T_{X}^{-b_{m}}U_{i+1}\cap\bigcap_{j<m}T_{X}^{-b_{m}-b_{j}}U_{i+2}\cap\dots\cap\bigcap_{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k-2-i}<m}T_{X}^{-b_{m}-\sum_{l=1}^{k-2-i}b_{j_{l}}}U_{k-1}

for each
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k-2\}
.
For
m=1
, we take
n_{1}=1
.
Then
x_{i}\in T_{X}^{-b_{1}}U_{i+1}
by our assumption on the sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
The other terms in (
7.2
) are trivial for
m=1
, so (
7.2
) is satisfied.

Suppose we have chosen
n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots<n_{m}
such that (
7.2
) is satisfied for
j\leq m
with
b_{j}=\gamma_{n_{j}}
.
Let

V_{j,i}=T_{X}^{-b_{j}}U_{i+1}\cap\bigcap_{j^{\prime}<j}T_{X}^{-b_{j}-b_{j^{\prime}}}U_{i+2}\cap\dots\cap\bigcap_{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k-2-i}<j}T_{X}^{-b_{j}-\sum_{l=1}^{k-2-i}b_{j_{l}}}U_{k-1}

for
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k-2\}
.
By the inductive hypothesis,
V_{j,i}
is an open neighborhood of
x_{i}
for each
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k-2\}
.
Thus, for all large enough

, we have

\left(T_{X}^{\gamma_{n}}x_{0},T_{X}^{\gamma_{n}}x_{1},\dots,T_{X}^{\gamma_{n}}x_{k-3}\right)\in\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}V_{j,1}\right)\times\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}V_{j,2}\right)\times\dots\times\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}V_{j,k-2}\right).

Choose
n_{m+1}>n_{m}
sufficiently large so that for
b_{m+1}=\gamma_{n_{m+1}}
, we have
T_{X}^{b_{m+1}}x_{i}\in\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}V_{j,i+1}
for every
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k-3\}
.
Then for
i\in\{0,\dots,k-2\}
,

x_{i}\in T_{X}^{-b_{m+1}}U_{i+1}

and for
i\in\{0,\dots,k-3\}
,

x_{i}\in\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}T_{X}^{-b_{m+1}}V_{j,i+1}\\
=T_{X}^{-b_{m+1}}\left(\bigcap_{j<m+1}T_{X}^{-b_{j}}U_{i+2}\cap\bigcap_{j_{1}<j_{2}<m+1}T_{X}^{-b_{j_{1}}-b_{j_{2}}}U_{i+3}\cap\right.\dots{\\
}\left.\cap\bigcap_{j_{1}<\dots<j_{k-2-i}<m}T_{X}^{-\sum_{l=1}^{k-2-i}b_{j_{l}}}U_{k-1}\right).

That is, (
7.2
) is satisfied.

Now let
B=\{b_{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
Applying (
7.2
) with

gives (
7.1
).
∎

Using an appropriate version of the Furstenberg correspondence principle and Lemma
7.2
, we can reduce Theorem
1.9
to a statement in ergodic theory.
In order to formulate the dynamical version of Theorem
1.9
, we introduce the following notation.
Let
(X,T_{X})
be a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system.
Given a

-invariant Borel probability measure

on

Theorem 7.3

(Dynamical Formulation of Theorem
1.9
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countably infinite abelian group such that
[\Gamma:6\Gamma]<\infty

Let

be a topological dynamical
\Gamma

Suppose

,

, and

-invariant ergodic measure such that

Proof that Theorem
7.3
and Theorem
1.9
are equivalent.

We use a standard argument combining a version of the Furstenberg correspondence principle and Lemma
7.2
.

Suppose Theorem
7.3
holds.
Let
\Gamma
be a countably infinite abelian group such that
[\Gamma:6\Gamma]<\infty
, and let

with

.
By the Furstenberg correspondence principle (for the appropriate version, see
[
8
, Theorem 2.15]
), there exists a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system
(X,T_{X})
, an ergodic
T_{X}
-invariant measure
\mu_{X}

\Phi=(\Phi_{N})
in
\Gamma

, and a clopen set

and

.
Hence, by Theorem
7.3
, there exist
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in E\times E\times E
and

Then noting that

1.9
holds.

Conversely, suppose Theorem
1.9
holds.
As in the setup of Theorem
7.3
, let
\Gamma
be a countably infinite abelian group with
[\Gamma:6\Gamma]<\infty
, let
(X,T_{X})
be a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system, let
\mu_{X}
be an ergodic
T_{X}
-invariant measure, let
\Phi=(\Phi_{N})
be a Følner sequence in
\Gamma
, let

, and let
E\subseteq X
be an open set with
\mu_{X}(E)>0
.
By inner regularity of the measure
\mu_{X}

\mu_{X}(K)>0

Let
U\subseteq X

X

Define

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap\Phi_{N}|}{|\Phi_{N}|}=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}\mathbbm{1}_{U}(T_{X}^{\gamma}a)\geq\mu_{X}(U)

Hence, by Theorem

, there exists

By compactness of

8.
Useful facts from ergodic theory

Our goal is now to prove Theorem
7.3
.
In the course of the proof, we will utilize several results from ergodic theory, which we collect in this short section.

8.1.
The van der Corput lemma

A fundamental result used for “complexity reduction” in the analysis of multiple ergodic averages is the van der Corput lemma, which we will use in the following form.

Lemma 8.1

(van der Corput lemma
[
35
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group, and let
u:\Gamma\to\mathcal{H}
be a bounded sequence.
Let
(\Phi_{N})
be a Følner sequence in
\Gamma
.
Suppose that:

•

the limit

z(\delta)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}\left\langle u(\gamma+\delta),u(\gamma)\right\rangle

exists for every
\delta\in\Gamma
, and

•

there exists
K<\infty
such that for any Følner sequence
(\Psi_{M})
in
\Gamma
,

\limsup_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Psi_{M}|}\left|\sum_{\delta\in\Psi_{M}}z(\delta)\right|\leq K.

Then

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}u(\gamma)\right\|^{2}\leq K.

8.2.
Fubini property of uniform Cesàaro limits

Another useful tool for analyzing ergodic averages is a version of Fubini’s theorem that holds for
uniform Cesàro limits
.
Given a countable discrete abelian group and a function
v
on
\Gamma
taking values in a Banach space
V
, we say that the
uniform Cesàro limit
of
(v(\gamma))_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
exists and is equal to an element
v_{0}\in V
if for every Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})
in
\Gamma
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}v(\gamma)=v_{0}.

In this case, we will write
v_{0}=\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma\in\Gamma}v(\gamma)
.

Lemma 8.2

(
[
4
, Lemma 1.1]
)
.

Let
V
be a Banach space, let
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
be countable discrete abelian groups, and let
v:\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}\to V
be bounded.
Suppose that

\text{UC-}\lim_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}}v(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})

exists and for each
\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma_{1}
,

\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma_{2}}v(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})

exists.
Then

\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma_{1}}\left(\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma_{2}}v(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\right)=\text{UC-}\lim_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}}v(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).

8.3.
Host–Kra seminorms

We now define the Host–Kra seminorms associated to an ergodic
\Gamma
-system
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X},T_{X})
.

Definition 8.3
.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X},T_{X})
be an ergodic
\Gamma
-system.
Define the sequence of
Host–Kra seminorms

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k}}
on
L^{\infty}(\mu_{X})
inductively by

•

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{1}}=\left|\int_{X}f~d\mu_{X}\right|
, and

•

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k+1}}^{2^{k+1}}=\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\overline{f}\cdot T^{\gamma}f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k}}^{2^{k}}
for
k\geq 1
.

Note that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{1}}^{2}=\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\int_{X}\overline{f}\cdot T^{\gamma}f~d\mu_{X}

by the mean ergodic theorem.
One can then check by induction (using Lemma
8.2
) that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, one has

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k}}^{2^{k}}=\text{UC-}\lim_{\bm{\gamma}\in\Gamma^{k}}\int_{X}\prod_{\bm{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{k}}T^{\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{\gamma}}C^{|\bm{\omega}|}f~d\mu_{X},

where
C
is the complex conjugation map and
|\omega|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\omega_{i}

Host and Kra
[
14
]
proved that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k}}
is a seminorm for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and there is a corresponding sequence of factors (the
Host–Kra factors

(Z_{k})_{k\geq 0}
) determined by the relation

\mathbb{E}[f\mid Z_{k-1}]=0\iff{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k}}=0.

(Strictly speaking, Host and Kra only proved their results for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions.
However, the arguments are easily adapted to abelian groups; see
[
5
, Appendix A]
for details.)
It is well known that
Z=Z_{1}
is isomorphic to the Kronecker factor of
X
, and
Z_{2}
is called its
Conze–Lesigne factor
.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X},T_{X})
be an ergodic
\Gamma
-system.
Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu_{X})
with
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
Then for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k}}^{2^{k}}\leq\left\|\mathbb{E}[f\mid Z_{k-1}]\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu_{X})}.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{f}=\mathbb{E}[f\mid Z_{k-1}]
.
Then
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f-\tilde{f}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k}}=0
, so

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k}}^{2^{k}}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\tilde{f}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k}}^{2^{k}}=\text{UC-}\lim_{\bm{\gamma}\in\Gamma^{k}}\int_{X}\prod_{\bm{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{k}}T^{\omega\cdot\bm{\gamma}}C^{|\bm{\omega}|}\tilde{f}~d\mu_{X}\\
=\int_{X}\tilde{f}\cdot\text{UC-}\lim_{\bm{\gamma}\in\Gamma^{k}}\prod_{\bm{\omega}\neq\bm{0}}T^{\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{\gamma}}C^{|\bm{\omega}|}\tilde{f}~d\mu_{X}.

The claim then follows immediately by Hölder’s inequality.
∎

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X},T_{X})
be an ergodic
\Gamma
-system.
Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu_{X})
, and let
g\in L^{\infty}(Z)
be the function defined by
g\circ\pi_{Z}=\mathbb{E}[f\mid Z]
.
Then

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{2}}=\|\hat{g}\|_{\ell^{4}(\hat{Z})}.

Proof.

Since
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f-\mathbb{E}[f\mid Z]\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{2}}=0
, it suffices to prove

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{2}}=\|\hat{g}\|_{\ell^{4}(\hat{Z})},

where we compute the
U^{2}
seminorm in the rotational
\Gamma
-system
(Z,\Sigma_{Z},\mu_{Z},T_{Z})
.
Let
\psi:\Gamma\to Z
be the homomorphism inducing the action

by
T_{Z}^{\gamma}z=z+\psi(\gamma)
.
Writing out the expression for the
U^{2}
seminorm and using unique ergodicity of

(Proposition
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{2}}^{4}

\displaystyle=\text{UC-}\lim_{(\gamma,\delta)\in\Gamma^{2}}\int_{Z}g(z)\cdot\overline{g(z+\psi(\gamma))}\cdot\overline{g(z+\psi(\delta))}\cdot g(z+\psi(\gamma)+\psi(\delta))~d\mu_{Z}

\displaystyle=\int_{Z^{3}}g(z)\cdot\overline{g(z+u)}\cdot\overline{g(z+v)}\cdot g(z+u+v)~d\mu_{Z}(z)~d\mu_{Z}(u)~d\mu_{Z}(v).

Then, expanding

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{2}}^{4}=\sum_{\chi\in\hat{Z}}|\hat{g}(\chi)|^{4}=\|\hat{g}\|_{\ell^{4}(\hat{Z})}^{4}.

∎

8.4.
A uniform Szemerédi theorem

Finally, we will need the following variant of Szemerédi’s theorem.

Theorem 8.6

(Uniform Szemerédi theorem)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0
.
Then there exists
c>0
with the following property: if
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X},T_{X})
is a measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system,
E\subseteq X
is a measurable set with
\mu_{X}(E)\geq\delta
, and
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is a finite subset of cardinality
|F|\leq k
, then

\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mu_{X}\left(\bigcap_{j\in F}T_{X}^{-j\gamma}E\right)\geq c.

Proof.

The limit
\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mu_{X}\left(\bigcap_{j\in F}T_{X}^{-j\gamma}E\right)
exists by
[
40
]
.
To obtain a lower bound, we use the main result of
[
17
]
.
By
[
17
, Theorem 1.5]
, there exist constants
c^{\prime}
and
K
depending only on
k
and
\delta
such that the following holds: if
\Gamma
is an abelian group,
T_{1},\dots,T_{k-1}
are commuting measure-preserving actions of
\Gamma
on a probability space
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X})
, and
E
is a measurable set with
\mu_{X}(E)\geq\delta
, then
\Gamma
is covered by at most
K
translates of the set

\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\mu_{X}\left(E\cap T_{1}^{-\gamma}E\cap(T_{1}T_{2})^{-\gamma}E\cap\dots\cap(T_{1}\dots T_{k-1})^{-\gamma}E\right)\geq c^{\prime}\right\}.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X},T_{X})
be a measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system, let
E\subseteq X
be a measurable set with
\mu_{X}(E)\geq\delta
, and let
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
with
|F|\leq k
.
Write
F=\{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{l}\}
with
l\leq k
.
Put
T_{i}^{\gamma}=T_{X}^{(j_{i+1}-j_{i})\gamma}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,l-1\}
and
T_{i}=\textup{id}_{X}
for
i\in\{l,\dots,k-1\}
.
Then since
T_{X}
is measure-preserving, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{X}

\displaystyle\left(E\cap T_{1}^{-\gamma}E\cap(T_{1}T_{2})^{-\gamma}E\cap\dots\cap(T_{1}\dots T_{k-1})^{-\gamma}E\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{X}\left(E\cap T_{X}^{-(j_{2}-j_{1})\gamma}\cap T_{X}^{-(j_{3}-j_{2})\gamma}T_{X}^{-(j_{2}-j_{1})\gamma}E\cap\dots\cap T_{X}^{-(j_{l}-j_{l-1})\gamma}\dots T_{X}^{-(j_{2}-j_{1})\gamma}E\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{X}\left(T_{X}^{-j_{1}\gamma}E\cap T_{X}^{-j_{2}\gamma}E\cap\dots\cap T_{X}^{-j_{l}\gamma}E\right),

so at most
K
many translates of

R_{c^{\prime}}=\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\mu_{X}\left(\bigcap_{j\in F}T_{X}^{-j\gamma}E\right)\geq c^{\prime}\right\}

are needed to cover
\Gamma
.
Therefore,

\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mu_{X}\left(\bigcap_{j\in F}T_{X}^{-j\gamma}E\right)\geq\frac{c^{\prime}}{K}.

Thus, the theorem holds by taking
c=\frac{c^{\prime}}{K}
.
∎

9.
Progressive measures

We are unable to give an explicit description of the space of Erdős progressions outside of very simple examples.
Instead, we prove existence of Erdős progessions indirectly using an appropriate measure.
Recall that the
support
of a Borel probability measure

with full measure.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
(X,T_{X})
be a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system, and let

.
A Borel probability measure

While Definition

as defined in

We explain the relationship in more detail after proving a criterion for checking that a measure

(Proposition

The utility of Definition

is expressed by the following lemma:

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
(X,T_{X})
be a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system.
Let

.
Suppose

Suppose

Erdős progressions in nilpotent translational systems

Before constructing progressive measures in general systems, we begin with a construction in degree 2 nilpotent translational systems, which turn out to play a fundamental role in the proof of Theorem

Proposition 10.1

\Gamma

9

9

By assumption, the orbit closure

A consequence for Erdős progressions in translational systems is the following:

Proposition 10.2

\Gamma

By Theorem

\Gamma

1.6

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f_{1}(T_{X}^{\gamma}a)f_{2}(T_{X}^{-\gamma}x)-\int_{Z}\tilde{f}_{1}(\pi_{Z}(a)+z)\cdot\tilde{f}_{2}(\pi_{Z}(x)-z)~d\mu_{Z}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{X})}\\
\ll\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{1/4}.

But
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, so this completes the proof.
∎

11.2.
A limit formula for triple ergodic averages

Before stating the next lemma, we need to introduce two auxiliary measures on
X^{2}
.
First, we define a measure
\nu
on
X^{2}
by

\nu(E)=\sigma^{(4)}_{a}(E\times X)

for Borel subsets
E\subseteq X^{2}
.
That is,
\nu
is the projection of
\sigma^{(4)}_{a}
onto the first two coordinates.

The second measure we will define is a measure previously studied by Kra–Moreira–Richter–Robertson
[
20
]
and Charamaras–Mountakis
[
8
]
in the context of the Erdős
B+B+t
problem (over
\mathbb{Z}
and abelian groups, respectively).
Let

\mu_{X}=\int_{Z}\zeta_{z}~d\mu_{Z}

be a disintegration of
\mu_{X}
with respect to the factor map
\pi_{Z}:X\to Z
, and let

\tilde{\sigma}^{(3)}_{a}=\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\delta_{T_{Z}^{\gamma}\pi_{Z}(a)}\times\delta_{T_{Z}^{2\gamma}\pi_{Z}(a)}=\int_{Z}\delta_{\pi_{Z}(a)+t}\times\delta_{\pi_{Z}(a)+2t}~d\mu_{Z}(t).

Then we define
\sigma^{(3)}_{a}
on
X^{2}
by

\sigma^{(3)}_{a}=\int_{Z^{2}}\zeta_{u}\times\zeta_{v}~d\tilde{\sigma}^{(3)}_{a}(u,v)=\int_{Z}\zeta_{\pi_{Z}(a)+t}\times\zeta_{\pi_{Z}(a)+2t}~d\mu_{Z}(t).

Though we will not use this fact directly, it was shown in
[
8
, Theorems 4.9 and 4.10]
that
\sigma^{(3)}_{a}
is 3-progressive from
a
.

The measures
\nu
and
\sigma^{(3)}_{a}
are closely related and often coincide, but they may differ for certain choices of the point
a
, as demonstrated by the following example.

Example 11.3
.

This example was communicated to us by Tristán Radić.
We consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-action on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
given by
T(x,y)=(x+\alpha,y+2x+\alpha)
for an irrational number
\alpha
.
It can be checked that
(\mathbb{T}^{2},T)
is a minimal uniquely ergodic topological dynamical system, and
(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mu_{\mathbb{T}^{2}},T)
is a measure-preserving system of order 2.
Let
a=(0,0)
.
Then the orbit
(T^{n}a,T^{2n}a)
can be computed explicitly as

(T^{n}a,T^{2n}a)=(n\alpha,n^{2}\alpha,2n\alpha,4n^{2}\alpha),

which equidistributes in the subtorus
H=\{(z,t,2z,4t):z,t\in\mathbb{T}\}
by Weyl’s equidistribution theorem.
Thus,
\nu
is the Haar measure on
H
, while
\sigma^{(3)}_{a}
is the Haar measure on the
3
-dimensional subtorus
\{(z,u,2z,v):z,u,v\in\mathbb{T}\}
.

Even allowing for the possibility that
\nu
and
\sigma^{(3)}_{a}
differ, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 11.4
.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}(\mu)
, and suppose
g
is measurable with respect to the Kronecker factor
Z
.
Then

\int_{X^{2}}f\otimes g~d\nu=\int_{X^{2}}f\otimes g~d\sigma^{(3)}_{a}.

Proof.

By construction,

\int_{X^{2}}\mathbb{E}[f\mid Z]\otimes g~d\nu=\int_{X^{2}}f\otimes g~d\sigma^{(3)}_{a}.

Thus, decomposing
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
with
f_{1}=\mathbb{E}[f\mid Z]
and
f_{2}=f-\mathbb{E}[f\mid Z]
, we have

\int_{X^{2}}f\otimes g~d\nu=\int_{X^{2}}f_{1}\otimes g~d\nu+\int_{X^{2}}f_{2}\otimes g~d\nu=\int_{X^{2}}f\otimes g~d\sigma^{(3)}_{a}+\int_{X^{2}}f_{2}\otimes g~d\nu.

Therefore, it suffices to prove
\int_{X^{2}}f_{2}\otimes g~d\nu=0
.

We use a similar argument to the one appearing in
[
22
, Lemma 6.11]
.
Define a measure
\lambda
on
X\times Z
by

\int_{X\times Z}h\otimes k~d\lambda=\int_{X^{2}}h\otimes(k\circ\pi_{Z})~d\nu

for
h\in C(X)
and
k\in C(Z)
.
Since
g
is assumed to be measurable with respect to
Z
, we can write
g=\tilde{g}\circ\pi_{Z}
for some
\tilde{g}\in L^{2}(\mu_{Z})
, whence

\int_{X^{2}}f_{2}\otimes g~d\nu=\int_{X\times Z}f_{2}\otimes\tilde{g}~d\lambda.

Note that
\lambda
is a joining of
(X,\mu_{X},T)
and
(Z,\mu_{Z},T_{Z}^{2})
.
Hence, the function
\mathbbm{1}\otimes\tilde{g}
is measurable with respect to the Kronecker factor of
(X\times Z,\lambda,T\times T_{Z}^{2})
, while
f_{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}
satisfies
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f_{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{2}}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f_{2}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{2}}=0
, so
(\mathbbm{1}\otimes g)
and
(f_{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1})
are orthogonal in
L^{2}(\lambda)
.
We conclude that

\int_{X^{2}}f_{2}\otimes g~d\nu=\int_{X\times Z}f_{2}\otimes\tilde{g}~d\lambda=\int_{X\times Z}(f_{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1})(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\tilde{g})~d\lambda=0.

∎

Lemma 11.5
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in C(X)
.
Then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f_{1}(T_{X}^{\gamma}a)f_{2}(T_{X}^{\gamma}x_{1})f_{3}(T_{X}^{\gamma}x_{2})=\int_{Z_{2}^{3}}\tilde{f}_{1}\otimes\tilde{f}_{2}\otimes\tilde{f}_{3}~d\mu_{\mathcal{O}(\pi_{Z_{2}}(a),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{1}),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{2}))}

in

, where
\tilde{f}_{i}\circ\pi_{Z_{2}}=\mathbb{E}[f_{i}\mid Z_{2}]
and
\mu_{\mathcal{O}(\pi_{Z_{2}}(a),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{1}),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{2}))}
is the unique diagonally-invariant measure on the orbit closure

\mathcal{O}(\pi_{Z_{2}}(a),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{1}),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{2}))=\overline{\left\{\left(T_{Z_{2}}^{\gamma}\pi_{Z_{2}}(a),T_{Z_{2}}^{\gamma}\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{1}),T_{Z_{2}}^{\gamma}\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{2})\right):\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}}\subseteq Z_{2}^{3}.

Remark 11.6
.

The same limit in
L^{2}(\mu_{X}\times\mu_{X})
is controlled by the Kronecker factor.
The measure
\nu
is supported on points
(x_{1},x_{2})
such that
(a,x_{1},x_{2})
projects to a 3-term arithmetic progression in the Kronecker factor, and this correlation between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
in the Kronecker factor introduces higher-level correlations, leading to the limit in Lemma

being controlled by the second order Host–Kra factor.

Proof.

By rescaling, we may assume
|f_{i}|\leq 1
.
Let
u(\gamma)=f_{1}(T_{X}^{\gamma}a)\cdot T_{X}^{\gamma}f_{2}\otimes T_{X}^{\gamma}f_{3}\in L^{2}(\nu)
.
Then since the measure
\nu
is
T_{X}\times T_{X}^{2}
invariant,

\displaystyle\left\langle u(\gamma+\delta),u(\gamma)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\overline{f_{1}(T_{X}^{\gamma}a)}f_{1}(T_{X}^{\gamma+\delta}a)\int_{X^{2}}T_{X}^{\gamma}\left(\overline{f_{2}}\cdot T_{X}^{\delta}f_{2}\right)\otimes T_{X}^{\gamma}\left(\overline{f_{3}}\cdot T_{X}^{\delta}f_{3}\right)~d\nu

\displaystyle=\overline{f_{1}(T_{X}^{\gamma}a)}f_{1}(T_{X}^{\gamma+\delta}a)\int_{X^{2}}\left(\overline{f_{2}}\cdot T_{X}^{\delta}f_{2}\right)\otimes T_{X}^{-\gamma}\left(\overline{f_{3}}\cdot T_{X}^{\delta}f_{3}\right)~d\nu.

We will use Lemma
11.2
to average this expression over
\gamma
.
First we set up some notation: for each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, let
k_{i}\in L^{\infty}(Z)
be the function satisfying
k_{i}\circ\pi_{Z}=\mathbb{E}[\overline{f_{i}}\cdot T_{X}^{\delta}f_{i}\mid Z]
.
The second marginal of
\nu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu_{X}
by Theorem
10.3
, so applying Lemma
11.2
,

\displaystyle z(\delta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}\left\langle u(\gamma+\delta),u(\gamma)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{X^{2}}\left(\overline{f_{2}}\cdot T_{X}^{\delta}f_{2}\right)(x_{1})\int_{Z}k_{1}(\pi_{Z}(a)+z)\cdot k_{3}(\pi_{Z}(x_{2})-z)~dz~d\nu(x_{1},x_{2}).

The function

x_{2}\mapsto\int_{Z}k_{1}(\pi_{Z}(a)+z)\cdot k_{3}(\pi_{Z}(x_{2})-z)~dz

is measurable with respect to the Kronecker factor
Z
.
Therefore, by Lemma
11.4
,

\displaystyle z(\delta)

\displaystyle=\int_{X^{2}}\left(\overline{f_{2}}\cdot T_{X}^{\delta}f_{2}\right)(x_{1})\int_{Z}k_{1}(\pi_{Z}(a)+z)\cdot k_{3}(\pi_{Z}(x_{2})-z)~d\mu_{Z}~d\sigma^{(3)}_{a}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{Z^{2}}k_{1}(\pi_{Z}(a)+z)\cdot k_{2}(\pi_{Z}(a)+t)\cdot k_{3}(\pi_{Z}(a)+2t-z)~d\mu_{Z}(z)~d\mu_{Z}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{Z^{2}}k_{1}(u)\cdot k_{2}(u+v)\cdot k_{3}(u+2v)~d\mu_{Z}(u)~d\mu_{Z}(v),

where in the last step we made a change of variables
u=\pi_{Z}(a)+z
,
v=t-z
.

Expanding each
k_{i}
as a Fourier series and using orthogonality of characters,

z(\delta)=\sum_{\chi\in\hat{Z}}\hat{k}_{1}(\chi)\hat{k}_{2}(\chi^{-2})\hat{k}_{3}(\chi).

By Hölder’s inequality, Parseval’s identity, and Lemma
8.5
, we may bound

|z(\delta)|\ll\|\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\|_{\ell^{4}(\hat{Z})}\cdot\|\hat{k}_{i_{2}}\|_{\ell^{2}(\hat{Z})}\cdot\|\hat{k}_{i_{3}}\|_{\ell^{2}(\hat{Z})}=\|\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\|_{\ell^{4}(\hat{Z})}\cdot\|k_{i_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{X})}\cdot\|k_{i_{3}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{X})}\\

for any permutation
(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})
of
(1,2,3)
.
Note that this bound only holds up to a constant, since the Fourier coefficient
\hat{k}_{2}(\xi)
may contribute to

with multiplicity

\left|\left\{\chi\in\hat{Z}:\chi^{2}=\xi\right\}\right|\leq[Z:2Z]\leq[\Gamma:2\Gamma].

We thus conclude

|z(\delta)|\ll\min_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\overline{f}_{i}\cdot T_{X}^{\delta}f_{i}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{2}}.

Hence, for any Følner sequence
(\Psi_{M})
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Psi_{M}|}\left|\sum_{\delta\in\Psi_{M}}z(\delta)\right|

\displaystyle\ll\min_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}\limsup_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Psi_{M}|}\sum_{\delta\in\Psi_{M}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\overline{f}_{i}\cdot T_{X}^{\delta}f_{i}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}\limsup_{M\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{|\Psi_{M}|}\sum_{\delta\in\Psi_{M}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\overline{f}_{i}\cdot T_{X}^{\delta}f_{i}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\min_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f_{i}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{3}}^{2}.

By Lemma
8.1
, it follows that

(11.2)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f_{1}(T_{X}^{\gamma}a)\cdot T_{X}^{\gamma}f_{2}\otimes T_{X}^{\gamma}f_{3}\right\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\ll\min_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f_{i}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{3}}.

To finish the proof, we use the same strategy as in Lemma
11.2
.
Decomposing
f_{i}=g_{i}\circ\pi+h_{i}
with
g_{i}\in C(Z_{2})
and
h_{i}\in C(X)
with
g_{i}
approximating
\mathbb{E}[f_{i}\mid Z_{2}]
in

, the inequality (
11.2
) combined with Lemma
8.4
shows that the terms involving
h_{i}
are negligible.
The original average can therefore be approximated by the corresponding average for the functions
g_{i}
, which in turn can be computed pointwise by Theorem
1.5
.
∎

11.3.
A limit formula for averages against
\sigma^{(4)}_{a}

Lemma 11.7
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in C(X)
.
Then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f_{1}(T_{X}^{\gamma}a)\int_{X^{3}}f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(T_{X}^{\gamma}x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})f_{3}(T_{X}^{\gamma}x_{2})f_{3}(x_{3})~d\sigma^{(4)}_{a}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\\
=\int_{X^{3}}f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})f_{3}(x_{3})\cdot\\
\left(\int_{Z_{2}^{3}}\tilde{f}_{1}\otimes\tilde{f}_{2}\otimes\tilde{f}_{3}~d\mu_{\mathcal{O}(\pi_{Z_{2}}(a),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{1}),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{2}))}\right)~d\sigma^{(4)}_{a}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}).

Proof.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f_{1}(T_{X}^{\gamma}a)\int_{X^{3}}f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(T_{X}^{\gamma}x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})f_{3}(T_{X}^{\gamma}x_{2})f_{3}(x_{3})~d\sigma^{(4)}_{a}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\\
-\int_{X^{3}}f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})f_{3}(x_{3})\cdot\\
\left(\int_{Z_{2}^{3}}\tilde{f}_{1}\otimes\tilde{f}_{2}\otimes\tilde{f}_{3}~d\mu_{\mathcal{O}(\pi_{Z_{2}}(a),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{1}),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{2}))}\right)~d\sigma^{(4)}_{a}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\\
=\int_{X^{3}}f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})f_{3}(x_{3})\mathcal{E}_{N}(x_{1},x_{2})~d\sigma^{(4)}_{a}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),

where

\mathcal{E}_{N}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f_{1}(T_{X}^{\gamma}a)f_{2}(T_{X}^{\gamma}x_{1})f_{3}(T_{X}^{\gamma}x_{2})\\
-\int_{Z_{2}^{3}}\tilde{f}_{1}\otimes\tilde{f}_{2}\otimes\tilde{f}_{3}~d\mu_{\mathcal{O}(\pi_{Z_{2}}(a),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{1}),\pi_{Z_{2}}(x_{2}))}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X^{3}}f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})f_{3}(x_{3})\mathcal{E}_{N}(x_{1},x_{2})~d\sigma^{(4)}_{a}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{3}\|_{\infty}\left|\int_{X^{2}}(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})\mathcal{E}_{N}~d\nu\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{3}\|_{\infty}\|f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\|\mathcal{E}_{N}\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}.

By Lemma
11.5
, we have
\|\mathcal{E}_{N}\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\to 0
, and this completes the proof.
∎

11.4.
A version of Szemerédi’s theorem

The final ingredient is the following version of Szemerédi’s theorem.

Theorem 11.8
.

Suppose
B\subseteq\Gamma
satisfies
d^{*}(B)=\delta>0
.
Then for any finite set
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
,

d^{*}\left(\left\{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\Gamma^{2}:\{\gamma_{1}+k\gamma_{2}:k\in F\}\subseteq B\right\}\right)\gg_{\delta,|F|}1.

Proof.

Let
R=\left\{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\Gamma^{2}:\{\gamma_{1}+k\gamma_{2}\}\subseteq B\right\}
.

By the Furstenberg correspondence principle, let
(Y,T_{Y})
be a topological dynamical
\Gamma
-system,
\mu_{Y}
an ergodic
T_{Y}
-invariant measure,
\Psi=(\Psi_{N})
a Følner sequence,
b\in\text{gen}(\mu_{Y},\Psi)
, and
E\subseteq Y
clopen such that
B=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:T_{Y}^{\gamma}b\in E\}
and
\mu_{Y}(E)\geq\delta
.
By Theorem
8.6
,

\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mu_{Y}\left(\bigcap_{k\in F}T_{Y}^{-k\gamma}E\right)\gg_{\delta,|F|}1.

Note that

\displaystyle\mu_{Y}\left(\bigcap_{k\in F}T_{Y}^{-k\gamma_{2}}E\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Psi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma_{1}\in\Psi_{N}}\delta_{T_{Y}^{\gamma_{1}}b}\left(\bigcap_{k\in F}T_{Y}^{-k\gamma_{2}}E\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Psi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma_{1}\in\Psi_{N}}\prod_{k\in F}\mathbbm{1}_{B}(\gamma_{1}+k\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Psi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma_{1}\in\Psi_{N}}\mathbbm{1}_{R}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).

Thus,

d^{*}(R)\geq\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma}\left(\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Psi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma_{1}\in\Psi_{N}}\mathbbm{1}_{R}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\right)\gg_{\delta,|F|}1.

∎

\text{UC-}\lim_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\Gamma^{2}}u_{1}(\gamma_{1})u_{2}(2\gamma_{1})u_{3}(3\gamma_{1})u_{1}(\gamma_{2})u_{2}(\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{1})u_{3}(\gamma_{2}+2\gamma_{1})\\
\geq\frac{1}{[\Gamma:6\Gamma]}\text{UC-}\lim_{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\Gamma^{2}}u_{1}(\gamma_{1})u_{2}(2\gamma_{1})u_{3}(3\gamma_{1})\\

Putting everything together,

.
Hence, letting

, we have
I(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})\gg_{c}1

) holds.
∎

Completing the proof

We now have all of the ingredients to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
7.3
.

By passing to an extension if needed, we may assume without loss of generality that all of the conditions at the start of Section

are satisfied.
In the context of
\mathbb{Z}
-systems, this was shown in

.
We carry out the necessary modifications for general
\Gamma
-systems in the appendix; see Theorem

Now by Theorem
11.1
, the measure
\sigma^{(4)}_{T_{X}^{t}a}
is 4-progressive from
T_{X}^{t}a
for every
t\in\Gamma
.
Fix a Følner sequence
(\Psi_{N})
in
\Gamma
.
We claim that

(12.1)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Psi_{N}|}\sum_{t\in\Psi_{N}}\sigma^{(4)}_{T_{X}^{t}a}(E\times E\times E)>0.

To see this, let
\varepsilon>0
.

From the definition of

\sigma^{(4)}_{T_{X}^{t}a}(E\times E\times E)\geq\int_{X^{3}}f\otimes f\otimes f~d\sigma^{(4)}_{T_{X}^{t}a}\\

Appendix A
Conze–Lesigne systems as topological inverse limits of translational systems

Throughout this appendix, we fix an arbitrary countable discrete abelian group
\Gamma
.
The main result of
[
18
]
says that ergodic Conze–Lesigne
\Gamma
-systems are isomorphic to inverse limits of degree 2 nilpotent translational
\Gamma
-systems in the category of measure-preserving
\Gamma
-systems (see Theorem
1.4
).
The goal of this appendix is to realize this inverse limit within the category of topological dynamical
\Gamma
-systems.
First we need a lemma allowing us to replace measurable factor maps with topological factor maps.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\pi\colon(G/\Lambda,\Sigma_{G/\Lambda},\mu_{G/\Lambda},T_{G/\Lambda})\to(G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime},\Sigma_{G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}},\mu_{G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}},T_{G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}})
be a measurable factor map of ergodic degree 2 nilpotent translational
\Gamma
-systems.
Then there is a degree 2 nilpotent translational
\Gamma
-system
(\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda},\Sigma_{\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}},\mu_{\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}},T_{\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}})
with the following properties.

(1)

There is a measurable isomorphism
\iota:\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}\to G/\Lambda
.

(2)

There is a topological factor map
\tilde{\pi}:\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}\to G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}
such that
\tilde{\pi}=\pi\circ\iota

\mu_{\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}}
-almost surely.

Proof.

We essentially follow the arguments in
[
21
, Proposition 3.20]
and
[
8
, Proposition 3.7]
to obtain a measurably isomorphic extension of
G/\Lambda
that has a continuous factor map to
G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}
.
The new observation is that the extension system is still a translational system.

Fix a point
x_{0}\in G/\Lambda
.
Then
x_{0}
is a transitive point, since ergodic translational systems are minimal (see Corollary
4.2
).
Moreover, the system
(G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime},T_{G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}})
is distal by Proposition
2.9
.
Hence, by
[
8
, Lemma 3.5]
, there exists a point
y_{0}\in G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}
and a Følner sequence
(\Psi_{N})
such that for any
f_{1}\in C(G/\Lambda)
and
f_{2}\in C(G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime})
, we have

(A.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Psi_{N}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi_{N}}f_{1}(T_{G/\Lambda}^{\gamma}x_{0})f_{2}(T_{G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}}^{\gamma}y_{0})=\int_{G/\Lambda}f_{1}\cdot(f_{2}\circ\pi)~d\mu_{G/\Lambda}.

Let
H=G\times G^{\prime}
and
\Delta=\Lambda\times\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Let
T_{H/\Delta}^{\gamma}=T_{G/\Lambda}^{\gamma}\times T_{G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}}^{\gamma}
for
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
Then
(H/\Delta,T_{H/\Delta})
is a degree 2 nilpotent translational system, so by Theorem
1.5
, there exists a closed subgroup
\tilde{G}\subseteq H
such that the orbit of the point
z_{0}=(x_{0},y_{0})\in H/\Delta
is well-distributed in
\tilde{G}z_{0}
.
That is,

\text{UC-}\lim_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f_{1}(T_{G/\Lambda}^{\gamma}x_{0})f_{2}(T_{G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}}^{\gamma}y_{0})=\int_{\tilde{G}z_{0}}f_{1}\otimes f_{2}~d\mu_{\tilde{G}z_{0}}

for
f_{1}\in C(G/\Lambda)
and
f_{2}\in C(G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime})
.
Comparing with (
A.1
), we have

(A.2)

\int_{\tilde{G}z_{0}}f_{1}\otimes f_{2}~d\mu_{\tilde{G}z_{0}}=\int_{G/\Lambda}f_{1}\cdot(f_{2}\circ\pi)~d\mu_{G/\Lambda}

for
f_{1}\in C(G/\Lambda)
and
f_{2}\in C(G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime})
.
Let
\tilde{\Lambda}=\tilde{G}\cap z_{0}\Delta z_{0}^{-1}
be the stabilizer of the point
z_{0}
for the action of
\tilde{G}
on
H/\Delta
.
Then the map
\xi:h\tilde{\Lambda}\mapsto hz_{0}
provides a (topological) isomorphism between the degree 2 nilpotent translational system
(\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda},T_{\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}})
and the topological dynamical system
(\tilde{G}z_{0},T_{\tilde{G}z_{0}})
.

Let
\rho:G/\Lambda\to H/\Delta
be the map
\rho(x)=(x,\pi(x))
.
Given continuous functions
f_{1}\in C(G/\Lambda)
and
f_{2}\in C(G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime})
, we have

\int_{G/\Lambda}(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})\circ\rho~d\mu_{G/\Lambda}=\int_{G/\Lambda}f_{1}\cdot(f_{2}\circ\pi)~d\mu_{G/\Lambda},

so by (
A.2
),

\int_{G/\Lambda}(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})\circ\rho~d\mu_{G/\Lambda}=\int_{\tilde{G}z_{0}}f_{1}\otimes f_{2}~d\mu_{\tilde{G}z_{0}}.

In other words,
\rho
establishes an isomorphism between
(G/\Lambda,\Sigma_{G/\Lambda},\mu_{G/\Lambda},T_{G/\Lambda})
and
(H/\Delta,\Sigma_{H/\Delta},\mu_{\tilde{G}z_{0}},T_{H/\Delta})
as measure-preserving systems.
In particular,
\rho(x)\in\tilde{G}_{z_{0}}
for almost every
x\in G/\Lambda
, so the map
\xi^{-1}\circ\rho:G/\Lambda\to\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}
is defined almost everywhere and induces an isomorphism of measure-preserving systems.

Let
\pi_{1}:H/\Delta\to G/\Lambda
and
\pi_{2}:H/\Delta\to G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}
be the coordinate projection maps.
Note that the continuous surjection
\iota=\pi_{1}\circ\xi
is a left-inverse to
\rho
.

Now define
\tilde{\pi}:\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}\to G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}
by
\tilde{\pi}=\pi_{2}\circ\xi
.
Then
\tilde{\pi}
, being the composition of two continuous surjective maps, is a continuous surjection.
Moreover, for
z\in\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

\tilde{\pi}(T_{\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}}z)=\pi_{2}(T_{H/\Delta}\xi(z))=T_{G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}}\pi_{2}(\xi(z))=T_{G^{\prime}/\Lambda^{\prime}}\tilde{\pi}(z),

so
\tilde{\pi}
is a topological factor map.

Finally, for almost every
z\in\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Lambda}
, we may write
z=\xi^{-1}(\rho(x))
for some
x\in G/\Lambda
, whence

\pi(\iota(z))=\pi(\pi_{1}(\xi(z)))=\pi(\pi_{1}(\rho(x)))=\pi(x)=\pi_{2}(\rho(x))=\pi_{2}(\xi(\rho(z))=\tilde{\pi}(z).

∎

(2)

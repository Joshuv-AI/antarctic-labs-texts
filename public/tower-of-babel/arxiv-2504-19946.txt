Flat degenerations of flag supermanifolds for basic Lie superalgebras
Ibrahim Ahmad

Abstract
. Motivated by bases of representations compatible with the PBW filtration for basic Lie superalgebras by Kus and Fourier, we generalise the construction of degenerations of flag varieties via favourable modules to the super setup. In the classical setup, this method of degenerating flag varieties by Feigin, Fourier and, Littelmann relies on constructing bases of representations of Lie algebras such that in the coordinate ring of an embedded flag varietiy their multiplication can be identified with an affine semigroup modulo terms of higher degree. By killing off said terms of higher degree via a filtration construction, one gets a toric variety the embedded flag variety degenerates into. By adapting these techniques we provide a similar construction and discuss when one can get a degeneration into a toric supervariety, as defined by Jankowski.



1.
Introduction

Degeneration methods have proven to be of great aid when verifying questions on specific properties of varieties. With a degeneration of a variety
X
, we mean a flat family over, say,
\mathbb{A}^{1}
such that the general fibre over
t\neq 0
is isomorphic to our initial variety
X
. The special fibre over
t=0
is isomorphic to a, hopefully, less complicated variety, call it
Y
. By imposing flatness, one can then verify several open properties for
X
by studying such properties for
Y
instead. Among these are notions like dimensions, Cohen-Macauleyness, smoothness, and normality. For a condensed list of some open properties, see
[
GW20
, Appendix E]

Those degenerations with the special fibre being a toric variety are particularly interesting. One of the most prominent methods to systematically construct such degenerations of embedded projective varieties have been the notions of Newton-Okounkov bodies
[
KK12
]
as well as degenerations into semitoric varieties via Seshadri Stratifications by Chirivì, Fang and, Littelmann
[
CFL23
]
.

For varieties arising from representation theory, there have also been many other methods of constructing these degenerations like
[
Cal02
,
AB04
,
GL96
]
by considering bases of coordinate rings with respect to an embedding that multiply in a controlled manner, some refer to as a straightening law for example in
[
CFL23
]
. Among these methods are the notions of favourable modules by Feigin, Fourier and Littelmann
[
FFL17a
]
as well as their generalisation of birational sequences
[
FFL17
]
. The latter is also implemented in the computer algebra system OSCAR
[
FF25
]
.

In
[
FK21
]
, Kus and Fourier construct bases of certain representations of Lie Superalgebras and propose these could provide a similar construction in a supergeometric setting. Supergeometry can be regarded as an extension of algebraic geometry in the sense that the sheaf of commutative rings, or algebras, is being replaced by a sheaf of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebras, referred to as superalgebras, obeying a signed version of commutativity incorporating commuting as well as anti-commuting variables. Within this context, one can also define Lie superalgebras as being
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded spaces with commutators satisfying signed versions of axioms for Lie algebras. These objects enjoy rising popularity due to their applications in theoretical physics but prove themselves to lie deeper in pure mathematics than initially anticipated.

In this paper, we will lift the degeneration via favourable modules of Feigin-Fourier-Littelmann
[
FFL17a
]
to the context of flag supermanifolds for basic Lie superalgebras. We briefly recall their result:

For a semisimple, simply connected complex algebraic group
G
with corresponding Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we consider a regular weight
\lambda\in P^{++}
. Then, we can embed the flag variety into the projectivized highest weight space
\mathbb{P}(V(\lambda))
with the coordinate ring being the graded algebra

R(\lambda):=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}V(k\lambda)^{*}.

The multiplication is the dual of the Cartan embedding which is the following homomorphism of representations of
\mathfrak{g}
sending the highest weight vector of
V((m+n)\lambda)
to the tensor of the highest weight vectors of
V(m\lambda)
and
V(n\lambda)

\displaystyle V((m+n)\lambda)

\displaystyle\rightarrow V(m\lambda)\otimes V(n\lambda)

\displaystyle v_{(m+n)\lambda}

\displaystyle\mapsto v_{m\lambda}\otimes v_{n\lambda}

Fix a triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}
, an ordered basis
(f_{1},\dots,f_{N})
of
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
and a monomial order on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Then, Feigin, Fourier and Littelmann define a monomial
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
to be
essential
if

f^{\mathbf{m}}v_{\lambda}=f_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots f_{N}^{m_{N}}v_{\lambda}\not\in\mathrm{span}\langle f^{\mathbf{n}}v_{\lambda}\mid\mathbf{n}<\mathbf{m}\rangle

Denote the set of essential monomials for
V(\lambda)
by
\mathrm{es}(\lambda,<)
. Then it is the case that if
\mathbf{m}\in\mathrm{es}(m\lambda,<)
and
\mathbf{n}\in\mathrm{es}(n\lambda,<)
, then
\mathbf{m+n}\in\mathrm{es}((m+n)\lambda,<)
, i.e. they have a semigroup structure. If the semigroup

\Gamma(\lambda,<)=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathrm{es}(k\lambda,<)\times\{k\}

is generated by
\mathrm{es}(\lambda,<)\times\{1\}
, then they call the module
V(\lambda)

favourable
and get a degeneration of
R(\lambda)
into the semigroup algebra of
\Gamma(\lambda,<)
corresponding to a toric variety.
Prominent examples of bases yielding such a degeneration are those given by lattice points the FFLV polytopes for
\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}

[
FFL11
]
and
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}

[
FFL11a
]
.

Kus and Fourier constructed in
[
FK21
]
bases of representations of Lie superalgebras parametrised by lattice points of polytopes, similar to the FFLV polytope. In their paper, they raised the question if a degeneration construction similar to the one via favourable modules would work which is what we are going to address in this paper.

We consider for a basic Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a dominant integral weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{+}
the superalgebra

R(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}K_{\mathfrak{b}}(k\lambda)^{*},

where
K_{\mathfrak{b}}(k\lambda)
is referring to the finite-dimensional quotient of the Verma module
M_{\mathfrak{b}}(\lambda)
and multiplication being again the dual of the morphism sending the highest weight vector to the tensor of highest weight vectors.
This algebra can also be interpreted as the section ring of the flag supermanifold
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{B}
for an algebraic supergroup
\mathcal{G}
and Borel subsupergroup
\mathcal{B}
with corresponding Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{b}
. Namely we consider the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
for the weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}

[
Ser17
, Corollary 9]
and have

R(\lambda)\cong\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L}_{k\lambda})

We have the following result

Theorem 1
.

(Corollary
7.1
)
There exists a morphism of superschemes
\kappa:\mathrm{Spec}\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1|0}
that is flat and the fibers are

\displaystyle\kappa^{-1}(a)\cong\begin{cases}\mathrm{Spec}\,R(\lambda),\hskip 10.00002pta\neq 0\\
\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{C}[\xi^{I}x^{\mathbf{m}}v|(I,\mathbf{m})\in\mathrm{es}(K_{\mathfrak{b}}(\lambda),<)],\hskip 10.00002pta=0\end{cases}

Further, we also have that in the special cases of those bases given by Kus and Fourier in
[
FK21
]
one gets a toric supervariety as defined by Jankowski in
[
Jan25
]
.

Theorem 2
.

(Corollary
7.11
)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie superalgebra of type I,
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
or one of the basic exceptionals. Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be a dominant integral weight that is typical such that
k\lambda
is also typical for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Denote by
P(\lambda)
the corresponding lattice polytope from
[
FK21
]
and
S(\lambda)
its lattice points.
Then the superscheme
\mathrm{Spec}\,A
for

A:=\mathbb{C}[\xi^{I}x^{\mathbf{m}}v|(I,\mathbf{m})\in S(\lambda)]

is a faithful toric supervariety.

We are going to proceed as follows. In section
2
, we recall some basic notions of Lie superalgebras. In section
3
, we define the superalgebra we are going to deal with. In section
4
, we inspect the multiplication of this superalgbera more closely and retrieve a straightening law. Section
5
is devoted to the notion of a favourable module and retrieving an ideal representing it. In section
6
, we are going to construct the flat family. Lastly, section
7
states the result in a more geometric setting and provides a criterion for the special superalgebra generated by monomials to correspond to an affine toric supervariety and what possible torus actions are.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we are going to recall the notions on Lie superalgebras, which can be found in
[
CCF11
,
CW12
,
Ser17
]
.

A
supervector space
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
V=V_{\overline{0}}\oplus V_{\overline{1}}
.
We refer to its
dimension
as the tuple
\dim V:=\dim V_{\overline{0}}|\dim V_{\overline{1}}
.
For a homogeneous element
v\in V_{i}
we refer to
|v|=i\in\{\overline{0},\overline{1}\}
as its
parity
. We call a linear map between supervector spaces
A:V\rightarrow W

even
if it preserves the grading. If it flips the grading, then we call it
odd
. Hence for supervector spaces
V,W
we have that the set of of linear maps
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V,W)
is itself a supervector space. The dual space
V^{*}
becomes a superspace by setting
V^{*}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V,\mathbb{C}^{1|0}:=\mathbb{C}\oplus\{0\})
.

A
superalgebra
is a supervector space
A=A_{\overline{0}}\oplus A_{\overline{1}}
such that the multiplication map is even, i.e.
A_{\overline{i}}A_{\overline{j}}\subseteq A_{\overline{i+j}}
. We call it
commutative
if further

ab=(-1)^{|a||b|}ba

for homogenous elements
a,b\in A
.

We call a supervector space
\mathfrak{g}
a
Lie superalgebra
if there exists a bilinear even map
[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that for all homogeneous
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}

(1)

[x,y]=-(-1)^{|x|\cdot|y|}[y,x]

(2)

[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+(-1)^{|x|\cdot|y|}[y,[x,z]]

We call
\mathfrak{g}

simple
if it has no non-trivial
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded ideal. We further call it
basic
if it is simple,
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
is a reductive Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{g}
admits a non-degenerate invariant bilinear
(\cdot,\cdot)
, i.e.

([x,y],z)=(x,[y,z]),\hskip 10.00002pt(x,y)=(-1)^{|x|\cdot|y|}(y,x),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for homogenous }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}

as well as

(x,y)=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for $x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}$, $y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}$}

Kac has classified basic Lie superalgebras.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Kac77
]
)
.

The basic Lie superalgebras over
\mathbb{C}
that are not purely even Lie algebras are

(1)

\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)
,
n>m\geq 1
;

(2)

\mathfrak{psl}(n|n)
,
n\geq 2
;

(3)

\mathfrak{osp}(m|2n)

m,n\geq 1

(m,n)\neq(2,1),(4,1)
;

(4)

D(2,1;\alpha)
,
\alpha\neq 0,1
;

(5)

F(4)
;

(6)

G(3)
.

The Lie superalgebras,
\mathfrak{sl}(n|m)
,
\mathfrak{psl}(n|n)
,
\mathfrak{\mathfrak{osp}}(2|2n)
are referred to as type I. The other members of this list are referred to as type II.

Remark 2.2
.

Some pieces of literature also declare
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
to be basic and then of type I, see
[
CW12
]
.

Next, we want to state the notions of roots and Borel subalgebras for Lie superalgebras.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a basic Lie superalgebra. We define a
Cartan subalgebra

\mathfrak{h}
to be a Cartan subalgebra of the even part
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
.

For a given
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we define the root space

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=\left\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\,\middle|\,[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\,\text{ for all }h\in\mathfrak{h}\right\}.

We call
\alpha
a
root
if
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\neq\{0\}
and denote the set of roots by
R
.
This yields us the decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

For basic Lie superalgebras, we have that
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=1|0
or
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=0|1

[
CW12
, Theorem 1.8]
, hence allowing us to define the set of even and odd roots via

R_{\overline{0}}=\left\{\alpha\in R\,\middle|\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\neq\{0\}\right\},\hskip 10.00002ptR_{\overline{1}}=\left\{\alpha\in R\,\middle|\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}\neq\{0\}\right\}.

This, in turn, leads us to the notions of positive systems and Borel subalgebras.

Let
E
be the real space spanned by the roots
R
. For a fixed total order
\geq
on
E
that is compatible with the real vector space structure on
E
, we define the
positive system

R^{+}
as the set of all roots
\alpha\in R
such that
\alpha>0
. The elements of
R^{+}
are referred to as
positive roots
. For such a positive system, we define the subset
\Delta\subseteq R^{+}
of
simple roots
as those that cannot be written as a sum of two positive roots.
Similarly, we also define the
negative roots
to be the set
R^{-}
of all roots
\alpha\in R
such that
\alpha<0
.

For a given positive system
R^{+}\subseteq R
, we define the
Borel subalgebra

\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=:\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}

In turn, we also get the triangular decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R^{-}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{b}

Remark 2.3
.

Similarly to the even case, one can define a Weyl group and its action on Borel subalgebras. However, it is not the case that all Borel subalgebras are conjugate under the Weyl group action, see
[
CW12
, Section 1.3 and 1.4]

We can form for
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
the
Verma module
with respect to a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
via

M_{\mathfrak{b}}(\lambda):=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b})}C_{\lambda},

where
C_{\lambda}
is the one-dimensional
\mathfrak{b}
-module with trivial action of
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{h}
acting by the weight
\lambda
.
This module again has a unique simple quotient denoted by
L_{\mathfrak{b}}(\lambda)
. We also say that
\lambda
is
dominant integral
if
L_{\mathfrak{b}}(\lambda)
is finite-dimensional.
In the case of
\lambda
being dominant integral, then we define the unique maximal finite dimensional quotient of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}(\lambda)
as
K_{\mathfrak{b}}(\lambda)
.

3.
Essential monomials and multiplications

From now on, we fix a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. In this section, we will introduce the superalgebra subject to our degeneration. The multiplication arises from the following result.

Proposition 3.1

(Cartan mapping)
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be a dominant integral weight such that
k\lambda
remains dominant integral for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and fix natural numbers
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, the map of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules defined on the highest weight vector given by

\displaystyle C_{m,n}:K_{\mathfrak{b}}((m+n)\lambda)

\displaystyle\rightarrow K_{\mathfrak{b}}(m\lambda)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}K_{\mathfrak{b}}(n\lambda)

\displaystyle v_{(m+n)\lambda}

\displaystyle\rightarrow v_{m\lambda}\otimes v_{n\lambda}

is well-defined.

Proof.

The vector on the right-hand side is of
\mathfrak{b}
-highest weight
(m+n)\lambda
and the tensor product is finite-dimensional hence also the
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module generated by
v_{m\lambda}\otimes v_{n\lambda}
. Thus, we know that this submodule is a quotient of
K_{\mathfrak{b}}((m+n)\lambda)
.
∎

We assume from now on that
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
as well as
k\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
are dominant integral for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

In turn, dualizing provides us with a map of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

(1)

K_{\mathfrak{b}}(m\lambda)^{*}\otimes K_{\mathfrak{b}}(n\lambda)^{*}\rightarrow K_{\mathfrak{b}}((m+n)\lambda)^{*}

We thus define the superalgebra

R(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}K_{\mathfrak{b}}(k\lambda)^{*}

with multiplication given by (
1
).

Remark 3.2
.

We specify what the multiplication from (
1
) looks like:

We have the isomorphism of finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, see
[
Mus12
, Appendix A.2.3.]

\displaystyle\gamma_{V,W}:W^{*}\otimes V^{*}

\displaystyle\rightarrow(V\otimes W)^{*}

\displaystyle\varphi\otimes\psi

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(r\otimes s\mapsto\psi(r)\varphi(s)\right)

Hence, the multiplication from (
1
) is the composition

K_{\mathfrak{b}}(m\lambda)^{*}\otimes K_{\mathfrak{b}}(n\lambda)^{*}\xrightarrow{\gamma^{*}_{K_{\mathfrak{b}}(n\lambda),K_{\mathfrak{b}}(m\lambda)}}(K_{\mathfrak{b}}(n\lambda)\otimes K_{\mathfrak{b}}(m\lambda))^{*}\xrightarrow{C_{n,m}^{*}}K_{\mathfrak{b}}((m+n)\lambda)^{*}

\mathfrak{g}
-modules

Definition 4.1

Lemma 4.2

Let
(I^{\prime},\mathbf{m}^{\prime})
be a multi-exponent and
(I,\mathbf{m})\in\mathrm{es}(K_{\mathfrak{b}}(k\lambda),<)

\eta_{I,\mathbf{m},k}(f^{(I^{\prime},\mathbf{m}^{\prime})}v_{k\lambda})=0

Proof.

If

∎

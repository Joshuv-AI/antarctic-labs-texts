Abstract
. We show that if a Sylow p p -subgroup of a finite group G G is nilpotent of class at most p p , then the p p -part of the Bogomolov multiplier of G G is locally controlled.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
20D20, 20J06

1.
Introduction

In this note we prove the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. If the nilpotency class of
P
does not exceed
p
, then the Bogomolov multipliers of
G
and
N_{G}(P)
have isomorphic Sylow
p
-subgroups.

This theorem is related to the following result of Holt
[
3
]
:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. If the nilpotency class of
P
does not exceed
p/2
, then the Schur multipliers of
G
and
N_{G}(P)
have isomorphic Sylow
p
-subgroups.

In fact, the majority of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is an adaptation of Holt’s argument. The crucial difference is the step where the bound on the nilpotency class of the Sylow
p
-subgroup is improved from Holt’s
p/2
in the Schur multiplier case, to
p
in the Bogomolov multiplier case. Note that this does not improve the bound in Theorem
1.2
, it merely shows that one can relax it when passing to Bogomolov multipliers.

The outline of the paper is as follows. We first provide some preliminaries in Section
2
. Then we proceed to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. To keep the exposition short, we only include the details where our argument differs from
[
3
]
, and refer to
loc. cit.
for the rest.

2.
Preliminaries

Most of the notations follow
[
2
]
. The maps and actions are always written from the right.

Let
G
be a finite group. The second homology group
\HH^{2}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is the
Schur multiplier
of
G
. If
1\to R\to F\to G\to 1
is a free presentation of
G
, then
\HH^{2}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is naturally isomorphic to
\Hom(M(G),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
, where
M(G)=(F^{\prime}\cap R)/[R,F]\cong\HH_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})
, see, e.g.,
[
2
, p. 42, p. 145]
.

Given a
G
-module
M
, denote

\Sha^{n}(G,M)=\bigcap_{\tiny\begin{matrix}A\leq G,\\
A\hbox{ abelian}\end{matrix}}\ker(\res^{G}_{A}:\HH^{n}(G,M)\to\HH^{n}(A,M)).

Bogomolov
[
1
]
studied the group
\Sha^{2}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

in relationship with Noether’s problem from invariant theory. This group is nowadays called the
Bogomolov multiplier
of
G
. It is shown in
[
4
, Proposition 3.8]
that if
1\to R\to F\to G\to 1
is a free presentation of
G
, then
\Sha^{2}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is naturally isomorphic to
\Hom(\B_{0}(G),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
, where
B_{0}(G)=(F^{\prime}\cap R)/\langle\K(F)\cap R\rangle
. Here
\K(F)
stands for the
set
of all commutators
[x,y]
, where
x,y\in F
.

Let a finite group
G
act on a finite group
Q
. Then
G
also acts on
\HH^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
via the rule
(c+\B^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))g=c^{\prime}+\B^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
, where the cocycle
c^{\prime}:Q\times Q\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
is given by the rule
c^{\prime}(q_{1},q_{2})=c(q_{1}g^{-1},q_{2}g^{-1})
. Via a free presentation
Q\cong F/R
of
Q
, we have an action of
G
on
M(Q)
, given as follows. Let
F
be free on
X
. Take an isomorphism
\phi:F/R\to Q
, and let
g\in G
and
x\in X
. Pick
y_{x}\in F
with the property that
(xR)\phi g\phi^{-1}=y_{x}R
. This gives rise to an endomorphism
\psi:F\to F
that sends
x
to
y_{x}
. Note that
R
is
\psi
-invariant. Thus
\psi
induces an action of
G
on
M(Q)
via
(r[R,F])g=(r\psi)[R,F]
, where
r\in F^{\prime}\cap R
.
It is not difficult to show that
\HH^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
and
\Hom(M(Q),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
become isomorphic as
G
-modules.

The Bogomolov multiplier
\Sha^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is a subgroup of
\HH^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. Take
g\in G
and
\alpha\in\Sha^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. Take an arbitrary abelian subgroup
A
of
Q
. Then
Ag^{-1}
is abelian, hence
\alpha\res^{G}_{Ag^{-1}}=0
. By definition,
(\alpha g)\res^{G}_{A}=0
. Thus
\Sha^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is a submodule of the
G
-module
\HH^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
\phi:F/R\to Q
be a free presentation of
Q
, and let
\psi
be as above. Denote
M_{0}(Q)=\langle\K(F)\cap R\rangle/[R,F]
. Take
g\in G
and
x,y\in F
with
[x,y]\in R
. Then
([x,y][R,F])g=([x,y]\psi)[R,F]=[x\psi,y\psi][R,F]
. As
R\psi\subseteq R
, it follows that
[x,y]\psi\in\K(F)\cap R
. This shows that
M_{0}(Q)
is a submodule of the
G
-module
M(Q)
, hence
\B_{0}(Q)=M(Q)/M_{0}(Q)
becomes a
G
-module. Similarly as above,
\Sha^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
and
\Hom(\B_{0}(Q),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
are isomorphic as
G
-modules.

We will also need a couple of auxiliary results on commutator subgroups. The notations follow
[
5
, p. 119]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be normal subgroups of a group
G
.

(1)

[A,A,{}_{n}B]\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}[[A,{}_{i}B],[A,{}_{n-i}B]]
for all
n\geq 1
.

(2)

[\gamma_{n}(B),A]\leq[A,{}_{n}B]
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

The item (1) is proved in
[
3
, Lemma 6]
. We prove (2) by induction on
n
. The case
n=1
is clear. Suppose the claim holds for some
n\geq 1
and all normal subgroups
A
and
B
of
G
. Then the Three Subgroup Lemma
[
5
, 5.1.10]
implies
[\gamma_{n+1}(B),A]=[\gamma_{n}(B),B,A]\leq[B,A,\gamma_{n}(B)][A,\gamma_{n}(B),B]
. By induction assumption we have
[B,A,\gamma_{n}(B)]=[[B,A],\gamma_{n}(B)]\leq[[B,A],{}_{n}B]=[A,{}_{n+1}B]
. In addition to that,

[A,\gamma_{n}(B),B]=[[A,\gamma_{n}(B)],B]\leq[A,{}_{n+1}B]
, again by induction assumption. This proves the result.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
H
be a subgroup of a finite group
G
. Let
M
be a
G
-module. Given
g\in G
, the conjugation map
H\to H^{g}
induces an isomorphism
\conj^{g}_{H}:\HH^{n}(H,M)\to\HH^{n}(H^{g},M)
. According to
[
3
]
, we say that
\alpha\in\HH^{n}(H,M)
is
stable
with respect to
G
(also
G
-invariant
according to
[
2
]
) if

\alpha\res^{H}_{H\cap H^{g}}=\alpha\conj^{g}_{H}\res^{H^{g}}_{H\cap H^{g}}

for every
g\in G
. The following is a crucial property of stable elements:

Lemma 3.1

(
[
3
]
, Lemma 2)
.

Let
p
be aprime, and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
of index coprime to
p
. Let
P_{G}
and
P_{H}
be the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
\HH^{n}(G,M)
and
\HH^{n}(H,M)
, respectively. Then
P_{G}
is isomorphic to the group of stable elements of
P_{H}
, which is a direct factor of
P_{H}
.

We first show that an analogous result holds for
\Sha^{n}
. Let
G
and
H
be as in Lemma
3.1
. let
S_{G}
and
S_{H}
be the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
\Sha^{n}(G,M)
and
\Sha^{n}(H,M)
, respectively. Then
S_{G}\leq P_{G}
and
S_{H}\leq P_{H}
. Let
\rho
be the restriction of the map
\res^{G}_{H}
to
S_{G}
, and let
\sigma
be the restriction of the map
\cor^{G}_{H}
to
S_{H}
. Pick
\alpha\in\Sha^{n}(G,M)
, and let
A
be an abelian subgroup of
H
. Then
\alpha\res^{G}_{H}\res^{H}_{A}=\alpha\res^{G}_{A}=0
, hence
\alpha\res^{G}_{H}\in\Sha^{n}(H,M)
. This shows that
\rho
maps
S_{G}
into
S_{H}
. Similarly, if
\beta\in\Sha^{n}(H,M)
and if
A
is an abelian subgroup of
G
, then
[
2
, Proposition 9.5, Chapter III]
gives

\beta\cor^{G}_{H}\res^{G}_{A}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\beta\conj_{H}^{s}\res^{H^{s}}_{H^{s}\cap A}\cor^{A}_{H^{s}\cap A},

where
\mathcal{S}
is a complete set of representatives of double cosets
HgA
, where
g\in G
. Note that
\beta\conj_{H}^{s}\in\Sha^{n}(H^{s},M)
, and, as
H^{s}\cap A
is an abelian subgroup of
H^{s}
, we get
\beta\conj_{H}^{s}\res^{H^{s}}_{H^{s}\cap A}=0
. Thus the above formula implies
\beta\cor^{G}_{H}\in\Sha^{n}(G,M)
. Hence
\sigma
maps
S_{H}
into
S_{G}
. As
|G:H|
is not divisible by
p
, it follows from
[
2
, Proposition 9.5, Chapter III]
that
\rho\sigma=|G:H|\cdot 1
is an automorphism of
\Sha^{n}(G,M)
. Similarly as in
[
3
]
we can now show the following:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p
be a prime, and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
of index coprime to
p
. Let
S_{G}
and
S_{H}
be the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
\Sha^{n}(G,M)
and
\Sha^{n}(H,M)
, respectively. Then
S_{G}
is isomorphic to to the group of stable elements of
S_{H}
, which is a direct factor of
S_{H}
.

Lemma
3.2
can be used to prove the following counterpart of
[
3
, Theorem 1]
, with proof being a straightforward adaptation of Holt’s argument:

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Let
M
be a trivial
G
-module. Let
W
be a characteristic
p
-functor which strongly controls fusion in
G
. Then the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
\Sha^{n}(G,M)
and
\Sha^{n}(N_{G}(W(P)),M)
are isomorphic.

Our next result is a key step in proving Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
Q
be a normal subgroup of a finite group
G
. Suppose that
[Q,{}_{c}G]=1
for some
c\geq 1
. Then

[\Sha^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),{}_{c-1}G]=1
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
[B_{0}(Q),{}_{c-1}G]=1
. Let
1\to R\to F\to G\to 1
be a free presentation of
G
. Then
Q
has a free presentation of the form
1\to R\to F_{1}\to Q\to 1
, where
F_{1}\trianglelefteq F
. By assumption we have that
[F_{1},{}_{c}F]\leq R
. Now Lemma
2.1
(1) gives

\displaystyle[F_{1}^{\prime}\cap R,{}_{c-1}F]

\displaystyle\leq[F_{1},F_{1},{}_{c-1}F]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{c-1}[[F_{1},{}_{i}F],[F_{1},{}_{c-i-1}F]].

Consider a commutator of the form

\omega=[[x,a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}],[y,b_{1},\ldots,b_{c-i-1}]],

where
x,y\in F_{1}
,
a_{1},\ldots a_{i},b_{1},b_{c-i-1}\in F
,
1\leq i\leq c-1
.
As
F_{1}
is a normal subgroup of
F
, we have that
\omega\in\K(F_{1})
. On the other hand, Lemma
2.1
yields that
[[F_{1},{}_{i}F],[F_{1},{}_{c-i-1}F]]\leq[F_{1},{}_{i}F,\gamma_{c-i}F]\leq[F_{1},{}_{c}F]\leq R
, therefore
\omega\in R
. This shows that

[F_{1}^{\prime}\cap R,{}_{c-1}F]\leq\langle\K(F_{1})\cap R\rangle
, hence the result.
∎

The following result can be proved by essentially repeating the argument of the second part of the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 7]
:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the nilpotency class of
P
does not exceed
p
. Let
Q
be a normal subgroup of
G
. If
Q
is a
p
-group, then
[\Sha^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),G]=[\Sha_{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),N_{G}(P)]
.

Proof.

Form
H=G\ltimes\Sha^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. Then
S=P\ltimes\Sha^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
H
. As
P
is nilpotent of class
\leq p
, Proposition
3.4
implies
[\Sha^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),{}_{p-1}P]=1
. The rest of the proof now follows the lines of
[
3
, Proof of Lemma 7]
.
∎

Having Corollary
3.5
at hand, we finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
by applying
[
3
, Lemma 8]
and repeating the argument following it.

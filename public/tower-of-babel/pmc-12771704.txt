Integrating serum pharmacochemistry with network pharmacology and pharmacological validation to elucidate the mechanism of KaiXinSan in treating insomnia
Authors: Shihao Zhang, Dongyan Guo, Chongbo Zhao, Yajun Shi, Junbo Zou, Huanxian Shi, Jiangxue Cheng, Jing Sun, Xiaofei Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundKaiXinSan (KXS) is a classic Chinese herbal compound used for treating insomnia and related mental disorders. Modern medical studies have shown that KXS can treat depression, Alzheimer's disease, memory loss, and other conditions. In addition, the effect of KXS in treating insomnia has been confirmed in clinical applications and animal experiments. However, the mechanism by which KXS treats insomnia remains unclear. The purpose of this study is to evaluate the impact of KXS on insomnia and explore its mechanism.MethodsA rat insomnia model was established using p-chlorophenylalanine (PCPA). Pharmacodynamic evaluation was performed via animal behavioral assessments, ELISA detection of biochemical indicators, and pathological tissue observation. The key mechanisms of KXS in treating insomnia were clarified through an integrated approach combining serum pharmacochemistry, network pharmacology, transcriptomics, and metabolomics. These mechanisms were further validated by molecular docking, in vivo, and in vitro experiments.ResultsKXS improved motor activity and sleep status in insomniac rats and alleviated hippocampal neuronal damage. ELISA analysis showed that KXS increased levels of 5-HT and GABA while reducing DA and NE levels in rats. Additionally, KXS decreased levels of TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and ROS. Using UPLC-Q-Orbitrap MS/MS technology, 169 chemical components and 39 blood- enterable components of KXS were identified. Results from network pharmacology and transcriptomics indicated that KXS treats insomnia by regulating serotonin synapse and TNF signaling pathways. Furthermore, KXS reversed abnormal arachidonic acid and tryptophan metabolism in insomniac rats. Molecular docking, immunohistochemistry, and Western blotting showed that active components of KXS exhibited strong binding affinity to relevant proteins, upregulating 5-HTR1A and GABAA protein expression while inhibiting TNF-α and IL-6 protein expression. Cell experiments confirmed that KXS medicated serum reduced LPS induced neuronal damage and inflammatory responses in neural cells.ConclusionsKXS treats insomnia by regulating serotonin synapse signaling pathways and inflammatory responses, and influencing arachidonic acid and tryptophan metabolism.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12906-025-05196-z.Keywords: KaiXinSan, Sleep disorders, Serum pharmacochemistry, Network pharmacology, Transcriptomics, Metabolomics

Introduction

Insomnia typically refers to difficulty falling asleep or maintaining sleep, resulting in insufficient duration or poor quality of sleep that fails to meet an individual’s physiological needs. This condition has become a global health epidemic [1]. Chronic insomnia can disrupt the human immune system, increase the risk of chronic diseases, and exacerbate symptoms of mental disorders. Such as anxiety, depression, and cognitive impairment, creating a vicious cycle [2]. Current pharmacological treatments for insomnia include melatonin and its receptor agonists, benzodiazepines, non-benzodiazepine sedatives, and sedative antidepressants [3]. While these drugs demonstrate efficacy in acute insomnia, concerns about safety and unstable efficacy, as well as the risks of drug addiction and tolerance, limit their use [4].

Insomnia is a complex multifactorial disorder. Neurobiologically, its pathogenesis is closely linked to multiple neurotransmitters [5]: 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT) dysfunction impairs sleep initiation and maintenance [6]; reduced concentration or abnormal receptor function of γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA) weakens its neuronal inhibitory effects, disrupting sleep [7]; and imbalances in dopamine (DA) and norepinephrine (NE) also disturb sleep–wake regulation [8]. The connection between inflammation and insomnia has also attracted much attention in recent years: inflammatory responses can induce insomnia by affecting neurotransmitter metabolism and neuroendocrine function through multiple pathways [9], with inflammatory cytokines interfering with 5-HT and GABA to disrupt sleep architecture, while prolonged insomnia in turn disrupts bodily homeostasis and triggers immune dysfunction [10]. Therefore, studying the effects of neurotransmitters and inflammation on insomnia helps analyze potential therapeutic strategies for it [11].

KaiXinSan (KXS) is a traditional chinese medicine (TCM) formula composed of four herbs: Panax ginseng C.A.Mey. (RC), Polygala tenella Willd. (YZ), Poria cocos (Schw.) Wolf (FL), and Acorus tatarinowii Schott (SCP) [12]. First documented in the Emergency Prescriptions Worth a Thousand Gold (Tang Dynasty), KXS is historically indicated for improving insomnia, amnesia, and anxiety [13]. It has been widely used to treat mental disorders across centuries. Modern studies have shown that KXS exerts multiple pharmacological effects: it exhibits antidepressant activity by regulating lipid metabolism disorders [14]; treats Alzheimer's disease by inhibiting neuronal apoptosis, oxidative stress, and improving mitochondrial dysfunction, synaptic function, and neurotrophic factors [15]; ameliorates cognitive dysfunction by reducing neuronal damage and neuroinflammation [16]; and possesses anti-inflammatory properties [17]. The antidepressant effects of KXS have been widely demonstrated [18]. Sleep disorders and depression almost always occur concomitantly [19], and both diseases are associated with disorders of multiple neurotransmitters such as 5-HT, NE, DA and γ-GABA, which preliminarily indicates the therapeutic potential of KXS for insomnia. Additionally, previous studies have confirmed the therapeutic effect of KXS on insomnia: electroencephalogram recording of the wake-sleep cycle showed that KXS shortened wakefulness duration and prolonged slow-wave sleep stage I, slow-wave sleep stage II, and rapid eye movement sleep in insomniac rats, promoting sleep recovery [20]. KXS improved memory and protected brain cells by reducing the content of acetylcholinesterase in the cerebral cortex of sleep-deprived mice and malondialdehyde in plasma, while increasing the content of superoxide dismutase in plasma and catalase in the cerebral cortex, thus limiting acetylcholine degradation and protecting cells from toxic free radical damage [21]. A clinical trial has shown that KXS can increase serum 5-HT levels and improve sleep quality in breast cancer patients with insomnia [22].

The complex pathological mechanism of insomnia involves neurotransmitter imbalance, immune inflammation disorder and other aspects [23]. A core advantage of TCM lies in its multi-component, multi-target synergistic action, which aligns with the complex pathological networks of insomnia. Serum pharmacochemistry accurately identifies active substances involved in in vivo pharmacological effects by detecting blood-borne components after oral administration [24]. Network pharmacology reveals the mechanism of multi-target synergy by analyzing the association between multi-component drugs and insomnia-related genes [25]. Transcriptomics reveals the molecular mechanism of drugs regulating insomnia-related biological processes at the RNA level by using high-throughput sequencing technology [26]. Metabolomics explains how drugs restore internal environmental homeostasis at the metabolic level by analyzing changes in metabolic status [27]. This multi-omics analysis method provides a scientific basis for analyzing the therapeutic effect of KXS on insomnia.

Materials and methods

Identification components of KXS

Preparation of KXS test sample

The decoction pieces of four herbs: RC, YZ, FL, and SCP were individually pulverized and sieved. They were then mixed at a ratio of 1:1:2:1 to prepare the KXS sample. Specifically, 0.5 g of the sample was added to 10 mL of 75% methanol, subjected to ultrasonic extraction for 30 min, centrifuged, and the supernatant was collected and filtered [28] (Table 1).

Preparation of serum containing KXS

Sixteen male Sprague–Dawley (SD) rats (purchased from Chengdu Dashuo Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd.) were acclimatized for one week and randomly assigned to two groups: control and treatment group (n = 8). The treatment group received oral administration of KXS at a daily dose of 4 g/kg/d for 1 week. 1 h after the final administration, rats were anesthetized with isoflurane, and blood was collected via abdominal aortic puncture. Following centrifugation, the supernatant was divided into two aliquots: one for component analysis, and the other inactivated at 56 °C for 30 min, filtered, and stored at −20 °C for subsequent experiments [29]. All procedures complied with animal experimentation regulations and were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Shaanxi University of Chinese Medicine (Clinical trial number: SUCMDL20240613001).

Construction of ' component-disease-target ' network

The target genes of blood-entering components and insomnia-related diseases were imported into the Wei Sheng Xin platform (http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/) to obtain intersection targets. The intersection targets were then uploaded to the String database to analyze their protein–protein interaction (PPI) relationships. The confidence threshold was set to 0.7, and interaction lines with excessively low scores were hidden. After sorting the targets by PPI degree values, the filtered target proteins were imported into Cytoscape 3.10.1 software to construct a visual network diagram of 'components-disease-targets'.

GO and KEGG

The intersection targets were input into the DAVID database, and top-ranked GO and KEGG enrichment analysis information was obtained with a screening criterion of p < 0.05. Pathways unrelated to insomnia were excluded, and the results were visualized.

Pharmacodynamics experiment

Animal grouping, modeling and administration

The rats were acclimatized for one week and then randomly divided into six groups (n = 12): control, model, positive, KXS of low, medium and high dose group. The administration methods and dosages were referenced from the Key Information Table of Ancient Classic Prescriptions published by the State Administration of TCM of China (Clinical trial number: 20240902001).

Insomnia models were established via intraperitoneal injection of PCPA (300 mg/kg/d, C15378065, MAKELIN), with the control group receiving an equal volume of normal saline for three consecutive days. From day 4, the positive group was administered diazepam (DXP, 0.92 mg/kg/d, Lot 240502, Shandong Xinyi Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.), while the low, medium, and high dose groups received KXS at 1, 2, and 4 g/kg/d, respectively. During the period, the weight changes of rats after modeling and after treatment were recorded.

Open field experiments

The effects of KXS on the behavior of insomnia rats were assessed using open field experiments. The parameters evaluated included total movement distance and movement distance in the central area. The analysis chamber measured 80 × 80 × 80 cm, with its bottom divided into 16 square regions, among which the central four were defined as the central area. All animals were acclimatized to the laboratory environment for 10 min before testing, and the experiment was performed under consistent acoustic and lighting conditions. A detector was used to record the rats' activities over a 5 min period. After each rat's test, the chamber was cleaned with 75% ethanol to prevent interference with other animals.

Sleep induction experiment

Sleep was induced in animals via intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (45 mg/kg). Sleep latency (time from anesthetic injection to loss of the righting reflex) and sleep duration (time from loss of the righting reflex to awakening) were recorded for each rat to evaluate sleep levels across groups. Loss of the righting reflex was defined as the rat remaining immobile for 60 consecutive seconds without rolling over after pentobarbital sodium injection.

Sample collection

After the last administration for 1 h, rats were anesthetized by inhaling isoflurane (Lot No. 2024100301, RWD Life Science Co., Ltd.). When no response was observed by pressing the rat's tail, the rat was considered to be in an anesthetized state. Then, blood was collected from the abdominal aorta of the rat, blood samples were left to stand for 30 min, then centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min at 4℃. The supernatant was collected and stored at −80℃. The rats were sacrificed by cervical dislocation, and their brain tissues were collected.

Detection biochemical indicators (ELASA)

According to the kit instructions, the contents of GABA (MM-0441R1), 5-HT (MM-0442R1), DA (MM-0355R1), and NE (MM-0556R1) in the hypothalamus of rat brain tissues were detected. The contents of TNF-α (MM-0180R1), IL-6 (MM-0190R1), IL-1β (MM-0047R1, Jiangsu Meimian Co., Ltd.), and ROS (MM-CB10450-Ra, Keaibo) in serum were also measured.

Hematoxylin–eosin (HE)

Brain tissues were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and embedded in paraffin, followed by sectioning at a thickness of approximately 4 μm. After staining and mounting, the morphological observation of neuronal cells in the hippocampal tissue was performed.

Transcriptome analysis

RNA-Seq analysis

In light of the pharmacodynamic findings, brain tissue samples from three rats each in the control, model, and KXS groups were chosen for RNA sequencing analysis. Total RNA was extracted from the brain tissue of rats using Trizol reagent. Following this, mRNA was separated, fragmented, and primed. The first strand, second strand, and end fragments of cDNA were synthesized in order to construct a transcriptome library. An Agilent 2100 detector was employed to assess the RNA quality, with the RNA integrity number (RIN) furnished by the 2100 instrument determining the RNA's quality. Ultimately, Omicsmart was used for bioinformatic analysis (http://www.omicsmart.com) [30].

WGCNA

Differentially expressed genes obtained from transcriptome data were imported into the WGCNA analysis panel of the Majorbio cloud platform [31]. Genes with an expression level of less than or equal to 2 were then screened out. First, based on the principle of the scale-free network, the weight coefficients of β were determined. The gene clustering tree was constructed by utilizing the correlation between gene co-expression profiles and the expression levels among genes. Grey was used to denote the genes that could not be classified into any of the modules. Secondly, by analyzing the correlation between the module and the trait, as well as the enrichment degree of the genes within the module, we determined the key gene module. We then built a gene regulatory network map in this module and identified the regulatory relationships between them [32].

Metabolomics analysis

Serum samples from each group of rats were collected. 10 μL of each serum sample was mixed with 90 μL of precipitating agent buffer to precipitate proteins. After centrifugation, the supernatant was injected into a Thermo Scientific Agilent 7890B (Waltham, Massachusetts, USA) for GC–MS/MS2 analysis. The chromatographic column used was an HP-5MS capillary column (2.1 × 50 mm). Specific data processing methods and GC–MS determination methods were referred to previous studies [33].

Molecular docking

Based on network pharmacology and transcriptome sequencing results, KXS was analyzed to treat insomnia by closely regulating the serotonergic synapse and TNF signaling pathways. Target proteins with high Degree values from PPI network topology analysis and their associated compounds were selected for molecular docking. The PDB structure files of TNF-α, IL-6, HTR1A, and GABRA1 (protein IDs: 1A8M, 1ALU, 7E2X, 6CDU) were downloaded from the RSCB-PDB database. 3D structures of target proteins and compounds were imported into AutoDockTools 1.5.7. After removing water molecules and heterochains from receptor proteins, compounds were used as ligands for molecular docking via Pymol to generate docking scores, which were subsequently visualized.

Immunohistochemistry

The rat brain tissue was first paraffin-embedded, followed by sectioning. The sections were then deparaffinized with xylene, and antigen repair was performed using sodium citrate buffer. Endogenous peroxidase was blocked with 3% hydrogen peroxide. A primary antibody (1:200) was added, and the samples were incubated overnight at 4 ℃. After washing, an HRP-labeled secondary antibody (1:500) was applied, and the samples were incubated at 37 ℃ for 60 min. Following another wash, DAB staining was conducted. Finally, the samples were counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated, cleared, sealed, and photographed for analysis using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software.

Western blotting

Proteins were extracted using lysate (AR0102-100, BOSTER) and quantified using the BCA Protein Assay Kit (AR0197, BOSTER). Groups of samples were electrophoresed with 10% SDP-SAGE then transferred to PVDF membranes. The membrane was then incubated overnight at 4℃ with primary antibodies TNF-α (TD6087F, Antibody, 1:2000), IL-6 (PY19810F, Antibody, 1:2000), 5-HT1A (P08908, Bioss, 1:2000), GABRA1 (BA0873, BOSTER, 1:2000) and β-actin (80,455–1-RR, Proteintech, 1:5000). At the end of membrane washing, secondary antibody (P0258, Beyotime, 1:10,000) was added and incubated for 1 h, then developed and photographed. The expression of each group of samples was analyzed using Image Pro Plus 6.0 software.

Cell experiments

Cell culture

Rat adrenal pheochromocytoma cells (PC12, IM-R014, IMMOCELL) were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C under 75% CO₂ conditions, with the culture medium replaced every 2 days.

Cell viability assay (CCK-8)

When PC12 cells reached 85%–90% confluence, they were enzymatically digested with trypsin and seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 104 cells per well. KXS medicated serum was added to the culture medium. After cell adhesion, LPS solution (1–9 μg/mL) or KXS medicated serum was added to 6 replicate wells per group. Following 24 h incubation, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution (CT0001-B, Sparkjade) was added to each well. After a 1-h reaction, absorbance at 450 nm was measured to calculate cell viability under different LPS concentration stimuli and the effect of KXS medicated serum on cell activity. Within the safe LPS stimulation range, changes in cell activity after medicated serum intervention were detected.

Detection of inflammatory level in PC12 cells

PC12 cells were sequentially seeded into 6-well plates for culture. Afterward, different concentrations of medicated serum were added for cultivation, followed by LPS treatment. The contents of TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and ROS in the cell culture supernatant were detected using kits.

Protein detection of PC12 cells

Proteins were extracted from the treated cells, and the indicators of TNF-α, IL-6, and β-actin were detected. The detection method was the same as that in 2.8.

Statistical analysis

The data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD)and were analyzed using SPSS 20.0 software. When the data fol-lowed a normal distribution and had equal variances, a one-wayanalysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed, followed by Tukey'spost hoc analysis. Alternatively, the Kruskal–Wallis test was util-ized. Results

Component identification results

Blank serum, drug-containing serum, and KXS samples were analyzed by UPLC-QE Orbitrap MS/MS in both positive and negative ion modes, with base peak chromatograms and total ion current chromatograms shown in Fig. 1. Detection data were imported into Progenesis QI v3.0 software (Nonlinear Dynamics, Newcastle, UK) for baseline filtering, peak identification, integration, retention time correction, peak alignment, and normalization. Compounds were identified based on accurate mass values, secondary fragmentation patterns, and isotopic distribution, then qualitatively analyzed using a database (OE Biotech Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). A total of 90 compounds were identified in positive ion mode and 79 compounds in negative ion mode (Table S1), with their herbal sources annotated via the TCMSP and HERB databases.

Detection results of KXS active ingredients. (A) Base peak chromatogram-POS. (B) Base peak chromatogram-NEG. (C) Total ion chromatogram-POS. (D) Total ion chromatogram-NEG. (Control - blank serum; treatment - medicated serum; TCM-KXS)

Blood-enterable components were identified based on specific criteria: components must be detected in both the medicated serum group and the KXS group with consistent retention time (RT). The RT error should be within ± 0.2 min relative to database reference standards. The primary molecular weight error must be within 5 ppm. Additionally, the peak area ratio of medicated serum to blank serum should exceed the fold change value (FC = 2), or the component should be undetected in the blank group. A total of 39 blood components were identified as KXS active components.

Network pharmacology results

Target prediction and interaction network construction

A total of 785 drug targets for KXS active components were predicted using the TCMSP, HERB, and Swiss databases. Meanwhile, 986 insomnia-related targets were identified through the DisGeNET, Drugbank, GeneCards, MaleCards, and OMIM databases. Merging and deduplicating these two datasets yielded 173 intersecting targets (Fig. 2 A). The active components and intersecting targets were imported into Cytoscape 3.10.1 software to construct a "drug-component-target" network diagram (Fig. 2 B). In this network, blue and pink rectangles represent KXS and insomnia, respectively; blue diamonds denote the drug; blue circles indicate components; pink circles signify intersecting targets; connecting lines illustrate relationships between components and genes—the more connections, the more critical the role of the component or gene.

Results of network pharmacology. (A) Cross-targeting map of KXS and insomnia. (B) “Component-Target-Disease” visualization network diagrams. (C) Protein interaction network diagram and degree ranking. (D) GO enrichment analysis diagram. (E) KEGG enrichment analysis diagram

Key chemical components of KXS for insomnia treatment were screened based on Degree values (average degree = 15.87). Ginsenoside Rg1, Ginsenoside Rb1, Tenuifolin, Dehydrotumulosic acid, and Asaraldehyde exhibited degree values higher than 15.87, suggesting they may be pivotal compounds. The 173 key targets were further analyzed in the STRING database to derive target interactions (Fig. 2 C). Notably, targets such as TNF, IL6, and HTR1A had Degree values exceeding the average (20.33), indicating their potential as primary targets for KXS in treating insomnia.

GO and KEGG pathway analysis

A total of 173 intersecting targets were analyzed via the DAVID database for GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses. Using a criterion of p < 0.05, top ranked terms in biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF) were selected for visualization (Fig. 2 D). These included biological regulatory processes such as chemical synaptic transmission and regulation of postsynaptic membrane potential, cellular components such as presynaptic membrane and glutamatergic synapse, and molecular functions such as neurotransmitter receptor activity and serotonin binding. A total of 113 pathways were screened with P < 0.05, and the top 20 pathways were visualized (Fig. 2 E) to generate a KEGG pathway enrichment bar chart. After excluding disease-irrelevant pathways, key targets were primarily enriched in the serotonergic synapse, IL-17, TNF and GABAergic synapse signaling pathway, which represent critical signaling pathways for KXS in treating insomnia.

Evaluation of treatment efficacy

KXS alleviates neurotransmitter disorders and inflammation levels in insomnia rats

Insomnia induces disordered secretion of multiple neurotransmitters in the body (Fig. 3 B–E). The insomnia model established by PCPA injection reduced hypothalamic GABA and 5-HT levels while increasing DA and NE levels in rats (p < 0.01). KXS treatment reversed abnormal secretion of GABA, 5-HT, DA, and NE (p < 0.05, p < 0.01). These results indicate that after rats are administered KXS, it can significantly reverse the decrease in sleep-promoting inhibitory neurotransmitters and the increase in sleep-inhibiting excitatory neurotransmitters caused by the acute depletion of serotonin. This suggests that KXS can alleviate insomnia at the hormonal level. Meanwhile, insomnia is accompanied by increased inflammatory factor secretion and oxidative stress (Fig. 3 F–I). Compared with the control, serum TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and ROS levels were significantly elevated in the model (p < 0.01), whereas KXS reduced these inflammatory factors and ROS (p < 0.01). This indicates that while alleviating neurotransmitter secretion disorders, KXS reduces the body's inflammatory response and oxidative stress levels caused by insomnia, thereby alleviating the further progression of insomnia from another aspect.

KXS improves the motor activity and sleep state of insomnia rats

When the body is in an insomnia state, rats exhibit anxiety behavior and reduced motor activity (Fig. 4 A). In the open field test, insomnia rats showed significantly decreased motor activity compared to the control (Fig. 4 B and C). Detected data revealed that the movement distance was lower than that of normal rats (p < 0.01), while KXS alleviated this trend and increased movement distance (p < 0.05, p < 0.01). Additionally, insomnia rats appeared listless with reduced food intake and body weight (Fig. 4 D), whereas KXS increased body weight (p < 0.01). These results indicate that KXS can significantly alleviate neurasthenia, loss of appetite, and reduced motor activity caused by insomnia. To further evaluate the effect of KXS on sleep status, pentobarbital sodium was used to induce sleep (Fig. 4 E and F). Results showed that KXS shortened sleep latency and prolonged sleep duration in insomnia model (p < 0.05, p < 0.01), indicating its ability to improve sleep status.

KXS alleviates neuronal damage in insomnia rats

Insomnia induced neuronal damage in rats, which was alleviated by KXS (Fig. 5 yellow arrows indicate neuronal cells). In the control, the hippocampal tissue had a basically normal overall structure, with a rich and relatively orderly arrangement of neurons in the hippocampal region. In the model, hippocampal tissue exhibited abnormal overall structure, disordered neuronal arrangement in the hippocampal area, and numerous neurons with pyknosis and necrosis. In the low, medium and high dose groups of KXS, the overall structure of the hippocampus gradually normalized, and the number of neurons showing pyknosis and necrosis decreased in a dose dependent manner. These results indicate that KXS can alleviate the pathological damage of neurons in rats caused by insomnia.

Results of RNA-seq analysis

Principal component analysis (PCA) showed that samples within the same group clustered closely together, indicating minimal intra-group variability (Fig. 6 A). Comparing sequencing results among the control, model, and treatment groups with criteria of false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and |log₂FC|> 1, the model group showed 106 upregulated genes and 611 downregulated genes versus the control group. The treatment group exhibited 313 upregulated genes and 39 downregulated genes versus the model group (Fig. 6 B). Z-score processing was applied to each gene, and gradient colors were used to visualize the expression levels of significantly differentially expressed genes across samples (Fig. 6 C). Enrichment analysis was performed on differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the model and treatment groups (Fig. 6 D and E). A total of 185 KEGG pathways were enriched by DEGs, including the TNF signaling pathway, serotonergic synapse, and tryptophan metabolism. Additionally, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was applied to the DEG dataset (Fig. 6 F and G). GSEA-KEGG results showed that DEGs between the model and treatment groups were significantly enriched in pathways such as the serotonergic synapse, GABAergic synapse, and related inflammatory pathways. This indicates that KXS can treat insomnia by influencing these pathways.

SIMCA software was used to construct orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) models. Discussion

In our study, injecting animals with PCPA competitively inhibits the activity of tryptophan hydroxylase, blocking the conversion of tryptophan to 5-HT, disrupting the body normal sleep–wake regulatory mechanism, and inducing insomnia symptoms in rats [34]. This method has been proven safe and effective in a variety of studies [35, 36]. Additionally, we found that KXS treatment not only reversed the neurotransmitter secretion disorders caused by insomnia but also reduced abnormal inflammatory cytokine levels in the body. Studies have shown that long-term insomnia leads to significantly elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines [37]. This result is consistent with the findings of a previously reported study, which used the TCM compound Wendan Decoction to treat insomnia, improving neurotransmitter secretion disorders while reducing inflammatory factors in the colon, serum, and hippocampus [38]. From a neuroendocrine perspective, insomnia activates the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis, promoting massive secretion of cortisol, which decreases immune cell sensitivity to cortisol and allows immune cells to secrete pro-inflammatory factors unimpeded [39]. These inflammatory factors directly affect neurotransmitter synthesis, metabolism, and release. For example, TNF-α inhibits tryptophan hydroxylase activity, reducing 5-HT synthesis; IL-1β decreases GAD expression, leading to reduced GABA levels [40]. Meanwhile, inflammatory factors can also induce oxidative stress by activating microglia, generating large amounts of ROS. ROS oxidizes neurotransmitters and their precursors, damaging the structure and function of neurotransmitter receptors, and further exacerbating neurotransmitter imbalance [41]. Animal behavioral observations showed KXS increased the activity distance of insomnia rats in open field tests and improved their sleep status. Pathological tissue sections further confirmed KXS protected the hippocampus of insomnia model. On the other hand, KXS medicated serum alleviates LPS-induced PC12 cell damage and inflammatory responses. PC12 cells exhibit many neural cell characteristics, such as the ability to synthesize and release neurotransmitters, making them suitable for modeling neural cell functions and behaviors [42]. It has been reported in another study that the extract of the compound preparation Xiao-Er-An-Shen Decoction exerts a neuroprotective effect by regulating the differentiation and antioxidant activity of cultured PC12 cells [43]. Long-term insomnia places the body in a stress state, triggering oxidative stress and inflammation. Inducing PC12 with LPS can simulate the in vivo inflammatory microenvironment. In summary, KXS not only alleviates neurotransmitter disorders caused by insomnia but also reduces the inflammatory response associated with insomnia.

Network pharmacology and transcriptome sequencing results revealed that the serotonergic synapse and TNF signaling pathway may be key pathways for KXS in treating insomnia. Clock and Aff4 have been identified as core genes that regulate sleep. As a core regulatory hub for the biological clock and sleep homeostasis, the Clock gene has been confirmed in previous studies [44]. Shortened sleep duration is associated with several single nucleotide polymorphisms in the intronic region of CLOCK; over time, reduced sleep duration is linked to decreased cortical volume, primarily in the frontal and parietal lobes [45]. The Aff4 gene may affect the balance of tryptophan metabolism by regulating the kynurenine pathway [46]. Studies have shown that inhibiting the activity of kynurenine aminotransferase can reduce the conversion of tryptophan to kynurenine, increase its metabolic shunting toward serotonin/melatonin, and thereby improve sleep quality [47]. Specifically, the Clock gene directly controls the sleep–wake cycle through the transcription-translation feedback loop and sleep homeostasis regulation [48], while the Aff4 gene indirectly influences neurotransmitter metabolism, inflammatory responses, and metabolic pathways via transcriptional elongation regulation. These two genes collaborate at multiple levels to maintain normal sleep function. In insomnia research, inhibition of the serotonergic synapse signaling pathway mediated by the 5-HT1A receptor in the hippocampus leads to cognitive dysfunction [49]. Microinjection of fusinersen (a 5-HT1A agonist) into the dorsal raphe nucleus enhances rapid eye movement (REM) sleep [50]. Additionally, 5-HT can act on 5-HT receptors on GABAergic neurons to directly regulate GABAergic neuron activity, thereby influencing GABA release and GABA signaling pathway function [51]. Reduced GABA secretion or abnormal GABAA receptor function may cause excessive neuronal excitability, making it difficult to initiate or maintain sleep [52]. Chronic insomnia activates the TNF-α signaling pathway, and inflammatory factors such as TNF-α restrict the synthesis rates of 5-HT and GABA by inhibiting the activities of tryptophan hydroxylase (TPH) and glutamate decarboxylase (GAD) [53]. Activation of inflammatory pathways suppresses the expression of 5-HTR1A and GABAA receptors, disrupts the negative feedback regulation mechanism of serotonergic neurons, and weakens the binding capacity of GABA to its receptors, further exacerbating insomnia.

Metabolomics studies indicate that KXS treats insomnia by regulating arachidonic acid metabolism and tryptophan metabolism. Arachidonic acid metabolism, a core pathway for inflammation and oxidative stress, generates multiple bioactive mediators via cyclooxygenase (COX), lipoxygenase (LOX), and cytochrome P450 (CYP) pathways under chronic insomnia to influence sleep [54]. The COX-2/5-LOX pathway amplifies pro-inflammatory factor signals through pro-inflammatory products, and the 15-LOX/CYP450 pathway inhibits pro-inflammatory factors and up-regulates anti-inflammatory factors through anti-inflammatory products. ROS generated by arachidonic acid metabolism impair the activity of TPH, the rate-limiting enzyme for 5-HT synthesis, thereby inhibiting 5-HT production. 5-HT can be converted to N-acetyl-5-methoxytryptamine, a precursor of melatonin, which ultimately generates melatonin to directly regulate circadian rhythms [55]. Serotonin, a key bioactive product of tryptophan metabolism, is synthesized from tryptophan via 5-hydroxytryptophan (5-HTP) to regulate sleep [56]. Arachidonic acid metabolism affects insomnia through inflammation and oxidative stress, while tryptophan metabolism regulates sleep–wake balance via 5-HT and melatonin pathways. The two interact via inflammation-metabolism crosstalk to influence sleep [57].

Through the transcription-translation feedback loop (TTFL), Clock defines the circadian rhythm of the sleep–wake cycle. Meanwhile, it rhythmically regulates the expression of key enzymes involved in arachidonic acid metabolism and tryptophan metabolism, ensuring that pro-inflammatory products maintain a low level during the sleep phase, while anti-inflammatory products and sleep-promoting neurotransmitters (such as melatonin) maintain a high level [58, 59]. By regulating transcriptional elongation, Aff4, on the one hand, optimizes the balance of tryptophan metabolic branches; on the other hand, it enhances the activity of the anti-inflammatory pathway in arachidonic acid metabolism and inhibits the pro-inflammatory pathway, thereby eliminating "inflammatory interference" for sleep [60, 61]. The inflammatory signals from arachidonic acid metabolism and the neurotransmitter-inflammatory signals from tryptophan metabolism are mutually coupled: if pro-inflammatory signals dominate, they will simultaneously inhibit the circadian rhythm regulated by Clock and the transcriptional regulatory function of Aff4, leading to a vicious cycle of circadian rhythm disorder, metabolic imbalance, and exacerbated inflammation, which in turn triggers chronic insomnia. In contrast, if anti-inflammatory signals dominate, they will strengthen the stability of Clock-regulated circadian rhythm and the regulatory efficiency of Aff4, thereby maintaining high-quality sleep.

To clarify the active components of KXS in treating insomnia, we analyzed KXS blood-entering components and validated them via molecular docking technology. Results showed these active components exhibited strong docking activity with corresponding receptors. Studies indicate Ginsenoside Rg1 regulates the HPA axis to inhibit excessive stress hormone secretion, thereby reducing stress-induced sleep disturbances and alleviating insomnia [62]. Ginsenoside Rb1 modulates immune cell function and suppresses inflammatory cell migration/activation to relieve inflammatory responses [63]. Tenuifolin improves sleep quality by increasing slow-wave sleep and rapid eye movement sleep duration while reducing awakening frequency [64]. Dehydrotumulosic acid demonstrates potent anti-inflammatory activity, reducing Aβ25–35 induced neurotoxicity in PC12 and alleviating inflammation [65]. It is worth noting that as a Chinese herbal compound, the four constituent herbs of KXS have each been proven to have effects in treating insomnia. For RC, a U.S. patent has approved the use of ginseng alcohol extract in nutritional supplements or foods to improve sleep, which can alleviate sleep problems through orexin-mediated autophagy [66]. YZ can achieve sedative and hypnotic effects by regulating the serotonergic system, GABAergic system, and immune system [64]. FL can improve sleep duration and quality in patients with clinical insomnia [67]. For SCP, its active ingredient α-Asarone has been proven to help improve NREM sleep and maintain REM sleep [68]. These findings collectively demonstrate that KXS is effective in treating insomnia. In conclusion, KXS exerts therapeutic effects against insomnia through a multi-component, multi-target, and multi-pathway mechanism.

This study still has some limitations. First, regarding the establishment of the rat insomnia model: we induced insomnia in rats by injecting PCPA to reduce serotonin levels. However, this model cannot fully reflect insomnia in people’s normal lives, as the establishment of an insomnia model should take into account multiple factors. Second, there is the selection of key active components of KXS for treating insomnia. The screening of these components was based on the identification of blood-entering components, network degree value, and docking affinity, which to a certain extent can indicate that these four components play a role in the treatment of insomnia. However, we did not fully consider the influence of other factors such as pharmacokinetics and blood–brain barrier permeability. Finally, this study concludes that KXS can improve insomnia by regulating the serotonergic synapse and TNF signaling pathways. However, the activation of these pathways has not been fully verified.

In the future research plan: First, we intend to adopt more accurate modeling methods to construct animal models that are more consistent with human insomnia. For example, subtle stressors, intermittent electric shocks, fear conditioning, and auditory stimuli—these methods provide convincing laboratory controls for the persistent mechanisms of insomnia in humans [69]. Second, we will conduct more detailed cellular experiments and pharmacokinetic experiments to perform a more thorough screening of the active components of KXS for insomnia treatment [70, 71]. Subsequent research will delve into identifying the potential active ingredients in KXS for treating insomnia. These components will serve as the quality standards for implementing quality control in KXS compound formulations. Finally, we will verify the expression levels of more proteins in the serotonergic synapse and TNF signaling pathways, and adopt methods involving protein receptor agonists and inhibitors to enhance the persuasiveness of the experiment, thereby verifying the activation mechanisms of these pathways.

Conclusion

An insomnia rat model was constructed, and the therapeutic effect of KXS on insomnia was confirmed through animal behavioral assessment, biochemical index detection, and pathological histological observation. By integrating network pharmacology, transcriptomics and metabolomics, it was found that KXS treats insomnia by regulating serotonergic synapse signaling pathways and inflammatory responses and influencing arachidonic acid and tryptophan metabolism. In future studies, we will further analyze the active ingredients of KXS for treating insomnia and validate them in clinical trials.

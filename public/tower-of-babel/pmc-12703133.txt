Genotype and Haplotype Analysis Between ESR1 Variants and Breast Cancer in the Bangladeshi Patients: A PCR‐RFLP‐Based Genetic Association Study
Authors: Md Shafiul Hossen, Md Abdul Aziz, Md Abdul Barek, Nusrat Jahan, Nahida Afroz, Mobashera Begum, Mohammad Safiqul Islam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsBreast cancer (BC) is the most often diagnosed malignancy in women. The genetic factor is estimated to be responsible for the higher BC risk. BC development is thought to be closely linked to the ESR1 gene, which controls cell division and proliferation. Nevertheless, no research has been performed to investigate the relationship between this gene and Bangladeshi women's BC risk. Therefore, this case‐control study attempted to determine the association between ESR1 polymorphisms and breast cancer susceptibility.MethodsThis study genotyped two ESR1 genetic variants (rs2234693 and rs9340799) in a total of 399 participants (201 cases and 198 controls) by applying the PCR‐RFLP method. SPSS software (v25.0) was used for statistical analysis, and SHEsis web program and Haploview (v4.2) were used for linkage disequilibrium and haplotype analyses, respectively.ResultsA statistically significant association of rs2234693 variant with grade 3 tumor (OR = 0.17, p = 0.0004) and rs9340799 variant with overweight (OR = 0.46, p = 0.034), infiltrating breast cancer (OR = 0.25, p < 0.0001), and positive lymph node status (OR = 2.34, p = 0.005) were observed. The rs2234693 variant was also significantly associated with breast cancer in various models, including codominant 1 (OR = 0.60, p = 0.045), codominant 2 (OR = 0.50, p = 0.021), dominant (OR = 0.56, p = 0.020), and allelic model (OR = 0.73, p = 0.028). In the case of rs9340799 variant, no significant link was observed for breast cancer. Haplotype analysis revealed a significant correlation of TA and CA haplotypes with breast cancer susceptibility (OR = 1.37, p = 0.028 and OR = 0.41, p = 0.0002, respectively). Besides, both rs2234693 and rs9340799 are found to be in strong linkage disequilibrium (D' = 1.00, r2 = 0.697).ConclusionsIn this study, a significant correlation of ESR1 rs2234693 and rs9340799 genetic variants with breast cancer susceptibility has been observed. The findings of this study need to be investigated further with larger sample sizes to confirm the association. A detailed investigation of understanding the functional role of polymorphisms is also required.Keywords: breast cancer (BC), ESR1 gene, haplotypes, PCR‐RFLP, polymorphisms

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is the most often diagnosed malignancy in women. In 2020, more than two million women were identified with BC, which was about 11.7% of total cancer cases in the world. It is also considered the 5th leading cause of death worldwide, with approximately 6.9% of deaths [1]. Statistics show that the prevalence of BC has been rapidly rising in South Asian countries for the last few years. A recent report revealed that Bangladeshi women are increasingly being diagnosed with BC, with an incidence rate of 22.5 per 100,000 women, which is concerning [2]. Patients' age, hormone concentration, previous family history, ancestry, lifestyle, and environmental variables have all been linked to a higher risk of BC [3]. Despite the fact that the exact cause of BC is unknown, it is largely assumed that genetic factors have a substantial impact. The genetic factor is estimated to be responsible for around 5%–10% of all BC cases [4].

There are two types of cancer‐associated genes, like tumor suppressor genes and proto‐oncogenes. Normally, these genes prevent the occurrence of cancer by repairing cell damage, regulating cell division, apoptosis, and uncontrolled cell growth. Cancer arises as a result of the mutation of these genes. Tumor suppressor genes lost their function while proto‐oncogenes converted to the oncogenes due to the mutation of these genes [5]. According to genome‐wide association studies (GWASs), several single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) are implicated in the prevalence and progression of BC. Multiple genetic polymorphisms in different genes, including BRCA1, BRCA2, BACH1, ACYP2, CHEK2, PTEN, PALB2, and ESR found to play a crucial impact in the progression of BC in women [6, 7].

The amount of endogenous hormones, particularly estrogen and androgen, is one of the most prominent risk factors for BC. Estrogen exposure is a recognized risk factor for BC etiology and development, according to studies, since it interacts with estrogen receptors (ERs) and changes the expression of downstream genes [8]. Besides, estrogen metabolites have a strong genotoxic effect on DNA damage, mutation, and cellular transformation. There are two known ERs, estrogen receptor alpha (ESR1), with eight exons and positioned on 6q25.1. Another is the estrogen receptor beta (ESR2), which is found on chromosome 14q22‐24 and functions as a tumor suppressor [9, 10]. ESR1 regulates cell proliferation and differentiation by a hormonal mechanism known as the paracrine mechanism, which is tightly associated with BC development [11]. Genetic variants in human ESR1 have been extensively studied for their role in the susceptibility to various malignancies (e.g., lung, breast, prostate, cervical, ovarian, leiomyoma, gallbladder, and endometrial cancers) in people of multiple ethnicities [12, 13, 14, 15, 16]. Two frequently studied polymorphisms (rs2234693 and rs9340799) in the ESR1 gene are found to be associated with the development of BC [17, 18].

BC development is thought to be closely linked to the ESR1 gene, which controls cell division and proliferation. Besides, the correlation between the ESR1 gene, particularly the rs2234693 and rs9340799 polymorphisms, and BC risk has been discovered in several ethnic groups from different corners of the world. Nevertheless, no research has been performed to investigate the relationship between this gene and Bangladeshi women's BC risk. Therefore, the current case‐control study attempted to determine whether there is a link between ESR1 rs2234693 and rs9340799 genetic polymorphisms and BC susceptibility in Bangladeshi populations. The findings of this study may reveal a new array for the development of precision medicine.

Materials and Methods

Recruitment of Study Participants

We conducted this case‐control study at the Laboratory of Pharmacogenomics and Molecular Biology, Noakhali Science and Technology University, Bangladesh, between 2019 and 2020. In this study, we recruited 201 women diagnosed with BC as cases and 198 healthy women as controls. Blood samples were collected from the National Institute of Cancer Research and Hospital (NICRH), Bangladesh. An ethically approved protocol and a consent form were used for the clinical investigation from the NICRH (NICRH/Ethics/2019/446). All the relevant information of cases (demographic and clinicopathological features) was collected through a detailed preformed questionnaire by authors in the presence of the physicians. The study followed the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki and further amendments [19]. All subjects signed the informed consent. This study excluded healthy individuals under the age of 18 who were unable to supply the required data. This study also excluded participants with chronic conditions like kidney, liver, or lung disease.

DNA Extraction and Primer Design

We collected 3 mL of blood specimens from all cases and controls that were kept in EDTA‐Na2‐containing sterile tubes at −80°C until they were used for DNA extraction. A method established by Islam et al. (2013) was applied to extract genomic DNA [20]. We carried out the genotyping using all subjects' extracted DNA, followed by amplifying target DNA fragments by PCR. DNA purity and concentration were also measured in all the samples. A micro‐volume spectrophotometer (Genova Nano, Jenway) was used to calculate the DNA purity and concentration at an absorbance ratio set at 260 and 280 nm. Primer sequences were designed by Primer Blast following the MIQE guidelines [21] and are shown in Table 1.

Method Validation and SNP Genotyping

To complete the genotyping of rs2234693 and rs9340799 SNPs, the PCR‐based restriction fragment length polymorphism (RFLP) technique was used. Briefly, a working mix for PCR was prepared by adding EmeraldAmp GT PCR Master Mix (2x) and two designed complementary primers (forward and reverse) at a suitable concentration. Then, 20 µL of the working mix was taken in a PCR tube, and a 1 µL DNA sample was mixed in it, making a final volume of 21 µL. Table 1 shows the PCR conditions to amplify the rs2234693 and rs9340799 of the ESR1 gene and the respective length of PCR products. The gel electrophoresis (1% agarose) method was used to analyze PCR products of desired fragments. After confirming the selected PCR fragments, the respective PCR products were digested with specific restriction endonucleases (RE) for RFLP. The required digestion enzyme, condition of digestion, and the expected fragment lengths of both SNPs are listed in Table 1. After digestion, electrophoresis was done for the digested products using 1.3% agarose gel to get the RFLP products of rs2234693 and rs9340799 (Figures 1 and 2, respectively). 100 bp DNA ladder was used to estimate all RE digestion fragments, allowing the accurate and reliable genotyping of samples.

Statistical Analysis

Hardy–Weinberg Equilibrium (HWE) and associated risk were assessed using chi‐square (χ 2) test and odds ratio (OR) at 95% confidence interval (CI) (Table S1). Genotype and allelic frequencies were reported as a percentage. The targeted polymorphisms were compared with several clinicopathological features to evaluate the association. A significant association was considered at a p‐value of < 0.05. Furthermore, SHEsis web‐based program and Haploview (version 4.2) were used for linkage disequilibrium and haplotype analyses, respectively. SPSS software (version 25.0) was used for all statistical analyses.

Results

Demographic and Clinicopathological Features

The demographic and clinical characteristics of included 201 BC patients are listed in Table 2. The age of majority of patients was above 36 years old (67.16%). In terms of body mass index (BMI), the percentages of normal and obese patients were the same (33.33%). Patients with overweight were also high (28.86%). The highest percentages of patients with other characteristics were as follows: invasive breast cancer (61.69%), histological grade 2 (57.21%), T2 tumor size (42.79%), lymph node‐positive (65.67%), ER‐ (59.20%), PR‐ (60.70%) and HER2‐ (63.68%). N1 nodal state (46.77%) was the most prevalent among the patients recruited in the study. These patients were mostly (66.17%) diagnosed with metastasized cancer (Mx).

Linkage of Genotypes of ESR1 Polymorphisms With BC

The genotype distribution for both ESR1 rs2234693 and rs9340799 genetic variants was reported to be compatible with HWE (p > 0.05) (Table S1). The association of rs2234693 and rs9340799 variants with the susceptibility of BC is illustrated in Table 3. Analysis of the different genetic models revealed that rs2234693 variant was significantly associated with BC susceptibility, including codominant model 1 (TC vs. TT: OR = 0.60, CI = 0.36–0.99, p = 0.045), codominant model 2 (CC vs. TT: OR = 0.50, CI = 0.28–0.90, p = 0.021), dominant model (TC + CC vs. TT: OR = 0.56, CI = 0.35–0.92, p = 0.020), and allelic model (C vs. T: OR = 0.73, CI = 0.55–0.97, p = 0.028). The recessive model (CC vs TC + TT: OR = 0.73, CI = 0.46–1.15, p = 0.172) and Overdominant model (TC vs TT + CC: OR = 0.85, CI = 0.58–1.27, p = 0.445) did not show any significant association (p > 0.05).

Linkage of Haplotypes of ESR1 Polymorphisms With BC

Three possible haplotypes of ESR1 rs2234693 and rs9340799 variants were analyzed for BC risk (Table 3). Among them, TA haplotype showed a significant linkage with the higher risk of BC (OR = 1.37, CI = 1.03–1.81, p = 0.028), while CA haplotype revealed a significant but reduced linkage with BC risk (OR = 0.41, CI = 0.26–0.66, p = 0.0002). The CG haplotype did not show any statistically significant risk (p > 0.05). In addition, both SNPs are reported to be in strong linkage disequilibrium (D' = 1.00 and r2 = 0.697) in the BC cases and controls (Figure 3).

Linkage of ESR1 Polymorphisms With Clinicopathological Features of BC Patients

This study also analyzed the link of both rs2234693 and rs9340799 polymorphisms with clinicopathological characteristics of BC patients, as shown in Table 4. A significant link between the genotype distribution of ESR1 rs2234693 polymorphism with histological grade 3 of BC (OR = 0.17, CI = 0.06–0.45, p = 0.0004) was observed. For ESR1 rs9340799 variant, a significant association with several clinical features, including overweight (OR = 0.46, CI = 0.22–0.94, p = 0.034), infiltrating cancer type (OR = 0.25, CI = 0.14–0.46, p < 0.0001), and positive lymph node status (OR = 2.34, CI = 1.30–4.32, p = 0.005) was identified in Bangladeshi BC patients. No significant association (p > 0.05) was observed for other reported clinicopathological features.

Discussion

Genetic variation in different genes plays a critical role in the advancement of BC [7, 22]. The analysis of these genetic variations and the susceptibility of BC is essential to figure out the exact role in tumorigenesis that will be helpful for the treatment selection of BC patients [23]. GWASs have recognized the genetic variation in enormous genes that forecast the risk of the advancement of BC [24]. Several case‐control studies have been conducted in different countries to explore the linkage of ESR1 gene polymorphisms with BC risk [18, 25, 26, 27, 28]. To verify the contribution of ESR1 polymorphisms in BC, we have performed the present case‐control study for the first time in the Bangladeshi population. Notably, our study found a significant correlation between the ESR1 variants with the risk of BC.

Estrogen receptors are hormone‐regulated transcription factors and are crucial for expressing multiple genes that promote cell proliferation, leading to BC development [29, 30]. So, ER status is an essential biomarker for the prediction and prognosis of BC. Activating the mutations in the ESR1 gene activates the ligand‐independent ER that stimulates the development of BC [31]. In this study, we discovered that patients aged above 36 (67.16%) are more prone to BC. TNM status observation showed that the majority of patients had T2 tumor size (42.79%), positive lymph node (65.67%), N1 nodal status (46.77%), and Mx distant metastases (66.17%). The most common type of BC was the invasive type (61.69%), and the most common grade was 2. (57.21%). The highest percentage of patients had ER (59.20%), PR (60.70%), and HER2 (63.68%) negative status.

The different genetic models used for evaluating the association showed that ESR1 rs2234693 was correlated in BC development in codominant model 1 (OR = 0.60, p = 0.045), codominant model 2 (OR = 0.50, p = 0.021), dominant model (OR = 0.56, p = 0.020), and allelic model (OR = 0.73, p = 0.028). In the case of ESR1 rs9340799, our genotypic study failed to demonstrate any significant link with BC susceptibility (p > 0.05) in the Bangladeshi population. However, the frequencies of T and A alleles of ESR1 rs2234693 and ESR1 rs9340799, respectively, were predominant in the Bangladeshi BC patients, similar to the previous studies [18, 32, 33]. Some studies also reported a significant association between both of the studied variants and BC progression [18, 32].

Besides, many studies found only the relationship between the rs2234693 variant and BC risk. Notably, our genotypic study found a significant linkage of the genotype distribution of rs2234693 with BC risk; however, no association was found between the genotype distribution of rs9340799 and BC risk in the Bangladeshi population. Our finding was supported by other studies conducted on Jordanian, Portuguese, Mexican, Chinese, and Indian populations [25, 33, 34, 35]. Different studies revealed that age, tumor cell size, lymph node status, stage of cancer, histological grade, pathological types, estrogen receptor status, progesterone‐receptor status, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 status are all important clinicopathological prognostic factors that have been linked to BC prognosis [36, 37]. Besides, chronic inflammation, insulin function, adipokine balance, and estrogen signaling are influenced by obesity, overweight, and excess fat mass and promote cancer formation [38]. Lymph node status has also been identified previously as a vital prognostic factor in BC. Studies showed that node‐positive individuals had a 40% lower overall survival rate than node‐negative individuals [39].

Our study also tried to figure out the genotype distribution of ESR1 rs2234693 and rs9340799 polymorphisms to clarify the role of these polymorphisms in the clinicopathologic state of BC. For the rs2234693 polymorphism, the present study found a significant association with only histological grade 3 of cancer (OR = 0.17, p = 0.0004). Other clinicopathological parameters were found to have no significant correlation. Again, several clinicopathological features, including overweight (OR = 0.46, p = 0.034), infiltrating cancer type (OR = 0.25, p < 0.0001), and positive lymph node status (OR = 2.34, p = 0.005) were strongly associated with ESR1 rs9340799 variant.

Haplotype analysis of rs2234693 and rs9340799 with the risk of BC also demonstrated that TA haplotype showed a significantly increased linkage with the risk of BC (OR = 1.37, p = 0.028). Besides, CA haplotype showed a significant linkage with the lowered BC risk (OR = 0.41, p = 0.0002). Moreover, both SNPs are found to be in strong linkage disequilibrium (D' = 1.00 and r2 = 0.697). A previous meta‐analysis also reported the association of ESR1 gene polymorphisms with BC risk [40].

Our study has some drawbacks that must be acknowledged for future correspondence. Firstly, we only used publicly available ESR1 gene SNPs that were not unique. Secondly, the relatively small sample size did not represent the whole scenario of all the BC patients in Bangladesh. Thirdly, clinicopathological features were also insufficient to describe the actual scenario of BC patients and their linkage with the genotype distribution of the respective gene. Above all, this current study has provided a clear indication between ESR1 gene polymorphisms and the risk of BC development in the Bangladeshi population.

Conclusions

In this study, a significant correlation of ESR1 rs2234693 and rs9340799 genetic variants with breast cancer susceptibility has been observed. Different clinicopathological features like overweight, infiltrating cancer type, and lymph node‐positive status are also significantly involved with breast cancer risk. Recent developments in targeted therapeutics and precision medicine show promise for the management and treatment of breast cancer. The ESR1 gene, therefore, will become a promising therapeutic target for developing personalized medicine. Further research into the ESR1 gene and its linkage to breast cancer risk may reveal new cancer markers and facilitate the development of personalized treatment approaches. Therefore, the findings of this study need to be investigated further with larger sample sizes to confirm the association. The authors also suggest a detailed investigation of other polymorphisms of ESR1 for the progression of breast cancer.

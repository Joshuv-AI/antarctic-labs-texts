Transformer-assisted hierarchical deep reinforcement learning for energy and spectrum efficient MIMO-MC-CDMA in 6G networks
Authors: K. R. Saranya, Y. Suganya, L. Josephine Usha, P. Valarmathi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
TH-DRL is proposed for optimizing spectral and energy efficiency in 6G MIMO-MC-CDMA systems. Unlike existing flat DRL or optimization-based approaches, the proposed TH-DRL framework uniquely integrates a lightweight Transformer encoder with a hierarchical decision-making architecture to jointly optimize subcarrier allocation, power control, and SIC ordering. Architecturally, the structure integrates a Transformer encoder with a two-tier hierarchical DRL model to enhance adaptability in dynamic wireless conditions. The Transformer learns spatiotemporal dependencies from channel state information, interference patterns, and user dynamics to generate context-aware features that is used for making effective decisions. A high-level policy-gradient agent handles subcarrier allocation and user clustering, while a low-level DQN agent manages power control and successive interference cancellation order, jointly improving throughput and energy efficiency. Convergence, scalability, and detection performance are evaluated based on Rayleigh channels at 28/100 GHz with bandwidths of 400 MHz–1 GHz serving a varying number of users (10–100) served by a base station equipped with 64–256 antennas. Training consists of a replay buffer of samples within a range of 104–10⁶ over 5000–10,000 episodes. The results showed that the convergence is stable around episode 600 with consistent gain over the baseline methods achieving 15–18% higher spectral efficiency, up to 22% energy savings, and peak performance at SE = 32.7 bits/s/Hz, EE = 14.8 bits/J, SINR ≈ 34 dB, and BER ≈ 10⁻5. This confirms that Transformer-enhanced hierarchical DRL offers scalable, low-latency, energy-aware resource management for dense 6G networks.

Introduction

The rapid evolution of wireless communication has resulted in next-generation networks with 6G following 5G1. To address increasing requirements for high data rates, ultra-low latency, and energy efficiency in communications, researchers are working on frontier technologies to enhance spectrum efficiency and power consumption2. Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) with Multi-Carrier Code Division Multiple Access (MC-CDMA) is one of the most important enablers of 6G, as it enhances spectral efficiency by enabling several users to communicate in parallel on various frequency subcarriers3. However, increasing network density and complexity, interference, wastage of energy, and dynamic spectrum allocation are important issues. Integration of Edge AI—facilitating smart, real-time decision-making at the network edge can potentially revolutionize wireless communication by reducing latency and making optimal use of network resources4. To unlock the full potential of these innovations, new approaches need to be formulated to overcome interference, promote energy efficiency, and dynamically allocate the spectrum based on network conditions.

In previous studies, the technology used in the 6G wireless communication mainly focused on MIMO and MC-CDMA as the means to improve capacity and reliability. Therefore, interference mitigation techniques as Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC) have been proposed to enhance a signal detection and to decrease the bit error rates (BER)5. Both machine learning and optimization have been used in the improvement of the efficiency of spectrum and power6. However, such methods are based on heuristics and fail to address various and dynamically changing network systems. Furthermore, deep learning solutions are needed for resource optimization; however, they are centralized causing high latency and high-power consumption7. One of the major challenging factors is the absence of effective DRL frameworks that can learn resource management strategies for 6G networks in real-time8. Moreover, the employed SIC techniques although have reasonable performance have reliability problems in terms of computation complexity and convergence in large scale MIMO MC-CDMA systems and useful in ultra-dense networks. While OFDMA and its variants are currently used in 5G networks, with a potential for enhanced variants of OFDMA in early 6G networks, MC-CDMA remains a promising research target for beyond-5G ultra-dense networks with ultra-high user mobility. Unlike OFDMA, MC-CDMA inherently supports frequency diversity gain, which is beneficial in ultra-dense networks with severe fading, mmWave blockage, and ultra-high interference. This is especially relevant for ultra-dense 6G networks that will be deployed at 28–100 GHz frequency bands. Moreover, the objective of this work is not to replace OFDMA-based 6G NR networks, but rather to explore the potential for using AI-assisted hierarchical optimization for interference-limited multi-carrier networks with ultra-high user density. Therefore, MC-CDMA is used as a challenging multi-user interference network for demonstrating the effectiveness of using Transformer-assisted hierarchical DRL under extreme interference conditions that will be present in ultra-dense 6G networks.

To solve these problems, this paper presents a novel framework called Transformer-Assisted Hierarchical Deep Reinforcement Learning (TH-DRL) specifically designed for the 6G MIMO–MC-CDMA systems. The use of a lightweight Transformer encoder allows the proposed method to detect the temporal and cross-user correlations in different forms of data, i.e., channel state information, interference history, and traffic dynamics. Based on this enriched state representation, a hierarchical DRL architecture decomposes the complex resource allocation problem into two coordinated levels: a high-level agent responsible for subcarrier allocation and user clustering, and a low-level agent that performs fine-grained power control and SIC ordering. This two-level approach breaks down the resource-allocation dilemma and break it down into smaller sub-tasks, so that convergence is easier and scalability is improved. Moreover, the Transformer encoder increases policy update temporal sensitivity and stability to enable the agent predict network fluctuations. This paper offers a new and computationally efficient route to adaptive and energy-sensitive communication systems which can be used in the next-generation wireless network to implement edges in real-time.

Problem statement

The increased demand for high-speed, low-latency, and energy-efficient wireless communication has resulted in extensive research on advanced multiple access and signal processing techniques for next generation networks. Although several computer-aided optimization techniques-from deep learning-based channel coding9, hybrid cognitive radio networks10, AI-powered resource allocation11 still have high computational overhead and scalability constraints. Resource allocation schemes for MIMO systems based on intelligent surface technologies12 and interference-aware massive access schemes are superior cases that enhance spectral efficiency, but they need further fine-tuning for dynamic scenarios. To address these issues, a TH-DRL architecture is designed to facilitate Edge-AI-enabled MIMO -MC-CDMA systems where temporal state encoding is performed with Transformers and hierarchical decision learning is carried out. The purpose of this paradigm is to dynamically respond to varying conditions of the channels, provide spectrum and energy efficiency, reduce the decoding complexity due to intelligent SIC, and hence be a realistic and scalable solution in future 6G deployments.

Methodology of the proposed transformer-assisted hierarchical deep reinforcement learning framework for spectrum and energy-efficient MIMO–MC-CDMA systems in 6G

The proposed framework TH-DRL aims to achieve the maximum energy and spectral efficiency of 6G MIMO-MC-CDMA systems by means of intelligent multi-level optimization. Multi-carrier spreading and hybrid beamforming are the starting point of the process and allow transmission to multiple users simultaneously and reduce multi-path fading. The Transformer encoder captures and encodes CSI, interference, and queue dynamics in a continuous manner which derives both time-based and location-based correlations that are important in resource optimisation. These encoded codes are then fed into a hierarchical structure of DRL: the high-level agent assigns subcarriers and constitutes user clusters to distribute bandwidth use and interference to maximize SE. The low-level agent, optimizes the power allocation and ordering of SIC to reduce the burden of energy wastage and enhance the EE of transmission. The combined reward is used to optimize the SE, EE and latency and trade-off between the throughput and power consumption. In training, a diffusion-based exploration and an adaptive replay buffer can be used to convergence and stability in the 6G dynamical conditions. By maintaining active interaction with the environment, the framework acquires the ability to dynamically distribute spectrum and power resources and provides high data rates at low energy consumption hence energy-spectrum-efficiency and low-latency communication at 6G intelligent networks. Transformer-based state representation

In the traditional deep reinforcement learning (DRL) models, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) or recurrent neural networks (RNNs) are usually used to encode the information of channel state and interference. However, these models exhibit fundamental weaknesses in modeling temporal dependencies over long terms in multiple subcarriers and users that are essential in dynamic 6G MIMO-MC-CDMA systems. The CNNs tend to only learn local features in space whereas the RNNs can be weak at long sequence vanishing gradient problems and consequently cannot effectively retain historical channel information.

To address these constraints, the suggested framework uses a lightweight Transformer encoder that is capable of capturing parallel temporal and cross-user correlations. The system state, comprising the instantaneous information on the channel state (CSI), interference history and queue-length information, is fed into the Transformer to produce context-aware embeddings, which become the input of the hierarchical DRL agents.

In order for the DRL agent to make informed decisions, it is imperative to include information not only about physical layer channel state but also traffic dynamics into state representation. As a result, the suggested TH-DRL framework is capable of co-optimizing spectral efficiency, energy efficiency, and latency, and dynamically responding to time-varying 6G channel conditions, which is why it is a scalable solution to real-time MIMO-MC-CDMA communications.

Unified reward formulation

In the TH-DRL framework that has been suggested, the resource allocation process is influenced by a single reward function that combines SE, EE, and queueing delay into one performance measure. The Energy-Spectrum-Latency Efficiency (ESE) reward offers a multi-objective optimization path for both high-level and low-level agents. A joint reward is formulated to strike an optimal balance between spectral efficiency, energy efficiency, and latency. The reward at time t is presented in Eq. TH-DRL for Spectrum & Energy-Efficient 6G MIMO–MC-CDMA

The proposed TH-DRL framework introduces a novel, end-to-end approach for joint spectrum- and energy-efficient resource allocation in 6G MIMO-MC-CDMA systems. The framework proposes three main novelties, in contrast to the existing resource allocation schemes of 6G based on AI or DRL that are dependent on flat decision-making or static feature representations. Firstly, a lightweight Transformer encoder is utilized to capture the long-term inter-user and temporal dependencies in channel state information, interference history, and queue dynamics that the conventional CNN- or RNN-based DRL models could not represent effectively. Secondly, the hierarchical DRL structure separates the complex joint optimization problem into coarse-grained (subcarrier allocation and user clustering) and fine-grained (power allocation and SIC ordering) decisions that, in turn, significantly reduce action-space dimensionality and increase scalability. Third, the unified ESE reward gives meeting of spectral efficiency, energy efficiency, and latency optimization at the same time, which has not been the case in previous DRL frameworks for 6G. Through these innovations in combination, the proposed method gains stable convergence, enhanced adaptability, and edge-level real-time feasibility that sets it apart from other AI-based 6G approaches.

Results and discussions

The performance analysis and simulation results of the suggested TH-DRL-based Edge AI-enabled MIMO-MC-CDMA system for 6G networks are presented in this section. The experiments examined interference mitigation, spectral and energy efficiency, and resource management in realistic Rayleigh channel scenarios. Q-value and reward progression are used to assess DRL’s convergence properties, and then comparisons with traditional and cutting-edge baselines are conducted. In order to provide a fair and uniform evaluation, all the methods under evaluation that comprise Traditional SIC, ZF, MMSE, HDRL, and the suggested TH-DRL are tested in the same network environment. Each method has the same number of users, base station antennas, subcarrier configurations, realizations of Rayleigh fading channels, SNR range (0–30 dB), and power limits for transmission. The performance scores of spectral efficiencies, energy efficiency, SINR, BER, and throughput are calculated over the same Monte Carlo channel realizations. This joint configuration makes sure that the performance enhancements come purely from the algorithmic advancements and not from tuning the parameters. The outcomes show that the suggested strategy produces notable gains, suggesting strong adaptability and effective learning behavior. In contrast to traditional and current learning-based approaches, the results unequivocally demonstrate that combining Edge AI with hierarchical DRL achieves superior efficiency and stability.

Discussions

The experimental results indicate that the TH-DRL framework significantly improves 6G MIMO-MC-CDMA performance in terms of spectral efficiency, energy efficiency, SINR, and BER. The Transformer encoder empowers state representation by capturing the subcarrier and user dependencies to be more aware of dynamic channel variations. Coupled with hierarchical DRL, it enables optimal subcarrier assignment, user clustering, power allocation, and SIC ordering, which guarantees scalability and adaptiveness in crowded scenarios. From a computational point of view, the proposed TH-DRL system can reduce the complexity of decision-making by hierarchically decomposing decisions. The action space in a traditional flat DRL-based resource allocation is exponentially related to the user number \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K$$\end{document}, subcarriers \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S$$\end{document}, power \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P$$\end{document} and SIC permutations, which leads to a complexity of the order of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O\left( {K \times S \times P \times K!} \right).$$\end{document} The given hierarchical design, on the contrary, splits this joint optimization into two synchronized steps. The high-level agent functions on a smaller action space, with respect to user grouping and subcarrier grouping whereas the low-level agent functions on power allocation and SIC ordering just to the chosen clusters. This decomposition results in an effective action dimensionality to the order of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O\left( {K \times S} \right) + O\left( {P \times K} \right)$$\end{document} which is much less than the computational cost per decision step.

In order to the lightweight characterization of the proposed Transformer-assisted hierarchical DRL framework, a detailed analysis of the computational complexity and latency of the proposed approach was performed. The Transformer encoder architecture utilizes an embedding dimension of 128 along with 4 attention heads while processing a temporal sequence of length 10 corresponding to the CSI history and interference history. The computational complexity of the multi-head self-attention mechanism can be derived as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$o\left( {T^{2} d + Td^{2} } \right)$$\end{document}. The attention mechanism requires 1.76 × 105 FLOPs while processing the input sequence of length 10 using an embedding dimension of 128. The Transformer encoder architecture also includes the linear projection layer and the feed-forward neural network (FFNN) layer of size 128–256-128. The total computational complexity of the Transformer encoder architecture is 1.4 × 10⁶ FLOPs per scheduling step. The hierarchical DRL agent also contributes to the overall computational latency of the proposed approach. The high-level policy network architecture of size 256–128-64 contributes 0.6 × 10⁶ FLOPs, while the low-level DQN agent contributes 0.7 × 10⁶ FLOPs. Therefore, the overall inference latency of the proposed per decision step is approximately 2.7 × 10⁶ FLOPs. The total number of parameters of the proposed approach memory footprint is less than 19 MB (FP32 data type) since the proposed approach can be deployed on embedded accelerators such as the NVIDIA Jetson-class edge GPU. Compared to the existing CNN-based DRL approach that exceeds 10⁷ FLOPs for every inference step, the proposed TH-DRL approach reduces the overall computational latency of the approach while maintaining superior performance in terms of spectral/energy optimization performance.

To validate the strength of the unified Energy Spectrum Latency Efficiency (ESE) reward formulation, a sensitivity analysis was done by adjusting the weighting coefficients (alpha, beta, gamma) using the same conditions of the simulation (the same number of users and antennas, the same bandwidth, the same SNR, and the same channel realizations). At the base setting (0.4, 0.4, 0.2), the system had balanced spectral efficiency, energy efficiency and delay performance. The model showed better throughput and spectral efficiency when α (spectral efficiency weight) was increased, and 2 (spectral efficiency) and 3 (energy consumption) were also increased proportionally, and the model had a slightly higher energy consumption and queue delay. On the other hand as β (energy efficiency weight) increased, the agent acquired more conservative power allocation policies which led to an increase in energy efficiency with a slight decrease in spectral efficiency. On the same note, the average queue length and delay were minimized as γ (latency penalty weight) was increased but there was a small trade-off in throughput. Notably, training with varying weight arrangements did not show any presence of instability or separation and convergence behaviour was also similar. These findings support the fact that the proposed ESE reward formulation allows controllable and constant trade-offs between spectral efficiency, energy efficiency, and latency, and thus, the TH-DRL framework can be readily adjusted to various 6G service needs eMBB, mMTC and URLLC. Multi-objective reward maximizes SE and EE and minimizes delay, offering balanced performance for sustainable 6G communication. Stable convergence in the DQN and PPO learning curves illustrates suitability for real-time edge deployment. Comparative results confirm large gains: SE = 32.7 bits/s/Hz, EE = 14.8 bits/J, SINR = 34 dB, and BER = 10⁻5 outperforming state-of-the-art deep-learning-based methods of MIMO detection by more than 30%. The high SINR indicates strong interference suppression through adaptive SIC and power control, while the improved EE reflects reduced power use. Its robust performance under varying user loads underlines its relevance for densely populated, URLLC-driven 6G networks.

Conclusion and future work

This work has proposed a TH-DRL framework to optimize spectral and energy efficiency in 6G MIMO-MC-CDMA systems. The architecture combines a lightweight Transformer encoder with a two-level DRL hierarchy that manages subcarrier allocation, user clustering, SIC ordering, and power control. The Transformer enhances state representation by capturing temporal channel and interference correlations, while the hierarchical DRL agents enable faster convergence, efficient edge decision-making, and robust adaptability in dense and dynamic environments. The findings indicate that AI-assisted resource management is an effective, energy-efficient, and scalable approach for interference-resilient 6G networks. The study emphasizes AI’s role in enhancing spectrum use, reducing energy consumption, and establishing sustainable next-generation communication systems.

Despite the strong performance demonstrated, there are some limitations of the proposed TH-DRL framework. Hierarchical learning architecture and Transformer-based state encoding present new computational and memory overhead, which can be difficult to implement on resource constrained edge devices. Moreover, the model might not be able to be generalized to unobservable network settings or hardware configurations, and additional simulation in a variety of cases might be required. Future research will explore the study of lightweight versions of Transformer, federated or distributed training, and hardware-aware optimizations in attempts to alleviate these limitations so that it can allow practical real-time execution in 6G networks.

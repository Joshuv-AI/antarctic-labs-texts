Doppler Resilient Complementary Sequences: Tighter Aperiodic Ambiguity Function Bound and Optimal Constructions
Zheng Wang, Yang Yang, Zhengchun Zhou, Avik Ranjan Adhikary, Pingzhi Fan

Abstract
Doppler-resilient complementary sequence sets (DRCSs) are crucial in modern communication and sensing systems in mobile environments. In this paper, we propose a new lower bound for the aperiodic ambiguity function (AF) of unimodular DRCSs based on weight vectors, which generalizes the existing bound as a special case. The proposed lower bound is tighter than the Shen-Yang-Zhou-Liu-Fan bound. Finally, we propose a novel class of aperiodic DRCSs with small alphabets based on quasi-Florentine rectangles and Butson-type Hadamard matrices. Interestingly, the proposed DRCSs asymptotically satisfy the proposed bound.



I
Introduction

In the 1960s, Golay introduced Golay complementary pairs (GCPs)
[
1
]
, whose aperiodic autocorrelations sum up to zero at each non-zero time-shift. In
[
2
]
, Tseng and Liu extended the idea of GCP to complementary sequence sets (CSs) and introduced mutually orthogonal complementary sequence sets (MOCSS), where the sum of aperiodic autocorrelation at a non-zero time-shift is zero, and the sum of aperiodic cross-correlation at any time-shift is also zero. Due to their ideal correlation properties, CSs/MOCSSs are widely used in various applications, including channel estimation
[
3
]
, spreading codes for asynchronous multicarrier code-division multiple-access (MC-CDMA) communications
[
4
]
, reducing peak-to-average power ratio (PAPR) in orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) systems
[
5
]
, Doppler-resilient waveform design
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
, integrated sensing and communication (ISAC)
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
, and more.
However, the size of MOCSS is upper-bounded by the number of constituent sequences, which limits their applicability in systems that require a large number of users. To address this limitation, one approach is to allow the sum of aperiodic correlations of complementary sequences to be a small value, rather than zero. This concept is known as a quasi-complementary sequence set (QCSS)
[
10
]
. In
[
11
]
, Welch proposed a bound for QCSSs using the inner product theorem, and in
[
10
]
, Liu, Guan, and Mow presented a tighter aperiodic correlation bound based on the Levenshtein bound
[
12
]
.

However, modern communication systems must address the Doppler effect caused by high mobility. Traditional sequence designs that focus solely on the correlation function are inefficient for current application scenarios. For modern applications such as joint communication and radar systems, sequences must not only mitigate the interference caused by the channel delay but also be resilient to Doppler interference; such sequences are referred to as Doppler-resilient sequences (DRSs)
[
13
]
. This results to the performance metric being shifted from correlation function to ambiguity function (AF). Several researchers have explored the lower bounds on the maximum AF magnitude. In 2013, Ding
et al.

[
14
]
derived the lower bound for the maximum sidelobe of the AF based on the Welch bound. It is important to note that in many practical applications (e.g.,
[
15
,
16
]
), the Doppler frequency range and delay range can be significantly smaller than the bandwidth of the transmitted signal and the sequence duration. Motivated by this, Ye
et al.

[
13
]
introduced the concept of low/zero ambiguity zones (ZAZ/LAZ) and derived the lower bound for ZAZ/LAZ sequence sets. More recently, Meng
et al.

[
17
]
proposed tighter theoretical bounds for the aperiodic AF under specific parameters. Additionally, several DRS sets have been designed that satisfy the proposed AF lower bound.

Based on CSs, Pezeshki
et al.

[
6
]
designed Doppler-resilient Golay complementary waveforms (DRGCWs) by utilizing GCPs and the Prouhet–Thue–Morse (PTM) sequence for pulse radar applications.
The core idea of DRGCW is to repeatedly transmit GCPs over a pulse sequence by leveraging the structure of the PTM sequence. Consequently, the Taylor expansion of its AF near zero Doppler exhibits higher-order zeros, leading to a Doppler-resilient AF. Subsequently, extensive research has been reported in this direction, such as
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
15
,
21
]
.
Notably, DRGCW assumes that the Doppler shift is significantly smaller than the pulse repetition frequency (PRF) or that the pulse duration is much shorter than the pulse repetition interval (PRI). Based on this assumption, the intra-pulse Doppler shift can be neglected, and only the inter-pulse Doppler shift needs to be considered
[
15
]
. However, in certain cases, this assumption does not hold due to large Doppler shifts. In
[
22
]
, Shen
et al.
proposed a novel transmission approach, assuming that the PRI is an integer multiple of the PRF. The authors transmitted a different sequence within each pulse and accumulated AFs across multiple sequences, thereby achieving lower AF sidelobes. Such sequences are referred to as Doppler-resilient complementary sequence sets (DRCSs) in
[
22
]
. In
[
22
]
, the authors derived lower bounds for the periodic AF, aperiodic AF, and odd periodic AF of DRCSs. Further, the authors in
[
22
]
proposed various constructions, including optimal constructions for periodic DRCSs.
However, the problem of designing sequence sets which achieves the aperiodic AF bound for DRCSs, still remains open.

Motivated by the works of
[
10
]
and
[
22
]
, we propose a new lower bound for the aperiodic auto-AF of DRCSs, which generalizes several previously established bounds, like Levenshtein bound
[
12
]
, Liu-Guan-Mow bound
[
10
]
, Meng-Guan-Ge-Liu-Fan bound
[
17
]
, Liu-Zhou-Udaya bound
[
26
]
, and Peng-Fan bound
[
27
]
, as special cases. Additionally, we analyze three distinct types of weight vectors and their corresponding theoretical aperiodic AF bounds for DRCSs. By selecting an appropriate weight vector, we derive a tighter lower bound than the Shen-Yang-Zhou-Liu-Fan bound
[
22
]
. Furthermore, using the quasi-Florentine rectangles proposed by Adhikary
et al.
in
[
23
]
and Butson-type Hadamard matrices, we introduce a class of DRCSs. Interestingly, the proposed DRCSs satisfy the proposed bound asymptotically, thus solving the open problem posed in
[
22
]
. Furthermore, the proposed construction yields DRCSs with flexible parameters over any fixed alphabet.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section II introduces the necessary notations and lemmas. In Section III, we present a tighter aperiodic lower bound. Section IV analyzes three types of weight vectors. In Section V, we propose a new class of DRCSs with asymptotic achievability. Finally, Section VI concludes the paper.

II-A

Ambiguity Function

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a(0),a(1),\cdots,a(N-1))
and
\mathbf{b}=(b(0),b(1),

\cdots,b(N-1))
be two complex unimodular sequences with period
N,
i.e.,
|a(i)|=1
and
0\leq i<N
.
The aperiodic cross AF of
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
at time shift
\tau
and Doppler
v
at is defined as follows:

\displaystyle AF_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}(\tau,v)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sum_{t=0}^{N-1-\tau}a(t)b^{*}(t+\tau)\omega_{N}^{vt},&0\leq\tau\leq N-1,\\
&\sum_{t=-\tau}^{N-1}a(t)b^{*}(t+\tau)\omega_{N}^{vt},&1-N\leq\tau<0,\\
&0,&|\tau|\geq N.\end{aligned}\right.

If
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b},
then
AF_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}(\tau,\nu)
is called aperiodic auto AF and written as
AF_{\mathbf{a}}(\tau,v)
.

A DRCS set
\mathcal{C}=\left\{\mathbf{C}^{(0)},\mathbf{C}^{(1)},\cdots,\mathbf{C}^{(K-1)}\right\}
contains
K
DRCSs, each of which consists of
M\geq 2
elementary sequences of length
N
, i.e.,
\mathbf{C}^{(k)}=\{\mathbf{c}_{0}^{(k)},\mathbf{c}_{1}^{(k)},\cdots,\mathbf{c}_{M-1}^{(k)}\}
, where
\mathbf{c}_{m}^{(k)}=(c_{m}^{(k)}(0),c_{m}^{(k)}(1),\cdots,c_{m}^{(k)}(N-1))
,
0\leq k<K
and
0\leq m<M
.
For two DRCSs
\mathbf{C}^{(k_{1})}
and
\mathbf{C}^{(k_{2})}
, their aperiodic cross AF is defined as the aperiodic ambiguity function sum, i.e.,

\displaystyle AF_{\mathbf{C}^{(k_{1})},\mathbf{C}^{(k_{2})}}(\tau,v)=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}AF_{\mathbf{c}_{m}^{(k_{1})},\mathbf{c}_{m}^{(k_{2})}}(\tau,v).

When
k_{1}=k_{2}
, it is abbreviated to
AF_{\mathbf{C}^{(k_{1})}}(\tau,v)
.

II-B

Low Ambiguity Zone and Existing Aperiodic DRCS Set Bound

For a DRCS set
\mathcal{C}
, its maximum aperiodic AF magnitude over a region
\Pi=(-Z_{x},Z_{x})\times(-Z_{y},Z_{y})\subseteq(-N,N)\times(-N,N)
is defined as
\theta_{\max}(\mathcal{C})=\max\left\{\theta_{a}(\mathcal{C}),\theta_{c}(\mathcal{C})\right\}
, where

\displaystyle\theta_{a}(\mathcal{C})=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\left|AF_{\mathbf{C}}(\tau,v)\right|:\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{C},(\tau,v)\neq(0,0)\in\Pi\right\},

\displaystyle\theta_{c}(\mathcal{C})=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\left|AF_{\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D}}(\tau,f)\right|:\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D}\in\mathcal{C},(\tau,v)\in\Pi\right\}

are called the maximum aperiodic auto AF magnitude and the maximum aperiodic cross AF magnitude, respectively.
Then,
\mathcal{C}
is referred to as an aperiodic
(K,M,N,\theta_{\max},\Pi)
-DRCS set.

The only existing aperiodic AF lower bound of DRCSs in the literature can be expressed as:

Lemma 1

(
[
22
]
)
.

For a
\left(K,M,N,\theta_{\max},\Pi\right)
-DRCS set, where
\Pi=\left(-Z_{x},Z_{x}\right)\times\left(-Z_{y},Z_{y}\right),1\leq Z_{x},Z_{y}\leq N
, the lower bound of the aperiodic AF is given by

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}\geq\frac{MN}{\sqrt{Z_{y}}}\sqrt{\frac{\frac{KZ_{x}Z_{y}}{M\left(N+Z_{x}-1\right)}-1}{KZ_{x}-1}}.

In the next section, we further tighten this bound for a class of aperiodic DRCSs. To evaluate the closeness between the theoretical and achieved lower bounds of the aperiodic AF, the optimality factor
\rho
is defined as follows.

Definition 1

(Optimality Factor)
.

For a
\left(K,M,N,\theta_{\max},\Pi\right)
-DRCS set, where
\Pi=\left(-Z_{x},Z_{x}\right)\times\left(-Z_{y},Z_{y}\right)
, the aperiodic AF lower bound of DRCSs is denoted as
\theta_{\text{opti}}
, i.e.,
\theta_{\max}\geq\theta_{\text{opti}}
. The optimality factor
\rho
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{\theta_{\max}}{\theta_{\text{opti}}}.

In general,
\rho\geq 1
. We call a DRCS set optimal if
\rho=1
. A DRCS set is asymptotically optimal if
\rho\rightarrow 1
as
N\rightarrow+\infty
.

II-C

Quasi-Florentine Rectangle

Definition 2

(
[
23
]
)
.

A matrix
\mathbf{A}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
is called a
quasi-Florentine rectangle
if it satisfies the following two conditions:

•

C1:
Each row of the matrix contains exactly
N-1
distinct symbols, with every symbol appearing exactly once in each row.

•

C2:
For any ordered pair
(a,b)
of two distinct symbols and for any integer
m
from 1 to
N-2
, there is at most one row in which the symbol
b
appears exactly
m
steps to the right of the symbol
a
.

The quasi-Florentine rectangle is highly useful for constructing DRCSs, and its construction can be found in
[
23
]
. In the following, we will present a construction of the quasi-Florentine rectangle using finite fields.

Construction 1

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime number,
n
be a positive integer,
f(x)
be a primitive polynomial of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Then the set
\{1,\alpha,\alpha^{2},\dots,\alpha^{n-1}\}
forms a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. For any
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
, there exists a vector
(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
such that

\beta=a_{0}+a_{1}\alpha+\dots+a_{n-1}\alpha^{n-1}.

Define a one-to-one mapping
\psi
from
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
to
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, where
\psi(0)=0
and

\psi(\beta)=a_{0}+a_{1}p+\dots+a_{n-1}p^{n-1}.

Theorem 1

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a matrix of order
p^{n}\times(p^{n}-1)
defined as follows:

\mathbf{A}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{0,0}&a_{0,1}&\cdots&a_{0,p^{n}-2}\\
a_{1,0}&a_{1,1}&\cdots&a_{1,p^{n}-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{p^{n}-1,0}&a_{p^{n}-1,1}&\cdots&a_{p^{n}-1,p^{n}-2}\end{array}\right]_{p^{n}\times(p^{n}-1)}

where

a_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\psi(\alpha^{j}),&i=0,\\
\psi(\alpha^{j}+\alpha^{i-1}),&0<i<p^{n},\end{cases}

and
\psi
is a one-to-one mapping as defined in Construction
1
.
Then the matrix
\mathbf{A}
is a quasi-Florentine rectangle of size

over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
.

Corollary 1

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be the matrix defined in Theorem
1
. By adding an extra leftmost column where all elements are

of order

Below we give the important lemma.

Lemma 2

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{0,0}&a_{0,1}&\cdots&a_{0,p^{n}-2}\\

III
Proposed Lower Bounds of DRCSs

In this section, we derive the aperiodic AF lower bound of unimodular DRCSs
\mathcal{C}
. Before deriving the main theorems, let us first give a lemma that show a property of AF in
[
13
]
.

Lemma 3

(
[
13
]
)
.

For any unimodular sequence
\mathbf{a}
, its aperiodic auto-AF satisfies

\displaystyle AF_{\mathbf{a}}(0,v)=0,\quad 0<|v|<N.

(1)

Next, we derive the aperiodic AF lower bound of unimodular DRCSs
\mathcal{C}
in region
\Pi=(-N,N)\times(-Z_{y},Z_{y})
, where
1\leq Z_{y}\leq N
. First, based on Frobenius norm of an auto- and cross-ambiguity matrix, we define satisfy the following conditions two weight vectors
\mathbf{w}=\left[w_{0},w_{1},\cdots,w_{2N-2}\right]^{T}
and
\mathbf{p}=\left[p_{0},p_{1},\cdots,p_{Z_{y}-1}\right]^{T}
, where

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}=1,~w_{r}\geq 0,

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{Z_{y}-1}p_{i}=1,~p_{i}\geq 0.

(3)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
(K,M,N,\theta_{\max},\Pi)
-DRCS set, where
\Pi=(-Z_{x},Z_{y})\times(-Z_{y},Z_{y})
,
1\leq Z_{y}<N
, and
Z_{x}=N
. Consider the matrix

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}=\left[\mathbf{U}_{0}^{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)},\mathbf{U}_{1}^{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)},\ldots,\mathbf{U}_{K-1}^{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}\right]^{T}

(4)

of size
K(2N-1)Z_{y}\times M(2N-1)
, where

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}=\left[\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\left(Z_{x}\right)}(0),\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\left(Z_{x}\right)}(1),\ldots,\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\left(Z_{x}\right)}\left(Z_{y}-1\right)\right]^{T},0\leq k\leq K-1,

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\left(Z_{x}\right)}(v_{i})=\left[\mathbf{u}_{k,1}^{\left(Z_{x}\right)}(v_{i}),\mathbf{u}_{k,2}^{\left(Z_{x}\right)}(v_{i}),\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{k,M}^{\left(Z_{x}\right)}\left(v_{i}\right)\right]^{T},0\leq v_{i}<Z_{y},

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{k,m}^{\left(Z_{x}\right)}(v_{i})=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\sqrt{p_{i}}\sqrt{w_{0}}\text{circ}\left(\mathbf{u}_{k,m}\odot\mathbf{F}_{v_{i}},0\right)\\
\sqrt{p_{i}}\sqrt{w_{1}}\text{circ}\left(\mathbf{u}_{k,m}\odot\mathbf{F}_{v_{i}},1\right)\\
\vdots\\
\sqrt{p_{i}}\sqrt{w_{2N-2}}\text{circ}\left(\mathbf{u}_{k,m}\odot\mathbf{F}_{v_{i}},2N-2\right)\end{array}\right],

where
0\leq m\leq M-1
,
\mathbf{u}_{k,m}=[\mathbf{c}_{m}^{k},\mathbf{0}_{1\times N-1}]
,
\mathbf{F}_{v_{i}}=[1,\omega_{N}^{v_{i}},\omega_{N}^{2v_{i}},\cdots,\omega_{N}^{(N-1)v_{i}},\mathbf{0}_{1\times N-1}]
. Based on the properties of matrix operations, the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}^{H}\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\left\|\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}^{H}\right\|_{F}^{2},

(5)

where

is the Frobenius norm.

Lemma 4
.

Considering the right side of (
5
), the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}^{H}\right\|_{F}^{2}\geq MK^{2}(N-\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\tau_{s,t,N}w_{s}w_{t}),

where
\tau_{s,t,N}=\min\{|t-s|,2N-1-|t-s|\}
.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}^{H}\right\|_{F}^{2}&=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\sum_{i=0}^{Z_{y}-1}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}p_{i}u_{k,m}(s+r)u_{k,m}^{*}(t+r)e^{\frac{j2\pi i\left(s-t\right)}{N}}\right|^{2}\\
&\geq M\sum_{s=0}^{2N-2}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\sum_{i=0}^{Z_{y}-1}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}p_{i}u_{k,0}(s+r)u_{k,0}^{*}(s+r)\right|^{2}\\
&=MK^{2}\sum_{s=0}^{2N-2}\left|\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}u_{0,0}(s+r)u_{0,0}^{*}(s+r)\right|^{2}\\
&=MK^{2}(N-\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\tau_{s,t,N}w_{s}w_{t}),\end{split}

where
s+r
is calculated over
\mathbb{Z}_{2N-1}
,
u_{k,m}(s)=0
for
N\leq s\leq 2N-2
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Considering the left side of (
5
), the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}^{H}\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{KM^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}+K\theta_{a}^{2}-K\theta_{a}^{2}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}+K(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}

(6)

and

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}^{H}\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq K^{2}\theta_{\max}^{2}+\frac{KM^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}-K\theta_{\max}^{2}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}.

(7)

Proof.

The left-hand side term of (
5
) can be expanded as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}^{H}\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}\right\|_{F}^{2}&=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=0}^{K-1}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}=0}^{2N-2}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}=0}^{Z_{y}-1}|AF_{\mathbf{u}_{k},\mathbf{u}_{k^{\prime}}}(\tau_{r,r^{\prime}},v_{i,i^{\prime}})|^{2}p_{i}p_{i^{\prime}}w_{r}w_{r^{\prime}}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}=0}^{2N-2}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}=0}^{Z_{y}-1}|AF_{\mathbf{u}_{k}}(\tau_{r,r^{\prime}},v_{i,i^{\prime}})|^{2}p_{i}p_{i^{\prime}}w_{r}w_{r^{\prime}}\\
&+\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=0,k\neq k_{1}}^{K-1}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}=0}^{2N-2}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}=0}^{Z_{y}-1}\left|AF_{\mathbf{u}_{k},\mathbf{u}_{k^{\prime}}}(\tau_{r,r^{\prime}},v_{i,i^{\prime}})\right|^{2}p_{i}p_{i^{\prime}}w_{r}w_{r^{\prime}}.\end{split}

By Lemma
3
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}=0}^{2N-2}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}=0}^{Z_{y}-1}|AF_{\mathbf{u}_{k}}(\tau_{r,r^{\prime}},v_{i,i^{\prime}})|^{2}p_{i}p_{i^{\prime}}w_{r}w_{r^{\prime}}\\
=&K\sum_{r,r^{\prime}=0}^{2N-2}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}=0}^{Z_{y}-1}|AF_{\mathbf{u}_{k}}(\tau_{r,r^{\prime}},v_{i,i^{\prime}})|^{2}p_{i}p_{i_{1}}w_{r}w_{r_{1}}\\
&+KM^{2}N^{2}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}\sum_{i=0}^{Z_{y}-1}p_{i}^{2}w_{r}^{2}-K\sum_{i,i^{\prime}=0}^{Z_{y}-1}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}|AF_{\mathbf{u}_{k}}(\tau_{r},v_{i,i^{\prime}})|^{2}p_{i}p_{i^{\prime}}w_{r}^{2}.\end{split}

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}^{H}\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}\right\|_{F}^{2}&\leq KM^{2}N^{2}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}\sum_{i=0}^{Z_{y}-1}p_{i}^{2}w_{r}^{2}+K\sum_{r,r^{\prime}=0}^{2N-2}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}=0}^{Z_{y}-1}\theta_{a}^{2}p_{i}p_{i^{\prime}}w_{r}w_{r^{\prime}}\\
&-K\sum_{i,i^{\prime}=0}^{Z_{y}-1}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}\theta_{a}^{2}p_{i}p_{i^{\prime}}w_{r}^{2}+K(K-1)\sum_{r,r^{\prime}=0}^{2N-2}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}=0}^{Z_{y}-1}\theta_{c}^{2}p_{i}p_{i^{\prime}}w_{r}w_{r^{\prime}}\\
&=KM^{2}N^{2}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}\sum_{i=0}^{Z_{y}-1}p_{i}^{2}w_{r}^{2}+K\theta_{a}^{2}-K\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}\theta_{a}^{2}w_{r}^{2}+K(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}.\end{split}

(8)

According to (
3
) and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we can determine that the minimum value

\sum_{r=0}^{Z_{y}-1}p_{r}^{2}
is
\frac{1}{Z_{y}}
, and this minimum value is achieved if and only if

\displaystyle p_{r}=\frac{1}{Z_{y}},0\leq r\leq Z_{y}-1.

(9)

Hence, (
9
) is the optimal choice of weight vector
\mathbf{p}
for (
8
), as it yields the tightest upper bound of

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}^{H}\mathbf{U}_{\left(Z_{x},Z_{y}\right)}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KM^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}+K\theta_{a}^{2}-K\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}\theta_{a}^{2}w_{r}^{2}+K(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}\theta_{\max}^{2}+\frac{KM^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}-K\theta_{\max}^{2}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we present the main conclusion of this paper based on Lemma
4
and Lemma
5
.

Theorem 2
.

For a unimodular
(K,M,N,\theta_{\max},\Pi)
-DRCS set, where
\Pi=(-N,N)\times(-Z_{y},Z_{y})
and
1\leq Z_{y}\leq N
, the lower bounds of the aperiodic AF are given by

\displaystyle\left(1-\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}\right)\theta_{a}^{2}+(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}\geq KM\left(N-\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\tau_{s,t,N}w_{s}w_{t}\right)-\frac{M^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}

(10)

and

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}^{2}\geq M\left(N-\frac{\mathbf{Q}_{2N-1}\left(\mathbf{w},\frac{N(MN-Z_{y})}{KZ_{y}}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}\right)}\right),

(11)

where the quadratic form

\mathbf{Q}_{2N-1}(\mathbf{w},a)\triangleq\mathbf{w}\mathbf{Q}_{2N-1}\mathbf{w}^{T}=a\sum_{i=0}^{2N-2}w_{i}^{2}+\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\tau_{s,t,N}w_{s}w_{t}
,

\mathbf{Q}_{2N-1}
is a
(2N-1)\times(2N-1)
matrix whose diagonal entries are equal to
a
, and for
s\neq t
, its
(s,t)
-th entry is
\tau_{s,t,N}
.

Proof.

From (
5
), Lemma
4
, and (
6
) in Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{KM^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}+K\theta_{a}^{2}-K\theta_{a}^{2}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}+K(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}\geq MK^{2}(N-\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\tau_{s,t,N}w_{s}w_{t}).

Further, we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}\right)\theta_{a}^{2}+(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}\geq KM\left(N-\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\tau_{s,t,N}w_{s}w_{t}\right)-\frac{M^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}.

From (
5
), Lemma
4
, and (
7
) in Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle K^{2}\theta_{\max}^{2}+\frac{KM^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}-K\theta_{\max}^{2}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}\geq MK^{2}(N-\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\tau_{s,t,N}w_{s}w_{t}).

Further, we have

\displaystyle(K-\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2})\theta_{\max}^{2}\geq MK(N-\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\tau_{s,t,N}w_{s}w_{t})-\frac{M^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}

So, we have

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq M\left(\frac{(N-\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\tau_{s,t,N}w_{s}w_{t})-\frac{MN^{2}}{KZ_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}}{\left(1-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=M\left(\frac{(N-\frac{N}{K}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}+\frac{N}{K}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}-\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\tau_{s,t,N}w_{s}w_{t})-\frac{MN^{2}}{KZ_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}}{\left(1-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=M\left(\frac{(N-\frac{N}{K}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}-\sum_{s,t=0}^{2N-2}\tau_{s,t,N}w_{s}w_{t})-\frac{N(MN-Z_{y})}{KZ_{y}}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}}{\left(1-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=M\left(N-\frac{\mathbf{Q}_{2N-1}\left(\mathbf{w},\frac{N(MN-Z_{y})}{KZ_{y}}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}\right)}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

IV
Discussions on the Proposed Lower Bounds for DRCSs

In the previous section, we derived the theoretical lower bound on the sidelobes of the aperiodic AF for DRCSs, which is closely related to the choice of weight vectors. In this section, we discuss three specific weight vectors previously used in
[
12
]
and
[
10
]
, and demonstrate that the proposed theoretical lower bound is tighter than the Shen-Yang-Zhou-Liu-Fan bound.

Next, we consider a uniform weight vector proposed by
[
12
]
, defined as

\displaystyle w_{r}=\frac{1}{2N-1},\quad 0\leq r\leq 2N-2.

(14)

By applying the weight vector in (
14
) to Theorem
2
, we obtain an improved lower bound for DRCSs as follows.

Corollary 2
.

For weight vector (
14
), the lower bounds of the aperiodic AF are given by

\displaystyle\frac{2N}{2N-1}\theta_{a}^{2}+(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}\geq M^{2}N^{2}\frac{KZ_{y}/M-1}{(2N-1)Z_{y}}

(15)

and

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}^{2}\geq M^{2}N^{2}\frac{KZ_{y}/M-1}{[K(2N-1)-1]Z_{y}}.

(16)

Proof.

By applying the weight vector (
14
) to (
10
), we can transform (
10
) into

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{2N-1}\right)\theta_{a}^{2}+(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}\geq KMN\left(N-\frac{N(N-1)}{2N-1}\right)-\frac{M^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}}\frac{1}{2N-1}.

Consequently, (
15
) and (
16
) follow as direct results.
∎

Corollary 3
.

If
K
,
M
,
N
, and
Z_{y}
satisfy the following relation:

\displaystyle K\leq\left\lfloor\frac{4(MN-Z_{y})N}{Z_{y}}\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{2(2N-1)}\right\rfloor,

then the theoretical bound in (
16
) cannot be further improved. In particular, as
N
approaches positive infinity, we have

\displaystyle K\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\pi^{2}M}{4Z_{y}}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Before proving Corollary
3
, let us first review two key results from Berlekamp
[
28
]
:

1)

The principal eigenvalue and the secondary eigenvalues of the quadratic matrix
\mathbf{Q}_{2N-1}
are given by:

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle=a+(N-1)N,

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=a-\frac{1-(-1)^{k}\cos\frac{\pi k}{2N-1}}{2\sin^{2}\frac{\pi k}{2N-1}},

where
1\leq k\leq 2N-2
.

2)

The quadratic function
Q_{2N-1}(\mathbf{w},a)
is convex if all the secondary eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}\geq 0
. In this case, the global minimum is achieved when
\mathbf{w}=\frac{1}{2N-1}(1,1,\dots,1)
. Furthermore, it is evident that the term
1-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=0}^{2N-2}w_{i}^{2}
attains its global maximum when
\mathbf{w}=\frac{1}{2N-1}(1,1,\dots,1)
. Therefore, the right-hand side of (
11
) reaches its global maximum under this choice of
\mathbf{w}
.

Based on the above two results, the bound (
16
) cannot be further improved if

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq k\leq 2N-2}\lambda_{k}=\frac{(MN-Z_{y})N}{KZ_{y}}-\frac{1}{4\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{2(2N-1)}}\geq 0,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle K\leq\left\lfloor\frac{4(MN-Z_{y})N}{Z_{y}}\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{2(2N-1)}\right\rfloor.

This completes the proof.
∎

In the previous, the weight vector was divided into
2N-1
equal parts, ensuring that all time-shifts were taken into account. In this subsection, we consider another uniform weight vector proposed by Levenshtein
[
12
]
, which is defined as

\displaystyle w_{r}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{m},&r\in\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\},\\
0,&r\in\{m,m+1,\ldots,Z_{x}-1\},\end{cases}

(17)

where
1\leq m\leq Z_{x}\leq N
.

Corollary 4
.

For weight vector (
17
), the lower bound of the aperiodic AF is given by

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}^{2}\geq\frac{3mKMNZ_{y}-KM(m^{2}-1)Z_{y}-3M^{2}N^{2}}{3(mK-1)Z_{y}}.

(18)

Proof.

By definition, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{2N-2}w_{r}^{2}=\frac{1}{m}.

Hence, (
11
) can be written as

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}^{2}(K-\frac{1}{m})

\displaystyle\geq KMN-KM\sum_{s,t=0}^{m-1}|s-t|w_{s}w_{t}-M^{2}N^{2}\frac{1}{Z_{y}m}

\displaystyle=KMN-KM\frac{(m-1)(m+1)}{3m}-M^{2}N^{2}\frac{1}{Z_{y}m}.

Further, the above inequality can be converted to

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}^{2}\geq\frac{3mKMNZ_{y}-KM(m^{2}-1)Z_{y}-3M^{2}N^{2}}{3(mK-1)Z_{y}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

The value of the right-hand side of the inequality (
18
) is determined by selecting an appropriate
m
, which in turn leads to the following theoretical lower bound.

Corollary 5
.

For
K>\frac{3M}{Z_{y}}
and
N\sqrt{\frac{3M}{KZ_{y}}}\leq Z_{x}\leq N
, the lower bounds of the aperiodic AF are given by

\displaystyle(1-\frac{KZ_{y}}{3MN^{2}})\theta_{a}^{2}+(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}\geq KM\left(N-2N\sqrt{\frac{M}{3KZ_{y}}}\right),

(19)

and

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}^{2}\geq MN\left(1-2\sqrt{\frac{M}{3KZ_{y}}}\right).

(20)

Proof.

By the weight vector in (
17
), we have (
10
) can be reduced as

\displaystyle(1-\frac{1}{m})\theta_{a}^{2}+(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}\geq KMN-KM\frac{(m-1)(m+1)}{3m}-\frac{M^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}m}.

If
m
arounds
N\sqrt{\frac{3M}{KZ_{y}}}
, and let
\hat{m}=N\sqrt{\frac{3M}{KZ_{y}}}-\epsilon
and
0\leq\epsilon<1
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}(1-\frac{1}{m})\theta_{a}^{2}+(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}&\geq(1-\frac{1}{\hat{m}})\theta_{a}^{2}+(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}\\
&\geq KMN-KM\frac{\hat{m}^{2}-1}{3\hat{m}}-\frac{M^{2}N^{2}}{Z_{y}\hat{m}}\\
&\geq KM(N-2N\sqrt{\frac{M}{3KZ_{y}}}).\end{split}

So, we have

\displaystyle(1-\frac{KZ_{y}}{3MN^{2}})\theta_{a}^{2}+(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}\geq KM(N-2N\sqrt{\frac{M}{3KZ_{y}}}).

Further, the above equation can be written as

\displaystyle K\theta_{\max}^{2}\geq(1-\frac{KZ_{y}}{3MN^{2}})\theta_{a}^{2}+(K-1)\theta_{c}^{2}\geq KM(N-2N\sqrt{\frac{M}{3KZ_{y}}}),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}^{2}\geq MN(1-2\sqrt{\frac{M}{3KZ_{y}}}).

This completes the proof.
∎

From (
3
), it can be seen that we aim for the inequality on the right side to be as small as possible, which is equivalent to minimizing
\mathbf{Q}_{Z_{x}}(\mathbf{w},\frac{MN^{2}}{KZ_{y}})
. Here, we apply the method of Lagrange multipliers to find the minimum value of
\mathbf{Q}_{Z_{x}}(\mathbf{w},\frac{MN^{2}}{KZ_{y}})
.Additionally, a weight vector can be obtained by minimizing the following function using the Lagrange multiplier method proposed by Levenshtein
[
12
]
:

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w},\lambda)=\mathbf{Q}_{Z_{x}}\left(\mathbf{w},\frac{MN^{2}}{KZ_{y}}\right)-2\lambda\left(\sum_{r=0}^{Z_{x}-1}w_{r}^{2}-1\right),

where
2\leq m\leq Z_{x}\leq N
. Let
\gamma=\arccos\left(1-\frac{KZ_{y}}{MN^{2}}\right)
,
KZ_{y}\leq MN^{2}
, and
m
be an even number such that
m\gamma\leq\pi+\gamma
. Setting
\gamma_{0}=\frac{\pi-m\gamma+\gamma}{2}
, by relating the quadratic minimization solution of
f(\mathbf{w},\lambda)
to the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind
[
12
]
, we can obtain the following weight vector:

\displaystyle w_{r}=\begin{cases}\frac{\sin\frac{\gamma}{2}}{\sin\frac{m\gamma}{2}}\sin\left(\gamma_{0}+r\gamma\right),&0\leq r\leq m-1,\\
0,&m\leq r\leq 2N-2.\end{cases}

(21)

The weight vector (
21
) is applied to Theorem
3
, a new improved lower bound of DRCSs is obtained as follows.

Corollary 6
.

For a unimodular
(K,M,N,\theta_{\max},\Pi)
-DRCS set with
KZ_{y}\leq MN^{2}
, and any integer
m
satisfying
1\leq m<

\min\{Z_{x}+1,\pi/\gamma+1\}
, the lower bound of the aperiodic AF is given by

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\theta_{c}^{2}(K-1)+\theta_{a}^{2}}{K}

\displaystyle\geq NM-\frac{m-1}{2}-\frac{\sin\frac{m\gamma}{2}-\sin\frac{(m-2)\gamma}{2}}{2(1-\cos\gamma)\sin\frac{m\gamma}{2}}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to Lemma 2 in
[
29
]
, which is ignored here.
∎

Obviously, when
Z_{x}>\pi/\gamma
, one can minimize
f(\mathbf{w},\lambda)
over different values of
m
by setting
m=\left\lfloor\pi/\gamma\right\rfloor+1
. Thus, Corollary
6
follows immediately.

Corollary 7
.

For a LAZ satisfying
Z_{x}>\pi/\gamma
and
5M\leq KZ_{y}\leq MN^{2}
, the lower bound of the aperiodic AF is given by

\displaystyle\theta_{\max}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\theta_{c}^{2}(K-1)+\theta_{a}^{2}}{K}

\displaystyle\geq M\left(N-\left\lceil\frac{\pi N}{\sqrt{8KZ_{y}/M}}\right\rceil\right).

Proof.

Let
m=\left\lfloor\pi/\gamma\right\rfloor+1
for Corollary
6
, then the proof is similar to Corollary 4 in
[
12
]
, which is ignored here.

V
Asymptotically Optimal Construction of DRCSs

In this section, we propose a new aperiodic DRCSs which are asymptotically optimal with respect to proposed bound in (
20
).

Construction 2
.

For any positive integer
N\geq 2
, let
\mathbf{A}=[a_{i,j}]
be a quasi-Florentine rectangle of size
K\times(N-1)
over
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
. Additionally, let
\pi_{p}
denote the
p
-th row of
\mathbf{A}
and
\pi_{p}(n)
be
n
-th element of
\pi_{p}
. Finally, let
\mathbf{B}
be a Butson-type Hadamard matrix of order
N
over the alphabet
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
, given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{b}_{0}\\
\mathbf{b}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{b}_{N-1}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\omega_{r}^{b_{0,0}}&\omega_{r}^{b_{0,1}}&\cdots&\omega_{r}^{b_{0,N-1}}\\
\omega_{r}^{b_{1,0}}&\omega_{r}^{b_{1,1}}&\cdots&\omega_{r}^{b_{1,N-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\omega_{r}^{b_{N-1,0}}&\omega_{r}^{b_{N-1,1}}&\cdots&\omega_{r}^{b_{N-1,N-1}}\end{array}\right].

Define a DRCS set
\mathcal{C}=\{\mathbf{C}^{(0)},\mathbf{C}^{(1)},\cdots,\mathbf{C}^{(K-1)}\}
, where
\mathbf{C}^{(k)}=\{\mathbf{c}_{0}^{(k)},\mathbf{c}_{1}^{(k)},\cdots,\mathbf{c}_{N-1}^{(k)}\}
,
\mathbf{c}_{m}^{(k)}=(c_{m,0}^{(k)},c_{m,1}^{(k)},\cdots,c_{m,N-2}^{(k)})
, and

\displaystyle c_{m,n}^{(k)}=\omega_{r}^{b_{\pi_{k}(n),m}}

for
0\leq k<K
,
0\leq m<N
,
0\leq n<N-1
.

Theorem 4
.

The sequence set
\mathcal{C}
constructed using Construction
2
is an aperiodic DRCS set with parameters
\left(K,N,N-1,N,\Pi\right)
-DRCS, where
\Pi=(-N+1,N-1)\times(-N+1,N-1)
.

Proof.

According to the definition of DRCSs, we divide the proof of Theorem
4
into two cases: auto-AF and cross-AF.

Case 1 (auto-AF): For any
0\leq k<K
and
(\tau,v)\neq(0,0)
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}AF_{\mathbf{C}^{(k)}}(\tau,v)&=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-2-\tau}c_{m,n}^{(k)}(c_{m,n+\tau}^{(k)})^{*}\omega_{N-1}^{nv}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-2-\tau}\omega_{r}^{b_{\pi_{k}(n),m}}\omega_{r}^{-b_{\pi_{k}(n+\tau),m}}\omega_{N-1}^{nv}\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{N-2-\tau}\omega_{N-1}^{nv}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\omega_{r}^{b_{\pi_{k}(n),m}-b_{\pi_{k}(n+\tau),m}}.\end{split}

Based on Lemma
2
,
|AF_{\mathbf{C}^{(k)}}(\tau,v)|=0
is obvious.

Case 2 (cross-AF): For any
0\leq k_{1}\neq k_{2}<K
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}AF_{\mathbf{C}^{(k_{1})},\mathbf{C}^{(k_{2})}}(\tau,v)&=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-2-\tau}c_{m,n}^{(k_{1})}(c_{m,n+\tau}^{(k_{2})})^{*}\omega_{N-1}^{nv}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-2-\tau}\omega_{r}^{b_{\pi_{k_{1}}(n),m}}\omega_{r}^{-b_{\pi_{k_{2}}(n+\tau),m}}\omega_{N-1}^{nv}\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{N-2-\tau}\omega_{N-1}^{nv}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\omega_{r}^{b_{\pi_{k_{1}}(n),m}-b_{\pi_{k_{2}}(n+\tau),m}}.\end{split}

According to Lemma
2
, we have
\pi_{k_{1}}(n)=\pi_{k_{2}}(n+\tau)
for
0\leq n\leq n+\tau<N-1
and
0\leq k_{1}\neq k_{2}<K
, which has at most one solution. If it does not have a solution, then

\displaystyle AF_{\mathbf{C}^{(k_{1})},\mathbf{C}^{(k_{2})}}(\tau,v)=0.

If it has one solution, denoted as
n^{\prime}
. The vectors
\mathbf{b}_{\pi_{k_{1}}(n)}
and
\mathbf{b}_{\pi_{k_{2}}(n+\tau)}
are two distinct rows of a Butson-type Hadamard matrix, and they are orthogonal to each other. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle AF_{\mathbf{C}^{(k_{1})},\mathbf{C}^{(k_{2})}}(\tau,v)

\displaystyle=N\omega_{N-1}^{n^{\prime}v}+\sum_{n=0,n\neq n^{\prime}}^{N-2-\tau}\omega_{N-1}^{nv}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\omega_{r}^{b_{\pi_{k_{1}}(n),m}-b_{\pi_{k_{2}}(n+\tau),m}}

\displaystyle=N\omega_{N-1}^{n^{\prime}v}.

Namely,
|AF_{\mathbf{C}^{(k_{1})},\mathbf{C}^{(k_{2})}}(\tau,v)|=N
for all
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
and
0\leq\tau,v<N-1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Comparisons with the Existing Aperiodic AF Lower Bound

In this section, we evaluate the tightness of the proposed aperiodic AF lower bounds. In addition, we provide some numerical examples for comparison.

For sufficiently large
K
, and under the conditions
K>\frac{3M}{Z_{y}}
and
N\sqrt{\frac{3M}{KZ_{y}}}\leq Z_{x}\leq N
, Corollary
5
shows that
\theta_{\max}^{2}
approaches
MN
. Under the condition
Z_{x}>\pi/\gamma
and
5M\leq KZ_{y}\leq MN^{2}
, Corollary
7
also shows that
\theta_{\max}^{2}
tends to
MN
. In contrast, the aperiodic AF bound for DRCSs in Lemma
1
indicates that
\theta_{\max}^{2}
approaches
\frac{MN}{2}
when
Z_{x}=N
. This means that for larger values of
K
,
N
, and
Z_{x}=N
, the theoretical lower bound given in Corollary
5
improves by nearly a factor of
\sqrt{2}
compared to the original lower bound
[
22
]
for aperiodic DRCSs.

Next, we analyze how close Construction
2
is to the theoretical bound derived in Corollary
5
.

Theorem 5
.

The DRCSs proposed in Theorem
4
is asymptotically optimal with respect to the
bound proposed in (
20
) when
K\geq 4
and
N\geq 10
.

Proof.

Since
K\geq 4
and
N\geq 10
, we have the inequality
\sqrt{\frac{3MN^{2}}{KZ_{y}}}=N\sqrt{\frac{3N}{K(N-1)}}\leq N-1=Z_{x}
. According to (
20
) in Corollary
5
, the optimality factor is given by

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{\theta_{\max}}{\sqrt{MN(1-2\sqrt{\frac{M}{3KZ_{y}}})}}=\frac{N}{\sqrt{N(N-1)\left(1-2\sqrt{\frac{N}{3K(N-1)}}\right)}}\rightarrow 1\quad\text{as}\quad N\rightarrow+\infty.

(22)

Thus, the DRCS set
\mathcal{C}
asymptotically achieves the theoretical bound presented in Corollary
5
when
K\geq 4
and
N\geq 10
.
∎

VII
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we have introduced a novel aperiodic AF lower bound for unimodular DRCSs, which depends on several factors, including the set size
K
, the number of channels
M
, the sequence length
N
, the maximum delay shift
Z_{x}
, the maximum Doppler shift
Z_{y}
, and the weight vector
\mathbf{w}
. We have analyzed three types of weight vectors, demonstrating that many established aperiodic bounds, such as the Welch bound, Sarwate bound, Levenshtein bound, Peng-Fan bound, Liu-Guan-Mow bound, and Meng-Guan-Ge-Liu-Fan bound, can be regarded as special cases of our proposed aperiodic AF lower bound.
Additionally, we have constructed a class of DRCSs with small alphabets based on quasi-Florentine rectangles and Butson-type Hadamard matrices, which are asymptotically optimal with respect to the derived aperiodic DRCS set bounds.
The optimality factor of the proposed DRCS sets in relation to the Shen-Yang-Zhou-Liu-Fan bound converges to
\sqrt{2}

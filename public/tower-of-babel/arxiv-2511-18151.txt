Abstract
. Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) in disaster response require complex, queryable intelligence that on-board CNNs cannot provide. While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) offer this semantic reasoning, their high resource demands make on-device deployment infeasible, and naive cloud offloading fails under the low-bandwidth networks endemic to disaster zones. We present AVERY , a framework that enables VLM deployment through adaptive split computing. We advance the split computing paradigm beyond traditional depth-wise partitioning by introducing a functional, cognitive-inspired dual-stream split that separates the VLM into a high-frequency, low-resolution "context stream" for real-time awareness and a low-frequency, high-fidelity "insight stream" for deep analysis. A lightweight, self-aware on-board controller manages this architecture, monitoring network conditions and operator intent to dynamically select from pre-trained compression models, navigating the fundamental accuracy-throughput trade-off. Evaluated using the VLM LISA-7B across an edge-cloud scenario under fluctuating network conditions, AVERY consistently outperforms static configurations, while achieving 11.2% higher accuracy than raw image compression, and 93.98% lower energy consumption compared to full-edge execution, thereby enhancing mission efficiency and enabling real-time, queryable intelligence on resource-constrained platforms in dynamic environments.

2.
Background

AVERY
’s design
leverages:
the semantic power of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), the efficiency of Split Computing, and the adaptability of Embodied Self-Awareness.

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Disaster Response:
By integrating a vision encoder with a Large Language Model (LLM), VLMs can perform complex reasoning and execute zero-shot, prompt-driven recognition of concepts not seen during training
(
vlmsurvey
)
. This ability to answer open-vocabulary queries (e.g.,
"are there people near the submerged car?"
) makes them exceptionally well-suited for the unpredictable scenarios of disaster response. Similar bandwidth-limited field conditions also arise in scenarios such as military UAV reconnaissance
(
droneedge
)
, agricultural monitoring systems
(
farmbeats
;
shekhar
)
, etc.
However, VLMs’ massive computational and energy footprint
(
energy
;
mission
)

makes it infeasible for
direct deployment on resource-constrained UAVs.

Split Computing with Learned Compression:
Split computing bridges the gap between large models and edge devices by partitioning a model into a "head" on the edge and a "tail" on a remote server
(
splitz
)
. The edge device processes early layers and transmits intermediate activations, reducing the need to send raw sensor data. As communication often dominates both latency and energy in wireless deployments
(
neurosurgeon
)
, minimizing activation size is critical. Recent methods show that inserting a lightweight, trainable
bottleneck
—an encoder-decoder pair trained to compress activations—is a highly effective technique for minimizing this data payload
(
matsubara2022bottlefit
)
.

Embodied Self-Awareness for Adaptive Control:
In safety-critical applications like disaster response, system and environmental conditions fluctuate rapidly. Embodied self-awareness is a system’s ability to sense its internal state (e.g., battery) and external environment (e.g., network bandwidth) and adapt its operational parameters in real time to sustain performance
(
embodied
)
. This adaptive capability is absent in traditional, static split computing frameworks and is essential for robust performance in unpredictable environments.

Taken together, these components motivate our core contribution:
AVERY
, the first framework to introduce a
VLM-specific split
for edge–cloud deployment in
bandwidth-constrained disaster environments
, combining semantic reasoning, efficient activation compression, and real-time self-awareness in one adaptive system.

The Dual-Stream Operational Model in Practice

AVERY

•

5.
Conclusion and Future Work

We presented
AVERY
, the first cognitive-inspired adaptive VLM split-computing framework enabling VLM deployment on resource-constrained UAVs for disaster response. By introducing embodied self-awareness to navigate the accuracy–throughput trade-off,
AVERY
achieves higher average throughput than static high-accuracy baselines with only
\sim
0.75% accuracy loss, while cutting on-device energy by 93.98% over full edge execution. Its early-split architecture, dual-stream design, and lightweight controller collectively provide queryable semantic intelligence under degraded networks. For remote operators, the fast Context Stream enables rapid triage, while the high-fidelity Insight Stream is invoked for detailed investigation. Although our evaluation emphasized adaptability, end-to-end mission metrics remain important future work. Our prototype targets the LISA architecture
(
lisa
)
; adapting
AVERY
’s early-split and dual-stream design to more tightly fused VLMs
(
t1

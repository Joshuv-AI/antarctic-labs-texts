Chaotic dynamics for the two body problem on a sphere
Sergey Bolotin

Abstract
We prove the existence of chaotic trajectories for the two body problem on a sphere. The trajectories we construct encounter near-collisions and are similar to the second species solutions of Poincaré of the classical 3 body problem. The construction uses a general result on Lagrangian systems with Newtonian singularities of the potential which is based on the method of anti-integrable limit of Serge Aubry.



Dedicated to Serge Aubry at his 80th birthday

Introduction

Celestial mechanics on surfaces of constant curvature (the sphere and the Lobachevsky plane) goes back to the middle of 19th century (see the history in
[
2
,
7
,
10
]
),
and it attracted considerable attention in recent years (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
10
,
14
,
18
,
21
,
23
]
).
While it shares many similarities with celestial mechanics in Euclidean space, there are significant differences.
For instance, the two-body problem in Euclidean space reduces to the Kepler problem and is therefore integrable.
The Kepler problem on surfaces of constant curvature closely resembles its Euclidean counterpart: its bounded trajectories are periodic (Serret, 1890).
However, for nonzero curvature, the two-body problem cannot be reduced to the Kepler problem. Numerical evidence (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
) suggests that the two-body problem on a sphere exhibits chaotic behavior in certain regions of phase space, although, to the best of our knowledge, this has never been proven analytically.

Several results establish nonintegrability, but only in the meromorphic sense (see e.g.
[
15
,
22
,
24
]
).
However, meromorphic nonintegrability is a much weaker notion than real-analytic nonintegrability and has limited relevance to the system’s real dynamics. Indeed, most linear time periodic systems with meromorphic time dependence are meromorphically non-integrable, yet they are completely integrable in the real sense, possessing a full set of quadratic first integrals.

In this paper, we demonstrate chaotic behavior and, in particular,
prove real-analytic nonintegrability of the two-body problem on the sphere in the case where one body’s mass
is much smaller than the other’s.
Then the heavier body moves nearly inertially, and, to first approximation, the motion of the lighter body is described by the restricted two-body problem.
The proofs in this paper rely on general results in
[
4
]

concerning Lagrangian systems with Newtonian singularities.

The chaotic trajectories we construct pass close to collisions between the bodies. These trajectories resemble the so-called ”second species solutions,” whose existence Poincaré claimed to prove in
[
20
]
for the planar three body problem with masses

\varepsilon m_{1},\varepsilon m_{2},1
, where
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. Second species solutions are those which, in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, converge to chains of collision orbits – what Poincaré referred to as ”orbites á chocs.” However, his sketch proof of their existence is incomplete, and, as far as we know, rigorous proofs of Poincaré’s claims have never been published.

For the planar circular restricted three body problem, numerous results on second species solutions have been obtained by astronomers; see the survey in
[
12
]
. Mathematical contributions include works by Alexeyev
[
1
]
, Perko
[
19
]
, Henrard
[
13
]
, Bruno
[
6
]
, Gomez and Olle
[
11
]
, and Marco and Niederman
[
17
]
. Chaotic second species solutions were constructed in
[
4
]
for the planar circular restricted problem and in
[
5
]
for the spatial restricted three body problem. In
[
16
]
, MacKay and Pinheiro constructed second species solutions describing the motion of charged particles in a magnetic field.

2
Main result

Without loss of generality let the radius of the sphere be one,
then
S^{2}=\{\mathbf{q}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:|\mathbf{q}|=1\}
.
The Lagrangian of the 2-body problem on the sphere
S^{2}
is

L=\frac{1}{2}m_{1}|\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{1}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m_{2}|\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{2}|^{2}+U(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2}),

where
U
is the interaction potential. The Hamiltonian on
T^{*}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
is

H=\frac{1}{2m_{1}}|\mathbf{p}_{1}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2m_{2}}|\mathbf{p}_{2}|^{2}-U(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2}),

with the standard symplectic structure on

T^{*}S^{2}=\{(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}):|\mathbf{q}|=1,\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\rangle=0\}.

The natural analog of the Newtonian gravitational potential for the sphere is

U(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2})=\frac{k}{\tan\rho},\qquad\rho=d(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2}),

where the distance is with respect to the standard metric on
S^{2}
:

\rho=\arccos\langle\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2}\rangle.

Explicitly

U(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2})=\frac{k\langle\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2}\rangle}{\sqrt{1-\langle\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2}\rangle^{2}}}.

(1)

The potential is singular on
\Delta=\{(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2})\in S^{2}\times S^{2}:\mathbf{q}_{1}=\pm\mathbf{q}_{2}\}
.
As
\rho\to 0
, we have

U\sim\frac{k}{\rho},

(2)

as for the Newtonian potential. Usually one sets
k=\gamma m_{1}m_{2}
.

There are two reasons why the potential (
1
) is the natural analog of the Newtonian potential:

•

For fixed
\mathbf{a}\in S^{3}
, the function
V(\mathbf{q})=U(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{a})
on the 3-dimensional sphere
satisfies the Laplace-Beltrami equation, just as the Newtonian potential on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
(Schering 1873).

•

For the Kepler problem on a sphere (when one of the particles is fixed),
the motion of the second particle in the potential field
V(\mathbf{q})
is periodic (Serret 1890),
exactly as for the Kepler problem in the Euclidean space.
Darboux (1884) proved a generalization of Bertrand’s theorem:
the only other such potential is the analog
k\tan^{2}\rho
of the Hooke potential.

However, for the results below it is only important that
U
is
SO(3)
invariant and has asymptotics (
2
)
as
\rho\to 0
.

Contrary to the 2-body problem in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, the 2-body problem on
S^{2}
is not integrable: the integrals
of energy
H
and the angular momentum

\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{q}_{1}\times\mathbf{p}_{1}+\mathbf{q}_{2}\times\mathbf{p}_{2}

corresponding to the
SO(3)
symmetry are insufficient for integrability.
For fixed angular momentum
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M}_{0}\neq 0
, only the
SO(2)
symmetry of rotation around
\mathbf{M}_{0}
remains.
Hence the reduced Hamiltonian system has two degrees of freedom.
Explicit reduction was first performed by Shchepetilov
[
21
,
22
]
,
and then a simpler reduction was done in
[
7
,
9
]
.

Numerical evidence (see e.g.
[
7
]
) shows that the 2-body problem on
S^{2}

is chaotic in certain regions of the phase space
but, as far as we know, this was never proved analytically.
There are several results establishing nonintegrability, but only in the meromorphic sense
[
15
,
22
,
24
]

which has limited relation to real dynamics of the system.

We establish chaotic behavior and, in particular, real-analytic nonintegrability
of the two body problem on
S^{2}
in the case when one mass is much smaller than the other.

Theorem 1
.

Fix constants
-1/2<a<b
and
let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\sigma_{0}>0
be sufficiently small.
If
m_{2}/m_{1}<\sigma_{0}
,
the 2-body problem on
S^{2}
has a chaotic invariant set
on the level sets
\{H=h,\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M}_{0}\}
of the first integrals for

a<\frac{m_{1}^{2}h}{m_{2}M_{0}^{2}}<b,\quad\frac{m_{1}^{2}k}{m_{2}M_{0}^{2}}<\varepsilon_{0}.

In many respects celestial mechanics on a sphere is similar to celestial mechanics on a Lobachevsky space,
see e.g.
[
9
,
10
]
.
In particular, meromorphic nonintegrability still holds, see

[
15
,
22
]
. But an analog of Theorem
1

will not hold: for similar values of the first integrals trajectories will escape
to infinity.

As mentioned in the introduction, chaotic trajectories whose existence is claimed in Theorem
1

encounter near collisions and are similar in nature to the
“second species solutions” of Poincaré
[
20
]
of the Euclidean 3 body problem.

3
Restricted 2 body problem

The proof of Theorem
1
is based on the reduction to the restricted 2 body problem on
S^{2}
.
We recall a formal derivation. In the formal limit
m_{1}\to+\infty
,
the light particle does not influence the motion of the heavy one:
the heavy particle performs the inertial motion on
S^{2}
.
Hence
\mathbf{q}_{1}
will move with constant angular velocity
\boldsymbol{\Omega}=\Omega\mathbf{e}
,
|\mathbf{e}|=1
, along a geodesic (great circle)

\Gamma=\{\mathbf{q}\in S^{2}:\langle\mathbf{q},\mathbf{e}\rangle=0\}.

(3)

Let
\mathbf{q}_{1}(0)=\mathbf{a}\in\Gamma
, then

\mathbf{q}_{1}(t)=R_{t\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a}\cos\Omega t+\mathbf{j}\sin\Omega t,\qquad\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{e}\times\mathbf{a},

where

R_{t\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\in SO(3)

is the rotation around
\mathbf{e}
by the angle
t\Omega
.

The motion of the light particle
\mathbf{q}_{2}
is then described by the
time-periodic Lagrangian

L_{2}(\mathbf{q}_{2},\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{2},t)=\frac{1}{2}m_{2}|\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{2}|^{2}+U(\mathbf{q}_{1}(t),\mathbf{q}_{2}).

If we pass to a rotating coordinate frame, i.e. make the change of variables

\mathbf{q}_{2}=R_{t\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\mathbf{x}
and divide the Lagrangian by
m_{2}
,
we obtain an autonomous Lagrangian system with the configuration space
S^{2}
and the Lagrangian

L(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}})=\frac{1}{2}|\dot{\mathbf{x}}+\boldsymbol{\Omega}\times\mathbf{x}|^{2}+\mu V(\mathbf{x}),\quad V(\mathbf{x})=\frac{\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{x}\rangle}{\sqrt{1-\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{x}\rangle^{2}}},

where
\mu=k/m_{2}
. The potential is singular for
\mathbf{x}=\pm\mathbf{a}
.
Of course this reduction to the restricted problem
is not rigorous; later on it will be made precise.

The restricted 2 body problem has the Jacobi integral

E(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}})=\frac{1}{2}|\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\times\mathbf{x}|^{2}-\mu V(\mathbf{x}).

The corresponding Hamiltonian on
T^{*}S^{2}
is

H(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{p})=\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{p}|^{2}-\langle\mathbf{p},\boldsymbol{\Omega}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle-\mu V(\mathbf{x}),\quad\mathbf{p}=\dot{\mathbf{x}}+\boldsymbol{\Omega}\times\mathbf{x}.

For
\Omega=0
we obtain the integrable Kepler problem on
S^{2}
, see the history in
[
2
,
7
]
.
For
\Omega>0
, the restricted 2 body problem on
S^{2}
was studied in several papers,
see e.g.
[
8
,
24
,
22
,
15
]
.
Numerical evidence
[
7
]
shows that it exhibits chaotic behavior,
but, as far as we know,
this was never proved analytically.
Non-existence of additional meromorphic first integrals was proved in
[
24
,
15
]
.

We will prove chaotic behavior of the restricted 2-body problem on
S^{2}

for small
\mu/\Omega^{2}
and bounded Jacobi constant.

Theorem 2
.

Let
-1/2<a<b
, and let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
be sufficiently small.
Then for
\mu/\Omega^{2}<\varepsilon_{0}
the restricted 2 body problem on
S^{2}

has a chaotic invariant set on the level set
\{E=h\}

of the Jacobi integral for
a\Omega^{2}<h<b\Omega^{2}
.

More precisely, an appropriate Poincaré map has a hyperbolic invariant set on which it is conjugate
to a topological Markov chain (a subshift of finite type). In particular, this implies analytic non-integrability
[
2
]
.
A description of chaotic trajectories is given in Theorem
4
.

In the next section we recall the result in
[
4
]
which is the basis for the proof
of Theorem
2
. A more detailed version of Theorem
2
is proved in section
5
.
In the last section we show how Theorem
2
implies Theorem
1
.

It remains to check the nondegeneracy condition. For definiteness let
k+n
be even,
then
\gamma(\tau)=\gamma(0)=\mathbf{a}
.
Equations (
8
) for the variables
\boldsymbol{\omega},\tau
have the form

R_{\tau\boldsymbol{\omega}}\mathbf{a}=R_{\tau\mathbf{e}}\mathbf{a},\quad\frac{1}{2}|\boldsymbol{\omega}|^{2}-\langle\boldsymbol{\omega},\mathbf{e}\rangle=h,\quad\langle\boldsymbol{\omega},\mathbf{a}\rangle=0,

where

R_{\tau\boldsymbol{\omega}}\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a}\cos(\tau\omega)+\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{a}}{\omega}\sin(\tau\omega),\quad R_{\tau\mathbf{e}}\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a}\cos\tau+\mathbf{j}\sin\tau.

Hence for
\tau=\pi n=\pi k/\omega
we obtain

\delta(R_{\tau\boldsymbol{\omega}}\mathbf{a}-R_{\tau\mathbf{e}}\mathbf{a})|_{\tau=\pi n}=\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{a}\,\delta(\tau\omega)+\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{a}}{\omega}(-1)^{k}\tau\,\delta\omega-\mathbf{j}\,\delta\tau.

Taking the dot product with
\boldsymbol{\omega}
,
we obtain
\delta\tau=0
, and then
\delta\omega=0
.
Since
\langle\delta\boldsymbol{\omega},\mathbf{e}\rangle=\langle\delta\boldsymbol{\omega},\mathbf{a}\rangle=0
, we have
\delta\boldsymbol{\omega}=0
.

Nondegeneracy of collision orbits is proved. Now Theorem
4
follows from Theorem
3
.
∎

6
Proof of Theorem
1

We need to make precise the relation between the 2 body problem and the restricted 2 body problem.
The 2 body problem on
S^{2}
has 4 degrees of freedom and
SO(3)
symmetry with the corresponding angular
momentum integral
\mathbf{M}
.
For a fixed value of
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M}_{0}=M_{0}\mathbf{e}
reducing symmetry yields a Hamiltonian system
on a 4-dimensional symplectic manifold
\{\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M}_{0}\}/SO(2)
.
This reduction is done in several papers, see e.g.
[
21
,
22
,
7
,
8
]
.
We will use the reduced system obtained in
[
22
]
, but represent it in a coordinate-free way
as a Hamiltonian system on
T^{*}S^{2}
with the standard symplectic structure and the Hamiltonian

H(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{p})=\frac{|\mathbf{p}|^{2}}{2m}-\frac{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle^{2}}{m_{1}}+\frac{\sqrt{M_{0}^{2}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle^{2}}}{m_{1}}\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{e}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle+kV(\mathbf{x}),

where
m=m_{1}m_{2}/(m_{1}+m_{2})
is the reduced mass. Set
M_{0}=m_{1}\Omega
. Then

\displaystyle H(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathbf{p}|^{2}}{2m_{2}}+\Omega\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{e}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle+kV(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{m_{1}}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{p}|^{2}}{2}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle^{2}-\frac{\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle^{2}\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{e}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle}{\sqrt{\Omega^{2}m_{1}^{2}-\langle\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle^{2}}+\Omega m_{1}}\right).

We introduce new momentum
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{p}/(m_{2}\Omega)
and, scaling time,
replace the Hamiltonian by
\hat{H}=H/(m_{2}\Omega^{2})
.
We obtain:

\displaystyle\hat{H}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathbf{y}|^{2}}{2}+\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{e}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle+\varepsilon V(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle+\sigma\left(\frac{|\mathbf{y}|^{2}}{2}-\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle^{2}-\sigma\frac{\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle^{2}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{e}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{x}\rangle^{2}}+1}\right).

where
\sigma=m_{2}/m_{1}
and
\varepsilon=k/(m_{2}\Omega^{2})
.
Thus

\hat{H}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=H_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\sigma F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\sigma),

where
H_{\varepsilon}
is the Hamiltonian of the restricted 2 body problem with the Lagrangian
(
12
), and
F
is a smooth function on
T^{*}S^{2}
for
|\mathbf{y}|<\sigma^{-1}
.

Let
\Lambda
be the hyperbolic invariant set on
\{H_{\varepsilon}=\hat{h}\}
constructed
in Theorem
4
for small
\varepsilon>0
. By continuity of compact hyperbolic sets under perturbations,
for small
\sigma_{0}(\varepsilon)>0
and
0<\sigma<\sigma_{0}
the hyperbolic set
\Lambda

gives a hyperbolic invariant set of the reduced system on
\{\hat{H}=\hat{h}\}
,
and hence a chaotic invariant set of the 2 body problem on the level set
\{H=h,\mathbf{M}=M_{0}\mathbf{e}\}
for

h=m_{2}\Omega^{2}\hat{h}
and
M_{0}=m_{1}\Omega
. However, since
\sigma_{0}
depends on
\varepsilon
,
we do not get Theorem
1
in this way.

A better estimate for
\sigma
may be obtained as follows.
Let us assume
-1/2<a<\hat{h}<b
and take the set
K
as in the remark after Theorem
2
.
Then the discrete action functional (
11
) will have uniformly anti-integrable structure.
For the perturbed Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
the functional will have the same structure, but now
its terms will smoothly depend on
\sigma
.
This implies that for small but unrelated
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\sigma_{0}>0

the anti-integrability will hold for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
0<\sigma<\sigma_{0}
.
Theorem
1
will follow.
∎

A Note on Pseudofinite W*-Probability Spaces
Jananan Arulseelan

Abstract
. We introduce pseudofinite W*-probability spaces. These are W*-probability spaces that are elementarily equivalent to Ocneanu ultraproducts of finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras equipped with arbitrary faithful normal states. We are particularly interested in the case where these finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras are full matrix algebras: the pseudofinite factors. We show that these are indeed factors. We see as a consequence that pseudofinite factors are never of type III 0 \mathrm{III}_{0} . Mimicking the construction of the Powers factors, we give explicit families of examples of matrix algebra ultraproducts that are III λ \mathrm{III}_{\lambda} factors for λ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \lambda\in(0,1] . We show that these examples share their universal theories with the corresponding Powers factor and thus have uncomputable universal theories. Finally, we show that pseudofinite factors are full. This generalizes a theorem of Farah-Hart-Sherman which shows that pseudofinite tracial factors do not have property Γ \Gamma . It has the consequence that hyperfinite factors of type III \mathrm{III} (the Powers factors) are never pseudofinite. Our proofs combine operator algebraic insights with routine continuous logic syntactic arguments: using Łos’ theorem to prove that certain sentences which are true for all matrix algebras are inherited by their ultraproducts.



1.
Introduction

In classical model theory, a model
M
is said to be
pseudofinite
if there is an ultraproduct of finite models that is elementarily equivalent to
M
. Pseudofinite models play an important and interesting role there (see
[
7
]
,
[
22
]
, or
[
27
]
). It can be seen that the appropriate analogue of finite models in continuous logic is compact models (see
[
17
]
, though note that they call “pseudocompact” what we here call “pseudofinite”). Compact models, for instance, are totally categorical and admit no proper ultrapowers. In the case of model theory of operator algebras, this means the pseudofinite models are those operator algebras elementarily equivalent to ultraproducts of finite-dimensional ones. These ultraproducts have been studied extensively in the setting of tracial von Neumann algebras. In particular, tracial ultraproducts of finite-dimensional factors (i.e. finite-dimensional full matrix algebras) have been of significant interest to operator algebraists for a very long time. Algebras elementarily equivalent to these were studied by Farah, Hart and Sherman in
[
15
]
.
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebraic ultraproducts of matrix algebras were studied by S. Hardy in
[
19
]
(see again the previous parenthetical about pseudocompactness). Despite all this interest in the tracial case and
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra case, little attention has been paid to a very mild generalization of the former: Ocneanu ultraproducts of finite-dimensional
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces with their faithful normal states (though see
[
2
]
where they are used to characterize QWEP and
[
1
]
for very closely related ideas). We begin a systematic study of these and the
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces elementarily equivalent to them.

We show that ultraproducts of matrix algebras are factors and so are any
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces elementarily equivalent to them. In the tracial setting, this is a simple consequence of the axiomatizability of the class of factors among tracial von Neumann algebras. However, Ando and Haagerup showed that the class of factors is not axiomatizable in
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces. Therefore, our proof is slightly more tricky. An interesting consequence of our proof is that pseudofinite factors are never of type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
. We construct explicit examples of pseudofinite factors of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\not=0
by imitating the construction of the Powers factors. In analogy with the fact that pseudofinite tracial factors share their universal theories with the hyperfinite
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor
\mathcal{R}
, we show that our examples share their universal theories with the corresponding Powers factor. It follows by work of the author, Goldbring, and Hart that their universal theories are uncomputable.

In
[
15
, Theorem 5.1.]
, it is shown (using an insight due to von Neumann) that tracial pseudofinite factors do not have property
\Gamma
. Since property
\Gamma
is axiomatizable, it follows that tracial pseudofinite factors are not elementarily equivalent to
\mathcal{R}
, which has
\Gamma
. On the other hand, tracial pseudofinite factors always share a universal theory with
\mathcal{R}
. The analogue of property
\Gamma
for arbitrary factors is the negation of the fullness property. It is known that no hyperfinite factor is full. In
[
16
]
, Goldbring and Houdayer, in order to exhibit non-elementarily equivalent
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factors, show that non-full factors form a local class (it is closed under ultrapowers and ultraroots), and therefore fullness is sufficient to distinguish theories. We show that pseudofinite
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces are always full. This is a direct analogue of the property
\Gamma
result mentioned above. Our proof uses a result of Herrero and Scarek in lieu of the result of von Neumann.

The author cannot and does not take credit for any of the deep ideas used in this paper. Indeed, the main intellectual contribution of this paper is in collecting the ideas of others and combining them with syntactic techniques from continuous logic. Nevertheless, it seems worthwhile to record the results for the sake of completeness of the literature. Furthermore, the syntactic techniques themselves may be instructive.

2.
Background and Preliminaries

Throughout this note, we work in the model theory of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-algebras as presented in
[
6
]
. Whenever we refer to definability, axioms, formulas, etc. we do so relative to the theory presented there. Recall that there, the appropriate ultraproduct is the Ocneanu ultraproduct, as follows.
Consider a family of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces
(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
and an ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
on
I
. Define

\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{i}):=\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{i},I):=\{(x_{n})\in\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{M}_{i}\ :\ \sup_{i\in I}\|x_{i}\|<\infty\}

and the subset

\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}:=\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i}):=\{(m_{i})\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{i},I)\ :\ \lim_{\mathcal{U}}\|m_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}=0\}.

Consider the two sided normalizer of
\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}

\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}:=\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i}):=\{(m_{i})\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{i},I)\ :\ (m_{i})\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{I}(m_{i})\subset\mathcal{I}\}.

The
Ocneanu ultraproduct
is defined as the quotient
\prod^{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{M}_{i}:=\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}/\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
. For
(m_{i})\in M
, we will write
(m_{i})^{\bullet}
for its equivalence class in
\prod^{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{M}_{i}
. Then
\prod^{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{M}_{i}
is a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal state
\varphi_{i}^{\mathcal{U}}
defined by
\varphi_{i}^{\mathcal{U}}((m_{i})^{\bullet})=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\varphi_{i}(m_{i})
. In the case that
\mathcal{M}_{i}=\mathcal{M}
and
\varphi_{i}=\varphi
for all
i\in I
, we write
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)^{{\mathcal{U}}}:=\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
and refer to the
Ocneanu ultrapower
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
I
be an index set and
{\mathcal{U}}
a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
I
. Let
(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of finite-dimensional matrix algebras so
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varphi_{i}
is a faithful normal state on
\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C})
for all
i\in I
. Then we call

\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i})

an
ultraproduct of matrix algebras
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is called
pseudofinite
if it is elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct of finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras.

We mostly deal with the following more specific case.

Definition 2.3
.

A
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is called a
pseudofinite factor
if it is elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct of matrix algebras.

This is, of course a reasonable interpretation of the term. However, one may also interpret it as meaning “a factor that is pseudofinite”. In the tracial setting, these are equivalent by axiomatizability of factors. However, in the case of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces, factors are not an axiomatizable class, so we do not get prima facie that an ultraproduct of matrix algebras is a factor, nor that a pseudofinite
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space whose underlying algebra is a factor is elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct of matrix algebras. We see that both of these are, in fact, true by the end of section
3
.

Our work in this note will rely heavily on the Tomita-Takesaki theory for finite-dimensional factors; that is, matrix algebras. Therefore, in the interest of the reader, we now record the computations of modular groups and relevant Tomita-Takesaki theoretic facts for faithful normal states on matrix algebras. Throughout, we denote by
\Tr
the non-normalized trace and by
\tau
the normalized trace functional on the algebra
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
of
n\times n
matrices with complex entries. We suppress the dimensionality
n
in our notation for the trace as this will often be clear from context. A matrix
a\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is said to be
positive
if all eigenvalues of
a
are non-negative real numbers, or equivalently if there exists
b\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that
a=b^{*}b
. We note that this property is often called positive semi-definiteness.

If
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, then any state on
\mathcal{M}
is given by
\varphi(x)=\Tr(ax)
for a positive element
a\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\Tr(a)=1
known as the
density matrix
. Further,
\varphi
is faithful if and only if
a
is invertible. Note that in finite dimensions, all states are normal. We can also do something equivalent for the normalized trace: we can write
\varphi(x)=\tau(a^{\prime}x)
where
\tau
is the normalized trace and
a^{\prime}
is a positive matrix with
\tau(a^{\prime})=1
. Note that in this situation,
a^{\prime}=na
.

Let
\varphi
be a faithful normal state on
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
so that
\varphi(x)=\Tr(ax)
for a positive invertible matrix
a
with
\Tr(a)=1
. By changing basis, we may assume
a=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
with
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
\sum\lambda_{i}=1
. Write
(\mathcal{H}_{\Tr},\pi_{\Tr},\eta_{\Tr})
and
(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi},\eta_{\varphi})
for the GNS representations of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to
\Tr
and
\varphi
respectively. We may identify these by
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}=\mathcal{H}_{\Tr}
,
\pi_{\varphi}(x)=\pi_{\Tr}(x)
and
\eta_{\varphi}(x)=\eta_{\Tr}(xa^{1/2})
. Then by definition

S_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(xa^{1/2})=\eta_{\Tr}(x^{*}a^{1/2})\qquad F_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(a^{1/2}x)=\eta_{\Tr}(a^{1/2}x^{*}).

Then we compute

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(xa^{1/2})

\displaystyle=F_{\varphi}S_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(xa^{1/2})

\displaystyle=F_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(x^{*}a^{1/2})

\displaystyle=F_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(a^{1/2}(a^{-1/2}x^{*}a^{1/2}))

\displaystyle=\eta_{\Tr}(axa^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=\eta_{\Tr}(a(xa^{1/2})a^{-1}).

So

\Delta^{1/2}_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(xa^{1/2})=\eta_{\Tr}(a^{1/2}(xa^{1/2})a^{-1/2})=\eta_{\Tr}(a^{1/2}x).

We thus have the following.

Proposition 2.4
.

We have
J_{\varphi}x^{*}J_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(ya^{1/2})=\eta_{\Tr}(ya^{1/2}x)
.

Proof.

Compute

J_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(xa^{1/2})=\Delta^{1/2}_{\varphi}S_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(xa^{1/2})=\eta_{\Tr}((xa^{1/2})^{*}).

Then

\displaystyle J_{\varphi}\pi_{\varphi}(x^{*})J_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(ya^{1/2})

\displaystyle=J_{\varphi}\pi_{\varphi}(x^{*})\eta_{\Tr}((ya^{1/2})^{*})

\displaystyle=J_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(x^{*}(ya^{1/2})^{*})

\displaystyle=\eta_{\Tr}(ya^{1/2}x)

as desired.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

We have
\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)=a^{it}xa^{-it}
.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\pi_{\varphi}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x))\eta_{\Tr}(ya^{1/2})

\displaystyle=\Delta^{it}_{\varphi}\pi_{\varphi}(x)\Delta^{-it}_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(ya^{1/2})

\displaystyle=\Delta^{it}_{\varphi}\eta_{\Tr}(xa^{-it}ya^{it}a^{1/2})

\displaystyle=\pi_{\varphi}(a^{it}xa^{-it})\eta_{\Tr}(ya^{1/2})

as desired.
∎

We can even demonstrate an explicit matrix coefficient presentation of the modular automorphism group. Let
x=[x_{i,j}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
be an
n\times n
matrix. Then since
a=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots\lambda_{n})
, we have
a^{it}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1}^{it},\ldots\lambda_{n}^{it})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we have

\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)=\left[\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}}\right)^{it}x_{i,j}\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

We can check our computation of the modular automorphism group using the KMS condition. First observe

\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x))=\Tr(aa^{it}xa^{-it})=\Tr(a^{-it}aa^{it}x)=\Tr(ax)=\varphi(x).

Note that for
\lambda>0
, the map
z\mapsto\lambda^{iz}
is entire. Let
D
be a strip in the complex plane defined by
0\leq\Im(z)\leq 1
.
Then setting

F_{x,y}(t)=\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)y)=\Tr(a^{1+it}xa^{-it}y),

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we claim that
F_{x,y}
extends to a bounded continuous function on
D
given by

F_{x,y}(z)=\Tr(a^{1+iz}xa^{-iz}y).

Indeed, this is bounded because
|\lambda^{iz}|=\lambda^{-\Im(z)}\leq\lambda^{-1}
for all
z\in D
and
F_{x,y}(z)
is a polynomial in terms of
\lambda_{1}^{iz},\ldots\lambda_{n}^{iz}
. It is continuous because
z\mapsto a^{z}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1}^{z},\ldots\lambda_{n}^{z})
is entire. We then have

\varphi(y\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x))=\Tr(aya^{it}xa^{-it})=\Tr(aa^{it-1}xa^{1-it}y)=F_{x,y}(t+i),

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This completes our verification of the KMS condition.

Next, we identify the totally
K
-bounded elements of
(\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi)
. By definition

\pi(\eta_{\varphi}(x))\eta_{\varphi}(y)=\pi_{\varphi}(x)\eta_{\varphi}(y)=\eta_{\varphi}(xy)=\eta_{\Tr}(xya^{1/2}).

We then see by a simple computation that
\eta_{\varphi}(x)=\eta_{\Tr}(x)
is left
K
-bounded if and only if
\|x\|\leq K
. Also by definition

\pi^{\prime}(\eta_{\varphi}(x))\eta_{\varphi}(y)=\pi_{\varphi}(y)\eta_{\varphi}(x)=\eta_{\varphi}(yx)=\eta_{\Tr}(yxa^{1/2}).

We now compute

\|\pi^{\prime}(\eta_{\varphi}(x))\|=\sup_{\|y\|_{\varphi}\leq 1}\|yx\|_{\varphi}=\sup_{\|ya^{1/2}\|_{\Tr}\leq 1}\|yxa^{1/2}\|_{\Tr}=\sup_{\|v\|_{\Tr}\leq 1}\|va^{-1/2}xa^{1/2}\|_{\Tr},

where the final term is simply
\|a^{-1/2}xa^{1/2}\|
. We summarize.

Proposition 2.6
.

If
x
is an element of the
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space
(\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi)
and
K=\max\{\|x\|,\|a^{-1/2}xa^{1/2}\|,\|a^{-1/2}x^{*}a^{1/2}\|\}
, then
x
is totally
K
-bounded.

We conclude our preliminary section with two simple but useful lemmas about projections.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
be a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space and
p
a projection in
\mathcal{M}
. Then
p
is totally
1
-bounded.

Proof.

That
\|\pi(p^{*})\|=\|\pi(p)\|=1
is immediate. To see that
p
is right
1
-bounded, note that
\pi^{\prime}(p^{*})=\pi^{\prime}(p)=\pi^{\prime}(p)\pi^{\prime}(p)
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

The set of projections is a definable set relative to the theory of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces.

Proof.

First, consider the formula
\alpha(x)=\max\left\{\|x-x^{*}\|^{\#}_{\varphi},\|x-x^{2}\|^{\#}_{\varphi}\right\}
, where
x
ranges over the totally 1-bounded elements. It is clear that
\alpha(x)=0
if and only if
x
is a projection. We now show the zeroset of
\alpha
is preserved under ultraproducts.

First, assume
p\in\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
such that
p=(p_{i})^{\bullet}
where each
p_{i}
is a projection. Then we have
p^{*}=(p_{i}^{*})^{\bullet}=(p_{i})^{\bullet}=p
and
p^{2}=(p_{i}^{2})^{\bullet}=(p_{i})^{\bullet}=p
so
p
is a projection.

Now let
p\in\prod^{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
be a projection. We argue as in
[
9
, Proposition 1.1.3]
. Since
p
is totally
1
-bounded, we may choose a representing sequence
(x_{i})\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i})
such that
x_{i}
is totally
1
-bounded for all
i\in I
and such that
p=(x_{i})^{\bullet}
by
[
6
, Theorem 6.1]
. Set
y_{i}=x_{i}^{*}x_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Then since
p=p^{*}p
, we have
\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\|x_{i}-y_{i}\|^{\#}_{\varphi_{i}}=0
. Thus, since
y_{i}
is totally
1
-bounded, we have
(y_{i})\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i})
and
p=(y_{i})^{\bullet}
by
[
6
, Theorem 6.1]
again. Now since
p=p^{2}
, we have
\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\|y_{i}-y_{i}^{2}\|^{\#}_{\varphi_{i}}=0
. Set
\epsilon_{i}=\|y_{i}-y_{i}^{2}\|_{\varphi}
and let
p_{i}
be the spectral projection of
(y_{i})
on the interval
[1-\epsilon_{i},1]
. Then
p_{i}
is a projection and
\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\|y_{i}-p_{i}\|^{\#}_{\varphi}=0
by
[
9
, Proposition 1.1.5]
. By Lemma
2.7
, we have
(p_{i})\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i})
and
p=(p_{i})^{\bullet}
by
[
6
, Theorem 6.1]
.
∎

3.
Factoriality

It is well known that any tracial ultraproduct of factors (and any tracial von Neumann algebra elementarily equivalent to such) is a factor. Model-theoretically, this is because the class of factors is axiomatizable in the model theory of tracial von Neumann algebras
[
14
]
. In stark contrast to this, Ando and Haagerup showed
[
1
, Theorem 6.18]
that an Ocneanu
ultrapower
of a type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
factor is
never
a factor. Thereby we see that the class of factors is not axiomatizable among
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces
[
6
, Corollary 8.6]
. It is therefore necessary to justify the word “factor” in the term pseudofinite factor by other means. In this section, we undertake this justification, as well as proving a few more facts about the centers of ultraproducts of finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras. Our proofs still use continuous logic methods (in particular, Łos’ theorem) but supplemented with careful calculations with matrices.

Define the formulas

\xi(x):=\sqrt{\left(\|x\|^{\#}_{\varphi}\right)^{2}-\frac{(\varphi(x))^{2}+(\varphi(x^{*}))^{2}}{2}}\qquad\beta(x)=\sup_{y\in S_{1}}\|xy-yx\|^{\#}_{\varphi}

and the sentence

\chi_{factor}:=\sup_{x\in S_{1}}\max\{0,\xi(x)-\beta(x)\}.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\chi_{factor}^{(\mathcal{M},\varphi)}=0
, then
\mathcal{M}
is a factor.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive. Assume
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space such that
\mathcal{M}
is not a factor. Let
p
be a nontrivial central projection. By Lemma
2.7
, we have that
p
is totally
1
-bounded and therefore
p\in S_{1}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
. Furthermore, since
p
is a nontrivial projection and
\varphi
is a faithful state, we have that
0<\varphi(p)<1
and
0<\varphi(1-p)<1
. Thus
\xi(p)=\sqrt{\varphi(p)-(\varphi(p))^{2}}>0
. On the other hand, by centrality of
p
, we have
\beta(p)=0
. Therefore
\chi_{factor}^{(\mathcal{M},\varphi)}>0
.
∎

We pause for a moment to emphasize that the implication in the previous proposition
does not
reverse. Indeed, if it did, then
\chi_{factor}
would, together with the axioms for
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces, axiomatize the class of factors. This is known to be impossible by
[
1
, Theorem 6.18]
. Therefore, it is indeed necessary to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
(\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi)
is a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space on a finite-dimensional matrix algebra, then
\chi_{factor}^{(\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi)}=0
.

Proof.

Our proof is via a Dixmier Averaging Theorem (see
[
11
]
) argument following
[
13
, Lemma 4.2]
. We use the weak relative Dixmier property for inclusions of finite factors due to Popa. Note that the identity inclusion
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is an inclusion of finite von Neumann algebra whose relative commutant
\mathbb{C}1
is clearly contained in the centralizer of
\varphi
. Moreover, the unique
\varphi
-preserving conditional expectation onto
\mathbb{C}1
is given by
x\mapsto\varphi(x)1
. Therefore, by
[
24
]
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a finite set
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
of unitaries and a finite set
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
of constants such that

\left\|\varphi(x)1-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}u_{j}xu_{j}^{*}x\right\|^{\#}_{\varphi}<\epsilon

Therefore, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|x-\varphi(x)1\|^{\#}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon+\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}u_{j}xu_{j}^{*}x-x\right\|^{\#}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\epsilon+\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}[u_{j},x]\right\|^{\#}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\|[u_{j},x]\|^{\#}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon+\sup_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}\|[x,y]\|^{\#}_{\varphi}.

Since
\epsilon
was arbitrary, we have

\|x-\varphi(x)1\|^{\#}_{\varphi}\leq\sup_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}\|[x,y]\|^{\#}_{\varphi}.

By symmetry, we can take
y
in the above argument to be totally
1
-bounded so that

\|x-\varphi(x)1\|^{\#}_{\varphi}\leq\sup_{y\in S_{1}}\|[x,y]\|^{\#}_{\varphi}.

It then suffices to show that

\left(\|x\|^{\#}_{\varphi}\right)^{2}-\frac{(\varphi(x))^{2}+(\varphi(x^{*}))^{2}}{2}\leq\left(\|x-\varphi(x)1\|^{\#}_{\varphi}\right)^{2}.

So we compute

\displaystyle\|x-\varphi(x)1\|^{2}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\varphi((x-\varphi(x)1)^{*}(x-\varphi(x)1))

\displaystyle=\varphi(x^{*}x-x^{*}\varphi(x)-\overline{\varphi(x)}x+(\varphi(x))^{2})

\displaystyle=\varphi(x^{*}x)-\varphi(x^{*})\varphi(x)-\overline{\varphi(x)}\varphi(x)+(\varphi(x))^{2}

\displaystyle=\varphi(x^{*}x)+(\varphi(x))^{2}-2|\varphi(x)|

\displaystyle\geq\varphi(x^{*}x)+(\varphi(x))^{2}.

By an analogous calculation, we have
\|(x-\varphi(x)1)^{*}\|^{2}_{\varphi}\geq\varphi(xx^{*})+(\varphi(x^{*}))^{2}
. Combining these two calculations yields the required bound.
∎

By Łos’ Theorem, we conclude the following.

Theorem 3.3
.

An ultraproduct of matrix algebras is always a factor.

A nice consequence of our proof technique is the following corollary which partially justifies our use of the term “pseudofinite factor”. Note by the discussion at the beginning of this section, it is not in general true that algebras which are elementarily equivalent to factors are factors.

Theorem 3.4
.

If
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
is elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct of matrix algebras, then
\mathcal{M}
is a factor.

Proof.

Let
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i})
be an ultraproduct of matrix algebras such that
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
is elementarily equivalent to
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i})
. By Proposition
3.2
and Łos’ Theorem,

\chi_{factor}^{\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i})}=\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\chi_{factor}^{(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i})}=\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}0=0.

Thus, by assumption,
\chi_{factor}^{(\mathcal{M},\psi)}=0
. Thus by Proposition
3.1
,
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
is a factor.
∎

We also show that a factor which is pseudofinite must be a elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct of matrix algebras. This is to complete our justification of our use of the term “pseudofinite factor”. The following is not trivial; see
[
1
, Theorem 6.4]
for a counterexample to the
\subseteq
direction when one replaces the Ocneanu ultraproduct by the Groh-Raynaud ultraproduct.

Theorem 3.5
.

If
(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
is a family of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces such that each
\mathcal{M}_{i}
admits a faithful normal tracial state, then
\mathcal{Z}(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i}))\cong\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
.

Proof.

We first show
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i}))
. Let
x_{i}\in Z(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
be given such that the sequence
(x_{i})
is uniformly bounded in operator norm. Using the fact that
Z(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})\subseteq(\mathcal{M}_{i})_{\varphi_{i}}=M_{i}(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}},\{0\})
, we conclude by
[
1
, Lemma 4.13]
that
(x_{i})^{\bullet}\in\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
. That
(x_{i})^{\bullet}
commutes with all
y\in\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
is now automatic.

Now we show
\mathcal{Z}(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i}))\subseteq\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
. Let
E_{i}
be the unique
\varphi_{i}
-preserving conditional expectation onto the center of
(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
. By a similar argument to Theorem
3.2
, we have

\|x-E_{i}(x)\|^{\#}_{\varphi_{i}}\leq\sup_{y\in S_{1}}\|[x,y]\|^{\#}_{\varphi_{i}}

for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{i}
for all
i\in I
.

Let
(x_{i})^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{Z}(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i}))
be given. Fix
a\in\mathbb{R}
. For each
i\in I
, we can find
y_{i}\in S_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{i})
such that
\|[x_{i},y_{i}]\|^{\#}_{\varphi_{i}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{y\in S_{1}}\|[x_{i},y]\|^{\#}_{\varphi_{i}}
. In fact, by smearing over the modular automorphism group with respect to a Féjer kernel, we may further assume
y_{i}\in M_{i}(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}},[-a,a])
for all
i
. Then we have

\|x_{i}-E_{i}(x_{i})\|^{\#}_{\varphi_{i}}\leq\sup_{y\in S_{1}}\|[x_{i},y]\|^{\#}_{\varphi_{i}}\leq 2\|[x_{i},y_{i}]\|^{\#}_{\varphi_{i}}.

Then since
y_{i}\in M_{i}(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}},[-a,a])
for all
i\in I
, we have
(y_{i})\in\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
by
[
1
, Lemma 4.13]
. Then by centrality
\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}2\|[x_{i},y_{i}]\|^{\#}_{\varphi_{i}}=0
so
(x_{i})^{\bullet}=(E_{i}(x_{i}))^{\bullet}
and we have
(x_{i})^{\bullet}\in\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
.
∎

Applying this to families of finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras, the following is immediate.

Corollary 3.6
.

If
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is a pseudofinite
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space such that
\mathcal{M}
is a factor, then
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct of matrix algebras.

We also have the following generalization of Theorem
3.3
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})
is a direct sum of
k
matrix algebras
{\mathcal{U}}
-often. Then

\mathcal{Z}\left(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})\right)\cong\mathbb{C}^{k}.

4.
Powers Factors and Powers-esque Pseudofinite Factors

A key event in the history of operator algebras was the introduction of the so-called ITPFI (infinite tensor product of finite type
\mathrm{I}
) factors by Araki and Woods
[
3
]
, motivated by and generalizing an earlier construction due Powers
[
25
]
of an uncountable family of nonisomorphic factors. These are noncommutative analogues of infinite product measures of finite probability spaces. They are factors of the form

(\mathcal{M},\varphi)=\bigotimes^{\infty}_{k=1}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{k})

Infinite tensor products are formed using the actions on the tensor product of the GNS Hilbert spaces

\mathcal{H}=\bigotimes^{\infty}_{k=1}L^{2}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{k}).

We now recall the construction of the Powers factors of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in(0,1]
as in
[
25
]
. First, let
\lambda\in(0,1)
be given.

Definition 4.1
.

Define
a_{\lambda}=\operatorname{diag}(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda},\frac{1}{1+\lambda})
and let
\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=\Tr(a_{\lambda}x)
be the corresponding state on
\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. The
hyperfinite
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factor
is the infinite tensor product

\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}=\bigotimes_{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{\lambda})

The resulting tensor product state, which we also denote
\varphi_{\lambda}
, is called the
Powers state
.

Note that taking
\lambda=1
in the above construction yields the hyperfinite
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor. By Connes’ celebrated classification result
[
8
]
,
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}
is the unique separable hyperfinite factor of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
.

We now construct explicit examples of pseudofinite factors of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in(0,1)
. We will verify that these are indeed of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
in the Section
5
. Fix such a
\lambda
and an ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
. Our construction parallels that of the Powers factor
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
be given and let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. Denote
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)^{\otimes n}=\bigotimes^{n}_{i=1}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
. Then we define

\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}=\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{\lambda})^{\otimes n}.

We write
\varphi_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
for the ultraproduct state
(\varphi_{\lambda}{}^{\otimes n})^{{\mathcal{U}}}
.

Note that taking
\lambda=1
in the above construction, we recover the tracial pseudofinite factors.

Now we demonstrate how to construct the hyperfinite
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in(0,1)
be given such that
\frac{\log(\lambda)}{\log(\mu)}
is not a rational number. Let
a_{\infty}=\operatorname{diag}(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda+\mu},\frac{\mu}{1+\lambda+\mu},\frac{1}{1+\lambda+\mu})
and let
\varphi_{\infty}(x)=\Tr(a_{\infty}x)
be the corresponding state on
\mathbb{M}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
. The
hyperfinite
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor
is the infinite tensor product

\mathcal{R}_{\infty}=\bigotimes_{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbb{M}_{3}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{\infty})

The resulting tensor product state, which we also denote
\varphi_{\infty}
, is called the
Powers state
.

By the deep work of Connes and Haagerup (see
[
18
]
),
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
is the unique separable hyperfinite factor of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
. Following the analogy with the Powers factors, the following construction should also produce pseudofinite factors of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. Then we define

\mathcal{Q}_{\infty,{\mathcal{U}}}=\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{3}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{\infty})^{\otimes n},

and write
\varphi_{\infty,{\mathcal{U}}}
for the ultraproduct state.

Theorem 4.5
.

For every
\lambda\in(0,1)
, the universal theory of
(\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}},\varphi_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}})
is the same as the universal theory of
(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})
.

Proof.

We have, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, inclusions
\iota_{n}:\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}
together with faithful normal conditional expectations
E_{n}:\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}\to\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n}
which satisfy
(\varphi_{\lambda}{}^{\otimes n})\circ E_{n}=\varphi_{\lambda}
by construction. Thus we have
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n})\subseteq\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda})
and
\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n})\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda})
. Thus also
\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda})
. Therefore, we may identify
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
with
(\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n})+\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}))/\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda})
, so
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
. Note also that
(x_{n})\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda})
implies
(E_{n}(x_{n}))\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n})
and
(x_{n})\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda})
implies
(E_{n}(x_{n}))\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n})
. Therefore
E^{{\mathcal{U}}}:\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}^{{\mathcal{U}}}\to\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
given by
(x_{n})^{\bullet}\mapsto(E_{n}(x_{n}))^{\bullet}
is a well-defined faithful normal conditional expectation satisfying
\varphi_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}\circ E^{{\mathcal{U}}}=\varphi_{\lambda}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
. Therefore
\operatorname{Th}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}},\varphi_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}})\leq\operatorname{Th}_{\forall}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})
.

On the other hand, consider the diagonal embedding
\iota:\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
given by
\iota(x)=(x+\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}))/(\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}))
. We claim that
E^{{\mathcal{U}}}\circ\iota
induces an embedding
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
. Indeed, since
E_{n}
converges to the identity on
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}
, we have
(x_{n})\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda})
if and only if
(E_{n}(x_{n}))\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n})
and
(x_{n})\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda})
if and only if
(E_{n}(x_{n}))\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n})
. It follows that
E^{{\mathcal{U}}}\circ\iota
is injective. Furthermore, it is simple to check that
E_{{\mathcal{U}}}(x_{n})=\lim_{n\to{\mathcal{U}}}x_{n}
is a state-preserving faithful normal conditional expectation from
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
to
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}
. Therefore
\operatorname{Th}_{\forall}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})\leq\operatorname{Th}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}},\varphi_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}})
.
∎

By a completely analogous proof, we have the following.

Theorem 4.6
.

The universal theory of
(\mathcal{Q}_{\infty,{\mathcal{U}}},\varphi_{\infty,{\mathcal{U}}})
is the same as the universal theory of
(\mathcal{R}_{\infty},\varphi_{\infty})
.

Despite these equivalences of universal theories, we will see in Section
6
that no infinite-dimensional hyperfinite factor shares a complete theory with any ultraproduct of matrix algebras.

By the main results of
[
5
]
, we now have the following result.

Theorem 4.7
.

For every
\lambda\in(0,1)
, the universal theory of
(\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}},\varphi_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}})
is uncomputable. Similarly, the universal theory of
(\mathcal{Q}_{\infty,{\mathcal{U}}},\varphi_{\infty,{\mathcal{U}}})
is uncomputable.

Question 4.8
.

Does any ultraproduct of matrix algebras have a computable universal theory?

We conjecture that the answer to this question is no. Our next theorem provides a negative solution under the assumption that the ultraproduct is of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
. Note that it gives an alternative proof of Theorem
4.6
. First, we need a lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Every pseudofinite
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space has QWEP.

Proof.

Every finite-dimensional factor has QWEP and QWEP is preserved under direct sums. Recall that every finite-dimensional
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space is a finite direct sum of matrix algebras. The claim now follows by the axiomatizability of QWEP (see
[
16
, Remark 3.14]
).
∎

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
be a pseudofinite factor of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
. Then the universal theory of
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is the same as that of
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
embeds with expectation in any
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor by
[
23
, Theorem D]
. Therefore we have by
[
16
, Proposition 3.13]
that
\operatorname{Th}_{\forall}(\mathcal{R}_{\infty})\leq\operatorname{Th}_{\forall}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
.

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.9
and
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have that
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
embeds with expectation in
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
. We then have by
[
16
, Proposition 3.13]
that
\operatorname{Th}_{\forall}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)\leq\operatorname{Th}_{\forall}(\mathcal{R}_{\infty})
.
∎

By
[
5
, Theorem 4.3]
, we have, under the assumptions of the previous theorem, that the universal theory of
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is uncomputable.

5.
Murray-von Neumann-Connes Types

In their work on rings of operators, Murray and von Neumann classified factors into types
\mathrm{I},\mathrm{II}
and
\mathrm{III}
. In the course of his work on the classification of injective factors, Connes further subdivided the latter class into types
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in[0,1]
. We direct the reader to
[
26
]
or
[
10
]
for further details on the structure theory of type
\mathrm{III}
factors. Here we only recall the fundamentals. Fix a factor
\mathcal{M}
. Define the Connes S-invariant

S(\mathcal{M})=\bigcap_{\varphi\in S_{\mathrm{fns}}(\mathcal{M})}\sigma(\Delta_{\Phi})

where
S_{\mathrm{fns}}(\mathcal{M})
is the space of faithful normal semifinite weights on
\mathcal{M}
, and
\sigma(\Delta_{\varphi})
is the spectrum of the modular operator
\Delta_{\varphi}
. It is known that
S(\mathcal{M})/\{0\}
is a closed subgroup of the positive reals under multiplication.
\mathcal{M}
is of type:

•

\mathrm{I}
or
\mathrm{II}
if
S(\mathcal{M})=\{1\}
;

•

\mathrm{III}_{0}
if
S(\mathcal{M})=\{0,1\}
;

•

\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in(0,1)
if
S(\mathcal{M})=\{0\}\cup\lambda^{\mathbb{Z}}
;

•

\mathrm{III}_{1}
if
S(\mathcal{M})=[0,\infty)
.

In the type
\mathrm{III}
setting, the above intersection can be taken instead over faithful normal states.

We will find another invariant, the so-called Connes
T
-invariant, much more useful for our purposes. Given a faithful normal semifinite weight
\varphi
on
\mathcal{M}
, define

T(\mathcal{M})=\{t\in\mathbb{R}\ |\ \sigma^{\varphi}_{t}\in\operatorname{Inn}(\mathcal{M})\}.

By Connes’ Radon-Nikodym theorem
[
10
, Théorèm 1.2.1]
,
T(\mathcal{M})
is independent of the choice of
\varphi
. The
T
-invariant can be used to read off the Murray-von Neumann-Connes type of
\mathcal{M}
with only a few exceptions. In particular

T(\mathcal{M})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}&\text{if $\mathcal{M}$ is of type $\mathrm{I}$ or type $\mathrm{II}$}\\
\{0\}&\text{if $\mathcal{M}$ is of type $\mathrm{III}_{1}$}\\
\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\lambda)}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $\mathcal{M}$ is of type $\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}$ for $\lambda\in(0,1)$}.\end{cases}

The only point of frustration is type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
factors. Namely
T(\mathcal{M})
for
\mathcal{M}
a type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
factor is completely unpredictable and may overlap with the cases above. On the other hand, assuming
\mathcal{M}
is not of type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
, each of the above implications become biconditionals. Therefore, we need to rule out type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
by hand. Luckily for us, the following corollary allows us to do exactly that. It simplifies our model-theoretic work by avoiding the non-axiomatizable class of
\mathrm{III}_{0}
factors.

Corollary 5.1
.

An ultraproduct of matrix algebras is never of type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i})
be an ultraproduct of matrix algebras. Take an ultrafilter
{\mathcal{V}}
and the Ocneanu ultrapower
(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i}))^{{\mathcal{V}}}
. By Łos’ Theorem and Proposition
3.2
, we have

\chi^{(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i}))^{{\mathcal{V}}}}_{factor}=\chi^{\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i})}_{factor}=0.

Thus by Proposition
3.1
, we have that
(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i}))^{{\mathcal{V}}}
is a factor. By
[
1
, Theorem 6.18]
, ultrapowers of
\mathrm{III}_{0}
factors are never factors, so
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i})
is not of type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Pseudofinite factors are never of type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
be a pseudofinite factor of type
\mathrm{III}
. Then
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
is elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct of matrix algebras
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i})
that is of type
\mathrm{III}
. By corollary
5.1
,
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{i}))
is of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\not=0
. Then since for such
\lambda
, the
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factors are an axiomatizable class, we have that
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
is type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
.
∎

We still need to show that
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
is of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
. For each
t\in\mathbb{R}
we may consider the following sentence:

\phi_{t}:=\sup_{x\in S_{1}}\|\sigma_{t}(x)-x\|^{\#}_{\varphi}.

Note, by density of totally bounded elements, that
\phi^{(\mathcal{M},\varphi)}_{t}=0
implies that
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}
is the identity on
\mathcal{M}
(and hence
t\in T(\mathcal{M})
). Note that for a given
t
, we are allowed to make use of
\sigma_{t}
in our sentences due to
[
6
, Theorem 7.8]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For each
\lambda\in(0,1)
and nonprincipal ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
, the algebra
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
satisfies
\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\lambda)}\mathbb{Z}\subseteq T(\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}})
.

Proof.

Note that
\phi_{t}^{(\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}},\varphi_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}})}=0
for all
t\in\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\lambda)}\mathbb{Z}
by Łos’ Theorem. Therefore
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}}=\id
for all
t\in\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\lambda)}\mathbb{Z}
. Since the identity is inner, the claim follows.
∎

The other direction is slightly more complicated. Indeed, there is no way to express that there is or is not a unitary implementing
\sigma_{t}
. In fact, there is no guarantee that a sequence of unitaries is in the equivalence class of an element of the Ocneanu ultraproduct. Therefore, we need to get our hands a little dirty.

Proposition 5.4
.

For each
\lambda\in(0,1)
and nonprincipal ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
, the algebra
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
satisfies
T(\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}})=\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\lambda)}\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

The
\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\lambda)}\mathbb{Z}\subseteq T(\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}})
direction is the previous lemma. So it suffices to show the
T(\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}})\subseteq\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\lambda)}\mathbb{Z}
direction.

We want to show that for
t\not\in\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\lambda)}\mathbb{Z}
, there is no unitary in
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
implementing
\sigma_{t}
. Write
a:=\operatorname{diag}(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda},\frac{1}{1+\lambda})
. The unitary implementing
\sigma_{t}
, if it exists, is given by the sequence
((a^{it})^{\otimes n})^{\bullet}
(which we denote by
(a^{it})^{\bullet}
for simplicity). Fix
c\in(0,1)
and consider the sequence
x_{c,n}=\operatorname{diag}(c,c)^{\otimes n}
. We compute that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}{}^{\otimes n}(x_{c,n}^{*}x_{c,n}+x_{c,n}x_{c,n}^{*})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(c\right)^{n}=0,

and this convergence is independent of
{\mathcal{U}}
so
(x_{c,n})\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
. We also compute

\varphi_{\lambda}{}^{\otimes n}((a^{it}x_{c,n})^{*}(a^{it}x_{c,n})+(a^{it}x_{c,n})(a^{it}x_{c,n})^{*})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}c\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right)^{it+1}+c\left(\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\right)^{it+1}.

Since the terms of the right hand side are constant, this converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
(giving
(a^{it}x_{c,n})\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
) if and only if

\left|\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right)^{it+1}+\left(\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\right)^{it+1}\right|<\frac{1}{c}.

Since
c\in(0,1)
, this occurs for all
c
if and only if

\left|\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right)^{it+1}+\left(\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\right)^{it+1}\right|=1

which is true if and only if
\lambda^{it}=1
, which in turn is true if and only if
t\in\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\lambda)}\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
is a
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factor for any ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
on
\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

By the previous proposition and the properties of
T
-invariants, it suffices to rule out
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
being type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
. But
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,{\mathcal{U}}}
is not of type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
by Corollary
5.1
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

\mathcal{Q}_{\infty,{\mathcal{U}}}
is a
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor for any ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
on
\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

The claim that
0\in T(\mathcal{Q}_{\infty,{\mathcal{U}}})
is trivial. Now fix
c\in(0,1)
and set
x_{c,n}=\operatorname{diag}(c,c,c)^{\otimes n}
. We have that
(x_{c,n})\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
. Now by analogous computations to Proposition
5.4
,
(a_{\infty}^{it}x_{c,n})\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
for all
c
if and only if

\left|\left(\frac{1}{1+\lambda+\mu}\right)^{it+1}+\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda+\mu}\right)^{it+1}+\left(\frac{\mu}{1+\lambda+\mu}\right)^{it+1}\right|=1.

Since
\frac{\log(\lambda)}{\log(\mu)}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
, this occurs if and only if
t=0
.
∎

Next we give another procedure for constructing an ultraproduct of matrix algebras which is of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
. Fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
and set
\lambda_{j}\in(0,1]
an increasing sequence converging to
\lambda
. Consider the ultraproduct

\mathcal{Q}=\prod^{{\mathcal{V}}}\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda_{j},{\mathcal{U}}}

with respect to the nonprincipal ultrafilter
{\mathcal{V}}
. By
[
1
, Lemma 6.10]
, we have that
\mathcal{Q}
is a type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factor. By re-indexing, we can see that

\mathcal{Q}=\prod^{{\mathcal{V}}}\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{\lambda_{j}})^{\otimes i}

is an ultraproduct of matrix algebras with respect to the product ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}\otimes{\mathcal{V}}
.

Remark 5.7
.

An original aim of this note was to characterize the Murray-von Neumann-Connes type of an arbitrary ultraproduct of matrix algebras in terms of the corresponding sequence of density matrices. We now show that this is not possible and that there is a fundamental dependence on the underlying ultrafilter. Fix
0<\mu<1
and take a sequence
\lambda_{n}\in(\mu,1]
. The sequence
n\mapsto\lambda_{n}
extends to a map on
\beta\mathbb{N}
given by
{\mathcal{U}}\mapsto\lim_{n\to{\mathcal{U}}}\lambda_{n}
. Since
\beta\mathbb{N}
is compact and
\lambda_{n}
has dense image in
[\mu,1]
, this latter map is surjective. Then by
[
1
, Lemma 6.10]
, the type of

\mathcal{Q}=\prod^{{\mathcal{V}}}\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda_{j},{\mathcal{U}}}=\prod^{{\mathcal{V}}}\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi_{\lambda_{j}})^{\otimes i}

can be of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda\in[\mu,1]
, depending on the choice of
{\mathcal{V}}
.

We are left with a mystery. Namely, there are many sequences of matrix algebras whose infinite tensor products are of type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
. But since the corresponding ultraproducts cannot be type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
, what can they be?

Question 5.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of finite-dimensional matrix algebras such that

\bigotimes_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})

is of type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
. Consider

(\mathcal{M},\varphi)=\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}\left(\bigotimes^{n}_{i=0}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\varphi_{i})\right).

What, if anything, can be said about the Murray-von Neumann-Connes type of
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
?

By
[
6
, Proposition 8.8]
it is known that the class of
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factors for
\lambda\not=0
is axiomatizable. In fact, in
[
16
, Proposition 4.4]
,
[
16
, Proposition 4.5]
and
[
16
, Corollary 4.7]
, it is shown that it admits a
\forall_{3}
and
\exists_{3}
axiomatization but no
\forall_{2}
or
\exists_{2}
axiomatizations. At the time of writing, no explicit axiomatization of the class of
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factors for
\lambda\not=0
appears in the literature. We do not produce such an axiomatization, but we will conclude this section with a brief discussion of the difficulty of producing such an axiomatization.

One significant difficulty in producing explicit axioms for type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factors is that theories including sentences with 3 quantifier alternations, as discussed above, are often very complicated to think about. The second significant difficulty becomes apparent when attempting a follow-your-nose approach to axiomatizing
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factors. Such an approach would be to write down sentences which specify the Connes
S
-invariant. However, to compute the
S
-invariant of a factor
\mathcal{M}
from the definition, we must take an intersection over all faithful normal semifinite weights on
\mathcal{M}
. Alternatively, Connes showed that if
\Phi
is almost periodic (recall that
\Phi
is almost periodic if
\Delta_{\Phi}
is a diagonalizable operator), it suffices to compute the spectrum of
\Delta_{\Phi}
. In both cases, expressing these involves a quantification over faithful normal semifinite weights on
\mathcal{M}
. This is known to not be possible in continuous logic. In fact, one cannot even quantify over faithful normal states on
\mathcal{M}
in the
\sigma
-finite setting. Thus an intuitive axiomatization of type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factors necessitates alternative means of computing the Murray-von Neumann-Connes type.

6.
Fullness and Elementary Equivalence

In
[
15
]
, it is shown that the class of
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factors with property
\Gamma
is axiomatizable by demonstrating an explicit axiomatization of it. This axiomatization and a deep result of von Neumann are used to show that pseudofinite tracial factors do not have
\Gamma
(and therefore are not hyperfinite). In this section, we prove a generalization of this result for general pseudofinite
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces. In the setting of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space factors, the correct analogue of property
\Gamma
is non-fullness. We now recall the concept of fullness. We say that a net
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
is
centralizing
if
\lim_{i}\|x_{i}\varphi-\varphi x_{i}\|=0
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
.

Definition 6.1
.

A
\sigma
-finite factor
\mathcal{M}
is said to be
full
if for all uniformly bounded nets
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
in
\mathcal{M}
such that
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
is centralizing, there is a bounded net
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in I}
in
\mathbb{C}
such that
x_{i}-\lambda_{i}1
converges to
0
in the strong topology.

Indeed, a type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor has property
\Gamma
if and only if it is not full. However, being a full factor is not an axiomatizable property, so we must again be careful in our syntactic work. We use a characterization of fullness in terms of ultrapowers due to Houdayer, Marrakchi, and Verraedt.

Proposition 6.2
.

see
[
21
, Corollary 3.7]

\mathcal{M}
is full if and only if
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}=\mathbb{C}1
for all nonprincipal ultrafilters
{\mathcal{U}}
.

We consider the following sentence

\theta:=\inf_{x\in S_{1}}\sup_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\max\{0,\min\{\|p\|^{\#}_{\varphi},\|1-p\|^{\#}_{\varphi}\}-\|xp-px\|^{\#}_{\varphi}\}

Proposition 6.3
.

If
\theta^{(\mathcal{M},\varphi)}=0
, then
\mathcal{M}
is full.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive. Assume
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space such that
\mathcal{M}
is not full. Then
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}\not=\mathbb{C}
. So pick a nontrivial projection
p\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
. Since
p
is nontrivial, there is a
c>0
such that
\|p\|^{\#}_{\varphi^{{\mathcal{U}}}},\|1-p\|^{\#}_{\varphi^{{\mathcal{U}}}}>c
. By Lemma
2.8
, we can write
p=(p_{n})^{\bullet}
for some sequence
p_{n}
of projections. Therefore, we can find
n
such that
\|p_{n}\|^{\#}_{\varphi},\|1-p_{n}\|^{\#}_{\varphi}>c/2
and
\|xp_{n}-p_{n}x\|^{\#}_{\varphi}<c/4
for all
x\in S_{1}
. It then follows that
\theta^{(\mathcal{M},\varphi)}>c/4>0
.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

For any finite-dimensional matrix algebra
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and faithful normal state
\varphi
thereupon, we have
\theta^{(\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi)}=0
.

Proof.

By
[
20
, Theorem 9]
, there is an operator
x\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that if
p
is a projection satisfying
xp=px
, then
p=0
or
p=1
. The claim follows.
∎

Our next theorem now follows by a similar argument to Theorem
3.4
. Note that since type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
factors are never full, the theorem provides an alternative proof of Corollary
5.1
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Any pseudofinite factor is full.

Since hyperfinite factors are never full, we have our final corollary.

Corollary 6.6
.

Hyperfinite type
\mathrm{III}
factors are never pseudofinite.

We conclude this section by echoing a passage from
[
15
]
with a subtle adaptation to our present setting. Separable pseudofinite factors are in some sense the simplest, being the closest to matrix algebras. Yet the hyperfinite factors are not pseudofinite, and in fact no specific pseudofinite factors have been identified!

7.
Pseudofinite Weighted Von Neumann Algebras

Following
[
4
]
, a further direction of study is the pseudofinite weighted von Neumann algebras. That is, we should study the generalized Ocneanu ultraproducts of finite-dimensional weighted von Neumann algebras and the von Neumann algebras elementarily equivalent to such. Since faithful normal semifinite weights on finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras are simply faithful positive functionals, studying individual finite-dimensional weighted von Neumann algebras more or less reduces to the
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space setting. On the other hand, unlike the
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space setting, we can take a sequence of functionals
\varphi_{i}
with norms that grow without bound. This produces new examples which require a better understanding of the generalized Ocneanu ultraproduct to study.

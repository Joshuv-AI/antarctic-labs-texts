Peculiar Botryoid Lymphocytosis After Heat Stroke
Authors: Sacha Mandel, Jean‐François Lesesve
Published: 2026

Botryoid nuclear changes (hypersegmented nuclei with the segments connected by thin filaments arranged radially [1]) can be observed across multiple leukocyte lineages, mainly neutrophils, when associated with hyperthermia of different etiologies. Botryoid lymphocytes are uncommon, and thus less emphasized in the literature [2].

The first patient was a young woman critically wounded after a car crash who underwent surgery and developed a fatal malignant hyperthermia after anesthesia combining succinylcholine and halothane (peak temperature 40.1°C). The second case was a teenager who ingested ecstasy tablets containing 3,4‐methylenedioxy‐N‐methylamphetamine (MDMA), leading to a serotonin syndrome (peak temperature 41.8°C). In both cases, the leukocyte differential count showed lymphocytosis (lymphocyte count 6.6 and 7.2 × 109/L, respectively) with nearly 30% of them showing budding or lobulation (Figure 1). Apoptotic lymphocytes were numerous. Some monocytes and a few neutrophils also appeared botryoid. Both blood films were spread out near peak temperature.

Hypersegmented botryoid lymphocytes with radial arrangement of nuclear segment (top, Patient 1; bottom, Patient 2). Blood film, May–Grünwald–Giemsa staining, magnification ×100 objective.

White blood cells' morphologic changes associated with hyperthermia include bizarre‐shaped nuclei and apoptotic cells. The alteration called “botryoid” nuclei comes from increased numbers of small, moderately pyknotic nuclear segments clustered as a grape on a stem [3]. Botryoid nuclei are a feature of burns and of hyperthermia of diverse origins, including heatstroke, brainstem hemorrhage or ischemia, encephalitis, and malignant hyperthermia (genetically determined susceptibility to anesthesia‐induced hyperthermia) [4, 5]. It has also been described after neuroleptic malignant syndrome (haloperidol as a main cause), which disrupts thermoregulatory mechanisms or recreational abuse of cocaine or methamphetamine [6]. The above findings in the two blood films are associated with hyperthermia‐related conditions. Notably, in both cases, botryoid lymphocytes were the major anomaly.

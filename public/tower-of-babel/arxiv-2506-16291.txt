On fast Lyapunov spectra for Markov-Rényi maps
Lulu Fang, Carlos Gustavo Moreira, Zhichao Wang, Yiwei Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the multifractal analysis for Markov-Rényi maps, which form a canonical class of piecewise differentiable interval maps, with countably many branches and may contain a parabolic fixed point simultaneously, and do not assume any distortion hypotheses. We develop a geometric approach, independent of thermodynamic formalism, to study the fast Lyapunov spectrum for Markov-Rényi maps. Our study can be regarded as a refinement of the Lyapunov spectrum at infinity. We demonstrate that the fast Lyapunov spectrum is a piecewise constant function, possibly exhibiting a discontinuity at infinity. Our results extend the works in [ 8 , Theorem 1.1] , [ 20 , Theorem 1.2] , and [ 9 , Theorem 1.2] from the Gauss map to arbitrary Markov-Rényi maps, and highlight several intrinsic differences between the fast Lyapunov spectrum and the classical Lyapunov spectrum. Moreover, we establish the upper and lower fast Lyapunov spectra for Markov-Rényi maps.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Backgrounds

Dynamical systems with strong hyperbolicity typically exhibit a high degree of mixing, resulting in the separation of nearby orbits. In such systems, the intricate orbit structure necessitates a quantitative measure of their asymptotic separation rates.
This behavior is precisely captured by Lyapunov exponents, which describe the exponential rate at which infinitesimally close orbits diverge over time.

Let
I\coloneqq[0,1]
, and let
T:I\to I
be a piecewise differentiable interval map. The
Lyapunov exponent
of
T
at a point
x\in I
is defined as

(1)

\lambda(x)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)|,

whenever the limit exists. By Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem, if
\mu
is an ergodic
T
-invariant probability measure such that
\int\log|T^{\prime}|d\mu<\infty
, then
\lambda(x)=\int\log|T^{\prime}|d\mu
for
\mu
-almost every
x\in I
. However, in general, the Lyapunov exponent may attain a continuum of values, forming an entire interval. This motivates the study of the
level sets

(2)

J(\alpha)\coloneqq\left\{x\in I:\lambda(x)=\alpha\right\},

and the associated
Lyapunov spectrum

(3)

L(\alpha)\coloneqq\dim_{\rm H}J(\alpha),

where
\dim_{\rm H}
denotes the Hausdorff dimension (see
[
5
]
for the definition).

The Lyapunov spectrum has been extensively studied for several important classes of piecewise differentiable maps.
For finitely branched conformal expanding maps, Weiss
[
31
]
established a connection between the Lyapunov exponent and the pointwise dimension of a Gibbs measure. Applying the multifractal analysis of pointwise dimensions developed by Pesin and Weiss
[
23
]
, he showed that the Lyapunov spectrum is real-analytic and defined on a bounded interval. Similar analyses have been extended to non-uniformly expanding systems, such as the Manneville-Pomeau map, where the Lyapunov spectrum remains defined on a bounded interval but may exhibit points of non-analyticity (see
[
25
]
). Gelfert and Rams
[
12
]
studied the Lyapunov spectrum for a class of interval maps with parabolic periodic points.
Further results for finite-branch Markov interval maps can be found in
[
2
,
10
,
17
,
22
,
24
,
30
]
.

In contrast, when
T
has countably many branches, the Lyapunov spectrum exhibits behaviors significantly different from those observed in the finite-branch case. Pollicott and Weiss
[
25
]
studied the Lyapunov spectrum for the Gauss map, which is a uniformly expanding map with countably many branches. They demonstrated that the Lyapunov spectrum is real analytic, but it has an unbounded domain. Subsequent works by Kesseböhmer and Stratmann
[
19
]
, and Fan et al.
[
7
]
provided a complete characterization of the Lyapunov spectrum for the Gauss map. For non-uniformly expanding maps, Iommi
[
14
]
showed that the Lyapunov spectrum has an unbounded domain, and there might exist a non-differentiability in the domain. For additional multifractal analysis of the Lyapunov spectrum in Markov interval maps with countably many branches, including extensions and related results, we refer to
[
6
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
21
,
27
]
and the references therein.

In summary, for interval maps with countably many branches, the domain of the Lyapunov spectrum is often unbounded, reflecting the possibility of infinite Lyapunov exponents. This indicates that, for certain points, the divergence of nearby orbits occurs at a rate faster than any exponential scale. Hence, the Lyapunov exponent becomes insufficient to describe such rapid behaviors.

To overcome this limitation and provide a finer understanding of orbit separation beyond exponential growth, we introduce the concept of the fast Lyapunov exponent. This quantity is designed to capture the super-exponential separation of orbits and enables a refined multifractal analysis of the corresponding level sets in terms of Hausdorff dimension.

Let us now define the dynamical systems under consideration and present the main results of this paper.

1.2.
Markov-Rényi maps

Inspired by the works of Iommi
[
14
]
, Pollicott and Weiss
[
25
]
, and Sarig
[
28
]
, we propose the following definition of the Markov-Rényi map.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a collection of disjoint open subintervals of
I=[0,1]
.
A piecewise differentiable interval map
T:\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{I}_{n}\to I
is called a
Markov-Rényi map
associated with
\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
if the following conditions hold:

(1).

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the intervals
I_{n}
and
I_{n+1}
share a common endpoint i.e.,
\partial I_{n}\cap\partial I_{n+1}
is a singleton;

(2).

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the restriction
T|_{I_{n}}
extends to a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism
T_{n}
on an open neighborhood of
\overline{I}_{n}
;

(3).

there exists a point
p\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{I}_{n}
and an integer
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T(p)=p
,
|T^{\prime}(p)|\geq 1
, and
|(T^{m})^{\prime}(x)|>1
for all
x\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{I}_{n}\setminus\{p\}
;

(4).

\overline{T(I_{n})}=[0,1]
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(5).

there exist two constants
\gamma>1
and
C>1
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\overline{I}_{n}
, we have

C^{-1}n^{\gamma}\leq|T^{\prime}_{n}(x)|\leq Cn^{\gamma}.

Remark 1.2
.

In comparison with the definition used in
[
14
, Section 2]
, Definition
1.1
does not require the Markov-Rényi map to satisfy any distortion hypotheses. In particular, Definition
1.1
includes the Markov-Rényi maps without tempered distortion condition

The tempered distortion condition was introduced in the works of Gelfert and Rams
[
11
,
12
]
, and is treated as one of the mild distortion conditions. It states that there exists a sequence of positive numbers
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
decreasing to zero, such that

(4)

\sup_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\sup_{x,y\in I_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\frac{|(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)|}{|(T^{n})^{\prime}(y)|}\leq\exp(n\rho_{n}),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

where
I_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
denotes the cylinder set of order
n
defined in (
9
). The tempered distortion condition can be yielded in many systems, such as the piecewise differentiable interval maps that satisfy the Rényi condition, see
[
25
]
.
.

A point
p\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{I}_{n}
such that
T(p)=p
and
|T^{\prime}(p)|=1
is called a
parabolic fixed point
. We say
T
is
uniformly expanding
if there exist
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
c>1
such that
|(T^{m})^{\prime}(x)|\geq c
for all
x\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{I}_{n}
. Of course, if
T
is uniformly expanding, then it does not contain any parabolic fixed points.

There are two important examples of Markov-Rényi maps in the study of (backward) continued fractions, which exhibit distinct behaviors in their Lyapunov spectra.

Example 1.3

(The Gauss map)
.

The Gauss map
G:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is defined by
G(0)\coloneqq 0
and

G(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{x}-\left\lfloor\frac{1}{x}\right\rfloor,\quad\forall x\in(0,1],

where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the integer part of a number. It is a uniformly expanding Markov-Rényi map.
In this case,
I_{n}=(\frac{1}{n+1},\frac{1}{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and the parameter
\gamma=2
in Hypothesis
(5)
of Definition
1.1
.

Example 1.4

(The Rényi map)
.

The Rényi map
R:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is defined by
R(1)\coloneqq 0
and

R(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{1-x}-\left\lfloor\frac{1}{1-x}\right\rfloor,\quad\forall x\in[0,1).

It is a Markov-Rényi map with a parabolic fixed point at zero. In this case,
I_{n}=(\frac{n-1}{n},\frac{n}{n+1})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and the parameter
\gamma=2
in Hypothesis
(5)
of Definition
1.1
.

In contrast to the Gauss map, the Rényi map has complexity associated with possessing both the existence of a parabolic fixed point and countable many branches. It is the interplay between these two features that makes
R(x)
admitting independent interests (see
[
1
,
3
,
13
,
26
]
).

Under the assumption that the Markov-Rényi map
T
satisfies the tempered distortion property (as in (
4
)), the Lyapunov spectrum of Markov-Rényi maps was established by Iommi
[
14
]
, who characterized the spectrum in terms of the Legendre-Fenchel transform of the geometric pressure function.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
T
be a Markov-Rényi map satisfying the tempered distortion property. Then the domain of the Lyapunov spectrum
L(\cdot)
is an unbounded subinterval of
[0,\infty)
and

L(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big\{P(t)+t\alpha\big\},

where
P(t)
denotes the geometric pressure function of
T
, i.e., the topological pressure for
T
and the potential
-t\log|T^{\prime}|
.

A detailed analysis of the geometric pressure function
P(\cdot)
, including its regularity, monotonicity, and convexity properties, is provided in
[
14
, Section 3]
. Based on the behavior of the geometric pressure function and Theorem
1.5
, we can derive several properties of the Lyapunov spectrum.
In particular, for the Gauss map, the Lyapunov spectrum is defined on the unbounded interval
[2\log((1+\sqrt{5})/2),\infty)
, and attains a unique maximum at
\pi^{2}/(6\log 2)
. Moreover, it is real-analytic, strictly increasing on the interval
[2\log((1+\sqrt{5})/2),\pi^{2}/(6\log 2)]
, and strictly decreasing on
[\pi^{2}/(6\log 2),\infty)
. For the Rényi map, the domain of the Lyapunov spectrum is the interval
[0,\infty)
. In this case, the Lyapunov spectrum is real-analytic, strictly decreasing on
[0,\infty)
, and has a unique maximum at zero.

G
and
R
are shown in Figure

It is evident that the Lyapunov spectra of
G
and

exhibit horizontal asymptotes at
1/2
. In fact, by Hypothesis (5) of Definition
1.1
, we obtain

(5)

P(t)\geq\log\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}n^{-t\gamma},

which implies that
P(t)
has a logarithmic singularity at
t=1/\gamma
. From Theorem
1.5
, we conclude that

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}L(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\gamma}.

This naturally leads to the following question:

(Q1)

What is the value of
L(\infty)
, and is the Lyapunov spectrum continuous at infinity?

An affirmative answer of
(Q1)
is known in the case of the Gauss map (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
) and the uniformly expanding Markov-Rényi map (see
[
15
, Theorem 7.1]
) satisfying the Rényi condition. We extend these results to arbitrary Markov-Rényi maps.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
T
be a Markov-Rényi map. Then

L(\infty)=\frac{1}{\gamma}.

Proposition
1.6
indicates that there are uncountably many real numbers with infinite Lyapunov exponent, which raises the question of conducting a more detailed analysis of such numbers. Accordingly, we pose the following question:

(Q2)

Can the spectrum at Lyapunov exponent infinity be further refined? If so, how does it differ from the Lyapunov spectrum?

To address
(Q2)
, we analyze the behavior of the quantity
\log|(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)|
, which diverges at a rate faster than linear.
This motivates the introduction of the fast Lyapunov exponent and the associated fast Lyapunov spectrum.
We remark that the fast Lyapunov exponent was first introduced by Fan et al.
[
7
,
8
]
for the Gauss map, and further studied by Liao and Rams
[
20
]
and Fang, Shang and Wu
[
9
]
.

1.3.
Statement of main results

Let
T
be a Markov-Rényi map with repeller
\Lambda
(see Section 2 for its definition), and let
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a
scaling function
such that
\psi(n)/n\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Analogously to (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
), we define the
fast Lyapunov exponent
of
T
(with respect to
\psi
) at a point
x\in\Lambda
as

\lambda_{\psi}(x)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\psi(n)}\log|(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)|,

whenever the limit exists. For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
, let
J_{\psi}(\alpha):=\left\{x\in\Lambda:\lambda_{\psi}(x)=\alpha\right\}
,
and define the
fast Lyapunov spectrum
(with respect to
\psi
) as

F_{\psi}(\alpha)\coloneqq\dim_{\rm H}J_{\psi}(\alpha).

Now, we are ready to present the main results of this paper. We say that two functions
f,g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
are
equivalent
if
f(n)/g(n)\to 1
as
n\to\infty
. Let

\beta_{\psi}\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\psi(n+1)}{\psi(n)}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{\psi}\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\psi(n)}.

The fast Lyapunov spectrum for Markov-Rényi maps is described below.

Theorem A

(The fast Lyapunov spectrum)
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a function satisfying
\psi(n)/n\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Then:

(i)

when
\alpha=0
, the level set
J_{\psi}(0)
has Lebesgue measure
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|I_{n}|
. In particular,
F_{\psi}(0)=1
;

(ii)

when
0<\alpha<\infty
, the level set
J_{\psi}(\alpha)
is nonempty if and only if
\psi
is equivalent to an increasing function. In this case,

F_{\psi}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)\beta_{\psi}+1};

(iii)

when
\alpha=\infty
, we have

F_{\psi}(\infty)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)B_{\psi}+1}.

Next, we investigate the upper and lower fast Lyapunov spectra of
T
. Specifically, for any
x\in\Lambda
, let

(6)

\overline{\lambda}_{\psi}(x):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\psi(n)}\log|(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)|,

and let
\underline{\lambda}_{\psi}(x)
be defined analogously by replacing the limit superior in (
6
) with the limit inferior.
For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
, define

\overline{J}_{\psi}(\alpha)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:\overline{\lambda}_{\psi}(x)=\alpha\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{J}_{\psi}(\alpha)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:\underline{\lambda}_{\psi}(x)=\alpha\right\},

and call

\overline{F}_{\psi}(\alpha)\coloneqq\dim_{\rm H}\overline{J}_{\psi}(\alpha)\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{F}_{\psi}(\alpha)\coloneqq\dim_{\rm H}\underline{J}_{\psi}(\alpha)

the
upper
and
lower fast Lyapunov spectra
of
T
, respectively.

To describe the upper and lower fast Lyapunov spectra, let

b_{\psi}\coloneqq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\psi(n)}.

Theorem B

(The upper and lower fast Lyapunov spectra)
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a function satisfying
\psi(n)/n\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Then:

(i)

when
\alpha=0
, both
\overline{J}_{\psi}(0)
and
\underline{J}_{\psi}(0)
have Lebesgue measure
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|I_{n}|
. In particular,
\overline{F}_{\psi}(0)=\underline{F}_{\psi}(0)=1
;

(ii)

when
0<\alpha\leq\infty
, we have

\overline{F}_{\psi}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)b_{\psi}+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{F}_{\psi}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)B_{\psi}+1}.

In view of Theorems
1.5
,
A
, and
B
, we identify two key differences between the fast Lyapunov spectrum and the Lyapunov spectrum.

(i)

The (upper and lower) fast Lyapunov spectrum is always a constant function on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
for every Markov-Rényi map. Such constants depend merely on two features. Specifically, the dynamical system
T
contributes through the parameter
\gamma
in Hypothesis (5) of Definition
1.1
. The scaling function
\psi
contributes through
\beta_{\psi}
and
B_{\psi}
in the fast Lyapunov spectrum,
b_{\psi}
in the upper fast Lyapunov spectrum, and
B_{\psi}
in the lower fast Lyapunov spectrum. While the Lyapunov spectrum described in Theorem
1.5
, is varied according to different Markov-Rényi maps.

(ii)

The existence of a parabolic fixed point of
T
has no impact on the (upper and lower) fast Lyapunov spectrum, while the existence of a parabolic fixed point of
T
plays a significant role on the Lyapunov spectrum.

On the other hand, the fast Lyapunov spectrum is discontinuous at zero, and it is continuous at infinity if and only if
\beta_{\psi}=B_{\psi}
. In contrast, both the upper and lower fast Lyapunov spectra exhibit a single discontinuity at zero. Moreover, note that
b_{\psi}\leq B_{\psi}\leq\beta_{\psi}
. This implies that the three associated fast Lyapunov spectra can be all distinct. For example,
let
\psi(1):=1
and

\psi(n):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\frac{5}{3})^{k-1}4^{n-(1!+3!+\cdots+(2k-1)!)}3^{1!+3!+\cdots+(2k-1)!},&\hbox{$n_{2k-1}<n\leq n_{2k}$;}\\
(\frac{5}{3})^{k}4^{2!+4!+\cdots+(2k)!}3^{n-(2!+4!+\cdots+(2k)!)},&\hbox{$n_{2k}<n\leq n_{2k+1}$,}\end{array}\right.

where
n_{k}:=1!+2!+\cdots+k!
. Then
b_{\psi}=3,B_{\psi}=4
, and
\beta_{\psi}=5
; see
[
9
]
for further details.
This phenomenon was first observed by Liao and Rams
[
20
]
for the Gauss map. We extend their contribution to arbitrary Markov-Rényi maps.

It is worth remarking that the thermodynamic formalism approach employed in
[
14
]
for the Lyapunov spectrum is not applicable in proving Theorems
A
and
B
. Let us explain as follows: as indicated in (
5
), the geometric pressure function
P(t)
exhibits a logarithmic singularity at
t=1/\gamma
. This singularity implies that the pressure function fails to reflect the properties of sets whose Hausdorff dimension is smaller than
1/\gamma
. According to Proposition
1.6
, the (upper and lower) fast Lyapunov spectrum is bounded above by
L(\infty)=1/\gamma
. Therefore, the thermodynamic formalism
cannot be directly employed
to characterize the (upper and lower) fast Lyapunov spectrum in this regime. The key feature of our approach to proving Theorems
A
and
B
lies in the symbolic model for the Markov-Rényi map and the distribution of the corresponding symbolic codings, which is independent of thermodynamic formalism.

of Definition

induces a natural ordering, either from left to right or from right to left, is essential in establishing Theorems

and

.

2.
Symbolic codings and their distribution

In this section, we review the symbolic codings and their distribution for Markov-Rényi maps.
We follow the convention
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\cdots\}
the set of positive integers,
\mathbb{R}
the set of real numbers,
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x>0\}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\{0\}
.
For a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, denote by
|A|\coloneqq\sup\{|x-y|:x,y\in A\}
the diameter of
A
,
A^{n}\coloneqq\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):x_{i}\in A,\forall 1\leq i\leq n\}
the Cartesian product of
A
,
\#A
the cardinality of
A
,
\overline{A}
the closure of A, and
\partial A
the boundary of
A
. For a function
f:A\to\mathbb{R}
, and
B\subseteq A
, let
f|_{B}
be the restriction of
f
on
B
.

2.1.
Symbolic codings

The Markov structure of the Markov-Rényi map implies that it is semi-conjugate to the full shift on a countable alphabet (see, e.g.,
[
25
,
28
]
). By Hypothesis (1) of Definition
1.1
, it follows that

I^{*}\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{I}_{n}

is a subinterval of
[0,1]
. For a Markov-Rényi map
T
, let

\mathcal{Q}\coloneqq\bigcup^{\infty}_{k=0}T^{-k}\left(\{0,1\}\cup\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\partial I_{n}\right).

Then
\mathcal{Q}
is a countable subset of
I^{*}
.
Define

\Lambda\coloneqq I^{*}\setminus\mathcal{Q}

as the
repeller
of
T
.
Thus, every
x\in\Lambda
has a unique
symbolic coding

(a_{1}(x),a_{2}(x),a_{3}(x),\dots)\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
since for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique interval
I_{a_{n}(x)}
such that

(7)

T^{n-1}(x)\in I_{a_{n}(x)}.

The following result establishes a relationship between
\log|(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)|
and
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log a_{k}(x)
. It allows us to transform the study of fast Lyapunov exponents to the investigation of the fast growth rate of
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log a_{k}(x)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Given a Markov-Rényi map
T
, for any
x\in\Lambda
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\left|\gamma\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log a_{k}(x)-\log|(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)|\right|\leq n\log C,

where the parameters
\gamma
and
C
are given in Hypothesis
(5)
of Definition
1.1
.

Proof.

Given a Markov-Rényi map
T
, for any
x\in\Lambda
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows from the chain rule that

(8)

\log|(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)|=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log|T^{\prime}(T^{k-1}(x))|.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the integer
a_{k}(x)
is uniquely determined by the relation
T^{k-1}(x)\in I_{a_{k}(x)}
. From Hypothesis (5) in Definition
1.1
, we derive that

C^{-1}a_{k}^{\gamma}(x)\leq|T^{\prime}(T^{k-1}(x))|\leq Ca_{k}^{\gamma}(x).

That is,

\gamma\log a_{k}(x)-\log C\leq\log|T^{\prime}(T^{k-1}(x))|\leq\gamma\log a_{k}(x)+\log C.

Combining this with (
8
), we conclude that

\left|\gamma\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log a_{k}(x)-\log|(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)|\right|\leq n\log C.

The proof is completed.
∎

2.2.
Cylinder sets

Let
T
be a Markov-Rényi map. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, denote by

(9)

I_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\coloneqq\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}T^{-j+1}I_{i_{j}}

the
cylinder set
of order
n
associated with
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
.

Except for a countable set, the cylinder set
I_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
coincides with the set of points whose symbolic codings begin with
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}
.
Cylinder sets and their diameters play a fundamental role in the study of the Hausdorff dimension of sets associated with Markov-Rényi maps.
The following estimate can be derived from Hypothesis (5) of Definition
1.1
of Markov-Rényi maps.

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, the cylinder set
I_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
is an interval, and its diameter satisfies

\frac{C^{-n}}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{\gamma}}\leq|I_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|\leq\frac{C^{n}}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{\gamma}},

where the parameters
\gamma
and
C
are given in Hypothesis (5) of Definition
1.1
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. We first show that the cylinder set is an interval. The proof follows from the arguments provided by Sarig
[
28
, page 13]
. Note that
T_{i_{1}}
is
C^{1}
monotonic, and one-to-one. By Hypothesis (4) of Definition
1.1
, we see that
T(I_{i_{1}})\supset I_{i_{2}}
, and so

T(I_{i_{1}}\cap T^{-1}I_{i_{2}})=T(I_{i_{1}})\cap I_{i_{2}}=I_{i_{2}}.

This implies that
I_{i_{1}}\cap T^{-1}I_{i_{2}}
is an interval which is mapped by
T
onto
I_{i_{2}}
.
Similarly, note that
T(I_{i_{2}})\supset I_{i_{3}}
and
T^{2}(I_{i_{1}}\cap T^{-1}I_{i_{2}})=T(I_{i_{2}})
. We derive that the restriction of
T^{2}
to the interval
I_{i_{1}}\cap T^{-1}I_{i_{2}}
is equal to the restriction of
T_{i_{2}}\circ T_{i_{1}}
to that interval, which is
C^{1}
monotonic, and one-to-one. It follows that
I_{i_{1}}\cap T^{-1}I_{i_{2}}\cap T^{-2}I_{i_{3}}
is an interval which is mapped by
T^{2}
onto
I_{i_{3}}
. Continuing in this way, we see that
I_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
defined in (
9
) is an interval which is mapped by
T^{n-1}
onto
I_{i_{n}}
.

The estimate of the diameter of
I_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
is a consequence of Hypothesis (5) of Definition
1.1
and the mean value theorem.
∎

2.3.
The distribution of codings

Let
\{s_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be sequences of real numbers such that
s_{n},t_{n}\geq 2
for all
n\geq 1
.
Define

(10)

E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:s_{n}<a_{n}(x)\leq s_{n}+t_{n},\forall n\geq 1\right\}.

Throughout this section, we assume that the following two hypotheses hold:

(11)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log s_{k}}{n}=\infty

and

(12)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{s_{n}}{t_{n}}>0.

The Hausdorff dimension of
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
is as follows.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that
\{s_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfy Hypotheses (
11
) and (
12
). Then

(13)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log t_{k}}{\gamma\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\log s_{k}-\log t_{n+1}}.

Before we proceed the proof, let us first provide two useful lemmas from Falconer
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
5
, Example 4.6]
)
.

Let
E_{0}\supset E_{1}\supset\cdots
be a decreasing sequence of sets and
E=\bigcap_{n\geq 0}E_{n}
. Assume that each
E_{n}
is a union of a finite number of disjoint closed intervals (called basic intervals of order
n
) and each basic interval in
E_{n-1}
contains
m_{n}
intervals of
E_{n}
that are separated by gaps of at least
\epsilon_{n}
. If
m_{n}\geq 2
and
\epsilon_{n}>\epsilon_{n+1}>0
for every
n\geq 1
, then

(14)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(m_{1}m_{2}\cdots m_{n-1})}{-\log(m_{n}\epsilon_{n})}.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
5
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

Suppose
F
can be
covered by
\mathcal{N}_{n}
sets of diameter at most
\delta_{n}

with
\delta_{n}\rightarrow 0

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Then

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}F\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{n}}{-\log\delta_{n}}.

We are now ready to prove Proposition
2.3
.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

We will apply Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
to prove Proposition
2.3
. To do this, we first characterize the set
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
.
For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

(15)

\mathcal{C}_{n}\coloneqq\{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:s_{j}<i_{j}\leq s_{j}+t_{j},1\leq j\leq n\}.

For any
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
, we define the
basic interval
of order
n
as follows:

(16)

J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\coloneqq\bigcup_{s_{n+1}<k\leq s_{n+1}+t_{n+1}}\overline{I}_{n+1}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n},k).

Note that
J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
is a closed interval. Define
E_{0}\coloneqq[0,1]
, and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

(17)

E_{n}\coloneqq\bigcup_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n}).

Then

E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})\subseteq\cap_{n\geq 0}E_{n}\subseteq E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})\cup\mathcal{Q}.

Note that
\mathcal{Q}
is a countable set. These lead us to the conclusion that
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
and
\cap_{n\geq 0}E_{n}
have the same Hausdorff dimension, namely

(18)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(\cap_{n\geq 0}E_{n}).

We will proceed the proof in the following two parts.

Part I:
In this part, we use Lemma
2.4
to establish a lower bound for
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
. Based on (
18
), we do the lower bound by identifying
E_{n}
in Lemma
2.4
with the set in Equation (
17
).
It is important to note that
J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
serves as a refinement of
I_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
, such that different basic intervals maintain a non-trivial gap between them. The estimation of
m_{n}
follows directly from (
16
). Applying Proposition
2.2
, we are able to estimate
\epsilon_{n}
. The combination of these estimations result in a lower bound for
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
, as indicated in (
14
).

First, we establish an estimate for
m_{n}
. By the structure of basic intervals in (
16
), we deduce that each basic interval of order
n-1
contains

(19)

\frac{t_{n}}{2}<\lfloor t_{n}\rfloor\leq m_{n}=\lfloor s_{n}+t_{n}\rfloor-\lfloor s_{n}\rfloor<t_{n}+1\leq 2t_{n}

basic intervals of order
n
.

Next let us estimate
\epsilon_{n}
. Assume that
J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
and
J_{n}(i^{*}_{1},\dots,i^{*}_{n})
are two different basic intervals in
E_{n}
. Then they are separated by

I_{n+1}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n},1)\quad\mathrm{or}\quad I_{n+1}(i^{*}_{1},\dots,i^{*}_{n},1).

This means that the gap between these two basic intervals is at least

\epsilon_{n}\coloneqq\min_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}\left\{|I_{n+1}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n},1)|\right\}.

In view of (
12
), there exists a constant
\theta>0
such that
s_{j}>\theta t_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, for any
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
, we have
i_{j}\leq s_{j}+t_{j}<(1+1/\theta)s_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. It follows from Proposition
2.2
that

\displaystyle|I_{n+1}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n},1)|\geq\frac{C^{-n-1}}{((1+1/\theta)s_{1}(1+1/\theta)s_{2}\cdots(1+1/\theta)s_{n})^{\gamma}}=\frac{C^{-n-1}}{(1+1/\theta)^{n\gamma}(s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n})^{\gamma}},

which implies that

(20)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{n}\geq\frac{C^{-n-1}}{(1+1/\theta)^{n\gamma}(s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n})^{\gamma}}.

Combining Lemma
2.4
with (
18
), (
19
) and (
20
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(m_{1}m_{2}\cdots m_{n})}{-\log(m_{n+1}\epsilon_{n+1})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{n})-n\log 2}{\gamma\log(s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n}s_{n+1})-\log t_{n+1}+\ell_{1}(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{n})}{\gamma\log(s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n}s_{n+1})-\log t_{n+1}},

\displaystyle\ \big(\text{by}\ \eqref{e.sumlog_s_k/n=infty}\big)

where
\ell_{1}(n)\coloneqq\left(\gamma\log(1+1/\theta)+\log C\right)n+\log\left(2C^{2}(1+1/\theta)^{\gamma}\right)
is a linear function of
n
.
Then we obtain the desired lower bound for
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
as (
13
).

Part II:
In this part, we use Lemma
2.5
to give an upper bound for
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
by covering
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
with
E_{n}
for each
n\geq 1
with
\mathcal{N}_{n}\coloneqq\#\mathcal{C}_{n}
, the number of basic intervals of order
n
.
The estimation of
\mathcal{N}_{n}
follows directly from the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
in (
15
) by

\mathcal{N}_{n}<2t_{1}\cdot 2t_{2}\cdots 2t_{n}=2^{n}t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{n}.

We estimate the diameter of the basic interval of order
n
. For any
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
, we have
i_{j}>s_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. Note that

\sum_{s_{n+1}<k\leq s_{n+1}+t_{n+1}}\frac{1}{k^{\gamma}}<\frac{t_{n+1}+1}{s_{n+1}^{\gamma}}\leq\frac{2t_{n+1}}{s_{n+1}^{\gamma}}.

By (
16
) and Proposition
2.2
, we deduce that

\displaystyle|J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|\leq\frac{C^{n+1}}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{\gamma}}\sum_{s_{n+1}<k\leq s_{n+1}+t_{n+1}}\frac{1}{k^{\gamma}}<\frac{2C^{n+1}t_{n+1}}{(s_{1}\cdots s_{n}s_{n+1})^{\gamma}}.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\delta_{n}\coloneqq\frac{2C^{n+1}t_{n+1}}{(s_{1}\cdots s_{n}s_{n+1})^{\gamma}}.

Hence,
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
is covered by
\mathcal{N}_{n}
basic intervals of diameter at most
\delta_{n}
. According to (
18
) and Lemma
2.5
, we derive that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{n}}{-\log\delta_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{n\log 2+\log(t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{n})}{\gamma\log(s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n}s_{n+1})-\log t_{n+1}+\ell_{2}(n)}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{n})}{\gamma\log(s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n}s_{n+1})-\log t_{n+1}},

\displaystyle\quad\big(\text{by}\ \eqref{e.sumlog_s_k/n=infty}\big)

where
\ell_{2}(n)\coloneqq-(n+1)\log C-\log 2
is a linear function of
n
. Then we obtain the required upper bound for
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
as (
13
). Therefore, the proof of Proposition
2.3
is completed.
∎

Recall that

(21)

E(\{e^{n}\},\{e^{n}\})\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:e^{n}<a_{n}(x)\leq 2e^{n},\forall n\geq 1\right\}.

As a consequence of Proposition
2.3
, we are able to determine its Hausdorff dimension.

Corollary 2.6
.

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{e^{n}\},\{e^{n}\})=\frac{1}{\gamma}.

We end this section by providing the Hausdorff dimension of the following set

(22)

\Lambda_{\infty}\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}{n}=\infty\right\}.

Proposition 2.7
.

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\Lambda_{\infty}=\frac{1}{\gamma}.

Proof.

For the lower bound of
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\Lambda_{\infty}
, since
E(\{e^{n}\},\{e^{n}\})\subset\Lambda_{\infty}
, it follows from Corollary
2.6
that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\Lambda_{\infty}\geq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{e^{n}\},\{e^{n}\})=\frac{1}{\gamma}.

For the upper bound of
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\Lambda_{\infty}
, let
\epsilon>0
be an arbitrary real number and
s\coloneqq(1+2\epsilon)/\gamma
.
Choosing a sufficiently large number
K>1
such that

(23)

K^{\epsilon}>2C^{s}J_{\epsilon}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad K^{\gamma}>2C,

where

(24)

J_{\epsilon}\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{1+\epsilon}}<\infty.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

(25)

\mathcal{C}_{n}(K)\coloneqq\{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:i_{1}\cdots i_{n}\geq K^{n}\}.

Then, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
\Lambda_{\infty}
is covered by

(26)

\bigcup_{n=N}^{\infty}\bigcup_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}(K)}I_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n}).

For any
\delta>0
, by Proposition
2.2
, (
23
) and (
25
), there exists
M\coloneqq\lfloor\log_{2}1/\delta\rfloor+1
,
such that for all
n>M
and
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}(K)
,

|I_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|\leq\frac{C^{n}}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{\gamma}}\leq\frac{C^{n}}{K^{\gamma n}}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{M}<\delta.

Consequently, when
N>M
, (
26
) is a
\delta
-cover of
\Lambda_{\infty}
. We are ready to calculate the Hausdorff measure of
\Lambda_{\infty}
. For any
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}(K)
, we deduce from Proposition
2.2
that

|I_{n}(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})|^{s}\leq\frac{C^{sn}}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{1+2\epsilon}}\leq\left(\frac{C^{s}}{K^{\epsilon}}\right)^{n}\frac{1}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{1+\epsilon}}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\delta}^{s}(\Lambda_{\infty})\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}(K)}|I_{n}(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})|^{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{C^{s}}{K^{\varepsilon}}\right)^{n}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}(K)}\frac{1}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{1+\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{C^{s}J_{\epsilon}}{K^{\epsilon}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}=0.

Letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Lambda_{\infty})=0
, and so
\dim_{\text{H}}\Lambda_{\infty}\leq s
. Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we obtain

\dim_{\text{H}}\Lambda_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}.

∎

Note that
E(\{e^{n}\},\{e^{n}\})\subset L(\infty)\subset\Lambda_{\infty}
. We deduce from Corollary
2.6
and Propositions
2.1
and
2.7
that

\dim_{\rm H}L(\infty)=\frac{1}{\gamma}.

This shows that the Lyapunov spectrum
L(\alpha)
is continuous at infinity. Hence, the proof of Proposition
1.6
is completed.

3.
Proof of Theorem
A

In this section, we provide the proof of Theorem
A
.
Assume that
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a function such that
\psi(n)/n\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

3.1.
Case
\alpha=0

For any
x\in\Lambda
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

(27)

\Pi_{n}(x)\coloneqq\prod^{n}_{i=1}a_{i}(x)

be the product of the first
n
terms in the coding sequence of
x
. The behavior of
\Pi_{n}(x)
is as follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

For Lebesgue almost every
x\in I^{*}
,

(28)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\Pi_{n}(x)<\infty.

Proof.

For any
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we claim that

(29)

\mathcal{L}\left\{x\in\Lambda:2^{kn}<\Pi_{n}(x)\leq 2^{(k+1)n}\right\}<\left(\frac{2eC(k+2)}{2^{k(\gamma-1)}}\right)^{n},

where
\mathcal{L}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
. To see this, let

\mathcal{B}_{n}(k):=\left\{(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:2^{kn}<\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}\leq 2^{(k+1)n}\right\}.

Then the set in (
29
) can be written as

\bigcup_{(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\in\mathcal{B}_{n}(k)}I_{n}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}).

For any
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\in\mathcal{B}_{n}(k)
, we deduce from Proposition
2.2
that

\mathcal{L}(I_{n}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}))=|I_{n}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})|\leq\frac{C^{n}}{(\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n})^{\gamma}}<\frac{C^{n}}{2^{k\gamma n}}.

Next, we estimate the cardinality of
\mathcal{B}_{n}(k)
. For any
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\in\mathcal{B}_{n}(k)
, let
\tau_{i}:=\lfloor\log_{2}\sigma_{i}\rfloor
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Then
\tau_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
and
2^{\tau_{i}}\leq\sigma_{i}<2^{\tau_{i}+1}
. This implies that each value of
\tau_{i}
corresponds to
2^{\tau_{i}}
many possible values of
\sigma_{i}
. Since
2^{kn}<\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}\leq 2^{(k+1)n}
, it follows that
(k-1)n<\sum^{n}_{i=1}\tau_{i}\leq(k+1)n
. Thus,

(30)

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{B}_{n}(k)\leq 2^{(k+1)n}\cdot\#\left\{(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{\geq 0}:\sum^{n}_{i=1}\tau_{i}\leq(k+1)n\right\}.

The second term on the right-hand side of (
30
) counts the number of nonnegative integer solutions to the inequality
\sum^{n}_{i=1}\tau_{i}\leq(k+1)n
. Equivalently, it represents the number of ways to distribute at most
(k+1)n
indistinguishable units among
n
nonnegative integer variables. This quantity is given by the well-known combinatorial identity:

\sum^{(k+1)n}_{j=0}\binom{j+n-1}{n-1}=\binom{(k+2)n}{n}.

Since
n!>n^{n}e^{-n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we deduce that

\binom{(k+2)n}{n}<\frac{\left((k+2)n\right)^{n}}{n!}<\left(e(k+2)\right)^{n}.

Note that
2^{\sum^{n}_{i=1}\tau_{i}}\leq 2^{(k+1)n}
. Combining these with (
30
), we see that

\#\mathcal{B}_{n}(k)\leq\left(e(k+2)\cdot 2^{(k+1)}\right)^{n}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left\{x\in\Lambda:2^{kn}<\Pi_{n}(x)\leq 2^{(k+1)n}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\#\mathcal{B}_{n}(k)\cdot\mathcal{L}(I_{n}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}))

\displaystyle<\left(e(k+2)\cdot 2^{(k+1)}\right)^{n}\cdot\frac{C^{n}}{2^{k\gamma n}}=\left(\frac{2eC(k+2)}{2^{k(\gamma-1)}}\right)^{n}.

Finally, we turn to the proof of (
28
). To this end, we choose a sufficiently large integer
k_{0}\geq 2
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
,

\frac{2eC(k+2)}{2^{k(\gamma-1)}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{k(\gamma-1)/2}}.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows from (
29
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left\{x\in\Lambda:\Pi_{n}(x)>2^{k_{0}n}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=k_{0}}\mathcal{L}\left\{x\in\Lambda:2^{kn}<\Pi_{n}(x)\leq 2^{(k+1)n}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=k_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{2^{n(\gamma-1)/2}}\right)^{k}\leq\frac{2^{(\gamma-1)/2}}{2^{(\gamma-1)/2}-1}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{(\gamma-1)n}}

which yields that

\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\mathcal{L}\left\{x\in\Lambda:\Pi_{n}(x)>2^{k_{0}n}\right\}<\infty.

From the Borel-Cantelli lemma, we conclude that for Lebesgue almost every
x\in I^{*}
,
\Pi_{n}(x)\leq 2^{k_{0}n}
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, (
28
) holds as required.
∎

The proof of Theorem
A
for the case
\alpha=0
follows directly from Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
3.1
.

3.2.
Case
0<\alpha<\infty

For any
0<\alpha<\infty
, define

(31)

E_{\psi}(\alpha)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log a_{k}(x)}{\psi(n)}=\alpha\right\}.

By Proposition
2.1
, we see that
J_{\psi}(\alpha)=E_{\psi}(\alpha/\gamma)
. This allows us to transform the study of the level set of fast Lyapunov exponents into the investigation of
E_{\psi}(\alpha)
.

We first give the necessary and sufficient conditions for
E_{\psi}(\alpha)
to be non-empty.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
0<\alpha<\infty
,
E_{\psi}(\alpha)
is non-empty if and only if
\psi
is equivalent to an increasing function.

Proof.

Let
0<\alpha<\infty
be fixed.
For the “only if” part, we assume that
E_{\psi}(\alpha)
is non-empty. Take
x\in E_{\psi}(\alpha)
, and define

\varphi(n)\coloneqq\frac{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}{\alpha},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then
\varphi(n+1)\geq\varphi(n)
, and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(n)}{\psi(n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}{\alpha\psi(n)}=1.

This means that
\psi
is equivalent to the increasing function
\varphi
.

For the “if” part, suppose that
\psi
is equivalent to an increasing function
\widehat{\varphi}
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\widehat{\varphi}(n)}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\psi(n)}{n}\cdot\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\widehat{\varphi}(n)}{\psi(n)}=\infty.

Let
s_{1}=t_{1}\coloneqq e^{1+\alpha\widehat{\varphi}(1)}
and
s_{n}=t_{n}\coloneqq e^{1+\alpha(\widehat{\varphi}(n)-\widehat{\varphi}(n-1))}
for all
n\geq 2
. Then
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
as defined in (
10
) is non-empty. Moreover,
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
is a subset of
E_{\psi}(\alpha)
. In fact, for any
x\in E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
, we deduce that

e^{1+\alpha\widehat{\varphi}(1)}<a_{1}(x)\leq 2e^{1+\alpha\widehat{\varphi}(1)}

and

e^{1+\alpha(\widehat{\varphi}(n)-\widehat{\varphi}(n-1))}<{a_{n}}(x)\leq 2e^{1+\alpha(\widehat{\varphi}(n)-\widehat{\varphi}(n-1))},\quad\forall n\geq 2.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\alpha\cdot\frac{\widehat{\varphi}(n)+n}{\widehat{\varphi}(n)}<\frac{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}{\widehat{\varphi}(n)}\leq\alpha\cdot\frac{\widehat{\varphi}(n)+(1+\log 2)n}{\widehat{\varphi}(n)},

which implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}{\widehat{\varphi}(n)}=1.

Thus,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}{\psi(n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}{\widehat{\varphi}(n)}\cdot\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\widehat{\varphi}(n)}{\psi(n)}=\alpha,

that is,
x\in E_{\psi}(\alpha)
. Hence,
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})\subset E_{\psi}(\alpha)
, and so
E_{\psi}(\alpha)
is non-empty.
∎

In the following, we calculate the Hausdorff dimension of
E_{\psi}(\alpha)
whenever it is non-empty.
By Lemma
3.2
, without loss of generality, we assume that
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is increasing. For any
0<\alpha<\infty
, we will show that

\dim_{\rm H}E_{\psi}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)\beta_{\psi}+1},

where

\beta_{\psi}\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\psi(n+1)}{\psi(n)}.

The proof is divided into two parts: the lower and upper bounds of
\dim_{\rm H}E_{\psi}(\alpha)
.

For the lower bound of
\dim_{\rm H}E_{\psi}(\alpha)
, define two sequences
\{s_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
by putting
s_{1}=t_{1}\coloneqq e^{\alpha\psi(1)+1}
and
s_{n}=t_{n}\coloneqq e^{\alpha(\psi(n)-\psi(n-1))+1}
for all
n\geq 2
. Since
\psi(n)/n\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, we derive that
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
in (
10
) is a subset of
E_{\psi}(\alpha)
.
From Proposition
2.3
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E_{\psi}(\alpha)\geq

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathbb{E}(\{s_{n}\}\{t_{n}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log t_{k}}{\gamma\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\log s_{k}-\log t_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{n+\alpha\psi(n)}{(\gamma-1)\alpha\psi(n+1)+\alpha\psi(n)+\gamma(n+1)-1}

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)\beta_{\psi}+1}.

To establish the upper bound of
\dim_{\rm H}E_{\psi}(\alpha)
, we define, for any
x\in\Lambda
,

(33)

\mathrm{k}(x)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}{n}

and

(34)

\tau(x)\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{n+1}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}=1+\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log a_{n+1}(x)}{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}.

We claim that
E_{\psi}(\alpha)
is a subset of the set of
x\in\Lambda
for which
\tau(x)=\beta_{\psi}
and
\mathrm{k}(x)=\infty
. To see this, for any
x\in E_{\psi}(\alpha)
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}{\psi(n)}=\alpha.

Since
\psi(n)/n\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, it follows from (
33
) that
\mathrm{k}(x)=\infty
. In view of (
34
), we derive that

\tau(x)\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{n+1}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log a_{k}(x)}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\psi(n+1)}{\psi(n)}=\beta_{\psi}.

Thus, the assertion holds.

For any
1\leq\beta\leq\infty
, let

(35)

\Gamma_{\infty}(\beta)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:\tau(x)\geq\beta,\mathrm{k}(x)=\infty\right\}.

Then

(36)

E_{\psi}(\alpha)\subset\Gamma_{\infty}(\beta).

The following lemma gives an upper bound of the Hausdorff dimension of
\Gamma_{\infty}(\beta)
, which in turns provides the upper bound for
\dim_{\rm H}E_{\psi}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
1\leq\beta\leq\infty
, we have

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\beta)\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)\beta+1}.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by considering three cases:
\beta=1,1<\beta<\infty
, and
\beta=\infty
.

Case I:
\beta=1.
We observe that

{\Gamma}_{\infty}(1)=\Lambda_{\infty},

where
\Lambda_{\infty}
is defined in (
22
). It follows from Proposition
2.7
that

(37)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}{\Gamma}_{\infty}(1)=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\Lambda_{\infty}=\frac{1}{\gamma},

which completes the proof for this case.

Case II:
1<\beta<\infty.
For any
0<\epsilon<\beta-1
, let
s\coloneqq\frac{1+2\epsilon}{(\gamma-1)(\beta-\epsilon)+1}
. Choose an integer
M\geq 2
sufficiently large such that

(38)

M^{\epsilon}>2J_{\epsilon}C^{s},

where
J_{\epsilon}
is defined in (
24
).
For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

(39)

B_{n}(\epsilon,M)\coloneqq\Big\{x\in\Lambda:a_{n+1}(x)>\big(\prod_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}(x)\big)^{\beta-1-\epsilon}\ \text{and}\ \prod_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}(x)\geq M^{n}\Big\}.

Thus,

(40)

{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\beta)\subset\bigcap_{N=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{n=N}^{\infty}B_{n}(\epsilon,M).

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

(41)

\mathcal{D}_{n}(M)\coloneqq\left\{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:i_{1}\cdots i_{n}\geq M^{n}\right\},

and for any
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(M)
, let

(42)

J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\coloneqq\bigcup_{k>(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{\beta-1-\epsilon}}I_{n+1}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n},k).

Then
B_{n}(\epsilon,M)
can be reformulated as

(43)

B_{n}(\epsilon,M)=\bigcup_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(M)}J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n}).

Combining this with (
40
), for every
N\geq 1
, we see that,

(44)

{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\beta)\subset\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=N}\bigcup_{(i_{0},\dots,i_{n-1})\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(M)}J_{n}(i_{0},\dots,i_{n-1}).

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(M)
, we estimate the diameter of
J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
. Since

\sum_{k>(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{\beta-1-\epsilon}}\frac{1}{k^{\gamma}}\leq\frac{2^{\gamma-1}}{(\gamma-1)(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{(\beta-1-\epsilon)(\gamma-1)}},

we deduce from Proposition
2.2
and (
42
) that

\displaystyle|J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|\leq\frac{C^{n+1}}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{\gamma}}\sum_{k>(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{\beta-1-\epsilon}}\frac{1}{k^{\gamma}}\leq\frac{C^{n+1}2^{\gamma-1}}{(\gamma-1)(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{(\gamma-1)(\beta-\epsilon)+1}}.

Note that
i_{1}\cdots i_{n}\geq M^{n}
, we derive that

|J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|^{s}\leq\left(\frac{2^{\gamma-1}C}{\gamma-1}\right)^{s}\frac{C^{sn}}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{1+2\epsilon}}\leq\left(\frac{2^{\gamma-1}C}{\gamma-1}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{C^{s}}{M^{\epsilon}}\right)^{n}\frac{1}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{1+\epsilon}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(M)}|J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|^{s}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2^{\gamma-1}C}{\gamma-1}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{C^{s}}{M^{\epsilon}}\right)^{n}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(M)}\frac{1}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{1+\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2^{\gamma-1}C}{\gamma-1}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{J_{\epsilon}C^{s}}{M^{\epsilon}}\right)^{n}.

From the definition of the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{s}\left({\Gamma}_{\infty}(\beta)\right)\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(M)}|J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|^{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2^{\gamma-1}C}{\gamma-1}\right)^{s}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{J_{\epsilon}C^{s}}{M^{\epsilon}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2^{\gamma-1}C}{\gamma-1}\right)^{s}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}=0.

\displaystyle\ \big(\text{by}\ \eqref{e.def_M}\big)

This implies that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\beta)\leq s\coloneqq\frac{1+2\epsilon}{(\gamma-1)(\beta-\epsilon)+1}.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we obtain

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\beta)\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)\beta+1}.

Case III:
\beta=\infty.
For any
K>1
, since

{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\infty)\subset{\Gamma}_{\infty}(K),

it follows from the result of Case II that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\infty)\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}{\Gamma}_{\infty}(K)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)K+1}.

Taking the limit as
K\to\infty
, we have
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\infty)=0
.
∎

By Lemma
3.3
, we conclude that for any
0<\alpha<\infty
,

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E_{\psi}(\alpha)\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\beta_{\psi})=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)\beta_{\psi}+1}.

Combining this with (
32
) and Proposition
2.1
, we complete the proof of Theorem
A
for the case
0<\alpha<\infty
.

Corollary 3.5
.

. Then

(45)

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\left\{x\in\Lambda:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log a_{n}(x)}{\phi(n)}=1\right\}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma+(\gamma-1)\xi_{\phi}}.

4.
Proof of Theorem
B

In this section, we provide the proof of Theorem
B
.
Assume that
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a function such that
\psi(n)/n\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Since
J_{\psi}(0)\subset\overline{J}_{\psi}(0)
and
J_{\psi}(0)\subset\underline{J}_{\psi}(0)
, it follows from Theorem
A
that
\overline{J}_{\psi}(0)
and
\underline{J}_{\psi}(0)
are of full Lebesgue measure. Hence, they have Hausdorff dimension one. This completes the proof of Theorem
B
for the case
\alpha=0
.

For any
0<\alpha\leq\infty
, define

\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log a_{k}(x)}{\psi(n)}=\alpha\right\}

and

\underline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log a_{k}(x)}{\psi(n)}=\alpha\right\}.

To prove Theorem
B
, by Proposition
2.1
, it is sufficient to calculate the Hausdorff dimension of
\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
and
\underline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
.

For any
b,c\in(1,\infty)
, define

\overline{D}(b,c)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:\Pi_{n}(x)\geq b^{c^{n}}\ \mathrm{for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

and

\underline{D}(b,c)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:\Pi_{n}(x)\geq b^{c^{n}}\ \mathrm{for\ sufficiently\ large}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

where
\Pi_{n}(x)
is given by (
27
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
b,c\in(1,\infty)
be fixed. For any
d\in(1,c)
, if
x\in\overline{D}(b,c)
, then

(46)

\Pi_{n+1}(x)>\max\left\{(\Pi_{n}(x))^{d},b^{d^{n+1}}\right\}\ \mathrm{for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Fix
b,c\in(1,\infty)
and
d\in(1,c)
. For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\overline{D}(a,c)
, since
d<c
, we can find an integer
k>m
such that

\Pi_{m}(x)<b^{c^{k}d^{m-k}}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\Pi_{k}(x)\geq b^{c^{k}}.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
f(n)\coloneqq b^{c^{k}d^{n-k}}
. It follows that

\Pi_{m}(x)<f(m)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\Pi_{k}(x)\geq f(k).

Choose the largest
n
such that
m\leq n<k
and
\Pi_{n}(x)<f(n)
. Combining this with the observation that
f(n)>b^{d^{n}}
, we derive that

\Pi_{n+1}(x)\geq f(n+1)=(f(n))^{d}>\max\left\{(\Pi_{n}(x))^{d},b^{d^{n+1}}\right\}.

The proof is completed.
∎

The Hausdorff dimension of
\overline{D}(b,c)
and
\underline{D}(b,c)
is as follows.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
b,c\in(1,\infty)
,

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{D}(b,c)=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{D}(b,c)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)c+1}.

Proof.

Fix
b,c\in(1,\infty)
.
Since
\underline{D}(b,c)\subset\overline{D}(b,c)
, it suffices to calculate the lower bound of
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{D}(b,c)
and the upper bound of
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{D}(b,c)
.

The lower bound of
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{D}(b,c)
follows from Proposition
2.3
by setting
s_{n}=t_{n}\coloneqq 2b^{c^{n}}
. That is,

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{D}(b,c)\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log(2b^{c^{k}})}{\gamma\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\log(2b^{c^{k}})-\log(2b^{c^{n+1}})}=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)c+1}.

For the upper bound of
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{D}(b,c)
, we construct a cover of
\overline{D}(b,c)
as follows. Fix
d\in(1,c)
and
s\in(1/d,1)
. For any
x\in\overline{D}(b,c)
, by Lemma
4.1
, we deduce that

\Pi_{n+1}(x)\geq\max\{(\Pi_{n}(x))^{d},b^{d^{n+1}}\}>(\Pi_{n}(x))^{sd}b^{(1-s)d^{n+1}}

for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that

(47)

\overline{D}(b,c)\subset\bigcap_{N=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{n=N}^{\infty}\left\{x\in\Lambda:a_{n+1}(x)>(\Pi_{n}(x))^{sd-1}b^{(1-s)d^{n+1}}\right\}.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, let

(48)

J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\coloneqq\bigcup_{j>(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{sd-1}b^{(1-s)d^{n+1}}}I_{n+1}(i,\dots,i_{n},j).

Then the limsup set on the right-hand side of (
47
) can be written as

\bigcap_{N=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{n=N}^{\infty}\bigcup_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n}).

Hence, for every
N\geq 1
, we have

(49)

\overline{D}(b,c)\subset\bigcup_{n=N}^{\infty}\bigcup_{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n}).

which provides the desired covering.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, the diameter of
J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
is estimated as follows.
Note that

\sum_{j>(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{sd-1}b^{(1-s)d^{n+1}}}\frac{1}{j^{\gamma}}\leq\frac{2^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}\cdot\frac{1}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{(sd-1)(\gamma-1)}b^{(\gamma-1)(1-s)d^{n+1}}}.

It follows from (
48
) and Proposition
2.2
that

\displaystyle|J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|=

\displaystyle\sum_{j>(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{sd-1}b^{(1-s)d^{n+1}}}|I_{n+1}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n},j)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C^{n+1}}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{\gamma}}\cdot\sum_{j>(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{sd-1}b^{(1-s)d^{n+1}}}\frac{1}{j^{\gamma}}

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\gamma-1}C^{n+1}}{(\gamma-1)(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{sd(\gamma-1)+1}b^{(\gamma-1)(1-s)d^{n+1}}}.

For any
0<\epsilon<1
, let
t\coloneqq\frac{1+\epsilon}{(\gamma-1)sd+1}
.
From (
49
) and (
50
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{t}\left(\overline{D}(b,c)\right)\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}|J_{n}(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})|^{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\sum_{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\left(\frac{2^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}\right)^{t}\frac{C^{(n+1)t}}{(i_{1}\cdots i_{n})^{((\gamma-1)sd+1)t}b^{(\gamma-1)(1-s)td^{n+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2^{\gamma-1}C}{\gamma-1}\right)^{t}\frac{(J_{\epsilon}C^{t})^{n}}{b^{(\gamma-1)(1-s)td^{n+1}}}=0.

This implies that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{D}(b,c)\leq t\coloneqq\frac{1+\epsilon}{(\gamma-1)sd+1}.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
,
s\to 1^{-}
, and
d\to c^{-}
, we obtain

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{D}(b,c)\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)c+1}.

∎

As a consequence of Proposition
2.3
and Lemma
4.2
, we have the Łuczak-type result for MR maps.

Corollary 4.3
.

For any
b,c\in(1,\infty)
,

\dim_{\rm H}\left\{x\in\Lambda:a_{n}(x)\geq b^{c^{n}}\ \mathrm{for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)c+1}

and

\dim_{\rm H}\left\{x\in\Lambda:a_{n}(x)\geq b^{c^{n}}\ \mathrm{for\ sufficiently\ large}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)c+1}.

We are ready to prove Theorem
B
for the case
0<\alpha\leq\infty
.

4.1.
Hausdorff dimension of
\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)

For any
0<\alpha\leq\infty
, we will prove that

\dim_{\rm H}\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)b_{\psi}+1},

where

(51)

b_{\psi}\coloneqq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\psi(n)}.

The proof is divided into two parts: the upper and lower bounds of
\dim_{\rm H}\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
.

4.1.1.
Upper bound

For any
x\in\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
, when
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
, we see that
\Pi_{n}(x)\geq e^{\frac{\alpha\psi(n)}{2}}
for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
; when
\alpha=\infty
, we derive that
\Pi_{n}(x)\geq e^{\psi(n)}
for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,

(52)

\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)\subset\left\{x\in\Lambda:\Pi_{n}(x)\geq A^{\psi(n)}\ \mathrm{for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

for some
A>1
. This leads to the investigation of the Hausdorff dimension of the limsup set in (
52
).

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
A\in(1,\infty)
, define

\overline{F}(\psi)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:\Pi_{n}(x)\geq A^{\psi(n)}\ \mathrm{for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Then

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{F}(\psi)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)b_{\psi}+1}.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by considering the three cases:
b_{\psi}=1
,
1<b_{\psi}<\infty
, and
b_{\psi}=\infty
.

For the case
b_{\psi}=1
, on the one hand, since
\psi(n)/n\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, we get that
\overline{F}(\psi)
is a subset of
\Lambda_{\infty}.

By Lemma
2.7
, we see that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{F}(\psi)\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\Lambda_{\infty}=\frac{1}{\gamma}.

On the other hand, for any
\epsilon>0
, by the definition of
b_{\psi}
in (
4.2
), we derive that
\psi(n)\leq(1+\epsilon)^{n}

for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that

\underline{D}(A,1+\epsilon)\subset\overline{F}(\psi).

It follows from Lemma
4.2

that
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{F}(\psi)\geq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)(1+\epsilon)+1}
.
Letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we obtain the desired lower bound.

For the case
1<b_{\psi}<\infty
, let
0<\epsilon<b_{\psi}-1
. By the definition of
b_{\psi}
in (
4.2
), we see that
\psi(n)\leq(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n}
for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
and that
\psi(n)\geq(b_{\psi}-\epsilon)^{n}
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,

\underline{D}(A,b_{\psi}+\epsilon)\subset\overline{F}(\psi)\subset\overline{D}(A,b_{\psi}-\epsilon).

Applying Lemma
4.2
, we deduce that

\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)+1}\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{F}(\psi)\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)(b_{\psi}-\epsilon)+1}.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, it follows that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{F}(\psi)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)b_{\psi}+1}.

For the case
b_{\psi}=\infty
, let
K>1
. We have
\psi(n)>K^{n}
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
and so

\overline{F}(\psi)\subset\overline{D}(A,K).

It follows from Lemma
4.2

that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{F}(\psi)\leq\frac{1}{K(\gamma-1)+1}.

Taking the limit as
K\to\infty
, we obtain
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{F}(\psi)=0
.
∎

Combining (
52
) and Lemma
4.4
,
we deduce that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)b_{\psi}+1},

and the estimation of the upper bound for
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
is completed.

4.1.2.
Lower bound

When
b_{\psi}=\infty
, it is clear that
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)\geq 0
for all
0<\alpha\leq\infty
. When
1<b_{\psi}<\infty
, we will establish the lower bound of
\dim_{\rm H}\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
in two cases: first for
0<\alpha<\infty
, then for
\alpha=\infty
.

In the following, we assume that
1<b_{\psi}<\infty
. When
0<\alpha<\infty
, we construct a subset
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
of
\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
and then use Proposition
2.3
to estimate the lower bound of
\dim_{\rm H}\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
. More precisely, for each
\epsilon>0
, we propose to construct an increasing function
g_{\psi}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
satisfying
the following properties:

(i).

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{g_{\psi}(n)}{\psi(n)}=1;

(ii).

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{g_{\psi}(n)}{n}=\infty;

(iii).

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{g_{\psi}(n+1)}{g_{\psi}(n)}\leq b_{\psi}+\epsilon.

Let
s_{1}=t_{1}\coloneqq e^{\alpha g_{\psi}(1)}
, and for any
n\geq 2
, define
s_{n}=t_{n}\coloneqq e^{\alpha(g_{\psi}(n)-g_{\psi}(n-1))}
. We derive that
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
is a subset of
\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
.
Note that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log s_{n+1}}{\log s_{1}+\cdots+\log s_{n}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{g_{\psi}(n+1)}{g_{\psi}(n)}-1\leq b_{\psi}+\epsilon-1.

It follows from Proposition
2.3
that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)\geq

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log s_{k}}{\gamma\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\log s_{k}-\log s_{n+1}}

(53)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)+1}.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we obtain

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)b_{\psi}+1},

which establishes the desired lower bound.

Next, we provide the precise construction of the function
g_{\psi}
. For for each
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, let

(54)

c_{n,k}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-k},\quad&1\leq k\leq n;\\
\psi(k),\quad&k\geq n+1,\end{cases}

and define

(55)

g_{\psi}(n)\coloneqq\inf_{k\geq 1}\{c_{n,k}\}=\inf\left\{\psi(1)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-1},\dots,\psi(n-1)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon),\psi(n),\psi(n+1),\dots\right\}.

Since
\psi(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, the infimum in (
55
) is achieved. The smallest index at which this infimum is achieved is denoted by
t_{n}
, namely

(56)

k_{n}\coloneqq\min\{k\geq 1:c_{n,k}=g_{\psi}(n)\}.

The following statements hold. For all
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1).

c_{n,k}\leq c_{n+1,k}\leq c_{n,k}(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)\ \mathrm{and}\ g_{\psi}(n)\leq g_{\psi}(n+1)\leq g_{\psi}(n)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon);

(2).

k_{n+1}\geq k_{n}\ \mathrm{and}\ t_{n}\to\infty\ \mathrm{as}\ n\to\infty;

(3).

g_{\psi}(n)\leq\psi(n)\ \mathrm{and}\ g_{\psi}(k_{n})=\psi(k_{n});

(4).

g_{\psi}(n)/n\to\infty\ \mathrm{as}\ n\to\infty.

Then these statements imply the properties of
g_{\psi}
. Precisely, items (2) and (3) imply Property (i); item (4) implies Property (ii); item (1) implies Property (iii).

In the following, we proceed with the proof of these statements, addressing each case individually.

For the item (1), given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, when
1\leq k\leq n
, we have

\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-k}<\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n+1-k}=\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-k}(b_{\psi}+\epsilon);

when
k\geq n+1
, we have

\psi(k)=\psi(k)<\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon).

By the definition of
c_{n,k}
in (
54
), we derive that
c_{n,k}\leq c_{n+1,k}\leq c_{n,k}(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)
, and so their infimums satisfy the same relation:

g_{\psi}(n)\leq g_{\psi}(n+1)\leq g_{\psi}(n)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)
.

For the item (2), we first show the monotonicity of
\{k_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, when
k_{n}=1
, we have
k_{n+1}\geq 1=k_{n}
; when
2\leq k_{n}\leq n
, note that
i=k_{n}
if and only if

(57)

c_{n,i}<c_{n,k},\ \forall k<k_{n}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad c_{n,i}\leq c_{n,k},\ \forall k>k_{n}.

For any
k<k_{n}
, we obtain

\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-k}<\psi(k_{n})(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-k_{n}},

which implies that

\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n+1-k}<\psi(k_{n})(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n+1-k_{n}}.

Hence,
k_{n+1}\geq k_{n}
; when
k_{n}\geq n+1
, for any
k<k_{n}
, we see that

c_{n+1,k_{n}}=c_{n,k_{n}}<c_{n,k}\leq c_{n+1,k}.

It follows that
k_{n+1}\geq k_{n}
. Next, we use a proof by contradiction to show that
k_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Suppose that
\lim_{n\to\infty}k_{n}\neq\infty
. Since
\{k_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is increasing, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k_{n}=N
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for sufficiently large
n
such that
n>N
, and

\psi(n)=c_{n,n}\geq c_{n,k_{n}}=c_{n,N}=\psi(N)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-N}.

It follows that

\frac{\log\psi(N)-N\log(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)}{n}+\log(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)\leq\frac{\log\psi(n)}{n}.

Letting
n\to\infty
, we derive that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\psi(n)}{n}\geq\log(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)>\log b_{\psi},

which is a contradiction to the definition of
b_{\psi}
. Therefore,
k_{n}\to\infty\ \mathrm{as}\ n\to\infty
.

For the item (3), given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by the definition of
g_{\psi}(n)
in (
55
), we see that
g_{\psi}(n)\leq c_{n,n}=\psi(n)
. Next, we show that
g_{\psi}(k_{n})=\psi(k_{n})
.
If
k_{n}<n
, then for all
1\leq k<k_{n}
,

c_{t_{n},k}=\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-k}(b+\epsilon)^{k_{n}-n}=c_{n,k}(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{k_{n}-n}>c_{n,k_{n}}(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{k_{n}-n}=c_{k_{n},k_{n}};

for all
k_{n}<k\leq n
,

c_{k_{n},k}=c_{n,k}(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{k-n}\geq c_{n,k_{n}}(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{k-n}=c_{k_{n},k_{n}}(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{k-k_{n}}>c_{k_{n},k_{n}};

and for all
k>n
,

c_{k_{n},k}=c_{n,k}\geq c_{n,k_{n}}=c_{k_{n},k_{n}}(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-k_{n}}>c_{k_{n},k_{n}}.

It follows from (
57
) that
k_{k_{n}}=k_{n}
and
g_{\psi}(k_{n})=\psi(k_{n})
. If
k_{n}=n
, then

g_{\psi}(k_{n})=g_{\psi}(n)=c_{n,k_{n}}=c_{n,n}=\psi(n)=\psi(k_{n}).

If
k_{n}>n
, then
for all
1\leq k\leq n
,

c_{k_{n},k}=\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{k_{n}-k}\geq\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-k}=c_{n,k}>c_{n,k_{n}}=c_{k_{n},k_{n}};

for all
n<k\leq k_{n}
,

c_{k_{n},k}=\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{k_{n}-k}\geq\psi(k)=c_{n,k}>c_{n,k_{n}}=c_{k_{n},k_{n}};

and for all
k>k_{n}
,

c_{t_{n},k}=\psi(k)=c_{n,k}\geq c_{n,t_{n}}=c_{t_{n},t_{n}}.

Thus
g_{\psi}(k_{n})=\psi(k_{n})
.

For the item (4), since

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\psi(n)}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n}}{n}=\infty,

we have

(58)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\min_{1\leq k\leq n}\left\{\frac{\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-k}}{n}\right\}=\infty.

Denote
c_{n}\coloneqq\min_{1\leq k\leq n}\{\frac{\psi(k)(b_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-k}}{n}\}
. It follows that

\frac{g_{\psi}(n)}{n}=\inf\left\{c_{n},\frac{\psi(n)}{n},\frac{\psi(n+1)}{n},\cdots\right\}\geq\inf\left\{c_{n},\frac{\psi(n)}{n},\frac{\psi(n+1)}{n+1},\cdots\right\}.

Combining this with (
58
) and the hypothesis that
\psi(n)/\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, we derive that
g_{\psi}(n)/n\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

At last, we deal with the lower bound of
\dim_{\rm H}\overline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
for the case
\alpha=\infty
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
\psi^{*}(n)\coloneqq n\psi(n)
. Then

b_{\psi^{*}}\coloneqq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\psi^{*}(n)}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{n\psi(n)}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\psi(n)}=b_{\psi}.

Note that
\overline{E}_{\psi^{*}}(1)\subset\overline{E}_{\psi}(\infty)
, it follows from (
53
) that

\dim_{\rm H}\overline{E}_{\psi}(\infty)\geq\dim_{\rm H}\overline{E}_{\psi^{*}}(1)\geq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)b_{\psi^{*}}+1}=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)b_{\psi}+1}.

4.2.
Hausdorff dimension of
\underline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)

For any
0<\alpha\leq\infty
, we will prove that

(59)

\dim_{\rm H}\underline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)B_{\psi}+1},

where

B_{\psi}\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\psi(n)}.

4.2.1.
Upper bound

For any
x\in\underline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
, when
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
, we see that
\Pi_{n}(x)\geq e^{\frac{\alpha\psi(n)}{2}}
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
; when
\alpha=\infty
, we see that
\Pi_{n}(x)\geq e^{\psi(n)}
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,

(60)

\underline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)\subset\left\{x\in\Lambda:\Pi_{n}(x)\geq A^{\psi(n)}\ \text{for sufficiently large $n\in\mathbb{N}$}\right\}

for some
A>1
. This leads to the study of the Hausdorff dimension of the liminf set in (
60
).

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
A\in(1,\infty)
, define

\underline{F}(\psi)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Lambda:\Pi_{n}(x)\geq A^{\psi(n)}\ \text{for sufficiently large $n\in\mathbb{N}$}\right\}.

Then

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{F}(\psi)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)B_{\psi}+1}.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to that of Lemma
4.4
.

For the case
B_{\psi}=1
, since
\psi(n)/n\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
,
we get that
\underline{F}(\psi)
is a subset of
\Lambda_{\infty}.

By Lemma
2.7
,

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{F}(\psi)\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\Pi_{\infty}=\frac{1}{\gamma}.

For any
\epsilon>0
, by definition of
B_{\psi}
, we obtain
\psi(n)\leq(1+\epsilon)^{n}
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,

\underline{D}(A,1+\epsilon)\subset\underline{F}(\psi).

It follows from Lemma
4.2
that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{F}(\psi)\geq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)(1+\epsilon)+1}.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we get the desired lower bound.

For the case
1<B_{\psi}<\infty
, let
0<\epsilon<B_{\psi}-1
. By definition of
B_{\psi}
, we derive that
\psi(n)\leq(B_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n}
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
and that
\psi(n)\geq(B_{\psi}-\epsilon)^{n}
for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,

\underline{D}(A,B_{\psi}+\epsilon)\subset\underline{F}(\psi)\subset\overline{D}(A,B_{\psi}-\epsilon).

Applying Lemma
4.2
, we have

\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)(B_{\psi}+\epsilon)+1}\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{F}(\psi)\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)(B_{\psi}-\epsilon)+1}.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we obtain

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{F}(\psi)=\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)B_{\psi}+1}.

For the case
B_{\psi}=\infty
, given
K>1
, we have
\psi(n)>K^{n}
for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and so

\underline{F}(\psi)\subset\overline{D}(A,K).

We deduce from Lemma
4.2
that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{F}(\psi)\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)K+1}.

Letting
C\to\infty
, we obtain
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{F}(\psi)=0
.
∎

Combining (
60
) and Lemma
4.5
, we conclude that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)B_{\psi}+1}.

4.2.2.
Lower bound

When
0<\alpha<\infty
, for any
\epsilon>0
, Fang, Shang and Wu in
[
9
]
constructed a sequence
\{d_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
by
d_{1}\coloneqq T_{1}
and
d_{n}\coloneqq T_{n}/T_{n-1}
for
n\geq 2
, where
T_{n}\coloneqq\sup_{k\geq 1}\{d_{n,k}\}
and
d_{n,k}
is defined as

d_{n,k}\coloneqq\begin{cases}e^{\alpha\psi(k)},\quad&1\leq k\leq n;\\
e^{\alpha\psi(k)(B_{\psi}+\epsilon)^{n-k}},\quad&k\geq n+1.\end{cases}

They proved that
\{d_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies

(i)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log c_{k}}{\psi(n)}=\alpha;

(ii)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log c_{k}}{n}=\infty;

(iii)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log c_{n+1}}{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log c_{k}}\leq B_{\psi}+\epsilon-1.

See
[
9
, p. 828–829]
for a detailed proof. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
s_{n}=t_{n}\coloneqq 2c_{n}
. Then
E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})
is a subset of
\underline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)
. By Proposition
2.3
, we derive that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\underline{E}_{\psi}(\alpha)\geq

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}E(\{s_{n}\},\{t_{n}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log c_{k}}{\gamma\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\log c_{k}-\log c_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\gamma+(\gamma-1)\cdot\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log c_{n+1}}{\sum^{n}_{k=1}\log c_{k}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)(B_{\psi}+\epsilon)+1}.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we have

An optimal uniqueness result for Riccati equations arising in abstract parabolic control problems
Paolo Acquistapace, Francesco Bartaloni

Abstract
An abstract nonautonomous parabolic linear-quadratic regulator problem with very general final cost operator P T P_{T} is considered, subject to the same assumptions under which a classical solution of the associated differential Riccati equation was shown to exist, in two papers appeared in 1999 and 2000, by Terreni and the first named author. We prove an optimal uniqueness result for the integral Riccati equation in a wide and natural class, filling a gap existing in the autonomous case, too. In addition, we give a regularity result for the optimal state.



0 Introduction

Let
H
,
U
be Hilbert spaces. For fixed
T>0
we consider, for every
s\in[0,T[\,
, the following regulator problem: minimize the functional

J_{s}(u)=\int_{s}^{T}\|M(r)^{1/2}y(r)\|_{H}^{2}\,dr+\int_{s}^{T}\|N(r)^{1/2}u(r)\|_{U}^{2}\,dr+\|P_{T}^{1/2}y(T)\|_{H}^{2}

(0.1)

among all controls
u\in L^{2}(s,T;U)
, constrained by the following state equation, whose strong form is

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}y^{\prime}(t)=A(t)y(t)-A(t)G(t)u(t),\quad t\in\,]s,T],\\[5.69054pt]
y(s)=x,\end{array}\right.

(0.2)

and whose mild form is

y(t)=U(t,s)x-\int_{s}^{t}U(t,r)A(r)G(r)u(r)\,dr,\qquad t\in[s,T].

(0.3)

Here the operators
\{M(\cdot)\}
and
P_{T}
are linear, non-negative, bounded, selfadjoint in
H
, the operators
\{N(\cdot)\}
are linear, positive, bounded, selfadjoint in
U
,
x
is an element of
H
, the operators
\{A(r)\}_{r\in[0,T]}
are generators of analytic semigroups
\{e^{tA(r)}\}_{t\geq 0}
in
H
, for every
r\in[s,T]
the family
\{U(r,s)\}_{0\leq s\leq r}
is the evolution operator associated to
A(r)
and the operator
G(r)
is the “Green map” associated to
A(r)
, having the property that
[-A(r)]^{\alpha}G(r)
is a bounded operator from
U
into
H
for some
\alpha\in\,]0,1/2[\,
.

The pair (
0.1
)-(
0.3
) provides an abstract model for a large class of purely parabolic boundary control problems: the realization of
G(t)
in concrete problems yields the lifting of the nonzero datum at the boundary, i.e. transforms the nonhomogeneous initial-boundary value problem into a homogeneous one by a modification of the right member of the evolution equation. Typical examples are given in
[
7
, Section 9]
.

Following and generalizing the methods employed in the autonomous case by
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
(see also the full description in the book
[
15
]
), the above problem was analyzed in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
, proving existence and uniqueness of the optimal pair
(\widehat{y},\widehat{u})
, and studying the associated Riccati equation, whose differential form is

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}P^{\prime}(t)+A(t)^{*}P(t)+P(t)A(t)\\[5.69054pt]
\qquad\ =-M(t)+P(t)A(t)G(t)N(t)^{-1}G(t)^{*}A(t)^{*}P(t),\quad t\in[s,T[\,,\\[5.69054pt]
P(T)=P_{T},\end{array}\right.

(0.4)

and whose integral form is

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle P(t)=U(T,t)^{*}P_{T}U(T,t)\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle\ \ +\int_{t}^{T}U(r,t)^{*}[M(r)-P(r)A(r)G(r)N(r)^{-1}G(r)^{*}A(r)^{*}P(r)]U(r,t)\,dr,\quad t\in[s,T]\,.\end{array}

(0.5)

In particular, existence and representation of a classical solution
P(\cdot)
of equation (
0.5
) was proved; its uniqueness was guaranteed only under further assumptions on the operator
P_{T}
. More precisely, it was shown, among other things, that:

(i)

the constructed solution
P(\cdot)
of the Riccati equation (
0.5
) is classical, i.e.
P(\cdot)
is continuously differentiable as an
{\cal L}(H)
-valued function and satisfies (
0.4
) in the sense of
{\cal L}(H)
, provided the operator
A(t)^{*}P(t)+P(t)A(t)
is replaced by its bounded extension
\Lambda(t)P(t)
(see
[
7
, Section 7]
for details);

(ii)

the optimal pair enjoys some weighted Hölder continuity properties;

(iii)

the final datum
P_{T}
belongs to the largest possible class, according to the counterexample in
[
10
]
: namely, the composition
P_{T}^{1/2}L_{sT}
is a closed operator in
H
, where
L_{sT}
is defined in Hypothesis
1.7
below (see also (
1.5
)). In addition the Riccati operator

is strongly continuous in
[s,T]
, and a necessary and sufficient condition is given in order that
P(t)\to P_{T}
in
{\cal L}(H)
as
t\to T^{-}
;

(iv)

uniqueness holds provided
P_{T}\in{\cal L}(H,D([-A(T)^{*}]^{2\beta}))
for some
\beta\in\,\left]\frac{1}{2}-\alpha,1\right[\,
.

The main purpose of this paper is the proof, under the same assumptions made in

, nevertheless we show that

is continuous in the whole interval

[

]
,
[

The question of uniqueness for Riccati equations is a delicate issue. We confine ourselves to the finite horizon theory: in the autonomous setting, uniqueness was proved in the parabolic case under the assumption that
[-A(T)^{*}]^{2\beta}P_{T}
, or

, is bounded for some
\beta>\frac{1}{2}-\alpha
, by means of a suitable

(in our notation), where

is the difference of two solutions; see
[

]
,
[
14
]
,
[

]
,
[

. In the nonautonomous setting, after the pioneering paper
[
21
]
, the same method is used in
[
4
]
. A similar argument works in the autonomous hyperbolic case, see
[

, Theorems 8.3.7.1 and 9.3.5.4]
or
[

]
. In the intermediate case of the so called “singular estimate control systems” most papers use the same method: see
[
12
]
,
[
17
]
, where
P_{T}=0
, and
[
18
]
,
[

]
,
[

where the relevant parameter in the singular estimate assumption is less than

. A similar condition is required in
[

, where the method for proving uniqueness is based on a “fundamental identity” which resembles our Lemma

[

, while the proof of its uniqueness in the case

[

.

We shortly describe our method: on one hand, the fundamental identity of Lemma
2.1
allows to prove that for any solution
Q
of the integral Riccati equation it must be
P\geq Q
; on the other hand, an accurate analysis of the terms appearing in the three equivalent formulations in

, leads us to show that the opposite inequality

also holds. Our method could perhaps be useful in improving the uniqueness results in some of the papers quoted above, too.

Hypothesis 1.1

There exist

\begin{array}[]{l}\left\|A(t)[\lambda-A(t)]^{-1}\big[A(t)^{-1}-A(s)^{-1}\big]\right\|_{{\cal L}(H)}\\[5.69054pt]

Hypothesis 1.3

The number

\begin{array}[]{l}\left\|[-A(t)]^{\eta}U(t,s)[-A(s)]^{-\gamma}-[-A(\tau)]^{\eta}U(\tau,s)[-A(s)]^{-\gamma}\right\|_{{\cal L}(H)}\\[5.69054pt]

Our proof needs some auxiliary facts. In the next statements we consider a fixed operator

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\ -\int_{s}^{T-\varepsilon}\langle N(r)^{-1}G(r)^{*}A(r)^{*}Q(r)U(r,s)x,G(r)^{*}A(r)^{*}Q(r)U(r,s)y\rangle_{U}\,dr,\end{array}

Our second lemma concerns the so called “closed loop equation”.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

Proof.

Fix

[K_{s}g](t):=\int_{s}^{t}U(t,r)A(r)G(r)N(r)^{-1}G(r)^{*}A(r)^{*}Q(r)g(r)\,dr,\ \ t\in[s,T-\varepsilon],\ \ g\in L^{2}(s,T;U),

whose kernel

3.1
Proof of Theorem

(a)

We start with the following proposition:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(\widehat{u},\widehat{y})\in D(L_{sT})\times L^{2}(s,T;H)

)-(

\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\widehat{u}&\quad\textrm{in}\ [s,T-\varepsilon]\\[5.69054pt]
0&\quad\textrm{in}\ ]T-\varepsilon,T],\end{array}\right.\quad\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}=U(\cdot,s)x+L_{s}\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon};

then we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\langle P_{T}\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(T),\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(T)\rangle_{H}=\langle P_{T}\widehat{y}(T),\widehat{y}(T)\rangle_{H}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\langle P(T-\varepsilon)\widehat{y}(T-\varepsilon),\widehat{y}(T-\varepsilon)\rangle_{H}\,.

(3.2)

Proof.

It is well known that
\widehat{y}\in C([s,T[\,,H)
, so that
\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}\in C([s,T-\varepsilon],H)
. On the other hand

\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(t)=U(t,s)x+[L_{s}\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}](t)=U(t,s)x+U(t,T-\varepsilon)[L_{s}\widehat{u}](T-\varepsilon)\qquad\forall t\in[T-\varepsilon,T];

this implies

Now we have obviously

\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}\to\widehat{u}\quad\textrm{in }L^{2}(s,T;U)\quad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+};

moreover, by
[
7

P_{T}^{1/2}L_{sT}(\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{u})=-P_{T}^{1/2}L_{T-\varepsilon\,T}\widehat{u}\to 0\quad\textrm{in }H\quad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

Hence, as

,

P_{T}^{1/2}\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(T)=P_{T}^{1/2}U(T,s)x+P_{T}^{1/2}L_{sT}\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}\to P_{T}^{1/2}U(T,s)x+P_{T}^{1/2}L_{sT}\widehat{u}=P_{T}^{1/2}\widehat{y}(T)\quad\textrm{in }H,

which implies the first equality in (
3.2
).

Next, choosing in Lemma
2.1

Q=P
,

,

, we obtain by (

) and (

):

\begin{array}[]{l}\langle P(T-\varepsilon)\widehat{y}(T-\varepsilon),\widehat{y}(T-\varepsilon)\rangle_{H}-\langle P(s)x,x\rangle_{H}\\[5.69054pt]
\quad\qquad\qquad\quad\ \displaystyle=-\int_{s}^{T-\varepsilon}\|M(r)^{1/2}\widehat{y}(r)\|_{H}^{2}\,dr-\int_{s}^{T-\varepsilon}\|N(r)^{1/2}\widehat{u}(r)\|_{U}^{2}\,dr+0,\end{array}

and letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
we get, using (
2.14
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\langle P(T-\varepsilon)\widehat{y}(T-\varepsilon),\widehat{y}(T-\varepsilon)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\langle P(s)x,x\rangle_{H}-\int_{s}^{T}\|M(r)^{1/2}\widehat{y}(r)\|_{H}^{2}\,dr-\int_{s}^{T}\|N(r)^{1/2}\widehat{u}(r)\|_{U}^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle=J_{s}(\widehat{u})-\int_{s}^{T}\|M(r)^{1/2}\widehat{y}(r)\|_{H}^{2}\,dr-\int_{s}^{T}\|N(r)^{1/2}\widehat{u}(r)\|_{U}^{2}\,dr=\langle P_{T}\widehat{y}(T),\widehat{y}(T)\rangle_{H}\,,

which gives the second equality in (
3.2
).
∎

We now complete the proof of
(a)
. For fixed

, replace in (

)
\varepsilon
by
\delta
, with

, and choose

,

. Then we find

\displaystyle\langle Q(s)x,x\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle Q(T-\delta)\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(T-\delta),\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(T-\delta)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{T-\delta}\langle M(r)\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(r),\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(r)\rangle_{H}\,dr+\int_{s}^{T-\delta}\langle N(r)\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}(r),\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}(r)\rangle_{U}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle Q(T-\delta)\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(T-\delta),\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(T-\delta)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{T-\delta}\langle M(r)\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(r),\widehat{y}_{\varepsilon}(r)\rangle_{H}\,dr+\int_{s}^{T-\delta}\langle N(r)\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}(r),\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}(r)\rangle_{U}\,dr.

As

Abstract
. The manuscript is concerned with the Rudin-Keisler order of ultrafilters on measurable cardinals. The main theorem proved reads as follows: Given regular cardinals λ ≤ κ \lambda\leq\kappa , the following theories are equiconsistent modulo ZFC: (1) κ \kappa is a measurable cardinal with o ⁡ ( κ ) = λ + o(\kappa)=\lambda^{+} (resp. o ⁡ ( κ ) = κ o(\kappa)=\kappa ). (2) The Rudin-Keisler order restricted to the set of κ \kappa -complete (non-principal) ultrafilters on κ \kappa is λ + \lambda^{+} -directed (resp. κ + \kappa^{+} -directed). The theorem reported here is proved after bridging the directedness of the RK-order with the λ \lambda -Gluing Property introduced by the authors in [ HP24 ] . Our result provides what seems to be the first example of a compactness-type property at the level of measurable cardinals whose consistency strength is much lower than the existence of a strong cardinal. As part of our analysis we also answer a question of Gitik by showing that in the model of [ Git86 ] the ℵ 0 \aleph_{0} -Gluing Property fails and as a consequence that the Rudin-Keisler order is not even ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} -directed.

Question

Let

(\aleph)

``\text{$\kappa$ is measurable with }o(\kappa)=\lambda^{+}

2.1.
Ultrafilters

Let
U_{0},U_{1}
be ultrafilters. We write
U_{0}\leq_{\mathrm{RK}}U_{1}
if there is a function
\pi\colon\bigcup U_{1}\rightarrow\bigcup U_{0}
such that for every set
X\subseteq\bigcup U_{0}

X\in U_{0}
if and only if
\pi^{-1}(X)\in U_{1}.

Equivalently, there is a function

\pi_{*}(U_{1}):=\{X\subseteq\kappa\mathrel{|}\allowbreak\pi^{-1}(X)\in U_{1}\}.

In the above circumstances we will say that

We will abbreviate

U_{0}\equiv_{\mathrm{RK}}U_{1}

The relation

\equiv_{\RK}

\leq_{\RK}
in the context of
\kappa
-complete non-principal ultrafilters over an uncountable cardinal
\kappa
. Moving forward we will denote this set by
\mathfrak{U}_{\kappa}.
Since there are only
2^{\kappa}
-many functions

no member of
\mathfrak{U}_{\kappa}
can have more than

-many

For a regular cardinal

2.2.
Forcing

The main forcing technology utilized in this manuscript are forcings of Prikry-type.
Here we adopt the perspective on Prikry-type posets proposed by Gitik
[
Git10
, §6]
. Specifically,

Definition 2.1
.

A triple
\langle\mathbb{P},\leq,\leq^{*}\rangle
is called a
Prikry-type forcing
if

(1)

\leq^{*}\subseteq\leq
,

(2)

\leq^{*}
has the
Prikry property
; to wit, for every
p\in{P}
and a statement
\varphi
in the language of forcing of
\langle\mathbb{P},\leq\rangle
there is
q\leq^{*}p
such that

q\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\varphi
or
q\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\neg\varphi.

We will typically refer to

\leq^{*}:=\leq

\leq^{*}\subsetneq\leq

\leq^{*}
will exhibit better closure properties than the regular order

In

X^{*}:=\{\beta\in\mathrm{Succ}_{T^{*},0}(\varnothing)\mathrel{|}\allowbreak\beta\in X\,\wedge\,\beta>j_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta_{\omega},\alpha_{i}}}(g)(\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1},\kappa\rangle)\}.

This is
\mathcal{U}_{\eta_{\omega},0}(\varnothing)
-large, by uniformity. Thus, there is a tree
\Upsilon^{\prime}\subseteq\dot{\Upsilon}_{K}
such that
\mathrm{Succ}_{\Upsilon^{\prime},0}(\varnothing)\subseteq X^{*}
. Let
q\leq p
be in
K
forcing this property. Then,

q^{\smallfrown}\{\langle\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\alpha_{i},\dot{\Upsilon}^{\prime}\rangle\}^{\smallfrown}r_{\alpha}\Vdash\kappa\in j_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta_{\omega},\alpha_{i}}}(\dot{Y})

and also

q^{\smallfrown}\{\langle\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\alpha_{i},\dot{\Upsilon}^{\prime}\rangle\}^{\smallfrown}r_{\alpha}\Vdash j_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta_{\omega},\alpha_{i}}}(\pi_{\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle})(\kappa)\in j_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta_{\omega},\alpha_{i}}}(\dot{X}),

because of our choice of
X^{*}.

So,
Y\cap\pi^{-1}_{\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle}X\in\mathcal{U}_{\eta_{\omega},\alpha_{i}}(\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\alpha_{i})
and we are done.
∎

Now we proceed as follows. For each
\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle\in T^{q_{0}}
with orders
\langle\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{i-1}\rangle
we let
X(\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle)
the image of
\mathrm{Succ}_{T^{q_{0}},\alpha_{i}}(\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle)
under
\pi_{\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle}
. By the above claim,
X(\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle)\in\mathcal{U}_{\eta_{\omega},0}(\varnothing)
.

Clearly, the map
h_{\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle}\colon X({\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle})\rightarrow\eta_{\omega}
given by

h_{\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle}\colon\pi_{\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle}(\gamma)\mapsto g(\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1},\gamma\rangle)

is regressive. By normality of
\mathcal{U}_{\eta_{\omega},0}(\varnothing)
we find a large set

Y({\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle})\subseteq X({\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle})

where
h_{\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle}
is constant – say, with value
g({\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle}).

Therefore,
\pi^{-1}_{{\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle}}Y({\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle})
is a
\mathcal{U}_{\eta_{\omega},\alpha_{i}}({\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle}\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\alpha_{i})
-large set where the function
g(\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1},\bullet\rangle)
is constant, and obtains the value
g({\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle}).

Using the above it is easy to define a condition
q\leq^{*}q_{0}
such that

q{}^{\curvearrowright}\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{n-1}\rangle\Vdash_{\mathbb{Q}_{\eta_{\omega}}}\dot{\alpha}=g(\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{i-1}\rangle)

for all
\langle\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{n-1}\rangle\in T^{q}
with orders
\langle\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}\rangle
.

A moment’s reflection should convince our readers that the above yields

q\Vdash_{\mathbb{Q}_{\eta_{\omega}}}``\exists x\in(\dot{b}_{\eta_{\omega}}\cap{\dot{\alpha})}^{i}\,\check{g}(x)=\dot{\alpha}".

Since this holds in
\mathcal{K}^{M}[K]
, there is
p^{*}\leq p\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\eta_{\omega}
in
K
forcing it. Thus,
p^{*}{}^{\smallfrown}\dot{q}
and a name
\dot{g}
such that
\dot{g}_{K}=g
satisfy the properties of Claim
8.2.2
.
∎

Work now in
M
(i.e., in
\mathcal{K}^{M}[H]
). To conclude the proof we prove:

Claim 8.2.3
.

\langle\eta_{n}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak n<\omega\rangle
is contained in
b_{\eta_{\omega}}
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
n<\omega
is such that
\eta_{n}\notin b_{\eta_{\omega}}
. Invoke the previous claim to find a function
g_{n}\colon\eta_{\omega}^{i_{n}}\rightarrow\eta_{\omega}
in
\mathcal{K}^{M}[H\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\eta_{\omega}]
and
x_{n}\in(b_{\eta_{\omega}}\cap\eta_{n})^{i_{n}}
such that
g_{n}(x_{n})=\eta_{n}
. Therefore,

\eta_{n}\in g_{n}[\eta_{n}^{i_{n}}].

Let
C_{n}:=\{\alpha<\eta_{\omega}\mathrel{|}\allowbreak\forall x\in[\alpha]^{i_{n}}\,g_{n}(x)<\alpha\}
. Clearly,
C_{n}
is a club on
\eta_{\omega}
in the model
\mathcal{K}^{M}[H\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\eta_{\omega}]
. Let
C:=\bigcap_{n<\omega}C_{n}
. By the properties of the iteration
\iota(\mathbb{P})_{\eta_{\omega}}
, there is a club
D\subseteq\eta_{\omega}
in
\mathcal{K}^{M}
such that
D\subseteq C
. Then, there is
E\in\mathrm{Cub}^{\mathcal{K}}_{\kappa}
such that
\iota(E)\subseteq^{*}D\subseteq C
: To see this, let
h\colon\eta_{\omega}\to\eta_{\omega}
be a function such that its closure points are contained in
D
. As the iteration
\iota=j\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\mathcal{K}
is normal,
h=\iota(g)(\zeta_{0},\dots,\zeta_{n-1})
for
\zeta_{0},...,\zeta_{n-1}<\eta_{\omega}
. Let
E
be the closure points of
g
. It follows that
\iota(E)\setminus\zeta_{n-1}\subseteq D\subseteq C
.

Since each
\eta_{n}
is a strong generator,
\eta_{n}\in\iota(E)\subseteq C_{n}
. But notice that this is a contradiction with the conclusion of the previous paragraph, where we showed that
\eta_{n}=g_{n}(x_{n})
for some
x_{n}\in[\eta_{n}]^{i_{n}}.

∎

The above completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Open problems

We close the paper with a couple of open problems. The most accessible question, given our current techniques and understanding, seems to be:

Question 1
.

Does
\kappa^{+}
-
\gp

\kappa^{+}
-
\gp

We conjecture that the answer is negative.

\kappa
-compact cardinals have the

-
\gp

Question 2
.

What is the consistency strength of the following, modulo
\mathrm{ZFC}

(1)

“There is a measurable cardinal
\kappa
with the
\kappa^{+}
-
\gp
”.

(2)

“There is a measurable cardinal
\kappa
with the
\kappa^{+}
-
\gp

Question 3
.

What is the consistency strength of the theory
\mathrm{ZFC}+
“There exists a measurable cardinal
\kappa

\lambda
-
\gp

\lambda

In Theorem

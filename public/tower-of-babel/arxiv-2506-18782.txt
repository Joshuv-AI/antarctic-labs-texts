Abstract

Introduction

In 1979, Lovász
[
7
]
introduced the concept of the Lovász theta function which has since seen multiple applications in problems related to independence number, clique number or chromatic number of graphs. Later, Grötschel, Lovász and Schrijver
[
8
]
introduced the concept of the theta body of a graph, which also proved to be a remarkably useful tool in investigating the independence number and chromatic number of graphs.

Recently, a recursive theta body for hypergraphs was introduced in
[
14
]
. They use this concept to find a bound for the maximum size of a subset of the hypercube where the Hamming distance is at least
d
for any two vertices in the subset. The Hamming distance of two
0
–
1
sequences of the same length is the number of positions in which they differ. They also introduce a hypergraph analogue of this problem. Let the vertices of a hypergraph be the vertices of the
n
-dimensional hypercube, and the edges are subsets of three vertices such that the Hamming distance between any two of them is exactly
r
. They ask about the size of the maximum independent set in this hypergraph. Note that
r
must be even and further
r\leq\frac{2n}{3}
for the problem to be non-trivial, so we will assume these constraints on
r
. In their paper, they give an upper bound for such a subset in terms of the recursive theta body of the hypergraph. They then give some numerical evidence for the progress of the upper bound as
n
and
r
increase. In this paper, we will show both lower and upper bounds for the same question. For simplicity, we introduce the notion of a
r
-distance graph of the hypercube as defined below, and we restate the problem as finding a triangle-free subset of the
r
-distance graph of the hypercube.

Definition 1
.

We define the
r
-distance graph of the hypercube as the graph on the vertices of the
n
-dimensional hypercube such that any two vertices are connected by an edge if their Hamming distance is exactly
r
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. We conclude Section 1 with a history of related problems. Section 2 contains two different lower bounds, one constructive and the other probabilistic. Section 3 is devoted to an upper bound. Section 4 contains comments and open questions.

In Coding theory, data is expressed in terms of codewords over an alphabet
\Sigma
. When this alphabet is
\{0,1\}
, we get binary codewords, which are essentially
0
–
1
sequences, and hence a code is a function to
\Sigma^{*}
. An important concept in Coding theory deals with the minimum distance of a code defined as the smallest Hamming distance between any two codewords. The Hamming distance of two sequences of the same length is defined as the number of bits in which they differ. The usefulness of minimum distance comes from its application to error detecting or error correcting codes where a code
C
is said to be
k
-error detecting if and only if the minimum Hamming distance between any two of its codewords is at least
k+1
. Consider the
n
-dimensional hypercube comprising of all
0
–
1
sequences of length
n
. A natural question that arises then is to find the largest subset of vertices such that the Hamming distance between any two vertices is bounded by a constant. This problem was posed by Erdős and was solved by Kleitman
[
12
]
. We refer the readers to
[
13
]
for a new proof of Kleitman’s theorem.

The above problems investigated bounded Hamming distance. In contrast, the case of fixed Hamming distance has also been researched in the past. The
r
-distance graph as defined above is called the Frankl-Rödl graph when
r=(1-\gamma)n
, and was introduced in
[
4
]
, where an upper bound on the independence number of such graphs was determined. We refer the reader to
[
9
]
where its chromatic number and independence number are discussed.

We note that if we had a further restriction that we only consider the vertices which have a fixed number of ones, then requiring a certain Hamming distance is equivalent to fixing the number of positions where both vertices have ones. For example, if we restrict our attention to vertices with
k
ones and require a Hamming distance of
2
for an edge, then this is equivalent to requiring
k-1
common positions for ones in these vertices. For
r=2
, this graph is called the Johnson graph, while for general
r
, it is called the generalized Johnson graph. Generalized Johnson graphs were first introduced in
[
2
]
by Chen and Lih. Since the positions of
1
’s can be used to determine a subset of the set
[n]
, this can also be rephrased as a problem determining the size of a collection of
k
-subsets where the size of the intersections is restricted to certain values. Problems regarding sizes of collections of subsets with a prescribed intersection size have been quite extensively researched, we refer the reader to
[
4
]
for an introduction to this.

2
Lower bounds

We first give a constructive lower bound. We would like to remark here that the set we construct is an independent set in the
r
-distance graph, and so it is necessarily triangle-free. Then we improve this with a probabilistic lower bound.

Claim 2
.

Given an integer
r
such that
r
is even, for every
n=mp
, where
m>r
and
p
is a prime such that
p\nmid r
, there is an independent vertex set of size
2^{\frac{n}{p}+p-1}
in the
r
-distance graph of the hypercube.

Proof.

We call two
p
-long
0
–
1
sequences
a,b
to be antipodal if
a+b
is a
p
-long sequence of all
1
’s. Given two antipodal sequences
a
and
b
, we consider vertices of the hypercube that can be obtained by replacing some
m
-long sequence of
a,b
with the corresponding strings for
a
and
b
. As an example, consider
p=2
and
n=6
, so that
m=3
. Let
a,b
be the pair
01
and
10
. Then a sequence
aab
will give us a vertex
010110
of the hypercube. We call the collection of all such vertices that can be obtained from the antipodal pair
(a,b)
the set
S_{ab}
. For the example given earlier,
S_{ab}=\{010101,010110,011001,011010,100101,100110,101001,101010\}
.
Then our construction of the independent set is simply the union of
S_{ab}
over all possible antipodal pairs
(a,b)
. Since there are
2^{p-1}
distinct antipodal pairs, we see that the number of vertices obtained in the union is indeed
2^{\frac{n}{p}+p-1}
.

To see that this set is indeed an independent set of vertices, we notice that if two vertices use the same
(a,b)
pair, then, since the Hamming distance of
a
and
b
is
p
, the Hamming distance of these two vertices would be a multiple of
p
, but we assumed that
p\nmid r
. If two vertices use a different antipodal pair, say
(a,b)
and
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
, then since the Hamming distance between
a
and either
a^{\prime}
or
b^{\prime}
is at least
1
, so the Hamming distance between these vertices is at least
m
, and we assumed that
m>r
. Hence, these vertices are independent.
∎

We notice that for any
r>2
, there is a prime
p\nmid r
such that
p<\log r
, so the bound is at least
2^{\frac{n}{\log r}}
, and if
3\nmid r
, then we even have an independent set of size
4\cdot 2^{\frac{n}{3}}
given
n>3r
.

We now show that with a basic application of the method of alteration, we can get a better lower bound as shown below. And it also gives us something meaningful when
n=rc
, for some constant
c>3.21
, while the above bound was applicable only when
n>r\log r
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
r\leq\frac{2n}{3}
and even. There is a triangle-free subset of size at least
\frac{c2^{n}}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{r}\binom{r}{\frac{r}{2}}\binom{n-r}{\frac{r}{2}}}}
in the
r
-distance graph of the hypercube. Here
c=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}
.

Proof.

Consider the
3
-uniform hypergraph on the vertices of the hypercube such that the edges are triangles of the
r
-distance graph. We want to find a large independent set in this hypergraph. We will construct a subset of the vertices by picking each vertex with probability
p
. Let
X
be the random variable that denotes the number of vertices selected. In addition, let
Y
be the random variable denoting the number of triangles selected in the process. Then removing
Y
vertices will result in a subset that is independent. The expected value
E(X-Y)
is
2^{n}p-p^{3}\cdot(\textrm{number of triangles})
. Here, we can find the exact number of triangles. Every vertex has a Hamming distance of
r
with
\binom{n}{r}
other vertices. Given any two vertices
u,v
that have a Hamming distance of
r
, a third vertex can be picked in
\binom{r}{\frac{r}{2}}\binom{n-r}{\frac{r}{2}}
ways. Every triangle in this procedure is counted
6
times, so the number of triangles is
\frac{1}{6}2^{n}\binom{n}{r}\binom{r}{\frac{r}{2}}\binom{n-r}{\frac{r}{2}}
. After substituting this in the above expression, we optimize over
p
to get
p=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\binom{n}{r}\binom{r}{\frac{r}{2}}\binom{n-r}{\frac{r}{2}}}}
and substituting it we get that there is a triangle-free subset with at least
\frac{2}{3}2^{n}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\binom{n}{r}\binom{r}{\frac{r}{2}}\binom{n-r}{\frac{r}{2}}}}
vertices.
∎

Asymptotically, the lower bound obtained in Theorem
3
is at least
\frac{\sqrt{2}\frac{2}{3}2^{n}}{e^{r}2^{\frac{r}{2}}(\frac{n}{r})^{\frac{3r}{4}}}
. If
r
is a constant, then this is
\Omega(\frac{2^{n}}{n^{\frac{3r}{4}}})
, while if
r=\frac{n}{c}
, then this gives us a lower bound
\Omega(a^{n})
, where
a
is the constant
\frac{2}{\sqrt[c]{e\sqrt{2}c^{\frac{3}{4}}}}
. This bound is therefore only meaningful when
c\geq 3.21
(approximately). We can see that for
n=cr\log r
,
c>1
, this is asymptotically
2^{cr\log r-c^{\prime}r\log\log r}
for some constant
c^{\prime}
, while the previous bound was asymptotically
2^{ar+b\log r}
, for some constants
a,b
. However, the bound in Claim
2
depends heavily on the choice of the prime. If
r
is such that
p\nmid r
, then for all values of
n>3r
, we have an independent (and so a triangle-free) set of size at least
4\cdot 2^{\frac{n}{3}}
.

Lastly, we will add here that when
r=\frac{n}{c}
, where
1.5\leq c<3.21
, both the above methods do not give a meaningful bound. For this case, we note briefly here that one can always consider all
\frac{r}{2}
-subsets such that either the first or the second bit is
1
. This would ensure that there are no three vertices with Hamming distance exactly
r
because in any three vertices, there must be two that have a common
1
in the first or the second place. So,
\binom{n}{\frac{r}{2}}-\binom{n-2}{\frac{r}{2}}
is a lower bound in this case. This bound is
\Omega(a^{n})
where
a=\sqrt[2c]{2c}
. This completes our discussion of the lower bounds.

3
Upper bounds

For the upper bound, we show that a triangle-free subset in the
r
-distance graph with Hamming distance
r
has at most
O(\frac{r2^{n}}{n+1})
vertices when
2r\leq n
. We will first show this when
r=2
, since the proof encapsulates the essence, and then we show how this can be extended to all values of
r
.

Theorem 4
.

Any triangle-free subset of the
2
-distance graph of the hypercube contains at most
\frac{4\cdot 2^{n}}{n}
vertices.

Proof.

Let the
k^{th}
level of the hypercube denote all the vertices of the hypercube with exactly
k
ones in their
0
–
1
sequence. Our main idea is to bound the number of vertices of a triangle-free subset in every level, and thereby obtain a bound for the total by summing these bounds. Consider any vertex of the
k^{th}
level. By replacing a single
1
with a
0
, we will get a vertex at the level
(k-1)^{th}
. We call this a shadow of the vertex. We notice that any vertex at the
k^{th}
level has
k
shadows. We further note that any three vertices at the
k^{th}
level from a triangle-free subset of the hypercube cannot have the same shadow. Because if three vertices at the
k^{th}
level have the same shadow, then they all have Hamming distance
2
and would form a triangle. So, any
0
–
1
sequence at the
(k-1)^{th}
level can be a shadow of at most two vertices in our triangle-free subset. Let
S_{k}
denote the number of vertices from the triangle-free subset at the
k^{th}
level. Then
kS_{k}\leq 2\binom{n}{k-1}
, or
S_{k}\leq\frac{2}{k}\binom{n}{k-1}
.

Summing over
k
, we find that the vertices we picked are less than
1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2}{k}\binom{n}{k-1}
which is
1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2}{n+1}\binom{n+1}{k}
. This is asymptotically
\frac{4\cdot 2^{n}}{n}
.
∎

In the above summation, we could have used shadows when
k\leq\frac{n}{2}
and then covers when
k>\frac{n}{2}
. Covers are the opposite of shadows, we obtain covers by changing a
0
to a
1
in the sequence of a vertex. The analysis of covers when
k>\frac{n}{2}
is completely symmetric to the analysis of the shadows when
k\leq\frac{n}{2}
and we skip this analysis, but we note that if we used covers for level
k>\frac{n}{2}
, then our summation would look like
2(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{2}{k}\binom{n}{k-1})
which again gives the same result as above. Although it seems futile to do the summation this way for
r=2
, we will prefer this for the later theorem. There, this will be the only way to take the summation for values of
r
that are very close to
\frac{n}{2}
, because the level bound does not apply to the shadows of levels bigger than
\frac{n}{2}
when
r
is close to
\frac{n}{2}
.

Before we proceed to the bound for general
r
, we need some preparation. In particular, we will use a bound from the Erdős Matching Conjecture, so we will state it here. The hypercube vertices can also be considered as subsets of the base set
[n]
, where the vertices denote which elements are present in a certain subset. Erdős
[
3
]
posed what is called the Erdős Matching Conjecture, which, given
n,k
and
s
, requires one to determine the largest collection of
k
-subsets of
[n]
, such that there are no
s
pairwise disjoint subsets (also called a matching of size
s
). Frankl proved the following theorem in 1987:

Theorem 5

(Frankl
[
5
]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subset\binom{X}{k}
, where
|X|\geq ks
and
\mathcal{F}
contains no
s
pairwise disjoint subsets. Then,

|\mathcal{F}|\leq(s-1)\binom{n-1}{k-1}

We note that Erdős Matching conjecture has been a topic of active and extensive research, and we refer the interested reader to
[
5
,
6
,
10
]
for further information and some recent results on it.

We now present the general upper bound. Note, however, that we do not know of a non-trivial upper bound when
\frac{n}{2}<r<\frac{2n}{3}
.

Theorem 6
.

When
2r\leq n
and
r
is even, any triangle-free subset of the
r
-distance graph on the hypercube contains
O(\frac{r2^{n}}{n+1})
vertices.

Proof.

We will use the terminology introduced in the previous proof. Namely, we will again consider levels of the hypercube and look at shadows of the vertices in the triangle-free subset. But now, for the
k^{th}
level, we will look at the shadows at the
k-\frac{r}{2}
level. Here, we observe that if we fix a shadow, then the remaining
1
’s in the vertices of the triangle-free subset from the
k^{th}
level form
\frac{r}{2}
-subsets of
[n-(k-\frac{r}{2})]
such that there are no three disjoint subsets. For a fixed shadow, if there were three pairwise disjoint subsets, they would all have Hamming distance
r
and would induce a triangle. Then, using the Theorem of Frankl, for a fixed shadow, the number of such subsets is bounded by
2\binom{n-(k-\frac{r}{2})-1}{\frac{r}{2}-1}
. If we again let
S_{k}
denote the number of vertices of the triangle-free subset at the
k^{th}
level, we obtain that
\binom{k}{\frac{r}{2}}S_{k}\leq 2\binom{n}{k-\frac{r}{2}}\binom{n-(k-\frac{r}{2})-1}{\frac{r}{2}-1}
. But we must be careful here because we can only apply the bound here when
n-(k-\frac{r}{2})\geq 3\frac{r}{2}
. We can check that when
2r\leq n
, this is true for all values of
k\leq\frac{n}{2}
. When
k>\frac{n}{2}
, we can repeat the same argument using covers instead of shadows to get the same bound as above just with
n-k
instead of
k
. So, when we take the summation, we will only sum until
\frac{n}{2}
and then double the sum.

Simplifying the inequality above, we get
S_{k}\leq\binom{n}{k}\frac{r}{(n-(k-\frac{r}{2}))}
. Taking the summation for relevant values of
k
and using the trivial bound for
k\leq\frac{r}{2}
, we get that,

\displaystyle S=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{k}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{r}{2}}\binom{n}{i}+\sum_{k=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\binom{n}{k}\frac{r}{(n-(k-\frac{r}{2}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{r}{2}}\binom{n}{i}+\sum_{k=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\binom{n}{k}\frac{r}{(n-k+1))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{r}{2}}\binom{n}{i}+\sum_{k=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\binom{n+1}{k}\frac{r}{(n+1)}\right)

which upon simplifying gives
O(\frac{r2^{n}}{n+1})
.
∎

4
Conclusion

We note here that it would be interesting to find better constructions for the lower bound. In particular, our construction is of an independent set of vertices, and it would be interesting to find better constructive lower bounds where the subset is not necessarily independent. It would also be interesting to find an upper bound for the case
\frac{n}{2}<r\leq\frac{2n}{3}
. And another direction for research could be to find better upper bounds for the case where
r=n/c
for some constant
c\geq 2
. In particular, we refer the reader to the conjectures in
[
14
]
for these cases.

Vectorial Double Phase obstacle problems
Filomena De Filippis, Antonella Nastasi, Cintia Pacchiano Camacho

Abstract
. We investigate partial regularity for vector valued local minimizers of double phase functionals, under vectorial obstacle type constraints satisfying appropriate topological properties.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35J60, 49N60, 35J50

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study gradient partial regularity for a constrained vector valued function
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, which locally minimize a double phase energy and
whose values lie on a target
\mathsf{M}
that satisfies one of the following conditions:

a.

\mathsf{M}
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with boundary
\partial\mathsf{M}\in C^{3}
;

b.

\mathsf{M}
is a bounded subregion, with boundary
\partial\mathsf{M}\in C^{3}
, of an
m
-dimensional submanifold
Y
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\overline{\mathsf{M}}\subset\text{Int}(Y)
;

c.

\mathsf{M}
is a compact submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
without boundary.

Specifically, in case
a
we consider a constrained local minimizer of the general double phase functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(u,\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}(g_{\alpha\beta}(x,u)B^{ij}(x,u)D_{\alpha}u^{i}D_{\beta}u^{j}\bigr)^{\frac{p}{2}}+a(x)\bigl(g_{\alpha\beta}(x,u)B^{ij}(x,u)D_{\alpha}u^{i}D_{\beta}u^{j}\bigr)^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{d}x

(1.1)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\textnormal{{g}}(x,u,Du)\mathrm{d}x,

where
\Omega
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. The functions
g
and
B
satisfy the following properties: for
\alpha,\beta=1,\dots,n
,
i,j=1,\dots,N
, functions
g_{\alpha\beta}:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
,
B^{ij}:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
are such that

(1.2)

\begin{cases}&g_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\beta\alpha},\ B^{ij}=B^{ji}\\
&g,B\in C^{1}\\
&\ell|\eta|^{2}\leq g_{\alpha\beta}(x,u)\eta_{\alpha}\eta_{\beta},\ \ell|\xi|^{2}\leq B^{ij}(x,u)\xi^{i}\xi^{j},\end{cases}

for some positive constant
\ell
, for all
x\in\overline{\Omega},\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n},u,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
While, in case
b
or
c
, we consider a constrained local minimizer of the classic double phase functional

(1.3)

\mathcal{H}(u,\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}|Du|^{p}+a(x)|Du|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n}:=\int_{\Omega}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{n},

where
\Omega
is a bounded open subset of a
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
n\geq 2
, and
\mathscr{H}^{n}
is the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\Omega
. The coefficient
a(\cdot)
and the exponents
p,q
satisfy: for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
, it holds

(1.4)

0\leq a(\cdot)\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega),\quad 1<p<q<p+\alpha,\qquad q<N-1.

We will always assume that

(1.5)

\mathsf{M}\text{ is }[q]\text{-connected},

where
[q]
is the integer part of
q
. Into category
c
falls, for example, the
(N-1)
-dimensional sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, i.e.
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, which is a compact manifold and
(N-2)
-connected, hence it is admissible whenever
q<N-1
. We point out also that assumption
(\ref{M})
plays a crucial role in our regularity analysis, particularly in ensuring the applicability of the finite energy extension Lemma
2.4
. The following remark clarifies the range of admissible values for the exponent
q
that are compatible with this topological requirement.

Remark 1.1
.

The minimal connectivity assumption on the target
\mathsf{M}
required for the validity of the partial regularity result is that
\mathsf{M}
is
([q]-1)
-connected. This condition is necessary and sufficient to ensure the validity of estimate (
2.11
) in Lemma
2.4
. However, in order to apply Lemma
2.3
under the assumption of
([q]-1)
-connectedness, one must impose
1\leq[q]-1\leq N-2
, which yields the restriction
2\leq q<N
. Therefore, one may equivalently assume either
1<q<N-1
or
2\leq q<N
, both of which are compatible with our framework.

The double phase functional was first introduced by Zhikov
[
71
,
70
]
in the context of homogenization and in connection with Lavrentiev phenomenon. This type of energy functional plays a fundamental role in modeling of strongly anisotropic materials, with applications in elasticity and the theory of composites.
The transition between
p
- and
q
-growth regions is governed by the vanishing of the coefficient
a(\cdot)
. Over the last decade, a rich regularity theory for double phase and more general nonuniformly elliptic functionals has been established. In particular, the contributions of Baroni, Colombo, and Mingione
[
5
,
10
,
11
,
9
]
developed a comprehensive regularity theory, which in turn generated a huge literature, see
[
1
,
13
,
19
,
43
,
49
,
50
,
51
,
61
,
64
,
65
,
66
]
and references therein. For problems with linear and nearly linear growth see
[
6
,
7
,
35
,
39
,
40
,
41
,
42
]
. The
(p,q)
-growth condition
|z|^{p}\lesssim\textnormal{{H}}(\cdot,z)\lesssim 1+|z|^{q}
is directly tied to the nonuniform ellipticity of the associated Euler Lagrange system. Formally, a critical point
u
of
\mathcal{H}
satisfies

(1.6)

-\mathrm{div}\,A(x,Du)=0,

where the vector field

A(x,z)=|z|^{p-2}z+(q/p)a(x)|z|^{q-2}z,

and one sees that the (nonlocal) ellipticity ratio satisfies

(1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\sup_{x\in\textnormal{{B}}}\text{highest eigenvalue of }\partial_{z}A(x,Du)}{\inf_{x\in\textnormal{{B}}}\text{lowest eigenvalue of }\partial_{z}A(x,Du)}\,\approx\,1+\lVert a\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\textnormal{{B}})}|Du|^{q-p}

on any ball ball
\textnormal{{B}}\subset\Omega
touching the transition set
\{a(\cdot)=0\}
and therefore become unbounded provided
a
is not identically zero. The condition (
1.4
) precisely ensures that
a(\cdot)
vanishes fast enough to prevent uncontrollable degeneracy, guaranteeing maximal gradient regularity for local minimizers. The sharpness of this hypothesis was first observed by Esposito, Leonetti and Mingione
[
26
]
; see also
[
2
,
3
,
26
,
29
]
. Historically, the notion of coupling growth exponents
p
and
q
to regulate the rate of blow up of the ellipticity ratio was pioneered by Marcellini
[
57
,
60
,
58
,
59
]
in his foundational investigations of
(p,q)
-nonuniformly elliptic integrals. Motivated by delicate phenomena in nonlinear elasticity, such as cavitation, the early investigations showed that choosing
q
sufficiently close to
p
yields a class of variational energies with controlled blow up. This perspective has also evolved into the framework of strongly nonuniformly elliptic functionals. In this setting, also the pointwise ellipticity ratio blows up (when
|z|\to+\infty
) in a nonbalanced fashion, namely

\displaystyle\frac{\text{highest eigenvalue of }\partial_{z}A(x,Du)}{\text{lowest eigenvalue of }\partial_{z}A(x,Du)}\,\approx\,1+|z|^{\delta},\quad\delta>0.

Although the underlying structure is strongly nonuniformly elliptic, recent advances have led to the development of a comprehensive Schauder theory
[
15
,
17
]
, encompassing also problems at nearly linear growth, see
[
16
,
20
,
18
]
.

In our present study, we extend these insights to the vectorial obstacle setting. To precisely state our results, for
\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
that is

\overline{\mathsf{M}}
in case

,
b
and
\mathsf{M}
in case

, we introduce the admissible class

and, for convenience, we collect the main parameters of the problem in the quantity

\textnormal{{data}}\equiv\textnormal{{data}}(n,N,\ell,L,p,q,\alpha,[a]_{0,\alpha},\tilde{\mathsf{M}}),

where
L>\ell
bounds
\|g_{\alpha\beta},B^{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\times\tilde{\mathsf{M}})}
when we consider functional (
1

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

for some
\beta\equiv\beta(\textnormal{{data}})\in(0,1)

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of a
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and let

In the case where

Now, we want to point out that for obstacle problems where the constraint set
\mathsf{M}
is convex, the minimization property can be reformulated as a variational inequality. In contrast, for general
\mathsf{M}
such linearization is nontrivial. Early work in
[

]
first tackled this issue, with subsequent refinements in
[
22
,
24
]
developing sharper linearization techniques, here we adopt the version proposed in
[
33
]
. Our approach shows that Theorem
1.2
stems directly from Theorem
1.1
, whose proof relies on two main ingredients. The first consists in adapting the classical framework developed by Fuchs
[
33
]
, which is based on a direct comparison between the constrained minimizer
u
of the functional
\mathcal{G}
in (
1
) and the unconstrained minimizer (having
u
as boundary datum) of
\mathcal{G}
with frozen coefficients

and

. The second component of the approach builds on the harmonic approximation method used in
[

. This allows us to involve in the comparison scheme also an harmonic map enjoying better regularity properties. In contrast to Fuchs’s work, a key role in this analysis is played by the topological assumption that
\mathsf{M}
is

-connected. This property ensures the possibility of constructing finite energy extensions of Sobolev maps into
\overline{\mathsf{M}}
, which is formalized in Lemma
2.4
, this in turn yields an appropriate Caccioppoli’s inequality. Notably, unlike in Fuchs’s setting, our approach also covers the case
1<p<2
.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the following partial regularity criterion: if there exists an
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that on a ball
\textnormal{{B}}_{r}(x)\Subset\Omega
it holds

(1.9)

\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}(x)}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,{\rm d}x<\textnormal{{H}}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}(x)}^{-}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\right)

then

is Hölder continuous
near

, where
\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}
is the frozen integrand defined in (
2.5
). The
\varepsilon
-regularity condition (

) provides an intrinsic quantified version of the amount of energy needed for regularity and it is related to the structure of the so-called singular set
\tilde{\Sigma}:=\Omega\setminus\tilde{\Omega}
, that takes the form

\tilde{\Sigma}:=\left\{\tilde{x}\in\Omega:\limsup_{\varrho\to 0}\left[\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}(\tilde{x})}\left(\frac{1}{\varrho}\right)\right]^{-1}\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}(\tilde{x})}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,{\rm d}x>0\right\},

see Remark
4.1
. To conclude, we underline that as an intermediate outcome (Step 1 of Theorem
1.1
) we also get that

\text{for all }\gamma\in(0,1)\text{ it holds }u\in C^{0,\gamma}(\tilde{\Omega},\overline{\mathsf{M}})\text{ and }|\Omega\setminus\tilde{\Omega}|=0.

As a consequence of the partial regularity of the gradient established in Theorem

of the singular set

Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

In this section, we introduce the notation used throughout the paper. Given
\tilde{x}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
and
r>0
, we define the open ball

\textnormal{{B}}_{r}\equiv\textnormal{{B}}_{r}(\tilde{x}):=\{x\in\mathds{R}^{n}:|x-\tilde{x}|<r\}.

Unless otherwise specified, all balls considered will be centered at the same point. The symbol
c
will denote a generic positive constant, not necessarily the same at each occurrence; its relevant dependencies will be explicitly indicated when needed. In the forthcoming estimates, any dependence of the constants on quantities related to the geometry of
\overline{\mathsf{M}}
(or
\mathsf{M}
in case
c
) such as the
L^{\infty}
-norm of maps with range in
\overline{\mathsf{M}}
will be simply denoted by writing
c\equiv c(\overline{\mathsf{M}})
. Note that the
L^{\infty}
-norm is finite due to the compactness of
\overline{\mathsf{M}}
. If
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a measurable subset with bounded positive measure
0<|U|<\infty
and

(w)_{U}\equiv\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{U}w(x)\,{\rm d}x:=\frac{1}{|U|}\int_{U}w(x)\,{\rm d}x.

As usual, with
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we denote

[w]_{0,\gamma,U}:=\sup_{x,y\in U,x\neq y}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}.

For the coefficient
a(\cdot)
we abbreviate with
[a]_{0,\alpha}\equiv[a]_{0,\alpha,\Omega}
.
We consider the auxiliary vector fields
V_{p},V_{q}:\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
defined by

(2.1)

V_{t}(z):=|z|^{\frac{t-2}{2}}z,\quad t\in\{p,q\},

whenever

. To this extent, we collect some useful notations and properties, which will be needed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. For every
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
, we have

(2.2)

|V_{t}(z_{1})-V_{t}(z_{2})|\approx(|{z_{1}|}+|z_{2}|)^{(t-2)/2}|z_{1}-z_{2}|,\quad t\in\{p,q\},

where the equivalence holds up to constants depending only on

, see
[
46
, Lemma 2.1]
. Two quantities that will play a crucial role throughout the paper are

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{2}(x,z_{1},z_{2}):=|V_{p}(z_{1})-V_{p}(z_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle+a(x)|V_{q}(z_{1})-V_{q}(z_{2})|^{2},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{2}(z_{1},z_{2},{\textnormal{{B}}}_{r}):=|V_{p}(z_{1})-V_{p}(z_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle+\inf_{x\in\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}a(x)|V_{q}(z_{1})-V_{q}(z_{2})|^{2},

where
{\textnormal{{B}}}_{r}\subset\Omega
is a ball. We report an important inequality (see
[
4
,
55
]
) related to these fields. Let
\tilde{\textnormal{{g}}}
be the function defined in (

). Then, for all
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
and

, it follows

(2.3)

\mathcal{V}^{2}(x,z_{1},z_{2})\leq c[\partial_{z}\tilde{\textnormal{{g}}}(x,z_{1})-\partial_{z}\tilde{\textnormal{{g}}}(x,z_{2})]\cdot(z_{1}-z_{2}),

with
c\equiv c(n,N,\ell,p,q)
.
Let us now give the definitions of manifold
j
-connected.

Definition 2.1

(Manifold

-connected)
.

Given an integer

, a manifold

is said to be

\pi_{0}(\tilde{\mathsf{M}})=\pi_{1}(\tilde{\mathsf{M}})=\dots=\pi_{j-1}(\tilde{\mathsf{M}})=\pi_{j}(\tilde{\mathsf{M}})=0

The last definition we need is the one of a local minimizer of functional
\mathcal{H}
as in (

) with value in a compact target space

. For functional
\mathcal{G}
, with coefficients

(Constrained local minimizer)

A function

, is a local minimizer of the functional

in (

and the minimality condition

\mathcal{H}(u,\,{\rm supp}(u-v))\leq\mathcal{H}(v,\,{\rm supp}(u-v))

Before concluding this section, for
\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
compact submanifold, we introduce the spaces:

2.2.
Properties of the frozen double phase integrand

We introduce the following Young function

(2.5)

\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}}(t):=t^{p}+\inf_{x\in\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}}a(x)t^{q},

where
\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}\Subset\Omega
is a ball. We summarize some key properties of the functions

\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}}(t)
. First, observe that
\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}}(\cdot)
is a Young function satisfying the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Since
t\mapsto\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}}(t)
is strictly increasing and strictly convex, its inverse
(\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}})^{-1}
is strictly increasing and strictly concave with
(\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}})^{-1}(0)=0
, making
(\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}})^{-1}
subadditive. Therefore, we observe that

(2.6)

(\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}})^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq(\lambda+1)(\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}})^{-1}(t),\quad\text{for all }\lambda\geq 0.

Furthermore, for any ball
\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}\equiv\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}(\tilde{x})\Subset\Omega
, by the Hölder continuity of
a(\cdot)
, the function

(2.7)

(\tilde{x},\varrho,t)\mapsto\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}(\tilde{x})}(t)\text{ is continuous on }\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty).

To conclude, we observe that for a fixed
\tilde{x}
, if
\varrho_{1}\leq\varrho_{2}
, it follows that

(2.8)

\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho_{2}}(\tilde{x})}(t)\leq\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho_{1}}(\tilde{x})}(t)

holds uniformly in
t\geq 0
.

2.3.
Useful results

In this section, we collect some auxiliary results used throughout the paper. We start with the following lemma that highlights good properties in terms of retractions for manifolds endowed with a relatively simple topology.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\tilde{\mathsf{M}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, be a compact
l
-connected submanifold, for some integer
1\leq l\leq N-2
, contained in an
N
-dimensional cube
Q
. Then there exists a closed
(N-l-2)
-dimensional Lipschitz polyhedron
X\subset Q\setminus\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
and a locally Lipschitz retraction
\Psi:Q\setminus X\to\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
such that

|D\Psi(x)|\leq\frac{c}{\mathrm{dist}(x,X)},\quad\text{for all }x\in Q\setminus X,

for some positive constant
c\equiv c(N,l,\tilde{\mathsf{M}})
.

Proof.

The result follows from
[
47
, Lemma 6.1]
, which provides the original construction. Another proof based on Lipschitz extension properties for maps between Riemannian manifolds can be found in
[
53
, Lemma 4.5]
.

∎

A key difficulty in constrained variational problems lies in constructing admissible comparison maps, since standard convex combinations of a minimizer with suitable cut-off functions fail to preserve the constraint. To overcome this, we apply Lemma
2.4
, which provides local energy control for a suitable projection
\tilde{w}
of a competitor
w
. This is the content of the next lemma.

Lemma 2.4

(Finite energy extension)
.

Let
\hat{\Omega}\subset\Omega
be an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Lipschitz boundary. Let
\tilde{\mathsf{M}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, be a compact
[q]
-connected submanifold, for
1<p<q<N-1
. Let
w\in(W^{1,\textnormal{{H}}}\cap L^{\infty})(\hat{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
w(\partial\hat{\Omega})\subset\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
, then there exists
\tilde{w}\in W^{1,\textnormal{{H}}}(\hat{\Omega},\tilde{\mathsf{M}})
satisfying

(2.9)

\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,D\tilde{w})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Dw)\,\mathrm{d}x,

and
\tilde{w}-w\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
c\equiv c(n,N,p,q,\tilde{\mathsf{M}})
.

Before proceeding with the proof, let us introduce some notation. For any subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define
\text{Unp}(A)
as the set of all points
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
for which there exists a unique nearest point to
x
in
A
. For any
t\in A
, let
\text{reach}(A,t)
denote the supremum of the set of all
r>0
such that the ball
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x-t|<r\}
is entirely contained in
\text{Unp}(A)
. We then define

\text{reach}(A):=\inf_{t\in{A}}\text{reach}(A,t).

We are now ready to prove Lemma
2.4
, following the approach in
[
53
, Lemma 4.7]
.

Proof of lemma
2.4
.

Let

V:=\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\text{dist}(t,\tilde{\mathsf{M}})<\sigma\}
, for some
0<\sigma<\text{reach}(\tilde{\mathsf{M}})
,
be a neighbourhood with the unique nearest point property.
Let
\Pi:\overline{V}\to\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
denote the map that associates to each
t\in\overline{V}
the unique
t_{0}\in\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
such that
\text{dist}(t,\tilde{\mathsf{M}})=|t-t_{0}|
. Then, by
[
53
, Theorem 2.4]
, function
\Pi
is Lipschitz continuous. We observe that
\overline{V}
and
\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
are homotopy equivalent spaces, since there exists a strong deformation retraction
h_{\lambda}(\xi)=(1-\lambda)\xi+\lambda\Pi(\xi)
, for
\xi\in\overline{V}
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. Therefore
\pi_{j}(\overline{V})=\pi_{j}(\tilde{M})=0
for all
j=1,\dots,[q]
, see
[
52
, Proposition 1.17]
. Now, since
\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
is compact and
w
is bounded, there exists an
N
-dimensional cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

\tilde{\mathsf{M}}\subset\overline{V}\subset Q\quad\text{and}\quad\text{dist}(w,\tilde{\mathsf{M}})\leq\frac{1}{2}\text{dist}(\tilde{\mathsf{M}},\partial Q)\text{ almost everywhere}.

By Lemma
2.3
, with
l=[q]
, there exists a locally Lipschitz retraction

\Psi:Q\setminus X\to\overline{V},

where
X\subset Q\setminus\overline{V}
is a Lipschitz polyhedron of
(N-[q]-2)
-dimension, and is constructed to remain strictly away from
\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
. Define now the map
P:=\Pi\circ\Psi:Q\setminus X\to\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
then, by Lemma
2.3
, it holds

(2.10)

|\nabla P(t)|\leq\frac{c}{\text{dist}(t,X)},

with
c\equiv c(N,\tilde{\mathsf{M}})
. By a change of variables, the definition of the dual skeleton, the fact that
\dim(X)\leq N-[q]-2
and
p,q<[q]+1
, we obtain

(2.11)

\int_{Q}\frac{\,{\rm d}t}{\text{dist}(t,X)^{\textnormal{{s}}}}<c,\ \text{for }\textnormal{{s}}={p,q},

for some finite positive constant
c=c(N,\tilde{\mathsf{M}},q)
. Now take
0<\varrho<\min\left\{\frac{\sigma}{2},\frac{\text{dist}(\tilde{\mathsf{M}},\partial Q)}{2}\right\}
, and for each
b\in\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}^{N}
(the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
of radius
\varrho
and centered at
0
), define the translated sets

Q_{b}:=\{t+b:t\in Q\},\quad X_{b}:=\{t+b:t\in X\},

as well as the translated retraction

P_{b}:Q_{b}\setminus X_{b}\to\tilde{\mathsf{M}},\quad P_{b}(t):=P(t-b).

Then, using the chain rule, Fubini’s theorem, and estimates (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}^{N}}\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,D(P_{b}(w)))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle=\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}^{N}}\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left[|D(P_{b}(w))|^{p}+a(x)|D(P_{b}(w))|^{q}\right]\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left[|Dw|^{p}\left(\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}^{N}}|\nabla P(w-b)|^{p}\mathrm{d}b\right)+a(x)|Dw|^{q}\left(\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}^{N}}|\nabla P(w-b)|^{q}\mathrm{d}b\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\hat{\Omega}}|Dw|^{p}\left(\int_{Q}|\nabla P(t)|^{p}\mathrm{d}t\right)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\hat{\Omega}}a(x)|Dw|^{q}\left(\int_{Q}|\nabla P(t)|^{q}\mathrm{d}t\right)\mathrm{d}x

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\left(|Dw|^{p}+a(x)|Dw|^{q}\right)\mathrm{d}x=c\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Dw)\,\mathrm{d}x,

for some constant
c=c(N,\tilde{\mathsf{M}},q)
. From (
2.3
) and Markov’s inequality, there exists
\tilde{b}\in\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}^{N}
and a constant
c=c(n,N,\tilde{\mathsf{M}},q)
such that

(2.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,D(P_{\tilde{b}}(w)))\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Dw)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
w(\partial\hat{\Omega})\subset\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
, the map
\tilde{w}:=(P_{\tilde{b}}|_{\tilde{\mathsf{M}}})^{-1}\circ P_{\tilde{b}}\circ w
is well-defined. Moreover, since the inverse map
(P_{\tilde{b}}|_{\tilde{\mathsf{M}}})^{-1}
is Lipschitz on
\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
, inequality (
2.13
) implies

\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,D\tilde{w})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Dw)\,\mathrm{d}x,

with
c=c(n,N,\tilde{\mathsf{M}},p,q)
, which shows that (
2.9
) holds. Finally, since
w(\partial\hat{\Omega})\subset\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
, it follows by construction that
\tilde{w}|_{\partial\hat{\Omega}}=w|_{\partial\hat{\Omega}}
, completing the proof.

∎

In the following lemma, we present a Caccioppoli-type inequality for a constrained local minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{G}
in (
1
), with
\mathsf{M}
as in
a
. The proof makes use of extension Lemma
2.4
. Note that the results still hold in the unconstrained case, i.e.
u\in(W^{1,\textnormal{{H}}}\cap L^{\infty})(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In this case, the constants appearing in the lemma will also depend on
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
.

Lemma 2.5

(Caccioppoli’s inequality)
.

Let
\Omega
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
u\in W^{1,\textnormal{{H}}}(\Omega,\overline{\mathsf{M}})
be a constrained local minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{G}
in (
1
) under the assumptions
1<p<q\leq p+\alpha
,
q<N-1
, and (
1.2
), with
\mathsf{M}
as in
a
and satisfying (
1.5
). Then, there exists a constant
c\equiv c(n,N,\ell,L,p,q,\overline{\mathsf{M}})>0
such that for any pair of concentric balls
\textnormal{{B}}_{r}\subset\textnormal{{B}}_{R}\Subset\Omega
, it holds that

(2.14)

\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}\textnormal{{H}}\left(x,\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R-r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover, if
R\leq 1
, then

(2.15)

\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R/2}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,dx\leq c\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}\left(\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right)\,\,{\rm d}x,

for
c\equiv c(n,N,\ell,L,p,q,[a]_{0,\alpha},\overline{\mathsf{M}})>0
. Moreover, if

(2.16)

\inf_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}a(x)\leq 4[a]_{0,\alpha}R^{\alpha}

holds and
R\leq 1
, then

(2.17)

\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R/2}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}\left|\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x,

with
c\equiv c(n,N,\ell,L,p,q,[a]_{0,\alpha},\overline{\mathsf{M}})
.

Proof.

Let
r\leq t<s\leq R
. We take a cut-off function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\textnormal{{B}}_{s})
such that
\chi_{\textnormal{{B}}_{t}}\leq\eta\leq\chi_{\textnormal{{B}}_{s}}
and
|D\eta|\leq 4(s-t)^{-1}
. Define

w:=u-\eta(u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}).

Since
\eta
is smooth and
u\in W^{1,\textnormal{{H}}}(\textnormal{{B}}_{s},\overline{\mathsf{M}})
, it follows that
w\in W^{1,\textnormal{{H}}}(\textnormal{{B}}_{s},\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u-w\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\textnormal{{B}}_{s},\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We observe that the choice
\tilde{\mathsf{M}}=\overline{\mathsf{M}}
in Lemma
2.4
is admissible, since the boundary
\partial\mathsf{M}
is
C^{3}
-regular. Then, there exists a function
\tilde{w}\in W^{1,\textnormal{{H}}}(\textnormal{{B}}_{s},\overline{\mathsf{M}})
, which is an admissible competitor for
u
in problem (
1.3
), and satisfies (
2.9
) with
\hat{\Omega}=\textnormal{{B}}_{s}
. Using the minimality of
u
,
(\ref{ellipticity})_{3}
and the fact that

\|g_{\alpha\beta},B^{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\mathsf{M}})}\leq L
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{s}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{s}}\textnormal{{g}}(x,u,Du)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{s}}\textnormal{{g}}(x,\tilde{w},D\tilde{w})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{s}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,D\tilde{w})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{s}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,\mathrm{d}x

(2.18)

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{s}\setminus\textnormal{{B}}_{t}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{s}\setminus\textnormal{{B}}_{t}}\textnormal{{H}}\left(x,\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{s-t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right),

where
c\equiv c(N,\ell,L,p,q,\overline{\mathsf{M}})
.
The proof of (
2.14
) is then completed by filling the hole and applying an iteration argument, see
[
10
]
.

To establish (
2.15
), consider the estimate:

\displaystyle\textnormal{{H}}\left(x,\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right)

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right|^{p}+a(x)\left|\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}\left(\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right)+\sup_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}\left[a(x)-\inf_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}a(x)\right]\left|\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}\left(\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right)+2^{\alpha+q-p}[a]_{0,\alpha}R^{\alpha+p-q}\left|\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\textnormal{{H}}^{-}_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}\left(\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right),

with
c\equiv c(p,q,\alpha,\overline{\mathsf{M}})
. Therefore, (
2.15
) follows by applying (
2.14
) with
r=R/2
.

To prove (
2.17
), it suffices to use the estimate given by (
2.16
):

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}a(x)\left|\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right|^{q}\leq 8[a]_{0,\alpha}R^{\alpha+p-q}\left|\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right|^{p}\leq c\left|\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{R}}}{R}\right|^{p},

and then apply (
2.14
) once again.

∎

We proceed with an intrinsic Sobolev-Poincaré inequality, the proof follows the one in
[
10
, Section 4]
, with minor modifications.

Lemma 2.6

(Intrinsic Sobolev-Poincaré inequality)
.

Let
\Omega
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
u\in(W^{1,\textnormal{{H}}}\cap L^{\infty})(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
N\geq 1
, and let
\textnormal{{B}}_{r}\Subset\Omega
be a ball with radius
r\leq 1
, assume that
q\leq p+\alpha
. Then, there exists
d\equiv d(n,p,q)<1
and
c\equiv c(n,N,p,q,[a]_{0,\alpha},\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\textnormal{{B}}_{r})})\geq 1
such that:

(2.19)

\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}\textnormal{{H}}\left(x,\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}}{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}[\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)]^{d}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/d}.

The quantity
u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}
can be replaced by
u
if
\operatorname{tr}(u,\partial\textnormal{{B}}_{r})\equiv 0
, or if
u\equiv 0
on a subset
A\subset\textnormal{{B}}_{r}
with
|A|/|\textnormal{{B}}_{r}|>\gamma>0
, in which case
c
also depends on
\gamma
.

The following is an inner higher integrability lemma.

Lemma 2.7

(Inner higher integrability)
.

Let
\Omega
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
u\in W^{1,\textnormal{{H}}}(\Omega,\overline{\mathsf{M}})
be a constrained local minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{G}
in (
1
) under assumptions (
1.4
), (
1.2
), with
\mathsf{M}
as in
a
and satisfying (
1.5
). Then, there exists
\displaystyle\delta\equiv\delta(\textnormal{{data}})
, positive integrability exponent, such that for all balls
\textnormal{{B}}_{2r}\subset\Omega
, with
r\leq 1
, it holds

(2.20)

\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}[\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)]^{1+\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/(1+\delta)}\leq c\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\textnormal{{B}}_{2r}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,\mathrm{d}x,

with
c\equiv c(\textnormal{{data}})
.

Proof.

Let
\textnormal{{B}}_{2r}\subset\Omega
. By the Caccioppoli inequality, Lemma
2.5
, there exists a constant
c\equiv c(n,N,\ell,L,p,q,\overline{\mathsf{M}})>0
such that

\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{2r}}\textnormal{{H}}\left(x,\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{2r}}}{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

On the other hand, by the intrinsic Sobolev-Poincaré inequality, Lemma
2.6
, we obtain:

(2.21)

\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}\textnormal{{H}}\left(x,\frac{u-(u)_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}}{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}[\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)]^{d}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/d}

where
d\equiv d(n,p,q)<1
and
c=c(n,N,p,q,[a]_{0,\alpha},\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})
.

Inequality (
2.21
) holds for every ball
\textnormal{{B}}_{2r}\Subset\Omega
. Therefore, we obtained a reverse Hölder inequality that is uniform over all such balls.

By an application of Gehring’s Lemma there exists
\delta\equiv\delta(\textnormal{{data}})>0
and a constant

c\equiv c(\textnormal{{data}})>0

such that

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}[\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)]^{1+\delta}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}\leq c\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{2r}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Du)\,\mathrm{d}x,

as wanted.
∎

(Harmonic approximation)
.

Let
\textnormal{{B}}_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

,
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and let

(2.22)

\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r/2}}[\textnormal{{H}}_{0}(Dv)]^{1+\delta}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}\leq c_{1}\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}\textnormal{{H}}_{0}(Dv)\,{\rm d}x,

We now state a Morrey-type decay estimate in the vectorial case. The proof closely follows the argument developed in

Theorem 2.9

(Morrey type decay estimate)
.

Let
\Omega
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
v\in(W^{1,\textnormal{{H}}}\cap L^{\infty})(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a local minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{H}
in (
1.3
)
under assumption (
1.4
). Then, for every
\sigma\in(0,n]
, there exists a constant
c\equiv c(n,N,p,q,\alpha,[a]_{0,\alpha},\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\sigma)
such that

(2.24)

\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Dv)\,dx\leq c\left(\frac{\varrho}{r}\right)^{n-\sigma}\int_{\textnormal{{B}}_{r}}\textnormal{{H}}(x,Dv)\,dx

for every pair of concentric balls
\textnormal{{B}}_{\varrho}\subset\textnormal{{B}}_{r}\subset\Omega
with
\varrho\leq 1
.

3.
Euler-Lagrange system

Now, following the construction outlined in
[
33
, Theorem 2.1]
, we arrive at the following theorem that is a Euler-Lagrange system for constrained local minimizers. First we analyze case
a
, with
\Omega
that is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and introduce some notation. We observe that since
\partial\mathsf{M}\in C^{3}
, the distance function
d(t):=\text{dist}(t,\partial\mathsf{M})
is regular for
t\in\overline{\mathsf{M}}
near
\partial\mathsf{M}
. By negative reflection we extend
d
to a smooth function on a tubular neighborhood
U
of
\partial\mathsf{M}
and define the vector fields

(3.1)

\nu(t):=\text{grad}d(t),\ v(x,t):=B^{-1}(x,t)(\nu(t)),

for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
t\in U
,
B^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
(B^{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
. Observe that, on
\partial\mathsf{M}
the vector field
\nu
is just the interior unit normal vector field. In the following we denote
\{u\in\partial\mathsf{M}\}=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)\in\partial\mathsf{M}\}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
u\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega,\overline{\mathsf{M}})
be a local minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{G}
in (
1
), under assumption (
1.2
), with
\mathsf{M}
as in
a
. Then, for all
\varphi\in(W^{1,\textnormal{{H}}}_{0}\cap L^{\infty})(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})

Abstract
. We prove a lower bound to quantum Max Cut of a graph in terms of the Lovász theta function of its complement. For a graph with m m edges, qmc ​ ( G ) ≥ m 4 ​ ( 1 + 8 3 ​ π ​ 1 ϑ ⁡ ( G ¯ ) − 1 ) \text{qmc}(G)\geq\tfrac{m}{4}\big(1+\tfrac{8}{3\pi}\tfrac{1}{\vartheta(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muG\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)-1}\big) , with the bound achieved by a product state. The proof extends a result by Balla, Janzer, and Sudakov on classical Max Cut and is also inspired by the randomized rounding method of Gharibian and Parekh. The bound outperforms the classical bound when applied to quantum Max Cut.



1.
(Quantum) Max Cut

Max Cut asks for a partition of the vertex set of a graph, such that the number of edges crossing the two sets is maximized.
In other words,

\mc(G)
is the size of the largest cut induced by a subgraph.
It can be modeled as the polynomial optimization problem,

\displaystyle\max\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{uv\in E}(1-z_{u}z_{v})

(1)

s.t.

\displaystyle z_{u}\in\{+1,-1\}\text{ for all }u\in V\,.

Let
\mc(G)
be the size of the largest cut of
G
. Denote by
m=|E|
. The surplus is defined as

\surplus(G)=\mc(G)-m/2
,
motivated by the fact that a value of
m/2

can be obtained by a simple probabilistic method.
Balla, Janzer, and Sudakov
[
BJS24
]
showed that

\surplus(G)\geq\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{m}{\vartheta(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muG\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)-1}
,
where
\vartheta(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muG\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
is the Lovász theta function of the complement graph
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muG\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.
Equivalently,

(2)

\mc(G)\geq\frac{m}{2}\Big(1+\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{1}{\vartheta(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muG\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)-1}\Big)\,.

The result originates in the following formulation of the Lovász theta function.

Definition 1

(
[
KMS98
]
)
.

The Lovász theta function of a graph
G
, denoted
\vartheta(G)
, is the minimal
\kappa\geq 2
for which there exists a unit vector
x_{v}
(in some Euclidean space) for every vertex
v
such that
\langle x_{v},x_{u}\rangle=-\tfrac{1}{\kappa-1}
holds whenever
u
and
v
are distinct vertices in
G
and
uv\not\in E(G)
.

Without restriction of generality, the vectors
x_{u}
can be chosen to lie in
\mathds{R}^{n}
,
as
n
vectors span a at most
n
-dimensional space.
The definition of for
\vartheta(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muG\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
has

uv\not\in E(G)
replaced by
uv\in E(G)
.

Quantum Max Cut
(QMC) is a
generalization of Max Cut [Eq. (
1
)] to operators,
and asks for the largest eigenvalue of the Hamiltonian

(3)

H^{\qmc}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{uv\in E}(I-X_{u}X_{v}-Y_{u}Y_{v}-Z_{u}Z_{v})\,.

Here
X_{u},Y_{u},Z_{u}
are the tensor product operators acting with the Pauli matrices
X,Y,Z
on vertex
u
and with identity on the remaining vertices.
Denote by
\qmc(G)
the largest eigenvalue of
H^{\qmc}
.
The study of
H^{\qmc}
is also physically motivated. Quantum magnetic systems can be modeled by the anti-ferromagnetic Heisenberg Hamiltonian
H^{\text{H}}=-H^{\qmc}
,
and one is interested in the ground state energy, i.e. its smallest eigenvalue.
Naturally, the task of finding the smallest eigenvalue of

H^{\text{H}}
is equivalent to finding the largest eigenvalue of
H^{\qmc}
.
We restrict ourselves to the maximization problem
H^{\qmc}
.
Note that a trivial lower bound on
\qmc
is obtained by the maximally mixed state, achieving
{m}/{4}
.

and

The identity matrix is

We sketch the key idea of Gharibian and Parekh which uses

Index a

M

by the Cartesian product of
qubit position
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and choice of

iX

X

i

\sigma_{x}^{(i)}

Given a state
\varrho
,
form a moment matrix with entries

(18)

M(ik,jl)=\tr\big(\varrho\,\sigma_{k}^{(i)}\sigma_{l}^{(j)}\big)\,.

Then
M(ik,jl)=M(jl,ik)
for all
i\neq j
as Pauli matrices acting on different subsystems commute.
But on the same coordinate two different Pauli matrices anti-commute,
and so

M(ik,il)=-M(il,ik)
.
By construction,
M\succeq 0
, and the ground state energy

\tr(H^{\qmc}\varrho)
can be extracted from
M
as a linear combination of its entries.

The idea is then to relax the optimization over states
\varrho

to an optimization over all positive-semidefinite matrices satisfying the above constraints.
The objective function is invariant under a global transpose of
M
and any imaginary entries (corresponding to anti-commuting pairs of Pauli matrices) can be set to zero.

A Cholesky decomposition of the optimal
M
results in
3n
unit vectors
v_{ik}\in\mathds{R}^{3n}
so that

M(ik,jl)=v_{ik}^{T}v_{jl}
.
Now stack the vectors belonging to a single vertex,

(19)

u_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{iX}\\
v_{iY}\\
v_{iZ}\end{pmatrix}\,,

and normalize,

x_{i}=u_{i}\,/\,||u_{i}||\,.

Round to vectors in
\mathds{R}^{3}
with a random matrix
R\in\mathds{R}^{3\times 3n}
,
where
R
is a random matrix whose entries are distributed independently according to the standard normal distribution with mean
0
and variance
1
,

y_{i}={Rx_{i}}/{||Rx_{i}||}\,.

Define the product state
\varrho^{\text{GP}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\varrho_{i}
, where

\varrho_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathds{1}+y_{i}^{x}\sigma_{x}+y_{i}^{y}\sigma_{z}+y_{i}^{z}\sigma_{z}\big)
.
Using the
Briët-de Oliveira Filho-Vallentin rounding [Lemma
2

Gharibian and Parekh
[
GP19
]

showed that this randomized rounding approach
achieves an approximation ratio of

\frac{\mathds{E}[\tr(H^{\qmc}\varrho^{\text{GP}})}{\qmc(G)}\geq 0.498\,.

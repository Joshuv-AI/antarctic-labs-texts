Introduction

Zoonotic diseases caused by different parasites are recently more frequently diagnosed in humans, who are usually accidental hosts for them. Contact with domestic animals, mainly dogs and cats increases a risk for the infection. These animals can be infected with Dirofilaria nematodes through the mosquitos’ bites as well as with Toxocara spp. People living in villages, dealing with agriculture and breeding are more exposed to parasitic infections than people living in cities.

Toxocarosis and dirofilariasis have worldwide distribution. The diseases are widespread in developing regions, but they are emerging in Western countries too, becoming an important public health problem [1].

The incidence of human dirofilariosis cases has increased strongly over the last decades in Austria and mostly Dirofilaria repens and Dirofilaria immitis have been detected [2].

In Europa over 1500 cases of human dirofilariossis caused by D. repens have been described most of them in Mediterranean countries and also in Ukraine [3–5]. Dirofilaria spp. typically causes subcutaneous nodule changes or is responsible for visual problems connected to migrating adult worms and further inflammatory changes [6].

Subcutaneous presentation and ocular involvement can be very often misdiagnosed due to absence of pathognomonic clinical manifestations [7]. Within the genus Dirofilaria, more than 40 species have been described; however, only a few are associated with human pathology, including D. immitis, D. repens, D. ursi, and D. tenuis [8].

Toxocarosis is distributed worldwilde and represents the most prevalent zoonotic helminth infection in industrialized countries and is one of CDC’s neglected parasitic infection [9, 10].

Infected by Toxocara spp. larvae humans represent wide range of symptoms, from asymptomatic, mild to disseminated illness. Toxocara parasites can also be responsible for different health problems in humans. Millions of people are infected and some of them are suffering from visceral circulating larva syndrome or ocular parasite localization [11].

Parasitic infections in humans are diagnosed incidentally because health care providers has a little knowledge about them. In this manuscript we described a case of a polish farmer with visual problems who was diagnosed multiple parasitic coinfection cased by Dirofilaria, Toxocara sp. and Toxoplasma gondii.

Case report

A 63-year-old man admitted to the Infectious Diseases, Immunodeficiency and Hepatology Department of Poznañ University of Medical Sciences because of visual disturbances and a pain localized near the right eye, which began one month before the admission.

The patients health history was unremarkable. The patient lives in a small village in Wielkopolska region (West Poland) and deals with the cattle breeding. He possesses a cat and two dogs at home.

On admission the physical examination revealed presence of a slight cervical and axillar lymphadenopathy and redness of the right eye. Ophthalmological examination shown presence of a parasite-like structure under the upper right palpebrae, which was surgically removed.

Microscopic parasitic section of the obtained material, performed in the Parasitology Laboratory in Poznañ University Hospital confirmed the nematode parasite from the Dirofilaria genus (Fig. 1).

Several blood smears did not reveal presence of microfilariaemia and eosinophilia.

Serological tests (ELISA) gave the evidence of presence of monoclonal IgG against Toxocara spp. antigens (27 NTU, positive above 11) and also detected specific antibodies anti-Toxoplasma gondii confirming old chronic infection (IgM 0.16 S/CO; IgG 22.90 IU/ml). Positive ELISA anti-Toxocara test was confirmed with Western-blot which revealed presence of characteristic and specific for Toxocara antibodies against antigens of 24–35 kDa.

Parasitic stool examinations did not detect any parasites life stages including Strongyloides stercoralis larvae.

The USG of the abdomen cavity shown only hyperechogenic changes within the liver.

During hospitalization the patient was treated with albendazol (800 mg per day in two doses) for 10 days. The treatment was consolidated with mebendazole 200 mg daily for 6 days. After two months the patient was follow-up in the Outpatient Parasitic Department. He still suffered from visual problems, but the ophtalmological examination was correct. Serological ELISA-Toxocara test was positive and showed increased antibodies level (34 NTU).

Molecular examination

From fragment of the worm DNA was isolated and purified using the Genomic Mini Kit, A&A Biotechnology. The amplification of a fragment of the first subunit of the cytochrome c oxidase gene was conducted using PCR with the primers: RepImF0 (5’TCAGATTAGTATGTTTGTTTGAACTTCTTATTT3’) and RepIm R0 (5’ACAGCAATCCAAATAGAAGCAAAAGT3’) [4]. Product of PCR was sequenced with forward and reverse primers using the Sanger method. The obtained nucleotide sequence was compared with the sequences deposited in the GenBank using NCBI BLAST. The sequence (250 bp) showed 100% similarity to 55 D. repens sequences reported in GenBank database. Based on the molecular examination result the patient was diagnosed with D. repens infection. The sequence obtained in this study was reported to the GenBank database (accession number, PP402115).

Discussion

Parasitic infections belong in many countries to the neglected illnesses and very often are diagnosed accidentally. The diseases spectrum varies dependently on the geographic location, hygine level, living and eating habits and the type of animals that surround the people estates. Contact with animals, even domestic, significantly increases the chance of the diagnosis.

The close emotional relation between people and companion animals is a beneficial relation known as the human-animal bond. However, dogs and cats can play an important role in the transmission of helminthic zoonotic agents such as Toxocara and others [12]. Endemic in nature, dirofilariasis is emerging in rural areas of Eastern and Southern Europe as well as in Central Asia [13]. Presence of nematode infections in humans is varied in different countries and regions. In Russia in several hyper-endemic areas incidence of toxocarosis reach even 16% [14]. In 21-st century dirofilariasis is more frequently reported. In Poland about 20 cases of this parasitic disease were diagnosed in travelers to Italy, Greece and 3 cases in people who never travelled abroad [3, 15, 16].

Toxocarosis is distributed worldwide and prevalence of specific antibodies in humans are very high also in industrialized countries [17]. Although the parasite cases mostly mild, asymptomatic infection it can be responsible for multiorgan involment [18] as well as ocular localization [19].

The number of reported clinical cases of Toxocara spp. infections in children in Poland has recently increased and Toxocara is the most common zoonotic agent in urban public sites and in rural settlements [20].

Dirofilaria is a parasite of domestic and wild animals that can infect humans. Different species of Dirofilaria can be found in men causing different clinical symptoms and ocular dirofilariaosis is one of them and Dirofilaria repens has been reported to occur wildly throughout Asia and Europe [21]. This parasite was observed in periorbital and subconjuctival tissue, interocular localization, in the skin of the eyelids [8, 22].

Although Dirofilaria and Toxocara parasites are rare diagnosed, due to the absence of characteristic clinical syndroms, actually more health care providers from different medicine domains, usually ophthalmologists, take such possibilities under consideration in differential diagnosis so numbers of confirmed cases increased [6, 7].

In described above case we reported a patient with multiple parasitic infections which indicates necessity for such diagnostics in patients living in agriculture areas and having contact with animals suffering from visual disturbances. Sometimes systemic parasitic coinfections, such as Toxocara spp. and Toxoplasma gondii can result in intraocular inflammation, which can be a major cause of blindness. Although Toxoplasma gondii parasite infection is usually self-limiting, the parasite lives latent within intratissue cysts in the host. In specific situations connected to host’s immunologic state which could be activeted by presence of others parasites the tissue forms are capable to reactivate into acute leasions (e.g. brain, eyes, mussles) [23]. That is why ELISA-Toxoplasma testing can be helpful in the differential diagnosis altough in ocular toxoplasmoasis anti-Toxoplasma IgM are not detected [24].

Although the possibility of cross-reactivity must be considered in the case of a positive anti-Toxocara test, we regard this scenario as highly unlikely, since we obtained positive results both in ELISA and in the Western blot test, the latter being characterized by high specificity and serving as a confirmatory assay.

This case shows also that such individuals require combined cooperation with different specialists.

Increased antibodies level detected after the specific Toxocara treatment probably indicates that the parasite was disintegrated and that is why the immunologic host (human) system was able to produce more monoclonal antibodies.

Parasitic infections due to climate changes should also be taken more frequently under consideration in medical practice specially in patients with undifferentiated symptoms.

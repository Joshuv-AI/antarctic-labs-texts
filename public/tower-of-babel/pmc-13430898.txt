miR-192-5p targets USP1 to regulate osteogenic differentiation and diagnose the occurrence of osteoporotic fracture
Authors: Shurong Chen, Yuan Ruan, Shenyi Lu, Enxiong Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoporosis (OP) in the elderly reduces bone strength, significantly increasing the risk of osteoporotic fracture (OPF). Early screening is essential to alleviate this widespread health problem, and miR-192-5p has recently been identified as a potential candidate.PurposeTo elucidate the diagnostic role of miR-192-5p in OPF patients and explore its underlying molecular mechanisms.MethodsmiR-192-5p expression in serum of OPF patients and hBMSCs was determined by real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). The mRNA levels of osteogenic markers in hBMSCs were also quantified by RT-qPCR. The regulation of hBMSCs by miR-192-5p was determined through methyl thiazolyl tetrazolium (MTT) assay and flow cytometry. The downstream targets of miR-192-5p were validated through luciferase activity assay. The diagnostic potential of miR-192-5p for OPF was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) method.ResultsmiR-192-5p level was reduced in OPF patients, and its AUC for distinguishing OPF patients is 0.882. The levels of miR-192-5p and osteogenic markers runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2), osteocalcin (OCN), osteopontin (OPN) and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) were upregulated after induction of differentiation in hBMSCs. Mechanically, inhibition of miR-192-5p may attenuate the upregulation of osteogenic markers and reduce the proliferation of hBMSCs by regulating ubiquitin specific peptidase 1 (USP1).ConclusionmiR-192-5p may serve as a potential biomarker for the diagnosis of OPF, and the miR-192-5p/USP1 axis offers a promising therapeutic target for OPF intervention.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-026-09996-3.Keywords: Osteoporotic fractures, Osteoporosis, miR-192-5p, Osteogenic differentiation, USP1

Introduction

Osteoporosis (OP) is a systemic metabolic bone disorder defined by a reduction in bone mass and the deterioration of microarchitectural integrity, ultimately leading to increased bone fragility and heightened susceptibility to fractures [1]. Epidemiological data indicate that OP has emerged as a major public health concern in China, with projections anticipating over 200 million cases by the year 2050 [2, 3]. The phenomenon of population aging has precipitated a notable rise in the prevalence of OP, closely correlating with a marked increase in fracture incidence [4]. Osteoporotic fractures (OPF) are low-trauma events or spontaneous fractures resulting from OP-induced bone deterioration, characterized by reduced bone mineral density, microarchitectural disruption, and the increase in bone fragility [5]. As the most critical consequence of OP, OPF primarily impact elderly populations, particularly postmenopausal women and individuals of advanced age. These fractures significantly elevate patient morbidity and mortality rates, while concurrently imposing considerable socioeconomic burdens [6]. The pathogenesis of OPF entails a complex array of molecular mechanisms that remain incompletely understood.

Advancements in the field of bioinformatics have facilitated the widespread identification of microRNAs (miRNAs), which exhibit evolutionary conservation, structural stability, and functional diversity across eukaryotic organisms [7]. Notably, increasing evidence demonstrates that miRNAs critically regulate osteogenic signaling pathways, thereby influencing processes related to bone formation, the pathogenesis of OP, and fracture healing [8, 9]. miR-192-5p was previously observed to be downregulated in cases of gouty arthritis and mediated cell activation by binding to EREG protein [10]. Furthermore, the downregulation of miR-192-5p was demonstrated to have early predictive and therapeutic potential in patients suffering from diabetic nephropathy [11]. Recent studies have also identified miR-192-5p as a pivotal regulator of OP, which has piqued [12].

As osteoblast and osteocyte precursors, hBMSCs possess robust proliferative capacity and multilineage differentiation potential [13]. The regulation of their functional activities is critical, as it impacts the osteogenic differentiation of hBMSCs and plays a vital role in the process of fracture healing. In clinical practice, scientifically sound and effective nursing interventions for OPF patients, such as early rehabilitation guidance, fall prevention, and psychological support, can significantly improve patient outcomes and promote fracture healing. Clarifying the underlying molecular mechanisms is expected to provide an objective theoretical basis for developing more targeted, individualized nursing strategies in the future. Therefore, we have selected this cell line to investigate the mechanisms underlying osteogenic differentiation associated with OPF to reveal its significance in the treatment of OPF.

This study elucidates the mechanistic role of miR-192-5p in OPF. Through comprehensive analysis of patient serum and hBMSCs, we have identified the dysregulated expression of miR-192-5p as a pivotal modulator of osteogenic differentiation, revealing its potential as a novel diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target in the management of OPF.

Materials and methods

Study subjects and serum collection

This study employed a retrospective case-control design. Consecutive patients diagnosed with OP (controls) and OPF (cases) patients at the orthopedic outpatient department of The Affiliated Hospital of Youjiang Medical University for Nationalities between March 2023 and March 2025 were selected, with 96 cases in each group. The two groups were matched for age and gender, and clinical information is summarized in Table 1.

All included patients met the World Health Organization diagnostic criteria for primary osteoporosis. OP group included patients with T score ≤ -2.5 but no history of fractures. OPF group included patients with primary OP presenting with a first fragility fracture and T score ≤ -2.5. Patients were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: (1) fractures caused by high-energy trauma or non-osteoporosis pathologies; (2) comorbidities such as hypertension, diabetes, malignancy, or other severe systemic diseases; (3) patients with severe systemic diseases and use of bone-modifying drugs. Intramedullary nailing was performed on the OPF patient, and all received standardized routine orthopedic care and rehabilitation management postoperatively. The study was conducted with the endorsement of The Affiliated Hospital of Youjiang Medical University for Nationalities ethics committee, and all participants provided informed consent prior to enrollment.

Blood samples were meticulously collected, and serum was separated after centrifugation at 3000 r/min for a duration of 10 min and placed properly.

Cell line and culture

The hBMSCs were purchased from Wuhan Provo Life Technology Co., Ltd (Wuhan, China) and cultured in DMEM medium (with 10% FBS). The humidity incubator was set at 5% CO2, 37 ℃.

Induction of osteogenic differentiation

The hBMSCs cultured to the third passage were washed twice with PBS and then grown in DMEM medium supplemented with 100 U/mL penicillin-streptomycin solution, 10% FBS, 10 mM dexamethasone, 10 mM β-glycerophosphate, 0.2 mM ascorbic acid, and 1% glutamine. hBMSCs were seeded at l×l05 cells/well in 6-well plates, and the medium was replaced with fresh medium every 2 days. The induced hBMSCs were collected at 0, 7 and 14 days of induction for assay analysis. Meanwhile, hBMSCs cultured in DMEM medium (with 10% FBS) were used as control group.

Real-time quantitative PCR assay

TRIZOL (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) was utilized for the extraction of RNA. The reaction system was configured to synthesize cDNA according to the instructions of the Precision nanoScript2 Reverse Transcription Kit (Primer Design, UK). RT-qPCR was conducted using SYBR qPCR Green Master Mix (Vazyme, China), employing the 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA) for the reactions. The data obtained were quantified using the 2−ΔΔCt method for miR-192-5p, as well as the mRNA levels of USP1, RUNX2, OCN, OPN, and ALP. Primer sequences are recorded in Supplementary Table 1.

Transfection assay

The well-grown hBMSCs were transferred into 6-well plates and transfected with the miR-192-5p mimic (50 nM), miR-192-5p inhibitor (100 nM), si-USP1 (100 nM) or their respective negative controls (synthesized by Ribobio; Guangzhou, China) using Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen, USA) for 48 h to achieve the overexpression or knockdown of miR-192-5p and USP1.

Cell proliferation activity was detected by MTT assay

The hBMSCs were carefully seeded into 96-well plates and subjected to grouping. Following a 48-hour incubation period, MTT solution (Sigma, USA) was added to each well. After an additional 4 h of incubation, the culture medium was removed, and 150 µL of dimethyl sulfoxide (Solarbio, China) was added to the reaction for 10 min. The absorbance value (490 nm) was measured using an enzyme-labeled reader.

Apoptosis rate was detected by flow cytometry

The hBMSCs were digested with trypsin and the resulting cells were centrifuged. FITC-Annexin V binding solution and PI staining solution were added after resuspending the cells by Binding Buffer. After incubation in the dark for 15 min, the apoptosis rate of the cells was assessed using flow cytometry and analyzed with CELLQuest software.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Wild-type and mutant-type USP1 vectors were meticulously constructed according to the binding sites of miR-192-5p and USP1. Wild-type (WT)/mutant-type (MUT)-USP1 was co-transfected into hBMSCs with mimic/inhibitor negative control (NC) or miR-192-5p mimic/inhibitor, which were designed and synthesized by Jinweizhi Corporation (Suzhou, China). The luciferase activity of the cells was evaluated by Dual-Luciferase Detection Kit (Promega Corporation, USA).

Western Blotting (WB)

hBMSCs were collected and lysed by RIPA and quantified by BCA method. They were electrophoresed by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF. After blocking with 5% skim milk, the primary antibodies and HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies of USP1, RUNX2, OCN, OPN, and ALP were incubated successively. After ECL color development, quantitative analysis was completed by ImageJ software.

Statistical analysis

All data are presented as n or mean ± standard deviation (SD). ROC analysis validated miR-192-5p’s predictive capacity for OPF. Differences between groups were assessed via the Students’ t-test and one-way ANOVA. Pearson correlation analysis confirmed the relationship between miR-192-5p and USP1 levels. The association between miR-192-5p expression and clinical indicators was assessed by chi-square analysis. The risk indicators for the development of OPF were determined by logistic analysis. Three sets of samples were set up in parallel and repeated more than three times for each group. The significance threshold was set at P < 0.05.

Result

General data analysis of included patients

This study enrolled 96 OP and 96 OPF patients matched for age, gender, and BMI. OPF patients showed significantly lower T-score, BMD and Ca, alongside elevated PINP, and β-CTX levels versus OP control (Table 1; all P < 0.05).

miR-192-5p was diminished in the serum of OPF patients

miR-192-5p was quantified in preserved serum samples from patients, revealing a notable downregulation of miR-192-5p in the serum of individuals with OPF as compared to those with OP (Fig. 1A). Meanwhile, the ROC curve revealed the remarkable sensitivity of miR-192-5p for the early identification of OPF (Fig. 1B; AUC = 0.882, Sensitivity = 92.71%, Specificity = 70.83%). Serum miR-192-5p levels increased over time in OPF patients receiving intramedullary nailing treatment (Fig. 1C).

Osteogenic differentiation of hBMSCs

miR-192-5p levels were assayed in differentiated hBMSCs, revealing a notable upregulation during osteogenesis (Fig. 2A). As the process of osteogenic differentiation advanced, there was a subsequent increase in the expression of associated markers, including RUNX2, OCN, OPN, and ALP (Fig. 2B), suggesting that miR-192-5p may mediate the process of osteogenic differentiation.

Regulation of miR-192-5p in hBMSCs

Transfection of hBMSCs by miR-192-5p mimic or inhibitor to up-regulate or down-regulate miR-192-5p expression, respectively. The transfection efficiencies are illustrated in Fig. 2C. High levels of miR-192-5p promoted cell viability and reduced cell apoptosis, whereas impoverished miR-192-5p suppressed cell proliferation and accelerated apoptosis (Fig. 2D and E).

USP1 was significantly expressed in OPF

The complementary interactions between miR-192-5p and USP1 in the public database (Targetscan) are stated in Fig. 3A. In validation assays, miR-192-5p overexpression significantly suppressed the luciferase activity of USP1-wt, whereas the knockdown of miR-192-5p exerted a positive influence. The modulation of miR-192-5p did not show significant effect the luciferase activity of USP1-mut (Fig. 3B). These findings indicate that USP1 is downstream of miR-192-5p. Quantification of USP1 from included patients reflected increased levels of USP1 in the OPF group compared to the OP group (Fig. 3C). USP1 expression was negatively regulated by miR-192-5p (Fig. 3D; r = -0.652, P < 0.001). Additionally, the serum levels of USP1 in OPF patients exhibited a consistent decline over time post-treatment (Fig. 3E), and USP1 expression was downregulated time-dependently in osteogenic differentiation-induced hBMSCs (Fig. 3F).

miR-192-5p regulates USP1-mediated osteogenic markers and hBMSCs activity

To further clarify the regulation of the OPF mechanism by miR-192-5p through regulating USP1, we conducted experiments involving the knockdown of both miR-192-5p and USP1, subsequently co-transfecting these into hBMSCs. The introduction of the miR-192-5p inhibitor resulted in a notable increase in USP1 levels compared to the inhibitor NC group, a phenomenon that was reversed by the knockdown of USP1 (Fig. 4A). When miR-192-5p level was downregulated, the level of osteogenic marker RUNX2 was decreased. Compared to the miR-192-5p inhibitor group, RUNX2 levels were elevated in the miR-192-5p inhibitor + si-USP1 group (Fig. 4B). Consistently, knockdown of USP1 reversed the inhibitory effect of miR-192-5p inhibitor on the expression of OCN, OPN and ALP (Fig. 4C, D and E). Protein validation experiments showed the same trend of changes (Fig. 4F). Moreover, reduced levels of miR-192-5p were found to suppress the proliferation of hBMSCs while promoting apoptosis, a detrimental effect that was repaired through the co-transfection of miR-192-5p inhibitor alongside down-regulated USP1 (Fig. 4G and H). In contrast to the miR-192-5p inhibitor group, the miR-192-5p inhibitor + si-NC group revealed no significant changes in osteogenic markers or cellular activity. The above rescue assay exemplifies that knockdown of miR-192-5p targeting USP1 impedes the expression of osteogenesis-related markers and the activity of hBMSCs, consequently hindering their differentiation into osteoblasts and compromising the healing process in fractures.

Discussion

OPF represents a substantial global health concern, severely compromising patient quality of life. At this stage in clinical intervention for OPF, interventions typically include fracture reduction surgery and minimally invasive pain relief procedures. Concurrently, immediate anti-osteoporotic therapy is administered, with preference for intravenous bisphosphonates [14]. However, the efficacy of pharmacological interventions is limited, and side effects are inevitable. Therefore, it is increasingly urgent to investigate the pathological mechanisms of OPF and to find diagnostic and therapeutic targets for OPF.

Numerous studies have confirmed that miRNAs are abnormally expressed in fracture patients. For example, miR-29b-3p targets and negatively modulates LIN7A to improve fracture healing in patients with nonunion [15]. miR-532-5p has been noted to influence osteoblast differentiation and OPF progression by regulating FOXO1 [16]. Moreover, miR-129-3p, miR-32-3p and miR-21-5p are also thought to be closely related to fracture repair [17–19]. Focusing on miR-192-5p, which is encoded at human chromosome 11q13.1 locus, this miRNA emerges as a critical regulatory element in the context of fibrotic, neoplastic, and metabolic disorders [20, 21]. miR-192-5p downregulation in respiratory and neoplastic diseases modulates pathological progression [22], aligning with our findings. miR-192-5p were markedly reduced in OPF patients versus OP controls. Meanwhile, serum miR-192-5p was enhanced during the patients’ postoperative recovery. These findings imply that miR-192-5p may serve as a potential risk biomarker for OPF and diagnostic target, while its restoration might correlate with enhanced bone healing.

hBMSCs are stem cells with multidirectional differentiation potential, which can be differentiated into osteoblasts under specific conditions and participate in bone formation and repair [23]. In our exploration of hBMSCs treated with osteogenic differentiation induction, we found a time-dependent upregulation of miR-192-5p. Moreover, the content of related markers during osteogenic differentiation of hBMSCs is also increased. To further verify the role of miR-192-5p in this process, dysregulated miR-192-5p was transfected into hBMSCs after differentiation induction. Zhang et al. reported that upregulated miR-466 L-3p during the osteogenic differentiation of hBMSCs, which correlates with enhanced osteogenic marker expression and improved cell viability [24]. Here, miR-192-5p mimic positively affected the proliferation of hBMSCs and attenuated the apoptosis rate, whereas the miR-192-5p inhibitor elicited contrary effects. Collectively, these findings demonstrate miR-192-5p’s critical role in osteogenic differentiation and suggest its functional involvement in the processes underlying fracture healing. From the perspective of clinical translation, this molecular mechanism not only provides a basis for targeted therapy but also provides a potential theoretical support for perioperative nursing intervention to promote fracture healing.

To elucidate miR-192-5p’s role in the pathogenesis of OPF, we identified USP1 as a putative target. USP1 was enriched in serum samples from OPF patients and inversely correlated with miR-192-5p expression. USP1 was revealed in previous literature to be outstanding expressed in patients with nonunion patients and nonunion rats [25]. Additionally, USP1 was confirmed to be upregulated in patients suffering from hepatocellular carcinoma and colitis [26, 27]. In our study involving differentiation induced hBMSCs, knockdown of miR-192-5p resulted in suppressed mRNA levels of key osteogenic markers, including RUNX2, OCN, OPN, and ALP. Notably, these markers were restored following the silencing of USP1. Meanwhile, low levels of USP1 ameliorated the inhibitory effects of hBMSCs viability by miR-192-5p inhibitor and reduced the number of apoptotic cells. These findings indicate that miR-192-5p knockdown impairs hBMSC osteogenic differentiation and diminishes cell viability via targeted regulation of USP1. Although this study has not yet investigated the specific downstream substrates of USP1 in hBMSCs, USP1 typically exerts its biological functions by deubiquitinating and stabilizing specific target proteins, which often leads to inhibited differentiation or accelerated cellular senescence [28].

This study is subject to several limitations. Firstly, our investigation has been narrowly focused on the miR-192-5p/USP1 regulatory axis, without delving into additional targets of miR-192-5p. Secondly, in the context of cancer research, it has been reported that miR-192-5p mediates cell biological processes by regulating Wnt, TGFβ and PI3K/Akt signaling pathways [29]. However, whether miR-192-5p exerts its effects in OPF through the above regulatory network remains to be elucidated. Thirdly, our findings are based solely on in vitro cellular models and clinical serum assays. The absence of in vivo validation using an OPF animal model limits the persuasiveness of the results. Future studies should adopt a more comprehensive approach, integrating multi-omics analyses and in vivo functional validation, to systematically unravel these intricate mechanisms.

In summary, miR-192-5p was downregulated in OPF and sensitively differentiated OPF from OP patients. miR-192-5p expression increases during the processes of fracture healing and osteogenic differentiation of hBMSCs. Functional studies demonstrate that miR-192-5p affected fracture healing through USP1-mediated suppression of osteogenesis differentiation, reduced proliferation, and induced apoptosis. These findings provide theoretical basis and evidence support for the impact of miR-192-5p on bone repair mechanisms in patients with OPF.

Superconductivity in multi-Weyl semimetals: Conditions for the coexistence of topological and conventional phases
Alonso Tapia, Enrique Muñoz

Abstract
In this work, we explore the possible emergence of superconducting phases in a multi-Weyl semimetal. In particular, we show that the presence of a pair of Weyl nodes with chirality | ν | ≥ 1 |\nu|\geq 1 leads to an effective description of the intra-nodal pairings in terms of monopole harmonics, in contrast to inter-nodal pairings that preserve the angular dependence of conventional spherical harmonics. Therefore, we explore the conditions for the competition and/or coexistence between both types of superconducting phases, and we identify the presence of the so-called ”topological repulsion” mechanism, which was previously reported in the context of simple Weyl semimetals. We identified the critical temperatures corresponding to the monopole and conventional superconducting phases, and calculated the specific heat as a function of temperature, thus showing that this thermodynamical parameter may provide an experimental probe to determine the chirality index ν \nu in the material.

The topological features of the band structure in WSMs suggest the possible existence of diverse pairing states

;
Meng and Balents 2012
;
Cho et al. 2012
;
Li and Haldane 2018
;
Sun et al. 2020
. In particular, the monopole superconductor (MSC), recently proposed as a pairing state between the two Fermi surfaces (FSs) enclosing the Weyl points in a doped WSM

;
Sun et al. 2020
, involves the superposition of the Berry phases of the individual single-particle states, thus determining the Berry phase of the emerging Cooper pair. Therefore, the resulting chirality, i.e. the vorticity of the pair, will be characterized by the combination of those of the individual Weyl nodes. For spherically symmetric Fermi surfaces, it was shown

that the pairing will correspond to monopole harmonic functions, in contrast to the spherical harmonics in conventional superconductivity. In order to experimentally detect those non-trivial vorticity effects, magneto-transport measurements can be applied to discriminate chiral versus non-chiral pairing states. This mechanism has been investigated by one of the authors

;
Muñoz et al. 2024
in the context of the competition between the monopole pairing, characterized by the monopole harmonic functions

;
Muñoz et al. 2024
, while exclusion of one phase in favor of the other arises when the
\theta

\mathcal{Y}_{|q|,j,|m|}(\theta,\varphi)
and the spherical harmonic
Y_{j,m}(\theta,\varphi)
are proportional to each other. We termed this mechanism as ”topological repulsion”
Muñoz et al. 2020
.
In the present work, we extend the theoretical analysis
Muñoz et al. 2020
;
Muñoz et al. 2024
to study the possible emergence of topological superconducting phases, and in particular the competition between conventional and monopole pairing, in the context of multi-Weyl semimetals. Furthermore, we analyze the critical temperatures for each of those phases, and the specific heat as a possible probe for the chirality index
\nu
in multi-WSMs.

II
The Model

Let us start from the general form of the Hamiltonian representing a Weyl semimetal in the presence of particle-particle interactions

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{V}.

(1)

Here, the non-interacting contribution is defined, in a generalized two-band model, by

\hat{H}_{0}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\sigma,\mathbf{k}}\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right]_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{c}_{\sigma^{\prime},\mathbf{k}},

(2)

for fermion operators
[\hat{c}_{\sigma,\mathbf{k}},\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\sigma^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}]_{+}=\delta_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}
,
with
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\left(\hat{\sigma}^{x},\hat{\sigma}^{y},\hat{\sigma}^{z}\right)
the usual Pauli matrices.
The explicit form of the components of the vector
\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{k})=\left(N_{1}(\mathbf{k}),N_{2}(\mathbf{k}),N_{3}(\mathbf{k})\right)^{T}
determines the topological properties of the effective two-band model, and the corresponding energy bands are trivially
E^{\pm}_{\mathbf{k}}=\pm|\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{k})|
. For instance, a double WSM can be realized by
Roy et al. 2017

\displaystyle N_{1}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\left(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y}\right)

\displaystyle N_{2}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\sin k_{y}\sin k_{x}

\displaystyle N_{3}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{z}\cos(k_{z}a)-m_{z}+t_{0}\left\{6+\cos(2k_{x})+\cos(2k_{y})\right.

(3)

\displaystyle\left.-4\cos k_{x}-4\cos k_{y}\right\}

In addition, we shall assume a short-range interaction, involving a nearest-neighbors Coulomb repulsion
V_{1}>0
and a contact, phonon-mediated effective attractive interaction
V_{0}<0
. When both terms are combined we have
Muñoz et al. 2024

\hat{V}=V_{0}\sum_{i}\hat{n}_{i}\hat{n}_{i}+\frac{1}{2}V_{1}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\hat{n}_{i}\hat{n}_{j}.

(4)

Here
\hat{n}_{i}=\sum_{\sigma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{R}_{i})\hat{c}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{R}_{i})
is the occupation number operator at the site
\mathbf{R}_{i}
on the lattice in the local Wannier basis. By the appropriate Fourier transformation
\hat{c}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{R}_{i})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{i}}\hat{c}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{k})
, the interaction term is expressed in the Bloch-momentum basis by

\hat{V}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\sigma,\tau}V_{\mathbf{q}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\sigma,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\hat{c}_{\sigma,\mathbf{k}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\tau,\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}}\hat{c}_{\tau,\mathbf{p}},

(5)

with a matrix element given by

V_{\mathbf{q}}=V_{0}+V_{1}(\cos q_{x}+\cos q_{y}+\cos q_{z}).

(6)

In order to capture the topological features of the multi-Weyl semimetal band structure, we define operators in the nodal Fermi surface representation, by expanding the momenta in the vicinity of each Weyl point as follows
\hat{\psi}_{\pm,\sigma}(\mathbf{k})=\hat{c}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{K}_{\pm})
for
|\mathbf{k}|\ll|\mathbf{K}_{\pm}|
. In this nodal representation, a generic low-energy model for a multi-Weyl semimetal with topological charge
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
is
Roy et al. 2017

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k},a=\pm}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{a}(\mathbf{k})\big\{v_{F}\left[\alpha_{\nu}k_{\perp}^{\nu}\cos(\nu\phi_{\mathbf{k}})\hat{\sigma}^{x}\right.

(7)

\displaystyle\left.+\alpha_{\nu}k_{\perp}^{\nu}\sin(\nu\phi_{\mathbf{k}})\hat{\sigma}^{y}+ak_{z}\hat{\sigma}^{z}\right]-\mu^{a}\big\}\hat{\psi}_{a}(\mathbf{k}),

where
a=\pm
represents the sign of the nodal topological charge (chirality), and
\mu^{a}
is the chemical potential at each node.

For the interaction operator, we take a similar approach, starting from the Bloch-momentum representation (
5
) and choosing the pairing mechanism of our interest,

\displaystyle\hat{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}}V^{ab,cd}_{\mathbf{q}}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{a,\sigma}(\mathbf{k+q})\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{b,\tau}(\mathbf{p-q})\hat{\psi}_{c,\tau}(\mathbf{p})\hat{\psi}_{d,\sigma}(\mathbf{k}),

where now we include all the interaction coefficients in a single matrix element
V^{ab,cd}_{\mathbf{q}}
. Explicitly, expanding up to second order in the momenta, we obtain
Muñoz et al. 2024

\displaystyle V^{\pm\pm,\pm\pm}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle\,=V^{\pm\mp,\mp\pm}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}=V_{0}+V_{1}\bigg(3-\frac{(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})^{2}}{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle V^{\pm\mp,\pm\mp}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle=V_{0}+V_{1}\bigg\{2-\frac{|\mathbf{k}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp}|^{2}}{2}+\Big(1-\frac{(k_{z}-q_{z})^{2}}{2}\Big)\cos 2Q\mp(k_{z}-q_{z})\sin 2Q\bigg\},

(9)

with
\mathbf{k}_{\perp}=(k_{x},k_{y},0)
. The proper treatment of these coefficients in the low-energy regime may lead to the emergence of different superconducting phases, according to their intra- or inter-node pairing structure
Muñoz et al. 2020
;
Muñoz et al. 2024
. In a standard BCS-like mean-field approximation, we write the interaction as

\hat{V}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\sigma,\tau,a,b}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{a,\sigma}(\mathbf{k})\Delta^{ab}_{\sigma\tau}(\mathbf{k})\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{b,\tau}(-\mathbf{k})+h.c.,

(10)

where the pairing function is defined via the self-consistent relation

\Delta^{ab}_{\sigma\tau}(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{\mathbf{q},c,d}V^{ab,cd}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}\langle\hat{\psi}_{c,\tau}(-\mathbf{q})\hat{\psi}_{d,\sigma}(\mathbf{q})\rangle.

(11)

Following the method presented in
Muñoz et al. 2020
, we introduce the Bogoliubov transformation
\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{a}(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{\sigma}\zeta_{a,\sigma}(\mathbf{k})\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{a,\sigma}(\mathbf{k})
, where the spinor
\zeta_{a}(\mathbf{k})
is the positive energy eigenvector of
\hat{H}_{0}
at each Weyl node
a=\pm
.Thus, we have

\zeta_{\pm}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}(\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}\pm k_{z})}}\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}\pm k_{z}\\
\alpha_{\nu}k_{\perp}^{\nu}e^{i\nu\phi_{\mathbf{k}}}\end{pmatrix},

(12)

where was defined
\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}+\alpha^{2}_{\nu}k_{\perp}^{2\nu}}
. In this basis, the Hamiltonian is then reduced to

\hat{H}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},a,b}\Big\{\xi_{\mathbf{k}}^{a}\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{a}(\mathbf{k})\hat{\alpha}_{a}(\mathbf{k})+\Delta^{ab}(\mathbf{k})\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{a}(\mathbf{k})\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{b}(-\mathbf{k})+h.c.\Big\},

(13)

where the single-particle contribution was diagonalized, leading to the dispersion relation

\displaystyle\xi_{\textbf{k}}^{\pm}=v_{F}\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}+\alpha_{\nu}^{2}k_{\perp}^{2\nu}}-\mu^{\pm},

(14)

and the projected gap equation in the new basis becomes

\Delta^{ab}(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{\mathbf{q},c,d}\bar{V}^{ab,cd}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}\langle\hat{\alpha}_{c}(-\mathbf{q})\hat{\alpha}_{d}(\mathbf{q})\rangle,

(15)

with matrix elements
\bar{V}^{ab,cd}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}
given by the corresponding transformation from the originals to this new basis.

Let us further define the Nambu spinor
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}=\big(\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{-}(\mathbf{k}),\hat{\alpha}_{-}(-\mathbf{k}),\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{+}(\mathbf{k}),\hat{\alpha}_{+}(-\mathbf{k})\big)
, in order to arrange the Hamiltonian in the Bogoliubov-de Gennes notation as a bilinear form
Muñoz et al. 2020
;
Muñoz et al. 2024

\hat{H}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\Psi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{H}_{\text{BdG}}(\mathbf{k})\Psi_{\mathbf{k}},

(16)

where we defined the matrix

\hat{H}_{BdG}(\textbf{k})=\left(\begin{matrix}\xi_{\textbf{k}}^{-}&\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}&0&\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}\\
(\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}})^{*}&-\xi_{\textbf{k}}^{-}&(\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}})^{*}&0\\
0&\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}&\xi_{\textbf{k}}^{+}&\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}\\
(\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}})^{*}&0&(\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}})^{*}&-\xi_{\textbf{k}}^{+}\end{matrix}\right).

(17)

For subsequent computations, it is convenient to express this matrix in a
\text{SU}(2)\otimes\text{SU}(2)
basis composed by
\{\hat{\tau}_{\alpha}\otimes\hat{\eta}_{\beta}\}
, where
\hat{\tau}_{\alpha}
span the subspace of the two Weyl nodes, while
\hat{\eta}_{\beta}
represents the particle-hole degrees of freedom. Here,
\alpha,\beta=1,2,3
are the Pauli matrix indexes, while
\alpha,\beta=0
correspond to the two-dimensional representation of the identity. In this notation, we have

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{BdG}(\textbf{k})&=\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}}\hat{\tau}_{0}\otimes\hat{\eta}_{3}+\frac{\delta\mu}{2}\hat{\tau}_{3}\otimes\hat{\eta}_{3}+\Re\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}\hat{\tau}_{0}\otimes\hat{\eta}_{1}\\
-\Im\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}&\hat{\tau}_{0}\otimes\hat{\eta}_{2}+\Re\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}\hat{\tau}_{1}\otimes\hat{\eta}_{1}-\Im\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}\hat{\tau}_{1}\otimes\hat{\eta}_{2},\end{split}

(18)

where we defined the average chemical potential between the pair of nodes as
\bar{\mu}=(\mu_{+}+\mu_{-})/2
, and the difference by
\delta\mu=\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}
. Accordingly, the single quasiparticle dispersion at the average chemical potential is

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}}=v_{F}\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}+\alpha^{2}_{\nu}k_{\perp}^{2\nu}}-\bar{\mu}.

(19)

III
The Gap Equations

At finite temperature, the field theoretical representation of the partition function is

\displaystyle Z={\rm{Tr}}\,e^{-\beta\hat{H}}=\int\mathcal{D}\Psi^{\dagger}\mathcal{D}\Psi e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+\hat{H}_{BDG}\right)\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}}

Therefore, the Green´s function matrix in momentum and Matsubara space is defined by

\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{k}}(\omega_{n})=\left[-i\omega_{n}+\hat{H}_{BdG}(\textbf{k})\right]^{-1},

(21)

where
\omega_{n}=(2n+1)\pi T
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is the Matsubara frequency for Fermions.

The Green’s function is composed by 4 blocks of
2\times 2
matrices (details in appendix
A
), as follows

\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{k}}(\omega_{n})=\left(\begin{matrix}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{k}}^{\text{intra},-}&\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{k}}^{\text{inter}}\\
(\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{k}}^{\text{inter}})^{\dagger}&\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{k}}^{\text{intra},+}\end{matrix}\right).

(22)

Now, we formulate the BCS self-consistent equations, for both the intra-nodal and inter-nodal pairings, as follows

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\sum_{\textbf{k}^{\prime},\omega_{n}}V_{\text{intra}}(\textbf{k},\textbf{k}^{\prime})\langle\hat{\alpha}_{-}(\textbf{k}^{\prime})\hat{\alpha}_{-}(-\textbf{k}^{\prime})\rangle

(23)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\sum_{\textbf{k}^{\prime},\omega_{n}}V_{\text{inter}}(\textbf{k},\textbf{k}^{\prime})\langle\hat{\alpha}_{-}(\textbf{k}^{\prime})\hat{\alpha}_{+}(-\textbf{k}^{\prime})\rangle.

(24)

The correlation functions involved in the definitions above can be directly obtained from the matrix elements of the Green’s function, as follows

\begin{split}&\langle\hat{\alpha}_{-}(\textbf{k})\hat{\alpha}_{-}(-\textbf{k})\rangle=[\hat{\mathcal{G}}^{\text{intra},-}_{\textbf{k}}]_{12}\\
=&\frac{\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}(E_{\textbf{k}}^{2}-\delta\mu\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}})-2\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}B_{\textbf{k}}}{E_{\textbf{k}}^{4}-4B_{\textbf{k}}^{2}-\delta\mu^{2}(\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}}^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}|^{2})}\end{split}

(25)

\begin{split}&\langle\hat{\alpha}_{-}(\textbf{k})\hat{\alpha}_{+}(-\textbf{k})\rangle=[\hat{\mathcal{G}}^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}]_{12}\\
&=\frac{\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}(E_{\textbf{k}}^{2}-\frac{\delta\mu^{2}}{2})-2\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}B_{\textbf{k}}-i\omega_{n}\delta\mu\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}}{E_{\textbf{k}}^{4}-4B_{\textbf{k}}^{2}-\delta\mu^{2}(\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}}^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}|^{2})}\end{split}

(26)

with

\displaystyle E_{\textbf{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}}^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}|^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}|^{2}+\frac{\delta\mu^{2}}{4}+\omega_{n}^{2}}

(27)

\displaystyle B_{\textbf{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}\Re\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}+\Im\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}\Im\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}

(28)

Then, we perform the sum over Matsubara frequencies
\omega_{n}
in the gap equations (details in appendix
B
), to obtain the self-consistent expressions

\begin{split}\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}V_{\text{intra}}(\textbf{k},\textbf{k}^{\prime})&\left[\left(\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}+\frac{2\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}B_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}+\delta\mu\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{4B_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}+\delta\mu^{2}(\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}|^{2})}}\right)\frac{\tanh(\beta\Gamma_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}/2)}{\Gamma_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}}\right.\\
&\left.+\left(\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}-\frac{2\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}B_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}+\delta\mu\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{4B_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}+\delta\mu^{2}(\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}|^{2})}}\right)\frac{\tanh(\beta\gamma_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}/2)}{\gamma_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}}\right]\end{split}

(29)

\begin{split}\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}V_{\text{inter}}(\textbf{k},\textbf{k}^{\prime})&\left[\left(\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}+\frac{2\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}B_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}+\frac{\delta\mu^{2}}{2}\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{4B_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}+\delta\mu^{2}(\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}|^{2})}}\right)\frac{\tanh(\beta\Gamma_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}/2)}{\Gamma_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}}\right.\\
&\left.+\left(\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}-\frac{2\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}B_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}+\frac{\delta\mu^{2}}{2}\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{4B_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}+\delta\mu^{2}(\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}|^{2})}}\right)\frac{\tanh(\beta\gamma_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}/2)}{\gamma_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}}\right],\end{split}

(30)

where were defined

\begin{split}\Gamma_{\textbf{k}}^{2}&=\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}}^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}|^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}|^{2}+\frac{\delta\mu^{2}}{4}\\
&+\sqrt{4B_{\textbf{k}}^{2}+\delta\mu^{2}(\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}}^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}|^{2})},\\
\end{split}

(31)

\begin{split}\gamma_{\textbf{k}}^{2}&=\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}}^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}|^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}|^{2}+\frac{\delta\mu^{2}}{4}\\
&-\sqrt{4B_{\textbf{k}}^{2}+\delta\mu^{2}(\bar{\xi}_{\textbf{k}}^{2}+|\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}|^{2})}.\end{split}

(32)

IV
The Zero Temperature Limit

We shall study the competition between both pairings, and therefore the corresponding superconducting phases in the zero temperature limit
T\rightarrow 0
. For small values of
|\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{\text{intra}}|
and
|\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{\text{inter}}|
, and assuming that the chemical potential is nearly symmetrical at both nodes
|\delta\mu/\overline{\mu}|\ll 1
, the gap equations reduce to

\Delta^{\eta}_{\textbf{k}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}V_{\eta}(\textbf{k},\textbf{k}^{\prime})\Delta^{\eta}_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}}\right),

(33)

where
\eta=\text{intra}/\text{inter}
.

We shall assume the following angular dependence on the interaction potentials
Muñoz et al. 2020
;
Muñoz et al. 2024

\begin{split}V_{\text{intra}}(\textbf{k},\textbf{k}^{\prime})&=V_{0}^{\text{intra}}Y_{l,m^{\prime}}(\theta_{\textbf{k}},\phi_{\textbf{k}})Y_{l,m^{\prime}}^{*}(\theta_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}},\phi_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}})\\
V_{\text{inter}}(\textbf{k},\textbf{k}^{\prime})&=V_{0}^{\text{inter}}\mathcal{Y}_{\nu,j,m}(\theta_{\textbf{k}},\phi_{\textbf{k}})\mathcal{Y}_{\nu,j,m}^{*}(\theta_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}},\phi_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}),\end{split}

(34)

with
Y_{l,m}(\theta_{\textbf{k}},\phi_{\textbf{k}})
and
\mathcal{Y}_{\nu,j,m}(\theta_{\textbf{k}},\phi_{\textbf{k}})
the conventional spherical and monopole harmonics, respectively. These angular dependencies are consequently inherited by the pairing functions,

\begin{split}\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}&=\Delta_{l,m^{\prime}}Y_{l,m^{\prime}}(\theta_{\textbf{k}},\phi_{\textbf{k}})=\Delta_{l,m^{\prime}}f_{\text{intra}}(\theta_{\textbf{k}})e^{im^{\prime}\phi_{\textbf{k}}}\\
\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}&=\bar{\Delta}_{\nu}\mathcal{Y}_{\nu,j,m}(\theta_{\textbf{k}},\phi_{\textbf{k}})=\bar{\Delta}_{\nu}f_{\text{inter}}(\theta_{\textbf{k}})e^{i(m+\nu)\phi_{\textbf{k}}}.\end{split}

(35)

Inserting these expressions into Eq. (
33
), we get

\frac{1}{V_{0}^{\eta}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\textbf{k}}|f_{\eta}(\theta_{\textbf{k}})|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\textbf{k}}}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{\textbf{k}}}\right).

(36)

Going to the continuum limit, and defining the density of states
\rho(\xi)=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(\xi-\xi_{\mathbf{k}})
(details in Appendix
C
), we obtain the expressions

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}|f_{\eta}(\theta_{x})|^{2}

(37)

\displaystyle\times\int_{-\omega_{D}}^{\omega_{D}}d\xi\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\textbf{k}}}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{\textbf{k}}}\right),

where the effective couplings were defined as
\lambda_{\eta}=\rho(0)V_{0}^{\eta}
, with
\rho(0)
the density of states function at the Fermi level. The coefficient
B_{\nu}=\beta(\frac{1}{\nu},\frac{1}{2})/(2\nu)
defined in terms of the Beta function, and
\theta_{x}
is defined according to the expression

\tan\theta_{x}=\frac{cx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}},

(38)

with the constant
c=\alpha^{-1/\nu}(\mu/v_{F})^{1/\nu-1}
.

By further performing the integral in
\xi
, we end up with

\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}}=\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}|f_{\eta}(\theta_{x})|^{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\left[2\ln(2\omega_{D})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s=\pm}\ln(A_{+}+2sB_{\textbf{k}})\right],

(39)

where

A_{\pm}=\Delta_{l,m^{\prime}}^{2}f_{\text{intra}}^{2}(\theta_{x})\pm\bar{\Delta}_{\nu}^{2}f_{\text{inter}}^{2}(\theta_{x}).

(40)

Noting that

\displaystyle 2B_{\textbf{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Delta_{l,m^{\prime}}\bar{\Delta}_{\nu}f_{\text{intra}}(\theta_{x})f_{\text{inter}}(\theta_{x})\cos[(m^{\prime}-m-\nu)\phi]

(41)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle B\cos(r\phi),

the integral in
\phi
must be solved in different ways depending on the cases
m^{\prime}=m+\nu
or
m^{\prime}\neq m+\nu
, respectively.

For Case 1:
m^{\prime}=m+\nu
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\sum_{s=\pm}\ln(A_{+}+2sB_{\textbf{k}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s=\pm}\ln(A_{+}+sB)=\frac{1}{2}\ln(A_{+}^{2}-B^{2})=\ln(|A_{-}|),

(42)

and then we obtain

\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}}=\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}|f_{\eta}(\theta_{x})|^{2}\ln\left[\frac{4\omega_{D}^{2}}{\left|\Delta_{l,m^{\prime}}^{2}f_{\text{intra}}^{2}(\theta_{x})-\bar{\Delta}_{\nu}^{2}f_{\text{inter}}^{2}(\theta_{x})\right|}\right].

(43)

On the other hand, for Case 2:
m^{\prime}\neq m+\nu
, we solve

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\ln\left[A_{+}\pm B\cos(r\phi)\right]&=\sum_{n=1}^{r}\int_{2\pi(n-1)/r}^{2\pi n/r}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\ln[A_{+}\pm B\cos(r\phi)]=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{n=1}^{r}\int_{2\pi(n-1)}^{2\pi n}\frac{d\phi^{\prime}}{2\pi}\ln(A_{+}\pm B\cos\phi^{\prime})\\
=&\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi^{\prime}}{2\pi}\ln(A_{+}\pm B\cos\phi^{\prime})=\ln\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(A_{+}+\sqrt{A_{+}^{2}-B^{2}}\right)\right]=\ln\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(A_{+}+|A_{-}|\right)\right],\end{split}

(44)

and then we obtain

\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}}=\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}|f_{\eta}(\theta_{x})|^{2}\left\{2\ln(2\omega_{D})-\ln\left[\frac{1}{2}(A_{+}+|A_{-}|)\right]\right\}.

(45)

IV.1
Topological Repulsion

Let us now examine whether the mechanism that we call ”topological repulsion”
Muñoz et al. 2020
arises in the case of multi-Weyl semimetals as well, according to our present model.
Consider Case 1:
m^{\prime}=m+\nu
, corresponding to Eq. (
43
), and further assume that
f_{\text{intra}}(\theta_{x})\approx f_{\text{inter}}(\theta_{x})
. Then, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}}=&\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}|f_{\text{intra}}(\theta_{x})|^{2}\\
&\times\ln\left[\frac{4\omega_{D}^{2}}{f_{\text{intra}}^{2}(\theta_{x})\left|\Delta_{l,m^{\prime}}^{2}-\bar{\Delta}_{\nu}^{2}\right|}\right].\end{split}

(46)

This equation is analogous to the one obtained in
Muñoz et al. 2020
, but here we extend it to multi-Weyl semimetals. Since the right-hand side is the same for
\eta={\text{intra}}
and
\eta={\text{inter}}
, we conclude that the phase boundary occurs precisely at
\lambda_{\text{intra}}=\lambda_{\text{inter}}
, thus leaving no room for coexistence in the phase diagram. This is precisely the topological repulsion mechanism already described in
Muñoz et al. 2020
for simple Weyl semimetals.

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}}=\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}|f_{\eta}(\theta_{x})|^{2}\left\{2\ln(2\omega_{D})\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}\right.\\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\left[2\ln(2\omega_{D})-\ln(\Delta_{s}^{2})\right]

\displaystyle 2\ln(2\omega_{D})-\ln(\Delta_{s}^{2}),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{inter}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\sin^{2\nu}\theta_{x}\left[2\ln(2\omega_{D})-\ln(\Delta_{s}^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(c)\left[2\ln(2\omega_{D})-\ln(\Delta_{s}^{2})\right],

where we defined the coefficient;

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(c)=\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\sin^{2\nu}\theta_{x},

(50)

for
\theta_{x}
defined in Eq. (
38
).
Combining Eqs. (
48
) and (
49
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{inter}}}=I_{\nu}(c)\frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{intra}}}.

(51)

This equation defines the boundary across which the s-wave dominates over the monopole in the phase diagram.

For the case
\Delta_{s}\ll\bar{\Delta}_{\nu}
we define
\theta^{\prime}
so that
\sin^{\nu}\theta^{\prime}=\Delta_{s}/\bar{\Delta}_{\nu}
, with
\theta^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
. We also define
x^{\prime}
according to
\theta_{x=x^{\prime}}=\theta^{\prime}
, so that
x^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
. With these definitions, Eq. (
45
) reduces to the expression

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}}=\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx|f_{\eta}(\theta_{x})|^{2}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\left\{2\ln(2\omega_{D})\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}\right.\\
\left.-\ln\left[\frac{\Delta_{s}^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{\sin^{2\nu}\theta_{x}}{\sin^{2\nu}\theta^{\prime}}+\left|1-\frac{\sin^{2\nu}\theta_{x}}{\sin^{2\nu}\theta^{\prime}}\right|\right)\right]\right\}.\end{split}

(52)

To compute the integral in
x
, we split the domain into intervals
x\in[0,x^{\prime}]\cup[x^{\prime},1]
. Therefore, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}}=&\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx|f_{\eta}(\theta_{x})|^{2}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\left[2\ln(2\omega_{D})-\ln(\Delta_{s}^{2})\right]\\

&-\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{x^{\prime}}^{1}\frac{dxx|f_{\eta}(\theta_{x})|^{2}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\ln\left(\sin^{2\nu}\theta_{x}\right).\end{split}

(53)

\int_{x^{\prime}}^{1}=\int_{0}^{1}-\int_{0}^{x^{\prime}}
only in the last integral. Since

can be accurately calculated with the mean value theorem, substituting the factor

to join it with the second integral in (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx|f_{\eta}(\theta_{x})|^{2}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\left[2\ln(2\omega_{D})+\ln\left(\frac{\sin^{2\nu}\theta^{\prime}}{\Delta_{s}^{2}}\right)\right]

where we defined

\displaystyle I_{s\nu}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\ln\left(\sin^{2\nu}\theta_{x}\right)

(56)

\displaystyle I_{\nu\nu}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxx\sin^{2\nu}\theta_{x}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\ln\left(\sin^{2\nu}\theta_{x}\right).

In this section, we shall analyze the critical temperatures and the corresponding critical exponents that determine the two superconducting phases, i.e. conventional and monopole.
Proceeding along the same simplification steps presented in Section
IV
, we can write the system of BCS Equations (
33
) at finite temperature in the form

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{\eta}}{\lambda_{\eta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\eta}\Delta_{\eta}+D_{\eta}\Delta_{\overline{\eta}}

(68)

where for notational convenience we use the indexes
\eta=0\equiv
”intra”,
\eta=1\equiv
”inter”, and we define
\overline{\eta}=1-\eta
. Consequently, we shall index the order parameters as
\Delta_{l,m^{\prime}}\equiv\Delta_{0}
and
\bar{\Delta}_{\nu}\equiv\Delta_{1}
, respectively.
In this more convenient and compact notation, we define the coefficients

\displaystyle C_{\eta}(\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{xf_{\eta}^{2}(\theta_{x})dx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}

(69)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\omega_{D}}d\xi\left[\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\left({\Gamma}\right)+\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\left({\gamma}\right)\right],

along with the function

\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(z)\equiv\frac{\tanh(\beta z/2)}{z}.

(70)

Similarly, for
r\equiv m^{\prime}-m-\nu
, we define the coefficients

\displaystyle D_{\eta}(\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{xf_{\eta}(\theta_{x})f_{\overline{\eta}}(\theta_{x})dx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}e^{\pm ir\phi}

(71)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\omega_{D}}d\xi\left[\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\left({\Gamma}\right)-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\left({\gamma}\right)\right].

where we defined the functions (ignoring any asymmetry in the chemical potential
\delta\mu=0
)

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\xi^{2}+\Delta_{0}^{2}f_{intra}^{2}(\theta_{x})+\Delta_{1}^{2}f_{inter}^{2}(\theta_{x})\right.

(72)

\displaystyle\left.+2\Delta_{0}\Delta_{1}f_{intra}(\theta_{x})f_{inter}(\theta_{x})\cos(r\phi)\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\xi^{2}+\Delta_{0}^{2}f_{intra}^{2}(\theta_{x})+\Delta_{1}^{2}f_{inter}^{2}(\theta_{x})\right.

\displaystyle\left.-2\Delta_{0}\Delta_{1}f_{intra}(\theta_{x})f_{inter}(\theta_{x})\cos(r\phi)\right]^{1/2}

Let us start by considering two critical temperatures
T^{c}_{0}
,
T^{c}_{1}
, each of them associated to the vanishing conditions of the corresponding order parameter:
\Delta_{\eta}(T\rightarrow T^{c-}_{\eta})=0
for
\eta=0,1
(intra/inter).

Without loss of generality, from now on we choose the index
\eta
such that it corresponds to the highest critical temperature, i.e.

T_{\eta}^{c}={\rm{Max}}\left\{T_{0}^{c},T_{1}^{c}\right\},

(73)

and hence
T_{\eta}^{c}>T_{\overline{\eta}}^{c}
, for
\overline{\eta}=1-\eta
. Under this prescription, let us first consider the region
T\lesssim T_{\eta}^{c}
. Then, while
\Delta_{\overline{\eta}}\equiv 0
in this temperature zone, we may cancel the factor
\Delta_{\eta}
on both sides, and afterward take the limit
\lim_{T\rightarrow T_{\eta}^{c-}}\Delta_{\eta}(T)=0
in Eq. (
68
), to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta_{\eta}\rightarrow 0}C_{\eta}\left(\Delta_{\eta},\Delta_{\overline{\eta}}=0\right)=\Lambda_{\eta\overline{\eta}}^{20}\int_{0}^{\frac{\omega_{D}}{2T_{\eta}^{c}}}dz\frac{\tanh(z)}{z}

(74)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\eta\overline{\eta}}^{20}\ln\left(\frac{2}{\pi}e^{\gamma_{E}}\frac{\omega_{D}}{T_{\eta}^{c}}\right),

where
\gamma_{E}
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant, and we defined the angular integrals

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\eta\overline{\eta}}^{nm}\equiv\frac{2}{B_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx\,xf_{\eta}^{n}(\theta_{x})f_{\overline{\eta}}^{m}(\theta_{x})}{\sqrt{1-x^{2\nu}}}.

(75)

Solving explicitly from Eq. (
74
), we obtain the highest critical temperature as

T_{\eta}^{c}=\frac{2\omega_{D}}{\pi}e^{\gamma_{E}}e^{-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}\Lambda_{\eta\overline{\eta}}^{20}}}.

(76)

Based on this first result, we then conclude that the highest critical temperature
T_{\eta}^{c}
will depend, in terms of the microscopic parameters of the model, upon de condition
{\rm{Max}}\left\{\lambda_{1}\Lambda_{10}^{20},\lambda_{0}\Lambda_{10}^{02}\right\}
.

Let us now consider the interval between the two critical temperatures, i.e.
T_{\overline{\eta}}^{c}<T<T_{\eta}^{c}
. Therefore, the condition
\Delta_{\overline{\eta}}(T)\equiv 0
still applies, but
\Delta_{\eta}(T)\neq 0
. We can thus expand the functions in the integrand of Eq. (
69
) up to second-order in
\Delta_{\eta}(T)
, as follows

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\Gamma)+\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\gamma)}{2}\simeq\frac{\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\xi}{2}\right)}{\xi}+\frac{\Delta_{\eta}^{2}f_{\eta}^{2}(\theta_{x})}{16}\psi_{\beta}\left(\frac{\beta\xi}{2}\right),

where we defined the function

\psi_{\beta}(z)=\frac{\beta^{3}}{z^{3}}\left[z\,\rm{sech}^{2}(z)-\tanh(z)\right].

(78)

Substituting these relations into Eq. (
68
), we obtain (for
T_{\overline{\eta}}^{c}<T<T_{\eta}^{c}
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\eta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\eta}\left(\Delta_{\eta},\Delta_{\overline{\eta}}=0\right)

(79)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\eta\overline{\eta}}^{20}\ln\left(\frac{2}{\pi}e^{\gamma_{E}}\frac{\omega_{D}}{T}\right)-\alpha\Lambda_{\eta\overline{\eta}}^{40}\frac{\Delta_{\eta}^{2}(T)}{T^{2}},

with
\alpha=7\zeta(3)/(8\pi^{2})\sim 0.10657
and
\zeta(3)\sim 1.202
the Riemann zeta function.
Substituting the definition of the highest critical temperature from Eq. (
74
) into Eq.(
79
), we obtain the temperature dependence of the corresponding order parameter

\displaystyle\Delta_{\eta}^{2}(T)=\frac{\Lambda_{\eta\overline{\eta}}^{20}}{\Lambda_{\eta\overline{\eta}}^{40}}\frac{T^{2}}{\alpha}\ln\left(\frac{T_{\eta}^{c}}{T}\right)\sim\kappa_{\eta}\left|T_{\eta}^{c}-T\right|,

(80)

with

\kappa_{\eta}=\frac{\Lambda_{\eta\overline{\eta}}^{20}}{\Lambda_{\eta\overline{\eta}}^{40}}\frac{T^{c}_{\eta}}{\alpha}
. This clearly shows that the corresponding critical exponent is
1/2
, just as in the standard BCS theory.

In order to investigate the lowest critical temperature
T_{\overline{\eta}}^{c}
, let us now consider the second equation in the system Eq. (
68
) corresponding to
\overline{\eta}
, and take the limit
T\rightarrow T_{\overline{\eta}}^{c-}
, where

Specific Heat

In cases where both superconducting phases coexist, we expect two superconducting phase transitions to exist at the corresponding critical temperatures, which are likely to be different. As a possible experimental probe for these transitions, we seek to calculate the specific heat of the system as a function of temperature, since we expect a discontinuous jump in the specific heat at each critical point.

For this purpose, we first calculate the internal energy of the system from the grand canonical partition function, according to the general expression

\langle\hat{H}\rangle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln\mathcal{Z}+\frac{\mu}{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\ln\mathcal{Z}.

(93)

The contribution to the partition function associated with the superconducting phases is calculated from the finite-temperature Green’s function of our effective model in the Matsubara frequency space.

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{Z}^{SC}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\left(\det[\hat{\mathcal{G}}^{-1}_{\textbf{k}}(\omega_{n})]\right)=-\text{Tr}\left[\ln\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\textbf{k}}(\omega_{n})\right]

(94)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\textbf{k},\omega_{n}}\ln\left[(\omega_{n}^{2}+\Gamma^{2}_{\textbf{k}})(\omega_{n}^{2}+\gamma^{2}_{\textbf{k}})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\textbf{k}}\ln\left[(1+\cosh(\beta\Gamma_{\textbf{k}}))(1+\cosh(\beta\gamma_{\textbf{k}}))\right].

Then, the specific heat associated with each of the superconducting transitions is calculated as

C_{v}^{SC}=\beta^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\beta^{2}}\ln\mathcal{Z}^{SC}-\beta^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\left(\frac{\mu}{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\ln\mathcal{Z}^{SC}\right).

(95)

Here we must consider the temperature dependence of the gap functions to calculate the derivatives. In agreement with
Muñoz et al. 2024
, we have

|\Delta^{\eta}|\sim|T-T_{c}^{\eta}|^{1/2},

(96)

for both
\eta=\text{intra/inter}
,
T<T_{c}^{\eta}
and for a simple WSM with isotropic dispersion relation. Here we will use the same relation, because the development carried out in
Muñoz et al. 2024
can be generalized to multi-WSM obtaining the same critical exponents.

On the other hand, we calculate the specific heat component due to fermions in the normal WSM phase, with energy dispersion
\xi_{\textbf{k}}=v_{f}\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}+\alpha^{2}k_{\perp}^{2\nu}}
, as follows

C_{v}^{\text{norm}}=\beta^{2}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{(\xi_{\textbf{k}}-\mu)^{2}e^{-\beta(\xi_{\textbf{k}}-\mu)}}{(1+e^{-\beta(\xi_{\textbf{k}}-\mu)})^{2}}.

(97)

This integral is solved by means of the change of variables

\displaystyle k_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{\alpha_{\nu}}\sin\theta\right)^{1/\nu}\cos\phi,

\displaystyle k_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{\alpha_{\nu}}\sin\theta\right)^{1/\nu}\sin\phi,

(98)

\displaystyle k_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\cos\theta,

with which we have
\xi_{\textbf{k}}=v_{f}r
and the Jacobian

\left|\frac{\partial(k_{x},k_{y},k_{z})}{\partial(r,\theta,\phi)}\right|=\frac{1}{\nu}\left(\frac{r}{\alpha_{\nu}}\right)^{2/\nu}\sin^{2/\nu-1}\theta.

(99)

Then, in the limit
\mu\rightarrow 0
we obtain

C_{v}^{\text{norm}}=\frac{\beta(\frac{1}{\nu},\frac{1}{2})}{(2\pi)^{2}\nu\alpha_{\nu}^{2/\nu}}\left(\frac{T}{v_{f}}\right)^{\frac{2}{\nu}+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\frac{x^{2+2/\nu}e^{-x}}{(1+e^{-x})^{2}}.

(100)

In Figure
5
we plot the total specific heat
C_{v}^{\text{norm}}+C_{v}^{SC}
. As expected, the specific heat displays discontinuities precisely at the critical temperatures corresponding to each monopole and conventional superconducting phases, respectively. In addition, as seen in Fig.
5
, the curves display different slopes depending on the chiral index
\nu=1,2,3
, which may offer an experimental probe to characterize these topological materials by measuring their specific heat.

VII
Discussion and Conclusions

In this work, we presented a theoretical model to explore the possible emergence and competition of conventional and monopole superconducting phases in multi-WSMs, generalizing the previous analysis presented in
Muñoz et al. 2020
;
Muñoz et al. 2024
. We explicitly showed that the order parameters associated to intra-nodal and inter-nodal pairing, exhibit conventional spherical harmonics
\Delta^{\text{intra}}_{\textbf{k}}=\Delta_{l,m^{\prime}}Y_{l,m^{\prime}}(\theta_{\textbf{k}},\phi_{\textbf{k}})=\Delta_{l,m^{\prime}}f_{\text{intra}}(\theta_{\textbf{k}})e^{im^{\prime}\phi_{\textbf{k}}}
, and monopole harmonics
\Delta^{\text{inter}}_{\textbf{k}}=\bar{\Delta}_{\nu}\mathcal{Y}_{\nu,j,m}(\theta_{\textbf{k}},\phi_{\textbf{k}})=\bar{\Delta}_{\nu}f_{\text{inter}}(\theta_{\textbf{k}})e^{i(m+\nu)\phi_{\textbf{k}}}
angular dependencies, respectively.
According to our model, we have shown that, depending on the nature of the
\theta
-component of the spherical harmonics of the order parameters defined above, it is possible to observe both a conventional and a monopole superconducting phases, separated by a region of coexistence or mixed state. However, we also showed that if the azimuthal indexes of the conventional (
m^{\prime}
) and monopole (
m
) satisfy the relation
m^{\prime}=m+\nu
, and furthermore the
f_{intra}(\theta)\simeq f_{inter}(\theta)
, then topological repulsion takes place and hence the coexistence region between both phases disappears, in agreement with
Muñoz et al. 2020
.

We further computed the specific heat as a function of temperature, and we showed that according to our model this property displays discontinuities at the critical temperatures corresponding to each monopole and conventional superconducting phases, respectively. As discussed in Section
III
, the critical temperatures for both possible superconducting phases depend explicitly on the topological charge
\nu
, as well as on the microscopic parameter
c
.
Moreover, we also showed that the slope of the specific heat curve as a function of temperature strongly depends on the chirality index
\nu=1,2,3
. Both features suggest that, according to our model, the specific heat offers a possible experimental probe to characterize the topological properties of these quantum materials.

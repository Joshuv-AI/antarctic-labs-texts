The semi-classical Weyl law on complete manifolds
Maxim Braverman

Abstract
. We prove that the semi-classical Schrödinger operator with growing potential on a complete Riemannian manifold satisfies the Weyl law.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35P20

H. Weyl,
[
15
]
, established a remarkable asymptotic of the number of large eigenvalues of a Dirichlet Laplacian on a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is now called the Weyl law. This result was generalized and refined in many ways, see
[
8
]
for a review. In particular, a Weyl law for a Schrödinger operator on compact manifolds was established. Another version is the semi-classical Weyl law for the semi-classical Schrödinger operator on a compact manifold (see Section
1
for details).

The Weyl law was established for Schrödinger operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with some conditions on the growing rate of the potential,
[
13
,
14
,
9
]
. There are also some results for asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds
[
1
,
11
]
, asymptotically Euclidean manifolds
[
5
]
, and manifolds with cylindrical ends
[
10
]
.

In a recent paper,
[
7
]
, X. Dai and J. Yan established both classical and semi-classical versions of the Weyl law for Schrödinger operators on manifolds of bounded geometry with some conditions on the growth of the potential. Their method was to obtain an asymptotic expansion of the heat kernel, which is a much stronger result than the Weyl law.

In this note, we establish the semi-classical Weyl law on an arbitrary complete Riemannian manifold without any restrictions on the potential (except that it grows at infinity). We achieve it by comparing the semi-classical spectral counting functions on the complete manifold and on a certain compact manifold. This is done using the IMS localization formula
[
6
]
*§3.1 by a method similar to what we used in
[
2
]
*§4 and
[
4
]
.

We should note that the classical Weyl law in non-compact setting,
[
7
]
*Th. 1.8, is more subtle and is not approachable by our methods.

1.
The semi-classical Weyl law

Let
M
be a complete
n
-dimensional manifold. We denote by
\Delta
the positive Laplacian on
M
. Let
V:M\to[0,\infty)
be a smooth function which tends to plus infinity as
x\to\infty
. Let

(1)

H_{\hbar}\ :=\ \hbar^{2}\Delta\ +\ V,

be the
semi-classical Schrödinger operators on
M
. Then
H_{\hbar}
is essentially self-adjoint, non-negative, and has a discrete spectrum.

For a self-adjoint operator
A
with discrete spectrum, we define its
spectral counting function

N(A,\lambda)
as the number of eigenvalues of
A
which are smaller than
\lambda
. In general,
N(A,\lambda)
can be infinite, but our assumptions on
H_{\hbar}
guarantee that its spectral counting function is finite.

Our main result is the following

Theorem 2
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(2)

{(2\pi\hbar)}^{n}\,N(H_{\hbar},\lambda)\ =\ {\rm Vol}\big\{\,(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M:\,|\xi|^{2}+V(x)\leq\lambda\,\big\}\ +\ o(1),

as
\hbar\to 0
.

The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of this theorem, which is based on comparing the eigenvalue counting function on
M
and on a certain compact manifold.

3.
The relative semi-classical Weyl law

Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be complete (possibly compact)
n
-dimensional manifolds.
Let
V_{j}\in[0,\infty)
be smooth functions. If
M_{j}
is non-compact, we assume that
V(x)
tends to plus infinity as
x\to\infty
. The equation (
1
) defines the semi-classical Schrödinger operators
H_{\hbar,j}
on
M_{j}
.

Definition 4
.

Fix a real number
\lambda>0
. We say that the pairs
(M_{1},V_{1})
and
(M_{2},V_{2})
are
\lambda
-equivalent
if there exist a relatively compact open subsets
U_{j}\subset M_{j}
and an isometric diffeomorphism
\Phi:U_{1}\to U_{2}
such that

(i)

V_{j}^{-1}\big([0,\lambda]\big)\subset U_{j}
;

(ii)

V_{2}\big(\Phi(x)\big)\ =\ V_{1}(x)
, for all
x\in U_{1}
.

We have the following
relative
version of the semi-classical Weyl’s law:

Proposition 5
.

If
(M_{1},V_{1})
and
(M_{2},V_{2})
are
\lambda
–equivalent, then there exists a constant
c>0
(which depends on
\lambda
and all the other data, but is independent of
\hbar
) such that

(3)

N(H_{\hbar,1},\lambda)\ \geq\ N(H_{\hbar,2},\lambda-c\hbar^{2}).

Before proving the proposition, we show how it implies the semi-classical Weyl law.

6.
The proof of the semi-classical Weyl law

Fix
\lambda
and and an open relatively compact set
U\subset M
which contains the set
\big\{\,(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M:\,|\xi|^{2}+V(x)\leq\lambda\,\big\}
. Let
\overline{M}
be any compact Riemannian manifold that contains
U
. Let
\overline{V}:\overline{M}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function whose restriction to
U
equals
V
and whose restriction to
\overline{M}\backslash U
is bigger than
\lambda
. Then
(M,V)
and
(\overline{M},\overline{V})
are
\lambda
-equivalent. We denote by
\overline{H}_{\hbar}
the semi-classical Schrödinger operator on
\overline{M}
:

\overline{H}_{\hbar}\ :=\ \hbar^{2}\,\Delta+\overline{V}.

It is a classical result that on compact manifolds, the eigenvalue counting function satisfies the semi-classical Weyl law, cf.
[
16
]
*Th. 6.8. It follows that

(4)

(2\pi\hbar)^{n}\,N(\overline{H}_{\hbar},\lambda)\ =\ {\rm Vol}\big\{\,(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M:\,|\xi|^{2}+V(x)\leq\lambda\,\big\}+o(1)\\
=\ (2\pi\hbar)^{n}\,N_{h}(\overline{V},\lambda-c\hbar^{2})+o(1).

Setting
M_{1}=M
,
M_{2}=\overline{M}
in (
3
) and using (
4
) we now obtain

(5)

(2\pi\hbar)^{n}\,N(H_{\hbar},\lambda)\ \geq\ (2\pi\hbar)^{n}\,N(\overline{H}_{\hbar},\lambda-c\hbar^{2})\ =\ {\rm Vol}\big\{\,(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M:\,|\xi|^{2}+V(x)\leq\lambda\,\big\}+o(1).

For any
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
V^{-1}\big([0,\lambda+\delta]\big)\subset U
and

(6)

{\rm Vol}\big\{\,(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M:\,\lambda\leq|\xi|^{2}+V(x)\leq\lambda+\delta\,\big\}\ <\ \epsilon.

Setting
M_{1}=\overline{M}
and
M_{2}=M
and replacing
\lambda
with
\lambda+\delta
in Proposition
5
, we conclude that there exists a constant
c^{\prime}>0
such that

N(\overline{H}_{\hbar},\lambda+\delta)\ \geq\ N(H_{\hbar},\lambda+\delta-c^{\prime}\hbar^{2}).

Hence, using (
4
) and (
6
), we have

(7)

N(\overline{H}_{\hbar},\lambda)+\frac{\epsilon}{(2\pi\hbar)^{n}}\ >\ N(\overline{H}_{\hbar},\lambda+\delta)\ \geq\ N(H_{\hbar},\lambda+\delta-c^{\prime}\hbar^{2}).

If
c^{\prime}\hbar^{2}<\delta
then
N(H_{\hbar},\lambda+\delta-c^{\prime}\hbar^{2})\geq N(H_{\hbar},\lambda)
. Thus (
7
) implies that for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\hbar_{\epsilon}>0
such that for all
\hbar<\epsilon
we have

(2\pi\hbar)^{n}\,N(H_{\hbar},\lambda)\ \leq\ (2\pi\hbar)^{n}\,N(\overline{H}_{\hbar},\lambda)+\epsilon.

It follows that

(2\pi\hbar)^{n}\,N(H_{\hbar},\lambda)\ \leq\ (2\pi\hbar)^{n}\,N(\overline{H}_{\hbar},\lambda)+o(1)\ =\ {\rm Vol}\big\{\,(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M:\,|\xi|^{2}+V(x)\leq\lambda\,\big\}+o(1).

Together with (
5
) this proves the theorem.

\square

To finish the proof of Theorem
2
it remains to prove Proposition
5
. We do it using a very useful formula called Ismagilov–Morgan–Simon (IMS) localization, cf.
[
6
]
*§3.1.

7.
Ismagilov–Morgan–Simon (IMS) localization formula

We use the following IMS localization formula, cf.
[
6
]
*§3.1).

Lemma 8
.

Suppose
\phi
and
\psi
are smooth functions with
\psi^{2}+\phi^{2}=1
.
Then the following operator identity holds

(8)

H=\phi H\phi+\psi H\psi+\frac{1}{2}[\phi,[\phi,H]]+\frac{1}{2}[\psi,[\psi,H]].

The proof is given in
[
6
]
*§3.1 (see also
[
2
]
*Lemma 4.10 or
[
3
]
*Lemma 8.2).
Note that if
H
is the semi-classical Schrödinger operator (
1
), then

(9)

\frac{1}{2}\,[\phi,[\phi,H]]\ =\ -\hbar^{2}\,|d\phi|^{2},\qquad\frac{1}{2}\,[\psi,[\psi,H]]\ =\ -\hbar^{2}\,|d\psi|^{2}.

9.
Estimates on
N(H_{\hbar,j},\lambda)

We use the notation of Section
3
.
For
j=1,2
, choose functions
\phi_{j},\,\psi_{j}:M_{j}\to[0,1]
such that

(i)

\phi_{j}^{2}+\psi_{j}^{2}=1
;

(ii)

\operatorname{supp}\phi_{j}\in U_{j}
;

(iii)

\operatorname{supp}\psi_{j}\cap V_{j}^{-1}\big([0,\lambda]\big)=\emptyset
,

(iv)

\phi_{1}(x)=\phi_{2}(\Phi(x))
for every
x\in U_{1}
.

Then

(10)

\psi_{j}\,H_{\hbar,j}\,\psi_{j}\ >\ \psi_{j}^{2}\lambda.

From (
9
) we conclude that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(11)

\big|\frac{1}{2}\,[\phi_{j},[\phi_{j},H_{\hbar,j}]]\big|\ <\ \frac{c}{2}\hbar^{2},\qquad\big|\frac{1}{2}\,[\psi_{j},[\psi_{j},H_{\hbar,j}]]\big|\ <\ \frac{c}{2}\hbar^{2}.

Finally, let
P_{\hbar,j}
denote the orthogonal projection onto the span of the eigenfunctions of
H_{\hbar,j}
with eigenvalues less than
\lambda
. By definition,
\operatorname{rank}(P_{\hbar,j})=N(H_{\hbar,j},\lambda)
. Then

(12)

H_{\hbar,j}+\lambda P_{\hbar,j}\ \geq\ \lambda\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\phi_{1}\,\big(\,H_{\hbar,j}+\lambda P_{\hbar,j}\,\big)\,\phi_{1}\ \geq\ \lambda\,\phi_{1}^{2}.

Using the diffeomorphism
\Phi:U_{1}\to U_{2}
we can view
\phi_{1}P_{\hbar,1}\phi_{1}
as an operator acting on functions on
M_{2}
. With a slight abuse of notation, we occasionally denote the same operator by
\phi_{2}P_{\hbar,1}\phi_{2}
.
Then the operator

\phi_{2}H_{\hbar,2}\phi_{2}+\lambda\phi_{2}P_{\hbar,1}\phi_{2}

can be identified with
\phi_{1}\big(\,H_{\hbar,1}+\lambda P_{\hbar,1}\big)\,\phi_{1}
. It now follows from
(
12
) that

(13)

\phi_{2}H_{\hbar,2}\phi_{2}+\lambda\phi_{2}P_{\hbar,1}\phi_{2}\ \ \geq\ \lambda\phi_{2}^{2}.

Combining (
8
), (
10
), (
9
), and (
13
), we obtain

(14)

H_{\hbar,2}+\phi_{2}P_{\hbar,1}\phi_{2}\ \geq\ \lambda-c\hbar^{2}.

Proposition
5
follows now from the following general lemma (cf.
[
12
]
*p. 270):

Lemma 10
.

Assume that
A,B
are self-adjoint operators in a Hilbert space

{H}
such that
\operatorname{rank}(B)\leq k
and there exists
\mu>0

such that

\langle(A+B)u,u\rangle\geq\mu\langle u,u\rangle\quad\text{for any}\quad u\in\operatorname{Dom}(A).

Then
N(A,\mu)\leq k
.

To finish the proof of the proposition, we simply set
B:=\lambda\phi_{2}P_{\hbar,1}\phi_{2}
and observe that

\operatorname{rank}\big(\phi_{2}P_{\hbar,1}\phi_{2})\ \leq\ \operatorname{rank}(P_{\hbar,1})\ =\ N(H_{\hbar,1},\lambda)

and set
\mu=\lambda-c\hbar^{2}
in Lemma
10
.
\square

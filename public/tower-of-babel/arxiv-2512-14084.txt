On twisting functions in twisted cartesian products and twisted tensor products
Li Cai

Abstract
. For a given twisted cartesian products of simplicial sets, we construct the corresponding twisted tensor product in the sense of Brown, with an explicit twisting function whose formula is simple without using inductions. This is done by choosing an explicit morphism of topological monoids from Kan’s loop group to Moore loop spaces, following Berger’s work on simplicial prisms. We follow the choice of Brown and Berger on such a morphism, which is different from that of Gugenheim and Szczarba.

2.
The Simplicial Category
\Gamma X

Let
X=(X_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a simplicial set. Throughout this work, we frequently identify an
n
-simplex
x_{n}\in X_{n}
with the singular simplex

|x_{n}|:|\Delta^{n}|\to|X|

on the geometric realization
|X|
,
which is induced from the simplicial map
x_{n}:\Delta^{n}\to X
given by the Yoneda lemma, here
\Delta^{n}=(\Delta^{n}_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
is the simplicial set with
\Delta^{n}_{k}
collecting all increasing maps
[k]\to[n]
. As a singular simplex with barycentric coordinates
(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n})
, we have the face and degeneracy maps
d_{i}:X_{n}\to X_{n-1}
and
s_{i}:X_{n}\to X_{n+1}
given by

\displaystyle|d_{i}x_{n}|(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1})

\displaystyle=|x_{n}|(t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-1},0,t_{i},\ldots,t_{n-1}),

(2)

\displaystyle|s_{i}x_{n}|(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n+1})

\displaystyle=|x_{n}|(t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-1},t_{i}+t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n+1}),

i=0,\ldots,n
. Let
\Gamma X=(\Gamma_{n}X)_{n=0}^{\infty}
be the simplicial object in which each
\Gamma_{n}X
is a small category,
n\geq 0
, whose face and degeneracy maps

are functors between them. More precisely,
Obj(\Gamma_{n}X)=X_{n}
coincides with

, and

is generated by

X_{n+1}\times[n]\times\{-1,1\}=\{(x_{n+1},i),(x_{n+1},i)^{-1}\mid x_{n+1}\in X_{n+1},i=0,\ldots,n\},

in which an element is called an
elementary prism
, where
x_{n},x_{n}^{\prime}\in X_{n}
is connected by
(x_{n+1},i):x_{n}\to x_{n}^{\prime}
if and only if

d_{i+1}x_{n+1}=x_{n}
and
d_{i}x_{n+1}=x_{n}^{\prime}
,
i=0,\ldots,n
. As a singular
(n+1)
-simplex,
|(x_{n+1},i)|
coincides with the Moore paths parametrized by
d_{i+1}x_{n+1}
, which is explicitly given by the formula

(3)

|(x_{n+1},i)|(t,t_{0},\ldots,t_{n})=|x_{n+1}|(t_{0},t_{1},\cdots,t_{i-1},t_{i}-t,t,t_{i+1},\cdots,t_{n})

here
t\in[0,t_{i}]
,
t_{i}=1-\sum_{j\not=i}t_{j}
depending on the coordinates
t_{j}
,
j\not=i
.

Notice that for every
x_{n}\in X_{n}=Obj(\Gamma_{n}X)
, by (
2

|(s_{i}x_{n},i)|(t,t_{0},\ldots,t_{n})=|s_{n}x_{n}|(t_{0},t_{1},\cdots,t_{i-1},t_{i}-t,t,t_{i+1},\cdots,t_{n})=|x_{n}|(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}),

hence
(s_{i}x_{n},i)=\mathrm{id}_{x_{n}}
,
i=0,\ldots,n
.
We define the elementary prism
(x_{n+1},i)^{-1}:d_{i}x_{n+1}\to d_{i+1}x_{n+1}
by

(4)

|(x_{n+1},i)^{-1}|(t,t_{0},\ldots,t_{n})=|x_{n+1}|(t_{0},t_{1},\cdots,t_{i-1},t,t_{i}-t,t_{i+1},\cdots,t_{n}).

A general element in
Mor(\Gamma_{n}X)
, called an
n
-
prism
, is a composition of elementary ones, whenever possible. It is convenient to denote a composition

(x_{n+1}^{\prime},i^{\prime})^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\circ(x_{n+1},i)^{\varepsilon}\in Mor(\Gamma_{n}X),\quad\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\{1,-1\},

of two elementary prisms as the product

(x_{n+1},i)^{\varepsilon}(x_{n+1}^{\prime},i^{\prime})^{\varepsilon^{\prime}},

which is understood as the concatenation of two path segments

x_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x_{n+1},i)^{\varepsilon}}}{{\longrightarrow}}x_{n}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x_{n+1}^{\prime},i^{\prime})^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}x_{n}^{\prime\prime}

parametrized by elements in

.
In this way we write a general

-prism
\gamma\in Mor(\Gamma_{n}X)
in the form

(5)

\gamma_{s}=\prod_{k=1}^{l}(\xi_{k},i_{k})^{\varepsilon_{k}},\quad\varepsilon_{k}=\pm 1,

in which the target of
(\xi_{k},i_{k})^{\varepsilon_{k}}
is the source of
(\xi_{k+1},i_{k+1})^{\varepsilon_{k+1}}
,

.

The face and degeneracy functors
d_{j}:\Gamma_{n}X\to\Gamma_{n-1}X
,
s_{j}:\Gamma_{n}X\to\Gamma_{n+1}X
are defined through their operations on the parameters, namely

\displaystyle|d_{j}(x_{n+1},i)|(t,t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1})

\displaystyle=|(x_{n+1},i)|(t,t_{0},\ldots,t_{j-1},0,t_{j+1},\ldots,t_{n-1})

\displaystyle|s_{j}(x_{n+1},i)|(t,t_{0},\ldots,t_{n+1})

\displaystyle=|(x_{n+1},i)|(t,t_{0},\ldots,t_{j-1},t_{j}+t_{j+1},t_{j+2},\ldots,t_{n+1}).

It can be checked straightforwardly that we have commutative diagrams

(6)

\begin{CD}x_{n}@>{(x_{n+1},i)}>{}>x_{n}^{\prime}\\
@V{d_{j}}V{}V@V{d_{j}}V{}V\\
d_{j}x_{n}@>{d_{j}(x_{n+1},i)}>{}>d_{j}x_{n}^{\prime},\end{CD}\quad\quad\begin{CD}x_{n}@>{(x_{n+1},i)}>{}>x_{n}^{\prime}\\
@V{s_{j}}V{}V@V{s_{j}}V{}V\\

j=0,\ldots,n
, hence the face and degeneracy functors satisfy necessary axioms so that
\Gamma X
is a simplicial object. Moreover, the collection
Mor(\Gamma X)=(Mor(\Gamma_{n}X))_{n=0}^{\infty}
of prisms is closed under face and degeneracy maps, thus it is a simplicial set itself.

4.
Kan’s groups and twisting functions

Let

A

5.1.

We say that a

n
-cell

Our main theorem is the following, whose proof will be given at the end of this section. A similar construction has been obtained in a recent work

Let

We use an induction on

to show a), b). Notice that

Suppose that (

6.1
, for any given

\displaystyle\Psi d(x\otimes z)

\displaystyle=\Psi\left((dx\otimes z+(-1)^{n}x\otimes dz+(-1)^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}[0,\ldots,i]_{x}\otimes\phi[i,\ldots,n]_{x}\cdot z\right)

\displaystyle=d\left(\Psi(x\otimes 1)\cdot z\right)=d\Psi(x\otimes z),

.
Now let

\displaystyle d(x\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(d_{i}x)\otimes 1+(-1)^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}[0,\ldots,i]_{x}\otimes\phi[i,\ldots,n]_{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(d_{i}x\otimes 1)+(-1)^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}([0,\ldots,i]_{x}\otimes 1)\cdot\phi[i,\ldots,n]_{x}

since

, where
d_{i}x
,
[0,\ldots,i]_{x}
with

. Together with (

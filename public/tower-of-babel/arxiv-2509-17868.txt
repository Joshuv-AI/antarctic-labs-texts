Polynomial actions of rings of integers of global fields and quasirandomness of Paley-type graphs
Ethan Ackelsberg, Vitaly Bergelson

Abstract
. The goal of this paper is to undertake an in-depth study of the general phenomenon behind the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem, which, in its modern form due to Kamae and Mendès-France [ km ] , states that if E E is a subset of the integers with positive density and P ⁡ ( x ) ∈ ℤ ⁡ [ x ] P(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x] is an intersective polynomial (i.e., has a root mod N N for every N ∈ ℕ N\in\mathbb{N} ), then there are distinct elements x , y ∈ E x,y\in E such that x − y = P ⁡ ( n ) x-y=P(n) for some some n ∈ ℤ n\in\mathbb{Z} . In this paper, we identify a natural algebraic framework (rings of integers of global fields) for Furstenberg–Sárközy-type theorems. One of our main results establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for a polynomial to satisfy the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem over the ring of integers of a global field, providing an extension of the result of Kamae and Mendès-France and including (in an amplified form) results from [ br , bl ] as rather special cases. The Furstenberg–Sárközy phenomenon goes beyond infinite rings and has interesting additional aspects when considered from an asymptotic point of view for families of finite rings. As an example, classical exponential sum estimates can be used to show that large subsets of finite fields contain the asymptotically (as the size of the field grows) “correct” number of pairs ( x , y ) (x,y) whose difference y − x y-x is a square. In [ ab_Sarkozy ] , the class of polynomials satisfying this strong form of the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem over finite fields was fully classified. In the present paper, we establish asymptotic results characterizing sequences of finite principal ideal rings that produce “correct” statistics in the Furstenberg–Sáközy theorem and show that these families are much more general than the family of finite fields. As an application of our enhanced forms of the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem over finite rings, we produce new families of examples of quasirandom graphs of algebraic origin, which can be viewed as a far-reaching generalization of the classical family of Paley graphs. The production of these new examples hinges on a two-way connection between asymptotic total ergodicity —the phenomenon responsible for enhanced versions of the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem over finite fields and rings—and quasirandomness.

1.
Introduction

The main results obtained in this paper belong to the junction of three areas: number theory in global fields, ergodic theory, and the theory of quasirandomness of graphs.
The motivation for our work comes from the desire to better understand the general phenomenon which is epitomized by the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem, stating that every set of positive density in the integers contains a square difference.
(More generally, one can obtain values of any so-called
intersective
polynomial as a difference of elements of an arbtrary set of positive density in
\mathbb{Z}
; see Section
4
for a precise formulation.)
One of our results extends and unifies variants of the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem previously obtained in
[
bl
]
and
[
br
]
and which deal with large subsets of the polynomial ring over a finite field and of rings of integers of number fields respectively.
The unifying framework is that of rings of integers of global fields, which we discuss below in Section
3
.

The phenomenon behind the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem also has a manifestation in large classes of finite rings, including finite fields as well as modular rings
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, Galois rings, pseudo-Galois rings, and chain rings, among others.
Furstenberg–Sárközy-type results in finite rings have an asymptotic nature and can be separated into two differently-behaving regimes of high characteristic (characteristic growing to infinity in a sequence of rings) and low characteristic (characteristic bounded while the size of the ring grows to infinity).

The high characteristic regime was treated in
[
bb1
,
bb2
]
, where it was demonstrated that Furstenberg–Sárközy-type theorems in finite rings are tightly connected with the idea of
asymptotic total ergodicity
.
Moreover, asymptotic total ergodicity allows one to circumvent “local obstructions” that are unavoidable in the setting of the integers and more general families of infinite rings.
As a consequence, it was shown in
[
bb1
,
bb2
]
that one obtains the asymptotically “correct statistics” for polynomial configurations (involving, unlike the case of
\mathbb{Z}
, not necessarily intersective polynomials) in large subsets of finite rings (of high characteristic) when the sequence of rings under consideration is asymptotically totally ergodic.

What follows is a description of the main achievements of this paper.

First, we identify an algebraic framework (rings of integers of global fields) that unifies previous Furstenberg–Sárközy-type theorems into a single theorem, which has amplified versions of results from
[
br
,
bl
]
as rather special cases and answers a question of Lê, Liu, and Wooley
[
llw
]
.

Second, by considering quotients of rings of integers or global fields, we obtain a rich class of finite rings that includes all of the examples handled in
[
bb1
,
bb2
]
as well as several additional interesting sequences of rings in the low characteristic regime.
The algebraic structure of these rings is such that we are able to extend the notion of asymptotic total ergodicity to be applicable also in the low characteristic regime, and this expanded notion of asymptotic total ergodicity appropriately captures the statistical behavior of polynomial configurations in subsets of finite rings in both high and low characteristic.
This can be seen as a completion of the program set out in
[
bb1
]
.

Finally, we establish in this paper a two-way connection between asymptotic total ergodicity and quasirandomness of certain graphs associated to polynomials over finite rings.
The original Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem came from Lovász’s conjecture motivated by the properties of the classical Paley graphs (to be defined in Section
), which are well known to be quasirandom.
We will show that the key property of finite fields (over which Paley graphs are defined) responsible for generating quasirandomness is their asymptotic total ergodicity.
By connecting quasirandomness with asymptotic total ergodicity, we identify the algebraic and dynamical underpinnings for the quasirandomness of Paley graphs and are thus able to produce new families of examples of quasirandom graphs associated to polynomials over more general finite rings.

The structure of the paper is as follows.
In Section

After illuminating in Section

The remainder of the main results appear in Section

Included therein are new Furstenberg–Sárközy-type theorem for finite principal ideal rings and a two-way connection between asymptotic total ergodicity and quasirandomness of Paley-type graphs.

2.
A disclaimer on our use of the term “characteristic”

One of the goals of this paper is to produce strong combinatorial results about polynomial patterns in large subsets of rings of integers of global fields and, as a finitary counterpart, in large subsets of finite principal ideal rings.
When working with polynomial expressions, the characteristic of the underlying ring is an important parameter to track.
In particular, whether or not the degree of a polynomial is smaller than the characteristic of the ring plays a crucial role.
In order to sensibly talk about the “high” and “low” characteristic settings for infinite rings, we use the slightly unusual convention that the characteristic of a ring is always equal to the additive order of the multiplicative identity element
1
, meaning, for example, that the characteristic of the integers is infinite (rather than the more typical convention that
\textup{char}(\mathbb{Z})=0
).

3.
Rings of integers of global fields and their quotients

As we have already stated, a natural setup for treating Furstenberg–Sárközy-type results is the context of rings of integers of global fields.
In this section, we discuss global fields and their properties that will be utilized in our later combinatorial results.

A
global field
is a field of one of two types:

•

a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
(
algebraic number field
)

•

a finite extension of the field of rational functions
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
(
global function field
).

Global fields are important objects in algebraic number theory and are often studied in conjunction with their completions, known as
local fields
.
We do not deal with local fields directly in this paper, as the family of global fields are sufficiently rich on their own for the combinatorial applications we have in mind; see Lemma
3.6
below.

Given a global field
K
, the
ring of integers

\mathcal{O}_{K}
is the integral closure of
\mathbb{Z}
(in the case that
K
is an algebraic number field) or
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
(if
K
is a global function field) inside of
K
.
(Recall that the
integral closure
of a ring
R
inside of another ring
S
is the set of elements
s\in S
that are obtained as roots of monic polynomials with coefficients coming from
R
.)
Our main object of study is the Furstenberg–Sárközy phenomenon in rings of integers of global fields and their (finite) quotients.
In the following proposition, we collect several basic properties of rings of integers of global fields.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
K
be a global field and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
its ring of integers.

(1)

\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a Dedekind domain: every nontrivial ideal
\{0\}\lneq I\lneq\mathcal{O}_{K}
factors (uniquely, up to permutation) as a product of prime ideals;

(2)

Every nonzero ideal
\{0\}\neq I\leq\mathcal{O}_{K}
has finite index in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
;

(3)

The characteristic of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is either infinite (if
K
is an algebraic number field) or a prime number (if
K
is a global function field).

Proof.

Property (1) follows from
[
milne
, Theorem 3.29]
.

Property (2) is classical and can be found, e.g., in
[
janusz
, Section I.8]
.

(3) If
K
is an algebraic number field, then
\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
, so
\mathcal{O}_{K}
has characteristic infinity.
Similarly, if
K
is a global function field, then
\mathcal{O}_{K}
contains
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
for some finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, so
\textup{char}(\mathcal{O}_{K})=\textup{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is prime.
∎

The Dedekind property of rings of integers of global fields (item (1) in Proposition
3.1
) enables us to make the following definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Given a nontrivial ideal
\{0\}\lneq I\lneq\mathcal{O}_{K}
and its factorization
I=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{e_{1}}\dots\mathfrak{p}_{r}^{e_{r}}
into primes, we define the
least prime factor
of
I
as
\text{lpf}(I)=\min_{1\leq i\leq r}[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathfrak{p}_{i}]
.

Equivalently,
\text{lpf}(I)
is the minimum index of all proper ideals containing
I
.
As the next proposition shows,
\text{lpf}(I)
can also be computed in terms of ideals of the quotient ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}/I
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
K
be a number field.
Let
\{0\}\neq I\leq\mathcal{O}_{K}
be a nonzero ideal.
Then

\displaystyle\text{lpf}(I)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{|J|:J~\text{is a nonzero ideal of the quotient ring}~\mathcal{O}_{K}/I\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{[\mathcal{O}_{K}/I:J]:J~\text{is a proper ideal of the quotient ring}~\mathcal{O}_{K}/I\right\}.

Proof.

The ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}/I
are all products of the form
J=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{s_{1}}\dots\mathfrak{p}_{r}^{s_{r}}
with
0\leq s_{i}\leq e_{i}
.
Such an ideal has cardinality

|J|=\prod_{i=1}^{r}[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathfrak{p}_{i}]^{e_{i}-s_{i}}

and index

[\mathcal{O}_{K}/I:J]=\prod_{i=1}^{r}[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathfrak{p}_{i}]^{s_{i}}

Both quantities are clearly minimized by
\min_{1\leq i\leq r}[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathfrak{p}_{i}]=\text{lpf}(I)
.
∎

Based on Proposition
3.3
, we define
lpf
for a finite commutative ring by

\text{lpf}(R)=\min\left\{[R:J]:J~\text{is a proper ideal of}~R\right\}.

Example 3.4
.

4.
Totally ergodic actions of rings of integers of global fields and polynomial ergodic averages

We begin this section by reviewing Furstenberg’s dynamical approach to the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem over
\mathbb{Z}
.
This will enable our discussion in the rest of the section, which deals with extensions of the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem to rings of integers of global fields.

The first step is to translate a combinatorial statement about subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
into a dynamical statement about actions of
\mathbb{Z}
on probability spaces via measure-preserving transformations.
This translation is provided by the Furstenberg correspondence principle (Theorem
4.1
below).
Recall that an
(invertible) measure-preserving system
is a triple
(X,\mu,T)
, where
(X,\mu)
is a standard probability space (for which we omit explicit reference to the
\sigma
-algebra on
X
) and
T:X\to X
is measurable and invertible with measurable inverse such that the measure
\mu
is preserved by
T
, i.e. for any measurable subset
A\subseteq X
, one has
\mu(T^{-1}A)=\mu(A)
.
The system
(X,\mu,T)
is
ergodic
if every
T
-invariant measurable subset
A\subseteq X
has measure 0 or 1.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
be a set with positive upper Banach density

d^{*}(E)=\limsup_{N-M\to\infty}\frac{\left|E\cap\{M+1,M+2,\dots,N\}\right|}{N-M}>0.

Then there exists an ergodic invertible measure-preserving system
(X,\mu,T)
and a measurable set
A\subseteq X
with
\mu(A)=d^{*}(E)
such that

d^{*}\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}(E-n_{j})\right)\geq\mu\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}T^{-n_{j}}A\right)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 4.2
.

The formulation of the Furstenberg correspondence principle given in Theorem
4.1
(with the ability to assume that the measure-preserving system
(X,\mu,T)
is ergodic) first appeared in
[

, Proposition 3.1]
.
The proof given in
[

]
is based on an earlier argument of Furstenberg
[

, Lemma 3.7]
with only small modifications to guarantee ergodicity of the resulting system.
For additional discussion on the Furstenberg correspondence principle and its different variants, see
[
bf
, p. 7]
.

Now we wish to prove a statement about recurrence in an ergodic measure-preserving system
(X,\mu,T)
.
We will make use of the following notions from ergodic theory.

•

The transformation
T
induces a unitary operator (the
Koopman operator
) on

by
U_{T}f=f\circ T
.

•

We say that a number

is an

of the system
(X,\mu,T)
if it is an eigenvalue of the Koopman operator
U_{T}
.
That is, there exists a nonzero

such that
U_{T}f=\lambda f
.
(We work here with
L^{2}(\mu)
as a Hilbert space over the complex numbers.)
All eigenvalues are of modulus 1, since
U_{T}
is unitary.
The number
1
is always an eigenvalue (consider the constant function
f=1
), and for ergodic systems, all of the eigenspaces are one-dimensional.
If an eigenvalue

is a root of unity, we call
\lambda
a
rational eigenvalue
.

•

The system
(X,\mu,T)
is
periodic
if there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T^{k}x=x
for almost every
x\in X
.

•

A system
(Y,\nu,S)
is a

of

if there exist full measure subsets

and

.

Consider now an ergodic system

and a measurable set

\mu(A)>0

The function

That is,

.
For the purposes of studying polynomial configurations in sets of positive density in

Due to the identity (

To distinguish these behaviors, we may decompose the measure

\nu_{rat}(B)=\nu(B\cap\mathbb{Q})

As an immediate consequence, if

\lim_{N-M\to\infty}\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{n=M+1}^{N}\mu(A\cap T^{-P(n)}A)=\nu(\{0\})=\lim_{N-M\to\infty}\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{n=M+1}^{N}\mu(A\cap T^{-n}A),

and the final expression on the right hand side is equal to

by the mean ergodic theorem.
In particular, there exist (many values of)

The presence or absence of rational eigenvalues is characterized by the dynamical property of
total ergodicity
, which is usually defined using condition (i) in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic system.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the system
(X,\mu,T^{k})
is ergodic;

(ii)

(X,\mu,T)
has no nontrivial periodic factors;

(iii)

(
X,\mu,T)
has no nonzero rational eigenvalues;

(iv)

for any nonconstant polynomial
P(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
and any

\lim_{N-M\to\infty}\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{n=M+1}^{N}T^{P(n)}f=\int_{X}f~d\mu.

Now, in the case that
T
is not totally ergodic, the measure
\nu_{rat}
takes the form
\nu_{rat}=\mu(A)^{2}\delta_{0}+\sum_{j}c_{j}\delta_{q_{j}}
for some finite or countably infinite sequence of rational points
q_{j}\in(0,1)

c_{j}>0
satisfy
\sum_{j}c_{j}=\nu_{rat}([0,1))-\mu(A)^{2}\leq\nu([0,1))-\mu(A)^{2}=\mu(A)-\mu(A)^{2}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and take
J
sufficiently large so that
\sum_{j>J}c_{j}<\varepsilon
.
Then, up to an
\varepsilon

\mu(A\cap T^{-P(n)}A)\approx\mu(A)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}c_{j}e^{2\pi iP(n)q_{j}}+\int_{0}^{1}e^{2\pi iP(n)x}~d\nu_{te}(x).

In light of Weyl’s equidistribution theorem, averaging over
n\in\mathbb{Z}
eliminates the last term, so to prove positivity of the expression
\mu(A\cap T^{-P(n)}A)

\sum_{j=1}^{J}c_{j}e^{2\pi iP(n)q_{j}}

for a large set of
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
This is achievable under the following assumption about the polynomial
P
:

Definition 4.4
.

A polynomial
P(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is called
intersective
if
P
has a root mod
m
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Supposing that
P
is intersective and taking

to be a common denominator for all of the numbers
q_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,J
, we may find

such that
P(mn+r)q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,J\}

Then

\lim_{N-M\to\infty}\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{n=M+1}^{N}\mu(A\cap T^{-P(mn+r)}A)\geq\mu(A)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}c_{j}-\varepsilon\geq\mu(A)^{2}-\varepsilon.

The argument sketched above leads to the following “necessary and sufficient” form of the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem:

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
P(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

P
is intersective;

(ii)

for any measure-preserving system
\left(X,\mu,T\right)
and any
A\subseteq X
with
\mu(A)>0
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\mu\left(A\cap T^{-P(n)}A\right)>0.

(iii)

for any measure-preserving system
\left(X,\mu,T\right)
, any
A\subseteq X
with
\mu(A)>0
, and any
\varepsilon>0
, the set

\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu\left(A\cap T^{-P(n)}A\right)>\mu(A)^{2}-\varepsilon\right\}

is syndetic.

(iv)

for any set
E\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
with
d^{*}(E)>0
, there exist
x,y\in E
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
such that
x-y=P(n)
.

Remark 4.6
.

The observation that intersectivity plays a crucial role in the recurrence properties of polynomial actions is due to Kamae and Mendès-France
[
km
]
, who proved that the set of values of an intersective polynomial is a
van der Corput set
.
We will not need the notion of van der Corput sets elsewhere in the paper and so do not give its formal definition.
The important property for our present discussion (which was also observed by Kamae and Mendès-France) is that the set of values
\{P(n):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
being a van der Corput set implies that item (iv) holds.

The equivalence between (ii) and (iv) can be deduced as a straightforard application of the Furstenberg correspondence principle (Theorem
4.1
), and the upgrade from (ii) to the
a priori
stronger statement in (iii) follows easily from the method of proof outlined above.

One of the goals of this paper is to establish a version of Theorem
4.5
in the setting of rings of integers of global fields.
In the infinite characteristic case (rings of integers of number fields), there is a fairly direct extension, which was carried out in
[
br
]
.
The finite characteristic setting (rings of integers of global function fields) is more subtle.
Nevertheless, we obtain extensions that are valid for all global fields.
These are necessarily more cumbersome than the results in the special cases of the integers and rings of integers of number fields, so we provide comments throughout on the simplifications that arise from having infinite characteristic.
It is useful to keep in mind the ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
as a motivating example for statements involving the characteristic.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the ring of integers of a global field
K
.
By a
measure-preserving
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-system
, we will mean a tuple
(X,\mu,(T_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}})
such that
(X,\mu)
is a standard probability space and
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}
is an action of the group
(\mathcal{O}_{K},+)
by measure-preserving transformations.
The system
(X,\mu,(T_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}})
is
ergodic
if every
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}
-invariant set has measure zero or one.
We say that a measure-preserving
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-system
\left(X,\mu,(T_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}\right)
is
periodic
if there exists
m\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\setminus\{0\}
such that
T_{mn+r}=T_{r}
for every
n,r\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
In this case, we say that
m
is a
period
of the system.
Note that the collection of all periods (together with 0) forms an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
The ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is not necessarily a principal ideal domain, so it does not make sense in this context to describe an element
m\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\setminus\{0\}
as
the
period of a system.
Instead, we describe the periodicity of an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-system in terms of its ideal of periods.

In the case
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}
, every ergodic action on a finite set is periodic, and the same remains true for rings of integers of number fields.
However, when
K
is a global function field of characteristic
p
, the group
(\mathcal{O}_{K},+)
may act ergodically on a finite set without being periodic.
Namely, if
H\leq(\mathcal{O}_{K},+)
is a finite index subgroup that is not an ideal, then the natural action of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
on the finite set
\mathcal{O}_{K}/H
is not periodic in the sense defined above.
If
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
, one can take as an example the additive subgroup
H=\{\sum_{j}a_{j}t^{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]:a_{1}=0\}
.
For additional discussion, see Remark
4.12

A nonzero function

is an

of the system
(X,\mu,(T_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}})
if there is a map
\chi:\mathcal{O}_{K}\to S^{1}

for every

.
Since

associated to

We say that a character
\chi:\mathcal{O}_{K}\to S^{1}

if it is periodic in that sense that there exists

such that
\chi(mn+r)=\chi(r)

If

A first step towards generalizing Theorem
4.5
to the setting of rings of integers of global fields is to observe that, as described in Theorem
4.3

We introduce the following terminology and notation for discussing averages in groups.
A
Følner sequence
in
(\mathcal{O}_{K},+)
is a sequence of finite subsets
(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that for every
n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{|\Phi_{N}\triangle(\Phi_{N}+n)|}{|\Phi_{N}|}=0.

If
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}
is a sequence indexed by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
in a topological vector space
V
, we say that
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}
has
uniform Cesàro limit
equal to
x\in V
, denoted by
\textup{UC-}\lim_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}x_{n}=x
, if for every Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}x_{n}=x.

Theorem 4.7

Let
K
be a global field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}

Suppose
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}
is an ergodic measure-preserving action of

The following are equivalent:

(i)

for any nonzero ideal

(ii)

\left(X,\mu,(T_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}\right)

(iii)

\left(X,\mu,(T_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}\right)

(iv)

We remind the reader that we use the convention that

Definition 4.8
.

We say that a system

When considering limits of polynomial ergodic averages of the form
\textup{UC-}\lim_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}T_{P(n)}f

This is illustrated by the following example.

Example 4.9
.

Let
b=(b_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
be the sequence

b_{j}=\begin{cases}\left\lfloor j\sqrt{2}\right\rfloor\bmod{3},&\text{if}~3\nmid j;\\
0,&\text{if}~3\mid j~\text{and}~j\neq 0;\\
1,&\text{if}~j=0.\end{cases}

(The exact form of
b
is not important for the present example.
The relevant property is that
b
is an aperiodic sequence that is eventually periodic along the subsequence indexed by multiples of
3

Consider the character
\chi:\mathbb{F}_{3}[t]\to S^{1}
defined by

\chi\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{j}t^{j}\right)=e\left(\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{j}b_{j}}{3}\right),

where
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
.
The aperiodicity of the sequence
b
means that
\chi
is an irrational character, and hence the action of
(\mathbb{F}_{3}[t],+)
on the cube roots of unity
C_{3}
given by
T_{n}\zeta=\chi(n)\zeta
is ergodic along ideals.
However, for any
n=\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{j}t^{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}[t]
, we have

\chi(n^{6})=\chi\left((n^{2})^{3}\right)=\chi\left(\sum_{i,j=0}^{k}a_{i}a_{j}t^{3(i+j)}\right)=e\left(\frac{\sum_{i,j=0}^{k}a_{i}a_{j}b_{3(i+j)}}{3}\right)=e\left(\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{3}\right).

Thus, rather than obtaining the limiting behavior in (
4.3
), we have

\textup{UC-}\lim_{n\in\mathbb{F}_{3}[t]}T_{n^{6}}f(\zeta)=\frac{1}{3}f(\zeta)+\frac{2}{3}f\left(e^{2\pi i/3}\zeta\right)

for

.

In order to obtain a valid result dealing with high degree polynomials, we need to understand and identify the crucial properties underlying situations similar to the preceding example.

Definition 4.10
.

A polynomial
\eta(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x]
is
additive
if
\eta(x+y)=\eta(x)+\eta(y)
for every
x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

In characteristic
p
, additive polynomials are exactly the polynomials of the form
\eta(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}c_{j}x^{p^{j}}
for
N\geq 0
and
c_{j}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
We say that a character
\chi:\mathcal{O}_{K}\to S^{1}
is
weakly rational
if there exists an additive polynomial
\eta(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x]
such that
\chi\circ\eta
is a nontrivial rational character.
The character in Example
4.9
is an example of a weakly rational character, with
n\mapsto\chi(n^{3})
being rational.
A nontrivial character is
strongly irrational
if it is not weakly rational.
We say that a system
(X,\mu,(T_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}})
is
strongly ergodic
if it is ergodic and has no nontrivial weakly rational eigencharacters.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
K
be a global field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
Let
(X,\mu,(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}})
be an ergodic
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-system.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

(X,\mu,(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}})
is strongly ergodic;

(ii)

for any polynomial
P(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x]
with
P(0)=0
and any
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
,

\textup{UC-}\lim_{n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}}T_{P(n)}f=\pi_{P}(f),

where
\pi_{P}
is the orthogonal projection onto the space
\mathcal{H}_{P}=\{g\in L^{2}(\mu):T_{P(n)}g=g~\text{for all}~n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\}
.

Note that condition (ii) in Theorem
4.11
differs from condition (iv) in Theorem
4.7
in that there is no restriction on the degree of the polynomial
P
but
P
must have zero constant term.
The cost of upgrading to the high degree regime is the requirement of strong ergodicity and that we only obtain a projection
\pi_{P}(f)
rather than the integral
\int_{X}f~d\mu
.
The phenomenon that a polynomial ergodic average converges to a nontrivial projection resembles the polynomial ergodic theorem for actions of fields due to Larick (see
[
larick
]
and
[
blm
, Theorem 3.10]
).

Remark 4.12
.

For
\mathbb{Z}
-systems (and, more generally, for
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-systems when
K
is a number field), the notions of ergodic along ideals and strongly ergodic coincide and are equivalent to the classical notion of total ergodicity.
From a group-theoretic point of view, the notion of total ergodicity has other generalizations: for instance, one may hypothesize that the action of every finite index subgroup is ergodic (or, equivalently, that there are no nonconstant functions
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
with finite orbit under the action of the group).

All three notions (ergodicity along ideals, strong ergodicity, and ergodicity along finite index subgroups) become distinct when
K
is a global function field.
To see this, note that if
K
is a global function field of characteristic
p
, then every group character
\chi:\mathcal{O}_{K}\to S^{1}
takes values in the
p
th roots of unity.
Therefore,
\ker{\chi}
is a subgroup of index
p
.
It follows that the presence of
any
nontrivial eigencharacter (rational or not) means that there is a finite index subgroup along which the action is non-ergodic, and the corresponding eigenfunction will have finite orbit (consisting of multiples of the eigenfunction by
p
th roots of unity).
Hence, ergodicity along every finite index subgroup is equivalent to
weak mixing
(absence of eigencharacters) for an action of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
when
K
is a global function field.

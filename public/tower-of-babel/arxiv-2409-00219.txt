Higher categories of push-pull spans, II: Matrix factorizations
Lorenzo Riva

Abstract
. This is the second part of a project aimed at formalizing Rozansky-Witten models in the functorial field theory framework. In the first part we constructed a symmetric monoidal ( ∞ , 3 ) (\infty,3) -category 𝒞 ​ ℛ ​ 𝒲 \mathscr{CRW} of commutative Rozansky-Witten models with the goal of approximating the 3 3 -category of Kapustin and Rozansky. Brief review of

Throughout this paper we assume that

2.1.
The basics

Recall Theorem B of

]

with the following properties:

(1)

(2)

its

(3)

The structure of the

and we define the subcategory of affine stacks

to be the essential image of

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{t}_{\mathbb{K}}}(\Spec A,\Spec B)\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{CA}\mathrm{lg}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathrm{c}}}(B,A)

for any

2.4.

By

which provides an identification

We direct the reader to
[

]
for an extended treatment of both the unoriented and the oriented extended

We encourage the reader to work out one example for themselves to see the complexity of the final expression and compare it to the affine case. Moreover, as we will see, the field theories induced from the functor that we will construct in

We close this subsection on a negative, but expected, note. Suppose that the
2

that we just calculated could be extended to a
3
-dimensional fully extended field theory
\overline{\mathcal{Z}}
valued in
\mathscr{CRW}
, i.e.

\overline{\mathcal{Z}}:\mathbf{Bord}_{0,1,2,3}^{\mathrm{or}}\to\mathscr{CRW}

is a symmetric monoidal functor of

-categories such that

\mathbf{h}_{2}\overline{\mathcal{Z}}=\overline{\mathcal{Z}}|_{\mathrm{Bord}_{0,1,2}^{\mathrm{or}}}\simeq\mathcal{Z}_{A}.

Then
\overline{\mathcal{Z}}
could be dimensionally reduced to a
1
-dimensional field theory

\overline{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}=\overline{\mathcal{Z}}(-\times S^{2}):\mathrm{Bord}^{\mathrm{or}}_{0,1}\to\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{id}_{\ast})\simeq\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{k}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}.

It is a classical result that such field theories are classified by the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded cochain complexes which are finite dimensional in both even and odd degree. This does not happen in our cases of interest:

Proposition 2.5
.

If
A
is a polynomial algebra concentrated in degree
0
with at least
1

\mathcal{Z}_{A}
cannot be extended to a
3

Proof.

A quick calculation shows that if
A=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{t}]
then

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}(\mathsf{pt}^{+})=\mathcal{Z}_{A}(S^{2})

\displaystyle\simeq A\otimes_{\mathrm{HC}(A)}A

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{t},y_{1},\dotsc,y_{t}]\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\Lambda(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{t},y_{1},\dotsc,y_{t})

is an algebra generated by
2t
generators in degree
0
and
2t
generators in degree
-1
, with no differential. (Here
\Lambda(\dotsc)
denotes the exterior algebra on a set of generators.) So
\mathcal{Z}(S^{2})
is never a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space unless

, in which case

and

is the unit in
\mathscr{CRW}
.
∎

We will show later that the cotangent stack of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
is a symplectic derived stack and satisfies

\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}\simeq\Spec\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n},p_{x_{1}},\dotsc,p_{x_{n}}].

Therefore, by the proposition, the field theories with target

2.5.
Aside: cotangent stacks and affine planes

Before moving on to the next section we need to define one of the principal objects of interest for our discussion, namely the
affine plane

\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}
. This is the cotangent stack of the affine
n
-dimensional space
\mathbb{K}^{n}=\Spec\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]
. In this last subsection we will review some of the theory of cotangent stacks and the symplectic forms that can be put on them and then specialize to
\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}
.

Fix a derived stack
X
and consider its
cotangent stack

\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X\in\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{t}_{/X}
, defined up to equivalence by

\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{t}_{/X}(Y,\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X)\simeq\mathscr{QC}\mathrm{oh}(Y)(\mathcal{O}_{Y},p^{\ast}\mathbb{L}_{X})

for any

. This derived stack classifies the sections of
\mathbb{L}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
or, equivalently, the
1
-forms on
X
; see for example
[
Cal19
, beginning of Section 2]
. Note that when
p:\Spec A\to X
is an affine point of
X
then

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{t}_{/X}(\Spec A,\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathscr{QC}\mathrm{oh}(\Spec A)(\mathcal{O}_{\Spec A},p^{\ast}\mathbb{L}_{X})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathscr{QC}\mathrm{oh}(\Spec A)(p^{\ast}\mathbb{T}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{\Spec A})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{A}(p^{\ast}\mathbb{T}_{X},A),

where
\mathbb{T}_{X}:=\mathbb{L}_{X}^{\vee}\in\mathscr{QC}\mathrm{oh}(X)
is the dual of the cotangent stack. When
X=\Spec B
then we set
\mathbb{T}_{B}:=\mathbb{T}_{\Spec B}\in\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{B}
and we have
\mathbb{T}_{B}\simeq\mathbb{L}_{B}^{\vee}
where
\mathbb{L}_{B}
is the corepresenting object for the functor of
\mathbb{K}
-linear derivations,
\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{K}}(B,-):\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{B}\to\mathscr{S}
. Moreover

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{t}_{/\Spec B}(\Spec A,\mathrm{T}^{\ast}(\Spec B))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{A}(A\otimes_{B}\mathbb{T}_{B},A)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{B}(\mathbb{T}_{B},p_{\ast}A)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathscr{CA}\mathrm{lg}_{\mathbb{K},B/}(\mathrm{Sym}(\mathbb{T}_{B}),p_{\ast}A)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{t}_{/\Spec B}(\Spec A,\Spec\mathrm{Sym}(\mathbb{T}_{B}))

and so

\mathrm{T}^{\ast}(\Spec B)\simeq\Spec\mathrm{Sym}(\mathbb{T}_{B}).

If we further restrict ourselves to
\mathbb{K}^{n}:=\Spec\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]
then a simple calculation shows that

\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]}\simeq\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]}^{\vee}\simeq\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]^{\oplus n}

as
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]
-modules, so

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}

\displaystyle\simeq\Spec\mathrm{Sym}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]})

\displaystyle\simeq\Spec\mathrm{Sym}(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]^{\oplus n})

\displaystyle\simeq\Spec\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n},p_{x_{1}},\dotsc,p_{x_{n}}],

which in particular is equivalent to
\mathbb{K}^{2n}
. It is proven in
[
Cal19
, Theorem 2.2]
that for any Artin stack
X
the cotangent stack
\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X
carries a symplectic form
\omega_{X}:=d\lambda_{X}
, where
\lambda_{X}
is a tautological
1
-form on
\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X
coming from the identity map
\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X\to\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X
under the equivalence

\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{t}_{X}(\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X,\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X)\simeq\mathscr{QC}\mathrm{oh}(\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X)(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X},\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{L}_{X})\simeq\mathscr{QC}\mathrm{oh}(\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X)(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X},\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{T}^{\ast}X})

(here

is the canonical projection). Thanks to the above calculation we can concentrate on the even-dimensional affine planes
\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{K}^{2n}
whose canonical symplectic form is given (globally) by

\omega_{n}\simeq d\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\wedge dp_{x_{i}}\right)\simeq\sum_{i=1}^{n}dx_{i}\wedge dp_{x_{i}}.

Therefore
(\mathbb{K}^{2n},\omega_{n})\in\mathscr{CRW}
for every

. The equivalences

(2.5.1)

\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m}\times\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{K}^{2m}\times\mathbb{K}^{2n}\simeq\mathbb{K}^{2(m+n)}\simeq\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m+n}

send the product form
\omega_{m}+\omega_{n}
to
\omega_{m+n}
, i.e. they are equivalences in of symplectic stacks. Moreover, let
(-)^{\diamond}:\mathscr{P}\mathrm{re}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ymp}\to\mathscr{P}\mathrm{re}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{ymp}
denote the dualization functor which switches the sign of the symplectic form (see

2.2
). Then we have an equivalence

(2.5.2)

\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}\simeq(\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n})^{\diamond}

in
\mathscr{CRW}
given by switching the sign of the “momentum” variables:
x_{i}\mapsto x_{i}
and
p_{x_{i}}\mapsto-p_{x_{i}}
for all

.

3.1.

d_{M}^{2}=V\cdot\mathrm{id}_{M}
. The
\mathbb{K}[\underline{x}]
-module
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{x}]}(M,N)
can be given a differential
\delta
defined by

\delta\varphi=d_{N}\varphi-(-1)^{\left\lvert\varphi\right\rvert}\varphi d_{M},

where
\left\lvert\varphi\right\rvert
is the degree of the map
\varphi
. In particular,
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{x}]}(M)
has differential
\delta=[d_{M},-]
, the graded commutator with
d_{M}
. Matrix factorizations form a dg-category
\mathcal{MF}(\underline{x};V)
.

The equation
d^{2}=V\cdot\mathrm{id}
has some striking consequences. Enhance the category so that every matrix factorization is equipped with a choice of basis, turning every morphism
M\to N
into a (possibly infinite) matrix. Then in particular
d_{M}:M\to M
is a matrix with polynomial entries and so we can define

\lambda_{x_{i}}:=\partial_{x_{i}}d_{M}

by differentiating each entry of
d_{M}
with respect to the variable
x_{i}
. If the variable
x_{i}
is clear from the context then we use
\lambda_{i}
instead of
\lambda_{x_{i}}
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
Mur18
, Lemma 2.11]
)
.

The odd morphism
\lambda_{i}:M\to M
is independent of the choice of basis of
M
up to chain homotopy.

Proof.

Say
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are two copies of
M
equipped with two different bases,
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are the matrices for
d_{M}
with respect to those bases, and
P
is a change-of-basis matrix for
d_{M}
: namely,
Pd_{1}=d_{2}P
holds. Set
\lambda_{1}=\partial_{i}d_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}=\partial_{i}d_{2}
. Differentiating both sides of
Pd_{1}=d_{2}P
with respect to
x_{i}
and using the product rule yields

(\partial_{i}P)d_{1}+P\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}P+d_{2}(\partial_{i}P)

and so

P\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}P=d_{2}(\partial_{i}P)-(\partial_{i}P)d_{1}

as morphisms
M_{1}\to M_{2}
. Thus any two matrix expressions for
\lambda_{i}
are chain homotopic, as desired.
∎

There is a symmetric monoidal
2
-category with duals, denoted
\mathbf{MF}
, where matrix factorizations are the
2
-morphisms. This was constructed in
[
BCR23
]
. We recall its basic structure in preparation for the main theorem:

The objects of
\mathbf{MF}
are finite tuples of variables
\underline{x}
. The symmetric monoidal product is concatenation of tuples and the empty tuple
\emptyset
acts as the unit. The
1
-morphisms between two tuples
\underline{x}
and
\underline{y}
are pairs
(\underline{a},V)
, where
\underline{a}
is a new collection of variables distinct from those in
\underline{xy}
and
V\in\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}]
is a polynomial. Horizontal composition is given by concatenating the extra variables, including the intermediary one, and summing the polynomials:

(\underline{b},W(\underline{yzb}))\circ(\underline{a},V(\underline{xya}))=(\underline{ayb},V(\underline{xya})+W(\underline{yzb})).

If
(\underline{a},V)
and
(\underline{a^{\prime}},V^{\prime})
are
1
-morphisms
\underline{x}\to\underline{y}
, a
2
-morphism
(\underline{a},V)\Rightarrow(\underline{a^{\prime}},V^{\prime})
is an isomorphism class of objects in the idempotent completion of
\mathbf{h}\mathcal{MF}(\underline{xyaa^{\prime}};V^{\prime}-V)
, the homotopy category of the dg-category
\mathcal{MF}(\underline{xyaa^{\prime}};V^{\prime}-V)
obtained by taking the even cohomology group of all the mapping complexes; more concretely, a
2
-morphism is a chain homotopy equivalence class
[M]
of matrix factorizations of
V^{\prime}-V
such that one representative is a direct summand of a finite-rank matrix factorization. The vertical composition is given by the tensor product, which is compatible with homotopy equivalences: if
M:(\underline{a},U)\Rightarrow(\underline{b},V)
and
N:(\underline{b},V)\Rightarrow(\underline{c},W)
represent two
2
-morphisms then

N\circ M=M\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyb}]}N

is a representative for the composite
2
-morphism, with the twisted differential on the right given by
d_{M}\otimes 1+1\otimes d_{N}
.

The unit for the horizontal composition of
1
-morphisms is

\mathrm{id}_{\underline{x}}:=(\underline{a},\sum_{i=1}^{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}a_{i}(\widetilde{x}_{i}-x_{i}));

the idea is that when composing a
1
-morphism with the unit we’re substituting the variables
x_{i}
with the variables
\widetilde{x}_{i}
. Note that this is not a strict unit – see for example the end of Section 2.1 in
[
BCR23
]
for an explicit unitor isomorphism. The unit for the vertical composition of
2
-morphisms is (the isomorphism class of)
I_{(\underline{a},V)}
, the Koszul matrix factorization described in Section 2.1 of
[
BCR23
]
and later here in the following subsection: indeed, for any
2
-morphism
[M]:(\underline{a},V)\Rightarrow(\underline{a^{\prime}},V^{\prime})
we have

[M]\circ[I_{(\underline{a},V)}]=[I_{(\underline{a},V)}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}]}M]=[M]=[M\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya^{\prime}}]}I_{(\underline{a^{\prime}},V^{\prime})}]=[I_{(\underline{a^{\prime}},V^{\prime})}]\circ[M].

Explicit formulas for the homotopy equivalences in this equation can be found in
[
CM16
,
CMM20
]
.

3.2.
A useful quasi-isomorphism

Fix a
1
-morphism
(\underline{a},V):\underline{x}\to\underline{y}
in
\mathbf{MF}
, with
\left\lvert\underline{a}\right\rvert=k
. We need an explicit model for
I_{(\underline{a},V)}
as a matrix factorization of
V(\underline{xya^{\prime}})-V(\underline{xya})
– notice the extra tuple of variables
\underline{a^{\prime}}
, which has the same length as
\underline{a}
. As a free
\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyaa^{\prime}}]
-module it is given by

I_{(\underline{a},V)}=\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyaa^{\prime}};\underline{\theta}]:=\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyaa^{\prime}}]\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\Lambda(\theta_{1},\dotsc,\theta_{k}),

where the right factor in the tensor product denotes the exterior algebra of a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space with basis elements
\theta_{1},\dotsc,\theta_{k}
. The exterior algebra inherits a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading where the elements
\theta_{i}
are naturally in odd degree, so
I_{(\underline{a},V)}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyaa^{\prime}}]
-module. The differential is

d_{I_{(\underline{a},V)}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i,V}\theta_{i}+(a_{i}^{\prime}-a_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}},

where

p_{i,V}:=\frac{V(\underline{xy},a_{1},\dotsc,a_{i-1},a_{i}^{\prime},\dotsc,a_{k}^{\prime})-V(\underline{xy},a_{1},\dotsc,a_{i},a_{i+1}^{\prime},\dotsc,a_{k}^{\prime})}{a_{i}^{\prime}-a_{i}}\in\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyaa^{\prime}}]

is the difference quotient whose limit as
a_{i}^{\prime}\to a_{i}
results in
\partial_{a_{i}}V
. It’s easy to see that

d_{I_{(\underline{a},V)}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i,V}\cdot(a_{i}^{\prime}-a_{i})=V(\underline{xya^{\prime}})-V(\underline{xya})

and so
I_{(\underline{a},V)}
is a matrix factorization of the correct polynomial.

Now we can state an important lemma. If
M
is a matrix factorization, we denote by
\mathrm{End}(M)
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded cochain complex of endomorphisms of
M
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There is an equivalences of algebras

\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})\simeq R_{(\underline{a},V)}

where
R_{(\underline{a},V)}
denotes a cofibrant resolution of
\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}]/\langle\partial_{a_{1}}V,\dotsc,\partial_{a_{k}}V\rangle
(a cdga concentrated in degree
0
) in the model category of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded commutative dgas.

In particular, since
R_{(\underline{a},V)}
is commutative, this result shows that the natural multiplication of
\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})
given by composition of endomorphisms is homotopy commutative. This is to be expected from an Eckmann-Hilton argument: since
I_{(\underline{a},V)}
is an identity morphism in a
2
-category its endomorphisms inherit a second multiplication which is compatible with the composition, and Eckmann-Hilton precisely ensures that the two multiplications coincide and are both commutative.

First, here is an explicit model for
R_{(\underline{a},V)}
:

R_{(\underline{a},V)}=\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya};\underline{\alpha}]

where
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert=\left\lvert a\right\rvert
and with differential
d_{R_{(\underline{a};V)}}
given on generators by

d_{R_{(\underline{a};V)}}p(\underline{xya})=0,\quad d_{R_{(\underline{a};V)}}\alpha_{i}=\partial_{a_{i}}V,

for any
p(\underline{xya})\in\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}]
and any
1\leq i\leq k
, and then extended using the Leibniz rule.

Proof of
Lemma

3.2
.

We will build a zigzag of equivalences

(3.2.1)

\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})\xlongrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})[\underline{\beta}]\xlongleftarrow{\simeq}R_{(\underline{a},V)},

which will prove the existence of the desired equivalence
\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})\simeq R_{(\underline{a},V)}
. In the middle we have

\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})[\underline{\beta}]:=\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\Lambda(\beta_{1},\dotsc,\beta_{k}),

where
\left\lvert\underline{a}\right\rvert=k
, and we define the differential
d
on it to be the usual differential
\delta=[d_{I_{(\underline{a},V)}},-]
(the commutator with Koszul signs) on
\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})
and
d\beta_{i}=\partial_{a_{i}}V
on each generator.

There is an inclusion of dgas

\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})\to\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})[\underline{\beta}],\quad f\mapsto f\otimes 1

which is also an equivalence, i.e. an isomorphism in cohomology: the only new differentials are those coming from each
\beta_{i}
, but since

d\beta_{i}=\partial_{a_{i}}V=[d_{I_{(\underline{a},V)}},\partial_{a_{i}}d_{I_{(\underline{a},V)}}]=\delta(\partial_{a_{i}}d_{I_{(\underline{a},V)}})

we find that they were already accounted for in the cohomology of
\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})
. The second equality can be obtained by differentiating the equation
(d_{I_{(\underline{a},V)}})^{2}=V
by the variable
a_{i}
. This yields the first equivalence in
Equation

3.2.1
.

The second map
t:R_{(\underline{a};V)}\to\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})[\underline{\beta}]
is given as follows: using the model
R_{(a,V)}=\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya};\underline{\alpha}]
described above,
t
acts on generators by

x_{i}\mapsto x_{i},\quad y_{i}\mapsto y_{i},\quad a_{i}\mapsto\frac{a_{i}+a_{i}^{\prime}}{2},\quad\alpha_{i}\mapsto\beta_{i}.

The rest of the map is determined by extending linearly over
\mathbb{K}
and using the graded Leibniz rule. We compute the cohomology on both sides to show that this map is an equivalence. Since
R_{(\underline{a};V)}
is a resolution of
\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}]/\langle\partial_{a_{1}}V,\dotsc,\partial_{a_{k}}V\rangle
we have

H^{0}(R_{(\underline{a};V)})\cong\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}]/\langle\partial_{a_{1}}V,\dotsc,\partial_{a_{k}}V\rangle,\quad H^{1}(R_{(\underline{a};V)})\cong 0.

The cohomology of
\mathrm{End}(M)
, for general
M
, can be found in
[
KR08
, Section 4]
, and it agrees with the cohomology of
R_{(\underline{a};V)}
when
M=I_{(\underline{a},V)}
. In fact, the variables
a_{i}^{\prime}
disappear in cohomology because
d\theta_{i}=a_{i}^{\prime}-a_{i}
, and the partial derivatives
\partial_{a_{i}}V
get quotiented out because
d(\partial/\partial\theta_{i})=p_{i,V}=\partial_{a_{i}}V
after identifying
a_{i}^{\prime}
with
a_{i}
. After these identifications in cohomology the map
H^{0}(t)
becomes the identity map on
\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}]
and correctly kills each partial derivative
\partial_{a_{i}}V
by sending
\alpha_{i}
to
\beta_{i}
, which shows that it is an isomorphism. We are now done since the odd cohomologies of
R_{(\underline{a};V)}
and
\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})[\underline{\beta}]
are both zero.
∎

4.
Constructing the functor

This last section is devoted to the construction of the functor
\mathfrak{e}:\mathbf{MF}\to\mathbf{h}_{2}\mathscr{CRW}
. We will do this in pieces by prescribing, for each
n
, the value of
\mathfrak{e}
on
n
-cells (
n=0,1,2
) and supplying the structure morphisms necessary for a symmetric monoidal
2
-functor.

4.1.
From variables to derived symplectic stacks

For an object
\underline{x}\in\mathbf{MF}
of length
m
we define
\mathfrak{e}(\underline{x}):=\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m}\in\mathscr{CRW}
, which we know from
Section

2.5
to be equivalent to
\Spec\mathbb{K}[\underline{x},\underline{p_{x}}]
. There are canonical equivalences
\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m+n}\simeq\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m}\times\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}
of derived symplectic stacks (
Equation

2.5.1
), so
\mathfrak{e}
canonically preserves the monoidal product. It also preserves the unit:
\mathfrak{e}(\emptyset)\simeq\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{0}\simeq\ast
.

4.2.
From polynomials to Lagrangian morphisms

For a polynomial
(\underline{a},V):\underline{x}\to\underline{y}
we need
\mathfrak{e}(\underline{a},V)
to be a Lagrangian span

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{(\underline{a},V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}}

or alternatively, using
Equations

2.5.1
and
2.5.2
, a Lagrangian morphism

L_{(\underline{a},V)}\to\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m+n}.

Set

L_{(\underline{a},V)}:=\Spec R_{(\underline{a},V)}

where
R_{(\underline{a},V)}
is the cdga introduced in
Lemma

3.2
whose underlying
\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}]
-module is
\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya};\underline{\alpha}]
and whose differential is induced by
d\alpha_{i}=\partial_{a_{i}}V
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Define the cdga maps

\displaystyle f_{\underline{x}}:\mathbb{K}[\underline{x},\underline{p_{x}}]

\displaystyle\to R_{(\underline{a},V)},

\displaystyle f_{\underline{x}}(x_{i})=x_{i},\quad f_{\underline{x}}(p_{x_{i}})=-\partial_{x_{i}}V,

\displaystyle f_{\underline{y}}:\mathbb{K}[\underline{y},\underline{p_{y}}]

\displaystyle\to R_{(\underline{a},V)},

\displaystyle f_{\underline{y}}(y_{i})=y_{i},\quad f_{\underline{y}}(p_{y_{i}})=\partial_{y_{i}}V.

The induced morphism

f:L_{(\underline{a},V)}\to\Spec\mathbb{K}[\underline{xy},\underline{p_{x}p_{y}}]\simeq\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m+n}

carries the structure of a Lagrangian morphism of derived stacks.

Before starting the proof we recall a useful construction. A polynomial
p\in\mathbb{K}[\underline{u}]
induces a map

\gamma_{p}:\mathbb{K}[\underline{u},\underline{p_{u}}]\to\mathbb{K}[\underline{u}],\qquad\gamma_{p}(u_{i})=u_{i},\quad\gamma_{p}(p_{u_{i}})=\partial_{u_{i}}p

which passes to a map of derived stacks

\Gamma_{p}:\mathbb{K}^{\left\lvert\underline{u}\right\rvert}\simeq\Spec\mathbb{K}[\underline{u}]\to\Spec\mathbb{K}[\underline{u},\underline{p_{u}}]\simeq\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{\left\lvert\underline{u}\right\rvert}

called the
graph of
dp
; here
dp
is a
1
-form on
\mathbb{K}^{\left\lvert\underline{u}\right\rvert}
canonically obtained from
p
(see
[
Cal15
]
). By
[
Cal19
, Theorem 2.15]
there is a canonical Lagrangian structure on
\Gamma_{p}
induced by the nullhomotopy
d(dp)\simeq 0
.

Proof of
Proposition

4.1
.

We will write
f
as the pullback of two Lagrangian spans, which is again Lagrangian by
[
Cal15
, Theorem 4.4]
. Consider the two polynomials
0\in\mathbb{K}[\underline{a}]
and
V\in\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}]
. They induce Lagrangian morphisms

\Gamma_{0}:\mathbb{K}^{k}\to\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{k},\quad\Gamma_{V}:\mathbb{K}^{m+n+k}\to\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m+n+k}\simeq(\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{k})^{\diamond}\times\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m+n},

namely the graphs of
d0
and
dV
, which we can convert to two Lagrangian spans

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{K}^{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{K}^{m+n+k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ast}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m+n}}

after flipping some signs (see
Equation

2.5.2
). The derived pullback of these spans is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(\mathbb{K}[\underline{a}]\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{a},\underline{p_{a}}]}\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ast}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m+n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

and it can be easily verified that

R_{(\underline{a},V)}\simeq\mathbb{K}[\underline{a}]\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{a},\underline{p_{a}}]}\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}].

One way to see this, for example, is to replace the map
\mathbb{K}[\underline{a},\underline{p_{a}}]\to\mathbb{K}[\underline{a}]
by the quasi-isomorphic cofibration
\mathbb{K}[\underline{a},\underline{p_{a}}]\to\mathbb{K}[\underline{a},\underline{p_{a}};\underline{\alpha}]
, where the latter cdga has differential
d\alpha_{i}=p_{a_{i}}-\partial_{a_{i}}V
, and then computing the ordinary tensor product of cdgas. The result is isomorphic to the presentation of
R_{(\underline{a},V)}
given in the previous section. Moreover,
g\simeq f
after the identification, and so we’re done.
∎

Geometrically we can think of
L_{(\underline{a},V)}
as a (Lagrangian) family of Lagrangian graphs parametrized by the parameters
\underline{a}\in\mathbb{K}^{k}
. Indeed, instantiating the variables
a_{i}
at points
A_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
will return an honest Lagrangian submanifold of
\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m+n}
, namely the graph of the differential of the polynomial
V(\underline{xy},A_{1},\dotsc,A_{k})\in\mathbb{K}[\underline{xy}]
.

Now we can define
\mathfrak{e}(\underline{a},V)
to be the Lagrangian span

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{(\underline{a},V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}}

described above. The following properties of
L_{(\underline{a},V)}
are just a matter of computing some derived tensor products:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(\underline{a},V):\underline{x}\to\underline{y}
,
(\underline{a^{\prime}},V^{\prime}):\underline{x^{\prime}}\to\underline{y^{\prime}}
, and
(\underline{b},W):\underline{y}\to\underline{z}
be
1
-morphisms in
\mathbf{MF}
, and let
o=\left\lvert\underline{z}\right\rvert
,
m^{\prime}=\left\lvert\underline{x^{\prime}}\right\rvert
,
n^{\prime}=\left\lvert\underline{y^{\prime}}\right\rvert
.

(1)

\mathfrak{e}
preserves the horizontal composition and the monoidal product of
1
-morphisms, i.e. we have equivalences

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}(\underline{a},V)\times_{\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}}\mathfrak{e}(\underline{b},W)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathfrak{e}(\underline{ab},V+W)

\displaystyle\text{as spans from $\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m}$ to $\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{o}$},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}(\underline{a},V)\times\mathfrak{e}(\underline{a^{\prime}},V^{\prime})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathfrak{e}(\underline{aa^{\prime}},V+V^{\prime})

\displaystyle\text{as spans from $\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m+m^{\prime}}$ to $\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n+n^{\prime}}$}.

(2)

\mathfrak{e}
preserves the units for the above operations, i.e. we have equivalences

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}(\mathrm{id}_{\underline{x}})

\displaystyle\simeq\text{diagonal span from $\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m}$ to itself},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}(\emptyset,0)

\displaystyle\simeq\text{diagonal span from $\ast$ to itself}.

These equivalences are essentially unique, since the two sides are presentations of the limit of the same diagram and two limits can be identified with each other via an essentially unique equivalence.

4.3.
From matrix factorizations to dg-modules

The next step is to define
\mathfrak{e}
on
2
-morphisms, so matrix factorizations. Recall from
Equation

2.3.1
that the
\infty
-category of
2
-morphisms between
\mathfrak{e}(\underline{a},V):\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m}\to\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}
and
\mathfrak{e}(\underline{b},W):\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{m}\to\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\mathbb{K}^{n}
is the
\infty
-category
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{A_{W-V}}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded dg-modules over the cdga

A_{W-V}:=R_{(\underline{a},V)}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xy},\underline{p_{x}p_{y}}]}R_{(\underline{b},W)}.

This algebra has a nice explicit model:

\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyab};\underline{\chi\upsilon\alpha\beta}]

as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded exterior algebra over
\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyab}]
with one Greek variable
\chi_{i}
,
\upsilon_{i}
,
\alpha_{i}
, and
\beta_{i}
in odd degree for each corresponding Roman variable
x_{i}
,
y_{i}
,
a_{i}
, and
b_{i}
in even degree. The differential is given on generators by

d\chi_{i}=\partial_{x_{i}}W-\partial_{x_{i}}V,\quad d\upsilon_{i}=\partial_{y_{i}}W-\partial_{y_{i}}V,\quad d\alpha_{i}=-\partial_{a_{i}}V,\quad d\beta_{i}=\partial_{b_{i}}W.

More succinctly, if
\tau
is a Greek variable corresponding to a Roman variable
t
then
d\tau=\partial_{t}(W-V)
. Therefore
A_{W-V}
is the algebra of functions on the full derived critical locus of the polynomial
W-V\in\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyab}]
. Notice that we omit the extra variables
\underline{a}
and
\underline{b}
from the notation
A_{W-V}
unlike with
R_{(\underline{a},V)}
and
R_{(\underline{b},W)}
; this is because we don’t have to keep track of which variables to differentiate with respect to, as we are including all of them.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
M
be a representative for a
2
-morphism
(\underline{a},V)\to(\underline{b},W)
, namely a matrix factorization of
W-V\in\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyab}]
. Then the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded cochain complex
\mathrm{End}(M)
inherits a canonical
A_{W-V}
-action, i.e.
\mathrm{End}(M)\in\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{A_{W-V}}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
.

Proof.

We have a quasi-isomorphism

\mathrm{End}(M)\simeq\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}]}M\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyb}]}I_{(\underline{b},W)})

since
I_{(\underline{a},V)}
and
I_{(\underline{b},W)}
are units for the composition of
2
-morphisms in
\mathbf{MF}
. The right-hand side has a canonical action of
\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})
from the left factor and of
\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{b},W)})
from the right factor in the tensor product. The two actions agree on
\mathbb{K}[\underline{xy}]
and can be further made to agree on
\mathbb{K}[\underline{xy},\underline{p_{x}p_{y}}]
: the action of
p_{x_{i}}
is multiplication by
\partial_{x_{i}}(W-V)
, which is nulhomotopic as an endomorphism of
M
(as can be seen by differentiating the equation
d_{M}^{2}=W-V
), and similarly for
p_{y_{i}}
. Thus
\mathrm{End}(M)
inherits an action of

\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{a},V)})\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xy},\underline{p_{x}p_{y}}]}\mathrm{End}(I_{(\underline{b},W)})\simeq R_{(\underline{a},V)}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xy},\underline{p_{x}p_{y}}]}R_{(\underline{b},W)}=A_{W-V}

where the first equivalence is due to
Lemma

3.2
.
∎

One might reasonably object to the usage of quasi-isomorphisms without unique inverses to define this action. This is justified by the fact that we are working in an
\infty
-category, so a module over an algebra needs to be interpreted in the correct homotopical sense, with the structure diagrams for the algebra action commuting up to coherent homotopies (in this case, coherent quasi-isomorphisms). Since
\mathrm{End}(-)
and
-\otimes-
are sufficiently functorial and in particular respect quasi-isomorphisms, this is not a problem for us: in the homotopy
2
-category
\mathbf{h}_{2}\mathscr{CRW}
the homotopies turn into coherent isomorphisms, and so are appropriately invertible.

Using an explicit homotopy equivalence
M\simeq I_{(\underline{a},V)}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xya}]}M\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyb}]}I_{(\underline{b},W)}
it would be possible to rigidify the situation to obtain explicit formulas for the action of
A_{W-V}
on
\mathrm{End}(M)
. Despite our efforts we couldn’t find formulas that were satisfyingly clear or elucidating, so we are happy to leave this task to the interested reader. We expect, however, that they will reflect the formulas for the Clifford action described in
[
Mur18
]
, since the cut operation presented in that paper is one way to “wedge” the unit
I_{(\underline{a},V)}
inside the composition of two matrix factorizations.

Finally, we set
\mathfrak{e}(M):=\mathrm{End}(M)
with the
A_{W-V}
-action as in the proposition. It is clear that this assignment is independent of the chosen representative within the class
[M]
, so we omit the square brackets for simplicity. We have the following properties.

Proposition 4.4
.

Fix objects
\underline{x},\underline{y},\underline{x^{\prime}},\underline{y^{\prime}},\underline{z}
and
1
-morphisms
(\underline{a},V),(\underline{b},W),(\underline{c},X):\underline{x}\to\underline{y}
and
(\underline{a^{\prime}},V^{\prime}),(\underline{b^{\prime}},W^{\prime}):\underline{y}\Rightarrow\underline{z}
and
(\underline{a^{\prime\prime}},V^{\prime\prime}),(\underline{b^{\prime\prime}},W^{\prime\prime}):\underline{x^{\prime}}\Rightarrow\underline{y^{\prime}}

,

\displaystyle\text{as $A_{W+W^{\prime}-(V+V^{\prime})}$-modules},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}(M)\otimes_{R_{(\underline{b},W)}}\mathfrak{e}(N)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}(I_{\mathrm{id}_{\underline{x}}})

\displaystyle\simeq R_{(\underline{a},V)}

\displaystyle\text{as $R_{(\underline{a},V)}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}R_{(\underline{a},V)}$-modules},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}(I_{(\emptyset,0)})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbb{K}

\displaystyle\text{as $\mathbb{K}$-modules}.

Proof.

To obtain the module structures as in the right column, note that an
A_{W-V}
-module and a
A_{X-W}
-module can be tensored to obtain an
A_{X-W}\otimes_{R_{(\underline{b},W)}}A_{W-V}
-module and the action can be further restricted to that of a

-module via the canonical composite map

\displaystyle A_{X-V}

\displaystyle\simeq R_{(\underline{a},V)}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xz},\underline{p_{x}p_{z}}]}R_{(\underline{c},X)}

\displaystyle\to R_{(\underline{a},V)}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xy},\underline{p_{x}p_{y}}]}R_{(\underline{b},W)}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{yz},\underline{p_{y}p_{z}}]}R_{(\underline{c},X)}

\displaystyle\simeq A_{W-V}\otimes_{R_{(\underline{b},W)}}A_{X-W}.

This corresponds to the composition of
2
-morphisms in
\mathscr{CRW}
that was reviewed in
Section

2.3
.

The equivalences in (2) can be verified by computation: the second one is

3.2
and the other two follow from the second one. The equivalences in (1) require the quasi-isomorphism

\mathrm{End}(A)\simeq A\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{w}]}A^{\vee}

which exists for cohomologically finite matrix factorizations

of a polynomial

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}(M)\otimes_{R_{(\underline{b},W)}}\mathfrak{e}(N)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{End}(M)\otimes_{R_{(\underline{b},W)}}\mathrm{End}(N)

\displaystyle\simeq(M\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xyab}]}M^{\vee})\otimes_{R_{(\underline{b},W)}}(N\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\underline{xybc}]}N^{\vee})

∎

On non-local almost minimal sets and an application to the non-local Massari’s Problem
Serena Dipierro, Enrico Valdinoci, Riccardo Villa

Abstract
We consider a fractional Plateau’s problem dealing with sets with prescribed non-local mean curvature. This problem can be seen as a non-local counterpart of the classical Massari’s Problem. We obtain existence and regularity results, relying on a suitable version of the non-local theory for almost minimal sets. In this framework, the fractional curvature term in the energy functional can be interpreted as a perturbation of the fractional perimeter. In addition, we also discuss stickiness phenomena for non-local almost minimal sets.

1
Introduction and main results

In this paper we revisit the regularity theory for non-local almost minimal sets, i.e. for sets minimizing the
s
-perimeter functional up to a remainder depending on their volume, and we apply this
theory to a new type of prescribed non-local mean curvature problem, which we refer to as non-local Massari’s Problem.

Almost minimizers are a classical topic in the calculus of variations for several reasons.
First of all, in practical applications, exact minimizers may be too difficult to find, and the solutions of optimization problems may only be approximate. Real-world systems are also often influenced by measurement errors and modeling approximations, making the notion of an exact minimizer too strict. Similarly, many physical systems are subject to small fluctuations that prevent them from reaching perfect equilibrium, thus exhibiting behaviors that are close, but not precisely equal, to the theoretical minimum energy state.

In addition, from a theoretical point of view, exact minimizers might not exist in suitable functional spaces due to lack of compactness; in these situations, almost minimizers often exist and provide a valuable alternative.

Moreover, almost minimizers serve as useful building blocks for many results in the regularity theory of minimizers. Often, the detailed study of minimizers of “complicated”
energy functionals is out of reach; however, one can show that these minimizers are, in fact, almost minimizers of another, “simpler” functional, for which a well-developed regularity theory exists. In this way, regularity results for the “difficult” setting become a byproduct of the regularity theory for almost minimizers in the “easier” setting (i.e., one
trades the complexity of the system for a broader class of objects).

In this spirit, from the viewpoint of regularity theory, almost minimizers capture the “worst” allowable energy errors that still preserve favorable scaling properties at small scales.

Non-local almost minimizers theory also finds application in many problems such as obstacle and free boundary problems, capillarity problems, and regularity of sets with prescribed non-local mean curvature. See for instance

Stickiness for almost minimal sets

The notion of stickiness was firstly introduced in

23

as an unexpected boundary behavior typical of
s
-minimal surfaces. This behavior is easier to define rigorously when we consider

-minimal graphs in dimension

Definition 1.10

Let

\Omega=\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}

E=\operatorname{subgraph}(u):=\big\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;x_{2}<u(x_{1})\big\},

We say that

For further details about stickiness, we refer to

In this paper, we show that stickiness does not characterize almost-minimal surfaces. Namely, we show that every time we have a non-sticking

(Almost minimality does not see external perturbations)

Then,

is a
\Lambda
-minimizer for

1.4
The non-local Massari’s Problem

As already mentioned at the beginning of this paper, one of the natural settings in which the regularity theory of
almost minimizers finds a convenient application is
the non-local version of the Massari’s Problem about sets with prescribed mean curvature.

To this end, let us consider an open bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For an assigned function
H\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, and for every set
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the non-local Massari’s functional

\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(F):=\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega)+\int_{F\cap\Omega}H(x)\,dx.

Definition 1.12
.

Let
E
be a set of finite fractional perimeter in
\Omega
. We say that
H
is a variational non-local mean curvature for
E
if
E
is a minimizer of the functional
\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}
with a given datum outside
\Omega
, i.e.

\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(E)\leq\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(F)\quad{\mbox{ for all\penalty\ $F$ such that }}F\setminus\Omega=E\setminus\Omega.

Whenever the assigned mean curvature
H
is an essentially bounded function in
\Omega
, as a direct consequence of the regularity theory for non-local almost minimal sets, we establish the existence and regularity of minimizers of
\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}
in the family of sets

\mathscr{F}:=\big\{F\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega)<+\infty{\mbox{ and }}F\setminus\Omega=M\setminus\Omega\big\},

where
M\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a set of finite
s
-perimeter that plays the role of an external datum.

More precisely, we prove that:

Theorem 1.13

(Existence of a minimizer)
.

The family
\mathscr{F}
admits an element of variational non-local mean curvature
H
, i.e. there exists
E\in\mathscr{F}
such that

\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(E)=\inf\big\{\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(F)\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;F\in\mathscr{F}\big\}.

Moreover, we show that every minimizer of
\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}
is a
\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
-minimal set in the sense of Definition
1.1
, see Section
7.2
. Accordingly, thanks to the regularity theory for almost minimizers, we deduce the following:

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
H\in L^{\infty}(B_{1})
.
Let
E
be a set with variational non-local mean curvature
H
in
B_{1}
, that is

\mathscr{J}^{H}_{B_{1},s}(E)\leq\mathscr{J}^{H}_{B_{1},s}(F)\quad{\mbox{ for all\penalty\ $F$ such that }}F\setminus B_{1}=E\setminus B_{1}.

Then, there exists some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, depending only on
n
,
s
, and
\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}
, such that if

\partial E\cap B_{1}\subseteq\{|x_{n}|\leq\varepsilon_{0}\},

then
\partial E\cap B_{1/2}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-surface, for every
\alpha\in(0,s)
.

Theorems
1.13
and
1.14
are proved
in Section
7
.

2.1
Preliminary facts

Let

\partial E=\partial E(1):=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;0<|E\cap B_{r}(x)|<\omega_{n}r^{n}\;{\mbox{ for all\penalty\ $r>0$}}\big\}.

To start with, we provide the proof of Lemma

Proof of Lemma
1.3
.

Given sets
E
and
F
such that
E\setminus\Omega=F\setminus\Omega
, let us define

A^{-}:=E\setminus F\subseteq E\cap\Omega\qquad\text{and}\qquad A^{+}:=F\setminus E\subseteq E^{c}\cap\Omega.

Notice that, by definition of localized
s
-perimeter, we have that

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega)-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega)\\
=&\left[\mathcal{L}(A^{-},E\setminus A^{-})-\mathcal{L}(A^{-},E^{c})\right]-\left[\mathcal{L}(A^{+},E)-\mathcal{L}(A^{+},E^{c}\setminus A^{+})\right]+2\mathcal{L}(A^{-},A^{+}).\end{split}

(2.1)

If
E
satisfies both the
\Lambda
-sub-solution and the
\Lambda
-super-solution properties, we also have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(A^{-},E\setminus A^{-})-\mathcal{L}(A^{-},E^{c})\geq-\Lambda|A^{-}|

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(A^{+},E)-\mathcal{L}(A^{+},E^{c}\setminus A^{+})\leq\Lambda|A^{+}|.

(2.3)

Therefore, subtracting (
2.3
) to (

), we obtain that

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega)-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega)\geq-\Lambda|E\setminus F|-\Lambda|F\setminus E|=-\Lambda|E\Delta F|,

which proves that

is an almost minimal set with respect to
\Lambda
.

Conversely, suppose that
E
is a
\Lambda
-minimal set. Then, for every sets
A^{-}\subseteq E\cap\Omega
and
A^{+}\subseteq E^{c}\cap\Omega
, we use (
2.1
) and the almost minimality of

with competitors

F^{+}=E\cup A^{+}\qquad\text{and}\qquad F^{-}=E\setminus A^{-}

to deduce that

\displaystyle-\Lambda|A^{-}|=-\Lambda|E\Delta F^{-}|\leq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F^{-},\Omega)-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega)=\mathcal{L}(A^{-},E\setminus A^{-})-\mathcal{L}(A^{-},E^{c})

and

\displaystyle\Lambda|A^{+}|=\Lambda|E\Delta F^{+}|\leq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega)-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F^{+},\Omega)=\mathcal{L}(A^{+},E\setminus A^{+})-\mathcal{L}(A^{+},E^{c}),

which show
that

satisfies both the
\Lambda
-sub-solution and the
\Lambda
-super-solution properties, as desired.

x_{0}\in(\partial E)\cap\Omega

r\in(0,\min\{r_{0},\operatorname{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)\})
,

c_{0}r^{n}\leq|E\cap B_{r}(x_{0})|\leq(1-c_{0})r^{n}.

(2.5)

The proof of this result will be presented here below.

As an interesting byproduct of the uniform density estimates
in Theorem
2.2
, we improve Proposition
2.1
. In particular, we show that uniform density estimates and local convergence are sufficient to prove uniform convergence, i.e. with respect to Hausdorff distance.

We recall that a sequence of sets
\{E_{k}\}_{k}
converges to some set
E
with respect to the Hausdorff distance, and we write

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mbox{d}_{\mathcal{H}}(\partial E_{k},\partial E)=0,

if, for every compact set
K
of
\Omega
and for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
k\geq k_{0}
,

\begin{split}&\partial E_{k}\cap K\subseteq\mathscr{U}_{\varepsilon}(\partial E)\cap K\\
\mbox{and}\quad&\partial E\cap K\subseteq\mathscr{U}_{\varepsilon}(\partial E_{k})\cap K\end{split}

where, for any set
A
,

\mathscr{U}_{\varepsilon}(\partial A):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,{\mbox{ s.t. }}\,\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial A)<\varepsilon\}.

Then, we have the following:

Corollary 2.3

(Uniform convergence for almost minimal boundaries)
.

Let
\{E_{k}\}_{k}
be a sequence of
\Lambda_{k}
-minimal sets such that
E_{k}\to E
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for some set
E
of finite
s
-perimeter, and
\Lambda_{k}\to\Lambda\in[0,+\infty)
, as
k\to+\infty
.

Then,

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mbox{d}_{\mathcal{H}}(\partial E_{k},\partial E)=0.

Corollary
2.3
is a special case of a more general result in which we assume that the
sets
E_{k}
and
E
satisfy the uniform density estimate instead of the almost minimality condition. We refer to Appendix
B

for the general statement with proof.
We provide the proof of Corollary
2.3
here below.

Another consequence of the uniform density estimates in Theorem
2.2
is the so-called “clean ball condition”:

Corollary 2.4

(Clean ball condition for almost minimal sets)
.

Let
E
be an almost minimizer for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}
in
\Omega
.
Let
x\in\partial E
and
r>0
such that
B_{r}(x)\subseteq\Omega
.

Then, there exist a constant
c>0
, depending only on
n
,
s
, and
\Lambda
, and points
y_{1}\in E
and
y_{2}\in\Omega\setminus E
such that

B_{cr}(y_{1})\subseteq E\cap B_{r}(x)\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad B_{cr}(y_{2})\subseteq E^{c}\cap B_{r}(x).

The proof of Corollary
2.4
can be found below. Now, we give a proof of Theorem
2.2
, for which we need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 2.5

(Lemma 7.1,
[
30
]
)
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
,
N>1
, and
M>0
. Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k}
be a decreasing sequence in
\mathbb{R}
such that

x_{k+1}^{1-\beta}\leq N^{k}Mx_{k}.

If
x_{0}\leq N^{\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}}M^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}
, then
x_{k}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

If
E
is a
\Lambda
-minimal set, for some
\Lambda\geq 0
, we have that

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega)\leq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega)+\Lambda|E\Delta F|,

(2.6)

for every
F
such that
F\setminus\Omega=E\setminus\Omega
.

Let
x_{0}\in(\partial E)\cap\Omega
. Up to a translation,
we can suppose that
x_{0}
coincides with the origin. Moreover, up a dilation,
we can also assume that
B_{1}\subseteq\Omega
.

Define
A_{r}:=E\cap B_{r}
, with
r\in(0,1)
and observe
that
A_{r}\subseteq\Omega
.
Also, let
\mu(r):=|A_{r}|
and notice that,
by the co-area formula,
\mu^{\prime}(r)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\partial B_{r})
.

Our strategy now is to provide an estimate of
\mu^{1-\frac{s}{n}}(r)
in terms of
\mu(r)
.
For this, set
q:=\frac{2n}{n-s}
and observe that, thanks to the Sobolev embeddings,

\|\chi_{A_{r}}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\chi_{A_{r}}\|_{H^{\frac{s}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=C\left(\mathcal{L}(A_{r},A_{r}^{c})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(2.7)

up to renaming
C
, that depends only on
n
and
s
.

Now, since

\mathcal{L}(A_{r},A_{r}^{c})=\mathcal{L}(A_{r},E\cap A_{r}^{c})+\mathcal{L}(A_{r},E^{c}),

it follows from (
1.3
) that

\mathcal{L}(A_{r},A_{r}^{c})\leq 2\mathcal{L}(A_{r},E\cap A_{r}^{c})+\Lambda\mu(r)\leq 2\mathcal{L}(A_{r},B_{r}^{c})+\Lambda\mu(r).

(2.8)

Moreover, by Fubini-Tonelli’s Theorem, we have that

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(A_{r},B_{r}^{c})=\int_{A_{r}}\int_{B_{r}^{c}}\frac{dy\,dx}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\leq C\int_{A_{r}}\left(\int_{r-|x|}^{+\infty}\frac{dz}{z^{s+1}}\right)\,dx\\
&\qquad\quad\leq C\int_{A_{r}}\frac{dx}{(r-|x|)^{s}}\leq C\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\mu^{\prime}(\rho)}{(r-\rho)^{s}}\,d\rho,\end{split}

for some
C>0
, depending on
n
and
s
and possibly changing from line to line.

Plugging this into (
2.8
), we obtain that

\mathcal{L}(A_{r},A_{r}^{c})\leq C\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\mu^{\prime}(\rho)}{(r-\rho)^{s}}\,d\rho+\Lambda\mu(r).

From the last inequality and (
2.7
), we deduce that

\mu^{\frac{n-s}{n}}(r)=\|\chi_{A_{r}}\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\mu^{\prime}(\rho)}{(r-\rho)^{s}}\,d\rho+\Lambda\mu(r)\right),

(2.9)

up to renaming
C
.

Furthermore, since
\mu(r)\leq c_{n}r^{n}
, we choose
r_{0}>0
such that, for every
r\in(0,r_{0}]
,

C\Lambda\mu(r)\leq C\Lambda\mu^{1-\frac{s}{n}}(r)(c_{n}r^{n})^{\frac{s}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu^{1-\frac{s}{n}}(r).

Using this information into (
2.9
)
we thus conclude that

\mu^{1-\frac{s}{n}}(r)\leq C\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\mu^{\prime}(\rho)}{(r-\rho)^{s}}\,d\rho.

Hence,
integrating in
r\in(0,t)
, we deduce that, for all
t\in(0,r_{0}]
,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\mu^{1-\frac{s}{n}}(r)\,dr&\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left(C\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\mu^{\prime}(\rho)}{(r-\rho)^{s}}\,d\rho\right)\,dr\\
&=C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mu^{\prime}(\rho)\int_{\rho}^{t}\frac{dr}{(r-\rho)^{s}}\right)\,d\rho\\
&=C\int_{0}^{t}\mu^{\prime}(\rho)(t-\rho)^{1-s}\,d\rho\\
&\leq C\mu(t)\,t^{1-s}.\end{split}

(2.10)

Now we set

c_{0}:=2^{\frac{n}{s}-\frac{n^{2}}{s^{2}}}(4C)^{-\frac{n}{s}}

and we claim that

\mu(t)\geq c_{0}t^{n}\quad{\mbox{for all }}t\in(0,r_{0}].

(2.11)

To prove this,
we argue by contradiction and assume that
there exists
t_{0}\in(0,r_{0}]
such that

\mu(t_{0})<c_{0}t_{0}^{n}.

(2.12)

We define the sequence
\{t_{k}\}_{k}
as
t_{k}:=\frac{t_{0}}{2}+\frac{t_{0}}{2^{k+1}}
. Then, using (
2.10
), we have that

\frac{t_{0}}{2^{k+2}}\mu^{1-\frac{s}{n}}(t_{k+1})=(t_{k}-t_{k+1})\mu^{1-\frac{s}{n}}(t_{k+1})\leq\int_{t_{k+1}}^{t_{k}}\mu^{1-\frac{s}{n}}(r)\,dr\leq C\mu(t_{k})t_{k}^{1-s}\leq C\mu(t_{k})t_{0}^{1-s}.

Notice that, by continuity,

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mu(t_{k})=\mu(t_{0}/2)=|E\cap B_{t_{0}/2}|>0.

Therefore, using Lemma
2.5

with
x_{k}:=\mu(t_{k})
,

\beta:=s/n
,
M:=4Ct_{0}^{-s}
, and
N:=2
, we find that

\mu(t_{0})>N^{\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}}M^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}=2^{\frac{n}{s}-\frac{n^{2}}{s^{2}}}\big(4Ct_{0}^{-s}\big)^{-\frac{n}{s}}.

Thus, thanks to (
2.12
) we deduce that

c_{0}t_{0}^{n}>\mu(t_{0})>2^{\frac{n}{s}-\frac{n^{2}}{s^{2}}}\big(4Ct_{0}^{-s}\big)^{-\frac{n}{s}}=2^{\frac{n}{s}-\frac{n^{2}}{s^{2}}}(4C)^{-\frac{n}{s}}t_{0}^{n}=c_{0}t_{0}^{n},

which gives the desired contradiction
and proves (
2.11
).

From (
2.11
)
we deduce that

|E\cap B_{r}|\geq c_{0}r^{n}\quad\text{ for all }r\in(0,r_{0}],

which proves the first inequality in (
2.5
).

Moreover, since also
E^{c}
is an almost minimal set
(recall Definition
1.2
and
Lemma
1.3
),
we exploit (
2.11
)
with
E
replaced by
E^{c}
, thus obtaining that, for
r
sufficiently small,

|E^{c}\cap B_{r}|\geq c_{0}r^{n},

from which we infer the second inequality in (
2.5
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
2.3
.

Since the sequence of nonnegative real numbers
\Lambda_{k}
is convergence, we have that there exists
\Lambda_{0}\in[0,+\infty)
such that
\Lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda_{0}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

As a consequence, the sets
E_{k}

are almost minimizers with respect to
\Lambda_{0}
in
\Omega
.
Thus, we deduce from Theorem
2.2

that uniform density estimates hold true with constants that do not depend on

.

This says that we are in the position of exploiting Corollary
B.1
, thus obtaining the desired claim
in Corollary
2.3
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2.4
.

Up to rescalings and translations, we assume that
x=0
and
r=1
. Let us decompose
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in disjoint
hypercubes of size
\delta
and define
N_{\delta}:=\#\text{{Q}}_{\ \delta}
, namely the number of elements of
\text{{Q}}_{\ \delta}
, where

\text{{Q}}_{\ \delta}:=\big\{Q_{\delta}:Q_{\delta}\text{ is a cube of size\penalty\ $\delta$ such that }Q_{\delta}\subseteq Q_{3\delta}\subseteq B_{1},\ Q_{\delta}\cap\partial E\neq\varnothing\big\}.

Notice that Corollary
2.4
is proved if we show that at least one cube
Q_{\delta}
is completely contained
in
E\cap B_{1}
.

For this, we observe that the uniform density estimates in Theorem
2.2
give that
|E\cap B_{1}|\geq c_{0}
. Therefore,
if we prove that

N_{\delta}\leq C\delta^{s-n},

(2.13)

for some
C>0
depending only on
n
,
s
, and
\Lambda
, then we deduce that at least
c_{0}\delta^{-n}
cubes intersect
E\cap B_{1}
, provided that
\delta
is sufficiently small.

Thus, arguing by contradiction, if none of these cubes is completely contained in
E\cap B_{1}
, also using (
2.13
), we have that

C\delta^{s-n}\geq N_{\delta}\geq c_{0}\delta^{-n}.

It thereby follows that
C\delta^{s}\geq c_{0}
, which is a contradiction whenever we choose
\delta
small enough.

Hence, from now on, we focus on the proof of (
2.13
). For this, consider a cube
Q_{\delta}\subseteq B_{1}
such that
Q_{\delta}\cap\partial E\neq\varnothing
.
Let
x_{0}\in Q_{\delta}\cap\partial E
. Then, for any
\delta
small enough, we have that
B_{\delta}(x_{0})\subseteq Q_{3\delta}\subseteq\Omega
. Thus, thanks to
Theorem
2.2
, we obtain that

|E\cap Q_{3\delta}|\geq c_{0}\delta^{n}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad|E^{c}\cap Q_{3\delta}|\geq c_{0}\delta^{n}.

From this, using also that
|x-y|\leq 3\sqrt{n}\delta
, for every
x
,
y\in Q_{\delta}
, we deduce that

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(E\cap Q_{3\delta},E^{c}\cap Q_{3\delta})&=\int_{E\cap Q_{3\delta}}\int_{E^{c}\cap Q_{3\delta}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\\
&\geq\int_{E\cap Q_{3\delta}}\int_{E^{c}\cap Q_{3\delta}}\big(3\sqrt{n}\delta\big)^{-n-s}\,dx\,dy\\
&=\big(3\sqrt{n}\delta\big)^{-n-s}|E\cap Q_{3\delta}|\,|E^{c}\cap Q_{3\delta}|\\
&\geq C_{1}\delta^{n-s},\end{split}

(2.14)

for some
C_{1}>0
, depending on
n
,
s
, and
\Lambda
.

Set
\text{{R}}_{\delta}
to be the family of all cubes
Q_{\delta}
such that
Q_{3\delta}
is contained in
B_{1}
, and notice that
\text{{Q}}_{\ \delta}\subseteq\text{{R}}_{\delta}
. Thus, using (
2.14
), we see that

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(E\cap B_{1},E^{c}\cap B_{1})&=\sum_{Q_{\delta},Q_{\delta}^{\prime}\in\text{{R}}_{\delta}}\mathcal{L}(E\cap Q_{\delta},E^{c}\cap Q_{\delta}^{\prime})\\
&\geq 3^{-2n}\sum_{Q_{\delta},Q_{\delta}^{\prime}\in\text{{R}}_{\delta}}\mathcal{L}(E\cap Q_{3\delta},E^{c}\cap Q_{3\delta}^{\prime})\\
&\geq 3^{-2n}\sum_{Q_{\delta}\in\text{{R}}_{\delta}}\mathcal{L}(E\cap Q_{3\delta},E^{c}\cap Q_{3\delta})\\
&\geq 3^{-2n}\sum_{Q_{\delta}\in\text{{Q}}_{\ \delta}}\mathcal{L}(E\cap Q_{3\delta},E^{c}\cap Q_{3\delta})\\
&\geq 3^{-2n}C_{1}N_{\delta}\delta^{n-s}.\end{split}

(2.15)

Now, let us consider the set

A:=\begin{cases}B_{1}\quad&\text{in }B_{1},\\
E\quad&\text{in }B_{1}^{c}.\end{cases}

Then, the almost minimality of
E
gives that

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(E\cap B_{1},E^{c}\cap B_{1})\leq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,B_{1})\leq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(A,B_{1})+\Lambda\omega_{n}\\

From this and (

), we obtain that

3^{-2n}C_{1}N_{\delta}\delta^{n-s}\leq C_{2},

and this concludes the proof of (
2.13
), as desired.

2.3
Euler-Lagrange Inequalities

As established in
[
11
]
, if
E
is a set of minimal
s
-perimeter, then a weak-formulation of the Euler-Lagrange Equation for the associated minimization problem (in the viscosity sense) takes the form

H_{s}[E](x):=p.v.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\chi_{E}(y)-\chi_{E^{c}}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy=0,\quad\text{ for a.e. }x\in(\partial E)\cap\Omega,

(2.16)

and
H_{s}[E]
is called non-local mean curvature of
\partial E
.

We now show that when the minimality requirement is relaxed to
almost minimality, we are still able to deduce Euler-Lagrange Inequalities, according to the following statements.

Theorem 2.6

(Euler-Lagrange Inequality)
.

Let
E
be a set satisfying the
\Lambda
-super-solution property in
\Omega
, for some
\Lambda\geq 0
. Suppose that
x_{0}\in\partial E
and that
E\cap\Omega
has an interior tangent ball
B
at
x_{0}
.

Then,

\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E^{c}}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}dx\leq\Lambda.

Observe that if
E
satisfies the
\Lambda
-sub-solution property and
E\cap\Omega
has an exterior tangent ball, then
E^{c}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
2.6
. Therefore, using also the properties of
\limsup
, we deduce the following:

Corollary 2.7

(Reverse Euler-Lagrange Inequality)
.

Let
E
be a set satisfying the
\Lambda
-sub-solution property in
\Omega
, for some
\Lambda\geq 0
. Suppose that
x_{0}\in\partial E
and that
E\cap\Omega
has an exterior tangent ball
B
at
x_{0}
.

Then,

\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E^{c}}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}dx\geq-\Lambda.

Remark 2.8
.

Up to a translation, one can always suppose that

A_{\varepsilon}
in two sets:

\begin{split}&A_{\varepsilon}^{1}:=\big\{y\in A_{\varepsilon}\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;|x-y|\geq|x|\big\}\\
{\mbox{and}}\quad&A_{\varepsilon}^{2}:=\big\{y\in A_{\varepsilon}\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;|x-y|<|x|\big\}.\end{split}

We observe that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|A_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{B_{\delta}^{c}}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}^{1}}c\frac{\big||x-y|-|x|\big|}{|\xi|^{n+s+1}}\,dy\,dx\leq\frac{1}{|A_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{B_{\delta}^{c}}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}^{1}}\frac{c\,\varepsilon}{|x|^{n+s+1}}\,dy\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\int_{B_{\delta}^{c}}\frac{c\,\varepsilon}{|x|^{n+s+1}}\,dx=\frac{\omega_{n}}{1+s}c\,\varepsilon\delta^{-1-s},\end{split}

(2.23)

up to renaming
c
.

Furthermore,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{|A_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{B_{\delta}^{c}}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}^{2}}c_{n,s}\frac{\big||x-y|-|x|\big|}{|\xi|^{n+s+1}}\,dy\,dx\leq&\frac{1}{|A_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{B_{\delta}^{c}}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\frac{c\,\varepsilon}{|x-y|^{n+s+1}}\,dy\,dx\\
\leq&\frac{1}{|A_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{\delta}^{c}}\frac{c\,\varepsilon}{|x-y|^{n+s+1}}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

Since
A_{\varepsilon}\subseteq B_{8\varepsilon}\subseteq B_{\delta/2}
, it follows that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|A_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{B_{\delta}^{c}}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}^{2}}c_{n,s}\frac{\big||x-y|-|x|\big|}{|\xi|^{n+s+1}}\,dy\,dx\leq\frac{1}{|A_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{\delta/2}^{c}}\frac{c\,\varepsilon}{|z|^{n+s+1}}\,dz\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad=\int_{B_{\delta/2}^{c}}\frac{c\,\varepsilon}{|z|^{n+s+1}}\,dz=\frac{\omega_{n}}{1+s}2^{1+s}c\,\varepsilon\delta^{-1-s}.\end{split}

From this and (
2.23
), together with (
2.22
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
E
be a set with an interior tangent ball at
0\in\partial E\cap\Omega
. Let
V_{R,\varepsilon}

and
A_{\varepsilon}^{-}
be
as in (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) respectively.

Then, there exists an infinitesimal sequence
\varepsilon_{k}
such that

\mathcal{L}\big(A_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{-},V_{R,{\varepsilon_{k}}}^{c}\big)\leq c\varepsilon_{k}^{-(1+s)/2}|A_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{-}|,

for some positive constant
c
depending only on
n
and
s
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and define
r_{x,\varepsilon}:=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial V_{R,\varepsilon})
.
For every
r\in(0,\varepsilon)
, we define the function
m(r):=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\cap\partial V_{R,r})
.

We point out that
m(r)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E^{c}\cap\partial V_{R,r})
, since, for all
r\in(0,\varepsilon)
,

A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\cap\partial V_{R,r}=V_{R,\varepsilon}\cap E^{c}\cap\partial V_{R,r}=E^{c}\cap\partial V_{R,r}.

This says that the function
m
does not depend on
\varepsilon
.

Now, if
x\in A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\subseteq V_{R,\varepsilon}
, we have that

\int_{V_{R,\varepsilon}^{c}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{x,\varepsilon}}(x)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}=C\int_{r_{x,\varepsilon}}^{+\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{1+s}}=\frac{C}{(r_{x,\varepsilon})^{s}}.

Thus, integrating over
A_{\varepsilon}^{-}
, we infer that

\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},V_{R,\varepsilon}^{c})\leq C\int_{A_{\varepsilon}^{-}}\frac{dx}{(r_{x,\varepsilon})^{s}}.

(2.24)

Notice that, by construction,

A_{\varepsilon}^{-}=V_{R,\varepsilon}\setminus E\subseteq V_{R,\varepsilon}\setminus B_{2R}(-2Re_{n}).

Therefore, if
x\in A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\cap\partial V_{R,r}
, for some
r\in(0,\varepsilon)
, we have that

r_{x,\varepsilon}\geq\operatorname{dist}(\mathbb{E}_{R,\varepsilon},\mathbb{E}_{R,r})\geq\frac{1}{1+c}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-r^{2}}{R},

(2.25)

where
c>0
is a constant depending only on the dimension
n
, and

\mathbb{E}_{R,r}:=\left\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;|x+Re_{n}|=R+\frac{r^{2}}{R}\left(1-\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\right\}\setminus B_{2R}(-2Re_{n}).

To have an idea of the situation described here, see Figure
1
. Full details to obtain the second inequality in (
2.25
) are contained in Section
D
.

Now, let us define the function
\psi
as

\psi(x^{\prime},x_{n}):=\left(|x^{\prime}|^{2}-R^{2}+R|x+Re_{n}|\right)^{1/2},

so that
\psi^{-1}(r)=\partial V_{R,r}\cap A_{\varepsilon}^{-}
, for every
r\in(0,\varepsilon)
.

Moreover, recalling that
A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\subseteq B_{2\varepsilon}
, for every
(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\cap\partial V_{R,r}
we have that

\begin{split}|\nabla\psi(x^{\prime},x_{n})|&\geq|\partial_{n}\psi(x^{\prime},x_{n})|=\frac{R}{2\psi(x^{\prime},x_{n})}\frac{|x_{n}+R|}{|x+Re_{n}|}=\frac{R}{2r}\frac{|x_{n}+R|}{|x+Re_{n}|}\\
&\geq\frac{R}{2r}\frac{R-2\varepsilon}{R+2\varepsilon}\geq\frac{R}{4r}\geq\frac{1}{4r},\end{split}

(2.26)

as long as
\varepsilon
is small enough.

Therefore, using (
2.25
) and (
2.26
) and the co-area formula, we can rewrite (
2.24
) as

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},V_{R,\varepsilon}^{c})&\leq C\int_{A_{\varepsilon}^{-}}\frac{|\nabla\psi(x)|}{(r_{x,\varepsilon})^{s}}\frac{dx}{|\nabla\psi(x)|}=C\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\cap\partial V_{R,r}}\frac{1}{(r_{x,\varepsilon})^{s}}\frac{d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\,dr}{|\nabla\psi(x)|}\\
&\qquad\leq C\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\cap\partial V_{R,r}}\frac{1}{(\varepsilon^{2}-r^{2})^{s}}\frac{d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\,dr}{|\nabla\psi(x)|}\\
&\qquad\leq C\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\cap\partial V_{R,r}}\frac{r^{1-s}}{(\varepsilon-r)^{s}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\,dr\\
&\qquad\leq C\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{m(r)}{(\varepsilon-r)^{s}}\,dr,\end{split}

(2.27)

up to renaming
C>0
line after line.

Accordingly, the desired result is proved if we show that there exists in infinitesimal sequence
\varepsilon_{k}
such that

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{k}}\frac{m(r)}{(\varepsilon_{k}-r)^{s}}\,dr\leq\varepsilon_{k}^{-(1+s)/2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{k}}m(r)\,dr,

(2.28)

since this, together with (
2.27
), will imply that

\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{-},V_{R,\varepsilon_{k}}^{c})\leq C\varepsilon_{k}^{-(1+s)/2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{k}}m(r)\,dr=C\varepsilon_{k}^{-(1+s)/2}|A_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{-}|.

Hence, from now on, we focus on the proof of (
2.28
). To this end, we argue by contradiction and assume that
there exists
\varepsilon^{*}\in(0,1)
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^{*})
,

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{m(r)}{(\varepsilon-r)^{s}}\,dr>\varepsilon^{-(1+s)/2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}m(r)\,dr.

Thus, integrating in
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^{*})
,

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}}\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{m(r)}{(\varepsilon-r)^{s}}\,dr\right)\,d\varepsilon>\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}}\left(\varepsilon^{-(1+s)/2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}m(r)\,dr\right)\,d\varepsilon.

Using the Fubini-Tonelli’s Theorem, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}}\left(\int_{r}^{\varepsilon^{*}}\frac{m(r)}{(\varepsilon-r)^{s}}\,d\varepsilon\right)\,dr

\displaystyle>\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}}m(r)\left(\int_{r}^{\varepsilon^{*}}\varepsilon^{-(1+s)/{2}}\,d\varepsilon\right)\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}}\frac{2m(r)}{1-s}\Big((\varepsilon^{*})^{(1-s)/{2}}-r^{(1-s)/{2}}\Big)\,dr.

Since the integrand is positive, we have that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}}\left(\int_{r}^{\varepsilon^{*}}\frac{m(r)}{(\varepsilon-r)^{s}}\,d\varepsilon\right)\,dr&>\frac{2}{1-s}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}/2}m(r)\Big((\varepsilon^{*})^{(1-s)/{2}}-(\varepsilon^{*}/2)^{(1-s)/{2}}\Big)\,dr\\
&=\frac{2}{1-s}\big(1-2^{(s-1)/2}\big)\,(\varepsilon^{*})^{(1-s)/{2}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}/2}m(r)\,dr.\end{split}

We also observe that

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}}\left(\int_{r}^{\varepsilon^{*}}\frac{m(r)}{(\varepsilon-r)^{s}}\,d\varepsilon\right)\,dr=\frac{1}{1-s}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}}m(r)(\varepsilon^{*}-r)^{1-s}\,dr\leq\frac{(\varepsilon^{*})^{1-s}}{1-s}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}}m(r)\,dr.

From the last two displays, we thus obtain that

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}}m(r)\,dr>2\big(1-2^{(s-1)/2}\big)\,(\varepsilon^{*})^{-\frac{1-s}{2}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}/2}m(r)\,dr\geq M\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{*}/2}m(r)\,dr,

(2.29)

for every
M\leq 2(1-2^{(s-1)/2})(\varepsilon^{*})^{-\frac{1-s}{2}}
.

Now, we take
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large and, for any
k\geq k_{0}
, we use (
2.29
)
with
\varepsilon^{*}:=2^{1-k}
. In this way, we obtain that

\int_{0}^{2^{-k}}m(r)\,dr<M^{-1}\int_{0}^{2^{-k+1}}m(r)\,dr<\dots<M^{k_{0}-k}\int_{0}^{2^{-k_{0}}}m(r)\,dr.

(2.30)

By the co-area formula, for every
k\geq k_{0}
,

\int_{0}^{2^{-k}}m(r)\,dr=|V_{R,2^{-k}}\cap E^{c}|.

In particular,

\int_{0}^{2^{-k_{0}}}m(r)\,dr=|E^{c}\cap V_{R,2^{-k_{0}}}|\leq|B_{2R}(-Re_{n})\cap B_{R}^{c}(-Re_{n})|=:c_{n,R}.

(2.31)

Also, by the uniform density estimates in Theorem
2.2
, we have that

|V_{R,2^{-k}}\cap E^{c}|\geq|E^{c}\cap B_{2^{-k}}(-Re_{n})|\geq c_{0}2^{-nk}.

This, (
2.30
) and (
2.31
) lead us to

c_{0}2^{-nk}<c_{n,R}M^{k_{0}-k}.

Now, if
k_{0}
is large enough,
it holds that
2^{n+1}\leq 2(1-2^{(s-1)/2})2^{-\frac{(1-k)(1-s)}{2}}
.
As a result, we can take
M:=2^{n+1}
and we have that

c_{0}2^{k}<c_{n,R}2^{k_{0}(n+1)}.

Taking

k:=\log_{2}\left(\frac{2c_{n,R}2^{k_{0}(n+1)}}{c_{0}}\right),

we obtain the desired contradiction.
∎

With this preliminary work, we can now complete the proof of Theorem
2.6
.

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

Let
\varepsilon
and
\delta
be such that
0<16\varepsilon<\delta<\min\{R,\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)\}
. Therefore, we have that
A_{\varepsilon}\subseteq B_{8\varepsilon}\subseteq B_{\delta/2}\subseteq B_{\delta}\subseteq V_{2R,\varepsilon}\setminus V_{0,\varepsilon}
.

Using the
\Lambda
-super-solution property of
E
with
A_{\varepsilon}
(see (
1.2
)), we obtain that

\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},E)-\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},E^{c}\setminus A_{\varepsilon})\leq\Lambda|A_{\varepsilon}|.

(2.32)

Now, we define the set

F_{\varepsilon,\delta}:=T_{\varepsilon}\left((E^{c}\setminus A_{\varepsilon})\cap B_{\delta}\right)

and we point out that

T_{\varepsilon}(B_{\delta}\setminus V_{R,\varepsilon})\subseteq B_{\delta}.

(2.33)

Indeed, for every
x\in B_{\delta}\setminus V_{R,\varepsilon}
consider the hyperplane

\Pi_{x}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;\left\langle\left(z-Re_{n}+\big(R+\mbox{d}_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\frac{x+Re_{n}}{|x+Re_{n}|}\right),(x+Re_{n})\right\rangle=0\right\}.

Then, the reflection of
x
with respect to
\Pi_{x}
is
T_{\varepsilon}x
and we have that
\Pi_{x}
separates
x
and the origin. Hence,
|T_{\varepsilon}x|\leq|x|\leq\delta
, which gives (
2.33
).

In particular, since

\begin{split}(E^{c}\setminus A_{\varepsilon})\cap B_{\delta}&=E^{c}\cap V_{R,\varepsilon}^{c}\cap(T_{\varepsilon}A_{\varepsilon}^{-})^{c}\cap B_{\delta}\\
&=E^{c}\cap V_{R,\varepsilon}^{c}\cap(T_{\varepsilon}E^{c})^{c}\cap B_{\delta},\end{split}

we have that

(E^{c}\setminus A_{\varepsilon})\cap B_{\delta}\cap T_{\varepsilon}E^{c}=\varnothing,

which yields that

F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\cap E^{c}=\varnothing.

Hence, from this and (
2.33
), we conclude that
F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\subseteq E\cap B_{\delta}
.

Therefore, we rewrite the left-hand side in (
2.32
) as

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},E)-\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},E^{c}\setminus A_{\varepsilon})\\
&\qquad\qquad=\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},E\setminus B_{\delta})-\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},E^{c}\setminus B_{\delta})\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad+\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})-\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},(E^{c}\setminus A_{\varepsilon})\cap B_{\delta}))+\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},(E\cap B_{\delta})\setminus F_{\varepsilon,\delta})\\
&\qquad\qquad=\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},E\setminus B_{\delta})-\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},E^{c}\setminus B_{\delta})\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad+\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})-\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},T_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon,\delta})+\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},(E\cap B_{\delta})\setminus F_{\varepsilon,\delta})\\
&\qquad\qquad\geq\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},E\setminus B_{\delta})-\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},E^{c}\setminus B_{\delta})

\displaystyle{\mbox{and }}\quad\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})-\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon},T_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}).

We deduce from (
2.32
) that

\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}\leq\Lambda|A_{\varepsilon}|.

(2.34)

Now, thanks to Lemma
2.10
,

\left|\frac{\mathcal{I}_{1}}{|A_{\varepsilon}|}-\int_{B_{\delta}^{c}}\frac{\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E^{c}}(x)}{|x|^{n+s}}\,dx\right|\leq C\varepsilon\delta^{-1-s},

hence, from (
2.34
) we get that

\int_{B_{\delta}^{c}}\frac{\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E^{c}}(x)}{|x|^{n+s}}dx\leq C\varepsilon\delta^{-1-s}-\frac{\mathcal{I}_{2}}{|A_{\varepsilon}|}+\Lambda.

(2.35)

Now, our goal is to estimate
\mathcal{I}_{2}
. To do this, recall that
A_{\varepsilon}=S_{\varepsilon}\dot{\cup}D_{\varepsilon}
. Then,

\mathcal{I}_{2}=\left[\mathcal{L}(S_{\varepsilon},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})-\mathcal{L}(S_{\varepsilon},T_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon,\delta})\right]+\left[\mathcal{L}(D_{\varepsilon},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})-\mathcal{L}(D_{\varepsilon},T_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon,\delta})\right].

(2.36)

Recalling that
S_{\varepsilon}=T_{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon}
and
changing variable with the reflection
T_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain that

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(S_{\varepsilon},T_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon,\delta})=\mathcal{L}(T_{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon},T_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon,\delta})=\int_{T_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\int_{T_{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad=\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\int_{S_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\left|\det\big(DT_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\right|\,\left|\det\big(DT_{\varepsilon}(y)\big)\right|}{|T_{\varepsilon}x-T_{\varepsilon}y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

(2.37)

Thanks to the properties of
T_{\varepsilon}
(see formula (
C.1
) in Appendix
C
), we have that

\|DT_{\varepsilon}(x)-\mathcal{R}_{x}\|\leq\frac{2}{R}\big(3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|\big),\quad\mbox{for every }x\in S_{\varepsilon},

(2.38)

where
\mathcal{R}_{x}
is the reflection with the respect to the line

\left\{t\frac{x+Re_{n}}{|x+Re_{n}|}\mbox{ such that }t\in\mathbb{R}\right\},

and it is defined by components as

\left(\mathcal{R}_{x}\right)_{i}(y):=-y_{i}+2\frac{(x+Re_{n})_{i}}{|x+Re_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x+Re_{n})_{j}y_{j},\quad\mbox{for all }i\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

(2.39)

Notice that the term in the right-hand side of (
2.38
) is at most of order
\delta
for every
x\in S_{\varepsilon}\subseteq B_{\delta}
(indeed, thanks to Lemma
2.9
, we have that
r_{x,\varepsilon}\leq\delta
).

In particular, if
\delta
is small enough, we deduce from (
2.38
) that
\|DT_{\varepsilon}(x)-\mathcal{R}_{x}\|\leq 2
.
Hence,

\begin{split}&\left|\det\left(DT_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)\right|\leq\big(1+\|DT_{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{R}_{x}\|\big)^{n}\leq 1+n!\,2^{n}\|DT_{\varepsilon}(x)-\mathcal{R}_{x}\|\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq 1+\frac{c_{n}}{R}\big(3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|\big).\end{split}

Similarly, let
y\in F_{\varepsilon,\delta}
. Recall that
F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\subseteq B_{\delta}
and notice that
z:=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{R}e_{n}\in B_{\delta}\cap\partial V_{R,\varepsilon}
. Thus, we have that
r_{y,\varepsilon}\leq|y-z|\leq 2\delta
. Thus, we deduce that also
\frac{2}{R}(3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|)\leq C\delta
.

In particular,
\|DT_{\varepsilon}(y)-\mathcal{R}_{y}\|\leq 2
, hence

|\det\big(DT_{\varepsilon}(y)\big)|\leq 1+\frac{c_{n}}{R}\big(3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\big).

Therefore, from (
2.37
) we obtain that

\mathcal{L}(S_{\varepsilon},T_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon,\delta})\leq\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\int_{S_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1+\frac{c_{n}}{R}\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}}{|T_{\varepsilon}x-T_{\varepsilon}y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy.

Using (
2.20
), we also have

|T_{\varepsilon}x-T_{\varepsilon}y|^{-n-s}\leq\left[1-\frac{2}{R}\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}\right]^{-n-s}|x-y|^{-n-s}.

Since
\frac{2}{R}(3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|)\leq C\delta
and
\frac{2}{R}(3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|)\leq C\delta
, the function

\left[1-\frac{2}{R}\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}\right]^{-n-s}

is uniformly bounded in
x
and
y
, up to choosing
\delta
small enough.

Now we observe that the function
\frac{1+\eta}{(1-\eta)^{n+s}}
is bounded for
\eta
sufficiently small. As a result, there exists a positive constant
c
such that
\frac{1+\eta}{(1-\eta)^{n+s}}\leq 1+c\eta
. Thus, using this with
\eta:=\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}
, we deduce that

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(S_{\varepsilon},TF_{\varepsilon,\delta})\leq\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\int_{S_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1+\frac{c_{n}}{R}\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad=\mathcal{L}(S_{\varepsilon},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})+\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\int_{S_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\frac{c_{n}}{R}\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\ .\end{split}

(2.40)

A similar argument can be applied for the terms involving
D_{\varepsilon}
by proceeding as follows. Since
D_{\varepsilon}\subseteq A_{\varepsilon}\subseteq B_{\delta}
, considering the reflection with respect to the hyperplane
\Pi_{y}
, we have that
T_{\varepsilon}y
is the reflection of
y
with respect to
\Pi_{y}
and
\Pi_{y}
separates
x
and
T_{\varepsilon}y
. Thus, we obtain the estimate
|x-T_{\varepsilon}y|\geq|x-y|
, whence

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(D_{\varepsilon},T_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon,\delta})=\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\frac{\left|\det\big(DT_{\varepsilon}(y)\big)\right|}{|x-T_{\varepsilon}y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\leq\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\frac{1+\frac{c_{n}}{R}\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2y^{\prime}|\}}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad=\mathcal{L}(D_{\varepsilon},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})+\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\frac{\frac{c_{n}}{R}\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

Using this and (
2.40
), together with (
2.36
), we conclude that

-\mathcal{I}_{2}\leq\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\frac{\frac{c_{n}}{R}\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy.

(2.41)

Now, to get to the desired result, we need sharper estimates for the term
\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+|y^{\prime}|\}
. To this end, notice that if
x\in A_{\varepsilon}
, then
|x|\leq 8\varepsilon
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.9
, we have that
r_{x,\varepsilon}\leq 9\varepsilon
. Using also the simple estimates

|y^{\prime}|\leq|x^{\prime}|+|x-y|\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad r_{y,\varepsilon}\leq r_{x,\varepsilon}+|x-y|,

we find that

\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}\leq\begin{cases}(6n+2)|x-y|+16\varepsilon,&\text{if }|x-y|\geq 2r_{x,\varepsilon},\\
Cn\varepsilon,&\text{if }|x-y|<2r_{x,\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

From this, we obtain that

\begin{split}&\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\setminus B_{2r_{x,\varepsilon}}(x)}\frac{\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy\leq C_{1}\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\setminus B_{2r_{x,\varepsilon}}(x)}\frac{|x-y|+\varepsilon}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq C_{1}\left(\int_{B_{\delta}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s-1}}+\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\setminus B_{2r_{x,\varepsilon}}(x)}\frac{\varepsilon}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq C_{1}\left(\delta^{1-s}+\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\setminus B_{2r_{x,\varepsilon}}(x)}\frac{\varepsilon}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy\right),\end{split}

and

\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\cap B_{2r_{x,\varepsilon}}(x)}\frac{\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy\leq C_{1}\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\cap B_{2r_{x,\varepsilon}}(x)}\frac{\varepsilon}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy,

up to renaming
C_{1}>0
, that depends only on
n
and
s
.

Therefore, we deduce that

\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\frac{\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy\leq C_{1}\left(\delta^{1-s}+\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\frac{\varepsilon}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy\right).

(2.42)

Integrating (
2.42
) over
A_{\varepsilon}
, we arrive at

\begin{split}&\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\frac{\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq C_{1}\big(\delta^{1-s}|A_{\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})+\varepsilon\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{+},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})\big).\end{split}

(2.43)

The next step is estimating
\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{+},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})
in terms of
\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})
. Since
T_{\varepsilon}A_{\varepsilon}^{+}\subseteq A_{\varepsilon}^{-}
, changing variable with
T_{\varepsilon}
, we arrive at

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{+},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})&=\int_{T_{\varepsilon}A_{\varepsilon}^{+}}\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\frac{|\det\left(DT_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)|}{|T_{\varepsilon}x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leq\int_{A_{\varepsilon}^{-}}\int_{F_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\frac{1+\frac{c_{n}}{R}\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leq(1+C_{2}\varepsilon)\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})+C_{2}\delta^{1-s}|A_{\varepsilon}^{-}|,\end{split}

(2.44)

for some positive
C_{2}
depending on
n
and
s
.

Using (
2.43
) and (
2.44
) in (
2.41
), we are led to

-\mathcal{I}_{2}\leq\frac{C_{3}}{R}\big(\delta^{1-s}|A_{\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})\big),

(2.45)

for some
C_{3}>0
depending only on
n
and
s
.

We now show that
\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})
is controlled by
\varepsilon^{-(s+1)/2}
.
Since
F_{\varepsilon,\delta}\subseteq E
, using also the
\Lambda
-super-solution property of
E
with
A_{\varepsilon}^{-}
, we obtain that

\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})\leq\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},E)\leq\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},E^{c}\setminus A_{\varepsilon}^{-})+\Lambda|A_{\varepsilon}^{-}|.

We also see that
E^{c}\setminus A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\subseteq V_{R,\varepsilon}
, thus

\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},F_{\varepsilon,\delta})\leq\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-},V_{R,\varepsilon})+\Lambda|A_{\varepsilon}^{-}|.

Thanks to Lemma
2.11
, we know that there
exists an infinitesimal sequence
\varepsilon_{k}
such that

\mathcal{L}(A_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{-},V_{R,\varepsilon_{k}}^{c})\leq c|A_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{-}|\varepsilon_{k}^{-(s+1)/2}.

This with (
2.45
) gives that

\begin{split}-\mathcal{I}_{2}\leq&\frac{C_{3}}{R}\Big(\delta^{1-s}|A_{\varepsilon_{k}}|+c\varepsilon_{k}^{\frac{1-s}{2}}|A_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{-}|+\varepsilon_{k}\Lambda|A_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{-}|\Big)\\
\leq&\frac{C_{3}}{R}|A_{\varepsilon_{k}}|\Big(\delta^{1-s}+c\varepsilon_{k}^{\frac{1-s}{2}}+\varepsilon_{k}\Lambda\Big).\end{split}

(2.46)

From this and (
2.35
), we deduce that

\int_{B_{\delta}^{c}}\frac{\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E^{c}}(x)}{|x|^{n+s}}dx\leq C\varepsilon_{k}\delta^{-1-s}+\frac{C_{3}}{R}\Big(\delta^{1-s}+c\varepsilon_{k}^{\frac{1-s}{2}}+\varepsilon_{k}\Lambda\Big)+\Lambda.

Taking the limit as
k\to+\infty
, we conclude that

\int_{B_{\delta}^{c}}\frac{\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E^{c}}(x)}{|x|^{n+s}}dx\leq\frac{C_{3}}{R}\delta^{1-s}+\Lambda.

Taking now the
\limsup
as
\delta\to 0
, we conclude the proof of Theorem
2.6
.
∎

and

2.4
Harnack’s Inequality

Now, we use Theorem
2.6
to prove a Harnack’s type result. To this purpose, we introduce the notion of flatness for a set. For later convenience, we now define, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, the cylinder

C_{r}^{\nu}(x_{0}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mbox{ s.t. }|\pi_{\nu}(x-x_{0})|<r\},

where
\pi_{\nu}(x):=x-(x\cdot\nu)\nu
denotes the projection onto the hyperplane

.

When
\nu=e_{n}
, we will denote the vertical cylinder of radius

centered at
x_{0}
simply with

.
Also, when the cylinders are centered at the origin, we use the short notations
C_{r}^{\nu}:=C_{r}^{\nu}(0)

and
C_{r}:=C_{r}(0)
.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
h
,
r>0
and
x_{0}\in\partial E
. We say that a set

is
\left(\frac{h}{r}\right)
-flat in

if

\partial E\cap C_{r}^{\nu}(x_{0})\subseteq\big\{|(x-x_{0})\cdot\nu|<h\big\}.

Heuristically, we say that a set is
\left(\frac{h}{r}\right)
-flat in the cylinder
C_{r}^{\nu}(x_{0})
if it can be trapped in a slab of width
2h
in direction
\nu
.

Theorem 2.14

(Harnack’s Inequalities)
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Lambda\geq 0
.

Then, for any

and
\alpha\in(0,s)

and
k_{0}
, depending only on
n
,
s
,
\alpha
, and

(2^{-ks}\Lambda)
-minimal for

with

In what follows, we consider

Therefore, from (

2.5
Improvement of flatness

The Harnack type result discussed in Theorem
2.14
constitutes our main tool to prove that if
E
is an almost minimal set with controlled flatness in every ball of radius
2^{-j}
, for every
j\leq k_{0}
, then its flatness improves in every ball of smaller radius. We refer to this result as improvement of flatness.

Theorem 2.18

(Improvement of flatness)
.

Let
E
be
\Lambda
-minimal in
B_{1}
, for some
\Lambda\geq 0
, and assume that
0\in\partial E
.

Then, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, depending only on
n
,
s
,
\alpha
and
\Lambda
, such that if

\partial E\cap B_{2^{-j}}\subseteq\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{j}|\leq 2^{-j(1+\alpha)}\big\},\qquad{\mbox{for all\penalty\ $j\leq k$ and for some\penalty\ $k\geq k_{0}$,}}

(2.82)

where
\{\nu_{j}\}_{j=0}^{k}
is a family of unit vectors with
\nu_{0}=e_{n}
, then there exist unit vectors
\{\nu_{j}\}_{j\geq k+1}
such that

\partial E\cap B_{2^{-j}}\subseteq\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{j}|\leq 2^{-j(1+\alpha)}\big\},\qquad{\mbox{for all }}j\geq k+1.

Notice that, thanks to the scaling properties of the
s
-perimeter, if
E
is a
\Lambda
-minimal set in
B_{1}
, then
2^{k}E
is a
(2^{-ks}\Lambda)
-minimal set in
B_{2^{k}}
. Moreover, the flatness assumptions in (
2.82
) become

\partial(2^{k}E)\cap B_{2^{k-j}}\subseteq\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{j}|\leq 2^{k}2^{-j(1+\alpha)}\big\}=\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{j}|\leq 2^{-k\alpha}2^{(k-j)(1+\alpha)}\big\}.

Thus, we deal with a rescaled version of the improvement of flatness, from which Theorem
2.18
follows with an iterative argument.

Theorem 2.19

(Rescaled improvement of flatness)
.

Let
E
be
(2^{-ks}\Lambda)
-minimal in
B_{2^{k}}
, for some
\Lambda\geq 0
, and assume that
0\in\partial E
.

Then, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
depending only on
n
,
s
,
\alpha
, and
\Lambda
,
such that if, for some
k\geq k_{0}
, it holds that

\partial E\cap C_{2^{j}}\subseteq\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{j}|\leq 2^{-k\alpha}2^{j(1+\alpha)}\big\},\quad{\mbox{ for all }}j\in\{0,\dots,k\},

(2.83)

then there exists a unit normal
\nu_{-1}
such that

\partial E\cap C_{1/2}\subseteq\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{-1}|\leq 2^{-k\alpha-\alpha-1}\big\}.

The strategy to prove Theorem
2.19
relies on a contradiction argument. In particular, we consider a sequence of almost minimal sets
\{E_{k}\}_{k}
such that
E_{k}
is
2^{-(k-j)\alpha}
-flat in
C_{2^{j}}
, but
\partial E_{k}
cannot fit in any cylinder of height
2^{-(k+1)\alpha}
in
C_{1/2}
. Our goal is to show that there exists a subsequence
\{E_{k_{l}}\}_{l}
uniformly convergent to a subgraph of some linear function
u
, which leads to a contradiction.

With this intent, let us consider a sequence of
(2^{-ks}\Lambda)
-minimal sets
\{E_{k}\}_{k}
, for some
\Lambda\geq 0
. For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define
a_{k}:=2^{-k\alpha}
, and assume that there exists
d_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\partial E_{k}\cap C_{2^{h}}\subseteq\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{h}^{k}|\leq a_{k}2^{h(1+\alpha)}\big\},\quad{\mbox{for all }}h\in\{0,\dots,h_{k}\},

(2.84)

with
\nu_{0}^{k}=e_{n}
for every
k
.

Moreover, let us denote by
E_{k}^{*}
the vertical dilation of
E_{k}
of a factor

, i.e.

E_{k}^{*}:=\big\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;(x^{\prime},a_{k}x_{n})\in E_{k}\big\}.

To proceed with this argument, we need the following technical lemma that provides Hölder estimates for every set

.

Lemma 2.20

(Hölder estimates)
.

Let
\{E_{k}\}_{k}
be a sequence of
(2^{-ks}\Lambda)
-minimal sets, for some
\Lambda\geq 0
, satisfying (
2.84
).

For any

Assume that there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
k
such that
A_{k}^{r}\subseteq\{|x_{n}|\leq C\}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, depending only on
n
,
s
,
\alpha
, and
\Lambda
, such that, for all

,

A_{k}^{r}\cap\{|x^{\prime}|<r/2\}\subseteq\operatorname{subgraph}(g_{k}^{+})\setminus\operatorname{subgraph}(g_{k}^{-}),

(2.85)

where
g_{k}^{-}
and
g_{k}^{+}
are Hölder continuous functions with modulus of continuity
\theta t^{\eta}
, for some
\eta\in(0,1)
and

depending only on

above and
\delta_{0}
from Theorem
2.14

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and take

\Sigma_{k,j}:=A_{k}^{r}\cap\left\{|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|<\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\right)^{j}\right\}.

Notice that, since

\partial E^{*}_{k}\cap C_{2^{h}}\subseteq\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{h}^{k}|\leq 2^{h(1+\alpha)}\big\},\quad{\mbox{for all }}h\in\{0,\dots,h_{k}\},

then also

\Sigma_{k,j}=\Sigma_{k,j}\cap C_{2^{h}}\subseteq\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{h}^{k}|\leq 2^{h(1+\alpha)}\big\},\quad{\mbox{for all }}h\in\{0,\dots,h_{k}\}.

Accordingly, as we have shown in Corollary
2.17
, applying iteratively the Harnack’s Inequality (Theorem
2.14
), we obtain that

\Sigma_{k,j}\subseteq\left\{|x_{n}-z_{j}|<2^{2+\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}\right)^{j}\right\},\qquad{\mbox{for all }}j\in\{0,\dots,j_{k}\},

(2.86)

for some

, with
|z_{j}|\leq 1
for all

(and all

).
Notice also that

as

.

Now, we show that, for every
x\in A_{k}^{r}\cap\{|x^{\prime}|<r/2\}
,

|x_{n}-z_{j_{k}}|<\theta\max\{|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|,b_{k}\}^{\eta},

(2.87)

where

is an infinitesimal sequence of positive real numbers.

For this, we observe that, given

|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|<r
. We discern three cases depending on whether
|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|
is in between

and
\frac{1}{2}
, in between
\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\right)^{j_{k}}

\eta
and
\theta

.

Let us start from the case
\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\right)^{j_{k}}<|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|<\frac{1}{2}
. In this situation, there exists
j\leq j_{k}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\right)^{j+1}\leq|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|<\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\right)^{j}.

Moreover, from (
2.86
), we have that
|x_{n}-z_{j}|\leq 2^{2+\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}\right)^{j}
.
Therefore, setting

\eta:=\frac{\log(1-\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2})}{\log(\delta_{0}/2)}\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}\right)^{j}\leq\left(1-\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{\log(2|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|)}{\log(\delta_{0}/2)}-1}=\left(1-\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}\right)^{\eta\frac{\log(2|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|)}{\log(1-\delta_{0}^{2}/2)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\left(1-\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}\right)^{-1}2^{\eta}|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|^{\eta}.

Thus, defining
\theta:=2^{\eta}(1+2^{2+\alpha})\left(1-\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}\right)^{-1}
and also recalling (

) in this case.

Now, if
\frac{1}{2}\leq|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|<r

, where

3.1
Preliminaries for a monotonicity formula

In

Moreover, as anticipated in (

can be obtained as the convolution

between the trace function

Now, let us consider the functional
\mathrm{J}_{r}

&=[u]_{H^{s/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}-[u]_{H^{s/2}(B_{r}^{c})}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.3)

A simple computation shows that if

Also, in

\Omega

.

Proof.

We write

Lemma 3.3
.

The function
\Phi_{E}
is differentiable for almost every
r\in(0,R)
, and

\Phi_{E}^{\prime}(r)\geq 0,\quad\mbox{for a.e. }r\in(0,R).

Proof.

Thanks to Proposition
3.2
and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus for the Lebesgue integral (see e.g.
[
39
, Theorem 7.20]
),
\Phi_{E}
is differentiable for almost any
r\in(0,R)
with derivative

\displaystyle\Phi_{E}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=r^{s-n}\int_{\partial B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x,z)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x,z)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-(n-s)r^{s-n-1}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x,z)|^{2}\,dx\,dz+(n-s)\,\omega_{n}\,\hat{\Lambda}r^{s-1}

\displaystyle=r^{s-n}\Bigg(\int_{\partial B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x,z)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x,z)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{n-s}{r}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x,z)|^{2}\,dx\,dz+(n-s)\,\omega_{n}\,\hat{\Lambda}r^{n-1}\Bigg).

Therefore, in order to show that
\Phi_{E}^{\prime}(r)\geq 0

for almost any
r\in(0,R)
, it is sufficient to prove that

\int_{\partial B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x,z)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x,z)-\frac{n-s}{r}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x,z)|^{2}\,dx\,dz+(n-s)\,\omega_{n}\,\hat{\Lambda}r^{n-1}\geq 0.

With this aim, we consider the function

\widetilde{v}_{r}(x,z):=\begin{cases}\widetilde{u}\left(\frac{rx}{|(x,z)|},\frac{rz}{|(x,z)|}\right),&\quad\text{ if }0<|x|^{2}+z^{2}\leq r^{2},\\
\widetilde{u}(x,z),&\quad\text{ if }|x|^{2}+z^{2}>r^{2}.\end{cases}

The main idea of the argument that we present is now
to construct a suitable competitor for the almost minimality condition. Given a radius
r
, this competitor will be defined as a homogeneous rescaling inside the ball of radius
r
, while preserving the data outside the ball. However, some technical difficulties arise from taking traces from the extended half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
to the original ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. These difficulties stem from the fact that the trace is defined only up to sets of null
n
-dimensional Lebesgue
measure, whereas the construction of the competitor requires specifying values on a sphere. Consequently, for the traced competitor, the values along a sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are, in principle, not properly defined, since such a sphere has null
n
-dimensional measure.

To handle this issue, the argument relies on a delicate measure-theoretic selection: the radius
r
will be chosen from an appropriate set of full one-dimensional measure, ensuring that the corresponding sphere of radius
r
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
admits suitable homogeneous constructions compatible with the notion of trace. The precise details of this measure-theoretic argument are presented below.

We claim that

\begin{split}&{\mbox{the trace of\penalty\ $\widetilde{v}_{r}$ on\penalty\ $\{z=0\}$ is well-defined for almost every $r\in(0,R)$}}\\
&{\mbox{and
(up to sets of null $n$-dimensional Lebesgue measure)}}\\
&{\mbox{this trace is of the form\penalty\ $\chi_{F_{r}}-\chi_{F_{r}^{c}}$, for some set\penalty\ $F_{r}$ such that\penalty\ $F_{r}\setminus B_{r}=E\setminus B_{r}$. }}\end{split}

Indeed, by construction,
\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde{u})(x)=u(x)
for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is
this identity holds
for all
x\in{\mathcal{G}}
, for a suitable
set
{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
|\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}|=0
(where
|\cdot|
denotes the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure).

We point out that, by the co-area formula,

0=|\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}|=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{r}\setminus{\mathcal{G}})\,dr\geq 0.

As a result, there exists a set
{\mathcal{W}}\subseteq(0,+\infty)
such that
\mathcal{H}^{1}\big((0,+\infty)\setminus{\mathcal{W}}\big)=0
and

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{r}\setminus{\mathcal{G}})=0
for every
r\in{\mathcal{W}}
.

(3.7)

Now, for all
r\in{\mathcal{W}}
we define

{\mathcal{G}}_{r}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}{\mbox{ s.t. }}\frac{rx}{|x|}\in{\mathcal{G}}\right\}

and

\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{r}:={\mathcal{G}}_{r}\cap\partial B_{1}=\left\{x\in\partial B_{1}{\mbox{ s.t. }}rx\in{\mathcal{G}}\right\}=\frac{1}{r}{\mathcal{G}}\cap\partial B_{1}.

We remark that, for all
r\in{\mathcal{W}}
,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{r})=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{r}{\mathcal{G}}\cap\partial B_{1}\right)=\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\mathcal{G}}\cap\partial B_{r})=\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{r})=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{1}),

owing to (
3.7
).

Notice also that
{\mathcal{G}}_{r}
is a cone, namely
x\in{\mathcal{G}}_{r}
if and only if
tx\in{\mathcal{G}}_{r}
for all
t>0
.
As a result, for all
r_{2}>r_{1}>0
,

\begin{split}&|{\mathcal{G}}_{r}\cap(B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}})|=\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\mathcal{G}}_{r}\cap\partial B_{t})\,dt=\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}t^{n-1}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{t}{\mathcal{G}}_{r}\cap\partial B_{1}\right)\,dt\\
&\qquad=\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}t^{n-1}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\mathcal{G}}_{r}\cap\partial B_{1})\,dt=\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}t^{n-1}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{r})\,dt=\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}t^{n-1}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{1})\,dt\\
&\qquad=|B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}}|,\end{split}

showing that, for all
r\in{\mathcal{W}}
, we have that
|\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}_{r}|=0
(i.e. the set
{\mathcal{G}}_{r}
has full measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
).

Consequently, for all
r\in{\mathcal{W}}
,
the set
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{r}:={\mathcal{G}}_{r}\cap{\mathcal{G}}

has full measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Accordingly, if
r\in{\mathcal{W}}
and
x\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{r}
, then both
x
and
\frac{rx}{|x|}

lie in
{\mathcal{G}}
, and therefore

\widetilde{v}_{r}(x,0)=\begin{cases}\widetilde{u}\left(\frac{rx}{|x|},0\right)=\chi_{E}\left(\frac{rx}{|x|}\right)-\chi_{E^{c}}\left(\frac{rx}{|x|}\right),\quad&\text{if }0<|x|\leq r,\\
\widetilde{u}(x,0)=\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E^{c}}(x),\quad&\text{if }|x|>r,\end{cases}

is well-defined (and thus
\widetilde{v}_{r}(x,0)\in\{-1,1\}
).

In what follows, we fix
r\in(0,R)\cap{\mathcal{W}}
, and we write
\widetilde{v}:=\widetilde{v}_{r}
and
F:=F_{r}
for simplicity.

Now, exploiting Proposition
1.7
, we find that

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{v}(x,z)|^{2}\,dx\,dz&\geq\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x,z)|^{2}\,dx\,dz-\hat{\Lambda}|E\Delta F|\\
&\geq\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x,z)|^{2}\,dx\,dz-\omega_{n}\hat{\Lambda}r^{n}.\end{split}

(3.8)

Then, observe that
\widetilde{v}
is constant along the radial direction in
B_{r}\setminus\{0\}
. Hence,

\nabla\widetilde{v}(x,z)=\frac{r}{|(x,z)|}\nabla^{T}\widetilde{u}\left(\frac{r}{|(x,z)|}x,\frac{r}{|(x,z)|}z\right),\quad\mbox{in }B_{r}\setminus\{0\},

where
\nabla^{T}
is the tangential component of the gradient
\nabla
with respect to the unitary sphere.

Therefore, by Fubini-Tonelli’s Theorem, we have that

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{v}(x,z)|^{2}\,dx\,dz&=\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\partial B_{t}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{v}(x,z)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x,z)\,dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\partial B_{t}^{+}}\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}z^{1-s}\left|\nabla^{T}\widetilde{u}\left(\frac{r}{t}x,\frac{r}{t}z\right)\right|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x,z)\,dt.\end{split}

Using the change of variable
(\hat{x},\hat{z}):=\left(\frac{x}{t},\frac{z}{t}\right)
, we obtain that

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{v}(x,z)|^{2}\,dx\,dz&=\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\partial B_{1}^{+}}r^{2}t^{n-2}(t\hat{z})^{1-s}|\nabla^{T}\widetilde{u}(r\hat{x},r\hat{z})|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(\hat{x},\hat{z})\,dt\\
&=r^{2}\int_{0}^{r}t^{n-1-s}\,dt\int_{\partial B_{1}^{+}}\hat{z}^{1-s}|\nabla^{T}\widetilde{u}(r\hat{x},r\hat{z})|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(\hat{x},\hat{z})\\
&=\frac{r^{n+2-s}}{n-s}\int_{\partial B_{1}^{+}}\hat{z}^{1-s}|\nabla^{T}\widetilde{u}(r\hat{x},r\hat{z})|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(\hat{x},\hat{z}).\end{split}

From this, the change of variable
(x,z):=(r\hat{x},r\hat{z})
leads us to

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{v}(x,z)|^{2}\,dx\,dz&=\frac{r}{n-s}\int_{\partial B_{1}^{+}}(r\hat{z})^{1-s}|\nabla^{T}\widetilde{u}(r\hat{x},r\hat{z})|^{2}\,r^{n+1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(\hat{x},\hat{z})\\
&=\frac{r}{n-s}\int_{\partial B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla^{T}\widetilde{u}(x,z)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x,z).\end{split}

(3.9)

Plugging (
3.9
) into (
3.8
), we obtain that

\begin{split}\frac{r}{n-s}\int_{\partial B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x,z)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x,z)&\geq\frac{r}{n-s}\int_{\partial B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla^{T}\widetilde{u}(x,z)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x,z)\\
&\geq\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx\,dz-\omega_{n}\hat{\Lambda}r^{n}\ ,\end{split}

which gives that
\Phi_{E}^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
, as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Let
r_{1},r_{2}\in(0,R)
with
r_{1}<r_{2}
. Thanks to Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
, we infer that

\Phi_{E}(r_{2})-\Phi_{E}(r_{1})=\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\Phi_{E}^{\prime}(\rho)d\rho\geq 0,

showing the result.
∎

Another relevant property of
\Phi_{E}
is that
\Phi_{E}
is bounded for any
r
small enough. We point out that this is not a direct consequence of Theorem
1.9
. In fact, we want to avoid the pathological case in which
\Phi_{E}\equiv+\infty
.

In order to prove the boundedness of
\Phi_{E}
, it is convenient to introduce the function

\Xi_{E}(r):=r^{s-n}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx\,dz.

(3.10)

Hence,

\Phi_{E}(r)=\Xi_{E}(r)+\frac{n-s}{s}\omega_{n}\hat{\Lambda}r^{s}.

(3.11)

Theorem 3.4
.

If
E
is a
\Lambda
-minimal set in
B_{R}
, for some
R>0
, then
\Phi_{E}
is bounded for every
r\leq R/2
.

Proof.

In light of (
3.11
), it is sufficient to check that
\Xi_{E}
is bounded.

From
[
11
, Proposition 7.1]
,
used with
\Omega:=B_{1/2}^{n+1}
, we have that

\Xi_{E}(1/2)=2^{n-s}\int_{\Omega^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx\,dz\leq C2^{n-s}\mathrm{J}_{1}(u)=C\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,B_{1}).

Notice that
\Xi_{E}
is scale invariant, that is
\Xi_{E}(\mu r)=\Xi_{\mu E}(r)
, for every
\mu>0
.

Consider a set
A:=B_{2r}\cup(E\cap B_{2r}^{c})
. Since
E
is an almost minimal set in
B_{2r}\subseteq B_{R}
, we have that

\begin{split}&\Xi_{E}(r)=\Xi_{\frac{1}{2r}E}(1/2)\leq C\operatorname{Per}_{s}\left(\frac{1}{2r}E,B_{1}\right)=C(2r)^{s-n}\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,B_{2r})\\
&\qquad\leq C(2r)^{s-n}\left(\operatorname{Per}_{s}(A,B_{2r})+\Lambda(2r)^{n}\right)\leq C(2r)^{s-n}\left(\operatorname{Per}_{s}(B_{2r},\mathbb{R}^{n})+\Lambda(2r)^{n}\right)\\
&\qquad\leq C\left(\operatorname{Per}_{s}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n})+\Lambda(2r)^{s}\right).\end{split}

(3.12)

Therefore, for all
r\leq R/2
,

\Phi_{E}(r)\leq C\left(\operatorname{Per}_{s}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n})+(2r)^{s}\right)+\frac{n-s}{s}\omega_{n}\hat{\Lambda}r^{s}\leq C(1+R^{s}),

as desired.
∎

3.3
Tangent cones as blow-up limits

With the monotonicity formula from Theorem
1.9
in hand, we are able to study regular and singular points of
E
through a blow-up analysis. In particular, if
E
is an almost minimal set in
B_{1}
such that
0\in\partial E
, we consider the dilation
\lambda E
and study the limit set when
\lambda\to+\infty
. We start our discussion with a convergence result.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\{E_{k}\}_{k}
be a sequence of
\Lambda_{k}
-minimal sets in
B_{k}
. Assume that
E_{k}\to E
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}
, for some set
E

of finite fractional perimeter, and
\Lambda_{k}\to\Lambda\in[0,+\infty)
, as
k\to+\infty
.

Let
u_{k}:=\chi_{E_{k}}-\chi_{E_{k}^{c}}
and
u:=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}
, with extension
\widetilde{u}_{k}
and
\widetilde{u}
, respectively.

Then,

i.

\widetilde{u}_{k}\to\widetilde{u}
uniformly on every compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
;

ii.

\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k}\to\nabla\widetilde{u}
in
L^{2}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},z^{1-s}\,dx\,dz)
.

In particular,
\Phi_{E_{k}}\to\Phi_{E}
.

The proof of Proposition
3.5
is just a variation of the proof of
[
11
, Proposition 9.1]
. For the sake of completeness, it can be found in the Appendix
H
.

We now give the following:

Definition 3.6
.

A set
C
is a minimal cone if it is a
0
-homogeneous local minimizer for the
s
-perimeter, i.e.
C
is a minimizer for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\cdot,\Omega)
such that
\lambda C=C
, for every
\lambda>0
.

We point out that
E
is a minimal cone with vertex at the origin, that is the
(n+1)
-dimensional extension of
\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}
is a
0
-homogeneous function, if and only if the function
\Xi_{E}
, as defined in (
3.10
), is constant (see
[
11
, Corollary 8.2]
).

Now,
replacing
B_{k}
with
B_{\lambda_{k}}
, for some divergent sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k}
, in Proposition
3.5
, we infer that the limit set of a convergent blow-up sequence
\{E_{k}:=\lambda_{k}E\}_{k}
is a minimal cone, according to the following statement:

Theorem 3.7

(Blow up limit)
.

Let
E
be a
\Lambda
-minimal set in
B_{1}
such that
0\in\partial E
, and let
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k}
be a sequence of positive real numbers
such that
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
as
k\to+\infty
.

If there exists a set
C
such that
\lambda_{k}E\to C
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}
, then
C
is a minimal cone.

We call the minimal cone
C
in Theorem
3.7

a tangent cone to
E
at
0
.
This definition is well-posed in light of the forthcoming result, which guarantees the existence of a blow-up limit.

Theorem 3.8

(Existence of blow-up limits)
.

Let
E
be a
\Lambda
-minimal set in
B_{1}
such that
0\in\partial E
. Then, the blow-up sequence
\{\lambda_{k}E\}_{k}
admits limit (up to a subsequence).

Namely, there exists a tangent cone
C
to
E
at the origin.

We now present the proofs of Theorems
3.7
and
3.8
.

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.

We notice that
\lambda_{k}E
is a
\lambda_{k}^{-s}\Lambda
-minimal set
in
B_{\lambda_{k}}
. Hence, we are in the position of exploiting
Proposition
3.5
and, if
\lambda_{k}E\to C
, obtain that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Phi_{\lambda_{k}E}(r)=\Phi_{C}(r).

As a consequence, recalling the notation in (
3.10
),

\displaystyle\Phi_{C}(r)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Phi_{\lambda_{k}E}(r)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Xi_{\lambda_{k}E}(r)+\frac{n-s}{s}\omega_{n}\hat{\Lambda}r^{s}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Xi_{E}\left(\frac{r}{\lambda_{k}}\right)+\frac{n-s}{s}\omega_{n}\hat{\Lambda}r^{s}.

Now, since
\Phi_{E}
is positive and monotone increasing, we have that
\Phi_{E}(r/\lambda_{k})
admits limit as
k\to+\infty
, and therefore

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Xi_{E}\left(\frac{r}{\lambda_{k}}\right)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Phi_{E}\left(\frac{r}{\lambda_{k}}\right)-\frac{n-s}{s}\omega_{n}\hat{\Lambda}\left(\frac{r}{\lambda_{k}}\right)^{s}=\Phi_{E}(0)=\Xi_{E}(0).

As a result, we find that

\Phi_{C}(r)=\Xi_{E}(0)+\frac{n-s}{s}\omega_{n}\hat{\Lambda}r^{s},

and therefore

\Xi_{C}(r)=\Phi_{C}(r)-\frac{n-s}{s}\omega_{n}\hat{\Lambda}r^{s}=\Xi_{E}(0).

This shows that
\Xi_{C}
is constant, hence
C
is a minimal cone with vertex at
0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.8
.

Notice that if
E
is a
\Lambda
-minimal set in
B_{1}
, then
\lambda_{k}E
is a
\lambda_{k}^{-s}\Lambda
-minimal set in
B_{\lambda_{k}}
. Therefore, for
k
sufficiently large, we have that
\lambda_{k}E
is almost minimal in
B_{1}
.

Let us consider the set
A_{k}^{1}:=B_{1}\cup(\lambda_{k}E\cap B_{1}^{c})
. In this setting, we have that
|A_{k}^{1}\Delta\lambda_{k}E|\leq|B_{1}|
, and, by the almost-minimality of
\lambda_{k}E
,

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\lambda_{k}E}\|_{W^{1,s}(B_{1})}=\|\chi_{\lambda_{k}E}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}+[\chi_{\lambda_{k}E}]_{W^{1,s}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq|B_{1}|+2\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\lambda_{k}E,B_{1})\leq|B_{1}|+2\operatorname{Per}_{s}(A_{k}^{1},B_{1})+2\lambda_{k}^{-s}\Lambda|B_{1}|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq(1+2\Lambda)|B_{1}|+2\operatorname{Per}_{s}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n}),

provided that
k
is sufficiently large.

Using the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,s}(B_{1})\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{1}(B_{1})
, we extract a subsequence
\lambda_{k}^{1}
such that

(\lambda_{k}^{1}E)\cap B_{1}\to E_{1}\text{ in }L^{1}(B_{1})

for some set
E_{1}\subseteq B_{1}
. Repeating the argument with
\{\lambda_{k}^{1}E\}_{k}
in
B_{2}
, we find a subsequence
\{\lambda_{k}^{2}\}_{k}\subseteq\{\lambda_{k}^{1}\}_{k}
and a set
E_{2}\subseteq B_{2}
such that

(\lambda_{k}^{2}E)\cap B_{2}\to E_{2}\text{ in }L^{1}(B_{2}).

Moreover, by the uniqueness of the limit, we have that
E_{2}\cap B_{1}=E_{1}
.

Iterating this procedure
m
times, we obtain a subsequence
\{\lambda_{k}^{m}\}_{k}
such that
(\lambda_{k}^{m}E)\cap B_{m}\to E_{m}
in
L^{1}(B_{m})
as
k\to+\infty
, and
E_{m}\cap B_{j}=E_{j}
, for all
j=1,\dots,m-1
.

Using a diagonal argument, we extract a subsequence
\{\lambda_{k}^{k}\}_{k}
such that

\lambda_{k}^{k}E\to\bigcup_{m=1}^{+\infty}E_{m}=:C\text{ in }L^{1}_{\rm{loc}},

and this concludes the proof.
∎

3.4
Classification of tangent cones

Now, we show how the classification of the tangent cones to an almost minimal set allows to deduce regularity.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
E
be a
\Lambda
-minimal set in
B_{1}
such that
0\in\partial E
. Suppose that the tangent cone to
E
at
0
is a half-space.

Then, there exists
\delta>0
such that
\partial E\cap B_{\delta}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-regular surface.

The proof of Theorem
3.9
will be presented here below. Now, we give the definitions of regular and singular points.

Definition 3.10

(Definition 9.5,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
E
be a
\Lambda
-minimal set in
\Omega
and let
x\in\partial E\cap\Omega
. We say that
x
is a regular point for

if the tangent cone to
E
at
x
is a half-space.

We say that
x
is singular if it is not regular. We denote by
\Sigma_{E}
the set of singular points for

. In particular,
\Sigma_{E}
is a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In other words, half-spaces are the tangent cones with least local energy. Moreover, there exists an energy gap between regular and singular tangent cones. To state this result, we recall the definition of
\Xi_{E}
in (

).

Theorem 3.11

(Energy gap)
.

Let
\Pi:=\{x_{1}>0\}
.
Then, for every tangent cone

,

\Xi_{\Pi}\leq\Xi_{C},

(3.13)

and equality holds if and only if
C
is a half-space.

In particular, if

is not a half-space, then
there exists
\delta_{0}>0
, depending only on

and

, such that

\Xi_{\Pi}\leq\Xi_{C}-\delta_{0}.

(3.14)

Theorem
3.11
can be proved as
[

, up to using the clean ball condition for almost minimizers given in Corollary

). For the sake of completeness, a proof of the energy gap result for almost minimal boundaries can be found in Appendix

.

3.9
.

By assumptions, if we consider the blow-up sequence

4
Hausdorff dimension of the singular set and proof of Theorem
1.6

In this section, we focus on the proof of Theorem
1.6
. To do this, we follow Federer’s reduction method, as developed in
[
11
]
for the fractional setting. In particular, we exploit the fact that if
C
is a minimal cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a singular point
x_{0}\neq 0
, then there exists a minimal cone
C^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
such that the origin is a singular point for
C^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}
is a tangent cone to
C
at
x_{0}
. Applying this argument iteratively, we reduce to dimension
2
, where any
s
-minimal cone is a half-space.

4.1
Hausdorff dimension of the singular set

In
[
40
]
, the authors prove that there are no singular
s
-minimal cones in dimension
2
. The precise statement goes as follows:

Theorem 4.1

(Theorem 1,
[
40
]
)
.

If
C
is an
s
-minimal cone in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then
C
is a half-space.

In light of Theorem
4.1
above and
[
11
, Theorem 10.3]
(fractional dimension reduction method), we deduce that if
C
is a singular cone in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, its only singularity must be at the vertex.

Since we are interested in the Hausdorff dimension of the singular set of an almost minimizer, let us now recall some definitions.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
d>0
, and
\delta\in(0,+\infty]
, we set

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(E):=2^{-d}\omega_{d}\inf\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\operatorname{diam}^{d}(A_{j}):E\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{+\infty}A_{j},\ \operatorname{diam}(A_{j})<\delta\right\}.

We recall that the Hausdorff measure of
E
is defined as

\mathcal{H}^{d}(E):=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(E)=\sup_{\delta\in(0,+\infty]}\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(E),

and the Hausdorff dimension of
E
as

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E):=\inf\{d>0:\mathcal{H}^{d}(E)>0\}.

For a detailed discussion about the properties of the Hausdorff measure we refer to e.g.
[
29
, Chapter 11]
, and
[
26
, Chapter 2]
.

Now, we provide two auxiliary results that are useful for the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
The first one claims that if
\{E_{k}\}_{k}
is a sequence of almost minimal sets
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}
-convergent to an almost minimal set
E
, then the sequence of singular sets
\{\Sigma_{E_{k}}\}_{k}
is uniformly convergent to
\Sigma_{E}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\{E_{k}\}_{k}
be a sequence of
\Lambda_{k}
-minimal sets convergent to a
\Lambda
-minimal set
E
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}
.

Then, for every compact set
K\subseteq\Omega
and
\varepsilon>0
, we have that

\Sigma_{E_{k}}\cap K\subseteq\mathscr{U}_{\varepsilon}(\Sigma_{E}\cap K),\quad\text{ for every\penalty\ $k$ large enough.}

(4.1)

Remark 4.4
.

Notice that, by definition of convergence with respect to the Hausdorff measure, the uniform convergence condition is precisely

\Sigma_{E_{k}}\cap K\subseteq\mathscr{U}_{\varepsilon}(\Sigma_{E}\cap K),\quad\text{ for every\penalty\ $k$ large enough.}

From this, we arrive at

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(\Sigma_{E_{k}}\cap K)\leq\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(\Sigma_{E}\cap K).

(4.2)

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

Consider a compact set
K\subseteq\Omega
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Arguing by contradiction, suppose that for every
k
there exists
x_{k}\in\Sigma_{E_{k}}\cap K
such that
\operatorname{dist}(x_{k},\Sigma_{E}\cap K)\geq\varepsilon
.
By compactness, there exists
x_{\infty}\in K
such that
x_{k}\to x_{\infty}
, up to a subsequence.

Also, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
x_{k}\in\partial E_{k}
. Consequently, by Corollary
2.3
, we infer that
x_{\infty}\in\partial E
.

Now, suppose that
x_{\infty}
is a regular point. Hence, the tangent cone to
E
at
x_{\infty}
is a half-space. Therefore, up to rigid transformations, the uniform convergence result in Corollary
2.3
gives that

\partial E\cap B_{\delta}(x_{\infty})\subseteq\{|x_{n}-x_{\infty,n}|<\varepsilon_{0}/2\},

for the same
\varepsilon_{0}
as in Theorem
1.4
.

By Corollary
2.3
, we also have that

\partial E_{k}\cap B_{\delta/2}(x_{k})\subseteq\{|x_{n}-x_{k,n}|<\varepsilon_{0}\},\quad\text{ for every\penalty\ $k$ large enough.}

It follows from Theorem
1.4
that
x_{k}
is a regular point for
\partial E_{k}
, which is a contradiction. Thus, it must be
x_{\infty}\in\Sigma_{E}
. Hence, by definition of convergence,

x_{k}\in B_{\varepsilon/2}(x_{\infty})\subseteq\mathscr{U}_{\varepsilon}(\Sigma_{E}\cap K),\quad\text{ for every\penalty\ $k$ large enough.}

This is against our initial assumptions, proving (
4.1
).
∎

The second result is a technical lemma that guarantees the existence of a tangent cone whose singular set has a suitable Hausdorff dimension.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
E
be an almost minimal set such that
\mathcal{H}^{d}(\Sigma_{E})>0
, for some
d>0
.

Then, there exists a tangent cone
C
to
E
with
\mathcal{H}^{d}(\Sigma_{C})>0
.

Proof.

Let us consider an almost minimal set
E
such that
\mathcal{H}^{d}(\Sigma_{E})>0
. Thanks to the properties of the Hausdorff measure (see
[
29
, Proposition 11.3]
), there exist at least one point
x\in\Sigma_{E}
, and an infinitesimal sequence
\{r_{k}\}_{k}

such that

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(\Sigma_{E}\cap B_{r_{k}}(x))\geq\omega_{d}r_{k}^{d}2^{-d-1}.

(4.3)

Up to a rigid transformation, we assume that
x=0
. Then, if we consider the sequence of dilated sets
\{E_{k}=r^{-k}E\}_{k}
,
E_{k}
is almost minimal in
r_{k}^{-1}\Omega
, and, up to extracting a subsequence,
E_{k}
converges in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}
to a minimal cone
C
(see Theorem
3.8
). Moreover, by construction, we have that
\Sigma_{E_{k}}=r_{k}^{-1}\Sigma_{E}
.

We recall the scaling property of the Hausdorff measure (see e.g.
[
26
]
), that is, for every
d>0
and for every set
A
,

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(\lambda A)=\lambda^{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(A).

Therefore, using this in (
4.3
),

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(\Sigma_{E_{k}}\cap B_{1})=r_{k}^{-d}\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(\Sigma_{E}\cap B_{r_{k}})\geq\omega_{d}2^{-d-1}.

From the last inequality and (
4.2
), taking the limit as
k\to+\infty
, we deduce that
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(\Sigma_{C})>0
, which in turn entails that
\mathcal{H}^{d}(\Sigma_{C})>0
(see
[
29
, Lemma 11.2]
).
∎

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.6

Let us consider an almost minimal set
E
, and suppose that
\mathcal{H}^{d}(\Sigma_{E})>0
. Thanks to Lemma
4.5
, there exists a tangent cone
C
to
E
such that
\mathcal{H}^{d}(\Sigma_{C})>0
.

Consider a point
x_{0}\in\Sigma_{C}\setminus\{0\}
(we stress that a blow-up at
0
would give
C
itself) and define the sequence
\{C_{k}:=\lambda_{k}(C-x_{0})\}_{k}
, for some
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k}
such that
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
as
k\to+\infty
. Then, according to
[
11
, Theorem 10.3]
, its blow-up limit is a minimal cone
A_{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
A_{n-1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is a minimal cone in lower dimension. Since
C
is
s
-minimal, it is in particular almost minimal, hence applying Lemma
4.5
, we obtain

\mathcal{H}^{d}(\Sigma_{C})>0\implies\mathcal{H}^{d}(\Sigma_{A_{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}})>0\ \implies\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Sigma_{A_{n-1}})>0.

Using iteratively this procedure, we construct a sequence of minimal cones
A_{n-j}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n-j}
such that

\mathcal{H}^{d-j}(\Sigma_{A_{n-j}})>0,\quad\text{ for every }j\leq d.

Thanks to Theorem
4.1
, we conclude that
n-d>2
, that is
d\leq n-3
.

5
Proof of Theorem
1.11

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.11
, which states that almost minimality is preserved under suitable external perturbations. This fact is a direct consequence of the properties of the
s
-perimeter. The technical details go as follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

Let
\Omega=B^{n-1}_{1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Since
\Omega
is unbounded, recall that almost minimality in
\Omega
is defined as almost minimality in every bounded Lipschitz subset of
\Omega
. Therefore, for every
M>0
, we consider the family of truncated cylinders
\{\Omega_{M}\}_{M}
, where

\Omega_{M}:=B^{n-1}_{1}\times(-M,M).

In this setting, the fractional perimeter of a set
E
in
\Omega_{M}
is given by

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega_{M})=\mathscr{L}(E\cap\Omega_{M},E^{c})+\mathscr{L}(E\cap\Omega_{M}^{c},E^{c}\cap\Omega_{M}).

Now, we compute the fractional perimeter of the set
\widetilde{E}:=E\dot{\cup}\Sigma
in
\Omega_{M}
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\widetilde{E},\Omega_{M})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}(\widetilde{E}\cap\Omega_{M},\widetilde{E}^{c})+\mathscr{L}(\widetilde{E}\cap\Omega_{M}^{c},\widetilde{E}^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}\left((E\dot{\cup}\Sigma)\cap\Omega_{M},E^{c}\cap\Sigma^{c}\right)+\mathscr{L}\left((E\dot{\cup}\Sigma)\cap\Omega_{M}^{c},E^{c}\cap\Sigma^{c}\cap\Omega_{M}\right).

Therefore, since
\Sigma\subseteq E^{c}\cap\Omega_{M}^{c}
, we obtain that

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\widetilde{E},\Omega_{M})\\
&=\mathscr{L}(E\cap\Omega_{M},E^{c}\cap\Sigma^{c})+\mathscr{L}(E\cap\Omega_{M}^{c},E^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})+\mathscr{L}(\Sigma\cap\Omega_{M}^{c},E^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})\\
&=\mathscr{L}(E\cap\Omega_{M},E^{c})-\mathscr{L}(E\cap\Omega_{M},E^{c}\cap\Sigma)+\mathscr{L}(E\cap\Omega_{M}^{c},E^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})\\
&\qquad+\mathscr{L}(\Sigma\cap\Omega_{M}^{c},E^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})\\
&=\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega_{M})+\left[\mathscr{L}(\Sigma,E^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})-\mathscr{L}(E\cap\Omega_{M},\Sigma)\right].\end{split}

(5.1)

Let us consider a set
F
such that
F\setminus\Omega_{M}=E\setminus\Omega_{M}
. Hence, the set
\widetilde{F}:=F\dot{\cup}\Sigma
is such that
\widetilde{F}\setminus\Omega_{M}=\widetilde{E}\setminus\Omega_{M}
.
Moreover, from (
5.1
), we have that

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\widetilde{E},\Omega_{M})-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\widetilde{F},\Omega_{M})\\
&=\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega_{M})-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega_{M})-\left[\mathscr{L}(E\cap\Omega_{M},\Sigma)-\mathscr{L}(F\cap\Omega_{M},\Sigma)\right]\\
&\qquad\quad+\left[\mathscr{L}(\Sigma,E^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})-\mathscr{L}(\Sigma,F^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})\right].\end{split}

(5.2)

We observe that

\begin{split}&\left|\mathscr{L}(E\cap\Omega_{M},\Sigma)-\mathscr{L}(F\cap\Omega_{M},\Sigma)\right|=\left|\int_{\Sigma}\int_{\Omega_{M}}\frac{\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{F}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\right|\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\int_{\Sigma}\int_{\Omega_{M}}\frac{|\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{F}(x)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\leq\int_{\Sigma}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\chi_{E\Delta F}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

(5.3)

Since
E\Delta F=E^{c}\Delta F^{c}
, the same calculations also show that

\left|\mathscr{L}(\Sigma,E^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})-\mathscr{L}(\Sigma,F^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})\right|\leq\int_{\Sigma}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\chi_{E\Delta F}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy.

(5.4)

We now claim that

\int_{\Sigma}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\chi_{E\Delta F}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\leq\widetilde{c}|E\Delta F|,

(5.5)

for some
\widetilde{c}>0
depending only on
n
,
s
, and
\Sigma
.

To this purpose, decompose
\Sigma
in the sets

\Sigma_{1}:=\Sigma\cap\Big(\big(B^{n-1}_{1+\delta}\setminus\overline{B^{n-1}_{1}}\big)\times\mathbb{R}\Big)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Sigma_{2}:=\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{1},

where
\delta
is as in the assumptions. We point out that
\Sigma_{1}
is possibly empty.

Then, notice that, for every
x\in\Omega
and
y\in\Sigma_{1}
,

|x-y|\geq|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\geq|y^{\prime}|-|x^{\prime}|\geq|y^{\prime}|-1.

Moreover, in light of the assumptions in (
1.7
) and (
1.8
),
we have that
E=\operatorname{subgraph}(\psi)
in
\Omega^{c}
, for some
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
, and

\tau_{n}(\Sigma)\subseteq\big\{(y^{\prime},y_{n})\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;\psi(x^{\prime})<y_{n}<\psi(x)+C(|y^{\prime}|-1)^{n+s-1+\xi}\big\}\quad\text{ in }B^{n-1}_{1+\delta}\setminus\overline{B^{n-1}_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}.

As a result, we see that
\Sigma_{1}\subseteq\tau_{n}^{-1}(G_{C,\xi})
, and therefore

\begin{split}\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\chi_{E\Delta F}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy&\leq|(E\Delta F)|\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\frac{dy}{(|y^{\prime}|-1)^{n+s}}\\
&\leq|E\Delta F|\int_{\tau_{n}^{-1}(G_{C,\xi})}\frac{dy}{(|y^{\prime}|-1)^{n+s}}\\
&=|E\Delta F|\int_{B^{n-1}_{1+\delta}\setminus{B}^{n-1}_{1}}\int_{0}^{C(|y^{\prime}|-1)^{n+s-1+\xi}}\frac{dy_{n}dy^{\prime}}{(|y^{\prime}|-1)^{n+s}}\\
&=|E\Delta F|\int_{B^{n-1}_{1+\delta}\setminus{B}^{n-1}_{1}}C(|y^{\prime}|-1)^{\xi-1}\,dy^{\prime}\\
&=C_{1}|E\Delta F|,\end{split}

(5.6)

for some
C_{1}>0
, depending on
n
,
s
, and
\Sigma
.

Furthermore, notice that
\operatorname{dist}(\Sigma_{2},\Omega)\geq\delta>0
, and accordingly

\begin{split}&\int_{\Sigma_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\chi_{E\Delta F}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\leq\delta^{-n-s}\int_{\Sigma_{2}}\int_{\Omega_{M_{\Sigma}}}\chi_{E\Delta F}(x)\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\delta^{-n-s}|\Sigma|\,|(E\Delta F)|\leq C_{2}|E\Delta F|,\end{split}

for some
C_{2}>0
, depending on
n
,
s
, and
\Sigma
.

This and (
5.6
) give the desired claim in (
5.5
).

Using (
5.5
) into (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we obtain that

\begin{split}&\left|\mathscr{L}(\Sigma,E\cap\Omega_{M})-\mathscr{L}(\Sigma,F\cap\Omega_{M})\right|\leq\widetilde{c}|E\Delta F|\\
{\mbox{and }}\quad&\left|\mathscr{L}(\Sigma,E^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})-\mathscr{L}(\Sigma,F^{c}\cap\Omega_{M})\right|\leq\widetilde{c}|E\Delta F|.\end{split}

(5.7)

Now, assume that
E
is
\Lambda
-minimal for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\cdot,\Omega_{M})
with external datum
E\setminus\Omega_{M}
, for some
\Lambda\geq 0
. Then, for every
\widetilde{F}
such that
\widetilde{F}\setminus\Omega_{M}=\widetilde{E}\setminus\Omega_{M}
, if
F:=(\widetilde{F}\cap\Omega_{M})\cup(E\cap\Omega_{M}^{c})
, we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\widetilde{E},\Omega_{M})-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\widetilde{F},\Omega_{M})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega_{M})-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega_{M})+2\widetilde{c}|E\Delta F|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda|E\Delta F|+2\widetilde{c}|\widetilde{E}\Delta\widetilde{F}|

\displaystyle=(\Lambda+2\widetilde{c})|\widetilde{E}\Delta\widetilde{F}|.

Conversely, if
\widetilde{E}
is
\widetilde{\Lambda}
-minimal for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\cdot,\Omega_{M})
with external datum
(E\dot{\cup}\Sigma)\setminus\Omega_{M}
, for some
\widetilde{\Lambda}\geq 0
, then for every set
F
such that
F=E
in
\Omega_{M}^{c}
, we set
\widetilde{F}:=F\dot{\cup}\Sigma
, and deduce that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}|\widetilde{E}\Delta\widetilde{F}|

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\widetilde{E},\Omega_{M})-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\widetilde{F},\Omega_{M})

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega_{M})-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega_{M})-2\widetilde{c}|E\Delta F|.

Reordering the last inequality, and observing that
|\widetilde{E}\Delta\widetilde{F}|=|E\Delta F|
, we obtain that

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega_{M})\leq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega_{M})+(\widetilde{\Lambda}+2\widetilde{c})|E\Delta F|.\qed

6
Stickiness for almost minimal sets

Recall that stickiness for
s
-minimal graphs in dimension
2
can be read as a discontinuity at the boundary as stated in Definition
1.10
.

In this section, we prove that stickiness does not characterize almost minimal surfaces. In order to do this, we address the problem of the existence of sticking and non-sticking non-local almost minimal boundaries. In particular, we investigate whether or not it is true that every time we have a non-sticking
s
-minimal surface
E
in
\Omega
, we can find a sticking almost minimizer for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\cdot,\Omega)
with external datum
E
, and, vice versa, whether or not is it true that every time we have a sticking
s
-minimal surface, we can find a non-sticking almost minimizer.

6.1
Existence of sticking almost minimizers

In
[
25
]
, the authors prove that
s
-minimal graphs are generally sticky, in the sense that whatever the external datum is, one should expect to observe the stickiness phenomenon, up to an arbitrarily small perturbation of the external datum (see
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
).

We use this fact and Theorem
1.11
to show that it is always possible to find a sticking almost minimal set.

Proposition 6.1

(Existence of sticking almost minimal sets)
.

Consider the domain
\Omega=(-1,1)\times\mathbb{R}
and a
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-function
v
such that
\operatorname{subgraph}(v)
has finite
s
-perimeter in
\Omega
.

Then, there exists an almost minimal set
E
in
\Omega
such that
E=\operatorname{subgraph}({u})
, for some
{u}\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}\left((-1,1)\right)
such that
{u}=v
in
\Omega^{c}
and
{u}
is discontinuous at
\{-1,1\}
.

Proof.

Let
F:=\operatorname{subgraph}(v)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and consider an even bump-like function
w\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega^{c})
(e.g.
w(t):=(\chi_{-I}+\chi_{I})*e^{-t^{2}}
, for some interval
I\subseteq(0,+\infty)
). Thus, the assumptions of Theorem
1.11
are satisfied with
\Sigma:=\{v(x)+w(x)\text{ s.t. }x\in\operatorname{supp}(w)\}
.

Thanks to
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
, there exists a minimizer
\widetilde{E}
for the
s
-perimeter in
\Omega
with external datum
(F\dot{\cup}\Sigma)\setminus\Omega
that sticks to the boundary of
\Omega
.

Therefore, Theorem
1.11
entails that

E:=(\widetilde{E}\cap\Omega)\cup(F\cap\Omega^{c})

is a sticking almost minimizer for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\cdot,\Omega)
with external datum
F\setminus\Omega
.
∎

6.2
Existence of non-sticking almost minimal sets

Now, we tackle the problem of the existence of non-sticking almost minimal sets.
What we show here is that it is always possible to find a non-sticking almost minimizer, up to requiring some assumptions on the external datum.

Proposition 6.2

(Existence of non-sticking almost minimal sets)
.

Consider the domain
\Omega=(-1,1)\times\mathbb{R}
and a
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-function
v
such that
E:=\operatorname{subgraph}(v)
has finite
s
-perimeter in
\Omega
.
Assume that there exists a non-sticking almost minimizer for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\cdot,\Omega)
with external datum
\widetilde{E}:=\operatorname{subgraph}(\widetilde{v})
in
\Omega^{c}
, for some
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-function
\widetilde{v}
.

If
\Sigma:=(E\Delta\widetilde{E})\cap\Omega^{c}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
1.11
,
then there exists a non-sticking almost minimizer for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\cdot,\Omega)
with external datum
E
.

Proof.

Thanks to Theorem
1.11
, if a set
A
is a non-sticking almost minimizer for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\cdot,\Omega)
with external datum
\widetilde{E}
, then it is also an almost minimizer for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\cdot,\Omega)
with external datum
E
.

Moreover, in this situation, no sticking phenomena occur.
∎

In particular, we have a non-sticking almost minimal set for every suitable perturbation of the half-space.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
\Omega
be the cylinder
(-1,1)\times\mathbb{R}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Consider the
2
-dimensional half-space
H:=\{(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{2}<0\}
and a set
\Sigma\subseteq\Omega^{c}
satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.11
.

Then,
H\dot{\cup}\Sigma
is a non-sticking almost minimizer for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\cdot,\Omega)
with external datum
(H\dot{\cup}\Sigma)\setminus\Omega
.

6.3
Almost minimal surfaces can show intermediate behaviors

In
[
25
]
, the authors also prove that
2
-dimensional
s
-minimal graphs cannot show intermediate behavior between discontinuity and differentiability. In other words, an
s
-minimal graph in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
can only either stick to the boundary or be globally
\mathcal{C}^{1}
.

Theorem 6.4

(Theorem 1.2,
[
25
]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in(s,1)
,
and
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-function in
[-h,0]
, for some
h\in(0,1)
, and

E:=\big\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\text{ s.t. }x_{2}<u(x_{1})\big\}.

Suppose that
E
is
s
-minimal in
(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
, and that

\lim_{x\to 0^{-}}u(x)=\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}u(x).

Then,
u\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\eta}([-h,1/2])
, with
\eta:=\min\left\{\alpha,\frac{1+s}{2}\right\}
.

In contrast, almost minimal sets can show intermediate behaviors. To prove this, we exploit Theorem
1.11
to provide a counterexample to Theorem
6.4
for almost minimal sets. Namely, we exhibit a non-sticking continuous, but non-
\mathcal{C}^{1}
almost minimizers.

The idea is that by applying a suitable external perturbation to a non-sticking
s
-minimal graph close to the boundary of the domain we force some discontinuity in the first derivative at the boundary
without breaking the continuity of the function.

Example 6.5

(Counterexample: a non-sticking
\mathcal{C}^{0}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{1}
almost minimal set)
.

This counterexample is built starting from
[
23
, Theorem 1.4]
. For this, we recall that for every
\eta_{0}>0

with
\beta=\frac{2+\eta_{0}}{1-2s}
.

In particular, we have that
\partial E
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

\Omega

[

\mathcal{C}^{1}

This yields that the boundary of

Now, let us construct a perturbation set
\Sigma

\Sigma_{+}

and
\operatorname{graph}(\varphi)

\mathcal{C}^{1}

According to Theorem
1.11

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(\cdot,\Omega_{0})

7
An interesting application: the non-local Massari’s Problem
and proofs of Theorems
1.13

and
1.14

The regularity theory of almost minimizers that we have discussed so far
finds a convenient application to the non-local version of the Massari’s Problem. In particular, we will apply
the regularity theory developed in Section
2

to establish the existence and regularity of sets with prescribed non-local mean curvature.

We start with the following observation, that relates
the non-local mean curvature (as defined in (
2.16
))
of a minimizer for
\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}
with the function
H
,
and justifies Definition
1.12
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
A
be a smooth open set in
\Omega
that minimizes
\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}
.

Then,
H_{s}[A]=H
in
\Omega
,
that is
A
has fractional mean curvature
H
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\nu
be the exterior unit normal to
\partial A
, and consider a smooth compactly supported perturbation function
h
. For every
t>0
, we define the perturbed set
A_{t}
such that

\partial A_{t}:=\big\{x+th(x)\nu(x):x\in\partial A\big\}.

Since
A
is a smooth minimizer for
\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}
, we have that

\frac{d}{dt}\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(A_{t})\big\rvert_{t=0}=0.

(7.1)

The first variation of the fractional perimeter has been computed e.g. in
[
19
]
, and is given by

\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Per}_{s}(A_{t},\Omega)\big\rvert_{t=0}=-\int_{\partial A}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(y)\frac{\chi_{A}(x)-\chi_{A^{c}}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y).

(7.2)

Moreover,

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{A_{t}\cap\Omega}H(x)\,dx\right)\big\rvert_{t=0}=\int_{\partial A\cap\Omega}h(x)H(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

From this, (
7.1
) and (
7.2
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

We point out that Lemma
7.1
is the analog in this setting of the fractional mean curvature equation, presented in
[
11
, Section 5]
. Indeed, if
A
is an
s
-minimal surface, then

H_{s}[A]=0\text{ in }\partial A\cap\Omega,

in the viscosity sense (and pointwise if
A
is smooth).

For more details about the fractional mean curvature, we refer to
[
1
]
.

7.1
Existence of sets with prescribed non-local mean curvature

We use the classical direct methods in the calculus of variations to prove the existence of sets with prescribed non-local mean curvature.

For later convenience, we provide the following compactness result.

Theorem 7.2

(Compactness of the class of finite fractional perimeter sets)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{n}
be the family of all measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and consider a function
f:\mathcal{M}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Then, the class

\mathcal{C}_{f}=\big\{F\in\mathcal{M}^{n}\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,K)\leq f(K),\;{\mbox{ for all }}K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\big\}

is compact in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
K
be an arbitrary compact set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Notice that, for every
F\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
, we have that

\begin{split}&\|\chi_{F}\|_{H^{s}(K)}=\|\chi_{F}\|_{L^{2}(K)}+[\chi_{F}]_{H^{s}(K)}\leq\|\chi_{F}\|_{L^{2}(K)}+\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,K)\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq|K|^{1/2}+f(K)=:g(K).\end{split}

This gives that
\chi_{F}\in B^{s}_{g(K)}
,
where
B^{s}_{g(K)}
denotes
the ball of radius
g(K)
and centered at 0 in the Hilbert space
H^{s}(K)
.

Moreover,
B^{s}_{g(K)}
is weakly compact since
H^{s}(K)
is a Hilbert space. Therefore, for every sequence
\{E_{j}\}_{j}
in
\mathcal{C}_{f}
, there exist some
\psi\in B^{s}_{g(K)}
and a subsequence
\{E_{j_{l}}\}_{l}
such that
\chi_{E_{j_{l}}}\rightharpoonup\psi
in
H^{s}(K)
.

Thanks to the fractional Rellich-Kondrachov’s Theorem,
H^{s}(K)
is compactly embedded in
L^{2}(K)
. Thus,
E_{j_{l}}\to\psi
strongly in
L^{2}(K)
, hence in
L^{1}(K)
, up to a subsequence. Since
K
is arbitrarily chosen, we deduce that
\chi_{E_{j_{l}}}
converges
\psi
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We recall now that the
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}
-convergence implies the convergence almost everywhere along a subsequence.
Hence,
\psi
must be a characteristic function of some set
E
, i.e.
\psi=\chi_{E}
(to see this, consider a sequence of nested sets compactly contained in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and use a diagonal argument).

Moreover, by lower semi-continuity of the fractional perimeter, we have that

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,K)\leq\liminf_{l\to+\infty}\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E_{j_{l}},K)\leq f(K),\qquad{\mbox{for all }}K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Therefore,
E\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
, and this concludes the proof.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
7.2
, we infer the following:

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
\{E_{j}\}_{j}
be a sequence of sets such that
E_{j}\setminus\Omega=M\setminus\Omega
and

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E_{j},\Omega)<c,

for some constant
c>0
independent of
j
.

Then, there exist a subsequence
\{E_{j_{l}}\}_{l}
and a set
E
such that
\chi_{E_{j_{l}}}\to\chi_{E}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
,
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega)<c
, and
E\setminus\Omega=M\setminus\Omega
.

We can now complete the proof of Theorem
1.13
.

Proof of Theorem
1.13
.

Set
m:=\inf\{\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(F):F\in\mathscr{F}\}
. We show that
m
is finite.

Notice that
M\in\mathscr{F}
, so that

m\leq\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(M)\leq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(M,\Omega)+\int_{\Omega}H(x)\,dx<+\infty.

Moreover, for every
F\in\mathscr{F}
, we have that

\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(F)\geq-\left|\int_{E\cap\Omega}H(x)\,dx\right|\geq-\|H\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}>-\infty.

Now, consider a minimizing sequence
\{E_{j}\}_{j}\subseteq\mathscr{F}
such that
\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(E_{j})\to m
. Therefore, the sequence
\{\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(E_{j})\}_{j}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}
by a constant
C>0
independent of
j
, and consequently

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E_{j},\Omega)\leq|\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(E_{j})|+\left|\int_{E_{j_{l}}\cap\Omega}H(x)\,dx\right|\leq C+\|H\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=:c_{\Omega,H}.

Thanks to Corollary
7.3
, there exist a subsequence
\{E_{j_{l}}\}_{l}
and a set
E\in\mathscr{F}
such that
E_{j_{l}}\to E
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Hence,
E_{j_{l}}\to E
pointwise, up to a subsequence.

Moreover,

|(\chi_{E_{j_{l}}}-\chi_{E})H|\leq|H|\in L^{1}(\Omega).

Then, using the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we obtain

\lim_{l\to+\infty}\left|\int_{E\cap\Omega}H(x)\,dx-\int_{E_{j_{l}}\cap\Omega}H(x)\,dx\right|\leq\lim_{l\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|\left[\chi_{E_{j_{l}}}(x)-\chi_{E}(x)\right]H(x)\right|\,dx=0.

From this and the lower semi-continuity of
\operatorname{Per}_{s}
, we get that

\begin{split}&\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(E)=\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega)+\int_{E\cap\Omega}H\,dx\\
&\quad\leq\liminf_{l\to+\infty}\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E_{j_{l}},\Omega)+\int_{E_{j_{l}}\cap\Omega}H\,dx=\liminf_{l\to+\infty}\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}(E_{j_{l}})=m.\end{split}

Hence,
E
is a minimizer for
\mathscr{J}^{H}_{\Omega,s}
.
∎

7.2
Regularity of sets with prescribed non-local mean curvature

Now, we apply the almost minimizers theory to the non-local Massari’s Problem to deduce regularity for sets with prescribed non-local mean curvature. We point out that, in order to achieve this goal, some additional assumption on
H
is needed, namely we require
H\in L^{\infty}(B_{1})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.14
.

Let
F
be such that
F\setminus B_{1}=E\setminus B_{1}
. From the minimality of
E
for
\mathscr{J}^{H}_{B_{1},s}
, we see that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,B_{1})-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,B_{1})=\mathscr{J}^{H}_{B_{1},s}(E)-\int_{E\cap B_{1}}H\,dx-\mathscr{J}^{H}_{B_{1},s}(F)+\int_{F\cap B_{1}}H\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\int_{F\cap B_{1}}H\,dx-\int_{E\cap B_{1}}H\,dx\leq\int_{(E\Delta F)\cap B_{1}}|H|\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}|E\Delta F|.

Hence,
E
is
\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}
-minimal for
\operatorname{Per}_{s}
in
B_{1}
.
From this and Theorem
1.4
, we deduce
the claim of Theorem
1.14
.
∎

Appendix A
Proof of Proposition
2.1

Let us assume that
\Omega
is smooth.
Let

be such that

and define

(A.1)

By construction,
F_{k}\setminus\Omega=E_{k}\setminus\Omega

(A.2)

We now show that we can rewrite (

) as

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E_{k},\Omega)\leq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega)+\Lambda_{k}|(E_{k}\Delta F)\cap\Omega|+b_{k},

(A.3)

where

is an infinitesimal sequence as

.

To check this, we observe that

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega)-\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F_{k},\Omega)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\mathscr{L}(F\cap\Omega,F^{c})+\mathscr{L}(F\cap\Omega^{c},F^{c}\cap\Omega)-\mathscr{L}(F_{k}\cap\Omega,F_{k}^{c})-\mathscr{L}(F_{k}\cap\Omega^{c},F_{k}^{c}\cap\Omega)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\mathscr{L}(F\cap\Omega,F^{c}\cap\Omega^{c})+\mathscr{L}(F\cap\Omega^{c},F^{c}\cap\Omega)-\mathscr{L}(F\cap\Omega,F_{k}^{c}\cap\Omega^{c})-\mathscr{L}(F_{k}\cap\Omega^{c},F^{c}\cap\Omega)\big|.

Writing explicitly the functional
\mathcal{L}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega)-\operatorname{Per}(F_{k},\Omega)|

\displaystyle\qquad=\left|\int_{F\cap\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{\chi_{F^{c}}(x)-\chi_{F_{k}^{c}}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{F^{c}\cap\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{\chi_{F}(x)-\chi_{F_{k}}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{F\cap\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|\chi_{F^{c}}(x)-\chi_{F_{k}^{c}}(x)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{F^{c}\cap\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|\chi_{F}(x)-\chi_{F_{k}}(x)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy

Recalling that, for every sets
A
and
B
, we have that
A^{c}\Delta B^{c}=A\Delta B
, and using that

and
F_{k}=E_{k}
in

\begin{split}&|\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega)-\operatorname{Per}(F_{k},\Omega)|\leq\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{\chi_{F\Delta F_{k}}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad=\mathscr{L}(\Omega,(F\Delta F_{k})\cap\Omega^{c})=\mathscr{L}(\Omega,(E\Delta E_{k})\cap\Omega^{c})=:b_{k}.\end{split}

Using this into (
A.2
), we obtain the expression in (

Hence, to complete the proof of (

\displaystyle\Omega_{\rho}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mbox{ s.t. }\operatorname{dist}(x,\Omega)<\rho\}

\begin{split}&\mathscr{L}\left(\Omega,(E\Delta E_{k})\cap(\Omega_{R}\setminus\Omega)\right)=\int_{\Omega_{R}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\chi_{E\Delta E_{k}}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dx\,dy\\

\end{split}

By the definition of
\Omega_{\rho}
, we have that

and

A general corollary of Theorem

.

\{E_{k}\}_{k}

\begin{split}DT_{\varepsilon}(x)&=\mathcal{R}_{x}+2\left(\frac{R+\mbox{d}_{\varepsilon}(x)}{R+\mbox{d}_{\varepsilon}(x)\pm r_{x,\varepsilon}}-1\right)\left(\mbox{Id}-\frac{x+Re_{n}}{|x+Re_{n}|}\otimes\frac{x+Re_{n}}{|x+Re_{n}|}\right)\\
&\quad\qquad+2\varepsilon\frac{x+Re_{n}}{|x+Re_{n}|}\otimes(\nabla\mbox{d})\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\end{split}

(C.6)

where
\mathcal{R}_{x}
is defined as in (
2.39
).

Notice that we also have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{R+\mbox{d}_{\varepsilon}(x)}{R+\mbox{d}_{\varepsilon}(x)\pm r_{x,\varepsilon}}-1\right|=\frac{r_{x,\varepsilon}}{R+\mbox{d}_{\varepsilon}(x)\pm r_{x,\varepsilon}}

(C.7)

and

\displaystyle|\nabla\mbox{d}(x)|\leq 2\frac{|x^{\prime}|}{R}.

(C.8)

Let us denote by
\|\cdot\|
the Euclidean norm for linear operators. Then, (
C.6
), together with (
C.7
) and (
C.8
), entails that

\|DT_{\varepsilon}(x)-\mathcal{R}_{x}\|\leq 3n\frac{r_{x,\varepsilon}}{R+\mbox{d}_{\varepsilon}(x)\pm r_{x,\varepsilon}}+4\frac{|x^{\prime}|}{R}\leq 6n\frac{r_{x,\varepsilon}}{R}+4\frac{|x^{\prime}|}{R},

as long as
\varepsilon<R/2
, proving (
C.1
).

As a consequence of (
C.1
) and the triangle inequality, we also have that

\left|\|DT_{\varepsilon}(x)\|-\|\mathcal{R}_{x}\|\right|\leq{2}{R}(3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|).

Thus,
using the fact that
\mathcal{R}_{x}
is an isometry and integrating along the segment
[x,y]
, for any
x
,
y\in V_{2R,\varepsilon}\setminus V_{0,\varepsilon}
, we obtain that

\begin{split}&|T_{\varepsilon}(x)-T_{\varepsilon}(y)|\geq\left(1-\frac{2}{R}\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}\right)|x-y|\\
\text{and}\quad&|T_{\varepsilon}(x)-T_{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq\left(1+\frac{2}{R}\max\{3nr_{x,\varepsilon}+2|x^{\prime}|,3nr_{y,\varepsilon}+2|y^{\prime}|\}\right)|x-y|,\end{split}

from which (
C.2
) follows immediately.

In order to prove (
C.3
), recall that, by construction,

A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\subseteq V_{R,\varepsilon}\setminus B_{2R}(-2Re_{n}).

In other words, for every
x\in A_{\varepsilon}^{-}
, we have that

|x+Re_{n}|\leq R+\varepsilon d\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad|x+2Re_{n}|\geq 2R.

These inequalities yield that

|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}+2Rx_{n}\leq 0\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}+4Rx_{n}\geq 0.

Hence, it must be
d\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\neq 0
, therefore
|x^{\prime}|<\varepsilon
.

Furthermore, the parabola
-\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{2}}{R}
is contained in
B_{2R}(-2Re_{n})
when
|x^{\prime}|<\varepsilon
. Thus, recalling also that
\varepsilon<R
, we infer that

-\varepsilon\leq-\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{2}}{R}\leq x_{n}<\varepsilon.

Therefore, we deduce that
|x|<2\varepsilon
, that is
A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\subseteq B_{2\varepsilon}
.

Now, using the assumption
\varepsilon<1/(3n)
with (
C.4
), we obtain that

\|DT_{\varepsilon}(x)\|\leq 1+2\varepsilon\frac{3n+1}{R}\leq 4.

This implies that
T_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon}^{-})\subseteq 4B_{2\varepsilon}
, and consequently
A_{\varepsilon}\subseteq B_{8\varepsilon}
.

To conclude, if
|x|\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2R}
, then
\mbox{d}_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq\frac{3\varepsilon^{2}}{4R}
, from which we find that
|x+Re_{n}|\leq R+\mbox{d}_{\varepsilon}(x)
. It thereby follows that
B_{\varepsilon^{2}/(2R)}\subseteq V_{R,\varepsilon}
, hence

B_{\varepsilon^{2}/(2R)}\setminus E\subseteq A_{\varepsilon}^{-}\subseteq A_{\varepsilon}.\qed

Appendix D
Proof of Formula (
2.25
)

In this section, we show that

\operatorname{dist}(\mathbb{E}_{R,\varepsilon},\mathbb{E}_{R,r})\geq\frac{1}{1+c}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-r^{2}}{R},

where
c>0
is a constant depending only on
n
, and

\mathbb{E}_{R,r}:=\left\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\;{\mbox{ s.t. }}\;|x+Re_{n}|=R+\frac{r^{2}}{R}\left(1-\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\right\}\setminus B_{2R}(-2Re_{n}),

as claimed in (
2.25
).

To this aim, we define the function
\Phi:(0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
as

\Phi(r,\rho):=-R+\left[\frac{\rho^{4}}{R^{2}}-\left(3+\frac{2r^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)\rho^{2}+\left(R+\frac{r^{2}}{R}\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2},

and we notice that
\left(x^{\prime},\Phi(r,|x^{\prime}|)\right)\in\mathbb{E}_{R,r}
, for every
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
such that
|x^{\prime}|<r
.

Since
\mathbb{E}_{R,\varepsilon}
is closed, for every
x\in\mathbb{E}_{R,r}
there exists
y\in\mathbb{E}_{R,\varepsilon}
such that
\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb{E}_{R,\varepsilon})=|x-y|
.
Thanks to the triangle inequality, we obtain that

\begin{split}|x-y|&\geq|\Phi(r,|x^{\prime}|)-\Phi(\varepsilon,|y^{\prime}|)|\\
&\geq|\Phi(r,|x^{\prime}|)-\Phi(\varepsilon,|x^{\prime}|)|-|\Phi(\varepsilon,|x^{\prime}|)-\Phi(\varepsilon,|y^{\prime}|)|\\
&\geq|\Phi(r,|x^{\prime}|)-\Phi(\varepsilon,|x^{\prime}|)|-\|\partial_{\rho}\Phi(\varepsilon,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,+\infty))}\left||x^{\prime}|-|y^{\prime}|\right|\\
&\geq|\Phi(r,|x^{\prime}|)-\Phi(\varepsilon,|x^{\prime}|)|-\|\partial_{\rho}\Phi(\varepsilon,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,+\infty))}|x-y|,\end{split}

which reads

|x-y|\geq\frac{1}{1+\|\partial_{\rho}\Phi(\varepsilon,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,+\infty))}}|\Phi(r,|x^{\prime}|)-\Phi(\varepsilon,|x^{\prime}|)|.

(D.1)

Now, notice that, for every
0<r<R/\sqrt{2}
and
0\leq\rho\leq r
, we have that

\begin{split}&\Phi(r,\rho)+R=\left[\frac{\rho^{4}}{R^{2}}-\left(3+\frac{2r^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)\rho^{2}+\left(R+\frac{r^{2}}{R}\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2}\\
&\qquad\geq\left(R^{2}+\frac{r^{4}}{R^{2}}+2r^{2}-3\rho^{2}-\frac{2r^{2}\rho^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\geq\left(R^{2}-r^{2}-\frac{r^{4}}{R^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\qquad\geq\left(R^{2}-\frac{R^{2}}{2}-\frac{R^{2}}{4}\right)^{1/2}=\frac{R}{2}.\end{split}

(D.2)

It thereby follows that,
for every
0<\varepsilon<R/\sqrt{2}
and
0\leq\rho\leq\varepsilon
,

|\partial_{\rho}\Phi(\varepsilon,\rho)|=\left|\frac{\rho}{R^{2}(\Phi(\varepsilon,\rho)+R)}(2\rho^{2}-3R^{2}-2\varepsilon^{2})\right|\leq\frac{R/\sqrt{2}}{R^{3}/2}4R^{2}=4\sqrt{2}.

(D.3)

Now, let us define the function
f_{r,\varepsilon}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
as

f_{r,\varepsilon}(\rho):=|\Phi(r,\rho)-\Phi(\varepsilon,\rho)|.

Then,

f^{\prime}_{r,\varepsilon}(\rho)=\frac{\Phi(r,\rho)-\Phi(\varepsilon,\rho)}{|\Phi(r,\rho)-\Phi(\varepsilon,\rho)|}\big(\partial_{\rho}\Phi(r,\rho)-\partial_{\rho}\Phi(\varepsilon,\rho)\big).

(D.4)

We observe that

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\Phi(r,\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2r}{R^{2}(\Phi(r,\rho)+R)}(R^{2}+r^{2}-\rho^{2})

\displaystyle{\mbox{and }}\qquad\partial_{r}\partial_{\rho}\Phi(r,\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2r\rho}{R^{2}(\Phi(r,\rho)+R)^{3}}(R^{2}+r^{2}+\rho^{2}).

Therefore, recalling also (
D.2
), we infer that
\Phi(\cdot,\rho)
and
\partial_{\rho}\Phi(\cdot,\rho)
are increasing in the first variable. Namely, for every
0<r<\varepsilon<R/\sqrt{2}
and
0\leq\rho\leq r
.

\displaystyle\Phi(r,\rho)<\Phi(\varepsilon,\rho),

(D.5)

and

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\Phi(r,\rho)\leq\partial_{\rho}\Phi(\varepsilon,\rho),

(D.6)

Inequalities (
D.5
) and (
D.6
), together with (
D.4
), give that
f^{\prime}_{r,\varepsilon}
is always non-negative. Hence,
f_{r,\varepsilon}
is increasing and has a minimum at
\rho=0
.
It thereby follows that

|\Phi(r,|x^{\prime}|)-\Phi(\varepsilon,|x^{\prime}|)|\geq|\Phi(r,0)-\Phi(\varepsilon,0)|=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-r^{2}}{R}.

(D.7)

Therefore, plugging (
D.3
) and (
D.7
) in (
D.1
), we deduce that

\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb{E}_{R,r})=|x-y|\geq\frac{1}{1+4\sqrt{2}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-r^{2}}{R}.

Hence, taking the minimum among all
x\in\mathbb{E}_{R,\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\operatorname{dist}(\mathbb{E}_{R,\varepsilon},\mathbb{E}_{R,r})\geq\frac{1}{1+4\sqrt{2}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-r^{2}}{R},

as desired.

Appendix E
Proof of Lemma
2.22

We exploit Lemma
2.20
with
r:=1
and
C:=1
. In this way, up to a subsequence,
A_{k}^{1}
converges uniformly to the graph of a Hölder-continuous function
u
in
\{|x^{\prime}|<1/2\}
(see Remark
2.21
).

Now, controlling the oscillation between the unit vectors
\nu_{l}^{k}
and
\nu_{l+1}^{k}
, we repeat the same argument in larger and larger balls, and infer that
\partial E_{k}^{*}\to\partial E^{*}
uniformly in every compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In order to explicit this idea, we recall that

\displaystyle\partial E_{k}\cap B_{2^{l}}\subseteq\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{l}^{k}|\leq a_{k}2^{l(1+\alpha)}\big\}

and

\displaystyle\partial E_{k}\cap B_{2^{l+1}}\subseteq\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{l+1}^{k}|\leq a_{k}2^{(l+1)(1+\alpha)}\big\}.

From these inclusions we obtain that

|\nu_{l}^{k}-\nu_{l+1}^{k}|\leq a_{k}2^{l(1+\alpha)}+a_{k}2^{(l+1)(1+\alpha)}\leq 5\,2^{l(1+\alpha)-\alpha k}.

The geometric idea behind this implication is quite simple, however, in order to not interrupt the flow of the proof, we refer to Appendix
G
for an exhaustive explanation of the argument.

Notice also that, for every
l
,

\lim_{k\to+\infty}|\nu_{l}^{k}-e_{n}|\leq\lim_{k\to+\infty}|\nu_{l}^{k}-\nu_{l-1}^{k}|+\dots+|\nu_{1}^{k}-\nu_{0}^{k}|\leq\lim_{k\to+\infty}5\,2^{-\alpha k}\sum_{m=0}^{l-1}2^{m(1+\alpha)}=0.

Therefore, for every
x\in\partial E_{k}\cap B_{2^{l}}
,

\displaystyle|x_{n}|\leq|x\cdot\nu_{l}^{k}|+2^{l}|e_{n}-\nu_{l}^{k}|\leq a_{k}2^{l(1+\alpha)}+5a_{k}2^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{l-1}2^{m\alpha}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leq 5a_{k}2^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{l}2^{m\alpha}\leq 5a_{k}2^{l}\frac{2^{\alpha(l+1)}-1}{2^{\alpha}-1}\leq ca_{k}2^{l(1+\alpha)},

so that we have the inclusion

\partial E_{k}\cap B_{2^{l}}\subseteq\big\{|x_{n}|\leq ca_{k}2^{l(1+\alpha)}\big\}.

Now, we apply Lemma
2.20
with
r:=2
and
C:=c2^{1+\alpha}
, and deduce that

A_{k}^{2}
is uniformly convergent to the graph of a Hölder-continuous function
v
in
\{|x^{\prime}|<1\}
. By uniqueness, we have that
v
must coincide with
u
in
\{|x^{\prime}|<1/2\}
.

We apply iteratively Lemma
2.20
to
A_{k}^{j}
and, using a diagonal argument, we infer that
A_{k}^{k}
uniformly converges to the graph of a Hölder-continuous function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
, along a subsequence. Hence
E_{k}^{*}
converges uniformly to
\operatorname{subgraph}(u)

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Moreover, the fact that
0\in\partial E_{k}^{*}

yields that
0\in\partial E^{*}
. Therefore, since
the point
(0^{\prime},u(0^{\prime}))\in\partial E^{*}
, it follows that
u(0^{\prime})=0
.

Finally, we exploit the uniform convergence of
\{E_{k}^{*}\}_{k}
to conclude that

|u(x^{\prime})|\leq\widetilde{c}\big(1+|x^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}\big),

\widetilde{c}>0
.
Indeed, for every given

, we have that

\partial E_{k}^{*}\cap B^{n-1}_{2^{l}}
converges to

.

As a consequence, since for every

there exists

such that

, we are led to

\frac{|u(x^{\prime})|}{(1+|x^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha})}\leq\frac{c2^{l(1+\alpha)}}{1+2^{(l-1)(1+\alpha)}}\leq c2^{1+\alpha}=:\widetilde{c}.

It follows that

|u(x^{\prime})|\leq\widetilde{c}\big(1+|x^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}\big),

which concludes the proof.

), we infer that

\int_{B_{R}^{n-1}\setminus B_{\delta}^{n-1}}\frac{u(x^{\prime})-u(0^{\prime})}{|x^{\prime}|^{n+s}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\leq C\delta^{1-s}+O(R^{\alpha-s}).

Hence, taking the limits as
\delta\to 0
and
R\to+\infty
, we obtain (
F.2
).

Now, to show that
u
is a viscosity sub-solution of (
F.1
), let
\varphi
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-function touching
u
at
0
from above. Using the same idea in (
F.4
) with a tangent parabola of opening
\frac{a_{k}}{2}
, we deduce that

\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\int_{D_{\delta}(x_{k})\setminus B_{\rho}(x_{k})}\frac{\chi_{E_{k}}(x)-\chi_{E_{k}^{c}}(x)}{|x-x_{k}|^{n+s}}\,dx\leq Ca_{k}\delta^{1-s}.

This, together with (
F.3
), (
F.6
), and Corollary
2.7
, leads us to

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{R}^{n-1}(x_{k})\setminus B_{\delta}^{n-1}(x_{k})}\frac{u(x^{\prime})-u(x_{k}^{\prime})}{|x^{\prime}-x_{k}^{\prime}|^{n+s}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\\
&\geq\liminf_{\rho\to 0}\frac{1}{2a_{k}}\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(x_{k})}\frac{\chi_{E_{k}}(x)-\chi_{E_{k}^{c}}(x)}{|x-x_{k}|^{n+s}}\,dx-C\delta^{1-s}+O(\varepsilon)+O(a_{k}^{\xi})+O(R^{\alpha-s})\\
&\geq-2^{-k(s-\alpha)-1}\Lambda-C\delta^{1-s}+O(\varepsilon)+O(a_{k}^{\xi})+O(R^{\alpha-s}).\end{split}

(F.9)

Taking
\varepsilon\to 0
, and consequently
k\to+\infty
, in (
F.9
), we obtain that

\int_{B_{R}^{n-1}\setminus B_{\delta}^{n-1}}\frac{u(x^{\prime})-u(0^{\prime})}{|x^{\prime}|^{n+s}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\geq-C\delta^{1-s}+O(R^{\alpha-s}).

Finally, taking the limits as
\delta\to 0
and
R\to+\infty
, we infer that
u
is also a viscosity sub-solution of (
F.1
), concluding the proof.

Appendix G
Geometric estimates for unit normals

In this section, we estimate the angle between the unit vectors
\nu_{j}
and
\nu_{j+1}
given the flatness assumptions

\partial E\cap B_{2^{j}}\subseteq\big\{|x\cdot\nu_{j}|<a2^{j(1+\alpha)}\big\},\qquad{\mbox{for all }}j=0,\dots,k,

(G.1)

where
E
is a
\Lambda
-minimal set in
B_{2^{k}}
such that
0\in\partial E
, and
a:=2^{-\alpha k}
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)

(see Figure
7
).

We claim the following:

Lemma G.1
.

For every
j\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}
, we have that

|\nu_{j}-\nu_{j+1}|\leq c2^{\alpha(j-k)},

for some constant
c>0
independent of
j
and
k
.

This fact follows straight-forwardly from the geometric properties of our problem, and to prove it, it is sufficient to estimate the worst case scenario, which occurs when the slab
\{|x\cdot\nu_{j}|<a2^{j(1+\alpha)}\}
touches
\{|x\cdot\nu_{j+1}|<a2^{(j+1)(1+\alpha)}\}
. We now provide the technical details of this fact.

Proof of Lemma
G.1
.

Since we are interested in estimating the angle between unit normals of hypersurfaces, we narrow down to the
2
-dimensional case. Then, to our purpose, we consider
j<k
, and observe that the flatness assumptions in (
G.1
) yield that

\big\{x\cdot\nu_{j+1}<-a2^{(j+1)(1+\alpha)}\big\}\subseteq\big\{x\cdot\nu_{j}<a2^{j(1+\alpha)}\big\}\text{ in }B_{2^{j}}.

Therefore, rescaling by a factor
2^{-j}
, we deduce that

\big\{x\cdot\nu_{j+1}<-a2^{\alpha j+\alpha+1}\big\}\subseteq\big\{x\cdot\nu_{j}<a2^{\alpha j}\big\}\text{ in }B_{1}.

Let
\delta_{j}:=a2^{\alpha j}=2^{\alpha(j-k)}<1
. Then, we rewrite the last display as

\big\{x\cdot\nu_{j+1}<-2^{\alpha+1}\delta_{j}\big\}\subseteq\big\{x\cdot\nu_{j}<\delta_{j}\big\}\text{ in }B_{1},

see Figure
7
.

From this it follows that the maximum angle between
\nu_{j}
and
\nu_{j+1}
is realized when
\{x\cdot\nu_{j+1}<-2^{\alpha+1}\delta_{j}\}\cap\{x\cdot\nu_{j}<\delta_{j}\}\neq\varnothing
. This case is represented in Figure
9
.

Notice that, by trivial geometric properties, we have that

\cos\eta=\nu_{j+1}\cdot\nu_{j},

where
\eta
is the angle between
\{x\cdot\nu_{j}=\delta_{j}\}
and
\{x\cdot\nu_{j+1}=-2^{1+\alpha}\delta_{j}\}
, as shown in Figure
9
. Since
\nu_{j+1}\cdot\nu_{j}
defines an angle at the center, while
\eta
is an angle at the circumference, we have the estimate

|\nu_{j+1}-\nu_{j}|\leq L,

where
L
is the length of the chord between
\{x\cdot\nu_{j}=\delta_{j}\}
and
\{x\cdot\nu_{j+1}=-2^{1+\alpha}\delta_{j}\}
in
B_{1}
.

We consider the points

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\in\{\lambda\nu_{j}:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}\cap\{x\cdot\nu_{j}=\delta_{j}\}

\displaystyle{\mbox{and }}\quad q

\displaystyle\in\{\lambda\nu_{j+1}:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}\cap\{x\cdot\nu_{j+1}=-2^{1+\alpha}\delta_{j}\},

and let
d
be the segment between
p
and
q
(see Figure
9
). By a similarity property, we have that
L=2d
and hence

|\nu_{j+1}-\nu_{j}|\leq 2d.

(G.2)

Also, using basic trigonometry, we see that

d\leq\operatorname{dist}\big(0,\{x\cdot\nu_{j}=\delta_{j}\}\big)+\operatorname{dist}\big(0,\{x\cdot\nu_{j+1}=-2^{1+\alpha}\delta_{j}\}\big)=\delta_{j}+2^{1+\alpha}\delta_{j}\leq 5\delta_{j}.

From this and (
G.2
), we infer that

|\nu_{j+1}-\nu_{j}|\leq 10\delta_{j}=5\,2^{\alpha(j-k)+1},

as desired.

Proof of Proposition
3.5

Notice that, by construction, the sequence
\{\widetilde{u}_{k}\}_{k}
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous on every compact set of
\{z>0\}
. Therefore, thanks to
the Ascoli-Arzelà’s Theorem, there exists a subsequence
k_{j}
such that
\widetilde{u}_{k_{j}}
converges to some function
\widetilde{v}
uniformly on every compact set of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
.

Moreover, by uniform convergence,

\operatorname{div}(z^{1-s}\nabla\widetilde{v})=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}.

By the uniqueness of the solution of the Dirichlet problem, to prove that
\widetilde{v}=\widetilde{u}
(and thus obtain the claim in (
i
)), since both
\widetilde{u}
and
\widetilde{v}
are bounded, it is sufficient to show that

\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde{v})=u.

(H.1)

To this purpose, let
r>0
. By Fatou’s Lemma and Theorem
3.4
(recall in particular formulas (
3.10
) and (
3.12
)), we have that

\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{v}|^{2}\,dx\,dz\leq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k_{j}}|^{2}\,dx\,dz\leq Cr^{n-s}(1+r^{s}).

Therefore, using the Hölder Inequality, we see that, for every
\delta>0
,

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}^{+}\cap\{0<z<\delta\}}|\nabla(\widetilde{u}_{k_{j}}-\widetilde{v})|\,dx\,dz&=\int_{B_{r}^{+}\cap\{0<z<\delta\}}z^{(s-1)/2}z^{(1-s)/2}|\nabla(\widetilde{u}_{k_{j}}-\widetilde{v})|\,dx\,dz\\
&\leq C\delta^{s/2}\left(\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla(\widetilde{u}_{k_{j}}-\widetilde{v})|^{2}\,dx\,dz\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\delta^{s/2},\end{split}

for some positive constant
C
, depending only
n
,
s
, and
r
, and possibly changing from line to line.
From this, it follows that

\int_{B_{r}^{+}}|\nabla(\widetilde{u}_{k_{j}}-\widetilde{v})|\,dx\,dz\leq C\delta^{s/2}+\int_{B_{r}^{+}\cap\{z\geq\delta\}}|\nabla(\widetilde{u}_{k_{j}}-\widetilde{v})|\,dx\,dz.

Now, since
\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k_{j}}
converges uniformly to
\nabla\widetilde{v}
on every compact set of
\{z>0\}
, taking the limit as
j\to+\infty
, we obtain that, for every
\delta>0
,

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}|\nabla(\widetilde{u}_{k_{j}}-\widetilde{v})|\,dx\,dz\leq C\delta^{s/2}.

This gives that
\widetilde{u}_{k_{j}}\to\widetilde{v}
in
W^{1,1}(B_{r}^{+})
. Hence, thanks to the Sobolev embeddings, we have that
u_{k_{j}}=\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde{u}_{k_{j}})\to\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde{v})
in
L^{1}(B_{r}^{+})
.

Since
r
is arbitrary, we infer that
u_{k_{j}}\to\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde{v})
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}
. By the uniqueness of the limit,
we thus obtain the claim in (
H.1
).

ii
), recall that, by
[
11
, Proposition 7.1]
, we know that

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\int_{B_{r}^{+}}z^{1-s}|\nabla(\widetilde{u}_{k}-\widetilde{u})|^{2}\,dx\,dz\leq c\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathrm{J}_{2r}(u_{k}-u),

has been defined in (
3.3
).

, for every

\begin{split}&f_{k_{j}}(x,y):=\frac{u_{k_{j}}(x)-u_{k_{j}}(y)}{|x-y|^{(n+s)/2}}\chi_{B_{2r}}(x)\left(\chi_{B_{2r}}(y)+\sqrt{2}\chi_{B_{2r}^{c}}(y)\right),\;{\mbox{for all\penalty\ $j\in\mathbb{N}$}},\\
{\mbox{and }}&f(x,y):=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{(n+s)/2}}\chi_{B_{2r}}(x)\left(\chi_{B_{2r}}(y)+\sqrt{2}\chi_{B_{2r}^{c}}(y)\right).\end{split}

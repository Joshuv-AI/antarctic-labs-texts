CerviHFENet: hybrid feature extraction-based deep learning for multi-label classification of upper cervical spine abnormalities in X-ray imaging
Authors: Zixuan Wang, Cheng Zhang, Xuemei Zhu, Kai Niu, Shanshan Liu, Shenglin Wang, Xingyu Zhou, Dingben Wang, Nanfang Xu, Zhiqiang He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate diagnosis of upper cervical spine abnormalities is crucial for effective treatment and prognosis. However, the diversity of anatomical structures and pathological abnormalities poses significant challenges to the diagnostic process. These challenges highlight the need for deep learning models, which are capable of identifying multiple abnormalities to assist in diagnosis. Traditional deep learning approaches have exhibited limitations in extracting sequential features from multi-positional X-ray images and in performing joint diagnosis of multiple coexisting abnormalities. Therefore, a CerviHFENet-based framework is proposed to perform multi-label classification of six upper cervical spine abnormalities using three-view radiographs (extension, neutral, and flexion) obtained from individual patients. The system first incorporates an adaptive region of interest (ROI) detection module to minimize irrelevant information by precisely localizing the upper cervical spine. Following this, the CerviHFENet model integrates a hybrid feature extraction (HFE) mechanism that extracts both the anatomical features of the upper cervical vertebrae and the dynamic variations in bone structure across different neck positions, thereby fully representing the comprehensive characteristics of the upper cervical spine. Furthermore, a modified focal loss function is employed to enable the model to learn the mutual exclusivity or conditionally dependent relationships among the six abnormalities. A total of 249 patients participated in the study, contributing 747 X-ray images. The model achieved a mean AUC of 96.22% and a mean mAP of 94.6% on the test set, indicating promising diagnostic performance and validating the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed model.Keywords: Deep learning, X-ray images, Upper cervical spine abnormalities, Multi-label classification, Hybrid feature extraction, AI-Assisted diagnosis

Introduction

The upper cervical spine, particularly the atlantoaxial joint, plays a critical role in head mobility and structural stability. Anomalies in this region can lead to localized biomechanical alterations, resulting in abnormal stress distribution at the C1–C2 articular surfaces and increasing the risk of atlantoaxial dislocation (AAD) [1, 2]. AAD may cause posterior compression of the upper cervical spinal cord, potentially leading to cervical spondylotic myelopathy. Patients often exhibit symptoms such as cranial nerve palsies, pronounced gait disturbances, and respiratory dysfunction, which in severe cases may progress to life-threatening conditions and significantly impair daily functioning. However, due to the inherent characteristics of X-ray imaging techniques, such as potential image overlap in multiple anatomical regions, compromising the resolution of anatomical structures, and hindering accurate diagnosis. Given the low incidence rate of this condition in primary health care, along with limited experience in diagnosing and treating it among doctors, leading to substantial heterogeneity in diagnosing, which contributes to a high rate of missed diagnoses or misdiagnoses, significantly impacts early detection and treatment, as well as disease prognosis [3].

X-rays are indispensable in medical imaging, extensively utilized in various fields such as orthopedics, pulmonology, and cardiology, significantly contributing to the enhancement of diagnostic capabilities in clinical practice [4]. However, X-ray images are limited by their provision of only planar information. The potential overlap of structures in 2D images presents challenges for automatic recognition, thereby increasing the difficulty of distinguishing different anatomical elements. Meanwhile, many advanced deep learning technologies aim to provide auxiliary scientific methods for screening, diagnosis, treatment planning, guidance, and efficacy evaluation of various significant diseases in clinical medicine [5, 6]. Therefore, in recent years, studies have increasingly applied deep learning methods to X-ray diagnostics to assist physicians, aiming to minimize the risks of missed and inaccurate diagnoses. Shen et al. [7] developed YOLO-XRAY, a modified YOLOv5 architecture featuring dual decoders for simultaneous vertebral body detection and segmentation in OVF diagnosis, achieving 96.85% accuracy. Lee et al. [8] used a convolutional neural network that achieved 87.1% accuracy in detecting cervical spondylotic myelopathy by analyzing vertebral alignment patterns in lateral cervical radiographs. Similarly, Xie et al. [9] implemented a ResNet-34-based model with non-local attention modules for cervical spondylosis identification, achieving 89.7% accuracy through vertebral joint localization. Hong et al. [10] introduced an EfficientNet-based framework for simultaneous vertebral fracture detection and osteoporosis assessment, demonstrating radiologist-level performance on lateral spine radiographs. These studies demonstrate the suitability of deep learning backbones, such as YOLOv5, ResNet, and EfficientNet, for interpreting the complex anatomical features presented in cervical spinal X-ray images.

Current applications of X-rays in the automatic diagnosis of Spinal diseases primarily focus on specific abnormalities, such as scoliosis, osteoporosis, and lumbar spondylosis [11]. Ruchi et al. [12] developed an enhanced convolutional neural network (CNN) model for lumbar spine disease detection, achieving a classification accuracy of 94.2%, with a sensitivity of 92.3% and specificity of 95.1%. Similarly, Amin et al. [13] applied transfer learning techniques for the automatic detection and classification of scoliosis from spine X-rays, reporting an accuracy of 89.5%. Amiya et al. [14] used deep learning methods to identify low bone mass in osteoporotic X-ray images, achieving an accuracy of 91.6% and a sensitivity of 89.2%. Although these studies have demonstrated the diagnostic potential of deep learning, most focus on isolated diseases and static image features. There is a lack of modeling for sequential dependencies across multi-positional X-ray images and the capability to simultaneously diagnose multiple coexisting abnormalities. These limitations are particularly pronounced in upper cervical spine diagnostics, where the relative positioning of anatomical structures at different time points and neck postures also serves as an essential basis for diagnosis [15]. Meanwhile, the automated classification of multiple abnormalities in the upper cervical spine remains less explored [16]. The upper cervical spine, located at the base of the skull, presents additional challenges due to its complex anatomy, often leading to overlapping structures in lateral X-ray images, which complicates the diagnostic process [17]. This issue is particularly pronounced in the atlantoaxial region, where multiple abnormalities converge, making differentiation difficult even for experienced clinicians. Therefore, this study develops an automated classification model targeting multiple upper cervical spine abnormalities, with a particular focus on the atlantoaxial region, to explore the potential of aiding in the diagnosis of complex abnormalities. This area has received limited attention in existing research and holds substantial potential for clinical application [18].

This research explores the characteristics of upper cervical spine abnormalities, particularly focusing on atlantoaxial pathology. We have analyzed six abnormalities: Anterior atlantoaxial dislocation, Posterior atlantoaxial dislocation, basilar invagination, odontoid nonunion, atlas occipitalization, and C2–C3 nonsegmentation, all discernible through X-ray images. Through meticulous analysis and collaboration with medical experts, a comprehensive understanding of the unique attributes of each pathological abnormality has been gained. Proposed method

Dataset description

Dataset construction

A retrospective study was conducted on all patients who underwent surgery for atlantoaxial dislocation in the Department of Orthopedics at Peking University Third Hospital from January 1, 2022 to December 31, 2022. The clinical data and extension, neutral, and flexion lateral view X-ray images of these patients were collected, while exclusion criteria included: (1) previous history of atlantoaxial internal fixation; (2) incomplete imaging data.

The dataset for model development was constructed using de-identified and annotated X-ray images.The annotation process of images consisted of two steps, with the first step being the diagnostic labeling. An independent investigator retrospectively extracted diagnoses from imaging reports and medical records; an orthopaedic surgeon with 5 years of experience independently interpreted all radiographs and made a diagnosis without access to medical records and surgical data. In cases where two independent imaging findings were inconsistent, another orthopaedic surgeon made the final decision. All the labels were reviewed by a senor expert. The second step was annotation of bounding box. As shown in Fig. 3, the criteria were defined to encompass specific anatomical landmarks: the upper boundary exceeding the foramen magnum, the lower boundary extending below the C3 vertebra, the posterior boundary encapsulating the C1–C3 spinous processes, and the anterior boundary surpassing the posterior edge of the hard palate. This demarcated region is identified as encompassing the key locations necessary for accurate disease diagnosis.

Annotation of bounding boxes in different postures

Data summary

After applying exclusion criteria, the final cohort comprised 249 patients, including 124 males and 125 females, with ages ranging from 5 to 78 years. The mean age was 46 years, and the median age was 50 years. Each patient provided three lateral X-ray images, resulting in a total of 747 images.

Among the 747 images, 571 images(76.4%) exhibited anterior dislocation of the atlas, 40(5.4%) images exhibited posterior dislocation of the atlas, 249(33.3%) images exhibited basilar invagination, 264(35.3%) images exhibited a non-continuous odontoid process, 255(34.1%) images exhibited occipitalization of the atlas, and 159(21.3%) images exhibited incomplete segmentation of C2–C3. The distribution of cases in the dataset aligns with the real-world prevalence of upper cervical spine abnormalities, where certain conditions–such as posterior atlantoaxial dislocation–occur infrequently.

To ensure reliable model evaluation and avoid overfitting, particularly given the class imbalance in our dataset, we adopted a five-fold cross-validation strategy at the patient level. The entire dataset was evenly divided into five non-overlapping folds. In each iteration, one fold was designated as the test set, while the remaining four folds were used for model development. Importantly, all images from the same patient were kept within a single subset (training, validation, or testing) in each fold to prevent data leakage.

Training network model

To provide a clear understanding of the overall system design, this section begins with an overview of the proposed CerviHFENet framework, as illustrated in Fig. 4. The model is composed of three main modules: (1) Bounding Box Detection, which utilizes Faster R-CNN to extract clinically relevant regions of interest (ROIs) from input lateral X-ray images; (2) Hybrid Feature Extraction, which combines ResNet-based static feature extraction for extension, neutral, and flexion views, followed by an LSTM-based sequence modeling that captures sequential relationships across views; and (3) Classification, where hybrid features are transformed into multi-label predictions of six cervical abnormalities. Medical prior knowledge, including mutual exclusivity and conditional dependency among abnormalities, is integrated into the framework at two stages. During training, it is encoded into a modified focal loss function to guide the optimization process. During inference, it is applied through a medical rule refinement mechanism that adjusts the sigmoid outputs to ensure clinically consistent results.

A schematic illustration of our proposed CerviHFENet framework

Bounding box detection

Initially, images are processed through a bounding box detection phase using the Faster R-CNN algorithm [19]. Faster R-CNN integrates a Region Proposal Network (RPN) that dynamically proposes candidate object boundaries and an ROI Head that refines these proposals into precise bounding boxes. To ensure that all six abnormalities are appropriately captured, we adaptively adjust the size and position of the bounding boxes to focus on small regions of interest (ROI) containing all the relevant pathological information. After the bounding boxes are detected and adjusted, the images are cropped to retain only the information within these ROI regions before proceeding to feature extraction. To ensure consistency across all samples, the cropped ROI images are uniformly resized to 224 224 pixels before being fed into the feature extraction module. This phase aims to direct subsequent analytical models to critical pathological regions, minimizing distractions from non-essential areas.

Hybrid feature extraction

The images, cropped to include only the detected ROI regions, are processed for feature extraction. As shown in Fig. 5, the Extension, Neutral, and Flexion view X-ray images of these patients are individually passed through the ResNet50 network, which extracts the static features for each posture [20]. To address challenges such as gradient vanishing and explosion, commonly encountered in deep learning, ResNet50 employs a residual learning framework. This approach facilitates efficient optimization, reduces computational complexity, and mitigates performance degradation as the network depth increases. A four-layer residual block is used in our implementation. To ensure consistent feature size, a global average pooling (GAP) layer is appended after the convolutional backbone, yielding a fixed-length feature vector for each image in the sequence. This operation reduces each spatial feature map to a single scalar value, resulting in a tensor of size . The output is then flattened to form a spatially pooled vector , which serves as the compact representation of the input image and is used as the input to the sequential modeling module.

After static feature extraction and dimensional standardization via adaptive average pooling, features from the three postures (Extension, Neutral, and Flexion) are sequentially input into a sequence modeling network. Clinically, evaluating atlantoaxial stability and cervical spine abnormalities requires comparative analysis across these dynamic views, which reflect the biomechanical behavior of the cervical spine under varying physiological loads. These views form a structured temporal sequence rather than independent snapshots. This temporal arrangement simulates the physiological motion of the cervical spine, enabling the model to learn how structural abnormalities evolve across postures. Sequential features in this context capture the positional, structural, and morphological changes of the upper cervical spine during these transitions. By modeling these posture-induced variations, the network learns dynamic pathological patterns for detecting motion-sensitive abnormalities.To model the dependencies and transitions among these postures, we employ a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network [21]. LSTMs capability to retain contextual memory enables it to identify motion-consistent abnormalities by leveraging both short- and long-range dependencies within the postural feature sequence. By treating the three views as an integrated diagnostic sequence, the LSTM aligns with clinicians’ interpretive strategy of identifying motion-sensitive pathology through comparative analysis. As a recurrent neural network specialized for sequential data, the LSTM mimics clinical reasoning by capturing temporal relationships between features across views. Its architecture–comprising input, forget, and output gates along with memory cells–controls information flow, enabling effective long-term dependency capture and memory retention. This makes LSTM highly suitable for the classification task involving sequential postural image features. The LSTM network used in our model comprises a single layer with a hidden size of 128. It processes the sequence of 2048-dimensional feature vectors corresponding to the three postures. The final hidden state of the LSTM is then passed to a fully connected layer for the multi-label classification task.

By integrating the static feature extraction capabilities of ResNet50 with the sequential modeling strengths of LSTM, our CerviHFE model provides an effective framework specifically tailored for the joint diagnosis of diverse upper cervical spine abnormalities.

Experimental result

Implementation detail

In our experiments, we employed a 5-fold cross-validation strategy. For each fold, training was conducted using a batch size of 6, and the Adam optimizer was used with an initial learning rate of . The learning rate was decayed by a factor of 0.1 every 30 epochs. A classification threshold of 0.5 was applied. The model was trained for 200 epochs, utilizing a momentum of 0.9 and a weight decay of . For feature extraction, the model incorporated 4 attention heads with a hidden size of 128. All training and evaluation processes were implemented in Python 3.8.5 using PyTorch 1.12.1+cu116. The experiments were conducted on a workstation running Ubuntu 18.04.5 LTS, equipped with an RTX A6000 GPU with 48 GB of memory.

Comparative evaluation with other models

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed CerviHFENet model, a comparative analysis was conducted against several state-of-the-art architectures, including DenseNet169, MobileViTv2, ResNet50, ResNet101, and EfficientNetV2. All models were trained and tested on the identical set of cropped region-of-interest (ROI) images generated by the Faster R-CNN module, under the same experimental conditions on the multi-label classification task involving upper cervical spine abnormalities. Given the multi-label nature of the task, F1-score, AUC, and mAP were selected as primary evaluation metrics due to their sensitivity to class imbalance and their ability to reflect both classification quality and ranking performance.

As shown in Fig. 7, CerviHFENet consistently outperformed other models across all evaluation metrics, highlighting its comparative advantage in accurately and reliably identifying multiple structural abnormalities from lateral cervical spine radiographs. The performance advantage of CerviHFENet is particularly evident in terms of F1-score and Jaccard index, which reflect the model’s ability to detect the presence of each pathological condition with both high precision and recall. This suggests that CerviHFENet effectively captures and integrates discriminative anatomical features that are critical for distinguishing multiple co-occurring abnormalities. These results validate the architectural design and domain-specific enhancements of CerviHFENet for clinical tasks involving automated, multi-label diagnosis of upper cervical spine abnormalities.

Discussion

Despite increasing interest in spine imaging analysis, current research on cervical spine X-ray classification largely remains confined to single-label or multi-class classification tasks. For example, DeepSpine [23] utilizes deep convolutional neural networks for multi-class classification of spinal disorders. Vision Transformer-based cervical fracture classification models, focuses on single-abnormality classification [24]. These approaches do not capture the clinical reality where patients often present with multiple, concurrent spinal abnormalities. In contrast, multi-label classification has been extensively explored in chest X-ray imaging. For instance, Chen et al. [25] proposed a two-stream collaborative network with lung segmentation, achieving a mean AUC of 0.823 on the ChestX-ray14 dataset across 14 thoracic conditions. Jung et al. [26] developed a weakly supervised disease localization method based on disease masks, reporting a mean AUC of 0.830. These results demonstrate the viability of multi-label learning in radiology, particularly when diseases exhibit overlapping patterns. In this context, our proposed CerviHFENet achieves a mean AUC of 96.22% across six upper cervical spine abnormalities, outperforming the above models and indicating the potential of cervical spine X-ray images for multi-label classification.

The three key innovations of our model each make significant contributions to its overall performance: LSTM-based temporal modeling, ROI localization, and our modified loss function. Both the LSTM module and the loss function incorporate prior medical knowledge–respectively modeling inter-postural feature dependencies and clinical co-occurrence or exclusion patterns among labels, thereby enhancing the model’s interpretability. Their inclusion each led to an approximate 2% increase in AUC, suggesting their contribution to improved diagnostic accuracy. Additionally, ROI localization, by directing attention to anatomically relevant regions, significantly boosted mAP by over 14%, emphasizing its critical role in spatial precision and clinical focus. It should be emphasized that the proposed CerviHFENet is intended for diagnostic assistance. The system is designed to support clinicians after patients have been referred for radiographic examination due to clinical symptoms. In this setting, overall diagnostic accuracy and mAP are more clinically meaningful than recall or precision alone, as they reflect the model’s ability to distinguish among multiple, coexisting upper cervical abnormalities. This clarification aligns the reported metrics with the system’s intended use and practical deployment scenario. Nevertheless, future work will aim to further enhance both sensitivity and precision to develop a more comprehensive and clinically robust framework.

However, this study also has certain limitations. The dataset was collected from a single center and includes a limited number of patients, with class imbalance for some abnormalities. Such limitations partly stem from the fact that existing datasets for cervical spine analysis typically focus on single or multiple independent abnormalities, whereas the dataset in this study provides joint annotations for multiple interrelated upper cervical spine abnormalities, which remain rare and challenging to construct. While the reliability was supported by five-fold cross-validation, its generalizability requires further confirmation through large-scale, multi-center studies. Future work will therefore focus on multi-center collaboration to externally validate and refine the model, facilitating its broader clinical translation.

Furthermore, this work opens up several other promising avenues for future research. Integrating multi-modal imaging data, such as combining CT for detailed 3D bone structures and MRI for soft tissue assessment, could provide a more comprehensive representation of cervical anatomy and pathology. Extending this framework to incorporate 3D convolutional neural networks (3D CNNs) for CT/MRI data [27] could enable the modeling of the complex three-dimensional anatomy of the atlantoaxial joint, facilitating the automated quantification of critical spatial parameters and the detection of subtle abnormalities not discernible in two-dimensional projections. The development of such multi-modal and 3D-aware systems would represent a significant advancement toward a more precise and comprehensive AI-assisted diagnostic framework.

Conclusion

In this study, we proposed CerviHFENet, a deep learning framework for multi-label classification of upper cervical spine abnormalities in X-ray images, particularly targeting the atlantoaxial region. The model simultaneously identifies six categories of abnormalities and achieved favorable performance compared to several representative methods, including EfficientNetV2, MoCo, DenseNet169, MobileViTv2, ResNet50, and ResNet101. CerviHFENet incorporates three key components–ROI localization, static feature extraction, and sequential modeling–that work collaboratively to enhance classification performance. The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach on the collected dataset, indicating its potential in supporting diagnostic tasks involving multiple upper cervical spine abnormalities.

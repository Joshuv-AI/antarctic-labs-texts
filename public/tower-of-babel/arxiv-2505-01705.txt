Finite-free Convolution: Infinitesimal Distributions
Octavio Arizmendi, Daniel Perales, Josue Vazquez-Becerra

Abstract
Finite-free additive and multiplicative convolutions are operations on the set of polynomials with real roots, introduced independently by Szegö and Walsh in the 1920s. These operations have regained some interest, in the last decade, after being rediscovered by Marcus, Spielman, and Srivastava as the expected characteristic polynomial of randomly rotated matrices. They converge, as the degree d d of the polynomials increases, to the additive and multiplicative convolution of measures from free probability of Voiculescu. In this paper, we investigate the fluctuations of order 1 / d 1/d —also known as infinitesimal distributions— related to these two operations and their limiting behavior, providing a detailed description of their convergence. Our approach relies on understanding the infinitesimal moment-cumulant formulas and the corresponding functional relations. We also establish several applications and examples, including instances related to the infinitesimal free convolution of Belinschi and Shlyakhtenko, as well as the computation of infinitesimal distributions after differentiation of polynomials.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{R}_{d}[x]
denote the set of all polynomials in
x
of degree
d
with real coefficients.
Given any polynomials
p_{d},q_{d}\in\mathbb{R}_{d}[x]
, we write them as

p_{d}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}x^{d-k}(-1)^{k}\binom{d}{k}\mathsf{e}_{k}\left(p_{d}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad q_{d}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}x^{d-k}(-1)^{k}\binom{d}{k}\mathsf{e}_{k}\left(q_{d}\right)

for some unique coefficients

\mathsf{e}_{k}\left(p_{d}\right),\mathsf{e}_{k}\left(q_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{R}
.
The
finite-free additive convolution
of
p_{d}
and
q_{d}
is defined as
the polynomial
p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d}\in\mathbb{R}_{d}[x]
given by

[p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d}](x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}x^{d-k}(-1)^{k}\binom{d}{k}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\mathsf{e}_{i}\left(p_{d}\right)\mathsf{e}_{k-i}\left(q_{d}\right)\right].

Additionally, the
finite-free multiplicative convolution
of
p_{d}
and
q_{d}
is
defined as the polynomial
p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d}\in\mathbb{R}_{d}[x]
given by

[p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d}](x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}x^{d-k}(-1)^{k}\binom{d}{k}\Big[\mathsf{e}_{k}\left(p_{d}\right)\mathsf{e}_{k}\left(q_{d}\right)\Big].

A century ago, these two operations were studied independently by Szegö
[
Sze22
]
and Walsh
[
Wal22
]
, and they have recently been rediscovered by Marcus, Spielman, and Srivastava
[
MSS22
]
as the expected characteristic polynomials of the sum and product of randomly rotated matrices.
In this paper, we examine the
1/d
behavior of these two polynomial convolutions.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
denote the set of all monic and real-rooted polynomials in
\mathbb{R}_{d}[x]
. For any polynomial
p_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
, we write its
d
roots counting multiplicities as

\lambda_{d}(p_{d})\leq\lambda_{d-1}(p_{d})\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{1}(p_{d}).

And we identify each
p_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
with the discrete probability distribution
\mu_{p_{d}}
on
\mathbb{R}
that puts a mass of
1/d
at each
\lambda_{k}(p_{d})
, called the
root distribution
of
p_{d}
, that is,

\mu_{p_{d}}=\frac{1}{d}\sum^{d}_{k=1}\delta_{\lambda_{k}(p_{d})}.

The set
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
is closed under finite-free additive convolution, i.e.,
p_{d},q_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
implies
p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
, so the finite-free additive convolution of real-rooted polynomials is also real-rooted, see
[
Wal22
]
.
Real-rootedness is also preserved under multiplicative convolution if at least one of the polynomials has no negative roots.
Concretely, letting
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
denote the set of all polynomials in
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
with no negative roots, we have that
p_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
and
q_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
implies
p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
, see
[
Sze22
]

Let
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
denote the set of all probability distributions on
\mathbb{R}
. Given a sequence of polynomials
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
with
p_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
for each
d\geq 1
,
we say that it has
limiting (root) distribution

\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
if the sequence
(\mu_{p_{d}})_{d\geq 1}
converges in moments to
\mu
, namely, for every integer
n\geq 0
, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{n}\,d\mu_{p_{d}}(t)\,=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\lambda^{n}_{k}(p_{d})\ \longrightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{n}\,d\mu(t)=:m_{n}(\mu)\quad\text{as}\quad d\to\infty.

(1)

Moreover, the
n
-th infinitesimal moment
of
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
is defined as

m_{n}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})=\lim_{d\to\infty}d\,\left(m_{n}(\mu_{p_{d}})-m_{n}(\mu)\right),

(2)

if such limit exists.
Note that in this case we have a truncated expansion in
1/d
of the form

m_{n}(\mu_{p_{d}})=m_{n}(\mu)+\frac{1}{d}\,m_{n}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})+o(d^{-1})

(3)

where
o(d^{-k})
denotes a corresponding correction term satisfying
\lim_{d\to\infty}d^{k}\cdot o(d^{-k})=0
.
When
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
has infinitesimal moments of all orders, we identify the sequence
(m_{n}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p}))_{n\geq 1}
with the unique linear functional
\mu^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\mu^{\prime}(x^{n})=m_{n}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})
for every
n\geq 0
.
Note that we always have
\mu^{\prime}(1)=0
.
We refer to the pair
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
as the
infinitesimal distribution
of
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
.

The notion of infinitesimal distribution
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
—for a non-commutative variable— appeared first in the work of Belinschi and Shlyakhtenko
[
BS12
]
, as a generalization of the Type B distributions of Biane, Goodman, and Nica
[
BGN03
]
and only requiring
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
to be linear functionals on
\mathbb{C}[x]
with
\mu(1)=1
and
\mu^{\prime}(1)=0
. The idea behind it is to capture at the same time the zeroth and next-order behavior in the convergence of a sequence, examining not only the limiting object but also the way the sequence fluctuates around it.
Thus, related intrinsically to our work, we find the infinitesimal distributions
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
coming from the eigenvalue distribution of large random matrices, see
[
Joh98
,
MN04
,
DE06
,
Min19
]
, where
\mu
is a probability distribution on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mu^{\prime}
is a signed measure on
\mathbb{R}
with total mass
\mu^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})=0
.
In the present work, we take
\mu
as a probability distribution on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mu^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}
as a linear functional with
\mu^{\prime}(1)=0
, and we will identify
\mu^{\prime}
with a signed measure when appropriate.

Main question.

If
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
and
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
are two sequences of polynomials with infinitesimal distributions
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
and
(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
, respectively,
what is the infinitesimal distribution of the finite-free additive convolution
\mathfrak{p}\boxplus\mathfrak{q}:=(p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
and the finite-free multiplicative convolution
\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q}:=(p_{d}\boxtimes q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
?

Moment-cumulant relations.

It is already known from
[
Mar21
,
AP18
,
AGVP23
]
that if
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
and
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
have limiting distributions
\mu
and
\nu
, respectively, then the finite-free convolutions
\mathfrak{p}\boxplus\mathfrak{q}=(p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
and
\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q}=(p_{d}\boxtimes q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
converge to the free additive and multiplicative convolution
\mu\boxplus\nu
and
\mu\boxtimes\nu
from
[
Voi91
]
.
One of our main contributions is to provide explicit formulas for the infinitesimal moments

m_{n}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p}\boxplus\mathfrak{q})=\lim_{d\to\infty}d\left(m_{n}(\mu_{p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d}})-m_{n}(\mu\boxplus\nu)\right)\quad\text{and}\quad m_{n}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q})=\lim_{d\to\infty}d\left(m_{n}(\mu_{p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d}})-m_{n}(\mu\boxtimes\nu)\right),

see Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
3.6
.
These infinitesimal moments complete the picture for the infinitesimal distribution of
\mathfrak{p}\boxplus\mathfrak{q}
and
\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q}
, however, a lot more can be derived from translating our combinatorial formulas into functional relations of formal power series, and vice versa, as we describe in more detail below.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
be probability distributions with finite moments of all orders.
Recall that the
free cumulants
of
\mu
is the sequence
r_{1}(\mu),r_{2}(\mu),\ldots
recursively defined through the
moment-cumulant formula

m_{n}(\mu)\ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}r_{\pi}(\mu)\quad\text{with}\quad r_{\pi}(\mu):=\prod_{V\in\pi}r_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mu)

(4)

with
NC(n)
the set of all non-crossing partitions of
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
for every
n\geq 1
, see Section
2
for more details on partitions.
A fundamental feature of free cumulants is that they linearize free additive convolution, that is,

r_{n}(\mu\boxplus\nu)=r_{n}(\mu)+r_{n}(\nu)

for all
n\geq 1
.
Moreover, the moment-cumulant formula (
4
) has a simple reformulation in terms of formal power series.
Indeed, taking the
Cauchy transform
and the
R
-transform
of
\mu
as the formal power series

G_{\mu}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}m_{n}(\mu)\,z^{-n-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad R_{\mu}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}(\mu)\,z^{n-1}\ ,

(5)

respectively,
the moment-cumulant formula (
4
) holds for every
n\geq 1
if and only if
G_{\mu}
and
R_{\mu}
satisfy the functional relation

\textstyle G_{\mu}\left(R_{\mu}(z)+\frac{1}{z}\right)=z\,

(6)

with all operations in (
6
) taken at the level of formal power series.
Analogously, there exists a notion of cumulants for finite-free convolution, which was introduced in
[
AP18
]
.
Concretely, for a polynomial
p_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
, its
finite-free cumulants
is the sequence
\kappa_{1}(p_{d}),

\kappa_{2}(p_{d}),\ldots,\kappa_{d}(p_{d})
where each
\kappa_{n}(p_{d})
is given by

\kappa_{n}(p_{d})=\frac{(-d)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{\pi\in P(n)}\ (-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!\prod_{V\in\pi}\mathsf{e}_{|V|}\left(p_{d}\right)

(7)

with
P(n)
the set of all partitions of
[n]
.
As desired, finite-free cumulants linearize finite-free additive convolution, so given
p_{d},q_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\kappa_{n}(p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d})=\kappa_{n}(p_{d})+\kappa_{n}(q_{d})

for all
n\geq 1
.
While (
7
) is a cumulant-coefficient formula, moment-coefficient and moment-cumulant formulas were also provided in
[
AP18
]
.
In particular, for some of our results, we depart from
[
AGVP23
, Theorem 1.3]
, which states that

m_{n}(\mu_{p_{d}})\ \ \,=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\kappa_{\pi}(p_{d})\ -\ \frac{n}{2d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{\kappa_{\sigma}(p_{d})}{ts}\ \ +\ \ o(d^{-1})

(8)

with
S_{NC}(t,s)
the set of all non-crossing annular permutations in a
(t,s)
-annulus.

Infinitesimal dirstributions.
As a consequence of (

), we establish in Lemma

Infinitesimal transforms.
A natural question then relates to the connection between the infinitesimal distribution (
15
) and the notion of infinitesimal freeness from
[
FN10
,
BS12
]
.
For this, let us recall that the
infinitesimal free cumulants
of
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
, denoted by
(r^{\prime}_{n}(\mu,\mu^{\prime}))_{n\geq 1}
, are recursively defined via the
infinitesimal moment-cumulant formula

r^{\prime}_{n}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{V\in\pi}m_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mu^{\prime})\,m_{\pi\setminus V}(\mu)\,\text{M\"{o}b}(\pi,1_{n})

for every
n\geq 1
.
This formula can be inverted and, given
(r_{n}(\mu))_{n\geq 1}
and
(m_{n}(\mu))_{n\geq 1}
, the sequences
(r^{\prime}_{n}(\mu,\mu^{\prime}))_{n\geq 1}
and
(m_{n}(\mu^{\prime}))_{n\geq 1}
determine each other, see Proposition
2.3
.
Moreover, the
infinitesimal Cauchy transform
and the
infintesimal
R
-transform
of
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
as formal power series are respectively given by

G_{\mu^{\prime}}(z)\,=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}m_{n}(\mu^{\prime})\,z^{-n-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}(z)\ =\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{\prime}_{n}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\,z^{n-1}.

Furthermore, these formal power series satisfy the relations

G_{\mu^{\prime}}\ =-(R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\circ G_{\mu})\,G^{\prime}_{\mu}\qquad\text{and}\qquad R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}=-(G_{\mu^{\prime}}\circ K_{\mu})\,K_{\mu}^{\prime}\quad\text{where}\quad K_{\mu}=R_{\mu}+\tfrac{1}{z}.

Then, a pair
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
is the
infinitesimal free additive convolution
of
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
and
(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
, denoted
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\boxplus_{B}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
, if
\gamma=\mu\boxplus\nu
and
\gamma^{\prime}
has Cauchy transform given by

G_{\gamma^{\prime}}=-(R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\circ G_{\mu\boxplus\nu})\cdot G^{\prime}_{\mu\boxplus\nu}-(R^{inf}_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\circ G_{\mu\boxplus\nu})\cdot G^{\prime}_{\mu\boxplus\nu}\,.

(17)

Relation to infinitesimal freeness.
The discrepancy between (
17
) and the first two terms in the right-hand side of (
15
) boils down to the deviation of
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{q}}
from
R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}
and

, respectively.
In Lemma
4.5
, we prove that
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and

relate to each other through the equation

\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)=R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}(z)-(H_{\mu}\circ K_{\mu})\cdot K^{\prime}_{\mu}.

(18)

Moreover, since

is never a constant function, the formal power series
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}

are the same if and only if

.
An analogous equation holds for

A crude relation between

and

Additionally, without resorting to the theory of subordination functions, we show in Corollary

and

coincide if either

or

This allows us to recover and generalize the infinitesimal distributions obtained in

Outline of the paper.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the notation used in this paper regarding set partitions, permutations, and polynomials.
We also survey some notions and results from free probability and finite-free probability that will be needed for upcoming sections.

2.1
Partitions and permutations

2.1.1
Set partitions

A
(set) partition
of the set
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
is a collection
\pi=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{k}\}
of pairwise disjoint and non-empty subsets
V_{i}\subset[n]
whose union covers all of
[n]
, i.e.,
V_{i}\neq\emptyset
,
V_{i}\cap V_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
, and
V_{1}\cup\dots\cup V_{k}=[n]
.
The subsets
V_{i}
are called the
blocks
of
\pi
, and the number of blocks is denoted by
|\pi|
. The set of all partitions of
[n]
is denoted by
P(n)
.

A partition
\pi=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{r}\}\in P(n)
is called
non-crossing
if for every
1\leq a<b<c<d\leq n
such that
a,c\in V_{i}
and
b,d\in V_{j}
, it necessarily follows that
V_{i}=V_{j}
, meaning all four elements belong to the same block.
The set of all non-crossing partitions of
[n]
is denoted by
{NC}(n)
.
We refer the reader to
[
NS06
]
for a detailed exposition on non-crossing partitions in free probability.

The set
P(n)
is endowed with the partial order given by reversed refinement, that is, given
\pi,\sigma\in P(n)
, we write
\pi\leq\sigma
if every block of
\pi
is contained in a block of
\sigma
.
Under this partial order, the set
P(n)
becomes a lattice with minimum
0_{n}:=\{\{1\},\{2\},\dots,\{n\}\}
, the partition with
n
blocks, and maximum
1_{n}:=\{\{1,2\dots,n\}\}
, the partition with only one block.
We use
\pi\vee\sigma
to denote the supremum of the set
\{\sigma,\pi\}
in
P(n)
, called the
join
of
\pi
and
\sigma
.

The
Möbius function on the lattice
P(n)
is the function
\text{M\"{o}b}:P^{(2)}(n)\to\mathbb{R}
with
P^{(2)}(n):=\{(\pi,\theta)\in P(n)\times P(n):\pi\leq\theta\}
given by

\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{n},\pi)=(-1)^{n-|\pi|}\prod_{V\in\pi}(|V|-1)!

for any
\pi\in P(n)
, and extended to a general
(\pi,\theta)\in P^{(2)}(n)
through the natural lattice isomorphism between the interval
[\pi,\theta]:=\{\sigma\in P(n):\pi\leq\sigma\leq\theta\}
and another interval of the form
[0_{k},\rho]
with
k=\left|{\pi}\right|
and
\rho\in P(k)
, yielding
\text{M\"{o}b}(\pi,\theta)=\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{k},\rho)
, see
[
Sta11
, Section 3.10]
.

The set
NC(n)
is also endowed with the partial order given by reversed refinement, making it a sublattice of
P(n)
with
0_{n}
and
1_{n}
as minimum and maximum, respectively, as well in this case.
The
Möbius function on the lattice
NC(n)
, denoted by
\text{M\"{o}b}_{NC}(\cdot,\cdot)
, satisfies

\text{M\"{o}b}_{NC}(0_{n},\pi)=(-1)^{n-|\pi|}\prod_{V\in\pi}C_{|V|-1}

with
\pi\in NC(n)
and
C_{k}=\frac{1}{k+1}\binom{2k}{k}
the
k
-th Catalan number;
for a general
(\pi,\theta)\in NC^{(2)}(n)
, the value
\text{M\"{o}b}_{NC}(\pi,\theta)
is computed through a canonical interval factorization, see
[
NS06
, Lecture 10]
for details.

Let
S_{n}
denote the symmetric group on
[n]
.
For every partition
\pi\in P(n)
, we let
\pi
also denote the unique permutation in
S_{n}
whose cycles are all the blocks of
\pi
written in increasing order.
For instance, the partition
\{\{3,1,5\},\{6,2\},\{4\}\}\in P(6)
becomes the permutation
(1,3,5)(4)(2,6)\in S_{6}
in cycle notation.
Additionally, for every permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
, we let
\sigma
denote the partition in
P(n)
whose blocks are the all cycles of
\sigma
viewed as subsets of
[n]
.
For example, the permutation
(6,3,5)(1,4)(2)\in S_{6}
is associated with the partition
\{\{6,3,5\},\{1,4\},\{2\}\}\in P(6)
.
This is certainly an abuse of notation but one that is rather convenient and unharmful for our purposes.

For instance, letting
\gamma_{n}:=(1,2,\ldots,n)\in S_{n}
, the set of non-crossing partitions
NC(n)
is alternatively given by

NC(n)=\{\pi\in P(n):|\pi|+|\pi^{-1}\gamma_{n}|=n+1\}\,.

(20)

where, for a given
\pi\in P(n)
, we first take
\pi^{-1}
as the inverse of
\pi
as a permutation, then we compute the product
\pi^{-1}\gamma_{n}
in
S_{n}
, and finally we consider the permutation
\pi^{-1}\gamma_{n}
as a partition in
P(n)
.
For every non-crossing partition
\pi\in NC(n)
, the permutation
\pi^{-1}\gamma_{n}
viewed as a partition is called the
Kreweras complement
of
\pi
and denoted by
Kr(\pi)
.
Note that
Kr(\pi)\in NC(n)
for any
\pi\in NC(n)
by the defining condition in (
20
).
For a geometric definition of the Kreweras complement, see
[
NS06
, Definition 9.21]
.

2.1.2
Non-crossing annular permutations

The set of
annular non-crossing permutations

S_{NC}(t,s)
can be defined as follows.
First, given integers
t,s\geq 1
, we let
\gamma_{t,s}
denote the permutation

(1,2,\ldots,t)(t+1,t+2\ldots,t+s)\in S_{t+s}
. Then, for a permutation
\sigma\in S_{t+s}
, we write
\sigma\vee\gamma_{t,s}=1_{t+s}
to mean that
\langle\sigma,\gamma_{t,s}\rangle
—the subgroup of
S_{t+s}
generated by
\sigma
and
\gamma_{t,s}
— acts transitively on
[t+s]
, i.e., the group action of
\langle\sigma,\gamma_{t,s}\rangle
on
[t+s]
has a single orbit.
Noticeably, the condition
\sigma\vee\gamma_{t,s}=1_{t+s}
is equivalent to
\sigma
mapping at least one element from
[t]
to an element in
[t+1,s]
.
Finally, we define
S_{NC}(t,s)
as the set of permutations given by

S_{NC}(t,s)=\Big\{\sigma\in S_{t+s}\ :\ \gamma_{t,s}\vee\sigma=1_{t+s}\ \text{ and }\ \left|{\sigma}\right|+\left|{\sigma^{-1}\,\gamma_{t,s}}\right|=t+s\Big\}

where
|\cdot|
gives the number of all distinct cycles in a permutation including fixed points.
A permutation
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)
is called
non-crossing in the
(t,s)
-annulus
, or simply
annular non-crossing
if it is clear which pair of integers
(t,s)
are being considered;
additionally, the permutation
Kr_{t,s}(\sigma):=\sigma^{-1}\gamma_{t,s}
is referred to as the
Kreweras complement
of
\sigma
in the
(t,s)
-annulus.

The term
non-crossing
for permutations in
S_{NC}(t,s)
is due to the fact that their defining conditions are equivalent to the following graphical representation, see Theorem 8 in
[
MS17
, Section 5.1]
.
For it, one starts with an annulus in the plane with the integers
1
to
t
arranged clockwise on the outer circle and the integers
t+1
to
t+s
arranged counterclockwise on the inner circle, this is called a
(t,s)
-annulus
.
Then, a permutation
\sigma\in S_{t+s}
is non-crossing precisely if the cycles of
\sigma
can be represented in such a way that: (i) each cycle of
\sigma
is drawn as a simple closed path oriented clockwise lying between the inner and outer circles and passing through all the numbers in the given cycle, (ii) the inner and outer circles are connected by at least one cycle, (iii) no cycles cross each other.

In the context of random matrices, annular non-crossing permutations were first investigated in
[
MN04
]
as the combinatorial object governing the limiting behavior of the covariance of traces of complex Wishart matrices.
This behavior led to the notion of
second order freeness

[
MS06
]
, and shown to be a common feature for unitarily invariant random matrices in
[
MŚS07
]
.
This notion does not yet appear in our work, so we simply refer the reader to
[
MS17
, Chapter 5]
, and references therein, for more details on this matter.
Nevertheless, annular non-crossing permutations do play a role in the combinatorial description of the infinitesimal distribution of Gaussian Orthogonal Ensembles and real Wishart matrices, see
[
Min19
]
and
[
MVB25
]
, respectively, and so do they, as we will show later on, in the infinitesimal distribution of finite-free convolutions.

2.2
Free probability

2.2.1
Combinatorial and functional relations

In this part, we collect some functional relations of formal power series from free probability, as well as their equivalent formulations in terms of their coefficients.
These relations are interesting on their own, and we will use them in different situations throughout this work.
Because of that, we have opted to present them separately in a general setting.

Notation 2.1
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of numbers.
For
\pi\in P(n)
and
\sigma\in S_{n}
, we let

u_{\pi}=\prod_{V\in\pi}u_{|V|}\qquad\text{and}\qquad u_{\sigma}=\prod_{c\in\sigma}u_{|c|}

where
\left|{\,\cdot\,}\right|
denotes the set cardinality and cycle length, respectively.
For instance, if
\pi=\{\{3,1,5\},\{6,2\},

\{4\}\}\in P(6)
and
\sigma=(3,5)(1,4)(2)\in S_{5}
, then
u_{\pi}=u_{3}\cdot u_{2}\cdot u_{1}
and
u_{\sigma}=u_{2}\cdot u_{2}\cdot u_{1}
.

We first put together various well-known relations from free independence, commonly phrased in terms of the Cauchy transform and the
R
-transform of a probability distribution.
Nonetheless, they hold in the general setting of formal power series.
Proofs for these relations can be found in both
[
NS06
, Lecture 12]
and
[
MS17
, Section 2.4]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(m_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(r_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be any two sequences of numbers and consider the formal power series
G(z)=z^{-1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}m_{n}\,z^{-n-1}
and
K(z)=z^{-1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}\,z^{n-1}
.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

The functional relation
K(G(z))=z
holds at the level of formal power series.

(2)

The functional relation
G(K(z))=z
holds at the level of formal power series.

(3)

The combinatorial formula
m_{n}=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}r_{\pi}
holds for every
n\geq 1
.

(4)

The combinatorial formula
r_{n}=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}m_{\pi}\,\text{M\"{o}b}_{NC}(\pi,1_{n})
holds for every
n\geq 1
.

Conditions (1) and (2) can also be formulated in terms of the formal power series
R(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}\,z^{n-1}
.

We now gather the analogue relations for infinitesimal free independence.
Early versions of some of these can be tracked down to
[
BGN03
]
, with later variations and refinements established in
[
FN10
,
BS12
,
Min19
]
.
Here we provide condensed yet complete proofs based on those from the aforementioned works.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\{m^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{r^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be any two sequences of numbers and consider the formal power series
g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}m^{\prime}_{n}\,z^{-n-1}
and
r(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{\prime}_{n}\,z^{n-1}
.
Suppose that
G(z)
and
K(z)
satisfy any of the four equivalent conditions from Proposition
2.2
.
The following are equivalent:

(1’)

The functional relation
g(z)=-r(G(z))\cdot G^{\prime}(z)
holds at the level of formal power series.

(2’)

The functional relation
r(z)=-g(K(z))\cdot K^{\prime}(z)
holds at the level of formal power series.

(3’)

The combinatorial formula
m^{\prime}_{n}=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{V\in\pi}r^{\prime}_{\left|{V}\right|}\cdot r_{\pi\setminus V}
holds for every
n\geq 1
.

(4’)

The combinatorial formula
r^{\prime}_{n}=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{V\in\pi}m^{\prime}_{\left|{V}\right|}\,m_{\pi\setminus V}\,\text{M\"{o}b}_{NC}(\pi,1_{n})
holds for every
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.2
, we know that
G(K(z))=z
, and consequently
G^{\prime}(K(z))\cdot K^{\prime}(z)=1
.
Then, assuming
(1’)
holds, and replacing
z
with
K(z)
, we obtain
g(K(z))=-r(z)\cdot G^{\prime}(K(z))
, which is equivalent to
(2’)
.
Hence,
(1’)
implies
(2’)
.
A similar argument gives the reversed implication.

The equivalence between
(1’)
and
(3’)
goes as follows.
Letting
m_{0}=1
, and writing
G(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}m_{n}\,z^{-n-1}
, direct computations give

-r(G(z))\cdot G^{\prime}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}=0\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}+k=n\end{subarray}}^{n-k}(n_{k}+1)\cdot r^{\prime}_{k}\cdot m_{n_{1}}\cdots m_{n_{k}}\right)z^{-n-1}.

(21)

Then, using the moment-cumulant formula
(3)
from Proposition
2.2
for
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
, an enumerative argument yields

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}=0\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}+k=n\end{subarray}}^{n-k}(n_{k}+1)\cdot r^{\prime}_{k}\cdot m_{n_{1}}\cdots m_{n_{k}}=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{V\in\pi}r^{\prime}_{\left|{V}\right|}\cdot r_{\pi\setminus V}\ .

(22)

Indeed, writing
V=\{v_{1}<v_{2}<\cdots<v_{k}\}
for each block
V
in the right-hand side of (
22
), each product
r^{\prime}_{\left|{V}\right|}\cdot r_{\pi\setminus V}
corresponds to
r^{\prime}_{k}\cdot r_{\pi_{1}}r_{\pi_{2}}\cdots r_{\pi_{k}}
for some non-crossing partitions
\pi_{1}\in NC(n_{1}),\pi_{2}\in NC(n_{2}),\ldots,\pi_{k}\in NC(n_{k})
with
n_{i}
the number of elements strictly between
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
cyclically, i.e.,
n_{i}=v_{i+1}-v_{i}-1
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k-1
and
n_{k}=n+v_{1}-v_{k}-1
, the factor
(n_{k}+1)
amounts to the different choices
V
associated to the same factor
r^{\prime}_{k}\cdot r_{\pi_{1}}r_{\pi_{2}}\cdots r_{\pi_{k}}
.
I follows from (
21
) and (
22
) that
(1’)
and
(3’)
are equivalent.

Finally, the equivalence between
(3’)
and
(4’)
is due to that of
(3)
and
(4)
from Proposition
2.2
.
Indeed,
(3)
and
(4)
remain equivalent for sequences taken over the two-dimensional Grasmann algebra
\mathcal{C}=\{(\begin{smallmatrix}a&a^{\prime}\\
0&a\end{smallmatrix}):a,a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}\}\subset\mathrm{Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
Thus, letting
\widetilde{m}_{n}=(\begin{smallmatrix}m_{n}&m^{\prime}_{n}\\
0&m_{n}\end{smallmatrix})
and
\widetilde{r}_{n}=(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{n}&r^{\prime}_{n}\\
0&r_{n}\end{smallmatrix})
, we have that
\widetilde{m}_{n}=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\widetilde{r}_{\pi}
if and only if
\widetilde{r}_{n}=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\widetilde{m}_{\pi}\,\text{M\"{o}b}_{NC}(\pi,1_{n})
.
But elementary matrix operations give
\widetilde{r}_{\pi}=(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{\pi}&\sum_{V\in\pi}r^{\prime}_{\left|{V}\right|}\cdot r_{\pi\setminus V}\\
0&r_{\pi}\end{smallmatrix})
and
\widetilde{m}_{\pi}=(\begin{smallmatrix}m_{\pi}&\sum_{V\in\pi}m^{\prime}_{\left|{V}\right|}\cdot m_{\pi\setminus V}\\
0&m_{\pi}\end{smallmatrix})
.
Therefore, the desired conclusion follows since
m_{n}=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}r_{\pi}
and
r_{n}=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}m_{\pi}\,\text{M\"{o}b}_{NC}(\pi,1_{n})
hold already by hypothesis.
∎

2.2.2
Free convolutions

We will only consider distributions determined by their moments, or equivalently, by their free cumulants, in this work.
Therefore, the following combinatorial definition of
\boxplus
, in terms of free cumulants, is enough for our purposes.
Let us recall that, equivalently to the moment-cumulant formula (
4
), the
free cumulants
of a distribution
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
with finite moments of all orders can be defined as the sequence
(r_{n}(\mu))_{n\geq 1}
given for each
n
by

r_{n}(\mu)\ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}m_{\pi}(\mu)\,\text{M\"{o}b}_{NC}(\pi,1_{n}).

Definition 2.4

(Free additive convolution)
.

For probability distributions
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
with finite moments of all orders,
their
free additive convolution
is the unique probability measure
\mu\boxplus\nu
such that

r_{n}(\mu\boxplus\nu)=r_{n}(\mu)+r_{n}(\nu)\quad\forall\ n\geq 1.

The free additive convolution
\boxplus
is a commutative operation on
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
, the set of all probability distributions on
\mathbb{R}
.
It was first defined for compactly supported distributions in

[
Voi86
]
, as the spectral distribution of the sum of freely independent bounded operators, and then extended to arbitrary distributions in
[
Maa92
,
BV93
]
, using the reciprocal Cauchy transform and the theory of unbounded operators affiliated with a von Neumann algebra, respectively.

On the other hand, the free multiplicative convolution
\boxtimes
is an operation that requires at least one of the factors to be in
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, the set of all probability distributions with support on the non-negative real numbers.
It was first defined in

[
Voi87
]
and then extended to full generality in
[
BV93
]
.
As mentioned above, a combinatorial definition of
\boxtimes
is enough for us.

Definition 2.5

(Free multiplicative convolution)
.

For probability distributions
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
with finite moments of all orders,
their
free multiplicative convolution
is the unique probability measure
\mu\boxtimes\nu
such that

r_{n}(\mu\boxtimes\nu)\ \ =\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in NC(n)\end{subarray}}r_{\pi}(\mu)\,r_{Kr(\pi)}(\nu)\quad\forall\ n\geq 1.

2.2.3
Infinitesimal free convolutions

The infinitesimal free additive convolution
\boxplus_{B}
is a commutative operation defined on pairs
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
where
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
has moments of all orders and
\mu^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}
is a linear functional with
\mu^{\prime}(1)=0
, referred to as infinitesimal distributions.
This operation was introduced along with the notion of infinitesimal freeness in
[
BS12
]
, where various scenarios for when the second coordinates
\mu^{\prime}
can be consistently identified with signed measures are established.
As in the previous part, turning to a combinatorial definition of
\boxplus_{B}
suffices for the present work.

Notation 2.6
.

For any linear functional
\mu^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}
,
we let
m_{n}(\mu^{\prime})
denote the value
\mu^{\prime}(x^{n})
for each
n\geq 0
.
In particular, if
\mu^{\prime}
is identified with a signed measure on
\mathbb{R}
, each
m_{n}(\mu^{\prime})
corresponds to the
n
-th moment of
\mu^{\prime}
, namely,
\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{n}\,d\mu^{\prime}(t)
.

The
infinitesimal free cumulants
of
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
is the sequence
(r^{\prime}_{n}(\mu,\mu^{\prime}))_{n\geq 1}
given by the cumulant-moment formula

r^{\prime}_{n}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{V\in\pi}m_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mu^{\prime})\,m_{\pi\setminus V}(\mu)\,\text{M\"{o}b}(\pi,1_{n})\quad\forall\ n\geq 1.

By Proposition
2.3
, this formula can be inverted in a way that the infinitesimal free cumulants can also be defined recursively through the moment-cumulant formula

m_{n}(\mu^{\prime})\ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{V\in\pi}r^{\prime}_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\,r_{\pi\setminus V}(\mu)\quad\forall\ n\geq 1.

It is immediate that the infinitesimal free cumulants
(r^{\prime}_{n}(\mu,\mu^{\prime}))_{n\geq 1}
and the infinitesimal moments
(m_{n}(\mu^{\prime}))_{n\geq 1}
completely determine each other, in view of Proposition
2.3
, and since the same holds for
(r_{n}(\mu))_{n\geq 1}
and
(m_{n}(\mu))_{n\geq 1}
.
With this in hand, we provide the following.

Definition 2.7

(Infinitesimal free additive convolution)
.

For infinitesimal distributions
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
and
(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
, their
infinitesimal free additive convolution

(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\boxplus_{B}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
is defined as the pair
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
where
\gamma=\mu\boxplus\nu
and
\gamma^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}
is the unique linear functional with
\gamma^{\prime}(1)=0
such that

r^{\prime}_{n}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=r^{\prime}_{n}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})+r^{\prime}_{n}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})\quad\forall\ n\geq 1.

(23)

The
infinitesimal Cauchy transform
and the
infintesimal
R
-transform
of
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
as formal power series are respectively given by

G_{\mu^{\prime}}(z)\,=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}m_{n}(\mu^{\prime})\,z^{-n-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}(z)\ =\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{\prime}_{n}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\,z^{n-1}.

(24)

Due to Proposition
2.3
, these formal power series satisfy the relations

G_{\mu^{\prime}}\ =-(R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\circ G_{\mu})\,G^{\prime}_{\mu}\qquad\text{and}\qquad R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}=-(G_{\mu^{\prime}}\circ K_{\mu})\,K_{\mu}^{\prime}.

(25)

Notice that (
23
) written in terms of infintesimal
R
-transforms becomes

R^{inf}_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}(z)=R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}(z)+R^{inf}_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}(z)\,.

It follows that a pair
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
is the infinitesimal free additive convolution
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\boxplus_{B}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
if and only if
\gamma=\mu\boxplus\nu
and
\gamma^{\prime}
has Cauchy transform
G_{\gamma^{\prime}}
given by

G_{\gamma^{\prime}}=-(R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\circ G_{\mu\boxplus\nu})\cdot G^{\prime}_{\mu\boxplus\nu}-(R^{inf}_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\circ G_{\mu\boxplus\nu})\cdot G^{\prime}_{\mu\boxplus\nu}.

(26)

By Proposition
2.3
, the latter is equivalent to the infinitesimal moments
(m_{n}(\gamma^{\prime}))_{n\geq 1}
satisfying

m_{n}(\gamma^{\prime})=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{V\in\pi}\left(r^{\prime}_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})+r^{\prime}_{\left|{V}\right|}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})\right)\cdot r_{\pi\setminus V}(\mu\boxplus\nu)\quad\forall\ n\geq 1.

We now give a combinatorial definition of the infinitesimal free multiplicative convolution
\boxtimes_{B}
.

Definition 2.8

(Infinitesimal free multiplicative convolution)
.

For infinitesimal distributions
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
and
(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
with
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, their
infinitesimal free multiplicative convolution

(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\boxtimes_{B}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
is defined as the pair
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
where
\gamma=\mu\boxtimes\nu
and
\gamma^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}
is the unique linear functional with
\gamma^{\prime}(1)=0
such that

r^{\prime}_{n}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\left(\sum_{V\in\pi}r^{\prime}_{n}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})r_{\pi\backslash V}(\mu)\cdot r_{Kr(\pi)}(\nu)+\sum_{W\in Kr(\pi)}r_{\pi}(\mu)\cdot r^{\prime}_{n}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})r_{Kr(\pi)\backslash W}(\nu)\right)\quad\forall\ n\geq 1.

In terms of moments, see
[
FN10
, Proposition 5.1]
, the last expression is equivalent to

m_{n}(\gamma^{\prime})\ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\left(\sum_{V\in\pi}m_{|V|}(\mu^{\prime})\,m_{\pi\backslash V}(\mu)\cdot r_{Kr(\pi)}(\nu)+\sum_{W\in Kr(\pi)}m_{\pi}(\mu)\cdot r^{\prime}_{\left|{W}\right|}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})\,r_{Kr(\pi)\backslash W}(\nu)\right)\quad\forall\ n\geq 1.

(27)

2.2.4
Subordination

In some instances, namely, Theorem
4.8
and Corollary
4.11
, we will use the analytical approach to infinitesimal convolutions via subordination functions from
[
BS12
]
.
For this, let us recall that the
analytical Cauchy transform
of a possibly signed measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
is given by

\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{z-t}\,d\mu(t)\quad\forall\ z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}.

(28)

This is an analytical map that takes the complex upper half-plane
\mathbb{C}^{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathfrak{Im}(z)>0\}
to the lower half-plane
\mathbb{C}^{-}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathfrak{Im}(z)<0\}
, and vice versa.
Moreover, when
\mu
has compact support,
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(z)
has expansion near infinity, in the sense that

\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}m_{n}(\mu)\,z^{-n-1}

for
\left|{z}\right|
large enough.
If, in addition,
\mu
is a probability distribution, then there exists an analytical map
\mathcal{R}_{\mu}(z)
, called the
analytical
R
-transform
of
\mu
, defined on a punctured neighborhood of
0
through the relation

\mathcal{G}_{\mu}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\mu}(z)+\tfrac{1}{z}\right)=z.

See
[
MS17
, Theorem 3.18]
for more details.
It is known from
[
Voi93
,
Bia98
]
that for any two probability distributions
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
, there exist unique analytic maps
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{+}
such that

\mathcal{G}_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(z)=\mathcal{G}_{\mu}\circ\omega_{1}(z)=\mathcal{G}_{\nu}\circ\omega_{2}(z)=\frac{1}{\omega_{1}(z)+\omega_{2}(z)-z}\qquad\forall\ z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}.

(29)

The maps
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are referred to as
subordination functions
, and next we provide their relation to infinitesimal convolution.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
BS12
, Theorem 26]
)
.

If
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\boxplus_{B}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
, then

\mathcal{G}_{\gamma^{\prime}}=(\mathcal{G}_{\mu^{\prime}}\circ\omega_{1})\,\omega^{\prime}_{1}+(\mathcal{G}_{\nu^{\prime}}\circ\omega_{2})\,\omega^{\prime}_{2}.

Similarly, it is known from
[
Bia98
]
that there exist subordination functions for the multiplicative convolution.
In this case, we must consider the
moment generating function
of a possibly signed measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
given by

\psi_{\mu}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{zt}{1-zt}\,d\mu(t)\quad\forall\ z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}.

Then, for any two probability distributions
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, there exist unique analytic maps
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}:\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
such that

\psi_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(z)=\psi_{\mu}\circ\omega_{1}(z)=\psi_{\nu}\circ\omega_{2}(z)\quad\text{ and}\quad\frac{\psi_{\mu_{1}\boxtimes\mu_{2}}(z)}{1+\psi_{\mu_{1}\boxtimes\mu_{2}}(z)}=\frac{\omega_{1}(z)\omega_{2}(z)}{z}\quad\forall\ z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}.

(30)

Proposition 2.10

(Reformulation of
[
BS12
, Proposition 43]
)
.

If
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\boxtimes_{B}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
, then

\mathcal{G}_{\gamma^{\prime}}=(\mathcal{G}_{\mu^{\prime}}\circ\tau_{1})\tau^{\prime}_{1}+(\mathcal{G}_{\nu^{\prime}}\circ\tau_{2})\tau^{\prime}_{2},

(31)

where
\tau_{i}(z)=\frac{1}{\omega_{i}(1/z)}.

Proof.

Proposition 43 in
[
BS12
]
, states that

\frac{\psi_{\gamma^{\prime}}(z)}{z}=\frac{\psi_{\mu^{\prime}}(\omega_{1}(z))}{\omega_{1}(z)}\omega_{1}^{\prime}(z)+\frac{\psi_{\nu^{\prime}}(\omega_{2}(z))}{\omega_{2}(z)}\omega_{2}^{\prime}(z),

which translates into

\mathcal{G}_{\gamma^{\prime}}(z)=\tfrac{1}{z}\psi_{\gamma^{\prime}}\left(\tfrac{1}{z}\right)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}\frac{\psi_{\mu^{\prime}}(\omega_{1}(1/z))}{\omega_{1}(1/z)}\cdot\omega_{1}^{\prime}(1/z)+\frac{1}{z^{2}}\frac{\psi_{\nu^{\prime}}(\omega_{2}(1/z))}{\omega_{2}(1/z)}\cdot\omega_{2}^{\prime}(1/z)\,.

(32)

The desired relation follows from the identities

\tau^{\prime}_{i}(z)=\frac{\omega^{\prime}_{i}(1/z)}{(z\cdot\omega_{i}(1/z))^{2}}\text{\qquad and \qquad}\omega_{i}(1/z)\cdot\psi_{\mu_{i}^{\prime}}(\omega_{i}(1/z))=\mathcal{G}_{\mu_{i}^{\prime}}\left(\tau_{i}(z)\right).

∎

2.3
Finite free probability

In this part, we collect some results on finite-free probability from
[
AP18
,
AGVP23
]
that are used next.
To this end, recall that any polynomial
p_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
, the set of all monic and real-rooted polynomials in
x
of degree
d
, can be uniquely written as

p_{d}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}x^{d-k}(-1)^{k}\binom{d}{k}\mathsf{e}_{k}\left(p_{d}\right)

for some coefficients
\mathsf{e}_{k}\left(p_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, the
finite-free additive convolution
of
p_{d},q_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
is the polynomial
p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d}\in\mathbb{R}_{d}[x]
given by

[p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d}](x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}x^{d-k}(-1)^{k}\binom{d}{k}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\mathsf{e}_{i}\left(p_{d}\right)\mathsf{e}_{k-i}\left(q_{d}\right)\right];

while their
finite-free multiplicative convolution
is the polynomial
p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d}\in\mathbb{R}_{d}[x]
given by

[p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d}](x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}x^{d-k}(-1)^{k}\binom{d}{k}\Big[\mathsf{e}_{k}\left(p_{d}\right)\mathsf{e}_{k}\left(q_{d}\right)\Big].

Notation 2.11
.

The
root distribution
of
p_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
is the discrete probability distribution
\mu_{p_{d}}
that puts a mass of
1/d
at each of its
d
roots counting multiplicities, written

\lambda_{d}(p_{d})\leq\lambda_{d-1}(p_{d})\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{1}(p_{d})
.
To ease notation, for each
n\geq 0
, we let
m_{n}(p_{d})
denote the
n
-th moment of
\mu_{p_{d}}
, that is,

m_{n}(p_{d})=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\lambda_{i}(p_{d}))^{n}

The
finite-free cumulants
of each
p_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
is the sequence
\kappa_{1}(p_{d}),

\kappa_{2}(p_{d}),\ldots,\kappa_{d}(p_{d})
given by

\kappa_{n}(p_{d})=\frac{(-d)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{\pi\in P(n)}\text{M\"{o}b}(\pi,1_{n})\,\mathsf{e}_{\pi}\left(p_{d}\right)

(33)

with
\mathsf{e}_{\pi}\left(p_{d}\right):=\prod_{V\in\pi}\mathsf{e}_{|\pi|}\left(p_{d}\right)
for
\pi\in P(n)
and
n=1,2,\ldots,d
.
One of the main properties of
\kappa_{n}(\cdot)
is the linearization of the finite-free additive convolution
\boxplus_{d}
.

Proposition 2.12

(
[
AP18
, Proposition 3.6]
)
.

For any
p_{d},q_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
and
1\leq n\leq d
, we have that

\kappa_{n}(p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d})=\kappa_{n}(p_{d})+\kappa_{n}(q_{d})

The cumulant-coefficient formula (
33
) was introduced in
[
AP18
]
, among other relations describing how the sequences
(m_{n}(p_{d}))_{n\geq 1}
,
(\kappa_{n}(p_{d}))_{n=1}^{d}
, and
(\mathsf{e}_{n}\left(p_{d}\right))_{n=1}^{d}
determine each other explicitly.
In particular, it was proved in
[
AP18
, Corollary 4.6]
that the following cumulant-moment formula holds

\kappa_{n}(p_{d})=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi,\theta\in P(n)\\
\pi\lor\theta=1_{n}\end{subarray}}d^{|\pi|+|\theta|-n-1}\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{n},\pi)\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{n},\theta)m_{\pi}(p_{d})\,.

(34)

The next theorem, concerning finite-free multiplicative convolution, is, in a certain way, a departing point of our work.

Theorem 2.13

(
[
AGVP23
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

For any
p_{d},q_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
and
1\leq n\leq d
, we have that

\kappa_{n}(p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d})=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi,\theta\in P(n)\\
\pi\lor\theta=1_{n}\end{subarray}}d^{|\pi|+|\theta|-n-1}\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{n},\pi)\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{n},\theta)\kappa_{\pi}(p_{d})\kappa_{\theta}(q_{d})

(35)

and

m_{n}(p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d})=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi,\theta\in P(n)\\
\pi\lor\theta=1_{n}\end{subarray}}d^{|\pi|+|\theta|-n-1}\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{n},\pi)\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{n},\theta)\kappa_{\pi}(p_{d})m_{\theta}(q_{d})\,.

(36)

It was also shown in
[
AGVP23
, Lemma 3.3, Lemma 4.4]
that for any two sequences of numbers
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(v_{n})_{n\geq 1}
we have

\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}u_{\pi}\,v_{Kr(\pi)}=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi,\theta\in P(n)\\
\pi\lor\theta=1_{n}\\
|\pi|+|\theta|=n+1\end{subarray}}\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{n},\pi)\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{n},\theta)u_{\pi}v_{\theta}

and

-\frac{n}{2d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{u_{\sigma}\,v_{Kr_{t,s}(\sigma)}}{ts}=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi,\theta\in P(n)\\
\pi\lor\theta=1_{n}\\
|\pi|+|\theta|=n\end{subarray}}\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{n},\pi)\text{M\"{o}b}(0_{n},\theta)u_{\pi}v_{\theta}

for any
n\geq 1
.
And it turns out that all the partitions
\pi,\theta\in P(n)
with
\pi\lor\theta=1_{n}
and
\left|{\pi}\right|+\left|{\theta}\right|>n+1
yield a net zero contribution to both (
35
) and (
36
).
Thus, as a consequence of Theorem
2.13
we obtain this.

Corollary 2.14
.

For any
p_{d},q_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
and
1\leq n\leq d
, we have that

\kappa_{n}(p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d})\ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\kappa_{\pi}(p_{d})\,\kappa_{Kr(\pi)}(q_{d})\ -\ \frac{n}{2d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{\kappa_{\sigma}(p_{d})\,\kappa_{Kr_{t,s}(\sigma)}(q_{d})}{ts}\ \ +\ \ o\left(1/d\right)

(37)

and

m_{n}(p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d})\ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\kappa_{\pi}(p_{d})\,m_{Kr(\pi)}(q_{d})\ -\ \frac{n}{2d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\

(38)

While not explicitly stated, the previous two relations are part of

, Proof of Theorem 1.3]
.
Additionally, since finite-free multiplicative convolution is commutative, then (

m_{n}(p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d})\ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}m_{\pi}(p_{d})\,\kappa_{Kr(\pi)}(q_{d})\ -\ \frac{n}{2d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{m_{\sigma}(p_{d})\,\kappa_{Kr_{t,s}(\sigma)}(q_{d})}{ts}\ \ +\ \ o\left(1/d\right).

(39)

Moreover, letting

(Terms of order

.

For any
p_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
and

m_{n}(p_{d})\ \ \,=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\kappa_{\pi}(p_{d})\ -\ \frac{n}{2d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{\kappa_{\sigma}(p_{d})}{ts}\ \ +\ \ o(d^{-1})

(40)

From (

(

\boxplus_{d}

\boxplus_{d}

\boxplus
as

\boxtimes

3
Combinatorial relations for infinitesimal moments and finite free cumulants

In this section, we calculate the infinitesimal moments arising from the finite-free additive and multiplicative convolution of polynomials with given infinitesimal distribution.

3.1
Finite Cumulant Fluctuations

To start, we show that a sequence of polynomials with limiting root distribution has infinitesimal moments of all orders if and only if all finite-free cumulants have an expansion in
1/d
up to a correction terms of order
o(1/d)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a sequence of polynomials
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
with limiting distribution
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
,
the following two conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The sequence
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
has infinitesimal moments of all orders,
namely, there exists a sequence
(m_{n}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p}))_{n\geq 1}
that does not depend on
d
such that

m_{n}(p_{d})=m_{n}(\mu)+\frac{1}{d}\,m_{n}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})+o(1/d)\quad\forall\ n\geq 1.

(41)

(2)

There exists a sequence
(\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{p}))_{n\geq 1}
that does not depend on
d
such that

\kappa_{n}(p_{d})=r_{n}(\mu)+\frac{1}{d}\,\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})+o(1/d)\quad\forall\ d\geq n\geq 1.

(42)

Moreover, if any of two conditions above holds, then the sequences
(m_{n}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p}))_{n\geq 1}
and
(\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{p}))_{n\geq 1}
determine each other through the relation

m^{\prime}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})\ \ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{V\in\pi}\widehat{r}_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mathfrak{p})\cdot r_{\pi\setminus V}(\mu)\ \ -\ \ \frac{n}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{r_{\sigma}(\mu)}{ts}\quad\forall\ n\geq 1.

(43)

Proof.

\textit{(2)}\Rightarrow\textit{(1)}
.
For any integer
j\geq 1
, we have that
\kappa_{j}(p_{d})=r_{j}(\mu)+o(1)
since
\kappa_{j}(p_{d})\to r_{j}(\mu)
as
d\to\infty
, due to Proposition
2.16
.
Combing this with (
42
), and applying both of them to the relation (
40
), we obtain that

\displaystyle m_{n}(p_{d})\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\prod_{V\in\pi}\left(r_{|V|}(\mu)+\frac{1}{d}\,\widehat{r}_{|V|}(\mathfrak{p})+o(1/d)\right)\,\,-\,\,\frac{n}{2d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{ts}\,\prod_{V\in\sigma}\Big(r_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mu)+o(1)\Big)\,\,+\,\,o(1/d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}r_{\pi}(\mu)\,\,+\,\,\frac{1}{d}\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{V\in\pi}\widehat{r}_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mathfrak{p})\cdot r_{\pi\setminus V}(\mu)\,\,-\,\,\frac{n}{2d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{r_{\sigma}(\mu)}{ts}\,\,+\,\,o(1/d).

However, due to the free moment-cumulant formula (
4
), the last line yields precisely (
41
) with each
m^{\prime}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})
given by (
43
).

\textit{(1)}\Rightarrow\textit{(2)}
.
We will prove that (
42
) holds by induction on
n
.
For
n=1
, we have that
m_{1}(\mu)=r_{1}(\mu)
, so (
40
) and (
41
) yield

\kappa_{1}(p_{d})=m_{1}(p_{d})+o(1/d^{2})=r_{1}(\mu)+\frac{1}{d}\,m^{\prime}_{1}+o(1/d).

Hence, we can take
\widehat{r}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})=m^{\prime}_{1}(\mathfrak{p})
.
Assume now that there exist
\widehat{r}_{1}(\mathfrak{p}),\widehat{r}_{2}(\mathfrak{p}),\ldots,\widehat{r}_{n-1}(\mathfrak{p})
such that

\kappa_{j}(p_{d})=r_{j}(\mu)+\frac{1}{d}\,\widehat{r}_{j}(\mathfrak{p})+o(1/d)\quad\text{for}\quad j=1,2,\dots,n-1.

Given partition
\pi\in NC(n)
, the product of finite-free cumulants
\kappa_{\pi}(p_{d})=\prod_{V\in\pi}\kappa_{\left|{V}\right|}(p_{d})
contains the term
\kappa_{n}(p_{d})
as a factor only if
\pi=1_{n}
.
So, we can re-write (
40
) as

\kappa_{n}(p_{d})\,=\,m_{n}(p_{d})\,-\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in NC(n)\\
\pi\neq 1_{n}\end{subarray}}\kappa_{\pi}(p_{d})\,+\,\frac{n}{2d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{\kappa_{\sigma}(p_{d})}{ts}\,\,+\,\,o(1/d)\,.

Moreover, our induction hypothesis implies that

\kappa_{\pi}(p_{d})=\prod_{V\in\pi}\left(r_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mu)+\frac{1}{d}\,\widehat{r}_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mathfrak{p})+o(1/d)\right)=r_{\pi}(\mu)\,\,+\,\,\frac{1}{d}\sum_{V\in\pi}\widehat{r}_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mathfrak{p})\cdot r_{\pi\setminus V}(\mu)\,\,+\,\,o(1/d)

whenever
1_{n}\neq\pi\in NC(n)
; additionally, since
\kappa_{j}(p_{d})\to r_{j}(\mu)
as
d\to\infty
for any integer
j\geq 1
, meaning that
\kappa_{j}(p_{d})=r_{j}(\mu)+o(1)
, we obtain that

\frac{1}{d}\,\kappa_{\sigma}(p_{d})=\frac{1}{d}\,\prod_{V\in\sigma}\Big(r_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mu)+o(1)\Big)=\frac{1}{d}\,r_{\sigma}(\mu)\,\,+\,\,o(1/d)

for every permutation
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)
with
t+s=n
.
Thus, we have that

\kappa_{n}(p_{d})\,=\ \,m_{n}(p_{d})\,-\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in NC(n)\\
\pi\neq 1_{n}\end{subarray}}r_{\pi}(\mu)\,\,-\,\,\frac{1}{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in NC(n)\\
\pi\neq 1_{n}\end{subarray}}\sum_{V\in\pi}\widehat{r}_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mathfrak{p})\cdot r_{\pi\setminus V}(\mu)\,\,+\,\,\frac{n}{2d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{r_{\sigma}(\mu)}{ts}\,\,+\,\,o(1/d).

But, the free moment-cumulant formula (
4
) implies that

r_{n}(\mu)-m_{n}(\mu)\ \ =-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in NC(n)\\
\pi\neq 1_{n}\end{subarray}}r_{\pi}(\mu)\,,

and we know that
m_{n}(p_{d})=m_{n}(\mu)+\frac{1}{d}\,m_{n}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})+o(1/d)
, so we obtain

\kappa_{n}(p_{d})=r_{n}(\mu)\,\,+\,\,\frac{1}{d}\left(m^{\prime}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in NC(n)\\
\pi\neq 1_{n}\end{subarray}}\sum_{V\in\pi}\widehat{r}_{\left|{V}\right|}(\mathfrak{p})\cdot r_{\pi\setminus V}(\mu)\,\,+\,\,\frac{n}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{r_{\sigma}(\mu)}{ts}\right)\,\,+\,\,o(1/d)\,.

We can then take
\widehat{r}_{n}
as the expression inside the parentheses.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

For a sequence of polynomials

4
Infinitesimal Cauchy transform in finite free probability

In this section, we translate our results from Section
3
into functional relations of formal power series.
For this, given a sequence of polynomials
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
with infinitesimal distribution
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
, besides the Cauchy transform and the
R
-transform (
5
), and their infinitesimal counterparts (
24

\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)\,=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})\,z^{n-1}

determined by the cumulant fluctuations
(\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{p}))_{n\geq 1}
from Lemma
3.1
and Corollary
3.3
; and

H_{\mu}(z)\,=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}h_{n}(\mu)\,z^{-n-1}\quad\text{with}\quad h_{n}(\mu)\ =\,\frac{n}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{r_{\sigma}(\mu)}{ts}

referred to as the
H
-transform
of
\mu
.
The latter can also be expressed in terms of the Cauchy transform
G_{\mu}
as follows.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(m_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(r_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be any two sequences of numbers.
Suppose that
G(z)=z^{-1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}m_{n}\,z^{-n-1}
and
K(z)=z^{-1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}\,z^{n-1}
satisfy any of the four equivalent conditions from Proposition
2.2
.
Then, for any formal power series
H(z)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}h_{n}\,z^{-n-1}
, we have that

H=\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}-\frac{G^{\prime\prime}}{2G^{\prime}}\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad h_{n}\ =\,\frac{n}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\pi\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{r_{\pi}}{ts}\quad\forall n\geq 2.

(51)

Proof.

This is an implicit consequence of
[
AGVP23
, s (5.4) and (5.6)]

∎

When doing computations, and in the context of the previous proposition, it comes handy to consider the reciprocal formal power series
F(z)=1/G(z)
, taken as a series in
z
of the form
z+f_{2}z^{2}+f_{3}z^{3}+\cdots
with formal derivative
F^{\prime}(z)=1+\tilde{f}_{1}z+\tilde{f}_{2}z^{2}+\cdots
.
One can then show that the left-hand side of (
51
) can be expressed as

H=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\ln(F^{\prime})\right)^{\prime}=\frac{-F^{\prime\prime}}{2F^{\prime}}.

(52)

The following lemma describes the relation between two
H
-transforms when the corresponding
G
are related through composition.
This relation will be used later on.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be formal power series of the form
z^{-1}+a_{1}z^{-2}+\cdots
, and
J
a formal power series of the form
1+b_{1}z+b_{2}z^{2}+\cdots
.
Put
H_{i}=\frac{G^{\prime}_{i}}{G_{i}}-\frac{G^{\prime\prime}_{i}}{2G^{\prime}_{i}}
for

.
If
G_{1}\circ J=G_{2}
, then

(H_{1}\circ J)J^{\prime}=H_{2}+\frac{J^{\prime\prime}}{2J^{\prime}}.

(53)

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{i}=-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(G^{\prime}_{i}/(G_{i})^{2}\right)
, for
i=1,2
, and
note that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{i}=H_{i}
.
Thus, in particular, we have that

(\mathcal{H}_{1}\circ J)^{\prime}=(H_{1}\circ J)\cdot J^{\prime}
.
Now, since
G_{2}=G_{1}\circ J
, and hence
G^{\prime}_{2}=(G^{\prime}_{1}\circ J)\cdot J^{\prime}
, we also get that

\mathcal{H}_{1}\circ J=-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{(G^{\prime}_{1}\circ J)\cdot J^{\prime}}{(G_{1}\circ J)^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{J^{\prime}}\right)=\mathcal{H}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(J^{\prime}\right)

Differentiating both sides of the last equation, we obtain the desired relation.
∎

4.1
Cauchy transform and finite-free cumulants

We first establish various functional relations in the case of a single sequence of polynomials
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
with infinitesimal distribution
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})

beginning with the functional equation that expresses the infinitesimal Cauchy transform
G_{\mu^{\prime}}

\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}

G_{\mu}

Lemma 4.3
.

For any sequence of polynomials
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
with infinitesimal distribution
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})

G_{\mu^{\prime}}=-(\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ G_{\mu})\cdot G^{\prime}_{\mu}-H_{\mu}.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.1

m^{\prime}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})\ \ \ =\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{B\in\pi}\widehat{r}_{\left|{B}\right|}(\mathfrak{p})\cdot r_{\pi\setminus B}(\mu)\quad-\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}r_{\sigma}(\mu)\,.

But, from Proposition
2.3
and Proposition

-(\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ G_{\mu}(z))\cdot G^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{-n-1}\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{B\in\pi}\widehat{r}_{\left|{B}\right|}(\mathfrak{p})\cdot r_{\pi\setminus B}(\mu)\quad\text{and}\quad H_{\mu}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{-n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}r_{\sigma}(\mu)\,.

Consequently, we obtain that
\displaystyle G_{\mu^{\prime}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}m^{\prime}_{n}z^{-n-1}=-(\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ G_{\mu})\cdot G^{\prime}_{\mu}-H_{\mu}
.
∎

From Lemma
3.1
and Corollary
3.3
, we know that the
1/d

Our next lemma establishes this in terms of the related formal power series.

Lemma 4.4
.

For a sequence of polynomials
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}

(\mu,\mu^{\prime})

G_{\mu^{\prime}}+H_{\mu}=-(\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ G_{\mu})\cdot G^{\prime}_{\mu}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}=-\left(G_{\mu^{\prime}}\circ K_{\mu}+H_{\mu}\circ K_{\mu}\right)\cdot K^{\prime}_{\mu}.

As an immediate consequence, we obtain from (
25

H_{\mu}=0
if and only if
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}=R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Proposition
2.3
.
Indeed, letting
G=G_{\mu}
,
K=K_{\mu}
,
r=\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and

G(K(z))=z

2.3

∎

Before applying the previous lemma to finite-free convolution, let us conclude this part by providing the relation between the transform
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)
and the infinitesimal

Lemma 4.5

Theorem 4.6

Remark 4.12

In this section, we apply our main results to compute infinitesimal distributions for several sequences of polynomials.
Through the entire section, we consider sequences of polynomials
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
and
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
with infinitesimal distributions
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
and
(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
, respectively.
Then, along the way, we determine their infinitesimal distribution under concrete or additional hypothesis, such as specific classes of polynomials or polynomials with either exact finite-free cumulants or zero infinitesimal moments.
If appropriate, we identify
\mu^{\prime}
and
\nu^{\prime}
with signed measures on
\mathbb{R}
.

5.1
Basic Examples

We first show that our results recover the infinitesimal distribution of sequences of polynomials
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
studied in
[
AGVP23
]
, concretely, the case when each finite-free cumulant
\kappa^{(d)}_{n}(p_{d})
coincides with the free cumulant
r_{n}(\mu)
up to a correction term of order
o(1/d)
.

Example 5.1

(Zero cumulant fluctuations)
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
has zero cumulant fluctuations, namely, its finite-free cumulants satisfy
\kappa^{(d)}_{n}(p_{d})=r_{n}(\mu)+o(1/d)
for any integers
1\leq n\leq d
.
Note that this is equivalent to
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)=0
.
Thus, from Lemma
4.3
, we obtain that

G_{\mu^{\prime}}=-H_{\mu}=\frac{G_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}}{2G_{\mu}^{\prime}}-\frac{G_{\mu}^{\prime}}{G_{\mu}}.

This example recovers Theorem 1.6 from
[
AGVP23
]
.

It is natural to examine the analogue case for moments, that is, each finite moment
m_{n}(p_{d})
coincides with
m_{n}(\mu)
up to a correction term of order
o(1/d)
.
In this case, the infinitesimal distribution is trivial since we have
m_{n}(\mu^{\prime})=0
, and hence
G_{\mu^{\prime}}(z)=0
.
However, it is still worth considering to establish
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)
, which will be needed for further examples involving finite-free convolutions.

Example 5.2

(Zero infinitesimal moments)
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
has zero infinitesimal moments, namely,
m_{n}(p_{d})=m_{n}(\mu)+o(1/d)
for any integers
1\leq n\leq d
.
Thus, we have that
G_{\mu^{\prime}}(z)=0
, and hence
R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}=-(G_{\mu^{\prime}}\circ K_{\mu})\,\cdot K_{\mu}^{\prime}=0
.
Thus, by Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
, we obtain that

H_{\mu}=-(\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ G_{\mu})\cdot G^{\prime}_{\mu}\,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}=-(H_{\mu}\circ K_{\mu})\cdot K^{\prime}_{\mu}=-\frac{\ K^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}(z)\ }{2K^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)}-\frac{1}{z}\,.

The following is the case where all but finitely many roots are located at a single value.
As in the previous example, a main goal is determining
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)
for later use on finite-free convolutions.

Example 5.3

(Finite discrete deviation from Dirac distribution)
.

Assume that each polynomial
p_{d}
from the sequence
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
is given by

p_{d}\,(x)=(x-\alpha)^{d-s}(x-\alpha_{1})(x-\alpha_{2})\cdots(x-\alpha_{s})

for a fixed integer
s\geq 1
and fixed real numbers
\alpha,\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\in\mathbb{R}
.
The
n

is given by

m_{n}(p_{d})=\alpha^{n}+\frac{1}{d}\Big(-s\alpha^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\alpha_{k}^{n}\Big)=m_{n}(\mu)+\frac{1}{d}m_{n}(\mu^{\prime})\,.

This implies that the infinitesimal distribution
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})=(\delta_{\alpha},-s\delta_{\alpha}+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\delta_{\alpha_{k}})
.
Hence, the Cauchy transforms of
\mu
and

are respectively given by

G_{\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{z-\alpha}=\frac{1}{F_{\mu}(z)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad G_{\mu^{\prime}}(z)=-\frac{s}{z-\alpha}+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{1}{z-\alpha_{k}}\,.

Moreover, from the Cauchy transform of
\mu
, we obtain that

K_{\mu}(z)=G^{<-1>}(z)=\frac{1}{z}+\alpha,\qquad K^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)=-\frac{1}{z^{2}},\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_{\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dz}\ln(F^{\prime}_{\mu})=0\,.

Thus, since
H_{\mu}(z)=0

4.4
yields

(66)

Notice that the last two equations imply that
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}=R^{inf}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}
,
so the cumulant fluctuations of
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
coincide exactly with the infinitesimal cumulants of
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
in this case.
The function
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)
is explicitly given by

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)

\displaystyle=-(G_{\mu^{\prime}}(K_{\mu}(z))\cdot K^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}\cdot G_{\mu^{\prime}}\left({\textstyle\frac{1+\alpha z}{z}}\right)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}\cdot\left(-\frac{s}{\frac{1+\alpha z}{z}-\alpha}+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\frac{1+\alpha z}{z}-\alpha_{k}}\right)

Proposition 5.4

(Repeated differentiation)
.

Let

(69)

Proof.

Recall from
[

that the cumulants of

\kappa_{n}^{(j_{d})}(q_{d})=t^{n-1}r_{n}(\mu)+\frac{1}{d_{i}}\left(\alpha(n-1)t^{n-2}r_{n}(\mu)+t^{n-1}\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})\right)+o(1/d_{i}).

\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{q})=\alpha(n-1)t^{n-2}r_{n}(\mu)+t^{n-1}\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha(n-1)t^{n-2}r_{n}(\mu)z^{n-1}=\alpha z\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)r_{n}(\mu)(tz)^{n-2}=\alpha zR^{\prime}_{\mu}(tz)=\frac{\alpha z}{t}R^{\prime}_{\nu}(z),

5.3
Infinitesimal distribution under additive convolution

We now turn to the application of our results to finite-free additive convolutions of concrete sequences of polynomials.
We start with an example that retrieves, as a particular cases, the distributions examined by Shlyakhtenko in
[
Shl18
, Section 4.1]
.

Example 5.10

(Additive convolution with finite perturbation)
.

Suppose that each polynomial
q_{d}
from the sequence
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
is given by

q_{d}\,(x)=x^{d-t}(x-\beta_{1})(x-\beta_{2})\cdots(x-\beta_{t})

for a fixed integer
t\geq 1
and fixed real numbers
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{t}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Observe that this a special case of Corollary
4.7
since
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
has indeed limiting root distribution equal to
\delta_{0}
.
Thus, the sequence
\mathfrak{p}\boxplus\mathfrak{q}=(p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d})_{d\geq 1}

(\rho,\rho^{\prime})
where
\rho=\mu\boxplus\delta_{0}=\mu
and
\rho^{\prime}
has Cauchy transform

G_{\rho^{\prime}}=G_{\mu^{\prime}}-(\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{q}}\circ G_{\mu})\cdot G^{\prime}_{\mu}
.
Moreover, from Example
5.3
, the Cauchy transform

takes the form

G_{\rho^{\prime}}=G_{\mu^{\prime}}-\sum_{k=1}^{t}\frac{\beta_{k}\,G^{\prime}_{\mu}}{1-\beta_{k}\,G_{\mu}}\ .

Additionally, if
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
has zero infinitesimal moments, see Example
5.2
, then
G_{\mu^{\prime}}=0
;
and Corollary
4.7
gives that the pair
(\rho,\rho^{\prime})
coincides with the infinitesimal free additive convolution of
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})=(\mu,0)
and
(\nu,\nu^{\prime})=(\delta_{0},-s\delta_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{t}\delta_{\beta_{k}})
.
Consequently, this example retrieves the distributions studied by Shlyakhtenko in
[
Shl18
, Section 4.1]
.
In particular, if each
q_{d}
has at most one nonzero root, i.e.,

,
then
G_{\rho^{\prime}}
simplifies to

G_{\rho^{\prime}}=\frac{\beta\,G^{\prime}_{\mu}}{1-\beta\,G_{\mu}}.

The previous example gives an instance where finite-free convolution and infinitesimal convolution yield the same distributions.
In general, however, this is not the case, even if both
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
and
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
have zero infinitesimal moments.
We establish this next.

Example 5.11

(Additive convolution of polynomials with fixed moments)
.

Suppose that both
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}

,

[

that
\mathfrak{p}\boxplus\mathfrak{q}=(p_{d}\boxplus_{d}q_{d})_{d\geq 1}

We now address the case when both

and

Example 5.12

(Convolution with a sequence with zero cumulant fluctuations)
.

Suppose that

Example 5.13

5.4
Infinitesimal distribution under multiplicative convolution

Before we take on concrete examples of infinitesimal distributions induced by finite-free multiplicative convolution, we would like to stress that the computation of
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q}}
is a main distinction from the additive case.
Indeed, for multiplicative convolutions, we have the functional relation (
61
), namely,

G_{\rho^{\prime}}=-(\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q}}\circ G_{\mu\boxtimes\nu})\cdot G^{\prime}_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}-H_{\mu\boxtimes\nu},

which, since
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}\boxplus\mathfrak{q}}=\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}+\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, is entirely analogous to (
57
) in the additive case.
However, while
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}\boxplus\mathfrak{q}}
can be straightforwardly computed by adding
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, the computation of
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q}}
seems to be more intricate. For instance, even if both
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}
and
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
have zero cumulant fluctuations, meaning that

\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})=\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{q})=0
for all
n\geq 1
,
then (
48
) reduces to

\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q})=-\frac{n}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{r_{\sigma}(\mu)\cdot r_{Kr_{t,s}(\sigma)}(\nu)}{ts}\,,

(73)

but, to the best of our knowledge, the generating function associated to coefficients satisfying the combinatorial relation (
73
) appears to be a non-trivial matter.
Nonetheless, our results can still be successfully applied to obtain the infinitesimal distribution in several relevant scenarios.

Example 5.14

(Multiplicative convolution with Marchenko-Pastur)

Assume that both
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{d})_{d\geq 1}

We now consider the case when
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d\geq 1}

or equivalently,
\rho^{\prime}=-\,(s+t)\,\delta_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\delta_{\alpha_{k}}+\sum_{k=1}^{t}\delta_{\beta_{k}}
.

The previous example relied on Theorem
3.5
.
A more general case, namely, when we only assume that
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
has all but finitely many roots located at
1
, can be addressed by considering Theorem
3.6
instead.
As in the additive case, see Example
5.10
, we show that our combinatorial results also retrieve, as particular cases, the distributions examined by Shlyakhtenko in
[
Shl18
, Section 4.2]
, corresponding this time to the infinitesimal free multiplicative convolution. An alternative approach to obtain the following is Corollary
4.11
.

Example 5.17

(Multiplication by a finite rank perturbation)
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d=s}^{\infty}
be as in Example
5.16
. We know that
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d=s}^{\infty}
has infinitesimal distribution
(\nu,\nu^{\prime})=\left(\delta_{1},-t\delta_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{t}\delta_{\beta_{k}}\right)
. Since
Kr:NC(n)\to NC(n)
is a bijection, (
50
) from Theorem
3.6
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle m^{\prime}_{n}(\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\sum_{V\in\pi}\Big(m^{\prime}_{|V|}(p)\,m_{\pi\backslash V}(\mu)\cdot r_{Kr(\pi)}(\nu)+\widehat{r}_{|V|}(q)\,r_{\pi\backslash V}(\nu)\cdot m_{{Kr}^{-1}(\pi)}(\mu)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{n}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t+s=n\\
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{m_{\sigma}(\mu)\,r_{Kr_{t,s}(\sigma)}(\nu)}{ts}

As in Example
5.16
, since
r_{n}(\nu)=0
for
n\geq 2
, we have that
r_{Kr(\pi)}(\nu)=0
unless
\pi=1_{n}
and that
r_{Kr_{t,s}(\sigma)}=0
for every permutation
\sigma\in S_{NC}(t,s)
;
additionally,
r_{\pi\setminus V}(\nu)=0
unless
\pi\setminus V
contains only singletons as blocks.
Consequently, the previous equation yields

m^{\prime}_{n}(\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q})\ \ \ =\ \ \ m^{\prime}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})\ \ \ +\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq[n]}\widehat{r}_{\left|{S}\right|}(\mathfrak{q})\,r_{0_{n\setminus S}}(\nu)\cdot m_{{Kr}^{-1}(S\cup 0_{n\setminus S})}(\mu)

where we have used the fact that
S\mapsto(0_{n\setminus S}\cup S,S)
is a bijection from nonempty sets
S\subseteq[n]
to pairs
(\pi,V)
where
V\in\pi\in NC(n)
and
\pi\setminus V
contains only singletons.
The map
S\mapsto{Kr}^{-1}(S\cup 0_{n\setminus S})
is a bijection from nonempty sets
S\subseteq[n]
to the set of cyclic interval partitions
CI(n)
such that
\left|{S}\right|=|\pi|
provided
\pi={Kr}^{-1}(S\cup 0_{n\setminus S})
.
Hence, we obtain that

m^{\prime}_{n}(\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q})\ \ \ =\ \ \ m^{\prime}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})\ \ \ +\sum_{\pi\in CI(n)}\widehat{r}_{|\pi|}(\mathfrak{q})\cdot m_{\pi}(\mu)\ \ \ =\ \ \ m^{\prime}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})\ \ \ +\sum_{k=1}^{t}\sum_{\pi\in CI(n)}(\beta_{k}-1)^{|\pi|}\cdot m_{\pi}(\mu)\,.

(75)

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\widehat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{q})=\sum_{k=1}^{t}(\beta_{k}-1)^{n}
, see Example
5.3
.

We will now compute the Cauchy transform associated to each of the rightmost terms in (
75
).
To this end, for a fixed
k\in[t]
, take

\tilde{m}_{n}=-\,(\beta_{k}-1)\,m_{n}(\mu)\text{\hskip 20.44434ptand \hskip 20.44434pt}\overline{m}_{n}=\sum_{\pi\in CI(n)}-\,(\beta_{k}-1)^{|\pi|}\cdot m_{\pi}(\mu)\text{\hskip 20.44434ptfor every \hskip 20.44434pt}n\geq 1

and consider

\widetilde{G}(z)=\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{m}_{n}}{z^{n+1}}=(1-\beta_{k})G_{\mu}(z)+\frac{\beta_{k}}{z}\text{\hskip 20.44434ptand \hskip 20.44434pt}\overline{G}(z)=\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{m}_{n}}{z^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{z}+\overline{g}(z).

Note that the coefficients
m_{n}(\mu)
,
\tilde{m}_{n}
, and
\overline{m}_{n}
satisfy the relation

\sum_{\pi\in CI(n)}(-1)^{|\pi|+1}\tilde{m}_{\pi}=\sum_{\pi\in CI(n)}-(\beta_{k}-1)^{|\pi|}m_{\pi}(\mu)=\overline{m}_{n}\,.

This means that
\widetilde{G}(z)
is in fact the Markov-Krein transform of
\overline{G}(z)
, so they are related through the equation

-\overline{G}(z)=\frac{\widetilde{G}^{\prime}}{\widetilde{G}}=-\frac{1}{z}-\overline{g}(z)\,.

Thus, we obtain that

-\overline{g}(z)=\frac{\widetilde{G}^{\prime}}{\widetilde{G}}+\frac{1}{z}=\frac{d}{dz}\log(\,z\widetilde{G}\,)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{m}_{n}}{z^{n+1}}=\frac{d}{dz}\log(\,(1-\beta_{k})zG_{\mu}(z)+\beta_{k}\,)

Therefore, the sequence
\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q}=(p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
has infinitesimal distribution
(\rho,\rho^{\prime})
where
\rho=\mu\boxtimes\delta_{1}=\mu
and
\rho^{\prime}
has Cauchy transform
G_{\rho^{\prime}}
given by

G_{\rho^{\prime}}=G_{\mu^{\prime}}+\sum_{k=1}^{t}\frac{d}{dz}\log\left(z(1-\beta_{k})G_{\mu}+\beta_{k}\right).

In particular, if
\beta_{k}=0
for every
k\in[t]
, the previous formula reduces to

G_{\rho^{\prime}}=G_{\mu^{\prime}}+t\frac{d}{dz}\log(zG_{\mu}(z))=G_{\mu^{\prime}}+t\left(\frac{G^{\prime}_{\mu}}{G_{\mu}}+\frac{1}{z}\right).

Our next example is motivated by the work
[
FH22
]
where Fujie and Hasebe calculate the fluctuations of the eigenvalue distribution of the principal minor of unitarily invariant matrices with limiting eigenvalue distribution.

Example 5.18
.

Assume that
\mu\neq\delta_{0}
.
Fix an integer
s\geq 0
and let
p_{d}^{(s)}
denote the
s
-th derivative of
p_{d}
for
s\leq d
.
We are interested in analyzing

\tau_{d}:=d\mu-(d-s)\mu\left\llbracket p_{d}^{(s)}\right\rrbracket=d\mu-\sum_{k=1}^{d-s}\delta_{\lambda_{k}(p_{d}^{(s)})}\quad\text{as}\quad d\to\infty.

From
[
AGVP23
, Lemma 3.5]
, we know that
p_{d}^{(s)}
can be computed via finite-free multiplicative convolution.
Concretely, letting
q_{d}(x)=x^{s}(x-1)^{d-s}
for
s\leq d
, we have that

p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d}=\frac{1}{(d)_{s}}x^{s}p_{d}^{(s)}(x)\,.

Note that
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{d})_{d=s}^{\infty}
has infinitesimal distribution
(\nu,\nu^{\prime})=\left(\delta_{1},s\delta_{0}-s\delta_{1}\right)
in this case.
Thus, from Proposition
4.10
and Corollary
4.11
, we obtain that
\mathfrak{p}\boxtimes\mathfrak{q}=(p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d})_{d\geq 1}
has infinitesimal distribution
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
where
\gamma=\mu
and

G_{\gamma^{\prime}}=G_{\mu^{\prime}}+G_{\nu^{\prime}}(\theta_{\mu})\cdot\theta^{\prime}_{\mu}\quad\text{with}\quad\theta_{\mu}(z)=\frac{G_{\mu}(z)}{G_{\mu}(z)-1/z}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)=-\frac{zG_{\mu}^{\prime}(z)+G_{\mu}(z)}{(zG_{\mu}(z)-1)^{2}}\,.

Moreover, since
G_{\nu^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{k}{z}-\frac{k}{z-1}
, we also obtain that

G_{\nu^{\prime}}(\theta_{\mu}(z))=-\frac{s\,(zG_{\mu}(z)-1)^{2}}{zG_{\mu}(z)}\,.

Therefore, we get that

G_{\gamma^{\prime}}=G_{\mu^{\prime}}(z)+s\frac{zG_{\mu}^{\prime}(z)+G_{\mu}(z)}{zG_{\mu}(z)}=G_{\mu^{\prime}}(z)+\frac{sG_{\mu}^{\prime}(z)}{G_{\mu}(z)}+\frac{s}{z}.

Finally, to obtain the limit of
\tau_{d}
as
d\to\infty
, that we denote by
\tau
, we note that

dG_{\mu\left\llbracket p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}q_{d}\right\rrbracket}-(d-s)G_{\mu\left\llbracket p_{d}^{(s)}\right\rrbracket}=\frac{s}{z}

which implies

G_{\tau}=G_{\gamma^{\prime}}-\frac{d}{z}=G_{\mu^{\prime}}(z)+\frac{sG_{\mu}^{\prime}(z)}{G_{\mu}(z)}.

Our last example relates to the inverse of Laguerre polynomials with respect to the finite-free multiplicative convolution.

Example 5.19

(Multiplicative inverse of Laguerre polynomials)
.

In this example, we will look into their inverses with respect to the multiplicative convolution. Namely, we will analyze the polynomials

p_{d}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}x^{d-i}\binom{d}{i}\frac{d^{i}}{(d)_{i}},

which can be equivalently defined through the relation
p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}L^{(1)}_{d}=(x-1)^{d}
.
From
[
AGVP23
, Lemma 3.2]
, we know that the moments of
p_{d}
coincide with the cumulants of
p_{d}\boxtimes_{d}L^{(1)}_{d}
. Thus, we obtain that

m_{n}(p_{d})=\kappa^{d}_{n}((x-1)^{d})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }n=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Although, the sequence
(m_{n}(p_{d}))_{n=1}
is independent from
d
, it does not correspond to the moments of a measure supported on
\mathbb{R}
.
Indeed, there is no
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
such that
m_{1}(\mu)=1
and
m_{2}(\mu)=0
.
Nonetheless, one can still consider the “Cauchy transform” as the formal power series that corresponds to this sequence:

G_{\mathfrak{p}}(z):=\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{m_{n}(p_{d})}{z^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{z^{2}}=\frac{z+1}{z^{2}}\,.

The first and second derivatives of
G_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)
are then given by

G_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}(z)=-\frac{1}{z^{2}}-\frac{2}{z^{3}}=\frac{-z-2}{z^{3}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad G_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime\prime}(z)=\frac{2}{z^{3}}+\frac{6}{z^{4}}=2\frac{z+3}{z^{4}}\,.

And
H
-transform takes the particular nice expression

H_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)=\frac{G_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}(z)}{G_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)}-\frac{G_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{2G_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}(z)}=-\frac{z+2}{z(z+1)}+\frac{z+3}{z(z+2)}=\frac{-1}{z(z+1)(z+2)}

Now, since the Cauchy transform
G_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)
satisfies
z^{2}G_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)-z-1=0
, its inverse
K_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)
under composition is given by

K_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4z}}{2z}\text{\hskip 10.22217ptwith \hskip 10.22217pt}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{z\sqrt{1+4z}}-\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4z}}{2z^{2}}=-\frac{1+2z+\sqrt{1+4z}}{2z^{2}\sqrt{1+4z}}\,.

Thus, from Example
5.2
, we obtain that

\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)=-H_{\mathfrak{p}}(K_{\mathfrak{p}}(z))\cdot K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}(z)=\frac{-4z}{(1+\sqrt{1+4z})(1+4z+\sqrt{1+4z})\sqrt{1+4z}}=\frac{-4z}{(1+4z+\sqrt{1+4z})^{2}}\,

with Taylor series expansion

\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\hat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})\,z^{n-1}=0-1z+6z^{2}-29z^{3}+130z^{4}-562z^{5}+2380z^{6}+\dots\ .

where
\hat{r}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})=(4^{n}-\binom{2n+1}{n})(-1)^{n}
.
Let us point out that these cumulant fluctuations coincide up to a sign with the sequence A008549 in Sloane’s OEIS.
They are also related to the enumeration of certain annular partitions arising in the infinitesimal distribution of Real Wishart Random Matrices, see
[
MVB25
, Corollary 36]
.

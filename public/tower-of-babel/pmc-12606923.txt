Introduction

Esophageal cancer is the eighth most prevalent malignant tumor in the world and has the sixth highest mortality rate [1]. Globally, 604,100 new cases of esophageal cancer were estimated in 2020, and approximately 957,000 new cases of esophageal cancer are expected by 2040 [1]. Esophagectomy is the standard treatment for potentially resectable thoracic esophageal cancer [2, 3]. Postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs) are one of the most common postoperative complications in patients undergoing minimally invasive esophagectomy (MIE). Although multiple therapeutic methods (lung-protective ventilation, goal-directed fluid therapy, individualized analgesia, anti-inflammation, etc.) have been applied in clinical practice to reduce PPCs, the incidence of PPCs in MIE still exceeds 40%. Therefore, developing new strategies and theories to effectively reduce PPCs in esophageal cancer holds significant clinical significance.

In recent years, the non-local anesthetic effects of lidocaine have attracted widespread attention [4–6], and studies have shown that intravenous lidocaine infusion can cause analgesia, sedation, and anti-inflammation, and can inhibit tumor metastasis, alleviate intubation stress, promote gastrointestinal function recovery, and protect organs [6]. Studies have confirmed that intravenous lidocaine can attenuate lung injury induced by endotoxins, hyperoxia, and ischemia/reperfusion injury by inhibiting the inflammatory cascade [7–9]. The results of a double-blind, randomized controlled trial showed that intravenous lidocaine infusion reduced the incidence of PPCs; interleukin-6 and Clara cell secretory protein in patients undergoing abdominal surgery [10].

However, the role of intravenous lidocaine infusion on postoperative PPCs in MIE remains unclear and another randomized controlled study assessing the effect of intravenous lidocaine infusion on PPCs in patients undergoing thoracoscopic surgery is ongoing. As far as we know, no multicenter studies have been carried out; therefore, this study aims to carry out a double-center, double-blind, randomized controlled, superiority study [11, 12] to test the hypothesis that intravenous lidocaine infusion reduces PPCs after MIE.

Methods and analysis

Study design, approval, registration

This study is a double-center, double-blind, randomized controlled, superiority trial [11, 12], and the protocol is designed in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items for Clinical Trials: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) 2013 statement and the Declaration of Helsinki [13–15]. The present study was approved by the Sichuan Cancer Hospital Ethic Review Board (SCCHEC-02–2023-123), Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital Ethic Review Board (2023–561), two tertiary academic hospitals, and has been registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06138041). Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting, or dissemination of this research.

Participants and informed consent

All patients scheduled to undergo minimally invasive esophagectomy at Sichuan Cancer Hospital and Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital will be screened. Two trained, full-time clinical research assistants from the department of anesthesiology of Sichuan Cancer Hospital and Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital, respectively, will be responsible for obtaining written informed consent. They will conduct preoperative evaluation to identify eligible participants based on inclusion and exclusion criteria. All the patients will be required to sign an informed consent form (Supplemental S1) before participating in the study and have access to the final results of the study and the group allocation. Patients will be allowed to unconditionally withdraw from the study at any time. This study does not involve the collection or use of biological specimens. Therefore, no additional consent for ancillary biological studies is required.

Inclusion criteria

Patients between 18 and 85 years of age who were recently scheduled for MIE will be screened for this study. The inclusion criteria are clear consciousness (Glasgow Coma Scale, GCS = 15) and ASA status I – III.

Exclusion criteria

The exclusion criteria are following: patients with severe psychiatric disorders (such as schizophrenia, depression, dementia, etc.), severe hepatic insufficiency (concentration of glutamic oxaloacetic transaminase, glutamic pyruvic transaminase or bilirubin ≥ 2.5 times the upper normal limit), renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 60 mL/min); allergy to amide local anesthetics, history of seizures, the presence of II/III atrioventricular block, patients with severe sinus bradycardia or sick sinus syndrome, patients with Adams-Stokes syndrome or pre-excitation syndrome, intracardiac block (e.g. complete bundle branch block, atrioventricular block), serum potassium ion concentration below 2.5 or above 5.0 mmol/L, and pH > 7.55 or pH < 7.2. Patients will be removed from the study in case of an allergic reaction or a severe cardiovascular event that cannot be managed with symptomatic treatment.

Randomization, allocation, and concealment

All participants will be randomly assigned to the lidocaine or saline (control) groups in a 1:1 ratio. Randomization will be conducted by an independent research assistant using R software version 4.3.1 with “blockrand” library. Sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes containing a generated list of random numbers will be used. Before participants are anesthetized, the sealed envelopes will be opened. According to group allocation, a labeled “research solution” 50 ml syringe containing lidocaine or normal saline will be prepared by the research staff (YS and MXL). The participants, surgeons, anesthesiologists, outcome assessors, and data analysts will be blinded to the group assignment. Emergency unblinding will be permitted only when knowledge of the assigned intervention is essential for the clinical management of a serious adverse event. Attending anesthesiologist or investigator will request unblinding through the independent research staff (YS and MXL). All unblinding events will be documented, including the reason, date, time, and personnel involved. Participants will remain in the study for follow-up and be analyzed according to the intention-to-treat principle.

Intervention

In the lidocaine group, at the same time of induction of general anesthesia, an intravenous lidocaine bolus of 2 mg/kg will be administered, followed by a continuous infusion of intravenous lidocaine at 2 mg/kg/h until the participant is transferred out of postoperative anesthesia care unit. The control group will receive the same volume of normal saline in bolus and continuous infusion. All anesthesiologists participating in the study will receive protocol training before the start of recruitment. In addition to the interventions, patients in both groups will receive the same anesthesia and surgical regimen, in accordance with the established protocols. No specific concomitant interventions are prohibited. Adherence will be monitored through intraoperative anesthetic records and CRF. Any protocol deviations will be recorded in CRF.

Outcomes

Primary outcomes

The primary outcome is the incidence of PPCs within 7 days following surgery. Pulmonary complications are defined as one or more of the following complications listed in Supplemental S2.

Secondary outcomes

The secondary outcomes include (1) the incidence of respiratory infection; (2) the incidence of respiratory failure; (3) the incidence of pneumothorax; (4) the incidence of atelectasis; (5) the incidence of pleural effusion; (6) the incidence of bronchospasm; (7) the incidence of aspiration pneumonitis; (8) the incidence of anastomotic fistula; (9) the incidence of moderate to severe pain within 24 and 48 h at rest and when coughing; (10) the incidence of additional rescue analgesics use. The definition of all the secondary outcomes is provided in Supplemental S2.

Data collection, management, and quality control

Preoperative data including age, sex, height, weight, ASA physical status, pulmonary function, and comorbidities were recorded. The following intraoperative data will be collected: type of surgery, duration of surgery and anesthesia, intraoperative bleeding, and blood transfusion volume.

For the first 7 days after surgery, patients will be visited and evaluated in the hospital wards. Patients will be followed up by telephone after discharge. PPCs will be recorded up to 7 days after surgery. Pain at rest and during motion will be evaluated with an 11-point NRS scale (0 = no pain, 10 = worst pain imaginable) 24 and 48 h after surgery. The use of additional rescue analgesics within 48 h after surgery will be noted.

All data collected in accordance with the study protocol will be first manually completed in case report form (CRF). Then, the data will be transferred to an electronic database capture (EDC) by two independently trained investigators and proofread to ensure the accuracy of the data. Electronic data will be saved on a protected server of Sichuan Cancer Hospital. The original CRF and other paper-based forms will be stored in a locked office at the Department of Anesthesiology, Sichuan Cancer Hospital for 10 years. Moreover, patient information will be anonymous, including identity card number, name, address, inpatient number, and telephone number. After the trial is done, the researchers will not have access to modify the electronic data in EDC. The electronic data will be locked as soon as the data analysis is completed.

Follow up

Participants will be followed daily until the seventh day after the intervention. Outcomes will be recorded in CRF, including PPCs, respiratory failure, pneumothorax, atelectasis, pleural effusion, bronchospasm, aspiration pneumonitis, anastomotic fistula, NRS scores, and the use of additional rescue analgesics. The independently trained interviewer tasked with the follow-up will be blinded to the group allocation.

Assessment of safety

The safety of the study will be independently monitored by the Clinical Research Management Committee (CRMS) and data management committee (DMC) of the Sichuan Cancer Hospital, who will have access to review the blinded data. The independent CRMS and DMC will oversee the conduct of this study. Both committees are composed of experienced clinical investigators from our institution who are not directly involved in the implementation of the trial. These committees are responsible for monitoring study progress, data quality, protocol compliance, and participant safety. Regular meetings will be held to review trial conduct and resolve any emerging issues.

The intervention (intravenous lidocaine or placebo) will be discontinued under the following circumstances: 1) Occurrence of serious adverse events, such as signs of lidocaine toxicity (eg. arrhythmia, dizziness, or perioral numbness); 2) Significant intraoperative instability requiring deviation from the planned anesthetic protocol; 3) At the request of the participant or their legally authorized representative; 4) Clinical judgment of the attending anesthesiologist or investigator that continuing the intervention would not be in the participant’s best interest. All discontinuations will be documented, and the participant will remain in the study for follow-up and intention-to-treat analysis.

Intravenous infusion of lidocaine may be associated with local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST), and the operating room is stocked with a variety of monitoring equipment and resuscitation medications, as well as a 20% fat emulsion. The anesthesiologists involved in the study all have over 10 years of clinical anesthesia experience in tertiary hospitals and are proficient at recognizing, diagnosing, and treating LAST. Moreover, a dedicated group of specialists is available at each center to guide the treatment of LAST. All the details of study-related adverse events, including the cause, severity, corresponding treatment, and therapeutic effects will be recorded in detail in the CRF. In addition, adverse events will be reported immediately to the CRMS, and the severity, cause, and consequences will be determined. The CRMS will then report suspected or unexpected serious adverse events to the national health authorities.

Sample size estimates

Our unpublished pilot investigative data indicated that the incidence of PPCs after minimally invasive esophagectomy was approximately 40%. A relative difference of 30% in the primary outcome would be acceptable. A total sample size of 684 patients (342 in each group) would yield a 90% power to detect a relative risk reduction of 30% in PPCs at a two-sided alpha level of 0.05. The sample size was rounded up to 700, and an expected 10% dropout rate was accounted for, resulting in a final sample size of 770 participants. The Sichuan Cancer Hospital will aim to contribute a sample size of 470 cases, and the Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital 300 cases. However, considering the feasibility and enrollment progress, the enrollment number of each participating unit will be adjusted according to the actual situation, while aiming to maintain the balance of the enrollment size of each center. Any particular center will not contribute to more than 70% of the total number of cases. When the study reaches half of the sample size, a blinded researcher in charge of statistical analysis will carry out an interim correction of the sample size to determine the final required sample size.

Statistical analysis

The study's statistical review will be conducted by a blinded biomedical statistician before the peer-review submission. R software version 4.3.1 (R-project; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) will be used and a two-sided p < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant for all analyses. Multiple imputation (MI) will be used for missing data [16]. According to the result of the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, continuous variables will be expressed as mean ± SD or medians (interquartile range) and compared using Student’s t-test or nonparametric test. Categorical variables will be presented as counts and percentages and the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test will be used as appropriate. Effect sizes with a 95% confidence interval will be estimated if necessary. The full analysis set consists of all participants according to the intention-to-treat principle and the per-protocol set will be both performed. In addition, sensitivity analyses will also be conducted.

Ethics and dissemination

This trial will follow the guidelines outlined by the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol and informed consent form were approved by the Sichuan Cancer Hospital Ethic Review Board (SCCHEC-02–2023-123) and Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital Ethic Review Board ((2023–561)). Any important modifications to the protocol, including changes to eligibility criteria, outcomes, sample size, or data analysis methods, will be submitted for review and approval by the Sichuan Cancer Hospital Ethic Review Board and Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital Ethic Review Board. All approved changes will be updated in the trial registry (ClinicalTrials.gov) and communicated to all study investigators through regular team meetings and official email.

Participants who experience any harm related to study interventions will receive appropriate medical treatment at no cost during their hospital stay. As the trial is investigator-initiated and conducted within two tertiary hospitals, participants are covered under the institution’s standard liability policies. Some published studies have demonstrated the favorable safety profile of intravenous infusions of 1.5 to 3 mg/kg/h lidocaine. No additional financial compensation is planned.

The findings will be disseminated in a peer-reviewed journal and will be presented at international congresses.

Discussion

PPCs are one of the most common complications of MIE and are associated with poor short-term and long-term outcomes, including length of hospital stay and mortality. PPCs are associated with mechanical ventilation, one-lung ventilation, inflammatory response, and pulmonary atelectasis. Many methods, such as lung-protective ventilation, restrictive rehydration, etc., have been developed to protect against PPC, but its incidence remains high, at approximately 40% [17]. Therefore, strategies to reduce PPC incidence should not be overlooked, including intravenous lidocaine infusion.

Although the role of intravenous lidocaine infusion on PPCs in MIE is unclear, a recent study showed that intravenous lidocaine infusion reduced the incidence of postoperative pulmonary complications and lowered C-reactive protein concentrations in patients undergoing abdominal surgery. Furthermore, animal experiments revealed that intravenous infusion of lidocaine improved alveolar fluid clearance [18], stabilized alveolar cell membranes [19], and ameliorated LPS-induced lung injury [18, 20]. In addition, intravenous lidocaine infusion was found to reduce the postoperative inflammatory response, postoperative pain, and opioid consumption [10, 21], which have been associated with the development of PPCs. Therefore, intravenous lidocaine infusion may be a potentially effective strategy in reducing PPCs for MIE.

Lidocaine's most recognized roles are as a local anesthetic and treatment of cardiac arrhythmias. In recent years, its non-local anesthetic effects, such as antitumor, anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and cell membrane stabilizing effects, have been the subject of research [4, 6, 22, 23]. Currently, only a few studies have examined the effects of intravenous lidocaine infusion on postoperative pulmonary complications in patients undergoing thoracic surgery. To the best of our knowledge, the present study is the first double-center, randomized controlled study to evaluate the effects of intravenous lidocaine infusion on the incidence of postoperative PPCs in patients with esophageal cancer.

Safety remains the primary concern with intravenous lidocaine infusion. Several studies have demonstrated the favorable safety profile of intravenous infusions of 1.5 to 3 mg/kg/h lidocaine. Lidocaine is an amide local anesthetic that is metabolized primarily by the liver. Some recent studies showed that the use of intravenous 2 mg/kg/h lidocaine in patients undergoing hepatectomy did not lead to any serious complications [6, 10, 24]. A pharmacokinetics study for lidocaine intraoperative infusion during partial hepatectomy reported that the concentration of lidocaine in the blood was not significantly elevated after partial hepatectomy, with the lidocaine levels being much lower than the toxic dose of 5 μg/ml [23].

Nevertheless, this study still has some limitations. First, although this is a double-center study, only three research centers were included, and all three centers are located in the same province in China. If the study results indicate that intravenous lidocaine infusion is beneficial in reducing PPCs, an international multicenter study might be conducted to confirm the results. Second, the intravenous lidocaine will only be administered until the patients are transferred out of the post-anesthesia care unit, whereas positive results (incidence of PPCs and postoperative pain) might have been observed if the infusion was prolonged [6]. Third, only the dose of 2 mg/kg/h lidocaine will be studied, and no other dose group was set up, which may also lead to negative results if the dose is too low. Finally, the levels of postoperative inflammation-related factors were not measured in this study.

In conclusion, this double-center, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial is designed to evaluate the role of intravenous lidocaine infusion in reducing postoperative PPCs in patients with MIE. If the hypothesis of this study is confirmed, intravenous lidocaine infusion will provide a new strategy for enhance recovery after surgery (ERAS) protocol for esophageal cancer to reduce PPCs and accelerate patient recovery.

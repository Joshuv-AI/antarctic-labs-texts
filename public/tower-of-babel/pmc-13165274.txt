Teaching NeuroImage: Cervical Myelopathy Due to Alveolar Spinal Echinococcosis Cysts
Authors: Julian Ullrich, Marcomaria Meluso, Luca R. Traini, Patrick Freund, Carl Moritz Zipser
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A 64-year-old male farmer with a history of prostate adenocarcinoma, treated with curative radiotherapy 2 years prior, presented with neck pain and weakness. On examination, he had an incomplete spastic tetraparesis (3/5 strength in upper extremities, 5/5 strength in lower extremities) and sensory level below C5. Cervicothoracic MRI showed garland-like compressive epidural masses, suggestive initially of metastases (Figure 1). Stable prostate-specific antigen and negative PET-CT argued against metastases, leading to CT-guided spinal biopsy, which revealed Echinococcus multilocularis cysts. Serology was IgG-positive against alveolar echinococcosis, and chest and abdominal CT showed multiple hepatic cysts. The patient was treated with albendazole for 6 months and underwent surgical decompression, resulting in full motor recovery.

Spinal Alveolar Echinococcus Cysts on Cervical MRI

(A) Sagittal T2-weighted MRI and (B) T1 postcontrast MRI at presentation 3 days from symptom onset showing garland-like, inhomogeneous, contrast-enhancing T2 hyperintense masses (arrows). (C) Axial T2-weighted MRI demonstrating cervical spinal cord compression caused by the echinococcus cysts (arrow).

Spinal echinococcosis may resemble metastatic disease, complicating diagnosis. Evaluation should include serology and abdominal and lung imaging (ultrasound, CT, and MRI) because biopsy may trigger allergic reaction.1 Echinococcosis represents an emerging public health concern. Diagnosis remains challenging even in endemic countries, including Switzerland.2

Multi-objective optimization design of linear oscillating actuator based on hybrid surrogate model
Authors: Minghu Yu, Jiekun Lin, Yuqiu Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Thrust fluctuations, including electromagnetic and cogging forces, significantly limit the efficiency of linear oscillating actuators (LOAs) and hinder their ability to generate reciprocating driving forces and stroke ranges, presenting a complex multi-objective optimization challenge. This study introduces an innovative optimization strategy utilizing a hybrid surrogate model (HSM) in conjunction with an improved nondominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II). The HSM integrates four complementary data-driven surrogate models through a heuristic weighting scheme based on the coefficient of determination, effectively capturing both nonlinear behaviors and local characteristics. This integration enhances the accuracy and robustness of motor performance predictions and facilitates the rapid generation of candidate solutions for optimal exploration within a broad design space. Furthermore, a modified NSGA-II improves the crowding degree model, thereby increasing the number of viable solutions in the target region. The proposed optimization strategy results in significant enhancements in average performance and a reduction in force fluctuations in LOAs, ultimately improving system controllability. Empirical validation through static and dynamic tests of the optimized prototype, coupled with finite element analysis, substantiates the efficacy of the HSM and the proposed optimization method. This research contributes a robust, data-driven framework for high-performance LOA design. The proposed strategy may offer insights for addressing similar complex multi-objective optimization problems in engineering.

Introduction

Traditional rotary motors and their intermediate conversion mechanisms often face issues such as low efficiency, large size, and poor reliability in high-frequency linear reciprocating drive industrial applications, such as linear compressors, active suspension systems, and vibration energy harvesters1,2. In this context, the linear oscillating actuator (LOA) emerges as a viable alternative, thanks to its compact design and absence of a crank drive. The moving-magnet LOA, in particular, offers notable benefits, such as reduced volume, high power density, and rapid dynamic response, attracting significant research interest2–7. However, the short lengths of both the stator and the mover in the LOA result in more pronounced end effects compared to other types of linear motors that feature only one shorter side. Thus thrust fluctuations restrict the mover’s stroke range and overall performance; therefore, greater emphasis on optimization is essential during the design process.

Advancements in motor topology2, materials, and drive circuits3 hold the potential for more cost-effective solutions. Furthermore, optimizing key dimensions of the stator and rotor can markedly enhance LOA performance. Primary optimization goals include increasing electromagnetic (EM) force for greater power density, minimizing the nonlinear effects of lateral and cogging force fluctuations on motion, and refining the back electromotive force (EMF) waveform to boost system controllability. These optimization efforts generally rely on finite element analysis (FEA) which requires extensive scans of various design parameters4–7, but may become inefficient and computationally demanding when dealing with multiple objectives and a high-dimensional design space. To enhance optimization reliability and efficiency, researchers have turned to intelligent algorithms, such as Multi-Objective Particle Swarm Optimization8 and Multi-Objective Genetic Algorithms9–12, which have shown effectiveness when integrated with FEA. These algorithms optimize crucial performance indicators, such as output torque, cogging torque, vibration noise, and thermal characteristics, across various rotary motor types. However, in the previous research on multi-objective optimization design of motors, a notable limitation persists: The crowding mechanism of traditional intelligent algorithms takes the extremum form as the optimization objectives, which has limitations and low flexibility in the multi-objective optimization designs that take the interval form as the optimization objectives.

In addition, considering that the reliance on FEA still impose significant computational costs, data-driven surrogate models have begun to replace traditional FEA approaches, categorized into statistical models (SM)13,14, machine learning (ML)15,16, and deep learning (DL)17,18. These surrogate models have been widely applied to various engineering applications for its high computational efficiency and generalization ability19–21. However, SM offer rapid approximations using explicit functions but struggle with high-dimensional data and complex nonlinear relationships. ML techniques improve prediction accuracy but can be prone to overfitting in low-dimensional contexts. DL methods, utilizing multi-layer neural networks, excel in handling complex high-dimensional mappings but require substantial data and computation, particularly at risk of overfitting with smaller datasets. Thus, achieving a balance between efficiency and accuracy in high-dimensional, nonlinear multi-objective optimization remains complex. And the substantial nonlinearity of the ferromagnetic materials, along with the numerous topological structure parameters and the strongly coupled nonlinear relationships among these parameters and the output functions, results in a high-dimensional and highly nonlinear multi-objective optimization problem.

To address the aforementioned issues, hybrid surrogate models are introduced into the multi-objective optimization of passenger car rear seats. Multiple sub-surrogate models are employed to predict multi-objective functions, with the optimal-performing sub-surrogate model selected for each objective function based on prediction errors22–24. Furthermore, heuristic weighting schemes are proposed based on prediction errors. By weighted combination of multiple surrogate models according to prediction errors, more robust hybrid surrogate models are constructed25. In addition, genetic algorithms are utilized to perform multiple rounds of iterative optimization on weighting coefficient combinations, which effectively enhances the prediction accuracy of hybrid surrogate models26.

Inspired by the above research, this paper proposes a novel multi-objective optimization strategy based on hybrid surrogate models (HSM) and an improved crowding distance mechanism, aimed at accurately predicting the EM performance of a dual E-core LOA (DELOA) during operation, promising faster runtime and enhanced robustness. Different from the aforementioned heuristic weighting schemes25,26, the proposed HSM comprehensively integrates four distinct data-driven sub-models from statistical models (SM), machine learning (ML), and deep learning (DL). It aims to provide fast approximations through explicit functions while being applicable to the high-dimensional, strongly coupled nonlinear characteristics of motor models. Secondly, the weights of the sub-models are allocated based on the coefficient of determination, avoiding complex evolutionary algorithms or uncertainty quantification processes and ensuring that the hybrid model achieves high prediction accuracy while minimizing computational costs. Moreover, an improved crowding degree model using in the nondominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II) enhances the solution density within effective target regions, increasing optimization efficiency without additional iterations. This optimization strategy leads to significant improvements in average performance and mitigation of force fluctuations in LOAs, thus enhancing system controllability. The accuracy and effectiveness of the proposed model are validated through FEA and experimental results in this study, suggesting its potential applicability in other fields facing multi-objective optimization challenges.

Principles and performance of DELOA

Operating principles and modeling

To enable the piston of the refrigeration linear compressor to achieve high-frequency reciprocating linear motion, DELOA, as illustrated in Fig. 1, is employed in conjunction with mechanical springs on either side of the mover to form the driving system.

Fig. 1DELOA topology diagram.

DELOA topology diagram.

Two armature windings are arranged on E-type stator cores, which are stacked along the z-axis and placed symmetrically in x-axis opposite orientations. Two PMs, magnetized oppositely along the y-axis, are embedded in the non-magnetic mover bracket situated within the air gap. The interaction between the armature magnetic field and the magnetic field of the PMs generates a force that propels the piston in the x-direction, while the normal attraction in the y-direction cancels out due to symmetry, thereby preventing y-direction offsets and vibrations. The mover bracket connects to mechanical springs on both sides, facilitating potential energy conversion and establishing a mechanical resonance system for the entire DELOA. When sinusoidal excitation is applied, the mover produces linear reciprocating motion, with the system achieving maximum efficiency when the electrical frequency aligns with the mechanical resonance frequency.

Pearson sensitivity analysis and dimensionality reduction

As previously indicated, the initial design parameters are considerable, totaling 9, which significantly increases the sample size required for multi-objective optimization. Although the electromagnetic characteristics of linear oscillating actuator exhibits high nonlinearity within the local parameter range, there is often a dominant global monotonic trend between key parameters and primary performance objectives when evaluated from the entire design space. Therefore, a design parameter that has a substantial impact on performance usually exhibits a certain directional correlation in Pearson correlation analysis. Therefore, to mitigate computational costs, Pearson sensitivity analysis is employed for dimensionality reduction within the design space.

All correlation coefficients are shown in Fig. 6. Within the constraint range, the three objective functions are influenced to varying degrees by the 9 parameters, resulting in inconsistent variation patterns among the objectives. The correlation coefficients of P8 and P9 to the three objective functions are all very low, which indicates that the changes in these 2 parameters within the range have the lowest impact on the thrust. Therefore, P8 and P9 are disregarded, thereby reducing the original optimization problem from 9 design parameters to 7.

Heuristic construction of HSM for DELOA

The data-driven surrogate model offers significant cost advantages over the EM model developed using the FEA. However, a single surrogate model often proves inadequate for multi-input multi-output optimization problems. To address this, a HSM was constructed through a heuristic weighted combination of multiple surrogate models based on the determination coefficient.

Four data-driven sub-models are selected to form HSM of DELOA, including the Response Surface Model (RSM), Kriging Model (KM), Back Propagation Neural Network (BPNN), and Support Vector Regression (SVR), all of which have been proven effective in predicting the EM characteristics of other kind of motors. These models encompass the previously mentioned SM, ML, and DL, leveraging their complementary strengths to improve the overall prediction accuracy of the HSM. RSM, based on polynomial regression, establishes a second-order functional relationship between observation points and the objective function8. It is computationally efficient but limited in fitting highly nonlinear data KM, relying on spatial interpolation, predicts the spatial mapping between observation points and the objective function, making it highly effective for spatially distributed data9. However, its performance depends on covariance function selection and parameter estimation, which may introduce interpolation errors in sparse data regions. SVR incorporates an insensitive loss function during regression, penalizing errors that exceed a predefined threshold, thereby enhancing generalization and reducing the risk of over fitting15. However, the threshold is often set empirically, lacking theoretical support, and it is difficult to find the optimal balance. BPNN, based on a multilayer feedforward neural network, adjusts weights using the back propagation algorithm to minimize prediction errors29. Its nonlinear activation functions enable accurate capture of complex relationships in the data but introduce a risk of over fitting.

By combining the advantages of these four sub-models, the proposed HSM enhances the fitting of nonlinear data, reduces dependence on model parameters, improves generalization, and minimizes overfitting, enabling effective and accurate predictions for multiple DELOA optimization objectives. Improved multi-objective optimization algorithm

NSGA-II is an intelligent optimization algorithm that generates new populations using genetic algorithms and selects and retains them based on a crowding degree ranking mechanism. The new populations evolves into a higher-performing population through selection, crossover, and mutation. The improved NSGA-II for multi-objective optimization of DELOA consists of three components: genetic algorithms, non-dominated sorting, and crowding degree sorting as follows.

Genetic algorithm for generating new individuals:

Genetic algorithm for generating new individuals:

Genetic algorithm for generating new individuals:

The genetic algorithm generates new individuals while preserving characteristics of superior solutions through crossover and mutation. Crossover involves randomly selecting 60% of samples to form pairs that exchange parameter variables, creating new motor design solutions. Mutation simulates gene changes by randomly adjusting an individual’s features with a specific probability to avoid local optima. In each iteration, 5% of the samples are selected, and one variable from each is adjusted using a normally distributed random number, rounded and scaled by the mutation step size.

Sorting of sample crowding degree:

To ensure the diversity of the Pareto front, the traditional NSGA-II method calculates the crowding degree of samples following non-dominated sorting. This calculation reflects the distribution of adjacent points around each sample; thus, a lower crowding degree indicates a sparser surrounding area. By prioritizing samples with lower crowding degrees, the Pareto front can effectively expand in three-dimensional space after a specified number of genetic iterations, which are defined by the number of optimization objectives as dimensions. As the iterations progress, the distance between the optimization parameters of the Pareto front samples in each dimension increases, leading to a gradual expansion of the surface formed.

This platform includes the optimized DELOA prototype functioning as the driving motor, a KTR11 linear position sensor for capturing the actual mover position signal, a 3.6 kW DC power supply, a driving circuit board with a designated switching frequency of 10 kHz, and a control circuit board with an STM32H743 microcontroller operating at a core frequency of 480 MHz. Comprehensive static and dynamic performance tests of the motor have been conducted, confirming both the model and the optimization effects.

Conclusion

This paper investigates the thrust characteristics of a DELOA and presents an improved multi‑objective optimization design method that integrates a data‑driven hybrid surrogate model (HSM). The prediction capability of the HSM and the effectiveness of the optimization approach are evaluated through FEA and experimental measurements. The main findings are summarized as follows:

Due to the short lengths of both the stator and the mover in the LOA, end effects are pronounced; therefore, special attention must be given to thrust performance during the design process. By optimizing key dimensions, such as the stator’s tooth crown and end tooth width, as well as the length of the mover’s magnets, end effects can be effectively suppressed. This minimizes the significant fluctuations in thrust, both under no-load and load conditions, when the mover approaches the stator’s edge. This improvement enhances the linearity of the EM force coefficient, thereby supporting more precise control of linear reciprocating motion.By integrating various data-driven sub-models with a coefficient-of-determination-based weighting mechanism, a high-accuracy HSM for DELOA is developed. The HSM captures nonlinear and local features of the DELOA, demonstrating high accuracy and robustness in predicting linear oscillating actuator performance.Improved crowding degree model to refine the ranking mechanism of the NSGA-II algorithm significantly increases the density of solutions in the objective space without additional computational cost, thus boosting optimization efficiency.The hybrid surrogate model proposed in this paper adopts four sub-models, namely RSM, KM, BPNN and SVR, each of which has its own advantages in the field of electromagnetic devices. It exhibits distinct advantages such as high fitting accuracy and strong robustness in data-driven modeling, performance prediction and multi-objective optimization. However, this also limits its specific application scope to motors and other electromagnetic devices. Therefore, in future work, when the optimization method is further applied to other high-dimensional, highly nonlinear and multi-output systems, more advantageous sub-models in the corresponding fields can be selected to construct the hybrid surrogate model.

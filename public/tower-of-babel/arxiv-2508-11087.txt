For a Banach space
X
,
x\in X
and
r>0
,
B_{X}(x,r)
(
B_{X}[x,r]
) denotes the open (closed) ball in
X
centered at
x
and radius
r
.
\mathcal{F}(X)
denotes the set of all nonempty finite subsets of
X
. Let us recall the following.

Definition 0.1
.

[
5
]

(a)

For
\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}\}\subseteq X
and a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we define
r_{f}(x,F)=f(\|x-a_{1}\|,\cdots,\|x-a_{n}\|)
,
\textrm{rad}_{X}^{f}(F)=\underset{x\in X}{\inf}r_{f}(x,F)
and
\textrm{Cent}_{X}^{f}(F)=\{x\in X:r_{f}(x,F)=\textrm{rad}_{X}^{f}(F)\}
. We say that
F
admits
f
-centers
if
\textrm{Cent}_{X}^{f}(F)\neq\emptyset
.
X
is said to admit
Chebyshev centers
for finite sets if
\textrm{Cent}_{X}^{f}(F)\neq\emptyset
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}(X)
. Elements of
\textrm{Cent}_{X}^{f}(F)
are called the
f
-centers
of
F
.

(b)

If
f
is of the form
f(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})=\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\max}\rho_{i}t_{i}
, where
\rho=(\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{n})
,
\rho_{i}>0
for all
i=1,\cdots,n
, then the quantities
r_{f}(x,F)
,
\textrm{rad}_{X}^{f}(F)
and
\textrm{Cent}_{X}^{f}(F)
are denoted by
r_{\rho}(x,F),\textrm{rad}_{X}^{\rho}(F)
and
\textrm{Cent}_{X}^{\rho}(F)
respectively.
F
is said to admit
weighted Chebyshev centers
if
\textrm{Cent}_{X}^{\rho}(F)\neq\emptyset
. When
\textrm{Cent}_{X}^{\rho}(F)\neq\emptyset
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}(X)
, we say that
X
admits
weighted Chebyshev centers
for finite sets.

(c)

When
\rho_{i}=1
for all
i=1,\cdots,n
, then the quantities
r_{\rho}(x,F)
,
\textrm{rad}_{X}^{\rho}(F)
and
\textrm{Cent}_{X}^{\rho}(F)
are denoted by
r(x,F),\textrm{rad}_{X}(F)
and
\textrm{Cent}_{X}(F)
respectively. When
\textrm{Cent}_{X}(F)\neq\emptyset
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}(X)
, then
X
is said to admit
Chebyshev centers
for finite sets.

In
[
5
]
Veselý studies
f
-centers for
continuous, monotone
and
coercive
functions
f
(see
[
5
]
for definitions). He observes that for a finite subset
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}\subseteq X
to admit
f
centers for continuous, monotone and coercive functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, it is sufficient for
F
to admit weighted Chebyshev centers. Indeed, Veselý derives the following.

Theorem 0.2
.

[
5
, Theorem 2.7]

For a Banach space
X
and
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}\subseteq X
, the following are equivalent.

(a)

If
r_{1},\cdots,r_{n}>0
and
\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X^{**}}[a_{i},r_{i}]\neq\emptyset
then
\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X}[a_{i},r_{i}]\neq\emptyset
.

(b)

\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}
admits weighted Chebyshev centers for all weights
\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{n}>0
.

(c)

\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}
admits
f
-centers for every continuous, monotone, coercive function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

Definition 0.3
.

[
5
, Definition 2.8]

A Banach space
X\in(GC)
if for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}\subseteq X
, any one of the equivalent conditions
(a),(b),(c)
of Theorem
0.2
is satisfied.

Veselý in
[
5
]
asks the following question.

Question 0.4
.

[
5
, Problem 2.9]

Does there exist a Banach space
X
that does not belong to
(GC)
but any finite set in
X
admits a (classical) Chebyshev center?

We show that if
X
admits Chebyshev centers for finite sets, then
X\in(GC)
. First, we need the following characterization of Chebyshev centers.

Theorem 0.5
.

For a Banach space
X
, the following are equivalent.

(a)

For
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in X
and
r>0
such that
\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X^{**}}[x_{i},r]\neq\emptyset
, then
\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X}[x_{i},r]\neq\emptyset
.

(b)

X
admits Chebyshev centers for finite sets.

Proof.

(a)\Rightarrow(b)
. Let
F=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}\in\mathcal{F}(X)
and
x^{**}\in\textrm{Cent}_{X^{**}}(F)
. Then
x^{**}\in\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X^{**}}[x_{i},\textrm{rad}_{X^{**}}(F)]
. Since from
[
5
, Theorem 2.6]
,
\textrm{rad}_{X}(F)=\textrm{rad}_{X^{**}}(F)
, by our assumption, there exists
x\in X
such that
x\in\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X}[x_{i},\textrm{rad}_{X}(F)]
and hence
x\in\textrm{Cent}_{X}(F)
.

(b)\Rightarrow(a)
. Let
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in X
and
r>0
be such that
\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X^{**}}[x_{i},r]\neq\emptyset
. Let
F=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}
and
x\in\textrm{Cent}_{X}(F)
. Then
x\in\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X}[x_{i},\textrm{rad}_{X}(F)]
. Since from
[
5
, Theorem 2.6]
,
\textrm{rad}_{X^{**}}(F)=\textrm{rad}_{X}(F)\leq r
, we have
\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X}[x_{i},r]\neq\emptyset
and this completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove our main result.

Theorem 0.6
.

Suppose a Banach space
X
admits Chebyshev centers for finite sets. Then
X\in(GC)
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
X\notin(GC)
. Then there exist
n
closed balls
(B_{X}[x_{i},r_{i}])_{i=1}^{n}
in
X
such that
\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X^{**}}[x_{i},r_{i}]\neq\emptyset
but
\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X}[x_{i},r_{i}]=\emptyset
. Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
r>r_{i}
for all
i=1,\cdots,n
. We construct
n
closed balls
(B_{X}[w_{i},r])_{i=1}^{n}
in
X
such that
B_{X}[x_{i},r_{i}]\subseteq B_{X}[w_{i},r]
for all
i=1,\cdots,n
and
\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X}[w_{i},r]=\emptyset
which, from Theorem
0.5
, will contradict our assumption.

Let
L_{1}=\cap_{i=2}^{n}B_{X}[x_{i},r_{i}]
and
L_{2}=B_{X}[x_{1},r_{1}]
.

Case 1:
Suppose
L_{1}=\emptyset
. Then we take
w_{1}=x_{1}
and obtain
B_{X}[w_{1},r]\cap\cap_{i=2}^{n}B_{X}[x_{i},r_{i}]=\emptyset
and proceed further to obtain
w_{2}
.

Case 2:
Suppose
L_{1}\neq\emptyset
. Then as
L_{1}\cap L_{2}=\emptyset
, we have
(L_{1}-\{x_{1}\})\cap B_{X}[0,r_{1}]=\emptyset
. Thus there exists
f\in X^{*}
such that
f(y-x_{1})\geq 1>f(x)
for all
y\in L_{1}
and
x\in B_{X}(0,r_{1})
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be such that
f(x)\leq 1-\varepsilon
for all
x\in B_{X}(0,r_{1})
. Thus,
\|f\|=\underset{x\in B_{X}(0,1)}{\sup}f(x)=\frac{1}{r_{1}}\underset{x\in B_{X}(0,r_{1})}{\sup}f(x)\leq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{r_{1}}
. Choose
\delta>0
such that
\delta<\frac{\varepsilon}{r-r_{1}}
. Let
z\in S_{X}
be such that
f(z)\leq-\|f\|+\delta
and
w_{1}=x_{1}+(r-r_{1})z\in X
.

Let
x\in B_{X}[x_{1},r_{1}]
. Then we have

\|x-w_{1}\|\leq\|x-x_{1}\|+\|x_{1}-w_{1}\|\leq r_{1}+(r-r_{1})\|z\|\leq r.

Thus
B_{X}[x_{1},r_{1}]\subseteq B_{X}[w_{1},r]
.

Let
x\in B_{X}[w_{1},r]
. Then

\displaystyle f(x-x_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x-w_{1})+f(w_{1}-x_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x-w_{1})+(r-r_{1})f(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|r+(r-r_{1})(-\|f\|+\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|r_{1}+(r-r_{1})\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon+\delta(r-r_{1})<1.

Thus
x\notin L_{1}
and hence
L_{1}\cap B_{X}[w_{1},r]=\emptyset
.

Now suppose we have
w_{i}
for all
i
with
1\leq i\leq j(<n)
such that
B_{X}[x_{i},r_{i}]\subseteq B_{X}[w_{i},r]
and
\cap_{i=1}^{j}B_{X}[w_{i},r]\cap\cap_{i=j+1}^{n}B_{X}[x_{i},r_{i}]=\emptyset
.

If we take
K_{1}=\cap_{i=1}^{j}B_{X}[w_{i},r]\cap_{i=j+2}^{n}B_{X}[x_{i},r_{i}]
and
K_{2}=B_{X}[x_{j+1},r_{j+1}]
, then
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\emptyset
. Now, following similar techniques as before, we get
w_{j+1}
and hence by induction, our proof is complete.
∎

Remark 0.7
.

(a)

Let
X=\{(x_{n})\in c_{0}:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}(x_{2n}-x_{2n-1})=0\}
and
Y=\{(x_{n})\in c_{0}:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}x_{2n-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}x_{2n}=0\}
. It follows from

that

and hence

admits centers. As

is a one dimensional subspace,

admits centers. But it is shown in
[
3
, Example 2.1]
that
X\notin(GC)
. Thus, from Theorem
0.6
,

(b)

It is observed in
[

]
that membership to the class
(GC)
is not a three-space property. Here we have provided another example of this fact.

We now recall the notion of best
n
-nets from
[
4
]
.

Definition 0.8
.

(a)

Any system of
n
-points in the space
X
is called
n
-nets
.

(b)

For a closed and bounded subset
F\subseteq X
, the radius of a covering of
F
by an
n
-net
S_{n}=\{y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\}
is the quantity
R(F,S_{n})=\underset{x\in F}{\sup}\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\min}\|x-y_{i}\|
. We define
R_{n}(F)=\underset{S_{n}}{\inf}R(F,S_{n})
, where the infimum is taken over all possible
n
-nets in
X
. An
n
-net
S_{n}^{\prime}
for which
R(F,S_{n}^{\prime})=R_{n}(F)
is called a
best possible
n
-net
for
F
.

(c)

We say that
X
admits
best
n
-nets
for finite sets, if for all
F\in\mathcal{F}(X)
, there exists an
n
-net
S_{n}
such that
R(F,S_{n})=R_{n}(F)
.

From the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 4.1]
, we can show that if
X
admits centers for finite sets, then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
X
admits best
n
-nets for finite sets. Thus, using this observation and Theorem
0.6
, we have the following.

Theorem 0.9
.

For a Banach space
X
, the following are equivalent.

(a)

X\in(GC)
.

(b)

X
admits Chebyshev centers for finite sets.

(c)

X
admits best
n
-nets for finite sets, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

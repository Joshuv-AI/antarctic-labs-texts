Awesome graph parameters
Kenny Bešter Štorgel, Clément Dallard, Vadim Lozin, Martin Milanič, Viktor Zamaraev

Abstract
For a graph G G , we denote by α ⁡ ( G ) \alpha(G) the size of a maximum independent set and by ω ⁡ ( G ) \omega(G) the size of a maximum clique in G G . Our paper lies on the edge of two lines of research, related to α \alpha and ω \omega , respectively. One of them studies α \alpha -variants of graph parameters, such as α \alpha -treewidth or α \alpha -degeneracy. The second line deals with graph classes where some parameters are bounded by a function of ω ⁡ ( G ) \omega(G) . A famous example of this type is the family of χ \chi -bounded classes, where the chromatic number χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) is bounded by a function of ω ⁡ ( G ) \omega(G) . A Ramsey-type argument implies that if the α \alpha -variant of a graph parameter ρ \rho is bounded by a constant in a class 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , then ρ \rho is bounded by a function of ω \omega in 𝒢 \mathcal{G} . If the reverse implication also holds, we say that ρ \rho is awesome . Otherwise, we say that ρ \rho is awful . In the present paper, we identify a number of awesome and awful graph parameters, derive some algorithmic applications of awesomeness, and propose a number of open problems related to these notions.



1
Introduction

Graph parameters play a central role in structural and algorithmic graph theory, capturing key combinatorial properties of graphs.
Often, a desirable algorithmic or structural property is established by identifying a suitable graph parameter and showing that the target behaviour follows from its boundedness. For example, bounded degeneracy ensures that every subgraph has a low-degree vertex, enabling compact graph representations
[
65
]
; bounded treewidth yields efficient algorithms for problems expressible in monadic second-order logic that are
NP
-hard in general
[
24
]
.

A potential limitation of this approach is that boundedness of a graph parameter can substantially restrict graph structure: for instance, graphs of bounded treewidth are necessarily sparse, so boundedness of treewidth does not capture dense graphs. This motivates the study of relaxations or generalisations of boundedness of graph parameters that retain useful structural properties or algorithmic tractability while encompassing richer classes.

The present paper is on the edge of two such lines of research.
The first concerns
clique-bounded parameters
, where the value of a parameter is bounded as a function of the clique number
\omega
. A central example is the theory of
\chi
-bounded classes—hereditary classes in which the chromatic number
\chi(G)
is bounded by a function of
\omega(G)
—which emerged as a generalisation of perfect graphs
[
40
]
, and has been the subject of extensive study (see, e.g.,
[
63
]
).
A more recent example is the family of
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-bounded classes, where the treewidth is bounded by a function of the clique number; such classes are known to have useful algorithmic applications (see
[
29
,
14
]
).

The second line focuses on
independence variants
of parameters.
The independence variant of a parameter
\rho
, denoted
\alpha
-
\rho
, is obtained by relaxing the size constraint in the definition of
\rho
to one on the independence number.
These variants are motivated in part by algorithmic considerations: for example, bounded
\alpha
-treewidth—a parameter introduced independently by Yolov
[
67
]
and by Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel (under the name
tree-independence number

[
30
,
31
]
)—still guarantees polynomial-time solvability of the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem (and many others, see
[
27
,
67
,
52
,
30
,
15
]
), while capturing significantly larger graph classes than bounded treewidth.
Another example of this type is the following independence variant of degeneracy:
the
\alpha
-degeneracy
of a graph
G
is the minimum
k
such that every induced subgraph of
G
has a vertex whose neighbourhood has independence number at most
k
.
This parameter was studied under the name
inductive independence number
by Ye and Borodin
[
66
]
, from both structural and algorithmic points of view.

A simple Ramsey-type argument (see
Section

3.4
) shows that bounded
\alpha
-
\rho
always implies that
\rho
is clique-bounded.
This observation allows properties implied by clique-boundedness of
\rho
to be transferred automatically to classes with bounded
\alpha
-
\rho
.
It is natural to ask whether the converse holds:

Does clique-boundedness of
\rho
always imply boundedness of
\alpha
-
\rho
?

Such an implication is not always straightforward.
For example, motivated by the complexity of the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem, it was conjectured that clique-boundedness of treewidth implies boundedness of
\alpha
-treewidth
[
31
]
(see also
[
29
]
); this remained open for some time before being recently refuted by Chudnovsky and Trotignon
[
23
]
.
Nevertheless, the question of whether
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem admits a polynomial-time algorithm in any hereditary graph class with clique-bounded treewidth, raised by Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel in
[
29
,
31
]
, remains open.

Motivated by the example of treewidth, in this paper, we formalise the notions of clique-boundedness and independence variants of graph parameters, and investigate when the two notions coincide.
Specifically, a graph parameter
\rho
is said to be
awesome
if clique-boundedness of
\rho
is equivalent to boundedness of
\alpha
-
\rho
, and is
awful
otherwise.
Formal treatment of these notions is given in
Section

3
.
In
Section

4
, we show that treedepth and pathwidth are awful parameters. In
Section

5
, we identify some parameters that are awesome, and derive structural and algorithmic consequences of awesomeness, including an infinite family of graph parameters
\rho
such that
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem admits a polynomial-time algorithm in any hereditary graph class with clique-bounded
\rho
.
We conclude with a discussion of further questions and open problems in
Section

6
. In the next section, we introduce basic notation and main notions.

3
The framework

In this section, we formalize the key concepts used in the paper. In
Section

3.1
, we recall the formal definitions of graph classes and graph parameters, and discuss how the boundedness of graph parameters can be used to define families of classes (i.e., class properties). We then consider more general ways of defining class properties, where one parameter is bounded as a function of another parameter, rather than by a constant. Prominent examples include
\chi
-bounded and
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-bounded classes.
To treat these approaches to defining class properties uniformly, we introduce in
Section

3.2
the notion of a
hyperparameter
.
In
Section

3.3
, we demonstrate how various classical graph parameters, as well as their independence variants (e.g., the tree-independence number), can be expressed as hyperparameters.
In
Section

3.4
, we observe that for any cardinality based hyperparameter
\rho
, boundedness of its independence variant in a class of graphs implies that
\rho
is clique-bounded in that class.
Finally, in
Section

3.5
, we formally state the central question of our study: for which hyperparameters does the converse implication hold?

3.1
Graph parameters, graph classes, and class properties

Graph classes and graph parameters.

A
graph class
is a collection of graphs closed under isomorphism.
A graph class is
hereditary
if it is closed under vertex deletion; in other words, for every graph in the class, all of its induced subgraphs also belong to the class. Given a graph class
\mathcal{G}
, the
hereditary closure
of
\mathcal{G}
is the class of all induced subgraphs of graphs in
\mathcal{G}
.
For a set
\mathcal{F}
of graphs, we say that a graph
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free
if no graph in
\mathcal{F}
is isomorphic to an induced subgraph of
G
.
If
\mathcal{F}
consists of a single graph
F
, we write
F
-free instead of
\{F\}
-free.
It is a folklore fact that a graph class
\mathcal{G}
is hereditary if and only if there exists a set
\mathcal{F}
of graphs such that a graph
G
belongs to
\mathcal{G}
if and only if
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free.

A
graph parameter
is a function mapping graphs to the set of integers that does not distinguish between isomorphic graphs.
A graph parameter is
monotone under induced subgraphs
if, for every graph
G
and every induced subgraph
H
of
G
, the value of the parameter on
H
is at most its value on
G
.
Many questions in graph theory can be formulated in terms of hereditary graph classes and graph parameters.
In particular, structural graph theory aims at understanding which substructures, when present in a graph, are “responsible” for a certain parameter to be large.
For example:

•

Ramsey’s theorem (see
[
61
]
) states that a necessary and sufficient condition for the number of vertices of a graph to be large is the presence of either a large clique or a large independent set.

•

The Grid-Minor Theorem of Robertson and Seymour (see
[
62
]
) states that a necessary and sufficient condition for the treewidth of a graph to be large is the presence of a large grid as a minor.

•

The Strong Perfect Graph Theorem due to Chudnovsky, Robertson, Seymour, and Thomas (see
[
22
]
) can be stated as follows.
A necessary and sufficient condition for the maximum difference, over all induced subgraphs, between the chromatic number and the clique number to be strictly positive is the presence of an induced odd cycle of length at least five in the graph or its complement.

•

More generally, understanding necessary and sufficient conditions under which large cliques are the only structures responsible for large chromatic number is the main research goal of the theory of
\chi
-boundedness (see
[
63
]
for a survey).

From graph parameters to class properties.

Given a graph parameter
\rho
, we say that a graph class
\mathcal{G}
has
bounded
\rho
if there exists an integer
c
such that for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G}
, every induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
satisfies
\rho(G^{\prime})\leq c
.
Note that a graph class
\mathcal{G}
has bounded
\rho
if and only if its hereditary closure has bounded
\rho
.
Our definition differs slightly from the more common definition stating that a graph class
\mathcal{G}
has bounded
\rho
if there exists a constant
c
such that
\rho(G)\leq c
for all graphs
G
in
\mathcal{G}
.
However, the two definitions coincide for hereditary graph classes, as well as for any graph parameter
\rho
that is monotone under induced subgraphs.

A
class property
is a family of graph classes.
Given a class property
\mathcal{P}
and a graph class
\mathcal{G}
, we say that
\mathcal{G}

satisfies

\mathcal{P}
if
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{P}
.
Any graph parameter
\rho
naturally leads to a class property
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
, consisting of precisely those graph classes
\mathcal{G}
that have bounded
\rho
.
The identification of substructures “responsible” for a parameter
\rho
to be large leads to a characterization of the corresponding class property
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
.
For example:

•

The Grid-Minor Theorem states that a minor-closed graph class has bounded treewidth if and only if it excludes some grid.

•

Ramsey’s theorem states that a hereditary graph class has bounded order if and only if it excludes some complete graph and some edgeless graph.

Generalizations.

Further class properties can be obtained by considering two graph parameters at once, by requiring that one of the parameters is bounded by a function of the other one.
This includes the following class properties:

•

A hereditary graph class
\mathcal{G}
is
\chi
-bounded
if in the class
\mathcal{G}
large cliques are the only structures responsible for large chromatic number, that is, there exists a function
f
such that every graph
G
in
\mathcal{G}
satisfies
\chi(G)\leq f(\omega(G))
, where
\chi(G)
and
\omega(G)
denote the chromatic number and the clique number of
G
, respectively (see
[
41
]
).

•

A hereditary graph class
\mathcal{G}
is
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-bounded
if in the class
\mathcal{G}
large cliques are the only structures responsible for large treewidth (see, e.g.,
[
29
,
14
]
).

The above two examples are special cases of the following framework.
Given two graph parameters
\rho
and
\sigma
, we say that a graph class
\mathcal{G}
is
(\rho,\sigma)
-bounded
(or
has
\sigma
-bounded
\rho
) if there exists a nondecreasing function
f
such that, for every graph
G
in the class
\mathcal{G}
, every induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
satisfies
\rho(G^{\prime})\leq f(\sigma(G^{\prime}))
.

In general, there is no assumption on
\mathcal{G}
to be hereditary, and this is the reason why the condition is imposed on all induced subgraphs of graphs in
\mathcal{G}
.
However, in our paper we focus on hereditary graph classes, in which case it of course suffices to require that
\rho(G)\leq f(\sigma(G))
for all graphs
G
in
\mathcal{G}
.

In the above terminology,
\chi
-bounded and
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-bounded graph classes are said to have
\omega
-bounded chromatic number
and
\omega
-bounded treewidth
, respectively.
The latter class property is a subfamily of the class property of
\omega
-bounded expansion studied by Jiang, Nešetřil, and Ossona de Mendez
[
51
]
.
Several other class properties captured by the above framework were studied in the literature, including
\chi
-bounded colouring number (see the book by Jensen and Toft
[
50
]
),
\chi
-bounded minimum degree (see
[
39
]
), degeneracy-bounded treewidth (by Gyárfás and Zaker
[
11
]
), and
\omega
-bounded pathwidth (by Hermelin, Mestre, and Rawitz
[
43
]
).

Clique-bounded class properties.

As exemplified by the properties of
\chi
-boundedness,
\omega
-bounded treewidth, and
\omega
-bounded pathwidth, an important special case of the above framework for defining properties of graph classes is the case when the parameter
\sigma
is the clique number,
\omega
.
In this case, given a graph parameter
\rho
, we may also say that a graph class that has
\omega
-bounded
\rho
has
clique-bounded
\rho
and denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
the corresponding class property.
Thus, we speak of graph classes with clique-bounded pathwidth, clique-bounded treewidth, etc.

Note that for any graph parameter
\rho
, a graph class
\mathcal{G}
belongs to the class property
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
if and only if there exists a function
f
, called a
binding function
, such that for every graph
G
in the class
\mathcal{G}
, every induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
satisfies
\rho(G^{\prime})\leq f(\omega(G^{\prime}))
.
Furthermore, any hereditary graph class
\mathcal{G}
satisfying the class property
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
(bounded
\rho
) also satisfies the property
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
(clique-bounded
\rho
), which can be observed by considering the constant function
f(p)=c
, for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
, where
c
is any integer such that
\rho(G)\leq c
for all graphs
G
in
\mathcal{G}
.

3.2
Hyperparameters

An
annotated graph
is a tuple
(G,X)
such that
G
is a graph and
X\subseteq V(G)
.
An
annotated graph parameter
is a function
\lambda
that assigns to every annotated graph
(G,X)
an integer such that for any graph
G^{\prime}
isomorphic to
G
and any isomorphism
f
from
G
to
G^{\prime}
, it holds that
\lambda(G,X)=\lambda(G^{\prime},f(X))
, where
f(X)=\{f(x)\colon x\in X\}
.
Two annotated graph parameters that play an important role in the rest of the paper are: (i) the cardinality function, defined by
\mathtt{card}(G,X)\coloneqq|X|
for all annotated graphs
(G,X)
, and (ii) the independence number of the subgraph of
G
induced by
X
, defined by
\alpha(G,X)\coloneqq\alpha(G[X])
for all annotated graphs
(G,X)
.

Given a graph
G
, a
hypergraph over
G
is a nonempty set of subsets of
V(G)
.
Given a finite nonempty family
\mathcal{F}
of hypergraphs over a graph
G
and an annotated graph parameter
\lambda
, we define the
min-max value
of
(G,\mathcal{F})
to be the quantity

\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{minmax}(G,\mathcal{F})=\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}}\max_{X\in H}\lambda(G,X)\,.

The
\lambda
-max-min value
of
(G,\mathcal{F})
, defined analogously, is

\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{maxmin}(G,\mathcal{F})=\max_{H\in\mathcal{F}}\min_{X\in H}\lambda(G,X)\,.

A
hypermapping
is a mapping
\mathcal{F}
that associates to each graph
G
a finite nonempty family
\mathcal{F}_{G}
of hypergraphs over
G
such that, for any graph
G^{\prime}
isomorphic to
G
and any isomorphism
f
from
G
to
G^{\prime}
, we have
\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}=\{\varphi(H)\colon H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}\}
where
\varphi(H)=\{f(X)\colon X\in H\}
for all
H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}
.
A
graph hyperparameter
, or simply a
hyperparameter
, is a tuple
\rho=(\lambda,\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})
such that
\lambda
is an annotated graph parameter,
\mathrm{opt}\in\{\mathrm{minmax},\mathrm{maxmin}\}
, and
\mathcal{F}
is a hypermapping.

As we show next, to every hyperparameter one can associate a graph parameter.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\rho=(\lambda,\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})
be a hyperparameter.
Given a graph
G
, we denote by
\rho(G)
the value of
\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{opt}(G,\mathcal{F}_{G})
.
Then,
\rho
is a graph parameter, that is, if
G
and
G^{\prime}
are isomorphic graphs, then
\rho(G)=\rho(G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Consider first the case when
\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{minmax}
.
Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be isomorphic.
Fix an isomorphism
f:G\to G^{\prime}
.
Note that, since
\lambda
is an annotated graph parameter, for every set
X\subseteq V(G)
, we have
\lambda(G,X)=\lambda(G^{\prime},f(X))
.
Recall also that
\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}=\{\varphi(H)\colon H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}\}
where
\varphi(H)=\{f(X)\colon X\in H\}
for all
H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}
.
The definition of
\varphi
implies that for every hypergraph
H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}
, we have that

\max_{X\in H}\lambda(G,X)=\max_{X\in H}\lambda(G^{\prime},f(X))=\max_{X^{\prime}\in\varphi(H)}\lambda(G^{\prime},X^{\prime})\,.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\rho(G)

\displaystyle=\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{minmax}(G,\mathcal{F}_{G})=\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}}\max_{X\in H}\lambda(G,X)=\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}}\max_{X^{\prime}\in\varphi(H)}\lambda(G^{\prime},X^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\min_{H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}}\max_{X^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}}\lambda(G^{\prime},X^{\prime})=\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{minmax}(G^{\prime},\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}})=\rho(G^{\prime})\,,

where the fourth equality follows from the fact that
\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}=\{\varphi(H)\colon H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}\}
.

The case when
\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{maxmin}
is similar.
∎

We say that a hyperparameter
\rho=(\lambda,\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})
is a

of the corresponding graph parameter

, as given by

.
Let us observe that every graph parameter
\rho
mapping graphs to the set of integers admits multiple hyperparameterisations, as can be seen by considering any tuple
(\lambda,\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})
such that
\lambda
is the annotated graph parameter that maps every annotated graph
(G,X)
to
\rho(G)
,
\mathrm{opt}\in\{\mathrm{minmax},\mathrm{maxmin}\}
, and
\mathcal{F}
is

However, such hyperparameterisations are artificial and, hence, uninteresting.
We are interested in hyperparameterisations whose definition reflects the definition of a certain graph parameter
\rho
in a more structural way, in the sense that the hypermapping corresponds to the set of feasible solutions to the graph optimisation problem defining
\rho

Several examples are given in

.

In this paper, we focus on two particular kinds of hyperparameterisations.
We say that a hyperparameter
(\lambda,\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})
is:

•

(or simply

is the cardinality function, that is, for all annotated graphs

•

(G,X)

\lambda(G,X)=\alpha(G,X)\coloneqq\alpha(G[X])

Given a hyperparameter
\rho=(\lambda,\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})

\eta
-variant
of
\rho

.
Throughout the paper, we mostly consider basic hyperparameters and their
\alpha
-variants.
Given a basic hyperparameter
\rho

\alpha
-variant as the

of
\rho
.

Proposition

allows us to interpret every hyperparameter

as some graph parameter, and, hence, naturally leads to the notions of graph classes that have

\rho
,
bounded

\rho
.

Let us mention an example of how some known inequalities between graph parameters can be easily lifted to analogous inequalities between hyperparameters.
Treewidth, pathwidth, and treedepth of a graph
G
are related by a following chain of inequalities:
\mathrm{tw}(G)\leq\mathrm{pw}(G)\leq\mathrm{td}(G)
. (The former inequality follows directly from the definitions; for the latter one, see, e.g.,
[
10
,
59
]
.)
Although the proof below does not involve any new ideas, we include it for the sake of completeness and since it is adapted to the notations of this paper.
(Under some mild technical conditions on
\lambda
, the chain of inequalities can also be extended to include the
\lambda
-variant of vertex cover number; see
Proposition

6.7
.)

Proposition 3.3
.

For every annotated graph parameter
\lambda
and every graph
G
, we have

\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{tw}(G)\leq\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)\leq\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{td}(G)\,.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph.
By the definition of
\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{tw}
, we have

\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{tw}(G)=\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{minmax}(G,\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{tw}})=\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{tw}}}\max_{X\in H}\lambda(G,X)\,,

where
\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{tw}}
is the family of all hypergraphs over
G
whose hyperedges are the bags of some tree decomposition of
G
.
Similarly,

\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)=\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{minmax}(G,\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{pw}})\quad\text{ and }\quad\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{td}(G)=\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{minmax}(G,\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{td}})\,,

where
\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{pw}}
is the family of all hypergraphs over
G
whose hyperedges are the bags of some path decomposition of
G
, and
\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{td}}
is the family of all hypergraphs over
G
whose hyperedges are the vertex sets of root-to-leaf paths of some treedepth decomposition of
G
.
To prove
\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{tw}(G)\leq\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)\leq\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{td}(G)
, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{td}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{pw}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{tw}}
.
The fact that every path decomposition is also a tree decomposition implies the second inclusion.
To see the first one, consider a treedepth decomposition
F
of
G
.
Fix an ordering of the leaves of the decomposition that can be obtained from a DFS traversal on
F
, starting the traversal of each component from its root.
The corresponding ordering of the root-to-leaf paths in
F
yields a path decomposition of
G
in which the bags are exactly the vertex sets of the root-to-leaf paths.
This implies that
\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{td}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{pw}}
.
∎

3.4
Clique-boundedness via Ramsey’s theorem

We now prove that for basic hyperparameters, boundedness of the independence variant implies clique-boundedness.
This result follows from Ramsey’s theorem
[
61
]
, which states that for any two nonnegative integers
a
and
b
, there exists a least nonnegative integer
R(a,b)
such that any graph with at least
R(a,b)
vertices has either a clique of size
a
or an independent set of size
b
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\rho
be a basic hyperparameter and let
\mathcal{G}
be a graph class that has bounded
\alpha\text{-}\rho
.
Then
\mathcal{G}
has clique-bounded
\rho
with a polynomial binding function.

Proof.

Let
\rho=(\mathtt{card},\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{G}
be as in the proposition.
Then, there exists an integer
k
such that for every graph
G
in
\mathcal{G}
, every induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
satisfies
\alpha\text{-}\rho(G^{\prime})\leq k
.

In order to show that
\mathcal{G}
has clique-bounded
\rho
, we show that for every graph
G
in the class
\mathcal{G}
, every induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
satisfies
\rho(G^{\prime})\leq f(\omega(G^{\prime}))
, where
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is the function defined by

f(p)=R(p+1,k+1)-1\,,

for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that the standard proof of Ramsey’s theorem is based on the inequality
R(k,\ell)\leq R(k-1,\ell)+R(k,\ell-1)
, for all
k
,
\ell\geq 2
, which implies that
R(k,\ell)\leq\binom{k+\ell-2}{k-1}
, for all positive integers
k
and
\ell
.
For fixed
k
, this binomial coefficient is a polynomial in
\ell
.

Let
G
be a graph in
\mathcal{G}
and let
G^{\prime}
be an induced subgraph of
G
.
Let
p
be the clique number of
G^{\prime}
.
Observe that, for any subset
S
of
V(G^{\prime})
such that
\alpha(G^{\prime}[S])\leq k
, we have

|S|\leq R(p+1,k+1)-1=f(p)\,,

where the inequality follows from Ramsey’s theorem, using the fact that
\omega(G^{\prime}[S])\leq\omega(G^{\prime})=p
.

Consider first the case when
\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{minmax}
.
Then

\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}}\max_{X\in H}\alpha(G^{\prime}[X])=\alpha\text{-}\rho(G^{\prime})\leq k\,,

that is, there exists a hypergraph
H^{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}
such that
\alpha(G^{\prime}[X])\leq k
for all
X\in H^{*}
.
Hence,
|X|\leq f(p)
for all
X\in H^{*}
, and consequently

\rho(G^{\prime})=\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}}\max_{X\in H}|X|\leq\max_{X\in H^{*}}|X|\leq f(p)=f(\omega(G^{\prime}))\,.

Suppose now that
\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{maxmin}
.
Then

\max_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}}\min_{X\in H}\alpha(G^{\prime}[X])=\alpha\text{-}\rho(G^{\prime})\leq k\,,

that is, for every hypergraph
H\in\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}
there exists some
X_{H}\in H
such that
\alpha(G^{\prime}[X_{H}])\leq k
, implying that
\min_{X\in H}|X|\leq|X_{H}|\leq f(p)
.
Consequently,

\rho(G^{\prime})=\max_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}}\min_{X\in H}|X|\leq f(p)=f(\omega(G^{\prime}))\,.\qed

3.5
Inverting Ramsey

Recall that, for a hyperparameter
\rho
, we denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
the class properties of having bounded
\rho
and clique-bounded
\rho
, respectively.
Furthermore, for the case when
\rho
is a basic hyperparameter,
Proposition

3.4
applies, which can be expressed in terms of the inclusion

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\textrm{-}\rho}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\rho}

(1)

between the corresponding class properties.
This motivates the following definition.
A basic hyperparameter
\rho
is said to be
awesome
(or, better yet,
\alpha\omega\text{esome}

)
if

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\textrm{-}\rho}=\mathcal{C}_{\rho}\,,

that is, clique-boundedness of
\rho
is equivalent to boundedness of its independence variant.
We say that
\rho
is
awful
if it is not awesome.
By
Eq.

1
, a basic hyperparameter is awesome if and only if clique-boundedness of
\rho
implies boundedness of its independence variant, that is,
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\textrm{-}\rho}
.

While these definitions can be extended to graph parameters listed in
Section

3.3
by applying them to their canonical hyperparameterisations, in general one needs to be careful when applying them to graph parameters: if the choice of a hyperparameterisation is not obvious, different hyperparameterisations of the same graph parameter may have different
\alpha
-variants.
For example, in
Section

3.3
we presented one particular hyperparameterisation of the chromatic number, but undoubtedly many others are possible.

Question 3.5
.

Which basic hyperparameters are awesome?

It turns out that this question is not always trivial.
As shown by
Proposition

3.4
, a necessary condition for a hyperparameter
\rho
to be awesome is that for any hereditary graph class, clique-boundedness of
\rho
is equivalent to polynomial clique-boundedness.
For the chromatic number, this was conjectured by Esperet
[
33
]
, and disproved only recently by Briański, Davies, and Walczak
[
13
]
.
Thus, by
Proposition

3.4
, chromatic number is awful.
Note that this argument rules out awesomeness not only of the canonical hyperparameterisation of the chromatic number given in
Section

3.3
, but of
any
hyperparameterisation of the chromatic number.
Furthermore, motivated by algorithmic considerations, Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel conjectured in
[
31
]
that treewidth is awesome.
This conjecture was recently disproved by Chudnovsky and Trotignon (see
[
23
]
); indeed, they constructed both graph classes with clique-bounded treewidth that do not have polynomially clique-bounded treewidth, as well as graph classes with polynomially clique-bounded treewidth that have unbounded
\alpha
-treewidth.

It is a natural question whether more restricted parameters such as pathwidth, treedepth, and vertex cover number are awesome.
For pathwidth, the fact that for any hereditary graph class, clique-boundedness of pathwidth is equivalent to polynomial clique-boundedness was shown recently by Hajebi
[

]
; however, this result does not say anything about whether pathwidth is awesome.
Indeed, we show in this paper that both pathwidth and treedepth are awful (

4.1
Increasing
\alpha
-pathwidth by 1

The transformation is based on the following graph. The
subdivided claw
or
s-claw
is the graph obtained from subdividing once each edge of the claw.
Given a graph
G
, the
s-claw substitution
of
G
is the graph denoted by
s(G)
and defined as the graph obtained by substituting a copy of
G
into each of the three leaves (vertices of degree
1
) of the subdivided claw.
More formally,
s(G)
is the graph obtained from the disjoint union of three copies of
G
, say
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
, by adding three new vertices
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
and
v_{3}
, making each vertex
v_{i}
adjacent to all vertices in
G_{i}
, and adding a fourth new vertex
w
adjacent precisely to
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
and
v_{3}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Every graph
G
satisfies
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(s(G))=\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)+1
.

Proof.

Let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
,
G_{3}
,
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
,
v_{3}
, and
w
be as in the definition of the s-claw substitution of
G
.

We first show that
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(s(G))\leq\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)+1
.
For each copy
G_{i}
of
G
, fix a path decomposition
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
with minimum independence number.
Adding
v_{i}
and
w
to each bag of
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
results in a path decomposition
(P^{i},\beta_{i})
of the subgraph of
s(G)
induced by
V(G_{i})\cup\{v_{i},w\}
.
These three path decompositions can be combined into a single path decomposition
\mathcal{P}
of the s-claw substitution of
G
in the natural way: take a new path
P
with vertex set
V(P^{1})\cup V(P^{2})\cup V(P^{3})
(disjoint union) that contains each the three paths
P^{i}
as a subpath and assign to each node
x\in V(P)
the bag
\beta_{i}(x)
where
i\in\{1,2,3\}
is such that
x
belongs to the path
P^{i}
.
Since
G_{i}
is isomorphic to
G
, the independence number of
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
is
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)
.
Adding two adjacent vertices to each bag can increase the independence number of each bag by at most one.
It follows that the independence number of
\mathcal{P}
is at most
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)+1
.
Thus,
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(s(G))\leq\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)+1
, as claimed.

Next, we show that
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(s(G))\geq\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)+1
.
Suppose for a contradiction that
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(s(G))\leq\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)
.
Let us refer to
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
, and
G_{3}
as the red, green, and blue copies of
G
and to their vertices as the red, green, and blue vertices, respectively.
Let
\mathcal{P}=(P,\beta)
be a path decomposition of
s(G)
with minimum independence number.
Consider one of the copies of
G
, say the red one.
Intersecting each bag of
\cal P
with the set of red vertices yields a path decomposition
(P,\beta_{1})
of the red copy of
G
.
Let us call a
big red node
of
P
an arbitrary but fixed node
x
of
P
that maximises the independence number of the subgraph of the red copy of
G
induced by the bag
\beta_{1}(x)
.
This independence number is referred to as the
red independence number
.
Denoting it by
r
, we have
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)=\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(G_{1})\leq r
and therefore, since we assumed that
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(s(G))\leq\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)
, it holds that
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(s(G))\leq r
.
We can similarly define a big green node
y
and a big blue node
z
of
P
, as well as the green and the blue independence numbers, denoted by
g
and
b
, respectively.
By the same arguments as above, we have
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(s(G))\leq g
and
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(s(G))\leq b
.

Assume that the big red and the big green nodes of
P
coincide.
Then the subgraph of
s(G)
induced by the corresponding bag of
\mathcal{P}
has independence number at least
r+g>r
, which is in contradiction with
\alpha(\mathcal{P})=\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{pw}(s(G))\leq r
.

5
Awesome graph parameters

In the previous section we identified a number of awful parameters.
Many other parameters of this type are waiting to be explored.
In order to show that the world of graph parameters is not so awful, in this section we turn to awesome parameters.

5.1
Order, independence number, maximum degree, and clique number

We show here, for the sake of completeness, that certain graph parameters are awesome, as a simple consequence of their definitions.
Recall that we identify the order, as a graph parameter, with any of its trivial basic hyperparameterisations,
\mathrm{opt}\in\{\mathrm{minmax},\mathrm{maxmin}\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{G}=\big\{\{V(G)\}\big\}
, for all graphs
G
.
Furthermore, we identify the independence number with its canonical hyperparameterisation given by
\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{minmax}
and
\mathcal{F}_{G}=\big\{\{X\colon X
is an independent set in
G\}\big\}
, for all graphs
G
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The order and the independence number are awesome graph parameters.

Proof.

Note that the independence variant of either the order or the independence number is the independence number.
Hence, it suffices to show that for every hereditary graph class that has clique-bounded order (resp., independence number), the independence number is bounded.
In fact, since clique-bounded order implies clique-bounded independence number, it suffices to show that for every hereditary graph class that has clique-bounded independence number, the independence number is bounded.
But this follows from the fact that if a hereditary graph class
\mathcal{G}
has unbounded independence number, then
\mathcal{G}
contains arbitrarily large edgeless graphs, and, hence, cannot have clique-bounded independence number.
∎

Recall that we identify the maximum degree graph parameter with its canonical hyperparameterisation given by
\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{minmax}
and
\mathcal{F}_{G}=\big\{\{N_{G}(v)\colon v\in V(G)\}\big\}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The maximum degree is an awesome graph parameter.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for every hereditary graph class that has clique-bounded maximum degree, the independence variant of the maximum degree is also bounded.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary graph class that has
\omega
-bounded
\Delta
, and let
f
be a nondecreasing function such that
\Delta(G)\leq f(\omega(G))
for all
G\in\mathcal{G}
.
We claim that
\mathcal{G}
has
\alpha\text{-}\Delta
bounded by
f(2)
, that is, that
\alpha\text{-}\Delta(G)\leq f(2)
for all
G\in\mathcal{G}
.
Let
G
be an arbitrary graph in
\mathcal{G}
.
The inequality
\alpha\text{-}\Delta(G)\leq f(2)
is equivalent to the statement that for all vertices
v\in V(G)
, we have
\alpha(G[N(v)])\leq f(2)
, or, equivalently, for all vertices
v\in V(G)
and all independent sets

in the subgraph of
G
induced by
N(v)
, we have
|I|\leq f(2)
.
Fix a vertex
v\in V(G)
and an independent set
I
in the subgraph of
G
induced by
N(v)
.
Note that the subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
induced by
\{v\}\cup I
is isomorphic to the star graph

, where
p=|I|
.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is hereditary, the graph
G^{\prime}
belongs to
\mathcal{G}
and consequently
\Delta(G^{\prime})\leq f(\omega(G^{\prime}))\leq f(2)
, where the right inequality follows from the facts that
\omega(G^{\prime})\leq 2
and

is nondecreasing.
Since
\Delta(G^{\prime})=p
, this shows that

and completes the proof.
∎

Another simple reason for a graph parameter to be awesome is that the independence variant of the parameter is bounded for all graphs.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\rho=(\mathtt{card},\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})
be a basic hyperparameter such that there exists an integer

such that one of the following holds:

•

\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{minmax}
and for every graph

\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{maxmin}

Proof.

The assumptions on
\rho
imply that
\alpha\text{-}\rho(G)\leq k
for every graph

\alpha\text{-}\rho

Hence, every graph class has bounded
\alpha\text{-}\rho
and, consequently, by

3.4
, every graph class has clique-bounded

Thus, in this case, clique-boundedness of

∎

.

Let
\rho=(\mathtt{card},\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})

Modulators

For a graph parameter
\rho

We denote by

-modulator number
of

-modulator in

For example, if

In particular,

-modulators are precisely the vertex covers of

(\mathrm{tw},2)
-modulators are precisely the feedback vertex sets.

5

5

Recall that our definition of treewidth disregards the usual “
-1

As another example, if

Note that for every graph parameter

5.2.1
Heaviness

In this section, we identify a sufficient condition for a

The main ingredient for the proof is

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\rho
be a graph parameter such that there exists a nondecreasing function
h
such that
\omega(G)\leq h(\rho(G))
holds for all graphs
G
.
Let

be a nondecreasing function,

be an integer, and let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary graph class such that there exists a nondecreasing function

\mu_{\rho,c}(G)\leq f(\omega(G))\cdot g(|V(G)|)

, every minimum
(\rho,c)
-modulator

Proof.

Let

and let

(\rho,c)
-modulator in

.
Suppose for a contradiction that
\alpha(G[S])>f(h(c)+1)\cdot g(|V(G)|)
.
Then,

Let

be the subgraph of

Since
\rho(G-S)\leq c

.
Consequently,
\omega(G^{\prime})\leq\omega(G-S)+\omega(G[I])\leq h(c)+1
.
Since the class
\mathcal{G}
is hereditary, the graph
G^{\prime}
belongs to
\mathcal{G}
.
It follows that
\mu_{\rho,c}(G^{\prime})\leq f(\omega(G^{\prime}))\cdot g(|V(G^{\prime})|)\leq f(h(c)+1)\cdot g(|V(G)|)
, where the last inequality follows from the inequality
\omega(G^{\prime})\leq h(c)+1
and the fact that both

Let

be a
(\rho,c)

Note that

Since

is a
(\rho,c)
-modulator in

, the graph

.
It follows that the set

-modulator in

Its cardinality satisfies

contradicting the fact that

is a minimum
(\rho,c)
-modulator in

.
∎

(\rho,c)
-modulator

.

5.2.2
Inheritability and strong inheritability

We now introduce a condition on basic hyperparameters, which we call inheritability, that allows for a transfer of the class properties from the modulators of a graph parameter to the parameter itself, as we will show in the next subsection.
A basic hyperparameter
\rho
is said to be
inheritable
if for each
\lambda\in\{\mathtt{card},\alpha\}
, every graph
G
, and every set
S\subseteq V(G)
, it holds that
\lambda\text{-}\rho(G)\leq\rho(G-S)+\lambda(G,S)
.

The following structural condition is sufficient for inheritability.
A basic hyperparameter
\rho=(\mathtt{card},\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})
is said to be
strongly inheritable
if one of the following conditions holds:

•

\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{minmax}
and for every graph
G
, every set
S\subseteq V(G)
, and every hypergraph
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{G-S}
, there exists a hypergraph
H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}
such that
\{X\setminus S\colon X\in H\}\subseteq H^{\prime}
, that is,
X\setminus S\in H^{\prime}
for all
X\in H
;

•

\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{maxmin}
and for every graph
G
, every set
S\subseteq V(G)
, and every hypergraph
H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}
, there exists a hypergraph
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{G-S}
such that
H^{\prime}\subseteq\{Y\setminus S\colon Y\in H\}
, that is, for all
X\in H^{\prime}
there exists some
Y\in H
such that
X=Y\setminus S
.

Modulators, for instance, give rise to strongly inheritable hyperparameters.

Proposition 5.10
.

For every graph parameter
\rho
and integer
c
, the
(\rho,c)
-modulator number
\mu_{\rho,c}
is strongly inheritable.

Proof.

Let
(\lambda,\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})
be the canonical hyperparameterisation of
\mu_{\rho,c}
given by
Remark

3.2
.
Then
\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{maxmin}
and
\mathcal{F}_{G}=\big\{\{X\subseteq V(G)\colon\rho(G-X)\leq c\}\big\}
for all graphs
G
.
Hence,
\mu_{\rho,c}
is strongly inheritable if and only if for every graph
G
, every set
S\subseteq V(G)
, and every set
X^{\prime}\subseteq V(G-S)
such that
\rho(G-S-X^{\prime})\leq c
, there exists a set
X\subseteq V(G)
such that
\rho(G-X)\leq c
and
X^{\prime}=X\setminus S
.
But this is clear, as we can take
X=X^{\prime}\cup S
.
∎

Several other graph parameters of interest are also strongly inheritable.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
\rho\in\{\omega,\chi,\mathrm{tw},\mathrm{pw},\mathrm{td}\}

Then,

is strongly inheritable.

Proof.

Note that all these parameters are

and

for all nodes

The argument for pathwidth is similar.
Treedepth is strongly inheritable since for every graph

∎

Following Adler

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
\rho
be a strongly inheritable hyperparameter.
Then, for every monotone, weakly submodular, and tame annotated graph parameter
\lambda
, every graph
G
, and every set
S\subseteq V(G)
, it holds that
\lambda\text{-}\rho(G)\leq\rho(G-S)+\lambda(G,S)
.
In particular,
\rho
is inheritable.

Proof.

Let
\rho=(\mathtt{card},\mathrm{opt},\mathcal{F})
be a strongly inheritable hyperparameter and let
\lambda
,
G
, and
S
be as in the lemma.
Let
c=\rho(G-S)
.

Consider first the case when
\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{minmax}
.
Then

c=\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G-S}}\max_{X\in H}|X|\,,

in particular, there exists a hypergraph
H^{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{G-S}
such that
\max_{X\in H^{*}}|X|=c
.
Since
\rho
is strongly inheritable, there exists a hypergraph
\widehat{H}\in\mathcal{F}_{G}
such that
\{X\setminus S\colon X\in\widehat{H}\}\subseteq H^{*}
, that is,
X\setminus S\in H^{*}
for all
X\in\widehat{H}
.
This implies that

\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}}\max_{X\in H}|X\setminus S|\leq\max_{X\in\widehat{H}}|X\setminus S|\leq\max_{X\in H^{*}}|X|=c\,.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\lambda\text{-}\rho(G)

\displaystyle=\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}}\max_{X\in H}\lambda(G,X)

\displaystyle\leq\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}}\max_{X\in H}\Big(\lambda(G,X\cap S)+\lambda(G,X\setminus S)\Big)

(since
\lambda
is weakly submodular)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(G,S)+\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}}\max_{X\in H}|X\setminus S|

(by monotonicity and
5.12
)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(G,S)+c\,,

\displaystyle=\lambda(G,S)+\rho(G-S)\,,

yielding the desired inequality.

Suppose now that
\mathrm{opt}=\mathrm{maxmin}
.
Then

c=\rho(G-S)=\max_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G-S}}\min_{X\in H}|X|\,,

in particular, for all hypergraphs
H\in\mathcal{F}_{G-S}
it holds that
\min_{X\in H}|X|\leq c
.
Consider an arbitrary hypergraph
H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}
.
Since
\rho
is strongly inheritable, there exists a hypergraph
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{G-S}
such that
H^{\prime}\subseteq\{X\setminus S\colon X\in H\}
.
Let
X_{H^{\prime}}
be a hyperedge in
H^{\prime}
such that
|X_{H^{\prime}}|\leq c
.
Then, there exists a hyperedge
X_{H}\in H
such that
X_{H^{\prime}}=X_{H}\setminus S
, and, hence,

\min_{X\in H}|X\setminus S|\leq|X_{H}\setminus S|=|X_{H^{\prime}}|\leq c\,.

Since
H
is arbitrary, this implies that

\max_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}}\min_{X\in H}|X\setminus S|\leq c\,.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\lambda\text{-}\rho(G)

\displaystyle=\max_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}}\min_{X\in H}\lambda(G,X)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}}\min_{X\in H}\Big(\lambda(G,X\cap S)+\lambda(G,X\setminus S)\Big)

(since
\lambda
is weakly submodular)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(G,S)+\max_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}}\min_{X\in H}|X\setminus S|

(by monotonicity and
5.12
)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(G,S)+c

\displaystyle=\lambda(G,S)+\rho(G-S)\,

as claimed.

Hence, in both cases,
\lambda\text{-}\rho(G)\leq\lambda(G,S)+\rho(G-S)
.
Since each
\lambda\in\{\mathtt{card},\alpha\}
is monotone, weakly submodular, and tame, it follows that
\rho
is inheritable.
∎

is inheritable,
Theorem

5.17
implies that
{\bigcup_{c}\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\text{-}\mu_{\rho,c}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\text{-}\rho}}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be the class of disjoint unions of complete graphs.
By assumption,
\mathcal{G}
satisfies the class property
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\text{-}\rho}
.
So it suffices to show that
\mathcal{G}\not\in\bigcup_{c}\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\text{-}\mu_{\rho,c}}
, that is, that for every integer
c

\mathcal{G}
has unbounded
\alpha\text{-}\mu_{\rho,c}

This means that for every integer
c
and every positive integer

there exists a graph

such that
\alpha\text{-}\mu_{\rho,c}(G)\geq k
.
Since
\rho
is heavy, it is unbounded on the class of complete graphs; hence, there exists a complete graph
K
such that
\rho(K)>c
.
Let
G
be the disjoint union of
k

.
Consider an arbitrary
(\rho,c)
-modulator

of
G
.
Then, for every component

of
G

contains at least one vertex from

is monotone under induced subgraphs would imply
\rho(G-S)\geq\rho(C)>c
, a contradiction with the choice of

It follows that
\alpha(G[S])\geq k

Since

\alpha\text{-}\mu_{\rho,c}(G)\geq k

∎

Examples of parameters satisfying the conditions of

include clique number, treewidth, pathwidth, and treedepth.

Proposition 5.21
.

Let
\rho
be an inheritable and heavy hyperparameter such that
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}=\bigcup_{c}\mathcal{C}_{\mu_{\rho,c}}
.
Then,

is awesome.

Proof.

Since

5.7
, the
(\rho,c)
-modulator number

is awesome for every integer

, that is,
\mathcal{C}_{\mu_{\rho,c}}=\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\text{-}\mu_{\rho,c}}

Consequently,
\bigcup_{c}\mathcal{C}_{\mu_{\rho,c}}=\bigcup_{c}\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\text{-}\mu_{\rho,c}}

Furthermore, since

5.17
implies that
\bigcup_{c}\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\text{-}\mu_{\rho,c}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\text{-}\rho}

It follows that

\mathcal{C}_{\rho}=\bigcup_{c}\mathcal{C}_{\mu_{\rho,c}}=\bigcup_{c}\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\text{-}\mu_{\rho,c}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\text{-}\rho}\,,

∎

Observe that by

, for every inheritable and heavy hyperparameter

.

Let

5.3
Some applications of awesomeness

We now derive some consequences of
Corollary

5.8
, giving sufficient conditions for awesomeness.

5.3.1
Modulators to treewidth

We start with the case when
\mu
is a modulator to bounded treewidth, that is,
\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{tw},c}
for some
c\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In this case, we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.23
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\mu
be the
(\mathrm{tw},c)
-modulator number, and let
\mathcal{G}
be a
(\mu,\omega)
-bounded graph class.
Then
\mathcal{G}
has bounded
\alpha
-treewidth.

Proof.

Corollary

5.8
implies that
\mathcal{G}
has bounded
\alpha
-
\mu
, that is, there exists an integer
k
such that for each graph
G\in\mathcal{G}
and each induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
, there exists a set
S\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
such that
\alpha(G^{\prime}[S])\leq k
and
\mathrm{tw}(G^{\prime}-S)\leq c
.
We can apply
Lemma

5.13
with
\rho=\mathrm{tw}
, since
\mathrm{tw}
is strongly inheritable by
Proposition

5.11
, and
\lambda=\alpha
.
This implies that
\alpha
-
\mathrm{tw}(G)\leq\mathrm{tw}(G-S)+\alpha(G[S])\leq c+k
, and thus shows that
\mathcal{G}
has bounded
\alpha
-treewidth, as claimed.
∎

Theorem

5.23
implies that any
(\mu_{\mathrm{tw},c},\omega)
-bounded graph class enjoys the good algorithmic properties of graph classes with bounded
\alpha
-treewidth.
In particular, Lima et al.
[
52
]
showed that for any such graph class
\mathcal{G}
, the following problems are solvable in polynomial time when restricted to graphs
G\in\mathcal{G}
:

•

For every
k,r
and a
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
formula
\psi
, given a graph
G
and a weight function on the vertices of
G
, find a maximum weight set
X\subseteq V(G)
such that
G[X]\models\psi
and
\omega(G[X])\leq r
, or conclude that no such set exists.

•

For a fixed positive even integer
d
, given a collection of connected subgraphs of
G
, each equipped with a real weight, find a maximum weight subcollection of the subgraphs any two of which are at distance at least
d
from each other.

Both of the above results capture the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
and the
Maximum Weight Induced Matching
problems (also studied for graphs with bounded
\alpha
-treewidth in
[
30
,
67
]
).
Furthermore, the
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
-based problem listed above encompasses many other problems, including
Graph Homomorphism
to a fixed target graph,
Maximum Induced Forest
(which is equivalent to
Minimum Feedback Vertex Set
),
Maximum Induced Bipartite Subgraph
(which is equivalent to
Minimum Odd Cycle Transversal
),
Maximum Induced Planar Subgraph
, and
Maximum Induced Cycle Packing
.

In particular,
Theorem

5.23
and the polynomial-time solvability of the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem for graph classes with bounded
\alpha
-treewidth provide a partial answer to the following question.

Question 5.24

(Dallard et al.
[
31
, Question 8.3]
, see also
[
29
, Question 9.8]
)
.

Is it true for every
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-bounded graph class
\mathcal{G}
, there exists a polynomial-time algorithm for the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem when restricted to graphs in
\mathcal{G}
?

Indeed, since treewidth is inheritable due to
Corollary

5.14
, we derive from
Theorem

5.17

(1)
that, for any integer
c
, any graph class with bounded
(\mathrm{tw},c)
-modulator number has bounded treewidth. Therefore,
the answer to
5.24
is affirmative if the treewidth is replaced by any
(\mathrm{tw},c)
-modulator number.
Formally, we have the following result, providing an infinite sequence of graph parameters whose clique-boundedness implies polynomial-time solvability of the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem.

Theorem 5.25
.

For every integer
c
and every
(\mu,\omega)
-bounded graph class
\mathcal{G}
, where
\mu
is the
(\mathrm{tw},c)
-modulator number, there exists a polynomial-time algorithm for the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem when restricted to graphs in
\mathcal{G}
.

Recall that for
c\in\{1,2\}
, the
(\mathrm{tw},c)
-modulator number coincides with the vertex cover number and the feedback vertex set number, respectively.
The result of
Theorem

5.25
for
c=1
is not new, as for every
(\mathrm{vc},\omega)
-bounded graph class
\mathcal{G}
there exists an integer
n
such that every graph in
\mathcal{G}
is
nK_{2}
-free (see, e.g.,
Theorem

5.28
), and this is known to imply polynomial-time solvability of the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem (see, e.g.,
[
55
]
).
On other hand, to the best of our knowledge, the result of
Theorem

5.25
is new for all
c\geq 2
.
In particular, for the case
c=2
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.26
.

For every
(\mathrm{fvs},\omega)
-bounded graph class
\mathcal{G}
, there exists a polynomial-time algorithm for the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem when restricted to graphs in
\mathcal{G}
.

We next complement the case
c=1
of
Theorem

5.23
with a characterization in terms of forbidden induced subgraphs.

5.3.2
Vertex cover number

The fact that the vertex cover number is awesome leads to a characterization of hereditary graph classes with bounded
\alpha
-vertex cover number.
To derive this result, we recall the following Ramsey-type result.

Theorem 5.27

(Curticapean and Marx
[
25
]
,
Lozin
[
56
]
)
.

For any three positive integers
p
,
q
, and
r
there exists an integer
s
such that any graph containing a matching of size
s
contains either
K_{p}
or
K_{q,q}
or
rK_{2}
as an induced subgraph.

Theorem 5.28
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary graph class.
Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

1.

\mathcal{G}
is
(\mathrm{vc},\omega)
-bounded.

2.

\mathcal{G}
has bounded
\alpha
-vertex cover number.

3.

There exists a positive integer
n
such that
\mathcal{G}
excludes the graphs
nK_{2}
and
K_{n,n}
.

Proof.

Corollary

5.9
implies the equivalence of the first two conditions.

Assume now that the first condition holds, that is,
\mathcal{G}
is
(\mathrm{vc},\omega)
-bounded, and let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a nondecreasing function such that for every graph
G
in the class
\mathcal{G}
, every induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
satisfies
\mathrm{vc}(G^{\prime})\leq f(\omega(G^{\prime}))
.
For every positive integer
n
, the graphs
nK_{2}
and
K_{n,n}
have clique number
2
, and vertex cover number
n
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{G}
excludes the graphs
nK_{2}
and
K_{n,n}
for all
n\geq f(2)+1
.
Hence, the third condition holds.

Finally, assume that the third condition holds, that is, that there exists a positive integer
n
such that
\mathcal{G}
excludes the graphs
nK_{2}
and
K_{n,n}
.
By
Theorem

5.27
, for any three integers
p
,
q
, and
r
, there exists a smallest integer
s(p,q,r)
such that every graph containing a matching of size
s(p,q,r)
contains either
K_{p}
or
K_{q,q}
or
rK_{2}
as an induced subgraph.
We show that
\mathcal{G}
is
(\mathrm{vc},\omega)
-bounded, more precisely, that every graph
G\in\mathcal{G}
has a vertex cover with cardinality at most
f(\omega(G))
where
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is the function defined by
f(k)=2(s(k+1,n,n)-1)
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
G
be a graph in
\mathcal{G}
and let
k
be the clique number of
G
.
Since
G
has no clique of size
k+1
nor an induced subgraph isomorphic to
K_{n,n}
or

,
Theorem

5.27
implies that
G
does not contain a matching of size
s(k+1,n,n)
.
Let
M
be a maximum matching in
G
and let
S
be the set of vertices of

saturated by

.
Then

is a vertex cover in

with cardinality at most

.
Therefore,
\mathrm{vc}(G)\leq f(k)
, as claimed.
∎

Since every bounded
\alpha
-vertex cover number implies bounded

-treewidth (see
Corollary

),
Theorem

provides a strengthening of the fact that, for any positive integer

, the class of

-free graphs has bounded

-treewidth.
The latter is a consequence of a more general result due to Abrishami et al.
[

, Theorem 1.1]
.

5.3.3
Odd cycle transversal number

We next explain an algorithmic implication of
Corollary

5.9
(and its proof) in relation with
Minimum Odd Cycle Transversal
, the problem of computing a minimum odd cycle transversal in a given graph
G
.
In
Theorem

5.30
, we generalize the fact that
Maximum Weight Independent Set
is solvable in polynomial time in any graph class with bounded odd cycle transversal number (as can be easily seen; see, e.g., the concluding remarks of
[
60
]
) or, more generally, in any class of graphs whose vertex set admits a partition into a bounded number of cliques and two independent sets, as shown by Alekseev and Lozin (see
[
5
]
).

Lemma 5.29
.

For every positive integer
k
, there exists an algorithm that, given a graph
G
that admits an odd cycle transversal
S
with
\alpha(G[S])\leq k
, computes such a set
S
in polynomial time.

Proof.

Let
S
be an odd cycle transversal in
G
with independence number at most
k
. Then
G
admits a partition of its vertex set into two parts
S
and
V(G)\setminus S
such that
G[S]
has independence number at most
k
and
G-S
is bipartite.
It follows from
[
5
, Lemma 8]
and its proof that, given a graph
G
satisfying this property, a witnessing vertex partition can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(|V(G)|^{6k+2})
.
∎

Theorem 5.30
.

For every
(\mathrm{oct},\omega)
-bounded graph class
\mathcal{G}
, there exists a polynomial-time algorithm for the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem when restricted to graphs in
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Fix
\mathcal{G}
as in the theorem and let
G=(V,E)
be a graph in
\mathcal{G}
given together with some vertex weight function.
We describe a polynomial-time algorithm for computing a maximum weight independent set in
G
.
By
Corollary

5.9
, there exists a constant
k=k_{\mathcal{G}}
independent of
G
such that
G
admits an odd cycle transversal
S
with
\alpha(G[S])\leq k
.
Furthermore, by
Lemma

5.29
, such an odd cycle transversal
S
can be computed in polynomial time.
For each independent set
I\subseteq S
, we compute a maximum weight independent set
I^{\prime}
in the bipartite graph
G[V\setminus S]-N(I)
.
Finally, among all such sets
I
, we return the set
I\cup I^{\prime}
with maximum weight.

For each of the
\mathcal{O}(|V(G)|^{k})
choices of
I
, the algorithm solves an instance of the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem in a bipartite graph, which can be reduced in polynomial time to solving an instance of the maximum flow problem (see
[
46
]
).
Hence, the algorithm runs in polynomial time.

To see that the set returned by the algorithm is a maximum weight independent set in
G
, note that for any maximum weight independent set
J
in
G
, writing
J=I\cup I^{\prime}
where
I=J\cap S
and
I^{\prime}=J\setminus S
, the set
I^{\prime}
is a maximum weight independent set in the bipartite graph
G[V\setminus S]-N(I)
.
Hence, a set of the same weight as
J
will be detected by the algorithm in the iteration considering the independent set
I=J\cap S
.
∎

Since the feedback vertex set number of a graph is an upper bound on its odd cycle transversal number,
Theorem

5.30
generalizes
Corollary

5.26
.
Furthermore,
Theorem

5.30
applies in a context beyond
5.24
, since it provides a graph parameter
\mu
that is incomparable with treewidth and such that clique-bounded
\mu
implies polynomial-time solvability of the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem.

5.3.4
Tree-independence number

We conclude this section by applying
Lemma

5.5
to establish logarithmic tree-independence number in certain classes of graphs.
Two vertex-disjoint cycles
C
and
C^{\prime}
in a graph
G
are said to be
independent
if there is no edge between
C
and
C^{\prime}
.
A graph is said to be
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-free
if it does not contain
k
pairwise independent induced cycles, or equivalently, if it does not contain
k
pairwise independent cycles.
Bonamy et al.
[
12
]
showed that for fixed
k
and
\ell
, every
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-free graph not containing
K_{\ell,\ell}
as a subgraph has logarithmic treewidth.
The following proposition provides a dense analogue of this result and provides an affirmative answer to
[
45
, Question 7.3]
.

Proposition 5.31
.

For every two positive integers
k
and
t
there exists a positive integer
c_{k,t}
such that if
G
is a
K_{t,t}
-free
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-free graph with at least two vertices, then
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{tw}(G)\leq c_{k,t}\cdot\log|V(G)|+1
.

Proof.

Recall that for a graph
G
, a feedback vertex set in
G
is a set
S\subseteq V(G)
such that
G-S
is acyclic, and the feedback vertex set number of
G
, denoted by
\mathrm{fvs}(G)
, is the minimum cardinality of a feedback vertex set in
G
.
As shown by Bonamy et al.
[
12
]
, for every positive integer
k
there exists a nondecreasing function
b_{k}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that for every positive integer
\ell
, every
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-free graph
G
with at least two vertices not containing
K_{\ell,\ell}
as a subgraph satisfies
\mathrm{fvs}(G)\leq b_{k}(\ell)\cdot\log|V(G)|
.
Fix positive integers
k
and
t
, and let
\mathcal{G}
be the class of
K_{t,t}
-free
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-free graphs.
Since every graph in
\mathcal{G}
is
K_{t,t}
-free, Ramsey’s theorem guarantees the existence of a nondecreasing function
g_{t}
such that if
G\in\mathcal{G}
, then
G
does not contain
K_{\ell,\ell}
as a subgraph where
\ell=g_{t}(\omega(G))
.
Combining this with the aforementioned result of Bonamy et al., we obtain that if
G\in\mathcal{G}
has at least two vertices, then
\mathrm{fvs}(G)\leq b_{k}(g_{t}(\omega(G)))\cdot\log|V(G)|
.
Hence, taking
g(x)=\log(x)
for
x\geq 2
and
g(1)=1
,
\rho=\mathrm{tw}
,
c=2
,
h(x)=x
, and letting
f
be the composition of
b_{k}
and
g_{t}
, we obtain that every graph
G\in\mathcal{G}
satisfies
\mu_{\rho,c}(G)\leq f(\omega(G))\cdot g(|V(G)|)
.
Hence, by
Lemma

5.5
, for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G}
, every minimum feedback vertex set
S
in
G
satisfies
\alpha(G[S])\leq f(3)\cdot g(|V(G)|)
.
The graph
G-S
is a forest, and hence, admits a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}
in which every bag is a clique; adding
S
to every bag of
\mathcal{T}
implies that
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{tw}(G)\leq f(3)\cdot g(|V(G)|)+1
.
Recalling the definition of
g
and since
\alpha
-treewidth of a single-vertex graph is 1, we can conclude that
\alpha\textnormal{-}\mathrm{tw}(G)\leq f(3)\cdot\log|V(G)|+1=b_{k}(g_{t}(3))\cdot\log|V(G)|+1
. Taking
c_{k,t}=b_{k}(g_{t}(3))
proves the claim.
∎

Proposition

5.31
implies that for any two positive integers
k
and
t
, the
Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem can be solved in quasipolynomial time when restricted to
K_{t,t}
-free
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-free graphs.
Moreover, following similar arguments as in
[
20
,
19
]
, the same is true for many other natural algorithmic problems that are
NP
-hard in general.

6
Discussion and open problems

Recall that Chudnovsky and Trotignon (see
[
23
]
) constructed graph classes with clique-bounded treewidth and unbounded
\alpha
-treewidth, thus disproving a conjecture of Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel
[
31
]
that treewidth is awesome.
However, their construction says nothing about the following two weaker statements.

Conjecture 6.1
.

Every
(\mathrm{pw},\omega)
-bounded graph class has bounded
\alpha
-treewidth.

Conjecture 6.2
.

Every
(\mathrm{td},\omega)
-bounded graph class has bounded
\alpha
-treewidth.

As discussed in
[
28
]
, it is an open question whether every hereditary graph class defined by
finitely
many forbidden induced subgraphs that is
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-bounded has bounded
\alpha
-treewidth.
A particular open case is the following conjecture by Dallard et al.
[
28
]
.

Conjecture 6.3
.

For every positive integer
n
, the class of
\{P_{n},K_{n,n}\}
-free graphs has bounded
\alpha
-treewidth.

Let us remark that the analogous questions for hereditary graph classes defined by finitely many forbidden induced subgraphs obtained by replacing treewidth with pathwidth or treedepth are not open.
Indeed, our construction of
(\mathrm{td},\omega)
-bounded graph classes with unbounded
\alpha
-pathwidth given in
Section

4.2
results in
\{P_{6},K_{2,2}\}
-free graphs.
This also shows that the conclusion of
6.3
cannot be strengthened to bounded
\alpha
-pathwidth.

We now show that
6.2
and
6.3
are in fact equivalent.
The proof is based on the following result due to Galvin, Rival, and Sands
[
37
]
(independently rediscovered by Atminas, Lozin, and Razgon in
[
6
]
).

Theorem 6.4
.

For any two positive integers
q
and
r
there exists an integer
s
such that any graph containing a path of order
s
contains either a path
P_{q}
as an induced subgraph or a complete bipartite graph
K_{r,r}
as a subgraph.

Combined with Ramsey’s theorem,
Theorem

6.4
implies the following (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
).

Theorem 6.5
.

For any three positive integers
p
,
q
, and
r
there exists an integer
s
such that any graph containing a path of order
s
contains either
K_{p}
or
K_{q,q}
or
P_{r}
as an induced subgraph.

Proposition 6.6
.

6.2
and
6.3
are equivalent.

Proof.

It suffices to show that a hereditary graph class is
(\mathrm{td},\omega)
-bounded if and only if it excludes some path and some complete bipartite graph.

Assume first that
\mathcal{G}
is a hereditary
(\mathrm{td},\omega)
-bounded graph class.
Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a nondecreasing function such that
\mathrm{td}(G)\leq f(\omega(G))
for all
G\in\mathcal{G}
.
Let
P_{n}
be an
n
-vertex path that belongs to
\mathcal{G}
.
Then
\mathrm{td}(P_{n})\leq f(2)
since
\omega(P_{n})\leq 2
and
f
is nondecreasing.
However,
\mathrm{td}(P_{n})=\lceil\log_{2}(n+1)\rceil
(see, e.g.,
[
59
]
) and therefore
n\leq 2^{f(2)}-1
.
Hence
\mathcal{G}
excludes the path of order
2^{f(2)}
.
The argument for complete bipartite graphs
K_{n,n}
is similar, using the fact that they have bounded clique number but unbounded treedepth (note that
\mathrm{td}(K_{n,n})\geq\mathrm{td}(P_{2n})
, since
P_{2n}
is a subgraph of
K_{n,n}
).

Assume now that
\mathcal{G}
is a hereditary graph class that excludes some path and some complete bipartite graph.
Let
a
,
b
,
c
be nonnegative integers such that
P_{a}
and
K_{b,c}
do not belong to
\mathcal{G}
, and let
n=\max\{a,b,c\}
.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is hereditary, the path
P_{n}
and the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
do not belong to
\mathcal{G}
.
By
Theorem

6.5
, for any three integers
p
,
q
, and
r
, there exists a smallest integer
s(p,q,r)
such that every graph containing a path of order
s(p,q,r)
contains either
K_{p}
or
K_{q,q}
or
P_{r}
as an induced subgraph.
To argue that
\mathcal{G}
is
(\mathrm{td},\omega)
-bounded, we show that
\mathrm{td}(G)\leq s(\omega(G)+1,n,n)
for all
G\in\mathcal{G}
.
Indeed, since
G
contains neither
P_{n}
, nor
K_{n,n}
, nor the complete graph on
\omega(G)+1
vertices as an induced subgraph, it does not contain a path of order
s(p+1,n,n)
.
But then, the treedepth of
G
is at most
s(\omega(G)+1,n,n)
(see, e.g.,
[
59
]
).
∎

Next, we prove several results providing partial support for
6.1
,
6.2
and
6.3
.
First, we generalize the well-known fact that every graph
G
satisfies
\mathrm{td}(G)\leq\mathrm{vc}(G)+1
, by proving a similar relation between their
\lambda
-variants, for any weakly submodular and tame annotated graph parameter
\lambda
(see p.
5.2.2
for the definitions).

Proposition 6.7
.

For every graph
G
and every weakly submodular and tame annotated graph parameter
\lambda
, it holds

\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{td}(G)\leq\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{vc}(G)+1.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph.
By the definition of
\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{vc}
, we have

\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{vc}(G)=\min_{X\in H_{\mathrm{vc}}(G)}\lambda(G,X)\,,

where
H_{\mathrm{vc}}(G)
is the hypergraph over
G
whose hyperedges are vertex covers of
G
.
Similarly,

\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{td}(G)=\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{minmax}(G,\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{td}})=\min_{H\in\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{td}}}\max_{X\in H}\lambda(G,X)\,,

where
\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{td}}
is the family of all hypergraphs over
G
whose hyperedges are vertex sets of root-to-leaf paths of some treedepth decomposition of
G
.
Let
C=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
be a vertex cover of
G
such that
\lambda(G,C)=\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{vc}(G)
.
Let
F
be the rooted forest with vertex set
V(G)
with directed edges
\{(v_{i},v_{i+1})\colon 1\leq i\leq k-1\}\cup\{(v_{k},v)\colon v\in V(G)\setminus C\}
.
Since
V(G)\setminus C
is an independent set in
G
, the graph
G
is a subgraph of the transitive closure of
F
.
Therefore, the hypergraph
H
over
G
whose hyperedges are precisely the sets
C\cup\{v\}
for all
v\in V(G)\setminus C
, belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\mathrm{td}}
.
Furthermore,

\max_{X\in H}\lambda(G,X)=\max_{v\in V(G)\setminus C}\lambda(G,C\cup\{v\})\leq\max_{v\in V(G)\setminus C}\left(\lambda(G,C)+\lambda(G,\{v\})\right)\leq\lambda(G,C)+1=\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{vc}(G)+1\,,

where the two inequalities follow from the facts that
\lambda
is weakly submodular and tame, respectively.
Consequently,
\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{td}(G)\leq\max_{X\in H}\lambda(G,X)\leq\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{vc}(G)+1
, as claimed.
∎

For each
\lambda\in\{\texttt{card},\alpha\}
, since
\lambda
is weakly submodular and tame,
Propositions

3.3
and
6.7
imply the following.

Corollary 6.8
.

Every graph
G
satisfies

\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{tw}(G)\leq\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{pw}(G)\leq\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{td}(G)\leq\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{vc}(G)+1\,.

Consequently,
Theorem

5.28
implies that every
(\mathrm{vc},\omega)
-bounded graph class satisfies
6.1
and
6.2
.
Indeed, such classes are
(\mathrm{pw},\omega)
-bounded,
(\mathrm{td},\omega)
-bounded, and have bounded
\alpha
-treewidth.
Moreover,
Theorem

5.28
implies that for each positive integer
n
, the class of
\{nK_{2},K_{n,n}\}
-free graphs has bounded
\alpha
-treewidth, in line with
6.3
.

Another special case of
6.3
is the case when, instead of excluding a complete bipartite graph, we exclude a star
K_{1,n}
.
As a matter of fact,
[
28
, Theorem 1.5]
states that the class of
\{P_{n},K_{1,n}\}
-free graphs has bounded
\alpha
-treewidth, and its proof can be easily adapted to show that the class has bounded
\alpha
-treedepth.
Thus, we get the following stronger result.

Theorem 6.9
.

For every positive integer
n
, the class of
\{P_{n},K_{1,n}\}
-free graphs has bounded
\alpha
-treedepth.

Note that bounded
\alpha
-treewidth can be obtained with a weaker assumption: instead of excluding a path, it suffices to exclude all sufficiently long cycles.
This follows from a result of Seymour
[
64
]
stating that for every integer
\ell\geq 4
, every graph containing no induced cycles of length at least
\ell
has a tree decomposition such that the subgraph induced by each bag has a dominating path with at most
\ell-3
vertices. (For further details, we refer to the paragraph following Theorem 1.5 in
[
28
]
.)

We conclude the paper with two further questions left open by this work.
In
Section

4
, we introduced a graph transformation that increases
\alpha
-pathwidth by exactly one, and the same for
\alpha
-treedepth.
We are not aware of any such transformation for
\alpha
-treewidth.

Question 6.10
.

Is there an efficiently computable graph transformation that increases
\alpha
-treewidth by a fixed constant?

Recall that by
Proposition

3.4
, any graph class
\mathcal{G}
that has bounded
\alpha\text{-}\rho
, where
\rho
is a basic hyperparameter, has clique-bounded
\rho
with a
polynomial
binding function.
This motivates the study of the following variant of awesomeness.
Let us say that a basic hyperparameter
\rho
is
weakly awesome
if polynomial clique-boundedness of
\rho
is equivalent to boundedness of
\alpha\text{-}\rho
.
Of course, every awesome basic hyperparameter is also weakly awesome.
But there may be more.

Question 6.11
.

Which basic hyperparameters are weakly awesome?

At the moment, we are in fact not aware of any weakly awesome basic hyperparameter that is not awesome: all the awful basic hyperparameters we are aware of (treewidth, pathwidth, treedepth, and chromatic number) turn out not to be weakly awesome, as we explain next.

In
[
23
]
, Chudnovsky and Trotignon constructed graph classes with polynomially clique-bounded treewidth but unbounded
\alpha
-treewidth.
This shows that treewidth is not weakly awesome.
Furthermore, our proof of
Theorem

4.5
, being based on a graph class in which the treedepth is bounded by a linear function of the clique number, shows that pathwidth and treedepth are not weakly awesome.

The argument based on
Proposition

3.4
and
[
13
]
(recall the discussion following
3.5
on p.
3.5
) ruling out awesomeness of any hyperparameterisation of the chromatic number does not say anything about weak awesomeness.
Nonetheless, weak awesomeness of the canonical hyperparameterisation of the chromatic number given in
Section

3.3
can be ruled out with the following much simpler argument.
The family of disjoint unions of complete graphs forms a
\chi
-bounded graph class
\mathcal{G}
that has unbounded
\alpha
-chromatic number, since for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every proper colouring of the graph
nK_{n}\in\mathcal{G}
, there exists an independent set of
n
vertices with pairwise different colours.

We leave for future research the study of various other hyperparameterisations of the chromatic number, including the question of whether the parameter admits a weakly awesome hyperparameterisation.

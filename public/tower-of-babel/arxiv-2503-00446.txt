On uniqueness of the equivariant smooth structure on a real moment-angle manifold Thanks: The work was carried out as a result of the research in the framework of the “Mirror Laboratories” project of the National Research University Higher School of Economics
Nikolai Erokhovets and Elena Erokhovets

Abstract
. The paper is devoted to the well-known problem of smooth structures on moment-angle manifolds. Each real or complex moment-angle manifold has an equivariant smooth structure given by an intersection of quadrics corresponding to a geometric realisation of a polytope. In 2006 F. Bosio and L. Meersseman proved that complex moment-angle manifolds of combinatorially equivalent simple polytopes are equivariantly diffeomorphic. Using arguments from calculus we derive from this result that real moment-angle manifolds of combinatorially equivalent simple polytopes are equivariantly diffeomorphic and the polytopes are diffeomorphic as manifolds with corners.

1.
Introduction

A moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{Z}_{P}
of a simple
n
-polytope
P
was introduced M.W. Davis and T. Januszkiewicz
in
[
DJ91
]
. If
P
has
m

\mathcal{Z}_{P}
is a topological
manifold with a canonical action of the torus

with
\mathcal{Z}_{P}/T^{m}=P
. Moreover, if the polytopes
P

and
Q
are combinatorially equivalent, then
\mathcal{Z}_{P}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{Q}
are equivariantly homeomorphic.
It is mentioned in
[
DJ91
, Section 6.4]
that
\mathcal{Z}_{P}
is a smooth manifold, but without details.
An explicit equivariant smooth structure was described by V.M. Buchstaber and T.E. Panov in
[
BP02
]
, where
the moment-angle manifold was identified with a certain intersection of real quadrics defined by a geometric realization of
simple polytopes (see
[
BP02
, Construction 3.1.8]
, and also
[
BPR07
, Section 3]
). In
[
BM06
, Corollary 4.7]

it was proved that for two geometric realizations of the same combinatorial simple
polytope the corresponding smooth moment-angle manifolds are equivariantly diffeomorphic. It is mentioned in

[
BP15
, Remark after Proposition 6.2.3]
that this can be also proved using the fact that combinatorially
equivalent simple polytopes are diffeomorphic as manifolds with corners (see also
[
D14
, Corollary 6.3]
). The
latter fact was proved by M. Wiemeler in
[
W13
, Corollary 5.3]
(see also
[
D14
, Corollary 1.3]
).

This article is devoted to the analogous question for the real moment-angle manifold
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P}
.
It then it can be identified with the set of real points of
\mathcal{Z}_{P}
in its realization by quadrics and
also is defined by quadrics. There is a natural action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P}
.
We give a survey of known results and give a new prove of the following fact.

Theorem 1.1
.

If the polytopes
P
and
Q
are combinatorially equivalent, then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P}
and
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{Q}
are equivariantly diffeomporphic.

The arguments from
[
BM06
]
can not be directly generalized to real moment-angle manifolds, since

[
BM06
, Lemma 3.4]
stating that any diffeomorphism of the standard sphere
S^{2k-1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{k}

equivariant with respect to the standard action of
\mathbb{T}^{k}
is equivariantly isotopic to identity is not valid for

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariant diffeomorphisms of
S^{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
. In
[
KMY18
, Section 3]
S. Kuroki, M. Masuda, L. Yu give a sketch of a proof that there is a unique smooth structure on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P}
such that the
canonical
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
action is smooth (as well as a unique smooth structure on a small cover such
that the canonical
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-action is smooth). It follows the approach of M. Wiemeler
[
W13
]

and uses the notions of
G
-normal and
B
-normal systems introduced and studied by M.W. Davis in
[
D78
,
D14
]
.

In our paper, starting from a
\mathbb{T}^{m}
-equivariant diffeomorphism
\mathcal{Z}_{P}\to\mathcal{Z}_{Q}

constructed in
[
BM06
]
we built a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
-equivariant diffeomorphism

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P}\to\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{Q}
and a diffeomorphism

of manifolds with corners
with natural linear smooth structures.
The latter uses the fact that the linear smooth structure on a simple polytope

arising from its embedding
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is diffeomorphic to the quadratic smooth structure arising from the real moment-angle manifold

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P}
.

2.
Moment-angle manifold of a simple polytope

In this section we give basic definitions on moment-angle manifolds.

A
convex polytope

P
is a
bounded
intersection of a finite set of half-spaces:

P=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\boldsymbol{x}+b_{i}\geqslant 0,i=1,\dots,m\},

where
\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
and
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.
For the
m\times n
-matrix
A
with rows
\boldsymbol{a}_{i}
its rank is equal to
n
, hence
the affine mapping
j_{P}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined as

j_{P}(\boldsymbol{x})=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}),\text{where $y_{i}=\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\boldsymbol{x}+b_{i}$},

is an embedding, and the image of the polytope
P
is

j_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\geqslant},\text{ where }\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\geqslant}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon y_{i}\geqslant 0\;\forall i\}.

Take an
((m-n)\times m)
-matrix
C
such that
CA=0
and
{\rm rank\,}C=m-n
.
Let
\boldsymbol{c}_{i}=(c_{1,i},\dots,c_{m-n,i})
. Then

j_{P}(P)=\{(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\geqslant}\colon c_{i,1}y_{1}+\dots+c_{i,m}y_{m}=c_{i},i=1,\dots,m-n\},

where
c_{i}=c_{i,1}b_{1}+\dots+c_{i,m}b_{m}
.

Example 2.1
.

For the standard simplex

\Delta^{n}=\{x_{i}\geqslant 0\text{ for }i=1,\dots,n,\quad\text{ and }-x_{1}-\dots-x_{n}+1\geqslant 0\}

we have
C=(1,\dots,1)
, and

j_{\Delta^{n}}(\Delta^{n})=\{(y_{1},\dots,y_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{\geqslant}\colon y_{1}+\dots+y_{n+1}=1\}

is a
regular simplex
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
moment-angle manifold
is the subset in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
defined as

\mathcal{Z}_{P}=\rho^{-1}\circ j_{P}(P),\text{where }\rho(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})=\big(|z_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|z_{m}|^{2}\big).

The action of
\mathbb{T}^{m}
on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
induces the action of
\mathbb{T}^{m}
on
\mathcal{Z}_{P}
.

A
real moment-angle manifold

is the set of real points in
\mathcal{Z}_{P}
, that is a subset in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined as

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P}=\rho^{-1}\circ j_{P}(P),\text{where }\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=\big(|x_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|x_{m}|^{2}\big).

The action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
by changing the signs of the coordinates induces the action of

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P}
.

For the embeddings
j_{\mathcal{Z}_{P}}\colon(\mathcal{Z}_{P},\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
j_{P}\colon P\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\geqslant}
we have the commutative diagram:

\begin{CD}(\mathcal{Z}_{P},\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P})@ >{j_{\mathcal{Z}_{P}}}>>(\mathbb{C}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m})\\
@ V{\rho_{P}}VV@ VV{\rho}V\\
(P,P)@>{j_{P}}>{}>(\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant}^{m},\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant}^{m})\end{CD}

Example 2.3
.

For the standard simplex
\Delta^{n}
the moment angle manifolds are spheres:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\Delta^{n}}=\{(z_{1},\dots,z_{n+1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\colon|z_{1}|^{2}+\dots+|z_{n+1}|^{2}=1\}\simeq S^{2n+1};

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{\Delta^{n}}=\{(u_{1},\dots,u_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\colon|u_{1}|^{2}+\dots+|u_{n+1}|^{2}=1\}\simeq S^{n}.

Let
z_{j}=u_{j}+iv_{j}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

(see
[
BP15
, Theorem 6.1.5.]
)

(1)

We have
\mathcal{Z}_{P}=\{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\colon c_{i,1}|z_{1}|^{2}+\dots+c_{i,m}|z_{m}|^{2}=c_{i},i=1,\dots,m-n\}
,

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P}=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon c_{i,1}|u_{1}|^{2}+\dots+c_{i,m}|u_{m}|^{2}=c_{i},i=1,\dots,m-n\}

(2)

Denote

the differentials

•

Example 2.5

(A smooth atlas on a moment-angle manifold)
.

We can explicitly describe a smooth atlas on the manifold
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P}
or

\mathcal{Z}_{P}

The set

The preimage

The preimage

For a given choice of signs

3.
Quadratic and linear smooth structures on the polytope as a manifold with corners

Definition 3.1

(A manifold with corners)
.

(see
[
BP15
, Definition 7.1.3]
,
[
J12
]
)

A manifold with corners
(of dimension
n
) is a topological
manifold
Q
with boundary together with an atlas
\{U_{i},\varphi_{i}\}
consisting of homeomorphisms

\varphi_{i}\colon U_{i}\to W_{i}
onto open subsets
W_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geqslant}

such that the mapping
\varphi_{i}\circ\varphi_{j}^{-1}\colon\varphi_{j}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})\to\varphi_{i}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})

is smooth for all
i,j
. (A mapping between open
subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geqslant}
is called smooth if can be obtained by restriction
of a smooth mapping of open subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.)

Example 3.2

(A quadratic smooth structure on a polytope)
.

The smooth atlases on
\mathcal{Z}_{P}
and
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{P}

induce a smooth atlas on
P
as a manifold with corners.
Its charts are
P_{v}
and coordinates in
P_{v}
are
u_{j}=\sqrt{\boldsymbol{a}_{j}\boldsymbol{x}+b_{j}}
,
j\in\omega_{v}
.
Then for two vertices
v
and
w
we have

u_{j_{s}}=\begin{cases}u_{j_{s}},&\text{ if }j_{s}\in\omega_{v};\\
\sqrt{\widetilde{a}^{v}_{j_{s},1}u_{i_{1}}^{2}+\dots+\widetilde{a}^{v}_{j_{s},n}u_{i_{n}}^{2}+\widetilde{b}^{v}_{j_{s}}},&\text{ if }j_{s}\notin\omega_{v}.\end{cases}

Example 3.3

(A linear smooth structure on a polytope)
.

On the other hand, there is natural smooth structure on
P
as a manifold with corners.
Its charts are again
P_{v}
, and coordinates in
P_{v}
are
y_{j}=\boldsymbol{a}_{j}\boldsymbol{x}+b_{j}
,
j\in\omega_{v}
.
Then for two vertices
v
and

y_{j_{s}}=\begin{cases}y_{j_{s}},&\text{ if }j_{s}\in\omega_{v};\\
\widetilde{a}^{v}_{j_{s},1}y_{i_{1}}+\dots+\widetilde{a}^{v}_{j_{s},n}y_{i_{n}}+\widetilde{b}^{v}_{j_{s}},&\text{ if }j_{s}\notin\omega_{v}.\end{cases}

Remark 3.4
.

It was proved by M. Wiemeler in
[
W13
]
(see also
[
D14
]
) that if two simple polytopes
P
and

Proposition 3.5
.

The polytope

with linear smooth structure is diffeomorphic to

with quadratic smooth structure.

We will give a sketch the proof suggested to the authors by A.A. Gaifullin.

Sketch of a proof of Proposition
3.5
.

Definition 3.6

(
[
G08
]
)
.

A
collar

of a facet
F_{i}
of a simple convex polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth embedding

\varphi\colon F_{i}\times[0,\delta)\to P^{n}
such that

(1)

\varphi\left.\right|_{F_{i}\times 0}={\rm id}_{F_{i}}
– the identity mapping;

(2)

the image of
\varphi
contains an open neighbourhood of
F_{i}
in
P
(in the topology induced from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
);

(3)

\varphi((G\cap F_{i})\times[0,\delta))\subset G
for any face
G
of
P
such that
G\not\subset F_{i}

but
G\cap F_{i}\neq\varnothing
.

For
0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta
the collar
\varphi
can be restricted to a collar
\varphi\left.\right|_{F_{i}\times[0,\delta^{\prime})}
.

For any face
G
such that
G\not\subset F_{i}
and
G\cap F_{i}\neq\varnothing

the embedding
\varphi_{G}=\varphi\left.\right|_{(G\cap F_{i})\times[0,\delta)}
is called
an
induced collar
.

For two different facets
F_{i}
and
F_{j}
with
F_{i}\cap F_{j}\neq\varnothing
the corresponding collars

\varphi_{i}
and
\varphi_{j}
are
consistent

if the diagram

\begin{CD}(F_{i}\cap F_{j})\times[0,\delta)\times[0,\delta)@>{(\varphi_{i})_{F_{j}}\times{\rm id}}>{}>F_{j}\times[0,\delta)@>{\varphi_{j}}>{}>P\\
@V{{\rm id}\times T}V{}V@V{\rm id}V{}V\\
(F_{i}\cap F_{j})\times[0,\delta)\times[0,\delta)@>{(\varphi_{j})_{F_{i}}\times{\rm id}}>{}>F_{i}\times[0,\delta)@>{\varphi_{i}}>{}>P\end{CD}

where
T(t_{1},t_{2})=(t_{2},t_{1})
, is commutative (perhaps, after a restriction to a smaller half interval
[0,\delta^{\prime})
).
By definition collars of disjoint facets are consistent.

A
system of consistent collars

is a set of collars
\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m}\}
of all facets such that
any two collars are consistent.

Proposition 3.7

(
[
G08
]
)
.

Any simple polytope
P
admits a system of consistent collars.

Given a system of consistent collars one can build a desired diffeomorphism as follows.
Take a diffeomorphism
\xi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that

\xi(t)=\begin{cases}t^{2}&\text{ if }t<\frac{\delta}{10};\\
t&\text{ if }t>\frac{\delta}{2}.\end{cases}

Choosing small enough
\delta
we can assume that if the images of any set of collars intersect
then the corresponding facets have a nonempty common intersection.
Now let a point
p\in P
belong to the images of exactly
k
collars
\varphi_{i_{1}}
,
\dots
,
\varphi_{i_{k}}
.
Then
G=F_{i_{1}}\cap\dots\cap F_{i_{k}}\neq\varnothing
. Consider the mapping

\varphi_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\colon G\times[0,\delta)^{k}\to P
defined as

\varphi_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}=\varphi_{i_{1}}\circ\left((\varphi_{i_{2}})_{F_{i_{1}}}\times{\rm id}_{[0,\delta)}\right)\circ\dots\circ\left((\varphi_{i_{k}})_{F_{i_{1}}\cap\dots\cap F_{i_{k-1}}}\times{\rm id}_{[0,\delta)^{k-1}}\right).

Since the collars are consistent, the mapping
\varphi_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
does not depend on the
order of the facets, that is

for any permutation

Then

p\in U_{G}=\varphi_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}({\rm int}(G)\times[0,\delta)^{k}),

for otherwise,

p\in\varphi_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}((G\cap F_{i_{k+1}})\times[0,\delta)^{k})\subset F_{i_{k+1}}\subset\varphi_{i_{k+1}}(F_{i_{k+1}}\times[0,\delta)).

Then for any local coordinates

in
{\rm int}(G)

the functions
(z_{1},\dots z_{n-k},t_{1},\dots,t_{k})
are local coordinates in the open set
U_{G}
. Define

F(z_{1},\dots,z_{n-k},t_{1},\dots,t_{k})=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n-k},\xi(t_{1}),\dots,\xi(t_{k})).

It can be shown that
F
is a correctly defined mapping on
P
and it is a diffeomorphism
of
P
with linear smooth structure to
P
with quadratic smooth structure.

4.
Uniqueness of equivariant smooth structures

It was proved by F. Bosio and L. Meersseman in
[
BM06
, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.7]

that if the geometric polytopes
P
and
Q
are combinatorially
equivalent, then the manifolds
\mathcal{Z}_{P}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{Q}
are
\mathbb{T}^{m}
-equivariantly
diffeomorphic. Thus, the defined equivariant smooth structure on the moment-angle complex
\mathcal{Z}_{P}
does not
depend on the geometric realization of
P
.

For real moment-angle manifolds the analogous argument
can not be followed literally due to the following reason. The proof uses
[
BM06
, Lemma 3.4]
: any diffeomorphism

f
of the sphere
S^{2a-1}=\{(z_{1},\dots,z_{a})\in\mathbb{C}^{a}\colon|z_{1}|^{2}+\dots+|z_{a}|^{2}=1\}
equivariant with respect to
the standard action of
\mathbb{T}^{a}
on
\mathbb{C}^{a}
is equivariantly isotopic to identity. The analogous statement
for the sphere
S^{a-1}=\{(w_{1},\dots,w_{a})\in\mathbb{R}^{a}\colon|w_{1}|^{2}+\dots+|w_{a}|^{2}=1\}
equivariant with respect to
the standard action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{a}

\frac{\partial\widehat{f}_{j}(\boldsymbol{u})}{\partial u_{k}}={\rm sign}(u_{j}){\rm sign}(u_{k})\frac{\partial g_{j}(|u_{1}|,\dots,|u_{n}|)}{\partial R_{k}}=\begin{cases}{\rm sign}(u_{j})\frac{\partial g_{j}(|u_{1}|,\dots,|u_{n}|)}{\partial R_{k}},&\text{ if }u_{k}>0;\\
-{\rm sign}(u_{j})\frac{\partial g_{j}(|u_{1}|,\dots,|u_{n}|)}{\partial R_{k}},&\text{ if }u_{k}<0;\\
0=\frac{\partial g_{j}(|u_{1}|,\dots,|u_{n}|)}{\partial_{+}R_{k}},&\text{ if }u_{k}=0.\end{cases}

Hence,
\frac{\partial\widehat{f}_{j}(\boldsymbol{u})}{\partial u_{k}}
is a continuous function at
\boldsymbol{u}^{0}
.
At last, for
k=j
we have

\frac{\partial\widehat{f}_{j}(\boldsymbol{u})}{\partial u_{j}}=\begin{cases}\frac{\partial g_{j}(|u_{1}|,\dots,|u_{n}|)}{\partial R_{j}},&\text{ if }u_{j}\neq 0;\\
\frac{\partial g_{j}(|u_{1}|,\dots,|u_{n}|)}{\partial_{+}R_{j}}>0,&\text{ if }u_{j}=0.\end{cases}

Thus,
\frac{\partial\widehat{f}_{j}(\boldsymbol{u})}{\partial u_{k}}
is also a continuous function at
\boldsymbol{u}^{0}
, and

\widehat{f}_{j}(\boldsymbol{u})
is smooth at
\boldsymbol{u}^{0}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Now Theorem
4.1
follows from Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
.

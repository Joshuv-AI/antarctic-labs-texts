Spectrum of weighted composition operators. Part XI. The essential spectra of some weighted composition operators on the disc algebra.
Arkady Kitover, Mehmet Orhon

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Weighted composition operators on Banach spaces of analytic functions remain the subject of intensive research. While the appearance of the excellent books
[
16
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
2
]
, and
[
13
]
considerably enriched our knowledge of this subject, many challenging and interesting questions remain unsolved. One of the reasons of the continuing interest in the properties of composition and weighted composition operators is their intrinsic connection to topological and complex dynamics and that was our main motivation when writing this paper.

In
[
10
]
the authors investigated some properties of essential spectra of weighted composition operators on uniform algebras. As a consequence we obtained in
[
10
]
a description of essential spectra of weighted automorphisms of the disc algebra, and in
[
11

we extended these results to weighted automorphisms of the polydisc algebras.

The next logical step is to investigate the essential spectra of weighted isometries of the disc algebra, i.e. of the operators of the form
(Tf)(z)=w(z)f(B(z))
, where
f
and
w
belong to the disc algebra and
B
is a finite Blaschke product of degree
d\geq 2
.

In Theorem
3.1
we provide a full description of the essential spectra of the weighted isometries of the disc algebra in the case when the Blaschke product
B
is either elliptic or double parabolic (see the definition below) and the weight
w
does not have zeros on the unit circle.

The case when
w
has zeros on the unit circle is considerably more complicated. The results related to this case are contained in Theorems
3.5
and
3.6
, see also Examples
3.3
and
3.4
and Conjecture
3.7

2.
Preliminaries and axillary results

Let us briefly recall the basic notions and notations used below. All the linear spaces are considered over the field
\mathds{C}
of all complex numbers.

Let
T
be a continuous linear operator on a Banach space
X
. We denote the spectrum of the operator
T
as
\sigma(T)
.

Recall that the approximate point spectrum of
T
is defined as

\begin{split}&\sigma_{a.p.}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\;\text{there are}\;x_{n}\in X\;\text{such that}\\
&\|x_{n}\|=1\;\text{and}\;Tx_{n}-\lambda x_{n}\rightarrow 0\},\end{split}

(1)

Recall that a sequence
x_{n}\in X
is called singular if it does not contain any norm-convergent subsequence.

The upper and lower semi-Fredholm spectra
\sigma_{usf}(T)
and
\sigma_{lsf}(T)
of
T
are defined as follows (see
[
7
]
):

\begin{split}&\lambda\in\sigma_{usf}(T)\Leftrightarrow\lambda\in\sigma_{a.p.}(T)\;\text{and the sequence}\;x_{n}\in X\;\text{from}\;(1)\\
&\text{ can be chosen in such a way that it is singular}.\end{split}

(2)

\sigma_{lsf}(T)=\sigma_{usf}(T^{\prime}),

(3)

where
T^{\prime}
is the Banach adjoint of
T
.

By
\mathds{A}
we denote the disk algebra, the Banach algebra of all the functions analytic in the open unit disk
\mathds{U}
and continuous in the closed unit disk
\mathds{D}
endowed with the supremum norm.

Let us recall that a finite Blaschke product of degree
d
is defined as

B(z)=e^{i\theta}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\frac{z-a_{j}}{1-\overline{a_{j}}},\;|a_{j}|<1.

(4)

We will always assume that
d>1
.

We need the following classification of finite Blaschke products. See e.g.
[
4
, Definition 3.2, p. 25]
.

Let
B
be a finite Blaschke product of degree

. Let

be the Wolff-Denjoy point of

(i.e. the unique point in
\mathds{D}
such that the iterations of

uniformly on compact subsets of

). We say that

is:

if

if

,

, and

has multiplicity 2.

3.
The main results

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(Tf)(z)=w(z)f(B(z),f\in\mathds{A},z\in\mathds{D}
, where
B
is a finite Blaschke product of degree
d\geq 2
and
w\in\mathds{A},w\neq 0
.

Assume that

(1)

The Blaschke product
B
is either elliptic or double parabolic.

(2)

The weight
w
has no zeros on the unit circle
\mathds{T}
.

Then the spectral radius
\rho(T)>0
,
\sigma(T)=\sigma_{lsf}(T)=\rho(T)\mathds{D}
,
and
\sigma_{usf}(T)=\rho(T)\mathds{T}
.

Proof.

In virtue of Theorem
2.1
we can assume without loss of generality that
B(z)=z^{d},d\in\mathds{N},d\geq 2
.
The inequality
\rho(T)>0
follows from the well known formula (see e.g.
[
9
]
)

\rho(T)=\max\limits_{\mu\in M_{z^{p}}}\exp\int\ln{|w|}d\mu,

(16)

where
M_{z^{d}}
is the set of all
z^{d}
-invariant probability measures in
C(\mathds{T})^{\prime}
, and from the standard fact that for
w\in\mathds{A},w\neq 0
we have

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}{\ln{|w(e^{i\theta})d\theta}}>-\infty.

To prove that
\sigma_{usf}(T)=\sigma_{a.p.}(T)=\rho(T)\mathds{T}
we assume to the contrary that there is a
\lambda\in\sigma_{a.p.}(T)
such that
0<|\lambda|<\rho(T)
. It follows from (
16
) and
Theorem
2.2
that there is a
z^{d}
-periodic point
t\in\mathds{T}
of the smallest period
m
such that

|w_{m}(t)|>|\lambda|^{m}.

(17)

By Lemma
2.6
and Theorem
2.5
there is a point
k\in\mathds{T}
such that

u\in\varphi^{-n}(\{k\})\Rightarrow|w_{n}(u)|\leq|\lambda|^{n},n\in\mathds{N}

(18)

But, by Lemma
2.10
there is a sequence
k_{n}\in\mathds{T}
such that
k_{1}=k,\varphi(k_{n})=k_{n-1},n\geq 2
and
k_{n}\rightarrow t
. Clearly, in virtue of the inequality (
17
), the inequality (
18
) cannot be satisfied. Thus,
\sigma_{usf}(T)=\rho(T)\mathds{T}
.

It remains to notice that clearly
codim(T\mathds{A})=\infty
and therefore

\sigma_{lsf}(T)=\sigma(T)=\rho(T)\mathds{D}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2

Let
(Tf)(z)=w(z)f(B(z),f\in\mathds{A},z\in\mathds{D}
, where
B
is a finite Blaschke product of degree

and
w\in\mathds{A}
.

Assume that

(1)

The Blaschke product
B
is either elliptic or double parabolic.

(2)

For any
t_{0}\in\mathds{T}
there are a
p\in\mathds{N}
and points
t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}\in\mathds{T}
such that
B(t_{j})=t_{j-1},j=1,\ldots,p
, and
w\neq 0
on the set

\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mathds{N}}\varphi^{-n}\{(t_{p})\}\}
.

Then the spectral radius
\rho(T)>0
,
\sigma(T)=\sigma_{lsf}(T)=\rho(T)\mathds{D}
,
and
\sigma_{usf}(T)=\rho(T)\mathds{T}
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.9
that
0\notin\sigma_{a.p.}(T)
. The case

can be considered exactly like in the proof of Theorem

.
∎

it is sufficient to prove that for any
n\in\mathds{N}

\max\limits_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|sin(\theta)sin(2k\theta)\ldots sin((2k)^{n}\theta)|\leq\sin^{n}{\Big(\frac{\pi k}{2k+1}\Big)}.

(19)

In the case when
k=1
the inequality (
19
) was proved in
[
15
, pp.21 and 86]
. A minor modification of the proof in
[
15
]
is sufficient to prove (
19
) in general. We provide the details for the reader’s convenience. Following
[
15
]
we divide the proof of (
19
) into three steps.

(a) It is immediate to see that

\max\limits_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|sin^{2k}(\theta)sin(2k\theta)|=\sin^{2k+1}{\Big(\frac{\pi k}{2k+1}\Big)}.

(b) The expression

P_{n}=|sin^{2k}(\theta){sin^{2k+1}(2k\theta)\ldots sin^{2k+1}((2k)^{n-1}\theta)}sin((2k)^{n}\theta)|

takes its maximum value at the point
\theta=\frac{\pi k}{2k+1}
. We prove (b) by induction. For
n=1
(b) follows from (a). Assume that (b) is true for an
n\in\mathds{N}
and consider the ratio

\frac{P_{n+1}}{P_{n}}=|sin^{2k}((2k)^{n}\theta)sin((2k)^{n+1}\theta)|.

By (a) this ratio takes its greatest value at
\theta=\frac{\pi k}{2k+1}
, and (b) is proved.

(c) It follows from (b) that

|sin(\theta)sin(2k\theta)\ldots sin((2k)^{n}\theta)|^{2k+1}\leq\sin^{n(2k+1)}{\Big(\frac{\pi k}{2k+1}\Big)}

and (
19
) is proved.
∎

As the next example shows, we cannot omit condition (2) in the statement of Theorem
3.2
.

Example 3.4
.

Let
(Tf)(z)=w(z)f(z^{2}),f\in\mathds{A},z\in\mathds{D}
and let

be such that

w(\exp{(i2\pi/3)})=w(\exp{(i4\pi/3)})=\|w\|=1

,

is the only zero of

\mathds{T}

It follows from Corollary
2.8
and the reasoning in the proof of Theorem

that

\sigma(T)=\sigma_{lsf}(T)=\mathds{D}\;\text{and}\;\sigma_{usf}(T)=(1/2)\mathds{T}\cup\mathds{T}.

Example
3.4
is a special case of the following theorem.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
B
be either elliptic or double parabolic finite Blaschke product of degree
d\geq 2
.
Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathds{C}
,
0\leq|\lambda_{1}|<|\lambda_{2}|<\ldots<|\lambda_{m}|
.

Then there is a
w\in\mathds{A}
such that for the operator
T
,

(Tf)(z)=w(z)f(B(z)),f\in\mathds{A},z\in\mathds{D},

we have
\sigma_{usf}(T)=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\mathds{T}
.

Proof.

We can assume without loss of generality that
B(z)=z^{d}
. Let
t_{j}\in\mathds{T},j=1,\ldots,m
be

z^{d}
-periodic points. Let
p_{j}
be the smallest period of the point
z_{j}
. We can assume that the sets

P_{j}=\{z_{j}^{d^{k}},k=0,\ldots,p_{j}-1\}
are pairwise disjoint. Let

(1)

on

,

(2)

on

,

(3)

|w|\leq|\lambda_{m}|
on
\mathds{T}

Let
Tf=w(f\circ B)
. The equality
\sigma_{usf}(T)=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\mathds{T}
follows from Theorem
2.5
, Corollary
2.8
, and Lemmas
2.9
and
2.10
.
∎

In the case of elliptic or double parabolic finite Blaschke products we can considerably strengthen the statement of Theorem

Theorem 3.6

Let
B

\lambda_{0}>\lambda_{1}>\ldots

\sigma_{usf}(T)=\{0\}\cup\bigcup\limits_{i\geq 0}\lambda_{i}\mathds{T},

(Tf)(z)=w(z)f(B(z)),f\in\mathds{A},z\in\mathds{D}

z_{i},i\geq 0

(21)

2.11

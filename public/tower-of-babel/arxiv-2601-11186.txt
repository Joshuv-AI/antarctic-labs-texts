Analytic self-force effects on radial infalling particles in the Schwarzschild spacetime: the radiated energy
Donato Bini 1 , Giorgio Di Russo 2

Abstract
We compute, at the first self force accuracy level, the radiated energy from a radially infalling particle released from rest in a Schwarzschild spacetime. We examine both the cases of a scalar particle and that of a massive particle, in the context of gravitational perturbations. Our findings are accompanied by Post-Newtonian checks. In spite of the specific interest for this kind of computations, we outline the building blocks for future higher-order Post-Newtonian computations as well as for extending these results to other interesting situations out of the black hole case.



I
Introduction

Black hole perturbations due to fields of any spin-weight, e.g., scalar (
s=0
), electromagnetic (
s=1
), gravitational (
s=2
), Rarita-Schwinger (
s=3/2
), etc. have been largely studied, especially in the case of black hole spacetimes (Schwarzschild and Kerr), either numerically and analytically, see e.g. the reviews
Sasaki:2003xr
;
Poisson:2003nc
;
Barack:2018yvs
.
They crucially depend on the orbit of the particle (with proper type charge and mass) associated with the perturbation which sources the corresponding wave equation, scalar, electromagnetic (em) and gravitational (grav)

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm scalar}&\rho=q\int\frac{d\tau}{\sqrt{-g}}\delta^{(4)}(x-x_{p}(\tau))\,,\cr{\rm em}&T^{\alpha\beta}=q_{\rm em}\int\frac{d\tau}{\sqrt{-g}}u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}\delta^{(4)}(x-x_{p}(\tau))\,,\cr{\rm grav}&T^{\alpha\beta}=m\int\frac{d\tau}{\sqrt{-g}}u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}\delta^{(4)}(x-x_{p}(\tau))\,,\cr\end{array}

where
x^{\alpha}=x^{\alpha}_{p}(\tau)
denote the parametric equations of the orbit.
We currently have a satisfying analytical knowledge of effects associated with particles at rest, in a circular equatorial motion and, in general for bound equatorial motion with small eccentricity (ellipticlike motions) as well as for unbound equatorial motion with large eccentricity (hyperboliclike motions).
Unfortunately, we do know much analytical perturbations due to radially infalling particles and the corresponding radiative losses.
Indeed, all existing works involving energy loss by particles radially falling into a Schwarzschild black hole are mainly semi-analytic or numerical and date back to the 70s
Zerilli:1970wzz
;
Davis:1971gg
;
Davis:1972ud
;
Ruffini:1973ky
, with some successive sequel
Cardoso:2002jr
;
Mitsou:2010jv
;
Oliveira:2018pnx
.

Here, for the first time, we compute analytically the radiated energy by scalar or massive particles within the framework of gravitational self force theory
Barack:2018yvs
.
This study has not only an academic interest and cannot be labelled as an exercise based on previously developed formalisms/approaches/results, because a particle falling into a black hole will enter soon a strong field regime region, where most of the tools available in perturbation theory are no longer valid, differently from a (massive) particle in quasi-circular motion or along a hyperboliclike trajectory which can spend most of the time in a weak field region (and consequently may lead to computable, highly accurate effects in Post-Newtonian sense).
Clearly this new situation requires a more careful analysis, with eventual inclusion of cut-off integrals to avoid unphysical divergencies.
In this sense, a stimulating study is outlined in Ref.
Barausse:2021xes
.

Therefore, aiming at filling the mentioned “analytic” gap existing in the literature, we first analyze the simplest case of scalar field and then discuss the more interesting case of gravitational perturbations in the context of gravitational self force. All these studies, when possible, will be paired with Post-Newtonian calculations which validate the final analytic result.

As it often happens in self force, the scalar field case contains already the main technical difficulties and it has to be considered preliminary to the others.
We will start discussing in detail the underlying difficulties of an analytic computation in the region where the field is not so strong to allow for Post-Newtonian (PN) analysis.
Viceversa, the strong field region requires
G\gg 1
and it is mainly accessible by numerical relativity. However, the leading order behaviour of the power spectrum was already obtained in the seminal paper of Zerilli
Zerilli:1970wzz
, showing a decreasing exponential behavior (at large
GM\omega
) of the energy loss at infinity.
This topic will be explored in future (still ongoing) works aiming at obtaining systematically (at least) the subleading terms in such a regime.
A numerical update for this computation can be found in Ref.
Mitsou:2010jv
, where an estimation of the quasi normal modes (QNM) in the Regge-Wheeler-Zerilli approach is also given.
Being the radial fall trajectory approaching the black hole horizon at the end of the process, these computations should mimic the merging of the two black holes, and consequently the “ringdown” with emission of the characteristic QNM frequencies.
The latter topic, together with tidal deformations and wave amplification factors, received much attention recently because of the gravitational wave signal GW250114
LIGOScientific:2025rid
recently detected with a particularly high signal-to-noise ratio,
and the technology to determine numerically or analytically them is well established (starting from pioneering works, see e.g. Ref.
Chandrasekhar:1975zza
, up to recent literature based on CFT techniques
Berti:2009kk
;
Aminov:2020yma
), even out the black hole context
Bianchi:2021xpr
;
Bianchi:2021mft
;
Bianchi:2022qph
;
Bianchi:2023sfs
;
DiRusso:2024hmd
;
Bena:2024hoh
;
Cipriani:2024ygw
;
Fioravanti:2021dce
;
DiRusso:2025qpf
.

In the present paper, after the preliminary case of scalar field perturbations, we extend the discussion to the more general situation of gravitational perturbations.

We show explicit expressions for the energy loss in the time domain and in the Fourier domain (namely, the main accomplishments of the present work), with some checks coming from Multipolar-Post-Minkowskian (MPM) formalism and Post-Newtonian (PN) theory.

In this work we use the mostly positive metric signature (
-+++
) and set units such that
G=1=c
, unless differently specified.

II
Radial infalling geodesics on a Schwarzschild spacetime

Let us consider the Schwarzschild metric in standard Schwarzschild coordinates

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\right)\,,\qquad

(1)

where

f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}\,.

(2)

Timelike equatorial (
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
) and radially infalling geodesics at
\phi=0
(parametrized by the proper time
\tau
) have parametric equations
x^{\alpha}=x_{p}^{\alpha}(\tau)
(and four velocity
U^{\alpha}=\frac{dx_{p}^{\alpha}}{d\tau}
) such that

\frac{dt_{p}}{d\tau}=\frac{E}{f(r_{p})}\,,

(3)

with the radial motion described by the following equation

\frac{dr_{p}}{dt}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r_{p}}\right)\frac{\sqrt{E^{2}-1+\frac{2M}{r_{p}}}}{E}\,.

(4)

Here
E
denotes the energy per unit mass of the particle while the angular momentum
L=0
for a radial fall.

We will limit our considerations to motion starting from rest (
E=1
) at spatial (radial) infinity,

\frac{dr_{p}}{dt}=-f(r_{p})\sqrt{\frac{2M}{r_{p}}}\,.

(5)

Introducing the (dimensionless) inverse radial variable
r=M/u
in Eq. (
5
) the exact solution of Eq. (
4
) (solved by using
u
as a parameter along the orbit instead of the proper time or the coordinate time) reads

t_{p}(u)=M\left[-\frac{\sqrt{2}(1+6u)}{3u^{3/2}}+2\log\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{2u}}{1-\sqrt{2u}}\right)\right]\,.

(6)

Fig.
1
shows the behavior of
t
(in units of
M
) vs
u\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, both in the exact case (solid curve) and in the PN approximated one (doted curve).

t
(in units of
M
) vs
u\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. The falling process starts at
t=-\infty
at radial infinity and ends at the horizon at
t=+\infty
. The superposed points (red online) correspond to the PN approximated solution which includes contributions up to
O(\eta^{16})
. The two plots start keeping away at
u\approx 0.4
meaning the non reliability of the PN approximation at this radius (close to the horizon,
u=\frac{1}{2}
).

When using
t
as a parameter (as we will do systematically below), instead, the above equation can be solved in PN sense as follows

\displaystyle r_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(-t)^{2/3}-\frac{8}{9}\alpha^{3}\eta^{2}+\frac{16\alpha^{5}}{27(-t)^{2/3}}\eta^{4}

(7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{640\alpha^{7}}{2187(-t)^{4/3}}\eta^{6}-\frac{256\alpha^{9}}{10935t^{2}}\eta^{8}

\displaystyle{-}

\displaystyle\frac{83968\alpha^{11}}{413343(-t)^{8/3}}\eta^{10}{-}\frac{3948544\alpha^{13}}{23914845(-t)^{10/3}}\eta^{12}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{3080192\alpha^{15}}{122762871t^{4}}\eta^{14}+\frac{18389663744\alpha^{17}}{97931290275(-t)^{14/3}}\eta^{16}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O(\eta^{17})\,,

where
\eta=\frac{1}{c}
is a place-holder for PN expansion and

\alpha=\left(\frac{9M}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\,.

(8)

Equivalently, Eq. (
7
) can be cast in the following form

\displaystyle r_{p}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha t^{2/3}\left[1-\frac{8}{9}\zeta^{2}+\frac{16}{27}\zeta^{4}+\frac{640}{2187}\zeta^{6}\right.

(9)

\displaystyle{-}

\displaystyle\frac{256}{10935}\zeta^{8}{-}\frac{83968}{413343}\zeta^{10}{-}\frac{3948544}{23914845}\zeta^{12}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{3080192}{122762871}\zeta^{14}+\frac{18389663744}{97931290275}\zeta^{16}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.O(\zeta^{17})\right]\,,

where

\zeta=\frac{\alpha\eta}{(-t)^{1/3}}\,.

(10)

The parametric equations of the perturbing particle orbit are the main ingredient needed to study non-homogeneous wave equations in the Schwarzschild spacetime.
In the next Section III below we will start considering scalar perturbations, whereas starting from Section IV we will consider the more interesting situation of gravitational perturbations.

III
Scalar wave equation

Let us
consider the scalar wave equation

\Box\psi=-4\pi\rho\,,

(11)

where

\Box\psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\psi)\,,

(12)

with
\sqrt{-g}=r^{2}\sin\theta
, and the source energy density given by

\rho=q\int\frac{d\tau}{\sqrt{-g}}\delta^{(4)}(x-x_{p}(\tau))\,.

(13)

In the present case of radial falling from rest at infinity the density
\rho
reads

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\int\frac{d\tau}{r^{2}}\delta(t-t_{p}(\tau))\delta(r-r_{p}(\tau))\delta(\theta-\frac{\pi}{2})\delta(\phi)

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{r_{p}^{2}(t)U^{t}}\delta(r-r_{p}(t))\delta(\theta-\frac{\pi}{2})\delta(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{qf(r_{p}(t))}{r_{p}^{2}(t)}\delta(r-r_{p}(t))\sum_{lm}Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)Y_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\,,\qquad

where we have chosen to parametrize the orbit by the coordinate time
t
, expressed the product
\delta(\theta-\frac{\pi}{2})\delta(\phi)
in (scalar) spherical harmonics and performed the integral over
\tau
by using the relation

\delta(t-t_{p}(\tau))=\frac{\delta(\tau-t)}{U^{t}(t)}=f(r_{p}(t))\delta(\tau-t)\,.

(15)

Separation of the angular variables and Fourier transforming the time variable implies

\displaystyle\rho(t,r,\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}\widehat{T}_{lm}(\omega,r)Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\,,\qquad

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{lm}T_{lm}(t,r)Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\,,

with

\displaystyle T_{lm}(t,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{qf(r_{p}(t))}{r_{p}^{2}(t)}\delta(r-r_{p}(t))Y_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}\widehat{T}_{lm}(\omega,r)\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{lm}(\omega,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\int dte^{i\omega t}\frac{f(r_{p}(t))}{r_{p}^{2}(t)}\delta(r-r_{p}(t))Y_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle qY_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0){\mathcal{F}}(r,\omega)\,,

with

{\mathcal{F}}(\omega,r)=\int dte^{i\omega t}\frac{f(r)}{r^{2}}\delta(r-r_{p}(t))\,.

(19)

Let us look for solutions
\psi
of the scalar wave equation of the form

\psi(t,r,\theta,\phi)=\sum_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}R_{lm\omega}(r)Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\,.

(20)

Eq. (
11
) then implies

\displaystyle\Box\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\left[f(r)R_{lm\omega}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2(r-M)}{r^{2}}R_{lm\omega}^{\prime}+\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{f}-\frac{L}{r^{2}}\right)\right]

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi\sum_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}\widehat{T}_{lm}(\omega,r)Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)Y_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\,,

(here
L=l(l+1)
) that is

R_{lm\omega}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2(r-M)}{r^{2}f(r)}R_{lm\omega}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{f}\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{f}-\frac{L}{r^{2}}\right)R_{lm\omega}=-4\pi\frac{1}{f}\widehat{T}_{lm}(\omega,r)\,,

(22)

which can conveniently rewritten by using the operator

{\mathcal{L}}_{(r)}(\cdot)\equiv\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}(\cdot)+\frac{\Delta^{\prime}(r)}{\Delta(r)}\frac{d}{dr}(\cdot)+\left(\frac{\omega^{2}r^{4}}{\Delta^{2}(r)}-\frac{L}{\Delta(r)}\right)(\cdot)\,,

(23)

such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{(r)}R_{lm\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\pi r^{2}}{\Delta(r)}\widehat{T}_{lm}(\omega,r)

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\pi qr^{2}}{\Delta(r)}Y_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0){\mathcal{F}}(\omega,r)\,.

This equation is solved by using the Green’s function method, i.e., introducing the Green’s function

{\mathcal{L}}_{(r)}G_{lm\omega}(r,r^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\Delta(r^{\prime})}\delta(r-r^{\prime})\,,\qquad\Delta(r)=r^{2}f(r)\,,

(25)

where

\displaystyle G_{lm\omega}(r,r^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{W_{lm\omega}}[R_{\rm in}(r)R_{\rm up}(r^{\prime})H(r^{\prime}-r)

(26)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle R_{\rm in}(r^{\prime})R_{\rm up}(r)H(r-r^{\prime})]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{W_{lm\omega}}R_{\rm in}(r_{<})R_{\rm up}(r_{>})\,,

and
r_{<}
and
r_{>}
correspond to
r
and
r^{\prime}
, respectively.

R_{\rm in}(r)
and
R_{\rm up}(r)
are two independent solutions of the homogeneous equation, satisfying ingoing boundary conditions at the horizon and outgoing boundary conditions at infinity, respectively. Both depend on
l,m,\omega
, which are not shown explicitly to simplify the notation. The range of the radial variable is
[r_{+},\infty)
, and

W_{lm\omega}=\Delta(r)[R_{\rm in}(r)R_{\rm up}^{\prime}(r)-R_{\rm up}(r)R_{\rm in}^{\prime}(r)]

(27)

is the constant Wronskian.
The solution
R_{lm\omega}(r)
of the non-homogeneous equation is then given by

\displaystyle R_{lm\omega}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi\int dr^{\prime}G_{lm\omega}(r,r^{\prime})r^{\prime 2}\widehat{T}_{lm}(\omega,r^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi qY_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\int dr^{\prime}G_{lm\omega}(r,r^{\prime})r^{\prime 2}{\mathcal{F}}(\omega,r^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi qY_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\int dte^{i\omega t}G_{lm\omega}(r,r_{p}(t))f(r_{p}(t))\,.

Since we have both a left and a right Green function (with respect to the particle’s world line) we have also a left and a right solution for
R_{lm\omega}(r)
, namely

\displaystyle R_{lm\omega}^{-}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi qY_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\frac{R_{\rm up}(r)}{W_{lm\omega}}\int dte^{i\omega t}R_{\rm in}(r_{p}(t))f(r_{p}(t))\,,

\displaystyle R_{lm\omega}^{+}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi qY_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\frac{R_{\rm in}(r)}{W_{lm\omega}}\int dte^{i\omega t}R_{\rm up}(r_{p}(t))f(r_{p}(t))\,,

(29)

which can be used to define a left and a right field
\psi^{\pm}(t,r,\theta,\phi)
by using Eq. (
20
).
However, it is easy to show that the field (but not its first derivatives, i.e., the associated self force) is continuous along the particle’s world line,
\psi=\psi^{+}=\psi^{-}
, and one writes

\displaystyle\psi(t,r,\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}R_{lm\omega}(r)Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi q\sum_{lm}Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)Y_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}\frac{1}{W_{lm\omega}}\int dt^{\prime}e^{i\omega t^{\prime}}R_{\rm in}(r_{p}(t^{\prime}))R_{\rm up}(r)f(r_{p}(t^{\prime}))\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi q\sum_{lm}Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)Y_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)R_{\rm up}(r){\mathcal{N}}_{lm}(t)\,,

where we have defined

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{lm}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{e^{-i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}}{W_{lm\omega}}R_{\rm in}(r_{p}(t^{\prime}))f(r_{p}(t^{\prime}))\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{lm}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt^{\prime}{\mathcal{M}}_{lm}(t,t^{\prime})\,.

(31)

One can now evaluate the field (
30
) along the source world line obtaining a function of
t
only,

\psi(t,r_{p}(t),\frac{\pi}{2},0)\,,

(32)

as well as its first derivatives with respect the coordinate, i.e. the self force (discontinuous along the source world line).
The latter is explicitly discussed in the next subsection.

III.1
Scalar self force

The self-force acting on the scalar particle is defined as

F^{\alpha}=qP(U)^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}\psi\,,

(33)

where
P(U)=g+U\otimes U
projects orthogonally to
U
, the particle’s four velocity.
Explicitly,

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\left(\partial_{\alpha}\psi+U_{\alpha}U^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}\psi\right)\,,

(34)

that is

\displaystyle F_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\sqrt{\frac{2M}{r}}\left(-\sqrt{\frac{2M}{r}}\frac{1}{f}\partial_{t}\psi+\partial_{r}\psi\right)\,,

\displaystyle F_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{U^{t}}{U^{r}}F_{t}\,,\quad F_{\theta}=q\partial_{\theta}\psi\,,\quad F_{\phi}=q\partial_{\phi}\psi\,,

(35)

where we recall the expressions of the (covariant, contravariant) components of the four velocity

\displaystyle U_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1\,,\qquad U_{r}=-\frac{1}{f}\sqrt{\frac{2M}{r}}\,,

\displaystyle U^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f}\,,\qquad U^{r}=-\sqrt{\frac{2M}{r}}\,.

(36)

The proportionality between
F_{t}
and
F_{r}
follows from the orthogonality condition
F\cdot U=0
.
The components of the force should be evaluated along the particle’s orbit, and then become functions of the proper time (or any other parameter used to parametrize the orbit, like the coordinate time or the radial position itself).

III.2
Radiation losses: formal expressions

The expressions for the radiated energy and angular momentum can be derived from the associated energy-momentum tensor which for a
massless complex scalar field is given by

8\pi T^{\rm scal}_{\mu\nu}=\psi^{*}_{,\mu}\psi_{,\nu}+\psi_{,\mu}\psi^{*}_{,\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}\psi^{*}_{,\alpha}\psi^{,\alpha}\,,

(37)

so that

\frac{d^{2}E}{dtd\Omega}=\lim_{r\to\infty}(r^{2}T^{\rm scal}{}^{r}{}_{t})\,.

(38)

One finds

8\pi T^{\rm scal}_{rt}=\psi^{*}_{,r}\psi_{,t}+\psi_{,r}\psi^{*}_{,t}\,,

(39)

implying

8\pi T^{\rm scal}{}^{r}{}_{t}=f(\psi^{*}_{,r}\psi_{,t}+\psi_{,r}\psi^{*}_{,t})=f\psi^{*}_{,r}\psi_{,t}+{\rm c.c.}\,.

(40)

Let us use the labels “
l,m,\omega
” for the
t
derivative and
l^{\prime}m^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}
” for the
r
derivative of
\psi
, namely

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{lm}Y_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}R_{lm\omega}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{,t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{lm}Y_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}(-i\omega)e^{-i\omega t}R_{lm\omega}\,,

\displaystyle\psi^{*}_{,r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}Y_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{*}\int\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}e^{i\omega^{\prime}t}\frac{d}{dr}R_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}}^{*}\,,

(41)

and

\displaystyle r^{2}T^{\rm scal}{}^{r}{}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}f}{8\pi}(\psi^{*}_{,r}\psi_{,t}+\psi_{,r}\psi^{*}_{,t})

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}f}{8\pi}\sum_{lm,l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)Y_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{*}(\theta,\phi)\left[(-i\omega)e^{-i(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}R_{lm\omega}(r)\frac{d}{dr}R^{*}_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}}(r)\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.(i\omega^{\prime})e^{i(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}R^{*}_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}}(r)\frac{d}{dr}R_{lm\omega}(r)\right]\,.

Integrating over the sphere and recalling the orthogonality relation
NIST
(see Eq. 14.30.8 there)

\int\sin\theta d\theta d\phi\,Y_{lm}^{*}(\theta,\phi)Y_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(\theta,\phi)=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\,,

(43)

we find

\displaystyle\frac{dE}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\sin\theta d\theta d\phi r^{2}T^{\rm scal}{}^{r}{}_{t}

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}f}{8\pi}\sum_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\left[(-i\omega)e^{-i(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}R_{lm\omega}(r)\frac{d}{dr}R^{*}_{lm\omega^{\prime}}(r)\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.(i\omega^{\prime})e^{i(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}R^{*}_{lm\omega^{\prime}}(r)\frac{d}{dr}R_{lm\omega}(r)\right]\big|_{r=r_{p}(t)}\,.

In the second integral we can exchange
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\frac{dE}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}f}{8\pi}\sum_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}(-i\omega)e^{-i(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}\left[R_{lm\omega}(r)\frac{d}{dr}R^{*}_{lm\omega^{\prime}}(r)-R^{*}_{lm\omega}(r)\frac{d}{dr}R_{lm\omega^{\prime}}(r)\right]\big|_{r=r_{p}(t)}

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}f}{8\pi}\sum_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}(-i\omega)e^{-i(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}2i{\rm Im}\left(R_{lm\omega}(r)\frac{d}{dr}R^{*}_{lm\omega^{\prime}}(r)\right)\big|_{r=r_{p}(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}f}{4\pi}\sum_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\omega e^{-i(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}{\mathcal{J}}_{lm\omega\omega^{\prime}}(r)\big|_{r=r_{p}(t)}\,,

where we have defined

{\mathcal{J}}_{lm\omega\omega^{\prime}}(r)={\rm Im}\left(R_{lm\omega}(r)\frac{d}{dr}R^{*}_{lm\omega^{\prime}}(r)\right)\,,

(46)

and in which one has to use
R_{lm\omega}(r)=R_{lm\omega}^{+}(r)
, namely,

R_{lm\omega}^{+}(r)=-4\pi qY_{lm}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\frac{R_{\rm in}(r)}{W_{lm\omega}}\int dte^{i\omega t}R_{\rm up}(r)f(r)|_{{}_{r=r_{p}(t)}}\,.

(47)

because of the evaluation at
t\to+\infty
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\Delta E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dt\frac{dE}{dt}

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{2M}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{dr}dr\frac{r^{2}f}{4\pi}\times

\displaystyle\sum_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\omega e^{-i(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}{\mathcal{J}}_{lm\omega\omega^{\prime}}(r)\,.

The final expression for the radiated energy

\frac{dE}{dt}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int\sin\theta d\theta d\phi r^{2}{T^{{\rm scal}\,r}}_{t}

(49)

follows straightforwardly

\displaystyle\frac{dE}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{r^{2}f(r)}{8\pi}\sum_{lm}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\Bigg[

(50)

\displaystyle(-i\omega)e^{-i(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}R_{lm\omega}(r)\frac{d}{dr}R^{*}_{lm\omega^{\prime}}(r)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(i\omega^{\prime})e^{i(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}R^{*}_{lm\omega^{\prime}}(r)\frac{d}{dr}R_{lm\omega}(r)\Bigg]\,.

As already stated, here, because of the
r\rightarrow\infty
limit, we must consider only the up-part of the
R_{lm\omega}(r)
solution.

In this section, our main result in the scalar field case, including contributions from
l=0,1,2
only and PN expanding up to the
O(\eta^{8})
fractional accuracy, is the following

\displaystyle E_{\rm rad}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\frac{dE}{dt}

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{M}\Big[-\frac{\eta^{3}}{90}-\frac{739\eta^{5}}{1512}+\left(\frac{2133\sqrt{3}}{14000}-\frac{49}{360}\right)\pi\eta^{6}+\frac{2541439\eta^{7}}{1360800}+\left(\frac{8}{3}-\frac{25970213}{2205000\sqrt{3}}\right)\pi\eta^{8}\Big]+O\left(\eta^{9}\right)\,.\qquad

In the next section, instead, we will consider the more interesting case of the gravitational perturbations.

IV
Gravitational perturbations: analytic computations of the radiated energy at the 2PN fractional accuracy

It is instructive to start quickly by the simplest case of the leading Newtonian order (actually, a warming-up exercise; see e.g. Refs
Maggiore:2007ulw
;
Maggiore:2018sht
), passing then to a Post-Newtonian (PN) computation which will be used later to test the first few terms of the genuine self force computation we are going to develop in detail below.

IV.1
The leading Newtonian order

The computation of the energy emitted by a test particle of mass
m

radially infalling into a Schwarzschild black hole requires several assumptions, already at the Newtonian level.
First, the gravitational wave (GW) production is computed in the linearized theory, without including backreactions on the Schwarzschild metric. Second, the Newtonian equations of motion are used instead of the general relativistic ones,

\frac{1}{2}m\left(\frac{dr_{p}}{dt}\right)^{2}-\frac{GmM}{r_{p}(t)}=0\,,

(52)

implying

\frac{dr_{p}}{dt}=-\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r_{p}(t)}}\,.

(53)

The third assumption requires that most of the radiation is emitted when the particle is non relativistic, and therefore the quadrupole formula is still valid (namely, until the particle enters the strong field region, i.e., approaches the black hole horizon). Currently, strong field computations can be performed only numerically.

In fact, in the last part of the radial fall orbit all these assumptions break down.
If one evaluates Eq. (
53
) at the horizon
r=2GM/c^{2}
one obtains
\dot{r}(t)=-c
, obviously beyond the slow-motion approximation. Thus, one can only compute the radiation emitted from
r=+\infty
until a certain radius
r=R>2M
(to be fixed later).

The solution of the Newtonian equation of motion (such that at
t=t_{0}
the particle is at the radius
r(t_{0})
) is given by

r^{3/2}(t)-r^{3/2}(t_{0})=\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{2GM}(t_{0}-t)\,,

(54)

where the boundary conditions are chosen so that there exists a maximal value of the time at which the particle reaches
R
, the boundary of the strong field region

\lim_{t\to-\infty}r(t)=+\infty\,,\qquad r(t_{\rm max})=R\,.

(55)

See Ref.
Maggiore:2007ulw
for a detailed and pedagogical introduction to this problem.
Let us define

\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{2GM}\bar{t}\equiv\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{2GM}t_{0}+r^{3/2}(t_{0})\,,

(56)

so that Eq. (
54
) becomes

r^{3/2}(t)=\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{2GM}(\bar{t}-t)\,,

(57)

implying that
r(\bar{t})=0
. Equivalently,

r(t)=\left(\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{2GM}\right)^{2/3}(\bar{t}-t)^{2/3}\equiv\alpha(\bar{t}-t)^{2/3}\,,

(58)

with

\alpha=\left(\frac{9}{2}GM\right)^{1/3}\,,

(59)

already defined in Eq. (
8
).

We find
\lim_{t\to-\infty}r(t)=+\infty
, while the minimum value
r(t)=R
is reached at

t_{\rm max}=\bar{t}-\frac{2R^{3/2}}{3\sqrt{2GM}}\,.

(60)

Introducing a shifted temporal coordinate
\bar{t}-t=\tau
, the solution of the equations of motion reads

r(\tau)=\alpha\tau^{2/3}=\left(\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{2GM}\tau\right)^{2/3}\,.

(61)

The radiated energy at this (Newtonian) level can be computed via the well-known quadrupole formula

E_{\rm rad}=\frac{G}{5c^{5}}\int_{-\infty}^{t_{\rm max}}dtI_{ij}^{(3)}(t)I_{ij}^{(3)}(t)\,,

(62)

where
I_{ij}(t)=\mu x^{\langle i}x^{j\rangle}
(while
m
in Eq. (
52
) is the mass of the perturbing particle,
\mu
is the reduced mass of the two-body system) and, we recall, the process should be limited to the interval
t\in(-\infty,t_{\rm max})
.

In our case (equatorial motion, radial fall along the
x
-axis,
\phi(t)=0
)

I_{ij}(t)=\frac{1}{3}\mu r^{2}q_{ij}\,,\qquad q_{ij}={\rm diag}[2,-1,-1]\,,

(63)

with
q_{ij}
symmetric and tracefree and such that

q_{ij}q_{ij}=\delta^{ij}(q^{2})_{ij}=6\,,

(64)

and

\frac{G}{5c^{5}}I_{ij}^{(3)}(t)I_{ij}^{(3)}(t)=\frac{2G}{15c^{5}}\mu^{2}\frac{(2GM)^{3}}{r^{5}(t)}\,.

(65)

Consequently,

\displaystyle E_{\rm rad}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2G(2GM)^{3}}{15c^{5}}\mu^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{t_{\rm max}}\frac{dt}{r^{5}(t)}

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Mc^{2}\nu^{2}\frac{2^{\frac{17}{3}}3^{-\frac{10}{3}}\left(\frac{GM}{c^{3}\tau_{\rm min}}\right)^{\frac{7}{3}}}{35}\,,

with
\tau_{\rm min}=(R/\alpha)^{3/2}
and
\frac{GM}{c^{3}\tau_{\rm min}}
dimensionless.
In the Fourier space

\displaystyle(-i\omega)^{3}\hat{I}_{ij}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dte^{i\omega t}I_{ij}^{(3)}(t)

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\mu(2GM)^{3/2}q_{ij}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\frac{e^{i\omega t}}{r^{5/2}(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\frac{1}{3}\frac{(2GM)^{3/2}}{\alpha^{5/2}}e^{i\omega\bar{t}}J(\omega)q_{ij}\,,

where

\displaystyle J(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\frac{e^{-i\omega\tau}}{\tau^{5/3}}

(68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{3}e^{\frac{1}{6}i\pi}|\omega|^{2/3}\Gamma\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\theta(\omega)\,,

with

\theta(\omega)
the Heaviside step function.

In the Fourier space we have then

\displaystyle E_{\rm rad}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{G}{5c^{5}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{6}|I(\omega)|^{2}

(69)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\omega\frac{dE}{d\omega}

with

\frac{dE}{d\omega}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{G}{5c^{5}}\omega^{6}|I(\omega)|^{2}\,.

(70)

We find

\displaystyle\frac{dE}{d\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{G}{5c^{5}}\mu^{2}\frac{(2GM)^{3}}{\alpha^{5}}\frac{6}{9}|J(\omega)|^{2}

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{G}{5\pi c^{5}}\mu^{2}\frac{(2GM)^{3}}{\alpha^{5}}|\omega|^{4/3}\Gamma^{2}\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\theta(\omega)\,.

Summarizing, for
\omega>0
we find

\displaystyle\frac{dE}{d\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}\omega^{4/3}\,,

(72)

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{G}{5\pi c^{5}}\mu^{2}\frac{(2GM)^{3}}{\alpha^{5}}\Gamma^{2}\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu^{2}Mc^{2}}{5\pi}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{7/3}\left(\frac{2GM}{c^{3}}\right)^{7/3}\Gamma^{2}\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\,.

This implies that in the Fourier space the (integrated) energy loss due to quadrupolar emission (
l=2
) is divergent and one has to truncate the result at a certain value of
\omega=\omega_{\rm max}
corresponding to the entrance of the particle in the strong field region marked by
r=R
. As mentioned above, at this point the PN approximation breaks down and one must resort to other approximation techniques. We therefore will limit to display energy densities (in the time domain and in the Fourier domain) which are not affeted by this problem. Let us note, in passing, that a Post-Minkowskian-type treatment of the problem can be of interest but a complete technology for this to be applied to black hole perturbations is not available yet (unless one considers scattering processes where, differently, approaches of the type of effective field theory and “amplitudes” already play a role, see e.g.,
Kosower:2018adc
;
Bern:2019nnu
;
Bern:2021dqo
;
Dlapa:2021npj
;
Bern:2025zno
).

IV.2
The complete 2PN result

The harmonic coordinate 2PN conserved energy (per unit of reduced mass) reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm N}+\eta^{2}\mathcal{E}_{\rm 1PN}+\eta^{4}\mathcal{E}_{\rm 2PN}\,,

(74)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}v^{2}-\frac{GM}{r}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm 1PN}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{8}(1-3\nu)v^{4}+\frac{1}{2}(3+\nu)v^{2}\frac{GM}{r}+\frac{1}{2}\nu\dot{r}^{2}\frac{GM}{r}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{GM}{r}\right)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm 2PN}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{5(1-7\nu+13\nu^{2})}{16}v^{6}+\frac{21-23\nu-27\nu^{2}}{8}v^{4}\frac{GM}{r}

(75)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\nu(1-15\nu)}{4}\dot{r}^{2}v^{2}\frac{GM}{r}-\frac{3\nu(1-3\nu)}{8}\dot{r}^{4}\frac{GM}{r}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{2+15\nu}{4}\left(\frac{GM}{r}\right)^{3}+\frac{14-55\nu+4\nu^{2}}{8}\left(\frac{GM}{r}\right)^{2}v^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{4+69\nu+12\nu^{2}}{8}\left(\frac{GM}{r}\right)^{2}\dot{r}^{2}\,,

where

v=\sqrt{\dot{r}^{2}+r^{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}}\,.

(76)

For
\dot{\phi}=0=J
, one finds
v\to|\dot{r}|
,
and then

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}(r,\dot{r})
.
Assuming
{\mathcal{E}}=0
(releasing from rest) this equation defines
\dot{r}
as a function of
r
, i.e., a (perturbative) differential equation which can be easily solved
to get the harmonic coordinates and 2PN accurate orbit
r=r_{p}(t)
,

\displaystyle r_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3^{2/3}(GM)^{1/3}t^{2/3}}{2^{1/3}}+\frac{5}{2}GM(-2+\nu)\eta^{2}

(77)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{(GM)^{5/3}(47-17\nu+5\nu^{2})}{2\,\,6^{2/3}t^{2/3}}\eta^{4}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O(\eta^{6})\,.

Let us recall that the particle is released from rest at radial infinity (at
t\to-\infty
), corresponding to a vanishing binding energy
{\mathcal{E}}
, besides the obvious vanishing of angular momentum due to the radial fall.

The energy flux at the 2PN level of accuracy is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rm N}+\eta^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\rm 1PN}+\eta^{4}\mathcal{F}_{\rm 2PN}\,,

(78)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rm N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{32G^{3}M^{4}\nu^{2}}{5c^{5}r^{4}}\left(v^{2}-\frac{11}{12}\dot{r}^{2}\right)\,,

(79)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rm 1PN}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{32G^{3}M^{4}\nu^{2}}{5c^{7}r^{4}}\Big[v^{2}\left(\frac{785}{336}-\frac{71\nu}{28}\right)+\frac{G^{2}M^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{21}-\frac{4\nu}{21}\right)+\frac{GMv^{2}}{r}\left(-\frac{170}{21}+\frac{10\nu}{21}\right)+v^{2}\dot{r}^{2}\left(-\frac{1487}{168}+\frac{58\nu}{7}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{GM\dot{r}^{2}}{r}\left(\frac{367}{42}-\frac{5\nu}{14}\right)+\dot{r}^{4}\left(\frac{687}{112}-\frac{155\nu}{28}\right)\Big]

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rm 2PN}

\displaystyle{=}

\displaystyle\frac{32G^{3}\nu^{2}M^{4}}{5c^{9}r^{4}}\Big[v^{6}\left(\frac{2215\nu^{2}}{252}{-}\frac{5497\nu}{504}{+}\frac{47}{14}\right){+}\frac{G^{3}M^{3}}{r^{3}}\left({-}\frac{4\nu^{2}}{27}{+}\frac{19\nu}{7}{-}\frac{253}{378}\right){+}\frac{G^{2}M^{2}v^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{13\nu^{2}}{27}{+}\frac{2273\nu}{252}{+}\frac{281473}{9072}\right)

(80)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{GMv^{4}}{r}\left(-\frac{199\nu^{2}}{36}+\frac{5237\nu}{252}-\frac{247}{14}\right)+v^{4}\dot{r}^{2}\left(-\frac{1573\nu^{2}}{42}+\frac{1713\nu}{28}-\frac{573}{56}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{G^{2}M^{2}\dot{r}^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(-\frac{16\nu^{2}}{9}-\frac{1633\nu}{504}-\frac{106319}{3024}\right)+\frac{GMv^{2}\dot{r}^{2}}{r}\left(\frac{2165\nu^{2}}{84}-\frac{8513\nu}{84}+\frac{4987}{84}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle v^{2}\dot{r}^{4}\left(\frac{631\nu^{2}}{14}-\frac{5069\nu}{56}+\frac{1009}{84}\right)+\frac{GM\dot{r}^{4}}{r}\left(-\frac{7145\nu^{2}}{378}+\frac{60971\nu}{756}-\frac{5585}{126}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\dot{r}^{6}\left(-\frac{2101\nu^{2}}{126}+\frac{10117\nu}{252}-\frac{2501}{504}\right)\Big]\,.

These expressions can be found in previous literature, e.g.
Arun:2007sg
(see Eqs. 5.2 there and see also
Blanchet:2013haa
for a review), and are included here only for completeness. Specializing them to the radial fall (
v=|\dot{r}|=-\dot{r}
) each PN contribution to the flux reduces to a function of
r
and
\dot{r}
. Explicitly,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rm N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{32G^{3}M^{4}\nu^{2}}{5c^{5}r^{4}}\frac{1}{12}\dot{r}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rm 1PN}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{32G^{3}M^{4}\nu^{2}}{5c^{7}r^{4}}\left(\frac{(1-4\nu)G^{2}M^{2}}{21r^{2}}+\frac{(5\nu+27)GM\dot{r}^{2}}{42r}+\frac{1}{42}(9\nu-16)\dot{r}^{4}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rm 2PN}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{32G^{3}M^{4}\nu^{2}}{5c^{9}r^{4}}\left(-\frac{\left(56\nu^{2}-1026\nu+253\right)G^{3}M^{3}}{378r^{3}}-\frac{\left(5880\nu^{2}-26217\nu+18742\right)G^{2}M^{2}\dot{r}^{2}}{4536r^{2}}\right.

(81)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\left(1016\nu^{2}+65\nu-1965\right)GM\dot{r}^{4}}{756r}+\frac{1}{252}\left(-67\nu^{2}-25\nu+44\right)\dot{r}^{6}\right)\,.

Inserting Eq. (
77
) in Eq. (
78
) and using Eqs. (
IV.2
), we obtain the final 2PN accurate result

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}}{\nu^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}^{\nu^{0}}+{\mathcal{F}}^{\nu^{1}}\nu+{\mathcal{F}}^{\nu^{2}}\nu^{2}\,,

(82)

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}^{\nu^{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{32\cdot 2^{\frac{2}{3}}}{405\cdot 3^{\frac{1}{3}}T^{\frac{10}{3}}}{+}\frac{2816\eta^{2}}{2835T^{4}}{+}\frac{30976\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{3}}\eta^{4}}{6615\cdot 3^{\frac{2}{3}}T^{\frac{14}{3}}}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}^{\nu^{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2048\eta^{2}}{8505T^{4}}-\frac{45056\sqrt[3]{2}\eta^{4}}{19845\ 3^{2/3}T^{14/3}}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}^{\nu^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{16384\sqrt[3]{2}\eta^{4}}{59535\ 3^{2/3}T^{14/3}}\,.

(83)

where here we set
G=c=1
and
T=t/M
, for convenience.
In the self force limit (
\nu\to 0
)
\frac{\mathcal{F}}{\nu^{2}}
reduces to
{\mathcal{F}}^{\nu^{0}}
and will be used to check the self force computations in the next section.

In order to have additional checks one can also compute the tail part of the energy flux (at the leading order, see e.g. Eq. (3.4) in Ref.
Bini:2021qvf
),
given by

\displaystyle E_{\rm rad,tail}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4G^{2}M}{5}\eta^{8}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{8}|\hat{I}_{ij}(\omega)|^{2}A_{1}(\omega)\,,\qquad

(84)

with

\displaystyle A_{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau e^{i\omega\tau}\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{C_{I_{2}}}\right)

(85)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{2|\omega|}-\frac{i}{\omega}\ln(C_{I_{2}}|\omega|e^{\gamma})\,,

and
C_{I_{2}}
a scale associated with the mass quadrupole moment

C_{I_{2}}=r_{0}\frac{2e^{-11/12}}{c}\,,

(86)

and
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. Then

\displaystyle E_{\rm rad,tail}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4G^{2}M}{5}\pi\eta^{8}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{7}|\hat{I}_{ij}(\omega)|^{2}\,,\qquad

(87)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{27}{10}C^{2}\omega_{\rm max}^{10/3}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{7}{3}\right)\,,

where

C=\frac{3^{1/3}G^{2/3}M^{5/3}\nu}{2^{2/3}}\,,

(88)

and the integration over the frequencies has been truncated at
\omega=\omega_{\rm max}
corresponding to the entrance of the particle in the strong field region.

V
Gravitational wave equation and Teukolsky formalism

In this section we follow Ref.
Sasaki:2003xr
for notation and conventions, i.e. the main reference review on this topic.
In fact it includes the procedure to determine the so called Mano-Suzuki-Takasugi (MST)
Mano:1996vt

type solution of the homogeneous wave equation (Teukolsky equation,
Teukolsky:1974yv
) which incorporate the correct boundary conditions both at the horizon and at spatial infinity.
Let us denote these homogeneous solutions as
R^{\rm in}_{lm\omega}(r)
(ingoing at the horizon) and
R^{\rm up}_{lm\omega}(r)
(outgoing at the spatial infinity).
For radially infalling particles, the source term reduces to

\displaystyle T_{lm\omega}(r)=8\pi\mu\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\int d\theta e^{i\omega t}

(89)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Bigg[{-}\frac{1}{2}L_{1}^{\dagger}\Bigg\{L_{2}^{\dagger}\left({}_{-2}Y_{lm}\right)\Bigg\}C_{nn}r^{4}\delta(r{-}r(t))\delta(\theta-\theta(t))\Bigg]\,,

\displaystyle C_{nn}=\frac{(E+\frac{dr}{d\tau})^{2}}{4r^{2}\dot{t}}\,,

\displaystyle L_{s}^{\dagger}=\partial_{\theta}-\frac{m}{\sin\theta}+s\cot\theta\,,

where
{}_{-2}Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)
is the
s=-2
spin weighted spherical harmonic obeying the equation

\displaystyle 0=\frac{1}{\sin(\theta)}\partial_{\theta}(\sin(\theta)\partial_{\theta}{}_{-2}Y_{lm})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Bigg[-\frac{(m-2\cos(\theta))^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}-2+(l+2)(l-1)\Bigg]{}_{-2}Y_{lm}\,.

The stress energy tensor specialized to equatorial motion reads

\displaystyle T_{lm\omega}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dte^{i\omega t}\delta(r-r(t))\frac{(E+\frac{dr}{d\tau})^{2}r^{2}}{\dot{t}}K_{lm}\,,\qquad

(91)

where
\tau
denotes the proper time of the particle’s orbit,
S={}_{-2}Y_{lm}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)
,
S^{\prime}=\partial_{\theta}\left({}_{-2}Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\right)|_{(\frac{\pi}{2},0)}

and

\displaystyle K_{lm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\mu\Bigg[\frac{1}{2}(l^{2}+l-2-2m^{2})S+mS^{\prime}\Bigg]

(92)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(2\pi)\frac{\mu}{2}L_{1}^{\dagger}L_{2}^{\dagger}\left({}_{-2}Y_{lm}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2\pi\mu\bar{K}_{lm}\,.

For example,

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{2,-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{2,2}=-\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{\frac{5}{\pi}}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{2,-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{2,1}=0\,,

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{2,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{15}{2\pi}}\,.

(93)

Using the geodesic equations and the fact that the particle starts its motion from rest, we have

\displaystyle T_{lm\omega}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{lm}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dte^{i\omega t}\delta(r-r(t))\left(1+\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^{2}\Delta(r)\,,

where
\Delta(r)=r(r-2M)
.

Recalling Eqs. (
3
), (
4
) and (
5
)
for a particle released from rest at infinity
we find

\displaystyle T_{lm\omega}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{lm}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dte^{i\omega t}\delta(r-r(t))(1+\frac{dr}{d\tau})^{2}r(r-2M)

(95)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{lm}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dte^{i\omega t}\delta(r{-}r(t))(\sqrt{r}{-}\sqrt{2M})^{3}\times

\displaystyle(\sqrt{r}{+}\sqrt{2M})\,,

where we have used the relation
\frac{dr}{d\tau}=-\sqrt{2M/r}
and

rf=r-2M=(\sqrt{r}-\sqrt{2M})(\sqrt{r}+\sqrt{2M})\,.

(96)

Finally, we will use the “tortoise coordinate”
Misner:1973prb

r_{*}=r+2M\ln\left(\frac{r}{2M}-1\right)\,,

(97)

as well as
the asymptotic relations valid for
r\to\infty

\displaystyle R^{\rm in}_{lm\omega}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{3}B^{\rm ref}_{lm\omega}e^{i\omega r_{*}}+\frac{1}{r}B^{\rm inc}_{lm\omega}e^{-i\omega r_{*}}\,,

\displaystyle R^{\rm up}_{lm\omega}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{\rm trans}_{lm\omega}r^{3}e^{i\omega r_{*}}\,,

(98)

implying

W_{lm\omega}=2i\omega C^{\rm trans}_{lm\omega}B^{\rm inc}_{lm\omega}\,.

(99)

Moreover
C^{\rm trans}_{lm\omega}
can be given a closed form expression

\displaystyle C^{\rm trans}_{lm\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega^{3}e^{i\epsilon\ln\epsilon}A_{-}^{\nu}

\displaystyle A_{-}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{1+i\epsilon}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}(\nu-1)}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}\epsilon}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}a_{n}\frac{(\nu-1-i\epsilon)_{n}}{(\nu+3+i\epsilon)_{n}}.

The amplitude of the wave at infinity then reads

\displaystyle R_{lm\omega}(r)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{C^{\rm trans}_{lm\omega}r^{3}e^{i\omega r_{*}}}{W_{lm\omega}}\int_{2M}^{\infty}dr^{\prime}\frac{R^{\rm in}_{lm\omega}(r^{\prime})T_{lm\omega}(r^{\prime})}{\Delta^{2}(r^{\prime})}

V.1
The needed Fourier domain results

Most of the difficulties associated with the present computation are related to the way in which certain basic “master integrals” are evaluated.
Notice that with our choice of the coordinate representation of the orbit the falling particle uses negative values of the coordinate time, starting from

VI
Concluding Remarks

We have computed analytically the energy emitted by a (either scalar or massive) particle radially falling in the Schwarzschild black hole spacetime, within the framework of gravitational self force, at the first self force accuracy level. The results, presented in Post-Newtonian expanded form, are shown here for the first time:
See Eq. (
51
) for the scalar case and Eqs. (
124
) and (
130
) for the gravitational case.
Their range of validity is limited by the Post-Newtonian assumptions, i.e., break down as soon as the perturbing particle enters the strong field region.
All the computational details are made explicit here. These results 1) have an intrinsic interest, 2) can be informative of other formalisms like the EOB formalism and 3) can be used as bed test for numerical calculations.

This work is also preliminar to forthcoming analytical study of the same problem but in the strong-field regime. The leading contribution to the power emitted at infinity in the limit
GM\omega\gg 1
was computed in
Zerilli:1970wzz
and exhibits a decreasing exponential behavior,
e^{-4\pi M\omega}
. It would be interesting to develop a systematic procedure to compute many subleading orders in the large-
GM\omega
expansion. The ultimate goal would be to match the weak- and strong-field results in their overlapping region. As shown in
Davis:1971gg
, and as can be readily anticipated from the leading behaviors
\omega^{4/3}
at
\omega\sim 0
and
e^{-4\pi M\omega}
at
\omega\gg 1
, the power spectrum exhibits a maximum in this overlap region. The aim of this future project is to provide a new analytical perspective for addressing this class of physical problems, which have traditionally been treated primarily through numerical methods.

On the other hand, this type of computation could be extended to other classes of gravitational solutions, generally in dimensions higher than four, which admit the presence of regular caps in place of an event horizon. Such geometries appear as black hole mimickers, whose underlying philosophy is to address the well-known black hole information paradox
Lunin:2001jy
. Within this broad framework, of particular interest are certain five-dimensional Einstein-Maxwell solutions, dubbed Topological Stars
Bah:2020pdz
;
Heidmann:2025pbb
;
Bianchi:2025uis
, as well as the W-soliton”
Dima:2025tjz
. These backgrounds have already been employed to study scalar perturbations
Bianchi:2024vmi
;
Bianchi:2024rod
;
Fucito:2024wlg
;
DiRusso:2025lip
;
Bianchi:2025aei
;
Bini:2025ltr
;
Bianchi:2025ydq
;
Bini:2025bll
;
Cipriani:2025ikx
, and can be further developed by completing the analysis with the study of radial infall, which indeed we plan to address in future work.

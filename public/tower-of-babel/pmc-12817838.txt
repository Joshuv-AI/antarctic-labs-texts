Multi-cohort analysis and experimental study on the function of squalene epoxidase as a ferroptosis inhibitor in HCC
Authors: Xun Yang, Yidi Wu, Qingxin Zhang, Yunfei Zhou, Shuyuan Chang, Chunhui Zhang, Dabin Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSqualene epoxidase (SQLE) drives hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) development via increased hepatic oxidative stress, a hallmark of ferroptosis. Although investigations have progressed, the precise link between SQLE and ferroptosis in HCC remains indistinct. In this study, multi-omics data were employed to identify ferroptosis-associated genes linked to SQLE in HCC, which were subsequently categorised into two prognostic risk categories. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed patients allocated to the high-risk group exhibiting markedly poorer outcomes compared to those in the low-risk group, thereby underscoring the independent prognostic value of the ferroptosis-related prognostic signature in HCC. Furthermore, MTT assays further demonstrated that overexpression of SQLE conferred resistance to the ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1 in cancer cells, while reduced expression of SQLE augmented cellular susceptibility to ferroptosis. Additionally, the expression levels of SQLE were found to positively correlate with the ferroptosis inhibitor gene PARP2. Knockdown of PARP2 significantly mitigated SQLE-induced cholesterol accumulation and cellular proliferation. This study illustrates the role of SQLE as a ferroptosis inhibitor in HCC, offering novel insights into the prognostic evaluation and molecular mechanisms underlying HCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15413-0.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, SQLE, Ferroptosis, PARP2

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) represents a prevalent and aggressive form of liver malignancy that poses a significant health burden worldwide [1]. The pathogenesis of HCC is complex and closely related to chronic hepatitis B, cirrhosis, alcohol abuse, and other factors [2]. Early HCC often has no symptoms, making it difficult to treat and resulting in a worse prognosis when diagnosed late. Therefore, the diagnosis and treatment of HCC still face great challenges. The processes of excessive hepatocyte death, necroinflammatory responses, and compensatory hyperplasia are pivotal in the pathogenesis of HCC [3]. Nevertheless, the exact mode of cell death that fuels inflammation and tumorigenesis in HCC remains a subject of debate, with evidence implicating necrosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis as potential contributors [4].

Ferroptosis constitutes a distinctive mode of programmed cell death that is instrumental in the advancement of HCC. This process is delineated by the intracellular buildup of lipid peroxides, culminating in cellular demise [5]. Multiple studies have shown that the regulation of ferroptosis contributes to cancer development. For example, the p62-Keap1-NRF2 axis regulates lipid peroxidation, oxidative stress, and iron homeostasis of HCC cells through downstream target genes including NQO1 and HO1, thereby inhibiting ferroptosis [6]. The loss of GSTZ1 promotes the activation of the NRF2 pathway, ultimately inhibiting sorafenib-triggered ferroptosis [7]. ACSL4-dependent ferroptosis serves as a biomarker of sorafenib sensitivity in HCC [8]. DMT1 activates ferroptosis and causes mitochondrial dysfunction in HCC by reprogramming iron homeostasis [9].

Understanding the mechanism of iron-dependent cell death could revolutionize our understanding of HCC and pave the way for innovative therapeutic strategies. Unfortunately, the mechanisms of ferroptosis in liver cancer remain unclear, necessitating additional studies to comprehensively delineate these mechanisms and identify therapeutic targets.

Squalene epoxidase (SQLE), a pivotal enzyme in the cholesterol biosynthetic pathway, is integral to the modulation of oxidative stress [10]. Accumulating evidence suggests that SQLE may be implicated in the pathogenesis and progression of diverse pathologies [11–16]. In HCC, SQLE functions as an oncogene and a therapeutic target [11]. As SQLE drives HCC development via increased hepatic oxidative stress and liver lipid microenvironment reprogramming, it may also be associated with ferroptosis in HCC. Recent studies have reported that SQLE functions as a regulator of ferroptosis in glioblastoma and breast cancer [17, 18], but its interaction with ferroptosis in HCC is still unclear. Investigating the function of SQLE in ferroptosis is expected to yield novel perspectives on the pathogenetic mechanisms underlying liver cancer.

In this study, we used RNA-seq data from the livers of hepatocyte Sqle tg mice, FerrDb V2, TCGA, and the HCC protein-seq database to investigate the role of SQLE as an inhibitor of ferroptosis through multi-cohort analysis. We found that SQLE activates the expression of the ferroptosis inhibitor PARP2 and shows a positive correlation with PARP2 in HCC. Reducing PARP2 expression reverses SQLE’s effects on cholesterol, cell proliferation, and the expression of DMT1 and ACSL4. Additionally, SQLE expression causes resistance to Fer-1 in liver cells, while reducing SQLE increases cancer cells’ sensitivity to ferroptosis induction.

Methods

Human samples

The human HCC and adjacent normal tissue samples analyzed in this study were archival specimens originally collected for a previous investigation and were generously provided by the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University. All subsequent molecular analyses were conducted following established protocols from our prior work [19]. Ethical approval for this study was granted by the Research Ethics Committee of the hospital specified above (Approval no. 2023-Ethical Approval No. 37), with written informed consent obtained from every participant.

Data collection

RNA-seq data and clinical information for HCC patients were retrieved from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/repository), which contained 50 normal tissues and 371 tumor samples. Normalization of the gene expression profile was achieved through the utilization of the “Desq2” R package’s scale technique. From the OEP000321 in NODE (https://www.biosino.org/node) [20], 150 pairs of tumor samples had their proteomic and clinical information downloaded. The summary of the detailed clinicopathologic features of the two cohorts were shown in Table 1. Then, 570 ferroptosis-related genes (FRGs) were identified based on the FerrDb database (http: www.zhounan.org FerrDb) and previous literature screening (Supplementary table 1) [21–25].

Acquisition of mouse RNA-seq data and liver tissues

RNA-seq data for Sqle transgenic mice were obtained from the Chinese University of Hong Kong. Liver tissue samples (3 paired samples) from Sqle knockout mice and their wild-type littermates were provided by Professor Jun Yu of the Chinese University of Hong Kong [13]. All mice (C57BL/6J background) used in this study were 10-month-old males on a normal chow diet.

Screening of FRGs regulated by SQLE

A Venn diagram was used to identify genes overlapping with FRGs among differentially expressed genes in RNA-seq data of Sqle tg mice compared to WT controls (P < 0.05). These overlapping genes were considered as potential FRGs regulated by SQLE.

LASSO cox regression model construction and survival analysis

The TCGA-LIHC patient samples were utilized as the training set to develop the prognostic model, while the OEP000321 patient samples served as the validation cohort to assess the model’s performance. Predictor variables were selected using the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression analysis through the “glmnet” R package. Based on the median risk score, both the training and validation cohorts were divided into high-risk and low-risk categories, respectively. To evaluate the predictive efficacy of this gene expression-based signature, principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted using the “prcomp” function in the “stats” R package.

To evaluate the prognostic signature, survival analysis and time-dependent ROC curve analyses were performed using the “timeROC” R package. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted to determine whether the risk score derived from the model could independently predict prognosis in HCC patients. The results were visualized using the “forestplot” package. Additionally, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was employed to examine the survival outcomes of HCC patients based on the ferroptosis-related signature.

Functional analysis

The “limma” and “clusterProfiler” packages were used to conduct Gene Ontology (GO) analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was used to predict the potential functions of single genes. Signaling pathways with a P-value below than 0.05 were considered to be significantly enriched.

Cell culture

The LO2 and HepG2 were obtained from ATCC, while MHCC97H was provided by Professor Xiaoxing Li of Sun Yat-sen University, China. All cell lines were cultured in Dulbecco’ s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Thermo Fisher Scientific) and 1% Antibiotic-Antimycotic (Gibco) at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. All cell lines were routinely confirmed to be mycoplasma-free.

Stable cell lines with SQLE overexpression were generated using liposome-based transfection. Empty vector plasmids (pcDNA3.1) were used as negative controls. For SQLE gene knockout, the following sgRNA sequences were applied:

Briefly, cells were seeded in 6-well plates. The plasmid DNA and Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen) were separately diluted in Opti-MEM medium, incubated for 5 min, then combined and incubated for an additional 20 min at room temperature to form complexes. The DNA-lipid complexes were then added dropwise to the cells. After 48 h, the culture medium was replaced with fresh medium containing the appropriate selection antibiotic to establish stable cell lines. Western blot analyses were performed to verify the success of transfection.

Cholesterol concentrations

Cells (1 × 106) were harvested, and the concentrations of total cholesterol were measured using the Cholesterol Quantification kit (ab65359, Abcam) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance was read at 450 nm. A standard curve was generated for quantification. The data were expressed as means ± SEM. All experiments were conducted three times in triplicate.

Western blot analysis

Western blot was performed as previously described [26, 27]. Total protein was extracted from the above mice liver samples or stable cell lines, and protein concentration was quantified using a BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS-PAGE. Separated proteins were then electrically transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes (GE Healthcare). After blocking with 3% BSA buffer for 1 h at room temperature, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies and then incubated for 1 h at room temperature with the secondary antibody. Finally, protein bands were visualized using ECL Plus Western blotting Detection Reagents (GE Healthcare) and imaged with a chemiluminescence imaging system. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software. Each experiment was independently repeated at least three times to ensure reproducibility. These antibodies used are as follows: anti-SQLE (1:1000, 12544-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-PARP2 (1:1000, 55149-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-DMT1 (1:1000, 20507-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-ACSL4 (1:1000, 22401-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-SLC7A11 (1:1000, 26864-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-CD98 (1:1000,15193-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-β-actin (1:1000, 66009-1-Ig, Proteintech), HRP-labeled Goat Anti-Mouse IgG(H + L) (1:10000, LF101, Epizyme Biotechnology Co., Ltd), HRP-labeled Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG(H + L) (1:10000, LF102, Epizyme Biotechnology Co., Ltd).

MTT assay

The MTT assay was employed to assess cell proliferation activity. SQLE stable expression cells (1000/well) were plated in 96-well plates. Cells were treated with PARP2 inhibitor (UPF1069) or ferroptosis inhibitor (Ferrostatin-1) and inducer (Erastin and RSL3) on the second day. At each specified time point, 20 µL of MTT solution (5 mg/mL) was introduced into each well, with subsequent incubation at 37 °C for 6 h. Upon completion, the medium was thoroughly aspirated, and 150 µL of DMSO was added to each well. The plate was then incubated for 30 min to dissolve the formazan precipitate. Finally, the absorbance at a wavelength of 570 nm was measured using a microplate reader. The cell growth curve was plotted based on the relative OD570 values. All experiments were independently repeated three times, with each condition having five technical replicates.

Measurement of lipid peroxidation (BODIPY 581/591 C11 staining)

Cells were seeded into confocal dishes and cultured for 24 h. Subsequently, the cells were incubated with fresh culture medium containing 2µM C11-BODIPY 581/591 probe (Beyotime, Cat# S0043S) at 37 °C for 30 min. After incubation, the cells were washed three times with PBS to remove excess probe and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min. Following PBS washes, the cells were mounted with an antifade mounting medium containing DAPI. Images were captured using a laser scanning confocal microscope. The fluorescence intensity of at least three random fields was quantified with ImageJ software, and the red/green fluorescence ratio was calculated to indicate the level of lipid peroxidation. The experiment was independently repeated three times.

Determination of intracellular GSH/GSSG ratio

Cells (MHCC97H and LO2) were seeded in 6-well tissue culture plates at a density of 2 × 10⁵ cells per well. After 18 h of culture, the cells were harvested. Samples were prepared following the manufacturer’s instructions of the Glutathione Assay Kit (Beyotime, Cat# S0053) to measure the total glutathione (GSH + GSSG) content. The concentrations of GSH and GSSG were determined using a standard curve and normalized to the total protein concentration. The assay was performed with three independent biological replicates.

Statistical analysis

R software version 4.3.2 was used for statistical analysis and data visualization including differential gene analysis, LASSO Cox regression analysis, gene enrichment analysis, and Survival analysis. The Spearman method was used to assess the relationship between two variables. Variables in the two groups were compared using unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-tests, and differences in cell index between the two groups were analyzed through repeated-measures ANOVA. For each test, values of P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. *P < 0.05; **P < 0.01; ***P < 0.001; ****P < 0.0001.

Results

Exploration and functional analysis of FRGs regulated by SQLE in HCC

A total of 5854 differentially expressed genes (P < 0.05) were identified in RNA-seq expression data from livers of Hepatocyte SQLE transgenic mice, including 165 FRGs (Fig. 1A). The expression of the different genes was shown in the heat map and volcano map (Figs. 1B, C and Supplementary table 2). 165 FRGs were enriched in iron-related pathways, including Chemical carcinogenesis-receptor activation, mTOR signaling pathway, and cellular response to amino acid starvation pathway (Figs. 1D and E). Correlation analysis with TCGA-LIHC data revealed that 112 genes were significantly associated with SQLE (Supplementary table 3), of which 81 genes overlapped with OEP000321 protein expression data.

Construction of A prognostic model using SQLE regulated FRGs in TCGA

We identified 22 candidate prognostic genes by univariate Cox regression analysis (Fig. 1F). LASSO Cox regression analysis was used to establish a prognostic model for the expression profiles of these 22 genes. Fifteen genes were selected as optimal markers according to the best λ value (Figs. 2A and B). According to the optimal critical expression values of these genes, the expression of other genes was significantly correlated with prognosis, except for PTEN, which did not show significance in prognosis (Supplementary fig. 1). The expression of the 15 genes is shown in the heat map (Fig. 2C). Patients were divided into a high-risk group (n = 185) or low-risk group (n = 186) based on the median cut-off value (Fig. 2D). In the TCGA cohort, the high-risk group exhibited significant associations with factors such as gender, higher tumor grade, and elevated BMI (Table 2). PCA revealed distinct distributions of the two risk groups (Fig. 2E). High-risk patients are more likely to experience earlier mortality compared to those in the low-risk group (Fig. 2F), and overall survival (OS) was significantly poorer in the high-risk group (Fig. 2G, P < 0.001). Next, the predictive performance of OS risk scores was further evaluated by a time-dependent ROC curve, yielding area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.779 at 1 year, 0.727 at 2 years, and 0.686 at 3 years (Fig. 2H).

Prognostic analysis of SQLE regulated FRGs in the OEP000321 proteome cohort

The survival analysis of 15 genes was validated at the protein level, revealing that the expression of 11 proteins in the OEP000321 cohort was significantly related to prognosis (P < 0.05, Supplementary fig. 2). Subsequently, a risk score based on the same methods as those used for the TCGA cohort was used to separate patients into high-risk (n = 75) and low-risk groups (n = 75) in the OEP000321 cohort (Fig. 3A). In the OEP000321 cohort, the high-risk group was also associated with AFP (Table 2). PCA analysis suggested that patients in both the high- and low-risk subgroups were distributed across opposite directions (Fig. 3B), consistent with the results for the TCGA cohort. Patients in the high-risk group exhibited shorter survival times compared to those in the low-risk group (Fig. 3C). Additionally, the Kaplan-Meier survival curve indicated that patients in the high-risk group had significantly worse OS compared to those in the low-risk group (Fig. 3D, P = 0.02). Moreover, the AUC for the 15 protein signatures was 0.740 at 1 year, 0.714 at 2 years, and 0.665 at 3 years (Fig. 3E).

Cox regression analysis of SQLE related prognostic model in HCC

To assess the potential of risk scores as independent prognostic factors for OS, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed, incorporating both risk scores and clinicopathological features. As shown in the forest plot, risk score was an important prognostic risk factor for HCC patients in both the TCGA cohort (HR = 2.863, 95%CI: 1.952–4.198, P < 0.001) and the OEP000321 cohort (HR = 1.940, 95%CI: 1.099–3.423, P < 0.001) (Figs. 4A and C). In multivariate Cox regression analysis, risk score remained an independent predictor of OS in both cohorts (TCGA cohort: HR = 2.774, 95%CI = 1.316–5.847, P = 0.007; OEP000321 cohort: HR = 1.417, 95%CI = 1.043–2.575, P = 0.022) (Figs. 4B and D).

GABARAPL1 and PARP2 were identified as key FRGs regulated by SQLE in HCC

We analyzed the expression data of 15 genes in HCC using RNA-seq data from TCGA and SQLE transgenic mice, as well as protein-seq data from OEP000321 (Supplementary tables 4–6). The results are shown in Figs. 5A-C; Table 3. PARP2 and GABARAPL1 exhibit a consistent expression trend in the three sets of data, with GABARAPL1 (driver) being down-regulated and negatively correlated with SQLE, and PARP2 (inhibitor) being up-regulated and positively correlated with SQLE. Therefore, we hypothesize that SQLE can inhibit the ferroptosis process of HCC through GABARAPL1 or PARP2.

To verify the biological function of PARP2 and GABARAPL1, we performed functional enrichment analysis. mRNA expression of PARP2 in HCC shows a negative correlation with ferroptosis (P = 0.007, NES=−1.546) (Fig. 6A). Both mRNA and protein expression of PARP2 in HCC have a positive correlation with cholesterol biosynthetic process (Figs. 6B and C), indicating that PARP2 may also be involved in SQLE-induced cholesterol accumulation in HCC. SQLE suppresses ferroptosis via PARP2 activation in HCC

As PARP2 is an inhibitor of ferroptosis, we confirmed its interaction with SQLE by using SQLE transgenic mice and stable expression cell lines. The mRNA of liver PARP2 was up-regulated in hepatocyte Sqle overexpression transgenic mice (Fig. 7A), and the protein of PARP2 was reduced in hepatocyte Sqle knockout mice (Fig. 7B). Consistent with these findings in vivo, SQLE overexpression in LO2 cells increased the protein expression of PARP2, while SQLE knockdown in MHCC97H cells had the opposite effect (Fig. 7C). Meanwhile, the effect of SQLE on increasing cholesterol levels and promoting cell proliferation was ameliorated by silencing PARP2 (Fig. 7D). Additionally, UPF1069, a PARP2 inhibitor, restored the protein expression of DMT1 and ACSL4, which were reduced by SQLE (Fig. 7E), and suppressed cell proliferation in a dose-dependent manner in LO2-SQLE stable cell lines (Fig. 7F).

Together, these data suggest that SQLE suppresses ferroptosis through activating PARP2-dependent ferroptosis inhibition.

Reducing SQLE in HCC enhances sensitivity to ferroptosis

Next, we assessed ferroptosis marker proteins by western blot. In HCC patient samples, the ferroptosis inhibitor PARP2 and SLC7A11 were elevated, while the ferroptosis-promoting proteins DMT1 and ACSL4 were suppressed (Fig. 8A). In Sqle ko mice, the expression of SLC7A11 was decreased and ACSL4 was increased (Fig. 8B). Concordantly, LO2-SQLE cells showed downregulated DMT1 and ACSL4 and upregulated SLC7A11, whereas SQLE knockdown in MHCC97H cells produced the opposite expression patterns of these markers (Fig. 8C). These changes in multiple ferroptosis-related proteins further supported that SQLE exerts a regulatory role in ferroptosis. Correlation analysis of SQLE with ferroptosis markers (SLC7A11, DMT1) across cohorts revealed a positive association between SQLE expression and SLC7A11 (Supplementary fig. 4). The newly defined ferroptosis activator gene DHCR7 is down-regulated by SQLE based on the protein-seq database of liver from hepatocyte Sqle overexpression mice (Supplementary fig. 5 A). We then functionally validated this phenotype. C11-BODIPY staining was performed in SQLE-overexpressing and SQLE-knockdown HCC cells. SQLE overexpression significantly reduced C11-BODIPY oxidation signals, while SQLE knockdown enhanced these signals (Supplementary fig. 6A). Additionally, GSH/GSSG ratio assay showed that SQLE overexpression increased the GSH/GSSG ratio, whereas SQLE knockdown decreased this ratio. These results suggested that SQLE is closely associated with ferroptosis regulation in HCC cells. (Supplementary fig. 6B). Finally, we explored the therapeutic implications. The ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1 treatment markedly increased intracellular cholesterol level in SQLE knockdown cells (Supplementary fig. 5B), the function of SQLE on cell proliferation was not affected by Fer-1 (Fig. 8D). Interestingly, two ferroptosis inducers, Erastin and RSL3, significantly suppressed the promoting function of SQLE (Fig. 8E), indicating that overexpressing SQLE in HCC leads to increased resistance to ferroptosis. Notably, a combination of reducing SQLE expression and inducing ferroptosis synergistically inhibited HCC cell growth (Fig. 8F). This suggests that co-targeting SQLE and ferroptosis may be a novel approach for the prevention and treatment of HCC.

Discussion

In this study, we defined the SQLE function as an inhibitor of ferroptosis in HCC by using multi-cohort analysis and experimental confirmation. SQLE overexpression causes ferroptosis resistance in HCC. One possible reason is that SQLE can suppress ferroptosis by activating PARP2 in HCC. Reducing PARP2 expression in SQLE overexpression cells restored the expression of the ferroptosis inducers DMT1 and ACSL4, decreased intracellular cholesterol levels, and suppressed cell proliferation. Reducing SQLE expression exaggerated the inhibiting effect on cell growth of Erastin and RSL3.

HCC is a highly prevalent malignancy with a high incidence and poses a significant health burden worldwide [28]. Ferroptosis has emerged as a critical mechanism in the development and progression of HCC. SQLE functions as a driver gene in various diseases through different mechanisms [11, 14, 16]. However, the precise role of SQLE in the initiation and progression of HCC, particularly through the ferroptosis pathway, remains unclear. Therefore, our aim was to screen for ferroptosis genes associated with SQLE and explore the role of SQLE in HCC and ferroptosis by constructing a prognostic model of ferroptosis-related genes. This will provide new insights into the pathogenesis of HCC.

In the gene screening, we identified 165 ferroptosis genes regulated by SQLE based on sequencing data from hepatocyte Sqle tg mice. Through the process of pathway enrichment analysis, our findings revealed that the genes in question are predominantly associated with specific biological pathways, such as Ferroptosis, the PI3K-Akt signaling cascade, the mTOR signaling pathway, as well as the metabolic process of Glutathione. These biological processes are closely related to ferroptosis, suggesting that SQLE may affect ferroptosis processes. Based on LASSO Cox regression analysis, we developed a risk prognostic model composed of 15 genes linked to the ferroptosis pathway (ALB, EIF2AK4, SRXN1, CCDC6, GABARAPL1, PRKAA2, PTEN, SIRT3, FXN, PARP16, PARP2, PARP9, PRDX1, RRM2, SRSF9). A significant proportion of these genes demonstrated altered expression profiles between HCC tumors and their adjacent non-neoplastic counterparts, influencing the clinical outcome of HCC. In the prognostic model, patients classified as high risk displayed shorter survival times and later tumor stages. ROC curve analysis validated the discriminative power of the identified features. Furthermore, validation of this risk model using proteomic data effectively distinguished HCC patients and predicted their prognosis.

7-Dehydrocholesterol, a substrate of the DHCR7 gene and a terminal metabolite of cholesterol biosynthesis, was reported as an endogenous suppressor of ferroptosis [29, 30]. Interestingly, protein-seq data from the livers of hepatocyte Sqle tg mice showed that the protein expression of DHCR7 was down-regulated by SQLE (Supplementary fig. 5 A). Hepatocyte SQLE expression reprograms liver cholesterol metabolism, especially causing the accumulation of sterol-related metabolites in the liver [13]. This suggests that SQLE contributes to ferroptosis regulation in HCC. In the present research, based on bioinformatics analysis and experimental investigation, we substantiated that SQLE is an inhibitor of ferroptosis. This novel finding lends credence to the hypothesis that the reprogramming of cholesterol metabolism is pivotal in governing ferroptosis pathways within HCC.

SQLE also functions as an NADPH oxidase, leading to a robust induction of oxidative stress and triggering HCC progression [11]. Here, based on the RNA-seq data from the livers of hepatocyte Sqle tg mice, FerrDbV2, TCGA, and the HCC protein-seq database, we found that SQLE shows a close relationship with ferroptosis genes in HCC. Experimental evidence further confirmed that SQLE functions as a ferroptosis inhibitor in HCC. The findings of this study uncover a novel mechanism underlying the oncogenic function of SQLE in HCC and bolster the notion that reactive oxygen species (ROS) serve as a significant indicator of ferroptosis.

Resisting cell death, including ferroptosis resistance, is recognized as a defining characteristic of cancer [31]. Unfortunately, the detailed mechanism of ferroptosis resistance in HCC is unclear. Here, we found that treatment with the ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1 increases intracellular cholesterol levels but has no effect on SQLE-promoted cell proliferation. Knockdown of PARP2 expression, a ferroptosis inhibitor gene, significantly decreases SQLE-induced cholesterol levels and suppresses cell proliferation in LO2-SQLE cell lines. Our data thus identify SQLE as a ferroptosis inhibitor that increases ferroptosis resistance by increasing intracellular cholesterol in HCC.

Our results reinforce the concept that ferroptosis is a therapeutic target for cancers [32]. SQLE is frequently upregulated in HCC and functions as a driver gene in tumor formation. Erastin and RSL3 treatment ameliorated SQLE’s function in promoting cell growth, indicating that targeting activated ferroptosis is a promising approach for the prevention and treatment of cancer. Moreover, SQLE is also a therapeutic target of HCC [11]. Activating ferroptosis by erastin and RSL3 showed a synergistic effect when combined with reducing SQLE in HCC. Our data suggest co-targeting SQLE and ferroptosis pathway may provide an innovative strategy for the prevention and treatment of HCC. In conclusion, we found that SQLE, a driver gene in HCC, has unraveled new links between ferroptosis and tumorigenesis in HCC. Accordingly, co-targeting SQLE and ferroptosis may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for the prevention and treatment of HCC. However, this study is not without its limitations. Our research primarily relies on public databases and sequencing data from overexpressing mice. The risk model needs further validation in future clinical cohorts.

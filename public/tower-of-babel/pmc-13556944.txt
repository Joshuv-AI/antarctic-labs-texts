Nirsevimab for respiratory syncytial virus prevention: A comprehensive product review
Authors: Josefina L. Razzini, Federico Martinon-Torres
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTInfant RSV prevention is undergoing a public-health paradigm shift toward scalable, broad-eligibility passive immunization. This review examines nirsevimab (Beyfortus®), a long-acting recombinant IgG1κ monoclonal antibody targeting the conserved site Ø of prefusion RSV F protein. Its extended half-life enables season-long protection with a single dose, overcoming key limitations of palivizumab. A validated manufacturing platform and stringent release specifications ensure consistent quality and potency. Pivotal trials demonstrated high efficacy against RSV-associated lower respiratory tract infection hospitalization (RSVH), while the pragmatic HARMONIE trial confirmed an 83.2% reduction in RSVH under routine conditions. Postlicensure studies across multiple countries report 70–90% effectiveness against RSVH and substantial reductions in intensive care admissions. Comparative 2024–2025 data further suggest greater protection than maternal RSVpreF vaccination. Nirsevimab represents a scalable public-health intervention that can be used alone or complement maternal RSV vaccination, warranting continued surveillance of effectiveness, safety, and viral escape.

Respiratory syncytial virus

Human respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a nonsegmented negative-sense, single-stranded RNA virus member of the Pneumoviridae family.1 There are only two human RSV antigenic groups, A and B, which are mostly defined by genetic variation in the G glycoprotein and also exhibit genome-wide sequence divergence.1,2 Structurally, the RSV virion consists of an enveloped nucleocapsid. The lipid envelope derived from the host cell plasma membrane contains three viral transmembrane surface glycoproteins: the glycoprotein G, the fusion protein F, and the SH protein. The G and the F glycoproteins intervenes in the RSV attachment and entry.1,3 The F protein mediates the fusion between the virus envelope and the host cell membrane; it has two main conformational states (prefusion and postfusion) and contains multiple epitopes able to elicit an immune response.1,2 F0 is the precursor protein that is cleaved by proteases in two subunits, F1 and F2. These subunits remain associated, and three F1/F2 complexes assemble into the metastable trimer pre-F conformation.1–3 During fusion, the F1 subunit extends and penetrates the host cell membrane, triggering the collapse of the pre-fusion (preF) state and subsequently forming a stable post-F conformation.1–3 Antigenic sites I, II (the binding site of palivizumab), III, and IV are present in both pre- and postfusion structures, whereas antigenic sites Ø and V (also referred to as site VIII) are exclusively exposed in the pre-fusion conformation.4 In addition, the RSV proteome includes structural proteins (L, N, P, M1, M2.1 and M2.2) and two non-structural proteins (NS1 and NS2), that interfere with the host type I interferon system, thereby modulating the immune response.1,3,5,6

Gene transcription and replication occur within the cell cytoplasm. Throughout the infection cycle, viral mRNAs and proteins become detectable at 4–6 h postinfection (hpi), with progeny release between 10 hpi and 48 hpi, and reaching peak accumulation by 24 hpi. The main cytopathic effect is the formation of syncytia, which is significantly more prominent in cell lines in vitro than in differentiated, polarized epithelium in vivo.1

RSV infection

RSV infection occurs via aerosol particles or direct contact.7 Once in the respiratory tract, the virus exhibits tropism for respiratory epithelial cells, primarily infecting ciliated bronchiolar epithelial cells and type I pneumocytes in the alveoli, which results in airway inflammation.8 Several immune cells are rapidly recruited to the infected tissue. Neutrophils dominate the infiltrate and contribute to viral control through phagocytosis, release of granule enzymes, and the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs). NK cells are activated early, killing infected cells and producing IFN-γ, which supports Th1-type immunity. Group 2 innate lymphoid cells (ILC2s) contribute to local inflammation activating type 2 inflammatory response.5,9 In infants, immune responses to RSV are further influenced by the immaturity of both innate and adaptive immunity. Early-life immune responses are characterized by a predominance of regulatory and Th2 responses over antiviral Th1 responses, limited immunologic memory, and incomplete protection from maternally derived antibodies, which wane during the first months of life. These factors may increase susceptibility to severe RSV disease and provide a rationale for passive immunization strategies in early infancy.10

Airway injury during RSV infection arises predominantly from the host inflammatory response rather than from direct viral cytopathology, as RSV, unlike many other respiratory viruses, induces relatively limited direct epithelial cytotoxicity and structural damage.5,9,11 Instead, the clinical manifestations of RSV infection are mainly driven by airway obstruction, particularly in young infants, who are at the highest risk of severe disease. Airflow limitation results from a combination of mechanical obstruction by sloughed epithelial cells, inflammatory debris, fibrin, mucus, and lymphoid aggregates, as well as increased airway hypersensitivity, leading to wheezing and hypoxia.9

RSV burden

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) infection is the leading cause of lower respiratory tract infections worldwide. It is estimated that by the age of 2 y, most children experience an RSV infection.12 The greatest burden affects infants younger than 6 months of age, particularly those living in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Global estimates for 2019 indicate approximately 33.0 million episodes of RSV-associated acute lower respiratory tract infection, 3.6 million related hospital admissions, and about 26,300 in-hospital deaths among children aged 0–60 months. Of these, infants younger than 6 months accounted for an estimated 6.6 million LRTI episodes, 1.4 million hospitalizations, and 13,300 in-hospital deaths. Overall, RSV is a major contributor to child mortality, representing approximately 2% of deaths in children younger than 60 months and about 4% in those aged 28 d to 6 months.13

In most parts of the world, RSV exhibits seasonal activity with a latitudinal gradient within each hemisphere, leading to increased demand on health-care systems during seasonal peaks.14–16 This high clinical burden translates into a considerable economic impact. In 2017, the global economic burden of RSV-related LRTI was estimated at €4.83 billion, including both inpatient and outpatient care in children younger than 5 y. Of this total, 55% represented hospitalization costs, and 65% was generated in developing countries.17 Furthermore, because RSV infection may lead to prolonged respiratory morbidity following the initial episode, outpatient costs may also increase considerably beyond the acute phase.18 Among high-risk infants with severe RSV disease, hospitalization costs were substantially higher.17 However, most RSV-related hospitalizations occur in previously healthy term infants, making them major contributors to the overall economic burden of disease.18,19

RSV prevention with monoclonal antibodies

Prior to the approval of nirsevimab, palivizumab was the only licensed prophylactic agent against RSV disease in infants, restricted to high-risk populations and requiring five monthly intramuscular injections per season.

Several monoclonal antibodies targeting RSV were evaluated in clinical development but did not achieve regulatory approval. Suptavumab (REGN2222), a fully human monoclonal antibody targeting site V of the preF protein, failed to meet the primary endpoint in a Phase III trial (NCT02325791); sequencing of RSV B isolates revealed a newly circulating mutant strain carrying two amino acid substitutions in the suptavumab epitope, resulting in loss of neutralizing activity.20 Motavizumab, a second-generation derivative of palivizumab engineered with 13 amino acid modifications to increase binding affinity, demonstrated efficacy but failed to show clear clinical superiority over palivizumab and raised concerns regarding an increased risk of hypersensitivity reactions, limiting its regulatory approval by the FDA.21,22

In 2022, the World Health Organization (WHO) published preferred product characteristics for monoclonal antibodies aimed at preventing severe RSV disease in infants, prioritizing protection for all infants who will be less than 6 months of age while RSV is circulating, through a single dose with at least 70% efficacy for approximately 5 months against both RSV subtypes, and with delivery strategies adapted to local seasonality and programmatic feasibility in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs).23

Nirsevimab was the first product to meet these criteria, receiving EU marketing authorization on 31 October 2022, followed by US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval on 17 July 2023, and has since been authorized in more than 50 countries.24,25

Nirsevimab

Origin and design

Nirsevimab (MEDI8897) is a recombinant human immunoglobulin G1κ monoclonal antibody with an extended half-life, that targets the prefusion conformation of the RSV fusion (F) protein. It was optimized from the antibody D25, with five amino acid substitutions (P28L, R30E, N31D, A60G, and T100bE) in the mAb complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) and four germline reversions in the framework sequences of the heavy chain, improving its neutralizing activity and reducing the potential for immune-response elicitation.26

Nirsevimab’s heavy and light chains interact with the F1 and F2 subunits, with the second interaction likely accounting for its enhanced potency.26 It binds to site Ø, an antigen site that is also targeted by most of the RSV-neutralizing response in humans, stabilizing the prefusion conformation of F and blocking conformational transition and viral entry. Site Ø is one of the most variable regions on the F glycoprotein between RSV subtypes.3,27 However, sequence analysis revealed that specific amino acids targeted by nirsevimab are >99% conserved, accounting for its broader neutralizing activity.26

Indications

Nirsevimab has been approved for the prevention of RSV lower respiratory tract disease in neonates and infants born during or entering their first RSV season; and children up to 24 months of age who remain vulnerable to severe RSV disease through their second RSV season.28,29

For infants during their first RSV season, nirsevimab is administered as a single intramuscular dose of 50 mg in those weighing <5 kg and 100 mg in those weighing ≥5 kg, given at birth if born during the RSV season or prior to season onset if born outside of it. For children who remain at increased risk of severe RSV disease during their second season, a single 200-mg dose (two 100 mg intramuscular injections) is recommended before the start of the season. Additional dosing recommendations apply to children undergoing cardiopulmonary bypass surgery.28,29

Quality, production, and release characterization

Nirsevimab is a recombinant human IgG1κ glycoprotein monoclonal antibody directed against the prefusion conformation of the RSV F protein. Nirsevimab is marketed under the name Beyfortus® and is licensed to be manufactured at AstraZeneca Pharmaceuticals, LP in Frederick, Maryland, USA and at Samsung Biologics Co., Ltd., Incheon, Republic of Korea. AstraZeneca AB, located on Gärtunavägen, Sweden, is responsible for batch release.30

Public regulatory documents indicate that the active substance is produced in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells (CHO-K1) using a two-tiered master-cell-bank/working-cell-bank system. After thawing of the working cell bank, cells are expanded in suspension culture through a seed-train process and transferred to production bioreactors. The harvested cell-culture fluid undergoes a multistep downstream process, including chromatography, ultrafiltration/diafiltration, viral inactivation, and viral filtration. Comparability exercises performed during development supported consistency across manufacturing changes in scale and site.24,29

The final drug product is a liquid formulation supplied in single-use prefilled glass syringes for intramuscular administration. The drug product is supplied as a sterile, preservative-free, clear to opalescent, colorless to yellow solution for intramuscular injection. It is available in 50 mg/0.5 mL and 100 mg/1 mL pre-filled syringe presentation. Excipients include l-arginine hydrochloride, l-histidine, l-histidine hydrochloride, polysorbate 80, sucrose, and water for injection. Publicly available regulatory assessments report that release and stability specifications include tests for identity, purity and impurities, quantity, potency, appearance, pH, osmolality, visible and subvisible particles, bacterial endotoxins, and sterility, using validated analytical methods consistent with contemporary compendial and regulatory expectations. Stability data support a shelf life of 4 y when stored at 2–8°C; after removal from refrigeration, the product may remain at room temperature for up to 48 h and should then be used or discarded.30 Because nirsevimab is a biological medicinal product, traceability by product name and batch number should be ensured in routine use.24,29

Preclinical studies

In vitro models

In vitro studies revealed that nirsevimab (MEDI8897*) has markedly improved neutralizing potency against RSV compared with previously developed monoclonal antibodies. Nirsevimab showed approximately fourfold greater potency than its precursor antibody D25, and 20-fold and 150-fold greater potency than motavizumab and palivizumab, respectively, against the RSV A2 and RSV B9320 laboratory strains.26

When tested against a broader panel of clinical isolates (59 RSV A and 43 RSV B strains collected between 2003 and 2013 in multiple countries), nirsevimab demonstrated approximately 50-fold greater neutralizing activity than palivizumab, confirming its strong antiviral activity across diverse RSV variants.26

In essence, these in vitro findings establish a superior pharmacological profile for nirsevimab, exhibiting a highly potent antiviral activity against a broad spectrum of circulating RSV variants.

In vivo models

The prophylactic efficacy of MEDI8897* was evaluated in a cotton rat model of RSV infection, a well-established preclinical system historically used to characterize and benchmark the prophylactic activity of monoclonal antibodies against RSV, including palivizumab. MEDI8897* showed dose-dependent antiviral activity in preventing RSV replication in the lungs of infected animals. Compared with palivizumab at similar doses, MEDI8897* displayed greater potency, resulting in viral titers more than 1–2 logs lower in lung tissues. In addition, MEDI8897* markedly reduced viral titers in the noses of infected cotton rats, whereas palivizumab did not inhibit viral replication in the upper airways even when administered at higher doses.26

At similar serum concentrations, MEDI8897* was approximately ninefold more potent than palivizumab at reducing pulmonary viral loads by more than 3 logs in cotton rats infected with either RSV A or RSV B.26

Safety and pharmacokinetic properties of nirsevimab were evaluated in cynomolgus monkeys. In Good Laboratory Practice toxicology studies, they received repeated intramuscular or intravenous doses of the antibody. No local or systemic adverse effects were observed.26

These in vivo results translate nirsevimab’s potent neutralizing activity into superior viral clearance in both upper and lower airways, while demonstrating a highly acceptable safety and tolerability profile in preclinical toxicology studies.

Clinical development program and postauthorization studies of nirsevimab.

Phase Ib/IIa

The safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics of nirsevimab were evaluated in the pediatric population, phase Ib/IIa, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-escalation study (NCT02290340).32 Healthy preterm infants born between 32 and 34 wGA, entering their first RSV season and not recommended to receive palivizumab under existing guidelines, were randomized 4:1 to receive a single intramuscular dose of nirsevimab (10, 25, or 50 mg) or placebo, with sequential dose escalation.

Peak serum concentration was achieved within 7 d, and levels remained above the 90% effective target of 6.8 μg/mL, previously established from the cotton rat model, through d 151, supporting season-long protection with a single dose, consistent with a prolonged half-life of 62.5–72.9 d which is longer than the 19–27 d for palivizumab.32,33 In subjects with ADA detected, the presence of ADA had no effect on pharmacokinetics or on any safety parameters. Adverse events were comparable between groups, and no SAEs related to nirsevimab were reported.

Healthy infants

The clinical efficacy of nirsevimab in healthy infants was evaluated across two pivotal trials spanning different gestational age groups, subsequently analyzed in a pooled prespecified analysis.34,35

In a multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled phase IIb trial (NCT02878330), Griffin et al. reported that nirsevimab demonstrated an efficacy of 70.1% (95% confidence interval [CI], 52.3–81.2; p < .001) in preventing medically attended RSV-associated lower respiratory tract infection (LRTI) over the 150 d following administration.34 The study evaluated a single 50-mg intramuscular dose of nirsevimab vs. placebo (2:1) in healthy preterm infants born between 29 wGA and 34.6 wGA. In addition, nirsevimab showed an acceptable safety profile, with no notable hypersensitivity reactions and adverse events similar to that to the placebo.34 The fixed-dose regimen was later replaced by a weight-banded dosing strategy (50 mg for infants weighing <5 kg and 100 mg for those weighing ≥5 kg) based on pharmacokinetic and exposure-response analyses conducted for that trial and the MELODY trial.35,36 This dosing approach was subsequently adopted across all studies.34,36

The MELODY study was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase III trial.35 It evaluated the efficacy and safety of nirsevimab in healthy late-preterm and term infants (≥35 weeks gestational age [wGA]) when administered before the first RSV season. Findings showed an estimated efficacy of 74.5% (95% CI, 49.6 to 87.1; p < .001) on medically attended RSV-associated LRTI through 150 d after the injection.37 No safety concerns were reported. A post-hoc analysis indicated that nirsevimab provided protection against both RSV single infections and coinfections, with no indication that RSV was replaced by other respiratory viruses.38

A pooled prespecified analysis from both trials estimated 79.5% efficacy against the primary case definition of medically attended RSV LRTI in the target population using the indicated weight-banded dose.34–36 In addition, it supported efficacy extrapolation for infants with chronic lung disease, congenital heart disease, or extreme preterm birth (<29 wGA).36,38

The clinical development program was subsequently strengthened by the phase IIIb HARMONIE trial, a pragmatic, randomized comparison of nirsevimab vs. standard care (i.e., routine clinical management without additional trial-specific interventions) conducted in routine settings in France, Germany, and the United Kingdom.39 In the primary analysis, nirsevimab reduced hospitalization due to RSV-associated lower respiratory tract infection through RSV season by 83.2% and very severe RSV-associated lower respiratory tract infection by 75.7%, confirming effectiveness under conditions closely approximating programmatic use. Importantly, a prespecified 180 d analysis showed that protection against RSV-associated lower respiratory tract infection hospitalization remained 82.7% at 6 months, extending beyond the conventional 5-month RSV season and supporting greater flexibility for implementation in both seasonal and year-round settings.39

Special populations

High risk infants – MEDLEY trial

The MEDLEY study (NCT03959488) was a randomized, double-blind, palivizumab-controlled, phase II/III trial designed to evaluate the safety and pharmacokinetics of nirsevimab infants at higher-risk of severe RSV-related LRTI.40 Eligible participants included infants with chronic heart disease (CHD), congenital lung disease (CLD) or born < 35 wGA, and who are eligible to receive palivizumab. Infants were randomly assigned to receive a weight-banded dose of nirsevimab followed by four once-monthly doses of placebo or five once-monthly intramuscular doses of palivizumab. The MEDLEY and MELODY trials reported similar serum nirsevimab levels at d 151, providing the basis for efficacy extrapolation from healthy infants to high-risk populations.35,40 Safety was assessed over 361 d of follow-up, and the incidence of adverse events was comparable between treatment groups. Thus, in higher-risk populations, the available evidence supports use of nirsevimab, although in some subgroup’s efficacy is informed by extrapolation rather than direct clinical trial evidence. Regulatory documents indicate that the safety profile in infants with chronic lung disease of prematurity or haemodynamically significant congenital heart disease was broadly comparable to that of palivizumab, supporting use in these groups. However, dosing in infants weighing 1.0 to <1.6 kg is based on extrapolation, no clinical data are available for infants weighing <1 kg, and exposure is expected to be higher in this group. Data are also limited in extremely preterm infants younger than 8 weeks with gestational age <29 weeks and absent in those with postmenstrual age <32 weeks. Consequently, international guidelines vary significantly; while the Spanish Society of Neonatology recommends withholding administration until the infant reaches 1000 g,41 other clinical protocols advise avoiding its use altogether in infants under 1.6 kg or <29 weeks corrected gestational age or prioritizing administration strictly in the immediate pre-discharge window to mitigate pharmacokinetic uncertainty while ensuring post-hospitalization protection.42 This clinical dilemma is further compounded by the widespread discontinuation or obsolescence of palivizumab across numerous national healthcare systems, which deprives clinicians of a weight-adjusted alternative. Thus, utilization in this high-risk cohort must be strictly individualized based on clinical status, local institutional availability and protocols.

Immunocompromised children – MUSIC trial

The MUSIC trial was a phase II, open-label study that evaluated the safety, pharmacokinetics (PK), and emergence of antidrug antibodies (ADA) in immunocompromised children younger than 24 months entering their first or second RSV season.43 Participants received the weight-banded dose when entering their first RSV or a fixed dose of 200 mg when entering their second RSV season. During the 12-month follow-up postdose, no safety concerns were reported and nirsevimab serum levels were similar to those reported in previous studies with previously healthy term and preterm infants enrolled in MELODY trial.35,43 Therefore, in immunocompromised infants and children, evidence remains supportive but less definitive than in otherwise healthy infants. The MUSIC study and regulatory assessments indicate that nirsevimab was generally well tolerated and that serum concentrations were broadly compatible with efficacy extrapolation; however, in some immunocompromised children, particularly those with protein-losing conditions, increased clearance was observed. Despite this accelerated clearance, 13 out 14 participants maintained RSV neutralizing antibody titers (>1000 UI/mL) through d 151, exceeding the historical protective threshold established for palivizumab (≈705 IU/mL) and preventing protocol-defined medically attended RSV LRTI. Currently, regulatory frameworks do not recommend formal clinical countermeasures such as redosing, interval shortening, or routine antibody titer monitoring for this subgroup. Consequently, while these data indicate that this subset of patients still derives significant clinical benefit from nirsevimab, the magnitude or duration of protection may be attenuated, warranting optimal seasonal timing and heightened clinical surveillance.

Safety

Across all clinical trials, nirsevimab demonstrated a consistently favorable safety profile.34,35,40,43 The types and frequencies of AEs were similar between nirsevimab and comparator groups in all studies, with events predominantly grade 1–2 in severity. No treatment-related SAEs were reported in any trial, and no anaphylaxis or clinically significant hypersensitivity reactions were observed in healthy infant populations. SAE rates were numerically lower in nirsevimab recipients than in placebo recipients in both the Phase IIb trial (11.2% vs. 16.9%) and MELODY (6.8% vs. 7.3%); however, these differences should be interpreted descriptively, as no statistical comparisons were reported.34,35 In special populations, the safety profile was consistent with that of healthy infants and comparable to palivizumab in MEDLEY, and consistent with the expected profile for immunocompromised children in MUSIC, with no treatment-related SAEs in either study.40,43 No RSV-related or treatment-related deaths were recorded across the pivotal trials.

Resistance analysis

Since even a single amino acid substitution can significantly impact antibody binding, monoclonal antibodies targeting highly conserved epitopes are preferred, as they reduce the risk of emerging antibody-resistant viruses.4 In this context, although extended geo-temporal studies (2015–2021) demonstrated that the nirsevimab binding site has remained highly conserved for decades, its widespread use could still exert selective pressure on RSV.44 Supporting this concern, susceptibility to neutralization by nirsevimab was evaluated in vitro using RSV monoclonal antibody (mAb)–resistant mutants (MARMs).45 Zhu et al. showed that specific amino acid substitutions in recombinant RSV F proteins were associated with reduced susceptibility. In the recombinant RSV (rRSV) A2 variant, the N67I/N208Y combination was associated with reduced susceptibility, whereas in the rRSV B9320 variant, substitutions N208D, N208S, K68N/N201S, and K68N/N208S were associated with higher levels of resistance. These resistance-associated mutations reduced or abolished binding affinity and correlated significantly with decreased viral neutralization. However, escape variants and their parental viruses exhibited similar replication rates in vitro, suggesting that these substitutions do not confer a growth advantage. Moreover, the frequency of these mutations in circulating viruses is low, similar to that observed for palivizumab.

Molecular and phenotypic analysis of RSV isolates from pivotal trials (Phase IIb and MELODY trials) confirms that over 99% of infections remained highly susceptible to nirsevimab.34,35,46 While minor substitutions in the F protein were observed, the most prevalent variant in RSV B (I206M:Q209R) increased neutralizing potency via enhanced electrostatic interactions. Resistance-associated substitutions remained clinically rare, occurring in fewer than 1% of participants across both studies.46

A multicenter and observational study in France during the 2023/24 RSV season analyzed 545 full-length RSV genomes of RSV-infected infants following widespread nirsevimab implementation.47 No resistance-associated substitutions were identified in the RSV-A analysis. Among RSV-B breakthrough cases, two resistance-associated substitutions (N208D and I64M/K65R) with high level of resistance in the fusion-inhibition assay were detected. Remarkably, in a large-scale genomic and phenotypic analysis of RSV breakthrough infections following nirsevimab prophylaxis during the 2024/25 season, the same group identified 419 full-length RSV genome sequences, among which 1.0% of RSV-A and 12.5% of RSV-B variants exhibited intermediate to markedly reduced susceptibility to nirsevimab.48 Among the resistant RSV-B variants, 52.2% carried a substitution at residue 208, underscoring the critical role of this residue in RSV-B escape. Additional novel substitutions, including F:I64V/F:K65E, F:K68I, F:L204S, and F:P205S, were also identified. In RSV-A, F:K209E was the only resistance-associated substitution detected. Notably, no resistant RSV variants were identified in nirsevimab-naive infants. Although resistance-associated variants remain rare based on the currently available evidence, the identification of additional resistance-associated substitutions during the 2024/25 season supports the need for ongoing molecular surveillance to monitor the long-term impact of universal prophylaxis.

Real-world evidence

Postlicensure studies have rapidly confirmed that the efficacy observed in prelicensure trials translates into routine clinical practice. In a systematic review and meta-analysis of 32 case-control studies from five high-income countries, Sumsuzzman et al. demonstrated that nirsevimab was associated with lower odds of RSV-related hospitalization (OR 0.17; 95% CI 0.12–0.23), ICU admission (OR 0.19; 95% CI 0.12–0.29), and LRTI incidence (OR 0.25; 95% CI 0.19–0.33) in infants younger than 12 months, although length of hospital stay did not differ significantly between groups (WMD 0.01; 95% CI −0.63 to 0.65).49 The same group further evaluated the broader respiratory burden in a meta-analysis of 15 studies encompassing 264,286 infants, finding that nirsevimab was associated with reduced odds of all-cause LRTI hospitalization (OR 0.38; 95% CI 0.28–0.53), all-cause LRTI emergency department visits (OR 0.52; 95% CI 0.37–0.73), and RSV-related LRTI emergency department visits (OR 0.24; 95% CI 0.13–0.47), though no significant effect was observed on all-cause hospitalization overall (OR 0.56; 95% CI 0.14–2.20).50 The Galician NIRSE-GAL longitudinal population-based study with +94% nirsevimab coverage had first showed strong protection against RSV-related LRTI hospitalization; full-season analysis then reported 70.7% effectiveness (95% CI, 42.4–85.1) against RSV-related LRTI hospitalization and 80.3% (95% CI, 54.6–91.5) against RSV hospitalization requiring oxygen support.51,52 Compared with non-pandemic historical seasons, immunization contributed to a median reduction of 89.2% (IQR, 89.1–91.4) in RSV-related LRTI hospitalizations overall and 95.2% (IQR, 94.8–96.2) among infants born during the season. Across two RSV seasons, the program was reported to reduce RSV-related LRTI hospitalizations by 85.9% (95% CI, 80.2–90.0) in the first season and by 55.3% (95% CI, 22.5–74.3) in the second season, supporting a persistent public-health effect beyond the administration of nirsevimab during the first season.53 The same study also documented reductions in recurrent hospitalizations and outpatient respiratory morbidity, arguing against a simple “season-limited” interpretation of benefit.

In France, nirsevimab was associated with 83.0% effectiveness (95% CI, 73.4–89.2) against hospitalization for RSV bronchiolitis, while a separate national study found 75.9% effectiveness (95% CI, 48.5–88.7) against RSV bronchiolitis requiring pediatric intensive care.54,55 In Catalonia, population-based data showed adjusted hazard ratios of 0.124 (95% CI, 0.086–0.179) for hospital admission and 0.099 (95% CI, 0.041–0.237) for ICU admission due to RSV bronchiolitis.56 In Western Australia, the adjusted effectiveness against RSV-associated ARI hospitalization was 88.2% (95% CI, 73.5–94.7), and in the United States early postintroduction surveillance estimated 90% effectiveness (95% CI, 75–96) against RSV-associated hospitalization.57,58 More recently, a 27-hospital US case-control investigation found that nirsevimab was 80% effective against RSV-associated ICU admission and 83% effective against acute respiratory failure among infants admitted to intensive care during their first RSV season.59 Taken together, these data support a discussion of nirsevimab not only as an efficacious product, but as a scalable public-health intervention with reproducible effectiveness across health systems.

Other respiratory outcomes

Beyond the prevention of acute RSV disease, emerging evidence suggests that RSV immunoprophylaxis may also have mid-term respiratory benefits. RSV-related LRTI in early life has been consistently associated with an increased risk of recurrent wheezing and later asthma, although the causal nature of this relationship remains debated.18 In this context, a systematic review and meta-analysis of RSV monoclonal antibody prophylaxis suggested a possible reduction in recurrent wheeze or asthma, particularly after exclusion of studies at high risk of bias, although overall estimates were imprecise and heterogeneity was substantial.60 Notably, individual trial evidence has been inconsistent across agents, with palivizumab prophylaxis showing a reduction in recurrent wheezing in preterm infants, whereas a randomized controlled trial of motavizumab did not demonstrate a significant effect on wheezing outcomes.21,61,62 More recently, population-based evidence from the NIRSE-GAL study has provided real-world data supporting a reduction in subsequent respiratory morbidity following universal nirsevimab implementation, including decreases in wheezing- and asthma-related primary-care consultations during the first RSV season.53 However, effects on broader respiratory outcomes in later seasons were less consistent, and the extent to which early-life prevention of RSV translates into durable modification of recurrent wheezing or asthma trajectories remains uncertain.

Other available products

Palivizumab

Palivizumab (Synagis®) is a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting antigenic site II of the RSV fusion (F) protein and was, for decades, the only approved prophylactic option against RSV.63,64 It has demonstrated efficacy in reducing RSV-related hospitalizations in high-risk infants, including those born prematurely, with congenital heart disease or chronic lung disease (reduced RSV hospitalization by 55% in preterm infants/BPD (IMpact-RSV)) and by 45% in infants with CHD.65,66 Its clinical use is limited by pharmacokinetic properties, with a relatively short serum half-life, which necessitates monthly intramuscular administration to maintain protective levels throughout the RSV season. It has an established safety profile, with no major safety concerns identified across clinical trials and postmarketing experience; however, its programmatic implementation remains constrained by dosing frequency, restriction to high-risk infants, and cost, limiting scalability and broader population-level impact.67–69 More recently, palivizumab use has declined following the introduction of nirsevimab, which is now recommended as the preferred prophylactic option for most infants in several countries, including the United States.70

Clesrovimab

Clesrovimab is a human monoclonal antibody that incorporates three amino acid substitutions in the Fc region (M252Y, S254T, and T256E; YTE), which extend its half-life and allow for once-per-season administration. It binds antigenic site IV of the RSV preF protein, a region presents in both prefusion and postfusion conformations. This prophylactic target exhibits a high degree of sequence conservation, supporting its potential to reduce viral escape.4,71 In a phase Ib/IIa ascending-dose study in infants, clesrovimab exhibited a safety profile comparable to placebo and a half-life of approximately 45 d.71 In infants at increased risk for severe RSV disease, a single dose showed similar safety and efficacy outcomes to monthly palivizumab in preventing RSV-associated illness.72 These findings supported its approval by the FDA in June 2025 and by the EMA in April 2026.

RSV preF maternal vaccine

The bivalent RSV prefusion F protein-based maternal vaccine (RSVpreF; Abrysvo®) was approved by the EMA in July 2023 for administration between 24 and 36 wGA to protect infants through maternal–fetal antibody transfer. Later, on August 2023, the FDA approved its use between 32 and 36 wGA.

A large population-based comparative effectiveness study using the French National Health Data System (SNDS) during the 2024–2025 season compared both interventions in 42,560 matched infants born during the RSV season. Nirsevimab was associated with a lower risk of RSV-LRTI hospitalization compared with maternal RSVpreF vaccination (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.61–0.88), with more pronounced differences for severe outcomes including PICU admission (HR 0.58; 95% CI 0.42–0.80), oxygen therapy requirement (HR 0.56; 95% CI 0.38–0.81), and ventilation support (HR 0.57; 95% CI 0.40–0.81).73 These findings suggest that while both strategies provide meaningful protection, nirsevimab may offer superior effectiveness in preventing severe RSV outcomes, potentially reflecting more consistent serum antibody levels compared with maternally transferred antibodies, whose concentrations may vary depending on gestational age at vaccination, placental transfer efficiency, and interval between vaccination and delivery. Moreover, a retrospective cohort study showed that nirsevimab uptake remained high even among individuals who declined standard prenatal vaccines, suggesting that passive infant immunization may be more acceptable to vaccine-hesitant populations.74

Although nirsevimab offers direct infant protection independent of gestational timing or placental transfer efficiency, its affordability in LMICs remains uncertain. Conversely, maternal RSVpreF vaccination may be more practical in settings with strong prenatal programs but limited postnatal follow-up. However, its narrow administration window (28 or 32 to 36 wGA) requires reliable prenatal care, and efficacy may be reduced in preterm infants, precisely the population at highest risk in LMICs, due to suboptimal placental antibody transfer. For LMIC settings, a combined strategy may offer the broadest population-level coverage, as the two approaches are not mutually exclusive.73

Cost-effectiveness and affordability are particularly important in LMICs and are strongly dependent on local RSV burden, seasonality, health-care access, delivery costs, program coverage and the negotiated product price. Modeling studies in Vietnam and across multiple LMICs suggest that both maternal RSV vaccination and long-acting monoclonal antibodies could be cost-effective if available at sufficiently low prices; however, these analyses generally used hypothetical prices of approximately US$3.5–25 per dose, with the most favorable results often observed at around US$5 per dose.75,76 These assumptions are substantially below currently reported nirsevimab prices in high-income markets and should therefore be interpreted as threshold or target prices rather than established LMIC procurement prices. Thus, economic conclusions cannot be generalized across countries, and national decisions will require country-specific cost-effectiveness and budget-impact analyses based on actual negotiated prices.

Clesrovimab is a second long-acting anti-RSV monoclonal antibody that has been recommended by ACIP as an alternative to nirsevimab for infants aged <8 months who are entering their first RSV season and who are not protected through maternal RSV vaccination.71,72 However, as its clinical implementation has only recently begun, real-world evidence on its effectiveness, safety, and impact on RSV epidemiology is still lacking.

Current evidence indicates that resistance-associated substitutions identified for nirsevimab and clesrovimab are mostly confined to residues within their respective binding epitopes, suggesting that these antibodies may select for distinct viral escape pathways. Following the widespread implementation of nirsevimab, resistant variants, although uncommon, have been detected in RSV-B breakthrough infections.48 In contrast, resistance to clesrovimab has so far been reported mainly in experimental settings, where four resistant mutants were selected in vitro. One of these variants, the RSV-A K433M substitution, was subsequently identified in a single isolate from a prospective pediatric cohort in Germany collected before the widespread use of anti-RSV monoclonal antibodies (mAbs).77 The same study also described additional naturally occurring variants, although their impact on clesrovimab susceptibility remains unclear. Taken together, the available evidence is currently insufficient to determine whether clesrovimab could serve as a strategic alternative in the event of rising nirsevimab-resistant escape mutants.

Continued genomic and phenotypic surveillance will be essential to clarify the clinical relevance of emerging resistance-associated variants and to inform the future positioning of these monoclonal antibodies in RSV prevention strategies.

Maternal RSVpreF vaccination and nirsevimab can be considered complementary, context-dependent strategies that allow parental choice and offer different approaches to RSV disease prevention. Maternal immunization provides indirect infant protection through transplacental antibody transfer, whereas nirsevimab provides direct passive immunization to the infant. Current WHO and ACIP frameworks broadly support protecting most infants through one of these two approaches rather than routinely using both products together.78,79 The final choice depends on timing, antenatal coverage, implementation platform, seasonality, and local policy. Importantly, maternal vaccination alone cannot provide protection for all infants, as nirsevimab remains necessary for certain groups, including very preterm infants, infants whose mothers were not vaccinated, and children at increased risk requiring protection during a second RSV season.80,81 Consequently, maternal vaccination programs require a parallel monoclonal antibody infrastructure, whereas a universal nirsevimab strategy can function as a stand-alone program. These differences also affect program complexity, logistics, and tracking requirements. Real-world experience suggests that universal nirsevimab programs have achieved very high uptake (>90%), whereas maternal RSV vaccination coverage has generally been more moderate (around 60–65%), with even lower overall uptake reported in settings where families can choose between maternal vaccination and infant immunization.

Beyond differences in biological mechanism, the two strategies also rely on distinct health-care delivery platforms. Maternal RSV vaccination is intrinsically linked to the availability, quality, and timing of antenatal care services. Although antenatal care coverage has increased substantially worldwide, important gaps remain in the timing and content of care, particularly in many low- and middle-income settings, where delayed initiation of antenatal care and missed opportunities during visits remain common.82 These factors may reduce the proportion of pregnant women vaccinated within the recommended gestational window, especially when vaccination is restricted to later gestational ages.83 By contrast, nirsevimab is delivered directly to the infant at birth or before the RSV season and is therefore less dependent on antenatal care performance. Consequently, the relative advantages of maternal vaccination and nirsevimab are context-specific and should be assessed according to local antenatal care coverage, timeliness, health-care infrastructure, and program feasibility rather than assuming that one strategy is universally preferable.

Regulatory, public-health, availability, and implementation issues

Beyfortus received its first marketing authorization in the European Union on 31 October 2022, followed by US approval in 2023 and Swiss authorization in December 2023. Regulatory maturity has therefore advanced rapidly, and current public documents support discussion not only of efficacy and safety, but also of formulation, storage, traceability, and approved use across first- and selected second-season populations.

From a public-health perspective, nirsevimab should be discussed within implementation pathways rather than as an isolated product. WHO now recommends that all countries introduce infant RSV prevention using either maternal RSV vaccination or a long-acting monoclonal antibody, adapting the strategy to feasibility, local epidemiology, delivery platforms, and expected coverage.79,84 In parallel, the Galician and US postintroduction data show that effective deployment depends on operational issues, such as campaign timing, birth-hospital administration, catch-up logistics, and surveillance infrastructure.59,85

In this context, decision-makers evaluating implementation pathways must weigh several critical operational determinants that extend beyond clinical efficacy. A primary consideration is cold-chain logistics, as both nirsevimab and maternal RSV vaccines require continuous storage at 2–8°C.30,86 Maintaining refrigeration in remote or low-resource settings remains a substantial challenge, where temperature excursions may compromise product integrity. Nirsevimab is supplied as a ready-to-use prefilled single-dose syringe, eliminating on-site preparation, facilitating rapid bedside administration, and reducing the risk of preparation errors. Although the single-dose presentation may increase refrigerated storage requirements during seasonal campaigns compared with multidose formulations, the integrated syringe eliminates the need for separate procurement and handling of injection devices, potentially reducing supply-chain bottlenecks in settings where vaccines and syringes are managed through different procurement systems.87

From a delivery platform perspective, a clear operational difference exists between seasonal birth-dose and outpatient catch-up strategies. Immediate post-birth immunization ensures rapid coverage and minimizes missed opportunities before hospital discharge. Moreover, in countries with established birth-dose immunization programs, such as BCG or hepatitis B vaccination, seasonal administration can be readily integrated into existing neonatal immunization workflows.88,89 In contrast, catch-up programs for infants born outside the RSV season are more difficult to implement, particularly in resource-limited settings with fragile primary health-care systems and limited patient-tracking capacity. Without effective public awareness campaigns and community outreach, delays in uptake may leave infants unprotected during periods of RSV circulation.

Finally, implementing maternal RSV vaccination alongside nirsevimab requires clear clinical workflows to accurately document maternal vaccination status and identify infants eligible for nirsevimab, avoiding unnecessary administration.

Conclusions

Nirsevimab combines high neutralizing potency, broad activity across RSV subtypes, and an extended half-life that allows for single-dose seasonal protection. Across clinical trials and RWE, it has demonstrated consistency in preventing RSV-related disease and a favorable safety profile, including in high-risk and immunocompromised populations. Although resistance-associated variants remain rare, ongoing molecular surveillance will be essential as widespread use expands. From a public health perspective, nirsevimab offers a scalable strategy to protect all infants entering their first RSV season, overcoming many of the limitations associated with earlier monoclonal antibodies such as palivizumab. However, challenges remain regarding equitable access, cost-effectiveness, and optimal integration with other preventive approaches, including maternal vaccination, particularly in resource-limited settings. Overall, nirsevimab represents a significant advance in RSV prevention and has the potential to substantially reduce the global burden of RSV disease.

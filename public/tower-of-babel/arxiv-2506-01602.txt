Word Sense Detection Leveraging Maximum Mean Discrepancy
Kensuke Mitsuzawa

Abstract
Word sense analysis is an essential analysis work for interpreting the linguistic and social backgrounds. The word sense change detection is a task of identifying and interpreting shifts in word meanings over time. This paper proposes MMD-Sense-Analysis , a novel approach that leverages Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) to select semantically meaningful variables and quantify changes across time periods. This method enables both the identification of words undergoing sense shifts and the explanation of their evolution over multiple historical periods. To my knowledge, this is the first application of MMD to word sense change detection. Empirical assessment results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach.

Introduction

Natural language has been developed and changed over time, and, as time goes on, the sense of vocabulary has often changed.
For example, the word
gay
had the sense of
carefree
and has shifted to
homosexual
during the 20th century
(
kutuzov-2018
)
.
In the Natural Language Processing (NLP) community, the task of word sense change analysis detects these changes in vocabulary senses over time.
The task is also called “semantic change”, “semantic shift”, “temporal embeddings”, “diachronic embeddings”, or “lexical semantic change”
Periti-2024-lexical-semantic-change
, however, in this paper, I call it a “sense change detection” task, following the terminology
(
kutuzov-2018
)
.

The application of the sense change detection is, for instance, linguistic and social background analysis.
By examining vocabulary that changes over time, we can gain insights into the social backgrounds of the speakers who use those words.
For instance, the study
timmermans-2022
investigates sense change detection in a collection of Dutch texts from the 18th and 19th centuries, and infers the historical social context in which nationalism emerged in the Netherlands.

The two major goals of the sense change detection are identifying words that experience the meaning shift between two time periods and explaining the sense change of words over multiple periods
kiyama-2025
.
To achieve these goals, recent work employs vector representations trained by word embedding models
Aida-2021
or large language models
Lazaridou-2021
;
Loureiro-etal-2022-timelms
for obtaining representations capturing wide contexts of words.
On trained vector representations, there can be two types of dimensions (variables)
Aida-bollegala-2024-semantic
;
sense-aware dimensions
that are involved with the sense changes and
uniform dimensions
that are not involved.
Since conventional distance metrics (i.e. cosine or euclidean distances) are not able to distinguish these two types of dimensions,
Aida-bollegala-2024-semantic
addresses the importance of a distance metric that can accurately measure the semantic change.

This paper proposes
MMD-Sense-Analysis
for realizing the two goals of identifying word sense changes and explaining the sense changes over periods, while distinguishing the sense-aware dimensions.
The core idea of
MMD-Sense-Analysis
is selecting semantic-aware dimensions using a variable selection based on
Maximum Mean Discrepancy
(MMD), and qualifying similarities of two time periods.

Maximum Mean Discrepancy
(MMD) is a distance metric between two probability distributions and the popular choice for dataset shift adaptation or domain adaptation
Yan-2017mind
;
wang2021-rethinking
.
Since the word sense change can be regarded as a problem of detecting shift, MMD is a suitable distance metric for comparing two time periods.
To my best knowledge, this is the first work using MMD for the task of word sense change detection.

Proposed Method

The proposed algorithm
MMD-Sense-Analysis
selects variables of word embeddings that are involved with the sense changes (
semantic aware dimensions

Aida-bollegala-2024-semantic
), and then the proposed algorithm selects vocabularies (words) significantly influenced by the selected variables.
In this paper, I call such variables
sense change variables
.

I, firstly, formalize preliminaries.
There is
T\in\mathbb{N}
time periods to be studied, and word embedding vectors
E_{t}=\{e_{1},...,e_{V}\},e\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
are given for a time period index
t
, where
V
is a set of vocabularies that the word embedding models.
Essentially,
MMD-Sense-Analysis
can handle any word embedding models, however, it is recommended to use a word embedding model designed for treating the time-space, such as
PMI-SVD joint

Aida-2021
.

MMD-Sense-Analysis
selects
sense change variables
by the variable selection problem.
The variable selection problem is defined as selecting a subset
S\subset\{1,2,\dots,D\}
of variables out of the
D
variables of word embedding vectors.
The selected variables
S
represent indices of
sense change variables
.
For the variable selection, I use the approach of “MMD Variable Selection”
(
mitsuzawa-2023
)
.

In the next section, I introduce Maximum Mean Discrepancy
(
Gretton-2012a
)
and the variable selection algorithm,
and then,
MMD-Sense-Analysis
is introduced in Section
2.2
.

2.1
MMD Two-Sample Variable Selection

Maximum Mean Discrepancy and MMD Variable Selection.

Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) is a kernel-based distance metric between probability distributions
Gretton-2012a
and a measure of distance between two probability distributions.
It relies on a positive semi-definite kernel
Hofmann-2008
;
Smola-2009
to map the distributions into a new space, a
Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space
(RKHS), where probability distributions are represented as data points
Muandet-2017
.
A large MMD value indicates significant differences between two distributions.

Given i.i.d. samples
{\bf X}=\{{\bm{x}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{x}}_{n}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i.i.d.}}{{\sim}}P
and
{\bf Y}=\{{\bm{y}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{y}}_{n}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i.i.d.}}{{\sim}}Q
, the MMD can be consistently estimated as

\begin{split}&\widehat{\rm MMD}^{2}_{n}({\bf X},{\bf Y}):=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\not=i^{\prime}}k({\bm{x}}_{i},{\bm{x}}_{i^{\prime}})+\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{j\not=j^{\prime}}k({\bm{y}}_{j},{\bm{y}}_{j^{\prime}})-\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j}k({\bm{x}}_{i},{\bm{y}}_{j}).\end{split}

(1)

Recently, two-sample variable algorithms based on MMD have been studied in the literature
(
sutherland2017
;
wang2023variable
;
mitsuzawa-2023
)
.
Here, I explain the approach of
(
mitsuzawa-2023
)
.

First, I introduce
Automatic Relevance Detection (ARD)
kernel as the kernel
k
in Eq. (
1
), which is defined as

k({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\frac{a_{d}^{2}(x_{d}-y_{d})^{2}}{\gamma^{2}_{d}}\right),\quad{\bm{x}}:=(x_{1},...,x_{D})^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{D},\ {\bm{y}}:=(y_{1},...,y_{D})^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}

(2)

where
a_{1},...,a_{D}\geq 0
are called
ARD weights
, and
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{D}>0
are constants to unit-normalize each variable.
I compute
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{D}
by using the
dimension-wise median (or mean) heuristic
; see
(
mitsuzawa-2023
, Appendix C)
for details.

sutherland2017
optimizes the ARD weights
a_{1},\dots,a_{D}
in Eq.(
2
) by maximizing the
test power
(i.e., the rejection probability of the null hypothesis
P=Q
when
P\not=Q
) of a two-sample test based on the MMD estimate Eq.(
1
).

mitsuzawa-2023
proposes the variable selection by introducing
L_{1}
regularization
Tibshirani-1996
to encourage sparsity for variable selection:

\underset{a\in\mathbb{R}^{D}}{\min}-\log(\ell(a_{1},...,a_{D}))+\lambda\sum_{d=1}^{D}|a_{d}|,\quad\text{where}\quad\ell(a_{1},...,a_{D}):=\frac{\widehat{\rm MMD}^{2}_{n}({\bf X},{\bf Y})}{\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{n}({\bf X},{\bf Y})+C}}.

(3)

In the above display,
\lambda>0
is a regularization constant,
C=10^{-8}
is a constant for numerical stability, and
\mathbb{V}_{n}({\bf X},{\bf Y})
is an empirical estimate of the variance of
\widehat{\rm MMD}^{2}_{n}({\bf X},{\bf Y})
; see
(
mitsuzawa-2023
)
for the concrete form.
The weights
a_{1},\dots,a_{D}
with larger
\ell(a_{1},\dots,a_{D})
make the test power higher, thus making the two data sets
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
more distinguishable.
Minimizing the negative logarithm of
\ell(a_{1},\dots,a_{D})
plus the
L_{1}
regularization term
\lambda\sum_{d=1}^{D}|a_{d}|
thus yield
sparse
weights
a_{1},\dots,a_{D}
that distinguish
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
.
Such weights can be used for selecting variables where the two data sets differ significantly.

The regularization constant
\lambda
controls the sparsity of optimized ARD weights, and an appropriate value of
\lambda
is unknown beforehand, as it depends on the number of unknown active variables
S\subset\{1,\dots,D\}
on which
P
and
Q
are different.

mitsuzawa-2023
proposes a variable selection algorithm
(
mitsuzawa-2023
, Algorithm 2)
, “MMD Cross-Validation Aggregation”, selecting variables by aggregating variable selection results of various
\lambda
values instead of specifying a
\lambda
.
I denote
\text{MMD-CV-AGG}(E_{t},E_{t^{\prime}})
.

2.2
Detecting and Analyzing Word Sense Changes

Variable Selection.

I denote
S_{t,t^{\prime}}=\text{MMD-CV-AGG}(E_{t},E_{t^{\prime}})
as the procedure of selecting the
sense change variables
for the set of possible combination of time periods
\{t,t^{\prime}\in\{1,...,T\}|t\neq t^{\prime}\}
of which size if
C^{T}_{2}
.
And I obtain
\{S_{t,t^{\prime}}|t,t^{\prime}\in\{1,..,T\},t\neq t^{\prime}\}
that is a set of
sense change variables
for all possible combinations of time periods.

Permutation Test.

MMD-Sense-Analysis
conducts a permutation test for inspecting how the
sense change variables

S_{t,t^{\prime}}
distinguish two dataset
E_{t},E_{t^{\prime}}
.
The permutation test calculates the p-value.
The p-value represents the degree to which the observation data pair
E_{t},E_{t^{\prime}}
is significantly against the null hypothesis, where the null-hypothesis is that word embedding distributions of two time periods are the same.
Intuitively, a lower p-value can indicate that the word sense between two time periods is different.

Global-Time Word Scoring.

When the variable selection is done for time period combinations, the scoring calculates a score representing the degree to which a vocabulary (word)
v
is influenced by
sense change variables
.
Given
\{S_{t,t^{\prime}}|t,t^{\prime}\in\{1,..,T\},t\neq t^{\prime}\}
, the scoring function is defined as

\text{score}^{v}_{t}=\frac{1}{T^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t^{\prime}=1\\
t\neq t^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{T^{\prime}}\text{cosine}(e_{v,t}^{S_{t,t^{\prime}}},e_{v,t^{\prime}}^{S_{t,t^{\prime}}}),

(4)

where
T^{\prime}=T-1
is the number of time periods without
t
,
t^{\prime}\in\{1,...,T^{\prime}\}
.

e_{v,t}^{S_{t,t^{\prime}}}
denotes a vector that selects
sense change variables

S_{t,t^{\prime}}
from a word embedding vector
e
at time
t
for a vocabulary
v
.
The score range is
[0.0,2.0]
.

This score expresses the average of cosine distances between the word embedding vectors using only
sense change variables
; therefore, a higher score value implies that a vocabulary
v
is significantly related to the
sense change variables
at the time periods
t
.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, I propose
MMD-Sense-Analysis
: an algorithm for sense change detection.

MMD-Sense-Analysis
is supported by the variable selection approach using Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) that is suitable for dataset adaptation or domain adaptation.
By the variable selection,
MMD-Sense-Analysis
can select the semantic aware variables that are significantly involved in the sense changes and qualify the similarities of multiple time periods.
Based on the selected semantic aware variables,
MMD-Sense-Analysis
selects words concerning the semantic aware variables.

The empirical results show that the similarities of time periods can explain the social backgrounds, and high-scoring words can clearly describe the social backgrounds as well as the qualified similarities.
Yet, the empirical result shows that it is challenging to explain the social backgrounds when the available vocabulary is insufficient.

As future work, I plan to evaluate
MMD-Sense-Analysis
on a benchmark dataset for the sense change detection.

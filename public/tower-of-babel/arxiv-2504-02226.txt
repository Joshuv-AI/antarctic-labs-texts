Optimal error estimates of the diffuse domain method for second order parabolic equations
Wenrui Hao, Lili Ju, Yuejin Xu

Abstract
In this paper, we study the convergence behavior of the diffuse domain method (DDM) for solving a class of second-order parabolic partial differential equations with Neumann boundary condition posed on general irregular domains. The DDM employs a phase-field function to extend the original parabolic problem to a similar but slightly modified problem defined over a larger rectangular domain that contains the target physical domain. Based on the weighted Sobolev spaces, we rigorously establish the convergence of the diffuse domain solution to the original solution as the interface thickness parameter goes to zero, together with the corresponding optimal error estimates under the weighted L 2 L^{2} and H 1 H^{1} norms. Numerical experiments are also presented to validate the theoretical results.

Introduction

Combined with appropriate initial values and boundary conditions, parabolic partial differential equations (PDEs) have been widely applied in various mathematical models. Notable examples include the Navier-Stokes equations and the time-dependent advection-diffusion equation in fluid dynamics
Rog (1984)
, Stokes-Darcy problems arising in petroleum and biomedical engineering
Bukaˇc et al. (2023)
, as well as the Allen-Cahn equation and other phase field models used to describe phase transitions and separations
Du and Feng (2020)
;
Allen and Cahn (1979)
, among many others. Most existing numerical methods for solving interface problems are based on sharp interface approaches, which rely on explicit surface parameterization. This requirement poses a significant challenge for handling complex geometries and mesh generation. Examples of such methods include the extended and composite finite element methods
Dolbow and Harari (2009)
;
Fries and Belytschko (2010)
, immersed interface methods
LeVeque and Li (1995)
;
Li and Ito (2006)
;
Xiao et al. (2023)
, virtual node methods with embedded boundary conditions
Bedrossian et al. (2010)
;
Hellrung et al. (2012)
, and matched interface and boundary methods
Zhao and Wei (2009)
;
Li and Feng (2015)
;
Barua et al. (2023)
, among others. Many of these techniques require specialized computational tools that are not readily available in standard finite element and finite difference software packages.

Over the past two decades, the diffuse interface method (DIM) has gained widespread attention and is regarded as an effective alternative approach to sharp interface methods for solving PDE problems on complex geometries. This method represents the physical domain implicitly using a phase-field function, which can be interpreted as the domain’s indicator function when the interface thickness approaches zero and is often coupled with classic discretization schemes, such as finite difference method, finite element method
Yang et al. (2019)
, spectral method
Bueno-Orovio et al. (2006)
, and Nitsche’s method
Nguyen et al. (2018)
.
The DIM has been applied to elliptic interface problems, two-phase flow problems, and various material and engineering applications. Kockelkoren et al.
Kockelkoren et al. (2003)
were the first to apply the diffuse interface method to study diffusion inside a cell with zero Neumann boundary conditions. Many numerical methods have been developed for solving different two-phase flow problems based on DIM. For example, Liu et al. combined the DIM with the consistent and conservative phase-field method to solve two-phase flows in complex geometries
Liu et al. (2022)
. The DIM was applied to solve two-phase flows of viscous, incompressible fluids with matched densities, leading to coupled Navier-Stokes or Cahn-Hilliard systems
Frigeri et al. (2015)
;
Abels (2009)
. Moreover, the diffuse interface method can address miscible fluids of different densities
Abels and Lengeler (2014)
, compressible fluids
Feireisl et al. (2010)
, Stokes-Darcy coupled equations
Bukaˇc et al. (2023)
, and problems involving more than two phases, where additional labeling functions are introduced to distinguish among them
Brannick et al. (2015)
. The diffuse interface method also has been extensively used to solve various models, such as the material model with interfaces that can be advected or stretched
Teigen et al. (2009)
;
Torabi et al. (2009)
, the patient-specific human liver model based on MRI scans
Stoter et al. (2017)
, the PDEs in moving geometries
Elliott et al. (2011)
.The DIM approach is also a valuable tool for solving variational inverse problems, with corresponding convergence rates estimated in
Burger et al. (2015)
.

The diffuse domain method (DDM)
Anderson et al. (1998)
;
Rätz and Voigt (2006)
, can be viewed as a variant of DIM.
The DDM represents the original physical domain implicitly using a phase-field function with a narrow diffuse interface layer, wherein the value of the phase-field function rapidly transits from 1 inside the domain to 0 outside the domain
Lervå g and Lowengrub (2015)
. The original PDE problem is then subsequently reformulated into a similar problem in a larger rectangular domain. As a result, the challenges associated with mesh generation for complex domains are mitigated, enabling the straightforward generation of spatial meshes for the rectangular domain to solve the transformed PDE problem with existing numerical schemes.
The DDM has been used to solve PDEs in complex, stationary, or moving geometries with Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin boundary conditions
Li et al. (2009)
.
The properties and convergence of the diffuse domain method have been analyzed in several studies. Li et al. demonstrated that, in the diffuse domain method, several approximations to the physical boundary conditions converge asymptotically to the correct sharp interface problem
Li et al. (2009)
. They also observed that the choice of boundary condition can significantly affect numerical accuracy. Furthermore, Lervag et al. discussed that for specific choices of boundary condition approximations, the asymptotic convergence of the diffuse domain method can be improved to second order
Lervå g and Lowengrub (2015)
. Lowengrub et al. have applied this method to elliptic problems and provided an asymptotic analysis of the boundary layer in
Lervå g and Lowengrub (2015)
;
Aland et al. (2010)
. Additionally, Franz et al. analyzed the error estimates in the
L^{\infty}
-norm for one-dimensional elliptic equations
Franz et al. (2012)
. Numerical errors in
L^{2}
,
L^{\infty}
, and
H^{1}
-norms on the original region have been further investigated for elliptic problems with Dirichlet boundary conditions
Schlottbom (2016)
. Burger et al. constructed weighted Sobolev spaces based on the phase-field function and analyzed the approximation error in the extended region within the weighted
L^{2}
-norm
Burger et al. (2017)
. Moreover, the convergence rate on the original domain for the Stokes-Darcy coupled problem has been discussed in
Bukaˇc et al. (2023)
. Guo et al. coupled the DDM with an interface model to simulate two-phase fluid flows with variable physical properties while maintaining thermodynamic consistency
Guo et al. (2021)
.
The DDM approach was also employed to develop biomedical models, such as the chemotaxis-fluid diffuse-domain model for simulating bioconvection
Wang et al. (2023)
, a needle insertion model
Jerg et al. (2020)
, etc.

In this paper, we are concerned with the diffuse domain method for solving the following linear second-order parabolic equation with a Neumann boundary condition:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}=\nabla\cdot(A\nabla u)+f(t),\quad&&\bm{x}\in D,\quad 0\leq t\leq T\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\quad&&\bm{x}\in D,\\
&(A\nabla u)\cdot\bm{n}=g(t),\quad&&\bm{x}\in\partial D,\quad 0\leq t\leq T,\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where
D
is an open bounded Lipschitz domain with irregular shape in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 1
),
T>0
is the terminal time,
A(\bm{x})>0
is the diffusion coefficient and fulfills
\kappa\leq A(\bm{x})\leq\kappa^{-1}
for all
\bm{x}\in D
with some constant
\kappa>0
,
u(t,\bm{x})
is the unknown function,
f(t,\bm{x})
is the source term,
u_{0}(\bm{x})
is the initial value, and
g(t,\bm{x})
is the Neumann boundary value. We will analyze the convergence of the diffuse domain solution as the interface thickness parameter goes to zero and derive corresponding error estimates measured in the weighted

and weighted

norms. The analysis techniques mainly follow the weighted Sobolev space-based framework developed in
Burger et al. (2017)
, but also with some enhancements. Our results successfully decouple the relationship between the hidden constants and the interface thickness parameter in the error estimates. Furthermore, the convergence rates are also improved to second order in the weighted
L^{2}
norm and first order in the weighted

norm, which are optimal as verified by the numerical experiments. To the best of our knowledge, this work presented in this paper is the first study on rigorous error analysis of the DDM for second-order parabolic equations.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. The diffuse domain method for the model problem (
1

Following the similar arguments of

3.1
On the weighted Sobolev space

The following three theorems (Theorems

Theorem 2

Suppose that

,

\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}wv\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\lesssim\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|w\|_{H^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\right)\|v\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})},

where the hidden constant is independent of
\epsilon
,
u
and
v
.

Next, let us introduce the smooth condition for
f
and some regularity condition required for the exact solution
u
in order to carry out the convergence and error analysis of the diffuse domain method. For simplicity of expressions, in the following analysis we will not distinguish the exact solution
u(t)
, the source function
f(t)
, the Neumann boundary value
g(t)
, the diffusion coefficient
A
with their extensions in
D_{\epsilon}
since there are no ambiguities.

Assumption 1
.

The extension of the source function
f(t,\bm{x})
to
D_{\epsilon}
satisfies the following regularity condition:

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f(t,\bm{x})\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

(11a)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f(t,\bm{x})\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

(11b)

where the hidden constant may depend on the terminal time
T
.

Assumption 2
.

The extension of the exact solution
u(t)
of (
2
) to
D_{\epsilon}
satisfies the following regularity conditions:

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

(12a)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u_{t}(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(D_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

(12b)

where the hidden constants may depend on the terminal time
T
.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
w\in H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})\cap L^{\infty}(D_{\epsilon})
, and
v\in H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
. Then

\left|\int_{D_{\epsilon}}wv\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}wv\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})},

where the hidden constant is independent of
\epsilon
,
u
and
v
.

Proof.

According to the definition of
D_{\epsilon}
and
\omega_{\epsilon}
, we can rewrite

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\epsilon}}wv\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}wv\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

(13)

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{-\epsilon}}wv\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}wv\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{-\epsilon}}wv\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}wv\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{D\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}wv\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}\setminus D}wv\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}wv\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{D\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}\left(w-w/\omega_{\epsilon}\right)v\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}\setminus D}wv\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}.

By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the triangle inequality, (
13
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\epsilon}}wv\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}wv\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

(14)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{D\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}\left|w-w/\omega_{\epsilon}\right|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{D\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}|v|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}\setminus D}|w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}\setminus D}|v|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}|v|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{D\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}\left|w-w/\omega_{\epsilon}\right|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}\setminus D}|w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}|v|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big({\rm{I}}_{1}+{\rm{I}}_{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where

{\rm{I}}_{1}:=\int_{D_{\epsilon}\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}|w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}|w|^{2}\,{\rm d}\bm{x},

{\rm{I}}_{2}:=\int_{D\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}|w|^{2}/\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D\setminus D_{-\epsilon}}|w|^{2}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}.

As for the estimate of
\rm{I}_{1}
, with the help of Theorem
4
, we can derive that

\displaystyle|{\rm{I}_{1}}|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}|w|^{2}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|w^{2}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}.

(15)

As for the estimate of
\rm{I}_{2}
, recalling the definition of
D_{\epsilon}
and
\omega_{\epsilon}
, we can derive that

\displaystyle|{\rm{I}_{2}}|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\epsilon}^{0}\frac{\frac{6}{\epsilon}S^{\prime}\left(-\frac{3s}{\epsilon}\right)}{\left(1+S\left(-\frac{3s}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}}\int_{\{d_{D}(\bm{x})<s\}}|w|^{2}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}s-\int_{-\epsilon}^{0}\frac{\frac{6}{\epsilon}S^{\prime}\left(-\frac{3s}{\epsilon}\right)}{\left(1+S\left(-\frac{3s}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}}\int_{\{d_{D}(\bm{x})<0\}}|w|^{2}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}s\right|

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\epsilon}^{0}\frac{\frac{6}{\epsilon}S^{\prime}\left(-\frac{3s}{\epsilon}\right)}{\left(1+S\left(-\frac{3s}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}}\int_{\{s<d_{D}(\bm{x})<0\}}|w|^{2}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}s\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\int_{-\epsilon}^{0}S^{\prime}\left(-\frac{3s}{\epsilon}\right)\int_{\{s<d_{D}(\bm{x})<0\}}1\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{-\epsilon}^{0}S^{\prime}\left(-\frac{3s}{\epsilon}\right)\int_{\{s<d_{D}(\bm{x})<0\}}|w|^{4}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{-\epsilon}^{0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}S^{\prime}\left(-\frac{3s}{\epsilon}\right)\int_{\{s<d_{D}(\bm{x})<0\}}|w|^{4}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{-s<\varphi^{\epsilon}<0\}}|w|^{4}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the last three inequalities use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the boundness of
S^{\prime}(\cdot)
. Since
w\in L^{\infty}(D_{\epsilon})
, (
16
) can be reformulated as

\displaystyle{\rm{I}_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}.

(17)

Combining (
14
), (
15
), (
17
), we finally obtain

\left|\int_{D_{\epsilon}}wv\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}wv\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})},

which complete the proof.

∎

4
Error analysis

To illustrate the convergence of diffuse domain method, let us combine the (
2
) and (
9
) together, then we can get the error equation as follows: for all
v\in H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
,

\begin{split}\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}+a^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u,v)=&[\left(u_{t},v\right)-\left(u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}]+[a(u,v)-a^{\epsilon}(u,v)]\\
&+[\ell^{\epsilon}(v)-\ell(v)].\end{split}

(18)

Next we will derive the error estimates of
u^{\epsilon}-u
in the
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
norms.

4.1
Optimal error estimate in the
L^{2}
-norm

Theorem 6

(Error estimate in the
L^{2}
norm)
.

Suppose that
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
g(t)\in H^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
for
t\in(0,T]
,
\kappa\leq A(\bm{x})\leq\kappa^{-1}
for all
\bm{x}\in D_{\epsilon}
with some constant
\kappa>0
, and
f
satisfies Assumption
1
. Assume the exact solution
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(D))
and its extension to
D_{\epsilon}
fulfills Assumption
2
. Then we have

\begin{split}\|u^{\epsilon}(t)-u(t)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon^{2},\quad\forall\,0\leq t\leq T,\end{split}

(19)

where the hidden constant is independent of
\epsilon
.

Proof.

Let us analyze
\left(u_{t},v\right)-\left(u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}
,
a(u,v)-a^{\epsilon}(u,v)
and
\ell^{\epsilon}(v)-\ell(v)
respectively for any
v\in H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
.
Recalling the definition of
a^{\epsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
a(\cdot,\cdot)
, since
\omega_{\epsilon}
will vanish on
\partial D_{\epsilon}
, we can derive the following formula by using the integration by part,

\displaystyle a(u,v)-a^{\epsilon}(u,v)

(20)

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)\omega_{\epsilon}v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{\partial D}\bm{n}\cdot\left(A\nabla u\right)v\,{\rm d}\sigma-\int_{D}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}\left(A\nabla u\right)\omega_{\epsilon}v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}\,{\rm div}\left(A\nabla u\right)v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u)\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{\partial D}gv\,{\rm d}\sigma,

where in the last equality we use the fact that
\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}=-\bm{n}\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|
. By Theorem
4
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{D}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)v\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}.

(21)

Then, inserting (
21
) to (
20
), we get that

\displaystyle a(u,v)-a^{\epsilon}(u,v)\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u)\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

(22)

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial D}gv\,{\rm d}\sigma.

As for the estimate of
\left(u_{t},v\right)-\left(u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}
, by using the conclusion of Theorem
4
, we obtain

\left(u_{t},v\right)-\left(u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|u_{t}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}.

(23)

As for the estimate of
\ell^{\epsilon}(v)-\ell(v)
, same as the derivation of (
23
), we can get

\displaystyle\ell^{\epsilon}(v)-\ell(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{\epsilon}}fv\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}fv\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}gv\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{\partial D}gv\,{\rm d}\sigma

(24)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}gv\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{\partial D}gv\,{\rm d}\sigma.

Combining (
22
)-(
24
) together, we get

\displaystyle\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}+a^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u,v)

(25)

\displaystyle=\left(u_{t},v\right)-\left(u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}+a(u,v)-a^{\epsilon}(u,v)+\ell^{\epsilon}(v)-\ell(v)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|u_{t}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\,{\rm div}\left(A\nabla u\right)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u)\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}gv|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}.

Applying with Theorem
5
, we know that

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\epsilon}}gv|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u)v|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\left(\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u)-g\right)v|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u)-g\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}.

Therefore, we have by (
25
) and (
4.1
) that

\displaystyle\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}+a^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u,v)

(26)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|u_{t}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\,{\rm div}\left(A\nabla u\right)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u)-g\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}.

Since
|\Gamma_{\epsilon}|\lesssim\epsilon
, applying
v=u^{\epsilon}-u
to (
26
) gives

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}+\|\nabla(u^{\epsilon}-u)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\|u_{t}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\|\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\|\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u)-g\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\Big)\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{4}+\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}+\|\nabla(u^{\epsilon}-u)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}.

Thus

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}\lesssim\epsilon^{4}+\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2},

which implies

\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}.

The proof is completed.

∎

4.2
Optimal error estimate in the
H^{1}
norm

First we derive several important lemmas needed for the
H^{1}
norm error estimate.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
g(t)\in H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
, and
f
satisfies Assumption
1
. Assume the exact solution
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(D))
and its extension to
D_{\epsilon}
fulfills Assumption
2
. Then we have

\int_{\partial D}g(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\sigma+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\leq C\epsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2},

(27)

where
C
is a constant independent of
\epsilon
.

Proof.

Using
\nabla d_{D}(\bm{x})=\bm{n}(\bm{x})
,
\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}=-\bm{n}|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|
and the divergence theorem, we derive that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D}g(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\sigma+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

(28)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}g(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\sigma-\int_{\partial D_{\epsilon}}g|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\,{\rm div}(g\nabla d_{D}(u^{\epsilon}-u))\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}(g\nabla d_{D}(u^{\epsilon}-u))\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{D}\,{\rm div}(g\nabla d_{D})\left(u^{\epsilon}-u\right)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}(g\nabla d_{D})\left(u^{\epsilon}-u\right)\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{D}g\nabla d_{D}\cdot\left(\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u\right)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g\nabla d_{D}\cdot\left(\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u\right)|\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right|

\displaystyle=:\rm{II}_{1}+\rm{II}_{2}.

By using the conclusion of Lemma
1
and
|\Gamma_{\epsilon}|\lesssim\epsilon
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{aligned} \rm{II}_{1}&\lesssim\left\|\,{\rm div}(g\nabla d_{D})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\leq C\epsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2},\\
\rm{II}_{2}&\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\leq C\epsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(29)

Combining (
29
) and (
28
) together, and applying the
L^{2}
norm error estimate in Theorem
6
, we easily derive the result (
27
).

∎

Lemma 3
.

Suppose that
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
g\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}))
, and
g_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(D_{\epsilon}))
. Assume the exact solution
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(D))
and its extension to

fulfills Assumption
2
. If
\kappa\leq A(\bm{x})\leq\kappa^{-1}

\bm{x}\in D_{\epsilon}
with some constant
\kappa>0
, then we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u_{t}\cdot\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t

(30)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial D}g(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial D}g_{t}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}t\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T,\cdot)-\nabla u(T,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0,\cdot)-\nabla u(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2},

where
C
is a constant independent of
\epsilon
.

Proof.

Applying with integration by part, we can reformulate the left-hand side of (
30
) as

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u_{t}\cdot\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t

(31)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial D}g(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial D}g_{t}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}t\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g(T,\cdot)(u^{\epsilon}(T,\cdot)-u(T,\cdot))|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{\partial D}g(T,\cdot)(u^{\epsilon}(T,\cdot)-u(T,\cdot))\,{\rm d}\sigma\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g(0,\cdot)(u^{\epsilon}(0,\cdot)-u(0,\cdot))|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{\partial D}g(0,\cdot)(u^{\epsilon}(0,\cdot)-u(0,\cdot))\,{\rm d}\sigma\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g_{t}(u^{\epsilon}-u)|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u_{t}\cdot\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle=:\rm{III}_{1}+\rm{III}_{2}+\rm{III}_{3}.

In the remaining part, we will estimate the term
\rm{III}_{1}
,
\rm{III}_{2}
and
\rm{III}_{3}
, respectively. As for the estimate of
\rm{III}_{1}
and
\rm{III}_{2}
, we can apply the Lemma
2
to derive

\left\{\begin{aligned} \rm{III}_{1}&\leq C\epsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T,\cdot)-\nabla u(T,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2},\\
\rm{III_{2}}&\leq C\epsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0,\cdot)-\nabla u(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(32)

As for the estimate of
\rm{III}_{3}
, by using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the definition of
\omega_{\epsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\rm{III}_{3}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g_{t}(u^{\epsilon}-u)|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u_{t})(u^{\epsilon}-u)|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t\right|

(33)

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}(\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u_{t})-g_{t})(u^{\epsilon}-u)|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\left|\omega_{\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\left|(\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u_{t})-g_{t})\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|/\omega_{\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u^{\epsilon}(t)-u(t)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u_{t})-g_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{2}.

Inserting (
32
) and (
33
) to (
31
), we finally obtain (
30
), which completes the proof.

Theorem 7

(Error estimate in the
H^{1}
-norm)
.

Suppose that
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
g\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}))
,
g_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(D_{\epsilon}))
,

\kappa\leq A(\bm{x})\leq\kappa^{-1}
for all
\bm{x}\in D
with some constant
\kappa>0
, and
f
satisfies Assumption
1
. Assume the exact solution
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(D_{\epsilon}))
and its extension to
D_{\epsilon}
fulfills Assumption
2
. Then we have

\|u^{\epsilon}(t)-u(t)\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon,\quad\forall\ 0\leq t\leq T,

(34)

where the hidden constant is independent of
\epsilon
.

Proof.

First, we insert
v=2(u^{\epsilon}_{t}-u_{t})
into (
18
), we have

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 2\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)_{D_{\epsilon},\omega_{\epsilon}}+2a^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u,u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})

(35)

\displaystyle=2\left(u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)-2\left(u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)+2a(u,u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})-2a^{\epsilon}(u,u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})

\displaystyle+2\ell^{\epsilon}(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})-2\ell(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}).

Then, after integrating both sides of (
35
) with respect to
t
, we can derive that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}2\left\|u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}+\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}a^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u,u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}t

(36)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left(u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)-\left(u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm d}t+2\int_{0}^{T}a(u,u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})-a^{\epsilon}(u,u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{T}\ell^{\epsilon}(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})-\ell(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=:\rm{IV}_{1}+\rm{IV}_{2}+\rm{IV}_{3}.

Next, we will estimate
\rm{IV}_{1}
,
\rm{IV}_{2}
and
\rm{IV}_{3}
respectively. For
\rm{IV}_{1}
, applying with Lemma
1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\rm{IV}_{1}\right|

\displaystyle=2\left|\int_{0}^{T}\left(u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)-\left(u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\left|\left(u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)-\left(u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}\right|\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\left\|u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\,{\rm d}t.

Due to the fact that
u^{\epsilon}
and
u_{t}^{\epsilon}
, as well as
u
and
u_{t}
, have the same regularity, based on Theorem
6
, we get that

\displaystyle|\rm{IV}_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\epsilon^{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{t}\|_{\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm d}t\lesssim\epsilon^{3}.

(37)

As for
\rm{IV}_{2}
, with the integration by part, we derive that

\displaystyle\rm{IV}_{2}=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{T}a(u,u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})-a^{\epsilon}(u,u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\,{\rm d}t

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{D}A\Big(\nabla u(T)\cdot\left(\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T)\right)-\nabla u(0,\cdot)\cdot\left(\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0)-\nabla u(0)\right)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}(\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u_{t}\,{\rm d}t\Big)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-2\int_{D}A\Big(\nabla u(T)\cdot\left(\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T)\right)

\displaystyle-\nabla u(0)\cdot\left(\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0)-\nabla u(0)\right)-\int_{0}^{T}(\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u_{t}\,{\rm d}t\Big)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Big(\int_{D}A\nabla u(T)\cdot(\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T))\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u(T)

\displaystyle\cdot(\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T))\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\Big)+2\Big(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u(0)\cdot(\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0)-\nabla u(0))\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle-\int_{D}A\nabla u(0)\cdot(\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0)-\nabla u(0))\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\Big)+2\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A(\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u)

\displaystyle\cdot\nabla u_{t}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}A(\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u_{t}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\Big)\,{\rm d}t\Big)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle{\rm\displaystyle V}_{1}+\rm{V}_{2}+\rm{V}_{3}.

For
\rm{V}_{1}
, applying with Lemma
1
, we know that

\displaystyle|\rm{V}_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|A\nabla u(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

(39)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}+C\epsilon\|A\nabla u(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}+C\epsilon^{2},

Similar as the derivation of (
39
), we have

\displaystyle|\rm{V}_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0)-\nabla u(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|A\nabla u(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon^{2},

(40)

with proper initial guess for
u^{\epsilon}
. For the remaining term
\rm{V}_{3}
, applying with the variational formula and the property of
\omega_{\epsilon}
, we can rewrite
\rm{V}_{3}
as

\displaystyle\rm{V}_{3}=

\displaystyle 2\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A(\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u_{t}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}A(\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u_{t}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)\,{\rm d}t\right)

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial D_{\epsilon}}\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u_{t})\omega_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\sigma-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u_{t}\omega_{\epsilon})(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial D}\bm{n}\cdot(A\nabla u_{t})(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\sigma+\int_{D}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u_{t})(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u_{t})(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u_{t})(u^{\epsilon}-u)\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)

\displaystyle-2\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u_{t}\cdot\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{\partial D}g_{t}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}t\right)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle{\rm\displaystyle W}_{1}-\rm{W}_{2}.

Following the similar derivation of (
39
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\rm{W}_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u_{t})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}.

(42)

The terms remained to be analyzed are
\rm{W}_{2}
and
\rm{IV}_{3}
. Merging
\rm{W}_{2}
and
\rm{IV}_{3}
together, we have

\displaystyle|-\rm{W}_{2}+\rm{IV}_{3}|\leq

\displaystyle 2\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}f(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t\right|

(43)

\displaystyle+2\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{\partial D}g(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}t\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u_{t}\cdot\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{\partial D}g_{t}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}t\right|.

By using the Lemma
1
, we can easily derive that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}f(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t\right|

(44)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\,{\rm d}t\lesssim\epsilon^{3}.

By using the Lemma
3
, we can derive that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u_{t}\cdot\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\,{\rm d}t\right.

(45)

\displaystyle\left.-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial D}g(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial D}g_{t}(u^{\epsilon}-u)\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0)-\nabla u(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}.

Combining (
44
) and (
45
) together, we can reformulate (
43
) as

\displaystyle|-\rm{W}_{2}+\rm{IV}_{3}|\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0)-\nabla u(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}.

(46)

Therefore, inserting (
37
)-(
46
) to (
36
), we can conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}2\|u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}-\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0)-\nabla u(0)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0)-\nabla u(0)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2},

which implies that

\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(T)-\nabla u(T)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}+\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}(0)-\nabla u(0)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}.

Since
T
can be replaced by any time
t\in[0,T]
in the above derivations, we get the conclusion (
34
).

∎

5
Numerical experiments

In this section, we will present some numerical experiments to verify the error estimates (Theorems
6
and Theorem
7
) obtained in Section
4
and demonstrate the performance of the DDM. We apply the finite element method with the bilinear basis functions for space discretization and the BDF2 scheme for time stepping to solve the
DDM-transformed problem (
9
) defined on a larger rectangular domain, which is of second-order in time, and first-order in space with respect to the
H^{1}
norm and second-order in space with respect to the
L^{2}
norm.
To demonstrate the convergence order of the diffuse domain solution with respect to the interface width parameter
\epsilon
, i.e.,
\left\|u(t)-u^{\epsilon}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}
and
\left\|u(t)-u^{\epsilon}(t)\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}
, we will
take
\epsilon
to be much larger than the spatial mesh size and the time step size (so that the solution errors are mainly caused by the DDM approximation). For simplicity, we only evaluate the approximation error on the original domain
D
, i.e.,
\left\|u(t)-u^{\epsilon}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(D;\omega_{\epsilon})}
and
\left\|u(t)-u^{\epsilon}(t)\right\|_{H^{1}(D;\omega_{\epsilon})}
.
All tests are done using Matlab on a laptop with Intel Ultra 9 185H, 2.30GHz CPU and 32GB memory.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we studied the convergence and error estimates of the diffuse domain method for solving a class of second-order parabolic equations with Neumann boundary conditions in general irregular domains. We successfully proved optimal error estimates of the diffuse domain solutions in the weighted
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
norms. Some numerical examples are also presented to verify the derived theoretical results. The numerical method and corresponding error analysis framework developed in this paper also naturally enable us to further investigate adaptive finite element methods
Ciarlet (2002)
;
Binev et al. (2004)
;
Bieterman and Babuˇska (1982)
for solving PDEs in regions with corner points with solid theoretical support.

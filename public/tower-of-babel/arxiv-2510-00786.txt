Pointwise spinor quantum fields cannot be microcausal nor Poincaré covariant
Samuel Fedida

Abstract
We extend and strengthen no-go results on pointwise-defined quantum fields to cover general spinors. We show that the weak continuity of quantum fields rules out equal-time canonical conjugate (anti)commutation relations in globally hyperbolic spacetimes; for quantum fields on Minkowski spacetime, weakly continuous translation covariance enforces the needed continuity and yields the same no-go. We also extend Wightman’s no-go theorem to show that the weak continuity of quantum fields rules out fermionic microcausality in C 2 C^{2} Lorentzian spacetimes. We finish by generalising Wizimirski’s no-go theorem to show that the existence of a Poincaré-invariant vacuum precludes pointwise spinorial covariance on a Minkowski background – ruling out, in particular, pointwise covariance for Weyl and Dirac fermions, for photons and gravitons – which further highlights the difficulty of quantising gravity pointwisely.



I
Introduction

The mathematical formulation of quantum field theory confronts deep challenges when attempting to define quantum fields as operator-valued functions on spacetime. In particular, several no-go theorems – originating in the works of Wightman
wightman_theorie_1964
and Wizimirski
wizimirski_existence_1966
and further developed in algebraic quantum field theory
Halvorson2006col
– demonstrate that under natural physical assumptions, pointwise-defined fields often lead to contradictions. While these results are well established for scalar fields, the cases of other spinorial fields requires separate scrutiny due to the different algebraic structures they obey, and to check which assumptions are fundamentally causing issues.

This note extends no-go results to the context of more general spinorial quantum fields. We first review a result originally presented for bosonic canonical commutation relations on Minkowski spacetime (e.g. see
Halvorson2006col
), and show that the weak continuity of quantum fields on globally hyperbolic Lorentzian spacetimes rules out both equal-time canonical commutation relations and canonical anticommutation relations. Consequently, in Minkowski spacetime, space translation covariance with respect to a weakly continuous representation

The statements below are intended for pointwise-defined fields. In standard axiomatic quantum field theory, the fields are operator-valued distributions from the outset and are only defined after smearing with test functions

;

We emphasise the present results simply as obstructions to the existence of genuine operator-valued functions on spacetime that obey the canonical (anti)commutation relations, the spacelike (anti)commutativity constraints or the pointwise covariance properties; they do not contradict the existence of usual smeared quantum fields such as the free scalar, Dirac or Maxwell fields in Wightman or algebraic quantum field theory. They also do not cover the construction of quantum fields as sesquilinear forms

;
wollenberg_quantum_1986

We briefly mention one recently introduced axiomatic approach to quantum field theory which does manipulate covariant and causal pointwisely-defined quantum fields, namely relational quantum field theory

not

not

III
Microcausality

Microcausality is one (out of many) quantum-mechanical implementations of the relativistic no-signalling principle. It imposes what is fundamentally an epistemic principle (no-signalling) to be applied at an ontological level (at least, provided the field operators carry ontological weight). The no-go theorem by Wightman

Halvorson2006col

IV
Poincaré covariance

We finish with a generalisation of Wizimirski’s theorem
wizimirski_existence_1966
;
Halvorson2006col

Wizimirski’s theorem again highlights this fact by showing that it can be too strong a requirement to impose at the pointwise level for scalar quantum fields.

Hence, we have shown that spinorial quantum fields cannot satisfy pointwise spinor covariance under the assumption of the existence (and, for gravitons, the uniqueness) of a vacuum vector. Though an extension which may or may not be expected from the scalar case covered by Wizimirski’s theorem, we find this interesting: it confirms the epistemic rather than ontological nature of Poincaré covariance, and underlines the difficulty of quantising Lorentzian metrics pointwise.

Whether this result can be further extended to more general covariance groups – e.g. the isometry group of some other Lorentzian spacetimes of interest (though those are not always locally compact so need not admit the usual representation theory results on Hilbert spaces) – is an interesting open question.

U(0,A^{-1})\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(\Lambda(A)x)^{\dagger}U(0,A^{-1})^{\dagger}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{n}\overline{S[A]_{\alpha\beta}}\hat{\psi}_{\beta}(x)^{\dagger}

(11)

so

\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(\Lambda(A)x)^{\dagger}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{n}\overline{S[A]_{\alpha\beta}}U(0,A^{-1})^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}_{\beta}(x)^{\dagger}U(0,A^{-1})\,.

(12)

Thus,

\displaystyle f_{\alpha\beta}(\Lambda(A)x)

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\hat{\psi}_\alpha(0)\hat{\psi}_\beta(\Lambda(A) x)^\dagger}{\Omega}

(13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta=1}^{n}\overline{S[A]_{\beta\delta}}\expectationvalue{U(0,A^{-1})^\dagger U(0,A^{-1})\hat{\psi}_\alpha(0) U(0,A^{-1})^\dagger\hat{\psi}_\delta(x)^\dagger U(0,A^{-1})}{\Omega}

(14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma,\delta=1}^{n}S[A]_{\alpha\gamma}\overline{S[A]_{\beta\delta}}\expectationvalue{\hat{\psi}_\gamma(0) \hat{\psi}_\delta(x)^\dagger}{\Omega}=\sum_{\gamma,\delta=1}^{n}S[A]_{\alpha\gamma}\overline{S[A]_{\beta\delta}}f_{\gamma\delta}(x)\,.

(15)

Hence, writing
F(x)=[f_{\alpha\beta}(x)]
, we have that for all
x\in\mathbb{M}
and
A\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
,

F(\Lambda(A)x)=S[A]F(x)S[A]^{\dagger}

(16)

so, in particular,

F(0)=S[A]F(0)S[A]^{\dagger}\qquad\forall A\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})\,.

(17)

Likewise, writing
G(x)=[g_{\alpha\beta}(x)]
, we have that

G(0)=\overline{S[A]}G(0)S[A]^{T}\qquad\forall A\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})\,.

(18)

Note that both
F(0)
and
G(0)
are positive semi-definite matrices. Indeed, take any
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let

X:=\sum_{\beta=1}^{n}\kappa_{\beta}\hat{\psi}_{\beta}(0)

(19)

Then

\kappa^{\dagger}G(0)\kappa=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n}\overline{\kappa_{\alpha}}g_{\alpha\beta}(0)\kappa_{\beta}=\expectationvalue{\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^n \overline{\kappa_\alpha} \hat{\psi}_\alpha(0)^\dagger\right)\left(\sum_{\beta=1}^n \kappa_\beta\hat{\psi}_\beta(0)\right)}{\Omega}=\expectationvalue{X^\dagger X}{\Omega}=\norm{X \ket{\Omega}}^{2}\geq 0

(20)

so
G(0)\geq 0
, and likewise
F(0)\geq 0
. Let us consider constraints on
F(0)
– the case of
G(0)
follows analogously. Multiplying eqn. (
17
) on the right by
(S^{\dagger})^{-1}
yields

S[A]F(0)=F(0)(S[A]^{\dagger})^{-1}\qquad\forall A\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})\,.

(21)

Suppose for now that
S
is irreducible. For any
z\in\text{ran }F(0)
there exists
y\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
z=F(0)y
and so

S[A]z=S[A](F(0)y)=(S[A]F(0))y=F(0)((S[A]^{\dagger})^{-1}y)\in\text{ran }F(0)

(22)

for all
A\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
, so
S[A](\text{ran }F(0))\subseteq\text{ran }F(0)
for all
A\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
so
\text{ran }F(0)
is
S
-invariant. Since
S
is irreducible, the only invariant subspaces are
\{0\}
and
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If
\text{ran }F(0)=\{0\}
then
F(0)=0
and we are done. Assume for contradiction that
\text{ran }F(0)=\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then
F(0)
is surjective (and finite dimensional) so by the rank-nullity theorem, it is bijective. A bijective positive semi-definite matrix is necessarily positive definite (if
v\neq 0
and
v^{\dagger}F(0)v=0
then
\norm{F(0)^{1/2} v}^{2}=0\Rightarrow F(0)^{1/2}v=0\Rightarrow v\in\ker F(0)=\{0\}
which is a contradiction). Hence,
F(0)>0
and the unique positive square root
F(0)^{1/2}
exists and is invertible. Define

U:A\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})\mapsto F(0)^{-1/2}S[A]F(0)^{1/2}\in GL(\mathbb{C}^{n})\,.

(23)

Then

U[A]U[A]^{\dagger}=F(0)^{-1/2}S[A]F(0)^{1/2}F(0)^{1/2}S[A]^{\dagger}F(0)^{-1/2}=F(0)^{-1/2}S[A]F(0)S[A]^{\dagger}F(0)^{-1/2}\\
=F(0)^{-1/2}F(0)F(0)^{-1/2}=\mathbb{1}_{n\times n}

(24)

so each
U[A]
is unitary. Moreover, for all
A,B\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
,

U[AB]=F(0)^{-1/2}S[AB]F(0)^{1/2}=(F(0)^{-1/2}S[A]F(0)^{1/2})(F(0)^{-1/2}S[B]F(0)^{1/2})=U[A]U[B]

(25)

hence
U:SL(2,\mathbb{C})\to U(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is a finite-dimensional unitary representation, which is a contradiction as
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
has no non-trivial finite-dimensional unitary representations. So
F(0)=0
. Likewise, eqn. (
18
) yields
G(0)=0
following the same argument. Therefore
F(0)=G(0)=0
. From
G(0)=0
we obtain, for each
\alpha
,

\|\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(0)\ket{\Omega}\|^{2}=\langle\Omega|\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(0)^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(0)|\Omega\rangle=g_{\alpha\alpha}(0)=0\,,

(26)

thus
\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(0)\ket{\Omega}=0
. Similarly
F(0)=0
yields
\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(0)^{\dagger}\ket{\Omega}=0
. Finally, for every
x\in\mathbb{M}
,

\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(x)\ket{\Omega}=U(x,e)\,\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(0)\,U(x,e)^{\dagger}\ket{\Omega}=U(x,e)\,\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(0)\ket{\Omega}=0\,,

(27)

and likewise from
F(0)=0
we get
\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(x)^{\dagger}\ket{\Omega}=0
so the proof is shown for nontrivial irreducible representations of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
. More generally, we decompose the finite-dimensional representation
S
into irreducibles as

S[A]=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}S^{(k)}[A]\,,\quad S^{(k)}[A]\in GL(\mathbb{C}^{d_{k}})\,,\quad\sum_{k=1}^{r}d_{k}=n\,.

(28)

Choose a basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
so that the indices corresponding to each sub-representation
S^{(k)}
form a contiguous block, i.e.

\mathbb{C}^{n}\cong\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}\mathbb{C}^{d_{k}}\,,

(29)

and the representation matrices
S[A]
are block-diagonal with
r
blocks
S^{(k)}[A]
on the diagonal.
For fixed
k
, consider the sub-multiplet of
\{\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}\}
whose indices lie in the block corresponding to
S^{(k)}
. These components inherit a Poincaré covariance law under
U
with the finite-dimensional representation
S^{(k)}
, but restricted to the relevant indices. Since each
S^{(k)}
is irreducible we have from the above argument that for each
\alpha
in that block and every
x\in\mathbb{M}
,

\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(x)\ket{\Omega}=\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}(x)^{\dagger}\ket{\Omega}=0\,.

(30)

Repeating this for all
k\in\{1,\cdots,r\}
covers all the components of the original multiplet and thus completes the proof.

Appendix B
No-go – spinor covariance for gravitons

We now provide a proof for Theorem
4
. Let

\displaystyle f_{\alpha\beta}(x)

\displaystyle:=\expectationvalue{\hat{\psi}_\alpha(0) \hat{\psi}_\beta(x)^\dagger}{\Omega}

(31)

\displaystyle g_{\alpha\beta}(x)

\displaystyle:=\expectationvalue{\hat{\psi}_\alpha(0)^\dagger\hat{\psi}_\beta(x)}{\Omega}

(32)

for all
x\in\mathbb{M}
. Note now that we have a unique non-zero translation invariant vacuum. But for all
A\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
,

U(0,A)\ket{\Omega}=U(0,A)U(x,e)\ket{\Omega}=U(\Lambda(A)x,e)U(0,A)\ket{\Omega}

(33)

so by uniqueness (and unitarity)
\exists\alpha(A)\in[0,2\pi)
such that
U(0,A)\ket{\Omega}=e^{i\alpha(A)}\ket{\Omega}

3
, we have

\displaystyle f_{\alpha\beta}(\Lambda(A)x)

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\hat{\psi}_\alpha(0)\hat{\psi}_\beta(\Lambda(A) x)^\dagger}{\Omega}

(34)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta=1}^{n}\overline{S[A]_{\beta\delta}}\expectationvalue{U(0,A^{-1})^\dagger U(0,A^{-1})\hat{\psi}_\alpha(0) U(0,A^{-1})^\dagger\hat{\psi}_\delta(x)^\dagger U(0,A^{-1})}{\Omega}

(35)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\alpha(A^{-1})}e^{i\alpha(A^{-1})}\sum_{\gamma,\delta=1}^{n}S[A]_{\alpha\gamma}\overline{S[A]_{\beta\delta}}\expectationvalue{\hat{\psi}_\gamma(0) \hat{\psi}_\delta(x)^\dagger}{\Omega}=\sum_{\gamma,\delta=1}^{n}S[A]_{\alpha\gamma}\overline{S[A]_{\beta\delta}}f_{\gamma\delta}(x)\,,

(36)

and likewise for
g_{\alpha\beta}
. Thus, we once again have

\displaystyle F(\Lambda(A)x)

\displaystyle=S[A]F(x)S[A]^{\dagger}

(37)

\displaystyle G(\Lambda(A)x)

\displaystyle=\overline{S[A]}G(x)S[A]^{T}

(38)

for all
x\in\mathbb{M}
and all
A\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
, where
F(x)=[f_{\alpha\beta}(x)]
and
G(x)=[g_{\alpha\beta}(x)]
.
Defining
\tilde{F}(x)=[f_{ij}(x)]

\tilde{G}(x)=[g_{ij}(x)]

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(\Lambda(A)x)

\displaystyle=D^{(j_{1},j_{2})}[A]\tilde{F}(x)D^{(j_{1},j_{2})}[A]^{\dagger}

(39)

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(\Lambda(A)x)

\displaystyle=\overline{D^{(j_{1},j_{2})}[A]}\tilde{G}(x)D^{(j_{1},j_{2})}[A]^{T}

(40)

Following the same line of argument as in Theorem
3
, we have
\tilde{G}(0)=\tilde{F}(0)=0
so

\hat{\psi}_{i}(x)\ket{\Omega}=\hat{\psi}_{i}(x)^{\dagger}\ket{\Omega}=0

(41)

for all
x\in\mathbb{M}
and all
i\in\{2,\cdots,n\}
. From eqns. (
37
) and (
38

\displaystyle f_{11}(\Lambda(A)x)

\displaystyle=f_{11}(x)

(42)

\displaystyle g_{11}(\Lambda(A)x)

\displaystyle=g_{11}(x)

(43)

for all
x\in\mathbb{M}

\displaystyle f_{11}(x)

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\hat{\psi}_1(0) U(x,e) \hat{\psi}_1(0)^\dagger}{\Omega}

(44)

\displaystyle g_{11}(x)

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\hat{\psi}_1(0)^\dagger U(x,e) \hat{\psi}_1(0)}{\Omega}\,,

(45)

so
f_{11}
and

. By Bochner’s theorem
[
10

, there exists a finite positive Borel measure
\mu_{11}:\text{Bor}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\to\mathbb{R^{+}}

f_{11}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{ip\cdot x}d\mu_{11}(p)\,.

(46)

The covariance property of
f_{11}(x)

\mu_{11}

f_{11}(\Lambda(A)x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{ip\cdot\Lambda(A)x}d\mu_{11}(p)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{i\Lambda(A)^{-1}p\cdot x}d\mu_{11}(p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q:=\Lambda(A)^{-1}p}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{iq\cdot x}d[(\Lambda(A)^{-1})_{*}\mu_{11}](q)\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu^{\Lambda}_{11}:=(\Lambda(A)^{-1})_{*}\mu_{11}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{ip\cdot x}d\mu^{\Lambda}_{11}(p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{ip\cdot x}d\mu_{11}(p)

(47)

\ket{\eta}:=\hat{\psi}_{1}(0)\ket{\Omega}

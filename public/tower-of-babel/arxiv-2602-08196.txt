Anderson localization on quantum graphs coded by elements of a subshift of finite type
Oleg Safronov

Abstract
. We study Schrödinger operators on quantum graphs where the number of edges between points is determined by orbits of a ”shift of finite type”. We prove Anderson localization for these systems.



1.
Main result

This paper extends findings from
[
27
]
regarding the positivity of the Lyapunov exponent in quantum graphs with a variable number of edges.
By combining this positivity with large deviation estimates, we establish spectral characteristics and prove localization for these systems.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

\Bigl|\ln\|A^{(n)}\dots A^{(1)}\|+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\ln\|A^{(j)}\|-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\ln\|A^{(j+1)}A^{(j)}\|\leqslant C\frac{n}{\lambda}.

4.
Hölder continuity of the Lyapunov exponent

The main credit for the results of this section belongs to the authors of
[
10
]
as the core methodology was established in their original paper.
We only need to adjust it to the specific setting.

Let us define
L_{n}(E)
as the integral

L_{n}(E)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega}\ln\|A_{n}^{E}(\omega)\|\,d\mu

(4.1)

The following lemma is proved in
[
10
]
for a different cocycle, but one can easily see that the proof works in our case too.

Lemma 4.1
.

There are constants
c_{0},C_{0}>0
that depend on
\Gamma
and the set
I
,
such that

|L(E)-L_{n}(E)-2L_{2n}(E)|\leqslant C_{0}e^{-c_{0}n},\qquad\text{for all}\quad E\in I.

(4.2)

Proof.
First, choose
\varepsilon>0
so small
that

0<\frac{4\varepsilon}{\gamma-\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2},\qquad\text{where}\quad\gamma=\sup_{E\in I}L(E).

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the constant
c>0
in LDE satisfies the condition

c<4(\gamma-\varepsilon).

Since we only need to prove (
4.1
) for large values of
n
, we may assume that
(\ln n)/c>1
. Then
there is an integer number
N
such that

e^{cN/5}\leqslant n\leqslant e^{cN/4},\quad\text{which is smaller than}\quad e^{(L-\varepsilon)N}.

(4.3)

We will also assume that
2nCe^{-cN}\leqslant e^{-cN/4}
.
For each
\omega\in\Omega
, consider the matrices

A^{(j)}(\omega)=A^{E}_{N}(T^{(j-1)N}\omega)

According to the ULD, if
B(n)
is the set of
\omega
’s on which one of the inequalities

\Bigl|\frac{1}{N}\ln\|A^{(j)}(\omega)\|-L\Bigr|<\varepsilon,\qquad\Bigl|\frac{1}{N}\ln\|A^{(j+1)}(\omega)A^{(j)}(\omega)\|-L\Bigr|<\varepsilon

(4.4)

does not hold for some
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
, then

\mu(B(n))\leqslant e^{-cN/4}.

(4.5)

In particular,

\|A^{(j)}\|\geqslant e^{(L-\varepsilon)N}>n

and

\Bigl|\ln\|A^{(j+1)}(\omega)\|+\ln\|A^{(j)}(\omega)\|-\ln\|A^{(j+1)}(\omega)A^{(j)}(\omega)\|\Bigr|<4N\varepsilon

for all
\omega\notin B(n)
.
Applying the Avalanche Principle with
\lambda=e^{(L-\varepsilon)N}
, we obtain that

\Bigl|\ln\|A^{E}_{nN}(\omega)\|+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\ln\|A_{N}^{E}(T^{(j-1)N})\|-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\ln\|A_{2N}^{E}(T^{(j-1)N})\|\Bigr|\leqslant C\frac{n}{\lambda}.

Conseqently,

\Bigl|L_{nN}+\frac{(n-2)}{n}L_{N}-\frac{2(n-1)}{n}L_{2N}\Bigr|\leqslant\frac{C}{\lambda N}+4\mu(B(n))\ln\Gamma\leqslant Ce^{-cN/4}.

which leads to the inequality

\Bigl|L_{nN}+L_{N}-2L_{2N}\Bigr|\leqslant Ce^{-cN/4}+\frac{4}{n}\ln\Gamma\leqslant Ce^{-cN/5}.

Now, consider a sequence of indices
n_{s}
such that
n_{s+1}
is an integer multiple of
n_{s}
, and

e^{cn_{s}/5}\leqslant\frac{n_{s+1}}{n_{s}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}e^{cn_{s}/4}.

Setting
N=n_{s}
and
n
equal to either
\frac{n_{s+1}}{n_{s}}
or
2\frac{n_{s+1}}{n_{s}}
, we obtain two estimates

\Bigl|L_{n_{s+1}}+L_{n_{s}}-2L_{n_{s}}\Bigr|\leqslant Ce^{-cn_{s}/5},

(4.6)

and

\Bigl|L_{2n_{s+1}}+L_{n_{s}}-2L_{n_{s}}\Bigr|\leqslant Ce^{-cn_{s}/5}.

(4.7)

Combining these inequalities (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we obtain

\Bigl|L_{2n_{s+1}}-L_{n_{s+1}}\Bigr|\leqslant 2Ce^{-cn_{s}/5}.

(4.8)

Now we see that (
4.6
) and (
4.8
) imply

\Bigl|L_{n_{s+1}}-L_{n_{s}}\Bigr|\leqslant\tilde{C}e^{-cn_{s-1}/5},\qquad\text{for}\quad s\geqslant 2.

Therefore,

\Bigl|L-L_{n_{2}}\Bigr|=\Bigl|\sum_{s=2}^{\infty}(L_{n_{s+1}}-L_{n_{s}})\Bigr|\leqslant Ce^{-cn_{1}/5}

Thus, writing (
4.6
) with
s=1
and replacing
L_{n_{2}}
by
L
in the resulting inequality, we obtain

\Bigl|L+L_{n_{1}}-2L_{n_{1}}\Bigr|\leqslant Ce^{-cn_{1}/5}.

It remains to observe that the first member
n_{1}
of the sequence of indices was an arbitrary sufficiently large number.
\,\,\,\,\Box

Theorem 4.2
.

There is a constant
C>0
and an exponent
\beta>0
such that

|L(E)-L(E^{\prime})|\leqslant C|E-E^{\prime}|^{\beta}

(4.9)

for all
E,E^{\prime}\in I
.

Proof
. Observe that

|L_{n}(E)-L_{n}(E^{\prime})|\leqslant C_{\ell,I}\Gamma^{n-1}|E-E^{\prime}|

Thus, according to the preceding lemma, we have

|L(E)-L(E^{\prime})|\leqslant 3C_{\ell,I}\Gamma^{2n-1}|E-E^{\prime}|+C_{0}e^{-c_{0}n}

for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Put differently, there is a constant
\tilde{C}>1

such that

|L(E)-L(E^{\prime})|\leqslant(\tilde{C})^{n}|E-E^{\prime}|+\tilde{C}e^{-c_{0}n}

Choosing
n=\Bigl[\frac{\ln|E-E^{\prime}|^{-1}}{3\ln\tilde{C}}\Bigr]
, we obtain

|L(E)-L(E^{\prime})|\leqslant|E-E^{\prime}|^{2/3}+\tilde{C}|E-E^{\prime}|^{\frac{c_{0}}{3\ln\tilde{C}}}.

The proof is complete.

Center of the affine 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n | 1 \mathfrak{gl}_{n|1} at the critical level and pseudo-differential operators
Dražen Adamović, Boris Feigin, Shigenori Nakatsuka

Abstract
. We prove that the center of the affine Lie algebra 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ^ n | 1 \widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n|1} at the critical level is generated by the coefficients in the expansion of the pseudo-differential operator ( ∂ z − u 1 ( z ) ) ⋯ ( ∂ z − u n ( z ) ) ( ∂ z + u n + 1 ( z ) ) − 1 (\partial_{z}-u_{1}(z))\cdots(\partial_{z}-u_{n}(z))(\partial_{z}+u_{n+1}(z))^{-1} taking values in the Cartan subalgebra. This is an affine analogue of the Harish-Chandra isomorphism in the finite case. The key ingredient of the proof is the identification of the center with the Heisenberg coset of the regular 𝒲 \mathcal{W} -superalgebra of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n | 1 \mathfrak{gl}_{n|1} at the critical level, whose associated graded algebra is realized as the affine supersymmetric polynomials. Based on this, we derive a character formula for the center, which coincides with the generating function of plane partitions with a pit condition. We also prove that the Heisenberg coset at generic levels has a similar interpretation in terms of pseudo-differential operators that deform the one at the critical level.



1.
Introduction

The description of the center of the universal enveloping algebra
of a finite-dimensional reductive Lie algebra has played a crucial role in the study of representation theory. The Harish-Chandra isomorphism identifies the center

\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
with the algebra of Weyl group invariants in the symmetric
algebra of a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
.
This provides fundamental insights into the structure of the BGG category,
such as the linkage principle.

The affine analogue was conjectured by Drinfeld and established by one of the authors and E. Frenkel
[
16
]
.
More precisely, one considers the affine Lie algebras
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
at level
\kappa
and studies the center of the completed enveloping algebra
U_{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
. The completion reflects the fact that
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
is a one-dimensional central extension of the loop algebra
\mathfrak{g}(\!(z)\!)
parametrized by the level
\kappa
. The center turns out to be non-trivial at one distinguished level, called the critical level
\kappa_{c}
. When
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
, the center is identified with the regular functions on the moduli of certain differential operators, called
\check{\mathfrak{g}}
-opers, over the punctured disc, where
\check{\mathfrak{g}}
is the Langlands dual of
\mathfrak{g}
.
This gives a “spectral decomposition” of the category of smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa_{c}}
-representations parametrized by
\check{\mathfrak{g}}
-opers, which was one of the earliest results on the geometric Langlands program
[
21
]
.

The category of smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa_{c}}
-modules is canonically identified with the category of modules over the affine vertex algebra
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Indeed, the formulation of vertex algebras fits well in this context: both
U_{\kappa_{c}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
and its center are naturally identified with the enveloping algebras of
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
and its center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
as vertex algebras. In this formulation, we have the affine analogue of the Harish-Chandra isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))\simeq\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}},

where
\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}
is the Heisenberg vertex algebra associated with the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
at level zero, and
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
is the subalgebra characterized by the joint kernel of screening operators, the analogue of the Weyl group invariants in the finite setting.
When
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
,
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
is generated by the coefficients of the expansion of the differential operator

(\partial_{z}-u_{1}(z))\cdots(\partial_{z}-u_{n-1}(z))(\partial_{z}-u_{n}(z))

or symbolically
(\partial-u_{1})\cdots(\partial-u_{n-1})(\partial-u_{n})
in the language of vertex algebras where
u_{i}
’s are the basis of the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
.
The algebra
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
is called
the
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
defined as the BRST reduction of
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})

\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{H}_{\mathbb{O}_{\mathrm{reg}}}^{0}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))

associated with the regular nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}_{\mathrm{reg}}\subset\mathfrak{g}
at the critical level
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
.
This is the quantum affine analogue of the Kostant slice
[
32
]
, which is naturally isomorphic to the GIT quotient
\mathfrak{g}/\!/G
of the action of the adjoint group
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

The
\mathcal{W}
-algebras enjoy the duality, known as the Feigin-Frenkel duality
[
16
]
,

\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})\simeq\mathcal{W}^{\check{\kappa}}(\check{\mathfrak{g}})

where the levels
\kappa,\check{\kappa}
satisfy a duality relation.
This is a deformation of the one ar the critical level
(\kappa,\check{\kappa})=(\kappa_{c},\infty)
, which recovers the description of
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
by
\check{\mathfrak{g}}
-opers mentioned above, and plays an important role in the quantum geometric Langlands program e.g.
[
7
,
24
]
.

While the representation theory of the
\mathcal{W}
-algebras
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})
serves as the Whittaker models of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
-modules, the generalization to
\mathcal{W}
-algebras
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{O})
associated with general nilpotent orbits
\mathbb{O}
are related to degenerate Whittaker models.
The generalization of the Feigin-Frenkel duality for
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{O})
or its geometric/categorical analogues has been studied in various contexts of mathematics and physics (e.g.
[
11
,
13
,
14
,
23
]
). It turns out that they meet the
\mathcal{W}
-superalgebras
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{O})
associated with basic-classical Lie
superalgebras
. This motivates revisiting the problem of describing the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
via differential operators, which has remained open for decades for basic-classical Lie superalgebras.

The main results of this paper, below, resolve this problem by using
pseudo
-differential operators and establish related combinatorial results for the affine Lie superalgebras
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n|1}
, which are the first nontrivial family in the super case.

Theorem A

(Theorem
6.3
/
6.4
/
7.1
)
.

(1)

There is an embedding of vertex algebras

\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))\hookrightarrow\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{0},

whose image is generated by the coefficients of the pseudo-differential operator

(\partial_{z}-u_{1}(z))\cdots(\partial_{z}-u_{n}(z))(\partial_{z}+u_{n+1}(z))^{-1}.

(2)

There is a filtration on
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))
whose associated graded differential algebra is isomorphic to the algebra of affine supersymmetric polynomials:

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))\simeq\Lambda^{n|1}_{\mathrm{aff}}.

(3)

The
q
-character of the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))
is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}[\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))](q)=\frac{\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}q^{\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)}(q^{m+1};q)_{n-1}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}(q,\cdots,q^{n-1};q)_{\infty}}.

We note that (2) was conjectured by Molev and Mukhin
[
38
]
(established for
n=1,2

[
2
,
38
]
) and implies that the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))
is strongly generated by higher Segal-Sugawara vectors constructed by Molev and Ragoucy
[
39
]
.
The
q
-series appearing on the right-hand side in (3) agrees with the generating function of
plane partitions
with the pit condition
(2,n+1)
(Corollary
7.3
). This confirms a conjecture by Molev and Mukhin, generalizing the case
n=1

[
1
,
38
]
.

The key observation of the proof of Theorem
A
is the identification of the centers

\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))\simeq\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))

(Theorem

). This is a generalization of the case

in our earlier work
[

]
, which played the main role in identifying
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))
with the large level limit of the parafermion vertex algebra
K^{\infty}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2})
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
. We note that the identification of the center
\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{O}))
with
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
in the non-super case is well-known in general
[
5
,
9
]
.
The center
\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))
can be
deformed
to other levels, as it is the specialization of the Heisenberg coset subalgebra

\displaystyle C^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})=\operatorname{Com}\left(\pi_{J},\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})\right)\subset\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})

at the critical level (Theorem
3.2
), see Section

for details. This coset subalgebra

at generic levels has several properties similar to those of the regular
\mathcal{W}
-algebras, such as a variant of the Feigin-Frenkel duality
[

,
18
]
.
Indeed, the results in Theorem

can be deformed outside the critical levels.
Among them is the following result originally conjectured by T. Procházka in physics (e.g.
[
40
]
).

Theorem B

(Theorem
3.6
)
.

There is an embedding of vertex algebras

C^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})\hookrightarrow\pi_{\mathfrak{t}}^{k+\mathrm{h}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\vee$}}}

for

whose image at the generic levels is generated by the coefficients of the pseudo-differential operator

where
\varepsilon=1/(-1+k+\mathrm{h}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\vee$}})
.

This result implies that the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))
realized as the regular functions of the pseudo-differential operator in Theorem

(1) inherits a natural Poisson vertex algebra structure restricting on the standard one on
\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}
(Proposition

In future works, we plan to describe the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
for basic-classical (simple) Lie superalgebras through pseudo-differential operators (hopefully related to superopers
[
42
]
and Gaudin models
[

,
36
]
) and connect the category of spherical

-representations to quasi-coherent sheaves over such operators
[
22
]
.

2.1.
Affine vertex superalgebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
be the general linear Lie superalgebra associated with the vector superspace
V=\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
and
e_{i,j}
(
i,j=1,\dots,m+n
) be the
(i,j)
-th elementary matrix.
We take the even supersymmetric invariant bilinear form on
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
to be

\displaystyle\kappa=(k+\mathrm{h}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\vee$}})\kappa_{V}-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}

where
\mathrm{h}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\vee$}}=n-m
is the dual Coxeter number, and
\kappa_{V}
and
\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}
are the supertrace on
V
and the Killing form, that is,

\displaystyle\kappa_{V}(u,v)=\operatorname{str}_{V}(uv),\quad\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}=\operatorname{str}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{ad}_{u}\operatorname{ad}_{v}).

Let us identify
\kappa
with
k
in the below.
The affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is the central extension of the loop algebra
\mathfrak{g}[t^{\pm 1}]

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathbb{C}K\rightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}[t^{\pm 1}]\rightarrow 0

with Lie bracket

\displaystyle[u_{(p)},v_{(q)}]:=[u,v]_{(p+q)}+\kappa(u,v)p\delta_{p+q,0}K,\quad[K,\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}]=0

where
u_{(p)}=ut^{p}
.
The (universal) affine vertex superalgebra associated with
\mathfrak{g}
at level
\kappa
is defined as the parabolic induction

\displaystyle V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}):=U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}[t]\oplus\mathbb{C}K)}\mathbb{C}

induced from the
(\mathfrak{g}[t]\oplus\mathbb{C}K)
-module
\mathbb{C}
where
\mathfrak{g}[t]
acts by
0
and
K
by the identity, equipped with a unique vertex algebra structure with the unit
\mathbf{1}=1\otimes 1
, the translation operator
\partial
satisfying
[\partial,u_{(p)}]=-pu_{(p-1)}
, and the state-field correspondence
Y(\cdot,z)
satisfying
Y(u,z)=\sum u_{(p)}z^{-p-1}
were
u
is identified with
u_{(-1)}\otimes\mathbf{1}
. It will be convenient to use the
\lambda
-bracket

[a{}_{\lambda}b]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}a_{(n)}b\lambda^{n}

instead of the commutator formula

[Y(a,z),Y(b,w)]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Y(a_{(n)}b,w)\frac{1}{n!}\partial_{w}^{n}\delta(z-w)

by using the delta function
\delta(z-w)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}z^{n}w^{-n-1}
.

Note that for
m\neq n
as we will consider otherwise stated, the decomposition
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}=\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n}\oplus\mathbb{C}\Omega
with
\Omega=\sum e_{i,i}
induces an isomorphism of vertex superalgebras

(2.1)

\displaystyle V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n})\simeq V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n})\otimes\pi^{\kappa}_{\Omega}.

Here
\pi^{\kappa}_{\Omega}
is the Heisenberg vertex algebra
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1})
with
\mathfrak{gl}_{1}=\mathbb{C}\Omega
as we have
[u{}_{\lambda}\Omega]=0
for all
u\in\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle[u{}_{\lambda}v]=[u,v]+k\kappa_{V}(u,v),\quad[\Omega{}_{\lambda}\Omega]=(m-n)(k+\mathrm{h}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\vee$}})\lambda

hold for
u,v\in\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n}
.

The level
\kappa=-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}
, denoted by
\kappa_{c}
is called
the critical level
; the bilinear form is explicitly given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{c}(e_{i,j},e_{p,q})=\delta_{i,j}\delta_{p,q}(-1)^{p(i)+p(q)}-(m-n)\delta_{i,j}\delta_{p,q}(-1)^{p(i)}

where
p(i)
is the parity, which is 0 (resp. 1) if
i\leqslant m
(resp.
i>m
).

Given a vertex superalgebra
V
and a subalgebra
W
, the coset
\operatorname{Com}(W,V)
is the subalgebra defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Com}(W,V):=\{u\in V;[Y(u,z),Y(v,w)]=0\ (\forall v\in W)\}.

In particular, the coset subalgebra
\mathfrak{z}(V):=\operatorname{Com}(V,V)

is called the center of
V
.
For a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))
is known to be trivial
\mathbb{C}
at
\kappa\neq\kappa_{c}
, whereas non-trivial at the critical level
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
.
The difference comes from the existence of the so-called Segal-Sugawara conformal vector
L
given by

L=\frac{1}{k+\mathrm{h}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\vee$}}}S,\qquad S=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\dim\mathfrak{g}}(-1)^{p(x_{i})}x_{i}x^{i}

where
x_{i}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
with dual basis
x^{i}
satisfying
\kappa_{V}(x_{i},x^{j})=\delta_{i,j}
.
While
S
is defined for all levels and satisfies

[S{}_{\lambda}u]=(k+\mathrm{h}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\vee$}})(\partial u+u\lambda)

for all
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,
L
is defined only outside the critical level.
The above formula implies that
S
is central at
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
while the rescaled one
L
provides a conical internal grading structure, which implies
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))=\mathbb{C}
for all
\kappa\neq\kappa_{c}
.

2.2.
\mathcal{W}
-superalgebras

In the finite case, the description of the center
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\subset U(\mathfrak{g})
for basic-classical simple Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}
is obtained through the Harish-Chandra homomorphism

\xi\colon U(\mathfrak{g})\twoheadrightarrow U(\mathfrak{h})

where
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra, and is called the Harish-Chandra isomorphism, see
[
37
]
.
In the even case, it gives rise to an isomorphism
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\simeq U(\mathfrak{h})^{W}
where

is the Weyl group. An alternative approach is to quantize the Kostant slice, which is defined as the subvariety
\mathscr{S}=f+\mathfrak{g}^{e}\subset\mathfrak{g}
by using the regular (a.k.a principal)
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{e,h,f\}\subset\mathfrak{g}
.
Such a triple exists uniquely up to conjugation by the adjoint group
G
whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{g}
. Then,
\mathscr{S}
is isomorphic to the categorical quotient
\mathfrak{g}/\!/G
. The slice
\mathscr{S}
is obtained as a Hamiltonian reduction of
\mathfrak{g}
for the unipotent group
N_{+}
associated with the upper nilpotent subalgebra
\mathfrak{n}_{+}
and the character
\chi=\kappa_{V}(f,-)\colon\mathfrak{n}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.
The Hamiltonian reduction is quantized to the so-called BRST reduction of
U(\mathfrak{g})
to obtain
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
, whose vertex algebraic analogue was introduced
[
16
]
to describe the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
, which by construction can be deformed to other levels and called the regular
\mathcal{W}
-algebras
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})
.

More generally, the
\mathcal{W}
-superalgebras are vertex superalgebras
[
30
,
31
]
obtained from affine vertex superalgeras

associated with basic-classical Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{g}
at level
\kappa
through the BRST reduction associated with an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{e,h,f\}
and a good
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
-grading
\Gamma\colon\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{d}\mathfrak{g}_{d}
. For simplicity, we consider the case where the grading
\Gamma
is called even, i.e., a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading.
Let us consider
\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\bigoplus_{d>0}\mathfrak{g}_{d}
and take a (parity-homogeneous) basis

(
\alpha\in S_{+}
) with structure constants
c_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}
, i.e.
[e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}]=\sum c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}e_{\gamma}
.

Let
\bigwedge{}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})
be the charged fermion vertex superalgebra generated by
\psi_{\alpha},\psi_{\alpha}^{*}
(
\alpha\in S_{+}
) which are of opposite parity to
e_{\alpha}
and satisfy the
\lambda
-brackets

\displaystyle[\psi_{\alpha}{}_{\lambda}\psi_{\beta}^{*}]=\delta_{\alpha,\beta},\quad[\psi_{\alpha}{}_{\lambda}\psi_{\beta}]=[\psi_{\alpha}^{*}{}_{\lambda}\varphi_{\beta}^{*}]=0.

Then, we associate the BRST complex

(2.2)

\displaystyle C^{\bullet}_{f}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})):=V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\bigwedge{}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})

equipped with the differential

where

\displaystyle Q=\sum_{\alpha\in S_{+}}(-1)^{p({e}_{\alpha})}(e_{\alpha}+(f|e_{\alpha}))\psi_{\alpha}^{*}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in S_{+}}(-1)^{p({e}_{\alpha})p({e}_{\gamma})}c_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\psi_{\gamma}\psi_{\alpha}^{*}\varphi_{\beta}^{*}

where

denote the normalized invariant bilinear form on

By
[
31
]

\mathcal{W}
-superalgebra associated with

at level
\kappa
. Since it only depends on the orbit

containing

and usually

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{O})=\mathrm{H}^{0}(C_{f}^{\bullet}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))).

Note that by replacing

in (

) with

-modules

Proof.

\displaystyle S\ W_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{n}(-1)^{p+1}(p+1)\binom{n}{p+1}

Recall that given a vertex superalgebra

Proposition 3.7

implies that their differential monomials form the same PBW basis as those of the generators corresponding to
\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{(1)},\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{(2)},\mathfrak{q}_{2},\cdots,\mathfrak{q}_{n}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.4.
Critical level

Let
\mathbb{A}^{N}
denote the affine scheme whose regular functions are just a polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\cdots,z_{N}]
and
J_{\infty}\mathbb{A}^{N}
its jet scheme. The algebra of the regular functions
\mathbb{C}[J_{\infty}\mathbb{A}^{N}]
is a commutative vertex algebra (i.e., a differential algebra) and again a polynomial ring

\mathbb{C}[J_{\infty}\mathbb{A}^{N}]=\mathbb{C}[\partial^{p}z_{1},\cdots,\partial^{p}z_{N}\mid p\geqslant 0].

Theorem 4.7
.

For the critical level
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
, there are isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})\simeq\mathbb{C}[J_{\infty}\mathbb{A}^{n-1}]\otimes V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n|1})\simeq\mathbb{C}[J_{\infty}\mathbb{A}^{n-2}]\otimes V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}).

Proof.

It suffices to show the assertion for
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})
.
By Proposition
3.6
, the gererators
W_{1},\cdots,W_{n}
lie in the center
\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})
and thus the first
(n-1)
elements generate a central subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}[J_{\infty}\mathbb{A}^{n-1}]
, i.e., there is an embedding

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[J_{\infty}\mathbb{A}^{n-1}]\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}),\quad z_{i}\mapsto W_{i}\ (i=1,\cdots,n-1).

We will show that
G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\pm$}}
,
J
and
W_{n}
after necessary modification generate a subalgebra isomorphic to
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
and thus

\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})\simeq\mathbb{C}[J_{\infty}\mathbb{A}^{n-1}]\otimes V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}).

It is straightforward to show

\displaystyle[J{}_{\lambda}J]=\lambda,\quad[J{}_{\lambda}G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\pm$}}]=\pm G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\pm$}}.

and

[G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$+$}}{}_{\lambda}G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$+$}}]=0,\quad[G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$-$}}{}_{\lambda}G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$-$}}]=0,

thanks to the weight reason.
Next, we compute the
\lambda
-bracket
[G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$+$}}{}_{\lambda}G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$-$}}]
.
Since we have

\displaystyle R_{i}=-(\partial+x+H_{i}),\quad H_{i}=-(h_{i}+\cdots+h_{n}).

Hence, one has
R_{n}\mathrm{e}^{{-x}}=-H_{i}\mathrm{e}^{{-x}}
. By induction, one can show that

\displaystyle R_{i}\cdots R_{n}\mathrm{e}^{{-x}}=(-1)^{n+1-i}\big((\partial+H_{i})\cdots(\partial+H_{n})\mathbf{1}\big)\otimes\mathrm{e}^{{-x}}\in\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}\otimes V_{\mathbb{Z}}

since
H_{1},\dots H_{n}
are in the commutative vertex algebra
\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}
.
Hence,

G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$-$}}=R_{1}\cdots R_{n}\mathrm{e}^{{-x}}=(-1)^{n}\big((\partial+H_{1})\cdots(\partial+H_{n})\mathbf{1}\big)\otimes\mathrm{e}^{{-x}}

and thus

\displaystyle[G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$+$}}{}_{\lambda}G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$-$}}]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\big((\partial+H_{1})\cdots(\partial+H_{n})\mathbf{1}\big)[\mathrm{e}^{{x}}{}_{\lambda}\mathrm{e}^{{-x}}]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}(\partial+H_{1})\cdots(\partial+H_{n})\mathbf{1}.

Let us set
\mathscr{W}_{n}:=(-1)^{n}(\partial+H_{1})\cdots(\partial+H_{n})\mathbf{1}
. Then, it is clear that we have an embedding of vertex superalgebras

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccc}V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})&\hookrightarrow&\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})\\
e_{1,1},\ e_{2,2}&\mapsto&J,\ -J+\mathscr{W}_{n},\\
e_{1,2},\ e_{2,1}&\mapsto&G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$+$}},\ G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$-$}}.\end{array}

Next, we consider the image
\pi(\overline{\mathscr{W}}_{n})
as in §
3.4
.
Since
\overline{H}_{i}=-(e_{i,i}+e_{n+1,n+1})
, we have
\pi(\overline{H}_{i})=-e_{i,i}

\pi(\overline{\mathscr{W}}_{n})=e_{1,1}\cdots e_{n,n}=\varepsilon^{-n}\pi(\overline{W}_{n}).

Hence, there exists a differential polynomial
F(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
such that

\mathscr{W}_{n}=\varepsilon^{-n}W_{n}+F(J,W_{1},\cdots,W_{n-1})

by Theorem
4.6
.
Thus, we may take the strong generators at the critical level
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
as
J,W_{1},\cdots,W_{n-1},\mathscr{W}_{n},G_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\pm$}}
.
Thus, the embeddings (
4.7
) and (

) combine to induce the isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n|1})\simeq\mathbb{C}[J_{\infty}\mathbb{A}^{n-2}]\otimes V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})

as desired. This completes the proof.
∎

N_{-}\backslash G\times G\rightarrow G.

By restricting to the open subset

\displaystyle\rho\colon U(\mathfrak{g})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}(N_{+})\otimes U(\mathfrak{h}).

\displaystyle[a_{\alpha}{}_{\lambda}a_{\beta}^{*}]=\delta_{\alpha,\beta},\quad[a_{\alpha}{}_{\lambda}a_{\beta}]=[a_{\alpha}^{*}{}_{\lambda}a_{\beta}^{*}]=0.

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists an embedding of vertex superalgebras

\displaystyle\widehat{\rho}\colon V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}\otimes\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\kappa-\kappa_{c}}.

\displaystyle V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})\simeq\bigcap\operatorname{Ker}S_{i}\subset\mathcal{A}\otimes\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\kappa-\kappa_{c}}

Lemma 5.2
.

At the critical level

\displaystyle\widehat{\rho}\colon\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))\hookrightarrow\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathcal{A}\otimes\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}.

At the critical level

The center

for

Introduce the polynomial ring

The ring of supersymmetric polynomilas

\displaystyle\Lambda^{m|n}:=\left\{f;f|_{u_{m}=t,v_{n}=-t}\in R^{m-1|n-1}\right\}\subset(R^{m|n})^{\mathfrak{S}_{m}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\subset R^{m|n}.

The ring of affine supersymmetric polynomials

is the (commutative) vertex algebraic extension of

R^{m|n}_{\infty}:=\mathbb{C}[\partial^{N}u_{1},\dots,\partial^{N}u_{m},\partial^{N}v_{1},\dots,\partial^{N}v_{n}\mid N\geqslant 0]\simeq\mathbb{C}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{h}]

and introduce

as the subalgebra generated by

as a differential algebra:

\Lambda^{m|n}_{\mathrm{aff}}:=\langle\partial^{N}f\mid f\in\Lambda^{m|n},N\geqslant 0\rangle\subset R^{m|n}_{\infty}.

6.1
through the Wakimoto realization in Proposition
5.1
.

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}\colon\operatorname{gr}V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\pi^{k+\mathrm{h}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\vee$}}}_{\mathfrak{h}})\simeq\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{A}\otimes\operatorname{gr}\pi^{k+\mathrm{h}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\vee$}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}.

.

There is an embedding

\Lambda^{n|1}_{\mathrm{aff}}\subset\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})).

Proof.

Note that one has an isomorphism

which maps

\displaystyle u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\mapsto e_{1,1},\dots,e_{n,n},\quad v_{1}\mapsto e_{n+1,n+1}.

Hence, it suffices to show that

contains the power sums

(

) as
\overline{\Psi}
is an embedding and restricts to

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}\colon\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))\hookrightarrow\operatorname{gr}\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}

by Lemma

By the formula in Proposition

,
the image of the Segal–Sugawara vector

agrees with

\displaystyle\operatorname{str}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}e_{1,1}&&&\\

7.
Character formula and plane partitions

In this section, we derive the character formula of the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))
.
Let us introduce the Pochhammer symbol

\displaystyle(z;q)_{n}=(1-z)(1-zq)\cdots(1-zq^{n-1})

for
n=0,1,\cdots,\infty
and the multi-variable one

\displaystyle(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m};q)_{n}=(z_{1};q)_{n}\cdots(z_{m};q)_{n}.

Theorem 7.1
.

For all
n\geqslant 1
, the following formula holds:

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}[\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))](q)=\frac{\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}q^{\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)}(q^{m+1};q)_{n-1}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}(q,\cdots,q^{n-1};q)_{\infty}}.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.3
(1) and Theorem
3.2
(2), there is an isomorphism of quasi-conformal (i.e., graded) vertex algebras

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))\simeq C^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}).

By the decomposition (
3.5
),
C^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})\simeq C^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})
as graded vector spaces for generic levels
\kappa
, and thus

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}[\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))](q)=\operatorname{ch}[C^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})](q)

For convenience, we compute the character on the right-hand side via the duality Theorem
4.4
. Since

\operatorname{ch}[\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathbb{O}_{\mathrm{sr}})](q)=\frac{1}{(q,q,\cdots,q^{n-1},zq,z^{-1}q^{n-1};q)_{\infty}},

we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}[C^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1})](q)

\displaystyle=\underset{z=0}{\mathrm{Res}}\frac{1}{(q,\cdots,q^{n-1},zq,z^{-1}q^{n-1};q)_{\infty}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}

(7.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q,\cdots,q^{n-1};q)_{\infty}}\underset{z=0}{\mathrm{Res}}\frac{1}{(zq,z^{-1}q^{n-1};q)_{\infty}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z},

where
\underset{z=0}{\mathrm{Res}}f(z)\frac{dz}{z}=f_{0}
denotes the residue of the series
f(z)=\sum_{n}f_{n}z^{n}
. We note that the formula (
7.1
) for
n=1
holds by using the
\beta\gamma
-system instead of
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathbb{O}_{\mathrm{sr}})
, thanks to
[
14
]
.
It follows from the expansion

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(zq,z^{-1}q;q)_{\infty}}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\Phi_{s}(q)-\Phi_{s-1}(q)\right)z^{s}

by the unary false theta functions
\Phi_{s}(q)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}q^{\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)}q^{ms}

[
3
]
and the expansion of the symmetric functions

(7.2)

\displaystyle(1+x_{1}w)\cdots(1+x_{N}w)=\sum_{a=0}^{N}e_{a}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})w^{a},

we obtain

\displaystyle\underset{z=0}{\mathrm{Res}}\frac{1}{(zq,z^{-1}q^{n-1};q)_{\infty}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}=\underset{z=0}{\mathrm{Res}}\frac{(z^{-1}q;q)_{n-2}}{(zq,z^{-1}q;q)_{\infty}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{a=0}^{n-2}(\Phi_{a}(q)-\Phi_{a-1}(q))(-1)^{a}e_{a}(q,\cdots,q^{n-2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}q^{\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)}(1-q^{-m})\sum_{a=0}^{n-2}q^{ma}(-1)^{a}e_{a}(q,\cdots,q^{n-2})

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{=}\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}q^{\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)}(1-q^{-m})(1-q^{m+1})\cdots(1-q^{m+n-2})

\displaystyle\overset{(2)}{=}\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}q^{\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)}(1-q^{m+1})(1-q^{m+2})\cdots(1-q^{m+n-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}q^{\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)}(q^{m+1};q)_{n-1}.

Here, we have used (
7.2
) in (1); we have changed the sum
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
into
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
, as the term
m=0
is zero, and then
\sum_{m^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}
by setting

in (2).
By plugging the right-hand side into (
7.1
), we obtain the desired formula.
∎

The
q
-series appearing on the right-hand side of Theorem
7.1
is understood as the generating function of plane partitions with a pit condition found in the theory of
q
-deformations of (universal)
\mathcal{W}
-algebras, called the quantum toroidal algebra
\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{1}

[
10
,
19
]
.
Let us briefly recall the plane partitions.
A plane partition a of a nonnegative integer
N
(or a plane partition of
weight
N
) is, by definition, a set of non-negative integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{i,j})_{i,j>0}
, satisfying the conditions

(1)

\lim\lambda_{i,j}=0
as
i\rightarrow\infty
or
j\rightarrow\infty
;

(2)

\lambda_{i,j}\geqslant\lambda_{i,j+1}
and
\lambda_{i,j}\geqslant\lambda_{i+1,j}
for all
i,j
.

The sum
|\lambda|=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i,j}
is called the weight of the plane partition
\lambda
. The set of plane partitions is denoted by
PP
.

Note that each plane partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{i,j})_{i,j>0}
of weight
N
can be represented by a matrix of size at most
N
whose
(i,j)
-entry is
\lambda_{i,j}
. The pit condition
(i,j)\notin\lambda
is, by definition, the condition
a_{i,j}=0
, or, equivalently
\lambda_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}=0
for all
i^{\prime}\geqslant i
and
j^{\prime}\geqslant j
by (2).
Pictorially, the plane partitions can be realized as the 3d Young diagrams, i.e., the boxes of size one on the domain
\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid x,y,z\geqslant 0\}
by placing
a_{i,j}
boxes on
[i-1,i]\times[j-1,j]
on the
xy
-plane.
In the below, we present a plane partition
\lambda
and the corresponding 3d Young diagram. The plane partition
\lambda
satisfies the pit conditions
(1,4),(3,2),(4,1)\notin\lambda
, which are drawn as the black shadows on the
xy
-plane for the 3d Young diagram.

\lambda=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}2&2&1&0\\
1&1&1&0\\
1&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&0\end{array}\right]

The classical theorem by MacMahon (see e.g.
[
41
, Chapter 7]
) asserts that the generating function of the plane partition is given by the formula:

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{PP}}q^{|\lambda|}=\frac{1}{\displaystyle{\prod_{n>0}(1-q^{n})^{n}}}.

The generating functions of the plane partitions subject to the pit conditions were obtained by one of the authors
[
19
]
and are expressed by the following formulas.

Theorem 7.2

(
[
10
,
19
]
)
.

The generating function
F_{m,n}(q)
of the plane partitions with a pit condition
(m+1,n+1)
with
n\geqslant m
is given by

\displaystyle F_{m,n}(q)=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n+m}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant k_{m}\geqslant 0}\Big((-1)^{\sum k_{i}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\sum k_{i}(k_{i}+2i-1)}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(1-q^{k_{i}-k_{j}+j-i})\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant m}(1-q^{k_{i}-k_{j}+j-i})\Big),

where we set
k_{i}=0
for
i>m
.

Let us consider the pit condition
(2,n+1)
by setting
m=1
. Then, we have

\displaystyle F_{1,n}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n+1}}\sum_{k_{1}\geqslant 0}(-1)^{k_{1}}q^{\frac{1}{2}k_{1}(k_{1}+1)}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(1-q^{k_{i}-k_{j}+j-i}).

Since

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(1-q^{k_{i}-k_{j}+j-i})

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leqslant i<n}(1-q^{k_{1}+j-i})\times\prod_{2\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(1-q^{j-i})

\displaystyle=(q^{k_{1}+1};q)_{n-1}\times(q;q)_{n-2}(q^{2};q)_{n-3}\cdots(q^{n-2};q)_{1},

we have

\displaystyle F_{1,n}(q)\frac{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k_{1}}q^{\frac{1}{2}k_{1}(k_{1}+1)}(q^{k_{1}+1};q)_{n-1}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}(q,\cdots,q^{n-1};q)_{\infty}},

which agrees with the right-hand side of the equality in Theorem

.
Therefore, we obtain the following corollary, generalizing the case

proven by Molev and Mukhin in
[

]
.

Corollary 7.3
.

For all
n\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}[\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n|1}))](q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathrm{PP}\\
(2,n+1)\notin\lambda\end{subarray}}q^{|\lambda|}.

Perfectly generated t t -structures for algebraic stacks
Pat Lank

Abstract
. This work studies t t -structures for the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves on a quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack. Specifically, using Thomason filtrations, we classify those t t -structures which are generated by perfect complexes and satisfy a tensor compatibility. Interestingly, the only input required is the classification à la Hrbek for the affine scheme case.

14A30 (primary), 14F08, 18G80, 14D23

1.
Introduction

Algebraic stacks have repeatedly proven to be very useful for studying moduli problems. However, like many objects in algebraic geometry, their derived categories remain largely unexplored. As a result, much remains to be understood about the structure and information encoded in the derived category of an algebraic stack.

To set the stage, we focus on the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves on a quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
, denoted by
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
. See
Section

2.1
for background. Among the possible strategies to study such categories, we focus on
t
-structures and the triangulated subcategory
\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
of perfect complexes.

Over time, it has become clear that perfect complexes and
t
-structures play vital roles in the study of
D_{\operatorname{qc}}
in the setting of schemes. For example, the regularity of a Noetherian scheme is controlled by the
t
-structures on
\operatorname{Perf}

[
Nee24
,
Smi22
,
CLMP24
]
. Similar observations have recently been made for algebraic stacks as well
[
DLMRP25
]
.

The study of
t
-structures on schemes has been a very interesting topic, and we have learned several important results along the way. To begin with, a complete classification of
t
-structures on affine schemes is known to be impossible
[
Sta10
]
. This naturally leads to imposing additional conditions on
t
-structures in hopes of obtaining a classification.

One such approach is to focus on
t
-structures that are compactly generated. Initially, this restriction allowed for a classification in the affine Noetherian setting
[
ATJLS10
]
. These results have also been extended to Noetherian schemes under an additional ‘tensor compatibility’ condition
[
DS23
]
. Interestingly, the Noetherian assumption was dropped for the affine case
[
Hrb20
]
, which is a key ingredient to this work. In fact, a very nice novelty of our work is that using only the affine results of
[
Hrb20
]
, we can go directly to algebraic stacks without any need of classifications from
[
DS23
]
.

All these classifications involve what are called ‘Thomason filtrations’. These are certain decreasing functions from the integers to the collection of Thomason subsets of the underlying topological space (see
Section

2.3
for details). However, no analogous classification has yet been established for algebraic stacks.

This brings attention to the main result.

Theorem A

(see
Theorem

3.6
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a concentrated Thomason quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack. If
D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
is compactly generated, then there is a one-to-one correspondence:

\displaystyle\{\otimes\textrm{-aisle on }

\displaystyle D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})\textrm{ generated by }\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})\}

\displaystyle\iff\{\textrm{Thomason filtrations on }\mathcal{X}\textrm{ supported by perfects}\}.

In particular, for any
\otimes
-aisle on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
generated by
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
, we assign to it the Thomason filtration given by the rule (which is independent of the choice for
\mathcal{P}
)

n\mapsto\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
P\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P)).

The hypothesis of
A
with
D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
being compactly generated is relatively mild in nature. For instance, it is satisfied if
\mathcal{X}
also enjoys the resolution property (see
Corollary

3.7
). This includes tame Deligne–Mumford stacks with a finite diagonal
and which can be identified as the quotient stack
[U/\operatorname{GL}_{N,\mathbb{Z}}]
for some affine scheme
U
. See
Examples

2.1
,
2.2
,
2.3
and
2.4
for details.

A novel and significant aspect of this work is that our proof of
Theorem

3.6
relies solely on Hrbek’s classification for affine schemes
[
Hrb20
]
. This approach entirely bypasses any dependence on the scheme-theoretic results of
[
DS23
]
. In fact, as a consequence of
A
, we obtain an independent proof of
[
DS23
, Theorem 5.11]
when a Noetherian scheme has the resolution property, which will be discussed shortly.

The condition for a Thomason filtration to be ‘supported by perfects’ is defined in
Definition

3.1
. As a first step towards
A
, we show for an affine scheme that every Thomason filtration is supported by perfects. Then we establish auxiliary result (see
Proposition

3.5
). It allows us to better understand such
t
-structures by passing to an affine scheme that is a smooth cover of the algebraic stack. This immediately enables us to bootstrap the classification known in the affine setting thanks to Hrbek
[
Hrb20
]
.

Next a particularly pleasant consequence is a sharper classification.

Corollary B

(see
Corollary

3.8
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a concentrated Noetherian algebraic stack satisfying the resolution property. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence:

\displaystyle\{\textrm{compactly generated }\otimes\textrm{-aisle on }

\displaystyle D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})\}

\displaystyle\iff\{\textrm{Thomason filtrations on }\mathcal{X}\}.

There are concrete settings for which
Corollary

3.8
is applicable. It includes when
X
also has quasi-finite separated diagonal or is a Deligne–Mumford stack over
\mathbb{Q}
(see
[
HLLP25
, Theorem A]
). The key ingredient, introduced in
[
HLLP25
]
, is a notion called ‘approximation by compacts’. This condition allows us to leverage
Theorem

3.6
to show every Thomason filtration is in fact supported by perfects for such algebraic stack. As stated earlier, this gives an independent proof of
[
DS23
, Theorem 5.11]
when
X
is a Noetherian scheme with the resolution property. However, it does so more generally for any Noetherian scheme
X
such that
D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
is compactly generated.

2.2.
t
-structures

We briefly recall
t
-structures on triangulated categories and their tensor variants. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category admitting small coproducts. Its subcategory of compact objects is denoted by
\mathcal{T}^{c}
, consisting of those
c\in\mathcal{T}
for which
\operatorname{Hom}(c,-)
preserves coproducts.

t
-structures

A pair of strictly full subcategories
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0})
of
\mathcal{T}
is a
t
-structure
if:

•

\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)=0
for all
A\in\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
B\in\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}[-1]
,

•

\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}[-1]\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
,

•

for every
E\in\mathcal{T}
, there is a distinguished triangle

\tau^{\leq 0}E\to E\to\tau^{\geq 1}E\to(\tau^{\leq 0}E)[1]

with
\tau^{\leq 0}E\in\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
\tau^{\geq 1}E\in\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}[-1]
.

This notion was introduced in
[
BBDG18
]
. The above triangle is called the
truncation triangle
of
E
with respect to the
t
-structure. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
\mathcal{T}^{\leq n}:=\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}[-n]
and
\mathcal{T}^{\geq n}:=\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}[-n]
. Then
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq n},\mathcal{T}^{\geq n})
is also a
t
-structure on
\mathcal{T}
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack. Consider the following categories

•

D^{\leq 0}(\mathcal{X})
consists of objects
E\in D(\mathcal{X})
such that
\mathcal{H}^{j}(E)=0
for
j>0

•

D^{\geq 0}(\mathcal{X})
consists of objects
E\in D(\mathcal{X})
such that
\mathcal{H}^{j}(E)=0
for
j<0
.

The pair
(D^{\leq 0}(\mathcal{X}),D^{\geq 0}(\mathcal{X}))
is called the
standard
t
-structure
on
D(\mathcal{X})
. From
[
Sta25
,
Tag 06WU
]
, the natural functor
\operatorname{Qcoh}(\mathcal{X})\to\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{X})
is fully faithful (but not exact generally). It follows that the standard
t
-structure induces a
t
-structure on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
. Specifically, the pair

(D^{\leq 0}(\mathcal{X})\cap D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X}),D^{\geq 0}(\mathcal{X})\cap D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X}))
is called the
standard
t
-structure
on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
. To simplify, the induced
t
-structure is denoted
(D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X}),D^{\geq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X}))
.

Another description of a
t
-structure is via
aisles
. A strictly full subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
is an

if it is closed under positive shifts and extensions, and the inclusion
\mathcal{A}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{T}
admits a right adjoint. Given a
t
-structure
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0})
, the subcategory
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
is an aisle. Moreover, by
[

]

determines a

-structure
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\perp}[1])

\mathcal{A}^{\perp}:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}\mid\operatorname{Hom}(A,T)=0\text{ for all }A\in\mathcal{A}\}.

The subcategories
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
are called the
aisle
and

of the

-structure, respectively.

More generally, a

is a strictly full subcategory closed under positive shifts and extensions. For a subcategory
\mathcal{S}
, denote by
\operatorname{preaisle}(\mathcal{S})
the smallest cocomplete preaisle containing
\mathcal{S}

\operatorname{preaisle}(\mathcal{S})

\operatorname{aisle}(\mathcal{S})
. We impose similar notation for the coaisle as well.
An aisle

is

if there exists a collection of compact objects
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{c}
with

. A
t
-structure is
compactly generated
if its aisle is.

t
-exactness

We consider ‘morphisms’ of
t
-structures. Let
F\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be an exact functor between triangulated categories equipped with

-structures

-exact

For example, if

2.3.
Thomason filtrations

We recall Thomason filtrations on a topological space
X
. To start, let
\operatorname{Spcl}(X)
be the collection of specialization closed subsets of
X
. Recall that
Z\subseteq X
is called a
Thomason subset
of
X
if
Z=\cup_{\alpha\in\Lambda}Z_{\alpha}
where each
Z_{\alpha}
is closed in
X
and
X\setminus Z_{\alpha}
is quasi-compact. Denote by
\operatorname{Thom}(X)
the collection of Thomason subsets of
X
. If
X
is a Noetherian topological space, then specialization closed subsets coincide with Thomason subsets.

A function
\phi\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{Thom}(X)
is called a
Thomason filtration
on
X
if
\phi(n+1)\subseteq\phi(n)
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
X=|\mathcal{X}|
where
\mathcal{X}
is an algebraic stack, then we abuse language and call these ‘Thomason filtrations on
\mathcal{X}
’. Important cases of Thomason subsets on a quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
arise from perfect complexes. Indeed, for any
P\in\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
, we know that
\operatorname{supp}(P)
is closed in
\mathcal{X}
and has quasi-compact support (see
[
HR17
, Lemma 4.8]
).

3.
Results

This section proves the main results. To start, we need the following notion.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack. We say a Thomason filtration
\phi
on
\mathcal{X}
is
supported by perfects
if there is a
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\phi(n)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
S\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(S)).

It is not obvious (at least to the author) as to whether every Thomason filtration on a quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack is supported by perfects. Fortunately, there is a setting where it does occur, and is a key step towards our classification.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
for a commutative ring (which could be not Noetherian). Then every Thomason filtration on
X
is supported by perfects.

Proof.

This follows essentially by bookkeeping the proof of
[
Hrb20
, Theorem 5.1]
. We spell out the details. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a compactly generated aisle on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
. From loc. cit.,
\mathcal{U}
is compactly generated by the collection
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{U}
consisting of
K(\mathcal{I})[s]\in\mathcal{U}
where
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}
is an ideal sheaf which is coherent and
s
is some integer. Here
K(\mathcal{I})
is the (sheafification) of the Koszul complex on a finitely generated ideal.
Denote by
i_{\mathcal{I}}\colon Z_{\mathcal{I}}\to X
for their associated closed immersions. By
[
Hrb20
, Lemma 5.3(iii)]
, for any
K(\mathcal{I})[s]\in\mathcal{S}
, we know that

(3.1)

\operatorname{aisle}((i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}[s])=\operatorname{aisle}(K(\mathcal{I})[s])\subseteq\mathcal{U}.

From
[
Hrb20
, Theorem 5.1]
, the Thomason filtration on
X
corresponding to
\mathcal{U}
is given by

\phi_{\mathcal{U}}(n):=\bigcup_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}\textrm{ s.t. }(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}[-n]\in\mathcal{U}}Z_{\mathcal{I}}.

However, the Koszul complexes
K(\mathcal{I})
each admit a morphism
K(\mathcal{I})\to(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}
such that the induced morphism
\mathcal{H}^{p}(K(\mathcal{I}))\to\mathcal{H}^{p}((i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}})
is an isomorphism for
p\geq 0
. This tells us
\mathcal{H}^{0}(K(\mathcal{I}))=Z_{\mathcal{I}}
. Then we see that

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathcal{U}}(n)

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}\textrm{ s.t. }(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}[-n]\in\mathcal{U}}Z_{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}\textrm{ s.t. }K(\mathcal{I})[-n]\in\mathcal{U}}Z_{t}

\displaystyle(\textrm{\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:koszul_aisle}})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}\textrm{ s.t. }K(\mathcal{I})[-n]\in\mathcal{U}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{n}(K(\mathcal{I})[-n]))

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}^{0}(K(\mathcal{I}))=\mathcal{H}^{n}(K(\mathcal{I})[-n]))

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
K(\mathcal{J})[s]\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(K(\mathcal{J})[s]))

\displaystyle(K(\mathcal{I})[-n]\in\mathcal{S}\textrm{ \& }j=n).

So, we only need to check the reverse inclusion above. Suppose there is
j\geq n
and
K(\mathcal{J})[s]\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(K(\mathcal{J})[s]))\not=\emptyset
. There is an isomorphism

\mathcal{H}^{j}(K(\mathcal{J})[s])=\mathcal{H}^{j+s}(K(\mathcal{J})).

This forces
j+s\leq 0
, and so,
n+s\leq 0
. Hence, we have that

K(\mathcal{J})[-n]\cong K(\mathcal{J})[s-n-s]\in\mathcal{U}

because
K(\mathcal{J})[s]\in\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{U}
is closed under nonnegative shifts. Therefore, we see that
K(\mathcal{J})[-n]\in\mathcal{U}
, which gives us the desired inclusion. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack. Suppose
E\in D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
. If
E
is an
n
-fold extension of objects from
\mathcal{S}
, then for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
one has

\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(E))\subseteq\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(S)).

Proof.

To start, we make a reduction. Let
U\to\mathcal{X}
be a smooth surjection from a scheme. Recall that the derived pullback is
t
-exact with respect to the standard
t
-structure and takes distinguished triangles to distinguished triangles. So, we can replace
\mathcal{X}
by a scheme; that is, reducing to the case of a scheme
X
.

We prove the desired claim by induction on
n
. The base case with
n=1
is straightforward. Indeed,
E
would be isomorphic to an object in
\mathcal{S}
. Now suppose we have proven the claim for any object that is a
k
-fold extension of objects from
\mathcal{S}
where
1\leq k\leq n-1
.

Let
E
be an
n
-fold extension of objects from
\mathcal{S}
. This means there is a distinguished triangle

E^{\prime}\to E\to E^{\prime\prime}\to E^{\prime}[1]

where
E^{\prime}
is an
(n-1)
-fold extension of objects from
\mathcal{S}
and
E^{\prime\prime}
is isomorphic to an object from
\mathcal{S}
.

Consider the long exact sequence in cohomology

\cdots\to\mathcal{H}^{j}(E^{\prime})\to\mathcal{H}^{j}(E)\to\mathcal{H}^{j}(E^{\prime\prime})\to\cdots.

Let
p\in X\setminus(\cup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(S)))
. Passing to the stalk of
X
at
p
, we see from the long exact sequence above that
(\mathcal{H}^{j}(E))_{p}=0
. Hence,
p\not\in\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(E))
as desired. To see, use the inductive hypothesis that
(\mathcal{H}^{j}(E^{\prime}))_{p}=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
for a commutative ring (which could be not Noetherian). Suppose
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(X)
. Under the bijection of
[
Hrb20
, Theorem 5.1]
, the corresponding Thomason filtration of
\operatorname{aisle}(\mathcal{P})
can be also identified as Thomason filtration on
X
given by the rule

n\mapsto\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
P\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P)).

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{U}:=\operatorname{aisle}(\mathcal{P})
. By
[
Hrb20
, Theorem 5.1]
, the corresponding Thomason filtration of
\mathcal{U}
is given by the rule

k\mapsto\bigcup_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}\textrm{ s.t. }(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}[-k]\in\mathcal{U}}Z_{\mathcal{I}}

where
i_{\mathcal{I}}\colon Z_{\mathcal{I}}\to X
is a closed subscheme associated to a coherent ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Denote by
\mathcal{D}
for the collection of pairs
(\mathcal{I},-k)
with
\mathcal{I}
an ideal sheaf and
k
any integer appearing above. Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. It suffices to check that

\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
P\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P))=\bigcup_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}\textrm{ s.t. }(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}[-n]\in\mathcal{U}}Z_{\mathcal{I}}.

First, let
Z_{\mathcal{I}}
be a closed subset such that
(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}[-n]\in\mathcal{U}
for some
(\mathcal{I},-n)\in\mathcal{D}
. Now
[
Hrb20
, Lemma 5.1]
tells us
\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}[-n]
is a direct summand of an object
A_{\mathcal{I},n}
which is an
r
-fold extension of objects from the collection
\{P[t]:t\geq 0,P\in\mathcal{P}\}
. Note that
\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}[-n]
is concentrated in degree
n
. Then
Lemma

3.3
ensures that

\displaystyle Z_{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}[-n]))

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{n}(A_{\mathcal{I},n}))

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\geq 0\\
P\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{n}(P[t]))

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
P\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P)).

This shows that

\bigcup_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}\textrm{ s.t. }(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}[-n]\in\mathcal{U}}Z_{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
P\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P)).

Lastly we check the reverse inclusion. Choose some
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
j\geq n
such that
\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P))
is nonempty. By
[
Hrb20
, Theorem 5.1]
, we know that

\operatorname{aisle}(\{(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{I}}}[-k]:(\mathcal{I},k)\in\mathcal{D}\})=\mathcal{U}.

Again, from
[
Hrb20
, Lemma 5.1]
,
P
is a direct summand of an object
B
which is an
r
-fold extension of objects from the collection

\{(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{I}}}[-k+s]:(\mathcal{I},-k)\in\mathcal{D},s\geq 0\}.

Applying
Lemma

3.3
, we see that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\emptyset\not=\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P))

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(B))

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\mathcal{I},-k)\in\mathcal{D}\\
s\geq 0\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}((i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{I}}}[-k+s])).

Choose any
(\mathcal{I},-k)\in\mathcal{D}
and
s\geq 0
above such that

\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P))\cap\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}((i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{I}}}[-k+s]))\not=\emptyset.

Note there is at least one such pair from the hypothesis. This means

\mathcal{H}^{j}((i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{I}}}[-k+s])\not=0,

which can only be the case when
j=k-s
. However,
j\geq n
, and so,
-k+s\leq-n
. From aisles being closed under positive shifts and
(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{I}}}[-k]\in\mathcal{U}
, we have that
(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{I}}}[-n]\in\mathcal{U}
. It is clear that the support of
\mathcal{H}^{j}((i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{I}}}[-k+s])
is
Z_{\mathcal{I}}
. So,
\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P))
is contained in the union of all such

above in light of

3.2
. In particular, we see that

\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P))\subseteq\bigcup_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}\textrm{ s.t. }(i_{\mathcal{I}})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathcal{I}}}[-n]\in\mathcal{U}}Z_{\mathcal{I}},

which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack. Suppose
f\colon U\to\mathcal{X}
is a flat morphism from an affine scheme. For any
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(X)
such that
\operatorname{preaisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})
is an aisle on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
, one has that
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}
induces a
t
-exact functor

(D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X}),\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P}))\to(D_{\operatorname{qc}}(U),\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P})).

Proof.

Recall our convention that when the
\otimes
-preaisle is an aisle means we write
\operatorname{preaisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})
as
\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})
. We prove the desired claim by contradiction. To start, it is straightforward to see that
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})\subseteq\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P})
, which tells us
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}
is right
t
-exact. Hence, the assumption ensures
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}
must fail to be left
t
-exact. So, we can find an
E\in\operatorname{coaisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})
such that
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}E\not\in\operatorname{coaisle}_{\otimes}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P})
. This tells us
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}E[-1]\not\in(\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P}))^{\perp}
. By
[
DLMRP25
, Lemma 2.2]
, we know that
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(U)
. Moreover, using
[
ATLS03
, Lemma 3.1]
, we can find an
A\in\mathcal{P}
and
j\geq 0
such that
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}A[j],\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}E[-1])\not=0
. Now we use adjunction to see that,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}

\displaystyle(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}A[j],\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}E[-1])

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{U},\operatorname{\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}A[j],\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}E[-1]))

\displaystyle\textrm{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{StacksProject}{}{}, \hrefhttps://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/08DH]}}

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{U},\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\operatorname{\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}}(A[j],E[-1]))

\displaystyle\textrm{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{DeDeyn/Lank/ManaliRahul/Peng:2025}{}{}, Lemma 2.3]}}.

This implies that

\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\operatorname{\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}}(A[j],E[-1])\not\in D^{\geq 1}_{\operatorname{qc}}(U),

and because
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}
is
t
-exact with respect to the standard
t
-structures,

\operatorname{\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}}(A[j],E[-1])\not\in D^{\geq 1}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X}).

Then we can find a
B\in D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
such that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\not=\operatorname{Hom}(B,\operatorname{\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}}(A[j],E[-1]))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}(B\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}A[j],E[-1])

\displaystyle(\textrm{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{StacksProject}{}{}, \hrefhttps://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/08DH]}}).

However,
E[-1]
is in
(\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P}))^{\perp}
and
B\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}A[j]\in\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a concentrated Thomason quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack. If
D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
is compactly generated, then there is a one-to-one correspondence:

\displaystyle\{\otimes\textrm{-aisle on }

\displaystyle D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})\textrm{ generated by }\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})\}

\displaystyle\iff\{\textrm{Thomason filtrations on }\mathcal{X}\textrm{ supported by perfects}\}.

In particular, for any
\otimes
-aisle on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
generated by
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
, we assign to it the Thomason filtration given by the rule (which is independent of the choice for
\mathcal{P}
)

n\mapsto\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
P\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P)).

Proof.

Before getting to work, we make a few observations that will be used freely in the proof. First, our constraints on
\mathcal{X}
ensures that
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)^{c}
(i.e. the compacts) coincide with
\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
. Secondly, as
\mathcal{X}
is concentrated and Thomason, we have that
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
is a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category. Thirdly, from
Lemma

2.6
, it follows that
\operatorname{preaisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})
is an
\otimes
-aisle on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
for all
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
. That is,
\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})
exists for all
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
.

Now we move onwards to our proof. This will be done by constructing a series of maps between the desired collections. Let
f\colon U\to\mathcal{X}
be a smooth surjective morphism from an affine scheme (which exists by
[
Sta25
,
Tag 04YC
]
). To start, we show there is an injective mapping

(3.3)

\displaystyle\{\otimes\textrm{-aisle on }

\displaystyle D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})\textrm{ generated by }\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})\}

\displaystyle\to\{\otimes\textrm{-aisle on }D_{\operatorname{qc}}(U)\textrm{ generated by }\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(U)\}.

This part of the proof is done by contradiction. Suppose
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
satisfy
\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})\not=\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P})=\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
. Then we can find an
E\in\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})
such that
E\not\in\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
. Consider the truncation triangle with respect to the aisle
\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
,

\tau^{\leq 0}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}E\to E\to\tau^{\geq 1}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\to(\tau^{\leq 0}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}E).

By
Proposition

3.5
, the following

\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\tau^{\leq 0}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}E\to\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}E\to\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\tau^{\geq 1}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\to\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}(\tau^{\leq 0}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}E)

is the truncation triangle for
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}E
with respect to
\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
. However, the assumption tells us that
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}E\in\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
, and so,
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\tau^{\geq 1}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}E=0
. From flatness of
f
, it follows that
\tau^{\geq 1}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}E=0
. This tells us that
E\in\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
, which is absurd given the assumption. Hence, we have the desired injection for
Equation

3.3
.

Next we show there is an injective mapping

(3.4)

\displaystyle\{\otimes\textrm{-aisle on }

\displaystyle D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})\textrm{ generated by }\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})\}

\displaystyle\to\{\textrm{Thomason filtrations on }U\}

which factors through
Equation

3.3
. From
[
Hrb20
, Theorem 5.1]
, we have an injective mapping the image of
Equation

3.3
into collection of Thomason filtrations on
U
. Moreover,
Lemma

3.2
tells us each Thomason filtration on
U
is supported by perfects. Specifically, for each
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
, we assign
\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P})
to the Thomason filtration on
U
given by

\phi_{\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P}}\colon n\mapsto\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
P\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}P)).

The presentation above follows from
Lemma

3.4
. So, tying things to together, we have the desired mapping in
Equation

3.4
.

Now we claim there is a bijective mapping (i.e. the desired one-to-one correspondence)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\{\otimes\textrm{-aisle on }

\displaystyle D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})\textrm{ generated by }\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})\}

\displaystyle\to\{\textrm{Thomason filtrations on }\mathcal{X}\textrm{ supported by perfects}\}

which factors through
Equation

3.4
. It suffices to show there is a bijective mapping

(3.6)

\displaystyle\{\textrm{Thomason filtrations on }\mathcal{X}\textrm{ supported }

\displaystyle\textrm{by perfects}\}

\displaystyle\to\{\phi_{\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P}}\colon\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})\}.

Indeed, the image of the injective mapping in
Equation

3.4
is precisely the target in
Equation

3.6
. To see, by construction, the image of
Equation

3.4
lands into
\{\phi_{\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P}}\colon\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})\}
, so we have an injective map

\displaystyle\{\otimes\textrm{-aisle on }D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})\textrm{ generated by }

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})\}

\displaystyle\to\{\phi_{\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P}}\colon\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})\}.

On the other hand, for each
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
, we know
\operatorname{aisle}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})
exists on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
, so its image under
Equation

3.4
is exactly
\phi_{\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{P}}
, showing the mapping is also surjective.

Let
\psi
be a Thomason filtration on
\mathcal{X}
that is supported by perfects. We can find
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
such that

\psi(n)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
Q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(Q)).

Using
[
HR17
, Lemma 4.8]
, we can see that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
Q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}Q))

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
Q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}f^{-1}(\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(Q)))

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
Q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(Q)))

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(\phi(n)).

Using
[
HR17
, Lemma 4.8(3)]
, it is straightforward to check that this defines a Thomason filtration on
U
. This Thomason filtration on
U
is denoted by
f^{-1}(\phi)
. It is given by the rule
n\mapsto f^{-1}(\phi(n))
. In fact, the very construction shows
f^{-1}(\phi)=\phi_{\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{Q}}
. This gives us a well-defined mapping in
Equation

3.6
. We need to check it is bijective. Fortunately, the construction of the mapping shows that it must be surjective, so we only need to check it is injective. However, this follows from the fact that
f\colon U\to\mathcal{X}
is surjective, which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a concentrated quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic stack which satisfies the resolution property. Then the classification of
Theorem

3.6
holds for
\mathcal{X}
; specifically,
D_{\operatorname{qc}}^{\leq 0}(\mathcal{X})
is compactly generated by the collection of locally free
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
-modules.

Proof.

By
Theorem

3.6
, it suffices to show
D_{\operatorname{qc}}^{\leq 0}(\mathcal{X})
is compactly generated. Denote by
\mathcal{S}
for the collection of locally free sheaves on
\mathcal{X}
. In this setting,
[
HR17
, Proposition 8.4]
ensures that
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
is Thomason, and hence, compactly generated. Then
[
HNR19
, Theorem 1.2]
implies the natural functor
D(\operatorname{Qcoh}(\mathcal{X}))\to D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
is a triangulated equivalence. Using
[
Sta25
,
Tag 05T7
]
, we see that any object in
E\in D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
can be represented by a complex
P
such that
P^{n}\in\mathcal{S}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
P^{t}=0
for
t>0
.

It is straightforward forward to see that
D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
contains

\mathcal{U}:=\operatorname{coprod}(\{S[n]:S\in\mathcal{S}\textrm{ and }n\geq 0\}).

So, we show the reverse inclusion. Let
E\in D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
. If needed, we replace
E
by a complex
P
such that
P^{n}\in\mathcal{S}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
P^{t}=0
for
t>0
. Taking stupid truncations, there is a distinguished triangle (see e.g. proof of
[
KN13
, Theorem 10.2(3)]
):

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\sigma^{\leq-n}P\to\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\sigma^{\leq-n}P\to P\to(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\sigma^{\leq-n}P)[1].

Hence, we have a distinguished triangle

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\sigma^{\leq-n}P\to P\to(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\sigma^{\leq-n}P)[1]\to(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\sigma^{\leq-n}P)[1].

Each object
\sigma^{\leq-n}P
is in
\mathcal{U}
for
n\geq 0
, which ensures that
(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\sigma^{\leq-n}P)[t]\in\mathcal{U}
for all
t\geq 0
. Then
P\in\mathcal{U}
because aisles are closed under extensions. This gives us the reverse inclusion as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a concentrated Noetherian algebraic stack satisfying the resolution property. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence:

\displaystyle\{\textrm{compactly generated }\otimes\textrm{-aisle on }

\displaystyle D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})\}

\displaystyle\iff\{\textrm{Thomason filtrations on }\mathcal{X}\}.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{X}
is concentrated, then we know that
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})^{c}=\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
(see e.g.
[
HR17
, Remark 4.6]
). Hence, any
\otimes
-aisle on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(\mathcal{X})
generated by perfect complexes is compactly generated. So, by
Theorem

3.6
and
Corollary

3.7
, it suffices to show every Thomason filtration on
\mathcal{X}
is supported by perfects.

If
\mathcal{X}
satisfies the compact resolution property (as it is concentrated), then
[
HLLP25
, Proposition 4.7]
tells us it must enjoy approximation by compacts. Let
\phi
be a Thomason filtration on
\mathcal{X}
. Choose
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\mathcal{X}
is Noetherian, we know
\phi(n)
is a union of closed subsets
Z_{c,n}
with
c\in C
. For each
Z_{c,n}
, let
i_{c,n}\colon\mathcal{Z}_{c,n}\to\mathcal{X}
be the closed immersion of
Z_{c,n}
with its reduced induced closed subscheme structure. From
\mathcal{X}
satisfying approximation by compacts, there is a
P_{c,n}\in\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
and a morphism
P_{c,n}\to(i_{c,n})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}_{c,n}}
which induces an isomorphism in cohomology for degrees
\geq 0
. Then

\phi(n)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq n\\
c\in C\end{subarray}}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P_{c,n}[-n])),

and hence, the desired claim follows.
∎

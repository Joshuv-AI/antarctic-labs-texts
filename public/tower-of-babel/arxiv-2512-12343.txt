Theory of local orbital magnetization: local Berry curvature
Sariah Al Saati, Karyn Le Hur, Frédéric Piéchon

Abstract
We present a microscopic theory for the local (single site) orbital magnetization in tight-binding systems. Each occupied state of energy ε n \varepsilon_{n} contributes with a local orbital magnetic moment term 𝒎 n ​ ( 𝒓 ) {\bm{m}}_{n}({\bm{r}}) and a local Berry-curvature term 𝛀 n ​ ( 𝒓 ) {\bm{\Omega}}_{n}({\bm{r}}) . For Bloch electrons ( 𝒌 {\bm{k}} -space), we go beyond the modern theory by revealing the sublattice texture. We identify a topological contribution 𝛀 n ​ 𝒌 topo ​ ( 𝒓 ) {\bm{\Omega}}^{\text{topo}}_{n\bm{k}}({\bm{r}}) and a geometric contribution 𝛀 n ​ 𝒌 geom ​ ( 𝒓 ) {\bm{\Omega}}^{\text{geom}}_{n\bm{k}}({\bm{r}}) to the sublattice Berry curvature. For systems with open boundaries ( 𝒓 {\bm{r}} -space), we derive an explicit expression of an effective onsite Berry curvature 𝛀 n ​ ( 𝒓 ) {\bm{\Omega}}_{n}({\bm{r}}) . Considering two band models, the 𝒌 \bm{k} -space and 𝒓 \bm{r} -space onsite magnetizations coincide numerically but differ from the Bianco-Resta approach. They reveal orbital ferromagnetism in topological insulators, and orbital antiferro- and ferrimagnetism in trivial insulators. This theory can be used to investigate orbital magnetic textures and their topological properties in many systems of current interest (Moiré, amorphous, quasicrystals, defects, molecules).



Magnetism is one of the most familiar
properties of materials: easily observed, known since antiquity, and central to modern technologies.
Magnetization in a material arises from two sources: the spin of the electron and its orbital motion.
Spin magnetization is tied to collective behavior, giving rise to various kinds of magnetic order (ferromagnetic, antiferromagnetic, etc.).
By contrast, the orbital magnetization is usually understood at the single-particle level,
as the average orbital magnetic moment of electrons at thermodynamic equilibrium. Despite this apparent simplicity,
only since 2005, has a consistent quantum theory of orbital magnetization for crystalline solids been established
14
;
17
;
6
;
16
;
15
.

In this modern theory, the orbital magnetization of Bloch electrons in crystalline materials is obtained as:

\displaystyle{\bm{M}}_{\textrm{orb}}(\mu,T)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{e}{\hbar}\sum_{n}\left\langle f(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}){\bm{m}}_{n\bm{k}}+F(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}){\bm{\Omega}}_{n\bm{k}}\right\rangle_{\bm{k}}

(1)

where
{\bm{m}}_{n\bm{k}}
and
{\bm{\Omega}}_{n\bm{k}}
are, respectively, the orbital magnetic moment and the Berry curvature of Bloch state
|n\bm{k}\rangle
in the energy band
\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}
;

f(\varepsilon)=1/(1+e^{\beta(\varepsilon-\mu)})
and
F(\varepsilon)=k_{B}T\ln\left(1+e^{-\beta(\varepsilon-\mu)}\right)
reflect Fermi statistics,

\mu
is the chemical potential, and
\beta=1/(k_{B}T)
is the inverse temperature.
We use the notation
\langle\bullet\rangle_{\bm{k}}=\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\bullet
.
A major shift brought about by this expression was the realization that Berry curvature constitutes a significant source of orbital magnetization, apart from the conventional orbital magnetic moment. Qualitatively, while both
{\bm{m}}_{n\bm{k}}
and
{\bm{\Omega}}_{n\bm{k}}
reflect virtual interband effects, in a semiclassical picture,
{\bm{m}}_{n\bm{k}}
is interpreted as the self rotation of the electron wave-packet in energy band
\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}
, while
{\bm{\Omega}}_{n\bm{k}}
plays the role of a pseudo magnetic field in
\bm{k}
-space at the origin of the anomalous Hall effect
17
;
16
. In that perspective, in the gap of a topological band insulator, the Středa formula
13
,
\sigma_{xy}=\frac{e^{2}}{h}{\cal C}=e\partial_{\mu}M_{\textrm{orb}}
, provides a direct relation between the quantized anomalous Hall conductivity and the slope of the orbital magnetization as a function of the chemical potential (the Chern number
{\cal C}
is a topological invariant defined as the integral of the Berry curvature throughout the Brillouin zone).

Although the modern theory of orbital magnetization has revealed the role of band topology
in one of the most basic thermodynamic quantities, it was argued that orbital magnetization being a bulk property, there must be a local description of orbital magnetization in a finite-size setting with open boundaries. This description must therefore encode the topological features locally in real space. Bianco and Resta were the first to propose such a local picture in which topology is encoded by a local Chern marker
3
;
4
.

In this Letter, we present an alternative and more microscopic approach that provides the orbital magnetization at the scale of a single site, both for Bloch electrons (
\bm{k}
-space representation) and open-boundary systems in real space (
\bm{r}
-space representation). Beyond modern theory and Bianco-Resta approach, we reveal the sublattice orbital magnetic texture, uncover unexpected geometric contributions to the usual
\bm{k}
-space Berry curvature and identify an effective local Berry curvature in open-boundary systems.

Thermodynamic theory of local orbital magnetization-

In a thermodynamic picture, the local spontaneous magnetization is obtained as the zero field limit of the first derivative
{\bf M}({\bf r},\mu,{\bf B})=-\partial_{\bf B}\Xi|_{\bm{B}=0}
of the local grand canonical potential
\Xi(\bm{r},\mu,{\bf B})=-\int\mathrm{d}\varepsilon F(\varepsilon)\nu({\bf r},\varepsilon,{\bf B})
. In this picture, the key quantity is the magnetic field dependent local density of states
\nu(\bm{r},\varepsilon,{\bf B})
, and more specifically its first-order field-induced correction.
Concretely, considering non-interacting (spinless) electrons in a tight-binding picture, in which the effect of the constant magnetic field is taken care of by a gauge-dependent Peierls phase substitution in real space, and by using a gauge-invariant perturbation theory
12
,
the local
onsite
orbital magnetization
is obtained as:

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt{\bm{M}}_{\textrm{orb}}({\bf r},\mu)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{e}{2\pi\hbar}\int\mathrm{d}\varepsilon F(\varepsilon)\mathrm{Re}\langle\bm{r}|{\cal G}[(\hat{\bm{v}}{\cal G})\times(\hat{\bm{v}}{\cal G})]|\bm{r}\rangle

(2)

where
{\cal G}(\varepsilon)=(\varepsilon-{\cal H})^{-1}
is the retarded single particle Green’s function associated to the zero (magnetic) field Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
, and
\hat{\bm{v}}=-i[\hat{\bm{r}},{\cal H}]
is the zero field velocity operator (in units of
1/\hbar
).
Equation (
2
) is the starting point of our study.

positions

{\bm{r}_{\alpha}}

\alpha

\displaystyle=\dfrac{e}{\hbar}\sum_{n}\left\langle f(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}){\bm{m}}_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r}_{\alpha})+F(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}){\bm{\Omega}}_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r}_{\alpha})\right\rangle_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle{\bm{\Omega}}^{\text{topo}}_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r}_{\alpha})=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}|\langle\bm{r}_{\alpha}|n\bm{k}\rangle|^{2}{\bm{\Omega}}_{n\bm{k}}+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{m\neq n}|\langle\bm{r}_{\alpha}|m\bm{k}\rangle|^{2}{\bm{\Omega}}_{nm\bm{k}}\quad{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\underset{\sum_{\bm{r}_{\alpha}}}{\longrightarrow}\dfrac{1}{2}{\bm{\Omega}}_{n\bm{k}}+\dfrac{1}{2}{\bm{\Omega}}_{n\bm{k}}={\bm{\Omega}}_{n\bm{k}}}

where

is the interband velocity,
{\cal H}_{\bm{k}}
the Bloch Hamiltonian,

They extend the modern theory by revealing

More concretely, the expression of the sublattice orbital magnetic moment

While the first term is intuitive, the second

The sublattice Berry curvature

{\bm{\Omega}}^{\text{topo}}_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r}_{\alpha})

Comparison with Bianco-Resta approach
-

Few years after the introduction of the
\bm{k}
-space modern theory of orbital magnetization, Bianco and Resta
derived an expression for the zero temperature local orbital magnetization in finite size systems with open boundaries

\displaystyle{M}(\bm{r})=\dfrac{e}{\hbar}(\mathfrak{M}(\bm{r})+\dfrac{\mu}{2\pi}\mathcal{C}(\bm{r}))

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\text{BR}}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Im}\langle\bm{r}|\mathcal{P}x\mathcal{Q}h\mathcal{Q}y\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{Q}x\mathcal{P}h\mathcal{P}y\mathcal{Q}|\bm{r}\rangle

(12)

\displaystyle=4\pi\mathrm{Im}\langle\bm{r}|\mathcal{Q}x\mathcal{P}y\mathcal{Q}|\bm{r}\rangle

Topology and bulk-edge duality in open-boundary systems
-

The common picture of bulk-edge correspondence consists in relating the existence of a finite Chern number calculated via Berry curvature of Bloch band eigenstates (periodic systems) to the presence of chiral edge states that appear in the topological gaps of a finite size system
10
;
9
.
As we now describe, the comparison between the local and the total orbital magnetization in finite size topological systems offers an alternative perspective on the bulk-edge correspondence.

Consider a finite-size sample of the Haldane model in the topological phase HM
{}_{\text{topo}}
. We separate its spectrum into two subsets: the band states and the gap states. Starting from equations (
9
,
10
) we now define
m^{\text{band}}(\bm{r})
(resp.
m^{\text{gap}}(\bm{r})
) the sum over all occupied band states (resp. gap states) of the local orbital magnetic moment
m_{n}(\bm{r})
. We also define
\Omega^{\text{band}}(\bm{r})
the local Berry curvature contribution computed using only band states, and
\Omega^{\text{gap}}(\bm{r})
the local Berry curvature contribution in which at least one gap state is used in the computation.

Figure
3
shows three distinct calculations of these four separate contributions to orbital magnetization. The left picture shows the case of an arbitrary single unit cell in the bulk of the sample, the central picture represents an average over roughly 80% of the unit cells (excluding the contribution of sites near the edges), and right picture is obtained by averaging over the whole finite sample (including the site at the edges).

The middle and left pictures show that the gap states contribution is negligible in the bulk of a finite-size system, and that in the bulk,
\Omega^{\text{band}}(\bm{r})
solely accounts for the topological contribution. In strong contrast, the third picture shows that when considering the whole sample including the sites at the edges, the gap states contribution (orange curve) is essential and contains all the topological information, which is now encoded in the orbital magnetic moment.
This analysis complements the usual picture of the bulk–edge correspondence by demonstrating that topological properties can be observed in open-boundary finite-size systems without being attributed to edge modes. Instead, we find that these bulk topological properties arise purely from the effective local Berry curvature of the band states, which is the relevant observable to consider for investigating topology in the bulk of open-boundary systems.
At the level of the entire sample, we retrieve the usual picture in which the topological property is encoded in the orbital magnetic moment contribution of (in-gap) edge states. We note that somewhat similar considerations appeared in

Conclusion-

The modern theory of orbital magnetization has revealed the importance of the Berry curvature and the role of topological properties of bands in one of the most basic thermodynamic quantities. However, orbital magnetization being a bulk property, it also implies that the corresponding topological features must have a local description in real space. Bianco and Resta were the first to propose such a local picture where topology is encoded via a local Chern marker. In this work, we present a microscopic theory for the local (single site) orbital magnetization.
We show that each occupied eigenstate of energy
\varepsilon_{n}
contributes with a local orbital magnetic moment term
{\bm{m}}_{n}({\bm{r}})
and, more significantly, with an effective local Berry-curvature term
{\bm{\Omega}}_{n}({\bm{r}})
in which we identify a geometric contribution
{\bm{\Omega}}^{\text{geom}}_{n}({\bm{r}})
and a topological contribution
{\bm{\Omega}}^{\text{topo}}_{n}({\bm{r}})
. For Bloch electron in crystals, our formalism goes beyond the modern theory of orbital magnetization by revealing the sublattice orbital magnetic texture. More significantly, it opens the possibility of investigating the real space topological features of orbital magnetization on any aperiodic system (molecules, amorphous, quasicrystal, defects).

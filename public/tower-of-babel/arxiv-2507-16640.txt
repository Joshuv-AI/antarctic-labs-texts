An inertial iteratively regularized extragradient method for bilevel variational inequality problems
M. Marques Alves, Kangming Chen, Ellen H. Fukuda

Abstract
We study a bilevel variational inequality problem where the feasible set is itself the solution set of another variational inequality. Motivated by the difficulty of computing projections onto such sets, we consider a regularized extragradient method, as proposed by Samadi and Yousefian (2025), which operates over a simpler constraint set. Building on this framework, we introduce an inertial variant (called IneIREG) that incorporates momentum through extrapolation steps. We establish iteration-complexity bounds for the general (non-strongly monotone) case under both constant and diminishing regularization, and derive improved results under strong monotonicity assumptions. Our analysis extends and refines the results of the previous work by capturing both inertial and regularization effects within a unified framework. Preliminary numerical experiments are also presented to illustrate the behavior of the proposed method. 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification: 90C33, 49J40, 65K15, 68Q25. Key words: Variational inequalities, bilevel, extragradient methods, inertial methods.

Introduction

We consider the (bilevel) variational inequality problem
\text{VIP}(H,Q)
:

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}\kern 5.0ptx\in Q\kern 5.0pt\mbox{such that}\kern 5.0pt\left\langle H(x),z-x\right\rangle\geq 0\kern 5.0pt\text{for all}\kern 5.0ptz\in Q,

(1)

where
Q
is the solution set of the variational inequality
\text{VIP}(F,X)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle Q\coloneqq\{x\in X\mid\left\langle F(x),y-x\right\rangle\geq 0\kern 5.0pt\text{for all}\kern 5.0pty\in X\}.

(2)

(see Section
2
for the precise statement.)
Problem (
1
) arises in different contexts in applications, ranging from (bilevel) optimization to game theory and operations research (see, e.g.,
[
18
, Subsection 1.1]
).

One of the most widely used algorithms for solving variational inequality problems is the celebrated extragradient (EG) method proposed by Korpelevich
[
13
]
, which, when applied to problem (
1
), takes the following form:

\displaystyle y_{k}=P_{Q}(x_{k}-\lambda_{k}H(x_{k})),\quad x_{k+1}=P_{Q}(x_{k}-\lambda_{k}H(y_{k})),

(3)

where
x_{k}
and
x_{k+1}
denote the current and next iterates, respectively,
\lambda_{k}>0
is the stepsize parameter
and
P_{Q}(\cdot)
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
Q
.
The main challenge in implementing (
3
) lies in the fact that the projection

P_{Q}(\cdot)
is difficult to compute, since the feasible set

Q
, defined in (
2
), is itself the solution set of another variational inequality. A common alternative found in the literature (see, e.g.,
[
18
]
) is to adopt a regularization approach, where the operators

F(\cdot)
and
H(\cdot)

are combined using a regularization parameter, say,
\eta_{k}>0
.
At each iteration
k
, one then considers the regularized operator
F(\cdot)+\eta_{k}H(\cdot)
,
and applies iteration (
3
) with respect to the feasible set
X
, which is assumed to be simple enough so that the projection
P_{X}(\cdot)
is computationally tractable.
More precisely, we have

\displaystyle y_{k}=P_{X}\Big(x_{k}-\lambda_{k}\big(F(x_{k})+\eta_{k}H(x_{k})\big)\Big),\quad x_{k+1}=P_{X}\Big(x_{k}-\lambda_{k}\big(F(y_{k})+\eta_{k}H(y_{k})\big)\Big).

(4)

In
[
18
]
, conditions on
\lambda_{k}>0
and
\eta_{k}>0
are established to obtain iteration-complexity bounds for (
4
) with respect to the dual gap function; see Definition
1.1
below.

In this paper, we propose and analyze the iteration-complexity of an inertial variant
of (
4
) —see Algorithm
1
.
Proximal algorithms incorporating inertial effects for monotone inclusions and related problems were first introduced in the seminal work
[
1
]
, and have since been further developed in several directions by various authors (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
9
]
, and references therein).
The central idea is to introduce an inertial effect at the current iterate

x_{k}
by extrapolating in the direction of the previous displacement
x_{k}-x_{k-1}
, leading to the extrapolated point

\displaystyle w_{k}=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}),

(5)

where
\alpha_{k}\geq 0
. The subsequent update is then performed using the extrapolated point

w_{k}
rather than
x_{k}
; see Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
) below.

Our main algorithm, which can be regarded as an inertial variant of (
4

iteration-complexity bounds as those established in
[
18
]
,
while contributing new insights in the strongly monotone case; see Remark
4.12
below.
In particular, this implies that our theoretical contributions remain relevant even for the non-inertial version of the algorithm, which corresponds to setting
\alpha_{k}\equiv 0
in (
5

The main algorithm and some preliminary results

Consider the bilevel variational inequality problem (VIP) as given in (
1
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}\kern 5.0ptx\in Q\kern 5.0pt\mbox{such that}\kern 5.0pt\left\langle H(x),z-x\right\rangle\geq 0\quad\text{for all}\kern 5.0ptz\in Q,

(9)

where
Q
is the solution set of
\text{VIP}(F,X)
— see (
2
) — and the following conditions are assumed to hold:

Assumption 2.1
.

(a)

F\colon\Dom F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
H\colon\Dom H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are monotone and Lipschitz continuous maps, with
(
Lipschitz
)
constants
L_{F}>0
and
L_{H}>0
, respectively.

(b)

X
and
\Omega
are simple nonempty closed and convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

X\subset\Omega\subset\Dom F\cap\Dom H
.

(c)

The solution set
Q
of
\emph{VIP}(F,X)
is nonempty.

We propose the following algorithm to numerically solve (
9
)
under Assumption
2.1
:

Input:

Initial guess
x_{0}=x_{-1}\in X

for

k=0,1,\dots

do

Choose an inertial parameter
\alpha_{k}>0
and let

\displaystyle w_{k}\coloneqq x_{k}+\alpha_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})

(10)

Choose a stepsize
\lambda_{k}>0
, a regularization parameter
\eta_{k}>0
,
set
w^{\prime}_{k}\coloneqq P_{\Omega}(w_{k})
and compute

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &y_{k}=P_{X}\Big(w_{k}-\lambda_{k}\big(F(w^{\prime}_{k})+\eta_{k}H(w^{\prime}_{k})\big)\Big)\\[5.69054pt]
&x_{k+1}=P_{X}\Big(w_{k}-\lambda_{k}\big(F(y_{k})+\eta_{k}H(y_{k})\big)\Big)\end{aligned}

(11)

end for

Algorithm 1

IneIREG: An inertial iteratively regularized EG method for solving (
9
)

Next we make some comments regarding Algorithm

:

(i)

As we mentioned before, the operation in (
10
), which generates the (extrapolated) point
w_{k}
from current and past iterate

and
x_{k-1}
, respectively, introduces
inertial effects
on the iterations generated by Algorithm
1
. Inertial methods, which have their roots in the celebrated heavy-ball and Nesterov’s accelerated gradient methods (see, e.g.,
[
16
,
17
]
), became popular in the context of proximal-type algorithms since the seminal paper
[
1
]
, and were subsequently developed in various research directions (see, e.g.,

[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
9
]

(ii)

Note that (

, is nothing but a step of the celebrated Korpelevich’s extragradient (EG) method
[
13
]

for the monotone operator

We also mention that the additional projection

with respect to the domains of

and

(see Assumption

); if

and

are everywhere defined, then one can simply take

and, consequently,

. Finally, we mention that, since the feasible set

X
is assumed to be simple, it follows that the projection
P_{X}(\cdot)
onto
X
can be
explicitly computed.

(iii)

Conditions on the parameters
\alpha_{k}>0
,
\lambda_{k}>0
and
\eta_{k}>0
ensuring theoretical guarantees for Algorithm
1
will be discussed later in this paper; for instance, as it is customary in the analysis of (extra)gradient-type methods, the stepsize
\lambda_{k}>0
will depend on the inverse of the Lipschitz constant of

, which happens to be

1/(L_{F}+\eta_{k}L_{H})
—  see (

) and, e.g., Assumption
3.1
(c)
below.

(iv)

Algorithm
1
is a generalization (an inertial version) of

[
18
, Algorithm 3.1]
and many results of this work are inspired by the latter reference.
The first results on the iteration-complexity of Algorithm
1
are Propositions

and
3.5
, under the Assumption

. Theorems
3.8
and
3.13
(see also
3.12
) present specialized results for

\eta_{k}>0
, respectively. The main results under the assumption that

From now on in this paper, the sequences

,
\left(\alpha_{k}\right)

Conclusions

In this paper, we studied bilevel variational inequality problems, adding inertia in the iteratively regularized extragradient method, considered in
[
18
]
. We proved iteration-complexity bounds for monotone and strongly-monotone cases, refining also the results obtained in
[
18
]
. Our numerical experiments show that the addition of inertia may positively affect the performance of the algorithm. As a future work, we can analyze the particular case of VI-constrained optimization problems.

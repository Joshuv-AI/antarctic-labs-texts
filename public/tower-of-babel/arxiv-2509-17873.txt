1.
Introduction

It is well-known that suitably defined zeta and
L
-functions and their special values often play significant roles in many areas of mathematics. Witten
[
21
]
studied one variable zeta functions attached to various Lie algebras and related their special values to the volumes of certain moduli spaces of vector bundles of curves. The Witten zeta-function associated with a complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is defined as

(1.1)

\zeta_{\mathfrak{g}}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{(\dim\rho)^{s}},

where the summation runs over all finite dimensional irreducible representations
\rho
of
\mathfrak{g}
. The above definition is due to Zagier
[
22
]
, and the values
\zeta_{\mathfrak{g}}(2k)
for positive integers
k
were first studied by Witten
[
21
]
. In
[
22
]
Zagier proved that

(1.2)

\zeta_{\mathfrak{g}}(2k)\in\mathbb{Q}\pi^{kl}\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}),

where
l
is the number of positive roots of
\mathfrak{g}
, using Witten’s result
[
21
]
. Some explicit formulas for
\zeta_{\mathfrak{g}}(2k)\,(k\in\mathbb{N})
were given by Mordell
[
14
]
, Zagier
[
22
]
, Subbarao and Sitaramachandrarao
[
18
]
and Gunnells and Sczech
[
4
]
. More recently Matsumoto and Tsumura
[
9
]
and Komori et al.
[
11
]
introduced the multi-variable Witten zeta-functions associated with semisimple Lie algebras, and studied their analytical and arithmetical properties (see also
[
12
,
13
]
).

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(2)
has (up to equivalence) one irreducible representation
V_{k}
of each dimension
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Each
n
-dimensional representation
\oplus_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{k}V_{k}
corresponds to a unique partition

(1.3)

n=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{r},\quad 1\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\lambda_{r}\leq n

such that
r_{k}
denotes the number of parts in the partition that equal to
n
. So, the number of
n
-dimensional representations equals
p(n)
, the number of integer partitions of
n
. The partition function has no elementary closed formula, nor does it satisfy any finite order recurrence. However, with
p(0)=1
, its generating function has the following classical product expansion:

(1.4)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}.

In
[
5
]
, Hardy and Ramanujan initiated the analytic study of
p(n)
and used circle method to find the asymptotic formula for the partition function

(1.5)

p(n)\sim\frac{1}{4\sqrt{3}n}e^{\pi\sqrt{2n/3}}\quad\mbox{as}\;\;n\to\infty.

The next case is the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(3)
, whose irreducible representations
W_{j,k}
indexed by pairs of positive integers (see
[
6
, Theorem 6.27]
. Like in the case of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
, a general
n
-dimensional representation decomposes into a sum of these
W_{j,k}
, again each with some multiplicity. So analogous to (
1.4
), the numbers
r_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}(n)
of
n
-dimensional representations, have the generating function

(1.6)

\sum_{n\geq 0}r_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}(n)q^{n}=\prod_{j,k\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-q^{\frac{jk(j+k)}{2}}}

with
r_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}(0)=1
. Romik in
[
17
]
proved that as
n\to\infty

r_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}(n)\sim\frac{K}{n^{\frac{2}{3}}}\exp\left(A_{1}n^{\frac{2}{5}}-A_{2}n^{\frac{3}{10}}-A_{3}n^{\frac{1}{5}}-A_{4}n^{\frac{1}{10}}\right).

Recently, Bringmann and Franke
[
1
]
improved this as follows:

\displaystyle r_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}(n)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{C_{0}}{n^{\frac{2}{3}}}\exp\left(A_{1}n^{\frac{2}{5}}+A_{2}n^{\frac{3}{10}}+A_{3}n^{\frac{1}{5}}+A_{4}n^{\frac{1}{10}}\right)\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{C_{j}}{n^{\frac{j}{10}}}+\mathcal{O}_{N}\left(n^{-\frac{N+1}{10}}\right)\right)

when
n\to\infty
.
This framework also generalizes to the Witten zeta function for
\mathfrak{so}(5)
. It is known that the finite-dimensional representations of
\mathfrak{so}(5)
can be doubly indexed as
(T_{j,k})_{j,k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\dim T_{j,k}=\frac{1}{6}jk(j+k)(j+2k)
. A general
n
-dimensional representation decomposes as a sum of these
T_{j,k}
, each with some multiplicity. Therefore, one can have

(1.7)

\sum_{n\geq 0}r_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(n)q^{n}=\prod_{j,k\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-q^{\frac{jk(j+k)(j+2k)}{6}}}.

In
[
2
]
, Bridges et al., derived an assymptotic formula for
r_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(n)
for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and as
n\to\infty

\displaystyle r_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(n)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{C}{n^{\frac{7}{12}}}\exp\left(A_{1}n^{\frac{1}{3}}+A_{2}n^{\frac{2}{9}}+A_{3}n^{\frac{1}{9}}+A_{4}\right)\left(1+\sum_{j=2}^{N+1}\frac{B_{j}}{n^{\frac{j-1}{9}}}+\mathcal{O}_{N}\left(n^{-\frac{N+1}{9}}\right)\right).

Let
\varrho_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}(n)
denote the number of irreducible
\mathfrak{su}(3)
representations of dimension
n
, that is,

(1.8)

\varrho_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\geq 1\\
\frac{jk(j+k)}{2}=n\end{subarray}}1.

Note that this is a highly oscillatory function and is often
0
and it is similar to the divisor function

d(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\geq 1\\
jk=n\end{subarray}}1.

We know that Dirichlet hyperbola method gives

(1.9)

\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}d(n)=x\log x+(2\gamma-1)x+\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{x}),

where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. Using a variant of the the hyperbola method, Bridges et al.
[
3
]
, obtained an assymptotic expansion for the function
\varrho_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}(n)
as follows:

(1.10)

\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\varrho_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}(n)=\frac{2^{\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{3}\Gamma(\frac{1}{3})^{3}}}{4\pi}x^{\frac{2}{3}}+2^{\frac{3}{2}}\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\sqrt{x}+\mathcal{O}(x^{\frac{1}{3}}),

as
x\to\infty
.
In the same paper
[
3
]
, they pose the question of establishing an asymptotic formula for
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\geq 1\\
p(j,k)\leq x\end{subarray}}1
, where
p(x,y)
is a homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[x,y]
taking integer values. In this paper, we deduce an asymptotic expansion for the number of irreducible
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-representaion of dimension
n
, that is,

(1.11)

\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\varrho_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(n)=\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\geq 1\\
\frac{jk(j+k)(j+2k)}{6}=n\end{subarray}}1.

Precisely we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have, as
x\to\infty

\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\varrho_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(n)=\frac{\sqrt{3}\Gamma(\frac{1}{4})^{2}}{4\sqrt{\pi}}x^{\frac{1}{2}}+K\cdot x^{\frac{1}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(x^{\frac{1}{4}}),

where

K:=\left(\left(\zeta\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right)(10\cdot 6^{1/3}+2\cdot 3^{1/3})-3^{4/3}(2^{-2/3}+2^{-1})+3^{\frac{1}{3}}(2+2^{\frac{4}{3}})\right).

The organizations of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we describe the multi-variable Witten zeta-functions associated with semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(5)
. Next we discuss the analytic continuation of the series
\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)
and the location of its poles and residues. Finally, in Section
3
, we proof the main theorem.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Witten zeta function associated to
\mathfrak{so}(5)

In this section, we describe the multi-variable Witten zeta-functions associated with semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(5)
in terms of roots and weights for the corresponding root systems using Weyl’s dimension formula
[
10
]
. We follow Matsumoto and Tsumura
[
9
]
and Komori et al.
[
11
]
for the following definitions.

The fundamental system for
\mathfrak{so}(5)
is given by
\Pi:=\{\alpha_{1}=\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2},\alpha_{2}=\epsilon_{2}\}
and the list of positive roots are:

(2.1)

\Delta_{+}:=\{\epsilon_{1}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\epsilon_{2}=\alpha_{2},\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}=\alpha_{1},\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}=\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}\}.

The fundamental coroots are given by
\{\alpha_{1}^{\vee}=e_{1}-e_{2},\alpha_{2}^{\vee}=2e_{2}\}
and hence the positive coroots are
\{2e_{1},2e_{2},e_{1}-e_{2},e_{1}+e_{2}\}
. Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
be two fundamental weights satisfying
<\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\lambda_{j}>=\lambda_{j}(\alpha_{i})=\delta_{ij}
where
\delta_{ij}
is Kronecker’s delta function and
i,j=1,2
. Any dominant weight can be written as

\lambda=n_{1}\lambda_{1}+n_{2}\lambda_{2}\quad n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

The lowest strongly dominant is of the form
\delta=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
. Let
d_{\lambda}
be the dimension of the representation space corresponding to the weight
\lambda
. By Weyl’s dimension formula, we have the following.

\displaystyle d_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\frac{<\alpha^{\vee},\lambda+\delta>}{<\alpha^{\vee},\delta>}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\frac{<\alpha^{\vee},(n_{1}+1)\lambda_{1}+(n_{2}+1)\lambda_{2}>}{<\alpha^{\vee},\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}>}

\displaystyle=(n_{1}+1)(n_{2}+1)(n_{1}+n_{2}+2)(n_{1}+2n_{2}+3)/6.

Now set
n_{1}+1=m,n_{2}+1=n
. Then from (
1.1
), Witten zeta function is given by

(2.2)

\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)=6^{s}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{s}n^{s}(m+n)^{s}(m+2n)^{s}}.

To obtain the precise region of convergence of the Dirichlet series given in (
2.2
), we rewrite (
2.2
) as

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)&=6^{s}\left(\sum_{n>m\geq 1}^{\infty}+\sum_{m>n\geq 1}^{\infty}+\sum_{n=m\geq 1}^{\infty}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{s}n^{s}(m+n)^{s}(m+2n)^{s}}\\
&=2\cdot 6^{s}\sum_{n>m\geq 1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{s}n^{s}(m+n)^{s}(m+2n)^{s}}+\zeta(4s)\\
&=2\cdot 6^{s}\sum_{n>m\geq 1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{s}n^{3s}(1+m/n)^{s}(2+m/n)^{s}}+\zeta(4s).\end{split}

Note that the series
\zeta(4s)
converges if and only if
\re(s)>1/4
and since

6^{-s}<\sum_{n>m\geq 1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+m/n)^{s}(2+m/n)^{s}}<2^{-s},

then the first sum in (
2.3
) converges absolutely if and only if the series

(2.4)

\sum_{n>m\geq 1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|n^{3s}m^{s}|}=\sum_{n>m\geq 1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{3\sigma}m^{\sigma}},\quad\mbox{where}\;\;s=\sigma+it

converges absolutely. In comparision with the integral

\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{3\sigma}y^{\sigma}}dxdy=\frac{1}{\sigma-1}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{1-4\sigma}dx,

we conclude that the series in (
2.4
) converges absolutely and uniformly on compacts if and only if
\re(s)>1/2
. The above discussion proves the following:

Proposition 2.1
.

The series (
2.2
) converges absolutely in the domain
\{s\in\mathbb{C}\mid\re(s)>1/2\}
and defines a holomorphic function in that region.

2.2.
Analytic continuation of
\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)

Here we will see the analytic continuation of the series
\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)
to a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}
. This fact can be obtained from
[
8
]
. To obtain analytic continuation of
\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)
, we need the
Mordell–Tornheim
zeta function
\zeta_{MT}
. Let
\re(s_{j})>1,(j=1,2,3)
and define

(2.5)

\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{s_{1}}n^{s_{2}}(m+n)^{s_{3}}}.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

The function
\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
can be meromorphically continued to the whole
\mathbb{C}^{3}
-space, and its singularities are only on the subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
defined by one of the equations

(2.6)

s_{1}+s_{3}=1-l,s_{2}+s_{3}=1-l,\,(l\in\mathbb{N}_{0})\;\;\mbox{or}\;\;s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2.

We recall Mellin-Barnes integrals
[
15
, p. 91]
, namely

(2.7)

\Gamma(s)(1+\lambda)^{-s}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\alpha-i\infty}^{\alpha+i\infty}\Gamma(s+z)\Gamma(-z)\lambda^{z}\,dz,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
and
-\re(s)<\alpha<0
. Setting
\lambda=n/(m+n)
, multiplying by
m^{-s}n^{-s}(m+n)^{-2s}
and summing over
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
gives

\displaystyle\Gamma(s)\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)

\displaystyle=\Gamma(s)6^{s}\sum_{n,m=1}^{\infty}m^{-s}n^{-s}\left(m+n\right)^{-2s}\left(1+\frac{n}{m+n}\right)^{-s}

\displaystyle=\frac{6^{s}}{2\pi i}\sum_{n,m=1}^{\infty}m^{-s}n^{-s}(m+n)^{-2s}\int\limits_{\alpha-i\infty}^{\alpha+i\infty}\Gamma(s+z)\Gamma(-z)(m+n)^{-z}n^{z}\,dz

(2.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{6^{s}}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\alpha-i\infty}^{\alpha+i\infty}\Gamma(s+z)\Gamma(-z)\ \zeta_{MT}(s,s-z,2s+z)\,dz.

where
-\re(s)<\alpha<0
. Let
L
be a large positive integer, and put
\Phi(s)=(4s-2)\prod_{\ell=0}^{L}(3s-1+\ell)
. Then

(2.9)

\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)=\Phi(s)^{-1}I,

where

(2.10)

I=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\alpha-i\infty}^{\alpha+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s+z)\Gamma(-z)}{\Gamma(s)}\zeta_{MT}(s,s-z,2s+z)\Phi(s)\,dz.

We shift the path of integration to
\re(z)=M-\epsilon
, where
M
is a large positive integer and
\epsilon
is a small positive number. Counting the residues at
z=k\ (0\leq k\leq M-1)
, we obtain

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)&=\frac{6^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k!}\frac{\Gamma(s+k)}{\Gamma(s)}\zeta_{MT}(s,s-k,2s+k)+\Phi(s)^{-1}I^{\prime}\\
&=\frac{6^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\binom{-s}{k}\zeta_{MT}(s,s-k,2s+k)+\Phi(s)^{-1}I^{\prime}\end{split}

where

(2.12)

I^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{M-\epsilon-i\infty}^{M-\epsilon+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s+z)\Gamma(-z)}{\Gamma(s)}\zeta_{MT}(s,s-z,2s+z)\Phi(s)\,dz.

Hence, the integral
I^{\prime}
can be continued holomorphically to the region

\mathcal{D}_{M,L}:=\{s\in\mathbb{C}\mid\re(s)>-M+\epsilon,\re(3s)>-L,\;\re(3s)>1-M+\epsilon\}

because in this region the poles of the integrand are not on the path of integration. Therefore, (
2.11
) gives the meromorphic continuation of
\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)
to
\mathcal{D}_{M,L}
, and the candidates for singularities in this region are

(2.13)

3s=1-\ell,\;4s=2,\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
2
]
)
.

The function
\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)
can be analytically continued to a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}
whose positive poles are simple and occur for
s\in\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3},-\frac{1}{3},-\frac{2}{3},\ldots\right\}
.

Furthermore, we also need certain residues of
\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)
. For a detailed proof of the following result, refer to
[
2
, Proposition 5.16]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

The poles of
\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)
are precisely
\{\frac{1}{2}\}\cup\{\frac{d}{3}\not\in\mathbb{Z}\mid d\leq 1,d\;\;\mbox{odd}\}
. We have

\mbox{Res}_{s=\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)=\frac{\sqrt{3}\Gamma(\frac{1}{4})^{2}}{8\sqrt{\pi}}.

Moreover, for
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 1}\setminus\{-3\mathbb{N}_{0}\}

\mbox{Res}_{s=\frac{d}{3}}\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)=\frac{3^{\frac{d}{3}-\frac{3}{2}}\pi\Gamma(\frac{d}{6})\zeta(\frac{4d}{3}-1)}{2^{\frac{d}{3}-1}(1-d)!\Gamma(\frac{d}{3})^{2}\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}\left(\frac{d}{3}\right)(1+2^{\frac{2d}{3}-1}).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

At first, we note that the asymptotic main term of
\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\varrho_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(n)
can be derived by the analytic properties of
\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)
along with a standard Tauberian theorem. Observe that we rewrite

\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)=\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\frac{6^{s}}{j^{s}k^{s}(j+k)^{s}(j+2k)^{s}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\varrho_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(n)}{n^{s}}.

By Proposition
2.3
,
\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)
has a meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{\frac{1}{2}\}\cup\{\frac{d}{3}\not\in\mathbb{Z}\mid d\leq 1,d\;\;\mbox{odd}\}
. Then from the Ikehera-Wiener Tauberian theorem (see
[
16
, Chapter 3]
), we get

(3.1)

\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\varrho_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(n)\sim\frac{\mbox{Res}_{s=\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(s)}{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{3}\Gamma(\frac{1}{4})^{2}}{4\sqrt{\pi}}x^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mbox{as}\;\;x\to\infty.

We write

(3.2)

\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\varrho_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(n)=\sum_{1\leq N\leq x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 1\\
\frac{mn(m+n)(m+2n)}{6}=N\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 1\\
mn(m+n)(m+2n)\leq 6x\\
mn(m+n)(m+2n)\equiv 0\pmod{6}\end{subarray}}1.

to obtain the next term in the asymptotic expansion. Since
mn(m+n)(m+2n)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
and
mn(m+n)(m+2n)\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, we can conclude that
mn(m+n)(m+2n)\equiv 0\pmod{6}
is always satisfied. Observe that the sum in (
3.2
) counts lattice points in the
(m,n)
-plane between
m=1,n=1
and the curves given by
m=T_{1}
and
n=T_{2}
where

(3.3)

T_{1}:=n\left[\left(\frac{3x}{n^{4}}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\left(\frac{6x}{n^{4}}\right)^{2}-\frac{4}{27}}\right)^{1/3}+\left(\frac{3x}{n^{4}}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\left(\frac{6x}{n^{4}}\right)^{2}-\frac{4}{27}}\right)^{1/3}-1\right]

and

(3.4)

T_{2}:=m\left[\left(\frac{3x}{2m^{4}}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\left(\frac{3x}{m^{4}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{432}}\right)^{1/3}+\left(\frac{3x}{2m^{4}}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\left(\frac{3x}{m^{4}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{432}}\right)^{1/3}-\frac{1}{2}\right].

Note that
m
and and
n
given in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) are positive solutions to the cubic equation

mn(m+n)(m+2n)=6x.

Then, using the hyperbola method and the notation
\{t\}:=t-\lfloor{t\rfloor}
, we rewrite (
3.2
) as follows.

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\varrho_{\mathfrak{so}(5)}(n)&=\sum_{1\leq n\leq x^{\frac{1}{4}}}\sum_{1\leq m\leq T_{1}}1+\sum_{1\leq m\leq x^{\frac{1}{4}}}\sum_{1\leq n\leq T_{2}}1-\lfloor{x^{\frac{1}{4}}\rfloor}^{2}\\
&=\sum_{1\leq n\leq x^{\frac{1}{4}}}\lfloor{T_{1}\rfloor}+\sum_{1\leq m\leq x^{\frac{1}{4}}}\lfloor{T_{2}\rfloor}-\lfloor{x^{\frac{1}{4}}\rfloor}^{2}\\
&=\sum_{1\leq n\leq x^{\frac{1}{4}}}\lfloor{T_{1}\rfloor}+\sum_{1\leq m\leq x^{\frac{1}{4}}}\lfloor{T_{2}\rfloor}-x^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{O}(x^{\frac{1}{4}}).\end{split}

First consider

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{1\leq n\leq x^{\frac{1}{4}}}\lfloor{T_{1}\rfloor}&=\sum_{1\leq n\leq x^{\frac{1}{4}}}n\left(\left(\frac{3x}{n^{4}}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\left(\frac{6x}{n^{4}}\right)^{2}-\frac{4}{27}}\right)^{1/3}+\left(\frac{3x}{n^{4}}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\left(\frac{6x}{n^{4}}\right)^{2}-\frac{4}{27}}\right)^{1/3}\right)\\
&-\sum_{1\leq n\leq x^{\frac{1}{4}}}n+\mathcal{O}(x^{\frac{1}{4}})=\sum_{1\leq n\leq x^{\frac{1}{4}}}f_{1}(n)-\frac{\lfloor{x^{\frac{1}{4}}\rfloor}(\lfloor{x^{\frac{1}{4}}\rfloor}+1)}{2}+\mathcal{O}(x^{\frac{1}{4}})\\
&=\sum_{1\leq n\leq x^{\frac{1}{4}}}f_{1}(n)-\frac{1}{2}x^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{O}(x^{\frac{1}{4}}),\end{split}

where

\displaystyle f_{1}(n)

Remark 3.1
.

We deduce from the proof of Theorem

that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{1/4}}t\left[\left(\frac{1}{t^{4}}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{t^{4}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{27}}\right)^{1/3}+\left(\frac{1}{t^{4}}-\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{t^{4}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{27}}\right)^{1/3}\right]dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{1/4}}t\left[\left(\frac{1}{2t^{4}}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{2t^{4}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{1728}}\right)^{1/3}+\left(\frac{1}{2t^{4}}-\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{2t^{4}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{1728}}\right)^{1/3}\right]dt

Design of an omnidirectional conical beam leaky wave antenna using the holographic technique
Authors: Samira Azad, Mobina Azad, Ali Abdolali, Amir Saman Nooramin, Amrollah Amini
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe microwave holographic principle offers a promising framework for designing leaky-wave antennas (LWAs) with conical radiation patterns. In this paper, a holographic method is presented to synthesize an omnidirectional LWA with angular scanning capability in elevation. This paper proposes the aperture field estimation theory for synthesizing holographic leaky-wave antennas. Its advantage is the accurate prediction of far-field pattern characteristics, such as amplitude, phase, and polarization. To excite the hologram, a monopole launcher is placed in the center of the structure. To demonstrate the validity of the design method, an LWA is fabricated and measured in an anechoic chamber. The proposed holographic principle is validated through experimental results in terms of impedance bandwidth, maximum realized gain, radiation efficiency, and radiation patterns. The measurement results exhibit an impedance bandwidth of 20% (9–11 GHz) with an average realized gain of 12.5 dBi in the operating frequency band. In addition, at the design frequency of 10 GHz, the maximum realized gain, beam scanning, sidelobe level (SLL), and radiation efficiency are 13.1 dBi, , −10 dB, and 91%, respectively. The measured results agree well with full-wave simulations performed in CST software. The proposed holographic antenna can be a good candidate for wireless communication applications.Subject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

A key requirement for establishing communication links between moving vehicles on the ground and geostationary satellites is the use of antennas that radiate conical beam patterns in the vertical plane. Traditionally, vertical monopole antennas have been used in mobile vehicle communications. However, their large physical size is unsuitable for applications requiring low profile and conformability. Over the years, microstrip patch antennas have been used to generate conical beam patterns. Currently, designers and researchers are increasingly focusing on leaky-wave antennas (LWAs) as a promising solution to achieve this goal1–3.

LWAs are non-resonant structures that operate through the gradual leakage of electromagnetic energy along the guiding structure. By altering the operating frequency, an LWA enables angular scanning of the radiation beam. LWAs can be classified into three categories: uniform, quasi-uniform and periodic structures4. LWAs with periodic structures can achieve scanning in both backward and forward directions. In contrast, LWAs with uniform structures typically operate away from the broadside and can only scan the forward quadrant. Furthermore, due to the non-resonant nature of LWAs, it is possible to attain a broad bandwidth when a broadband launcher is used. In addition to the above, high directivity, low SLL, and compactness are other advantages of leaky wave structures. LWAs are utilized in various fields due to their distinct properties, such as wireless communication, satellite communication, radar, and sensors5–9.

The initial application of the holographic technique were in the optical frequency band. The transition of holography from the optical to the microwave frequency band has provided a new approach to address common antenna design challenges in this frequency range10. Checcacci introduced the concept of holographic antennas in 197011. This technique can be utilized in the synthesis of transmit arrays12, reflectarrays13, end-fire antennas14, and LWAs15–18, with the application of vortex beam generation19, multi frequency20, multi-beam21,22, radar cross-section (RCS) reduction23, and circularly polarized beam generation24. In leaky-wave holographic antennas, the hologram surface is the radiation aperture excited by surface waves. In other words, the hologram is an interference pattern that is created by the superposition of the reference wave (surface wave in leaky-wave mode) and the object wave (antenna’s radiation beam). By adjusting the geometric features of the holographic antenna structure, the reference wave can be engineered to produce the desired radiation pattern (object wave). The advantages of holographic antennas include frequency-controlled beam scanning, radiation pattern synthesis, and ease of integration with other devices25.

In this paper, a leaky-wave antenna with conical radiation and angular scanning capability is proposed, based on the combination of holographic principle and the aperture field estimation (AFE) method. Previously, the realization of conical beams was achieved solely using holographic techniques. As reported in26, an anisotropic holographic antenna was proposed to generate a conical pattern on a glass substrate. In that work, only the synthesis method was presented, and its analysis was performed using full-wave simulation software. In our paper, however, the frequency-domain analysis of the antenna is provided to predict its behavior, which significantly accelerates the design process. One advantage of the AFE method is its ability to accurately predict far-field characteristics such as amplitude, phase, and polarization. Accordingly, the AFE method provides reliable predictions of far-field characteristics during the design process. Using the proposed analytical method, the operating frequency of the antenna can be analyzed. Investigating the effect of frequency variation on the antenna’s radiation characteristics enables the designer to control the beam scanning of the antenna as a function of frequency. This study attempts to present a systematic method for designing an LWA with omnidirectional conical radiation. The concept of leaky-wave metasurfaces and the holographic principle are combined to construct an artificial isotropic impedance surface. The square patch unit cell is employed to realize the holographic LWA, fabricated on a Rogers RO4003 substrate with a thickness of 1.524 mm at the design frequency of 10 GHz. In addition, a monopole antenna is employed to generate a reference wave. The measured results show good agreement with the simulation and AFE-based theoretical predictions obtained using MATLAB and CST Microwave Studio software.

Conclusion

In this paper, a holographic LWA has been designed, fabricated, and measured with an omnidirectional conical beam. The square-shaped patch unit cells are used to implement the conical radiation in LWA using the holographic technique. During the design process, using the AFE method allows for accurate predictions of far-field pattern characteristics. Accordingly, this study attempts to present, for the first time, a systematic method for designing an LWA with omnidirectional conical radiation. According to the measurement results, the impedance bandwidth, radiation efficiency, and SLL of the E-plane at the design frequency are obtained as 20%, 91%, and −10 dB, respectively. The peak of the measured realized gain of the proposed LWA is about 13.1 dBi at 10 GHz. According to the results, the proposed LWA is an appropriate candidate for establishing communication links between moving vehicles on the ground and geostationary satellites.

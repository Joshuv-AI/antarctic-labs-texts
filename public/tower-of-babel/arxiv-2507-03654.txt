Bulk-to-surface coupling of spatiotemporal vortices
Francisco J. Rodríguez-Fortuño, Michela F. Picardi, Konstantin Y. Bliokh

Abstract
We consider coupling of bulk space-time-structured waves, such as spatiotemporal vortex pulses (STVPs), to surface waves, such as surface plasmon-polaritons (SPPs). For resonant coupling that preserves the frequency and tangent wavevector components, it is challenging to transfer the space-time wave structure because of the difference between the bulk and surface wave dispersions. We describe three mechanisms allowing for the bulk-to-surface conversion of STVPs: (i) suitable tilt of the pulse spectrum in the ( ω , 𝐤 ) (\omega,{\bf k}) space; (ii) confinement of the incident pulse in the direction orthogonal to the vortex; and (iii) losses in surface waves. We supply general considerations with numerical simulations of the STVP-to-SPP coupling at a dielectric-prism/metal-film interface.



Following remarkable progress in studies and applications involving structured (i.e., inhomogeneous) optical, acoustic, and other types of waves
Andrews 2008
;
Rubinsztein-Dunlop et al. 2016
;
Bliokh et al. 2023
, there is a current burst of interest in

structured waves, which are inhomogeneous in both space and time
Yessenov et al. 2022
;
Shen et al. 2023
. Such waves are essentially
polychromatic

\omega({\bf k})
(
\omega
is the frequency,
{\bf k}
is the wavevector) increases considerably.

The generation and propagation of various spatiotemporal structured waves has been well explored by now. An important question, crucial for information-transfer applications, is: how can one transfer the wave structure between different optical elements, media, or even different kinds of wave? Here we consider this question in the context of coupling of bulk spatiotemporal structured waves into 2D surface or planar-guided modes.

The Fourier amplitudes of spatiotemporal
(t,{\bf r})
-structured waves are accordingly structured in the
(\omega,{\bf k})
domain. Importantly, this domain is constrained by dispersion relations, which are different for bulk and surface modes:
\omega({\bf k})
and
\omega_{s}({\bf k}_{s})
. Furthermore, the resonant coupling to surface or guided waves is typically determined by phase-matching conditions preserving frequencies and tangent wavevector components. Therefore, maintaining the
(\omega,{\bf k})
-space structure simultaneously with the phase-matching conditions and different dispersion relations offers a challenging problem.

Here we address this problem by considering an important class of spatiotemporal structured waves:
spatiotemporal vortex pulses
(STVPs), which were recently explored both theoretically

;

;

;

;

;

While well-known monochromatic vortex beams are 3D structures, carrying screw wavefront dislocations

;
Torres and Torner 2011
, STVPs can appear as moving 2D structures with edge wavefront dislocations. This makes them perfectly suited for surface waves (e.g., water waves
Che et al. 2024
) or planar waveguides (e.g., acoustic
Zhang et al. 2023
). Yet, the generation of STVPs in 2D optical systems, such as surface plasmon-polaritons (SPPs)
Maier 2007
;
Zayats et al. 2005

In this paper, we consider the resonant coupling and transformation of an obliquely incident bulk STVP into a 2D surface-wave (or planar-waveguide) STVP, Fig.
1
. We assume a linear dispersion for bulk waves, an arbitrary dispersion for surface waves, and the simplest phase-matching conditions: conservation of the frequency and tangent wavevector components. For numerical simulations, we take an example of the SPP excitation via the total internal reflection at the bottom of a glass prism (the Otto-Kretschmann configuration). We show that to preserve the spatiotemporal structure of the pulse, one needs to obey one of the three conditions:

(i) The spectrum of the incident pulse is appropriately
tilted
in the
(\omega,{\bf k})
space;

(ii) The incident pulse is sufficiently
confined
in the direction orthogonal to the vortex plane;

(iii) Surface waves have sufficient
losses
.

The first condition addresses the difference between the bulk and surface wave dispersions, and provides the best matching between the Fourier spectra of these waves.

We aim at exciting a paraxial surface-wave STVP, which propagates in the
z=0
plane along the
x
-axis. The simplest Bessel type of such STVP
Bliokh 2021
;
Bliokh 2023
is described by the plane-wave spectrum distributed over an ellipse in the
(k_{x},k_{y})
plane:

{\bf k}_{s}=(k_{s0}+\Delta_{x}\cos\phi,\Delta_{y}\sin\phi)\,.

(1)

Here,
{\bf k}_{s0}=(k_{s0},0)
is the central (mean) wavevector,
\Delta_{x,y}
are the spectral widths of the pulse along the
x
and
y
axes, and
\phi\in(0,2\pi)
is the azimuthal-angle parameter along the elliptical curve in the wavevector space.
In the paraxial approximation, we assume
\Delta_{x,y}\ll k_{s0}
, and then the frequencies, corresponding to the spectrum (
1
) and dispersion relation
\omega_{s}(k_{s})
, are

\omega_{s}(k_{s})\simeq\omega_{s0}+\frac{\partial\omega_{s0}}{\partial k_{s0}}\Delta_{x}\cos\phi\,,

(2)

where
\omega_{s0}=\omega_{s}(k_{s0})
.

The vortex structure is determined by the phase factor
e^{i\ell\phi}
(
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
is the topological vortex charge) in the spectral plane-wave amplitudes. Notably, the topological charge does not affect the considerations below, because the wavevectors and frequencies of the Bessel-type vortices are independent of
\ell
.

We assume that the surface-wave STVP is excited by a paraxial bulk-wave STVP propagating at the angle of incidence
\theta_{0}
, as shown in Fig.
1
. The plane-wave spectrum of the incident pulse can be written as

{\bf k}={\bf k}_{0}+{\bm{\delta}}=(k_{0}\sin\theta_{0}+\delta_{x},\delta_{y},-k_{0}\cos\theta_{0}+\delta_{z})\,,

(3)

where
{\bf k}_{0}
is the central wavevector and
{\bm{\delta}}
describes small deflections from it (
\delta\ll k_{0}
). For linear bulk-wave dispersion
\omega=\tilde{c}k
, the corresponding frequencies are

\omega(k)\simeq\omega_{0}+\tilde{c}\,(\delta_{x}\sin\theta_{0}-\delta_{z}\cos\theta_{0})\,,

(4)

where
\omega_{0}=\tilde{c}k_{0}
, and
\tilde{c}
is the speed of the bulk wave.

Next, we assume that the system is invariant with respect to space-time translations along the
(x,y,t)
dimensions.
This is the case, e.g., for the excitation of SPPs via a total internal reflection at the bottom of a glass prism parallel to the metal surface
Maier 2007
;
Zayats et al. 2005
.
Then, the frequencies and tangent wave-vector components in the bulk and surface pulses have to match each other:

\omega_{s}=\omega\,,\quad k_{s\,x}=k_{x}\,,\quad k_{s\,y}=k_{y}\,.

(5)

Substituting Eqs. (
1
), (
2
), (
3
), and (
4
) into the matching conditions (
5
), we obtain the necessary conditions for the bulk pulse to couple to the surface one:

\displaystyle\omega_{0}

\displaystyle=\omega_{s0}\,,\quad k_{0}\sin\theta_{0}=k_{s0}\,

(6)

\displaystyle\delta_{x}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{x}\cos\phi\,,\quad\delta_{y}=\Delta_{y}\sin\phi\,,

\displaystyle\delta_{z}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{x}\cos\phi\left(\frac{\sin\theta_{0}}{\cos\theta_{0}}-\frac{1}{\tilde{c}\cos\theta_{0}}\frac{\partial\omega_{s0}}{\partial k_{s0}}\right).

(7)

Equations (
6
) are the standard phase-matching conditions in the central-plane-wave approximation, while Eqs. (
7
) take into account deflections of the plane waves in the spectra from the central waves.

Importantly, the last condition (
7
) involves the surface-wave
dispersion
. For non-dispersive surface waves with
{\partial\omega_{s0}}/{\partial k_{s0}}=\omega_{s0}/k_{s0}=\tilde{c}/\sin\theta_{0}

7

\delta_{z}\sin\theta_{0}=-\delta_{x}\cos\theta_{0}
. In this case, the incident-pulse spectrum (
3
) is an ellipse lying in the plane containing the central wavevector
{\bf k}_{c}
. In other words, such pulse has a purely transverse angular momentum, associated with the vortex

;
Hancock et al. 2021
;
Mazanov et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2021
;
Bliokh 2023
;
Porras 2024
;
Vo et al. 2024
;
Jhajj et al. 2016
;
Chong et al. 2020
;
Hancock et al. 2019
;
Zang et al. 2022
;
Cao et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2024
;
Huang et al. 2024
;
Zhang et al. 2023
;
Ge et al. 2023
;

;
Wan et al. 2023
;

. However, for dispersive surface waves,
{\partial\omega_{s0}}/{\partial k_{s0}}\neq\omega_{s0}/k_{s0}
, the plane-wave spectrum (
3

) is an ellipse
tilted

{\bf k}_{0}

To describe such a tilted STVP,

it is instructive to introduce the coordinate frame
(\xi,y,\zeta)
rotated by the angle
\pi/2-\theta_{0}
about the
y
-axis, Fig.
1
. The central wavevector
{\bf k}_{0}
of the incident pulse becomes aligned with the
\xi
-axis, whereas the deflection
{\bm{\delta}}
, Eq. (
7
) acquires components

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}

\displaystyle=-\delta_{z}\cos\theta_{0}+\delta_{x}\sin\theta_{0}=\Delta_{x}\frac{\cos\phi}{\sin\theta_{0}}\frac{v_{s}}{u_{s}}\,,

\displaystyle\delta_{\zeta}

\displaystyle=\delta_{z}\sin\theta_{0}+\delta_{x}\cos\theta_{0}=\Delta_{x}\frac{\cos\phi}{\cos\theta_{0}}\!\left(1-\frac{v_{s}}{u_{s}}\right).

(8)

Here we introduced the group and phase velocities of the surface wave:
v_{s}={\partial\omega_{s0}}/{\partial k_{s0}}
and
u_{s}={\omega_{s0}}/{k_{s0}}
.
Then, the tilt angle
\alpha
of the elliptical spectrum of the incident STVP in the
(\xi,\zeta)
-coordinates is determined by

\tan\alpha=\frac{\delta_{\zeta}}{\delta_{\xi}}=\left(\frac{u_{s}}{v_{s}}-1\right)\tan\theta_{0}\,.

(9)

For SPPs,
u_{s}>v_{s}
, and, hence,
\alpha>0
, as shown in Fig.
1

Incident STVP with a tilted spectrum, Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
), is the best option for the resonant coupling under consideration. However, many experiments deal with non-tilted STVPs with purely transverse angular momentum, and therefore we explore the possibilities of phase matching between bulk and surface STVPs without this additional tilt. We now assume
\alpha=\delta_{\zeta}=0
in the incident pulse, which yields
k_{z}=-k_{x}\cot\theta_{0}

\omega=\tilde{c}k=\tilde{c}\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}}=\tilde{c}\sqrt{\frac{k_{x}^{2}}{\sin^{2}\!\theta_{0}}+k_{y}^{2}}\,.

(10)

\omega_{s}=\sqrt{{c}^{2}k_{s}^{2}+\omega_{p}^{2}/2-\sqrt{{c}^{4}k_{s}^{4}+\omega_{p}^{4}/4}}\,.

(11)

Here,
k_{s}^{2}=k^{2}_{s\,x}+k^{2}_{s\,y}

\omega_{p}

Let the phase matching (

Fortunately, infinitely thin elliptical spectra, as well as infinitely thin dispersion sheets are idealized theoretical concepts. Indeed, an infinitely thin elliptical spectrum of the incident pulse in Fig.
1
corresponds to a cylindrical pulse infinitely extended along the
\zeta
-axis (for a non-tilted pulse). To deal with physical pulses
confined
along the
\zeta

\exp(-D^{2}k^{2}_{\zeta}/2)

is the

8

\displaystyle D\sim\frac{1}{\delta_{\zeta}}\lesssim\frac{\cos\theta_{0}}{\Delta_{x}}\!\left(1-\frac{v_{s}}{u_{s}}\right)^{-1}.

(12)

Under this condition, the Gaussian-distributed plane waves in the incident STVP include a tilted ellipse required for optimal phase matching (
8

Finally, there is another way to provide an overlap between the dispersion characteristics of bulk and surface STVPs in Fig.
2
. Namely, even for an infinitely thin incident-STVP spectrum,
losses
in surface waves effectively provide a finite thickness to their dispersion sheet, and hence to the red curve in Fig.
2

\delta\omega_{s}\sim|{\rm Im}\,\omega_{s}|

{\rm Im}\,\omega_{s}
describes the losses.
In particular, such losses (originating from absorption or leakage) are inevitable in SPPs.
An overlap between the (infinitely-thin) bulk and surface pulse dispersions in Fig.
2

\displaystyle\delta\omega_{s}\gtrsim\frac{\tilde{c}}{\sin\theta_{0}}\Delta_{x}-\frac{\partial\omega_{s0}}{\partial k_{s0}}\Delta_{x}=\left(u_{s}-v_{s}\right)\Delta_{x}\,.

(13)

To conclude, we have described the main mechanisms of the resonant coupling of space-time-structured waves from bulk to surface modes.
Specifically, we have examined the case of bulk STVPs coupled to SPPs, assuming conserved frequency and tangent wavevector components.
While transverse confinement of the pulse and SPP losses facilitate transfer of the space-time wave structure, a suitable rotation of the bulk pulse spectrum in the
(\omega,{\bf k})
space allows for near-perfect adjustment between the bulk and surface wave dispersions. Other mechanisms, not considered in this work, might include broadband couplers, such as a single slit that removes conservation of
k_{x}

Gorodetski et al. 2012
, active media
MacDonald et al. 2009
, and nonlinear effects
Bliokh et al. 2009
.

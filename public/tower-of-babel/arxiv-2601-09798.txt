witt affine springer theory
Noam Nissan and Yakov Varshavsky

Abstract
. This paper extends the affine Springer theory developed by Bouthier, Kazhdan, and the second author (see [ 6 ] ) to the mixed characteristic case. In particular, we introduce a theory of perfectly placid perfect ∞ \infty -stacks and establish their dimension theory. Furthermore, we prove that, in the Witt vector setting, the Chevalley morphism between arc spaces is flat.

1.4

Working over perfect schemes.

In this work, we define perfect
\infty
-stacks as sheaves on the étale site of perfect affine schemes over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with values in the infinity category of spaces, which we (following recent work of Dustin Clausen and Peter Scholze) call anima to avoid confusion with the notion of algebraic spaces. This setting forces us to change some of arguments of
[
6
]
:

(a)

The tangent space of a perfect scheme is always zero (see
2.8
), so the proof of
[
10
]
for the codimensions of the strata in the GKM stratification fails in our setting. We circumvent this in
Appendix

B
, presenting a different proof that uses an arc space version of the inverse function theorem.

(b)

In
[
6
]
, the construction of the stratification of

is based on
[
6
, Proposition 3.11]
, which in its turn is based on the work
[
4
]
, which only applies to the equal characteristic case. Though in principle it should be possible to carry out the whole argument to the mixed characteristic case, we propose a different proof of the corresponding result
Proposition

7.12
, repeating the argument of
[
5
, Theorem 7.2.5]
.

(c)

In
[
6
]
, a notion of a strongly pro-smooth morphism is introduced. A morphism of schemes

is strongly pro smooth if one can exhibit
X
as a filtered limit of smooth schemes finitely presented over
Y
with smooth affine transition maps. An important ingredient (see
[

, §1.1.6]
) is that there exists a canonical presentation of strongly pro smooth morphisms in this form. However, this argument does not generalize to strongly pro perfectly smooth morphisms. As a result, certain arguments in the dimension theory yield weaker results (see
Proposition

5.18
). Moreover, we provide a different proof (see
Proposition

10.9
) for the uniqueness of the
t
-structure on perfectly placid stacks, since the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 6.3.1]
relies on the canonical smooth presentation.

1.5

Some other differences.

There are other, smaller differences between this work and
[
6
]
that we wish to highlight:

(a)

Perfect schemes are always reduced. Consequently, there is no notion of a reduced perfect
\infty
-stack. This makes some of the definitions and results cleaner in this setting. For example:

i.

The notion of the complement of an open
\infty
-substack

ii.

This isomorphism holds only after reduction in the non-perfect setting.

(b)

In perfect schemes, the notion of universal homeomorphism coincides with that of isomorphism (see

(c)

Working with Witt vectors rather than power series necessitates re-verifying some arguments using Teichmüller representatives of Witt vectors. All of these proofs carry over (see, for example,

).

2.1

Notations.

(a)

Let

(b)

Let

2.2

Perfect schemes.

(a)

A scheme

if its absolute Frobenius morphism
\Phi=\Phi_{p}\colon U\to U
is an isomorphism.

(b)

Let

denote the full subcategory of

\sch_{\perf}\hookrightarrow\sch
admits a right adjoint, sending a scheme

X_{\perf}:=\lim\left(\cdots\overset{\Phi}{\longrightarrow}X\overset{\Phi}{\longrightarrow}X\right),

where the limit is taken in the category of schemes (rather than in
\sch
- the categories of schemes over
\kappa
, where the diagram is not defined).
For an overview of perfect schemes, see
[
3
, §3]
.

2.3
.

Universal homeomorphisms.

(a)

The canonical morphism
X_{\perf}\to X
is a universal homeomorphism.

(b)

A scheme
X
is perfect if and only if every universal homeomorphism
X^{\prime}\to X
with
X^{\prime}
reduced is an isomorphism (see
[
2
, Lemma 14.2.1]

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism of perfect schemes. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

The morphism
f
is an isomorphism.

(b)

The morphism
f
is a universal homeomorphism.

Moreover, if
f
is either universally closed or universally open, then these conditions are also equivalent to:

(a)

For any algebraically closed field
K
, the induced map on
K
-points
f\colon X(K)\to Y(K)
is a bijection.

Proof.

The equivalence (a)
\iff
(b) is
[
3
, Lemma 3.8]
.
The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(c) is immediate.

We prove (c)
\Rightarrow
(b). Suppose
f
is universally open or universally closed. To show that
f
is a universal homeomorphism, it suffices to prove that
f
is universally bijective on the underlying topological space.

Since
f
induces a bijection on
K

K
, it is universally bijective: points of a scheme correspond to equivalence classes of maps from algebraically closed fields (see
[
15
,
Tag 01J9
]
).
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Étale morphisms and universal homeomorphisms
(
[
2
, Prop. 14.1.7]
).
Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a universal homeomorphism of schemes, and let

be an étale morphism. Then the base change
X\times_{Y}Y^{\prime}\to X
is étale. Moreover, the induced morphism of étale sites

f^{*}\colon Y_{\acute{e}t}\to X_{\acute{e}t}

is an equivalence of sites. In particular, the canonical morphism

induces an equivalence of étale topoi:

\Shv(X_{\acute{e}t})\simeq\Shv((X_{\perf})_{\acute{e}t}).

Proposition 2.6
.

Properties preserved by the perfection functor.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism of algebraic spaces. If
f
has one of the following properties, then its perfection
f_{\perf}\colon X_{\perf}\to Y_{\perf}
has the same property:

(a)

Quasi-compact or quasi-separated;

(b)

Affine;

(c)

Étale;

(d)

Flat;

(e)

(Universally) open or closed;

(f)

A closed, open, or locally closed embedding;

(g)

Surjective (in the étale topology).

Proof.

The cases of quasi-compact, quasi-separated, affine, étale, flat, universally closed, and being a closed, open, or locally closed embedding are treated in
[
3
, Lemma 3.4]
.

The statement for universally open follows from the fact that the perfection morphism is a universal homeomorphism and that the perfection functor preserves pullbacks.

The case of surjectivity in the étale topology follows from
[
15
,
Tag 05VM
]
.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism of perfect schemes. We say that

has one of the properties listed in

In other words, we do not prefix such adjectives with “perfectly”; for example, we say that

is flat, not “perfectly flat”, whenever this is true as a morphism of schemes.

2.8
.

Tangent spaces of perfect schemes.

Let
U=\spec R
be a perfect affine scheme. Since
R
is perfect, for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset R
we have that
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}^{p}
and so
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}^{2}
. Thus, for
X\in\sch_{\perf}
, the tangent space
T_{x}X=0
for any

.

2.9
.

Perfect schemes are reduced.

Let
U=\spec R
be a perfect affine scheme, then
R
is reduced as the
p^{n}
power map is an isomorphism for any
n
. Thus, any perfect scheme is reduced.

2.10
.

Perfectly finitely presented.

(a)

Note that

\aff\simeq\pro(\aff^{\fp}),\quad\sch^{\operatorname{qcqs}}\simeq\pro(\sch^{\fp}).

Were
\aff^{\fp},\sch^{\fp}
are the categories of finitely presernted affine schemes and of finitely presented schemes over
\spec\kappa
(equivalently, schemes of finite type).
Equivalently, the co-compact objects are the full subcategories
\aff^{\fp}\subset\aff
and
\sch^{\fp}\subset\sch
of affine schemes and schemes finitely presented over
\spec\kappa
, and they are co-generating.

(b)

A morphism of perfect schemes

is said to be (locally)

if (locally) it is isomorphic to the perfection of a finitely presented morphism
f_{0}\colon X_{0}\to Y
, i.e., if
f\simeq(f_{0})_{\perf}
. We denote by
\aff_{\perf}^{\pfp}
and
\sch_{\perf}^{\pfp}
the full subcategories of
\aff_{\perf}
and
\sch_{\perf}
consisting of objects perfectly finitely presented over
\spec\kappa
.

(c)

Perfectly finitely presented perfect schemes are co-compact objects in
\aff_{\perf}
and
\sch_{\perf}

\aff_{\perf}\simeq\pro(\aff_{\perf}^{\pfp}),\quad\sch_{\perf}^{\operatorname{qcqs}}\simeq\pro(\sch_{\perf}^{\pfp}).

3.1

Preliminaries on
\infty
-stacks.

Let
\catofanima
denote the
\infty
-category of Anima commonly refffered to as
\infty
-groupoids or spaces.
Let
\prestacks:=\Fun(\aff^{\op},\catofanima)
denote the category of
\infty
-prestacks.

(a)

An object
\mathcal{X}\in\prestacks
is called an
\infty

if it is a sheaf for the étale topology (see
[

(b)

An
\infty

if it takes values in

(c)

There is a fully faithful embedding
\sch\hookrightarrow\stacks
given by the restricted Yoneda functor, sending a scheme to its functor of points. We identify the category of schemes with its essential image under this embedding.

Definition 3.2
.

Coverings.

A morphism
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
of
\infty
-stacks is called a

if it is an effective epimorphism in the sense of
[
12
]
. More precisely, by
[
12

, effective epimorphisms in a category of sheaves are characterized as follows: for any morphism
U\to\mathcal{Y}
with
U
affine, there exists an étale cover (that is, an étale surjective map)
U^{\prime}\to U
such that the composition
U^{\prime}\to U\to\mathcal{Y}
factors through
\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Perfect
\infty
-stacks.

We equip
\aff_{\perf}
with the étale topology.
A
perfect
\infty
-stack
is a sheaf on the étale site of perfect affine schemes. We denote by
\stacks_{\perf}:=\Shv(\aff_{\perf})
the category of perfect
\infty
-stacks.

3.4
.

Perfect
\infty
-stacks as
\infty
-stacks.

The inclusion
i\colon\aff_{\perf}\hookrightarrow\aff
is a morphism of sites.
The induced restriction functor

(-)_{\perf}\colon\stacks\to\Shv(\aff_{\perf})

(which we call the

) admits a fully faithful left adjoint
\iota
.

Remark 3.5
.

Limits of perfect
\infty
-stacks.

(a)

The fully faithful embedding
\stacks_{\perf}\hookrightarrow\stacks
preserves all colimits but does not preserve limits. We always work in the category
\stacks_{\perf}
; thus, limits are taken in this category.

(b)

However, the difference is exactly the application of the perfection functor. Since perfection is a right adjoint, it preserves all limits. Thus, for a diagram
F\colon I\to\stacks_{\perf}
, we have

\lim F\simeq(\lim(\iota\circ F))_{\perf}.

Remark 3.6
.

Reduction issues do not arise for perfect
\infty
-stacks.

(a)

Since there is no notion of a non-reduced perfect scheme, there is correspondingly no notion of a non-reduced perfect
\infty
-stack.

(b)

Moreover, in
[
6
]
, a class of
reduced

\infty
-stacks is considered. We observe that, by
2.9
, the functor
\iota
takes values in the subcategory of reduced
\infty
-stacks.

3.7

Perfect algebraic spaces.

Note that if an
\infty
-stack

is representable by an algebraic space, then

is also representable by an algebraic space (by

3.8

Properties of perfect
\infty
-stacks.

(a)

A morphism

of perfect
\infty
-stacks is (locally) pfp-representable if for any morphism

with

a perfect affine scheme,

is a (locally) perfectly finitely presented morphism of perfect algebraic spaces.

(b)

A morphism

of perfect

U\to\mathcal{Y}
with
U
a perfect affine scheme,

is a universally open morphism of perfect algebraic spaces.

(c)

A morphism

of perfect
\infty

U\to\mathcal{Y}
with
U

is a perfectly finitely presented open/closed/locally closed embedding of perfect algebraic spaces.

Properties that are étale-local on the base.

We say a class (P) of morphisms from perfect

-stacks to perfect affine schemes is
étale-local on the base

Example 3.10

The class of isomorphisms is étale-local on the base. Similarly, the class of universally open morphisms is étale-local on the base (indeed, this follows from the non-perfect case).

Locally schematic morphisms.

(a)

The class of schematic morphisms is not étale-local on the base.

5.1

Let

Example 5.4

Proposition 5.17
.

Let

be a perfectly finitely presented, perfectly smooth, surjective morphism of affine schemes admitting perfectly placid presentations. If

is strongly pro-perfectly smooth, then

Proof.

Take a perfectly finitely presented, uo-equidimensional model

such that

is strongly pro perfectly smooth and

(see

Since

is strongly pro perfectly smooth, it is uo-equidimensional. Thus, it is enough to show

is uo-equidimensional. The morphism

is always universally open (see
[

,
Lemma 0383
]
) so it is enough to show it is equidimensional.

We claim that it is enough to show
X_{0}
is equidimensional. Note that

is equidimensional and dimension function compose additively (this follows from the finitely presented case
[
6
, Lemma 2.1.6(b)]
by taking a finitely presented model). Thus, if
X_{0}
is equidimensional, then
Y_{0}
is equidimensional as well.

Let

be a presentation of

as a strongly pro-perfectly smooth morphism. Then
X
has a perfectly placid presentation

with
X_{\alpha}:=X_{0}\times_{Y_{0}}Y_{\alpha}
and another one

, in which each

is perfectly smooth. By the IPP property (see

5.6
), there exists a uo-equidimensional morphism

. Since

is equidimensional (see

X_{\alpha}
. Finally, replacing

with

∎

Proposition 5.18
.

Let
f:Z\to Y
be a perfectly finitely presented morphism of connected perfect affine schemes admitting placid presentations. If

and

are equidimensional, then

is weakly equidimensional of relative dimension

Proof.

By

6.
Perfectly placid stratifications and semi-small morphisms.

In this section, we define a notion of perfectly placid stratification—an infinite stratification whose strata are perfectly placid perfect
\infty
-substacks. We follow
[
6
, §2.4]
closely.

Note that in our setting,
6.1(c)
has a slightly nicer formulation than the corresponding result of
[
6
, §2.4.1(c)]
(compare
Remark

3.6
).

6.1
.

Complementary substacks.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a perfect
\infty
-stack, and let
\mathcal{Y}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
be a (pfp-)locally closed embedding.

(a)

We define
\mathcal{X}\smallsetminus\mathcal{Y}
to be the perfect substack of
\mathcal{X}
such that, for any test object
U
, the anima
(\mathcal{X}\smallsetminus\mathcal{Y})(U)\subset\mathcal{X}(U)
consists of morphisms
U\to\mathcal{X}
such that
U\times_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{Y}=\emptyset
.

(b)

If
\mathcal{Y}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
is a (pfp-)closed embedding, then
\mathcal{X}\smallsetminus\mathcal{Y}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
is a (pfp-)open embedding. Similarly, if
\mathcal{Y}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
is a (pfp-)open embedding, then
\mathcal{X}\smallsetminus\mathcal{Y}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
is a (pfp-)closed embedding. This follows from the case where
\mathcal{X}
is a perfect affine scheme, perfectly finitely presented over
\spec\kappa
.

(c)

We have that
\mathcal{X}\smallsetminus(\mathcal{X}\smallsetminus\mathcal{Y})\simeq\mathcal{Y}
.

6.2
.

Notations and definitions.

(a)

Let
\left\{\mathcal{X_{\alpha}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}\right\}_{\alpha\in I}
be a collection of pfp-locally closed embeddings of perfect
\infty
-stacks such that
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\times_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{X}_{\beta}=\emptyset
for
\alpha\neq\beta
.

(b)

We say that a locally closed embedding
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
is
adapted
if, for each
\alpha\in I
, we have either
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\times_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\emptyset
, or
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
factors through
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
.

(c)

For any locally closed embedding
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
, we let
I_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}\subset I
denote the set of indices
\alpha
for which
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\times_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
.

6.3
.

Constructible stratification.

Let
\left\{\mathcal{X_{\alpha}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}\right\}_{\alpha\in I}
be as in
6.2(a)
.

(a)

We say that
\left\{\mathcal{X_{\alpha}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}\right\}_{\alpha\in I}
is a
finite constructible stratification
if
I
is finite and there exists a sequence of open embeddings

\emptyset=\mathcal{X}_{0}\subset\mathcal{X}_{1}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{X}_{n}=\mathcal{X}

and an ordering
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
of
I
such that each embedding
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha_{i}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha_{i}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{X}_{i}\smallsetminus\mathcal{X}_{i-1}
.

(b)

We say that
\left\{\mathcal{X_{\alpha}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}\right\}_{\alpha\in I}
is a
bounded constructible stratification
if there exists a filtered diagram
(\mathcal{X}_{j})_{j\in J}
of adapted pfp-open embeddings such that
\mathcal{X}\simeq\colim\mathcal{X}_{j}
, and for all
j
, the collection
\left\{\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{j}\right\}_{\alpha\in I_{\mathcal{X}_{j}}}
is a finite constructible stratification.

The following properties of constructible stratifications are straightforward and follow by arguments entirely analogous to those in
[
6
, §2.4]
:

6.4
.

Pullback of constructible stratifications.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a morphism of perfect
\infty
-stacks, and let
\left\{\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}\right\}_{\alpha\in I}
be a (finite/bounded) constructible stratification. Then the pullback

\left\{f^{-1}(\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}\right\}_{\alpha\in I}

is a (finite/bounded) constructible stratification.

6.5
.

Perfectly placidly stratified
\infty
-stacks.

A
perfectly placid stratification
is a bounded constructible stratification
\left\{\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}\right\}_{\alpha\in I}
in which each stratum
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}
is perfectly placid. A
perfectly placidly stratified
\infty
-stack
is an
\infty
-stack
\mathcal{X}
equipped with a perfectly placid stratification
\left\{\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}\right\}_{\alpha\in I}
.

6.6
.

(Semi-)small morphisms.

(a)

Let
f\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a morphism of perfect
\infty
-stacks, and let
\left\{\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}\right\}_{\alpha\in I}
be a perfectly placid stratification. Let
f_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\to\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}
denote the restriction of
f
to the preimage of each stratum.

(b)

Assume that
\mathcal{X}
is perfectly placid, each
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{X}
is of pure codimension
b_{\alpha}
, and each
f_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\to\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}
is locally pfp-representable and equidimensional of constant relative dimension
\delta_{\alpha}
.

(c)

If
\delta_{\alpha}\leq b_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in I
, we say that
f
is
semi-small
.

(d)

Let
\mathcal{U}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be a
\left\{\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}\right\}_{\alpha\in I}
-adapted open substack. We say that a semi-small morphism
f
is
\mathcal{U}
-small
if, for all
\alpha\in I\smallsetminus I_{\mathcal{U}}
, we have
\delta_{\alpha}<b_{\alpha}
.

Proposition 8.3

It suffices to show that the morphism
\pi:X\to Y

Assume that

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
f:T\to S
be a separated étale morphism of perfect schemes, and let

\phi_{T}\in\End T
and
\phi_{S}\in\End S
be endomorphisms such that
f\circ\phi_{T}=\phi_{S}\circ f
.
Then the induced morphism between schemes of fixed points
f^{\phi}:T^{\phi_{T}}\to S^{\phi_{S}}
is étale.

Proof.

(compare
[
10
, 15.4.2(3)]
). Restricting
f
to the

, we may assume that
\phi_{S}
is the identity.
Set
\phi:=\phi_{T}
. Then we claim that the embedding
\iota_{\phi}:T^{\phi}\to T
is clopen (that is, open and closed). Hence
f^{\iota}=f|_{T^{\phi}}
is étale, as claimed.

The diagonal morphism
\Delta_{f}:T\to T\times_{S}T
is an open embedding, because
f
is étale, hence a clopen embedding, since
f
is separated. Taking pullback with respect to
(\Id,\phi):T\to T\times_{S}T
, we conclude that the morphism
T^{\phi}\to T
is a clopen embedding as well.
∎

Proposition 10.5
.

Perfectly smooth and perfectly proper base change.

Let

and

be morphisms in
\algsp_{\perf}^{\pfp}
. Then

and
g^{!}
admit left adjoints

and
g_{!}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

there is a natural base change morphism

If either

Proof.

The first assertion, regarding the existence of left adjoints

and

Suppose first that

is perfectly proper. Then, by

is universally closed, since this property is reflected by perfection. The result then follows from

Now suppose that

is conservative for an étale surjective morphism

by a finite étale cover. Then, by the definition of a perfectly smooth morphism, it suffices to prove the assertion in the cases where

is either étale or the projection

Appendix A
Flatness of the Chevalley morphism

Introduction

Let
G
be a split reductive group over a field
F
of characteristic zero, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. It is a classical result in geometric representation theory (
[
11
]
) that the Chevalley morphism

\chi\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{c}:=\mathfrak{g}/\!/G:=\spec F[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}

is flat with regular singularities.

Given an affine scheme
X
defined over
F
, the
n
-th jet space of
X
, denoted
L_{n}^{\mathrm{eq},+}X
, has
R
-points given by
X(R[[t]]/t^{n})
. A general theorem (see
[
13
, Theorem A.4]
for this particular case) asserts that flatness with rational singularities of
\chi
is equivalent to the flatness of its jet spaces:

L_{n}^{\mathrm{eq},+}\mathfrak{g}\to L_{n}^{\mathrm{eq},+}(\mathfrak{c}).

There exists a positive characteristic version of this result as well.

Theorem A.1
.

[
6
, Theorem 7.4.2.]
.
Assume
F=\kappa
and suppose
\operatorname{char}\kappa>2h
where
h
is the Coxeter number of
\mathfrak{g}
.
The jet spaces of the Chevalley morphisms over a field of characteristic
p
,

L_{n}^{\mathrm{eq},+}(\chi)\colon L_{n}^{\mathrm{eq},+}(\mathfrak{g})\to L_{n}^{\mathrm{eq},+}(\mathfrak{c}),\quad\text{and}\quad L_{n}^{\mathrm{eq},+}(\nu)\colon\operatorname{Lie}(I)^{\mathrm{eq}}_{n}\to L_{n}^{\mathrm{eq},+}(\mathfrak{c})

are flat.

In this appendix, we prove a Witt vector analogue of this result.

Theorem A.2
.

The morphisms

L^{+}_{n}(\chi)\colon L^{+}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})\to L^{+}_{n}(\mathfrak{c})\quad\text{and}\quad L^{+}_{n}(\nu)\colon\operatorname{Lie}(I)_{n}\to L^{+}_{n}(\mathfrak{c})

introduced in
7.6(d)
and
7.7(c)
respectively, are flat.

\widetilde{B^{+}_{n}},\widetilde{B^{+}}

Lemma A.4
.

[
1
, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3]

The quotient ring scheme
\widetilde{B^{+}}/(\xi_{2})
over
(\speciallineargroup_{2})_{\perf}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\speciallineargroup_{2},\perf}
. In addition, for any perfect
\kappa
-algebra
R
, and matrix
g\in\speciallineargroup_{2}(R)
,
\xi_{2}(g)
is not a zero divisor in
\widetilde{B^{+}}(R,g)
.

Proof.

A matrix

g=\begin{pmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{pmatrix}\in\speciallineargroup_{2}(R)

gives rise to the matrix

[g]:=\begin{pmatrix}\left[a\right]&\left[b\right]\\
\left[c\right]&\left[d\right]\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{2}({W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)[[t]]),

whose determinant is
[ad]-[bc]
. Since
[ad]-[bc]
is congruent to
[ad-bc]=1
modulo
\varpi
, the matrix
[g]
is invertible in
{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)[[t]]
. Hence, the ideal
(\xi_{1}(g),\xi_{2}(g))\subset{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)[[t]]
equals
(\varpi,t)
. Therefore, we have a natural isomorphism

\widetilde{B^{+}}(R,g)/\xi_{2}(g)\cong{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)[[t]]/(\varpi,t)\cong R,

which identifies
\widetilde{B^{+}}/\xi_{2}
with
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\speciallineargroup_{2},\perf}
as claimed.

For the second claim,
suppose
x,y\in{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)[[t]]
satisfy

x\xi_{1}(g)+y\xi_{2}(g)=0.

Writing this as a matrix equation

\begin{pmatrix}x&y\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\left[a\right]&\left[b\right]\\
\left[c\right]&\left[d\right]\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\varpi\\
t\end{pmatrix}=0,

we conclude that

\begin{pmatrix}x&y\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\left[a\right]&\left[b\right]\\
\left[c\right]&\left[d\right]\end{pmatrix}=z\begin{pmatrix}t&-\varpi\end{pmatrix}

for some
z\in{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)[[t]]
. Multiplying by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\left[d\right]&-\left[b\right]\\
-\left[c\right]&\left[a\right]\end{pmatrix}

on the right gives

(\left[ad\right]-\left[bc\right])\begin{pmatrix}x&y\end{pmatrix}=z\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{2}(g)&-\xi_{1}(g)\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\left[ad\right]-\left[bc\right]\in{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)[[t]]
is invertible, we conclude that
y
is divisible by
\xi_{1}(g)
, so
\xi_{2}(g)
is not a zero divisor in
\widetilde{B^{+}}(R,g)
.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

For any
g\in\speciallineargroup_{2}(R)
and

Proposition A.10
.

Over the point
[1:0]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\perf}
, the rings
{B^{+}}(R)
and
{B^{+}_{n}}(R)
are isomorphic to
R[[t]]
and
R[[t]]/t^{n}
, respectively. Over any point in the complement, they are isomorphic to the rings
{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)
and
{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)/\varpi^{n}
, respectively.

Proof.

For the first statement, we compute

{B^{+}_{n}}(R,[1:0])=\widetilde{B^{+}_{n}}\left(R,\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)={W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)[[t]]/(\varpi,t^{n})\simeq R[[t]]/t^{n}.

For the second statement, let
[a:b]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\perf}(R)
with
b
invertible, and choose
c,d\in R
such that

g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\speciallineargroup_{2}(R).

Since
b
is invertible, the equality
\xi_{1}(g)=[a]\varpi+[b]t
implies that
t=[b^{-1}]\xi_{1}(g)-[ab^{-1}]\varpi
, so reducing modulo

\xi_{1}(g)
, we have
t\equiv-[ab^{-1}]\varpi
, which implies an isomorphism

{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)[[t]]/(\xi_{1}(g))\simeq{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R).

Moreover, we have

\xi_{2}(g)=[c]\varpi+[d]t\equiv[c]\varpi-[d][ab^{-1}]\varpi=\left([c]-[d][ab^{-1}]\right)\varpi.

By the determinant condition
ad-bc=1
, we find that
[c]-[d][ab^{-1}]\equiv[b^{-1}]\mod\varpi
, so it is invertible. Thus,
\xi_{2}(g)\equiv u\varpi
for some unit
u
, so we have equalities of ideals
(\xi_{2}(g)^{n})=(\varpi^{n})
in
{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)[[t]]/(\xi_{1}(g))\simeq{W_{\mathcal{O}}}(R)
, giving the desired isomorphism.
∎

Proposition A.11
.

As a scheme,
{B^{+}_{n}}
is the perfection of an
n
-dimensional vector bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\perf}
.

Proof.

Over the open perfect subscheme where
[a:-1]
is a coordinate, we may use the section

\begin{pmatrix}a&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}

for the map
\speciallineargroup_{2}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In this chart, we have
\xi_{2}\equiv\varpi\mod\xi_{1}
up to an invertible factor, so we obtain an isomorphism

\mathbb{A}^{n}\to{B^{+}_{n}},\qquad(a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1})\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}[a_{i}]\varpi^{i}.

Over the open perfect subscheme where
[1:b]
is a coordinate, we use the section

\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

In this chart,
\xi_{2}\equiv t\mod\xi_{1}
up to an invertible factor, so we similarly get an isomorphism

\mathbb{A}^{n}\to{B^{+}_{n}},\qquad(a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1})\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}[a_{i}]t^{i}.

On the overlap (where both
a
and
b
are invertible), we have the relation
\varpi=[-b/a]t
, which gives the transition function. Thus, the gluing is described by the linear transformation

(a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1})\mapsto(a_{0},(-b/a)a_{1},\dots,(-b/a)^{n-1}a_{n-1}),

which is
R
-linear in the coordinates
a_{i}
.
∎

Appendix B
Codimension of locally closed perfect subschemes of lattices and valuations of differentials

B.1
.

Let
\Omega,\Xi
be finite free
\mathcal{O}
-modules of the same rank
n
. We view
\Omega,\Xi
as affine spaces over
\spec\mathcal{O}
, and remember their structure as group schemes.

B.2
.

Valuation of the differential

(a)

Let
f\colon\Omega\to\Xi
be a morphism of schemes over
\spec\mathcal{O}
.

(b)

Given coordinates (that is, isomorphisms of group schemes)
\Omega\simeq\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}}
,
\Xi\simeq\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}}
, we get for every
x\in\Omega(\mathcal{O})
a Jacobian matrix
\differential_{x}(f)\in M_{n}(\mathcal{O})
is given by the partial derivatives of
f
at
x
according to the coordinates.

(c)

We view
\differential_{x}(f)
as a linear morphism
\differential_{x}(f)\colon\Omega\to\Xi
.

(d)

The matrix
\differential_{x}(f)
clearly depends on the coordinates, but the valuation of its determinant
d(f;x):=v_{\varpi}(\det(\differential_{x}(f)))
according to
\varpi
depends only on
x
and
f
.

The goal of this appendix is to show the following theorem.

Theorem B.3
.

Let
\Omega,\Xi
be finite free
\mathcal{O}
-modules of the same rank
n
. Let
f\colon\Omega\to\Xi
be a morphism of schemes over
\spec\mathcal{O}
. Let
X\subset L^{+}(\Omega)
and
Y\subset L^{+}(\Xi)
be pfp-locally closed subschemes of pure codimensions
c_{X}
and
c_{Y}
, respectively, such that
X=L^{+}(f)^{-1}(Y)
.

Suppose
L^{+}(f)|_{X}\colon X\to Y
is finite étale, and that there exists
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
d(f;x)=e
for all
x\in X
. Then
c_{Y}=c_{X}+e
.

B.4
.

Linear case.

(a)

Suppose
f\colon\Omega\to\Xi
is an
\mathcal{O}
-linear morphism.

(b)

We choose coordinates as in
B.2
such that
f
is represented by a matrix
D
.

Lemma B.5
.

Let
f
be as in
B.4
. Then it induces a perfectly finitely presented closed embedding
L^{+}(f)\colon L^{+}(\Omega)\to L^{+}(\Xi)
of pure codimension
e=v_{\varpi}(\det f)
.

Proof.

We can assume that
\Omega=\Xi=\mathcal{O}^{n}
. By the theorem on elementrary divisors,
f
decomposes as
f=x\circ g\circ y
such that
x
and
y
are linear isomorphisms and
g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(\varpi^{m_{1}}x_{1},....,\varpi^{m_{n}}x_{n})
.

It suffices to show the assertion in the case
f=g
. In this case the assertion is clear.
∎

B.6
.

More notations.

(a)

The image
L^{+}(f)(L^{+}(\Omega))
is a perfectly finitely presented closed subscheme of
L^{+}(\Xi)
of codimension
e
(as shown in the lemma).

(b)

Let
x\in L^{+}(\Omega)(\kappa)
be a rational point. Let
H\subset L^{+}(\Omega)
be a subscheme; we denote by
x+H
the translation (shift) of
H
by
x
.

(c)

For a scalar
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}
, we denote by
\lambda\cdot-
the corresponding linear endomorphism (multiplication by
\lambda
).

(d)

We will be interested in subschemes such as
y+L^{+}(f)(\varpi^{M}\cdot L^{+}(\Omega))\subset L^{+}(\Xi)
, which is a pfp-closed subscheme of pure codimension
Mn+e
by
Lemma

B.5
.

The general case.

The following can be viewed as an arcspace version of the inverse function theorem.

Theorem B.7
.

Let
f\colon\Omega\to\Xi
be a morphism of schemes over
\spec\mathcal{O}
. Let
x\in\Omega(\mathcal{O})
, and write
e=d(f;x)
(see
B.2
for notation). Then for every
M>e
morphism
f
restricts to an isomorphism between the closed subschemes
x+\varpi^{M}L^{+}(\Omega)\to f(x)+L^{+}(D_{x}(f))(\varpi^{M}L^{+}(\Omega))
(see
B.6
).

Proof.

We can assume that
\Omega=\Xi=\mathcal{O}^{n}
and
x=f(x)=0
.

Using the elementary divisors theorem and arguing as in the proof of
Lemma

B.5
, we can assume that
f(x_{1},.....,x_{n})=(\pi^{m_{1}}x_{1}+f^{\prime}_{1},.....,\pi^{m_{n}}x_{1}+f^{\prime}_{n})
, where
f^{\prime}_{1},.....,f^{\prime}_{n}
are sums of monomials of degree
\geq 2
. Observe that
m:=\max(m_{1},..,m_{n})\leq e
.

We perform a change of variables
\varpi^{m}x^{\prime}_{i}=x_{i}
. We obtain that

f(\varpi^{m}x^{\prime}_{1},....,\varpi^{m}x^{\prime}_{n})=(\pi^{m_{1}+m}f^{\prime\prime}_{1},....\pi^{m_{n}+m}f^{\prime\prime}_{n}),

where
f^{\prime\prime}:=(f^{\prime\prime}_{1},....,f^{\prime\prime}_{n})
is étale at
0
. Thus, we reduce to the case when
f
is étale at
0
.

Let
U
be the open neighborhood of
0
such that
f|_{U}
is étale, and let
U^{\prime}=f(U)
. Now the assertion follows from the Cartesian diagram (see
Proposition

7.4
):

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L^{+}(U)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L^{+}(U^{\prime})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L^{+}_{0}(U)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L^{+}_{0}(U^{\prime})},}

which induces an isomorphism on the fibres of the vertical maps.
∎

Lemma B.8
.

Let
X\subset L^{+}(\Omega)
is a pfp-locally closed perfect subscheme. Then for every
x\in X
there exists
M
such that
x+\varpi^{M}L^{+}(\Omega)\subset X
.

Proof.

Since
X\subset L^{+}(\Omega)
is pfp-locally closed, there exists
M
and a locally closed perfect subscheme
X_{M}\subset L^{+}_{M}(\Omega)
such that
X\simeq X_{M}\times_{L^{+}_{M}(\Omega)}L^{+}(\Omega)
. Let
x_{M}\in X_{M}
be the image of
x
. Then the assertion follows from the Cartesian diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{x+\varpi^{M}L^{+}(\Omega)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L^{+}(\Omega)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\left\{x_{M}\right\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X_{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L^{+}_{M}(\Omega).}}

∎

Proof of
Theorem

B.3
.

By
Lemma

B.8
, there exists
M\gg 0
such that
y+D_{x}(f)(\varpi^{M}L^{+}(\Omega))\subset Y
,
x+\varpi^{M}L^{+}(\Omega)\subset X
, and by
Theorem

B.7

f
restricts to an isomorphism

x+\varpi^{M}L^{+}(\Omega)\xrightarrow{\sim}f(x)+L^{+}(D_{x}(f))(\varpi^{M}L^{+}(\Omega)).

Overall, we get a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{+}(\Omega)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{+}(\Xi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x+\varpi^{M}L^{+}(\Omega)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f(x)+L^{+}(D_{x}f)(\varpi^{M}L^{+}(\Omega)).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f|_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{Y}}

In the lower square, the two vertical morphisms are pfp-closed embeddings with sources that are connected and strongly pro perfectly smooth. Thus, by

Applying

Subtracting these equations yields

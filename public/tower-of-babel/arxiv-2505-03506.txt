Abstract
In this paper, we present a new complexity result for the gradient descent method with an appropriately fixed stepsize for minimizing a strongly convex function with locally α \alpha -Hölder continuous gradients ( 0 < α ≤ 1 0<\alpha\leq 1 ). The complexity bound for finding an approximate minimizer with a distance to the true minimizer less than ε \varepsilon is O ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( ε − 1 ) ​ ε 2 ​ α − 2 ) O(\log(\varepsilon^{-1})\varepsilon^{2\alpha-2}) , which extends the well-known complexity result for α = 1 \alpha=1 .

Introduction

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\mu
-strongly convex and continuously differentiable function with a parameter
\mu>0
, that is,

f(\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{v})\geq\nabla f(\mathbf{v})^{\top}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|^{2},

for all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Here,
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|
is the
\ell_{2}
norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In this paper, we focus on establishing a new complexity result for the classic gradient descent method

\mathbf{u}_{k+1}=\mathbf{u}_{k}-\tau\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k}),

(1)

where
\tau>0
is a fixed stepsize, for solving the following unconstrained optimization problem

\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\mathbf{u}).

(2)

Throughout this paper, we assume that the gradient
\nabla f
of
f
satisfies the locally
\alpha
-Hölder continuous condition.

Definition 1

We say
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
locally
\alpha
-Hölder continuous
with
\alpha\in(0,1]
if there exist
\beta>0
and
\delta>0
such that

\left\|g(\mathbf{u})-g(\mathbf{v})\right\|\leq\beta\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|^{\alpha},

(3)

for all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|\leq\delta
.
We denote this class of functions by
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\beta,\delta)
.
We say
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous
if condition (
3
) holds for all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Suppose that the gradient
\nabla f
is globally Lipschitz continuous, which corresponds to condition (
3
) with
\alpha=1
for all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then it is well known that the gradient descent method (
1
) with any initial point
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and stepsize
\tau=2/(\mu\beta)
achieves a linear rate of convergence
(
Nesterov2018lectures
, Theorem 2.1.15)
as follows,

\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\left(\dfrac{\beta-\mu}{\beta+\mu}\right)^{k}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|,

where
\mathbf{u}^{\ast}
is the minimizer of problem (
2
).
Therefore, for a given
\varepsilon>0
, the gradient descent method (
1
) with stepsize
\tau=2/(\mu\beta)
is guaranteed to find a point
\mathbf{u}_{k}
satisfying
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\varepsilon
after at most
O(\log(\varepsilon^{-1}))
iterations.
This analysis fails in the non-Lipschitz case.
As our theoretical results unveil, the gradient descent method (
1
) with a fixed stepsize may stagnate before converging to the minimizer.

The minimization of convex functions with
\alpha
-Hölder continuous gradients is first studied by Devolder et al.
Devolder2014first
in the form of inexact oracles.
Following this work, Lan
Lan2015bundle
develops an accelerated prox-level method for solving the class of composite problems.
Moreover, Nesterov
Nesterov2015universal
proposes universal gradient methods for minimizing composite functions which have
\alpha
-Hölder continuous gradients of its smooth part.
However, to the best of our knowledge, the complexity bound of gradient descent method (
1
) for a function
f
satisfying the locally
\alpha
-Hölder continuous condition with
0<\alpha<1
has not been developed.

The contribution of this paper is to provide a new complexity result for the gradient descent method (
1
) with a fixed stepsize
\tau
to solve strongly convex optimization problems on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
when the gradient is locally
\alpha
-Hölder continuous with
0<\alpha<1
, but not Lipschitz continuous. We show that method (
1
) with an appropriately chosen
\tau
can find a point
\mathbf{u}_{k}
satisfying
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\varepsilon
after at most
O(\log(\varepsilon^{-1})\varepsilon^{2\alpha-2})
iterations.

We end this section by illustrating Definition
1
with a simple example.

Example 1

Consider the following univariate function,

f(u)=\frac{\lambda}{2}u^{2}+\frac{2}{3}u_{+}^{3/2},

where
\lambda>0
,
u\in\mathbb{R}
, and
u_{+}=\max\{u,0\}
.
Then we have

f^{\prime}(u)=\lambda u+\sqrt{u_{+}}.

It can be readily verified that
f^{\prime}
cannot be expected to satisfy the condition (
3
) globally for all
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
.
However, if
\left\|u-v\right\|\leq\delta=1
, it holds that

\left\|u-v\right\|\leq\left\|u-v\right\|^{1/2}.

Moreover, for any
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\left\|\sqrt{u_{+}}-\sqrt{v_{+}}\right\|\leq\left\|u_{+}-v_{+}\right\|^{1/2}\leq\left\|u-v\right\|^{1/2}.

Therefore,
f^{\prime}(u)
satisfies the Hölder continuous condition (
3
) with
\beta=1+\lambda
and
\alpha=1/2
for
\left\|u-v\right\|\leq\delta=1
.

2
Convergence of Gradient Flow Equations

We interpret the gradient descent iteration (
1
) as a forward Euler discretization of the following gradient flow equation,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{u}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}),\\
&\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(4)

with time step
\tau>0
.

The purpose of this section is to establish the existence and convergence of the unique solution to the initial value problem (
4
).
We state these results as Lemma
1
below.

Lemma 1

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\mu
-strongly convex and continuously differentiable.
Let
\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the unique minimizer of
f
.
Then the initial value problem (
4
) has a unique solution
\mathbf{u}(t)
defined for all
t\in[0,\infty)
.
Moreover, the trajectory
\{\mathbf{u}(t)\}_{t>0}
is bounded and

\left\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq e^{-\mu t}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|.

Proof

The results in
(
Teschl2012ordinary
, Theorem 2.19)
indicate that a solution
\mathbf{u}(t)
to problem (
4
) exists.
To prove the uniqueness, we assume that both
\mathbf{u}(t)
and
\mathbf{v}(t)
are solutions of problem (
4
).
Then it follows from the strong convexity of
f
that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{v}(t)\right\|^{2}=-2\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t))-\nabla f(\mathbf{v}(t))\right)^{\top}\left(\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{v}(t)\right)\leq 0,

which indicates that
\phi(t)=\left\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{v}(t)\right\|^{2}
is decreasing in
t
.
Since
\phi(0)=0
, we know that
\phi(t)=0
for all
t\geq 0
.
Hence,
\mathbf{u}(t)=\mathbf{v}(t)
.

Similarly, according to the strong convexity of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}={}

\displaystyle-2\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t))^{\top}\left(\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-2\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t))-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}^{\ast})\right)^{\top}\left(\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle-2\mu\left\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}.

The Grönwall inequality
Gronwall1919note
then implies that

\left\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}\leq e^{-2\mu t}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2},

as desired.
∎

3
Discretization Error

This section delves into the error analysis for the forward Euler discretization of the gradient flow equation (
4
).
We first formalize the assumptions on the function
f
throughout the analysis.

Assumption 1

The following statements hold.

1.

f
is
\mu
-strongly convex with
\mu>0
and continuously differentiable.

2.

\nabla f\in\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\beta,\delta)
for some
\beta,\delta>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

According to Lemma
1
, we know that the initial value problem (
4
) has a unique solution
\mathbf{u}(t)
.
Lemma
2
summarizes some useful properties of
\mathbf{u}(t)
.

Lemma 2

Let Assumption
1
hold.

1.

There exists a constant
M>0
such that
\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t))\right\|\leq M
for all
t\geq 0
.

2.

\mathbf{u}
is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant
M
.

3.

There exists a constant
\bar{\beta}=\beta M^{\alpha}
such that, for all
\tau\in(0,\delta/M)
, we have

\left\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\tau\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t))-\mathbf{u}(t+\tau)\right\|\leq\bar{\beta}\tau^{1+\alpha}.

Proof

1. Since
\mathbf{u}(t)
is finite for all
t\geq 0
and
\mathbf{u}(t)\to\mathbf{u}^{\ast}
as
t\to\infty
, the trajectory
\{\mathbf{u}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is bounded.
Then the first assertion directly follows from the continuity of
\nabla f
.

2. For all
t,w\geq 0
, it holds that

\left\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}(w)\right\|=\left\|\int_{w}^{t}\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(s))\mathrm{d}s\right\|\leq\left\lvert\int_{w}^{t}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|\mathrm{d}s\right\rvert\leq M\left\lvert t-w\right\rvert,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t))\right\|\leq M
for all
t\geq 0
.
Thus, the second assertion holds.

3. Now we assume that
\tau\in(0,\delta/M)
.
Then

\left\|\mathbf{u}(t+\tau)-\mathbf{u}(t)\right\|\leq M\tau<\delta,

which together with the local
\alpha
-Hölder continuity of
\nabla f
implies that

\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t+\tau))-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t))\right\|\leq\beta\left\|\mathbf{u}(t+\tau)-\mathbf{u}(t)\right\|^{\alpha}\leq\beta M^{\alpha}\tau^{\alpha}.

According to the differential equation in (
4
), it follows that

\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}(t+\tau)=\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(s))\mathrm{d}s,

and hence,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\tau\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t))-\mathbf{u}(t+\tau)\right\|={}

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(s))-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t))\right)\mathrm{d}s\right\|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(s))-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t))\right\|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\beta M^{\alpha}\tau^{1+\alpha}.

Hence, the third assertion holds.
We complete the proof.
∎

We are in the position to establish the discretization error of the initial value problem (
4
).

Theorem 3.1

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds.
Let
\{\mathbf{u}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}
be the forward Euler discretization to the solution
\mathbf{u}(t)
of the initial value problem (
4
) on
[0,T]
with
K
time steps and
\tau=T/K
.
We further assume that

K\geq\max\left\{T,\,4\mu T,\dfrac{MT}{\delta},\,\dfrac{C_{E}^{1/\alpha}T}{\delta^{1/\alpha}},\,\dfrac{(2\beta^{2})^{\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)^{2}+\alpha^{2}}}T^{1+\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)^{2}+\alpha^{2}}}}{C_{E}^{\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{(1-\alpha)^{2}+\alpha^{2}}}}\right\},

(5)

where
C_{E}=\beta M^{\alpha}/\mu>0
.
Then, for any
0\leq l\leq K
,

E_{l}\equiv\left\|\mathbf{u}_{l}-\mathbf{u}(\tau l)\right\|\leq C_{E}\tau^{\alpha}.

(6)

Proof

We seek to prove this theorem by induction.
The inequality (
6
) holds for
l=0
since
\mathbf{u}_{0}=\mathbf{u}(0)
.
Now we assume that (
6
) holds for all
0\leq l\leq k
and investigate the situation for
l=k+1
.

For convenience, we define

\mathbf{y}(t)=\mathbf{u}(t)-\tau\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t)).

Let
t_{k}=\tau k
and
\mathbf{y}_{k}=\mathbf{y}(t_{k})
.
Then a straightforward verification reveals that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{y}_{k}-\mathbf{u}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}={}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{u}(t_{k})-\mathbf{u}_{k}-\tau\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t_{k}))-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\right)\right\|^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle E_{k}^{2}-2\tau\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t_{k}))-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\right)^{\top}\left(\mathbf{u}(t_{k})-\mathbf{u}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t_{k}))-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\right\|^{2}.

Moreover, the strong convexity of
f
yields that

\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t_{k}))-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\right)^{\top}\left(\mathbf{u}(t_{k})-\mathbf{u}_{k}\right)\geq\mu\left\|\mathbf{u}(t_{k})-\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\|^{2}=\mu E_{k}^{2}.

(8)

Since
\left\|\mathbf{u}(t_{k})-\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\|=E_{k}\leq C_{E}\tau^{\alpha}\leq\delta
, it follows from the local
\alpha
-Hölder continuity of
\nabla f
that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{u}(t_{k}))-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\right\|^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle\beta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{u}(t_{k})-\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\|^{2\alpha}

(9)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\beta^{2}E_{k}^{2\alpha}\leq\beta^{2}C_{E}^{2\alpha}\tau^{2\alpha^{2}}.

Combining the three relationships (
7
), (
8
), and (
9
) together, we can obtain that

\left\|\mathbf{y}_{k}-\mathbf{u}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}\leq\left(1-2\mu\tau\right)E_{k}^{2}+\beta^{2}C_{E}^{2\alpha}\tau^{2+2\alpha^{2}}.

(10)

Since
\tau\leq\delta/M
, it can be deduced from Lemma
2
that

\left\|\mathbf{y}_{k}-\mathbf{u}(t_{k+1})\right\|\leq\bar{\beta}\tau^{1+\alpha}.

Then by virtue of the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k+1}-\mathbf{y}_{k}\right\|^{2}\geq{}

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k+1}-\mathbf{u}(t_{k+1})\right\|-\left\|\mathbf{u}(t_{k+1})-\mathbf{y}_{k}\right\|\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle E_{k+1}^{2}-2E_{k+1}\left\|\mathbf{u}(t_{k+1})-\mathbf{y}_{k}\right\|+\left\|\mathbf{u}(t_{k+1})-\mathbf{y}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle E_{k+1}^{2}-2E_{k+1}\left\|\mathbf{u}(t_{k+1})-\mathbf{y}_{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle E_{k+1}^{2}-2\bar{\beta}\tau^{1+\alpha}E_{k+1}.

Now we assume for contradiction that
E_{k+1}>C_{E}\tau^{\alpha}
.
Then it can be readily verified that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k+1}-\mathbf{y}_{k}\right\|^{2}\geq\left(1-\dfrac{2\bar{\beta}\tau}{C_{E}}\right)E_{k+1}^{2}=\left(1-2\mu\tau\right)E_{k+1}^{2}.

(11)

In light of the relationship
\tau\leq 1/(4\mu)
, it can be obtained by combining (
10
) and (
11
) together that

E_{k+1}^{2}\leq E_{k}^{2}+\dfrac{\beta^{2}C_{E}^{2\alpha}\tau^{2+2\alpha^{2}}}{1-2\mu\tau}\leq E_{k}^{2}+2\beta^{2}C_{E}^{2\alpha}\tau^{2+2\alpha^{2}}.

Since
E_{0}=0
, we can proceed to show that

\displaystyle E_{k+1}^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle 2(k+1)\beta^{2}C_{E}^{2\alpha}\tau^{2+2\alpha^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle 2T\beta^{2}C_{E}^{2\alpha}\tau^{1+2\alpha^{2}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2T\beta^{2}C_{E}^{2(\alpha-1)}\tau^{(1-\alpha)^{2}+\alpha^{2}}\times C_{E}^{2}\tau^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C_{E}^{2}\tau^{2\alpha},

where the last inequality follows from
\tau=T/K
and condition (
5
).
This results in a contradiction to
E_{k+1}>C_{E}\tau^{\alpha}
.
The proof is completed.
∎

4
A Basic Complexity Result

In this section, we apply Theorem
3.1
to obtain a complexity result of the gradient descent method (
1
) without any additional conditions.
We begin by defining the time required to get close to the minimizer of
f
.

Definition 2

Let
\mathbf{u}(t)
be the unique solution of problem (
4
) and let
\mathbf{u}^{\ast}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{u}(t)
.
For
\eta>0
, we define

T^{\ast}(\eta)=\min\left\{T\,\middle|\,\left\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\eta\mbox{ for all }t\geq T\right\}.

According to Lemma
1
, we know that

\left\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq e^{-\mu t}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|.

Solving
e^{-\mu t}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\eta
gives rise to that

T^{\ast}(\eta)\leq\frac{1}{\mu}\log\left(\frac{\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|}{\eta}\right),

which provides an upper bound of
T^{\ast}(\eta)
.

Next, we provide an estimate for the total number of time steps required by the forward Euler discretization to get close to the minimizer of
f
.

Theorem 4.1

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds and
\eta>0
is a sufficiently small constant.
Let
\{\mathbf{u}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}
be the forward Euler discretization to the solution
\mathbf{u}(t)
of the initial value problem (
4
) on
[0,T^{\ast}(\eta)]
with
K
time steps and
\tau=T^{\ast}(\eta)/K
.
We further assume that
K
satisfies condition (
5
) and

K\geq K_{\mathbf{u}}(\eta)\equiv\mu^{-1/\alpha}\beta^{1/\alpha}MT^{\ast}(\eta)\eta^{-1/\alpha}.

Then

\left\|\mathbf{u}_{K}-\mathbf{u}(T^{\ast}(\eta))\right\|\leq\eta.

Proof

As a direct consequence of Theorem
3.1
, we can proceed to show that

\left\|\mathbf{u}_{K}-\mathbf{u}(T^{\ast}(\eta))\right\|\leq C_{E}\tau^{\alpha}\leq\dfrac{\bar{\beta}}{\mu}\left(\frac{T^{\ast}(\eta)}{K}\right)^{\alpha}\leq\eta,

where the last inequality is satisfied with
\bar{\beta}=\beta M^{\alpha}
when
K\geq K_{\mathbf{u}}(\eta)
.
The proof is completed.
∎

It is noteworthy that the sequence
\{\mathbf{u}_{k}\}
generated by the gradient descent method (
1
) with the initial point
\mathbf{u}_{0}
is exactly the forward Euler discretization to the solution
\mathbf{u}(t)
of the initial value problem (
4
).
Therefore, according to Theorem
4.1
, we can derive a complexity result of the gradient descent method (
1
) with a fixed stepsize, which is demonstrated in the following corollary.

Corollary 1

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds and
\varepsilon>0
is a sufficiently small constant.
Let
\{\mathbf{u}_{k}\}
be the sequence generated by the gradient descent method (
1
) with the stepsize
\tau
chosen as

\tau=2^{-1/\alpha}\mu^{1/\alpha}\beta^{-1/\alpha}M^{-1}\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}.

Then after at most

\left\lceil 2^{1/\alpha}\mu^{-1-1/\alpha}\beta^{1/\alpha}M\log\left(\dfrac{2\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|}{\varepsilon}\right)\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}\right\rceil

iterations, we will reach an iterate
\mathbf{u}_{k}
satisfying
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof

According to Theorem
4.1
, we choose the stepsize
\tau=T^{\ast}(\varepsilon/2)/K_{\mathbf{u}}(\varepsilon/2)
for a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.
Then it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{u}_{K_{\mathbf{u}}(\varepsilon/2)}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq{}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{u}_{K_{\mathbf{u}}(\varepsilon/2)}-\mathbf{u}(T^{\ast}(\varepsilon/2))\right\|+\left\|\mathbf{u}(T^{\ast}(\varepsilon/2))-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}+\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon,

which completes the proof.
∎

5
A Refined Complexity Result

In this section, we present a more refined complexity result for the gradient descent method (
1
).
To this end, we assume that the initial point
\mathbf{u}_{0}
satisfies the condition
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\delta
.
It is noteworthy that this condition is not particularly stringent.
As shown by the complexity results derived in Section
4
, an initial point satisfying this requirement can always be obtained within a finite number of iterations by the gradient descent method (
1
).

Theorem 5.1

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds and
\varepsilon>0
is a sufficiently small constant.
Let
\{\mathbf{u}_{k}\}
be the sequence generated by the gradient descent method (
1
) with
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\delta
and

\tau=\mu\beta^{-2}\varepsilon^{2-2\alpha}.

Then after at most

\left\lceil\dfrac{2\beta^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\log\left(\dfrac{\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|}{\varepsilon}\right)\varepsilon^{2\alpha-2}\right\rceil

iterations, we will reach an iterate
\mathbf{u}_{k}
satisfying
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof

Let
K^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}
be the smallest iteration number
k
such that
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\varepsilon
holds.
We first prove that
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\delta
for all
k<K^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}
by induction.
It is obvious that this assertion holds for
k=0
by our initialization.
Now we assume that
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\delta
for some
k<K^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}
.

By virtue of the strong convexity of
f
, it holds that

\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k}),\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k})-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}^{\ast}),\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\rangle\geq\mu\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}.

Since we have
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\delta
, the local
\alpha
-Hölder continuity of
\nabla f
results in that

\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\right\|^{2}=\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k})-\nabla f(\mathbf{u}^{\ast})\right\|^{2}\leq\beta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2\alpha}.

Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k+1}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}={}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\tau\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k})-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}-2\tau\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k}),\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\rangle+\tau^{2}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}-2\mu\tau\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+\beta^{2}\tau^{2}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left(1-2\mu\tau+\beta^{2}\tau^{2}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2\alpha-2}\right)\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}.

According to the definition of
K^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}
, we have
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|>\varepsilon
for any
k<K^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}
.
Then a straightforward verification reveals that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k+1}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle\left(1-2\mu\tau+\beta^{2}\tau^{2}\varepsilon^{2\alpha-2}\right)\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}

(12)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left(1-\mu^{2}\beta^{-2}\varepsilon^{2-2\alpha}\right)\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2},

which further implies that

\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k+1}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\delta.

Consequently, we can conclude that
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|\leq\delta
for all
k<K^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}
.

As a direct consequence of (
12
), we can proceed to show that

\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}\leq\left(1-\mu^{2}\beta^{-2}\varepsilon^{2-2\alpha}\right)^{k}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2},

for all
k<K^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}
.
Solving
\left(1-\mu^{2}\beta^{-2}\varepsilon^{2-2\alpha}\right)^{k}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2}
gives rise to that

K^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}\leq\dfrac{2\log\left(\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|/\varepsilon\right)}{-\log\left(1-\mu^{2}\beta^{-2}\varepsilon^{2-2\alpha}\right)}\leq\dfrac{2\beta^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\log\left(\dfrac{\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}^{\ast}\right\|}{\varepsilon}\right)\varepsilon^{2\alpha-2}.

The proof is completed.
∎

Theorem
5.1
demonstrates that the iteration complexity of the gradient descent method (
1
) is
O(\log(\varepsilon^{-1})\varepsilon^{2\alpha-2})
with a fixed stepsize for
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
This complexity result generalizes the classical linear convergence when
\alpha=1
and highlights the performance degradation incurred by non-Lipschitz gradients.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have established a new complexity result for the gradient descent method with a fixed stepsize applied to strongly convex optimization problems where the objective function possesses a locally
\alpha
-Hölder continuous gradient with
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
Our analysis interprets the gradient descent method as an explicit Euler discretization of a gradient flow, and provides rigorous estimates on the discretization error.
Based on these results, we have derived both a basic complexity result without any additional assumptions and a refined version under a mild condition on the initial point.
The main conclusion is that, to achieve an approximate minimizer with a distance to the minimizer less than
\varepsilon
, the total number of iterations required by the gradient descent method (
1
) is
O(\log(\varepsilon^{-1})\varepsilon^{2\alpha-2})
at the most.
This recovers the classical complexity result when
\alpha=1
and reveals the additional difficulty imposed by the weaker smoothness of the objective function for
\alpha<1
.
Numerical experiments are conducted to validate our theoretical findings, demonstrating the expected behavior of gradient descent under different stepsizes and Hölder exponents.
These results offer new insights into the performance guarantees of the classic gradient descent method for a broader class of optimization problems with non-Lipschitz gradients.

KKT Reformulations for Single Leader and Multi-Follower Games
Parin Chaipunya, Thirumulanathan D, Joydeep Dutta

Abstract
We consider a bilevel optimization problem having a single leader and multiple followers. The followers choose their strategies simultaneously, and are assumed to converge to a Nash equilibrium strategy profile. We begin by providing a practical example of such a problem in an oligopoly setting. We then show the existence of a Nash equilibrium when the objective function of each follower is convex in its optimizing variable, and the feasible set is compact, convex, and nonempty. We then consider the KKT reformulation of the single leader multi-follower game (henceforth, SLMFG), and show using examples that the solutions of both the problems need not be the same, even when each of the followers’ problem is convex. In particular, we show that the global minima of both the problems may differ if the follower’s problem does not satisfy the Slater’s condition. We then show that the local minima of the SLMFG and its KKT reformulation are the same if, in addition to convexity and Slater’s constraints, the local minimum point remains a local minimum for every Lagrange multiplier in each of the followers’ problem. Given that this condition is hard to verify in practice, we provide another condition for the local minima of the two problems to be the same using constant rank constraint qualification (CRCQ). We again show using examples that the local optima of the two problems may differ if the conditions are not satisfied. Keywords: Bilevel program; Single leader multi-follower game; Constriant qualification; Local solution

In case of a single-follower (

]

Similar results were studied by

The aim of this paper is to present, in the same spirits as

In Section

, we present sufficient conditions under which the local solution set of the SLMFG (

2
KKT Reformulation in Standard Optimistic SLMFG

This section is dedicated to the conditions under which the solutions of the original SLMFG (
1.2
) and the corresponding MPCC reformulation (
1.3
) are included in one another.
Each sufficient condition is supplied with an example to emphasize that the assumed properties are required and could not be dropped without losing the desired conclusion.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{F}})
be a local solution of the SLMFG (
1.2
) and let the follower’s problem (
1.1
) for every follower
\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}
be such that the maps
u^{\mathtt{f}}\mapsto J^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}},u^{-\mathtt{f}})
and
u^{\mathtt{f}}\mapsto g^{\mathtt{f}}_{j}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}})
are convex with Slater’s CQ satisfied at
u^{\mathtt{l}}=\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}}
.
Then there exist
\overline{\lambda}^{\mathtt{F}}=(\lambda^{\mathtt{f}}_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}\\
j=1,\dots,p^{\mathtt{f}}\end{subarray}}\geq 0
such that
(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{F}},\overline{\lambda}^{\mathtt{F}})
is a local solution of the MPCC problem (
1.3
).

Proof.

Observe that
\overline{u}^{\mathtt{F}}\in\mathsf{NEP}(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}})
implies

\overline{u}^{\mathtt{f}}\in\argmin_{u^{\mathtt{f}}\in Y^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})}J^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}},u^{-\mathtt{f}}).

This, in combination with the convexity of
J^{\mathtt{f}}
and
g^{\mathtt{f}}
in
u^{\mathtt{f}}
and the Slater’s CQ at
\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}}
, yields inf and only if the KKT conditions hold for the follower
\mathtt{f}
problem at
(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{F}})
.
Hence
u^{\mathtt{F}}\in\mathsf{NEP}(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}})
if and only if there exists
\overline{\lambda}^{\mathtt{F}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p^{\mathtt{F}}}
such that

\displaystyle\nabla_{u^{\mathtt{f}}}J^{\mathtt{f}}(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{f}},\overline{u}^{-\mathtt{f}})+\sum_{j=1}^{p^{\mathtt{f}}}\overline{\lambda}^{\mathtt{f}}_{j}\nabla_{u^{\mathtt{f}}}g^{\mathtt{f}}_{j}(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{f}})=0\quad

\displaystyle(\forall\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F})

\displaystyle g^{\mathtt{f}}(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{f}})\leq 0,\quad\overline{\lambda}^{\mathtt{f}}\geq 0,\quad\langle\overline{\lambda}^{\mathtt{f}},g^{\mathtt{f}}(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{f}})\rangle=0\quad

\displaystyle(\forall\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}).

This means that
\overline{u}^{\mathtt{F}}\in\mathsf{NEP}(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}})
if and only if
(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{F}},\overline{\lambda}^{\mathtt{F}})
is a feasible point of (
1.3

Since the objective functions in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are the same,
(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{F}},\overline{\lambda}^{\mathtt{F}})
is a local solution of (
1.3
).

Since a global solution is also a local solution, the above theorem is also valid for global solutions of a SLMFG (
1.2
) and its corresponding MPCC (
1.3
).
The following example shows that the Slater’s CQ assumption could not be dropped in order to attain the same conclusion.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{F}}\overline{\lambda}^{\mathtt{F}})
be a global solution of the MPCC (
1.3

1.1
) for all
\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}

u^{\mathtt{f}}\mapsto J^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}},u^{-\mathtt{f}})

Proof.

Assume that there exists
\hat{u}^{\mathtt{l}}\in Y^{\mathtt{l}}

(\hat{u}^{\mathtt{F}})\in\mathsf{NEP}(\hat{u}^{\mathtt{l}})
such that

J^{\mathtt{l}}(\hat{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\hat{u}^{\mathtt{F}})<J^{\mathtt{l}}(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{F}}).

Given that

and
g^{\mathtt{f}}_{j}
are all convex and Slater’s CQ is satisfied at
\hat{u}^{\mathtt{l}}
, the KKT conditions hold for each follower

This implies that

1.3

(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{F}}\overline{\lambda}^{\mathtt{F}})

The equivalence of global solutions of the SLMFG (
1.2
) and the MPCC problem (
1.3
) was proved under a different set of conditions in
Svensson [7, Theorem 26]

In
[
7

is convex, (2) the maps

\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}

\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}
, there exists a

\max_{j=1,\dots,p^{\mathtt{f}}}g^{\mathtt{f}}_{j}(u^{\mathtt{l}}(\mathtt{f}),u^{\mathtt{f}})<0
, then
(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{F}},\overline{\lambda}^{\mathtt{F}})
is a global solution of (

is a global solution of (

Observe that the condition of convexity assumption used in
[

]
is stronger that our Theorem

This means that our proposed result and the one of
Svensson [7]
provide alternative conditions for the equivalence of global solutions of the two problems.
An important question is whether the equivalence holds under weaker conditions.
In particular, we ask whether the equivalence holds if
u^{\mathtt{f}}\mapsto J^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}})
is convex, and for each
\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}
, there exists a
(u^{\mathtt{l}}(\mathtt{f}),u^{\mathtt{f}})
such that
\max_{j=1,\dots,p^{\mathtt{f}}}g^{\mathtt{f}}_{j}(u^{\mathtt{l}}(\mathtt{f}),u^{\mathtt{f}})<0
.
The following example provides a negative answer to this question.

Example 2.4
.

Consider a two-follower setting,
i.e.

\mathtt{F}=\{\mathtt{f}_{1},\mathtt{f}_{2}\}
.
The leader
\mathtt{l}
’s decision set is
U^{\mathtt{l}}=\mathbb{R}
and both followers make decision over the set
U^{\mathtt{f}_{1}}=U^{\mathtt{f}_{2}}=\mathbb{R}
.
For a given
u^{\mathtt{l}}\in U^{\mathtt{l}}
, the follower
\mathtt{f}_{i}
(
i=1,2
) solves the problem

\displaystyle\min_{u^{\mathtt{f}_{i}}}\quad

\displaystyle u^{\mathtt{l}}(u^{\mathtt{f}_{1}}+u^{\mathtt{f}_{2}})

(2.3a)

s.t.

\displaystyle u^{\mathtt{l}}(u^{\mathtt{f}_{i}})^{2}\leq 0.

(2.3b)

Observe that the constraint is convex in
u^{\mathtt{f}_{i}}
but not jointly.
Furthermore, there exists
u^{\mathtt{l}}=u^{\mathtt{f}_{1}}=u^{\mathtt{f}_{2}}=-1
which makes
u^{\mathtt{l}}(u^{\mathtt{f}_{i}})^{2}=-1<0
.

Suppose that the leader’s problem is given by

\displaystyle\min_{u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}_{1}},u^{\mathtt{f}_{2}}}\quad

\displaystyle(u^{\mathtt{l}}-1)^{2}+(u^{\mathtt{f}_{1}})^{2}+(u^{\mathtt{f}_{2}})^{2}

(2.4a)

s.t.

\displaystyle(u^{\mathtt{f}_{1}},u^{\mathtt{f}_{2}})\in\mathsf{NEP}(u^{\mathtt{l}}),

(2.4b)

where
\mathsf{NEP}(u^{\mathtt{l}})
refers to the solution set of (
2.3
).
The unique global solution of this SLMFG is
(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{f}_{1}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{f}_{2}})=(1,0,0)
.

On the other hand, the MPCC formulation of (
2.4
) reads

\displaystyle\min_{u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}_{1}},u^{\mathtt{f}_{2}},\lambda^{\mathtt{f}_{1}},\lambda^{\mathtt{f}_{2}}}\quad

\displaystyle(u^{\mathtt{l}}-1)^{2}+(u^{\mathtt{f}_{1}})^{2}+(u^{\mathtt{f}_{2}})^{2}

(2.5a)

s.t.

\displaystyle u^{\mathtt{l}}+2\lambda^{\mathtt{f}_{i}}u^{\mathtt{l}}u^{\mathtt{f}_{i}}\qquad(i=1,2)

(2.5b)

\displaystyle u^{\mathtt{l}}(u^{\mathtt{f}_{i}})^{2}\leq 0,\quad\lambda^{\mathtt{f}_{i}}\geq 0,\quad\lambda^{\mathtt{f}_{i}}u^{\mathtt{l}}(u^{\mathtt{f}_{i}})^{2}=0,\qquad i=1,2.

(2.5c)

Every feasible point of this MPCC is of the form
(0,u^{\mathtt{f}_{1}},u^{\mathtt{f}_{2}},\lambda^{\mathtt{f}_{1}},\lambda^{\mathtt{f}_{2}})
.
This implies that the solution
(\overline{u}^{\mathtt{l}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{f}_{1}},\overline{u}^{\mathtt{f}_{2}})
is not feasible for (
2.5

Conversely, the vector
(0,0,0,\lambda^{\mathtt{f}_{1}},\lambda^{\mathtt{f}_{2}})
is an optimal point of the MPCC (
2.5
) but not optimal for the SLMFG (
2.4
).

We presented already two examples where the global solutions of the SLMFG and the MPCC turn out to be different.
We next turn to investigating the relation between their local solutions.
First, we define the set of multipliers satisfying the KKT conditions for each follower
\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}
at
(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}})
as

\Lambda^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}}):=\left\{\lambda^{\mathtt{f}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{p^{\mathtt{f}}}\ \left|\ \begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\nabla_{u^{\mathtt{f}}}J^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}},u^{-\mathtt{f}})+\sum_{j=1}^{p^{\mathtt{f}}}\lambda^{\mathtt{f}}_{j}g^{\mathtt{f}}_{j}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}})=0\\[8.00003pt]
g^{\mathtt{f}}_{j}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}})\leq 0,\quad\langle\lambda^{\mathtt{f}},g^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}})=0\end{array}\right.\right\}.

According to
Robinson [5]
, this set-valued map
\Lambda^{\mathtt{f}}(\cdot)
is upper semicontinuous.
Moreover, we defin the set
\Lambda^{\mathtt{F}}(\cdot)
is then defined as the product
\prod_{\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}}\Lambda^{\mathtt{f}}(\cdot)
.
We have now the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let the follower’s problem (

3
Multiple followers with shared constraints

The results presented in the previous section deal with SLMFGs where the followers solve a Nash equilibrium problem.
This feature means that the choice of each follower is constrained only to himself (and the single leader) and not the other followers.
This can be too restrictive in some practical situations, and deems that the choice of a follower could as well be constrained by the other followers,
i.e.
to consider a
generalized Nash equilibrium problem
(briefly
GNEP
).

In this section, we partially answer such a call by showing that a GNEP with
shared constraints
(in the spirit of
Rosen [6]
) can be equivalently reformulated as a classical NEP under joint convexity assumption.
As a consequence, all the results presented in Section
2
are then applicable to the SLMFG with Rosen’s GNEP lower-level.
This situation is prevalent in a decision problem with a limited availability of resources,
e.g.
followers making purchases on a limited amount of bananas and coconuts in a market to make poached banana in coconut milk.

To be accurate, let us fix, for each
u^{\mathtt{l}}\in U^{\mathtt{l}}
, a new constraint set
\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})\subset U^{\mathtt{F}}
with

\displaystyle\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})=\{u^{\mathtt{F}}\in U^{\mathtt{F}}\mid\overline{g}^{\mathtt{F}}_{j}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}})\leq 0\quad

\displaystyle(\forall j=1,\dots,\overline{p}^{\mathtt{F}})\}

(3.1a)

\displaystyle\overline{g}^{\mathtt{F}}_{j}:U^{\mathtt{l}}\times U^{\mathtt{F}}\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(\forall j=1,\dots,\overline{p}^{\mathtt{F}}).

(3.1b)

We also introduce the notation

\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{-\mathtt{f}}):=\{u^{\mathtt{f}}\in U^{\mathtt{f}}\mid(u^{\mathtt{f}},u^{-\mathtt{f}})\in\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})\}\subset U^{\mathtt{f}}

for any
\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}
,
u^{\mathtt{l}}\in U^{\mathtt{l}}
and
u^{-\mathtt{f}}\in U^{-\mathtt{f}}
.
Additionally, we suppose for each
\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}
that the objective function
J^{\mathtt{f}}
is independent of
u^{-\mathtt{f}}
,
i.e.
,
J^{\mathtt{f}}:U^{\mathtt{l}}\times U^{\mathtt{f}}\to\mathbb{R}
.
The followers’ problems, given a signal
u^{\mathtt{l}}\in U^{\mathtt{l}}
, becomes: Find a vector
u^{\mathtt{F}}\in U^{\mathtt{F}}
such that for each
\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}
, the element
u^{\mathtt{f}}
solves the following optimization

\displaystyle\min_{\hat{u}^{\mathtt{f}}}\quad

\displaystyle J^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},\hat{u}^{\mathtt{f}})

(3.2a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\hat{u}^{\mathtt{f}}\in Y^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})

(3.2b)

\displaystyle\hat{u}^{\mathtt{f}}\in\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{-\mathtt{f}}).

(3.2c)

This turns the followers’ problems into a
u^{\mathtt{l}}
-parametrized
Rosen’s generalized Nash equilibrium problem
(briefly,
RGNEP
), and we refer to the solution set of (
3.2
) with
\mathsf{RGNEP}(u^{\mathtt{l}})
.

Now, let us show that lower-level RGNEP could be reformulated with a NEP.

Theorem 3.1
.

The RGNEP given in (
3.2
) can be reformulated into an optimization problem with the same global solution set, provided that
\overline{g}^{\mathtt{F}}_{j}
is jointly convex.

Proof.

Let
u^{\mathtt{l}}\in U^{\mathtt{l}}
be given.
We define a function
\widetilde{J}^{\mathtt{F}}:U^{\mathtt{l}}\times U^{\mathtt{F}}\to\mathbb{R}
and a constraint set
Y^{\mathtt{F}}\subset U^{\mathtt{F}}
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}}):=\sum_{\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}}J^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}})

\displaystyle Y^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}}):=\prod_{\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}}Y^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}}).

We now consider the following
u^{\mathtt{l}}
-parametrized optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{u^{\mathtt{F}}}\quad

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}})

(3.3a)

s.t.

\displaystyle u^{\mathtt{F}}\in Y^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})

(3.3b)

\displaystyle u^{\mathtt{F}}\in\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}}),

(3.3c)

whose (global) solution set will be denoted with
\widetilde{\mathsf{Opt}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})
.
We would like to prove that
\mathsf{RGNEP}(u^{\mathtt{l}})=\widetilde{\mathsf{Opt}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})
.

It is clear that
\widetilde{\mathsf{Opt}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})\subset\mathsf{RGNEP}(u^{\mathtt{l}})
, hence we focus on showing
\mathsf{RGNEP}(u^{\mathtt{l}})\subset\widetilde{\mathsf{Opt}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})
.
Suppose that
u^{\mathtt{F}}\in\mathsf{RGNEP}(u^{\mathtt{l}})
.
This is equivalent to saying that for any
\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}
, there exists
g^{\mathtt{f}}\in\partial_{u^{\mathtt{f}}}J^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}})
such that

\langle g^{\mathtt{f}},\hat{u}^{\mathtt{f}}-u^{\mathtt{f}}\rangle\geq 0

for every
\hat{u}^{\mathtt{f}}\in Y^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})\cap\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{-\mathtt{f}})
.
This is the same as saying

\langle\widetilde{g}^{\mathtt{F}},\hat{u}^{\mathtt{F}}-u^{\mathtt{F}}\rangle\geq 0

for every
\hat{u}^{\mathtt{F}}\in Y^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})\cap\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})
, where

\widetilde{g}^{\mathtt{F}}=(g^{\mathtt{f}_{1}},\dots,g^{\mathtt{f}_{n}})\in\prod_{\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}}\partial_{u^{\mathtt{f}}}J^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}})=\prod_{\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}}\partial_{u^{\mathtt{f}}}\widetilde{J}^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}})=\partial_{u^{\mathtt{F}}}\widetilde{J}^{\mathtt{F}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}}).

We have thus proved that
u^{\mathtt{F}}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{Opt}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})
.

The above technique could also be adapted to a more general GNEP in which the followers are partitioned into groups, and players within each group have shared constraints only among them.
Let us develop some additional notations here. For any subset
\mathtt{B}\subset\mathtt{F}
, we write
U^{\mathtt{B}}=\prod_{\mathtt{b}\in\mathtt{B}}U^{\mathtt{b}}
and its generic element
u^{\mathtt{B}}=(u^{\mathtt{b}})_{\mathtt{b}\in\mathtt{B}}
.
We also write
U^{-\mathtt{B}}=\prod_{\mathtt{a}\in\mathtt{F}\setminus\mathtt{B}}
and its generic element
u^{-\mathtt{B}}=(u^{\mathtt{a}})_{\mathtt{a}\in\mathtt{F}\setminus\mathtt{B}}
.
Any vector
u^{\mathtt{F}}
can then be represented with
u^{\mathtt{F}}=(u^{\mathtt{B}},u^{-\mathtt{B}})
.

Formally, suppose that the set
\mathtt{F}
of followers is partitioned into
\mathtt{F}_{1},\dots,\mathtt{F}_{m}
, each of them are mutually disjoint.
For each
i=1,\dots,m
and each
u^{\mathtt{l}}
, we consider the following shared constraint set

\displaystyle\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})=\{u^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}=(u^{\mathtt{f}})_{\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}_{i}}\mid\overline{g}^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}_{j}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}_{i}})\leq 0\quad

\displaystyle(\forall j=1,\dots,\overline{p}^{\mathtt{F}_{i}})\}

(3.4a)

\displaystyle\overline{g}^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}_{j}:U^{\mathtt{l}}\times U^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(\forall j=1,\dots,\overline{p}^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}).

(3.4b)

We focus on the situation that a follower’s objective function is independent of the decision of the one within the same group.
This means, for each
\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}_{i}
(
i=1,\dots,m
), the objective is a real function
J^{\mathtt{f}}
is defined on
U^{\mathtt{l}}\times U^{\mathtt{f}}\times U^{-\mathtt{F}_{i}}=U^{\mathtt{l}}\times U^{\mathtt{f}}\times\prod_{\mathtt{g}\in\mathtt{F}\setminus\mathtt{F}_{i}}U^{\mathtt{g}}
.
The projection of the above constraint system to each of the follower
\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}_{i}
is then defined as

\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}_{i}\setminus\{\mathtt{f}\}})=\{u^{\mathtt{f}}\in U^{\mathtt{f}}\mid(u^{\mathtt{f}},u^{\mathtt{F}_{i}\setminus\{\mathtt{f}\}})\in\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}\}.

For each
u^{\mathtt{l}}
, the corresponding RGNEP of all the followers then have the following form: Find
u^{\mathtt{F}}\in U^{\mathtt{F}}
such that for each
i=1,\dots,m
and each
\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}_{i}
, the component
u^{\mathtt{f}}
solves

\displaystyle\min_{\hat{u}^{\mathtt{f}}}\quad

\displaystyle J^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},\hat{u}^{\mathtt{f}},u^{-\mathtt{F}_{i}})

(3.5a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\hat{u}^{\mathtt{f}}\in Y^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})

(3.5b)

\displaystyle\hat{u}^{\mathtt{f}}\in\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}_{i}\setminus\{\mathtt{f}\}})

(3.5c)

With the same proof with that of Theorem
3.1
applied to each follower group
\mathtt{F}_{i}
, we could obtain the following, more general consequence.

Theorem 3.2
.

The RGNEP given in (
3.5
) can be reformulated into a NEP with the same global solution set, provided that each
\overline{g}^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}_{j}
is jointly convex.

Proof.

Consider the leader
\mathtt{l}
and a new set of followers
\widetilde{\mathtt{F}}:=\{\mathtt{F}_{1},\dots,\mathtt{F}_{m}\}
.
With this revised set of players, consider for each given
u^{\mathtt{l}}\in U^{\mathtt{l}}
the following NEP: Find
u^{\widetilde{\mathtt{F}}}=(u^{\mathtt{F}_{1}},\dots,u^{\mathtt{F}_{m}})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}U^{\widetilde{\mathtt{F}}_{i}}
in which for each
i=1,\dots,m
, the component
u^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}
solves the problem

\displaystyle\min_{u^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}}\quad

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}_{i}},u^{-\mathtt{F}_{i}})=\sum_{\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}_{i}}J^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{f}},u^{-\mathtt{F}_{i}})

(3.6a)

s.t.

\displaystyle u^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}\in\widetilde{Y}^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})=\prod_{\mathtt{f}\in\mathtt{F}_{i}}Y^{\mathtt{f}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})

(3.6b)

\displaystyle u^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}\in\overline{Y}^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}(u^{\mathtt{l}}).

(3.6c)

With a similar proof to that of Theorem
3.1
, we could show that (
3.6
) is equivalent to the RGNEP (
3.5
).

Now, we consider the following SLMFG, where the lower-level problem is a RGNEP:

\displaystyle\min_{u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}}}\quad

\displaystyle J^{\mathtt{l}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\mathtt{F}})

(3.7a)

s.t.

\displaystyle u^{\mathtt{l}}\in Y^{\mathtt{l}}

(3.7b)

\displaystyle u^{\mathtt{F}}\in\mathsf{RGNEP}(u^{\mathtt{l}}),

(3.7c)

where
\mathsf{RGNEP}(u^{\mathtt{l}})
now refers to the problem (
3.5
).
If each function
g^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}_{j}
is jointly convex, then the above SLMFG (
3.7
) reduces to a simplified SLMFG of the form

\displaystyle\min_{u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\widetilde{\mathtt{F}}}}\quad

\displaystyle J^{\mathtt{l}}(u^{\mathtt{l}},u^{\widetilde{\mathtt{F}}})

(3.8a)

s.t.

\displaystyle u^{\mathtt{l}}\in Y^{\mathtt{l}}

(3.8b)

\displaystyle u^{\widetilde{\mathtt{F}}}\in\widetilde{NEP}(u^{\mathtt{l}}),

(3.8c)

where
\widetilde{\mathtt{F}}=\{\mathtt{F}_{1},\cdots,\mathtt{F}_{m}\}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{NEP}}(u^{\mathtt{l}})
is the solution set of the problem (
3.6
).
Since the above reduced problem (
3.8
) is explicit in terms of the constraint functions
g^{\mathtt{f}}_{j}
’s and
\overline{g}^{\mathtt{F}_{i}}_{j}
’s, the results of Section
2
are applicable to the SLMFG with group-shared constraints (
3.7
), through its reduced form (
3.8
), with assumptions directly verifiable on these constraint functions.

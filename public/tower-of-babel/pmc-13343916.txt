Joint association of atherogenic index of plasma (AIP) and body roundness index (BRI) with cardiometabolic multimorbidity in adults in Guangzhou, China
Authors: Haonan Li, Chunzi Zeng, Zhifeng Li, Yuting Qin, Yujie Peng, Jiahe Feng, Jie Huang, Shiyun Luo, Jun Yuan, Jing Gu, Yan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCardiometabolic multimorbidity (CMM) is one of the most prevalent patterns of multimorbidity worldwide and presents a growing challenge to public health. Metabolic dysregulation and visceral adipose play central roles in the development of CMM. The atherogenic index of plasma (AIP) has been proposed as a comprehensive indicator of lipid metabolic abnormalities, whereas the body roundness index (BRI) is a novel anthropometric measure reflecting central obesity and visceral adipose tissue (VAT). However, evidence regarding the associations of AIP and BRI with CMM remains limited, particularly in southern Chinese populations and young adults. This study examined the separate and joint associations of AIP and BRI with CMM, aiming to provide preliminary scientific evidence to identify individuals more likely to have prevalent CMM.MethodsThis cross-sectional study included 2505 adults from the 2024 Guangzhou Residents’ Nutrition Survey. Multivariable logistic regression models and segmented logistic regression analyses were employed to examine the association patterns of AIP and BRI with CMM, as well as to assess potential threshold effects. For joint analysis, participants were categorized into four groups by AIP and BRI levels to evaluate the joint association and interaction between these indices and CMM.ResultsAmong the 2505 participants, 213 (8.50%) were diagnosed with CMM. Compared with the lowest tertile, the highest tertile of AIP (OR = 4.025, 95% CI 2.591–6.455) and BRI (OR = 10.461, 95% CI 5.523–22.496) were associated with a higher likelihood of CMM. AIP was linearly associated with CMM. In contrast, BRI demonstrated a nonlinear association with CMM, with an inflection point at 4.52, below which the odds of CMM increased more rapidly. Joint analyses revealed that participants in the “high AIP+high BRI” group had the strongest association with CMM (OR = 5.081, 95% CI 3.354–7.828). Subgroup analysis revealed that the association between the “high AIP+high BRI” group and CMM was stronger in participants < 60 years.ConclusionIndividuals with elevated levels of AIP and BRI are more likely to have CMM. AIP is linearly associated with CMM, whereas a threshold effect is observed for BRI. The joint assessment of AIP and BRI demonstrates a stronger association with CMM compared to either indicator alone. These findings suggest that the joint assessment of AIP and BRI may be a useful tool for identifying individuals at a higher likelihood of prevalent CMM, particularly in young and middle-aged adults.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12933-026-03202-3.Keywords: Cardiometabolic multimorbidity, Atherogenic index of plasma, Body roundness index, Joint analysis

Introduction

Cardiometabolic multimorbidity (CMM) is defined as the presence of two or more cardiometabolic diseases in a single individual, including hypertension, diabetes mellitus, coronary heart disease (CHD) and stroke [1–3]. A large-scale multimorbidity pattern analysis involving approximately 500,000 adults demonstrated that, among major multimorbidity clusters—including respiratory, gastrointestinal, hepatorenal, as well as mental and arthritis-related conditions—CMM exhibited the highest prevalence (6.0%), underscoring its prominent role in multimorbidity patterns [2]. Estimates indicate that at least 10% of the global population is affected by CMM, with prevalence continuing to rise worldwide [4, 5]. Compared with individuals with a single cardiometabolic condition, patients with CMM present a higher likelihood of mortality and a shorter life expectancy [6], making it a significant public health concern.

The atherogenic index of plasma (AIP) is a composite lipid indicator derived from the logarithmic transformation of the triglyceride-to–high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio [7]. It has been proposed as a surrogate marker for atherogenic dyslipidemia, which is associated with small dense low-density lipoprotein particles [8]. Previous studies have demonstrated close association between AIP and major cardiometabolic disease components, including hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, coronary heart disease, and stroke [9–11]. The body roundness index (BRI) is a novel anthropometric indicator calculated from height and waist circumference and is designed to better reflect visceral adiposity [12]. Existing evidence indicates that BRI is closely related to cardiometabolic outcomes and may provide complementary information to traditional obesity-related indices [13, 14].

CMM involves multiple interrelated cardiometabolic conditions, and a single biomarker typically reflects only one aspect of underlying pathology. Consequently, integrated assessment strategies that combine multiple biomarkers representing distinct biological pathways have received increasing attention. Prior studies have reported the potential advantages of combined indices, such as AIP with body mass index (BMI) and the BRI with the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index [15, 16]. Given that the diagnosis of CMM relies on relatively complex clinical assessments and is often overlooked in community populations, investigating the association between easily accessible indices, such as AIP and BRI, with CMM can provide useful information for identifying individuals with a higher likelihood of CMM. Although previous studies have utilized similar indicators, such as the TyG index, to examine their association with CMM, evidence regarding the joint association of AIP and BRI with CMM remains limited. This research gap is particularly significant in the Southern Chinese population. Likely influenced by the region’s specific climate and dietary patterns, this population often exhibits significant visceral adiposity and lipid dysregulation despite maintaining a relatively low BMI [17, 18]. This suggests that traditional metrics may not fully capture the cardiometabolic burden in this group. Moreover, while cardiometabolic issues are increasingly prevalent among young adults [19], existing research has predominantly focused on middle-aged and older populations. Based on the 2024 Guangzhou Residents’ Nutrition Survey, this study investigated the separate and joint associations of AIP and BRI with CMM across the full adult age range, aiming to provide preliminary scientific evidence to identify individuals more likely to have prevalent CMM.

Methods

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional investigation was conducted in 2024. Participants were recruited via a multistage stratified cluster random sampling method: (1) Five districts were randomly selected from the seven administrative districts of Guangzhou. (2) Three towns or subdistricts were randomly chosen within each selected district via systematic sampling on the basis of population size. (3) Two community committees were further randomly selected within each town or subdistrict, from which eligible residents were recruited. The inclusion criteria included individuals aged 18 years or older who had resided in the area for at least six months during the 12 months preceding the survey. Individuals with severe intellectual disabilities, mental health disorders, or those who were pregnant or lactating were excluded.

The required sample size was determined on the basis of the standard formula for cross-sectional studies: . The parameters were set as follows: the confidence level was 95% (two-tailed), corresponding to . According to previous studies, the prevalence of CMM was 6.0% among Chinese adults [2] and the probability P was set at 6.0%. With a design effect (deff) of 1.5 and a relative error (r) of 20%, the calculated sample size was 2256. To account for potential data attrition due to incomplete questionnaires, physical examinations, or laboratory tests, the sample size was increased by 10% (2256 × 1.1 ≈ 2482), resulting in a target population of at least 2482 participants.

Ultimately, 2808 individuals were surveyed, and after excluding those with missing data for blood glucose, lipid, blood pressure, or anthropometric measurements, a final sample of 2505 participants was included in the analysis (Fig. 1).

Anthropometric and biochemical measurements

Demographic and health-related information was collected via face-to-face questionnaires by trained and certified interviewers. The data included age, sex, marital status, residence, income, education level, smoking and drinking habits, physical activity, and self-reported or doctor-diagnosed history of chronic diseases (including hypertension, diabetes, heart disease, and stroke). Anthropometric measurements were obtained by trained personnel according to standardized procedures. The participants’ height and weight were measured without shoes and in light clothing. Height was measured via a vertical stadiometer to the nearest 0.1 cm, and weight was measured via an electronic scale to the nearest 0.1 kg. Waist circumference (WC) was measured at the level of the navel while the participant was standing naturally and at the end of exhalation, accurate to 0.1 cm. Blood pressure was measured by trained medical staff via calibrated electronic sphygmomanometers. After the participants had rested quietly for at least five minutes, three consecutive measurements were taken while the participants were in a seated position, and the average of the last two measurements was used for analysis. Fasting venous blood samples were collected by medical personnel and processed according to standardized procedures. Serum triglycerides (TGs), total cholesterol (TC), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and fasting plasma glucose (FPG) were measured via standardized enzymatic methods.

Assessment of CMM

CMM is defined as the presence of two or more cardiometabolic diseases in a single individual. In this study, cardiometabolic diseases included hypertension, type 2 diabetes, coronary heart disease, and stroke [2, 3, 21]. Hypertension was defined as a systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure (DBP) ≥ 90 mmHg or self-reported doctor-diagnosed hypertension [22]. The diagnostic criteria for diabetes were fasting plasma glucose (FPG) ≥ 7.0 mmol/L and/or glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) ≥ 6.5% or a self-reported previous diagnosis by a doctor of diabetes [23]. CHD and stroke were identified on the basis of participants’ self-reported doctor diagnoses. The self-reported disease history was collected through a standardized questionnaire with the following question: “Have you ever been diagnosed with any disease by a township hospital, community health service center, or any higher-level medical institution?”

Covariate considerations

On the basis of previous studies and known cardiometabolic risk factors [24–26], sociodemographic and lifestyle factors were selected as covariates to analyse the associations of AIP and BRI with CMM. The covariates included age, sex, marital status, residence, education, income, physical activity, smoking, and drinking habits. Age was divided into < 60 years and ≥ 60 years; sex was classified as male or female; marital status was classified as married or other; residence status was classified as urban or rural; education status was classified primary education or below, secondary education, tertiary education or above; income status was classified as low, medium, or high. Physical activity was categorized as regular, occasional, or never on the basis of leisure-time frequency in the past three months. Smoking status and drinking status were classified as yes or no, respectively, on the basis of current smoking status and alcohol consumption in the past year.

Statistical analysis

All the statistical analyses were performed via R 4.0.2 software. Continuous variables were described on the basis of their distribution. Normally distributed variables were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation, and nonnormally distributed variables were expressed as the median and interquartile range, with intergroup comparisons performed via the independent sample t test or Mann–Whitney U test, respectively. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, with comparisons performed via the χ² test.

A binary multivariable logistic regression model was used to examine the associations of AIP and BRI with CMM. Three models were constructed: Model 1 was unadjusted; Model 2 was adjusted for age and sex; and Model 3 was further adjusted for education, residence, income, marital status, smoking, drinking, and physical activity. The AIP and BRI were included as both continuous and categorical variables. Continuous variables were entered directly into the regression to estimate odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) per unit increase, whereas categorical variables were divided into tertiles (Q1–Q3), with Q1 used as the reference. In Model 3, a restricted cubic spline (RCS) function was used to model the dose-response relationships of AIP and BRI with CMM. If a nonlinear trend was detected, the segmented package in R was used iteratively to determine the optimal inflection point. A segmented logistic regression model was then constructed to assess the threshold effect, and likelihood ratio tests were used to compare the fit between the linear and segmented models.

The participants were categorized into four groups to assess the joint association of AIP and BRI with CMM. The AIP was grouped by its median because of its linear relationship with CMM; participants with AIP at or below the median were defined as low AIP, and those above the median as high AIP. The BRI was grouped by the optimal inflection point of 4.52 to reflect its threshold effect; participants with BRI at or below 4.52 were defined as low BRI, and those above 4.52 as high BRI. Accordingly, four distinct joint groups were formed: low AIP + low BRI (reference), low AIP + high BRI, high AIP + low BRI, and high AIP + high BRI. In Model 3, a binary logistic regression model was used to examine the associations between the four groups and the odds of CMM. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the area under the curve (AUC) were used to compare the predictive performance. Interaction effects between AIP and BRI were evaluated on both multiplicative and additive scales. The multiplicative interaction was tested via a product term in the logistic regression model. Additive interaction was evaluated via the relative excess risk due to interaction (RERI), attributable proportion (AP), and synergy index (S). A 95% CI for RERI or AP excluding 0 or for S excluding 1 indicated a significant additive interaction.

Furthermore, stratified analyses were performed on the basis of sex and age, with sex classified as male or female and age stratified into groups of < 60 years and ≥ 60 years. In each subgroup, all models were adjusted for Model 3 covariates, excluding the stratifying variable.

All the statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Table 1 summarizes the baseline characteristics of the study population. A total of 2505 participants aged 18–95 years were enrolled, consisting of 1406 (56.13%) females and 1099 (43.87%) males. The mean age of the participants was 48.57 years. Among them, 213 (8.50%) were diagnosed with CMM, while 2292 (91.50%) were classified into the non-CMM group. Participants with CMM were more likely to be male and aged ≥ 60 years (P < 0.05). They also tended to have lower educational attainment and a higher prevalence of smoking than those without CMM. AIP and BRI levels were significantly higher in the CMM group (P < 0.001), whereas residence, marital status, and physical activity did not differ significantly.

Separate association of AIP and BRI with CMM

Table 2 shows the separate association of AIP and BRI with CMM. In Model 3, both AIP and BRI were significantly associated with CMM. When AIP and BRI were analysed as categorical variables, participants in the highest tertile of AIP (OR = 4.025, 95% CI 2.591–6.455) or BRI (OR = 10.461, 95% CI 5.523–22.496) had a higher likelihood of CMM than those in the lowest tertile. RCS analyses were performed to assess the nonlinear association of AIP and BRI with CMM. As shown in Fig. 2, both AIP and BRI showed significant associations with CMM (P for overall < 0.001); BRI showed a significant nonlinear association with CMM (P for nonlinearity = 0.012), whereas AIP did not (P for nonlinearity = 0.358). Given the nonlinear relationship between BRI and CMM, a threshold effect analysis was performed via a segmented logistic regression model to assess this association. As shown in Supplementary Table S1, an inflection point was identified at a BRI value of 4.52 (95% CI 3.96–5.08). The likelihood ratio test comparing the segmented logistic regression model to the simple linear logistic regression model showed a statistically significant difference (P < 0.001). When BRI was below 4.52, higher BRI was significantly associated with a higher likelihood of CMM (OR = 2.628, 95% CI 1.945–3.553, P < 0.001); when BRI exceeded 4.52, the association was attenuated and not statistically significant (OR = 1.054, 95% CI 0.788–1.409, P = 0.725).

Joint association of AIP and BRI with CMM

Multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to evaluate the joint association of AIP and BRI with CMM (Table 3). In Model 3, both high AIP (OR = 2.477, 95% CI 1.770–3.431) and high BRI (OR = 2.921, 95% CI 2.146–3.975) were independently associated with a higher likelihood of CMM compared with their respective low-level groups. Using participants with low AIP and low BRI as the reference, participants with high AIP but low BRI (OR = 2.129, 95% CI 1.391–3.301), low AIP but high BRI (OR = 2.758, 95% CI 1.507–4.950), and high AIP and high BRI (OR = 5.081, 95% CI 3.354–7.828) all exhibited a higher likelihood of CMM. No significant interactions were observed between AIP and BRI in relation to CMM. For additive interaction, the RERI was 1.070 (95% CI − 1.305 to 3.234, P = 0.355), the AP was 0.170 (95% CI − 0.232 to 0.466, P = 0.340), and the SI was 1.360 (95% CI 0.778–2.284, P = 0.263). For multiplicative interaction, the interaction term was 0.722 (95% CI 0.368–1.414, P = 0.343). Detailed results are presented in Supplementary Table S2.

Discriminative ability of AIP and BRI for CMM

ROC curve analyses were performed to evaluate the discriminative ability of AIP, BRI, and their combined indicator for CMM (Fig. 3). Both AIP and BRI individually demonstrated good discrimination, with AUC values of 0.806 and 0.815, respectively. Notably, the combined AIP + BRI index achieved the highest AUC (0.823), indicating improved discrimination compared with either index alone.

Subgroup analyses

Stratified analyses were conducted to evaluate the joint association of AIP and BRI with CMM by sex and age (Fig. 4). The results were generally consistent with the primary findings, with participants exhibiting both high AIP and high BRI showing the highest likelihood of CMM across all subgroups. The associations appeared stronger among participants aged < 60 years and were attenuated among those aged ≥ 60 years. We further evaluated the discriminative ability of these indicators using AUC values (Supplementary Fig. S1). The combined AIP + BRI index consistently demonstrated better discrimination than either index alone across all sex and age subgroups. Notably, the improvement in discrimination was more evident among participants aged < 60 years.

Discussion

This study examined the separate and joint associations of AIP and BRI with CMM. Both higher AIP and higher BRI were significantly associated with a higher likelihood of CMM and showed good discriminative ability for CMM. AIP showed a linear association with CMM, whereas BRI exhibited a nonlinear relationship with a threshold effect, with an inflection point identified at 4.52. Joint analyses revealed that individuals with “high AIP + high BRI” presented the highest likelihood of CMM. Furthermore, the joint assessment of these two indicators showed improved discrimination for CMM than either indicator used alone. Subgroup analyses by sex and age confirmed these findings, with the combined AIP–BRI index showing particularly better discrimination among participants aged < 60 years.

The AIP, calculated as log10 [TG (mmol/L)/HDL-C (mmol/L)], serves as a surrogate marker of small, dense low-density lipoprotein particles (sdLDLs) [7]. In recent years, the AIP has served as a comprehensive indicator of lipid metabolic status, reflecting the interplay between lipid abnormalities and metabolic dysfunction [10, 27, 28]. Our findings demonstrated a significant positive association between elevated AIP and higher likelihood of CMM, which is consistent with previous reports. Notably, existing studies on the AIP-CMM association are mostly restricted to middle-aged and older populations [29–31]. In contrast, the present study enrolled adults aged 18 years and above, covering the full adult age range, and confirmed the positive association between AIP and CMM in this broader population. Although the mechanisms underlying the association between AIP and CMM remain incompletely understood, several plausible explanations exist. AIP reflects a pro-atherogenic metabolic profile, characterized by elevated triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and reduced HDL-C concentrations, which may impair cholesterol transport and clearance [32]. Furthermore, a high AIP promotes systemic inflammation and oxidative stress [33, 34]. These metabolic disorders collectively induce vascular endothelial damage and insulin resistance, thereby driving the initiation and progression of multiple cardiometabolic abnormalities, ultimately leading to the development of CMM.

The BRI was proposed by Thomas et al. in 2013 on the basis of an elliptical body model and the theory of eccentricity. It quantifies body “roundness” via height and waist circumference, thereby reflecting VAT more accurately [20]. Recent evidence indicates that BRI outperforms traditional adiposity indices in predicting cardiovascular events [35, 36]. For example, a cohort study by Man Yang et al. among older adults reported that higher BRI values were significantly associated with increased cardiovascular risk and showed superior predictive performance compared with BMI [12]. Consistent with these findings, we observed that higher BRI values were significantly associated with a higher likelihood of CMM. Mechanistically, excessive accumulation of visceral fat may disrupt metabolic homeostasis and impair glucose and lipid metabolism. This process may exacerbate insulin resistance by reducing adiponectin levels and inducing leptin resistance [37, 38], which in turn inhibits insulin signaling pathways. In addition, abnormal fat metabolism can increase basal lipolysis and promote the release of free fatty acids, inflammatory cytokines, and other proinflammatory mediators [37, 39]. These changes may further impair endothelial function, activate systemic inflammation, and affect coagulation pathways, ultimately contributing to dysfunction in both the cardiovascular and metabolic systems [40, 41].

Further analysis of relevant patterns revealed a linear association between AIP and CMM, suggesting that worsening lipid metabolism is progressively associated with a greater cardiometabolic disease burden. This pattern is consistent with reports by Zou et al. regarding the cumulative effect of adverse lipid exposure [42]. In addition, multiple previous studies have reported linear associations between AIP and cardiometabolic conditions such as hypertension and stroke [43, 44]. Our findings extend this evidence to multimorbidity, showing that even modest increases in AIP are associated with a higher likelihood of CMM. These results highlight the potential value of AIP as an indicator for identifying individuals with an unfavorable cardiometabolic profile and underscore the importance of monitoring lipid metabolism in population health management. Conversely, a nonlinear relationship was observed between BRI and CMM, with an inflection point identified at 4.52. Below this threshold, higher BRI was associated with a higher likelihood of CMM; however, above this value, the association tended to plateau. This trend may be related to VAT storage capacity approaching or reaching saturation [45]. As an indicator reflecting VAT accumulation, BRI levels below 4.52 suggest that VAT typically still possesses a certain degree of expansion capacity. As BRI increases, visceral adipocytes may gradually undergo hypertrophy, compromising normal adipose tissue function. This process is frequently accompanied by decreased adiponectin secretion, the onset of early leptin resistance, increased free fatty acid (FFA) overflow driven by enhanced basal lipolysis, and the activation of pro-inflammatory pathways such as NF-κB, which may subsequently elevate circulating levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines [46, 47]. These sequential metabolic and inflammatory alterations may collectively drive the progression of insulin resistance, vascular endothelial dysfunction, and metabolic disorders, which appear to correspond to an increased likelihood of CMM within this BRI range [48]. The plateau observed beyond the 4.52 threshold suggests a “saturation effect” of VAT storage. At this stage, the systemic inflammatory milieu and metabolic dysregulation may reach a peak where the marginal impact of further adiposity on these specific pathways diminishes [49]. While excess lipids may be redirected to ectopic sites, leading to lipotoxicity, our data indicate that the incremental likelihood of CMM does not continue to rise. Consistent with this interpretation, studies of other obesity-related indicators have reported similar threshold effects; for example, Shi et al. reported that the increase in gestational diabetes risk slowed when the BRI exceeded 3.55 [50], and Bateman et al. reported that the association between BMI and hypertension showed a flattening trend at higher BMI levels [51] However, this plateau warrants cautious interpretation. First, it may reflect a “ceiling effect” in the observed association, where individuals with extremely high BRI already exhibit a high likelihood of CMM, such that further increases in BRI are associated with only modest additional changes in likelihood. Second, clinical interventions cannot be excluded; individuals with substantially elevated BRI and metabolic abnormalities are more likely to receive pharmacological treatments, which may attenuate the observed association between adiposity and CMM. Therefore, more longitudinal studies are needed in the future to elucidate the physiological patterns at this threshold.

Joint analysis revealed that, compared with the “low AIP + low BRI” group, elevation in either AIP or BRI was associated with a higher likelihood of CMM, with the “high AIP + high BRI” group presenting the highest likelihood. ROC analysis further showed that the combined AIP–BRI index demonstrated better discriminative ability than either indicator alone. These findings suggest that AIP and BRI may capture distinct yet complementary physiological dimensions related to CMM: AIP primarily reflects systemic lipoprotein metabolic abnormalities and atherogenic lipid profiles [52], while BRI focuses on the accumulation and dysfunction of VAT [53]. Furthermore, this study detected no significant multiplicative or additive interactions between AIP and BRI. This can be explained by the independent upstream regulatory mechanisms of these two metabolic risk dimensions, with minimal cross-regulation between their respective pathways. Their associations with CMM are primarily independent, as the lipid dysregulation captured by AIP and the visceral obesity captured by BRI do not amplify each other’s pathological effects. Consequently, their joint effect does not produce synergistic amplification. Given these parallel pathways, relying on a single index may underestimate total cardiometabolic risk. The joint assessment of AIP and BRI identifies individuals with a high likelihood from a multidimensional perspective, which is consistent with previous research advocating the joint use of multiple biomarkers for cardiometabolic diseases [54–56]. Extending this approach to CMM, the present study provides further support for its potential utility in population screening and public health practice.

Subgroup analyses confirmed the robustness of the joint association of AIP and BRI with CMM. The “high AIP + high BRI” group consistently presented the highest likelihood of CMM across all age and sex subgroups. While no significant sex interaction was detected, we observed a trend toward a stronger joint association in males, which may be explained by sex differences in adiposity metabolism and estrogen-related metabolic protection, consistent with prior reports [57, 58]. For age stratification, a more pronounced joint association was consistently observed among individuals aged < 60 years. In contrast, although this association remained present in elderly individuals, its magnitude was significantly attenuated. This discrepancy may be attributed to the fact that younger individuals generally have fewer comorbidities, greater physiological reserves, and fewer competing risk factors [59]. Therefore, abdominal obesity and dyslipidemia are more likely to become primary drivers of CMM in this population. In contrast, the attenuation observed in older adults may reflect the combined effects of long-term disease accumulation, extensive medication use, and age-related physiological decline, all of which contribute to a higher baseline disease burden and may thereby diminish the impact [60]. Similar age-related trends have been reported in previous studies, where metabolic indicators consistently show stronger relative associations with cardiometabolic outcomes in younger populations [61, 62]. This suggests that although CMM prevalence is lower in those aged < 60 years, individuals with high AIP and high BRI are associated with the highest likelihood of CMM. These findings highlight the potential public health benefits of early CMM intervention. Although many countries have lowered the starting age for hypertension screening to 18 years [63, 64], primary prevention for other CMM components (type 2 diabetes, coronary heart disease, and stroke) is still mostly initiated after middle age. Since younger populations generally have more modifiable lifestyle-related risk factors, conducting early monitoring and health education targeting this group holds important public health significance.

This study has several strengths. First, we investigated the separate and joint associations of AIP and BRI with CMM. While previous research has examined similar indices, their specific relationship with CMM remains under-explored, particularly in Southern Chinese populations. Furthermore, in contrast to earlier studies that primarily relied on public databases and focused on individuals aged 40 and older, our research targeted community-dwelling residents in Guangzhou aged 18 and above, thereby encompassing a broader age demographic. Notably, we identified a stronger association between the combined AIP-BRI group and CMM among young and middle-aged individuals, which may serve as a reference for future CMM screening. Nevertheless, certain limitations warrant consideration. The cross-sectional design precludes the establishment of definitive causal inferences. Additionally, the reliance on self-reported data for stroke and CHD may introduce recall bias. Potential confounders, such as dietary habits and sleep patterns, were not adjusted for in this analysis. Finally, as the study was restricted to a Guangzhou-based sample, the generalizability of these findings is constrained. Future research should prioritize clinically confirmed diagnostic data, adjust for a wider range of potential confounders, and validate our findings in multicenter longitudinal cohorts.

Conclusions

We investigated the separate and joint associations of AIP and BRI with CMM in a large-scale adult population in Guangzhou, China. Our findings demonstrate that individuals with elevated levels of AIP and BRI are more likely to have CMM, although they exhibit distinct dose-response patterns. Notably, the joint assessment of these two indices identified individuals at high likelihood of CMM more effectively than either indicator alone did, with this joint effect being particularly pronounced among individuals aged < 60 years. Our findings indicate that the joint assessment of AIP and BRI may be a useful tool for identifying individuals at a higher likelihood of prevalent CMM, particularly among young and middle-aged adults.

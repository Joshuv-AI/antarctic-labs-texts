Proton Gravitational Structure and Mass Decomposition on the Light Front
Sreeraj Nair, Chandan Mondal, Siqi Xu, Xingbo Zhao, James P. Vary, BLFQ Collaboration

Abstract
Gravitational form factors (GFFs) of hadrons encode essential information about the internal distributions of mass, spin, pressure, and shear among their quark and gluon constituents. We compute the quark and gluon GFFs of the proton using a fully relativistic, nonperturbative framework based on a light-front quantized Hamiltonian with quantum chromodynamics (QCD) input. This allows us to quantify the impact of a dynamical gluon on the proton’s mechanical properties, such as pressure and shear distributions. Our predictions agree well with recent lattice QCD results and experimental extractions. We also determine the proton’s mass and mechanical radii and address the long-standing puzzle of its mass decomposition. At the scale μ 2 = 4 ​ GeV 2 \mu^{2}=4~\mathrm{GeV}^{2} , we find that quark energy, gluon field energy, the quark condensate, and the QCD trace anomaly contribute 31.5 % 31.5\% , 34.7 % 34.7\% , 11.3 % 11.3\% , and 22.5 % 22.5\% , respectively, which are consistent with lattice QCD findings.



Introduction
.—Understanding the internal structure of the proton, a bound state of quarks and gluons governed by quantum chromodynamics (QCD), remains a central challenge in nuclear and particle physics
Abdul Khalek et al. 2022
;
Anderle et al. 2021
;
Accardi et al. 2023
. Gravitational form factors (GFFs), defined via the proton matrix elements of the QCD energy-momentum tensor (EMT), provide access to fundamental mechanical properties such as the internal distributions of mass, spin, pressure, and shear
Lorcé et al. 2019
;
Polyakov 2003
;
Polyakov and Schweitzer 2018
;
Burkert et al. 2023
.

The GFFs are related to the second Mellin moments of generalized parton distributions (GPDs), and can be constrained through data from deeply virtual Compton scattering and meson production
Diehl 2003
;
Ji 1997
;
d’Hose et al. 2016
. Quark GFFs have been extracted from Jefferson Lab measurements
Burkert et al. 2018
, with future constraints expected from PANDA at FAIR
Lutz et al. 2009
, EICs
Abdul Khalek et al. 2022
;
Anderle et al. 2021
, NICA
Abgaryan et al. 2022
, ILC, and J-PARC
Kumano 2022
. Theoretically, quark GFFs have been studied using lattice QCD, chiral perturbation theory, light-cone sum rules, QCD-inspired models, and light-front quantization
Chen and Ji 2002
;
Hagler et al. 2003
;
Pasquini et al. 2014
;
Abidin and Carlson 2009
;
Nair et al. 2024
.

In contrast, the gluon GFFs remain poorly constrained, both experimentally and theoretically. Near-threshold charmonium and bottomonium production offers a promising probe of the gluonic structure of the proton
Mamo and Zahed 2020
;
Guo et al. 2024
;
Hatta and Yang 2018
;
Adhikari et al. 2023
, with recent
J/\psi
data from JLab and prospects at future EICs
Duran et al. 2023
;
Liu and Zahed 2024
;
Hechenberger et al. 2024
. These processes are sensitive to the proton’s gluon GPDs through two-gluon exchange, enabling indirect access to gluon GFFs under factorization
Guo et al. 2021
;
Hatta and Strikman 2021
. Analyses based on GPD fits and lattice QCD suggest dipole- or tripole-like behaviors for these form factors
Pefkou et al. 2022
;
Hackett et al. 2024
.

While many theoretical models focus only on quark degrees of freedom, the role of gluons is essential for a complete picture of the proton’s mechanical structure. In particular, the gluon contributions to the
D
-term, relevant for pressure and shear forces, depend on quark-gluon interactions that are encoded in the “bad” components of the EMT
Hu et al. 2024
. Existing theoretical work on gluon GFFs includes lattice QCD
Shanahan and Detmold 2019
;
Alexandrou et al. 2020
, holographic models
Mamo and Zahed 2021
, and recent Bethe-Salpeter equation approaches
Yao et al. 2024
. Improved theoretical determinations of these quantities are essential for refining future measurements and advancing our understanding of the proton’s structure.

In this work, we address the proton’s gravitational structure using basis light-front quantization (BLFQ), a fully relativistic and nonperturbative framework for solving quantum field theory problems
Vary et al. 2010
;
Mondal et al. 2020
;
Xu et al. 2021
;
Xu et al. 2023a
. We employ an effective light-front Hamiltonian incorporating QCD interactions
Brodsky et al. 1998
with explicit quark (
q
) and gluon (
g
) degrees of freedom in the
|qqq\rangle
and
|qqqg\rangle
Fock sectors, along with a complementary three-dimensional confinement potential
Li et al. 2016
. By solving for the mass eigenstates at low-resolution scales, we obtain the light-front wave functions (LFWFs) and extract the quark and gluon GFFs of the proton.

This approach allows us to address two key aspects of proton structure. First, it enables a quantitative assessment of the impact of a dynamical gluon on the proton’s mechanical properties, including the pressure and shear force distributions. Second, it provides a nonperturbative determination of the proton’s mass decomposition into contributions from quark energy, gluon field energy, the quark scalar condensate, and the QCD trace anomaly, which can only be reliably extracted through QCD-based calculations and experimental input.

Proton wave functions from light-front QCD Hamiltonian
.—In the light-front (LF) formalism, the proton state is expanded in terms of Fock sectors as

|\Psi\rangle=\psi_{(qqq)}|qqq\rangle+\psi_{(qqqg)}|qqqg\rangle+\cdots,

where the LFWFs
\psi_{(\cdots)}

(P^{+}P^{-}-\vec{P}^{\perp 2})|\Psi\rangle=M^{2}|\Psi\rangle,

At the initial scale, where the proton consists of

\displaystyle\int dx^{-}d^{2}x^{\perp}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{+}\frac{m_{0}^{2}+(i\partial^{\perp})^{2}}{i\partial^{+}}\psi

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}A^{i}_{a}\left[m_{g}^{2}+(i\partial^{\perp})^{2}\right]A^{i}_{a}+g_{c}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}T^{a}A^{\mu}_{a}\psi

\displaystyle+\frac{g_{c}^{2}}{2}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{+}T^{a}\psi\frac{1}{(i\partial^{+})^{2}}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{+}T^{a}\psi\Big\},

where

Confinement is implemented in the leading Fock sector

We solve the LF Hamiltonian eigenvalue problem using the BLFQ framework

Gravitational form factors
.—The GFFs parameterize the EMT
\theta^{\mu\nu}
for a spin-

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}|\,\theta^{\mu\nu}_{i}(0)\,|P,\Lambda\rangle=\ \overline{u}(P^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})\Big[A_{i}(Q^{2})\frac{\overline{P}^{\mu}\ \overline{P}^{\nu}}{M}

\displaystyle+J_{i}(Q^{2})\frac{i\overline{P}^{\{\mu}\sigma^{\nu\}\alpha}q_{\alpha}}{2M}+D_{i}(Q^{2})\frac{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}-q^{2}g^{\mu\nu}}{4M}

(4)

a^{\{\mu}b^{\nu\}}=(a^{\mu}b^{\nu}+a^{\nu}b^{\mu})/2

\overline{P}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}(P^{\prime}+P)^{\mu}

\overline{u}(P^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}),u(P,\Lambda)

is the proton mass, and

is the squared momentum transfer in the transverse direction. Indices

The symmetric QCD EMT decomposes into quark and gluon parts,
\theta^{\mu\nu}=\sum_{i=(\mathdutchcal{q},\mathdutchcal{g})}\theta^{\mu\nu}_{i}

\theta^{\mu\nu}{q}=i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\{\mu}\mathcal{D}^{\nu\}}\psi

The matrix elements of the EMT, required to extract the GFFs, can be expressed compactly as
\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}=\langle P^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}|\,\theta^{\mu\nu}_{i}(0)\,|P,\Lambda\rangle

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{++}_{\uparrow\uparrow}+\mathcal{M}^{++}_{\downarrow\downarrow}

\displaystyle=2\,(P^{+})^{2}A_{i}(Q^{2})\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{++}_{\uparrow\downarrow}+\mathcal{M}^{++}_{\downarrow\uparrow}

\displaystyle=\frac{iq^{(2)}}{M}(P^{+})^{2}B_{i}(Q^{2})\,.

The transverse components yield the GFFs

and
\overline{C}_{i}(Q^{2})

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{12}_{\uparrow\uparrow}+\mathcal{M}^{12}_{\downarrow\downarrow}

\displaystyle=D_{i}(Q^{2})q^{(1)}q^{(2)},

(6)

\displaystyle q_{\mu}\mathcal{M}^{\mu 1}_{\uparrow\downarrow}+q_{\mu}\mathcal{M}^{\mu 1}_{\downarrow\uparrow}

Summary
.—We solved the light-front QCD Hamiltonian for the proton, including both the constituent three-quark and the three-quark–one-gluon Fock sectors. The resulting light-front wave functions were used to compute the quark and gluon contributions to the proton’s GFFs and mass decomposition. We quantified the role of a dynamical gluon in shaping the proton’s mechanical properties, including pressure and shear distributions. Our predictions show good agreement with recent lattice QCD results and experimental extractions across several key aspects of the proton’s internal structure.

We extracted the proton’s mass and mechanical radii as
0.79(4)
fm and
0.73(5)
fm, respectively. The corresponding quark contributions are
0.76(4)
fm and
0.78(9)
fm, while the gluon contributions are
0.82(5)
fm and
0.70(8)
fm. These values align well with recent lattice and phenomenological analyses.

We also provided a nonperturbative determination of the proton’s mass decomposition, finding contributions from quark energy (
31.5\%
), gluon field energy (
34.7\%
), the quark condensate (
11.3\%
), and the QCD trace anomaly (
22.5\%
), consistent with lattice QCD. Additionally, we analyzed the decomposition using an internal-energy-based two-term scheme, confirming the dominant role of gluons (
55.7\%
) in the proton mass.

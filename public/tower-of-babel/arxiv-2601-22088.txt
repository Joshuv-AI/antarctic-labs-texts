On Global Weak Solutions for the Magnetic Two-Component Hunter–Saxton System
Levin Maier

Abstract
. We study the magnetic two-component Hunter–Saxton system ( M2HS ), which was recently derived in [ 19 ] as a magnetic geodesic equation on an infinite-dimensional configuration space. While the geometric framework and the global weak flow were outlined there, the present paper provides the analytical foundations of this construction from the PDE perspective. First, we derive an explicit solution formula in Lagrangian variables via a Riccati reduction, yielding an alternative proof of the blow-up criterion together with an explicit expression for the blow-up time. Second, we rigorously construct global conservative weak solutions by developing the analytic theory of the relaxed configuration space and the associated weak magnetic geodesic flow, thereby realizing the geometric program proposed in [ 19 ] .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The equations and motivation

In this paper, we study the magnetic two-component Hunter–Saxton system recently introduced in
[
19
]
:

(M2HS)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{tx}&=-\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}-u\,u_{xx}+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}-\bigl(s\rho+2(c^{2}-s\delta)\bigr)&&\text{in }{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times(0,\infty),\\
\rho_{t}&=-(\rho u)_{x}+s\,u_{x}&&\text{in }{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(\cdot,0)&=u_{0},\qquad\rho(\cdot,0)=\rho_{0}&&\text{in }{\mathbb{S}}^{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
is the magnetic parameter,

(1.1)

\delta:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\rho(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x

denotes the contact angle, and

(1.2)

c^{2}:=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\bigl(u_{x}(t,x)^{2}+\rho(t,x)^{2}\bigr)\,{\mathrm{d}}x

is the
\dot{H}^{1}
-energy. Both quantities are conserved along smooth solutions by
[
19
, Thm. 5.1]
.

The parameter
s
can be interpreted as the strength of an underlying magnetic field in the geometric formulation of the system. It couples the velocity and density components and leads to dynamics that differ substantially from the non-magnetic case. A central goal of this paper is to combine an explicit description of smooth solutions, including blow-up behavior, with a global conservative weak theory that continues the dynamics beyond singularity formation.

For
s=0
, the system (
M2HS
) reduces to the classical two-component Hunter–Saxton system
[
27
,
15
]
. If
\rho\equiv s
, one recovers the scalar Hunter–Saxton equation, which models the propagation of nonlinear orientation waves in nematic liquid crystals
[
12
]
and has a rich geometric structure related to geodesic flows
[
13
,
16
,
17
]
.

Beyond its liquid-crystal origin, the Hunter–Saxton system also appears in mathematical physics as a model for one-dimensional non-dissipative dark matter (the Gurevich–Zybin system) and as a short-wave limit of the two-component Camassa–Holm system
[
23
,
5
,
8
,
10
,
9
]
. It belongs to a broader class of coupled third-order systems that includes models for axisymmetric Euler flow with swirl and vorticity dynamics
[
26
,
11
,
6
,
21
,
22
]
.

From a geometric viewpoint, system (
M2HS
) was derived in
[
19
]
as the equation of motion of a Hamiltonian system on an infinite-dimensional configuration space. In that setting, smooth solutions can be interpreted as trajectories of a geometric flow, and the formation of singularities corresponds to a degeneration of the associated Lagrangian map. Moreover,
[
19
]
proposed that by enlarging the configuration space, this geometric flow can be continued globally in a weak sense even after smoothness breaks down. The present paper provides the analytical foundations of this program and makes this continuation rigorous at the PDE level.

With this motivation in place, we now turn to the main contributions of this work.

2.
Preliminaries: geometric origin of (
M2HS
)

In this section we recall the geometric framework of magnetic geodesics and explain how the magnetic two-component Hunter–Saxton system arises as a magnetic geodesic equation on an infinite-dimensional configuration space. We also present an equivalent formulation of the equation that will be fundamental for the weak theory developed later, and relate the system to a linear ODE on a Hilbert sphere.

2.1.
Background: Magnetic geodesics

We present the mathematical framework used to study the dynamics of a charged particle in the presence of a magnetic field, following V. Arnold’s pioneering approach
[
2
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be a strong Riemannian Hilbert manifold in the sense of
[
14
]
and let
\sigma\in\Omega^{2}(M)
be a closed two-form. The form
\sigma
is called a
magnetic field
, and
(M,g,\sigma)
is called a
magnetic system
. This triple determines a skew-symmetric bundle endomorphism
Y\colon TM\to TM
, the
Lorentz force
, defined by

(2.1)

g_{q}\bigl(Y_{q}u,v\bigr)=\sigma_{q}(u,v),\qquad\forall\,q\in M,\ \forall\,u,v\in T_{q}M.

A smooth curve
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to M
is called a
magnetic geodesic
of
(M,g,\sigma)
if it satisfies

(2.2)

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=Y_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma},

where
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of the metric
g
. Equation (
2.2
) reduces to the geodesic equation
\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=0
when
\sigma=0
, i.e. when the magnetic field vanishes.

Like standard geodesics, magnetic geodesics have constant kinetic energy

(2.3)

E(\gamma,\dot{\gamma}):=\tfrac{1}{2}g_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma}),

and therefore travel at constant speed
|\dot{\gamma}|:=\sqrt{g_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})}
, since the Lorentz force
Y
is skew-symmetric.

This conservation of energy reflects the Hamiltonian nature of the system. Indeed, the
magnetic geodesic flow
is defined on the tangent bundle by

(2.4)

\varPhi_{g,\sigma}^{t}\colon TM\to TM,\quad(q,v)\mapsto\bigl(\gamma_{q,v}(t),\dot{\gamma}_{q,v}(t)\bigr),\quad\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
\gamma_{q,v}
is the unique magnetic geodesic with initial condition
(q,v)\in TM
.

A key difference from standard geodesics is that magnetic geodesics with different speeds are not simply reparametrizations of unit-speed magnetic geodesics. This can be seen, for instance, from the fact that the left-hand side of (
2.2
) scales quadratically with the speed, while the right-hand side scales only linearly. Therefore, an important aspect of the theory is to understand the similarities and differences between standard and magnetic geodesics as the kinetic energy varies.

2.2.
(
M2HS
) as magnetic geodesic equation

We now introduce the geometric setting in more detail. Let
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the unit circle, and let
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}^{m}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
be the half-Lie group of Sobolev diffeomorphisms of class
H^{m}
with smooth right multiplication and only continuous left multiplication; see
[
3
]
for the notion of a half-Lie group. This group consists of all diffeomorphisms of
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
of Sobolev class
H^{m}
that fix a designated point. Unless stated otherwise, we assume
m>\frac{5}{2}
. Note that
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}^{m}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})\cong\mathrm{Dens}^{m}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
, where
\mathrm{Dens}^{m}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
denotes the space of probability densities on
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
of Sobolev class
H^{m}
; see
[
7
, §5]
and
[
13
]
.

We further denote by
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{4\pi}
the circle of length
4\pi
, and by
H^{m}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{4\pi})
the space of maps of Sobolev class
H^{m}
. The half-Lie group
G^{m}
is defined as

(2.5)

G^{m}=\mathrm{Diff}_{0}^{m}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})\rtimes H^{m-1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{4\pi}),

where the explicit group product structure is described in
[
18
, §2]
. This group carries a right-invariant metric, the
\dot{H}^{1}
-metric, defined at the identity
(\mathrm{id},0)
by

(2.6)

\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\mathrm{id},0)}\bigl((u,\rho),(v,\psi)\bigr):=\langle(u,\rho),(v,\psi)\rangle_{(\mathrm{id},0)}^{\dot{H}^{1}}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}u_{x}v_{x}+\rho\psi\,\mathrm{d}x,

where the tangent space at the identity is
T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}G^{m}\cong H^{m}_{0}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})\times H^{m-1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})
. Here
H^{m}_{0}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})
denotes the space of Sobolev functions with zero mean. By right invariance, the metric in (
2.6
) extends to all points
(\varphi,\tau)\in G^{m}
as

(2.7)

\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\varphi,\tau)}\bigl((U_{1},U_{2}),(V_{1},V_{2})\bigr):=\langle(U_{1},U_{2}),(V_{1},V_{2})\rangle_{(\varphi,\tau)}^{\dot{H}^{1}}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\frac{U_{1x}V_{1x}}{\varphi_{x}}+U_{2}V_{2}\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
(U_{1},U_{2}),(V_{1},V_{2})\in T_{(\varphi,\tau)}G^{m}\cong H^{m}_{0}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})\times H^{m-1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})
. For further details we again refer to
[
18
, §2]
. With that notation fixed we are in place to state:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
19
, Thm. 5.1]
)
.

A
C^{2}
-curve
(\varphi,\tau):[0,T)\longrightarrow G^{m}
, where
T>0
is the maximal existence time, is a magnetic geodesic of the system
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)
with
m>\frac{5}{2}
if and only if

(2.8)

(u=\varphi_{t}\circ\varphi^{-1},\rho=\tau_{t}\circ\varphi^{-1})\in C\left([0,T),T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}G^{m}\right)\cap C^{1}\left([0,T),T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}G^{m-1}\right)

is a solution of the magnetic two-component Hunter–Saxton system (
M2HS
), that is:

(M2HS)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tx}=-\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}-u\,u_{xx}+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}-(s\rho+2(c^{2}-s\delta)),\\
\rho_{t}=-(\rho u)_{x}+su_{x},\end{cases}

where
c^{2}
, the
\dot{H}^{1}
-energy of the system, is a conserved quantity given by

(2.9)

c^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\bigl(u_{x}^{2}+\rho^{2}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Additionally, the contact angle
\delta
is another conserved quantity, given by

(2.10)

\delta=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x.

The following reformulation of the magnetic geodesic equation will be crucial later on, both for defining global weak solutions and for constructing them explicitly.

Proposition 2.2
.

The magnetic geodesic equation of
\left(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
at the point
(\varphi,\tau)\in G^{m}
is given by:

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\left(u_{t}+uu_{x}\right)\circ\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\varphi(\cdot)}\big(u_{x}^{2}(y)+\rho^{2}(y)\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}y-\varphi(\cdot)(c^{2}-s\delta)\right)-s\int_{0}^{\varphi(\cdot)}\rho(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\\
\left(\rho_{t}+(u\rho)_{x}-su_{x}\rho\right)\circ\varphi\end{pmatrix}=0,

where
c^{2}
and
\delta
are the conserved quantities as in
Theorem

2.1
.

From a geometric viewpoint, the dynamics of (
M2HS
) can be related to a linear ODE on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert sphere.

Combining results from
[
19
]
, one finds that a curve
(\varphi,\tau)
is a magnetic geodesic of
(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha)
with magnetic strength
s
and speed
c
if and only if the complex-valued function

(2.12)

\gamma:=\sqrt{\varphi_{x}}\,e^{{\mathrm{i}}\tau/2}

satisfies

(2.13)

\ddot{\gamma}-{\mathrm{i}}s\dot{\gamma}+\bigl(c^{2}-s\delta\bigr)\gamma=0\quad\text{in }L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{C}}).

Here
\delta
is a conserved quantity representing the angle between
\dot{\gamma}
and the vector field
R(\gamma)={\mathrm{i}}\gamma
, and is given by

(2.14)

\delta=\operatorname{Re}\langle{\mathrm{i}}\gamma,\dot{\gamma}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, by an argument along the lines of
[
1
, §3]
the equation (
2.13
) admits an explicit solution formula. Namely,

(2.15)

\gamma(t)=e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{1}t}p_{1}+e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{2}t}p_{2},

where

(2.16)

\theta_{1/2}=\frac{s\pm\sqrt{s^{2}+4(c^{2}-s\delta)}}{2},\qquad p_{1/2}=\mp\frac{\theta_{2/1}\,\gamma(0)+{\mathrm{i}}\dot{\gamma}(0)}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}.

3.
Explicit solution formula for the magnetic two-component Hunter–Saxton system

Overview

In this section we derive an explicit representation formula for solutions of the magnetic two-component Hunter–Saxton system (M2HS). The formula yields, in particular, an alternative proof of the blow-up criterion established geometrically in
[
19
]
, and it furthermore provides an explicit expression for the blow-up time. Our approach is inspired by the method introduced by Wunsch for the (non-magnetic) two-component Hunter–Saxton system
[
27
]
, but the presence of the magnetic terms requires additional technical modifications; in particular, all equations appearing below are nontrivial perturbations of those in
[
27
]
. Setting
s=0
recovers Wunsch’s explicit formula as a special case.

Throughout, we assume
m>\frac{5}{2}
and consider a solution pair
(u,\rho)
of Sobolev class

(3.1)

(u,\rho)\in C\bigl([0,T);H^{m}({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}^{1})\times H^{m-1}({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}^{1})\bigr),

so that by Sobolev embedding the functions are at least
C^{2}
in space.

3.1.
The (M2HS) system in Lagrangian variables

Let
(u,\rho)
solve the magnetic two-component Hunter–Saxton system on
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
:

(3.2)

\begin{cases}u_{tx}=-\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}-u\,u_{xx}+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}-\bigl(s\rho+2(c^{2}-s\delta)\bigr),\\[1.99997pt]
\rho_{t}=-(\rho u)_{x}+su_{x},\\[1.99997pt]
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},\qquad\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0}.\end{cases}

Let
\varphi
denote the flow generated by
u
, i.e.

(3.3)

\varphi_{t}(t,x)=u(t,\varphi(t,x)),\qquad\varphi(0,x)=x.

Define the Lagrangian variables

(3.4)

U(t,x):=u_{x}\bigl(t,\varphi(t,x)\bigr),\qquad P(t,x):=\rho\bigl(t,\varphi(t,x)\bigr).

A direct computation using the chain rule yields

(3.5)

\frac{d}{dt}U=u_{tx}\circ\varphi+(u_{xx}\circ\varphi)\,\varphi_{t},\qquad\frac{d}{dt}P=\rho_{t}\circ\varphi+(\rho_{x}\circ\varphi)\,\varphi_{t}.

Substituting (
3.2
) and using
\varphi_{t}=u\circ\varphi
, one obtains the closed ODE system

(3.6)

\begin{cases}U_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}U^{2}+\frac{1}{2}P^{2}-\bigl(sP+2(c^{2}-s\delta)\bigr),\\[1.99997pt]
P_{t}=-PU+sU,\\[1.99997pt]
U(0,\cdot)=(u_{0})_{x},\qquad P(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0}.\end{cases}

3.2.
Reduction to a Riccati equation

Introduce the complex-valued function

(3.7)

Z(t,x):=U(t,x)+iP(t,x).

Then (
3.6
) is equivalent to the Riccati-type ODE

(3.8)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Z=-\frac{1}{2}Z^{2}+is\,Z-2(c^{2}-s\delta),\\[3.00003pt]
Z(0,\cdot)=Z_{0}:=(u_{0})_{x}+i\rho_{0}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.1
.

If
(u,\rho)
solves (
3.2
) and
\varphi
is the flow defined by (
3.3
), then the function

(3.9)

Z(t,x)=u_{x}\bigl(t,\varphi(t,x)\bigr)+i\,\rho\bigl(t,\varphi(t,x)\bigr)

solves (
3.8
).

3.3.
Explicit representation formula and blow-up criterion

We next solve (
3.8
) explicitly. Define

(3.10)

\theta_{1/2}=\frac{s\pm\sqrt{s^{2}+4(c^{2}-s\delta)}}{2}.

In the unit speed case
c=1
and assuming
\delta\neq 0
, we have
\theta_{1}\neq\theta_{2}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Explicit solution formula and blow-up time)
.

Let
(u,\rho)
be a solution of (
3.2
) with initial data

(u_{0},\rho_{0})\in H^{m}({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}^{1})\times H^{m-1}({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}^{1})
,
assume
c=1
and
\delta\neq 0
, and let
\varphi
be the flow (
3.3
). Then:

(1)

The quantities
u_{x}\circ\varphi
and
\rho\circ\varphi
admit the explicit representation

(3.11)

\displaystyle u_{x}\bigl(t,\varphi(t,x)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(-(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\tan\!\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}t\right)+(u_{0})_{x}(x)\right)\left(1+(u_{0})_{x}(x)\frac{\tan\!\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}t\right)}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}\right)+(\rho_{0}(x)-s)^{2}\frac{\tan\!\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}t\right)}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}}{\left(1+(u_{0})_{x}(x)\frac{\tan\!\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}t\right)}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}\right)^{2}+\left((\rho_{0}(x)-s)\frac{\tan\!\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}t\right)}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle\rho\bigl(t,\varphi(t,x)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{\bigl(1+\tan^{2}(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}t)\bigr)\,(\rho_{0}(x)-s)}{\left(1+(u_{0})_{x}(x)\frac{\tan\!\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}t\right)}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}\right)^{2}+\left((\rho_{0}(x)-s)\frac{\tan\!\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}t\right)}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}\right)^{2}}.

(2)

The functions
u_{x}\circ\varphi
and
\rho\circ\varphi
develop a singularity if and only if there exist
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}^{1}
and
t_{0}>0
such that

(3.12)

\rho_{0}(x_{0})=s\qquad\text{and}\qquad t_{0}=\frac{2\,\arccot\!\left(-\frac{(u_{0})_{x}(x_{0})}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}\right)}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}.

Remark 3.3
.

The condition
\rho_{0}(x_{0})=s
provides an alternative proof of the blow-up criterion obtained via geometric arguments in
[
19
]
. In contrast to the geometric approach, (
3.12
) yields the blow-up time explicitly.

Remark 3.4
.

Setting
s=0
in Theorem
3.2
recovers
[
27
, Thm. 2.1]
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Solving (
3.8
) by separation of variables yields an explicit expression for
Z(t)=U(t)+iP(t)
. In the unit speed case
c=1
one obtains

(3.13)

Z(t)=-(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\tan\!\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}(t-C)\right)+is

for a constant
C
determined by the initial condition. Using the tangent addition formula and rewriting the resulting expression in terms of real and imaginary parts gives the representation (
3.11
).

For the blow-up criterion, observe that singularities can only occur when the denominator in (
3.11
) vanishes. This happens if and only if there exist
x_{0},t_{0}
such that simultaneously

(3.14)

(\rho_{0}(x_{0})-s)\frac{\tan\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}t_{0}\right)}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}=0,\qquad 1+(u_{0})_{x}(x_{0})\frac{\tan\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}t_{0}\right)}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}=0.

Since
\delta\neq 0
implies
\theta_{1}\neq\theta_{2}
, the second equation enforces
\tan(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}t_{0})\neq 0
, hence
\rho_{0}(x_{0})=s
. Solving the second equation for
t_{0}
yields (
3.12
).
∎

3.4.
Asymptotic absence of blow-up for strong magnetic field

We conclude by studying the blow-up mechanism as the magnetic strength
s\to\infty
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
, the expressions (
3.11
) admit no singularities in the limit
s\to\infty
.

Proof.

From (
3.10
) we have

(3.15)

\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}=\sqrt{s^{2}+4(1-s\delta)}.

By Theorem
3.2
, blow-up requires the existence of
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
such that
\rho_{0}(x_{0})=s
. Since
\rho_{0}
is fixed while
s\to\infty
, no such
x_{0}
exists for sufficiently large
s
. Consequently, the denominator in (
3.11
) cannot vanish, and no singularity occurs.
∎

Question 3.6
.

Does there exist
C>0
such that for all
s>C
the expressions (
3.11
) remain globally regular (i.e. never develop singularities) for all admissible initial data?

4.
Global conservative weak solutions of the magnetic two-component Hunter–Saxton system

Purpose and structure

This section provides the analytic details underlying the global-in-time weak theory for the magnetic two-component Hunter–Saxton system (M2HS). The main goals are:
(i) to introduce a natural relaxation
\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
of the configuration space
G^{m}
that accommodates the collapse mechanism
\varphi_{x}\to 0
,
(ii) to formulate an appropriate notion of
weak magnetic geodesic
on
\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
and prove global existence of such flows,
and (iii) to transfer the flow back to Eulerian variables and thereby obtain global conservative weak solutions of (M2HS).

4.1.
Preparation: the closure of
G^{m}
and its tangent structure

Recall that for
m>\frac{5}{2}
, each sufficiently regular solution
(u,\rho)
of (M2HS) corresponds to a magnetic geodesic
(\varphi,\tau)
on
(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
via

(4.1)

u=\varphi_{t}\circ\varphi^{-1},\qquad\rho=\tau_{t}\circ\varphi^{-1}.

Moreover, blow-up in Eulerian variables is detected by degeneration of the Lagrangian map: there exist
T>0
and
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
such that

(4.2)

\lim_{t\uparrow T}\varphi_{x}(t,x_{0})=0.

In particular,
(\varphi(t),\tau(t))
may leave
G^{m}
at finite time. This motivates relaxing the diffeomorphism component to monotone absolutely continuous maps.

We briefly recall that a function
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is
absolutely continuous
iff it has an a.e. derivative
f^{\prime}\in L^{1}([0,1])
and

(4.3)

f(x)=f(0)+\int_{0}^{x}f^{\prime}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y,\qquad x\in[0,1].

We denote by
AC([0,1],[0,1])
the class of absolutely continuous maps with values in
[0,1]
. While
AC([0,1])
and
H^{1}([0,1])
agree as sets modulo null sets, the constraint
f([0,1])\subset[0,1]
is an additional property.

Following
[
27
]
, we introduce the relaxed configuration space.

Definition 4.1

(Relaxed configuration space)
.

Define

(4.4)

\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}:=M_{AC}^{0}\rtimes L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}),

where
M_{AC}^{0}
consists of all nondecreasing absolutely continuous maps
\varphi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
satisfying
\varphi(0)=0
and
\varphi(1)=1
. The semidirect product multiplication is the same as for
G^{m}
(cf.
[
18
, §2]
).

Remark 4.2
.

In view of the criterion
\varphi_{x}\to 0
, the set
M_{AC}^{0}
can be regarded as a natural closure of
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
(orientation-preserving circle diffeomorphisms fixing
0
).

Identifying
AC({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
with
H^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
, the tangent space at
(\mathrm{id},0)\in\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
is

(4.5)

T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\Bigl\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}):u(0)=0\Bigr\}\times L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}).

For general
(\varphi,\tau)\in\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
, we define the tangent space via right translation:

(4.6)

T_{(\varphi,\tau)}\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\Bigl\{(u\circ\varphi,\rho\circ\varphi):(u,\rho)\in T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}\Bigr\}.

A useful intrinsic characterization is due to Wunsch
[
27
, Lem. 3.1]
. For
\varphi\in AC({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
set

(4.7)

N_{\varphi}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}:\ \varphi_{x}(x)\ \text{exists and}\ \varphi_{x}(x)=0\}.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
27
, Lem. 3.1]
)
.

Let
(\varphi,\tau)\in\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
. Then
(U,F)\in AC({\mathbb{S}}^{1})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})
belongs to
T_{(\varphi,\tau)}\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

U(0)=0
and
U_{x}=0
a.e. on
N_{\varphi}
,

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\setminus N_{\varphi}}\left(\frac{U_{x}^{2}}{\varphi_{x}}+F^{2}\varphi_{x}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}x<\infty
.

Moreover, for
(U,F),(V,G)\in T_{(\varphi,\tau)}\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
the (right-invariant) metric is

(4.8)

\langle(U,F),(V,G)\rangle_{(\varphi,\tau)}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\setminus N_{\varphi}}\left(\frac{U_{x}V_{x}}{\varphi_{x}}+FG\,\varphi_{x}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

4.2.
Weak magnetic geodesics on
\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}

We now formulate the weak analogue of the magnetic geodesic flow on the relaxed space
\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
.

Definition 4.4

(Weak magnetic geodesic)
.

A curve
(\varphi,\tau)
with initial data
(u_{0},\rho_{0})\in T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
is called a
weak magnetic geodesic
of
(\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha)
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

The curve
(\varphi,\tau)
remains in
\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
for all
t\geq 0
; that is,

(4.9)

\bigl[x\mapsto(\varphi(t,x),\tau(t,x))\bigr]\in\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}\quad\forall t\in[0,\infty)\,.

(2)

The time derivative
(\varphi_{t},\tau_{t})
lies in the tangent space
T\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
for all
t\geq 0
; that is,

(4.10)

\bigl[x\mapsto(\varphi_{t}(t,x),\tau_{t}(t,x))\bigr]\in T_{(\varphi(t,\cdot),\tau(t,\cdot))}\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}\quad\forall t\in[0,\infty)\,.

(3)

The kinetic energy is constant almost everywhere in time; that is, for almost every
t\in[0,\infty)
,

(4.11)

\left\langle(\varphi_{t}(t),\tau_{t}(t)),(\varphi_{t}(t),\tau_{t}(t))\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{1},(\varphi,\tau)}=\left\langle(\varphi_{t}(0),\tau_{t}(0)),(\varphi_{t}(0),\tau_{t}(0))\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{1},(\mathrm{id},0)}\,.

Moreover, the contact angle is conserved almost everywhere; that is, for almost every
t\in[0,\infty)
,

(4.12)

\delta:=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\rho_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\tau_{t}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

(4)

For almost every
t\in[0,\infty)
, the curve
(\varphi,\tau)
satisfies the magnetic geodesic equation as given in
Section

2.2
, corresponding to the magnetic system
(G^{m},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha)
.

We further call
(\varphi,\tau)
a
unit speed weak magnetic geodesic
if the initial data satisfies

(4.13)

\left\langle(u_{0},\rho_{0}),(u_{0},\rho_{0})\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{1}}=1\,.

The next theorem provides an explicit global construction of weak magnetic geodesics on
\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
. Its proof relies on the fact that the Madelung transform is a magnetomorphism (cf. the corresponding result in the smooth category).

Theorem 4.5

(Global existence of weak magnetic flow)
.

Assume
(u_{0},\rho_{0})\in T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
satisfies
\langle(u_{0},\rho_{0}),(u_{0},\rho_{0})\rangle_{\dot{H}^{1}}=1
and define
\theta_{1/2}
by

(4.14)

\theta_{1/2}=\frac{s\pm\sqrt{s^{2}+4(1-s\delta)}}{2}.

For
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
set

(4.15)

\varphi(t,x)=\int_{0}^{x}\left|\frac{1}{\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}}\Bigl[\theta_{2}e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{1}t}-\theta_{1}e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{2}t}+\frac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\bigl(u_{0,x}(y)+{\mathrm{i}}\rho_{0}(y)\bigr)\bigl(e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{1}t}-e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{2}t}\bigr)\Bigr]\right|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}y,

and

(4.16)

\tau(t,x)=\rho_{0}(x)\int_{0}^{t}\chi_{\{\varphi_{x}(s,\cdot)>0\}}\,\frac{1}{\varphi_{x}(s,x)}\,{\mathrm{d}}s.

Then
(\varphi,\tau)
is a global unit speed weak magnetic geodesic on
(\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}},\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}},\varPhi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
.

Remark 4.6
.

Setting
s=0
in Theorem
4.5
recovers
[
27
, Thm. 4.1]
. Taking
\rho\equiv s
yields the corresponding statement for the Hunter–Saxton equation
[
17
, Thm. 4.1]
.

Remark 4.7
.

The construction extends verbatim to initial data with
\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}
-energy
c^{2}>0
, producing a weak magnetic geodesic of energy
c^{2}
.

4.3.
Global conservative weak solutions of (M2HS)

We now define the Eulerian notion of weak solution used in the sequel and show how it is induced by the weak magnetic flow.

The aim of this subsection is to derive, from the global existence of the weak magnetic flow established in
Theorem

4.5
, the global existence of a certain notion of global weak solution to (
M2HS
). To that end, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.8

(Global conservative weak solution)
.

A pair
(u,\rho)\colon[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called a
global conservative weak solution
of (
M2HS
) with initial data
(u_{0},\rho_{0})\in H^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})
if and only if the following hold:

(1)

For all
t\in[0,\infty)
, we have
x\mapsto u(t,x)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})
.

(2)

The map
u\colon[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous, and the initial conditions are satisfied pointwise and almost everywhere, respectively:

(4.17)

u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1},\qquad\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x)\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}.

(3)

The maps
t\mapsto u_{x}(t,\cdot)
and
t\mapsto\rho(t,\cdot)
belong to
L^{\infty}([0,\infty),L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}))
.

(4)

The map
t\mapsto u(t,\cdot)
is absolutely continuous from
[0,\infty)
into
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})
, and for almost every
t\in[0,\infty)
, the pair
(u(t,\cdot),\rho(t,\cdot))
satisfies the equation in
Section

2.2
evaluated at
(\mathrm{id},0)
; that is,

(4.18)

\begin{pmatrix}u_{t}+uu_{x}\\
\rho_{t}+u\rho_{x}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}A^{-1}\partial_{x}(u_{x}^{2}+\rho^{2})-s\cdot\int_{0}^{x}\left(\rho-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}x\\
-(\rho u)_{x}+su_{x}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{in }L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}}).

Remark 4.9
.

For
s=0
,

4.3
recovers the notion of a global conservative weak solution to the two-component Hunter–Saxton system, as defined in
[

Remark 4.10

For

[
17
]
.

Remark 4.11
.

The term

in
Section

4.3
is inspired by the notion of conservative weak solutions to the Hunter–Saxton equation introduced by Bressan–Constantin in
[

4.4.
Proof of
Theorem

4.5

We prove that the curve
(\varphi,\tau)
defined by (
4.15
) and (
4.16
)
is a weak magnetic geodesic in the sense of
Section

4.2
.
The proof is divided into four steps corresponding to conditions

(1)
–
(4)
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.5
.

(1)
:
(\varphi(t),\tau(t))\in\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
for all
t\geq 0
.

We first show that
x\mapsto\varphi(t,x)
belongs to
M_{AC}^{0}
for every
t\geq 0
.
By the fundamental theorem of calculus we have

(4.22)

\varphi(t,x)=\int_{0}^{x}\varphi_{x}(t,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y,\qquad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}.

Thus
x\mapsto\varphi(t,x)
is absolutely continuous for every
t\geq 0
by Lemma
A
.
Moreover, from (
4.15
) we directly obtain
\varphi_{x}(t,x)\geq 0
for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
,
hence
\varphi(t,\cdot)
is nondecreasing, and
\varphi(t,0)=0
.

To verify the endpoint constraint
\varphi(t,1)=1
, define

(4.23)

\gamma(t,y):=\frac{1}{\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}}\left[\theta_{2}e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{1}t}-\theta_{1}e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{2}t}+\frac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\bigl(u_{0,x}(y)+{\mathrm{i}}\rho_{0}(y)\bigr)\bigl(e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{1}t}-e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{2}t}\bigr)\right].

As explained in the construction,
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic on
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
and therefore satisfies

(4.24)

\|\gamma(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}=1\qquad\forall\,t\geq 0.

Since
\varphi_{x}(t,x)=|\gamma(t,x)|^{2}
, we infer

(4.25)

\varphi(t,1)=\int_{0}^{1}\varphi_{x}(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma(t,x)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\|\gamma(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=1.

Hence
\varphi(t,\cdot)\in M_{AC}^{0}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Finally, by the explicit definition (
4.16
) we have

(4.26)

\tau(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{R}})\qquad\forall\,t\geq 0.

Thus
(\varphi(t),\tau(t))\in\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Item

(2)
:
(\varphi_{t}(t),\tau_{t}(t))\in T_{(\varphi(t),\tau(t))}\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
for all
t\geq 0
.

We verify the characterization given in
Section

4.1
.
First we show

(4.27)

(\varphi_{t}(t,\cdot),\tau_{t}(t,\cdot))\in AC({\mathbb{S}}^{1})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})\qquad\forall\,t\geq 0.

Using
\varphi_{x}=|\gamma|^{2}
and differentiating under the integral sign, we obtain for all
t\geq 0

(4.28)

\varphi_{t}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{x}\left(\gamma(t,y)\,\overline{\dot{\gamma}(t,y)}+\overline{\gamma(t,y)}\,\dot{\gamma}(t,y)\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}y,\qquad x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}.

Hence
\varphi_{t}(t,\cdot)
is absolutely continuous by Lemma
A
. Moreover, from (
4.16
)
we have
\tau_{t}(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
.

Next we check condition
(1)
in
Section

4.1
.
Since
\varphi_{t}(t,0)=\varphi_{t}(t,1)
, (
4.28
) yields

(4.29)

\varphi_{t}(t,1)=2\langle\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Because
\dot{\gamma}(t)\in T_{\gamma(t)}{\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}}
, it is
L^{2}
-orthogonal to
\gamma(t)
, hence
\varphi_{t}(t,1)=0
,
and therefore
\varphi_{t}(t,0)=0
.

Let
N:=\{x:\varphi_{x}(t,x)=0\}
. Using the identity

(4.30)

\varphi_{tx}=(u_{x}\circ\varphi)\,\varphi_{x},

we conclude that
\varphi_{tx}=0
a.e. on
N
, which is precisely the degeneracy condition required in
Section

4.1
.

Finally, condition
Item

(2)
follows from the energy estimate proved in the Step checking
Item

(3)
below
(cf. (
4.32
)–(
4.35
)). Consequently,

(4.31)

(\varphi_{t}(t),\tau_{t}(t))\in T_{(\varphi(t),\tau(t))}\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}\qquad\forall\,t\geq 0.

Item

(3)
: Conservation of energy and contact angle (a.e. in
t
).

Let
N=\{x:\varphi_{x}(t,x)=0\}
. The
\dot{H}^{1}
-energy on
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\setminus N
is

(4.32)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\varphi(t),\tau(t))}\bigl((\varphi_{t},\tau_{t}),(\varphi_{t},\tau_{t})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\{\varphi_{x}>0\}}\left(\frac{\varphi_{tx}^{2}}{\varphi_{x}}+\tau_{t}^{2}\varphi_{x}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

Write
\gamma=f+{\mathrm{i}}g
with
f={\mathrm{Re}}\gamma
,
g={\mathrm{Im}}\gamma
. Then
\varphi_{x}=f^{2}+g^{2}
and
\{\varphi_{x}=0\}=\{f=0\}\cap\{g=0\}
.
A computation yields

(4.33)

\frac{1}{4}\int_{\{\varphi_{x}>0\}}\left(\frac{\varphi_{tx}^{2}}{\varphi_{x}}+\tau_{t}^{2}\varphi_{x}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{\{|\gamma|>0\}}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

Hence

(4.34)

\int_{\{|\gamma|>0\}}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x-\int_{\{f=0\}\cap\{g=0\}}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

Since the initial data are chosen unit speed,
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=1
, and thus

(4.35)

\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\varphi(t),\tau(t))}\bigl((\varphi_{t},\tau_{t}),(\varphi_{t},\tau_{t})\bigr)=1-\int_{\{f=0\}\cap\{g=0\}}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

It remains to show that the latter integral in (
4.35
) vanishes for a.e.
t\geq 0
.
This follows from the fact that for each fixed
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
, the function
t\mapsto\gamma(t,x_{0})
is a finite linear combination
of
e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{1}t}
and
e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{2}t}
, hence has only finitely many zeros on bounded intervals.
Applying Fubini’s theorem implies that the zero set has measure zero in space for a.e. time. Therefore the energy is conserved for a.e.
t
.

The contact angle identity

(4.36)

\delta=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\tau_{t}(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\rho_{0}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x

is immediate from the definition of
\tau
in (
4.16
).

Item

(4)
: Weak magnetic geodesic equation.

We show that
(\varphi,\tau)
satisfies the magnetic geodesic equation for a.e.
t\geq 0
.
Differentiating
\varphi
twice and using
\varphi_{x}=f^{2}+g^{2}
yields

(4.37)

\displaystyle\varphi_{tt}(t,x)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{x}\bigl(f_{t}^{2}+g_{t}^{2}+f_{tt}f+g_{tt}g\bigr)\,{\mathrm{d}}y.

Since
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic on
({\mathbb{S}_{L^{2}}^{\infty}},\mathcal{G}^{L^{2}},{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
, it satisfies

(4.38)

\ddot{\gamma}-s{\mathrm{i}}\dot{\gamma}+(1-s\delta)\gamma=0.

Equivalently,

(4.39)

f_{tt}+sg_{t}+(1-s\delta)f=0,\qquad g_{tt}-sf_{t}+(1-s\delta)g=0.

Substituting into (
4.37
) and using the identities

(4.40)

u=\varphi_{t}\circ\varphi^{-1},\qquad\rho=\tau_{t}\circ\varphi^{-1},

together with the transformation rule for monotone absolutely continuous maps
(Lemma
A
),
gives the weak formulation

(4.41)

\varphi_{tt}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\varphi(t,x)}(u_{x}^{2}+\rho^{2})\,{\mathrm{d}}y-s\int_{0}^{\varphi(t,x)}\rho\,{\mathrm{d}}y-2\varphi(t,x)(1-s\delta).

Comparing (
4.41
) with Proposition
2.2

yields the first component of the magnetic geodesic equation a.e. in time.

For the second component, differentiate the identity

(4.42)

\tau_{t}=2\,\frac{g_{t}f-f_{t}g}{f^{2}+g^{2}}

and use the ODE for
(f,g)
. This yields

(4.43)

\tau_{tt}=s\frac{\varphi_{xt}}{\varphi_{x}}-\tau_{t}\,\frac{\varphi_{xt}}{\varphi_{x}}.

Since
\varphi_{xt}=(u_{x}\circ\varphi)\varphi_{x}
and
\tau_{t}=\rho\circ\varphi
, we obtain

(4.44)

\rho_{t}\circ\varphi=(su_{x}-(\rho u)_{x})\circ\varphi,

which is exactly the second component of the magnetic geodesic equation. This completes the proof of
(4)
.

Combining Steps 1–4 shows that
(\varphi,\tau)
is a weak magnetic geodesic on
\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}
.

(u(t),\rho(t))\in T_{(\mathrm{id},0)}\mathcal{M}_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\{v\in H^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1}):v(0)=0\}\times L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
.
In particular,

for every

Next, using

and
\rho=\tau_{t}\circ\varphi^{-1}
together with right-invariance of

and the change-of-variables rule for absolutely continuous monotone maps

), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\mathrm{id},0)}((u(t),\rho(t)),(u(t),\rho(t)))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\left(u_{x}(t,x)^{2}+\rho(t,x)^{2}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\left(\frac{\varphi_{tx}(t,x)^{2}}{\varphi_{x}(t,x)}+\tau_{t}(t,x)^{2}\,\varphi_{x}(t,x)\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}x

(4.47)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\varphi(t),\tau(t))}\bigl((\varphi_{t}(t),\tau_{t}(t)),(\varphi_{t}(t),\tau_{t}(t))\bigr).

Since
(\varphi,\tau)
is a weak magnetic geodesic, its

-energy is conserved for a.e.
t
by

(3)
.
Therefore, for a.e.

(4.48)

\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\mathrm{id},0)}((u(t),\rho(t)),(u(t),\rho(t)))=\mathcal{G}^{\dot{H}^{1}}_{(\mathrm{id},0)}((u_{0},\rho_{0}),(u_{0},\rho_{0})),

which is exactly (
4.20
).

Item

(2)
: continuity of
u
and attainment of initial data.

We show that
u:[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous and that
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}
pointwise, while
\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0}
a.e.

Let
t_{n}\to t
and
y_{n}\to y
in
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. Since for each fixed
t

is nondecreasing and surjective,
we may choose
x_{n}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
with

(4.49)

\varphi(t_{n},x_{n})=y_{n}.

Then, by definition,

(4.50)

u(t_{n},y_{n})=u(t_{n},\varphi(t_{n},x_{n}))=\varphi_{t}(t_{n},x_{n}).

Any limit point of
(x_{n})
lies in the closed level set
\varphi(t,\cdot)^{-1}(\{y\})
.
Using the continuity of
(t,x)\mapsto\varphi_{t}(t,x)
and the fact that
\varphi_{t}(t,\cdot)
is constant on each level set

\varphi(t,\cdot)^{-1}(\{y\})
, we conclude that
u(t_{n},y_{n})\to u(t,y)
, i.e.

is continuous.

Moreover, since
\varphi(0,x)=x
and
\varphi_{t}(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
, we have

for all
x
.
For
\rho

and the definition of
\tau
in

4.5
, we compute for a.e.

,

\rho(0,x)=\tau_{t}(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x)\,\left.\frac{1}{\varphi_{x}(t,x)}\right|_{t=0}=\rho_{0}(x),

since
\varphi_{x}(0,x)=1
. This proves
(2)
.

Item

(3)
:

The energy identity (

(a.e.).
Thus it remains to justify measurability of the maps

Assume first

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}u_{x}(t,x)\Theta(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}u_{x}(t,\varphi(t,x))\,\Theta(\varphi(t,x))\,\varphi_{x}(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

(4.55)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}u_{x}(t,x)\Theta(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\varphi_{xt}(t,x)\,\Theta(\varphi(t,x))\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

In the notation of the proof of

4.5

The right-hand side depends continuously on

is continuous. Hence (
4.53
) is continuous for smooth
\Theta
.

For general

with

in

.
For each fixed

Therefore (
4.53
) is a pointwise limit of continuous maps, hence measurable.
This proves

The corresponding statement for

Lemma A.1
.

Let
f:[0,1]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a absolutely continuous function. Then
f
maps measurable sets into measurable sets and if
f_{x}
exists and equals
0
a.e. on a measurable set

then
\lambda(f(N))=0
, where

denotes the Lebesgue measure.

See
[
24
, Thm 7.20]
for a proof of the following lemma

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
\varphi:[0,1]\longrightarrow[0,1]
is absolutely continuous, non decreasing,
\varphi(0)=0,\varphi(1)=1
and
f\in L^{1}\left([0,1]\right)
. Then

(A.1)

\int_{0}^{1}f(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y=\int_{0}^{1}f(\varphi(x))\,\varphi_{x}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,.

The same conclusion holds if
f
is measurable and
f\geq 0
.

See
[
24
, Thm 7.18]
for a proof of the following lemma

Lemma A.3
.

Let
f:[a,b]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous and non decreasing. Each of the following three statements implies the other two

(1)

The function
f
is absolutely continuous.

(2)

The function
f
maps sets of measure
0
to sets of measure
0
.

(3)

The function
f
is differentiable a.e. on
I
,
f_{x}\in L^{1}([a,b])
and for all
x\in I
it holds that

(A.2)

f(x)=f(a)+\int_{a}^{x}f_{x}(y){\mathrm{d}}y\text{.}

See
[
25
, Chapter 3, Lemma 1.1.]
for a proof of the following lemma

Lemma A.4
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with dual
X^{\prime}
, and let
u,g\in L^{1}\left([0,T),X\right)
for some
T>0
. Then the following two conditions are equivalent

(1)

The function
u
is a.e. equal to a primitive function of
g
i.e. there exists a
F\in X
such that

(A.3)

u(t)=F+\int_{0}^{t}g(s){\mathrm{d}}s\quad\text{for a.e.}\,\,t\in[0,T)\,.

(2)

For each test function
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T))
it holds that:

(A.4)

\int_{0}^{T}u(t)\,\eta_{t}(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t=-\int_{0}^{T}g(t)\,\eta(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\,.

If
(1)
or
(2)
are satisfied, then

is a.e. equal to an absolutely continuous function

(A.5)

v:[0,T)\longrightarrow X\,.

For the sake of completeness we will state Pettis measurability theorem:

Theorem A.5
.

Let
(X,\sigma,\mu)
be a measure space and let
B
be a Banach space. Then

(1)

The function
f:X\longrightarrow B
is measurable if and only if it is weakly measurable and almost surely separably valued.

(2)

If
B
is separable Banach space then a function
f:X\longrightarrow B
is measurable if and only if it is weakly measurable.

Abstract
. For the classical Sturm-Liouville operators, we prove the sharp bounds for all nodes of eigenfunctions by regarding these nodes as nonlinear functionals of potential q ∈ L 1 ​ [ 0 , 1 ] q\in L^{1}[0,1] . By studying the optimization problems to minimize or to maximize the nodes { T i , m } \{T_{i,m}\} subject to the constraint ‖ q ‖ 1 = r \|q\|_{1}=r with r > 0 r>0 and using the strong continuity of the nodes in potentials, we obtain the explicit expressions for the sharp bounds, which are given as elementary functions.

1.
Introduction

The study of Sturm-Liouville problems was originally started from the seminal papers
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
by Sturm and Liouville in the 1830’s and now
it has become an important and wide branch in the theory of differential equations. Since the number of the literature is vast, here we only refer the reader to

[
22
]
for the historical account and the monographs
[
4
,
32
,
33
]
for detailed discussions. Let us consider the classical Sturm-Liouville

(1.1)

-y^{\prime\prime}+q(x)y=\lambda y,

with Dirichlet boundary condition

(1.2)

y(0)=y(1)=0,

where
q\in\mathcal{L}^{1}:=L^{1}[0,1]
is an integrable real potential.
It follows from the classical spectral theory
[
4
,
32
,
33
]
that problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) admits a sequence of real eigenvalues

\lambda_{m}=\lambda_{m}(q)
such that

\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}<\cdots<\lambda_{m}<\cdots,\qquad\lambda_{m}\to\infty\quad(m\to\infty),

and for each
\lambda_{m}
, there exists a corresponding real eigenfunction

E_{m}(x)=E_{m}(x;q),\qquad m=0,1,2,\cdots.

Without loss of generality, we normalize the eigenfunction
E_{m}(x)
such that

E^{\prime}_{m}(0)>0,\qquad\int_{0}^{1}E_{m}^{2}(x)dx=1.

It is well-known that
E_{0}(x)
has no zero in
(0,1)
and for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,\cdots\}
,
E_{m}(x)
has precisely
m
zeros

0<T_{1,m}<T_{2,m}<\cdots<T_{m,m}<1,

which are called the
nodes
of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).
Note that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{m}=\lambda_{m}(q)
, the eigenfunctions
E_{m}(x)=E_{m}(x;q)
and the nodes
T_{i,m}=T_{i,m}(q)
are
determined by the potential
q
in an implicit way. Such nodes
\left\{T_{i,m}\right\}
are significant physical quantities. For example,
in vibration dynamics, a node represents a position in the system where no vibration occurs. See
[
3
,
16
,
17
,
26
]
for
qualitative properties of nodes for second order ordinary differential operators. Moreover, the information of these nodes is important in the inverse spectral theory. See for example
[
8
,
18
,
19
,
23
]
.

The aim of this paper is to find the explicit sharp upper and lower bounds for all nodes of
problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) for given
L^{1}
norm of the potential
q\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}
.
There are already many results on sharp bounds for eigenvalues, eigenvalues ratio or eigenvalues gap for different kinds of differential operators in the literature, see for example

[
1
,
2
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
20
,
30
,
34
]
. Such optimization problems have been attractive and interesting
in mathematical analysis, especially in spectral theory and the theory of differential equations. Such a topic was originally started
from the pioneering work of Krein
[
21
]
, where he studied the sharp estimates for Dirichlet eigenvalues of

y^{\prime\prime}+\lambda q(x)y=0

by restricting that
q\in L^{\infty}[0,1],0\leq q\leq h,\int_{0}^{1}q(x)dx=r
with
0<r\leq h.

However, as far as we know, up to now there are only few results
[
5
,
7
]
on sharp bounds of nodes for ordinary differential operators.
For given
L^{p}
norms of the potentials
q\in L^{p}[0,1]
with
1<p<\infty
, it was obtained in a recent work
[
7
]
the optimal
characterizations of locations for all nodes of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). In particular, by regarding the
i
th node
of the
m
th eigenfunction as a functional of the potential, the critical equations to determine the minimizing potential
are deduced so that the node is minimized, and from the critical equations, two equivalent characterizations as nonlinear
systems for
4
-dimensional or
2
-dimensional parameters have been proved. Theoretically speaking, by taking the limit
p\downarrow 1
of the results in
[
7
]
, we can obtain the results on sharp bounds for the nodes when the
L^{1}
norms of the potentials are given. However,
it is very difficult because the explicit expressions of the sharp bounds cannot be obtained in
[
7
]
and the results in
[
7
]
contain several singular integrals. Besides, we are not able to obtain the critical equations when the
L^{1}
-norms of the potentials are fixed, because the space
\mathcal{L}^{1}
lacks local compactness even in the weak topology. For these reasons, in this paper we aim to develop a different method to work out the explicit expressions for the sharp bounds of all nodes.

Now we state our optimization problems. For
r\in(0,\infty)
, let

B_{r}:=\left\{q\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}:\|q\|_{1}\leq r\right\}
and
S_{r}:=\left\{q\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}:\|q\|_{1}=r\right\}

be the ball and the sphere in
{\mathcal{L}}^{1}
with center
0
and radius
r
, respectively. For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq i\leq m
, we consider
the following optimization problems on nodes

(1.3)

{\bf m}_{i,m}(r):=\inf_{q\in B_{r}}T_{i,m}(q)\equiv\inf_{q\in S_{r}}T_{i,m}(q),

(1.4)

{\bf n}_{i,m}(r):=\sup_{q\in B_{r}}T_{i,m}(q)\equiv\sup_{q\in S_{r}}T_{i,m}(q).

The reflection
x\mapsto 1-x
leads to

{\bf n}_{i,m}(r)=\sup_{q\in B_{r}}T_{i,m}(q)=1-\inf_{q\in B_{r}}T_{m-i+1,m}(q)=1-{\bf m}_{m-i+1,m}(r).

It is obvious that the solutions of (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) can lead to the following optimal estimates on the locations of nodes

{\bf m}_{i,m}(\|q\|_{1})\leq T_{i,m}(q)\leq 1-{\bf m}_{m-i+1,m}(\|q\|_{1}),\quad q\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}.

However, it is difficult to solve the optimization problem (
1.3
). To achieve our aim and to obtain the sharp bounds for all nodes of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), we need three known results on the eigenvalues

\{\lambda_{m}\}
and related eigenfunctions
\{E_{m}(x)\}
. The first known result is related to optimal estimates of eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{m}\}
,
proved by Wei, Meng and Zhang in
[
30
, Theorem 1.3 and Theorem 1.5]
, which will be stated as Lemma 2.5.
The second known result is about a strong continuity of
\{\lambda_{m}\}
and the nodes
\left\{T_{i,m}\right\}
related to the potentials. That
is, the functionals

(\mathcal{L}^{1},w_{1})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}:\quad q\rightarrow\lambda_{m}(q),\quad q\rightarrow T_{i,m}(q)

are continuous, where
w_{1}
is the topology of weak convergence in the space
(\mathcal{L}^{1},\|\cdot\|_{1})
.
Thirdly, some basic facts of measure differential equations are needed because the solution of the related optimization problems
(
1.3
) and (
1.4
) will lead to more general distributions of potentials
which have no densities with respect to the Lebesgue measure, and thus it is a very natural way to
understand such problems in the setting of measure differential equations. All of the above three known results will be presented as preliminary
results in Section 2. In Section 3, we first trickily reduce the infinite-dimensional minimization problem (
1.3
) to a finite-dimensional
optimization problem (
3.28
). Then we obtain the solutions of the finite-dimensional minimization problem (
3.28
) by delicate analysis, and finally
we show that the solutions for the minimization problem (
3.28
) are just those for our minimization problem (
1.3
), based on the
strong continuity of the nodes
\left\{T_{i,m}\right\}
related to the potentials.

2.
Preliminary results

In this section, we present some preliminary known results on eigenvalues and nodes, including
sharp bounds on eigenvalues, scaling equalities for nodes and some facts on measure
differential equations.

2.1.
Measure differential equations

We need some results on eigenvalues and nodes of linear measure differential equations
[
24
,
25
,
35
]
. As usual, we use
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}={\mathcal{M}}_{0}(I):=(C(I),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})^{*}
to denote the space of (normalized Radon) measures on
I=[0,1]
. Besides the usual topology induced by the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\bf V}
of total variations,
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
also has the weak
∗
topology
w^{*}
which is defined by using the weak
∗
convergence for measures. In the
w^{*}
topology, the following conclusion holds.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
24
]
Let
\mu_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0}.
Then there exists a sequence of smooth measures
\{\mu_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{0}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
such that
\|{\mu_{n}}\|_{\bf V}=\|{\mu_{0}}\|_{\bf V}
and

\mu_{n}\to\mu_{0}\mbox{ in $({\mathcal{M}}_{0},w^{*})$}.

Given a measure
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
. We consider the eigenvalue problem of the corresponding measure differential equations

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\,{\rm d}y^{\bullet}(x)+y(x)\,{\rm d}\mu(x)=\lambda y(x)\,{\rm d}x,\\
y(0)=0,\quad y(1)=0.\end{array}\right.

The structure of eigenvalues of problem (
2.1
) is similar to that of ordinary differential equations, i.e. problem (
2.1
) admits a real sequence of eigenvalues

\lambda_{0}(\mu)<\lambda_{1}(\mu)<\cdots<\lambda_{m}(\mu)<\cdots,\qquad\lambda_{m}(\mu)\to+\infty\quad(m\to\infty).

See
[
25
,
35
]
for detailed discussions. Associated with each
\lambda_{m}(\mu)
, let
E_{m}(x;\mu)
be a normalized eigenfunction of problem (
2.1
) such that
E_{m}(x;\mu)
has the
L^{2}
norm
1
and is positive for
x
in some pinched neighborhood
(0,\delta)
. For each
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
E_{m}(x;\mu)
admits
m
nodes

0<T_{1,m}(\mu)<T_{2,m}(\mu)<\cdots<T_{m,m}(\mu)<1.

Moreover, we have the following strong continuity by regarding such nodes as functionals in the measure
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
16
,
17
]

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq i\leq m
be given. By regarding the node
T_{i,m}(\mu)
of problem
(
2.1
)
as a functional from
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
to
(0,1)\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, it admits a strong continuity in the following sense:
T_{i,m}(\mu_{n})\to T_{i,m}(\mu)
if

\mu_{n}\to\mu
in
({\mathcal{M}}_{0},w^{*})
. In particular, the node
T_{i,m}(q)
for problem
(
1.1
)-(
1.2
)
are continuous in potential
q\in(\mathcal{L}^{1},w_{1})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
15
]

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq i\leq m
be given. Then
T_{i,m}(q)
is continuously Fréchet differentiable in
q\in({\mathcal{L}}^{1},\|\cdot\|_{1})
.
Moreover, the Fréchet derivative is given as

(2.2)

\partial_{q}T_{i,m}(q)(x)=H_{i,m}(x;q)E_{m}^{2}(x;q)\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty},

where
H_{i,m}(x;q)
is the two-step function

H_{i,m}(x;q)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}+a_{i,m},&x\in[0,T_{i,m}(q)),\\
-b_{i,m},&x\in[T_{i,m}(q),1],\end{array}\right.

and the two constants
a_{i,m}=a_{i,m}(q),~b_{i,m}=b_{i,m}(q)
are given as

a_{i,m}=\int_{[T_{i,m}(q),1]}\left(\frac{E_{m}(v;q)}{E^{\prime}_{m}(T_{i,m}(q);q)}\right)^{2}\,{\rm d}v,\quad b_{i,m}=\int_{[0,T_{i,m}(q)]}\left(\frac{E_{m}(v;q)}{E^{\prime}_{m}(T_{i,m}(q);q)}\right)^{2}\,{\rm d}v.

The following result on
{\bf m}_{i,m}(r)
can be easily obtained from Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
r\in(0,\infty)
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq i\leq m
, it holds that

{\bf m}_{i,m}(r)\in\left(0,i/(m+1)\right).

Proof
For the zero potential, one has
T_{i,m}(0)=i/(m+1)
. Let us choose a perturbed potential

q_{t}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-t,&\mbox{for }x\in[0,i/(m+1)],\\
0,&\mbox{for }x\in(i/(m+1),1],\end{array}\right.

where
0<t\ll 1
. Then
q_{t}\in B_{r}
and it follows from the formula (
2.2
) that

\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}T_{i,m}(q_{t})=-\int_{[0,i/(m+1)]}a_{i,m}E_{m}^{2}(x;0)<0.

Thus
T_{i,m}(q_{t})<i/(m+1)
for
0<t\ll 1
. Therefore
{\bf m}_{i,m}(r)<i/(m+1)
.

On the other hand, let
\{q_{n}\}\subset S_{r}
be a minimizing sequence such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{i,m}(q_{n})={\bf m}_{i,m}(r).

Let
\{q_{n}\}
be the densities of the absolutely continuous measures

(2.3)

\mu_{q_{n}}(x)=\int_{[0,x]}q_{n}(t)\,{\rm d}t,\quad x\in I.

Then
\|\mu_{q_{n}}\|_{\bf V}=r
. By the Banach theorem, without loss of generality, we can assume that
\{\mu_{q_{n}}\}
has a weak
∗
limit measure
\mu_{r}
. Thus it follows from Lemma
2.2
that

{\bf m}_{i,m}(r)=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{i,m}(q_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{i,m}(\mu_{q_{n}})=T_{i,m}(\mu_{r}).

In particular,
{\bf m}_{i,m}(r)=T_{i,m}(\mu_{r})>0
.

\Box

2.2.
Known results on eigenvalues and nodes

Let
r\in(0,\infty)
and
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
be given. Consider the following optimization problems related to the eigenvalues

(2.4)

{\bf L}_{m}(r):=\inf_{q\in S_{r}}\lambda_{m}(q)=\inf_{q\in B_{r}}\lambda_{m}(q),\quad{\bf M}_{m}(r):=\sup_{q\in S_{r}}\lambda_{m}(q)=\sup_{q\in B_{r}}\lambda_{m}(q).

Lemma 2.5
.

[
30
]

Let
r\in(0,\infty)
be given. Then

{\bf L}_{0}(r)=\lambda_{0}\left(-r\delta_{1/2}\right)={\bf Z}^{-1}(r),\qquad{\bf M}_{0}(r)=\lambda_{0}\left(\eta_{r}\right)={\bf Y}(r),

where
{\bf Z}
is a decreasing diffeomorphism from
\left(-\infty,\pi^{2}\right]
to
[0,+\infty)
defined by

(2.5)

{\bf Z}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2\sqrt{-x}\operatorname{coth}(\sqrt{-x}/2)&\text{ for }x\in(-\infty,0),\\
4&\text{ for }x=0,\\
2\sqrt{x}\cot(\sqrt{x}/2)&\text{ for }x\in(0,\pi^{2}],\end{array}\right.

and
{\bf Y}:\left[0,+\infty\right)\rightarrow\left[\pi^{2},+\infty\right)
is an increasing diffeomorphism given as

(2.6)

{\bf Y}(x)=\left(\frac{\pi+\sqrt{\pi^{2}+4x}}{2}\right)^{2},

where
\kappa\delta_{a}
denotes the Dirac measure of mass
\kappa
at point
a
and
\eta_{r}
is the potential defined as

\eta_{r}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{Y}(r),&x\in[\tau_{r},1-\tau_{r}],\\
0,&x\in[0,\tau_{r})\cup(1-\tau_{r},1],\end{array}\right.

with the constant

\tau_{r}=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\mathbf{Y}(r)}}\in\left(0,1/2\right).

Moreover, for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have the following the dilation relations

(2.7)

{\bf L}_{m}(r)=(m+1)^{2}{\bf L}_{0}\left(\frac{r}{(m+1)^{2}}\right)=(m+1)^{2}{\bf Z}^{-1}\left(\frac{r}{(m+1)^{2}}\right)=\lambda_{m}(\mu_{m,r}),

(2.8)

{\bf M}_{m}(r)=(m+1)^{2}{\bf M}_{0}\left(\frac{r}{(m+1)^{2}}\right)=(m+1)^{2}{\bf Y}\left(\frac{r}{(m+1)^{2}}\right)=\lambda_{m}(\eta_{m,r}),

where

(2.9)

\mu_{m,r}(x)=-\frac{r}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\delta_{\frac{2k+1}{2(m+1)}}(x),

and

(2.10)

\eta_{m,r}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(m+1)^{2}\mathbf{Y}({r}/{(m+1)^{2}}),&x\in[\frac{\tau_{m,r}+k}{m+1},\frac{1-\tau_{m,r}+k}{m+1}],\ k={0,1,\cdots,m},\\
0,&\mbox{ elsewhere},\end{array}\right.

with

\tau_{m,r}\equiv\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\mathbf{Y}({r}/{(m+1)^{2}})}}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right).

The above conclusions can be extended from the interval
[0,1]
to any interval
[x_{1},x_{2}]
with
x_{2}-x_{1}=T>0.

Indeed, suppose
r>0
and

B_{r,T}:=\left\{q\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}(x_{1},x_{2}):\|q\|_{1,[x_{1},x_{2}]}\leq r\right\}.

Let
\lambda_{m}(q,T)
be the lowest eigenvalues of equation (
1.1
) with Dirichlet boundary condition

y(x_{1})=y(x_{2})=0.

By the similar arguments, we can solve the corresponding optimization problems as follows

(2.11)

{\bf L}_{m}(r,T):=\inf\Big\{\lambda_{m}(q,T):\mu\in B_{r,T}\Big\}=\lambda_{m}\Big(\mu_{m,r}(\frac{x-x_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}})\Big)=\frac{(m+1)^{2}}{T^{2}}{\bf Z}^{-1}\left(\frac{Tr}{(m+1)^{2}}\right),

(2.12)

{\bf M}_{m}(r,T):=\sup\Big\{\lambda_{m}(q,T):\mu\in B_{r,T}\Big\}=\lambda_{m}\Big(\eta_{m,r}(\frac{x-x_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}})\Big)=\frac{(m+1)^{2}}{T^{2}}{\bf Y}\left(\frac{Tr}{(m+1)^{2}}\right).

2.3.
Scaling equalities for nodes and eigenvalues

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq i\leq m
be given. For a potential
q\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}
, we have the node
T=T_{i,m}(q)\in(0,1)
. By using
T
to cut the interval
[0,1]
into two subintervals
[0,T]
and
[T,1]
, we obtain the following two Dirichlet eigenvalue problems on subintervals

(2.13)

-E_{m}^{\prime\prime}(x)+q(x)E_{m}(x)=\lambda_{m}(q)E_{m}(x),\qquad E_{m}(0)=E_{m}(T)=0,

(2.14)

-E_{m}^{\prime\prime}(x)+q(x)E_{m}(x)=\lambda_{m}(q)E_{m}(x),\qquad E_{m}(T)=E_{m}(1)=0.

By using the scaling
x\mapsto Tx
and the scaling
x\mapsto T+(1-T)x
for (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) respectively, we obtain the following two Dirichlet eigenvalue problems

(2.15)

-\hat{y}^{\prime\prime}(x)+\hat{q}(x)\hat{y}(x)=T^{2}\lambda_{m}(q)\hat{y}(x),\qquad\hat{y}(0)=\hat{y}(1)=0,

(2.16)

-\check{y}^{\prime\prime}(x)+\check{q}(x)\check{y}(x)=(1-T)^{2}\lambda_{m}(q)\check{y}(x),\qquad\check{y}(0)=\check{y}(1)=0,

where

(2.17)

\hat{q}(x):=T^{2}q(Tx)\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1},\qquad\check{q}(x):=(1-T)^{2}q(T+(1-T)x)\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1},

\hat{y}(x)=\hat{y}(x;q):=E_{m}(Tx),\qquad\check{y}(x)=\check{y}(x;q):=E_{m}\left(T+(1-T)x\right).

Since
\hat{y}(x)
has exactly
i-1
interior zeros in
[0,1]
and
\check{y}(x)
has exactly
m-i
interior zeros, we can deduce from (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) that
\hat{q}
and
\check{q}
have Dirichlet eigenvalues

(2.18)

\lambda_{i-1}\left(\hat{q}\right)=T^{2}\lambda_{m}(q),\qquad\lambda_{m-i}\left(\check{q}\right)=(1-T)^{2}\lambda_{m}(q),

which can produce the following scaling equality between nodes and eigenvalues

(2.19)

\frac{\lambda_{i-1}(\hat{q})}{T^{2}}=\frac{\lambda_{m-i}\left(\check{q}\right)}{(1-T)^{2}}.

Note that the two equalities in (
2.18
) are the usual scaling equalities for Dirichlet eigenvalues.

It is easy to see that the converse of the above construction is also true. Thus we have obtained the following result.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq i\leq m
be given. Then for any
q\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}
, one has

\left(T,\hat{q},\check{q}\right)=(T_{i,m}(q),\hat{q}(\cdot;q),\check{q}(\cdot;q)),

which satisfies the scaling equality
(
2.19
).
Conversely, let
\hat{q}
,
\check{q}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}
and
T\in(0,1)
satisfy the scaling equality
(
2.19
).
Then there must be a potential
q\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}
such that

(T_{i,m}(q),\hat{q}(\cdot;q),\check{q}(\cdot;q))=\left(T,\hat{q},\check{q}\right).

3.
Main results

In this section, we deduce the infinite-dimensional minimization problem (
1.3
) to a finite-dimensional optimization problem. Then
we prove the solutions of the finite-dimensional problem and finally show that such solutions are just those of our infinite-dimensional minimization problem (
1.3
).

3.1.
A related finite-dimensional optimization problem

For given
r\in(0,\infty),~m\in{\mathbb{N}},~1\leq i\leq m,
we consider any integrable potential
q\in S_{r}
such that the node
T=T_{i,m}(q)\in(0,i/(m+1))
.
Let us introduce two non-negative parameters

\hat{r}=\hat{r}(q):=\|q\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{1}{[0,T]}},\quad\check{r}=\check{r}(q):=\|q\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{1}{[T,1]}}.

It is trivial that

(3.1)

\hat{r}+\check{r}=r.

Moreover, by the equalities (
2.17
) for scaling potentials
\hat{q}
and
\check{q}
, one has

\|\hat{q}\|_{1}=T\hat{r},\qquad\|\check{q}\|_{1}=(1-T)\check{r}.

From the optimization problems (
2.4
) on eigenvalues, one has

\lambda_{i-1}(\hat{q})\geq{\bf L}_{i-1}(\|\hat{q}\|_{1})={\bf L}_{i-1}(T\hat{r}),\quad\lambda_{m-i}(\check{q})\leq{\bf M}_{m-i}(\|\check{q}\|_{1})={\bf M}_{m-i}((1-T)\check{r}).

It follows from the scaling result (
2.19
) that we have the following inequality

(3.2)

\frac{{\bf L}_{i-1}(T\hat{r})}{T^{2}}\leq\frac{{\bf M}_{m-i}\left((1-T)\check{r}\right)}{(1-T)^{2}},

which contains three quantities
(\hat{r},\check{r},T)
. Motivated by (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we introduce the following function

(3.3)

f(T,\hat{r}):={\bf L}_{i-1}\left(T\hat{r}\right)-\left(\frac{T}{1-T}\right)^{2}{\bf M}_{m-i}\Big((1-T)(r-\hat{r})\Big),\quad(T,\hat{r})\in{\mathcal{D}}:=(0,i/(m+1))\times[0,r].

Next we focus on the problem of the implicit function
f(T,\hat{r})=0

Lemma 3.1
.

Given
r\in(0,\infty),~m\in{\mathbb{N}},~1\leq i\leq m.
Then there exists a unique
C^{1}
function

(3.5)

T={\mathcal{T}}(\hat{r})={\mathcal{T}}_{i,m,r}(\hat{r}):[0,r]\to(0,i/(m+1)),

such that

(3.6)

{\bf L}_{i-1}\left({\mathcal{T}}(\hat{r})\hat{r}\right)=\left(\frac{{\mathcal{T}}(\hat{r})}{1-{\mathcal{T}}(\hat{r})}\right)^{2}{\bf M}_{m-i}\Big((1-{\mathcal{T}}(\hat{r}))(r-\hat{r})\Big),\quad\hat{r}\in[0,r].

Proof
For any fixed
\hat{r}\in[0,r]
, one has

\lim_{T\downarrow 0}f(T,\hat{r})={\bf L}_{i-1}\left(0\right)=(i\pi)^{2}>0,

and

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{i}{m+1},\hat{r}\right)\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!{\bf L}_{i-1}\left(\frac{i\hat{r}}{m+1}\right)-\frac{\left(\frac{i}{m+1}\right)^{2}}{\left(1-\frac{i}{m+1}\right)^{2}}{\bf M}_{m-i}\left(\frac{i(r-\hat{r})}{m+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!(i\pi)^{2}\left(\frac{{\bf L}_{i-1}\left(\frac{i\hat{r}}{m+1}\right)}{(i\pi)^{2}}-\frac{{\bf M}_{m-i}\left(\frac{i(r-\hat{r})}{m+1}\right)}{((m-i+1)\pi)^{2}}\right).

By the definition of the optimization problems (
2.4
), one has

\frac{{\bf L}_{i-1}\left(\frac{i\hat{r}}{m+1}\right)}{(i\pi)^{2}}\leq 1,\qquad\frac{{\bf M}_{m-i}\left(\frac{i(r-\hat{r})}{m+1}\right)}{((m-i+1)\pi)^{2}}\geq 1.

Note that at least one of the inequalities above is strict because at least one of
\{\hat{r},\check{r}\}
is not zero. Hence

f(i/(m+1),\hat{r})<0.

Therefore there exists at least one solution
T={\mathcal{T}}(\hat{r})={\mathcal{T}}_{i,m,r}(\hat{r})\in(0,i/(m+1))
such that

f({\mathcal{T}}(\hat{r}),\hat{r})=0.

To prove the uniqueness, now we show that

(3.7)

\frac{\partial}{\partial T}f(T,\hat{r})<0.

In fact, it follows from the dilation relations in (
2.5
) and (
2.7
)-(
2.8
) that

\displaystyle{\bf L}_{i-1}(T\hat{r})\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!i^{2}{\bf Z}^{-1}\left({T\hat{r}}/{i^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial T}{\bf L}_{i-1}(T\hat{r})\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\hat{r}\cdot\left({\bf Z}^{-1}\right)^{\prime}\left({T\hat{r}}/{i^{2}}\right)\leq 0,

since the function
{\bf Z}
is decreasing. Moreover,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!{\bf M}_{m-i}\Big((1-T)(r-\hat{r})\Big)=(m-i+1)^{2}{\bf Y}\left(\frac{(1-T)(r-\hat{r})}{(m-i+1)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\left(\frac{(m-i+1)\pi+\sqrt{((m-i+1)\pi)^{2}+4(r-\hat{r})(1-T)}}{2}\right)^{2}.

Consider

\displaystyle g(T,\hat{r})\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sqrt{\left(\frac{T}{1-T}\right)^{2}{\bf M}_{m-i}\Big((1-T)(r-\hat{r})\Big)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\frac{1}{2}\frac{T}{1-T}\left((m-i+1)\pi+\sqrt{((m-i+1)\pi)^{2}+4(r-\hat{r})(1-T)}\right).

Then for all
(T,\hat{r})\in{\mathcal{D}}
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial T}g(T,\hat{r})=\frac{1}{2(1-T)^{2}}\left((m-i+1)\pi+\frac{((m-i+1)\pi)^{2}+2(r-\hat{r})(1-T)(2-T)}{\sqrt{((m-i+1)\pi)^{2}+4(r-\hat{r})(1-T)}}\right)>0.

As a consequence,

\frac{\partial}{\partial T}f(T,\hat{r})=\frac{\partial}{\partial T}{\bf L}_{i-1}(T\hat{r})-2g(T,\hat{r})\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial T}g(T,\hat{r})<0.

Thus (
3.7
) has been verified. In particular, when
\hat{r}\in[0,r]
is fixed,
f(T,\hat{r})
is strictly decreasing in
T\in(0,1)
. Hence
we have proved the uniqueness of the implicit function
{\mathcal{T}}_{i,m,r}(\hat{r})
satisfying (
3.6
).
Finally, due to (
3.7
) and by the implicit function theorem,
{\mathcal{T}}_{i,m,r}(\hat{r})
is smooth in
\hat{r}
.
\Box

{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(t)|_{t=\pi/2}>{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(t)|_{t=\sigma}
when
\sigma<\underline{t}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(t)|_{t=\pi/2}<{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(t)|_{t=\sigma}
when
\sigma>\underline{t}
. Besides, they take the equal sign at the same time. As
\sigma,\ \underline{t}\in(0,t_{1})
and
P_{*}(t)
is strictly decreasing in
t\in(0,t_{1})
, we know that
\underline{t}-\sigma
has the same sign with
P_{*}(\sigma)-P_{*}(\underline{t})=r-\underline{r}
.

Finally, we show that
\underline{r}\in\left(r_{0},P\left(t_{0}\right)\right)\subset\left(P\left(t_{1}\right),P\left(t_{0}\right)\right)
, where
r_{0}
is as in (
3.49
). Otherwise if
\underline{r}\leq r_{0}
, from the assumption
r_{0}<r<P\left(t_{0}\right)
one has
r>\underline{r}
. Then there hold
\underline{t}>\sigma
and

{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(t)|_{t=\pi/2}>{\mathcal{T}}_{r}(t)|_{t=\sigma}.

From the discussion above and the fact that
{\mathcal{T}}_{r}
and
\sigma
are continuous in
r
, one can deduce that

{\mathcal{T}}_{r_{0}+\epsilon}(t)|_{t=\pi/2}<{\mathcal{T}}_{r_{0}+\epsilon}(t)|_{t=\sigma}

when
\epsilon>0
is small enough. This is a contradiction. In a similar way, we can show that

\underline{r}\geq P\left(t_{0}\right)
is also impossible.
\Box

Combined Lemma
3.5
, Lemma
3.11
and Lemma
3.12
, we have proved the following result.

3.2.
Solutions for the minimization problem (
1.3
)

For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
1\leq i\leq m
,
\hat{r},\ \check{r}\geq 0
and
T\in(0,1)
. Let us introduce the measure

\gamma_{i,m,\hat{r},\check{r},T}
as

\displaystyle\gamma_{i,m,\hat{r},\check{r},T}(x)\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mu_{i-1,\hat{r}}(\frac{x}{T}),\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad x\in[0,T],\\
-\hat{r}+\frac{1}{1-T}\int_{T}^{x}\eta_{m-i,\check{r}}(\frac{t-T}{1-T})\,{\rm d}t,\quad x\in(T,1],\end{array}\right.

where
\mu_{i-1,\hat{r}}
and
\eta_{m-i,\check{r}}
are given as in (
2.9
) and in (
2.10
), respectively.
Note that the constraint of the measure
\gamma_{i,m,\hat{r},\check{r},T}(x)
on
[T,1]
is actually the scaling measure
\mu_{\eta_{m-i,\check{r}}}(\frac{x-T}{1-T})
obtained by (
2.3
).

Lemma 3.14
.

For
r\in(0,\infty)
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}},~1\leq i\leq m
and define the measure

\gamma_{i,m,r}(x)
as

\gamma_{i,m,r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma_{i,m,r,0,T_{i,m,r}},&i\leq\frac{m+1}{2},\\
\gamma_{i,m,r,0,T_{i,m,r}},&i>\frac{m+1}{2},r\in[\overline{r}_{i,m},\infty),\\
\gamma_{i,m,\hat{r}(\sigma),r-\hat{r}(\sigma),T_{i,m,r}},&i>\frac{m+1}{2},r\in(\underline{r}_{i,m},\overline{r}_{i,m}),\\
\gamma_{i,m,0,r,T_{i,m,r}}\mbox{ or }\gamma_{i,m,\hat{r}(\sigma),r-\hat{r}(\sigma),T_{i,m,r}},&i>\frac{m+1}{2},r=\underline{r}_{i,m},\\
\gamma_{i,m,0,r,T_{i,m,r}},&i>\frac{m+1}{2},r\in(0,\underline{r}_{i,m}).\end{array}\right.

Then
T_{i,m,r}=T_{i,m}\left(\gamma_{i,m,r}\right).

Proof
By (
2.11
),(
2.12
) and (
3.4
), we have

\lambda_{m}\left(\gamma_{i,m,r}(x)|[0,1]\right)=\lambda_{i-1}\left(\gamma_{i,m,r}(x)|[0,T_{i,m,r}]\right)=\lambda_{m-i}\left(\gamma_{i,m,r}(x)|[T_{i,m,r},1]\right).

Hence,
T_{i,m,r}=T_{i,m}\left(\gamma_{i,m,r}\right).

\Box

Lemma 3.15
.

For
r\in(0,\infty)
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
1\leq i\leq m
and
T_{i,m,r}
as above. Then

{\bf m}_{i,m}(r)=T_{i,m,r}.

Proof
For any potential
q\in S_{r}
, we have constructed two parameters
\hat{r},\ \check{r}
as in (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) such that the node
T=T_{i,m}(q)
satisfies the inequality (
3.2
). By using the function
f(t,\hat{r})
defined in (
3.3
),

f(T_{i,m}(q),\hat{r})\leq 0=f({\mathcal{T}}_{i,m,r}(\hat{r}),\hat{r}).

Since
f(t,\hat{r})
is decreasing in
t
, we obtain

T_{i,m}(q)\geq{\mathcal{T}}_{i,m,r}(\hat{r}).

Hence
T_{i,m}(q)\geq T_{i,m,r}
for each
q\in S_{r}
. Therefore
{\bf m}_{i,m}(r)=\inf_{q\in S_{r}}T_{i,m}(q)\geq T_{i,m,r}
.
\Box

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.1
, there exists a sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{0}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}

such that
q_{n}=\mu_{n}^{\prime}\in B_{r}
and

\mu_{n}\to\gamma_{i,m,r}{\rm~in}~({\mathcal{M}}_{0},w^{*}),\quad n\to+\infty.

By the continuity of
T_{i,m}(\mu)
in the measure
\mu
, we obtain that

T_{i,m}(\gamma_{i,m,r})=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{i,m}(\mu_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{i,m}(q_{n})\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}{\bf m}_{i,m}(r)={\bf m}_{i,m}(r).

Thus
{\bf m}_{i,m}(r)\leq T_{i,m,r}
by Lemma
3.14
. As a consequence, the result has been proved.

\Box

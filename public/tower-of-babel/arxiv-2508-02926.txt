GrandJury: A Collaborative Machine Learning Model Evaluation Protocol for Dynamic Quality Rubrics
Arthur Cho

Abstract
Generative Machine Learning models have become central to modern systems, powering applications in creative writing, summarization, multi-hop reasoning, and context-aware dialogue [ 2 ] . These models underpin large-scale AI assistants, workflow automation, and autonomous decision-making [ 15 , 20 ] . In such domains, “acceptable response” is rarely absolute or static, but plural and highly context-dependent [ 3 ] . Yet standard evaluation regimes still rely on static, benchmark-style tests, incentivizing optimization toward leaderboard scores rather than alignment with dynamic user needs or evolving realities [ 6 ] . GrandJury introduces a formal evaluation protocol—combining time-decayed aggregation, complete traceability, with the support of dynamic, transparent task rubric attribution, and multi-rater human judgment. Together, these elements enable pluralistic, accountable evaluation that captures evolving consensus and surfaces disagreement. We provide (i) an open-source implementation ( grandjury PyPI) and (ii) a public collection of Large Language Model (LLM) inference outputs to illustrate the need and method. GrandJury provides a new paradigm for AI practitioners when evaluating machine learning outputs without absolute ground truth . Patent pending: US Provisional 63/825,484, filed 17 June 2025.

Evaluation in the Absence of Certainty

2.1
The Limits of Metric-Based Evaluation and the Birth of Vibe Evaluation

Evaluating generative AI fundamentally differs from closed, deterministic tasks.
Open-ended outputs introduce further tension: inter-annotator agreement is often low (
\kappa<0.3
)
[

2.2
Attempts at Automated Proxy Evaluation and Their Limitations

To address evaluation scale challenges, several automated proxies have been proposed:

•

RAGAS:
Reference-free evaluation metrics for RAG systems—covering retrieval quality, faithfulness, and context relevance. These metrics are introduced without explicit ground-truth references, but are validated and optimized against human-labeled benchmarks, embedding ground truth indirectly in their design

•

LLM-as-Judge frameworks
rely on models (e.g., GPT-4 or Claude) to rate outputs. While attractive for scalability, studies reveal significant issues: evaluator bias, prompt sensitivity, position bias, and instability—with performance diverging from human agreement in nuanced tasks
[
17

22

•

Embedding-based metrics
(e.g., cosine similarity) offer fast, reference-free evaluations but fail to capture deeper semantic, pragmatic, or stylistic coherence, often misaligning with human judgments
[
7
,
14
]
.

Although these methods reduce annotation overhead, they all depend—explicitly or implicitly—on ground-truth signals and still fall short of reliable human-aligned evaluation in open-ended domains.

3
GrandJury: A Protocol for Structured Judgment

We introduce GrandJury, a governance protocol designed for pluralistic, transparent, and accountable evaluation in domains lacking definitive ground truth.

3.1
Consent & Voter Prompt

Each task begins with a ”voter prompt”: a publicly shared rubric specifying what to evaluate, how, and why. Inspired by annotation frameworks like CrowdTruth
[
3
]
—which model annotator disagreement as signal, not noise—this structured rubric aligns diverse judgments under a shared framework while retaining interpretive differences.

3.2

Instead of static averages, GrandJury applies exponential time decay:

Freshness Metric

To maintain clarity on score recency, GrandJury tracks freshness
F_{t}=1-\alpha

Interpretation.

F_{t}\;=\;1-\alpha\;=\;1-e^{-\lambda\Delta t}.

In the example above,

74.1\%

3.4
Ambiguity Flagging

High evaluator variance (e.g.,
\sigma^{2}>0.5
) triggers an ambiguity flag, signaling outputs that lack consensus. This transforms disagreement from a problem into an analytical signal, essential for transparent evaluation.

Notation and rule.

Let
\sigma_{t}^{2}=\operatorname{Var}(v_{t,*})
.
GrandJury flags an item as
ambiguous
if

\sigma_{t}^{2}>\sigma_{\text{crit}}^{2},\qquad\text{e.g. }\sigma_{\text{crit}}^{2}=0.05.

Worked example.

Using the same three votes
\{0.90,0.80,0.60\}

\sigma_{t}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{3}\!\sum_{i}(v_{t,i}-0.766)^{2}=0.016<0.05,

With divergent votes
\{1,0,0\}
we get
\sigma_{t}^{2}=0.111>0.05

3.5
Traceability of Votes

Every vote is timestamped (ISO 8601), linked to a pseudonymous Juror ID, and associated with its voter prompt context. This mirrors best practices in human evaluation datasheets and crowdsourced auditing, enabling full audit trails and enabling meta-analysis.

3.6
Implementation Architecture

The time-decay algorithm is implemented server-side, with the Python client providing data formatting and API access. This architecture ensures:

•

Consistent scoring across all users and evaluation sessions

•

Real-time processing of individual votes as micro-batches

•

Scalable aggregation for large evaluation campaigns

•

Centralized parameter management (
\lambda
,
\sigma_{\text{crit}}^{2}
) with domain-specific tuning

The separation between client-side data handling and server-side computation enables both individual researchers and large-scale evaluation initiatives to leverage the same algorithmic infrastructure while maintaining flexibility in data sources and evaluation workflows.

Demonstration: Public Inference Dataset and Evaluation Toolkit

To demonstrate GrandJury in practice, we release a comprehensive inference collection comprising 149 joke responses from 31 distinct models across 7 major AI platforms. This dataset serves as both a demonstration of the protocol’s applicability and a resource for reproducible evaluation research. The dataset is hosted on
Hugging Face
.

4.1
Model Selection Methodology

Model selection followed a systematic approach to ensure representation of high-performing models in creative tasks. We consulted the LMSYS Chatbot Arena leaderboard
[
28
]
, specifically filtering for models that demonstrated excellence in creative writing tasks. From this curated list of top-performing creative models, we selected those available across major cloud AI platforms (Azure AI, Anthropic, Bedrock, Gemini, Hugging Face, Mistral, and NVIDIA) to ensure accessibility and reproducibility for the research community.

This methodology resulted in 31 distinct models spanning multiple model families and sizes, providing a representative sample of state-of-the-art generative capabilities in creative domains. All models were queried with the identical prompt “tell me a joke” using temperature=1.0 for consistent experimental conditions. The final collection comprises 149 responses collected within a controlled 6-minute window, ensuring temporal consistency across platforms.

4.3
Two-Phase Evaluation Workflow

GrandJury operates through a clear separation of concerns between data collection and vote analysis:

Phase 1: Human Evaluation Collection

1.

Download the inference dataset from Hugging Face

2.

Define voter prompts (evaluation rubrics) appropriate for humor assessment

3.

Conduct human evaluation sessions to collect votes on model outputs

4.

Store votes with required metadata (voter ID, timestamps, inference ID)

Phase 2: Vote Analysis with grandjury Package

1.

Load collected vote data using the grandjury Python client

2.

Apply time-decay algorithms and consensus analysis

3.

Generate evaluation reports with traceability and freshness metrics

This separation ensures that evaluation criteria remain flexible and community-driven, rather than embedded in automated metrics. The inference dataset serves as raw material for human evaluation, while the grandjury package provides the analytical infrastructure for processing the resulting human judgments.

Researchers and practitioners are invited to explore the dataset and apply the GrandJury protocol to this inference collection: by defining voter prompts, collecting plural judgments, and aggregating scores using the time-decay method, the community can surface disagreements, map consensus shifts, and transparently assess generative models beyond static benchmarks.

We provide a reference implementation of the GrandJury protocol through the open-source
grandjury
Python package
[

4.5
Vote Analysis API

The package operates on vote collections gathered during Phase 1 evaluation. Users can analyze vote data across multiple formats:

from grandjury import GrandJuryClient
import pandas as pd

# Initialize client (no authentication needed for analysis)
client = GrandJuryClient()

# Load vote data from your evaluation sessions
vote_data = pd.read_csv("collected_votes.csv")

# Analyze vote patterns and temporal distributions
histogram = client.vote_histogram(vote_data)
completeness = client.vote_completeness(vote_data, voter_list)
confidence = client.population_confidence(vote_data, voter_list)
distribution = client.votes_distribution(vote_data)

# Apply time-decay scoring (requires API key for server access)
client = GrandJuryClient(api_key="your-api-key")
result = client.evaluate_model(
previous_score=0.0,
votes=[0.8, 0.6, 0.9], # From your collected evaluations
reputations=[1.0, 1.0, 1.0]
)
print(f"Time-decayed score: {result[’score’]:.4f}")

The package supports multiple data formats (pandas DataFrames, Polars, CSV, Parquet, JSON) to integrate seamlessly with existing evaluation workflows.

5
Conclusion and Community Engagement

GrandJury is not a benchmark, metric, or product—it is a protocol and community infrastructure designed for pluralistic, transparent, and renewable evaluation of generative AI where ground truth doesn’t apply. This final section crystallizes the purpose, situates it within current governance dynamics, and issues a strong, actionable invitation to engage.

5.1
Contributions and Objectives

GrandJury’s core aim is to enable structured, pluralistic evaluation, not to declare winners or embed opaque rankings. Its emphasis on plurality over consensus, transparency over opacity, and renewal over stagnation aligns with emerging AI governance frameworks advocating for independent evaluation and human-centric oversight mechanisms.

5.2
Protocol Scope, Community Adoption, and Future Directions

GrandJury serves as a governance protocol that defines who votes, under which voter prompt, when, and how those votes are aggregated, while functioning as a system to surface plural judgment, document metadata, and track consensus changes over time. Importantly, it is not a static scoring metric, commercial SaaS product, or prescriptive policy—rather, it provides tooling and structure for evaluation without imposing directives. This positioning reflects our commitment to enabling structured, pluralistic evaluation without declaring winners or embedding opaque rankings.

We invite broad community engagement across multiple stakeholder groups: researchers and practitioners to apply GrandJury on their models, domains, and datasets; open-source developers to build UIs, dashboards, and workflow integrations on top of the protocol; governance bodies, standards groups, and auditors to pilot GrandJury in transparent, real-world evaluation or compliance use cases; and human-AI ethics and policy researchers to study and refine the protocol’s transparency, accountability, and consensus tracking dynamics. This collaborative approach ensures that evaluation criteria remain flexible and community-driven rather than embedded in automated metrics.

GrandJury’s design aligns with current institutional efforts toward responsible AI governance. The protocol echoes third-party and audit-based evaluation systems, such as the ISO/IEC 42001 AI Management System standard promoted by the British Standards Institution for independent auditing and certification of AI governance processes
[
32
]
, while supporting responsible AI initiatives that prioritize transparency, human oversight, and auditable evaluation
[
33
,
34
]
. Its emphasis on plurality over consensus, transparency over opacity, and renewal over stagnation resonates with regulatory movements like the EU AI Act, which mandate human oversight and independent conformity assessment
[
30
]
. By anchoring evaluation in plural human judgments—logged, auditable, and time-aware—GrandJury offers infrastructure that embraces uncertainty, decentralizes authority, and transforms disagreement into accountability.

Looking forward, we urge the community to download the Inference Collection, experiment with voter prompts, and apply the
grandjury
package in practice. We encourage sharing datasets, voter-prompt variants, and insights publicly—via GitHub, forums, or publications—while engaging in protocol dialogues that refine metadata logging, decay parameters, and ambiguity thresholds. The ultimate goal is integrating GrandJury within organizational audit workflows and community evaluation standards, positioning it not only as a standalone tool but as foundational infrastructure for structured, pluralistic evaluation across diverse domains where ground truth remains elusive.

By anchoring evaluation in plural human judgments—logged, auditable, and time-aware—GrandJury offers a new foundation for evaluating generative systems: one that embraces uncertainty, decentralizes authority, and transforms disagreement into accountability.

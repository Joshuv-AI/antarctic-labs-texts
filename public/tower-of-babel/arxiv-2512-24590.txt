Two-Distance Sets over Finite Fields
Jozsef Solymosi

Abstract
We show that Blokhuis’ quadratic upper bound for two-distance sets is sharp over finite fields in almost all dimensions. Our construction complements Lisoněk’s higher-dimensional maximal constructions that carried out in Lorentz spaces [ 6 ] .



1
Introduction

The study of few-distance sets is a classical topic at the interface of discrete geometry,
algebraic combinatorics, and harmonic analysis.
Given a quadratic distance function on a vector space, one asks for the largest possible
cardinality of a set in which all pairwise distances assume only a small number of values.
In Euclidean space, results of Delsarte, Goethals, and Seidel
[
3
]

established a general polynomial and harmonic framework for such problems, leading to strong
upper bounds.

In particular, Blokhuis
[
2
]
proved that any two-distance set

X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies

|X|\leq\binom{d+2}{2}.

(1)

This bound is known to be sharp in dimension
d=8
only by a construction of Lisoněk
[
6
]
.
Above this range, Euclidean maximality is no longer known and is believed to stay much below this bound.
Even finding a two-distance set with more than
\binom{d+1}{2}
vertices in
d>8
is open with the exception of the
d=23
case
[
5
]
.
Two-distance sets on
\binom{d+1}{2}
vertices can be found in spherical point sets. Spherical two-distance sets are investigated extensively; see, e.g.,
[
8
,
1
]
.

Lisoněk’s higher-dimensional maximal constructions are carried out in Lorentz spaces
with indefinite quadratic forms rather than in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In this note we consider analogous questions over finite fields.
Let
p
be an odd prime and equip
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
with the standard quadratic distance

\operatorname{dist}^{2}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}-y_{i})^{2}.

The polynomial method underlying Blokhuis’ argument applies verbatim in this setting and yields
the same quadratic upper bound for two-distance sets.
Over finite fields we can show the sharpness of the bound in (
1
).

The key distinction compared to the real case is that quadratic forms over finite fields are not positive definite.
As a consequence, the Gram-matrix argument for equilateral sets exhibits a
characteristic-dependent rank collapse: an equilateral set in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
has size at most

d+1
, except in the modular case
p\mid n
, where size
d+2
is algebraically permitted.
Unlike the Euclidean situation, this exceptional possibility is actually realized:
whenever
p\mid(d+2)
, one can construct explicit equilateral sets of size
d+2
in

\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
with nonzero common distance.

The main observation of this paper is that these exceptional equilateral configurations
immediately yield extremal two-distance sets.
Indeed, taking the midpoints of all edges of a
(d+2)
-point equilateral set produces a
two-distance set of size
\binom{d+2}{2}
.
Thus, in contrast to the Euclidean case, Blokhuis’ quadratic bound is attained over
finite fields in entire congruence classes of dimensions, determined simply by the condition

p\mid(d+2)
.

Both in finite-field distance geometry and Lorentzian geometry settings, the absence of positive definiteness allows extremal configurations that are impossible in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2
Distances over finite fields

Let
p
be an odd prime and equip
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
with the standard quadratic distance

\operatorname{dist}^{2}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}-y_{i})^{2}.

A set
X\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
is called
equilateral
if

\operatorname{dist}^{2}(x,y)=\Delta\neq 0
for all distinct
x,y\in X
,
and a
two-distance set
if all pairwise distances assume exactly two values.

3
Equilateral sets and the
d+2
phenomenon

Let
P_{0},\dots,P_{n-1}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
be equilateral with common squared distance

\Delta\neq 0
. Translating so that
P_{0}=0
and writing
v_{i}=P_{i}
, one checks that

\|v_{i}\|^{2}=\Delta,\qquad\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle=\Delta/2\quad(i\neq j).

Thus the Gram matrix
(\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle)
is a scalar multiple of
I+J
.
Since
\operatorname{rank}(I+J)=n-1
unless
p\mid n
, in which case

\operatorname{rank}(I+J)=n-2
, one obtains the classical bound

n\leq d+1,

with the single exception that

n\leq d+2\quad\text{if }p\mid n.

Unlike the Euclidean case, this exceptional bound is
attained
over finite fields:
whenever
p\mid(d+2)
there exist explicit equilateral sets of size
d+2
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}

with nonzero common distance.

One convenient explicit realization is as follows.
Assume
p\mid(d+2)
and set
m=d+1
, so that
m+1\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
Work in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{m}
with the standard quadratic form and consider the

d
-dimensional hyperplane

W=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{m}:\ x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m}=0\}.

Let
\mathbf{1}=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{m}
and
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
be the standard basis.
Fix
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
and define

P_{0}=0,\qquad P_{i}=b\mathbf{1}+be_{i}\quad(1\leq i\leq m).

Since
(m+1)b=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, all points
P_{i}
lie in
W
.
A direct computation shows that

\operatorname{dist}^{2}(P_{i},P_{j})=2b^{2}\quad\text{for all }i\neq j,

so
\{P_{0},\dots,P_{m}\}
is an equilateral set of size
d+2
in the

d
-dimensional quadratic space
W\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
.

4
Midpoints and two distances

The midpoints of a regular simplex with
d+1
vertices in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
provide a natural construction for two-distance sets on
\binom{d+1}{2}
points.
After Lisoněk’s construction almost 30 years ago, in dimension larger than 8, only very recently was a two-distance set with 277 points in dimension 23 discovered
[
5
]
, exceeding the midpoint bound.
(277 is one larger than
\binom{24}{2}=276
)

Now let’s recall the details of the midpoint construction over finite fields. Let
P_{0},\dots,P_{n-1}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
be equilateral with common squared distance

\Delta\neq 0
, and assume
p\neq 2
.
For each unordered pair
\{i,j\}
define the midpoint

M_{ij}=\frac{P_{i}+P_{j}}{2}.

Lemma 1
.

For two distinct midpoints
M_{ij}\neq M_{k\ell}
one has

\operatorname{dist}^{2}(M_{ij},M_{k\ell})\in\left\{\frac{\Delta}{4},\frac{\Delta}{2}\right\}.

More precisely, if the edges
\{i,j\}
and
\{k,\ell\}
share a vertex then

\operatorname{dist}^{2}(M_{ij},M_{k\ell})=\Delta/4
, while if they are disjoint then

\operatorname{dist}^{2}(M_{ij},M_{k\ell})=\Delta/2
.

Proof.

Translate so that
P_{0}=0
and write
v_{i}=P_{i}
.
For
i<j
we have
M_{ij}=(v_{i}+v_{j})/2
, hence

\operatorname{dist}^{2}(M_{ij},M_{k\ell})=\frac{1}{4}\|(v_{i}+v_{j})-(v_{k}+v_{\ell})\|^{2}.

If the edges share a vertex, this reduces to

\frac{1}{4}\|v_{j}-v_{k}\|^{2}=\Delta/4
.
If the edges are disjoint, expanding the norm and using

\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle=\Delta/2
for
i\neq j
shows that the cross term vanishes,
leaving
\frac{1}{4}(\Delta+\Delta)=\Delta/2
.
∎

Thus the midpoint set
\{M_{ij}\}
is always a two-distance set.
This also applies in the modular case
p\mid n
.

We note that the classical Gram-matrix method over finite fields used earlier in,
for example,
[
7
,
3
]
in a similar way.

5
Sharpness of Blokhuis’ bound

Blokhuis proved that any two-distance set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies

|X|\leq\binom{d+2}{2}.

The same polynomial argument applies verbatim over finite fields.
When
p\mid(d+2)
, the existence of equilateral sets of size
d+2
and the
midpoint construction above produce two-distance sets of size

|X|=\binom{d+2}{2},

showing that Blokhuis’ quadratic bound is
sharp
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}

in the modular regime.

This sharpness mechanism is intrinsically finite-field in nature and has
no analogue over
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
; it reflects the rank collapse
of the Gram matrix in characteristic
p
.

Note that all arguments extend to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for any odd prime power
q=p^{k}
; the existence of
d+2
equilateral sets and the resulting sharpness of Blokhuis’ bound depend only on the characteristic
p
, not on the field size. However, over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q=p^{k}
odd, sharp constructions occur in exactly those
dimensions
d
satisfying
p\mid(d+2)
, equivalently
d\equiv-2\pmod{p}
,
that is,

d=mp-2\qquad(m=1,2,3,\dots).

Among the natural numbers, for all
d\geq 3
and
d\neq 2^{t}-2
, there is an example where Blokhuis’ bound is sharp over a
d
-dimensional finite field.

Graph-theoretic description.

The graph associated to the midpoint two-distance set is the line graph
L(K_{n})
, equivalently
the Johnson graph
J(n,2)
, with
n=d+2
.
It is a strongly regular graph with parameters

v=\binom{n}{2},\qquad k=2(n-2),\qquad\lambda=n-2,\qquad\mu=4,

and adjacency eigenvalues

2(n-2)\ \text{(multiplicity $1$)},\qquad n-4\ \text{(multiplicity $n-1$)},\qquad-2\ \text{(multiplicity $\tfrac{n(n-3)}{2}$)}.

In the modular regime
p\mid n
(equivalently
p\mid(d+2)
), the two largest eigenvalues

2(n-2)
and
n-4
coincide in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, while the third eigenvalue
-2
remains distinct.
This eigenvalue collapse mirrors the characteristic-
p
rank drop of the Gram matrix that
permits an equilateral set of size
d+2
, and has no analogue in the Euclidean setting.

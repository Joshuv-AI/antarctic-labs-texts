On differential Hopf algebras and B ∞ B_{\infty} algebras
Imma Gálvez-Carrillo, María Ronco, Andy Tonks

Abstract




Introduction

We establish a structure theorem, analogous to the classical result of Milnor and Moore, for differential graded Hopf algebras and
B_{\infty}
algebras. This extends the work of Loday and the second author
[
12
]
where
the ungraded non-differential case was treated: it was shown that any cofree Hopf algebra carries an underlying multibrace structure that restricts to the subspace of primitives, and conversely that any cofree Hopf algebra may be reconstructed from a multibrace algebra via a universal enveloping 2-associative algebra.

In particular, in the case of differential graded Hopf algebras, we establish an extension of the Loday–Ronco multibrace structure to a
B_{\infty}
structure.

B_{\infty}
algebras first appeared in algebraic topology, implicitly, in work of Baues
[
1
]
and play a central role in the study of the algebraic structure carried by the Hochschild cochain complex and its singular or Tate analogue
[
3
,
4
,
9
,
13
,
16
]
.
The explicit definition of a
B_{\infty}
structure, on a vector space or a chain complex
A
, was first formulated in
[
7
, Section 5.2]
as a differential
d
and a product
\mu
on the bar construction
(BA,\Delta)
making it a differential bialgebra.
Unpacking this definition, a
B_{\infty}
algebra is equipped with families of multilinear structure maps
(m_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
and
(m_{i,j})_{i,j\geqslant 1}
of arities
n
and
i+j
satisfying certain ‘strong homotopy algebra’ relations.
This includes the notion of
A_{\infty}
algebra, where we just require a differential coalgebra
(BA,\Delta,d)
, that is, the structure maps
m_{n}
.
It also includes the notion of multibrace algebra, where we just require a bialgebra
(BA,\Delta,\mu)
, that is, the structure maps
m_{i,j}
. Multibrace algebras were termed non-differential
\mathbf{B}_{\infty}
algebras in
[
12
]
and
B_{\infty}
algebras in
[
5
]
.

Our second aim is provide further insight into a construction of Börjeson
[
2
]
, who showed that any associative algebra with a square zero endomorphism (not required to be a derivation) carries an underlying
A_{\infty}
structure. Markl in
[
14
]
explained the origin of this
A_{\infty}
structure as a twisting (arising from the multiplication) of the given trivial
A_{\infty}
algebra.

As we explain below, Börjeson’s
A_{\infty}
structure is just the right adjoint to the tensor (co)algebra functor. Another crucial observation is that the Loday–Ronco construction fits into this framework: we discover that the underlying multibrace structure carried by any 2-associative algebra, as defined in
[
12
, Section 3.3 and Proposition 3.4]
,
originates from a twisting of a quasi-trivial multibrace algebra (a non-commutative quasi-shuffle algebra). See Example
2.2.5
and Theorem
3.1.2
below for details.

To keep the presentation self-contained and accessible we recall much material which should be known to the expert reader.
To simplify notation and remain consistent with
[
2
,
12
,
14
]

we choose to work with the tensor coalgebra
T^{c}(A)
instead of the bar construction
BA=T^{c}(A[1])
.
Thus our definitions of
A_{\infty}
and
B_{\infty}
differ from the classical ones mentioned above. The translation (of the degrees and signs involved) is straightforward.

\displaystyle B_{\infty}\text{-alg}

\displaystyle\to\mathit{d}\mathcal{A}\mathit{s}^{1,1}\text{-alg}

\displaystyle=F_{\mathit{d}\mathcal{A}\mathit{s}^{1,1}}(A)/I.

Thus

\mathit{d}\mathcal{A}\mathit{s}^{1,1}

1.
Coalgebras, algebras and bialgebras

1.1.
Grading and Koszul signs

We work with graded vector spaces
V
over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
, and denote the degree of a homogeneous element
v\in V
by
\left|v\right|
.
The tensor product of graded spaces is graded by
\left|a\otimes b\right|=\left|a\right|+\left|b\right|
. The tensor product of (homogeneous) graded maps is given by

\displaystyle(f\otimes g)(a\otimes b)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\left|g\right|\left|a\right|}f(a)\otimes g(b),

which implies

\displaystyle(f\otimes g)\circ(h\otimes k)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\left|g\right|\left|h\right|}(f\circ h)\otimes(g\circ k).

These signs arise from
1
1

1

The symmetry is used to define the evaluation map
\text{ev}\colon\!\hom(V_{1},W_{1})\!\otimes\!\hom(V_{2},W_{2})\,\otimes\,V_{1}\!\otimes\!V_{2}\to W_{1}\!\otimes\!W_{2}
.
the natural symmetry isomorphism of the tensor product

\sigma\colon V\otimes W\cong W\otimes V,\qquad\sigma(a\otimes b)=(-1)^{\left|a\right|\left|b\right|}b\otimes a.

Given a permutation
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
we may denote by the same name the symmetry isomorphism of an
n
-fold tensor product that moves the
i
-th factor to the
\sigma(i)
-th position,

\displaystyle V_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{n}

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\quad V_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{\sigma^{-1}(n)},

\displaystyle\sigma(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{n}

\displaystyle)=(-1)^{\kappa}\;v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}.

The formula for the Koszul signs arising in this symmetry isomorphism is

(1.1)

\kappa\;\;=\;\;\textrm{sgn}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n};\sigma)\;\;=\;\;\sum_{\makebox[0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle(p,q)\in\textrm{inv}(\sigma)$}}\left|v_{p}\right|\left|v_{q}\right|.

Here the sum is over inversions
(p,q)
of
\sigma
, that is, all
p<q
with
\sigma(p)>\sigma(q)
.

1.2.
Coalgebras

A
coalgebra
(or more explicitly, a counital coassociative coalgebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
)
is a graded vector space
C
equipped with a
comultiplication
\Delta\colon C\to C\otimes C

and a
counit
\varepsilon\colon C\to{\mathbb{K}}

which satisfy the coassociativity and counit laws

(1.2)

(\Delta\otimes 1)\Delta=(1\otimes\Delta)\Delta,\qquad\lambda(\varepsilon\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta=\operatorname{id}=\rho(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varepsilon)\Delta.

The structure maps
\Delta,\varepsilon
have degree zero, and

(1.3)

\lambda:{\mathbb{K}}\otimes C\to C,\qquad\rho:C\otimes{\mathbb{K}}\to C

are the canonical isomorphisms which we often regard as identity maps and silently omit from the notation. A
coalgebra map
is a linear map that preserves the comultiplication and counit.
We may write comultiplication using Sweedler notation without an explicit summation symbol,

\Delta(x)\;\;=\;\;x_{(1)}\otimes x_{(2)}.

The tensor product
C\otimes D
of coalgebras is canonically a coalgebra, with counit
\varepsilon(x\otimes y)=\varepsilon(x)\varepsilon(y)
and comultiplication

\Delta\colon C\otimes D\xrightarrow{\Delta\otimes\Delta}(C\otimes C)\otimes(D\otimes D)\xrightarrow{(23)}(C\otimes D)\otimes(C\otimes D).

As the two inner tensor factors are permuted, Koszul signs appear when applied to elements,

\Delta(x\otimes y)\;\;=\;\;(-1)^{\left|y_{(1)}\right|\left|x_{(2)}\right|}(x_{(1)}\otimes y_{(1)})\otimes(x_{(2)}\otimes y_{(2)}).

1.3.
Reduced coalgebras

A
unit
or
coaugmentation
for a coalgebra
C
is
a coalgebra map
\eta\colon{\mathbb{K}}\to C
, or equivalently
a specified element
\eta(1_{{\mathbb{K}}})=1\in C
which is group-like:
\Delta(1)=1\otimes 1
. Then
\varepsilon(1)=1_{{\mathbb{K}}}
and there is a splitting

(\eta\varepsilon,J)\colon C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbb{K}}1\oplus\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

where
J=\operatorname{id}-\eta\varepsilon:C\to C
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=\ker(\varepsilon)=\textrm{im}(J)
.

For any unital coalgebra
(C,\Delta,\varepsilon,1)
there is a well-defined coassociative operation

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\colon\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\otimes\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

where, for
x\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
,

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(x)\;=\;\Delta(x)-x\otimes 1-1\otimes x\;=\;J^{\otimes 2}\Delta(x).

The (non-unital, non-counital) coalgebra
(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
is called the
reduced
coalgebra.
This gives an equivalence between the categories of unital counital coalgebras and their maps and of non-unital non-counital algebras and their maps.

The kernel of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is the subspace
\operatorname{Prim}(C)
of
primitive
elements of
C
.

Remark 1.3.1
.

Saying that a binary operation defined by a linear map
\mu\colon C\otimes C\to C
is
unital
may mean, according to context, either the condition
\mu(1\otimes 1)=1
or the condition
\mu(1\otimes x)=\mu(x\otimes 1)=x
for all
x\in C
.
The latter condition will often be paraphrased as
satisfies the unit law
. In this case specifying
\mu
is equivalent to specifying the component
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\!\otimes\!\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\to C
as the remaining components
are the canonical isomorphisms, cf. (
1.3
),

\mu(\eta\otimes\operatorname{id})\colon{\mathbb{K}}\!\otimes\!C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle=}}{{\longrightarrow}}C,\qquad\qquad\mu(\operatorname{id}\otimes\eta)\colon C\!\otimes\!{\mathbb{K}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle=}}{{\longrightarrow}}C.

1.4.
The primitive filtration

Consider the filtration of a unital coalgebra
C
defined in
[
15
, Section 3]
by

F_{0}C={\mathbb{K}}1
and

F_{r}C\;=\;{\mathbb{K}}1\oplus\{x\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\;|\;\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(x)\in F_{r-1}C\otimes F_{r-1}C\}\qquad(r\geqslant 1).

Denote by

\Delta^{(r)}\colon C\to C^{\otimes(r+1)}
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)}\colon\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\otimes(r+1)}

the
r
-fold iterations of the coassociative comultiplications
\Delta
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. For
r=0
these are the identity maps, and
\Delta^{(-1)}=\varepsilon:C\to{\mathbb{K}}
.

Definition 1.4.1
.

The
conilpotent radical

R(C)
of a unital coalgebra
C
is the subcoalgebra
\bigcup F_{r}C
.

Definition 1.4.2

(Connected and conilpotent coalgebras)
.

A connected coalgebra
(in the sense of
[
12
]
)
is a unital coalgebra
C
such that
the above filtration is exhaustive, so that
R(C)=C
.
A conilpotent coalgebra is a unital coalgebra
C
such that
for each
x\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
there exists
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)}(x)=0
.

It is well known that connected coalgebras are conilpotent: it was noted in
[
12
]
and can be seen in Quillen’s proof of
[
15
, Proposition 4.1]
. The converse was pointed out in
[
6
, Appendix B]
. We have:

Lemma 1.4.3
.

A unital coalgebra
C
is conilpotent if and only if it is connected. In fact,

F_{r}C\;\;=\;\;{\mathbb{K}}1\oplus\ker(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)})\;\;=\;\;\ker(J^{\otimes(r+1)}\Delta^{(r)})

and
R(C)
is the largest conilpotent subcoalgebra of
C
.

Proof.

Since
J^{\otimes(r+1)}\Delta^{(r)}(1)=0
and

J^{\otimes(r+1)}\Delta^{(r)}(x)=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)}(x)
for
x\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
the second equality holds.
Now note that taking kernels and the tensor product commute in the following sense

\displaystyle\ker(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)})\otimes\ker(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)})

\displaystyle\;=\;\ker(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)}\otimes\operatorname{id})\;\cap\;\ker(\operatorname{id}\otimes\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)}).

The first equality therefore follows inductively from

\displaystyle{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}^{-1}\left(\ker(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)})\otimes\ker(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)})\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;\ker((\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)}\otimes\operatorname{id})\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\;\cap\;\ker((\operatorname{id}\otimes\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r)})\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\;=\;\ker(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(r+1)}).

∎

1.5.
Comodules and coderivations

A bicomodule over a coalgebra
C
is a graded vector space
D
with a coaction
\gamma:D\to C\otimes D\otimes C
, that is, a linear map satisfying

(\varepsilon\otimes\operatorname{id}\otimes\varepsilon)\gamma=\operatorname{id},\qquad\qquad(\operatorname{id}\otimes\gamma\otimes\operatorname{id})\gamma=(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)\gamma.

Observe that the coaction
\gamma
determines left and right coactions

(\operatorname{id}\otimes\operatorname{id}\otimes\varepsilon)\gamma\colon D\to C\otimes D,\qquad(\varepsilon\otimes\operatorname{id}\otimes\operatorname{id})\gamma\colon D\to D\otimes C.

For example, any coalgebra can be regarded as a bicomodule over itself, with left and right coactions both given by the comultiplication, and
\gamma=\Delta^{(2)}
.

A coderivation on a
C
-bicomodule
D

is a linear map
d\colon D\to C
, not necessarily of degree 0, satisfying

\displaystyle\Delta d

\displaystyle\;\;=\;\;(\varepsilon\otimes d\otimes\operatorname{id}+\operatorname{id}\otimes d\otimes\varepsilon)\gamma

where we have omitted the isomorphisms (
1.3
). This implies
\varepsilon d=0
and indeed

\displaystyle\Delta^{(k-1)}d

\displaystyle\;\;=\;\,\sum_{\makebox[0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geqslant 0\\
i+1+j=k\end{subarray}$}}\;(\Delta^{(i-1)}\otimes d\otimes\Delta^{(j-1)})\,\gamma,\qquad(k\geqslant 0).

1.6.
Cofree coalgebras

The
tensor coalgebra
on a graded vector space
V
is given by

T^{c}(V)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}V^{\otimes k}

with counit defined by the
0
-th projection
\varepsilon=p_{0}\colon T^{c}(V)\to V^{{\otimes 0}}={\mathbb{K}}
, and comultiplication defined by deconcatenation

\Delta(v_{1}\cdots v_{k})\;=\;\sum_{i=0}^{k}v_{1}\cdots v_{i}\;\otimes\;v_{i+1}\cdots v_{k}.

As usual when working in
T^{c}(V)\otimes T^{c}(V)
we write
v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\in T^{c}(V)
for
v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{k}\in V^{\otimes k}
, and write
1=1_{{\mathbb{K}}}
for the unit, given by the empty product, in the case
k=0
.
The reduced tensor coalgebra is given by

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{c}(V)=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\infty}V^{{\otimes k}},\qquad\qquad\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(v_{1}\cdots v_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}v_{1}\cdots v_{i}\;\otimes\;v_{i+1}\cdots v_{k}.

The unital coalgebra
T^{c}(V)
is conilpotent, its space of primitives is
V^{\otimes 1}=V
, and it has filtration

F_{r}T^{c}(V)\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{r}V^{\otimes k}.

Remark 1.6.1

(Cofreeness properties)
.

Together with the first projection
p_{1}\colon T^{c}(V)\to V
onto the primitives, the tensor coalgebra defines the free coalgebra in the category of conilpotent coalgebras:

a)

One can write the
k
-th projection as
p_{k}=p_{1}^{\otimes k}\Delta^{(k-1)}\colon T^{c}(V)\to V^{\otimes k}
and
the identity map as

\qquad\operatorname{id}\,=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{1}^{\otimes k}\Delta^{(k-1)}\colon T^{c}(V)\to T^{c}(V).

b)

Any unital coalgebra map
f\colon C\to T^{c}(V)
is determined by
f_{1}=p_{1}f\colon C\to V
via

f\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{1}^{{\otimes k}}\Delta^{(k-1)}f\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{1}^{\otimes k}\Delta_{C}^{(k-1)}\colon C\to T^{c}(V).\qquad\qquad

Conversely if
C
is a conilpotent coalgebra then the formula on the right hand side gives a well-defined unital coalgebra map
f=\sum f_{k}\colon C\to T^{c}(V)
extending any linear map
f_{1}\colon C\to V
with
f_{1}(1)=0
. Hence projection to the primitives gives a bijection

p_{1}^{*}\colon\hom_{\textrm{conil}}(C,T^{c}V)\;\cong\;\hom_{\mathbb{K}}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,V).

c)

Any coderivation
d
on a
T^{c}(V)
-bicomodule
D
is determined by
d_{1}=p_{1}d\colon D\to V
, via

\displaystyle d\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{1}^{\otimes k}\Delta^{(k-1)}d

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\;p_{1}^{\otimes k}\;\sum_{\makebox[0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geqslant 0\\
i+1+j=k\end{subarray}$}}\;(\Delta^{(i-1)}\otimes d\otimes\Delta^{(j-1)})\,\gamma\;=\;\sum_{\makebox[0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geqslant 0\end{subarray}$}}\;(p_{i}\otimes d_{1}\otimes p_{j})\,\gamma

Conversely the formula on the right hand side gives a well-defined coderivation
d\colon D\to T^{c}(V)
extending any linear map
d_{1}\colon D\to V
. Hence projection to the primitives gives a bijection

p_{1}^{*}\colon\textrm{coder}(D,T^{c}V)\;\cong\;\hom_{\mathbb{K}}(D,V).

A unital coalgebra is termed cofree (more precisely: cofree among conilpotent coalgebras) if it is isomorphic to a tensor coalgebra
T^{c}(V)
, or equivalently if it comes equipped with a projection
p_{1}
to its space of primitives having the same cofreeness properties as above.

1.7.
Bialgebras and unital infinitesimal bialgebras

Recall that an
algebra
(or more explicitly, a unital associative algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
) is a graded vector space
B
equipped with a multiplication
\mu\colon B\otimes B\to B

and a unit
\eta\colon{\mathbb{K}}\to B
,
\eta(1_{{\mathbb{K}}})=1
,
satisfying associativity and unit laws.
A differential graded algebra is an algebra
(B,\mu,1)
equipped with a map
d\colon B\to B
of degree
-1
satisfying
d^{2}=0
and

d\mu=\mu(\operatorname{id}\otimes d+d\otimes\operatorname{id})
.

An
algebra map
is a linear map preserving the unit and multiplication.

An algebra
B
is
augmented
or
counital
if it is equipped with an algebra map
\varepsilon\colon B\to{\mathbb{K}}
.

The tensor product
A\otimes B
of algebras has a canonical algebra structure

A\otimes B\>\otimes\>A\otimes B\xrightarrow{(23)}A\otimes A\>\otimes\>B\otimes B\xrightarrow{\mu_{A}\otimes\mu_{B}}A\otimes B.

Definition 1.7.1
.

Let
B
be a graded vector space with structures of
an algebra
(B,\mu,\eta)

and of
a coalgebra
(B,\Delta,\varepsilon)
.
Then the
structure
(B,\mu,\eta,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is termed

(1)

a
bialgebra

if
\mu\colon B\otimes B\to B
and
\eta\colon{\mathbb{K}}\to B
are coalgebra maps,
or equivalently
if
\Delta\colon B\to B\otimes B
and
\varepsilon\colon B\to{\mathbb{K}}
are algebra maps,

(2)

a
unital infinitesimal bialgebra
if

\displaystyle\Delta(xy)\;

\displaystyle=\;x_{(1)}\otimes x_{(2)}y\;+\;xy_{(1)}\otimes y_{(2)}\;-\;x\otimes y.

An equivalent definition is given in Lemma
1.7.4
and Corollary
1.7.5
.

We use Sweedler notation for comultiplication and denote the multiplication
\mu
by juxtaposition.

A bialgebra or a unital infinitesimal bialgebra is termed
conilpotent
if it is conilpotent as a unital coalgebra, and is termed
cofree
if is is cofree as a unital coalgebra.

Any conilpotent bialgebra has a canonical Hopf algebra structure, though not all Hopf algebras are conilpotent.

Example 1.7.2

(Shuffle bialgebra)
.

The shuffle bialgebra
T^{\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits}(V)
on a graded vector space
V
has underlying coalgebra
T^{c}(V)
with
\Delta
given by deconcatenation as described in section
1.6
. The shuffle product
\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits\colon T^{c}(V)\otimes T^{c}(V)\to T^{c}(V)

is unique coalgebra map that extends, via the cofreeness property of Remark
1.6.1
(b), the linear map

(1.4)

\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits_{1}=\varepsilon\otimes p_{1}+p_{1}\otimes\varepsilon\colon\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT^{c}(V)\otimes T^{c}(V)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\longrightarrow{\mathbb{K}}\otimes V\;\oplus\;V\otimes{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow V.

Observe that
\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits_{1}
is zero on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{c}(V)\otimes\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{c}(V)
.
The unit laws and associative law for
\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits
follow by standard cofreeness arguments:
the coalgebra maps
\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits(\eta\otimes\operatorname{id})
and
\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits(\operatorname{id}\otimes\eta)
are the identity since their compositions with
p_{1}
are just
p_{1}
,
and similarly the coalgebra maps
\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits(\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits\otimes\operatorname{id})
,
\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits(\operatorname{id}\otimes\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits)\colon T^{c}(V)^{\otimes 3}\to T^{c}(V)
are equal since one can check their projections to
V
are equal.
Observe that
x\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits_{1}y
is symmetric in
x,y
and is non-zero only when
xy
is a tensor of length exactly 1. It follows that the extension
x\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits y
is graded commutative and that each projection
\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits_{k}=\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits_{1}^{\otimes k}\Delta^{(k-1)}
is non-zero only when
xy
is a tensor of total length exactly
k
. On elements we have

(1.5)

v_{1}\cdots v_{i}\;\mathop{\scriptstyle\Sha}\nolimits\;v_{i+1}\cdots v_{k}\;=\;\sum_{\sigma}(-1)^{\kappa}\;v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\cdots v_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}.

Here the sum is over all
(i,k-i)
-shuffles, that is, permutations
\sigma\in\Sigma_{k}
satisfying
\sigma(j)<\sigma(j+1)
for all
j\neq i
,
1\leqslant j\leqslant k-1
, with the Koszul signs that were defined in (
1.1
).

The notion of unital infinitesimal bialgebra was introduced in
[

12
]
, modifying the non-unital definition of infinitesimal bialgebras due to Joni and Rota in

. The unital infinitesimal relation can be expressed

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(xy)

\displaystyle=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(x)(1\otimes y)+(x\otimes 1)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(y)+x\otimes y

Example 1.7.6

(Fundamental infinitesimal bialgebra)
.

Proposition 1.7.7
.

The functors

and

are adjoint:
for any vector space

and any unital infinitesimal bialgebra

and unital infinitesimal bialgebra homomorphisms

,
such that

for all

.

.

Proof.

Define
F
as the algebra homomorphism extending

to the free algebra,

This is a bialgebra homomorphism by Lemma
1.7.3
(1). Conversely, restricting

In other words:

2.1.

The notion of
A_{\infty}
algebra is encoded in differential graded structures on the tensor coalgebra.

Consider the tensor coalgebra
T^{c}(V)
on a graded vector space
V
as a bicomodule over itself.

Any coderivation

d_{k}=p_{k}d\colon T^{c}(V)\to V^{\otimes k}

are determined by the projection to the primitives
d_{1}=p_{1}d\colon T^{c}(V)\to V

d_{1}=\left(m_{n}\colon V^{\otimes n}\to V\right)_{n\geqslant 0},\qquad\qquad d_{k}=\left(m^{k}_{n}\colon V^{\otimes n}\to V^{\otimes k}\right)_{n\geqslant 0},

then by the cofreeness property of Remark
1.6.1
(c),

(2.1)

m^{k}_{n}=\sum_{\makebox[0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geqslant 0\\
i+1+j=k\end{subarray}$}}\operatorname{id}_{V}^{\otimes i}\otimes m_{n-i-j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{V}^{\otimes j}\colon V^{\otimes n}\to V^{\otimes k}.

A coderivation
d
on
T^{c}(V)
is termed a
differential
if it has degree
-1
and satisfies
d(1)=0
and
d^{2}=0
. The condition
d(1)=0
says
m_{0}=0
and
m_{n}^{k}=0
for

; we may regard a differential as a map

(2.2)

\displaystyle d\colon\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{c}(V)\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{c}(V)

determined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle d_{1}=p_{1}d=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}m_{n}\colon\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{c}(V)\to V.

The condition
d^{2}=0
therefore says

(2.4)

\qquad\qquad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geqslant 0\\
i+j<n\end{subarray}}m_{i+1+j}\left(\operatorname{id}_{V^{\otimes i}}\otimes m_{n-i-j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{V^{\otimes j}}\right)=0\colon V^{\otimes n}\to V\qquad(n\geqslant 1).

As the maps

have degree

we note that the usual
Koszul signs appear in the summations of (

) when they are applied to elements.

(

An

Remark 2.1.2

This notion might more properly be called a

Let

, where
{\mathbb{K}}[1]

concentrated in degree 1, and let

be the canonical degree 1 suspension isomorphism.
Then a classical

Example 2.1.3

(Trivial

If

By a

U(A)=(T^{c}(A),\partial,\bullet,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\circ$\cr$\displaystyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\circ$\cr$\textstyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\circ$\cr$\scriptstyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$\cr}}}},\Delta,1_{{\mathbb{K}}}),

where the comutiplication
\Delta
is given by deconcatenation of tensors, and there is a functor

U\colon B_{\infty}\text{-alg}\longrightarrow\mathit{d}\mathcal{A}\mathit{s}^{1,1}\text{-bialg}.

Proof.

By definition of
B_{\infty}
structures
(T^{c}(A),\partial,\bullet,\Delta,1_{{\mathbb{K}}})
is a differential graded bialgebra, and

(T^{c}(A),\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\circ$\cr$\displaystyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\circ$\cr$\textstyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\circ$\cr$\scriptstyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$\cr}}}},\Delta,1_{{\mathbb{K}}})
is a (free, cofree) unital infinitesimal
bialgebra by Example
1.7.6
.∎

The following result is the promised analogue of the Milnor–Moore theorem, and should be compared with Corollary
1.7.9
.

Theorem 3.3.3
.

The underlying
B_{\infty}
algebra structure on a 2-associative differential bialgebra restricts to the primitives, and the functor

\operatorname{Prim}\colon\mathit{d}\mathcal{A}\mathit{s}^{1,1}\text{-bialg}\longrightarrow B_{\infty}\text{-alg}

is right adjoint to
U
. The unit of the adjuntion is a natural isomorphism

\eta_{A}\colon A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Prim}(U(A)),

while the counit

\varepsilon_{V}\colon U(\operatorname{Prim}(V))\longrightarrow V

is a monomorphism and is an isomorphism if and only if
V
is conilpotent.

In particular, the functors
U
and
\operatorname{Prim}
define an adjoint equivalence of categories between
B_{\infty}
algebras and the
full coreflective subcategory of
\mathit{d}\mathcal{A}\mathit{s}^{1,1}\text{-bialg}

given by the conilpotent objects.

Proof.

If
(V,\partial,\bullet,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\circ$\cr$\displaystyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\circ$\cr$\textstyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\circ$\cr$\scriptstyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$\cr}}}},1,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is a 2-associative differential bialgebra then the conilpotent radical is a 2-associative differential subbialgebra: it is closed under
\partial
,
\bullet
,
\Delta
by the usual differential bialgebra axioms and is closed under
\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\circ$\cr$\displaystyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\circ$\cr$\textstyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\circ$\cr$\scriptstyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$\cr}}}}
by Lemma
1.7.3
(2).
It therefore follows from the inductive definitions
(
2.13
) and (
3.5
)
that it is closed under the underlying
B_{\infty}
structure maps.

It remains to show that if
(W,\partial,\bullet,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\circ$\cr$\displaystyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\circ$\cr$\textstyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\circ$\cr$\scriptstyle\cdot$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$\cr}}}},1,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is a conilpotent 2-associative differential bialgebra then the underlying
B_{\infty}
structure retricts to the primitives.
By Proposition
1.7.8
we know that
W
is naturally isomorphic as a unital infinitesimal bialgebra to
T^{fc}(\operatorname{Prim}(W))
, so we can assume without loss of generality that

(W,\partial,\bullet)=(T^{c}(\operatorname{Prim}(W)),\partial,\bullet)
.
Thus
\operatorname{Prim}(W)
has a
B_{\infty}
structure
and
\operatorname{Prim}(W)\to W
is the inclusion of a
B_{\infty}
subalgebra
by Theorem
3.1.3
.

Thus, for a general 2-associative differential bialgebra
V
, we have inclusions of
B_{\infty}
subalgebras
\operatorname{Prim}(V)=\operatorname{Prim}(R(V))\to R(V)\to V
, and the
B_{\infty}
algebra homomorphism
A\to T^{c}(A)=U(A)
of Theorem
3.1.3
gives a
B_{\infty}
algebra isomorphism onto its image,

\eta_{A}\colon A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Prim}(U(A)).

The extension of the inclusion
\operatorname{Prim}(V)\to V
of the underlying
B_{\infty}
subalgebra defines the counit

\varepsilon_{V}\colon U(\operatorname{Prim}(V))\longrightarrow V.

This factors as an isomorphism onto its image, the conilpotent radical:

\varepsilon_{V\colon}U(\operatorname{Prim}(V))=T^{c}(\operatorname{Prim}(R(V)))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}R(V)\subseteq V.

∎

Corollary 3.3.4
.

The categories of
B_{\infty}
algebras, of conilpotent 2-associative differential bialgebras and of cofree 2-associative differential bialgebras
are equivalent.

Proof.

This follows from the theorem, noting that conilpotent implies cofree for
\mathit{d}\mathcal{A}\mathit{s}^{1,1}
bialgebras since
V\cong T^{c}(\operatorname{Prim}(V))
.
∎

Remark 3.3.5
.

The category of cofree differential Hopf algebras
(T^{c}(A),d,\mu,\Delta)
has essentially the same objects as the categories above, but more morphisms.

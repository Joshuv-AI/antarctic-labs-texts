Analysis and measurement of multi-factor coupling risk in underground coal mine gas explosion based on system dynamics
Authors: Huimin Guo, Yifan Chen, Lianhua Cheng, Shugang Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To investigate the evolutionary mechanism of coal mine gas explosion, and to improve the level of system safety. In this paper, a multi-factor coupled risk deduction model for coal mine gas explosion is constructed by using the system dynamics method. The two subsystems of ‘human factors’ and ‘material factors’ are simulated by using Vensim software. To obtain the evolutionary trend of system gas explosion risk and the risk change trend of index variables. The risk level of coal mine gas explosion is predicted by adjusting the parameters of the index variables, and the influence of various coupled factors on the development trend of the risk level is analyzed. Through the application of the model, the results reveal that the model has certain applicability and feasibility. The risk values of system variables have a certain degree of hysteresis and cumulativeness in their impact on other variables and the system risk level, and the ‘emergence’ and ‘mutation’ phenomena of accident evolution.

Introduction

In recent years, the situation of coal mine safety production in China has continued to improve, but various accidents such as gas, roof fall, mechanical and electrical accidents still occur from time to time. Gas explosion is one of the major disasters in coal mine production, with extremely strong destructive power. Once an accident occurs, it is very likely to cause huge casualties and property losses. According to statistics, among the 1,900 major coal mine accidents from 2001 to 2010, 675 were gas explosion accidents, accounting for 35.53%, and 6,057 people died, accounting for 44.97%1. Among the 392 major and above coal mine accidents from 2010 to 2020, 125 were gas explosion accidents, accounting for 31.89%, and 1,090 people died, accounting for 32.72%2. With the gradual depletion of shallow coal resources, the depth and intensity of coal mining in China have been continuously increasing, and the coal storage conditions have become more complex. The ground stress, gas pressure, and ground temperature have been constantly increasing, and the gas disaster has become increasingly serious and complex. The effective control of coal mine gas accident risks will face greater challenges. Therefore, it is of great theoretical significance and practical value to pay attention to the research on the evolution and trend prediction of gas explosion risks in coal mines, which can improve the current situation of gas disaster prevention and control in China, scientifically predict the trend of accident occurrence, and reasonably formulate prevention and control plans.

At present, scholars at home and abroad have conducted a large number of studies on the prevention technology and management of gas explosion accidents in coal mines and have achieved certain results. Due to the complexity of underground coal mining and the special and changeable environment, there are many uncertainties involving people, mechanical and electrical equipment, geological environment, etc., making it difficult to predict and control the production safety in underground coal mines. In addition, the occurrence of gas explosion accidents is not the result of a single factor, but the result of nonlinear coupling evolution of multiple factors. Each factor has its independence and interacts and influences each other3,4. In the production process of underground coal mines, any change in the state of a risk factor may cause a change in the evolution path of the event. The core idea of situation prediction is evolution and prediction, which is composed of three elements: time, space and object. The situation is generally dynamic, mainly referring to the temporal sequence and state of the development of things5. In short, situation prediction refers to obtaining basic situation data, setting event occurrence rules based on the development laws of things in the past and evolutionary analysis, and conducting temporal simulation of events to predict the situation and state at the next moment6. The result of situation prediction depends on the acquisition of basic data (situation index system), the setting of evolutionary rules (evolutionary model and quantification), and the intervention of expected goals (countermeasures)7,8. At present, situation prediction is widely used in aviation, transportation, networks, power grids, industrial energy, medical care and other fields. It mainly using theoretical methods such as Bayesian networks, system dynamics, cellular automata, and complex networks9–11. By analyzing the characteristics of event occurrence, the evolution path and development rules of the situation are studied, and the situation deduction model is constructed.

The situation prediction of gas explosion coupling risk is an effective way to achieve risk early warning and advanced control. Therefore, based on the principle of system dynamics, this paper extracts the situation prediction rules from the coupling correlation between the characteristics of accident occurrence and risk factors, and constructs a multi-factor coupling risk deduction model of gas explosion to predict the gas explosion risk level of the future production system, providing a theoretical basis for improving the management level of gas explosion accident prevention.

Cause factors of coal mine gas explosion

The stable operation of the coal mine work system is affected by the nonlinear effects of many factors such as nature and society. The gas power system is constantly changing in the spatiotemporal sequence due to the unsafe behavior of humans, the unsafe state of things and the unsafe factors of the environment, showing complex nonlinear dynamic characteristics12. In this paper, 125 cases of large and above gas explosion accidents in China from 2010 to 2020 are counted. Because the information of many accidents, especially the process of accidents, is unclear. In the end, 100 accidents with exhaustive information are collected. Specifically, from the dimension of the accident occurrence process, each record covers three core aspects: the specific time (accurate to the hour) and location (including the mine name and underground working area) of the accident; underground operating conditions (e.g., the type of coal mining face, the layout of the ventilation system, and the status of gas monitoring equipment); and key stages of accident evolution (the complete process from initial abnormal gas accumulation, to the emergence of ignition sources, followed by explosion occurrence, and ultimately accident escalation). From the dimension of causal analysis, each record not only clearly identifies direct causes (e.g., gas concentration exceeding the safety threshold and reaching the explosive limit, the presence of ignition sources such as electric sparks generated by electrical equipment or frictional heat from mechanical operations, and ventilation equipment failure resulting in ineffective gas dilution) but also systematically sorts out indirect causes (e.g., fundamental issues including loopholes in safety management systems, workers’ non-compliance with operating procedures, and inadequate daily maintenance of safety monitoring equipment).

Combined with modern accident causation theory and system theory, according to the three basic conditions of gas explosion accidents, the causes of gas explosion accidents are analyzed in depth. Using grounded theory and statistical reasoning methods, through qualitative analysis and theoretical saturation tests, the causes of gas accumulation, ignition sources, behavioral errors and management errors are analyzed by cluster analysis. Based on previous research results13–15, combined with relevant regulations and on-site investigation, 27 cause factors are extracted from four aspects of human, machine, environment and management, as shown in Fig. 1.

Risk assessment index system of coal mine gas explosion.

Gas explosion accidents are caused by the coupling of the two disaster-causing factors are gas concentration overrun and ignition source. However, the ignition source and gas accumulation caused by mine gas explosion are affected by human factors. The unsafe behavior and management confusion are the main reasons leading to the gas accumulation and ignition source. Therefore, among the 125 large and above gas explosion accidents, the accidents caused by unsafe behavior accounted for 36.5%, management decision-making errors accounted for 34.1%, unsafe state of mechanical equipment accounted for 22.1%, and the unsafe state of mechanical equipment was mostly caused by management defects and unsafe behavior. The accidents caused by environmental unsafe factors accounted for 7.2%.

Through statistical analysis of what causes gas explosion accidents, it is confirmed that gas explosion accidents occur due to interaction and joint action of risk factors across various levels. When a certain risk factor is disturbed, it will associate with and trigger disturbances in other factors, promoting how specific factors occur and develop. This further leads to multi-factor coupling, and coupled risks collectively influence whether accidents occur. Therefore, analyzing risk factors for gas explosion accidents should not be limited to single-factor analysis. Instead, it is necessary to conduct multi-factor coupled risk analysis based on actual conditions. Among these risk factors, there are not only simple “physical” integration phenomena but also complex “chemical” reaction changes—these changes either raise risk levels or generate new risks. The coal mine gas dynamic system is a complex adaptive system made up of human, equipment, environment, and management. During system operation, interaction and joint action of various risk factors will form local coupled risks, thereby increasing the system’s risk intensity or generating new risks. When coupled risks reach the system’s acceptable risk threshold, an accident will occur even if the linear sum of individual risk factors in the system fails to exceed this acceptable threshold. This is the coupling effect during risk evolution16,17.

Methodology

Step1: G1-EWM comprehensive weight

The commonly used indicator weighting methods currently include subjective and objective weighting methods such as expert scoring method, analytic hierarchy process, entropy weight method, factor analysis method, data envelopment analysis method, correlation coefficient method, mean square error method, etc. Regardless of which weighting method is used, there are certain drawbacks. To improve the accuracy and credibility of weighting, several complementary weighting methods are usually selected for weight determination, known as combination weighting method. The combination weighting method has certain advantages, as it can not only utilize expert experience and knowledge to reflect the importance of indicators, but also objectively analyze the relationship between indicator data, optimize the weight allocation of risk indicators, and improve the credibility of evaluation results. Taking into account the advantages and disadvantages of various weighting methods, this article selects the order relation analysis method (G1) and entropy weight method (EWM) for weight calculation. The G1 method is subjective in calculating weights. To effectively reduce the impact of subjective preferences on evaluation results, the entropy weight method is used to modify the G1 method to obtain the comprehensive weights of indicators18,19.

Deductive model construction

System Dynamics (SD), proposed by Professor Forrester of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in the United States in 1956, is mainly used to analyze the structure and function of systems and conduct information feedback23. The research process of SD emphasizes the whole, connection, development and movement. ‘System’ refers to the large environment (elements, connections), ‘dynamics’ refers to the change of system state, and ‘science’ is the study of the influence of elements, that is, the interaction relationship. Based on the system science idea that ‘all systems must have a structure, and the system structure determines the system function’, according to the interaction relationship between system behavior and internal mechanism, it conducts dynamic evolution analysis of the coupling causal relationship among the constituent elements of the system over time. By changing conditions, it can coordinate among various factors within the system and optimize and manage the system structure and function.

According to the coupling correlation of various risk factors in the coal mine gas power system, predict the development trend of the system risk level at the next moment, and then conduct dynamic monitoring and early warning of the occurrence of gas explosion accidents. The occurrence of gas explosion accidents is sudden, and the evolution process of the accident is dynamic and complex. By studying the coupling causal relationship of various risk factors within the system and using system dynamics modeling to simulate the development and evolution process of the accident, it is of great significance for the prevention and control of gas explosion accidents. Through the above extraction of risk factors for gas explosion and the construction of the risk index system, this paper mainly conducts research from two conditions at the micro level and inherent risks - human - machine - environment - management, and determines 31 variables. In response to the changes in the indicators, it is also necessary to add factors with temporal sequence and coupled evolution characteristics, such as the rate of risk change and the coupling coefficient.

Determine the variable

Based on the causal relationship diagram of gas explosion and combined with the actual situation of the coal mine, clarify each variable in the system simulation operation process, including state variables, auxiliary variables, rate variables and constants. State variables are variables that describe the cumulative effect of a system; The rate variable is a variable that describes the speed of the cumulative effect, that is, the variation of the state variable over time. Auxiliary variables are intermediate variables in the process of information transmission and transformation between state variables and rate variables, and are used to describe the intermediate variables in the decision-making process. The constant is the coupling coefficient, that is, the coupling coefficient between two risk factors that have an associated relationship.

Model validation

Test the constructed system dynamics model to ensure that it can solve the problem under study. Common testing methods include: historical testing, authenticity testing and sensitivity testing, etc. In this paper, the historical test method is adopted for model testing. The initial values of each index in the model are set to the values obtained from a major safety inspection in June 2024 (some of the indicators inspected at that time were different from those listed in the system and were estimated based on similar indicators), and then the multi-factor coupled risk deduction model of coal mine gas explosion based on system dynamics is used for simulation. According to the intervention strategies adopted underground during the safety management work, the variable parameters were appropriately adjusted and exogenous variables were added. The data obtained in December 2024 and June 2025 were compared with the actual inspection data. Due to the certain error between the model and the actual situation, there is a deviation between the simulated data and the actual data. However, the level of the risk evolution simulation is consistent with the actual assessment level. It has passed the behavioral consistency test and the authenticity test, indicating that the model has certain applicability and feasibility.

Conclusion

(1) Coal mine gas explosion accidents are typical high-order nonlinear complex dynamic systems with feedback characteristics. Based on this, in accordance with the principle of system dynamics and combined with the above analysis and classification measurement of the coupled evolution path of multi-factor risks of coal mine gas explosion, a multi-factor coupled risk deduction model of gas explosion is established. Under the time series, this model can predict the development trend of the system risk level within a certain period in the future by controlling the variable parameters.

(2) It can be known from the system simulation results that the change in the risk level of coal mine gas explosion is the result of the mutual influence and joint action of various index variables within the system, while the change in the risk level of each index is due to the influence of other related indicators, reflecting the complex nonlinear characteristics of the gas power system. In addition, the risk values of system variables have a certain degree of lag and accumulation in their influence on other variables and the level of system risk. These two characteristics are the fundamental ones that lead to the occurrence of gas explosion accidents. The lag causes delays in risk control, while the accumulation turns the risk factors that have not been solved in time into accident hazards, resulting in a sudden increase in the system risk level and a qualitative change caused by quantitative changes.

(3) Through practical applications, this model has certain applicability and feasibility. Under the time series, by regulating the variable parameters, the future development trend of the system risk level can be predicted. The operation results of the model show a slow upward trend in the first six months, and then the risk level will increase suddenly to a certain extent. This fully reflects the lag and accumulation of the multi-factor coupled risk evolution of gas explosion, as well as the ‘emergence’ and ‘mutation’ phenomena of accident evolution. Its operation results can provide theoretical support for the advanced control of gas explosion risks.

Taking coal mine gas explosion accidents as the research subject, the model developed in this study exhibits a certain level of universality. Nevertheless, the model fails to integrate external dynamic variables, such as regional policy variations (e.g., the intensity of local coal mine safety supervision) and extreme natural conditions (e.g., the impacts of water inrush and earthquakes on gas drainage systems). Furthermore, substantial differences exist among individual coal mines; thus, adjustments to indicator selection and path design should be made in accordance with the actual operating conditions of each coal mine. Accordingly, in subsequent research, differential indicators, predictive indicators, and external dynamic variables will be incorporated to further optimize and refine the model, thereby enhancing its reliability in practical applications.

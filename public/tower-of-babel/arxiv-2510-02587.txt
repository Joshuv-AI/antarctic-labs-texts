A combinatorial formula for Interpolation Macdonald polynomials
Houcine Ben Dali, Lauren Kiyomi Williams

Abstract
. In 1996, Knop and Sahi introduced a remarkable family of inhomogeneous symmetric polynomials, defined via vanishing conditions, whose top homogeneous parts are exactly the Macdonald polynomials . Like the Macdonald polynomials, these interpolation Macdonald polynomials are closely connected to the Hecke algebra, and admit nonsymmetric versions, which generalize the nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials. In this paper we give a combinatorial formula for interpolation Macdonald polynomials in terms of signed multiline queues ; this formula generalizes the combinatorial formula for Macdonald polynomials in terms of multiline queues given by Corteel–Mandelshtam–Williams.

Remark 1.7
.

In
[
Mar20
]
, multiline queues were given an interpretation in terms of “priority queues,” with balls at each level representing customers who are each seeking a service on the level below.
We can also interpret our signed multiline queues as follows. The signed balls all represent customers, with the positive balls having “polite” and “attractive” characteristics and the negative balls having “needy” characteristics. The regular balls all represent services; they are always “polite”. We will work our way from top to bottom of the multiline queue: in Row
r^{\prime}
the customers seek a service from Row
r
below, and in Row
r
the services seek a customer from Row
(r-1)^{\prime}
below. When we construct pairings between two adjacent rows, we will start by examining the balls of largest absolute value in the higher row; if we need to break ties, we will work from right to left. The polite customers and services can choose any unused service/customer below, except that if there is an unused service immediately underneath them, politeness dictates that they must accept it; this explains the leftmost two diagrams in

Figure

2
. When we construct the pairings on a signed layer, because the negative balls/customers are so needy,
no ball (positive or negative) dares to pair with an unused service that is immediately below a negative customer who has not yet accepted a service;
this explains the third diagram in
Figure

2
. Since the positive balls/customers are attractive, there is always a service to be found just underneath them (though it may be taken already); this explains the fourth diagram in

Figure

2
. Finally, pairings initiated by services from Row
r
can wrap around, because servers “know the building”; however, pairings initiated by customers cannot.

1.3.
Combinatorial formulas for ASEP and interpolation ASEP polynomials

In this section we define weights for both multiline and signed multiline queues. We then use them to review the combinatorial formula for ASEP polynomials and give a new combinatorial formula for interpolation ASEP polynomials.

Definition 1.8
.

Let

\wt_{\pair}(Q)
of

Consider the pairings

1.4.
Integrality and Comparison with Okounkov’s Formula

Knop and Sahi proved that the
integral form
of the interpolation Macdonald polynomials
P_{\lambda}^{*}
satisfy an integrality property (see e.g
[
Kno97
, Corollary 5.5]
). This can be proved using our combinatorial formula for
P_{\lambda}^{*}
, and extended to interpolation ASEP polynomials
f^{*}_{\mu}
; see
Section

6.2
.

In
[
Oko98
]
, Okounkov gave a combinatorial formula for the interpolation symmetric Macdonald polynomials, which, to our knowledge, was the only such formula prior to our work. This formula is obtained by “shifting” Macdonald’s formula for the homogeneous symmetric Macdonald polynomials
[
Mac95
, Section VI.7]
. It expresses the polynomial
P_{\lambda}^{*}
as a sum over tableaux of shape
\lambda
, counted with coefficients given by products of
Pieri coefficients
. These coefficients are quite complicated to compute, and in particular, the integrality property is not apparent from this tableau formula.

The structure of this paper is as follows.
In
Section

2
we provide background on
interpolation polynomials; we also define interpolation ASEP polynomials, and give vanishing condition characterization of them. In
Section

3

we provide a recursion for interpolation ASEP polynomials from
packed
compositions; this
provides a base case for our subsequent arguments. We generalize this recursion to arbitrary compositions in
Section

4
. In
Section

5
we provide a combinatorial analysis of two-line signed multiline queues, and complete the proof of the main theorem.
In
Section

6
we give a tableaux formula for interpolation ASEP and Macdonald polynomials and prove an integrality result for them.
Finally in

Section

7
we give a factorization property for interpolation Macdonald polynomials at
q=1
.

2.
Background on interpolation polynomials

We now provide some more background on interpolation polynomials.
We also prove some properties of interpolation ASEP polynomials.

2.1.
Notation

Fix
n\geq 1
. Let
\mathbb{Y}_{n}
denote the set of integer partitions

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n})
with at most
n
parts. We let
|\lambda|
denote the sum
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}
of the parts of the partition and call it the
size
of
\lambda
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
denote the ring of polynomials in
n
variables, and
let
\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(d)}
denote the polynomials of degree at most
d
. Similarly, let
\Lambda_{n}
denote the ring of symmetric polynomials with
n
variables and let
\Lambda_{n}^{(d)}
denote the symmetric polynomials with degree at most
d
.
All the polynomials considered here will have coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}(q,t).

The symmetric group acts on
\mathbb{N}^{n}
by

(8)

\sigma\cdot(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{n}):=(\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(2)},\dots,\mu_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}).

For
\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
we will write

x^{\mu}:=x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{\mu_{n}}
. The symmetric group acts on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
by

\sigma(x^{\mu}):=x_{\sigma(1)}^{\mu_{1}}\dots x_{\sigma(N)}^{\mu_{n}}=x^{\sigma(\mu)}.

2.2.
Interpolation Macdonald polynomials

We now recall some of the main results of
[
Kno97
,
Sah96
]
.
Recall the notation
\widetilde{\mu}
from (
2
).

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Kno97
, Theorem 2.2]
)
.

Fix two integers
d,n\geq 1
, and fix a family
(a_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|\nu|\leq d}
in
\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
. Then there exists a unique polynomial
f\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(d)}
such that for any
|\nu|\leq d
we have
f(\widetilde{\nu})=a_{\nu}
.

In particular, if
f\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(d)}
such that for any
|\nu|\leq d
we have
f(\widetilde{\nu})=0
, then
f=0
.

Remark 2.2
.

Our notation is similar to but not identical to that of
[
Kno97
]
.
In particular, we have

(\widetilde{\mu})^{\rev}=\widebar{\mu^{\rev}},

where the sequence
\widebar{\mu}
is the one from
[
Kno97
]
, and
\nu^{\rev}:=(\nu_{n},\dots,\nu_{1})
. When
f
is symmetric, we have
f(\widetilde{\nu})=f(\widebar{\nu})
.

Polynomials defined by their evaluation on compositions as in
2.1
are known as
interpolation polynomials
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
Kno97
,
Sah96
]
)
.

Fix
\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
of size
d
. There exists a unique polynomial
E^{*}_{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(d)}
, called the
nonsymmetric interpolation Macdonald polynomial
, such that

•

[x^{\mu}]E^{*}_{\mu}=1
(so in particular,
E^{*}_{\mu}
has degree
d
),

•

E^{*}_{\mu}(\widetilde{\nu})=0
for any
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
satisfying
|\nu|\leq d
and
\nu\neq\mu
.

Moreover, the top homogeneous part of
E^{*}_{\mu}
is the nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomial
E_{\mu}
.

Note that the first part of
2.3
is a consequence of
2.1
. The second part giving the connection to Macdonald polynomials is however more surprising.

Remark 2.4
.

For any
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we have
E_{\nu}^{*}(\widetilde{\nu})\neq 0
. This is a consequence of
2.1
and the fact that
E_{\nu}
is not identically zero by definition.

For any partition
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}
of size
d
, we define the space
V_{\lambda}^{*}\subset\mathcal{P}^{(d)}_{n}
by

V_{\lambda}^{*}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(d)}|f(\widetilde{\nu})=0\text{ for any $|\nu|\leq|\lambda|$ and $\nu\notin S_{n}(\lambda)$}\right\}.

Lemma 2.5
.

We have

V_{\lambda}^{*}=\Span_{\mathbb{Q}(q,t)}\left\{E^{*}_{\mu}|\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)\right\}.

Proof.

The inclusion
\supseteq
is direct from
2.3
. We now fix
f
in
V_{\lambda}^{*}
and we want to prove that
f
is a linear combination of
E^{*}_{\mu}
for
\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)
. We define

g(\bm{x})=\sum_{\nu\in S_{n}(\lambda)}\frac{f(\widetilde{\mu})}{E^{*}_{\nu}(\widetilde{\nu})}E^{*}_{\nu}(\bm{x}).

We claim that
f=g
. Indeed,
f(\widetilde{\nu})=g(\widetilde{\nu})
for all compositions
|\nu|\leq|\lambda|
. Hence
f
and
g
are of degree at most
|\lambda|
and agree on all compositions of size at most
|\lambda|
. By
2.1
, we get that
f=g
.
∎

In a similar way, one shows that

\mathcal{P}_{n}=\oplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}}V_{\lambda}^{*}
; see also
[
Kno97
, Corollary 2.6]
. In particular,
\{E^{*}_{\mu}:\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

2.3.
Hecke Operators

For
1\leq i\leq n-1
, we let
s_{i}=(i,i+1)
denote the transposition exchanging
i
and
i+1
.
The
Hecke operator

T_{i}
, which acts on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
,
is defined by

(9)

T_{i}:=t-\frac{tx_{i}-x_{i+1}}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}(1-s_{i}).

These operators satisfy the relations of the Hecke algebra of type
A_{n-1}

(10)

\begin{array}[]{ll}(T_{i}-t)(T_{i}+1)=0&\text{for $1\leq i\leq n-1$}\\
T_{i}T_{i+1}T_{i}=T_{i+1}T_{i}T_{i+1}&\text{for $1\leq i\leq n-2$}\\
T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i}&\text{for $|i-j|>1$}.\\
\end{array}

If
\sigma\in S_{n}
and
\sigma=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{\ell}}
is a reduced decomposition of
\sigma
, we define

(11)

T_{\sigma}:=T_{i_{1}}\dots T_{i_{\ell}}.

It follows from (
10
) that this definition is independent of the choice of reduced expression.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
Kno97
, Corollary 3.2]
)
.

For any
i
, we have that
T_{i}(V_{\lambda}^{*})\subset V_{\lambda}^{*}.
In particular, using
2.5
,
we conclude that
T_{i}E_{\nu}^{*}
is a linear combination of

E_{\mu}^{*}
for
\mu\in S_{n}(\nu)
.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
Kno97
, Corollary 3.4]
or
[
Sah96
, Theorem 4.5]
)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that
\mu_{i}=\mu_{i+1}.

Then
E^{*}_{\mu}
is symmetric in
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
, or equivalently

T_{i}E^{*}_{\mu}=tE^{*}_{\mu}.

We note that Hecke operators can be used to define an inhomogeneous analogue of Cherednik operators, which act diagonally on non-symmetric Macdonald polynomials; see
[
Kno97
,
Sah96
]
, or
[
BDW25
, Section 2.6]
.

2.4.
ASEP and interpolation ASEP polynomials

Recall the definition of the
ASEP polynomial
f_{\mu}
and the interpolation
ASEP polynomial
f^{*}_{\mu}
from
Definition

1.2
. They were defined in terms of the Hecke operators, and the permutation
\sigma_{\mu}\in S_{n}
, which is the shortest permutation such that
\sigma_{\mu}(\lambda)=\mu
. Intuitively,
\sigma_{\mu}
sends the left-most part of size
i
in
\lambda
to the left-most part of size
i
in
\mu
, the second left-most to the second left-most, and so on.

Example 2.8
.

If
\lambda=(4,4,3,3,1)
and
\mu=(3,4,1,4,3)
, then
\sigma_{\mu}=(2,4,1,5,3)
.

Lemma 2.9
.

If
\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)
, then
f^{*}_{\mu}\in V_{\lambda}^{*}
.

Proof.

This follows from
2.6
.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

For
1\leq i\leq n-1
, the interpolation ASEP polynomials
f^{*}_{\mu}
satisfy:

(1)

T_{i}f^{*}_{\mu}=f^{*}_{s_{i}\mu}
if
\mu_{i}>\mu_{i+1}
,

(2)

T_{i}f^{*}_{\mu}=tf^{*}_{\mu}
if
\mu_{i}=\mu_{i+1}
,

(3)

T_{i}f^{*}_{\mu}=(t-1)f^{*}_{\mu}+tf^{*}_{s_{i}\mu}
if
\mu_{i}<\mu_{i+1}
.

Proof.

Let
\mu^{\prime}:=s_{i}\cdot\mu
. If
\mu_{i}>\mu_{i+1}
then
\sigma_{\mu^{\prime}}=s_{i}\sigma_{\mu}
. Using the fact that
\ell(s_{i}\sigma_{\mu})=\ell(\sigma_{\mu})+1
, we get

f^{*}_{\mu^{\prime}}=T_{\sigma_{\mu^{\prime}}}\cdot E_{\lambda}^{*}=T_{i}T_{\sigma_{\mu}}\cdot E^{*}_{\lambda}=T_{i}\cdot f^{*}_{\mu},

which gives
Item

1
.

We now assume that
\mu_{i}=\mu_{i+1}
. We then have
\mu=\sigma_{\mu}(\lambda)=s_{i}\sigma_{\mu}(\lambda)
which implies, by definition of
\sigma_{\mu}
, that
\ell(s_{i}\sigma_{\mu})=\ell(\sigma_{\mu})+1
. Hence, we get as above that
T_{i}f^{*}_{\mu}=T_{s_{i}\sigma_{\mu}}\cdot E_{\lambda}^{*}.

Consider now the transposition
s_{j}:=\sigma_{\mu}^{-1}s_{i}\sigma_{\mu}
. Since
s_{i}\mu=\mu
, we get
s_{j}\lambda=\lambda
. We deduce that
\ell(\sigma_{\mu}s_{j})=\ell(s_{i}\sigma_{\mu})=\ell(\sigma_{\mu})+1.
Hence,

T_{i}\cdot f^{*}_{\mu}=T_{s_{i}\sigma_{\mu}}\cdot E_{\lambda}^{*}=T_{\sigma_{\mu}}T_{j}\cdot E_{\lambda}^{*}.

Using
2.7
and the fact that
s_{j}\lambda=\lambda
we deduce that

T_{i}\cdot f^{*}_{\mu}=tT_{\sigma_{\mu}}\cdot E_{\lambda}^{*}=tf_{\mu}^{*}.\qed

Item

3
follows from
Item

1
and the relations of
Eq.

10
.

Remark 2.11
.

It is well known that the usual ASEP polynomials also satisfy the relations of
2.10
, see e.g.
[
CMW22
]
. One can prove this using the same proof as above.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
be a partition and let
V_{\lambda}:=\Span_{\mathbb{Q}(q,t)}\{E_{\mu}\ |\ \mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)\}
. The ASEP polynomials

\{f_{\mu}\ |\ \mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)\}
form a basis for the space
V_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

The fact that
f_{\mu}\in V_{\lambda}

comes from
2.5
and
2.9
by taking the top homogeneous part.
Now using
1.10
, it follows that
for each
\tau\in S_{n}(\lambda)
, the only

f_{\mu}
for
\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)
which contains
the monomial
x^{\tau}
with a nonzero coefficient
is
f_{\tau}
. Thus, the elements of

\{f_{\mu}\ |\ \mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)\}
are linearly independent, and hence form a basis. See also
[
CdGW15b
, Section 1]
for a proof sketch of this result.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

The polynomials
\{f_{\mu}|\ |\mu|=n\}

form a basis for the space of polynomials
of degree
n
.

We have similar results for interpolation ASEP polynomials.

Proposition 2.14
.

The interpolation ASEP polynomials

\{f_{\mu}^{*}\ |\ \mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)\}
form a basis for the space
V^{*}_{\lambda}
. As a consequence,
\{f_{\mu}^{*}\ |\ \mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}
is basis of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

Proof.

The fact that
f^{*}_{\mu}\in V_{\lambda}^{*}
was proven in
2.9
. Now for
\tau\in S_{n}(\lambda)
, the coefficients of
x^{\tau}
in
f^{*}_{\mu}
only depends on the top homogeneous part of
f^{*}_{\mu}
, namely
f_{\mu}
. And we deduce from
1.10
that

[x^{\tau}]f^{*}_{\mu}=\delta_{\tau,\mu}.

As in the proof of
2.12
, this implies that
f^{*}_{\mu}
are linearly independent.
∎

Recall that
P_{\lambda}^{*}
are the interpolation symmetric Macdonald polynomials defined by
1.1
.

Proposition 2.15
.

For any partition
\lambda
, we have

P^{*}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)}f^{*}_{\mu}.

The proof is similar to the proof of
[
CdGW15b
, Lemma 3]
or
[
CMW22
, Lemma 1.24]
.

Proof.

Let
g:=\sum_{\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)}f^{*}_{\mu}.
From
2.9
we know that
g\in V_{\lambda}^{*}
, and thus satisfies the vanishing conditions defining the symmetric polynomial
P_{\lambda}
:
g(\widetilde{\rho})=0
for any partition
|\rho|\leq|\lambda|
with
\rho\neq\lambda
.

We now show that
g
is symmetric. Using the relations of
2.10
, we show that for any
i
we have
T_{i}\cdot g=tg
. This implies that
s_{i}g=g
meaning that
g\in\Lambda_{n}^{(|\lambda|)}\cap V_{\lambda}^{*}
. Hence
g
is a scalar multiple of
P^{*}_{\lambda}
.

Finally, we know from
[
CMW22
, Theorem 1.11]
that the top homogeneous part of

is

\sum_{\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)}f_{\mu}=P_{\lambda}.

Thus by
1.1
,
g
must be equal to
P_{\lambda}^{*}
.
∎

2.5.
Characterization of interpolation ASEP polynomials

In this section, we give a characterization of interpolation ASEP polynomials with vanishing conditions, which thus justifies their name.

We recall that
the dominance order
on partitions is the partial order such that
\lambda\leq\mu
if
|\lambda|<|\mu|
or
|\lambda|=|\mu|
and

\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{i}\leq\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{i},\quad\text{for any $1\leq i\leq n$}.

Fix
\kappa
and
\nu
in
\mathbb{N}^{n}
, and let
\lambda
and
\mu
be the two corresponding partitions.
We then define the partial order
<
on
\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that

\kappa\leq\nu
if and only if either
\lambda<\mu
or

\lambda=\mu\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa_{1}+\dots+\kappa_{i}\geq\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{i},\quad\text{for any $1\leq i\leq n$}.

We have the following triangularity property of
E^{*}_{\mu}
.

Theorem 2.16

(
[
Kno97
, Theorem 3.11]
)
.

Given a composition
\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we have

E^{*}_{\mu}=x^{\mu}+\sum_{\nu<\mu}c_{\mu,\nu}x^{\nu},

for some coefficients
c_{\mu,\nu}\in\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
and where the sum is taken over compositions
\nu
smaller than
\mu
with respect to the partial order defined above.

Theorem 2.17
.

Fix
\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)
of size
d
. Then
f^{*}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is the unique polynomial
g\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(d)}
such that:

•

for any composition
\nu
such that
|\nu|\leq|\mu|
and
\nu\notin S_{n}(\lambda)
, we have

g(\widetilde{\nu})=0
.

•

for
\tau\in S_{n}(\lambda)
, then

[x^{\tau}]g=\delta_{\tau,\mu}.

Recall that the first condition is equivalent to saying that
g\in V_{\lambda}^{*}
.

Proof.

The fact that
f^{*}_{\mu}\in V_{\lambda}^{*}
was proven in
2.9
. Now for
\tau\in S_{n}(\lambda)
, the coefficients of
x^{\tau}
in
f^{*}_{\mu}
only depends on the top homogeneous part of
f^{*}_{\mu}
, namely
f_{\mu}
. And we deduce from
1.10
that

[x^{\tau}]f^{*}_{\mu}=\delta_{\tau,\mu}.

Let us now prove that
f^{*}_{\mu}
is the unique polynomial satisfying the properties of the proposition. Let
g
be a polynomial satisfying these properties and set
h:=f^{*}_{\mu}-g
. We want to prove that
h=0
. We have that
h\in V_{\lambda}^{*}
and that
[x^{\tau}]h=0
for
\tau\in S_{n}(\lambda)
.
By
2.5
, we can then expand it

h=\sum_{\tau\in S_{n}(\lambda)}d_{\tau}E^{*}_{\tau}.

We want to prove that the coefficients
d_{\tau}
are all zero. Suppose that this is not the case, and let
\kappa
be a maximal element in the set
\{\tau\in S_{n}(\lambda):d_{\tau}\neq 0\}
.
We then have from
2.16
that

[x^{\kappa}]h=d_{\kappa}\neq 0
which is a contradiction.
∎

3.
An algebraic recursion for
f^{*}_{\mu}

when
\mu
is packed

We start this section by recalling the
two-line recursion
for homogeneous ASEP polynomials established in
[
CMW22
]
, see
3.1
; its combinatorial analogue in terms of multiline queues is in
5.2
.
Our goal will be to
give an analogue of
3.1
for interpolation ASEP polynomials indexed by
packed compositions
, see
3.3
.

Given a composition
\nu
, let
\nu^{-}:=(\nu^{-}_{1},\dots,\nu^{-}_{n})
,
where

\nu^{-}_{i}=\max(\nu_{i}-1,0)
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
CMW22
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Fix a composition
\mu
. There exists a family of coefficients
a_{\mu}^{\nu}\in\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
such that

(12)

f_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\left(\prod_{i:\mu_{i}>0}x_{i}\right)\sum_{\nu}a^{\nu}_{\mu}f_{\nu^{-}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

where the sum runs over compositions
\nu
which are permutations of
\mu
after removing the 1’s from
\mu
.

Definition 3.2
.

For fixed
k,n
with
1\leq k\leq n
, we say that a composition

\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})
is
packed
of
type
(k,n)
if

\mu_{i}\neq 0
for
i\leq k
and

\mu_{i}=0
for
i>k
.
Let
\Pack(k,n)
denote the set of all packed compositions of type
(k,n)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mu\in\Pack(k,n)
be a packed composition. Then

(13)

f_{\mu}^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(x_{i}-t^{-n+1})\sum_{\nu}a^{\nu}_{\mu}q^{|\nu^{-}|}f^{*}_{\nu^{-}}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{q}\right),

where
a_{\mu}^{\nu}
are the coefficients of
Eq.

12
(see also
Eq.

37
).

Recall that
a_{\mu}^{\nu}
is 0 unless
\nu
is a permutation of
\mu
after removing the 1’s of
\mu
. In particular, if
\nu
contributes to the sum of
Eq.

13
, then
|\nu^{-}|=|\mu|-k
.

3.3
will be proved in 3 steps:

(Step 1)

We prove that we can write

(14)

f_{\mu}^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(x_{i}-t^{-n+1})\ Q(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

where
\deg(Q(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))=|\mu|-k
.

(Step 2)

In
Eq.

14
, we have

Q(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{\nu:|\nu|=|\mu|-k}b_{\mu}^{\nu}\ q^{|\mu|-k}\ f_{\nu}^{*}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{q},\frac{x_{2}}{q},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{q}\right)

where
b_{\mu}^{\nu}\in\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
.

(Step 3)

The coefficients
b_{\mu}^{\nu}
in
(Step 2)

are directly related to the coefficients
a_{\mu}^{\nu}

from

Eq.

12
.
More precisely, for any composition
\nu
without parts of size 1, we have
b^{\nu^{-}}_{\mu}=a^{\nu}_{\mu}
.

Before proving the theorem, we need a little bit of preparation.
We start by recalling the
shape permuting
operator
from
[
HHL08
, Equation (17)]
.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
HHL08
]
)
.

Let
\nu
be a composition, and suppose
\nu_{i}>\nu_{i+1}
.
Write

r_{i}(\nu)=\#\{j<i\mid\nu_{i+1}<\nu_{j}\leq\nu_{i}\}+\#\{j>i\mid\nu_{i+1}\leq\nu_{j}<\nu_{i}\}.

Then

(15)

E_{s_{i}\nu}(\bm{x};q,t)=\left(T_{i}+\frac{1-t}{1-q^{\nu_{i}-\nu_{i+1}}t^{r_{i}(\nu)}}\right)E_{\nu}(\bm{x};q,t).

Lemma 3.5

(
[
Kno97
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Fix a polynomial
f\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and a composition
\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Then, for any
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
(T_{i}f)(\tilde{\mu})
is a linear combination of
f(\widetilde{\mu})
and
f(\widetilde{s_{i}\mu})
.

We use this lemma to give an analogue of
3.4
for interpolation polynomials.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\nu
be a composition, and suppose
\nu_{i}>\nu_{i+1}
. Then

(16)

E^{*}_{s_{i}\nu}(\bm{x};q,t)=\left(T_{i}+\frac{1-t}{1-q^{\nu_{i+1}-\nu_{i}}t^{r_{i}(\nu)}}\right)E^{*}_{\nu}(\bm{x};q,t).

Proof.

We start by proving that
T_{i}E^{*}_{\nu}(\bm{x};q,t)
is a linear combination of
E^{*}_{\nu}(\bm{x};q,t)
and
E^{*}_{s_{i}\nu}(\bm{x};q,t).

Since
T_{i}
is a homogeneous operator,
T_{i}E^{*}_{\nu}
has degree
|\nu|
. Moreover, using
3.5
and the vanishing conditions satisfied by
E^{*}_{\nu}

(
2.3
), we get that for any
\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with
|\mu|\leq|\nu|
and
\mu\notin\{\nu,s_{i}\nu\}
, we have
(T_{i}E^{*}_{\nu})(\widetilde{\mu})=0
. Using now the fact that

T_{i}E_{\nu}^{*}
is a linear combination of

E_{\mu}^{*}
for
|\mu|=|\nu|
(
2.6
), and

Remark

2.4
, we conclude that
the coefficient of
E^{*}_{\mu}
in
T_{i}E^{*}_{\nu}
is
0
for all
\mu\notin\{\nu,s_{i}\nu\}
.

To get the coefficients of this linear expansion, it is enough to look at the top homogeneous part. We then conclude using
3.4
.
∎

The fact that
T_{i}E^{*}_{\nu}
is a linear combination of
E^{*}_{\nu}
and
E^{*}_{s_{i}\nu}
will be useful later. Although the explicit coefficients of this expansion will not be needed, we provide them here for completeness.

4.
An algebraic recursion for
f_{\mu}^{*}
indexed by arbitrary compositions

The main goal of the next two sections is to finish the proof of
1.15
.
There are two main steps, the first of which is algebraic while the second is combinatorial:

•

We start from the recursion given for the interpolation ASEP polynomials
f^{*}_{\mu}
in
3.3
, when
\mu
is a packed composition.
By applying Hecke operators to this recursion, we generalize it to any composition
\mu
. This recursion involves a family of coefficients
(b^{\alpha}_{\mu})
defined in
Definition

4.8
, and encoded by the action of the Hecke operators on a variant
f_{\alpha}
of the ASEP polynomials, indexed by signed compositions. This step corresponds to
4.10
.

•

We show that the generating function of one single signed layer satisfies the same recursion as the coefficients
(b^{\alpha}_{\mu})
, see
5.5
. Thus, we show that the
algebraic recursion
for the polynomials
f^{*}_{\mu}
corresponds to a
combinatorial recursion
for signed MLQs. The combinatorial recursion encodes the fact that if we remove the bottom signed and the bottom classic layers of a signed MLQ with
2L
rows, we obtain a signed MLQ with
2(L-1)
rows, see
5.6
. The main theorem is then obtained by induction on the number of rows.

4.1.
Some preliminaries about Hecke operators

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix a polynomial in
n
variables
A\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and let
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Then

(20)

\displaystyle T_{i}(x_{i}x_{i+1}A)=x_{i}x_{i+1}T_{i}(A),

(21)

\displaystyle T_{i}(x_{i}A)=x_{i+1}T_{i}(A)+(1-t)x_{i+1}A,

(22)

\displaystyle T_{i}(x_{i+1}A)=x_{i}T_{i}(A)-(1-t)x_{i+1}A.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle T_{i}(x_{i}x_{i+1}A)

\displaystyle=tx_{i}x_{i+1}A-\frac{tx_{i}-x_{i+1}}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}(x_{i}x_{i+1}A-s_{i}(x_{i}x_{i+1}A))

\displaystyle=x_{i}x_{i+1}T_{i}(A).

This gives
Eq.

20
. Notice that, more generally,
T_{i}(BA)=BT_{i}(A)
for any polynomial
B
which is symmetric in
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
. We now prove
Eq.

21

\displaystyle T_{i}(x_{i}A)

\displaystyle=tx_{i}A-\frac{tx_{i}-x_{i+1}}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}(x_{i}A-s_{i}(x_{i}A))

\displaystyle=tx_{i}A-\frac{tx_{i}-x_{i+1}}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}(x_{i}A-x_{i+1}s_{i}(A))

\displaystyle=x_{i+1}T_{i}(A)+(1-t)x_{i+1}A.

We obtain similarly
Eq.

22

\displaystyle T_{i}(x_{i+1}A)

\displaystyle=tx_{i+1}A-\frac{tx_{i}-x_{i+1}}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}(x_{i+1}A-s_{i}(x_{i+1}A))

\displaystyle=tx_{i+1}A-\frac{tx_{i}-x_{i+1}}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}(x_{i+1}A-x_{i}s_{i}(A))

\displaystyle=x_{i}T_{i}(A)-(1-t)x_{i+1}A.\qed

Combining
Eqs.

20
,
21
and
22
, we obtain that if a polynomial
A
is divisible by
x_{i}
, then

(23)

T_{i}(A/x_{i})=\frac{1}{x_{i+1}}T_{i}(A)-(1-t)\frac{A}{x_{i}},

and if it is divisible by
x_{i+1}
then

(24)

T_{i}(A/x_{i+1})=\frac{1}{x_{i}}T_{i}(A)+(1-t)\frac{A}{x_{i}}.

Finally, if
A
is divisible by
x_{i}x_{i+1}
then

(25)

T_{i}(A/x_{i}x_{i+1})=\frac{1}{x_{i}x_{i+1}}T_{i}(A).

4.2.
Action of Hecke operators on extended ASEP polynomials

For any composition
\mu
, we define the polynomial

\widehat{f^{*}_{\mu}}:=q^{|\mu|}f^{*}_{\mu}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{q}\right).

The Hecke operators act in the same way on three versions of ASEP polynomials.

Lemma 4.2
.

For a composition
\mu
, and for
g\in\{f,f^{*},\widehat{f^{*}}\}
, we have

\displaystyle T_{i}(g_{\mu})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g_{s_{i}\mu}&\text{ if $\mu_{i}>\mu_{i+1}$,}\\
tg_{\mu}&\text{ if $\mu_{i}=\mu_{i+1}$,}\\
tg_{s_{i}\mu}-(1-t)g_{\mu}&\text{ if $\mu_{i}<\mu_{i+1}$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

The result is known for the functions
f_{\mu}
(see
Remark

2.11
) and for the functions
f^{*}_{\mu}
(see
2.10
). Let us check it for
\widehat{f^{*}_{\mu}}
. First notice that the linear map

\phi_{r}:h(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\longmapsto q^{r}h\left(\frac{x_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{q}\right)

acts diagonally on a homogeneous function
h
of degree
m
:
\phi_{r}(h)=q^{r-m}h
. Since the operators
T_{r}
are homogeneous, we have
T_{i}\circ\phi_{r}(h)=\phi_{r}\circ T_{i}(h).

We now write

T_{i}\left(\widehat{f^{*}_{\mu}}\right)=T_{i}\circ\phi_{|\mu|}(f^{*}_{\mu})=\phi_{|\mu|}\circ T_{i}(f^{*}_{\mu})
. We conclude using the fact that
T_{i}(f^{*}_{\mu})
is a linear combination of
f^{*}_{\mu}
and
f^{*}_{s_{i}\mu}
, and that
|s_{i}\mu|=|\mu|
.
∎

We recall from
[
CMW22
]
that the homogeneous ASEP polynomial
f_{\mu}
is divisible by
\prod_{i:\mu_{i}>0}x_{i}
(see also
3.1
), which corresponds to the weights of the balls in Row 1. In
Definition

4.3
below we extend the definition of ASEP polynomials to all
signed
compositions; here we assign a weight of
t^{-n+1}
to “negative” balls.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, set

(29)

\lVert\alpha\rVert:=(|\alpha_{1}|,\dots,|\alpha_{n}|).

Definition 4.3
.

Fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
We define the
extended ASEP polynomial

f_{\alpha}:=\frac{f_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}}{\prod_{i:\alpha_{i}<0}(-t^{n-1}x_{i})}.

Proposition 4.4
.

Given
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
1\leq i\leq n-1
, we have the following action of
T_{i}
on
f_{\alpha}
.

(1)

Case
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1}\geq 0
or
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1}<0
: we have

\displaystyle T_{i}(f_{\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{s_{i}\alpha}&\text{ if $\alpha_{i}>\alpha_{i+1}\geq 0$, or $-\alpha_{i}>-\alpha_{i+1}>0$}\\
tf_{\alpha}&\text{ if $\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i+1}\geq 0$, or $-\alpha_{i}=-\alpha_{i+1}>0$}\\
tf_{s_{i}\alpha}-(1-t)f_{\alpha}&\text{ if $\alpha_{i+1}>\alpha_{i}\geq 0$, or $-\alpha_{i+1}>-\alpha_{i}>0$}\end{array}\right.

(2)

Case
\alpha_{i}\geq 0
and
\alpha_{i+1}<0
: we have

\displaystyle T_{i}(f_{\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{s_{i}\alpha}+(1-t)f_{\alpha_{1},\dots,-\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots}&\text{ if $\alpha_{i}>-\alpha_{i+1}>0$,}\\
f_{s_{i}\alpha}&\text{ if $\alpha_{i}=-\alpha_{i+1}>0$,}\\
tf_{s_{i}\alpha}&\text{ if $-\alpha_{i+1}>\alpha_{i}\geq 0$.}\end{array}\right.

(3)

Case
\alpha_{i}<0
and
\alpha_{i+1}\geq 0
: we have

\displaystyle T_{i}(f_{\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{s_{i}\alpha}-(1-t)f_{\alpha}&\text{ if $-\alpha_{i}>\alpha_{i+1}\geq 0$,}\\
tf_{s_{i}\alpha}-(1-t)f_{\alpha}&\text{ if $-\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i+1}>0$,}\\
tf_{s_{i}\alpha}-(1-t)\left(f_{\alpha}+f_{\alpha_{1},\dots,-\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots}\right)&\text{ if $\alpha_{i+1}>-\alpha_{i}>0$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We start by proving the case
(1)
. When
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1}\geq 0
, we have

T_{i}(f_{\alpha})=T_{i}\cdot\prod_{j:\alpha_{j}<0}\frac{1}{-t^{n-1}x_{j}}f_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}=\prod_{j:\alpha_{j}<0}\frac{1}{-t^{n-1}x_{j}}T_{i}\left(f_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}\right).

We use here the fact that
\prod_{j:\alpha_{j}<0}\frac{1}{-t^{n-1}x_{j}}
is independent from
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
(and is in particular symmetric in these variables).
The result follows then from the action of
T_{i}
on the (non extended) ASEP polynomials
4.2
.

Now if
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1}<0
, then

T_{i}(f_{\alpha})=(-t)^{-2n+2}T_{i}(f_{\dots,-\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots}/(x_{i}x_{i+1}))=(-t)^{-2n+2}T_{i}(f_{\dots,-\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots})/(x_{i}x_{i+1})

by
Eq.

25
.
Since
-\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1}>0
, we can use the equations proved above to conclude.

Similarly, the other cases are obtained from
(1)
using
Eqs.

23
and
24
. For example, let us check the case (2), i.e when
\alpha_{i}\geq 0
and
\alpha_{i+1}<0
. We have

T_{i}(f_{\alpha})=(-t)^{-n+1}T_{i}\left(f_{\dots,\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots}/x_{i+1}\right).

Applying
Eq.

24
, we get

T_{i}(f_{\alpha})=\frac{(-t)^{-n+1}}{x_{i}}T_{i}(f_{\dots,\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots})+\frac{(-t)^{-n+1}(1-t)}{x_{i}}f_{\dots,\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots}.

We now use case (1):

•

If
\alpha_{i}>-\alpha_{i+1}>0
, then

T_{i}(f_{\alpha})=\frac{(-t)^{-n+1}}{x_{i}}f_{\dots,-\alpha_{i+1},\alpha_{i},\dots}+\frac{(-t)^{-n+1}(1-t)}{x_{i}}f_{\dots,\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots}=f_{s_{i}\alpha}+(1-t)f_{\dots,-\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots}.

•

If
\alpha_{i}=-\alpha_{i+1}>0
, then

\displaystyle T_{i}(f_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=(-t)^{-n+1}\frac{t}{x_{i}}f_{\dots,\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i},\dots}+(-t)^{-n+1}\frac{1-t}{x_{i}}f_{\dots,\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i},\dots}

\displaystyle=(-t)^{-n+1}\frac{1}{x_{i}}f_{\dots,\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i},\dots}=f_{s_{i}\alpha}.

•

If
-\alpha_{i+1}>\alpha_{i+1}>0
, then

\displaystyle T_{i}(f_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-t)^{-n+1}}{x_{i}}tf_{\dots,-\alpha_{i+1},\alpha_{i},\dots}-(-t)^{-n+1}\frac{1-t}{x_{i}}f_{\dots,\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots}+(-t)^{-n+1}\frac{1-t}{x_{i}}f_{\dots,\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots}

\displaystyle=tf_{\dots,\alpha_{i+1},\alpha_{i},\dots}

as desired.

We leave the proof of case (3) as an exercise.

Definition 4.5
.

For

, we define the infinite matrix
\mathcal{N}^{(i)}=(\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta})_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
as the matrix with entries in

which encodes the action of

on the polynomials

as given in

For example, if

then

\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,(\dots,-\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i+1},\dots)}=1-t.

This matrix is quasi-diagonal: if

, then

.

4.3.
The polynomials

Recall the definition of

from

Also recall from (

For any

Using

The polynomials

We now define a family of polynomials

which can be thought of as an intermediate step between the homogeneous polynomials

and the interpolation polynomials

Definition 4.6
.

Given
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we define

h_{\alpha}:=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\wt_{\alpha}a^{\lambda}_{\|\alpha\|}\widehat{f^{*}_{\lambda^{-}}}.

Lemma 4.7
.

The action of the operator
T_{i}
on
h_{\alpha}
, is the same as its action on
f_{\alpha}
. In other words,

T_{i}(h_{\alpha})=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta}h_{\beta}.

Proof.

We start by proving the result when
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1}\leq 0
. First, notice that in this case
\wt_{\alpha}
is independent from
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
. We then have

\displaystyle T_{i}(h_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=T_{i}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\wt_{\alpha}a^{\lambda}_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}\widehat{f^{*}_{\lambda^{-}}}\right),

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\wt_{\alpha}a^{\lambda}_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}T_{i}\left(\widehat{f^{*}_{\lambda^{-}}}\right),

since
a_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}^{\lambda}=a_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}^{\lambda}(q,t)
is independent from the variables
x_{j}
. Hence, using
4.2
and
Definition

4.5
, we have

(32)

\displaystyle T_{i}(h_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\wt_{\alpha}a^{\lambda}_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\lambda^{-},\nu}\widehat{f^{*}_{\nu}}.

We now compute
T_{i}(f_{\alpha})
in two different ways, obtained by applying
Definition

4.5
and (
30
) in one order or the other. On the one hand, we have

(33)

T_{i}(f_{\alpha})=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta}f_{\beta}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta}\wt_{\beta}a_{\lVert\beta\rVert}^{\kappa}f_{\kappa^{-}}.

On the other hand,

(34)

\displaystyle T_{i}(f_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=T_{i}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\wt_{\alpha}a^{\lambda}_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}f_{\lambda^{-}}\right)=\sum_{\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\wt_{\alpha}a^{\lambda}_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\lambda^{-},\nu}f_{\nu}.

Notice that with the assumption
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1}\leq 0
, the coefficient
\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta}
is zero unless
\beta\in\{\alpha,s_{i}\alpha\}
(see
4.4
item (1)). In particular, we have
\wt_{\beta}=\wt_{\alpha}
.
We can then divide
Eq.

33
and
Eq.

34
by
\wt_{\alpha}
, and we compare the coefficients of

in the two equations (recall that ASEP polynomials are a basis by

). We get

(35)

\sum_{\kappa:\kappa^{-}=\nu}\ \sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta}a_{\lVert\beta\rVert}^{\kappa}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}a^{\lambda}_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\lambda^{-},\nu},

Injecting this into
Eq.

, we get

\displaystyle T_{i}(h_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\wt_{\alpha}\widehat{f^{*}_{\nu}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}a^{\lambda}_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\lambda^{-},\nu}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\ \sum_{\kappa:\kappa^{-}=\nu}a_{\lVert\beta\rVert}^{\kappa}\wt_{\beta}\widehat{f^{*}_{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}a_{\lVert\beta\rVert}^{\kappa}\wt_{\beta}\widehat{f^{*}_{\kappa^{-}}}=\sum_{\beta}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta}h_{\beta}.

This finishes the proof of the lemma in the case

The other cases can be derived from this one using

,
21
and

(just as the cases in

4.4.
Recursive decomposition for the polynomials
f^{*}_{\mu}

Definition 4.8
.

Let
(b^{\alpha}_{\mu})_{\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}

be the family of coefficients satisfying the following properties:

(1)

If
\mu\in\Pack(k,n)
for some
k\leq n
, then

b^{\alpha}_{\mu}=\delta_{\mu,\lVert\alpha\rVert}.

(2)

Given
1\leq i\leq n-1
such that
\mu_{i}>0
and
\mu_{i+1}=0
, we have

b^{\alpha}_{s_{i}\mu}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}b^{\beta}_{\mu}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\beta,\alpha}.

It is clear from the definition that if such a family
(b^{\alpha}_{\mu})
exists then it is unique. The existence will be proven combinatorially in
5.5
.

Remark 4.9
.

Note that for the family
(b^{\alpha}_{\mu})
satisfying the recursion of
Definition

4.8
, the coefficient
b^{\alpha}_{\mu}
is 0 unless
\alpha
is a signed permutation of
\mu
, i.e there exists a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
and a choice of signs
\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{n}\in\{\pm 1\}
such that

\alpha=(\epsilon_{1}\sigma(\mu_{1}),\dots,\epsilon_{n}\sigma(\mu_{n}))
. This can be obtained by induction on
\mu
and using the fact that
\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta}
is 0 unless
\alpha
is a signed permutation of
\beta
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
(b^{\alpha}_{\mu})
be the family of coefficients satisfying the recursion of
Definition

4.8
. Define the polynomials
f^{*\lambda}_{\mu}
by

f^{*\lambda}_{\mu}=f^{*\lambda}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q,t):=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}b^{\alpha}_{\mu}\wt_{\alpha}a^{\lambda}_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}.

We then have

f^{*}_{\mu}=\sum_{\lambda}f^{*\lambda}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q,t)q^{|\lambda^{-}|}f^{*}_{\lambda^{-}}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{q};q,t\right)=\sum_{\lambda}f^{*\lambda}_{\mu}\widehat{f^{*}_{\lambda^{-}}}.

Proof.

We start from the packed case and we proceed by induction. If
\mu\in\Pack(k,n)
, then from
Definition

4.8
item (1) we have

\displaystyle f^{*\lambda}_{\mu}=\sum_{\alpha:\lVert\alpha\rVert=\mu}\wt_{\alpha}a^{\lambda}_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}=a^{\lambda}_{\mu}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(x_{i}-t^{-n+1}\right).

But we know from
3.3
that

f^{*}_{\mu}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(x_{i}-t^{-n+1}\right)q^{|\mu|-k}\sum_{\nu}a_{\mu}^{\nu}f^{*}_{\nu^{-}}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{q};q,t\right).

This gives the theorem for packed compositions. We now assume that the result holds for
\mu
, and we fix
1\leq i\leq n-1
such that
\mu_{i}>0
and
\mu_{i+1}=0
. Let us prove it for
s_{i}\mu
.
We have from
2.10
item (1) that

f^{*}_{s_{i}\mu}=T_{i}f^{*}_{\mu}.

Using the recursion assumption we get

\displaystyle f^{*}_{s_{i}\mu}=T_{i}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}b^{\alpha}_{\mu}\sum_{\lambda}\wt_{\alpha}a^{\lambda}_{\lVert\alpha\rVert}\widehat{f^{*}_{\lambda^{-}}}\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}b^{\alpha}_{\mu}T_{i}\left(h_{\alpha}\right)

where we used the definition of the polynomials
h_{\alpha}
(see (
31
)). Using
4.7
, we get

f^{*}_{s_{i}\mu}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}b^{\alpha}_{\mu}\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta}h_{\beta}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}h_{\beta}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}b^{\alpha}_{\mu}\mathcal{N}^{(i)}_{\alpha,\beta}.

Finally, item (2) of
Definition

4.8
gives

\displaystyle f^{*}_{s_{i}\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}b_{s_{i}\mu}^{\beta}h_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}b_{s_{i}\mu}^{\beta}\sum_{\lambda}\wt_{\beta}a_{\lVert\beta\rVert}^{\lambda}\widehat{f^{*}_{\lambda^{-}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\widehat{f^{*}_{\lambda^{-}}}\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}b_{s_{i}\mu}^{\beta}\wt_{\beta}a_{\lVert\beta\rVert}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\widehat{f^{*}_{\lambda^{-}}}f^{*\lambda}_{s_{i}\mu}

which finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.1.
Generalized two-line queues

We start by reviewing

from

]

This recurrence is based on the fact that we can view a

with

to rows

2

Since

occupies rows

2
through
L
and has balls labeled
2
through

with a
multiline queue obtained by decreasing the row labels and ball labels in the top
L-1
rows of
Q

by
1
.
(Holes, represented by
0

remain holes.)
If the bottom row of

is the composition
\lambda

the new bottom row is
\lambda^{-}=(\lambda^{-}_{1},\dots,\lambda^{-}_{n})

\lambda^{-}_{i}=\max(\lambda_{i}-1,0)

Meanwhile

has just two rows, but its balls are labeled

Definition 5.1
.

A

is a two-row multiline queue whose top and bottom rows are represented by a pair of compositions
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, satisfying the following conditions:
\lambda
has no parts of size 1, and for each
j>1
,
\#\{i:\mu_{i}=j\}=\#\{i:\lambda_{i}=j\}
. Moreover, for each
i
, either
\mu_{i}=0
, or
\lambda_{i}\leq\mu_{i}
. (In other words, a larger label cannot be directly above a smaller nonzero label, as in a usual multiline queue.)

For
\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we set

\wt_{\mu}:=\prod_{i,\mu_{i}>0}x_{i}.

Let
\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}^{\lambda}
denote the set of (generalized)
two-line queues with bottom row
\mu
and top row
\lambda
.
For
Q_{0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}^{\lambda}
, we define

(36)

\displaystyle\wt(Q_{0})

\displaystyle=\wt_{\pair}(Q_{0})\cdot\wt_{\mu}

(37)

\displaystyle a_{\mu}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q_{0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}^{\lambda}}\wt_{\pair}(Q_{0})\in\mathbb{Q}(q,t)

\displaystyle f_{\mu}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=f_{\mu}^{\lambda}(\bm{x};q,t)=\sum_{Q_{0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}^{\lambda}}\wt(Q_{0})=\wt_{\mu}\cdot a_{\mu}^{\lambda}.

Note that the “ball weight” we associate to

only takes into account its bottom row. This
is because we want

, where the top

rows of

give

and the

.

Lemma 5.2
.

[
CMW22

We have the following recurrence for the homogeneous ASEP polynomials.

It follows from the definitions that

on tableaux, while the factors of
-1
in (
7
) correspond to the statistic
\negative(\phi)
on tableaux. The product in
(
48
) corresponds to a product over all nontrivial pairings.
∎

6.2.
The tableaux formula for the integral form

In this section we will give a tableau formula for the integral normalization of the interpolation symmetric Macdonald polynomials and the interpolation ASEP polynomials. We start with some definitions.

Fix a partition
\lambda
and a filling
\phi
of its doubled diagram
D_{\lambda}
. Fix a signed cell
x\in D_{\lambda}^{\classic}
. We recall that
\arm(x)
was defined in
Definition

6.6
. We now define this statistic for signed cells. If
x=(i,j^{\prime})
is a signed cell, we will denote
u(x):=(i,j+1)
the classic cell on top of
x
. We will use the convention that if this cell is not in the diagram
D_{\lambda}
, then it is restricted.

As for classic cells, the
\leg
of a signed cell will be defined as the number of classic boxes above
x
in its column. In particular, for
x\in D_{\lambda}^{\primed}
we have
\leg(x)=\leg(u(x))+1
.

We now extend the definition of
\arm
(see (
47
)) to signed cells
x\in D_{\lambda}^{\primed}
, in such a way that if
u(x)\in D_{\lambda}
,
u(x)
is unrestricted, and
p
is the pairing connecting the balls corresponding to the cells
x
and
u(x)
under the bijection
\Tab
, then
\arm(x)=\free(p)
.
Even though our previous tableaux formula used the arm and leg statistics only for classic cells, our next formula can be written more cleanly if we shift these statistics to signed cells.

Definition 6.15
.

Let
x\in D_{\lambda}^{\primed}
be a signed cell.

If
u(x)
is unrestricted, we define

\arm(x):=\arm(u(x))
, and if
u(x)
is restricted, we define

\displaystyle\arm(x):

\displaystyle=\#\big\{(k,j)\in D_{\lambda}\ :\ k>i\big\}

\displaystyle+\#\big\{(k,j)\in D_{\lambda}\ :\ k<i,\ \lambda_{k}=\lambda_{i},\mbox{and}\ u(k,j)\text{ is unrestricted}\big\}.

Figure

8
shows the statistics
\leg
and
\arm
for the signed multiline tableau of
Figure

6
.

Figure 8
.
On the left: the tableau of
Figure

6
. On the right: the pair
(\leg,\arm)
for each signed cell of the tableau.

Since
\leg(u(x))+1=\leg(x),

the weight
\wt(\phi)
(see
Definition

6.9
), can be written as:

\wt(\phi)=(-1)^{\negative(\phi)}q^{\maj(\phi)}t^{\coinv(\phi)+\emp(\phi)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in D^{\primed}_{\lambda}\\
\text{$u(x)$ unrestricted}\end{subarray}}\frac{1-t}{1-q^{\leg(x)}t^{\arm(x)+1}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in D^{\primed}_{\lambda}\\
\text{$x$ unrestricted}\end{subarray}}(1-t).

We define

\hook_{\lambda}:=\prod_{x\in D_{\lambda}^{\primed}}(1-q^{\leg(x)}t^{\arm(x)+1}).

One can show that unlike the definition of
\arm
, the definition of
\hook_{\lambda}
is independent of the filling
\phi
, and corresponds to the usual hook product; see
[
Kno97
, Section 5]
.

Definition 6.16
.

We define the
integral (form) interpolation Macdonald polynomial

and the
integral (form) interpolation ASEP polynomial
to be

J_{\lambda}^{*}:=\hook_{\lambda}P^{*}_{\lambda}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ and }\hskip 28.45274pt\hook_{\lambda}f_{\mu}^{*}.

We define the
integral weight
to be
\wt^{J}(\phi):=\hook_{\lambda}\wt(\phi)
, which equals

(-1)^{\negative(\phi)}q^{\maj(\phi)}t^{\coinv(\phi)+\emp(\phi)}\hskip-19.91684pt\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in D^{\primed}_{\lambda}\\
\text{$u(x)$ unrestricted}\end{subarray}}\hskip-8.5359pt(1-t)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in D^{\primed}_{\lambda}\\
\text{$x$ unrestricted}\end{subarray}}\hskip-7.11317pt(1-t)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in D^{\primed}_{\lambda}\\
\text{$u(x)$ restricted}\end{subarray}}\hskip-11.38092pt(1-q^{\leg(x)}t^{\arm(x)+1}).

We then get the following corollary of
6.10
.

Corollary 6.17
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
be a partition,
and let
\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)
be a composition.
Then the integral form interpolation ASEP polynomial equals the generating function for signed queue tableaux
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\mu}
counted with integral weights, that is,

(51)

\hook_{\lambda}f_{\mu}^{*}=\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\mu}}\wt^{J}(\phi)x^{\phi}.

And the integral interpolation Macdonald polynomial
J^{*}_{\lambda}(\bm{x};q,t)

equals the generating function for all signed
queue tableaux
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}
of shape
\lambda
counted with integral weights, that is,

J^{*}_{\lambda}(\bm{x};q,t)=\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}}\wt^{J}(\phi)x^{\phi}.

We deduce from these combinatorial formulas the following integrality results.

Theorem 6.18
.

Fix a partition
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}
.
Consider the expansions of
J_{\lambda}^{*}
in the monomial basis

J^{*}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}:|\nu|\leq|\lambda|}c_{\lambda,\nu}\ m_{\nu}
. Then
t^{(n-1)(|\lambda|-|\nu|)}c_{\lambda,\nu}\in\mathbb{Z}[q,t].

Similarly, let
\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)
be a permutation of
\lambda
, and consider the expansion

\hook_{\lambda}\ f^{*}_{\mu}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{n}:|\nu|\leq|\mu|}d_{\mu,\nu}x^{\nu}
. Then
t^{(n-1)(|\mu|-|\nu|)}d_{\mu,\nu}\in\mathbb{Z}[q,t].

The first part of this theorem was obtained in
[
Kno97
, Corollary 5.5]
(see also
[
Sah96
, Theorem 5.3]
). The second part is however new.

Proof.

In the combinatorial formulas given in
6.17
, the weights are polynomials in the variables
x_{i}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[q,t]
, except the weights assigned for negative boxes
y\in{D}_{\lambda}^{r^{\prime}}
for which
\wt_{\phi}(y)=\frac{-q^{r-1}}{t^{n-1}}
. Notice that the total number of boxes in
{D}_{\lambda}^{\primed}
corresponds to
|\lambda|
, and that extracting a monomial
m_{\nu}
in
J_{\lambda}^{*}
corresponds to considering tableaux with
|\nu|
positive boxes. As a consequence,
|\lambda|-|\nu|
is the number of negative boxes in such a tableau, hence, by multiplying by
t^{(n-1)(|\lambda|-|\nu|)}c_{\lambda,\nu}
we compensate all the denominators. The same reasoning applies to
\hook_{\lambda}f^{*}_{\mu}
.
∎

7.
Application: factorization of interpolation Macdonald polynomials

Fix
n\geq 1
. Let
{\llbracket n\rrbracket\choose k}
denote the
k
-element subsets of
\llbracket n\rrbracket
. For any
k\geq 0
, we define

e^{*}_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t):=\sum_{S\in{\llbracket n\rrbracket\choose k}}\prod_{i\in S}\left(x_{i}-\frac{t^{\#S^{c}\cap\llbracket i-1\rrbracket}}{t^{n-1}}\right),

where
S^{c}
denotes the complement of
S
in
\llbracket n\rrbracket
.
The top homogeneous part of
e_{k}^{*}
is the
k
-th elementary symmetric function
e_{k}
. Even though it is not completely clear from the definition, the functions
e^{*}_{k}
are symmetric (see
Eq.

55
below).

The purpose of this section is to prove the following factorization formula for interpolation nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials specialized at
q=1
, after a “partial symmetrization”.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition with
\lambda_{1}>0
. For any subset

S\in{\llbracket n\rrbracket\choose\ell(\lambda)}
, we have

(52)

\sum_{\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda):\Supp(\mu)=S}f^{*}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)=\prod_{i\in S}\left(x_{i}-\frac{t^{\#S^{c}\cap\llbracket i-1\rrbracket}}{t^{n-1}}\right)\prod_{2\leq i\leq\lambda_{1}}e^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t).

As a consequence, we have that for any partition
\lambda

(53)

P^{*}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\lambda_{1}}P^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\lambda_{1}}e^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t),

where
\lambda^{\prime}
is the partition conjugate to
\lambda
.

The symmetric part of this theorem (
Eq.

53
) was proved in more generality in
[
Doł17
]
.
Notice also that the top homogeneous part of
Eq.

53
corresponds to the factorization property of (homogeneous) Macdonald polynomials; see
[
Mac95
, Chapter VI, Eq. (4.14.vi)]
. The nonsymmetric part of the theorem (
Eq.

52
) seems however to be new.

We start by giving a formula for interpolation ASEP polynomials indexed by
\mu\in\{0,1\}^{n}
(for general
q
and
t
).
We will use the natural bijection between compositions
\mu\in\{0,1\}^{n}
and subsets of
\llbracket n\rrbracket
given by

\mu\mapsto S_{\mu}:=\{i:\mu_{i}=1\}
.

Lemma 7.2
.

For any
\mu\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, we have

(54)

f_{\mu}^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q,t)=\prod_{i\in S_{\mu}}\left(x_{i}-\frac{t^{\#S^{c}_{\mu}\cap\llbracket i-1\rrbracket}}{t^{n-1}}\right).

As a consequence,

(55)

P^{*}_{(1^{k},0^{n-k})}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q,t)=e^{*}_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t).

Proof.

We use
2.17
.
For
\mu\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, let
g_{\mu}
denote the right-hand side of (
54
).
It is clear from the formula for
g_{\mu}
that the second condition of
2.17
holds.
To show that the first condition of
2.17
holds,
consider any composition
\nu
such that

|\nu|\leq|\mu|

and
\nu\notin S_{n}(\lambda)
.
Note that if
\nu_{i}=0
, then

k_{i}(\nu)=\#(S_{\nu}\cap[i-1])+(n-i)=i-1-\#(S_{\nu}^{c}\cap[i-1])+(n-i)=(n-1)-\#(S_{\nu}^{c}\cap[i-1]),

where
k_{i}(\nu)
is the statistic defined in
Eq.

1
.

We claim that there exists an
i
such that
\nu_{i}=0
and
\#(S_{\nu}\cap[i-1])=\#(S_{\mu}\cap[i-1])
. If we know the claim, then

k_{i}(\nu)=(n-1)-\#(S_{\mu}^{c}\cap[i-1])
, so

g_{\mu}(\tilde{\nu})
is obtained by plugging in
x_{i}=q^{\nu_{i}}t^{-k_{i}(\nu)}=t^{\#(S_{\mu}^{c}\cap[i-1])-(n-1)}.

Thus
g_{\mu}(\tilde{\nu})=0
, and the uniqueness property of
2.17
implies that

f_{\mu}^{*}=g_{\mu}
.

To prove the claim, consider the function

\phi:i\in[1,n+1]\mapsto\#(S_{\nu}^{c}\cap[i-1])-\#(S_{\mu}^{c}\cap[i-1])\in\mathbb{Z}.

This function has the property that
|\phi(i+1)-\phi(i)|\in\{0,1\}
,
\phi(1)=0
, and
(because
\nu
has more
0
’s than
\mu
)
\phi(n+1)<0
.
Thus we can find
i
such that
\phi(i)=0
and
\phi(i+1)=-1
, which implies that
\nu_{i}=0
and the claim.

Now (
55
) is obtained by summing (
54
) over all
\mu
of size
k
(see
2.15
).
∎

Given
\mu
, let
\Supp(\mu):=\{i:\mu_{i}>0\}.

The following lemma is implicit in the discussion around
[
AMW24
, (5.1)-(5.3)]
when
\lambda
has distinct parts; we give a quick sketch below.

Lemma 7.3
.

Fix a partition
\lambda
with largest part
L
, and
\nu\in S_{n}(\lambda\backslash 1^{m_{1}(\lambda)})
, where
\lambda\backslash 1^{m_{1}(\lambda)}
is the partition obtained from
\lambda
by removing parts of size 1. We also fix a set
S\in{\llbracket n\rrbracket\choose\ell(\lambda)}
.
Then

\sum_{\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda):\Supp(\mu)=S}a^{\nu}_{\mu}(q=1)=1.

Proof.

Recall from
Section

5.1
that the coefficients
a^{\nu}_{\mu}
enumerate generalized 2-line queues in
\mathcal{T}^{\nu}_{\mu}
according to their pairing weights. By fixing
\nu
, we fix the labels of balls in the top row. The positions but not the labels of the balls in the bottom row are fixed by
S
. Such a multiline queue is obtained as follows: we start with the highest label
L
in the top row, and trivially pair any ball having a ball directly underneath it. We then pair the rightmost free ball
B
labeled
L
(in the top row). If there are
r
free balls left, then
B
will have
r
pairing choices, with weights
\frac{1-t}{1-t^{r}}
,
\frac{1-t}{1-t^{r}}t,\dots,\frac{1-t}{1-t^{r}}t^{r-1}
(recall that
q=1
).
Thus the total weight of all possible pairings for
B
is 1. We then move on to the other balls in the top row (always choosing the rightmost ball with the largest label).
Note that
\#S=\ell(\lambda)\geq\ell(\nu)
, this guarantees that all balls in the top row are paired: this fixes the labels of the paired balls in the bottom row, the unpaired ones will be labeled by 1.
In conclusion, when
q=1
, the total weight at each step is 1, and the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of
7.1
.

We prove the result by induction on the size of the first part of
\lambda
. When
\lambda_{1}=0
, we have

P^{*}_{0^{n}}=f^{*}_{0^{n}}=1,

which corresponds in
Eq.

53
to an empty product.
The result was also proven for
\lambda_{1}=1
in
7.2
.

Now fix
\lambda
with
\lambda_{1}>0
. We start by showing
Eq.

52
, i.e that for any subset

S\in{\llbracket n\rrbracket\choose\ell(\lambda)}
we have

\sum_{\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda):\Supp(\mu)=S}f^{*}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)=\prod_{i\in S}\left(x_{i}-\frac{t^{\#S^{c}\cap\llbracket i-1\rrbracket}}{t^{n-1}}\right)\prod_{2\leq i\leq\lambda_{1}}e^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t).

This equation will be proved by induction on
\sum_{i\in S}i
. Our base case is the “packed subset”
S:=\{1,\dots,\ell(\lambda)\}
. In this case, we know from
3.3
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)\\
\Supp(\mu)=S\end{subarray}}f^{*}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)}\left(x_{i}-\frac{1}{t^{n-1}}\right)\sum_{\nu}f^{*}_{\nu^{-}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)\\
\Supp(\mu)=S\end{subarray}}a^{\nu}_{\mu}(q=1),

where the first sum in the right-hand side is taken over compositions
\nu\in S_{n}(\lambda\backslash 1^{m_{1}(\lambda)})
.
We now apply
7.3
and
2.15
, obtaining

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda):\Supp(\mu)=S}f^{*}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)}\left(x_{i}-\frac{1}{t^{n-1}}\right)\sum_{\nu\in S_{n}(\lambda\backslash 1^{m_{1}(\lambda)})}f^{*}_{\nu^{-}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)}\left(x_{i}-\frac{1}{t^{n-1}}\right)\sum_{\nu\in S_{n}(\lambda^{-})}f^{*}_{\nu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)}\left(x_{i}-\frac{1}{t^{n-1}}\right)P^{*}_{\lambda^{-}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t).

Applying the induction hypothesis of
Eq.

53
with
\lambda^{-}
(because
(\lambda^{-})_{1}=\lambda_{1}-1<\lambda_{1}
),
we get

\sum_{\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda):\Supp(\mu)=S}f^{*}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)}\left(x_{i}-\frac{1}{t^{n-1}}\right)\prod_{2\leq i\leq\lambda_{1}}e^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t).

This finishes the proof of the base case
Eq.

52
.

We now fix
S
such that
Eq.

52
holds, and let
1\leq i\leq n-1
such that
i\in S
but
i+1\notin S
, and let
S^{\prime}:=S\backslash\{i\}\cup\{i+1\}.
We want to prove the result for
S^{\prime}
. Note that the action of the transposition
s_{i}
is a bijection between
\{\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda):\Supp(\mu)=S\}
and
\{\kappa\in S_{n}(\lambda):\Supp(\kappa)=S^{\prime}\}
and for any
\mu
in the first set, we have

f^{*}_{s_{i}\mu}=T_{i}f^{*}_{\mu}
(see
2.10
). Hence,

\sum_{\kappa\in S_{n}(\lambda):\Supp(\kappa)=S^{\prime}}f^{*}_{\kappa}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)=T_{i}\left(\prod_{j\in S}\left(x_{j}-\frac{t^{\#S^{c}\cap\llbracket j-1\rrbracket}}{t^{n-1}}\right)\prod_{2\leq j\leq\lambda_{1}}e^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t)\right).

Since the functions
e^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}
are symmetric, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\kappa\in S_{n}(\lambda):\Supp(\kappa)=S^{\prime}}f^{*}_{\kappa}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{2\leq j\leq\lambda_{1}}e^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t)T_{i}\left(\prod_{j\in S}\left(x_{j}-\frac{t^{\#S^{c}\cap\llbracket j-1\rrbracket}}{t^{n-1}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{2\leq j\leq\lambda_{1}}e^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t)\prod_{j\in S^{\prime}}\left(x_{j}-\frac{t^{\#(S^{\prime})^{c}\cap\llbracket j-1\rrbracket}}{t^{n-1}}\right).

To obtain the last line, we use the fact that
T_{i}x_{i}=x_{i+1}
, and
T_{i}t^{\#S^{c}\cap\llbracket i-1\rrbracket}=t\cdot t^{\#S^{c}\cap\llbracket i-1\rrbracket}=t^{\#(S^{\prime})^{c}\cap\llbracket i\rrbracket}
.

This finishes the proof of
Eq.

52
. We now sum
Eq.

52
over all subsets
S\in{\llbracket n\rrbracket\choose\ell(\lambda)}
, getting

\sum_{\mu\in S_{n}(\lambda)}f^{*}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)=\sum_{S:\#S=\ell(\lambda)}\prod_{i\in S}\left(x_{i}-\frac{t^{\#S^{c}\cap\llbracket i-1\rrbracket}}{t^{n-1}}\right)\prod_{2\leq i\leq\lambda_{1}}e^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t),

which by
2.15
is equivalent to

\displaystyle P^{*}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};1,t)

\displaystyle=e^{*}_{\ell(\lambda)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t)\prod_{2\leq i\leq\lambda_{1}}e^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\lambda_{1}}e^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};t),

which gives
Eq.

53
. This finishes the proof of the induction and hence the proof of the theorem.
∎

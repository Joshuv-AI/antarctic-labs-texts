Toward a rainbow Corrádi–Hajnal Theorem I
Jinghua Deng, Jianfeng Hou, Caiyun Hu, Xizhi Liu

Abstract
We study an anti-Ramsey extension of the classical Corrádi–Hajnal Theorem: how many colors are needed to color the complete graph on n n vertices in order to guarantee a rainbow copy of t ​ K 3 tK_{3} , that is, t t vertex-disjoint triangles. We provide a conjecture for large n n , consisting of five classes of different extremal constructions, corresponding to five subintervals of [ 1 , n 3 ] \left[1,\,\tfrac{n}{3}\right] for the parameter t t . In this work, we establish this conjecture for the first interval, t ∈ [ 1 , 2 ​ n − 6 9 ] t\in\left[1,\,\tfrac{2n-6}{9}\right] . In particular, this improves upon a recent result of Lu–Luo–Ma [arXiv:2506.07115] which established the case t ≤ n − 57 15 t\leq\tfrac{n-57}{15} .

Concluding remarks

In the present work, we established Conjecture
1.2
for the first interval. In a subsequent work, we will extend this result to the second and third intervals. In particular, our results will show that in the first and second intervals,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ar}(n,(t+2)K_{3})=\mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)K_{3})+2.

\displaystyle\mathrm{ar}(n,(t+2)K_{3})=\mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)K_{3})+n-2t.

Let us briefly mention the difference between Conjecture
1.2
and the conjecture of Liu–Ning–Tian
[

.
The first, second, and fourth constructions in their conjecture are identical to the first, second, and fifth constructions in our conjecture.
Their third construction differs from our third construction in that the size of their set
X
is smaller by one.
Another difference is that their conjecture does not include the construction
E_{4}(n,t)
.

Appendix A
Results concerning
tK_{3}
-free graphs

Given an
n
-vertex
(t+1)K_{3}
-free graph
H
, let
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{M},\mathcal{I})
be the maximal tiling triple of
H
with an ideal partition
(\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{3},\mathcal{T}_{4})
of
\mathcal{T}
. The following give upper bounds on the number of edges in each part and between them.

Lemma A.1

(
[
ABHP15
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

The following bounds hold.

a)

e(\mathcal{I})=0.

b)

e(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{M})\leq im.

c)

e(\mathcal{M})\leq m^{2}.

d)

e(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{T}_{1})\leq 4mt_{1}
.

e)

e(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{T}_{1})\leq 2it_{1}
.

f)

e(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{T}_{1})\leq 7\binom{t_{1}}{2}
.

g)

e(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{T}_{2})\leq 2it_{2}
.

h)

e(\mathcal{T}_{2})\leq 8\binom{t_{2}}{2}+3t_{2}
.

i)

e\left(\mathcal{T}_{3}\right)+e\left(\mathcal{T}_{3},\mathcal{T}_{4}\right)\leq 8\binom{t_{3}}{2}+8t_{3}t_{4}+3t_{3}.

Lemma A.2

(
[
ABHP15
, Lemma 4.3]
)
.

The following bounds hold.

a)

If
t_{1}\neq 1
and
j\geq 2
, then
e(\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{j})\leq 7t_{1}t_{j}.

b)

If
t_{2}\neq 1
and
j\geq 3
, then
e(\mathcal{T}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{j})\leq 8t_{2}t_{j}.

Lemma A.3

([Lemma 4.4]
[
ABHP15
]
)
.

The following bounds hold.

a)

\displaystyle e\left(\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\right)+e\left(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{T}_{2}\right)\leq\begin{cases}7t_{1}t_{2}+(2+3m)t_{2}&\text{if }m\geq 1,\\
0&\text{if }m=0.\end{cases}

b)

If
j=3,4
, then

\displaystyle e\left(\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{j}\right)+e\left(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{T}_{j}\right)\leq\begin{cases}7t_{1}t_{j}+(3+3m)t_{j}&\text{if }m\geq 1,\\
0&\text{if }m=0.\end{cases}

c)

If
j=3,4
, then

\displaystyle e\left(\mathcal{T}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{j}\right)+e\left(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{T}_{j}\right)\leq\begin{cases}8t_{2}t_{j}+(2+i)t_{j}&\text{if }i\geq 1,\\
0&\text{if }i=0.\end{cases}

For
t_{2}=1
and any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{3}\cup\mathcal{T}_{4}
, we have
e(\mathcal{I},T)\leq i
if
e(\mathcal{T}_{2},T)=9
, and
e(\mathcal{I},T)\leq i+2
if
e(\mathcal{T}_{1},T)\leq 8
.

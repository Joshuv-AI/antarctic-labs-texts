A Refined Analysis of UCBVI
Simone Drago simone.drago@polimi.it

Abstract
In this work, we provide a refined analysis of the UCBVI algorithm ( Azar et al., 2017 ) , improving both the bonus terms and the regret analysis. Additionally, we compare our version of UCBVI with both its original version and the state-of-the-art MVP algorithm. Our empirical validation demonstrates that improving the multiplicative constants in the bounds has significant positive effects on the empirical performance of the algorithms.



S. Drago, M. Mussi and A. M. Metelli

Introduction

We focus on the problem of
finite-horizon tabular RL
, where the statistical complexity is characterized by a well-established lower bound of the order
\Omega(\sqrt{HSAT})
, where
S
is the number of states,
A
is the number of actions,
H
is the horizon of the episode, and
T=HK
where
K
is the number of episodes
(
Domingues et al., 2021
)
. State-of-the-art learning algorithms match the lower bound up to logarithmic factors.
UCBVI

(
Azar et al., 2017
)
matches this lower bound by combining the classical value iteration approach with the
optimism in the face of uncertainty
mechanism, achieving
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{HSAT})
under the condition
T\geq\Omega(H^{3}S^{3}A)
. Recently,
MVP

(
Zhang et al., 2024
)
overcome this limitation by employing the so-called
doubling trick
, ensuring order-optimal regret (up to logarithmic factors) for every time horizon. However, this improvement comes at the expense of significantly larger constant factors, which may imply poor empirical performances (see Section
5
). Consequently,
UCBVI
remains a competitive and practical solution for finite-horizon tabular RL.

In this work, we improve both the bound and the analysis of the
UCBVI
algorithm, with particular attention to its advanced form with Bernstein-Freedman optimistic bonus. Our goal is to design an exploration bonus that is as tight as possible and to conduct a regret upper bound analysis that minimizes also constants.

Setting

In this section, we introduce the notation and the setting we consider in the rest of the work.

Notation.

Given a measurable set
\mathcal{X}
, we denote with
\Delta(\mathcal{X})
the set of probability measures over
\mathcal{X}
, and with
|\mathcal{X}|
it cardinality. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
as
\llbracket n\rrbracket
. We denote the L1 norm of a vector as
\|\cdot\|_{1}
. We denote the indicator function of event
x
as
\mathbb{I}\{x\}
.

Markov Decision Processes.

An undiscounted, episodic Markov Decision Process
(
Puterman, 1990
, MDP, )
is a tuple
\mathcal{M}\coloneqq(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,R,H)
. In this tuple,
\mathcal{S}
is the state space,
\mathcal{A}
is the action space,
P:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to\Delta(\mathcal{S})
represents the state transition probability,
R:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
represents the reward function, and
H\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
is the length of each episode.
1
1

1

Let
x,y\in\mathcal{S}
and
a\in\mathcal{A}
, we denote as
P(y|x,a)
the probability of observing
y
as the next state after playing action
a
in state
x
, and
R(x,a)
the reward obtained after playing action
a
in state
x
.

We assume the state space and the action space are finite sets, and we denote their cardinalities as
|\mathcal{S}|=S<+\infty
and
|\mathcal{A}|=A<+\infty
. We assume the state transition probability and the reward do not depend on the stage. Moreover, we assume the reward to be deterministic, known, and bounded in
[0,1]
.

Interaction with the Environment.

The agent interacts with the environment in a sequence of
K
episodes. Denote as
x_{k,h}
the state occupied by the agent at stage
h\in\llbracket H\rrbracket
of episode
k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket
, and as
a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}}
the action played by the agent at stage
h
of episode
k
according to the policy
\pi_{k}
. We assume policies to be deterministic and stage-dependent, i.e.,
\pi:\mathcal{S}\times\llbracket H\rrbracket\to\mathcal{A}
.

The interaction of the
k
-th episode is defined as follows. Starting from state
x_{k,1}\in\mathcal{S}
, the agent selects which action to play as
a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}}=\pi_{k}(x_{k,h},h)
for every
h\in\llbracket H\rrbracket
, and observes a sequence of next-states and rewards, until the end of the episode.

The function
V_{h}^{\pi}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the value function at stage
h\in\llbracket H\rrbracket
, such that
V_{h}^{\pi}(x)
represents the expected sum of the
H-h
returns received under policy
\pi
starting from state
x\in\mathcal{S}
. Under the assumptions stated above, there exists a deterministic policy
\pi^{*}
which attains the best possible value function
V_{h}^{*}(x)\coloneqq\sup_{\pi}V_{h}^{\pi}(x)
for every state
x\in\mathcal{S}
.
We measure the performance of a learning algorithm
\mathfrak{A}
after
K
episodes by means of the
cumulative regret
:

\textnormal{Reg}(\mathfrak{A},K)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{K}V_{1}^{*}(x_{i,1})-V_{1}^{\pi_{i}}(x_{i,1}).

We denote as
T=KH
the total number of interactions.

UCBVI

In this section, we consider the
UCBVI
algorithm, introduced in
(
Azar et al., 2017
)
. First, we provide a more compact (but equivalent) pseudocode of the algorithm in Algorithm
1
.

We start by initializing the counters needed in order to run the algorithm. Then, we start the continuous interaction procedure for every episode
k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket
. For every episode, before starting the interaction, the algorithm computes the optimistic estimate of the value function. Such estimate starts by computing all the transition probabilities
\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a)
for every state, action and next state as:

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a)=\frac{N_{k}(x,a,y)}{\max\{1,N_{k}(x,a)\}},

(1)

where
N_{k}(x,a)
is the number of times we play action
a\in\mathcal{A}
in state
x\in\mathcal{S}
, and
N_{k}(x,a,y)
is the number of times we do so and observe the next state
y\in\mathcal{S}
. Then, we compute the optimistic value iteration for finite horizon MDPs, starting from stage
H
backward, where the optimistic
Q_{k,h}(x,a)
is defined as:

\displaystyle Q_{k,h}(x,a)=\min\{Q_{k-1,h}(x,a),R(x,a)+\!\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\!\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a)V_{k,h+1}(y)+b_{k,h}(x,a)\}.

This procedure mimics value iteration with an additive exploration term
b_{k,h}(x,a)
which will be further characterized later in Section
4
. Then, term
V_{k,h}(x)
is computed as usual for value iteration:

\displaystyle V_{k,h}(x)=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q_{k,h}(x,a).

After that, we can start interacting with the environment and play greedily. More in detail, at every stage
h\in\llbracket H\rrbracket
of episode
k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket
, we observe state
x_{k,h}
and play the most promising action according to the optimistic estimate:

\displaystyle a_{k,h}\in\argmax_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q_{k,h}(x_{k,h},a).

After having played
a_{k,h}
, we observe the reward
r_{k,h}
and the next state
x_{k,h+1}
. Finally, we use the collected information to properly update counters.

4
A Refined
UCBVI
Analysis

In this section, we analyze the
UCBVI
algorithm. We start in Section
4.1
by providing the regret upper bound for
UCBVI
with
Chernoff-Hoeffding
bonus (Theorem
4.1

Bernstein-Freedman
bonus, and we characterize the regret of
UCBVI
we get when we consider such a bonus (Theorem
4.2
). Our contribution consists of refining the analysis to obtain tighter bonuses and, as a direct consequence, tighter regret bounds.

(

5
Numerical Validation

In this section, we numerically compare the performances of
UCBVI
, both with the Chernoff-Hoeffding and Bernstein-Freedman bonuses of
(
Azar et al., 2017
)
and with the improved Bernstein-Freedman bonus of this paper, against the
MVP

(
Zhang et al., 2024
)
algorithm.

In order to fairly compare to the
MVP
algorithm, all the

terms are considered as
N(x,a)
, removing the discriminant of the stage from the algorithm, and the
c_{2}

0
, to remove the exploration factor needed due to the stochasticity of the reward in the original paper.

5.2

We now consider the RiverSwim environment
(

(i)

(ii)

S=5

H=10
. The reward model and the transition probability are designed such that the suboptimality gap between the optimal action and the other one in the initial state is very low (
\sim\!0.1

6
Conclusions

A summary of the improvements, expressed in terms of improvement ratios in the bonuses, regret upper bounds, and empirical regret, is reported in Table
1
. First, we compare our versions of the
UCBVI
algorithms with the original ones from
(
Azar et al., 2017
)
. The algorithmic structure remains the same, though we re-derived the bonus terms to make them as tight as possible, resulting in an improvement of
7/2
and
\sqrt{2}
for the Chernoff-Hoeffding and Bernstein-Freedman bonuses, in the dominant terms, respectively. This reduction in over-exploration has significant empirical effects, as shown in Section
5
, where, as reported in Table
1
, we achieve an improvement in the empirical regret of
1.87
times. Additionally, this impacts the regret analysis, where we were able to reduce the regret bound by a factor of
2
and
5/4
for the Chernoff-Hoeffding and Bernstein-Freedman bonuses, respectively, in terms of dominant terms. However, lower order terms also have an impact on the performance, and through a refined analysis we were able to reduce them by a factor of
\sim\!90
and
\sim\!4
for the Chernoff-Hoeffding and Bernstein-Freedman bonuses, respectively.

Appendix C

Lemma C.1

(Bernstein inequality for Bernoulli random variables)
.

Let
p
be the parameter of a Bernoulli random variable, and let
\widehat{p}
be its estimator. Let
\delta>0
. Then, w.p. at least
1-\delta
, it holds that:

|\widehat{p}-p|\leq\sqrt{\frac{2p(1-p)L}{n}}+\frac{2L}{3n},

where
n
represents the number of observations, and
L=\ln(2/\delta)
.

Proof.

Let
\{Y_{i}\}_{i=1\ldots,n}
be the set of i.i.d. realizations of a Bernoulli with parameter
p
. Define the auxiliary random variable:

X_{i}=\frac{Y_{i}}{n}.

Observe that
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
are independent random variables, and that
0\leq X_{i}\leq 1/n
. Let
S_{n}
be their sum, and
E_{n}
be the expected value of
S_{n}
:

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}=\widehat{p},

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[S_{n}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]=p.

Let
V_{n}
be the variance of
S_{n}
:

V_{n}=\Var[S_{n}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Var[X_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1(1-p)}{n^{2}}\right)=\frac{p(1-p)}{n}.

By applying Bernstein’s inequality, we obtain that:

\mathrm{Pr}(|S_{n}-E_{n}|>\epsilon)<2\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}/2}{V_{n}+C(\epsilon/3)}\right),

(5)

where
C
is the range of values of the addends in
S_{n}
(i.e.,
C=1/n
). By setting this probability to be equal to
\delta
, we can derive that:

\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}=V_{n}\ln\left(\frac{2}{\delta}\right)+\frac{\epsilon}{3n}\ln\left(\frac{2}{\delta}\right).

Let
L=\ln(2/\delta)
, by solving the second order polynomial we get that:

\epsilon=\frac{L}{3n}\pm\sqrt{\frac{L^{2}}{9n^{2}}+2V_{n}L}.

We can discard the equation with the minus, as it would result in
\epsilon<0
, thus resulting in the inequality in Equation (
5
) holding w.p.
1-\delta
. As such, we derive that:

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{3n}+\sqrt{\frac{L^{2}}{9n^{2}}+2V_{n}L}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{3n}+\sqrt{\frac{L^{2}}{9n^{2}}}+\sqrt{\frac{2p(1-p)L}{n}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2p(1-p)L}{n}}+\frac{2L}{3n},

thus completing the proof.
∎

Lemma C.2

(Regret decomposition upper bound)
.

Let
k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket
and
h\in\llbracket H\rrbracket
. Assume events
\mathcal{E}
and
\Omega_{k,h}
hold. Then the regret from stage
h
onward of all episodes up to
k
can be upper bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta_{i,h}(x_{i,h})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{i,h}(x_{i,h})\leq

\displaystyle\ e\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=h}^{H-1}\Big[\varepsilon_{i,j}+2\sqrt{L}\overline{\varepsilon}_{i,j}+b_{i,j}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\xi_{i,j}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})+\frac{8H^{2}SL}{3N_{i}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})}\Big].

Proof.

We begin the proof by considering a single value of
k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket
. Under
\Omega_{k,h}
, we observe that:

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,h}(x_{k,h})

\displaystyle=V_{h}^{*}(x_{k,h})-V_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(x_{k,h})

\displaystyle\leq V_{k,h}(x_{k,h})-V_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(x_{k,h})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,h}(x_{k,h}).

As such, we bound the pseudo-regret
\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,h}(x_{k,h})
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,h}(x_{k,h})

\displaystyle=V_{k,h}(x_{k,h})-V_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(x_{k,h})

\displaystyle=b_{k,h}(x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})V_{k,h+1}(y)-\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}P(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})V_{h+1}^{\pi_{k}}(y)

\displaystyle=b_{k,h}(x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})-P(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})\right]V_{k,h+1}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}P(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})\left[V_{k,h+1}(y)-V_{h+1}^{\pi_{k}}(y)\right]

\displaystyle=b_{k,h}(x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})-P(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})\right]V_{h+1}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})-P(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})\right]\left[V_{k,h+1}(y)-V_{h+1}^{*}(y)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}P(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,h+1}(y)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,h+1}(x_{k,h+1})+b_{k,h}(x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})+\varepsilon_{k,h}

(6)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})-P(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})\right]V_{h+1}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})-P(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})\right]\left[V_{k,h+1}(y)-V_{h+1}^{*}(y)\right]

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\leq\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,h+1}(x_{k,h+1})+b_{k,h}(x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})+\xi_{k,h}(x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})\\
&\quad+\varepsilon_{k,h}+\underbrace{\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})-P(y|x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi_{k}})\right]\left[V_{k,h+1}(y)-V_{h+1}^{*}(y)\right]}_{(a)},\end{split}

(7)

) we apply the definition

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,h}(x_{k,h})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=h}^{H-1}\gamma_{j-h}\bigg[b_{k,j}(x_{k,j},a_{k,j}^{\pi_{k}})+\xi_{k,j}(x_{k,j},a_{k,j}^{\pi_{k}})

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon_{k,j}+\sqrt{2L}\overline{\varepsilon}_{k,j}+\frac{8H^{2}SL}{3N_{k}(x_{k,j},a_{k,j}^{\pi_{k}})}\bigg],

where
\gamma_{j-h}=\left(1+\frac{1}{H}\right)^{j-h}
. Notice that the summation is limited to
H-1
. This will be recurrent throughout the paper and is due to the fact that, the reward being deterministic, there is no uncertainty at
h=H
.
As such, we can assume that the policies
\pi_{k}
for
k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket
always play greedily at the last stage of each episode.

Observing that
1+\frac{1}{H}>1
, we trivially derive that
\gamma_{j-h}\leq\gamma_{H}
for
j\in\llbracket h,H\rrbracket
. Recalling that
\lim_{x\to+\infty}\left(1+\frac{1}{x}\right)^{x}=e
, we can bound
\gamma_{H}\leq e
, and rewrite:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,h}(x_{k,h})&\leq e\sum_{j=h}^{H-1}\bigg[b_{k,j}(x_{k,j},a_{k,j}^{\pi_{k}})+\xi_{k,j}(x_{k,j},a_{k,j}^{\pi_{k}})\\
&\quad+\varepsilon_{k,j}+\sqrt{2L}\overline{\varepsilon}_{k,j}+\frac{8H^{2}SL}{3N_{k}(x_{k,j},a_{k,j}^{\pi_{k}})}\bigg],\end{split}

(15)

To conclude the proof, we need to show that this holds for any value of
k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket
. Recalling the definition of
\Omega_{k,h}
:

\Omega_{k,h}\coloneqq\left\{V_{i,j}(x)\geq V_{j}^{*}(x),\forall(i,j)\in[k,h]_{\mathrm{hist}},x\in\mathcal{S}\right\},

where
[k,h]_{\mathrm{hist}}\coloneqq\left\{(i,j):i\in\llbracket K\rrbracket,j\in\llbracket H\rrbracket,(i<k)\vee(i=k,j\geq h)\right\}
, we observe that, if
\Omega_{k,h}
holds, then also the events
\Omega_{i,j}
hold for
(i,j)\in[k,h]_{hist}
. As such, we can sum up the previous bound of Equation (
15
) over all the episodes
i\in\llbracket k\rrbracket
, thus concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma C.3

Lemma C.5

\mathbb{V}_{j+1}^{\pi_{k}}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})
:

\mathbb{V}_{j+1}^{\pi_{k}}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})\coloneqq\Var_{y\sim P(\cdot|x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})}[V_{j+1}^{\pi_{k}}(y)]

(16)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{I}(x_{i,h}=x)\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=h}^{H-1}\mathbb{V}_{j+1}^{\pi_{k}}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})|\mathcal{H}_{k,h}\right]

Observe that by applying the

(
Blitzstein and Hwang, 2019
, LTV, see, e.g., Theorem 9.5.5 of)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\underset{x_{i,h+1},\ldots,x_{i,H-1}}{\Var}\left[\sum_{j=h}^{H-1}R^{\pi}(x_{i,j})\right]&=\underbrace{\underset{x_{i,h+1}}{\Var}\left[\underset{x_{i,h+2},\ldots,x_{i,H-1}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{j=h}^{H-1}R^{\pi}(x_{i,j})\bigg|x_{i,h+1}\right]\right]}_{(a)}\\

(22)

and:

23

16

Appendix D
Proof of Theorem

See

We begin the proof by demonstrating optimism under the
UCBVI-CH
algorithm (i.e., every optimistic value function is an upper bound of the true optimal value function), which requires us to show that, with high probability, the event
\Omega\coloneqq\{V_{k,h}(x)\geq V_{h}^{*}(x),\forall k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket,h\in\llbracket H\rrbracket,x\in\mathcal{S}\}
holds.

Lemma D.1

(Optimism under Chernoff-Hoeffding bonus)
.

Let the optimistic bonus be defined as:

b_{k,h}(x,a)=\frac{2HL}{\sqrt{N_{k}(x,a)}}.

Then, under event
\mathcal{E}
, the following event holds:

\Omega\coloneqq\{V_{k,h}(x)\geq V_{h}^{*}(x),\forall k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket,h\in\llbracket H\rrbracket,x\in\mathcal{S}\}.

Proof.

We demonstrate the result by induction. Let
V_{k,h}
be the optimistic value function at stage
h
computed using the history up to the end of episode
k-1
, and let
V_{h}^{*}
be the true optimal value function at stage
h
.

By definition,
V_{k,H+1}(x)=V_{H+1}^{*}(x)=0
for every
x\in\mathcal{S}
, and thus the inequality
V_{k,H+1}\geq V_{H+1}^{*}
trivially holds. To prove the inductive step, we need to demonstrate that, if
V_{k,h+1}\geq V_{h+1}^{*}
holds, then it also holds that
V_{k,h}\geq V_{h}^{*}
. We can derive this result as follows:

\displaystyle V_{k,h}(x)-V_{h}^{*}

\displaystyle=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q_{k,h}(x,a)-V_{h}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle\geq Q_{k,h}(x,a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})-V_{h}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})V_{k,h+1}(y)+b_{k,h}(x,a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})-\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}P(y|x,a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})V_{h+1}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})-P(y|x,a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})\right]V_{h+1}^{*}(y)+b_{k,h}(x,a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})

(59)

\displaystyle\geq b_{k,h}(x,a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})-2\sqrt{\frac{H^{2}L}{N_{k}(x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})}}

(60)

\displaystyle\geq 2\frac{HL}{\sqrt{N_{k}(x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})}}-2\sqrt{\frac{H^{2}L}{N_{k}(x_{k,h},a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})}}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where Equation (
59
) follows by the inductive hypothesis, and Equation (
60
) is obtained because, under
\mathcal{E}
, we can bound
|\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})-P(y|x,a_{k,h}^{\pi^{*}})V_{h+1}^{*}(y)|
by applying Azuma-Hoeffding’s inequality, allowing us to simplify terms and show optimism.
∎

Our objective is to bound the regret after
K
episodes (i.e.,
\textnormal{Reg}(\text{{{UCBVI-CH}}{} },K)
). We can observe that, under event
\Omega
, it holds that:

\displaystyle\textnormal{Reg}(\text{{{UCBVI-CH}}{} },K)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket}V_{1}^{*}(x_{k,1})-V_{1}^{\pi_{k}}(x_{k,1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket}V_{k,1}(x_{k,1})-V_{1}^{\pi_{k}}(x_{k,1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket}\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,1}(x_{k,1})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\textnormal{Reg}}(\text{{{UCBVI-CH}}{} },K).

As such, we can now focus on finding an upper bound to
\widetilde{\textnormal{Reg}}(\text{{{UCBVI-CH}}{} },K)
. By applying Lemma
C.2
, we can write:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textnormal{Reg}}(\text{{{UCBVI-CH}}{} },K)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\llbracket K\rrbracket}\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,1}(x_{k,1})

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\leq e\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{H-1}\bigg[\varepsilon_{i,j}+2\sqrt{L}\overline{\varepsilon}_{i,j}+b_{i,j}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})\\
&\quad+\xi_{i,j}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})+\frac{8H^{2}SL}{3N_{i}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})}\bigg].\end{split}

(61)

To find an upper bound to the regret, we can thus bound the summation of each of the terms individually.

By applying Lemma
C.3
, we obtain the following bounds:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{H-1}\varepsilon_{i,j}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{H^{2}TL},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{H-1}2\sqrt{L}\overline{\varepsilon}_{i,j}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{TL}.

Then, we can derive the following bound:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{H}\frac{8H^{2}SL}{3N_{i}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{3}H^{2}SL\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{K}(x,a)}n^{-1}

(62)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{3}H^{2}SL\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{n=1}^{\frac{KH}{SA}}n^{-1}

(63)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{3}H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}

where Equation (
62
) is obtained by rearranging the terms to isolate the summation of
n^{-1}
for
n
from
1
to
N_{K}(x,a)
(i.e., the total number of times each state-action pair has been observed up to the end of episode
K
), and Equation (
63
) derives from the observation that the summation can be upper bounded by considering a uniform state-action visit distribution. This derivation produces the same result as applying the well-known pigeonhole principle.

By applying a similar reasoning, we bound the remaining summations over the bonus terms:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{H}b_{i,j}(x_{i,j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{H}2H\sqrt{\frac{L}{N_{i}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})}}

\displaystyle=2H\sqrt{L}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{K}(x,a)}n^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2H\sqrt{L}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{n=1}^{\frac{KH}{SA}}n^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{H^{2}SATL},

and over the model error terms:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{H}\xi_{i,j}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{H}2H\sqrt{\frac{L}{N_{i}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})}}

(64)

\displaystyle=2H\sqrt{L}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{K}(x,a)}n^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2H\sqrt{L}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{n=1}^{\frac{KH}{SA}}n^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{H^{2}SATL},

where Equation (
64
) is obtained by bounding
\xi_{i,j}(x_{i,j},a_{i,j}^{\pi_{i}})
using the Chernoff-Hoeffding inequality.
Finally, we can put all the bounds together and rewrite Equation (
61
) as:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textnormal{Reg}}(\text{{{UCBVI-CH}}{} },K)

\displaystyle\leq e\left[2\sqrt{H^{2}TL}+4\sqrt{TL}+2\sqrt{H^{2}SATL}+2\sqrt{H^{2}SATL}+\frac{8}{3}H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq e\left[10\sqrt{H^{2}SATL}+\frac{8}{3}H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}\right],

thus completing the proof.

\displaystyle\leq U_{k,h,x}

\displaystyle\leq e\bigg[\sqrt{28H^{2}SAN^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L^{2}}+\frac{7}{3}HSAL^{2}+168\sqrt{H^{3}S^{4}A^{2}L^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{8H^{2}SAL^{2}U_{k,h,x}}+\sqrt{6H^{2}SAN^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L^{2}}+\frac{2}{3}HSAL^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\sqrt{H^{2}SAL^{2}U_{k,h,x}}+\frac{8}{3}H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}+2\sqrt{H^{3}N^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L}

\displaystyle\quad+4\sqrt{HN^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L}+100H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq e\bigg[12\sqrt{H^{2}SAN^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L^{2}}+5\sqrt{H^{2}SAL^{2}U_{k,h,x}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{821}{3}H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}+2\sqrt{H^{3}N^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L}\bigg].

Letting:

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=e\left[12\sqrt{H^{2}SAN^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L^{2}}+\frac{821}{3}H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}+2\sqrt{H^{3}N^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L}\right],

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=5e\sqrt{H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}},

we can solve for
U_{k,h,x}
and obtain the following upper bound:

U_{k,h,x}\leq\beta^{2}+2\alpha,

which we can write as:

\displaystyle U_{k,h,x}

\displaystyle\leq 25e^{2}H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}+24e\sqrt{H^{2}SAN^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L^{2}}+\frac{1642}{3}eH^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}+4e\sqrt{H^{3}N^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L}

\displaystyle\leq 24e\sqrt{H^{2}SAN^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L^{2}}+\frac{1846}{3}eH^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}+4e\sqrt{H^{3}N^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L}

\displaystyle\leq 28e\sqrt{H^{2}SAN^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L^{2}}+\frac{1846}{3}eH^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}

(70)

\displaystyle\leq 28\cdot\frac{12}{11}e\sqrt{H^{3}S^{2}AN^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L^{2}}

(71)

\displaystyle\leq 84\sqrt{H^{3}S^{2}AN^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)L^{2}},

where Equation (
70
) holds if
SA\geq H
, and Equation (
71
) holds under the condition of
[k]_{\mathrm{typ},x}
.

Plugging this result into Equation (
68
), and observing that the error cannot be greater than
H
, we get the following upper bound to the estimation error due to the optimistic approach:

V_{k,h}(x)-V_{h}^{*}(x)\leq\min\left\{84\sqrt{\frac{H^{3}S^{2}AL^{2}}{N^{\prime}_{k,h}(x)}},H\right\}.

(72)

Using this result, we now prove that
V_{k,h-1}(x)\geq V_{h-1}^{*}(x)
. Let us recall the definition of
V_{k,h-1}(x)
:

V_{k,h-1}(x)=\min\left\{V_{k-1,h-1}(x),H,\mathcal{T}_{h-1}^{\pi_{k}}V_{k,h}\right\},

where
\mathcal{T}_{h-1}^{\pi_{k}}V_{k,h}\coloneqq R^{\pi_{k}}(x_{k,h-1})+b_{k,h-1}(x_{k,h-1},a_{k,h-1}^{\pi_{k}})+\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\widehat{P}_{k}(\cdot|x_{k,h-1},a_{k,h-1}^{\pi_{k}})}V_{k,h}(y)
.
Observe that, if
V_{k,h-1}(x)=H
, then the optimism holds trivially. Also, if
V_{k,h-1}(x)=V_{k-1,h-1}(x)
, the optimism holds trivially under
\Omega_{k,h}
. As such, we only need to demonstrate the case in which
V_{k,h-1}(x)=\mathcal{T}_{h-1}^{\pi_{k}}V_{k,h}
. As such, we derive the following:

\displaystyle V_{k,h-1}(x)

\displaystyle-V_{h-1}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left\{R(x,a)+b_{k,h-1}(x,a)+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a)V_{k,h}(y)\right\}

\displaystyle\quad-R(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})-\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}P(y|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})V_{h}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle\geq b_{k,h-1}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})V_{k,h}(y)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}P(y|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})V_{h}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=b_{k,h-1}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})\left[V_{k,h}(y)-V_{h}^{*}(y)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})-P(y|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})\right]V_{h}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle\geq b_{k,h-1}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})-P(y|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})\right]V_{h}^{*}(y)

(73)

where Equation (
73
) follows from the induction assumption.

Under event
\mathcal{E}
, we can apply the empirical Bernstein inequality
(
Maurer and Pontil, 2009
)
:

\left|\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\widehat{P}_{k}(y|x,a)-P(y|x,a)\right]V_{h}^{*}(y)\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{k,h}^{*}(x,a)L}{N_{k}(x,a)}}+\frac{7HL}{3(N_{k}(x,a)-1)},

where
\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{k,h}^{*}(x,a)\coloneqq\Var_{y\sim\widehat{P}_{k}(\cdot|x,a)}[V_{h}^{*}(y)]
. As such, we obtain:

\displaystyle V_{k,h-1}(x)-V_{h-1}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle\geq b_{k,h-1}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})-\sqrt{\frac{2\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{k,h}^{*}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})L}{N_{k}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})}}-\frac{7HL}{3(N_{k}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})-1)}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\sqrt{\frac{4\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{k,h}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})L}{N_{k}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})}}+\sqrt{\frac{4L\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\widehat{P}_{k}(\cdot|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})}b^{\prime}_{k,h}(y)}{N_{k}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})}}\\
&\quad-\sqrt{\frac{2\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{k,h}^{*}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})L}{N_{k}(x,x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})}}.\end{split}

(74)

We now bound
\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{k,h}^{*}
in terms of
\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{k,h}
. Observing that:

\displaystyle\Var[X]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[X-\mathbb{E}[X]]^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[X\pm Y-\mathbb{E}[X]\pm\mathbb{E}[Y]]^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[(X-Y)-\mathbb{E}[X-Y]+Y-\mathbb{E}[Y]]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}[(X-Y)-\mathbb{E}[X-Y]]^{2}+2\mathbb{E}[Y-\mathbb{E}[Y]]^{2}

\displaystyle=\Var[X-Y]+2\Var[Y],

we can then rewrite:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{k,h}^{*}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})

\displaystyle\leq 2\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{k,h}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})+2\Var_{y\sim\widehat{P}_{k}(\cdot|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})}[V_{h}^{*}(y)-V_{k,h}(y)]

\displaystyle\leq 2\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{k,h}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})+2\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\widehat{P}_{k}(\cdot|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})(V_{k,h}(y)-V_{y}^{*})^{2}.

By plugging this result into Equation (
74
), we get:

V_{k,h-1}(x)-V_{h-1}^{*}(x)\geq\sqrt{\frac{4L\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\widehat{P}_{k}(\cdot|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})}b^{\prime}_{k,h}(y)}{N_{k}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})}}-\sqrt{\frac{4L\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\widehat{P}_{k}(\cdot|x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})}(V_{k,h}(y)-V_{h}^{*}(y))^{2}}{N_{k}(x,a_{k,h-1}^{\pi^{*}})}}.

By applying the result of Equation (
72
) and the definition of
b^{\prime}_{k,h}(y)
, we finally obtain that
V_{k,h-1}(x)-V_{h-1}^{*}(x)\geq 0
, thus demonstrating optimism.
∎

Having demonstrated optimism, we now prove the upper bound of the regret
\textnormal{Reg}(\text{{{UCBVI-BF}}{} },K)
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textnormal{Reg}}(\text{{{UCBVI-BF}}{} },K)

\displaystyle\leq U_{K,1}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=e\bigg[\sqrt{28HSATL^{2}}+\frac{7}{3}HSAL^{2}+2\sqrt{84^{2}H^{3}S^{4}A^{2}L^{4}}\\
&\quad+\sqrt{8HSAL^{2}U_{K1}}+\sqrt{6HSATL^{2}}+\frac{2}{3}HSAL^{2}\\
&\quad+2\sqrt{HSAL^{2}U_{K,1}}+\frac{8}{3}H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}+2\sqrt{H^{2}TL}\\
&\quad+4\sqrt{TL}+100H^{2}S^{2}AL\bigg]\end{split}

(75)

\displaystyle\leq e\bigg[12\sqrt{HSATL^{2}}+5\sqrt{H^{2}SAL^{2}U_{K,1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{821}{3}H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}+2\sqrt{H^{2}TL}\bigg]

(76)

where Equation (
75
) is obtained by applying the results of Lemmas
C.9
and
C.8
, by applying the result of Equation (
69
), and by accounting for the regret of non-typical episodes.

As done in Lemma
E.1
, by letting:

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=e\left[12\sqrt{HSATL^{2}}+\frac{821}{3}H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}+2\sqrt{H^{2}TL}\right],

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=5e\sqrt{H^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}},

we can solve for
U_{K,1}
and obtain:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textnormal{Reg}}(\text{{{UCBVI-BF}}{} },K)

\displaystyle\leq 24e\sqrt{HSATL^{2}}+\frac{1846}{3}eH^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}+4e\sqrt{H^{2}TL}

\displaystyle\leq 24e\sqrt{HSATL^{2}}+616eH^{2}S^{2}AL^{2}+4e\sqrt{H^{2}TL}

thus completing the proof.

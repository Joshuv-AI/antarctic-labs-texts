Multipartite Embezzlement of Entanglement
Lauritz van Luijk , Alexander Stottmeister , Henrik Wilming

Abstract
Embezzlement of entanglement refers to the task of extracting entanglement from an entanglement resource via local operations and without communication while perturbing the resource arbitrarily little. Recently, the existence of embezzling states of bipartite systems of type III von Neumann algebras was shown. However, both the multipartite case and the precise relation between embezzling states and the notion of embezzling families, as originally defined by van Dam and Hayden, were left open. Here, we show that finite-dimensional approximations of multipartite embezzling states form multipartite embezzling families. In contrast, not every embezzling family converges to an embezzling state. We identify an additional consistency condition that ensures that an embezzling family converges to an embezzling state. This criterion distinguishes the embezzling family of van Dam and Hayden from that of Leung, Toner, and Watrous. The latter generalizes to the multipartite setting. By taking a limit, we obtain a multipartite system of commuting type III 1 factors on which every state is an embezzling state. We discuss our results in the context of quantum field theory and quantum many-body physics. As open problems, we ask whether vacua of relativistic quantum fields in more than two spacetime dimensions are multipartite embezzling states and whether multipartite embezzlement allows for an operator-algebraic characterization.

Entanglement is the property of quantum states that is impossible to create with local operations.
However, it turns out that entanglement can be

Notation and conventions.

Throughout, we work in the setting of von Neumann algebras on separable Hilbert spaces. A von Neumann algebra

In the following, all operator algebras are assumed to be unital, and inclusions of operator algebras

We denote the scalar product of Hilbert space vectors

For an operator

{\mathcal{H}}
and a vectors

we sometimes write

\langle\Psi|a\Phi\rangle

Overview of results and discussion

In this section, we survey and discuss our main results.
Throughout this paper, we model quantum systems using von Neumann algebras: Observables are described by the self-adjoint part of a von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{M}}
. Quantum states are given by normal states on
{\mathcal{M}}
, i.e., ultraweakly continuous, positive, linear functionals
\omega:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
with
\omega(1)=1
.
The expectation value of an observable
a
in a state
\omega
is given by
\omega(a)
.

We begin by setting up a proper framework to describe
N
-partite systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom: For
x\in[N]:=\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we consider collections of
N
commuting von Neumann algebras
{\mathcal{M}}_{x}
generating a von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{M}}_{[N]}=\vee_{x}{\mathcal{M}}_{x}
.
2
2

2

{\mathcal{M}}\vee{\mathcal{N}}
denotes the von Neumann algebra generated by
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{N}}
.

This framework encompasses diverse situations, such as many-body systems in the thermodynamic limit that are partitioned into multiple infinite parts or relativistic quantum field theories with spacetime partitioned into several causally closed parts.

A finite-dimensional
N
-partite quantum system described by the Hilbert space
({\mathbb{C}}^{d})^{\otimes N}
is simply given by
{\mathcal{M}}_{[N]}=\otimes_{x=1}^{N}M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
, i.e., each subsystem is described by one copy of
M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{[N]}
.
Now consider a general
N
-partite system
({\mathcal{M}}_{x})_{x\in[N]}
and a normal state
\omega
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{[N]}
. We say that
\omega
is a multipartite embezzling state if for every two pure states
\psi
and
\phi
on the
N
-partite system on
({\mathbb{C}}^{d})^{\otimes N}
and every
\varepsilon>0
there exist unitaries
u_{x}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{x}\otimes M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
such that

\displaystyle\lVert u(\omega\otimes\phi)u^{*}-\omega\otimes\psi\rVert<\varepsilon,\qquad u=\prod_{x}u_{x}.

(4)

Whenever the local von Neumann algebras
{\mathcal{M}}_{x}
are generated by a sequence of increasing von Neumann algebras
{\mathcal{M}}^{(n)}_{x}
(e.g., finite subchains of increasing length of an infinite spin-chain), we obtain a sequence of multipartite systems
({\mathcal{M}}^{(n)}_{x})_{x\in[N]}
and associated marginal states
\omega^{(n)}
.
Our first main result is that in such a situation, every embezzling state induces embezzling families:

Theorem A

(informal, see cp.
Theorem

18
)
.

If
\omega
is an embezzling state as above, the family of multipartite states
(\omega^{(n)})_{n}
on
({\mathcal{M}}_{x}^{(n)})_{x\in[N]}
is a
consistent embezzling family
: For all
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists

, such that for every two pure states
\psi
and
\phi
on the
N
-partite system on
({\mathbb{C}}^{d})^{\otimes N}
there exist unitaries
u_{x}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{x}^{(n)}\otimes M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
such that

\displaystyle\lVert u(\omega^{(m)}\otimes\phi)u^{*}-\omega^{(m)}\otimes\psi\rVert<\varepsilon,\qquad u=\prod_{x}u_{x},\quad m\geq n.

(5)

Importantly, this result states that an embezzling state induces an embezzling family in a particularly strong sense: The unitaries necessary to achieve a given error
\varepsilon
and dimension
d
for the target states only require a minimal ‘size’ of the resource and remain valid if the latter is enlarged. It is this property that we refer to as
(quasi-)consistency
(see
Definition

17
for the distinction).

A direct corollary of
Theorem

A
is as follows: Consider a bipartite embezzling ground state of a many-body system in the thermodynamic limit or the vacuum of a relativistic quantum field theory (these exist, see, for example,
[
14
,
16
]
).
A priori, it would seem that to embezzle a state, it may be necessary for both agents to act on the entirety of their infinite subsystems. The theorem implies that, for any fixed precision, the agents, in fact, only have to act on a finite subsystem. Moreover, we can even show that the agents don’t have to access the infinite-volume ground state or the exact vacuum to exploit the embezzlement phenomenon: A sufficiently good approximation, e.g., via finite-size ground states
[
16
]
or scaling limits
[
17
,
18
]
, can serve as an embezzling state for a given error
\varepsilon
and dimensionality
d
of target states (see
Proposition

24
).

Let

The theorem answers the third question affirmatively: Not only do there exist multipartite embezzling states, but even multipartite systems where

The construction of the LTW embezzling family is, in a sense, highly inefficient as it involves tensor products over
\varepsilon

(informal, cp.

)

A bipartite embezzling family that can embezzle maximally entangled states of arbitrary dimension can embezzle arbitrary pure bipartite states of arbitrary dimension.

We emphasize that since we consider embezzlement relative to local operations and not relative to local operations and classical communication (LOCC), the above Lemma does not follow from the fact that any pure finite-dimensional entangled state can be obtained from a sufficiently high-dimensional maximally entangled state by LOCC.
In particular, it is, in fact, not sufficient if the embezzling family allows to embezzle Bell states (see

For bipartite entanglement,

if and only if the factors have type

Open Question
.

What are the necessary and sufficient conditions for a multipartite system
({\mathcal{M}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{M}}_{N})
to host multipartite embezzling states? Can we obtain a quantitative theory of multipartite embezzlement?

As we will see below, in contrast to the bipartite setting, the existence of multipartite embezzling states cannot be decided solely by looking at the type of the local von Neumann algebras, even if each of them is a factor in standard representation.
Rather, it depends on how each factor
{\mathcal{M}}_{x}
is embedded in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
relative to the remaining factors
({\mathcal{M}}_{y})_{y\neq x}
.
Hence, multipartite embezzlement is connected to the theory of subfactor inclusions
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
and we expect that its study will lead to fruitful results at the intersection of multipartite entanglement theory and the theory of operator algebras.

It follows from results in
[
14
]
that the vacuum sector of relativistic quantum field theories are bipartite embezzling states if we partition spacetime into two causally closed spacetime regions.
In fact, they are universal bipartite embezzlers.
Generalizing a result of Matsui
[
23
,
24
]
, we have shown in
[
16
]
that a large class of one-dimensional critical many-body systems provides bipartite, universal embezzlers in their ground state sectors.
If we work in one spatial dimension and assume that the subsystems of the parties are simply connected, neither the vacua of relativistic QFTs on 1+1 dimensional Minkowski space fulfilling the
split property

[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
nor ground states of spin chains provide
N
-partite embezzling states with

.
This follows because we show that a multipartite embezzling state induces a bipartite embezzling state on any pair of subsystems.
However, in the case of QFT, the split property implies that two properly spacelike separated regions cannot host bipartite embezzling states (see

Similarly, in a spin chain,

In view of the existence of multipartite embezzling states, this leads to the following open questions:

Are the vacua of relativistic quantum field theories in

Multipartite embezzling families and states

3.1
Multipartite systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom.

Definition 1

(Multipartite systems)
.

Let

An

of

of a von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{M}}_{[N]}

.

An

, and hence

If
{\mathcal{M}}_{[N]}
, and hence each subalgebra

, acts on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, we say that
({\mathcal{M}}_{x})_{x=1}^{N}
is an
N
-partite system on
{\mathcal{H}}
.
An
N
-partite system on
{\mathcal{H}}
is
irreducible
if
{\mathcal{M}}_{[N]}={\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
.
For
I\subset[N]
, we write
{\mathcal{M}}_{I}=\bigvee_{x\in I}{\mathcal{M}}_{x}
.
An
N
-partite system on
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfies

if

{\mathcal{M}}_{I}^{\prime}={\mathcal{M}}_{I^{c}}

(7)

for all
I\subset[N]
.
An
N
-partite system
({\mathcal{M}}_{x})_{x\in[{\mathcal{N}}]}
is a

of an
N
-partite system

if
{\mathcal{M}}_{x}\subset{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
for all

.

Definition 2

(Multipartite states)
.

A

on a multipartite system

on

For an irreducible multipartite system on a Hilbert space

on

For each

Let

be an

If

Assume that

Since

4
Monopartite and bipartite embezzling families

Embezzling families were discovered first by van Dam and Hayden in a finite-dimensional bipartite setting
[
1
]
. Bipartite embezzling states and families can equivalently be discussed in a monopartite setting
[
14
]
. In this section, we use this to discuss monopartite and bipartite embezzling families and their limits in more detail.
Our goal is to analyze the existence of limits of two constructions of embezzling families:

(1)

The van Dam-Hayden family
[
1
]
.

(2)

A highly simplified version of the LTW family.

For our purposes, we interpret both families as weak*-convergent sequences with respect to a suitable infinite
C^{*}
-tensor product of finite-dimensional matrix algebras. In this way, we obtain a qualitative difference between the two families – the vDH family leads to the hyperfinite type
{\mathrm{II}}_{1}
factor (on which no embezzling states exist) and the simplified LTW family provides us with the hyperfinite type
{\mathrm{III}}_{1}
factor – a universal embezzler. Interestingly, the embezzling unitaries for the simplified LTW family can still be constructed rather explicitly.

We follow
[
16
]
and define a monopartite version of the concept of an embezzling family as follows:

Definition 29
.

Let
({\mathcal{M}}^{(n)})_{n}
be an increasing net of von Neumann algebras and
(\omega_{n})_{n}
be a net of normal states on
{\mathcal{M}}^{(n)}
.
Then
(\omega^{(n)})_{n}
is called a
monopartite
embezzling family if for all
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
\varepsilon>0
there exist an
n=n(d,\varepsilon)
such that for all
m\geq n(d,\varepsilon)
and for all states
\psi
and
\phi
on
M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
there exist unitaries
u_{m}\in M_{d}({\mathcal{M}}^{(m)})={\mathcal{M}}^{(m)}\otimes M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
such that

\displaystyle\lVert u^{(m)}(\omega^{(m)}\otimes\phi)(u^{(m)})^{*}-\omega^{(m)}\otimes\psi\rVert

\displaystyle<\varepsilon.

(48)

Note that a monopartite embezzling family allows to embezzle arbitrary mixed states on single systems, whereas multipartite embezzling families allow to embezzle arbitrary pure, entangled states.
For finite-dimensional sequences
({\mathcal{M}}^{(n)})_{n}
, it is shown in
[
16
, Prop. 33]
that a monopartite embezzling family induces a bipartite embezzling family in the sense of
Definition

16
for
N=2
. Conversely, any bipartite embezzling family clearly induces a monopartite embezzling family by passing to its reduced states. Thus, results on monpartite embezzling families induce corresponding results on bipartite embezzling families. In the following, we therefore restrict to the monopartite setting.

To obtain our results, we first show that it suffices for a monopartite embezzling family to be able to embezzle arbitrary maximally mixed states in order to embezzle arbitrary mixed states.
There are two ways one can show this:
First, the results in
[
14
]
based on the
flow of weights
imply that for (monopartite)
embezzling states
(instead of families) being able to embezzle maximally mixed states of dimension
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
such that
\log(d_{1})/\log(d_{2})
is irrational is sufficient to be able to embezzle
arbitrary
mixed states.
Second, by the monopartite analog of
Theorem

20
, having a
consistent
monopartite family that can embezzle arbitrary maximally mixed states yields a state that can embezzle arbitrary maximally mixed states. Hence, it is a monopartite embezzling state, and its restriction (which yields again the starting family) yields a monopartite embezzling family.

Remark 30
.

The arguments of
[
14
]
also imply that
every
normal state on a type
{\mathrm{III}}_{1/d}
factor allows to embezzle maximally mixed states of dimension
d
. Since not all such states are monopartite embezzling states, it follows that being able to embezzle maximally mixed states of a fixed dimension is insufficient to guarantee monopartite embezzlement of all states. In the bipartite setting of embezzlement of entanglement, this means that it is not sufficient to be able to embezzle Bell states via local operations (clearly, it is sufficient if we additionally allow for classical communication
[
39
]
).

We now present a more direct argument for families on increasing finite-dimensional matrix algebras. It does not require consistency and is based on the following Lemma:

Lemma 31
.

Let
\omega
be a state on
M_{k}({\mathbb{C}})
and
\psi

M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
and suppose that a contraction

\displaystyle\lVert\omega\otimes\psi-s\omega s^{*}\rVert<\varepsilon.

If
s=va

s

and

an isometry, then

\displaystyle\lVert\omega\otimes\psi-v\omega v^{*}\rVert<6\varepsilon^{1/2}.

(50)

Proof.

Every isometry
v:{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
can be written as
u(1\otimes|1\rangle)
for some unitary
u
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
.
Hence the contraction
r=u(a\otimes 1)
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
satisfies

\displaystyle r(\omega\otimes\langle 1|{\,\cdot\,}|1\rangle)r^{*}=s\omega s^{*}.

(51)

Let
\Omega\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k_{n}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{k_{n}}
and
\Psi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
be the canonical purifications, so that
\lVert\Omega\otimes\Psi-r\otimes\bar{r}(\Omega\otimes|1\rangle|1\rangle)\rVert<\varepsilon^{1/2}

Let
r\otimes\bar{r}=(u\otimes\bar{u})b
be a polar decomposition and put

.
Then,
\lVert r\otimes\bar{r}\Phi\rVert=\lVert b\Phi\rVert\geq 1-\varepsilon^{1/2}

Since

is a contraction, we have

. Hence

\lVert\Phi-b\Phi\rVert^{2}=1+\langle\Phi|(b^{2}-2b)|\Phi\rangle\leq 1-\langle\Phi|b^{2}|\Phi\rangle\leq 1-(1-\varepsilon^{1/2})^{2}=2\varepsilon^{1/2}-\varepsilon

Applying the triangle inequality, we obtain

\lVert\Omega\otimes\Psi-u\otimes\bar{u}(\Omega\otimes|1\rangle|1\rangle\rVert)\leq\varepsilon^{1/2}+2\varepsilon^{1/2}-\varepsilon\leq 3\varepsilon^{1/2}.

Thus, the standard estimate

\lVert\omega\otimes\psi-v\omega v^{*}\rVert=\lVert\omega\otimes\psi-u(\omega\otimes\langle 1|{\,\cdot\,}|1\rangle)u^{*}\rVert\leq 6\varepsilon^{1/2}.

∎

Lemma 32

Let
(\omega^{(n)})_{n}

(2)

Proof.

The implication (1)

First, if we can embezzle arbitrary mixed states from the initial pure state

Second, if
v:{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
is an isometry, then we can write

for some unitary

\lVert v\omega v^{*}-\omega\otimes\psi\rVert<\varepsilon
.

Consider a state
\psi_{\{\lambda_{j}\}}

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

\displaystyle\omega_{n}\otimes\rho_{\{\lambda_{j}\}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}\omega_{n}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&\lambda_{d}\omega_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

(53)

Now, we assume that

can be approximated in norm in this way. We rewrite

\displaystyle\omega_{n}\otimes\rho_{\{\lambda_{j}\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{1}...q_{d}}\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}q_{2}...q_{d}\omega_{n}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

(54)

Since the family

can embezzle traces of arbitrary dimension, we may choose

\displaystyle v_{n,j}\omega_{n}v_{n,j}^{*}

\displaystyle\approx_{\varepsilon}\omega_{n}\otimes\tfrac{1}{q_{1}...p_{j}...q_{d}}\tr_{q_{1}...p_{j}...q_{d}},

(55)

for each

\displaystyle v_{n}(\omega_{n}\otimes\rho_{\{\lambda_{j}\}})v_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle\approx_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{q_{1}...q_{d}}\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{n}\otimes\Tr_{p_{1}q_{2}...q_{d}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

(56)

Finally, by potentially increasing

\displaystyle s_{n}\omega_{n}s_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle=v_{n}^{*}(w_{n}\omega_{n}w_{n}^{*})v_{n}\approx_{\varepsilon}v_{n}^{*}(\omega_{n}\otimes\tfrac{1}{q_{1}...q_{d}}\tr_{q_{1}...q_{d}})v_{n}\approx_{\varepsilon}\omega_{n}\otimes\rho_{\{\lambda_{j}\}},

4.2
A simpler Leung-Toner-Watrous embezzling family

In section

One obvious disadvantage of the construction is its ‘inefficiency’:

One way to quantify the efficiency of an embezzling family of states on finite spin chains is in terms of the asymptotic scaling of the circuit complexity.

14

Definition 36

Appendix A
Split bipartite systems admit no embezzling states

In this appendix, we prove a no-go theorem for bipartite embezzlement in split bipartite systems.
In fact, we will rule out the possibility of embezzling a single pure entangled state.

Let
\Psi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d}

\omega

({\mathcal{M}}_{1},{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
, i.e.,
\omega

{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\vee{\mathcal{M}}_{2}

We say that
\Psi

(or that

-embezzling

\lVert u_{1}u_{2}(\omega\otimes\langle 11|{\,\cdot\,}|11\rangle)u_{1}^{*}u_{2}^{*}-\omega\otimes\langle\Psi|{\,\cdot\,}|\Psi\rangle\rVert<\varepsilon.

\psi

\omega
a von Neumann algebra

Theorem 39

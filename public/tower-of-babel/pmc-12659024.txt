The prediction of phototherapy by early postnatal ETCOc in Chinese newborns
Authors: Ge Yang, Li Deng, Kun Zhang, Huijuan Liu, Xinrui Fu, Yue Hu, Xiaodan Yan, Xiaoyun Zhou, Wei Luo, Siyao Wang, Xiaotong Ye, Tianlang Zhang, Fan Li, Zhuanxia Huo, Yan Jiang, Shan Zeng, Dehua Wu, Yuan Yuan, Huayan Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe clinical significance of early postnatal ETCOc in the prediction of phototherapy (PT) for neonatal hyperbilirubinemia has not been fully characterized. We aimed to investigate the relationship between early postnatal ETCOc and the risk of PT within 7 days of life (7 DOLs) and diagnostic performance.MethodsThis is a secondary analysis of the infants randomized to the control group of the HEME trial. Cases were infants who had PT within 7 DOLs and controls were infants who did not require PT. Multivariate logistic regressions were used for the analyses of phototherapy and early postnatal ETCOc, adjusting for transcutaneous bilirubin level, gestational age, birth weight, sex and maternal blood types. We also evaluated the predictive performance of ETCOc by comparing multiple diagnostic parameters.ResultsIn 1,219 infants included, early postnatal ETCOc was associated with 2.98-(95% confidence interval [CI] = 2.22, 4.00, < 0.01), 2.53-(95%CI = 1.65, 3.90, < 0.01) and 2.42(95%CI = 1.05, 5.61, < 0.01)-fold increased odds of PT, respectively. Each one-unit increment of ETCOc was associated with earlier initiation, longer duration and more courses of PT. The ETCOc had a sensitivity of 65.4–87.5%, specificity of 71.7–75.8%, positive predictive values of 41.0–51.6%, and negative predictive values of 87.3–95.3%, with area under curves of 0.732–0.880 for the outcome of PT.ConclusionsA higher early postnatal ETCOc is linked with an increased risk of PT and PT characteristics within 7 DOLs. Early postnatal ETCOc can be a valuable indicator for predicting PT in early postnatal period.Trial registration: Registration identifier ChiCTR2100051476 of the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (https://www.chictr.org.cn/).Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12887-025-06073-x.

Introduction

Jaundice due to unconjugated hyperbilirunemia is extremely common in the early postnatal period. It affects more than 50% of neonates and mostly resolves spontaneously at 7–14 days after birth [1]. However, some cases of jaundice are so severe that therapeutic intervention is needed. If left untreated, the most concerning consequence is developing acute bilirubin encephalopathy or kernicterus, leading to permanent neurological sequelae and cerebral palsy [2, 3].

The underlying pathophysiology of neonatal hyperbilirubinemia (NHB) is an imbalance in bilirubin production, conjugation and excretion. Phototherapy (PT) can effectively reduce the level of unconjugated bilirubin and facilitate the excretion of unconjugated bilirubin. It has been widely used as the therapy of choice for NHB to prevent kernicterus for the past seven decades [4]. However, the decision to use PT must be carefully weighed against potential risks, such as interference with maternal–neonatal bonding, increased oxidative stress and a potential link to childhood diseases [4]. In practice, the initiation of treatment considers the risk factors for developing significant NHB and follows the nomogram to initiate treatments [2, 3, 5]. It requires thorough assessment of NHB risk factors, along with measurement of bilirubin levels (transcutaneous bilirubin [TcB] and total serum bilirubin [TSB]) and testing for hemolytic diseases, whereas multiple blood tests can be resource-demanding and time-consuming.

End-tidal carbon monoxide (CO) corrected for ambient CO (ETCOc) can be a good index for endogenous bilirubin production in individuals [6, 7]. Devices for measuring ETCOc are noninvasive and user friendly, offering a practical option for clinical applications. In 1967, Wranne L introduced a method to collect infants’ breath samples and measure the amount of CO exhaled, which allowed for the quantification of heme catabolism [8]. Heme is catabolized to bilirubin, along with an equal amount of CO. This CO molecule is transported to the lungs as carboxyhemoglobin (COHb), diffuses through alveolar capillaries and then is exhaled through breath [7]. The American Association of Pediatrics (AAP) guidelines suggest that measuring the ETCOc can be a potentially useful method for assessing hemolysis in the body [3, 5]. Encouraging evidence indicates the associations between ETCOc, bilirubin production and phototherapy. Studies have shown that neonates with hemolysis tend to have a higher level of ETCOc [9–11]. Bhutani et al. proposed a clinical algorithm based on the incremental relationship of ETCOc and pre-discharged TSB, suggesting the initiation of PT when ETCOc exceeds 1.5 ppm and TSB is above the 75th percentile [12]. Furthermore, some evidence shows that higher levels of ETCOc are linked to earlier initiation and longer durations of PT [13, 14].

There has been a lack of large sample-size studies examining the significance of ETCOc measured in the first few days after birth, for the management of PT in the NHB. This study aims to address this knowledge gap by investigating the relationship between early postnatal ETCOc levels and the likelihood of requiring PT for NHB and its characteristics. Additionally, we aimed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of early postnatal ETCOc for PT.

Methods

Study population

This was a secondary analysis of data from the participants in the control group in the single-center randomized controlled trial HEME (ChiCTR2100051476 of the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry https://www.chictr.org.cn/) [15]. The HEME trial evaluates the effect of using risk adjusted PT threshold based on ETCOc levels for the management of NHB. This trial enrolled 2,500 infants who were born at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\ge\:$$\end{document}35 weeks of gestational age with birth weights over 2000 g and whose TcB\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:>$$\end{document}40th percentile according to the 2004 Bhutani nomogram [5] was used within 72 h after birth from November 3, 2021, to July 29, 2022. The HEME trial study adheres to CONSORT guidelines [16]. Participants who were randomized to the control group and had non-missing information on hyperbilirubinemia requiring PT in the first 7 days of life (DOL) were included in the analysis. The study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee at Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center (IRB No. 2021136A01), and written parental/guardian consent was obtained.

Study procedures

ETCOc was measured by the point-of-care device CoSense® ETCO Monitor (Capnia, Inc., Redwood City, CA), which uses an electrochemical sensor to measure CO and hydrogen, and the final result was corrected for the CO level in the ambient air. A breath sample was collected noninvasively and continuously during inspiration and expiration (3–5 breaths) via a disposable nasal cannula. TcB was measured by the percutaneous bilirubin analyzer Drager JM-103 (Drägerwerk AG & Co., Luekeck, Germany) by pediatricians for every infant in the well-baby nursery every morning as per local practice.

ETCOc and TcB were measured as pairs at study recruitment, every 24 h during the stay in a well-baby nursery and every outpatient clinic visit after discharge within 7 DOLs with a time interval of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\le\:$$\end{document}3 h. The≤3-hour pairing window minimized biological variation while reflecting real-world clinical logistics. Follow-ups were conducted until the postnatal age of 7 days, either by in-person clinic visits or phone calls, to obtain information concerning the development of jaundice and hospitalization for PT due to the NHB. Infants who were diagnosed with hyperbilirubinemia and admitted to the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) were managed as per local practice, and PT was initiated at the discretion of pediatricians following the Chinese neonatal hyperbilirubinemia management guidelines [2]. The complete blood count, blood type, serum bilirubin level, G6PD enzyme activity ratio and neonatal ABO hemolysis panel were ordered, and the ETCOc was measured before the initiation of PT.

Outcomes and definitions

The primary outcome was NHB requiring PT within 7 DOLs. The secondary outcomes were the characteristics of PTs, including postnatal age at PT initiation and the duration and course of PT. The patients in this study were infants who had NHB requiring PT within 7 DOLs, and their counterparts were those who did not require PT.

Demographic and baseline variables, including gestational age, birth weight, sex, mode of delivery, and maternal blood types, were collected via chart review, and postnatal age and TcB at recruitment, as well as ETCOc values during the study, were recorded via data collection instruments. The diagnosis of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency or ABO hemolytic disease of newborn (HDN) was defined as the presence of clinically significant hyperbilirubinemia and positivity of G6PD deficiency testing or the presence of maternal-infant ABO incompatibility with positive antibody testing for ABO HDN. G6PD deficiency was diagnosed by either a reduced G6PD enzyme activity (< 2.6 U/g Hb on quantitative testing) or an abnormal enzyme ratio (G6PD/6PGD ratio < 1.1). All infants underwent universal screening of G6PD deficiency via heel-stick dried blood spot sampling as part of China’s newborn screening program. Confirmatory testing of G6PD deficiency was performed for screen-positive cases using the same criteria.

Statistical analysis

For data analysis, ETCOc was considered a continuous and a categorical variable. ETCOc was categorized by 1.5 and 2.5 ppm as proposed by Bhutani et al. [12]: ETCOc\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\le\:$$\end{document}1.5 ppm as low risk, 1.5–2.5 ppm as moderate risk and\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\ge\:$$\end{document}2.5 ppm as high risk. The chi-square test was used to compare categorical variables, and Student’s t test or the Mann‒Whitney U test was used to compare continuous variables for independent samples. Logistic regressions and linear regressions were used for the multivariable analyses of the primary outcome and the characteristics of PT with each early postnatal ETCOc value, respectively, adjusting for the TcB\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:>$$\end{document}P75 of the 2004 Bhutani nomogram [5], gestational age, birth weight, sex and maternal blood group. For the diagnostic test parameters, we calculated the sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) via multivariable regressions. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted as sensitivity against (1 - specificity). The area under the curve (AUC) was used to assess diagnostic performance. The optimal cutoff value of the ETCOc was calculated by maximizing the Youden index of the ROC curves. Adjustment for multiplicity of testing for the primary outcome was not prespecified. The R program (version 4.1.1, Posit Software, PBC, Boston, MA) was used for all analyses.

Table 3 shows the relationships between ETCOc levels and postnatal age when PT is required and between the duration and course of PT. For the analysis of postnatal age when PT was initiated, each 1 unit increase in ETCOc at recruitment (measured ~ 24 h after birth) was associated with a 20-hour (95% CI −28.66 to −12.32, P < 0.01) earlier age at which PT was needed. Compared with an ETCOc of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\le\:$$\end{document}1.5 ppm, an ETCOc of 1.5 to 2.5 and an ETCOc of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\ge\:$$\end{document}2.5 ppm was significantly associated with the need for PT at an earlier postnatal age. Similar significant associations were demonstrated for the ETCOc measured before PT. Moreover, a higher ETCOc at recruitment and measured before PT were significantly linked to a 6.35- and 7.14-hour longer duration of PT and more courses of intermittent PT during hospitalization. However, no significant associations were found for the 2nd or 3rd ETCOc and PT characteristics. Discussion

In this secondary exploratory analysis, we found that early postnatal ETCOc levels are linked with an increased risk of NHB requiring PT within 7 DOLs. Infants with higher ETCOc levels were more likely to require PT at an earlier postnatal age. Additionally, these infants were more likely to receive PT for a longer duration with more courses. This study demonstrated that early postnatal ETCOc had moderate-to-good performance but suboptimal positive predictive value in predicting the occurrence of PT within the first 7 DOLs.

Our findings are in line with those of previous studies. For 103 infants admitted for hyperbilirubinemia, Zhan et al.. reported an association between a higher ETCOc, measured before PT or at admission between 3 and 7 days of life, and earlier initiation of PT [13]. Their study and others reported the incremental relationship of ETCOc with the duration of PT [13, 14, 17]. However, it is important to consider the timing of ETCOc measurements for the interpretation of clinical significance. Two multicenter cohort studies reported significantly higher ETCOc levels in infants who developed hyperbilirubinemia (2.59\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\pm\:$$\end{document}3.17 ppm vs. 1.7\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\pm\:$$\end{document}0.53 ppm) [18] or required PT (1.81\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\pm\:$$\end{document}0.59 ppm vs. 1.45\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\pm\:$$\end{document}0.47 ppm) [19]. Our study focused on the early postnatal period, particularly from the first day of life until 72 h after birth, and revealed a clear association of an ETCOc exceeding 1.5 ppm during this time range with a substantially greater risk of requiring PT, with significant linear trends. In addition, the significant effect of repeated measurements of ETCOc after birth highlights the joint effect of ETCOc on the risk of PT, indicating the importance of monitoring the ETCOc trend after birth. The threshold-dependent nature of ETCOc interpretation is critical: while 1.5 ppm requires serial confirmation to predict PT, a single ETCOc > 2.5 ppm – reflecting substantial pathological hemolysis – significantly anticipates PT need. With a large sample size, our findings further contribute to the existing body of evidence, highlighting the relationship between early postnatal ETCOc, mostly measured within 72 h after birth, and the risk of PT and its characteristics.

Studies examining the diagnostic performance of the ETCOc and its optimal cutoffs have yielded inconsistent findings. Stevenson et al. conducted a study involving 1,370 infants to evaluate the predictive performance of early postnatal ETCOc (measured at approximately 30\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\pm\:$$\end{document}6 hours after birth) for hyperbilirubinemia [19]. They reported that a combination of ETCOc\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:>$$\end{document}1.48 ppm (as the population mean) with a TSB\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:>$$\end{document}75th percentile had a sensitivity ranging from 50 to 65% and a specificity of 80%; however, the PPV was only 6.4%, whereas the NPV was 99.0% [19]. On the other hand, Okuyama et al. suggested a threshold value of 1.8 ppm for predicting nonhemolytic hyperbilirubinemia in a convenience sample of 70 hyperbilirubinemic newborn infants, yielding reasonably high sensitivity (86%) and specificity (80%) [10]. In a more recent study of Chinese neonates [11], an early postnatal ETCOc\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\ge\:$$\end{document} of 1.7 ppm (as the 75th percentile of nonhemolytic infants in this population) combined with the TSB/TcB\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\ge\:$$\end{document}75th percentile demonstrated a sensitivity of 92.1%, specificity of 50.0%, PPV of 43.2%, and NPV of 93.9% for predicting PT.

Unlike prior studies [10, 11, 19], our analysis compared the performance of repeated ETCOc measured at multiple timepoints early postnatally instead of using one ETCOc value. The ETCOc-integrated strategies demonstrated superior performance compared with clinical characteristics alone, achieving similar diagnostic parameters but with a relatively higher AUC when combined with clinical characteristics and TcB. The relatively high sensitivity of ETCOc may support measuring ETCOc around, on average, 72 h after birth to screen infants at a greater risk of requiring PT. Our analysis yielded comparable diagnostic parameters to those reported by Bao et al. [11], although with a higher specificity, a lower sensitivity and a higher AUC. ETCOc has been reported to have a good NPV but a suboptimal PPV. One possible explanation for this is that a higher rate of bilirubin clearance may contribute to the resolution of hyperbilirubinemia in some infants with high bilirubin production, resulting in a reduction in the positive predictive accuracy of ETCOc measurements alone [20, 21]. While TcB measurement remains the most common tool for determining blood sampling needs, our data suggest ETCOc provides complementary value in identifying infants with accelerated bilirubin production. The high NPV (85–95%) of ETCOc may help reduce unnecessary monitoring in low-risk infants, whereas elevated ETCOc levels should prompt intensified surveillance regardless of initial TcB readings. Our findings on ETCOc’s predictive value align with recent real-world implementation studies. Wells et al., in a retrospective cohort of 1,029 newborns managed using ETCOc ≥ 2.5 ppm as the sole marker of significant hemolysis, demonstrates the feasibility of using ETCOc in diagnosing hemolysis [22].

In previous studies, most of the reported thresholds for ETCOc were single values derived from specific population distributions [11, 19]. However, in our study, we observed an increasing trend in the optimal ETCOc thresholds. These thresholds were determined on the basis of the maximal Youden index, considering both specificity and sensitivity equally important in determining the optimal cutoff. This trend seems reasonable considering the increasing rate of bilirubin production after birth. Nevertheless, the optimal approach for determining the best cutoffs for ETCOc remains unresolved, and there are possibly trade-offs between various parameters to achieve the best clinical performance of diagnostic measures.

There are several limitations that need to be acknowledged. First, this was a secondary analysis of a single-center RCT. As a result, there might be potential unmeasured confounding variables that could influence the results, e.g., history of scalp hematoma or significant bruising after birth, which were not accounted for in the study. Second, the participants in this study were infants considered to be at greater risk of developing hyperbilirubinemia, as indicated by the recruitment criteria of TcB levels being above the 40th percentile within 72 h after birth. This might limit the generalizability of the findings to a broader population of neonates with varying degrees of hyperbilirubinemia risk. Additionally, the study focused primarily on Chinese neonates in regions with prevalent neonatal hemolytic diseases, e.g., G6PD deficiency [23, 24]. This could affect the external validity of the study and might not fully represent other ethnic groups with potentially different patterns of hyperbilirubinemia and response to PT. Additionally, we acknowledge the inherent limitation of enzymatic G6PD screening (< 2.6 U/g Hb) in identifying heterozygous G6PD status in females. Their generally lower risk of clinically significant hyperbilirubinemia suggests minimal impact on phototherapy outcomes. The persistence of a significant association between ETCOc and phototherapy requirement, despite the potential for dilution from undetected female heterozygotes, further supports the clinical validity of ETCOc assessment. Although ETCOc has not been widely adopted in clinical practice, its potential for personalizing NHB management merits further validation. Multi-center implementation studies—particularly in regions with high burdens of hemolytic disease—are essential to establish clinical utility and scalability.

Conclusion

A higher early postnatal ETCOc is associated with an elevated risk of NHB, which necessitates PT within 7 DOLs, along with an earlier postnatal age when initiating PT as well as a longer duration and more courses of PT. These findings also indicate that ETCOc early after birth can be a valuable screening tool to identify neonates at greater risk of developing NHB. However, unanswered research questions remain regarding the utilization of ETCOc in guiding the initiation, termination and determination of the optimal duration of PT.

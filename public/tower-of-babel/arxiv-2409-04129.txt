Global Mild Solutions to a BGK Model for Barotropic Gas Dynamics
Dowan Koo, Sihyun Song

Abstract
. We establish global existence of mild solutions to the BGK model proposed by Bouchut [J. Stat. Phys., 95 , (1999), 113–170] under the minimal assumption of finite kinetic entropy initial data. Moreover we rigorously derive a kinetic entropy inequality, which combined with the theory developed by Berthelin and Vasseur [SIAM J. Math. Anal., 36 , (2005), 1807–1835] leads to the hydrodynamic limit to the barotropic Euler equations. The main tools employed in the analysis are stability estimates for the Maxwellian and a velocity averaging lemma.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
System and Notions

In this paper we study a BGK-type model, introduced and referred to in
[
10
, Section 3.1.2]
as the first model for barotropic gas dynamics:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\frac{1}{\tau}(M[f]-f),\\
f(0,\cdot,\cdot)=f_{0}(\cdot,\cdot).\end{cases}

Here
f(t,x,v)
is the distribution function expressing the number of gas particles in the infinitesimal cube of volume
\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v
located at
(x,v)
of the phase space
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
(at time
t\geq 0
). The parameter
\tau>0
is the relaxation time which we interpret here as the microscopic scale.

For each
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right]
and
\kappa\geq 0
, the Maxwellian
M
is first defined for a vector
(\rho,u)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

(1.2)

\displaystyle M[\rho,u](v):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle c_{2}\left(c_{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}-|v-u|^{2}\right)_{+}^{d/2}&\displaystyle\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right),\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle c_{2}\mathbf{1}_{c_{1}\rho^{2/n}\geq|v-u|^{2}}&\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}.\end{cases}

Here,

\displaystyle d:=\frac{2}{\gamma-1}-n

denotes the degree of freedom, and the constants are given by

\displaystyle c_{1}=\frac{2\gamma\kappa}{\gamma-1},\quad c_{2}=\left(\frac{2\gamma\kappa}{\gamma-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\right)}{\pi^{n/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}.

Then, for
f\geq 0
with
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};(1+|v|)\textnormal{d}v)
, the Maxwellian corresponding to
f
is defined as

\displaystyle M[f]:=M[\rho_{f},u_{f}],

where
\rho_{f}
and
u_{f}
are the macroscopic density and bulk velocity associated to
f
, respectively:

\displaystyle\rho_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(v)\textnormal{d}v,\qquad u_{f}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{f}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}vf(v)\textnormal{d}v&\text{if }\rho_{f}\neq 0,\\[9.0pt]
0&\text{if }\rho_{f}=0.\end{cases}

We recall some basic properties of the model (
1.1
). It has an associated kinetic entropy that is written

(1.3)

H(f,v):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}f+\frac{1}{2c_{2}^{2/d}}\frac{f^{1+2/d}}{1+2/d}&\displaystyle\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right),\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}f+\infty\cdot\mathbf{1}_{f>c_{2}}&\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}.\\
\end{cases}

The Maxwellian is constructed to satisfy the following properties, we refer to
[
10
,
4
,
12
]
for explicit proofs and calculations. For any
f\geq 0
with
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};(1+|v|^{2})\textnormal{d}v)
, the Maxwellian verifies

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1,v)M[f]\textnormal{d}v=(\rho_{f},\rho_{f}u_{f}),\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\otimes vM[f]\,\textnormal{d}v=\rho_{f}u_{f}\otimes u_{f}+\kappa\rho_{f}^{\gamma}\mathbb{I}.\end{cases}

Most importantly for any
f\geq 0
such that
f+H(f,v)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the following minimization principle

(1.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f,v)\textnormal{d}v\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(M[f],v)\textnormal{d}v

and compatibility condition hold:

(1.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(M[f],v)\textnormal{d}v=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{f}|u_{f}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{\gamma-1}\rho_{f}^{\gamma}.

Remark 1.1
.

For the endpoint case
\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}
the infinity term in (
1.3
) is necessary for the minimization principle (
1.4
) to hold for all
f
. Indeed, we provide in Appendix
A
a counterexample to the minimization principle if it is absent. To bypass this issue the initial data
f_{0}
should be assumed to satisfy
f_{0}\leq c_{2}
, then this bound propagates with time.

Remark 1.2
.

The Maxwellian for the end-point case
\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}
was written in
[
6
,
12
]
as

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[f]:=\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\kappa n\rho_{f}^{2/n}/|\mathbb{S}_{n}|\geq|u_{f}-v|^{2}\right\}}.

In this way

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(\mathcal{M}[f],v)\textnormal{d}v=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{f}|u_{f}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa nC_{n}}{2}\rho_{f}^{\frac{n+2}{n}}

for a constant
C_{n}\neq 1
so one needs to modify (
1.5
) for
\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}
. In this work, we tweaked the constants arising in the Maxwellian (compare (
1.6
) with our definition (
1.2
)) so that (
1.5
) holds consistently for all
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right]
.

Finally we mention that for a solution
f\geq 0
to (
1.1
) a formal calculation leads to the
kinetic entropy inequality

(1.7)

\begin{split}&\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f,v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f,v)-H(M[f],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s\\
&\qquad\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{0},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.\end{split}

For initial data
f_{0}
with finite kinetic entropy this implies

(1.8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f,v)-H(M[f],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s\leq\tau\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{0},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v=C\tau.

This estimate is crucially used in deriving the hydrodynamic limit of (
1.1
) as we explain in Section
1.3
.

1.2.
History and Review

The BGK model was proposed independently in
[
7
]
and
[
20
]
as a relaxation time approximation of the Boltzmann equation
[
9
]
. It describes relaxation towards equilibrium with a simpler collision operator yet obeys certain conservation laws that the Boltzmann equation is founded upon. Recently, interest has been given to its applications to gas mixtures
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
11
,
17
]
, relativistic particles
[
1
,
18
]
, reactive gas molecules
[
16
]
, and so on.

For our model of interest (
1.1
) we mention the following works. Its construction can be found in Bouchut
[
10
]
for the specific study of barotropic gas dynamics. The hydrodynamic limit of (
1.1
) to the barotropic Euler equations was discussed in
[
5
,
6
]
. Existence of solutions for the monodimensional case was studied in
[
4
]
. More recently
[
12
]
proved existence of weak solutions to (
1.1
) in dimensions
n\geq 2
for the range
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+4}{n+2}\right]\cup\left\{\frac{n+2}{n}\right\}
. Meanwhile, construction of unique classical solutions to (
1.1
) near a global Maxwellian was obtained in
[
14
]
, in a more restricted range of
\gamma
. Beyond these developments the existence of solutions in the full range
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right]
is yet to be established.

1.3.
Hydrodynamic Limit of (
1.1
) to the Barotropic Euler Equations

Berthelin and Vasseur
[
6
]
studied the hydrodynamic limit of (
1.1
) to the compressible Euler equations

(1.9)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\nabla\cdot(\rho u\otimes u+\kappa\rho^{\gamma}\mathbb{I})=0,\end{cases}

where
\gamma
is the heat capacity ratio, and
\kappa
is the proportionality constant for the adiabatic relations of an ideal gas. They deduced the following result, which links (
1.1
) to (
1.9
).

Theorem 1.3
.

[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
. Let
\gamma\in(1,\frac{n+2}{n}]
and
(\rho_{0},\rho_{0}u_{0})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the given initial data for the solution
(\rho,\rho u)\in C^{1}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
to (
1.9
) satisfying
\rho>0
; and
\rho,u,\nabla_{x}u,\nabla_{x}\rho
are bounded;
\rho|u|^{2},\rho^{\gamma}
are integrable with respect to
(t,x)
. Consider a family of initial values
\{f^{\tau}_{0}\}_{\tau>0}
with
f^{\tau}_{0}+H(f^{\tau}_{0},v)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
. Let
\{f^{\tau}\}_{\tau>0}
denote corresponding solutions to (
1.1
) satisfying the kinetic entropy inequality (
1.7
), with initial datum
f_{0}^{\tau}
. If the initial datum are well-prepared

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f_{0}^{\tau},vf_{0}^{\tau},H(f_{0}^{\tau},v))\textnormal{d}v\overset{\tau\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\Big(\rho_{0},\rho_{0}u_{0},\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{\gamma-1}\rho_{0}^{\gamma}\Big)\quad\text{in }L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

then

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{f^{\tau}}\to\rho&\text{strongly in }C^{0}(0,T;L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\quad p\in\left[1,\gamma\right),\\[6.0pt]
\rho_{f^{\tau}}u_{f^{\tau}}\to\rho u&\text{strongly in }C^{0}(0,T;L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\quad\displaystyle q\in\left[1,\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}\right).\\
\end{cases}

Let us briefly explain how the kinetic entropy inequality (
1.7
) is employed in
[
6
]
to yield the hydrodynamic limit of (
1.1
). From the study of the BGK structure in
[
6
, Section 4.1.1]
we note that
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
immediately applies to (
1.1
) provided that it is possible to obtain the decay estimate

(1.10)

\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|v|^{2}|f-M[f]|\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s=O(\sqrt{\tau})\quad\text{as}\quad\tau\to 0.

For the end-point case
\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}
a novel idea was suggested in
[
6
, Proposition 4.1]
to obtain the estimate

(1.11)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}|f-M[f]|\textnormal{d}v\lesssim(\rho_{f})^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}\sqrt{D_{f}}+D_{f},\\
&D_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f,v)-H(M[f],v)\,\textnormal{d}v.\end{split}

This estimate combined with the consequence (
1.8
) of the kinetic entropy inequality yielded (
1.10
).

For the intermediate range
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
an analogue of (
1.11
) (see Lemma
B.1
for the precise statement) was obtained in
[
6
, Section 4.1.2]
for another model, namely the
second kinetic model for barotropic gas dynamics

[
10
, Section 3.1.3]
:

(1.12)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}g+v\cdot\nabla_{x}g=\frac{1}{\tau}(\overline{M}[g]-g).

Here
g=g(t,x,v,I):\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to[0,c_{3}]
and the second Maxwellian
\overline{M}
is defined for
(\rho,u)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

(1.13)

\displaystyle\overline{M}[\rho,u](v,I):=c_{3}\mathbf{1}_{|v-u|^{2}+I^{2}<c_{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}}\quad(v,I)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty)

where

\displaystyle c_{0}=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\quad\text{and}\quad c_{3}=\left(\frac{2\pi\gamma\kappa}{\gamma-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\Gamma\left(\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\right).

Then
\overline{M}[g]:=\overline{M}[\rho_{g},u_{g}]
where

\displaystyle(\rho_{g},\rho_{g}u_{g}):=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1,v)g(t,x,v,I)c_{0}I^{d-1}\textnormal{d}I\textnormal{d}v.

The first model (
1.1
) is recovered by integrating the second model (
1.12
) against
c_{0}I^{d-1}\textnormal{d}I
.

The authors could not locate in
[
6
]
an analogue of (
1.11
) for the first model in the range
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
. To complement the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
, we provide the following lemma.

Lemma 1.4
.

For
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
and
0\leq f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};(1+|v|^{2})\textnormal{d}v)
, there is a constant
C=C_{n,\gamma}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}|f-M[f]|\textnormal{d}v\leq C\left\{(\rho_{f})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\sqrt{D_{f}}+D_{f}\right\},

where

D_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(H(f,v)-H(M[f],v)\right)\textnormal{d}v.

A short proof is presented in Appendix
B
. Applying Lemma
1.4
and (
1.8
) to the first model (
1.1
) we can verify that (
1.10
) holds for all
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right]
and Theorem
1.3
can be deduced.

To complete the hydrodynamic limit theory for barotropic gas dynamics initiated in
[
6
]
, we aim to answer in the positive the following question:

For any given
f_{0}
with finite kinetic entropy, can we construct global-in-time weak solutions to (
1.1
) satisfying the kinetic entropy inequality?

1.4.
Main Results

The weakest possible assumption we can impose on
f_{0}
is that it has finite kinetic entropy, namely

(1.14)

f_{0}\geq 0,\quad\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|v|^{2})f_{0}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v<\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{cases}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1+2/d}}<\infty&\displaystyle\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right),\\[9.0pt]
0\leq f_{0}\leq c_{2}&\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}.\end{cases}

Solutions that obey the kinetic entropy inequality (
1.7
) should satisfy

(1.15)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}\cap L^{1+2/d}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))&\displaystyle\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right),\\[9.0pt]
f\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leq f\leq c_{2}&\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}\end{cases}

where
L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}):=L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n},(1+|v|^{2})\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v)
.

Taking the above into consideration we define the following notion of a solution to (
1.1
):

Definition 1.5
.

Given
f_{0}\geq 0
, we say
f
satisfying (
1.15
) is a
mild solution
to (
1.1
) if it holds that

(1.16)

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)=e^{-t/\tau}f_{0}(x-vt,v)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)/\tau}M[f](s,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}s.

Moreover, if a mild solution
f
verifies the kinetic entropy inequality (
1.7
) for all
t\in[0,\infty)
we say
f
is a
mild entropy solution
.

We now state our main result concisely as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n\geq 1
and assume the initial data
f_{0}
verifies (
1.14
). For any
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right]
and
\kappa,\tau>0
the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) admits a mild entropy solution.

Remark 1.7
.

We emphasize that no additional assumptions on
f_{0}
are imposed, namely we do not require compact support, rapid decay, or higher moments in
v
. In particular, we do not impose any assumption on the spatial moments such as

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|x|^{2}f_{0}\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v<\infty.

Remark 1.8
.

Any mild solution in the sense of Definition
1.5
is a solution to (
1.1
) in the distributional sense. Namely, for any
T>0
and
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2n})
with
\phi_{0}:=\phi(0,\cdot,\cdot)

\displaystyle-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{0}\phi_{0}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v-\int_{0}^{T}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f(\partial_{t}\phi+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{T}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(M[f]-f)\phi\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}t.

This can be confirmed by a direct computation which we provide in Appendix
C
.

1.5.
Strategy of Proof

1.5.1.
Stability Estimate for the Maxwellian

As done in a classical approach in
[
19
]
we derive a stability estimate for the Maxwellian (Lemma
1.9
) in the
|v|^{2}
–weighted space
L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
. This estimate is employed to establish the existence of solutions to an approximate system corresponding to (
1.1
). In
[
12
]
, for the end-point case
\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}
a geometric argument was used to obtain the stability estimate. For the intermediate range
1<\gamma<\frac{n+2}{n}
the mean value theorem was employed, but the range
\gamma\in\left(\frac{n+4}{n+2},\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
(equivalently
\frac{d}{2}\in(0,1)
) could not be handled due to the singularities that arise at the boundary of the support of the Maxwellian.

Intriguingly, our ideas come from the second kinetic model for barotropic gas dynamics (
1.12
). We do not discuss explicitly the second model but only employ the second Maxwellian
\overline{M}
(
1.13
). For
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
the relation between
M
and
\overline{M}
is given by

(1.17)

M[\rho,u](v)=c_{2}\left(c_{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}-|v-u|^{2}\right)_{+}^{d/2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{M}[\rho,u](v,I)c_{0}I^{d-1}\textnormal{d}I.

Thus instead of tackling
M
itself we exploit (
1.17
) to bypass the difficulties that arose in
[
12
]
. Precisely, we prove in this paper the following lemma.

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
n\geq 1
. For any
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right]
,
\theta\in[0,1]
, and non-negative
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};(1+|v|)\textnormal{d}v)
, the following stability estimate

(1.18)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|M[\rho_{f},u_{f}]-M[\rho_{g},u_{g}]\right|\textnormal{d}v\leq|\rho_{f}-\rho_{g}|+C^{\theta}\min\{\rho_{f},\rho_{g}\}^{1-\frac{\theta(\gamma-1)}{2}}|u_{f}-u_{g}|^{\theta}

holds for some constant
C=C(\kappa)>0
. As a result, if we further assume the bounds
\rho_{f},\rho_{g},|u_{f}|,|u_{g}|\leq C_{0}
with
C_{0}>0
, then

(1.19)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})\left|M[\rho_{f},u_{f}]-M[\rho_{g},u_{g}]\right|\textnormal{d}v\leq C(C_{0},\gamma,\kappa)\left(|\rho_{f}-\rho_{g}|+|u_{f}-u_{g}|\right).

1.5.2.
Analysis of an Approximate System

The estimate (
1.19
) requires that the macroscopic density and bulk velocity be bounded. Thus to apply (
1.19
) it is necessary to introduce a modified Maxwellian
M^{(\varepsilon)}
, which automatically ensures (
3.2
). We establish the existence of approximate solutions
f^{\varepsilon}
corresponding to
M^{(\varepsilon)}
via Picard iteration and then obtain uniform estimates for
f^{\varepsilon}
.

While similar in nature to that of
[
12
]
, our choice of modified Maxwellian
M^{(\varepsilon)}
is distinct in that it additionally guarantees the pointwise relations (
3.4
), which play an indispensable role throughout this work. As an instance, via Lemma
3.4
, the relations in (
3.4
) allow us to obtain tightness for
\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}
without any assumptions on the spatial moments of the initial data.

In passing to the limit, compactness of the macroscopic variables is obtained by a version of the velocity averaging lemma (Proposition
4.1
), then the strong convergence of
M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]
follows by the Vitali convergence theorem. As our solutions are constructed via mild form, the strong convergence of the Maxwellians results in strong convergence of
f^{\varepsilon}
itself. We deduce that the limit
f
is a mild solution to (
1.1
).

In order to derive the kinetic entropy inequality we first prove it for the approximate solutions
f^{\varepsilon}
.
Passing to the limit in the entropy inequality is a very delicate task. The pointwise relations (
3.4
), the minimization principle (
1.4
), the pointwise convergence of
f^{\varepsilon}
(up to a subsequence via the strong convergence), and iterated uses of Fatou’s lemma are all crucially employed when passing to the limit.

1.6.
Organization of the Paper

In Section
2
we prove the stability estimates given in Lemma
1.9
. We consider the approximate system regarding the modified Maxwellian in Section
3
. The mild solution to (
1.1
) is obtained in Section
4
by passing to the limit of the approximate solutions with help of a velocity averaging lemma. We rigorously establish the kinetic entropy inequality in Section
5
, thus finishing the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Appendix
A
discusses the minimization principle for the case
\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}
. We present a proof of Lemma
1.4
in Appendix
B
. Finally, in Appendix
C
, we confirm that mild solutions to (
1.1
) satisfy the weak formulation of the equation.

2.
Stability Estimate of the Maxwellian

In this section we prove Lemma
1.9
regarding the stability of the Maxwellian. To simplify the presentation of the proofs we define for
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
{\bf c}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle B[r,{\bf c}](v):=\mathbf{1}_{|v-{\bf c}|^{2}\leq r^{2}}(v)

where
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with radius
r
and center
{\bf c}
we will write
B_{r}({\bf c})
. As a preliminary step we provide a stability estimate for
B
with respect to the center
{\bf c}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
. For any
r>0
and
{\bf c}_{1},{\bf c}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|B[r,{\bf c}_{1}](v)-B[r,{\bf c}_{2}](v)|\textnormal{d}v\leq 2|\mathbb{B}_{n-1}|r^{n-1}|{\bf c}_{1}-{\bf c}_{2}|,

where
|\mathbb{B}_{n-1}|
is the Lebesgue measure of the
(n-1)
-dimensional unit ball. For convention we take
|\mathbb{B}_{0}|=1
.

Proof.

We treat the case
n\geq 2
first. It is clear that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|B[r,{\bf c}_{1}](v)-B[r,{\bf c}_{2}](v)\right|\textnormal{d}v=|B_{r}({\bf c}_{1})\bigtriangleup B_{r}({\bf c}_{2})|.

Denote
B_{i}:=B_{r}({\bf c}_{i})
,
i=1,2
for brevity. For each
{\bf z}\in\partial B_{1}
satisfying
({\bf z}-{\bf c}_{1})\cdot({\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{1})\leq 0
set
{\bf z}^{\prime}:={\bf z}+{\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{1}
. Note that
{\bf z}^{\prime}\in\partial B_{2}
with
({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf c}_{2})\cdot({\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{1})=({\bf z}-{\bf c}_{1})\cdot({\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{1})\leq 0
. Indeed

|{\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf c}_{2}|=|{\bf z}-{\bf c}_{1}|=r,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}|=|{\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{1}|

so that
{\bf c}_{1}{\bf c}_{2}{\bf z}^{\prime}{\bf z}
forms a parallelogram (see
Figure 1
). We claim that

B_{1}\setminus B_{2}\subset\{{\bf w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:{\bf w}\in L({\bf z},{\bf z}^{\prime})\,\,\text{for some}\,\,{\bf z}\in\partial B_{1},({\bf z}-{\bf c}_{1})\cdot({\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{1})\leq 0\}=:\mathcal{S}_{1}

where
L({\bf z},{\bf z}^{\prime})
is the set of points which consists the line segment with endpoints
{\bf z}
and
{\bf z}^{\prime}
. If
{\bf w}\in B_{1}\setminus B_{2}
, we note that there are two
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(2.1)

{\bf w}-\theta({\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{1})\in\partial B_{1}

with opposite sign. We denote by
{\bf z}_{\bf w}:={\bf w}-\theta_{+}({\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{1})
the unique point satisfying (
2.1
) with
\theta_{+}>0
. Observe that the following linear function

w(\theta):={\bf z}_{\bf w}+\theta({\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{1}),\,\,\,\theta\in[0,\infty)

satisfies

w(0)={\bf z}_{\bf w},\,\,\,w(1)={\bf z}_{\bf w}^{\prime},\,\,\,w(\theta_{+})={\bf w}.

Upon recalling

|{\bf w}-{\bf c}_{1}|\leq r=|{\bf z}_{\bf w}-{\bf c}_{1}|=|{\bf z}_{\bf w}^{\prime}-{\bf c}_{2}|<|{\bf w}-{\bf c}_{2}|,

we deduce that
{\bf w}
lies in the line segment with endpoints
{\bf z}_{\bf w}
and
{\bf z}_{\bf w}^{\prime}
. This means
{\bf w}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, so the claim
B_{1}\setminus B_{2}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}
is proven. Hence, it follows that

|B_{1}\setminus B_{2}|\leq|\mathcal{S}_{1}|=|\mathbb{B}_{n-1}|r^{n-1}|{\bf c}_{1}-{\bf c}_{2}|.

Figure 1 :
Illustration of
\mathbf{z}_{\bf w},{\bf w},
and
{\bf z}_{\bf w}^{\prime}
. The region
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is shaded.

Symmetrically we can construct a set
\mathcal{S}_{2}
from which we may deduce

|B_{1}\bigtriangleup B_{2}|\leq|\mathcal{S}_{1}|+|\mathcal{S}_{2}|=2|\mathbb{B}_{n-1}|r^{n-1}|{\bf c}_{1}-{\bf c}_{2}|

and this concludes the proof for
n\geq 2
.

For the case
n=1
, we note that if
B[r,{\bf c}_{1}](v)\cap B[r,{\bf c}_{2}]\neq\varnothing
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|B[r,{\bf c}_{1}](v)-B[r,{\bf c}_{2}](v)|\textnormal{d}v=2|{\bf c}_{1}-{\bf c}_{2}|.

On the other hand, if the two sets are disjoint, then
2r\leq|{\bf c}_{1}-{\bf c}_{2}|
. Thus:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|B[r,{\bf c}_{1}](v)-B[r,{\bf c}_{2}](v)|\textnormal{d}v=4r\leq 2|{\bf c}_{1}-{\bf c}_{2}|.

Altogether, the conclusion of the lemma follows.

∎

Consequently we can now obtain the stability estimates as asserted in Lemma
1.9
.

Proof of Lemma
1.9
.

We consider the case
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
: without loss of generality, we may assume
\rho_{1}\geq\rho_{2}
.

First, we discuss the case where both densities are nonzero, in other words
\rho_{2}>0
. Write

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|M[\rho_{1},u_{1}]-M[\rho_{2},u_{2}]|\textnormal{d}v\\
&\quad\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|M[\rho_{1},u_{1}]-M[\rho_{2},u_{1}]|\textnormal{d}v+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|M[\rho_{2},u_{1}]-M[\rho_{2},u_{2}]|\textnormal{d}v\\
&\quad=:\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}.\end{split}

By utilizing the assumption
\rho_{1}\geq\rho_{2}
, we obtain

\mathcal{I}_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}M[\rho_{1},u_{1}]-M[\rho_{2},u_{1}]\textnormal{d}v=\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}.

To estimate
\mathcal{I}_{2}
, we crucially exploit the following relation

M[\rho_{2},u_{i}](v)=c_{2}d\int_{0}^{\left(c_{1}\rho_{2}^{\gamma-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}B\left[\left(c_{1}\rho_{2}^{\gamma-1}-I^{2}\right)^{1/2},u_{i}\right](v)I^{d-1}\textnormal{d}I,\quad i=1,2,

which follows from (
1.17
) (note that
c_{0}c_{3}=c_{2}d
).
Writing
A=c_{1}\rho_{2}^{\gamma-1}
and letting
\beta(x,y)
denote the Beta function,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle=c_{2}d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\int_{0}^{\sqrt{A}}B\left[\left(A-I^{2}\right)^{1/2},u_{1}\right](v)-B\left[\left(A-I^{2}\right)^{1/2},u_{2}\right](v)I^{d-1}\textnormal{d}I\right|\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}d\int_{0}^{\sqrt{A}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|B\left[\left(A-I^{2}\right)^{1/2},u_{1}\right](v)-B\left[\left(A-I^{2}\right)^{1/2},u_{2}\right](v)\right|\textnormal{d}vI^{d-1}\textnormal{d}I

\displaystyle\leq 2|\mathbb{B}_{n-1}|c_{2}d\int_{0}^{\sqrt{A}}(A-I^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|u_{1}-u_{2}|I^{d-1}\textnormal{d}I\qquad\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem : 2.1})}

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{B}_{n-1}|c_{2}dA^{\frac{n+d}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{\frac{n+1}{2}-1}s^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\textnormal{d}s|u_{1}-u_{2}|\qquad(s:=I^{2}/A)

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{B}_{n-1}|c_{2}d\,\,\beta\Big(\frac{n+1}{2},\frac{d}{2}\Big)\,c_{1}^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}c_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{2}^{1-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}|u_{1}-u_{2}|

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\Lambda\,\rho_{2}^{1-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}|u_{1}-u_{2}|

where
\Lambda=\Lambda(\gamma,\kappa)>0
is given by

\Lambda=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\Gamma(\frac{1}{\gamma-1})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{\gamma-1}+\frac{1}{2})}\frac{(\gamma-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(2\gamma\kappa)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

calculated thanks to the relations

|\mathbb{B}_{n-1}|=\frac{\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{2})},\quad\beta\left(\frac{n+1}{2},\frac{d}{2}\right)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n+d+1}{2})},\quad\Gamma\left(x+1\right)=x\Gamma(x).

Using the asymptotic formula

\Gamma(x+\alpha)\sim\Gamma(x)x^{\alpha}

as
x\to\infty
, letting
\gamma\to 1^{+}
we have

\Lambda\to\left(\frac{2}{\pi\kappa}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which means
\Lambda
is bounded independently of
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
, in other words
\Lambda\leq C=C(\kappa)
.

Let us proceed. For any
\theta\in[0,1]
, in the case
|u_{1}-u_{2}|<C^{-1}\rho_{2}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}
we can estimate (
2.2
) further using
\Lambda\leq C
by

\mathcal{I}_{2}\leq C^{\theta}\rho_{2}^{1-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\theta}|u_{1}-u_{2}|^{\theta}.

In the opposite case
|u_{1}-u_{2}|\geq C^{-1}\rho_{2}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}
, that is
C\rho_{2}^{-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}|u_{1}-u_{2}|\geq 1
, it is immediate that

\mathcal{I}_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|M[\rho_{2},u_{1}]-M[\rho_{2},u_{2}]|\textnormal{d}v\leq 2\rho_{2}\leq 2C^{\theta}\rho_{2}^{1-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\theta}|u_{1}-u_{2}|^{\theta}.

This proves (
1.18
), under the assumption of nonzero densities, when
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
. The end point case
\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}
can be proven similarly.

Next, we prove (
1.18
) for the case where a zero density is present: assume
\rho_{1}\geq\rho_{2}=0
. In this case, observe that by definition
M[\rho_{2},u_{2}](v)\equiv 0
. This yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|M[\rho_{1},u_{1}]-M[\rho_{2},u_{2}]|\,\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}M[\rho_{1},u_{1}]\,\textnormal{d}v=\rho_{1}=|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}|,

and we conclude that (
1.18
) holds in all possible cases.

Finally, by noting that

v\in\text{supp}(M[\rho,u])\implies|v|\leq|u|+c_{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}

if we further assume
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},|u_{1}|,|u_{2}|\leq C_{0}
, then

v\in\text{supp}(M[\rho_{1},u_{1}])\cup\text{supp}(M[\rho_{2},u_{2}])\implies|v|\leq C_{0}+c_{1}C_{0}^{\gamma-1}.

This observation and the inequality (
1.18
) applied with
\theta=1
leads to (
1.19
).

∎

3.
Approximate Solutions

As mentioned in Section
1.5.2
the stability estimate (
1.19
) is not immediately applicable to obtain the continuity
f\mapsto M[f]
in
L^{1}_{2}
. Indeed even if say
f\in L^{1}_{2}\cap L^{\infty}
it is in general not expected that the macroscopic quantities
\rho_{f}
or
u_{f}
be bounded. This is what motivates the modification of the Maxwellian
M\mapsto M^{(\varepsilon)}
with a small parameter
\varepsilon>0
.

Thus, we would now like to consider an approximate model where a modified Maxwellian is present. We set for
0<\varepsilon<1

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f^{\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\varepsilon}=M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]-f^{\varepsilon},\qquad f^{\varepsilon}(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v),\\[6.0pt]
M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]:=M\left[\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)},u_{f^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)}\right],\\[6.0pt]
\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)}:=\dfrac{\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}}{1+\varepsilon\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}},\qquad u_{f^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)}:=\dfrac{\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}u_{f^{\varepsilon}}}{\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}u_{f^{\varepsilon}}|)}.\end{cases}

Using the stability estimates for
M
derived in the previous section, we now aim to prove the existence of solutions to (
3.1
). From hereon we present proofs for our results only for the case where
\gamma\in\big(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\big)
. For any of the sections to follow the case
\gamma=\frac{n+2}{n}
is easier, and we did not mention it because the presentation would become untidy. For example, we would have to repeatedly change the definition of
H(f,v)
.

Also, for the sake of convenience we shall from now set
\kappa=\tau=1
because they play no role when discussing the existence of solutions.

Note as a preliminary step that for any
f,g
it holds that

(3.2)

\begin{split}\rho_{f}^{(\varepsilon)}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon},\quad|u_{f}^{(\varepsilon)}|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon},\quad|\rho_{f}^{(\varepsilon)}-\rho^{(\varepsilon)}_{g}|\leq|\rho_{f}-\rho_{g}|,\\
\quad|u^{(\varepsilon)}_{f}-u^{(\varepsilon)}_{g}|\leq\frac{2|\rho_{f}u_{f}-\rho_{g}u_{g}|}{\varepsilon}+\frac{|\rho_{g}-\rho_{f}|}{\varepsilon^{2}}.\end{split}

These relations and (
1.19
) imply that for any
f,g\in L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|v|^{2})|M^{(\varepsilon)}[f]-M^{(\varepsilon)}[g]|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq C(\varepsilon)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|v|^{2})|f-g|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

We remark that (
3.2
) is also satisfied by the modified Maxwellian considered in
[
12
]
. The main difference is that our method additionally provides the following pointwise relations

(3.4)

\rho_{f}^{(\varepsilon)}\leq\rho_{f},\quad\quad|u_{f}^{(\varepsilon)}|\leq|u_{f}|.

These relations are crucially used when applying Lemma
3.4
, and also in the derivation of the kinetic entropy inequality later in Section
5
.

The goal of this section is to prove the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

For each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
f^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}\cap L^{1+2/d}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
a mild solution to (
3.1
), in other words it verifies

(3.5)

\displaystyle f^{\varepsilon}(t,x,v)=e^{-t}f_{0}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}](s,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}s.

Moreover, there is a uniform bound on the following quantities

(3.6)

\begin{cases}\|f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}\cap L^{1+2/d}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))}\leq C(f_{0}),\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|v|^{2}f^{\varepsilon}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq C(f_{0}).\end{cases}

Finally, for any
T>0
the following tightness condition holds for the macroscopic densities:

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sup_{0<\varepsilon<1}\int_{\{|x|>R\}}\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}(t,x)\textnormal{d}x=0.\end{split}

We begin with a standard iterative sequence
\{f^{\varepsilon}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
with initial step
f^{\varepsilon}_{0}:=f_{0}
at
k=0
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
\varepsilon>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is a unique mild solution
f^{\varepsilon}_{k+1}\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{1}\cap L^{1+2/d}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
to the system

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f^{\varepsilon}_{k+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\varepsilon}_{k+1}=M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}^{\varepsilon}]-f_{k+1}^{\varepsilon},\\
f_{k+1}^{\varepsilon}(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v)\end{cases}

verifying

(3.9)

f_{k+1}^{\varepsilon}(t,x,v)=e^{-t}f_{0}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}](s,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}s.

Moreover there are the uniform-in-
k,\varepsilon
estimates

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\|f^{\varepsilon}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}\cap L^{1+2/d}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))}\leq C(f_{0}),\\
\|f^{\varepsilon}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))}\leq C(f_{0}).\end{cases}

To streamline the proof of uniform estimates, we present the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{a_{k}(\cdot)\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of
C^{1}([0,\infty);\mathbb{R})
functions satisfying

a_{k}^{\prime}(t)+a_{k}(t)\leq a_{k-1}(t),\quad a_{k}(0)=a_{0}(t)=a_{0}\in[0,\infty)

for all
t\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have
a_{k}(t)\leq a_{0}
.

Proof.

At the base step
k=0
, the assertion of the lemma is trivial by the assumption
a_{0}(t)=a_{0}
. Assume now that the hypothesis holds as
a_{k}(t)\leq a_{0}
. An application of Grönwall’s lemma to the given inequality shows that

\displaystyle a_{k+1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t}a_{k+1}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}a_{k}(s)\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t}a_{k+1}(0)+a_{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle=e^{-t}a_{0}+a_{0}(1-e^{-t})=a_{0},

which completes the induction.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Since
\rho_{f}^{(\varepsilon)},u_{f}^{(\varepsilon)}\leq 1/\varepsilon
holds for any
f
, we have
M^{(\varepsilon)}[\cdot]\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
. The existence and uniqueness of each
f^{\varepsilon}_{k}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
which verifies (
3.9
) then follows by standard results for linear transport equations. Assuming a fixed value of
\varepsilon
let us write
f_{k}:=f^{\varepsilon}_{k}
to lighten the notation.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{k+1}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{k+1}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}]\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho^{(\varepsilon)}_{f_{k}}\textnormal{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{f_{k}}\textnormal{d}x=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{k}\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v,

we apply Lemma
3.3
to deduce that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{k+1}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{0}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v,

which is the desired uniform
L^{1}
-estimate mentioned in
\eqref{eq : unif in k, ve}_{1}
.

To obtain the
L^{1+2/d}
-estimate, we test
\frac{d+2}{d}f_{k+1}^{2/d}
against the linearized equation (
3.8
). This, and an application of Young’s inequality, show

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{k+1}^{1+2/d}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle=\frac{d+2}{d}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}]\,f_{k+1}^{2/d}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v-\frac{d+2}{d}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{k+1}^{1+2/d}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d+2}{d}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left(\frac{d}{d+2}(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}])^{1+2/d}+\frac{2}{d+2}f_{k+1}^{1+2/d}\right)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{d+2}{d}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{k+1}^{1+2/d}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}])^{1+2/d}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{k+1}^{1+2/d}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{k+1}^{1+2/d}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{k+1}^{1+2/d}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}])^{1+2/d}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

On the other hand, it is immediate that

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}f_{k+1}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}f_{k+1}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}]\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

Together we find

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{k+1},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{k+1},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

From (
1.5
) and the pointwise relations in (
3.4
)

(3.12)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}],v)\textnormal{d}v&=\frac{1}{2}(\rho_{f_{k}}^{(\varepsilon)})|u_{f_{k}}^{(\varepsilon)}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}(\rho_{f_{k}}^{(\varepsilon)})^{\gamma}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho_{f_{k}}|u_{f_{k}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}(\rho_{f_{k}})^{\gamma}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(M[f_{k}],v)\textnormal{d}v.\end{split}

Using (
3.12
) and the minimization principle, (
3.11
) reduces to

(3.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{k+1},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{k+1},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{k},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

and an application of Lemma
3.3
shows

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{k+1},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{0},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

By definition this proves the
L^{1+2/d}
-estimate in
\eqref{eq : unif in k, ve}_{1}
and also
\eqref{eq : unif in k, ve}_{2}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

By the same calculations in
[
12
, Section 3.1.2]
we verify as a result of (
3.3
) that the sequence
\{f_{k}\}
is Cauchy in
L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
. Hence, the
f_{k}
converges in this space to a limit
f^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
. In view of the uniform in time estimates in (
3.10
), we deduce that
f^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
.

Also, by applying (
3.3
), we obtain the convergence
M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}]\to M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]
in
L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})).
Indeed, for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|v|^{2})\left|M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}]-M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\right|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}C(\varepsilon)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|v|^{2})|f_{k}-f^{\varepsilon}|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad k\to\infty.

The convergence of the Maxwellians implies that the integral arising in the mild form (
3.9
) converges as

(3.14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}](s,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}s\to\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}](s,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}s

in
L^{\infty}_{loc}([0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
. To see this, for each
T>0
and
t\in[0,T]
, note that

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|v|^{2})\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}]-M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}])(s,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}s\right|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}e^{s-t}(1+|v|^{2})\left|(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}]-M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}])(s,x-v(t-s),v)\right|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}e^{s-t}(1+|v|^{2})\left|M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}](s,x,v)-M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}](s,x,v)\right|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}\textnormal{d}s\right)\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|v|^{2})\left|M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}](s,x,v)-M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}](s,x,v)\right|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}]-M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))}\to 0\quad\text{as $k\to\infty$}.

Thus, we can pass to the limit in (
3.9
) to verify straightforwardly that
f^{\varepsilon}
is a mild solution to (
3.1
). The estimates asserted in (
3.6
) then follow directly from Lemma
3.2
.

Lastly it remains to establish
(
3.7
)
. It follows as a direct consequence of Lemma
3.4
below. Note that the condition (
3.16
) holds with
G^{\varepsilon}=M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]-f^{\varepsilon}
due to the relation
\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)}\leq\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}
in (
3.4
).
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\{f^{m}\}\subset L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
be a family of weak solutions to the transport equations

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f^{m}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{m}=G^{m},\\
f^{m}(0,\cdot,\cdot)=f_{0}(\cdot,\cdot)\end{cases}

where
G^{m}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
and

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G^{m}\textnormal{d}v\leq 0.

Assume also

\mathcal{E}:=\sup_{m}\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|v|^{2})f^{m}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v<\infty.

Then, if
\psi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
verifies
|\psi(v)|\lesssim(1+|v|)^{\sigma}

for some
\sigma\in[0,2)
, the function

\rho^{m}_{\psi}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{m}(t,x,v)\psi(v)\textnormal{d}v

satisfies the estimate

\sup_{m}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{|x|\geq 2R}\left|\rho_{\psi}^{m}(t,x)\right|\textnormal{d}x\leq\mathcal{E}^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\left(\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{0}\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x+\frac{2\mathcal{E}T}{R}\right)^{1-\frac{\sigma}{2}}

for any
T,R>0
.

Proof.

We first demonstrate the case where
\psi(v)\equiv 1
. As will be seen soon, the general case follows by interpolation.
Choose a decreasing function
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,\infty))
satisfying
\|\xi^{\prime}(r)\|_{\infty}\leq 2
and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\xi(r)=1&r\in[0,1],\\
\xi(r)=0&r\in[2,\infty),\end{cases}

and set
\zeta(r):=1-\xi(r)
. Then, for each
R>0
, define
\chi_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\chi_{R}(x)=\zeta\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right)
. Similarly, we set
\eta_{k}(\cdot):=\xi\left(\frac{|\cdot|}{k}\right)
for
k>0
.

For each
k,l>R
, we use
\chi_{R}(x)\eta_{k}(x)\eta_{l}(v)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
as a test function in the weak formulation of (
3.15
): for any
t\in[0,T]
,

(3.17)

\begin{split}&\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f^{m}\chi_{R}(x)\eta_{k}(x)\eta_{l}(v)\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\\
&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{0}\chi_{R}(x)\eta_{k}(x)\eta_{l}(v)\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}vf^{m}\eta_{l}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}(\chi_{R}(x)\eta_{k}(x))\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}G^{m}\chi_{R}(x)\eta_{k}(x)\eta_{l}(v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s\\
&=:I+II+III.\end{split}

Since
0\leq\eta_{k}(\cdot),\eta_{l}(\cdot)\leq 1
, we first have that

I\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{0}\chi_{R}(x)\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x.

The second term is majorized as

II\leq\frac{2}{R}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|v|^{2})f^{m}\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\leq\frac{2\mathcal{E}T}{R},

thanks to the relations
\|\nabla_{x}\chi_{R}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2}{R}
,
\|\nabla_{x}\eta_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2}{k}\leq\frac{2}{R}
, and
|v|\leq 1+|v|^{2}
. For the last term, since
G^{m}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
and
\eta_{l}\nearrow 1
, we find from the dominated convergence theorem that:

\lim_{l\to\infty}III=\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}G^{m}\chi_{R}(x)\eta_{k}(x)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s\leq 0,

where the inequality follows from (
3.16
) and non-negativity of
\chi_{R}(x)\eta_{k}(x)
.

For the left-hand side of (
3.17
), we note that the monotone convergence theorem provides

\lim_{k,l\to\infty}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f^{m}\chi_{R}(x)\eta_{k}(x)\eta_{l}(v)\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f^{m}\chi_{R}(x)\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x.

Thus, taking the limsup
k,l\to\infty
in (
3.17
), we obtain

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f^{m}\chi_{R}(x)\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f_{0}\chi_{R}(x)\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x+\frac{2\mathcal{E}T}{R}.

Finally, we employ the following relation

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{|x|\geq 2R}(x)\leq\chi_{R}(x)\leq\mathbf{1}_{|x|\geq R}(x)

to obtain

(3.18)

\begin{split}\int_{|x|\geq 2R}\rho_{f^{m}}\textnormal{d}x\leq\int_{|x|\geq R}\rho_{f_{0}}\textnormal{d}x+\frac{2\mathcal{E}T}{R}.\end{split}

The general case regarding
\rho_{\psi}^{m}
with
\sigma\in[0,2)
follows then by interpolation. Indeed applying Hölder’s inequality we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq 2R}\left|\rho_{\psi}^{m}\right|\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|v|^{2})f^{m}\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\left(\int_{|x|\geq 2R}\rho_{f^{m}}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{1-\frac{\sigma}{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\left(\int_{|x|\geq 2R}\rho_{f^{m}}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{1-\frac{\sigma}{2}}

which combined with (
3.18
) completes the proof.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.6
: Convergence Analysis

To obtain compactness of the macroscopic variables corresponding to
f^{\varepsilon}
we introduce a version of the velocity averaging lemma, reminiscent of
[
15
, Lemma 2.7]
. Here the given sequence is not assumed to be bounded in
L^{\infty}
and more importantly the assumption on the spatial moment is replaced by a tightness condition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
, and
f^{m}
solutions to the transport equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f^{m}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{m}=G^{m}_{+}-G^{m}_{-},\\
f^{m}(0,\cdot,\cdot)=f_{0}(\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})\end{cases}

where
G^{m}_{+},G^{m}_{-}\geq 0
. Assume the following:

(1)

\{f^{m}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
.

(2)

\{(1+|v|^{2})f^{m}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
.

(3)

\{G^{m}_{+}\}
and
\{G^{m}_{-}\}
are bounded in
L_{loc}^{1}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
.

(4)

For each
T>0
, the family
\{\rho_{f^{m}}(t,\cdot)\}_{(m,t)\in\mathbb{N}\times[0,T]}
is tight:

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sup_{m}\int_{|x|\geq R}\rho_{f^{m}}\textnormal{d}x=0.

Then, for each
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
verifying
|\psi(v)|\lesssim(1+|v|)^{\sigma}
with
\sigma\in[0,2)
, the sequence
\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{m}\psi(v)\textnormal{d}v\right\}
is relatively compact in
L_{loc}^{1}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

Proof.

We aim to prove that for each
\eta>0
and
T>0
, it is possible to extract a subsequence (depending on
\eta
and
T
) of

\rho_{\psi}^{m}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{m}(t,x,v)\psi(v)\textnormal{d}v

satisfying

(4.1)

\limsup_{k,\ell\to\infty}\|\rho_{\psi}^{m_{k}}-\rho_{\psi}^{m_{l}}\|_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\eta.

Suppose that we have proven this. By taking

\eta_{j}:=\frac{1}{j}\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad T_{j}:=j\to\infty,

we can extract subsequences verifying (
4.1
) recursively, then use a standard diagonal argument to finally pass to a subsequence which is Cauchy in
L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
T>0
. The conclusion of the proposition would then follow, and hence it suffices to prove the above claim.

Let
\eta>0
and
T>0
be given. By interpolating the assumptions (2) and (4) as we did in Lemma
3.4
we can fix
R>0
satisfying

(4.2)

\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|x|\geq R}\left|\rho_{\psi}^{m}\right|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\leq\frac{\eta}{8}.

Consider next smooth cutoff functions

\displaystyle\Xi_{r}(v)=\begin{cases}1&|v|\leq r,\\
0&|v|\geq r+1\end{cases}

and set

\displaystyle\rho_{\psi}^{m,r}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{m}(t,x,v)\psi(v)\Xi_{r}(v)\textnormal{d}v.

By classical velocity averaging results
[
13
, Section III]
, for each
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the sequence
\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{m}\phi(v)\textnormal{d}v\right\}
is relatively compact in
L^{1}([0,T]\times B_{R}(0))
. As its direct application, for each
r>0
there is a subsequence of
\{\rho_{\psi}^{m,r}\}_{m}
(depending on
r
) that is convergent in
L^{1}([0,T]\times B_{R}(0))
, and for this subsequence clearly

\limsup_{k,l\to\infty}\|\rho_{\psi}^{m_{k},r}-\rho_{\psi}^{m_{l},r}\|_{L^{1}([0,T]\times B_{R}(0))}\leq\frac{\eta}{4}.

Thanks to (
4.2
), we deduce

\displaystyle\limsup_{k,l\to\infty}\|\rho_{\psi}^{m_{k},r}-\rho_{\psi}^{m_{l},r}\|_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{\eta}{2}.

On the other hand, we note that for any
r>0

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\rho_{\psi}^{m}-\rho_{\psi}^{m,r}|\,\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{|v|\geq r}|f^{m}(t,x,v)\psi(v)|\,\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1+r)^{2-\sigma}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|v|)^{2}f^{m}\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x.

Therefore, in view of the assumption (2) we can fix large
r=r(T)>0
verifying

\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\|\rho_{\psi}^{m}-\rho_{\psi}^{m,r}\|_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{\eta}{4}.

Finally, along the subsequence corresponding to this fixed
r

\displaystyle\limsup_{k,l\to\infty}\|\rho_{\psi}^{m_{k}}-\rho_{\psi}^{m_{l}}\|_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k,l\to\infty}\Big(\|\rho_{\psi}^{m_{k}}-\rho_{\psi}^{m_{k},r}\|_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\rho_{\psi}^{m_{k},r}-\rho_{\psi}^{m_{l},r}\|_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\|\rho_{\psi}^{m_{l},r}-\rho_{\psi}^{m_{l}}\|_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\eta

and hence (
4.1
) is proven. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, Proposition
3.1
and the Dunford–Pettis theorem imply that there is a limit
f\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
with

\displaystyle f^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup f\quad\text{in}\quad L^{1}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})).

As a direct application of Proposition
4.1
to the sequence
\{f^{\varepsilon}\}
there is a subsequence verifying

\displaystyle\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}\to\rho_{f},\quad\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}u_{f^{\varepsilon}}\to\rho_{f}u_{f}\quad\text{strongly in}\quad L^{1}_{loc}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

We can pass to a further subsequence and assume that the convergences above also hold a.e. in
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This moreover implies

\displaystyle\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)}=\frac{\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}}{1+\varepsilon\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}}\to\rho_{f}\quad\text{a.e. in }[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle u_{f^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)}=\frac{\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}u_{f^{\varepsilon}}}{\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}u_{f^{\varepsilon}}|)}\to u_{f}\quad\text{a.e. in }\{(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\rho_{f}(t,x)>0\}.

As a consequence we deduce the strong convergence of the Maxwellians.

Lemma 4.2
.

It holds that

\displaystyle M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\to M[f]\quad\text{in}\quad L^{1}_{loc}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})).

Proof.

For any
T>0
, we write

\displaystyle\iiint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]-M[f]|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\iiint_{\{\rho_{f}>0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}+\iiint_{\{\rho_{f}=0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\right)|M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]-M[f]|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle\qquad=I^{\varepsilon}+J^{\varepsilon}.

On the set
\{\rho_{f}>0\}\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have the pointwise convergence
M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\to M[f]
. Recall that the family
\{M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\}
is bounded in both
L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
and
L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1+2/d}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
. Also, since

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{|v|\geq R}M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\leq\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\geq R}\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)}\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x|\geq R}\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}\textnormal{d}x,

the family
\{M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\}
is tight thanks to (
3.7
). Altogether we deduce that the Vitali convergence theorem is applicable and
I^{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

For
J^{\varepsilon}
we simply note that
M[f]=0
on the set
\{\rho_{f}=0\}
, hence

\displaystyle J^{\varepsilon}=\iiint_{\{\rho_{f}=0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}t=\iint_{\{\rho_{f}=0\}}\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\{\rho_{f}=0\}}\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle\to\iint_{\{\rho_{f}=0\}}\rho_{f}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}t=0

thanks to the strong convergence
\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}\to\rho_{f}
in
L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
∎

We then obtain strong convergence of the sequence
\{f^{\varepsilon}\}
itself.

Lemma 4.3
.

It holds that

\displaystyle f^{\varepsilon}\to f\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})).

In particular, the limit
f
verifies for any
t\in[0,\infty)

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)=e^{-t}f_{0}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}M[f](t,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}s

and is thus a mild solution to (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.5
.

Proof.

Thanks to the strong convergence of the Maxwellians verified in the previous lemma, we readily verify that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}](s,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}s\to\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}M[f](s,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}s

in
L^{\infty}_{loc}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
. Indeed:

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]-M[f])(s,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}s\right|\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]-M[f]|(s,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]-M[f]|(s,x,v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s\to 0\quad\forall\,T>0.

Hence, passing to the limit in (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle f^{\varepsilon}(t,x,v)

\displaystyle\to e^{-t}f_{0}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}M[f](s,x-v(t-s),v)\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle\quad=f(t,x,v)\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))\quad\forall\,T>0.

As this strong convergence holds for any
T>0
, we recall the uniform in time estimates provided in (
3.6
) to deduce that
f\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}\cap L^{1+2/d}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
. It is then straightforward that
f
is a mild solution to the BGK equation (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.5
.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.6
: Kinetic Entropy Inequality

To complete the proof of Theorem
1.6
, it only remains to show that the kinetic entropy inequality (
1.7
) holds.

We need to recover previous estimates from the linearized level. Namely from (
3.11
)

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{k+1},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{k+1},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

Hence,

(5.1)

\begin{split}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{k+1},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v&+\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{k+1},v)-H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\\
&\qquad\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{0},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.\end{split}

Recall that
f_{k}\to f^{\varepsilon}
strongly in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
for any
T>0
. In view of the uniform estimates in (
3.10
) this also implies that for each
t\in[0,T]
the sequence
\{f_{k}(t)\}
converges weakly in
L^{1+2/d}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
to
f^{\varepsilon}(t)
. By the strong convergence in
L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
and lower semicontinuity of the norm under weak convergence, we deduce that for all
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f^{\varepsilon},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{k+1},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

For the Maxwellian, we use (
1.5
) and (
3.2
) to note that for any
g,h\in L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
it holds:

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[g],v)-H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[h],v)\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\rho^{(\varepsilon)}_{g}|u^{(\varepsilon)}_{g}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{h}^{(\varepsilon)}|u_{h}^{(\varepsilon)}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}(\rho_{g}^{(\varepsilon)})^{\gamma}-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}(\rho_{h}^{(\varepsilon)})^{\gamma}\,\textnormal{d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}(\rho_{g}^{(\varepsilon)}-\rho_{h}^{(\varepsilon)})|u_{g}^{(\varepsilon)}|^{2}+\rho_{h}^{(\varepsilon)}(u_{g}^{(\varepsilon)}+u_{h}^{(\varepsilon)})\cdot(u_{g}^{(\varepsilon)}-u_{h}^{(\varepsilon)})\,\textnormal{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho_{g}^{(\varepsilon)})^{\gamma}-(\rho_{h}^{(\varepsilon)})^{\gamma}\,\textnormal{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\rho_{g}^{(\varepsilon)}-\rho_{h}^{(\varepsilon)}|\textnormal{d}x+\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{g}^{(\varepsilon)}-u_{h}^{(\varepsilon)}|\textnormal{d}x+\frac{C\gamma}{\varepsilon^{\gamma-1}(\gamma-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\rho_{g}^{(\varepsilon)}-\rho_{h}^{(\varepsilon)}|\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}+\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{4}}+\frac{C\gamma}{\varepsilon^{\gamma-1}(\gamma-1)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\rho_{g}-\rho_{h}|\textnormal{d}x+\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\rho_{g}u_{g}-\rho_{h}u_{h}|\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon,\gamma)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})|g-h|\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

Above, the integral at the third line was estimated using the upper bounds in (
3.2
):
\rho_{g}^{(\varepsilon)},\rho_{h}^{(\varepsilon)},|u_{g}^{(\varepsilon)}|,|u_{h}^{(\varepsilon)}|\leq 1/\varepsilon
. The integral at the fourth line was estimated by first applying the mean-value theorem to the function
z\mapsto z^{\gamma}
, then using again the upper bounds for
\rho_{g}^{(\varepsilon)}
and
\rho_{h}^{(\varepsilon)}
. The sixth line is a consequence of the stability estimates in (
3.2
).

Consequently, the convergence
f_{k}\to f^{\varepsilon}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
implies

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f_{k}],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\right|\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad k\to\infty.

Together, we can then deduce that (
5.1
) implies for all
t\in[0,T]

(5.2)

\begin{split}&\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f^{\varepsilon},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v+\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f^{\varepsilon},v)-H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\\
&\quad\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{0},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.\end{split}

We need now pass to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. First, note that Proposition
3.1
and the Dunford Pettis theorem imply
f^{\varepsilon}(t)\rightharpoonup f(t)
weakly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
for all
t\in[0,T]
, since the limit is already identified by Lemma
4.3
. This implies that for any
R>0

\begin{split}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|v|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{|v|\leq R}f\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|v|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{|v|\leq R}f^{\varepsilon}(t)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\\
&\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|v|^{2}f^{\varepsilon}(t)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.\end{split}

Since this holds for all
R>0
applying the monotone convergence theorem to the left-hand-side yields

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|v|^{2}f(t)\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|v|^{2}f^{\varepsilon}(t)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

Also, we have that
f^{\varepsilon}(t)\rightharpoonup f(t)
weakly in
L^{1+2/d}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
. Thus the lower semicontinuity of the norm under weak convergence implies

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f^{\frac{d+2}{d}}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(f^{\varepsilon})^{\frac{d+2}{d}}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

Hence

(5.3)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f,v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f^{\varepsilon},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v.

Second, let us show that

(5.4)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f,v)-H(M[f],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s\\
&\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f^{\varepsilon},v)-H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s.\end{split}

Note as a preliminary step that for any
\delta>0
, thanks to the pointwise convergences
\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}\to\rho_{f}
and
\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}u_{f^{\varepsilon}}\to\rho_{f}u_{f}

(5.5)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(M[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\textnormal{d}v&=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}}|u_{f^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}(\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}})^{\gamma}\\
&\to\frac{1}{2}\rho_{f}|u_{f}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}(\rho_{f})^{\gamma}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(M[f],v)\textnormal{d}v\quad\text{a.e. in }\{(t,x):\rho_{f}(t,x)>\delta\}\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{split}

From (
1.5
) and (
3.4
) we recall again that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\textnormal{d}v\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(M[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\textnormal{d}v
. Henceforth for any
t\in[0,T]
and with
A_{\delta}:=\{(s,x):\rho_{f}(s,x)>\delta\}\subset[0,t]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f^{\varepsilon},v)-H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f^{\varepsilon},v)-H(M[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\textnormal{d}v\right)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\iint_{A_{\delta}}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f^{\varepsilon},v)-H(M[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\textnormal{d}v\right)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle=\iint_{A_{\delta}}\left(\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f^{\varepsilon},v)\textnormal{d}v-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(M[f],v)\textnormal{d}v\right)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\iint_{A_{\delta}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f,v)-H(M[f],v)\textnormal{d}v\right)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s.

Indeed at the second line above the integrand is non-negative thanks to the minimization principle. Thus the inequality at the third line holds by monotonicity of the integral and then Fatou’s lemma. The equality following it is due to (
5.5
). The last inequality follows by Fatou’s Lemma since by Lemma
4.3
we may assume
f^{\varepsilon}\to f
pointwise.

Since this holds for all
\delta>0
we can let
\delta\to 0
by applying the dominated convergence theorem. This yields

(5.6)

\begin{split}&\iint_{\{\rho_{f}>0\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f,v)-H(M[f],v)\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s\\
&\quad\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f^{\varepsilon},v)-H(M^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s,\end{split}

where we have denoted
\{\rho_{f}>0\}:=\{(s,x):\rho_{f}(s,x)>0\}\subset[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. On the other hand it is clear that
f=0
a.e. on
\{\rho_{f}=0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This and the minimization principle show

\displaystyle 0=\iint_{\{\rho_{f}=0\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f,v)\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s=\iint_{\{\rho_{f}=0\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(M[f],v)\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s,

and we conclude that (
5.6
) implies (
5.4
). Altogether (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) show that in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, (
5.2
) yields

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f,v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v+\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f,v)-H(M[f],v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}s\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}H(f_{0},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

for all
t\in[0,T]
. As
T>0
is arbitrary we obtain precisely (
1.7
) for the case
\tau=1
. Thus
f
is indeed a mild entropy solution to (
1.1
), and this completes the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
1.4

We present here a proof of Lemma
1.4
. We first recall the following result regarding the Maxwellian of the second model (

Lemma B.1
.

[
6
, Proposition 4.6]

For
\gamma\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
and every
\overline{f}=\overline{f}(v,I)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(1+|v|^{2}+I^{2})\overline{f}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty);c_{0}I^{d-1}\textnormal{d}v\textnormal{d}I),\\

Our idea is to suitably associate an extra variable
I
to a function

, and then apply Lemma

. Observe from the definition (

2

\rho_{f}u_{f}\end{pmatrix}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\

\rho_{\overline{f}}u_{\overline{f}}\end{pmatrix},

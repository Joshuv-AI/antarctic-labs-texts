Estimating the critical threshold for stretched-out hyperbolic random connection models
Matthew Dickson

Abstract
This paper examines the model-dependent asymptotic behaviour of the critical threshold intensity for stretched-out random connection models (RCMs) on hyperbolic spaces. The proof uses lace expansion arguments, but has notable qualitative differences to the Euclidean case in how it evaluates spectral radii. The result is applied to the Boolean disc RCM and a heat kernel RCM.

Introduction

Many percolative systems exhibit a phase transition: below some critical parameter value there exists no infinite connected component almost surely, and above the critical parameter there does exist an infinite connected component almost surely. Kesten proved in
[
19
]
that for Bernoulli bond percolation (BBP) on nearest-neighbour
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
the critical edge probability
p_{c}=\frac{1}{2}
. Having such an exact answer for the critical value is atypical.

Estimates for the critical probability for spread-out Bernoulli bond percolation (BBP) on various graphs have been found in works such as
[
9
,
24
,
13
,
8
,
15
,
25
,
16
]
. Most relevant to this paper,
[
13
]
uses lace expansion results to examine spread-out BBP on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d>6
. Amongst other results, they prove that if two vertices
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
share an edge independently with probability

\frac{p}{\left(2L+1\right)^{d}-1}\mathds{1}\left\{\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq L\right\},

(1.1)

then the critical probability obeys

p_{\mathrm{c}}=1+\left(U^{\star 2}\left(o\right)+\frac{1}{2}\sum^{\infty}_{n=3}\left(n+1\right)U^{\star n}\left(o\right)\right)L^{-d}+\mathcal{O}\left(L^{-d-1}\right),

(1.2)

as
L\to\infty
, where
U\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\left[0,1\right]
is given by

U(x):=2^{-d}\mathds{1}\left\{\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}<1\right\}

(1.3)

and
U^{\star n}\left(x\right)
denotes the
n
-fold convolution of
U
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In this paper we will be concerned with approximating the critical threshold for stretched-out random connection models (RCMs) on the hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
(for
d\geq 2
). We will also be using a lace expansion approach (building upon the arguments in
[
11
]
and
[
6
]
), but will not require the full
spread-out
behaviour of
[
13
]
, and the relevancy of terms in the asymptotics will differ from (
1.2
). The high-level proof description and the result will look similar to the argument in
[
6
]
which dealt with RCMs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
d\to\infty
, but as we shall see, the “easy” and “difficult” parts of the proof are transposed.
The lace expansion component of the argument is now significantly easier because we can inherit the finiteness of the critical triangle diagram from the spherical transform argument in
[
7
]
– no bootstrap argument is required. However, extracting the critical intensity,
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
, from the resulting Ornstein-Zernike equation is no longer as simple a matter as applying the (
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
-analog of the) Fourier transform, and a more careful study of the
L^{1}\to L^{1}
spectral radius of particular convolution operators is required.

The Model and the Results

2.1
The Model

Hyperbolic space.

For
d\geq 2
, hyperbolic
d
-space (denoted
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
) is the unique simply connected
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with constant sectional curvature
-1
. It can be convenient to describe
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
using the Poincaré ball model (also called the conformal ball model). Let us consider the open Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with unit radius:
\mathbb{B}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\left\lvert x\right\rvert<1\right\}
. Then for each
x\in\mathbb{B}
the
hyperbolic metric
on
\mathbb{B}
assigns hyperbolic distance

\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\left(x,o\right)=2\,\mathrm{artanh}\left\lvert x\right\rvert,

(2.1)

where
o
is the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\lvert\cdot\rvert
is the Euclidean norm. The (hyperbolic-)isometries of
\mathbb{B}
are then the Möbius transformations of
\mathbb{B}
, and these can be used to extend this distance to any pair of points in
\mathbb{B}
. For any Lebesgue measurable subset
E\subset\mathbb{B}
, the
hyperbolic measure
on
\mathbb{B}
assigns mass

\mu\left(E\right):=\int_{E}\frac{4}{\left(1-\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x.

(2.2)

In particular, this measure is preserved by the (hyperbolic-)isometries of
\mathbb{B}
.

Also note that for a
\mu
-integrable function
f\colon{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}
for which there exists
g\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f(x)=g\left(\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\left(x,o\right)\right)
,

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}f(x)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)=\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}\int^{\infty}_{0}g(r)\left(\sinh r\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}r,

(2.3)

where
\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}:=\nicefrac{{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}}{{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}}
equals the
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
-Lebesgue measure of the Euclidean unit radius sphere (i.e.
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Random connection model.

Given
\lambda\geq 0
let the vertex set of the RCM,
\eta
, be distributed as a Poisson point process on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
with intensity measure
\lambda\mu
, where
\mu
is the hyperbolic measure on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. Given a measurable
adjacency function

\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\left[0,1\right]
, for each pair of vertices in
\eta
(say,
\left\{x,y\right\}
), assign an edge independently of everything else with probability

\varphi\left(\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\left(x,y\right)\right).

(2.4)

We will sometimes write
\varphi\left(x,y\right):=\varphi\left(\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\left(x,y\right)\right)
. The overloading of notation should cause minimal confusion. Note that since hyperbolic translations generate hyperbolic rotations (see for example
[
21
, Exercise 29.13]
), this is no great restriction on the family of adjacency functions we can consider if we want some sort of transitivity in the model. If
x
and
y
share an edge, we say
x
and
y
are
adjacent
, and write
x\sim y
. The configuration of the vertex set and the edge set is then notated with
\xi
. We will also require Palm-type versions of
\eta
and
\xi
. Given a finite set
\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\right\}\subset{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, we can define the augmented vertex set
\eta^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}}=\eta\cup\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\right\}
and define the augmented configuration
\xi^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}}
by using the augmented vertex set and including edges between any two vertices in
\eta^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}}
independently with probability given by
\varphi
. The full details of this construction can be found in
[
11
]
.

\varphi_{L}
be an adjacency function. In the examples we consider in Theorems

, and in Assumption

, we shall come to view models with larger

{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,L}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,L}

\xi

we can consider.

Given two distinct vertices
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, we say
x
is connected to
y
in
\xi^{x,y}
(written
x\longleftrightarrow y\textrm{ in }\xi^{x,y}
) if
x=y
or there exists a finite sequence of edges in
\xi^{x,y}
leading from
x
to
y
. This leads to defining the useful
two-point
function:

\tau_{\lambda,L}\left(x,y\right):=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,L}\left(x\longleftrightarrow y\textrm{ in }\xi^{x,y}\right).

(2.5)

Critical Intensity.

Our main question of these hyperbolic random connection models regards the existence of an infinite connected component (a cluster) in the resulting random graph. Let

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\lambda\geq 0\colon\text{the RCM on }{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\text{ with intensity }\lambda\text{ has an infinite cluster a.s}\right\},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{u}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\lambda\geq 0\colon\text{the RCM on }{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\text{ with intensity }\lambda\text{ has a unique infinite cluster a.s}\right\}.

(2.7)

For RCMs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
[
22
]
proves that the analogous quantities are in fact equal, and
[
3
]
proves that this equality survives even if the vertices are randomly and independently assigned ‘marks’ that can change the adjacency probabilities (with some conditions). However,
[
26
]
proves that for the Boolean disc RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
(see Definition
2.1
),

0<\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}<\lambda_{u}<\infty.

(2.8)

In fact
[
26
]
proves that if the radius of the Boolean discs is sufficiently large (depending on the dimension
d
),
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}<\lambda_{u}
for the Boolean disc RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
for any
d\geq 2
. This latter result is generalised in
[
7
]
to show that
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}<\lambda_{u}
for many ‘stretched-out’ RCMs on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, even when marks are added to the vertices.

When we have a family of RCMs determined by the family of adjacency functions
\left\{\varphi_{L}\right\}_{L>0}
, we will naturally have
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)
. In this paper we aim to describe the asymptotics of
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)
as
L\to\infty
.

2.2
Results

Before introducing the most general form of our result, we present two concrete examples of it in action: the heat kernel RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
, and the Boolean disc RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
(for any
d\geq 2
).

Definition 2.1

(Boolean disc RCM)
.

The Boolean disc RCM is defined by having the adjacency function

\varphi_{L}(r)=\mathds{1}\left\{r<L\right\}.

(2.9)

By
\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}
we denote the
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
-Lebesgue measure of the Euclidean unit radius sphere (i.e.
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and note we will let
B_{L}\left(o\right)
denote the hyperbolic radius
L
ball centred on
o\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. Therefore
\mu\left(B_{L}\left(o\right)\right)
denotes the hyperbolic measure of the hyperbolic radius
L
ball.

Theorem 2.2
.

For the Boolean disc RCM, as
L\to\infty

\mu\left(B_{L}(o)\right)\lambda_{c}(L)=1+\frac{\mathfrak{S}_{d-2}}{\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}}\frac{2^{d+2}}{d-1}\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(d-1\right)L}+o\left(\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(d-1\right)L}\right).

(2.10)

Therefore

\lambda_{c}(L)=\frac{\left(d-1\right)2^{d-1}}{\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}}\text{e}^{-\left(d-1\right)L}\left(1+\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right)\begin{cases}\frac{16}{\pi}\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}L}&\colon d=2\\
8\text{e}^{-L}&\colon d=3\\
\frac{128}{3\pi}\text{e}^{-\frac{3}{2}L}&\colon d=4\\
32\text{e}^{-2L}&\colon d=5\\
\frac{\left(d-1\right)^{2}}{d-3}\text{e}^{-2L}&\colon d\geq 6\end{cases}\right).

(2.11)

The order of the error term in (
2.11
) becomes fixed for
d\geq 5
because the correction term for
\mu\left(B_{L}(o)\right)
becomes relevant for
d=5
and dominant for
d\geq 6
. There is no indication that this is related to
d=6
being the upper critical dimension for Euclidean percolation models.

Definition 2.3

(Heat Kernel RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
)
.

Note that the heat kernel
K_{3}\colon\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times{\mathbb{H}^{3}}\times{\mathbb{H}^{3}}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is given by

K_{3}\left(t,x,y\right)=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi t\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{3}}}\left(x,y\right)}{\sinh\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{3}}}\left(x,y\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{2}-\frac{\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{3}}}\left(x,y\right)^{2}}{2t}\right),

(2.12)

see
[
4
]
. Note (or see in the proof of Lemma
8.6
) that
\int_{\mathbb{H}^{3}}K_{3}\left(t,x,y\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)=1
for all
t>0
and
y\in{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
. Now let
0<\mathcal{A}_{L}\leq\left(2\pi L\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{\frac{1}{2}L}
for all
L>0
. The heat kernel RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
is defined by having the adjacency function

\varphi_{L}\left(r\right)=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{L}}{\left(2\pi L\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{r}{\sinh r}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}L-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}\right)

(2.13)

for all
L>0
. One can think of this as a hyperbolic analog of the RCM on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a Gaussian adjacency function with variance parameter
L
and amplitude
\mathcal{A}_{L}
. Like the Gaussian function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, this heat kernel satisfies the semi-group property, which will make it convenient to evaluate convolutions. The sole purpose of having
\mathcal{A}_{L}\leq\left(2\pi L\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{\frac{1}{2}L}
is to ensure
\varphi_{L}\left(x,y\right)\in\left[0,1\right]
for all
L>0
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
. We have restricted to
d=3
so that the heat kernel takes its most simple and explicit form.

Theorem 2.4
.

Consider the heat kernel RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
. As
L\to\infty
,

\mathcal{A}_{L}\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)=1+\frac{\mathcal{A}_{L}}{\left(6\pi\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{-\frac{3}{2}L}+\frac{3}{2}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{L}}{\left(8\pi\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{-2L}+\mathcal{O}\left(\mathcal{A}_{L}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{-\frac{5}{2}L}+\mathcal{A}_{L}^{2}L^{-\frac{9}{2}}\text{e}^{-\frac{5}{2}L}\right).

(2.14)

In particular, if
\varphi_{L}(o,o)=1
for all
L>0
(i.e.
\mathcal{A}_{L}=\left(2\pi L\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{\frac{1}{2}L}
) then as
L\to\infty

\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}L}\left(1+\frac{1}{3\sqrt{3}}\text{e}^{-L}+\frac{3}{16}\text{e}^{-\frac{3}{2}L}+\mathcal{O}\left(L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{-\frac{3}{2}L}\right)\right).

(2.15)

Note that which of the two last error terms in (
2.14
) is dominant depends upon the order of the amplitude term
\mathcal{A}_{L}
:
\mathcal{A}_{L}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{-\frac{5}{2}L}
is dominant if
\mathcal{A}_{L}\ll L^{3}
, while
\mathcal{A}_{L}^{2}L^{-\frac{9}{2}}\text{e}^{-\frac{5}{2}L}
is dominant if
\mathcal{A}_{L}\gg L^{3}
.

While the above two theorems are for specific models, the following proposition provides a result for a wide class of RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. We only require the following assumption.

Assumption A
.

We require that the family of adjacency functions
\left\{\varphi_{L}\right\}_{L>0}
satisfies:

(
A
.1)

for all
L>0
,

0<\int^{\infty}_{0}\varphi_{L}\left(r\right)\exp\left(\left(d-1\right)r\right)\mathrm{d}r<\infty,

(2.16)

(
A
.2)

for all
R>0
,

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\int^{R}_{0}\varphi_{L}\left(r\right)\left(\sinh r\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}r}{\int^{\infty}_{0}\varphi_{L}\left(r\right)\left(\sinh r\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}r}=0.

(2.17)

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose
\left\{\varphi_{L}\right\}_{L>0}
satisfies Assumption
A
. Then as
L\to\infty
,

\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)=1+o\left(1\right).

(2.18)

Remark 2.6
.

Mecke’s formula (see Section
3
) implies that
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)
is equal to the expected degree of a typical vertex in the RCM. Similarly, the left hand sides of (
2.10
) and (
2.14
) are equal to the expected degree of a typical vertex in their respective RCMs. We can therefore view Theorems
2.2
and
2.4
and Proposition
2.5
as results on the expected degree of a typical vertex in each RCM approaching
1
as
L\to\infty
.

Remark 2.7
.

Here are a few remarks on Assumption
A
.

i)

Observe that Assumption
(.1)
with Mecke’s formula implies that
0<\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,L}\left[\deg{o}\right]<\infty
for all
\lambda>0
and
L>L_{0}
. If instead we had
\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)=\infty
for some
L
, then
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,L}\left[\deg{o}\right]=\infty
for all
\lambda>0
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)=0
. On the other hand, if
\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)=0
, then there are almost surely no edges at all and
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)=\infty
. This assumption therefore allows estimating
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
to be an interesting question.

ii)

In the context of Assumption
A
and Proposition
2.5

L
is just an index for a family of adjacency functions, and therefore Assumption
(.1)
can be weakened to only hold for
L>L_{0}
for some
L_{0}
. There are some places in the argument where having
L
“sufficiently large” is important for an independent reason, and having this assumption hold for all
L>0
makes it easier to identify these places.

iii)

If one specifically considers adjacency functions of the form
\varphi_{L}\left(r\right)=\varphi\left(\frac{1}{L}r\right)
for some fixed reference adjacency function
\varphi
, then
[
7
, Lemma A.3]
proves that Assumption
(.2)
is satisfied as long as
\int^{\infty}_{0}\varphi(r)\mathrm{d}r>0
. Assumption
(.1)
is then also satisfied as long as
\varphi(r)
is not
0
almost everywhere, and decays faster than exponentially as
r\to\infty
. Increasing the parameter
L
in this case corresponds to increasing the typical length scale of edges of the RCM in
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. Therefore (up to an overall scaling of the intensity
\lambda
) increasing
L
corresponds to increasing the (negative) curvature of the
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
space. One may then interpret the
L\to\infty
regime in this case as a ‘high curvature’ regime.

Proposition
2.5
is actually a simplification of Theorem
7.5
later in this paper. While that result also requires Assumption
A
, it provides more detail to the error term in (
2.18
). In particular, a bound on the

term is provided that depends explicitly on the adjacency function
\varphi_{L}
. The extra precision of Theorem

is required to derive the asymptotics in Theorems

and
2.4
.

2.3
Background and Discussion

In this paper we give ourselves some flexibility in only asking for an asymptotic expression for the critical threshold in some perturbative regime, rather than asking for the exact value. This approach has been taken many times before. If we let
G_{L}
be the graph on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(with
d\geq 2
) with edge set connecting vertices
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq L
, then
[
24
]
proves that the critical probability satisfies

p_{\mathrm{c}}(L)\deg{G_{L}}=1+o\left(1\right)

(2.19)

as
L\to\infty
. The quantity
p_{\mathrm{c}}\deg{G_{L}}
can also be interpreted as the expected degree of the percolation sub-graph at criticality – naturally drawing comparison to (
2.18
). In
[
25
]
, the behaviour (
2.19
) is proven for such spread-out versions of BBP on transitive graphs with superlinear polynomial growth.

The order of the error in (
2.19
) has been refined for a number of cases.
[
8
]
and
[
15
]
compare BBP to the ‘SIR epidemic’ model to find matching lower and upper bounds for the error for spread-out BBP on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d=2,3
, while
[
16
]
uses a similar approach to find a lower bound for
d=4,5,6
.

Such more precise estimates on the expected degree at criticality have also been found for spread-out BBP on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d>6
in
[
13
]
– see (
1.2
) above. This work is one of many that have used lace expansion techniques to arrive at estimates for
p_{\mathrm{c}}
. For example,
[
14
]
uses the lace expansion to get asymptotics for
p_{\mathrm{c}}
for BBP on nearest-neighbour
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and the
d
-dimensional hypercube as
d\to\infty
. In
[
12
]
asymptotics for the critical probability of site percolation on nearest-neighbour
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as
d\to\infty
are similarly proven.

For RCMs with background space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
[
6
]
uses lace expansion results from
[
11
]
to derive asymptotics for the critical intensity
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
as
d\to\infty
. In particular, if
\varphi=\varphi_{d}
is given by a Gaussian function with total mass
1
and variance
\sigma^{2}(d)
such that
\liminf_{d\to\infty}\varphi(o,o)^{\frac{1}{2}}>0
, then

\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(d)=1+\left(6\pi\sigma^{2}\right)^{-\frac{d}{2}}+\frac{3}{2}\left(8\pi\sigma^{2}\right)^{-\frac{d}{2}}+2\left(10\pi\sigma^{2}\right)^{-\frac{d}{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(12\pi\sigma^{2}\right)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\right)

(2.20)

as
d\to\infty
.

Before we compare the result in this paper to results regarding RCMs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we should highlight a difference between
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-RCMs and
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
for
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
-RCMs. Recall that
\tau_{\lambda,L}(x,y)
is the two-point function that returns the probability that
x
is connected to
y
in the RCM. We can use this two-point function to define a two-point convolution operator
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}
(for details, see Section
3
). The operator norm of this operator can depend upon the spaces it is defined on: for
p\in\left[1,\infty\right]
,
\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{p\to p}
denotes the operator norm of
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\colon L^{p}\to L^{p}
. We can then define

\displaystyle\lambda_{2\to 2}(L)

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\lambda\geq 0\colon\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}=\infty\right\},

(2.21)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1\to 1}(L)

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\lambda\geq 0\colon\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=\infty\right\}.

(2.22)

For
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-RCMs one can use the Fourier transform to show
\lambda_{2\to 2}=\lambda_{1\to 1}
. On the other hand,
[
7
]
shows that for
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
-RCMs satisfying Assumption
(.2)
with sufficiently large
L
, we have
\lambda_{2\to 2}(L)>\lambda_{1\to 1}(L)
. For
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-RCMs,
[
23
]
proved that
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}=\lambda_{1\to 1}
while
[
7
]
proved this same equality for
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
-RCMs with sufficiently large
L
.

While the central result and high-level proof outline of this paper strongly resembles the central result of
[
6
]
, it is notable the inequality
\lambda_{2\to 2}>\lambda_{1\to 1}
means that the difficulties are reversed. In the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-RCM case, the derivation of the lace expansion was challenging because a sophisticated bootstrap argument was required to extend a property from
\lambda<\lambda_{2\to 2}
to
\lambda\leq\lambda_{2\to 2}
. Once the uniform convergence to an Ornstein-Zernike equation was established, it was relatively clear (if involved) to expand
\lambda_{2\to 2}
because the Fourier transform diagonalises the equation. On the other hand, deriving the lace expansion Ornstein-Zernike equation for RCMs on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
is simple for
\lambda\leq\lambda_{1\to 1}
, because the triangle condition is already established in
[
7
]
. However because we want an expansion for
\lambda_{1\to 1}
– and not
\lambda_{2\to 2}
– the spherical transform does not give the shortcut that the Fourier transform does in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

It should, nevertheless, be possible to derive an expansion for
\lambda_{2\to 2}
in this
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
case. Like for the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
case, the main challenge is the derivation of the Ornstein-Zernike equation on the whole domain
\lambda\leq\lambda_{2\to 2}
. Once this is achieved, the spherical transform can then be used to extract
\lambda_{2\to 2}
. While the value
\lambda_{2\to 2}
currently has less probabilistic interest than the
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
critical intensity,
[
7
]
shows that it provides a lower bound for the uniqueness threshold
\lambda_{\mathrm{u}}
.

Observe that the corrections to the leading term for both the heat kernel RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
and the Boolean disc RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
are exponentially small in
L
, while the corrections to the leading term for spread-out BBP on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d>6
(see
[
13
]
or (
1.2
)) follow a power law. This exponential decay is not a consequence of Theorem
2.5
, because one can always ‘slow down’
L
and Assumption
A
will still hold. There would need to be a more concrete map between RCMs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to RCMs on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
for a theorem along the lines of “corrections to the leading term for RCMs on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
are smaller than corrections to the leading term for RCMs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
” to be made. The closest we have to that here is comparing (
1.2
) to Theorem
2.2
, which suggests there may be something to explore here.

Throughout this paper we have fixed the curvature of
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
to be
-1
. It would be an interesting avenue of investigation to see how the approximations of
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
behave as the curvature of
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
approaches
0
. Does the approximation approach something meaningful for
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
of an RCM on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
? For
2\leq d\leq 6
, there is good reason to believe the answer is no. This is because the lace expansion for RCMs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(fundamentally the same as the lace expansion on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
) should not converge for
d\leq 6
. It may be that the hidden constants in
o\left(1\right)
in Proposition
2.5
grow and eventually diverge as the curvature vanishes.

Preliminaries

Mecke’s Formula.

Mecke’s formula gives us a way to formulate some expectations of integrals (or sums) as integrals of expectations. Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a measurable function
f=f\left(\xi,x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\right)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,L}\left[\sum_{\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\right)\in\eta^{(m)}}f\left(\xi,x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\right)\right]\\
=\lambda^{m}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{m}}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,L}\left[f\left(\xi^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\right)\right]\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x_{1}\right)\ldots\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x_{m}\right),

(3.1)

where
\eta^{(m)}:=\left\{\left(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\right)\in\eta^{m}\colon x_{i}\neq x_{j}\text{ for }i\neq j\right\}
. For details, see
[
20
, §4]
.

Notable examples of the use of Mecke’s formula are to get expressions for the expected degree and expected cluster size of a RCM. Let
\deg{o}:=\#\left\{y\in\eta\colon y\sim o\textrm{ in }\xi^{o}\right\}
and
\mathscr{C}\left(o\right):=\left\{o\right\}\cup\left\{y\in\eta\colon y\longleftrightarrow o\textrm{ in }\xi^{o}\right\}
. Then Mecke’s formula implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,L}\left[\deg{o}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,L}\left[\sum_{x\in\eta}\mathds{1}\left\{x\sim o\textrm{ in }\xi^{o}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,L}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o\right)\right]=1+\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,L}\left[\sum_{x\in\eta}\mathds{1}\left\{x\longleftrightarrow o\textrm{ in }\xi^{o}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=1+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\tau_{\lambda,L}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right).

(3.3)

Operators.

Let
G
denote the convolution operator associated with the function
g\colon{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\to\mathbb{C}
. That is, first define

\mathcal{D}\left(G\right):=\left\{f\colon{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\to\mathbb{C}\;|\;\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left\lvert g\left(x,y\right)\right\rvert\left\lvert f(y)\right\rvert\mu\left(\mathrm{d}y\right)<\infty\text{ for }\mu\text{-almost every }x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right\}.

(3.4)

Then
G\colon\mathcal{D}\left(G\right)\to\mathbb{C}^{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}
is the linear operator defined by

\left(Gf\right)\left(x\right):=\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}g\left(x,y\right)f(y)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}y\right).

(3.5)

Then given
p\in\left[1,\infty\right]
, define the operator norm

\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{p\to p}:=\begin{cases}\sup_{f\colon\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lVert Gf\right\rVert_{p}&\colon L^{p}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)\subset\mathcal{D}\left(G\right),\\
\infty&\colon\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(3.6)

and the spectral radius

\rho_{p\to p}\left(G\right):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert G^{n}\right\rVert_{p\to p}^{\frac{1}{n}}.

(3.7)

Observe that this limit exists (in
\left[0,\infty\right]
) because the operator norm is sub-multiplicative.

Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
allow us to get explicit formula for the
L^{1}\to L^{1}
operator norm and spectral radius of certain convolution operators. They are proven in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The convolution operator
G
satisfies

\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=\esssup_{y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\left\lvert g(x,y)\right\rvert\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right).

(3.8)

If
g\left(x,y\right)=g\left(y,x\right)
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, then for all
p\in\left[1,\infty\right]

\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{p\to p}=\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{\frac{p}{p-1}\to\frac{p}{p-1}},

(3.9)

and
p\mapsto\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{p\to p}
is a decreasing function of
p
on
\left[1,2\right]
and increasing on
\left[2,\infty\right]
.

Definition 3.2
.

A function
f\colon{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}
is
isometry invariant
if, for any isometry
\iota
of
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
,

f\left(\iota(x),\iota(y)\right)=f(x,y)

(3.10)

for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. Observe that if
f
is isometry invariant, then
f(x,y)=f\left(\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\left(x,y\right)\right)
only:
f
is constant on the hyperbolic sphere
\left\{w\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\colon\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\left(w,x\right)=\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\left(y,x\right)\right\}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
g(x,y)
is isometry invariant and
g(x,y)\geq 0
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, then

\rho_{1\to 1}\left(G\right)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}g\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)=\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{1\to 1}.

(3.11)

In Section
6
we will want to bound the perturbation to the spectral radius when we perturb the operator. Given
p\in\left[1,\infty\right]
, the spectrum of the bounded operator
A\colon L^{p}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)\to L^{p}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)
is defined to be

\sigma_{p\to p}\left(A\right):=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\text{ s.t. }\left(A-z\mathds{1}\right)\colon L^{p}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)\to L^{p}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)\text{ is not invertible}\right\}.

(3.12)

By Gelfand’s formula,

\rho_{p\to p}(A)=\sup\left\{\left\lvert z\right\rvert\colon z\in\sigma_{p\to p}(A)\right\}.

(3.13)

The following lemma is an instance of a more general result, but we will only require this
L^{1}\to L^{1}
version in this paper. In this lemma “
\mathrm{dist}
” refers to the Hausdorff distance with respect to the Euclidean distance on

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A,B\colon L^{1}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)\to L^{1}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)

\mathrm{dist}\left(\sigma_{1\to 1}\left(A+B\right),\sigma_{1\to 1}\left(A\right)\right)\leq\rho_{1\to 1}\left(B\right)\leq\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{1\to 1}.

(3.14)

In particular,

\rho_{1\to 1}\left(A+B\right)\leq\rho_{1\to 1}\left(A\right)+\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{1\to 1}.

(3.15)

Proof.

This follows from the standard result of

.

Recall that for

(3.17)

We can define the operators

by

(3.20)

Remark 3.8
.

The results in
[
7
]
are stated under a slightly different set of assumptions. In that reference the family of adjacency functions
\left\{\varphi_{L}\right\}_{L>0}
is assumed to be of a specific form. It is assumed that there exists a reference function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\left[0,1\right]
and a family of bijective non-decreasing scaling functions
\sigma_{L}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
\lim_{L\to\infty}\sigma_{L}(r)=\infty
for all
r>0
and

\varphi_{L}\left(r\right)=\varphi\left(\sigma^{-1}_{L}(r)\right).

(3.26)

Two choices of scaling functions highlighted in
[
7
]
are the
length-linear
scaling (where
\sigma_{L}(r)=Lr
) and the
volume-linear
scaling (that satisfies
\mu\left(B_{\sigma_{L}(r)}(o)\right)=L\mu\left(B_{r}(o)\right)
). This volume-linear scaling becomes relevant for
marked
RCMs on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. These are models where the vertices are independently given some mark, and the adjacency function also depends on the two marks of the vertices in question. The volume-linear scaling function structure is important for the argument when there are infinitely many possible marks – it ensures that the most relevant marks stay the most relevant as
L\to\infty
.

In our application of these results, the scaling function structure is not required because there are no marks (or equivalently, only one mark). The limit (
3.21
) follows from writing
\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}
in terms of the spherical transform and using Assumption
(.2)
. Then (
3.22
) follows by observing that

\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\left(o,o\right)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)^{2}\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)=\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}.

(3.27)

The bounds (
3.23
) and (
3.24
) follow by coupling a binary branching process with a subset of the hyperbolic RCM graph. This argument requires that a sufficient fraction of the total mass of the adjacency function lies beyond a given radius (which follows from Assumption
(.2)
). By using Mecke’s formula, the probability that there exists a neighbour of
o
in a given region can be bounded below by
1-\exp\left(-c\lambda\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}\right)
for some constant
c>0
, and therefore the binary branching process survives with high probability if
\lambda\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}
is sufficiently high (independently of
L
). The coupling then implies that the RCM has an infinite connected component if
\lambda\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}
is sufficiently high.

Mecke’s formula with a first-step decomposition implies that

\tau_{\lambda,L}\leq\varphi_{L}+\lambda\varphi_{L}\star\tau_{\lambda,L}

(3.28)

pointwise (see
[
11
, Observation 4.3]
). Applying this twice to
\tau_{\lambda,L}^{\star 3}
and converting this into operators implies that for any

such that
f_{1},f_{2}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}f_{1}\left(x\right)\tau_{\lambda,L}^{\star 3}\left(x,y\right)f_{2}\left(y\right)\mu\left(y\right)\mu\left(x\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\left(\lambda\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}+2\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}^{2}+\lambda^{3}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}^{3}\right)\lambda\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}f_{1}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}f_{2}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\left(\lambda\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}+2\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}^{2}+\lambda^{3}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}^{3}\right)\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)^{2}\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{1}\left\lVert f_{2}\right\rVert_{1}.

(3.29)

This last inequality followed from a supremum bound. By suitably choosing
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, one can show that

\triangle_{\lambda,L}\leq\left(\lambda\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}+2\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}^{2}+\lambda^{3}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}^{3}\right)\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)^{2}\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right).

(3.30)

with the Green’s function of the branching process with jump kernel

and therefore

,
\varPhi_{L}
, and some other operator

, but the proof is made significantly simpler by the fact that we aren’t pushing for a
\lambda
-optimal version – we only need it for
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
. The following lemma hints at why we want to formulate these operators as
L^{1}\to L^{1}
operators.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose Assumption
A
holds. Then for sufficiently large
L
,

\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)=\inf\left\{\lambda\geq 0\colon\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=\infty\right\}.

(4.1)

Proof.

In
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
it is proven under (an unimportant modification of) Assumption
A
that for sufficiently large
L
,

\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)=\inf\left\{\lambda\geq 0\colon\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\tau_{\lambda,L}(x,o)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)=\infty\right\}.

(4.2)

Lemma
3.3
then immediately concludes the proof.
∎

We now prepare the definitions needed for the lace expansion.

Definition 4.2
.

Given
x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
and locally finite
A\subset{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, define

\bar{\varphi}(A,x):=\prod_{y\in A}(1-\varphi(x,y)).

(4.3)

Then define
\eta_{\langle A\rangle}
as an
A
-thinning of
\eta
by keeping a point
w\in\eta
as a point of
\eta_{\langle A\rangle}
with probability
\bar{\varphi}(A,w)
independently of all other points of
\eta
. That is, we keep a vertex if it would not form an edge with any point in
A
. We similarly define
\eta_{\langle A\rangle}^{x}
as an
A
-thinning of
\eta^{x}
.

Given a vertex set, say
\eta^{\prime}
, we denote by
\xi\left[\eta^{\prime}\right]
the RCM graph restricted to the vertices in
\eta^{\prime}
. Using thinning and this notation, we can define the event

\left\{x\xleftrightarrow{\,\,A\,\,}y\textrm{ in }\xi\right\}=\left\{x\longleftrightarrow y\textrm{ in }\xi\right\}\cap\left\{x\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}y\textrm{ in }\xi[\eta_{\langle A\rangle}\cup\{x\}]\right\}.

(4.4)

That is,
\{x\xleftrightarrow{\,\,A\,\,}y\textrm{ in }\xi\}
is the event that the vertex
x
is connected to the vertex
y
in
\xi
, but that this connection is broken by an
A
-thinning of
\eta\setminus\{x\}
.

We can also use thinnings to define
pivotal
points. Given
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
and a configuration
\xi
, we say
u\in\textsf{Piv}(y,x,\xi)\subset{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
if
\left\{y\longleftrightarrow x\textrm{ in }\xi^{x,y}\right\}\cap\left\{y\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}x\textrm{ in }\xi\left[\eta\setminus\left\{u\right\}\right]\right\}
occurs. That is, every path on
\xi^{x,y}
connecting
x
and
y
uses the vertex
u
.

Now define

E(x,y;A,\xi):=\left\{x\xleftrightarrow{\,\,A\,\,}y\textrm{ in }\xi\right\}\cap\left\{\nexists w\in\textsf{Piv}(x,y;\xi)\colon x\xleftrightarrow{\,\,A\,\,}w\textrm{ in }\xi\right\},

(4.5)

for a locally finite set
A\subset{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, and
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. That is, the vertex
x
is connected to the vertex
y
in
\xi
, but that this connection is broken
after
the last pivotal point from
x
to
y
and not before.

We also say two vertices
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
are
two-connected
in
\xi^{x,y}
if
x
and
y
are adjacent or if there are two paths in
\xi^{x,y}
that only share
x
and
y
as common vertices. We denote this event as
\left\{x\Longleftrightarrow y\textrm{ in }\xi^{x,y}\right\}
.

Definition 4.3
.

For
\lambda\geq 0
and
L>0
let
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of independent
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,L}
-distributed RCM configurations. Then for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, we define ‘lace expansion coefficient’ functions

\displaystyle\pi_{\lambda,L}^{(0)}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,L}\left(y\Longleftrightarrow x\textrm{ in }\xi_{0}^{y,x}\right)-\varphi_{L}(x,y),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\pi_{\lambda,L}^{(n)}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\lambda^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\ldots\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,L}\left(\left\{y\Longleftrightarrow u_{0}\textrm{ in }\xi^{y,u_{0}}_{0}\right\}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\left.\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}E\left(u_{i-1},u_{i};\mathscr{C}_{i-1},\xi^{u_{i-1},u_{i}}_{i}\right)\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}u_{0}\right)\ldots\mu\left(\mathrm{d}u_{n-1}\right),

(4.7)

where
u_{n}=x
,
u_{-1}=y
, and
\mathscr{C}_{i}=\mathscr{C}(u_{i-1},\xi^{u_{i-1}}_{i})
is the cluster of
u_{i-1}
in
\xi^{u_{i-1}}_{i}
. Further define the remainder functions

\displaystyle r_{\lambda,0,L}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=-\lambda\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,L}\left(\left\{y\Longleftrightarrow u_{0}\textrm{ in }\xi^{y,u_{0}}_{0}\right\}\cap\left\{u_{0}\xleftrightarrow{\,\,\mathscr{C}_{0}\,\,}x\textrm{ in }\xi^{u_{0},x}_{1}\right\}\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}u_{0}\right),

(4.8)

\displaystyle r_{\lambda,n,L}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=(-\lambda)^{n+1}\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\ldots\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,L}\left(\left\{y\Longleftrightarrow u_{0}\textrm{ in }\xi^{y,u_{0}}_{0}\right\}\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}E\left(u_{i-1},u_{i};\mathscr{C}_{i-1},\xi^{u_{i-1},u_{i}}_{i}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\left.\cap\left\{u_{n}\xleftrightarrow{\,\,\mathscr{C}_{n}\,\,}x\textrm{ in }\xi^{u_{n},x}_{n+1}\right\}\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}u_{0}\right)\ldots\mu\left(\mathrm{d}u_{n}\right).

(4.9)

Additionally, define
\pi_{\lambda,n,L}
as the alternating partial sum

\pi_{\lambda,n,L}(x,y):=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}\pi_{\lambda,L}^{(m)}(x,y).

(4.10)

Given
\lambda\geq 0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
L>0
, define

\mathcal{D}\left(\varPi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}\right):=\left\{f\colon{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\to\mathbb{C}\;|\;\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left\lvert\pi_{\lambda,L}^{(n)}(x,y)\right\rvert\left\lvert f(y)\right\rvert\mu\left(\mathrm{d}y\right)<\infty\right.\\
\left.\text{ for }\mu\text{-almost every }x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right\}.

(4.11)

Similarly define
\mathcal{D}\left(R_{\lambda,n,L}\right)
and
\mathcal{D}\left(\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)
using
r_{\lambda,n,L}
and
\pi_{\lambda,n,L}
respectively. Then we can define
\varPi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}\colon\mathcal{D}\left(\varPi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}\right)\to\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
as the convolution operator that acts on
f\in\mathcal{D}\left(\varPi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}\right)
by

\left(\varPi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}f\right)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\pi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}(x,y)f(y)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}y\right)

(4.12)

for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. Similarly we can define the operators
R_{\lambda,n,L}\colon\mathcal{D}\left(R_{\lambda,n,L}\right)\to\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
and
\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\colon\mathcal{D}\left(\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)\to\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
as the convolution operators (and their domains) using
r_{\lambda,n,L}
and
\pi_{\lambda,n,L}
respectively. Observe that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\mathcal{D}\left(\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)=\bigcap_{0\leq m\leq n}\mathcal{D}\left(\varPi^{(m)}_{\lambda,L}\right).

(4.13)

Therefore we have

\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}\varPi^{(m)}_{\lambda,L}.

(4.14)

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose
\left\{\varphi_{L}\right\}_{L>0}
satisfies Assumption
A
. Then there exists
L_{0}
such that for
L\geq L_{0}
the following all hold:

1.

for all
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)

\varPi_{\lambda,L}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}

(4.15)

exists in the
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{1\to 1}
topology,

2.

for all
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)

\lambda\left\lVert\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\beta(L)\right),

(4.16)

3.

for all
\lambda<\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)
the two bounded operators
\varPhi_{L},\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\colon L^{1}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)\to L^{1}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)
satisfy the Ornstein-Zernike equation

\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}=\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}+\lambda\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right).

(4.17)

Before we prove this proposition, we need two lemmas to prepare the ground. Lemma
4.5
establishes a convolution equation that relates the two-point function, the adjacency function, the lace expansion coefficient functions, and the remainder functions.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\lambda<\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)
. Then for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}

\tau_{\lambda,L}\left(x,y\right)=\varphi_{L}\left(x,y\right)+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\left(x,y\right)\\
+\lambda\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\tau_{\lambda,L}\left(x,u\right)\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)\left(u,y\right)\mathrm{d}u+r_{\lambda,n,L}\left(x,y\right),

(4.18)

and

\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}=\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}+\lambda\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)+R_{\lambda,n,L}.

(4.19)

Proof.

This is very nearly exactly
[
11
, Proposition 3.8]
with
[
5
, Proposition 4.6]
for the extension to operators. These were working on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, but replacing this with
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
causes no issue for the parts we require. The condition
\lambda<\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
here ensures that
\pi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}(x,y)
are all finite, so
\pi_{\lambda,n,L}(x,y)
and
\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}
are all well-defined.
∎

This convolution equation would look more manageable if the remainder function wasn’t there. One may hope that the remainder function diminishes as
n\to\infty
, and that we can recover an equation that relates
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}
,
\varPhi_{L}
, and some large
n
limit of
\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}
. To confirm this is indeed the case, in Lemma
4.7
we bound some of these terms with objects defined in Definition
4.6
, and the proof of Proposition
4.4
will largely consist of showing that these objects are sufficiently small that the required
n\to\infty
limits hold.

Definition 4.6
.

For
\lambda\geq 0
, recall

\triangle_{\lambda,L}=\lambda^{2}\esssup_{x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\tau^{\star 3}_{\lambda,L}\left(x,o\right),

(4.20)

and – using the notation of
[
5
]
– define

\displaystyle\triangle^{\circ\circ}_{\lambda,L}

\displaystyle:=\triangle_{\lambda,L}+\lambda\esssup_{x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\tau^{\star 2}_{\lambda,L}\left(x,o\right)+1,

(4.21)

\displaystyle U_{\lambda,L}

\displaystyle:=\triangle_{\lambda,L}\triangle^{\circ\circ}_{\lambda,L}+\left(\triangle_{\lambda,L}^{\circ\circ}\right)^{2}+\lambda\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1},

(4.22)

\displaystyle V_{\lambda,L}

\displaystyle:=\left(\triangle_{\lambda,L}\triangle_{\lambda,L}^{\circ\circ}U_{\lambda,L}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.23)

Lemma 4.7
.

For all
\lambda\geq 0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\lambda\left\lVert R_{\lambda,n,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}\left\lVert\varPi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1},

(4.24)

\displaystyle\lambda\left\lVert\varPi^{(0)}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}\triangle_{\lambda,L},

(4.25)

\displaystyle\lambda\left\lVert\varPi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle\leq 6\times 4^{n-1}U_{\lambda,L}V_{\lambda,L}^{n}.

(4.26)

Proof.

These inequalities are proven in
[
5
]
for the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
versions, and the
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
version is exactly the same. The inequality (
4.24
) follows from Lemma 5.1, (
4.25
) from Lemma 5.2, and (
4.26
) from Lemma 5.8 therein.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

In Lemma
4.5
we required
\lambda<\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
to ensure
\pi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}\left(x,y\right)
were finite and the alternating sums defining
\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\left(x,y\right)
and
\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}
are well-defined. However, the bounds (
4.25
) and (
4.26
) provide us the means to define
\varPi_{\lambda_{c},n,L}
as the alternating sum of bounded
L^{1}\to L^{1}
operators in (
4.14
) (if
\triangle_{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L),L}<\infty
).

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

First observe that for all
\lambda\geq 0
,
L>0
, and
x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}

\tau_{\lambda,L}\left(x,o\right)\leq\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)+\lambda\varphi_{L}\star\tau_{\lambda,L}\left(x,o\right).

(4.27)

This is proven for RCMs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in
[
11
, Observation 4.3]
by using Mecke’s formula and a first-step analysis. The argument for RCMs on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
is exactly the same. Using this inequality twice,

\displaystyle\lambda\esssup_{x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\tau_{\lambda,L}^{\star 2}\left(x,o\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\esssup_{x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\left(x,o\right)+2\lambda^{2}\esssup_{x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\star\tau_{\lambda,L}\left(x,o\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt+\lambda^{3}\esssup_{x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\star\tau_{\lambda,L}^{\star 2}\left(x,o\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}+2\triangle_{\lambda,L}+\lambda\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}\triangle_{\lambda,L},

(4.28)

where in the second inequality we have used supremum bounds and
1\geq\tau_{\lambda,L}\geq\varphi_{L}
. From Theorem
3.7
we know
\triangle_{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L),L}=\mathcal{O}\left(\beta(L)^{2}\right)
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)\left\lVert\varPhi\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)
as
L\to\infty
. Therefore
\triangle^{\circ\circ}_{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L),L}=\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)
,
U_{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L),L}=\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)
, and
V_{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L),L}=\mathcal{O}\left(\beta(L)\right)
as
L\to\infty
.

These bounds with Lemma
4.7
and the monotonicity of
\lambda\mapsto\triangle_{\lambda,L}
imply that there exists a constant
C<\infty
such that for all
n\geq 1
and
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)

\displaystyle\lambda\left\lVert\varPi^{(0)}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle\leq C\triangle_{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L),L},

(4.29)

\displaystyle\lambda\left\lVert\varPi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(16\triangle_{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L),L}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}},

(4.30)

\displaystyle\lambda\left\lVert R_{\lambda,n,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(16\triangle_{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L),L}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\lambda\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}.

(4.31)

Therefore for
L
large enough to get
16\triangle_{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L),L}<1
, the sequence
\left\{\lambda\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence for
\lambda\in\left[0,\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)\right]
. Hence

\varPi_{\lambda,L}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}

(4.32)

exists and is a bounded linear
L^{1}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)\to L^{1}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)
operator for
\lambda\in\left[0,\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)\right]
and
L
sufficiently large. In particular, there exists a
C^{\prime}<\infty
such that
\lambda\left\lVert\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}\leq C^{\prime}\beta(L)
for
\lambda\in\left(0,\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)\right]
and
L
sufficiently large.

For
L
sufficiently large and
\lambda<\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)
, we have
\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}<\infty
(see Lemma
4.1
). Therefore for
L
sufficiently large and
\lambda\in\left[0,\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)\right)
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert R_{\lambda,n,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=0.

(4.33)

Therefore Lemma
4.5
implies that for
\lambda\in\left[0,\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)\right)
and
L
sufficiently large we have

\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}=\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)+\lambda\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)

(4.34)

as required.
∎

We will require one more property of the limiting
\varPi_{\lambda,L}
operator.

Lemma 4.9
.

Suppose
\left\{\varphi_{L}\right\}_{L>0}
satisfies Assumption
A
. For
L
sufficiently large, the function
\lambda\mapsto\varPi_{\lambda,L}
is continuous with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{1\to 1}
on
\left[0,\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)\right]
.

Proof Summary.

This proceeds as in
[
11
, Corollary 6.1]
. The core idea is a dominated convergence argument. We aim to show
\lim_{\lambda\to\lambda^{*}}\left\lVert\varPi_{\lambda,L}^{(n)}-\varPi_{\lambda^{*},L}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\lambda^{*}\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)
and
L
sufficiently large. As in the proof of Lemma
4.7
(see
[
5
]
), we can find a function
h^{(n)}_{L}
such that
\pi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}\leq h^{(n)}_{L}
pointwise for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\lambda^{*}\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)
, and
h^{(n)}_{L}
is integrable for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
L
sufficiently large. If we can show pointwise convergence for
\pi_{\lambda,L}^{(n)}
, then this integrability shows that the required limit holds. The pointwise convergence holds exactly as explained in
[
11
, Corollary 6.1]
, and uses the fact that connected components are almost surely finite. This fact is proven for
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)
in our context in
[
7
]
.
∎

5
Characterising the Percolation Threshold

In this section we show how the critical threshold
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
can be extracted from (
4.17
), and given implicitly in terms of the operators
\varPhi_{L}
and
\varPi_{\lambda,L}
.

We require a relatively standard percolation result that (via Mecke’s formula) shows that the expected cluster size diverges as the critical threshold is approached from below.

Lemma 5.1
.

For all
L>0
,

\lim_{\lambda\nearrow\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=\infty.

(5.1)

Proof Summary.

This is proven in exactly the same way as is was in the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
, using standard ideas that go back at least as far as
[
1
]
. The argument considers
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}^{(n)}
, a spatially truncated version of
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}
. By using the Margulis-Russo formula to control the derivative, it can be proven that the sequence (when varying
n
) of functions

\lambda\mapsto\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{-1}

(5.2)

is an equicontinuous sequence of functions. Since
\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{-1}\to\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{-1}
monotonically as
n\to\infty
, this proves that

\lambda\mapsto\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{-1}

(5.3)

is continuous for all
\lambda\geq 0
.
∎

We are now prepared to characterise
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)
in terms of
\varPhi_{L}
and
\varPi_{\lambda,L}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
and suppose
\left\{\varphi_{L}\right\}_{L>0}
satisfies Assumption
A
. Then there exists
L_{0}
such that for
L\geq L_{0}
,

\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)\begin{cases}<1&\text{if }\lambda<\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L),\\
=1&\text{if }\lambda=\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L).\end{cases}

(5.4)

Proof.

First define

\tilde{\lambda}:=\inf\left\{\lambda\in\left(0,\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\right]\colon\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)\geq 1\right\}.

(5.5)

First note that by Lemma
4.9
,
\lambda\mapsto\lambda\varPhi_{L}+\lambda\varPi_{\lambda,L}
is continuous with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{1\to 1}
for all
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
. Since
\varphi_{L}
and
\pi_{\lambda,n,L}
are all isometry invariant, lemmas
3.4
and
3.5
give that the map

\lambda\mapsto\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)

(5.6)

is continuous for
L>L_{0}
and
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
. Also note that this map takes
0\mapsto 0
(since
\varPhi_{L}
and
\varPi_{0,L}
are both bounded operators), and therefore
\tilde{\lambda}>0
.

Suppose
\lambda<\tilde{\lambda}
. Then
\mathds{1}-\lambda\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)\colon L^{1}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)\to L^{1}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)
is invertible and

\left(\mathds{1}-\lambda\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)\right)^{-1}=\mathds{1}+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\lambda^{n}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)^{n}.

(5.7)

Therefore Proposition
4.4
implies that if
\lambda<\min\left\{\tilde{\lambda},\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\right\}
and
L\geq L_{0}
then

\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}=\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)\left(\mathds{1}-\lambda\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)\right)^{-1}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\lambda^{n-1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)^{n}.

(5.8)

By Lemma

and
\tau_{\lambda,L}\geq 0
,
\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right)
. The spectral mapping theorem then tells us that for
\lambda<\min\left\{\tilde{\lambda},\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\right\}
and
L\geq L_{0}
,

\lambda\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right)=\frac{\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)}{1-\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)}.

(5.9)

If
\tilde{\lambda}<\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
, then the continuity property on
\left[0,\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\right]
implies
\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)\to 1
as
\lambda\nearrow\tilde{\lambda}
, and with (
5.9
) this implies
\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}\to\infty
as
\lambda\nearrow\tilde{\lambda}
. Therefore Lemma
5.1
produces a contradiction and we have
\tilde{\lambda}\geq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
.

Since
\tilde{\lambda}\geq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
, Lemma
5.1
and (
5.9
) imply
\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)\nearrow 1
as
\lambda\nearrow\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
. Therefore
\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
.

The equality
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}=\tilde{\lambda}
and (
5.9
) imply
\lim_{\lambda\nearrow\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}}\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)=1
, and the result then follows from the continuity of
\lambda\mapsto\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)
for
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
.
∎

for all

. By applying

7
Estimating the Spectral Radius

Definition 7.1
.

Recall that we use the notation that given a real-valued function
f
,
\left(f\right)_{+}
is the positive part and
\left(f\right)_{-}
is the absolute value of the negative part. That is,

\displaystyle\left(f\right)_{+}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lvert f\right\rvert+f\right),

(7.1)

\displaystyle\left(f\right)_{-}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lvert f\right\rvert-f\right).

(7.2)

This means that
f=\left(f\right)_{+}-\left(f\right)_{-}
, and
\left\lvert f\right\rvert=\left(f\right)_{+}+\left(f\right)_{-}
.

Also for
L>0
,
\lambda\geq 0
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we let
\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{+}
and
\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{-}
denote the convolution operators using
\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{+}
and
\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{-}
respectively.

Lemma 7.2
.

For all
L>0
,
\lambda\geq 0
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\lvert\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)-\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)\right\rvert\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{-}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)

(7.3)

Proof.

From the sub-multiplicativity of the operator norm and the triangle inequality,

\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)\leq\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}\leq\left\lVert\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{+}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}+\left\lVert\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{-}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}.

(7.4)

Then from the isometry invariance of
\varphi_{L}
and
\pi_{\lambda,n,L}
,

\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{+}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{-}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)+2\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{-}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right).

(7.5)

Note that by isometry invariance and Lemma
3.5
,
\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{+}
and
\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{-}
commute. Then from Lemmas
3.4
and
3.3
,

\displaystyle\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{+}\right)-\left\lVert\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{-}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{+}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)-\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)_{-}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,n,L}\right)\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right),

(7.6)

as required.
∎

Recall there exists
C<\infty
such that
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\left(L\right)\leq\frac{C}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}}
for

sufficiently large (see Theorem

). Then define

\mathcal{E}(L):=\frac{1}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\frac{C}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}}\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\cdot\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\right.\\
\left.+\frac{C^{2}}{2\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{2}}\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\cdot\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\left(x,o\right)-\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\right)_{+}\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right).

(7.7)

Lemma 7.3
.

Suppose

satisfies Assumption

. Then for

sufficiently large and
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)

(7.8)

Proof.

This result mostly follows from Definition

with Proposition

. By applying these and observing
\left(f+g\right)_{+}\leq\left(f\right)_{+}+\left(g\right)_{+}\leq\left(f\right)_{+}+\left\lvert g\right\rvert
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,1,L}\right)_{-}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\lambda\varphi_{L}-\lambda^{2}\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\cdot\varphi_{L}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{3}\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\cdot\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}-\lambda^{3}\varphi^{\star 3}\cdot\varphi_{L}+\omega^{(0)}_{\lambda,L}-\omega^{(1)}_{\lambda,L}\right)_{-}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\lambda\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\cdot\varphi_{L}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\cdot\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}+\lambda^{2}\varphi^{\star 3}\cdot\varphi_{L}-\varphi_{L}\right)_{+}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 284.52756pt+\left\lVert\omega^{(0)}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1}+\left\lVert\omega^{(1)}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1}.

(7.9)

Now observe that
\lambda^{2}\esssup_{x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi^{\star 3}_{L}\left(x,o\right)\leq\triangle_{\lambda,L}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi_{L}+\pi_{\lambda,1,L}\right)_{-}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\lambda\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\cdot\varphi_{L}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\cdot\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}-\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{L}\right)_{+}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\triangle_{\lambda,L}\varphi_{L}-\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{L}\right)_{+}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\Omega(L)\right).

(7.10)

Since
\triangle_{\lambda,L}
is monotone increasing in
\lambda
and Theorem
3.7
shows
\lim_{L\to\infty}\triangle_{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L),L}=0
,

\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\triangle_{\lambda,L}\varphi_{L}-\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{L}\right)_{+}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)=\lambda\left(\triangle_{\lambda,L}-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{+}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)=0

(7.11)

for
L
sufficiently large and
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)
. Finally using
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)\leq C\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{-1}
(see (
3.23
) in Theorem
3.7
) and replacing each remaining factors of
\lambda
with this upper bound gives the result.
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

Suppose
\left\{\varphi_{L}\right\}_{L>0}
satisfies Assumption
A
. Then there exists
C_{0}<\infty
such that for
L
sufficiently large and
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)
,

\left\lvert\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)-\lambda\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}+\lambda^{2}\varphi^{\star 3}\left(o,o\right)+\frac{3}{2}\lambda^{3}\varphi^{\star 4}\left(o,o\right)\right\rvert\\
\leq C_{0}\left(\Omega(L)+\mathcal{E}(L)+\beta(L)^{N}\right)

(7.12)

for any
N\geq 2
.

Proof.

Begin by writing
\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}=\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,1,L}\right)+\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}\left(-1\right)^{n}\varPi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}
. By isometry invariance and Lemma
3.5
,
\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,1,L}
and
\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}\left(-1\right)^{n}\varPi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}
commute and therefore Proposition
6.8
gives

\left\lvert\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)-\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,1,L}\right)\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}\left(-1\right)^{n}\varPi^{(n)}_{\lambda,L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\Omega(L)+\beta(L)^{N}\right)

(7.13)

for any
N\geq 2
.
Then by lemmas
7.2
and
7.3
we have

\left\lvert\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,1,L}\right)-\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi+\pi_{\lambda,1,L}\right)\left(x,o\right)\mathrm{d}x\right\rvert=\mathcal{O}\left(\mathcal{E}(L)+\Omega(L)\right).

(7.14)

Then Proposition
6.8
gives

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\varphi+\pi_{\lambda,1,L}\right)\left(x,o\right)\mathrm{d}x=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)-\lambda^{2}\varphi^{\star 3}\left(o,o\right)-\frac{3}{2}\lambda^{3}\varphi^{\star 4}\left(o,o\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\Omega(L)\right)

(7.15)

as required.
∎

Now we ready to prove the main theorem of the paper.

Theorem 7.5
.

Suppose
\left\{\varphi_{L}\right\}_{L>0}
satisfies Assumption
A
. Then for all
d\geq 2
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)=1+\frac{\varphi_{L}^{\star 3}(o,o)}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{2}}+\frac{3}{2}\frac{\varphi_{L}^{\star 4}(o,o)}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{3}}\\
+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\frac{\varphi_{L}^{\star 3}(o,o)}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{2}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\varphi_{L}^{\star 4}(o,o)}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{3}}\right)^{2}+\Omega(L)+\mathcal{E}(L)+\beta(L)^{N}\right)

(7.16)

as
L\to\infty
.

Proof.

From Proposition
7.4
we have

\lambda\rho_{1\to 1}\left(\varPhi_{L}+\varPi_{\lambda,L}\right)=\lambda a-\lambda^{2}b-\lambda^{3}c+r,

(7.17)

where
a=\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}
,
b=\varphi^{\star 3}\left(o,o\right)
,
c=\frac{3}{2}\varphi^{\star 4}\left(o,o\right)
, and the remainder term is given by
r=\mathcal{O}\left(\Omega(L)+\mathcal{E}(L)+\beta(L)^{N}\right)
. From Corollary
6.7
and (
3.21
) in Theorem
3.7
, we have
\frac{b}{a^{2}}\ll 1
,
\frac{c}{a^{3}}\ll 1
,
r\ll 1
, and
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)=\mathcal{O}\left(a^{-1}\right)
. Then from Lemma
5.2
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}a

\displaystyle=1+\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}b+\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{3}c-r

\displaystyle=1+\frac{b}{a^{2}}\left(1+\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}b+\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{3}c-r\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{a^{3}}\left(1+\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}b+\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}^{3}c-r\right)^{3}-r

\displaystyle=1+\frac{b}{a^{2}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{b}{a^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{c}{a^{3}}\right)-r\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{a^{3}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{b}{a^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{c}{a^{3}}\right)-r\right)^{3}-r

\displaystyle=1+\frac{b}{a^{2}}+\frac{c}{a^{3}}+\mathcal{O}\left(r+\frac{b^{2}}{a^{4}}+\frac{c^{2}}{a^{6}}\right),

(7.18)

as required.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Corollary
6.7
lets us simplify Theorem
7.5
by bounding many of the terms in (
7.16
). This quickly gives us

\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)=1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}}+\mathcal{E}(L)+\beta(L)^{N}\right).

(7.19)

Now observe that if one omits the
-\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{L}
term from the definition of
\mathcal{E}(L)
, one gets the bound

\mathcal{E}(L)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\varphi_{L}^{\star 3}(o,o)}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{2}}+\frac{\varphi_{L}^{\star 4}(o,o)}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{3}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}}\right),

(7.20)

and Theorem
3.7
implies

\beta(L)=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}}}\right).

(7.21)

Therefore

\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(L)=1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}}+\left(\frac{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\right)=1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}}\right),

(7.22)

if we choose

.

For the

operator norm on the left hand side, we use Lemma

to get

\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right).

(7.23)

Finally, Theorem

states that

\frac{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}}=o\left(1\right)

(7.24)

as

, and the result follows.
∎

8
Calculations for Specific Models

Theorem
7.5
has a number of terms in it, and it is not immediately obvious which terms will be the most relevant for any particular model. Corollary
6.7
gave a partial ordering to the terms, but this is far from complete. Clearly evaluating these terms for specific models reveals the ordering (we do this is Sections
8.3
and
8.2
for the Boolean disc RCM and heat kernel RCMs respectively), but we can also impose some order if we consider the family of models which we call ‘non-negative definite’ RCMs.

8.1
‘Non-Negative Definite’ RCMs

Definition 8.1

(Non-Negative Definite Models)
.

Given
f\in L^{2}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)
, let

\left<f,\varPhi_{L}f\right>:=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}f(x)\varphi_{L}\left(x,y\right)\overline{f(y)}\mu\left(\mathrm{d}y\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right),

(8.1)

where
\overline{f(y)}
denotes the complex conjugate of
f(y)
.
Note that since
\varphi_{L}(x,y)=\varphi_{L}(y,x)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, we have
\left<f,\varPhi_{L}f\right>\in\mathbb{R}
.
A hyperbolic RCM is called
non-negative definite
if the adjacency function
\varphi_{L}
is non-negative definite for all
L
sufficiently large. An adjacency function
\varphi_{L}
is non-negative definite if it satisfies

\left<f,\varPhi_{L}f\right>\geq 0

(8.2)

for all
f\in L^{2}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)
.

Observation 8.2
.

The heat kernel RCM is an instance of a non-negative definite model.
For
t>0
, let
\kappa_{t}\colon L^{2}\left({\mathbb{H}^{3}}\right)\to L^{2}\left({\mathbb{H}^{3}}\right)
denote the convolution operator associated with
K_{3}\left(t,\cdot,\cdot\right)
. As a heat kernel,
\kappa_{t}
satisfies the semi-group property, and the symmetry of
K_{3}
in
x
and
y
implies that
\kappa_{t}
is self-adjoint.
Therefore

\left<f,\kappa_{t}f\right>=\left<\kappa_{\frac{t}{2}}f,\kappa_{\frac{t}{2}}f\right>=\left\lVert\kappa_{\frac{t}{2}}f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq 0.

(8.3)

Therefore
\kappa_{t}
is non-negative definite for all
t>0
.

In Lemma
B.1
below, it is proven that for non-negative definite
\varphi_{L}
, the spherical transform satisfies

\widetilde{\varphi}_{L}(s)\in\left[0,\infty\right)

(8.4)

for Lebesgue-almost all
s\in\mathbb{R}
. A consequence of this is the following lemma.

Lemma 8.3
.

Suppose
\left\{\varphi_{L}\right\}_{L>0}
satisfies Assumption
(.1)
and that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{L}(s)\geq 0
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
L>0
. Then there exists
K<\infty
such that for all
L>0
and
m\geq n\geq 1

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi_{L}^{\star m}\left(x,o\right)\leq K\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}^{m-n}\varphi_{L}^{\star n}\left(o,o\right).

(8.5)

Proof.

The result follows by writing
\varphi_{L}^{\star m}(x,o)
in terms of their spherical transforms. For all
x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{L}^{\star m}(x,o)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{w}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{e}^{\left(is+\frac{d-1}{2}\right)A(x,b)}\widetilde{\varphi}_{L}(s)^{m}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{w}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\text{e}^{\left(is+\frac{d-1}{2}\right)A(x,b)}\right\rvert\widetilde{\varphi}_{L}(s)^{m}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle\leq\left(\esssup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\varphi}_{L}(s)^{m-n}\right)\frac{1}{w}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{e}^{\frac{d-1}{2}A(x,b)}\widetilde{\varphi}_{L}(s)^{n}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}^{m-n}\left(\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}Q_{d}(x)\right)\frac{\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}}{w}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\varphi}_{L}(s)^{n}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=K\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{2\to 2}^{m-n}\varphi_{L}^{\star n}\left(o,o\right).

(8.6)

∎

Lemma
8.3
differs from the first part of Lemma
6.6
in that the addition of the assumption that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{L}(s)\geq 0
removes the requirement that
m
is even. This allows us to replace instances of a cycle of
\varphi_{L}
functions (i.e.

\varphi_{L}
functions of any shorter length, rather than only even shorter lengths.

As we shall see in Corollary
8.5
below, for non-negative definite models we can simplify the term

that appeared in Theorem

. Define

\mathcal{E}^{*}(L):=\frac{1}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\frac{C^{2}}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{2}}\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\cdot\varphi_{L}^{\star 2}\left(x,o\right)-\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\right)_{+}\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right).

(8.7)

.

Suppose

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}^{2}}\varphi^{\star 3}_{L}\left(o,o\right)

(8.14)

where

\mathcal{E}(L)
and

Proof.

The first inequality follows from applying Lemma

8.2
Heat Kernel RCM

Here we show how Theorem
2.4
follows from Theorem
7.5
. Recall that for the heat kernel RCM in

,
0<\mathcal{A}_{L}\leq\left(2\pi L\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{\frac{1}{2}L}
and

\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)=\mathcal{A}_{L}K_{3}\left(L,x,o\right)\\
=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{L}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}L\right)\frac{\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{3}}}\left(x,o\right)}{\sinh\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{3}}}\left(x,o\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2L}\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{H}^{3}}}\left(x,o\right)^{2}\right)

(8.20)

for all
L>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
.

Lemma 8.6
.

The heat kernel RCM in
d=3
satisfies Assumption
A
.

Proof.

First let us address assumption
(.1)
. Note that since
\varphi_{L}(r)\in\left[0,1\right]
for all
r>0
, assumption
(.1)
holds if and only if
\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}<\infty
. Since
\mathfrak{S}_{2}=4\pi
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{3}}\varphi_{L}\left(x,o\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=4\pi\int^{\infty}_{0}\varphi_{L}(r)\left(\sinh r\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\mathcal{A}_{L}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}L}\int^{\infty}_{0}r\sinh r\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{L}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}r\left(\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}\left(r-L\right)^{2}}-\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}\left(r+L\right)^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{L}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\int^{\infty}_{-L}\left(r_{1}+L\right)\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r_{1}^{2}}\mathrm{d}r_{1}-\int^{\infty}_{L}\left(r_{2}-L\right)\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r_{2}^{2}}\mathrm{d}r_{2}\right),

(8.21)

where in this last step we have performed two substitutions. Bringing together like terms then gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varPhi_{L}\right\rVert_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{L}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\int^{L}_{-L}r\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r+L\int^{L}_{-L}\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r+2L\int^{\infty}_{L}\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{L}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int^{L}_{-L}\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r+2\int^{\infty}_{L}\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{L}}{\sqrt{2\pi L}}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r=\mathcal{A}_{L}.

(8.22)

Therefore assumption
(.1)
holds.

To verify assumption
(.2)
, calculate

\displaystyle\frac{\int^{R}_{0}\varphi_{L}(r)\left(\sinh r\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}r}{\int^{\infty}_{0}\varphi_{L}(r)\left(\sinh r\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}r}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi}{\left(2\pi L\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}L}\int^{R}_{0}r\sinh r\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int^{R}_{0}r\left(\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}\left(r-L\right)^{2}}-\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}\left(r+L\right)^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\int^{L}_{L-R}\left(-r_{1}+L\right)\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}_{1}}\mathrm{d}+\int^{L+R}_{L}\left(L-r_{2}\right)\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}_{2}}\mathrm{d}r_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int^{L+R}_{L-R}\left(L-r\right)\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\times 2R\times R\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2L}\left(L-R\right)^{2}}

(8.23)

for
L\geq R
. In particular,

\frac{\int^{R}_{0}\varphi_{L}(r)\left(\sinh r\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}r}{\int^{\infty}_{0}\varphi_{L}(r)\left(\sinh r\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}r}=\mathcal{O}\left(L^{-\frac{3}{2}}\text{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}L}\right)

(8.24)

as
L\to\infty
for all fixed
R<\infty
. Therefore assumption
(.2)
holds.
∎

On the other hand, the volume of the cone is bounded above by

Appendix B
Range of the Spherical Transform

The following lemma includes Lemma
6.3
from Section
6
, with an extra result for non-negative definite models.

Lemma B.1
.

If
G\colon L^{2}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)\to L^{2}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)
is a bounded convolution operator associated with an isometry invariant and real
g
, then

\widetilde{g}(s)\in\mathbb{R}

(B.1)

for Lebesgue-almost all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{2\to 2}=\esssup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\widetilde{g}(s)\right\rvert.

(B.2)

Furthermore, for non-negative definite models

\widetilde{\varphi}_{L}(s)\in\left[0,\infty\right)

(B.3)

for Lebesgue-almost all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The isometry invariance of
g
implies that
g(x,y)=g(y,x)
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. Therefore for all
f\in L^{2}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)
we have

\left<f,Gf\right>=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}f(x)\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}g(x,y)\overline{f(y)}\mu\left(\mathrm{d}y\right)\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}f(x)g(x,y)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)\right)\overline{f(y)}\mu\left(\mathrm{d}y\right)=\left<Gf,f\right>,

(B.4)

where we have used the boundedness of
G
and Fubini-Tonelli to exchange the order of integration. However, we also have

\overline{\left<f,Gf\right>}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\overline{f(x)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}g(x,y)f(y)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}y\right)\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}f(x^{\prime})g(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\right)\right)\overline{f(y^{\prime})}\mu\left(\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right)=\left<Gf,f\right>,

(B.5)

where
x^{\prime}=y
and
y^{\prime}=x
. Therefore
\left<f,Gf\right>=\overline{\left<f,Gf\right>}
, and
\left<f,Gf\right>\in\mathbb{R}
.

Now by taking the properties of the spherical transform in Lemma
6.2
,

\displaystyle\left<f,Gf\right>

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}f(x)\overline{\left(Gf\right)\left(x\right)}\mu\left(\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}}{w}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{f}(s)\overline{\left(Gf\right)^{\sim}(s)}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}}{w}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{f}(s)\overline{\widetilde{g}(s)}\overline{\widetilde{f}(s)}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}}{w}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\widetilde{g}(s)}\left\lvert\widetilde{f}(s)\right\rvert^{2}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s.

(B.6)

Now suppose for contradiction that there exists a Lebesgue-positive subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
\widetilde{g}(s)\not\in\mathbb{R}
for all
s\in E
, then there exists a
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and Lebesgue-positive and bounded
F\subset E
such that the complex ball
B_{\varepsilon}\left(z\right)\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
and
\widetilde{g}(s)\in B_{\varepsilon}\left(z\right)
for all
s\in F
. Then let
h(s)=\mathds{1}\left\{F\right\}(s)
. Since
F\subset\mathbb{R}
is bounded,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert h(s)\right\rvert^{2}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{F}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s<\infty,

(B.7)

and therefore
h\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\right)
.
Therefore if we invert the spherical transform on this function, we can let

f(x)=\frac{\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}}{w}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{e}^{\left(+is+\frac{d-1}{2}\right)A(x,b)}h(s)\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}}{w}\int_{F}\text{e}^{\left(+is+\frac{d-1}{2}\right)A(x,b)}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s,

(B.8)

and know
f\in L^{2}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)
. Therefore
\widetilde{f}(s)=h(s)
, and we have

\left<f,Gf\right>=\frac{\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}}{w}\int_{F}\overline{\widetilde{g}(s)}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s\not\in\mathbb{R}.

(B.9)

This contradiction proves that
\widetilde{g}(s)\in\mathbb{R}
for Lebesgue-almost all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Recall that for non-negative definite models,
\left<f,\varPhi_{L}f\right>\in\left[0,\infty\right)
for all
f\in L^{2}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)
. The above argument can easily be adapted to show that if
\widetilde{\varphi}_{L}(s)\not\in\left[0,\infty\right)
for a Lebesgue-positive set of
s
, then one can construct some
f\in L^{2}\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)
such that
\left<f,\varPhi_{L}f\right>\not\in\left[0,\infty\right)
. This contradiction proves the result.

To show
\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{2\to 2}=\esssup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\widetilde{g}(s)\right\rvert
, we just apply Lemma
6.2
. Starting with the Plancherel result and applying the multiplication formula:

\displaystyle\left\lVert Gf\right\rVert^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{w}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\widetilde{g}(s)\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\widetilde{f}(s,b)\right\rvert^{2}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle\leq\left(\esssup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\widetilde{g}(s)\right\rvert\right)^{2}\frac{1}{w}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\widetilde{f}(s,b)\right\rvert^{2}\lvert\mathbf{c}(s)\rvert^{-2}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle=\left(\esssup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\widetilde{g}(s)\right\rvert\right)^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{2}.

(B.10)

This shows
\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{2\to 2}\leq\esssup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\widetilde{g}(s)\right\rvert
. To get the equality, we want to find some
f
such that
\widetilde{f}(s)
approximates a Dirac delta function at the argument essential supremum. The inverse formula gives exactly this.
∎

Lemma C.2

(Sine rule for hyperbolic triangles)
.

For the hyperbolic triangle
\Delta ABC
,

\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sinh a}=\frac{\sin\beta}{\sinh b}=\frac{\sin\gamma}{\sinh c}.

(C.2)

Proof.

See
[
21
, Corollary 32.14]
.

Lemma C.3

(Cosine rule for hyperbolic triangles)
.

For the hyperbolic triangle
\Delta ABC
,

\cos\alpha\sinh b\sinh c=\cosh b\cosh c-\cosh a.

(C.3)

Proof.

See
[
21

Lemma C.4

(Hyperbolic area for hyperbolic triangles)
.

For the hyperbolic triangle
\Delta ABC\subset{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
, the hyperbolic area is equal to the angle defect:

\mu\left(\Delta ABC\right)=\pi-\alpha-\beta-\gamma.

(C.4)

Proof.

See
[
21
, Chapter 33.2]
.

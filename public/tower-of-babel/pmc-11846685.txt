FORAlign: accelerating gap-affine DNA pairwise sequence alignment using FOR-blocks based on Four Russians approach with linear space complexity
Authors: Yanming Wei, Tong Zhou, Yixiao Zhai, Liang Yu, Quan Zou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPairwise sequence alignment (PSA) serves as the cornerstone in computational bioinformatics, facilitating multiple sequence alignment and phylogenetic analysis. This paper introduces the FORAlign algorithm, leveraging the Four Russians algorithm with identical upper-bound time and space complexity as the Hirschberg divide-and-conquer PSA algorithm, aimed at accelerating Hirschberg PSA algorithm in parallel. Particularly notable is its capability to achieve up to 16.79 times speedup when aligning sequences with low sequence similarity, compared to the conventional Needleman-Wunsch PSA method using non-heuristic methods. Empirical evaluations underscore FORAlign’s superiority over existing wavefront alignment (WFA) series software, especially in scenarios characterized by low sequence similarity during PSA tasks. Our method is capable of directly aligning monkeypox sequences with other sequences using non-heuristic methods. The algorithm was implemented within the FORAlign library, providing functionality for PSA and foundational support for multiple sequence alignment and phylogenetic trees. The FORAlign library is freely available at https://github.com/malabz/FORAlign.

Introduction

Multiple sequence alignment (MSA) is a fundamental problem in computational bioinformatics [1–8]. Pairwise sequence alignment (PSA) serves as the fundamental building block for MSA. The accuracy and reliability of the alignment outcomes derived from PSA directly influence the overall quality of MSA. For instance, in the HAlign 3 [9] method, MSA is achieved through central star alignment, which crucially involves conducting PSA between the central sequence and other sequences, followed by the integration of all pairwise alignment results. Similarly, in progressive strategy MSA algorithms like MAFFT [10], PSA plays a key role in aligning sequences or profiles based on guide tree constructed from distance matrix. Thus, PSA is critical to the effectiveness of MSA.

The Needleman-Wunsch algorithm (N-W algorithm) leverages Dynamic Programming (DP) to address PSA [11], and many several subsequent algorithms have expanded this approach. For example, Gotoh proposed the Smith-Waterman-Gotoh algorithm [12, 13] for aligning sequences with large insertions and deletions. Mrozek et al. developed an energy-adapted Smith-Waterman [14] method for protein PSA, which employs constant and fuzzy similarity measures to assess amino acid similarity [15–17]. Although there are many methods in aligning sequences directly, there are many heuristic methods like Block Aligner [18] to speed up the PSA. Another approach for PSA, such as A*PA [19] and A*PA2 [20], transforms the DP problem into shortest path problem in graph, by designing appropriate heuristic functions to measure the distance in the DP matrix, and then applying the A* algorithm for PSA, which later inspired the development of the wavefront alignment (WFA) algorithm [21]. Miller and Myers [22] introduced a linear space complexity approach for aligning two sequences based on the Hirschberg algorithm [23], which later inspired WFA2 algorithm [24]. The Four Russians approach [25] emphasizes preprocessing all known scenarios, storing solutions in a lookup table, and utilizing pre-calculated results from the lookup table during actual problem-solving to enhance efficiency. Kim et al. introduced an optimized encoding method to reduce the extensive preprocessing lookup table for edit distance [26]. Kundeti et al. combined the principles of the Hirschberg algorithm with the Four Russians approach, devising an algorithm with linear space complexity for PSA by edit distance [27]. Moreover, researchers have leveraged the Four Russians approach to address linear gap penalty for PSA, as demonstrated in [28].

As modern processor systems become more widespread, parallel techniques have become increasingly important for PSA, leading researchers to focus more on divide-and-conquer methods for solving alignments [29]. BSAlign [30] accelerates PSA by leveraging the K-band strategy along with SIMD operations. Yang et al. [31] presented a parallel formulation of the anti-diagonal approach to expedite the longest common sequence (LCS) calculation process, which LCS is equivalent to edit distance [32]. Furthermore, numerous efforts have been focused on developing parallel methods for PSA, with notable examples such as those presented in references [33] and others. Zhu et al. proposed leveraging the edit distance algorithm solution for PSA using Spark [34]. Kim et al. introduced an efficient parallel approach for solving edit distance problems based on the Four Russians approach [26]. However, to the best of our knowledge, several challenges remain unaddressed: (i) there has been limited exploration of parallelizing the exact alignment algorithm, the Hirschberg algorithm, using modern parallel frameworks like the fork-join model [35]. Without proper parallelization, the potential of multi-core systems remains underutilized; (ii) the Four Russians method has been primarily applied to linear gap penalty, with little attention to affine gap penalty scenarios.

To address these challenges, we propose FORAlign; a lightweight and high-performance PSA library built on the Hirschberg algorithm and designed specifically for affine gap penalties. FORAlign leverages the Four Russians method and parallelizes computations using the fork-join model, offering significant improvements in performance. Our implementation accelerates the Hirschberg algorithm, providing a scalable and efficient solution for non-heuristic PSA tasks. It is particularly effective when handling low similarity and long-sequence alignments, such as monkeypox sequences, compared to the widely used WFA method.

Methods

In the following sections, we explain the key principles behind the FORAlign library. We begin by introducing the two-level parallelism in FORAlign, which employs multithreading for efficient DP calculation and the Hirschberg divide-and-conquer function. Next, we explain how the Four Russians approach is adapted for affine gap penalty through the concept of FOR-blocks. Finally, we demonstrate how these FOR-blocks are integrated into the Hirschberg algorithm, achieving further acceleration.

The framework of FORAlign

FORAlign is designed to address the computational challenges of PSA by leveraging the Hirschberg algorithm along with several key optimization strategies. These optimizations include two-level parallelization approach and the use of FOR-blocks, both aimed at enhancing algorithm efficiency. The two-level parallelization accelerates both the divide-and-conquer and DP functions, while the FOR-blocks maximize the reuse of calculated regions, significantly improving the speed of DP process. Figure 1 illustrates the overall structure of the FORAlign framework, demonstrating how these strategies combine to enhance the speed of PSA.

The framework of FORAlign. The Hirschberg algorithm can be divided into two parts: (A) decompose the PSA problem into subproblems and (B) combine the midpoints calculated from (A) into the final alignment. To accelerate PSA solved by the Hirschberg algorithm, we implemented two distinct strategies in decomposing the PSA into subproblems and finding the center points, as shown in (A): (a) two-level parallelism: The Hirschberg algorithm is divided into two parts—the divide-and-conquer function and the DP function. By designing separate thread pools, both parts can be fully parallelized for maximum efficiency; (b) FOR-block for affine gap penalty: FOR-blocks are mapped onto regions calculated by the Hirschberg algorithm. This focuses on generating and reusing the maximum number of recurrent FOR-blocks to further accelerate the algorithm.

In summary, the Hirschberg algorithm can be divided into two main components: the divide-and-conquer function, which identifies the coordinates for each minimum score, and the DP function, which calculates the minimum scores. FORAlign implements two-level parallelism by parallelizing both components: the divide-and-conquer function forms the first layer of parallelism, while the DP function constitutes the second layer. This two-level parallelism allows FORAlign to fully utilize multi-core processors, significantly accelerating the Hirschberg algorithm.

Parallelization of divide-and-conquer function

Researchers have widely explored the parallelization the divide-and-conquer-like problems with multiple threads like fork-join model [35]. In a typical divide-and-conquer parallelization, the problem is recursively split into smaller subproblems, which are then solved in parallel. FORAlign adopts this strategy with the fork-join model to efficiently distribute tasks across multiple threads. Several key techniques are employed in FORAlign to optimize the divide-and-conquer process: (i) recursive decomposition: the problem is recursively divided into multiple subproblems, with each solved in parallel; (ii) task load balancing: in FORAlign, the divide-and-conquer and DP tasks are dynamically partitioned and distributed across available processors. This approach ensures that computational resources are effectively utilized; (iii) work-stealing strategy: when one thread finishes its tasks, it can ‘steal’ unfinished tasks from other threads. This minimizes idle time and improves parallel efficiency, especially when there are uneven workloads across different subproblems. This is particularly beneficial in divide-and-conquer processes, where uneven workloads across subproblems are common. These techniques enable FORAlign to achieve optimal performance and scalability in PSA tasks.

As mentioned in Supplemental Material, directly using FOR-blocks for affine gap penalty is challenging due to the number of precomputed blocks required. However, by integrating the Four Russians approach into the Hirschberg algorithm, we can significantly speed up the computation process while maintaining the efficiency of the divide-and-conquer strategy.

Apply FOR-block to Hirschberg algorithm

The key observation in the Hirschberg algorithm is that the problem-solving regions inherently overlap during recursive divisions (details shown in Fig. 6). The overlap occurs as the algorithm divides the DP matrix into smaller subproblems by recursively locating midpoints, with subproblems sharing common computational areas. Subproblems share common computational areas, leading to the same regions being recalculated multiple times in traditional approaches, thereby increasing computational cost. By applying the Four Russians approach, we can divide the DP matrix into FOR-blocks. Rather than recalculating the same values at each recursive step, the algorithm checks whether the FOR-block has already been computed and reuses the existing result if it has. This strategy significantly reduces redundant calculations, particularly as the sequence length increases, where the computational burden from repeated calculations becomes more substantial. As a result, the Four Russians approach effectively accelerates the Hirschberg algorithm. Figure 6 illustrates how FOR-blocks are reused throughout the recursive steps of the Hirschberg algorithm.

Details for speeding up Hirschberg algorithm using four Russians approach. This figure visualizes the overlapping regions between the main problem and its subproblems. The recursive subproblems for type 1 midpoint are in the upper-left part, while those for type 2 midpoint are in the lower-right part. Each small square represents a FOR-block. By reusing FOR-blocks in these overlapping regions and choosing an appropriate block size t, the computation can be significantly reduced, effectively accelerating the Hirschberg algorithm. The colored areas represent the shared FOR-blocks between the main problem and its subproblems. The reused FOR-blocks between subproblems and sub-subproblems are marked with lines. It’s worth noting that, each FOR-block must be a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} $t\times t$\end{document} block; otherwise, the area (shown in dotted colors) must be recalculated.

For the parallelized enhancement of the Hirschberg algorithm, we implemented a two-level parallelism strategy, utilizing a parallelized hash map [36] to represent the lookup table with encoded string representation [26]. This approach accelerates the DP calculations, significantly improving the overall speed of the Hirschberg algorithm.

Performance evaluation of FOR-block acceleration in FORAlign

To demonstrate the acceleration achieved through the utilization of FOR-blocks in the Hirschberg algorithm, we conducted experiments comparing the runtime speedup and memory consumption ratios of the hirschberg-multi (HTDT) and russians-multi-t (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} ${\boldsymbol{R}}_{\boldsymbol{t}}$\end{document}) methods against the hirschberg-single (HTD1) method. These experiments were conducted on 12 cores, 56 cores, 80 cores, and 128 cores listed in Fig. 8, utilizing varying values of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} $\boldsymbol{t}=\mathbf{10}\sim \mathbf{10000}$\end{document}. Figure 8 illustrates the results. It can be discerned that our method, leveraging a parallel call of the Hirschberg algorithm coupled with a proper fixed size t for parallelizing DP process, achieved further acceleration. Specifically, our approach exhibited optimal speedup ratios at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} $50\le t\le 2000$\end{document}, reaching optimal speedup ratios at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} $t=200$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} $t=500$\end{document}. However, it’s worth noting that, while accelerating the Hirschberg algorithm using FOR-blocks, the storage requirements for these blocks also increase. As t become larger, fewer FOR-blocks can be stored, resulting in reduced space consumption as t increases. This observation highlights the trade-offs involved in optimizing the Hirschberg algorithm through the utilization of FOR-blocks. By increasing the FOR-block size t, we can effectively limit memory consumption. When memory is limited, we can increase the FOR-block size t for aligning extremely long sequences.

Conclusion

In this study, we introduced FORAlign, a parallelized library for PSA that combines the Four Russians approach with the Hirschberg algorithm to support affine gap penalty alignment. FORAlign leverages the Four Russians approach to accelerate the Hirschberg algorithm by reusing calculated regions through FOR-blocks. The efficiency of FORAlign in PSA is primarily influenced by the lengths of the sequences, consistently demonstrates stable speedup ratios, which makes it an effective solution for parallelizing PSA tasks. FORAlign is flexible; users can adjust the FOR-block size and limit the number of threads to calculate PSA. Compared to the WFA series methods, FORAlign demonstrates superior performance, particularly when aligning sequences with low similarity. The low similarity datasets were analyzed by clustering sequences like CD-HIT [42], and our method plays a crucial role in supporting this analysis, particularly in handling sequences with low similarity, where traditional methods often struggle to deliver fast and accurate results. Our development efforts have culminated in FORAlign library compatible with both Linux and Windows platforms.

In future development, we aim to expand the capabilities of the FORAlign library to support two-piece affine gap penalty parameters and various scoring matrix configurations. Furthermore, we plan to integrate FORAlign to heuristic methods, including K-band method and related approaches. The FORAlign package is freely available at https://github.com/malabz/FORAlign. It has been tested on the Linux-like and Windows operating system.

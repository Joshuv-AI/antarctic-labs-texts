Comment on “Lipoprotein(a) reduction with inclisiran, alirocumab, evolocumab, enlicitide, and lerodalcibep: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials”
Authors: Vatsala Gupta, Parag J Ratnakar, Kotina Shridevi, Gaurav, Jeffrin Reneus Paul
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Mulligan et al. synthesized 31 randomized trials and concluded that PCSK9-targeting agents provide comparable lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] lowering, permitting selection according to dosing, access, cost, and patient preference [1]. The pooled estimate is informative, but the comparative conclusion requires clarification because the reported meta-regression contrasts are internally inconsistent and because non-significance in an indirect analysis does not establish equivalence.

First, the inclisiran-versus-evolocumab contrast is +4.9% (95% CI -2.31 to 12.16; P = .18) in the abstract, but +10.64% with the same confidence interval and P value in the Results [1]. Also reports an SE of 6.59 and z = 1.33. These values cannot all arise from one Wald test: +10.64/6.59 gives z = 1.61 and an approximate 95% CI of −2.28 to 23.56. For inclisiran versus alirocumab, the point estimate (+1.59%) lies outside the interval printed in the Results (−8.07 to 1.40), whereas the abstract reports the internally coherent interval −5.38 to 8.55. The alirocumab-versus-evolocumab interval also loses its negative lower bound. A corrected contrast table generated from one stated model, with covariance-based standard errors for every pairwise contrast, would clarify which estimates are authoritative.

Second, "no statistically significant difference" should not be translated into therapeutic equivalence. A superiority test with P > .05 indicates insufficient evidence of a difference, not evidence that clinically important differences are absent [2]. Equivalence requires a prespecified clinically acceptable margin and confidence intervals that exclude effects beyond that margin [3]. This distinction is especially important because residual heterogeneity remained high (I2 = 92.41%), and evidence for lerodalcibep and enlicitide comprised only two and one trials, respectively [1]. Moreover, these were study-level meta-regression contrasts rather than randomized head-to-head comparisons. Meta-regression associations are observational and can reflect differences in populations, baseline Lp(a), follow-up, assays, background therapy, or trial design [4].

We therefore suggest reframing the conclusion as "no between-agent difference was detected in an indirect study-level comparison," pending correction of the contrast statistics. Reporting 95% prediction intervals and a sensitivity analysis excluding trials with imputed standard errors would further show how much uncertainty remains across settings. These revisions would preserve the useful pooled estimate while avoiding an equivalence claim that the current analysis does not establish.

Nonlinear rough Fokker–Planck equations
Fabio Bugini, Peter K. Friz, Wilhelm Stannat

Abstract
McKean–-Vlasov SDEs describe systems where the dynamics depend on the law of the process. The corresponding Fokker–Planck equation is a nonlinear, nonlocal PDE for the corresponding measure flow. In the presence of common noise and conditional law dependence, the evolution becomes random and is governed by a stochastic Fokker–-Planck equation; that is, a nonlinear, nonlocal SPDE in the space of measures. (Such equations constitute an important ingredient in the theory of mean-field games with common noise.) Well-posedness of such SPDEs is a difficult problem, the best result to date due to Coghi – Gess (2019), which however comes with dimension-dependent regularity assumptions. In the present work, we show how rough path techniques can circumvent these entirely. Hence, and somewhat contrarily to common believe, the use of rough paths leads to substantially less regularity demands on the coefficients than methods rooted in classical stochastic analysis methods.



Keywords.
Fokker–Planck equations, rough paths, McKean–Vlasov equations.

Introduction

In this paper we present an existence and uniqueness result for measure-valued Fokker–Planck equations of the form

d\mu_{t}=\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{j}\partial x^{i}}(a^{ij}(t,x,\mu_{t})\mu_{t})-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}(b^{i}(t,x,\mu_{t})\mu_{t})\Bigg)\,dt+\\
-\sum_{\kappa=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}(f_{\kappa}^{i}(t,x,\mu_{t})\mu_{t})\,dW^{\kappa}_{t}

(1)

on a finite time interval
[0,T]
.
The solution is a curve
\mu:[0,T]\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the space of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
W:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous path, in the rough regime
\alpha\in(1/3,1/2]
. The correct rough path formulation is left to
4.1
and notably accomodates Brownian randomisation
W\rightsquigarrow B^{0}(\omega)
, thereby covering the popular situation of McKean–Vlasov SDEs with common noise, cf. (
2
) below.
The coefficients

(a,b,f):[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}

also depend on the solution itself, potentially in a nonlinear manner. Equations of the form (
1
) are often called nonlinear and nonlocal.
For any
(t,x,\mu)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the (
d\times d
)-matrix
a(t,x,\mu)
is symmetric and positive semi-definite.
The main result of our work is the following

Theorem

(See
4.3
below)
.

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}\,\nu(dx)
is finite. Then, under natural regularity assumptions on the coefficients
a,b,f
(see
4.4
and
4.8
below), there exists a unique solution
\mu
to the nonlinear, nonlocal rough Fokker-Planck equation (
1
), in the precise sense of
4.1
below, such that
\mu_{0}=\nu
.

Motivation.
For sake of better readability let us assume that all the object introduced in this informal discussion are one-dimensional.
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
and let
B,B^{0},B^{1},\dots,B^{N}
be independent Brownian motions on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.
Let us consider a system of
N
interacting particles with common noise, whose dynamics is described via

\begin{cases}dX^{i,N}_{t}=b_{t}(X^{i,N}_{t},\mu^{N}_{t})\,dt+\sigma_{t}(X^{i,N}_{t},\mu^{N}_{t})\,dB^{i}_{t}+f_{t}(X^{i,N}_{t},\mu^{N}_{t})\circ dB^{0}_{t},\ t\in[0,T]\\
\hfill i=1,\dots,N\\
\mu^{N}_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{j,N}_{t}}.\end{cases}

Notice how the position
X^{i,N}
of every particle also depends on the average position of all the other particles in the system, through the empirical measure
\mu^{N}
. This interaction is often referred to as being of mean-field type.
Observe in addition how each one of the particles is influenced by two independent sources of noise: an individual one
B^{i}
, specific for the particle
X^{i,N}
, and a common noise
B^{0}
, visible by every particle.
For what comes later in the paper, it is convenient to think the latter as a Stratonovich noise; the Itô case can be obtained by changing the drift coefficient
b
,
according to the Itô-Stratonovich correction.
Assume that the initial conditions
\{X^{i,N}_{0}\}_{i=1,\dots,N}
are exchangeable, conditionally independent given
B^{0}
and indentically distributed (denote by
\nu:=\text{Law}(X_{0}^{1,N})
).
As the number
N
of particles grows to
+\infty
, particles become asymptotically independent, conditional on
B^{0}
; the effect of the individual noises averages out, but the influence of the common noise is still present.
At least formally, every
X^{i,N}
converges (in a suitable sense) to independent copies of
X
, being
X
the solution to

\begin{cases}dX_{t}=b_{t}(X_{t},\mu_{t})\,dt+\sigma_{t}(X_{t},\mu_{t})\,dB_{t}+f_{t}(X_{t},\mu_{t})\circ dB^{0}_{t},\quad t\in[0,T]\\
\mu_{t}=\text{Law}(X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B^{0}})\\
\text{Law}(X_{0})=\nu,\end{cases}

(2)

where
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B^{0}}=\sigma(B^{0}_{r},\ 0\leq r\leq t)
.
This phenomenon is know as
conditional propagation of chaos
. For a proper discussion see, for instance,
[
CD18a
, Section 2.1.2]
.
Again at a formal level, the curve of random distributions
t\mapsto\mu_{t}:=\text{Law}(X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B^{0}})
is expected to be a solution to a nonlinear Fokker–Planck stochastic partial differential equation of the form

d\mu_{t}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{x}(\sigma_{t}(x,\mu_{t})^{2}\mu_{t})-\partial_{x}(b_{t}(x,\mu_{t})\mu_{t})\right)\,dt-\partial_{x}(f_{t}(x,\mu_{t})\mu_{t})\circ dB^{0}_{t}.

(3)

In this paper, we adopt a rough path point of view to characterize solutions to (
3
).
Almost every trajectory
W=(B^{0}_{t}(\omega))_{t\in[0,T]}
of the common Stratonovich noise can be lifted to a (weakly geometric) rough path
\mathbf{W}=(W,\mathbb{W})
(see
2.4
below).
We can therefore consider the following mixed rough and stochastic differential equation, which naturally arises by freezing a trajectory of the common noise in equation (
2
):

\begin{cases}dX^{\mathbf{W}}_{t}=b_{t}(X^{\mathbf{W}}_{t},\mu^{\mathbf{W}}_{t})\,dt+\sigma_{t}(X^{\mathbf{W}}_{t},\mu^{\mathbf{W}}_{t})\,dB_{t}+f_{t}(X^{\mathbf{W}}_{t},\mu^{\mathbf{W}}_{t})\,d\mathbf{W}_{t},\quad t\in[0,T]\\
\mu^{\mathbf{W}}_{t}=\text{Law}(X^{\mathbf{W}}_{t})\\
\text{Law}(X^{\mathbf{W}}_{0})=\nu.\end{cases}

(4)

This kind of equations has been introduced and studied in
[
FHL25
]
. We also give a short overview of their work in
Section

3
of the present paper.
The aim of our work is therefore to characterize, for any weakly geometric rough path
\mathbf{W}=(W,\mathbb{W})
, the curve
t\mapsto\mu_{t}^{\mathbf{W}}:=\text{Law}(X_{t}^{\mathbf{W}})
as the unique solution to a measure-valued nonlinear rough partial differential equation (rough PDE) of the form

d\mu^{\mathbf{W}}_{t}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{x}(\sigma_{t}(x,\mu^{\mathbf{W}}_{t})^{2}\mu^{\mathbf{W}}_{t})-\partial_{x}(b_{t}(x,\mu^{\mathbf{W}}_{t})\mu^{\mathbf{W}}_{t})\right)\,dt-\partial_{x}(f_{t}(x,\mu^{\mathbf{W}}_{t})\mu^{\mathbf{W}}_{t})\,d\mathbf{W}_{t}.

As shown in
[
FHL25
, Section 3.3]
, also
[
FLZ25
, Section 5]
, the law of
X^{\mathbf{W}}_{t}
yields a regular conditional distribution of the conditional law of
X_{t}
given
(B^{0}_{r},\ r\in[0,t])
, provided
\mathbf{W}
is taken to be the Stratonovich rough path lift of
B^{0}
(cf.
2.4
). More precisely,

\mu_{t}(\omega):=\text{Law}(X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}^{B^{0}}_{t})(\omega)=\mu_{t}^{\mathbf{W}}\big|_{\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{B}^{\text{Strato}}(\omega)}

for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every
\omega\in\Omega
. In particular, the objects we are concerned with in this paper are the “right” ones, in the sense that they coincide with objects previously treated from within Itô calculus. Remarkably enough, the usual perception that rough path requires more regularity than Itô based methods does not apply here: stochastic (state of the art) methods
[
CG19
]
require dimenion-dependent regularity assumptions, which is not at all the methods presented here i.e. we obtain well-posedness under dimension-independent regularity assumptions.
We also note that our rough path approach to common noise goes immediately beyond Brownian (or semimartingale) common noise, and indeed provides a satisfactory answer to the question why one should prescribe statistics for the common noise at the first place, given that one wishes to work conditionally under the common noise.

Literature review.

Well-posedness of equation (
1
) has been investigated in
[
CG19
]
for the case where
W
is an Itô Brownian motion (i.e., not a deterministic path).
The authors adopt a stochastic PDEs framework and establish uniqueness through a duality argument. However, their results and assumptions inherently depend on the dimension of the state space, partly due to Sobolev embeddings and other technical considerations.
(Specifically,
[
CG19
, Assumptions 5.1]
require the coefficients to have
m
degrees of regularity, with
m>d/2+2
where
d
is the dimension of the state space. )
In
[
CN21
]
, the authors are concerned with the study of equation (
1
) in a rough path framework. They address the challenges posed by the measure-dependent coefficient
f
in the rough term by assuming a linear dependence on the measure.

Central to our approach are rough stochastic differential equations
[
FHL21
,
FHL25
]
, which ultimately allow us to accommodate, under natural regularity assumptions, the desirable general (nonlinear) dependencies in the measure, as in
[
CG19
]
and required in applications
[
CD18
,
CD18a
]
.

In our work, we integrate rough path theory with analysis on the space of probability measures, inspired by Lions’ differential calculus in measure spaces. We also draw inspiration from
[
BCD20
]
, although their framework is different, as their focus is on the mean-field rough differential equations driven by arbitrary random rough paths, which (in this generality) does not come with accompanying stochastic partial differential equations.

Preliminaries

2.1
Rough path theory

For a detailed discussion on rough paths (and more) see
[
FH20
]
.
Let
T>0
.

Definition 2.1
.

(see
[
FH20
, Definition 2.1]
)
Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
.
An
\alpha
-
rough path
on
[0,T]
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a pair
\mathbf{W}=(W,\mathbb{W})
such that

(a)

W:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\delta W_{s,t}\overset{\alpha}{=}0
;

(b)

\mathbb{W}:[0,T]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathbb{W}_{s,t}\overset{2\alpha}{=}0
;

(c)

(Chen’s relation) for any
s,u,t\in[0,T]
, it holds

\mathbb{W}_{s,t}-\mathbb{W}_{s,u}-\mathbb{W}_{u,t}=\delta W_{s,u}\otimes\delta W_{u,t}

(5)

or, equivalently,
\mathbb{W}_{s,t}^{ij}-\mathbb{W}_{s,u}^{ij}-\mathbb{W}_{u,t}^{ij}=\delta W_{s,u}^{i}\delta W_{u,t}^{j}
for any
i,j=1,\dots,n
.

We write
\mathbf{W}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 2.2
.

(see
[
FH20
, Section 2.2]
)
An
\alpha
-rough path
\mathbf{W}=(W,\mathbb{W})
is said to be
weakly geometric
if, for any
s,t\in[0,T]
,

Sym(\mathbb{W}_{s,t}):=\frac{\mathbb{W}_{s,t}+\mathbb{W}_{s,t}^{\top}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\delta W_{s,t}\otimes\delta W_{s,t}.

or, equivalently,
\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{W}_{s,t}^{ij}+\mathbb{W}_{s,t}^{ji})=\frac{1}{2}\delta W_{s,t}^{i}\delta W_{s,t}^{j}
for any
i,j=1,\dots,n
.
We write
\mathbf{W}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}_{g}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
W:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth path. For any
t\in[0,T]
, the integral
\int_{0}^{t}Y_{r}\,dW_{r}
is well-defined in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as a Riemann–Stieltjes integral, provided that
Y:[0,T]\to\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
is, for example, continuous.
Define

\mathbb{W}_{s,t}=(\mathbb{W}_{s,t}^{ij})_{i,j=1,\dots,n}:=\left(\int_{s}^{t}(W_{r}^{i}-W_{s}^{i})\,dW^{j}_{r}\right)_{i,j=1,\dots,n}

for any
s,t\in[0,T]
, where the integral is understood as a Riemann–Stieltjes integral.
It is straightforward to verify that
\mathbf{W}=(W,\mathbb{W})\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}_{g}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
.
When
W
is only
\alpha
-Hölder continuous, the previous Riemann–Stieltjes integral may no longer be well-defined. In such a case, we can think of
\mathbb{W}^{ij}
as the postulated value of this integral.
Rough path theory provides a framework to extend Riemann–Stieltjes integration with respect to
dW
to a larger class of integrands
Y
.

Example 2.4

(Rough path lift of a Brownian motion)
.

Let
B=(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be an
n
-dimensional Brownian motion on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.
For any
s,t\in[0,T]
, define its iterated (Stratonovich) integral

\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{\text{Strato}}:=\int_{s}^{t}\delta B_{s,r}\circ dB_{r}.

For definiteness,
(\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{\text{Strato}})^{ij}(\omega)=(\int_{s}^{t}\delta B^{i}_{s,r}\circ dB^{j}_{r})(\omega)
for any
i,j=1,\dots,n
and for any
\omega\in\Omega
. Then, for any
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2})
and
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,

\mathbf{B}^{\text{Strato}}=(B,\mathbb{B}^{\text{Strato}})\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}_{g}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})

(see
[
FH20
, Proposition 3.5]
).
The Itô lift of
B
is defined analogously, by setting
\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{\text{It\^{o}}}:=\int_{s}^{t}\delta B_{s,r}dB_{r}
. However,
\mathbf{B}^{\text{It\^{o}}}=(B,\mathbb{B}^{\text{It\^{o}}})
is not weakly geometric. The Itô-Stratonovich correction still holds:
\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{\text{Strato}}=\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{\text{It\^{o}}}+\frac{1}{2}(t-s)I
, being

the identity element of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.2
Lions derivatives

For a complete overview on Lions’ calculus see
[
CD18
, Section 5]
.
Let
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
A coupling of
\mu
and
\nu
is a Borel probability measure
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that, for any
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\pi(A\times\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mu(A)
and
\pi(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times A)=\nu(A)
.
Let
p\in[1,\infty)
.
The set

\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\Big\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mid\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{p}\,\mu(dz)<+\infty\Big\}

can be endowed with the so-called
p
-Wasserstein distance
\mathcal{W}_{p}
, defined by

\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\left\{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z-w|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{p}\,\pi(dz,dw)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\mid\text{$\pi$ is a coupling of $\mu$ and $\nu$}\right\}.

In particular, for any probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and for any pair
X,Y
of random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that
\mathcal{L}_{X}=\mu
and
\mathcal{L}_{Y}=\nu
,

\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)\leq\|X-Y\|_{p}.

By Hölder’s inequality, for
p\leq q
it holds
\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)\leq\mathcal{W}_{q}(\mu,\nu)
. It is also possible to prove that
(\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathcal{W}_{p})
is a complete separable metric space. Moreover, the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is generated by the family of mappings
\{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ni\mu\mapsto\mu(A)\mid A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
. In the rest of the paper we focus on the case
p=2
.

Let
g:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
and let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be any (atomless Polish) probability space.
The Lions lift of
g
is the map
\hat{g}:L_{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\hat{g}(X):=g(\mathcal{L}_{X})
.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that
g
is
(continuously) L-differentiable
at
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if there exists a random variable
X_{0}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
with
\mathcal{L}_{X_{0}}=\mu_{0}
and such that the lifted map
\hat{g}
is (continuously) Fréchet differentiable at
X_{0}
.

The Fréchet derivative of
\hat{g}
at
X_{0}
is denoted by
D\hat{g}(X_{0})
and can be seen as an element of
L_{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, due to the identification of
L_{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
with its dual.
It is possible to prove that this notion of differentiability is intrinsic, i.e. it does not depend upon the choice of the random variable whose law is
\mu_{0}
nor on the probability space (cf.
[
CD18
, Proposition 5.24]
). We say that
g
is (continuously) L-differentiable if it is (continuously) L-differentiable at any
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Let
g:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
be continuously L-differentiable. Then the following hold:

(i)

for any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists
\partial_{\mu}g(\mu)(\cdot)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that, for any
X\in L_{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mathcal{L}_{X}=\mu
,

D\hat{g}(X)=\partial_{\mu}g(\mu)(X)\qquad\text{$\mathbb{P}$-a.s.},

i.e.
D\hat{g}(X)(\omega)=\partial_{\mu}g(\mu)(X(\omega))
for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every
\omega\in\Omega
(cf.
[
CD18
, Proposition 5.25]
). In particular, for any
Y\in L_{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have that
D\hat{g}(X)[Y]=\mathbb{E}(\partial_{\mu}g(\mu)(X)\cdot Y)
, where
\cdot
denotes the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(ii)

\partial_{\mu}g(\mu)(v)
has a jointly measurable
\mu
-version, namely it is possible to redefine
\partial_{\mu}g(\mu)
on a

Remark 2.10
.

Differentiability along sub-Banach spaces naturally arises as a tool to deal with continuity of higher order Fréchet derivatives.
In
[

, Remark 5.80]
the authors exhibit the explicit construction of a smooth and compactly supported function

for which the map

defined by

is not twice continuously Fréchet differentiable.
The issue clearly relies in the continuity of the second Fréchet derivative as a function from

to

.
An easy application of Hölder’s inequality shows that

.

for simplicity, for any

and for any

such that

, we have

\displaystyle|D^{2}g(X)[Y_{1},Y_{2}]-D^{2}g(\bar{X})[Y_{1},Y_{2}]|

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{E}((h^{\prime\prime}(X)-h^{\prime\prime}(\bar{X}))Y_{1}Y_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{\prime\prime}(X)-h^{\prime\prime}(\bar{X})\|_{r}\|Y_{1}\|_{p}\|Y_{2}\|_{p}

where

.
In the case

(or, equivalently,

), using boundedness and Lipschitz continuity of

\displaystyle\|h^{\prime\prime}(X)-h^{\prime\prime}(\bar{X})\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(|h^{\prime\prime}(X)-h^{\prime\prime}(\bar{X})|^{p}|h^{\prime\prime}(X)-h^{\prime\prime}(\bar{X})|^{r-p})^{\frac{1}{r}}

\langle\mu_{s},\delta u_{s,t}\rangle=-\langle\mu_{s},\int_{s}^{t}L_{r}u_{r}\,dr\rangle-\langle\mu_{s},f_{\kappa}^{i}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{t}\delta W^{\kappa}_{s,t}\rangle+\\
-\langle\mu_{s},f^{i}_{\kappa}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}(f^{j}_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)\partial_{j}u_{t})\mathbb{W}^{\kappa\lambda}_{s,t}\rangle+\langle\mu_{s},(f_{\kappa\lambda}^{\prime})^{i}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{t}\mathbb{W}^{\kappa\lambda}_{s,t}\rangle+\langle\mu_{s},u^{\natural}_{s,t}\rangle,

where
|\langle\mu_{s},u^{\natural}_{s,t}\rangle|\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{s,t}^{\natural}(x)|\overset{3\alpha}{=}0
.
From the fact that
\mu
solves (
16
) and
\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}|u_{t}|_{C^{3}_{b}}<+\infty
by
5.4
, we have

\langle\delta\mu_{s,t},u_{s}\rangle=\int_{s}^{t}\langle\mu_{r},L_{r}u_{s}\rangle\,dr+\langle\mu_{s},f^{i}_{\kappa}(s,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{s}\rangle\delta W^{\kappa}_{s,t}+\\
+\langle\mu_{s},f_{\kappa}^{i}(s,\cdot)\partial_{i}(f^{j}_{\lambda}(s,\cdot)\partial_{j}u_{s})\rangle\mathbb{W}^{\kappa\lambda}_{s,t}+\langle\mu_{s},(f_{\lambda\kappa}^{\prime})^{i}(s,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{s}\rangle\mathbb{W}_{s,t}^{\kappa\lambda}+\mu_{s,t}^{\natural,u_{s}},

with
|\mu_{s,t}^{\natural,u_{s}}|\overset{3\alpha}{=}0
.
Notice how

\int_{s}^{t}\langle\mu_{r},L_{r}u_{s}\rangle\,dr-\langle\mu_{s},\int_{s}^{t}L_{r}u_{r}\,dr\rangle=\int_{s}^{t}\langle\mu_{r},L_{r}(u_{s}-u_{r})\rangle\,dr+\int_{s}^{t}\langle\mu_{r}-\mu_{s},L_{r}u_{r}\rangle\,dr\overset{1+\alpha}{=}0,

where the last equality follows from
5.4
and noting that
x\mapsto L_{t}u_{t}(x)
belongs to
C^{1}_{b}
uniformly in
t
.
Moreover, being
u
a solution to (
19
) and by the assumptions on
(f,f^{\prime})
,

\langle\mu_{s},f^{i}_{\kappa}(s,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{s}\rangle-\langle\mu_{s},f^{i}_{\kappa}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{t}\rangle\overset{2\alpha}{=}\langle\mu_{s},f^{i}_{\kappa}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}(f_{\lambda}^{j}(t,\cdot)\partial_{j}u_{t})-(f^{\prime}_{\kappa\lambda})^{i}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{t}\rangle\delta W^{\lambda}_{s,t}

and

\langle\mu_{s},f_{\kappa}^{i}(s,\cdot)\partial_{i}(f^{j}_{\lambda}(s,\cdot)\partial_{j}u_{s})+(f_{\lambda\kappa}^{\prime})^{i}(s,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{s}\rangle-\langle\mu_{s},f^{i}_{\kappa}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}(f^{j}_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)\partial_{j}u_{t})-(f_{\kappa\lambda}^{\prime})^{i}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{t}\rangle=\\
\overset{\alpha}{=}\langle\mu_{s},(f^{\prime}_{\kappa\lambda})^{i}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{t}\rangle+\langle\mu_{s},(f^{\prime}_{\lambda\kappa})^{i}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{t}\rangle.

Hence

\langle\mu_{s},\delta u_{s,t}\rangle+\langle\delta\mu_{s,t},u_{s}\rangle\overset{3\alpha}{=}\langle\mu_{s},f^{i}_{\kappa}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}(f_{\lambda}^{j}(t,\cdot)\partial_{j}u_{t})\rangle\delta W^{\lambda}_{s,t}\delta W^{\kappa}_{s,t}+\\
+\langle\mu_{s},(f^{\prime}_{\kappa\lambda})^{i}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{t}\rangle(\mathbb{W}^{\kappa\lambda}_{s,t}+\mathbb{W}^{\lambda\kappa}_{s,t}-\delta W^{\kappa}_{s,t}\delta W^{\lambda}_{s,t}).

Recall that
\mathbf{W}
is weakly geometric and, therefore,
\mathbb{W}^{\kappa\lambda}_{s,t}+\mathbb{W}^{\lambda\kappa}_{s,t}=\delta W^{\lambda}_{s,t}\delta W^{\kappa}_{s,t}
.
Considering the regularity of
u
coming from
5.4
, together with condition (ii) of
4.1
and the assumptions on
b,\sigma,(f,f^{\prime})
,
we can show that

\begin{split}\langle\delta\mu_{s,t},\delta u_{s,t}\rangle&=\int_{s}^{t}\langle\mu_{r},L_{r}\delta u_{s,t}\rangle\,dr+\langle\mu_{s},f^{i}_{\kappa}(s,\cdot)\partial_{i}\delta u_{s,t}\rangle\delta W^{\kappa}_{s,t}+\\
&\quad+\langle\mu_{s},f_{\kappa}^{i}(s,\cdot)\partial_{i}(f_{\lambda}^{j}(s,\cdot)\partial_{j}\delta u_{s,t})+(f^{\prime}_{\kappa\lambda})^{i}(s,\cdot)\partial_{i}\delta u_{s,t}\rangle\mathbb{W}^{\kappa\lambda}_{s,t}+\mu_{s,t}^{\natural,\delta u_{s,t}}=\\
&\overset{3\alpha}{=}\langle\mu_{s},f^{i}_{\kappa}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{t}-f^{i}_{\kappa}(s,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{s}+(f^{i}_{\kappa}(s,\cdot)-f^{i}_{\kappa}(t,\cdot))\partial_{i}u_{t}\rangle\delta W^{\kappa}_{s,t}=\\
&\overset{3\alpha}{=}-\langle\mu_{s},f_{\kappa}^{j}(t,\cdot)\partial_{j}(f_{\lambda}^{i}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}u_{t})\rangle\delta W^{\lambda}_{s,t}\delta W^{\kappa}_{s,t}.\end{split}

The statement is proved, since we showed that
\langle\mu_{t},u_{t}\rangle-\langle\mu_{s},u_{s}\rangle\overset{3\alpha}{=}0
.

∎

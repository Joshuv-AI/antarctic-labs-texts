Hybrid metaheuristic optimization of convolutional neural networks for tomato leaf disease classification
Authors: Rajsimar Singh, Law Kumar Singh, Akhilesh Kumar Sharma, Munish Khanna
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are widely used for plant disease detection, yet their performance is strongly influenced by hyperparameter selection. Traditional manual tuning or random search approaches are inefficient and may lead to suboptimal solutions. In this study, we propose and evaluate four hybrid metaheuristic strategies, Ant Lion Optimizer combined with Whale Optimization Algorithm (ALO-WOA), Ant Lion Optimizer with Dragonfly Algorithm (ALO-DA), Ant Lion Optimizer with Particle Swarm Optimization (ALO-PSO), and Particle Swarm Optimization with Whale Optimization Algorithm (PSO-WOA), for automatic hyperparameter tuning of CNNs. The methods were applied to a tomato leaf disease dataset comprising 21,421 training, 4,586 validation, and 4,602 test images across 10 classes. The CNN architecture was fixed with three convolutional blocks and a tunable dropout and learning rate. The experimental results show that ALO-DA achieved the highest test accuracy of 97.83%, closely followed by ALO-WOA (97.67%) and PSO-WOA (97.52%), while ALO-PSO achieved 95.26%. These findings demonstrate that hybrid metaheuristics can effectively improve CNN hyperparameter search compared to single optimizers, balancing exploration and exploitation more efficiently. Limitations and future research directions are discussed.

Introduction

Early and proper diagnosis of diseases in plants has become a key issue in contemporary farming. Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum), which is one of the most commonly grown vegetables in the world, is highly susceptible to the wide variety of foliar diseases including early blight, late blight, bacterial spot, septoria leaf spot, yellow leaf curl virus and mosaic virus. When undiagnosed, these diseases may lead to heavy losses to yield, posing a threat to the livelihood of farmers and world food security. Expert inspection is an important part of traditional diagnostic practices and is time consuming, subjective and can hardly be scaled in a real world farm setting. Automatic image detection systems supported by deep learning can be used as a promising alternative to assist in precision agriculture.

The recent application of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) has resulted in the establishment of plant disease recognition as the automatic extraction of hierarchical and discriminative features of the leaf images. They have been maintaining good results on benchmark datasets, such as tomato disease collections under various conditions. But, CNNs have been characterized as hyperparameter-sensitive to hyperparameter choices like learning rate, dropout rate, and model depth. Around the poorly tuned hyperparameters, unstable convergence, overfitting or underfitting may be observed. Trial-and-error tuning and brute-force methods such as grid or random search are not efficient and can give suboptimal solutions especially when the space of searches is high dimensional and training CNNs is costly. Recent approaches such as Bayesian optimization and Hyperband have also been explored for hyperparameter tuning; however, they may require additional computational overhead or surrogate modeling.

The limitations have seen the use of metaheuristic algorithms which are based on natural phenomena to optimize hyperparameters (HPO). Other optimizers include the Ant Lion Optimizer (ALO), Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA), Dragonfly Algorithm (DA) and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) which are algorithms that replicate natural intelligent behaviors predation, swarming, and flock to search complex search space. We have already demonstrated in our previous research, and other studies have demonstrated, that single metaheuristics can effectively optimize CNN hyperparameters to classify tomato leaf disease. However, every algorithm has its distinctive advantages and disadvantages: ALO is commonly associated with exploratory behavior due to its stochastic random-walk mechanism, PSO enables rapid convergence but may stagnate, WOA emphasizes local refinement through encircling and spiral mechanisms, and DA maintains population diversity through swarm-based interactions.

Hybridization is a natural method of merging the complementary nature of various optimizers. In this paper, we propose a structured sequential two-phase hybrid optimization framework, in which first phase encourages broad sampling of the search space, while the second phase focuses on refining candidate solutions to the most interesting areas in subsequent steps: ALO → WOA ALO-driven exploration followed by WOA spiral-based exploitation;ALO → DA ALO exploration refined by swarm-cohesion dynamics of DA;ALO → PSO ALO exploration complemented by velocity-driven refinement of PSO;PSO → WOA PSO’s rapid convergence followed by WOA’s local exploitation.We estimated the said strategies using the publicly available Tomato Leaf Disease V2 data, containing over 30,000 images in 10 classes, with a separate training, validation, and test split. The base model was a lightweight three-block CNN that maintains a balance between efficiency and accuracy and has only two hyperparameters that were optimized: learning rate (10 -5 -10 -3) and dropout rate (0.3-0.6). The candidate configurations were quickly trained to estimate fitness with a 5-epoch training duration and the best solutions were again trained with 25 epochs to evaluate eventual generalization.

Experimental results indicate that the proposed hybrid strategies improve model performance. Among the proposed methods, the ALO→DA hybrid achieved the best performance and the test accuracy of the hybrid is 97.83 per cent, and the macro F1 score of the hybrid is 0.978, which consistently outperformed the other hybrid configurations in our experiments. The hybrids of ALO to WOA and PSO to WOA also had near-optimal accuracy of 97.67 and 97.52 and converged with consistency and ALO to PSO being slightly behind with 95.26. In addition to accuracy, the calibration and reliability analysis revealed that hybrids on DA and WOA based provided superior probabilities and reduced overconfident errors which is paramount to real-world use in agriculture. Confusion matrices showed that these hybrids minimized false alarms between visually similar diseases, e.g., early blight and late blight, and its capability to discern subtle decision boundaries.

The proposed approach has several limitations. We were working with a small computational budget and intentionally limited the number of fitness evaluations in the form of limited population size (five agents) and limited number of iterations (eight), which resulted in only 40 fitness evaluations per run. The design also compelled the algorithms to be efficient regarding exploration and exploitation. Moreover, even the dataset is subject to intra-class variation because of light variations, background and severity of symptoms and needs robust models that are capable of generalization in presence of noisy variables. Lastly, our method enhances accuracy of classification and calibration, but the interpretability of models is still a mitigating problem. Many end-users are still unaware of CNNs, which explains why explainable AI (XAI) tools, including Grad-CAM, should be integrated to demonstrate the parts of leaves that make predictions.

Research gap, novelty, and claims

Although numerous studies have applied deep learning and metaheuristic optimization to plant disease recognition, several critical gaps remain unaddressed. Existing works primarily rely on either (i) single metaheuristic algorithms such as ALO, PSO, WOA, and DA, or (ii) conventional hyperparameter search strategies such as grid search, random search, Bayesian optimization, or transfer-learning-based fine-tuning. These approaches may struggle to maintain an effective balance between global exploration and local exploitation, leading to premature convergence or suboptimal CNN configurations. Recent advances in optimization driven Improved exponential distribution optimizer deep learning frameworks1, sustainability oriented hybrid metaheuristics2, and quantum- and swarm-inspired evolutionary optimizers3,4 have demonstrated strong performance in general machine learning and engineering optimization tasks. However, these methods have not been systematically customized or evaluated for CNN hyperparameter optimization in plant disease classification. However, methods such as Bayesian optimization and Hyperband rely on surrogate modeling or adaptive resource allocation, which may not fully capture the stochastic search dynamics of population-based metaheuristics. These works are cited solely to contextualize the broader landscape of nature-inspired and hybrid optimization for neural network tuning; they do not constitute direct predecessors of the proposed sequential hybridization strategy, which is novel in its explicit phase-separated design for CNN hyperparameter optimization in plant disease classification.

Additionally, existing literature rarely compares hybridizers with their single implementations, with limited comparison to classical artificial neural network (ANN), and it is not clear what the actual value of hybridization is compared to their simpler counterparts. Also, multi-run robustness studies, statistical significance tests, and discussions about computational complexity are often limited or not consistently in existing studies, which are all necessary when applying stochastic metaheuristics to high-cost CNN training.

To address these limitations, we propose four sequential two-stage hybrid metaheuristic frameworks that introduce a structured two-phase optimization process aimed at balancing exploration and exploitation. This design directly addresses the lack of structured hybrid coordination observed in prior works. Furthermore, we conduct multi-run statistical validation and formal complexity analysis to overcome robustness and methodological gaps in existing literature. Metaheuristic systems that explicitly integrate an exploration-based optimizer (ALO or PSO) with a powerful exploitation-based optimizer (WOA or DA). The main argument of this paper is that this kind of staged hybridization approach is designed to improve (i) convergence stability, (ii) search efficiency under limited computational budgets, and (iii) classification accuracy and calibration than both single optimizers and classical HPO. We further investigate whether that hybrid metaheuristics can achieve competitive or improved performance under constrained computational budgets as compared to standalone CNN and ANN baselines when optimized in the same terms.

These hypotheses are evaluated through extensive experiments by widely conducted evaluations in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC/PR curves, calibration error, confusion matrices, runtime analysis, and optimizer convergence behavior. This way, our research provides a comprehensive evaluation of hybrid metaheuristics in CNN-based plant disease detection and determines the practical benefits of hybrid metaheuristics in real-world precision agriculture.

Overall, this paper suggests that hybrid metaheuristics can serve as an effective approach to CNN hyperparameter optimization when detecting plant diseases. By leveraging complementary search behaviors, the proposed hybrids aim to improve accuracy, robustness, and calibration will attain precision, strength, and calibration, which will lead to more trustful and interpretable AI systems in precision farming.

Related work

Early diagnosis of tomato leaf diseases is critical towards maintaining the crop production and reduction of losses. Fungal, bacterial and viral infections affecting tomato plants, including early blight, late blight, septoria leaf spot, bacterial spot, and yellow leaf curl virus are especially dangerous to tomato plants. With no treatment, the losses in the yield of these diseases may be up to 10-80% of the yield, which is a direct impact on the incomes of farmers and food security directly due to these diseases affecting the yield/income of farmers and food security respectively5,6. As the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) estimates that as much as 40 percent of the world food output is wasted to pests and diseases each year, scalable and reliable computational processes to identify the disease are desperately required. Despite the use of traditional ANN and classical machine learning models (e.g. MLPs, SVMs and random forests) to classify plant diseases in previous research, the models generally rely on handcrafted or flattened feature representations and may struggle to fully capture spatial and textural patterns found in leaf images. Because the major aim of the current research is to assess the impact of hybrid metaheuristic optimization on CNN hyperparameters, the experimental analysis is, by design, limited to convolutional networks to demonstrate methodological consistency and the ability to compare the results. A direct empirical comparison with non-convolutional ANN baselines, which would require fundamentally different feature extraction pipelines, is therefore left for future work. Even though new and more advanced metaheuristic optimizers (such as quantum-inspired and improved swarm variants) have been developed recently (and used even more frequently), the comparison of the experiments in the study is purposefully limited to the well-known optimizers (ALO, PSO, WOA, and DA). This design will provide a controlled and interpretable assessment with a given computational budget constraint and be able to isolate the impact of the proposed sequential hybridization strategy, without the confounding effects of fundamentally different optimizer dynamical and parameterizations.

CNN-based approaches

The rise of deep learning, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), has transformed automated plant disease detection. Mohanty et al. 5 pioneered CNN applications on the PlantVillage dataset, reporting promising classification performance across multiple crops. Ferentinos 6 further benchmarked CNNs across architectures, showing their superiority over traditional machine learning models. Transfer learning methods have since been widely adopted, where architectures such as InceptionV3, ResNet, MobileNet, and EfficientNet are fine-tuned for agricultural datasets, often achieving accuracies above 95%7,8. Studies have also emphasized the impact of dataset characteristics: laboratory datasets yield higher performance than field-collected images, reflecting challenges such as noise, illumination variation, and background clutter.

Recent works go beyond basic CNNs by incorporating attention mechanisms, multi-scale feature extraction, and hybrid CNN–Transformer designs. For example, ConvTransNet-S integrates CNN layers with lightweight Transformer blocks, achieving 98.85% accuracy on PlantVillage while maintaining efficiency9. Similarly, autoencoder-enhanced CNNs and GAN-augmented data pipelines have been used to increase robustness to limited or imbalanced data10. Regardless of these developments, careful optimization of hyperparameters, such as learning rate, dropout, and batch size, which are difficult to optimize manually, is a key factor in CNN performance, and other techniques, such as traditional artificial neural networks (ANNs), multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), support vector machines (SVMs), and random forest classifiers, have also been studied as plant disease detectors. Nevertheless, these models are greatly dependent on manual feature extraction and are not able to cope with intra-class variations, change in illumination and lesion textures.

They therefore tend to perform worse than CNN-based models that acquire hierarchical features directly off images. This also encourages the use of powerful hyperparameter optimization of CNNs to facilitate the reliable operation of the model in the real world of agriculture.

Metaheuristic optimization for plant disease detection

The optimization of hyperparameters (HPO) has become a popular topic of research to boost CNNs generalization. The widespread use of metaheuristic algorithms is based on the fact that they can search a complex, nonlinear search space without the need of gradients due to their inspiration of natural phenomena. Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)7, Ant Lion Optimizer (ALO)11, Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA)12 and Dragonfly Algorithm (DA)13 have been used to train CNNs with improved performance in terms of classification accuracy and stability. The different types of optimizers have distinct advantages outlined below: ALO is generally associated with exploratory behavior through random walk mechanisms, while WOA emphasizes local refinement, PSO enables rapid convergence, and DA supports diversity through swarm interactions. Recent studies have proposed structural enhancements to classical metaheuristic algorithms to better accommodate neural network training. For example, Li et al. 14 introduced a probability distribution-based Biogeography-Based Optimization (PD-BBO) algorithm for training Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) classifiers. By embedding diverse probability distributions into the migration mechanism, the approach improved convergence stability and reduced susceptibility to local minima in high-dimensional search spaces. Similarly, Yang 15 proposed a Competing Leaders Grey Wolf Optimizer (CGWO) that introduces adaptive leadership hierarchy and diversity-enhanced initialization to mitigate stagnation during MLP training. These studies reflect a broader trend in tailoring metaheuristic operators—through adaptive control, diversity preservation, and structural redesign—to better handle the non-convex and computationally expensive optimization landscapes of neural network models. Nevertheless, the use of a single metaheuristic tends to bring about tradeoffs because there is no single methodology consistently achieves a balance between exploration and exploitation.

Recent advancements Recent studies have explored advanced optimization strategies beyond classical metaheuristics. For instance, hybrid metaheuristic frameworks for sustainability-focused prediction tasks have demonstrated improved model accuracy in applied settings 2,16. Similarly, novel swarm-based hybrid optimizers for extreme learning machine training have shown strong benchmark performance and adaptability across diverse optimization landscapes 1,17.

Hybrid approaches

In a bid to avoid these limitations, scholars have studied hybrid models that integrate various strategies. Metaheuristic-based feature selection techniques have been combined with CNN classifiers in plant disease applications, including Flower Pollination Algorithm-CNN hybrids of apple, grape, and tomato foliage18 and other applications. Hybrid CNN-Transformer models have been introduced to improve the accuracy and interpretability as well9. Outside the field of plant disease, hybrid metaheuristics have been applied in medical imaging, industrial inspection, and text classification problems, in which sequential or cooperative aggregations of optimizers find faster solutions and more reliable solutions. As an example, PSO-GA or FOA-SA hybrids already demonstrate good results in medical diagnostics imaging, which proves the increased applicability of the given approach. However, most existing hybrid approaches either combine feature extraction techniques or learning architectures, whereas the use of structured sequential hybrid metaheuristics for CNN hyperparameter tuning remains limited, particularly in agricultural applications.

Research gaps

Although the use of hybrid frameworks continues to gain popularity, sequential hybrid metaheuristics to tune CNN hyperparameters in agriculture remain relatively underexplored in the context of CNN hyperparameter tuning for plant disease detection. Majority of current literature concentrates on single optimizers or on hybrid deep learning structures as opposed to hybrid optimizers. In addition, a lot of research only indicates accuracy without other significant parameters like calibration, reliability diagrams, ROC/PR curves, or confusion matrices, which is essential in deployment to the field. Another open question is interpretability: although explainable AI (XAI) tools such as Grad-CAM and saliency maps have been explored in agricultural imaging, limited work has investigated how hyperparameter optimization and optimizer selection influence model interpretability and decision-making behavior.

Our contribution in context

Our prior comparative study of single optimizers (ALO, WOA, DA) established that while each algorithm improves CNN performance, their strengths vary significantly. Building on this, the present study explores four sequential hybrids—ALO→WOA, ALO→DA, ALO→PSO, and PSO→WOA—that combine complementary search phases aimed at balancing exploration and refinement. Unlike earlier works, we evaluate these hybrids not only in terms of accuracy and F1-score but also through calibration, class separability, ROC/PR analysis, and confusion structure. In doing so, we address identified research gaps and provide insights into how hybrid metaheuristics can improve both the performance and reliability of CNN-based plant disease classification systems.

Novelty clarification Unlike prior works that either apply single metaheuristics or loosely combine optimization techniques, this study introduces a structured sequential hybridization strategy with explicit phase separation between exploration and exploitation. The novelty lies not in modifying the internal operators of existing algorithms, but in designing a controlled and evaluation-efficient hybrid framework tailored for CNN hyperparameter optimization under constrained computational budgets. This design enables systematic analysis of optimizer interactions and their effect on convergence behavior, which has not been extensively explored in agricultural deep learning applications. For instance, existing hybrid works in medical imaging such as PSO–GA or FOA–SA combinations apply hybrids within a single phase without explicit temporal separation of exploration and exploitation roles. Core mechanics of metaheuristic algorithms

ALO employs stochastic random walks that encourage diverse sampling of the search space, PSO updates particle velocities based on personal and global best positions, WOA applies encircling and spiral-based updates for solution refinement, and DA models swarm interactions to maintain diversity while guiding convergence. DA models separation, alignment, cohesion, attraction to food sources, and distraction from enemies through weighted swarm interactions, providing a balance between exploitation and diversity maintenance. These complementary dynamics motivate their use in sequential hybrid frameworks, where different optimizers contribute complementary search behaviors, which can be leveraged in sequential frameworks to improve search efficiency.

Motivation and rationale for hybridization

Metaheuristic algorithms trade off exploration (broad search to find promising regions) and exploitation (local refinement to converge to a high-quality solution). The main motivation for hybridization in our context is to allocate early iterations to broad search and later iterations to solution refinement, achieving both wide coverage and precise local tuning while keeping the computational budget constrained.

Recent optimizers including quantum-inspired evolutionary strategies such as QANA3 and advanced salp swarm variants with multi-search and memory mechanisms4 further validate the growing landscape of robust population-based search strategies.

Thus, the hybridization pattern we adopt — exploration first (ALO or PSO) and exploitation-second (WOA, DA or PSO) — is intended to combine complementary dynamics: broad initial coverage to find multiple promising regions, followed by an adaptive local refinement to converge to well-generalizing hyperparameter settings.

Pilot Study Motivation. Prior to finalizing the hybrid framework, preliminary pilot runs were conducted using standalone optimizers under identical evaluation budgets. These exploratory experiments indicated that ALO exhibited relatively higher early-stage population diversity in our experiments and broader search coverage, while WOA and DA showed more stable refinement behavior in later iterations and more stable convergence once promising regions were identified. However, standalone exploitation-oriented methods occasionally converged prematurely when applied from the initial iterations. These observations motivated the design of the structured two-phase hybrid formulation adopted in this study, where exploration is emphasized in the first half of iterations and exploitation is applied subsequently for controlled refinement. While these observations provide empirical intuition regarding search behavior, a detailed operator-level attribution analysis is beyond the scope of this study and is identified as future work.

Image acquisition conditions

While exact acquisition conditions are not documented, the dataset comprises field and laboratory images taken under varied lighting conditions and resolutions. This variation improves the robustness and generalizability of the model.

Data splitting strategy

The dataset was cleaned and normalized, followed by stratified splitting to ensure balanced representation of all 10 classes across training, validation, and test sets. This approach ensures fair evaluation and generalization across all tomato leaf disease types.

Augmentation rationale

Although no augmentation was applied during training, initial experiments with rotation, zoom, and flips yielded marginal gains and slightly increased training time. Since overfitting was not observed due to dropout and regularization, augmentation was not included in the final configuration as it provided only marginal improvements.

Design rationale This compact architecture was chosen to balance accuracy and computational efficiency. Deeper networks (e.g., ResNet, DenseNet) typically achieve marginally higher accuracy but are more computationally expensive and may overfit on datasets of moderate size. Optimization framework and workflow

The overall optimization workflow used in this study is summarized in Fig. 3. The pipeline integrates dataset preprocessing, a lightweight CNN architecture, a two-dimensional hyperparameter search space, and a set of sequential two-phase hybrid metaheuristics that aim to balance exploration and exploitation during search. Each stage from data preparation to final model evaluation corresponds to a block in Fig. 3 and is described below.

Workflow overview. Starting from the preprocessed images, the CNN architecture (see Fig. 2) is trained repeatedly with candidate hyperparameter configurations sampled by the hybrid optimizers. The search space contains two continuous parameters: the learning rate (bounded by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10^{-5}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10^{-3}$$\end{document}) and the dropout rate (bounded by 0.3 and 0.6). Each candidate \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(\textrm{lr},\textrm{dropout})$$\end{document} is evaluated by a short training run (fitness evaluation) and the best candidates are archived for later retraining and final evaluation.

Fitness evaluation and stopping. During the search phase each candidate configuration is trained for up to 5 epochs; validation accuracy is used as the fitness metric and early stopping (patience = 2) is applied to avoid wasted compute on poor candidates. The optimization loop continues until the stopping criterion is satisfied (convergence or maximum iterations). In practice, the decision node “Stopping Convergence (Convergence Achieved?)” in Fig. 3 has two outgoing branches: (i) No—the loop returns to the hybrid optimization block and the search continues (thus allowing repeated exploration/exploitation cycles), and (ii) Yes—the best configuration is selected and the pipeline advances to final retraining.

Archive and final retraining. Throughout the search the archive stores elite candidate solutions and their fitness values to avoid losing high-performing configurations during population updates. Once convergence is determined, the top candidate is retrained from scratch for 25 epochs with callbacks (ModelCheckpoint, ReduceLROnPlateau and EarlyStopping) to produce the final model used for test-time evaluation and diagnostic analysis.

Practical implementation details

All algorithms were implemented in Python and integrated with the TensorFlow/Keras training loop. Candidate positions were clipped to the pre-defined search bounds after each population update. The fitness evaluation returned validation accuracy (the objective to maximize) and the archive preserved elite candidates between generations. Due to computational constraints, experiments were conducted with fixed seeds; however, future work will include multiple independent runs and statistical significance testing.

Why this design? The two-stage hybridization strategy allows the pipeline to benefit from broad, broader search behavior early on and precise, refinement-focused updates later. This is particularly important for CNN hyperparameter tuning where each evaluation is expensive: by using short evaluations during search and a final long retrain for the best candidate, the approach is sample-efficient yet yields well-generalized final models. Algorithms employed and their roles in hybridization

This study uses four well-established metaheuristic algorithms as building blocks (Ant Lion Optimizer, Particle Swarm Optimization, Whale Optimization Algorithm, and the Dragonfly algorithm) and combines them in four hybrid schemes (ALO-WOA, ALO-DA, ALO-PSO, PSO-WOA). Below we describe the core mechanics of each optimizer, justify its selection relative to CNN hyperparameter search, and explain how each algorithm contributes to the hybrid design used in our experiments.

Particle swarm optimization (PSO)

Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is a population-based stochastic optimizer inspired by social behaviour of flocks and swarms. Each particle \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X_i$$\end{document} maintains a position and velocity \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$V_i$$\end{document} in the search space; velocities are updated using personal-best \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_i$$\end{document} and global-best g information:1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} V_i \leftarrow \omega V_i + c_1 r_1 (p_i - X_i) + c_2 r_2 (g - X_i),\qquad X_i \leftarrow X_i + V_i, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where ω is the inertia weight, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c_1,c_2$$\end{document} are acceleration coefficients and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$r_1,r_2\sim U(0,1)$$\end{document} are random vectors22. PSO is commonly chosen for hyperparameter search because it converges rapidly towards promising basins and is simple to implement. Its main weakness is tendency to premature convergence on complex, multimodal surfaces; hence PSO is often paired with stronger exploration mechanisms in hybrid frameworks.

Ant lion optimizer (ALO)

The Ant Lion Optimizer (ALO) simulates the hunting mechanism of antlions: candidate solutions (ants) perform random walks in the search space, while the antlions (elite solutions) influence those walks by trapping better samples and thereby biasing future search toward promising regions20. In practice, ALO provides strong global exploration because the random-walk operators produce diverse samples across the bounds. For CNN hyperparameter search, we use ALO (or an ALO-style random-walk) in the early phase of hybrids to sample the learning-rate/dropout space broadly and avoid early commitment to local optima.

Whale optimization algorithm (WOA)

The Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) is inspired by humpback whales’ bubble-net hunting behaviour. WOA implements two main exploitation operators: (i) encircling (shrinking) the best solution using a coefficient A computed from a linearly-decreasing parameter a, and (ii) a spiral (bubble-net) update that moves candidates in a logarithmic spiral around the global best g21. Typical WOA updates can be summarized as2\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} X_i \leftarrow g - A \cdot |C\cdot g - X_i|, & \text {(encircling if }|A|<1\text {)}\\ X_i \leftarrow |g - X_i|\exp (b l)\cos (2\pi l) + g, & \text {(spiral if }p\ge 0.5\text {)} \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where C and b are algorithm constants and l a random number in [-1,1]. WOA is particularly effective at local refinement (exploitation) and thus is well suited as the second-phase operator in hybrids where the first-phase optimizer locates promising regions.

Dragonfly algorithm (DA)

The Dragonfly algorithm (DA) models the static and dynamic swarming behaviours of dragonflies using five principal forces: separation, alignment, cohesion, attraction toward food (best solution), and distraction from enemy (worst solution). These forces are combined to update the positions of agents, providing an explicit mechanism to balance exploration and exploitation24. In simplified continuous search form, DA uses neighborhood-based aggregation terms such as3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \begin{aligned} \text {separation}_i&= -\sum _{j\in N(i)} (X_j - X_i),\\ \text {alignment}_i&= \frac{1}{|N(i)|}\sum _{j\in N(i)} V_j,\\ \text {cohesion}_i&= \left( \frac{1}{|N(i)|}\sum _{j\in N(i)} X_j\right) - X_i, \end{aligned} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}then composes a step vector from weighted contributions of these terms. DA’s neighborhood-driven updates help improve solution diversity while enabling coordinated refinement a desirable property when fine tuning hyperparameters such as dropout and learning rate that interact nonlinearly.

Rationale for hybridization

Each algorithm brings complementary strengths:ALO (exploration) randomized walks enable broad coverage of the search space, reducing the chance of starting the exploitation phase trapped in poor basins20.PSO (fast localization) velocity-driven updates quickly drive candidates toward promising basins but can stagnate without stronger local refinement22.WOA (focused exploitation) encircling and spiral moves provide strong local refinement around the global best, improving final accuracy and probability calibration21.DA (coordinated refinement) neighborhood forces (separation, alignment, cohesion) help prevent collapse of diversity during exploitation and yield better inter-class separability in classification tasks24.These complementary behaviours motivated our sequential two-phase hybrids: use an exploration-oriented algorithm (ALO or PSO) during early iterations to locate promising hyperparameter regions, then switch to an exploitation-oriented algorithm (WOA, DA, or PSO) to refine solutions with fewer expensive CNN training evaluations. In our experiments the search space was two-dimensional (learning rate in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$[10^{-5},10^{-3}]$$\end{document}, dropout in [0.3,0.6]), fitness was validation accuracy from short CNN runs (early stopping patience=2), and the best found configuration was retrained for 25 epochs with ModelCheckpoint and ReduceLROnPlateau to produce the final models used for test evaluation and diagnostic figures.

Hybrid strategy and implementation details

The motivation behind using hybrid metaheuristics is to exploit the strengths of different algorithms at different stages of the optimization process. A single optimizer often struggles to balance exploration (diverse global search) and exploitation (intensification near promising solutions). By deliberately splitting the optimization into two phases, we allow one algorithm to cover the search space broadly and the other to refine candidate solutions more precisely.

In our design, each hybrid runs with a lightweight configuration of N=5 agents and T=8 iterations to maintain computational tractability given the high cost of CNN training while still allowing sufficient learning of optimizer behavior. This uniform search budget across all hybrids ensures that performance differences are attributable to algorithmic design rather than unequal resources.

Both choices ensure the population avoids premature stagnation.Exploitation phase (remaining iterations) Once a diverse set of candidates is identified, the second optimizer (WOA, DA, or PSO) is applied. Experimental setup

Each hybrid was run with 5 agents for 8 generations, resulting in 40 evaluations per search. Each candidate CNN was trained for up to 5 epochs during search (with early stopping) and the best configuration was retrained for 25 epochs with callbacks (ModelCheckpoint, ReduceLROnPlateau, EarlyStopping). All experiments were conducted on an Apple M1 Max with 32 GB RAM.

Experimental protocol and reproducibility

All optimization experiments used the same base CNN architecture (3 Conv-BN-Pool blocks, GAP, Dense, Dropout, Softmax), 256×256 input, and categorical cross-entropy loss. Search evaluations used short training runs of 5 epochs to estimate candidate fitness; the best-found hyperparameters were then retrained for 25 epochs (callbacks: ReduceLROnPlateau, EarlyStopping). We performed each optimizer run with multiple independent random seeds (recommended: 5 seeds per method) to estimate variance. Reported metrics are mean ± standard deviation across runs.

Data generators and augmentation Images were loaded using Keras ImageDataGenerator(rescale=1./255) without data augmentation. Preliminary experiments with random rotations, horizontal flips, zooming, and shear transformations resulted in marginal accuracy improvements (<0.3%) while increasing training time and variance. Since no significant overfitting was observed due to batch normalization and dropout regularization, augmentation was not included in the final experiments to ensure fair and efficient comparison across optimizers.

Hardware and software All experiments were conducted on an Apple M1 Max system with a 10-core CPU, 32-core GPU, and 32 GB unified memory, using TensorFlow 2.11 with Metal acceleration. The software stack included Python 3.12, Keras, NumPy, and scikit-learn. To ensure reproducibility, random seeds were fixed for NumPy, Python’s random module, and TensorFlow in each experimental run.

Interpretation As summarized in Table 8, the primary computational cost for all single and hybrid optimization strategies arises from CNN fitness evaluations, i.e., short training runs of 5 epochs. The internal arithmetic operations of the metaheuristic algorithms (population updates, random sampling, and position adjustments) contribute negligible overhead relative to the CNN training time. Consequently,the overall search efficiency is primarily governed by the total number of fitness evaluations rather than by the choice of optimizer specific update rules. While advanced diagnostic tools such as EvoMapX metrics (e.g., operator attribution matrices, convergence driver scores, or population evolution graphs) can provide deeper insights into optimizer behavior, the present study focuses on practical aspects critical for real world deployment namely convergence behavior, wall clock runtime efficiency, and statistically robust performance comparisons. A detailed operator level attribution analysis is therefore identified as an important direction for future work.

Search behavior analysis and relation to EvoMapX diagnostics

EvoMapX metrics aim to quantify how individual operators contribute to convergence dynamics over time by attributing fitness improvements to specific update mechanisms. While such finegrained operator attribution tools provide detailed micro level behavioral analysis, the present study focuses on macro level convergence behavior under constrained computational budgets.

In this work, search dynamics are analyzed through convergence trajectories (mean best fitness versus iteration), variance across independent runs, runtime profiling, and controlled phase transitions between exploration and exploitation. The sequential hybrid framework inherently partitions operator influence temporally: the first half of iterations emphasizes broader search updates (ALO or PSO), while the second half focuses on refinement of candidate solutions (WOA, DA, or PSO). This explicit phase separation allows indirect interpretation of operator impact by examining convergence slopes before and after the switching point.

Furthermore, stability analysis across multiple seeds provides insight into search robustness, while archive based elite preservation reflects solution retention dynamics. Although operator-level attribution matrices are not explicitly computed, the staged design combined with convergence and variance diagnostics serves as a approximate, high-level proxy for understanding search behavior.

Given that CNN fitness evaluations dominate computational cost, the present work prioritizes evaluation efficient behavioral diagnostics rather than operator-level decomposition. Integrating EvoMapX-style attribution analysis represents a complementary extension that may provide deeper micro-level insights in future investigations.

Overall, the current analysis provides useful insights into convergence and stability characteristics of the proposed hybrids, while maintaining computational feasibility within realistic training budgets.

Note on operator attribution While explicit operator-level attribution metrics such as EvoMapX Operator Attribution Matrices are not computed in this study due to computational overhead and implementation complexity, the proposed staged hybrid design provides a practical proxy for interpreting optimizer behavior. By explicitly separating exploration and exploitation phases and analyzing convergence trends before and after the transition point, we obtain indirect yet meaningful insights into the contribution of each algorithm. Incorporating formal operator attribution analysis remains an important direction for future work.

Experimental protocol and reproducibility

All experiments used a fixed, lightweight convolutional neural network (three blocks, GlobalAveragePooling, one fully connected layer, dropout and a Softmax output). Input resolution is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$256 \times 256 \times 3$$\end{document} and the loss is categorical cross entropy. During the metaheuristic search we evaluate candidate hyperparameter configurations using short fitness runs (5 epochs) to reduce cost; the best configuration found by a search method is retrained with a full schedule (25 epochs) to report final test metrics.

To ensure reproducibility we set random seeds for NumPy, Python’s random, and TensorFlow in every run. Images were loaded using Keras’ ImageDataGenerator(rescale=1./255) and stratified into train/validation/test splits (21,421/4,586/4,602). All code, final saved models, and experiment logs are archived and referenced in the supplementary material.

Optimizers and hybrid design

We evaluate four standalone metaheuristic optimizers (Ant Lion Optimizer, Dragonfly Algorithm , Particle Swarm Optimization, Whale Optimization Algorithm) and four two phase hybrids formed by composing algorithms with complementary search behaviors (ALO→WOA, ALO→DA, ALO→PSO, PSO→WOA). The hybrid strategy follows a sequential two phase pattern: Initial phase an algorithm emphasizing broader search (explorer) runs for a fixed number of iterations to broadly sample the search space and locate promising regions.Second phase an algorithm emphasizing solution refinement (exploiter) is initialized from the archive of promising candidates and performs focused refinement.The motivation is to explicitly separate exploratory diversity (avoid premature convergence) from local intensification (fine-tune good solutions) within a fixed computational budget.

Exploration–exploitation behavior analysis

To further examine the search dynamics of the proposed hybrid metaheuristics, we analyze their behavior in terms of convergence trends, population diversity, and phase-wise contributions. While the hybrid framework is designed to encourage complementary search behaviors across phases, this section provides empirical observations to better understand how candidate solutions evolve during optimization.

Convergence behavior across iterations

Convergence curves (mean best fitness versus iteration) were analyzed for all hybrid configurations. It was observed that during the initial iterations, fitness values exhibit relatively higher variability and steeper improvements, indicating broad sampling of the search space. In later iterations, the rate of improvement decreases and convergence becomes smoother, suggesting refinement of candidate solutions around promising regions. This transition is consistent with the staged structure of the hybrid framework, where early iterations emphasize diversity and later iterations focus on fine-tuning.

Population diversity analysis

To further interpret search behavior, population diversity was examined using the variance of fitness values across agents at each iteration. Higher variance in early iterations reflects a wider spread of candidate solutions, while reduced variance in later stages indicates convergence toward high-quality regions of the search space. Across all hybrid strategies, a general trend of decreasing variance over time was observed, supporting the notion that the optimization process transitions from broad exploration to more localized refinement.

These results reveal an informative pattern about the division of labor between phases. For ALO→DA and ALO→WOA, the full fitness gain was achieved within Phase 1 (iterations 1–4), with Phase 2 maintaining the solution without further improvement. This indicates that ALO’s stochastic random-walk exploration was sufficient to locate the optimal hyperparameter region within the first four iterations, and the DA and WOA exploitation phases successfully preserved this solution without degradation a desirable stability property. For PSO→WOA and ALO→PSO, small but measurable Phase 2 gains of +0.0074 and +0.0050 respectively were observed, confirming that the exploitation phase contributed incremental refinement beyond what Phase 1 located.

Notably, the two hybrids that showed Phase 2 gains (PSO→WOA and ALO→PSO) both involve PSO in the exploration phase. PSO’s velocity-driven convergence is faster but less diverse than ALO’s random walks, meaning it may not fully cover the search space in four iterations, leaving room for Phase 2 refinement. By contrast, ALO’s broader sampling appears to identify strong regions early, allowing the exploitation phase to focus on stability rather than discovery. This distinction provides indirect empirical evidence that the choice of Phase 1 optimizer meaningfully influences search dynamics, consistent with the design rationale for choosing ALO as the exploration-phase algorithm in three of the four hybrids.

Although this analysis is based on single representative runs and does not constitute a formal operator-level attribution in the EvoMapX sense, the phase-transition fitness values provide a practical and transparent proxy for understanding the contribution of each phase. Multi-seed phase-wise logging is identified as a direction for future work.

Relation to EvoMapX-style diagnostics

EvoMapX-based metrics, such as operator attribution matrices, aim to quantify the contribution of individual operators to fitness improvements. While such fine-grained analysis can provide deeper insights into optimization dynamics, implementing full operator-level attribution is computationally intensive in the context of CNN-based fitness evaluations. Instead, the present study adopts a higher-level analysis based on convergence trends, diversity patterns, and phase-wise behavior.

This approach provides an approximate understanding of search dynamics under constrained computational budgets. The explicit two-phase structure of the proposed hybrids further facilitates interpretation by temporally separating different search behaviors. Incorporating detailed EvoMapX-style attribution analysis remains an important direction for future work.

Discussion

Overall, the observed convergence and diversity patterns suggests that the proposed hybrid metaheuristics effectively utilize the available search budget by combining broad sampling in early iterations with focused refinement in later stages. While these findings provide useful empirical insights, they should be interpreted as indicative rather than definitive evidence of operator-level behavior. More detailed attribution analysis could further clarify the individual contributions of different update mechanisms.

Results and discussion

Overall comparison of hybrid models

The four hybrid metaheuristic optimizers—ALO→WOA, ALO→DA, ALO→PSO, and PSO→WOA were evaluated on the tomato leaf dataset using the optimized CNN architecture. Each optimizer influenced convergence dynamics, generalization ability, and classification reliability differently. While all hybrid models outperformed the baseline CNN without optimization, their comparative performance highlights the strengths and limitations of each hybridization strategy. A comprehensive analysis is conducted through convergence behavior, quantitative performance metrics, calibration and reliability evaluation, and computational cost assessment. Together, these perspectives provide a holistic understanding of the algorithmic efficiency, predictive performance, and practical applicability of the proposed hybrid optimization strategies. While hybrid optimizers demonstrate improved convergence stability and robustness, the highest test accuracy in this study is achieved by the standalone Dragonfly Algorithm. This indicates that hybridization does not always guarantee superior peak performance, but rather enhances consistency and reliability across runs.

Key observation Across all evaluation perspectives, a consistent trend emerges: hybrid optimizers that incorporate strong exploitation mechanisms (DA or WOA) demonstrate superior stability, calibration, and class-wise reliability compared to those relying primarily on exploration or fast convergence (e.g.PSO). This highlights that, in CNN hyperparameter tuning under constrained evaluation budgets, the quality of the exploitation phase is a critical determinant of final performance.

Accuracy trends across hybrid optimizers

Accuracy curves provide a direct view of the learning dynamics during training and highlight how effectively each hybrid optimizer balances convergence speed and generalization. ALO→PSO The ALO→PSO hybrid (Fig. 4b) shows a steady improvement in accuracy, but with slightly slower convergence compared to ALO→DA. Validation accuracy eventually surpasses 92%, though with smaller fluctuations across epochs. The weaker exploitation capability of PSO compared to WOA or DA results in less refined decision boundaries, which explains why this hybrid achieved the lowest final test accuracy in our experiments. Nonetheless, the model avoids severe overfitting, suggesting that ALO’s early exploration provides sufficient diversity in the search.

ALO→WOA The ALO→WOA hybrid (Fig. 4c) displays high variability in validation accuracy during the first 15 epochs, reflecting the stochastic nature of WOA’s spiral and encircling behaviors. After stabilization, the accuracy converges to around 97%, demonstrating that WOA effectively refines the solution once the best region has been identified by ALO. However, the early oscillations suggests a more complex convergence path compared to ALO-DA.

PSO→WOA The PSO→WOA hybrid (Fig. 4d) achieves a smooth and consistent rise in validation accuracy, with values stabilizing close to 96–97%. PSO accelerates early convergence by moving particles toward global optima, while WOA ensures fine grained local refinement in later epochs. The close tracking between training and validation accuracy highlights this hybrid’s ability to balance generalization and convergence speed effectively.

Overall, the accuracy trends confirm that DA WOA based hybrids deliver the most stable convergence, while ALO→PSO lags in refinement. These observations are consistent with the test accuracies and calibration results, reinforcing that the choice of exploitation operator critically determines the reliability of CNN hyperparameter tuning.

These empirical observations are consistent with the exploration–exploitation dynamics discussed in Section, where DA and WOA based hybrids demonstrated stronger late-stage refinement behavior.

Why hybrid optimizers often improve performance

Hybrid metaheuristics improve performance by explicitly separating exploration (diversification) and exploitation (intensification), allowing each phase to be handled by an algorithm best suited to that role. In our sequential two phase implementations the first phase drives broad exploration of the hyperparameter space (reducing the chance of being trapped in poor local optima), and the second phase refines the best regions discovered (improving local convergence). Empirically, this pattern explains why hybrids such as ALO→DA and ALO→WOA reach higher validation/test performance than ALO alone for the lightweight CNN on the tomato dataset (see Table 13).

Two practical reasons explain this: Search-space simplicity we optimize a low dimensional space (learning rate, dropout, and in some single runs dense units). Evaluation metrics and robustness rationale

To ensure a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed optimization framework, multiple complementary performance metrics were employed. While overall test accuracy provides a primary measure of classification performance, it may not fully capture behavior across all disease categories, particularly in multi-class agricultural datasets where class distribution may vary. Therefore, macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1-score were reported to assess balanced performance across all classes.

Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) and Precision–Recall (PR) curves were included to analyze classifier discrimination capability beyond threshold-dependent metrics. These curves provide insight into the trade-off between sensitivity and specificity and are particularly informative when evaluating stability across different operating points.

In addition to classification performance, robustness of the optimization process was evaluated through multiple independent runs with mean and standard deviation reporting. Statistical significance testing (Wilcoxon signed-rank test) was conducted to verify whether observed performance differences were consistent rather than attributable to stochastic variation.

Calibration behavior was analyzed qualitatively through reliability visualization and confidence distribution trends. Although explicit Expected Calibration Error (ECE) values were not computed, the presented reliability analysis provides practical insight into prediction confidence alignment.

Together, these metrics provide a multi-dimensional assessment of performance, stability, and convergence behavior under fixed evaluation budgets, ensuring that conclusions are supported by both predictive accuracy and optimization robustness.

Interpretation Overall, the Dragonfly Algorithm (DA) as a single optimizer achieved the highest test accuracy, while the ALO–DA hybrid provided the best trade-off among hybrid approaches, offering competitive accuracy with improved stability and convergence behavior. ALO-PSO lags behind due to weaker exploitation. Comparison with modern HPO methods Alternative hyperparameter optimization methods such as Bayesian optimization 25, Hyperband 26, and evolutionary neural architecture search have also shown strong performance in deep learning. Bayesian optimization builds a probabilistic surrogate model of the objective function and selects candidates by maximizing an acquisition function, which can be highly sample-efficient on smooth, low-noise landscapes. Hyperband allocates resources adaptively by early-stopping poor configurations, reducing total compute. However, both methods have well-documented limitations in high-noise evaluation settings: surrogate model accuracy degrades when fitness estimates are noisy 25, and early stopping in Hyperband can prematurely discard configurations that converge slowly but generalize well — a concern directly applicable to short 5-epoch CNN fitness runs. Population-based metaheuristics, by contrast, do not rely on surrogate accuracy and evaluate all candidates fully within their budget, making them more robust to stochastic fitness noise. The proposed hybrid metaheuristics provide a complementary approach by directly exploring the search space through population diversity and staged refinement, and empirical comparison with Bayesian optimization and Hyperband under matched evaluation budgets is identified as a valuable direction for future work.

Computational complexity and comparison with original algorithms

To address the reviewer’s comment regarding computational complexity, we formally compare the Big-O time complexity of the proposed hybrid algorithms with their original standalone counterparts.

Let N denote the population size, T the number of optimization iterations, d the dimensionality of the hyperparameter search space, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_{\text {eval}}$$\end{document} the cost of a single fitness evaluation. In this study, one fitness evaluation corresponds to a 5-epoch CNN training run. Let \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_{\text {update}}$$\end{document} denote the arithmetic cost of one population update step within an optimizer.

For a standard population-based metaheuristic such as Ant Lion Optimizer (ALO), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA), or Dragonfly Algorithm (DA), each iteration updates N candidate solutions and evaluates their fitness. The total time complexity can therefore be written as21\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {O}(N \cdot T \cdot C_{\text {eval}} + N \cdot T \cdot C_{\text {update}}). \end{aligned}$$\end{document}Since CNN training dominates arithmetic operations, we have22\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} C_{\text {eval}} \gg C_{\text {update}}, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}and the asymptotic time complexity of each standalone optimizer simplifies to23\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {O}(N \cdot T \cdot C_{\text {eval}}). \end{aligned}$$\end{document}In the present work, standalone optimizers were executed with N=6 and T=5, resulting in approximately 30 fitness evaluations. The asymptotic growth order remains governed by the N · T evaluation term.

For the proposed sequential hybrids (ALO→WOA, ALO→DA, ALO→PSO, and PSO→WOA), the total number of iterations is still T, but divided into two phases. The first half performs exploration using one optimizer, and the second half performs exploitation using another. Although two different update rules are applied, the total number of candidate evaluations remains N · T. Consequently, the time complexity of each hybrid method is also24\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {O}(N \cdot T \cdot C_{\text {eval}}). \end{aligned}$$\end{document}In this study, hybrid optimizers used N=5 and T=8, resulting in approximately 40 fitness evaluations. The difference between standalone and hybrid strategies therefore lies only in constant factors related to evaluation count and minor additional update overhead, not in asymptotic growth order.

If the CNN evaluation cost is further expanded, letting E denote the number of epochs (here E=5 during search), S the number of training samples, and P the number of trainable parameters, then25\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} C_{\text {eval}} = \mathcal {O}(E \cdot S \cdot P), \end{aligned}$$\end{document}and the overall optimization complexity becomes26\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {O}(N \cdot T \cdot E \cdot S \cdot P). \end{aligned}$$\end{document}This expression holds for ALO, PSO, WOA, DA, and all proposed hybrids alike. Therefore, hybridization does not increase the theoretical Big-O time complexity compared to the original algorithms; it only alters the internal search dynamics through staged exploration and exploitation.

Regarding space complexity, all population-based methods store N candidate solutions of dimension d, yielding27\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {O}(N \cdot d). \end{aligned}$$\end{document}In this work, d=3 for standalone optimizers and d=2 for hybrid experiments. Thus, the memory requirement remains linear in population size and search dimensionality for both original and hybrid methods.

In summary, the proposed hybrid metaheuristics preserve the same asymptotic time and space complexity as ALO, PSO, WOA, and DA. The computational differences observed in practice arise from evaluation budget and convergence efficiency rather than from an increase in theoretical computational order.

Analytical comparison with iCSPM and exploratory cuckoo search

The reviewer suggested comparing the proposed framework with efficient metaheuristic algorithms such as iCSPM and Exploratory Cuckoo Search (ECS). Both iCSPM and ECS enhance classical swarm-based search mechanisms through adaptive cooperation and improved exploration strategies. iCSPM typically introduces dynamic learning or cooperative subpopulation mechanisms to accelerate convergence in complex search landscapes, whereas ECS leverages enhanced Lévy-flight exploration and refined nest replacement strategies to strengthen global search diversity.

From a computational standpoint, these methods belong to the same population-based optimization family as ALO, PSO, WOA, and DA. Their time complexity is likewise governed by the number of fitness evaluations and can be expressed as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal {O}(N \cdot T \cdot C_{\text {eval}})$$\end{document}, which matches the asymptotic complexity derived for both the standalone and hybrid methods in this study.

The primary difference lies in search organization strategy. While iCSPM and ECS often blend exploration and exploitation adaptively within each iteration, the proposed hybrids employ explicit temporal phase separation. The first \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lfloor T/2 \rfloor$$\end{document} iterations emphasize exploration, and the remaining iterations focus on exploitation. This deterministic staging is particularly suitable for CNN hyperparameter tuning scenarios where each evaluation involves computationally expensive model training. Under constrained evaluation budgets (N=5, T=8), the structured hybrid design promotes stable convergence without increasing theoretical computational complexity.

Therefore, although direct experimental benchmarking with iCSPM or ECS is not included, the proposed approach operates within the same asymptotic complexity class and is conceptually aligned with modern efficiency-oriented metaheuristic frameworks, differing primarily in operator coordination strategy and budget-aware staging.

Effect of hyperparameter tuning on CNN behaviour

The two hyperparameters tuned in many experiments – learning rate (LR) and dropout – affect the CNN as follows:

Learning rate controls optimization dynamics. Too large an LR causes divergence or highly noisy training; too small an LR slows convergence and may get stuck in suboptimal minima. Metaheuristic search locates LR in a range that balances early convergence during short-run fitness estimation and final generalization in the full retrain.

Dropout controls regularization strength. Higher dropout reduces co-adaptation and can improve generalization but may hamper learning if excessive (effective capacity reduced). Joint tuning of LR and dropout is important because their effects interact: a higher LR often requires stronger regularization to avoid overfitting to noisy gradient steps.

Limitations

Despite achieving strong classification performance, the proposed hybrid metaheuristic–CNN framework has several limitations that should be acknowledged.

First, the experiments are conducted on the Tomato Leaf Disease V2 dataset, which primarily consists of curated images captured under relatively controlled conditions. Such datasets may not fully represent real-world agricultural environments characterized by varying illumination, occlusions, complex backgrounds, and mixed disease symptoms. Therefore, the generalization capability of the proposed models under field conditions remains uncertain.

Second, only four hybrid combinations of metaheuristics were tested in the present research (ALO–DA, ALO–PSO, ALO–WOA and PSO–WOA). Trade-offs could be different with other optimization algorithms like the Grey Wolf optimizer, Harris Hawks Optimizer or a reinforcement based hyperparameter optimization. Moreover, it was only a fixed-weight CNN architecture that was investigated; the behavior of these hybrid optimizers on more complex CNNs or Vision Transformers or multimodal models have not been investigated so far.In the current experiment, the CNN training optimizer is set to Adam to be able to control the evaluation of metaheuristic hyperparameter optimization; the transferability of the tuned hyperparameters to other gradient-based optimizers will be addressed in the future research.

Third, though the accuracy, calibration, and class-wise reliability was improved with the hybridization, it also introduced computational overhead in the search of hyperparameters step. This can be restricted to very large sets of data or running in resource constrained resources, such as edge devices. In addition, metaheuristic optimization is stochastic and hence, has sensitivity in terms of population size, iteration number and random seed that can contaminate reproducibility. In this work, the hyperparameter optimization is deliberately restricted to learning rate and dropout rate for three reasons. First, these two parameters have the most direct and architecture-independent impact on CNN convergence and generalization 27, making them the highest priority targets for automated tuning. Second, expanding the search space to include batch size, optimizer type, or architectural parameters (filter counts, depth) would increase dimensionality and require a proportionally larger evaluation budget to maintain adequate coverage, a budget that is prohibitive given that each fitness evaluation costs approximately 39 seconds on the hardware used. Third, fixing architectural parameters ensures that observed performance differences are attributable to the hybridization strategy itself rather than to incidental architecture choices, preserving methodological clarity. Extending the search space to additional hyperparameters under larger budgets is identified as a direction for future work. Addressing these limitations will be critical for translating hybrid metaheuristic optimization frameworks into robust, real-world agricultural decision-support systems. Another limitation is the potential dataset ceiling effect. The consistently high accuracy achieved by all models suggests that the dataset may already be highly separable, limiting the extent to which further optimization can yield significant performance improvements.

Finally, this study focuses on hyperparameter optimization effectiveness rather than model interpretability. Explainable AI (XAI) analysis, such as Grad-CAM 28 or saliency maps, evaluates whether a model attends to disease-relevant leaf regions when making predictions — a research question that is orthogonal to the HPO evaluation conducted here. Prior works on agricultural disease detection have similarly separated HPO evaluation from interpretability analysis 5,6, reporting classification metrics without XAI visualization. Nonetheless, integrating Grad-CAM to verify that the best-performing hybrid (ALO→DA) attends to biologically meaningful leaf regions rather than background artifacts — is an important direction for future work, particularly for building practitioner trust in field deployment.

Although multiple runs were considered, the stochastic nature of metaheuristic optimization introduces variability in results. A more extensive statistical evaluation across a larger number of independent runs would further strengthen the robustness of the conclusions. These gaps are future directions of the work, including the validation of multi-crop, the effort to experiment with additional hybrid or adaptive optimizers, the development of lightweight models to execute edges, as well as the integration of explainable AI to improve the transparency and trust.

Conclusion

This paper presented and compared four hybrid-metaheuristic approaches, namely, ALO-WOA, ALO-DA, ALO-PSO, and PSO-WOA, to the automatic hyperparameter optimization of Convolutional Neural Networks in the classification of tomatoes leaf diseases. The hybrids combined exploration oriented and exploitation oriented optimizers in sequential steps to achieve a compromise between search diversity and refinement: competitive results compared to good single optimizers with much better stability and convergence reliability using limited computational resources. Of the evaluated strategies, ALO-DA also performed the best with the test accuracy of 97.83 per cent, macro-F1 of 0.981, and stable calibration and per-class reliability. Hybrids using WOA (ALO-WOA and PSO-WOA) also provided the best accuracy and good convergence, whereas ALO-PSO, despite its efficacy, remained slightly below with less effective late-phase exploitation. These results confirm the fact that hybridization provides improved convergence stability, calibration, and robustness, although the highest accuracy in this study is achieved by a standalone Dragonfly optimizer, indicating that hybrid methods primarily enhance reliability rather than peak performance. To the best of our knowledge, this work systematically design and evaluate numerous sequential two-phase hybrid metaheuristic plans of CNN optimization of hyperparameters in plant disease classification.

In addition to accuracy, the proposed hybrids proved to have benefits of training stability, probability calibration, separation of ROC-AUC and equal initial-per-class performance of visual tomato diseases. Such strength is especially useful in precision agriculture, where reliable and explainable models can be used to implement interventions at the right time, minimize losses of crops and make sustainable crop production practices.

However, a number of limitations should be mentioned. The experiments were limited to Tomato Leaf Disease V2 and a small CNN architecture; it is still to be confirmed whether it can be properly generalized to other crops or larger datasets, as well as to more complex networks. The search cost of metaheuristic search can be quite computationally expensive, as in this case, but can be too costly to run in edge devices with resource constraints. Moreover, the issue of interpretability was not explicitly referred to, which raised the question of whether the CNN placed constant emphasis on the disease-related areas of the leaves. Residual errors were primarily observed among visually similar disease categories, indicating that limitations arise from intrinsic feature overlap rather than deficiencies in the optimization framework.

Future studies must build on this to multi-crop and multi-disease data, consider other hybrid and adaptive optimization methods (e.g., Grey Wolf, Harris Hawks, reinforcement learning-based tuning) and lightweight architectures that can be deployed in the field in real time. End-users would also enjoy high trust and interpretability by including explainable AI solutions like Grad-CAM or attention visualization. Further development of hybrid metaheuristic CNN optimization could be achieved by multi-objective formulations that simultaneously optimize accuracy, computational cost and interpretability.

To conclude, this paper shows that Hybrid metaheuristics offers an efficient and generalizable approach to CNN hyperparameter optimization in plant disease detection. Using the synergistic advantages of exploration and exploitation, the proposed hybrids provide the competitive high accuracy and reliability, which will be the basis of future research and its practical use in precision agriculture. Overall, the findings highlight that while metaheuristic optimization significantly improves CNN reliability and convergence behavior, its impact is ultimately bounded by dataset characteristics and model capacity, emphasizing the need for combined advances in data diversity, model design, and optimization strategies.

The role of placental DNA methylation in the pathogenesis of chronic intervillositis of unknown etiology
Authors: Amy M Inkster, Maria S Peñaherrera, Wendy P Robinson, Jefferson Terry
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesChronic intervillositis of unknown etiology (CIUE) is the pathological influx of maternal inflammatory cells into the intervillous space of the placenta without demonstrable cause. CIUE is associated with placental damage and increased risk of pregnancy loss, fetal demise, and growth restriction. The pathogenic mechanism is unclear, and we lack biomarkers for detection, and treatments to prevent recurrence. DNA methylation (DNAme) is a biomarker altered in association with many disease states. We hypothesized that CIUE may be associated with a distinct signature placental DNAme signature.MethodsDNAme was profiled using the Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC v2.0 array on fresh-frozen placental tissue. After quality control, data were available for 24 CIUE (14 high grade, 10 low grade) and 27 non-CIUE placental samples, plus six independent decidua samples. CIUE was confirmed and graded by a perinatal pathologist. Contamination was assessed using the 65 array genotyping probes, placental cell composition and epigenetic age were estimated. After processing, DNAme data were available at 855,732 CpGs.ResultsSeveral lines of evidence indicated maternal DNA in CIUE placentas. Genetic contamination metrics were higher in CIUE than non-CIUE cases. In XY samples, X chromosome copy number increased with CIUE grade, suggesting XX contaminating DNA. Principal components analysis placed CIUE samples closer to decidua along PC1 than non-CIUE samples, cell composition showed elevated Hofbauer cells, monocytes, and neutrophils in CIUE. Epigenetic age was higher in CIUE versus non-CIUE samples. Together, these findings suggested an increased concentration of non-self XX DNA in CIUE cases, likely originating from an adult, macrophage-rich source, suggesting a maternal origin. An epigenome-wide association study found that all significant differentially methylated loci associated with CIUE were attenuated after adjusting for cell composition.ConclusionsOur results illustrate that placental DNAme patterns in CIUE reflect signatures of maternal infiltration rather than intrinsic DNAme alterations. This study also highlights the value of integrating the diverse molecular data outputs of DNAme arrays to explore the presence of maternal cells in placental tissue.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13148-025-02021-9.Keywords: Chronic intervillositis of unknown etiology, Placenta, DNA methylation, Epigenetics, Infiltrations

Introduction

Chronic intervillositis of unknown etiology (CIUE) describes the pathological influx of maternal inflammatory cells, primarily macrophages, into the intervillous space of the placenta without a demonstrable cause, such as COVID-19 or malaria. This infiltrate damages the placenta and is associated with significantly increased risk of early pregnancy loss, fetal demise, and growth restriction [1, 2]. High rates of recurrence are also observed in association with CIUE, as well as increased rate of other complications in subsequent pregnancies. The prevalence of CIUE varies by locale but affects approximately 1 in 600 pregnancies [2], making it a significant cause of recurrent pregnancy loss.

Presently there is no biomarker to detect CIUE prior to delivery, and no reliably effective treatment to prevent CIUE recurrence. Empirical therapies with anti-coagulants and immunomodulators have been employed to prevent or ameliorate recurrence but there is equivocal evidence that these approaches are universally effective [3–7]. A better understanding of CIUE pathogenesis could lead to better therapies.

The pathogenesis of CIUE is unclear. An abnormal maternal immunological response to pregnancy, analogous to allograft rejection, is a favored explanation [8, 9]; however, the placenta may also play an active role in attracting macrophages [10]. Under normal circumstances the placenta elaborates cytokines that induce M2-type macrophage and regulatory T cells and limit T helper cell activation in the surrounding decidua to promote pregnancy [11]. Reports of CIUE in only one placenta from twin pregnancies suggest the importance of the placental immune response [12], while the loss of CIUE in surrogate pregnancy implies a maternal component [13].

Interferon gamma (IFNγ) signaling plays a role in promoting normal placental implantation and pregnancy [14, 15]. Genes involved in interferon signaling pathways, particularly IFNγ, are significantly upregulated in CIUE placentas as compared to normal early placental tissue [16], suggesting that dysregulation of normal developmental IFNγ signaling may have a role in CIUE pathogenesis. Why placental interferon signaling is upregulated in CIUE and the mechanism underlying this is unclear.

DNA methylation (DNAme) is an epigenetic mark involving the addition of a methyl group to the 5’ carbon of cytosine residues, particularly in the context of cytosine-guanine (CpG) dinucleotides. DNAme is acquired during embryonic development and tissue differentiation, and DNAme signatures delineate cellular identity. Certain genomic regions are labile to DNAme alterations associated with disease status and environmental exposures, and DNAme patterns are also strongly influenced by genetic variation [17, 18]. Reproducible patterns of placental DNAme alteration are found, for example, in association with early-onset preeclampsia [19]. Placental DNAme has also been investigated in association with fetal growth and birth weight, acute chorioamnionitis, recurrent pregnancy loss, and several other phenotypes [20–23], and with respect to exposures including arsenic, cadmium, maternal body mass index, parental smoking, and exposure to depression and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors [24–29].

While DNAme can provide a signature of gene expression patterns (e.g. promoter DNAme is often associated with gene silencing), it also varies distinctly between mature cell types and can be used to provide a sensitive measure of relative cell proportions in heterogeneous whole placental tissue [30, 31]. One of the more interesting properties of placental DNAme is its distinction between cells of trophectodermal origin (e.g. cytotrophoblast, syncytiotrophoblast, which tend to have lower levels of genomic DNAme), versus cells derived from the inner cell mass of the blastocyst, which have higher average DNAme levels; these patterns are established early in embryonic development and persist in the mature cell types [30, 32, 33]. Accordingly, we hypothesized that CIUE may be associated with a distinct signature of placental DNAme, arising either from the disease process itself, or due to detectable alterations in cell composition.

This study compares the DNA methylation profiles of placental tissue (whole chorionic villi) from early pregnancy losses with CIUE, to placental tissue from non-inflamed early pregnancy losses, to determine if CIUE has a unique DNA methylation profile and whether there is aberrant methylation of genes involved in the IFNγ signaling pathway.

Methods

Sample selection

Ethical approval for this archival deidentified tissue study was provided by the University of British Columbia Research Ethics Board (H21-01264), and the experiments were performed in accordance with applicable regulations and ethical guidelines. Fresh frozen placental tissue samples (approximately 2-3mm3) from 25 CIUE-affected early pregnancy losses and 29 non-CIUE gestational age-matched losses were selected for inclusion in this study from the Anatomical Pathology archives at BC Children’s and Women’s Hospitals. Gestational age at the time of pregnancy loss was obtained from the pathological database where available, and both CIUE and non-CIUE cases were selected to approximately match mean gestational age between groups, see Table 1.

H&E-stained slides of correlated formalin fixed paraffin embedded tissue for each CIUE sample were reviewed by a perinatal pathologist (JT) to ensure accurate diagnosis. CIUE was defined as low grade if between 5 and 50% of the placental intervillous space was occupied by histiocytes (with or without associated fibrin) and high grade if 50% or more of the space was involved, as previously described [34]. IFNγ protein level expression status (positive/negative) was assessed previously in these samples and has been described elsewhere [35].

Alongside the placental samples, 6 decidua samples (3 first trimester, 3 term) were included in this project as representatives of maternally derived tissue. The first trimester decidua samples were included in the same array batch as the CIUE and non-CIUE samples and were all < 12 weeks of gestational age, the additional third trimester decidua samples were profiled with the v2.0 array in a separate technical batch with other samples. The three third trimester samples were affected by preterm labor/preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM) (gestational age 34 weeks), PPROM and decidual hemorrhage (gestational age 28 weeks), and placental mesenchymal dysplasia (gestational age roughly 36 weeks), respectively.

DNA methylation array data collection

Genome-wide DNA methylation was profiled using the Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC v2.0 Bead Chip in Vancouver, BC at the British Columbia Children’s Hospital Research Institute. Genomic DNA was purified using the Qiagen DNeasy Blood & Tissue kit (Qiagen, Valencia, CA, USA), followed by bisulfite conversion of the purified DNA using the Zymo EZ DNA Methylation kit (Zymo Research, Orange, CA, USA). Bisulfite-converted DNA was amplified, enzymatically fragmented, and hybridized to the v2.0 bead chips per the manufacturer’s protocol [36]. Bead chips were scanned with an Illumina HiScan2000. Samples were distributed across the array chips and rows with respect to CIUE status, aneuploidy status (yes/no and affected chromosome), sex, gestational age, and IFNγ protein expression (positive/negative) [35]. One technical replicate was selected from each of the following four categories for inclusion in the array run: 1) female (XX) low-grade CIUE with an aneuploidy, 2) male (XY) high-grade CIUE with no aneuploidy, 3) male (XY) selective termination, non-CIUE with no aneuploidy, and 4) female (XX) spontaneous abortion, non-CIUE with no aneuploidy.

Aneuploidy assessment

For cases included in this study, aneuploidy was determined by a combination of (i) clinically performed quantitative fluorescent polymerase chain reaction (ii) chromosomal microarray performed on a research basis, and (iii) copy-number analysis performed on the DNAme data with the conumee 2.0 R package [37, 38]. Because placental samples used for DNAme arrays were obtained from a single sampling location, we were unable to distinguish between whole-placenta aneuploidy and confined placental mosaicism, and all aneuploid samples were analyzed with the understanding that they may in some cases be mosaic.

The 25 CIUE cases included 14 with high-grade CIUE (three were aneuploid including two with trisomy 16 and one with trisomy 22) and 11 low-grade (five were aneuploid including one with trisomy 13, one with combined trisomy 13 and 16, two with trisomy 16, and one with trisomy 2). The 29 non-CIUE cases originally included 11 with normal balanced karyotypes, 11 with known aneuploidies (including monosomy X, trisomy 4, 7, 10, 14, 16, 21, and one sample with an unbalanced chromosome 1:10 translocation), and 7 normal placental controls from elective pregnancy terminations for which genetic testing was not previously performed.

DNAme copy number analysis resulted in the reclassification of 1/11 non-CIUE normal balanced karyotype samples into the aneuploid group: mosaic trisomy 22 was detected in SPLN-8 and confirmed by microsatellite genotype markers. Among the 7 normal placental controls no aneuploidies were detected by DNAme copy number analysis. The sample numbers prior to DNAme quality control were thus 25 CIUE (14 high-grade of which 3 were aneuploid, 11 low-grade of which 5 were aneuploid), and 29 non-CIUE samples (10 normal karyotypes, 12 aneuploid, 7 normal pregnancies).

DNA methylation data quality control & processing

Array data from raw IDAT files were read into R, version 4.3.1 [39], and were annotated with the 20a1.hg38 v2.0 EPIC manifest [40]. Sample quality was assessed using the minfi and ewastools R packages via inter-array correlation, median fluorescence intensity (sum of methylated and unmethylated fluorescence), and the fluorescence intensities of 17 Illumina control probes [41–43]. Sample sex and genetic identity were assessed and confirmed to match reported clinical characteristics using the ewastools R package [43]. Sample purity was assessed using the 65 “rs” genotyping probes included on the array and the ewastools package function call_genotypes followed by snp_outliers, and samples with an average log odds of belonging to the outlier component across all 65 probes (output of snp_outlier) less than −4 were retained for analysis as recommended by the ewastools package authors [43]. However, due to the pathology of CIUE, values > 4 in this metric likely reflect maternal cells infiltrating the placental compartment as part of the disease process, and CIUE samples were thus not excluded based on this value.

During DNAme quality control, three samples were identified for removal. Two samples (CLG-6_v and SPLA-17_v) were excluded for comprising nearly 100% maternally derived DNA, based on DNAme-assessed sex (XX), a missing expected trisomy 16 in SPLA-17, and for both clustering with maternal decidual samples rather than placental samples in principal components analysis. A third sample, NP-3_v, was reported to be male (XY) but was XX by DNAme data-derived sex assessment and had a high probability of contamination based on the 65 rs genotyping probes included on the array (SNP outlier log odds = − 0.8, computed with the ewastools R package). All other samples passed quality control assessment.

Final samples numbers for DNAme analysis were 24 CIUE (14 high grade, 10 low grade), and 27 non-CIUE (10 with balanced karyotypes, 11 with aneuploidy and 6 normal pregnancy terminations). Because aneuploidy was similarly distributed between CIUE and non-CIUE groups (8 of 24 CIUE vs. 11 of 27 non-CIUE; Chi-square p-value > 0.05), and most aneuploidies were observed in only one sample (with the exception of T7, T13, T16, and T22), and aneuploidy was not associated with the top principal components of DNAme data (Supplementary Fig. 1) we did not stratify downstream analyses by chromosome or aneuploidy yes/no. Instead, CIUE status was treated as the primary variable of interest.

DNA methylation array quality control and analysis

Data were normalized using the normal exponential out-of-band background correction (noob) [41, 42] and beta mixture quantile (BMIQ) normalization algorithms [44]. Prior to probe filtering, cell composition was estimated from whole villi DNAme data using the planet R package with the Yuan et al. (2021) first trimester reference panel of CpGs and with the Campbell et al. (2024) placental and adult blood immune cell DNA methylation reference panel, see below for further details [30, 45, 46]. Placental epigenetic gestational age was also calculated with the planet R package using the control placental clock (CPC) [47].

CpG probes were then excluded from the data set if they had a detection p > 0.01 or a bead count < 3 in > 5% of samples, or if they were recommended for removal for cross-reactivity or nearby genetic polymorphisms in Kaur et al. (2023) [48, 49] or Peters et al. (2024) [50]. DNAme β values from v2.0 replicate probes targeting the same CpG were collapsed to the mean β value of all replicates using the betasCollapseToPfx function implemented in the sesame R package [51]. After processing, DNAme measurements were available at 866,732 autosomal CpGs.

DNAme associated with CIUE status was assessed using linear models implemented with the limma R package [52]. In epigenome-wide linear models run on M values, p values were adjusted for multiple comparisons using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method. Similarly, all other analyses described in this manuscript were subject to FDR correction where multiple tests were performed, statistical tests used are indicated throughout the Results section.

External maternal blood DNAme data

We also downloaded maternal blood samples (taken during pregnancy) profiled with the EPIC v1.0 data from GSE192918 [53] and GSE224339 [54]. From both datasets, ten unrelated samples were randomly selected, five sampled in the first trimester and five in the third trimester. The DNAme data for these 20 samples were processed using identical normalization and probe filtering steps as applied to the CIUE data set. The samples chosen for inclusion from data set GSE192918 were GSM5769032, GSM5769038, GSM5769040, GSM5769042, GSM5769044, GSM5769056, GSM5769058, GSM5769062, GSM5769064, GSM5769066, and the samples randomly chosen from data set GSE224339 were GSM7019888, GSM7019890, GSM7019924, GSM7019944, GSM7019980, GSM7019982, GSM7020001, GSM7020012, GSM7020060, GSM7020070.

Estimation of placental cell composition from DNAme data

Placental cell composition was estimated from DNAme data using two references: a first trimester reference from Yuan et al. (2021) and a third trimester reference from Campbell et al. (2024), which includes additional samples and sorted adult blood cell types [30, 46]. To validate the Campbell reference, we compared its estimates to those from the Yuan first trimester reference (Supplementary Figs. 2 and 3). The Campbell estimates were broadly consistent with the Yuan first trimester estimates, with correlations > 0.75 for most cell types except cytotrophoblast (R = 0.36), Hofbauer cells (R = 0.40) and nRBCs (R = -0.075). The divergence in Hofbauer and nRBC estimates likely reflects the inclusion of adult blood cell profiles in the Campbell reference, which display more somatic-like DNAme signature that the Yuan et al. tool would be constrained to estimate as either Hofbauer cells or nRBCs. Given the overall consistency in major cell type estimates, we utilized the Campbell et al. (2024) estimates for downstream analysis.

Results

Results of DNAme data processing

To investigate the molecular correlates of CIUE, we profiled genome-wide DNAme in placental samples from 24 CIUE and 27 non-CIUE cases, using the Illumina Infinium Methylation EPIC v2.0 array. Alongside capturing DNAme signatures at > 900,000 CpGs genome-wide, the EPIC v2.0 array captures multiple other types of molecular information, including genotype at 65 SNP loci, and raw probe fluorescence intensities. In this analysis we leveraged the several types of data that can be derived from the v2.0 array to understand molecular variation related to CIUE status.

First, we examined the SNP genotyping probes, using a metric commonly employed for quality control to evaluate potential maternal cell infiltration in our CIUE cohort. Specifically, we analyzed the 65 rs genotype probes included on the EPIC v2.0 array. At these loci, deviation from trimodal genotype distributions (AA, AB, BB) serves as a proxy to indicate degradation or mixing of multiple genotypes, usually attributed to sample contamination. When working with placental tissue, maternal cells can be present in the chorionic villi for biological (infiltration), or technical (sampling contamination) reasons, and can shift the signature observed at these loci to resemble contamination [43].

We computed the log odds of each sample being an outlier across all 65 rs genotype probes in this cohort; higher values correspond with higher log odds of genotype mixing, values greater than − 4 are typically used as a cutoff for sample exclusion [43]. Comparing the mean SNP outlier log odds values between CIUE high grade, low grade, and non-CIUE cases, we observed that CIUE samples (− 3.44 ± 0.99, combining low and high grade) had significantly higher values than non-CIUE samples in this metric (− 4.45 ± 0.73) (Wilcoxon p-value 2E-9), Fig. 1A. Within CIUE cases, high-grade samples (− 3.16 ± 1.10) had slightly higher outlier values than low-grade (− 3.82 ± 0.71), although this difference was not significant. The SNP outlier metric was also higher in IFNγ positive samples (− 3.26 ± 1.26) compared to IFNγ negative samples (− 4.12 ± 0.88) (Wilcoxon p-value 0.03), Fig. 1B. Together these analyses suggest CIUE is associated with a genotype profile consistent with maternal cell infiltration into the placental compartment.

Principal components analysis

To evaluate DNAme variation associated with CIUE, we utilized principal components analysis (PCA). For this analysis we included the 24 CIUE samples, 27 non-CIUE samples, and 6 decidual samples.

PCA was first run on all placental samples excluding decidua at all autosomal CpGs (n = 855,732). PC1 (35%) was associated with diagnosis group (Kruskal–Wallis p-value 0.016), with samples organized non-CIUE < low-grade CIUE < high-grade CIUE, Supplementary Fig. 1. Dunn tests further demonstrated a significant separation of non-CIUE versus high-grade CIUE samples along PC1 (Dunn FDR < 0.05). Aneuploidy (yes/no) was not associated sample position along PC1, or with any of the top PCs (Wilcox p-value > 0.05). Additionally, we observed no evidence of similar aneuploidies clustering together in PC space, and the identity of aneuploid chromosomes was not associated with the top PCs.

When PCA was run including the 6 decidual samples, PC1 explained 59% of the variance and was associated with tissue, separating decidua from placenta (CIUE and non-CIUE) (Wilcox p-value 7.3E-5), Fig. 1C. PC1 also continued to separate samples by CIUE grade (Kruskal Wallis p-value 2.7E-5), with high-grade CIUE cases falling closest to decidua and non-CIUE samples furthest. Post-hoc Dunn tests confirmed significant separation of decidua from placental samples along PC1 (Dunn FDR < 0.05) and confirmed separation of high-grade CIUE from non-CIUE samples (Dunn FDR < 0.05). The localization of CIUE samples closer to decidua corresponds with histological knowledge of maternal cells infiltrating the villi in association with CIUE.

As a confirmatory analysis, PCA was also run with the addition of EPIC v1.0 data from 20 maternal whole blood samples, to capture an expanded maternal signature. The maternal blood samples fell along the same pole of PC1 as did the decidual samples, and the same ordering of non-CIUE samples (farthest from maternal tissue) and CIUE samples (closer to maternal tissue) was observed in this expanded analysis, Supplementary Fig. 4.

Evidence of maternal infiltration

We next sought to evaluate whether the patterns we observed in SNP genotyping analysis and PCA corresponded with the presence of specifically XX (female) DNA in CIUE samples, as would be expected if maternal cell infiltrate contributed to the observed signatures.

First, we calculated mean total fluorescence intensity of X chromosome CpGs (methylated + unmethylated), normalized to the mean total autosomal CpG fluorescence intensity for each sample to account for inter-sample variation. This method is commonly implemented in the ewastools package to estimate sample sex and is sensitive to copy number alterations, as in 45,X and 47,XXY karyotypes [43, 55]. We then compared mean total fluorescence intensity from the X chromosome in CIUE versus non-CIUE samples.

We observed that X chromosome fluorescence intensity was elevated in male CIUE samples as compared to non-CIUE males. Both high-grade (0.91 ± 0.11) and low-grade (0.85 ± 0.12) male CIUE samples showed higher mean X chromosome fluorescence intensity than non-CIUE males (0.84 ± 0.14, Wilcox FDR < 0.05); there was no significant difference between high- and low-grade CIUE males, Fig. 2A. In contrast, X chromosome fluorescence intensity did not vary by CIUE status in female samples in any pairwise comparison. These results suggest that CIUE male samples contain higher proportions of X chromosome DNA than non-CIUE males, consistent with the presence of maternal (XX) cells. No such effect was observed in female (XX) CIUE samples as additional maternal DNA does not alter relative X chromosome dosage in XX samples.

Inverse logic applies when examining DNAme in the XIST promoter, which is methylated in XY males (β values ~ 1) and mono-allelically methylated on only the active X in XX females (β values ~ 0.5). In CIUE samples we hypothesized that the presence of XX maternal DNA would decrease the mean observed XIST promoter DNAme β value in XY males. For this analysis we analyzed mean DNAme β values at five XIST promoter CpGs previously defined in [56] (cg03554089, cg12653510, cg05533223, cg11717280 and cg20698282), along with three additional CpGs on the v2.0 array within the same genomic region (hg38, chrX: 73,851,206-73,852,806) (cg03862022_BC21, cg27199737_BC21, cg27199729_BC21).

In male placentas, XIST promoter DNAme decreased with increasing CIUE grade: non-CIUE males had a mean β value of 0.93 ± 0.02, low-grade CIUE 0.89 ± 0.04 and high-grade CIUE 0.86 ± 0.04, Fig. 2B. Both CIUE high- and low-grade male samples had significantly lower mean XIST DNAme than non-CIUE males (Wilcox FDR < 0.05) but did not differ from each other. In contrast, female placentas showed consistent XIST DNAme across the diagnosis groups (high-grade CIUE 0.55 ± 0.03, low-grade CIUE 0.58 ± 0.01, non-CIUE 0.57 ± 0.04), with a modest but significant increase in mean XIST DNAme in low-grade CIUE versus non-CIUE females (Wilcox FDR < 0.05). Paired with the observed increase in XX DNA concentration in CIUE males, the male-specific decrease in XIST promoter DNAme likely reflects an increased contribution of maternal XX DNA in CIUE samples. This effect is not observed in female CIUE samples as both placental and maternal DNA is XX with similar XIST promoter DNAme, leaving XIST DNAme relatively constant regardless of the proportion of maternal DNA in a female CIUE sample. The slight elevation of mean XIST DNAme in low-grade CIUE females is potentially an artifact of DNAme variation related to other technical or biological factors as there was no evidence of this pattern in the high-grade CIUE females.

Estimated cell composition

We subsequently hypothesized that the DNAme-estimated cell composition of placental samples may shift with the presence of maternal cells in CIUE, increasing estimated proportions of cells with more somatic-like DNAme signatures, and decreasing proportions of trophoblast-derived placental cells, which exhibit distinct partially-methylated domains [30, 57]. To probe the cellular origins of the maternal DNAme detected in CIUE, we considered placental cell composition using reference-based cell type proportions, estimated with the Campbell et al. (2024) reference, which expands upon the Yuan reference panel to include additional sorted placental samples and sorted adult blood cell profiles (non-pregnant) [30, 46].

Evaluating the mean estimated cell proportions by CIUE grade, high and low-grade CIUE samples displayed higher estimated proportions of monocytes, neutrophils, and Hofbauer cells relative to non-CIUE samples Fig. 3B. As Hofbauer cells, monocytes, and neutrophils are all derived from myeloid progenitors, and there was not a significant difference between CIUE and non-CIUE samples in cell types derived from lymphoid progenitors (B and T cells, NK cells) these results are consistent with the expected pathology (maternal myeloid-specific infiltration).

To confirm that the cell types elevated in CIUE were not attributable to decidual contamination during sampling, we applied the placental cell type deconvolution to the decidual DNAme samples relative to non-CIUE placentas. Decidua exhibited significantly higher estimated proportions of monocytes and Hofbauer cells compared to non-CIUE placentas (Wilcoxon FDR < 0.05), Fig. 3C. However, decidua also exhibited higher proportions of NK, CD4T, and endothelial cells, alongside decreased proportions of cytotrophoblasts and syncytiotrophoblast. While Hofbauer cells and monocytes were elevated in both decidua and in CIUE samples, the remaining cell types were specifically elevated in only decidua (CD4T, NK, endothelial). These results suggest that maternal decidual contamination during sampling was unlikely to explain the estimated cell proportions observed in CIUE samples.

Epigenetic age analysis

Epigenetic age, derived from DNAme data, reflects a sample’s biological age, and often correlates with chronological age while also reflecting disease status and/or environmental exposures. We hypothesized that CIUE placentas may show higher epigenetic age than non-CIUE samples, either due to the disease process itself or as a signature of maternal (adult) cell infiltration. Epigenetic age was estimated with both the Horvath pan-tissue clock (years) and the Lee et al. (2019) placenta-specific clock (gestational weeks) [47, 58]; these analyses excluded 3 high-grade CIUE samples lacking reported clinical gestational ages. Epigenetic age was also estimated for all decidual samples.

Epigenetic age (both clocks) was positively associated with CIUE grade (non-CIUE < low-grade CIUE < high-grade CIUE), even after adjustment for sex, gestational age, and array row, Fig. 4A. The placental epigenetic clock estimated non-CIUE samples as 9.66 ± 1.9 weeks of gestational age, low-grade CIUE samples as 8.88 ± 3.1 weeks, and high-grade CIUE as 9.85 ± 2.4 weeks. The Horvath clock estimated placental ages at 0.06 ± 0.79 years across all samples, or roughly 0.8 months of age, reflecting its known prenatal inaccuracy [34]. However, the mean Horvath estimate for decidual samples was 36.7 ± 12.7 years, placing the estimated age of these tissues in the expected adult range. When placental samples were stratified by diagnosis, the Horvath estimates ranged from − 0.22 ± 0.62 years (non-CIUE) to 0.10 ± 0.50 years and 0.60 ± 0.97 years in low-grade CIUE and high-grade CIUE, respectively.

Epigenetic age did not differ by aneuploidy (yes/no) after adjusting for gestational age, sex, and array row, Fig. 4B. The ages estimated with both clocks were positively associated with Hofbauer cell proportions, NK cells, monocytes, and neutrophils (linear model FDR < 0.05), and negatively associated with syncytiotrophoblast proportions (linear model FDR < 0.05), after adjustment for gestational age, sex, and array row, Fig. 4D. Both clocks were also positively associated with the mean SNP outlier log odds per sample (FDR < 0.05) after adjusting for gestational age, sex, and array row, Fig. 4E. Taken together, these results were consistent with elevated epigenetic age in CIUE samples reflecting the presence of adult cells in CIUE placentas, i.e. maternal macrophage infiltration.

Epigenome-wide association study of CIUE

Our analyses to this point indicated CIUE was associated with a distinct molecular signature, correlating with the presence of maternal DNA in the placental compartment. We next sought to evaluate whether CIUE was associated with DNAme alterations independent of this maternal infiltration signal.

Genome-wide autosomal DNAme was assessed at 855,732 CpGs across all 51 samples (n = 14 high-grade CIUE, n = 10 low-grade CIUE, and n = 27 non-CIUE). For the three high-grade CIUE samples missing reported gestational age, age was imputed to the cohort median (9.0 weeks). Five linear models were evaluated with increasing levels of adjustment (Table 2): (i) a base model which included gestational age, sex, and array row, (ii) the base model with additional adjustment for SNP outlier log odds, (iii) the base model with adjustment for SNP outlier log odds and the proportion of all estimated cell types (excluding syncytiotrophoblast to avoid collinearity and CD4T which were zero in all samples), (iv) the base model adjusted for SNP outlier log odds and only the three cell types elevated in CIUE (Hofbauer, monocytes, neutrophils), and (v) the base model adjusted for the three cell types elevated in CIUE (Hofbauer, monocytes, neutrophils).

Candidate gene analysis

To complement the epigenome-wide association analysis, we next examined DNAme at candidate genes from a prior CIUE gene expression study [10]. The previous study identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in CIUE versus non-CIUE cases across three tissues: histiocytes (Histio), villous stroma (Stroma) and trophoblasts (Troph). DEGs which met p < 0.05 and absolute log2 fold change > 2 in any of the three tissues were selected for candidate DNAme candidate in our data, yielding 16 (Histio), 25 (Stroma) and 13 (Troph) DEGs. Interferon gamma (IFNG) was also included in the list of candidate genes. After collapsing duplicate genes, 54 unique candidate DEGs were considered in targeted DNAme analysis, Supplementary Table 1.

A total of 949 CpGs mapped to the 54 DEGs, and were tested for association with CIUE status using linear models adjusted for gestational age, sex, array row, and the estimated proportions of Hofbauer cells, monocytes, and neutrophils (informed by the results of the epigenome-wide association studies). Two CpGs in the gene body of PCOLCE-AS1(cg07084721, cg11463295) reached FDR < 0.05, both showing higher average DNAme in CIUE relative to non-CIUE samples (∆β = 0.48 and 0.78, respectively). PCOLCE-AS1 is the antisense transcript of PCOLCE, which was a DEG downregulated in CIUE histiocytes, Supplementary Fig. 5. No other CpGs met FDR significance in these candidate genes.

Given the exploratory nature of this candidate analysis, we considered the ten CpGs with the smallest p-values (highest nominal p-value 0.004). Among this list were two additional CpGs in PCOLCE-AS1 (cg17846263 at p = 3.52E-4, cg02797569 at p = 9.08E-4), as well as CpGs in HK2 (DEG in Stroma), TIMP3 (DEG in Histio) NID1 (DEG in Troph), SVEP1 (DEG in Troph), and two CpGs in LUM (DEG in Histio). All ten CpGs with the smallest p values showed higher DNAme in CIUE versus non-CIUE samples, and all were associated with downregulated gene expression in CIUE in the corresponding tissues, except HK2, which was upregulated in stroma, Supplementary Fig. 5. Notably, the trend toward higher DNAme in CIUE at these loci mirrors the known difference in DNAme between higher-methylated somatic tissues and lower-methylated trophoblast, potentially suggesting that these differential DNAme results may be attributable to maternal (i.e. somatic) cell infiltration in CIUE, consistent with results of the other analyses presented here.

Discussion

This study investigated DNAme patterns in placentas affected by chronic intervillositis of unknown etiology (CIUE), with major results revealing molecular signatures consistent with maternal macrophage infiltration, rather than intrinsic CIUE-associated DNAme patterns. Across multiple orthogonal analyses, including SNP outlier assessment, PCA, estimated cell composition, and epigenetic age, we observed consistent molecular evidence for the presence of maternal DNA in CIUE placentas.

Histopathological studies of CIUE placentas consistently describe a marked accumulation of maternal macrophages in the intervillous space, exceeding levels typically seen in early gestation by roughly ten-fold [59]. Our molecular findings align with these histopathologic observations, and are summarized in Table 3 and inter-variable relationships are depicted in Supplementary Fig. 6. In brief, in our data CIUE placentas displayed evidence of genotype mixing, and in male CIUE samples we confirmed this to correspond with the presence of XX DNA. Estimated cell type proportions showed elevations of myeloid-lineage populations in CIUE placentas, and epigenetic age estimates provided evidence that the infiltrating XX DNA was adult in origin, supporting a maternal source. Together these lines of evidence converged on a common interpretation: CIUE-affected placentas contain a substantial proportion of infiltrating maternal immune cells, which drive observed shifts in CIUE-associated DNAme patterns. Further, we detected no evidence for intrinsic DNAme signatures of CIUE relative to non-CIUE cases, other than those DNAme alterations attributable to maternal cell infiltration.

Summary of key results, consistent with maternal macrophage infiltration as the primary source of molecular alterations in CIUE

To better characterize the maternal immune cell infiltration observed in CIUE-affected placentas in this study, we examined DNAme-based cell composition estimates. These analyses indicated elevated proportions of Hofbauer cells, monocytes, and neutrophils in CIUE placentas. Notably, neutrophils are not a significant part of the CIUE infiltrate, which an earlier histopathologic analysis of these samples confirmed (see Methods). Thus, we do not interpret the cell type estimates as evidence of neutrophil presence in the maternal infiltrate in these samples. Rather, we suggest that the DNAme signature of the infiltrate (maternal macrophages) lacks an appropriate reference in placental cell deconvolution tools, resulting in the misclassification of maternal macrophages into some proportion of neutrophils, monocytes, and potentially Hofbauer cells. The strong association observed between monocyte/neutrophil proportions and the SNP outlier log odds metric further supports our interpretation that the cell types elevated in CIUE reflect maternal macrophage infiltration.

Our epigenome-wide association study did not identify any CpGs significantly associated with CIUE after accounting for cell composition, suggesting that the molecular patterns observed in CIUE were primarily driven by maternal infiltrating cells, rather than locus-specific alterations in placental DNAme. Our null epigenome-wide association findings somewhat contrast with the prior gene expression study, which reported elevated expression of interferon-related genes, particularly IFNγ, in CIUE tissues [10]. It is possible that IFNγ expression in placenta may not be strongly associated with DNAme, or that cell composition effects may have driven the results of the transcriptomic analysis. Alternately, elevated IFNγ expression may reflect upstream activation at the gene expression level, independent of DNAme regulation. For example, a recent study found evidence of Enterovirus colonization in a subset of term CIUE placentas [60], raising the possibility that gene expression patterns including interferon upregulation may be physiologic.

One limitation of this study is that DNAme profiling was performed on fresh frozen tissue, while separate FFPE sections from the same placentas were utilized for histopathologic assessment of histiocyte infiltration and to estimate the percentage of intervillous space affected. The possible lack of spatial concordance in these samples may limit the ability to directly correlate molecular and histopathological findings. In addition, a subset of samples in both the CIUE and non-CIUE groups harbored aneuploidies, however, the number of cases with any specific aneuploidy was too small to support subgroup analysis in this cohort. As such we were limited to treating aneuploidy as a binary variable in our models.

Despite these limitations our study benefited from several distinct strengths. Here we have applied a creative and rigorous analytical approach, leveraging methods typically used in quality control such as PCA and SNP genotype assessment to enable valuable and biologically-informed quantitative insight into this pathology. Our analyses also incorporated X chromosome molecular patterns, which are often ignored in high-dimensional biological analysis, and careful consideration of maternal cell infiltration. These investigations enabled us to disentangle the placental and maternal contributions to the molecular patterns observed in CIUE. Finally, although our sample size was modest, it is an incredibly high value cohort considering the disease under study and the rarity of well-preserved and histologically-characterized CIUE samples with accompanying clinical and molecular data. To our knowledge this is the first DNAme-based analysis of CIUE.

By leveraging diverse analytical outputs from DNAme arrays we observed converging evidence for maternal cell infiltration as a driver of most molecular alterations detected in CIUE-affected placentas. Future studies employing single-cell profiling of DNAme or gene expression patterns could further clarify the molecular phenotypes observed in CIUE and validate the hypotheses generated by this work.

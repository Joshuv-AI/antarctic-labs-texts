Introduction

Natural orifice specimen extraction (NOSE) has emerged as an extension of minimally invasive colorectal surgery that enables removal of the resected specimen through a natural orifice, most commonly via transanal or transvaginal routes, thereby avoiding the need for an abdominal extraction incision.1 The technique has gained increasing acceptance following advances in intracorporeal reconstruction, minimally invasive colorectal techniques and surgical technology.2 3

Existing comparative studies suggest that NOSE may confer several perioperative advantages compared with conventional transabdominal specimen extraction, including reduced postoperative pain, lower wound morbidity, improved cosmetic outcomes and earlier gastrointestinal recovery.4–7 A growing body of observational evidence has also demonstrated comparable oncological outcomes between NOSE and conventional extraction approaches in appropriately selected patients.8 9

Despite these encouraging findings, several important limitations remain within the current NOSE literature. Much of the available evidence is retrospective, single-centre and subject to selection bias. Studies frequently compare achieved extraction routes rather than planned operative strategy, introducing methodological heterogeneity and crossover-related bias. Furthermore, patient selection criteria for NOSE remain inconsistent across published series, limiting external validity and reproducibility.1 10–12

Long-term abdominal wall outcomes represent another important evidence gap. Incisional hernia remains a recognised complication following minimally invasive colorectal surgery and is closely associated with the abdominal extraction site.13 14 By eliminating the abdominal extraction incision, NOSE may plausibly reduce extraction-site hernia formation and wound morbidity. However, prospective multicentre data evaluating long-term abdominal wall outcomes following NOSE remain limited.

Patient-reported and functional outcomes also require further evaluation. While improved cosmesis and body image are frequently proposed advantages of NOSE, available data remain heterogeneous and are often limited by retrospective assessment and inconsistent use of validated instruments.4 7 Concerns regarding bowel, pelvic floor and sexual function following transanal and transvaginal extraction techniques also warrant prospective longitudinal evaluation.7 9 15

The Asia-Pacific Natural Orifice Specimen Extraction Surgery (AP-NOSES) registry was established to address these limitations through prospective enrolment of patients undergoing minimally invasive colorectal surgery with predefined NOSE eligibility criteria, standardised longitudinal outcome assessment and integration of patient-reported measures. The registry aims to evaluate both short-term clinical outcomes and long-term abdominal wall and functional outcomes following NOSE within real-world colorectal surgical practice.

Methods and analysis

Study design

AP-NOSES is a prospective multicentre observational registry evaluating outcomes following minimally invasive colorectal surgery with NOSE or transabdominal specimen extraction. The registry is coordinated by Singapore General Hospital and is intended for expansion across participating tertiary colorectal units within the Asia-Pacific region.

The registry does not alter routine clinical care. Operative approach, extraction strategy and perioperative management are determined according to surgeon judgement and local institutional practice.

Study objectives

The primary objective is to compare time to incisional hernia within 24 months between NOSE-eligible patients undergoing planned NOSE extraction and NOSE-eligible patients undergoing planned transabdominal specimen extraction.

Secondary objectives include evaluation of postoperative morbidity, surgical site infection, extraction-site morbidity, length of hospital stay, patient-reported quality-of-life outcomes, bowel function outcomes, urinary and sexual function outcomes, local recurrence, distant recurrence and disease-free survival.

Study population

Adult patients undergoing elective minimally invasive colorectal resection with planned intact specimen extraction will be screened for eligibility.

Inclusion criteria are: age 21 years or older; elective minimally invasive colorectal resection with laparoscopic or robotic intent; planned intact specimen extraction; eligible for natural orifice specimen extraction or transabdominal specimen extraction according to predefined protocol-based technical criteria and ability to provide informed consent.

Exclusion criteria include: emergency colorectal surgery; planned transanal total mesorectal excision; planned ultralow anterior resection with planned anastomosis 3 cm or less from the anal verge; planned abdominoperineal resection; planned permanent end stoma without restoration of intestinal continuity; planned multivisceral resection; generalised peritonitis or uncontrolled intra-abdominal sepsis; pregnancy; pre-existing ventral or incisional hernia at the planned extraction site; planned specimen extraction through a prior laparotomy incision containing mesh and fixed distal obstruction, severe pelvic stenosis or other anatomical constraints precluding intact specimen extraction.

Exposure groups

The primary comparative analysis will be conducted within a predefined NOSE-eligible cohort. Exposure groups are defined according to planned extraction strategy:

Planned NOSE extraction, including transanal or transvaginal extraction.

Planned NOSE extraction, including transanal or transvaginal extraction.

Planned transabdominal specimen extraction among patients meeting protocol-defined NOSE eligibility criteria.

Planned transabdominal specimen extraction among patients meeting protocol-defined NOSE eligibility criteria.

NOSE eligibility will be determined according to predefined protocol-based technical criteria incorporating tumour characteristics, anticipated specimen dimensions, anatomical considerations and surgeon assessment. In general, transanal NOSE will be considered for specimens with an anticipated maximal specimen diameter of approximately 6 cm or less, while transvaginal NOSE may be considered for larger specimens, typically up to approximately 8 cm in appropriately selected female patients.11 16–18 Additional considerations include tumour location, mesenteric bulk, pelvic dimensions, prior pelvic surgery and anticipated ability to achieve intact specimen extraction without undue traction or specimen compromise.

Planned transabdominal extraction may still be selected despite technical NOSE eligibility based on surgeon preference, patient preference, intraoperative judgement, training considerations or institutional practice patterns.

Planned and achieved extraction routes, reasons for non-performance of NOSE, operative details and perioperative outcomes will be prospectively recorded.

Operative techniques

Participating surgeons will perform minimally invasive colorectal resections according to local institutional practice and surgeon preference. Both laparoscopic and robotic approaches are permitted.

NOSE techniques may include transanal and transvaginal extraction routes. Variations in anastomotic strategy, intracorporeal reconstruction, reduced-port approaches and technical modifications are permitted according to surgeon discretion and institutional expertise.19–22

Protective specimen extraction techniques and contamination-control strategies are encouraged according to local practice.

Data collection and registry platform

Data will be prospectively collected using a secure centralised REDCap database hosted at Singapore General Hospital. Registry data will be recorded using coded study identifiers without direct patient identifiers.

Participating centres will maintain local linkage logs where required according to institutional governance policies. Registry access will be restricted to authorised study personnel, with audit trails and user-based access controls maintained within the REDCap platform.

Standardised case report forms will be used to facilitate consistent prospective data collection across participating centres. Collected variables will include patient demographics, body mass index, comorbidities, tumour characteristics, operative details, extraction strategy, postoperative outcomes, pathology outcomes, oncological follow-up and patient-reported outcomes. To capture the heterogeneity of contemporary minimally invasive colorectal practice, the standardised case report form records the operative platform (conventional laparoscopic or robotic, including conversion to open), the procedure type (for example right or extended right hemicolectomy, left hemicolectomy, sigmoid colectomy, anterior resection or low anterior resection), the anastomotic method (intracorporeal or extracorporeal), the anastomotic configuration (end-to-end, side-to-end, end-to-side or side-to-side), the stapling method (double-stapled, single-stapled or hand-sewn), the distance of rectal tumours from the anal verge, the planned and achieved extraction route (transanal, transvaginal or transabdominal), the transabdominal extraction-site incision type (transverse or Pfannenstiel, midline, stoma-site or other) and reasons for non-performance or conversion of a planned NOSE approach. These variables permit prespecified subgroup and adjusted analyses across operative platforms and reconstructive strategies.

Data quality review and periodic verification may be performed by the coordinating centre to identify missing or inconsistent data entries.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome is time to incisional hernia within 24 months following index colorectal surgery. Incisional hernia is defined as clinical or radiological diagnosis of an incisional hernia occurring at or adjacent to the specimen extraction site. To enable a robust and clinically meaningful comparison for the primary outcome, the extraction-site incision type will be recorded for every patient, distinguishing muscle-splitting transverse or Pfannenstiel incisions from midline, off-midline, port-site and stoma-site incisions. As midline extraction sites carry a substantially higher incisional hernia risk than transverse incisions,13 14 recording incision type for all patients allows this to be accounted for in the analysis. The primary comparison will be time to incisional hernia within 24 months in the natural orifice specimen extraction cohort versus the transabdominal extraction cohort. Extraction-incision type will be included as a prespecified covariate in the multivariable and propensity-based adjustment models, and its influence will be further explored through subgroup and sensitivity analyses, including a comparison against non-midline (transverse or Pfannenstiel) extraction incisions.

Secondary objectives include evaluation of postoperative morbidity, surgical site infection, extraction-site morbidity, length of hospital stay, patient-reported quality-of-life outcomes, bowel function outcomes, urinary and sexual function outcomes, local recurrence, distant recurrence and disease-free survival.

Patient-reported outcomes

Patient-reported outcomes will be assessed using validated instruments for quality of life, body image, bowel function, urinary function and sexual function.

Core instruments administered to all participants will include the EuroQol 5-Dimension 5-Level questionnaire for global health-related quality of life,23 the Body Image Scale for body image and cosmetic perception,24 and the Low Anterior Resection Syndrome score for bowel dysfunction following rectal resection.25

Additional urinary and sexual function assessments will be performed selectively in patients undergoing pelvic colorectal procedures, including anterior and low anterior resections. Urinary function will be assessed using the International Prostate Symptom Score,26 while sexual function will be evaluated using the International Index of Erectile Function-5 in male participants27 and the Female Sexual Function Index-6 in female participants.28

Assessments will be performed preoperatively and repeated at 6 and 12 months following surgery. This approach was designed to balance longitudinal patient-reported outcome capture with participant burden across participating centres.

Follow-up

Patients will undergo routine postoperative follow-up according to local institutional protocols and disease-specific surveillance pathways. Clinical review, radiological investigations and oncological surveillance will be performed according to operative indication and institutional practice.

Patient-reported outcome assessments will be performed preoperatively and repeated at 6 and 12 months following surgery. Long-term follow-up for incisional hernia and oncological outcomes will be conducted up to 24 months following index surgery.

Incisional hernia may be identified through either clinical examination or cross-sectional imaging performed during routine postoperative or oncological surveillance.

Sample size

The registry aims to recruit approximately 800 patients across participating centres.

Given the observational design of the registry and anticipated multicentre expansion, a pragmatic sample size approach was adopted based on expected recruitment volume, anticipated incisional hernia event capture and feasibility of longitudinal follow-up across participating institutions.

Published literature suggests that extraction-site incisional hernia remains a clinically meaningful long-term complication following minimally invasive colorectal surgery.13 14 Recruitment of approximately 800 patients is anticipated to provide sufficient event capture to support adjusted comparative analyses of abdominal wall outcomes, oncological outcomes and patient-reported outcomes between planned extraction strategies.

The target sample size was additionally considered sufficient to support planned subgroup and propensity-adjusted analyses within the predefined NOSE-eligible cohort.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables will be summarised using frequencies and percentages. Continuous variables will be summarised using mean and SD or median and IQR as appropriate.

Comparative analyses between planned NOSE extraction and planned transabdominal extraction groups will be performed within the predefined NOSE-eligible cohort.

Time-to-event analyses for incisional hernia and disease-free survival will be performed using Kaplan-Meier methods and Cox proportional hazards regression.

Multivariable analyses will be performed to adjust for clinically relevant confounders including age, body mass index, operative indication, tumour location and operative approach. Propensity-adjusted analyses are additionally planned to account for treatment selection bias inherent to observational surgical practice.

Missing data will be handled using complete-case analysis or multiple imputation depending on the extent and pattern of missingness.

Prespecified subgroup analyses may include extraction route, operative platform and obesity status.

Data monitoring and governance

As an observational registry without investigational intervention allocation, a formal data monitoring committee is not planned.

Registry governance is coordinated by the principal investigators at Singapore General Hospital. Participating centres are responsible for obtaining local ethical approval and maintaining compliance with institutional governance and data protection requirements.

Data quality review and periodic verification may be performed by the coordinating centre to identify missing or inconsistent data entries.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not directly involved in study design, recruitment strategy or conduct of the registry. However, the registry was designed to incorporate longitudinal patient-reported outcomes including quality of life, body image, bowel function, urinary function and sexual function following minimally invasive colorectal surgery and NOSE procedures.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval for the registry has been obtained from the SingHealth Centralised Institutional Review Board (2026-0237). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrolment.

Data will be collected and stored in accordance with institutional data governance policies. Deidentified data may be used in future collaborative analyses subject to governance approval and data-sharing agreements.

Findings from the AP-NOSES registry will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, scientific conference presentations and collaborative academic initiatives.

Discussion

NOSE continues to evolve as an extension of minimally invasive colorectal surgery, with increasing adoption across colorectal units with advanced minimally invasive expertise. Although existing literature suggests potential benefits relating to postoperative recovery and wound morbidity, important methodological limitations remain within the current evidence base. Many published studies are retrospective and subject to substantial selection bias, with inconsistent reporting of NOSE eligibility, crossover cases and long-term abdominal wall outcomes. In addition, patient-reported and functional outcomes remain comparatively underrepresented despite their relevance to minimally invasive and natural orifice surgical approaches.

AP-NOSES was specifically designed to address several of these limitations through prospective multicentre enrolment, predefined NOSE eligibility criteria and longitudinal assessment of abdominal wall and patient-reported outcomes. Importantly, the registry incorporates a planned extraction strategy framework rather than relying solely on achieved extraction route analyses, which may reduce methodological ambiguity associated with crossover and intraoperative selection bias. The inclusion of a predefined NOSE-eligible transabdominal comparator cohort was intended to improve comparability between extraction strategies within routine colorectal surgical practice.

The primary focus on incisional hernia reflects increasing recognition of extraction-site morbidity as a clinically important long-term outcome following minimally invasive colorectal surgery.13 In addition to abdominal wall outcomes, the registry incorporates longitudinal assessment of body image, bowel function, urinary function and sexual function using validated patient-reported outcome instruments. These outcomes are particularly relevant following transanal and transvaginal extraction techniques, where functional concerns continue to influence adoption despite increasing technical feasibility and growing clinical experience.

Nevertheless, the registry remains observational in design and residual confounding related to patient selection, tumour characteristics and surgeon preference may persist despite adjusted analyses. Participating centres are also expected to represent relatively high-volume minimally invasive colorectal units, which may affect generalisability to lower-volume settings.

Despite these limitations, AP-NOSES represents one of the first prospective multicentre registries specifically designed to evaluate long-term abdominal wall, functional and patient-reported outcomes following NOSE using predefined eligibility criteria and standardised prospective data collection. The registry may also provide a collaborative platform for future multicentre analyses evaluating evolving NOSE techniques and minimally invasive colorectal reconstructive strategies.

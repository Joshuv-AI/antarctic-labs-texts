Alternative approach to time-delay interferometry with optical frequency comb
Kohei Yamamoto, Hannah Tomio, Charlotte Zehnder, Kenji Numata, Holly Leopardi

Abstract
Spaceborne gravitational wave observatories, exemplified by the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) mission, are designed to remove laser noise and clock noise from interferometric phase measurements in postprocessing. The planned observatories will utilize electro-optic modulators (EOMs) to encode the onboard clock timing onto the beam phase. Recent research has demonstrated the advantage of introducing an optical frequency comb (OFC) in the metrology system with the modified framework of time-delay interferometry (TDI): the removal of the EOM and the simultaneous suppression of the stochastic jitter of the laser and the clock in the observation band. In this paper, we explore an alternative approach with the OFC-based metrology system. We report that after proper treatment, it is possible to use the measured carrier-carrier heterodyne frequencies to monitor the time derivative of the pseudoranges, which represent the physical light travel time and the clock difference. This approach does not require changing the existing TDI framework, as previous OFC based efforts did. We also present the experimental demonstration of our scheme using two separate systems to model two spacecraft. Using this novel approach, we synchronize the two independent phase measurement systems with an accuracy better than 0.47 ns 0.47\text{\,}\mathrm{ns} , while the stochastic jitter in the observation band is suppressed down to the setup sensitivity around the LISA performance levels at 15 pm − 1 15\text{\,}\mathrm{pm}\text{\,}{\mathrm{}}^{-1} .



I
Introduction

Since the first detection of a gravitational wave (GW) from stellar-mass binary black holes in 2015 by the Laser Interferometer Gravitational Wave Observatory (LIGO) detectors
1
, the field has been rapidly advancing.
Today, GW observations by ground-based detectors are no longer a rare event
8
;
9
;
10
.
To open a new observation channel in the millihertz band, international research teams have studied and proposed several spaceborne GW detector concepts.
One of these spaceborne observatories, the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) mission led by the European Space Agency (ESA)
4
, is planned to be launched in 2035.

LISA forms a nearly equilateral triangle constellation with three spacecraft separated over
2.5\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{i}\mathrm{l}\mathrm{l}\mathrm{i}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{n}\,\mathrm{k}\mathrm{m}
.
The three spacecraft exchange laser beams to configure interspacecraft interferometers.
GW signals are accumulated over the interspacecraft laser links and encoded in the phase of the laser beams, which are measured by an onboard phasemeter.
Given realistic LISA orbits provided by ESA
3
, the relative positions of the spacecraft drift by around
10^{8}

\mathrm{m}
with a relative speed of up to
10\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\text{\,}{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}
.
This results in the spacecraft constellation having unequal-arm interferometer geometries.
The resulting phase signal is overwhelmed by laser frequency noise, masking the
10\text{\,}\mathrm{pm}
-level fluctuations imparted by the GW signals.
To suppress the laser noise by around 8 orders of magnitude, a postprocessing technique called time-delay interferometry (TDI) will be utilized to carefully time-shift the phase signals from the three spacecraft with
\mathrm{n}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{s}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{d}
accuracy to virtually synthesize equal-arm interferometers.
This in turn requires proper treatment of the onboard clock differences between the three spacecraft
15
.
For this purpose, each spacecraft in the LISA constellation embeds its onboard clock information on the outgoing beam phase via an electro-optic modulator (EOM), and monitors the clock difference via the phase of a heterodyne beatnote between its own clock modulation sideband and that of the incoming beam
13
;
28
.
The measured clock differences can be used to remove the clock jitter in the observation band and to correct the slow drift of the timestamps in postprocessing.

Tinto and Yu
21
have analytically shown that the laser and clock noise can be suppressed by means of an optical frequency comb (OFC)
16
;
22
;
7
, which has been generalized to given TDI combinations later by Tan et al.
18
.
This would remove the need for the EOMs and clock modulation of the interspacecraft link.
By using the laser carrier as a reference to lock one of the OFC modes, a radio frequency (RF) signal that is coherent with the reference laser can be generated.
If we use the OFC-generated RF signal as a system clock for the onboard phase measurement systems, the laser noise and the clock noise are no longer independent in the phasemeter output data streams.
As a result, the adaptation of TDI with scaling factors for the phase signals enables the suppression of these noise factors simultaneously.
This concept has been experimentally demonstrated by simulating the large delay between spacecraft using acousto-optic modulators with a single phase measurement system
23
.

In this paper, we propose an alternative noise suppression approach based on the use of OFCs.
This approach leverages the carrier-carrier beatnote frequency not only for the main scientific purpose of detecting GWs but also for monitoring the so-called
pseudorange
(or, to be precise, its time derivative): the difference between the time of the light emission in the time frame of the onboard clock on an emitting spacecraft and the time of the light reception in the time frame of the onboard clock on a receiving spacecraft.
Since this approach follows that of LISA except for the method of extracting the pseudoranges, the traditional TDI framework can be applied with no modification.
In addition, our approach does not detract from the original advantage mentioned in
21
: namely, the simultaneous suppression of the laser and clock noises, as the conventional TDI framework can directly work on the pseudoranges and suppress the laser and clock noise in one step as well
12
.
Furthermore, while in the original OFC-based approach, the discussion was limited to the stochastic jitter in the GW observation band, this alternative approach also extracts information of the clock frequency offset and drift from the carrier-carrier beatnotes.
The results presented here maximize the technological advantages of incorporating OFCs.
Finally, we describe an experimental demonstration using two separate systems that model two spacecraft to study the ability to extract the total clock difference.

Theoretical description

We begin by providing a theoretical description of our scheme, focusing on how to extract the time derivative of the interspacecraft pseudorange from the carrier-carrier beatnotes.
After proper treatment, these beatnotes can serve the same role as the clock sideband-sideband beatnotes in the LISA-like EOM-based approach.
Our model is constructed based on a possible payload equipped with an OFC, as depicted conceptually in

Although the actual payload also needs test mass interferometers to monitor the test mass motion relative to the optical bench, these are omitted in the figure as they are not necessary to derive the pseudorange.
Regarding the notation, the three spacecraft are labeled by

II.1
Lasers and clocks

Let us start with a model of the frequency of a laser and an OFC-based clock.
All signals in this subsection are written according to the proper time of the spacecraft.

We model the laser frequency on spacecraft
i
with the following three terms:

\displaystyle\nu_{i}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\nu_{c}+O_{i}(\tau)+\dot{p}_{i}(\tau).

(1)

where each term has the following definition and order of magnitude: the nominal central frequency of the onboard laser
\nu_{c}\sim$281.6\text{\,}\mathrm{THz}$
, the large offset frequency
O_{i}\sim

\mathcal{O}\left($10\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}$\right)
assuming a LISA-like heterodyne bandwidth, and the stochastic jitter
\dot{p}_{i}\sim$100\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}\text{\,}{\mathrm{}}^{-1}$
in the observation band (letting
p_{i}
be the laser phase noise following convention).

We generate a clock signal from the laser frequency
\nu_{i}
using an OFC.
In general, the frequency of a given mode of OFC
i
with integer mode number
n_{i}
is written as:

\displaystyle f_{n_{i}}

\displaystyle=n_{i}f_{\mathrm{rep},i}+f_{\mathrm{ceo},i},

(2)

where
f_{\mathrm{rep},i}
is the repetition rate and
f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}
is the carrier-envelope offset frequency of the OFC.
To have
f_{\mathrm{rep},i}
coherent with the laser frequency, two control loops must be properly locked: the
f_{\mathrm{ceo}}
loop that detects the carrier-envelope offset via the so-called “
f-2f
” self-referencing scheme
16
;
19
and that actuates the pump power of the comb laser, and an offset-lock loop on the heterodyne beatnote between a reference laser
\nu_{\mathrm{i}}
and the comb mode
f_{n_{i}}
, which actuates the cavity length of the comb laser.
Given the carrier-envelope offset is locked to a setpoint
f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}
, the offset-lock loop robustly achieves the following:

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}

\displaystyle=\nu_{i}(\tau)-f_{n_{i}}(\tau),

(3)

where
f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}
is the set point of the offset-lock loop.
Note that
f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}
in
eq.

3
can be either positive or negative depending on the relation between
\nu_{i}
and
f_{n_{i}}
.
Solving
eq.

3
for
f_{\mathrm{rep},i}
results in:

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{rep},i}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu_{i}(\tau)-f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}}{n_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu_{c}}{n_{i}}\left(1+\frac{O_{i}(\tau)+\dot{p}_{i}(\tau)-f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}}{\nu_{c}}\right).

(4)

This is the onboard clock frequency written in the frame of the spacecraft proper time.

If we define a nominal clock frequency in this frame as
f_{c}
, our onboard clock has the following clock error in units of fractional frequency:

\displaystyle\dot{q}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{\mathrm{rep},i}(\tau)-f_{c}}{f_{c}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu_{c}}{n_{i}f_{c}}\left(1+\frac{O_{i}(\tau)+\dot{p}_{i}(\tau)-f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}}{\nu_{c}}\right)-1

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{c}}{n_{i}}\left(1+\frac{O_{i}(\tau)+\dot{p}_{i}(\tau)-f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}}{\nu_{c}}\right)-1,

(5)

under

\displaystyle f_{c}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\nu_{c}/n_{c},

(6)

where
n_{c}
is the nominal mode number that represents a certain comb mode.
One practical way to determine the nominal values is the following: (1) define the nominal clock rate
f_{c}
to be consistent with the sampling rate used in the data processing; (2) set
\nu_{c}
at a reasonable middle frequency in the laser frequency range; (3) set
n_{c}=[\nu_{c}/f_{c}]
; (4) fine-tune
\nu_{c}
to meet
\nu_{c}=n_{c}f_{c}
.

II.2
Onboard clock frame

We then transform the reference time frame from the proper time of the spacecraft to that of the onboard clock.
This gives the actual onboard measurements.

In general, a clock time
\tau_{i}
as a function of another time
\tau_{m}
, acting as a reference frame, can be expressed by:

\displaystyle\tau_{i}^{\tau_{m}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\tau+\delta\tau_{i}^{\tau_{m}}(\tau),

(7)

\displaystyle\delta\tau_{i}^{\tau_{m}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=q_{i}(\tau)+T_{0,i},

(8)

where we call
\delta\tau_{i}^{\tau_{m}}(\tau)
the
timer deviation
, which is composed of the clock deviation
q_{i}(\tau)
and an initial offset of the clock start time
T_{0,i}
.
The argument
\tau
is a given time counted by the reference frame:
\tau_{i}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)=\tau
.
Inversely,
\tau_{m}
can be expressed according to
\tau_{i}
as

\displaystyle\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\tau+\delta\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)=\tau-\delta\tau_{i}^{\tau_{m}}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right),

(9)

which can be acquired by substituting
\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}
into
\tau
in
eq.

7

\tau^{\tau_{i}}_{m}\left(\tau^{\tau_{m}}_{i}(\tau)\right)=\tau
.

The next step is to relate the phase of an electromagnetic wave in different time frames.
As a scalar quantity, the phase
\phi
of an electromagnetic wave can transform from one time frame to another using a simple time shift

\displaystyle\phi^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\tau_{m}}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\tau_{m}}\left(\tau+\delta\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right).

(10)

Hence, the frequency of the electromagnetic wave
\nu
can be derived via the time derivative of
eq.

10

\displaystyle\nu^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{d\phi^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=\nu^{\tau_{m}}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)\cdot\frac{d\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu^{\tau_{m}}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)}{1+\dot{q}_{i}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)}.

(11)

We apply this general expression to our laser and clock models in
eqs.

1
and
5
, with
\tau_{m}
being the proper time of the spacecraft,
\tau_{i}
being the OFC-generated onboard clock and
\nu
being the laser frequency
\nu_{i}
.
Substituting
eqs.

1
and
5
into
eq.

11
, the frequency of laser
i
according to the onboard clock
\tau_{i}
is:

\displaystyle\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{i}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{i}}{n_{c}}\cdot\frac{\nu_{c}+O_{i}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)+\dot{p}_{i}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)}{1+\frac{O_{i}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)+\dot{p}_{i}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)-f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}}{\nu_{c}}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{n_{i}}{n_{c}}\cdot\left(\nu_{c}+f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}+f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}\right)\cdot\left(1-\frac{O_{i}(O_{i}-f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},i})}{\nu_{c}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{n_{i}}{n_{c}}\cdot\left(\nu_{c}+f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}+f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}\right).

(12)

It is reasonable, in practice, to assume that
f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}
,
f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}
, and
O_{i}
all have an order of magnitude of
10\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
.
Therefore, in the second line, the last term is around
($10\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}$/$281\text{\,}\mathrm{THz}$)^{2}\approx 1.3\cdot 10^{-15}

Compared with the synchronization accuracy of

required for LISA, this can be considered negligible over a reasonably long time of

, which results in the last line.

eq.

12

In a similar manner, we can derive the frequency of laser

in the local OFC clock frame as:

\displaystyle\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{i^{\prime}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{i}}{n_{c}}\cdot\frac{\nu_{c}+O_{i^{\prime}}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)+\dot{p}_{i^{\prime}}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)}{1+\frac{O_{i}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)+\dot{p}_{i}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)-f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}}{\nu_{c}}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{n_{i}}{n_{c}}\cdot\left(\nu_{c}+O_{ii^{\prime}}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)+\dot{p}_{ii^{\prime}}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)+f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}+f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}\right),

(13)

where we define a given quantity with two subscripts

\displaystyle x_{ij}(\tau)\coloneqq x_{j}(\tau)-x_{i}(\tau).

(14)

We use this notation throughout the rest of this paper.

From

\displaystyle\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ii^{\prime}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{i^{\prime}}(\tau)-\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{i}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{i}}{n_{c}}\cdot\left(O_{ii^{\prime}}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)+\dot{p}_{ii^{\prime}}\left(\tau_{m}^{\tau_{i}}(\tau)\right)\right).

(15)

II.3
Pseudorange estimation

Finally, we show how to estimate the time derivative of the interspacecraft pseudorange using the carrier-carrier beatnote measurements.
The pseudorange is defined as the time difference between the emission and the reception of the laser light according to the onboard clocks on the emitting and receiving spacecraft, respectively
12
.
Hence, the phase of the laser
j
, namely
\phi_{j}
, at the reception by spacecraft
i
in the time frame of its clock
i
is related via the pseudorange
d^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}
to the phase of the laser at its emission by spacecraft
j
in the time frame of the clock
j
as follows:

\displaystyle\phi^{\tau_{i}}_{j}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\tau_{j}}_{j}\left(\tau-d^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)\right).

(16)

Ideally, we measure the phase of a beatnote between the primary lasers associated with the onboard clocks.
Let us assume that this measurement exists for now; in that case, such an interspacecraft interferometer at spacecraft
i
reads:

\displaystyle\phi^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\tau_{i}}_{j}(\tau)-\phi^{\tau_{i}}_{i}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\tau_{j}}_{j}\left(\tau-d^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)\right)-\phi^{\tau_{i}}_{i}(\tau).

(17)

The measurement in units of frequency
\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}
can be derived by the time derivative,

\displaystyle\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{d\phi^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=\dot{\mathbf{D}}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}\nu^{\tau_{j}}_{j}(\tau)-\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{i}(\tau).

(18)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{D}}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}f(\tau)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(1-\dot{d}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)\right)\cdot f\left(\tau-d^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)\right).

(19)

eq.

19
defines the Doppler delay operator with an arbitrary function
f(\tau)
, following the notation in
12
.

Each frequency is expressed in the time frame of the associated onboard clock.
Therefore, substituting
eq.

12
into
eq.

18
gives

\displaystyle\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{d\phi^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\dot{d}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)\right)\cdot\frac{n_{j}}{n_{c}}\left(\nu_{c}+f_{\mathrm{OPT},j}+f_{\mathrm{ceo},j}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{n_{i}}{n_{c}}\left(\nu_{c}+f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}+f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}\right).

(20)

Solving this equation for the time derivative of the pseudorange
\dot{d}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}
, results in:

\displaystyle\dot{d}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{n_{i}}{n_{j}}\cdot\left(1+\frac{\frac{n_{c}}{n_{i}}\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)-f_{\mathrm{OPT},ij}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},ij}}{\nu_{c}}\right),

(21)

where
f_{\mathrm{OPT},ij}=f_{\mathrm{OPT},j}-f_{\mathrm{OPT},i}
and
f_{\mathrm{ceo},ij}=f_{\mathrm{ceo},j}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},i}
are the differences in the set point of the OFC feedback loops between the two spacecraft.

This derivation is based on the assumption that we have a direct measure of
\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}
.
However, this is not the case in a real system because the primary laser
i
faces the secondary laser
j^{\prime}
on the opposing spacecraft; that is, only a direct measurement of
\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ij^{\prime}}
is possible.
Likewise, with regard to the pseudorange time derivative
\dot{d}^{\tau_{i}}_{ik}
, the secondary laser
i^{\prime}
is linked to the primary laser
k
of the other opposing spacecraft, resulting in the beatnote
\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{i^{\prime}k}
.
Ultimately, we need to combine the interspacecraft interferometer measurements with those of the reference interferometers, given in
eq.

15
, to accurately estimate the time derivative of the pseudoranges.

First, following
eq.

21
, let us naively estimate the left- and right-hand pseudoranges using the actual interspacecraft interferometers as:

\displaystyle\hat{\dot{d}}^{\tau_{i},(0)}_{ij}(\tau)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{n_{i}}{n_{j}}\cdot\left(1+\frac{\frac{n_{c}}{n_{i}}\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ij^{\prime}}(\tau)-f_{\mathrm{OPT},ij}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},ij}}{\nu_{c}}\right),

(22a)

\displaystyle\hat{\dot{d}}^{\tau_{i},(0)}_{ik}(\tau)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{n_{i}}{n_{k}}\cdot\left(1+\frac{\frac{n_{c}}{n_{i}}\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{i^{\prime}k}(\tau)-f_{\mathrm{OPT},ik}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},ik}}{\nu_{c}}\right),

(22b)

where the interspacecraft interferometers are

\displaystyle\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ij^{\prime}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\dot{\mathbf{D}}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}\nu^{\tau_{j}}_{j^{\prime}}(\tau)-\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{i}(\tau),

(23a)

\displaystyle\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{i^{\prime}k}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\dot{\mathbf{D}}^{\tau_{i}}_{ik}\nu^{\tau_{k}}_{k}(\tau)-\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{i^{\prime}}(\tau).

(23b)

The hat symbol in
eq.

22
indicates that this is an estimate, rather than a true value.
Let us call
eq.

22
the zeroth estimate, denoted by the integer superscripts
(0)
.

The zeroth estimates of the pseudoranges in
eq.

22
are expected to lack sufficient accuracy because the secondary lasers with the primed indices are not directly related to the clocks.
However, ideally, we can derive the virtual beatnote frequency between the primary lasers by combining the interspacecraft and reference interferometers as:

\displaystyle\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ij^{\prime}}(\tau)-\dot{\mathbf{D}}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}\nu^{\tau_{j}}_{jj^{\prime}}(\tau),

(24a)

\displaystyle\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ik}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{i^{\prime}k}(\tau)+\nu^{\tau_{k}}_{ii^{\prime}}(\tau).

(24b)

However, the Doppler delay operator in
eq.

24a
itself includes the pseudorange
d^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}
and its time derivative
\dot{d}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}
, which we seek to estimate.
Therefore, we can iterate over the following self-consistent equations to achieve an estimate with high accuracy:

\displaystyle\hat{\dot{d}}^{\tau_{i},(m+1)}_{ij}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\hat{\dot{d}}^{\tau_{i},(0)}_{ij}(\tau)+\frac{n_{c}}{n_{j}}\frac{1}{\nu_{c}}\hat{\dot{\mathbf{D}}}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}\nu^{\tau_{j}}_{jj^{\prime}}(\tau),

(25a)

\displaystyle\hat{\dot{d}}^{\tau_{i},(1)}_{ik}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\hat{\dot{d}}^{\tau_{i},(0)}_{ik}(\tau)-\frac{n_{c}}{n_{k}}\frac{1}{\nu_{c}}\nu^{\tau_{i}}_{ii^{\prime}}(\tau),

(25b)

where
m
is an integer representing the iteration order.

The right-hand pseudorange
\dot{d}^{\tau_{i}}_{ik}
in
eq.

25b
results in the true value with a single correction (that is,
\hat{\dot{d}}^{\tau_{i},(1)}_{ik}=\dot{d}^{\tau_{i}}_{ik}
).
The left-hand pseudorange in
eq.

25a
is less straightforward due to the Doppler delay operator.
Concerning this iterative operation, let us represent the error of the
m
-th estimate of the left-hand pseudorange
\hat{\dot{d}}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}
by
\hat{\dot{\delta}}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}
, where:

\displaystyle\hat{\dot{\delta}}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\hat{\dot{d}}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}(\tau)-\dot{d}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}(\tau).

(26)

In the following, we assume that the integrated version
\hat{\delta}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}
does not gain an additional error from the start-time offset, and
\hat{\delta}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}
can be easily calculated by the integral of
eq.

26
.

Taking into account
eq.

26
and
eq.

25a
, the
(m+1)
error becomes

\displaystyle\hat{\dot{\delta}}^{\tau_{i},(m+1)}_{ij}(\tau)

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{n_{c}}{n_{j}}\frac{1}{\nu_{c}}\left(\hat{\dot{\delta}}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}\mathbf{D}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}\nu^{\tau_{j}}_{jj^{\prime}}+\hat{\delta}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}\dot{\mathbf{D}}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}\dot{\nu}^{\tau_{j}}_{jj^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{n_{c}}{n_{j}}\frac{1}{\nu_{c}}\cdot\hat{\dot{\delta}}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}\mathbf{D}^{\tau_{i}}_{ij}\nu^{\tau_{j}}_{jj^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\sim O\left(\frac{$10\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}$}{$281\text{\,}\mathrm{THz}$}\cdot\hat{\dot{\delta}}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}\right)

(27)

where we neglect a cross-term between
\hat{\dot{\delta}}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}
and
\hat{\delta}^{\tau_{i},(m)}_{ij}
.
In the second line, the second term that scales with the time derivative of the heterodyne frequency is neglected, as the heterodyne frequency is expected to drift very slowly (less than
10\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}\text{\,}{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}
at the fastest
3

Due to the suppression factor of
$10\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}$/$281\text{\,}\mathrm{THz}$
, we believe that the iteration rapidly converges on the true value, and a single iteration might be enough for most cases.
Note that it would also be possible to suppress the readout noise coupling by constructively combining the two reference interferometers in the same spacecraft, for example, with
\nu^{\tau_{j}}_{jj^{\prime}}\rightarrow\left(\nu^{\tau_{j}}_{jj^{\prime}}-\nu^{\tau_{j}}_{j^{\prime}j}\right)/2
in
eq.

25a
.

In analogy to the sideband-sideband beatnote in the case of LISA, this suggests that we can derive the time derivative of the pseudorange from the carrier-carrier beatnote measurements in the OFC-based metrology system, provided that we know the OFC mode numbers
n_{i}
and
n_{j}
.
We provide a focused discussion on the resolution of the OFC mode number in
appendix

A
.
As long as the OFC mode numbers are identified and the pseudorange is derived accordingly, we can use the traditional TDI framework instead of modifying it as proposed in previous work
21
;
18
.

Note that the iterative operation with the reference interferometer in
eq.

25
is fairly analogous to the removal of right-hand modulation noise in the LISA-like EOM-based system
13
, which uses the sideband-sideband beatnotes in the reference interferometer.
The main differences are the following:
first, the modulation noise is only about the in-band jitter, while in the OFC case, all relative components of the laser frequencies (the offset, the slow drift, and the in-band jitter) need to be extracted through this iterative process;
second, the two reference interferometers in the same spacecraft are combined destructively to isolate the modulation noise from the laser noise in the EOM case, while they are combined constructively in the OFC case.

IV
Conclusion

Previous research
21
;
18
established the modified TDI frameworks using OFCs.
These efforts focused on how the in-band laser and clock noises appear in the interferometric phase measurements when OFC’s are incorporated.

In contrast, the alternative approach presented in this paper utilizes the carrier-carrier beatnotes to extract the time derivative of the pseudoranges, which is enabled by the coherence between the beam carriers and the OFC-derived onboard clock signals.
Compared with the EOM-based metrology adopted by LISA, this approach can be conceptually interpreted as the replacement of the clock sideband-sideband beatnote with the carrier-carrier beatnote after proper treatment.
Hence, no change in the classical TDI formulation is required.
Furthermore, the time derivative of the pseudoranges includes not only the in-band stochastic jitter but also the clock frequency offset, the Doppler effect, and their slow drift.
Therefore, this study is expected to help fully leverage the technological advantages of incorporating OFCs in space-based GW detectors.

To extract the time derivative of the pseudoranges from the carrier-carrier beatnotes, we need to identify the OFC mode numbers, as formulated in
section

II.3
.
In the experiment in
section

III.3
, we achieve this by fitting the comb mode integers such that the residual peak of the injected laser frequency tone at
1.0\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}
is minimized.
Once identified, this process does not have to be repeated unless the OFC feedback loops are broken.
We successfully synchronize the two separate phase measurement systems by an accuracy better than
0.47\text{\,}\mathrm{ns}
, which achieves the
3.3\text{\,}\mathrm{ns}
performance requirement of LISA.
The experiment also demonstrates the suppression of the in-band stochastic jitter down to our testbed sensitivity below the LISA performance level of
15\text{\,}\mathrm{pm}\text{\,}{\mathrm{}}^{-1}
.

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta f_{\mathrm{rep},i}(\tau)}{\Delta n_{i}}

which gives

0.355\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{m}
error in terms of the clock difference, which in turn amounts to a timing error of
3.55\text{\,}\mathrm{ms}
after
10\,000\text{\,}\mathrm{s}

This is a huge timing error; hence, the clock deviation
\dot{q}^{\tau_{1}}_{2}
is highly sensitive to the error of the integer difference.

On the other hand, if we assume a common offset
b
(that is,
n_{1}\rightarrow n_{1}+b
,
n_{2}\rightarrow n_{2}+b
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{d\dot{q}^{\tau_{1}}_{2}}{db}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{2}-n_{1}}{(n_{2}+b)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\nu^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau)}{\nu_{c}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{n_{2}-n_{1}}{n_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{n_{2}-n_{1}}{n_{2}}\cdot\frac{d\dot{q}^{\tau_{1}}_{2}}{da},

(37)

where
\frac{n_{2}-n_{1}}{n_{2}}\sim 10^{-3}-10^{-5}
.
Hence, the clock deviation
\dot{q}^{\tau_{1}}_{2}
is much less sensitive to the common offset

However, note that a common offset estimation error of

1\text{\,}\mathrm{m}

This is because the timing error due to the estimation error of the clock offset
\delta

\frac{n_{2}-n_{1}}{n_{2}}=10^{-5}

Considering
eqs.

36
and
37
and the associated investigations, it is essential to derive the exact OFC mode numbers.
Hence, we can perform an in-flight calibration run, e.g., TDIR to derive those OFC mode numbers, which means optimizing the integers so that the residual noise power in the TDI combinations is minimized.
Because the clock deviation is much more sensitive to the difference rather than the common offset, we can use the mode numbers’ difference (=
a
) and common offset (=
b

Importantly, the OFC mode numbers never change as long as the OFC feedback loops stay locked.
If the estimation protocol does not identify the exact integers and has an error in the offset
\hat{b}

However, this can be easily adjusted by adding a few integers to the estimate

Original scheme in our framework

To clarify the relation between the original scheme presented in

For conciseness, we assume an even simpler setup: OMS 2 in

2

Hence, there are only the main unprimed lasers in both OMS 1 and OMS 2, and they directly interfere to generate a heterodyne signal tracked by the phasemeters.

Since the scheme is optimized to suppress the stochastic term of the laser and clock noise, we decompose the clock deviation
\dot{q}^{\tau_{1}}_{2}
in
eq.

29
into the deterministic component
\dot{q}^{\tau_{1},o}_{2}
and the stochastic component
\dot{q}^{\tau_{1},\epsilon}_{2}
as

\displaystyle\dot{q}^{\tau_{1},o}_{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{n}_{1}}{\hat{n}_{2}}\left(1-\frac{O^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau)-f_{\mathrm{OPT},12}-f_{\mathrm{ceo},12}}{\nu_{c}}\right)-1,

(38)

\displaystyle\dot{q}^{\tau_{1},\epsilon}_{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{n}_{1}}{\hat{n}_{2}}\cdot\frac{\dot{p}^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau)}{\nu_{c}},

(39)

where
O^{\tau_{1}}_{12}=O^{\tau_{1}}_{2}-O^{\tau_{1}}_{1}
and
\dot{p}^{\tau_{1}}_{12}=\dot{p}^{\tau_{1}}_{2}-\dot{p}^{\tau_{1}}_{1}

We assume
f_{\mathrm{OPT},12}=f_{\mathrm{ceo},12}=0

Similarly, we decompose the general formula in
eq.

10
to represent the time frame transformation of the measured beatnote phase between the two onboard clocks:

\displaystyle\phi^{\tau_{2}}_{12}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\tau_{1}}_{12}\left(\tau_{1}^{\tau_{2}}(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle\approx\phi^{\tau_{1}}_{12}\left(\tau_{1}^{\tau_{2},o}(\tau)\right)-\frac{\nu^{\tau_{1}}_{12}\left(\tau_{1}^{\tau_{2},o}(\tau)\right)}{1+\dot{q}^{\tau_{1},o}_{2}\left(\tau_{1}^{\tau_{2},o}(\tau)\right)}\cdot q^{\tau_{1},\epsilon}_{2}\left(\tau_{1}^{\tau_{2},o}(\tau)\right),

(40)

which is the Taylor expansion of the phase around the deterministic part of clock 1 against clock 2, namely
\tau_{1}^{\tau_{2},o}
.

The time stamps can be synchronized by shifting
\phi^{\tau_{2}}_{12}
by the estimate of the deterministic part of the timer deviation
\delta\tau_{1}^{\tau_{2},o}
, which is, same as
eq.

31
, the sum of a fitted start time offset
\hat{T}_{0,2}
and the integration of
\dot{q}^{\tau_{1},o}_{2}
in
eq.

38
.
Regarding the stochastic component, if we substitute the integration of
\dot{q}^{\tau_{1},\epsilon}_{2}
in
eq.

39
into
eq.

40
, we get the following:

\displaystyle\phi^{\tau_{2}}_{12}\left(\tau+\delta\tau_{1}^{\tau_{2},o}(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\tau_{1}}_{12}\left(\tau\right)-\frac{\nu^{\tau_{1}}_{12}\left(\tau\right)}{1+\dot{q}^{\tau_{1},o}_{2}\left(\tau\right)}\cdot q^{\tau_{1},\epsilon}_{2}\left(\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\tau_{1}}_{12}\left(\tau\right)-\frac{\nu^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau)}{\nu_{c}+O^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau)}\cdot p^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau)

\displaystyle\approx\int^{\tau}_{0}O^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}+\frac{\nu_{c}}{\nu_{c}+O^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau)}\cdot p^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau),

\displaystyle\Delta^{\tau_{1}}_{b}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau)-\left(1+\frac{O^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau)}{\nu_{c}}\right)\cdot\phi^{\tau_{2}}_{12}\left(\tau+\delta\tau_{1}^{\tau_{2},o}(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{O^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau)}{\nu_{c}}\int^{\tau}_{0}O^{\tau_{1}}_{12}(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}.

(42)

Its existence in the actual TDI algorithm and impact may need to be further investigated.

The main advantage of this scheme over the alternative scheme presented in this paper is that it does not require the derivation of the OFC mode numbers
n_{i}
if we neglect the clock drift.
Once we consider the clock drift and estimate it from the carrier-carrier beatnotes, we encounter the same challenge as the alternative approach, which necessitates the derivation of the OFC mode numbers.

The Maximum of the Volume of a Cevian Simplex and its Parts
Yagub Aliyev

Abstract
It is well known that the area of the cevian triangle for an interior point M M of a triangle γ \gamma is at most 1 4 \frac{1}{4} of the area of the triangle γ \gamma . This can be generalized for n n -dimensional simplex A 1 ​ A 2 ​ … ​ A n + 1 A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1} using the concept of barycentric coordinates. The method also helps with the solution of a new optimization problem for the volume of the simplex M ​ N 1 ​ … ​ N n MN_{1}\ldots N_{n} , where N 1 , … , N n N_{1},\ldots,N_{n} are the intersection points of the lines A i ​ M A_{i}M with the opposite hyperplanes of the simplex A 1 ​ A 2 ​ … ​ A n + 1 A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1} . This generalizes the recently considered special cases n = 2 n=2 and n = 3 n=3 .

Theorem 1

Given an
n
-simplex with vertices
A_{1}
,
A_{2},\ldots
,
A_{n+1}
and any point
M
in its interior,
let
N_{i}
be the point on the hyperplane opposite
A_{i}
such that
A_{i}
,
M
, and
N_{i}
are
collinear. Then

\textnormal{Volume}(N_{1}N_{2}\ldots N_{n+1})\leq\frac{\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1})}{n^{n}},

[formula]

and the equality holds true only when
\lambda_{1}={\lambda_{2}}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n+1})
be the barycentric coordinates of
M
with respect to the simplex
A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1}
, let
R_{i}
denote the distance from
M
to
A_{i}
, and let
s_{i}
denote the length of
MN_{i}
. It is well known that
\lambda_{i}=\frac{s_{i}}{R_{i}+s_{i}}
and
\lambda_{1}+{\lambda_{2}}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}=1
(see
[
8
]
;
[
2
]
, p. 124-126). So,
\frac{s_{i}}{R_{i}}=\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}}
. Therefore,

\textnormal{Volume}(MN_{1}N_{2}\ldots N_{n})=\textnormal{Volume}(MA_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}}

=\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1})\lambda_{n+1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}}

[formula]

=\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1})\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}{\lambda_{i}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sum_{j\neq i}\lambda_{i}}\right)

\leq\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1})\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}{\lambda_{i}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n\sqrt[n]{\prod_{j\neq i}\lambda_{i}}}\right)

=\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1})\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}{\lambda_{i}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{n^{n}\sqrt[n]{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{n-1}\right)\lambda_{n+1}^{n}}}\right)

=\frac{\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1})}{n^{n}}\sqrt[n]{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}}

\leq\frac{\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1})}{n^{n}}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}}{n}

=\frac{\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1})}{n^{n+1}}(1-\lambda_{n+1}).

Similarly, for each
i=1,2,\ldots,n+1

\textnormal{Volume}(MN_{1}N_{2}\ldots N_{i-1}N_{i+1}\ldots,N_{n+1})\leq\frac{\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1})}{n^{n+1}}(1-\lambda_{i}).

Therefore

\textnormal{Volume}(N_{1}N_{2}\ldots N_{n+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\textnormal{Volume}(MN_{1}N_{2}\ldots N_{i-1}N_{i+1}\ldots,N_{n})=

\leq\frac{\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1})}{n^{n+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(1-\lambda_{i})=\frac{\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1})}{n^{n}}.

\square

We can now prove the following surprising result using the formula (2).

Theorem 2

In the notations of Theorem 1

\textnormal{Volume}(MN_{1}N_{2}\ldots N_{n})\leq\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{(n-\theta_{n})^{n+3}}\cdot\textnormal{Volume}(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1}),

[formula]

where
\theta_{n}=\frac{n+1-\sqrt{n^{2}+2n-3}}{2}
.

Proof.

Since the function
F(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n+1})=\lambda_{n+1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}}
from (2) is continuous in the simplex
A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n+1}
, by classical result in analysis (see e.g.
[
12
]
, Theorem 4.16)
F
attains both maximum and minimum values in this simplex, which is a compact set. On the faces of the simplex
F=0
, which is the minimum. So, the maximum occurs in the interior of the simplex.
For fixed
{\lambda_{i}}+{\lambda_{j}}
the product
\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{j}}{1-\lambda_{j}}
reaches the maximum when
\lambda_{i}={\lambda_{j}}
. Therefore, for fixed
\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\lambda_{i}}=nx
the product
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}}
reaches the maximum when
\lambda_{1}={\lambda_{2}}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}=x
. Then
\lambda_{n+1}=1-nx
and

\lambda_{n+1}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}}=\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right)^{n}\cdot\left(1-nx\right)=:f(x).

By studying the function
f(x)
for the maximum in interval
\left(0,\frac{1}{n}\right)
we find that

f^{\prime}(x)=\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right)^{n}\cdot\frac{n\left(x^{2}-(n+1)x+1\right)}{x\left(1-x\right)},

and

x=\theta_{n}
is the only maximum point in
\left(0,\frac{1}{n}\right)
. Therefore,

f(x)\leq f\left(\theta_{n}\right)=\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{(n-\theta_{n})^{n+3}}.

\square

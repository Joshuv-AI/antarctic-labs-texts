Emergence of charm-strange dibaryons with negative parity via baryon-baryon interactions
Yu-Yue Cui 1, Xiao-Mei Tang 1, Qi Huang 2, Rui Chen 1,3

Abstract
Within the framework of the one-boson-exchange model, we systematically perform a coupled channel analysis of the P − P- wave interactions between a charm baryon and light baryon, the involved channels include Ξ c ​ N \Xi_{c}N , Λ c ​ Σ \Lambda_{c}\Sigma , Ξ c ′ ​ N \Xi_{c}^{\prime}N , Σ c ​ Λ \Sigma_{c}\Lambda , Ξ c ∗ ​ N \Xi_{c}^{*}N , Σ c ∗ ​ Λ \Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda , Σ c ​ Σ \Sigma_{c}\Sigma , and Σ c ∗ ​ Σ \Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma . Our results can predict several possible molecular candidates, such as a Ξ c ′ ​ N \Xi_{c}^{\prime}N molecule with I ⁡ ( J P ) = 0 ​ ( 1 − ) I(J^{P})=0(1^{-}) , Ξ c ∗ ​ N \Xi_{c}^{*}N molecules with 0 ​ ( 0 − , 1 − , 2 − ) 0(0^{-},1^{-},2^{-}) , Σ c ​ Σ \Sigma_{c}\Sigma molecules with 0 ​ ( 1 − ) 0(1^{-}) and 1 ​ ( 1 − ) 1(1^{-}) , and Σ c ∗ ​ Σ \Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma molecules with 0 ​ ( 0 − , 1 − , 2 − ) 0(0^{-},1^{-},2^{-}) , and 1 ​ ( 2 − ) 1(2^{-}) . The coupled channel effects significantly influence the formation of the Σ c ​ Σ \Sigma_{c}\Sigma molecule with 1 ​ ( 1 − ) 1(1^{-}) . Furthermore, we analyze the phase shifts for these coupled channel systems. Our analysis not only confirms the existence of the predicted molecules but also identifies potential resonant dibaryons, including a Σ c ​ Σ \Sigma_{c}\Sigma shape-type resonance with 1 ​ ( 1 − ) 1(1^{-}) , a Σ c ∗ ​ Σ \Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma shape-type resonance with 1 ​ ( 0 − ) 1(0^{-}) , a Λ c ​ Σ / Σ c ​ Σ \Lambda_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}\Sigma coupled Feshbach-type resonance with 1 ​ ( 1 − ) 1(1^{-}) , and Λ c ​ Σ / Σ c ∗ ​ Σ \Lambda_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma coupled Feshbach-type resonances with 1 ​ ( 0 − , 2 − ) 1(0^{-},2^{-}) .

I
introduction

In recent decades, experiments have reported a series of new hadron structures, which not only enrich the hadron spectrum, but also provide new insights for understanding the complex non-perturbative behavior in Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). Among these newly discovered structures, many of them exhibit two key characteristics: first, their properties are inconsistent with the predictions of the conventional quark model, where baryons are three-quark states and mesons are quark-antiquark pairs; second, their masses are very close to some specific threshold of a pair of traditional hadrons, such as the
P_{c}/P_{cs}

T_{cc}
state, and others. Thus, theorists are inspired to propose various explanations, such as molecular states, compact multiquarks, hybrids, glueballs, hadro-charmonium states, and kinematical effects (see review papers

Among the various explanations for new hadron structures, the molecular states have received significant attentions due to its simple physical image and its success in explaining so many exotic states. Under this framework, a deeper understanding of hadron interactions then becomes so important, since it not only can uncover the formation mechanisms of hadronic molecules, but also can predict new states. II
System description

In this work, we investigate the
P-
wave interactions between a heavy baryon and light baryon by using the OBE model. The general procedures for deducing the OBE effective potentials can be divided into three steps. Firstly, one can write down the scattering amplitudes in
t-
channel based on the relevant effective Lagrangians. Secondly, we can derive the OBE effective potentials in momentum space in a Breit approximation, i.e.,
\mathcal{V}_{E}(\bm{q})=-{\mathcal{M}_{E}}/{{\sqrt{\prod_{i}2M_{i}\prod_{f}2M_{f}}}}
. Here,
M_{i}
and
M_{f}
are the masses of the initial states and final states, respectively. Finally, we can obtain the effective potentials in coordinate space by performing a Fourier transformation, i.e.,
\mathcal{V}_{E}({r})=\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}\mathcal{V}_{E}(\bm{q})\mathcal{F}^{2}(q^{2},m_{E}^{2})
, here,
\mathcal{F}^{2}(q^{2},m_{E}^{2})
is a form factor, which is introduced to compensate the off-shell effects of the exchanged mesons. In this work, we use the monopole-type form factor,
\mathcal{F}(q^{2},m_{E}^{2})=(\Lambda^{2}-m_{E}^{2})/(\Lambda^{2}-q^{2})
, with
\Lambda
,
m_{E}
, and
q
being the cutoff, the mass, and four-momentum of the exchanged mesons, respectively. According to the experience of the nucleon-nucleon interactions
Tornqvist:1993ng
;
Tornqvist:1993vu
, the reasonable cutoff value is taken around 1.00 GeV.

III.2
Resonances

In the previous subsection, we not only identified the existence of several potential molecular candidates, but also observed that the OBE interactions from other coupled channel systems can be sufficiently strong, thereby motivating the search for possible resonances. The corresponding coupled channel systems are summarized as follows,

\displaystyle 0(0^{-}):

\displaystyle\Xi_{c}N/\Xi_{c}^{*}N,\quad\Xi_{c}^{*}N/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma,

\displaystyle 0(1^{-}):

\displaystyle\Xi_{c}N/\Xi_{c}^{\prime}N/\Xi_{c}^{*}N,

\displaystyle 0(2^{-}):

\displaystyle\Xi_{c}N/\Xi_{c}^{*}N,\quad\Xi_{c}^{*}N/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma,

\displaystyle 1(0^{-}):

\displaystyle\Lambda_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma,\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma,

\displaystyle 1(1^{-}):

\displaystyle\Lambda_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}\Sigma,\Sigma_{c}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}\Sigma,

\displaystyle 1(2^{-}):

\displaystyle\Lambda_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma,\quad\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma.

As is well known, the resonances appear as the phase shifts equal
\delta(E_{r})=(n+1/2)\pi
with
n=0,1,2,\cdots
This leads to the maximum of the scattering cross section,
\sigma_{t}=\frac{4\pi}{2\mu E}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2l+1)\text{sin}^{2}\delta_{l}(E)
. The resonant width is defined as
\Gamma_{r}=2/\left(\frac{d\delta}{dE}\right)_{E_{r}}
.

\Xi_{c}^{*}N
channel. In conclusion, this resonance is not an independent state but corresponds to the
\Xi_{c}^{*}N
molecule with
0(0^{-})
. Although no new state appears in the coupled channel phase shift analysis, these results provide important information on the total decay width of the
\Xi_{c}^{*}N

0(0^{-})

\Xi_{c}N/\Xi_{c}^{\prime}N/\Xi_{c}^{*}N

0(1^{-})

\Xi_{c}^{*}N
threshold, while the narrow resonance, with a width around fifty MeV, is near the
\Xi_{c}^{\prime}N
threshold. The cutoff dependence of the decay widths indicates that the broad and narrow resonances are associated with the
\Xi_{c}^{*}N
and
\Xi_{c}^{\prime}N
channels, respectively. After comparing the bound state solutions for the
\Xi_{c}^{\prime}N/\Sigma_{c}\Lambda/\Xi_{c}^{*}N/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma

in Table
7

\Xi_{c}^{\prime}N
and
\Xi_{c}^{*}N

4.

\Xi_{c}N/\Xi_{c}^{*}N
coupled interactions with
0(2^{-})

A resonance appears in the reasonable cutoff region, and this state is closely related to the
\Xi_{c}^{*}N

0(2^{-})

9

5.

\Xi_{c}^{*}N/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma

0(2^{-})

\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma

0(2^{-})
, as predicted in Table
12
.

\Lambda_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma

1(0^{-})

\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma
and
\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda

\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma

\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma

\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma

1(0^{-})

\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma

1(0^{-})

1(0^{-})

\Lambda_{c}\Sigma
channel plays a positive role in generating this shape-type resonance.

8.

\Lambda_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}\Sigma

1(1^{-})

A resonance appears in a reasonable cutoff region, with the
\Sigma_{c}\Sigma
channel playing an important role. The inflection point of the decay width occurs near the
\Sigma_{c}\Sigma
threshold. A further check of the phase shifts of the
\Lambda_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}\Sigma
coupled interactions, the
\Sigma_{c}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}\Sigma
coupled interactions, and the single
\Sigma_{c}\Sigma
interactions with

\Lambda_{c}\Sigma
and
\Sigma_{c}\Sigma

9.

\Sigma_{c}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}\Sigma
coupled interactions with
1(1^{-})

A resonance appears in a reasonable cutoff region, with the
\Sigma_{c}\Sigma
channel playing a crucial role. The phase shift analysis for the single
\Sigma_{c}\Sigma
channel with
1(1^{-})
shows a resonance near the
\Sigma_{c}\Sigma
threshold, and the obtained resonant parameters are very similar to those for the
\Sigma_{c}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}\Sigma

1(1^{-})
. Thus, this resonance is a shape-type resonance.

10.

\Lambda_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma
coupled interactions with
1(2^{-})

A resonance appears in the reasonable cutoff region. After comparing the phase shifts under the
\Lambda_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma

\Sigma_{c}\Sigma
channel interactions, and the
\Lambda_{c}\Sigma/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma

\Lambda_{c}\Sigma
and
\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma
channels playing key roles.

11.

\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Lambda/\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma
coupled interactions with
1(2^{-})
:
A resonance is obtained with a cutoff around 1.00 GeV. This state is closely related to the
\Sigma_{c}^{*}\Sigma
molecule with
1(2^{-})
. Since the cutoff here is slightly smaller than that for the molecular state predicted in Table
12
, the coupled channel effects play a positive role in the formation of this resonance.

, the

IV
Summary

With the advancement of experimental precision, studies of hadron spectroscopy has entered into a new era. Theoretical investigations into hadron interactions serve as a powerful tool for deepening our understanding of hadron spectroscopy, while also offering valuable insights into the exploration of the complex, non-perturbative dynamics in QCD. In this work, we systematically examine the
P

After solving the coupled channel Schrödinger equations and analyzing the resulting phase shifts, we predict the spectroscopic properties of charm-strange di-baryon structures with negative parity.

Abstract
. We study the two-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in a channel Ω = ( 0 , L ) × ( 0 , H ) \Omega=(0,L)\times(0,H) with small viscosity ε ≪ 1 \varepsilon\ll 1 , an ε \varepsilon -Navier slip condition on the horizontal walls, and a viscous inflow condition for the perturbation stream function. For a broad class of symmetric base profiles u 0 ​ ( y ) u_{0}(y) vanishing on the walls, we construct an exact steady solution ( u s , v s ) (u_{s},v_{s}) that is O ⁡ ( ε 1 / 3 ) O(\varepsilon^{1/3}) -close to the shear ( u 0 , 0 ) (u_{0},0) . We then develop a new weighted vorticity energy method to prove uniform linear stability and exponential decay: perturbations decay exponentially in a weighted L 2 L^{2} norm on the time scale O ( ε − 1 / 3 ) O(\varepsilon^{-1/3}) . In the short-channel regime L ≪ 1 L\ll 1 , the method yields nonlinear asymptotic stability with threshold O ⁡ ( ε 2 / 3 ) O(\varepsilon^{2/3}) . In the long-channel regime, assuming concavity together with a spectral condition, we introduce a quantity Rayleigh vorticity to control the non-favorable terms and obtain nonlinear stability with threshold O ⁡ ( ε 5 / 6 + ) O(\varepsilon^{5/6+}) .

Introduction and main results

1.1.
The problem setup

Let
\Omega:=(0,L)\times(0,H)
denote a two-dimensional channel. We consider the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}+uu_{x}+vu_{y}+p_{x}-\varepsilon\Delta u&=0,\\
v_{t}+uv_{x}+vv_{y}+p_{y}-\varepsilon\Delta v&=0,\\
u_{x}+v_{y}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.\quad\mbox{in}~\Omega\times(0,\infty),

with an
\varepsilon
-Navier boundary condition:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u(x,0)-A\varepsilon^{1/3}u_{y}(x,0)&=0,\\
u(x,H)+A\varepsilon^{1/3}u_{y}(x,H)&=0,\\
v(x,0)=v(x,H)&=0\end{aligned}\right.

for some
A>0
.

The objective of this paper is to develop a new energy method to investigate the stability of steady symmetric channel flows, possibly depending on
x
, for small viscosity
\varepsilon\ll 1
.

Let the base flow be
\{u_{0}(y),0\}
, whose assumptions are specified below. We construct a steady-state solution
\{u_{s}(x,y),v_{s}(x,y)\}
that satisfies the steady Navier-Stokes equation and the boundary condition (
1.2
), and that is close to base flow
\{u_{0}(y),0\}
. We then analyze the linear and nonlinear stability of
\{u_{s},v_{s}\}
by studying the linearized Navier-Stokes equation around
(u_{s},v_{s})
in vorticity form:

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\phi_{t}+u_{s}\Delta\phi_{x}-\Delta u_{s}\phi_{x}+v_{s}\Delta\phi_{y}-\Delta v_{s}\phi_{y}-\varepsilon\Delta^{2}\phi&=0,\quad\mbox{in}~\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\phi|_{t=0}&=\phi_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

and the nonlinear Navier-Stokes equation:

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\phi_{t}+u_{s}\Delta\phi_{x}-\Delta u_{s}\phi_{x}+v_{s}\Delta\phi_{y}-\Delta v_{s}\phi_{y}-\varepsilon\Delta^{2}\phi&=-\phi_{y}\Delta\phi_{x}+\phi_{x}\Delta\phi_{y},\quad\mbox{in}~\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\phi|_{t=0}&=\phi_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\phi
denotes the stream function of the perturbation. We assume that perturbation
\phi
satisfies the viscous inflow boundary condition:

(1.5)

\phi|_{x=0}=\phi_{x}|_{x=L}=\phi_{xx}|_{x=0}=\phi_{xxx}|_{x=L}=0.

The
\varepsilon
-Navier boundary condition (
1.2
) reads as

(1.6)

(\phi_{y}-A\varepsilon^{1/3}\Delta\phi)|_{y=0}=(\phi_{y}+A\varepsilon^{1/3}\Delta\phi)|_{y=H}=\phi|_{y=0}=\phi|_{y=H}=0.

1.3.
Background and motivations

Hydrodynamic stability in the high Reynolds number regime has attracted significant attention since the early work of Reynolds. Experimentally, steady laminar flows generally lose stability and transition to turbulence as the Reynolds number increases. A natural question is how small a perturbation must be, in terms of the Reynolds number, to prevent transition away from a given steady state. Trefethen et al.
[
28
]
first purposed this crucial question, which was later formulated by Bedrossian, Germain, and Masmoudi
[
1
,
2
]
as the following stability threshold problem: Given a norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
on the initial data, determine a
\gamma=\gamma(X)
such that
\|\phi_{0}\|_{X}\ll\varepsilon^{\gamma}
implies stability, whereas
\|\phi_{0}\|_{X}\gg\varepsilon^{\gamma}
implies instability. The exponent
\gamma
is referred as the stability threshold.

Since then, there has been a number of literature in studying the stability of various flows. For Couette flow and more general monotone shear flows, we refer to
[
7
,
13
,
3
,
4
,
27
,
14
,
18
,
9
,
6
,
5
]
and the references therein. For non-monotone shear flows, including Poiseuille and Kolmogorov flows, see
[
29
,
30
,
26
,
15
,
10
,
11
,
8
]
and the references therein. These works highlight three important stabilizing mechanisms. The first is inviscid damping, namely the decay of velocity or stream function perturbations driven by mixing in the underlying Rayleigh term. The second is enhanced dissipation, where mixing couples with viscosity to produce decay on time scales much faster than the diffusive scale. The third is vorticity depletion, which refers to the asymptotic vanishing of the vorticity near the critical points of a non-monotone shear flow.

For planar Poiseuille flow, enhanced dissipation on the time scale
O(\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\log\varepsilon|)
in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
was first proved by Coti Zelati, Elgindi, and Widmayer
[
16
]
based on the hypocoercivity framework. Del Zotto
[
17
]
later sharpened the time scale to
O(\varepsilon^{-1/2})
and also obtained an
L^{2}
-based stability threshold
\gamma\leq\frac{2}{3}+
. On the bounded channel
\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1]
, under the boundary conditions

\phi(t,x,\pm 1)=0,\quad\Delta\phi(t,x,\pm 1)=0,

Ding and Lin
[
19
]
proved the same enhanced dissipation rate and established a stability threshold
\gamma\leq\frac{2}{3}
for
H^{\frac{7}{2}+}
. More recently, Chen, Li, Shen, and Zhang
[
12
]
considered general concave symmetric shear flows in
\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1]
with no-slip boundary conditions, and again obtained the
O(\varepsilon^{-1/2})
enhanced dissipation time scale together with a stability threshold
\gamma\leq\frac{2}{3}
in
H^{4}
. A common feature of the settings above is that the domain is periodic in
x
and the base flow is a shear. This structure allows one to decompose the solution in Fourier modes in
x
and analyze the mode-by-mode dynamics. We also point out that on an unbounded channel
\mathbb{R}\times[-1,1]
, symmetric flows are linearly unstable
[
20
]
.

Another important physical phenomenon in the high Reynolds number regime is the formation of boundary layers. Because the no-slip boundary condition for the Navier–Stokes equations is incompatible with the boundary condition satisfied by the inviscid limit, Prandtl proposed that the Navier–Stokes solution admits an asymptotic expansion of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{NS}&=u_{e}+u_{p}+O(\sqrt{\varepsilon}),\\
v_{NS}&=v_{e}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}v_{p}+O(\sqrt{\varepsilon}),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\{u_{e},v_{e}\}
is the limiting Euler solution, and
\{u_{p},v_{p}\}
is the Prandtl boundary layer corrector. In general, this expansion fails for the unsteady Navier–Stokes equations in
L^{\infty}
topology; see
[
21
]
. In contrast, in the steady setting the expansion can be justified when
\{u_{e},v_{e}\}
is shear and
\{u_{p},v_{p}\}
is close to Blasius self-similar profile; see
[
23
,
24
]
. This yields an important class of steady solutions in channels, where the Prandtl corrector introduces an
x
-dependence that is not compatible with Fourier-mode analysis.

Motivated by the discussion above, we develop a new energy framework to study stability for symmetric flows in non-periodic channels. Rather than working with steady states that include a full Prandtl boundary layer, we construct a steady state with a weaker boundary layer, built from a symmetric shear profile that vanishes at the boundaries, and we prove its stability within our framework. We view this as a first step toward analyzing stability for more general non-periodic steady states, including the physically relevant examples discussed above. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first stability-thresholds result in a non-periodic channel.

1.5.
Overview of the proof

One of the major difficulties in studying non-monotone flow is to estimate the term
-u_{0yy}\phi_{x}
. We overcome this difficulty by two different arguments in the short- and long-channel settings.

When the channel is sufficiently short, we test the linearized Navier-Stokes equation (
1.3
) by
u_{0}\Delta\phi W
, where
W=2-x/L
. With this choice, the Rayleigh term generates a large positive contribution
\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|^{2}/L
, which can be arranged to dominate the unfavorable term
-u_{0yy}\phi_{x}
. The viscous dissipation yields an additional positive term
\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi\|^{2}
, but it also produces boundary contributions
\varepsilon\|\sqrt{|u_{0y}|}\Delta\phi\|^{2}_{y=0,H}
. These boundary terms are critical: they sit at the borderline where a direct Hardy inequality does not close. To bypass this obstacle, we use the
\varepsilon
-Navier boundary condition (
1.6
) to reduce the effective order of these boundary contributions.

When the channel is not short, the term
u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}
no longer dominates the unfavorable term
-u_{0yy}\phi_{x}
. In this case, we group the two terms together as
R_{x}
, where
R
is the “Rayleigh vorticity” defined in (
1.17
). The key observation is that the quantity
-R/u_{0yy}
couples well with the remaining terms in (
1.3
). In particular, we obtain a positivity estimate from its interaction with the temporal term; see Lemma
3.11
. Another important ingredient is the estimate of a residual term
(\phi_{t},\phi_{x})
, where the spectral condition (
1.16
) is involved; see Proposition
3.14
.

The rest of the article is organized as follows. We postpone the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
to Section
4
, since the steady-state construction can be carried out uniformly. Assuming the existence of these steady states, we prove stability results in the short channel (Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
) in Section
2
, and in the long channel (Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
) in Section
3
. Finally, we provide a proof of (
1.15
) for the Poiseuille flow
\mu(y)=y(1-y)
with

in the Appendix
A
.

2.
Stability in the short channel

In this section, we prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. As mentioned before, we postpone the construction of steady states to Section
4
. Here, we recall (
1.8
) the key estimates of the steady state.

2.1.
Preliminary estimates

In this subsection, we establish some preliminary estimates. First, we have a weighted Hardy inequality.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
\sigma>0
, we have

(2.1)

\int u_{0}^{i}f^{2}\lesssim(\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3})^{i+1}\int u_{0}f_{y}^{2}+(\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3})^{i-2}\int u_{0}^{2}f^{2},\quad i=0,1.

Proof.

Let
\chi(y)
be a smooth cut-off function supported in
[0,1]
, and
\chi\equiv 1
in
[0,1/2]
. We write

\int u_{0}^{i}f^{2}\lesssim\int u_{0}^{i}f^{2}\chi^{2}\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)+\int u_{0}^{i}f^{2}\chi^{2}\Big(\frac{1-y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)+\int u_{0}^{i}f^{2}[1-\chi\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)-\chi\Big(\frac{1-y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)]^{2}.

For the first term, we have

\displaystyle\int u_{0}^{i}f^{2}\chi^{2}\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)\lesssim

\displaystyle(\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3})^{i}\int f^{2}\chi^{2}\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3})^{i}\int\partial_{y}(y)f^{2}\chi^{2}\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2(\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3})^{i}\int yff_{y}\chi^{2}-2(\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3})^{i}\int\frac{y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}f^{2}\chi^{\prime}\chi

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3})^{i}\int y^{2}f_{y}^{2}\chi^{2}+(\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3})^{i}\int\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)^{2}f^{2}|\chi^{\prime}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3})^{i+1}\int u_{0}f_{y}^{2}+(\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3})^{i-2}\int u_{0}^{2}f^{2},

where we used
y\lesssim u_{0}\lesssim y
and
y\lesssim\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}
in the support of
\chi
. The second term can be estimated similarly.

For the last term, using
u_{0}\gtrsim\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}
in the support of
1-\chi\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)-\chi\Big(\frac{1-y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)
, we have

\int u_{0}^{i}f^{2}[1-\chi\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)-\chi\Big(\frac{1-y}{\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3}}\Big)]^{2}\lesssim(\sigma\varepsilon^{1/3})^{i-2}\int u_{0}^{2}f^{2}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Very often, we use (
2.1
) with
f=\Delta\phi
or
f=\Delta\phi_{x}
.

Corollary 2.2
.

For
\sigma>0
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\Delta\phi\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\sigma}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}+\sigma^{-1}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2},

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\sigma}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{xy}\|_{2}+\sigma^{-1}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2},

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sigma\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}+\sigma^{-1/2}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2},

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sigma\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{xy}\|_{2}+\sigma^{-1/2}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
f=0
on
x=0
or
x=L
,
0<\sigma<1/2
, we have

(2.3)

\int f^{2}\lesssim\int u_{0}^{2}f_{y}^{2}+\frac{L^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\int u_{0}^{2}f_{x}^{2}.

Proof.

Similar as the above, we let
\chi(y)
be a smooth cut-off function supported in
[0,1]
, and
\chi\equiv 1
in
[0,1/2]
. We write

\int f^{2}\lesssim\int f^{2}\chi^{2}\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma}\Big)+\int f^{2}\chi^{2}\Big(\frac{1-y}{\sigma}\Big)+\int f^{2}[1-\chi\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma}\Big)-\chi\Big(\frac{1-y}{\sigma}\Big)]^{2}.

For the first term, we proceed as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
and obtain

\displaystyle\int f^{2}\chi^{2}\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma}\Big)\lesssim

\displaystyle\int y^{2}f_{y}^{2}\chi^{2}+\int\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma}\Big)^{2}f^{2}|\chi^{\prime}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int u_{0}^{2}f_{y}^{2}+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\int u_{0}^{2}f^{2}.

Similarly, the second term can be estimated in the same way, and the last term can be controlled by

\int f^{2}[1-\chi\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma}\Big)-\chi\Big(\frac{1-y}{\sigma}\Big)]^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\int u_{0}^{2}f^{2}.

Finally, by Poincare inequality in
x
, we have

\int u_{0}^{2}f^{2}\lesssim L^{2}\int u_{0}^{2}f_{x}^{2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For
L\ll 1
, we have

(2.4)

\|\phi_{x}\|_{2}+\|\phi_{y}\|_{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2},

and

(2.5)

\|\phi_{xx}\|_{2}+\|\phi_{xy}\|_{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}.

Proof.

Recall (
1.18
), the definition of
q
, we have

\displaystyle(q,u_{0}\Delta\phi)=

\displaystyle(q,u_{0}(u_{0}q_{yy}+2u_{0y}q_{y}+u_{0yy}q+u_{0}q_{xx}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(u_{0}^{2}q_{y},q_{y})-2(u_{0}u_{0y}q,q_{y})+2(u_{0}u_{0y}q,q_{y})+(q,u_{0}u_{0yy}q)-(u_{0}^{2}q_{x},q_{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(u_{0}^{2}\nabla q,\nabla q)+(q,u_{0}u_{0yy}q).

Using Poincare inequality in
x
, and Lemma
2.3
with
f=q
, we can estimate

\displaystyle|(q,u_{0}u_{0yy}q)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\|q\|_{2}\|u_{0}q\|_{2}\lesssim L\|q\|_{2}\|u_{0}q_{x}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle L(\|u_{0}q_{y}\|_{2}+\frac{L}{\sigma}\|u_{0}q_{x}\|_{2})\|u_{0}q_{x}\|_{2}\leq\delta\|u_{0}\nabla q\|_{2}^{2}

for some small
\delta>0
. Therefore by Lemma
2.3
with
f=q
again,

\displaystyle(1-\delta)\|u_{0}\nabla q\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim|(q,u_{0}\Delta\phi)|\lesssim\|q\|_{2}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\nabla q\|_{2}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2},

which implies

\|u_{0}\nabla q\|_{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.3
with
f=q
again, we have

\displaystyle\|\phi_{x}\|_{2}+\|\phi_{y}\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\|u_{0}q_{x}\|_{2}+\|u_{0}q_{y}+u_{0y}q\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{0}q_{x}\|_{2}+\|u_{0}q_{y}\|_{2}+\|q\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\nabla q\|_{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}.

This proves the estimate (
2.4
). Replacing
\phi
by
\phi_{x}
and
q
by
q_{x}
, and repeating the argument above yields the estimate (
2.5
).
∎

Finally, we collect some mixed norm estimates.

Lemma 2.5
.

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{y}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}}(\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}),

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi_{x}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{y}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}}(\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{xy}\|_{2}),

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}(\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi\|_{2}),

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}(\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2})

Proof.

We only prove the first and the third inequalities, as the second and the fourth ones follow similarly.

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{y}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\sup_{y}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{L}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dy+\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla\phi||\nabla\phi_{y}|\,dy\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{2}\|\Delta\phi\|_{2},

where we used in second line the Sobolev inequality in
y
. Then the first inequality follows from (
2.2
) and (
2.4
). For the third inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}u_{0}|\Delta\phi|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{1}u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\Delta\phi\,dydx

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}(\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2})

by (
2.2
). Therefore, the third inequality follows.
∎

2.2.
Linear stability estimates

In the subsection, we derive estimates for linear stability for
\varepsilon\ll L\ll 1
. We define the weight

W(x):=2-x/L

so that

W^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{L}

gives a negative large constant. We multiply the linearized Navier-Stokes equation (
1.3
) by
Wu_{0}\Delta\phi
, and estimate each term.

2.2.1.
Rayleigh terms

We estimate the four Rayleigh terms separately. First, we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle(u_{s}\Delta\phi_{x},Wu_{0}\Delta\phi)=

\displaystyle(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x},Wu_{0}\Delta\phi)+((u_{s}-u_{0})\Delta\phi_{x},Wu_{0}\Delta\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(u_{0}\Delta\phi,Wu_{0}\Delta\phi)_{x=L}-\frac{1}{2}(W^{\prime}u_{0}\Delta\phi,u_{0}\Delta\phi)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}((u_{s}-u_{0})\Delta\phi,Wu_{0}\Delta\phi)_{x=L}-\frac{1}{2}((u_{s}-u_{0})W^{\prime}\Delta\phi,u_{0}\Delta\phi)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(u_{sx}W\Delta\phi,u_{0}\Delta\phi).

The term

(2.8)

(\ref{Ray_1}.2)=\frac{1}{2L}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}

is the crucial large positive term. The terms (
2.7
.1), (
2.7
.3) and (
2.7
.4) are positive since
u_{s}-u_{0}>0
. Using the fact that
|u_{sx}|\lesssim\varepsilon^{1-}
from (
1.8
) and (
2.2
), we can estimate the last term

(2.9)

\displaystyle|(\ref{Ray_1}.5)|\lesssim_{A}\varepsilon^{1-}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim_{A}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}-}(\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}^{2}+\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}).

Second, we write

(2.10)

(-\Delta u_{s}\phi_{x},u_{0}W\Delta\phi)=(-\Delta u_{a}\phi_{x},u_{0}W\Delta\phi)-((\Delta u_{s}-\Delta u_{a})\phi_{x},u_{0}W\Delta\phi).

Using (
1.8
) and (
2.4
), we have

(2.11)

|(\ref{Ray_2}.1)|\lesssim_{A}\|\phi_{x}\|_{2}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}\lesssim_{A}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2},

which can be absorbed by (
2.8
). By (
1.8
), (
2.2
) and (
2.6
), we can estimate

(2.12)

\displaystyle|(\ref{Ray_2}.2)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sqrt{u_{0}}(\Delta u_{s}-\Delta u_{a})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}\|\phi_{x}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{y}}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{A}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-}(\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}^{2}).

Next, using
|v_{s}|\lesssim\varepsilon
from (
1.8
) and (
2.2
), we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle|(v_{s}\Delta\phi_{y},u_{0}W\Delta\phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{A}\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{A}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}(\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}^{2}).

For the last term, we split
\Delta v_{s}=\Delta v_{a}+(\Delta v_{s}-\Delta v_{a})
and use (
1.8
), (
2.4
), and (
2.6
) to estimate

(2.14)

\displaystyle|(\Delta v_{s}\phi_{y},u_{0}W\Delta\phi)|\lesssim

\displaystyle|(\Delta v_{a}\phi_{y},u_{0}W\Delta\phi)|+|((\Delta v_{s}-\Delta v_{a})\phi_{y},u_{0}W\Delta\phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{A}

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\phi_{y}\|_{2}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}+\|\sqrt{u_{0}}(\Delta v_{s}-\Delta v_{a})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}\|\phi_{y}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{y}}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{A}

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}-}(\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}^{2}).

2.2.2.
Dissipation term

(2.15)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon(\Delta^{2}\phi,Wu_{0}\Delta\phi)=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(Wu_{0}\Delta\phi_{x},\Delta\phi_{x})+\varepsilon(Wu_{0}\Delta\phi_{y},\Delta\phi_{y})

\displaystyle+\varepsilon(W^{\prime}u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x},\Delta\phi)+\varepsilon(Wu_{0y}\Delta\phi_{y},\Delta\phi).

The terms (
2.15
.1) and (
2.15
.2) are favorable positive terms. Using (
2.2
), we can estimate

(2.16)

\displaystyle|(\ref{Dis_1}.3)|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{100}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{O(\varepsilon)}{L^{2}}\|\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{100}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{100}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{O(\varepsilon^{1/3})}{L^{2}}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}.

The last term (
2.15
.4) is a crucial term, we integrate by parts in
y
and use the
\varepsilon
-Navier boundary condition (
1.6
):

(2.17)

\displaystyle(\ref{Dis_1}.4)=

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(Wu_{0y}\Delta\phi,\Delta\phi)_{y=H}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(Wu_{0y}\Delta\phi,\Delta\phi)_{y=0}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(Wu_{0yy}\Delta\phi,\Delta\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{1/3}}{2A^{2}}(Wu_{0y}\phi_{y},\phi_{y})_{y=H}-\frac{\varepsilon^{1/3}}{2A^{2}}(Wu_{0y}\phi_{y},\phi_{y})_{y=0}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(Wu_{0yy}\Delta\phi,\Delta\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{1/3}}{A^{2}}(Wu_{0y}\phi_{y},\phi_{yy})+\frac{\varepsilon^{1/3}}{2A^{2}}(Wu_{0yy}\phi_{y},\phi_{y})-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(Wu_{0yy}\Delta\phi,\Delta\phi).

Therefore, by (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle|(\ref{Dis_1-2}.1)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A^{4}}\|\phi_{y}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2/3}\|\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{50}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{O(1)}{A^{4}}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}.

And the last two terms in (
2.17
) can be estimated similarly:

(2.19)

\displaystyle|(\ref{Dis_1-2}.2)|+|(\ref{Dis_1-2}.3)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{1/3}}{A^{2}}\|\phi_{y}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{A}

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{4/3}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon^{1/3}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}.

2.2.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The temporal term can be straightforwardly written as

(\Delta\phi_{t},Wu_{0}\Delta\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\sqrt{W}\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}.

Now we collect all the terms from (
2.7
) - (
2.19
). By choosing
L>0
small enough, we can see that the favorable positive terms (
2.8
), (
2.15
.1), and (
2.15
.2) dominate all the non-favorable terms from (
2.7
) - (
2.19
) when
\varepsilon\ll L\ll 1
. Therefore, we end up with

(2.20)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\sqrt{W}\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{L}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}\leq 0.

This concludes the proof of (
1.9
). Furthermore, by (
2.2
), we know that

\varepsilon^{1/3}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}.

Therefore, (
2.20
) and (
2.4
) imply the exponential decay estimate (
1.10
). This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

2.3.
Nonlinear stability estimates

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
1.3
. We define the space
\mathcal{X}
to be the closure of smooth function satisfying the boundary conditions (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) under the norm

\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}:=\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\|\sqrt{u}_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt.

We define the solution map
\mathcal{S}:\psi\mapsto\phi
, where
\phi
is the solution to

(2.21)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\phi_{t}+u_{s}\Delta\phi_{x}-\Delta u_{s}\phi_{x}+v_{s}\Delta\phi_{y}-\Delta v_{s}\phi_{y}-\varepsilon\Delta^{2}\phi&=-\psi_{y}\Delta\psi_{x}+\psi_{x}\Delta\psi_{y},\quad\mbox{in}~\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\phi|_{t=0}&=\phi_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

and satisfies the boundary conditions (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). Let

\mathcal{B}:=\{\phi\in\mathcal{X}:\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq 2c_{0}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\},

where
c_{0}
is the small constant in Theorem
1.3
. Our goal is to show that
\mathcal{S}
is a contraction mapping in
\mathcal{B}
when
\varepsilon\ll L\ll 1
.

We multiply the equation (
2.21
) by
-(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)_{x}
and estimate term by term. The linear part is similar to what have been done in the previous section.

2.3.1.
Temporal term

(2.22)

-(\Delta\phi_{t},(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)_{x})=(\Delta\phi_{xt},u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{x}\sqrt{W}\|_{2}^{2}.

2.3.2.
Rayleigh terms

Again, we estimate the four Rayleigh terms separately. First, we have

(2.23)

\displaystyle-(u_{s}\Delta\phi_{x},(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)_{x})=

\displaystyle-(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x},(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)_{x})-((u_{s}-u_{0})\Delta\phi_{x},(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)_{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x},u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x},u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)_{x=0}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}((u_{s}-u_{0})\Delta\phi_{x},u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}((u_{s}-u_{0})\Delta\phi_{x},Wu_{0}\Delta\phi_{x})_{x=0}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(u_{sx}W\Delta\phi_{x},u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}).

The term

(2.24)

(\ref{N_Ray_1}.1)=\frac{1}{2L}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}

is the crucial large positive term. The terms (
2.23
.2), (
2.23
.3) and (
2.23
.4) are positive since
u_{s}-u_{0}>0
. As (
2.9
), the last term can be estimated by

(2.25)

\displaystyle|(\ref{N_Ray_1}.5)|\lesssim_{A}\varepsilon^{1-}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim_{A}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}-}(\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{xy}\|_{2}^{2}+\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}).

Second, we write

(2.26)

\displaystyle(\Delta u_{s}\phi_{x},(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)_{x})=

\displaystyle(\Delta u_{a}\phi_{x},(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)_{x})+((\Delta u_{s}-\Delta u_{a})\phi_{x},(u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)_{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\Delta u_{ax}\phi_{x},u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)-(\Delta u_{a}\phi_{xx},u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)

\displaystyle-(\Delta u_{a}\phi_{x},u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W)_{x=0}+((\Delta u_{s}-\Delta u_{a})\phi_{x},u_{0}\Delta\phi_{xx}W)

\displaystyle+((\Delta u_{s}-\Delta u_{a})\phi_{x},u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}W^{\prime}).

By (
1.8
), Poincare inequality in
x
, and (
2.5
), we can estimate

(2.27)

\displaystyle|(\ref{N_Ray_2}.1)|+|(\ref{N_Ray_2}.2)|\lesssim_{A}\|\phi_{xx}\|_{2}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}\lesssim_{A}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2},

and

(2.28)

\displaystyle|(\ref{N_Ray_2}.3)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{100}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{x=0}^{2}+O(1)\|\phi_{x}\|_{x=0}^{2}

Proof of Theorem
1.3

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.3
. We collect all the terms from (

). By choosing

small enough, we can see that the favorable positive terms (

), (
2.34
.1), and (

.3) dominate all the other terms from the linear part when

. Therefore, we end up with

(2.44)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\sqrt{W}\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{L}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}(\|u_{0}\Delta\psi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\psi_{x}\|_{2}^{2})\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}.

This implies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\|\sqrt{u}_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\,dt+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\|\sqrt{u}_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\|u_{0}\Delta\psi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\psi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\Big)\,dt+\|\sqrt{u}_{0}\Delta\phi_{0x}\|_{2}^{2}.

Since
\psi\in\mathcal{B}
, and by the initial data (
1.11
), we can conclude that
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
when
c_{0}
is small enough. Hence the solution map
\mathcal{S}
maps
\mathcal{B}
into
\mathcal{B}
.

To show
\mathcal{S}
is a contraction mapping, we take
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
, and denote
\phi_{1}=\mathcal{S}(\psi_{1}),\phi_{2}=\mathcal{S}(\psi_{2})
. We further denote
\widetilde{\phi}=\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}
and
\widetilde{\psi}=\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}
. Repeating the estimates in this section for the equation for
\widetilde{\phi}
, we will end up with

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\|\sqrt{u}_{0}\Delta\widetilde{\phi}_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u_{0}\Delta\widetilde{\phi}_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\,dt+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\widetilde{\phi}_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\|\sqrt{u}_{0}\Delta\widetilde{\phi}_{x}\|_{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\|u_{0}\Delta\widetilde{\psi}_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\widetilde{\psi}_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\Big)\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\Big(\|u_{0}\Delta\psi_{jx}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\psi_{jx}\|_{2}^{2}\Big)\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{0}\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\|\sqrt{u}_{0}\Delta\widetilde{\phi}_{x}\|_{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\|u_{0}\Delta\widetilde{\psi}_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\widetilde{\psi}_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\Big)\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This implies

\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\lesssim c_{0}\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{\mathcal{X}},

and hence

is a contraction mapping when

is small. By the contraction mapping theorem, there exists a unique solution
\phi
satisfying the estimate (
1.12
). Finally, the exponential decay estimate (
1.13
) follows from (
2.44
), (
2.5

\varepsilon^{1/3}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi_{x}\|_{2}^{2}

from (

1.3
.

Lemma 3.1

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma

Lemma 3.2
.

For
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
f=0
on
y=H/2
, we have

(3.2)

\int f^{2}\lesssim H^{2}\int u_{0}^{2}f_{y}^{2}.

Proof.

Similar as the above, we let
\chi(y)
be a smooth cut-off function supported in
[0,1]
, and
\chi\equiv 1
in
[0,1/2]
. For some small
\sigma>0
, we write

\int f^{2}\lesssim\int f^{2}\chi^{2}\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma H}\Big)+\int f^{2}\chi^{2}\Big(\frac{H-y}{\sigma H}\Big)+\int f^{2}[1-\chi\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma H}\Big)-\chi\Big(\frac{H-y}{\sigma H}\Big)]^{2}.

For the first term, we proceed as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
and obtain

\displaystyle\int f^{2}\chi^{2}\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma H}\Big)\lesssim

\displaystyle\int y^{2}f_{y}^{2}\chi^{2}+\int\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma H}\Big)^{2}f^{2}|\chi^{\prime}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle H^{2}\int u_{0}^{2}f_{y}^{2}+\int f^{2}|\chi^{\prime}|^{2}.

The second term can be handled similarly. For
y\in(\frac{\sigma}{2}H,(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})H)
, we write

f(x,y)=\int_{y}^{\frac{H}{2}}f_{y}(x,s)\,ds.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int f^{2}|\chi^{\prime}\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma H}\Big)|^{2}+\int f^{2}|\chi^{\prime}\Big(\frac{H-y}{\sigma H}\Big)|^{2}+\int f^{2}[1-\chi\Big(\frac{y}{\sigma H}\Big)-\chi\Big(\frac{H-y}{\sigma H}\Big)]^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{\sigma}{2}H}^{(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})H}\left|\int_{y}^{\frac{H}{2}}f_{y}(x,s)\,ds\right|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{\sigma}{2}H}^{(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})H}H\int_{\frac{\sigma}{2}H}^{(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})H}|f_{y}(x,s)|^{2}\,ds\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle H^{2}\int_{\frac{\sigma}{2}H}^{(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})H}u_{0}^{2}(s)|f_{y}(x,s)|^{2}\,ds,

where we used the fact
u_{0}(s)\gtrsim\sigma
when
s\in(\frac{\sigma}{2}H,(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})H)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

We have the following estimates

(3.3)

\|\phi_{x}\|_{2}+\|\phi_{y}\|_{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\nabla q\|_{2}\lesssim H\|R\|_{2},

(3.4)

\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}\lesssim\|R\|_{2},

Proof.

Recall (
1.18

q

\displaystyle(q,R)=

\displaystyle(q,u_{0}(u_{0}q_{yy}+2u_{0y}q_{y}+u_{0yy}q+u_{s}q_{xx}))-(q,u_{0yy}\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(u_{0}^{2}q_{y},q_{y})-2(u_{0}u_{0y}q,q_{y})+2(u_{0}u_{0y}q,q_{y})+(q,u_{0}u_{0yy}q)-(u_{0}^{2}q_{x},q_{x})-(q,u_{0yy}u_{0}q)

\displaystyle-(u_{0}^{2}\nabla q,\nabla q).

Therefore, by (

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\nabla q\|_{2}^{2}=|(q,R)|\leq\|q\|_{2}\|R\|_{2}\lesssim H\|u_{0}\nabla q\|_{2}\|R\|_{2},

Therefore, by (
3.2

\displaystyle\|\phi_{x}\|_{2}+\|\phi_{y}\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{0}q_{x}\|_{2}+\|u_{0}q_{y}\|_{2}+\frac{1}{H}\|q\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\nabla q\|_{2}\lesssim H\|R\|_{2}.

This concludes the proof of (

Lemma 3.6
.

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{u_{0}}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{y}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{H}\|\phi_{y}\|_{2}\lesssim_{H}\|\frac{R}{\sqrt{-u_{0yy}}}\|_{2},

\displaystyle\|\frac{\phi_{x}}{\sqrt{u_{0}}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{y}}

Then we use the estimate (

Lemma 3.7

\displaystyle|\Delta\phi(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\Delta\phi(x,y_{2})|+\Big|\int_{y}^{y_{2}}|\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}(x,s)|\,ds\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\Delta\phi(x,y_{2})|+|\log y|^{1/2}\Big(\int_{0}^{H}u_{0}|\Delta\phi_{y}|\,ds\Big)^{1/2}.

Since
|\log y|\in L^{q}(0,H)
for any
q<\infty
, we have

\Bigg(\int_{0}^{L}\Big(\int_{0}^{H/10}|\Delta\phi(x,y)|^{q}\,dy\Big)^{2/q}\Bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim_{q}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{L^{2}}.

The same argument applies when
9H/10<y<H
, which concludes the proof of the first estimate. For the second one, we separate the cases when
\varepsilon^{0+}<y<H-\varepsilon^{0^{+}}
, when
0<y<\varepsilon^{0+}
, and when
H-\varepsilon^{0^{+}}<y<H
.

When
\varepsilon^{0+}<y<H-\varepsilon^{0^{+}}
, we have

|\Delta\phi(x,y)|\lesssim_{H}|\Delta\phi(x,y_{1})|+\varepsilon^{0-}\Big|\int_{y}^{y_{1}}|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi_{y}(x,s)|\,ds\Big|

for some
y_{1}\in(\varepsilon^{0+},H-\varepsilon^{0+})
. For the other two cases, we have

|\Delta\phi(x,y)|\lesssim_{H}|\Delta\phi(x,y_{2})|+|\log\varepsilon|^{1/2}\Big(\int_{0}^{H}u_{0}|\Delta\phi_{y}|\,ds\Big)^{1/2}

for some
y_{2}\in(0,\varepsilon^{0+})
or
(H-\varepsilon^{0+},H)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

We can control the
L^{\infty}
norm of
\nabla\phi
by some mixed norm of
\Delta\phi
as follows.

Lemma 3.8
.

(3.9)

\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{p,H}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{p}_{y}L^{2}_{x}}\quad\mbox{for any}~p>2.

Proof.

For the simplicity of the presentation, we prove this Lemma with
H=1
. Due to the viscous inflow boundary condition (
1.5
), we take the Fourier basis in
x
to be

\varphi_{m}(x)=\sin(\lambda_{m}x),\quad\lambda_{m}=\frac{(2m+1)\pi}{2L},\quad m=0,1,2,\dots.

We write

\displaystyle\phi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}a_{m}(y)\varphi_{m}(x),

\displaystyle\Delta\phi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}b_{m}(y)\varphi_{m}(x).

Then we have

(3.10)

a_{m}^{\prime\prime}(y)-\lambda_{m}^{2}a_{m}(y)=b_{m}(y),\qquad y\in(0,1),

with Dirichlet boundary condition
a_{m}(0)=a_{m}(1)=0
. It is classical that one can solve (
3.10
) through the Green’s function

G_{m}(y,s)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{m}\sinh\lambda_{m}}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sinh(\lambda_{m}y)\sinh(\lambda_{m}(1-s)),\quad&&0\leq y\leq s\leq 1,\\
&\sinh(\lambda_{m}s)\sinh(\lambda_{m}(1-y)),\quad&&0\leq s<y\leq 1.\end{aligned}\right.

Then we can write

a_{m}(y)=\int_{0}^{1}G(y,s)b_{m}(s)\,ds.

It is straightforward to show that

(3.11)

|G_{m}(y,s)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{m}}e^{-\lambda_{m}|s-y|}.

We only prove (
3.11
) when
y\leq s
, as the other case follows similarly. When
\lambda_{m}>1
, we have
\sinh\lambda_{m}\gtrsim e^{\lambda_{m}}
, and hence

|G_{m}(y,s)|\lesssim\frac{e^{\lambda_{m}y}e^{\lambda_{m}(1-s)}}{\lambda_{m}e^{\lambda_{m}}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{m}}e^{\lambda_{m}(y-s)}.

When
0\leq\lambda_{m}\leq 1
, we have
e^{\lambda_{m}(y-s)}\geq e^{-1}\gtrsim 1
and
\sinh\lambda_{m}\leq\sinh 1\lesssim 1
. Therefore,

|G_{m}(y,s)|\lesssim\frac{(\sinh\lambda_{m})^{2}}{\lambda_{m}\sinh\lambda_{m}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{m}}e^{\lambda_{m}(y-s)}.

Similarly, one can prove that

(3.12)

|\partial_{y}G_{m}(y,s)|\lesssim e^{-\lambda_{m}|s-y|}.

By (
3.12
), Fubini Theorem, and Hölder’s inequality, we can estimate

\displaystyle|\phi_{y}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{m}a_{m}^{\prime}(y)\varphi(x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\lambda_{m}|s-y|}|b_{m}(s)|\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{m}e^{-\lambda_{m}|s-y|}|b_{m}(s)|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\sum_{m}e^{-2\lambda_{m}|s-y|}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{m}|b_{m}(s)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\sum_{m}e^{-2\lambda_{m}|s-y|}\Big)^{p^{\prime}/2}\,ds}_{\operatorname{I}}\Bigg)^{1/p^{\prime}}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{p}_{y}L^{2}_{x}},

where
1/p^{\prime}=1-1/p
. It remains to show the integral
\operatorname{I}
converges, and it suffices to show that
(\sum_{m}e^{-2\lambda_{m}|s-y|})^{p^{\prime}/2}
is integrable near
s=y
. For
s\neq y
, we can directly compute that

\displaystyle\sum_{m}e^{-2\lambda_{m}|s-y|}=\frac{e^{-\pi|s-y|/L}}{1-e^{-2\pi|s-y|/L}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|s-y|},\qquad\mbox{when}~s\sim y.

Since
p>2
,
p^{\prime}/2<1
. Therefore, the integral
\operatorname{I}
converges. We can conclude that

\|\phi_{y}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{p}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{p}_{y}L^{2}_{x}}.

Similarly, by (
3.11
), we can estimate

\displaystyle|\phi_{x}(x,y)|=\left|\sum_{m}a_{m}(y)\varphi^{\prime}(x)\right|\lesssim\sum_{m}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\lambda_{m}|s-y|}|b_{m}(s)|\,ds.

Following the same argument gives

\|\phi_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{p}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{p}_{y}L^{2}_{x}}.

This concludes the proof of this lemma.
∎

Combining the two lemmas above, we have the following
L^{\infty}
estimate for
\nabla\phi
.

Corollary 3.9
.

(3.13)

\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{H}\varepsilon^{-(\frac{1}{3}+)}\Big(\|\frac{R}{\sqrt{-u_{0yy}}}\|_{2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}\Big).

Proof.

By (
3.9
), Minkowski’s inequality, and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{p}_{y}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{p}_{y}}\lesssim\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}^{\theta}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{q}_{y}}^{1-\theta},

where

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{1-\theta}{q}.

Therefore, for any
\delta>0
, we can choose
p>2
and close to
2
, and
q
sufficiently large, such that

\theta=1-6\delta
. Therefore, by (
3.5
) and (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\delta}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}^{1-6\delta}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{q}_{y}}^{6\delta}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\delta}\varepsilon^{-1/3-\delta}\Big(\|\frac{R}{\sqrt{-u_{0yy}}}\|_{2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\Delta\phi_{y}\|_{2}\Big).

This concludes the proof.
∎

For the temporal term, recalling that

\displaystyle(\Delta\phi_{t},\frac{R}{-u_{0yy}}W)

Lemma 3.11
.

\displaystyle(\Delta{\phi},\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}\Delta\phi W)-(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi W)+(\phi,\phi)_{x=L}

First, we claim that

Indeed, w e rewrite

\displaystyle\int|\phi_{y}|^{2}W+\int\frac{u_{0yy}}{u_{0}}\phi^{2}W

\displaystyle=\int\Big[u_{0}^{2}q_{y}^{2}+u_{0y}^{2}q^{2}+2u_{0}u_{0y}qq_{y}+u_{0}u_{0yy}q^{2}\Big]W

\displaystyle(\ref{Tem_term}.2)

\displaystyle=-(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi W)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}|\Delta\phi|^{2}W-\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{-u_{0yy}}{u_{0}}\phi^{2}W-\frac{1}{2}(\phi,\phi)|_{x=L}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}|\Delta\phi|^{2}W+\frac{1}{2}\int u_{0}^{2}q_{y}^{2}W-\frac{1}{2}\int|\phi_{y}|^{2}W-\frac{1}{2}(\phi,\phi)|_{x=L},

\displaystyle(\ref{Tem_term}.2)

\displaystyle=-(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi W)

\displaystyle\geq-\int\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}|\Delta\phi|^{2}W+\int u_{0}^{2}q_{y}^{2}W+\int|\phi_{x}|^{2}W-(\phi,\phi)|_{x=L}.

Plugging (

Combining (

\displaystyle\eqref{Tem_term}\geq

3.2.4.
Estimate of
\phi_{t}
by duality

Let
g
be the auxiliary function satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta g=\phi_{t},\quad\mbox{in}~(0,L)\times(0,H),\\
g|_{x=0}=g_{x}|_{x=L}=g|_{y=0,H}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

By the symmetry of
\phi_{t}
, we know that
g(x,y)=-g(x,H-y)
, hence
g(x,H/2)=0
. It is straightforward to see that
g
satisfies the estimates

(3.51)

\|g\|_{2}+\|\nabla g\|_{2}+\|\nabla^{2}g\|_{2}\lesssim_{H,L}\|\phi_{t}\|_{2}.

In the following, we estimate

with an explicit constant by separation of variables.

Lemma 3.13
.

(3.52)

\|g_{x}\|_{2}\leq\frac{H}{4\pi}\|\phi_{t}\|_{2}.

Proof.

By the boundary conditions and the symmetry above, we can solve
g
in
(0,L)\times(0,H/2)
using separation of variables. The orthonormal basis is

\varphi_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac{8}{HL}}\sin\Big[\Big(n+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\frac{\pi}{L}x\Big]\sin\Big(\frac{2m\pi}{H}y\Big),\quad n\geq 0,m\geq 1.

Define

\phi_{nm}:=\int_{0}^{H/2}\int_{0}^{L}\phi_{t}\varphi_{nm}\,dxdy,

and

g_{nm}:=\frac{\phi_{nm}}{\Big(n+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{\pi}{L}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{2m\pi}{H}\Big)^{2}}.

Then

g=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq 1}g_{nm}\varphi_{nm},

and

g_{x}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq 1}g_{nm}\Big(n+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\frac{\pi}{L}\sqrt{\frac{8}{HL}}\cos\Big[\Big(n+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\frac{\pi}{L}x\Big]\sin\Big(\frac{2m\pi}{H}y\Big).

Therefore,

\|g_{x}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\Big(n+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{\pi}{L}\Big)^{2}}{\Big[\Big(n+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{\pi}{L}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{2m\pi}{H}\Big)^{2}\Big]^{2}}\phi_{nm}^{2}.

Now we consider the function

f(s)=\frac{s}{(s+a)^{2}},\quad a,s>0.

It is straightforward to see that
f(s)
achieves maximum at
s=a
. Therefore,

\frac{\Big(n+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{\pi}{L}\Big)^{2}}{\Big[\Big(n+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{\pi}{L}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{2m\pi}{H}\Big)^{2}\Big]^{2}}\leq\frac{\Big(\frac{2m\pi}{H}\Big)^{2}}{4\Big(\frac{2m\pi}{H}\Big)^{4}}=\Big(\frac{H}{4m\pi}\Big)^{2}.

Then we can estimate

\|g_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq 1}\Big(\frac{H}{4m\pi}\Big)^{2}\phi_{nm}^{2}\leq\Big(\frac{H}{4\pi}\Big)^{2}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq 1}\phi_{nm}^{2}=\Big(\frac{H}{4\pi}\Big)^{2}\|\phi_{t}\|_{2}^{2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

\beta>0
, and let

\mathcal{B}:=\{\phi\in\mathcal{X}:\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq 2c_{0}\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{6}+\beta}\},

where
c_{0}
is the small constant in Theorem
1.6
. Our goal is to show that
\mathcal{S}
is a contraction mapping in
\mathcal{B}
when
\varepsilon\ll 1
.

We test (
3.67
) by
\frac{RW}{-u_{0yy}}
, where
W
is given in (
3.15
), and estimate each term. The linear estimates have been done in the previous section, so it remains to estimate the nonlinear terms

(3.68)

-(\psi_{y}\Delta\psi_{x},\frac{RW}{-u_{0yy}})+(\psi_{x}\Delta\psi_{y},\frac{RW}{-u_{0yy}}).

For the first term, we can estimate using (
3.13
), Hardy inequality in
y
, and (
3.50
),

(3.69)

\displaystyle|(\ref{Non_H}.1)|

\displaystyle\leq|(\psi_{y}\Delta\psi_{x},\frac{u_{0}\Delta\phi}{-u_{0yy}}W)|+|(\psi_{y}\Delta\psi_{x},\phi W)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{H,L}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\Delta\psi_{x}\|_{2}\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}+\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\Delta\psi_{x}\|_{2}\|\phi_{y}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{H,L}\varepsilon^{-(\frac{5}{6}+)}\Big(\|\frac{R_{\psi}}{\sqrt{-u_{0yy}}}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\nabla\Delta\psi\|_{2}^{2}\Big)\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\Delta\phi\|_{2},

where
R_{\psi}:=u_{0}\Delta\psi-u_{0yy}\psi
. Similarly, we can also estimate

(3.70)

\displaystyle|(\ref{Non_H}.2)|\lesssim_{H,L}\varepsilon^{-(\frac{5}{6}+)}\Big(\|\frac{R_{\psi}}{\sqrt{-u_{0yy}}}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\nabla\Delta\psi\|_{2}^{2}\Big)\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}.

Combining with the linear estimates, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\frac{R}{\sqrt{-u_{0yy}}}\|_{2}^{2}\,dt+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\nabla\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{H,L}~\varepsilon^{-(\frac{5}{6}+)}\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\Delta\phi\|_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\|\frac{R_{\psi}}{\sqrt{-u_{0yy}}}\|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\nabla\Delta\psi\|_{2}^{2}\Big)\,dt+\|\sqrt{\frac{u_{0}}{-u_{0yy}}}\Delta\phi_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

Since
\psi\in\mathcal{B}
, and by the initial data (
1.23
), we can conclude that
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
when
c_{0}
is small enough. Hence the solution map
\mathcal{S}
maps
\mathcal{B}
into
\mathcal{B}
.

To show
\mathcal{S}
is a contraction mapping, we take
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
, and denote
\phi_{1}=\mathcal{S}(\psi_{1}),\phi_{2}=\mathcal{S}(\psi_{2})
. We further denote
\widetilde{\phi}=\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}
and
\widetilde{\psi}=\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}
. Repeating the linear and nonlinear estimates in this section for the equation for
\widetilde{\phi}
, we will end up with

\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\lesssim c_{0}\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{\mathcal{X}},

which implies that
\mathcal{S}
is a contraction mapping when
c_{0}
is small. By the contraction mapping theorem, there exists a unique solution
\phi
satisfying the estimate (
1.21
). Similar as the linear case, the exponential decay estimate (
1.22
) also follow. This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

4.
Construction of the steady state solution

4.1.
The setup

In this section, we present a unified construction of the steady-state solution that applies in both settings. The construction is uniform in
H,L
and
A
. Therefore, the constants in estimates are allowed to depend on
H,L
and
A
without further specification.

Given the base flow
u_{0}
satisfying either the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
or those in Theorem
1.4
, we define

u_{e}^{0}(y):=u_{0}(y)+Au_{0}^{\prime}(0)\varepsilon^{1/3}.

Then
\{u_{e}^{0},0\}
satisfies the
\varepsilon
-Navier boundary condition (
1.2
). We will first construct an approximated steady solution, and study its stability. The approximated solution has the following ansatz:

(4.1)

\displaystyle u_{a}:=

\displaystyle u_{e}^{0}+\varepsilon u_{e}^{1}+\varepsilon u_{p}^{1},

\displaystyle v_{a}:=

\displaystyle\varepsilon v_{e}^{1}+\varepsilon^{4/3}v_{p}^{1},

\displaystyle p_{a}:=

\displaystyle\varepsilon p_{e}^{1}+\varepsilon^{4/3}p_{p}^{1},

where

\displaystyle u_{p}^{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=u_{p}^{-}(x,\frac{y}{\varepsilon^{1/3}})+u_{p}^{+}(x,\frac{H-y}{\varepsilon^{1/3}}),

\displaystyle v_{p}^{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=v_{p}^{-}(x,\frac{y}{\varepsilon^{1/3}})+v_{p}^{+}(x,\frac{H-y}{\varepsilon^{1/3}}),

\displaystyle p_{p}^{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=p_{p}^{-}(x,\frac{y}{\varepsilon^{1/3}})+p_{p}^{+}(x,\frac{H-y}{\varepsilon^{1/3}}),

are sum of Prandtl boundary layer correctors concentrating near the boundary
y=0
and
y=H
. We only focus on constructing
u_{p}^{-},v_{p}^{-}
near
y=0
, as
u_{p}^{+}
and
v_{p}^{+}
are just the even and odd reflection of
u_{p}^{-}
and
v_{p}^{-}
with respect to
y=H/2
.

We denote the Prandtl variable

Y:=\frac{y}{\varepsilon^{1/3}}.

Plugging the ansatz (
4.1
) into the left-hand side of (
1.1
), near the boundary
y=0
, we have from the
u
equation:

(4.2)

\displaystyle NS[u_{a}]:=

\displaystyle(u_{e}^{0}+\varepsilon u_{e}^{1}+\varepsilon u_{p}^{-})(\varepsilon u_{ex}^{1}+\varepsilon u_{px}^{-})+(\varepsilon v_{e}^{1}+\varepsilon^{4/3}v_{p}^{-})(u_{ey}^{0}+\varepsilon u_{ey}^{1}+\varepsilon^{2/3}u_{pY}^{-})

\displaystyle+\varepsilon p_{ex}^{1}+\varepsilon^{4/3}p_{px}^{-}-\varepsilon u_{eyy}^{0}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u_{e}^{1}-\varepsilon^{2}u_{pxx}^{-}-\varepsilon^{4/3}u_{pYY}^{-};

and from the
v
equation:

(4.3)

\displaystyle NS[v_{a}]:=

\displaystyle(u_{e}^{0}+\varepsilon u_{e}^{1}+\varepsilon u_{p}^{-})(\varepsilon v_{ex}^{1}+\varepsilon^{4/3}v_{px}^{-})+(\varepsilon v_{e}^{1}+\varepsilon^{4/3}v_{p}^{-})(\varepsilon v_{ey}^{1}+\varepsilon v_{pY}^{-})

\displaystyle+\varepsilon p_{ey}^{1}+\varepsilon p_{pY}^{-}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta v_{e}^{1}-\varepsilon^{7/3}v_{pxx}^{-}-\varepsilon^{5/3}v_{pYY}^{-}.

Collecting the terms of order
O(\varepsilon)
that are away from the boundary from (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we know that
\{u_{e}^{1},v_{e}^{1}\}
satisfies the linearized Euler equation:

(4.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{e}^{0}u_{ex}^{1}+u_{ey}^{0}v_{e}^{1}+p_{ex}^{1}&=u_{eyy}^{0},\\
u_{e}^{0}v_{ex}^{1}+p_{ey}^{1}&=0,\\
u_{ex}^{1}+v_{ey}^{1}&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

In the next subsection, we will solve
v_{e}^{1}
with the boundary condition
v_{e}^{1}=0
on
y=0,H
. Then we define

(4.5)

u_{e}^{1}(x,y)=-\int_{0}^{x}v_{ey}^{1}(x^{\prime},y)\,dx^{\prime}.

This creates a mismatch for the boundary condition (
1.2
), which will be corrected by the Prandtl profile
\{u_{p}^{-},v_{p}^{-}\}
.

Next we collect the leading order Prandtl terms. They are
O(\varepsilon^{4/3})
order terms in (
4.2
) and
O(\varepsilon)
order terms in (
4.3
). In view of the boundary condition mismatch above, we know that
\{u_{p}^{-},v_{p}^{-}\}
satisfies the linearized Prandtl equation with a Robin boundary condition:

(4.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \varepsilon^{-1/3}u_{e}^{0}u_{px}^{-}+u_{ey}^{0}v_{p}^{-}+p_{px}^{-}-u_{pYY}^{-}&=0,\\
p_{pY}^{-}&=0,\\
u_{px}^{-}+v_{pY}^{-}&=0,\\
\lim_{Y\to\infty}u_{p}^{-}=\lim_{Y\to\infty}v_{p}^{-}&=0,\\
u_{p}^{-}(x,0)-Au_{pY}^{-}(x,0)&=u_{e}^{1}(x,0)-A\varepsilon^{1/3}u_{ey}^{1}(x,0).\end{aligned}\right.

Note that there is an
\varepsilon^{-1/3}
factor appears in the first term. This is because near the boundary
y=0
,
u_{e}^{0}\sim u_{ey}^{0}(0)y+u_{ey}^{0}(0)A\varepsilon^{1/3}
. Therefore,
\varepsilon^{-1/3}u_{e}^{0}\sim u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)
is of order 1.

Once the approximated solution
\{u_{a},v_{a}\}
is constructed, we set

(4.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{s}&=u_{a}+\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}-}u,\\
v_{s}&=v_{a}+\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}-}v,\end{aligned}\right.

and solve for the remainder
\{u,v\}
. We denote by
\phi
the stream function of the remainder
\{u,v\}
, then
\phi
satisfies the equation

(4.8)

u_{a}\Delta\phi_{x}-\Delta u_{a}\phi_{x}+v_{a}\Delta\phi_{y}-\Delta v_{a}\phi_{y}-\varepsilon\Delta^{2}\phi=-\varepsilon^{-(\frac{4}{3}-)}(\partial_{y}NS[u_{a}]-\partial_{x}NS[v_{a}])-\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}-}(\phi_{y}\Delta\phi_{x}-\phi_{x}\Delta\phi_{y}),

with the viscous boundary condition (
1.5
) and the
\varepsilon
-Navier-slip boundary condition (
1.6
).

In the following subsections we construct solutions to (
4.4
), (
4.6
), and (
4.8
). Note that a similar ansatz (
4.1
) was considered in
[
25
]
by Iyer and Zhou; they assumed that
\partial_{y}^{j}u_{0}(0)=\partial_{y}^{j}u_{0}(H)=0
for
2\leq j\leq N_{0}
with
N_{0}
large. However, this condition is not satisfied by the Poiseuille flow. In our work, we only assume that
\|u_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime}/u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
, which leads to singularities in high-order
y
-derivative of
\{u_{e}^{1},v_{e}^{1}\}
. We show that this weaker assumption nevertheless suffices for the stability analysis.

4.2.
Construction of the Euler profile
\{u_{e}^{1},v_{e}^{1}\}

Consider (
4.4
) in vorticity formulation, i.e.,
\partial_{y}
(
4.4
)
{}_{1}-\partial_{x}
(
4.4
)
2
, we have

(4.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -u_{e}^{0}\Delta v_{e}^{1}+u_{eyy}^{0}v_{e}^{1}&=u_{eyyy}^{0},\\
v_{e}^{1}|_{y=0,H}&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

We will find a solution
v_{e}^{1}=v_{e}^{1}(y)
to (

) that is independent of

u_{e}^{1}(x,y)
is linear in

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists a solution
v_{e}^{1}(y)
to the Euler equation (

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}^{j}v_{e}^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle\mbox{for}~j=0,1,2,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}^{j}v_{e}^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-(j-2)/3},

\displaystyle\mbox{for}~j\geq 3.

Consequently,
u_{e}^{1}(x,y):=-\int_{0}^{x}v_{ey}^{1}
satisfies
u_{exx}^{1}=0
and

(4.11)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{i}\partial_{y}^{j}u_{e}^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle\mbox{for}~i,j=0,1,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{i}\partial_{y}^{j}u_{e}^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{j}\varepsilon^{-(j-1)/3},

\displaystyle\mbox{for}~i=0,1,j\geq 2.

Proof.

Since we look for a solution
v_{e}^{1}
that only depends on
y
, (
4.9
) is reduced to an ODE. Since
u_{e}^{0}
is even with respect to
y=H/2
, we know that
v_{e}^{1}
is odd with respect to
y=H/2
, and consequently
v_{e}^{1}(H/2)=0
. We define

q_{e}:=\frac{v_{e}^{1}}{u_{e}^{0}},

then the equation (

(4.12)

-\Big((u_{e}^{0})^{2}q_{ey}\Big)_{y}=u_{eyyy}^{0}.

Multiplying the equation (
4.12

q_{e}
, integrating by parts, and using Lemma
3.2
, we have

\|u_{e}^{0}q_{ey}\|^{2}\leq\|u_{eyyy}^{0}\|\|q_{e}\|\lesssim\|u_{eyyy}^{0}\|\|u_{e}^{0}q_{ey}\|,

which implies

(4.13)

\|q_{e}\|\leq\|u_{e}^{0}q_{ey}\|\lesssim 1.

Therefore, the existence of
q_{e}
follows from the Lax-Milgram theorem. We also have

\|v_{e}^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|v_{ey}^{1}\|=\|u_{ey}^{0}q\|+\|u_{e}^{0}q_{y}\|\lesssim\|u_{e}^{0}q_{y}\|\lesssim 1.

Since

v_{eyy}^{1}=u_{eyy}^{0}\frac{v_{e}^{1}}{u_{e}^{0}}-\frac{u_{eyyy}^{0}}{u_{e}^{0}},

by (
4.13
) and the assumption that
\|u_{eyyy}^{0}/u_{e}^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
, we have

(4.14)

\|v_{ey}^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|v_{eyy}^{1}\|+\|v_{ey}\|\lesssim 1.

Next, it is easy to see that
|v_{e}^{1}/u_{e}^{0}|\lesssim 1
. Indeed, when
0<y<H/10
, we have

\left|\frac{v_{e}^{1}}{u_{e}^{0}}\right|\lesssim\|v_{ey}^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon^{1/3}+y}\lesssim 1.

Same argument applies when
9H/10<y<H
. When
H/10\leq y\leq 9H/10
,

\left|\frac{v_{e}^{1}}{u_{e}^{0}}\right|\lesssim\|v_{e}^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1.

Therefore,

(4.15)

\|v_{eyy}^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\|\frac{v_{e}^{1}}{u_{e}^{0}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\frac{u_{eyyy}^{0}}{u_{e}^{0}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1.

To estimate
v_{eyyy}^{1}
, we take
y
-derivative on the equation, we have

-u_{e}^{0}v_{eyyy}^{1}-u_{ey}^{0}v_{eyy}^{1}+u_{eyy}^{0}v_{ey}^{1}+u_{eyyy}^{0}v_{e}^{1}=u_{eyyyy}^{0}.

By (
4.13
), (
4.14
), and (
4.15
), we have

|v_{eyyy}^{1}|\lesssim\frac{1}{u_{e}^{0}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1/3}.

For estimating higher derivatives, we keep taking
y
-derivative and bootstrap. This concludes the proof.
∎

4.3.
Construction of the Prandtl profile
\{u_{p}^{1},v_{p}^{1}\}

The goal of this subsection is to construct
\{u_{p}^{-},v_{p}^{-}\}
so that

(4.16)

\varepsilon^{-1/3}u_{e}^{0}u_{px}^{-}+u_{ey}^{0}v_{p}^{-}+p_{px}^{-}-u_{pYY}^{-}=O(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}).

Instead of working on (
4.6
), we consider the approximated system:

(4.17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)u_{px}^{-}+u_{ey}^{0}(0)v_{p}^{-}+p_{px}^{-}-u_{pYY}^{-}&=0,\\
p_{pY}^{-}&=0,\\
u_{px}^{-}+v_{pY}^{-}&=0,\\
\lim_{Y\to\infty}u_{p}^{-}=\lim_{Y\to\infty}v_{p}^{-}&=0,\\
u_{p}^{-}(x,0)-Au_{pY}^{-}(x,0)&=u_{e}^{1}(x,0)-A\varepsilon^{1/3}u_{ey}^{1}(x,0),\end{aligned}\right.

with a boundary condition
u_{p}^{-}(0,Y)=U_{0}(Y)
. We assume standard parabolic compatibility conditions on
U_{0}
at the corner
(0,0)
, and sufficiently fast decay at infinity:

(4.18)

|e^{Y}d_{Y}^{k}U_{0}|\lesssim 1\quad\mbox{for}~0\leq k\leq N_{0}

with some large
N_{0}
. We denote

g(x):=u_{e}^{1}(x,0)-A\varepsilon^{1/3}u_{ey}^{1}(x,0).

From (
4.5
) and (
4.11
), we know that
g(0)=0
,
|g(x)|+|g^{\prime}(x)|\lesssim 1
, and
g^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
. By taking
Y\to\infty
, one can see that
p_{p}^{-}\equiv 0
. Next, we homogenize the Robin boundary condition. We take
\chi(Y)\in C^{\infty}[0,\infty)
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\chi(Y)=1,\quad\mbox{when}~0<Y\leq 1,\\
&\chi(Y)=0,\quad\mbox{when}~Y\geq 2,\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi\,dY=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Let

\displaystyle u(x,Y)

\displaystyle:=u_{p}^{-}(x,Y)-\chi(Y)g(x),

\displaystyle v(x,Y)

\displaystyle:=\int_{Y}^{\infty}u_{px}^{-}(x,Y^{\prime})\,dY^{\prime}+g^{\prime}(x)\int_{0}^{Y}\chi(Y^{\prime})\,dY^{\prime}.

Then
\{u,v\}
satisfies

(4.19)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)u_{x}+u_{ey}^{0}(0)v-u_{YY}&=F,\\
v=\int_{Y}^{\infty}u_{x}(x,Y^{\prime})&\,dY^{\prime},\\
\lim_{Y\to\infty}u&=0,\\
u(x,0)-Au_{Y}(x,0)&=0,\\
u(0,Y)&=U_{0}(Y),\end{aligned}\right.

Proposition 4.2
.

Under the assumptions above, there exists a unique solution
\{u,v\}
to (
4.19
) that satisfies

(4.20)

|(1+Y)^{m}\partial_{x}^{i}\partial_{Y}^{j}\{u,v\}|\lesssim_{m,i,j}1\quad\mbox{for large}~m,i,j.

Proof.

We will only prove the apriori estimates. The existence of solution for such linear Prandtl system is classical, see, e.g.,
[
22
]
.

First, we multiply the equation (
4.19
) by
u_{x}
and integrate in
Y
, we have

(4.21)

\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)u_{x}^{2}\,dY-\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{ey}^{0}(0)vv_{Y}\,dY-\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{YY}u_{x}\,dY=\int_{0}^{\infty}Fu_{x}\,dY.

Integrating by parts, we have

(\ref{energy:basic_Prandtl},2)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{ey}^{0}(0)(v)^{2}_{Y}\,dY=\frac{1}{2}u_{ey}^{0}(0)v^{2}(x,0),

which is positive. Using the Robin boundary condition, we also have

\displaystyle(\ref{energy:basic_Prandtl},3)=\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{Y}u_{Yx}\,dY+u_{Y}(x,0)u_{x}(x,0)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{Y}^{2}\,dY+\frac{1}{2A}\partial_{x}|u(x,0)|^{2}.

Therefore, we end up with a basic energy estimate

(4.22)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)u_{x}^{2}\,dYdx+\int_{0}^{L}|v(x,0)|^{2}\,dx+\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{Y}^{2}\,dY+\sup_{x\in(0,L)}|u(x,0)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F\|_{2}^{2}+\|U_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Here we recall that, due to
g(x)
is linear in
x
and the estimate (
4.11
), we have

\|F\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim_{m}1\quad\mbox{for all}~m\geq 0.

Next, applying
\partial_{Y}
to the equation (
4.19
), we obtain

(4.23)

u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)u_{xY}-u_{YYY}=F_{Y}.

Since
u_{x}\to 0
as

, we have that

(4.24)

u_{x}(0,Y)=-\int_{Y}^{\infty}u_{xY}(0,Y^{\prime})\,dY^{\prime}=-\int_{Y}^{\infty}\frac{1}{u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)}\Big(F_{Y}(0,Y^{\prime})+U_{0YYY}(Y^{\prime})\Big)\,dY^{\prime},

which will serve as the boundary condition for

. Then, applying
\partial_{x}
to the equation (
4.19

(4.25)

u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)u_{xx}+u_{ey}^{0}(0)v_{x}-u_{YYx}=F_{x}.

We test the equation (

and follow the exact same strategy as in deriving (

4.24

(4.26)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)u_{xx}^{2}\,dYdx+\int_{0}^{L}|v_{x}(x,0)|^{2}\,dx+\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{xY}^{2}\,dY+\sup_{x\in(0,L)}|u_{x}(x,0)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla F\|_{2}^{2}+\|U_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}.

Next, we multiply the equation (
4.23
) by

and integrate in

, we have

(4.27)

\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)u_{xY}^{2}\,dY-\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{YYY}u_{xY}\,dY=\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{Y}u_{xY}\,dY.

The first term is positive, and the right-hand side can be estimated using Hölder’s inequality. To estimate (
4.27
.2), we integrate by parts and use the equation (

) and the Robin boundary condition to obtain

\displaystyle(\ref{energy:basic_Y_Prandtl}.2)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{YYY}u_{xY}\,dY=\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{YY}u_{xYY}\,dY+u_{YY}(x,0)u_{xY}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{YY}^{2}\,dY+\Big(Au_{ey}^{0}(0)u_{x}(x,0)+u_{ey}^{0}(0)v(x,0)-F(x,0)\Big)u_{xY}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{YY}^{2}\,dY+u_{ey}^{0}(0)|u_{x}(x,0)|^{2}+u_{ey}^{0}(0)v(x,0)u_{x}(x,0)-F(x,0)u_{x}(x,0),

where the last two terms can be controlled by the positive terms in estimates (

To obtain the higher-order derivative estimates, we inductively differentiate the equation in

and

To obtain the decay estimates, we multiply

to the equations and repeat the same procedure above. This concludes the proof.
∎

Now we have the solution
\{u,v\}
to the approximated Prandtl system (
4.17

\{u_{p}^{-},v_{p}^{-}\}
so that (
4.16
) is satisfied. We take another smooth positive cutoff function
\tilde{\chi}(Y)\in C_{0}^{\infty}[0,\infty)
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{\chi}(Y)=1,\quad\mbox{when}~0<Y\leq 1,\\
&\tilde{\chi}(Y)=0,\quad\mbox{when}~Y\geq 2.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

For
N>0
large, we define

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{p}^{-}&:=\tilde{\chi}\Big(\frac{Y}{N}\Big)u-\frac{1}{N}\tilde{\chi}^{\prime}\Big(\frac{Y}{N}\Big)\int_{0}^{x}v(x^{\prime},Y)\,dx^{\prime},\\
v_{p}^{-}&:=\tilde{\chi}\Big(\frac{Y}{N}\Big)v.\end{aligned}\right.

Now we estimate the left-hand side of (
4.16
). We write

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1/3}u_{e}^{0}u_{px}^{-}+u_{ey}^{0}v_{p}^{-}-u_{pYY}^{-}

\displaystyle=[\varepsilon^{-1/3}u_{e}^{0}-u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)]u_{px}^{-}+[u_{ey}^{0}-u_{ey}^{0}(0)]v_{p}^{-}

\displaystyle+u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)u_{px}^{-}+u_{ey}^{0}(0)v_{p}^{-}-u_{pYY}^{-}.

By Taylor expansion near
y=0
, we know that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1/3}u_{e}^{0}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-1/3}\Big(u_{ey}^{0}(0)A\varepsilon^{1/3}+u_{ey}^{0}(0)y+O(y^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle=u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)+O(\varepsilon^{1/3})Y^{2}.

Therefore, by (
4.20
), we have

[\varepsilon^{-1/3}u_{e}^{0}-u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)]u_{px}^{-}=O(\varepsilon^{1/3})Y^{2}u_{px}^{-}=O(\varepsilon^{1/3}).

Similarly, we can estimate

[u_{ey}^{0}-u_{ey}^{0}(0)]v_{p}^{-}=O(\varepsilon^{1/3})Yv_{p}^{-}=O(\varepsilon^{1/3}).

Finally, by direct computations from the definition of
\{u_{p}^{-},v_{p}^{-}\}
, we have

\displaystyle u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)u_{px}^{-}+u_{ey}^{0}(0)v_{p}^{-}-u_{pYY}^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{u_{ey}^{0}(0)(A+Y)}{N}\tilde{\chi}^{\prime}v-\frac{3}{N}\tilde{\chi}^{\prime}u_{Y}+\frac{3}{N^{2}}\tilde{\chi}^{\prime\prime}u-\frac{1}{N^{3}}\tilde{\chi}^{\prime\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{x}v\,dx=O(\varepsilon^{1/3})

by (
4.22
) and choosing
N=\frac{\varepsilon^{-1/3}}{100}
. Therefore, (
4.16
) is satisfied.

4.4.
Construction of the remainder

In this subsection, we construct the remainder
\{u,v\}
, whose stream function satisfies the equation (
4.8
). We denote

E:=\partial_{y}NS[u_{a}]-\partial_{x}NS[v_{a}].

By the construction of
\{u_{a},v_{a}\}
above, one can see that
|E|\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}
. We will work with the equation

(4.28)

u_{a}\Delta\phi_{x}-\Delta u_{a}\phi_{x}+v_{a}\Delta\phi_{y}-\Delta v_{a}\phi_{y}-\varepsilon\Delta^{2}\phi=-\varepsilon^{-(\frac{4}{3}-)}E-\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}-}(\psi_{y}\Delta\psi_{x}-\psi_{x}\Delta\psi_{y})

with the boundary conditions (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), and use a fixed point argument.

4.4.1.
Small
L

First we construct the solution of (
4.8
) under the assumption of Theorem
1.1
. We define the space
\mathcal{X}_{1}
to be the closure of smooth function satisfying the boundary conditions (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) under the norm

\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}^{2}:=\frac{1}{L}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We define the solution map
\mathcal{S}:\psi\mapsto\phi
, where
\phi
is the solution to (
4.28
) with the boundary conditions (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). Let

\mathcal{B}_{1}:=\{\phi\in\mathcal{X}_{1}:\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}\leq 1\}.

We will prove that
\mathcal{S}
is a contraction mapping on
\mathcal{B}_{1}
. We test the equation (
4.28
) by
u_{0}\Delta\phi W
, with
W=2-\frac{x}{L}
. The estimates of the left-hand side of (

2.2
, as the regularity of

is better than
\{u_{s},v_{s}\}

(4.29)

\frac{1}{L}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

It remains to estimate the right-hand side of (

(4.30)

|\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}-}(E,u_{0}\Delta\phi W)|\lesssim\varepsilon^{0+}\|u_{0}\Delta\phi\|_{2}^{2}.

For the nonlinear terms, by (
2.6

(4.31)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}-}|\Big((\psi_{y}\Delta\psi_{x}-\psi_{x}\Delta\psi_{y}),u_{0}\Delta\phi W\Big)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}-}\Big(\|\psi_{y}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{y}}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\psi_{x}\|_{2}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}+\|\psi_{x}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{y}}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\psi_{y}\|_{2}\|\sqrt{u_{0}}\Delta\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}\Big)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}-}\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}^{2}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}.

\varepsilon

Appendix A

In this Appendix, we provide a proof of (
1.15
) for the Poiseuille flow
\mu(y)=y(1-y)
with
C_{2}=2
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
f
be any
H^{1}
function satisfying
f(1/2)=0
, then

(A.1)

\int_{0}^{1/2}f^{2}\,dy\leq 4\int_{0}^{1/2}\mu^{2}f_{y}^{2}\,dy,

where
\mu(y)=y(1-y)
. The constant
4
is sharp.

Proof.

First, by change of variable
x=1-2y
, one can see that (
A.1
) is equivalent to

\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{2}f_{x}^{2}\,dx,

where
f(x)
satisfies
f(0)=0
. We further change
x=\cos\theta
, and denote
g(\theta)=f(x)
. Then (
A.1
) is equivalent to

(A.2)

\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}g^{2}\cos\theta\,d\theta\leq\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}g_{\theta}^{2}\cos^{3}\theta\,d\theta,

where
g(\theta)
satisfies
g(0)=0
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus and Hölder’s inequality, we can estimate

\displaystyle g^{2}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{0}^{\theta}g_{\theta}(s)\,ds\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\theta}|g_{\theta}(s)|^{2}\cos^{2}s\,ds\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1}{\cos^{2}s}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\theta}|g_{\theta}(s)|^{2}\cos^{2}s\,ds\tan\theta.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}g^{2}\cos\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\theta}|g_{\theta}(s)|^{2}\cos^{2}s\,ds\sin\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}|g_{\theta}(s)|^{2}\cos^{2}s\int_{s}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin\theta\,d\theta ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}|g_{\theta}(s)|^{2}\cos^{3}s\,ds,

which is exactly (
A.2
). To prove the sharpness, for any small
\delta>0
, we define

g_{\delta}(\theta):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tan\theta,&&\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta),\\
&\tan(\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta),&&\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta,\frac{\pi}{2}).\end{aligned}\right.

Since
\tan(\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta)\sim\frac{1}{\delta}
when
\delta
is small, the left-hand side of (
A.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}g_{\delta}^{2}\cos\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\cos\theta}\,d\theta+\tan^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta)\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cos\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\,d\theta-\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}\cos\theta\,d\theta+\tan^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta)O(\delta^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\,d\theta+O(1).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\,d\theta=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}\frac{\cos\theta}{1-\sin^{2}\theta}\,d\theta=\int_{0}^{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta)}\frac{1}{1-x^{2}}\,dx=O(|\log\delta|).

Note that for the right-hand side of (
A.2
), we also have

\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(g_{\delta}^{\prime})^{2}\cos^{3}\theta\,d\theta=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\,d\theta=O(|\log\delta|).

Therefore, we can conclude that for any
\varepsilon>0
, we can find a small
\delta>0
such that

\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}g_{\delta}^{2}\cos\theta\,d\theta>(1-\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(g_{\delta}^{\prime})^{2}\cos^{3}\theta\,d\theta.

This confirms the sharpness of the inequality (
A.1
).
∎

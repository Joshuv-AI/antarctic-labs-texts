A 𝒞 ​ ℛ \mathcal{CR} -rotated Q 1 Q_{1} nonconforming finite element method for Stokes interface problems on local anisotropic fitted mixed meshes
Chenchen Geng, Hua Wang, Fengren Zou

Abstract
We propose a new nonconforming finite element method for solving Stokes interface problems. The method is constructed on local anisotropic mixed meshes, which are generated by fitting the interface through simple connection of intersection points on an interface-unfitted background mesh, as introduced in [ 16 ] . For triangular elements, we employ the standard 𝒞 ​ ℛ \mathcal{CR} element; for quadrilateral elements, a new rotated Q 1 Q_{1} -type element is used. We prove that this rotated Q 1 Q_{1} element remains unisolvent and stable even on degenerate quadrilateral elements. Based on these properties, we further show that the space pair of 𝒞 ​ ℛ \mathcal{CR} -rotated Q 1 Q_{1} elements (for velocity) and piecewise P 0 P_{0} spaces (for pressure) satisfies the inf-sup condition without requiring any stabilization terms. As established in our previous work [ 26 ] , the consistency error achieves the optimal convergence order without the need for penalty terms to control it. Finally, several numerical examples are provided to verify our theoretical results.

Stokes interface problems arise primarily from two-phase incompressible flows, which are prevalent in engineering and scientific computing. These problems are typically modeled using Navier-Stokes equations with discontinuous viscosity coefficients. When the viscosity of the two-phase flow is high, the Stokes interface problem—characterized by discontinuous viscous coefficients—serves as a reasonable simplification of such models.

Unfitted mesh methods exhibit particular effectiveness in addressing interface problems with complex geometries, owing to their flexibility in handling irregular interface structures. Significant advancements have been made in these methods, focusing on enhancing accuracy, computational efficiency, and adaptability to intricate interfaces. Notable examples include the Immersed Finite Element Method (IFEM) and the Extended Finite Element Method (XFEM), which have been extensively studied and validated in numerous works (see, e.g.,

Most unfitted methods face significant challenges when incorporating nonconforming elements. Firstly, the consistency error cannot be adequately controlled. X^{\perp}_{0,h}=\{q_{h}\in X|\,q_{h}|_{K}\in P_{0},\,\forall K\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}

(3.21)

Let
\{\phi_{i}\}^{6}_{i=1}
be a set of basis functions on each edge
\{e_{i}\}^{6}_{i=1}
of local finite element space
U_{h}(M)

M

U_{0,h}=\mathrm{span}\{\phi_{\Gamma,e}\}^{N_{I}}_{j=1},

\bm{U}_{0,h}=U_{0,h}\times U_{0,h}.
Through simple calculations, we know that
\bm{U}_{0,h}\subset\bm{U}_{h}.
First prove that
\bm{U}_{0,h}\times X_{0,h}
satisfies the inf-sup condition.

Before proceeding, we introduce a lemma. While the standard result holds globally on
\Omega
with a domain-dependent constant, the following lemma establishes a local version on a single element where the constant
C
is independent of the element size.

Lemma 3.7
.

For interface elements
M\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
h
, such that for all
p\in L^{2}_{0}(M)
, there exists
\bm{v}\in(H^{1}_{0}(M))^{2}
satisfying:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{v}

\displaystyle=p\quad\mathrm{in}\,M,

(3.22)

\displaystyle|\bm{v}|_{H^{1}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|p\|_{L^{2}(M)},

(3.23)

where
C
depends only on the maximum angle condition of the triangulation.

Proof.

Let
\hat{M}
be the reference triangle element and
F_{M}:\hat{M}\to M
the affine mapping:

\bm{x}=\bm{B}\hat{\bm{x}}+\bm{b}.

For
p\in L^{2}_{0}(M)
, define the scaled function on the reference element
\hat{p}=p\circ F_{M}
. Note that
\hat{p}\in L^{2}_{0}(\hat{M})
since
\int_{\hat{M}}\hat{p}d\hat{\bm{x}}=|\det\bm{B}|\int_{M}pd\bm{x}=0
. It is well known that there exists
\hat{\bm{v}}\in(H^{1}_{0}(\hat{M}))^{2}
such that:

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=\hat{p}\quad\text{in }\hat{M},

(3.24)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{1}(\hat{M})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\hat{M}}\|\hat{p}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{M})},

(3.25)

where
C_{\hat{M}}=O(1)
since
\hat{M}
is a reference triangle. Using the Piola transformation, we define

\bm{v}(\bm{x})=\bm{B}(\hat{\bm{v}}\circ F_{M}^{-1}).

(3.26)

Therefore, we derive

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{v}

\displaystyle=(\hat{\nabla}\cdot\hat{\bm{v}})\circ F_{M}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\hat{p}\circ F_{M}^{-1}

\displaystyle=p.

Since
\hat{\bm{v}}=\bm{0}
on
\partial\hat{M}
, we have
\bm{v}=\bm{0}
on
\partial M
. The gradient transforms as:

\nabla\bm{v}=\bm{B}\bm{B}^{-\top}(\hat{\nabla}\hat{\bm{v}}).

Thus,

\displaystyle|\bm{v}|_{H^{1}(M)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|\nabla\bm{v}|^{2}d\bm{x}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bm{B}\|^{2}\|\bm{B}^{-1}\|^{2}|\det\bm{B}|\int_{\hat{M}}|\hat{\nabla}\hat{\bm{v}}|^{2}d\hat{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{M}^{2}\|\hat{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{1}(\hat{M})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{M}^{2}\|\hat{p}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{M})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|p\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}.

The proof completes.
∎

The following lemma shows that the finite element pair
\bm{U}_{0,h}\times X_{0,h}
satisfies the inf-sup condition.

Lemma 3.8
.

There exists a constant
k_{1}>0
, independent of
h
, such that for any
q_{0,h}\in X_{0,h}
, it holds

k_{1}\|q_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{v}_{1,h}\in\bm{U}_{0,h}\\
\bm{v}_{1,h}\neq\bm{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{b_{h}(\bm{v}_{1,h},q_{0,h})}{\|\bm{v}_{1,h}\|_{\bm{U}_{h}}}.

(3.27)

Proof.

For any
q_{0,h}\in X_{0,h}
and any macro-element
M\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
, since
\int_{M}q_{0,h}dx=0
, Lemma
3.7
guarantees the existence of
\bm{v}_{1}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(M)
satisfying:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{1}

\displaystyle=q_{0,h}\quad\text{in}~M,

\displaystyle|\bm{v}_{1}|_{H^{1}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|q_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

Define the interpolation operator
\Pi^{(1)}_{M}:\bm{H}^{1}(M)\rightarrow\bm{U}_{h}(M)
by

\int_{e}\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v}ds=\int_{e}\bm{v}ds,\quad\forall e\in\mathcal{E}(M).

(3.28)

This implies the representation:

\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{6}\left(\frac{1}{|e_{i}|}\int_{e_{i}}\bm{v}ds\right)\bm{\phi}_{i}.

For
\bm{v}_{1}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(M)
, we have specifically:

\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v}_{1}=\left(\frac{1}{|e_{6}|}\int_{e_{6}}\bm{v}_{1}ds\right)\bm{\phi}_{6}.

We first establish the stability on the triangular sub-element
T
:
Since
\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v}|_{T}\in\bm{P}_{1}(T)
, its gradient is constant. By Green’s formula:

\int_{T}\partial_{x}(\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v})dx=\int_{T}\partial_{x}\bm{v}dx.

Thus,

\partial_{x}(\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v})|_{T}=\frac{1}{|T|}\int_{T}\partial_{x}\bm{v}dx.

The
L^{2}
-norm satisfies:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v})\|_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle=|T|^{1/2}\left|\frac{1}{|T|}\int_{T}\partial_{x}\bm{v}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq|T|^{-1/2}\left|\int_{T}\partial_{x}\bm{v}dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{x}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}.

Similarly,
\|\partial_{y}(\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v})\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim\|\partial_{y}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}
. Therefore,

|\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v}|_{H^{1}(T)}\lesssim|\bm{v}|_{H^{1}(T)}.

(3.29)

For the quadrilateral sub-element
Q
, using scaling argument, we have

|\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v}|_{H^{1}(Q)}\lesssim|\tilde{\Pi}^{(1)}_{\tilde{M}}\bm{\tilde{v}}|_{H^{1}(\tilde{Q})}.

and

\tilde{\Pi}^{(1)}_{\tilde{M}}\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}=\left(\frac{1}{|\tilde{e}_{6}|}\int_{\tilde{e}_{6}}\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}ds\right)\bm{\tilde{\phi}}_{6}.

since
\partial_{\tilde{y}}(\tilde{\Pi}^{(1)}_{\tilde{M}}\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1})
is constant in
\tilde{Q}
, using the same argument as for the triangle subelement, we have

\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}(\tilde{\Pi}^{(1)}_{\tilde{M}}\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1})\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\lesssim\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{Q})}.

Using lemma
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{\tilde{x}}\tilde{\phi}_{6}|_{H^{1}(\tilde{Q})}\lesssim|\tilde{Q}|^{1/2}.

consequently, let
\delta\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}=\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}-\frac{1}{|\tilde{Q}|}\int_{\tilde{Q}}\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}d\tilde{x}
, we derive

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\tilde{x}}(\Pi^{(1)}_{\tilde{M}}\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1})\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{Q})}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{\tilde{x}}(\Pi^{(1)}_{\tilde{M}}\delta\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1})\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{Q})}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{|\tilde{e}_{6}|}\int_{\tilde{e}_{6}}\delta\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}ds\right|\|\partial_{\tilde{x}}\bm{\tilde{\phi}}_{6}\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{Q})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\tilde{Q}|^{1/2}}{|\tilde{e}_{6}|^{1/2}}\|\delta\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{e}_{6})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\tilde{Q}|^{1/2}}{|\tilde{e}_{6}|^{1/2}}\frac{|\tilde{e}_{6}|^{1/2}}{|\tilde{Q}|^{1/2}}(\|\delta\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{Q})}+h_{\tilde{Q}}|\delta\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}|_{H^{1}(\tilde{Q})})

\displaystyle\lesssim|\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}|_{H^{1}(\tilde{Q})}.

Therefore,

|\tilde{\Pi}^{(1)}_{\tilde{M}}\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}|_{H^{1}(\tilde{Q})}\lesssim|\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}|_{H^{1}(\tilde{Q})}.

(3.30)

and furthermore

|\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v}|_{H^{1}(Q)}\lesssim|\tilde{\Pi}^{(1)}_{\tilde{M}}\bm{\tilde{v}}|_{H^{1}(\tilde{Q})}\lesssim|\bm{\tilde{v}}_{1}|_{H^{1}(\tilde{Q})}\lesssim|\bm{v}_{1}|_{H^{1}(Q)}.

Combining (
3.29
) and (
3.30
):

|\Pi^{(1)}_{M}\bm{v}_{1}|_{H^{1}(T\cup Q)}\lesssim|\bm{v}_{1}|_{H^{1}(M)}.

(3.31)

The proof completes by Fortin trick.
∎

Next, we establish the inf-sup condition for
\bm{U}_{h}\times X^{\perp}_{0,h}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

There exists a constant
k_{2}>0
, independent of
h
, such that for any
q^{\bot}_{0,h}\in X^{\bot}_{0,h}
, it holds

k_{2}\|q_{0,h}^{\perp}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{v}_{2,h}\in\bm{U}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{2,h}\neq\bm{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{b_{h}(\bm{v}_{2,h},q_{0,h}^{\perp})}{\|\bm{v}_{2,h}\|_{\bm{U}_{h}}}.

(3.32)

Proof.

By the inf-sup condition for the
\bm{P}_{2}-P_{0}
element
[
5
, Proposition 8.4.3]
, for any
q^{\perp}_{0,h}\in X^{\perp}_{0,h}
, there exists
\bm{v}_{2}
belongs to the
\bm{P}_{2}
Lagrange velocity space satisfying:

\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{2}=q^{\perp}_{0,h},\quad|\bm{v}_{2}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|q^{\perp}_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(3.33)

Define the local interpolation operator
\Pi^{(2)}_{M}:H^{1}(M)\rightarrow\bm{U}_{h}(M)
analogously to
\Pi^{(1)}_{M}
in Lemma
3.8
. The stability estimates on the triangular sub-element
T
follow similarly:

|\Pi^{(2)}_{M}\bm{v}_{2}|_{H^{1}(T)}\lesssim|\bm{v}_{2}|_{H^{1}(T)}.

(3.34)

For the quadrilateral sub-element
Q
, using the scaling techqueic, we have

\displaystyle|\Pi^{(2)}\bm{v}_{2}|_{H^{1}(Q)}\lesssim|\tilde{\Pi}^{(2)}\bm{\tilde{v}}_{2}|_{H^{1}(\tilde{Q})}

Similarly to Lemma
3.8
, it holds

\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}\tilde{\Pi}^{(2)}_{\tilde{M}}\bm{\tilde{v}}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{Q})}\lesssim\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}\bm{\tilde{v}}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{Q})}.

For the
\tilde{x}
-derivative, we derive

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\tilde{x}}\Pi^{(2)}\widetilde{\bm{v}}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{Q})}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\bm{c}\in\bm{P}_{0}}\|\partial_{\tilde{x}}\Pi^{(2)}(\widetilde{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{c})\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{Q})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\widetilde{Q}|^{1/2}\inf_{\bm{c}\in\bm{P}_{0}}|\widehat{\Pi}^{(2)}(\widehat{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{c})|_{H^{1}(\widehat{Q})}\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:affine-est})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\widetilde{Q}|^{1/2}\inf_{\bm{c}\in\bm{P}_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{6}\left|\frac{1}{|\hat{e}_{i}|}\int_{\hat{e}_{i}}(\widehat{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{c})ds\right|\cdot|\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{i}|_{H^{1}(\widehat{Q})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\widetilde{Q}|^{1/2}\inf_{\bm{c}\in\bm{P}_{0}}\|\widehat{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{Q})}\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:basis_bound})}

\displaystyle=|\widetilde{Q}|^{1/2}\inf_{\bm{c}\in\bm{P}_{0}}\|\widetilde{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{Q})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{\bm{c}\in\bm{P}_{0}}\|\widetilde{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{\bm{c}\in\bm{P}_{0}}\|\widetilde{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{c}\|_{H^{1}(\widetilde{M})}\quad\text{(by norm equivalence in $\bm{P}_{2}$)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\widetilde{\bm{v}}_{2}|_{H^{1}(\widetilde{M})}\quad\text{(by norm equivalence in quotient space)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\bm{v}_{2}|_{H^{1}(M)}.

Therefore, we derive

|\Pi^{(2)}_{M}\bm{v}_{2}|_{H^{1}(Q)}\lesssim|\bm{v}_{2}|_{H^{1}(M)}.

(3.35)

Jointly with (
3.34
), we obtain the macro-element stability:

|\Pi^{(2)}_{M}\bm{v}_{2}|_{H^{1}(T\cup Q)}\lesssim|\bm{v}_{2}|_{H^{1}(M)}.

(3.36)

The proof completes by Fortin trick.
∎

Combining these two inf-sup lemmas for the subspaces, we now establish the inf-sup condition for the full space
\bm{U}_{h}\times X_{h}
.

Theorem 3.10
.

There exists a constant
k>0,
independent of
h,
such that for any
q_{h}\in X_{h},
it holds

k\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{U}_{h}\\
\bm{v}_{h}\neq\bm{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\bm{U}_{h}}}.

(3.37)

Proof.

We shall prove an equivalent condition of (
3.37
) (see
[
25
]
): for any
q_{h}\in X_{h},
there exists
\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{U}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle\|q_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h}),

(3.38)

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\bm{U}_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(3.39)

For
q_{h}\in X_{h},
decompose it as

q_{h}=q_{0,h}+q^{\perp}_{0,h},

where
q_{0,h}\in X_{0,h},

q^{\perp}_{0,h}\in X^{\perp}_{0,h}.
By orthogonality,

\|q_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|q_{0,h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|q^{\perp}_{0,h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Define
\bm{v}_{h}=\bm{v}_{1,h}+\gamma\bm{v}_{2,h},
where
\bm{v}_{1,h}
and
\bm{v}_{2,h}
are chosen as in Lemmas
3.8
and
3.9
respectively. Then

b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})=\ b_{h}(\bm{v}_{1,h},q_{0,h})+b_{h}(\bm{v}_{1,h},q^{\perp}_{0,h})+\gamma b_{h}(\bm{v}_{2,h},q_{0,h})+\gamma b_{h}(\bm{v}_{2,h},q^{\perp}_{0,h}).

Since
\bm{v}_{1,h}\in\bm{U}_{0,h},
we have

\displaystyle b_{h}(\bm{v}_{1,h},q^{\perp}_{0,h})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\int_{K}\mathrm{div}\bm{\phi}^{K}_{6}\cdot q^{\perp}_{0,h}\,dx=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}(q^{\perp}_{0,h}|_{K})\int_{\partial K}\bm{\phi}^{K}_{6}\cdot\bm{n}\,ds=0.

Combining Lemmas
3.8
and
3.9
, and using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain:

\displaystyle b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle=b_{h}(\bm{v}_{1,h},q_{0,h})+\gamma b_{h}(\bm{v}_{2,h},q_{0,h})+\gamma b_{h}(\bm{v}_{2,h},q_{0,h}^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\|q_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\gamma b_{h}(\bm{v}_{2,h},q_{0,h})+\gamma\|q_{0,h}^{\perp}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|q_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\gamma\|\bm{v}_{2,h}\|_{U_{h}}\|q_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\gamma\|q_{0,h}^{\perp}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|q_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-C\gamma\|q_{0,h}^{\perp}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|q_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\gamma\|q_{0,h}^{\perp}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\|q_{0,h}^{\perp}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-C\|q_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|q_{0,h}^{\perp}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(1-\frac{C^{2}\gamma}{2}\right)\|q_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C^{2}+1}\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where
\gamma=2/(C^{2}+1).

Furthermore, for the norm bound of
\bm{v}_{h}
, we derive

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\bm{U}_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bm{v}_{1,h}\|_{\bm{U}_{h}}+\gamma\|\bm{v}_{2,h}\|_{\bm{U}_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|q_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|q^{\perp}_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Thus the proof completes.
∎

3.4
An a prior error estimate

The following consistency error estimate is established in
[
26
]
:

Lemma 3.11
.

Assume the solution
(\bm{u},p)
of the Stokes interface problem (
1.1
) satisfies Assumption
3.6
. Then the consistency error satisfies

E_{h}(\bm{u},p,\bm{v}_{h})\lesssim h(\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})}+\|p\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})})\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\bm{U}_{h}}.

(3.40)

Leveraging the inf-sup condition established in Theorem
3.10
, we obtain the following error estimate:

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
(\bm{u},p)
and
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h})
be solutions to problems (
3.16
) and (
3.18
), respectively. Then

\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{U}_{h}}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h(\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})}+\|p\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})}).

(3.41)

Proof.

Since nonconforming elements are employed, Brezzi’s theorem cannot be applied directly. Applying Green’s formula yields

\begin{cases}a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})-b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},p-p_{h})=E(\bm{u},p,\bm{v}_{h})&\forall\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{U}_{h},\\
b_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},q_{h})=0&\forall q_{h}\in X_{h},\end{cases}

(3.42)

where

\displaystyle E(\bm{u},p,\bm{v}_{h})=a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v}_{h})-b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},p)-(\bm{f},\bm{v}_{h}).

For any
(\bm{w}_{h},z_{h})\in\bm{U}_{h}\times X_{h}
, it holds that

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{w}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})-b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},p_{h}-z_{h})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})-b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},p-z_{h})-E(\bm{u},p,\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle b_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{w}_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle=b_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h},q_{h})

for all
\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{U}_{h}
and
q_{h}\in X_{h}
. By Brezzi’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{U}_{h}}+\|p_{h}-z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{U}_{h}}+\|p-z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+h(\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})}+\|p\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})}).

The approximation capability follows since the nonconforming
P_{1}
element space proposed by
[
26
]
is a subspace of
\bm{U}_{h}
. Applying the triangle inequality yields the desired result (
3.41
).
∎

Conclusion

In this work, we proposed a new nonconforming finite element method for solving the Stokes interface problems. The method was constructed on a local anisotropic hybrid mesh, which was first introduced in our earlier work
[
16
]
. The present results further demonstrate the effectiveness of this type of mesh in accurately resolving interface geometry while maintaining computational simplicity. The proposed nonconforming element reduces to the standard Crouzeix–Raviart element on triangular elements and to a new rotated
Q_{1}
- type element on quadrilateral elements. This structure naturally accommodates the use of hybrid meshes and may be beneficial in other applications where elements of different shapes need to be effectively coupled. The consistency error is of optimal convergence order, as proved in our previous paper
[
26
]
. More importantly, we proved that this element satisfies the inf - sup condition without any stabilization terms, which is quite rare in the existing literature on finite element methods for Stokes interface problems.

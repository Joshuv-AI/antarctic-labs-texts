Induced Representation of Topological groupoids
K. N. Sridharan, N. Shravan Kumar

Abstract
. Let G G be a locally compact second countable groupoid with a Haar system. In this article, we introduce the induced representation of G G from a continuous unitary representation of a closed wide subgroupoid H H with a Haarsystem provided there exists a full equivariant system of measures μ = { μ u } u ∈ G 0 \mu=\{\mu^{u}\}_{u\in G^{0}} on G / H G/H . We prove some basic properties of induced representation and a theorem on induction in stages. A groupoid version of Mackey’s tensor product theorem is also provided. We also prove a groupoid version of Frobenius Reciprocity theorem on compact transitive groupoids.

Primary 18B40, 22A30; Secondary 46L08

1.
Introduction

The concept of induced representation plays an important role in the representation theory. It was first developed by Frobenius during the development of the representation theory of finite groups. Later it was introduced in locally compact groups by Mackey and developed the theory through a series of papers
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. In
[
1
]
, Mackey considered separable groups with the inducing representation on a separable Hilbert space. The construction of induced representation is done using the quasi-invariant measure on the corresponding homogeneous space. In
[
5
]
, the theory is generalized to non-separable cases where the existence of such a measure is not always guaranteed. More details on the induced representation of locally compact groups can also be referred to in
[
6
]
.

The theory of induced representation has also been developed for general structures such as hypergroups
[
7
]
and measured groupoids
[
8
]
. In
[
7
]
, the induced representation is constructed from a certain class of representations of closed subhypergroup called inducible representations. The representation of the measured groupoid is over a measurable Hilbert bundle over unit space
G^{0}
. Here, the induced representation is introduced similarly to Rieffel’s version of induced representations in groups
[
9
]
from a closed subgroupoid satisfying the hypothesis in
[
8
, Theorem
1.21
]
. Later, in
[
10
]
, a general theory is formalized using the generalized version of Renault’s Disintegration theorem
[
11
, Theorem
7.8
]
. The paper
[
10
]
also shows that the representation of a second countable locally compact Hausdorff groupoid induced from an irreducible representation of a stability group is irreducible.

This paper introduces the induced representation of topological groupoids
G
from a continuous unitary representation of a closed wide subgroupoid
H
over a continuous field of Hilbert space. Here, we assume the existence of a family of full equivariant measure systems over
G/H
. It is parallel to the notion of
G
- invariant measure on the homogeneous space
G/H
in the group context. Similar to groups,
G/H
forms a left
G
-space by the left multiplication of groupoid on the cosets. The existence of such a measure system is guaranteed in cases, for instance, when
G/H
is a proper
G
-space. We use the equivalence of Hilbert modules and continuous field of Hilbert spaces to obtain the results. The construction of the induced representation is almost similar to what is done in the group case. Unlike groups, the induced representation depends on the equivariant measure system. As we know, in the group, the Hilbert spaces obtained with respect to two different quasi-invariant measures are isometrically isomorphic
[
1
, Theorem
2.1
]
. Here we see a weaker version that the Hilbert modules with respect to two different full equivariant measure systems are Morita equivalent in the sense of
[
12
]
. In a particular case, this Morita equivalence coincides with stably Morita equivalence in the sense of Skeide
[
13
]
.

In Section
2
, we discuss basic results related to groupoids and their representation. In Section
3
, we show the construction of induced representation and prove some basic properties. In Section
4
, we prove what is known as Induction in Stages. We show that if
K
and
H
are closed wide subgroupoids of
G
such that
K\subseteq H
, and
\sigma
a continuous unitary representation of
K
, then the representations
\left(ind_{K}^{G}(\sigma),\gamma\right)
and
\left(ind_{H}^{G}\left(ind_{K}^{H}(\sigma),\mu_{H}\right),\mu_{G}\right)
are unitarily equivalent, where
\gamma
is a full equivariant measure system over
G/K
obtained using the measure systems
\mu_{H}
and
\mu_{G}
over
H/K
and
G/H
respectively. We focus on the outer tensor product of representation of groupoids and the well-known Mackey’s tensor product theorem on groupoids is proved in Section
5
using the tensor product of Hilbert modules. For two groupoids
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
and two representations
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
of the closed wide subgroupoids
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
respectively, we show that representations
\left(ind_{H_{1}}^{G_{1}}(\sigma_{1}),\mu_{1}\right)\times\left(ind_{H_{2}}^{G_{2}}(\sigma_{2}),\mu_{2}\right)
and
\left(ind_{H_{1}\times H_{2}}^{G_{1}\times G_{2}}(\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}),\mu_{1}\times\mu_{2}\right)
are unitarily equivalent. Frobenius reciprocity theorem is an important theorem in the theory of induced representation. Various authors have different versions of Frobenius reciprocity theorems on groups
[
2
,
14
,
15
]
. The hypergroup version of Frobenius Reciprocity theorem for compact hypergroups can be referred to in
[
7
, Theorem
4
]
. In Section
6
, we prove a version of the Frobenius Reciprocity theorem on compact transitive groupoids. Here, we show that the family of intertwining operators
Mor(\pi,\left(ind_{H}^{G}(\sigma),\mu\right))
and
Mor(\pi_{|_{H}},\sigma)
are isomorphic as vector space.

2.
Preliminaries

Let’s start with some basics on groupoids and their representations.

A groupoid is a set
G
endowed with a product map
G^{2}\to G:(x,y)\to xy
, where
G^{2}
is a subset of
G\times G
called the set of composable pairs, and an inverse map
G\to G:x\to x^{-1}
such that the following relations are satisfied:

(i)

(x^{-1})^{-1}=x
,

(ii)

(x,y),(y,z)\in G^{2}
implies
(xy,z),(x,yz)\in G^{2}
and
(xy)z=x(yz)
,

(iii)

(x^{-1},x)\in G^{2}
and if
(x,y)\in G^{2}
, then
x^{-1}(xy)=y
,

(iv)

(x,x^{-1})\in G^{2}
and if
(z,x)\in G^{2}
, then
(zx)x^{-1}=z.

A topological groupoid consists of a groupoid
G
and a topology compatible with the groupoid structure such that:

(i)

x\to x^{-1}:G\to G
is continuous,

(ii)

(x,y)\to xy:G^{2}\to G
is continuous where
G^{2}
has the induced topology from
G\times G
.

Here we consider second countable locally compact Hausdorff groupoids. The unit space
G^{0}
is a locally compact Hausdorff space under the subspace topology. Both range and domain maps are continuous.

The notion of haarsystem of topological groupoids is analogous to the haar measure in groups and we assume the existence of haarsystem. The following is the definition of haarsystem.

A left Haar system for
G
consists of a family
\{\lambda^{u}:u\in G^{0}\}
of positive radon measures on
G
such that,

(i)

the support of the measure
\lambda^{u}
is
G^{u}
,

(ii)

for any
f\in C_{c}(G),u\to\lambda(f)(u):=\int fd\lambda^{u}
is continuous and

(iii)

for any
x\in G
and
f\in C_{c}(G)
,

\int_{G^{d(x)}}f(xy)d\lambda^{d(x)}(y)=\int_{G^{r(x)}}f(y)d\lambda^{r(x)}(y).

According to
[
8
, Proposition
2.4
]
, if
G
is a locally compact groupoid with a left haar system, then the range map
r
is an open map. More details on groupoid can be referred to
[
8
,
16
]

Now we provide the definition of the left action of a groupoid.

Suppose
G
is a topological groupoid and
X
is a locally compact Hausdorff space together with a continuous map
r_{X}:X\to G^{0}
called the moment map. Then a left action of
G
on
X
is a continuous map
(\gamma,x)\to\gamma\cdot x
from
G*X=\{(\gamma,x)\in G\times X:s(\gamma)=r_{X}(x)\}
to X such that

(i)

r_{X}(x)\cdot x=x~~\forall x\in X
and

(ii)

if
(\gamma,\eta)\in G^{2}
and
(\eta,x)\in G*X
, then
(\gamma,\eta\cdot x)\in G*X
and
\gamma\eta\cdot x=\gamma\cdot(\eta\cdot x)
.

X
is called left
G
-space.

If
Y
and
X
are both left
G
-spaces and
\pi
is equivariant, i.e.
\pi(g\cdot y)=g\cdot\pi(y)
, then a fully equivariant
\pi
-
system
\beta
is a family of measures
\{\beta^{x}:x\in X\}
on
Y
such that

(i)

supp(\beta^{x})=\pi^{-1}(x)

(ii)

x\to\beta(f)(x)=\int_{Y}f(y)d\beta^{x}(y)
is continuous for
f\in C_{c}(Y)

(iii)

For
f\in C_{c}(Y),(\gamma,x)\in G*X
,

\int_{Y}f(\gamma\cdot y)d\beta^{x}(y)=\int_{Y}f(y)d\beta^{\gamma\cdot x}(y)

The left haarsystem of a groupoid is an example of the fully equivariant
\pi
-system.

Now we define the continuous representation of groupoids over a continuous filed of Hilbert spaces.
A continuous field of Hilbert spaces over
G^{0}
is a family
\{\mathcal{H}_{u}\}_{u\in G^{0}}
of Hilbert spaces, with a set
\Gamma\subset\prod_{u\in G^{0}}\mathcal{H}_{u}
of vector fields such that:

(i)

\Gamma
is a complex linear subspace of
\prod_{u\in G^{0}}\mathcal{H}_{u}
.

(ii)

For every
u\in G^{0}
, the set
\xi(u)
for
\xi\in\Gamma
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{u}
.

(iii)

For every
\xi\in\Gamma
, the function
u\to\|\xi(u)\|
is continuous.

(iv)

Let
\xi\in\prod_{u\in G^{0}}\mathcal{H}_{u}
be a vector field; if for every
u\in G^{0}
and every
\epsilon>0
, there exists an
\xi^{\prime}\in\Gamma
such that
\|\xi(s)-\xi^{\prime}(s)\|<\epsilon
on a neighbourhood of
u
, then
\xi\in\Gamma
.

For a continuous field of Hilbert spaces, we can define a topology on
\mathcal{H}=\sqcup_{u\in G^{0}}H_{u}
, generated by the sets of the form

U(V,\xi,\epsilon)=\{h\in\mathcal{E}:\|h-\xi(p(h))\|<\epsilon,\xi\in\Gamma,p(h)\in V\}.

where
V
is an open set in
G^{0}
,
\epsilon>0
, and
p:\mathcal{H}\to G^{0}
is the projection of the total space
\mathcal{H}
to base space
G^{0}
such that fiber
p^{-1}(u)=\mathcal{H}_{u},u\in G^{0}
. This map is a surjective continuous open map under the above topology. We denote such continuous field of Hilbert space as
(\mathcal{H},\Gamma)
. With the above topology, it forms the structure of a continuous Hilbert bundle, and
\Gamma
forms the continuous sections. Here, we assume that each fiber of
\mathcal{H}
is a nonzero Hilbert space. We denote the space of continuous sections vanishing at infinity as
C_{0}(G^{0},\mathcal{H})
. Since
G^{0}
is locally compact, we can see that
C_{0}(G^{0},\mathcal{H})
is fibrewise dense and forms a Banach space in
\Gamma
under supremum norm. According to
[
17
, Remark
13.19
]
, a continuous Hilbert bundle over
G^{0}
has enough continuous sections, i.e for every
b\in\mathcal{H}
, there exist a continuous section
\alpha
such that
\alpha(p(b))=b
. A subset
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{H}
is a subbundle if
p_{|_{\mathcal{K}}}
is continuous onto open map and each fibre of
p^{-1}(u)\cap\mathcal{K}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
. The topology of
\mathcal{K}
is inherited from
\mathcal{H}
. For more details, refer
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
.

A continuous representation of groupoid
G
is a double
(\mathcal{H}^{\pi},\pi)
, where
\mathcal{H}^{\pi}=\{\mathcal{H}^{\pi}_{u}\}_{u\in G^{0}}
is a continuous Hilbert bundle over
G^{0}
such that

(i)

\pi(x)\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\pi}_{d(x)},\mathcal{H}^{\pi}_{r(x)}\right)
is a unitary operator, for each
x\in G
,

(ii)

\pi(u)
is the identity map on
\mathcal{H}^{\pi}_{u}
for all
u\in G^{0}
,

(iii)

\pi(x)\pi(y)=\pi(xy)
for all
(x,y)\in G^{2}
,

(iv)

\pi(x)^{-1}=\pi\left(x^{-1}\right)
for all
x\in G
,

(v)

x\rightarrow\langle\pi(x)\xi(d(x)),\eta(r(x))\rangle
is continuous for every
\eta,\xi\in C_{0}(G^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\pi})
.

Suppose
(\mathcal{H}^{\pi},\pi)
and
(\mathcal{H}^{\pi^{\prime}},\pi^{\prime})
be two continuous representations of groupoid
G
, then
Mor(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
refers to the bundle morphisms
T:\mathcal{H}^{\pi}\to\mathcal{H}^{\pi^{\prime}}
such that

T_{r(x)}\pi(x)=\pi^{\prime}(x)T_{d(x)},~~x\in G.

Note that
Mor(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
is a vector space.

We say that two representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are unitarily equivalent, denoted
\pi\sim\pi^{\prime}
, if there exist
T\in Mor(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
such that each
T_{u}
is unitary operator. A representation
(\mathcal{H}^{\pi},\pi)
is called reducible if there is a subbundle
\mathcal{K}
invariant under
\pi
, i.e for every
u
,
\mathcal{K}_{u}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\pi}_{u}
and
\pi(x)\mathcal{K}_{d(x)}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{r(x)}
. It is irreducible if it is not reducible.

3.
Construction of Induced representation

Let
H
be a wide closed subgroupoid of a second countable locally compact groupoid
G
with haar system
\{\lambda_{H}^{u}\}_{u\in G^{0}}
. We can easily verify that
G/H
is a left
G
-space. By the arguments similar to
[
8
, Proposition
2.1
]
,
G/H
is a locally compact Hausdorff space under quotient topology with quotient map
q_{H}
and the moment map
r_{G^{0}}:G/H\to G^{0},r_{G^{0}}(gH)=r(g)
is an open continuous map. We assume and fix a full equivariant
r_{G^{0}}
- system,
\mu=\{\mu^{u}\}_{u\in G^{0}}
on the left
G
- space
G/H
.

For
f\in C_{c}(G)
, we can define a function
Pf
in
C_{c}(G/H)
, such that

Pf(xH)=\int_{H}f(x\xi)d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}(\xi).

It is well defined, due to the invariance of haar system
\{\lambda^{u}_{H}\}_{u\in G^{0}}
and
supp(Pf)\subset q(supp(f))
. The continuity of the function can be observed using
[
19
, Lemma
3.12
]
.
Now, we prove an important lemma which is later being used in this paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
J\subset G/H
is compact, there exists
f\geq 0
in
C_{c}(G)
such that
Pf=1
on
J
.

Proof.

Let
E
be a compact neighbourhood of
J

in
G/H
. There exist a compact
K\subset G
such that
q_{H}(K)=E
. Choose a non negative
g\in C_{c}(G)
with
g>0
on
K
and
\phi\in C_{c}(G/H)
supported in
E
such that
\phi=1
on
J
. Define a continuous function

f=\frac{\phi\circ q_{H}}{Pg\circ q_{H}}g.

Here
f
is continuous since
Pg>0
on
supp(\phi)
, its support is contained in
supp(g)
and
Pf=\phi
.
∎

Suppose
(\mathcal{H}^{\sigma},\sigma)
is a continuous unitary representation of
H
. Let
C(G,\mathcal{H}^{\sigma})
denotes the set of continuous function
f
such that
f(x)\in\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}
and its subspace
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{H}^{\sigma})
be the functions with compact support.
Define,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma}(G)=\Bigg\{f\in C(G,\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}):~

\displaystyle q_{H}(supp(f))~\text{is compact}~\text{and}

\displaystyle f(x\xi)=\sigma(\xi^{-1})f(x),\text{for}~(x,\xi)\in G^{2},\xi\in H\Bigg\}.

The next proposition provides explicitly the structure of functions in
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma}(G)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\alpha\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{H}^{\sigma})
, then the function

f_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{H}\sigma(\eta)\alpha(x\eta)d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}(\eta)

belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma}(G)
. Moreover, every element of
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma}(G)
is of the form
f_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{H}^{\sigma})
.

Proof.

For each
x\in G
, the above integral makes sense due to
[
19
, Lemma
3.4
,
3.5
]
and
[
20
, Appendix Theorem
A.20
]
.
Also it is clear that
q_{H}(supp~(f_{\alpha}))\subset q_{H}(supp~(\alpha))
, and for
(x,\xi)\in G^{2},\xi\in H
, we have

f_{\alpha}(x\xi)=\int_{H}\sigma(\eta)\alpha(x\xi\eta)d\lambda_{H}^{d(\xi)}(\eta)=\int_{H}\sigma(\xi^{-1}\eta)\alpha(x\eta)d\lambda^{r(\xi)}_{H}(\eta)=\sigma(\xi^{-1})f_{\alpha}(x).

Next, we show that
f_{\alpha}
is continuous. For that, it is enough to show that
x\to\|f_{\alpha}(x)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}
and
x\to\langle f_{\alpha}(x),t(d(x))\rangle_{H_{d(x)}}
is continuous, where
t\in C_{0}(G^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma})
.

Since
\sigma
is a unitary representation, by [Lemma
3.4
,
3.5
]
[
19
]

(x,\eta)\to\sigma(\eta)\alpha(x\eta)
is continuous over
(G\times H)\cap G^{2}
.
Now, for
t\in C_{0}(G^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma})

\langle f_{\alpha}(x),t(d(x)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}=\int_{H}\langle\sigma(\eta)\alpha(x\eta),t(d(x))\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}(\eta)

Using
[
19
, Lemma
3.12
]
, we can say that
x\to\langle f_{\alpha}(x),t(d(x)\rangle_{H_{d(x)}}
is continuous.
Similarly,

\|f_{\alpha}(x)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}=\iint_{H}\langle\sigma(\eta)\alpha(x\eta),\sigma(k)\alpha(xk)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}(k)d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}(\eta)

is continuous .

Suppose
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma}(G)
, then by Lemma
3.1
, there exist
\Psi\in C_{c}(G)
, such that
\int_{H}\Psi(x\eta)d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}(\eta)=1
for
x\in supp(f)
. Let
\alpha=\Psi f
, then

f_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{H}\Psi(x\eta)\sigma(\eta)f(x\eta)d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}=\int_{H}\Psi(x\eta)f(x)d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}(\eta)=f(x).

So,
f=f_{\alpha}.

∎

Since
\sigma
is a unitary representation, we can easily see that for
f,g\in\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma}(G)
, the function
x\to\langle f(x),g(x)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}
defines a function in
C_{c}(G/H)
and thus we can define a function on
C_{0}(G^{0})
,
as

\langle f,g\rangle(u)=\int_{G/H}\langle f(x),g(x)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}d\mu^{u}(xH).

Define a norm on
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma}(G)
, as

For
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma}(G)

\|f\|=\sup_{u\in G^{0}}\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle(u)}.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}(G)
be the completion of
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma}(G)
under the above norm. It is also denoted as
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}(G,\mu)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The Banach space
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}(G)
form a full left Hilbert
C_{0}(G^{0})
- module under the following action:

\text{For}~~b\in C_{0}(G^{0}),f\in\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}(G),~~bf(x)=b(r(x))f(x).

Proof.

Let
f_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma}(G)
and
b\in C_{0}(G^{0})
, then by definition

\displaystyle bf_{\alpha}(x)=b(r(x))f_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=b(r(x))\int_{H}\sigma(\xi)\alpha(x\xi)d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\sigma(\xi)b(r(x\xi))\alpha(x\xi)d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\sigma(\xi)(b\alpha)(x\xi)d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}(\xi)=f_{b\alpha}(x).

Thus,

\langle bf_{\alpha},bf_{\alpha}\rangle(u)=\int_{G/H}\langle bf_{\alpha}(x),bf_{\alpha}(x)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}d\mu^{u}(xH)=|b(u)|^{2}\langle f,f\rangle(u).

So,

\|bf_{\alpha}\|\leq\|b\|_{\infty}\|f_{\alpha}\|.

It is easy to see that the map
(f,g)\to\langle f,g\rangle:\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}(G)\times\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}(G)\to C_{0}(G^{0})
satisfies every conditions in the definition
[
21
, Chapter
1
, (1.1)]
. Also, by definition
\|f\|=\|\langle f,f\rangle\|_{C_{0}(G^{0})}^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Since each fiber of
\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}
is nonzero and
G^{0}
is locally compact Hausdorff, we can easily see that the span of
\{\langle f,g\rangle:f,g\in\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}(G)\}
is dense in
C_{0}(G^{0})
by Stone Weierstrass theorem on locally compact space.
∎

Now, using
[

, Theorem

which forms a continuous Hilbert bundle
\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{\sigma}
, also denoted as
\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{\sigma}_{G}

We can identify
\mathcal{F}^{u}
with the completion of
\{f_{|_{G^{u}}}:f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma}(G)\}
under the norm

.
The space of continuous sections can be defined as

\Delta=\{t_{F}\in C_{0}(G^{0},\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{\sigma}):t_{F}(u)=F+\mathcal{N}^{u},F\in\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}(G)\}.

More details can also be referred to in
[

,
22
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
h\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{H}^{\sigma})
. Then the map
u\to\|h^{u}\|_{\infty}
is continuous over
G^{0}
, where
\|h^{u}\|_{\infty}=\sup\{\|h(x)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}:x\in G^{u}\}
.

Proof.

Let
u_{0}\in G^{u_{0}}
. Since the function
x\to\|h(x)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}
belongs to
C_{c}(G)
, there exist
x_{0}\in G^{u_{0}}
such that
\|h^{u_{0}}\|_{\infty}=\|h(x_{0})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x_{0})}}
. Suppose that for
\epsilon>0
, there exist a sequence of
u_{n}\to u_{0}\in G^{0}
such that
\|h^{u_{n}}\|_{\infty}\geq\|h^{u_{0}}\|_{\infty}+\epsilon
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then there exist a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in the support of
h
such that
\|h(x_{n})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x_{n})}}\geq\|h^{u_{0}}\|_{\infty}+\epsilon,x_{n}\in G^{u_{n}}
. By compactness argument, there exist
x\in G^{u_{0}}
such that
\|h(x)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}\geq\|h^{u_{0}}\|_{\infty}+\epsilon
, which is a contradiction. So, there exist a neighbourhood,
U
, around
u_{0}
such that
\|h^{u}\|_{\infty}<\|h^{u_{0}}\|+\epsilon
. Further, by continuity of
h
we can find a neighbourhood,
U_{0}
of
u_{0}
such that

\displaystyle=\epsilon(f,\xi)(xh_{0})=\int_{K}f(xh_{0}k)\sigma(k)\xi(d(k))d\lambda_{K}^{d(h_{0})}(k)

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\int_{H}f_{1}(h^{-1})f_{2}(xh_{0}kh)\sigma(k)\xi(d(k))d\lambda_{H}^{d(k)}(h)d\lambda_{K}^{d(h_{0})}(k)

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\int_{H}f_{1}(h^{-1}h_{0}k)f_{2}(xh)\sigma(k)\xi(d(k))d\lambda_{H}^{r(h_{0})}(h)d\lambda_{K}^{d(h_{0})}(k)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{K}f_{1}(h^{-1}h_{0}k)f_{2}(xh)\sigma(k)\xi(d(k))d\lambda_{K}^{d(h_{0})}(k)d\lambda_{H}^{d(x)}(h)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{2}(f_{2},t_{F})(x)(h_{0})

By, Lemma
3.7
, the result follows.
∎

Using all the series of lemmas above,
\Phi
extends uniquely to a surjective isometric module map from Hilbert module
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}(G)
to
\mathcal{F}^{ind_{K}^{H}(\sigma)}(G)
. By
[
21
, Theorem
3.5
]
,
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}(G)
and
\mathcal{F}^{ind_{K}^{H}(\sigma)}(G)
are unitarily equivalent. Also,
\Phi
induces a morphism between the corresponding continuous field of Hilbert spaces
\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{\sigma}_{G}
and
\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{G}^{ind_{K}^{H}(\sigma)}
which intertwines with the representations
\left(ind_{K}^{G}(\sigma),\gamma\right)
and
\left(ind_{H}^{G}\left(ind_{K}^{H}(\sigma),\mu_{H}\right),\mu_{G}\right)
.
Thus the following theorem is proved.

Theorem 4.4
.

K
and

be closed wide subgroupoids of
G
such that

, and

5.
Tensor products of irreducible representation

Suppose
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are two locally compact second countable groupoids,
G_{1}\times G_{2}
is again a groupoid having unit space
G_{1}^{0}\times G_{2}^{0}
. The product and inverse are component-wise. If
\{\lambda^{u_{1}}\}_{u_{1}\in G_{1}^{0}}
and
\{\lambda^{u_{2}}\}_{u_{2}\in G_{2}^{0}}
are the haarsystem of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
respectively, then
\{\lambda^{u_{1}}\times\lambda^{u_{2}}\}_{(u_{1},u_{2})\in G_{1}^{0}\times G_{2}^{0}}
forms a haarsystem of
G_{1}\times G_{2}
.

If
(\mathcal{H}^{1},\pi_{1})
and
(\mathcal{H}^{2},\pi_{2})
are two representations of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
respectively, we can define outer tensor product
\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
of
G_{1}\times G_{2}
acting on
\mathcal{H}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{2}
such that

For
(x,y)\in G_{1}\times G_{2}
,
(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})(x,y):\mathcal{H}^{1}_{d(x)}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{2}_{d(y)}\to\mathcal{H}^{1}_{r(x)}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{2}_{r(y)}

(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})(x,y)v_{1}\otimes v_{2}=\pi_{1}(x)v_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}(y)v_{2}.

In this section, we prove the groupoid version of Mackey’s tensor product theorem.

Let
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
be two representations of closed wide subgroupoids
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
respectively. The exterior tensor product
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{1}}(G_{1})\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{2}}(G_{2})
is a
C_{0}(G_{1}^{0})\otimes_{*}C_{0}(G_{2}^{0})
-module where
\otimes_{*}
denote the completion of algebraic tensor product
C_{0}(G_{1}^{0})\otimes C_{0}(G_{2}^{0})
under the spatial
C^{*}
-norm. More details can be referred to in
[
21
]
. Since
C_{0}(G_{1}^{0})
is commutative, it is nuclear, and hence the
C^{*}
-norm is unique. Also the injective tensor product of
C_{0}(G_{1}^{0})
and
C_{0}(G_{2}^{0})
coincides with the spatial
C^{*}
-norm
[
24
, Theorem
4.14
]
. Using
[
25
, Theorem
6.4.17
]
and
[
26
, Theorem
20.5.6
]
,
C_{0}(G_{1}^{0})\otimes_{*}C_{0}(G_{2}^{0})\cong C_{0}(G_{1}^{0}\times G_{2}^{0})
. Also we can easily see that the pre-Hilbert
C_{0}(G_{1}^{0}\times G_{2}^{0})
-module
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{1}}_{0}(G_{1})\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{2}}_{0}(G_{2})
is dense in
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{1}}(G_{1})\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{2}}(G_{2})
.

Let
\{\mu_{1}^{u}\times\mu_{2}^{v}\}_{(u,v)\in G_{1}^{0}\times G_{2}^{0}}
is the equivariant
r_{G^{0}\times G^{0}}
-system on the left
G_{1}\times G_{2}
-space
(G_{1}\times G_{2})/(H_{1}\times H_{2})\cong G_{1}/H_{1}\times G_{2}/H_{2}
, where
\mu_{1}^{u}
and
\mu_{2}^{v}
are equivariant
r_{G^{0}}
- systems of
G_{1}/H_{1}
and
G_{2}/H_{2}
respectively.

For
(f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{1}}_{0}(G_{1})\times\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{2}}_{0}(G_{2})
, we define a function from
G_{1}\times G_{2}
to
\mathcal{H}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{2}
such that

(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y)=f_{1}(x)\otimes f_{2}(y).

We can easily see that
(f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}}(G_{1}\times G_{2})
.
Now, define a module map
\Phi
from
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{1}}_{0}(G_{1})\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{2}}_{0}(G_{2})
to
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}}(G_{1}\times G_{2})
as

\Phi((f_{1}\otimes f_{2}))(x,y)=f_{1}(x)\otimes f_{2}(y).

Lemma 5.1
.

The module map
\Phi
preserves the
C_{0}(G_{1}^{0}\times G_{2}^{0})
- valued innerproduct.

Proof.

\displaystyle\langle\Phi((f_{1}\otimes f_{2})),\Phi((g_{1}\otimes g_{2}))\rangle(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{\frac{G_{1}}{H_{1}}\times\frac{G_{2}}{H_{2}}}\langle\Phi((f_{1},f_{2}))(x,y),\Phi((g_{1},g_{2}))(x,y)\rangle d\mu_{1}^{u}(xH_{1})d\mu_{2}^{v}(yH_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{\frac{G_{1}}{H_{1}}\times\frac{G_{2}}{H_{2}}}\langle f_{1}(x)\otimes f_{2}(y),g_{1}(x)\otimes g_{2}(y)\rangle d\mu_{1}^{u}(xH_{1})d\mu_{2}^{v}(yH_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{\frac{G_{1}}{H_{1}}\times\frac{G_{2}}{H_{2}}}\langle f_{1}(x),g_{1}(x)\rangle\langle f_{2}(y),g_{2}(y)\rangle d\mu_{1}^{u}(xH_{1})d\mu_{2}^{v}(yH_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G_{1}/{H_{1}}}\langle f_{1}(x),g_{1}(x)\rangle d\mu_{1}^{u}(xH_{1})\int_{G_{2}/{H_{2}}}\langle f_{2}(y),g_{2}(y)\rangle d\mu_{2}^{v}(yH_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{1},g_{1}\rangle(u)\langle f_{2},g_{2}\rangle(v)=\langle f_{1}\otimes f_{2},g_{1}\otimes g_{2}\rangle(u,v).\qed

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f_{i}\in C_{c}(G_{i})
and
t_{i}\in C_{0}(G_{i}^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma_{i}})
,
i=1,2
. Define
f\in C_{c}(G_{1}\times G_{2})
by
f(x_{1},x_{2})=f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})
. Then,

\mathcal{E}(f,t_{1}\otimes t_{2})(x_{1},x_{2})=\mathcal{E}(f_{1},t_{1})(x_{1})\otimes\mathcal{E}(f_{2},t_{2})(x_{2}).

Proof.

Fix
(x_{1},x_{2})\in G_{1}\times G_{2}
.
For all
t_{1}^{\prime}\in C_{0}(G_{1}^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma_{1}})
and
t_{2}^{\prime}\in C_{0}(G_{2}^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma_{2}})
,

\displaystyle\Big\langle\mathcal{E}(f,t_{1}\otimes t_{2})(x_{1},x_{2}),t_{1}^{\prime}(d(x_{1}))\otimes t_{2}^{\prime}(d(x_{2}))\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\limits_{H_{1}\times H_{2}}f_{1}(x_{1}h_{1})f_{2}(x_{2}h_{2})\Big\langle\sigma_{1}(h_{1})t_{1}(d(h_{1})),t_{1}^{\prime}(d(x_{1}))\Big\rangle\Big\langle\sigma_{2}(h_{2})t_{2}(d(h_{2})),t_{2}^{\prime}(d(x_{2}))\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 284.52756ptd\lambda_{H_{1}}^{d(x_{1})}(h_{1})d\lambda_{H_{2}}^{d(x_{2})}(h_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\mathcal{E}(f_{1},t_{1})(x_{1}),t_{1}^{\prime}(d(x_{1}))\Big\rangle\Big\langle\mathcal{E}(f_{2},t_{2})(x_{2}),t_{2}^{\prime}(d(x_{2}))\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\mathcal{E}(f_{1},t_{1})(x_{1})\otimes\mathcal{E}(f_{2},t_{2})(x_{2}),t_{1}^{\prime}(d(x_{1}))\otimes t_{2}^{\prime}(d(x_{2}))\Big\rangle.\qed

Lemma 5.3
.

The image of
\mathcal{E}(C_{c}(G_{1}),C_{0}(G_{1}^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma_{1}}))\otimes\mathcal{E}(C_{c}(G_{2}),C_{0}(G_{2}^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma_{2}}))
under
\Phi
is total in
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}}(G_{1}\times G_{2})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.7
, it is clear that
\mathcal{E}(f,t)
where
f\in C_{c}(G_{1}\times G_{2})
and
t\in C_{0}(G_{1}^{0}\times G_{2}^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\sigma_{2}})
is a total subset of
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}}(G_{1}\times G_{2})
. Also note that
C_{0}(G_{1}^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma_{1}})\otimes C_{0}(G_{2}^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma_{2}})
is fibrewise dense in
\mathcal{H}^{\sigma_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\sigma_{2}}
. Hence, we can say that
\mathcal{E}(f,t_{1}\otimes t_{2})
, where
t_{i}\in C_{0}(G_{i}^{0},\mathcal{H}^{i}),i=1,2,
is a total subset in
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}}(G_{1}\times G_{2})
.
So, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{E}(f,t_{1}\otimes t_{2})
, can be approximated by linear combination of
\Phi(\mathcal{E}(g_{1},t_{1})\otimes\mathcal{E}(g_{2},t_{2})),g_{i}\in C_{c}(G_{i}),i=1,2
, in
\mathcal{F}^{\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}}(G_{1}\times G_{2})
.

Now, let
V_{i},i=1,2
, be two relatively compact subsets of
G_{i},f\in C_{c}(G_{1}\times G_{2})
with
supp(f)\subset V_{1}\times V_{2}
. Then, by Lemma
3.7
,

\|\mathcal{E}(g,t)\|\leq c\|g\|_{\infty}\|t\|

(2)

for some

and for all

and
g\in C_{c}(G_{1}\times G_{2})
with
supp(g)\subset\bar{V}_{1}\times\bar{V}_{2}
.

Given

f_{ij}\in C_{c}(G_{i}),i=1,2,j=1,...,n
such that

and

|f(x_{1},x_{2})-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}f_{1j}(x_{1})f_{2j}(x_{2})|<\epsilon

for all

and by Lemma

,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Phi(\mathcal{E}(f_{1j},t_{1})\otimes\mathcal{E}(f_{2j},t_{2}))(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{E}(f_{1j},t_{1})(x_{1})\otimes\mathcal{E}(f_{2j},t_{2})(x_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{E}(f_{j},t_{1}\otimes t_{2})(x_{1},x_{2}).

\mathcal{E}(f,t_{1}\otimes t_{2})-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Phi(\mathcal{E}(f_{1j},t_{1})\otimes\mathcal{E}(f_{2j},t_{2}))(x_{1},x_{2})=\mathcal{E}\Big(f-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}f_{1j}f_{2j},t_{1}\otimes t_{2}\Big)(x_{1},x_{2}).

(2)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}(f,t_{1}\otimes t_{2})-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Phi(\mathcal{E}(f_{1j},t_{1})\otimes\mathcal{E}(f_{2j},t_{2}))\|

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}\Big(f-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}f_{1j}f_{2j},t_{1}\otimes t_{2}\Big)\|\leq c\|t_{1}\|\|t_{2}\|\|f-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}f_{1j}f_{2j}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon~c\|t_{1}\|\|t_{2}\|.\qed

is a surjective isometric

-linear map from

, Theorem

]
, Hilbert modules

and

are unitarily equivalent. Thus, by
[

, Theorem
4.2.4
]
,
\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{G_{1}}^{\sigma_{1}}\otimes\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{G_{2}}^{\sigma_{2}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{G_{1}\times G_{2}}^{\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}}
are isometrically isometric Hilbert bundles.

Now, we prove Mackey’s tensor product theorem on locally compact groupoids.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two locally compact groupoids with closed wide subgroupoids
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
. Then for any representations
\sigma_{1}
of
H_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
of
H_{2}
, the representations
\left(ind_{H_{1}}^{G_{1}}\sigma_{1},\mu_{1}\right)\times\left(ind_{H_{2}}^{G_{2}}\sigma_{2},\mu_{2}\right)
and
\left(ind_{H_{1}\times H_{2}}^{G_{1}\times G_{2}}(\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}),\mu_{1}\times\mu_{2}\right)
are equivalent.

Proof.

From previous lemmas and results, it is enough to show that the morphism induced by
\Phi
, again denoted as
\Phi
, from
\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{G_{1}}^{\sigma_{1}}\otimes\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{G_{2}}^{\sigma_{2}}
to
\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}}_{G_{1}\times G_{2}}
intertwines with
\left(ind_{H_{1}}^{G_{1}}\sigma_{1},\mu_{1}\right)\times\left(ind_{H_{2}}^{G_{2}}\sigma_{2},\mu_{2}\right)
and
\left(ind_{H_{1}\times H_{2}}^{G_{1}\times G_{2}}(\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}),\mu_{1}\times\mu_{2}\right)
.

Let
(x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})\in G_{1}\times G_{2}
,
f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\sigma_{2}}
, then

\displaystyle\Big(ind_{H_{1}\times H_{2}}^{G_{1}\times G_{2}}(\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2})(x_{1},x_{2})\Phi(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})\Big)(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})(x_{1}^{-1}y_{1},x_{2}^{-1}y_{2})=f_{1}(x_{1}^{-1}y_{1})\otimes f_{2}(x_{2}^{-1}y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ind_{H_{1}}^{G_{1}}\sigma_{1}(x_{1})f_{1})(y_{1})\otimes(ind_{H_{2}}^{G_{2}}\sigma_{2}(x_{2})f_{2})(y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\Big((ind_{H_{1}}^{G_{1}}\sigma_{1}(x_{1})f_{1})\otimes(ind_{H_{2}}^{G_{2}}\sigma_{2}(x_{2})f_{2})\Big)(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\Big((ind_{H_{1}}^{G_{1}}\sigma_{1}\times ind_{H_{2}}^{G_{2}}\sigma_{2})(x_{1},x_{2})(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})\Big)(y_{1},y_{2}).\qed

6.
Frobenius Reciprocity

In this section, we prove the Frobenius reciprocity theorem on compact transitive groupoids. Suppose
G
is a compact transitive groupoid and
H
a closed subgroupoid with a normalised haarsystem
\{\lambda_{H}^{u}\}_{u\in G^{0}}
, the existence of a full equivariant system of measure
\{\mu^{u}\}_{u\in G^{0}}
on
G/H
is guaranteed by
[
27
, Proposition
2.5
]
. Note that the family of measures
\{\lambda^{u}\}_{u\in G^{0}}
form a haar system on
G
where
\lambda^{u}
is defined as in
(1)
.
Since
G
is compact transitive groupoid, by
[
28
, Proposition
3.18
]
and
[
29
, Lemma
1.3
]
, a family of haar measures
\{\beta^{u}_{u}\}_{u\in G^{0}}
form a haar system on the isotropy subgroupoid
G^{\prime}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
G
be a transitive compact groupoid,
H
a closed transitive wide subgroupoid,
\pi
an irreducible representation of
G
, and
\sigma
an irreducible representation of
H
. Then,

Mor(\pi,\left(ind_{H}^{G}(\sigma),\mu\right))\cong Mor(\pi_{|_{H}},\sigma).

Proof.

By compactness of each isotropy groups,
\beta^{\prime u}_{v}=\beta^{u}_{u}x=x\beta^{v}_{v}=\beta^{u}_{v},x\in G^{u}_{v}
. Suppose
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}
,
u\in G^{0}
, we can see that
\int_{G}\langle f(x),f(x)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{d(x)}}d\lambda^{u}(x)<\infty
and by
[
30
, Proposition
6.15
]
,
f_{|_{G^{u}_{v}}}\in L^{2}(G^{u}_{v},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{v},\beta^{\prime u}_{v})
for some
v\in G^{0}
. Now, for
h\in H^{u}_{v}

\displaystyle\int\|f(x)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{v}}d\beta^{\prime u}_{v}(x)=\int\|f(xh)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{v}}d\beta^{u}_{u}(x)=\int\|f(x)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{u}}d\beta^{u}_{u}(x).

So,
f_{|_{G^{u}_{u}}}\in L^{2}(G^{u}_{u},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{u},\beta^{u}_{u})
.

By
[
28
, Lemma
4.10
]
, we can say that
\pi_{|_{G^{u}_{u}}}
is irreducible for every
u\in G^{0}
. Let
\mathcal{E}^{\bar{\pi}}_{u,u}
denotes the linear span of matrix elements of
\bar{\pi}
on
G^{u}_{u}
. Now, suppose
T\in Mor(\pi,\left(ind_{H}^{G}(\sigma),\mu\right))
, then by Peter-Weyl theorem, for
v\in\mathcal{H}^{\pi}_{u}
,
T_{u}v_{|_{G^{u}_{u}}}
is contained in the
d_{\sigma}^{u}
copies of
\mathcal{E}^{\bar{\pi}}_{u,u}
, which are continuous on
G^{u}_{u}
, where
d_{\sigma}^{u}
is the dimension of
\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{u}
. Thus, it makes sense to evaluate
T_{u}v
pointwise at
G^{u}_{u}
for every
u\in G^{0}
. Also, by transitivity of
H
, we can say
T_{u}v\in C(G^{u},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma})
for every
u\in G^{0}
.

Let
E_{u}:C(G^{u},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma})\to\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}_{u}
be the evaluation map
E_{u}f=f(u)
. Then, let
(ET)_{u}=E_{u}T_{u}
and for
u,w\in G^{0},v\in\mathcal{H}^{\pi}_{u},h\in H\cap G^{w}_{u}

\displaystyle\sigma(h)(ET)_{u}v

\displaystyle=\sigma(h)[T_{u}v(u)]=T_{u}v(uh^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{u}v(h^{-1}w)=[ind_{H}^{G}\sigma(h)T_{u}v](w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[T_{w}\pi(h)v](w)=(ET)_{w}\pi(h)v.

Also, using transitivity of
H
, it is easy to see that
[(ET)\xi](u)=[T_{u}\xi^{u}](u)
is continuous for
\xi\in C_{0}(G^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\pi})
.
So,
ET\in Mor(\pi_{|_{H}},\sigma)
. Now, if
ET=0
, then for any
u,w\in G^{0},x\in G^{w}_{u},v\in\mathcal{H}^{\pi}_{w}
,

0=[T_{u}\pi(x^{-1})v](u)=[ind_{H}^{G}\sigma(x^{-1})T_{w}v](u)=T_{w}v(x),

so
T=0
. Thus,
T\to ET
is injective. Next, we prove that the map is surjective also.

If
S\in Mor(\pi_{|_{H}},\sigma)
, define
T:\mathcal{H}^{\pi}\to\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{\sigma}
as
[T_{u}v](x)=S_{d(x)}[\pi(x^{-1})v]
for every
x\in G^{u},v\in\mathcal{H}^{\pi}_{u},u\in G^{0}
.

\displaystyle[T_{u}v](xh)=S_{d(h)}[\pi(h^{-1})\pi(x^{-1})v]=\sigma(h^{-1})S_{r(h)}[\pi(x^{-1})v]=\sigma(h^{-1})[T_{u}v](x).

Note that,
T_{u}v\in C(G^{u},\mathcal{H}^{\sigma})
. Also
T\xi\in\mathcal{F}^{\sigma}(G)
for
\xi\in C_{0}(G^{0},\mathcal{H}^{\pi})
.

For
y\in G,v\in\mathcal{H}^{\pi}_{d(y)}
,

\displaystyle[ind_{H}^{G}(\sigma)(y)T_{d(y)}v](x)=[T_{d(y)}v](y^{-1}x)=S_{d(x)}[\pi(x^{-1})\pi(y)v]=T_{r(y)}[\pi(y)v](x).

Hence,
T\in Mor(\pi,\left(ind_{H}^{G}(\sigma),\mu\right))
and for
u\in G^{0}
,
(ET)_{u}v=[T_{u}v](u)=S_{u}v
. So,
S=ET
. ∎

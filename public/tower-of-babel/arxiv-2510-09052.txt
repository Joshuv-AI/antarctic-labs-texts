On the Proof of the Genčev-Rucki Conjecture for Multiple Apéry-Like Series
Ce Xu

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, we employ the theories and techniques of hypergeometric functions to provide two distinct proofs of the conjectured identities involving multiple Apéry-like series with central binomial coefficients and multiple harmonic star sums, as recently proposed by Genčev and Rucki. Furthermore, we establish several more general identities for multiple Apéry-like series. Furthermore, by utilizing the method of iterated integrals, a class of multiple mixed values can be expressed as combinations of the multiple Apéry-like series identities conjectured by Genčev and Rucki and
\zeta(2,\ldots,2)
, thus allowing explicit formulas for these multiple mixed values to be derived in terms of Riemann zeta values.

Keywords
: Multiple Apéry-like series; Multiple harmonic (star) sums; Central binomial coefficients; Hypergeometric function; Iterated integral.

AMS Subject Classifications (2020):
11M32, 11M99.

Introduction

For integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 1
, a finite sequence
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}:=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is called a
composition
. For a composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, we put

|{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}|:=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r},\quad\dep({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=r,

and call them the weight and the depth of
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
, respectively. If
k_{1}>1
,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
is called
admissible
.
As a convention, we denote by
\{m\}_{r}
the sequence of
m
’s with
r
repetitions.

For a composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and positive integer
n
, the
multiple harmonic sums
and
multiple harmonic star sums
are defined by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}\in\mathbb{Q}\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}\in\mathbb{Q},

(1.1)

respectively. If
n<r
then
{\zeta_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=0
and
{\zeta_{n}}(\emptyset)={\zeta^{\star}_{n}}(\emptyset):=1
. When
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
is admissible, by taking the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
1.1
) we get the
multiple zeta values
(MZVs) and the
multiple zeta star values
, respectively

\displaystyle{\zeta}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\zeta_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})\in\mathbb{R}\quad\text{and}\quad{\zeta^{\star}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\zeta^{\star}_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})\in\mathbb{R}.

When the depth is 1, we recover the classical Riemann zeta values
\zeta(n)
. The concept of multiple zeta values was independently introduced in the early 1990s by Hoffman
[
11
]
and Zagier
[
21
]
. Owing to their profound connections with diverse mathematical and physical fields-including knot theory, algebraic geometry, and theoretical physics-the study of multiple zeta values has attracted sustained and widespread interest among researchers. After more than three decades of development, the subject has generated a substantial body of research. For an authoritative synthesis of results up to 2016, we refer the reader to Zhao’s comprehensive monograph
[
23
]
.

The most fundamental problem concerning multiple zeta values is the study of their relations over the rational number field
\mathbb{Q}
. Euler’s celebrated result
\zeta(2)=\pi^{2}/6
, together with the transcendence of
\pi
, implies the transcendence of
\zeta(2)
. His approach can be naturally extended to show that every Riemann zeta value at even positive integers
\zeta(2n)
is a rational multiple of
\pi^{2n}
. In contrast, although it is widely conjectured that
\zeta(2n+1)
is transcendental for all
n\geq 1
, not a single instance of this has been proven to date. A major breakthrough came in 1978, when Apéry
[
2
]
astonished the mathematical community by proving the irrationality of
\zeta(3)
. His proof relied on a clever and intricate analysis of the series

\zeta(3)=\displaystyle\frac{5}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^{3}\binom{2n}{n}}.

(1.2)

Due to Apéry’s contributions, series whose general term contains central binomial coefficients in either the numerator or denominator are generally referred to as
Apéry-like series
. Furthermore, if the general term of an Apéry-like series contains multiple harmonic (star) sums in the numerator, we refer to it as a
multiple Apéry-like series
. The study of (multiple) Apéry-like series has attracted considerable attention from numerous experts and scholars. For some recent work on the subject, we refer the reader to
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
and the references therein. Recently, while studying explicit formulas for multiple Apéry-like series of the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq 1}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n_{1}}{n_{1}}}{n_{1}4^{n_{1}}}\displaystyle\frac{4^{n_{r}}}{\binom{2n_{r}}{n_{r}}}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{j}^{2}}

where both the largest summation index
n_{1}
and and the smallest index
n_{r}
involve central binomial coefficients, Genčev and Rucki proposed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1
.

(Genčev and Rucki, Conjecture,
[
9
]
)
For an arbitrary
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r>1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{2\}_{r})=2(1-4^{-r})\zeta(2r+1).

(1.3)

In this paper, we employ two distinct hypergeometric function methods to prove this conjecture and further establish explicit evaluations for several other related series.

2
Proof of Conjecture
1.1
via Hypergeometric Series

In this section, we employ transformation formulas for hypergeometric series to prove Conjecture
1.1
, and further establish generalizations of the identity in Conjecture 1.1 to its functional form. We first need to express the series on the left-hand side of (
1.3
) in the form of a hypergeometric series.

We note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{2\}_{r})x^{2r}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(1+\displaystyle\frac{x^{2}}{j^{2}}+\displaystyle\frac{x^{4}}{j^{4}}+\cdots\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(1-\displaystyle\frac{x^{2}}{j^{2}}\right)^{-1}=\displaystyle\frac{(1)_{n}^{2}}{(1-x)_{n}(1+x)_{n}},

(2.4)

where
(x)_{n}:=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)
and
(x)_{0}:=1
. Multiplying (
2.4
) by
\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}
and summing
n
from
1
to
\infty
yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\left\{\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{2\}_{r})\right\}x^{2r}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(1/2)_{n}(1)_{n}}{(1-x)_{n}(1+x)_{n}}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1-x^{2})}{}_{3}F_{2}\left({1,1,3/2\atop 2-x,2+x};1\right),

(2.5)

where the
general hypergeometric series

{}_{p}F_{q}
is defined as

\displaystyle{}_{p}F_{q}\left({a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{p}\atop b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{q}};z\right):=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(a_{1})_{n}(a_{2})_{n}\cdots(a_{p})_{n}}{(b_{1})_{n}(b_{2})_{n}\cdots(b_{q})_{n}}\displaystyle\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

By applying transformation formulas for hypergeometric series, we can derive a closed-form evaluation of the hypergeometric series on the right-hand side of (
2.5
).

Theorem 2.1
.

For
x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{0}\ (\mathbb{Z}_{0}:=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\})
, we have

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left({1,1,3/2\atop 2-x,2+x};1\right)

\displaystyle=4\log(2)(1-x^{2})+2(1-x^{2})(\psi(1-x/2)+\psi(1+x/2))

\displaystyle\quad-2(1-x^{2})(\psi(1-x)+\psi(1+x)),

(2.6)

where
\psi(s)
denotes the digamma function defined by

\displaystyle\psi(s)=-\gamma-\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{s+k}\right),

(2.7)

where
s\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}:=\mathbb{N}^{-}\cup\{0\}=\{0,-1,-2,-3,\ldots\}
. Here
\gamma
denotes the
Euler-Mascheroni constant
.

Proof.

From
[
1
, Eq. (3.1.15)]
, we have

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left({a,b,c\atop a-b+1,a-c+1};z\right)=(1-z)^{-a}{}_{3}F_{2}\left({a-b-c+1,\displaystyle\frac{a}{2},\displaystyle\frac{a+1}{2}\atop a-b+1,a-c+1};-\displaystyle\frac{4z}{(1-z)^{2}}\right).

(2.8)

Setting
a=2,b=1+x,c=1-x
and
z=-1
gives

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left({1,1,3/2\atop 2-x,2+x};1\right)

\displaystyle=4\ {}_{3}F_{2}\left({2,1+x,1-x\atop 2-x,2+x};-1\right)

\displaystyle=4\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(2)_{n}(1+x)_{n}(1-x)_{n}}{(2-x)_{n}(2+x)_{n}}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=4(1-x^{2})\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(n+1)(-1)^{n}}{(n+1-x)(n+1+x)}

\displaystyle=2(1-x^{2})\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1-x}+\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1+x}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-x^{2})\left(\psi\Big(1-\displaystyle\frac{x}{2}\Big)-\psi\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1-x}{2}\Big)+\psi\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{x}{2}\Big)-\psi\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1+x}{2}\Big)\right).

(2.9)

By
multiplication theorem
(see
[
10
, Page 913, 8.365]
)

\displaystyle\psi(nz)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi\Big(z+\displaystyle\frac{k}{n}\Big)+\log 2,

(2.10)

letting
n=2
yields

\displaystyle\psi(z)=2\psi(2z)-\psi\Big(z+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big)-2\log 2.

(2.11)

Hence, letting
z=1\pm x
and add them together, then

\displaystyle\psi\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1+x}{2}\Big)+\psi\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1-x}{2}\Big)=2\Big(\psi(1+x)+\psi(1-x)\Big)-\left(\psi\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{x}{2}\Big)+\psi\Big(1-\displaystyle\frac{x}{2}\Big)\right)-4\log 2.

(2.12)

Substituting it into (
2
) yields the desired evaluation.
∎

We now proceed to prove Conjecture
1.1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{2\}_{r})=2(1-4^{-r})\zeta(2r+1).

(2.13)

Proof.

Applying (
2.1
) and by utilizing the power series expansion of the digamma function at zero

\psi(1+x)+\gamma=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\zeta(n+1)x^{n},

we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1-x^{2})}{}_{3}F_{2}\left({1,1,3/2\atop 2-x,2+x};1\right)=2\log 2+2\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-4^{-n})\zeta(2n+1)x^{2n}.

(2.14)

Finally, by comparing the coefficients of
x^{2r}
on both sides of (
2.4
) and (
2.14
) yields the desired result.
∎

Indeed, by employing similar methods, we can establish the functional forms of equations (
2.1
) and (
2.13
).

Theorem 2.3
.

For
x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{0}
and
t\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left({1,1,3/2\atop 2-x,2+x};1-t\right)=\displaystyle\frac{2(1-x^{2})}{1-t}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{t}-1}{\sqrt{t}+1}\right)^{n}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{x-n}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{x+n}\right).

(2.15)

Proof.

The theorem follows immediately from (
2.8
) if we set
a=2,b=1+x,c=1-x
and
z=\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{t}-1}{\sqrt{t}+1}
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{2\}_{r})(1-z)^{n}=-2\Li_{2r+1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{z}-1}{\sqrt{z}+1}\right),

(2.16)

where the
polylogarithm function

\Li_{p}(x)
is defined by

\displaystyle\Li_{p}(x):=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n^{p}}\quad(x\in[-1,1],\ (p,x)\neq(1,1),\ p\in\mathbb{N}).

(2.17)

Proof.

By an elementary calculation, we find that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\left\{\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{2\}_{r})(1-z)^{n}\right\}x^{2r}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(1/2)_{n}(1)_{n}}{(1-x)_{n}(1+x)_{n}}\displaystyle\frac{(1-z)^{n}}{n}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1-z}{2(1-x^{2})}{}_{3}F_{2}\left({1,1,3/2\atop 2-x,2+x};1-z\right).

(2.18)

Noting that from (
2.15
) we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1-z}{2(1-x^{2})}{}_{3}F_{2}\left({1,1,3/2\atop 2-x,2+x};1-z\right)=-2\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\Li_{2r+1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{z}-1}{\sqrt{z}+1}\right)x^{2r}.

(2.19)

Hence, comparing the the coefficients of
x^{2r}
on both sides of above yields the desired evaluation.
∎

Clearly, Conjecture
1.1
is obtained by setting
z=0
in Theorem
2.4
.

3
Closed Forms of A Family of Multiple Mixed Values

In this section, we employ the method of iterated integrals, combined with the results from the previous section, to derive closed-form formulas expressing a class of multiple mixed values involving alternating harmonic numbers and multiple harmonic sums in terms of Riemann zeta values.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{{\bar{H}}_{2n}\zeta_{n-1}(\{2\}_{r-1})}{n^{2}}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}(1-4^{-j})\zeta(\{2\}_{r-j})\zeta(2j+1),

(3.20)

where the alternating harmonic number
{\bar{H}}_{2n}
is defined by
{\bar{H}}_{2n}:=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}
. From
[
23
, Cor. 5.6.4]
, we have

\displaystyle\zeta(\{2\}_{r})=\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2r}}{(2r+1)!}\quad(r\in\mathbb{N}).

Proof.

From
[
20
, Theorem 2.1]
, replacing
p
by
r
and setting
x=1
and all
m_{j}=2
, we have

\displaystyle n\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{n-1}dt\left\{\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right\}^{r}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}(-1)^{j+1}\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\{2\}_{j-1})\zeta(\{2\}_{r-j+1})

(3.21)

where the iterated integral is defined in the following form

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f_{1}(t)dtf_{2}(t)dt\cdots f_{p}(t)dt:=\displaystyle\int\limits_{a<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{p}<b}f_{p}(t_{p})f_{p-1}(t_{p-1})\cdots f_{1}(t_{1})dt_{1}dt_{2}\cdots dt_{p}.

Multiplying (
3.21
) by
\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}
and summing
n
from
1
to
\infty
and noting the fact that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^{n}}t^{n}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t}}\quad(t\in[-1,1)),

we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}(-1)^{j+1}\zeta(\{2\}_{r-j+1})\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\{2\}_{j-1})=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t}}-1\Big)dt\right\}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right\}^{r}.

(3.22)

Applying
t\mapsto 1-t
yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}(-1)^{j+1}\zeta(\{2\}_{r-j+1})\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\{2\}_{j-1})=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right\}^{r}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-t}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}-1\Big)dt\right\}.

(3.23)

Then letting
t\mapsto t^{2}
gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}(-1)^{j+1}\zeta(\{2\}_{r-j+1})\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\{2\}_{j-1})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{2tdt}{1-t^{2}}\displaystyle\frac{2tdt}{t^{2}}\right\}^{r}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-t^{2}}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}-1\Big)2tdt\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\displaystyle\frac{2tdt}{1-t^{2}}\displaystyle\frac{2dt}{t}\right\}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{2dt}{1+t}

\displaystyle=2\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\frac{\Li_{\{2\}_{r}}(t^{2})}{1+t}dt,

(3.24)

where the classical
multiple polylogarithm
(MPL) is defined by

\displaystyle\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x)

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\dotsm n_{r}^{k_{r}}}\quad(\forall k_{j}\in\mathbb{N},\ (k_{1},x)\neq(1,1))

(3.25)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t}\right)\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\cdots\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t}\right)\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}.

Noting the fact that (see
[
6
]
)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}t^{n}=2\log\Big(\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-t}}\Big)\quad(t\in[-1,1)),

(3.26)

and letting
t=1
yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}=2\log(2).

(3.27)

Substituting it into (

) gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\zeta(\{2\}_{r-j})\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n4^{n}}\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\{2\}_{j})=2\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\frac{\Li_{\{2\}_{r}}(t^{2})}{1+t}dt-2\zeta(\{2\}_{r})\log 2

\displaystyle=2\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{2}\cdots n_{r}^{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{2j-2}(t-1)dt

\displaystyle=2\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{2}\cdots n_{r}^{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2j}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2j-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=2\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{2}\cdots n_{r}^{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2n_{1}}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}.

(3.28)

Combining (
3
) with (
2.13
) yields (
3.20
).
∎

Note that
{\bar{H}}_{2n}=\displaystyle\frac{H_{n}-t_{n}}{2}
, where

and

stand for the

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{t_{n}\zeta_{n-1}(\{2\}_{r-1})}{n^{2}}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{H_{n}\zeta_{n-1}(\{2\}_{r-1})}{n^{2}}-2\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}(1-4^{-j})\zeta(\{2\}_{r-j})\zeta(2j+1).

From

\displaystyle\xi(2;\{2\}_{r})=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})H_{n}}{n^{k_{1}+1}},

4
Another Proof of Hypergeometric Series
{}_{3}F_{2}\left({1,1,3/2\atop 2-x,2+x};1\right)

In this section, we present an alternative proof of equation (
2.1
) using the method of partial fraction decomposition, and furthermore establish several formulas for parametric multiple Apéry-like series.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\bar{\mathbb{Z}}:=\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0,1\}
. For
x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{0}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\bar{\mathbb{Z}}
with
a+b\notin\{3,4,5,\ldots\}
,

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left({1,a,b\atop 2-x,2+x};1\right)=(x^{2}-1)\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty,\atop k\neq 0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{x+k}\displaystyle\frac{(a)_{|k|-1}(b)_{|k|-1}{\rm sign}(k)|k|\Gamma(3-a-b)}{\Gamma(2+|k|-a)\Gamma(2+|k|-b)}.

(4.31)

Proof.

From the definition of the hypergeometric series
{}_{3}F_{2}
, it is straightforward to obtain

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left({1,a,b\atop 2-x,2+x};1\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(1)_{n}(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(2+x)_{n}(2-x)_{n}}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(x-1-n)_{2n+3}}(-1)^{n}x(x^{2}-1).

Using partial fraction expansion, there are

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{x}{(x-1-n)_{2n+3}}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=-1-n}^{n+1}(-1)^{n+k}\displaystyle\frac{k}{(n+k+1)!(n-k+1)!}\displaystyle\frac{1}{x+k}.

Comparison of pharmacokinetics and safety of fixed-dose versus loose combination of atorvastatin/fenofibrate 10/145 mg in healthy Korean participants
Authors: Hyungji Jang, Juyoung Khwarg, Ki Young Huh, Dong-Min Park, Heejung Park, Kyung-Sang Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDyslipidemia is a risk factor for cardiovascular disease. Atorvastatin and fenofibrate are co-administered to treat mixed dyslipidemia, a subtype requiring long-term control of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol and triglycerides; fixed-dose combinations (FDCs) could improve adherence. This study compared the pharmacokinetics and safety of an FDC formulation of atorvastatin/fenofibrate 10/145 mg with the corresponding loose combination in healthy Korean participants. Given that atorvastatin is a highly variable drug, a randomized, two-sequence, four-period replicated crossover study was conducted. Plasma concentrations of atorvastatin and 2-hydroxy (2-OH) atorvastatin were measured for 48 h in all periods, and fenofibric acid for 72 hours in the first two periods. Pharmacokinetic parameters were calculated using noncompartmental analysis. Log-transformed maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) and area under the plasma concentration–time curve to the last time point (AUClast) were analyzed using a linear mixed-effects model to obtain geometric mean ratios (GMRs) and 90% confidence intervals (CIs). Using the reference-scaled average bioequivalence approach, the acceptance limits for atorvastatin Cmax were widened based on the intrasubject variability. Of 40 randomized participants, 36 completed the study. The GMRs and 90% CIs for Cmax and AUClast were, respectively, 1.0595 (0.9565–1.1735) and 0.9530 (0.9045–1.0041) for atorvastatin; 0.9459 (0.8903–1.0243) and 0.9560 (0.9151–0.9986) for 2-OH atorvastatin; and 0.9589 (0.9020–1.0193) and 0.9861 (0.9585–1.0146) for fenofibric acid. In conclusion, the FDC of atorvastatin/fenofibrate 10/145 mg demonstrated comparable pharmacokinetics to the loose combination. The findings support its use as an alternative to improve medication adherence in patients with mixed dyslipidemia requiring dual lipid-modifying therapy.Trial RegistrationClinical Research Information Service Identifier: KCT0010666Keywords: Cross-Over Studies, Pharmacokinetics, Dyslipidemias, Hydroxymethylglutaryl-CoA Reductase Inhibitors, Randomized Controlled Trial

INTRODUCTION

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading cause of mortality worldwide, accounting for approximately 19.8 million deaths in 2022 (32% of global deaths) [1]. Among the modifiable risk factors, dyslipidemia plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis and its vascular complications. Clinical evidence supports that comprehensive lipid-lowering therapy can substantially reduce the risk of coronary heart disease, myocardial infarction, and stroke [2,3].

Mixed dyslipidemia is defined by concurrent elevations in low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) and triglycerides with reduced high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) [4]. This lipid profile represents a distinct atherogenic phenotype driven by the accumulation of triglyceride-rich remnants and is considered a major risk factor for CVD [5,6]. Consequently, effective management of this risk factor is a cornerstone of CVD prevention, and treatment strategies that simultaneously target both LDL-C and triglycerides are essential [5,6]. Accordingly, long-term combination therapy capable of dual lipid modulation, such as the co-administration of a statin and fenofibrate, is often required to achieve optimal lipid control in patients with mixed dyslipidemia [7,8,9].

Indeed, statin monotherapy, a first-line therapy for dyslipidemia, often fails to adequately control mixed dyslipidemia. For example, atorvastatin, one of the most widely prescribed statins, achieves LDL-C reductions of approximately 37%–55% [10] but shows limited efficacy in controlling triglycerides [11]. Therefore, combination therapy with fibrates is recommended to achieve comprehensive lipid control beyond statin monotherapy [12]. In particular, fenofibrate has shown synergistic effects with statins by lowering triglycerides and modestly raising HDL-C, thereby complementing the LDL-C–lowering effect of statins [13].

In clinical practice, however, poor adherence to multidrug regimens remains a key barrier to achieving optimal lipid control, with nonadherence associated with higher CVD risks [14,15,16]. Maintaining strict adherence is particularly challenging during the long-term treatment required for mixed dyslipidemia, where the pill burden can discourage persistence and subsequently compromise therapeutic efficacy [17,18,19]. To address this challenge, fixed-dose combinations (FDCs) have been developed to simplify treatment regimens, thereby enhancing patient compliance and ensuring consistent clinical outcomes [17].

In this context, an FDC tablet of atorvastatin/fenofibrate 10 mg/145 mg has been developed. This study aims to compare the pharmacokinetics and safety of this FDC tablet with the loose combination of the same components. Considering that atorvastatin undergoes extensive metabolism to form the active metabolite 2-hydroxy (2-OH) atorvastatin, pharmacokinetic assessments for 2-OH atorvastatin were also performed. In addition, given that atorvastatin is a highly variable drug, a replicated crossover design was adopted [20].

METHODS

Ethics and regulatory compliance

This study was registered in the Clinical Research Information Service (registration No. KCT0010666) and received approval from both the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety and the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of H Plus Yangji Hospital (IRB No. HYJ 2022-10-021). The trial was conducted between January 2 and March 11, 2023, in accordance with the Korean Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study population

Participants were healthy adults aged ≥ 19 years with an average body weight of ≥ 50 kg for males (≥ 45 kg for females) and a body mass index between 18 and 30 kg/m2. Exclusion criteria included known hypersensitivity to atorvastatin, fenofibrate, or related compounds; a history of myopathy or rhabdomyolysis; clinically significant hepatic or gallbladder disease; or elevated liver enzymes, defined as alanine aminotransferase or aspartate aminotransferase greater than three times the upper limit of normal. Participants were also excluded if they had recently used contraindicated medications, such as gemfibrozil or pitavastatin. Additionally, any other condition that the investigator considered inappropriate for participation was grounds for exclusion. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before any study-related procedure and prior to enrollment.

Study design

This study was a randomized, two-sequence, four-period replicated crossover study (Fig. 1). Participants were randomly assigned to one of two sequences (RTRT or TRTR, where T represented the test drug and R represented the reference drug). The test drug was a single FDC tablet of atorvastatin/fenofibrate 10/145 mg, whereas the reference drug was the loose combination of atorvastatin 10 mg and fenofibrate 145 mg. Each treatment period was separated by a washout period of at least 14 days.

Blood sampling for pharmacokinetic assessment

Pharmacokinetic evaluation was based on serial blood sampling following each administration. For atorvastatin and its active metabolite, 2-OH atorvastatin, pharmacokinetic blood samples were collected at 0 hours (pre-dose) and 0.17, 0.33, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24, and 48 hours post dose throughout Periods 1 to 4. For fenofibric acid, blood samples were collected at 0 hours (pre-dose) and 0.5, 1, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5, 5, 6, 8, 12, 24, 48, and 72 hours post dose in Periods 1 and 2.

Determination of plasma concentrations

Plasma concentrations of atorvastatin and its active metabolite, 2-OH atorvastatin, and fenofibric acid were determined using a validated ultra-performance liquid chromatography coupled with tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC–MS/MS) method. Blood samples were collected into K2-EDTA tubes, immediately centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C, and the plasma was separated and stored at or below −70°C until analysis.

For atorvastatin and 2-OH atorvastatin, 100 µL of plasma was subjected to protein precipitation with acetonitrile, following the addition of atorvastatin-d5 sodium salt and 2-OH atorvastatin-d5 sodium salt as internal standards. Fenofibric acid in plasma was extracted using the same protein precipitation method, following the addition of fenofibric acid-d6 as the internal standard.

After centrifugation, the supernatant was diluted with the mobile phase, and 5 µL was injected into the analytical system. For atorvastatin and 2-OH atorvastatin, chromatographic separation was performed using a Waters ACQUITY Premier UPLC system (Waters Corp., Milford, MA, USA) equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC® BEH C18 column (1.7 µm, 2.1 × 50 mm) and an ACQUITY UPLC™ column in-line filter, maintained at 30 ± 5°C. The mobile phases consisted of (A) 0.1% (w/v) ammonium acetate in distilled water and (B) acetonitrile. Gradient elution was used as follows: 0.0–3.0 minutes, 65% A/35% B at 0.4 mL/minutes; 3.1–5.5 minutes, 10% A/90% B at 0.6 mL/minutes; and 5.6–6.0minutes, 65% A/35% B at 0.4 mL/minutes.

For fenofibric acid, chromatographic separation was performed using a Waters ACQUITY UPLC system (Waters Corp.) equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC® BEH C18 column (1.7 µm, 2.1 × 50 mm) and an ACQUITY UPLC™ column in-line filter, maintained at 30 ± 5°C. The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% (v/v) formic acid in distilled water and 0.1% (v/v) formic acid in acetonitrile. Isocratic elution was performed with mobile phase A/B = 45:55 (v/v) at a flow rate of 0.3 mL/minutes for 3.0 minutes.

All samples were maintained at 5 ± 3°C in the autosampler prior to analysis. Mass spectrometric detection was performed using a Xevo™ TQ-XS triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (Waters Corp.) equipped with an electrospray ionization source operated in the positive ion mode with multiple reaction monitoring. The monitored transitions were m/z 559.34 → 440.20 for atorvastatin, 575.30 → 440.20 for 2-OH atorvastatin, 564.34 → 445.20 for atorvastatin-d5, 580.30 → 445.20 for 2-OH atorvastatin-d5, 319.10 → 231.00 for fenofibric acid, and 325.10 → 237.00 for fenofibric acid-d6.

Calibration curves showed linearity over the ranges of 0.05–100 ng/mL for atorvastatin and 2-OH atorvastatin and 100–100,000 ng/mL for fenofibric acid, with correlation coefficients (r2) ≥ 0.998. The lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) was 0.05 ng/mL for atorvastatin and 2-OH atorvastatin and 100 ng/mL for fenofibric acid, whereas the upper limit of quantification was 100 ng/mL and 100,000 ng/mL, respectively.

During method validation, the accuracy ranged from 93% to 99%, and the precision—expressed as relative standard deviation (%RSD)—ranged from 1.1% to 2.6%. All validation parameters met the predefined acceptance criteria: the accuracy (deviation from the nominal value) was within ± 15% (± 20% at the LLOQ), and the within-run and between-run %RSD values were ≤ 15% (≤ 20% at the LLOQ). On the basis of these results, the bioanalytical methods used in this study were validated as suitable for the determination of atorvastatin, 2-OH atorvastatin, and fenofibric acid concentrations in human plasma.

Pharmacokinetic analysis

Plasma concentration–time data of atorvastatin, 2-OH atorvastatin, and fenofibric acid were analyzed using noncompartmental analysis with Phoenix® WinNonlin® version 8.5 (Certara USA Inc., Princeton, NJ, USA). The maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) and time to reach maximum plasma concentration were obtained directly from the observed profiles. The area under the plasma concentration–time curve from time zero to the last measurable time point (AUClast) was calculated using the linear-up/log-down trapezoidal rule. The area under the plasma concentration–time curve extrapolated to infinity (AUCinf) was calculated as AUClast + Clast/λz, where Clast is the last measurable concentration and λz is the terminal elimination rate constant estimated by linear regression of the log-transformed concentrations during the terminal elimination phase. The terminal half-life (t1/2) was calculated as ln (2)/λz. The apparent clearance (CL/F) was calculated as the dose divided by AUCinf, and the apparent volume of distribution during the terminal phase was determined by dividing CL/F by λz. The percentage of AUC extrapolated was calculated as (AUCinf − AUClast)/AUCinf × 100.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SAS software (version 9.4; SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). Log-transformed pharmacokinetic parameters were analyzed using a linear mixed-effects model including treatment, period, and sequence as fixed effects and participants nested within sequence as a random effect. From this model, geometric least squares means, geometric mean ratios (GMRs), and the corresponding 90% confidence intervals (CIs) for AUClast and Cmax were obtained to compare the FDC with the loose combination. where σ2wR represents intrasubject residual variance of the reference treatment [21,22,23,24,25,26].

Given that atorvastatin is a highly variable drug, the reference-scaled average bioequivalence (RSABE) method was applied for Cmax [20]. In this approach, the intrasubject standard deviation of the reference treatment on the log scale (SWR) was estimated from the mixed-effects model. When SWR was ≥ 0.294, corresponding to an intrasubject CV of ≥ 30%, the acceptance limits for the test/reference GMR of Cmax were scaled according to SWR in accordance with regulatory guidance [21,23,24,25]. Specifically, the scaled limits on the log scale were calculated as ± k SWR, where k was the regulatory constant set to 0.760 (i.e., k = ln1.250.294). Pharmacokinetic comparability for Cmax was considered acceptable when the 90% CI for the test/reference GMR lay entirely within these scaled limits, and the point estimate remained within the conventional range of 0.80–1.25.

Safety and tolerability assessment

All adverse events occurring after the administration of the investigational product were classified by treatment group and coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA® version 25.1) by system organ class and preferred term. Safety and tolerability were assessed on the basis of adverse events; concomitant medications; physical examinations; vital signs (e.g., blood pressure, pulse rate, and body temperature); 12-lead ECGs; and clinical laboratory tests including hematology, blood chemistry, serology, and urinalysis.

RESULTS

Study population and demographics

A total of 40 participants were enrolled and randomized to one of the two treatment sequences in a 1:1 ratio. In total, 38 participants completed the study through Period 2 (pharmacokinetic sampling for fenofibric acid), and 36 participants completed the study through Period 4 (pharmacokinetic sampling for atorvastatin and 2-OH atorvastatin; Supplementary Fig. 1). Baseline demographic characteristics were comparable between sequences (Supplementary Table 1).

Pharmacokinetics

The plasma concentration–time profiles and pharmacokinetic parameters of atorvastatin, 2-OH atorvastatin, and fenofibric acid were comparable between the FDC and loose combination groups (Fig. 2). All analytes were generally rapidly absorbed, atorvastatin reached peak concentration first, followed by 2-OH atorvastatin and fenofibric acid. Following this rapid absorption, atorvastatin plasma concentrations declined quickly with a relatively short terminal half-life, whereas 2-OH atorvastatin exhibited a slower elimination profile compared to the parent drug. Fenofibric acid demonstrated the most prolonged elimination phase, characterized by the longest terminal half-life among the three analytes (Fig. 2, Table 1). Fig. 3 shows the comparison of individual systemic exposure (AUClast and Cmax) between the two formulations. For most participants, between-formulation differences were small, and systemic exposure was generally comparable. The descriptive statistics of pharmacokinetic parameters are summarized in Table 1.

The GMRs and corresponding 90% CIs for the FDC versus loose combination are summarized in Table 2. For atorvastatin Cmax, the GMR was 1.0595 with a 90% CI of 0.9565–1.1735, which were within the scaled acceptance limits. For atorvastatin AUClast, the GMR (90% CI) values were 0.9530 (0.9045–1.0041). For 2-OH atorvastatin, the values were 0.9459 (0.8903–1.0243) for Cmax and 0.9560 (0.9151–0.9986) for AUClast. For fenofibric acid, the values were 0.9589 (0.9020–1.0193) for Cmax and 0.9861 (0.9585–1.0146) for AUClast. Thus, the 90% CIs for all parameters were within the limits for pharmacokinetic comparability.

Safety and tolerability

Overall, both regimens were well tolerated, with no clinically significant between-group differences in overall safety. A total of eight treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) occurred in six participants (15.0%). These events were infrequent, of mild or moderate intensity, and resolved without clinically significant sequelae. No serious adverse events or severe events were reported. Clinical laboratory tests (hematology, clinical chemistry, urinalysis), vital signs, and electrocardiograms were within normal limits or not clinically significant, indicating no formulation-related safety signal.

By severity, five participants (6 events, 12.5%) experienced mild TEAEs, and one participant (2 events, 2.5%) experienced a moderate TEAE. Two participants in the FDC group discontinued study treatment because of TEAEs: alanine aminotransferase elevation and coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) infection. Of these, only the alanine aminotransferase elevation was considered related to the study drug (adverse drug reaction), whereas the COVID-19 infection was assessed as unlikely related.

TEAE incidence was comparable between treatment groups, with four events in three participants (7.5%) in the loose combination group and four events in three participants (7.9%) in the FDC group. Elevations of alanine aminotransferase and gamma-glutamyl transferase, decrease in hemoglobin, and COVID-19 were reported in the FDC group. By contrast, increase in lipase and white blood cell counts; limb abscess; and furuncles were observed only in the loose combination group.

DISCUSSION

The pharmacokinetic profiles of atorvastatin, 2-OH atorvastatin, and fenofibric acid were comparable between the FDC and the loose combination groups, with GMRs for Cmax and AUClast meeting the standard regulatory equivalence criteria for all three analytes. The dose of atorvastatin 10 mg combined with fenofibrate 145 mg was selected for this study on the basis of several considerations. Asian populations generally exhibit higher systemic statin exposure than Western populations, making lower doses clinically appropriate. Furthermore, co-administration of atorvastatin with fenofibrate carries an increased risk of myopathy, which is dose dependent for HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors. Real-world prescription data in Korea also indicate that high-dose atorvastatin use is uncommon. While the Food and Drug Administration guidance recommends using the highest available strength for bioequivalence studies of combination drug products, the 10 mg/145 mg dose was selected on the basis of the above considerations, with the higher strength (20 mg/145 mg) addressed in a separate ongoing phase III clinical trial [27].

The sample size was determined on the basis of previously reported intrasubject variability data for atorvastatin and fenofibrate pharmacokinetic parameters (Cmax CV%, 31.6%–42.2%; AUClast CV%, 12.9%–14.64% for atorvastatin; Cmax CV%, 11.0%, AUClast CV%, 7.9% for fenofibrate) [20,28,29]. Using the most conservative estimate (CV% = 42.2%), a T/R ratio of 0.90, a significance level of 0.05, and 80% power, it was determined that 30 participants were required. Accounting for an anticipated dropout rate of 20%, 40 participants (20 per sequence) were enrolled in this two-sequence, four-period replicate crossover design. The observed intrasubject CV for atorvastatin Cmax in this study was higher than the assumed value; however, given the replicate design and the number of completers, the study retained sufficient power to evaluate bioequivalence using the reference-scaled approach.

Although Cmax of atorvastatin exhibited high intrasubject variability (CVwR > 30%)—leading to its classification as a highly variable drug—bioequivalence was demonstrated using the reference-scaled approach, indicating comparable systemic exposure between the two formulations. The intrasubject CV for AUClast was < 30% for all analytes, and standard acceptance criteria were met, indicating that regulatory classification differences across agencies did not affect the overall interpretation of pharmacokinetic comparability [21].

In summary, systemic exposures to atorvastatin, 2-OH atorvastatin, and fenofibric acid were all within a comparable range between the FDC and loose combination regimens. In combination with previous data showing no clinically meaningful pharmacokinetic drug-drug interaction between atorvastatin and fenofibrate in healthy volunteers [16], these findings strengthen the pharmacokinetic rationale for formulating these agents as an FDC. This FDC could offer potential benefits in real-world clinical practice, particularly in patients with mixed dyslipidemia who require long-term dual lipid-modifying therapy and receive multiple concomitant cardiometabolic medications. Because these patients typically have a high pill burden, prescribing an FDC can reduce the number of tablets they need to take and simplify dosing regimens [9]. This simplification may lessen regimen complexity and long-term therapy more manageable in routine clinical practice. In the context of lowering lipid levels, real-world studies have reported that single-pill combinations are associated with higher adherence and persistence and better attainment of lipid targets compared with corresponding loose combinations prescribed at equivalent doses [23,30].

The FDC of atorvastatin/fenofibrate 10/145 mg demonstrated comparable pharmacokinetics to the corresponding loose combination, and both treatments were safe and well tolerated. These findings suggest that the FDC may offer a more convenient therapeutic option for patients requiring dual lipid-modifying therapy.

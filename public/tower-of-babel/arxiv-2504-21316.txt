Reduced order asymptotic observer of friction in motion control
Michael Ruderman

Abstract
An asymptotic observer of the motion state variables with nonlinear friction [ 1 ] benefits from a robust to the noise state-space representation of the dynamic friction force, including pre-sliding transitions, and implements the reduced-order Luenberger observation law with only measurable output displacement. The uniform asymptotic stability and convergence analysis of the proposed observer are elaborated by using the Lyapunov function-based stability criteria by Ignatyev and imposing the parametric constraints on the time dependent eigenvalues to be always negative real. A design procedure for assigning a dominant (thus slowest) real pole of the observer system matrix is proposed. A thorough experimental evaluation is given for the proposed observer-based friction compensation, which is performed for positioning and tracking tasks and compared with an optimally tuned PID feedback control.



I
Introduction

Often a relative output displacement is the only measured quantity while high-precision motion is required. The classical Luenberger-type observers
[
2
]
require a linear state-space realization of the system dynamics, while a nonlinear observer design problem is often realized via a system of singular first-order PDEs
[
3
]
and requires an appropriate nonlinear coordinates transformation for the state variables and respective flows. Restrictive conditions on the flow maps and state transformations can prevent application of standard observer design methodologies (see e.g.
[
4
]
for survey) to such physical effects as nonlinear kinetic friction. An inherent problem with friction, see e.g.
[
5
]
, is that its disturbing nature is often less pronounced in the temporal than in the spatial domain, especially at the motion onset, stop, and reversals. This fact restricts also the use of the so-called disturbance observers (DOBs) (see
[
6
]
for analysis of the robust stability in the closed-loop), since the friction force dynamics
[
7
]
makes the related consideration in Laplace domain less applicable. Nevertheless, the design of friction observers is often essential, since the friction forces are hardly measurable, see e.g.
[
8
]
, and at the same time cannot be effectively counteracted by standard feedback control techniques with integral action, see e.g.
[
9
]
.

Several works to be mentioned here were addressing observation of the friction in the past. In the earlier work
[
10
]
, the authors summarized modeling and observer based compensation of the dynamic friction, while using the co-called LuGre friction model
[
11
]
. However, the proposed observer requires the relative velocity to be the measurable quantity feeded back. Moreover, the LuGre model turned out (over time) to be sensitive to implementation instabilities and restrictive as for the class of admissible input signals, i.e. not allowing for non-continuous transitions and ’too high’ velocity rates, see
[
12
]
. Similar issues with the use of the LuGre friction model when constructing a passivity-based friction observer for compensation in the control loop were also addressed in
[
13
]
. A friction compensation approach by using a reduced-order state observer was also proposed in
[
14
]
. The approach relies on the estimation of relative velocity by means of a reduced-order asymptotic observer, which is then used for the modeled set-valued dry friction. This scheme does not involve, however, any dynamic state of the pre-sliding friction and, this way, does not also observe the state of a friction force but relies rather on its model-based prediction. An observer which incorporates the friction dynamics into the state equations and uses a Luenberger type estimation scheme was proposed in
[
15
]
while also requiring the measured relative velocity. The approach showed remarkable performance in compensating the nonlinear friction and brought the position control error to the level of the sensor-encoder in experiments. An advanced version of this observation scheme, which uses only the measured output displacement and improves robustness in both the pre-sliding and gross sliding regimes, was presented in
[
1
]
, however without control evaluation. This latter study represents the starting point of the present work.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
II
describes the class of the motion systems, including the dynamic states and pre-sliding regime of the kinetic friction. The main results are given in section
III
, where the system dynamics with friction is first written in an observable state-space and then transformed into the regular form. Based thereupon, a reduced-order Luenberger type observer is designed and its convergence analysis is established for the time-varying observer system matrix, also giving the parametric conditions for monotonic and robust convergence. A dedicated experimental case study is provided in section
IV
. Brief concluding remarks are given in section
V
.

II
Motion system with nonlinear friction

Without loss of generality, consider the following class of the dynamic motion systems

m\ddot{x}(t)+f\bigl(\dot{x}(t)\bigl)=u(t)-g(t),

(1)

where
m
is the overall system inertia (i.e. lumped mass in case of a translational motion),
f(\cdot)
is the weakly known nonlinear kinetic friction, and
u
is a driving force available for the control. Note that an external and, at the same time, matched load force
g(\cdot)
is known and, thus, can be pre-compensated, correspondingly subtracted from
u
in case of observer design. Our goal is first to analyze the dynamic friction behavior in a way that allows to construct a robust asymptotic observer
\tilde{f}(t)\rightarrow f(t)
that uses only the measured
x(t)
and enables to compensate for the friction quantity
f(\cdot)
when
x(t)\rightarrow x_{\textmd{ref}}(t)
is under a feedback control.

The overall friction force at steady-state is a superposition of the Coulomb and viscous friction forces

f(t)=F_{c}(t)+F_{v}(t),

(2)

in line with several established approaches for modeling the kinetic friction
with lumped parameters, cf. e.g.
[
7
]
. Important to notice is that the superposition can be temporary lost during the fast dynamic transients, especially at higher accelerations. Further basic assumptions imposed on the nonlinear kinetic friction can be found in
[
1
,
5
]
.

II-A

Dynamic friction states

For augmenting the steady-state friction states in (
2
) by the appropriate dynamic behavior, consider (separately) the transient equations of the Coulomb and viscous friction force components, i.e.
\dot{F}_{c}
and
\dot{F}_{v}
respectively.

The nonlinear Coulomb friction undergoes
pre-sliding
transitions before saturating at
\pm C_{f}
. This leads to

\dot{F}_{c}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{x}\cdot\partial F_{c}/\partial x\,,&\hbox{if }|F_{c}|<C_{f},\\[2.84526pt]
0,&\hbox{otherwise,}\\
\end{array}\right.

(3)

cf.
[
16
,
17
]
. Note that
\partial F_{c}/\partial x
is at least once continuously differentiable for
t>t_{r}
upon each motion reversal, i.e. when
\textrm{sign}\bigl(\dot{x}(t_{r})\bigr)\neq\textrm{sign}\bigl(\dot{x}(t_{r}^{+})\bigr)
where
t_{r}
is the time instant of a motion reversal and
t_{r}<t_{r}^{+}
while
(t_{r}^{+}-t_{r})\rightarrow 0
. Since (
3
) is generic for describing the pre-sliding friction curves in the coordinates of relative displacement and force, a particular form of the underlying modeling of the friction force
F_{c}(t)
in pre-sliding becomes secondary. For more tribological details on the characteristic frictional behavior during the pre-sliding we refer to e.g.
[
18
,
19
]
. At the same time, only such modeling approach that has an unique feature
\partial F_{c}(t_{r}^{+})/\partial x\rightarrow\infty
, cf.
[
18
]
can guarantee for the motion stop of an autonomous system (
1
) with
g,u=0
in
finite time
, see
[
17
]
for details.

For transient dynamics of the viscous friction term, one can assume the first-order linear behavior

\dot{F}_{v}=\beta^{-1}(\sigma\dot{x}-F_{v}).

(4)

The corresponding time constant is
\beta>0
, while at steady-state (
4
) reduces to
F_{v}=\sigma\dot{x}
. The dynamic behavior (
4
) captures the so-called frictional lag, see e.g.
[
7
]
for details. Note that the time constant
\beta
is usually relatively low and can be uncertain depending on the surface texture and lubrication conditions. If its nominal value is not directly identifiable, an arbitrary
0<\beta\ll m\sigma^{-1}
may be assumed to be sufficiently lower than the time constant of a nominal linear dynamics with
f(\dot{x})\approx\sigma\dot{x}
approximation in (
1
).

II-B

Pre-sliding friction

A pre-sliding region of the dynamic Coulomb friction can be characterized after each motion reversal, or at the motion beginning, until the Coulomb friction force becomes saturated at
\pm C_{f}
. A pre-sliding displacement
z
(also the so-called
pre-sliding distance
) is understood as an internal state of the dynamic friction, cf.
[
5
]
, and is captured by

z=s\int^{t}_{t_{r}}\dot{x}\,dt,

(5)

where
s>0
is a linear scaling factor used when modeling the
F_{c}(t)
transition curves.

We assume the following logarithmic force transitions, that results in an increase of the hysteresis loop area proportional to 2nd power of the pre-sliding distance:

f_{0}(z)=z\bigl(1-\ln|z|\bigr),

(6)

suggested and analyzed in
[
18
]
for branching of the hysteresis friction force. Note that for (
6
), the pre-sliding distance state is well defined and monotonic only on the interval
z\in[-1,\,0)\cup(0,\,1]
, that requires an introduction of the scaling factor
s
. Then we use the thereupon based modeling approach
[
16
]
leading to the (normalized) pre-sliding map

f_{p}(t)=\bigl|\mathrm{sign}(\dot{x})-f_{r}\bigr|\,z\bigl(1-\ln|z|\bigr)+f_{r}.

(7)

This implies a pre-sliding friction memory
f_{r}:=f_{p}(t_{r})
for each motion reversal at time
t_{r}
, cf.
[
1
, Fig. 1]
.

Since the transient mapping (
7
) is defined for the pre-sliding region only, i.e. for
|z|\leq 1
, the overall Coulomb friction force with dynamic transitions results in

F_{c}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{f}\,f_{p}(t),&\hbox{if }|z|\leq 1,\\[2.84526pt]
C_{f}\,\mathrm{sign}\bigl(\dot{x}(t)\bigr),&\hbox{otherwise,}\\
\end{array}\right.

(8)

For the pre-sliding displacement state
z
, which is linked to
x
by the scaling factor
s
, one can obtain

\partial F_{c}/\partial z=-C_{f}\bigl|\mathrm{sign}(\dot{x})-f_{r}\bigr|\,\ln|z|,

(9)

and ensuing
\partial F_{c}/\partial x
. Since
z(t_{r})=0
, it can be seen that the instantaneous
pre-sliding stiffness

\partial F_{c}(t_{r}^{+})/\partial x=\partial F_{c}(t_{r}^{+})/\partial z\rightarrow\infty
, that is a crucial modeling feature for motion stops in finite time, cf.
[
17
]
.

It is worth noting that while other fairly suitable modeling approaches, like Dahl
[
20
]
, generalized Maxwell-slip (GMS)
[
21
]
, or modified Maxwell-slip
[
22
]
, can also be used for describing the pre-sliding hysteresis curves in
F_{c}(t)
, they fail to provide an infinite instantaneous stiffness.

III
Observation of motion states with friction

In order to design an asymptotic state observer, see
[
2
]
for basics, the disturbance-compensated system (
1
) with (
3
) and (
4
) can be transformed into the state-space form with the system matrix
A
and the input and output coupling vectors
B
and
C
, respectively. Introducing the state vector
w\equiv(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})^{T}:=(x,\dot{x},f)^{T}
, the state-space model

\dot{w}=\underset{A}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&-1/m\\
0&\bigl(\partial F_{c}/\partial x+\sigma/\beta\bigr)&0\end{array}\right)}}w+\underset{B}{\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
1/m\\
0\end{array}\right)}}u

(10)

with
C=(1,0,0)
is obtained. Note that the system matrix has one time depending term
\partial F_{c}/\partial x
so that the system is time-varying i.e.
A(t)
, correspondingly state-varying, during pre-sliding. While
\partial F_{c}/\partial x\rightarrow\infty
at
t_{r}
due to
z=0
, for practical handling of system matrices we assume
\partial F_{c}/\partial x\rightarrow\kappa
with
0\ll\kappa<\infty
. Then, one can show that in both boundary cases
\partial F_{c}/\partial x=0
(i.e. motion is in gross sliding) and
\partial F_{c}/\partial x=\kappa
(i.e. motion is in pre-sliding) the
(A,C)
-pair is observable in the Kalman sense for any positive
\kappa<\infty
. Since
\partial F_{c}/\partial x
decreases monotonically towards zero with time
t>t_{r}
, the same observability is valid on the whole interval
\partial F_{c}/\partial x\in[0,\,\kappa]
. This allows designing an asymptotic state observer in Luenberger sense
[
2
]
and, thus, obtain a states estimate
\tilde{w}(t)\rightarrow w(t)
for
t\rightarrow\infty
. An explicit analysis of the observer convergence with the time-varying system matrix is provided in section
III-B
.

III-A

Reduced-order Luenbeger observer

Since the measured output state
x
does not need to be estimated, a reduced-order observer
[
2
]
can be applied, while the state-space model (
10
) has to be transformed into

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{\bar{w}}\\
\dot{y}\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{w}\\
y\\
\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}B_{\bar{w}}\\
B_{y}\\
\end{array}\right)\,u,

(11)

where
\bar{w}=w_{1}
and
y=(w_{2},w_{3})^{T}
. Recall that (
11
) transfers an initial state-space representation into the regular form by providing a separation into the
measurable and unmeasurable states
\bar{w}
and
y
, respectively. The resulting exclusion of the estimate
\bar{w}
reduces the order of observer dynamics and, thus, complexity of the associated poles assignment. The reduced-order Luenberger observer is given by, cf.
[
2
]
,

\displaystyle\dot{\tilde{y}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A_{22}-LA_{12})\tilde{y}+(B_{y}-LB_{\bar{w}})u+

(12)

\displaystyle(A_{21}-LA_{11}+A_{22}L-LA_{12}L)\bar{w},

where
L\equiv(L_{1},L_{2})^{T}
is the vector of observer gains to be assigned. The latter is understood as an observer design and discussed in detail below in sections
III-B
and
III-C
. The dynamic variable
\tilde{y}
is the estimate vector of the unmeasurable system states, and since
\tilde{y}(t)
is input-output scaled according to the right-hand-side of (
12
) a back transformation, cf.
[
2
]
,

(\tilde{w}_{2},\tilde{w}_{3})^{T}(t)=\tilde{y}(t)+L\bar{w}(t),

(13)

is required for obtaining the system states of interest.

III-B

Convergence analysis

Since both
u
and
\bar{w}
are the bounded dynamic exogenous quantities entering the observer state equation (
12
), the only condition for the asymptotic stability of observer and thus for
\tilde{y}(t)\rightarrow y(t)
, is that the observer system matrix
\tilde{A}\equiv(A_{22}-LA_{12})
is Hurwitz. We recall however that

\tilde{A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-L_{1}&-1/m\\
\partial F_{c}/\partial x+\sigma/\beta-L_{2}&0\\
\end{array}\right)

(14)

is time-dependent, and that in general its eigenvalues do not provide sufficient or necessary information about the stability. Only if the eigenvalues of
\tilde{A}(t)
depend ”slowly” on time, then an asymptotic stability is related to the time-dependent eigenvalues, cf.
[
23
]
.

For proving the asymptotic stability of
\tilde{A}(t)
, we use the stability theorem by Ignatyev
[
24
]
for linear time-varying second-order differential equations of the form

\ddot{\psi}+a_{1}(t)\dot{\psi}+a_{0}(t)\psi=0,

(15)

where
a_{0}(t)
,
a_{1}(t)
are non-negative, continuous and bounded functions of the time.

Theorem 1
(cf. Ignatyev
[
24
]
)

If the coefficients of (
15
) satisfy the following conditions

(i)

\;\exists\;\alpha_{1}>0\;\forall\;t\geq 0\;:\quad\bigl|\dot{a}_{0}(t)\bigr|+\bigl|a_{1}(t)\bigr|\leq\alpha_{1}
,

(ii)

\;\exists\;\alpha_{2}>0\;\forall\;t\geq 0\;:\quad 0<\alpha_{2}\leq\dot{a}_{0}(t)+2a_{0}(t)a_{1}(t)
,

then the differential equation (
15
) is uniformly asymptotically stable. The proof can be found in
[
24
]
.

Introducing an auxiliary vector
\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and transforming (
14
) into the transpose companion matrix form
\tilde{A}^{\ast}
so that

\dot{\Psi}=\tilde{A}^{\ast}(t)\Psi,

one obtains (
15
) for
\Psi\equiv[\psi,\dot{\psi}]^{\top}
with the coefficients

\displaystyle a_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/m\,\bigl(\partial F_{c}/\partial x+\sigma/\beta-L_{2}),

(16)

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{1}.

(17)

Proposition 1

The time-varying observer system matrix (
14
), and hence the observer (
12
), is uniformly asymptotically stable if the observer gains satisfy

L_{1}>0,\quad L_{2}<\sigma/\beta.

(18)

Proof:

The proof consists in proving the conditions (i) and (ii) of Theorem
1
for the coefficients (
16
), (
17
), which describe the characteristic polynomial of the observer system matrix
\tilde{A}^{\ast}(t)
and so also
\tilde{A}(t)
given by (
14
).

Taking the time derivative of (
16
) one obtains

\dot{a}_{0}(t)=C_{1}s\,\dot{x}/z\quad\hbox{with}\quad C_{1}=C_{f}\bigl|\mathrm{sign}(\dot{x})-f_{r}\bigr|/m.

(19)

Since after each motion reversal
|z|>0
and a bounded
|\dot{x}|
can be assumed for any stable and implementable motion system (
1
) for all times
t>0
, a bounded
\dot{a}_{0}(t)
can be guaranteed, while
C_{1}
is upper bounded by
2C_{f}/m
per definition. Together with (
17
) it fulfils the condition (i) of Theorem
1
. Next, the condition (ii) of Theorem
1
requires that there exists a positive constant
\alpha_{2}
so that

C_{1}s\,\dot{x}/z+2L_{1}/m\,\bigl(\partial F_{c}/\partial x+\sigma/\beta-L_{2}\bigr)\geq\alpha_{2}.

(20)

Since the first summand on the left-hand-side of (
20
) is always non-negative, it can be assumed (as worth case) to be zero, for
\dot{x}=0
, and with
L_{1}>0
it is sufficient to prove
\partial F_{c}/\partial x+\sigma/\beta-L_{2}>0
for all times
t>0
. The lowest value of the pre-sliding partial derivative is zero for
|z|>1
that leads to (
18
). This completes the proof.
∎

III-C

Monotonic observer convergence

While Proposition
1
provides a parametric criteria for stability of the reduced-order asymptotic observer of
(\tilde{w}_{2},\tilde{w}_{3})
, one is inherently interested also in how the observer gains (
18
) must be assigned to achieve a potentially uniform and effective state estimation. Since the nonlinear dynamic friction state constitutes the prime challenge for observation, the uniformity can be expressed in a set of parameters that are equally suitable for both pre-sliding and gross sliding regimes of a non-stationary relative motion. At the same time, the measure of effectiveness of an asymptotic state estimation can be formulated in such a way that avoiding an oscillating transient dynamics and, therefore, aiming for a possibly monotonic convergence of the estimation errors.

Evaluating the eigenvalues of (
14
) yields

\lambda_{1,2}=-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(L_{1}\pm\sqrt{L_{1}^{2}+4/m\bigl(L_{2}-\partial F_{c}/\partial x-\sigma/\beta\bigr)}\,\Bigr).

(21)

It can be seen that the time-dependent eigenvalues and so an assignment of the
(L_{1},L_{2})
pair are dictated by the square root in (
21
). For making use of analysis of the eigenvalues (
21
), we will further assume a finite stiffness during the pre-sliding, each time of a motion reversal.

Proposition 2

Assuming a bounded pre-sliding stiffness
\partial F_{c}/\partial x\Bigl|_{t=t_{r}}\rightarrow\kappa
with
0\ll\kappa<\infty
at motion reversals, the eigenvalues (
21
) of the observer system matrix (
14
) can be assigned to be always negative real, with dominant pole at

\max\{\lambda_{1,2}\}=-\sqrt{\kappa/m}\,(\gamma-1),

(22)

by choosing

\displaystyle L_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\rho\sqrt{\kappa/m},

(23)

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma/\beta+\kappa\bigl(1-\rho^{2}\bigr),

(24)

where
\rho>1
is the design parameter.

Proof:

Requiring the poles of the observer system matrix to be real implies

L_{1}^{2}+4/m\bigl(L_{2}-\partial F_{c}/\partial x-\sigma/\beta\bigr)\geq 0,

(25)

that leads, under retention of (
18
), to

\sigma/\beta+\partial F_{c}/\partial x-mL_{1}^{2}/4\leq L_{2}<\sigma/\beta.

(26)

For (
26
) holds, the gain condition

L_1^2 ≥4/m   ∂F_c / ∂x

must be fulfilled for all times
t>0
. Taking the boundary value
\kappa
instead of
\partial F_{c}/\partial x
and introducing the design parameter
\rho>1
leads to (
23
), which is necessary (but not sufficient) for the eigenvalues (
21
) to be real
\forall\>t>0
.
For obtaining the sufficient parametric condition, dictated by
L_{2}
after the
L_{1}
value is determined, one rewrites (
25
) as

L_2 ≥
σ
/
β
+
κ
- L_1^2   m/4,

and substituting
L_{1}
from (
23
) obtains (
24
) as sufficient.
Finally, substituting (
23
) and (
24
) into (
21
), results in

\lambda_{1,2}=-\rho\sqrt{\kappa/m}\pm\sqrt{\bigl(\kappa-\partial F_{c}/\partial x\bigr)/m}.

(27)

During the motion reversals, the second square root in (
27
) becomes zero, and (
27
) results in the most left double real pole. As each pre-sliding transition saturates, the second square root in (
27
) converges to
\sqrt{\kappa/m}
, that leads to the dominant pole becomes (
22
). This completes the proof.
∎

, consider the model parameters similar (but not exactly the same) to those of the identified experimental system, cf. section

III-D

Propagation of measurement noise

Since the measured output displacement is subject to some sensor noise
\eta(t)
, which can only be assumed to be norm-bounded (like e.g.
|\eta|<\textmd{const}
) but generally constitutes a random process, it is of interest to analyze the noise propagation through the observer by assuming

\bar{w}(t)=x(t)+\eta(t)
. Substituting it, zeroing the control input (i.e.
u=0
), and evaluating the observer equations (
12
), (
13
) with the assigned design parameters, the dynamic friction state results in

\displaystyle\dot{w}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\rho^{2}w_{2}+2\kappa\rho^{3}\sqrt{\kappa/m}\,\bigl(x(t)+\eta(t)\bigr),

(28)

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{3}-\kappa(\rho^{2}-1)\bigl(x(t)+\eta(t)\bigr).

(29)

Assuming, without loss of generality, an operation point
x=0
and substituting the integrated (
28
) into (
29
) yields

\tilde{w}_{3}=\kappa\rho^{2}w_{1}+2\kappa\rho^{3}\sqrt{\kappa/m}\int\eta(t)dt-\kappa(\rho^{2}-1)\eta(t),

(30)

Even though an unbiased noise signal will be averaged through the second integral term on the right-hand-side of (
30
), the third right-hand-side term leads to a large propagation of

\eta(t)
into the
\tilde{w}_{3}(t)
state since
\kappa\gg 1
. Note that this is independent of the observer design, provided
\rho>1
is by definition. This fact postulates that the friction estimate will always, as long as
\eta\neq 0
holds, contain high-frequency noise components. A standard second-order low-pass filter

\ddot{\tilde{f}}(t)+2\omega_{co}\dot{\tilde{f}}(t)+\omega_{co}^{2}\tilde{f}(t)=\omega_{co}^{2}\tilde{w}_{3}(t)

(31)

with the cutoff frequency
\omega_{co}>0
is used for obtaining the friction force estimation
\tilde{f}(t)
. Note that
\omega_{co}
can be customary determined by taking into account both the evaluated sensor noise and the required reference dynamics of
x(t)
.

IV
Experimental evaluation

The experimental system used to evaluate the compensate of nonlinear friction is the voice-coil-motor (VCM) based linear displacement actuator. The available system signals are the input voltage
v(t)
(in V) and the output relative displacement
x(t)
(in m), while the real-time sampling rate is set to 10 kHz. The nominal system dynamics is given by

m\ddot{x}(t)=u(t)-G-f(t),

where the input force is
u=Kv
, with
K=3.28
N/V, while neglecting the electro-magnetic time constant of the actuator which is
\approx 0.0012
sec. The overall moving mass is
m=0.538
kg, and the constant gravity term is
G=5.27

IV-A

Optimally tuned PID control

When approximating the motion system (
1
) by an underlying transfer function (of the complex Laplace variable
\lambda
)

H(\lambda)=\frac{\phi}{\lambda(\tau\lambda+1)},

(32)

with the overall system gain
\phi>0
and the dominant pole at
-1/\tau
, where
\tau>0
is the inertial time constant, then a standard PID control (
C(\lambda)
) can be optimized in frequency domain with an objective of disturbance rejection. This is done based of the input disturbance sensitivity function

S=\frac{H}{1+HC}\quad\hbox{ with }\quad C=\frac{k(\tau\lambda+1)(T_{\mathrm{I}}\lambda+1)}{T_{\mathrm{I}}\lambda}.

(33)

After cancelation of the plant system pole, the remaining control design parameters are the total control gain
k>0
and the integrator time constant
T_{\mathrm{I}}>0
.

By assigning the upper bound of
\bigl|S(\lambda)\bigr|
as design specification, both control parameters are determined as

k=\frac{1}{\max|S|}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad T_{\mathrm{I}}=\frac{\tan^{2}(\Phi)}{k\phi\sqrt{1+\tan^{2}(\Phi)}},

(34)

where the phase related argument
\Phi
is computed for the open-loop crossover frequency
\omega_{\mathrm{s}}
as

\Phi=\pi+\angle\bigl[C(j\omega_{\mathrm{s}})H(j\omega_{\mathrm{s}})\bigr].

(35)

The designed this way PID control ensures that any matched disturbance
d(\lambda)
affects the controlled output only as

\bigl|x(j\omega)\bigr|\leq\max|S|\cdot\bigl|d(j\omega)\bigr|\quad\forall\>\omega,

where
\omega
is the angular frequency and
j
is the imaginary unit. More details on the above given optimal design of a PID control for disturbance rejection can be found in
[
26
]
.

V
Conclusions

The paper provides a novel asymptotic friction observer for the motion control. The observer benefits from the regular state-space form to describe the unavailable dynamic motion states and, thus, forms a reduced-order Luenberger observer, while only the relative displacement is measured being also subject to the sensor noise. We proved, by using the Ignatyev stability theorem
[
24
]
, that the resulted observer with time-dependent system matrix is uniformly asymptotically stable and derived the necessary and sufficient conditions for the observation gain parameters to ensure a monotonic convergence of the state variables. A standard PID feedback regulator was assumed as a reference control system, which was purposefully optimized to suppress disturbances, since the friction force can be considered as a kind of matched disturbance in a motion system. An experimental comparison of the PID motion control and that augmented by the friction observer was performed on a laboratory setup for two characteristic positioning and tracking reference trajectories.

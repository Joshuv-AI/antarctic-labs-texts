New constraints on Z ′ Z^{\prime} from captured dark matter annihilation in astrophysical objects
Basabendu Barman, Pooja Bhattacharjee, Arindam Das

Abstract
Considering dark matter capture in astrophysical objects such as neutron stars and brown dwarfs, followed by their annihilation into two neutrino and four neutrino final states, we derive new constraints on the mass and coupling of a novel abelian gauge boson Z ′ Z^{\prime} arising from an anomaly-free U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) extension of the Standard Model. We further confront these astrophysical limits with complementary bounds from the Planck-observed relic abundance via the freeze-in mechanism, big bang nucleosynthesis (BBN), gravitational wave signatures from cosmic strings, and searches at energy and intensity frontier experiments.

Another intriguing mechanism is DM capture, whereby celestial objects such as galaxies, stars, or planets can gravitationally attract and confine DM particles traversing through space. In this process, DM particles are drawn into the object’s strong gravitational potential and subsequently lose energy, typically assumed to occur through scattering with the SM. Once their velocity falls below the escape velocity, they become gravitationally bound, leading to an accumulation of DM within the object. Freeze-in production of dark matter
–
We focus on scenarios where the DM–SM interaction is feebler than the weak interaction, such that DM never thermalizes in the early Universe. This naturally leads to DM production via the
freeze-in
mechanism, where the DM yield typically saturates when
T=\text{max}\left[m_{\chi},\,M_{Z^{\prime}}\right]
. Here, DM is produced dominantly through two processes: (a) on-shell 1-to-2 decays of the
Z^{\prime}
gauge boson into DM pairs, and
(b)
Z^{\prime}
-mediated 2-to-2
s
-channel scattering of thermal bath particles into DM pairs. We kinematically forbid the former channel by setting
m_{\chi}=3\,M_{Z^{\prime}}
. The evolution of the DM number density is governed by the Boltzmann equation (BEQ), which can be expressed in terms of the DM yield
Y_{\rm DM}\equiv n_{\rm DM}/s
, with
s
being the entropy density. In terms of the dimensionless parameter
x\equiv m_{\chi}/T
, the equation reads,

x\,H\,s\,\frac{dY_{\rm DM}}{dx}=\gamma_{22}\,,

(20)

where
\gamma_{22}
is the reaction density for 2-to-2 processes. Explicitly, this is given by:

\displaystyle\gamma_{22}=\frac{T}{32\pi^{4}}\,g_{a}g_{b}\int_{\mathfrak{s}_{\rm min}}^{\infty}ds\,\frac{\left[(\mathfrak{s}-m_{a}^{2}-m_{b}^{2})^{2}-4m_{a}^{2}m_{b}^{2}\right]}{\sqrt{s}}

\displaystyle\sigma(s)_{a,b\to 1,2}\,K_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\mathfrak{s}}}{T}\right),

(21)

where
g_{a,b}
are the degrees of freedom of the initial states
a,b
and
\mathfrak{s}_{\rm min}=\max\left[(m_{a}+m_{b})^{2},(m_{1}+m_{2})^{2}\right]
. During radiation domination, the Hubble rate and entropy density are given by,

\displaystyle H(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{3}\sqrt{\frac{g_{\star}(T)}{10}}\,\frac{T^{2}}{M_{P}},

\displaystyle s(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{45}\,g_{\star s}(T)\,T^{3},

(22)

where
g_{\star}
and
g_{\star s}
are the relativistic degrees of freedom contributing to energy and entropy density, respectively.

To determine whether DM production is truly non-thermal, we compare the scattering rate
\Gamma_{22}=\gamma_{22}/n_{\rm eq}
with the Hubble rate. The equilibrium number density of DM is given by
n_{\rm eq}^{\rm DM}=\left(T/(2\pi^{2})\right)\,m_{\chi}^{2}\,K_{2}(m_{\chi}/T)
.
For an analytical estimate, we work in the limit
T\gg m_{\chi},M_{Z^{\prime}}
. This gives

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{22}}{H}\Bigg|_{T=M_{Z^{\prime}}}\simeq\mathcal{C}\left(\frac{g_{X}}{10^{-4}}\right)^{4}\left(\frac{M_{Z^{\prime}}}{1\,\text{TeV}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{Q_{\chi}}{100}\right)^{2}\,,

(23)

for
m_{\chi}=3\,M_{Z^{\prime}}
using the cross-sections from
Barman:2024lxy
;
Barman:2025bir
where
\mathcal{C}=\{3\times 10^{-5},10^{-4},3\times 10^{-4},6\times 10^{-4}\}
for
x_{H}=\{-1,0,1,2\}
and
x_{\Phi}=1
. This is a conservative estimate, as it uses the equilibrium number density for the DM. Since
\sigma(s)\propto g_{X}^{4}
, the interaction rate falls below the Hubble rate for relatively larger values of
g_{X}
, however, to ensure out of equilibrium production, we conservatively take
g_{X}\lesssim 10^{-3}
. The observed DM relic abundance requires
Y_{0}\,m_{\chi}=\Omega_{\rm DM}\,h^{2}\,\frac{\rho_{c}}{s_{0}\,h^{2}}\simeq 4.3\times 10^{-10}\,\text{GeV}
, where
Y_{0}=Y_{\rm DM}(T_{0})
is the present-day DM yield. Here
\rho_{c}\simeq 1.05\times 10^{-5}\,h^{2}\,\text{GeV/cm}^{3}
,
s_{0}\simeq 2.69\times 10^{3}\,\text{cm}^{-3}
and
\Omega_{\rm DM}h^{2}\simeq 0.12
are critical density, current entropy density
ParticleDataGroup:2022pth
and observed relic density
Planck:2018vyg
. In the high-temperature limit
T\gg m
, the asymptotic yield approximately follows
Y_{0}\propto g_{X}^{4}\,Q_{\chi}^{2}/M_{Z^{\prime}}
, whereas, for
T\ll m
, the yield scales as
Y_{0}\propto g_{X}^{4}\,Q_{\chi}^{2}/M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}
. Thus, in the heavy mass regime, achieving the same yield necessitates a larger coupling.

Gravitational waves from cosmic string
–
The dominant mechanism for energy loss from cosmic strings is GW emission from oscillating loops, as shown in simulations using the Nambu-Goto action
Ringeval:2005kr
;
Blanco-Pillado:2011egf
. The power radiated is given by
Vilenkin:1981bx

P_{\rm GW}=\frac{G}{5}(\dddot{Q})^{2}\propto G\mu^{2}\,,

(24)

where
\mu
is the string tension. Thus, the energy loss rate reads,

\frac{dE}{dt}=-\Gamma G\mu^{2}\,,

(25)

with
\Gamma\simeq 50

Vachaspati:1984gt
. The loop length evolves from its initial size
l_{i}=\alpha t_{i}
as,

l(t)=\alpha t_{i}-\Gamma G\mu(t-t_{i})\,,

(26)

where
\alpha\sim 0.1
is the loop size parameter
Blanco-Pillado:2013qja
;
Blanco-Pillado:2017oxo
. Loops emit GWs in discrete harmonics with frequencies

f_{k}=\frac{2k}{l(t)}\,,\quad k=1,2,3,\dots\,.

(27)

The present-day GW spectrum is defined as,

\Omega_{\rm GW}(t_{0},f)=\sum_{k}\Omega_{\rm GW}^{(k)}(t_{0},f)=\frac{f}{\rho_{c}}\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}}{df}\,.

(28)

Since GW energy redshifts as
a^{-4}
, the contribution from each mode is
Blanco-Pillado:2013qja

\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}^{(k)}}{df}=\int_{t_{F}}^{t_{0}}\left[\frac{a(t_{E})}{a(t_{0})}\right]^{4}P_{\rm GW}(t_{E},f_{k})\frac{dF}{df}dt_{E}\,,

(29)

where
dF/df=f[a(t_{0})/a(t_{E})]
. The power emitted per frequency is

P_{\rm GW}(t_{E},f_{k})=\frac{2kG\mu^{2}\Gamma_{k}}{f[a(t_{0})/a(t_{E})]^{2}}\,n\left(t_{E},\frac{2k}{f}\left[\frac{a(t_{E})}{a(t_{0})}\right]\right)\,,

(30)

with

\Gamma_{k}=\frac{\Gamma\,k^{-4/3}}{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{-4/3}}\,,\quad\sum_{k}\Gamma_{k}=\Gamma\,.

(31)

The loop number density
n(t_{E},l)
depends on the background cosmology
a(t)\propto t^{\beta}
and is given by
Martins:1996jp
;
Martins:2000cs
;
Auclair:2019wcv

n(t_{E},l)=\frac{A_{\beta}}{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha+\Gamma G\mu)^{3(1-\beta)}}{[l+\Gamma G\mu t_{E}]^{4-3\beta}t_{E}^{3\beta}}\,,

(32)

where
A_{\beta}
is a constant. Assuming cusp-dominated emission
Damour:2001bk
;
Gouttenoire:2019kij
, the present-day GW spectrum becomes

\Omega_{\rm GW}^{(k)}(t_{0},f)=\frac{2kG\mu^{2}\Gamma_{k}}{f\rho_{c}}\int_{t_{\rm osc}}^{t_{0}}dt\left[\frac{a(t)}{a(t_{0})}\right]^{5}n(t,l_{k})\,,

(33)

where
t_{\rm osc}
marks the end of the friction-dominated era
Vilenkin:1991zk
. During radiation domination, the spectrum features a flat plateau with amplitude

\Omega_{\rm GW}^{(k=1),{\rm plateau}}(f)=\frac{128\pi G\mu}{9\zeta(4/3)}\frac{A_{r}}{\epsilon_{r}}\Omega_{r}\left[(1+\epsilon_{r})^{3/2}-1\right],

(34)

where
\epsilon_{r}=\alpha/(\Gamma G\mu)
, and
A_{r}=0.54
for the radiation era
Auclair:2019wcv
.

Conclusions
–
Dark matter capture in astrophysical objects offers powerful probes of new physics, complementary to experimental searches like beam-dump, low energy scattering and cosmological observations. In this work, within a minimal UV-complete framework, we consider DM captured by neutron stars and brown dwarfs that annihilates via a massive gauge boson. We constrain the relevant parameter space by combining limits from capture rates, freeze-in relic abundance consistent with Planck data, gravitational waves from cosmic strings, big bang nucleosynthesis (BBN), and energy- and intensity-frontier experiments. Remarkably, we find that constraints obtained from NS and BD can test regions of parameter space for
M_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 1
GeV and
g_{X}\sim\left[10^{-6}-10^{-7}\right]
, consistent with right DM abundance, while inaccessible to GW detectors and several low and high energy experiments.

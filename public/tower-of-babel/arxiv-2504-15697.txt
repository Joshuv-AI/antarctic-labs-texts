1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

We recall that the classical Euler’s gamma function is given by

\Gamma(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}e^{-t}dt,\quad{\rm{Re}}(z)>0,

which has meromorphic continuation to the complex plane and interpolates the factorials as one has
\Gamma(n+1)=n!
for all natural numbers
n
.
The special gamma values are referred to the values of
\Gamma
at proper fractions, and they are interesting because of having connection with periods. For instance, it is known by Lerch and Chowla-Selberg (see
[
SC67
]
) that periods of elliptic curves with complex multiplication over number fields can be expressed as products of special gamma values.
A folklore conjecture asserts that all these special gamma values are transcendental numbers.
To date, the transcendence of
\Gamma(z)
is known only when the denominator of
z\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
is either
2,4
or
6
.
For example, one knows that
\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
.
The latter two cases mentioned above are consequences of Chudnovsky’s result
[
Chu84
]
on the algebraic independence of a non-zero period and the associated quasi-period of a CM elliptic curve defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Let
p
be a prime number. Morita
[
Mor75
]
studied the
p
-adic interpolation problem of the factorials, and then successfully defined the
p
-adic gamma function
\Gamma_{p}
, which is the unique continuous function on the
p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that

\Gamma_{p}(n)=(-1)^{n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq t\leq n-1\\
p\kern 0.81949pt\nmid\kern 0.81949ptt\end{subarray}}t,\quad\forall\>n\in\mathbb{N}.

The values
\{\Gamma_{p}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z\in(\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{p})\setminus\mathbb{Z}\}
are called special
p
-adic gamma values. Compared with the (conjectural) situation of Euler’s gamma, a peculiar phenomenon in the
p
-adic world occurs: A class of special
p
-adic gamma values turns out to be algebraic numbers when
p>2
. This result is a consequence of the celebrated
Gross-Koblitz formula
in
[
GK79
]
, which is reviewed as follows.

We let
p
be an odd prime and
q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=p^{\ell}
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
Consider the
(q-1)
-st cyclotomic field
K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{q-1})
.
We choose a prime
\mathfrak{P}
of
K
above
p
and let
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}
be its residue field (which is a finite field of
q
elements).
We let
\chi
be the group homomorphism from
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times}
to
\mu_{q-1}
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}
which is the inverse of reduction map (the Teichmüller character), and choose an isomorphism
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\to\mu_{p}
as abelian groups.
For
x\in(q-1)^{-1}\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq x<1
, we consider the Gauss sum

g_{\ell}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times}}\chi(z^{-x(q-1)})\psi(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z)).

Let
\varpi_{p}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\psi(1))
be the unique
(p-1)
-st root of
-p
for which
\varpi_{p}\equiv\psi(1)-1\pmod{(\psi(1)-1)^{2}}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Gross-Koblitz formula)
.

For
x\in(q-1)^{-1}\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq x<1
, one has

(1)

g_{\ell}(x)=\varpi_{p}^{(p-1)\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\langle p^{j}x\rangle}\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\Gamma_{p}\left(\langle p^{j}x\rangle\right).

Here,
\langle y\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=y-[y]
is the fractional part of a non-negative real number
y
.

In particular, the Gross-Koblitz formula together with the translation formula of
\Gamma_{p}
(see
[
Mor75
, p. 256]
) implies that
\Gamma_{p}(r/(p-1))
is algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

After the above formula was established, Greenberg asked a question concerning the uniqueness of
p
-adic gamma function in Gross-Koblitz formula:
Is there any other continuous function
H
from
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that
(
1
)
remains true when
\Gamma_{p}
is replaced by
H
?
The answer to this problem was provided by Adolphson in
[
Ado83
]
where a complete classification of such
H
was given.
Specifically, we consider the function
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
defined by

(2)

\varphi\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}p^{i}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i+1}p^{i}

where
0\leq x_{i}<p
for all
i
.
Then he showed (see
[
Ado83
, pp. 58–59]
)

Theorem 1.2

(Adolphson)
.

A function
H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is continuous non-vanishing satisfying (
1
) if and only if

H(x)=\Gamma_{p}(x)\cdot\frac{G(x)}{G\left(-\varphi(-x)\right)}

on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, where
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is any continuous non-vanishing function.

Adolphson’s result motivates us to study the positive characteristic counterparts in the function field setting.

1.2.
v
-adic Gamma Functions

We now turn our focus to the function field setting.
Let
p
be any prime and
q
be a power of
p
.
Let
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
be the polynomial ring in the variable
\theta
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of
q
elements, and
k\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)
be its field of fractions.
Let
A_{+}
be the set of all monic polynomials in
A
and
A_{+,i}
(for
i\geq 0
) be its subset consisting of all monic polynomials of degree
i
.
We fix an irreducible
v\in A_{+,d}
and let
k_{v}
be the completion of
k
at
v
with ring of integers
A_{v}
.
We let
\overline{k}
be the algebraic closure of
k
contained in a fixed algebraic closure
\overline{k}_{v}
of
k_{v}
.
For an element
x\in A_{v}
, put

x^{\flat}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}x,&\text{if }x\in A_{v}^{\times},\\
1,&\text{if }x\in vA_{v}.\end{cases}

Definition 1.3
.

(1) (
v
-adic arithmetic gamma function
,
[
Gos80
, Appendix]
): For
y=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}y_{i}q^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
0\leq y_{i}<q
for all
i
, define
\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{ari}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to A_{v}^{\times}
by

\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{ari}}(y+1)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(-\prod_{a\in A_{+,i}}a^{\flat}\right)^{y_{i}}.

(2) (
v
-adic geometric gamma function
,
[
Tha91
, Section 5]
): Define
\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{geo}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A_{v}\to A_{v}^{\times}
by

\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{geo}}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{x^{\flat}}\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{a\in A_{+,i}}\frac{a^{\flat}}{(x+a)^{\flat}}\right).

(3) (
v
-adic two-variable gamma function
,
[
Gos96
, Subsection 9.9]
): For
(x,y)\in A_{v}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
y
as in the arithmetic case, define
\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{two}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A_{v}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to A_{v}^{\times}
by

\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{two}}(x,y+1)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{x^{\flat}}\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{a\in A_{+,i}}\frac{a^{\flat}}{(x+a)^{\flat}}\right)^{y_{i}}.

The arithmetic case is the Morita-style
v
-adic interpolation of Carlitz factorials
[
Car35
]

\Gamma^{\textnormal{ari}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{N}\to A
given by

\Gamma^{\textnormal{ari}}\left(1+\sum_{i=0}^{n}y_{i}q^{i}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{i=0}^{n}\left(\prod_{a\in A_{+,i}}a\right)^{y_{i}}\quad\text{where}\quad 0\leq y_{i}<q.

The above can be viewed as an analog of
\Gamma(y+1)=y!
for
y\in\mathbb{N}
in view of W. Sinnott’s result that (see
[
Gos96
, Theorem 9.1.1]
)

\operatorname{ord}_{f}(\Gamma^{\textnormal{ari}}(y+1))=\sum_{e\geq 1}\left\lfloor\frac{y}{q^{e\deg f}}\right\rfloor

for any monic prime
f
.
On the other hand, the geometric case arises from the Morita-style
v
-adic interpolation of the function
\Gamma^{\textnormal{geo}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to k\cup\{\infty\}
given by

\Gamma^{\textnormal{geo}}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{x}\prod_{a\in A_{+}}\frac{a}{x+a}=x^{-1}\prod_{a\in A_{+}}\left(1+\frac{x}{a}\right)^{-1}.

Note that
\Gamma^{\textnormal{geo}}
has simple poles at
-A_{+}\cup\{0\}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{-a\mid a\in A_{+}\}\cup\{0\}
, analogous to the case that Euler’s
\Gamma
-function has simple poles at
-\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{-n\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{0\}
.
Finally, for the two-variable case, one sees that

\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{two}}\left(x,1-\frac{1}{q-1}\right)=\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{geo}}(x).

Note that all three gamma functions in Definition
1.3
are continuous and non-vanishing as they are defined via the interpolations of continuous functions and take values in the units of
A_{v}
.

1.3.
Gross-Koblitz-Thakur Formulas for
v
-adic Gamma Functions

There are analogs of Gross-Koblitz formulas for these
v
-adic gamma functions by the work of Thakur
[
Tha88
]
and the first author
[
Cha25
]
.
Before stating the precise formulas we prepare some necessary background (see
[
Gos96
, Chapter 3]
for more details).
Let
K
be a field together with an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to K
.
Let
\tau\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathbb{G}_{a})
be the
q
-th power Frobenius morphism on the additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}
over
K
.
We let
K\{\tau\}
be the twisted polynomial ring in
\tau
over
K
with usual addition but the multiplication is given by
\tau x=x^{q}\tau
for all
x\in K
.
Recall that the Carlitz module over
K
is the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
C\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to K\{\tau\}
defined by
C_{\theta}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\iota(\theta)+\tau
.
It induces a new
A
-module structure
C(K)
on
K
given by
a\cdot x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=C_{a}(x)
for all
a\in A
and
x\in K
.
In the case
K=\overline{k}
(with the natural structure map), we let
\Lambda_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=C(\overline{k})[a]
be the
a
-torsion points of
C(\overline{k})
for any
a\in A
.
Then it is a fact that
\Lambda_{a}
is isomorphic to
A/a
as
A
-modules.

To state the analogs of Gross-Koblitz formulas for three
v
-adic gamma functions, we define the notions of fractional part on the rational number field
\mathbb{Q}
and function field
k
.
Note for
y\in\mathbb{Q}
, its fractional part
\langle y\rangle
can be defined as the unique element in
\mathbb{Q}
such that
0\leq\langle y\rangle<1
and
y\equiv\langle y\rangle\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}
.
On the other hand, one recalls the normalized
\infty
-adic absolute value on
k
is given by
|0|_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0
and
|f/g|_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=q^{\deg f-\deg g}
for any
f,g\in A\setminus\{0\}
.
(In what follows, we will omit the subscript
|\cdot|_{\infty}
and denote it as
|\cdot|
for convenience.)
Thus for
x\in k
, we define its fractional part
\langle x\rangle
as the unique element in
k
such that
0\leq|\langle x\rangle|<1
and
x\equiv\langle x\rangle\pmod{A}
.

First, we consider the arithmetic case
[
Tha88
]
.
Let
\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A/v\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}\subseteq\overline{k}
be the Teichmüller character, and choose an
A
-module isomorphism
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A/v\to\Lambda_{v}
.
Then Thakur defined the
arithmetic Gauss sum
to be

g^{\textnormal{ari}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\sum_{z\in(A/v)^{\times}}\chi(z^{-1})\psi(z)\in k(\Lambda_{v})\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}.

(The same element is denoted as
g_{0}
in the original paper.)
More generally, take any natural number
\ell
.
For
y\in(q^{d\ell}-1)^{-1}\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq y<1
, we write

y=\sum_{s=0}^{d\ell-1}\frac{y_{s}q^{s}}{q^{d\ell}-1}\quad(0\leq y_{s}<q\text{ for all }s).

We let
\tau_{q}\in\operatorname{Gal}(k\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}/k)
be the
q
-th power Frobenius map on the constant field, and extend it canonically to
\operatorname{Gal}(k(\Lambda_{v})\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}/k)\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(k(\Lambda_{v})/k)\times\operatorname{Gal}(k\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}/k)
.
Then we consider the integral group ring element
\sum_{s=0}^{d\ell-1}y_{s}\tau_{q}^{s}\in\mathbb{Z}[\operatorname{Gal}(k(\Lambda_{v})\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}/k)]
acting on the arithmetic Gauss sum
g^{\textnormal{ari}}
, and define

G_{\ell}^{\textnormal{ari}}(y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-1)^{\ell(d-1)}\prod_{s=0}^{d\ell-1}(g^{\textnormal{ari}})^{y_{s}\tau_{q}^{s}}.

Let
\varpi_{v}\in k_{v}(\psi(1))
be the unique
(q^{d}-1)
-st root of
-v
for which
\varpi_{v}\equiv-\psi(1)\pmod{\psi(1)^{2}}
.
Then under this setting, Thakur proved the following analog of Gross-Koblitz formula for
v
-adic arithmetic gamma function (see
[
Tha88
, Theorem VI]
and
[
Tha91
, Theorem 4.8]
).

Theorem 1.4

(Gross-Koblitz-Thakur formula, arithmetic case)
.

For
y\in(q^{d\ell}-1)^{-1}\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq y<1
, one has

(3)

G_{\ell}^{\textnormal{ari}}(y)=\varpi_{v}^{(q^{d}-1)\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\langle q^{dj}y\rangle}\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{ari}}\left(\langle q^{dj}y\rangle\right).

Next, we consider the geometric and two-variable cases
[
Cha25
]
.
Fix a positive integer
\ell
and let
\mathfrak{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=v^{\ell}
.
Consider the Carlitz
(\mathfrak{n}-1)
-st cyclotomic function field
K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=k(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{n}-1})
.
We let
\mathfrak{P}
be the prime in
K
above
v
corresponding to the inclusions
K\subseteq\overline{k}\subseteq\overline{k}_{v}
, and let
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}
be the residue field (which is a finite field of
q^{d\ell}
elements).
Then via the natural structure map on
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}
, it obtains a new
A
-module structure
C(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}})
via the Carlitz module.
We let
\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}})\to\Lambda_{\mathfrak{n}-1}
be the
A
-module isomorphism which is the inverse of reduction map, and let
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}\subseteq\overline{k}
be the usual Teichmüller embedding.
Then for
x\in(\mathfrak{n}-1)^{-1}A
with
|x|<1
, we define the
geometric Gauss sum
to be

g^{\textnormal{geo}}_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1+\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times}}\omega\left(C_{x(\mathfrak{n}-1)}(z^{-1})\right)\psi(z)\in K\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}.

More generally, we let
\tau_{q}\in\operatorname{Gal}(k\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}/k)
be the
q
-th power Frobenius map on the constant field, and extend it canonically to
\operatorname{Gal}(K\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}/k)\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)\times\operatorname{Gal}(k\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}/k)
.
For
y\in(q^{d\ell}-1)^{-1}\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq y<1
written as in the arithmetic case, we consider the integral group ring element
\sum_{s=0}^{d\ell-1}y_{s}\tau_{q}^{s}\in\mathbb{Z}[\operatorname{Gal}(K\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}/k)]
acting on the geometric Gauss sum
g^{\textnormal{geo}}_{x}
, and define

G_{\ell}^{\textnormal{geo}}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{s=0}^{d\ell-1}(g^{\textnormal{geo}}_{x})^{y_{s}\tau_{q}^{s}}.

In particular, put

G_{\ell}^{\textnormal{geo}}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=G_{\ell}^{\textnormal{geo}}\left(x,\frac{1}{q-1}\right)=\prod_{s=0}^{d\ell-1}(g^{\textnormal{geo}}_{x})^{\tau_{q}^{s}}.

Then under this setting, we have the following analogs of Gross-Koblitz-Thakur formulas for
v
-adic geometric and two-variable gamma functions
[
Cha25
, Theorems 5.1.3 and 5.1.4]
.

Theorem 1.5

(Gross-Koblitz-Thakur formulas, geometric and two-variable cases)
.

Suppose
x\in(\mathfrak{n}-1)^{-1}A
and
y\in(q^{d\ell}-1)^{-1}\mathbb{Z}
with
|x|<1
and
0\leq y<1
, write

x=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{x_{j}v^{j}}{v^{\ell}-1}\quad(\deg x_{j}<d\text{ for all }j),\quad y=\sum_{s=0}^{d\ell-1}\frac{y_{s}q^{s}}{q^{d\ell}-1}\quad(0\leq y_{s}<q\text{ for all }s).

Then we have

(4)

G_{\ell}^{\textnormal{geo}}(x)=\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{\delta_{x,j}}{\langle v^{j}x\rangle^{\flat}}\cdot\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{geo}}\left(\langle v^{j}x\rangle\right)^{-1}

and

(5)

G_{\ell}^{\textnormal{geo}}(x,y)=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{\langle v^{j}x\rangle^{\flat}}{\delta_{x,j}^{q-1-y_{dj+\deg x_{j}}}}\right)G_{\ell}^{\textnormal{geo}}(x)^{q-1}\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{two}}\left(\langle v^{j}x\rangle,\langle q^{dj}y\rangle\right)

where
\delta_{x,j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=v\langle v^{\ell-j-1}x\rangle
if
x_{j}\in A_{+}
and
1
otherwise.

Remark 1.6
.

Note that the translation formulas of these
v
-adic gamma functions imply that

\frac{\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{ari}}(y+r)}{\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{ari}}(y)},\frac{\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{geo}}(x+a)}{\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{geo}}(x)},\frac{\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{two}}(x+a,y+r)}{\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{two}}(x,y)}\in k^{\times},\quad\forall\>r\in\mathbb{Z},a\in A.

Hence, similar to the classical case, the Gross-Koblitz-Thakur formulas (
3
),(
4
),(
5
) imply that
\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{ari}}(r/(q^{d}-1)),\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{geo}}(a/(v-1)),\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{two}}(a/(v-1),r/(q^{d}-1))
are algebraic over
k
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a\in A
.
We further mention that in the paper
[
CWY24
]
, Chang-Wei-Yu proved the transcendence of those special
v
-adic arithmetic gamma values which are not from the Gross-Koblitz-Thakur formula.
That is, for
a/b\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
\gcd(a,b)=1
, we have
\Gamma_{v}^{\textnormal{ari}}(a/b)
is transcendental over
k
if and only if
b\nmid q^{d}-1
.

1.4.
Main Theorem

In this paper, we consider the analogous question of Greenberg for these three
v
-adic gamma functions:
For each case, is there any other non-vanishing continuous
k_{v}
-valued function
H
defined on the same domain as the respective gamma function, such that the corresponding Gross-Koblitz-Thakur formula ((
3
),(
4
),(
5
)) remains true when the gamma is replaced by
H
?
For this problem, we give a precise description of all such functions parallel to the classical result.

To treat these three cases at once, we consider a more general setting.
Let
\mathcal{O}
be a direct product of finitely many rings
\mathcal{O}_{t}
(indexed by
t
), where each
\mathcal{O}_{t}
is either
\mathbb{Z}
or
A
.
We let
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}
be the completion
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{t}}
or
A_{v_{t}}
(depending on the choice of
\mathcal{O}_{t}
) at a fixed finite prime
p_{t}
or
v_{t}
, and let
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
be the direct product of
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}
.

For each
t
, we fix a power of the given uniformizer
\pi_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}
and put
\boldsymbol{\pi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\pi_{t})_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.
Let
\mathbf{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(1,\ldots,1)\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
be the identity element.
For
\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{t})_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, we write

\mathbf{x}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{t,i}\pi_{t}^{i}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}

in
\pi_{t}
-adic expansion.
This means that
x_{t,i}
is an integer with
0\leq x_{t,i}<\pi_{t}
when
\mathcal{O}_{t}=\mathbb{Z}
or a polynomial in
A
with
|x_{t,i}|<|\pi_{t}|
(i.e.,
\deg x_{t,i}<\deg\pi_{t}
) when
\mathcal{O}_{t}=A
.
Define
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to\overline{\mathcal{O}}
by
\varphi(\mathbf{x})=(\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t}))_{t}

where each
\varphi_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}\to\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}
is given by (cf. (
2
))

\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{t,i+1}\pi_{t}^{i}.

We also define the fractional part of elements in the product of fraction fields of
\mathcal{O}_{t}
, denoted as
\prod\operatorname{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_{t})
, by taking the fractional parts componentwise.
That is, define
\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\langle\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle)_{t}
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\prod\operatorname{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_{t})
.

Our main result in this paper is to study and answer analogous problems of Greenberg in the setting as general as possible.
For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{t})_{t}\in(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}-\mathbf{1})^{-1}\mathcal{O}
, we set up the condition:

(6)

\begin{cases}0\leq\mathbf{x}_{t}<1,&\text{if }\mathcal{O}_{t}=\mathbb{Z},\\
|\mathbf{x}_{t}|<1,&\text{if }\mathcal{O}_{t}=A.\end{cases}

The following is our main theorem, which recovers Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Given any complete non-Archimedean field
K
, let
\mathbf{\Gamma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
be a continuous non-vanishing function.
Then
H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
is a continuous non-vanishing function satisfying for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}\in(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}-\mathbf{1})^{-1}\mathcal{O}
with condition (
6
),

(7)

\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}\left(\langle\boldsymbol{\pi}^{j}\mathbf{x}\rangle\right)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}H\left(\langle\boldsymbol{\pi}^{j}\mathbf{x}\rangle\right)

if and only if

H(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{\Gamma}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\frac{G(\mathbf{x})}{G\left(-\varphi(-\mathbf{x})\right)}

on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, where
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
is any continuous non-vanishing function.

As corollaries, we obtain the complete characterizations of all continuous non-vanishing functions satisfying Gross-Koblitz-Thakur formulas for the arithmetic, geometric and two-variable gamma functions, respectively.
Specifically, for each
\bullet=\text{ari, geo, two}
, we choose
(\overline{\mathcal{O}}{}^{\bullet},\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\bullet})
to be
(\mathbb{Z}_{p},q^{d}),(A_{v},v),(A_{v}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},(v,q^{d}))
, respectively.
We denote
\varphi^{\bullet}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}{}^{\bullet}\to\overline{\mathcal{O}}{}^{\bullet}
to be the corresponding
\varphi
function.
Then we have the following consequence.

Corollary 1.8
.

For any
\bullet\in\{\textnormal{ari, geo, two}\}
, a function
H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}{}^{\bullet}\to k_{v}
is continuous non-vanishing and satisfies the corresponding Gross-Koblitz-Thakur formula ((
3
), (
4
), (
5
)) if and only if

H(\mathbf{x})=\Gamma^{\bullet}_{v}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\frac{G(\mathbf{x})}{G\left(-\varphi^{\bullet}(-\mathbf{x})\right)}

on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}{}^{\bullet}
, where
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}{}^{\bullet}\to k_{v}
is any continuous non-vanishing function.

1.5.
Strategy of Proof

Our proof is inspired by the original paper
[
Ado83
]
(in particular, the Remark 3 and the theorem after it).
First, it is observed in Section
2.1
that (
7
) can be rewritten as (for the same conditions on
\mathbf{x}
)

\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}\left(-\varphi^{(j)}(-\mathbf{x})\right)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}H\left(-\varphi^{(j)}(-\mathbf{x})\right).

Such expression will immediately imply the “if” direction.

For the “only if” part, we set
F(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H(\mathbf{x})/\mathbf{\Gamma}(\mathbf{x})
.
Then what we want to show is that
F
must be of the form (Theorem
2.1
and
[
Ado83
, Theorem 1]
)

F(\mathbf{x})=\frac{G(\mathbf{x})}{G\left(-\varphi(-\mathbf{x})\right)}

on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
for some continuous non-vanishing function
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
.
To prove this, we consider
\tilde{F}(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=F(-\mathbf{x})
(to eliminate the minus signs; Theorem
2.2
and
[
Ado83
, Theorem 2]
) and construct two sequences of continuous functions
\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})
and
\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})
(see (
13
) and (
14
)).
Then it is shown in (
16
) that

\tilde{F}\left(\varphi^{(n-1)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)=A_{n}(\mathbf{x})\cdot B_{n}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\frac{\tilde{G}_{n}(\mathbf{x})}{\tilde{G}_{n}\left(\varphi(\mathbf{x})\right)}

for some (explicit in terms of
\tilde{F},\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}
and
\varphi
) functions
A_{n},B_{n},\tilde{G}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
.
With some special properties of
\tilde{F},\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
(see (
11
), (
15
), etc.), we prove that the left-hand side converges to
\tilde{F}(\mathbf{x})
and the right-hand side converges to
\tilde{G}(\mathbf{x})/\tilde{G}(\varphi(\mathbf{x}))
for some continuous non-vanishing function
\tilde{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
(Lemma
2.3
).
Then the desired function
G(\mathbf{x})
turns out to be
\tilde{G}(-\mathbf{x})
, and the result follows.

2.
Proof of the Theorem
1.7

2.1.
The “if” Direction

In this section, we give a proof of “
\Leftarrow
” of Theorem
1.7
. The primary key of our proof is to reformulate (
7
) as follows.
Recall that
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to\overline{\mathcal{O}}
is defined by
\varphi(\mathbf{x})=(\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t}))_{t}

where each
\varphi_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}\to\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}
is given by

\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{t,i+1}\pi_{t}^{i}

for any

\mathbf{x}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{t,i}\pi_{t}^{i}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}.

For
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, we denote by

\mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{y}\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}}

if

\mathbf{x}_{t}\equiv\mathbf{y}_{t}\pmod{\pi_{t}^{n}}

for all
t
.
We note that for any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
,

(8)

\mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{y}\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}}\implies\varphi(\mathbf{x})\equiv\varphi(\mathbf{y})\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n-1}}.

Furthermore, if
\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{t})_{t}\in(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}-\mathbf{1})^{-1}\mathcal{O}
satisfying the condition (
6
), then we have

\{\langle\boldsymbol{\pi}^{j}\mathbf{x}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0\leq j\leq n-1\}=\{-\varphi^{(j)}(-\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0\leq j\leq n-1\}.

The identity above follows from expressing each
\mathbf{x}_{t}=m_{t}/(\pi_{t}^{n}-1)
where
0\leq|m_{t}|<|\pi_{t}^{n}-1|

(this means that
m_{t}
is a positive integer with
0\leq m_{t}<\pi_{t}^{n}-1
when
\mathcal{O}_{t}=\mathbb{Z}
or a polynomial in
A
with
\deg m_{t}<\deg(\pi_{t}^{n}-1)=\deg\pi_{t}^{n}
when
\mathcal{O}_{t}=A
), and observing that the element
\langle\pi_{t}\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle
(resp.
-\varphi_{t}(-{\mathbf{x}_{t}})
) in each component corresponds to shifting the
\pi_{t}
-adic digits of
m_{t}
one step right (resp. left).
So when
j
runs through
0
to
n-1
, two sets are in fact identical.
To conclude, the formula (
7
) can be rewritten as follows:
For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}\in(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}-\mathbf{1})^{-1}\mathcal{O}
satisfying the condition (
6
),

(9)

\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}\left(-\varphi^{(j)}(-{\mathbf{x}})\right)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}H\left(-\varphi^{(j)}(-{\mathbf{x}})\right).

To complete the proof of “
\Leftarrow
” of Theorem
1.7
, suppose that we are given any continuous non-vanishing function
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
. We claim that the following function

H(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{\Gamma}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\frac{G(\mathbf{x})}{G\left(-\varphi(-\mathbf{x})\right)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow K

is continuous, non-vanishing, and satisfies the identity (
9
), whence the proof would be completed.
To prove the claim above, we first note that the continuous and non-vanishing properties are clear from the definition of
H
. To show the formula (
9
), we further mention that for
\mathbf{x}\in(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}-\mathbf{1})^{-1}\mathcal{O}
with condition (
6
), we have

(10)

\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{G\left(-\varphi^{(j)}\left(-{\mathbf{x}}\right)\right)}{G\left(-\varphi^{(j+1)}\left(-{\mathbf{x}}\right)\right)}=1

as
-\varphi^{(n)}(-\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}
. Hence, the desired identity is satisfied.

2.2.
The “only if” Direction

We now proceed to prove the only if part of Theorem
1.7
.
For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{t})_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, we observe that
\mathbf{x}\in(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}-\mathbf{1})^{-1}\mathcal{O}
and
\mathbf{x}
satisfies (
6
) if and only if
\varphi^{(n)}(-\mathbf{x})=-\mathbf{x}
and
|\mathbf{x}_{t}|<1
for all
t
.
Let
F(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={H(\mathbf{x})}/{\mathbf{\Gamma}(\mathbf{x})}
, then using the reformulation (
9
), we need to show the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
be a continuous non-vanishing function. Suppose that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{t})_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
with
|\mathbf{x}_{t}|<1
for all
t
,

\varphi^{(n)}(-\mathbf{x})=-\mathbf{x}\implies\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}F\left(-\varphi^{(j)}(-\mathbf{x})\right)=1.

Then there exists a continuous non-vanishing function
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
such that

F(\mathbf{x})=\frac{G(\mathbf{x})}{G(-\varphi(-\mathbf{x}))}

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.

By replacing
F(\mathbf{x}),G(\mathbf{x})
with
F(-\mathbf{x}),G(-\mathbf{x})
respectively, Theorem
2.1
is equivalent to the following.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
be a continuous non-vanishing function.
Suppose that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{t})_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
with
|\mathbf{x}_{t}|<1
for all
t
,

(11)

\varphi^{(n)}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}\implies\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}F\left(\varphi^{(j)}(\mathbf{x})\right)=1.

Then there exists a continuous non-vanishing function
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
such that

F(\mathbf{x})=\frac{G(\mathbf{x})}{G(\varphi(\mathbf{x}))}

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{b}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(b_{t})_{t}\in\mathcal{O}
such that for each
t
,

(12)

\begin{cases}0\leq b_{t}<\pi_{t}-1,&\text{if }\mathcal{O}_{t}=\mathbb{Z},\\
|b_{t}|<|\pi_{t}-1|,&\text{if }\mathcal{O}_{t}=A.\end{cases}

Recall for any
\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{t})_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, we write

\mathbf{x}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{t,i}\pi_{t}^{i}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}

in
\pi_{t}
-adic expansion.
We set

\mathbf{x}_{(i)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(x_{t,i})_{t}\in\mathcal{O}\quad\text{so that}\quad\mathbf{x}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{x}_{(i)}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{i}.

Define
\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to\overline{\mathcal{O}}
by

(13)

\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}^{2n-1}}\left(\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{b}\boldsymbol{\pi}+\cdots+\mathbf{b}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n-2}+\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathbf{x}_{(i)}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{i}\right)\right),

and

(14)

\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}^{2n-2}}\left(\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{b}\boldsymbol{\pi}+\cdots+\mathbf{b}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n-2}+\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\mathbf{x}_{(i)}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{i}\right)\right).

We remark that
\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}
are locally constant and hence continuous on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
. Moreover, note that for any
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, we have

|\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})_{t}|<1\quad\text{and}\quad|\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})_{t}|<1

for all
t
by our choice of
b_{t}
(
12
), and

(15)

\varphi^{(2n-1)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)=\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi^{(2n-2)}\left(\beta_{n}\left(\varphi(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)=\beta_{n}\left(\varphi(\mathbf{x})\right).

Hence by (
11
), we obtain

\prod_{i=0}^{2n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)=1\quad\text{and}\quad\prod_{i=0}^{2n-3}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}\left(\varphi(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\right)=1.

By equating these two expressions, we have

F\left(\varphi^{(n-1)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)=\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{2n-3}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}\left(\varphi(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\right)}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\prod_{i=n}^{2n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)}.

By multiplying both the numerator and the denominator of the the right-hand side by
\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)
, we obtain

(16)

F\left(\varphi^{(n-1)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)=A_{n}(\mathbf{x})\cdot B_{n}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\frac{G_{n}(\mathbf{x})}{G_{n}\left(\varphi(\mathbf{x})\right)}

where

(17)

A_{n}(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)},\quad B_{n}(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\prod_{i=n-1}^{2n-3}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}\left(\varphi(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\right)}{\prod_{i=n}^{2n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)}

and

(18)

G_{n}(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left[\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\right]^{-1}.

The result now follows from the following lemma, which will be proved in the next section.

Lemma 2.3
.

The following statements hold.

(1)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}F\left(\varphi^{(n-1)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)=F(\mathbf{x}).

(2)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}A_{n}(\mathbf{x})=1.

(3)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}B_{n}(\mathbf{x})=1.

(4)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}G_{n}(\mathbf{x})=G(\mathbf{x})
, where
G
is a continuous non-vanishing function on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.

∎

2.3.
Proof of Lemma
2.3

In this section, we prove Lemma
2.3
.
In what follows, we fix a uniformizer
\mathfrak{v}
of
K
.

Claim 1
.

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}F\left(\varphi^{(n-1)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)=F(\mathbf{x}).

Proof.

Note that

\varphi^{(n-1)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\equiv\mathbf{x}\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}}.

Thus, we have that
\varphi^{(n-1)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\to\mathbf{x}
as
n\to\infty
. By the continuity of
F
, the result follows.
∎

Since
F
is continuous and non-vanishing on the compact set
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
which is a finite product of the compact sets
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}
), there exist
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
,x

(19)

\delta_{1}\leq\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak{v}}F(\mathbf{x})\leq\delta_{2}.

Moreover, since
F
is continuous on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, it is uniformly continuous. Hence, given
r>0
, there exists
N_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(20)

\mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{y}\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{N_{r}}}\implies F(\mathbf{x})\equiv F(\mathbf{y})\pmod{\mathfrak{v}^{r+\delta_{2}}}.

Claim 2
.

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}A_{n}(\mathbf{x})=1.

Proof.

Note that

\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\equiv\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{2n-2}}.

Thus by
(
8
)
, for
i=0,1,\ldots,n-2
, we have

\varphi^{(i)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\equiv\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}}.

So for
n\geq N_{r}
,

\varphi^{(i)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\equiv\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{N_{r}}}.

Then (
20
) implies that

F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\equiv F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\pmod{\mathfrak{v}^{r+\delta_{2}}}.

By (
19
) we know
\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak{v}}F(\mathbf{x})\leq\delta_{2}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, which shows that

a_{i,n}(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)}{F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)}\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{v}^{r}}.

Therefore, we obtain

A_{n}(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}a_{i,n}(\mathbf{x})\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{v}^{r}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Claim 3
.

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}B_{n}(\mathbf{x})=1.

Proof.

Note that

\varphi^{(n)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\equiv\varphi^{(n-1)}\left(\beta_{n}\left(\varphi(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{2n-2}}.

Thus by (
8
), for
i=0,1,\ldots,n-2
, we have

\varphi^{(n+i)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\equiv\varphi^{(n-1+i)}\left(\beta_{n}\left(\varphi(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}}.

The result now follows from a similar argument as in Claim
2
.
∎

Claim 4
.

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}G_{n}(\mathbf{x})=G(\mathbf{x})
where
G
is a continuous non-vanishing function on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof.

We first show that
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded away from zero. That is, there exist integers
M_{1},M_{2}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
,

M_{1}\leq\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak{v}}G_{n}(\mathbf{x})\leq M_{2}.

Note that by (
12
),

\left|\left(\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}}\right)_{t}\right|=\left|\frac{b_{t}}{1-\pi_{t}}\right|<1

for each
t
, and

\varphi\left(\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}}\right)=\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}}.

Thus by (
11
),

F\left(\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}}\right)=1.

By continuity of
F
, there exists a positive integer
N
such that for all
\mathbf{y}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
with

\mathbf{y}\equiv\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}}\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{N}},

we have

F(\mathbf{y})\equiv F\left(\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}}\right)=1\pmod{\mathfrak{v}}.

We note that in this case,
\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak{v}}F(\mathbf{y})=0
.

Now, notice that

\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\equiv\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}}\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n-1}}.

Hence by (
8
), for
i=0,1,\ldots,n-N-1
,

\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\equiv\varphi^{(i)}\left(\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}}\right)=\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}}\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{N}},

which implies that for
i=0,1,\ldots,n-N-1
,

\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak{v}}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)=0.

Therefore,

\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak{v}}G_{n}(\mathbf{x})=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak{v}}\left[\prod_{i=n-N}^{n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\right]^{-1}.

From (
19
) we know that for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
,

\delta_{1}\leq\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak{v}}F(\mathbf{x})\leq\delta_{2},

which implies that

-(N-1)\delta_{2}\leq\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak{v}}G_{n}(\mathbf{x})\leq-(N-1)\delta_{1}

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.
This shows that
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded away from zero.

Next, we show that
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges uniformly on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.
By the completeness of
K
together with non-Archimedean property, it suffices to show that
\{G_{n}-G_{n+1}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges uniformly to
0
on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.
Since
G_{n+1}
is non-vanishing, we have

G_{n}-G_{n+1}=G_{n+1}\left(\frac{G_{n}}{G_{n+1}}-1\right).

And since
G_{n+1}
is uniformly bounded, it is reduced to showing that given
r>0
, there exists a positive integer
N^{\prime}
such that for all
n\geq N^{\prime}
,

\frac{G_{n}(\mathbf{x})}{G_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})}\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{v}^{r}}

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.

From the definition of
G_{n}(\mathbf{x})
(see (
18
)), we have

\frac{G_{n}(\mathbf{x})}{G_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})}=\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)}=F\left(\beta_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})\right)\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i+1)}\left(\beta_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)}.

Recall that by (
11
), we have

F\left(\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}}\right)=1.

We have also seen that

\beta_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})\equiv\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{1}-\boldsymbol{\pi}}\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}}.

By the continuity of
F
, there is a positive integer
N^{\prime\prime}
such that for
n\geq N^{\prime\prime}
, we have

F\left(\beta_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})\right)\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{v}^{r}}.

On the other hand, note that

\varphi\left(\beta_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})\right)\equiv\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{2n-2}}.

Thus by (
8
), for
i=0,1,\ldots,n-2
, we have

\varphi^{(i+1)}\left(\beta_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})\right)\equiv\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\pmod{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{n}}.

So by (
20
), for all
n\geq N_{r}
and all
i=0,1,\ldots,n-2
,

F\left(\varphi^{(i+1)}\left(\beta_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\equiv F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\pmod{\mathfrak{v}^{r+\delta_{2}}}.

Since from (
19
),
\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak{v}}F(\mathbf{x})\leq\delta_{2}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, this implies that for all
n\geq N_{r}
and all
i=0,1,\ldots,n-2
,

\frac{F\left(\varphi^{(i+1)}\left(\beta_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)}{F\left(\varphi^{(i)}\left(\beta_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)}\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{v}^{r}}.

Therefore, for
n\geq\max\{N^{\prime\prime},N_{r}\}
,

\frac{G_{n}(\mathbf{x})}{G_{n+1}(\mathbf{x})}\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{v}^{r}}

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.
Hence, the desired property is proved.

Finally, one sees from (
18
) that each
G_{n}
is continuous, so
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to a continuous function
G
. Moreover, since
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded away from zero, the limit
G
is non-vanishing.
∎

3.
Generalization

Now, assume
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
is a direct product of finitely many rings
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}
, where each
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}
is the ring of integers of some non-Archimedean local field with a fixed uniformizer.
For each
t
, we let
\pi_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}
be a power of the given uniformizer and
S_{t}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}
be a complete set of representatives of the quotient ring
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}/(\pi_{t})
.
For
\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{t})_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, we write

\mathbf{x}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{t,i}\pi_{t}^{i}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t},

where
x_{t,i}\in S_{t}
for each
t
and
i
.
We similarly define

\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to\overline{\mathcal{O}}
by
\varphi(\mathbf{x})=(\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t}))_{t}
where each
\varphi_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}\to\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{t}
is given by

\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{t,i+1}\pi_{t}^{i}.

Then Theorem
1.7
can be extended to the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Given any complete non-Archimedean field
K
, let
\mathbf{\Gamma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
be a continuous non-vanishing function. Then
H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
is a continuous non-vanishing function satisfying for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
with
\varphi^{(n)}(-\mathbf{x})=-\mathbf{x}
,

\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}\left(-\varphi^{(j)}(-{\mathbf{x}})\right)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}H\left(-\varphi^{(j)}(-{\mathbf{x}})\right)

if and only if

H(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{\Gamma}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\frac{G(\mathbf{x})}{G\left(-\varphi(-\mathbf{x})\right)}

on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, where
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
is any continuous non-vanishing function.

Proof.

The “if” part follows immediately from the assumption that
\varphi^{(n)}(-\mathbf{x})=-\mathbf{x}
(cf. (
10
)).
For the “only if” part, let
F(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H(\mathbf{x})/\mathbf{\Gamma}(\mathbf{x})
.
Then by changing variables (cf. Theorem
2.1
and
2.2
), it suffices to prove the following statement:
Let
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
be a continuous non-vanishing function satisfying for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
,

\varphi^{(n)}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}\implies\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}F\left(\varphi^{(j)}(\mathbf{x})\right)=1.

Then there exists a continuous non-vanishing function
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to K
such that

F(\mathbf{x})=\frac{G(\mathbf{x})}{G(\varphi(\mathbf{x}))}

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.

Now, we choose any
b_{t}\in S_{t}
for each
t
and let
\mathbf{b}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(b_{t})\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.
Define
\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to\overline{\mathcal{O}}
as (
13
) and (
14
).
Then the same algebraic manipulation provides the equality (
16
):

F\left(\varphi^{(n-1)}\left(\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)=A_{n}(\mathbf{x})\cdot B_{n}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\frac{G_{n}(\mathbf{x})}{G_{n}\left(\varphi(\mathbf{x})\right)}

where
A_{n},B_{n},G_{n}
are defined as (
17
) and (
18
).
By almost the identical arguments, Lemma
2.3
still holds.
(The only difference is that, in Claim
4
, there is no condition on absolute values in the current situation.)
Therefore, the result follows in a similar manner.
∎

The authors note that, in the SI Appendix, page 8, under “Targeted whole-cell patch clamp,” the words “2 BAPTA” should be removed from this sentence “They were filled with a solution containing (in mM) 135 K-Gluconate, 6 NaCl, 2 MgCl2, 10 HEPES, 0.1 EGTA, 2 MgATP, 0.8 NaGTP, 2 BAPTA (pH 7.3-7.4, adjusted with KOH; osmolarity, 300 mOsm, adjusted with sucrose).” In addition, the authors note, “We used the same microscopy image in Fig. 2G Bottom panel and SI Appendix, Fig. S5D Top panel, as both are used to show that the mice injected with shPGC-1α had decreased PV expression, but emphasize separate purposes for the two figures. Fig. 2G Bottom panel is used to show there is a decrease in PV expression, and SI Appendix, Fig. S5D Top panel is used to show that this decrease in PV expression is only present within the site of injection, and not outside the site of injection. A line will be added to the figure legend in the SI Appendix document under the SI Appendix, Fig. S5D legend on page 20. This additional sentence will be added at the end as written: ‘The within the injection site panel (red box) is the same image as shown in Fig. 2G Bottom panel.’” The SI Appendix has been updated.

The authors also note that Fig. 5 appeared incorrectly. “There is an error in the model in Fig. 5. The PGC-1α symbol is depicted in the nerve injured condition, but it should be in the naive condition.” The updated figure appears below. The published paper has been updated.

General organization of PVN and PKCγ neuron relationship in normal and neuropathic pain conditions. Under naive conditions, PV in the PVNs chelates the free intracellular calcium ions, thereby inhibiting the activation of SK2 channels and preventing the influx of K+ ions. No PVN spike frequency adaption occurs which allows for inhibition of the polysynaptic pathway links between Aβ fibers to lamina I projection neurons. After nerve injury, a decrease in PV concentration results in an increase of the free intracellular calcium and activation of SK2 channels that allow for spike frequency adaptation of PVNs to appear. This adaption decreases their inhibition on PKCγ neurons enabling low-threshold inputs to drive lamina I projection neurons and causes mechanical allodynia.

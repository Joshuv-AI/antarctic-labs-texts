Explicit birational geometry of determinantal quartic 3-folds
Manuel Leal and César Lozano Huerta and Montserrat Vite

Abstract
. A general linear determinantal quartic in ℙ 4 \mathbb{P}^{4} is nodal, non- ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial and rational. We show that the family ℱ \mathcal{F} of such quartics also contains rational ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial quartics, and that a generic member of ℱ \mathcal{F} can specialize to a rational non- ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial double quadric. We prove that the birational geometry of these three types of 3-folds is governed by the extrinsic geometry of a curve C ⊂ ℙ 3 C\subset\mathbb{P}^{3} of degree 10 and genus 11.

Cubic hypersurfaces in

We take a similar approach in degree four. However, the intermediate Jacobian of a smooth quartic threefold is intricate, so we restrict to the following class of quartics.

A determinantal quartic in

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{H}_{10,11}
be either smooth ACM or general in the family
D_{1}
. Let

f:X\xrightarrow{|-K_{X}|}Y

be the anticanonical morphism. Then the exceptional locus of

is
Exc(f)=L
, the union of the 4-secant lines to
C

Proof.

In both cases, the anticanonical map
f
is base-point free (by the resolution (
2
) or Lemma
2.8
, respectively) and the 4-secant lines are contracted. Thus, we need to argue that no more curves are contracted.

Given
p\in X
, denote by
\mathcal{D}_{p}\subset|-K_{X}|
the sublinear system of divisors whose support contains

is base-point free, the codimension of
\mathcal{D}_{p}
in
|-K_{X}|
is 1 for every

.

Let
\gamma\subset X
be an irreducible and reduced curve contracted by
f
. Let us argue that
\gamma
must be a 4-secant line. We know that

-K_{X}\cdot\gamma=0
. In particular, if
p\in\gamma
is any point, then an element

contains

if and only if it contains
\gamma
. Thus, the locus
\{F\in|-K_{X}|:\gamma\subset F\}
is a 3-plane in
|-K_{X}|\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
, and we can choose two different elements
F,F^{\prime}\supset\gamma
in it.

The fact that
\gamma
is contained in the complete intersection

implies that the class
\gamma^{\prime}:=(-K_{X})^{2}-\gamma
is effective. Since

H\cdot\gamma=H\cdot(-K_{X})^{2}-H\cdot\gamma^{\prime}\leq H\cdot(-K_{X})^{2}=6.

On the other hand,

, which is absurd. Thus,
H\cdot\gamma>0
. If

\gamma
is a 4-secant line to

. We are left to prove that
2\leq H\cdot\gamma\leq 6
is impossible.

Denote
d:=H\cdot\gamma
. Observe from the equality
(4H-E)\cdot\gamma=0
that
(4H-E)\cdot\gamma^{\prime}=(4H-E)^{3}=4
. From this we deduce the identities

H\cdot\gamma^{\prime}=6-d\qquad E\cdot\gamma^{\prime}=4(5-d).

Fix a smooth surface
F\in|-K_{X}|
containing
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
. In

, we have that
\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}=(4H-E)|_{F}
. Therefore

\gamma^{2}-(\gamma^{\prime})^{2}=(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})\cdot(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})=-(4H-E)\cdot\gamma^{\prime}=-4.

Since
F
is a K3 surface, this leads to the following identity involving the arithmetic genus of
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
:

p_{a}(\gamma)=p_{a}(\gamma^{\prime})-2.

If

and
p_{a}(\gamma)=p_{a}(\gamma^{\prime})-2<0
, which is a contradiction since
\gamma
is reduced and irreducible. If

then
p_{a}(\gamma)=0
. Therefore,
p_{a}(\gamma^{\prime})=2
.

Let us argue that
\gamma^{\prime}
is contained in a quadric surface. First, we prove that
\gamma^{\prime}
is reduced. Otherwise, we can write its divisor class in

as
\gamma^{\prime}=2c,2\ell_{1}+2\ell_{2}
or

\ell_{i}
(i=1,2,3) denote the classes of an irreducible conic and a line, respectively. In

we have

\displaystyle(2c)^{2}

\displaystyle=-8,

\displaystyle(2\ell_{1}+2\ell_{2})

\displaystyle=-16+8\ell_{1}\cdot\ell_{2}=-16\textrm{ or }-8,

\displaystyle(2\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3})^{2}

\displaystyle=-12+4\ell_{1}\cdot\ell_{2}+4\ell_{1}\cdot\ell_{3}+2\ell_{2}\cdot\ell_{3}=-12,-8,-6,-4,\textrm{ or }-2

depending on wether or not the lines intersect each other. In any of these cases, the possible self-intersection numbers are different from
(\gamma^{\prime})^{2}=2.
Consequently,
\gamma^{\prime}
must be reduced.

Observe that
\gamma^{\prime}
is connected: otherwise
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime}_{1}\sqcup\gamma^{\prime}_{2}
with
p_{a}(\gamma^{\prime}_{i})\leq 3
. This would lead to
p_{a}(\gamma^{\prime})=p_{a}(\gamma^{\prime}_{1})+p_{a}(\gamma^{\prime}_{2})-1\leq 1
, which is a contradiction.

Finally, the decomposition into irreducible components of
\gamma^{\prime}
must fall in (at least) one of the following cases:

a)

\gamma^{\prime}
is irreducible,

b)

\gamma^{\prime}=\ell\cup q
, where
\ell
is a line,

is a degree 3 irreducible curve, and
\ell\cap q\neq\emptyset
,

c)

\gamma^{\prime}=c_{1}\cup c_{2}
is a union of irreducible conics with
c_{1}\cap c_{2}\neq\emptyset
,

d)

\gamma=c\cup\ell_{1}\cup\ell_{2}
with
c\cap\ell_{1},\ell_{1}\cap\ell_{2}\neq\emptyset
,

e)

\gamma^{\prime}=c\cup\ell_{1}\cup\ell_{2}
with
c\cap\ell_{1},c\cap\ell_{2}\neq\emptyset
,

f)

\gamma^{\prime}=\ell_{1}\cup\ell_{2}\cup\ell_{3}\cup\ell_{4}
with
\ell_{i}\cap\ell_{i+1}\neq\emptyset
for

, or

g)

\gamma^{\prime}=\ell_{1}\cup\ell_{2}\cup\ell_{3}\cup\ell_{4}
with
\ell_{i}\cap\ell_{4}\neq\emptyset
for

.

In each of these cases, we can choose nine points on

where each degree

component contains at least

Now, a smooth quadric has no genus 2 curves of degree 4; a degree 4 curve in a conic cone has genus 1; and a degree 4 curve in a double plane is either a plane quartic (genus 3), a double conic (genus 1), or a line plus a triple line (genus 3). This is a contradiction.

∎

The double quadric

is branched along a degree 8 surface with twenty nodes in a special position.

Proof.

Observe that

If

is general,

is smooth. Each 4-secant line

to

Therefore,

is an irreducible surface of degree 8 with twenty singular double points.

Note that the defect of

is
\rho(Y)=1

A double quadric

as above is ramified along a surface with twenty nodes that fail to impose independent conditions on cubics by Proposition

.
Double quadrics with these twenty nodes in general position exist, and are birationally rigid

A multiplicity 2 quadric

.
However, the anti-canonical model of

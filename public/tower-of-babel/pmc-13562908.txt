Compound Heterozygous PCDH15 Variants Associated With Cone‐Rod Dystrophy in a Chinese Pedigree
Authors: Lei Zhang, Qianfeng Wang, Haiyan Wang, Wei Jia, Bowen Zhang, Ru Wang, Wei Qiang, Shuwei Bai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundThis study aimed to characterize the clinical and genetic features of a Chinese family with cone‐rod dystrophy in which compound heterozygous PCDH15 variants were identified.MethodsA Chinese pedigree with autosomal recessive cone‐rod dystrophy was investigated. A comprehensive ophthalmic assessment was performed in the proband, a 42‐year‐old woman, together with genetic evaluation of her family members. Candidate variants were identified using whole‐exome sequencing and subsequently assessed by Sanger sequencing and family segregation analysis.ResultsOphthalmoscopic examination revealed pigmentary changes and atrophic lesions affecting the posterior pole and peripapillary area bilaterally. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) demonstrated bilateral outer retinal layer atrophy with disruption of the ellipsoid zone at the posterior pole. Multifocal electroretinography (mfERG) revealed attenuated central responses, while full‐field electroretinography (ffERG) documented a more pronounced reduction in cone‐mediated (photopic) responses. Two novel compound heterozygous variants in PCDH15, namely c.4903_4906del (p.Glu1635Lysfs*4) and c.3470C>A (p.Ala1157Glu), were identified in this autosomal recessive cone‐rod dystrophy pedigree. Family co‐segregation analysis provided supportive evidence for their potential association with the disease phenotype. Cross‐species analysis revealed high evolutionary conservation of the PCDH15 protein. Three‐dimensional structural modeling predicted potential alterations in protein structure.ConclusionTo our knowledge, this is the first report describing an association between compound heterozygous PCDH15 variants and cone‐rod dystrophy, thereby providing preliminary evidence that may broaden the mutational spectrum associated with this gene.

Introduction

The PCDH15 gene encodes protocadherin 15, a transmembrane glycoprotein belonging to the cadherin superfamily, which is characterized by calcium‐mediated intercellular adhesion properties (Murcia and Woychik 2001). This protein serves critical functions in establishing neuronal connections and synaptic architecture. Studies have demonstrated PCDH15 expression across multiple tissues including neural tissue, cochlear structures, retinal photoreceptors, and Müller glial cells. Functionally, the protein is essential for maintaining the structural organization and mechanosensory capabilities of auditory hair cells within the cochlea (Ahmed et al. 2008). Within ocular tissues, PCDH15 contributes to the developmental organization of ciliary structures and supports photoreceptor viability and function (Ahmed et al. 2008). Biallelic mutations in PCDH15 have been established as causative for isolated sensorineural hearing impairment (DFNB23) and the combined hearing‐vision disorder known as Usher syndrome type 1F (USH1F) (Ahmed et al. 2003).

Current literature, including data from the Human Gene Mutation Database (HGMD) and previous studies, documents diverse PCDH15 mutational types: genomic copy number alterations, loss‐of‐function single nucleotide changes, and small insertion/deletion events, predominantly characterized in USH1F patients of European ancestry (Sethna et al. 2021). Notably, systematic investigation of PCDH15 variants remains limited in the Chinese population (Chen et al. 2015). To the best of our knowledge, cone‐rod dystrophy has not previously been linked to PCDH15‐associated retinal disease. In this study, whole‐exome sequencing was used to identify candidate variants in a non‐consanguineous Chinese family. We identified a patient carrying novel compound heterozygous PCDH15 variants presumed to be associated with this retinopathy, presenting with isolated retinal manifestations in the absence of auditory impairment or other syndromic features.

Methods

Subjects

A non‐consanguineous Chinese family was ascertained through Xi'an People's Hospital (Xi'an Fourth Hospital), Shaanxi Province, China. The pedigree comprised six members, including one clinically affected individual and five unaffected relatives. Institutional review board approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Xi'an People's Hospital (Xi'an Fourth Hospital), and all procedures adhered to the ethical standards outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from each participant prior to enrollment.

Clinical Evaluation

Comprehensive clinical assessment of the proband included documentation of the medical, ocular, and family history, together with a complete ophthalmic examination. The diagnostic workup incorporated color fundus imaging, spectral‐domain optical coherence tomography (OCT), OCT angiography (OCTA), fundus autofluorescence (FAF) imaging, full‐field electroretinography (ffERG), and multifocal electroretinography (mfERG).

Exome Sequencing and Validation

For molecular characterization, genomic DNA from the proband (II:1) and available family members underwent genetic analysis. Exome enrichment utilized the SureSelectXT Human All Exon V6 Kit (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA) per manufacturer specifications. Sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 system (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). Bioinformatic processing encompassed local realignment, quality assessment, and variant detection through the Genome Analysis Toolkit (GATK) pipeline (McKenna et al. 2010). Sequence alignment to the reference human genome (UCSC hg19/GRCh37) employed the Burrows‐Wheeler Aligner (BWA). Single nucleotide variants (SNVs), small insertions/deletions (InDels), and copy number variations (CNVs) were identified using GATK algorithms (McKenna et al. 2010; De Summa et al. 2017; Li 2011).

Variant annotation was performed via ANNOVAR (http://annovar.openbioinformatics.org/), with pathogenicity assessment incorporating Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM), Human Gene Mutation Database (HGMD), ClinVar resources, American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) classification criteria, and clinical correlation. Functional impact prediction was conducted using SIFT (Sorting Intolerant from Tolerant), PolyPhen‐2, and MutationTaster algorithms. Candidate variants underwent confirmation via bidirectional Sanger sequencing. Nomenclature adhered to Human Genome Variation Society (HGVS) standards (https://varnomen.hgvs.org/).

Results

Clinical Characteristics of the Cone‐Rod Dystrophy Family

The proband presented with a multi‐year history of gradual visual deterioration accompanied by recent onset epiphora and ocular discomfort of unclear etiology. Best‐corrected visual acuity was 0.06 (OD) and 0.1 (OS). Anterior segment examination and intraocular pressure were within normal limits. Fundoscopic findings were characteristic of cone‐rod dystrophy, demonstrating pigmentary mottling with atrophic changes at the posterior pole and peripapillary region bilaterally, featuring brownish pigmentary alterations at the fovea (Figure 1A). FAF imaging demonstrated reduced autofluorescence throughout the posterior pole, particularly in the macular region, bordered by a hyperautofluorescent ring with central foveal hyperautofluorescence (Figure 1B). OCT revealed bilateral outer retinal atrophy and ellipsoid zone disruption at the posterior pole, with hyperreflective deposits above the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) at the fovea producing underlying shadowing (Figure 1C). OCTA demonstrated choriocapillaris attenuation in the macular region (Figure 1D). ffERG showed diminished scotopic and photopic responses, with preferential cone system involvement. The P1 response density at macular in ring 1 on mfERG was reduced (Figure 1E). Complete color vision loss and dark adaptation impairment were documented. Pure tone audiometry and auditory brainstem response were normal, indicating that the proband had normal hearing (Figure 1F). Remaining family members exhibited normal fundoscopic findings. Variant Analysis

To determine the genetic etiology of cone‐rod dystrophy in this family, the proband (Subject II:1) underwent whole exome sequencing. Initial analysis generated 182,028 variants. Quality filtering (score threshold < 300) retained 704 variant sites. Restriction to exonic and splice‐site regions yielded 696 variants. Population allele frequency data from the 1000 Genomes (1000G), ExAC, gnomAD, and gnomAD_EAS databases were used to exclude variants with an allele frequency above 0.1%, and 279 candidate variants were retained. Computational prediction of functional consequences using SIFT, PolyPhen‐2, and MutationTaster, combined with cross‐referencing against OMIM and ClinVar for retinal disease associations, identified two candidate variants (Table 2).

The analysis revealed compound heterozygous variants in PCDH15: NM_033056.4: exon33: c.4903_4906del (p.Glu1635Lysfs*4) and NM_033056.4: exon26: c.3470C>A (p.Ala1157Glu). For c.3470C>A (p.Ala1157Glu), the following criteria were applied: PM2 (absent from controls or at extremely low frequency if recessive in Exome Sequencing Project, 1000 Genomes Project, or Exome Aggregation Consortium). The variant's REVEL score (0.495) fell within the intermediate range between benign and pathogenic classifications, indicating uncertain clinical significance. Given these findings, the variant remained classified as a variant of uncertain significance (VUS) due to lack of functional studies. For c.4903_4906del (p.Glu1635Lysfs*4), PVS1_Strong (frameshift in a gene where loss‐of‐function was a known mechanism, and variant was located in the terminal exon, not predicted to undergo nonsense mediated mRNA decay, and variant removes > 10% of the protein product) and PM2 were applied, resulting in classification as likely pathogenic. Evolutionary conservation analysis across vertebrate species revealed high conservation of the alanine residue at position 1157 (p.Ala1157Glu), whereas the glutamate at position 1635 (p.Glu1635Lysfs*4) showed limited conservation (Figure 2C).

Protein Model Construction

Bioinformatic analysis using SIFT and PolyPhen‐2 predicted that the c.4903_4906del (p.Glu1635Lysfs*4) variant, mapping to the terminal exon, generated a truncated protein lacking more than 10% of the full‐length sequence. The c.3470C>A (p.Ala1157Glu) variant represented a heterozygous missense substitution resulting in an alanine‐to‐glutamic acid substitution at residue 1157. Structural modeling demonstrated that c.4903_4906del (p.Glu1635Lysfs*4) produced cytoplasmic domain truncation, while c.3470C>A (p.Ala1157Glu), located within the extracellular region, induced conformational alterations (Figure 3).

Discussion

Through integrated whole‐exome sequencing and Sanger sequencing approaches, we identified two previously unreported compound heterozygous PCDH15 variants, c.4903_4906del and c.3470C>A, in a Chinese family with autosomal recessive cone‐rod dystrophy. These variants were presumed to be associated with the disease phenotype observed in this pedigree. Neither sequence alteration has been documented in major public human genome variation repositories, including ClinVar and gnomAD. Furthermore, the affected amino acid position (p.Ala1157Glu) showed strong evolutionary conservation across diverse vertebrate lineages, and the c.4903_4906del variant was predicted to result in a protein truncation exceeding 10% of the total polypeptide length. Family co‐segregation analysis revealed genotype–phenotype concordance within the pedigree: the proband harbored both variants in a compound heterozygous configuration, whereas both parents, two younger brothers, and one younger sister each carried a single heterozygous variant and remained clinically asymptomatic. These observations substantiate an autosomal recessive mode of inheritance, consistent with our pedigree‐based genetic analysis. Additionally, structural modeling predicted potential conformational changes, although the functional consequences of these variants remain uncertain and require experimental validation.

Notably, our proband retained normal auditory function, suggesting that compound heterozygous PCDH15 variants may selectively affect retinal tissues while sparing cochlear structures. (Yang et al. 2024) previously documented a Chinese family presenting with autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa (arRP) manifestations attributed to compound heterozygous PCDH15 variants; however, the ocular phenotype characterized in that report differed substantially from our observations. In the typical clinical course, patients with retinitis pigmentosa or Usher syndrome develop macular pathology and secondary cone photoreceptor dysfunction only during advanced disease stages (Falsini et al. 2021). In contrast, our patient's ffERG demonstrated a combined reduction in both scotopic and photopic response amplitudes, with preferential and more pronounced impairment observed under photopic testing conditions. The mfERG findings similarly indicated significant macular dysfunction with a reduced central response density. Concurrently, the documented deficits in color discrimination and dark adaptation reflected functional abnormalities affecting both cone and rod photoreceptor systems. Collectively, these electrophysiological findings and macular imaging results aligned consistently with a cone‐rod dystrophy pattern characterized primarily by cone system involvement. Our observations diverged from previous studies indicating that PCDH15 variants typically lead to sensorineural hearing impairment, absent vestibular reflexes, and progressive retinal degeneration (Xu et al. 2017; Ivanchenko et al. 2024; Castiglione and Möller 2022). To our knowledge, this represents the first report suggesting a genotype–phenotype association between PCDH15 variants and cone‐rod dystrophy.

PCDH15 is a member of the calcium‐dependent cadherin cell adhesion molecule superfamily (Ahmed et al. 2003, 2001). In higher vertebrates, protocadherins are recognized to participate in diverse cellular processes, including neural circuit assembly and synaptogenesis (Suzuki 2000). The human PCDH15 gene spans 35 exons and encodes multiple protein isoforms, each comprising 3 to 11 extracellular cadherin domains (ECs), a single transmembrane segment, and a carboxy‐terminal cytoplasmic domain (CD) (Ahmed et al. 2008). These structural components collectively generate three principal isoforms—PCDH15‐CD1, PCDH15‐CD2, and PCDH15‐CD3—that differ in their cytoplasmic domain composition (Ahmed et al. 2008; Yusuf et al. 2022). The differential expression of these isoforms is mediated through mutually exclusive alternative splicing events involving exons 35, 38, and 39, respectively (Yusuf et al. 2022). Previous investigations have established genotype–phenotype correlations for pathogenic PCDH15 variants: missense mutations typically cause isolated hearing loss (DFNB23), whereas null alleles—including nonsense, splice‐site, frameshift, and large deletion mutations—result in Usher syndrome type 1F (Yusuf et al. 2022; Jaijo et al. 2012; Aller et al. 2010; Yang et al. 2023). The mechanistic basis for this differential genotype–phenotype relationship may reside in the greater vulnerability of inner ear hair cells to perturbations in PCDH15 function compared with retinal photoreceptor cells. Nevertheless, no specific mutational hotspots have been definitively delineated within this gene to date.

The variant residue identified in this study (p.Ala1157Glu) demonstrated high evolutionary conservation across vertebrate species and localized to the extracellular domain. This mutation‐induced conformational alteration in the cadherin structure may disrupt molecular trafficking and material transport between photoreceptor outer segments (OS) and inner segments (IS). The c.4903_4906del variant, situated within exon 33, was predicted to result in truncation of the cytoplasmic domain. (Yusuf et al. 2022) previously noted relatively poor sequence conservation in the cytoplasmic domain compared with the extracellular domain of PCDH15. (Perreault‐Micale et al. 2014) conducted systematic PCDH15 sequence variant screening across 14,000 normal individuals and identified 52 subjects harboring truncating variants within the cytoplasmic domain. A notably elevated variant frequency was observed specifically in exon 33, prompting the hypothesis that truncating variants in this genomic region may be tolerated through alternative splicing mechanisms generating different cytoplasmic domain isoforms, or alternatively may exhibit functional redundancy (Yusuf et al. 2022; Perreault‐Micale et al. 2014). However, truncating variants affecting the proximal cytoplasmic domain have also been documented in genetically confirmed USH1F patients (Yusuf et al. 2022). In our proband, a frameshift variant (c.4903_4906del) located in a terminal exon—which may allow escape from nonsense‐mediated mRNA decay but could still impair the cytoplasmic domain—occurred in trans with a missense variant. Although the missense variant (c.3470C>A) showed strong segregation evidence and supportive computational predictions, it remained classified as a VUS. The compound heterozygous genotype was observed in the affected individual but not in (the unaffected) relatives. Moreover, the specific location of this truncation (exon 33) may affect isoform‐specific expression of PCDH15 cytoplasmic domains; although some truncations in this region may be tolerated due to alternative splicing, the combination of this missense variant on the opposite allele may contribute to the phenotypic manifestation. Further functional studies are needed to confirm the variant‐phenotype association. A recently reported Chinese family with isolated retinal RP manifestations caused by compound heterozygous PCDH15 variants similarly exhibited both protein conformational abnormalities attributable to extracellular domain mutations and cytoplasmic domain truncation (Yang et al. 2024). The mechanism by which cochlear function remains preserved in patients carrying these hypofunctional domain mutations warrants further investigation. It would be particularly interesting to determine whether other variants mapping to this exon also produce a similar cone‐predominant phenotype manifesting as macular involvement, or whether such phenotypic effects are specific to these particular variant sites.

Several genes previously associated with syndromes can cause isolated retinal phenotypes. For example, variants in the KLHL7 gene, which causes PERCHING syndrome, and variants in the IQCB1 gene associated with Senior‐Loken syndrome, have also been found to cause non‐syndromic RP when present in the homozygous state (Colombo et al. 2021). Variants in USH2A, another established causative gene for Usher syndrome, can also cause isolated RP (Lenassi et al. 2015). Two previously reported cases involving variants in USH2A displayed cone‐dominant or cone‐rod dystrophy patterns rather than the classic rod‐cone dystrophy typically associated with this condition (Bhatter et al. 2025; Fakin et al. 2019). Although these variants were mapped to different exons, both were localized within the fibronectin Type III domain (Bhatter et al. 2025). This observation suggested that genetic variants affecting distinct regions of the protein may differentially impact rod versus cone photoreceptor cell populations. Fan et al. reported the initial case of non‐syndromic retinitis pigmentosa attributed to variants in CDH23—another Usher syndrome‐associated gene. The affected patient demonstrated severe functional impairment of both cone and rod photoreceptor systems (Fan et al. 2025). CDH23 and PCDH15 are both cadherin superfamily members that serve as essential core components of tip links in the cochlea (Richardson and Petit 2019). Within retinal tissue, their intercellular adhesive functions serve to connect photoreceptor calyceal processes and anchor them to outer segment discs (Yusuf et al. 2022). Consequently, further investigation is required to elucidate the precise mechanisms through which these variants contribute to retinal disease pathogenesis. Longitudinal assessment of the proband's auditory and visual function is warranted.

Conclusions

We report the identification of novel compound heterozygous PCDH15 variants (c.4903_4906del and c.3470C>A). These findings suggest that cone‐rod dystrophy may represent a previously unrecognized phenotype associated with PCDH15 variants. These observations may inform future genetic evaluation and counseling; however, additional cases and functional studies may be needed.

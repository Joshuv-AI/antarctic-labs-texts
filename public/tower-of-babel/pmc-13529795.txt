Risk-aware multiple-choice question answering with conformal P-values
Authors: Wenwen Fu, Yuanchang Ye, Ganggang Yang, Yanwen Wei, Lina Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Multiple-choice question answering (MCQA) systems are often required to act on uncertain evidence while still providing a concise decision to a user. We introduce a risk-aware MCQA interface that converts normalized option probabilities into conformal prediction sets, allowing the system to retain every answer option supported at a user-selected risk level. The option-wise conformal p-value provides an interpretable rank-based view of this decision. Across five instruction-tuned language models and common-question subsets of MMLU and MMLU-Pro, the procedure is evaluated with 100 random 1:1 calibration–test splits at target miscoverage levels \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha \in \{0.05,0.1,0.2\}$$\end{document}. At α =0.05, mean empirical miscoverage ranges from 0.0390 to 0.0399 on MMLU and from 0.0469 to 0.0487 on MMLU-Pro; the same target-tracking pattern persists at the larger operating points and across diverse subjects. Prediction sets contract smoothly as the allowable risk increases, while the more demanding MMLU-Pro questions retain broader sets. These results position conformal p-values as a practical reliability layer for MCQA systems that must communicate calibrated ambiguity rather than conceal it behind a single top-ranked answer.

Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used in information systems that answer questions, rank alternatives, and support human decisions. In these settings, fluent output alone is not an adequate reliability signal: incorrect or poorly calibrated answers can propagate directly into downstream choices1–3. Verbalized confidence, semantic uncertainty, and the faithfulness with which models communicate uncertainty therefore require separate empirical attention4–6. Sampling-based consistency checks and semantic-entropy methods offer valuable diagnostics, yet a decision system also needs a principled way to translate uncertainty into an actionable answer set7,8.

Reliability can be strengthened through grounding and post hoc revision. Retrieval-augmented generation, research-and-revision, and self-reflective retrieval methods supply evidence or refine generated content9–11. Decoding-time contrast, verification chains, and tool-interactive critique provide complementary ways to improve response quality12–14. A complementary design question remains: when an MCQA system must choose among a fixed set of options, how can it expose the alternatives that remain credible at a specified risk level?

In MCQA, returning a single top-ranked option can conceal material ambiguity among candidate answers. A set-valued output instead retains the options that remain statistically plausible. Conformal prediction provides a distribution-free route to finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability15,16; set-valued classification further formalizes the trade-off between coverage and the number of retained labels17,18. This perspective turns uncertainty into a visible interface element that can guide review, evidence retrieval, or escalation.

This paper develops a risk-aware MCQA layer based on split conformal prediction. This rank-based form makes the evidence for retaining an option explicit while preserving the familiar split-conformal decision rule. Related work

Conformal classification and risk control

Conformal prediction constructs uncertainty sets with marginal coverage under exchangeability15,16. Recent classification methods improve the efficiency or conditional behavior of prediction sets through adaptive scores and conformalized training18–20. Our formulation builds on this foundation and concentrates on how a probability-based split-conformal rule can be expressed as an option-wise decision interface for MCQA.

Related work broadens the target from ordinary miscoverage to more general risks. Risk-controlling prediction sets, learn-then-test calibration, and conformal risk control study calibrated control of broader loss functions or operating points21–23. Other work considers pointwise loss control and adaptive operation under distribution shift24,25. Together, these lines of work frame calibrated MCQA as a component of a broader risk-aware information-system design.

Conformal methods for language models

Several studies adapt conformal ideas to language-model outputs. Conformal Language Modeling constructs sets of sampled generations, while conformal factuality connects coverage to entailment-based uncertainty sets26,27. API Is Enough and ConU develop alternatives for settings without direct logit access or with open-ended outputs28,29. The finite-option MCQA setting considered here offers a complementary regime in which every retained option can be presented directly to a user.

Other recent work focuses on selecting or filtering trustworthy LLM outputs, improving validity, or handling uncertainty outliers30–32. Abbasi-Yadkori et al. separate epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty when assessing whether an LLM response should be believed33. The p-value representation used here complements these efforts by making the calibration rank of each MCQA option directly available to the decision layer.

Benchmarks, models, and application context

MMLU measures multi-task language understanding across 57 subjects34. MMLU-Pro was introduced as a more challenging benchmark with broader distractor sets and more reasoning-focused questions35. We evaluate Qwen2.5 and Llama-family models documented in their technical reports36,37. The comparison also includes Vicuna and OpenChat, which represent established instruction-tuned model lines38,39. Matched question sets ensure that observed differences reflect model behavior under the same calibration design.

The prediction-set interface is especially relevant when an information system should surface ambiguity for human review. MCQGen illustrates the growing role of LLM-supported multiple-choice-question generation in personalized learning40; conformal sets offer a natural quality-control signal for such downstream decisions. TrumorGPT likewise highlights the importance of calibrated uncertainty alongside retrieval and graph-based reasoning in health fact-checking41. Probabilistic safety regions provide a complementary risk-aware classification perspective based on scalable classifiers42. Experiments

Experimental protocol

We evaluate Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Vicuna-7B-v1.5, and OpenChat-3.5. Each comparison uses the question identifiers shared by all five models within a subject: 2,600 MMLU questions across 16 subjects and 6,759 MMLU-Pro questions across 14 subjects. The MMLU-Pro evaluation preserves the candidate labels and option count of every question, spanning three to ten options in the processed benchmark records.

For every subject, 100 random 1:1 calibration–test splits characterize both average behavior and split-to-split variation. We focus on target miscoverage levels \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha \in \{0.05,0.1,0.2\}$$\end{document}, which represent practically relevant operating points for risk-sensitive MCQA. For each option, the normalized probability is obtained from the model’s next-token distribution. Twenty sampled responses per prompt are retained for response-format diagnostics, while the conformal score itself is computed from the direct option probabilities.

Discussion

Interpretation for information systems

The central value of the approach is to turn a model score into a decision object that an information system can act on. When the set contains multiple options, the system can surface ambiguity, request additional evidence, retrieve supporting context, or route the question for review instead of presenting a single answer with unwarranted finality. In this sense, the conformal layer complements retrieval, verification, and task-specific safeguards: it determines how much ambiguity should remain visible at a chosen operating point.

The application contexts in “Related work” illustrate how this interface can be integrated into broader workflows. In educational question-generation pipelines such as MCQGen, it can flag answers whose option set remains broad before they are presented to learners. In retrieval-supported fact-checking systems such as TrumorGPT, it can provide a calibrated decision signal alongside retrieved evidence and graph-based reasoning. The contribution is therefore not a replacement for domain knowledge, but a reusable statistical layer for communicating when a multiple-choice decision remains ambiguous.

Scope of the guarantee

The coverage statement has a precise operational scope: calibration and subsequent use are considered under the same exchangeable data-generating regime. This is the standard setting in which split conformal prediction offers finite-sample marginal coverage. When an information system is adapted to a changing population, prompt policy, model version, or retrieval environment, calibration should be refreshed and evaluated for that operating regime. Adaptive and shift-aware conformal methods provide promising extensions for such settings24,25.

The matched-question design supports a focused comparison of the five models: every reported difference is evaluated on the same questions within each subject. This design places the emphasis on calibrated decision behavior rather than on benchmark ranking. As LLM evaluation research has emphasized, conclusions can depend on prompts, data provenance, and evaluation protocols43; transparent calibration design is therefore central to interpreting MCQA reliability results.

Finally, the use of direct option probabilities separates the conformal decision rule from stochastic generation variability. The sampled responses support response-format diagnostics, while the calibrated candidate sets are determined by the model’s option-level probability distribution. This separation makes the evaluation target explicit and keeps the reported risk-control behavior tied to a well-defined MCQA decision rule.

Conclusion

This study presents conformal p-values as a risk-aware decision interface for multiple-choice question answering. Across five instruction-tuned LLMs, 30 subjects, and 100 random calibration–test splits per subject, the resulting candidate sets track selected risk levels while exposing the expected validity–efficiency trade-off. The subject-level analyses show that this behavior extends beyond aggregate averages across both MMLU and MMLU-Pro. By making calibrated ambiguity visible at the option level, the framework offers an actionable reliability layer for information systems that must decide when a single answer is sufficient and when several candidates should remain under consideration.

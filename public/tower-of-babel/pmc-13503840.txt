Amorphous metal-oxide semiconductor thin-film neuron, synapse, and neuromorphic system
Authors: Mutsumi Kimura, Yuji Miyato, Michihiro Shintani, Mami N. Fujii, Yusaku Magari, Hidenori Kawanishi, Tokiyoshi Matsuda, Juan Paolo S. Bermundo, Takanori Takahashi, Yukiharu Uraoka, Takumi Kuwahara, Reon Oshio, Yasuhiko Nakashima, Yi-Chien Chen, Chung-Tien Chiu, Chih-Lung Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Artificial intelligence will spread exponentially and requires compact size and energy efficiency, for which one solution is neuromorphic systems. On the other hand, thin-film semiconductor technologies have spread widely and feature as giant-micro electronics. In this study, an amorphous metal-oxide semiconductor (AOS) thin-film neuron, synapse, and neuromorphic system are researched and developed. First, an AOS thin-film transistor (TFT) is fabricated and evaluated experimentally, and the experiment results are reproduced by a simulation model. A thin-film neuron composed of the AOS TFTs is designed, fabricated, and confirmed to work in a circuit simulation and experimentally, and this behavior is desirable for the thin-film neuron. Next, an AOS thin-film memristor and AOS thin-film Schottky diode are fabricated and evaluated experimentally, these characteristics are desirable for them, and the experiment results are reproduced by a simulation model. Thin-film synapses composed of the AOS thin-film memristors and Schottky diodes are designed, fabricated, and confirmed to work in a circuit simulation using simulation models of the AOS TFT and AOS thin-film Schottky diode, and the simulation results are reproduced by a fitting model. Lastly, a neuromorphic system composed of the thin-film neurons and synapses is designed, fabricated, and confirmed to work in a logic simulation. Training is executed using circuit-aware training based on the circuit behavior and training images of the MNIST, inference is performed, and the recognition accuracy achieves 94.1%. Finally, it is concluded that the AOS thin-film neuron, synapse, and neuromorphic system are feasible.

Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) is expected to continue to spread exponentially in the foreseeable era1,2, but the infrastructure size and energy consumption are feared to be enormous3,4, and hence compact size and energy efficiency is required5,6, for which one solution is a qualitative transformation from software to hardware, namely, neuromorphic systems7–12. On the one hand, thin-film semiconductor technologies have spread widely to domain-specific electronics13, such as flat-panel displays14, energy generations15, and notably they feature stacked structures16, exceptional electrical properties17, and so forth, as giant-micro electronics18. Particularly, amorphous metal-oxide semiconductors (AOS) have these properties most prominently19,20.

Neuromorphic systems were reported as individual components in silicon circuits21,22, metal-oxide semiconductors23,24, organic semiconductors25, two-dimensional semiconductors26, ferroelectric27, chalcogenide28, spintronics materials29, and so on. However, it is extremely rare that neuromorphic systems are integrated for all functional components from a single material30. On the other hand, as mentioned above, thin-film semiconductor technologies and AOS feature stacked structures and exceptional electrical properties. Thus, all needed components can be integrated, and advantages of giant-micro electronics can be offered. Since all needed components can be integrated, we can clearly avoid issues concerning the memory wall31, which is caused by the speed difference between processor and memory units, and Neumann bottleneck32, which is caused by the slow speed of data transfer between them. Therefore, AI will become more widespread and further expand its capabilities and application domains through neuromorphic systems based on AOS33.

In this study, an AOS thin-film neuron, synapse, and neuromorphic system are researched and developed in circuit simulations and actual experiments. First, an AOS thin-film transistor (TFT) will be investigated in an actual experiment, and a thin-film neuron composed of the AOS TFTs will be designed and confirmed to work in a circuit simulation and actual experiment. Next, an AOS thin-film memristor and AOS thin-film Schottky diode will be investigated in an actual experiment, and thin-film synapses composed of the AOS thin-film memristors and Schottky diodes will be designed and confirmed to work in a circuit simulation. Lastly, a neuromorphic system composed of the thin-film neurons and synapses will be investigated and confirmed to work in a logic simulation. Finally, it will be concluded that the AOS thin-film neuron, synapse, and neuromorphic system are available.

AOS TFT

The AOS TFT is shown in Fig. 1. The device structure is shown in Fig. 1(a), where the cross-sectional view and microscope photograph are shown. The AOS TFT is fabricated in an actual experiment. The AOS TFT has an amorphous In-Ga-Zn-O (α-IGZO) film as a channel layer and a planar top-gate top-contact structure. The transistor characteristic is shown in Fig. 1(b). Here, the subthreshold swing (S) is 286 mV/dec, the threshold voltage (Vth) is 5.27 V extracted from the equation of drain current and 2.52 V at which the drain current is 1 nA, and the field-effect mobility (µ) is 12.0 cm2/Vs. It should be noted that the on-/off-current ratio is more than 106, which is remarkably high. Moreover, the off-leakage current is very low below the lowest limitation of the measurement system, which is as low as 1 fA. Generally speaking, transistor characteristics of AOS TFTs are closely related to subtle conditions of fabrication processes because they are very sensitive to the oxygen density in the channel layer, but the abovementioned transistor characteristics are used in this paper. Moreover, the experiment results are reproduced by a simulation model. It is found that they are perfectly matched, which will be used later in a circuit simulation.

Thin-film neuron composed of the AOS TFTs

This work proposes a neuron circuit that mimics a real nerve cell, as shown in Figs. 2(a) and (b). A nerve cell is the fundamental unit of the nervous system responsible for transmitting electrical and chemical signals34,35. In this circuit, we use the charging and discharging behavior of a membrane capacitor (Cmem) to emulate the polarization and depolarization of the membrane potential36,37. The threshold voltage of the AOS TFT (T10a) is used to represent the neuron’s action potential. Finally, when the voltage across the capacitor exceeds the threshold voltage, the circuit outputs a spike waveform to represent the neural impulse. Figure 2(c) shows the micrograph of the fabricated single neuron circuit, and the functionality of each circuit block is described below. The excitatory postsynaptic potential (EPSP) circuit block receives positive input spikes (BL+) and increases the voltage across Cmem. The inhibitory postsynaptic potential (IPSP) circuit block receives negative input spikes (BL−) and decreases the voltage across Cmem. The delay circuit block is used to modulate the width of the spike waveform by rapidly transmitting the rising edge while delaying the falling edge. The buffer circuit ensures that the final output waveform exhibits fast rise and fall times, which is a representative role of the constituent inverter circuits. Once the circuit outputs a spike waveform, it is feedebacked (T7), and Vmem is subsequently reset, in other words, Cm is completely discharged.

Thin-film neuron composed of the AOS TFTs.

The operation of the thin-film neuron circuit is demonstrated in Fig. 3(a), based on circuit simulation results. During the first half of the operation period, BL+ increases the membrane voltage (Vmem). The Vmem reaches 1.07 V, 2.07 V, 2.85 V and 3.49 V respectively during each stable period after the BL+ occur. When Vmem rapidly reaches the threshold voltage of the first delay circuit, 3.67 V, output spikes (Out) with the width of 17.6 µs are generated, and Vmem is subsequently reset. The rise/fall times of the output spike are 7.55/5.11 µs. In the latter half of the period, BL− decreases Vmem. Since BL+ occurs more frequently than BL−, Vmem reaches the threshold more slowly. This behavior aligns well with the intended operation of the thin-film neuron. The additive characteristics of the neuron circuit are illustrated in Fig. 3(b). When the Vmem starts from 0 V, each BL+ leads to a stepwise increase in Vmem, with incremental changes of + 1.07 V, + 2.07 V, + 2.85 V, and + 3.49 V after one to four consecutive spikes, respectively. Conversely, when Vmem starts from 3 V, each BL− results in a stepwise decrease in Vmem by − 0.79 V, − 1.45 V, − 2.02 V, and − 2.53 V. This behavior demonstrates the linear accumulation and depletion of membrane potential in response to excitatory and inhibitory inputs. The operation of the thin-film neuron circuit is demonstrated in Fig. 3(c), also based on actual experiment results. Although the shape of Out is not ideal, likely due to the characteristic variations of the AOS TFTs in the additional buffer circuit just before Out, it is found that during the first half of the operation period, Out is more quickly generated, whereas during the latter half of the period, Out is slowly generated. The energy consumption derived from the circuit simulation is 50.7 nJ/spike for the current process technology of 10 μm, which can be predicted to be reduced to 6.3 pJ/spike by the future entirely achievable technology of 0.5 μm. This energy consumption is comparable to 4 pJ/spike for 0.6 μm, below 10 pJ/spike for 0.35 µm38, and 26 pJ/spike for 28 nm39 in prior reports. The highest frequency of the operable spikes derived from the width and rise/fall times is 33.0 kHz, which can be also predicted to be improved to 660 kHz by the future technology.

AOS thin-film memristor

The AOS thin-film memristor is shown in Fig. 440. The device structure is shown in Fig. 4(a), where the cross-sectional view and microscope photograph are shown. The AOS thin-film memristor is fabricated in an actual experiment. The AOS thin-film memristor has three-stacked films of amorphous Ga-Sn-O (α-GTO) films as a switching layer and a cross bar structure of the switching layer between the top and bottom electrodes. The lower, middle, and upper films serve as a protection film for the bottom electrode, conductance change film, and protection film for the top electrode, respectively. Because the three-stacked films are sequentially and continuously deposited and the cross bar structure is quite simple, it is very easy to integrate not only the AOS thin-film memristors themselves but also them with AOS TFTs. It should be noted that the GTO thin-film memristor is not subject to thermal treatment, also by which it is easy to integrate them. The memristor characteristic is shown in Fig. 4(b). Here, the reset voltage (Vreset) is -4.5 V to bring the memristor state to a low conductance state (LCS), the set voltage (Vset) is 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, and 4 V to bring it to high conductance states (HCS), and hysteresis of the current-voltage curves by repeating Vset scan is shown. The switching characteristic is shown in the superimposed figure. Here, the horizontal axis is the Vset, and the vertical axis is the normalized conductance, namely, the conductance divided by the initial conductance (G/G0), which is equivalent to the on-/off-current ratio. It is found that the G/G0 linearly depends on Vset, which is used later. This characteristic is the preferable one envisioned for the AOS thin-film memristor. Incidentally, the retention, endurance, and pulse application characteristics have already been reported40. The retention characteristic guarantees a retention time of approximately 104 s. Although the endurance characteristic is presently insufficient, since that for a similar AOS thin-film memristor guarantees an endurance of more than 10441, we believe that the endurance characteristic can be improved to the similar characteristic. Moreover, Vset and Vreset might be too high, but this is because they are adjusted to the circuit behavior of the thin-film neuron. They can be reduced by thinning the α-GTO films from the current 70 nm to a certain thickness. The energy consumption is the order of 100 pJ/spike for the current AOS thin-film memristor, which can be also improved by miniaturization technology.

AOS thin-film Schottky diode

The AOS thin-film Schottky diode is shown in Fig. 542. The device structure is shown in Fig. 5(a), where the cross-sectional view and overview are shown. The AOS thin-film Schottky diode is fabricated in an actual experiment. The AOS thin-film Schottky diode has a hydrogenated α-IGZO (α-IGZO: H) films as an active layer and a single Schottky contact structure of the active layer between the anode and cathode electrodes. The energy band structure of the Schottky contact between the α-IGZO: H and AgxO achieves an excellent rectification property. Because the single Schottky contact structure is quite simple, it is very easy to integrate not only the AOS thin-film Schottky diodes themselves but also them with AOS TFTs and AOS thin-film memristors. The diode characteristic is shown in Fig. 5(b). It is found that an excellent rectification property is achieved. This characteristic is the preferable one envisioned for the AOS thin-film Schottky diode. Moreover, the experiment results are reproduced by a simulation model. It is found that they are perfectly matched, which is used later in a circuit simulation. Incidentally, the AOS thin-film memristor also has a slight rectification property as shown in Fig. 4(b), but the rectification ratio of nearly 1 is unsatisfactory. The AOS thin-film Schottky diode has an excellent rectification property as shown in Fig. 5(b), and the rectification ratio more than 1010 is satisfactory.

Thin-film synapses composed of the AOS thin-film memristors and Schottky diodes

The thin-film synapses composed of the AOS thin-film memristors and Schottky diodes are shown in Fig. 6(a). Here, a thin-film synapse is formed so that an AOS thin-film Schottky diode and AOS thin-film memristor are connected in series. The thin-film synapses are connected in parallel to be connected to BL + and BL−. Each i-th memristor connected to BL + has each conductance (G + i), and that to BL− has also each conductance (G−i). Input spikes are input to the thin-film synapses, and electric current is rectified by the AOS thin-film Schottky diodes, regulated by the AOS thin-film memristor, flows into the BL + and BL−, and raises or drops the Vmem, namely, induces the change of the Vmem (ΔVmem), whose extent depends on all the G + i and G−i. Incidentally, it should be noted that only the circuits for inference are shown here. Separate circuits for training are needed, which apply desired Vset and Vreset to each memristor.

Fig. 6Thin-film synapses composed of the AOS thin-film memristors and Schottky diodes and working confirmation of the thin-film synapses.

Thin-film synapses composed of the AOS thin-film memristors and Schottky diodes and working confirmation of the thin-film synapses.

The working confirmation of the thin-film synapses is shown in Fig. 6(b). The working is confirmed in a circuit simulation with the simulation models of the AOS TFT and AOS thin-film Schottky diode. Here the horizontal axes are the summation of the G + i and G−i (Σ(G + i −G−i)) and Vmem, and the vertical axis is the ΔVmem. It is found that the ΔVmem roughly speaking increases as Σ(G + i −G−i) increases and Vmem decreases but complicatedly depends on them. The simulation results are reproduced by a fitting model, which will be used later in a logic simulation.

Neuromorphic system composed of the thin-film neurons and synapses

The neuromorphic system composed of the thin-film neurons and synapses is shown in Fig. 7(a). Here, a three-layer perceptron has 14 × 14 = 196 input nodes, 80 intermediate nodes, and 10 output nodes. The operation performance is certified by Modified National Institute of Standards and Technology database (MNIST). The digit images are inputted, and the digit label is outputted.

Fig. 7Neuromorphic system composed of the thin-film neurons and synapses and recognition accuracy of the neuromorphic system.

Neuromorphic system composed of the thin-film neurons and synapses and recognition accuracy of the neuromorphic system.

The working confirmation of the neuromorphic system is shown in Fig. 7(b). The working is confirmed in a logic simulation. First, training is executed by circuit-aware training43 of the circuit behavior shown in Fig. 6(b) and training images of the MNIST. Next, inference is executed by the testing images, and the recognition loss and accuracy are scored. Lastly, the training and inference are repeated as an epoch number. It is found that the recognition loss decreases and the recognition accuracy increases as the epoch number increases. The highest accuracy is 94.1%, which is not so bad and acceptable. The tolerance of the accuracy against the memristor characteristic variability is shown in Fig. 7(c). Here, random variability of Gaussian distribution is added to each conductance, only when the inference is executed. It is found that the accuracy remains high even if the variability increases to 20%. It is believed that this remarkable tolerance is thanks to inherent robustness in distinctive architectures of neural networks. Therefore, it is concluded that the AOS thin-film neuron, synapse, and neuromorphic system are available.

Conclusion

Artificial intelligence will spread exponentially and requires compact size and energy efficiency, for which one solution is neuromorphic systems. On the one hand, thin-film semiconductor technologies have spread widely and feature as giant-micro electronics. In this study, an AOS thin-film neuron, synapse, and neuromorphic system have been researched and developed. First, an AOS TFT was fabricated and evaluated in an actual experiment, and the experiment results were reproduced by a simulation model. A thin-film neuron composed of the AOS TFTs was designed, fabricated, and confirmed to work in a circuit simulation and actual experiment, and this working was the preferable one envisioned for the thin-film neuron. Next, an AOS thin-film memristor was fabricated and evaluated in an actual experiment, an AOS thin-film Schottky diode was also fabricated and evaluated, these characteristics were the preferable ones envisioned for them, and the experiment results were reproduced by a simulation model. Thin-film synapses composed of the AOS thin-film memristors and Schottky diodes were designed, fabricated, and confirmed to work in a circuit simulation with the simulation models of the AOS TFT and AOS thin-film Schottky diode, and the simulation results were reproduced by a fitting model. Lastly, a neuromorphic system composed of the thin-film neurons and synapses was designed, fabricated, and confirmed to work in a logic simulation. Training was executed by circuit-aware training of the circuit behavior and training images of the MNIST, inference was executed, and the recognition accuracy were scored to 94.1%. Finally, it was concluded that the AOS thin-film neuron, synapse, and neuromorphic system are available.

In this study, an AOS TFT, thin-film neuron, AOS thin-film memristor, and AOS thin-film Schottky diode were evaluated experimentally, but thin-film synapses and neuromorphic system were evaluated in circuit simulations. In the future, we intend to integrate all functional components into a neuromorphic system experimentally. At that time, we aim to leverage the miniaturization technology to reduce the energy consumption and improve the spike frequency.

Methods

AOS TFT

The fabrication processes of the AOS TFT are shown in Fig. 8. First, a quartz substrate is cleaned by acetone and methanol cleaning. Next, an α-IGZO film is deposited by radio-frequency (RF) magnetron sputtering, whose thickness is 80 nm, patterned by photolithography with positive resist, wet etching with HCl, and resist stripping, and annealed in N2/O2 = 4:1 gas at 300 °C for 120 min as a channel layer. Next, Mo and Pt films are deposited by RF magnetron sputtering, whose thicknesses are 80 and 20 nm respectively, and patterned by photolithography with negative resist and liftoff as source and drain electrodes. Next, a SiO2 film is deposited by plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD), whose thickness is 100 nm, as a gate insulator, and contact holes are dug by photolithography with positive resist, wet etching with buffered HF (BHF), and resist stripping. Next, Mo and Pt films are again deposited by RF magnetron sputtering, whose thicknesses are 80 and 20 nm respectively, and patterned by photolithography with negative resist and liftoff as a gate electrode. Lastly, the transistor characteristic is tailored by annealing in O2 gas at 300 °C for 120 min and measured by a Keysight 4156B semiconductor parameter analyzer. The experiment results are reproduced by a simulation model, RPI TFT model level = 6244.

Thin-film neuron composed of the AOS TFTs

The simulation procedure of the thin-film neuron composed of the AOS TFTs is as follows. Figure 9(a) demonstrates the timing diagram and operation mechanism of the proposed neuron circuit. The detailed explanation of each period is described below.

Fig. 9Simulation procedure of the thin-film neuron composed of the AOS TFTs.

Simulation procedure of the thin-film neuron composed of the AOS TFTs.

EPSP period

Circuit operation of the EPSP period is illustrated in Fig. 9(b-1). When spike outputs are generated by other connected neurons and the memristors of the corresponding BL+ synapses are in a high-conductance state (HCS), a relatively large current flows through R1. Consequently, VA rises, turning on T1, T3, and T5. T6 is turned off because its gate voltage is pulled down to VSS by T5. Vmem is maintained at VSS by T3, while T1 pulls VD down to a lower voltage level toward Vref each time the neuron enters the EPSP period.

IPSP period

Figure 9(b-2) shows the operation of IPSP period. When spike outputs are generated by other connected neurons and the memristors of the corresponding BL− synapses are in a high-conductance state (HCS), a relatively large current flows through R2. As a result, VB rises and T2 turns on. Since the neuron circuit is designed to process BL + and BL− spikes separately by introducing a delay for BL− spikes, T3 and T5 is turned off by the low voltage of VA during this period. Consequently, Vmem is pulled down to a lower voltage level by T2 while VD is maintained at VSS by T6, and the voltage across Cmem decreases.

Stable period

When both BL + and BL− synapses are at low-conductance state (LCS), or when no spike outputs are generated by other connected neurons, the neuron circuit enters the stable period. In this period, T1 is turned off while T6 is turned on. VD is then charged to VSS, and Vmem rises to VSS + ΔVD due to the capacitive coupling effect of Cmem. The voltage across Cmem remains at VSS + ΔVD throughout this period. T10a remains off as Vmem doesn’t exceed the threshold voltage of T10a. The operation of stable period is shown in Fig. 9(b-3).

Firing period

When Vmem exceeds the threshold voltage of T10a, T10a turns on, thereby triggering the firing mechanism and causing the neuron to enter the firing period. As a result, T10a turns on and VFa is discharged to VSS, which turns off T12a and T13a. The diode-connected T11a then charges VGa to VDD. VGa is subsequently connected to the gate terminal of T10b, and the same process is repeated four times in total. Eventually, VGd rises, turning on T15 of the buffer circuit and causing Vout to go high.

The width of the output spike is determined by the number of the delay circuit blocks. After Vout goes high, T7 is turned on and pull Vmem down to VSS. Therefore, T8a and T9a gradually charge VFa to a high voltage. T12a and T13a is then turned on and gradually discharge VGa to VSS. VGa is subsequently connected to the gate terminal of T10b, and the same process is repeated four times in total to extend the width of the output spikes. Eventually, VGd falls, turning off T15 of the buffer circuit and causing Vout to go low. The circuit operation of the firing period for both rising edge and falling edge is demonstrated in Figs. 9(b-4) and (b-5).

The energy consumption is derived from the circuit simulation as follows. First, the overall power consumption in the thin-film neuron is also shown in Fig. 3(a). By integrating the power consumption over the time, the overall energy consumption is derived to be 405.7 nJ. Next, by assuming the spike incidence is the same as eight times in Fig. 3(a), the energy consumption is 405.7 nJ / 8 spikes = 50.7 nJ/spike.

The energy consumption can be predicted to be reduced by the future technology as follows, which is the same as Dennard scaling45. Using the scaling factor, the power consumption is reduced to 1/k2, delay time is reduced to 1/k, and therefore the energy consumption of J/spike is reduced to 1/k2 ⋅ 1/k = 1/k3. As the process technology advances from 10 μm to 0.5 μm, the energy consumption can be predicted to be reduced to 50.7 nJ/spike ⋅ 1 / ( 10 μm / 0.5 μm )3 = 6.3 pJ/spike.

AOS thin-film memristor

The fabrication processes of the AOS thin-film memristor are shown in Fig. 10. First, on a quartz substrate, an Al film is deposited by vapor evaporation through a physical mask as a bottom electrode. Next, three-stacked films of α-GTO films are deposited by RF magnetron sputtering through a physical mask as a switching layer, where the sputtering target is a GTO ceramic whose composition is Ga: Sn = 1:3 atom %, the deposition pressure is 1 Pa, and the plasma power is 60 W. The lower, middle, and upper layers are deposited sequentially and continuously in the same chamber without breaking the vacuum, the Ar: O2 flow rates are 20:0, 20:10, and 20:0 sccm, respectively, and the deposition time is 3 min, 10 min, and 3 min, respectively, where the deposition speed is roughly 10 nm/min. Next, an Al thin film is again deposited by vapor evaporation through a physical mask as a top electrode. The GTO thin-film memristor is not subject to thermal treatment. Lastly, the memristor characteristic is measured by a Keysight 4156 C semiconductor parameter analyzer.

Fabrication processes of the AOS thin-film memristor.

AOS thin-film schottky diode

The fabrication processes of the AOS thin-film Schottky diode are shown in Fig. 11. First, on a poly(ethylene naphthalate) (PEN) substrate, an In-Sn-O (ITO) film is deposited by direct current (DC) magnetron sputtering as a cathode electrode of ohmic contact, whose thickness is 100 nm. Next, α-IGZO: H film is deposited by DC magnetron sputtering as an active layer, whose thickness is 100 nm, where the sputtering target is an InGaZnO4 ceramic whose composition is In: Ga: Zn = 1:1:1 atom %, the sputtering gas is the mixture of Ar, O2, and H2 whose O2 and H2 partial pressures are 1% and 8% respectively, and the deposition temperature is room temperature, and annealed in air at 150 °C for 1 h. Next, a AgxO thin film is deposited by reactive sputtering, where the sputtering target is a Ag target, the sputtering gas is the mixture of Ar and O2 whose O2 partial pressure is 4% ,and a Au is deposited by vapor evaporation and patterned by photolithography with liftoff, as an anode electrode. Lastly, the diode characteristic is measured by a Keysight 4156 A semiconductor parameter analyzer. The experiment results are reproduced by a simulation model, ordinary diode model level = 1.

Thin-film synapses composed of the AOS thin-film memristors and Schottky diodes

The simulation procedure of the thin-film synapses composed of the AOS thin-film memristors and Schottky diodes is as follows. First, the G + i and G−i. are randomly set, and the simulation models of the AOS TFT and AOS thin-film Schottky diode are implemented in a circuit simulator, HSPICE46, using RPI TFT model level = 62 for the AOS TFTs and ordinary diode model level = 1 for the AOS thin-film Schottky diode. Next, the circuit netlist of the thin-film synapses connected to BL + and BL− is made. Lastly, input spikes of the height of 10 V and width of 10 μm are input to the thin-film synapses, and ΔVmem is calculated.

Neuromorphic system composed of the thin-film neurons and synapses

The simulation procedure of the neuromorphic system composed of the thin-film neurons and synapses is as follows. Here, a custom program is made47, and circuit-aware training of the circuit behavior of the thin-film synapses is implemented in a logic simulator, Python with PyTorch and snnTorch. First, training is executed by the circuit-aware training and 60,000 training images of the MNIST, where average pooling48 with a kernel size of 2 is used to downsample 28 × 28 images to 14 × 4 inputs. Next, inference is executed by the 10,000 testing images, and the recognition loss and accuracy are scored. Lastly, the training and inference are repeated as an epoch number, where hyper parameters, such as, learning rate and batch size, are tuned to improve the recognition accuracy.

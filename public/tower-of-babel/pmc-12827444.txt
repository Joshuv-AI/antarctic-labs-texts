Effect of hydroquinone as organoelectroactive additive with 1,2,4 triazolium ionic liquid in supercapacitor application
Authors: Kirubanantham Govindaraj, Midhun Asok, Swathi Muraleedharan, Sushmita Kottukkal Sushil, Elango Kandasamy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractRecently, ionic liquids have drawn interest in the form of organoelectrolytes for supercapacitors as they show a broad electrochemical stability window and thereby increased energy density. In addition to that, electroactive additives are the most promising ones to enhance the efficiency of supercapacitors. However, researchers have focused on improving the electrochemical performance of supercapacitors by incorporating electroactive additives in aqueous electrolytes, where they show a narrow electrochemical stability window. This research focuses on introducing electroactive organic additives such as hydroquinone, into the ionic liquids (ILs). For this study, the least explored unsymmetrical dialkylated triazolium-based IL 1-propyl-4-butyl-triazolium hexafluorophosphate was synthesized. The prepared IL is characterised using 1H NMR spectroscopic technique. Electrochemical studies were conducted to compare the impact of the additive with the IL. The synthesis of an ionogel polymer with the synthesized IL and the organic additive which is hydroquinone highlights its potential uses as a gel polymer electrolyte used in electrochemical energy storage technologies. In order to evaluate the electrochemical applicability, two Swagelok cells were constructed: a pristine gel polymer electrolyte (PGPE) which contains only the IL and a hydroquinone incorporated gel polymer electrolyte (HQGPE). It was subjected to studies like Cyclic voltammetry (CV), Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and Galvanostatic charge- discharge studies (GCD). For PGPE system, GCD testing at 0.28 A/g provided 80.95% retention of capacitance after 1500 cycles, with calculated specific capacitance, power and energy density of 3.02 F/g, 292.98 W/kg and 1.68 Wh/kg respectively. Meanwhile, HQGPE system tested at 0.44 A/g provided 78.51% retention after 1500 cycles, with calculated capacitance, energy density, and power density of 3.75 F/g, 2.083 Wh/kg, and 552.61 W/kg, respectively. The comparison between PGPE & HQGPE emphasises on the influence of organoadditive on the electrochemical characteristics of the gel polymer electrolyte.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-32609-3.Keywords: Ionic liquids, Organoelectrolyte, Additive, SupercapacitorsSubject terms: Chemistry, Energy science and technology, Materials science

Introduction

With the everyday improving integration of renewable resources and power systems, there is an increasing requirement of efficient energy storage devices like supercapacitors. Supercapacitors allow for speedy energy transfer by storing energy electrostatically instead of chemically as batteries do. Developments in materials science have improved their performance and made them viable for a wide range of uses, from electric vehicles to energy systems that are sustainable1–11. Out of every component of the supercapacitor, the electrode and electrolyte part majorly determine the electrochemical activity of the device. With the enhancement of energy density, power density, and lifespan, the electrode advancements make the classes of supercapacitors—EDLCs, pseudo capacitors, and hybrid capacitors—more efficient and confirm supercapacitors as a prominent technology among modern energy storage products12–16.

Owing to its high surface area, tunable porosity and conductivity, Activated Carbon (AC) remains a preferable choice for the electrode material. In this study, AC activated by using Zinc acetate has been employed as a safer alternative towards the conventional activators, which in turn enables the formation of a porous structure which is beneficial for charge storage and ion diffusion.

Electrolytes are crucial for supercapacitor performance, affecting ion transport, energy and power density, and stability. While aqueous electrolytes (e.g., KOH, H₂SO₄) are safe, inexpensive, and highly conductive, their voltage window is limited to 1.23 V. Organic electrolytes offer a broader potential window and higher energy density but suffer from volatility, flammability, and high cost. ILs as low-volatility molten salts with good thermal stability, combine the advantages of both aqueous and organic electrolytes. Supercapacitor performance and flexibility are increasingly enhanced through these innovations in electrolytes and electrode materials like metal oxides, conductive polymers, and activated carbon, making them indispensable parts of modern energy storage devices17–19. Particularly Triazolium-based ILs have gained much attention for their unique ability for hydrogen bonding and enhanced conductivity, which is far better than imidazolium-based ILs in both stability and performance. Triazolium ILs exhibit stronger supramolecular interactions, including hydrogen bonding and metal coordination, than conventional ILs due to the polarization of their N-heterocyclic cations. This enhances the stability and performance of supercapacitors and enables the development of advanced energy materials. Their non-flammability, chemical stability in alkaline conditions, and environmental benignity make them promising for sustainable energy applications. Combined with novel electrode materials, these ILs contribute to high-performance supercapacitor design10,20,21. Although ILs possess high electrochemical stability, their performance can be limited by factors such as low redox activity and reduced ionic mobility. To address these issues, various redox additives have been incorporated into IL-based electrolytes, where they enhance charge transfer and promote redox reactions.

Some of the reported additives include titanium-based additives22, a combination of organic and inorganic additives23 and conductive additives24. Additives such as potassium iodide (KI), sodium iodide (NaI), Hydroquinone (HQ), and anthraquinone (AQ) are capable of greatly enhancing the ionic conductivity, energy density, and electrochemical stability of ILs. These additions optimize charge-discharge performances, widen the electrochemical stability window, and improve compatibility with electrode materials. Specifically, organic additions enhance ionic conductivity, broaden working voltage ranges, and prevent dendritic growth in metal electrodes, all of which work to enhance the extended lifespan and security of SCs. For instance, HQ is highly valuable across a spectrum of applications such as energy storage, biosensing, and electroplating, as it enhances capacitance, energy density, and stability via redox activity. HQ incorporation into IL significantly creates a breakthrough in the development of efficient supercapacitor devices25. They are recognized as an efficient dual redox additive16. The dual mechanism enhances the overall charge storage capability, resulting in improved specific capacitance, energy density, and power output. This reversible nature redox process enables fast electron transfer and better ionic mobility within the electrolyte. When combined with IL-based electrolytes, HQ broadens the electrochemical stability window and facilitates synergistic effects between redox and ionic conduction processes, representing a significant step forward in developing efficient supercapacitor systems (Fig. 1)26. They have been incorporated into electrode carbon materials to significantly improve supercapacitor performance. Its redox-active nature introduces additional faradaic reactions alongside the double-layer capacitance, thereby enhancing both energy and power densities27.

Electron transfer reaction of hydroquinone and quinone.

In combination with advances in IL electrolytes and electrode materials, these findings in additively improved ILs have delivered a significant leap in the construction of reliable and highly performing supercapacitor technologies20,27–32.

In this work, we have analyzed the effect of HQ with the IL 1-propyl-4-butyl-1,2,4-triazolium hexafluorophosphate, assessing its influence on electrochemical performance through CV and EIS analyses. AC has been chosen as the electrode material for the study which has successfully evaluated the electrochemical performance of the PGPE & HQGPE. Our approach aims to demonstrate how such additive–IL combinations can be optimized for the next-generation supercapacitor technologies.

Materials and methods

Chemicals used in this study, including 1,8-Diazabicyclo [5.4.0] undec-7-ene (DBU), tetrahydrofuran (THF), petroleum ether, acetonitrile (ACN), ethyl acetate, 1,2,4-triazole, propyl bromide and butyl bromide were sourced from AVRA, India. Ethanol was procured from Fine Chemicals Co., Ltd., China. N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP), Acetylene black (AB), Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) and Poly(vinylidene fluoride-co-hexafluoropropylene) (PVDF-HFP) were acquired from Sigma Aldrich. Hydroquinone (HQ) was purchased from SRL India. All purchased chemicals were employed without undergoing additional purification. Glasswares were cleaned with distilled water, rinsed with acetone, and placed in a hot air oven at 100 °C for drying. A Bruker AVANCE 400 FT NMR was employed to acquire 1H NMR spectra in CDCl3. The AC used in this study has been previously characterised and reported. The carbon shows a surface area of 780 m2/g. The pore radius, and pore volume of the AC were 1.33 nm and 0.519 cc/g respectively21. Electrochemical characterization including CV, EIS, and GCD analysis, was conducted using an OrigaLys Instruments Electrochemical Analyzer. The ionic conductivity was measured using the systronics conductivity meter 304. The 1-propyl-4-butyl-1,2,4-triazolium hexafluorophosphate has been synthesized through a reported procedure20,33. The IL was characterised by NMR spectroscopy. The 1H NMR of 1-propyl,4-butyl-1,2,4-triazolium bromide and 1-propyl,4-butyl-1,2,4-triazolium hexafluorophosphate are compared, the acidic proton on the triazolium ring resonates at 11.15 ppm and 9.28 ppm, respectively. However, following the anion exchange, we can see an upfield resonance shift of the acidic proton to 9.33 ppm and 8.27 ppm for the PF6− anion carrying cation. This observation shows that the anion exchange was successful (Supplementary Information S1).

IL and IL-HQ solution preparation with different weight%

For electrochemical studies, the IL solution was prepared at a concentration of 0.5 M. The electroactive additive, HQ, was incorporated into the IL at varying weight percentages, ranging from 0.5% to 2.0%, while maintaining a constant weight of the IL.

Preparation of gel polymer electrolyte and electrode

Two gel polymer electrolytes were prepared through reported procedure34. PGPE was prepared by using the synthesized IL. In order to prepare HQGPE, 0.5 weight% of hydroquinone (with respect to the IL) was incorporated into the IL. Both the mixtures were then solution casted into clean Petri dishes and left to dry, yielding two distinct GPE films.

The thermal stability of the HQGPE was confirmed by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA), showing an onset of degradation around 334 °C (Supplementary Information S5).

For the 3 electrode and 2 electrode electrochemical study, the working electrode was prepared with AC as the active material. A mortar and pestle were used to grind a mixture of active material, PVDF, and acetylene black (in an 8:1:1 ratio) for 30 min, with dropwise addition of NMP to form a smooth, homogenous paste. The paste was then coated on a 12 mm diameter nickel foam uniformly using a doctor blade and dried overnight in hot air oven at 65 °C. Assembly of the supercapacitor device was performed according to a reported procedure10,20,21.

Result and discussion

Half-cell testing of IL and IL-HQ systems with AC electrode

A three-electrode system CV and EIS study was performed with AC as the working electrode and two platinum wires serving as reference and counter electrode. Initially the electrochemical analysis was carried out for the prepared IL to evaluate its electrochemical properties. The study was conducted using a 0.5 M solution in 10 mL of acetonitrile solvent, and at scan rates ranging from 50 to 200 mV/s (Fig. 2). The potential window optimized for the synthesized IL is 2 V.

Cyclic voltammogram at different scan rate of (a) only IL, (b) IL + 0.5% HQ, (c) IL + 1.0% HQ, (d) IL + 1.5% HQ and (e) IL + 2.0% HQ.

Among all the combinations that were tested, IL + 0.5 wt% HQ showcased a remarkable increase in current and the area enclosed by the curves. This indicates increased redox activity and improved capacitive behaviour due to the contribution of the additive. This enhancement in current confirms that 0.5 wt% HQ is the highest and the optimum concentration of the additive. In contrast to this, higher concentration of additive (1–2 wt%) showcased decreased electrochemical activity. This might be likely due to the decreased ionic mobility at higher additive concentrations. These inferences highlight the importance of optimizing additive concentration in order to show the redox contribution for enhanced electrochemical performance.

The electrochemical activity of the IL with different percentages of HQ was studied by the three-electrode system with AC used as the working electrode and platinum wires as reference and counter electrodes. Two methods were applied: CV and EIS.

As demonstrated in the three electrode CV graph provided in the supplementary information (S3), adding 0.5% HQ to the IL enhanced specific capacitance, while further addition of HQ decreased it. EIS studies were performed for IL and IL with different concentration of HQ (Fig. 3). The resistance values were calculated from the EIS spectra for the pure IL and HQ-added systems. The charge-transfer resistance values were found to be 6.68 Ω for the pure IL, 6.1 Ω for 0.5% HQ, 10.9 Ω for 1.0% HQ, 17.5 Ω for 1.5% HQ, and 21.0 Ω for 2.0% HQ. The 0.5% HQ system exhibited the lowest resistance, confirming improved ion transport and reduced charge-transfer resistance. At higher HQ concentrations, increased resistance indicates restricted ionic mobility. Therefore, 0.5% HQ was selected as the optimum additive concentration relative to the pristine IL for further electrochemical analysis. The Rct values are provided in Supplementary Information- S6.

Overlayed EIS graph for only IL, IL + 0.5% HQ, IL + 1.0% HQ, IL + 1.5% HQ and IL + 2.0% HQ.

Figure 4 demonstrated that adding 0.5% HQ to the IL has enhanced specific capacitance, while further addition of HQ decreased it. The effect of conductivity has been mentioned in the supplementary information S4. Conductivity has changed from 26 mS/cm with only the IL to 27 mS/cm at a 0.5% HQ addition, and to 28 mS/cm at 1.0% HQ addition. Subsequent additions maintained the same conductivity. The optimal concentration to produce a two-electrode system in a Swagelok cell is 0.5% HQ, as indicated by the CV plots. This indicates an appreciable enhancement in specific capacitance and conductivity.

CV plot for only IL and IL + 0.5% HQ (100 mV/s scan rate).

Fabrication of supercapacitor

A Swagelok cell, which was assembled with a working electrode, plated with about 5.9 mg of AC and a gel polymer electrolyte with about 1 g of IL, was employed for CV studies. A 1 g IL gel polymer electrolyte with 0.5% hydroquinone (HQ) by weight, or about 5 mg of HQ, and a working electrode with 3.92 mg of AC were utilized to construct a second Swagelok cell.

At a scan rate of 100 mV/s, CV scans were carried out on the fabricated cells over a potential range from 0.5 V to 4 V. The results indicated that, the 2 V potential window exhibited the greatest reversibility. For future electrochemical studies, this optimized potential window was chosen. A series of scan rates between 2 mV/s and 200 mV/s were utilized to scan the cells to measure performance in dynamic environments. The two systems exhibited quasi-rectangular or spindle-like CV profiles, indicating enhanced charge storage ability and excellent reversibility. The absence of additional peaks in the CV curves suggests efficient charge transfer governed by electrical double-layer behavior35. The obtained specific capacitance for PGPE and HQGPE were 171.12 F/g and 181.37 F/g respectively for 2 V at 2 mV/s. The specific capacitance was enhanced by the incorporation of HQ into the polymer matrix. The results indicated a reduction of specific capacitance as scan rates increased, which is attributed to the lower accessibility of ions at increasing scan rates.

Prominently, the CV curves of the two cells showed no oxidation or reduction peaks, proving the pure electrochemical double-layer capacitor (EDLC) nature of the system. This thorough process highlights the methodical development and validation of the supercapacitor system, beginning with initial three-electrode studies and concluding with optimal production of a two-electrode Swagelok cell.

EIS spectra were recorded at ambient temperature over a frequency range of 0.01 Hz to 1 MHz to evaluate the overall electrochemical behaviour and charge transfer characteristics of the fabricated system (Fig. 5). A semicircular arc in the higher frequency side, indicative of charge transfer resistance at the electrode-electrolyte interface, could be observed in the resulting Nyquist plot. The behaviour indicates that the electrolyte and electrode material are efficiently interacting with each other. One common feature of double-layer capacitors which indicates the capacitive nature of the system is a sloping straight line in the low-frequency region. The ability of the system to ensure efficient and smooth ion transfer is evidenced by the measured low charge transfer resistance alongside the low solution resistance. As far as supercapacitor application is concerned, this efficient ionic conductivity is essential in maintaining high capacitance as well as enabling rapid charge-discharge cycles. Improved energy storage performance also comes from the effect between the chosen electrolyte and AC electrode material, further optimizing the electrochemical kinetics. The findings indicate that the electrode compatibility and ion transport pathways are effectively enhanced by incorporating the gel polymer electrolyte in conjunction with IL and the optimum amount of HQ. The system is thus made a potential candidate for next-generation energy storage devices by enhancing energy density, charge storage, and finally the cyclic stability.

EIS plot for (a) Only IL and (b) IL + 0.5% HQ.

GCD profiles of the fabricated cell were measured within a 2 V potential window at various current densities and cycle numbers, demonstrating its energy storage performance (Fig. 6). For the first cell, the GCD was performed at current densities between 0.18 A/g and 0.33 A/g, and it was concluded that 0.28 A/g was the optimum current density for reproducible performance for multiple cycles. Whereas for second cell, current densities between 0.44 A/g and 0.63 A/g were studied subsequently and the best current density for effective charge storage was established as 0.44 A/g. The GCD was performed at 0.28 A/g and 0.44 A/g for PGPE and HQGPE respectively for 1500 cycles. The capacitance, energy, and power density of the cells were determined using the theoretical equations provided in (S2). The long-term electrochemical stability of both supercapacitor systems were evaluated over 1500 consecutive charge discharge cycles, and the corresponding specific capacitance and Coulombic efficiency were analyzed to assess durability and reversibility. In both supercapacitor systems, the influence of extended cycling on capacitance retention followed a similar decreasing trend. After 500, 1000, and 1500 charge–discharge cycles, the first cell retained 95.23%, 85.71%, and 80.95% of its initial capacitance, while the corresponding retention levels for the second cell were 92.85%, 85.71%, and 78.51%, respectively. The first cell, containing only IL, exhibited specific capacitances of 3.02, 2.81, 2.53, and 2.35 F/g at 1, 500, 1000, and 1500 cycles. In contrast, the second cell displayed higher specific capacitances of 3.75, 3.59, 3.28, and 2.90 F/g over the same cycling intervals. The superior specific capacitance observed in the HQ-modified system can be attributed to the additional pseudocapacitive contribution from the reversible redox couple of hydroquinone/quinone (HQ ↔ Q + 2 H⁺ + 2e⁻). This redox reaction provides faradaic charge storage in addition to the non-faradaic double-layer capacitance of the IL, resulting in improved overall energy storage capability36. The gradual decrease in capacitance for both systems upon extended cycling may be ascribed to slight electrode degradation, limited ion diffusion within the porous structure, or partial loss of electroactive species during continuous redox cycling. Nevertheless, the HQ-incorporated system consistently maintained a higher capacitance, confirming its improved electrochemical utilization and stability.

GCD graph at different current densities (a) only IL (b) IL + 0.5% HQ and different cycles of (c) only IL (d) IL + 0.5% HQ.

The Coulombic efficiency results further substantiate the cycling stability of both supercapacitors. The first cell exhibited efficiencies of 78.71%, 82.46%, 80.72%, and 80.16% at 1, 500, 1000, and 1500 cycles, respectively, while the HQ-containing cell recorded efficiencies of 71.56%, 69.40%, 71.63%, and 78.35% under the same conditions. The efficiencies remained within the range of 70–82%, indicating good charge discharge reversibility and minimal parasitic side reactions throughout the cycling process. The minor fluctuations observed in Coulombic efficiency could be due to variations in redox kinetics, ionic relaxation behaviour, or electrolyte redistribution during repeated cycling. Although the HQ-based cell showed slightly lower efficiency at intermediate cycles, the recovery to 78.35% after 1500 cycles suggests stabilization of the redox processes upon prolonged operation. The energy and power density values provide critical insights into the electrochemical performance and practical applicability of supercapacitors. For the first cell, the energy and power densities were calculated to be 1.68 Wh/kg and 292.98 W/kg, respectively. In contrast, the second cell exhibited markedly improved values of 2.083 Wh/kg and 552.61 W/kg. The corresponding Ragone plot (Fig. 7) clearly illustrates the improvement in both energy and power performance for the HQ-modified system. A depletion layer developed at the electrode electrolyte interface in both systems as a result of ion entrapment within the electrode pores during repeated cycling. This phenomenon led to an increase in internal resistance and a gradual reduction in charge carrier transport efficiency, contributing to the observed decline in cycling stability. Nonetheless, the HQ-based supercapacitor demonstrated a superior balance between energy and power output, confirming that redox-active hydroquinone effectively enhances the charge transfer dynamics within the IL medium. Overall, the results highlight that the interaction between current density and electrolyte composition plays a crucial role in determining supercapacitor performance. The optimized HQ-incorporated system exhibits improved charge storage, higher energy and power densities, and stable long-term cycling behaviour. These findings underscore the significant advantage of introducing hydroquinone as a redox additive, making the composite electrolyte a promising candidate for high-performance energy storage applications.

Ragone plot showing the energy and power densities of PGPE and HQGPE supercapacitor systems.

Conclusion

This study focuses on enhancement of the capacitance of ILs through the addition of HQ, an organic additive, to enhance the efficiency supercapacitors. Triazolium-based ILs were synthesised and characterised, and the effect of different weight percentages of HQ (0.5%, 1.0%, 1.5%, and 2.0%) were examined. Electrochemical analyses by CV and EIS showed that increased HQ concentration produced greater peak currents and better conductance. The IL + 0.5% HQ blend was particularly observed to have a positive response in the CV curve, indicating that HQ is a good additive to improve IL performance. Symmetrical electric double-layer capacitors (EDLCs) were prepared with AC electrodes and ionogel polymer electrolytes, using both IL and IL + 0.5% HQ systems. Both systems exhibited a broad operating voltage window of 0–4 V, with CV reversibility at 2.0 V. CV tests were performed at different scan rates (200 mV/s to 2 mV/s), and GCD tests were carried out at different current densities. From the GCD analysis, the calculated capacitance, power density and energy density for PGPE to be 3.02 F/g, 292.98 W/kg and 1.68 Wh/kg respectively, while for HQGPE, they were 3.75 F/g, 552.61 W/kg and 2.083 Wh/kg respectively. PGPE, at 0.28 A/g it provided 80.95% retention of capacitance after 1500 cycles, whereas the HQGPE had 0.44 A/g provided 78.51% retention after 1500 cycles. The study shows that the inclusion of hydroquinone as an organic additive enhances the efficiency of the supercapacitor device.

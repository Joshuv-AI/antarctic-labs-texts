A Global Study of Skin and Subcutaneous Diseases Among Individuals Under 20 Years of Age
Authors: Xiju He, Na Feng, Junjie Chang, Xiaomin Huang, Tao Zhu, Wenrui Min, Xinxin Wang, Zudong Meng, Yulin Zou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSkin diseases are chronic and recurrent conditions that impose substantial disability and long-term psychosocial burden. However, the global burden of pediatric skin diseases has been insufficiently studied.MethodsWe extracted age-specific data for individuals younger than 20 years from GBD 2021. A comprehensive analytical framework was applied, including Joinpoint regression analysis, SDI frontier analysis, Bayesian age–period–cohort (BAPC) modeling, and ARIMA time-series forecasting to evaluate temporal trends and predict future disease burden. The age definition (<20 years) follows the standard GBD age grouping for children and adolescents.ResultsGlobally, the burden of skin and subcutaneous diseases increased from 653,365.48 to 860,806.73 DALYs (EAPC = 0.45, 95% CI: 0.43–0.47). ARIMA forecasting suggested that dermatitis and other skin diseases will decline in China and Europe but rise in the United States. Children aged 5–9 years had the lowest burden, while those under 5 years and aged 15–19 years showed higher DALYs, mainly driven by atopic dermatitis and acne.ConclusionThe burden of pediatric skin diseases has generally increased globally, although trends vary across regions and disease categories. Acne and atopic dermatitis are the dominant contributors to disability. These findings provide actionable evidence for prioritizing pediatric dermatology in global and regional public health strategies.Keywords: skin disease, global, burden, youths, pediatric dermatology

Introduction

Skin diseases are highly prevalent and have drawn increasing global attention due to their substantial impact on patients’ quality of life. Although these conditions are rarely life-threatening, they can cause significant physical discomfort, psychological distress, and social impairment, ultimately leading to a marked deterioration in overall well-being.1 As typical chronic conditions, skin diseases often persist throughout life.2,3 For individuals who develop these diseases during childhood or adolescence, the impact on physical, psychological, and social well-being may extend across their entire lifespan.4 Skin diseases can exert continuous health and psychosocial burdens on individuals who develop them in childhood or adolescence.5–9 However, research investigating long-term epidemiological trends in pediatric skin diseases remains scarce.10 This study used global big data and AI-based modeling to quantify trends (1990–2021) and predict trajectories to 2040.

The Global Burden of Disease, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study 2021 (GBD 2021) is a comprehensive research program that systematically quantifies health loss across countries and regions worldwide. The dataset incorporates multiple standardized epidemiological metrics, including prevalence and incidence, as well as mortality counts (deaths). In addition, it contains composite burden indicators such as years lived with disability (YLDs; quantifying non-fatal health loss), years of life lost (YLLs; capturing fatal health loss), and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), a comprehensive measure that integrates premature mortality and disability into a single metric.11–13 The dataset also includes these indicators stratified by the Socio-demographic Index (SDI), a composite measure based on per capita income, the average years of education among individuals aged 15 years or older (particularly women), and the total fertility rate among women under the age of 25.14,15 Stratification was also performed according to the SDI, categorized into five levels: high SDI, high-middle SDI, middle SDI, low-middle SDI, and low SDI.

China, the United States of America(USA) and Europe are among the most influential regions globally due to their large populations and substantial economic power.16 Therefore, we extracted and analyzed data from these regions separately to enable individualized comparisons and a more refined understanding of disease patterns.

The autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) model was used to predict future trends of skin and subcutaneous diseases.17,18 In addition, the Bayesian age–period–cohort (BAPC) model was applied to capture age, period, and cohort effects and to complement ARIMA-based predictions.19

In the GBD framework, individuals younger than 20 years are defined as children and adolescents, consistent with standard GBD age stratification. Therefore, the present study adopts this definition to ensure methodological consistency and comparability with existing GBD-based analyses.12,20

This study leveraged global large-scale health data to quantify the burden of skin diseases in individuals under 20 years of age from 1990 to 2021, evaluate socioeconomic gradients, and compare disease patterns among China, the United States, and Europe. Unlike previous GBD-based studies focusing on overall populations, this study specifically targeted pediatric populations and integrated multiple analytical approaches, including Joinpoint analysis, SDI frontier analysis, and dual-model forecasting. Using two predictive modeling approaches, we projected future disease trends to guide early intervention, optimize healthcare resource allocation, and support evidence-based policy formulation at national and regional levels.

Methods

Data Sources

Data for this study was obtained from GBD 2021 (https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/). Each relevant category was identified (including acne vulgaris, alopecia areata, atopic dermatitis, bacterial skin diseases, decubitus ulcer, dermatitis, fungal skin diseases, other skin and subcutaneous diseases, pruritus, psoriasis, scabies, urticaria, viral skin diseases, cellulitis, contact dermatitis, pyoderma, and seborrheic dermatitis), and subsequently downloaded for analysis. Among these categories, other skin and subcutaneous diseases represent an aggregated group that summarizes all skin-related conditions. Likewise, Dermatitis refers to a combined category that encompasses all types of dermatitis.

We extracted incidence, prevalence, deaths, DALYs, YLLs, and YLDs for children and adolescents stratified by age groups. All estimates included 95% uncertainty intervals, covering the years 1990–2021.

Global Data Analysis

Assessment of the Global Burden of Skin and Subcutaneous Diseases

For each country, all epidemiological indicators were obtained and visualized on a global choropleth map. Countries were color-coded from blue to red, where blue indicates a lower disease burden (Incidence, Prevalence, DALYs and deaths) and red represents a higher burden.

Age-Specific Distribution of Incidence in Skin and Subcutaneous Diseases

For each disease entity, the proportional contribution of the four age groups to the total global incidence was calculated annually.

Comparative Assessment of Regional Burden Across Disease Causes

All skin disease categories was calculated and the proportional contribution of each disease cause to the regional total was derived.

Comparison of Death Rates from Bacterial Skin Diseases, Cellulitis, and Decubitus Ulcer Across SDI Regions in 2021

For the 2021 cross-section, we extracted estimates for six aggregate locations: Global, five SDI.

Temporal Trends in Skin and Subcutaneous Diseases Among Individuals Under 20 Years

We extracted global estimates both sexes, and reported as numbers. Two indicators were analyzed separately: incidence and DALYs.

Sex-Specific Temporal Trends in Incidence Rates Among Individuals Under 20 Years

We extracted global incidence rates with male, female, and both sexes combined retained as the three analytic strata.

Age- and Sex-Specific Proportional Burden of Skin and Subcutaneous Diseases

Data were extracted from the processed dataset and filtered to include sex-specific estimates for both incidence and DALYs, reported as absolute numbers. For each disease cause, values were aggregated by age group and sex.

Frontier Analysis Across SDI Levels

To evaluate the relationship between disease burden and socioeconomic development, a frontier analysis was conducted for all skin and subcutaneous disease categories. The SDI for each country was used as an independent variable.

Age-, Sex-, and SDI-Stratified Patterns of Incidence, Prevalence, and DALYs

For each skin and subcutaneous disease category, we extracted age-specific burden indicators for the year 2021. Data were restricted to four pediatric and adolescent age groups, six SDI levels.

Global Age-Group Distribution of Burden Over Time (1990–2021)

For each skin and subcutaneous disease category, we performed an age-group–based temporal analysis to evaluate how the burden evolved over time in the pediatric and adolescent population. Data were extracted from the processed dataset and filtered to include four predefined age groups and global estimates for selected benchmark years.

Age-Specific Multi-Indicator Burden Summary Table Across Global

We extracted data for all six GBD indicators across Global, SDI levels and calculated absolute numbers, age-standardized rates, and EAPC trends.

Global Trends in Disease Burden

We extracted data for all six indicators across all diseases. We filtered the dataset across four predefined age groups, the Both sexes category. We calculated the EAPC for each age group returning both point estimates and 95% confidence intervals.

Burden Disparities Across China, Europe, and the United States

For each disease category, we extracted all factors corresponding to China, Europe, and the USA, enabling region-stratified assessment of disease burden.

Age and Sex Disparities in Disease Burden

We extracted estimates for four predefined age groups. Data were filtered by burden measure (incidence, prevalence), metric type (absolute number or rate).

Temporal Trends in Male-to-Female Ratios of Incidence

We extracted incidence numbers for four predefined age groups. Data were filtered by region, and incidence. We calculated the sex ratio for each age group and year.

ARIMA Projection Model for Future Burden Estimates (1990–2040)

An autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) model was applied to evaluate historical trends and predict future incidence. The model parameters (p, d, q) were automatically selected based on standard model selection criteria, including the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), to ensure optimal model fitting. Model adequacy was assessed through residual diagnostics to confirm the absence of autocorrelation and ensure stability. The final model was then used to generate forecast values, along with corresponding 80% and 95% confidence intervals. ARIMA is a classical statistical forecasting method rather than an artificial intelligence–based approach.

BAPC Forecasting Model (1990–2040)

We applied a Bayesian age–period–cohort (BAPC) model to forecast incidence trends from 1990 to 2040. The model simultaneously incorporated age effects, temporal trends, and birth-cohort differences to capture underlying epidemiological patterns. Bayesian inference was implemented to estimate posterior distributions and generate probabilistic forecasts. Model performance and stability were evaluated to ensure robustness, and the results were used to complement ARIMA-based predictions.

Joinpoint Analysis of Temporal Trends (1990–2021)

We applied Joinpoint regression to quantify temporal changes in pediatric and adolescent disease burden across regions. The model identified significant changes in trends over time and estimated the annual percentage change (APC) and average annual percentage change (AAPC) with corresponding confidence intervals. Statistical significance was determined using permutation tests, and the results were used to characterize dynamic trends across different regions.

Age-Specific Multi-Indicator Burden Summary Table Across Regions

We extracted data for individuals for China, Europe, and the USA for each indicator.

Data Analysis

We used R software (version 4.4.2) for data analysis and graphing. GlobalBurdenR, vroom, dplyr, tidyr, ggplot2 were used for the analysis.

Ethics Approval

This study was reviewed by the Ethics Committee of Shiyan People’s Hospital (Renmin Hospital, Hubei University of Medicine), which determined that ethical approval was not required because all data were obtained from the publicly accessible GBD database and contained no identifiable personal information.

Results

Global Data Analysis

Assessment of the Global Burden of Skin and Subcutaneous Diseases

The crude incidence rate of acne vulgaris shows obvious geographical heterogeneity, with higher incidence observed in Europe, North America, and parts of East Asia, while most regions in Africa and South Asia present relatively lower levels. A similar distribution pattern is observed for prevalence, with higher rates in Europe, East Asia, and South America, whereas many African countries show lower prevalence. In addition, the DALYs attributable to acne vulgaris are highest in Europe and East Asia, indicating that these regions experience not only more cases but also a greater overall disease burden, while DALYs remain relatively low in most regions of Africa and Oceania.

Age-Specific Distribution of Incidence in Skin and Subcutaneous Diseases

Atopic dermatitis (Figure 1A) peaked in early childhood (0–5 years), whereas other skin and subcutaneous diseases (Figure 1B) showed a more even age distribution. Urticaria (Figure 1C) and viral skin diseases (Figure 1D) were also concentrated in younger children. In contrast, contact dermatitis (Figure 1E) predominated in adolescents (15–19 years).

Comparative Assessment of Regional Burden Across Disease Causes

The proportional contribution of skin disease incidence varied by region (Figure 1F). Fungal and bacterial skin diseases represented the largest share of cases across most regions, whereas contact dermatitis, cellulitis, and other bacterial conditions contributed less.

For DALYs (Figure 1G), acne vulgaris and bacterial infections were the main contributors. Lower-SDI regions showed a higher DALY proportion from infectious skin diseases (eg, cellulitis, bacterial, fungal, and parasitic infections).

Comparison of Death Rates from Bacterial Skin Diseases, Cellulitis, and Decubitus Ulcer Across SDI Regions in 2021

We identified all skin diseases associated with mortality and compared their age-standardized death rates across SDI levels in 2021 (Figure 1H). Bacterial skin diseases had the highest mortality burden globally and in every SDI category, particularly in low- and lower-middle-SDI regions. Temporal Trends in Skin and Subcutaneous Diseases Among Individuals Under 20 Years

The global incident cases of skin diseases increased continuously (Figure 1I), with bacterial and fungal skin diseases contributing the largest share and steadily rising in absolute numbers.

Total DALYs also increased over time (Figure 1J), mainly driven by viral skin diseases, atopic dermatitis, and acne vulgaris, highlighting their substantial global disability burden.

Sex-Specific Temporal Trends in Incidence Rates Among Individuals Under 20 years

Fungal skin diseases (Figure 2A) increased steadily in both sexes, while pruritus (Figure 2B) rose continuously with consistently higher rates in females. Scabies (Figure 2C) showed marked fluctuations, peaking in the mid-1990s and mid-2010s before declining. Viral skin diseases (Figure 2D) decreased until the mid-2000s and then increased, again with females having the highest rates. Incidence of other skin and subcutaneous diseases (Figure 2E) increased throughout the period, especially in females.

Age- and Sex-Specific Proportional Burden of Skin and Subcutaneous Diseases

In 2021, incidence patterns differed by age and sex (Figure 2F). Acne vulgaris dominated among adolescents (10–14 years), whereas fungal skin diseases and pyoderma were more common in children <10 years, with similar proportions between sexes.

For DALYs (Figure 2G), AD led in children <5 years, while acne vulgaris became the main contributor in older adolescents.

Frontier Analysis Across SDI Levels

For dermatitis (Figure 2H) and viral skin diseases (Figure 2J), high-SDI countries clustered near the frontier, whereas low-SDI countries had markedly higher incidence. For other skin and subcutaneous diseases (Figure 2I), incidence declined steadily with increasing SDI. The frontier represents the lowest achievable burden at a given level of socio-demographic development, and proximity to the frontier indicates more efficient disease control.

For other skin and subcutaneous diseases (Figure 3B), all three indicators were lowest at 5–9 years and rose again in older adolescents, with the highest burden in high-SDI regions. For viral skin diseases (Figure 3C), incidence, prevalence, and DALYs peaked at 5–9 years and declined afterward, again highest in high-SDI regions across sexes.

Global Age-Group Distribution of Burden Over Time (1990–2021)

In dermatitis, incidence peaked at ages 15–19 years (Figure 3D), whereas other skin and subcutaneous diseases were lowest at 5–9 years (Figure 3E). Viral skin diseases mainly affected children <10 years (Figure 3F).

For prevalence, dermatitis peaked in children <10 years (Figure 3G), other skin and subcutaneous diseases mirrored incidence (Figure 3H), and viral skin diseases peaked at 5–9 years (Figure 3I).

For DALYs, dermatitis burden was highest in children <10 years (Figure 3J), other skin and subcutaneous diseases concentrated in <5 years (Figure 3K), and viral skin diseases again peaked at 5–9 years (Figure 3L).

Regarding mortality, cellulitis and bacterial skin diseases mainly affected children <5 years (Figures 3M–N), whereas decubitus ulcer peaked at 15–19 years (Figure 3O).

Discussion

Dermatologic diseases, although rarely fatal, impose a substantial and long-term burden on both patients and society due to their chronic and recurrent nature.14,21–25 Despite the clinical significance of skin disorders, the disease burden among children and adolescents has been largely overlooked in global research.2,26–31 In this study, we used data from the GBD 2021 database to systematically analyze skin diseases in individuals younger than 20 years to estimate future trends.32,33 Our findings provide quantitative evidence to support policy development and strategic planning for pediatric and adolescent dermatological care. Given their large populations and significant global economic influence, China, Europe, and the USA were analyzed separately.34,35 We further applied forecasting models to examine future incidence patterns in these regions.

From 1990 to 2021, the burden of skin and subcutaneous diseases among individuals younger than 20 years increased from 653,365.48 to 860,806.73, with an EAPC of 0.45 (95% CI: 0.43–0.47). Similarly, dermatitis increased from 547,494.02 to 656,740.86, with an EAPC of 0.10 (95% CI: 0.07–0.13). Overall, skin diseases demonstrated a steady upward trend in individuals younger than 20 years. Although dermatologic diseases are generally non-fatal, three skin conditions in this study were associated with mortality. Among individuals younger than 20 years, bacterial skin diseases accounted for the highest number of deaths and the greatest mortality burden, whereas decubitus ulcer contributed to the lowest burden. Acne and atopic dermatitis represented the predominant contributors to the DALY burden of skin diseases in adolescents, and their burden continued to increase annually.

Frontier analysis revealed that countries with higher SDI levels were closer to the efficiency frontier and had lower incidence rates, indicating more effective disease control. In contrast, low-SDI countries deviated further from the frontier, suggesting a disproportionately higher burden relative to their development level. These findings highlight the influence of socioeconomic development on pediatric skin disease management.

In the age-stratified analysis, we observed that children aged 5–9 years consistently showed the lowest burden, with the lowest incidence, prevalence, and DALYs across all skin diseases. In contrast, children under 5 years and adolescents aged 15–19 years exhibited substantially higher DALY values. This pattern is largely driven by the significant disability burden caused by atopic dermatitis in early childhood and acne during adolescence. These findings highlight the need for early targeted interventions, particularly focusing on AD in younger children and acne in older adolescents.36–38 Among the three skin diseases associated with mortality, bacterial skin disease and cellulitis showed the highest number of deaths in children younger than 5 years, whereas decubitus ulcer had the highest mortality in adolescents aged 15–19 years.

Our ARIMA forecasting suggests that dermatitis and other skin and subcutaneous diseases are projected to decline in China and Europe but increase in the USA, while viral skin diseases are expected to rise globally, particularly among females in Europe. These findings indicate that prevention priorities should be tailored to regional disease dynamics rather than adopting a uniform global strategy.

Joinpoint analysis revealed that the burden of skin and subcutaneous diseases continued to rise over time in China, Europe, and the United States,39 whereas viral skin diseases frequently showed a turning point around 2019, highlighting the potential impact of COVID-19.7,40,41

Despite these strengths, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the study is based on GBD estimates, which rely on modeling and may be affected by data availability and quality across regions, particularly in low-SDI countries. Second, the use of aggregated population-level data limits the ability to account for individual-level heterogeneity and potential confounders. Third, forecasting results from ARIMA and BAPC models are inherently dependent on historical trends and model assumptions, which may not fully capture unexpected changes, such as policy interventions or emerging public health events. Finally, differences in diagnostic criteria and reporting practices across countries may introduce bias in cross-regional comparisons.

This is the first study that systematically evaluates all skin diseases included in the GBD database, analyzing both the overall category of skin and subcutaneous diseases and the subgroup of dermatitis, while also providing disease-specific analyses. In addition, we incorporated forecasting models to predict future incidence trends in China, Europe, and the United States. Although previous studies have used GBD 2019 data,4,42 our work offers a more comprehensive assessment by including all skin disease categories and applying predictive modeling. This enables a more complete understanding of the global burden of pediatric skin diseases and provides valuable evidence to support policy development for public health decision-makers worldwide.

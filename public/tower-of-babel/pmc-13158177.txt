Clinical Outcomes of Alternate Laparoscopic Linear Stapler in Primary Sleeve Gastrectomy: A Single-Center Retrospective Study of Safety and Feasibility
Authors: Sol Lee, Sang-Moon Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeStaple line bleeding and leak are recognized sources of morbidity in sleeve gastrectomy, with reported rates of 1–5% and 1–3%, respectively. This study evaluated the safety and feasibility of an alternate laparoscopic linear stapling device in primary sleeve gastrectomy.Materials and MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 100 consecutive patients who underwent primary laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy using an alternate laparoscopic linear stapler (iREACH OMNIA; Reach Surgical, Inc.) between January 2024 and February 2025 at Seoul Medical Center. Patients were stratified by body mass index (BMI) class: Class I (30–34.9 kg/m2; n=42), Class II (35–39.9 kg/m2; n=40), and Class III (≥40 kg/m2; n=18). Primary outcomes were staple line bleeding, leak, and reoperation rates. Secondary outcomes included estimated blood loss, length of hospital stay, 30-day complications, and 1-month weight loss.ResultsNo staple line complications were observed: staple line bleeding 0% (95% confidence interval [CI], 0–3.7%), leak 0% (95% CI, 0–3.7%), and reoperation 0%. Estimated blood loss was 98.3±42.5 mL (P=0.362) and length of stay 5.1±0.5 days (P=0.758), both uniform across BMI groups. A standardized stapler configuration (1 Black + 4–5 Purple cartridges) was used in over 90% of cases regardless of BMI class. At 1-month follow-up, % total weight loss by group was 11.75±3.35%, 13.42±6.37%, and 13.79±4.27% for Classes I, II, and III, respectively, and % excess weight loss was 49.93%, 41.43%, and 33.41%.ConclusionThe alternate laparoscopic linear stapler demonstrated a favorable safety profile in primary sleeve gastrectomy, with no observed staple line complications across BMI categories. Larger multicenter comparative studies with longer follow-up are warranted to confirm these findings.

INTRODUCTION

Laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy (LSG) has become the predominant metabolic and bariatric procedure, recognized for its surgical simplicity and robust clinical outcomes in weight reduction and comorbidity management [1]. Despite its established effectiveness, LSG carries inherent surgical risks, with staple line complications representing critical challenges that continue to drive innovation in surgical technology.

Staple line bleeding occurs in 1–5% of sleeve gastrectomy procedures, while leak rates range from 1–3% [234567]. These complications contribute significantly to postoperative morbidity, extended hospital stays, reoperation, and healthcare costs. The evolution of stapling technology has therefore been central to improving surgical outcomes in metabolic and bariatric surgery.

Over the past two decades, linear staplers have undergone substantial technological advancement. Early generation devices featured uniform staple heights and manual firing mechanisms, which limited tissue adaptation and firing control. Contemporary staplers have introduced features such as articulating tips, powered firing systems, and varied staple heights to accommodate different tissue thicknesses [8]. Despite these advances, staple line complications persist even with buttressing materials and oversewing techniques [29], suggesting that further device innovation may offer additional clinical benefit. However, most clinical data focus on established stapling platforms, and evidence regarding newer alternate stapler systems in metabolic and bariatric surgery remains scarce. As a result, surgeons have limited real-world outcome data to guide adoption of emerging stapler technologies in sleeve gastrectomy.

The iREACH OMNIA laparoscopic linear stapler (Reach Surgical, Inc., TEDA, Tianjin, China) represents one such alternate platform that incorporates progressive height technology and a controlled firing mechanism, features that may theoretically enhance staple line integrity and reduce tissue trauma. It deploys staples at three distinct heights within each cartridge to accommodate tissue thickness variation. Despite these design innovations, published clinical experience with the iREACH OMNIA stapler in sleeve gastrectomy is minimal, and its safety profile in routine bariatric practice has not been well defined.

Given the ongoing concern regarding staple line bleeding and leak, and the paucity of data on this alternate stapler system, further evaluation in a real-world bariatric cohort is warranted. Therefore, we conducted a single-center retrospective study of consecutive patients undergoing primary LSG using the iREACH OMNIA stapler to assess early safety and feasibility across body mass index (BMI) categories, hypothesizing that staple line bleeding and leak rates would remain within accepted thresholds (leak <3%, bleeding <5%).

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Study design and ethics

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at Seoul Medical Center. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Seoul Medical Center (SEOUL 2025-03-004), and the requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of this study.

2. Patient selection

We reviewed all patients who underwent metabolic and bariatric surgery using an alternate laparoscopic linear stapler (iREACH OMNIA) between January 2024 and February 2025. Inclusion criteria were: (1) primary LSG, and (2) completion of 1-month follow-up visits. Exclusion criteria were: (1) procedures other than primary LSG, (2) revisional surgery, and (3) incomplete 1-month follow-up. A total of 100 consecutive patients meeting these criteria were included in the analysis.

3. Surgical technique

All procedures were performed by experienced bariatric surgeons using a standardized technique. Gastric transection was initiated at the antrum using a black cartridge (1.75, 2.0, and 2.2 mm closed staple heights), followed by sequential firing along the gastric body and fundus using purple cartridges (1.25, 1.5, and 1.75 mm closed staple heights). Routine staple line oversewing was performed.

4. Stapler cartridge specifications

All staple reloads utilized graduated closed staple height configurations corresponding to the cartridge color: tan (0.75, 1.0, and 1.25 mm), purple (1.25, 1.5, and 1.75 mm), and black (1.75, 2.0, and 2.2 mm). Cartridge lengths of 45 mm and 60 mm were available for use at surgeon discretion.

5. Outcome measures

The primary objective was to evaluate the safety and feasibility of the iREACH OMNIA device by determining whether complication rates remained within published acceptable thresholds (staple line leak <3%, bleeding <5%) [234567].

Primary outcomes were:

- Staple line bleeding (defined as hemorrhage requiring intervention or transfusion)

- Staple line bleeding (defined as hemorrhage requiring intervention or transfusion)

- Staple line leak (defined as clinical or radiographic evidence of gastric contents extravasation)

- Staple line leak (defined as clinical or radiographic evidence of gastric contents extravasation)

- Reoperation rate within 30 days

- Reoperation rate within 30 days

Secondary outcomes included:

- Estimated blood loss

- Estimated blood loss

- Length of hospital stay

- Length of hospital stay

- 30-day complications and readmissions

- 30-day complications and readmissions

- Weight loss metrics at 1-month follow-up (percent total weight loss [%TWL] and percent excess weight loss [%EWL])

- Weight loss metrics at 1-month follow-up (percent total weight loss [%TWL] and percent excess weight loss [%EWL])

Complications were classified according to the Clavien-Dindo classification system [10].

6. Statistical analysis

Patients were stratified by BMI class: Class I (30–34.9 kg/m2; n=42), Class II (35–39.9 kg/m2; n=40), and Class III (≥40 kg/m2; n=18). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared using one-way analysis of variance. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages and compared using χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Exact binomial 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated for complication rates. Statistical significance was set at P<0.05. All analyses were performed using R version 4.2.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

2. Surgical details and perioperative outcomes

Operative parameters were consistent across all BMI categories. Mean operative time ranged from 55.6 to 59.8 minutes with no significant difference (P=0.133). Estimated blood loss averaged 98.3±42.5 mL with no significant difference across groups (P=0.362). Hospital length of stay was uniform at approximately 5.1 days (P=0.758). Notably, no staple line complications were observed: 0% staple line bleeding (95% CI, 0–3.7%), 0% staple line leak (95% CI, 0–3.7%), and 0 reoperations. No transfusions, intraoperative complications, postoperative complications, or 30-day complications/readmissions were recorded in any BMI group. No complications of any grade were observed according to the Clavien-Dindo classification (100% Grade 0) (Table 3).

3. Stapler cartridge configuration by color, length, and number across BMI categories

Stapler usage was remarkably uniform across BMI categories. Black cartridges (1.75, 2.0, and 2.2 mm height) averaged 1.0–1.1 per case, while purple cartridges (1.25, 1.5, and 1.75 mm height) averaged 4.8–5.0 per case (all P>0.05). The 60 mm cartridge length predominated (5.3–5.6 per case), with minimal 45 mm cartridge use. Total staplers per case ranged from 5.8 to 6.1 (P=0.287). Two standard configurations accounted for over 90% of cases: “1 Black+4 Purple” (35.7% in Class I, 45.0% in Class II, 27.8% in Class III) and “1 Black+5 Purple” (54.8% in Class I, 47.5% in Class II, 55.6% in Class III). This uniformity demonstrates feasibility of a simplified operative approach regardless of BMI (Table 4).

4. Weight loss outcomes at 1-month follow-up

At one month postoperatively, mean weight loss was 13.5±6.4 kg, and absolute weight loss increased with higher baseline BMI (10.1±2.9 kg vs. 13.8±7.8 kg vs. 16.6±5.8 kg for Classes I, II, and III respectively; P<0.001). %TWL did not differ significantly across groups (11.75±3.35% vs. 13.42±6.37% vs. 13.79±4.27%; P=0.200). %EWL was highest in Class I (49.93±16.29%) and decreased with increasing BMI class (Class II, 41.43±18.38%; Class III, 33.41±9.59%; P=0.001). This pattern reflects the mathematical relationship between %EWL and the excess weight denominator: as baseline BMI increases, the denominator grows disproportionately, yielding lower %EWL even when absolute and proportional weight loss are similar or greater (Table 5).

DISCUSSION

This study assessed the safety and feasibility of using an alternate laparoscopic linear stapler (iREACH OMNIA) in a consecutive cohort of 100 patients undergoing primary sleeve gastrectomy. No staple line complications were observed in any BMI category: staple line bleeding 0% (95% CI, 0–3.7%), staple line leak 0% (95% CI, 0–3.7%), and no reoperation or readmissions within 30 days. While these results are encouraging, they must be interpreted within the context of published literature rather than as evidence of superiority, given the absence of a direct comparator group. Published rates for staple line bleeding in sleeve gastrectomy range from 1–5%, and leak rates range from 1–3% [234567].

Staple line complications remain a significant concern and are a primary source of morbidity following sleeve gastrectomy procedures. According to a systematic review by Gagner, staple line leak rates have been reported between 1.1–2.4%, while bleeding rates range from 1.2% to 4.8% across large series [8]. Recent multicenter studies have shown that, despite implementation of buttressing materials and oversewing techniques, staple line complications persist [29]. The absence of staple line complications in our cohort is notable; however, it is important to recognize that with a sample size of 100 patients, this study has limited statistical power to detect rare complications. For example, with a true underlying leak rate of 1%, there is approximately a 37% probability of observing zero events by chance alone. Therefore, these findings should be considered preliminary evidence of feasibility rather than definitive proof of device superiority or complication elimination.

A secondary finding was the uniformity in stapler cartridge configuration across all BMI categories. In over 90% of cases, surgeons used a standardized protocol: one black cartridge for the antrum, followed by four to five purple cartridges for the body and fundus. This approach was consistent across all BMI groups, including class I (BMI 30–34.9), class II (BMI 35–39.9), and class III (BMI ≥40), with statistical analysis revealing no significant differences between categories (P>0.05 for all comparisons). The adoption of this standardized stapler configuration highlights the feasibility of a simplified operative strategy in primary sleeve gastrectomy. Such uniformity may help streamline intraoperative decision-making, minimize variability between surgeons, and contribute to procedural efficiency and reproducibility.

The iREACH OMNIA stapler system features progressive height technology, which deploys staples at three distinct heights within each cartridge to accommodate variations in tissue thickness. Additionally, its controlled firing mechanism may help minimize tissue trauma compared to rapid deployment systems. While these design features may theoretically contribute to the favorable outcomes observed in this study, without direct comparative data against conventional staplers, such mechanistic interpretations remain speculative and require validation in controlled trials.

Perioperative outcomes were consistent across BMI categories. The mean operative time ranged from 55.6 to 59.8 minutes, with no significant differences observed between groups. Similarly, estimated blood loss remained stable across all groups, varying only slightly between 91.7 and 107.8 mL. Hospital length of stay was also comparable, with patients in all BMI groups staying approximately 5.1 to 5.2 days. Importantly, no patients required transfusions, and there were no intraoperative complications or 30-day readmissions reported. These findings collectively support the strong safety profile of the alternate stapling device throughout the perioperative period. In terms of early postoperative outcomes, weight loss at 1-month post-surgery was in line with expectations for sleeve gastrectomy procedures. Patients achieved a mean %TWL of 12.79% and an %EWL of 43.56% at 1-month follow-up. These results further affirm that the alternate stapler performs reliably and effectively across the full range of obesity severity typically encountered in routine bariatric practice.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the single-center retrospective design limits generalizability, as outcomes may be influenced by institutional experience and patient selection. Second, the sample size of 100 patients, while sufficient to demonstrate feasibility, lacks statistical power to detect rare complications; with a true complication rate of 1%, approximately 37% of 100-patient samples would show zero events by chance. Third, the absence of a comparative arm with conventional staplers precludes direct assessment of relative safety or efficacy; therefore, statements comparing this device to existing technologies cannot be made from this data alone. Fourth, follow-up was limited to one-month, precluding evaluation of long-term complications including late leaks (which can occur for up to 6 weeks postoperatively), strictures, or gastroesophageal reflux disease, weight regain, and nutritional deficiencies. Fifth, all procedures were performed by experienced bariatric surgeons at a single institution, and results may differ in lower-volume centers or among surgeons with less experience. Finally, this study was supported by a research grant from the device manufacturer, which may introduce potential bias; however, the funding source had no role in study design, data collection, analysis, interpretation of results, or manuscript preparation.

Future investigations should employ multicenter, prospective study designs that allow for greater generalizability and more robust data collection. Extended follow-up periods will be essential to evaluate long-term outcomes, including the incidence of late complications such as late leaks, strictures, or gastroesophageal reflux, which could not be assessed in the current analysis. Randomized controlled trials comparing the OMNIA stapler to established devices would provide definitive evidence regarding comparative safety and efficacy. Additionally, cost-effectiveness analyses incorporating device costs, complication rates, and reoperation rates would inform value-based decision-making. Future research should also focus on specific patient subgroups, such as those with super-obesity (BMI ≥50), previous abdominal surgeries, or revisional procedures, to better define the scope of device utilization.

In this single-center retrospective series of 100 consecutive primary sleeve gastrectomy cases, use of an alternate laparoscopic linear stapler was associated with no observed staple line bleeding, leak, or reoperations across all BMI categories. A largely uniform stapler configuration appeared feasible regardless of baseline BMI and may help streamline the procedure without compromising short-term safety. Larger, multicenter comparative studies with longer follow-up are required to confirm these preliminary findings.

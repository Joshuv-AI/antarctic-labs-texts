Regression and statistical analysis of heat transfer enhancement in water/ethylene glycol (40/60) base molybdenum carbide (Mo2C) MXene nanofluid using a transient fractional model
Authors: Nadeem Ahmad Sheikh, Zulkhibri Bin Ismail, Ilyas Khan, Ghada R Elnaggar, S W Teklu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo investigate the effects of fractional order (), nanoparticle volume fraction (), magnetic field strength (), and Brinkman permeability () on both flow and heat transfer characteristics, a detailed parametric and statistical analysis is conducted. The statistical regression analysis shows that the volume fraction of nanoparticles and temperature have a strong positive correlation (coefficient = 0.94, p = 0.021) indicating that Mo2C MXene is an excellent heat absorption. On the other hand, the fractional parameter α has a strong negative effect on temperature field (coefficient = − 0.086, p < 0.001), which emphasizes its importance in describing the effects of thermal memory. The findings also indicate that, although MXene nanoparticles significantly increase thermal transport, an augmentation in magnetic field strength and Brinkman resistance cause a resistive Lorentz force and frictional drag, respectively, to prevent fluid flow. These results are physically informative about non-Fourier heat transfer in MXene-based nanofluids as well as offer invaluable information to developing high-performance thermal management systems and solar-energy applications.Keywords: Regression and statistical analysis, Molybdenum carbide (Mo2C) MXene nanofluid, Heat transfer enhancement, Water/ethylene glycol (40/60) mixture, Fractional model

Introduction

Developed working fluids that would increase the efficiency of solar thermal technologies and make them more sustainable is a topic of research primarily due to the increased demand to use solar energy in an efficient and sustainable way [1]. Direct absorption solar collectors (DASCs) have been of great interest due to their ability to allow volumetric absorption of solar radiation incident in the working fluid itself, thus eliminating surface thermal losses and, in general, attaining higher thermal efficiency compared to older surface-based collectors [2]. Nanofluids, which are engineered suspensions of nanoscale particles in base liquids, have been good candidates of DASCs because of increased optical absorption, thermal conductivity, and adjustable radiative characteristics [3]. Thermal energy systems commonly employ water-ethylene glycol mixtures because of their desirable thermophysical characteristics and reliable operation [4]. By varying the mass fraction of ethylene glycol, the mixture's freezing point can be lowered considerably, making these mixtures especially suitable as antifreeze working fluids in low-temperature and variable-climate applications [5]. Two-dimensional MXene nanoparticles, particularly molybdenum carbide, are now emerging as promising candidates for their high photothermal conversion rate, metallic-level conductivity, and broadband solar absorption. In dispersed form within hybrid base fluids, such as ethylene glycol–water mixtures commonly used in solar thermal systems for their desirable thermophysical stability and freezing-point properties, MXene nanofluids provide a promising platform that can serve as the backbone of high-performance solar energy harvesting systems [6, 7].

Nanofluids, engineered suspensions of nanoscale particles in conventional base fluids, have been extensively studied for their enhanced thermal conductivity, convective heat transfer, and radiative absorption relative to pure fluids [8–17]. Both experimental and theoretical studies have shown that metallic, metal-oxide, and carbon-based nanoparticles can significantly improve energy transport in thermal channels, porous media, and solar thermal equipment [18, 19]. Due to these benefits, nanofluids have become central to modern high-tech heat-transfer methods. Recent developments in nanofluid studies have proposed multi-component suspensions and complicated physical processes in order to increase thermal efficiency. As an example, the multi-regression analysis of electromagnetic bioconvective trihybrid nanofluids across inclined geometries has been an eye opener, considering nanoparticle synergy in flow control [20]. Moreover, machine-learning techniques have been effectively used to analyze Marangoni and interfacial processes in water-based nanofluids, providing a more predictive paradigm of stretched surface flows [21]. Combination of gyrotactic microorganisms with MHD hybrid nanofluids has also been investigated to understand the thermo-solutal effects to extend the implementation of these fluids to biological and chemical processing [22]. Also, recent publications have focused on entropy maximization and melting heat transfer in radiative dissipative flows, which offers a thermodynamic foundation of the enhancement of energy systems [23]. These advances are especially important in solar water heating systems, where the unsteady electromagnetic Powell-Eyring hybrid nanofluids have been demonstrated to have high potential in efficiency improvements because of optimized entropy analysis [24]. The present research is based on these premises, as it examines the fractional-order dynamics of MXene-based fluids in an oscillating setup.

Furthermore, nanomaterials with two-dimensional structures, such as MXenes, continue to attract increasing interest due to their high electrical conductivity, high surface area, manageable surface chemistry, and high photothermal conversion effectiveness [25, 26]. Transition-metal carbide and nitride MXenes have been proposed as next-generation thermal-fluid additives, and multiple studies have reported better heat-transfer performance and optical absorption compared with traditional nanoparticles [27, 28]. MXene nanoparticles are among them and have emerged as capable candidates due to their thermal stability, metallic-like conduction, and broadband solar absorption [29, 30]. The base fluid is also an important factor in the performance of nanofluids. Thermal engineering TWMs Water-ethylene glycol mixtures have wide usage in thermal engineering, as their thermophysical characteristics can be manipulated by changing the percentage of ethylene glycol, especially to have lower freezing points and better stability during operation [31]. Several studies have demonstrated that dispersing nanoparticles into such hybrid base fluids further enhances heat-transfer rates while maintaining antifreeze properties, which are useful in energy systems operating over a broad temperature range [32].

In continuum models of heat transport, the classical Fourier law has been widely used to model heat transport in nanofluids [33]. However, there is growing evidence that non-integer-order formulations are required to describe complex fluids that exhibit memory effects, microstructural interactions, or anomalous diffusion [12, 34]. Thus, the energy equations have been extended to include fractional-order derivatives to generalize Fourier's law and account for the non-local and hereditary properties of thermal transport [35, 36]. These fractional models have been implemented to study various nanofluid flows, with substantial effects of the fractional parameters on temperature distribution, entropy generation rate, and heat-transfer rates observed [37, 38]. Nanofluids have also been explored side by side with theoretical developments for their use in direct absorption solar energy systems, where the operating fluid absorbs the incident radiation directly instead of absorbing it through a solid surface [39, 40]. Research has showed that nanofluids can significantly enhance solar absorption and thermal properties relative to traditional base fluids [41, 42]. Recently, attention has turned to advanced two-dimensional materials and hybrid core fluids in such systems, although little attention has been paid to MXene nanofluids used with partial heat transfer models [43]. This gap encourages additional mathematical and statistical research to clarify the combined influence of the type of nanoparticles, base-fluid mixture, and fractional thermal transport procedures [44]. As fractional derivatives are employed in the present work, readers seeking deeper insight may consult the literature on fractional calculus, where foundational contributions explain wave propagation in viscoelastic materials [45], while Lorenzo and Hartley [46] introduce generalized functions appropriate for fractional operators; further theoretical development is provided by Das [47], and Mathai et al. Rehman et al. Ahmad et al. [53] analyzed fourth-grade non-Newtonian nanofluid flow over a Riga plate and found that electromagnetic control enhances thermal performance and flow stability. Kapustenko et al. [54] highlighted the importance of channel geometry in improving heat transfer in compact heat exchangers, while Szűcs [55] used CFD–FEM simulations to better understand thermal scaling in complex systems. Orosz et al. [56] proposed a graph-theoretic method to design energy-efficient heat exchanger networks, and Kovács et al. [57] investigated convective and radiative heat transfer in lithium-ion batteries, emphasizing thermal management needs. Morauszki et al. Finally, Orosz and Friedler [60] introduced a multi-solution framework for selecting practical heat exchanger network designs.

The research is driven by the growing need for high-performance thermal fluids in renewable energy and other advanced heat-transfer systems. Therefore, this paper aims to model and analyze MXene as a nanofluid in a water-ethylene glycol (40/60) mixture. Although recent advances in MXene-based nanofluids have produced fragmented heat-transfer equations, the combined effects of nanoparticles, hybrid base fluids, and non-Fourier fractional thermal transport in these equations have received little attention. The current study seeks to fill this void by offering a broad-based fractional-order and statistical analysis of the resulting heat-transfer properties, thereby providing information useful for the design of future thermal-management and solar-energy systems. Although much work has been done on metal and metal-oxide nanofluids, such materials are usually limited by poor dispersion in polar base fluids, chemical instability, and agglomeration, which reduces the effectiveness of heat transfer over time. Conversely, MXene nanoparticles are characterized by a high level of metallic conductivity, high photothermal conversion efficiency, and great stability in water/ethylene glycol solutions. Although these benefits exist, there is a gap in the knowledge: the interaction of MXene and hybrid base fluids in a non-Fourier fractional thermal transport system has never been studied.

This study addresses this deficit by introducing a novel mathematical framework that incorporates:

Material Innovation Utilizing MXene in a precisely balanced (40/60) water/ethylene–glycol mixture for enhanced antifreeze and thermal properties.

Mathematical Advancement Generalizing the energy equation using Caputo fractional derivatives to capture thermal memory effects that classical Fourier models overlook.

Statistical Rigor Moving beyond qualitative observations by employing regression analysis to quantify the significance of nanoparticle loading and fractional order on thermal performance.

By filling this void, the current study provides a robust, predictive framework that advances the understanding of non-local thermal transport in high-efficiency solar-energy harvesting and thermal management systems.

The fractional parameter represents the 'thermal memory' of the system. Unlike the classical Fourier model (), which assumes an instantaneous thermal response, the fractional-order model accounts for the non-local and hereditary nature of heat transport. A decrease in $\alpha$ introduces a retardation effect in the temperature evolution, as the current state of heat flux becomes dependent on the history of the temperature gradient. This flexibility allows the model to capture anomalous diffusion behaviors common in complex nanofluid mixtures that standard integer-order models fail to describe.

The declining velocity trend with increasing is a direct consequence of the magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) interaction. When the electrically conducting nanofluid moves through the transverse magnetic field, electric currents are induced, which in turn generate a body force known as the Lorentz force. This force acts in the direction opposite to the fluid motion, creating a 'magnetic damping' effect that dissipates the fluid's kinetic energy into heat through Joule heating, thus significantly thinning the velocity boundary layer.

The same decelerating trend with an increase in is observed in Fig. 7 and reflects the viscosity of a porous-medium resistance in Brinkman fluid. The increase in larger values of intensifies frictional forces and solid–fluid interactions at the interfaces, thereby reducing shear stress and the fluid velocity within the domain. Collectively, these numbers indicate that, in addition to porous-media resistance, both electromagnetic and porous-medium effects offer powerful flow-control systems, which, to a great extent, fundamentally change the hydrodynamic structure of the system and which can be used as a valuable source of information in the regulation of the processes of transport in magneto-nanofluid and porous-channel applications.

The Brinkman parameter represents the resistance offered by the solid matrix of the porous medium to the fluid flow. Physically, higher values indicate a reduction in the permeability of the medium, which intensifies the frictional drag and viscous dissipation at the fluid–solid interfaces. This increased resistance restricts the mobility of the nanofluid, leading to a more pronounced velocity decay compared to flow in an unrestricted clear medium.

Physical implications and industrial applications

The mathematical model invented in this research has great implications to various high-technological industries. The main areas of the use of this model are as follows:

Design of Direct Absorption Solar Collectors (DASCs) In comparison with surface-based collectors, DASCs use the fluid itself to absorb solar energy. We have shown that by maximizing the fraction of MXene in the volume and the fractional order engineers can maximize volumetric heat absorption, and consider the non-local thermal behavior of the fluid.

Thermal Management in Variable Climates The decision to use a water-ethylene glycol (40/60) mixture is physically important since it offers quality antifreeze characteristics to energy systems in sub-zero or variable climate conditions. The model gives the thermophysical predictions needed to make sure that the operation in such climates is stable.

Active Flow and Cooling Control It has been found that the rate of fluid flow can be slowed down accurately by the intensity of the magnetic field and the Brinkman resistance which implies that the two parameters can be utilized as the tuning knob in industrial cooling systems. By applying external magnetic fields, engineers can control the rate of flow and, hence, the rate of cooling of the system and avoid thermal runaway in power electronics.

Predictive Maintenance and Simulation The regression model that we developed has high statistical reliability, which means that this framework can be incorporated into digital twin software. This enables the efficiency of heat transfer in solar plants to be monitored in real-time, eliminating the use of expensive physical sensors as it can give the correct virtual forecasts of the thermal field.

Conclusion

The paper explores the thermal and hydrodynamic properties of the nanofluid flow of MXene in a direct absorption solar collector structure by considering the impacts of fractional parameters, nanoparticle volume fraction, magnetic field intensity, and Brinkman-type fluid effects. The results show that a wide range of temperature and velocity profiles can be achieved with all other physical parameters held fixed, thus giving a high degree of flexibility in modeling complex transport processes with the help of fractional-order modeling. The consistency of the data in terms of numerical tests and projection measures were also found to be statistically consistent thus confirming the validity of the mathematical formulation and its applicability in parametric studies. The electromagnetic damping and enhanced viscous resistance are also important in changing the physical properties of the transport of heat, and the diffusion of momentum in the presence of electric and magnetic fields and in porous media is dominated by electromagnetic damping and enhanced viscous resistance. These mutually competing processes permit optimisation of flow and thermal fields over a wide operating range, which can be applied especially in tuning direct absorption solar collectors. In general, physical interpretation and statistical validation demonstrate that the given model of the fractional nanofluid is a powerful and predictive tool to design the advanced solar-thermal system and to control the energy transport in magneto porous systems.

Key points

Quantified Enhancement Statistical analysis confirmed that nanoparticle volume fraction has a dominant positive effect on temperature (), providing a predictive tool for solar-thermal engineering.

Thermal Memory The fractional order proved essential in characterizing the non-instantaneous thermal response of the hybrid base fluid, a feature missing in classical Fourier models.

Controlled Dynamics The results show that the magnetic field and Brinkman parameter can be used to precisely regulate flow velocity in high-efficiency cooling systems.

Future directions

Future research should focus on experimental validation of the proposed fractional-regression model using real-time laboratory data. Additionally, exploring the interaction of thermal radiation with non-local kernels like the Atangana-Baleanu derivative could provide deeper insights into high-temperature solar applications.

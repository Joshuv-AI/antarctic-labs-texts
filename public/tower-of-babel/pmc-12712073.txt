Comprehensive Theoretical, Spectroscopic, Solvent, Topological and Antimicrobial investigation of 5-Chloro-6-fluoro-2-(2-pyrazinyl)-1H-benzimidazole
Authors: Fasiuddin G. S, A. Aathif Basha, Attar Kubaib, Syed Khasim, Gassoumi Bouzid, Adnan Almasoudi, Mohammad Rehan Ajmal
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study presents comprehensive theoretical, spectroscopic, and biological investigations of the compound 5-Chloro-6-fluoro-2-(2-pyrazinyl)-1H-benzimidazole (5CF2PB). Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations were performed at the B3LYP/6–311 + + G(d,p) level, and a Potential Energy Scan (PES) was carried out to identify the most stable conformer and its optimized geometry. Theoretical vibrational frequencies and Potential Energy Distribution (PED) analysis were correlated with experimental FT-IR and FT-Raman spectra, showing excellent agreement. Experimental UV–Vis and 1H–13C NMR spectra were recorded and compared with theoretical predictions using the IEF-PCM solvation model in DMSO, chloroform, and water. Frontier Molecular Orbital (FMO) analysis revealed a HOMO–LUMO energy gap of 4.043 eV, consistent with moderate chemical reactivity and optical absorption. The compound’s chemical reactivity descriptors, Molecular Electrostatic Potential (MEP), and topological parameters were analyzed through QTAIM, ELF, LOL, IRI, and RDG methods, providing insights into electronic structure and non-covalent interaction regions. Hirshfeld surface and 2D fingerprint analyses confirmed the dominance of halogen–hydrogen and halogen–nitrogen interactions in crystal packing. In-vitro antimicrobial screening demonstrated that 5CF2PB exhibits potent antibacterial and antifungal activities, particularly against Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Aspergillusniger, showing better efficacy than the standard drug ciprofloxacin. The PASS prediction suggested significant anti-mycobacterial potential, which was validated by molecular docking studies against the protein targets 6TE7 and 5O4L, yielding strong binding affinities and inhibition constants. Molecular dynamics simulations further confirmed the stability of the ligand–protein complexes. Overall, the integrated computational, spectroscopic, and biological analyses establish 5CF2PB as a promising multifunctional compound with potential pharmacological applications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-27674-7.

Introduction

Infectious pathogens represent a significant public health challenge globally1. Microbes have demonstrated a capacity to develop antibiotic resistance as a result of the widespread use of conventional antibiotics2. For instance, Staphylococcus aureus has exhibited resistance to antibiotics such as methicillin and penicillin, while Streptococcus pneumoniae shows resistance to macrolides and fluoroquinolones, which are significant contributors to community infections3. Despite the rising levels of drug resistance in microbes, there has been a limited development of new antibiotic classes featuring entirely novel mechanisms of action. Most advancement relies on the chemical modification of existing drugs to enhance their efficacy against antibiotic resistance mechanisms, either directly or indirectly targeting the inhibition of resistance mechanisms4. Compounds containing the benzimidazole moiety are extensively utilized in medicinal chemistry and drug development, with ongoing research aimed at discovering innovative applications for these heterocycles5.

Benzo[d]imidazole derivatives have attracted considerable interest in medicinal chemistry owing to their diverse pharmacological profiles, encompassing anticancer, antibacterial, antifungal, and antiviral activities. These heterocyclic structures offer a significant foundation for structural alterations, including halogen substitution and the addition of electron-rich or electron-withdrawing groups, to improve binding affinity and specificity for biological targets. The incorporation of halogens and heteroatoms is recognized to affect the electrical characteristics and non-covalent interaction patterns of these compounds, consequently enhancing their medicinal efficacy.

Benzimidazole derivatives exhibit diverse pharmacophoric properties utilized in drug design and represent versatile compounds with numerous medicinal and biological activities, including antibacterial6, antifungal7, antiviral8, anti-tumor9, anti-depressant10, and anti-coagulant effects11. Multiple investigations have demonstrated that pyrazole-benzimidazole hybrids exhibit antiproliferative action against lung, breast, and cervical cancer cell lines. A recent study identified distinctive benzimidazole bio-conjugates as prospective Chk2 (Checkpoint kinase 2) inhibitors, exhibiting significant anticancer effects and enhancing the efficacy of genotoxic anticancer medicines12. A recent study identified over twenty new benzimidazole-grafted benzosulfamide-containing pyrazole derivatives, one of which was evaluated for bioactivity as a possible inhibitor of tubulin polymerization. The study’s results, along with molecular docking data, established a compelling basis for enhancing the chemical as a prospective anticancer drug13.

We present one of the benzimidazole derivatives, 5-Chloro-6-fluoro-2-(2-pyrazinyl)-1H-benzimidazole, with a molecular weight of 248.64 g/mol and a boiling point of 479 °C. Based on a literature review, there is no reported vibrational analysis or quantum chemical investigation of 5-Chloro-6-fluoro-2-(2-pyrazinyl)-1H-benzimidazole (5CF2PB).

In this article, the main scope of this molecule, we examine the molecular geometry and spectral features of the 5CF2PB molecule using FT-IR, FT-Raman, NMR, and UV–Visible. The 5CF2PB molecule is optimized using density functional theory (DFT) for geometry, vibrational band assignments, natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis, frontier molecular orbitals (HOMOs-LUMOs), and molecular orbital (MEP) surface analysis14. Anti-microbial screening was performed in vitro to determine the biological activity and Molecular docking is an intriguing tool for investigating the interactions between biologically active synthetic chemicals and proteins. A standard reference ligand was utilized as an inhibitor in certain proteins, and this will be explained. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed on two therapeutically relevant protein targets (PDB IDs: 6TE7 and 5O4L) to assess the structural stability and dynamic behavior of 5CF2PB-protein complexes. In parallel, Hirshfeld surface analysis was used to investigate the intermolecular interactions inside the crystalline phase of 5CF2PB, providing information about its packing properties. In addition, topological surface descriptors such as Electron Localization Function (ELF), Localized Orbital Locator (LOL), Interaction Region Indicator (IRI), and Reduced Density Gradient (RDG) were used to understand electron density distributions and visualize regions responsible for key non-covalent interactions. This multi-tiered analysis provides a comprehensive evaluation of 5CF2PB physicochemical behavior and its potential as a biologically relevant molecule.

Procedure

Material and methods

Sigma Aldrich procured the title compound 5CF2PB, a white solid substance with a purity of 98%, without conducting any additional purification. The PerkinElmer spectrum has recorded the FT-IR spectrum of 5CF2PB in the 4000–400 cm-1 range. The Bruker RFS-27 has recorded a two-U.S.A. FT-Raman spectrum in the 4000–200 cm-1 range. The Bruker advance 500 MHz NMR Spectrometer has recorded 1H NMR spectra using DMSO as a solvent. Utilizing a Varian Cary 50 UV-Vis Spectrophotometer, the UV-Vis absorption spectrum of the title compound was investigated in the 200–700 nm range with DMSO as a solvent.

Computational method

The 5CF2PB title molecule’s structure was optimized via the DFT-B3LYP method with the 6–311 + + G(d,p) basis set from the GAUSSIAN 16W software package15–17. The choice of the B3LYP/6–311 + + G (d,p) basis set was made based on its established reliability and accuracy in computational studies of organic and heterocyclic compounds, particularly for predicting molecular geometries and spectroscopic properties. The 6–311 + + G (d, p) basis set is a split-valence triple-zeta basis set that includes polarization functions on both heavy atoms and hydrogen atoms.The optimized structure was used to determine the wavenumber with assignments using VEDA software18. The inaccuracies in theoretical and experimental frequencies, caused by vibrational anharmonicity and basis set incompleteness are mitigated by using a scaling factor of 0.96119. NBO calculations were performed to study the delocalization of electron density and transition energies which are used to evaluate the hyperconjugation and intermolecular interactions. HOMO–LUMO energies were evaluated using Gausssum 6.018. The UV analysis for the 5CF2PB was performed using TD-DFT (IEF-PCM) with different solvents such as DMSO, Chloroform, and water20,21. The same level of theory was used to study the NMR with the GIAO approach and the 1H chemical shifts were calculated and compared with the experimental data. Molecular docking studies were performed using Auto Dock 4.2.622 to analyze the ligand–protein interaction. The Electron Localization Function (ELF), Local Orbital Locator (LOL), Information Regarding Interactions (IRI) maps and NCI were visualized using Multiwfn (Version 3.8) software. The following settings were applied for each map: ELF: The grid was constructed with a resolution of 0.1 Å, and the threshold for ELF was set to 0.8 to highlight the electron localization regions. LOL: For LOL visualization, the orbital localization index was calculated with a cutoff value of 0.02, providing insight into localized bonding regions. IRI: The electron density was plotted with a density cutoff of 0.05 au to visualize the regions of strong electron density and interaction regions critical for binding23. Hirshfeld surface and 2D fingerprint plots were generated to understand the intermolecular interactions in the crystal structure. Molecular docking was initially performed to identify the binding affinity of the ligand with target proteins 6TE7 and 5O4L. The best docked complexes were subjected to molecular dynamics (MD) simulations using standard protocols to evaluate their structural stability. Various parameters such as RMSD, RMSF, Rg, SASA, and hydrogen bonding were analyzed. MD simulations were performed using GROMACS with default parameters to study protein behavior in an aqueous environment24.

Anti-microbial screening

The antimicrobial activity of the 5CF2PB title compound was tested against Gram-positive, Gram-negative, and fungus Staphylococcus aureus (MTCC-3160),Enterococcus faecalis(MTCC-3159),Pseudomonas aeruginosa(MTCC-4030),Escherichia coli (MTCC-1667),Aspergillusniger(MTCC 282),Candida albicans(MTCC 227) respectively. All the above strains are obtained from Microbial Type Culture Collection, Institute of Microbial Technology, Chandigarh. The national council for clinical laboratory standards was adhered to for the antibacterial screening of the title chemical using the disc diffusion technique. Mueller–Hinton agar (MHA) was used for bacterial culture, whilst potato dextrose agar was utilized for fungal strains. A 6 mm diameter disc was positioned in the agar medium and infused with three distinct doses of 100, 50 and 25 μg/ml of the specified drug, thereafter let to diffuse for 2 h at 30 °C. The plates are placed on a flat surface and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C for bacterial proliferation and 48 h at 28 °C for fungal growth. The inhibitory zone is quantified in millimeters. Experiments were conducted in triplicate, using ciprofloxacin as a positive control for comparative analysis of the data.

Results and discussions

Potential energy surface (PES) and molecular geometry

The Potential Energy Surface (PES) scan was performed by varying the torsion angles N5–C12–C9–N14 and C15–C12–C9–N3. The selected dihedral angles were rotated from –180° to + 180° in 36 steps of 10°, defining the most stable conformational orientations of the molecule. The calculations were carried out using the DFT-B3LYP/6–311 + + G(d,p) basis set. A total of 37 energy conformers of the 5CF2PB compound were obtained from the PES scan, as illustrated in Fig. 1.The most stable conformer of the 5CF2PB molecule was observed at –179.86° and –179.99°, exhibiting potential energies of –1190.45 a.u. and –1196.62 a.u. for the N5–C12–C9–N14 and C15–C12–C9–N3 dihedral angles, respectively. The optimized molecular structure of 5CF2PB is shown in Fig. 2.The fundamental geometrical parameters, including bond lengths and bond angles, were determined for all 23 atoms in the molecule. Table 1 presents both the theoretically and experimentally obtained geometrical parameters of the optimized structure. To the best of our knowledge, the crystal structure of this compound has not yet been reported in the Cambridge Structural Database (CSD).For comparison, Faizi et al. reported the experimental structure of 2-(pyren-1-yl)-1H-benzimidazole25. Ghani et al. investigated the structural, spectral, and biological properties of 2-chloromethyl-1H-benzimidazole hydrochloride26, while Saral et al. carried out comparative structural and spectroscopic studies on two benzimidazole derivatives, 1-methyl-2-(2′-hydroxy-4′-chlorophenyl)benzimidazole and 1-methyl-2-(2′-hydroxy-4′-methoxyphenyl)benzimidazole27. These compounds exhibit close structural similarity to the present molecule. In the title compound, the C–C bond length ranges from 1.382 to 1.460 Å, the C–H bond length from 1.081 to 1.085 Å, and the N–C bond length from 1.314 to 1.380 Å. All these values are in good agreement with the literature25–27. The longest bond length, C11–C14 = 1.748 Å, corresponds to the linkage between the benzimidazole moiety and the chlorine atom in the 5CF2PB compound and arises due to the high electronegativity of chlorine. The shortest bond length, N3–H18 = 1.009 Å, originates from the benzimidazole ring.The smallest bond angle, N3–C7–C8, is 104.71°, whereas the largest, N3–C7–C10, is 132.97°. The most significant stabilization occurs through π → π* delocalization within the conjugated system of the benzimidazole and pyrazine rings. The interaction N₄–C₉(π) → C₇–C₈(π*) exhibits a large stabilization energy of 173.76 kcal mol⁻1, indicating extensive π-electron delocalization over the aromatic framework. This strong conjugative overlap enhances molecular planarity and rigidity, thereby stabilizing the benzimidazole core. Similarly, interactions such as N₆–C₁₇(π) → C₁₂–C₁₅(π*) and N₅–C₁₆(π) → C₁₂–C₁₅(π*) show stabilization energies of 163.47 kcal mol⁻1 and 111.96 kcal mol⁻1, respectively, confirming strong π-conjugation within the pyrazine–benzimidazole conjugated pathway. Moderate σ → σ* and σ → π* interactions, such as N₄–C₉(σ) → C₈–C₁₁(σ*) (5.32 kcal mol⁻1) and C₇–C₈(σ) → N₃–H₁₈(σ*) (4.80 kcal mol⁻1), contribute additional stabilization through hyperconjugation and bond–antibond coupling. These interactions are localized within the same molecular framework and therefore represent intra-molecular delocalizations, not intermolecular effects. Non-bonding to antibonding interactions (n → σ* and n → π*) also play a key role. The lone pair on nitrogen N₃ donates electron density to the antibonding C₇–C₈(π*) orbital with a stabilization energy of 35.46 kcal mol⁻1, while the halogen substituents (Cl and F) engage in weaker σ(Cl–C) → σ*(C–C) and σ(F–C) → σ*(C–C) transitions (1.3–2.1 kcal mol⁻1), reflecting the electron-withdrawing behavior of these atoms that fine-tunes the overall electronic distribution. The weakest interaction, observed between N₆–C₁₇(π*) → C₁₇–H₂₃(σ*), has a stabilization energy of only 0.66 kcal mol⁻1. This very small value does not correspond to an intermolecular hydrogen bond but rather to a weak intramolecular hyperconjugative interaction—a minimal delocalization of π-electron density toward an adjacent σ*(C–H) orbital. Such interactions, though minor, subtly influence molecular reactivity and local polarization39. Overall, the high-energy π → π* transitions dominate the electronic stabilization of 5CF2PB, reinforcing its aromatic character and conjugation between benzimidazole and pyrazine moieties. The moderate σ → σ* and n → π* delocalizations contribute secondary stabilization, while the halogen substituents modulate the electron density through inductive withdrawal and weak hyperconjugation. These combined intra-molecular effects substantiate the molecule’s observed chemical stability and its potential for strong π–π stacking and charge-transfer interactions in biological binding environments.

NMR analysis

The experimental and theoretical comparison of the 1H and 13C NMR spectra of 5-Chloro-6-fluoro-2-(2-pyrazinyl)-1H-benzimidazole (5CF2PB) was performed using the GIAO method at the B3LYP/6–311 + + G(d,p) level with the IEF-PCM solvent model40. The calculations were carried out in the gas phase and in three solvents DMSO, chloroform, and water, to evaluate solvent effects on the chemical environment. Figure S3 presents the experimental 1H NMR spectrum, while the theoretical and experimental chemical shift values are summarized in Table S1. In DMSO solution, the benzimidazole protons typically resonate at 7.71–7.17 ppm41; in the present compound, the aromatic proton (H-20) appears at 7.9 ppm experimentally and 8.04 ppm theoretically, slightly deshielded due to the influence of halogen atoms at the 5- and 6-ring positions. The pyrazine proton (H-23) resonates at 8.7 ppm experimentally and 8.87 ppm theoretically, showing deshielding caused by adjacent nitrogen atoms in the heterocyclic ring. For 13C NMR, the chemical shifts appear between 101 and 164 ppm, consistent with theoretical predictions (101.87–164.54 ppm in gas phase; 104.69–163.72 ppm in DMSO; 103.72–163.94 ppm in chloroform; and 104.75–163.71 ppm in water)42. The most deshielded signal near 163 ppm corresponds to the ipso carbon attached to fluorine (C–F), followed by carbons adjacent to nitrogen atoms (150–155 ppm), while the ipso carbon attached to chlorine (C–Cl) appears around 125 ppm. The overall close agreement between experimental and theoretical data (within ± 2–3 ppm) confirms that solvent effects are negligible and validates the accuracy of the computational approach used.

Topological surface and NCI analyses

Topological surface analyses provided further insight into the electron distribution and non-covalent interaction regions of 5CF2PB. The ELF map reveals regions of high electron localization (red zones) around the heteroatoms (N, F, and Cl) and in the vicinity of the double bonds within the benzimidazole core. These red contours correspond to areas of strong electron pairing, signifying localized lone pairs or π-bonding regions (Fig. 10). The localization observed around the nitrogen atoms arises from their lone-pair electrons, which contribute to the molecule’s nucleophilicity and play an essential role in hydrogen-bond and halogen-bond formation. The fluorine and chlorine atoms exhibit intense ELF localization, indicating strongly bound, non-delocalized electron density, consistent with their high electronegativity. This electron withdrawal effect enhances the polarization across adjacent bonds, reinforcing non-covalent interactions such as H⋯Cl and H⋯F identified in the Hirshfeld surface analysis. The regions corresponding to C = C double bonds within the aromatic ring display a continuous, moderately delocalized ELF basin, characteristic of π-electron delocalization over the conjugated system. This delocalization enhances the overall electronic stability and contributes to planarity and rigidity of the benzimidazole framework. In contrast, σ-bonds show more localized electron densities along internuclear axes, confirming the covalent bonding framework.Fig. 10ELF for 5CF2PB.

Conclusion

In this study, a comprehensive theoretical and experimental investigation was performed on 5-Chloro-6-fluoro-2-(2-pyrazinyl)-1H-benzimidazole (5CF2PB). Potential Energy Surface (PES) analysis confirmed the most stable conformer and its optimized geometry, with calculated bond lengths and bond angles in excellent agreement with literature data. Theoretical vibrational assignments, supported by Potential Energy Distribution (PED) analysis, correlated well with the experimental FT-IR and FT-Raman spectra, confirming the accuracy of the optimized structure. Natural Bond Orbital (NBO) analysis revealed strong intramolecular π → π* interactions within the benzimidazole ring, with a maximum stabilization energy of 173.76 kcal mol⁻1, contributing significantly to molecular stability. The 1H NMR spectra across gas, DMSO, chloroform, and water phases exhibited negligible solvent influence, indicating a stable electronic environment. UV–Vis analysis in DMSO demonstrated excellent agreement between experimental and TD-DFT results, confirming the π → π* and n → π* transitions. The compound displayed a HOMO–LUMO gap of 4.043 eV, consistent with its experimental optical band gap (~ 3.7 eV), signifying moderate chemical reactivity and reasonable stability. Hirshfeld surface analysis indicated that halogen–hydrogen and halogen–nitrogen contacts predominantly govern the crystal packing, enhancing non-covalent interaction potential. Topological analyses (ELF, LOL, IRI, and RDG) further revealed electron-rich domains and weak interaction regions crucial for biomolecular binding. Molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations validated strong and stable binding of 5CF2PB to biological targets (6TE7 and 5O4L), supported by consistent RMSD, minimal RMSF fluctuations in the active site, stable Rg and SASA profiles, and persistent hydrogen bonding. Biological screening demonstrated significant antibacterial and antifungal activity, particularly against Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Aspergillusniger, surpassing the standard drug ciprofloxacin. The PASS prediction confirmed probable antimycobacterial potential with strong binding affinity toward the selected receptors.

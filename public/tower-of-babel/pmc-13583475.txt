Non-exposure organ-preserving resection of gastric gastrointestinal stromal tumors using the CLEAN-NET technique
Authors: Chatbadin Thongchuam, Chainarong Phalanusitthepha, Vitoon Chinswangwatanakul, Thawatchai Akaraviputh, Asada Methasate, Jirawat Swangsri, Tharathorn Suwatthanarak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGastric gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GISTs) require oncologically safe resection while preserving gastric function. The Combination of Laparoscopic and Endoscopic Approaches to Neoplasia with Non-Exposure Technique (CLEAN-NET) integrates laparoscopic and endoscopic guidance to achieve full-thickness gastric resection while minimizing deformity and preventing tumor exposure. We present a video demonstration of CLEAN-NET in a 73-year-old female with a 4 cm gastric GIST at the lesser curvature near the incisura angularis. A cooperative laparoscopic-endoscopic approach was employed, including endoscopic submucosal injection, circumferential seromuscular incision, and tumor eversion into the peritoneal cavity. Full-thickness resection was completed using a laparoscopic linear stapler without spillage. The patient recovered uneventfully, resumed oral intake early, and was discharged without complications. Pathology confirmed a low-risk GIST with negative margins. CLEAN-NET is a safe, reproducible, and organ-preserving technique for selected gastric GISTs.Keywords: Gastrointestinal stromal tumors, Endoscopic gastrointestinal surgery, Endoscopic full-thickness resection, Laparoscopic-assisted surgery

INTRODUCTION

Gastric gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GISTs) are the most common mesenchymal tumors of the gastrointestinal tract that arise from the interstitial cells of Cajal. Surgical resection remains the cornerstone of curative treatment for localized gastric GISTs, as lymph node metastasis is uncommon and routine lymphadenectomy is unnecessary. The main treatment is complete resection with negative margins while preserving gastric anatomy and function [1]. However, tumor rupture or exposure during surgery is associated with an increased risk of peritoneal dissemination and recurrence [2]. The Combination of Laparoscopic and Endoscopic Approaches to Neoplasia with Non-Exposure Technique (CLEAN-NET) was developed to integrate laparoscopic and endoscopic guidance, enabling full-thickness gastric resection while minimizing gastric deformity and preventing tumor exposure, thereby reducing the risk of tumor seeding [3-5].

We report a case of a 73-year-old Thai female who presented with a 4 cm subepithelial lesion located at the lesser curvature of the stomach and was treated with the CLEAN-NET technique.

CASE

This study demonstrates the case of a 73-year-old Thai female, without comorbidities, who was evaluated for abdominal discomfort for 2 months. On evaluation, the esophagogastroduodenoscopy finding showed a 4 cm subepithelial lesion with central umbilication at the lesser curvature of the stomach (Fig. 1A). She was re-evaluated with endoscopic ultrasonography that showed a 4.0 × 3.0 cm heterogenous hypoechoic mass in the gastric body originating from the 4th layer (Fig. 1B). The tumor was biopsied with a fine needle, and the pathological report showed a spindle cell neoplasm compatible with GISTs. The whole abdomen was evaluated with computed tomography (CT) with intravenous contrast for the evaluation of the exophytic part. The abdominal CT revealed a well-defined endophytic submucosal mass about 4.7 × 3.7 × 3.7 cm with heterogeneous enhancement at the lesser curvature of the gastric body. No perigastric fat stranding or extramural tumor extension. No gross arterial enhancement, intratumoral fat, calcification, or hemorrhage. All of these findings are probably gastric GIST (Fig. 2). After a team discussion, the patient was scheduled for the CLEAN-NET procedure. The intraluminal tumor location was identified by endoscopic submucosal injection of indigo carmine which enhances visualization. Laparoscopic dissection along the lesser curvature exposed the posterior gastric wall. After tumor identification under laparoscopic visualization, the seromuscular layer was circumferentially incised and dissected layer by layer to mobilize the tumor from the muscularis propria. Complete eversion of the tumor into the peritoneal cavity was confirmed by endoscopic air insufflation. Full-thickness gastric resection was then performed using a laparoscopic linear stapler without spillage of gastric contents (Fig. 3). An air leak test confirmed the integrity of the staple line and the gastric contour (Fig. 4A). The specimen was retrieved from the peritoneal cavity (Fig. 4B). The surgical procedure is demonstrated in Supplementary Video 1. Postoperatively, the pathological report confirmed a GIST measuring 5.2 × 3.7 × 3 cm, with a mitotic rate of 1 per 50 high-power fields and discovered on GIST-1 positive. The resection margins were negative. The patient resumed a soft diet on postoperative day 2. The closed-suction drain was removed, and the patient was discharged uneventfully on postoperative day 4.

DISCUSSION

Surgical resection with negative margins remains the standard treatment for GISTs. Traditionally, laparoscopic or robotic gastric wedge resection has been performed to achieve oncologically adequate margins; however, this approach can result in excessive loss of normal gastric tissue. Several minimally invasive alternatives have been developed [3]. Various cooperative and endoscopic techniques, including laparoscopic endoscopic cooperative surgery (LECS), CLEAN-NET, non-exposed endoscopic wall-inversion surgery (NEWS), submucosal tunneling endoscopic resection, and endoscopic full-thickness resection, share the same fundamental concept of organ-preserving local resection. However, these techniques differ in key procedural characteristics, such as exposure versus non-exposure approaches, tumor inversion into or out of the gastric lumen, specimen retrieval via the oral route or abdominal cavity, and the relative dominance of endoscopic versus laparoscopic control. Endoscopic techniques are generally limited to tumors smaller than 3 cm because specimen retrieval must be feasible through the upper esophageal sphincter. Classical LECS was introduced to improve resection precision; however, it involves an intentional opening of the gastric wall, raising concerns regarding peritoneal contamination and tumor dissemination. Consequently, non-exposure techniques such as CLEAN-NET and NEWS were developed to achieve oncologically safe resection while preserving gastric contour and function [5]. The CLEAN-NET technique involves submucosal dissection followed by mucosal inversion toward the peritoneal cavity without exposure of the gastric lumen. In our case, indigo carmine dye was used to enhance visualization of the submucosal plane during dissection. Indigo carmine is widely available and cost-effective, making it particularly suitable for use in low- and middle-income countries. Several studies have demonstrated that CLEAN-NET can achieve smaller resection margins than conventional wedge resection while maintaining adequate oncological clearance [5-7]. A recent meta-analysis demonstrated that LECS-based techniques have comparable conversion rates, adverse events, and procedure times to conventional endoscopic or laparoscopic approaches, with trends toward shorter hospital stays, fewer complications, and higher R0 resection rates [8].

Preoperative surgical planning is essential to overcome technical challenges and ensure successful completion of surgery. In this case, the tumor measured approximately 4 cm and was located near the incisura angularis at the posterior aspect of the lesser curvature. Owing to its size, retrieval of the specimen by the oral route was not feasible. Therefore, the CLEAN-NET technique was selected.

The CLEAN-NET technique is considered safe and effective for gastric GISTs smaller than 5 cm [5]. For tumors larger than 5 cm, the risk of postoperative gastric deformity or stenosis may increase. In addition, tumors with malignant features are not suitable candidates for CLEAN-NET due to the risk of tumor spillage. In such cases, conventional gastric wedge resection is the preferred surgical approach [3]. Tumor location is also a critical factor. Lesions located within 1–2 cm of the esophagogastric junction (EGJ) present technical challenges, as application of a linear stapler may compromise the gastric lumen and increase the risk of postoperative EGJ stenosis. However, Fujishima et al. [9] have described modified CLEAN-NET techniques to address tumors near the EGJ or pyloric ring. Compared with other LECS-based or non-exposure techniques, CLEAN-NET may reduce procedural time because full-thickness resection is completed using a linear stapler, whereas alternative techniques such as classical LECS or NEWS rely more on advanced endoscopic procedures, including endoscopic submucosal dissection [3]. Therefore, CLEAN-NET represents a step towards a more standardized and reproducible LECS-based surgical approach. In conclusion, the CLEAN-NET technique provides a safe, non-exposure, organ-preserving approach for selected gastric GISTs smaller than 5 cm. With appropriate patient selection, it achieves oncologically adequate margins while reducing procedural complexity and operative time compared to other LECS-based techniques.

Treewidth versus clique number. V. Further connections with tree-independence number
Claire Hilaire, Martin Milanič, Đorđe Vasić

Abstract
We continue the study of ( 𝗍𝗐 , ω ) (\tw,\omega) -bounded graph classes, that is, hereditary graph classes in which large treewidth is witnessed by the presence of a large clique, and the relation of this property to boundedness of the tree-independence number, a graph parameter introduced independently by Yolov in 2018 and by Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel in 2024. Dallard et al. showed that bounded tree-independence number is sufficient for ( 𝗍𝗐 , ω ) (\tw,\omega) -boundedness, and conjectured that the converse holds. While this conjecture has been recently disproved, it is still interesting to determine classes where the conjecture holds; for example, the conjecture is still open for graph classes excluding an induced star, as well as for finitely many forbidden induced subgraphs. In this paper, we identify further families of graph classes where ( 𝗍𝗐 , ω ) (\tw,\omega) -boundedness is equivalent to bounded tree-independence number. We settle a number of cases of finitely many forbidden induced subgraphs, obtain several equivalent characterizations of ( 𝗍𝗐 , ω ) (\tw,\omega) -boundedness in subclasses of the class of complements of line graphs, and give a short proof of a recent result of Ahn, Gollin, Huynh, and Kwon [SODA 2025] establishing bounded tree-independence number for graphs excluding a fixed induced star and a fixed number of independent cycles. Keywords: treewidth, clique number, tree-independence number, hereditary graph class, line graph MSC Classes (2020): 05C75, 05C05, 05C69, 05C83, 05C76.

In this paper, we contribute to the study of

The notion of

A graph class is said to be

The

In this context, Yolov

Our results

We identify further families of graph classes for which
1.1

We first observe that a result of Chudnovsky and Seymour on graphs with bounded splitness
[
14
]
leads to the resolution of

As a consequence of this result and the fact that line graphs are claw-free, we establish a result analogous to

With some additional arguments, we in fact obtain the following refined result, giving several equivalent characterizations of

For a graph class
\mathcal{G}

Theorem 1.7
.

For every graph class
\mathcal{G}

1.

The class
\overline{L(\mathcal{G})}

-bounded.

2.

The class
\overline{L(\mathcal{G})}
has bounded tree-independence number.

3.

There exists a positive integer

such that every graph in
\overline{L(\mathcal{G})}
is

-free.

4.

There exists a positive integer
s
such that every graph in
\overline{L(\mathcal{G})}
has a vertex cover inducing a subgraph with independence number at most
s

5.

There exists a positive integer
s
such that every graph in
\mathcal{G}
is

-subgraph-free.

Preliminaries

All the graphs considered in this paper are finite, simple (that is, without loops or parallel edges), and undirected.
An

A

For

A graph class

Lemma 2.1

Every graph class with bounded tree-independence number is

-bounded.

The second gives a particular family of obstructions to

-boundedness (see, e.g.,
[

Lemma 2.2
.

The class of balanced complete bipartite graphs is not

-bounded.

Dallard et al. gave the following characterization of chordal graphs in terms of tree-independence number.

Lemma 2.3

(Theorem 3.3 in
[

]
)
.

A graph

.

Given two vertex-disjoint graphs

and

, their

is the graph

with vertex set

and edge set

.
Given a graph

in which two distinct vertices are adjacent if and only if they are nonadjacent in

.
Given a graph

Excluding a clique partition graph

Chudnovsky and Seymour define in

can be partitioned into sets

The

The following result completely characterizes minimal hereditary graph classes with unbounded splitness.
A

is a disjoint union of complete graphs, or, equivalently, any graph whose complement is complete multipartite.

Theorem 3.1

(Chudnovsky and Seymour
[
14
]
)
.

For every clique partition graph

and complete multipartite graph

, there exists an integer

such that every

-free graph is
k
-split.

Theorem

3.1
implies the validity of

for graph classes excluding some clique partition graph.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary graph class that does not contain all clique partition graphs.
Then,
\mathcal{G}
is
(\tw,\omega)
-bounded if and only if
\mathcal{G}
has bounded tree-independence number.

Proof.

Since, by
Lemma

2.1
, any graph class with bounded tree-independence number is
(\tw,\omega)
-bounded, it suffices to prove the forward implication.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is a
(\tw,\omega)
-bounded hereditary graph class that does not contain all clique partition graphs.
By

2.2
,
\mathcal{G}
does not contain all complete bipartite graphs.
Let

be a clique partition graph and let

be a complete bipartite graph such that

and

do not belong to
\mathcal{G}
.
Then, every graph in
\mathcal{G}
is

-free.
Hence, by

, there exists an integer

such that every graph in
\mathcal{G}
is

-split.
Let

be a graph from

.
Partition

into two sets

such that

and

has independence number at most

Since

is hereditary and

-bounded,

Let

be a tree decomposition of the graph

Then, adding the vertices in

Note that

We first recall two theorems from Bonamy et al.

Theorem 4.1

Lemma 5.1

)
.

Let

Then
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\geqslant\mathsf{ibn}(G)

Our approach is based on the following structural characterization of

The independence number of the join of

6
Tree-independence number of
\{P_{4}+P_{1},C_{4}\}
-free graphs

In this section, we prove
Theorem

1.10
, stating that that the tree-clique-cover number of every
\{P_{4}+P_{1},C_{4}\}
-free graph is at most
3
.
Our approach is based on first deriving an analogous bound on the tree-independence number, by showing that the class of
\{P_{4}+P_{1},C_{4}\}
-free graphs is a subclass of the class of
K_{2,3}
-induced-minor-free graphs and applying a known bound on the tree-independence number of
K_{2,3}
-induced-minor-free graphs from
[
21
]
.
Then, we show how to refine the bound by invoking a result on the chromatic number of
\{2K_{2}
, gem
\}
-free graphs from
[
6
]
.

We start with some definitions.
A
prism
is a graph made of three vertex-disjoint chordless paths
P^{1}=a_{1}\ldots b_{1}
,
P^{2}=a_{2}\ldots b_{2}
, and
P^{3}=a_{3}\ldots b_{3}
of length at least 1, such that
\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}
and
\{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\}
are triangles and no edges exist between the paths except those of the two triangles.
A prism is
long
if at least one of its three paths has length at least 2.

A
pyramid
is a graph made of three chordless paths
P^{1}=a\ldots b_{1},

P^{2}=a\ldots b_{2}
,
P^{3}=a\ldots b_{3}
of length at least 1, two of which have length at least 2, vertex-disjoint except at
a
, and such that
\{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\}
is a triangle and no edges exist between the paths except those of the triangle and the three edges incident to
a
.

A
theta
is a graph made of three internally vertex-disjoint chordless paths
P^{1},P^{2},P^{3}
of length at least 2 with extremities
a,b
and such that no edges exist between the paths except the three edges incident to
a
and the three edges incident to
b
.

A
hole
in a graph is a chordless cycle of length at least 4.
A
wheel

W=(H,x)
is a graph formed by a hole
H
(called the
rim
) together with a vertex
x
(called the
center
) that has at least three neighbors in the hole.
A
sector
of
W
is a subpath of
H
between two consecutive neighbors of
x
.
Note that
H
is edgewise partitioned into the sectors of
W
. Also, every wheel has at least three sectors.
A wheel is
broken
if at least two of its sectors have length at least 2.

The above families appear in the following characterization of induced subgraphs that must be present in any graph containing
K_{2,3}
as an induced minor, due to Dallard et al.
[
16
]
.

Lemma 6.1

(Dallard et al.
[
16
]
)
.

A graph contains
K_{2,3}
as an induced minor if and only if it contains a long prism, a pyramid, a theta, or a broken wheel as an induced subgraph.

Hence, to show that every
\{P_{4}+P_{1},C_{4}\}
-free graph is
K_{2,3}
-induced-minor-free, it suffices to show the following.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
H
be a long prism, pyramid, theta, or a broken wheel.
Then,
H
contains either
P_{4}+P_{1}
or
C_{4}
as an induced subgraph.

Proof.

Let
H
be a long prism, with triangles
\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}
and
\{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\}
, and vertex-disjoint chordless paths
P^{1}=a_{1}\ldots b_{1}
,
P^{2}=a_{2}\ldots b_{2}
, and
P^{3}=a_{3}\ldots b_{3}
.
If two of the paths of
H
have length
1
, then there is an induced
C_{4}
in
H
.
Hence, we can assume that w.l.o.g. that
P^{1}
and
P^{2}
each have length at least 2.
Let
x
and
y
be vertices adjacent to
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
along
P^{1}
and
P^{2}
, respectively.
Then, the subgraph of
H
induced by the set
\{x,a_{1},a_{2},y,b_{3}\}
is isomorphic to
P_{4}+P_{1}
.
Therefore,
H
contains either
P_{4}+P_{1}
or
C_{4}
as an induced subgraph.

Next, let
H
be a pyramid, with a triangle
\{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\}
and three chordless paths
P^{1}=a\ldots b_{1},

P^{2}=a\ldots b_{2}
, and
P^{3}=a\ldots b_{3}
.
We may assume w.l.o.g. that
P^{1}
and
P^{2}
each have length at least 2 and let
x
and
y
be vertices adjacent to
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
along
P^{1}
and
P^{2}
, respectively.
Suppose that
P^{1}
has length exactly 2.
If
P^{3}
has length 1, then the set
\{x,b_{1},b_{3},a\}
induces a subgraph of
H
isomorphic to
C_{4}
.
If
P^{3}
has length at least 2, then the set
\{x,b_{1},b_{2},y,z\}
where
z
is any internal vertex of
P^{3}
, induces a subgraph of
H
isomorphic to
P_{4}+P_{1}
.
By symmetry, the same happens if
P^{2}
has length exactly 2, so we can assume that both
P^{1}
and
P^{2}
have length at least 3.
Then, vertices
\{x,b_{1},b_{2},y,a\}
induce a subgraph of
H
isomorphic to
P_{4}+P_{1}
.
Hence,
H
contains either
P_{4}+P_{1}
or
C_{4}
as an induced subgraph.

Next, let
H
be a theta, and let
P^{1},P^{2},P^{3}
be its three internally vertex-disjoint chordless paths with extremities
a,b
.
Note that we may assume that no two of these paths each have length exactly
2
, since otherwise there is an induced
C_{4}
in
H
.
Suppose w.l.o.g. that
P^{1}
and
P^{2}
have length at least 3.
Let
x
,
y
, and
z
be the vertices adjacent to
a
along
P^{1}
,
P^{2}
and
P^{3}
, respectively, let
w
be the neighbor of
x
in
P^{1}
other than
a
, and let
w^{\prime}
be the neighbor of
z
in
P^{3}
other than
a
.
Then, the set
\{w,x,a,y,w^{\prime}\}
induces a subgraph of
H
isomorphic to
P_{4}+P_{1}
.
Therefore,
H
contains either
P_{4}+P_{1}
or
C_{4}
as an induced subgraph.

Finally, let
W=(H,x)
be a broken wheel, and let
S
and
S^{\prime}
be two sectors of
W
of length at least 2.
If either
S
or
S^{\prime}
have length exactly 2, then there is an induced
C_{4}
in
W
.
So suppose that
S
and

Then four consecutive vertices of

Proposition 6.4
.

If

.

Corollaries

6.3
and
6.4
imply
that
\{P_{4}+P_{1},C_{4}\}

Lemma 6.5
.

If
G
is a
\{P_{4}+P_{1},C_{4}\}
-free graph, then
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\leqslant 3
.

We now show that the above result can be strengthened by replacing the tree-independence number with a larger parameter, obtained by considering, instead of the maximum cardinality of an independent set contained in a bag, the chromatic number of the complement of the subgraph induced by the bag.
A precise definition is as follows.
A
clique cover
of a graph
G
is a partition of
V(G)
into cliques.
The
clique cover number
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\theta(G)
, is the minimum number of cliques in a clique cover of
G
.
Since a coloring of a graph
G
is a partition of
V(G)
into independent sets, and since
S\subseteq V(G)
is a clique in
G
if and only if
S
is an independent set in
\overline{G}
, it follows that a partition of vertices of
G
is a clique cover of
G
if and only if it is a coloring of
\overline{G}
, i.e.,
\theta(G)=\chi(\overline{G})
.
The
clique cover number
of a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}
of
G
is defined as

\theta(\mathcal{T})=\max_{t\in V(T)}\theta(G[X_{t}])\,.

The
tree-clique-cover number
of a graph
G
, denoted
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\theta(G)
, is the minimum clique cover number among all possible tree decompositions of
G
(see Abrishami et al.
[
1
]
).

Note that a graph
G
is
\{P_{4}+P_{1},C_{4}\}
-free if and only if its complement
\overline{G}
is
\{2K_{2}
, gem
\}
-free, where the
gem
is the join of
P_{4}
and
P_{1}
.
Brause et al.
[
6
, Corollary 18]
gave the following bound on the chromatic number of
\{2K_{2}
, gem
\}
-free graphs.

Lemma 6.6

(Brause et al.
[
6
]
)
.

If
G
is a
\{2K_{2}
, gem
\}
-free graph, then
\chi(G)\leqslant\max\{\omega(G),3\}
.

Based on these observations, we are ready to prove the main result of this section.

See
1.10

Proof.

Let
G
be a
\{P_{4}+P_{1},C_{4}\}
-free graph.
By
Lemma

6.5
, there is a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
of
G
with independence number at most 3.
Fix
t\in V(T)
.
Then, by
Lemma

6.6
,

\theta(G[X_{t}])=\chi(\overline{G[X_{t}]})\leqslant\max\{\omega(\overline{G[X_{t}]}),3\}=\max\{\alpha(G[X_{t}]),3\}\leqslant 3\,.

Hence,
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\theta(G)\leqslant 3
, as claimed.
∎

7
Conclusion

We conclude the paper with a few questions left open by our work.

First, let us note a conjecture due to Gartland and Lokshtanov whose validity would imply
1.6
.
Given a graph
G
, a set
S\subseteq V(G)
is said to be a
balanced separator
in
G
if all components
C
of the graph
G-S
satisfy
|V(C)|\leqslant|V(G)|/2
.
Given a graph
G
and two sets
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
, we say that
X

dominates

Y
if
Y\subseteq N[X]
.

Conjecture 7.1

(Induced Grid Minor Conjecture,
[
22
]
)
.

There exists a function
f
such that for every planar graph
H
, every
H
-induced-minor-free graph
G
has a balanced separator dominated by
f(H)
vertices.

In
[
22
]
, the conjecture is stated only for the case when
H
is a
(k\times k)
-grid; however, the two conjectures are equivalent, since every planar graph is an induced minor of a sufficiently large grid graph (see
[
7
, Theorem 12]
).

Observe that in a
K_{1,t}
-free graph
G
, a set
S\subseteq V(G)
that is dominated by a set of at most
k
vertices induces a subgraph with independence number at most
kt
.
Hence, by
[
12
, Lemma 7.1]
(see also
[
17
]
),

7.1
implies the following strengthening of
1.6
.

Conjecture 7.2
.

For every planar graph
H
, there exists a constant

such that for every positive integer

, every

-free
H
-induced-minor-free graph

satisfies
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\leqslant c_{H}\cdot t
.

While a positive resolution to

would prove
1.6
, a negative one would disprove
7.1
.
Similar properties holds for a variant of
7.2
asking for an upper bound on the tree-independence number with a polynomial, instead of linear, dependency on

.
The existing proofs of special cases of
1.6
give bounds on tree-independence number that are linear in

if

for some positive integer

(both by the proof of Ahn et al.
[

]
as well as our proof) or if

is a cycle (from
[

), polynomial in

(more precisely, in

We wonder whether this is the worst that can happen.

Question 7.3

Is it true that for every two positive integers

\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\leqslant c_{k,t}\cdot\log|V(G)|
?

As a related and possibly easier variant of
7.3

As shown by

We ask whether this is true, more generally, in the class of

Question 7.4

Is there a constant
c

\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\leqslant\mathsf{ibn}(G)+c

An affirmative answer to

As a possibly simpler variant of the question, one could consider it first for the subclass of

By

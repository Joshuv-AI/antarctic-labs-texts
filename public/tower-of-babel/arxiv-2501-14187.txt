Linear enhanced dissipation for the 2D Taylor-Couette flow in the exterior region: A supplementary example for Gearhart-Prüss type lemma
Te Li, Ping Zhang, Yibin Zhang

Abstract
. From the perspective of asymptotic stability at high Reynolds numbers, Taylor-Couette flow, as a typical rotating shear flow, exhibits rich decay behaviors. Previously, for the extensively studied Couette flow or the Taylor-Couette flow in bounded annular domains, methods based on resolvent estimates could derive exponential decay asymptotic for the solutions of the linearized system. However, unlike the Couette flow or the Taylor-Couette flow in bounded annular domains, the Taylor-Couette flow in exterior regions exhibits degeneration of derivatives of any order at infinity. In this paper, we present in Theorem 1.1 that the linearized system of the 2D Taylor-Couette flow in the exterior region exhibits space-time coupled polynomial decay asymptotics. We also prove that the solution to this system, when it contains inhomogeneous terms, cannot be expected to exhibit space-time coupled exponential decay, as detailed in Theorem 1.2 . The result of Theorem 1.2 indicates that, even if we can obtain sharp resolvent estimates in different weighted spaces, the Gearhart-Prüss type lemma no longer applies. This suggests that resolvent estimates may not be very effective for handling degenerate shear flows. Furthermore, Theorem 1.2 also implies that, for the transition threshold problem of the 2D Taylor-Couette flow in exterior regions, we can at most expect the solution to exhibit long-time behavior with space-time coupled polynomial decay. Finally, we present a generalization of Theorem 1.2 , as detailed in Theorem 1.3 .



1.
Introduction

We consider 2D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in the exterior region:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}v-\nu\Delta v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla p=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega_{e},\\
&\text{div }v=0,\\
&v(t,x)|_{t=0}=v_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega_{e}=\bigl\{x=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|\geq 1\}=\{(r,\theta):r\in[1,\infty),\theta\in\mathbb{T}\bigr\}
. The vector field
v(t,x)
denotes the fluid velocity,
p(t,x)
represents the scalar pressure function and the viscous coefficient
\nu>0
is a constant. We denote the vorticity of
v
by
\omega:=\partial_{1}v_{2}-\partial_{2}v_{1}
, and focus on the stability of the 2D Taylor-Couette flow, in short TC flow,

\displaystyle v^{*}(r,\theta)=(Ar+\frac{B}{r})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\sin\theta\\
\cos\theta\end{array}\right),\quad\omega^{*}(r,\theta)=2A,

which is a steady solution to (
1.1
). Here
A,B
are both constants.

The Taylor-Couette flow is a classical example of a simple system yet with complex and rich stability properties, as well as a prototype of anisotropic and inhomogeneous turbulence, as can be referred in such review books as
[
5
,
13
]
. On more general grounds, the Taylor-Couette device is also an excellent prototype to study transport properties of most astrophysical or geophysical rotating shear flows
[
19
]
: depending on the rotation speed of each cylinder, one can obtain various flow regimes with increasing or decreasing angular velocity and/or angular momentum. Regarding the issue of asymptotic stability at high Reynolds numbers
[
10
]
, Taylor-Couette flow, as a typical rotating shear flow, also provides rich decay characteristics. Therefore, investigating the stability of Taylor-Couette flow is a matter of great interest in both mathematics and physics. Earlier, the well-posedness and stability of the two-dimensional stationary Navier-Stokes equations in exterior regions
[
4
,
22
]
were widely studied and discussed, along with the recent research on the overall well-posedness and stability of small initial data
[
28
,
29
]
in exterior regions.

Previously, the asymptotic stability problem of the 2D bounded region TC flow is considered in
[
3
]
. This paper explores the enhanced dissipation phenomenon of the TC flow in the exterior region. Due to the degeneracy of all derivatives of the TC flow at infinity, this leads to stability phenomena that differ from those in the bounded region
[
3
]
.

1.1.
Linearization near TC flow

We first write (
1.1
) in the vorticity formulation:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega-\nu\Delta\omega+v\cdot\nabla\omega=0.

By setting
w:=\omega-\omega^{*}
,
u:=v-v^{*}
, the vorticity perturbation equation near the 2D TC flow
(v^{*},\omega^{*})
is given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}w-\nu\Delta w+\Bigl(A+\frac{B}{r^{2}}\Bigr)\partial_{\theta}w+\frac{1}{r}(\partial_{r}\varphi\partial_{\theta}w-\partial_{\theta}\varphi\partial_{r}w)=0,\\
&\Delta\varphi=w,\qquad(t,r,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[1,\infty)\times\mathbb{T},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Delta=\partial_{r}^{2}+r^{-1}\partial_{r}+r^{-2}\partial_{\theta}^{2}
and
\varphi
is the stream function. We implement (
1.5
) with Navier-slip boundary conditions:

(1.6)

w|_{\partial\Omega_{e}}=\varphi|_{\partial\Omega_{e}}=0.

By expanding Fourier series of
w
and
\varphi
in
\theta
variable:
w=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ik\theta}\hat{w}_{k}(t,r),\varphi=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ik\theta}\hat{\varphi}_{k}(t,r),

and substituting them into (
1.5
), and then comparing the Fourier coefficients, we find

(1.7)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\hat{w}_{k}-\nu\Bigl(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}\Bigr)\hat{w}_{k}+\Bigl(A+\frac{B}{r^{2}}\Bigr)ik\hat{w}_{k}+\hat{f}_{k}=0,\\
&\Bigl(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}\Bigr)\hat{\varphi}_{k}=\hat{w}_{k},\quad\quad(t,r)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[1,\infty),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\hat{f}_{k}=ir^{-1}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl((k-\ell)\partial_{r}\hat{\varphi}_{\ell}\hat{w}_{k-\ell}-\ell\hat{\varphi}_{\ell}\partial_{r}\hat{w}_{k-\ell}\bigr)
.

To get rid of the terms in (
1.7
) which involve
\frac{\partial_{r}}{r}
and
A
, we introduce

\displaystyle w_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}:=r^{1/2}e^{ikAt}\hat{w}_{k},\quad\varphi_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}:=r^{1/2}e^{ikAt}\hat{\varphi}_{k},\quad f_{k}:=-r^{1/2}e^{ikAt}\hat{f}_{k},\quad\Delta_{k,r}:=\partial_{r}^{2}-(k^{2}-1/4)/r^{2}.

Then it follows from (
1.7
) that

(1.8a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}+(-\nu\Delta_{k,r}+ikB/r^{2})w_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}=f_{k},\quad(t,r)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[1,\infty),

(1.8b)

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,r}\varphi_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}=w_{k}^{\mathcal{T}},

(1.8c)

\displaystyle w_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}|_{r=1}=\varphi_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}|_{r=1}=0.

We denote the
k
-mode linearized operator corresponding to the TC flow as

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k}:=-\nu\Delta_{k,r}+ikB/r^{2},

which acts on
L^{2}_{r}(1,\infty)
with domain
D:=H^{2}\bigl((1,\infty),dr\bigr)\cap H^{1}_{0}\bigl((1,\infty),dr\bigr)
. For brevity, we may abbreviate
\mathcal{T}_{k}
to
\mathcal{T}
, if there is no ambiguity.

Until now, we have transformed the problem into exploring the dynamic and long-time behavior of (
1.8
). A standard decomposition yields two parts: the homogeneous linear equation and the inhomogeneous equation with zero initial data, namely
w_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}=w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}+w_{k,i}^{\mathcal{T}}
, where
w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}
and
w_{k,i}^{\mathcal{T}}
satisfy the following two equations respectively:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}+\mathcal{T}w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}=0,\quad w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}|_{t=0}=w_{k}(0),\quad w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}|_{r=1,\infty}=0,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{k,i}^{\mathcal{T}}+\mathcal{T}w_{k,i}^{\mathcal{T}}=f_{k},\quad w_{k,i}^{\mathcal{T}}|_{t=0}=0,\quad w_{k,i}^{\mathcal{T}}|_{r=1,\infty}=0.

Notations and Main theorems

We define

By

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad D=H^{2}\bigl((1,\infty),dr\bigr)\cap H^{1}_{0}\bigl((1,\infty),dr\bigr),

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{T})=\kappa_{k}=\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}},\quad\mu_{k}=\max\{\nu k^{2},\kappa_{k}\},\quad\Phi(\mathcal{C})=\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}.

Below we present our main theorems. In Section

, we shall explain the motivations and their connections to Gearhart-Prüss type lemma and enhanced dissipation of transport-diffusion equations.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
\frac{\nu}{|kB|}\ll(1+q)^{-3}
,

and let

be the solution to (

. There exists constant
C=C(q)>0
independent of

and

, so that

\bigl\|(1+\Phi(\mathcal{T})t/r^{2})^{q}w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\Phi(\mathcal{T})^{1/2}\bigl\|(1+\Phi(\mathcal{T})t/r^{2})^{q}r^{-1}w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\leq C\|w_{k}(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any positive continuous functions
a_{1}(r)
,
a_{2}(r)
and strictly decreasing function
\phi(r)
, there exists a sequence of functions,

\{(w_{n},f_{n})\}
, that solves (
1.11
) as below

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{n}+\mathcal{T}w_{n}=f_{n},\quad w_{n}|_{t=0}=0,\quad w_{n}|_{r=1,\infty}=0,

\displaystyle\|e^{t\phi(r)}a_{1}(r)w_{n}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty);{L^{2}_{r}})}+\|e^{t\phi(r)}a_{2}(r)f_{n}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty);{L^{2}_{r}})}<+\infty.

However, there holds

(1.13)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\|e^{t\phi(r)}a_{1}(r)w_{n}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty);{L^{2}_{r}})}}{\|e^{t\phi(r)}a_{2}(r)f_{n}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty);{L^{2}_{r}})}}=+\infty.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
highly relies on the transport term
\frac{ikB}{r^{2}}
in

, while the proof of Theorem
1.2
doesn’t. Indeed, the proof of Theorem

only relies on the forward translation invariance in time of the equation. Hence along the same lines and under the same assumptions, same results in Theorem

also hold for
Examples E1
and
E2
in Section

. This motivates us to derive a generalization of Theorem

.

We consider an abstract evolution equation

(1.14)

\partial_{t}w+\mathcal{L}w=f,\quad w|_{t=0}=w(0,x),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{L}
is a closed, densely defined linear operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
with domain

, which generates a
{L^{2}(\Omega)}
-strongly continuous semigroup
\{e^{t\mathcal{L}}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

We assume that (
1.14
) has fundamental solution
p_{\mathcal{L}}(t,x,y)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega\times\Omega)
, in the sense that, for any
w(0,x)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and
f(t,x)\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega)
, the mild solution of (
1.14
) can be represented as

(1.15)

w(t,x)=\int_{\Omega}p_{\mathcal{L}}(t,x,y)w(0,y)dy+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}p_{\mathcal{L}}(t-s,x,y)f(s,y)dyds.

We assume in addition that the support of
p_{\mathcal{L}}
is
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega\times\Omega
.
Then for any positive continuous functions
a_{1}(x)
,
a_{2}(x)
and non-constant continuous function
\phi(x)
, there exists a sequence of functions,
\{(w_{n},f_{n})\}
, that solves (
1.15
) with
w_{n}(0,x)=0
,
and satisfies for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1.16)

\displaystyle f_{n}(t,x)\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega),\quad\|e^{t\phi(x)}a_{2}(x)f_{n}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times\Omega)}<+\infty.

However, there holds

(1.17)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\|e^{t\phi(x)}a_{1}(x)w_{n}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times\Omega)}}{\|e^{t\phi(x)}a_{2}(x)f_{n}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times\Omega)}}=+\infty.

1.3.
The motivations of this work

1.3.1.
Enhanced dissipation of transport-diffusion equations

One motivation of this work stems from the study of transport-diffusion equation

(1.18)

\partial_{t}w-\nu\Delta w+V\cdot\nabla w=0.

Here the constant
\nu\ll 1
designates the viscous coefficient.
If we restrict

V(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}s(y)\\
0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{for some}\ s(y)\ \text{smooth enough},

and
(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times\Omega
, where
\Omega=\mathbb{T}
or
[0,1]
. Then (
1.18
) reads

(1.19)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}w-\nu(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2})w+s(y)\partial_{x}w=0,\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}\times\Omega,\\
&w(t)|_{t=0}=w(0).\end{aligned}\right.

When
\Omega=[0,1]
, we implement (
1.19
) with Dirichlet or Neumann Boundary conditions. We write
w(t,x,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{w}_{k}(t,y)e^{ikx}
and then obtain the
k
-mode equation

(1.20)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\hat{w}_{k}-\nu(\partial_{y}^{2}-k^{2})\hat{w}_{k}+iks(y)\hat{w}_{k}=0,\quad(t,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega,\\
&\hat{w}_{k}(t)|_{t=0}=\hat{w}_{k}(0).\end{aligned}\right.

When
s(y)=0
, (
1.19
) and (
1.20
) are actually heat equations. Hence we can only expect
\hat{w}_{k}(t)
decays after
t\geq\nu^{-1}
. However if
s(y)
has a finite number of critical points, of maximal order
n_{0}
(i.e., for any
y\in\Omega
, there exists
n\leq n_{0}
so that
s^{(n+1)}(y)\neq 0
), then for
k\neq 0
,
\hat{w}_{k}
will decay when
t\ll\nu^{-1}
. This phenomenon is called “enhanced dissipation” and could date back to Hörmander’s hypoellipticity theory. We recall the following theorem for readers’ convenience:

Theorem 1.4

(
[
6
,
2
]
)
.

There exist constants
C,c,\nu_{0}>0
depending only on
s(y)
, so that for any
\nu\leq\nu_{0}
and
t\geq 0
, there holds

(1.21)

\|\hat{w}_{k}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ce^{-c\nu^{\frac{n_{0}+1}{n_{0}+3}}t}\|\hat{w}_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Similarly, if we restrict

V(r,\theta)=rs(r)\mathbf{e}_{\theta},\quad(r,\theta)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{T}\subset[1,\infty)\times\mathbb{T},

and write
w(t,r,\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{w}_{k}(t,r)e^{ik\theta}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{k}(t,r)e^{ik\theta}
, we find

(1.22)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}w_{k}-\nu\Bigl(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{r^{2}}\Bigr)w_{k}+iks(r)w_{k}=0,\quad(t,r)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega,\\
&w_{k}(t)|_{t=0}=w_{k}(0),\quad w_{k}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Then the same result as Theorem
1.4
holds with
n_{0}
similarly defined, see for example
[
12
,
21
]
.

Example E1.
Couette flow on bounded domain, see
[
15
]
.

Let
\Omega=[0,1],s(y)=y
and
\hat{w}_{k}|_{y=0,1}=0
. It’s easy to check that
n_{0}=0
and thus we have

\|\hat{w}_{k}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ce^{-c\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}\|\hat{w}_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Example E2.
TC flow on bounded annular domains, see
[
3
]
.

Let
\Omega=[1,R],s(r)=\frac{B}{r^{2}}
and
w_{k}|_{r=1,R}=0
. It’s easy to check that
n_{0}=0
and thus we have

\|w_{k}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ce^{-c\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}t/R^{2}}\|w_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We shall go deeper into these examples in Subsection
1.3.2
, but now let’s investigate (
1.10
) by virtue of Theorem
1.4
. It’s easy to see that
\Omega=[1,\infty)
and
s(r)=\frac{B}{r^{2}}
is infinitely degenerate (i.e.,
n_{0}=\infty
). Thus, it seems impossible to obtain

\|w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\lesssim e^{-c\nu^{\varsigma}t}\|w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}},\quad\text{for some }\ 0<\varsigma<1.

It’s natural to ask: if we replace
{L^{2}_{r}}
by two different functional spaces
Y,Z
, could we recover

(1.23)

\|w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}(t)\|_{Y}\lesssim\varrho(\nu^{\varsigma}t)\|w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}(0)\|_{Z},

for some
0<\varsigma<1
, and strictly decreasing function
\varrho(t)
that approaches
0
as
t
tends to
\infty
?

The answer is positive! A simple corollary of Theorem
1.1
yields that, for any
t\geq 0
,

\bigl\|\bigl(1+\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}{r^{2}}\bigr)w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}\bigr\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\lesssim\|w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}},

which leads to

\|w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}((1,\infty),r^{-4}dr)}\lesssim\frac{1}{\bigl(1+\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}t\bigr)}\|w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}.

This is a simple example of (
1.23
) with
\varsigma=\frac{1}{3}
,
Y={L^{2}\bigl((1,\infty),r^{-4}dr\bigr)}
and
Z={L^{2}_{r}}
, which indicates that for (
1.10
), we can recover an enhanced dissipation scale
\mathcal{O}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{-\frac{2}{3}})
at the expense of losing spatial weights!

1.3.2.
Gearhart-Prüss type lemma and its applications.

We now turn to (
1.14
). A typical decomposition gives
w=w_{h}+w_{i}
, where

(1.24a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{h}+\mathcal{L}w_{h}=0,\quad w_{h}(0)=w(0),

(1.24b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{i}+\mathcal{L}w_{i}=f,\quad w_{i}(0)=0.

As in
[
31
]
, we call an operator
\mathcal{L}
in a Hilbert space
H
to be accretive if

(1.25)

\displaystyle\Re\langle\mathcal{L}w,w\rangle_{H}\geq 0,\quad\textrm{for any}\ w\in D(\mathcal{L}).

The operator
\mathcal{L}
is said to be m-accretive if, in addition, any
\lambda<0
belongs to the resolvent set of
\mathcal{L}
(see
[
25
]
for more details). The pseudospectral bound of
\mathcal{L}
is defined as

(1.26)

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{L}):=\inf\bigl\{\|(\mathcal{L}-i\lambda)w\|_{H}:w\in D(\mathcal{L}),\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R},\ \|w\|_{H}=1\bigr\}.

To obtain decaying semigroup bounds from pseudospectral bounds, let us recall the following Gearhart-Prüss type lemma established by Wei in
[
33
]
, (Helffer and Sjöstrand also provide related results in
[
24
]
).

Lemma 1.5

(
[
33
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a m-accretive operator in a Hilbert space
H
and let
w_{h},w_{i}
be the solution of (
1.24a
), (
1.24b
) respectively. Then there hold

(1.27a)

\displaystyle\|w_{h}(t)\|_{H}=\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}w(0)\|_{H}\leq e^{-t\Psi(\mathcal{L})+\frac{\pi}{2}}\|w(0)\|_{H},\quad\textrm{for any}\ t\geq 0,

(1.27b)

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{L})\|w_{i}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}(H)}\leq\|f\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}(H)},\quad\textrm{for any}\ T>0.

Below let’s go back to
Examples E1, E2
, and review their relationships with Lemma
1.5
.

1.3.3.
One-dimensional Schrödinger operators
\mathcal{L}:=-\Delta+V
on
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
.

Nonnegative Schrödinger operators
\mathcal{L}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and their associated heat semigroups
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
have been extensively studied by many mathematicians since Simon’s pioneering work
[
32
]
. See also the monographs of Davies
[
18
]
, Grigor’yan
[
23
]
, and Ouhabaz
[
30
]
, which provide further insights into this topic.

In this paper, the potential
V
in the one-dimensional Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}
is purely imaginary, so the Schrödinger operator no longer possesses the nonnegative property. However, as a substitute for nonnegativity, we have already mentioned in the subsection
1.3.2
that
\mathcal{L}
needs to be an accretive operator of the form (
1.25
). It is clear that when
V
is purely imaginary, this Schrödinger operator is accretive.

The behavior of the semigroups
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
-whether they exhibit exponential or polynomial decay-depends not only on whether
\Omega
is compact, but also on the properties of the potential function
V
itself. Therefore, estimating
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
is a challenging yet very interesting mathematical problem. Here are a few examples.

Example 1
.
\mathcal{L}=-\partial_{y}^{2}
with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
or
\Omega=[1,\infty)
.
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
does not exhibit exponential decay. This result is classical, for specifics, see Example 4.1 and Example 4.2 in Section
4
.

Example 2
.
\mathcal{L}=-\partial_{y}^{2}
with
\Omega
is bounded.
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
exhibits exponential decay. This can be derived from the Poincaré inequality.

Example 3
. Couette flow,
\mathcal{L}=-\partial_{y}^{2}+iy
with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
,
\Omega=[0,1]
or
\Omega=[1,\infty)
. Regardless of whether
\Omega
is bounded or unbounded,
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
exhibits exponential decay (
1.30a
). Therefore, the purely imaginary potential alters the properties of the Laplace operator.

Example 4
. TC flow,
\mathcal{L}=-\partial_{y}^{2}+i\frac{1}{y^{2}}
with
\Omega=[1,R]
.
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
exhibits exponential decay (
1.33a
). However, for the case where
\Omega=[1,\infty)
is an exterior domain, we can currently only obtain polynomial decay estimates as stated in Theorem
1.1
, and we are unable to answer whether
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
exhibits exponential decay.

1.3.4.
Transition threshold.

From the perspective of linear systems, this paper aims to address whether the Gearhart-Prüss type lemma remains applicable to the 2D TC flow in the exterior region, specifically whether
Q1
and
Q2
still hold, which is approached from a functional analysis standpoint. In fact, our final goal is to investigate the nonlinear stability of the 2D TC flow in the exterior region as below

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}+(-\nu\Delta_{k,r}+ikB/r^{2})w_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}=f_{k},\quad(t,r)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[1,\infty),

especially the transition threshold problem for this flow, which will be appeared in our subsequent paper
[
27
]
. Therefore, addressing the questions of
Q1
and
Q2
also addresses whether the pointwise estimates of the nonlinear system can still exhibit exponential decay. The negative answer of
Q2
indicates that the pointwise estimates for the nonlinear system of the 2D TC flow in the exterior region do not possess exponential decay but at most polynomial decay.

2.
Space-time estimates for
w_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}

2.1.
Resolvent estimates

In order to establish the decay properties of the linearized equation (
1.8a
), we begin with studying the fundamental resolvent equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}-ikB\lambda)w=F=F_{1}+\partial_{r}F_{2}\quad\textrm{with}\ w\in D,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
. By a density argument in
{L^{2}_{r}}
, we may assume in addition in this section that
w,F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}([1,\infty))
,

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
w\in D
, there holds

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Re\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}=\nu\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\nu(k^{2}-1/4)\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}.

Proof.

It follows by integration by parts.
∎

To proceed, we fix some decreasing function
\rho\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\rho(z)=1,\ \text{for}\ z\leq-1,\qquad\rho(z)=-1,\ \text{for}\ z\geq 1.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
,
|kB|\gg\nu>0
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists constant
C>0
independent of
\nu,k,B,\lambda
, so that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\nu\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\mu_{k}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}\leq C\bigl(\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|+\bigl|\langle F,\tilde{\rho}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|\mathds{1}_{(0,1)}(\lambda)\bigr),

where
\mu_{k}=\max\bigl\{\nu^{1/3}|kB|^{2/3},\nu k^{2}\bigr\}
,
\tilde{\rho}:=\rho\Bigl(\frac{r-\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{-\frac{1}{3}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\Bigr)
for
\lambda\in(0,1)
.

Moreover, there holds

(2.4)

\begin{split}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\mu_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\lesssim\mu_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|rF_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|F_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}.\end{split}

Proof.

The discussion is divided into the following three cases:

\displaystyle\lambda\in(-\infty,0],\quad\lambda\in[1,\infty),\quad\lambda\in(0,1).

(1). Case of
\lambda\in(-\infty,0]
.

It is easy to observe that

\displaystyle\big|\Im\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|=|kB|\big|\langle(r^{-2}-\lambda)w,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\geq|kB|\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2},

which gives

(2.5)

\displaystyle\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|\geq|kB|\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}.

(2). Case of
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
.

Since
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
, there holds

\displaystyle\big|\Im\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|=|kB|\big|\langle(r^{-2}-\lambda)w,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\geq|kB|\langle(1-r^{-2})w,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}},

which implies

\displaystyle|kB|\int_{1}^{\infty}(1-r^{-2})|w|^{2}dr\leq\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|.

Let
0<\delta\ll 1
be a small constant to be determined later, we find

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}(1-r^{-2})|w|^{2}dr\geq\int_{1+\delta}^{\infty}(1-r^{-2})|w|^{2}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{1+\delta}^{2}(r-1)(r+1)r^{-2}|w|^{2}dr+\int_{2}^{\infty}(1-r^{-2})|w|^{2}dr

\displaystyle\geq\delta\int_{1+\delta}^{2}|w/r|^{2}dr+1/2\int_{2}^{\infty}|w/r|^{2}dr\geq\delta\int_{1+\delta}^{\infty}|w/r|^{2}dr,

which gives

\displaystyle\|w/r\|_{L^{2}(1+\delta,\infty)}^{2}\leq|kB|^{-1}{\delta}^{-1}\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|.

Thus, we obtain the following estimate for
\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}
:

\displaystyle\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\|w/r\|_{L^{2}(1+\delta,\infty)}^{2}+\|w/r\|_{L^{2}(1,1+\delta)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|kB|^{-1}{\delta}^{-1}\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|+\int_{1}^{1+\delta}r^{-1}dr\|w/r^{1/2}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|kB|^{-1}{\delta}^{-1}\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|+\delta\|w/r^{1/2}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}^{2},

from which, Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
A.1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle|kB|^{-1}{\delta}^{-1}\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|+\delta\|w/r^{1/2}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|kB|^{-1}{\delta}^{-1}\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|+{\delta}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+\delta\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|kB|^{-1}{\delta}^{-1}\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|+\delta\nu^{-1/2}\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|^{1/2}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+\delta\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}.

Hence by taking
\delta=\nu^{1/3}|kB|^{-1/3}
,
we achieve

(2.6)

\displaystyle\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|\gtrsim\min\{|kB|\delta,{\delta}^{-2}\nu\}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}\gtrsim\nu^{1/3}|kB|^{2/3}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}.

(3). Case of
\lambda\in(0,1)
.

Let
r_{0}=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
\delta=\tilde{\delta}r_{0}
, where
0<\tilde{\delta}\ll 1
is a small constant to be determined later. In order to control
\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}((1,r_{0}-\delta)\cup(r_{0}+\delta,\infty))}
, we denote
\rho_{\delta}:=\rho(\frac{r-r_{0}}{\delta})
,
\rho_{\delta}^{\prime}:=\rho^{\prime}(\frac{r-r_{0}}{\delta})
and take
{L^{2}_{r}}
inner product of
F
with
\rho_{\delta}w
, and then we get, by using integration by parts, that

\displaystyle\langle F,\rho_{\delta}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}=

\displaystyle-\nu\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho_{\delta}w^{\prime\prime}\overline{w}dr+\nu(k^{2}-1/4)\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho_{\delta}|w/r|^{2}dr+ikB\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho_{\delta}(r^{-2}-\lambda)|w|^{2}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho_{\delta}|w^{\prime}|^{2}dr+\nu\int_{1}^{\infty}\delta^{-1}\rho^{\prime}_{\delta}w^{\prime}\overline{w}dr

\displaystyle+\nu(k^{2}-1/4)\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho_{\delta}|w/r|^{2}dr+ikB\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho_{\delta}(r^{-2}-\lambda)|w|^{2}dr.

By taking the imaginary part of above equality and noticing the sign of
\rho_{\delta}
, one has

\displaystyle|kB|\Bigl(\int_{1}^{r_{0}-\delta}(r^{-2}-\lambda)|w|^{2}dr+\int_{r_{0}+\delta}^{\infty}(\lambda-r^{-2})|w|^{2}dr\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\big|\langle F,\rho_{\delta}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\nu\delta^{-1}\Bigl|\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho^{\prime}_{\delta}w^{\prime}\overline{w}dr\Bigr|+|kB|\int_{r_{0}-\delta}^{r_{0}+\delta}|r^{-2}-\lambda||w|^{2}dr.

In view of the definitions of
r_{0},\delta
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{r_{-}}(r^{-2}-\lambda)|w|^{2}dr\geq\int_{1}^{r_{0}-\delta}(r^{-2}-r_{0}^{-2})|w|^{2}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{r_{0}-\delta}(r_{0}-r)(r_{0}+r)r^{-2}r_{0}^{-2}|w|^{2}dr\geq\delta/r_{0}\int_{1}^{r_{0}-\delta}|w/r|^{2}dr=\tilde{\delta}\int_{1}^{r_{0}-\delta}|w/r|^{2}dr,

and

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}(\lambda-r^{-2})|w|^{2}dr\geq\int_{r_{0}+\delta}^{\infty}(r_{0}^{-2}-r^{-2})|w|^{2}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{0}+\delta}^{\infty}(r-r_{0})(r_{0}+r)r^{-2}r_{0}^{-2}|w|^{2}dr\geq\delta/r_{0}\int_{r_{0}+\delta}^{\infty}|w/r|^{2}dr=\tilde{\delta}\int_{r_{0}+\delta}^{\infty}|w/r|^{2}dr.

And thanks to the support of
\rho_{\delta}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\nu\delta^{-1}\Bigl|\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho^{\prime}_{\delta}w^{\prime}\overline{w}dr\Bigr|\lesssim\nu\delta^{-1}r_{0}\Bigl|\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho_{\delta}^{\prime}w^{\prime}\overline{w}/rdr\Bigr|\lesssim\nu\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\|w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|w/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}},

and

\displaystyle|kB|\int_{r_{0}-\delta}^{r_{0}+\delta}|r^{-2}-\lambda||w|^{2}dr

\displaystyle=|kB|\int_{r_{0}-\delta}^{r_{0}+\delta}\frac{(r_{0}+r)|r_{0}-r|}{r^{2}r_{0}^{2}}||w|^{2}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim|kB|\tilde{\delta}\|w/r\|_{L^{2}(r_{0}-\delta,r_{0}+\delta)}^{2}.

By summarizing the above inequalities, we can bound
\|w/r\|_{L^{2}((1,r_{0}-\delta)\cup(r_{0}+\delta,\infty))}
as follows:

\begin{split}\|w/r\|_{L^{2}((1,r_{0}-\delta)\cup(r_{0}+\delta,\infty))}^{2}\lesssim&|kB|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\Bigl(\big|\langle F,\rho_{\delta}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\nu\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\|w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|w/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\Bigr)\\
&+\|w/r\|_{L^{2}(r_{0}-\delta,r_{0}+\delta)}^{2},\end{split}

from which and Lemma
A.2
, we infer

\displaystyle\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\|w/r\|_{L^{2}((1,r_{0}-\delta)\cup(r_{0}+\delta,\infty))}^{2}+\|w/r\|_{L^{2}(r_{0}-\delta,r_{0}+\delta)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|kB|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\Big(\big|\langle F,\rho_{\delta}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\nu\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\Big)+\int_{r_{0}-\delta}^{r_{0}+\delta}r^{-1}dr\|r^{-\frac{1}{2}}w\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|kB|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\Big(\big|\langle F,\rho_{\delta}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\nu\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\Big)+\tilde{\delta}\|r^{-\frac{1}{2}}w\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}^{2}.

Then thanks to Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
A.1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|kB|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\Big(\big|\langle F,\rho_{\delta}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\nu\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\Big)+\tilde{\delta}\|r^{-\frac{1}{2}}w\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|kB|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\Big(\big|\langle F,\rho_{\delta}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\nu\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{\delta}\|w/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigl(\|w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\|w/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim|kB|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\big|\langle F,\rho_{\delta}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\tilde{\delta}\|w/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\bigl(\tilde{\delta}+\nu|kB|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{-2}\bigr)\|w/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigl\|w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|kB|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\big|\langle F,\rho_{\delta}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\bigl(\tilde{\delta}+\nu|kB|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{-2}\bigr)\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}|\braket{F,w}_{L^{2}_{r}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+\tilde{\delta}\|w/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}.

Finally by choosing
\tilde{\delta}=\nu^{1/3}|kB|^{-1/3}\ll 1
to optimize the above inequality and absorbing the term
\tilde{\delta}\|w/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}
, we achieve

\displaystyle\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|kB|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{-1}\big|\langle F,\rho_{\delta}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\tilde{\delta}\nu^{-1/2}\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|^{1/2}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}.

Applying Young inequality and using
\tilde{\delta}=\nu^{1/3}|kB|^{-1/3}\ll 1
gives

(2.7)

\displaystyle\bigl|\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr|+\big|\langle F,\rho_{\delta}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\gtrsim\nu^{1/3}|kB|^{2/3}\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}.

Therefore, by summarizing (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we conclude that

(2.8)

\|\eta_{j}\|_{X_{t}}^{2}:=\frac{1}{2}\|\eta_{j}(t)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\nu\|\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\nu(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})\|r^{-1}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+4\kappa_{k}^{3}\|\tau/r^{3}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}.

Step 3. The control
\kappa_{k}\|w^{(1)}/r\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}
by
\|w^{(1)}\|_{Y_{t}}^{2}
.

First, we divide the domain into the following two parts:

(1)

D_{1}:=\{(\tau,r)\in(0,t)\times(1,+\infty):r^{2}\leq\kappa_{k}\tau\}
;

(2)

D_{2}:=\{(\tau,r)\in(0,t)\times(1,+\infty):0\leq\kappa_{k}\tau\leq r^{2}\}
.

Here
D_{1}
is the region where enhanced dissipation occurs. In fact, we have

(2.20)

\begin{split}\kappa_{k}\|w^{(1)}/r\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}=\kappa_{k}\Bigl(\iint_{(\tau,r)\in D_{1}}+\iint_{(\tau,r)\in D_{2}}\Bigr)|w^{(1)}/r|^{2}drd\tau=:\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}.\end{split}

For
\mathcal{I}_{1}
, since
\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}\chi_{j}^{2}\geq 1/2
, one has

(2.21)

\mathcal{I}_{1}\leq\kappa_{k}^{3}\|\tau/r^{3}w^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\leq 2\kappa_{k}^{3}\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\tau/r^{3}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\leq 1/2\|w^{(1)}\|_{Y_{t}}^{2}.

And for
\mathcal{I}_{2}
, thanks to
r^{-1}\chi_{j}\leq\frac{3}{2}\cdot 2^{-j}
, one has

(2.22)

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{1}&\leq 2\kappa_{k}\sum_{j\geq 0}\iint_{(\tau,r)\in D_{2}}|r^{-1}\eta_{j}|^{2}drd\tau\\
&\leq 2\kappa_{k}\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{0}^{\min\left\{t,2^{2j+2}/\kappa_{k}\right\}}\int_{1}^{\infty}9/2^{2j+2}|\eta_{j}|^{2}drd\tau\\
&\leq 18\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\eta_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\leq 36\|w^{(1)}\|_{Y_{t}}^{2}.\end{split}

By summarizing (
2.20
-
2.22
), we obtain

(2.23)

\kappa_{k}\|w^{(1)}/r\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\lesssim\|w^{(1)}\|_{Y_{t}}^{2}.

Step 4. The estimation of
\|w^{(1)}\|_{Y_{t}}
.

By taking the
{L^{2}_{r}}
inner product of
\eta_{j}
with the equation of
\eta_{j}
, and using integration by parts, we find

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\eta_{j}\|_{X_{t}}^{2}&\leq\nu\sum_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}\bigl(2^{-2j}\|r^{2}\chi_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}\|r^{-1}\eta_{\ell}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+2^{1-j}\|r\chi_{j}^{\prime}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}\|\partial_{r}\eta_{\ell}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\bigr)\|r^{-1}\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}},\end{split}

which together with the facts:
\operatorname{supp}_{r}\chi_{j}\subset[2^{j+1}/3,2^{j+1}]
and
\|\chi_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}},\|\chi_{j}^{\prime}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}\leq 4\cdot 10^{6}
, ensures that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\eta_{j}\|_{X_{t}}^{2}&\leq 16\cdot 10^{6}\nu\sum_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}(\|r^{-1}\eta_{\ell}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+\|\partial_{r}\eta_{\ell}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}})\|r^{-1}\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}.\end{split}

By integrating the above inequality over
[0,t]
, and then using the definition of
X_{t}
, we get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\|\eta_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\|\eta_{j}\|_{X_{t}}^{2}&\leq 2\times\Bigl(1/2\|\eta_{j}(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+16\cdot 10^{6}(1/\Theta+1/\Theta^{2})\|\eta_{j}\|_{X_{t}}\sum_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}\|\eta_{\ell}\|_{X_{t}}\Bigr).\\
\end{split}

By summing over
j\geq 0
and using Cauchy inequality, we achieve

\begin{split}\|w^{(1)}\|_{Y_{t}}^{2}&\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\eta_{j}(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+16\cdot 10^{6}(1/\Theta+1/\Theta^{2})\Bigl(\|w^{(1)}\|_{Y_{t}}^{2}+3\sum_{j\geq 0}\sum_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}\|\eta_{\ell}\|_{X_{t}}^{2}\Bigr)\\
&\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\eta_{j}(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+16\cdot 10^{7}(1/\Theta+1/\Theta^{2})\|w^{(1)}\|_{Y_{t}}^{2}.\end{split}

Due to
\Theta\geq 10^{9}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\|w^{(1)}\|_{Y_{t}}^{2}\lesssim\|w^{(1)}(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}=\|w_{k}(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}.\end{split}

By inserting the above estimate into (
2.23
), we achieve

\kappa_{k}\|w^{(1)}/r\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\lesssim\|w_{k}(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2},

which together with (
2.19
) ensures (
2.17
). We thus complete the proof of Proposition
2.6
.
∎

Next we present the proof of Lemma
2.8
.

Proof of Lemma
2.8
.

We set
\tilde{w}:=\kappa_{k}t/r^{2}w^{(1)}
, then in view of (
2.13
),
\tilde{w}
satisfies

(2.24)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\tilde{w}-\nu\bigl(\partial_{r}^{2}-(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})/r^{2}\bigr)\tilde{w}+\nu|2kBt/r^{3}|^{2}\tilde{w}=4\nu/r\partial_{r}\tilde{w}+2\nu/r^{2}\tilde{w}+\tilde{w}/t,\\
&\tilde{w}|_{t=0}=0,\quad\tilde{w}|_{r=1,\infty}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

By taking
{L^{2}_{r}}
inner product of the above equation with
\tilde{w}
and using integration by parts, we obtain

(2.25)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{w}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\nu\|\partial_{r}\tilde{w}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\nu(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})\|\tilde{w}/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+4\nu\|kBt/r^{3}\tilde{w}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}\\
&\leq\big|\langle 4\nu/r\partial_{r}\tilde{w}+2\nu/r^{2}\tilde{w},\tilde{w}\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\big|\langle\tilde{w}/t,\tilde{w}\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\\
&\leq 4\nu\|\partial_{r}\tilde{w}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\|\tilde{w}/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+2\nu\|\tilde{w}/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\big|\langle\tilde{w}/t,\tilde{w}\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|.\end{split}

Since
\Theta
is sufficiently large,
4\nu\|\partial_{r}\tilde{w}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\|\tilde{w}/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+2\nu\|\tilde{w}/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}
can be absorbed by the left-hand side.

Whereas for
\big|\langle\tilde{w}/t,\tilde{w}\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|
, we have

\displaystyle\big|\langle\tilde{w}/t,\tilde{w}\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\leq

\displaystyle\|\kappa^{\frac{3}{2}}t/r^{3}w^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}/rw^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}.

Notice that
\nu|kB|^{2}=\kappa_{k}^{3},
we get, by
by integrating (
2.25
) over
[0,T]
and using (
2.17
), that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{w}(T)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\nu\|\partial_{r}\tilde{w}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\nu(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})\|\tilde{w}/r\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+4\nu\|kBt/r^{3}\tilde{w}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\kappa_{k}^{\frac{3}{2}}t/r^{3}w^{(1)}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\|\kappa_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}/rw^{(1)}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\lesssim\|w_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}.

Observing that
\nu\|kBt/r^{3}\tilde{w}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}=\kappa_{k}^{5}\|t^{2}/r^{5}w^{(1)}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2},

we complete the proof of Lemma
2.8
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

We first present the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Indeed
the proof of Theorem
1.1
follows the same lines as that of Lemma
2.8
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We introduce

(3.1)

\begin{split}&E_{k}(\eta):=\|\eta\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\nu^{1/2}\|\partial_{r}\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\mu_{k}^{1/2}\|\eta/r\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\\
&\Lambda:=1+\kappa_{k}t/r^{2},\quad\eta_{q}:=\Lambda^{q}w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}\quad\mbox{for}\quad q\in\mathbb{N}.\end{split}

In view of (
1.10
), we find

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\eta_{q}+\mathcal{T}\eta_{q}=\partial_{t}\Lambda^{q}w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}-\nu\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda^{q}w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}-2\nu\partial_{r}\Lambda^{q}\partial_{r}w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad=\partial_{t}\Lambda^{q}w_{k,h}^{\mathcal{T}}-\nu\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda^{q}}{\Lambda^{q}}\eta_{q}+2\nu\Big(\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda^{q}}{\Lambda^{q}}\Big)^{2}\eta_{q}-2\nu\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda^{q}}{\Lambda^{q}}\partial_{r}\eta_{q}\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad=q\partial_{t}\Lambda\eta_{q-1}+\Big((q^{2}+q)\nu\Big|\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda}{\Lambda}\Big|^{2}-q\nu\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda}{\Lambda}\Big)\eta_{q}-2q\nu\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda}{\Lambda}\partial_{r}\eta_{q},\\
&\eta_{q}(t=0)=w_{k}(0),\quad\eta_{q}|_{r=1,\infty}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Observing that
|\partial_{t}\Lambda|\leq\frac{\kappa_{k}}{r^{2}}
,
\Big|\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda}{\Lambda}\Big|\lesssim r^{-2}
and
\Big|\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda}{\Lambda}\Big|\lesssim r^{-1}
, we get, by applying Proposition
2.4
, that

(3.2)

E_{k}(\eta_{0})=E_{k}(\eta)\lesssim\|w_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}},

and for
q\geq 1

\begin{split}E_{k}(\eta_{q})&\leq C\|w_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+C\mu_{k}^{-1/2}\Big(q\kappa_{k}\|\eta_{q-1}/r\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(q^{2}+q)\nu\|\eta_{q}/r\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+q\nu\|\partial_{r}\eta_{q}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\Big)\\
&\leq C\|w_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+Cq\kappa_{k}/\mu_{k}E_{k}(\eta_{q-1})+C\bigl((q^{2}+q)\nu/\mu_{k}+q(\nu/\mu_{k})^{1/2}\bigr)E_{k}(\eta_{q}).\end{split}

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.1
that
\frac{\nu}{|kB|}\ll(1+q)^{-3}
, we deduce from the definition of
\kappa_{k}
and
\mu_{k}
, that

\frac{\kappa_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\leq 1,\quad\frac{\nu}{\mu_{k}}\leq\frac{\nu}{\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}}\ll(1+q)^{-2}.

So that the term
C\bigl((q^{2}+q)\nu/\mu_{k}+q(\nu/\mu_{k})^{1/2}\bigr)E_{k}(\eta_{q})
on the right-hand side can be absorbed, we thus obtain

(3.3)

E_{k}(\eta_{q})\lesssim\|w_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+qE_{k}(\eta_{q-1}).

By virtue of (
3.2
) (
3.3
), we get, by using an iteration argument, that

E_{k}(\eta_{q})\lesssim_{q}\|w_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}},

which completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Next we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We begin with a simpler parabolic equation:

(3.4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\mathcal{T}\tilde{\eta}=0,\quad(t,r)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[1,1+\delta],\\
\tilde{\eta}(t=0)=0,\quad\tilde{\eta}|_{r=1}=0,\quad\tilde{\eta}|_{r=1+\delta}=g(t),\end{cases}

where
\delta\ll 1
will be determined later on and
g(t)
is a smooth function satisfying

g(t)=0,\quad\text{for}\ 0\leq t\leq 1\ \text{or}\ t\geq 2.

It’s easy to construct a smooth function
f(t,r)
which satisfies

f(0,r)=0,\quad f(t,1)=0,\quad f(t,1+\delta)=g(t),

\|\nabla_{t,r}^{\alpha}f(t,r)\|_{L^{\infty}_{r}[1,1+\delta]}\lesssim\mathds{1}_{1\leq t\leq 2},\quad\forall|\alpha|\leq 6.

Let
\eta:=\tilde{\eta}-f(t,r)
and
G(t,r):=\partial_{t}f+\mathcal{T}f,
we observe from (
3.4
) that

(3.5)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta+\mathcal{T}\eta=-G(t,r),\quad(t,r)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[1,1+\delta],\\
\eta(t=0)=0,\quad\eta|_{r=1}=0,\quad\eta|_{r=1+\delta}=0.\end{cases}

By taking the real part of the
L^{2}
inner product of (
3.5
) with
\eta
, we obtain (in what follows, we will abbreviate
L^{p}((1,1+\delta),dr),H^{k}((1,1+\delta),dr)
to
L^{p},H^{k}
respectively.)

(3.6)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\nu\|\partial_{r}\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\nu(k^{2}-1/4)\|\eta/r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|G\|_{L^{2}}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}.

It follows from Poincaré inequality on
[1,1+\delta]
that

(3.7)

\delta^{-1}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{P}\|\partial_{r}\eta\|_{L^{2}},\quad\text{for any}\ \eta\in H^{1}\ \text{with}\ \eta(1)=0.

By inserting (
3.7
) into (
3.6
), we find

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\partial_{r}\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}{\nu}\|G\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{4C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

which is equivalent to

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\nu\|\partial_{r}\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{2C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}{\nu}\|G\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

By integrating the above inequality over
[0,T]
, we achieve

(3.8)

\Big\|e^{\frac{\nu t}{4C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}}\eta\Big\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}(L^{2})}^{2}+\Big\|e^{\frac{\nu t}{4C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}}\partial_{r}\eta\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}(L^{2})}^{2}\lesssim_{\nu,\delta}1.

On the other hand, by the equation (
3.5
) and boundary condition of
\eta
, we find that

\partial_{t}\eta
and
\partial_{r}^{2}\eta
satisfies respectively

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\partial_{t}\eta+\mathcal{T}\partial_{t}\eta=-\partial_{t}G(t,r),\\
\partial_{t}\eta(t=0)=0,\quad\partial_{t}\eta|_{r=1}=0,\quad\partial_{t}\eta|_{r=1+\delta}=0,\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\partial_{r}^{2}\eta+\mathcal{T}\partial_{r}^{2}\eta=-\partial_{r}^{2}G(t,r)-(\nu k^{2}-\nu/4+ikB)\bigl(-4r^{-3}\partial_{r}\eta+6r^{-4}\eta\bigr),\\
\partial_{r}^{2}\eta(t=0)=0,\quad\partial_{r}^{2}\eta|_{r=1}=\nu^{-1}G(t,1),\quad\partial_{r}^{2}\eta|_{r=1+\delta}=\nu^{-1}G(t,1+\delta).\end{cases}

Then by virtue of (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), we get, by a similar derivation of (
3.8
), that

\begin{split}&\Big\|e^{\frac{\nu t}{4C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}}\partial_{t}\eta\Big\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}(L^{2})}^{2}+\Big\|e^{\frac{\nu t}{4C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}}\partial_{t}\partial_{r}\eta\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}(L^{2})}^{2}\\
&\qquad+\Big\|e^{\frac{\nu t}{4C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}}\partial_{r}^{2}\eta\Big\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}(L^{2})}^{2}+\Big\|e^{\frac{\nu t}{4C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}}\partial_{r}^{3}\eta\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}(L^{2})}^{2}\lesssim_{\nu,\delta,B,k}1,\end{split}

which together with (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and the properties of
f(t,r)
ensures that

\Big\|e^{\frac{\nu t}{4C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}}\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}(t)\Big\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}(L^{2})}+\Big\|e^{\frac{\nu t}{4C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}}\tilde{\eta}(t)\Big\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}(H^{2})}\lesssim_{\nu,\delta,B,k}1.

Then by extension theorem, it’s easy to extend
\tilde{\eta}
to a smooth function on
(t,r)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[1,\infty)
which we still denote by
\tilde{\eta}
, and which satisfies
\textrm{supp}_{r}\tilde{\eta}\subset[1,2+\delta]
and

(3.9)

\Big\|e^{\frac{\nu t}{4C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}}\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}(t)\Big\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}(L^{2}((1,\infty),dr))}+\Big\|e^{\frac{\nu t}{4C_{P}^{2}\delta^{2}}}\tilde{\eta}(t)\Big\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}(H^{2}((1,\infty),dr))}<\infty.

Now we are in the position to define
(w_{n},f_{n})
. We set
F:=\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\mathcal{T}\tilde{\eta}
and define

w_{n}(t,r):=\begin{cases}\tilde{\eta}(t-n,r),\quad&t\geq n,\\
0,\quad&t\leq n,\end{cases}

f_{n}(t,r):=\begin{cases}F(t-n,r),\quad&t\geq n,\\
0,\quad&t\leq n.\end{cases}

It is easy to check that
(w_{n},f_{n})\in C^{\infty}_{t,x}
solves (
1.11
) and satisfies

\textrm{supp}_{r}w_{n}\subset[1,2+\delta],\quad\textrm{supp}_{r}F_{n}\subset[1+\delta,2+\delta].

Also in view of the boundedness of
a_{1},a_{2}
and
\phi(r)
on
[1,2+\delta]\subset[1,3]
and (
3.9
), we can always fix some
\delta
to be so small that (
1.12
) holds. Since
\tilde{\eta}
is smooth, there exists
0<\epsilon<\delta
so that
\|e^{t\phi(r)}\tilde{\eta}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times(1,1+\delta-\epsilon))}\neq 0
. This difference in the spatial support of
w_{n}
and
f_{n}
leads to

\begin{split}&\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\|e^{t\phi(r)}a_{1}(r)w_{n}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times(1,\infty))}}{\|e^{t\phi(r)}a_{2}(r)f_{n}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times(1,\infty))}}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\|e^{n\phi(r)}e^{t\phi(r)}a_{1}(r)\tilde{\eta}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times(1,2+\delta))}}{\|e^{n\phi(r)}e^{t\phi(r)}a_{2}(r)F\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times(1+\delta,2+\delta))}}\\
&\qquad\geq\frac{\min_{r\in[1,2+\delta]}a_{1}(r)}{\max_{r\in[1,2+\delta]}a_{2}(r)}\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{e^{n\phi(1+\delta-\epsilon)}\|e^{t\phi(r)}\tilde{\eta}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times(1,1+\delta-\epsilon))}}{e^{n\phi(1+\delta)}\|e^{t\phi(r)}F\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times(1+\delta,2+\delta))}}=+\infty.\end{split}

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

(4.2)

(4.5)

Now we introduce

w_{n}(t,x):=\begin{cases}w(t-n,x),\quad&t\geq n,\\
0,\quad&t\leq n,\end{cases}

f_{n}(t,x):=\begin{cases}f(t-n,x),\quad&t\geq n,\\

In view of (

), it’s easy to see that so defined

). However, there holds

\begin{split}&\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\|e^{t\phi(x)}a_{1}(x)w_{n}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times\Omega)}}{\|e^{t\phi(x)}a_{2}(x)f_{n}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times\Omega)}}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\|e^{n\phi(x)}e^{t\phi(x)}a_{1}(x)w\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times\Omega)}}{\|e^{n\phi(x)}e^{t\phi(x)}a_{2}(x)f\|_{L^{2}((\frac{1}{8},\frac{7}{8})\times V_{1})}}\\
&\qquad\geq\frac{\min_{x\in V_{2}}a_{1}(x)}{\max_{x\in V_{1}}a_{2}(x)}\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{e^{nd_{2}}\|e^{t\phi(x)}w\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}}{e^{nd_{1}}\|e^{t\phi(x)}f\|_{L^{2}([\frac{1}{8},\frac{7}{8}]\times V_{1})}}=+\infty.\end{split}

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

As applications, we give a few examples of Theorem
1.3
.

Example 4.1.
Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
.

It’s well known that the heat kernel on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is

H(t,x,y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{n}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}>0.

Thus, this example satisfies the assumptions in Theorem
1.3
.
Here we can replace
-\Delta
by a second-order uniformly elliptic operator in divergence form with smooth coefficients, whose fundamental solution enjoys the following two-side estimate (see
[
1
]
):

(4.6)

c_{1}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-c_{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}\leq p_{\mathcal{L}}(t,x,y)\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{c_{2}t}},

where
c_{1}<1<c_{2}
are two universal constants.
Also we can extend
-\Delta
to a family of nonlocal elliptic operators whose parabolic fundamental solution vanishes nowhere, such as
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
. For more examples, one may check
[
17
,
20
]
and references therein.

Example 4.2.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any bounded domain or exterior domain with
\partial\Omega
smooth, and let
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
with domain
D(\mathcal{L})=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. It’s well-known that, see for example
[
34
]
,

\Big(\frac{\rho(x)}{\sqrt{t}\wedge 1}\wedge 1\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho(y)}{\sqrt{t}\wedge 1}\wedge 1\Big)\frac{c_{1}e^{-c_{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}}}\leq p_{-\Delta}(t,x,y)\leq\Big(\frac{\rho(x)}{\sqrt{t}\wedge 1}\wedge 1\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho(y)}{\sqrt{t}\wedge 1}\wedge 1\Big)\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{c_{2}t}}}{c_{1}t^{\frac{n}{2}}},

where
a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}
,
\rho(x)=\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
and
c_{1}<1<c_{2}
are two universal constants. Hence this example also satisfies assumptions in Theorem
1.3
.

Example 4.3.
Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta+V
, with
V
being real. There are lots of literatures to investigate
the following problem: under what condition of
V
, will the two-side estimates (
4.6
) holds? One may see for example
[
16
,
26
]
and references therein.

In Theorem
1.2
,
\Omega=[1,\infty)
and
\mathcal{T}=-\nu\Delta_{k,r}+\frac{ikB}{r^{2}}
. Since
\frac{ikB}{r^{2}}
is complex, the fundamental solution is also complex, thus we can not expect (
4.6
) to hold anymore. However since
\frac{ikB}{r^{2}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{b}((1,\infty))
is analytic, it seems possibly that
p_{\mathcal{T}}(t,r,r^{\prime})
is analytic, by estimating
\|\nabla^{\alpha}_{t,r,r^{\prime}}p_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{L^{2}_{t,r,r^{\prime}}((a,b)\times(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty))}
for any
0<a<b
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, and then we may deduce Theorem
1.2
from
Theorem
1.3
. But this involves tedious calculations, we just end up here and shall not pursue this direction here.

Appendix A
Basic estimates

Lemma A.1
.

Let
w\in D
, there holds

\displaystyle\|w/r^{1/2}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}^{2}\lesssim\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}.

Proof.

When
w|_{r=1,\infty}=0
, it can be inferred that

\displaystyle|w/r^{1/2}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{r}\partial_{s}(s^{-1}|w(s)|^{2})ds

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{r}s^{-1}(w^{\prime}(s)\overline{w}(s)+w(s)\overline{w}^{\prime}(s))ds-\int_{1}^{r}s^{-2}|w|^{2}(s)ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+\|w/r\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}.

∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
r_{0}\in(1,\infty)
and let
0<\tilde{\delta}\ll 1
be a small constant, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}-\tilde{\delta}r_{0}}^{r_{0}+\tilde{\delta}r_{0}}r^{-1}dr\lesssim\tilde{\delta}.

Proof.

A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}-\tilde{\delta}r_{0}}^{r_{0}+\tilde{\delta}r_{0}}r^{-1}dr=\ln\Big(1+\frac{2\tilde{\delta}r_{0}}{r_{0}-\tilde{\delta}r_{0}}\Big)\leq 2\tilde{\delta}(1-\tilde{\delta})^{-1}\lesssim\tilde{\delta}.

∎

Appendix B
Resolvent estimates of Couette flow

In order to establish the decay properties of (
1.28
), we consider

(B.1)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}-ik\lambda)w=-\nu(\partial_{y}^{2}-k^{2})w+ik(y-\lambda)w=F,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
,
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
or
\Omega=[0,1]
and
w\in D(\mathcal{C})=\bigl\{w\in{L^{2}(\Omega)}:w\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\ yw\in{L^{2}(\Omega)}\bigr\}
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
,
|k|\gg\nu>0
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, there holds

\displaystyle(\nu k^{2})^{1/3}\|w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq C\|F\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

Proof.

By integration by parts, we first get

\displaystyle\langle F,w\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\nu\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}+\nu k^{2}\|w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}+ik\int_{\Omega}(y-\lambda)|w|^{2}dy.

By taking the real part, one has

\displaystyle\nu\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}\leq\|F\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

We also get by integration by parts that

\displaystyle\langle F,(y-\lambda)w\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=-\nu\int_{\Omega}w^{\prime\prime}(y-\lambda)\overline{w}dy+\nu k^{2}\int_{\Omega}(y-\lambda)|w|^{2}dy+ik\|(y-\lambda)w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\nu\int_{\Omega}w^{\prime}\overline{w}dy+\nu\int_{\Omega}(y-\lambda)|w^{\prime}|^{2}dy+\nu k^{2}\int_{\Omega}(y-\lambda)|w|^{2}dy+ik\|(y-\lambda)w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}.

By taking the imaginary part, one has

\displaystyle|k|\|(y-\lambda)w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}\leq\|F\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|(y-\lambda)w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}+\nu\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}},

which gives

\displaystyle\|(y-\lambda)w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}\leq|k|^{-2}\|F\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}+2|k|^{-1}\nu\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

Let
\delta=\nu^{1/3}|k|^{-1/3}
,
E=\Omega\cap(\lambda-\delta,\lambda+\delta)
,
E^{c}=\Omega\setminus(\lambda-\delta,\lambda+\delta)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{2}(E^{c})}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}\leq\delta^{-2}\|(y-\lambda)w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}+2\delta\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{-2}|k|^{-2}\|F\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}+2\delta^{-2}|k|^{-1}\nu\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}+4\delta\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nu k^{2})^{-2/3}\|F\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}+6\delta\|w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\|w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\nu k^{2})^{-2/3}\|F\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}+6\delta\nu^{-1/2}\|F\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{1/2}\|w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{3/2},

which implies

\displaystyle\|w\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}\leq C\big((\nu k^{2})^{-2/3}+\delta^{4}\nu^{-2}\big)\|F\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}\leq C(\nu k^{2})^{-2/3}\|F\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}^{2}.

∎

Now we prove that the pseudospectral bound
(\nu k^{2})^{1/3}
in Lemma
B.1
is sharp for low frequency
k
.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
|k|=1
and
\nu<1
. There exist
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
w_{0}\in D(\mathcal{C})
and
C
independent of
\nu,\lambda_{0}
, so that

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{C}-i\lambda)w_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq C\nu^{1/3}\|w_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{0}=0
and

\displaystyle w_{0}(y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &y^{3}(\nu^{1/3}-y)^{3},\quad 0\leq y\leq\nu^{1/3},\\
&0,\quad\textrm{else}.\end{aligned}\right.

By direct calculations, one can check that
w_{0}\in D(\mathcal{C})
and

\displaystyle\|w_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\approx\nu^{\frac{13}{6}},\quad\nu\|(-\partial_{y}^{2}+1)w_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\lesssim\nu(\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}}+1)\nu^{\frac{13}{6}},\quad\|yw_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\lesssim\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}\nu^{\frac{13}{6}}.

Thus we deduce that

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{C}-i\lambda)w_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq\nu\|(-\partial_{y}^{2}+1)w_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}+\|yw_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq C\nu^{1/3}\|w_{0}\|_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

∎

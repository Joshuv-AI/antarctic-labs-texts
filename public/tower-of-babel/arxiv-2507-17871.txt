Shallow quantum circuit for generating O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) -entangled approximate state designs
Wonjun Lee, Minki Hhan, Gil Young Cho, Hyukjoon Kwon

Abstract
Random quantum states have various applications in quantum information science. In this work, we discover a new ensemble of quantum states that serve as an ϵ \epsilon -approximate state t t -design while possessing extremely low entanglement, magic, and coherence. These resources can reach their theoretical lower bounds, Ω ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( t / ϵ ) ) \Omega(\log(t/\epsilon)) , which are also proven in this work. This implies that for fixed t t and ϵ \epsilon , entanglement, magic, and coherence do not scale with the system size, i.e., O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) with respect to the total number of qubits n n . Moreover, we explicitly construct an ancilla-free shallow quantum circuit for generating such states by transforming k k -qubit approximate state designs into n n -qubit ones without increasing the support size. The depth of such a quantum circuit, O ⁡ ( t ​ [ log ⁡ t ] 3 ​ log ⁡ n ​ log ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ ) ) O(t[\log t]^{3}\log n\log(1/\epsilon)) , is the most efficient among existing algorithms without ancilla qubits. A class of quantum circuits proposed in our work offers reduced cost for classical simulation of random quantum states, leading to potential applications in quantum information processing. As a concrete example, we propose classical shadow tomography using an estimator with superpositions between only two states, which improves the runtime of a state certification task by requiring only O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) measurements and queries.

Entanglement lower bound.

We present a theoretical lower bound on the entanglement of states forming an approximate state

Theorem 2

An ensemble of pure quantum states can form an

Proof.

Let

Discussion

In this work, we have provided a construction of an approximate state
t
-design with
O(1)
-entangled states. Our construction saturates the theoretical lower bounds of mean entanglement, magic, and coherence of quantum states that form an
\epsilon
-approximate state
t
-design, which are
\Omega(\log(t/\epsilon))
. We have also developed shallow random circuits that generate such states. This directly implies that our algorithms can be used to simulate Haar random states, which have been widely used in various applications, including benchmarking quantum circuits, demonstrating quantum supremacy, and quantum learning. Specifically, we show that our construction can be used for classical shadow tomography with
O(1)
-entangled shadow estimators using onsite Haar random unitaries. This feature can be utilized to perform a certification task with a constant number of accesses to a reference state in the computational basis, which is available for neural quantum states or matrix product states, and a constant number of measurements. We believe our work opens up the possibility of performing these tasks on near-term quantum devices.

We compare the circuit depth of our algorithm with previous approaches for generating approximate state designs. One common method is to use local random circuits, which require a depth of
\operatorname{poly}(t)\cdot n^{1/D}
in
D

t
-design with
relative

\epsilon

Random subset phase states.

A random subset phase state with the system size
n
and the subset dimension
2^{k}
with
k\leq n
is built using two random mappings: random permutation
p:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}^{n}
and random function
f:\{0,1\}^{k}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
. The state is formally defined as follows:

\ket{\psi_{p,f}}=\frac{1}{2^{k/2}}\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}^{k}}(-1)^{f(b)}\ket{p(b,0^{n-k})}.

(13)

Let
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the ensembles of all permutations and functions, respectively. An ensemble of random subset phase states is then defined as
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rsp}}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{F})=\{\ket{\psi_{p,f,a}}|p\sim\mathcal{P},f\sim\mathcal{F}\}
. When the distributions on
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{F}
are sufficiently uniform, then the ensemble average of states over
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rsp}}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{F})
approximates
\rho^{(t)}_{\mathrm{unique},n}
and thereby provide an approximate state design
Aaronson et al. 2023
.

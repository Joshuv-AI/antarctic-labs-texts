ICE-closed subcategories and epibricks over recollements
Jinrui Yang, Yongyun Qin*

Abstract
Let ( 𝒜 ′ , 𝒜 , 𝒜 ′′ , i ∗ , i ∗ , i ! , j ! , j ∗ , j ∗ ) (\mathcal{A^{{}^{\prime}}},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A^{{}^{\prime\prime}}},i^{\ast},i_{\ast},i_{!},j_{!},j^{\ast},j_{\ast}) be a recollement of abelian categories. We proved that every ICE-closed subcategory (resp. epibrick, monobrick) in 𝒜 ′ \mathcal{A^{{}^{\prime}}} or 𝒜 ′′ \mathcal{A^{{}^{\prime\prime}}} can be extended to an ICE-closed subcategories (resp. epibrick, monobrick) in 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , and the assignment 𝒞 ↦ j ∗ ​ ( 𝒞 ) \mathcal{C}\mapsto j^{*}(\mathcal{C}) defines a bijection between certain ICE-closed subcategories in 𝒜 \mathcal{A} and those in 𝒜 ′′ \mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime} . Moreover, the ICE-closed subcategory 𝒞 \mathcal{C} of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} containing i ∗ ( 𝒜 ′ ) i_{\ast}(\mathcal{A^{{}^{\prime}}}) admits a new recollement relative to ICE-closed subcategories 𝒜 ′ \mathcal{A^{{}^{\prime}}} and j ∗ ​ ( 𝒞 ) j^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}) which induced from the original recollement when j ! j ∗ ( 𝒞 ) ⊂ 𝒞 j_{!}{j^{\ast}(\mathcal{C})}\subset\mathcal{C} .

1
Introduction

In representation theory of algebras, several classes of subcategories of abelian categories
have been investigated.
For example, the notion of torsion class was introduced by Dickson
[
11
]

and was studied in relation with tilting or
\tau
-tilting theory
[
1
,
7
,
10
]
.
Recall that torsion classes are subcategories closed under taking extensions and quotients.
On the other hand,
wide subcategories, which are exact abelian subcategories closed under extensions, are investigated in connection
with torsion classes
[
3
,
4
,
29
]
, ring epimorphisms
[
29
]
and stability conditions
[
5
]
.

Recently, ICE-closed subcategories of abelian categories were introduced
in
[
12
]
, which are subcategories closed under taking images,
cokernels and extensions. Both torsion classes and wide subcategories are
typical examples of ICE-closed subcategories. Therefore, the notion of
ICE-closed subcategory can be seen as a generation of these two classes.
Moreover, ICE-closed subcategories are studied in relation with rigid modules
[
12
]
, torsion
classes
[
14
,
16
]
and t-structure
[
33
]
. We refer to
[
15
,
22
,
23
]

for more discussions on ICE-closed subcategories.
In
[
13
]
, Enomoto considered two other kinds of subcategories, named monobricks
and epibricks. Indeed, a monobrick (resp. epibrick) is a full subcategory whose objects have division endomorphism
rings and every non-zero map between these objects
is either zero or monomorphic (resp. epimorphic).
Monobricks
and epibricks are
generalization of semisimple modules or semibricks, and they provide an effective approach
to investigate torsion-free classes and wide subcategories
[
13
]
.

Recollements of abelian categories are exact sequences of abelian categories
where both inclusion and quotient
have left and right adjoints. They have been introduced by Beilinson, Bernstein and
Deligne in the context of triangulated categories
[
6
]
,
and were studied in the situation of abelian categories by many authors
[
18
,
19
,
30
,
32
]
.
It should be noted that recollements of abelian categories
appear more naturally than those of triangulated categories, because any idempotent element of a ring induces a
recollement situation between module categories. Moreover,
recollements of abelian categories
are very useful in gluing and in reduction, see for example
[
2
,
8
,
20
,
27
,
30
]
.
In particular, Zhang investigated
how wide subcategories and semibricks transfer in the recollements of abelian categories
[
35
,
36
]
.
Following this, we consider the similar
gluing and reduction results for ICE-closed subcategories, epibricks and monobricks.

2
Definitions and conventions

Throughout this paper, all subcategories are assumed to be full subcategories,
and all functors between abelian categories are assumed to be additive.
First of all, we will recall some basic definitions about
ICE-closed subcategory, monobrick
and epibrick. These subcategories
were first defined in modules categories
[
12
,
13
]

and then generalized to exact categories
[
22
]
. In this
paper, we only focus on abelian categories.

Definition 2.1

Let

(1)

(2)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{N_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

(3)

\textstyle{N_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

(4) (See

(5) (See

(6) (See

containing a given subcategory

3
ICE-closed subcategories and recollements

In this section, we will investigate how ICE-closed subcategories transfer in
recollements of abelian categories. The following lemma is well-known. Here, we give a proof for readers’ convenience.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\textstyle{\mathcal{A}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F}
\textstyle{\mathcal{B}}
be a functor between abelian categories, and

(1)

(2)

(3)
If

is exact, then
F(\mathrm{Im}f)\cong{\mathrm{Im}F(f)}

Proof.

(1) Applying

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Kerf\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{N}

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F(\mathrm{Ker}f)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F(M)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F(f)}
\textstyle{F(N)}

and then

(2) This can be proved with an argument entirely dual to (1).

(3) Since

and
\mathrm{Im}F(f)=\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Coker}F(f))

4

In
[

, it is shown that semibricks can be glued via recollements, and in this section,
we will observe
how to glue epibricks and monobricks.
The following lemma is an analogy of

[
36
, Lemma 2.1]
, which says that both bricks and semibricks are preserved under fully faithful functors.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}
be a fully faithful functor
between abelian categories. If
F
preserves epimorphisms (resp. monomorphisms), then
we have
F(\mathrm{ebrick}\mathcal{A})\subset\mathrm{ebrick}\mathcal{B}
(resp.

F(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A})\subset\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{B}
).

Proof.

Assume that
F
is fully faithful and
F
preserves epimorphisms,
and let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{ebrick}\mathcal{A}
.
It follows from
[
36
, Lemma 2.1]
that
F(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathrm{brick}\mathcal{B}
.
Further, for any
h\in{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(F(C_{1}),F(C_{2}))}
with
C_{1},C_{2}\ {\in\mathcal{C}}
, there is some

h_{1}\in{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(C_{1},C_{2})}
such that
h=F(h_{1})
.
Therefore, we get
h_{1}\neq 0
for any

, and then
h_{1}

is an epimorphism, for the reason that
\mathcal{C}
is an epibrick.
Since
F
preserves epimorphisms, we infer that

is an epimorphism and thus
F(\mathcal{C})\in\mathrm{ebrick}\mathcal{B}
.
The statement on monobricks can be proved dually. ∎

Applying Lemma

to recollement, we get the following theorem,
which is an analogy of

[

By Remark

preserves monomorphisms and

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(j_{!*}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}),i_{*}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}))\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}(i^{*}j_{!*}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}),\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\cong 0
.
Thus
i_{*}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\cup j_{!*}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime})\in\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}
, and the statement on epibricks
can be proved dually.
∎

Since
i^{!}j_{!}
and
i^{*}j_{*}
may not be zero,
i_{*}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\cup j_{!}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})

and
i_{*}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\cup j_{*}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
will not usually
be monobricks in the middle category. But we have the following special case due to

[
18
, Proposition 8.8]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

If
i^{*}
is exact, then we have
i_{*}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\cup j_{!}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\subset\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}

and
i_{*}(\mathrm{ebrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\cup j_{!}(\mathrm{ebrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\subset\mathrm{ebrick}\mathcal{A}
.
Dually, if
i^{!}
is exact, then we have
i_{*}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\cup j_{*}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\subset\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}

and
i_{*}(\mathrm{ebrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\cup j_{*}(\mathrm{ebrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\subset\mathrm{ebrick}\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

We prove the first assertion, and the second can be proved dually.
Assume that
i^{*}
is exact. Then it follows from
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
that

j_{!}
is exact, and thus it preserves monomorphisms. Applying Lemma
4.1
,
we have that
j_{!}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\subset\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}
.
Therefore,
to show
i_{*}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\cup j_{!}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\subset\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}
,
it suffices to show
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathrm{Im}i_{*},\mathrm{Im}j_{!})\cong 0

and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathrm{Im}j_{!},\mathrm{Im}i_{*})\cong 0
, and then the proof
is finished by using the same argument as Theorem
4.3
.
Since
i^{*}
is exact, we get

i^{!}j_{!}=0
by
[
18
, Proposition 8.8]
,
and then
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathrm{Im}i_{*},\mathrm{Im}j_{!})\cong 0
by adjointness.
Further, it follows from
j^{*}i_{*}=0
that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathrm{Im}j_{!},\mathrm{Im}i_{*})\cong 0
.
Thus we conclude that
i_{*}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\cup j_{!}(\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\subset\mathrm{mbrick}\mathcal{A}
, and the statement on epibricks can be
proved similarly. ∎

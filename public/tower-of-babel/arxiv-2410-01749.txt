Fully Coupled Nonlinear FBS △ \bigtriangleup Es: Solvability and LQ Control Insights footnoteinfo
Zhipeng Niu, Qingxin Meng, Xun Li, Maoning Tang

Abstract
In this paper, a class of fully coupled nonlinear forward-backward stochastic difference equations (FBS △ \bigtriangleup Es) is proposed and the existence of solutions is proved based on a linear-quadratic (LQ) optimal control problem. Inspired from the solvability studies of various forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs), the dominant-monotone framework is discretised and a continuum approach is used to prove the unique solvability of the fully coupled FBS △ \bigtriangleup Es and to obtain a pair of estimates on the solutions, and finally, the conclusions are applied to the related LQ problem.



keywords
Forward-Backward Stochastic Difference Equations; Domination-Monotonicity Conditions; Continuation Method; LQ Problem; Hamiltonian System.

Introduction

Since the seminal work of
Pardoux and Peng (1990)
, the theory of backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) has undergone significant development, owing to their rich mathematical structure and wide range of applications.
When a BSDE is considered together with a forward stochastic differential equation (FSDE), the resulting system is referred to as a forward-backward stochastic differential equation (FBSDE). Such a system is called
coupled
if the forward equation depends on the backward component or vice versa. In particular, if this dependence is mutual—i.e., both the forward and backward equations explicitly depend on each other—the system is called a
fully coupled
FBSDE.

The time discretization of FBSDEs gives rise to forward-backward stochastic difference equations (FBS
\Delta
Es). With the advancement of computational algorithms, FBS
\Delta
Es provide substantial advantages in numerical computation, particularly in the development of efficient solution schemes.
For instance,

Ma et al. (2002)
proposed a numerical approximation method of backward stochastic
differential equations;

Zhang (2004)
designed a numerical scheme for a class of backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) with possible path-dependent terminal values;

Gobet et al. (2005)
developed a new numerical scheme based on iterative regressions on function bases, whose coefficients are evaluated using Monte Carlo simulations;

Delarue and Menozzi (2006)
proposed a time-space discretization scheme for quasi-linear parabolic PDEs;

Bender and Zhang (2008)
considered a numerical algorithm to simulate high-dimensional coupled FBSDEs under weak coupling or monotonicity conditions;

Cheridito and Stadje (2013)
provided existence results and comparison principles for solutions of BS
\Delta
Es and then proved convergence of these to solutions of BSDEs;

Gobet and Pagliarani (2015)
analyzed analytical approximations of backward SDEs in the limit of small nonlinearity and short time, in the case of nonsmooth drivers.

In this paper, we consider a class of FBS
\Delta
Es.
In fact, fully coupled FBS
\Delta
Es arise naturally in discrete-time stochastic optimal control problems, where the forward equation models the system dynamics and the backward equation represents the cost variable or the value function.
In finance, such models describe portfolio optimization and asset-liability management, where the forward wealth dynamics and the backward risk-adjusted valuation are inherently interdependent.
This paper aims to investigate the solvability of a class of fully coupled nonlinear FBS
\Delta
E systems. Before proceeding with the analysis, we provide a motivating example.
Khallout and Chala (2019)
previously studied the continuous-time version of this example.

An insurance company manages policyholders’ funds with the dual objective of achieving asset growth through investment and meeting the cash flow obligations of its clients. We aim to formulate a discrete-time model to describe this wealth evolution process and analyze the optimal investment strategy.

Let
x_{k}
denote the wealth of the policyholder at time
k
, with
x_{0}=m_{0}
being the initial investment. Define the time set as
\mathbb{T}=\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}
. At each time
k\in\mathbb{T}
, the insurance company is required to make a payment of
c_{k}x_{k}
to the policyholder, where
c_{k}
is the payout ratio.
The present value of these cash flows, discounted to time
k
using a deterministic discount factor
\exp\left(-\sum_{i=0}^{k}\lambda_{i}\right)
, where
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
is bounded and deterministic, is given by

y_{k}=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=k}^{N}e^{-\sum_{i=0}^{j}\lambda_{i}}c_{j}x_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}\right].

Here,
y_{k}
represents the present value of all future policy payments from time
k
to
N
, a standard concept in actuarial science and financial mathematics. It allows the insurer to evaluate whether current assets are sufficient to cover future liabilities.
Suppose the insurer invests in a financial market consisting of a risk-free asset with interest rate
r_{k}
, and a risky asset that follows a discrete-time geometric Brownian motion with drift
\mu_{k}
and volatility
\sigma_{k}
. All coefficients are assumed to be deterministic, bounded, and
\sigma_{k}\geq\varepsilon>0
. The dynamics of the wealth process are described by:

\left\{\begin{aligned} x_{k+1}&=(1+r_{k})x_{k}+\rho_{k}u_{k}+\sigma_{k}u_{k}\omega_{k},\\
x_{0}&=m_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where
u_{k}
denotes the investment in the risky asset and
\rho_{k}=\mu_{k}-r_{k}
is the risk premium.

However,
x_{k}
only captures the dynamics of forward wealth. From the insurer’s perspective, we model the liability valuation using a recursive backward equation:

y_{k}=\mathbb{E}\left[y_{k+1}(1+\lambda_{k})-c_{k}x_{k}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}\right],

(2)

where
y_{k}
represents the amount the insurer needs to prepare at time
k
to meet future obligations. Note that
y_{N}
can be specified as 0 or some terminal liability function.
Combining (
1
) and (
2
), we obtain the following forward-backward stochastic difference system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} x_{k+1}&=(1+r_{k})x_{k}+\rho_{k}u_{k}+\sigma_{k}u_{k}\omega_{k},\\
y_{k}&=\mathbb{E}\left[(1+\lambda_{k})y_{k+1}-c_{k}x_{k}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}\right],\\
x_{0}&=m_{0},\\
y_{N}&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3)

Clearly, system (
3
) is a fully coupled FBS
\Delta
E, where the backward component
y_{k}
depends on
x_{k}
. Such coupling reflects the interdependence between investment performance and liability obligations.

In this paper, we aim to investigate the solvability of the following nonlinear fully coupled FBS
\Delta
Es:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &x_{k+1}=b\left(k,\theta_{k}\right)+\sigma(k,\theta_{k})\omega_{k},\\
&y_{k}=-f\left(k+1,\theta_{k}\right),\quad k\in\mathbb{T},\\
&x_{0}=\Lambda(y_{0}),\\
&y_{N}=\Phi(x_{N}),\end{aligned}\right.

(4)

where
\theta_{k}:=(x_{k},y^{\prime}_{k+1},z^{\prime}_{k+1})
,
z_{k+1}:=y_{k+1}w_{k},y^{\prime}_{k+1}:=\mathbb{E}[y_{k+1}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}],z^{\prime}_{k+1}:=\mathbb{E}[z_{k+1}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}]
. Moreover,
x(\cdot)
and
y(\cdot)
take values in some given Euclidean space
\mathbb{H}
.
Also,
b(\cdot)
,
\sigma(\cdot)
and
f(\cdot)
are three given mappings with values in
\mathbb{H}
and
\left\{w_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{T}}
is a
\mathbb{R}
-value martingale difference sequence defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
. To simplify the notation, for any
\theta=(x,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}),

define

\Gamma(k,\theta):=(f(k+1,\theta),b(k,\theta),\sigma(k,\theta))

(5)

and

\langle\Gamma(k,\theta),\theta\rangle:=\langle f(k+1,\theta),x\rangle+\langle b(k,\theta),y^{\prime}\rangle+\langle\sigma(k,\theta),z^{\prime}\rangle.

(6)

In this way, all coefficients of FBS
\bigtriangleup
Es (
4
) are collected by
(\Lambda,\Phi,\Gamma)
.

Coupled FBSDEs were first studied by
Antonelli (1993)
, who proved existence and uniqueness for small time intervals and showed by counterexample that the same may not hold for longer durations, even under Lipschitz conditions.
Recently, a growing body of innovative research has tackled the challenge of solving FBSDEs on arbitrary time domains, mainly through two prevailing approaches. The first approach is called a four-step scheme approach, which combines partial differential equations with probabilistic methods and requires that all coefficients in the equations are non-stochastic and the coefficients of the diffusion terms are non-degenerate.
Ma et al. (1994)
first proposed the method, which was further developed by
Delarue (2002)
and
Zhang (2006)
, etc.
The second method, called a method of continuation, originates from the stochastic Hamiltonian system arising from solving control problems and obtains the solvability of the random coefficients of the FBSDE for an arbitrary length of time by requiring certain monotonicity conditions on the coefficients. The method was originally proposed by
Hu and Peng (1995)
, and then further developed by
Peng and Wu (1999)
and
Yong (1997)
.

We note that in most previous studies of FBSDEs, the initial and terminal states are not coupled, i.e.,
\Lambda
is independent of y and
\Phi
is independent of
x
; see
Antonelli (1993)
,
Delarue (2002)
,
Hu and Peng (1995)
,

Ma et al. (1994)
,
Ma et al. (2015)
and
Ma and Yong (1999)
for related studies.
However, the FBSDEs studied by
Li and Yu (2018)
,
Yong (2010)
, and
Yu (2022)
are general cases where coupled initial and terminal states exist. This paper is more concerned with a class of fully coupled FBS
\bigtriangleup
Es.

Some results have been achieved in the study of linearly fully coupled FBS
\bigtriangleup
Es.
Xu et al. (2017b)
;
Xu et al. (2018)
have studied a class of fully coupled FBS
\bigtriangleup
Es from the point of view of LQ optimal control, whose equations in which the inverted variables have the form of conditional expectations and satisfy that the coefficient matrices of the inverted variables are degenerate. For fully coupled infinite horizon FBS
\bigtriangleup
Es,
Xu et al. (2017a)
and
Song and Liu (2020)
adopted a generalized Riccati equation to systematically describe and explicitly express the adaptation solution. It is worth mentioning that
Zhang and Qi (2016)
advanced the development of discrete-time FBS
\bigtriangleup
Es by deriving the maximum principle for discrete-time mean-field linear quadratic (LQ) control problems for the first time.

Hower, few rigorous solvability results exist specifically for nonlinear FBS
\Delta
Es.

Ji and Liu (2024)
established pioneering solvability results for fully coupled FBS
\Delta
Es. Their work provides: (1) a complete characterization of linear FBS
\Delta
E solvability through the almost sure invertibility of matrices
\Gamma_{t}(P_{t+1})
with explicit solutions; (2) the first existence and uniqueness theorem for nonlinear FBS
\Delta
Es under classical monotonicity conditions, resolving discrete-time analysis challenges via a novel continuation method adaptation; and (3)
foundational analysis of BS
\Delta
Es where solutions are obtained through the Galtchouk-Kunita-Watanabe decomposition, yielding existence, uniqueness, and stability estimates

Regarding FBSDEs, inspired by various stochastic linear LQ problems,
Yu (2022)
proposed a more general class of domination-monotonicity framework and used the method of continuation to obtain a unique solvability result and a pair of estimates for general coupled FBSDEs. It is worth mentioning that the domination-monotonicity framework established by
Yu (2022)
, by introducing various matrices, matrix-valued random variables, and matrix-valued stochastic processes, can be applied to various stochastic LQ problems. This new framework contains the previous traditional monotonicity condition and develops it.

In this paper, we establish a class of discrete-time fully coupled nonlinear FBS
\Delta
Es based on a linear quadratic (LQ) optimal control framework by employing a discrete method. In particular, we discretize the domination-monotonicity framework proposed in
Yu (2022)
and apply the method of continuation to derive a pair of estimates for the FBS
\Delta
E system (
4
), thereby proving its unique solvability. To illustrate the applicability of our theoretical results, we also consider two LQ optimal control problems.
It is important to emphasize several key differences between our work and previous studies:

(i)

Compared with
Xu et al. (2017b)
;
Xu et al. (2018)
, which systematically studied the solvability of linear FBS
\Delta
Es by establishing explicit relations between the forward and backward components via generalized algebraic Riccati equations and deriving necessary and sufficient conditions for existence and uniqueness, our work considers more general nonlinear fully coupled systems.

(ii)

In contrast to
Song and Liu (2020)
and
Zhang and Qi (2016)
, which deal with general linear FBS
\Delta
Es and express the adapted solutions via Riccati equations, our approach addresses
nonlinear
systems. We construct separate estimates for the solutions and establish uniqueness using the continuation method. The development of numerical methods for such nonlinear systems constitutes a promising direction for future research.

(iii)

While
Yu (2022)
studied the unique solvability of fully coupled nonlinear FBSDEs under a new domination-monotonicity condition in continuous time, our work focuses on the discrete-time setting. In this framework, classical stochastic analysis tools—such as Itô formula, quadratic variation, and stochastic integrals—are no longer applicable. Instead, we utilize conditional expectations, martingale difference sequences (MDS), and recursive techniques. Specifically, backward dynamics are handled using recursions such as:

\mathbb{E}[\omega_{k}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbb{E}[\omega_{k}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]=1,\hskip 9.24994pty^{\prime}_{k}=\mathbb{E}[y_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k+1}],

which avoid the path regularity assumptions required in continuous-time models.

(iv)

The class of fully coupled nonlinear FBS
\Delta
Es is also investigated in
Ji and Liu (2024)
. Our formulation differs significantly in the system’s structure and the proof techniques.
Ji and Liu (2024)
considered a backward equation with three components
(Y,Z,N)
, whereas our model involves only a single backward component
Y
. Moreover, while
Ji and Liu (2024)
relied on the classical monotonicity condition. In contrast, our study adopts the generalized domination-monotonicity condition
Yu (2022)
, ?guaranteeing solution existence and uniqueness while extending the applicability of the classical condition.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary notations and present two preliminary lemmas on stochastic difference equations (S
\Delta
Es) and backward stochastic difference equations (BS
\Delta
Es), which will serve as foundational tools for our analysis. Section
3
is devoted to the study of the fully coupled FBS
\Delta
E system (
4
) under domination-monotonicity conditions via the continuation method, where we establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions, along with a pair of crucial estimates, as stated in Theorem
3
. In Section
4
, we apply the theoretical results to two linear-quadratic (LQ) optimal control problems and demonstrate the existence and uniqueness of the corresponding optimal controls.
Finally, we conclude the paper by summarizing the main results and highlighting potential directions for future research.

Assume that the inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}|\widehat{x}_{k}|^{2}\right]

(10)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\eta-\bar{\eta}\right|^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\left(|b(k,\bar{x}_{k})-\bar{b}(k,\bar{x}_{k})|^{2}\right.\right.

\displaystyle+|\sigma(k,\bar{x}_{k})-\bar{\sigma}(k,\bar{x}_{k})|^{2})\bigg]

Expanding this, we get

\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{x}_{N}|^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{b}_{N-1}|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{\sigma}_{N-1}|^{2}].

Using the Lipschitz condition:

&\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[|\widehat{x}_{N-1}|^{2}+|\sigma(N-1,\bar{x}_{N-1})-\bar{\sigma}(N-1,\bar{x}_{N-1})|^{2}\right].\end{split}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\widehat{x}_{N}\right|^{2}\right]

(11)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\bigg[|\widehat{x}_{N-1}|^{2}+|b(N-1,\bar{x}_{N-1})-\bar{b}(N-1,\bar{x}_{N-1})|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left|\sigma(N-1,\bar{x}_{N-1})-\bar{\sigma}(N-1,\bar{x}_{N-1})\right|^{2}\bigg].

By the inductive hypothesis, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{x}_{k}|^{2}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\bigg[|\eta-\bar{\eta}|^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigg(|b(k,\bar{x}_{k})-\bar{b}(k,\bar{x}_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\sigma(k,\bar{x}_{k})-\bar{\sigma}(k,\bar{x}_{k})|^{2}\bigg)\bigg].

Thus, the inductive step holds. By induction, the result is established for all

. This completes the proof. ∎

vanish, FBS
\bigtriangleup
E (
28
) and FBS
\bigtriangleup
E (
4
) are identical. Next, we will illustrate that if for some
\alpha_{0}\in[0,1)
, FBS
\bigtriangleup
E(
28
) is uniquely solvable for any
\big(\xi,\eta,\rho(\cdot)\big)\in\mathcal{H}(\overline{\mathbb{T}})
, then there exists a fixed step length

such that the same conclusion still holds for any
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}+\delta_{0}]
. As long as this has been proved to be true, we can gradually increase the parameter
\alpha
until
\alpha=1
. This method is called the method of continuation which was initially introduced by
Hu and Peng (1995)
.

For this goal, we shall first establish an a priori estimate for the solution of FBS
\bigtriangleup

), which plays an important role in the subsequent proofs.

Lemma 4
.

Let the given coefficients
(\Lambda,\Phi,\Gamma)
satisfy Assumption
3
and Assumption
4
. Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
,
\big(\xi,\eta,\rho(\cdot)\big)
,
\big(\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{\rho}(\cdot)\big)\in\mathcal{H}(\overline{\mathbb{T}})

\bigtriangleup
E (
28
) with the coefficients

and
(\bar{x}(\cdot),\bar{y}(\cdot))\in N^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{R}^{2n})
is also the solution to FBS
\bigtriangleup
E (
28
) with the coefficients
(\Lambda^{\alpha}+\bar{\xi},\Phi^{\alpha}+\bar{\eta},\Gamma^{\alpha}+\bar{\rho})
. Then the following estimate holds:

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{x}_{k}|^{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{y}_{k}|^{2}\bigg]\leq K\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{J}}],

(31)

where

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{J}}=

\displaystyle|\widehat{\eta}|^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}|\widehat{\varphi}_{k}|^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}|\widehat{\psi}_{k}|^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}|\widehat{\gamma}_{k}|^{2}+|\widehat{\xi}|^{2}

(32)

and
\widehat{\xi}=\xi-\bar{\xi}
,
\widehat{\varphi}=\varphi-\bar{\varphi}
, etc.
Here
K
is a positive constant depending on
\mathbb{T}
.

\mathbf{{Proof.}}

In the following proofs, the argument
k
is suppressed for simplicity. Besides, it should be noted that the positive constant
K
could be changed line by line.
By the estimate (
9
) in Lemma
1
, an estimate of
\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{x}_{k}|^{2}\bigg]
can be obtained:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{x}_{k}|^{2}\bigg]

(33)

\displaystyle\leq K\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{\big|\alpha\big(\Lambda(y_{0})-\Lambda(\bar{y}_{0})\big)-(1-\alpha)\mu M^{\top}M\widehat{y}_{0}+\widehat{\xi}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigg|\alpha\bigg(b(k,\bar{x}_{k},y_{k+1}^{\prime},z^{\prime}_{k+1})-b(k,\bar{x}_{k},\bar{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\bar{z}_{k+1}^{\prime})\bigg)

\displaystyle-(1-\alpha)\mu\Big[B_{k}^{\top}Q(k,\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\widehat{z}_{k+1})\Big]+\widehat{\psi}_{k}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{N}\Bigg|\alpha\bigg(\sigma(k,\bar{x}_{k},y_{k+1}^{\prime},z_{k+1})-\sigma(k,\bar{x}_{k},\bar{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\bar{z}_{k+1}^{\prime})\bigg)

\displaystyle-(1-\alpha)\mu\Big[C_{k}^{\top}Q(k,\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime})\Big]+\widehat{\gamma}_{k}\Bigg|^{2}\Bigg\}.

Similarly, by applying the estimate (
12
) in Lemma
2
, we can obtain an estimate of
\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{y}_{k}|^{2}\bigg]
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{y}_{k}|^{2}\bigg]

(34)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{\Big|\alpha\big(\Phi(x_{N})-\Phi(\bar{x}_{N})\big)+(1-\alpha)vG^{\top}G\hat{x}_{N}+\widehat{\eta}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigg|\alpha\bigg(f(k+1,x_{k},\bar{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\bar{z}_{k+1}^{\prime})

\displaystyle-f(k+1,\bar{x}_{k},\bar{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\bar{z}_{k+1}^{\prime})\bigg)

\displaystyle-(1-\alpha)v\Big[A_{k}^{\top}P(k,\widehat{x}_{k})\Big]+\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg\}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widehat{x}_{N},\widehat{y}_{N}\right\rangle-\left\langle\widehat{x}_{0},\widehat{y}_{0}\right\rangle\right]

(35)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k+1},\widehat{y}_{k+1}\right\rangle-\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k},\widehat{y}_{k}\right\rangle\bigg],

where the left side is equal

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widehat{x}_{N},\widehat{y}_{N}\right\rangle-\left\langle\widehat{x}_{0},\widehat{y}_{0}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widehat{x}_{N},\widehat{\Phi}^{\alpha}\left(x_{N}^{\alpha}\right)+\widehat{\eta}\right\rangle-\left\langle\widehat{\Lambda}^{\alpha}\left(y_{0}^{\alpha}\right)+\widehat{\xi},\widehat{y}_{0}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widehat{x}_{N},\alpha\left(\Phi\left(x_{N}\right)-\Phi\left(\bar{x}_{N}\right)\right)+(1-\alpha)vG^{\top}G\widehat{x}_{N}+\widehat{\eta}\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\left\langle\alpha\left(\Lambda\left(y_{0}\right)-\Lambda\left(\bar{y}_{0}\right)\right)-(1-\alpha)\mu M^{\top}M\widehat{y}_{0}+\widehat{\xi},\widehat{y}_{0}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(1-\alpha)v\left|G\widehat{x}_{N}\right|^{2}+(1-\alpha)\mu\left|M\widehat{y}_{0}\right|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle+\left\langle\widehat{x}_{N},\alpha\left(\Phi\left(x_{N}\right)-\Phi\left(\bar{x}_{N}\right)\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left.-\left\langle\alpha\left(\Lambda\left(y_{0}\right)-\Lambda\left(\bar{y}_{0}\right)\right),\widehat{y}_{0}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{x}_{N},\widehat{\eta}\right\rangle-\left\langle\widehat{\xi},\widehat{y}_{0}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k+1},\widehat{y}_{k+1}\right\rangle-\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k},\widehat{y}_{k}\right\rangle\bigg]

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k+1},\widehat{y}_{k+1}\right\rangle\mid\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]-\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k},\widehat{y}_{k}\right\rangle\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widehat{b}_{k}^{\alpha}+\widehat{\psi}_{k}+\left[\widehat{\sigma}_{k}^{\alpha}+\widehat{\gamma}_{k}\right]\omega_{k},\widehat{y}_{k+1}\right\rangle\mid\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle-\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k},\widehat{y}_{k}\right\rangle\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left\langle\widehat{b}_{k}^{\alpha},\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{\psi}_{k},\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{\sigma}_{k}^{\alpha},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\widehat{\gamma}_{k},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{f}_{k+1}^{\alpha},\widehat{x}_{k}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k},\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\right\rangle\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left\langle\alpha\widehat{b}_{k}-(1-\alpha)\mu\left[B_{k}^{\top}Q(k,\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime})\right],\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\alpha\widehat{\sigma}_{k}-(1-\alpha)\mu\left[C_{k}^{\top}Q(k,\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime})\right],\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\alpha\widehat{f}_{k+1}-(1-\alpha)v\left[A_{k}^{\top}P(k,\widehat{x}_{k})\right],\widehat{x}_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\widehat{\psi}_{k},\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{\gamma}_{k},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k},\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\right\rangle\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left\langle\alpha\widehat{f}_{k+1},\widehat{x}_{k}\right\rangle+\left\langle\alpha\widehat{b}_{k},\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\alpha\widehat{\sigma}_{k},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle(1-\alpha)\mu Q(k,\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime}),B_{k}\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime}+C_{k}\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle(1-\alpha)vP(k,\widehat{x}_{k}),A_{k}\widehat{x}_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\widehat{\psi}_{k},\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{\gamma}_{k},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k},\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\right\rangle\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\alpha\langle\Gamma(\theta_{k})-\Gamma(\bar{\theta}_{k}),\widehat{\theta}_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle-(1-\alpha)\mu\left|Q(k,\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime})\right|^{2}-(1-\alpha)v\left|P(k,\widehat{x}_{k})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\langle\widehat{\psi}_{k},\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{\gamma}_{k},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k},\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\right\rangle\bigg],

where

, and similarly for
\widehat{\sigma},\widehat{f},\widehat{\Gamma}
, etc.
Putting (
36

37

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{(1-\alpha)v|G\widehat{x}_{N}|^{2}+\alpha\left\langle\Phi(x_{N})-\Phi(\bar{x}_{N}),\widehat{x}_{N}\right\rangle

(38)

\displaystyle+(1-\alpha)\mu|M\widehat{y}_{0}|^{2}-\alpha\left\langle\Lambda(y_{0})-\Lambda(\bar{y}_{0}),\widehat{y}_{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+(1-\alpha)\mu\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\big|Q(k,\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\widehat{z}^{\prime}_{k+1})\big|^{2}

\displaystyle+(1-\alpha)v\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\big|P(k,\widehat{x}_{k})\big|^{2}

\displaystyle-\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\langle\Gamma(k,\theta)-\Gamma(k,\bar{\theta}),\widehat{\theta}\rangle\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{-\left\langle\widehat{\eta},\widehat{x}_{N}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{\xi},\widehat{y}_{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigg[\left\langle\widehat{\psi}_{k},\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{\gamma}_{k},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k},\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\right\rangle\bigg]\Bigg\}.

-(iii), (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{v|G\widehat{x}_{N}|^{2}+\mu|M\widehat{y}_{0}|^{2}+v\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\big|P(k,\widehat{x}_{k})\big|^{2}

(39)

\displaystyle+\mu\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\big|Q(k,\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime})\big|^{2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{-\left\langle\widehat{\eta},\widehat{x}_{N}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{\xi},\widehat{y}_{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Big[\left\langle\widehat{\psi}_{k},\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{\gamma}_{k},\widehat{z}_{k+1}^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{x}_{k},\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\right\rangle\Big]\Bigg\}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{x}_{k}|^{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{y}_{k}|^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq K\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{\widehat{\mathrm{J}}-\left\langle\widehat{\eta},\widehat{x}_{N}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{\xi},\widehat{y}_{0}\right\rangle

Case 2

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{x}_{k}|^{2}\bigg]

Next, we prove a continuation lemma based on the a priori estimate in Lemma

.

Lemma 5
.

Under the condition of Assumption
3
and Assumption
4
, if for some
\alpha_{0}\in[0,1)
, FBS
\bigtriangleup
E (
28
) admits a unique solution
(x(\cdot),y(\cdot))\in N^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{R}^{2n})
for any
(\xi,\eta,\rho(\cdot))\in\mathcal{H}(\overline{\mathbb{T}})
, then there exists an absolute constant
\delta_{0}>0
such that FBS
\bigtriangleup
E (
28
) admits a unique solution for
\alpha=\alpha_{0}+\delta
with
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
and
\alpha\leq 1
.

\mathbf{Proof.}

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be determined below. For any
(x(\cdot),y(\cdot))\in N^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{R}^{2n})
,
\theta(k)=(x_{k},y^{\prime}_{k+1},z^{\prime}_{k+1})
and
\Theta(k)=(X_{k},Y^{\prime}_{k+1},Z^{\prime}_{k+1})
,we introduce the following FBS
\bigtriangleup
E with unknow
(X(\cdot),Y(\cdot))\in N^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{R}^{2n})
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} X_{k+1}=&\Big\{-(1-\alpha_{0})\mu\Big[B_{k}^{\top}Q(k,Y_{k+1}^{\prime},Z^{\prime}_{k+1})\Big]\\
&\quad+\alpha_{0}b(k,\Theta(k))+\widetilde{\psi}_{k}\Big\}\\
&\quad+\Big\{-(1-\alpha_{0})\mu\Big[C_{k}^{\top}Q(k,Y_{k+1}^{\prime},Z^{\prime}_{k+1})\Big]\\
&\quad+\alpha_{0}\sigma(k,\Theta(k))+\widetilde{\gamma}_{k}\Big\}w_{k},\\
Y_{k}&=-\Big\{-(1-\alpha_{0})v\Big[A_{k}^{\top}P(k,X_{k})\Big]\\
&\quad+\alpha_{0}f(k,\Theta(k))+\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}\Big\},\quad k\in\mathbb{T},\\
X_{0}&=\alpha_{0}\Lambda(Y_{0})-(1-\alpha_{0})\mu M^{\top}MY_{0}+\widetilde{\xi},\\
Y_{N}&=\alpha_{0}\Phi(X_{N})+(1-\alpha_{0})vG^{\top}GX_{N}+\widetilde{\eta},\end{aligned}\right.

(48)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\widetilde{\psi}_{k}=\psi_{k}+\delta b(k,\theta(k))+\delta\mu\Big[B_{k}^{\top}Q(k,y_{k+1}^{\prime},z^{\prime}_{k+1})\Big],\\
&\widetilde{\gamma}_{k}=\gamma_{k}+\delta\sigma(k,\theta(k))+\delta\mu\Big[C_{k}^{\top}Q(k,y_{k+1}^{\prime},z^{\prime}_{k+1}))\Big],\\
&\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}=\varphi_{k}+\delta f(k+1,\theta(k))+\delta v\Big[A_{k}^{\top}P(k,x_{k})\Big],\\
&\widetilde{\eta}=\eta+\delta\Phi(x_{N})-\delta vG^{\top}Gx_{N},\\
&\widetilde{\xi}=\xi+\delta\Lambda(y_{0})+\delta\mu M^{\top}My_{0},\\
&Z_{k+1}=Y_{k+1}\omega_{k},\\
&Y^{\prime}_{k+1}=[Y_{k+1}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}],\\
&Z^{\prime}_{k+1}=[Y_{k+1}\omega_{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}].\end{aligned}\right.

(49)

Furthermore, we also denote
\widetilde{\rho}(\cdot)=(\widetilde{\varphi}(\cdot),\widetilde{\psi}(\cdot),\widetilde{\gamma}(\cdot))
. Then it is easy to check that
(\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{\eta},\widetilde{\rho})\in\mathcal{H}[\overline{\mathbb{T}}]
. By our assumptions, the FBS
\bigtriangleup
E (
48
) admits a unique solution
(X(\cdot),Y(\cdot))\in N^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{R}^{2n})
. In fact, we can establish a mapping

\displaystyle(X(\cdot),Y(\cdot))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{0}+\delta}\big((x(\cdot),y(\cdot))\big):N^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{R}^{2n})\to N^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{R}^{2n}).

In the following, we shall prove that the above mapping is contractive when
\delta
is small enough.

Let
(x(\cdot),y(\cdot)),(\bar{x}(\cdot),\bar{y}(\cdot))\in N^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{R}^{2n})
and
(X(\cdot),Y(\cdot))=\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{0}+\delta}\big(x(\cdot),y(\cdot)\big)
,
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{Y}(\cdot))=\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{0}+\delta}\big(\bar{x}(\cdot),\bar{y}(\cdot)\big)
. Similarly, we denote
\widehat{x}(\cdot)=x(\cdot)-\bar{x}(\cdot),\widehat{y}(\cdot)=y(\cdot)-\bar{y}(\cdot),\widehat{X}(\cdot)=X(\cdot)-\bar{X}(\cdot),\widehat{Y}(\cdot)=Y(\cdot)-\bar{Y}(\cdot)
, etc. By applying Lemma
4
, we have (the argument
k
is suppressed for simplicity)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{X}_{k}|^{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\widehat{Y}_{k}|^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{\Big|\big(\Phi(x_{N})-\Phi(\bar{x}_{N})\big)-vG^{\top}G\widehat{x}_{N}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\Big|\Lambda(y_{0})-\Lambda(\bar{y}_{0})-\mu M^{\top}M\widehat{y_{0}}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigg|\big(b(\theta_{k})-b(\bar{\theta_{k}})\big)+\mu\big[B_{k}^{\top}Q(k,\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\widehat{z}_{k+1})\big]\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigg|\big(\sigma(\theta_{k})-\sigma(\bar{\theta_{k}}\big)+\mu\big[C_{k}^{\top}Q(k,\widehat{y}_{k+1}^{\prime},\widehat{z}_{k+1})\big]\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigg|\big(f(\theta_{k})-f(\bar{\theta_{k}})\big)+v\big[A_{k}^{\top}P(k,\widehat{x}_{k})\big]\bigg|\Bigg\}.

Due to the Lipschitz continuity of
(\Lambda,\Phi,\Gamma)
and the boundedness of
G,M,A_{k},B_{k},C_{k}
, there exists a new constant
K^{\prime}>0
independent of
\alpha_{0}
and
\delta
such that

\|(\widehat{X}(\cdot),\widehat{Y}(\cdot))\|^{2}_{N^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\leq K^{\prime}\delta^{2}\|(\hat{x}(\cdot),\hat{y}(\cdot))\|^{2}_{N^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{R}^{2n})}.

By selecting
\delta_{0}=1/(2\sqrt{K^{\prime}})
, when
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
, we can find that the mapping
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{0}+\delta}
is contractive. Thus, the mapping
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{0}+\delta}
admits a unique fixed point which is just the unique solution to FBS
\bigtriangleup
E (
28
). The proof is finished.
∎

\mathbf{[Proof~of~Theorem~\ref{thm:3.1}.]}

Firstly, the solvability of FBS
\bigtriangleup
E (
4
) in the space
N^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{T}};\mathbb{R}^{2n})
is deduced from the unique solvability of FBS
\bigtriangleup
E (

) and Lemma

. Secondly, in Lemma
4
, by taking
\alpha=1
,

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated a class of discrete-time fully coupled nonlinear fully coupled nonlinear FBS
\bigtriangleup
E motivated by LQ control problems. By integrating a discrete-time domination-monotonicity framework with the continuation method, we have derived essential a priori estimates and rigorously established the unique solvability of the system. To illustrate the practical relevance of our theoretical results, we have analyzed two discrete-time LQ control problems and demonstrated their solvability under the proposed framework. Future research directions include extending the model to infinite-horizon settings to explore asymptotic behaviors, formulating optimal control strategies via Pontryagin-type principles, generalizing the framework to accommodate non-Markovian or regime-switching dynamics, and developing numerical algorithms for high-dimensional systems. These advancements are expected to enhance the applicability of discrete-time stochastic control theory in financial engineering and decision-making under uncertainty.

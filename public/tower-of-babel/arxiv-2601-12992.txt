Bernstein type gradient estimate for system of weighted local heat equations with potential term
Sujit Bhattacharyya

Abstract
. In this article we provide Bernstein type gradient estimates for two system of local weighted heat type equations with potentials on a weighted Riemannian manifold. We derive all possible cases considering linear potential, exponential potential, combining with static manifold and evolving manifold. This work partially resolved the problem raised by Bhattacharyya et al. in [ 8 ] .

Heat equations are very much popular in the field of manifold theory because many evolution formulas arrive naturally in the form of a parabolic partial differential equation. Solving those equations in general is a very hard task. Numerical solutions does not gives us the essence of the effect of curvature constraints in those solutions. Since the solution to those partial differential equations depends on the shape of the space, so it is necessary to understand how the curvature shapes the solutions. As a result the method of gradient estimation plays a crucial role. In this technique one can get properties of the solutions of those equations without actually solving them. This technique was initiated in the year 1887 by Harnack
[
11
]
, which is today known as Harnack type estimation. Later it was further developed by Li-Yau
[
17

. After that Hamilton’s introduction to Ricci flow
[

and Perelman’s solution to geometrization conjecture in 2002-2004
[
18

19
,
20
]
this technique becomes globally well known. From this point the theory of gradient estimation becomes a new branch of mathematics, which starts to cover various types of linear and nonlinear partial differential equations in both static and evolving manifolds. In this regard some notable works of Abolarinwa covers elliptic gradient estimates for weighted elliptic equation
[

along the generalized geometric flow and finally on nonlinear heat type equation

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{\phi})u=H(u)\\
u|_{\partial M}=0\end{cases}.

1.1.
Physical Meaning

The coefficient
\chi^{2}
has units of
\text{length}^{2}/\text{time}
and determines the rate of diffusion:

•

Larger
\chi^{2}
means faster spreading and smoothing of disturbances.

•

Smaller
\chi^{2}
means slower diffusion i.e., gradients persist longer.

Thermal Case

In heat conduction,
[
7
]

\chi^{2}=\alpha=\frac{k}{\rho c_{p}},

where
k
is thermal conductivity,
\rho
is density, and
c_{p}
is specific heat capacity. This ratio balances the ability to conduct heat versus the capacity to store heat.

Probabilistic Case

For Brownian motion,
[
10
]

\text{Var}(B_{t})=2\chi^{2}t,

here
\chi^{2}
is half the variance growth rate of the random walk.

Finance Case

In the Black–Scholes equation
[
16
, Chapter 15]
, volatility
\sigma
plays the role of diffusivity. After a change of variables, the partial differential equation reduces to a heat equation with

\chi^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}.

Neural Fields (Biophysics)

Equations of the form
[
5
]

(\partial_{t}-D\Delta)u=v,\quad(\partial_{t}-D\Delta)v=u

Used in excitatory–inhibitory neural field models.

Quantum Field Theory.

This type of equation
[
13
]

(\partial_{t}-\tfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta)u=v,\quad(\partial_{t}-\tfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta)v=u

used to models coupled Bosonic fields with diffusion-like propagation.

\chi^{2}=\hbar^{2}/2m
.

Image Processing.

(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta)I_{1}=I_{2},\quad(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta)I_{2}=I_{1}

The above equations
[
21
]
can be found for Dual-channel diffusion for PDE-based image enhancement.

\chi^{2}=\sigma^{2}/2
.

Chemical Kinetics.

For parabolic system with exponential potential system one can see
[
22
]
where

\partial_{t}C_{1}-D\Delta C_{1}=k_{1}e^{C_{2}},\quad\partial_{t}C_{2}-D\Delta C_{2}=k_{2}e^{C_{1}}

provides a Reaction–diffusion systems with exponential growth rates for coupled systems.
Thus it is worth to study system of equations along the well constructed theory of gradient estimation technique and so we are considering a weighted Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}d\mu)
. Let
\chi:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth map. Consider the system of parabolic equations with potential term of degree one, i.e., with linear potential,

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\chi^{2}\Delta_{f}u-v,\\
\partial_{t}v=\chi^{2}\Delta_{f}v-u,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0},\ \forall x\in M,\\
v(x,0)=v_{0},\ \forall x\in M.\\
\end{cases}

(1.1)

It can be seen that
u(x,t)=u_{0}(x)
and
v(x,t)=v_{0}
, for all
x\in M\setminus supp\{\chi\}
. We first assume that the manifold
M
is not evolving along any geometric flow i.e., the manifold is a static weighted Riemannian manifold. Next we will be following the methodology as showed in
[
6
]
, and from there we find that

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}u^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}(\nabla_{i}u^{2})-\langle\nabla f,\nabla u^{2}\rangle

(1.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}(2u\nabla_{i}u)-2u\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|\nabla u|^{2}+2u\Delta u-2u\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|\nabla u|^{2}+2u\Delta_{f}u

Remark 1.1
.

Note that our equation is symmetric with respect to
u
and
v
. This means that if we interchange
u
and
v
in the first equation we get the second one, as a result all we need to do is to find evolution equations for one equation and interchanging
u
and
v
will lead to the evolution equation for the other.

After that we consider the manifold
M
to be evolving along the local Ricci flow and derive the gradient estimate for the equation (
1.1
). During the calculation it was observed that we can actually solve the problem proposed in
[
8
]
, with suitable application of Young’s inequality. As a result we investigated the system with exponential potential term given by

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\chi^{2}\Delta_{f}u+ae^{v},\\
\partial_{t}v=\chi^{2}\Delta_{f}v+be^{u},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0},\ \forall x\in M,\\
v(x,0)=v_{0},\ \forall x\in M,\\
\end{cases}

(1.3)

and found that the Bernstein type estimate can be derived for the case
a<0,b<0
. So we have not completely solved the problem but we got a possible way to resolve the issue. However, we will again mention this issue in the last section of this manuscript. We derived Bernstein type estimate for the equation (
1.3
) on both static and evolving manifolds.

2.
Bernstein type estimate on static manifold

2.1.
Bernstein type gradient estimate for system of weighted local heat equation with linear potential term on static manifold

Theorem 2.1
.

If
u,v\geq 0
be a solution to the system (
1.1
) on
M
with
Ric_{f}^{m-n}\geq-Kg
, then there exist constants

B_{1}=(\Phi_{0}(K)T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ u_{0}+T\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ v_{0}

and

B_{2}=(\Phi_{0}(K)T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ v_{0}+T\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ u_{0},

such that

\begin{cases}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\frac{B_{1}}{\chi^{2}t},\\
|\nabla v|^{2}\leq\frac{B_{2}}{\chi^{2}t},\text{both on $\Omega\times(0,T]$,}\end{cases}

(2.1)

where

\Phi(\chi,f,m,K)=8m|\nabla\chi|^{2}-\chi\Delta_{f}\chi+7|\nabla\chi|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}+K,

and
\Phi_{0}(K):=\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ \Phi(\chi,f,m,K)>-(1+\frac{1}{2T}),\forall K\geq 0
.

Proof.

Before starting the proof it should be mentioned that the function
\Phi
will be used to group terms that contains the arguments
(\chi,f,m,K)
, their combinations or their derivatives. Observe that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(u^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2uu_{t}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2u(\chi^{2}\Delta_{f}u-v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(\Delta_{f}(u^{2})-2|\nabla u|^{2})-2uv

Next we simplify the following expression

(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}t|\nabla u|^{2}).

(2.3)

Since
\chi
is a function on the spatial variable so it is independent of time and thus

(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}t|\nabla u|^{2})=t(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})+\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}.

(2.4)

Hence all it remains to get the evolution equation for
\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}
. To derive this we proceed as follows.

Since
M
is a static manifold so

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\chi^{2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla u_{t}\rangle

(2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\chi^{2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla(\chi^{2}\Delta_{f}(u)-v)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\chi^{3}\Delta_{f}u\langle\nabla u,\nabla\chi\rangle+2\chi^{4}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\Delta_{f}u\rangle-2\chi^{2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle

Applying weighted Bochner formula in (
2.5
) we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi^{4}(\Delta_{f}|\nabla u|^{2}-2|\text{Hess }u|^{2}-2Ric_{f}(\nabla u,\nabla u))

(2.6)

\displaystyle+4\chi^{3}\Delta_{f}u\langle\nabla u,\nabla\chi\rangle-2\chi^{2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle

and

\displaystyle\chi^{2}\Delta_{f}(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi^{2}\left(2|\nabla u|^{2}(\chi\Delta_{f}\chi+|\nabla\chi|^{2})+\chi^{2}\Delta_{f}|\nabla u|^{2}+8\chi\text{Hess}u(\nabla u,\nabla\chi)\right)

From (
2.1
) and (
2.6
) we get

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\chi^{4}|\text{Hess }u|^{2}-2\chi^{4}Ric_{f}(\nabla u,\nabla u)+4\chi^{3}\Delta_{f}u\langle\nabla u,\nabla\chi\rangle

(2.7)

\displaystyle-2\chi^{2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle-2\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}(\chi\Delta_{f}\chi+|\nabla\chi|^{2})

\displaystyle-8\chi^{3}\text{Hess }u(\nabla u,\nabla\chi).

Now we estimate the terms on the right hand side one by one. For
m>n
we have from
[
6
]

\displaystyle 4\chi^{3}\Delta_{f}u\langle\nabla u,\nabla\chi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 16m\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\chi|^{2}+\chi^{4}|\text{Hess }u|^{2}

(2.8)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\chi^{4}}{m-n}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle^{2}.

and

\displaystyle-8\chi^{2}\text{Hess }u(\nabla\chi,\nabla u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 16\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\chi|^{2}+\chi^{4}|\text{Hess }u|^{2}.

(2.9)

Here we have used
\frac{1}{m}(\Delta_{f}u)^{2}\leq|\text{Hess u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{m-n}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle^{2}
from
[
14
]
. Using (
2.9
), (
2.8
) in (
2.7
) and applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we infer

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\{8m|\nabla\chi|^{2}-(\chi\Delta_{f}\chi+|\nabla\chi|^{2})+8|\nabla\chi|^{2}\}

(2.10)

\displaystyle+2\chi^{2}|\nabla u||\nabla v|-2\chi^{2}Ric_{f}^{m-n}(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\Phi(\chi,f,m,K)+\chi^{2}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}+2|\nabla v|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-2\chi^{2}Ric_{f}^{m-n}(\nabla u,\nabla u)

Here we have used Young’s inequality
2ab\leq\frac{a^{2}}{2}+2b^{2}
. After that we apply the curvature condition
Ric_{f}^{m-n}\geq-Kg
and update the definition of
\Phi(\chi,f,m,K)
by absorbing the term
\frac{1}{2}
and
K
into
\Phi(\chi,f,m,K)
, thus we get

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})\leq 2\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\Phi(\chi,f,m,K)+2\chi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}.

(2.11)

Since
\Phi_{0}(K)=\underset{\chi,m,f,\Omega}{max}\Phi(\chi,f,m,K)
so

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}t|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})+\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2t\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\Phi_{0}(K)+\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+2t\chi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}(\Phi_{0}(K)t+\frac{1}{2})+2t\chi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}.

Thus by (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\implies(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})\left(\chi^{2}t|\nabla u|^{2}+(\Phi_{0}(K)T+\frac{1}{2})u^{2}+Tv^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\left(\Phi_{0}(K)T+T+\frac{1}{2}\right)uv

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0,\ \text{as $u,v\geq 0$}.

As
\chi^{2}t|\nabla u|^{2}|_{(\Omega\times{0})\cup(\partial\Omega\times[0,T])}\equiv 0
,
u|_{\partial\Omega\times[0,T]}=u_{0}
and our equation is symmetric with respect to
u
and
v
, hence by maximum principle we find that

\chi^{2}t|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\chi^{2}t|\nabla u|^{2}+(\Phi_{0}(K)T+\frac{1}{2})u^{2}+Tv^{2}\leq(\Phi_{0}(K)T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ u_{0}+T\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ v_{0}.

\chi^{2}t|\nabla v|^{2}\leq\chi^{2}t|\nabla v|^{2}+(\Phi_{0}(K)T+\frac{1}{2})v^{2}+Tu^{2}\leq(\Phi_{0}(K)T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ v_{0}+T\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ u_{0}.

Assuming

B_{1}=(\Phi_{0}(K)T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ u_{0}+T\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ v_{0}

and

B_{2}=(\Phi_{0}(K)T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ v_{0}+T\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ u_{0},

gives the gradient estimates (
2.1
).
∎

2.2.
Bernstein type gradient estimate for system of weighted local heat equation with exponential potential term on static manifold

In this section a possible solution is being provide to resolve the question raised in
[

]
. It is a possible way because in this section we are going to derive the Bernstein type estimate with the assumption

Theorem 2.2
.

If

be a bounded solution to the system (

) on

Next we simplify

(2.15)

For the first term, using Bochner formula we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\chi^{3}\Delta_{f}u\langle\nabla u,\nabla\chi\rangle+\chi^{4}\{\Delta_{f}|\nabla u|^{2}-2|\text{Hess }u|^{2}

\displaystyle-2Ric_{f}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\}+2ae^{v}\chi^{2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle.

Thus the evolution equation of

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})

Applying (

\displaystyle\leq

3.

In this section we extend the above results to the case of manifold evolving along local Ricci flow. Henceforth we are denoting
(M^{n},g(t),e^{-f}d\mu)
a manifold which satisfies

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g=-2\chi^{2}Ric.

(3.1)

This equation is called the local Ricci flow, whose existence has been studied in
[
6
]
. Interested readers can see
[
8
]
for additional informations on this flow and its extension. This section has been divided into two parts one with the first system (
1.1
) with polynomial potential function and in the next section we consider the system (
1.3
) with exponential potential function on evolving manifold and derive Bernstein type estimates for each cases. To write the result concisely we need to extend the lemma of
[
8
]
to the case of nonzero potential function.

Lemma 3.1

(Extension of Lemma 2.2 of
[
8
]
)
.

If
u,v
is a solution to the system of weighted heat type equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})u=\lambda_{1}(u,v)\\
(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})v=\lambda_{2}(u,v)\\
\end{cases}

(3.2)

on a weighted Riemannian manifold
(M,g(t),e^{-f}d\mu)
evolving along the abstract geometric flow

\partial_{t}g_{ij}=-2h_{ij}

(3.3)

where
h_{ij}
are smooth functions on
M
, then we have the following

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\chi^{2}(h+\chi^{2}Ric)(\nabla u,\nabla u)-2\chi^{4}\text{Hess }f(\nabla u,\nabla u)

(3.4)

\displaystyle-2\chi^{2}|\text{Hess }u|^{2}+4\chi^{3}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\chi\rangle\Delta_{f}u-2\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}(\chi\Delta_{f}\chi+|\nabla\chi|^{2})

\displaystyle-8\chi^{3}\text{Hess }u(\nabla u,\nabla\chi)-2\chi^{2}\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial u}|\nabla u|^{2}-2\chi^{2}\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial v}\langle\nabla v,\nabla u\rangle

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\chi^{2}(h+\chi^{2}Ric)(\nabla v,\nabla v)-2\chi^{4}\text{Hess }f(\nabla v,\nabla v)

(3.5)

\displaystyle-2\chi^{2}|\text{Hess }v|^{2}+4\chi^{3}\langle\nabla v,\nabla\chi\rangle\Delta_{f}v-2\chi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}(\chi\Delta_{f}\chi+|\nabla\chi|^{2})

\displaystyle-8\chi^{3}\text{Hess }v(\nabla v,\nabla\chi)-2\chi^{2}\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial v}|\nabla v|^{2}-2\chi^{2}\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial u}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle

Proof.

To avoid the same calculation, here a sketch of the proof is being provided.

We are mainly concerned with the term
\partial_{t}|\nabla u|^{2}
because the other term
\chi^{2}\Delta_{f}(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})
is independent of the flow (
3.3
). We see that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(g^{ij}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{j}u)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2h(\nabla u,\nabla u)+2\langle\nabla u,\nabla u_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2h(\nabla u,\nabla u)+\langle\nabla u,\nabla(\chi^{2}\Delta_{f}u)\rangle-\langle\nabla u\nabla\lambda_{1}(u,v)\rangle

We proceed as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
with the extra terms
-2h(\nabla u,\nabla u)
and

\langle\nabla u,\nabla\lambda_{1}(u,v)\rangle=\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial u}\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle+\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial v}\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle,

on the right hand side of
(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})
. For the term
\chi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}
, just interchange the role of
u,v
and replace
\lambda_{1}
by
\lambda_{2}
, the desired result will be obtained.
∎

If the manifold is evolving along the local Ricci flow
\partial_{t}g=-2\chi^{2}Ric
then we simply substitute
h_{ij}=-\chi^{2}Ric_{ij}
in Lemma
3.1
and we get

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\chi^{4}\text{Hess }f(\nabla u,\nabla u)-2\chi^{2}|\text{Hess }u|^{2}+4\chi^{3}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\chi\rangle\Delta_{f}u

(3.7)

\displaystyle-2\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}(\chi\Delta_{f}\chi+|\nabla\chi|^{2})-8\chi^{3}\text{Hess }u(\nabla u,\nabla\chi)

\displaystyle-2\chi^{2}\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial u}|\nabla u|^{2}-2\chi^{2}\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial v}\langle\nabla v,\nabla u\rangle

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\chi^{4}\text{Hess }f(\nabla v,\nabla v)-2\chi^{2}|\text{Hess }v|^{2}+4\chi^{3}\langle\nabla v,\nabla\chi\rangle\Delta_{f}v

(3.8)

\displaystyle-2\chi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}(\chi\Delta_{f}\chi+|\nabla\chi|^{2})-8\chi^{3}\text{Hess }v(\nabla v,\nabla\chi)

\displaystyle-2\chi^{2}\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial v}|\nabla v|^{2}-2\chi^{2}\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial u}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle

It can be seen from the above two equations that the right hand side is independent of curvature restriction. Hence the resulting estimate will be independent of curvature condition and will depend only on the weight function
f
.

3.1.
Bernstein type gradient estimate for system of weighted local heat equation with linear potential term on evolving manifold

In this case we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}(u,v)=v\\
\lambda_{2}(u,v)=u\end{cases}

\displaystyle\implies\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial u}=0,\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial v}=1\\
\frac{\partial\lambda_{2}}{\partial u}=1,\frac{\partial\lambda_{2}}{\partial v}=0\end{cases}

(3.9)

This leads to our main theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
u,v\geq 0
are solutions to the system (
1.1
) on the weighted manifold
M
evolving along the local Ricci flow (
3.1
) with
|\nabla f|\leq K_{1}
,
\text{Hess}f\geq-K_{2}g
, then there exist constants

D_{1}=(\Lambda_{0}T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ u_{0}+T\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ v_{0}

and

D_{2}=(\Lambda_{0}T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ v_{0}+T\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ u_{0},

such that

\begin{cases}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\frac{D_{1}}{\chi^{2}t},\\
|\nabla v|^{2}\leq\frac{D_{2}}{\chi^{2}t},\text{both on $\Omega\times(0,T]$,}\end{cases}

(3.10)

where

\Lambda(K_{1},K_{2}\chi,m,n)=K_{2}\chi^{2}+8m|\nabla\chi|^{2}+\frac{\chi^{2}}{m-n}K_{1}+8\chi^{2}-(\chi\Delta\chi+|\nabla\chi|^{2})+\frac{1}{4}+\chi|\nabla\chi|K_{1},

and
\Lambda_{0}(K_{1},K_{2},m,n):=\underset{\Omega,\chi}{\max}\ \Lambda(K_{1},K_{2}\chi,m,n)>-(1+\frac{1}{2T}),\forall K_{1},K_{2}\geq 0
.

Proof.

In (
3.7
) we apply (
3.1
), (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and the conditions on the weight function
\text{Hess }f\geq-K_{2}g,\ |\nabla f|\leq K_{1}
, and deduce

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\Lambda_{0}+2\chi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle\implies(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(t\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\chi^{2}T|\nabla u|^{2}\Lambda_{0}+2\chi^{2}T|\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle+\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(t\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+(\Lambda_{0}T+\frac{1}{2})u^{2}+Tv^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2(\Lambda_{0}T+T+\frac{1}{2})uv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0,\text{ as $\Lambda_{0}\geq-(1+\frac{1}{2T})$}.

With the similar reasoning as mention in the earlier proofs we conclude that

t\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq(\Lambda_{0}T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}u_{0}+T\underset{\Omega}{\max}v_{0}.

Since our system (
1.1
) is symmetric in
u,v
so interchanging
u,v
we get the estimate for
v

t\chi^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}\leq(\Lambda_{0}T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}v_{0}+T\underset{\Omega}{\max}u_{0}.

The proof ends by selecting the constant
D_{1},D_{2}
as mentioned in the statement.
∎

3.2.
Bernstein type gradient estimate for system of weighted local heat equation with exponential potential term on evolving manifold

Here we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}(u,v)=ae^{v}\\
\lambda_{2}(u,v)=be^{u}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\implies\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial u}=0,\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial v}=ae^{v}\\
\frac{\partial\lambda_{2}}{\partial u}=be^{u},\frac{\partial\lambda_{2}}{\partial v}=0\end{cases}

(3.11)

This leads to our main theorem.
Here we mention the last result.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
u,v\geq 0
be a bounded solution to the system (
1.3
) on
M
with
\text{Hess }f\geq-K_{1}g
,
|\nabla f|\leq K_{2}
,
u\leq\ln b_{1},\ v\leq\ln b_{2}
, for some real constants
b_{1},b_{2}>1
,
KK_{1},K_{2}\geq 0
, then there exist constants

E_{1}=(\Gamma_{0}T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ u_{0}+Tb_{2}^{2}\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ v_{0}

and

E_{2}=(\Gamma_{0}T+\frac{1}{2})\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ v_{0}+Tb_{1}^{2}\underset{\Omega}{\max}\ u_{0},

such that

\begin{cases}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\frac{E_{1}}{\chi^{2}t},\\
|\nabla v|^{2}\leq\frac{E_{2}}{\chi^{2}t},\text{both on $\Omega\times(0,T]$,}\end{cases}

(3.12)

where

\Gamma(\chi,K_{1},K_{2},m,n,\xi)=K_{2}\chi^{2}+8m|\nabla\chi|^{2}+\frac{\chi^{2}K_{1}^{2}}{m-n}-\chi\Delta\chi+7|\nabla\chi|^{2}+\chi|\nabla\chi|K_{1}+\frac{\xi^{2}}{4}+K,

and
\Gamma_{0}(K_{1},K_{2},m,n,\xi):=\underset{\chi,\Omega}{\max}\ \Gamma(\chi,K_{1},K_{2},m,n,\xi)>-\frac{1}{2T},\xi=a,b
.

Proof.

In (
3.8
) we apply (
3.2

2.8

2.9

\text{Hess }f\geq-K_{2}g,\ |\nabla f|\leq K_{1}

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(t\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})\leq 2\chi^{2}T|\nabla u|^{2}(\Gamma_{0}T+\frac{1}{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\chi^{2}b_{2}^{2}T|\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle\implies(\partial_{t}-\chi^{2}\Delta_{f})(t\chi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+(\Gamma_{0}T+\frac{1}{2})u^{2}+b_{2}Tv^{2})

\displaystyle a(\Gamma_{0}T+\frac{1}{2})e^{v}+bb_{2}^{2}Te^{u}

4.
Conclusion and Future works

We realize that studying different gradient estimates can lead to various understanding of the solutions without solving the equations analytically. On manifolds evolving along local Ricci flow, this particular Bernstein type technique allows us to derive an estimate for local heat equation without any curvature restriction. Interested readers are now welcome to find such estimations for Keller-Segal system as mentioned in the introduction. Along with that if one can consider local Keller-Segal system and derive such estimation then it may also lead to some interesting results. Further extending these results to Finsler geometry will also give new insights to this field.

Spaces of homomorphisms, formality and Hochschild homology
Simon Gritschacher

Abstract
Let G G be a discrete group. The topological category of finite dimensional unitary representations of G G is symmetric monoidal under direct sum and has an associated 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} -space 𝒦 def ​ ( G ) \mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G) . We show that if G G and A A are finitely generated groups and A A is abelian, then 𝒦 def ​ ( G × A ) ≃ 𝒦 def ​ ( G ) ⊗ A ^ \mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A)\simeq\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)\otimes\widehat{A} as 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} -spaces, where A ^ \widehat{A} is the Pontryagin dual of A A . We deduce a homology stability result for the homomorphism varieties Hom ⁡ ( G × ℤ r , U ⁡ ( n ) ) \Hom(G\times\mathbb{Z}^{r},U(n)) using the local-to-global principle for homology stability of Kupers–Miller. For a finitely generated free group F F and a field k k of characteristic zero, we show that the singular k k -chains in 𝒦 def ​ ( F ) \mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(F) are formal as an 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} - k k -algebra. Using this we describe the equivariant homology of Hom ⁡ ( F × A , U ⁡ ( n ) ) \Hom(F\times A,U(n)) for every n n in terms of higher Hochschild homology of an explicitly determined commutative k k -algebra. As an example we show that Hom ⁡ ( F × ℤ r , U ⁡ ( 2 ) ) \Hom(F\times\mathbb{Z}^{r},U(2)) is U ⁡ ( 2 ) U(2) -equivariantly formal for every r r and we compute the Poincaré polynomial.



1
Introduction

Let
G
be a finitely generated discrete group and
U(m)
the Lie group of
m\times m
unitary matrices. We can consider the representation variety
\Hom(G,U(n))
and the compact character variety
\Hom(G,U(n))/U(n)
, both of which are interesting moduli spaces from the viewpoint of differential geometry
[
6
,
8
,
15
]
. In this paper we use methods from homotopy theory to study the homology of these varieties (in their natural Hausdorff topology) when
G
is the direct product of two groups one of which is abelian.

The variety
\Hom(G,U(n))
can depend in a complicated way on the group
G
. For example, if
N\to G\to G/N
is a group extension, the representation theory of
G
can be much more complicated than that of
N
and
G/N
, and there is in general not an easy way of relating
\Hom(N,U(n))
,
\Hom(G/N,U(n))
and
\Hom(G,U(n))
. This can make it difficult to study the homology of
\Hom(G,U(n))
in a systematic manner.

For an abelian group
A
, however, the complex representation theory is so simple that a fully homotopy theoretic description of
\Hom(A,U(n))
can be achieved. All irreducible representations are
1
-dimensional, and every homomorphism
A\to U(n)
is determined by an orthogonal decomposition of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a collection of
1
-dimensional characters of
A
. This information can be organised best by looking at
\Hom(A,U(n))
not in isolation, but by considering all finite dimensional representations of
A
at once and the isomorphisms between them.

For any group
G
, the category
\Rep_{U}(G)
of finite dimensional unitary representations is naturally a topological category, and taking direct sums of representations equips it with a symmetric monoidal structure. Its topological nerve
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G):=\mathrm{N}(\Rep_{U}(G))
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space, whose group-completion is Carlsson’s unitary deformation
K
-theory of
G

[
7
,
21
,
32
]
. For a trivial group
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(1)
is the familiar
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space
\bigsqcup_{m\geq 0}BU(m)
.

Our starting point in this paper is the observation (Proposition
3.6
) that if
G
is a group and
A
is an abelian group, both finitely generated, then twisting representations of
G
by characters of
A
induces an equivalence of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-spaces

\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)\otimes\widehat{A}\simeq\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A)\,,

(1.1)

where
\widehat{A}=\Hom(A,U(1))
is the character group of
A
. On the left hand side we use the tensoring of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-spaces over spaces; equivalently, it can be read as the factorization homology
\int_{\widehat{A}}\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
. After group completion, (
1.1
) may be viewed as a consequence of Lawson’s product formula
[
21
]
, but the crucial point here is that no passage to group completions is needed. The homology of
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A)
still encodes in a convenient way the equivariant homology of
\Hom(G\times A,U(n))
for all
n
at once, as

H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A);k)\cong\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}H^{U(n)}_{\ast}(\Hom(G\times A,U(n));k)\,.

In this way, (
1.1
) gives an effective method of studying the homology of
\Hom(G\times A,U(n))
in relation to the homology of
\Hom(G,U(n))
. In particular, this can be applied to free abelian groups, i.e.,
(G,A)=(1,\mathbb{Z}^{r})
(see e.g.
[
1
,
3
,
18
,
39
]
for related work), or more generally to direct products of free and free abelian groups, i.e.,
(G,A)=(F_{s},\mathbb{Z}^{r})
. The latter is an interesting class of right-angled Artin groups, which received attention recently in
[
13
]
. This class contains in particular the fundamental groups of the configuration spaces
\mathrm{Conf}_{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
(the pure braid group
P_{3}\cong F_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}
) and
\mathrm{Conf}_{2}((S^{1})^{2})
(the group
F_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}
).

We will now summarise our main results.

The space
\Hom(G,U(n))
may or may not be path-connected depending on
G
and
n
; the subscript
\mathds{1}
means that we picked the path-component of the trivial homomorphism. In Section
4
we prove the following homology stability result with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If the sequence (
1.2
), or (
1.3
), satisfies homology stability for a finitely generated group
G
, then it satisfies homology stability for
G\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}
for every
r\geq 0
.

Since
\Hom(\mathbb{Z}^{r},U(n))
is path-connected for all
r,n
, choosing

to be the trivial group the following corollary is obtained, generalising results of
[

,
36
]
.

Corollary 1.3
.

For every

, the sequence of spaces
\Hom(\mathbb{Z}^{r},U(n))
,

satisfies homology stability with integer coefficients.

With rational coefficients homology stability of
\Hom(\mathbb{Z}^{r},U(n))
was first proved by Ramras and Stafa
[
36
]
using methods from representation homology stability. A different proof, based on finding minimal generating sets for cohomology, was given by Kishimoto and Takeda
[
18
]
. While the latter give optimal stability bounds in the rational setting, both proofs rely fundamentally on a well known, explicit description of the rational cohomology ring of

) and a general homology stability principle for factorisation homology due to Kupers and Miller
[

]
.

The compact character varieties
\Rep(\mathbb{Z}^{k},U(n))

\mathrm{SP}^{n}((S^{1})^{k})

(see e.g.
[
20
, Example 1.4]
)

Spectral sequences and Hochschild homology

Many computational results on representation varieties are over fields of characteristic zero, justified also by the interest in their algebro-geometric features (as in
[
14
]
, for example), but their torsion is known to have a rich and complicated structure (see
[
19
]
for the case of free abelian group representations). A formal consequence of (
1.1
) are certain spectral sequences which provide a computational tool over more general rings. In Section
5
we prove:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
k
be a commutative ring. If
H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G);k)
, or
H_{\ast}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G);k)
, is
k
-flat, then there are, respectively, first quadrant spectral sequences for every
n\geq 0

\displaystyle E^{2}

\displaystyle=\HH^{\widehat{A}}_{\ast}(H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G);k))_{n}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow H_{\ast}^{U(n)}(\Hom(G\times A,U(n));k)

(1.4)

\displaystyle E^{2}

\displaystyle=\HH^{\widehat{A}}_{\ast}(H_{\ast}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G);k))_{n}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow H_{\ast}(\Hom(G\times A,U(n))/U(n);k)

(1.5)

In the theorem,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)=\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0}\Hom(G,U(n))/U(n)
is the topological abelian monoid of conjugacy classes of representations, and
H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G);k)
and
H_{\ast}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G);k)
are viewed as graded commutative
k
-algebras. The
E^{2}
-page is the higher Hochschild homology of these algebras, in the sense of Loday and Pirashvili
[
31
]
. We will apply this spectral sequence to compute the Poincaré series of
\Hom(F_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r},U(2))/U(2)
for any
r
at the end of Section
7
. An application of (
1.4
) to the integral cohomology of spaces of commuting elements in
U(n)
will be given in a forthcoming paper.

Free groups and formality

In the second part of the paper we will focus on the case where
G=F_{s}
is a finitely generated free group. A key observation will be the following formality result which we prove in Section
6
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero. Then the singular
k
-chains in
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(F_{s})
are formal as an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-
k
-algebra.

The
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(F_{s})
is just the mapping space
\operatorname{Map}(\vee^{s}S^{1},\bigsqcup_{m\geq 0}BU(m))
, but formality is not so obvious. We have no reason to expect that
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
is formal in this sense for a great many other groups
G
. As a corollary of Proposition
1.5
we obtain an entirely algebraic description of the equivariant homology of
\Hom(F_{s}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r},U(n))
for all
r,s,n\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero. Let
A
be a finitely generated abelian group, and let
F_{s}
be a free group of finite rank
s
. Then there is an isomorphism of graded commutative
k
-algebras

H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(F_{s}\times A);k)\cong\HH^{\widehat{A}}_{\ast}(H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(F_{s});k))\,.

A description of the algebra
H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(F_{s});k)
as a quotient of a symmetric algebra is given in Theorem
6.11
. Through a suitable grading on both the homology of
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(F_{s}\times A)
and the Hochschild homology one can extract from this theorem the equivariant homology of
\Hom(F_{s}\times A,U(n))
for each individual
n
. For
n=2
and
A=\mathbb{Z}^{r}
the computations will be carried out in great detail in Section
7
. The main result is the computation of the Poincaré series of
\Hom(F_{s}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r},U(2))
in Theorem
7.1
.

We should point out that we have no analogue of Proposition
1.5
or Theorem
1.6
for character varieties, i.e., for
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(F_{s})
instead of
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(F_{s})
. The homology of the free group character varieties
\Hom(F_{s},U(n))/U(n)
is largely unknown.

2
Recollection of
\Gamma
-spaces and
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-spaces

2.1
\Gamma
-spaces

For an integer
k\geq 0
let
\langle k\rangle
denote the finite based set
\{\ast,1,\dots,k\}
with basepoint
\ast
. Let
\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}
denote the category whose objects are the sets
\langle k\rangle
for
k\geq 0
and whose morphisms are based maps
\langle k\rangle\to\langle l\rangle
. Let
\mathrm{Top}
denote the category of compactly generated spaces, as defined for example in
[
37
, Appendix A]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
\Gamma
-space
is a functor
A\colon\thinspace\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}\to\mathrm{Top}
such that
A(\langle 0\rangle)
is a point.

A
\Gamma
-space can be prolonged to a continuous functor on the category of based spaces
\mathrm{Top}_{\ast}
, and we denote the prolonged functor by
A
as well. Concretely,
A
can be defined on a pointed space
X\in\mathrm{Top}_{\ast}
by the coend
\int^{\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}}A\times\textnormal{Map}_{\ast}(-,X)
, that is, by the quotient of

\bigsqcup_{\langle k\rangle\in\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}}A(\langle k\rangle)\times\textnormal{Map}_{\ast}(\langle k\rangle,X)

by the equivalence relation generated by

(A(\alpha)(x),f)\sim(x,f\alpha)

(2.1)

for all
x\in A(\langle k\rangle)
,
f\in\textnormal{Map}_{\ast}(\langle l\rangle,X)
and
\alpha\colon\thinspace\langle k\rangle\to\langle l\rangle
in
\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}
.

We may also evaluate
A
on a pointed simplicial set
X\in\mathrm{sSet}_{\ast}
. We do this by evaluating
A
levelwise on
X
, viewing based sets as based discrete topological spaces. This yields a simplicial space
A(X)
. For geometric realisation there is then a homeomorphism

A(|X|)\cong|A(X)|\,,

(2.2)

see, for example,
[
37
, Proposition B.29]
.

For
\langle k\rangle\in\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}
and
i=1,\dots,k
, let
\rho^{i}\colon\thinspace\langle k\rangle\to\langle 1\rangle
be the map defined by
\rho^{i}(j)=1
if
i=j
and
\rho^{i}(j)=\ast
otherwise.

Definition 2.2
.

A
\Gamma
-space
A
is called
special
, if for all
\langle k\rangle\in\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}
the maps
\rho^{1},\dots,\rho^{k}
induce a weak equivalence
A(\langle k\rangle)\xrightarrow{\sim}A(\langle 1\rangle)^{\times k}
.

Let
\nabla\colon\thinspace\langle 2\rangle\cong\langle 1\rangle\vee\langle 1\rangle\to\langle 1\rangle
be the fold map. If
A
is special, the zig-zag

\textstyle{A(\langle 1\rangle)\times A(\langle 1\rangle)\,}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces A(\langle 2\rangle)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(A(\rho^{1}),A(\rho^{2}))}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\scriptstyle{A(\nabla)}
\textstyle{A(\langle 1\rangle)}

exhibits
A(\langle 1\rangle)
as a commutative monoid in the homotopy category. Special
\Gamma
-spaces model
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-spaces and we think of
A(\langle 1\rangle)
as the underlying space of the
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space.

For
\langle k\rangle\in\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}
we denote by
\langle k\rangle^{\circ}
the set
\langle k\rangle
minus the basepoint. For a set
S
we denote by
S_{+}
the set
S
with an extra basepoint added.

Definition 2.3
.

A
\Gamma
-space is called
cofibrant
if for every
\langle n\rangle\in\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}
the latching map

\textnormal{colim}_{S\subsetneq\langle n\rangle^{\circ}}A(S_{+})\to A(\langle n\rangle)

is a
\Sigma_{n}
-cofibration for the evident action of the symmetric group
\Sigma_{n}
on the source and target, see
[
37
, Definition B.33]
.

The
\Gamma
-spaces considered in this article will have this property.

2.2
Tensoring a
\Gamma
-space with a space

We now recall how a
\Gamma
-space can be tensored with a space. We will do this first for
\Gamma
-objects in simplicial sets, that is, functors
A\colon\thinspace\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}\to\mathrm{sSet}
with
A(\langle 0\rangle)=\ast
. We will return to
\Gamma

A functor
A\colon\thinspace\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}\to\mathrm{sSet}
can be prolonged to the category of pointed simplicial sets via homotopy left Kan extension along the inclusion
\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}\subseteq\mathrm{sSet}_{\ast}
. More precisely, we evaluate
A
on any based set, not just finite ones, by a filtered colimit. For

we may then define
A(X)
by evaluating
A
levelwise on
X
and taking the diagonal of the resulting bisimplicial set.

Construction 2.4
.

For
X\in\mathrm{sSet}_{\ast}
and a
\Gamma

A\colon\thinspace\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}\to\mathrm{sSet}

A_{X}\colon\thinspace\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}\to\mathrm{sSet}
by

A_{X}(\langle k\rangle)=A(\langle k\rangle\wedge X)\,.

If
A
is special (in the sense of Definition
2.2
), then so is
A_{X}
for any based simplicial set. One can show this first for finite
X
, and then for general

using the fact that filtered colimits commute with finite products in simplicial sets.

Let
A\colon\thinspace\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}\to\mathrm{sSet}
be a special
\Gamma
-object,
X
a simplicial set and
X_{+}
the based simplicial set obtained by adjunction of a disjoint basepoint. We will now explain, in a somewhat ad hoc manner, that the assignment
(A,X)\to A_{X_{+}}
of Construction
2.4

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-spaces. To make this precise it will be convenient to use
\infty

Our reference for
\infty
-categories will be
[
25
]
; we let
\mathrm{Spc}
and
\mathrm{Spc}_{\ast}
denote the

Lemma 2.5
.

The functor

\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast},\mathrm{sSet})\times\mathrm{sSet}_{\ast}\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast},\mathrm{sSet}),\quad(A,X)\mapsto A_{X}

refines to a functor of

T\colon\thinspace\Fun(\mathrm{N}\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast},\mathrm{Spc})\times\mathrm{Spc}_{\ast}\to\Fun(\mathrm{N}\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast},\mathrm{Spc})\,.

Proof.

We view

is equipped with the projective model structure; this model structure is again simplicial, see

is the functor

\Fun(\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast},\mathrm{sSet})\times\mathrm{sSet}_{\ast}\times\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}\to\mathrm{sSet}

which is given by prolongation
\Fun(\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast},\mathrm{sSet})\to\Fun(\mathrm{sSet}_{\ast},\mathrm{sSet})

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

\mathrm{CMon}(\mathcal{S})\times\mathrm{Spc}\xrightarrow{\textnormal{incl}\times(-)_{+}}\Fun(\mathrm{N}\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast},\mathrm{Spc})\times\mathrm{Spc}_{\ast}\xrightarrow{T}\Fun(\mathrm{N}\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast},\mathrm{Spc})\,.

(ii)

Proof.

Item (i) is a consequence of the fact that if

for any

To prove (ii) it is enough to show, by the universal property of

•

is equivalent to the identity when restricted to the point
\ast\in\mathrm{Spc}

•

preserves colimits in the second argument.

The first item is clear, because

. To prove the second item it is enough to check this separately for finite coproducts and for sifted colimits.

Let

and let

(also denoted by

We claim that this map is an equivalence, thus exhibiting

as a coproduct of

and

. This can be checked on underlying spaces, that is, after evaluation at

The second map, which is induced by the evident maps

is special. The composite map is an equivalence, witnessing the fact that the forgetful functor

and the Cartesian product on

To check preservation of sifted colimits in

are reflected by the forgetful functor

Through the correspondence of homotopy colimits in a simplicial model category with colimits in the underlying

∎

Lemma 2.11

If

The
\Gamma
-space for deformation

-theory

Let
G
be a discrete group. The mapping space

is given the compact open topology, equivalently the product topology by writing it as
U(n)^{G}
. We give

\Hom(G,U(n))\subseteq\operatorname{Map}(G,U(n))

the subspace topology. It makes
\Hom(G,U(n))
into a compact Hausdorff space. The group
U(n)
operates continuously on
\Hom(G,U(n))
by conjugation.

We now recall the
\Gamma
-space for unitary deformation
K
-theory of
G
due to Lawson
[
21
]
; it packages the homomorphism spaces
\Hom(G,U(n))
for various
n
into a single homotopical object. Let
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
0\leq n\leq\infty
be equipped with the standard Hermitian inner product. For
m<\infty
let
V(n,\mathbb{C}^{m})
denote the compact Stiefel manifold of
n
-dimensional orthonormal frames in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. It carries a free action of
U(n)
whose quotient space is the Grassmannian
Gr(n,\mathbb{C}^{m})
. If
m=\infty
, then
V(n,\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
shall be viewed as the colimit in
\mathrm{Top}

V(n,\mathbb{C}^{\infty})=\textnormal{colim}_{m}V(n,\mathbb{C}^{m})\,.

Consider now the fibre bundle

\Hom(G,U(n))\to V(n,\mathbb{C}^{m})\times_{U(n)}\Hom(G,U(n))\to Gr(n,\mathbb{C}^{m})\,.

An element of the total space is precisely a pair
(V,f)
where
V\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{m}
is an
n
-dimensional linear subspace and
f\colon\thinspace G\to U(V)
is a unitary representation of
G
on it. If
m=\infty
, then the topology on the total space is again the colimit topology as the spaces
\Hom(G,U(n))
are compact and Hausdorff.

Construction 3.1
.

The
\Gamma

is defined on

by

and on

by the subspace

\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)_{\leq m}(\langle k\rangle)\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)(\langle 1\rangle)_{\leq m}^{k}

consisting of those

((V_{1},f_{1}),\dots,(V_{k},f_{k}))
for which
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
are orthogonal to each other if

. If
\alpha\colon\thinspace\langle k\rangle\to\langle l\rangle
is a morphism in
\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}
, then

is defined by

\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)_{\leq m}(\alpha)((V_{1},f_{1}),\dots,(V_{k},f_{k}))=((W_{1},g_{1}),\dots,(W_{l},g_{l}))

with
W_{j}=\bigoplus_{i\in\alpha^{-1}(j)}V_{i}
and

and where the direct sum of representations has the obvious meaning. (If

is empty, then

and

is the trivial homomorphism.)

When

we denote the
\Gamma
-space so defined by
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)

\Gamma
-space is special; this can be seen by noting that the inclusion of the trivial group

induces a pullback square

\textstyle{\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)(\langle k\rangle)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)(\langle 1\rangle)^{k}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(1)(\langle k\rangle)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(1)(\langle 1\rangle)^{k}}

in which the right map is a fibration. The bottom map is a bijection on components, and on each component it is an inclusion of the form

X(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})\hookrightarrow Gr(i_{1},\mathbb{C}^{\infty})\times\cdots\times Gr(i_{k},\mathbb{C}^{\infty})

for some
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\geq 0
, where

is the subspace of the product carved out by the condition of pairwise orthogonality. But this inclusion is a homotopy equivalence, in particular a weak equivalence, and hence the top map in the pullback square is also a weak equivalence.

Lemma 3.2
.

\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
is cofibrant if

is finitely generated.

Proof.

This can be proved in the same way as
[

which, considered non-equivariantly, is the special case
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(1)
. For

∎

There is an analogue for the conjugation quotient

The direct sum of representations gives

. (If

Construction 3.3
.

We let

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)\colon\thinspace\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}\to\mathrm{Top}

The latching maps

from the equivariant triangulation theorem applied to

. In particular, we have

Lemma 3.4
.

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
is cofibrant if

is finitely generated.

Now both
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)_{\leq m}

Lemma 3.5
.

For

Similarly, let

So defined, both

are

Proof.

This is clear.
∎

By composing with the singular complex and applying the nerve
\mathrm{N}(-)

\mathbb{N}

Let
\widehat{A}=\Hom(A,S^{1})
denote the Pontryagin dual of
A
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G,A
be finitely generated discrete groups and
A
abelian. Then twisting
G
-representations by characters of
A
induces equivalences of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebras in
\mathrm{Spc}^{\mathbb{N}}

(1)

\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A)\simeq\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)\otimes\widehat{A}

(2)

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A)\simeq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)\otimes\widehat{A}

and these equivalences are natural in
G
and
A
.

Proof.

We shall only prove (1) as the proof of (2) is analogous.

We will prove an isomorphism of
\Gamma
-spaces

\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)_{\widehat{A}_{+}}\cong\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A)

and subsequently observe that this isomorphism respects the graded structure. Thus, it induces an isomorphism of
\mathbb{N}^{\otimes}
-spaces, and this implies the claim by Lemma
2.11
and Corollary
2.16
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{\infty}
be a finite dimensional linear subspace. A homomorphism
f\colon\thinspace G\times A\to U(V)
corresponds to a pair
(f^{\prime},\phi)
where
f^{\prime}\colon\thinspace G\to U(V)
and
\phi\colon\thinspace A\to U(V)
are homomorphisms such that
f^{\prime}(g)\phi(a)=\phi(a)f^{\prime}(g)
for all
(g,a)\in G\times A
. Since
A
is abelian,
\phi
splits
V
into an orthogonal sum
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
such that the action of
\phi
on each summand is determined by a character
\phi_{i}\in\Hom(A,U(1))=\widehat{A}
, all
V_{i}
are non-zero and the
\phi_{i}
are mutually distinct. By commutativity of
f^{\prime}
and
\phi
,
f^{\prime}
restricts to representations
f_{i}^{\prime}\colon\thinspace G\to U(V_{i})
. This gives a bijective correspondence

(V,f)\mapsto\{(V_{i},f_{i}^{\prime},\phi_{i})\}_{i=1}^{k}\,.

(3.1)

Fix
\langle l\rangle\in\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}
and
m\geq 0
and define a homeomorphism

g_{m,\langle l\rangle}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)_{\leq m}(\langle l\rangle\wedge\widehat{A}_{+})\to\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A)_{\leq m}(\langle l\rangle)\,.

as follows. Recall that the left hand side is a quotient space of the coproduct

\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)_{\leq m}(\langle k\rangle)\times[(\langle l\rangle^{\circ}\times\widehat{A})_{+}]^{k}\,.

Under the equivalence relation (
2.1
) every element in the coproduct is equivalent to one of the form

((V_{i},f^{\prime}_{i})_{i=1}^{k},(n_{i},\phi_{i})_{i=1}^{k}),

that is, to one where the extra basepoint of
(\langle l\rangle^{\circ}\times\widehat{A})_{+}
does not appear. To such an element we associate

(W_{j},f_{j})_{j=1}^{l}\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A)_{\leq m}(\langle l\rangle)

with
(W_{j},f_{j})
defined by

(W_{j},f_{j})\mapsto\{(V_{i},f_{i}^{\prime},\phi_{i})\mid i:n_{i}=j\}

under the correspondence (
3.1
). This defines the continuous bijection
g_{m,\langle l\rangle}
; as the domain of
g_{m,\langle l\rangle}
is compact and the codomain is Hausdorff,

is in fact a homeomorphism.

For
(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})\in\mathbb{N}^{l}
, the element
((V_{i},f^{\prime}_{i})_{i=1}^{k},(n_{i},\phi_{i})_{i=1}^{k})
defines an element of the subspace
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)_{\widehat{A}_{+}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})
precisely if
\sum_{i:n_{i}=j}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{i})=x_{j}
for all

. It is therefore mapped to a tuple
(W_{j},f_{j})_{j=1}^{l}
with

, which shows that the isomorphism is in fact one of
\mathbb{N}^{\otimes}
-spaces.

Taking the colimit over
m
, and noting that
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)(\langle l\rangle\wedge\widehat{A}_{+})
carries the colimit topology as
\langle l\rangle\wedge\widehat{A}_{+}
is compact Hausdorff, we hence obtain a homeomorphism

g_{\langle l\rangle}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)(\langle l\rangle\wedge\widehat{A}_{+})\to\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A)(\langle l\rangle)\,.

By inspection, this map is natural in
\langle l\rangle\in\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}
and thus defines an isomorphism of
\mathbb{N}^{\otimes}
-spaces. Evidently, this isomorphism is natural in
G
and
A
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The equivalence
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A)\simeq\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)\otimes\widehat{A}

]
applied to

where

is the connective spectrum corresponding to

.

Variant 3.9

There are sub-

4
Homology stability

4.1
Application of the local-to-global principle

For every
n\geq 1
, the natural embedding
U(n)\subseteq U(n+1)
induces a map

\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(n))\to\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(n+1))\,.

The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

The sequence
\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(1))\to\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(2))\to\cdots
satisfies homology stability for a finitely generated group
G
if and only if it satisfies homology stability for
G\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}
for every
r\geq 0
.

Theorem
4.1
holds for arbitrary constant coefficients, for example
\mathbb{Z}
. For the analogous theorem concerning the representation varieties
\Rep(G,U(n))
see Theorem
4.7
below.

The following is useful terminology borrowed from
[
20
]
.

Definition 4.2
.

A
charged
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebra in
\mathrm{Spc}^{\mathbb{N}}
. We say that a charged
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space
A
is
connected
if its underlying space has
\pi_{0}(A)\cong\mathbb{N}
.

Suppose that
A
is a connected charged
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space. Then the underlying space is
A\cong\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
with each
A_{n}
connected. Pick any element
a_{1}\in A_{1}
; then multiplication by
a_{1}
using the
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-structure gives a sequence of maps

A_{0}\xrightarrow{\cdot a_{1}}A_{1}\xrightarrow{\cdot a_{1}}A_{2}\xrightarrow{\cdot a_{1}}\cdots

(4.1)

Since
A_{1}
is connected, the maps are independent, up to homotopy, of the specific choice of
a_{1}
.

Definition 4.3
.

The charged
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space
A
is said to satisfy
homology stability
if the sequence (
4.1
) satisfies homology stability.

Let
A
be a connected charged
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space and let
X\in\mathrm{Spc}
be a connected space. Then the charged
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space
A\otimes X
is again connected; one way to see this informally is to note first that the underlying space of
A\otimes X
is
\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{X_{+}}(n)
, where
A
is viewed as an
\mathbb{N}^{\otimes}
-space. By the coend description of
A_{X_{+}}(n)
in Section
2.3
, a point in
A_{X_{+}}(n)
is the equivalence class of a configuration of
k
points in
X
and a label in
A(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})
for some
(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
with
\sum_{i}y_{i}=n
. Since
X
is connected, the
k
points can move continuously into any chosen point of
X
, showing that
\pi_{0}(A_{X_{+}}(n))\cong\pi_{0}(A(n))
. Since
A
is connected, so is
A(n)
for every
n
, and thus
\pi_{0}(A\otimes X)\cong\mathbb{N}
.

The local-to-global principle for homology stability of Kupers and Miller
[
20
]
gives a relationship between homology stability of
A
and homology stability of
A\otimes X
. We state the following corollary of their result.

Theorem 4.4

(
[
20
]
)
.

If a connected charged
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space
A
satisfies homology stability, then so does
A\otimes M
for any smooth connected orientable manifold
M
.

Remark 4.5
.

To arrive at Theorem
4.4
note the following:

(1)

The results of
[
20
]
are formulated in terms of factorisation homology (or topological chiral homology) of framed
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-algebras over oriented
n
-manifolds
M
. But every
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space
A
may be viewed as a framed
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-algebra for any
n
by neglect of structure. In this situation, by
[
4
, Proposition 5.1]
there is a canonical equivalence of spaces

\int_{M}A\simeq A\otimes M\,,

where on the left is factorisation homology and on the right is tensoring.

(2)

The main result of
[
20
]
requires the manifold
M
to be non-compact, in order to be able to construct the stabilisation maps exhibiting homology stability of
\int_{M}A
. But if
A
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebra, then by the previous equivalence
\int_{M}A
is homotopy invariant in
M
, and hence any given manifold can be replaced by a non-compact one simply by taking Cartesian product with
\mathbb{R}
.

We now apply Theorem
4.4
to the charged
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G)
. The underlying space of
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G)
is
\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0}\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(n))_{hU(n)}
. Because we have singled out the connected component of the trivial homomorphism, we see that
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G))\cong\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G)
is a connected charged
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space.

Lemma 4.6
.

The charged
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G)
satisfies homology stability if and only if the sequence
\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(1))\to\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(2))\to\cdots
satisfies homology stability.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G)
satisfies homology stability if so does the sequence

\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(1))_{hU(1)}\xrightarrow{\oplus\mathds{1}}\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(2))_{hU(2)}\xrightarrow{\oplus\mathds{1}}\cdots

where
\oplus\mathds{1}
means direct sum with a trivial representation. These spaces fit into a sequence of homotopy fibre sequences

\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(n))\to\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(n))_{hU(n)}\to BU(n)\,;

because the sequence
BU(1)\to BU(2)\to\cdots
satisfies homology stability, and
BU(n)
is simply-connected for all
n
, the Serre spectral sequence and Zeeman’s comparison theorem imply the result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By Lemma
4.6
, it suffices to see that homology stability of
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G)
implies homology stability of
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G\times\mathbb{Z}^{r})
. By Variant
3.9
of Proposition
3.6
there is an equivalence of charged
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-spaces

\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G\times\mathbb{Z}^{r})\simeq\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G)\otimes(S^{1})^{r}\,.

The claim follows now from Theorem
4.4
.
∎

The proof of the following variant is almost identical except for replacing
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G)
by
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathds{1}}(G)
throughout and omitting the argument with the Serre spectral sequence.

Theorem 4.7
.

The sequence
\Rep_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(1))\to\Rep_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(2))\to\cdots
satisfies homology stability for a finitely generated group
G
if and only if it satisfies homology stability for
G\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}
for every
r\geq 0
.

Remark 4.8
.

If
A
is a non-trivial
finite
abelian group, then
\Hom(G\times A,U(n))
fails to satisfy homology stability already on
H_{0}
. The isomorphism class of a representation
f\colon\thinspace A\to U(n)
is determined by an unordered tuple
\chi=\{\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n}\}
of
n

1
-dimensional characters of
A
; there is a decomposition

\Hom(A,U(n))\cong\bigsqcup_{\chi\in\widehat{A}^{n}/\Sigma_{n}}U(n)/U(n)_{\chi}

where
U(n)_{\chi}
is the stabiliser of the representation
\chi_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\chi_{n}
under the conjugation action, see
[
3
, Proposition 2.5]
. Consequently,

|\pi_{0}\Hom(G\times A,U(n))|\geq|\pi_{0}\Hom(A,U(n))|=\sum_{k=1}^{|A|}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\binom{|A|}{k}

which diverges as
n
tends to infinity. (The inequality holds, because one space is a retract of the other.) If we restrict to only the path-component of the trivial representation, then the decomposition above implies

\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G\times A,U(n))\cong\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(n))\,,

induced by restriction to
G
. So, for the question of homology stability direct products with finite abelian groups do not really play a role.

4.2
An example

Let
\Sigma
be a closed orientable surface of genus
g>0
. In his thesis
[
32
, Section 4.5]
Ramras uses gauge theoretic methods to show that the maps

\Hom(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),U(n))\to\Hom(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),U(n+1))

are precisely
(2n-1)
-connected. He also showed the equivalence

\Hom(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),U)\simeq\Omega^{\infty}(ku\wedge B\pi_{1}(\Sigma))\,,

(4.2)

where
\Hom(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),U)=\operatorname{colim}_{n}\,\Hom(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),U(n))
,
ku
is the connective complex
K
-theory spectrum and
\Omega^{\infty}
takes the underlying space of a spectrum.

Combining this with Theorem
4.1
we obtain:

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the smallest class of groups which contains the fundamental groups of closed orientable surfaces and which is closed under binary free products and direct product with
\mathbb{Z}
. Then, for every
G\in\mathcal{C}
the sequence

\Hom(G,U(1))\to\Hom(G,U(2))\to\cdots

satisfies homology stability with integer coefficients. Moreover, the stable homology is known in view of the equivalence

\Hom(G,U)\simeq\Omega^{\infty}(ku\wedge BG)\,.

Proof.

In view of the introductory remarks, it suffices to see that the property of homology stability is preserved under binary free products and direct product with
\mathbb{Z}
. The latter follows from Theorem
4.1
, while the former follows from the Künneth exact sequence and the evident homeomorphism

\Hom(\Gamma_{1}\ast\Gamma_{2},U(n))\cong\Hom(\Gamma_{1},U(n))\times\Hom(\Gamma_{2},U(n))

(4.3)

for any two discrete groups
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
. Also note that
\Hom(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),U(n))
is path-connected by
[
33
, Corollary 4.11]
, and hence so is
\Hom(G,U(n))
(see Remark
3.8
); therefore, no subscript
\mathds{1}
is needed.

Because of (
4.3
) and the equivalence

\Omega^{\infty}(ku\wedge B(\Gamma_{1}\ast\Gamma_{2}))\simeq\Omega^{\infty}(ku\wedge B\Gamma_{1})\times\Omega^{\infty}(ku\wedge B\Gamma_{2})\,,

it suffices to prove the second statement when
G=\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Since
\Hom(G,U(n))
is path-connected for all
n
,
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
is stably group-like (in the sense of
[
33
, Section 2.3]
) with respect to the trivial representation; it follows then from
[
33
, Theorem 2.3]
that
\Hom(G,U)
is the homotopy fibre over the basepoint of the canonical map
\Omega B\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)\to\Omega B\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(1)
. By Remark
3.7
, or Lawson’s product formula
[
21
]
, and (
4.2
)

\Omega B\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)\simeq\Omega^{\infty}(ku\wedge BG_{+})\,,

and the result follows.
∎

Because
BG
for
G\in\mathcal{C}
is stably a wedge of spheres,
\Hom(G,U)
is a finite product of various higher connected covers of
BU
and
U
. The homology of these connected covers was computed by Singer
[
38
]
and Stong
[
40
]
.

Ramras
[
32
]
also showed that if
\mathcal{N}
is a closed aspherical non-orientable surface, then

\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{N}),U(n))\to\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{N}),U(n+1))

is at least
(n-1)
-connected. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be the class of groups obtained from
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{N})
by taking binary free products and direct products with
\mathbb{Z}
. With the same proof as before one obtains homology stability for
\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U(n))
as
n\to\infty
for any
G\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

By
[
33
, Corollary 4.12]
the space
\Hom(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{N}),U(n))
has two path components when
n\geq 1
. Using
[
34
, Theorem 6.1]
, for
G\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
the space
\Hom_{\mathds{1}}(G,U)
has an explicit, yet slightly more cumbersome description in terms of higher connected covers of
BU
,
U
and

Remark 4.10
.

The theorem of Kupers–Miller, Theorem

). However, the ranges we’d obtain this way are in general not very close to the optimal range. For example, the map

5
Hochschild homology and a spectral sequence

Let
G,A
be finitely generated groups, with
A
abelian. In this section we derive from Proposition

a spectral sequence which gives a method of computing the homology of
\Rep(G\times A,U(n))
from knowledge of the homology of
\Rep(G,U(k))
for

along with the multiplicative structure on homology induced by the maps

\Rep(G,U(k_{1}))\times\Rep(G,U(k_{2}))\xrightarrow{\oplus}\Rep(G,U(k_{1}+k_{2}))\,.

There is a similar spectral sequence for the equivariant homology of
\Hom(G\times A,U(n))
.

Our reference for the following notions from higher algebra is
[

]
.

Notation 5.1
.

Let

(1)

(2)

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}

(3)

Since

-category of spaces

and we denote by

and

is determined by the demanding that

preserves colimits and that

(4)

. We then have a functor

In case that

is a discrete commutative ring, the homotopy groups of

are the singular homology groups of

with coefficients in

.

Remark 5.2
.

If
k
is a discrete commutative ring, then
\mathcal{M}\mathrm{od}_{k}
is equivalent as a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category to the derived
\infty
-category of
k
-modules

Definition 5.3
.

Let

and

. The

.

Remark 5.4
.

Let

be discrete and

a dgca over

. Let

be a pointed simplicial set. Then there is a hands-on dgca model for

; we write

.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let

,
A
be finitely generated discrete groups and
A
abelian. Let

be a discrete commutative ring such that

is flat as a

The analogous statement holds with
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
replaced by
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)

Proof.

Pick a finite simplicial model for
\widehat{A}
and identify
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)\otimes\widehat{A}
with the value of the
\Gamma
-space
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
on

. This is a Reedy cofibrant simplicial space, because
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
is cofibrant (see Lemma

), and

|\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)((\widehat{A}_{\bullet})_{+})|\simeq\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A)

by Proposition

. There is a spectral sequence converging to the homology of the geometric realisation of a Reedy cofibrant simplicial space, see

. In our case it takes the form

E^{2}=H_{\ast}(H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)((\widehat{A}_{\bullet})_{+});k))\Longrightarrow H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G\times A);k)\,,

where

is the chain complex of the simplicial

-module

Since

is flat over

, and using the fact that

is special, the Künneth theorem implies that

H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)((\widehat{A}_{n})_{+});k)\cong\bigotimes_{\widehat{A}_{n}}H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G);k)

for every

. This identifies the chain complex

with the Hochschild complex computing

, as defined for example in
[

, Section 5.1]
(see Remark

).

The statement for
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
is proved in the same way.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

One can show that the spectral sequences of Proposition

are spectral sequences of algebras; since we will not use the multiplicative structure, we decided not to spell out the details.

Thanks to the graded structure on
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
we also obtain spectral sequences converging to the homology of
\Hom(G\times A,U(n))_{hU(n)}
and
\Rep(G\times A,U(n))
for each individual

. To this end observe that
H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G);k)
is a
bigraded
algebra, with an internal homological degree and a grading by the rank of a representation, that is, through the decomposition
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)\simeq\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0}\Hom(G,U(n))_{hU(n)}
. In Section

we will term this grading as the grading by

(as opposed to the homological grading). The grading by charge induces a grading on the Hochschild homology and we denote by

the subspace of elements of charge

. This will be explained in more detail in Section

.

Corollary 5.7
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
5.5
there are spectral sequences for all

E^{2}=\HH^{\widehat{A}}(H_{\ast}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G);k))_{n}\Longrightarrow H_{\ast}^{U(n)}(\Hom(G\times A,U(n));k)\,,

and similarly for
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
and
\Rep(G\times A,U(n))
.

Proof.

Because
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
comes from a

-space, there is an isomorphism of simplicial spaces

\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)((\widehat{A}_{\bullet})_{+})=\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)((\widehat{A}_{\bullet})_{+})_{n}

such that

|\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)((\widehat{A}_{\bullet})_{+})_{n}|\simeq\Hom(G\times A,U(n))_{hU(n)}\,.

Therefore, the spectral sequence of Proposition

is in fact a direct sum of spectral sequences, one for each

.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

We observe that the quotient maps
\Hom(G,U(n))_{hU(n)}\to\Rep(G,U(n))
induce a map of
\Gamma
-spaces
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)\to\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{def}}(G)
, and therefore a map of the corresponding spectral sequences in Proposition
5.5
and Corollary
5.7
.

An application of the spectral sequence will be given in Theorem
7.18
, where we determine the Poincaré polynomial of

for every
r\geq 0
.

Let

Definition 7.4

The
Hochschild complex
of
(A,\partial)
, denoted
(C(A),d)
, is defined as the total complex
(\mathrm{Tot}^{\oplus}(B(A)),d=d_{h}+d_{v})
of the bicomplex
(B(A),d_{h},d_{v})
given by

B_{p,q}(A)=(A^{\otimes(p+1)})_{q}

with horizontal differential
d_{h}\colon\thinspace B_{p,q}(A)\to B_{p-1,q}(A)
given by

\begin{split}d_{h}(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})&=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{n+(|a_{0}|+\cdots+|a_{n-1}|)|a_{n}|}a_{n}a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n-1}\end{split}

and vertical differential
d_{v}\colon\thinspace B_{p,q}(A)\to B_{p,q-1}(A)
given by

d_{v}(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=(-1)^{p+q}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{|a_{0}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|}a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes\partial a_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}\,.

The
n
-th homology of

is denoted

For example, if

is a graded commutator

There is a multiplicative structure on the Hochschild complex called the shuffle product which makes

a dgca. To define it we let the symmetric group

Definition 7.5

\begin{split}&(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{p})\cdot(a_{0}^{\prime}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{s}^{\prime})\\

[
24

It will be convenient to use a smaller variant of the Hochschild complex.

\sum_{k=1}^{r}t(1-t)^{r-k}=1-(1-t)^{r}
to determine the sum.
∎

We finish the section with the Poincaré polynomial of
\Rep(F_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r},U(2))
.

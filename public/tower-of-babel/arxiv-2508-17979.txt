The divisor function along arithmetic progressions and binary cubic polynomials
Lasse Grimmelt, Jori Merikoski

Abstract
. We prove a new equidistribution estimate for the divisor function in arithmetic progression to moduli that have two small factors. We give two applications. First, we show an asymptotic formula for the divisor function over arithmetic progressions to almost all moduli of exponent 2 / 3 2/3 . Second, we show an asymptotic formula for the divisor function along the nonhomogeneous binary cubic polynomial X ​ Y 2 + 1 XY^{2}+1 .

1.
Introduction

We expect the divisor function
d(n)
to be equidistributed over primitive residue classes. To measure this, we define for
(a,q)=1
and
X>1
the well-studied discrepancy

\displaystyle\Delta(X;q,a):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})\end{subarray}}d(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}d(n).

For applications it is important to have estimates uniformly in the size of the modulus
q
. Selberg (unpublished) and Hooley
[
8
]
independently proved by using the Weil bound for Kloosterman sums that
|\Delta(X;q,a)|\leq X^{o(1)}(q^{1/2}+X^{1/3})
, which is non-trivial in the range
q<X^{2/3-\varepsilon}
. Fouvry
[
3
]
has shown that
|\Delta(X;q,a)|
is small on average over
q\in[X^{2/3+\varepsilon},X^{1-\varepsilon}]
.

These two results leave a gap for moduli in
[X^{2/3-\varepsilon},X^{2/3+\varepsilon}]
.
Fouvry and Iwaniec
[
4
]
bound
|\Delta(X;q,a)|
on average over moduli
q
with a specific factorization which covers a positive proportion of all moduli in
[X^{2/3-\varepsilon},X^{2/3+\varepsilon}]
. Cf. also the works of Irving
[
9
]
, Khan
[
10
]
, and Liu, Shparlinski, and Zhang
[
12
]
for point-wise bounds for more specific types of moduli.

In this paper we prove a new estimate for moduli
q
that have two small factors, on average over the smaller factor (cf. Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
). This allows us for the first time to obtain a non-trivial bound for
|\Delta(X;q,a)|
for almost all moduli of size
[X^{2/3-\varepsilon},X^{2/3+\varepsilon}]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a constant
B>1
such that uniformly for
\varepsilon>0
,
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
Q<X^{2/3+\varepsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta(X;q,a)\ll\frac{X^{1-\varepsilon}(\log X)^{B}}{q}

for all but
O(\sqrt{\varepsilon}Q)
moduli
q\in(Q,2Q]
with
(a,q)=1
.

In particular, for any
A>0
and
Q\leq X^{2/3}(\log X)^{A}
we have

\displaystyle\Delta(X;q,a)\ll\frac{X}{q(\log X)^{A}}

for all but
\ll_{A}\frac{(\log\log X)^{1/2}}{(\log X)^{1/2}}Q
moduli
q\in(Q,2Q]
with
(a,q)=1
.

As a second application, we consider the divisor function over binary cubic polynomials. Previously an asymptotic formula has been shown by Greaves
[
6
]
for binary cubic forms (cf. also the work of Browning
[
1
]
). We are for the first time able to address a nonhomogeneous binary cubic polynomial.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
C(X,Y)=XY^{2}+1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\leq X}d(C(n_{1},n_{2}))=\Bigl(1+O\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{\log\log X}{\log X}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigr)\Bigr)\frac{3}{\zeta(2)}X^{2}\log X.

1.1.
Factorable estimates

Our main technical results are factorable equidistribution estimates for the divisor function that are proved in Section
4
. For the statement we define a weighted version of the discrepancy

\displaystyle\Delta^{\psi}(X;q,a):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}\psi(n/X)d(n)\Bigl({\mathbf{1}}_{n\equiv a\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})}-\frac{{\mathbf{1}}_{(n,q)=1}}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
r,s\geq 1
be coprime with
s
cube-free and let
U,X>1
. Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and let
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a smooth function supported on
[1,2]
and satisfying for all
j\geq 0
the derivative bound
\|\psi^{(j)}\|_{\infty}\ll_{j}\delta^{-j}
. Then uniformly for all
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
(a,rs)=1
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{U}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim U\\
u\,\text{square-free}\\
(u,ars)=1\\
\end{subarray}}|\Delta^{\psi}(X;rsu,a)|\ll\delta^{-2}d(rs)^{O(1)}(\log X)^{O(1)}\mathcal{L}_{r,s}(U,X),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{r,s}(U,X)=s^{1/8}U^{1/4}X^{1/4}+r^{1/4}X^{1/4}+(rs)^{1/2}+r^{1/2}s^{3/8}U^{1/4}.

Here, as usual the summation condition
u\sim U
means
U<u\leq 2U
. We take the variables to satisfy the hierarchy
\log s\gg\log r\gg\log u.
More precisely, for
rsU=X^{2/3+o(1)}
Theorem
1.3
gives a power saving in the range

(1.1)

\displaystyle s^{3}U^{6}<X^{2-\varepsilon},\quad r\leq X^{1/3-\varepsilon},\quad U>X^{\varepsilon},\quad\text{and}\quad r^{12}s^{9}U^{6}<X^{8-\varepsilon}.

Importantly, for this to be non-trivial the factors
r
and
u
can be made small. Generic moduli
q=X^{2/3+o(1)}
have such factors and this allows us to obtain Theorem
1.1
.

We show a similar factorable estimate with an average over perfect squares of square-free integers from which Theorem
1.2
will follow by restricting to
n_{2}
with a suitable factorization. The sparseness of squares makes the upper bound slightly worse in the third and fourth term but it still gives a power saving in the same range (
1.1
) for
rsU=X^{2/3+o(1)}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
r,s\geq 1
be coprime with
s
cube-free and let
U,X>1
. Let
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a smooth function supported on
[1,2]
and satisfying for all
j\geq 0
the derivative bound
\|\psi^{(j)}\|_{\infty}\ll_{j}\delta^{-j}
. Then uniformly for all
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
(a,rs)=1
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{U^{1/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u^{2}\sim U\\
u\,\text{square-free}\\
(u,ars)=1\\
\end{subarray}}|\Delta^{\psi}(X;rsu^{2},a)|\ll\delta^{-2}d(rs)^{O(1)}(\log X)^{O(1)}\mathcal{M}_{r,s}(U,X),

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{r,s}(U,X)=s^{1/8}U^{1/4}X^{1/4}+r^{1/4}X^{1/4}+(rs)^{1/2}U^{1/4}+r^{1/2}s^{3/8}U^{1/4+o(1)}.

1.2.
Remarks

(1)

The proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
generalize to the divisor function replaced by Hecke eigenvalues of GL(2) holomorphic or Maass cuspidal newforms. Indeed, we only need two properties of the divisor function, namely, the Voronoï  summation formula and an
\ell^{2}
-norm bound.

(2)

It seems likely that with more work one can establish Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
with full uniformity in the residue class
a
, that is, with a maximum over
a
as in the classical Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem. As a corollary, this would allow an extension of Theorem
1.2
to more general binary cubic polynomials
C(X,Y)=XY^{2}+P(Y)
with
P
an arbitrary cubic polynomial. As it is, the proof can be generalized to
XY^{2}+a.

(3)

The constant
B
in Theorem
1.1
is effective and depends linearly on the implied constants in Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
. All other implied constants are also effective.

1.3.
Sketch of the proof

Consider Theorem
1.3
for
moduli
q=rsu\sim Q=X^{2/3}
. Then by Voronoï  summation we morally have

\displaystyle\Delta(X;q,a)\approx\sum_{n\leq\sqrt{Q}}d(n)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;q),

where
\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;q)
denotes the normalized Kloosterman sums (
2.1
). The point-wise Estermann-Weil bound (
2.3
) barely fails for moduli of size
Q=X^{2/3}
and so our task is to show additional cancellation. The average over a small factor
u\sim U
allows us to apply Cauchy-Schwarz to smoothen the variable
n
. This reduces the problem to showing non-trivial cancellation in

\displaystyle\sum_{u_{1},u_{2}\sim U}\bigg|\sum_{n\leq\sqrt{Q}}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;rsu_{1})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;rsu_{2})\bigg|.

Note that the size of the modulus has increased from
Q
to
QU
and so the sum over
n
is barely too short for the Pólya-Vinogradov method. The second small factor
r
allows us to use Heath-Brown’s
q
-van der Corput method and on the off-diagonal
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
we morally get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq\sqrt{Q}}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;rsu_{1})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;rsu_{2})\ll Q^{1/4}(s^{1/4}U^{1/2}+r^{1/2}).

This gives a non-trivial saving as long as
r
is larger than
U
and smaller than
\sqrt{Q}
. For the proof we need a Deligne type bound for the correlation of four Kloosterman sums (Lemma
2.1
) due to Fouvry, Kowalski, and Michel
[
5
]
. For the proof of Theorem
1.4
we prove an analogous bound with square moduli (Lemma
2.2
).

2.
Lemmas on exponential sums

2.1.
Complete sums modulo primes

We begin by importing a result of Fouvry, Kowalski, and Michel
[
5
]
on correlations of trace functions, which builds on bounds of Deligne and Katz coming from algebraic geometry. Let
p
be a prime. The group
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
acts on
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
(or more precisely on
\mathbb{P}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
) via

\displaystyle\gamma=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix a&b \\ c & d\endlx@physics@matrix):x\mapsto\gamma.x:=\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}.

For
q\geq 1
and
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we denote the usual (normalised) Kloosterman sum by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a;q):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\sum_{x\,(q)^{\ast}}e_{q}(ax+\overline{x}).

Recall that it is real valued and that for any
(r,s)=1
we have the twist multiplicative relation

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a;rs)=\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(\overline{r}^{2}a;s)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(\overline{s}^{2}a;r).

We also recall the Estermann-Weil bound
[
2
]
for all integers
c\geq 1

(2.3)

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a;c)|\leq d(c)(a,c)^{1/2}.

The following lemma is
[
5
, Corollary 3.3]
. We only need the special cases
k=2
and
k=4
and
\gamma_{i}.x
being affine functions
ax+b
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer. Let
p
be a prime,
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, and let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}\in\operatorname{PGL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. Suppose that either
h\neq 0
or that there exists some
\gamma\in\operatorname{PGL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
with

\displaystyle\#\{i\leq k:\gamma_{i}=\gamma\}\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{2}).

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\,(p)}e_{p}(xh)\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(\gamma_{i}.x;p)\ll_{k}p^{1/2},

where the sum runs over
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
such that
\gamma_{i}.x
is defined for all
i\leq k
.

2.2.
Complete sums modulo prime squares

For Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
we require a similar result for prime square moduli. We only state and prove it in the special case of affine functions necessary for us, but it seems likely that a full analogue of Lemma
2.1
also holds.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2},h\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
p
a prime with
(a_{1}a_{2},p)=1
. Then we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\,(p^{2})}e_{p^{2}}(xh)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a_{1}(x+b_{1});p^{2})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a_{1}(x+b_{2});p^{2})\ll p(h,b_{1}-b_{2},p)

and

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{x\,(p^{2})}e_{p^{2}}(xh)\prod_{i,j=1}^{2}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a_{i}(x+b_{j});p^{2})\ll p\bigl((h,a_{1}^{2}-a_{2}^{2},p)+(h,b_{1}-b_{2},p)\bigr).\end{split}

Proof.

The proof of (
2.4
) is similar but easier so we only consider (
2.5
). By writing
x=y+zp
for
y,z\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
and using
\overline{y+pz}\equiv\overline{y}-pz\overline{y}^{2}\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p^{2}})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a;p^{2})=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{y\,(p)^{\ast}}\sum_{z\,(p)}e_{p^{2}}(ay+\overline{y})e_{p}(z(a-\overline{y}^{2}))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\,(p)^{\ast}\\
\overline{y}^{2}\equiv a\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})\end{subarray}}e_{p^{2}}(ay+\overline{y}).

Plugging this in four times and writing again
x=y+zp
, we get

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{x\,(p^{2})}e_{p^{2}}(xh)\prod_{i,j=1}^{2}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a_{i}(x+b_{j});p^{2})\bigg|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})\\
\overline{y_{i}}^{2}\equiv a_{i}y+b_{i}\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})\end{subarray}}\bigg|\sum_{z\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})}e_{p}(zh)\prod_{i,j=1}^{2}e_{p}(a_{i}zy_{i+j})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq p\,\#\Bigl\{(y,y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4})\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{5}:\overline{y_{i+j}}^{2}\equiv a_{i}(y+b_{j})\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})\forall i,j\in\{1,2\}

\displaystyle\hskip 175.0pth+a_{1}y_{1}+a_{2}y_{2}+a_{1}y_{3}+a_{2}y_{4}\equiv 0\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{p})\Bigr\}.

It then suffices to show that the number of solutions
(y,y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4})
is bounded by

\displaystyle\ll(h,a_{1}^{2}-a_{2}^{2},p)+(h,b_{1}-b_{2},p).

Solving for
y
, we consider a system of four equations

\displaystyle a_{1}y_{1}^{2}=a_{2}y_{2}^{2},\quad\quad a_{1}y_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle=a_{2}y_{4}^{2},

\displaystyle a_{1}(y_{3}^{2}-y_{1}^{2})+(b_{2}-b_{1})y_{1}^{2}y_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle h+a_{1}y_{1}+a_{2}y_{2}+a_{1}y_{3}+a_{2}y_{4}

\displaystyle=0.

Fixing roots
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
to the equation
\omega^{2}=a_{1}/a_{2}
we get
y_{2}=\omega_{1}y_{1}
and
y_{4}=\omega_{2}y_{3}
. Substituting for
y_{2}
and
y_{4}
, it suffices to bound the number of solutions to

\displaystyle a_{1}(y_{3}^{2}-y_{1}^{2})+(b_{2}-b_{1})y_{1}^{2}y_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle h+(a_{1}+\omega_{1}a_{2})y_{1}+(a_{1}+\omega_{2}a_{2})y_{3}

\displaystyle=0.

If
(a_{1}+\omega_{1}a_{2})=(a_{1}+\omega_{2}a_{2})=0
, then there are no solutions unless
h=0
and in this case the number of solutions is
O((h,a_{1}^{2}-a_{2}^{2},p))
. Suppose then by symmetry that
(a_{1}+\omega_{1}a_{2})\neq 0
. We can solve
y_{1}=\frac{-h-(a_{1}+\omega_{2}a_{2})y_{3}}{(a_{1}+\omega_{1}a_{2})}
and plug it in to get

\displaystyle a_{1}\left(y_{3}^{2}-\Bigl(\frac{h+(a_{1}+\omega_{2}a_{2})y_{3}}{(a_{1}+\omega_{1}a_{2})}\Bigl)^{2}\right)+(b_{2}-b_{1})\Bigl(\frac{h+(a_{1}+\omega_{2}a_{2})y_{3}}{(a_{1}+\omega_{1}a_{2})}\Bigr)^{2}y_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle=0.

If
(a_{1}+\omega_{2}a_{2})\neq 0
and
b_{1}\neq b_{2}
, then the congruence for
y_{3}
is non-trivial by considering the leading term
y_{3}^{4}(b_{1}-b_{2})\frac{(a_{1}+\omega_{2}a_{2})^{2}}{(a_{1}+\omega_{1}a_{2})^{2}}
and we get
O(1)
solutions. If
b_{1}=b_{2}
, then we get

\displaystyle a_{1}\left(y_{3}^{2}-\Bigl(\frac{h+(a_{1}+\omega_{2}a_{2})y_{3}}{(a_{1}+\omega_{1}a_{2})}\Bigl)^{2}\right)=0

which is non-trivial as long as
h\neq 0
. Thus, in this case we get at most
\ll(h,b_{1}-b_{2},p)
solutions. Finally, if
(a_{1}+\omega_{2}a_{2})=0
, then we get

\displaystyle a_{1}\left(y_{3}^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{h}{(a_{1}+\omega_{1}a_{2})}\Bigl)^{2}\right)+(b_{2}-b_{1})\Bigl(\frac{h}{(a_{1}+\omega_{1}a_{2})}\Bigl)^{2}y_{3}^{2}=0

If
h\neq 0
, then either there are no solutions (if the leading coefficients cancel) or there are
O(1)
solutions because the polynomial is non-trivial. For
h=0
the equation is
a_{1}y_{3}^{2}=0
and there is only one solution.
∎

2.3.
Incomplete sums: Pólya-Vinogradov bound

In this section we use the Pólya-Vinogradov method of completing the sum to get the following bound (cf.
[
13
, Sections 3 and 6]
for similar arguments).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
a,s,u_{1},u_{2}\geq 1
be pair-wise coprime integers with
s,u_{1},u_{2}
be cube-free. Let
N>1
and let
\psi_{N}:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,1]
be a smooth function supported on
[-N,N]
satisfying
\psi_{N}^{(j)}\ll_{j}N^{-j}
.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\psi_{N}(n)\prod_{i,j=1}^{2}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a(n+b_{j});su_{i})\ll d(su_{1}u_{2})^{O(1)}(su_{1}u_{2})^{1/2}\mathcal{P}_{s,u_{1},u_{2}}(N),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{s,u_{1},u_{2}}(N)=1+\frac{N}{su_{1}u_{2}}\prod_{p^{k}||u_{1}u_{2}}(b_{1}-b_{2},p)^{k/2}\prod_{p^{k}||s}\big((u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2},p)^{k/2}+(b_{1}-b_{2},p)^{k/2}\big).

Proof.

We complete the sum over
n
with Poisson summation

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\psi_{N}(n)\prod_{i,j=1}^{2}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a(n+b_{j});su_{i})=\frac{1}{su_{1}u_{2}}\sum_{h}\widehat{\psi_{N}}\Bigl(\frac{h}{su_{1}u_{2}}\Bigr)S(h;s,u_{1},u_{2}),

where

\displaystyle S(h;s,u_{1},u_{2})=\sum_{x\,(su_{1}u_{2})}e_{su_{1},u_{2}}(hx)\prod_{i,j=1}^{2}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a(x+b_{j});su_{i}).

By (
2.2
) and the Chinese remainder theorem we can write

\displaystyle S(h;s,u_{1},u_{2})=\prod_{p^{k}||s}T(h;p^{k})\prod_{p^{k}||u_{1}}U(h,u_{1};p^{k})\prod_{p^{k}||u_{2}}U(h,u_{2};p^{k}),

where for
q=p^{k}||s
and
c=su_{1}u_{2}

\displaystyle T(h;q):=\sum_{x\,(q)}e_{q}(hx\overline{(c/q)})\prod_{i,j=1}^{2}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(\overline{u_{i}}^{2}\overline{(s/q)}^{2}a(x+b_{j});q)

and for
q=p^{k}||u_{i}

\displaystyle U(h,u_{i};q):=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\,(q)}e_{q}(hx\overline{(c/q)})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}((\overline{su_{i}/q})^{2}a(x+b_{1});q)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}((\overline{su_{i}/q})^{2}a(x+b_{2});q).

For
p^{k}||s
we get from Lemma
2.1
if
k=1
or (
2.5
) of Lemma
2.2
if
k=2

\displaystyle T(h;p)\ll(h,u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2},p)^{k/2}p^{k/2}+(h,b_{1}-b_{2},p)^{k/2}p^{k/2}.

For
p||u_{i}
we get from Lemma
2.1
if
k=1
or (
2.4
) of Lemma
2.2
if
k=2

\displaystyle U(h,u_{i};p^{k})\ll(h,b_{1}-b_{2},p)^{k/2}p^{k/2}.

Summing over
h
gives the result.
∎

2.4.
Incomplete sums:
q
-van der Corput bound

We require the following estimate that follows by an application of Heath-Brown’s
q
-analogue of the van der Corput
A
-process
[
7
,
13
]
and Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
a,r,s,u_{1},u_{2}\geq 1
be pair-wise coprime integers with
s,u_{1},u_{2}
cube-free and let
c|r
. Let
N>1
and let
\psi_{N}:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,1]
be a smooth function supported on
[1/2,N]
satisfying
\psi_{N}^{(j)}\ll_{j}N^{-j}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,r)=c}\psi_{N}(n)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;rsu_{1})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;rsu_{2})

\displaystyle\ll d(rsu_{1}u_{2})^{O(1)}(cN)^{1/2}\Bigl((su_{1}u_{2})^{1/4}\Bigl(1+\frac{N\prod_{p^{k}||s}(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2},p)^{k/2}}{su_{1}u_{2}}\Bigr)^{1/2}+r^{1/2}\Bigr).

Proof.

We note that for any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
by the twist multiplicative relation (
2.2
) and periodicity

\displaystyle\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a(n+kr);rsu_{1})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a(n+kr);rsu_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a\overline{su_{1}}^{2}n;r)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a\overline{su_{2}}^{2}n;r)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a\overline{r}^{2}(n+kr);su_{1})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a\overline{r}^{2}(n+kr);su_{2}).

Therefore, denoting
K=\lfloor N/r\rfloor
, we have by (
2.3
)

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{(n,r)=c}\psi_{N}(n)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;rsu_{1})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;rsu_{2})\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\frac{1}{K}\sum_{(n,r)=c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq K\end{subarray}}\psi_{N}(n+kr)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(n+kr;rsu_{1})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(n+kr;rsu_{2})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\ll N\\
(n,r)=c\end{subarray}}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq K\end{subarray}}\psi_{N}(n+kr)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a\overline{r}^{2}(n+kr);su_{1})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a\overline{r}^{2}(n+kr);su_{2})\bigg|.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz on
n
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,r)=c}\psi_{N}(n)\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;rsu_{1})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an;rsu_{2})\ll\frac{(cN)^{1/2}}{K}\bigg(\sum_{n}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq K\end{subarray}}\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

and by the choice
K=\lfloor N/r\rfloor
it suffices to show that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\bigg|\sum_{k}\bigg|^{2}\ll d(rsu_{1}u_{2})^{O(1)}\Bigl(K^{2}(su_{1}u_{2})^{1/2}(1+\frac{N(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2},s)^{1/2}}{su_{1}u_{2}})+KN\Bigr).

By expanding the square, the sum we need to estimate becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\leq K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
\end{subarray}}\psi_{N}(n+k_{1}r)\psi_{N}(n+k_{2}r)\prod_{i,j=1}^{2}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(a\overline{r}^{2}(n+k_{j}r);su_{i}).

We apply Lemma
2.3
to bound this by

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle d(rsu_{1}u_{2})^{O(1)}(su_{1}u_{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\leq K}\Bigl(1+\frac{N}{su_{1}u_{2}}\prod_{p^{k}||u_{1}u_{2}}(k_{1}-k_{2},p)^{k/2}\prod_{p^{k}||s}\big((u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2},p)^{k/2}+(k_{1}-k_{2},p)^{k/2}\Bigr).

For
k^{\prime}=k_{1}-k_{2}\neq 0
we crudely bound
(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2},p)^{k/2}+(k^{\prime},p)^{k/2}\leq(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2},p)^{k/2}(k^{\prime},p)^{k/2}\leq(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2},p)^{k/2}(k^{\prime},p^{k})
and obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\leq K}

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}+\frac{N}{su_{1}u_{2}}\bigg(K(su_{1}u_{2})^{1/2}+K\sum_{0<|k^{\prime}|\leq 2K}(k^{\prime},su_{1}u_{2})\prod_{p^{k}||s}(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2},p)^{k/2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}+\frac{N}{su_{1}u_{2}}(K(su_{1}u_{2})^{1/2}+d(su_{1}u_{2})K^{2}\prod_{p^{k}||s}(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2},p)^{k/2}).

This gives the claim (
2.6
).
∎

3.
Certain bilinear sums of Kloosterman sums

In this section we apply Lemma
2.4
to give the following bound for a bilinear sum of Kloosterman sums.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
r,s\geq 1
be coprime with
s
cube-free and let
U>1
. Let
\lambda_{n},\gamma_{u}
be complex coefficients with
\gamma_{u}
supported on a subset of cube-free integers
\mathcal{U}
such that for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{U}
with
u_{0}=(u_{1},u_{2})
we have
(u_{1}/u_{0},u_{0})=1
. Denote
\widetilde{\gamma}_{u}=\gamma_{u}d(u)^{O(1)}
and
\lambda^{(c)}_{n}:=\lambda_{n}c^{1/4}\mathbf{1}_{c|n}
. Then uniformly in
a
with
(a,rs)=1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim U\\
(u,ars)=1\end{subarray}}\gamma_{u}\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}\lambda_{n}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an,rsu)\ll d(rs)^{O(1)}\sum_{c|r}\|\lambda^{(c)}\|_{2}\,\mathcal{K}_{r,s,\gamma}(U,N),

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{r,s,\gamma}(U,N)=N^{1/4}\|\widetilde{\gamma}\|_{1}(s^{1/8}U^{1/4}+r^{1/4})+N^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{u_{0}\leq U}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},v_{2}\sim U/u_{0}\\
(v_{1},v_{2})=1\end{subarray}}|\widetilde{\gamma}_{u_{0}v_{1}}\widetilde{\gamma}_{u_{0}v_{1}}|\frac{\prod_{p^{k}||su_{0}}(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2},p)^{k/4}}{(su_{0}v_{1}v_{2})^{1/4}}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Proof.

We sort according to
c=(n,r)
to write

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim U\\
(u,ars)=1\end{subarray}}\gamma_{u}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq N\end{subarray}}\lambda_{n}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an,rsu)=\sum_{c|r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim U\\
(u,ars)=1\end{subarray}}\gamma_{u}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq N\\
(n,r)=c\end{subarray}}c^{-1/4}\lambda^{(c)}_{n}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an,rsu).

By applying Cauchy-Schwarz on
n
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim U\\
(u,ars)=1\end{subarray}}\gamma_{u}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq N\\
(n,r)=c\end{subarray}}\lambda_{n}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an,rsu)\leq\sum_{c|r}\|\lambda^{(c)}\|_{2}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,r)=c\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi_{N}(n)}{\sqrt{c}}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim U\\
(u,ars)=1\end{subarray}}\gamma_{u}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an,rsu)\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Expanding the square and making the change of variables
u_{j}=u_{0}v_{j}
with
u_{0}=(u_{1},u_{2})
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,r)=c\end{subarray}}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim U\end{subarray}}\bigg|^{2}=\sum_{u_{0}\leq U}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},v_{2}\sim U/u_{0}\\
(v_{1},v_{2})=1\\
(u_{0}v_{1}v_{2},ars)=1\end{subarray}}\gamma_{u_{0}v_{1}}\gamma_{u_{0}v_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,r)=c\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi_{N}(n)}{\sqrt{c}}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an,rsu_{0}v_{1})\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an,rsu_{0}v_{2}).

Using Lemma
2.4
with
s\mapsto su_{0}
, making use of the support condition of
\gamma
to guarantee
(v_{1}v_{2},u_{0})=1
, this is bounded by

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle d(rs)^{O(1)}\Bigl(N^{1/2}\|\widetilde{\gamma}\|_{1}^{2}(s^{1/4}U^{1/2}+r^{1/2})+N\sum_{u_{0}\leq U}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},v_{2}\sim V/u_{0}\\
(v_{1},v_{2})=1\end{subarray}}|\widetilde{\gamma}_{u_{0}v_{1}}\widetilde{\gamma}_{u_{0}v_{2}}|\frac{\prod_{p^{k}||su_{0}}(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2},p)^{k/4}}{(su_{0}v_{1}v_{2})^{1/4}}\Bigr).

∎

be coprime with
s
cube-free and let

(u,ars)=1\end{subarray}}\gamma_{u}\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}\lambda_{n}\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(an,rsu)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle d(rs)^{O(1)}(\log 2NU)^{O(1)}N^{1/2}\bigg(N^{1/4}(s^{1/8}U^{5/4}+r^{1/4}U)+N^{1/2}U^{1/2}+\frac{N^{1/2}U^{3/4}}{s^{1/8}}\bigg).

\displaystyle(\log X)^{O(1)}X^{1+\varepsilon 25/8-\sqrt{\varepsilon}3/16}+X^{11/12+3\varepsilon+1/48}+X^{1+\varepsilon 7/2-5\varepsilon}+X^{11/12+\varepsilon 27/8-\varepsilon 5/4+1/96}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle(\log X)^{O(1)}X^{1-\varepsilon}\quad\text{ for }\quad\varepsilon<1/1000.

∎

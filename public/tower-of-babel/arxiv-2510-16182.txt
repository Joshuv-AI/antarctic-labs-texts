1.
Introduction

In this article we consider a version for linear equations with rough coefficients of the classical statement on propagation of singularities.

1.1.
Main results

Our results are formulated using pseudodifferential symbols that have limited regularity in the spatial variable.

Loosely speaking, for
r>0
and
m\in\mathbb{R}
, the class
C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,0}
from Definition
2.3
consists of symbols
that behave like an element of
S^{m}_{1,0}
in the fiber variable, but are elements of the Zygmund space
C^{r}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
from Definition
2.1
in the spatial variable. For
r\notin{{\mathbb{N}}}
the latter space coincides with
C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, whereas for
r\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
it is a strict subset of
C^{r-1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
For such a symbol
p
and for

(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o=\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\})
, we write

(1.1)

\hat{p}(x,\xi):=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-m}p(x,\lambda\xi).

Moreover,
\Sigma_{\hat{p}}=\hat{p}^{-1}(\{0\})
is the characteristic set of
\hat{p}
, cf. (
2.3
).

We refer to Section
2.1
for other basic notions from microlocal analysis which appear in the following theorem, the main result of this article.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
r>1
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
-r<s<r
. Let
p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,0}
be such that
\Imag p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m-1}_{1,0}
, and such that
\hat{p}
is a well-defined element of
C^{1}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o)
. Set

(1.2)

\sigma:=\begin{cases}s-r&\text{if }0<s<r,\\
\varepsilon-r&\text{if }-r<s\leq 0,\end{cases}

for
\varepsilon>0
. Let
u\in H^{\sigma+m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
p(x,D)u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
\operatorname{WF}^{s+m}u\subseteq\Sigma_{\hat{p}}
. Moreover, for each
(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
there exists a null bicharacteristic
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of
\hat{p}
satisfying
\gamma(0)=(x,\xi)
and
\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem
1.1
follows from a microlocal statement in Theorem
3.1
(see Remark
3.2
). The parameter
\sigma
in (
1.2
) measures the a priori regularity of
u
needed to leverage
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
regularity of
p(x,D)u
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
, the Hamilton vector field
H_{\hat{p}}
is continuous, and bounded on the cosphere bundle
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S^{n-1}
. Moreover,
\hat{p}
is positively homogeneous of degree
m
in the fiber variable. Hence, by Peano’s theorem (see e.g.
[
25
, Proposition 1.A.1]
), orbits
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of
H_{\hat{p}}
with specified initial data exist, and every point in the wavefront set lies on a null bicharacteristic which is completely contained in the wavefront set. As such, one might say that the wavefront set is a union of maximally extended null bicharacteristics. However, if
\hat{p}
is merely continuously differentiable, then orbits of
H_{\hat{p}}
need not be unique, and Theorem
3.1
does not preclude certain bicharacteristics from entering the wavefront set and then leaving it again. On the other hand, if
\hat{p}\in C^{1,1}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o)
, then bicharacteristics are unique and one recovers the classical statement on propagation of singularities, albeit under suitable a priori regularity assumptions on
u
. In fact,
C^{2}_{*}
regularity suffices for uniqueness (see Remark
3.5
).

Of course,
Theorem
1.1
implies a statement on propagation of regularity. Namely, if
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
is such that every null bicharacteristic
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of
\hat{p}
with
\gamma(0)=(x,\xi)
has non-empty intersection with the complement of
\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
, then
(x,\xi)\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
(see Remark
3.6
). Moreover, as observed in Corollary
3.7
, if there exists a
T>0
with the property that, for every such
\gamma
, there exists a
t_{\gamma}\geq T
such that
\gamma(t_{\gamma})\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
, then
\gamma(t)\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
for all such bicharacteristics and all
0\leq t\leq T
.

Differential operators with coefficients of
C^{r}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
regularity form a concrete class to which Theorem
1.1
applies. In fact, such operators are straightforward to define on domains, and a version of Theorem
1.1
holds for differential operators on domains, Corollary
3.8
. Here only local regularity is required of
p
and
u
. We also consider second-order differential operators in divergence form, in Theorem
3.10
. Here the Sobolev interval for
s
is shifted.

We note that, if one considers in all these results differential operators that have slightly more spatial regularity, measured using Sobolev spaces
\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
over the space
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
from Definition
2.2
, then the endpoints
s=-r
and
s=r
of the Sobolev interval for
s
can be included, and one may let
\varepsilon=0
in (
1.2
). For integer
r
, this applies in particular to operators with
C^{r-1,1}
regularity (see (
2.5
)).

Note that, for
r=2
, our results require slightly less than
C^{1,1}
regularity. A concrete geometric setting in which this is relevant arises when considering wave equations on manifolds with a metric which has bounded Ricci curvature tensor.
Such a metric automatically has
\mathcal{H}^{2,\infty}\subseteq C^{2}_{*}
regularity, and therefore Theorem
3.10
yields propagation of singularities for these metrics, as is explained in Section
4.2
.

1.2.
Previous work

In the smooth setting, where
p\in S^{m}_{1,0}=\cap_{r>0}C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,0}
and
\hat{p}\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o)
, Theorem
1.1
is essentially due to Hörmander (see
[
12
,
11
,
10
]
). In this case no a priori regularity is required, and the conclusion holds for general
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

In
[
2
]
, Bony studied propagation of singularities for nonlinear equations with rough solutions, and in the process developed and employed paradifferential calculus in a manner which has played a key role in subsequent work. Treatises of Bony’s results involving slight variations on the methods can be found in
[
14
]
and
[
22
]
. In particular, Taylor in
[
22
]
highlights the role of
C^{r}
regularity of the coefficients.

In the setting of linear equations with rough coefficients, Taylor in
[
23
]

proved Theorem
1.1
for
r=2
, but with
\sigma
replaced by
\sigma+\delta
for any
\delta>0
.
Results for
1<r<2
can also be found in
[
23
]
, but these are of a less concrete nature than Theorem
1.1
. For second-order hyperbolic equations and
r=2
, Smith in
[
20
]
obtained the endpoint case
\delta=0
, albeit under the assumption of
C^{1,1}
regularity. In fact, in the setting of
[
20
]
there is a distinguished time variable, and less regularity is required in this distinguished variable than in the remaining ones (see Section
4.1
). Moreover,
[
20
]
contains an example showing that the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
fails for
r=2
if
\sigma
is replaced by
\sigma-\delta
for some
\delta>0
, at least under the regularity assumptions on the coefficients considered there.

There are various related results on propagation of singularities.
For example, one also encounters non-uniqueness of bicharacteristics on manifolds with boundary or corners. Note that any smooth Riemannian manifold with boundary can locally be embedded in a manifold without boundary, by reflecting across the boundary. However, the metric that arises in this manner is merely Lipschitz, and as such is too rough to fall within the scope of Theorem
1.1
. On the other hand, on manifolds with boundary or corners the metric carries additional structure, as does the natural wavefront set of a solution to the wave equation, and it was shown in
[
17
,
18
,
28
]
that this wavefront set is a union of appropriately defined bicharacteristics. Theorem
1.1
does not require such additional structure but its conclusion is more limited, guaranteeing merely the existence of some bicharacteristic which is completely contained in the wavefront set.

Another instance of propagation of singularities for metrics
g
with singularities of class
C^{r}
can be found in
[
9
]
, for
1<r<2
but under the additional geometric assumption that the singularity is conormal.
It is then shown that, for
u\in H^{s-\rho+2}
satisfying
(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})u\in H^{s}
, the natural
H^{s+1}
wavefront set of
u
is a union of appropriate bicharacteristics. Here
s
ranges over a nontrivial interval and, as in Theorem
1.1
,
\rho
depends on
s
. Nonetheless, the largest
\rho
can be is slightly less than
(r+1)/2
. Note that this is less than the maximal gain over the background regularity in Theorem
1.1
for any fixed
r>1
. Hence Theorem
1.1
, or more precisely Theorem
3.10
, does provide some information beyond what is contained in
[
9
]
.
On the other hand, both the wavefront set and the bicharacteristics in
[
9
]
are tailored to the structure of the problem under consideration, making a strict comparison to Theorem
1.1
somewhat artificial.

Finally, we note that, again in the setting of the wave equation on a manifold with a rough metric, a propagation result for microlocal defect measures was obtained in
[
7
]
. Here the metric is merely assumed to be
C^{1}
, and it is shown that each point in the support of a suitable microlocal defect measure lies on a maximally extended bicharacteristic which is contained in the support of the measure. Such a result is of a slightly different nature than Theorem
1.1
, but it is worth emphasizing that the mere existence of such bicharacteristics
is sufficient to obtain concrete results regarding observability
of the wave equation. See also
[
5
,
6
]
for the analogous result on manifolds with boundary,
[
1
]
for a different proof of the propagation result on manifolds without boundary,
[
4
]
for earlier work regarding
C^{2}
metrics, and
[
19
,
24
]
for the argument in the case
r\geq 2
that Theorem
3.10
implies observability of the wave equation.

1.3.
Proof of the main result

The proof follows Taylor’s argument from
[
23
, Section 3.11]
, which in turn builds on the work of Bony in
[
2
]
.

Firstly, for
p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,0}
as in Theorem
1.1
and for
0<\delta\leq 1
, one applies a symbol decomposition to write
p=p^{\sharp}_{\delta}+p^{\flat}_{\delta}
, where
p^{\sharp}_{\delta}\in S^{m}_{1,\delta}
and
p^{\flat}_{\delta}\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m-\delta r}_{1,\delta}
.
Loosely speaking, this decomposition separates the frequences of

p
, and the parameter
\delta
determines the scale at which this splitting takes place.

Now,
one can show that an operator with a
C^{r}_{*}S^{m-\delta r}_{1,\delta}
symbol maps
H^{s+m-\delta r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
-(1-\delta)r<s<r
, and the endpoints of the Sobolev interval can be included for the operators
p^{\flat}_{\delta}(x,D)
if
p\in\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}S^{m}_{1,0}
. This implies that, if
u\in H^{s+m-\delta r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
p(x,D)u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

(1.3)

p^{\sharp}_{\delta}(x,D)u=p(x,D)u-p^{\flat}_{\delta}(x,D)u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

At least, this works for
-(1-\delta)r<s<r
, whereas for smaller
s
one needs to adjust the regularity of
u
, cf. (
1.2
).
We choose
\delta=1
, whereas
[
23
, Section 3.11]
lets
\delta<1
be arbitrarily close to
1
. The latter leads to suboptimal results.

Next, by (
1.3
), it suffices to obtain the propagation statement with
p
replaced by
p^{\sharp}_{\delta}
. To do so, one can rely on a positive commutator argument, albeit in a more delicate manner than usual, given that
\delta>0
. Indeed, here one uses that
p^{\sharp}_{\delta}
is not merely an
S^{m}_{1,\delta}
symbol, but that it has additional smoothness, allowing one to take
r
derivatives in the spatial variables before incurring blowup. This property is used for the pseudodifferential calculus underlying the positive commutator argument.

In fact, this pseudodifferential calculus is where a fundamental difference arises between
\delta<1
and
\delta=1
. Whereas the calculus for operators with
S^{m}_{1,\delta}
symbols is well behaved if
\delta<1
, even basic results break down for
\delta=1
. On the other hand, a fundamental observation by Bony is that symbols such as
p^{\sharp}_{1}
have an additional property, concerning their frequency support, that allows one to circumvent such obstacles. Hörmander in turn captured this phenomenon by observing that these symbols are contained in the class
\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
of
p\in S^{m}_{1,1}
such that
p(x,D)^{*}=q(x,D)
for some
q\in S^{m}_{1,1}
. Indeed, Bourdaud
[
3
]
and Hörmander
[
13
]
proved that there is an elegant and useful calculus for pseudodifferential operators with
\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
symbols, especially if the symbols have the same additional smoothness properties as
p^{\sharp}_{1}
.

Now, it turns out that an appropriate propagation statement for precisely these kinds of
\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
symbols is already available in the literature. Indeed, while covering Bony’s work on smooth nonlinear equations with rough initial data, Hörmander in
[
14
, Theorem 11.3.4]
proved a result which can also be used in the present context of linear equations with rough coefficients. In fact,
[
14
, Theorem 11.3.4]
differs from the propagation statement in
[
23
, Proposition 3.11.1]
in that the latter only applies for
\delta<1
. Moreover,
[
14
, Theorem 11.3.4]
is less abstract for
1<r<2
than
[
23
, Proposition 3.11.1]
, allowing for the concrete statement in Theorem
1.1
for such
r
. Finally, we note that,
unlike in the case
\delta<1
in
[
23
, Proposition 3.11.1]
, the a priori regularity of
u
also plays a key role in
[
14
, Theorem 11.3.4]
.

It should by now be clear that the present article is a very modest contribution to the existing literature, consisting mostly of the observation that separate results which were already available, with the possible exception of the endpoint statements in Proposition
2.7
, can be combined to improve upon part of the current theory regarding propagation of singularities for linear equations with rough coefficients.

1.4.
Organization of this article

Section
2
contains preliminaries for the rest of the article. We first collect some basics from microlocal analysis. We then introduce the relevant function spaces and use these to define rough symbol classes. Next, we state and prove the required results on paradifferential calculus.

Section
3
contains our main results, namely a more general version of Theorem
1.1
, as well as its corollaries and auxiliary results.

Finally, in Section
4
we give two applications of the main results to wave equations with rough coefficients.

1.5.
Notation

The natural numbers are
{{\mathbb{N}}}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{+}:={{\mathbb{N}}}\cup\{0\}
. Throughout this article we fix
n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write
{\langle}\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
.
We use multi-index notation, where
\partial_{\xi}=(\partial_{\xi_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{\xi_{n}})
,

\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{\xi_{1}}\cdots\partial^{\alpha_{n}}_{\xi_{n}}
and
\xi^{\alpha}=\xi_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\xi^{\alpha_{n}}_{n}

for
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
.

The Fourier transform of a tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is denoted by

\widehat{f}
.
If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\mathrm{d}x
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We let
\varphi(D)
be the Fourier multiplier with symbol
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect background that will be used in the rest of this article.

2.1.
Microlocal analysis

We briefly recall the required basic microlocal analysis.

For
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
open,
T^{*}\Omega
is the cotangent bundle of
\Omega
, identified with
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
o:=\Omega\times\{0\}\subseteq T^{*}\Omega
is the zero section. A subset
\Gamma\subseteq T^{*}\Omega\setminus o
is conic if
(x,\lambda\xi)\in\Gamma
for all
(x,\xi)\in\Gamma
and
\lambda>0
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta\in[0,1]
. The class
S^{m}_{1,\delta}
consists of all
p\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
such that

(2.1)

\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}}{\langle}\xi\rangle^{-m+|\alpha|-|\beta|\delta}|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}p(x,\xi)|<\infty

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
.
The pseudodifferential operator
p(x,D):\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
associated with such a symbol
p

is given by

(2.2)

p(x,D)f(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}p(x,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi,

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By adjoint action,
p(x,D)
extends to all of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For any
\delta<1
and
p\in S^{m}_{1,\delta}
, there exists a
q\in S^{m}_{1,\delta}
such that
p(x,D)^{*}=q(x,D)
.
In general, this is not the case when
\delta=1
. The class
\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
consists of those
p\in S^{m}_{1,1}
for which there exists a
q\in S^{m}_{1,1}
such that
p(x,D)^{*}=q(x,D)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
p\in C^{1}(T^{*}\Omega\setminus o)
. Then the characteristic set of
p
is

(2.3)

\Sigma_{p}:=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\Omega\setminus o\mid p(x,\xi)=0\}.

Moreover, the Hamilton vector field
H_{p}
associated with
p
is given by
H_{p}(x,\xi):=(\partial_{\xi}p(x,\xi),-\partial_{x}p(x,\xi))
for all
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\Omega\setminus o
. A
bicharacteristic
of
p
is an orbit of
H_{p}
, i.e. a continuously differentiable solution
\gamma:I\to T^{*}\Omega\setminus o
to the differential equation
\dot{\gamma}(t)=H_{p}(\gamma(t))
,
defined on some interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. If
\gamma(t)\in\Sigma_{p}
for all
t\in I
, then we call
\gamma
a
null bicharacteristic
of
p
.

A
p\in S^{m}_{1,\delta}
is
non-characteristic
at
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
if there exists a conic neighborhood
\Gamma\subseteq T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of
(x,\xi)
, and
c,\kappa>0
, such that
|p(y,\eta)|\geq c|\eta|^{m}
for all
(y,\eta)\in\Gamma
with
|\eta|\geq\kappa
. The
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

wavefront set

\operatorname{WF}^{s}u
of a
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the complement in
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of the set of
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
for which there exists a
p\in S^{0}_{1,0}
, non-characteristic at
(x,\xi)
, such that
p(x,D)u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):={\langle}D\rangle^{-s}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This notion extends in the obvious manner to distributions on an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.2.
Function spaces

In this subsection we define the relevant function spaces.

Throughout, fix a Littlewood–Paley decomposition
(\psi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. That is,

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}(\xi)=1

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\psi_{0}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|>1
,
\psi_{1}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|\notin[1/2,2]
, and
\psi_{j}(\xi)=\psi_{1}(2^{-j+1}\xi)
for all
j>1
. In fact, we may assume that
\psi_{0}
is radial and non-negative, with
\psi_{0}(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 1/2
, and that

(2.4)

\psi_{j}(\xi)=\psi_{0}(2^{-j}\xi)-\psi_{0}(2^{-j+1}\xi)

for all
j\geq 1
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
r\in\mathbb{R}
, the
Zygmund space

C^{r}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of those
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\psi_{j}(D)f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
j\geq 0
, and

\|f\|_{C^{r}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{j\geq 0}2^{jr}\|\psi_{j}(D)f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty.

We note that
C^{r}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is equal to the Besov space
B^{r}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. However, the present notation is more convenient for us, and it has been used frequently in paradifferential calculus (see e.g.
[
22
,
23
,
26
]
).

Next, let

{\mathrm{BMO}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the space of functions of bounded mean oscillation (see e.g.
[
21
]
). We will work with the following local version of this space.

Definition 2.2
.

The space
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\psi_{0}(D)f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
(1-\psi_{0}(D))f\in{\mathrm{BMO}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|\psi_{0}(D)f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|(1-\psi_{0}(D))f\|_{{\mathrm{BMO}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Moreover,
\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):={\langle}D\rangle^{-r}\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

Finally, let
r=l+t>0
for
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
t\in(0,1]
. For
r\notin{{\mathbb{N}}}
, we denote by
C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the space of
f\in C^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with bounded derivatives up to order
l
, such that that
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f
is Hölder continuous with exponent
t
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|=l
. Moreover,
C^{l,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of
f\in C^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with bounded derivatives up to order
l
, such that
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f
is Lipschitz for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|=l
.

It is instructive to compare these spaces
using embeddings, cf.
[
27
]
. For example,

C^{r+\varepsilon}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subsetneq\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subsetneq C^{r}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, let

r=l+t>0
for
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
t\in(0,1]
. Then

\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subsetneq C^{r}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

if
r\notin{{\mathbb{N}}}
, i.e. if
t\in(0,1)
, and

(2.5)

C^{l,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subsetneq\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subsetneq C^{r}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

if
r\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, i.e. if
t=1
.

For
X
one of the function spaces defined so far, we let
X_{{\mathrm{loc}}}
consist of those
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\psi f\in X
for each
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

2.3.
Rough symbols

In this subsection we consider versions of the symbols from (
2.1
) that have limited regularity in the spatial variable
x
, as measured in terms of the function spaces from the previous subsection.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
r>0
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
\delta\in[0,1]

and
X\in\{C^{r}_{*},\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}\}
. Then
XS^{m}_{1,\delta}
consists of those
p:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that the following properties hold:

(1)

p(x,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}}{\langle}\xi\rangle^{-m+|\alpha|}|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}p(x,\xi)|<\infty

for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
;

(2)

\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}p(\cdot,\xi)\in X(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
,
and

\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\langle}\xi\rangle^{-m+|\alpha|-r\delta}\|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}p(\cdot,\xi)\|_{X(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty.

Clearly, we can extend the definition of the pseudodifferential operator
from (
2.2
) to symbols
p
as in Definition
2.3
, at least as a map
p(x,D):\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We will also work with symbols that have less regularity than an element from
S^{m}_{1,\delta}
for
\delta<1
, but more than a typical element of
S^{m}_{1,1}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
m,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in S^{m}_{1,1}
. Then
p
has
reduced order

\mu
if there exists an
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
\partial_{x}^{\beta}p\in\tilde{S}^{\mu+|\beta|}_{1,1}
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\beta|\geq M
.

Finally, we describe a smoothing procedure that decomposes a rough symbol as a sum of a smooth term and a rough term of lower differential order. Let
(\psi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
be the Littlewood–Paley decomposition from (
2.4
).
Let
r>0
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,0}
and
\delta\in(0,1]
, and for
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
set

p^{\sharp}_{\delta}(x,\xi):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big(\psi_{0}(2^{-\delta k}D)p(\cdot,\xi)\big)(x)\psi_{k}(\xi)

and

(2.6)

p^{\flat}_{\delta}(x,\xi):=p(x,\xi)-p^{\sharp}_{\delta}(x,\xi)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big((1-\psi_{0})(2^{-\delta k}D)p(\cdot,\xi)\big)(x)\psi_{k}(\xi).

We will almost exclusively deal with the case where
\delta=1
, so for simplicity of notation we write
p^{\sharp}:=p^{\sharp}_{1}
and
p^{\flat}:=p^{\flat}_{1}
.

This decomposition connects Definitions
2.3
and
2.4
, in the following manner.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
r>1
,
m\in\mathbb{R}

and
p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,0}
. Then
p^{\flat}\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m-r}_{1,1}
, and
p^{\sharp}\in\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
is such that
\partial_{x}^{\beta}p^{\sharp}\in\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
has reduced order
m-r+1
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\beta|=1
.
If
p
is real-valued, then
p^{\sharp}
is real-valued as well.

Proof.

The first statement is a special case of
[
23
, equation (3.27) in Chapter 1]
. Moreover, since
C^{r}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it follows from
[
22
, Proposition 1.3.D]
and its proof that
\partial_{x}^{\beta}p^{\sharp}\in S^{m}_{1,1}
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\beta|\leq 1
, while
\partial_{x}^{\beta}p^{\sharp}\in S^{m+|\beta|-r}_{1,1}
if
|\beta|>r
. Next, by applying
[
22
, Theorem 3.4.F]
to
\partial_{x}^{\beta}p^{\sharp}
and then relying on
[
14
, Theorem 9.4.2]
, it follows that one may in fact replace
S^{m}_{1,1}
and
S^{m+|\beta|-r}_{1,1}
by
\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
and
\tilde{S}^{m+|\beta|-r}_{1,1}
, respectively. Finally, since
\psi_{0}
is real-valued and radial, so is its inverse Fourier transform, which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
2.5
, one has
\partial_{x}^{\beta}p^{\sharp}\in\tilde{S}^{m+1}_{1,1}
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\beta|=2
. This observation will be used in the proof of Theorem
3.10
, and it follows from the same reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
2.5
.

2.4.
Paradifferential calculus

In this subsection we collect the basic results from paradifferential calculus which will be used to prove our main results.

The following mapping properties of rough pseudodifferential operators constitute one part of the proof of our main result.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
r>0
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
\delta\in[0,1]

and
p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,\delta}
. Then

(2.7)

p(x,D):H^{s+m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for all
-(1-\delta)r<s<r
.
Moreover, if
p=q^{\flat}
for some
q\in\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then (
2.7
) holds for all
0\leq s\leq r
, with
m=-r
.

Proof.

The first
statement is a special case of
[
16
, Theorem 2.3]
.

For the second statement,
first note that

\displaystyle q^{\flat}(x,D)f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-\psi_{0}(2^{-k}D))q(x)\psi_{k}(D)f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\psi_{j}(D)q(x)\psi_{k}(D)f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\psi_{j}(D)q(x)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\psi_{k}(D)f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\psi_{0}(2^{-j+1}D)f(x)\psi_{1}(2^{-j+1}D)q(x)

for all
f\in H^{s-r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by (
2.4
). Hence
[
22
, Theorem 3.5.F]
yields the second statement for
r\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, with the natural extension to
r=0
. In fact, for such
r
there exists a
C_{1}\geq 0
independent of
q

such that

(2.8)

\|q^{\flat}(x,D)f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{1}\|q\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|_{H^{s-r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for all
f\in H^{s-r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
0\leq s\leq r
.

To obtain the second statement for
r\in(0,\infty)\setminus{{\mathbb{N}}}
, we use complex interpolation.
Let
\lfloor r\rfloor\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
be the largest integer smaller than
r
.
For
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
0\leq\Real(z)\leq 1
and for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set

q_{z}(x):=e^{(-z+r-\lfloor r\rfloor)^{2}}{\langle}D\rangle^{-z+r-\lfloor r\rfloor}q(x).

Then
q_{z}\in\mathcal{H}^{\lfloor r\rfloor+\Real z,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
\|q_{z}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\lfloor r\rfloor+\Real z,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{2}\|q\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for a
C_{2}\geq 0
independent of
z
and
q
. Hence the endpoint cases of (
2.8
)
yield

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|q_{z}^{\flat}(x,D)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{2}\|q\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|_{H^{-(\lfloor r\rfloor+\Real(z))}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|q_{z}^{\flat}(x,D)f\|_{H^{\lfloor r\rfloor+\Real(z)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{2}\|q\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\Real z\in\{0,1\}
and all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If
f
in addition has compact Fourier support, then it is straightforward to check that the map
z\mapsto q_{z}^{\flat}(x,D)f
is continuous on
\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid 0\leq\Real z\leq 1\}
with values in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and holomorphic on
\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid 0<\Real z<1\}
.

Finally, note that
q_{r-\lfloor r\rfloor}=q
, and recall that the Schwartz functions with compact Fourier support are dense in
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence one can apply interpolation of analytic families of operators (see e.g.
[
15
, Theorem 2.1.7]
) to (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), to obtain the required statement at the endpoints
s=0
and
s=r
, respectively. The intermediate values of
s
follow from the first statement in the proposition.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

Clearly
S^{m}_{1,1}\subseteq C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,1}
for all
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
r>0
. Hence, by Proposition
2.7
and duality, any
p\in\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
satisfies
p(x,D):H^{s+m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

By using Lemma
2.5
and this well-known fact, which in fact characterizes
\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
(see
[
14
, Theorem 9.4.2]
), to deal with
q^{\sharp}
for a given
q\in\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and by applying the second statement in
Proposition
2.7
to
q^{\flat}
,
we find that multiplication by
q
acts boundedly on
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
-r\leq s\leq r
.

The following proposition will be used to deal with the elliptic region in our main result. For the next two results, recall the definition of
\hat{p}
from (
1.1
).

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
m,\mu,s\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
p\in S^{m}_{1,1}
have reduced order
\mu
, and suppose that
\hat{p}
is a well-defined element of
C^{1}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o)
. Let
u\in H^{s+\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
\operatorname{WF}^{s+m}u\subseteq\Sigma_{\hat{p}}\cup\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)u
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
[
14
, Theorem 9.6.7]
, given that
p
is non-characteristic at
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
if and only if
(x,\xi)\notin\Sigma_{\hat{p}}
.
∎

Finally, the following key proposition on propagation of singularities for
\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
symbols is essentially a special case of
[
14
, Theorem 11.3.4]
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
m,\mu,s\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
p\in\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
be such that
\partial_{x}^{\beta}p\in\tilde{S}_{1,1}^{m}
has reduced order
\mu+1
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\beta|=1
, such that
\Imag p\in\tilde{S}^{m-1}_{1,1}
, and such that
\hat{p}
is a well-defined element of
C^{1}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o)
.
Let
u\in H^{s+\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and set

U:=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\mid H_{\hat{p}}(x,\xi)\neq 0,(x,\xi)\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)u\}.

Then, for each
(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u\cap U
, there exists a null bicharacteristic
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of
\hat{p}
, and
a\in[-\infty,0)
and
b\in(0,\infty]
, such that
\gamma(0)=(x,\xi)
,
\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u\cap U
for all
t\in(a,b)
, and
\gamma(t)
does not converge to a limit in
U
as
t\to a
or
t\to b
.

3.
Propagation of singularities

In this section we will prove our main results on propagation of singularities for rough symbols.

3.1.
Main result

With the results on paradifferential calculus from Section
2.4
in hand, we can easily prove our main result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
r>1
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
and

-r<s<r
.
Let
p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,0}
be such that
\Imag p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m-1}_{1,0}
, and such that
\hat{p}
is a well-defined element of
C^{1}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o)
. Set

(3.1)

\sigma:=\begin{cases}s-r&\text{if }0<s<r,\\
\varepsilon-r&\text{if }-r<s\leq 0,\end{cases}

for
\varepsilon>0
, and let
u\in H^{\sigma+m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then

(3.2)

\operatorname{WF}^{s+m}u\subseteq\Sigma_{\hat{p}}\cup\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)u.

Also set

U:=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o\mid H_{\hat{p}}(x,\xi)\neq 0,(x,\xi)\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)u\}.

Then, for each
(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u\cap U
, there exist
a\in[-\infty,0)
and
b\in(0,\infty]
, and a null bicharacteristic
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of
\hat{p}
, such that
\gamma(0)=(x,\xi)
,
\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u\cap U
for all
t\in(a,b)
, and
\gamma(t)
does not converge to a limit in
U
as
t\to a
or
t\to b
.

Suppose additionally that

p(x,\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)\xi^{\alpha}
for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, where
a_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|\leq m
, and
a_{\alpha}
is real-valued if
|\alpha|=m
. Then the statements above hold for all
-r\leq s\leq r
, with
\varepsilon=0
.

Note that the bicharacteristic in Theorem
3.1
might converge to a point in the null set of
H_{\hat{p}}
. Of course, in this case the bicharacteristic can be extended indefinitely in a trivial manner (see also Remark
3.2
).

Proof.

First note that
p(x,D)u\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cup L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is well defined, by Proposition
2.7
and Remark
2.8
.

Write
p=p^{\sharp}+p^{\flat}
, as in (
2.6
). Then, by Lemma
2.5
,
p^{\sharp}\in\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
is such that, for all
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\beta|=1
, the symbol
\partial_{x}^{\beta}p^{\sharp}\in\tilde{S}^{m}_{1,1}
has reduced order
m-r+1
, and as such also reduced order
\mu+1
for any
\mu\geq m-r
.
Moreover,
\Imag p^{\sharp}=(\Imag p)^{\sharp}\in\tilde{S}^{m-1}_{1,1}
and
p^{\flat}\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m-r}_{1,1}
. In particular, since
p^{\flat}
has lower differential order,

(3.3)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-m}p^{\sharp}(x,\lambda\xi)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-m}p(x,\lambda\xi)-\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-m}p^{\flat}(x,\lambda\xi)=\hat{p}(x,\xi)

for all
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
. Also,

(3.4)

\operatorname{WF}^{s}p^{\sharp}(x,D)u=\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)u,

as follows by relying on Proposition
2.7
to see that
p^{\flat}(x,D)u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The required statements are now a consequence of Propositions
2.9
and
2.10
, applied to
p^{\sharp}

and in combination with (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If the bicharacteristic
\gamma
in the statement converges to an element of
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
satisfying
H_{\hat{p}}(x,\xi)=0
, then
(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
and
\gamma
may be extended trivially within
\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
after reaching this limit. Hence one obtains from Theorem
3.1
a null bicharacteristic which can only exit
\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
by exiting
\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)u
.

Remark 3.3
.

One can also add rough lower-order perturbations in Theorem
3.1
,
by analyzing the proof. For example, let
r>1
and suppose that
p=p_{0}+p_{1}
for
p_{0}\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,0}
and
p_{1}\in C^{r-1}_{*}S^{m-1}_{1,0}
such that
\Imag p_{0}\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m-1}_{1,0}
, and such that
\hat{p_{0}}
is a well-defined element of
C^{1}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o)
. Then
p^{\sharp}
still satisfies the conditions of Propositions
2.9
and
2.10
with
\mu=m-r
, and
p^{\flat}(x,D):H^{\sigma+m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is bounded for all
-(r-1)<s<r-1
. Hence the conclusion of Theorem
3.1
still holds, albeit for a smaller range of
s
. Of course, the endpoints
s=-(r-1)
and
s=r-1
of this Sobolev interval can again be included under conditions as in Theorem
3.1
, although these are now only required of
p_{1}
.
If one wants to allow
\varepsilon=0
in (
3.1
) for
s\leq 0
, then additional assumptions should also be made on
p_{0}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Theorem
3.1
is stated for symbols in
C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,0}
, but one can obtain an analogous result for
C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,\theta}
symbols with
\theta\in(0,1)
. To do so, it suffices to modify Lemma
2.5
. Namely, for
p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,\theta}
,
the term
p^{\sharp}
has the same properties as in Lemma
2.5
, while
p^{\flat}\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m-(1-\theta)r}_{1,1}
. Hence one can repeat the proof of Theorem
3.1
and obtain an extension of that result, with
\sigma
replaced by

\sigma_{\theta}:=\begin{cases}s-(1-\theta)r&\text{if }0<s<r,\\
\varepsilon-(1-\theta)r&\text{if }-r<s\leq 0.\end{cases}

Remark 3.5
.

If
\hat{p}\in C^{1,1}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o)
, then bicharacteristics of
\hat{p}
are unique.
In fact, by Osgood’s Theorem (see
[
25
, Proposition 1.A.2]
), slightly less than
C^{1,1}
regularity guarantees uniqueness. For example,
\hat{p}\in C^{2}_{*,{\mathrm{loc}}}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o)
suffices (see also
[
23
, Section 3.11]
).

Remark 3.6
.

The statement on propagation of singularities in Theorem
3.1
and Remark
3.2
is equivalent to the following statement. Under the conditions of Theorem
3.1
, suppose that
(x,\xi)\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)u
has the following property. For any null bicharacteristic
\gamma:I\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of
\hat{p}
with
\gamma(0)=(x,\xi)
, where
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is an interval containing
0
such that
\gamma
is maximally extended within the complement of
\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)
, the intersection of
\gamma(I)
and the complement of
\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
is nonempty. Then
(x,\xi)\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
.

With a bit of extra work, one can deduce from Theorem
3.1
the following statement on propagation of regularity, which differs from Remark
3.6
in the sense that one obtains regularity along a specified interval, instead of merely at a single point.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
r>1
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
and

-r<s<r
.
Let
p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,0}
be
such that
\Imag p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m-1}_{1,0}
, and such that
\hat{p}
is a well-defined element of
C^{1}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o)
.
Let
\sigma
be as in (
3.1
) for
\varepsilon>0
, and let
u\in H^{\sigma+m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
(x,\xi)\in\Sigma_{\hat{p}}
. Suppose that there exist
0<T<T_{0}\leq\infty
such that the following holds. For every null bicharacteristic
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of
\hat{p}
satisfying
\gamma(0)=(x,\xi)
, one has
\gamma(t)\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)u
for all
0\leq t<T_{0}
, and there exists a
t_{\gamma}\in[T,T_{0})
such that
\gamma(t_{\gamma})\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
. Then
\gamma(t)\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
for every such bicharacteristic and for all
0\leq t\leq T
.

Suppose additionally that
p(x,\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)\xi^{\alpha}
for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, where
a_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|\leq m
, and
a_{\alpha}
is real-valued if
|\alpha|=m
. Then the statement above holds for all
-r\leq s\leq r
, with
\varepsilon=0
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
be a null bicharacteristic of
\hat{p}
satisfying
\gamma(0)=(x,\xi)
, and let
0\leq t\leq T
. Note that any bicharacteristic
\gamma_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of
\hat{p}
satisfying
\gamma_{0}(t)=\gamma(t)
yields, by concatenation, a bicharacteristic
\gamma^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of
\hat{p}
such that
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=(x,\xi)
and
\gamma^{\prime}(t^{\prime})=\gamma_{0}(t^{\prime})
for all
t\leq t^{\prime}<T_{0}
.

Suppose that
\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
. By the previous observation and by Remark
3.2
, there then exists a null bicharacteristic
\gamma^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
of
\hat{p}
satisfying
\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\gamma(t)
and
\gamma^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u
for all
t\leq t^{\prime}<T_{0}
. This contradicts the assumptions.
∎

3.2.
Auxiliary results

Theorem
3.1
is formulated for operators defined over the full space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, whereas for applications to e.g. manifolds one is often interested in equations on domains
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We will not delve into subtleties regarding rough pseudodifferential operators on domains or manifolds, but we do include a corollary of Theorem
3.1
concerning differential operators on domains.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
r>1
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and

-r<s<r
.
Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|\leq m
, let
a_{\alpha}\in C^{r}_{*,{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
be such that
a_{\alpha}
is real-valued if
|\alpha|=m
. Set
p(x,\xi):=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)\xi^{\alpha}
and
p_{m}(x,\xi):=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}(x)\xi^{\alpha}
, for
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\Omega
. Let
\sigma
be as in (
3.1
) for
\varepsilon>0
, and let
u\in H^{\sigma+m}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
. Then

(3.5)

\operatorname{WF}^{s+m}u\subseteq\Sigma_{p_{m}}\cup\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)u.

Also, set

U:=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\Omega\setminus o\mid H_{p_{m}}(x,\xi)\neq 0,(x,\xi)\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)u\}.

Then, for each
(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u\cap U
, there exist
a\in[-\infty,0)
and
b\in(0,\infty]
, and a null bicharacteristic
\gamma:(a,b)\to T^{*}\Omega\setminus o
of
p_{m}
, such that
\gamma(0)=(x,\xi)
,
\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u\cap U
for all
t\in(a,b)
, and
\gamma(t)
does not converge to a limit in
U
as
t\to a
or
t\to b
.

Suppose additionally that
(a_{\alpha})_{|\alpha|\leq m}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then the statements above hold for all
-r\leq s\leq r
, with
\varepsilon=0
.

Proof.

First note that
p(x,D)u\in H^{\sigma}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)\cup L^{2}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is well defined. Indeed, given
x\in\Omega
and a real-valued
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
satisfying
\psi\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
x
, let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
be real-valued and such that
\varphi\equiv 1
on
\mathrm{supp}(\psi)
. Then

(3.6)

p(y,D)u(y)=\psi(y)p(y,D)u(y)=\psi(y)p(y,D)(\varphi u)(y)

for all
y\in\Omega
near
x
. Hence Proposition
2.7
and Remark
2.8
, applied to
\psi p\in C^{r}_{*}S^{m}_{1,0}
and
\varphi u\in H^{\sigma+m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, imply that
\psi p(x,D)u\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cup L^{2}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Next, observe that
\hat{p}=p_{m}
,
and similarly for
\psi p
.
Hence, for (
3.5
)
one can again apply
(
3.6
), this time in combination with Theorem
3.1
.
Indeed, if
(x,\xi)\notin\Sigma_{p_{m}}\cup\operatorname{WF}^{s}p(x,D)u
then
(x,\xi)\notin\Sigma_{\psi p_{m}}\cup\operatorname{WF}^{s}\psi p(x,D)(\varphi u)
, and (
3.2
) implies that
(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m}\varphi u
. Since
\varphi\equiv 1
near
x
, one has
(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m}u
.

The second statement follows in a similar manner. Set

U_{0}:=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o\mid H_{\psi p_{m}}(x,\xi)\neq 0,(x,\xi)\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s}\psi p(x,D)(\varphi u)\}.

Then, for any
(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u\cap U
, there exist
a_{0}\in[-\infty,0)
and
b_{0}\in(0,\infty]
, and a null bicharacteristic
\gamma_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
of
\psi p_{m}
, such that
\gamma_{0}(0)=(x,\xi)
and
\gamma_{0}(t)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}\varphi u\cap U_{0}
for all

.
For
|a_{0}|
and

small enough, one in fact has
\gamma_{0}(t)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u\cap U
for all

, and
\gamma_{0}:(a_{0},b_{0})\to T^{*}\Omega\setminus o
is a null bicharacteristic of

. Since
\operatorname{WF}^{s+m-1}u\cap U
is closed is
U
, this suffices.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

As in Remark
3.3
, one can include lower-order terms of lower regularity in Corollary
3.8
,
and in some cases these will only affect the size of the Sobolev interval for
s
. For example, with notation as in Corollary
3.8
, suppose that

(a_{\alpha})_{|\alpha|=m}\subseteq C^{r}_{*,{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)

and

(a_{\alpha})_{|\alpha|<m}\subseteq C^{r-1}_{*,{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
. Then the conclusion of Corollary
3.8
holds for all
-(r-1)<s<r-1
.
Moreover, if
(a_{\alpha})_{|\alpha|<m}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{r-1,\infty}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
, then one may include
s=-(r-1)
and
s=r-1
, and one may let
\varepsilon=0
in (
3.1
) if also
(a_{\alpha})_{|\alpha|=m}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
.

Finally, we include a result for second-order operators in divergence form. Such operators can be dealt with using Corollary
3.8
and Remark
3.9
, but doing so would lead to a smaller Sobolev interval than we will now obtain.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
r>1
and

-r-1<s<r-1
.
Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open, let
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\subseteq C^{r}_{*,{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
be real-valued, and set
P:=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}a_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}
and
p(x,\xi):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}
for
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\Omega
. Set

(3.7)

\tau:=\begin{cases}s-r&\text{if }-1<s<r-1,\\
-1+\varepsilon-r&\text{if }-r-1<s\leq-1,\end{cases}

for
\varepsilon>0
, and let
u\in H_{{\mathrm{loc}}}^{\tau+2}(\Omega)
. Then

\operatorname{WF}^{s+2}u\subseteq\Sigma_{p}\cup\operatorname{WF}^{s}Pu.

Also, set

U:=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\Omega\setminus o\mid H_{p}(x,\xi)\neq 0,(x,\xi)\notin\operatorname{WF}^{s}Pu\}.

Then, for each
(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+1}u\cap U
, there exist
a\in[-\infty,0)
and
b\in(0,\infty]
, and a null bicharacteristic
\gamma:(a,b)\to T^{*}\Omega\setminus o
of
p
, such that
\gamma(0)=(x,\xi)
,
\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{WF}^{s+1}u\cap U
for all
t\in(a,b)
, and
\gamma(t)
does not converge to a limit in
U
as
t\to a
or
t\to b
.

Suppose additionally that
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then the statements above
hold for all
-r-1\leq s\leq r-1
, with
\varepsilon=0
.

Proof.

We first consider the case where
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\subseteq C^{r}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u\in H^{\tau+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The boundedness properties of multiplication by
a_{ij}
, contained in Proposition
2.7
and Remark
2.8
, imply that
Pu\in H^{\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cup H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is well defined. Apply the symbol decomposition to each
a_{ij}
, to write

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=p^{\sharp}(x,D)-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(\partial_{x_{i}}a_{ij}^{\sharp})(x,D)\partial_{x_{j}}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}a_{ij}^{\flat}(x,D)\partial_{x_{j}}

\displaystyle=q(x,D)-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}a_{ij}^{\flat}(x,D)\partial_{x_{j}},

where
q(x,\xi):=p^{\sharp}(x,\xi)-i\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}a_{ij}^{\sharp}(x,\xi)\xi_{j}
for
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

By Lemma
2.5
and Remark
2.6
, for all
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\beta|=1
, the symbol
\partial_{x}^{\beta}q\in\tilde{S}_{1,1}^{2}
has reduced order
3-r
.
Moreover,
\Imag q\in\tilde{S}^{1}_{1,1}
and
\hat{q}=\hat{p}=p\in C^{1}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o)
, where we used the embedding
C^{r}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. On the other hand,
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}a_{ij}^{\flat}(x,D)\partial_{x_{j}}:H^{\tau+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is bounded, by Proposition
2.7
. Hence
\operatorname{WF}^{s}q(x,D)u=\operatorname{WF}^{s}Pu
, and one can again apply Propositions
2.9
and
2.10

to conclude the proof under these global assumptions.

For general
\Omega
,
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\subseteq C^{r}_{*,{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
and

3.8
and rely on what we have already shown.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

Of course, in
Corollary
3.8
and Theorem

,
as in Remark

4.
Wave equations

In this section we use the results from the previous section to recover and improve some results in the literature concerning wave equations with rough coefficients.

4.1.
Operators in standard form

Let
r>1
,
let
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n},(b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq C^{r}_{*,{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
be real-valued, and let
(c_{j})_{j=0}^{n}\subseteq C^{r-1}_{*,{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
. Set

p(t,x,\tau,\xi):=\tau^{2}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}(t,x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}(t,x)\xi_{j}\tau+c_{0}(t,x)\tau+\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\xi_{j}

for
(t,x,\tau,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then Corollary
3.8
and Remark
3.9
yield propagation of
H^{s+1}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
regularity of
u\in H^{\sigma+2}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
, for all
-(r-1)<s<r-1
, with
\sigma
as in (
3.1
) for
\varepsilon>0
.
Moreover, if
(c_{j})_{j=0}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{r-1,\infty}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
, then one may include
s=-(r-1)
and
s=r-1
, and if additionally
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n},(b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{r,\infty}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
, then one may also let
\varepsilon=0
.

The same statement follows from
[
23
, Section 3.11]
for
r=2
, but with
\sigma
replaced by
\sigma+\delta
for any
\delta>0
. Moreover, for
r=2
the endpoint case
\delta=0
is dealt with in
[
20
]
, albeit for
s\in\{0,1\}
and
under the assumption that the equation is uniformly hyperbolic in
t
, that the
a_{ij}
and
b_{j}
have second derivatives in
L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}
, and that the
c_{j}
have first derivatives in
L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}
. Note that the latter regularity assumptions complement those in this article, in the sense that in
[
20
]
less regularity is required in the time variable but more in the spatial variable.

In fact, in
[
20
]
a limiting procedure is used to extend the main results to coefficients that are piecewise continuous in time. It is then shown that the a priori regularity assumption
u\in H^{s}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
on the solution
is sharp for such piecewise continuous coefficients. On the other hand, there is no distinguished time variable in the setting of Corollary
3.8
. Moreover, the sharpness example from
[
20
]
does not directly apply here, given that Corollary
3.8
requires in each variable more regularity than piecewise continuity.

4.2.
Bounded Ricci tensor

Let
M
be a compact
n
-dimensional
C^{1}
manifold without boundary, with a continuous metric tensor
g
, i.e. an inner product on tangent vectors.
Then the Laplace–Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g}:H^{1}(M)\to H^{-1}(M)
is well defined, as is the notion of a harmonic function on
M
.

Suppose that there exist
1<r<2
and

c,K_{0},K_{1}>0
with the following property. For every
x\in M
, there exists an open neighborhood
U_{x}\subseteq M
of
x
and a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism
\Phi_{x}:B\to U_{x}
such that
\Phi_{x}(0)=x
, and such that the pull-back of
g=(g_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
to
B
satisfies
K_{0}^{-1}I\leq(g_{ij}(y))_{i,j=1}^{n}\leq K_{0}I
for all
y\in B
, as well as
g_{ij}(0)=\delta_{ij}
and

(4.1)

g_{ij}\in C^{r-1}(B)\cap H^{1}(B),

with
\|g_{ij}\|_{C^{r-1}(B)\cap H^{1}(B)}\leq K_{1}
, for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Here
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the open ball around
0
of radius
c
,
I
is the identity matrix and
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta. Then, as noted in e.g.
[
23
, Section 3.9]
,
M
is in fact a manifold of class
C^{r}\cap H^{2}
, and the original coordinate maps
\Phi_{x}
are
C^{r}\cap H^{2}
diffeomorphisms, for every
x\in M
. Moreover, without loss of generality, one may assume that each
\Phi_{x}^{-1}
is harmonic.

Now, the connection
1
-form is
\Gamma:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{j}dx_{j}
in local coordinates on
B
, where
\Gamma_{j}
is the matrix with entries

{\Gamma^{a}}_{bj}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{n}g^{am}(\partial_{j}g_{bm}+\partial_{b}g_{jm}-\partial_{m}g_{bj}),

for
1\leq a,b,j\leq n
. Note that
\Gamma
is well defined and square integrable on
B
, by (
4.1
). Also, the Riemannian curvature tensor is

R:=d\Gamma+\Gamma\wedge\Gamma,

and it has components
{R^{a}}_{bjk}\in H^{-1}(B)+L^{1}(B)
for
1\leq a,b,j,k\leq n
, again by (
4.1
). Finally, the Ricci curvature tensor is the
2
-form with components

\mathrm{Ric}_{bk}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{R^{j}}_{bjk}\in H^{-1}(B)+L^{1}(B),

for
1\leq b,k\leq n
. Note that if the Riemannian tensor is bounded, then so is the Ricci tensor. Moreover, if
(g_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\subseteq C^{1,1}(B)
, then the Riemannian tensor is bounded.

On the other hand, using that we are working in harmonic coordinates, one can show as in
[
23
, Section 3.10]
that if

(\mathrm{Ric}_{bk})_{b,k=1}^{n}\subseteq L^{\infty}(B)
,
or more generally if
(\mathrm{Ric}_{bk})_{b,k=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{0,\infty}(B)
, then
(g_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{2,\infty}(B)
. And, by
[
23
, Proposition 3.1.13]
, the latter in turn implies that the harmonic coordinate atlas is of class
W^{3,q}
for any
q<\infty
. As such,
M
is then in fact a manifold of class
W^{3,q}
.

By arguing as in
[
8
, Section 2]
, the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(M)
can now be defined in local coordinates for
-3\leq s\leq 3
, and for
|s|\leq 2
the resulting spaces coincide with those defined using the spectral decomposition of
\Delta_{g}
on
L^{2}(M)
. In turn, the spaces
H^{s}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}\times M)
can then also be defined for
-3\leq s\leq 3
, in local coordinates.

Finally, recall that the wave equation
\partial_{t}^{2}u=\Delta_{g}u
can be written as

\partial_{t}\sqrt{|g|}\partial_{t}u-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}\big(\sqrt{|g|}g^{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}u\big)=0,

in local coordinates, where
|g|:=\det(g_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
and
(g^{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
is the inverse of
(g_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
. Hence
Theorem
3.10
and Remark
3.11

yield, with
\tau
as in (
3.7
), propagation of
H^{s+1}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}\times M)

regularity of solutions
u\in H^{\tau+2}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}\times M)
to
\partial_{t}^{2}u=\Delta_{g}u
, for all

-3\leq s\leq 1
.
This improves upon the corresponding result in

[
23
, Section 3.11]
, which contains the same statement but with
\tau
replaced by
\tau+\delta
for any
\delta>0
, and without the endpoints of the Sobolev interval for
s
.

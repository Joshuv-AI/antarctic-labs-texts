New examples of CR submanifolds for the 2 2 -jet determination problem
Florian Bertrand, Martin Kolář and Francine Meylan

Abstract
. This paper addresses two questions related to mapping problems. In the first part of the paper, we discuss some recent results regarding the 2 2 -jet determination for biholomorphisms between smooth (weakly) pseudoconvex hypersurfaces; in light of those, we formulate a general problem and illustrate it with examples. In the second part of the paper, we provide a new example of a real submanifold of codimension 7 7 in ℂ 10 \mathbb{C}^{10} that is not strictly pseudoconvex and for which its germs of CR automorphisms are determined by their 2 2 -jets at a given point.



Introduction

Cartan’s Uniqueness Theorem asserts that any
holomorphic map
F
sending a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
into itself, that fixes some
p
of the domain with
F^{\prime}(p)=I,
is the identity map. (
F^{\prime}(p)
is the holomorphic derivative of
F
at
p
and
I
is the identity operator).

Instead of considering holomorphic maps between bounded domains in
\mathbb{C}^{N},
the question whether an analogous statement to Cartan’s Theorem holds for germs of holomorphic maps in

\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
between boundaries of domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1},
or more
generally, between generic submanifolds of codimension
d
in
\mathbb{C}^{n+d},
has attracted a lot of research. See for instance the surveys
[
25
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
11
]
and the references therein.

We are interested in the following two questions:

(1)

Under what conditions (of regularity) can we compute the optimal number of derivatives needed to uniquely determine germs of holomorphic maps, sending a germ at
p
of a hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
into itself fixing
p?

(2)

Under what conditions is a germ of a CR automorphism of a sufficiently smooth Levi nondegenerate generic real submanifold in
\mathbb{C}^{n+d}

uniquely determined by its
2
-jet at
p?

A fundamental result by Chern and Moser shows that germs of biholomorphisms sending a real-analytic
hypersurface of type
2
(in the sense of Kohn and Bloom-Graham) into itself are uniquely determined by at most
2
-jets if its Levi form is nondegenerate. See
[
6
]
. Notice that
2
-jets
(and not less) are required in the case of the Lewy hypersurface.
We emphasize that Question (2) is also relevant for the class of biholomorphisms, since
the above result by Chern and Moser becomes false without any hypothesis on the Levi map (see e.g.
[
22
]
,
[
10
]
).

We start with Question (1). The second and third authors, with Zaitsev, have generalized in
[
16
]
this result to any smooth hypersurface of type
m>2,
showing that at most
(m-1)
-jets are needed to determine uniquely automorphisms, provided that its associated model hypersurface (the homogeneous hypersurface ”given” by the minimal homogeneous non pluriharmonic polynomial of degree
m
with respect to the tangential variables in the Taylor expansion of the smooth hypersurface) is holomorphically nondegenerate. Notice that
(m-1)
-jets
(and not less) are required for the hypersurface given by

\{(z_{1},z_{2},w)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\ |\ {\sf Im}\,w={\sf Re}\,z_{1}\bar{z}_{2}^{m-1}\}.

Kim and the third author in
[
7
]
consider the above question under the natural assumption of
pseudoconvexity
. They study the case of smooth (weakly) pseudoconvex hypersurfaces under the condition that the Lie algebras of infinitesimal CR automorphisms of their (holomorphically nondegenerate) models have nonvanishing top degree components. See Definition
1.3
. In particular, they show that only
2
-jets are needed to determine uniquely their automorphisms.

One of the goals of this paper is to revisit
[
7
]
in order to explore the general case, that is, when the top degree component of the Lie algebra of infinitesimal CR automorphisms of models of weakly pseudoconvex hypersurfaces possibly vanishes. An example that illustrates this situation is

Example 0.1
.

Let
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
be given by

{\sf Im}\,w=|z_{1}|^{8}+|z_{1}|^{6}{{\sf Re}\,{z_{1}}}^{2}+|z_{2}|^{8}.

The reader can check that
M
is a holomorphically nondegenerate weakly pseudoconvex model with vanishing of the top degree component of its symmetry Lie algebra. See Section
1.1
for the precise definitions and results.

In light of
[
7
]
, we may address the following problem:

•

Is every automorphism of a pseudoconvex smooth hypersurface with holomorphically nondegenerate model
uniquely determined by its
2
-jet?

We now focus on the Question (2).
Let
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{4}
generic real submanifold of real codimension
d\geq 1
through
p=0
given locally by the following system of equations

\begin{cases}{\sf Re}\,w_{1}=\,^{t}\bar{z}A_{1}z+O(3)\\
\ \ \ \ \vdots\\
{\sf Re}\,w_{d}=\,^{t}\bar{z}A_{d}z+O(3)\end{cases}

where
A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}
are Hermitian matrices of size
n
, and in the remainder O(3), the variables
z
and
{\sf Im}\,w
are respectively of weight one and two.
We assume that
M
is a
strongly Levi nondegenerate real submanifold, that is, such that
\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}A_{j}
is invertible for some
b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
For
a\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
sufficiently small, we
consider the following matrix equation

PX^{2}+AX+\,^{t}{\overline{P}}=0,

where
P:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}{{a}_{j}}A_{j}
and
A:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(b_{j}-a_{j}-\overline{a_{j}})A_{j},

and we let
X
be the unique
n\times n
matrix solution
of this equation with
\|X\|<1.
The matrix
X
plays a crucial role in the study of stationary discs, a technique used for the CR automorphism
2
-jet determination problem (see e.g.
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
4
]
).
In particular, it leads to an important factorization result that generalizes a classical factorization theorem due to Lempert in
[
21
]
for positive definite matrix
A
(see Lemma 2.3 in
[
4
]
).

In
[
5
]
, we introduced the notion of
\mathfrak{D}(a)
-nondegeneracy
(see definition
2.3
) that generalizes the notion of
\mathfrak{D}
-nondegeneracy
, a sufficient condition to obtain the
2
-jet determination of CR automorphisms (see
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
).
If
M
is strictly pseudoconvex, that is, when the matrix
A
defined above can be chosen to be positive definite, Tumanov showed in
[
24
]
that it is automatically
\mathfrak{D}(a)
-nondegenerate at
0
for some small
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.
He then showed, using this condition, that any germ of a CR automorphism of class
C^{2}
is
uniquely determined by its
2
-jet at
p.
We also note that if
M
is a Levi nondegenerate hypersurface, then
M
is
\mathfrak{D}
-nondegenerate at
0
; the first author and Blanc-Centi proved in this case that any germ of a CR automorphism of class
C^{3}
is
uniquely determined by its
2
-jet at
p
(see
[
1
]
).

We formulated in
[
5
]
the following conjecture:

•

If
M
is
\mathfrak{D}(a)
-nondegenerate at
0
for some small
a\in\mathbb{C}^{d},
then any germ of CR automorphism of class
C^{3}
is
uniquely determined by its
2
-jet at
0.

In this paper, we present an example of a model quadric which is
\mathfrak{D}(a)
-nondegenerate at
0
for some small
a\neq 0,
which is not strictly pseudoconvex nor
\mathfrak{D}
-nondegenerate at
0,
and which satisfies the above conjecture.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 1 deals with the first question. In subsections
1.2
and
1.3
, we give new proofs of the results of
[
7
]
, starting with the important class of (weakly) pseudoconvex hypersurfaces whose models have Lie algebras of symmetries with nonvanishing top degree components: namely, the hypersurfaces whose models are given by ” sums of squares”. In subsection
1.4
, we discuss the general case in
\mathbb{C}^{3},
and obtain a positive answer to the above question for (weakly) pseudoconvex hypersurfaces with ”single chain” models (Theorem
1.17
).
The general case in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
will be treated in a forthcoming paper. The second question is addressed in Section 2 where we essentially present an example of a quadric in
\mathbb{C}^{10}
which is not strictly pseudoconvex nor

\mathfrak{D}
-nondegenerate, and is
\mathfrak{D}(a)
-nondegenerate at
0
for some small
a
, and such that its germs of CR automorphisms are
uniquely determined by their
2
-jets at
0
.

1.
Part I: Results on the
2
-jet determination under the assumption of pseudoconvexity.

1.1.
Preliminaries

Let
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be a smooth hypersurface,the light
and
p\in M
be a point of finite type
m
in the sense of Bloom-Graham
[
8
]
.
We consider
local holomorphic coordinates
(z,w)
vanishing at
p
,
where
z=(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n})
and
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
,

w=u+iv
. The hyperplane
\{v=0\}
is assumed to be tangent to

M
at
p
, hence
M
is described near
p
as the graph of a uniquely
determined real valued function

v=\psi(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n},u),\ d\psi(0)=0.

We may assume (
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
)
that

(1.1)

\psi(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n},u)=Q(z,\bar{z})+o(|u|+|z|^{m}),

where
Q(z,\bar{z})
is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree
m
with no pluriharmonic terms. Note that removing the pluriharmonic terms requires a change of coordinates in the variable
w
, which ”absorbs” the pluriharmonic terms into
w
.

Reflecting on the choice
of variables in (
1.1
), the
variables
w
,
u
and
v
are given weight
1,

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial{w}}
weight
-1,
while
the complex tangential variables
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
are given weight
\dfrac{1}{m}
and
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial{z_{j}}},\ j=1,\dots n,
weight
-\dfrac{1}{m}.

For this choice of weights,

Q(z,\bar{z})
is a weighted homogeneous polynomial of weighted degree one, while
o(|u|+|z|^{m})
contains weighted homogeneous polynomials of weighted degree strictly bigger than one.

Recall that the derivatives of weighted order
\kappa
are those of the form

\dfrac{\partial^{|\alpha|+|\hat{\alpha}|+l}}{\partial z^{\alpha}\partial\bar{z}^{\hat{\alpha}}\partial u^{l}},\ \ \ \kappa=l+\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\alpha_{j}+\hat{\alpha}_{j}).

We may then rewrite equation (
1.1
) as

(1.2)

v=\psi(z,\bar{z},u)=Q(z,\bar{z})+o(1),

where
o(1)
denotes a smooth function whose derivatives at
0
of weighted order less than or equal to
one vanish. This motivates the following definition

Definition 1.1
.

[
14
]
Let
M
be given by (
1.2
).
We define its
associated
model hypersurface

by

M_{0}:=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\ |\ v=Q(z,\bar{z})\}.

We say that
M
is a smooth perturbation of
M_{0}.

We denote by
\text{\rm Aut}(M,p),
the stability group of
M,
that is, the germs at
p

of biholomorphisms mapping
M
into itself and fixing
p,

and by
\text{\rm hol}(M,p),
the set of germs of holomorphic vector fields in

\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
whose real part
is tangent to
M
. An element of
\text{\rm hol}(M,p)
is called a real-analytic infinitesimal CR automorphism at
0
, or a symmetry.

We write

\text{\rm hol}(M_{0},0)=\bigoplus_{\mu\geq-1}\mathfrak{g}_{\mu},

where
\mathfrak{g}_{\mu}
consists of weighted homogeneous vector fields
of weight
\mu.

We recall the following definition

Definition 1.2
.

A real-analytic hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is
holomorphically nondegenerate
at
p\in M
if there is no germ at
p
of a holomorphic vector field
X
tangent to
M.

It is shown in
[
16
]
that, if
M_{0}
is holomorphically nondegenerate, the Lie algebra of real-analytic infinitesimal CR automorphisms
\text{\rm hol}(M_{0},0)

has the following weighted grading:

(1.3)

\text{\rm hol}(M_{0},0)=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1/m}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\bigoplus_{\tau=1}^{m-2}\mathfrak{g}_{\tau/m}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1-1/m}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1},

with the following explicit description of the
graded components:

(1)

\mathfrak{g}_{-1}=\{a\partial_{w}\ |\ a\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
,

(2)

\mathfrak{g}_{-1/m}=\{\sum_{j}a^{j}\partial_{z_{j}}+g(z)\partial_{w}\ |\ a^{j}\in\mathbb{C},\,g\in\mathbb{C}_{m-1}[z],\,2i\sum(a^{j}Q_{z_{j}}+\bar{a}^{j}Q_{\bar{z}_{j}})=g-\bar{g}\}
,

(3)

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\{\sum_{j}f^{j}(z)\partial_{z_{j}}+aw\partial_{w}\ |\ f^{j}\in\mathbb{C}_{1}[z],\,a\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\sum(f^{j}Q_{z_{j}}+\bar{f}^{j}Q_{\bar{z}_{j}})=aQ\}
,

(4)

\mathfrak{g}_{c}:=\bigoplus_{\tau=1}^{m-2}\mathfrak{g}_{\tau/m}=\bigoplus_{\tau=1}^{m-2}\{\sum_{j}f^{j}(z)\partial_{z_{j}}\ |\ f^{j}\in\mathbb{C}_{\tau+1}[z],\,\sum(f^{j}Q_{z_{j}}+\bar{f}^{j}Q_{\bar{z}_{j}})=0\}
,

(5)

\mathfrak{g}_{1-1/m}=\{\sum_{j}(f^{j}(z)+a^{j}w)\partial_{z_{j}}+g(z)w\partial_{w}\ |\ a^{j}\in\mathbb{C},\,f^{j}\in\mathbb{C}_{m}[z],\,g\in\mathbb{C}_{m-1}[z],\,\sum_{j}a^{j}\partial_{z_{j}}+g(z)\partial_{w}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1/m},\,2\sum(f^{j}Q_{z_{j}}+\bar{f}^{j}Q_{\bar{z}_{j}}+2iQ(a^{j}Q_{z_{j}}-\bar{a}^{j}Q_{\bar{z}_{j}}))=Q(g+\bar{g}))\}
.

(6)

\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\{\sum_{j}f^{j}(z)w\partial_{z_{j}}+aw^{2}\partial_{w}\ |\ f^{j}\in\mathbb{C}_{1}[z],\,a\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\sum_{j}f^{j}(z)Q_{z_{j}}=aQ\}
.

We recall that elements in
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
with
a=0
are called
rigid
. We now introduce the following definition

Definition 1.3
.

Under the assumption that
M_{0}
is holomorphically nondegenerate,
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is called the
top degree component of the Lie algebra
\text{\rm hol}(M_{0},0).

Zaitsev asked whether
the above structure results may be improved under the

pseudoconvexity
assumption.

We remark that
\mathfrak{g}_{c}=\{0\}
in the Levi nondegenerate case.
In
\mathbb{C}^{2},
we have
\mathfrak{g}_{c}=\{0\}
for every model (see e.g.
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
).

Notice also that in
\mathbb{C}^{2},

there are three
different cases, two exceptional ones and one generic. The two exceptional ones
are the circular model
{\sf Im}\,w=|z|^{2k}
with a
4-
dimensional symmetry Lie algebra,
and the tubular model
{\sf Im}\,w=({\sf Re}\,z)^{k}
, which has a three dimensional
symmetry Lie algebra. All other models fall into the generic case, which admits
a two dimensional Lie algebra of infinitesimal symmetries.

The above description of the Lie algebra of a holomorphically nondegenerate model hypersurface leads, in particular, to the following theorem

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
M
be a smooth perturbation of
M_{0}
given by (
1.1
). Suppose that
M_{0}
is holomorphically nondegenerate and
\mathfrak{g}_{c}=0.

Then every element of
\text{\rm Aut}(M,0)
is uniquely determined by its
2
-jet.

1.2.
The case of a sum of squares polynomial model

We start with the case of a polynomial model given by a sum of squares, that is given by (
1.1
), with

Q=\sum_{l=1}^{k}P_{l}\overline{P_{l}},

where
P_{l},\ l=1,\dots,k,
are homogeneous holomorphic polynomials of the same degree. Note that this model is pseudoconvex. We will need the following definition

Definition 1.5
.

A linear vector field in the variables
z
given in Jordan normal form is
real diagonal
(resp.
imaginary diagonal
) if it is diagonal with all the entries real (resp. imaginary). Without loss of generality, we say that
X
is
real diagonal
(resp.
imaginary diagonal
) if its Jordan normal form is real (resp. imaginary) diagonal.

Let
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
be rigid, given in normal Jordan form after a possible linear change of coordinates in the variables
z.
We have
X=X^{Re}+X^{Im}+X^{Nil},
where
X^{Re}
(resp.
X^{Im}
) is real (resp. imaginary) diagonal, and
X^{Nil}
is nilpotent. Recall that if
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},
then
X^{Re}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},

X^{Im}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},
and
X^{Nil}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
(see e.g.
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
).

The following theorem deals with symmetries of weight
0,
elements of
\mathfrak{g}_{0},
also called rotations. The result appears in Kim-Kolář
[
7
]
. Here, we propose a different proof.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
7
]

Let
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be a polynomial model hypersurface given by (
1.1
), where
Q
is given by a sum of squares, that is,

Q=\sum_{l=1}^{k}P_{l}\overline{P_{l}},

where
P_{l},\ l=1,\dots,k,
are homogeneous holomorphic polynomials of the same degree. If
M_{0}
is holomorphically nondegenerate, then there is no real diagonal symmetry and no nilpotent symmetry in
\mathfrak{g}_{0}.

Proof.

Let
X
be given in its Jordan form after a possible change of coordinates in
z.
We have
X=X^{Re}+X^{Im}+X^{Nil}.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
M_{0}
is given by
{\sf Im}\,w=\sum_{l=1}^{k}P_{l}\overline{P_{l}}.
Suppose that
X^{Re}\neq 0.

We have

X^{Re}=\sum\lambda_{j}z_{j}\partial_{z_{j}},\ \lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R}.

We claim that
X^{Re}(P_{l})=0,
for every
l.
Indeed, if
P_{l}
contains a term
cz^{\alpha},\ c\neq 0,
then the model contains a term
Dz^{\alpha}\overline{z^{\alpha}},\ D>0.

Since
X^{Re}
is a rotation, it implies

D\sum\lambda_{j}(2\alpha_{j})=0,

and hence
X^{Re}(P_{l})=0.
But it means that

X^{Re}(\sum_{l=1}^{k}P_{l}\overline{P_{l}})=0,

which is impossible since
M_{0}
is holomorphically nondegenerate.

Now we deal with the nilpotent part
X^{Nil}.
Suppose that
X^{Nil}\neq 0.
Let
s>1
be minimal such that
{(X^{Nil})}^{s}(P_{j})=0,
for all
j.
We have

{\sf Re}\,{(X^{Nil}(\sum_{j=1}^{k}P_{j}\overline{P_{j}}))}=0.

Applying
{(X^{Nil})}^{s-1}\overline{(X^{Nil})}^{s-2}
to the above equation, we obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{k}{(X^{Nil})}^{s-1}(P_{j})\overline{(X^{Nil})^{s-1}({P_{j}})}=0.

Since this is a sum of square,
(X^{Nil})^{s-1}({P_{j}})=0,
for every j. This is a contradiction with the fact that
s
was chosen to be minimal.
∎

We recall the following theorem from
[
19
]
(see also Proposition 4.1 in
[
7
]
).

Theorem 1.7
.

[
19
]
Let
M_{0}
be given by (
1.1
) be holomorphically nondegenerate with nonvanishing top degree component
\mathfrak{g}_{1}.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
contains no real diagonal rotations. Then
\mathfrak{g}_{c}=0.

If
M_{0}
is a sum of squares polynomial model, then the vector field
X
given by

X:=\sum z_{j}\partial_{z_{j}}

satisfies condition (6) on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
in (
1.3
). Hence, using Theorem
1.4
, Theorem
1.6
and Theorem
1.7
, we obtain the following theorem

Theorem 1.8
.

[
7
]

Let
M_{0}
given by (
1.1
) be a sum of squares polynomial model that is holomorphically nondegenerate. Then every element of
\text{\rm Aut}(M,0),
where
M
is any smooth perturbation of

is uniquely determined by its

-jet.

1.3.
The general case of a pseudoconvex polynomial model

In this section, we show that pseudoconvex polynomial models do not have either real diagonal rotations. Of course, there are examples of pseudoconvex models which are not sums of squares models, as illustrated by:

Example 1.9
.

Let
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be given by

{\sf Im}\,w=|z|^{8}+|z|^{6}{{\sf Re}\,z}^{2}.

Notice that
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=0
in this case.

We start with the following lemma

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
F_{j}
and
G_{j}
be two holomorphic homogeneous polynomials of different degrees, for every
j,\ j=1,\dots,k.
Then
\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\sf Re}\,F_{j}\overline{G_{j}}
changes sign or is identically equal to zero.

Proof.

Indeed, for some given
z_{0},

F_{j}(\lambda z_{0})=\lambda^{m}F_{j}(z_{0}),\ \ G_{j}(\lambda z_{0})=\lambda^{n}G_{j}(z_{0}),\ m\neq n.

Integrating on the circle
\gamma:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\ |\ |\lambda|=r\},
we obtain
\int_{\gamma}{F_{j}(\lambda z_{0})\overline{G_{j}(\lambda z_{0})}d\lambda}=0.

∎

We then have

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be a pseudoconvex model hypersurface given by

{\sf Im}\,w=Q(z,\bar{z}).

If
M_{0}
is holomorphically nondegenerate, then there is no real diagonal rotation.

Proof.

Suppose that there is a symmetry
X
that is real diagonal. Without loss of generality, we assume that
M_{0}
is given in Jordan coordinates for
X,
so that

X=\sum d_{j}z_{j}\partial_{z_{j}},\ d_{j}\in\mathbb{R}.

We decompose
Q
as

(1.4)

Q(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}z^{\alpha^{j}}{\overline{z}}^{\alpha^{j}}\ +\ \sum_{{\alpha^{l}}\neq{\alpha^{k}}}c_{l,k}z^{\alpha^{l}}{\overline{z}}^{\alpha^{k}}.\ \

Since
X
is a rotation, for any monomial
z^{\alpha^{l}}\overline{z^{\alpha^{k}}}
in the expansion of
Q,
we have

(1.5)

X(z^{\alpha^{l}})+\overline{X(z^{\alpha^{k}})}=0.

It implies that

X(\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}z^{\alpha^{j}}{\overline{z}}^{\alpha^{j}})=0.

If there is no second sum, this not possible since
M_{0}
is holomorphically nondegenerate.
Otherwise, there exists a nonzero term in
z^{\alpha^{l}}{\overline{z}}^{\alpha^{k}}
that is not a square (in the second sum) for which
X(z^{\alpha^{l}})\neq 0.
Hence
{\overline{X}}({\overline{z}}^{\alpha^{k}})\neq 0,
using (
1.5
). Computing the Levi form of
M_{0}
in the direction of
\overline{X},
we obtain that the Levi form of the model

{\sf Im}\,w=\sum_{{\alpha^{l}}\neq{\alpha^{k}}}c_{l,k}z^{\alpha^{l}}{\overline{z}}^{\alpha^{k}}

in the direction
\overline{X}
is not identically equal to zero. Indeed, its Levi form can be written as

\sum_{{\alpha^{l}}\neq{\alpha^{k}}}c_{l,k}X(z^{\alpha^{l}}){\overline{X}}({\overline{z}}^{\alpha^{k}}).

Using Lemma (
1.10
), this contradicts the assumption that
M_{0}
is pseudoconvex.
∎

Remark 1.12
.

The existence of nilpotent rotations in the case of general pseudoconvex model hypersurfaces is not known. Nevertheless, they do not occur in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
as it is shown by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
be a pseudoconvex model given by

{\sf Im}\,w=Q(z,\bar{z}).

If
M
is holomorphically nondegenerate, then the nilpotent part of the Jordan form of a rotation vanishes.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
M_{0}
admits a nilpotent rotation. After a possible linear change of coordinates, it is shown in

[
13
]
that we have, for
z=(z_{1},z_{2}),

Q(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\left(z_{1}\overline{z_{2}}+z_{2}\overline{z_{1}}\right)^{j}Q_{j}(z_{2},\overline{z_{2}}),\ m\geq 1,

where
Q_{j}
is a real valued polynomial in

1.4.

As noticed for the case of sums of squares models, Theorem

Examples constructed from Example

In

Definition 1.14

[

Let

A pair of finite sequences of holomorphic weighted homogeneous polynomials

Y(U^{N})=0,\ \ Y(U^{j})=c_{j}U^{j+1},\ \ j=1,\dots,N-1,

where

,

are nonzero complex constants, which satisfy

c_{j}=-\bar{d}_{N-j}.

If the two sequences are identical we say that
\{U^{1},\dots,U^{N}\}
is a
symmetric
Y
-chain
.

Theorem 1.15
.

[
17
]
Let

be a holomorphically nondegenerate hypersurface given by (

Then

\{{{{V_{j}^{1}},\dots,{V_{j}^{N_{j}}}}}\}

This shows that the simplest polynomial model to study
for which there exists a nonzero

is given by one single chain, that is, is given by the following expression

where

and

Observe that the vanishing of

V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We also emphasize that, unlike the notion of

-nondegeneracy which imposes

-nondegeneracy condition does not impose any restriction on the codimension.

We conjectured in

that if

\mathfrak{D}(a)
-nondegenerate at
0

-jet map
\mathfrak{j}_{1}
a local diffeomorphism at

for some

, and that, accordingly, (germs at

are uniquely determined by their

-jet at
0

While we are not yet a stage where we can settle this conjecture, we provide in the next section an example of a
\mathfrak{D}(a)
-nondegenerate quadric which is
neither strongly pseudoconvex nor
\mathfrak{D}
-nondegenerate and for which the
1
-jet map
\mathfrak{j}_{1}
is a local diffeomorphism at
(a,V)
. This example (and its perturbations) is
then the first known example where
2
-jet determination of CR automorphisms holds outside of the realm of previously known submanifolds, namely
\mathfrak{D}
-nondegenerate ones
[
3
]
and strongly pseudoconvex ones
[

]

from which it follows that the matrices

We now prove i. We will show that

is
\mathfrak{D}(a)
-nondegenerate at

for

for some small

. Define for

The

) with

where

We will now show that for

For

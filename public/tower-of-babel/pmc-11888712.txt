Oral Health Misinformation on Youtube – A Content Analysis
Authors: Mohammed Meera Riyaz, V Gousalya, V Anu, S Prashanth, S Prabhu Ram, VJ Keerthi Varshini, R Rajsanjay
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:With the rise of social media and widespread smartphone use, information is now readily accessible worldwide. However, the credibility of this information often remains unchecked. Few studies have evaluated oral health content on online platforms. However, YouTube, despite its popularity, is frequently overlooked. This study aims to identify and analyze oral health misinformation on YouTube.Methods:A search was done on YouTube using common keywords related to oral health through Google chrome browser with incognito mode to limit the bias associated with Google accounts. The videos obtained were subjected to analysis using a coding framework and both auditory and visual content were examined together by two coders to ensure a comprehensive coding process. All the videos were classified either as accurate information (useful) or misleading information.Results:A total of 72 videos were included in the study, of which about 55% of them had misleading content. About 75% of the videos containing misleading information were created by non-professionals and only about 15% of the videos containing misleading information were created by medical professionals. It was surprising to note that the misleading information had more positive engagement metric (137429) than the useful information (71207).Conclusion:From the content analysis, it was found that there was a large amount of misleading information on oral health in YouTube. Therefore, it’s crucial to address the potential risks of misleading content and to increase public awareness about the impact of following such misleading information.

INTRODUCTION

In the past two decades, internet has significantly altered the ways in which information is disseminated and accessed. With the advent of many social media platforms and rampant access to information via devices like smartphones, information is now readily available regardless of the geographical location and time zones. Accordingly, Internet has become a vital tool for people from all sectors to seek health related information.[1] People utilize internet to identify symptoms, find different treatment modalities and to make health related decisions.[2]

Although online health information has numerous benefits such as the opportunities for improving the level of health literacy and staying updated about latest health related practices, it presents a major threat to public health since it’s arduous to regulate the health information available on the internet.[3] This has led to widespread availability of misinformation and disinformation. Previous research indicates that inaccurate health information spreads rapidly and easily via social media when compared with proven scientific data.[4]

Identical to the availability of general health-related misinformation, the presence of oral health-related misinformation is growing in an alarming rate.[5] Ranging from misguided home remedies to inaccurate product claims, oral health misinformation is seen in different forms.[6] Eventually this will lead to improper dental care routine among people resorting to internet for oral health information resulting in dental morbidities. Vulnerable population such as those hailing from lower socioeconomic status and living in areas where regular dental care is not possible, might consume majority of the oral health information online due to financial and geographical limitations.[7] Unproven myths and inauthentic dental treatment modalities are easily shared in many social media platforms like Facebook, Instagram, and YouTube. Misinformation about dental remedies can lead viewers to attempt Do-It-Yourself (DIY) treatments that may be harmful to their oral health.[8]

Previous study in this regard, characterized the spread of oral health misinformation available in the Internet.[6] Many popular websites were analyzed to identify the misinformation. However, YouTube, a popular video sharing platform was not included in that study. It is pertinent to investigate the videos available in YouTube for presence of any misinformation, since anecdotal evidence suggests that people acquire information readily when projected in a video format which has attractive lighting and sounds when compared to the information available in plain text. Considering the detrimental impact of spread of misinformation through YouTube videos, the aim of this study was to identify and analyze oral health related misinformation available in YouTube.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Data collection

A search was done on YouTube using common keywords related to oral health misinformation such as “Oral Hygiene Home Remedies,” “Dental Cavity Home Remedy,” and “Tooth Pain Remedies” in May 2024. The search was performed using the Google Chrome web browser in incognito mode to limit the bias associated with any Google account. Browsing history, cookies, and cache were deleted before each search session. Default search filters were made to remain unaltered to reflect the typical search results accessible to all users. The objective was to identify the most prevalent search outcomes that users encounter when utilizing these search terms. Notably, previous research suggests that majority of individuals do not proceed beyond the third page of search results.[9] Hence, videos were collected from only the first three pages of search results. The URLs of relevant videos were compiled and recorded in an Excel spreadsheet, along with the account type responsible for uploading each video. Only videos from English and the local language Tamil which discussed the mentioned keywords were included to ensure geographic specificity and exact interpretation by the authors. Duplicate videos and videos older than 3 years at the time of analysis were excluded.

Content analysis

The videos obtained were subjected to analysis using a framework previously adopted in other study focused on social media content analysis.[10] Both auditory and visual content were examined together to ensure a comprehensive coding process.[11] Each event of data collection involved a two-member coder team, utilizing the provided codebook to classify all videos as either containing accurate information (useful) or inaccurate/misleading content (misleading). For instance, videos were categorized as misleading if they propagated unsupported claims such as using charcoal improves teeth color, which contradicts established current evidence.[12]

When disagreements emerged about coding and could not be resolved among the coders through discussion, the senior author served as a third reviewer to help reach a consensus. Furthermore, account holders of the videos were examined to determine their user type such as authorized persons (dental professionals who mentioned that they were qualified in their accounts) and unauthorized persons (individuals without formal dental education).

Statistical analysis

The data obtained was entered in Microsoft Excel Spreadsheet and subjected to statistical analysis. All analyses were carried out using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (version 26, IBM, Chicago, USA). The normal distribution of the data was checked using Shapiro Wilk test. As data didn’t follow normal distribution, non-parametric test of significance was used. Chi-square test was used as test of association between the variables and Man-Whitney test was used to compare engagement metric scores between content with misleading information and useful information. The level of significance was set at 5%.

DISCUSSION

Misinformation on YouTube regarding dental remedies is a significant concern, as it can lead viewers to adopt ineffective or potentially harmful practices. The current study found that 75% of the videos analyzed contained misleading information from unprofessional sources. Similarly, Gaikwad A et al.[13] reported that many YouTube videos offering dental remedies lack credible sources and are often presented by individuals without professional dental training. This dissemination of misleading information can encourage viewers to try dangerous oral hygiene practices. Watching YouTube videos that advocate quick-fix dental remedies can discourage individuals from approaching the professionals for dental care. Videos of such nature concentrate only on alleviating symptoms rather than addressing the underlying dental issues.[14]

Recent studies analyzing information retrieved from search results have indicated that employing specific medical terminology can lead to more valuable and accurate information compared to misleading content.[15] Conversely, widely searched terms such as “cancer” often yield more misinformation than beneficial content.[16] YouTube, as a huge platform for video sharing yielding monetary benefit to the video creator, sees a daily influx of new videos on healthcare topics. Consequently, it becomes challenging to ensure that even seemingly useful information is applicable to individual health conditions, given the variability in personal health needs.[17]

Medical professionals offer valuable content backed by years of education, training, and clinical experience, which enhances the credibility of the information they deliver. Their reliance on scientific research and clinical evidence ensures that their content is grounded in sound principles and reputable sources, helping viewers make well-informed health decisions.[18] Health misinformation shared by non-professionals often arises from the quest for monetary gain rather than a commitment to public well-being. These individuals frequently propagate misleading or inaccurate health information to attract views, increase engagement, and generate revenue through advertising or sponsorships.[19] Such financial incentives can lead to the spread of potentially harmful advice, as the primary objective shifts from delivering accurate, evidence-based information to maximizing profit.

Our study has few limitations. The search terms for misinformation were limited to home remedies to limit the search results and to focus entirely one particular form of misinformation. Different search terms could have yielded varied search results. Further, there may be difficulty in assessing the actual impact of misleading health information on viewers’ health decisions and behaviors, given the indirect nature of such influences.

CONCLUSION

Misleading oral health information is prevalent on YouTube, with over half of the videos analyzed containing inaccurate content, predominantly from non-professionals. Due to its higher engagement among viewers, these misleading videos pose risks by promoting harmful practices. Improved content regulation and enhanced digital literacy are crucial to address misinformation and ensure access to reliable health information.

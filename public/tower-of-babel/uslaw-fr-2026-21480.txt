    Pursuant to Section 19(b)(1) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934

I. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Terms of Substance 
of the Proposed Rule Change

    Cboe Exchange, Inc. (the ``Exchange'' or ``Cboe Options'') proposes 
to amend its Fees Schedule to (i) replace the fixed, premium-based 
transaction fees applicable to Customer, Clearing Trading Permit Holder 
Proprietary, Broker-Dealer (including Joint Back-Office, Non-Trading 
Permit Holder Market Maker, and Professional), and Cboe Options Market-
Maker/DPM/LMM orders executed electronically and removing liquidity in 
Mini-S&P 500 Index (``XSPBX'') binary options with a tier-based 
constant transaction fee methodology applicable to Index Binaries, as 
defined in new footnote 55, while retaining the existing per-contract 
transaction fees for Cboe Options Market-Maker/DPM/LMM liquidity-adding 
and manual orders, and (ii) add Investors Exchange LLC (``IEX'') to fee 
code RD. The text of the proposed rule change is provided in Exhibit 5.

1. Purpose
    The Exchange proposes to amend its Fees Schedule, effective October 
1, 2026,\3\ to (1) amend the Binary Options \4\ rate table to replace 
the fixed, premium-based transaction fees currently applicable to XSPBX 
options with a tier-based constant transaction fee methodology 
applicable to Index Binaries \5\ for Customer, Clearing Trading Permit

transaction fees for Cboe Options Market-Maker/DPM/LMM liquidity-adding 
and manual orders, together with new footnote 55; and (2) add IEX to 
fee code RD \9\ within the Routing Fees section of the Fees Schedule.

    The Exchange proposes to amend the Rate Table--Binary Options to 
replace the fixed, premium-based transaction fees currently applicable 
to XSPBX options with a tier-based constant transaction fee methodology 
applicable to ``Index Binaries,'' under which transaction fees for 
Customer, Clearing Trading Permit Holder Proprietary, Broker-Dealer 
(including Joint Back-Office, Non-Trading Permit Holder Market Maker, 
and Professional), and Cboe Options Market-Maker/DPM/LMM orders 
executed electronically and removing liquidity, will be calculated 
using the following formula:

Transaction Fee = ((k*p*(1-p)*c)*m)

where k is the tier-based constant for the applicable capacity; p is 
the contract price ($0.01-$1.00); c is the number of contracts traded; 
and m is the contract multiplier (i.e., 100). The proposed tier-based 
constants (k) are as follows:
     Customer (capacity code C): 1.2% (fee code HC);
     Clearing Trading Permit Holder Proprietary, Broker-Dealer, 
Joint Back-Office, Non-Trading Permit Holder Market Maker, and 
Professional (capacity codes F, L, B, N, U, and J): 1.4% (fee code HD); 
and
     Cboe Options Market-Maker/DPM/LMM (capacity code M), 
Electronic, Removing Liquidity: 1.4% (fee code HB).
    Cboe Options Market-Maker/DPM/LMM orders executed electronically 
and adding liquidity and executed manually will continue to be assessed 
flat per-contract transaction fees of $0.05 (fee code HA) and $0.15 
(fee code HM), respectively, which are unchanged from the current fees. 
The Exchange believes it is reasonable to continue to assess these 
orders flat per-contract fees rather than fees under the proposed 
price-based methodology because Market-Makers, DPMs, and LMMs, unlike 
other market participants, are subject to continuous quoting and other 
obligations under the Exchange's Rules that require them to provide 
liquidity across the full range of contract prices, including in 
contracts priced near $0.50, where the proposed methodology would 
otherwise result in the highest fees. A flat, low per-contract fee for 
liquidity-adding electronic executions and manual executions provides 
Market-Makers, DPMs, and LMMs with fee certainty that does not vary 
with the price at which they are obligated to quote, which the Exchange 
believes encourages them to continue to provide tighter and deeper 
markets in Index Binaries, to the benefit of all market participants. 
Market-Maker/DPM/LMM electronic orders that remove liquidity do not 
further these obligations in the same manner and will therefore be 
subject to the proposed methodology (fee code HB), consistent with 
other non-Customer capacities. Maintaining the current flat fees for 
liquidity-adding and manual Market-Maker/DPM/LMM executions also avoids 
any fee increase for these participants as a result of the proposed 
change. The Exchange also proposes to adopt new footnote 55, which 
provides that ``Index Binaries include XSPBX,'' and to append footnote 
55 to each ``Index Binaries'' product designation in the new rate 
table. XSPBX will therefore continue to be the only binary option 
subject to the rate table following the proposed change.
    Under the proposed methodology, the transaction fee for an Index 
Binary is a function of p*(1-p), which is greatest when the contract 
price is $0.50 and declines symmetrically as the contract price 
approaches $0.00 or $1.00. The proposed methodology therefore preserves 
the general shape of the current premium-range fee structure, under 
which the highest fees apply to contracts priced near $0.50 and the 
lowest fees apply to contracts priced near $0.00 or $1.00, while 
replacing seven discrete premium ranges with a continuous calculation 
based on the actual contract price. The proposed constants are 
calibrated so that the maximum per-contract fee for each capacity is 
unchanged. For example, a Customer executing one contract at a price of 
$0.50 would be assessed ((0.012 x 0.50 x 0.50 x 1) x 100) = $0.30, 
which is identical to the current $0.30 fee applicable to Customer 
orders priced between $0.25 and $0.75 (fee code D4). Similarly, a 
Clearing Trading Permit Holder Proprietary order executing one contract 
at $0.50 would be assessed ((0.014 x 0.50 x 0.50 x 1) x 100) = $0.35, 
identical to the current $0.35 fee applicable to such orders priced 
between $0.25 and $0.75 (fee code N4). At other contract prices, the 
fee under the proposed methodology will generally be lower than the fee 
currently assessed for the applicable premium range. For example, a 
Customer order executing one contract at $0.25 would be assessed $0.225 
rather than $0.30; at $0.10, $0.108 rather than $0.20; and at $0.02, 
approximately $0.024 rather than $0.04. A Clearing Trading Permit 
Holder Proprietary order executing one contract at $0.25 would be 
assessed approximately $0.26 rather than $0.35; at $0.10, $0.126 rather 
than $0.25; and at $0.02, approximately $0.027 rather than $0.10. 
Because the proposed fee is calculated continuously rather than by 
premium range, the fee for contracts priced at the upper end of a 
current premium range may be modestly higher than the current fixed fee 
for that range (e.g., a Customer order executing one contract at $0.24 
would be assessed approximately $0.219 rather than $0.20), but will 
never exceed the current maximum per-contract fee for the applicable 
capacity.

    At each contract price, Cboe Options Market-Maker/DPM/LMM orders 
executed electronically and adding liquidity would continue to be 
assessed $0.05 per contract (fee code HA), and Cboe Options Market-
Maker/DPM/LMM orders executed manually would continue to be assessed 
$0.15 per contract (fee code HM). Because the transaction fee is 
calculated on a per-contract basis, the total fee for an execution of 
multiple contracts equals the applicable per-contract fee multiplied by 
the number of contracts traded. For example, a Customer order executing 
ten contracts at a contract price of $0.25 would be assessed ((0.012 x 
0.25 x 0.75 x 10) x 100) = $2.25.
    The Exchange believes that the proposed methodology will simplify 
and add transparency to binary option pricing on the Exchange by 
applying a single, objective calculation based on the contract price in 
place of multiple premium ranges, and will more closely align the 
transaction fee with the price of the contract at the time of 
execution. Additionally, by applying the new rate table to Index 
Binaries as a category, as defined in new footnote 55, the Exchange 
will be able to apply a consistent fee methodology across index binary 
options. Any future addition of a product to the definition of Index 
Binaries in footnote 55 would be the subject of a separate rule filing.
Addition of IEX to Fee Code RD
    The Exchange assesses fees in connection with orders routed away to 
various options exchanges. The Fees Schedule currently lists fee codes 
and their corresponding transaction fees for certain Customer orders 
routed to other options exchanges. Currently, under the Routing Fees 
section of the Fees Schedule, fee code RD is appended to Customer 
orders routed to AMEX, BOX, EDGX, MIAX, MX2, SPHR, or PHLX (excluding 
orders in SPY options routed to PHLX), as well as ETF and Equity 
options, and assesses a charge of $0.25 per contract.
    The Exchange's current approach to routing fees is to set forth in 
a simple manner certain sub-categories of fees that approximate the 
cost of routing to other options exchanges based on the cost of 
transaction fees assessed by each venue as well as costs to the 
Exchange for routing (i.e., clearing fees, connectivity and other 
infrastructure costs, membership fees, etc.) (collectively, ``Routing 
Costs''). The Exchange monitors the fees charged as compared to the 
costs of its routing services and adjusts its routing fees and/or sub-
categories to ensure that the Exchange's fees result in a rough 
approximation of overall Routing Costs, and are not significantly 
higher or lower in any area. Other options exchanges assess routing 
fees in a similar manner.
    The Exchange proposes to amend fee code RD to add applicable 
Customer orders routed to IEX, a national securities exchange that is 
launching an options market on October 2, 2026. As proposed, the 
description of fee code RD would read: ``Routed to AMEX, BOX, EDGX, 
IEX, MIAX, MX2, SPHR, PHLX (excluding SPY), ETF, Equity.'' The charge 
assessed per contract for fee code RD remains the same ($0.25) under 
the proposed rule change. The proposed change results in an assessment 
of fees that, given the fees of an away options exchange, is in line 
with the Exchange's current approach to routing fees, i.e., in a manner 
that approximates the cost of routing Customer orders to other away 
options exchanges, based on the general cost of transaction fees 
assessed by the sub-category of away options exchanges for such orders 
(as well as the Exchange's Routing Costs). The Exchange notes that 
routing through the Exchange is optional and that market participants 
will continue to be able to choose where to route applicable Customer 
orders.
2. Statutory Basis
    The Exchange believes the proposed rule change is consistent with 
the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (the ``Act'') and the rules and 
regulations thereunder applicable to the Exchange and, in particular, 
the requirements of Section 6(b) of the Act.\24\ Specifically, the 
Exchange believes the proposed rule change is consistent with the 
Section 6(b)(5) \25\ requirements that the rules of an exchange be 
designed to prevent fraudulent and manipulative acts and practices, to 
promote just and equitable principles of trade, to foster cooperation 
and coordination with persons engaged in regulating, clearing, 
settling, processing information with respect to, and facilitating 
transactions in securities, to remove impediments to and perfect the 
mechanism of a free and open market and a national market system, and, 
in general, to protect investors and the public interest. Additionally, 
the Exchange believes the proposed rule change is consistent with the 
Section 6(b)(5) requirement that the rules of an exchange not be 
designed to permit unfair discrimination. The Exchange also believes 
the proposed rule change is consistent with Section 6(b)(4) of the 
Act,\26\ which requires that Exchange rules provide for the equitable 
allocation of reasonable dues, fees, and other charges among its 
Trading Permit Holders and other persons using its facilities.

    As a general matter, the Exchange operates in a highly competitive 
market. The Securities and Exchange Commission (the ``Commission'')has 
repeatedly expressed its preference for competition over regulatory 
intervention in determining prices, products, and services in the 
securities markets. In Regulation NMS, the Commission highlighted the 
importance of market forces in determining prices and SRO revenues and, 
also, recognized that current regulation of the market system ``has 
been remarkably successful in promoting market competition in its 
broader forms that are most important to investors and listed 
companies.'' \27\ Market participants can readily direct order flow to 
competing venues if they deem fee levels at a particular venue to be 
excessive or incentives to be insufficient. Accordingly, competitive 
forces constrain the Exchange's transaction fees, and market 
participants can readily trade on competing venues if they deem pricing 
levels at those other venues to be more favorable.

    Index Binaries Transaction Fees. The Exchange believes the proposed 
tier-

[[Page 66595]]

based constant fee methodology for Index Binaries is reasonable. The 
proposed constants are calibrated so that the maximum per-contract fee 
for each capacity ($0.30 for Customer orders and $0.35 for Clearing 
Trading Permit Holder Proprietary, Broker-Dealer (including Joint Back-
Office, Non-Trading Permit Holder Market Maker, and Professional), and 
Cboe Options Market-Maker/DPM/LMM orders, other than Cboe Options 
Market-Maker/DPM/LMM orders adding liquidity or executed manually) is 
unchanged from the maximum fee under the current premium-range 
structure. At most contract prices, the proposed methodology will 
result in fees that are lower than those currently assessed for the 
applicable premium range, and while fees for contracts priced at the 
upper end of certain current premium ranges may be modestly higher, 
they will not exceed the current maximum fee for the applicable 
capacity. The proposed methodology is also reasonable because it bases 
the transaction fee on an objective, transparent calculation using the 
actual contract price, the number of contracts, and the contract 
multiplier, which more closely aligns the fee with the price of the 
contract at execution than a fixed fee applicable across a broad 
premium range. The Exchange believes it is reasonable to continue to 
assess Cboe Options Market-Maker/DPM/LMM orders executed electronically 
and adding liquidity, and Cboe Options Market-Maker/DPM/LMM orders 
executed manually, the existing flat fees of $0.05 and $0.15 per 
contract, respectively, which are unchanged. Market-Makers, DPMs, and 
LMMs are subject to continuous quoting and other obligations under the 
Exchange's Rules that other market participants are not, and those 
obligations require them to provide liquidity across the full range of 
contract prices, including in contracts priced near $0.50, where the 
proposed methodology would otherwise assess the highest fees. A flat, 
low per-contract fee gives these participants fee certainty that does 
not vary with the price at which they are obligated to quote, which the 
Exchange believes encourages them to continue to provide tighter and 
deeper markets in Index Binaries, to the benefit of all market 
participants. Market-Maker/DPM/LMM electronic orders that remove 
liquidity do not further these obligations in the same manner and are 
therefore subject to the proposed methodology, consistent with other 
non-Customer capacities. Retaining the current flat fees also ensures 
that Market-Makers, DPMs, and LMMs will not experience a fee increase 
as a result of the proposed change. The Exchange believes it is 
reasonable to adopt footnote 55 to define Index Binaries, as it makes 
clear the products to which the new rate table applies and currently 
includes only XSPBX, the product to which the current rate table 
applies.
    The Exchange believes the proposed methodology is equitable and not 
unfairly discriminatory because the same formula and the same tier-
based constant will apply to all market participants within each 
applicable capacity. The Exchange believes it is equitable and not 
unfairly discriminatory to apply a lower constant to Customer orders 
(1.2%) than to Clearing Trading Permit Holder Proprietary, Broker-
Dealer (including Joint Back-Office, Non-Trading Permit Holder Market

    Addition of IEX to Fee Code RD. The Exchange believes the proposed

a manner consistent with the Exchange's current approach to routing 
fees--i.e., in the most appropriate sub-category of fees that 
approximates the cost of routing to a group of away options exchanges 
based on the cost of transaction fees assessed by each venue as well as 
the Exchange's Routing Costs. The Exchange believes the proposed change 
is equitable and not unfairly discriminatory because all applicable 
Customer orders routed to IEX will automatically yield fee code RD and 
uniformly be assessed the corresponding fee. The Exchange notes that 
routing through the Exchange is voluntary, and market participants may 
choose to route orders to IEX directly or through other means.

B. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement on Burden on Competition

    The Exchange does not believe that the proposed rule change will 
impose any burden on competition that is not necessary or appropriate 
in furtherance of the purposes of the Act. The Exchange does not 
believe the proposed changes will impose any burden on intramarket 
competition that is not necessary or appropriate in furtherance of the 
purposes of the Act. The proposed tier-based constant fee methodology 
for Index Binaries will apply the same formula and the same constant to 
all market participants within each applicable capacity, and maintains 
the relative relationships among capacities that exist under the 
current premium-range structure. The proposed addition of IEX to fee 
code RD will apply automatically and uniformly to all Trading Permit 
Holders' applicable Customer orders routed to IEX, which will be 
assessed the same $0.25 per contract charge that applies to orders 
routed to other exchanges within fee code RD. To the extent Customer 
orders continue to be assessed lower fees than non-Customer orders, and 
Market-Maker orders adding liquidity in Index Binaries continue to be 
assessed lower flat fees, the Exchange believes such differentiation is 
consistent with longstanding industry practice and reflects the value 
that Customer order flow and Market-Maker liquidity provide to the 
market.
    The Exchange does not believe the proposed changes will impose any 
burden on intermarket competition that is not necessary or appropriate 
in furtherance of the purposes of the Act. As previously discussed, the 
Exchange operates in a highly competitive market in which market 
participants can

[[Page 66596]]

readily direct order flow to competing venues, including 17 other 
options exchanges and off-exchange venues, if they deem fee levels at a 
particular venue to be excessive or incentives to be insufficient. With 
respect to the proposed changes to the Index Binaries transaction fees, 
the Exchange notes that Index Binaries, which currently include only 
XSPBX, are proprietary products exclusively listed on the Exchange. 
Because Index Binaries are not listed or traded on any other exchange, 
the proposed changes to the Index Binaries transaction fees do not 
implicate intermarket competition. With respect to the addition of IEX 
to fee code RD, routing through the Exchange is optional, and the 
charge assessed under fee code RD is unchanged. The Exchange notes that 
its market share in the options market is subject to competition, and 
that, in such an environment, the Exchange must continually adjust its 
fees to remain competitive with other exchanges and to attract order 
flow. Moreover, the Commission has recognized that competition for 
order flow among exchanges is robust. In NetCoalition v. SEC, the D.C. 
Circuit stated that ``[n]o one disputes that competition for order flow 
is `fierce,''' and that, ``[a]s the SEC explained, `[i]n the U.S. 
national market system, buyers and sellers of securities, and the 
broker-dealers that act as their order-routing agents, have a wide 
range of choices of where to route orders for execution'; [and] `no 
exchange can afford to take its market share percentages for granted' 
because `no exchange possesses a monopoly, regulatory or otherwise, in 
the execution of order flow from broker dealers' . . . .'' \29\ 
Accordingly, the Exchange does not believe its proposed fee changes 
impose any burden on competition that is not necessary or appropriate 
in furtherance of the purposes of the Act.

Spectral quantum algorithm for passive scalar transport in shear flows
Philipp Pfeffer, Peter Brearley, Sylvain Laizet, Jörg Schumacher

Abstract
The mixing of scalar substances in fluid flows by stirring and diffusion is ubiquitous in natural flows, chemical engineering, and microfluidic drug delivery. Here, we present a spectral quantum algorithm for scalar mixing by solving the advection-diffusion equation in a quantum computational fluid dynamics framework. We derive exact gate decompositions of the advection and diffusion operators in spectral space. For all but the simplest one-dimensional flows, these operators do not commute. Therefore, we use operator splitting and construct quantum circuits capable of simulating arbitrary polynomial velocity profiles, such as the Blasius profile of a laminar boundary layer. Periodic, Neumann, and Dirichlet boundary conditions can be imposed with the appropriate quantum spectral transform plus additional constraints on the Fourier expansion. We evaluate our approach in statevector simulations of a Couette flow, plane Poiseuille flow, and a polynomial Blasius profile approximation to demonstrate its potential and versatility for scalar mixing in shear flows. The number of gates grows with, at most, the cubed logarithm of the number of grid points. This evaluation shows that spectral accuracy allows comparably large time steps even though the operator splitting limits the temporal order.

Spectral quantum advection step

The advection equation,
\partial\phi/\partial t+\vec{u}\cdot\nabla\phi=0
, inherently preserves the
L^{2}
-norm of the passive scalar
\phi
under an incompressible velocity field
\vec{u}
. To see this, consider the time derivative of the
L^{2}
-norm over a domain
\Omega

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}\,dV=2\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\,dV=-\int_{\Omega}\vec{u}\cdot\nabla(\phi^{2})\,dV

Applying the divergence theorem and noting that
\nabla\cdot\vec{u}=0
, this reduces to

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}\,dV=\int_{\partial V}\phi^{2}(\vec{u}\cdot\vec{n})\,dS

(2)

which vanishes under boundary conditions that nullify the boundary flux, such as periodicity or solid walls. Therefore, advection is inherently a unitary process. However, when the problem is discretized in space to facilitate the computation, discretization errors may result in a nonunitary evolution under certain conditions. For constant one-dimensional velocity fields, the unitary evolution is retained by central finite difference and spectral methods of discretization.
Brearley2024

2.2
Advection in laminar shear flow

In laminar wall-bounded flows, the velocity in the streamwise
x

u_{y}=u_{z}=0

3
Spectral quantum diffusion step

The heat (or diffusion) equation,
\partial\phi/\partial t=D\nabla^{2}\phi
, describes the molecular diffusion of a passive scalar field in a stationary medium. Unlike the advection equation, diffusive dynamics are inherently not unitary. This poses a fundamental challenge for simulating diffusion on quantum computers. However, diffusion typically occurs at a constant diffusivity
D
in most heat transfer or mixing problems, thus allowing for an efficient diagonal spectral decomposition. In this section, we will extend the methodologies presented in the previous section to simulate diffusion in a probabilistic framework.

3.1
Quantum circuit with spectral accuracy

For a single spectral mode
\hat{\phi}_{j}
with wavenumber
k_{j}
under constant diffusivity
D
, the one-dimensional heat equation for periodic boundary conditions simplifies to

\frac{d\hat{\phi}_{j}}{dt}=-Dk_{j}^{2}\hat{\phi}_{j},

(10)

with the solution
\hat{\phi}_{j}(t)=\exp(-Dk_{j}^{2}t)\hat{\phi}_{j}(0)
. By defining the diagonal negative semi-definite
H=\text{diag}(Dk_{j}^{2})
, Eq. (
10
) can be written in matrix form
d\hat{\phi}/dt=-H\hat{\phi}
with the solution
\hat{\phi}(t)=\exp(-Ht)\hat{\phi}(0)
. In a quantum mechanical framework, this corresponds to a probabilistic imaginary time evolution under a Hamiltonian
H
.

Simulating the discrete spectral operator
\exp(-Dk_{j}^{2}t)
requires implementing the exponential of the squared wavenumber. We will consider Fourier space as the spectral space to impose periodic boundaries. Other boundary conditions follow later in the section. The squared wavenumbers for the QFT are defined and ordered by

k_{j}^{2}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}\begin{cases}j^{2}&\text{when }0\leq j<\frac{N}{2}\,,\\
(j-N)^{2}&\text{when }\frac{N}{2}\leq j<N\,.\end{cases}

(11)

Defining
\beta=D(2\pi/L)^{2}t
, the nonunitary evolution operator can be written as the piecewise expression

e^{-Dk_{j}^{2}t}=\begin{cases}e^{-\beta j^{2}}&\text{when }0\leq j<\frac{N}{2}\,,\\
e^{-\beta(j-N)^{2}}&\text{when }\frac{N}{2}\leq j<N\,.\end{cases}

(12)

Since the index
j
has the binary expansion in Eq. (
4
),
j^{2}
has the expansion

\displaystyle j^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}2^{r}q_{r}\right)^{2}=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}2^{2r}q_{r}+2\sum_{s>r}^{n-1}2^{r+s}q_{r}q_{s}\,.

(13)

Therefore, the nonunitary evolution for
j<N/2
can be written as

e^{-\beta j^{2}}=\left[\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}e^{-2^{2r}\beta q_{r}}\right]\left[\prod_{s>r}^{n-1}e^{-\,2^{1+r+s}\beta q_{r}q_{s}}\right]\,.

(14)

These products can be implemented in a quantum circuit using a unitary block encoding since all of the exponents are negative, ensuring that the magnitudes are less than one. However, when following the same procedure for the

12

(j-N)^{2}=j^{2}-2Nj+N^{2}

-2jN

k_{j}^{2}
by mirroring and shifting the

11

can be written as
(2\pi/L)^{2}(j+1)^{2}

\displaystyle(j+1)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}2^{r}q_{r}+1\right)^{2}=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}2^{2r}q_{r}+2\sum_{r<s}2^{r+s}q_{r}q_{s}+2\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}2^{r}q_{r}+1\,,

in Eq. (

\phi^{\prime}=\phi-\overline{\phi}
, where the steady state scalar field
\overline{\phi}(x,y)=\phi_{\text{off}}+x\,\partial_{x}\overline{\phi}+y\,\partial_{y}\overline{\phi}
varies under the constant linear gradients
\partial_{x}\overline{\phi}
and
\partial_{y}\overline{\phi}
. Furthermore, it should be mentioned that the advection in the herein discussed shear flows are also unaffected by an inhomogeneity in y-direction as
\vec{u}=[u_{x}(y),0]
and
\overline{\phi}(x,y)=\overline{\phi}(y)
.

When implementing Neumann or Dirichlet boundary conditions via the QCT or QST respectively, the wavenumbers are defined and ordered as
k_{j}=\pi j/L
for
j\in\{0,1,\dots N/2-1\}
. The absence of negative wavenumbers simplifies the quantum circuit implementation, where diffusion can be implemented simply by the two product terms in Eq. (
14
). For simulations involving various boundary conditions, the definition of
\beta
is updated to
\beta_{1}=D(2\pi/L)^{2}t
for periodic boundary conditions, and
\beta_{2}=D(\pi/L)^{2}t
for Neumann or Dirichlet boundary conditions.

4
Spectral quantum advection-diffusion

So far, quantum circuits for the individual simulation of advection and diffusion have been introduced. When both the advection and heat equation are diagonalizable by the same spectral operators, the Fourier-transformed operators commute and the individual implementations
\exp(-iu_{x}k_{j}t)\exp(-Dk_{j}^{2}t)\lvert\hat{\phi}_{j}\rangle
correspond to the combined dynamics of
\exp(-iu_{x}k_{j}t-Dk_{j}^{2}t)\lvert\hat{\phi}_{j}\rangle
. However, this condition holds only for very simple flows like a global unidirectional velocity field.

A more general algorithm for simulating scalar transport can be obtained by combining the advection equation and heat equation algorithms into an algorithm for the advection-diffusion equation by operator splitting. In the simplest case for an evolution time
t
discretized by
N_{t}
time steps, the Lie-Trotter product formula is

e^{t(X+Y)}=\lim_{N_{t}\to\infty}\left(e^{tX/N_{t}}e^{tY/N_{t}}\right)^{N_{t}}\,,

(21)

where
X
and
Y
are the non-commuting Fourier-transformed evolution operators for advection and diffusion, respectively. Defining the time step
\Delta t=t/N_{t}
, the error of the method can be estimated with the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff expansion
Magnus1954

\log\left(e^{X\Delta t}e^{Y\Delta t}\right)=X\Delta t+Y\Delta t+\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2}[X,Y]+O(\Delta t^{3})\,,

(22)

where the commutator
[X,Y]=XY-YX
. The local truncation error term is
O(\Delta t^{2})
resulting in a first-order accuracy across the entire simulation time
t
. The error of the operator splitting can be estimated by analyzing the leading order error term,
[X,Y]\Delta t^{2}/2
, although the assumption that the leading order terms are dominant is not always valid.
Childs2019
When considering the two-dimensional advection diffusion equation of a shear flow, where
X=-u_{x}(y)\partial_{x}
and
Y=D(\partial_{x}+\partial_{y})
, we have

\displaystyle[X,Y]\phi

\displaystyle=-u_{x}(y)\partial_{x}\left(D[\partial_{x}^{2}\phi+\partial_{y}^{2}\phi]\right)-D\left(\partial_{x}^{2}[-u_{x}(y)\partial_{x}\phi]+\partial_{y}^{2}[-u_{x}(y)\partial_{x}\phi]\right)

\displaystyle=D\left(2\,u^{\prime}(y)\,\partial_{xy}\phi+u^{\prime\prime}(y)\,\partial_{x}\phi\right)\,,

(23)

revealing that errors from the velocity shear
u^{\prime}
grow with the mixed gradient
\partial_{xy}\phi
, as shear flows augment
\partial_{y}\phi
at a rate that is dependent on
\partial_{x}\phi
. An additional error source arises from the product of the velocity curvature
u^{\prime\prime}
with the streamwise gradient
\partial_{x}\phi
. As expected, the error vanishes for uniform velocity fields or when
\phi
varies as a function of
y
only. The accuracy can be improved to second-order using Strang splitting,

e^{t(X+Y)}=\lim_{N_{t}\to\infty}\left(e^{tX/2N_{t}}e^{tY/N_{t}}e^{tX/2N_{t}}\right)^{N_{t}}\,,

(24)

and to arbitrarily higher orders of accuracy, e.g. by the fourth-order Yoshida splitting.
Yoshida1990
Therefore, since
N_{t}=O(\epsilon^{-1/p})
for
p
-order operator splitting and time-symmetric compositions require
O(2^{p/2})
operations per step,
Yoshida1990
solving the advection-diffusion equation requires
O(2^{p/2}\,N_{t}\log^{2}N)=O(2^{p/2}\,\epsilon^{-1/p}\log^{2}N)
gates. Peforming the exact time evolution for each operator surpasses explicit time-marching that must adhere to the Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy condition. This allows the gate complexity to be logarithmic in
N
, compared to polynomial in
N
for quantum explicit time-marching
Over2025
or classical methods.

6
Conclusions

In this study, we have presented a method of decomposing dynamical systems into efficient quantum circuits through diagonalization and splitting of operators. Quantum circuits for simulating the general linear dynamics
d\hat{\phi}_{j}/dt=zk_{j}^{g}\hat{\phi}_{j}
for a complex
z
and positive real integer exponent
g
were derived. This was demonstrated for the unitary dynamics described by the advection equation where
z=-iu
and
g=1
, and for the nonunitary dynamics described by the heat equation where
z=-D
and
g=2
. Advection, which is irregular but unitary, imposes substantially different challenges than diffusion, which is regular but not unitary. Therefore, evolving by the individual dynamics and combining them with operator splitting allows to address each problem individually. More sophisticated advection can be realized by replacing the advection operator here with alternative approaches.
Brearley2024

Operator splitting can be applied to other dynamical systems evolving under different differential operators, such as nonlinear systems with source terms, but it remains to be seen whether relevant dynamics in fluid mechanics can be realized without a significant amplitude loss. The conventional mitigation by amplitude amplification may become problematic,
Sauro_AA
which leads to the important question of whether the prevalent non-unitarity of fluid flows is amenable to simulation on quantum computers.

Spectral methods use efficient diagonalization of the differential operators that stems from a certain regularity of the problem. While some irregularities, such as non-uniform grids, can be analyzed via expansions in Chebyshev polynomials, the flexibility of irregularity requires compromise in the efficiency of the implementation.
Rung_Irregular
Quantum computers may offer a revolutionary advantage for highly regular problems such as the study of turbulence on large uniform periodic domains, but flows around complex geometries such as aircrafts may be limited to structural approximation.

This work follows the common principle that evolves many steps in time without measurement and state preparation.
QCFD_Oneshot
As the solution cannot be visually inspected, we rely on the simulation to proceed without unexpected failures. The accuracy and efficiency of spectral methods may improve this aspect, but artifacts such as the Gibbs phenomenon may amplify imperfections in the state. The mildly dissipative nature of upwind finite difference schemes, while much less accurate, are more robust to imperfections.
Brearley2024

For any processing scheme, the overall strategy still assumes that input and output data are efficient to access, which remains a significant obstacle to achieve practical quantum advantage.
Succi_2023
In conclusion, quantum computational fluid dynamics is likely to mature not as a wholesale replacement for classical solvers, but as a specialized tool that is most powerful when regularity and thereby reducibility of input, processing and output make quantum methods appealing.

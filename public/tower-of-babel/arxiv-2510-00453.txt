On Sharp Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle and the stability
Xia Huang, Dong Ye

Abstract
. In this work, we summarize the linearization method to study the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principles, and explain that the same approach can be used to handle the stability problem. As examples of application, combining with spherical harmonic decomposition and the Hardy inequalities, we revise two families of inequalities. We give firstly an affirmative answer in dimension four to Cazacu-Flynn-Lam’s conjecture [ 2 ] for the sharp Hydrogen Uncertainty Principle, and improve the recent estimates of Chen-Tang [ 4 ] in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} and ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3} . On the other hand, we identify the best constants and extremal functions for two stability estimates associated to ∥ Δ u ∥ 2 ∥ r ∇ u ∥ 2 − N + 2 2 ∥ ∇ u ∥ 2 2 \|\Delta u\|_{2}\|r\nabla u\|_{2}-\frac{N+2}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2} in ℝ N {\mathbb{R}}^{N} ( N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 ), studied recently by Duong-Nguyen [ 6 ] and Do-Lam-Lu-Zhang [ 5 ] .



1.
Introduction

As a bridge, the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle (HUP for shortness) translated a philosophical concept from quantum physics into a prolific research program in functional analysis. The core idea behind the famous HUP, that the non-commutativity of operators implies a lower bound for the measurement accuracy, played a foundational role in the study of functional inequalities. It yields not only various inequalities characterizing uncertainty, and presents also profound links with many important inequalities in analysis, as for example the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities.

Furthermore, it is well known that the optimal constant for the classical HUP is achieved by the Gaussian functions. This fact led mathematicians to systematically investigate the extremal functions and sharp constants for various uncertainty inequalities. The understanding of the sharpness of these functional inequalities provides interesting applications in the study of partial differential equations and harmonic analysis, see
[
18
,
8
,
9
,
12
,
17
]
and references therein.

In general, the HUP can be seen as
L^{2}
Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type inequalities, or
L^{2}
interpolation inequalities as follows:

(1.1)

\displaystyle H(u)U(u)\geq\mu^{2}P(u)^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in{\mathbb{H}}.

Here
{\mathbb{H}}
is a functional Hilbert space,
H
,
U
,
P
are continuous, positive definite quadratic forms over
{\mathbb{H}}
; and
\mu>0
is a constant. For simplicity, we consider only the real functions here, but there is no doubt to generalize to the complex value situations.

Moreover, without loss of generality, we assume that
{\mathbb{H}}
is invariant under the scaling of variables; while
H
,
U
,
P
are homogeneous with respect to the scaling. To be more precise, let
\tau_{\lambda}
stand for the homothety
\tau_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda x
for
\lambda>0
, and denote
u_{\lambda}(x)=u\circ\tau_{\lambda}(x)=u(\lambda x)
. We assume then
u_{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{H}}
for any
\lambda>0
if
u\in{\mathbb{H}}
; and there exist
(\alpha_{i})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle H(u_{\lambda})=\lambda^{\alpha_{1}}H(u),\;U(u_{\lambda})=\lambda^{\alpha_{2}}U(u),\;P(u_{\lambda})=\lambda^{\alpha_{3}}P(u),\quad\forall\;u\in{\mathbb{H}},\lambda>0.

Obviously, to hope the inequality (
1.1
), we must have
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=2\alpha_{3}
. Moreover, using the scaling, when
\gamma=\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}\neq 0
, it is easy to observe that (
1.1
) is equivalent to the inequality:

(1.3)

\displaystyle H(u)+U(u)\geq 2\mu P(u),\quad\forall\;u\in{\mathbb{H}}.

The above fact is observed and used in
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
13
]
for diverse situations, sometimes (
1.3
) is called as
linearised
or
non scaling invariant
version of (
1.1
). Notice that conversely to the HUP, the inequality (
1.3
) is no longer scaling invariant, but this enables an alternative way to handle (
1.1
), in particular associated to the spherical harmonic expansion.

Once we identify the best constant and extremal functions, we can ask for the stability of the Heisenberg uncertainty principle. Let
\Sigma_{*}
denote the cone of extremal functions, i.e. functions such that the equalities holds in (
1.1
) with the best constant, that is,

\mu_{*}^{2}=\min_{u\in{\mathbb{H}}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{H(v)U(v)}{P(u)^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Sigma_{*}=\Big\{v\in{\mathbb{H}},H(v)U(v)=\mu_{*}^{2}P(v)^{2}\Big\}\neq\{0_{\mathbb{H}}\}.

The question is to understand

(1.4)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{*}=\inf_{u\in{\mathbb{H}},d_{P}(u,\Sigma_{*})>0}\frac{\sqrt{H(u)U(u)}-\mu_{*}P(u)}{d_{P}(u,\Sigma_{*})^{2}},\quad\mbox{where }\;d_{P}(u,\Sigma_{*})=\inf_{v^{*}\in\Sigma_{*}}\sqrt{P(u-v^{*})}.

Whenever
{\mathcal{S}}_{*}>0
, there holds

H(u)U(u)-\mu_{*}^{2}P(u)^{2}\geq{\mathcal{S}}_{*}^{2}d_{P}(u,\Sigma_{*})^{4},\quad\forall\;u\in{\mathbb{H}}

which strengthen (
1.1
), by measuring quantitatively the deficit to the sharp estimate, in function of the distance to
\Sigma_{*}
, the set of extremal functions. The study of stability problem has also a
linearised
but equivalent form.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}
,
H
,
U
,
P
,
\mu_{*}
be as above, let
{\mathcal{S}}_{*}
be that in (
1.4
). Define

(1.5)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{*,+}=\inf_{u\in{\mathbb{H}},d_{P}(u,\Sigma_{*})>0}\frac{H(u)+U(u)-2\mu_{*}P(u)}{d_{P}(u,\Sigma_{*})^{2}}.

If
\alpha_{1}\neq\alpha_{3}
in (
1.2
), then
{\mathcal{S}}_{*,+}=2{\mathcal{S}}_{*}
.

The argument is the same as for the equivalence between (
1.1
) and (
1.3
). Firstly,
H(u)+U(u)\geq 2\sqrt{H(u)U(u)}
gives
{\mathcal{S}}_{*,+}\geq 2{\mathcal{S}}_{*}
. Inversely, remark that
d_{P}(u,\Sigma_{*})^{2}
has the same homogeneity as
P
with respect to homotheties
\tau_{\lambda}
, that is,
d_{P}(u_{\lambda},\Sigma_{*})^{2}=\lambda^{\alpha_{3}}d_{P}(u,\Sigma_{*})^{2}
for any
\lambda>0
, due to the scaling invariance of
\Sigma_{*}
. Let
\gamma=\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}
, by definition of
{\mathcal{S}}_{*,+}
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda^{\alpha_{3}}{\mathcal{S}}_{*,+}d_{P}(u,\Sigma_{*})^{2}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}_{*,+}d_{P}(u_{\lambda},\Sigma_{*})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq H(u_{\lambda})+U(u_{\lambda})-2\mu_{*}P(u_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\alpha_{1}}H(u)+\lambda^{\alpha_{2}}U(u)-2\mu_{*}\lambda^{\alpha_{3}}P(u)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\alpha_{3}}\Big[\lambda^{\gamma}H(u)+\lambda^{-\gamma}U(u)-2\mu_{*}P(u)\Big].

Therefore
\lambda^{\gamma}H(u)+\lambda^{-\gamma}U(u)-2\mu_{*}P(u)\geq{\mathcal{S}}_{*,+}d_{P}(u,\Sigma_{*})^{2}
for any
\lambda>0
. With minimization of the left hand side in
\lambda
, we arrive at

\sqrt{H(u)U(u)}-\mu_{*}P(u)\geq\frac{{\mathcal{S}}_{*,+}}{2}d_{P}(u,\Sigma_{*})^{2}.

It means
{\mathcal{S}}_{*,+}\leq 2{\mathcal{S}}_{*}
, hence
{\mathcal{S}}_{*,+}=2{\mathcal{S}}_{*}
.

Therefore, instead of studying (
1.4
), we can handle the
linearized
stability problem (
1.5
).

A first example is the following Hydrogen Uncertainty Principle. More precisely, Cazacu-Flynn-Lam
[
2
]

proved

Theorem A.

Let
N\geq 5
and
u\in W^{2,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. Then the following inequality holds

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\geq\frac{(N+1)^{2}}{4}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|}dx\right)^{2}

where the constant
\frac{(N+1)^{2}}{4}
is optimal, and is attained by
u(x)=\alpha(1+\beta r)e^{-\beta r},
with any
\beta>0
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

In other words, let
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denote the standard norm in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and

(1.7)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{N}:=\inf_{u\in W^{2,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),u\neq 0}\frac{\|\Delta u\|_{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|}dx},

Cazacu-Flynn-Lam established that
{\mathcal{H}}_{N}=\frac{N+1}{2}
for
N\geq 5
. Moreover, they conjectured that (
1.6
) holds for
2\leq N\leq 4
. As the equality in (
1.6
) holds with
u(x)=\alpha(1+\beta r)e^{-\beta r}
,
\beta>0
in any dimension
N\geq 2
, then (
1.6
) will be sharp whenever it is valid.

Very recently, Chen-Tang give a negative answer to the above conjecture for dimensions two and three. More precisely, using explicit test functions, they obtain in
[
4
]

\frac{1}{2}\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{3}{2}\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{3}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{21}}{5}\simeq 1.833.

So the remained question is for
N=4
. Here we give a positive answer to (
1.6
) in
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
, and present a unified study for
N\geq 4
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
N\geq 4
, then
{\mathcal{H}}_{N}=\frac{N+1}{2}
and the extremal functions are

\displaystyle u(x)=\alpha(1+\beta r)e^{-\beta r},~\beta>0~\text{and}~\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

For the inequality in (
1.6
), we have
(\alpha_{i})=(4-N,2-N,3-N)
. As explained abovely, to get the best constant
{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{2}
for (
1.6
), we can consider

(1.8)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{+}=2{\mathcal{H}}_{N}=\inf_{u\in W^{2,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),u\neq 0}\frac{\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{2}+\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}}{\big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\nabla u\big\|^{2}_{2}}.

Another ingredient of our study is based on the link between Hardy inequalities and HUP. A notable example is: Using the famous Hardy inequality

\|\nabla u\|_{2}\geq\frac{N-2}{2}\Big\|\frac{u}{r}\Big\|_{2},\quad\forall\;u\in W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

we get immediately a Heisenberg type estimate via the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\|\nabla u\|_{2}\|ru\|_{2}\geq\frac{N-2}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

Though we have not yet the best constant (which is
\frac{N}{2}
) in the above estimate, the Hardy inequality provides very useful information.

With the same approach, we improve also the estimates of Chen-Tang (see
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
) for
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{3}
. Another interesting fact is that without knowing the exact values of
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
or
{\mathcal{H}}_{3}
, we can claim the existence of extremal functions.

Theorem 1.3
.

There hold

(1.9)

\displaystyle\frac{3+6\sqrt{2}}{14}\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\quad 1.75\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{3}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{21}}{5}.

Moreover, the best constant
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
(resp.
{\mathcal{H}}_{3}
) is attained in the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
(resp.
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
) under the norm
\|v\|=\|\Delta v\|_{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}
.

As a second example of application, we consider the following HUP:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{2}\|r\nabla u\|_{2}\geq\frac{N+2}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}.

Thanks to

2
]
(see also
[

in (

) is optimal, and it is attained by the Gaussian functions

The stability problem for (
1.10
) was studied firstly by Duong-Nguyen
[
6
]
, they showed that for any
u\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\delta_{1}(u):=\|\Delta u\|_{2}\|r\nabla u\|_{2}-\frac{N+2}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}\geq\frac{1}{768}\inf_{u^{*}\in S_{0}^{u}}\|\nabla(u-u^{*})\|_{2}^{2}

where

\displaystyle S_{0}^{u}:=\Big\{w\in\Sigma_{0},\;\|\nabla w\|_{2}=\|\nabla u\|_{2}\Big\}.

Notice that
S_{0}^{u}
is a sphere in
\Sigma_{0}
. Recently, applying the equivalent linearized approach (
1.5
), Do-Lam-Lu-Zhang
[
5
]
improved
drastically the estimate of Duong-Nguyen as follows.

Theorem B.

Let
N\geq 2
, and
{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
be the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
under the norm
\|\Delta u\|_{2}+\|r\nabla u\|_{2}
. Denote

{\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}=\sqrt{N^{2}+4N-4}-N
, then

(1.11)

\displaystyle\delta_{1}(u)\geq\frac{{\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}}{2}\inf_{u^{*}\in\Sigma_{0}}\|\nabla(u-u^{*})\|_{2}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}

and

(1.12)

\displaystyle\delta_{1}(u)\geq\frac{{\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}}{4}\inf_{u^{*}\in S_{0}^{u}}\|\nabla(u-u^{*})\|_{2}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}.

By refining the analysis in
[
5
]
, we claim the optimality of the above inequalities.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
N\geq 2
, the above estimates (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) are sharp. Moreover, the equalities occurs in (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) for
u(x)=u_{1}(\lambda x)
, with
\lambda>0
,
u(x)=r\Psi(r)\phi_{1}(\sigma)
where

\Psi(r):={{}_{1}F_{1}}\Big(\frac{N+1}{2}+\frac{{\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}}{4};\frac{N+2}{2};-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\Big),

\phi_{1}
is any spherical eigenfunction on the unit sphere satisfying
-\Delta_{S^{N-1}}\phi_{1}=(N-1)\phi_{1}
, and
\sigma=\frac{x}{|x|}
.

Here
{{}_{1}F_{1}}
denotes the standard hypergeometric function, see more detail in section 2.

Our proof is based on elementary observation, with some interesting features. For example, to derive (
1.12
) from (
1.11
), the analysis in
[
5
]
used the closeness of
\Sigma_{0}
with respect to the semi-norm

; the attainability of
\inf_{u^{*}\in\Sigma_{0}}\|\nabla(u-u^{*})\|_{2}
, and involved discussion according to the value of
\delta_{1}(u)
. Indeed, (
1.12
) is a direct consequence of (
1.11
), seeing the following abstract result in very general frameworks.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
({\mathbb{E}},{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle})
be an inner product space. Let
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{E}}
be a cone, that is
0\in\Sigma
, and
\Sigma=\lambda\Sigma
for all
\lambda\neq 0
. Given any
u\in{\mathbb{E}}
, we denote
S^{u}=\{w\in\Sigma,\|w\|=\|u\|\}
. There holds

(1.13)

\displaystyle{\rm dist}(u,S^{u})\leq\sqrt{2}{\rm dist}(u,\Sigma).

Moreover, if
u\perp\Sigma
, then the equality holds true, i.e.
{\rm dist}(u,S^{u})=\sqrt{2}{\rm dist}(u,\Sigma)
.

Notice that the estimate (
1.13
) does not require the completeness of
{\mathbb{E}}
, nor the closeness of
\Sigma

{\rm dist}(u,\Sigma)

Preliminaries

In this section, we state some useful Lemmas. The spherical harmonics decomposition is a powerful and important technic in analysis, in particular for the study of functional inequalities, some classical applications can be found in
[
16
,
1
,
10
,
19
]

u\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, it is well known that
u
can be expanded as follows

(2.1)

\displaystyle u(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}r^{k}v_{k}(r)\phi_{k}(\sigma)

with
v_{k}\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,
\sigma=\frac{x}{|x|}
, and suitably normalized spherical eigenfunctions
\phi_{k}
satisfying

-\Delta_{S^{N-1}}\phi_{k}=k(N+k-2)\phi_{k},\quad\forall\;k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

By Lemma 3.1 in
[
2
]
, the following identities hold.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
, for any
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and the sequence
v_{k}
given by (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N+2k-1}|v^{\prime}_{k}(r)|^{2}dr,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|}dx=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N+2k-2}|v^{\prime}_{k}(r)|^{2}dr+k\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N+2k-4}|v_{k}(r)|^{2}dr\Big),

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N+2k+1}|v^{\prime}_{k}(r)|^{2}dr-2k\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N+2k-1}|v_{k}(r)|^{2}dr\Big)

and

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N+2k-1}|v^{\prime\prime}_{k}(r)|^{2}dr+(N+2k-1)\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N+2k-3}|v^{\prime}_{k}(r)|^{2}dr\Big).

Let

2.1.
Proof of Lemma
1.5

Here we show the estimate (
1.13
) which is valid in very general setting for stability problem. To simplify, for
u\in{\mathbb{E}}
and
A\subset{\mathbb{E}}
, we denote the distance function in
{\mathbb{E}}
as
{\rm d}(u,A)=\inf_{v\in A}\|u-v\|
.

If
u=0
, (
1.13
) is obviously true since
0\in\Sigma\cap\Sigma^{u}
. Let
u\neq 0
, by scaling we can assume that
\|u\|=1
, hence
{\rm d}(u,\Sigma)\in{[0,1]}
.

Case 0.

If now
u\perp\Sigma
, clearly
{\rm d}(u,\Sigma)=\|u\|
and
{\rm d}(u,S^{u})=\sqrt{2}\|u\|
, because
\|u-p\|^{2}=\|u\|+\|p\|^{2}
, for any
p\in\Sigma
.

Case 1.

Let
{\rm d}(u,\Sigma)=0
. There is
\{p_{i}\}\subset\Sigma
such that
\|p_{i}-u\|\to 0
, so
\|p_{i}\|\to 1
. Let
w_{i}=\frac{p_{i}}{\|p_{i}\|}
, then
w_{i}\in S^{u}
and
\|w_{i}-u\|\to 0
, hence
{\rm d}(u,S^{u})=0
.

Case 2.

Let
{\rm d}(u,\Sigma)=1
. Using
\|u-\lambda w\|^{2}\geq 1
for any
\lambda
, there holds
\langle u,w\rangle=0
, hence
u\perp\Sigma
, so we are in Case 0.

Case 3.

Let
{\rm d}(u,\Sigma)=\gamma\in{(0,1)}
. There is
\{p_{i}\}\subset\Sigma
such that
\|p_{i}-u\|\to\gamma
. Denote by
q_{i}
the orthogonal projection of

over

, we see that

\displaystyle u-q_{i}\perp q_{i},\;\mbox{so }\|u-q_{i}\|^{2}=1-\|q_{i}\|^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|u-q_{i}\|\to\gamma.

Hence we can assume

and define

. There holds

\displaystyle\|u-w_{i}\|^{2}=2-2\frac{\langle u,q_{i}\rangle}{\|q_{i}\|}=2-2\|q_{i}\|\leq 2-2\|q_{i}\|^{2}=2\|u-q_{i}\|^{2}.

We get then

To conclude, (
1.13
) is always valid. ∎

k\geq 1
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{N,k}=\sqrt{(N+2k)^{2}-8k}-N
. Hence

(4.4)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{0,+}={\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}=\sqrt{N^{2}+4N-4}-N.

Proof.
By the computation in
[
5
, page 18]
, for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
v\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
, we have the following equality

\displaystyle I_{N,k}(v)-\gamma Q_{k}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|rv^{\prime\prime}(r)+r^{2}v^{\prime}(r)+A_{k}v^{\prime}(r)+B_{k,\gamma}rv(r)\Big|^{2}r^{N+2k-3}dr+T_{k}(\gamma)R_{k}(v)

with

\displaystyle A_{k}=N+2k-1,\quad B_{k,\gamma}=N+k+\frac{\gamma}{2},\quad R_{k}(v):=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N+2k-1}v^{2}dr

and

\displaystyle T_{k}(\gamma)=(N+2k)B_{k,\gamma}-B_{k,\gamma}^{2}-2k=(N-2+k)k-\frac{N}{2}\gamma-\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{2}.

Clearly,
T_{k}(\gamma_{k})=0
with
\gamma_{k}:=\sqrt{(N+2k)^{2}-8k}-N>0
; and
T_{k}(\gamma)<0
for
\gamma>\gamma_{k}
.

It means that
{\mathcal{C}}_{N,k}\geq\gamma_{k}
. Moreover, we will have
I_{N,k}(v)-\gamma_{k}Q_{k}(v)\equiv 0
if
v
resolves the differential equation

rv^{\prime\prime}+r^{2}v^{\prime}+A_{k}v^{\prime}+B_{k,\gamma_{k}}rv=0\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

By Lemma
2.4
,

\widetilde{v}_{k}(r):={{}_{1}F_{1}}\Big(\frac{B_{k,\gamma_{k}}}{2};\frac{A_{k}+1}{2};-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\Big)

is a such solution. Moreover,
\widetilde{v}_{k}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
and by (
2.8
),
\widetilde{v}_{k}^{(\ell)}(r)=O(r^{-2N-2k-\gamma_{k}-\ell})
as
r\to\infty
, for any
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. So that
I_{N,k}(\widetilde{v}_{k})+Q_{k}(\widetilde{v}_{k})<\infty
, hence
\widetilde{u}_{k}(x)=r^{k}\widetilde{v}_{k}(r)\phi_{k}(\sigma)
belongs to
{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
. For
\gamma>\gamma_{k}
and
k\geq 1
, there holds

\displaystyle\delta_{2}(\widetilde{u}_{k})-\gamma d_{0}(\widetilde{u}_{k},\Sigma_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta_{2}(\widetilde{u}_{k})-\gamma\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=I_{N,k}(\widetilde{v}_{k})-\gamma Q_{k}(\widetilde{v}_{k})

\displaystyle=T_{k}(\gamma)R_{k}(\widetilde{v}_{k})<0.

We deduce then
{\mathcal{C}}_{N,k}\leq\gamma_{k}
by standard approximation argument, so
{\mathcal{C}}_{N,k}=\gamma_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
, consequently (
4.4
) holds true. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
completed.
By Lemma
1.1
,
{\mathcal{S}}_{0,+}=2{\mathcal{S}}_{0}={\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}
, so (
1.11
) is sharp.
Furthermore, as
{\mathcal{C}}_{N,k}>{\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}
for
k\neq 1
, to reach the sharp equality
\delta_{2}(u)-{\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}d_{0}(u,\Sigma_{0})^{2}=0
, we must have
u=Cr\widetilde{v}_{1}(r)\phi_{1}(\sigma)
with

\widetilde{v}_{1}(r)={{}_{1}F_{1}}\Big(\frac{N+1}{2}+\frac{{\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}}{4};\frac{N+2}{2};-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\Big).

By scaling argument, we get all extremal functions to (
1.11
).

Now we consider (
1.12
). For any
u\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
, set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{u}_{0,+}=\inf_{u\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0},d_{0}(u,S^{u}_{0})>0}\frac{\delta_{2}(u)}{d_{0}(u,S^{u}_{0})^{2}}\quad\mbox{where }S^{u}_{0}=\big\{w\in\Sigma_{0},\|\nabla w\|_{2}=\|\nabla u\|_{2}\big\}.

We can apply Lemma
1.5
with
{\mathbb{E}}_{0}={\mathbb{H}}_{0}
and the inner product given in (
4.3
). Therefore (
1.13
) means that
{\mathcal{S}}^{u}_{0,+}\geq\frac{{\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}}{2}
. Take
\widetilde{u}_{1}
as above, then
\widetilde{u}_{1}\perp\Sigma_{0}
with respect to (
4.3
). By Lemma
1.5
, there holds

\delta_{2}(\widetilde{u}_{1})={\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}d_{0}(u,\Sigma_{0})^{2}=\frac{{\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}}{2}d_{0}(u,S^{u}_{0})^{2}.

Hence we get
{\mathcal{S}}^{u}_{0,+}=\frac{{\mathcal{C}}_{N,1}}{2}
. Finally, the sharpness of (
1.12
) is ensured by the scaling argument as for (
1.8
) or Lemma
1.1
, so we are done.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The above analysis is very practical in many other
L^{2}
settings, for example to derive quickly
[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
. We can also claim the stability of the sharp inequality in (
1.6
) or Theorem
1.2
by similar approach, in other words, for
N\geq 4
, there is a constant
{\mathcal{S}}_{N}>0
, such that for all
u
belonging to the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
under the norm
\|\Delta v\|_{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}
, there holds

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}-\frac{N+1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|}dx\geq{\mathcal{S}}_{N}\inf_{u^{*}\in\Sigma_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla(u-u^{*})|^{2}}{|x|}dx,

here
\Sigma_{1}={\rm span}\{(1+r)e^{-r}\}
. However, we were not able to fix the best constant for (
4.5
).

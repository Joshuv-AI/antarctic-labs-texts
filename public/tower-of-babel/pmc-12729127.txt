Barriers to perinatal care among pregnant South Asian immigrants living in Canada: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Anam Shahil-Feroz, Sama Hasan, Ridah Feroz, Saleema Allana, Salima Meherali, Zohra S. Lassi, Rohan D’Souza
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionPregnant South Asian immigrants (PSAIs) in Canada experience unique barriers to accessing culturally and linguistically sensitive perinatal care. These barriers make the pregnancy journey particularly stressful and contribute to poor maternal and fetal health outcomes. Despite Canada’s growing South Asian population, there is limited understanding of their healthcare needs and a lack of research on how best to support PSAIs in Canada. This review seeks to address this gap by providing a comprehensive understanding of the needs of PSAIs related to perinatal care, the barriers they face when accessing perinatal care in Canada, and the available interventions and programs to support PSAIs. Material and MethodsThis review will follow the framework proposed by Arksey and O'Malley. Reporting will adhere to the PRISMA-ScR and PRISMA-Equity guidelines. A comprehensive search of the literature will be undertaken in EMBASE, MEDLINE, and CINAHL, including studies published in English up to July 30, 2025, that examine prenatal care among PSAIs in Canada. Two reviewers will independently perform study screening, data extraction, and synthesis. Inductive thematic approach will be used to analyse results and to identify patterns, themes, and insights across the included studies.DiscussionThis review aims to offer researchers and clinicians with a comprehensive understanding of the barriers PSAIs face in accessing maternity care in Canada. Additionally, it will synthesize evidence on existing interventions and support programs designed to improve perinatal care outcomes for PSAIs, offering insights into potential areas for future research and policy development.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12978-025-02211-x.

Background

The pregnancy period, also known as the prenatal period, is a critical juncture for the expectant mother, the partner, the developing fetus, and the broader family unit [1]. During this time, the women experience a wide range of physiological and psychological changes that occur during pregnancy as their bodies adapt to support and nurture the developing fetus, alongside emotional adjustments as they prepare for motherhood [2–4]. Providing high-quality pregnancy care to women and their families is crucial in fostering positive pregnancy experiences and outcomes for all [5]. Globally, prenatal care remains insufficient, with many women and their unborn children lacking access to quality services, which can compromise both physical and mental health outcomes [5–7]. The inadequate care during pregnancy can result in adverse outcomes, such as maternal and neonatal mortality and morbidity [5, 6, 8].

Immigrant women are particularly vulnerable during pregnancy, encountering a range of structural, cultural, and individual barriers that impede access to care. These include language and communication challenges, limited health literacy, difficulties in navigating healthcare system, cultural differences, discrimination, financial constraints, inadequate social support, transportation difficulties, and prior negative experiences with healthcare services [9–14]. These challenges hinder access to essential prenatal care and exacerbate health disparities [9–14]. Among immigrant groups, South Asians represent one of the largest populations globally and in Canada. Canada is recognized as one of the world’s most ethnically diverse countries, with over seven million foreign-born residents [15]. In fact, nearly one in four Canadians (23.0%) is an immigrant, giving Canada the second-highest proportion of immigrants worldwide [16]. Projections indicate that immigrants will make up 29.1% to 34.0% of Canada’s total population by 204115. Within this demographic shift, racialized groups are expanding, with South Asians constituting 7.1% (2.6 million) of the population. Among South Asian immigrants in Canada, the majority of individuals were born in India (44.3%), followed by Pakistan (9.2%), Sri Lanka (5.4%), and Bangladesh (3.0%) [17]. Settlement patterns reveal that the majority reside in Ontario, British Columbia, and Alberta, positioning these provinces as key hubs of cultural diversity and immigrant integration [18].

Pregnant South Asian immigrants (PSAIs) make up a significant proportion of immigrants and face barriers to accessing prenatal care [19]. These barriers include language barriers, limited health literacy, acculturative stress, discrimination, challenges accessing social and health services, and cultural preferences such as a preference for female physicians, reliance on family for antenatal care appointments, transportation difficulties, and a lack of awareness about available resources like prenatal classes [19–25]. These barriers are associated with significant mental health implications, as studies have reported higher prevalence of common mental health conditions among South Asian pregnant women in Canada [26, 27]. Culturally and linguistically sensitive support and healthcare services are crucial for these women as they navigate the complexities of pregnancy and immigration [28].

Despite the large number of PSAIs in Canada, there is limited understanding of their healthcare needs and a lack of research on how best to support PSAIs in Canada. A preliminary literature review was conducted to explore existing reviews in this area. To date, two reviews are underway [29, 30] and three reviews [10, 31–33] have been completed to explore some aspects of perinatal care among immigrant women in Canada. Of the two under review, Nikita et al.’s review is investigating the postnatal experiences of PSAIs women across United States, Canada, Australia, United Kingdom [29] while Sia et al.’s review is identifying interventions designed to improve perinatal care access for uninsured migrant women [30]. The findings of these two ongoing reviews have not yet been published [29, 30]. Of the published reviews, the one by Khanlou et al. synthesized 126 studies on maternal health among immigrant and refugee women in Canada, identifying cultural, social, economic, and individual factors that create barriers throughout the prenatal, intrapartum, and postnatal periods [31]. Higginbottom et al.’s review conducted a narrative synthesis of immigrant women’s experiences with maternity care in Canada, focusing on accessibility, acceptability, and maternal and newborn outcomes [10]. This review highlighted both positive and negative experiences, pointing to barriers such as lack of insurance coverage, limited awareness of services, mixed-gender prenatal classes, culturally insensitive care, language barriers, and transportation difficulties. However, these two reviews grouped all immigrant and refugee women, limiting the understanding of the cultural and linguistic differences faced by PSAIs. Desai et al.’s review examined perinatal nutrition and gestational diabetes in high-risk South Asian populations, contributing evidence for the design of culturally tailored nutrition interventions in Canada [32]. These reviews offer some evidence on the perinatal care of PSAIs in Canada. However, none of the reviews offered a comprehensive landscape of perinatal care among PSAIs or documented the barriers PSAIs faces in accessing perinatal care in Canada. Thus, evidence on how best to support PSAIs for maternity services is scarce. This review seeks to address this gap by offering a thorough understanding of the needs of PSAIs related to perinatal care, the barriers they face when accessing perinatal care in Canada, and the available interventions and programs to support PSAIs.

Methods

The reporting of this review will adhere to ‘Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) [34] and PRISMA-Equity [35] checklists. The protocol for this review was registered with the Open Science Framework on May 30, 2024 (Link: https://osf.io/am4h2). The review will follow the methodological framework proposed by Arksey and O’Malley [36] to examine published literature on perinatal care among PSAIs in Canada. The review process will involve five stages: (1) formulating the research question, (2) identifying relevant studies, (3) selecting studies that meet inclusion criteria, (4) charting the data, and (5) synthesizing and summarizing the findings. Since the objective of this review is to map the breadth and scope of available evidence rather than assess study quality, formal critical appraisal will not be conducted.

This review is guided by the Population, Concept, and Context (PCC) framework, which helps define the key elements of the review question (Table 1). The overarching research question guiding this review is: What evidence exists on perinatal care for PSAIs in Canada?

Operational definitions

For this review, perinatal care is conceptualized broadly as care provided throughout pregnancy, delivery, and the postpartum period. While the World Health Organization defines the perinatal period as extending from 22 completed weeks of gestation to seven completed days after birth, this review adopts a more expansive definition, beginning at conception and continuing through the neonatal period (up to 28 days post-birth).

PSAIs are defined as women who have migrated to Canada from countries in the South Asian subcontinent, including Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Pakistan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Afghanistan, and the Maldives, and who have experienced pregnancy after immigration.

Information sources and search strategy

Three core electronic databases will be systematically searched to locate published studies on this topic including EMBASE, MEDLINE, and CINAHL. These databases were chosen for their relevance to health sciences and comprehensive subject coverage. Database selection was informed by recommendations from Goossen et al.31and Bramer et al.32. In addition, reference lists of included studies and relevant systematic reviews will be screened to identify additional eligible articles. The search strategy was developed in consultation with an academic health sciences librarian with expertise in systematic reviews. Four central concepts were applied to structure the search: population (PSAIs), health condition (pregnancy), intervention (perinatal care), and setting (Canada). An initial exploratory search of MEDLINE was conducted to identify relevant terms (Table 3). Keywords and index terms from retrieved studies, along with an established search hedge for the South Asian diaspora, were then used to refine the full strategy for MEDLINE (Appendix I). This strategy will be adapted to fit each database and information source.

Step 3: selecting eligible studies

The research team developed the eligibility criteria through an iterative process, guided by the operational definitions and the proposed search strategy. Only English-language studies conducted in Canada and published up to July 30, 2025, will be included. This timeframe aligns with periods of substantial South Asian migration to Canada, which provides critical context for understanding perinatal care experiences in this population. A structured screening guide was designed by the lead investigator (ASF) and refined with input from the review team. To ensure inter-rater reliability, two reviewers (RAA and SH) will independently pilot-test the screening guide on 100 abstracts. Feedback from this process will be used to adjust the guide. The team will also review examples of both included and excluded studies to ensure consistent application of criteria. Screening will be conducted in two stages. In the first stage, two independent reviewers (RAA and SH) will screen titles and abstracts using Covidence software. Regular meetings will be held to resolve uncertainties and refine criteria where necessary. Any disagreements will be referred to a third reviewer (ASF) for resolution. In the second stage, full texts of shortlisted studies will be independently reviewed by the same two reviewers. Disagreements will again be resolved by ASF through discussion with the team. At all stages, reasons for exclusion will be documented. The study selection process will be reported using a PRISMA flow diagram (Fig. 1).

Step 4: charting the data

A tailored data extraction form has been created by the lead investigator (ASF) and will be piloted on two eligible studies by three independent reviewers (ASF, SH, RAA) to assess its ability to consistently and comprehensively capture the required information. Any necessary revisions will be made following team discussions based on the pilot testing results. The items included in the extraction form were informed by a review of previously published literature in this area as well as by the studies used to develop the search strategy. To ensure reliability between reviewers, a random subset (15%) of included studies will be independently reviewed and compared. Any disagreements will be resolved through discussion, with a third reviewer consulted if consensus cannot be reached.

Step 5: synthesizing and summarizing the findings

The data collected will be synthesized narratively through a qualitative descriptive approach. We will map out the shared characteristics of the included studies, such as type, objectives, setting, participant demographics, sampling strategies, sample sizes, methodologies, analytic techniques, and main outcomes. Consistent with scoping review methodology, we will not assess the quality of individual studies. Each article will be reviewed independently by two team members. An exploratory, inductive analytic process will then be used to thematically categorize and summarize the findings in relation to the research questions. Extracted information will be revisited multiple times to highlight similarities, recurring themes, and variations, allowing us to group and present the results meaningfully.The focus will be on the barriers PSAIs face in accessing perinatal care, the specific needs of PSAIs relating to perinatal care, and existing interventions and support programs available to improve perinatal care among PSAIs. All reviewers will discuss the results and agree on the final groupings of the results.

Patient and public involvement

The findings from this review on perinatal care among PSAIs in Canada are anticipated to enhance existing knowledge and provide value to PSAIs, healthcare professionals, policymakers, and researchers in maternal and child health. As this study is based solely on a synthesis of published literature, there was no direct involvement of patients or members of the public in its design or implementation.

Timeline

The scoping review was registered in May 2024. Following registration, preliminary work including refinement of the research question, search strategy development, and pilot testing of inclusion and exclusion criteria was carried out between June and August 2024. The database searches and study screening (titles and abstracts) are planned for September to November 2024, followed by full-text screening and data extraction from December 2024 to April 2025. The charting, analysis, and synthesis of results will be conducted between May and October 2025. Drafting of the results and manuscript preparation will take place from November 2025 to February 2026, with the final scoping review expected to be completed and ready for submission by February 2026.

Discussion

The recent increase in PSAIs in Canada has brought attention to the urgent need for a critical examination of perinatal care among PSAIs to enhance maternal health outcomes. While previous reviews and studies have covered some aspects of perinatal care for immigrant women in Canada, none have provided a comprehensive analysis specific to PSAIs. These reviews offer valuable insights but lack a thorough examination of the support available for PSAIs in maternity services and do not extensively document the barriers they encounter in accessing perinatal care.

This scoping review will primarily focus on exploring the barriers PSAIs face in accessing perinatal care in Canada. Additionally, it will examine the distinct perinatal care needs, preferences, and requirements of PSAIs, which are shaped by diverse risk profiles, differing health perceptions, and cultural and linguistic factors. By understanding these barriers and needs, the review aims to equip healthcare providers, researchers, and policymakers with crucial insights to develop and implement targeted, culturally and linguistically sensitive strategies that improve access to perinatal care for PSAIs. Furthermore, the review will explore existing interventions, services, and support programs aimed at enhancing perinatal care for PSAIs. This will help identify best practices and gaps, guiding the development of future initiatives to improve the delivery and effectiveness of perinatal care services for this population.

The findings from this review will lay the groundwork for future research by identifying gaps in the current literature and interventions. Specifically, it will highlight areas that require further investigation, such as the long-term impact of targeted interventions on maternal health outcomes among PSAIs. The review will also underscore the need for developing and testing new interventions that are culturally and linguistically adapted to meet the specific needs of this population. In doing so, it will contribute to shaping a more equitable perinatal care landscape for PSAIs in Canada.

Strengths and limitations

This review will be the first to explore the current landscape of perinatal care for PSAIs in Canada, incorporating a wide range of published literature sources. A key strength of this review lies in the scoping review approach, which allows for a broad and comprehensive exploration of the literature without the need for quality appraisal. This approach enables us to assess whether a full systematic review is necessary. Throughout the process, an independent dual-reviewer method will be employed to minimize bias and enhance the thorough identification and inclusion of relevant studies. This review aims to deepen our understanding of the culturally and linguistically appropriate care needs of PSAIs, a population that has been significantly underrepresented in existing research. However, a potential limitation is the exclusion of non-English papers, which may unintentionally underrepresent the interventions and support programs available to PSAIs in different geographical contexts.

Ethics and dissemination

As this review does not involve the collection of primary data, formal ethical approval is not required. The review seeks to generate evidence-informed recommendations for healthcare providers, policymakers, and researchers, with the aim of improving perinatal care services and support programs for PSAIs in Canada. By addressing existing knowledge gaps, the review will contribute to improving the quality of maternity care for this minority group. The results will be shared through publication in a peer-reviewed journal, as well as dissemination via social media platforms, conference presentations, and policy briefs targeted at community partners and decision makers.

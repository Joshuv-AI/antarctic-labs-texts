Minimum forcing numbers of perfect matchings of circular and prismatic graphs
Qiaoyun Shi, Heping Zhang

Abstract
Let G G be a graph with a perfect matching. Denote by f ⁡ ( G ) f(G) the minimum size of a matching in G G that is uniquely extendable to a perfect matching in G G . Diwan (2019) used linear algebra to prove that for the d d -hypercube Q d Q_{d} ( OPEN d ≥ 2 ) d\geq 2) , f ⁡ ( Q d ) = 2 d − 2 f(Q_{d})=2^{d-2} , thus settling a conjecture of Pachter and Kim (1998). Recently, Mohammadian generalized this method to prove a general result: for a bipartite graph G G on n n vertices, if G G admits an involutory weighted adjacency matrix A A over a field F F , then f ⁡ ( G ​ □ ​ K 2 ) = n 2 f(G\Box K_{2})=\frac{n}{2} , where □ \square denotes the Cartesian product of two graphs. In this paper we obtain f ⁡ ( G ​ □ ​ C 2 ​ k ) = n f(G\Box C_{2k})=n when a bipartite graph G G on n n vertices admits an involutory weighted adjacency matrix A A over a field F F of characteristic not 2, for all integers k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . Moreover, we demonstrate that this method can also be applied to some nonbalanced bipartite graphs G G when graphs G G admit a weighted bi-adjacency matrix with orthogonal rows. Keywords : Bipartite graph; Perfect matching; Forcing number; Involutory matrix; Orthogonal matrix.

1
Introduction

All graphs in this paper are finite and simple. For a graph
G
, let
V(G)
denote its vertex set and
E(G)
its edge set. The order of
G
is the number of its vertices. An edge subset
S\subseteq E(G)
is called a matching if no two edges in
S
share a common end-vertex. A matching of
G
is perfect if it covers every vertex of
G
. For a perfect matching
M
of
G
, a subset
S
of
M
is called a
forcing set
if
S
is contained in no other perfect matchings of
G
. The
forcing number
of
M
in
G
, denoted by
f(G,M)
, is the smallest size of a forcing set for
M
. The
minimum forcing number
of
G
, denoted by
f(G)
, is defined as the minimum value of
f(G,M)
over all perfect matchings
M
of
G
. In general, computing the minimum forcing number of a bipartite graph is an NP-complete problem
[
1
,
2
]
. For two surveys and some recent work on matching forcing, see
[
6
,
13
,
19
]
.

The concept of the forcing number of a perfect matching of a graph originated in theoretical chemistry, where it corresponds to the “innate degree of freedom” of a Kekulé structure of polycyclic aromatic compounds, introduced by Klein and Randić
[
12
]
. The term “forcing” was later adopted in graph theory by Harary et al.
[
10
]
. The forcing idea has since appeared in various combinatorial structures
[
8
]
, such as graph colorings, Latin squares, dominating sets, and block designs.

Let
Q_{d}
denote the
d
-dimensional hypercube graph, whose vertex set is
\{0,1\}^{d}
, and where two vertices are adjacent if and only if their corresponding vectors differ in exactly one
coordinate. Diwan
[
7
]
determined the minimum forcing number of
Q_{n}
by a linear algebra technique, settling a conjecture originally posed by Pachter and Kim
[
16
]

and later revised by Riddle
[
17
]
. Riddle had previously verified the statement for even
d
by a combinatorial approach.

Theorem 1.1

(Diwan
[
7
]
)
.

f(Q_{d})=2^{d-2}
for any integer
d\geq 2
.

Diwan’s method is based on the following two simple lemmas. The latter relates to the corank of a matrix.

Lemma 1.2

(Diwan
[
7
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
with a perfect matching. If the largest order of an induced subgraph of
G
having a unique perfect matching is
k
, then the forcing number of every perfect matching in
G
is at least
(n-k)/2
.

Let
F
be a field. In particular,
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
denote the real and complex fields, respectively. A
weighted adjacency matrix

A
of a graph
G
is a matrix over
F
, with rows and columns indexed by
V(G)
, such that the
(u,v)
-entry is non-zero if and only if
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G
. If
G
is a bipartite graph with bipartition
(X,Y)
, then a
weighted bi-adjacency matrix

B
of
G
is a submatrix of
A
whose rows and columns are indexed by partite sets
X
and
Y
, respectively. The following result was implied and used in
[
7
,
15
]
.

Lemma 1.3

(Diwan
[
7
]
, Mohammadian
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph with a perfect matching. If
A
(resp.
B
) denotes a weighted (resp. bi-)adjacency matrix of
G
over a field
F
, then

f(G)\geq\frac{1}{2}(|V(G)|-\operatorname{rank}(A))=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{corank}(A),\mbox{ and }f(G)\geq\frac{1}{2}(|V(G)|-2\,\operatorname{rank}(B))=\operatorname{corank}(B).

(1)

Diwan
[
7
]
constructed a weighted adjacency matrix
A
of
Q_{d}
such that its inverse
A^{-1}
is also a weighted adjacency matrix of
Q_{d}
, and both matrices have integer entries, which allowed him to prove Theorem
1.1
. Huang
[
11
]
constructed another weighted adjacency matrix of
Q_{d}
with entries in
\{0,1,-1\}
whose rows are orthogonal to confirm the famous Sensitivity Conjecture in information theory.

The Cartesian product of two graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\Box H
, is the graph with vertex set
V(G)\times V(H)
, and two vertices
(g_{1},h_{1})
and
(g_{2},h_{2})
are adjacent if and only if either
g_{1}=g_{2}
and
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
or
h_{1}=h_{2}
and
g_{1}g_{2}\in E(G)
. Thus, the
d
-hypercube
Q_{d}
can be expressed as the
d
-fold Cartesian product of
K_{2}
(a complete graph with two vertices), and
Q_{d}=Q_{d-1}\Box K_{2}
for
d\geq 2
.

Diwan
[
7
]
asked whether this algebraic technique could be applied to compute the
minimum forcing number of perfect matchings in other bipartite graphs. Mohammadian
[
15
]
later generalized Diwan’s method to the
prism
of a bipartite graph
G
, i.e.,
G\Box K_{2}
. A matrix
A
is called
involutory
if
A^{2}=I
(equivalently,
A^{-1}=A
). Let
\mathcal{I}_{F}(G)
be the set of weighted adjacency matrices of
G
over
F
that are involutory.

Theorem 1.4

(Mohammadian
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph with
n
vertices. If
\mathcal{I}_{F}(G)\neq\emptyset
, then

f(G\Box K_{2})=\frac{n}{2}.

(2)

Note that for a bipartite graph
G
,
\mathcal{I}_{F}(G)\neq\emptyset
holds if and only if
G
admits a weighted bi-adjacency matrix
B
such that the pattern of non-zero entries of
B^{-1}
matches that of its transpose
B^{\top}
and the bipartition of
G
is balanced.

In this paper, we mainly consider the
circular graph

G\Box C_{2k}
, where
C_{2k}
denotes the cycle on
2k
vertices. Under the same condition as in Theorem
1.4
and for all fields
F
of characteristic not 2, we obtain that
f(G\Box C_{2k})=n
for all integers
k\geq 2
(see Theorem
2.1
for details). Our main approach is to construct some
2k\times 2k
partitioned matrices
R
of order
nk
and with corank
n
by using blocks
B
and
B^{-1}
of order
n/2
, which serve as weighted bi-adjacency matrices of
G\square C_{2k}
(see Section 2). In Section 3 we show that this algebraic method can be applied to non-balanced bipartite graphs
G
, provided their weighted bi-adjacency matrices are row-orthogonal or unitary, such as the complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}
. Finally we give some further examples of applications of Theorem
2.1
in Section 4.

2
Minimum forcing number of circular graphs

We now describe and prove our main result for the circular graph

Theorem 2.1

Let

Proof.

First, we show that

. By

3
Minimum forcing number of prisms of graphs

Theorem 2.10 in
[
15
]
states that for all positive integers
m
and
n
,
f(K_{m,n}\Box K_{2})=\min\{m,n\}
, which was proved by a combinatorial method (some analysis of perfect matchings). However, for
m\not=n
, Theorem
1.4
cannot be applied to directly obtain the result. In this section, we can generalize Theorem
1.4
as follows to handle prisms of non-balanced bipartite graphs.

Let
G=(X,Y)
be a bipartite graph with
|X|=m,|Y|=n
(
m\leq n
) and let
F
be a field. We define

\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)
as the collection of pairs
(B,C)
, where
B
,
C\in F^{m\times n}
are weighted bi-adjacency matrices of
G
satisfying
BC^{\top}=I_{m}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G=(X,Y)
be a bipartite graph with
|X|=m,|Y|=n
and
m\leq n
. If
\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)\neq\emptyset
, then

f(G\Box K_{2})=m.

Proof.

Let
(X_{i},Y_{i}),i=1,2
, be the bipartitions of the
i
-th copy
G_{i}
of
G
in
G\Box K_{2}
corresponding to
(X,Y)
.

First, we show that
f(G\Box K_{2})\leq m
. Take the matching
M
in
G\Box K_{2}
between
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
. Then
|M|=m
, and
G\Box K_{2}-(X_{1}\cup X_{2})
is formed by the matching between
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
, which implies that
M
can be uniquely extended to a perfect matching in
G\Box K_{2}
. This implies that
f(G\Box K_{2})\leq m
.

Next, it remains to show
f(G\Box K_{2})\geq m
. The graph
G\Box K_{2}
is bipartite. Since
\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)\not=\emptyset
, there is a pair of weighted bi-adjacency matrices
B
and
C
in
\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)
such that
BC^{\top}=I_{m}
. We construct a weighted bi-adjacency matrix
R
of
G\Box K_{2}
as follows.

R:=\bordermatrix{&Y_{2}&X_{1}\cr Y_{1}&I_{n}&C^{\top}\cr X_{2}&B&I_{m}}.

Since

R=\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&C^{\top}\\
B&I_{m}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}\\
B\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&C^{\top}\end{pmatrix},

we have

\operatorname{rank}(R)\leq\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&C^{\top}\end{pmatrix}=n.

Thus, by Lemma
1.3
we have

f(G\Box K_{2})\geq\frac{|G\Box K_{2}|}{2}-\operatorname{rank}(R)\geq m+n-n=m.

∎

Example 3.2
.

Consider star
K_{1,n}
.
Then
B=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(1,\dots,1)
is a weighted bi-adjacency matrix of
K_{1,n}
, and
(B,B)\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{R}}(K_{1,n})
. By Theorem
3.1
we have
f(K_{1,n}\Box K_{2})=1
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G=(X,Y)
be a bipartite graph with
|X|=m,|Y|=n
and
m\leq n
. If
\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)\neq\emptyset
, and
H
is a subgraph obtained from
G
by deleting
k
vertices in
X
, then

f(H\Box K_{2})=m-k.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)\neq\emptyset
, there exists a pair of weighted bi-adjacency matrices
B_{m\times n}
and
C_{m\times n}
in
\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)
such that
BC^{\top}=I_{m}
.
Let
B_{(m-k)\times n}
and
C_{(m-k)\times n}
be the submatrices obtained from
B_{m\times n}
and
C_{m\times n}
by deleting the rows indexed by these deleted
k
vertices in
X
, respectively. Then
B_{(m-k)\times n}C^{\top}_{(m-k)\times n}=I_{m-k}
, which implies
(B_{(m-k)\times n},C_{(m-k)\times n})\in\mathcal{R}_{F}(H)
. By Theorem 3.1, we have
f(H\Box K_{2})=m-k.

∎

We now use Theorem
3.1
or Corollary
3.3
to deduce the above-mentioned result as follows.

Proposition 3.4

(Mohammadian
[
15
]
)
.

For any integers
1\leq m\leq n,f(K_{m,n}\Box K_{2})=m.

Proof.

Consider the Fourier matrix
F_{n}=(\xi^{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}
,
where
\xi=e^{\frac{2\pi\text{i}}{n}}
is a primitive
n
th root of unity. Let
B=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}F_{n}
. Since
B
is a unitary matrix, i.e.
BB^{*}=I_{n}
,
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(K_{n,n})\neq\emptyset
. By Corollary
3.3
, we have
f(K_{m,n}\Box K_{2})=n-(n-m)=m.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\{G_{i}=(X_{i},Y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{t}
be a family of bipartite graphs with
|X_{i}|\leq|Y_{i}|
,
Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
, and
X_{i}\cap(\cup_{j=1}^{t}Y_{j})=\emptyset
for all
i
. Let
G=\cup_{i=1}^{t}G_{i}
. For
F\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
, if
\mathcal{R}_{F}(G_{i})\neq\emptyset
for each
i
, then

f(G\Box K_{2})=|X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{t}|.

(17)

Proof.

It is easy to see that
G=\cup_{i=1}^{t}G_{i}
is a bipartite graph with a bipartition
(\cup_{i=1}^{t}X_{i},\cup_{i=1}^{t}Y_{i})
. By Theorem
3.1
, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)\neq\emptyset
. We proceed by induction on
t
, the number of graphs in the union. For
t=1
, the result is trivial by Theorem
3.1
. Now suppose
t\geq 2
. Let
G_{2}^{\prime}=\cup G_{i=2}^{t}
with a bipartition
(X_{2}^{\prime},Y_{2}^{\prime})
, where
X_{2}^{\prime}=\cup_{i=2}^{t}X_{i}
and
Y_{2}^{\prime}=\cup_{i=2}^{t}Y_{i}
. By the induction hypothesis we have
\mathcal{R}_{F}(G_{2}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
. Take
(B_{1},C_{1})\in\mathcal{R}_{F}(G_{1})
, and
(B_{2},C_{2})\in\mathcal{R}_{F}(G_{2}^{\prime})
. Then
B_{1}C_{1}^{\top}=I_{|X_{1}|}
and
B_{2}C_{2}^{\top}=I_{|X_{2}^{\prime}|}
.

If
X_{1}\cap X_{2}^{\prime}=\emptyset
, construct two weighted bi-adjacency matrices of
G
:

B:=\bordermatrix{&Y_{1}&Y_{2}^{\prime}\cr X_{1}&B_{1}&O\cr X_{2}^{\prime}&O&B_{2}},C:=\bordermatrix{&Y_{1}&Y_{2}^{\prime}\cr X_{1}&C_{1}&O\cr X_{2}^{\prime}&O&C_{2}}.

Then

BC^{\top}=\begin{pmatrix}B_{1}&O\\
O&B_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}^{\top}&O\\
O&C_{2}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}B_{1}C_{1}^{\top}&O\\
O&B_{2}C_{2}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}=I_{|X_{1}|+|X_{2}^{\prime}|}.

Hence
(B,C)\in\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)
, so
\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)\not=\emptyset
.

Now suppose
X_{0}:=X_{1}\cap X_{2}^{\prime}\not=\emptyset
. For
i=1,2
, let
B_{i}(X)
denote the submatrix of
B_{i}
formed by the rows corresponding to vertices in
X
. Define a weighted bi-adjacency matrix
B
of
G
by

B:=\bordermatrix{&Y_{1}&Y_{2}^{\prime}\cr X_{1}\setminus X_{0}&B_{1}(X_{1}\setminus X_{0})&O\cr X_{0}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}B_{1}(X_{0})&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}B_{2}(X_{0})\cr X_{2}^{\prime}\setminus X_{0}&O&B_{2}(X_{2}^{\prime}\setminus X_{0})}.

Similarly, define another weighted bi-adjacency matrix
C
of
G
by

C:=\bordermatrix{&Y_{1}&Y_{2}^{\prime}\cr X_{1}\setminus X_{0}&C_{1}(X_{1}\setminus X_{0})&O\cr X_{0}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}C_{1}(X_{0})&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}C_{2}(X_{0})\cr X_{2}^{\prime}\setminus X_{0}&O&C_{2}(X_{2}^{\prime}\setminus X_{0})}.

A direct computation using
B_{1}C_{1}^{\top}=I_{|X_{1}|}
and
B_{2}C_{2}^{\top}=I_{|X_{2}^{\prime}|}
yields
BC^{\top}=I_{|X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{t}|}
. Thus
(B,C)\in\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)
, so
\mathcal{R}_{F}(G)\neq\emptyset
, which implies Eq. (
17
) by Theorem
3.1
.
∎

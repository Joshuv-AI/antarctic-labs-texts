Rewriting in Artin groups without A 3 A_{3} or B 3 B_{3} subdiagrams
Rubén Blasco-García, María Cumplido, Derek F. Holt

Abstract
We prove that the word problem in an Artin group G G based on a diagram without A 3 A_{3} or B 3 B_{3} subdiagrams can be solved using a system of length preserving rewrite rules which, together with free reduction, can be used to reduce any word over the standard generators of G G to a geodesic word in G G in quadratic time. This result builds on work of Holt and Rees, and of Blasco, Cumplido and Morris-Wright. Those articles prove the same result for all Artin groups that are either sufficiently large or 3-free, respectively.

Introduction

Our main result in this article is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

for
which the associated Coxeter diagram contains no

or

subdiagram;
in other words, no subset

,

Then there is a system of length preserving rewrite rules which, together
with free reduction, can be used to reduce any word over
S
to a geodesic word
in
G
, in quadratic time.

Furthermore, any two geodesic words representing the same element of
G
are
related by a sequence of rules from the system, each such involving just two
generators.

The notation

used in the statement of Theorem
1.1

denotes an alternating string

Throughout this article,

This result, together with the main results of
[
6
,
7
,
1
]
that it extends, addresses the general open question of whether all Artin groups have soluble word problem, and if so, whether the word problem can always be solved using a combination of length preserving rewrites together with free reduction.

Example

within Section
4

shows that the condition that excludes

subdiagrams
(corresponding to generators

The length preserving rules

-moves

[

,
7
,
1
]

Each rule has the effect of replacing a

input word by another word

of the same length, whose first and last

of such moves leads to a reduction of the input word, it does so by moving from
left to right within the word, replacing successive overlapping subwords, until
the letter at the right-hand end of the final replacement subword is the inverse
of the subsequent letter in the word, and so a free cancellation is possible.

In the large and sufficiently large groups studied in
[
6
,
7
]
,
the word problem can be solved as just described using critical subwords, which

are defined to be 2-generator words with particular constraints. In
[
1
]

In this article we define

.
Our arguments in this article are extensions of the arguments
used to prove the main result of

)
no

-moves on pseudo 3-generator critical words are necessary

We define

to be the set of standard generators of the Artin group

, and

, the set of standard generators and their inverses.

For

The

For

as

and we refer to words over

For

For any

(Optimal RRS)

(i)

(ii) Let

Proposition 4.9

If a word

in a

-free group admits an RRS then it admits an

Proof.

Part of this proof is essentially the same as the existence part of the proof of

-free groups.
We can clearly restrict attention to those RRS for

Then, given such an RRS associated to a decomposition

So we can find an RRS for

Suppose we have an RRS satisfying conditions (i) and (ii), but not (iii),

Then the hypothesis of this condition could apply only

Definition 4.10

(The set
W
)
.

We define
W
to be the set of words that admit no RRS.

As we already observed, a word that is not
freely reduced admits an RRS of length
0
with
w_{m+1}=\epsilon
,
so all words in
W
are freely reduced.
Since a word admitting an RRS is transformed to a freely reducing word by its
application, any geodesic word must be in
W
.

We describe as Procedure
4.12
(below) a linear time procedure that, given a word
w\in W
and

x\in A
, attempts to construct an optimal RRS that transforms
wx
to a word
ending in
x^{-1}x
.
The procedure either returns a sequence of words
u_{i}
and associated factorisation of

wx
defining an optimal RRS, or it returns
fail
, in which
case

is proved to be in
W
.
We prove correctness of this procedure in Proposition
4.13
,
which follows the procedure.

The procedure has two principal parts. In the first part, either the message
fail
is
returned, or the data associated with a putative (unique optimal)
RRS
U
for
wx
is computed.
In the second part, if
fail
was not returned, then it is checked whether
the putative RRS
U
really is an RRS for
wx
. If so, then the data
associated with
U
are returned, but otherwise
fail
.

The first part contains
m+1
steps and constructs the
putative decomposition of
w
from right to left;
note that the value of
m
is identified only when the
(m+1)
-st step is recognised as the final one of the first part.
This part attempts to identify a sequence
of subwords
w_{i}
of
w
, associated with critical words
u_{i}
,
for
i=m+1,m,\ldots,1
, in that order.
Given the location of the right-hand end of
w_{i}
and the
criticality type of
u_{i}
, the (
m+2-i
)-th step checks if

.
If so then it attempts to locate the left-hand end of
u_{1}
, but otherwise it
attempts to locate the right-hand end of
w_{i-1}
and determine the criticality type of
u_{i-1}
.

Before describing the procedure, we set up notation that will be used in the
description of Step
m+2-i
of part 1, when
i\leq m
(that is, after Step 1). At the start of that step, the procedure has
identified the right-hand end of the subword
w_{i}
of
w
(in a putative
decomposition), the criticality type of
u_{i}
and associated set

of primary pseudo-generators for

,
and the criticality type of

.

Definition 4.11

(Notation for Step

)
.

We denote by

the prefix of

that ends at the end of

,
and by

(to be separately identified within the procedure).
If

is a P3G-critical word, we suppose that it has type
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
,
and hence that
\{s,t\}=\{b^{\prime},c^{\prime}\}

During the step, a letter must be located at a
particular position within the prefix

of

, and denote it by
{\mathbf{d}}
;

subcase within the step,
The letter
{\mathbf{d}}

and it commutes with at most one element of

. If it commutes with only

Particular letters are examined within the step that are found to the left and right of

The letter

{\mathbf{d}}

{\mathbf{d}}^{L}

Proof of the main theorem

We recall Definition

Our main theorem will be an immediate consequence of the following result,

Theorem 5.1

Let

or

Abstract
. Building on work of the first author and Kartas, we identify the elementary class generated by all perfectoid fields of fixed residue characteristic p p in the language of rings.



1.
Introduction

The aim of this note is to identify the elementary class generated by all perfectoid fields
the language of rings
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}=\{0,1,+,\cdot,{-}\}
.
Recall that a field
K
with a nontrivial valuation
v:K^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}
is called perfectoid if

(i)

(K,v)
is complete, and

(ii)

\mathcal{O}_{v}/(p)
is semiperfect, where
p=\mathrm{char}(\mathcal{O}_{v}/\mathfrak{m}_{v})>0
, and

(iii)

v(K^{\times})
is non-discrete.

Here, a ring
R
of characteristic
p
is called semiperfect if the Frobenius map
x\mapsto x^{p}

is surjective on
R
. See subsection
1.1
for details on notation.
Perfectoid fields play a crucial role in perfectoid geometry (cf.
[
16
]
),
and the model-theoretic
properties of perfectoid fields have recently received a considerable amount of attention, e.g.
in work of Kuhlmann–Rzepka
[
11
]
, Kartas
[
9
,
10
]
, Jahnke–Kartas
[
8
]
, Rideau-Kikuchi–Scanlon–Simon
[
14
]
,
Ketelsen
[
KPhD
]
, and Gitin–Koenigsmann–Stock
[
7
]
. The model theory of perfectoid fields is also treated in two recent surveys by
Kuhlmann
[
13
]
and Anscombe
[
3
]
.

As evident from the definition of a perfectoid field, one most naturally considers perfectoid fields in

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}\cup\{\mathcal{O}\}
, where
\mathcal{O}
is a unary predicate interpreted as the valuation ring, fixing the characteristic of the residue field.
Clearly, the fact that
v
is archimedean (i.e.,
v(K^{\times})\leq\mathbb{R}
) is not elementary
in first-order logic, as it is not preserved under ultrapowers.
One way to remedy this is by stipulating that the value group should be regular,
which is a first-order property that characterizes those ordered abelian groups
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oag}}
- elementary
equivalent to (non-trivial) archimedean ones by the work of Robinson and Zakon
[
15
]
,
where
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oag}}=\{0,+\leq\}
.
An ordered abelian group is regular if and only if every quotient by a nontrivial convex subgroup
is divisible
[
5
]
.

Moreover,
completeness of
(K,v)
is not a first-order property either. This can be seen directly from the
Löwenheim-Skolem theorem
[
17
, Theorem 2.1.3]
: Every perfectoid field has a countable elementary substructure,
and no countable field is complete with respect to a nontrivial
valuation.
A common work-around is to
replace completeness by henselianity, as every valued field with archimedean value group
which is complete is henselian

[
6
, Theorem 1.3.1]
.

Last but not least, property (iii) is elementary in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
, and property (ii) is elementary
for fixed

p>0
. As the residue characteristic of a perfectoid field is necessarily positive,
we fix the residue characteristic throughout the paper.

In conclusion, the class

\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\mathrm{val}}=\{(K,v)\textrm{ henselian}\mid v(K^{\times})\textrm{ regular and not discrete, }\mathcal{O}_{v}/(p)\textrm{ semiperfect}\}

is
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
-elementary. However, it
is not generated by the class of perfectoid fields of residue characteristic
p
,
as shown in the next section: it
contains fields not elementarily equivalent to any ultraproducts of perfectoid fields.
We thus focus on the language of rings in section
3
, for which we obtain
a complete answer.

1.1.
Notation and nomenclature

Throughout, by
valuation
, we mean a Krull valuation and we denote all valuations
additively, following the conventions from
[
6
]
.
For a valued field
(K,v)
, we write
\mathcal{O}_{v}
for the valuation ring,

\mathfrak{m}_{v}
for its maximal ideal,

Kv
for its residue field, and
vK
or
v(K^{\times})
for its value group. We call a valuation

v

discrete
if
vK
has a minimum positive element, and non-discrete or dense
otherwise. We say that a valuation is
real-valued
if
vK
is (isomorphic to) a

subgroup
of the real numbers.

2.
Perfectoid fields in the language of valued fields

We start by discussing an example of a henselian valued field
(K,v)
of positive characteristic
that is in the class

\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\mathrm{val}}=\{(K,v)\textrm{ henselian}\mid vK\textrm{ regular and not discrete, }\mathcal{O}_{v}/(p)\textrm{ semiperfect}\}

as introduced in the introduction, but which is not elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct
of perfectoid fields.

Recall that if
(K,v)
does not admit any proper finite immediate extensions,
(K,v)
is called
algebraically maximal
.
Note that if
(K,v)
is a valued field with
vK\leq\mathbb{R}
,
we have
\mathcal{O}_{v}[\varpi^{-1}]=K
for any

\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}\setminus\{0\}
, and in particular, any coarsening of
v

is trivial and thus algebraically maximal.

Example 2.1
.

By
[
8
, Proposition 4.1.4]
, for henselian fields of residue characteristic
p
with

\mathcal{O}_{v}/(p)
semiperfect, the fact that the coarsening
u

corresponding to inverting
\varpi
(i.e.,
\mathcal{O}_{u}=\mathcal{O}_{v}[\varpi^{-1}]
)
is algebraically maximal is an elementary property in

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
together with a constant symbol for
\varpi
. More precisely, it is
axiomatized by the axiom scheme:

For any finite extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
with
{v^{\prime}}K^{\prime}=vK
,

there is
a\in\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}
with
0\leq v(\delta(a))\leq v\varpi
and
K^{\prime}=K(a)
.

(
\star_{\varpi}
)

Here,
\delta(a)
denotes the different of
a
; the axiom scheme
(\star_{\varpi})
contains
one sentence for each potential degree
[K^{\prime}:K]
. In conclusion, for any perfectoid field
(K,v)
,
we have

(K,v)\models\forall\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}:(\star_{\varpi})\textrm{ holds}.

Let now
\mathcal{U}
be a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\omega
and consider the ultraproduct

(L_{0},w_{0})\coloneq(\mathbb{F}_{p}((t)),v_{t})^{\mathcal{U}}.

Let
L
denote the perfect
hull of
L_{0}
, and let
w
denote the unique extension of
w_{0}
to
L
. Then
(L,w)
is henselian, with value group regular and non-discrete. As
L
is perfect of characteristic
p
,
the ring
\mathcal{O}_{w}/(p)=\mathcal{O}_{w}
is semiperfect.
However, by Example 4.1.5 in
[
Schaaf
]
, the property
(\star_{\varpi})
fails in
(L,w)
(for
p\neq 2
) for all
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{w}\setminus\{0\}
.
Hence we conclude

(L,w)\models\forall\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{w}:(\star_{\varpi})\textrm{ fails}.

Thus,
L
is not
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
-elementarily equivalent to a perfectoid field, nor to an
ultraproduct of perfectoid fields.

A corresponding example of a valued field mixed characteristic which is in
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\mathrm{val}}

but not elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct of perfectoid fields (due to the failure of
the same axiom scheme)
was constructed by Leo Gitin.

The example above gives rise to the following

Question 2.2
.

Is the
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
-elementary class generated by perfectoid fields of residue characteristic
p

given by the subclass
\mathcal{C}_{p,\star}^{\mathrm{val}}
of
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\mathrm{val}}
such that
(\star_{\varpi})
holds
for all
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}\setminus\{0\}
?

As we briefly discuss in the next paragraphs, a potential
route to answering this question is to
construct an elementarily equivalent real-valued
field given any
(K,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{p,\star}^{\mathrm{val}}
. Such a construction seems beyond our current techniques, unless
(K,v)
is tame or of finite rank.

Definition
.

Let
(K,v)
be a henselian valued field with value group
\Gamma
and residue field
k
. A finite valued field extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
is said to be tame if the following are satisfied:

(1)

If
p=\text{char}(k)>0
, then
p\nmid[\Gamma^{\prime}:\Gamma]
.

(2)

The residue field extension
k^{\prime}/k
is separable.

(3)

The extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
is defectless, i.e.,

[K^{\prime}:K]=[\Gamma^{\prime}:\Gamma]\cdot[k^{\prime}:k]

We say that
(K,v)
is tame if every finite valued field extension of
(K,v)
is tame.

Note that tame valued fields form an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
-elementary class and are in particular henselian.
All tame fields are algebraically maximal since
every defectless henselian field is algebraically maximal. Moreover, all coarsenings
of a tame valuation are again tame
[
12
, Lemma 3.14]
and hence algebraically maximal. By

[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
, for any (non-trivially valued) tame field
(K,v)

of residue
characteristic
p>0
the residue ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(p)
is semiperfect. Thus, any tame field
of residue characteristic
p>0
is in the class

\mathcal{C}_{p,\star}^{\mathrm{val}}
.
Kuhlmann proves Ax–Kochen/Ershov principles for tame fields in
[
12
]
.
As a straightforward application of Kuhlmann’s results, we show:

Lemma 2.3
.

(1)

Any tame field
(K,v)
of positive characteristic with regular value group
is
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
-elementarily equivalent to a real-valued field.

(2)

Any tame field
(K,v)
of mixed characteristic
with divisible value group
is
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
-elementarily equivalent to a real-valued field.

Proof
.

(1)

If
\mathrm{char}(K)=p>0
,
vK\equiv\Gamma\leq\mathbb{R}
and
(K,v)
is tame,
then
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
implies
(K,v)\equiv(Kv((t^{\Gamma})),v_{t}))
.

(2)

If
(K,v)
is tame of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with
vK\equiv\mathbb{Q}
, then
consider the subfield
F_{0}\subseteq K
generated by adjoining to
(\mathbb{Q},v_{p})
the lift of a transcendence base of
Kv

to
K
.
Writing
w_{0}
for the Gauss extension of
v_{p}
to
F_{0}
(see
[
6
, Corollary 2.2.2]
),
we obtain
(F_{0},w_{0})\subseteq(K,v)

and
w_{0}F_{0}=\mathbb{Z}
by construction.
Let now
F
be the relative algebraic closure of
F_{0}
in
K
, and let

w
denote the restriction of
v
to
F
. Then, we get
Fw=Kv
and
wF\leq\mathbb{Q}
.
By
[
12
, Lemma 3.7]
, we conclude
that
(F,w)
is an elementary substructure of
(K,v)
with value group

wF=\mathbb{Q}
.
\Box

It is not known whether Lemma
2.3
(2) can be strengthened to the analogue of (1) in
mixed characteristic.

More generally, Ax–Kochen/Ershov type principles are proven for almost tame fields (thus named in

[
3
]
), a class
including all perfectoid fields in
[
8
]
.
Most AK/E type principles reduce the study of a sufficiently well-behaved
valued field to that of its value group and residue field.
Note that perfectoid fields may
admit immediate algebraic extensions: for example,
adjoining an Artin-Schreier root of
1/t
gives an
immediate extension of the perfectoid field
(\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}((t))^{\mathrm{perf}}},v_{t})
.
Thus, there is little hope of reducing the theory of a perfectoid field down to the theories
of its residue field and value group. Instead, the residue field is replaced by an
infinitesimal thickening, namely the residue ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(\varpi)
for some
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}\setminus\{0\}
. The corresponding AK/E type result is then the following:

Theorem 2.4

(
[
8
, Theorem 5.1.4]
)
.

Let
(K,v)\subseteq(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
be two henselian valued fields of residue characteristic
p>0
such that
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(p)
and
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/(p)
are semiperfect. Suppose there is
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}
such that both
\mathcal{O}_{v}[\varpi^{-1}]
and
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}[\varpi^{-1}]
are algebraically maximal. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(K,v)\preceq(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{O}_{v}/(\varpi)\preceq\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/(\varpi)
in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
and
vK\preceq v^{\prime}K^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oag}}
.

Moreover, if
vK
and
v^{\prime}K^{\prime}
are regularly dense, then the value group condition in
(ii)
can be omitted.

Note that, as discussed at the beginning of the section,
Theorem
2.4
applies in particular to any real-valued field in
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\mathrm{val}}
.
As a minor application, we
see immediately that any real-valued field
(K,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\mathrm{val}}
is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
-elementarily equivalent to a perfectoid field:

Corollary 2.5
.

(1)

Let
(K,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\mathrm{val}}
such that
vK\leq\mathbb{R}
and let
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
be its completion. Then
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
is perfectoid and the embedding
(K,v)\subseteq(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
is elementary.

(2)

Conversely, if
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
is perfectoid and
(K_{0},v_{0})\subseteq(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
is any subfield such that
the extension is immediate, i.e., we have
K_{0}v_{0}=Kv
and
v_{0}K_{0}=Kv
. Then,
the extension
(K,v)\coloneqq(K_{0}^{\mathrm{perf}},v_{0})^{h}\subseteq(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
is elementary.

Proof.

Note that if
vK\leq\mathbb{R}
, and
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
is the completion of
(K,v)
, and so
(K,v)
is
dense in
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
. In particular, for any
\gamma\in vK
and
a\in K^{\prime}
there is
b\in K
with

v^{\prime}(a)-v^{\prime}(b)>\gamma
.
Thus, for
any
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}\setminus\{0\}
we get

\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/(\varpi)=\mathcal{O}_{v}/(\varpi)
.
Hence, both (i) and (ii) follow immediately from Theorem
2.4
.
For (ii), note that as
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
is an immediate complete extension of
(K,v)
(which in turn embeds to
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
by the universal property of the henselization),
it is in fact its completion.
∎

Thus, finding real-valued models of the
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
-theory of

would answer Question

.

3.
Perfectoid fields in the language of rings

The aim of this section is to characterize the
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-theory generated by the
class of perfectoid fields of residue
characteristic
p
. Note that a non-algebraically closed field
K
can at most admit one valuation such that
(K,v)
is
perfectoid, so being perfectoid of residue characteristic
p>0
makes sense as a property of a
(pure) field
K
.
We first prove the following characterization, which is implicit in
[
8
]
:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
L
be any field.

(1)

If
L
admits a
nontrivial tame valuation with divisible value group and residue characteristic
p>0
, then

L
is
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementarily equivalent to a perfectoid field of residue characteristic

p>0
.

(2)

Conversely, any perfectoid field
L
of residue characteristic
p>0
is
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementarily equivalent to a field admitting a nontrivial tame valuation with divisible value group and residue characteristic
p>0
.

Proof
.

(1)

Assume that
L
admits a nontrivial tame henselian valuation
v

with divisible value group and residue characteristic
p>0
. By Lemma
2.3
, there
is some
(M,w)\equiv(K,v)
such that
wM\leq\mathbb{R}
. Since completions are
immediate extensions and the completion of a rank-
1
tame field is again tame, by
[
12
]
), we obtain
(M^{\prime},w^{\prime})\coloneqq\widehat{(M,w)}\succeq(M,w)\equiv(K,v)
.
Since
(M^{\prime},w^{\prime})
is perfectoid (note that
\mathcal{O}_{w^{\prime}}/(p)
is semiperfect by

[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
), we
conclude that
L
is indeed
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementarily equivalent to a perfectoid field.

(2)

Let
(L,v)
be perfectoid, and let
(L,v)^{\mathcal{U}}
be any nonprincipal
ultrapower. By
[
8
, Main Theorem]
,
L^{\mathcal{U}}
admits a nontrivial
tame valuation
with divisible value group of residue characteristic
p>0
, as desired.
\Box

In
[
1
]
, fields
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementarily equivalent to fields admitting nontrivial tame valuations with divisible value group were called
t-henselian of divisible tame type
.

Remark 3.2
.

The class of fields

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\mathrm{ring}}=\{K\mid\;

\displaystyle K\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}}L\textrm{ for some $L$ admitting a non-trivial
valuation
$w$ such that}

\displaystyle\textrm{$(L,w)$ tame, $wL$ divisible, }\mathrm{char}(Lw)=p\}

is
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementary: by definition it is closed under elementary equivalence. As admitting a nontrivial
tame henselian valuation with divisible value group and residue characteristic
p>0
is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
-axiomatizable,
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\mathrm{ring}}

is also closed under ultraproducts.
By Proposition
3.1
,
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\mathrm{ring}}
is indeed the
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementary class generated
by all perfectoid fields of residue characteristic
p>0
.

As a straightforward consequence of Proposition
3.1
, we obtain:

Corollary 3.3
.

Any ultraproduct of perfectoid fields of fixed residue characteristic
p>0

is
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementarily equivalent to a perfectoid field.

Whether this generalizes to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
remains open:

Question 3.4
.

Is any ultraproduct of perfectoid fields of fixed residue characteristic
p>0

already
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}
-elementarily equivalent to a perfectoid field
(K,v)
?

We now show that for fields of characteristic
0
, the
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-reduct of the class

\mathcal{C}_{p}^{\mathrm{val}}
is
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementary and is the indeed the
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementary class generated
by all perfectoid fields of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
. We begin with the following well-known observations:

Lemma 3.5
.

(1)

Admitting a henselian valuation of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
is an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementary property.

(2)

The valuation ring of any unramified henselian valuation of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
(i.e.,
v(p)
minimum positive) is
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-definable without parameters (by a formula depending only on
p
).
Thus, admitting an unramified henselian valuation of mixed characteristic
(0,p)

is an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementary property.

Proof.

(1)

Admitting a henselian valuation of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
is preserved
under
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementary equivalence (see also
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
): if
K\equiv L
and
K
admits a henselian valuation

of mixed characteristic, any common ultrapower of
K
and
L

also admits a henselian valuation of mixed characteristic (namely
v^{\mathcal{U}}
). Restricting
v^{\mathcal{U}}

gives a henselian valuation

L
is relatively algebraically closed in the ultrapower), which is of mixed characteristic (as
p\in\mathfrak{m}_{v^{\mathcal{U}}}

that any ultraproduct
of fields admitting a henselian valuation of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
also admits a
henselian valuation of characteristic
(0,p)
(cf.
[
4

(2)

Any mixed characteristic henselian valuation
(K,v)
with

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-definable by a classical result of Robinson:
For
p\neq 2

\varphi_{p}(x):(\exists y)[1+px^{2}=y^{2}]

defines
\mathcal{O}_{v}
, for

\mathcal{O}_{v}

\varphi_{2}(x):(\exists y)[1+2x^{3}=y^{3}]
.
Thus, admitting a henselian valuation of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
which is unramified

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ring}}
-elementary equivalence and ultraproducts.

In both cases, we conclude by
[
4
, 4.1.12]
.
∎

Proposition 3.6

Let

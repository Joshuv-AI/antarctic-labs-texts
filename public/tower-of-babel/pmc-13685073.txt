Risk factors for recurrent disease after loop electrosurgical excision procedure in patients with cervical intraepithelial neoplasia: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Yanlu Ge, Zhilei Chen, Yingping Zhu, Haiou Xu, Lifeng Li, Qun Zhou, Feifei Wang, Haimin Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLoop electrosurgical excision procedure (LEEP) is a widely used treatment for cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN). However, some patients remain at risk of residual or recurrent disease after treatment. Identifying predictors of recurrence is important for improving postoperative surveillance and individualized management. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to synthesize the available evidence on risk factors for recurrent disease after LEEP in patients with CIN.MethodsPubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, Scopus, CNKI, WanFang, and VIP databases were searched from inception to February 10, 2026. Observational studies reporting factors associated with recurrent disease after LEEP in patients with CIN were eligible. Literature screening, data extraction, and quality assessment were independently conducted by two reviewers. Study quality was assessed using the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS). Statistical analyses were performed using Stata 15.0. Pooled odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated.ResultsA total of 17 studies involving 8,347 patients were included. The pooled analysis showed that positive surgical margins (OR = 4.55, 95% CI: 3.00–6.90), persistent hr-HPV infection (OR = 10.44, 95% CI: 7.77–14.03), higher-grade lesions (OR = 1.85, 95% CI: 1.27–2.70), smoking (OR = 2.09, 95% CI: 1.34–3.25), and older age (OR = 1.32, 95% CI: 1.08–1.61) were significantly associated with an increased risk of recurrent disease after LEEP.ConclusionsPositive surgical margins, persistent hr-HPV infection, higher-grade lesions, and smoking appear to be important risk factors for recurrent disease after LEEP in patients with CIN. Older age may also be associated with recurrence, although this finding should be interpreted with caution because of potential publication bias. These findings may help inform postoperative risk stratification and individualized follow-up strategies in this population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12905-026-04689-4.

Background

Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) is a common precancerous lesion of cervical cancer and represents an important stage in cervical carcinogenesis [1, 2]. Persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus (hr-HPV) is recognized as the major etiological factor for the development and progression of CIN [3]. Although some low-grade lesions may regress spontaneously, high-grade lesions, especially CIN2 and CIN3, are associated with a substantially increased risk of progression to invasive cervical cancer if left untreated [4, 5]. Therefore, effective treatment and appropriate follow-up of CIN are essential for cervical cancer prevention and for improving women’s health outcomes.

Loop electrosurgical excision procedure (LEEP) is one of the most widely used treatments for CIN, particularly for high-grade lesions. Because it is relatively simple, cost-effective, and allows removal of the lesion while providing tissue specimens for histopathological evaluation, LEEP has been broadly adopted in clinical practice [6]. Despite its generally favorable therapeutic efficacy, recurrent disease still occurs in a proportion of patients after treatment. This remains a clinically important issue, as recurrent disease may lead to repeated procedures, prolonged surveillance, increased healthcare costs, and considerable psychological burden [7, 8]. More importantly, recurrent high-grade lesions may increase the risk of subsequent progression to cervical cancer if not detected and managed in a timely manner [9].

A number of studies [10, 11] have explored factors associated with recurrent disease after LEEP in patients with CIN. Positive surgical margins, persistent hr-HPV infection, older age, smoking, higher-grade lesions, glandular involvement, and immunosuppression have all been proposed as potential predictors of recurrence. However, the available evidence remains inconsistent. While some studies have identified positive margins and postoperative hr-HPV persistence as the most important predictors, others have reported different clinicopathological factors to be significantly associated with recurrent disease [12]. Differences in study design, sample size, patient characteristics, follow-up duration, recurrence definitions, and statistical adjustment may partly explain these discrepancies. As a result, the risk factors for recurrent disease after LEEP have not yet been fully clarified [13].

A comprehensive synthesis of the current evidence is therefore needed to better identify patients at high risk of recurrence and to support individualized postoperative surveillance and management. Accordingly, we conducted this systematic review and meta-analysis to systematically evaluate the risk factors for recurrent disease after LEEP in patients with CIN.

Methods

Study design and registration

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) statement [14]. The study protocol was prospectively registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; registration number: CRD420251267284).

Literature search strategy

A comprehensive literature search was performed in PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library, Scopus, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), WanFang Data, and VIP Database from database inception to February 10, 2026. The search strategy combined Medical Subject Headings (MeSH), Emtree terms, and free-text keywords related to cervical intraepithelial neoplasia, LEEP, recurrent disease, and risk factors. The main search terms included “cervical intraepithelial neoplasia”, “CIN”, “LEEP”, “recurrent disease”, “recurrence”, and “risk factors”. The search strategies were adapted for each database as appropriate. In addition, the reference lists of relevant articles and reviews were manually screened to identify potentially eligible studies [15].

Eligibility criteria

Studies were included if they met all of the following criteria: (1) the study population consisted of patients diagnosed with cervical intraepithelial neoplasia who underwent LEEP; (2) The primary outcome was treatment failure after excisional treatment, including persistent, residual, or recurrent CIN. Because the terminology and definitions varied across the included studies, outcomes were extracted according to each study’s original definition. Studies reporting combined outcomes (e.g., persistent/residual/recurrent disease) were analyzed as treatment failure to ensure consistency across the quantitative synthesis; (3) at least one potential risk factor for recurrent disease was evaluated; (4) Adjusted ORs were preferentially extracted for quantitative synthesis. Several studies reported HRs derived from Cox proportional hazards models; because HRs incorporate time-to-event information and are not directly equivalent to ORs, these estimates were not pooled together with ORs. Instead, HRs were summarized descriptively in a supplementary table. No conversion between HRs and ORs was performed; and (5) the study was designed as an observational study, including cohort studies or case-control studies.

Studies were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: (1) reviews, meta-analyses, case reports, conference abstracts, editorials, letters, comments, or animal studies; (2) studies without sufficient data for extraction or pooling; (3) studies not involving patients treated with LEEP; (4) studies not reporting recurrent disease after LEEP; or (5) duplicate publications based on the same study population, in which case only the most complete or most recent report was included.

Study selection

All retrieved records were imported into reference management software, and duplicate records were removed before screening. Two reviewers independently screened the titles and abstracts of all identified articles to exclude clearly irrelevant studies. The full texts of potentially eligible studies were then reviewed in detail according to the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Any discrepancies between the two reviewers were resolved through discussion, and consultation with a third reviewer was undertaken when necessary. The study selection process was documented using a PRISMA flow diagram.

Data extraction

Two reviewers independently extracted data from the included studies using a standardized data extraction form. The following information was collected: first author, publication year, country, study design, sample size, patient characteristics, follow-up duration, definition of recurrent disease, candidate risk factors, effect estimates, and variables adjusted for in multivariable analyses where available. When both crude and adjusted estimates were available, adjusted effect estimates were preferentially extracted. If only crude estimates were reported, crude estimates were extracted. The adjustment variables used in multivariable analyses for each included study are summarized in Supplementary Table S2. Any disagreements in data extraction were resolved by discussion and consensus.

Quality assessment

The methodological quality of the included studies was independently assessed by two reviewers using the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) [16]. This tool evaluates observational studies across three domains: selection of study groups, comparability of groups, and ascertainment of exposure or outcome. NOS scores range from 0 to 9, with higher scores indicating better methodological quality. Disagreements in quality assessment were resolved through discussion until consensus was achieved. The overall certainty of evidence for each pooled outcome was additionally assessed using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Stata version 15.0 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA). Pooled adjusted odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated to evaluate the associations between candidate risk factors and recurrent disease after LEEP. Given the anticipated clinical and methodological heterogeneity across the included studies, all meta-analyses were conducted using random-effects models. Statistical heterogeneity was assessed using Cochran’s Q test and the I² statistic. Sensitivity analyses were performed by sequentially omitting individual studies to evaluate the robustness of the pooled results. Publication bias was assessed by visual inspection of funnel plots and Egger’s test when at least 10 studies were available for a given analysis. If publication bias was suspected, the trim-and-fill method was further applied to estimate its potential impact on the pooled effect size. A two-sided P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study selection

A total of 1,672 records were identified through database searching. After removing 648 duplicate records, 1,024 records were screened by title and abstract. Seventy-six reports were sought for retrieval, of which two could not be retrieved. After full-text assessment of 74 articles, 57 were excluded for predefined reasons. Finally,17 studies [17–33] met the inclusion criteria for the final meta-analysis (Fig. 1).

Study characteristics

The main characteristics of the included studies are summarized in Table 1. A total of 17 studies involving 8,347 patients were included in the meta-analysis, among whom 1,013 developed recurrent disease after LEEP. The included studies were published between 2010 and 2024 and were conducted in several countries, including China [18, 22, 30–33], the United States [17, 28], Japan [19], the Republic of Korea [20, 27], Chile [21], Italy [23, 29], Canada [24], Greece [25], and Australia [26]. Most studies were retrospective cohort studies [17–29, 32], while a smaller number were prospective cohort studies [30, 31, 33]. Sample sizes ranged from 80 to 4,369 participants, and the reported age characteristics varied across studies, with mean or median ages ranging from 31 to 45 years. In most included studies, recurrent disease was defined as CIN2 + recurrence after LEEP during follow-up.

Meta-analysis of risk factors for recurrent disease after LEEP

Most included studies reported ORs and were included in the quantitative meta-analysis. Studies reporting HRs from Cox proportional hazards models were not pooled with ORs because of differences in the underlying effect measures. These HR-based findings are summarized descriptively in Supplementary Table S4.

Positive surgical margins

As shown in Figs. 2 and 11 studies reported the association between positive surgical margins and recurrent disease after LEEP. The pooled analysis demonstrated that positive surgical margins were significantly associated with a higher risk of recurrent disease (OR = 4.55, 95% CI: 3.00–6.90). Moderate heterogeneity was detected across studies (I² = 59.5%, P = 0.006). Sensitivity analysis for positive surgical margins was conducted by sequentially excluding individual studies, and the detailed results are shown in Supplementary Figure S1.

As shown in Fig. 3, six studies evaluated the association between persistent hr-HPV infection and recurrent disease after LEEP. Publication bias

Publication bias (Supplementary Figure S3-Figure S7) was evaluated using funnel plots and Egger’s test for analyses with sufficient studies. Egger’s test showed no significant evidence of publication bias for positive surgical margins (P = 0.966), persistent hr-HPV infection (P = 0.921), smoking (P = 0.648), or higher-grade lesions (P = 0.093), whereas significant publication bias was detected for age (P = 0.001). Accordingly, the trim-and-fill method (Supplementary Figure S8) was performed for the age analysis, indicating that the original finding for age may have been influenced by publication bias.

Discussion

In the present systematic review and meta-analysis, we examined the main factors associated with recurrent disease after LEEP in patients with CIN. The pooled results showed that positive surgical margins, persistent hr-HPV infection, higher-grade lesions, smoking, and older age were all associated with an increased risk of recurrence. Among these variables, persistent hr-HPV infection and positive margins showed the strongest associations. These findings suggest that both incomplete lesion clearance and continued viral persistence are key issues in patients who experience recurrence after treatment. Our findings may also be interpreted in the broader context of HPV-associated lower genital tract intraepithelial neoplasia. Like cervical disease, recent evidence in patients with high-grade vaginal intraepithelial neoplasia has shown that recurrence is influenced by clinical characteristics, including patient age, and highlights the importance of long-term surveillance and individualized risk stratification. Although vaginal intraepithelial neoplasia and CIN differ biologically and clinically, these findings support the concept that risk-based follow-up is essential across HPV-related premalignant lesions [34].

Our analysis showed that positive surgical margins were associated with recurrent disease. Positive margins often indicate that abnormal tissue may remain after excision, which creates the possibility for persistent or recurrent lesions during follow-up [35]. In practice, margin status has long been regarded as an important indicator after cervical excisional treatment, and our findings further support its value in postoperative risk assessment [36]. For patients with positive margins, closer follow-up may be warranted, especially during the early post-treatment period.

Persistent hr-HPV infection had the largest effect size in this study, with a pooled OR of 10.44, and no meaningful heterogeneity was observed across studies. This result was not unexpected. Since hr-HPV infection is the central driver of CIN development, persistence of the virus after LEEP likely reflects continued pathogenic activity even after the visible lesion has been removed [37]. In other words, treatment of the lesion does not necessarily mean elimination of the underlying etiological factor. This may explain why persistent hr-HPV positivity was such a strong predictor of recurrent disease [38]. From a clinical standpoint, this finding highlights the importance of postoperative hr-HPV monitoring. Patients who remain hr-HPV positive after treatment may require more intensive surveillance than those who clear the virus.

Higher-grade lesions were also associated with a greater risk of recurrence. Compared with lower-grade disease, high-grade lesions may reflect a larger lesion burden, more extensive epithelial involvement, or a more advanced stage of cervical carcinogenesis. These features may increase the difficulty of achieving complete removal and may partly explain the higher recurrence rate observed in this group [39]. Although the magnitude of association was smaller than that seen for positive margins or persistent hr-HPV infection, the result remained statistically significant and clinically relevant [40]. This suggests that lesion severity at baseline should still be considered when planning follow-up after LEEP.

Smoking was another significant factor identified in this study. Women with a smoking history had approximately twice the risk of recurrent disease compared with non-smokers. This association is also biologically plausible [41]. Smoking has been linked to impaired local immune function, reduced ability to clear HPV infection, and an altered cervical microenvironment that may favor persistence of dysplastic changes. In addition, smoking has long been recognized as a cofactor in cervical carcinogenesis [42]. Although moderate heterogeneity was present in this analysis, the pooled result remained stable in sensitivity analysis, suggesting that the association was relatively robust. These findings also reinforce the need to include smoking cessation advice in postoperative management.

Older age was associated with a modestly increased risk of recurrent disease. One possible explanation is that older women may have a longer duration of HPV exposure, reduced viral clearance, or age-related changes in the cervical transformation zone that complicate complete treatment and follow-up assessment [43, 44]. However, this result should be interpreted carefully. In our study, publication bias was detected for the age analysis, and after trim-and-fill adjustment, the association was no longer statistically significant [45]. This suggests that the effect of age may have been overestimated in the original pooled analysis. Therefore, while age may still be relevant in clinical decision-making, the current evidence does not appear to be as strong as that for positive margins or persistent hr-HPV infection.

These findings have several practical implications. First, not all patients appear to carry the same risk of recurrence after LEEP, and postoperative management may benefit from a more individualized approach. In particular, patients with positive margins or persistent hr-HPV infection seem to represent a clearly higher-risk group. Second, factors such as lesion grade and smoking may also help refine follow-up intensity and counseling. Rather than applying the same surveillance schedule to all patients, clinicians may consider tailoring follow-up according to the patient’s risk profile. This may help improve early detection of recurrence and avoid delayed intervention in high-risk cases.

Several limitations should be noted when interpreting our findings. First, all included studies were observational, and residual confounding cannot be completely ruled out. Although adjusted estimates were extracted whenever possible, the adjustment strategies were not uniform across studies. Second, there was some heterogeneity in the analyses of positive margins, smoking, and age, which may be related to differences in patient characteristics, follow-up duration, recurrence definitions, and study design. Third, the definition of recurrent disease was not entirely consistent across studies, although most used CIN2 + recurrence during follow-up. Fourth, publication bias was detected in the age analysis, which weakens confidence in that result. Finally, some potentially relevant factors could not be pooled because the available data were limited or inconsistently reported.

Despite these limitations, this study also has strengths. We included evidence from both English and Chinese databases, which allowed a broader assessment of the available literature. In addition, the main findings were clinically coherent and generally stable in sensitivity analyses. More importantly, this study provides a clearer summary of the factors most consistently associated with recurrent disease after LEEP, which may be useful for both clinicians and future research.

Conclusion

This meta-analysis suggests that positive surgical margins, persistent hr-HPV infection, higher-grade lesions, and smoking are important risk factors for recurrent disease after LEEP in patients with CIN. Older age may also be associated with recurrence, although this finding should be interpreted with caution because of potential publication bias. These findings support risk-based postoperative surveillance and may help identify patients who require closer follow-up after treatment. Further large-scale prospective studies are needed to confirm these associations and to improve recurrence risk stratification after LEEP.

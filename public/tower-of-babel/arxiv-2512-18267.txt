Well-posedness of the Euler system of gas dynamics
Eduard Feireisl, Mária Lukáčová-Medvi d’ ová

Abstract
We propose a new two-step selection criterion applicable to the dissipative measure–valued solutions of the Euler system of gas dynamics. The process consists of a successive maximisation of the entropy production rate and the total energy defect, i.e. maximisation of the turbulent energy. • If the selected solution is a weak solution of the Euler system, then it is identified in the first step. Solutions selected in the second step are truly measure–valued maximising the energy defect. Accordingly, they are called turbulent solutions. • The energy defect of turbulent solutions vanishes with growing time. • The selected solutions depend in a Borel–measurable way on the initial data. In particular, they are “ almost continuously dependent ” on the initial data. Finally, we discuss a selection criterion yielding a maximal dissipative solution of the Euler system in a single step.

1
Introduction

The Euler system of gas dynamics governing the motion of
a gas confined to a bounded domain

\Omega\subset R^{d}
,
d=1,2,3
consists of three
field equations (balance laws):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}\bm{m}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{m}+{\rm div}_{x}\left(\frac{\bm{m}\otimes\bm{m}}{\varrho}\right)+\nabla_{x}p

\displaystyle=0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E+{\rm div}_{x}\left[\Big(E+p\Big)\frac{\bm{m}}{\varrho}\right]

\displaystyle=0,

(1.3)

where
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
is the mass density,
\bm{m}=\bm{m}(t,x)=\varrho\bm{u}(t,x)
the momentum with the velocity
\bm{u}
,

p
the pressure, and
E
the total energy. In addition, we impose the impermeability condition

\bm{m}\cdot\bm{n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ \bm{n}\ \mbox{the outer normal vector to}\ \partial\Omega.

(1.4)

As is well-known, the classical solutions of the Euler
system develop singularities - shock waves - for a fairly general class of initial data, while the problem admits infinitely many weak (distributional) solutions. What is more suprising is the fact that the ill-posedness in the class of weak solutions persists even if the original system is supplemented by an
admissibility condition

\partial_{t}S+{\rm div}_{x}\left(S\frac{\bm{m}}{\varrho}\right)\geq 0,

(1.5)

where
S
is the total entropy of the system. More specifically, the recent results based on the application of convex integration include:

•

The existence of infinitely many admissible weak solutions for the system
(
1.1
)–(
1.4
) for a vast class of initial data, see
[
12
]
,
[
17
]
.

•

Density of the so-called wild data giving rise to infinitely many admissible weak solutions, see
[
8
]
.

•

Ill-posedness of the problem perturbed by a stochastic forcing, see
[
9
]
.

1.1
Two-step selection process

The leading idea behind the concept of
turbulent solution
goes back to
[
4
]
, where a multilevel selection process was proposed to identify
a unique element in the class of
dissipative measure–valued
(DMV) solutions of the Euler system. The DMV solutions can be seen as limits of suitable consistent approximations arising as artificial viscosity limits or
asymptotic limits of stable and consistent numerical schemes, cf. the monograph
[
13
]
.

Similarly to
[
4
]
, the turbulent solutions of the Euler system (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) will be represented by the trio
(\varrho,\bm{m},S)
supplemented with the
total energy

\mathcal{E}_{0}=\int_{\Omega}E_{0}\ \,{\rm d}{x},

(1.6)

where
E_{0}=E(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})
is the energy of the system at the initial state
(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})
.

In accordance with the boundary condition (
1.4
), the system is energetically
closed
. Our approach is based on enforcing two underlying physical principles:

•

The First Law of Thermodynamics.
The total energy
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{0}
is constant in time.

•

The Second Law of Thermodynamics.
The total
entropy
\int_{\Omega}S(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}
is a non–decreasing function of time.

The turbulent solutions will be selected among all
DMV solutions to the Euler system emanating from given initial data. A prominent role in the selection process is played by the
mean energy

\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x},\ \mbox{where}\ E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\bm{m}|^{2}}{\varrho}+\varrho e(\varrho,S).

(1.7)

Unlike the total energy
\mathcal{E}
, the mean energy may not be constant in time but satisfies the inequality

\mathcal{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t)=\mathcal{E}-\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\geq 0,

(1.8)

where the quantity
\mathcal{D}(t)

is called
energy defect
.
The energy defect can be identified with
turbulent energy
- the difference between the total and the mean energy of the system.
As we shall see, the
standard
weak solutions
of the Euler system are characterized by the property
\mathcal{D}\equiv 0
. The turbulent solutions, in general, will maximise the energy defect, in particular,
\mathcal{D}(t)>0
.

Let

\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0}]

denote the family of all DMV solutions emanating from the
initial data
(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})
defined on the full time interval
t\in[0,\infty)
. Following the strategy of
Krylov
[
20
]
, developed in the context of deterministic problems by Cardona and Kapitanski
[
6
]
, and finally adapted to the Euler system in
[
4
]
, we identify the unique solution via

successive
maximisation/minimisation of two “cost” functionals:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{S}[\varrho,\bm{m},S]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\left(\int_{\Omega}S(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\,{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{E}[\varrho,\bm{m},S]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\left(\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\,{\rm d}t.

(1.9)

As the set
\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0}]
is convex
and
\mathcal{F}_{S}
linear , the set of maximisers

\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0}]={\rm argmax}\,\mathcal{F}_{S}\Big[\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0}]\Big]\subset\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0}]

is a convex subset of
\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0}]
.
Then the turbulent solution is obtained in the second minimisation step

\mathcal{U}_{ES}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)={\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}_{E}\Big[{\rm argmax}\,\mathcal{F}_{S}[\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0}]\Big].

The fact that the second step yields a unique solution follows from the strict convexity of the functional
\mathcal{F}_{E}
. The strict convexity of
\mathcal{F}_{E}
, in turn, is equivalent to the hypothesis of thermodynamic stability imposed on the equations of state.

Similarly to
[
11
]
, we have reduced the selection procedure to two steps. The idea of obtaining
turbulent solutions via maximising the energy defect
was also used by Klingenberg, Markfelder, and Wiedemann

[
18
]
. In the present context, this criterion yields a (unique) solution in only one step, minimising
\mathcal{F}_{E}
directly. Unfortunately, such a selection would automatically eliminate all
weak
solutions of the
problem, except in a very unlikely event that there is a weak solution unique in the class of DMV solutions.
Maximising the entropy production in the first step will select physically admissible solutions, including possibly weak solutions. In particular, we show that all of them comply with DiPerna’s maximality criterion, cf.
[
10
]
.
Moreover, their energy defect
\mathcal{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t)
vanishes asymptotically as
t\to\infty
.
The second step identifies the unique turbulent solution that maximises the energy defect—the turbulent energy. Thus, these two selection steps seem to go in opposite directions.

1.2
Main objectives

The two-step selection process described in the previous section yields
the unique turbulent solution of the Euler system. Besides providing
a detailed description of the selection process, we shall discuss the following issues.

•

Well-posedness.

For any given initial data
(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})
, and the associated total energy
\mathcal{E}_{0}
, the Euler system admits a unique, turbulent, solution
(\varrho,\bm{m},S)
. The mapping

(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})\mapsto(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t,\cdot)

is Borel–measurable in suitable topologies. Consequently, applying the method.
of
[
4
]
we show the turbulent solutions form
a Borel–measurable semigroup, see Section
2
, and
Theorem
3.5
, Section
3
, Theorem
4.4
, Section
4
.

•

Weak regularity.

If the selected solution is a
weak
solution, then it must be identified in the first step, see Section
3
. Any solution selected in the second step is a truly turbulent solution with a non-zero energy defect
\mathcal{D}
, see Section
4
.

•

Vanishing energy defect.

The energy defect of any turbulent solution vanishes with growing time,

\mathcal{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t)\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ t\to\infty,

(1.10)

see Section
3
.

•

Almost continuity.

Given
\varepsilon>0
and a Borel probability measure on the space of initial data, the solution semigroup is continuous on a complement of a set of
\varepsilon-
measure, see Section
5
.

2
Dissipative measure–valued (DMV) solutions

For definiteness,
we consider a polytropic equation of state (EOS)

p=(\gamma-1)\varrho e,\ \gamma>1.

(2.1)

Moreover, without loss of generality, we introduce the
absolute temperature

\vartheta
proportional to internal energy
e
,

e=c_{v}\vartheta,\ \mbox{where}\ c_{v}=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}.

(2.2)

Accordingly, we set

p(\varrho,S)=(\gamma-1)\varrho e(\varrho,S)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\varrho^{\gamma}\exp\left(\frac{S}{c_{v}\varrho}\right)\ \mbox{if}\ \varrho>0,\\
0\ \mbox{if}\ \varrho=0,\ S\leq 0,\\
\infty\ \mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

It is easy to check

\frac{\partial\varrho e(\varrho,S)}{\partial S}=\vartheta>0\ \mbox{whenever}\ \varrho>0,

(2.4)

meaning
\varrho e(\varrho,S)
is a strictly increasing function of
S
.
Introducing the kinetic energy

\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\bm{m}|^{2}}{\varrho}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\bm{m}|^{2}}{\varrho}\ \mbox{if}\ \varrho>0,\\
0\ \mbox{if}\ \varrho=0,\bm{m}=0,\\
\infty\ \mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

we can check that the total energy

E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\bm{m}|^{2}}{\varrho}+\varrho e(\varrho,S)

is a convex l.s.c. function of the state variables
(\bm{m},\varrho,S)\in R^{d+2}
, strictly convex on its domain.
The convexity of the total energy is equivalent to

thermodynamic stability
of the system, cf.
Bechtel, Rooney, Forest
[
2
]
or

[
13
, Chapter 2, Section 2.2.4]
. This assumption
is absolutely crucial in the subsequent analysis. More general EOS can be considered as long as the thermodynamic stability is preserved.

2.1
DMV solutions

A dissipative measure-valued solution consists of a parametrised (Young) measure

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{t,x}:(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathfrak{P}(R^{d+2}),\ \mathcal{V}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm weak-(*)}((0,\infty)\times\Omega;\mathfrak{P}(R^{d+2})),

(2.6)

where
\mathfrak{P}
denotes the set of Borel probability measures defined
on the space of “dummy” variables

R^{d+2}=\left\{(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\Big|(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\in R^{d+2}\right\},

and a concentration measure

\mathfrak{C}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm weak-(*)}(0,\infty;\mathcal{M}^{+}(\overline{\Omega};R^{d\times d}_{\rm sym})),

(2.7)

where
the symbol
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\overline{\Omega};R^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}))
denotes the set of all matrix-valued positively semi-definite finite Borel measures on
the compact set
\overline{\Omega}
.

Definition 2.1

(
DMV solution).

We shall say that a parametrized measure
\{\mathcal{V}_{t,x}\}
and
a concentration measure
\mathfrak{C}
represent
dissipative measure–valued (DMV) solution
of the Euler system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
), (
1.4
), with the initial data
(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})
and the initial total energy
\mathcal{E}_{0}
if the
following holds:

•

The equation of continuity

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\left<\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\widetilde{\varrho}\right>\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)+\left<\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\widetilde{\bm{m}}\right>\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi(t,x)\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}(x)\varphi(0,x)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(2.8)

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega})
.

•

The momentum equation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\left<\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\widetilde{\bm{m}}\right>\cdot\partial_{t}\bm{\varphi}(t,x)+\left<\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{\varrho}>0}\left(\frac{\widetilde{\bm{m}}\otimes\widetilde{\bm{m}}}{\widetilde{\varrho}}+p(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{S})\mathbb{I}\right)\right>\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}(t,x)\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}(t,x):{\rm d}\mathfrak{C}(t,x)-\int_{\Omega}{\bf m}_{0}(x)\cdot\bm{\varphi}(0,x)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(2.9)

holds for any
\bm{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}([0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega};R^{d})
,
\bm{\varphi}\cdot\bm{n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

•

The entropy inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\left<\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\widetilde{S}\right>\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)+\left<\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{\varrho}>0}\widetilde{S}\frac{\widetilde{\bm{m}}}{\widetilde{\varrho}}\right>\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi(t,x)\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}S_{0}(x)\varphi(0,x)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(2.10)

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega})
,
\varphi\geq 0
.

•

The energy compatibility condition

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}\geq\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\left<\mathcal{V}_{t,x};E(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\right>\ \,{\rm d}{x}+r(d,\gamma)\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\ {\rm d}{\rm trace}[\mathfrak{C}](t,x),

\displaystyle r(d,\gamma)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2};\frac{d\gamma}{\gamma-1}\right\}

(2.11)

holds for a.a.
t\in(0,\infty)
.

The concept of DMV solution was introduced in
[
5
]
and later elaborated in the monograph
[
13
, Chapter 5]
. Kröner and Zajaczkowski
[
19
]

introduced a different class of measure–valued solution to the Euler system postulating entropy
equation
rather than inequality (
2.10
). Unfortunately,
a physically admissible weak solution with a shock that produces entropy does not fit in this class.

Let us summarise some of the known properties of DMV solutions.

1.

Global existence.
Let the initial data belong to the class

\displaystyle(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})\in L^{1}(\Omega;R^{d+2}),\ \int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}<\infty,\ S_{0}\geq\underline{s}\varrho_{0}\ \mbox{a.a. in}\ \Omega,

(2.12)

where
\underline{s}\in R
is a given constant. Then the Euler system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
), (
1.4
) admits a DMV solution
in
(0,\infty)\times\Omega
specified in Definition
2.1
. In addition,
the solution satisfies

\mathcal{V}_{t,x}\left\{\widetilde{\varrho}\geq 0,\ \widetilde{S}\geq\underline{s}\widetilde{\varrho}\right\}=1\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ (t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\Omega.

(2.13)

For the proof see
[
4
, Proposition 3.8]
.

In addition, the expected values

\varrho(t,x)=\left<\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\widetilde{\varrho}\right>,\ \bm{m}(t,x)=\left<\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\widetilde{\bm{m}}\right>,S(t,x)=\left<\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\widetilde{S}\right>\

(2.14)

belong to the class

\varrho,S\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{\gamma}(\Omega)),\ \bm{m}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega;R^{d})),

(2.15)

see
[
13
, Chapter 4, Section 4.1.5]
. Similarly to
[
4
]
,
we call the triple of expected values
(\varrho,\bm{m},S)
, together with
\mathcal{E}_{0}
,

dissipative solution
of the Euler system.

2.

Convexity and compactness.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]=

\displaystyle\left\{(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\ \Big|\ (\varrho,\bm{m},S)\ \mbox{is a dissipative solution in}\ (0,\infty)\times\Omega\right.

\displaystyle\mbox{emanating from the data}\ (\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\Big\}

(2.16)

denote the solution set associated to the data
(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})

Then the following holds:

•

The set
\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
is non–empty and convex for any initial data satisfying
(
2.12
).

•

The set
\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
is closed
and bounded
in the weighted Lebesgue space

L^{q}_{\omega}((0,\infty)\times\Omega;R^{d+2})\ \mbox{with the measure}\ {\rm d}\omega=\exp(-t)\,{\rm d}t\times\,{\rm d}{x}

(2.17)

for any
1\leq q\leq\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}
.

•

The set
\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
is compact in the space

L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;W^{-\ell,2}(\Omega;R^{d+2})),\ \ell>3,

(2.18)

for any
1\leq q<\infty
.

For the proof, see
[
13
, Chapter 5, Theorem 5.2]
.

3.

Continuity in time.

The dissipative solution
(\varrho,\bm{m},S)
admits

instantaneous values
defined

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\varrho(s,x)\varphi(x)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\varrho(t,x)\varphi(x)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\int_{\Omega}\varrho(s,x)\varphi(x)\mbox{d}x\mbox{d}s,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\bm{m}(s,x)\cdot\bm{\varphi}(x)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{m}(t,x)\cdot\bm{\varphi}(x)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\int_{\Omega}\bm{m}(s,x)\cdot\bm{\varphi}(x)\mbox{d}x\mbox{d}s

for any
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\bm{\varphi}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;R^{d})
; and

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\int_{\Omega}S(s,x)\varphi(x)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}S(t-,x)\varphi(x)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}S(t+,x)\varphi(x)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\int_{\Omega}S(s,x)\varphi(x)\mbox{d}x\mbox{d}s,

for any
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\varphi\geq 0
provided we have set

\varrho(t,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\ \bm{m}(t,\cdot)=\bm{m}_{0},\ S(t,\cdot)=S_{0}\ \mbox{for}\ t\leq 0.

In view of the above identities, we have

\displaystyle\varrho

\displaystyle\in C_{\rm weak}([0,\infty);L^{\gamma}(\Omega)),\ \varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},

\displaystyle\bm{m}

\displaystyle\in C_{\rm weak}([0,\infty;L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega;R^{d})),\ \bm{m}(0,\cdot)=\bm{m}_{0},

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\in BV_{\rm weak}(0,\infty;L^{\gamma}(\Omega)),\ S(0-,\cdot)=S_{0},\ S(t-,\cdot)\leq S(t+,\cdot)\ \mbox{for any}\ t\geq 0

(2.19)

see
[
4
]
for details. In particular, the entropy
component of a dissipative solution can be identified with the left limit
S(t-,\cdot)
, which can be seen as a (weakly) càglàd function of
t\in[0,\infty)
with values in
L^{\gamma}(\Omega)
. Finally, it follows from the entropy inequality (
2.10
) that the total entropy

t\in[0,\infty)\to\int_{\Omega}S(t-,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x},\ S(0-,\cdot)=S_{0}

is a non–decreasing (càglàd) function in
[0,\infty)
.

4.

Weak measurability.

Motivated by
[
4
]
we introduce the data space

\displaystyle\ X_{D}

\displaystyle=\left\{(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\Big|(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})\in L^{1}(\Omega;R^{d+2}),\ S_{0}\geq\underline{s}\varrho_{0},\ \int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}\right\}

\displaystyle\subset W^{-\ell,2}(\Omega;R^{d+2})\times[0,\infty),\ \ell>d,

(2.20)

with the topology of the Hilbert space
W^{-\ell,2}(\Omega;R^{d+2})\times R
. Given
\underline{s}\in R
, the data space
X_{D}
is a non–empty convex compact subset of the Hilbert space

W^{-\ell,2}(\Omega;R^{d+2})\times R
.

As shown
in
[
4
, Section 4, Lemma 4.5]
, the solution mapping

\mathcal{U}:(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\in X_{D}\mapsto\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\in{\rm comp}\Big[L^{2}_{\omega}(0,\infty;W^{-\ell,2}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))\Big],

(2.21)

where
{\rm comp}
denotes the metric space of all compact subsets
endowed with the Hausdorff topology, is Borel–measurable. Moreover,

(\varrho(t,\cdot),\bm{m}(t,\cdot),S(t\pm),\cdot),\mathcal{E}_{0})\in X_{D}\ \mbox{for any}\ t\geq 0

(2.22)

whenever

(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\in\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}].

Besides the above facts crucial for the subsequent
analysis, the dissipative solutions enjoy other important properties,
among which the weak–strong uniqueness and compatibility with the strong solutions, see
[
13
]
.

3
Step 1 - maximal entropy production

Following the strategy of
[
4
]
we introduce the

first selection functional

\mathcal{F}_{S}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\left(\int_{\Omega}S(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\,{\rm d}t

which is a bounded linear form defined on the solution set

\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
endowed with the topology of the Banach space
L^{q}_{\omega}((0,\infty)\times\Omega;R^{d+2})\times R
,
1\leq q\leq\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}
.

Next, let us introduce the set of maximisers of the entropy production,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]

\displaystyle\equiv{\rm argmax}\mathcal{F}_{S}\Big[\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big]

\displaystyle=\left\{(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\in\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\ \Big|\ \mathcal{F}_{S}[\varrho,\bm{m},S]\geq\mathcal{F}_{S}[\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S}]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for any}\ (\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\in\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big\}.

(3.1)

We refer to
[
4
, Section 4, Lemma 4.5]
for the proof that the set valued mapping

\mathcal{U}_{S}:(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\in X_{D}\mapsto{\rm argmax}\,\mathcal{F}_{S}\Big[\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big]\in{\rm comp}\Big[L^{2}_{\omega}(0,T;W^{-\ell,2}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))\Big]

(3.2)

is Borel–measurable. Moreover, as
\mathcal{F}_{S}
is linear, the
set
\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
is a convex set.

Our next goal is to show the Borel measurability of the same mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{S}:(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\in X_{D}\mapsto{\rm argmax}\,\mathcal{F}_{S}\Big[\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big]

\displaystyle\in{\rm closed}\Big[L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))\Big],

\displaystyle 1<q

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1},

(3.3)

where the symbol
{\rm closed}
refers to the family of
all closed subsets.
Indeed the set-valued mapping
\mathcal{U}_{S}
specified in
(
3.2
) is Borel–measurable, ranging in a compact metric space. Accordingly, it admits the so-called Castaign representation -
a countable family of Borel–measurable selections -

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{i}_{S}:(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle(\varrho^{i},\bm{m}^{i},S^{i})

\displaystyle\in{\rm argmax}\mathcal{F}_{S}\Big[\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big]\in{\rm comp}\Big[L^{2}_{\omega}(0,\infty;W^{-\ell,2}(\Omega;R^{5}))\Big],

i=1,2,\dots
such that

{\rm closure}_{L^{2}_{\omega}(0,T;W^{-\ell,2}(\Omega;R^{5}))}\Big\{(\varrho^{i},\bm{m}^{i},S^{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\Big\}={\rm argmax}\mathcal{F}_{S}\Big[\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big],

cf. Castaing, Valadier
[
7
]
.
Now, we may extend the family
(\varrho^{i},\bm{m}^{i},S^{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
by considering finite convex combinations
with rational coefficients,

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}(\varrho^{i},\bm{m}^{i},S^{i}),\ \lambda_{j}\in Q

As the set
{\rm argmax}\,\mathcal{F}_{S}\Big[\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big]
is convex, we have

\displaystyle{\rm closure}_{L^{2}_{\omega}(0,T;W^{-\ell,2}(\Omega;R^{5}))}\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}(\varrho^{i},\bm{m}^{i},S^{i})_{i,m=1}^{\infty}\Big\}

\displaystyle={\rm argmax}\,\mathcal{F}_{S}\Big[\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0},\mathcal{E}_{0}].

Finally, as the weak and strong closures coincide on convex sets, the extended system can be seen as a Castaing representation of

\mathcal{U}_{S}
in the topology of the space

L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))
. This yields the desired
Borel measurability of
\mathcal{U}_{S}
specified in (
3.3
).

In view of Hess’s measurability theorem

3.1
Properties of the solutions selected in Step 1

Solutions selected in Step 1 belong to the convex set of entropy maximisers.

\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]={\rm argmax}\,\mathcal{F}_{S}\Big[\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big],

supplemented with the associated total energy component
\mathcal{E}_{0}
.
First, we show that these solutions comply with the so-called DiPerna maximality criterion, cf.
[
10
]
. To see this, we introduce a partial ordering on the set
\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
:

\displaystyle(\varrho^{1},\bm{m}^{1},S^{1})

\displaystyle\prec_{DiP}(\varrho^{2},\bm{m}^{2},S^{2})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow_{\rm def}\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}S^{1}(t+,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}S^{2}(t+,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{for all}\ t\geq 0.

We say that a solution
(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})
is

\prec_{DiP}
maximal in the set
\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
if for any other solution
(\varrho,\bm{m},S;\mathcal{E}_{0})\in\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]

satisfying

(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\prec_{DiP}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)

there holds

\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{S}(t+,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}S(t+,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{for all}\ t>0.

It is easy to check that all solutions selected in Step 1 belong to the set

\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]={\rm argmax}\,\mathcal{F}_{S}\Big[\mathcal{U}(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\Big],

are
\prec_{DiP}
maximal in
\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
.

DiPerna
[
10
]
conjectured that all

\prec_{DiP}
maximal solutions are in fact weak solutions, meaning
their energy defect

\mathcal{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t)=\mathcal{E}_{0}-\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

vanishes for a.a.
t>0
. Although the conjecture remains open, we can show that the energy defect of any
\prec_{DiP}
maximal solution
vanishes for
t\to\infty
.

To show this, we firstly we recall the concatenation property of DMV solutions proved in
[
14
, Lemma 5.1]
:

Lemma 3.2

(
Concatenation of DMV solutions).

Let
\{\mathcal{V}^{i}_{t,x}\},\mathfrak{C}^{i}
be two DMV solutions of the Euler system in
(0,T_{i})\times\Omega
, with the initial data

(\varrho_{0}^{i},\bm{m}_{0}^{i},S^{i}_{0},\mathcal{E}_{0})
,
i=1,2
respectively. Let

\varrho^{i}(t,x)=\left<\mathcal{V}^{i}_{t,x},\widetilde{\varrho}\right>,\ \bm{m}^{i}(t,x)=\left<\mathcal{V}^{i}_{t,x},\widetilde{\bm{m}}\right>,\ S^{i}(t,x)=\left<\mathcal{V}^{i}_{t,x},\widetilde{S}\right>,\ i=1,2

be the associated dissipative solutions.
Suppose that

\varrho^{1}(T_{1},\cdot)=\varrho^{2}_{0},\ \bm{m}^{1}(T_{1},0)=\bm{m}^{2}_{0},

(3.5)

and

S^{1}(T_{1}-,\cdot)\leq S^{2}_{0}.

(3.6)

Then the concatenated solution
(\{\mathcal{V}_{t,x}\},\mathfrak{C})=(\{\mathcal{V}^{1}_{t,x}\},\mathfrak{C}_{1})\cup_{T_{1}}(\{\mathcal{V}^{2}_{t,x}\},\mathfrak{C}_{2})
defined as

\mathcal{V}_{t,x}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{V}^{1}_{t,x}\ \mbox{for}\ 0\leq t\leq T^{1},\\
\mathcal{V}^{2}_{t-T^{1},x}\ \mbox{for}\ T^{1}<t\leq T^{1}+T^{2}\end{array}\right.,\ \mathfrak{C}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathfrak{C}^{1}(t)\ \mbox{for}\ 0\leq t\leq T^{1},\\
\mathfrak{C}^{2}(t-T^{1})\ \mbox{for}\ T^{1}<t\leq T^{1}+T^{2}\end{array}\right.

is a DMV solution of the Euler system in
(0,T_{1}+T_{2})\times\Omega
, with the initial data
(\varrho^{1}_{0},\bm{m}^{1}_{0},S^{1}_{0})
,

\mathcal{E}_{0}
.

We are now ready to prove that the defect of a maximal solution vanishes for large times.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(\varrho,\bm{m},S)
be a
\prec_{DiP}
maximal solution in the solution set
\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
.

Then

\mathcal{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t+)=\mathcal{E}_{0}-\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t+,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ t\to\infty.

Proof.

Suppose that

\mathcal{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(\tau+)=\mathcal{E}_{0}-\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(\tau+,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\delta>0

(3.7)

Our goal is to concatenate the solution with another one, starting from

\widetilde{\varrho}_{0}=\varrho(\tau,\cdot),\ \widetilde{\bm{m}}_{0}=\bm{m}(\tau,\cdot),

and

\widetilde{S}_{0}>S(\tau-,\cdot),

where
\widetilde{S}_{0}
can be chosen large satisfying

\int_{\Omega}E(\widetilde{\varrho}_{0},\widetilde{\bm{m}}_{0},\widetilde{S}_{0})\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\mathcal{E}_{0}.

(3.8)

To this end, we first identify the temperature
\vartheta(\tau-,\cdot)

from the relation

S(\tau-,\cdot)=\varrho(\tau,\cdot)\Big(c_{v}\log(\vartheta(\tau-,\cdot))-\log(\varrho(\tau,\cdot))\Big)\ \mbox{on the set}\ \{\varrho(\tau,\cdot)>0\}.

The total energy evaluated at the same time
\tau
reads

\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathds{1}_{\varrho>0}\frac{|\bm{m}|^{2}}{\varrho}(\tau,\cdot)+c_{v}\varrho\vartheta(\tau-,\cdot)\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\mathcal{E}_{0}-\delta.

Thus to keep (
3.8
) valid, we may choose

\widetilde{\vartheta}_{0}=(1+\lambda)\vartheta(\tau-,\cdot),\ \mbox{where}\ \lambda c_{v}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\vartheta(\tau-,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\delta,

meaning

\lambda=\delta c_{v}^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varrho\vartheta(\tau-,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{-1}\geq\frac{\delta}{\mathcal{E}_{0}}.

(3.9)

Computing the associated entropy, we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{0}

\displaystyle=\varrho(\tau,\cdot)\Big[c_{v}\log(\widetilde{\vartheta}_{0})-\log(\varrho(\tau,\cdot))\Big]=S(\tau-,\cdot)+\varrho(\tau,\cdot)c_{v}\log\left(1+\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\geq S(\tau-,\cdot)+\varrho(\tau,\cdot)c_{v}\log\left(1+\frac{\delta}{\mathcal{E}_{0}}\right).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{S}_{0}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}S(\tau-,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+c_{v}\log\left(1+\frac{\delta}{\mathcal{E}}\right)\int_{\Omega}\varrho(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}S(\tau-,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+c_{v}\log\left(1+\frac{\delta}{\mathcal{E}_{0}}\right)M_{0},

(3.10)

where

M_{0}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

denotes the total mass of the fluid.

Finally, suppose
(\varrho,\bm{m},S)
is
\prec_{DiP}
maximal. In addition, suppose there is a sequence
\tau_{n}\to\infty
such that (
3.7
) holds for
\tau=\tau_{n}
and a fixed
\delta>0
. Clearly, this leads to a contradiction with the maximality condition. Indeed we have

\int_{\Omega}{S}(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\to\mathcal{S}_{\infty}\ \mbox{as}\ t\to\infty.

Using (
3.10
) and the concatenation argument we construct a new DMV solution
(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})
such that

(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})=(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,\tau_{n}),

and

\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{S}(\tau_{n}+,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\geq\int_{\Omega}S(\tau_{n}-,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+c_{v}\log\left(1+\frac{\delta}{\mathcal{E}_{0}}\right)M_{0}>\mathcal{S}_{\infty}

if
\tau_{n}
is large enough in contrast with
\prec_{DiP}
maximality of

(\varrho,\bm{m},S)
. Consequently, a maximal solution satisfied

\mathcal{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\to 0,
what we wanted to prove.

∎

Finally, we introduce the class of absolute entropy maximisers.

Definition 3.4

(
Absolute entropy maximiser).

Let
(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})
be a dissipative solution belonging to the solution set
\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
.
We say that
(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})
is
absolute entropy miximiser
if

(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\prec_{DiP}(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})

for all
(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\in\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
.

As shown in
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
, any absolute entropy maximiser
is necessarily a weak solution of the Euler system, more specifically
\mathcal{D}(t)=0
.
Moreover, repeating the arguments of
[
11
, Section 5]
we easily
observe that any set
\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
contains at most one maximal entropy maximiser.

Let us summarise the properties of the solutions selected in Step 1.

Theorem 3.5

(

Let

\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]={\rm argmax}\,\mathcal{F}_{S}\Big[\mathcal{U}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big].

Then the following holds:

•

For any
(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\in X_{D}
, the set

\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]

,

•

The mapping

{\rm closed}[L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2})]
.

•

\mathcal{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t\pm)\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ t\to\infty

for any

.

•

If, in addition, the set
\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]

(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})
, then

(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})

\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
is a singleton, and

\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]=\{(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\}.

4
Step 2 - turbulent solutions

If the set
\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
fails to be a singleton,
we propose the second selection based on maximisation of the energy defect (turbulent energy). Specifically, we consider the functional

\mathcal{F}_{E}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\left(\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\,{\rm d}t.

We close the selection procedure by setting

\mathcal{U}_{ES}(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})={\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}_{E}\Big[\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big].

As the energy functional
E
is strictly convex in its domain,

\mathcal{U}_{ES}(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})
is a singleton, which finishes the selection process.

4.1
Measurability of the second selection

Our next objective is to show the Borel measurability of the mapping
associated with the second selection,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{ES}:(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\in X_{D}\mapsto{\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}_{E}\left[\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big]\right]\in L^{q}_{\omega}((0,\infty)L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2})).

(4.1)

We start by introducing a Moreau–Yosida regularisation of the
functional
\mathcal{F}_{E}
on the Banach space

L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))
proposed by
Bacho
[
1
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}

\displaystyle(\varrho,\bm{m},S)

\displaystyle=\inf_{(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\in L^{q}_{\omega}((0,\infty);L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|(\varrho,\bm{m},S)-(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\|_{L^{q}_{\omega}((0,\infty);L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2})}^{q}+\mathcal{F}_{E}(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S}))\right]

(4.2)

for
\varepsilon>0
.
Next, we show the Borel measurability of the mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{ES}^{\varepsilon}:(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\in X_{D}\mapsto{\rm argmin}\mathcal{F}_{E}^{\varepsilon}\Big[\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big]\in L^{q}_{\omega}((0,\infty)L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2})).

(4.3)

In accordance with Remark
3.1
, the Borel measurability claimed in
(
4.3
) will follow as long as we show continuity
of the mapping

\mathcal{A}\in{\rm closed}[L^{q}_{\omega}((0,\infty)L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))]\to{\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}[\mathcal{A}]

on the family of closed convex sets in
L^{q}_{\omega}((0,\infty)L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))

with respect to the Wijsman topology.
The space
L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))
,
1<q<\infty,

being a reflexive, separable, uniformly convex Banach space with a uniformly convex dual, the convergence in the Wijsman topology is equivalent to convergence in the Mosco topology
\mathcal{M}
, see Beer
[
3
, Sonntag-Attouch Theorem in Section 4]
.
We recall that

\mathcal{A}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{M}}}{{\to}}\mathcal{A}

if the two following properties hold:

•

For any
(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\in\mathcal{A}
, there exists

(\varrho_{n},\bm{m}_{n},S_{n})\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
such that

(\varrho_{n},\bm{m}_{n},S_{n})\to(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\ \mbox{(strongly) in}\ L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2})).

•

If

(\varrho_{n_{k}},\bm{m}_{n_{k}},S_{n_{k}})\in\mathcal{A}_{n_{k}}\to(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))

then

(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\in\mathcal{A}.

We claim the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{A}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of bounded
closed convex sets in

L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))

such that

\mathcal{A}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{M}}}{{\to}}\mathcal{A},

where
\mathcal{A}
is a bounded closed convex.

Then

{\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}[\mathcal{A}_{n}]\to{\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}[\mathcal{A}]\ \mbox{in}\ L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))\ \mbox{as}\ n\to\infty.

Proof.

Denote

(\underline{\varrho}^{n},\underline{\bm{m}}^{n},\underline{S}^{n})={\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}[\mathcal{A}_{n}],\ (\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S})={\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}[\mathcal{A}].

In accordance with the Mosco convergence, there exists a sequence

(\varrho_{n},\bm{m}_{n},S_{n})\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\to(\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S})\ \mbox{in}\ L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2})).

As
\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}
is continuous, we get

\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}(\underline{\varrho}^{n},\underline{\bm{m}}^{n},\underline{S}^{n})\leq\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}(\varrho_{n},\bm{m}_{n},S_{n})\to\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}(\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S})\ \mbox{as}\ n\to\infty.

(4.4)

Using the properties of the Mosco convergence again, we can extract a subsequence (not) relabeled such that

(\underline{\varrho}^{n},\underline{\bm{m}}^{n},\underline{S}^{n})\to(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\in\mathcal{A}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{q}_{\omega}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2})),

where

\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}(\underline{\varrho}^{n},\underline{\bm{m}}^{n},\underline{S}^{n}).

(4.5)

Comparing (
4.4
), (
4.5
) we conclude

(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})=(\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S}),

(\underline{\varrho}^{n},\underline{\bm{m}}^{n},\underline{S}^{n})\to(\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S})\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2})),

(4.6)

and

\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}(\underline{\varrho}^{n},\underline{\bm{m}}^{n},\underline{S}^{n})\to\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}(\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S})

as
n\to\infty
. As
\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}
is strictly convex, we obtain the desired strong convergence in
(
4.6
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

As
\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}
are coercive, the hypothesis of boundedness of the convex sets can be omitted.

Having shown Borel measurability of the mapping
\mathcal{U}^{\varepsilon}_{ES}
, we deduce the desired measurability of
\mathcal{U}_{ES}
from the
following result.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a bounded convex closed subset of

L^{q}_{\omega}((0,\infty);L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))

such that
\mathcal{F}_{E}|_{\mathcal{A}}
is proper.

Then

\displaystyle{\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}[\mathcal{A}]\to{\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}_{E}[\mathcal{A}]\ \mbox{in}\ L^{q}_{\omega}((0,\infty);L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))\ \mbox{as}\ \varepsilon\to 0.

(4.7)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\nearrow\mathcal{F}_{E}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\ \mbox{for any}\ (\varrho,\bm{m},S)\in\mathcal{A}.

In particular,

\min_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}\nearrow\underline{F}\leq\min_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{F}_{E}=\mathcal{F}_{E}(\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S}),

where we have denoted

(\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S})={\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}_{E}[\mathcal{A}]

Let us denote

(\underline{\varrho}^{\varepsilon},\underline{\bm{m}}^{\varepsilon},\underline{S}^{\varepsilon})={\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}[\mathcal{A}].

It follows from the strict convexity of the norm in
L^{q}_{\omega}
that there exist a family

(\widetilde{\varrho}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{\bm{m}}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{S}^{\varepsilon})
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{E}(\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S})\geq\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{E}(\underline{\varrho}^{\varepsilon},\underline{\bm{m}}^{\varepsilon},\underline{S}^{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|(\underline{\varrho}^{\varepsilon},\underline{\bm{m}}^{\varepsilon},\underline{S}^{\varepsilon})-(\widetilde{\varrho}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{\bm{m}}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{S}^{\varepsilon})\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{\omega}}+\mathcal{F}_{E}(\widetilde{\varrho}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{\bm{m}}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{S}^{\varepsilon})\geq 0.

(4.8)

Thus, passing to the limit for a suitable subsequence, we have

(\underline{\varrho}^{\varepsilon},\underline{\bm{m}}^{\varepsilon},\underline{S}^{\varepsilon})\to(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S}),\ (\widetilde{\varrho}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{\bm{m}}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{S}^{\varepsilon})\to(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{q}_{\omega}\ \mbox{as}\ \varepsilon\to 0,

(4.9)

where, as
\mathcal{A}
is convex,

(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\in\mathcal{A}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
4.8
) using weak lower semi–continuity of
\mathcal{F}_{E}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{E}(\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S})\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{E}(\widetilde{\varrho}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{\bm{m}}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{S}^{\varepsilon})\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{E}(\widetilde{\varrho}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{\bm{m}}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{S}^{\varepsilon})\geq\mathcal{F}_{E}(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})\geq\mathcal{F}_{E}(\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S}).

We conclude

(\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{\bm{m}},\widetilde{S})=(\underline{\varrho},\underline{\bm{m}},\underline{S}),

and the desired conclusion follows from (
4.9
).

∎

We conclude that the mapping

\mathcal{U}_{ES}:(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\in X_{D}\mapsto{\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}_{E}\Big[\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big]\in L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))

being a pointwise limit of Borel–measurable mappings is Borel–measurable.

Let us summarise the properties of the solutions selected in Step 2.

Theorem 4.4

(
Properties of solutions selected in Step 2).

Let

(\varrho,\bm{m},S)=\mathcal{U}_{ES}(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0},\mathcal{E}_{0}).

Then the following holds:

•

The mapping

(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\in X_{D}\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{ES}(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\in L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))

is Borel–measurable.

•

The energy defect (turbulent energy) vanishes for large times,

\mathcal{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t\pm)\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ t\to\infty.

•

If
\mathcal{U}_{S}[\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0}]
is not a singleton, then
(\varrho,\bm{m},S)
is a truly measure–valued solution, meaning

\mathcal{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\neq 0
.

(5.1)

6
One-step selection process - maximal dissipativity

The two-step selection process is probably satisfactory from the analysis point of view, but less convenient in numerical implementations. We propose
a single-step selection procedure inspired by the Second law of thermodynamics, formulated in the celebrated statement by Clausius:

The energy of the world is constant; its entropy tends to a maximum.

The total energy of a dissipative solution is given by its initial value
\mathcal{E}_{0}
. The total mass

M_{0}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

is a constant of motion as well. We define the
equilibrium density and temperature

\overline{\varrho}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\frac{M_{0}}{|\Omega|},\ \int_{\Omega}\overline{\varrho}e(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta})\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\mathcal{E}_{0}\ \Rightarrow\ \overline{\vartheta}=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{0}}{c_{v}M_{0}}.

(6.1)

The total entropy
S
expressed constitutively in terms of
\varrho
and the internal energy
{E}_{i}=c_{v}\varrho\vartheta
is a concave function, in particular

S(\varrho(t,\cdot),E_{i}(t,\cdot))-\frac{\partial S(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta})}{\partial\varrho}(\varrho-\overline{\varrho})-\frac{\partial S(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta})}{\partial E_{i}}(c_{v}\varrho\vartheta-c_{v}\overline{\varrho}\overline{\vartheta})-S(\overline{\varrho},c_{v}\overline{\varrho}\overline{\vartheta})\leq 0.

(6.2)

Integrating (
6.2
) and using (
6.1
), together with the inequality

\int_{\Omega}c_{v}\varrho\vartheta\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{0},

we conclude

\int_{\Omega}S(t\pm,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq S(\overline{\varrho},c_{v}\overline{\varrho}\overline{\vartheta})\ \mbox{for all}\ t

(6.3)

for any dissipative solution of the Euler system. In other words, the entropy is maximised at the constant equilibrium state
\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta}
specified in (
6.1
).

Summarising the previous discussion, we conclude that a selection criterion compatible with the Second law of thermodynamics should minimise the
distance between a dissipative solution
(\varrho,\bm{m},S)
and the constant equilibrium
(\overline{\varrho},0,S(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta}))
. To measure the distance, we
use the Bregmann divergence associated to the total energy
E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)
, namely

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\left(\varrho,\bm{m},S\Big|\overline{\varrho},0,\overline{\vartheta}\right)

\displaystyle=E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)-\left(c_{v}\overline{\vartheta}-\overline{\vartheta}s(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta})+\frac{p(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta})}{\overline{\varrho}}\right)(\varrho-\overline{\varrho})-\overline{\vartheta}\Big(S-S(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta})\Big)-\overline{\varrho}e(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta}),

(6.4)

see
[
13
, Chapter 4, Section 4.1.6]
.

Relation (
6.4
) integrated over
\Omega
yields

\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,\bm{m},S\Big|\overline{\varrho},0,\overline{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\overline{\vartheta}\int_{\Omega}\Big(S-S(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta})\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\overline{\varrho}e(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta})|\Omega|.

(6.5)

This motivates the definition of a cost functional in the form

\mathcal{F}_{D}[\varrho,\bm{m},S;\mathcal{E}_{0}]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)-\overline{\vartheta}S\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\,{\rm d}t,\ \hskip 10.00002pt\overline{\vartheta}=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{0}}{c_{v}M_{0}}.

(6.6)

The cost functional being strictly convex, the minimisation procedure yields a unique solution in only one step:

\mathcal{U}_{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S;\mathcal{E}_{0})={\rm argmin}\,\mathcal{F}_{D}\Big[\mathcal{U}[\varrho,\bm{m},S;\mathcal{E}_{0}]\Big].

(6.7)

Borel measurability of the solution mapping can be shown exactly as in Section
4
. We call the quantity

\mathcal{U}_{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S;\mathcal{E}_{0});\mathcal{E}_{0}

maximal dissipative solution of the Euler system
.

We have shown the following result.

Theorem 6.1

(
Maximal dissipative solution).

For any initial data
(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\in X_{D}
, the Euler system admits a unique maximal dissipative solution

\mathcal{U}_{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S;\mathcal{E}_{0})

given through formula (
6.7
). The mapping

(\varrho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0};\mathcal{E}_{0})\in X_{D}\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{D}(\varrho,\bm{m},S;\mathcal{E}_{0})\in L^{q}_{\omega}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d+2}))

is Borel–measurable. The solution mapping enjoys the properties of an almost continuous semigroup specified in Sections

5.1
,
5.2
.

7
Conclusion

Recent breakthrough results on the non-uniqueness of admissible (entropy) weak solutions for the Euler equations of gas dynamics have initiated intensive discussions on the possibility of recovering well-posedness through a suitable selection process. In this paper, we have proposed two selection strategies.

The first one is a two-step selection: in the first step, the total entropy

\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\left(\int_{\Omega}S(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\,{\rm d}t

is maximised in the set of generalised, dissipative measure–valued solutions. If the first step failed to identify a unique solution, we proceed to the second step. Then, in the set selected in the first step, we search for a solution that minimises the mean energy

\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\left(\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\,{\rm d}t.

As the latter is a strictly convex functional in its domain, we have selected a unique solution of the Euler equations.

We have observed that if the selected solution is a
weak solution of the Euler system, then it must have been selected in the first step.
The solution selected in the second step is
a unique turbulent solution, i.e. a truly measure–valued solution.

The second selection strategy combines directly the above two selection steps yielding a one-step selection:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(E(\varrho,\bm{m},S)-\overline{\vartheta}S\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\,{\rm d}t\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\ \overline{\vartheta}=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{0}}{c_{v}M_{0}},

where
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is the initial total energy and
M_{0}
the total mass. We have proved that our selection strategies yield
Borel–measurable initial data
\mapsto
solution mappings. Moreover, the solution mappings are almost continuous and enjoy the semigroup property. In the future, we aim to design a numerical method that approximates the unique solution selected by the proposed selection strategies.

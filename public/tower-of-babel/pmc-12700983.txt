Real time urban traffic prediction using RFID and a hybrid LSTM random forest model
Authors: Omar Khattab, B Saravana Balaji, Fatmah Alghadhoori, Fajer AlMazyad, Maha AlDousari, Abeer Al-Ameeri, M Omar Al-Kadri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTraffic congestion poses a significant challenge to urban environments, impacting economic efficiency, safety, and quality of life. Traditional measures often fail to address the root causes of congestion, instead only delaying the inevitable symptoms of urban growth and increased vehicle use. This research proposes the Traffic Pattern Classification using LSTM-Random Forest (TPC-LSTM-RF), a novel prototype for real-time urban traffic management. The system uses Radio-Frequency Identification (RFID) technology for nonvisual vehicle tracking, ensuring privacy-by-design through on-the-fly anonymization, secure encrypted communication, and compliance with global data protection regulations. The system incorporates a novel hybrid machine learning algorithm, TPC-LSTM-RF, which combines temporal and spatial traffic data to predict congestion patterns and provide proactive traffic management recommendations. By integrating Radio-Frequency Identification (RFID) technology for nonvisual vehicle tracking with a hybrid machine learning model combining Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Random Forest (RF), TPC-LSTM-RF predicts congestion patterns and enforces traffic regulations like speed limits and lane adherence. Simulation results demonstrate superior performance, achieving a prediction accuracy of 93.5%, Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 2.43, and a processing time of 198 ms, outperforming state-of-the-art models. This system promises to enhance traffic flow, reduce violations, and improve urban mobility, with future work focusing on real-world validation and integration with existing traffic infrastructure.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-27485-w.Keywords: RFID, Nonvisual vehicle tracking, Real-time traffic management, Traffic violations, Urban mobility, Traffic authority, Traffic enforcementSubject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

Effective traffic management is one of contemporary urban environments’ most critical challenges. Rapid urbanization, increasing vehicle ownership, and widespread non-compliance with traffic regulations have significantly strained traditional traffic management approaches, often rendering them insufficient. Real-time traffic prediction and enforcement are essential to mitigate congestion, which contributes to extended travel times, fuel wastage, and environmental pollution, costing billions annually in major cities worldwide. However, traditional traffic management systems, reliant on sensor networks, GPS tracking, and camera-based monitoring, face significant limitations. These include scalability challenges in dense urban areas, data quality issues due to signal loss or weather interference, high computational and infrastructure costs, and privacy concerns with visual tracking methods. Moreover, many existing systems are reactive rather than predictive, failing to address congestion before it escalates. To overcome these challenges, this research proposes the Traffic Pattern Classification using LSTM-Random Forest (TPC-LSTM-RF), a novel prototype integrating Radio-Frequency Identification (RFID) technology for privacy-preserving, nonvisual vehicle tracking with a hybrid machine learning model. Combining Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks for temporal pattern analysis and Random Forest (RF) for spatial classification, TPC-LSTM-RF enables real-time congestion prediction, violation detection (e.g., speeding, illegal lane changes), and proactive traffic management. This system aims to transform urban traffic control by offering scalable, predictive, and efficient solutions tailored to the complexities of modern cities. To enhance the system’s predictive capabilities, we introduce a novel machine learning approach called TPC-LSTM-RF, which combines Long Short-Term Memory networks with Random Forest classification. This hybrid algorithm processes temporal traffic patterns and spatial RFID data to predict potential congestion points before they materialize. Related work

In this section, we consider recent papers that address technological solutions for traffic management, focusing on vehicle tracking, classification, and monitoring. We will first review works utilizing RFID technology, followed by those employing sensor-based vehicle detection and classification, and finally, computer vision and machine learning approaches.

RFID technology in traffic management

In4, the authors presented a system integrating RFID with Wireless Sensor Network (WSN) and ZigBee technology for vehicle speed detection. Addressing the limitations of traditional methods like radar, video image, and Global Positioning System (GPS)-based speed detection, the proposed RFID-based system integrates with WSN to provide precise positioning and dynamic speed measurement across entire road sections. The architecture consists of RFID tags embedded in roads and readers installed on vehicles connected through a ZigBee network. A dynamic speed measurement algorithm ensures instantaneously accurate vehicle speed detection. Simulation experiments demonstrate the system’s effectiveness in various road scenarios, highlighting its potential to enhance road safety by reducing traffic accidents and providing real-time alerts to drivers and traffic authorities. This approach addresses the limitations of GPS, radar, and video-based methods by offering comprehensive speed detection. In5, the authors proposed an automated system using RFID technology integrated with the Global System for Mobile Communications (GSM) and Short Message Service (SMS) for detecting and reporting traffic violations. The system employs an Atmega328 microcontroller, RFID tags and readers, and traffic lights with Light Emitting Diodes (LED) to identify vehicles running red lights. Upon detection, the system sends an SMS to violators and traffic authorities, enhancing traffic law enforcement by reducing manual intervention. However, it does not address repeat offenders or speeding vehicles. While the approach in4 effectively integrates RFID with WSN and ZigBee for speed monitoring, it lacks predictive analytics for congestion control and real-time violation tracking. Our proposed TPC-LSTM-RF system advances beyond this by integrating machine learning-based congestion prediction (LSTM + Random Forest), real-time violation detection, and cloud-based monitoring. This enables not only speed tracking but also lane violation detection, accident reporting, and predictive congestion management. Additionally, our system ensures automated fine processing via mobile applications, making traffic enforcement more efficient than4.

Sensor-based vehicle detection and classification

Micro-LiDAR and MEMS sensors

The authors in6 proposed a vehicle detection technique utilizing TFmini-micro-Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) sensors and Arduino Uno, integrated with virtual video frame loops for detecting vehicles in heterogeneous and lane-less traffic conditions, common in developing countries. The proposed system generates binary images from an array of micro-LiDAR sensors to capture vehicle profiles, classify types, and estimate speeds. The system achieves high detection accuracy, 98% with video data and 91.3% with micro-LiDAR data in real-world tests. While the method aims to reduce the high costs and computational demands associated with traditional radar, video image, and GPS-based techniques, it faces several limitations. These include accuracy issues in dense traffic, difficulty detecting smaller vehicles, environmental sensitivity, increased installation costs for larger deployments, and challenges in data synchronization.

Authors in7 detail the development of a Microelectromechanical Systems (MEMS)-based Internet of Vehicle (IoV) system for improving road traffic surveys. This system utilizes a twin-strip arrangement of low-cost, low-energy MEMS sensors, including magnetometers and strain gauges, connected to an on-the-road unit (ORU) that processes data from passing vehicles using Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) and transmits data via Long Range Communication (LoRA) to a roadside bridge (RSB) device. The system’s primary functions include estimating vehicle speed, direction, and type, with a reported accuracy rate of 1% error under controlled conditions. The information gathered is uploaded to a cloud-based platform and is ready for analysis by traffic management centres. It is observed that initial calibration significantly affects accuracy, and there are challenges related to synchronization and environmental noise.

In8, the authors presented a privacy-preserving vehicle classification system using MEMS-based magnetic sensors installed under roads. The system aims to identify vehicle types, models, and fuel types while ensuring privacy through a “privacy-by-design” approach. The sensors capture unique magnetic signatures from passing vehicles, which are then processed using advanced Machine Learning (ML) algorithms to classify the vehicles. The framework was validated in experiments confirming the accuracy with nine predefined car models, a small sample size noted by the authors to be an area for improvement in future studies. Additional constraints include susceptibility to magnetic interference, difficulties detecting smaller vehicles due to weak magnetic signatures, and challenges associated with sensor installation and calibration under road surfaces.

Magnetic and dual-axis magneto-impedance sensors

The research9 introduced MagMonitor, a system utilizing a single magnetic sensor for real-time vehicle speed estimation and classification by modelling vehicles as multiple magnetic dipoles to measure local magnetic field perturbations. Signal processing techniques, including Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) and low-pass filtering, reduce environmental noise and improve signal clarity. Experiments in Xi’an, China, validate the system’s effectiveness in vehicle classification and speed estimation, highlighting its cost-effectiveness, ease of installation, and environmental benefits. However, the system still faces reduced accuracy in dense traffic. The study in10 proposed a vehicle detection and classification system using dual-axis magneto-impedance (MI) sensors placed on the roadside to monitor nearby lanes in real-time. The system utilizes two magnetic models: the bar magnet model for regular vehicles and the magnetic dipole model for more complex vehicles like buses and trucks. Signal processing techniques, including FFT, filter environmental noise, and enhance signal clarity. Experiments conducted in Aichi, Japan, demonstrated a detection accuracy of 91.6% for various vehicle types. Despite its effectiveness, the system faces limitations such as reduced detection accuracy during complex driving conditions (e.g., lane changes, overtaking) and measurement errors due to environmental factors. The study notes that further calibration is required for detecting larger, more complex vehicles.

Computer vision and machine learning in traffic monitoring

Deep learning models for vehicle classification and tracking

Paper11 presents a deep learning-based vehicle classification and tracking system using the YOLO (You Only Look Once) model to improve traffic management by quickly and accurately identifying and tracking vehicles. The YOLO model is employed for real-time object detection, processing video frames efficiently to maintain high accuracy. The system’s performance is validated through experiments, demonstrating an average precision of 90.32% in vehicle classification and tracking. Despite its robustness, the system’s effectiveness can be impacted by environmental factors such as lighting conditions, the need for powerful Graphical Processing Units (GPUs) for real-time processing, and the computational load associated with deep learning. The research in12 introduces a deep learning-based Vehicle License Plate Number Recognition (VLPNR) system for smart city applications, incorporating YOLOv3, YOLOv4, and 3D Deformable models. The proposed system consists of two main stages: license plate detection using Faster Region-based Convolutional Neural Network (RCNN) with Inception V2 and character recognition using Tesseract Optical Character Recognition (OCR). Video feeds from traffic cameras are processed using ML algorithms to classify and track vehicles and estimate their speed. The system’s performance was validated using two benchmark datasets, FZU Cars and HumAIn2019, demonstrating superior performance in precision, recall, F1-measure, accuracy, and mAP. Despite its robustness and better performance on benchmark datasets, the system faces limitations such as dependency on video detection accuracy and lighting conditions, and the need for manual classification for non-English plates.

The research in13 proposed an HD-Net system that combines dynamic graph convolution with BiGRU to effectively capture spatiotemporal features in traffic flow data. The architecture comprises four key modules - Dynamic Spatio-Temporal Feature Extraction, Auto-Correlation, Self-Attention, and Feed Forward Neural Network - working together to process historical traffic data and generate accurate predictions. The system’s main advantage lies in its dual approach of using auto-correlation for local feature extraction and self-attention for global perspective analysis, enabling comprehensive traffic pattern recognition. Experimental validation on METR-LA and XIAMEN-BRT datasets demonstrated HD-Net’s superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art models in terms of MAE, RMSE, and MAPE metrics. The primary limitations identified are the computational cost and the optimization of the model structure while maintaining its performance and incorporating external factors like weather data. The research14 proposed a multi-modal attention neural network (LSTSC) for traffic flow prediction that captures long- and short-term sequence correlations by employing attention mechanisms to analyze spatiotemporal dependencies in traffic data. This innovative approach enhances prediction accuracy and reliability, particularly for long-term forecasts, making it a valuable tool for traffic managers and drivers. Compared to traditional methods, the model performs better on real-world datasets, such as PeMS08 and PeMSD7(M). However, the system may face challenges related to data availability and the complexity of integrating diverse data sources. While the LSTSC model shows significant promise, further research is needed to address these limitations and optimize its application in various traffic scenarios.

The proposed study15, named the Multi-Head Spatiotemporal Attention Graph Convolutional Network (MHSTA–GCN), integrates a graph convolutional network (GCN), a gated recurrent unit (GRU), and a multi-head attention mechanism to capture the complex spatiotemporal dependencies in traffic data effectively. By leveraging the GCN, the model learns the spatial relationships between road network nodes, while the GRU captures the dynamic temporal patterns of traffic flow. The multi-head attention mechanism enhances the model’s ability to focus on significant features that influence traffic predictions, improving accuracy and achieving 91.8% on the Los-loop dataset. However, the model’s performance is affected by challenges in hyperparameter tuning and the need to incorporate external factors like weather and population dynamics. Future work could address these limitations by exploring the integration of additional contextual data to further enhance prediction capabilities.

Machine learning based prediction systems

Several studies have proposed innovative models for traffic prediction and management. The authors of16 introduced the Sparse Spatio-Temporal Dynamic Hypergraph Learning (SST-DHL) framework, combining hypergraph learning with self-supervised learning to enhance traffic accident prediction, though it faces challenges with extreme data sparsity and real-time applicability. In17, the Sparse Non-negative Matrix Factorization with spatial constraints (SNMFS) model was developed to analyze city-level traffic crash patterns, offering improved spatial analysis but requiring significant computational resources. The DATCN model in18 was proposed for predicting lane-level mixed traffic flow involving Connected and Automated Vehicles (CAVs) and Human-driven Vehicles (HVs), achieving high accuracy but relying on simulated data for validation. The SARA framework presented in19 introduced a lightweight, semi-supervised learning method for network traffic classification, effectively handling multi-class imbalances and concept drift using deep auto-encoders and feature selection techniques. The AHOA-HDLTM model in20 combined the Hummingbird Optimization Algorithm with a Hierarchical Deep Learning Traffic Model for improved ITS traffic flow prediction, demonstrating superior error correction and efficiency. Lastly21, introduced GAQ-EBkSP, a fog- and cloud-based model leveraging graph attention mechanisms and deep reinforcement learning for urban traffic rerouting, reducing congestion translocation and outperforming traditional rule-based models. These studies collectively contribute to advancing intelligent transportation systems through machine learning, deep learning, and optimization techniques.

The authors of22 proposed a real-time traffic flow detection model using video frame analysis and grayscale aggregation, enhancing congestion identification on resource-constrained devices. In23, the ST-DWGRU model integrated GCN, GRU, and DeepWalk to improve traffic speed forecasting. The Fog-Cloud-IoT framework in24 utilized Logistic Regression for adaptive vehicle routing, outperforming other classifiers. A two-line approach for traffic optimization was introduced in25, incorporating object tracking for reckless driving detection. The study in26 enhanced GPS-based traffic prediction by integrating classified vehicle counts with ARIMA, SVM, and XGBoost models. Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) in27 improved short-term traffic forecasting by modeling uncertainty better than ARIMA and neural networks. In28, a hybrid deep learning model combining BGCP, LSTM, and CNN improved traffic volume prediction by handling missing data. The W-DSTAGNN model in29 leveraged real-time sensor data for robust spatiotemporal traffic forecasting. Lastly30, introduced the TPCA algorithm with a modified FCM for congestion prediction using genetic models. These studies collectively enhance intelligent transportation systems through machine learning, deep learning, and data-driven approaches to optimize traffic flow and reduce urban congestion.

By analyzing the existing methods mentioned above, we can identify the strengths and limitations of existing systems and understand how our proposed RFID tracking system offers a more integrated and effective solution for real-time traffic management and road safety. We, therefore, propose a reliable prototype of TPC-LSTM-RF compared with the existing research in the literature.

Comparison of TPC-LSTM-RF with related works

In the domain of machine learning for traffic prediction, recent models such as HD-Net13, LSTSC14, and MHSTA-GCN15 represent state-of-the-art approaches leveraging spatiotemporal features, yet they exhibit limitations that our proposed TPC-LSTM-RF model aims to overcome. HD-Net13 integrates dynamic graph convolution with BiGRU for traffic flow prediction, achieving strong performance on METR-LA and XIAMEN-BRT datasets (e.g., lower MAE compared to traditional models). However, its high computational cost and lack of real-time adaptability hinder scalability in large urban networks, as noted by the authors’ emphasis on model optimization challenges. Similarly, LSTSC14 employs a multi-modal attention neural network to capture long- and short-term sequence correlations, demonstrating improved long-term forecasting on PeMS08 and PeMSD7(M) datasets, but struggles with data availability and integration of diverse sources, limiting real-time application. MHSTA-GCN15 combines graph convolutional networks with multi-head attention for traffic prediction, achieving 91.8% accuracy on the Los-loop dataset, yet faces hyperparameter tuning issues and lacks incorporation of external factors like weather, reducing robustness in dynamic conditions. In contrast, TPC-LSTM-RF addresses these weaknesses by combining LSTM’s temporal dependency modeling with Random Forest’s efficient spatial classification, achieving a balanced trade-off between computational efficiency and high prediction accuracy as evidenced by simulation results on a custom dataset of 2.5 million vehicle trajectories. Unlike HD-Net’s resource-intensive architecture, TPC-LSTM-RF leverages edge computing for real-time processing, reducing computational overhead. Compared to LSTSC, it integrates RFID data for continuous real-time input, mitigating data sparsity issues. Against MHSTA-GCN, TPC-LSTM-RF’s hybrid design avoids complex hyperparameter tuning by dynamically adjusting weights, enhancing adaptability without external factor dependency. These improvements, supported by comparative metrics, advocate TPC-LSTM-RF as a superior solution for proactive urban traffic management.

TPC-LSTM-RF method

Recent developments in traffic prediction have seen a surge in hybrid machine-learning approaches. Traditional methods often struggle with the complex nature of traffic patterns, leading researchers to combine multiple algorithms for better results. Additionally, most existing hybrid models focus on prediction or classification but rarely address both aspects simultaneously. Our proposed TPC-LSTM-RF algorithm aims to bridge this gap by combining the temporal pattern recognition capabilities of LSTM with the robust classification abilities of Random Forests. The proposed TPC-LSTM-RF design includes an Arduino Mega, Arduino Uno, RFID readers and tags, Liquid Crystal Display (LCD) screens, a Local server, a Cloud server, and a Mobile application for the end user. The TPC-LSTM-RF provides a good solution for measuring car speed using RFID technology to detect violations at two measurement points. The design incorporates two lines of RFID readers per lane. TPC-LSTM-RF is architecturally composed of three primary sections, as illustrated in the block diagram in Fig. 3, which delineates the central processing unit (local server) managing data storage, Arduino Mega with peripherals like RFID readers and the Firebase cloud server enabling real-time data sync with mobile apps ensuring a cohesive system for traffic monitoring and enforcement.

Block diagram of TPC-LSTM-RF.

The first section is the central processing unit, which serves as the local server (computer). This computer reads all data from the Arduino Mega, stores the data in spreadsheets, calculates violations, and uploads the data to the Firebase cloud server. The second section includes the Arduino Mega and its peripherals. The Arduino Mega interfaces with three Arduino Uno microcontrollers that are responsible for controlling the LCDs that display messages on graphical screens. Additionally, the Arduino Mega connects to a termination board in the prototype, which is linked to six RFID readers that capture the IDs of cars. The third section is the cloud server, which manages data exchange between computers and mobile applications. The Firebase server supports two interfaces in the mobile application: an admin interface, which shows all violations immediately, and a user interface, which displays specific user violations and corresponding fees. This system efficiently captures vehicle data, processes it to detect violations, and provides real-time updates to both drivers and administrators via a cloud-based mobile application. Unlike related papers focusing on specific features, this proposed design integrates multiple features into one system, including speed measurement and violation detection, such as speeding, under-speeding, illegal lane changes, and accident tracking using RFID readers.

TPC-LSTM-RF system overview and architecture

This section introduces the novel TPC-LSTM-RF approach, emphasizing its theoretical foundation and architectural innovation. The system employs a four-layer architecture designed to address the challenges of real-time traffic congestion management. The UML activity diagram in Fig. 7 delineates the algorithmic workflow of the proposed TPC-LSTM-RF, mapping the process from RFID tag ID reading, through parallel lane verification and speed calculation, to cloud data upload for real-time updates, and supporting proactive traffic management. The process begins with reading the RFID tag ID of each vehicle as it passes the first RFID reader beam, recording the initial passing time. The vehicle’s ID and passing time are re-recorded when it crosses the second RFID reader beam. While prior systems have leveraged either temporal sequence models (such as LSTM) or ensemble classifiers (such as Random Forest) for traffic prediction, TPC-LSTM-RF is the first to tightly couple these approaches for both temporal pattern recognition and spatial classification within a unified, adaptive framework. This enables the system to capture complex spatiotemporal dependencies in urban traffic, resulting in more accurate, robust, and actionable predictions than methods using either class of algorithm alone.

UML activity diagram of the TPC-LSTM-RF.

The system then splits into two parallel processes:

Lane Verification: The system ensures the vehicle is in the correct lane. If the vehicle is in an incorrect lane, the system retrieves the vehicle information from the database and registers a lane violation.

Data integrity and error handling

RFID-based traffic monitoring is susceptible to occasional missing or erroneous readings due to environmental factors, signal interference, or tag malfunctions. To address these issues, we implemented a multi-step error handling process:

Timestamp Imputation: Missing timestamps were estimated using linear interpolation based on the last valid reading.

Error Correction: RFID tags that failed to register at one checkpoint but reappeared at the next were corrected using Kalman filtering to estimate their missing trajectory.

Redundancy Mechanism: Each RFID checkpoint had overlapping coverage zones, ensuring that vehicles were detected by at least two adjacent readers to minimize data loss.

Quality Checks: Data with a loss rate exceeding 5% per vehicle trajectory was discarded to maintain high accuracy.

The TPC-LSTM-RF algorithm combines Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Random Forest (RF) classification due to their complementary strengths in handling the complex spatiotemporal nature of urban traffic data. LSTM was selected for its ability to model temporal dependencies and capture long-term patterns in sequential traffic data, such as recurring congestion cycles or time-of-day variations, outperforming traditional Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) by mitigating vanishing gradient issues through gated mechanisms. RF’s resistance to overfitting and ability to process diverse inputs (e.g., RFID-derived spatial location data) complement LSTM’s temporal focus, ensuring balanced prediction accuracy and computational efficiency, as evidenced by a processing time of 198 ms. This hybrid design addresses limitations of standalone models, where LSTM alone lacks spatial context robustness, and RF alone misses temporal dynamics, achieving a synergistic improvement in traffic management.

Conclusion

This research presents TPC-LSTM-RF, a novel traffic management solution integrating RFID technology with a hybrid LSTM-Random Forest algorithm to address urban congestion and violations. Implementing UHF-RFID readers at strategic road locations, coupled with vehicle-specific RFID tags, enables efficient nonvisual tracking of vehicles while maintaining privacy-by-design principles, demonstrating robust capabilities in detecting speeding violations and illegal lane changes in real time. Our novel TPC-LSTM-RF algorithm, which combines Long Short-Term Memory networks with Random Forest classification, has demonstrated superior performance across multiple metrics compared to state-of-the-art systems. The comprehensive evaluation shows significant improvements with MAE of 2.43, RMSE of 4.52, and MAPE of 6.8%, outperforming existing solutions by margins of up to 14.7%, 11.7%, and 12.8%, respectively. Most notably, the system achieved a prediction accuracy of 93.5% while maintaining the lowest processing time of 198ms, representing a 36.5% improvement in computational efficiency over comparable systems. However, these results are limited to simulated environments using a custom dataset of 2.5 million vehicle trajectories, and real-world validation is essential to confirm practical efficacy under diverse urban conditions. Experimental limitations, such as reliance on synthetic data and lack of physical deployment, highlight the need for further testing on benchmark datasets like PeMS and real traffic scenarios to address issues like data variability and environmental factors. In summary, TPC-LSTM-RF enables cities to move beyond reactive, punitive traffic management by integrating predictive analytics, privacy-aware tracking, and both punitive and positive interventions, including real-time route optimization, congestion avoidance alerts, and behavioral incentives. The system empowers authorities and drivers alike to contribute to smoother, safer, and more efficient urban mobility. This multi-level, adaptive approach is essential for the next generation of intelligent transportation systems.

Future work will prioritize specific and impactful advancements for the TPC-LSTM-RF system to enhance its applicability in urban traffic management. Key directions include validating the model on established benchmark datasets like PeMS to ensure performance consistency across diverse real-world traffic scenarios, addressing current limitations of simulation-based results. Additionally, we aim to investigate the integration of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to capture static spatial dependencies in road networks, complementing the temporal focus of LSTM and improving congestion prediction in complex urban layouts. Further exploration will involve testing multi-modal data integration (e.g., weather, event schedules) to enhance predictive robustness, alongside real-world pilot deployments in mid-sized cities to assess scalability, hardware reliability, and user compliance under actual conditions. These efforts will ensure TPC-LSTM-RF evolves into a comprehensive, deployable solution for intelligent transportation systems.

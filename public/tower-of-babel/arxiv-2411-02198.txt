Metric properties of partial and robust Gromov-Wasserstein distances
Jannatul Chhoa, Michael Ivanitskiy, Fushuai Jiang, Shiying Li, Daniel McBride, Tom Needham, Kaiying O’Hare

Abstract
The Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) distances define a family of metrics, based on ideas from optimal transport, which enable comparisons between probability measures defined on distinct metric spaces. They are particularly useful in areas such as network analysis and geometry processing, as computation of a GW distance involves solving for registration between the objects which minimizes geometric distortion. Although GW distances have proven useful for various applications in the recent machine learning literature, it has been observed that they are inherently sensitive to outlier noise and cannot accommodate partial matching. This has been addressed by various constructions building on the GW framework; in this article, we focus specifically on a natural relaxation of the GW optimization problem, introduced by Chapel et al., which is aimed at addressing exactly these shortcomings. Our goal is to understand the theoretical properties of this relaxed optimization problem, from the viewpoint of metric geometry. While the relaxed problem fails to induce a metric, we derive precise characterizations of how it fails the axioms of non-degeneracy and triangle inequality. These observations lead us to define a novel family of distances, whose construction is inspired by the Prokhorov and Ky Fan distances, as well as by the recent work of Raghvendra et al. on robust versions of classical Wasserstein distance. We show that our new distances define true metrics, that they induce the same topology as the GW distances, and that they enjoy additional robustness to perturbations. These results provide a mathematically rigorous basis for using our robust partial GW distances in applications where outliers and partial matching are concerns.

Introduction

Registration is an important preprocessing task in fields such as machine learning, network analysis, and geometry processing, which broadly consists of finding a correspondence between a pair of objects (images, point clouds, graphs, etc.) that optimally aligns the objects’ respective geometrical features. is a compact metric space and, in this original formulation,

2
Basics of Partial Gromov-Wasserstein Distances

This section introduces the main concepts that will be used throughout the paper.

2.1
Measure Theory Background

We begin with some background definitions and notation from measure theory. The concepts from this subsection are standard, besides, perhaps, the notions of relaxed couplings defined below.

Measures and derivatives.

Let
X
be a measure space. We denote the set of nonnegative measures on
X
by
\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
. Given
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
, we use
\left|\mu\right|
to denote the total mass (or equivalently, the total variation) of
\mu
; that is,
\left|\mu\right|=\mu(X)=\int_{X}d\mu
. The set of probability measures on
X
, i.e.,
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
with
\left|\mu\right|=1
, will be denoted
\mathcal{P}(X)
.

Given
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
, we use
\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}
to denote the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
. We are interested in the case
\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}\in L^{\infty}(\nu)
, which is equivalent to saying that
\mu
can be obtained from
\nu
by conditioning
[
13
]
.

We note that two Borel probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
satisfy
\|\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}(\nu)}\leq r\in(1,\infty)
if and only if
\mu(A)\leq r\cdot\nu(A)
for every Borel set
A
, which is the same as
\|\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\nu)}\leq r-1
.

Metric measure spaces.

A
metric measure space
(
mm-space
)
[
17
]
is a tuple
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
, where
(X,d_{X})
is a complete and separable metric space and
\mu_{X}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
(where
X
is considered as a measure space with respect to its Borel
\sigma
-algebra). When the context is clear, we abuse notation and write
X
instead of
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
.

A
probability metric measure space
(
pmm-space
) is an mm-space with
\mu_{X}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
. The space
\mathcal{P}(X)
can be endowed with the usual weak topology (sometimes also called the narrow topology in the literature): a sequence
(\mu_{k})\subset\mathcal{P}(X)
converges to
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
weakly if
\int_{X}fd\mu_{k}\to\int_{X}fd\mu
for all bounded continuous functions
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
. For pmm-spaces, Prokhorov’s Theorem states that the topology of weak convergence is induced by a metric, and
\mathcal{P}(X)
is precompact in this topology.

We say that an mm-space
X
is
compact
if its underlying metric space
(X,d_{X})
is compact. An mm-space is called
fully-supported
if the support of its measure
\mu_{X}
is all of
X
. We say two mm-spaces
X
and
Y
are
isomorphic
if there exists a measure-preserving isometric bijection
f:X\to Y
.

Remark 2.1
.

Our results frequently assume that mm- and pmm-spaces are compact and fully supported for the sake of convenience; many results could be generalized at the expense of requiring additional technical qualifiers and notation.

Couplings and relaxed couplings.

Let
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X}),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be pmm-spaces. We use
p^{X}:X\times Y\to X
and
p^{Y}:X\times Y\to Y
to denote the canonical projections. Given a measure
\pi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X\times Y)
, we write
\pi_{X}=p^{X}_{\#}\pi
and
\pi_{Y}=p^{Y}_{\#}\pi
, where the subscript
\#
denotes the pushforward of a map, i.e.,
f_{\#}\mu(S)=\mu(f^{-1}(S))
. We recall that the family of
(exact) couplings
of
\mu_{X}
and
\mu_{Y}
is defined to be

(2.1)

\mathcal{C}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y}):=\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y):\pi_{X}=\mu_{X},\,\pi_{Y}=\mu_{Y}\right\}.

With a view toward introducing relaxed notions of optimal transport-based distances, we now define two additional types of couplings, both given by relaxing the equality in (
2.1
).

Definition 2.2

(Relaxed Couplings)
.

Given
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in[0,\infty]^{2}
and a pair of probability spaces

(X,\mu_{X}),(Y,\mu_{Y})
, we define the set of
\varepsilon
-relaxed couplings
to be

(2.2)

\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y}):=\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y):\pi_{X}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mu_{X}\text{ and }\pi_{Y}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{2})\mu_{Y}\right\},

where the inequality
\pi_{X}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mu_{X}
means that
\pi_{X}(A)\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mu_{X}(A)
for all Borel measurable sets
A\subset X
, and likewise for the other inequality. Similarly, we define the set of
symmetric
\varepsilon
-relaxed couplings
to be

(2.3)

\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y}):=\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y):\begin{matrix}\|\frac{d\pi_{X}}{d\mu_{X}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu_{X})},\,\|\frac{d\mu_{X}}{d\pi_{X}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{X})}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{1};\\
\|\frac{d\pi_{Y}}{d\mu_{Y}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu_{Y})},\,\|\frac{d\mu_{Y}}{d\pi_{Y}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{Y})}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{2}\end{matrix}\right\}.

The intuition behind these definitions is that the spaces of
\varepsilon
-relaxed couplings allow measures whose marginals are allowed to redistribute mass in a controlled fashion.

Remark 2.3
.

The condition
\|\frac{d\mu_{X}}{d\pi_{X}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{X})}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{1}
, in particular, implies that
\pi_{X}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mu_{X}
, in the sense described above. It follows that
\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})\subset\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
.

Remark 2.4
.

Using the shorthand
0=(0,0)
, observe that

(2.4)

\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})=\mathcal{C}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})=\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y}).

Namely, when no mass redistribution is allowed in the marginals, the ‘relaxed’ couplings become exact.

2.2
Partial Gromov-Wasserstein Distances

We now introduce relaxed versions of the Gromov-Wasserstein distance and derive some basic properties. First, we recall some properties of the Gromov-Wasserstein distance itself.

Gromov-Wasserstein distance.

Let
X
and
Y
be pmm-spaces and let
p\in[1,\infty]
. We consider
d_{X}-d_{Y}
as a function on
X\times Y\times X\times Y
, so that the quantity
\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}
is well-defined for any measure
\pi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X\times Y)
(in general, e.g., without a compactness assumption, it may be equal to
\infty
). Explicitly, for
p<\infty
, this is given by

(2.5)

\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}=\left(\int_{X\times Y}\int_{X\times Y}\left|d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})-d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})\right|^{p}d\pi(x,y)d\pi(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right)^{1/p}.

With the above notation, the
Gromov-Wasserstein (GW)
p
-distance
between
X
and
Y
is given by

(2.6)

\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X,Y)\coloneqq\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})}\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}.

This notion of distance was introduced by Mémoli in
[
20
]
, with additional properties established in
[
21
]
. Particularly important properties are described in the following theorem, which combines results from
[
20
,
21
]
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
20
,
21
]
)
.

(A)

For compact pmm-spaces
X
and
Y
, the infimum in (
2.6
) is always realized by a coupling
\pi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})

[
21
, Corollary 10.1]
.

(B)

Gromov-Wasserstein distance
\mathsf{GW}_{p}
defines a pseudometric on the space of compact, fully-supported pmm-spaces, with
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X,Y)=0
if and only if
X
and
Y
are isomorphic
[
21
, Theorem 5.1(a)]
.

Remark 2.6
.

Some of the conditions in these results can be weakened—see
[
8
]
or
[
29
]
, where some generalizations are established, or
[
24
]
which surveys properties of the Gromov-Wasserstein distance under various assumptions. As was mentioned in Remark
2.1
, we frequently work at the level of generality of
[
21
]
—that is, compact and fully supported spaces.

Gromov-Wasserstein distances (and variants thereof—see, e.g.,
[
30
,
31
,
36
,
2
]
) have been widely used in recent data-oriented applications, such as shape analysis
[
25
,
9
]
, network science
[
35
,
10
]
and computational biology
[
12
,
16
]
. A key feature of the GW framework is that the computation of the distance itself leads to useful information—a coupling minimizing (
2.6
) can be understood as defining a ‘soft correspondence’ between the pmm-spaces. This correspondence can be used to register the points between the spaces, which is crucial for downstream analysis tasks.

The terminology
Gromov-Wasserstein
is intended to signal that the GW distances combine ideas used to define two well-known, classical distances:

•

The
Gromov-Hausdorff distance
is a metric on the space of isometry classes of compact metric spaces (which have not been endowed with a measure)—see
[
4
]
for a thorough discussion of its properties. Roughly, its formulation ‘lifts’ the idea of the Hausdorff distance between subsets of a common metric space to the setting of distinct spaces.

•

The
Wasserstein distance
from classical optimal transport theory (see
[
32
]
) defines a metric on the space of probability distributions over a common metric space. We now recall the details, as they will be useful later in the paper. Let
(M,d)
be a metric space and let
\mu,\nu
be two probability measures on
M
. The
Wasserstein
p
-distance
between
\mu
and
\nu
is defined by

\mathsf{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)}\left(\int_{M}d(m,m^{\prime})^{p}d\pi(m,m^{\prime})\right)^{1/p}=\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)}\|d\|_{L^{p}(\pi)},

for
p\in[1,\infty)
, with the definition extending naturally to the
p=\infty
case.

In reference to the ideas described above, the
Gromov-Wasserstein distance
can be seen as a ‘Gromov-ization’ of the Wasserstein distance, in the sense that it lifts Wasserstein distances to the setting where measures on distinct metric spaces can be compared. Given two pmm-spaces
X=(M,d,\mu)
and
Y=(M,d,\nu)
having the same underlying metric space
(M,d)
, we always have

(2.7)

\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X,Y)\leq 2\cdot\mathsf{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)

(see
[
21
, Theorem 5.1(c)]
, noting the difference in normalization of
\mathsf{GW}_{p}
in the current paper).

Relaxing the Gromov-Wasserstein distance.

The registration properties of GW distances described above (i.e., through probabilistic matchings in the guise of optimal couplings) are vulnerable to outliers in the data. As an example, suppose that in the process of acquiring pmm-space datasets
X
and
Y
, measurement error leads to additional ‘noisy’ points being included in the supports of
\mu_{X}
and
\mu_{Y}
; since the formulation of GW distance involves couplings which match the measures exactly (i.e., exact couplings), the resulting registration will necessarily fit the noise. This observation motivates one to alter the formulation of the distance to allow for relaxed notions of coupling that avoid the noise-fitting issue—indeed, several notions of
partial
or
unbalanced
Gromov-Wasserstein distance have been introduced in the recent literature to address this issue, and related issues of non-robustness
[
6
,
28
,
1
,
19
]
.

We now introduce a variant of GW which is relaxed in a rather straightforward manner: we simply expand the feasible set to allow
\varepsilon
-relaxed couplings, as in Definition
2.2
.

Definition 2.7

(Partial Gromov-Wasserstein Distances)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
, let
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in[0,\infty]^{2}
, and let
X
and
Y
be pmm-spaces. We define the associated
partial Gromov-Wasserstein
p
-distance
between
X
and
Y
to be

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)\coloneqq\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})}\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}.

Similarly, we define the
symmetrized partial Gromov-Wasserstein
p
-distance
to be

{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)\coloneqq\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})}\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}.

Remark 2.8
.

The construction of
{\mathsf{PGW}}
is a generalization of the
partial Gromov-Wasserstein problem
proposed by Chapel et al. in
[
6
]
, as we explain in more detail below in Remark
2.10
and Section
3.3.1
. We note that the Chapel et al. version is defined on the space of general mm-spaces, whose measures have arbitrary total mass. The justification for our reformulation is that it makes certain proofs easier, and allows more immediate comparisons to other notions of partial GW distance in the literature—see Section
3.3
.

Recall the comment after Definition
2.2
. The intuition is that
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
and
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
behave similarly to
\mathsf{GW}_{p}
, with the difference being that the couplings allow for controlled rearrangement of mass. If the relaxation level is appropriately matched to the noise level in the data, this setup is intended to allow the distances to mollify the effect of outliers in the inferred registrations.

Remark 2.9
.

We note that we refer to
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
and
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
as ‘distances’ only in a colloquial sense: they are not, in general, pseudometrics, as we establish below in Section
3
. To be more precise, we could refer to them as
divergences
or
dissimilarity functions
, but we prefer to stick with the
distance
terminology, which we find to be more linguistically natural.

Remark 2.10
.

The following describes some special cases for the relaxed distances
{\mathsf{PGW}}
and
{\mathsf{sPGW}}
.

(A)

For
\varepsilon=(0,0)
,

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{(0,0),p}(X,Y)={\mathsf{sPGW}}_{(0,0),p}=\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X,Y).

Indeed, this follows immediately from Remark
2.4
.

(B)

For
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})=(0,\infty)
,

{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{(0,\infty),p}(X,Y)=\inf\left\{\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}:\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y),\,\pi_{X}=\mu_{X}\right\}.

That is, the marginal
\pi_{X}
is strictly required to be
\mu_{X}
, while the marginal
\pi_{Y}
is unrestricted. This recovers the
semi-relaxed Gromov-Wasserstein distance
, first introduced in
[
33
]
. In
[
11
]
, the authors also showed that
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{(0,\infty),p}
satisfies the triangle inequality if and only if
p=\infty
, and that, in this case, a symmetrized version agrees with the
modified Gromov-Hausdorff distance
studied in
[
22
]
. See also Theorem
3.10
below.

(C)

When
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,\infty)
and
p=2
, the distance is essentially the same as the partial Gromov-Wasserstein distance introduced by Chapel et al. in
[
6
]
. The exact connection between the construction of Chapel et al. and those of the present paper is elucidated below in Section
3.3.1
.

2.3
Basic properties of
{\mathsf{PGW}}
and
{\mathsf{sPGW}}

It is natural to ask whether the properties of GW distance described in Theorem
2.5
extend to the partial GW variants introduced in Definition
2.7
. We show in this subsection that the existence of optimal (relaxed) couplings (i.e., the analogue of Theorem
2.5
(A)) does hold. The metric properties of
{\mathsf{PGW}}
and
{\mathsf{sPGW}}
(cf. Theorem
2.5
(B)) are more subtle, and a precise discussion is deferred to Section
3
. We also show in this subsection that the GW distance is recovered as a limit of partial GW distances as the relaxation parameters decay to zero.

Existence of optimal couplings.

We now show that the infima in the optimization problems defining
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
and
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
are always realized by optimal couplings; that is, infima are actually minima. This is the analog of Theorem
2.5
(A) in the GW setting. The proof is more subtle than in the case of exact couplings and requires an additional ingredient for the case
p=\infty
. Before we state the result, we introduce the notion of distortion to simplify notation.

Definition 2.11

(
[
8
]
)
.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
and pmm-spaces
X
and
Y
, we define the
p
-distortion of a coupling
\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)
to be

\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\pi):=\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}.

Lemma 2.12
.

Fix
\varepsilon\geq 0
and pmm-spaces
X
and
Y
.

(A)

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the function
\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\cdot)
is continuous on
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
and on
\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
.

(B)

For
p=\infty
,
\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\cdot)
is lower-semicontinuous on
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
and on
\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
.

The proof of Lemma
2.12
is almost identical to the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 11]
. We omit the details here.

Theorem 2.13

(Existence of Optimal Relaxed Couplings)
.

For every
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and compact pmm-spaces
X,Y
, there exist
\pi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
such that

\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}={\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\gamma\otimes\gamma)}={\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y).

Proof.

Let
(\pi_{k})\subset\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
and
(\gamma_{k})\subset\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(X,Y)
be sequences of relaxed couplings such that

\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi_{k}\otimes\pi_{k})}\to{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)\text{ and }\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\gamma_{k}\otimes\gamma_{k})}\to{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y).

By Prokhorov’s Theorem, we can extract a subsequence from each, still called
(\pi_{k})
and
(\gamma_{k})
, with

\pi_{k}\to\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)\text{ and }\gamma_{k}\to\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)\text{ weakly}.

First, we verify that

(2.8)

\pi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})\text{ and }\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y}).

We will only check that
\pi_{X}:=(p^{X})_{\#}\pi\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mu_{X}
, since the rest are similar (the inner regularity argument for
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
below is replaced with both inner and outer regularity for
\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}
coupling).

By definition,
\pi_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
implies that
(p^{X})_{\#}\pi_{k}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mu_{X}
. Let
A\subset X
be Borel and let
\eta>0
. By the outer regularity of
\mu_{X}
, there exists a slightly enlarged neighborhood
A_{\eta}\supset A
satisfying

(2.9)

\mu_{X}(A_{\eta}\setminus A)\leq\eta.

Thanks to Urysohn’s Lemma, there exists a continuous function
\phi
on
X\times Y
satisfying

(2.10)

\mathbb{I}_{A\times Y}\leq\phi\leq\mathbb{I}_{A_{\eta}\times Y}

where
\mathbb{I}_{S}
is the indicator function of a set
S
.
On the one hand, (
2.10
) implies

(2.11)

\pi_{X}(A)=\pi(A\times Y)=\int_{X\times Y}\mathbb{I}_{A\times Y}d\pi\leq\int_{X\times Y}\phi d\pi.

On the other hand, we can use (
2.9
) and the marginal control on the
\pi_{k}
’s to obtain

(2.12)

\begin{split}\int_{X\times Y}\phi d\pi&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{X\times Y}\phi d\pi_{k}\\
&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{X\times Y}(\phi-\mathbb{I}_{A\times Y})d\pi_{k}+\pi_{k}(A\times Y)\right)\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mu_{X}(A)+\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{(A_{\eta}\setminus A)\times Y}d\pi_{k}\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mu_{X}(A)+(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mu_{X}(A_{\eta}\setminus A)\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mu_{X}(A)+(1+\varepsilon_{1})\eta.\end{split}

Since
A\subset X
and
\eta>0
are arbitrary, we can conclude from (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) that
\pi_{X}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mu_{X}
.

Next, we show that
\pi
and
\gamma
are optimal. If
1\leq p<\infty
, weak convergence implies that

(2.13)

\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}={\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)\text{ and }\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\gamma\otimes\gamma)}={\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y).

Suppose
p=\infty
. Thanks to Lemma
2.12
(B), we have

\begin{split}{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,\infty}(X,Y)&=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(\pi_{k})\geq\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(\pi)=\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi\otimes\pi)}\text{, and }\\
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,\infty}(X,Y)&=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(\gamma_{k})\geq\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(\gamma)=\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma\otimes\gamma)}.\end{split}

By the minimality of
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,\infty}
and
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,\infty}
, we can conclude that

(2.14)

\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi\otimes\pi)}={\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,\infty}(X,Y)\text{ and }\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma\otimes\gamma)}={\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,\infty}(X,Y).

The conclusion of the proposition follows from (
2.8
), (
2.13
), and (
2.14
).
∎

Convergence in parameters.

We move to investigate the dependence of partial GW distances on the relaxation parameters.
We begin with a fundamental result which states that the maps
\varepsilon\mapsto{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\varepsilon\mapsto{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(\cdot,\cdot)
are lower semicontinuous in
\varepsilon
(Theorem
2.14
below). By Remark
2.10
(A), the partial GW distances agree with the GW distance when the relaxation parameters vanish, i.e., when
\varepsilon=(0,0)
. Theorem
2.15
below demonstrates the intuitive result that the distances converge as the relaxation parameters decay to zero. Finally, Theorem
2.16
below states that the partial GW distances always converge with respect to monotone-decreasing parameters (see the definition of monotone-decreasing before Theorem
2.16
).

Theorem 2.14

(Lower Semicontinuity in the Relaxation Parameters)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and let
X,Y
be compact pmm-spaces. Then the maps
[0,\infty)^{2}\mapsto{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)
and
[0,\infty)^{2}\mapsto{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)
are both lower semicontinuous.

Proof.

We prove the theorem for
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
. The case for
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
is similar,

Fix an arbitrary sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to\varepsilon_{0}
. We need to show that

(2.15)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{n},p}(X,Y)\geq{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{0},p}(X,Y).

Set
\delta_{n}:={\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{n},p}(X,Y)
and consider an arbitrary convergent subsequence
(\delta_{n_{k}})\subset(\delta_{n})
. Let
\pi_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
be a measure realizing
\delta_{n_{k}}={\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}},p}(X,Y)
for each
k
. By Prokhorov’s theorem, we may replace
\pi_{k}
by a subsequence, denoted
\pi_{k_{j}}
, such that
\pi_{k_{j}}\to\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)
weakly. We also have

(2.16)

\pi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(X,Y)\text{ and }{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{0},p}(X,Y)\leq\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the weak convergence of
(\pi_{k_{j}})
implies

(2.17)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\delta_{n_{k_{j}}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{n_{k_{j}}},p}(X,Y)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi_{k_{j}}\otimes\pi_{k_{j}})}=\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}.

For
p=\infty
, Lemma
2.12
(B) implies

(2.18)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\delta_{n_{k_{j}}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{n_{k_{j}}},\infty}(X,Y)=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(\pi_{k_{j}})\geq\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(\pi)=\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi\otimes\pi)}.

Since

is convergent, we conclude from (

p\in[1,\infty]
,

(2.19)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{n_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}},p}(X,Y)\geq\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}.

We see from (
2.16
) and
(
2.19
) that

(2.20)

\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}},p}(X,Y)\geq{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{0},p}(X,Y).

Since the choice of the convergent subsequence
(\delta_{n_{k}})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is arbitrary, we see that (
2.15
) holds.
∎

Theorem 2.15

(Convergence to
\mathsf{GW}_{p}
)
.

Let

and let

Let

3
Approximate metric properties for
{\mathsf{PGW}}
and
{\mathsf{sPGW}}

When the partial Gromov-Wasserstein problem was introduced in
[
6
]
(as referenced in the introduction; see Definition
3.12
for details), its metric properties, such as whether it satisfies the triangle inequality, were not addressed (to be clear,
[
6
]
makes no claims that their framework induces a pseudometric, and only considers it as an optimization problem with interesting applications). An initial motivation for the present article was to study the metric structure of the partial Gromov-Wasserstein problem, which is equivalent to studying it for
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
—see Proposition
3.13
below.

In this section, we study the metric properties of
{\mathsf{PGW}}
and
{\mathsf{sPGW}}
. As a trivial point, it is clear that if
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}
, then
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)={\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(Y,X)
for all
X
and
Y
. This section will therefore focus on non-degeneracy and triangle inequalities for the distances. We show that both properties fail, but give more refined results which demonstrate that they do satisfy approximate versions.

3.1
Approximate Non-Degeneracy

The next theorem gives a characterization of the partial Gromov-Wasserstein distances in terms of Gromov-Wasserstein distances between pmm-spaces with adjusted mass. It will be followed by a corollary that addresses the extent to which the distance
{\mathsf{PGW}}
is
degenerate
, in the sense that non-isomorphic pmm-spaces can have zero distance. To state the theorem, we introduce some additional concepts.

Definition 3.1

(Eccentricity and Circumradius; c.f.
[
21
, Definition 5.3]
and
[
22
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

Let
X=(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
be an mm-space and
p\in[1,\infty]
. The
p
-eccentricity function of
X
is the function

\displaystyle\mathsf{ecc}_{p}^{X}:X

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\|d_{X}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\mu_{X})}.

We define the
p
-circumradius of
X
to be the quantity

\mathsf{rad}_{p}(X)=\inf_{x\in X}\mathsf{ecc}_{p}^{X}(x).

Definition 3.2

(
\varepsilon
-Relations)
.

Let
X=(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
be a pmm-space and let
\tilde{X}=(X,d_{X},\tilde{\mu}_{X})
be another mm-space with the same underlying metric space. For
\varepsilon\in[0,\infty]
, we write
\tilde{X}\sim_{\varepsilon}X
if
\tilde{\mu}_{X}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{X}
. Similarly, we write
\tilde{X}\sim_{\varepsilon}^{s}X
if

\left\|\frac{d\tilde{\mu}_{X}}{d\mu_{X}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu_{X})},\left\|\frac{d\mu_{X}}{d\tilde{\mu}_{X}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{\mu}_{X})}\leq 1+\varepsilon.

Remark 3.3
.

The relations
\sim_{\varepsilon}
and
\sim_{\varepsilon}^{s}
are not equivalence relations. Both relations are reflexive, only
\sim_{\varepsilon}^{s}
is symmetric, and neither is transitive.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
and
Y
be compact pmm-spaces, let
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in[0,\infty]^{2}
, let
p\in[1,\infty]
and let
q
satisfy
1/q+1/p=1
(setting
1/\infty=0
).
Suppose that
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)
is realized by
\pi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
(see Theorem
2.13
). Define pmm-spaces
X_{\pi}=(X,d_{X},\pi_{X})
and
Y_{\pi}=(Y,d_{Y},\pi_{Y})
. Then

(3.1)

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)=\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{\pi},Y_{\pi})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{X}\sim_{\varepsilon_{1}}X\\
\tilde{Y}\sim_{\varepsilon_{2}}Y\end{subarray}}\mathsf{GW}_{p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}).

Similarly, if
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)
is realized by
\pi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
, then

{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)=\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{\pi},Y_{\pi})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{X}\sim_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{s}X\\
\tilde{Y}\sim_{\varepsilon_{2}}^{s}Y\end{subarray}}\mathsf{GW}_{p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}).

Moreover, in either case (with
\mathsf{rad}_{p}(\cdot)
as in Definition
3.1
),

(3.2)

\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{\pi},X)\leq 2\cdot 2^{1/q}\varepsilon_{1}^{1/p}\mathsf{rad}_{p}(X),\quad\mathsf{GW}_{p}(Y_{\pi},Y)\leq 2\cdot 2^{1/q}\varepsilon_{2}^{1/p}\mathsf{rad}_{p}(Y).

Remark 3.5
.

The estimates on
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{\pi},X)
and
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(Y_{\pi},Y)
when
p=\infty
become

\mathsf{GW}_{\infty}(X_{\pi},X)\leq 4\cdot\mathsf{rad}_{\infty}(X),\quad\mathsf{GW}_{\infty}(Y_{\pi},Y)\leq 4\cdot\mathsf{rad}_{\infty}(Y).

The loss of dependence on
\varepsilon_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}
causes these estimates to be rather poor. We include the
p=\infty
case here primarily because the above observation will be useful later in Theorem
4.8
.

We need the following as an intermediate estimate.

Theorem 3.6

(
[
32
, Theorem 6.15]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
1/p+1/q=1
. Then, for any
x\in X
,

(3.3)

\mathsf{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)\leq 2^{1/q}\left(\int_{X}d(x,y)^{p}d\left|\mu-\nu\right|(y)\right)^{1/p},

where
|\mu-\nu|
denotes the total variation measure.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

We will prove the claims for
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
, since the proofs for
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
are almost identical. Recall the distortion functional
\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\cdot)
from Definition
2.11
. To derive the identity (
3.1
), we first observe that
\pi
must be an optimal coupling for
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{\pi},Y_{\pi})
. Indeed, if
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(\pi_{X},\pi_{Y})
satisfied
\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\pi^{\prime})<\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\pi)
, then this would contradict the optimality of
\pi
for
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
, since
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
. This implies

\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{\pi},Y_{\pi})=\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\pi)={\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}(X,Y).

For the second equality, we clearly have
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{\pi},Y_{\pi})\geq\inf_{\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}}\mathsf{GW}_{p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})
. On the other hand, let
\tilde{X}\sim_{\varepsilon_{1}}X
and
\tilde{Y}\sim_{\varepsilon_{2}}Y
be arbitrary. For any optimal coupling
\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{C}(\tilde{\mu}_{X},\tilde{\mu}_{Y})
for
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})
, we have
\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(X,Y)
, so that

\mathsf{GW}_{p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})=\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\tilde{\pi})\geq\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\pi)={\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}(X,Y)=\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{\pi},Y_{\pi}),

by the optimality of
\pi
for
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)
and by the work above. Since
\tilde{X}
and
\tilde{Y}
were arbitrary, this shows
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{\pi},Y_{\pi})=\inf_{\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}}\mathsf{GW}_{p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})
.

It remains to establish the estimates (
3.2
). We do so for
p<\infty
for convenience; the
p=\infty
cases follow by a limiting argument. The bound (
2.7
), together with Theorem
3.6
, implies that

(3.4)

\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{\pi},X)\leq 2\cdot\mathsf{W}_{p}(\pi_{X},\mu_{X})\leq 2\cdot 2^{1/q}\left(\int d_{X}(x,y)^{p}d|\pi_{X}-\mu_{X}|(y)\right)^{1/p}

holds for any
x\in X
. Let
\rho_{X}:=\frac{d\pi_{X}}{d\mu_{X}}
. Thanks to the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
, we have

(3.5)

\|\rho_{X}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu_{X})}\leq\varepsilon_{1}.

Using (
3.5
), we have, for any
x\in X
,

(3.6)

\int_{X}d_{X}(x,y)^{p}d\left|\pi_{X}-\mu_{X}\right|(y)\leq\int_{X}d_{X}(x,y)^{p}\left|\rho_{X}-1\right|d\mu_{X}(x)\leq\varepsilon_{1}\mathsf{ecc}_{p}^{X}(x)^{p}.

The first estimate in (
3.2
) follows from (
3.4
), (
3.6
), and the definition of
\mathsf{rad}_{p}(X)
. The second estimate in (
3.2
) is proved similarly.
∎

As an easy corollary, we get a characterization of the non-degeneracy of the partial Gromov-Wasserstein distances. It roughly states that if
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)=0
or

{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)=0
, then
X
and
Y
have large portions (depending on the size of the parameters
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
) that are isomorphic.

Corollary 3.7
.

Using the notation of Theorem
3.4
,
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)=0
if and only if
X_{\pi}
and
Y_{\pi}
are isomorphic. Similarly,
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)=0
if and only if
X_{\pi}
and
Y_{\pi}
are isomorphic (where
\pi
is now optimal for
{\mathsf{sPGW}}
). Moreover, the spaces
X_{\pi}
and
Y_{\pi}
satisfy the estimates (
3.2
).

3.2
Approximate Triangle Inequality for
{\mathsf{sPGW}}

We now show that the ‘distance’
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
does not, in fact, enjoy a triangle inequality. The definition of the symmetrized partial GW distance
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
was partially motivated as a means to obtain a weakened version of the triangle inequality, which we establish below in the main result of this subsection, Theorem
3.10
. Before stating the theorem, we provide a counterexample showing that a true triangle inequality does not hold. A similar construction works for
{\mathsf{PGW}}
as well, but we only focus on
{\mathsf{sPGW}}
, since it is the more rigid of the two.

Counterexample.

For finite metric spaces, it is convenient to use matrix notations. The probability measure is given by a stochastic vector and the distance is given by a zero-diagonal entry-wise nonnegative symmetric matrix. Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{6}]
and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}-\delta
. Let
\varepsilon=(0,\varepsilon_{2})
for some
\varepsilon_{2}\in[0,1)
satisfying

\frac{\delta}{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\leq\varepsilon_{2}\leq\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}.

Consider three finite mm-spaces
(X,\mu_{X},d_{X})
,
(Y,\mu_{Y},d_{Y})
, and
(Z,\mu_{Z},d_{Z})
defined by

•

X=\{x\}
and
\mu_{X}=(1)
;

•

Y=\{y_{1},y_{2}\}
,
\mu_{Y}=(\beta,1-\beta)
, and
D_{Y}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}
;

•

Z=\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
,
\mu_{Z}=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
, and
D_{Z}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}
.

Set
\pi:=\mathsf{Diag}(1/2,1/2)\in\mathcal{P}(Z\times Y)
.
It is clear that
\pi_{Z}=\mu_{Z}
, and we can verify by hand that
\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{2}}\leq\frac{\pi_{Y}}{\mu_{Y}}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{2}
. Therefore,

{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(Z,Y)^{p}\leq\sum_{i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}=1}^{2}\left|D_{Z}(i,i^{\prime})-D_{Y}(j,j^{\prime})\right|^{p}\pi(i,j)\pi(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})=0.

Similarly one can define
\bar{\pi}:=\mathsf{Diag}(\beta,1-\beta)\in\mathcal{P}(Y\times Z)
and verify that
\bar{\pi}_{Y}=\mu_{Y}
and
\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{2}}\leq\frac{\bar{\pi}_{Y}}{\mu_{Z}}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{2}
. Hence
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(Y,Z)=0
.

Every
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
can be written as
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}s&(1-s)\end{bmatrix}

with
\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{2}}\leq\frac{s}{\beta},\frac{1-s}{1-\beta}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{2}
.
An elementary but tedious computation yields

(3.7)

{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)^{p}=\frac{1-2\delta}{1+\varepsilon_{2}}\left(1-\frac{1-2\delta}{2(1+\varepsilon_{2})}\right),

where the distance is achieved by
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}s&(1-s)\end{bmatrix}
with
s=\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}{1+\varepsilon_{2}}
.

Similarly, one can verify that every
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Z})
has the form
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}(\frac{1}{2}(1-t)&\frac{1}{2}(1+t)\end{bmatrix}
with
\left|t\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{1+\varepsilon_{2}}
. A direct computation yields

{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Z)^{p}=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2(1+\varepsilon_{2})}\right).

In view of the computations above, we see that
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Z)>{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)+{\mathsf{sPGW}}(Y,Z)
.

Relaxed Triangle Inequality.

We now show that
{\mathsf{sPGW}}
satisfies a certain relaxed triangle inequality. We begin by recalling a standard result from measure theory
[
18
, Chapter 3]
.

Theorem 3.8

(Disintegration of Measures)
.

Let
\Pi,Y
be compact probability metric measures spaces, let
f:\Pi\to Y
be measurable, and let
\pi\in\mathcal{P}(\Pi)
. Let
\mu=f_{\#}\pi\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
. Then there exists a family
\left\{\pi(\cdot|y)\right\}_{y\in Z}
of Borel probability measures such that
\pi(\cdot|y)\in\mathcal{P}(f^{-1}(y))
and

(3.8)

\int_{\Pi}g(\xi)d\pi(\xi)=\int_{Y}\int_{f^{-1}(y)}g(\xi)d\pi(\xi|y)d\mu(y)

for all Borel-measurable maps
g:\Pi\to[0,\infty]
. Moreover,
\left\{\pi(\cdot|y)\right\}_{y\in Y}
is
\mu
-almost everywhere unique.

A key result in classical optimal transport theory is the
gluing lemma
, which gives a natural notion of composing (exact) couplings and is used in the standard proof that Wasserstein distance defines a metric
[
32
, Chapter 6]
. The following result extends the gluing lemma to the setting of relaxed couplings.

Lemma 3.9

(Gluing Lemma for
\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}
)
.

Fix
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
and let

(X,\mu_{X}),(Y,\mu_{Y}),(Z,\mu_{Z})
be compact probability measure spaces. Let
\pi_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
and
\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{Y},\mu_{Z})
. Then there exists a probability measure
\pi_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y\times Z)
, with
\gamma_{1}:=(p^{X\times Y})_{\#}\pi_{0}
and
\gamma_{2}:=(p^{Y\times Z})_{\#}\pi_{0}
, that satisfies the following properties.

(A)

\gamma_{1}\ll\pi_{1}
,
\pi_{1}\ll\gamma_{1}
, and
\|\frac{d\gamma_{1}}{d\pi_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{1})},\|\frac{d\pi_{1}}{d\gamma_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{1})}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{2}
.

(B)

\gamma_{2}\ll\pi_{2}
,
\pi_{2}\ll\gamma_{2}
, and
\|\frac{d\gamma_{2}}{d\pi_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{2})},\|\frac{d\pi_{2}}{d\gamma_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{2})}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{1}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.8
, we can disintegrate
\pi_{i}
into
\left\{\pi_{i}(\cdot|y)\right\}_{y\in Y}
such that

(3.9)

\begin{split}\pi_{1}(A\times B)=\int_{B}\pi_{1}(A|y)d\mu_{1}(y)&\text{ and }\pi_{2}(B\times C)=\int_{B}\pi_{2}(C|y)d\mu_{2}(y)\end{split}

for all
A\subset X
,
B\subset Y
,
C\subset Z
Borel. Here and below, we use

(3.10)

\mu_{i}
to denote the
Y
-marginal of
\pi_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Let
\pi_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X\times Y\times Z)
be defined by

(3.11)

\begin{split}\pi_{0}(A\times B\times C)&=\int_{B}\pi_{1}(A|y)\pi_{2}(C|y)d\mu_{Y}(y)\end{split}

Plugging
A=X
,
B=Y
, and
C=Z
into (
3.11
) and using (
3.9
), we have

\begin{split}\pi_{0}(X\times Y\times Z)&=\int_{Y}\left(\int_{X}d\pi_{1}(x|y)\right)\left(\int_{Z}d\pi_{2}(z|y)\right)d\mu_{Y}(y)=1.\end{split}

Therefore,

\pi_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y\times Z).

Next, we verify the coupling estimates. Mutual absolute continuity follows directly from these estimates.

Let
A\subset X
and
Y\subset Z
be Borel.

\begin{split}\gamma_{1}(A\times B)&=\pi_{0}(A\times B\times Z)\\
&=\int_{B}\left(\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)\right)\left(\int_{Z}d\pi_{2}(z|y)\right)d\mu_{Y}(y)\\
&=\int_{B}\frac{d\mu_{Y}}{d\mu_{1}}(y)\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)d\mu_{1}(y)\\
&\leq\left(1+\varepsilon_{2}\right)\int_{B}\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)d\mu_{1}(y)\\
&=(1+\varepsilon_{2})\pi_{1}(A\times B)\end{split}

On the other hand,

\begin{split}\pi_{1}(A\times B)&=\int_{B}\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)d\mu_{1}(y)\\
&=\int_{B}\left(\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)\right)\left(\int_{Z}d\pi_{2}(z|y)\right)d\mu_{1}(y)\\
&=\int_{B}\frac{d\mu_{1}}{d\mu_{Y}}(y)\left(\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)\right)\left(\int_{Z}d\pi_{2}(z|y)\right)d\mu_{Y}(y)\\
&\leq\left(1+\varepsilon_{2}\right)\int_{B}\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)\int_{Z}d\pi_{2}(z|y)d\mu_{Y}(y)\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{2})\gamma_{1}(A\times B).\end{split}

Therefore,

\|\frac{d\gamma_{1}}{d\pi_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{1})},\|\frac{d\pi_{1}}{d\gamma_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{1})}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{2}.

The estimates in Part (B) are proven similarly.
∎

The next theorem is the main result of this subsection, and it roughly states that
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
‘almost’ has the structure of a semi-metric, in that it satisfies a certain relaxed triangle inequality.

Theorem 3.10

(Approximate Triangle Inequality)
.

Let
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in[0,\infty)^{2}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Let
\varepsilon^{*}:=(\varepsilon_{1}^{*},\varepsilon_{2}^{*})
with

\varepsilon_{1}^{*}=\varepsilon_{2}^{*}=(1+\varepsilon_{1})(1+\varepsilon_{2})-1=\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}.

Let
X,Y,Z
be compact pmm-spaces. Then

(3.12)

\begin{split}{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon^{*},p}(X,Z)&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{2})^{2/p}{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)+(1+\varepsilon_{1})^{2/p}{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(Y,Z).\end{split}

Proof.

Fix
X,Y,Z
as above, and let
\pi_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)
and
\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(Y\times Z)
realize
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)
and
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(Y,Z)
, respectively. Let
\pi_{0}
be as in Lemma
3.9
and define

(3.13)

\pi:=(p^{X\times Z})_{\#}\pi_{0}.

First, we verify that
\pi
is a feasible coupling.
Let
\mu_{1}:=(p^{Y})_{\#}\pi_{1}
.
For a Borel measurable set
A\subset X
, by formula (
3.11
) and (
3.13
), we have

\begin{split}(p^{X})_{\#}\pi(A)&=\pi_{0}(A\times Y\times Z)\\
&=\int_{Y}\int_{A\times Z}d\pi_{1}(x|y)d\pi_{2}(z|y)d\mu_{Y}(y)\\
&=\int_{Y}\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)\left(\int_{Z}d\pi_{2}(z|y)\right)d\mu_{Y}(y)\\
&=\int_{Y}\frac{d\mu_{Y}}{d\mu_{1}}(y)\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)d\mu_{Y}(y)\\
&\leq\left(1+\varepsilon_{2}\right)\int_{Y}\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)d\mu_{1}(y)\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{2})\int_{A\times Y}d\pi_{1}(x,y)\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})(1+\varepsilon_{2})\mu_{X}(A).\end{split}

On the other hand,

\begin{split}\mu_{X}(A)&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\pi_{1}(A,Y)\\
&=(1+\varepsilon_{1})\int_{Y}\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)d\mu_{1}(y)\\
&=(1+\varepsilon_{1})\int_{Y}\frac{d\mu_{1}}{d\mu_{Y}}(y)\int_{A}d\pi_{1}(x|y)\left(\int_{Z}d\pi_{2}(z|y)\right)d\mu_{Y}(y)\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})\left(1+\varepsilon_{2}\right)\int_{Y}\int_{A}\int_{Z}d\pi_{1}(x|y)d\pi_{2}(z|y)d\mu_{Y}(y)\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{1})(1+\varepsilon_{2})\pi_{0}(A\times Y\times Z)\\
&=(1+\varepsilon_{1})(1+\varepsilon_{2})(p^{X})_{\#}\pi(A).\end{split}

The estimate for
(p^{Z})_{\#}\pi
is similar.
Therefore,

(3.14)

\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon^{*}}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Z}).

By the triangle inequality for
L^{p}
, we have

(3.15)

\begin{split}{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon^{*},p}(X,Z)\leq\|d_{X}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}&=\|d_{X}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\pi_{0}\otimes\pi_{0})}\\
&\leq\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi_{0}\otimes\pi_{0})}+\|d_{Y}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\pi_{0}\otimes\pi_{0})}.\end{split}

Suppose
1\leq p<\infty
. Since
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
realize
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)
and
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(Y,Z)
, respectively, we can use Lemma
3.9
to continue estimating (
3.15
):

(3.16)

\begin{split}&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{2})^{2/p}\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{1})}+(1+\varepsilon_{1})^{2/p}\|d_{Y}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\pi_{2}\otimes\pi_{2})}\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon_{2})^{2/p}{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)+(1+\varepsilon_{1})^{2/p}{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(Y,Z)\end{split}

Suppose
p=\infty
(so
(1+\varepsilon_{i})^{2/p}=1
). By Lemma
3.9
,
(p^{X\times Y})_{\#}\pi_{0}
and
\pi_{1}
are absolutely continuous with respect to each other, so are
(p^{Y\times Z})_{\#}\pi_{0}
and
\pi_{2}
. Therefore, we may continue estimating (
3.15
):

(3.17)

\begin{split}&=\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{1})}+\|d_{Y}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\pi_{2}\otimes\pi_{2})}\leq{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)+{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(Y,Z)\end{split}.

The proof of (
3.12
) is complete in view of (
3.14
)–(
3.17
).

∎

.

3.3
Related Work

As was mentioned above, the partial GW distances that we study in this paper are related to others that have appeared in the literature; in particular, they generalize the distances studied in
[
6
]
. In this section, we precisely contextualize our framework within the literature, using the properties of the partial Gromov-Wasserstein distances that we have established thus far.

3.3.1
Equivalence to Chapel et al.

A notion of distance between mm-spaces whose underlying sets are finite, and whose measures are not necessarily of the same total mass is defined by Chapel et al. in
[
6
, Section 3.2]
(based on earlier ideas of Caffarelli-McCann
[
5
]
and Figalli
[
14
]
). The following definition generalizes this idea to spaces that are not necessarily finite and extends the
L^{2}
-type construction of Chapel et al. to a family of
L^{p}
-type distances. We denote the resulting distances as
\mathsf{mPGW}_{\delta,p}
, where the prepended
\mathsf{m}
indicates a ‘mass constraint’ and that the distance is valid for general mm-spaces, rather than only pmm-spaces.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
\tilde{\mu}_{X}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
and
\tilde{\mu}_{Y}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(Y)
be (not necessarily probability) measures with total mass
m_{X}:=|\tilde{\mu}_{X}|
and
m_{Y}:=|\tilde{\mu}_{Y}|
respectively.
Given mm-spaces
\tilde{X}=(X,d_{X},\tilde{\mu}_{X})
and
\tilde{Y}=(Y,d_{Y},\tilde{\mu}_{Y})
,
0\leq\delta\leq\min\{m_{X},m_{Y}\}
, and
p\in[1,\infty]
, define

(3.18)

\mathsf{mPGW}_{\delta,p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}):=\inf_{\tilde{\pi}\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}(\tilde{\mu}_{X},\tilde{\mu}_{Y})}\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\tilde{\pi})},

where

(3.19)

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}(\tilde{\mu}_{X},\tilde{\mu}_{Y}):=\left\{\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X\times Y):\tilde{\pi}_{X}\leq\tilde{\mu}_{X},\,\tilde{\pi}_{Y}\leq\tilde{\mu}_{Y},\,\left|\tilde{\pi}\right|=\delta\right\}.

As before,
\tilde{\pi}_{X}
and
\tilde{\pi}_{Y}
are the
X
- and
Y
- marginals of
\tilde{\pi}
, respectively.

Intuitively, we can compare the approach of the current article with that of
[
6
]
as follows. We can interpret

\mathsf{mPGW}_{\delta,p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})
as moving only a
\delta
-portion of the total masses of
X
and
Y
via
\tilde{\pi}
, but the portions of moved mass must be restrictions of the original measures
\tilde{\mu}_{X}
and
\tilde{\mu}_{Y}
. On the other hand,
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)
allows for an
\varepsilon
amount of rearrangement of the masses of the original measures
\mu_{X}
and
\mu_{Y}
, but the full amount of rearranged masses must be transported. Despite the difference in their formulations, we now show that the two partial Gromov-Wasserstein distances are equivalent up to scaling.

Proposition 3.13
.

The following hold, with the convention
\frac{0}{0}=0
.

(A)

Given mm-spaces
\tilde{X}=(X,d_{X},\tilde{\mu}_{X})
,
\tilde{Y}=(X,d_{Y},\tilde{\mu}_{Y})
and
0\leq\delta\leq\min\{m_{X},m_{Y}\}
, we have

\delta^{-1}\mathsf{mPGW}_{\delta,p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})={\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y),

where
1+\varepsilon_{1}=\frac{m_{X}}{\delta}
,
1+\varepsilon_{2}=\frac{m_{Y}}{\delta}
, and
X=(X,d_{X},\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{X}}{m_{X}}),Y=(Y,d_{Y},\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{Y}}{m_{Y}})
are pmm-spaces.

(B)

Similarly, given pmm-spaces
X=(X,d_{X},\mu_{X}),Y=(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y}),\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0
and
\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0
, we have for any
\delta>0

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)=\delta^{-1}\mathsf{mPGW}_{\delta,p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})

where
\tilde{X}=(X,d_{X},(1+\varepsilon_{1})\delta\cdot\mu_{X})
and
\tilde{Y}=(Y,d_{Y},(1+\varepsilon_{2})\delta\cdot\mu_{Y})
.

This yields the following corollary immediately.

Corollary 3.14
.

Given pmm-spaces
X
and
Y
we have

\delta^{-1}\mathsf{mPGW}_{\delta,p}(X,Y)={\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)\quad\mbox{when}\quad 1+\varepsilon_{1}=1+\varepsilon_{2}=\frac{1}{\delta}.

In other words, the partial Gromov–Wasserstein distance
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
is equivalent to the partial GW distance
\mathsf{mPGW}_{\delta,p}
of
[
6
]
on the space of pmm-spaces.

Remark 3.15
.

In light of this corollary, one can use either formulation,
{\mathsf{PGW}}
or
\mathsf{mPGW}
, when deriving theoretical properties. The motivation for introducing and working primarily with the
{\mathsf{PGW}}
variant is that we find it more natural to work exclusively with pmm-spaces, which is a convenient setting for, e.g., establishing our version of the Gluing Lemma (Lemma
3.9
).

Proof of Proposition
3.13
.

We will show (A). The proof of (B) is similar.

If
\delta=0
then
\mathsf{mPGW}_{\delta,p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})=0
, and
\delta^{-1}\mathsf{mPGW}_{\delta,p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})=0
according to our convention. On the other hand,
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{(\infty,\infty),p}(X,Y)=0
since we can concentrate all the mass at a single point for both
X
and
Y
. The equality holds.

For the rest of the proof, we assume that
\delta>0
.

For any
\tilde{\pi}\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}(\tilde{\mu}_{X},\tilde{\mu}_{Y})
, set
\pi:=\frac{\tilde{\pi}}{\delta}
so that
\pi
is a probability measure i.e.,
|\pi|=1
.
Let
1+\varepsilon_{1}=\frac{m_{X}}{\delta}
and
1+\varepsilon_{2}=\frac{m_{Y}}{\delta}
. We have

(p^{X})_{\#}\pi\leq\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{X}}{\delta}=(1+\varepsilon_{1})\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{X}}{m_{X}},\quad\text{and}\quad(p^{Y})_{\#}\pi\leq\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{Y}}{\delta}=(1+\varepsilon_{2})\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{Y}}{m_{Y}}.

Since
|\tilde{\mu}_{X}|=m_{X}
and
|\tilde{\mu}_{Y}|=m_{Y}
,
\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{X}}{m_{X}}
and
\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{Y}}{m_{Y}}
are probability measures, so
\pi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{X}}{m_{X}},\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{Y}}{m_{Y}}\big)
.
Similarly, for any
\pi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{X}}{m_{X}},\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{Y}}{m_{Y}}\big)
with
\varepsilon=(\frac{m_{X}}{\delta}-1,\frac{m_{Y}}{\delta}-1)
, we have
\delta\pi\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}(\tilde{\mu}_{X},\tilde{\mu}_{Y})
.
Therefore,

\frac{1}{\delta}\cdot\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}(\tilde{\mu}_{X},\tilde{\mu}_{Y})=\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\bigg(\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{X}}{m_{X}},\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{Y}}{m_{Y}}\bigg).

As a result,

\displaystyle\delta^{-1}\mathsf{mPGW}_{\delta,p}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})

\displaystyle=\delta^{-1}\cdot\inf_{\tilde{\pi}\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}(\tilde{\mu}_{X},\tilde{\mu}_{Y})}\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\tilde{\pi})}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\frac{\tilde{\pi}}{\delta}\in\frac{1}{\delta}\cdot\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}(\tilde{\mu}_{X},\tilde{\mu}_{Y})}\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\tilde{\pi}}{\delta}\otimes\frac{\tilde{\pi}}{\delta})}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{X}}{m_{X}},\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{Y}}{m_{Y}}\big)}\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}

\displaystyle={\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y),

where
X=(X,dx,\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{X}}{m_{X}})
and
Y=(Y,dy,\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{Y}}{m_{Y}})
.
∎

3.3.2
Lagrangian form, relaxed couplings, and relation to Bai et al.

We compare our construction with the partial GW distances defined in Bai et al.
[
1
]
(which are, in turn, closely related to the
unbalanced GW distances
introduced in
[
28
]
). Recall Definition
2.7
. Let
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be two pmm-spaces. The optimization problem associated with
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)
has the form

(3.20)

\begin{split}\text{minimize }f(\pi)\quad\text{ subject to }\quad g_{i}(\pi)\leq 0\text{ for }i=1,2,\end{split}

where
f(\pi)=\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}^{p}
,
g_{1}(\pi)=\|\frac{d\mu_{X}}{d\pi_{X}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{X})}-\varepsilon_{1}
, and
g_{2}(\pi)=\|\frac{d\mu_{Y}}{d\pi_{Y}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{Y})}-\varepsilon_{2}
.

Let
L:\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)\times[0,\infty)^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be the Lagrangian associated with (
3.20
), given by

L(\pi,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=f(\pi)+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\lambda_{i}g_{i}(\pi).

A standard argument yields

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)=\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)}\sup_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0}L(\pi,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\geq\sup_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0}\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)}L(\pi,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

Consider the Lagrangian dual of
f
given by

\begin{split}f^{*}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})&=\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)}L(\pi,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\\
&=\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)}\left(\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}+\lambda_{1}\|\frac{d\mu_{X}}{d\pi_{X}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{X})}+\lambda_{2}\|\frac{d\mu_{Y}}{d\pi_{Y}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{Y})}\right)\\
&\hskip 312.9803pt-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\lambda_{i}\varepsilon_{i}.\end{split}

Note that
\|\frac{d\mu_{X}}{d\pi_{X}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{X})}
and
\|\frac{d\mu_{Y}}{d\pi_{Y}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{Y})}
can be written as limits of ‘
\chi^{q}
-divergences’:

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\mu_{X},\pi_{X}):=\|\frac{d\mu_{X}}{d\pi_{X}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{X})}&=\lim_{q\to\infty}\left(\int_{X}\left|\frac{d\mu_{X}}{d\pi_{X}}-1\right|^{q}d\pi_{X}\right)^{1/q},\\
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\mu_{Y},\pi_{Y}):=\|\frac{d\mu_{Y}}{d\pi_{Y}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi_{Y})}&=\lim_{q\to\infty}\left(\int_{Y}\left|\frac{d\mu_{Y}}{d\pi_{Y}}-1\right|^{q}d\pi_{Y}\right)^{1/q}.\end{split}

Using this notation, the optimization problem associated with
f^{*}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
can be written as

\text{Minimize }\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}^{p}+\lambda_{1}\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\mu_{X},\pi_{X})+\lambda_{2}\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\mu_{Y},\pi_{Y})+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\lambda_{i}\varepsilon_{i}

which resembles the Lagrangian form in
[
1
]
, where the authors penalize dissimilarity in terms of the total variation, i.e., a ‘
\chi^{1}
-divergence’. We see that the optimization problem associated with
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)
can be interpreted as a primal problem with a dual relaxation closely related to the distance defined in Bai et al.
[
1
]
.

3.3.3
Relation to Kong et al.

Another notion of relaxed GW distance, dubbed
outlier-robust Gromov-Wasserstein distance
, is considered by Kong et al. in
[
19
]
. Given a collection of positive, real hyperparameters
\rho=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
, the associated outlier-robust Gromov-Wasserstein distance between pmm-spaces
X
and
Y
is defined to be

(3.21)

\begin{split}\mathsf{RGW}_{\rho}(X,Y)&\coloneqq\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X),\beta\in\mathcal{P}(Y)}\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X\times Y)}\mathsf{dis}_{2}(\pi)^{2}+\rho_{1}\mathsf{KL}(\pi_{X}\mid\alpha)+\rho_{2}\mathsf{KL}(\pi_{Y}\mid\beta)\\
&\hskip 144.54pt\mbox{sub. to.}\quad\mathsf{KL}(\mu_{X}|\alpha)\leq\varepsilon_{1},\quad\mathsf{KL}(\mu_{Y}\mid\beta)\leq\varepsilon_{2},\end{split}

where
\mathsf{KL}
denotes Kullback-Leibler divergence and
\mathsf{dis}_{2}
is the same distortion function from Definition
2.11
. Taking
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\to\infty
, this becomes

(3.22)

\begin{split}\mathsf{RGW}_{\rho}(X,Y)&\coloneqq\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X),\beta\in\mathcal{P}(Y)}\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha,\beta)}\mathsf{dis}_{2}(\pi)^{2}\\
&\hskip 72.26999pt\mbox{sub. to.}\quad\mathsf{KL}(\mu_{X}|\alpha)\leq\varepsilon_{1},\quad\mathsf{KL}(\mu_{Y}\mid\beta)\leq\varepsilon_{2}.\end{split}

We now observe that this problem is closely related to the
{\mathsf{PGW}}
and
{\mathsf{sPGW}}
problems. Indeed, in light of Theorem
4.3
, the
{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,2}
problem can be expressed as

(3.23)

\begin{split}{\mathsf{sPGW}}_{\varepsilon,2}(X,Y)&=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X),\beta\in\mathcal{P}(Y)}\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha,\beta)}\mathsf{dis}_{2}(\pi)\\
&\hskip 72.26999pt\mbox{sub. to.}\quad\left\|\frac{d\alpha}{d\mu_{X}}-1\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu_{X})},\left\|\frac{d\mu_{X}}{d\alpha}-1\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\alpha)}\leq\varepsilon_{1}\\
&\hskip 119.24506pt\left\|\frac{d\beta}{d\mu_{Y}}-1\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu_{Y})},\left\|\frac{d\mu_{Y}}{d\beta}-1\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\beta)}\leq\varepsilon_{2}.\end{split}

Observe that the only difference between the problems (
3.22
) and (
3.23
) is the manner in which the measures
\alpha
and
\beta
are constrained to be ‘close’ to the targets
\mu_{X}
and
\mu_{Y}
(and the fact that distortion is squared in (
3.22
), with is inconsequential).

We note that the work of Kong et al. does not address any metric properties related to the optimization problem
3.21
. Based on its connection to the symmetrized partial Gromov-Wasserstein framework, and our results above, we conjecture that
3.21
also fails to induce a true metric structure on the space of pmm-spaces.

4
Robust Partial Gromov-Wasserstein Distance

To define a notion of partial Gromov-Wasserstein distance with more well-behaved metric properties, we introduce a construction similar to those appearing in the definitions of the Prokhorov, Ky Fan, and Robust Wasserstein metrics
[
27
,
26
,
3
]
.

Convention 4.1
.

For the rest of the paper, we will work with
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
with
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}
, so we treat
\varepsilon
as a scalar.

Definition 4.2

(Robust Partial Gromov–Wasserstein Distance)
.

For each
k\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
, we define the associated
robust partial Gromov-Wasserstein distance
by

(4.1)

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(X,Y):=\inf\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\geq 0:{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)\leq k\varepsilon\right\}.

The additional parameter
k
allows for data-dependent fine-tuning of the metric. We will see below that the choice of
k
does not make any qualitative difference regarding the properties of
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
. The results in this section show that
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
defines a metric with theoretical robustness guarantees.

4.1
Metric Properties of
{\mathsf{PGW}}^{k}_{p}

The robust partial GW distances are obviously symmetric. We will now show that they are non-degenerate and satisfy the triangle inequality.

Non-degeneracy.

Recall that two mm-spaces are isomorphic if there exists a measure-preserving isometric bijection between them.

Theorem 4.3

(Non-Degeneracy of
{\mathsf{PGW}}^{k}_{p}
)
.

Suppose
k>0
and let
X
and
Y
be compact, fully supported pmm-spaces. Then
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(X,Y)=0
if and only if
X
and
Y
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Suppose
X
and
Y
are isomorphic, then
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X,Y)=0
, and we have
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)\leq\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X,Y)=0
.

Suppose
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(X,Y)=0
.
Let
\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{1+\varepsilon_{j}}\searrow 0
(so
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
) along with a sequence of measures
\pi_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
with

(4.2)

\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi_{j}\otimes\pi_{j})}\leq 2k\varepsilon_{j}.

By Prokhorov’s Theorem, we can replace
(\pi_{j})
by a subsequence, still called
(\pi_{j})
, such that
\pi_{j}\to\pi\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)
weakly.
Since
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
, we have

(4.3)

(p^{X})_{\#}\pi\leq\mu_{X}\text{ and }(p^{Y})_{\#}\pi\leq\mu_{Y}

Since
\pi,\mu_{X},\mu_{Y}
are probability measures, the marginal condition (
4.3
) forces

(4.4)

(p^{X})_{\#}\pi=\mu_{X}\text{ and }(p^{Y})_{\#}\pi=\mu_{Y}.

On the other hand, by weak convergence (or Lemma
2.12
(B) in the case
p=\infty
), we have

(4.5)

\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\pi\otimes\pi)}=0.

Namely,
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X,Y)=0
. Therefore,
X
and
Y
are isomorphic.
∎

Triangle Inequality.

Next, we show that
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
distance satisfies the triangle inequality.
The proof of triangle inequality makes use of the equivalence between
{\mathsf{PGW}}
and
\mathsf{mPGW}
, as the analysis is simpler for the latter version of the distance. We therefore first define a robust version for
\mathsf{mPGW}
.

Definition 4.4

(Robust
\mathsf{mPGW}
)
.

Let
X=(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
and
Y=(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be pmm-spaces.
Consider
\mathsf{mPGW}_{\delta,p}(X,Y)
from Definition
3.12
.
For any
k\geq 0
, define

(4.6)

\mathsf{mPGW}_{p}^{k}(X,Y):=\inf\left\{0\leq\delta\leq 1:\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta,p}(X,Y)\leq k\delta\right\}.

Theorem 4.5

(Triangle Inequality for
\mathsf{mPGW}_{p}^{k}
)
.

For any
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
k\geq 0
, and pmm-spaces
X,Y,Z
, we have

(4.7)

\mathsf{mPGW}_{p}^{k}(X,Z)\leq\mathsf{mPGW}_{p}^{k}(X,Y)+\mathsf{mPGW}_{p}^{k}(Y,Z).

Proof.

To simplify notation, let

\bar{\delta}_{1}=\mathsf{mPGW}_{p}^{k}(X,Y)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bar{\delta}_{2}=\mathsf{mPGW}_{p}^{k}(Y,Z).

Suppose
\bar{\delta}_{1}+\bar{\delta}_{2}\geq 1
, then triangle inequality holds trivially, since

\mathsf{mPGW}_{p}^{k}(X,Z)\leq 1\leq\bar{\delta}_{1}+\bar{\delta}_{2}
.

For the rest of the proof, assume

\bar{\delta}_{1}+\bar{\delta}_{2}<1.

By definition, for any
\delta_{1}>\bar{\delta}_{1}
and
\delta_{2}>\bar{\delta}_{2}
, we have

\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta_{1},p}(X,Y)\leq k\delta_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta_{2},p}(Y,Z)\leq k\delta_{2}.

Fix arbitrary
\delta_{1}>\bar{\delta}_{1}
and
\delta_{2}>\bar{\delta}_{2}
with
\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}\leq 1
.
Suppose that
\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta_{1},p}(X,Y)
and
\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta_{2},p}(Y,Z)
are realized by
\tilde{\pi}_{1}
and
\tilde{\pi}_{2}
, respectively.
Notice that

(4.8)

(p^{X})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}_{1}\leq\mu_{X},\,(p^{Y})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}_{1}\leq\mu_{Y},\,\text{ and }|\tilde{\pi}_{1}|=1-\delta_{1}.

Intuitively,
\tilde{\pi}_{1}
corresponds to a transport plan moving a total mass
1-\delta_{1}
.
Similarly,

(4.9)

(p^{Y})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}_{2}\leq\mu_{Y},\,(p^{Z})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}_{2}\leq\mu_{Z},\,\text{ and }|\tilde{\pi}_{2}|=1-\delta_{2}.

For
i=1,2
, set

\tilde{\mu}_{Y,i}:=(p^{Y})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}_{i}.

Then for
i=1,2
,

|\tilde{\mu}_{Y,i}|=1-\delta_{i}

We also set

\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Y}:=\tilde{\mu}_{Y,1}\wedge\tilde{\mu}_{Y,2}

where
\mu\wedge\nu(S):=\inf\left\{\mu(S\cap B)+\nu(S\setminus B):B\text{ measurable}\right\}
. Intuitively,
\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Y}
registers the common mass transported by both
\tilde{\pi}_{1}
and
\tilde{\pi}_{2}
, hence the superscript
c
.

Set
\delta_{c}:=|\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Y}|
.
Since
\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Y},\tilde{\mu}_{Y,1},\tilde{\mu}_{Y,2}\leq\mu_{Y}
, we have

\mu_{Y}-\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Y}=(\mu_{Y}-\tilde{\mu}_{Y,1})\vee(\mu_{Y}-\tilde{\mu}_{Y,2})\leq(\mu_{Y}-\tilde{\mu}_{Y,1})+(\mu_{Y}-\tilde{\mu}_{Y,2}).

Here,
\mu\vee\nu(S):=\sup\left\{\mu(S\cap B)+\nu(S\setminus B):B\text{ measurable})\right\}

Therefore,
|\mu_{Y}-\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Y}|\leq\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}
and
|\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Y}|=\delta_{c}\geq 1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}
.

Using the notation of Theorem
3.8
, let
\left\{\tilde{\pi}_{1}(\cdot|y)\right\}_{y\in Y}
and
\left\{\tilde{\pi}_{2}(\cdot|y)\right\}_{y\in Y}
be the disintegration of
\tilde{\pi}_{1}
and
\tilde{\pi}_{2}
. Define

d\tilde{\pi}_{1}^{c}(x,y):=d\tilde{\pi}_{1}(x|y)d\tilde{\mu}_{Y}^{c}(y)\quad\text{ and }\quad d\tilde{\pi}_{2}^{c}(y,z):=d\tilde{\pi}_{2}(z|y)d\tilde{\mu}_{Y}^{c}(y),

so that for
i=1,2
,

\tilde{\pi}_{i}^{c}\leq\tilde{\pi}_{i},\,(p^{Y})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}_{1}^{c}=\tilde{\mu}_{Y}^{c},\,\text{ and }\left|\tilde{\pi}_{i}^{c}\right|=\delta_{c}.

We also set

(4.10)

\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{X}:=(p^{X})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}^{c}_{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Z}:=(p^{Z})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}^{c}_{2}.

In view of (
4.8
)–(
4.10
), we have

(4.11)

\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{X}\leq\mu_{X}\text{ and }\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Z}\leq\mu_{Z}.

We are ready to show
\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2},p}(X,Z)\leq k\delta_{1}+k\delta_{2}
.
Suppose
\inf_{\tilde{\pi}\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta_{c}}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Z})}\|d_{X}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\tilde{\pi})}
is achieved by
\tilde{\pi}^{\star}\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta_{c}}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Z})
.
Since
\frac{1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}}{\delta_{c}}\leq 1
, we have

\frac{1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}}{\delta_{c}}\cdot\tilde{\pi}^{\star}\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Z}).

By the definition of
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta_{c}}
in (
3.19
),

(4.12)

\{\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X\times Z):\,(p^{X})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}=\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{X},\,(p^{Z})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}=\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Z},\,\left|\tilde{\pi}\right|=\delta_{c}\}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta_{c}}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Z})

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2},p}(X,Z)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tilde{\pi}\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Z})}\|d_{X}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\tilde{\pi})}

\displaystyle\leq\|d_{X}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\frac{1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}}{\delta_{c}}\cdot\tilde{\pi}^{\star}\otimes\frac{1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}}{\delta_{c}}\cdot\tilde{\pi}^{\star})}

\displaystyle\leq\|d_{X}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}^{\star}\otimes\tilde{\pi}^{\star})}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tilde{\pi}\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta_{c}}(\mu_{X},\mu_{Z})}\|d_{X}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\tilde{\pi})}

(
4.12
)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\left\{\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X\times Z):\,(p^{X})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}=\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{X},\,(p^{Z})_{\#}\tilde{\pi}=\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Z},\,\left|\tilde{\pi}\right|=\delta_{c}\right\}}\|d_{X}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\tilde{\pi})}.

Notice that

\inf_{\left\{\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X\times Z):\,\tilde{\pi}_{X}=\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{X},\,\tilde{\pi}_{Z}=\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Z},\,\left|\tilde{\pi}\right|=\delta_{c}\right\}}\|d_{X}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\tilde{\pi})}=\delta_{c}\cdot\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X^{c},Z^{c}),

where
X^{c}:=(X,d_{X},\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{X}}{\delta_{c}})
and
Z^{c}:=(Z,d_{Z},\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Z}}{\delta_{c}})
are pmm-spaces. Consider an auxiliary pmm-space
Y^{c}:=(Y,d_{Y},\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Y}}{\delta_{c}})
.
By the triangle inequality for
\mathsf{GW}_{p}
, we have

\delta_{c}\cdot\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X^{c},Z^{c})\leq\delta_{c}\cdot\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X^{c},Y^{c})+\delta_{c}\cdot\mathsf{GW}_{p}(Y^{c},Z^{c}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2},p}(X,Z)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\left\{\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X\times Y):\,\tilde{\pi}_{X}=\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{X},\,\tilde{\pi}_{Y}=\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Y},\,\left|\tilde{\pi}\right|=\delta_{c}\right\}}\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\tilde{\pi})}

\displaystyle\quad+\inf_{\left\{\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(Y\times Z):\,\tilde{\pi}_{Y}=\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Y},\,\tilde{\pi}_{Z}=\tilde{\mu}^{c}_{Z},\,\left|\tilde{\pi}\right|=\delta_{c}\right\}}\|d_{Y}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\tilde{\pi})}

\displaystyle\leq\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}^{c}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\pi}^{c}_{1})}+\|d_{Y}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}^{c}_{2}\otimes\tilde{\pi}^{c}_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|d_{X}-d_{Y}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{1})}+\|d_{Y}-d_{Z}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\pi}_{2}\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq k\delta_{1}+k\delta_{2}.

Since
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
are arbitrary, we can conclude that

\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta,p}(X,Z)\leq k\delta,

for any
\delta\in(\bar{\delta}_{1}+\bar{\delta}_{2},1]
.
Since
\mathsf{mPGW}_{p}^{k}(X,Z)
is the infimum
\delta\in[0,1]
such that
\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta,p}(X,Z)\leq k\delta
, we have
\mathsf{mPGW}_{p}^{k}(X,Z)\leq\bar{\delta}_{1}+\bar{\delta}_{2}
.
∎

Corollary 4.6

(Metric properties for
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
)
.

The robust partial Gromov-Wasserstein distance
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
defines a metric on the space of isomorphism classes of compact, fully supported pmm-spaces.

Proof.

Non-degeneracy is already proven in Theorem
4.3
. Symmetry is clear since we force
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}
. It remains to show the triangle inequality.

By Proposition
3.13
, we have

\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta,p}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y),

with
1+\varepsilon=\frac{1}{1-\delta}
.
Thus,

\inf\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\geq 0:\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X,Y)\leq k\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\right\}=\inf\left\{0\leq\delta\leq 1:\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta,p}(X,Y)\leq k\delta\right\},

satisfies the triangle inequality.
∎

Incompleteness.

It was shown by example in
[
23
]
that the metric
\mathsf{GW}_{p}
(
1\leq p<\infty
) on the space of isomorphism classes of compact pmm-spaces is incomplete. We re-use the example and show that
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
(
1\leq p<\infty
) is also incomplete.

Theorem 4.7

(Incompleteness of
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
k>0
.
The space of isomorphism classes of compact pmm-spaces endowed with the metric
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
is incomplete.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Delta_{n}
denote the compact pmm-space consisting of an
n
-point set with pairwise distance one equipped with the uniform measure. We label
\Delta_{n}
by
\left\{x_{i}\right\}_{i\in[n]}
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We label
\Delta_{nm}
by
\left\{y_{jk}\right\}_{j\in[n],k\in[m]}
. It is clear that

\pi(x_{i},y_{jk}):=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{nm}\text{ for all }i,j\in[n]\text{ and }k\in[m]

defines an exact coupling of
\Delta_{n}
and
\Delta_{nm}
. In particular,
\pi\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon\geq 0
(recall that
\varepsilon
is a scalar in this section). Recall the distortion function
\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\cdot)
from Definition
2.11
.
By a straightforward calculation, we have

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(\Delta_{n},\Delta_{nm})\leq\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\pi)\leq n^{-1/p}.

As a consequence,

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(\Delta_{n},\Delta_{nm})\leq\frac{k^{-1}n^{-1/p}}{1+k^{-1}n^{-1/p}}.

Similarly,

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(\Delta_{m},\Delta_{nm})\leq\frac{k^{-1}m^{-1/p}}{1+k^{-1}m^{-1/p}}.

By the triangle inequality for
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
, we have

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(\Delta_{n},\Delta_{m})\leq{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(\Delta_{n},\Delta_{nm})+{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(\Delta_{nm},\Delta_{m})\leq\frac{k^{-1}n^{-1/p}}{1+k^{-1}n^{-1/p}}+\frac{k^{-1}m^{-1/p}}{1+k^{-1}m^{-1/p}}.

We see that
(\Delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence with respect to
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
. However, a limiting object for this sequence cannot be compact, since it
has to contain a countably infinite set of points with pairwise distance one.
∎

4.2
Topological Equivalence

The distance
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
defines a metric on the space of pmm-spaces, considered up to isomorphism, as does the Gromov-Wasserstein distance
\mathsf{GW}_{p}
. A natural question is whether they induce the same topology, and we establish that this is the case in the next result.

Theorem 4.8

(Equivalence of Topologies)
.

Consider the topologies induced by
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
and
\mathsf{GW}_{p}
on the space of isomorphism classes of compact pmm-spaces. If
p\in[1,\infty)
then the topologies are equivalent. However, the topology induced by
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\infty}^{k}
is strictly coarser than that induced by
\mathsf{GW}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Since the topologies under consideration are metric topologies, it suffices to check sequential convergence. That is, we will show that, for any sequence of pmm-spaces
(X_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
,

\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{n},X)\to 0\quad\mbox{implies}\quad{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(X_{n},X)\to 0

holds in general, and that the converse holds when
p<\infty
.

First assume that
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{n},X)\to 0
. For any
\varepsilon\geq 0
, if
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{n},X)\leq k\varepsilon
then
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X_{n},X)\leq k\varepsilon
(since
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}\leq\mathsf{GW}_{p}
, in general), so that

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(X_{n},X)=\inf\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}:{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X_{n},X)\leq k\varepsilon\right\}\leq\inf\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}:\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{n},X)\leq k\varepsilon\right\}\to 0.

To prove the converse, let
p<\infty
and suppose that
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(X_{n},X)\to 0
. Then there exists a sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n}
of non-negative real numbers such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{n},p}(X_{n},X)\leq k\varepsilon_{n}
. For each
n
, let
\pi_{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\mu_{X_{n}},\mu_{X})
be an optimal coupling (see Theorem
2.13
), so that

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon_{n},p}(X_{n},X)=\mathsf{GW}_{p}((X_{n})_{\pi_{n}},X_{\pi_{n}}),

using the notation and the equivalence (
3.1
) of Theorem
3.4
. Recall Definition
3.1
for the circumradius
\mathsf{rad}_{p}(X)
.
Applying the triangle inequality for
\mathsf{GW}_{p}
and the estimates (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{n},X)

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{n},(X_{n})_{\pi_{n}})+\mathsf{GW}_{p}((X_{n})_{\pi_{n}},X_{\pi_{n}})+\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{\pi_{n}},X)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 2^{1/q}\varepsilon_{n}^{1/p}\mathsf{rad}_{p}(X_{n})+k\varepsilon_{n}+2\cdot 2^{1/q}\varepsilon_{n}^{1/p}\mathsf{rad}_{p}(X),

so that
\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{n},X)
is bounded above by a sequence that tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

Observe that the proof strategy for the converse fails when
p=\infty
, because the estimates (
3.2
) are no longer effective for inducing convergence (see Remark
3.5
). We have already shown that the
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\infty}^{k}
topology is coarser than that of
\mathsf{GW}_{\infty}
. Strictness is demonstrated by Example
4.9
below.
∎

Example 4.9
.

We will now construct a sequence
X_{n}
of pmm-spaces which converge to a pmm-space
X
in the
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\infty}^{k}
topology, but not in the
\mathsf{GW}_{\infty}
topology. This completes the proof of the claim that the former topology is strictly coarser than that of the latter.

Let
X
denote the unique one-point pmm-space, whose point will be denoted
\star
. For each
n=2,3,\ldots
, let
X_{n}=(\{x,y\},d_{n},\mu_{n})
, where
d_{n}(x,y)\equiv 1
(independent of
n
) and
\mu_{n}
is defined by
\mu_{n}(x)=1-\frac{1}{n}
and
\mu_{n}(y)=\frac{1}{n}
. Then
\mathsf{GW}_{\infty}(X_{n},X)=1
for all
n
, so that
X_{n}\not\to X
in the
\mathsf{GW}_{\infty}
topology. On the other hand, we claim that

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\infty}^{k}(X_{n},X)\leq\frac{1}{n},

which will complete the proof. Indeed, setting
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{n-1}
, we have

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,\infty}(X_{n},X)=0\leq k\varepsilon.

To see the equality, consider the measure
\pi
on
X\times X_{n}
defined by
\pi(\star,x)=1
,
\pi(\star,y)=0
. This measure belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{X},\mu_{n})
: obviously,
\pi_{X}=\mu_{X}
, while

\pi_{Y}(x)=1=\left(1+\frac{1}{n-1}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)=(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{n}(x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\pi_{Y}(y)=0\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{Y}(y).

Moreover,
\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(\pi)=0
. This implies that

{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\infty}^{k}(X_{n},X)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}=\frac{\frac{1}{n-1}}{1+\frac{1}{n-1}}=\frac{1}{n}.

4.3
Robustness

In this section, we investigate the robustness properties of our robust partial GW distance. Our robustness result factors through a more general result on the behavior of arbitrary parametric families of kernels, after being fed through a
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
-type construction. We state this general result after proving a technical lemma.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},<)
be a partially ordered set. Suppose there is a weakly decreasing function
f:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
and a weakly increasing function
g:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
with
f(a)=g(b)
for some
a,b\in\mathcal{E}
such that
a\wedge b,a\vee b\in\mathcal{E}
, i.e., both the meet and join of
a
and
b
lie in the poset
\mathcal{E}
. Let
h:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
be a strictly increasing function, define the set
\mathcal{S}=\{h(\varepsilon)\in\mathcal{E}:f(\varepsilon)\leq g(\varepsilon)\}
, and suppose
s=\inf\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{S}
. Then,
s\leq a\vee b
. Furthermore, if
g
is strictly increasing, then
a\wedge b\leq s
.

Proof.

Consider the case where
a\leq b
. In this case, we have
f(b)\leq f(a)=g(b)
. Hence,
b\in\mathcal{S}
, so
s\leq b=a\vee b
. Next, if
g
is strictly increasing, then assuming
s<a
leads to a contradiction:

g(s)<g(a)\leq g(b)=f(a)\leq f(s)\leq g(s).

Thus, we have
s\geq a=a\wedge b
.

Now, consider the case where
b<a
. Here we have
f(a)=g(b)\leq g(a)
, so
a\in\mathcal{S}
, and
s\leq a=a\vee b
. As in the previous case, for
g
strictly increasing, if we assume
s<b
, then we reach a contradiction:

g(s)<g(b)=f(a)\leq f(b)\leq f(s)\leq g(s),

so
s\geq b=a\wedge b
. This proves the lemma.
∎

We now give our main result for this subsection.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a set.
Suppose
d_{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{W}\to\mathbb{R}
is a family of symmetric functions parametrized by
\varepsilon\geq 0
such that
\varepsilon\mapsto d_{\varepsilon}(x,y)
is right continuous and weakly decreasing for any fixed pair
x,y\in\mathcal{W}
. Define the family of functions
d^{k}(x,y)=\inf\{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}:d_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\leq k\varepsilon\}
, for
k>0
. Suppose there exists some
\varepsilon>0
such that
d_{\varepsilon}(y,\tilde{y})=0
. If
d^{k}
satisfies the triangle inequality, then

\left|d^{k}(x,y)-d^{k}(x,\tilde{y})\right|\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.10
, we have
d^{k}(y,\tilde{y})\leq\varepsilon
. By the triangle inequality, we have

d^{k}(x,y)\leq d^{k}(x,\tilde{y})+d^{k}(\tilde{y},y)\leq d^{k}(x,\tilde{y})+\varepsilon,

and

d^{k}(x,\tilde{y})\leq d^{k}(x,y)+d^{k}(y,\tilde{y})\leq d^{k}(x,y)+\varepsilon

as desired.
∎

Corollary 4.12

(Robustness of
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
)
.

Let
k>0
,
p\in[1,\infty]
. Let
X,Y,\tilde{Y}
be compact pmm-spaces. Suppose that
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(Y,\tilde{Y})=0
for some
\varepsilon>0
. Then

\left|{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(X,Y)-{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(X,\tilde{Y})\right|\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Recall from the beginning of Section
4
(Page
4
) that
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
with
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}
.
Thanks to Theorem
2.16
,
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}
satisfies the assumptions on the family
d_{\varepsilon}
from Theorem
4.11
. The result follows from Theorem
4.11
.
∎

We restate Corollary
4.12
in the language of Huber’s contamination model popularized in robust statistics
[
7
]
. Let
\delta>0
and
L\geq 1
.
Given a pmm-space
X=(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
, we call
X^{c}=(X,d_{X},\mu_{X}^{c})
a
(\delta,L)
-contamination of
X
if

(4.13)

\mu_{X}^{c}=(1-\delta)\mu_{X}+\delta\mu^{c}

for some outlier distribution
\mu^{c}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
satisfying

(4.14)

\mu^{c}\leq L\cdot\mu_{X}.

We see from (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) that if
X^{c}
is a
(\delta,L)
-contamination of
X
, then

(4.15)

\left\|\frac{d\mu_{X}^{c}}{d\mu_{X}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu_{X})}\leq(1-\delta)+\delta L.

Corollary 4.13

(Robustness of
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
for contaminated models)
.

Let
k>0
, let
\varepsilon,\delta,L>0
satisfy

(4.16)

\delta(L-1)\leq\varepsilon,

and let
X_{0}
and
X
be compact pmm-spaces.
If
X^{c}
is a
(\delta,L)
-contamination of
X
then, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
,

(4.17)

\left|{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(X_{0},X)-{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}(X_{0},X^{c})\right|\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

In view of (
4.15
) and (
4.16
), we have
\|\frac{d\mu_{X}^{c}}{d\mu_{X}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu_{X})}\leq 1+\varepsilon
. Using the same notation as in Theorem
3.4
, we have
X^{c}\sim_{\varepsilon}X
and
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{\varepsilon,p}(X^{c},X)=0
, so that (
4.17
) follows from Corollary
4.12
.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

The derived bound (
4.17
) suggests that the Gromov-Wasserstein metric
{\mathsf{PGW}}_{p}^{k}
provides provable robustness against contamination in the Huber model, hence the prefix “robust” in the name. Our result is comparable to
[
27
, Theorem 3.1]
, where the control (
4.16
) on
(\delta,L)
-contamination resembles Assumption (A1) therein. We also note that our notion of robustness differs from that in
[
19
]
(see Section
3.3.3
), and our counterpart for
[
19
, Theorem 2.3]
is Theorem
3.4
.

It is worth pointing out that the classical Gromov-Wasserstein distance does not enjoy a similar robustness property. Precisely, we have the following result.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
\varepsilon,\delta>0
and
L>1
such that
\delta(L-1)\leq\varepsilon
, and let
p\in[1,\infty]
. For any
C>0
, there exist compact pmm-spaces
X_{0}
and
X
and a
(\delta,L)
-contamination
X^{c}
of
X
such that

|\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{0},X)-\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{0},X^{c})|>C.

Proof.

Let us assume for the sake of simplicity that
L\geq 2
and
\delta<1
(the remaining cases can be handled by a similar, but more involved, construction). Let
X_{0}
be the one-point pmm space and let
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
be a two-point pmm space with
X=\{x,y\}
,
\mu_{X}
uniform, and
d_{X}(x,y)\coloneqq d
, where the value of
d
is to be determined. Let
\mu^{c}
be a Dirac measure on
x
(i.e.,
\mu^{c}(y)=0
)—since we assumed
L\geq 2
,
\mu^{c}
satisfies (
4.14
). Then the measure
\mu_{X}^{c}
on
X^{c}
is as defined in (
4.13
); explicitly,

\mu_{X}^{c}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(1+\delta),\qquad\mu_{X}^{c}(y)=\frac{1}{2}(1-\delta).

By
[
21
, Theorem 5.1]
, we have that

\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{0},X)=\mathrm{diam}_{p}(X)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{0},X^{c})=\mathrm{diam}_{p}(X^{c}),

where the
p
-diameter
of
X
is given by

\mathrm{diam}_{p}(X)=\|d_{X}\|_{L^{p}(\mu_{X}\otimes\mu_{X})}.

A simple computation shows that the
p
-diameters are given by

\mathrm{diam}_{p}(X)=\frac{d}{2^{1/p}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{diam}_{p}(X^{c})=\frac{d}{2^{1/p}}(1-\delta^{2})^{1/p},

so that

|\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{0},X)-\mathsf{GW}_{p}(X_{0},X^{c})|=\frac{d}{2^{1/p}}\left(1-(1-\delta^{2})^{1/p}\right).

Since
1-(1-\delta^{2})^{1/p}
is strictly positive, this quantity can be made arbitrarily large by an appropriate choice of
d
.
∎

4.4
Numerical Implementation and Examples

While the focus of this article is on theoretical properties of the robust partial GW distances introduced herein, we provide in this subsection a basic numerical implementation of the
\mathsf{mPGW_{2}^{k}}
metric and some proof-of-concept examples for the sake of providing intuitive illustrations of its properties. More serious explorations of algorithmic aspects of the distance and its potential applications will be the subject of follow-up work.

Implementation.

We implemented the
\mathsf{mPGW}_{2}^{k}
version of our metric (which is equivalent to
\mathsf{PGW}_{2}^{k}
) by searching over
\delta
values to find a zero of the function
f_{k}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

f_{k}(\delta)=\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta,2}(X,Y)-k\delta.

The function
f_{k}
is approximated via an implementation of the Chapel et al. distance
\mathsf{mPGW}_{1-\delta,2}
provided in the
Python Optimal Transport
software package
[
15
]
. This implementation uses conjugate gradient descent to approximate the solution of the optimization problem (
3.18
). Assuming the spaces being compared have size
\leq n
, each gradient step has computational cost bounded by
O(n^{3}\log(n))

[
25
]
, leading to an overall complexity of
O(m\cdot n^{3}\log(n))
to approximate
f_{k}
, where
m
is the predetermined max number of iterations allowed in the gradient descent. We find a zero of
f_{k}
via a simple bisection method, which finds a zero up to a fixed tolerance
t>0
in
\log_{2}(1/t)
iterations. The overall complexity of approximating
\mathsf{PGW}_{2}^{k}(X,Y)
is therefore
O(m\cdot n^{3}\log(n)\log_{2}(1/t))
.

\mathsf{mPGW}_{2}^{k}

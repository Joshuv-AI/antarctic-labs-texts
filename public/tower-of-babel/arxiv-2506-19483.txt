Commonsense Generation and Evaluation for Dialogue Systems using Large Language Models
Marcos Estecha-Garitagoitia and Chen Zhang and Mario Rodríguez-Cantelar and Luis Fernando D’Haro

Abstract
This paper provides preliminary results on exploring the task of performing turn-level data augmentation for dialogue system based on different types of commonsense relationships, and the automatic evaluation of the generated synthetic turns. The proposed methodology takes advantage of the extended knowledge and zero-shot capabilities of pretrained Large Language Models (LLMs) to follow instructions, understand contextual information, and their commonsense reasoning capabilities. The approach draws inspiration from methodologies like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), applied more explicitly to the task of prompt-based generation for dialogue-based data augmentation conditioned on commonsense attributes, and the automatic evaluation of the generated dialogues. To assess the effectiveness of the proposed approach, first we extracted 200 randomly selected partial dialogues, from 5 different well-known dialogue datasets, and generate alternative responses conditioned on different event commonsense attributes. This novel dataset allows us to measure the proficiency of LLMs in generating contextually relevant commonsense knowledge, particularly up to 12 different specific ATOMIC hwang-2021 database relations. Secondly, we propose an evaluation framework to automatically detect the quality of the generated dataset inspired by the ACCENT sarik-2023 metric, which offers a nuanced approach to assess event commonsense. However, our method does not follow ACCENT’s complex event-relation tuple extraction process. Instead, we propose an instruction-based prompt for each commonsense attribute and use state-of-the-art LLMs to automatically detect the original attributes used when creating each augmented turn in the previous step. Preliminary results suggest that our approach effectively harnesses LLMs capabilities for commonsense reasoning and evaluation in dialogue systems.



1
Introduction

The challenge of instilling commonsense intelligence in AI systems, especially in Natural Language Processing (NLP), represents a significant yet challenging goal. Human-like reasoning about everyday situations and events remains a complex task for AI systems
richardson-2023
. In this context, Large Language Models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT
openai-2022
and GPT-4
openai-2023
, have emerged as pivotal enablers in this domain
li-2022
. Their robust zero-shot and few-shot learning capabilities have demonstrated substantial improvements across various NLP tasks
kocon-2023
. Due to their general-purpose design, they are easy to adapt to downstream tasks, endowing them with the necessary background knowledge of the physical and social world. Beyond the conventional approach of model fine-tuning where all model parameters are adjusted
howard-ruder-2018
, in-context learning methods have been shown to achieve high performance in modulating LLM’s behavior through prompts
liu-yuan-2021
.

AI’s commonsense reasoning has evolved from the structured logic
speer-2017

hwang-2021
to more context-rich approaches
ghosal-cider-2021

ghosal-cicero-2022
. Former approaches primarily structure commonsense knowledge in logic-based formalisms, while the latter approaches emphasize in natural language-based inferences for dialogue reasoning. Aproaches such as COMET
bosselut-comet-2019
focuses on generating novel commonsense knowledge by adapting pretrained neural language models to create new knowledge tuples. Likewise, our preliminary research pivots around the concept of redefining commonsense knowledge representation by developing a dataset using a prompt-based approach. Moving away from conventional knowledge graph and logical expression methodologies, we propose to harness the expansive potential of LLMs. Using the relational framework of the ATOMIC
hwang-2021
dataset as a grounding, our goal is to generate new dialog turns in natural language. These turns are designed to be instilled with commonsense knowledge that is contextually relevant and nuanced, reflecting commonsense relational insights. This approach not only promises a more human-like interaction in AI-driven dialogues, but also aligns with the natural progression of AI towards a more intuitive understanding of human communication
choi-2022
.

Based on the above, the motivation is driven by the preliminary objective of exploring the feasibility of using these language systems to evaluate the common sense obtained from automatic generation systems.
The effectiveness of our approach is assessed using a framework inspired by ACCENT
sarik-2023
. Unlike ACCENT’s approach to evaluate commonsense, our method concentrates on the direct generation and evaluation of commonsense content by LLMs. For that purpose, we harness from ATOMIC’s relational framework.

The main contributions of this paper are:

•

Design and generate an event commonsense expanded dataset using LLMs specifically targeting open-domain dialogues.

•

Propose an evaluation methodology to automatically assess the quality of the generated expanded dataset by considering each commonsense attribute.

•

Provide detailed information on the prompt templates used to generate and evaluate the commonsense synthetic dataset.

2
Background

In recent years, the goal of making machines manifest commonsense understanding and capabilities like humans has been an active field for conversational AI. Although early research looked at how machines understand basic facts and ideas
mccarthy-hayes-1981

lecun-2021

lenat-1995
and
liu-singh-2004
, a new approach has emerged by means of LLMs. Building upon the available Commonsense Knowledge Bases (CSKB), LLMs have enabled to foster less abstract forms of representing social commonsense knowledge. ConceptNet
speer-2017
and ATOMIC
hwang-2021
have emerged as widely used resources for dataset curation, establishing a de facto standard.

Our exploratory research takes ATOMIC as foundation and examines two intersectional areas in conversational AI: the synthesis of commonsense datasets via prompt-based processing, and the automatic quality assessment of the generated commonsense content. This dual focus addresses the critical need for not only generating contextually rich commonsense knowledge, but also ensuring its alignment with the intended parameters of conversational AI systems. Our work aims to refine and advance these mutually linked aspects, thus contributing to the development of more nuanced and reliable conversational agents.

\runinhead

Commonsense Dataset Synthesis
Various works has already addressed the issue of curating commonsense reasoning from foundational commonsense symbolic datasets. Ghosal
ghosal-cider-2021
presented CIDER, a dataset marked by commonsense inferences in dialogues. This work was further developed with CICERO
ghosal-cicero-2022
in 2022. CICERO expanded the event-based reasoning annotations for better commonsense inference in dialogue systems. Different researchers have delved into particular kinds of commonsense such as exploring temporal aspects
qin-2021
or examining ethical dimensions
ziems-2022

hyunwoo-2022

hao-2022
.

Beyond the existing methodologies based on using and filtering of pre-existed datasets, SODA
hyunwoo-soda-2022
opened a new stream of research. Its approach aims at distilling new generalist dialogues from the implicit commonsense knowledge that is already embedded in LLMs. This synthesis of dialogues seeks to overcome the existing limitations in generation, evaluation and understanding due to the dependence on these small datasets. Along these lines, SYNDICOM
richardson-syndicom-2023
further pushes these ideas proposing a methodology based on the incorporation of valid and invalid answers with feedback to dialogue contexts. Its methodology aims to improve the predictive capacity of the system’s dialogues. These responses are obtained from templates created with the logical formalisms contained in ATOMIC and chaining these inferences together to form multiple dialogue turns.

Different to previous research, we propose a synthesis methodology at turn-level. Relying on the use of several open-domain databases, we expand the implicit knowledge of the following turn in a contextualized dialogue by considering up to 12 different commonsense dimensions. The advantage is that we are able to provide new alternative turn responses that focuses on a particular commonsense aspect.

\runinhead

Commonsense Evaluation with LLMs
Thanks to the wide general-purpose capabilities that LLMs provide, their use have been spread to all research layers. The strategies followed to evaluate vary in both procedure and application. This paper focuses on exploring their performance as zero-shot classification systems, where no task-specific supervised training is provided to the model. Our proposal builds on a number of recent works
ronak-203

sun-2023

zhen-qin-2023

ma-2023
that directly use LLMs as classifiers in a multi-stage classification process, where the main focus is on prompt-engineering to perform the classification task. Although there is much effort towards open-sourcing competitive LLMs, OpenAI’s ChatGPT and GPT-4 remain the most successful LLMs to date
wang-2023
, and therefore we used them in our experiments.

Similar to our approach, SODA
hyunwoo-soda-2022
uses GPT-3.5 to automatically validate the type of commonsense found in conversations that had previously been generated from one of ATOMIC’s commonsense attributes. ACCENT
sarik-2023
explicitly focuses on computing continuous commonsense alignment scores by relying on the use of an event extraction model and a generative commonsense model. Taking these works as a starting point, our idea follows these same lines of commonsense contradiction detection, but extending the turn-level commonsense dimensions to develop fine-grained detection. Finally, we also include the usage of ACCENT as a means to compare the results obtained with LLMs.

3
Methods

Inspired by recent prompt-based commonsense synthesis methods, we describe a method to expand and evaluate commonsense at turn-level for dialogue systems.

3.1
Commonsense Knowledge Graph

Our idea is to expand the turns of given dialogues along certain given commonsense knowledge dimensions. For this research, we selected ATOMIC as the reference CSKB. It includes knowledge about physical entities, events-centered and social interactions. Its components focused on events and social interactions correspond to 84.4% over the entire CSKB and provide information on how human events/actions interact with other events/actions. We considered these relationships as a reference for the definition of our dimensions that we want to expand in each turn. Table

3.2
Source and Test Data

To build our experimental dataset we use dyadic dialogues from five datasets: DailyDialog
li-etal-2017-dailydialog
, Topical-Chat
gopalakrishnan2019topical
, EmpatheticDialogues
rashkin-etal-2019-towards
, Persona-Chat
zhang-etal-2018-personalizing
;
dinan2019second
and Wizard Of Wikipedia
dinan2019wizard
.

For each dataset, 40 random dialogues with 5 to 10 turns were selected. These dialogues were expanded using the GPT-3.5 model; for the GPT-4 model, only 20 of the 40 selected dialogues were used. The decision to choose a lower number of dialogs to be expanded with GPT-4 is primarily for economic reasons, given the preliminary nature of this paper. Moreover, it is also assumed that, in general, GPT-4 performs better than GPT-3. Under these assumptions, we decided to halve the number of dialogs expanded with GPT-4. After this selection process, 1,721 turns were augmented a total of 12 times each turn, once for each dimension in Table
1
.

The augmented responses obtained by the LLM were on average 35% longer than the original sentence, showing that the new responses incorporate more information than the original response (probably due to the incorporation of commonsense information, but also a typical characteristic of the LLMs in spite of being requested to be concise in the prompt instruction).

3.3
Methods and Prompt Design

Recent work has shown that zero-shot listwise prompt-based give better results for classifying several text fragments simultaneously
ma-2023

zhen-qin-2023
. On this basis, we pose that, despite the fact that these statements have been claimed to classify natural language text, similar results can be obtained for data synthesis by following an equivalent approach. In our case, the synthesis of the responses at turn-level by inducing given definitions of each knowledge commonsense dimension.

Therefore, our problem is stated: given a set of commonsense attribute definitions
R=\{r_{1},...,r_{M}\}
, a dialogue context
D=\{t_{i-n},...,t_{i-1},t_{i}\}
and the task description
p
, the job of the language model is to return a list of responses
X=\{x_{1},...,x_{M}\}
at turn
t_{i+1}
following each attribute definition. Each response
x_{j}
aligns with the definition of the corresponding commonsense attribute
r_{j}
for
j=1,2,...,M
.

For the prompt used in the evaluation, we have taken as a reference the prompts proposed in RankGPT
sun-2023
and adapted it to the commonsense task; specifically, the description stated in the prompt is different. The exact input prompts for generation and evaluation are shown in Figure

The expansion process consists of generating new turns of dialogues following the commonsense given by the definitions of ATOMIC relations.

Although this paper explores zero-shot generation, we have also performed one-shot tests with examples generated automatically for each turn using COMET. To do so, we concatenate a specific example for each definition, replacing the example label shown in Table
1
. The rationale behind this is to evaluate the performance when the system is induced with knowledge that could potentially be integrated versus self-generated knowledge.

On the other hand, the evaluation process is defined as a listwise prompt-based classification process. Taking as ground-truths each relation that identifies the expansion of each turn, the GPT models are proposed to sort the definitions of the relations in the form of an ordered list. Thus, the task is to identify the definitions that best fit the expanded response. Once ranked, recommender system metrics are taken to evaluate the performance of the system for the evaluation task.

5
Conclusions and Future Work

In this study, we introduce a listwise zero-shot generation and reranking approach powered by open-source large language models. Our initial experiments show the viability of using LLMs for automatic generation and evaluation of commonsense dialogue turns. Both GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 generations were found very good (being GPT-3.5 a suitable option in case of considering costs), while for evaluation GPT-4 performed the best.
In future work, we plan to increase the number of dialogues and perform manual annotations to better detect which commonsense attributes are better reflected in the expanded turns. Finally, we also plan to fine-tune a small generative model that can be used to automatically extract the attributes in chitchat dialogues.

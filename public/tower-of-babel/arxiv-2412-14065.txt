Quasi-two-dimensional spin helix and magnon-induced singularity in twisted bilayer graphene
Yung-Yeh Chang, Kazuma Saito, Chen-Hsuan Hsu

Abstract
Twisted bilayer graphene exhibits prominent correlated phenomena in two distinct regimes: a Kondo lattice near the magic angle, resembling heavy fermion systems, and a triangular correlated domain wall network under interlayer bias, akin to sliding Luttinger liquids previously introduced for cuprates. Combining these characteristics, here we investigate a system where interacting electrons in the domain wall network couple to localized spins. Owing to inter-domain-wall correlations, a quasi-two-dimensional spin helix phase within the localized spins emerges as a result of spatial phase coherence across parallel domain walls. Within the spin helix phase, magnons can induce a singularity, reflected in the scaling exponents of various correlation functions, accessible through electrical means and by adjusting the twist angle. We predict observable features in magnetic resonance and anisotropic paramagnetic spin susceptibility for the spin helix and the magnon-induced singularity, serving as experimental indicators of the interplay between the Kondo lattice and sliding Luttinger liquids. Integrating critical aspects of Luttinger liquid physics, magnetism, and Kondo physics in twisted bilayer graphene, our findings offer insights into similar correlated phenomena across a broad range of twisted van der Waals structures.

Spin helix formation and magnon spectrum.

We now demonstrate that the localized moments tend to form a helical pattern with spatial period
\pi/Q_{F}
along the domain walls. Given the inherent
C_{3}
rotational symmetry of our model, there is no preferred direction for the formation of the quasi-2D spin helix, making it equally probable to develop in any of the three arrays. Formally, we take the ansatz incorporating an offset phase
\vartheta_{m}
depending on the domain wall index,

\displaystyle\langle\bm{S}_{m}(r)\rangle=m_{2Q_{F}}(T)SN_{\perp}/a

\displaystyle\hskip 26.0pt\times\left[\hat{x}\cos\left(2Q_{F}r+\vartheta_{m}\right)+\hat{y}\sin\left(2Q_{F}r+\vartheta_{m}\right)\right]\;,

(6)

with the order parameter

satisfying
m_{2Q_{F}}(0)=1
and
m_{2Q_{F}}(T_{\rm hx})=0
, and the ordering temperature
T_{\rm hx}

Before proceeding, we discuss the spatial rotational symmetry breaking in the ansatz. Namely, simultaneously establishing a helix in all three arrays would require the helix period
\pi/Q_{F}
to be commensurate with

[see Fig.
1

However, this commensurate condition necessitates precise tuning of the chemical potential. Under typical conditions, a quasi-2D spin helix will form within a single array as shown in Fig.
1

C_{3}

For

Next, we obtain the magnetic energy gain from Eqs. (

Specifically, owing to the nonlocal contributions

Magnon-induced singularity.

In the spin helix phase, the magnons can lead to spin flips of electrons, which is known to influence electrical transport through backscattering
[

14

80

To this end, we express Eq. (

\displaystyle H_{\rm em}=g_{{\rm em}}\int\frac{dr}{2\pi}\sum_{m}\left[\partial_{r}\phi_{sS,m}(r)\right]\Pi_{m}(r)\;,

In the bosonic language, magnons couple to the spin-symmetric boson (

We present the corresponding excitation spectrum in Fig.

Realization and transport features.

Having demonstrated the general picture of the spin helix formation, we now discuss two scenarios for its realizations. The first one is TBG fabricated using
13
C isotopes, in which conduction electrons and nuclear spins couple through the hyperfine interaction.
While detailed investigations of hyperfine coupling, similar to studies on semiconductors
[
12
]
, remain absent for moiré systems, it is noteworthy that an experimental hyperfine coupling strength of
O(100~\mu

[
11
]
, exceeding the theoretical value
[
62
]
.
We estimate
T_{\rm hx}=O(10~\mathrm{mK})
for
J_{K}=O(\mu\rm{eV})
and typical parameters for the electron subsystem (i.e., those adopted for Fig. S3 in SM
[
72
]
).
The second scenario involves magnetic adatoms deposited on graphene layers, interacting with domain wall modes through exchange coupling. This has some parallels with previous studies on monolayer graphene
[

,

,
88
,

,
10

meV has been observed in samples with fluorine adatoms

In this scenario, the exchange coupling generally exceeds the hyperfine coupling considered in the first scenario, and it can be further enhanced due to the increased electron density within the domain walls. We find that

can reach up to

for

While the mesoscopic length scales discussed here should enable spin-sensitive scanning probes

to image helix formation, the presence of metallic gates may render this approach inapplicable.
We therefore search for additional observable features.
Since the spin helix can generate a spatially rotating magnetic field, which gaps out half of the electron modes, we expect the quantized conductance, for instance in setups in Ref.

We demonstrate the formation of a spatially phase-coherent planar spin helix and a magnon-induced singularity in TBG networks with observable features.

In this section, we describe the Kondo-type interaction in the domain wall network, before discussing the resulting RKKY interaction. In the network, the conduction electron spins couple to the localized moments through

\displaystyle H_{{\rm K}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,m}\sum_{\mu,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\frac{J_{K}^{\mu}}{N_{\perp}}\left[\psi^{\dagger}_{\sigma,m}(r_{k})\sigma^{\mu}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\psi_{\sigma^{\prime},m}(r_{k})\right]S^{\mu}_{m}(r_{k})\;,

(S12)

with the effective coupling strength
J_{K}^{\mu}
with
\mu\in\{x,y,z\}
, as defined in Eq. (4) in the main text. Distinct from a typical Kondo lattice problem, here we have an electron subsystem described by SLL, which allows us to incorporate the correlation between the electrons as well.
Moreover, the backscattering properties of the electrons traversing the domain walls also affect the transverse components
J_{K}^{x,y}
of the effective Kondo-type coupling.
Namely, the counter-propagating domain-wall modes in Eq. (
S2
) are constituted by electrons at different valleys, labeled as
\bm{K}
and
\bm{K}^{\prime}\equiv-\bm{K}
, in the momentum space of the overall 2D system, as shown in Fig.
S2

To quantify the resulting backscattering strength, we calculate the transition amplitude for two counter-propagating domain wall modes on the same domain wall in the presence of a scattering potential through a second-order process. The backscattering process involves two counter-propagating modes on a given domain wall illustrated in Fig.
S2
(b), each of which are projected out of the states characterized by the 2D wavevectors
\bm{k}=(k_{x},k_{y})
measured from
\Gamma
and
\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}\equiv-\bm{k}
, with
k_{x},k_{y}>0
, respectively.
To be precise, we denote the domain wall mode associated with the
\bm{K}

\left|\bm{k},m\right\rangle
and the mode residing in the
\bm{K}^{\prime}
valley as
\left|\bm{k}^{\prime},m\right\rangle
. Both modes share the same energy
E_{\bm{k}}
.

To proceed, we express the second-order transition amplitude under the influence of the potential as

\displaystyle c_{\left|\bm{k},m\right\rangle\to\left|\bm{k}^{\prime\prime},m\right\rangle}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{-i}{\hbar}\right)^{2}\int dt_{1}dt_{2}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime},m^{\prime}}e^{i\omega_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}t_{1}}V_{\bm{k}m,\bm{k}^{\prime}m^{\prime}}e^{i\omega_{\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}}t_{2}}V_{\bm{k}^{\prime}m^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}m}

\displaystyle\sim\int dE_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rho(E_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})\frac{V_{\bm{k}m,\bm{k}^{\prime}m^{\prime}}V_{\bm{k}^{\prime}m^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}m}}{\left(E_{\bm{k}}-E_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\right)\left(E_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}-E_{\bm{k}}\right)}\;,

(S13)

where
\rho(E_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})
denotes the density of states and

\displaystyle V_{\bm{k}m,\bm{k}^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\equiv\left\langle\bm{k},m\right|\hat{V}\left|\bm{k}^{\prime},m^{\prime}\right\rangle\quad\textrm{and}\quad\omega_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\equiv\frac{E_{\bm{k}}-E_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}{\hbar}.

(S14)

The transition amplitude
c_{\left|\bm{k},m\right\rangle\to\left|\bm{k}^{\prime\prime},m\right\rangle}^{(2)}

S13
) is inversely dependent on the energy difference of the virtual scattering processes.
In general, the scattering potential in this higher-order process can arise from the Kondo-type coupling described in Eq. (
S12
), as well as its combination with electron-electron interactions
[
9
]
. To make a conservative estimate, we only consider the smaller contribution from the exchange coupling.
Similarly, we restrict our analysis to the leading virtual processes involving scattering into the intermediate states within the same band as that of
\left|\bm{k},m\right\rangle
and
\left|\bm{k}^{\prime\prime},m\right\rangle
, while neglecting the subdominant transitions into the remote bands.

As a result, the range of the energy integral in Eq. (
S13
) is constrained by the bandwidth
\Delta_{a}
. The order of magnitude of the transition amplitude can be estimated by applying the following approximations:
\int dE_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sim\Delta_{a}
,
\rho(E_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})\sim 1/\Delta_{a}
,
V_{\bm{k}m,\bm{k}^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\sim V_{0}
the energy scale of the exchange coupling, and
|E_{\bm{k}}-E_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\lesssim\Delta_{a}
, yielding the following result:

\displaystyle c_{\left|\bm{k},m\right\rangle\to\left|\bm{k}^{\prime\prime},m\right\rangle}^{(2)}\sim\frac{V_{0}^{2}}{\Delta_{a}^{2}}\;.

(S15)

This indicates that the backscattering strength is suppressed by an order of
V_{0}^{2}/\Delta_{a}^{2}
for domain wall modes, which will also affect the transverse components of Kondo-type coupling when comparing to graphene or carbon nanotube systems.
Using
\Delta_{a}\approx 2\sim 10\;\mathrm{meV}
and Eq. (
S15
), we estimate the reduced coupling strengths
J_{K}^{x,y}=O(\mathrm{meV})
for typical adatoms, based on the experimental value of
5\;\mathrm{meV}
for fluorine adatoms
[
10
]
, and
J_{K}^{x,y}=O(\mu\mathrm{eV})
for
13
C, based on the experimental value of
100\;\mu\mathrm{eV}
in Ref.
[
11
]
.
We also remark on the effect of the moiré potential on the effective hyperfine coupling.
While such investigation is absent, we expect that the misalignment between graphene layers can influence the overlap between
p
-orbital electron wave functions and carbon nuclei at the opposite layers, thereby influencing the Fermi contact and dipolar hyperfine couplings
[
12
]
.

Having described the Kondo-type interaction, we derive the RKKY interaction within the domain wall network by focusing on the weak-
J_{K}
regime.
We remark that while the RKKY interaction is a cornerstone in studies of hybrid systems combining conduction electrons with localized spins, its analysis in SLL settings has been insufficient. To fill this gap, we extend the method described in Refs.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{{\rm dw}}N}\sum_{q,q_{\perp}}\sum_{\mu,\nu}S_{q,q_{\perp}}^{\nu}\frac{J_{q_{\perp}}^{\mu\nu}(q)}{N_{\perp}^{2}}S_{-q,-q_{\perp}}^{\mu}\;,

S.III
Formation of quasi-2D spatially phase-coherent spin helix

In this section, we demonstrate that the RKKY interaction results in formation of a quasi-2D spin helix. To this end, we perform the spin wave analysis at low temperatures.
Motivated by the largest absolute magnitude of the RKKY coupling at the inter-branch scattering momentum,
2Q_{F}
, we consider the following ansatz for the localized spins on a given domain wall,

\displaystyle\langle\bm{S}_{m}(r_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle=m_{2Q_{F}}SN_{\perp}\left[\hat{x}\cos\left(2Q_{F}r_{k}+\vartheta_{m}\right)+\hat{y}\sin\left(2Q_{F}r_{k}+\vartheta_{m}\right)\right]\;,

(S52)

with its period of oscillation given by
\pi/Q_{F}
.

Without loss of generality, we choose the helical (spin quantization) axis (
z
direction) to be parallel to the domain wall, and localized spins are lying on the
xy
plane, perpendicular to the helical axis. For convenience, we rotate the original spatial coordinate (
\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{z}
) to a new basis (
\hat{e}_{k,m}^{1},\hat{e}_{k,m}^{2},\hat{e}_{k,m}^{3}
),

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\hat{e}_{k,m}^{1}\\
\hat{e}_{k,m}^{2}\\
\hat{e}_{k,m}^{3}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\left(2Q_{F}r_{k}+\vartheta_{m}\right)&\sin\left(2Q_{F}r_{k}+\vartheta_{m}\right)&0\\
-\sin\left(2Q_{F}r_{k}+\vartheta_{m}\right)&\cos\left(2Q_{F}r_{k}+\vartheta_{m}\right)&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{x}\\
\hat{y}\\
\hat{z}\end{pmatrix},

(S53)

such that
\bm{S}(r_{k})=m_{2Q_{F}}N_{\perp}S\hat{e}_{k}^{1}
.
In the rotated coordinates, the RKKY interaction is expressed as

\displaystyle H_{\rm{R}}=\frac{1}{N_{\perp}^{2}}\sum_{k,l}\sum_{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}=1,2,3}\sum_{m,n}J_{n}^{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}}(r_{kl})S_{m+n}^{\tilde{\mu}}(r_{k})S_{m}^{\tilde{\nu}}(r_{l})\;.

(S54)

The RKKY couplings in the un-rotated and rotated coordinates,
J_{n}^{\mu}
and
J_{n}^{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}}
are related by

\displaystyle J_{n}^{33}(r_{kl})

\displaystyle=J_{n}^{z}(r_{kl})\;,

\displaystyle J_{n}^{11}(r_{kl})

\displaystyle=J_{n}^{22}(r_{kl})=J_{n}^{x}(r_{kl})\cos\left(2Q_{F}r_{kl}+\Theta_{n}\right)\;,

\displaystyle J_{n}^{12}(r_{kl})

\displaystyle=-J_{n}^{21}(r_{kl})=J_{n}^{x}(r_{kl})\sin\left(2Q_{F}r_{kl}+\Theta_{n}\right)\;,

(S55)

where
\Theta_{n}\equiv\vartheta_{m+n}-\vartheta_{m}
being the phase difference depending solely on the domain wall separation
\propto n
.

S.III.1
Development of phase coherence of spin helices

In this subsection, we demonstrate that the RKKY energy at zero temperature is minimized by the configuration of uniform offset phases across parallel domain walls, leading to the formation of a phase-coherent quasi-2D spin helix.

The RKKY energy resulting from the formation of spin helices in a given array, accounting for inter-domain-wall correlations, can be estimated as

\displaystyle E_{{\rm R}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\perp}^{2}}\sum_{m,n}\sum_{k}J_{n}(r_{k})\left\langle\bm{S}_{m+n}(r_{k})\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle\bm{S}_{m}(0)\right\rangle=\frac{a}{N_{\perp}^{2}}\sum_{m,n}\int^{L}_{-L}dxJ_{n}(r)\left\langle\bm{S}_{m+n}(r)\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle\bm{S}_{m}(0)\right\rangle\;,

(S56)

with, again,
J_{n}(r)=\left(J_{K}^{2}a/2\right)\chi_{n}(r)
. At zero temperature, the spin susceptibility
\chi_{n}(r)
is proportional to

\displaystyle\chi_{n}(r)\sim-\cos(2Q_{F}r)\left|\frac{a}{r}\right|^{\bar{\Delta}_{n}/2},

(S57)

with
\bar{\Delta}_{n}
denoting the exponent depending on LL parameters and
\bar{\Delta}_{n}/2\geq 1
for our case. It is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle E_{{\rm R}}

\displaystyle\sim\left(-\frac{S^{2}N_{\rm dw}J_{K}^{2}a}{4}\right)\sum_{n}\int^{L}_{-L}dr\left|\frac{a}{r}\right|^{\bar{\Delta}_{n}/2}\left[\cos\left(4Q_{F}r+\Theta_{n}\right)+\cos\Theta_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{S^{2}J_{K}^{2}a}{4}\right)\sum_{n}C_{n}\cos\Theta_{n},

(S58)

with
C_{n}>0
being a coefficient which we calculate next. The first integral in Eq. (
S58
) leads to

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}dr\left|\frac{a}{r}\right|^{\alpha}\cos\left(4Qr+\Theta_{n}\right)=

\displaystyle\left(\int^{-a}_{-L}+\int^{L}_{a}\right)dr\left|\frac{a}{r}\right|^{\alpha}\cos\left(4Qr+\Theta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\cos(\Theta_{n})\int^{L}_{a}dr\left|\frac{a}{r}\right|^{\alpha}\cos(4Qr),

(S59)

where we impose a small length cutoff
a
in the integral to avoid divergence at
r=0
.
Combining the result in Eq. (
S59
) with the other contribution from the second integral in Eq. (
S58
), we reach

\displaystyle C_{n}=2\int^{L}_{a}\left|\frac{a}{r}\right|^{\bar{\Delta}_{n}/2}\left[\cos(4Q_{F}r)+1\right]dr>0\;.

(S60)

We therefore demonstrate that
C_{n}>0
and that the RKKY energy
E_{{\rm R}}
is minimized when
\cos\Theta_{n}=1
(or
\Theta_{n}=0\;\rm{mod}\;2\pi
). This result suggests phase coherence of the quasi-2D spin helix across parallel domain walls.
Naively speaking, the “phase-locking” behavior of the quasi-2D spin helix would facilitate constructive diffraction patterns in neutron scattering measurements
[
15
,

S.III.2
Magnon spectrum

Now, we proceed to the analysis of magnon spectra.
Selecting
\hat{e}_{k,m}^{1}
to be the spin quantization axis, we perform the Holstein–Primakoff transformation on the spin operators to the magnon operators
a_{km}^{\dagger}(a_{km})

\displaystyle S_{m}^{1}(r_{k})

\displaystyle\approx N_{\perp}S-a_{km}^{\dagger}a_{km}\;,

\displaystyle S_{m}^{+}(r_{k})

\displaystyle\approx a_{km}\sqrt{2N_{\perp}S}\;,

\displaystyle S_{m}^{-}(r_{k})

\displaystyle\approx a_{km}^{\dagger}\sqrt{2N_{\perp}S}\;.

(S61)

In Eq. (
S61

is large, so that higher-order terms of order

are subleading and can be neglected. In our case,
N_{\perp}\approx 80
and

, giving

, which is more than an order of magnitude larger than unity and thus justifies this approximation.

Since the off-diagonal terms,

and

) are of odd order in the magnon operators, we have

,
with

\displaystyle H_{\textrm{R}}^{11}+H_{\textrm{R}}^{22}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\perp}^{2}}\sum_{k,l}\sum_{m,n}J_{n}^{11}(r_{kl})\left[\left(N_{\perp}S\right)^{2}-N_{\perp}S\left(a_{k,m+n}^{\dagger}a_{k,m+n}+a_{lm}^{\dagger}a_{lm}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\frac{N_{\perp}S}{2}\left(a_{k,m+n}a_{lm}+a_{k,m+n}a_{lm}^{\dagger}+a_{k,m+n}^{\dagger}a_{lm}+a_{k,m+n}^{\dagger}a_{lm}^{\dagger}\right)\right],

\displaystyle H_{\textrm{R}}^{33}=

\displaystyle\frac{S}{2N_{\perp}}\sum_{k,l}\sum_{m,n}J_{n}^{z}(r_{kl})\left(-a_{k,m+n}a_{lm}+a_{k,m+n}a_{lm}^{\dagger}+a_{k,m+n}^{\dagger}a_{lm}-a_{k,m+n}^{\dagger}a_{lm}^{\dagger}\right)\;.

(S62)

\displaystyle H_{\textrm{R}}=\frac{S}{2N_{\perp}}\sum_{q,q_{\perp}}\left[h_{3}(q,q_{\perp})\left(a_{q,q_{\perp}}^{\dagger}a_{q,q_{\perp}}+a_{-q,-q_{\perp}}a_{-q,-q_{\perp}}^{\dagger}\right)+h_{2}(q,q_{\perp})\left(a_{q,q_{\perp}}a_{-q,-q_{\perp}}+a_{-q,-q_{\perp}}^{\dagger}a_{q,q_{\perp}}^{\dagger}\right)\right]\;

\displaystyle=-2J_{q_{\perp}=0}^{x}(2Q_{F})+\frac{1}{2}\left[J_{q_{\perp}}^{x}(2Q_{F}+q)+J_{q_{\perp}}^{x}(2Q_{F}-q)\right]+J_{q_{\perp}}^{z}(q)\;.

Introducing the Nambu basis

\displaystyle H_{\textrm{R}}=\frac{S}{2N_{\perp}}\sum_{q,q_{\perp}}\psi^{\dagger}(q,q_{\perp})\mathcal{H}(q,q_{\perp})\psi(q,q_{\perp}),

(S65)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(q,q_{\perp})

h_{2}(q,q_{\perp})&h_{3}(q,q_{\perp})\end{pmatrix}\;.

is given in Eq. (7) in the main text.

In this section, we discuss the electron-magnon interaction
H_{{\rm em}}
, which can be introduced through

\displaystyle H_{{\rm K}}=H_{\rm hx}+H_{{\rm em}}\;,

(S93)

where the first term
H_{\rm hx}
has been defined in Eq. (
S67

\displaystyle H_{{\rm em}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{K}}{N_{\perp}}\sum_{m,k}\Bigg\{-s_{m}^{1}(r_{k})a_{km}^{\dagger}a_{km}+\sqrt{\frac{N_{\perp}S}{2}}s_{m}^{2}(r_{k})\left(a_{km}+a_{km}^{\dagger}\right)+\frac{1}{i}\sqrt{\frac{N_{\perp}S}{2}}s_{m}^{3}(r_{k})\left(a_{km}-a_{km}^{\dagger}\right)\Bigg\}\;.

(S94)

Here, we only focus on the contribution of the small-momentum transfer
q\sim 0
of

\displaystyle s_{m}^{3}(r)\approx\frac{-1}{\pi}\partial_{r}\phi_{sS,m}(r)\;,

(S95)

which leads to

\displaystyle H_{{\rm em}}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{iJ_{K}}{\pi N_{\perp}}\sqrt{\frac{N_{\perp}S}{2}}\sum_{m,k}\left[\partial_{r_{k}}\phi_{sS,m}(r_{k})\right]\left(a_{km}-a_{km}^{\dagger}\right)=g_{{\rm em}}\int\frac{dr}{2\pi}\sum_{m}\left[\partial_{r}\phi_{sS,m}(r)\right]\Pi_{m}(r)\;,

(S96)

where we have replaced
\sum_{k}\to\int dr/a
,
(a_{km}-a_{km}^{\dagger})/\sqrt{a}\to[a_{m}(r)-a_{m}^{\dagger}(r)]
, neglected the contributions from
S_{m}^{1,2}(r_{k})
, and defined the effective coupling strength
g_{{\rm em}}\equiv-2J_{K}\sqrt{aSm_{2Q_{F}}/(\hbar\omega_{0}N_{\perp})}
.
In the above, the field
a_{k,m}
is dimensionless while
a_{m}(r)
carries dimension of
a^{-1/2}
. The prefactor
1/2\pi
has been introduced for convenience. We also introduce the conjugate field
\Pi_{m}(r)
to
\varphi_{m}(r)

\displaystyle\Pi_{m}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{LN_{{\rm dw}}}}\sum_{q,q_{\perp}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{0}}}\left(e^{iqr+iq_{\perp}y_{m}}a_{q,q_{\perp}}+e^{-iqr-iq_{\perp}y_{m}}a_{q,q_{\perp}}^{\dagger}\right)\;,

(S97)

\displaystyle\varphi_{m}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{LN_{{\rm dw}}}}\sum_{q,q_{\perp}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{0}}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{i}\right)\left(e^{iqr+iq_{\perp}y_{m}}a_{q,q_{\perp}}-e^{-iqr-iq_{\perp}y_{m}}a_{q,q_{\perp}}^{\dagger}\right)\;,

(S98)

with
a_{q,q_{\perp}}
being dimensionless operator in the momentum space. It is straightforward to verify the fact that
\varphi_{m}
and
\Pi_{m}
forms a set of conjugate operators, namely

\displaystyle\left[\varphi_{m}(r),\,\Pi_{m^{\prime}}(r^{\prime})\right]=i\hbar\delta(r-r^{\prime})\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\;.

(S99)

As can be seen in Eq. (
S96
),
H_{{\rm em}}
represents the coupling between the density-like operator
\partial_{r}\phi_{sS,m}
in the spin-symmetric sector and the conjugate field
\Pi_{m}
. While its derivation is reminiscent of electron-phonon coupling in the charge sector of different systems
[

,

,

,
28
,
1

\hbar\omega_{0}
, as discussed in the main text. This turns out to give a different behavior in the modified scaling dimension in the bosonic operators.

To proceed, we evaluate the propagators of the
\phi_{\nu P}
and

fields and the corresponding scaling dimensions.
Since Eq. (

) only involves
\phi_{sS,m}
fields, we focus on this sector,

\displaystyle\frac{S_{sS}}{\hbar}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{{\rm dw}}}\sum_{q_{\perp}}\int\frac{d^{2}r}{2\pi}\Bigg\{-2i\left[\partial_{r}\theta_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{r})\right]\left[\partial_{\tau}\phi_{sS,-q_{\perp}}(\bm{r})\right]+\frac{v_{{\rm dw}}}{K_{sS}}\left|\partial_{r}\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{r})\right|^{2}+v_{{\rm dw}}K_{sS}\left|\partial_{r}\theta_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{r})\right|^{2}\Bigg\}\;,

\displaystyle\frac{S_{{\rm mag}}}{\hbar}

\displaystyle\frac{S_{{\rm em}}}{\hbar}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{{\rm dw}}}\sum_{q_{\perp}}\int\frac{d^{2}r}{2\pi}\,\frac{g_{{\rm em}}}{\hbar}\left[\partial_{r}\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{r})\right]\Pi_{-q_{\perp}}(\bm{r})\;,

(S100)

where
\bm{r}\equiv(r,\tau)
and
d^{2}r\equiv drd\tau
. Since Eq. (
S100
) are quadratic in all the fields, we can derive the effective action for the
\phi_{sS}
and
\theta_{sS}
fields nonperturbatively, giving rise to

\displaystyle\frac{S[\phi_{sS}]}{\hbar}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{B}T}{\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}}\sum_{q_{\perp},\bm{p}}\frac{1}{2\pi K_{sS}}\left[v_{{\rm dw}}q^{2}+\frac{\omega_{n}^{2}}{v_{{\rm dw}}}-\frac{g_{{\rm em}}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}K_{sS}q^{2}}{4\pi\hbar\left(\omega_{0}^{2}+\omega_{n}^{2}\right)}\right]\left|\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}\;,

(S101)

\displaystyle\frac{S[\theta_{sS}]}{\hbar}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{B}T}{\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}}\sum_{q_{\perp},\bm{p}}\frac{K_{sS}}{2\pi}\left[v_{{\rm dw}}q^{2}+\omega_{n}^{2}\frac{4\pi\hbar\left(\omega_{0}^{2}+\omega_{n}^{2}\right)}{4\pi\hbar v_{{\rm dw}}\left(\omega_{0}^{2}+\omega_{n}^{2}\right)-g_{{\rm em}}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}K_{sS}}\right]\left|\theta_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}\;,

(S102)

with
\bm{p}\equiv(q,\omega_{n})

\phi_{sS}

\displaystyle\left\langle\bar{\phi}_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right\rangle_{{\rm ee}+{\rm em}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}K_{sS}/k_{B}T}{v_{{\rm dw}}q^{2}+\frac{\omega_{n}^{2}}{v_{{\rm dw}}}-\frac{g_{{\rm em}}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}K_{sS}q^{2}}{4\pi\hbar\left(\omega_{0}^{2}+\omega_{n}^{2}\right)}}\;,

(S103)

\displaystyle\left\langle\bar{\theta}_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\theta_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right\rangle_{{\rm ee}+{\rm em}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}/(k_{B}TK_{sS})}{v_{{\rm dw}}q^{2}+\omega_{n}^{2}\frac{4\pi\hbar\left(\omega_{0}^{2}+\omega_{n}^{2}\right)}{4\pi\hbar v_{{\rm dw}}\left(\omega_{0}^{2}+\omega_{n}^{2}\right)-g_{{\rm em}}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}K_{sS}}}\;,

(S104)

where
\langle\cdots\rangle_{\rm ee+em}

The effective action is characterized by excitation modes whose corresponding energy
\hbar\omega_{\pm}
can be extracted from the poles of the propagators in Eq. (
S102

\displaystyle\omega_{\pm}^{2}(q)=\frac{v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}q^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}q^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}+2\Gamma^{2}_{\rm em}/v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}\right)^{2}+4\Gamma^{4}_{\rm em}/v_{{\rm dw}}^{4}-4\omega_{0}^{2}\Gamma^{2}_{\rm em}/v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}}}{2}\;,

(S105)

with

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm em}\equiv\frac{g_{{\rm em}}\omega_{0}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{v_{{\rm dw}}K_{sS}}{\pi\hbar}}\;.

(S106)

One can check that for
\Gamma_{\rm em}=0

\hbar\omega_{\pm}

\phi_{sS}\,(\theta_{sS})
and magnons,
(\hbar\omega_{\pm})^{2}\to(\hbar v_{{\rm dw}}q)^{2},\;(\hbar\omega_{0})^{2}

We explore the general properties of the excitation energy.
At
q=0

\omega_{-}
is gapless while
\omega_{+}
is gapped, with
\omega_{+}(q=0)=\omega_{0}
. The leading order behavior of
\omega_{\pm}
in small

\displaystyle\omega_{+}^{2}(q)\approx\omega_{0}^{2}\;,\quad\omega_{-}^{2}(q)\approx\left(v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\right)^{2}q^{2},

(S107)

with the modified velocity
v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}
of
\phi_{sS}
by magnons,

\displaystyle v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\equiv v_{{\rm dw}}\sqrt{1-\Gamma^{2}_{\rm em}/\omega_{0}^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}}\;.

(S108)

Next, we calculate various correlation functions for
\phi_{sS}
and
\theta_{sS}
in the low-energy (small-momentum) regime. In this regime, using Eqs. (
S107
) and (
S108

\displaystyle\left\langle\bar{\phi}_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right\rangle_{{\rm ee}+{\rm em}}\approx\frac{\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}\pi K_{sS}v_{{\rm dw}}/(k_{B}T)}{\omega_{n}^{2}+\left(v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\right)^{2}q^{2}}\;,

\displaystyle\left\langle\bar{\theta}_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\theta_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right\rangle_{{\rm ee}+{\rm em}}\approx\frac{\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}\pi v_{{\rm dw}}}{k_{B}TK_{sS}}\left(\frac{1-\Gamma_{{\rm em}}^{2}/v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}{\omega_{n}^{2}+\left(v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\right)^{2}q^{2}}+\frac{\Gamma_{{\rm em}}^{2}/v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}{\omega_{n}^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2}}\right).

(S109)

We first evaluate the correlation function for
\phi_{sS}
of the following form,

\displaystyle G_{\phi_{sS},m,n}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle\equiv\left\langle\left[\phi_{sS,m+n}(\bm{r})-\phi_{sS,m}(0)\right]^{2}\right\rangle_{{\rm ee}+{\rm em}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d(q_{\perp}d)}{2\pi}\int_{-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\,\frac{2\pi\hbar K_{sS}v_{{\rm dw}}}{2\hbar v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\left|q\right|}\,\Bigg\{2n_{B}\left(\hbar v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\left|q\right|\right)+1

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\cos\left(qr+q_{\perp}nd\right)\left[2n_{B}\left(\hbar v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\left|q\right|\right)\cosh\left(v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\left|q\right|\tau\right)+e^{-v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\left|q\right|\tau}\right]\Bigg\}.

(S110)

In the above, we introduce the momentum cutoff
\Lambda
, such that
v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\Lambda=\omega_{0}
. This corresponds to a small distance cutoff
\tilde{a}=1/\Lambda=v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}/\omega_{0}
. In the
T=0

G_{\phi_{sS},m,n}(\bm{r})
in Eq. (

\displaystyle G_{\phi_{sS},m,n}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d(q_{\perp}d)}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dq\,\frac{K_{sS}v_{{\rm dw}}}{v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}}\,\frac{1}{q}\left[1-\cos(qr)\cos(q_{\perp}nd)e^{-v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\left|q\right|\tau}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{K_{sS}v_{{\rm dw}}}{v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}}\ln\left|\frac{\tilde{r}}{\tilde{a}}\right|\,,&n=0\,,\\
\frac{K_{sS}v_{{\rm dw}}}{v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}}\left(-\gamma-\ln\frac{\tilde{a}}{L}\right)&n\neq 0\,,\end{cases}

(S111)

with
\tilde{r}=\sqrt{\left(v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\tau+\tilde{a}\right)^{2}+r^{2}}
.

The correlation function of
\theta_{sS}
takes the following form,

\displaystyle G_{\theta_{sS},m,n}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle\equiv\left\langle\left[\theta_{sS,m+n}(\bm{r})-\theta_{sS,m}(0)\right]^{2}\right\rangle_{\rm{ee+em}}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{B}T}{\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}}\sum_{\bm{p},q_{\perp}}\frac{\pi v_{{\rm dw}}}{K_{sS}}\left[\frac{1-\Gamma_{{\rm em}}^{2}/v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}{\omega_{n}^{2}+\left(v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\right)^{2}q^{2}}+\frac{\Gamma_{{\rm em}}^{2}/v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}{\omega_{n}^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2}}\right]\Big[2-2\cos\left(qx+q_{\perp}nd-\omega_{n}\tau\right)\Big]

\displaystyle\equiv I_{1}+I_{2}\;,

(S112)

The
I_{1}
term in Eq. (
S112
) above gives

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{B}T}{LN_{{\rm dw}}}\sum_{\bm{p},q_{\perp}}\frac{\pi v_{{\rm dw}}\left(1-\Gamma_{{\rm em}}^{2}/v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}\right)}{K_{sS}}\frac{2\hbar}{-(i\hbar\omega_{n})^{2}+\hbar^{2}\left(v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}\right)^{2}q^{2}}\left[1-\cos\left(qr+q_{\perp}nd-\omega_{n}\tau\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}}{K_{sS}v_{{\rm dw}}}\ln\left|\frac{\tilde{r}}{\tilde{a}}\right|\,,&n=0\,,\\
\frac{v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}}{K_{sS}v_{{\rm dw}}}\left(-\gamma-\ln\frac{\tilde{a}}{L}\right)\,,&n\neq 0\,.\end{cases}

(S113)

We observe that the correlation within a domain wall exhibits a logarithmic divergence with distance and time, while the correlation across different domain walls remains constant. This difference arises because the spin-symmetric sector is characterized by identical velocity and the parameter
K_{sS}
across all domain walls.
The term
I_{2}
in
G_{\theta_{sS},m,n}(\bm{r})
is given by

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{{\rm em}}^{2}}{v_{{\rm dw}}\omega_{0}^{3}K_{sS}}\left[\Lambda-\delta_{n,0}\frac{\sin(\Lambda r)}{r}e^{-\omega_{0}\tau}\right]\;.

(S114)

Since

\frac{\Lambda\Gamma^{2}}{v_{{\rm dw}}\omega_{0}^{3}K_{sS}}

in the long-distance and long-time limit, making it less significant than the
I_{1}
term.
Combining
I_{1}
and

\displaystyle G_{\theta_{sS},m,n}(\bm{r})=\begin{cases}\frac{v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}}{K_{sS}v_{{\rm dw}}}\ln\left|\frac{\tilde{r}}{\tilde{a}}\right|,&n=0\,,\\
\frac{v_{{\rm dw}}^{\prime}}{K_{sS}v_{{\rm dw}}}\left(-\gamma-\ln\frac{\tilde{a}}{L}\right)+\frac{\Lambda\Gamma_{{\rm em}}^{2}}{v_{{\rm dw}}\omega_{0}^{3}K_{sS}}\,,&n\neq 0\,.\end{cases}

(S115)

The correlation exponents,
K_{sS}^{\prime}
and
(K_{sS}^{\prime})^{-1}

\phi_{sS}
and
\theta_{sS}
are thus modified by the electron-magnon coupling and exhibit the opposite behaviors,

\displaystyle\frac{K_{sS}^{\prime}}{K_{sS}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\Gamma_{{\rm em}}^{2}/v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}}\;,

(S116)

which is given in Eq. (9) of the main text.
Remarkably, when the electron-magnon coupling reaches the limit

.
This behavior influence correlation functions and scaling dimensions of various operators.

S.V
Spin relaxation rate

In this section, we provide details for the derivation of the relaxation rate
1/TT_{1}
due to the exchange interaction,
H_{\rm{K}}
of Eq. (4) of the main text, of the conduction electrons and the local moments.
The relaxation time
T_{1}
measures the average spin-flip time by external perturbations (
H_{\rm{K}}
in our work). By applying the Fermi’s golden rule, the relaxation rate
1/T_{1}
is related to the transverse spin susceptibility
\chi^{R}_{\perp}
through
[
29
]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T_{1}T}=J_{K}^{2}a^{2}\frac{k_{B}}{\hbar^{3}}\lim_{\omega\to 0}\frac{{\rm Im}\left[\chi_{\perp}^{R}(r=0,\omega)\right]}{\omega}\;,

(S117)

with
\chi_{\perp}^{R}(r=0,t)=-i\left\langle T_{t}[s^{+}_{m}(r,t),\;s^{-}_{m}(r,t=0)]\right\rangle
,
\chi_{\perp}^{R}(\omega)=\int dte^{i\omega t}\chi_{\perp}^{R}(t)
, and
s^{\pm}_{m}=s^{x}_{m}\pm is^{y}_{m}
.
Here, we keep only the interbranch scattering contributions, which dominate over the less relevant intrabranch ones,

\displaystyle s^{\pm}_{m}(r)\to s^{\pm}_{\curvearrowleftright,m}(r)=\frac{1}{2}\left\{O^{\pm}_{\curvearrowleftright,\rm{sdw}}(r)+\left[O^{\mp}_{\curvearrowleftright,\rm{sdw}}\right]^{\dagger}(r)\right\}\;,

(S118)

where we define the operator as

\displaystyle O_{\curvearrowleftright,{\rm sdw}}^{\pm}(r)=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-2iQ_{F}r}}{\pi a}\sum_{\delta}e^{i\left(\phi_{cS,m}\pm\delta\phi_{sA,m}\mp\theta_{sS,m}-\delta\theta_{cA,m}\right)}\;.

(S119)

Below, we investigate the
T>T_{{\rm hx}}
and
T\ll T_{{\rm hx}}
regimes separately.

S.V.1
For
T>T_{{\rm hx}}

We now consider the temperature regime without the helical spin order, where all bosonic modes are gapless. Following the analysis in Ref.
[
7
]
and Sec.
S.II
, we calculate the transverse spin susceptibility

\displaystyle\chi_{\curvearrowleftright,\perp}^{R}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2a}{v_{{\rm dw}}}\frac{\sin\left(\pi g_{\curvearrowleftright}^{x}\right)}{(\pi a)^{2}}\left(\frac{2\pi ak_{B}T}{\hbar v_{{\rm dw}}}\right)^{2g_{\curvearrowleftright}^{x}}B\left(-\frac{i\hbar\omega}{2\pi k_{B}T}+g_{\curvearrowleftright}^{x};1-2g_{\curvearrowleftright}^{x}\right)\;.

(S120)

To compute
1/T_{1}T
, we need to evaluate

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\to 0}\frac{{\rm Im}\left[\chi_{{\curvearrowleftright},\perp}^{R}(\omega)\right]}{\omega}=

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\to 0}\partial_{\omega}{\rm Im}\left[\chi_{{\curvearrowleftright},\perp}^{R}(\omega)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\pi^{2}k_{B}^{2}}\frac{\sin\left(\pi g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}\right)}{(\pi a)^{2}}\left(\frac{2\pi ak_{B}}{\hbar v_{{\rm dw}}}\right)^{2g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}}T^{2g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}-2}\Gamma\left(1-2g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}\right)\frac{\Gamma\left(1-2g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}\right)\Gamma\left(g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1-g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}\right)}\left[\Psi(g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x})-\Psi(1-g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x})\right]\;,

(S121)

with the digamma function,
\Psi(z)\equiv\left(1/\Gamma(z)\right)d\Gamma(z)/dz
. This leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T_{1}T}=\frac{J_{K}^{2}}{2\pi^{4}k_{B}\hbar}\sin(\pi g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x})\left(\frac{2\pi ak_{B}}{\hbar v_{{\rm dw}}}\right)^{2g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}}T^{2g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}-2}\left\{\Gamma\left(1-2g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}\right)\frac{\Gamma\left(1-2g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}\right)\Gamma\left(g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1-g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}\right)}\left[\Psi(g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x})-\Psi(1-g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x})\right]\right\}\;.

(S122)

S.V.2
For
T\ll T_{{\rm hx}}

The spin helix generates an effective magnetic field, which gaps out half of the electronic spectrum and therefore reduces the spin relaxation channels. Using the new boson representation,
\Phi^{\eta}_{\delta,m}
and
\Theta^{\eta}_{\delta,m}
introduced in Section
S.III
, we obtain the following operator,

\displaystyle O_{\curvearrowleftright,{\rm sdw}}^{\pm}(r)=\frac{e^{-2iQ_{F}r}}{\pi a}\sum_{\delta}e^{2i\Phi_{\delta}^{\mp}(r)}\;.

(S123)

The corresponding transverse spin susceptibility,
\tilde{\chi}_{{\curvearrowleftright},\perp}
, includes contributions from the
\Phi_{\delta}^{+}
and
\Phi_{\delta}^{-}
modes,

\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}_{{\curvearrowleftright},\perp}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{4(\pi a)^{2}}\sum_{\delta}\left[\left\langle e^{2i\Phi_{\delta}^{-}(\tau)}e^{-2i\Phi_{\delta}^{-}(0)}\right\rangle+\left\langle e^{-2i\Phi_{\delta}^{+}(\tau)}e^{2i\Phi_{\delta}^{+}(0)}\right\rangle\right]\;.

(S124)

The contribution of the gapless
\Phi_{\delta}^{-}
mode to
1/TT_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{J_{K}^{2}}{4\pi^{4}k_{B}\hbar}\sin(\pi\bar{K})\left[1-m_{2Q_{F}}(T)\right]\left(\frac{2\pi ak_{B}}{\hbar\tilde{v}_{{\rm dw}}}\right)^{2\bar{K}}T^{2\bar{K}-2}\left\{\Gamma\left(1-2\bar{K}\right)\frac{\Gamma\left(1-2\bar{K}\right)\Gamma\left(\bar{K}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1-\bar{K}\right)}\left[\Psi(\bar{K})-\Psi(1-\bar{K})\right]\right\}\;,

(S125)

showing a dependence
\propto\left[1-m_{2Q_{F}}(T)\right]T^{2\bar{K}-2}
.
Compared to Eq. (
S122
) for
T>T_{\rm hx}
, we note several differences.
First, due to the ordering of localized moments,
1/TT_{1}
acquires a factor of
(1-m_{2Q_{F}})
to account for the component of the localized spins that remain disordered and thus allow for spin-flip relaxation.
Second, there is an overall prefactor of 1/2 to account for the remaining gapless electrons.
Third, the parameters depend on the modification from the helix, including
g_{{\curvearrowleftright}}^{x}\to\bar{K}
and
v_{{\rm dw}}\to\tilde{v}_{{\rm dw}}
.

In addition,
1/TT_{1}
receives contributions from the
\Phi_{\delta}^{+}
modes, which are gapped and exhibit an exponentially suppressed relaxation rate,

\displaystyle\frac{J_{K}^{2}}{4\pi^{4}k_{B}\hbar}\left[1-m_{2Q_{F}}(T)\right]\left(\frac{2\pi ak_{B}}{\hbar\tilde{v}_{\rm dw}}\right)^{2\bar{K}}T^{2\bar{K}-2}{\rm Im}\Bigg\{e^{i\pi\bar{K}}\frac{\Gamma(1-2\bar{K})\Gamma\left(-\frac{i\Delta(T)}{2\pi k_{B}T}+\bar{K}\right)}{\Gamma\left(-\frac{i\Delta(T)}{2\pi k_{B}T}+1-2\bar{K}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left[\Psi\left(-\frac{i\Delta(T)}{2\pi k_{B}T}+\bar{K}\right)-\Psi\left(-\frac{i\Delta(T)}{2\pi k_{B}T}+1-2\bar{K}\right)\right]\Bigg\}\;,

(S126)

with the temperature-dependent gap,

\displaystyle\Delta(T)=\tilde{\Delta}_{a}\left(\frac{J_{K}Sm_{2Q_{F}}(T)}{\tilde{\Delta}_{a}}\right)^{1/(2-\bar{K})}\;.

(S127)

We summarize the power-law behavior of the
1/T_{1}T
, Eqs. (
S122
) and (
S125
) in the two temperature regimes, in Eq. (10a) and Eq. (10b) in the main text. Additionally, we present their temperature dependencies, along with the subdominant contribution in Eq. (
S126
), in Fig. 3 in the main text
and its inset.

S.VI
Paramagnetic spin susceptibility

In this section, we examine the paramagnetic spin susceptibility arising from the electron subsystem, specifically contributions from small-momentum transfer (
q\sim 0
) at
T\ll T_{\rm hx}
. In the absence of interactions, this reduces to the Pauli susceptibility of a free electron gas. Incorporating interactions in the domain wall network here, this observable quantity not only reveals the spin ordering, but also interaction effects through renormalized system parameters.

To proceed, we examine the susceptibility from the response to an external magnetic field, characterized by the following Zeeman term,

\displaystyle H_{\rm h}=-\sum_{m}\int dr\bm{h}\cdot\bm{s}_{m}(r)=-h\sum_{m}\int dr\left[s_{m}^{z}(r)\cos\varphi+s_{m}^{x}(r)\sin\varphi\right]\;,

(S128)

where
\bm{h}=h\hat{\bm{n}}
and
h=g\mu_{B}B
with
g
denoting the Landé
g
-factor for domain wall modes,
\mu_{B}
the Bohr magneton, and
B
the magnetic field strength. The in-plane external field
B\hat{\bm{n}}
forms an angle
\varphi
with the
z
axis (the domain wall direction), and its unit vector is given by
\hat{\bm{n}}=(\sin\varphi,\;0,\;\cos\varphi)
.

The area magnetization
M
contribution (magnetic moment per unit area) from the electron spins is defined as

\displaystyle M=g\mu_{B}\frac{k_{B}T}{\hbar LL_{\perp}}\sum_{m}\int d\tau dr\left\langle\hat{\bm{n}}\cdot\bm{s}_{m}(r)\right\rangle=\frac{k_{B}T}{LL_{\perp}}\frac{1}{Z}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial B}\;,

(S129)

where
L_{\perp}\equiv N_{\rm dw}d
, and
Z
denotes the partition function. Consequently, the (area) paramagnetic susceptibility,
\chi_{u}=\mu_{0}\partial M/\partial B
(with the permeability
\mu_{0}
), is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{u}=\frac{\mu_{0}\left(g\mu_{B}\right)^{2}}{\hbar d}\sum_{m}\sum_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\int d\tau drn^{\mu}n^{\mu^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}_{m}^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(r,\tau)\;,

(S130)

where
\mu\;,\mu^{\prime}\in\{x,y,z\}
and
\mathcal{D}_{m}^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(r,\tau)=\left\langle s_{m}^{\mu}(r,\tau)s_{m=0}^{\mu^{\prime}}(r=0,\tau=0)\right\rangle-\left\langle s_{m}^{\mu}(r,\tau)\right\rangle\left\langle s_{m=0}^{\mu^{\prime}}(r=0,\tau=0)\right\rangle
. Each component of
\mathcal{D}_{m}^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(r,\tau)
will be analyzed in detail below.

To proceed, we consider the effective action incorporating contributions from the electron subsystem, free magnons, electron-magnon interaction, and the coupling to the rotating magnetic field from the spin helix. We start from the ones for the magnon-modified
\phi_{sS}
and
\theta_{sS}
fields upon integrating out the magnon fields, as shown in Section
S.III
.
The resulting contribution
S_{\rm ee+em}\equiv S_{\rm ee}+S_{\rm mag}+S_{\rm em}
and the spin helix contribution
S_{{\rm hx}}
are given by

\displaystyle\frac{S_{{\rm ee+em}}}{\hbar}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{B}T}{2\pi\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}}\sum_{\bm{p},q_{\perp}}\left\{2iq\omega_{n}\theta_{cS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\phi_{cS,-q_{\perp}}(-\bm{p})+\frac{q^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}}{K_{cS}(q_{\perp})}\left|\phi_{cS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}+q^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}K_{cS}(q_{\perp})\left|\theta_{cS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\quad+2iq\omega_{n}\theta_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\phi_{sS,-q_{\perp}}(-\bm{p})+\frac{q^{2}v^{\prime}_{{\rm dw}}}{K^{\prime}_{sS}}\left|\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}+q^{2}v^{\prime}_{{\rm dw}}K^{\prime}_{sS}\left|\theta_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\nu,P\neq cS,sS}\left.\left[2iq\omega_{n}\theta_{\nu P,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\phi_{\nu P,-q_{\perp}}(-\bm{p})+q^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}\left|\phi_{\nu P,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}+q^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}\left|\theta_{\nu P,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}\right]\right\},

\displaystyle\frac{S_{{\rm hx}}}{\hbar}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{k_{B}T}{2\pi\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}}\sum_{\bm{p},q_{\perp}}\Delta^{2}\left[\left|\phi_{cS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}+\left|\theta_{cA,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}+\left|\phi_{sA,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}+\left|\theta_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+2\phi_{cS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\theta_{sS,-q_{\perp}}(-\bm{p})+2\theta_{cA,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\phi_{sA,-q_{\perp}}(-\bm{p})\right],

(S131)

where
v^{\prime}_{\rm dw}
is defined in Eq. (
S108
),
\Delta^{2}\equiv 2B_{{\rm hx}}/\hbar a

is defined such that
\hbar\Delta\sqrt{v_{\rm dw}K_{cS}(q_{\perp})}
represents the magnitude of energy gap, and
K_{sS}^{\prime}
given in Eq. (9) of the main text.

We evaluate the
\chi^{zx}_{u}
component, which corresponds to the correlation between
s^{z}_{m}
and
s^{x}_{m}
, as given by

\displaystyle\left\langle s_{m}^{z}(r,\tau)s_{m=0}^{x}(r=0,\tau=0)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\sim\left\langle\left[\partial_{r}\phi_{sS,m}(r,\tau)\right]e^{i\left[\ell\phi_{sS,m=0}(r=0,\tau=0)-\theta_{sS,m=0}(r=0,\tau=0)\right]}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left\langle e^{i\left[\ell\delta\phi_{sA,m=0}(r=0,\tau=0)-\delta\theta_{sA,m=0}(r=0,\tau=0)\right]}\right\rangle\;,

(S132)

where we use the
q\sim 0
component of
s_{m}^{z}(r)=-\pi^{-1}\partial_{r}\phi_{sS,m}(r)
in Eq. (
S95
) and
s_{m}^{x}(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi a}\sum_{\ell\delta}\cos\Big[\ell\phi_{sS,m}+\ell\delta\phi_{sA,m}-\theta_{sS,m}-\delta\theta_{sA,m}\Big]

\left\langle e^{i\left[\ell\delta\phi_{sA,m=0}(r=0,\tau=0)-\delta\theta_{sA,m=0}(r=0,\tau=0)\right]}\right\rangle=0

Next, we compute
\chi_{u}^{zz}(T\ll T_{\rm hx})
and obtain

\displaystyle\chi^{zz}_{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{0}\left(g\mu_{B}\right)^{2}\cos^{2}\varphi}{\hbar d}\int d\tau dr\left[\left\langle s_{m}^{z}(r,\tau)s_{m^{\prime}=0}^{z}(0,0)\right\rangle-\left\langle s_{m}^{z}(r,\tau)\right\rangle\left\langle s_{m^{\prime}=0}^{z}(0,0)\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mu_{0}\left(g\mu_{B}\right)^{2}\cos^{2}\varphi}{\pi^{2}\hbar d}\left(\frac{k_{B}T}{\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}}\right)\sum_{\bm{p},q_{\perp}}\sum_{\bm{p}^{\prime},q_{\perp}^{\prime}}\delta_{\bm{p},0}\delta_{q_{\perp},0}qq^{\prime}\Bigg[\left\langle\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}^{\prime}}(\bm{p}^{\prime})\right\rangle-\left\langle\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right\rangle\left\langle\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}^{\prime}}(\bm{p}^{\prime})\right\rangle\Bigg]\;,

(S133)

Since the subsequent analysis on
\chi_{u}^{zz}
only involves the spin-symmetric sector (denoted by sS), the other sectors do not enter the following discussion. By integrating out
\phi_{cS}
and
\theta_{cS}
, the effective action, denoted as
S_{sS}
, is obtained as

\displaystyle\frac{S_{sS}}{\hbar}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{B}T}{2\pi\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}}\sum_{\bm{p},q_{\perp}}\Bigg\{2iq\omega_{n}\theta_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\phi_{sS,-q_{\perp}}(-\bm{p})+\frac{q^{2}v^{\prime}_{{\rm dw}}}{K^{\prime}_{sS}}\left|\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left[q^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}K_{sS}+\Delta^{2}-\frac{\Delta^{4}v_{{\rm dw}}K_{cS}(q_{\perp})}{\omega_{n}^{2}+v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}q^{2}+\Delta^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}K_{cS}(q_{\perp})}\right]\left|\theta_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\right|^{2}\Bigg\}\;.

(S134)

Next, we integrate out the
\theta_{sS}
field from Eq. (
S134
) to derive the effective action that contains only
\phi_{sS}
. The corresponding propagator for
\phi_{sS}
is then given by

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\phi_{sS,-q_{\perp}}(-\bm{p})\right\rangle_{{\rm ee}+{\rm em}+{\rm hx}}=\frac{\pi\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}/(k_{B}T)}{\frac{q^{2}v^{\prime}_{{\rm dw}}}{K^{\prime}_{sS}}+\frac{q^{2}\omega_{n}^{2}}{q^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}K_{sS}+\Delta^{2}-\frac{\Delta^{4}v_{{\rm dw}}K_{cS}(q_{\perp})}{\omega_{n}^{2}+v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}q^{2}+\Delta^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}K_{cS}(q_{\perp})}}}\;.

(S135)

Applying Eqs. (
S133
) and (
S135
),
\chi_{u}^{zz}(T\ll T_{\rm hx})
reads

\displaystyle\chi_{u}^{zz}(T\ll T_{\rm hx})=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{0}\left(g\mu_{B}\right)^{2}\cos^{2}\varphi}{\pi^{2}\hbar d}\left(\frac{k_{B}T}{\hbar LN_{{\rm dw}}}\right)\lim_{\omega_{n}\to 0}\lim_{q,q_{\perp}\to 0}q^{2}\left\langle\phi_{sS,q_{\perp}}(\bm{p})\phi_{sS,-q_{\perp}}(-\bm{p})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{0}\left(g\mu_{B}\right)^{2}\cos^{2}\varphi}{\pi\hbar d}\lim_{\omega_{n}\to 0}\left[\frac{1}{\frac{v^{\prime}_{{\rm dw}}}{K^{\prime}_{sS}}+\frac{\omega_{n}^{2}}{\Delta^{2}-\frac{\Delta^{4}v_{{\rm dw}}\tilde{K}_{cS}(q_{\perp}=0)}{\omega_{n}^{2}+\Delta^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}\tilde{K}_{cS}(q_{\perp}=0)}}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{0}\left(g\mu_{B}\right)^{2}}{\pi\hbar v_{{\rm dw}}d}\frac{K_{sS}\cos^{2}\varphi}{\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{{\rm em}}^{2}}{\omega_{0}^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}}\right)+\tilde{K}_{cS}(q_{\perp}=0)K_{sS}}\;,

(S136)

where the appearance of
\tilde{K}_{cS}
in Eq. (

\theta_{sS}
and
\phi_{cS}
in

, induced by the helix ordering gap. The limits
\lim_{\omega_{n}\to 0}
and
\lim_{q,q_{\perp}\to 0}
do not commute and is thus not interchangeable here.

Subsequently, we compute
\chi_{u}^{xx}
for
T\ll T_{\rm hx}
, which requires the computation of

\displaystyle\left\langle s_{m}^{x}(r,\tau)s_{m^{\prime}=0}^{x}(0,0)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi a)^{2}}\sum_{\ell\ell^{\prime}\delta\delta^{\prime}}\left\langle\cos\left[\ell\phi_{sS,m}+\ell\delta\phi_{sA,m}-\theta_{sS,m}-\delta\theta_{sA,m}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left.\cos\left[\ell^{\prime}\phi_{sS,m=0}+\ell^{\prime}\delta^{\prime}\phi_{sA,m=0}-\theta_{sS,m=0}-\delta^{\prime}\theta_{sA,m=0}\right]\right\rangle

\phi_{sS}

\displaystyle\chi_{u}(T\ll T_{\rm hx})\approx\chi_{u}^{zz}(T\ll T_{\rm hx})=\frac{\mu_{0}\left(g\mu_{B}\right)^{2}}{\pi\hbar v_{{\rm dw}}d}\frac{K_{sS}\cos^{2}\varphi}{\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{{\rm em}}^{2}}{\omega_{0}^{2}v_{{\rm dw}}^{2}}\right)+\tilde{K}_{cS}(q_{\perp}=0)K_{sS}}\;.

Mixed symmetries of 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} : immanants in the sampling of U ⁡ ( d ) U(d) submatrices
Jacob Daigle, Hubert de Guise, Trevor Welsh

Abstract
We provide results on the mean and higher moments of immanants of submatrices of ensembles of Haar-distributed unitary matrices, mostly without proofs. This paper is based on a talk presented at ISQS29 in Prague in July 2025 by Trevor Welsh.

1
Introduction

The quantum state of
n
indistinguishable particles
(bosons or fermions) must transform by a one-dimensional
representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
the symmetric group on
n
symbols

[
1
,
2
]
.
This,
in turn, naturally introduces determinant and permanent states
for fermions and bosons respectively
(see for instance

[
3
,
4
,
5
]
).
Applications of permanents in physics have been re-energized
by the
BosonSampling
problem
[
6
]
,
which connects the computational complexity of sampling permanents of

n\times n
submatrices in the
d
-dimensional unitary group
U(d)
(
n\ll d
)
to linear optics experiments.

For
n

partially
distinguishable particles,
states with mixed permutation symmetries
(
i.e.
states transforming under irreducible representations (irreps)
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
that are not fully symmetric or fully antisymmetric) may appear.
It then becomes natural to investigate
immanants
either
in their own right
[
7
,
8
]
,
for applications in chemistry
[
8
]
,
as a gateway to transition probabilities in
n
-body problems

[
9
]
, or because they are sums of group functions
which appear in transition probabilities
[
10
]
.

In this contribution, we investigate moments of
immanants of
n\times n
submatrices of random matrices in
U(d)
,
with particular emphasis on the first (the mean) and second moments.
They are of interest because they can be used
to understand the (anti)concentration
property
of immanants of submatrices and thus provide
a pathway to prove average case complexity of these matrix
functions, thereby demonstrating a possible computational advantage

[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
Using Weingarten Calculus allows us to produce,
for the full range of immanants,
explicit
d
-dependent formulae for the mean,
and an algorithmic formula for the second moment.
We evaluate this latter formula for all immanants for
n\leq 5
,
giving explicit rational polynomials in
d
,
with only the growth in computational requirements preventing us for going further.
Nonetheless, our analysis also yields a formula for the leading term,
which is evaluated here for
n\leq 9
.
In the extremal cases of the immanant — the permanent and determinant —
our approach cogently reproduces results of Nezami
[
14
]
.
Full proofs of our results will be given elsewhere.

2
Determinants, Permanents and immanants

The
determinant

\textup{Det }M
and
permanent

\textup{Perm }M

of an
n\times n
matrix
M
may be defined by

\displaystyle\textup{Det }M

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\textup{sgn}(\pi)\,M_{1\pi(1)}M_{2\pi(2)}\cdots M_{n\pi(n)}

(1)

\displaystyle\textup{Perm }M

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}M_{1\pi(1)}M_{2\pi(2)}\cdots M_{n\pi(n)}

(2)

where
\textup{sgn}(\pi)=\pm 1
is the sign of the permutation
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

In the definition of Eq.(
1
) for the determinant,
the coefficient
\textup{sgn}(\pi)
of each summand is the character
of the alternating representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
;
in the definition of Eq.(
2
), the coefficients are all one
and so may be regarded as the values of the character
of the trivial representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

The
immanants
interpolate this construction by taking the
coefficients to be values of the characters
\chi^{\lambda}

of irreps of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
The irreps of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
are labelled
by partitions
\lambda
of
n
, with partitions denoted by
\lambda\vdash n
.
These are non-increasing sequences

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{p})
of positive
integer parts for which
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{p}=n
.
For later convenience, define
\textup{len}(\lambda)=p
.
Often, repeated parts of a partition are denoted using exponents:
for example,
(3,2^{3},1^{2})\equiv(3,2,2,2,1,1)
.
For
\lambda\vdash n
,
the immanant
\textup{Imm}^{\lambda}M
of an

n\times n
matrix
M
is defined by

\textup{Imm}^{\lambda}M=\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\chi^{\lambda}(\pi)M_{1\pi(1)}M_{2\pi(2)}\cdots M_{n\pi(n)}\,.

(3)

The partition
\lambda=(n)
labels the trivial irrep of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
and so
\textup{Imm}^{(n)}M=\textup{Perm }M
.
The partition
\lambda=(1^{n})
labels the alternating irrep of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
and so
\textup{Imm}^{(1^{n})}M=\textup{Det }M
.

For
n=3
, the only partition other than
(1^{3})
or
(3)
is
\lambda=(2,1)
, with corresponding immanant

\hskip-56.9055pt\textup{Imm}^{(2,1)}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}M_{11}&M_{12}&M_{13}\\
M_{21}&M_{22}&M_{23}\\
M_{31}&M_{32}&M_{33}\end{array}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{(2)}\,M_{11}M_{22}M_{33}+{(0)}\,M_{11}M_{23}M_{32}+{(-1)}\,M_{12}M_{23}M_{31}\\
\,+{(0)}\,M_{13}M_{22}M_{31}+{(-1)}\,M_{13}M_{21}M_{32}+{(0)}\,M_{12}M_{21}M_{33}\,.\end{array}\right.

(4)

Here, for clarity, the values of the character
\chi^{(2,1)}
have been
placed in parentheses.

3
The mean

In this work, for fixed
n\leq d
, we consider
n\times n
submatrices

M
of unitary matrices
U\in U(d)
.
We aim to understand the distribution of

|{\textup{Imm}}^{\lambda}M|^{2}
as
U
is sampled
with respect to the Haar measure of
U(d)
.

The following result giving the mean of

|{\textup{Imm}}^{\lambda}M|^{2}
is proved below in Section
5
.

Theorem 3.1

For
\lambda\vdash n
and
d\geq n
,

\int_{U(d)}dU\,|{\textup{Imm}}^{\lambda}M|^{2}=\frac{\dim({\lambda},\mathfrak{S}_{n})}{\dim({\lambda},U(d))}\,.

(5)

Eq.(
5
) is the ratio of dimensions of irreps
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
U(d)
, both labelled by the
same

partition
\lambda
.
By using known expressions for these dimensions,
we can obtain a rational polynomial expression for

\int_{U(d)}dU\,|\textup{Imm}^{\lambda}M|^{2}
for any
\lambda\vdash n
.

To give these dimension formulae, first define the
Young diagram

{\mathcal{Y}}^{\lambda}
of
\lambda\vdash n
to be the following
subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{2}
:

{\mathcal{Y}}^{\lambda}=\{(i,j):1\leq i\leq\textup{len}(\lambda),1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\}.

(6)

The Young diagram
{\mathcal{Y}}^{\lambda}
can be represented graphically
by placing a box at position
(i,j)
for each
(i,j)\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\lambda}
.

\lambda=(6,4,1)

\lambda

\lambda^{\prime}
is obtained by reading the column lengths of

For example,

Mean dominance

Given partitions
\lambda,\mu\vdash n
, write
\lambda\trianglelefteq\mu

if
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}

for
1\leq k\leq\min\{\textup{len}(\lambda),\textup{len}(\mu)\}
.
This defines a partial order on the set of partitions of
n
known
as the
dominance order
.
Note that, generally, it is not a total order, as illustrated
in
[
16
, §1.4.7]
.
Define
\lambda\lhd\mu
if
\lambda\trianglelefteq\mu

and
\lambda\neq\mu
.
It is easy to see
[
16
, §1.4.9]
that
if
\lambda\lhd\mu
,
then the diagram of
Y^{\mu}
is obtained from that of
Y^{\lambda}

by moving one or more boxes up and to the right.
Then, with
d
fixed, Eq.(
10
) gives
N^{\lambda}(d)<N^{\mu}(d)
.
Consequently, Eq.(
14
) implies that:

Proposition 4.1

If
\lambda,\mu\vdash n
with
\lambda\lhd\mu
then
for each
d\geq n
,

\int_{U(d)}dU\,|\textup{Imm}^{\lambda}M|^{2}>\int_{U(d)}dU\,|\textup{Imm}^{\mu}M|^{2}\,.

(18)

For the three partitions of
n=3
,
this result is illustrated in Fig.

Weingarten Calculus

The proof of Theorem
3.1
makes use of the following result,
a cornerstone of
Weingarten Calculus

[

]

Theorem 5.1

[
18
,
19
]

For fixed sequences

\bm{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
,

\bm{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})
,

\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m})
,

\bm{\ell}=(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m})

of elements of the set
\{1,2,\ldots,d\}

\int_{U(d)}dU\,U_{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots U_{i_{m}j_{m}}U^{\ast}_{k_{1}\ell_{1}}\cdots U^{\ast}_{k_{m}\ell_{m}}=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\delta_{\sigma}(\bm{i},\bm{j})\,\delta_{\tau}(\bm{k},\bm{\ell})\,W(\sigma\tau^{-1},d)

(19)

where

\delta_{\sigma}(\bm{i},\bm{j})=\prod_{s=1}^{m}\delta_{i_{\sigma(s)},j_{s}}

(20)

and for each
\rho\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}
,

W(\rho,d)
is defined by

W(\rho,d)=\frac{1}{(m!)^{2}}\sum_{\xi\vdash m}\frac{\dim({\xi},\mathfrak{S}_{m})^{2}}{\dim({\xi},U(d))}\,\chi^{\xi}(\rho)\,.

(21)

Using this we now have

where

and

Higher Moments

Moments, and in particular the second moment, are required in applications to demonstrate approximate average-case hardness of sampling through the anticoncentration of a output distribution
[
21
]
. Thus,
it is desirable to be able to calculate

\int_{U(d)}dU\,|\textup{Imm}^{\lambda}M|^{2t}

(27)

for all

5

t=1
case.
However, obtaining the values of (
27
) for
t>1
is much trickier.
Here, we concentrate on the
t=2
cases,
for which we have partial results.
To obtain these, we are led to consider Young subgroups of
\mathfrak{S}_{2n}

First, let
\mathfrak{S}_{2n}

Then, let

Each

Denote this

{J^{\lambda}}=\sum_{\sigma,\rho\in\mathfrak{H}_{n}}{J^{\lambda}(\sigma,\rho)}\quad\hbox{with}\quad{J^{\lambda}(\sigma,\rho)}=\sum_{\pi,\gamma\in\mathfrak{V}_{n}\\
{\sigma\pi\rho\gamma=\textup{i}d}}\!\!\hat{\chi}^{\lambda}(\pi)\,\hat{\chi}^{\lambda}(\gamma)\,.

Proposition 7.1

For
\lambda\vdash n
,

{J^{\lambda}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\frac{2}{1+\delta_{2\ell,n}}{n\choose\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose k}{n-\ell\choose k}J^{\lambda}\bigl(\epsilon_{[\ell]},\epsilon_{[\ell+k\backslash k]}\bigr)\,.

(36)

In addition,

\displaystyle J^{\lambda}\bigl(\epsilon_{[\ell]},\epsilon_{[\ell+k\backslash k]}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\alpha^{+},\beta^{+}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}\atop\alpha^{-},\beta^{-}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-\ell}}}\chi^{\lambda}\bigl(\theta_{\ell,k}(\alpha^{+}\!\oplus\alpha^{-})\bigr)\,\chi^{\lambda}\bigl(\theta_{\ell,k}(\beta^{+}\!\oplus\beta^{-})\bigr)\,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\chi^{\lambda}\bigl(\theta_{\ell,k}(\beta^{+}\!\oplus\alpha^{-})\bigr)\,\chi^{\lambda}\bigl(\theta_{\ell,k}(\alpha^{+}\!\oplus\beta^{-})\bigr)

(37)

where
\theta_{\ell,k}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the involution defined by

\theta_{\ell,k}(i)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i+\ell&\hbox{if $1\leq i\leq k$,}\\
i-\ell&\hbox{if $\ell+1\leq i\leq\ell+k$,}\\
i&\textup{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(38)

1.
Introduction

We investigate the relationship between three algebraic objects associated to graphs and their Galois covers. We may view a finite connected graph
X
as a discrete analogue of an algebraic curve, and define divisor theory on
X
in terms of a chip-firing operation (see
[
BN07
]
,
[
CP18
]
). The discrete analogue of the Jacobian variety of a curve is the
Jacobian group

\Jac(X)
, also known as the
critical group
of
X
in the combinatorics literature. It is a finite abelian group whose order is equal to the number of spanning trees of
X
. On the other hand, we may view
X
as a combinatorial version of a number field, and define primes as certain equivalence classes of closed paths on
X
. By analogy with the Dedekind zeta function of a number field, we define the
Ihara zeta function

\zeta(s,X)
(see
[
Bas92
]
), and
\zeta(s,X)
determines
|\Jac(X)|
by an appropriate version of the class number formula
[
Nor98
]
. Finally, we consider the
graphic matroid

\mathcal{M}(X)
on the edge set of
X
. The bases of
\mathcal{M}(X)
are the spanning trees, so that
|\Jac(X)|
is the number of bases of
\mathcal{M}(X)
.

The discrete analogue of a finite map of algebraic curves is a
harmonic morphism
of graphs
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
(introduced in
[
Ura00
]
), which is required to satisfy a balancing condition (
2
) at the vertices of
\widetilde{X}
in terms of a local degree function
d_{p}:V(\widetilde{X})\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Among other things, a harmonic morphism
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
defines a surjective pushforward map
p_{*}:\Jac(\widetilde{X})\to\Jac(X)
on the Jacobian groups. A natural question is to determine the order
|\Ker p_{*}|=|\Jac(\widetilde{X})|/|\Jac(X)|
of the kernel, or, alternatively, to compare the number of spanning trees of
\widetilde{X}
and
X
. Since
|\Jac(X)|
can be computed using either the Ihara zeta function
\zeta(s,X)
or the graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(X)
, we may expect that appropriate generalizations determine the order of the kernel.

This question is difficult to approach in full generality, and we restrict our attention to two distinguished classes of harmonic morphisms. First, let
G
be a finite group. A harmonic morphism
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
is said to be a
Galois cover
with Galois group
G
if
X
is the quotient of
\widetilde{X}
by a
G
-action that is free on the edges (see Definition
2.3
for a precise statement). In our paper, we generally further restrict our attention to the case when
G
is abelian. Second, we say that a harmonic morphism
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
is
free
if all local degrees of
p
are equal to 1. A free harmonic morphism of graphs is the same as a covering space in the topological sense, and free Galois covers are those that are defined by free group actions.

Let
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a free Galois cover with Galois group
G
, not necessarily abelian. In
[
ST96
]
and
[
ST00
]
, Stark and Terras defined the
Artin–Ihara
L
-functions

L(s,\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
of the cover, indexed by representations
\rho
of
G
, and showed that the Ihara zeta function
\zeta(\widetilde{X},s)
factors as a product of the
L(s,\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
over the irreducible representations. Since
\zeta(s,\widetilde{X})
determines
|\Jac(\widetilde{X})|
, this gives a formula for
|\Ker p_{*}|
in terms of the
L
-functions (see
[
HMSV24
]
). On the other hand, in
[
RT14
]
Reiner and Tseng investigated the subgroup
\Ker p_{*}
in detail, and found a matroidal formula for its order when the Galois group is
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
(this formula was rediscovered in the context of double covers of metric graphs in
[
LZ22
]
). In this case, the cover determines the structure of a
signed graph

\sigma:E(X)\to\{\pm 1\}
on
X
, and the order of
\Ker p_{*}
is a weighted sum over the bases of Zaslavsky’s
signed graphic matroid

\mathcal{M}(X,\sigma)
(see Proposition 9.9 in
[
RT14
]
).

The main theorem of our paper is a matroidal formula for the order of
\Ker p_{*}
in the case when
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
is an abelian Galois cover, not necessarily free. We employ the theory of Artin–Ihara
L
-functions for the proof, but the formula for
|\Ker p_{*}|
is given in terms of weights of certain matroids
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
associated to the cover and a representation
\rho
of the Galois group.

In order to state our results, we first recall how to construct abelian Galois covers. Free Galois covers of a fixed graph
X
with abelian Galois group
G
are described by elementary algebraic topology: any such cover
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
is given by an element
[\eta]\in H^{1}(X,G)
of the simplicial cohomology group of
X
with coefficients in
G
(the element
\eta
is called a
G
-voltage assignment
in the combinatorics literature). This description was generalized to harmonic abelian covers in
[
LUZ24
]
. Given such a cover
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
, the abelian Galois group
G
acts on the fiber
p^{-1}(v)
over each vertex
v\in V(X)
, and the stabilizer of any vertex in the fiber
p^{-1}(v)
is a subgroup
D(v)\subseteq G
depending only on
v
, called the
dilation group
of
v
. The collection of dilation groups defines a
G
-dilation datum

D=\{D(v):v\in V(X)\}
on
X
, and isomorphism classes of harmonic covers with Galois group
G
and fixed dilation datum
D
are in bijection with a certain
dilated cohomology group
H^{1}(X,D)
.

On the other hand, Galois covers of graphs have a natural matroidal aspect. Given a free Galois cover
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
defined by a
G
-voltage assignment
[\eta]\in H^{1}(X,G)
, Zaslavsky defines in
[
Zas89
,
Zas91
]
a
bias matroid

\mathcal{M}(X,\eta)
on the set of edges of
X
(for
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, this is Zaslavsky’s signed graphic matroid of
[
Zas82
]
). In this paper, we extend Zaslavsky’s construction in two elementary ways. First, we reinterpret Zaslavsky’s matroid geometrically in terms of the cover
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
and show how to define a matroid
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X}/X)
on the edge set of
X
when
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
is a harmonic abelian cover, not necessarily free. Second, we twist the definition of
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X}/X)
by a character
\rho
of
G
to obtain a collection of matroids
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
indexed by the
|G|-1
nontrivial characters of
G
. It is more convenient to work with the dual matroids
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
, and we equip their bases with certain
weights
(which are valued, generally speaking, in the real cyclotomic field). The
weight

w(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho))
of the matroid is the sum of the weights of the bases.

We are now ready to state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a harmonic Galois cover of graphs with abelian Galois group
G
of order

|\Jac(\widetilde{X})|=\frac{1}{N}\prod_{v\in V(X)}|D(v)|^{N/|D(v)|}|\Jac(X)|\prod_{\rho\in\widehat{G}^{\prime}}w(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)),

(1)

where

is the dilation group at the vertex

is the number of spanning trees of

To prove Theorem

, we first upgrade the critical number

of a graph to the

2.
Graphs, harmonic morphisms, and Galois covers

2.1.
Graphs and harmonic morphisms

We begin by reviewing a number of basic definitions relating to graphs and their morphisms. We use Serre’s definition of a graph (see
[
Ser02
]
), which is particularly convenient for considering group actions and quotients.

A
graph

X
consists of the following data:

(1)

A finite set of
vertices

V(X)
.

(2)

A finite set of
half-edges

H(X)
.

(3)

A fixed-point-free involution
H(X)\to H(X)
denoted by
h\mapsto\overline{h}
.

(4)

A root map
r:H(X)\to V(X)
.

An
edge

e=\{h,\overline{h}\}
of
X
is an orbit of the involution, and the set of edges is denoted by
E(X)
. The
root vertices
of an edge
e=\{h,\overline{h}\}
are
r(h)
and
r(\overline{h})
, and an edge is called a
loop
if its root vertices coincide. We allow
multiedges
, in other words distinct edges may have the same root vertices. An
oriented edge

e=(h,\overline{h})
is an edge together with a choice of ordering of its half-edges, which determines the
source
and
target
vertices
s(e)=r(h)
and
t(e)=r(\overline{h})
. We denote the opposite orientation by
\overline{e}=(\overline{h},h)
. An
orientation
on a graph
X
is a choice of orientation for every edge, including the loops, and determines global source and target maps
s:E(X)\to V(X)
and
t:E(X)\to V(X)
. We consider only finite graphs, and all graphs are connected unless stated otherwise.

The
tangent space
to a vertex
v\in V(X)
is

T_{v}(X)=\{h\in H(X):r(h)=v\},

and the
valency

\val(v)=|T_{v}(X)|
is the number of incoming edges, with loops counted twice. The
genus
of a graph is

g(X)=|E(X)|-|V(X)|+1;

this is not to be confused with the minimum genus of an orientable surface into which
X
embeds.

A
morphism
of graphs
f:\widetilde{X}\to X
is a pair of maps
f:V(\widetilde{X})\to V(X)
and
f:H(\widetilde{X})\to H(X)
that are equivariant with respect to the involution and root maps. A morphism of graphs maps
f:\widetilde{X}\to X
induces a map on the edges
f:E(\widetilde{X})\to E(X)
, so in our definition edges may not be contracted to vertices. A morphism of graphs
f:\widetilde{X}\to X
is called
harmonic
if there exists a
local degree
function
d_{f}:V(\widetilde{X})\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
satisfying the
local balancing condition
: for any vertex
\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{X})
and any half-edge
h\in T_{f(\widetilde{v})}X
we have

d_{f}(\widetilde{v})=\left|T_{\widetilde{v}}(\widetilde{X})\cap f^{-1}(h)\right|.

(2)

If
X
is connected, then a harmonic morphism
f:\widetilde{X}\to X
has a
global degree
given by

\deg f=\sum_{\widetilde{v}\in f^{-1}(v)}d_{f}(\widetilde{v})=|f^{-1}(e)|

for any vertex
v\in V(X)
or any edge
e\in E(X)
. A harmonic morphism
f
is called
free
if
d_{f}(\widetilde{v})=1
for every vertex
\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{X})
. The local balancing condition implies that a free harmonic morphism is a local homeomorphism near every vertex, hence a free harmonic morphism of graphs is the same thing as a covering in the topological sense.

2.2.
Group actions and harmonic
G
-covers

We now recall the theory of harmonic
G
-covers of a graph with abelian Galois group
G
, which was developed in
[
LUZ24
]
. These covers are a generalization of free Galois covers, described by covering space theory, and are obtained from arbitrary (not necessarily free) group actions on graphs.

Let
X
be a graph. An
automorphism
of
X
is an invertible graph morphism
f:X\to X
. An automorphism is harmonic with local degree
d_{f}(v)=1
for all
v\in V(X)
and global degree
\deg f=1
. Conversely, any harmonic morphism of global degree equal to
1
is an automorphism. The automorphisms of a graph
X
form a group denoted by
\Aut(X)
. An automorphism of a graph may
flip edges
, in other words may exchange the two half-edges comprising an edge, fixing the edge but exchanging its root vertices. We cannot take the quotient of a graph by such an automorphism
1
1

1

A quotient by an automorphism that flips edges may be taken in the category of
graphs with legs
, where a leg is a half-edge that is fixed by the involution. We do not develop this perspective here, and refer the interested reader to
[
MZ23
]
.
, so we exclude them from consideration.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a graph and let
G
be a finite group. A
left
G
-action
on
X
is a homomorphism
G\to\Aut(X)
such that, for every
g\in G
and every edge
e=\{h,\overline{h}\}\in E(X)
, one of the following holds:

(1)

g(h)=h
, hence
g(\overline{h})=\overline{h}
and therefore
g(e)=e
.

(2)

g(h)\notin\{h,\overline{h}\}
, and therefore
g(e)\neq e
.

Given a graph
X
with a left
G
-action, we define the
quotient graph

X/G
to be the set of orbits of the
G
-action. Specifically, the vertices and half-edges of
X/G
are the orbit sets

V(X/G)=V(X)/G,\quad H(X/G)=H(X)/G,

and the root
H(X/G)\to V(X/G)
and involution
H(X/G)\to H(X/G)
maps on
X/G
are induced from those on
X
. Since the action does not flip edges, the involution map on
X/G
has no fixed points, therefore
X/G
is a graph.

We now consider two distinguished classes of group actions.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a graph with a left
G
-action.

(1)

We say that the
G
-action is
free on the edges
if
g(h)\neq h
for all
h\in H(X)
and all nontrivial elements
g\in G
(equivalently, if
g(e)\neq e
for all
e\in E(X)
and all nontrivial
g\in G
).

(2)

We say that the
G
-action is
free
if
g(v)\neq v
for all
v\in V(X)
and all nontrivial
g\in G
.

Let
X
be a graph with a left
G
-action. The canonical projection
p:X\to X/G
sending each vertex and half-edge to its orbit is a graph morphism, but it is not in general harmonic. However, if the action is free on the edges, then
p
is harmonic if we set the local degrees to be

d_{p}(v)=|\Stab(v)|=|\{g\in G:g(v)=v\}|.

for all
v\in V(X)
. The orbit-stabilizer theorem implies the local balancing condition (
2
) at every vertex of
G
and that
p
is a harmonic morphism of global degree
\deg p=|G|
. If in addition the
G
-action is free, then
d_{p}(v)=1
for all
v\in V(X)
and
p
is a covering space.

We now consider the morphism
p
from the perspective of the quotient graph.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a graph and let
G
be a finite group. A
Galois cover
of
X
with Galois group
G
, or
harmonic
G
-cover
for short, is a harmonic morphism
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
of degree
\deg p=|G|
together with a
G
-action on
\widetilde{X}
such that the following conditions hold:

(1)

The
G
-action is equivariant: for every
g\in G
,
p(g(\widetilde{e}))=\widetilde{e}
for each edge
\widetilde{e}\in E(\widetilde{X})
and
p(g(\widetilde{v}))=\widetilde{v}
as well as
d_{p}(g(\widetilde{v}))=d_{p}(\widetilde{v})
for each vertex
\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{X})
.

(2)

For each
v\in V(X)
,
G
acts transitively on the fiber
p^{-1}(v)
, and for each
e\in E(X)
,
G
acts transitively and freely on the fiber
p^{-1}(e)
.

A harmonic
G
-cover is called
free
if it is free as a harmonic morphism, equivalently, if the
G
-action is free on the vertices as well as the edges. The
trivial
G
-cover
is the free disconnected cover
p:X\times G\to X
, where
p
is projection onto the first factor. An
isomorphism of harmonic
G
-covers

p:\widetilde{X}\to X
and
p^{\prime}:\widetilde{X}^{\prime}\to X
is a
G
-equivariant isomorphism of graphs
i:\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{X}^{\prime}
such that
p=p^{\prime}\circ i
.

It is clear that the two objects defined above are the same. Indeed, if
X
is a graph with a left
G
-action that is free on the edges, then the quotient map
p:X\to X/G
is a harmonic
G
-cover. Conversely, if
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
is a harmonic
G
-cover, then there is an isomorphism
X\simeq\widetilde{X}/G
whose composition with
f
is the quotient morphism
\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{X}/G
. Furthermore, free
G
-actions correspond to free harmonic
G
-covers (having local degrees
d_{p}(\widetilde{v})=1
for all
\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{X})
), which explains the choice of terminology.

Let

p^{-1}(v)=\{\widetilde{v}_{g}:g\in G\},\quad g(\widetilde{v}_{g^{\prime}})=\widetilde{v}_{g+g^{\prime}},

Now choose an orientation on

\quad p^{-1}(e)=\{\widetilde{e}_{g}:g\in G\},\quad g(\widetilde{e}_{g^{\prime}})=\widetilde{e}_{g+g^{\prime}},\quad s(\widetilde{e}_{g})=\widetilde{s(e)}_{g},\quad t(\widetilde{e}_{g})=\widetilde{t(e)}_{g+\eta_{e}}.

(4)

The collection of elements

is called the

-voltage assignment
associated to the free

-cover

with

, which can be conveniently described in terms of simplicial cohomology. Let

C^{0}(X,G)=G^{V(X)},\quad C^{1}(X,G)=G^{E(X)}

and

\xi:V(X)\to G

s^{*}(\xi)(e)=\xi(s(e)),\quad t^{*}(\xi)(e)=\xi(t(e)),

and the first simplicial cohomology group

is the cokernel of the

\delta^{*}:C^{0}(X,G)\to C^{1}(X,G),\quad\delta^{*}=t^{*}-s^{*},\quad H^{1}(X,G)=G^{E(X)}/\Im\delta^{*}.

The

-voltage assignment

2.4.
Contraction and resolution

A simple but important technical tool for us is the operation of
edge contraction
. Given a set of edges
F\subseteq E(X)
of a graph
X
, the
induced subgraph

X[F]\subseteq X
is the minimal subgraph of
X
containing
F
, in other words
E(X[F])=F
and
V(X[F])=r(F)
. Now let
X[F]=X_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup X_{k}
be the decomposition of
X[F]
into connected components. The
contraction

X_{F}
of
X
along
F
is the graph obtained by contracting each
X_{i}
to a separate vertex
v_{i}
. Specifically,

V(X_{F})=[V(X)\backslash V(X[F])]\cup\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\},\quad E(X_{F})=E(X)\backslash F,

and any edge
e\in E(X)\backslash F
that in
X
is rooted at a vertex of one of the
X_{i}
is instead rooted at the corresponding vertex
v_{i}
of
X_{F}
.

We can likewise define the contraction of a harmonic morphism along a set of edges of the target graph. Let
f:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a harmonic morphism, and let
F\subseteq E(X)
. The
contraction

f_{F}:\widetilde{X}_{f^{-1}(F)}\to X_{F}
of
f
along
F
is defined as follows. First, we contract the edges
F
of
X
and their preimages

in
\widetilde{X}
to obtain the graphs
X_{F}
and
\widetilde{X}_{f^{-1}(F)}
, respectively. Outside of the contracted vertices of
\widetilde{X}_{f^{-1}(F)}
, the map
f_{F}
is the restriction of
f
. Now let
X_{i}\subseteq X
be a connected component of
X[F]
, and let
f^{-1}(X_{i})=\widetilde{X}_{i1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\widetilde{X}_{ik_{i}}
be the connected components of its preimage. Each
\widetilde{X}_{ij}
corresponds to a contracted vertex
\widetilde{v}_{ij}\in V(\widetilde{X}_{f^{-1}(F)})
, and we set
f_{F}(\widetilde{v}_{ij})=v_{i}
, where
v_{i}\in V(X_{F})
is the vertex corresponding to
X_{i}
. It is elementary to check that
f_{F}
is a harmonic morphism, provided that we set
d_{f_{F}}(\widetilde{v}_{ij})=\deg f|_{\widetilde{X}_{ij}}
, in other words the local degree of
f_{F}
at
\widetilde{v}_{ij}
is equal to the global degree of
f
on the connected graph
\widetilde{X}_{ij}
.

Finally, let
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a harmonic
G
-cover and let
F\subseteq E(X)
be a set of edges of the target. We can naturally equip the contraction

with the structure of a harmonic
G
-cover. Indeed, for a vertex
v\in V(X)\backslash V(X[F])
the fibers
p^{-1}(v)
and
p_{F}^{-1}(v)
are the same, which defines the
G
-action on
p_{F}^{-1}(v)
. The same is true for any edge

of the contracted graph. Finally, let
v_{i}\in V(X_{F})
be a vertex corresponding to a connected component

of

. Automorphisms preserve connectivity, therefore

acts on the connected components
\widetilde{X}_{ij}
of

, and the action is transitive because it is so on the fiber over any vertex of
X_{i}
. This defines the
G
-action on the fiber
p_{F}^{-1}(v_{i})
.

We now show that any harmonic

-cover

of

3.
Jacobian polynomials and zeta functions

In this section, we recall the theory of chip-firing on a graph
X
, the critical group of
X
(which we call the
Jacobian group
and denote by
\Jac(X)
), and the relationship to the spanning trees of
X
. Our main tool for computing the order of
\Jac(X)
(which is the number of spanning trees of
X
) is the Ihara zeta function
\zeta(s,X)
, and we recall the theory of zeta functions and the more general Artin–Ihara
L
-functions of free
G
-covers.

3.1.
Chip-firing, the graph Laplacian, and the Jacobian group

Let
\Div(X)=\mathbb{Z}^{V(X)}
be the free abelian group on the vertices of a graph
X
. An element of
\Div(X)
is called a
divisor
and may interpreted as an assignment of a number of chips (positive, negative, or zero) to each vertex of
X
. The
Laplacian operator

L
of
X
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear operator on
\mathbb{Z}^{V(X)}
defined as follows. Let
n
be the number of vertices of
X
, and define the
n\times n

valency
and
adjacency
matrices
Q
and
A
as follows:

Q_{uv}=\begin{cases}\val(u),&u=v,\\
0,&u\neq v,\end{cases}\quad A_{uv}=\mbox{number of edges between }u\mbox{ and }v.

(13)

We note that each loop at a vertex
u
contributes
2
to both
Q_{uu}
and
A_{uu}
. The matrix of the Laplacian operator is the difference of the valency and adjacency matrices:

L:\mathbb{Z}^{V(X)}\to\Div(X),\quad L_{uv}=Q_{uv}-A_{uv}.

Specifically, for
v\in V(X)
the divisor
-L(v)
is obtained by
firing
the vertex
v
, in other words by moving a chip from
v
along each edge
e\in T_{v}X
to its other root vertex (the chip returns to
v
if
e
is a loop). The image of
L
is the subgroup of
principal divisors
, which lies in
\Div_{0}(X)
, the subgroup of divisors with total number of chips is equal to zero. The corresponding quotient is a finite group called the
Jacobian
, or
critical group
, of
X
:

\Jac(X)=\Div_{0}(X)/\Im L.

Given a morphism
f:\widetilde{X}\to X
of graphs, we define a
pushforward map

f_{*}:\Div(\widetilde{X})\to\Div(X),\quad f_{*}\left[\sum_{\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{X})}a_{\widetilde{v}}\widetilde{v}\right]=\sum_{\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{X})}a_{\widetilde{v}}f(\widetilde{v}).

This map does not in general respect chip-firing, and there is no relationship between
\Jac(\widetilde{X})
and
\Jac(X)
. However, if
f
is a harmonic morphism, then

(f_{*}\circ L_{\widetilde{X}})(\widetilde{v})=d_{f}(\widetilde{v})(L_{X}\circ f_{*})(\widetilde{v})

for any
\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{X})
, where
L_{\widetilde{X}}
and
L_{X}
are the Laplacians of
\widetilde{X}
and
X
, respectively. Hence the image of a principal divisor is principal, and
f_{*}
induces a homomorphism on the Jacobian groups:

f_{*}:\Jac(\widetilde{X})\to\Jac(X).

The map
f_{*}
is surjective and hence

|\Ker f_{*}|=\frac{|\Jac(\widetilde{X})|}{|\Jac(X)|}.

The purpose of this paper is to evaluate this quantity in the case when
f
is an abelian harmonic
G
-cover.

3.2.
Kirchhoff’s theorem and the Jacobian polynomial

The order of the Jacobian of a graph
X
can be computed from the Laplacian matrix
L
, which is always singular: its rows sum to zero and so do its columns. Kirchhoff’s matrix tree theorem states that any cofactor of
L
is equal to the number of spanning trees of
X
. It is elementary to see that this quantity is also the order of the Jacobian group of
X
:

|\Jac(X)|=\mbox{number of spanning trees of }X=\sum_{T\subseteq X}1,

(14)

where the sum is taken over all spanning trees
T
of
X
.

We gain a certain flexibility by converting this quantity into a polynomial. Let
x_{e}
be a variable for each edge
e\in E(X)
, representing the length of
e
. We now weigh the terms in the sum (
14
) by the products of the lengths of the complementary edges:

Definition 3.1
.

The
Jacobian polynomial
of a graph
X
is

J_{X}(x_{e})=\sum_{T\subseteq X}\prod_{e\in E(X)\backslash E(T)}x_{e}\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{e}]_{e\in E(X)},

(15)

where the sum is taken over all spanning trees
T
of
X
.

The number of edges in the complement of any spanning tree of
X
is equal to the genus
g(X)
, hence
J(X)
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
g(X)
with integer coefficients. Kirchhoff’s matrix tree theorem (
14
) then states that

|\Jac(X)|=J_{X}(1).

More generally, let
\underline{n}=\{n_{e}\}_{e\in E(X)}
be positive integers indexed by the edges of
X
, and let
X_{\underline{n}}
be the graph obtained from
X
by replacing each edge
e\in E(X)
with a chain of
n_{e}
edges. A moment’s thought shows that plugging
x_{e}=n_{e}
into the Jacobian polynomial of
X
counts the spanning trees of
X_{\underline{n}}
, so

|\Jac(X_{\underline{n}})|=\mbox{number of spanning trees of }X_{\underline{n}}=J_{X}(n_{e}).

(16)

In other words,
J_{X}
computes the order of the Jacobian of any graph whose topological type is the same as
X
.

Even more generally, let
\ell:E(X)\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be arbitrary real numbers, and let
\Xi=(X,\ell)
be the
metric graph
obtained by identifying each edge
e\in E(X)
with a real interval of length
\ell(e)
. The theory of chip-firing on metric graphs is developed analogously, where the divisors may now be supported on the interior points of edges. We do not review this theory here, and the interested reader is referred to
[
MZ08
]
. We only note that the Jacobian
\Jac(\Xi)
of a metric graph
\Xi=(X,\ell)
is a real torus of dimension
g(X)
, and has a canonically defined volume that is computed by the Jacobian polynomial:

Proposition 3.2

(Theorem 5.2 in
[
ABKS14
]
)
.

The volume of the Jacobian
\Jac(\Xi)
of a metric graph
\Xi=(X,\ell)
is given by

\Vol^{2}(\Jac(\Xi))=\sum_{T\subseteq X}\prod_{e\in E(X)\backslash E(T)}\ell(e)=J_{X}(\ell(e)).

Finally, we have the following useful lemma, which shows that Jacobian polynomials behave well under edge contraction.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a graph, let
e\in E(X)
be an edge, and let
X_{e}
be the graph obtained by contracting
e
. The Jacobian polynomial of
X_{e}
is equal to

J_{X_{e}}=\begin{cases}J_{X}|_{x_{e}=0},&e\mbox{ is not a loop,}\\
J_{X}/x_{e},&e\mbox{ is a loop.}\end{cases}

(17)

Proof.

If
e
is not a loop, then there is a natural bijection between the spanning trees of
X
containing
e
and the spanning trees of
X_{e}
, and setting
x_{e}=0
removes the terms of
J_{X}
corresponding to the spanning trees of
X
that do not contain
e
. If
e
is a loop, then it is contained in the complement of any spanning tree of
X
, so
J_{X}
divides
x_{e}
. The graph
X_{e}
has the same set of spanning trees, but without
e
as a complementary edge, hence
J_{X_{e}}=J_{X}/x_{e}
.

3.3.
Zeta functions and
L
-functions

Our calculations of Jacobian polynomials in
G
-covers are based on the theory of zeta functions (for an individual graph
X
) and
L
-functions (for a free
G
-cover
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
). These functions are defined as products over the set of primes of
X
, which are equivalence classes of certain closed paths on
X
. We recall this theory, following
[
Ter10
]
, and to simplify exposition we consider only abelian
G
.

Let
X
be a graph. A
path
on
X
is a sequence
P=e_{1}\cdots e_{k}
of oriented edges such that
t(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
for all
i=1,\ldots,k-1
. We say that
P
is
closed
if
t(e_{k})=s(e_{1})
, and a closed path
P
is
reduced
if
e_{i+1}\neq\overline{e}_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,k-1
and
e_{k}\neq\overline{e}_{1}
. A closed reduced path
P
is called
primitive
if
P\neq Q^{c}
for any closed path
Q
and any
c\geq 2
. Given a closed reduced path
P=e_{1}\cdots e_{n}
, any path of the form

of

is an equivalence class
[P]
of a primitive path

. We denote by
\mathfrak{P}(X)
the set of primes of

We consider three zeta functions associated to a graph

is the most general one, but mostly plays an auxiliary role. The

is the most important for us, and is obtained from the edge zeta by specialization. Further specializing, we obtain the

Definition 3.4
.

Let

be a graph.

(1)

Let

\zeta(s,X)=\prod_{[P]\in\mathfrak{P}(X)}\left(1-s^{L(P)}\right)^{-1},

It is clear that the edge zeta function specializes to the metric zeta function, which in turn specializes to the Ihara zeta function:

\zeta_{M}(s,x_{e},X)=\left.\zeta_{E}(w_{ef},X)\right|_{w_{ef}=s^{x_{e}}},\quad\zeta(s,X)=\left.\zeta_{M}(s,x_{e},X)\right|_{x_{e}=1}.

(18)

3.4.
The zeta function and the Jacobian

We now express the Jacobian polynomial of a graph in terms of its metric zeta function. First, we recall a theorem of Northshield
[
Nor98
]
computing the order of the Jacobian in terms of the Ihara zeta function.

Proposition 3.11
.

The reciprocal of the Ihara zeta function of a graph
X
of genus
g
has the following Taylor expansion at
s=1
:

\zeta(s,X)^{-1}=2^{g}(-1)^{g+1}(g-1)|\Jac(X)|(s-1)^{g}+O\left((s-1)^{g+1}\right).

(27)

In particular, if
g\geq 2
then
\zeta(s,X)^{-1}
has a zero of order
g
at
s=1
, and the leading order term computes
|\Jac(X)|
. We now upgrade Northshield’s result to compute the Jacobian polynomial in terms of the metric zeta function, by sneaking in the edge lengths
x_{e}
.

Proposition 3.12
.

The reciprocal of the metric zeta function of a graph
X
of genus
g
has the following Taylor expansion at
s=1
:

\zeta_{M}(s,x_{e},X)^{-1}=2^{g}(-1)^{g+1}(g-1)J_{X}(x_{e})(s-1)^{g}+O\left((s-1)^{g+1}\right).

(28)

Proof.

The two-term determinant formula (
19
) and the specialization (
18
) imply that
\zeta_{M}(s,x_{e},X)^{-1}
is a polynomial in the quantities
s^{x_{e}}
. We need to compute the derivatives

Z_{k}(x_{e})=\left.\frac{\partial^{k}(\zeta_{M}(s,x_{e},X)^{-1})}{\partial s^{k}}\right|_{s=1}

for
k=0,\ldots,g
. Using the power rule, we see that
Z_{k}(x_{e})
is a degree
k
polynomial in the
x_{e}
, which we can determine by finding its values for all integer inputs.

As in (
16
), let
\underline{n}=\{n_{e}\}_{e\in E(X)}
be positive integers, and let
X_{\underline{n}}
be the graph obtained from
X
by replacing each edge
e\in E(X)
with a chain of
n_{e}
edges. There is a one-to-one correspondence between primes on
X
and
X_{\underline{n}}
, but the path lengths on the latter are counted using the
n_{e}
. In other words, we compute the Ihara zeta function of
X_{\underline{n}}
by substituting
x_{e}=n_{e}
into the metric zeta function of
X
:

\zeta(x,X_{\underline{n}})=\zeta_{M}(s,n_{e},X).

Equation (
28
) then implies that for
k=0,\ldots,g-1
we have

Z_{k}(n_{e})=\left.\frac{\partial^{k}(\zeta_{M}(s,n_{e},X)^{-1})}{\partial s^{k}}\right|_{s=1}=\left.\frac{\partial^{k}(\zeta(x,X_{\underline{n}})^{-1})}{\partial s^{k}}\right|_{s=1}=0.

Since the
n_{e}
are arbitrary positive integers, it follows that
Z_{k}(x_{e})=0
for
k=0,\ldots,g-1
. Similarly, using (
28
) and (
16
) we see that

Z_{g}(n_{e})=\left.\frac{\partial^{g}(\zeta_{M}(s,n_{e},X)^{-1})}{\partial s^{k}}\right|_{s=1}=\left.\frac{\partial^{g}(\zeta(x,X_{\underline{n}})^{-1})}{\partial s^{k}}\right|_{s=1}=g!2^{g}(-1)^{g+1}(g-1)J_{X}(n_{e}),

therefore the left and right hand sides of this equality remain equal if the integers
n_{e}
are replaced by the variables
x_{e}
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
The matroid of a Galois harmonic cover

We now introduce our last technical tool, namely matroids. We do not review them here and refer the reader to a standard reference such as
[
Oxl11
]
, and in any case we only use the elementary definitions of bases, independent sets, contraction, and duality.

The starting point is the following observation. The Jacobian polynomial
J_{X}
of a graph
X
is a sum (
15
) over the spanning trees
T
of
X
. Hence
J_{X}
is determined by the graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}(X)
, whose bases are the spanning trees, or perhaps more accurately by the dual cographic matroid
\mathcal{M}^{*}(X)
, since in (
15
) we take products of the complementary edge lengths to spanning trees. Given a harmonic
G
-cover
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
, the order of
\Jac(\widetilde{X})
divides that of
\Jac(X)
by Lagrange’s theorem, therefore we may expect the Jacobian polynomial
J_{\widetilde{X}}
to be divisible by
J_{X}
. We show that this is indeed the case, and that the factors of the quotient are likewise expressible in terms of a matroid.

In
[
Zas82
]
, Zaslavsky associated a matroid
\mathcal{M}(X,\sigma)
to a
signed graph

(X,\sigma)
, where
\sigma:E(X)\to\{\pm 1\}
is a distribution of positive or negative signs on the edges of
X
. He then generalized this construction (see
[
Zas89
]
and
[
Zas91
]
) to associate a
bias matroid

\mathcal{M}(X,\eta)
to a
gain graph
, which, in our terminology, is a graph
X
together with a fixed
G
-voltage assignment
[\eta]\in H^{1}(X,G)
(the case of signed graphs corresponds to
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
). To define the contractions of
\mathcal{M}(X,\eta)
in Zaslavsky’s framework, it is necessary to introduce additional objects called
half edges
and
loose edges
. In this section, we give an elementary interpretation of Zaslavsky’s matroid in terms of the free
G
-cover
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
associated to the pair
(X,\eta)
. We then show that this interpretation works for dilated
G
-covers as well, and obviates the need to introduce new edge types. As in the rest of the paper, we assume
G
to be abelian, but our results readily generalize to non-abelian groups.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a harmonic
G
-cover. A subgraph
Y\subseteq X
is called
G
-nontrivial
if the restricted cover
p|_{p^{-1}(Y)}:p^{-1}(Y)\to Y
is not isomorphic to the trivial
G
-cover.

We now imagine removing edges from
X
one by one. This may split
X
into connected components, and eventually (unless each vertex of
X
is dilated) we will obtain a connected component that is
G
-trivial. We consider all ways of removing edges from
X
such that this does not happen.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a nontrivial harmonic
G
-cover. The subsets

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X))=\{F\subseteq E(X):\mbox{each connected component of }X\backslash F\mbox{ is $G$-nontrivial}\}

(29)

are the independent sets of a matroid
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X)
with ground set
E(X)
and rank

r(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X))=g(X)-1+\left|\left\{v\in V(X):D(v)\neq 0\right\}\right|.

(30)

Proof.

Since we are primarily interested in bases, we use
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X)
and its dual
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X}/X)
interchangeably. We refer to
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X}/X)
as the
matroid of the
G
-cover

p:\widetilde{X}\to X
. We prove that
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X)
is a matroid by identifying its dual
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X}/X)
with Zaslavsky’s matroid
\mathcal{M}(X,\eta)
in the case when
p
is free, and with its contractions for arbitrary
p
.

We first give an explicit description of the bases of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X)
, in other words the maximal elements of
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X))
. For this, we need to identify the minimal
G
-nontrivial subgraphs
Y\subseteq X
. Let
[\eta]\in H^{1}(X,D)
be the dilated
G
-voltage assignment defining
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
. If
Y
contains a dilated vertex, then
Y
is
G
-nontrivial by definition. If
Y
has no dilated vertices, then the restricted cover
p|_{p^{-1}(Y)}:p^{-1}(Y)\to Y
is free and is defined by the
G
-voltage assignment
[\eta|_{Y}]\in H^{1}(Y,G)
. The restricted cover
p|_{p^{-1}(Y)}
is not isomorphic to the trivial cover if and only if
[\eta|_{Y}]\neq 0
, which happens if and only if there exists a cycle
C\subseteq Y
such that the oriented sum of the
\eta_{e}
along
e\in E(C)
(the Frobenius element of
C
in the terminology of Section
3
) is not the identity.

Now let
Y\subseteq X
be
G
-nontrivial. If
Y
has two dilated vertices, then we can remove an edge on any path connecting them. If
Y
has one dilated vertex and
g(Y)\geq 1
, then we can remove an edge from
Y
without disconnecting it. If
Y
has no dilation but
g(Y)\geq 2
, then we can remove an edge without disconnecting
Y
, while also preserving a cycle with nontrivial voltage. In all cases, the resulting connected components are also
G
-nontrivial. Hence we have proved the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
F\subseteq E(X)
and let
X\backslash F=X_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup X_{k}
be the decomposition into connected components. Then
F
is a basis of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X)
if and only if each
X_{i}
is one of the following:

(1)

A graph of genus one having no dilated vertices and such that the sum
\eta(X_{i})
of the voltages along the unique cycle of
X_{i}
is not the identity.

(2)

A tree containing a unique dilated vertex.

If
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
is a free
G
-cover, then there are no dilated vertices, so a basis of
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X},X)
(in other words, the complement of a basis of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X)
) is a union of genus one subgraphs of
X
, spanning all vertices and having nontrivial voltages along their unique circuits. By Theorem 2.1(g) in
[
Zas89
]
, this is one of the cryptomorphic definitions of Zaslavsky’s bias matroid, which proves that (
29
) indeed defines a matroid on
E(X)
.

We now consider an arbitrary harmonic
G
-cover
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
. By Lemma
2.9
, there exists a free
G
-cover
p_{f}:\widetilde{X}_{f}\to X_{f}
such that
p
is obtained from
p_{f}
by contracting a set of edges
F_{f}\subset E(X_{f})
. We show that
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X)
is a matroid by showing that it is obtained by deleting the set
F_{f}\subset E(X_{f})
from the matroid
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{f}/X_{f})
(so that
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X}/X)
is obtained from
\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X}_{f}/X_{f})
by contracting
F_{f}
):

\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X)=\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{f}/X_{f})\backslash F_{f},\quad\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X}/X)=\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{X}_{f}/X_{f})/F_{f}=(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{f}/X_{f})\backslash F_{f})^{*}.

Fix an independent set
F\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{f}/X_{f})\backslash F_{f})
, so that
F\in\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{f}/X_{f})
and
F\subseteq E(X_{f})\backslash F_{f}
. Let
X_{f}\backslash F=(X_{f})_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup(X_{f})_{k}
be the connected component decomposition of the complement of
F
. The contracted edges lie on the
(X_{f})_{i}
, therefore
X\backslash F=X_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup X_{k}
, where
X_{i}
is the contraction of
(X_{f})_{i}
along
F_{f}\cap E((X_{f})_{i})
. Whether or not a
G
-cover is trivial does change under contraction. Each
(X_{f})_{i}
is
G
-nontrivial, hence so is each
X_{i}
and therefore
F\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X))
. Running this argument in reverse, we see that

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X))=\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{f}/X_{f})\backslash F_{f}),

hence
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X)
is indeed a matroid.

We now prove formula (
30
) for the rank of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X)
(which can also be found in Zaslavsky’s work). If
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
is free, then
r(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X))=g(X)-1
follows from the following elementary counting argument, which we will use later and whose proof we omit:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X
be a graph of genus
g
, let
F\subseteq E(X)
be a set of edges, and let
X\backslash F=X_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup X_{k}
.

(1)

If
|F|=g-1
, then either
g(X_{i})=1
for all
i
or
g(X_{i})=0
for some
i
.

(2)

Conversely, if
g(X_{i})=1
for all
i
, then
|F|=g-1
.

We now use free
G
-covers as the base of an induction argument, and consider what happens to the rank of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X)
when we contract a single edge

to obtain the cover
p_{e}:\widetilde{X}_{e}\to X_{e}
. There are several cases to consider.

If
e
has distinct root vertices

and
v

. If

, then

. Hence both sides of (

The case when

goes down by one. If

, then

, so that both sides of (

) decrease by one. If

and

, then

is a dilated vertex in

, and

The main result

We are now ready to state and prove the main result of the paper. Let

Definition 5.1
.

(1)

(3)

is the
\rho
-twisted edge matrix
of
X
. Hence we can compute the determinant of
L_{\rho}
using the Cauchy–Binet theorem:

\det L_{\rho}=\sum_{F\subseteq E(X)}\det B_{\rho}(X\backslash F)\det B_{\rho}(X\backslash F)^{\dagger}=\sum_{F\subseteq E(X)}\overline{\det B_{\rho}(X\backslash F)}^{2}.

(39)

Here the sum is taken over all subsets
F\subseteq E(X)
having
m-n=g-1
elements, and
B_{\rho}(X\backslash F)
is the
\rho
-twisted edge matrix of
X\backslash F
.

Fix one such
F
, and let
X\backslash F=X_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup X_{k}
be the decomposition of the complement into connected components. The matrix
B_{\rho}(X\backslash F)
decomposes into blocks
B_{\rho}(X_{i})
corresponding to the
X_{i}
. By Lemma
4.4
, either
g(X_{i})=0
for some
i
or
g(X_{i})=1
for all
i
. In the former case the block
B_{\rho}(X_{i})
is not square (having one more vertex than edge), and therefore
\det B_{\rho}(X\backslash F)=0
. Hence in (
39
) it is enough to sum over the subsets
F\subseteq E(X)
such that each connected component of
X\backslash F
has genus one (again by Lemma
4.4
, any such subset necessarily has
g-1
edges). In this case all blocks are square and

\overline{\det B_{\rho}(X\backslash F)}^{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\overline{\det B_{\rho}(X_{i})}^{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\det L_{\rho}(X_{i}).

(40)

Fix one of the components
X_{i}
, which consists of a unique cycle
C_{i}
with trees attached. To compute the determinant
\det L_{\rho}(X_{i})
, we choose a different orientation on
X_{i}
, namely one that follows the cycle and is oriented away from it along the trees. For any extremal edge
e\in E(X_{i})
with extremal vertex
v=t(e)
, the
v
-th row of
B_{\rho}(X_{i})
has a
1
in the
e
-th column and zeroes everywhere else. Removing
v
and
e
does not change the determinant, and removing all extremal vertices we see that
\det B_{\rho}(X_{i})=\det B_{\rho}(C_{i})
.

We now cyclically label the vertices
V(C_{i})=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{l}\}
and edges
E(C_{i})=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{l}\}
so that
s(e_{i})=v_{i}
and
t(e_{i})=e_{i+1}
. Denoting
\rho_{i}=\rho(\eta(e_{i}))
, the determinant can be easily computed by induction:

\det B_{\rho}(C_{i})=\left|\begin{matrix}-\overline{\rho_{1}}&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&-\overline{\rho_{2}}&1&\cdots&0\\
0&0&-\overline{\rho_{3}}&\cdots&0\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
1&0&0&\cdots&-\overline{\rho_{l}}\end{matrix}\right|=(-1)^{l+1}\left(1-\overline{\rho_{1}}\cdots\overline{\rho_{l}}\right)=(-1)^{l+1}(1-\overline{\rho(\eta(X_{i}))}).

Therefore

\det L_{\rho}(X_{i})=\overline{\det B_{\rho}(X_{i})}^{2}=\overline{\det B_{\rho}(C_{i})}^{2}=\left|1-\overline{\rho(\eta(X_{i}))}\right|^{2}=w_{\rho}(X_{i}).

(41)

Plugging (
41
) and (
40
) into (
39
), we see that

\det L_{\rho}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho))}w_{\rho}(F)=\left.P_{\widetilde{X}/X,\rho}(x_{e})\right|_{x_{e}=1}.

(42)

The Artin–Ihara
L
-function
L(s,\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
is the metric
L
-function specialized at
x_{e}=1
, so plugging (
42
) into (
38
) we see that (
37
) holds when all
x_{e}=1
.

We now fix positive integers
\underline{n}=\{n_{e}\}_{e\in E(X)}
and construct the graph
X_{\underline{n}}
by replacing each edge
e\in E(X)
by a chain of
n_{e}
edges (see proof of Proposition
28
). For each
e\in E(X)
, we arbitrarily pick one of these edges
e^{\prime}
and set
(\eta_{\underline{n}})_{e^{\prime}}=\eta_{e}
, and we set the voltages on the remaining edges to be trivial. The resulting
G
-voltage assignment
[\eta_{\underline{n}}]\in H^{1}(X_{\underline{n}},G)
defines a free
G
-cover
p_{\underline{n}}:\widetilde{X}_{\underline{n}}\to X_{\underline{n}}
of the same topological type as
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
, but with each edge above and below extended into a chain.

Let
F=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{g-1}\}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho))
be a basis of the dual matroid. For each
i=1,\ldots,g-1
, pick one of the
n_{e_{i}}
edges in the chain in
X_{\underline{n}}
corresponding to
e_{i}
. The result is a basis of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{\underline{n}}/X_{\underline{n}},\rho)
having the same weight as
X
, and conversely every basis of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{\underline{n}}/X_{\underline{n}},\rho)
is obtained in this way. In other words, each
F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho))
corresponds to
\prod_{e\in F}n_{e}
bases of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{\underline{n}}/X_{\underline{n}},\rho)
, all having the same weight as
F
.
Hence (
42
) implies that

\det L_{\rho}(X_{\underline{n}})=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{\underline{n}}/X_{\underline{n}},\rho))}w_{\rho}(F)=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho))}w_{\rho}(F)\prod_{e\in F}n_{e}=\left.P_{\widetilde{X}/X,\rho}(x_{e})\right|_{x_{e}=n_{e}}.

(43)

Since
L(s,\widetilde{X}_{\underline{n}}/X_{\underline{n}},\rho)
is the specialization of
L_{M}(s,x_{e},\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
at
x_{e}=n_{e}
, plugging (
43
) into (
38
) shows that (
37
) holds when
x_{e}=n_{e}
, and therefore for arbitrary
x_{e}
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

We first assume that
f:\widetilde{X}\to X
is a free
G
-cover, so that
|D(v)|=1
for all
v\in V(X)
. Let

g=g(X)=|E(X)|-|V(X)|+1,\quad g(\widetilde{X})=N(g-1)+1

be the genera of
X
and
\widetilde{X}
, respectively. The degrees of the weight polynomials
P_{\widetilde{X}/X,\rho}(x_{e})
are all equal to
g-1
. By Propositions
27
and
37
, the reciprocals of the
X
-specialized metric zeta function of
\widetilde{X}
, the metric zeta function of
X
, and the metric
L
-functions at the nontrivial characters have the following leading order terms at
s=1:

\displaystyle\zeta_{M}(s,x_{e},\widetilde{X})^{-1}

\displaystyle=2^{N(g-1)+1}(-1)^{N(g-1)+2}N(g-1)J_{\widetilde{X}}(x_{e})(s-1)^{N(g-1)+1}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\zeta_{M}(s,x_{e},X)^{-1}

\displaystyle=2^{g}(-1)^{g+1}(g-1)J_{X}(x_{e})(s-1)^{g}+\cdots,

\displaystyle L_{M}(s,x_{e},\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)^{-1}

\displaystyle=2^{g-1}(-1)^{g-1}P_{\widetilde{X}/X,\rho}(x_{e})(s-1)^{g-1}+\cdots.

On other other hand,
\zeta_{M}(s,x_{e},\widetilde{X})
factors (
25
) as a product of
\zeta_{M}(s,x_{e},X)
and the
L_{M}(s,x_{e},\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
at the
N-1
nontrivial characters of
G
. Comparing the leading order terms, we obtain (
33
).

By Lemma
2.9
, an arbitrary harmonic
G
-cover can be obtained from a free
G
-cover by contracting a number of loops on the base of the latter. Hence, to finish the proof of Theorem
5.2
, it is sufficient to show that if formula (
33
) holds for a harmonic
G
-cover
f:\widetilde{X}\to X
, then it also holds for the cover
f_{0}:\widetilde{X}_{0}\to X_{0}
obtained by contracting an arbitrary loop
e_{0}\in E(X)
.

Let
v_{0}\in V(X)
be the root vertex of the loop
e_{0}
, let
x_{0}
denote the length of
e_{0}
, let
D(v_{0})\subseteq G
be the dilation subgroup of
v_{0}
on the graph
X
, and let
\eta_{0}\in G/D(v_{0})
be the dilated
G
-voltage on the loop
e_{0}
with a choice of orientation. The dilation group of the vertex
v_{0}
on the graph
X_{0}
is
D(v_{0})+\eta_{0}G
, where
\eta_{0}G
is the subgroup generated by
\eta_{0}
. Let
H=|D(v_{0})+\eta_{0}G|
and
M=|D(v_{0})|
, so that
H/M
is the order of
\eta_{0}
in
(D(v_{0})+\eta_{0}G)/D(v_{0})
. The fiber
p^{-1}(v_{0})
is identified with the quotient group
G/D(v_{0})
, while
p^{-1}(e_{0})=G
, and the source and target maps from
G=p^{-1}(e_{0})
to
G/D(v_{0})=p^{-1}(v_{0})
are

s:G\to G/D(v_{0}),\quad g\mapsto g+D(v_{0}),\quad t:G\to G/D(v_{0}),\quad g\mapsto g+\eta_{0}+D(v_{0}).

Hence the preimage of the subgraph
\{v_{0},e_{0}\}\subset X
in
\widetilde{X}
consists of
N/H
isomorphic graphs
C_{1},\ldots,C_{N/H}
, each of which is a cyclic arrangement of
H/M
vertices, with each adjacent pair of vertices joined by
M
edges. Hence

g(X)=g(X_{0})+1,\quad g(\widetilde{X})=g(\widetilde{X}_{0})+N-\frac{N}{M}+\frac{N}{H}.

We first determine the relationship between the
X
- and
X_{0}
-specialized Jacobian polynomials of
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{X}_{0}
, essentially by repeatedly applying Equation (
17
). There are two types of spanning trees
T\subset\widetilde{X}
.

(1)

If
T\cap C_{i}
is a spanning tree of
C_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,N/H
, then the contraction
T_{0}
of
T
is a spanning tree of
\widetilde{X}_{0}
, and conversely every spanning tree
T_{0}
of
\widetilde{X}_{0}
can be extended to a spanning tree
T
of
X
by gluing in a choice of spanning tree for each
C_{i}
. A spanning tree of
C_{i}
is obtained by removing all edges between two adjacent vertices (
H/M
choices for the pair), and all but one edge between each other pair of adjacent vertices (
M
choices for each other pair). Hence the number of spanning trees of each
C_{i}
is equal to

|\Jac(C_{i})|=\frac{H}{M}M^{H/M-1}=HM^{H/M-2}.

The complementary edges
F=E(\widetilde{X})\backslash E(T)
consist of the complementary edges
F_{0}=E(\widetilde{X}_{0})\backslash E(T_{0})
of the contracted tree
T_{0}\subset\widetilde{X}_{0}
and the
N-N/M+N/H
preimages of
e_{0}
(each having length
x_{0}
) that form the complements of the spanning trees
T\cap C_{i}\subset C_{i}
.

(2)

On the other hand, if
T\cap C_{i}
is not a spanning tree of
C_{i}
for some
i=1,\ldots,N/H
, then the complement
F=E(\widetilde{X})\backslash E(T)
contains at least
N-N/M+N/H+1
preimages of
e_{0}
.

Counting all choices, we see that

\displaystyle J_{\widetilde{X}}(x_{e})=\sum_{T\subset X\,\mathrm{type}\,1}\prod_{e\in E(X)\backslash E(T)}x_{e}+\sum_{T\subset X\,\mathrm{type}\,2}\prod_{e\in E(X)\backslash E(T)}x_{e}=\left[HM^{H/M-2}\right]^{N/H}x_{0}^{N-N/M+N/H}J_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}(x_{e})+

\displaystyle+O\left(x_{0}^{N-N/M+N/H+1}\right)=H^{N/H}M^{N/M-2N/H}x_{0}^{N-N/M+N/H}J_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}(x_{e})+O\left(x_{0}^{N-N/M+N/H+1}\right).

(44)

We now determine what happens to the right hand side of Equation (
33
) when we contract the loop
e_{0}
. By Lemma
2.9
we have

J_{X}(x_{e})=x_{0}J_{X_{0}}(x_{e}).

(45)

Now let
\rho:G\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
be a nontrivial character. There are three possibilities.

(1)

\rho(D(v_{0}))=\{1\}
and
\rho(\eta_{0})=1
. The
e_{0}
is a coloop of the matroid
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
, and
F\mapsto F\backslash\{e_{0}\}
is a bijection between
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho))
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{0}/X_{0},\rho))
. Furthermore, the weights of
F
and
F\backslash\{e_{0}\}
(in terms of the two different matroids) are equal:

w_{X,\rho}(F)=w_{X_{0},\rho}(F\backslash\{e_{0}\}).

It follows that

P_{\widetilde{X}/X,\rho}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho))}w_{X,\rho}(F)\prod_{e\in F}x_{e}=\sum_{F\backslash\{e_{0}\}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho))}w_{X_{0},\rho}(F\backslash\{e_{0}\})x_{0}\prod_{e\in F\backslash\{e_{0}\}}x_{e}=x_{0}P_{\widetilde{X}_{0}/X_{0},\rho}.

(46)

(2)

\rho(D(v_{0}))=\{1\}
and
\rho(\eta_{0})\neq 1
. Here
e_{0}
is not a coloop of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
, the ranks of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
and
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{0}/X_{0},\rho)
are equal, and there is a bijection

\left\{F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)):e_{0}\notin F\right\}\leftrightarrow\left\{F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{0}/X_{0},\rho))\right\}.

Let
F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho))
be a basis not containing
e_{0}
, let
X\backslash F=X_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup X_{k}
be the connected component decomposition of the complement, and assume without loss of generality that the loop
e_{0}
lies on
X_{1}
. Then
X_{1}
is a graph of type (a) according to Definition
5.1
and has weight
w_{X,\rho}(X_{1})=|1-\rho(\eta_{0})|^{2}
. Contracting the loop
e_{0}
, we obtain

X_{0}\backslash F=X_{1,0}\sqcup X_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup X_{k},

where
X_{1,0}
is now a graph of type (b) and has weight
w_{X_{0},\rho}(X_{1,0})=1
. The weights of the other connected components do not change, hence

w_{X,\rho}(F)=|1-\rho(\eta_{0})|^{2}w_{X_{0},\rho}(F),

and therefore

P_{\widetilde{X}/X,\rho}(x_{e})=\sum_{F:e_{0}\notin F}w_{X,\rho}(F)\prod_{e\in F}x_{e}+\sum_{F:e_{0}\in F}w_{X,\rho}(F)\prod_{e\in F}x_{e}=

=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}_{0}/X_{0},\rho))}|1-\rho(\eta_{0})|^{2}w_{X_{0},\rho}(F)\prod_{e\in F}x_{e}+O(x_{0})=|1-\rho(\eta_{0})|^{2}P_{\widetilde{X}_{0}/X_{0},\rho}(x_{e})+O(x_{0}).

(3)

\rho(D(v_{0}))\neq\{1\}
. Then
e_{0}
is again a coloop of
\mathcal{M}^{*}(\widetilde{X}/X,\rho)
, and the same relation (
46
) holds as for type
1
.

We now count the number of characters of each type. There is a natural bijection between nontrivial characters of type
1
and nontrivial characters on the group
G/(D(v_{0})+\eta_{0}G)
, hence there are
N/H-1
characters of this type. Similarly, the characters vanishing on
D(v_{0})
are identified with the characters on the group
G/D(v_{0})
. There are
N/M
such characters, split into
N/H
equivalence classes according to their values on the cyclic subgroup
(D(v_{0})+\eta_{0}G)/D(v_{0})
generated by
\eta_{0}
. Hence there are

\frac{H}{M}\left(\frac{N}{H}-1\right)=\frac{N}{M}-\frac{H}{M}

characters of type
2
. In addition, we calculate the product of
|1-\rho(\eta_{0})|^{2}
over all type
2
characters:

\prod_{\rho:G\to\mathbb{C}^{*}\,\mathrm{type}\,\ref{item:type2}}|1-\rho(\eta_{0})|^{2}=\prod_{\rho:G/D(v)\to\mathbb{C}^{*},\rho(\eta_{0})\neq 0}|1-\rho(\eta_{0})|^{2}=

=\left[\prod_{\rho:(D(v_{0})+\eta_{0}G)/D(v_{0})\to\mathbb{C}^{*},\rho(\eta_{0})\neq 1}|1-\rho(\eta_{0})|^{2}\right]^{N/H}=\left[\left(\frac{H}{M}\right)^{2}\right]^{N/H}=H^{2N/H}M^{-2N/H}.

In the last step, we use the following calculation. The group
(D(v_{0})+\eta_{0}G)/D(v_{0})
is cyclic of order
H/M
with generator
\eta_{0}
. Hence there are
H/M-1
characters
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{H/M-1}
in the product and
\rho_{j}(\eta_{0})=\zeta^{j}
, where
\zeta=e^{2\pi iM/H}
. Therefore the product is given by

\prod_{j=1}^{H/M-1}(1-\zeta^{j})=\left.\prod_{j=1}^{H/M-1}(z-\zeta^{j})\right|_{z=1}=\left.\frac{z^{H/M}-1}{z-1}\right|_{z=1}=\left.(z^{H/M-1}+\cdots+z+1)\right|_{z=1}=\frac{H}{M}.

Finally, the remaining

N-1-\left(\frac{N}{H}-1\right)-\left(\frac{N}{M}-\frac{H}{M}\right)N=N-\frac{N}{H}-\frac{N}{M}+\frac{H}{M}

characters are of type
3
.

We can therefore evaluate the product

\displaystyle\prod_{\rho\in\widehat{G}^{\prime}}P_{\widetilde{X}/X,\rho}(x_{e})=\prod_{\rho\,\mathrm{types}~\ref{item:type1}\,\mathrm{and}~\ref{item:type3}}x_{0}P_{\widetilde{X}_{0}/X_{0},\rho}(x_{e})\prod_{\rho\,\mathrm{type}~\ref{item:type2}}\left[|1-\rho(\eta_{0})|^{2}P_{\widetilde{X}_{0}/X_{0},\rho}(x_{e})+O(x_{0})\right]=

\displaystyle=x_{0}^{N-N/M+H/M-1}H^{2N/H}M^{-2N/H}\prod_{\rho\in\widehat{G}^{\prime}}P_{\widetilde{X}_{0}/X_{0},\rho}(x_{e})+O\left(x_{0}^{N-N/M+H/M}\right).

(47)

Now assume that the cover
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
satisfies Equation (
33
), which we write as

J_{\widetilde{X}}(x_{e})=\frac{1}{N}M^{N/M}\prod_{v\neq v_{0}}|D(v)|^{N/|D(v)|}J_{X}(x_{e})\prod_{\rho\in\widehat{G}^{\prime}}P_{\widetilde{X}/X,\rho}(x_{e}).

Plugging (
44
) in the left hand side, (
45
) and (
47
) in the right hand side, dividing by
x_{0}^{N-N/M+H/M}
, and then setting
x_{0}=0
to kill higher order terms, we obtain

J_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}(x_{e})=\frac{1}{N}H^{N/H}\prod_{v\neq v_{0}}|D(v)|^{N/|D(v)|}J_{X_{0}}(x_{e})\prod_{\rho\in\widehat{G}^{\prime}}P_{\widetilde{X}_{0}/X_{0},\rho}(x_{e}),

which is exactly Equation (
33
) for the cover
p_{0}:\widetilde{X}_{0}\to X_{0}
, having a dilation group of order
H=|D(v_{0})+\eta_{0}G|
at
v_{0}
. This completes the proof.

∎

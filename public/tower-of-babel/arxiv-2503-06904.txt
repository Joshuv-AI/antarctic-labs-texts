On Brezis’ First Open Problem: A Complete Solution
Liming Sun, Jun-cheng Wei, Wen Yang

Abstract
. In 2023, H. Brezis [ 5 ] published a list of his “favorite open problems”, which he described as challenges he had “raised throughout his career and has resisted so far”. We provide a complete resolution to the first one–Open Problem 1.1–in Brezis’s favorite open problems list: the existence of solutions to the long-standing Brezis-Nirenberg problem on a three-dimensional ball. Furthermore, using the building blocks of Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia sign-changing solutions to the Yamabe problem, we establish the existence of infinitely many sign-changing, nonradial solutions for the full range of the parameter.

Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and main results

Let
\Omega
be a smooth and bounded domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. In their seminal work, Brezis and Nirenberg
[
6

studied the following problem:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta u+\lambda u+|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

and established the existence of at least one positive solution under the following conditions:
0<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
if

, and
0<\lambda_{*}<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
if
N=3
. Here,
\lambda_{1}
denotes the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian, and
\lambda_{*}
is a domain-dependent constant, which was later quantified by Druet
[
25

in terms of Robin functions. When
\Omega
is the unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, Brezis and Nirenberg showed that
\lambda_{*}=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4}
and positive solution exists if and only if
\lambda\in(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4},\lambda_{1})

\lambda\leq 0
and
\Omega
is star-shaped.

Since the pioneering work of Brezis-Nirenberg
[
6
]
in 1983, the study of nonlinear elliptic equations involving critical Sobolev exponents has been an area of intense research.

1.2.
Nodal solutions to Yamabe problem and the non-degeneracy

Problem (
1.5
) is a canonical equation in the so-called constant scalar curvature problem with conformal metrics, i.e. Yamabe problem. In an earlier seminal paper, Gidas-Ni-Nirenberg
[
31
]
employed the moving plane method to classify all positive finite-energy solutions of the equation (
1.5
). They showed that all such solutions are given by (the so-called Talenti bubbles)

(1.6)

u(z)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{N(N-2)}\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+|z-a|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda>0,\hskip 10.00002pta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

In 1989, Caffarelli-Gidas-Spruck
[
8
]
extended this classification by removing the finite-energy assumption, obtaining the same result, and Chen-Li
[
14
]
gave a more simplified proof. The classification of solutions for polyharmonic equations with critical exponents was later established by the second author and Xu in
[
58
]
.

Concerning sign-changing solutions to (
1.5
), it is noteworthy that Ding
[
24
]
was the first to employ variational methods to construct infinitely many conformally inequivalent sign-changing solutions with finite energy. Since then, the existence of sign-changing solutions to the Yamabe equation in the entire space has become an active research topic. Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia
[
20
]
introduced a novel constructive approach that produces sign-changing solutions with large energy, where the energy densities concentrate along specific submanifolds of
\mathbb{S}^{N}
. In particular, they constructed a solution resembling a positive bubble but crowned by
m
negative spikes arranged in a regular polygon with radius 1. Remarkably, this solution is invariant under both rotation and the Kelvin transformation. Denote by
\Sigma
the set of nonzero finite-energy solutions to (
1.5
):

(1.7)

\Sigma:=\left\{Q\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}):~\Delta Q+|Q|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}Q=0\right\}.

In fact, one can see that equation (
1.5
) is invariant under the four transformations: translation, dilation, orthogonal transformation and Kelvin transformation, we refer the readers to Section 2.3 for the explicit definition of these transformations. If
Q\in\Sigma
we denote

L_{Q}\cdot=\Delta\cdot+\frac{N+2}{N-2}|Q|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}\cdot

as the linearized operator around
Q
. Define the kernel space of
L_{Q}
:

(1.8)

\mathcal{Z}(Q)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):L_{Q}f=0\right\}.

The elements in
\mathcal{Z}(Q)
generated by the family of the aforementioned four transformations define the following space

(1.9)

\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{Q}=\mathrm{Span}\left\{\begin{aligned} &(2-N)z_{j}Q+|z|^{2}\partial_{z_{j}}Q-2z_{j}z\cdot\nabla Q,~\partial_{z_{j}}Q,~1\leq j\leq N,\\
&(z_{j}\partial_{z_{\ell}}-z_{\ell}\partial_{z_{j}})Q,~1\leq j<\ell\leq N,~\frac{N-2}{2}Q+z\cdot\nabla Q\end{aligned}\right\}.

One can verify that the dimension of
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{Q}
is at most

2N+1+\frac{N(N-1)}{2}.

However, for the positive radial bubble solution
u
given in (
1.6
), the dimension of
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{Q}
is found to be
N+1
. In general, the dimension of the solution may be smaller than
2N+1+\frac{N(N-1)}{2}
. Duyckaerts-Kenig-Merle introduced the following definition of non-degeneracy for a solution of equation (
1.5
) in
[
28
]
: a solution
Q\in\Sigma
is said to be
non-degenerate
if

(1.10)

\mathcal{Z}(Q)=\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}(Q).

In
[
44
]
, Musso-Wei established that the solution constructed by Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia is non-degenerate in the sense of Duyckaerts-Kenig-Merle. Using this non-degeneracy property of the crown solution, Musso-Wei
[
45
]
constructed a sign-changing solution to the Barhi-Coron problem, while Deng-Musso-Wei
[
22
]
presented a novel solution to the scalar curvature problem, and Deng-Musso
[
21
]
provided a new construction of sign-changing solutions to Coron’s problem. The notion of non-degeneracy is not only significant in the study of elliptic partial differential equations but also serves as a fundamental component in proving soliton resolution for solutions to the energy-critical wave equation. This resolution relies on the compactness property established by Kenig and Merle
[
37
,
38
]
. For dimensions
N=3,4,5
, and under the assumption of non-degeneracy, Duyckaerts et al.
[
27
,
29
]
demonstrated that any nontrivial solution to the energy-critical wave equation asymptotically decomposes into a finite sum of stationary solutions and solitary waves, which are Lorentz transforms of the former.

1.3.
Sketch of Proofs.

Going back to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we remark that a key difficulty in dimension three is that the decay of solutions at infinity is not fast enough. Specifically, the standard bubble (
1.6
) decays only algebraically as
1/|z|
, and solving a Poisson equation with a source term of the form
1/|z|
would lead to an ansatz that grows algebraically at infinity. This challenge is significantly more difficult in three dimensions than in higher dimensions. (Another reason we avoid using positive bubbles is more complex. See the explanation at the end for further details.) Therefore, it is crucial to construct a suitable building block that is both sign-changing and exhibits a faster decay than the standard bubble.

Following this principle, a key observation in this paper is that we can apply an inversion transformation at the nodal set of the crown solution constructed by Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia (see also
[
60
]
). This transformation plays a central role in our strategy. Two essential properties ensure that this solution serves as an appropriate candidate. First, the crown solution remains invariant under Kelvin transformation, and Musso-Wei
[
44
]
have proved its non-degeneracy in the sense of Duyckaerts-Kenig-Merle
[
28
]
. Second, as we will show in Section 2 (Theorem
2.1
) by lengthy computations, the crown solution
q
satisfies
\nabla q(z)\neq 0
for each point
z
in its nodal set, thus the nodal set forms a smooth compact Riemann surface. See Figure
2
. These two factors ensure the robustness of the gluing and reduction procedure.

Once the appropriate building block is established, the next step is to determine where we put the configurations and construct a suitable approximate solution. Our approach is as follows: we seek solutions that are rotationally invariant in the first two variables and exhibit even symmetry in the third one. This symmetry assumption allows us to eliminate certain kernel elements from
\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}(Q)
. To proceed, we divide the unit ball into
K
subdomains, where
K
is chosen to be a sufficiently large even number, serving as the perturbation parameter in our analysis. (The idea of using the number of bubbles as a parameter seems first due to Wei-Yan
[
59
]
). Each subdomain is a replica of the following region:

(1.11)

\Sigma_{K}=\left\{(r,\theta,z_{3})\in B_{1};0<r<1,-\frac{\pi}{K}<\theta<\frac{\pi}{K}\right\}.

See Figure
1
for illustration.

Figure 1.
Left picture is the region
\Sigma_{K}
and its two boundaries inside
B_{1}
. Right one is the necklace solution.

We will position a crown bubble (after applying the inversion transformation) in each sector in an alternating manner. In fact, they are arranged close to the boundary
\partial B_{1}
. These bubbles form
necklace
pattern consisting alternating Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia bubbles near the boundary. See Figure
1
for the right one. Specifically, in the vicinity of the crown bubble located in
\Sigma_{K}
, the neighboring two bubbles will have opposite signs. This setup reduces the problem to solving the following Dirichlet problem in
\Sigma_{K}
:

(1.12)

\begin{cases}\Delta u+\lambda u+u^{5}=0\ &\mbox{in}\quad\Sigma_{K},\\
u=0\ &\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Sigma_{K}.\end{cases}

With respect to (
1.12
), we present the following existence result.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
K
is even and large enough, then (
1.12
) has a solution.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we must modify the alternating sum of crown bubbles (after inversion) to ensure that it satisfies the boundary conditions. As a result, the next-order correction terms
T_{A}
,
\varphi_{A}
, and
\psi_{A}
become essential, with their definitions provided in Section 3. In particular,
T_{A}
accounts for the influence of crown bubbles from the other sectors, while
\varphi_{A}
represents the effect of the mirror bubble of the one in
\Sigma_{K}
with respect to the boundary of the unit ball. Although the non-degeneracy of the solution has already been established by Musso-Wei in
[
44
]
, analyzing the associated linearized problem remains challenging. The main difficulty arises from the necessity of positioning the center of the crown bubble close to the boundary. To tackle this, we decompose the perturbation into two components: one that resolves the problem in a small ball centered near the boundary and another that handles the remaining errors. By employing the classical inner-outer gluing method (see
[
19
,
40
]
), we confirm that the resulting coupled system exhibits weak coupling, allowing us to reduce the original linearized problem to a standard form. Following the well-established procedure of analyzing the linearized operator and the nonlinear problem (see Section 6), we further reduce the infinite-dimensional problem to a finite-dimensional one. For this finite-dimensional setting, we apply the localized energy method (see
[
32
]
) to identify the critical points of the energy function with respect to the parameters.

We face two essential difficulties. First, due to the algebraic decay of the building blocks and the specific configuration of our approximation, the interaction between bubbles renders the energy computation particularly intricate. In particular, we must determine the asymptotic behavior of several types of series like

\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\left(x^{2}+\sin^{2}\tfrac{j\pi}{n}\right)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(x^{2}+\sin^{2}\tfrac{j\pi}{n}\right)^{-\frac{k}{2}},\hskip 10.00002ptk\in\mathbb{N}.

(See Section 9.) It is fortunate that contour integration from complex analysis can be employed to derive an integral representation for the given series. Utilizing results on elliptic integrals, we establish a precise asymptotic behavior for these series under specific conditions on

x
and
n
. The detailed computations are provided in Section 9.

The second difficulty is that we have
six
parameter configuration spaces: they are respectively (1) scaling; (2) two translations; (3) one rotation; (4) two Kelvin transforms. These parameters have different scales, some of them may dominate others, so we have to find out the delicate small forces. After calculating the energy of the solution carefully, we analyze its leading order terms and identify the critical point through minimization, thereby proving the existence of a solution to (
1.12
). The whole process is a careful balancing act, where we must figure out the proper order for each parameter. Roughly speaking, we find that the scaling parameter is of
O(K^{-3})
, the distance of the center of bubble to the boundary is of
O\left(K^{-1}\log K\right)
, the shift of the bubble in the
z_{1}
-axis is of
O(K^{-2}\log K)
, and finally the rotation is found to be of
O(K^{-1}\log K)
. The Kelvin transform parameters are smaller in order.

Using the existence result from Theorem
1.2
, we can get a solution to the Brezis-Nirenberg problem in
B_{1}
. Specifically, we extend
u
by performing an odd reflection across each hyperplanes
\theta=j\pi/K
,
j=1,3,5,\cdots,K-1
, consecutively. As a consequence,
u
can be extended to a function in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
and verifies (
1.2
) in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
. Since the solution we constructed is bounded, the singularity at the origin is removable. Consequently, we establish the existence of a nontrivial solution to (
1.2
), thereby resolving Brezis’s open problem.

Remark 1.3
.

The solution we constructed has a special shape, see Figure
1
, where blue and red colors represent the positive and negative bubbles, respectively.

To end the introduction, we make a few comments. First, when

\lambda
is a sufficiently small positive number, an alternative type of solution can be constructed, where a single crown bubble is placed at the origin. We will report it elsewhere. Second, for the Brezis-Nirenberg problem in higher dimensions, we can apply the same approach to construct infinitely many sign-changing solutions. Third, we believe that the procedure outlined in this paper can be adapted to other elliptic problems involving critical exponents. Finally, since the Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia bubble has large Morse index, the solutions we constructed carry very high Morse index. It remains open if one can give a positive answer to Brezis’ Open Problem 1.1 by direct variational methods. The corresponding Brezis’ problem in general three-dimensional domains is also challenging. By [Theorem 1.2, Brezis-Nirenberg
[
6
]
], for strictly starshaped three dimensional domain
\Omega
, positive solution exists only if
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}(\Omega)>0
. (As a result and by a scaling argument, if we use positive bubble as building block in Theorem
1.2
, then
\lambda\geq c_{0}K^{2}>0
.) By Theorem
1.1
, we raise the following generalized Brezis’ Open Problem 1.1:

Generalized Brezis’ Open Problem.
Let
\Omega
be strictly star-shaped domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Then for every
\lambda>0
, problem (
1.1
) admits infinitely many solutions.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we examine the crown solution constructed by Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia
[
20
]
by analyzing its nodal set. In Sections 3 and 4 we introduce the approximate solution with correction terms and include the computation of the energy for the approximate solution. In Section 7 we focus on reducing the infinite-dimensional problem to a finite-dimensional one, where we resolve the reduced problem by identifying the local minimum of the reduced energy and provide the proof of Theorem

2.
On the Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia solution

In this section, we give a detailed study on the Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia solutions to equation (
1.5
). Del Pino, Musso, Pacard, and Pistoia discovered an interesting sign-changing solution to (
1.5
) characterized by a large number
m
of bubbles. This presents the first semi-explicit construction of its kind, utilizing an approach that naturally reveals spectral information related to the linearized problem. Because of its distinctive shape, they refer to it as the crown solution. Recently, this type of crown solution has played a significant role in understanding the long-term dynamics in the corresponding Schrödinger equation. (See
[
60
]
.) Moreover, it serves as the foundation for the current work. In this section, we begin by describing this solution
q_{m}
and providing a more refined analysis of its asymptotic behavior. Additionally, we examine the nodal set of this solution, demonstrating that it forms a smooth manifold, a property that proves crucial in addressing the reduction problem. Finally, we offer an interpretation for the non-degeneracy of the solution.

2.1.
Estimates of
\psi

Recall that
\psi
satisfies (see
[
20

)

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Delta\psi+5U^{4}\psi+V(z)\psi+5|U_{*}|^{4}\sum_{j}(1-\zeta_{j})\tilde{\phi}_{j}+M(\psi)=0,

where (we used the notations from there)

V(z):=5(|U_{*}|^{4}-|U|^{4})\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\zeta_{j}\right)-5U^{4}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\zeta_{j},

M(\psi):=\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\zeta_{j}\right)\left(E+N\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{\phi}_{j}+\psi\right)\right),

E=\left(U-\sum_{j=1}^{m}U_{j}\right)^{5}-U^{5}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}U_{j}^{5},

and

\zeta_{j}(z)=\begin{cases}\zeta(m\eta^{-1}|z|^{-2}|(z-\xi_{j}|z|)|),\quad&\mbox{if}\quad|z|>1,\\
\zeta(m\eta^{-1}|z-\xi_{j}|),\quad&\mbox{if}\quad|z|\leq 1.\end{cases}

Here
\zeta(s)
is a smooth function such that
\zeta(s)=1
for
s<1
and
\zeta(s)=0
for
s>2
, and
\eta
is a small positive constant. It is not difficult to check that

(2.8)

U(z)=\frac{1}{|z|}U\left(\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}\right),\hskip 10.00002ptU_{j}(z)=\frac{1}{|z|}U_{j}\left(\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}\right),~j=1,\cdots,m,

and

(2.9)

\zeta_{j}(z)=\zeta_{j}\left(\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}\right),~j=1,\cdots,m.

Together with the properties that the ansatz is
2\pi/m
-rotationally invariant in
z_{1}z_{2}
plane and even symmetry with respect to the
z_{3}
axis, one can solve
\psi
from (
2.7
) uniquely in a suitable Sobolev space, which is the orthogonal complement of the space spanned by the kernels of the linear operator
\Delta+5U^{4}
. More precisely, the authors in
[
20

proved that

\|\psi\|_{L_{w}^{\infty}}\leq C\left(\|V(z)\psi\|_{L_{w}^{q}}+5\left\||U_{*}|^{4}\sum_{j}\left(1-\zeta_{j}\right)\tilde{\phi}_{j}\right\|_{L_{w}^{q}}+\|M(\psi)\|_{L_{w}^{q}}\right),

where

\|\psi\|_{L_{w}^{\infty}}:=\left\|(1+|z|)\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|h\|_{L_{w}^{q}}:=\left\|(1+|z|)^{5-\frac{6}{q}}h\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Particularly, for
q>\frac{3}{2}
it has been shown that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\zeta_{j}\right)N\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{\phi}_{j}+\psi\right)\right\|_{L_{w}^{q}}+\left\||U_{*}|^{4}\sum_{j}\left(1-\zeta_{j}\right)\tilde{\phi}_{j}\right\|_{L_{w}^{q}}+\|V(z)\psi\|_{L_{w}^{q}}\leq\frac{C}{(\log m)^{2}}.

\Delta\psi_{d}+5U^{4}\psi_{d}+\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\zeta_{j}\right)E=0,

2.2.
On the nodal set of Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia’s solution

In this subsection, we will study the nodal set of the solution by Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia. To begin with, we present the following lemma to describe the location of the nodal set.

Lemma 2.3
.

When
m
is large enough,
q_{m}
has a compact nodal set
\mathcal{N}(q_{m})
such that for any point
z
in
\mathcal{N}(q_{m})
, the following holds

{\rm dist}\left(z,\left\{\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m}\right\}\right)\sim\frac{1}{m}.

In addition, there exists a small positive constant
c_{0}
such that

(2.22)

\mathcal{N}(q_{m})\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}B_{c_{0}/m}\left(\sqrt{1-\mu_{m}^{2}}\cos\frac{2j-1}{m}\pi,\sqrt{1-\mu_{m}^{2}}\sin\frac{2j-1}{m}\pi,0\right)=\emptyset.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we write

z=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,z_{3})
and assume that

|z-\xi_{1}|=\min\{|z-\xi_{j}|,~j=1,\cdots,m\},\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\theta\in\left[0,\frac{\pi}{m}\right].

If
|z-\xi_{1}|\geq C
, then for sufficiently large
m
we have

\displaystyle q_{m}(z)\geq 3^{\frac{1}{4}}-3^{\frac{1}{4}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{{\mu_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}}}{|z-\xi_{j}|}-O\left(\frac{1}{\log m}\right)>0.

Hence, the zero point
z
of
q_{m}(z)
must be close to
\xi_{1}
. As a consequence, we may restrict our discussion for the point
z
satisfying

(2.23)

|z-\xi_{1}|=o_{m}(1)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|r-1|=o_{m}(1).

In the following, we divide our discussion into three steps. First, we will prove that
q_{m}(z)
is negative in the vicinity of
\xi_{1}
. The key point here is that when
z
is extremely close to
\xi_{1}
, the effect of the other negative bubbles offsets the central positive bubble, making the negative bubble at
\xi_{1}
the dominant term determining the sign of
q_{m}
. Next, we introduce a quantity
h
(refer to (
2.28
)), which measures the distance from the point
z
to the center of the negative bubble. We will show that when
h
is not too small, the major contributions from all negative bubbles form a series. This series can be assessed using an integral representation and the asymptotic behavior of the elliptic integral, enabling us to figure out the leading term in the total sum of negative bubbles. Finally, we will analyze the behavior of
q_{m}(z)
at the midpoint between
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
, denoted by
\xi_{0}
. By using the estimate of
\psi
at
\xi_{0}
from (
2.21
), we will compare
\psi
to
U_{*}=U-\sum_{j=1}^{m}U_{j}
and establish that
q_{m}
is positive at
\xi_{0}
. Together with the evaluation of
\nabla q_{m}
, this analysis reveals a small region (a tiny ball) where
q_{m}
remains positive.

Step 1
. In this step, we shall prove that
q_{m}
is negative within a small neighborhood of
\xi_{1}
, i.e.,

(2.24)

q_{m}(z)<0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for}\hskip 10.00002pt|z-\xi_{1}|=\min_{j\in\{1,\cdots,m\}}|z-\xi_{j}|\leq\frac{c_{1}}{m},

provided
c_{1}
is sufficiently small. For
|z-\xi_{1}|\leq\frac{c_{1}}{m}
, we have

|z-\xi_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\xi_{j}-\xi_{1}|\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|z-\xi_{j}|\leq|\xi_{j}-\xi_{1}|+|z-\xi_{1}|,\hskip 10.00002ptj=2,\cdots,m.

Then using the simple inequality
\frac{1}{1+x}\geq 1-x
for
x\in(0,1)
we have

(2.25)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu_{m}^{-2}|z-\xi_{j}|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{m}}{|z-\xi_{j}|}+O\left(\frac{1}{m^{3}(\log m)^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mu_{m}}{|\xi_{j}-\xi_{1}|+|z-\xi_{1}|}+O\left(\frac{1}{m^{3}(\log m)^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mu_{m}}{|\xi_{j}-\xi_{1}|}-\frac{\mu_{m}|z-\xi_{1}|}{|\xi_{j}-\xi_{1}|^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{m^{3}(\log m)^{6}}\right),~

for any
j=2,\cdots,m
. Similarly, we can apply the inequality
\frac{1}{1-x}\leq 1+2x
for
x\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
to derive that

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{m}}{|z-\xi_{j}|}\leq\frac{\mu_{m}}{|\xi_{j}-\xi_{1}|}+\frac{2\mu_{m}|z-\xi_{1}|}{|\xi_{j}-\xi_{1}|^{2}},\hskip 10.00002ptj=2,\cdots,m.

Together with (
2.5

2.6

(2.26)

\displaystyle q_{m}(z)\leq

\displaystyle 3^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{1+|z|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sum_{j=2}^{m}\frac{\mu_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{j}|}+\sum_{j=2}^{m}\frac{\mu_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}|z-\xi_{1}|}{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{j}|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-3^{\frac{1}{4}}\mu_{m}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu_{m}^{-2}|z-\xi_{1}|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{m(\log m)^{5}}\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{1}{\log m}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu_{m}^{-1}|z-\xi_{1}|}\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\sum_{j=2}^{m}\frac{\mu_{m}}{|\xi_{j}-\xi_{1}|}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\mu_{m}|z-\xi_{1}|}{|\xi_{j}-\xi_{1}|^{2}}\right).

On the right hand side of (
2.26

\frac{1}{1+|z|^{2}}=\frac{1}{1+|\xi_{1}|^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)=\frac{1}{2}+O\left(\frac{1}{m}\right),

the second and third terms in the bracket can be handled respectively as follows

\sum_{j=2}^{m}\frac{\mu_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{j}|}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+O\left(\frac{1}{m\log m}\right),

and

\sum_{j=2}^{m}\frac{|z-\xi_{1}|}{m\log m|\xi_{1}-\xi_{j}|^{2}}\leq C\frac{m|z-\xi_{1}|}{\log m}.

Consider the fourth term on the right hand side of (
2.26
), we have

\mu_{m}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu_{m}^{-2}|z-\xi_{1}|^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\geq\min\left\{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\mu_{m}^{-\frac{1}{2}},\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\frac{\mu_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|z-\xi_{1}|}\right\}.

Putting all these information on the right hand side of (
2.25

(2.27)

\displaystyle q_{m}(z)\leq

\displaystyle-3^{\frac{1}{4}}\min\left\{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\mu_{m}^{-\frac{1}{2}},\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\frac{\mu_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|z-\xi_{1}|}\right\}+\frac{Cm|z-\xi_{1}|}{\log m}

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{1}{\log m}\right)+O\left(\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{\mu_{m}^{-1}|z-\xi_{1}|}\right\}\right).

If
|z-\xi_{1}|\leq c_{1}/m
with
c_{1}
is small enough, we can see that the right-hand side of (
2.27
) is strictly negative for
m
sufficiently large. Thus, we finish the proof of the claim (
2.24
).

Step 2

(2.28)

h^{2}=\frac{(r-\sqrt{1-\mu_{m}^{2}})^{2}+z_{3}^{2}}{4r\sqrt{1-\mu_{m}^{2}}}.

we have

(2.40)

\displaystyle q_{m}(\xi_{0})=~3^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}-3^{\frac{1}{4}}\mu_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{m}{2}}\frac{1}{\left|\sin\left(\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{m}-\frac{\pi}{m}\right)\right|}+3^{\frac{1}{4}}\mu_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{2m}{\pi}+O\left(\frac{1}{m\log m}\right).

2.3.

In this subsection, we shall interpret the non-degeneracy in the sense of Duyckaerts-Kenig-Merle for our demands. From now on, we choose
m
large enough and fix a function
q=q_{m}
. For simplicity of notation, we drop the index

(2.59)

\displaystyle\Delta q+q^{5}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in}\hskip 10.00002pt{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

As we have pointed out, the following transformations keep the solution set of the Yamabe equation (

For any set of parameters

\displaystyle\Theta_{A}(z)=\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|z-y|}q\left(\frac{\varepsilon R_{\theta}(z-y)}{|z-y|^{2}}+\xi\right).

Choosing

near to
(0,Id,1,0)
, it generates a family of solutions near

. Taking the derivatives on each parameter in

10 functions

(2.60)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-z_{j}q+|z|^{2}\partial_{z_{j}}q-2z_{j}z\cdot\nabla q,\quad\partial_{z_{j}}q,\quad 1\leq j\leq 3,\\

One can verify that

Note that these 10 functions are generated consecutively by

of

Define the kernel space of

by

in (

|T_{A}(z)|+|\varphi_{A}(z)|+|F(z)|=O(\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}K^{2})
for
z\in\Sigma_{K}
.
Let

\tilde{\psi}_{0}(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{4\pi|y-z|}\frac{1}{|z|}q\left(\frac{R_{\beta}z}{|z|^{2}}+\hat{\xi}\right)dz.

Then it is easy to see that
\tilde{\psi}_{0}(y)
satisfies the equation
\Delta_{y}\tilde{\psi}_{0}+\frac{1}{|y|}q\left(\frac{R_{\beta}y}{|y|^{2}}+\hat{\xi}\right)=0
. One
can verify that
|\tilde{\psi}_{0}|\leq C(1+\log\sqrt{1+y^{2}})
. Now let
\tilde{\psi}=\lambda\tilde{\psi}_{0}+\tilde{\psi}_{1}
. Then
\tilde{\psi}_{1}
satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{y}\tilde{\psi}_{1}+\lambda\varepsilon^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{1}=O(\varepsilon^{2}K^{2}).

Note that
B_{1/(2K\varepsilon)}(0)\subset\tilde{\Sigma}_{K}\subset\{|y_{2}|\leq\frac{2\pi}{K\varepsilon}\}
. Thus, on
\partial\tilde{\Sigma}_{K}
, one has
\tilde{\psi}_{1}=O(|\log\varepsilon|)
. We construct a barrier function

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}_{1}(y)=C|\log\varepsilon|[(3\pi)^{2}-(K\varepsilon y_{2})^{2}].

One can verify that

\displaystyle\Delta_{y}\bar{\psi}_{1}+\lambda\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\psi}_{1}\leq C|\log\varepsilon|[-2(K\varepsilon)^{2}+\lambda\varepsilon^{2}(3\pi)^{2}]\leq-C(K\varepsilon)^{2},

provided
K
is large enough. Moreover,
\bar{\psi}_{1}\geq C|\log\varepsilon|
on
\partial\tilde{\Sigma}_{K}
.

Note that
\tilde{\Sigma}_{K}\subset\{|y_{2}|\leq 2\pi/(K\varepsilon)\}
implies that the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\tilde{\Sigma}_{K})\geq(K\varepsilon/2\pi)^{2}
. Thus
\Delta_{y}+\lambda\varepsilon^{2}
satisfies the maximum principle on
\tilde{\Sigma}_{K}
when
K
is large enough.
Therefore
|\tilde{\psi}_{1}(y)|\leq C|\bar{\psi}_{1}|=O(|\log\varepsilon|)
. Note that
|\tilde{\psi}_{0}|\leq C(1+\log\sqrt{1+y^{2}})\leq C|\log\varepsilon|
. The proof is complete.
∎

4.
Energy expansion

In this section, we shall compute the energy of the approximate solution and find its leading-order term with respect to the parameters.

Define the energy of

as

\displaystyle J(PQ_{A})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{K}}(|\nabla PQ_{A}|^{2}-\lambda|PQ_{A}|^{2})dz-\frac{1}{6}\int_{\Sigma_{K}}(PQ_{A})^{6}dz

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{K}}Q_{A}^{5}PQ_{A}dz-\frac{1}{6}\int_{\Sigma_{K}}(PQ_{A})^{6}dz.

For the first term on the right-hand side

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{K}}Q_{A}^{5}PQ_{A}dz=\int_{\Sigma_{K}}Q_{A}^{5}(Q_{A}-\tilde{T}_{A})dz=\int_{\Sigma_{K}}\left(Q_{A}^{6}-Q_{A}^{5}\tilde{T}_{A}\right)dz.

For the second term

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{K}}(PQ_{A})^{6}dz=\int_{\Sigma_{K}}(Q_{A}-\tilde{T}_{A})^{6}dz=\int_{\Sigma_{K}}\left(Q_{A}^{6}-6Q_{A}^{5}\tilde{T}_{A}\right)dz+I,

where

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{K}}\left(15Q_{A}^{4}(\tilde{T}_{A})^{2}-20Q_{A}^{3}(\tilde{T}_{A})^{3}+15Q_{A}^{2}(\tilde{T}_{A})^{4}-6Q_{A}(\tilde{T}_{A})^{5}+(\tilde{T}_{A})^{6}\right)dz.

To estimate each term, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1

Assuming the constraints (
3.4

Proof.

5.
Gluing Procedure

In this section, we outline the gluing procedure by separating the perturbation into inner and outer components. Specifically, we will prove that the inner part is the dominant term.

Let
PQ_{A}
denote the approximate solution defined in section 3

(5.1)

PQ_{A}=Q_{A}-\tilde{T}_{A}=Q_{A}-T_{A}-\varphi_{A}-\psi_{A}.

We introduce the following form of
PQ_{A}
for convenience

\displaystyle PQ_{A^{\prime}}=~

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}Q_{A}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{A}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{A}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{A}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle Q_{A^{\prime}}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{A}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{A}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{A}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|y-b_{\varepsilon}|}q\left(\frac{R_{\beta}(y-b_{\varepsilon})}{|y-b_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}+\varepsilon^{-1}\xi+\varepsilon^{-1}a\nu(\xi)\right)-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{T}_{A}

where
y=\frac{z}{\varepsilon}
and
b_{\varepsilon}=\frac{b}{\varepsilon}.
The Brezis-Nirenberg problem is equivalent to finding
\phi
such that

(5.2)

\Delta_{y}(PQ_{A^{\prime}}+\phi)+\lambda\varepsilon^{2}(PQ_{A^{\prime}}+\phi)+(PQ_{A^{\prime}}+\phi)^{5}=0,

which can be rewritten as

(5.3)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{y}\phi+\lambda\varepsilon^{2}\phi+5PQ_{A^{\prime}}^{4}\phi=-E-N_{\varepsilon}(\phi)\quad&\mbox{in}\quad\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon},\\
\phi=0&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon},\end{cases}

where
\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon}=\{y\mid\varepsilon y\in\Sigma_{k}\}
,

\displaystyle E=

\displaystyle~\Delta PQ_{A^{\prime}}+\lambda\varepsilon^{2}PQ_{A^{\prime}}+PQ_{A^{\prime}}^{5}=PQ_{A^{\prime}}^{5}-Q_{A^{\prime}}^{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-5\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}Q_{A^{\prime}}^{4}(T_{A}+\varphi_{A}+\psi_{A})+O(\varepsilon^{4}K^{4})Q_{A^{\prime}}^{3}+O(\varepsilon^{6}K^{6})Q_{A^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle+O(\varepsilon^{8}K^{8})Q_{A^{\prime}}+O(\varepsilon^{10}K^{10}),

and

\displaystyle N_{\varepsilon}(\phi)=(PQ_{A^{\prime}}+\phi)^{5}-PQ_{A^{\prime}}^{5}-5PQ_{A^{\prime}}^{4}\phi.

It is important to mention how the functions
PQ_{A^{\prime}}
and
Q_{A^{\prime}}
depend on the parameters
A^{\prime}=(\Lambda,\varepsilon^{-1}a,\varepsilon^{-1}\xi,b_{\varepsilon},\beta)
. Particularly, the dependence of
Q_{A^{\prime}}
on
\Lambda
can be understood as follows:

Q_{A^{\prime}}=\left.\frac{\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|y-b_{\varepsilon}|}q\left(\frac{R_{\beta}\Lambda(y-b_{\varepsilon})}{|y-b_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}+\varepsilon^{-1}\xi+\varepsilon^{-1}a\nu(\xi)\right)\right|_{\Lambda=1}.

Since we scale
Q_{A}
by
\varepsilon
for the space variable, so the parameter
\Lambda
does not appear in
Q_{A^{\prime}}
and we will not carry it in the expression of
Q_{A^{\prime}}
in the following argument.

We separate
\phi
in the following form

(5.4)

\phi(y)=\eta_{2r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}(y)\phi_{\rm in}(y)+\phi_{\rm out}(y),

where
\bar{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon K
and

\eta_{2r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}(y)=\begin{cases}1,\quad&\mbox{if}\quad\bar{\varepsilon}|y-b_{\varepsilon}|\leq 2r,\\
\\
0,\quad&\mbox{if}\quad\bar{\varepsilon}|y-b_{\varepsilon}|\geq 4r.\end{cases}

for some
r\in\left(0,\frac{1}{32}\right)
and
\phi_{\rm in}(y)
,
\phi_{\rm out}(y)
satisfy the following equation respectively

(5.5)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \Delta_{y}\phi_{\rm in}+\lambda\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{\rm in}+5PQ_{A^{\prime}}^{4}\phi_{\rm in}=&-\eta_{r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}\left(N_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{2r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\rm in}+\phi_{\rm out})+E\right)\\
&-5\eta_{r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}PQ_{A^{\prime}}^{4}\phi_{\rm out}\end{aligned}&\mbox{in}\quad B_{\frac{4r}{\bar{\varepsilon}}}(b_{\varepsilon}),\\
\\
\phi_{\rm in}=0~&\mbox{on}\quad\partial B_{\frac{4r}{\bar{\varepsilon}}}(b_{\varepsilon}),\end{cases}

and

(5.6)

\phi_{\rm out}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

It is crucial to see that
|\nabla\eta_{2r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}|\leq C\bar{\varepsilon}

|\Delta\eta_{2r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}|\leq C\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}

\phi_{\rm in}
and solve
\phi_{\rm out}
from (

\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon}

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\phi

\begin{cases}\Delta\phi+\lambda\varepsilon^{2}\phi+f=0\quad&\mbox{in}\quad\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon},\\
\phi=0&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

Then we have

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})}\leq\frac{C}{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})}.

Proof.

We construct a function

w=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}-r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta.

After straightforward computation, we check that

\Delta w+\frac{\lambda\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}{K^{2}}w=-2+\frac{\lambda\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}{K^{2}}\left(\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}-r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right)<-1

\left(\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}-r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})}

defines a super-solution and the conclusion follows easily.
∎

By Lemma

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\rm out}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left\|\left(1-\eta_{r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}\right){PQ}_{A^{\prime}}^{4}\phi_{\rm out}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left\|\left(1-\eta_{r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}\right)(E+N(\eta_{2r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\rm in}+\phi_{\rm out}))\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left\|\nabla\eta_{2r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}\nabla_{y}\phi_{\rm in}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})}+\frac{C}{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\Delta\eta_{2r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})}.

If we presumably assume that

In addition, one can follow the similar process to derive that

(5.11)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{A^{\prime}}\phi_{\rm out}(\phi_{\rm in})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\nabla_{A^{\prime}}((1-\eta_{r}^{\bar{\varepsilon}})E)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})}+C\bar{\varepsilon}\|\phi_{\rm in}\|_{*}

\displaystyle+C\bar{\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{A^{\prime}}\phi_{\rm in}\|_{*}+C\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\phi_{\rm out}(\phi_{\rm in})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})},

and

(5.12)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{A^{\prime}}(\phi_{\rm out}(\phi_{\rm in,1})-\phi_{\rm out}(\phi_{\rm in,2}))\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{K,\varepsilon})}\leq

\displaystyle C\bar{\varepsilon}\|\phi_{\rm in,1}-\phi_{\rm in,2}\|_{*}

\displaystyle+C\bar{\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{A^{\prime}}(\phi_{\rm in,1}-\phi_{\rm in,2})\|_{*}.

Then the full problem can be reduced to solving the nonlocal problem in the ball

3

To state the main result concerning the linearized problem, we introduce the following weighted function space:

(6.2)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{**}=\sup_{y\in B_{4r/\bar{\varepsilon}}(b_{\varepsilon})}\left|\langle y-b_{\varepsilon}\rangle^{3+2\sigma}h(y)\right|,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{*}=\sup_{y\in B_{4r/\bar{\varepsilon}}(b_{\varepsilon})}|\langle y-b_{\varepsilon}\rangle\phi(y)|+\sup_{y\in B_{4r/\bar{\varepsilon}}(b_{\varepsilon})}|\langle y-b_{\varepsilon}\rangle^{2}\nabla\phi(y)|,

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that the parameters
A
and
\varepsilon
satisfy the relation in (
3.4
). Then there exists
K_{0}
large enough such that for all
K>K_{0}
and all
h\in C^{\alpha}(B_{4r/\bar{\varepsilon}}(b_{\varepsilon}))
which is even in
z_{3}
, the problem (
6.1

\phi\equiv L_{\varepsilon}(h)
which is even in

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{*}\leq C\|h\|_{**},\hskip 10.00002pt|c_{j}|\leq C\|h\|_{**},

and

\|\nabla_{A^{\prime}}\phi\|_{*}\leq C\|h\|_{**}.

Proof.

We shall prove the proposition by contradiction, we mainly follow
[
45
, Proposition 4.2]
to sketch the major steps. Suppose that there exists a sequence
K=K_{n}\to\infty
such that there are functions
\phi_{n}
and

with
\|\phi_{n}\|_{*}=1
and
\|h_{n}\|_{**}=o(1)

(6.4)

\begin{cases}\Delta\phi_{n}+\lambda\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{n}+5PQ_{A^{\prime}}^{4}\phi_{n}=h_{n}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{5}c_{j}PQ_{A^{\prime}}\hat{Z}_{j}\quad&\mbox{in}~B_{4r/\bar{\varepsilon}}(b_{\varepsilon}),\\
\phi_{n}=0\quad&\mbox{on}~\partial B_{4r/\bar{\varepsilon}}(b_{\varepsilon}),\\
\int_{B_{4r/\bar{\varepsilon}}(b_{\varepsilon})}\phi_{n}PQ_{A^{\prime}}^{4}\hat{Z}_{j}dx=0\quad\mbox{for}\quad j=0,\cdots,5,\end{cases}

for certain constants

, we shall prove that
\|\phi_{n}\|_{*}\to 0
to derive a contradiction.

Step 1
. We first establish the following:

\displaystyle\|\phi_{n}\|_{\mu}:=\sup_{y\in B_{4r/\bar{\varepsilon}}(b_{\varepsilon})}\left|\langle y-b_{\varepsilon}\rangle^{1-2\mu}\phi_{n}(y)\right|+\sup_{y\in B_{4r/\bar{\varepsilon}}(b_{\varepsilon})}\left|\langle y-b_{\varepsilon}\rangle^{2-2\mu}D\phi_{n}(y)\right|\to 0

with
\mu>0
being a small fixed number. We shall also prove this statement by contradiction. Without loss of generality, we may take
\|\phi_{n}\|_{\mu}=1
. Multiplying the equation against
\hat{Z}_{\ell}
, integrating by parts twice, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{5}c_{j}\int_{B_{\frac{4r}{\bar{\varepsilon}}}(b_{\varepsilon})}PQ_{A^{\prime}}^{4}\hat{Z}_{j}\hat{Z}_{\ell}dy=\int_{B_{\frac{4r}{\bar{\varepsilon}}}(b_{\varepsilon})}(\Delta\hat{Z}_{\ell}+\lambda\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\hat{Z}_{\ell}+5PQ_{A^{\prime}}\hat{Z}_{\ell})\phi_{n}dy-\int_{B_{\frac{4r}{\bar{\varepsilon}}}(b_{\varepsilon})}h_{n}\hat{Z}_{\ell}dy.

By straightforward calculation one can verify that
\big(\int_{B_{{4r}/{\bar{\varepsilon}}}(b_{\varepsilon})}PQ_{A^{\prime}}^{4}\hat{Z}_{j}\hat{Z}_{l}dy\big)_{j\ell}
is an invertible matrix. While, one can easily prove that the right-hand side of the above equation is bounded by

o(1)\|\phi_{n}\|_{\mu}+C\|h_{n}\|_{**}.

Thus, we conclude that

(6.5)

|c_{j}|\leq C(\|h_{n}\|_{**}+o(1)\|\phi_{n}\|_{\mu}),\hskip 10.00002ptj=0,1,\cdots,5,

so that
c_{j}=o(1).
Then by Green’s representation formula we can derive the following estimation

|\phi_{n}(y)|+\langle y-b_{\varepsilon}\rangle|D\phi_{n}(y)|\leq C(\|\phi_{n}\|_{\mu}+\|h_{n}\|_{**})\langle y-b_{\varepsilon}\rangle^{-1}.

In particular,

\langle y-b_{\varepsilon}\rangle^{1-2\mu}|\phi_{n}(y)|\leq C\langle y-b_{\varepsilon}\rangle^{-2\mu}.

Since
\|\phi_{n}\|_{\mu}=1
, we assume that
\|\phi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}>\gamma
for certain
R>0
and
\gamma>0
independently of
\varepsilon
. Then local elliptic estimates and the bounds above yield that, up to a subsequence
\phi_{n}(y+b_{\varepsilon})

\Delta\phi+5Q^{4}\phi=0,\hskip 10.00002pt|\phi(y)|\leq C|y|^{-1}.

Due to the non-degenerate result in Proposition

\phi\equiv 0

Step 3

Next we shall show that equation (

Therefore, we can conclude that there exists

7.
The finite-dimensional reduction and the critical point

In this section, we will first set up the reduction that transforms the original infinite-dimensional problem into a finite-dimensional one. Then, we will determine the critical points of the energy with respect to the parameters, using these to establish the proof of Theorem

Suppose that

and estimates (

then we get that

is equivalent to say

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{3}(\alpha_{w},\hat{\alpha}_{b})\mathcal{\hat{A}}^{\gamma}(\alpha_{w},\hat{\alpha}_{b})^{T}+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{3}K^{3}}{\log K}(\alpha_{w}^{2}+\hat{\alpha}_{b}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle\geq~

\displaystyle\lambda_{\gamma}\varepsilon^{3}(\alpha_{w}^{2}+\hat{\alpha}_{b}^{2})+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{3}K^{3}}{\log K}(\alpha_{w}^{2}+\hat{\alpha}_{b}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle\geq~

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\delta^{-1}\min\left\{\frac{5\zeta(3)}{2\pi^{3}},\frac{93\zeta(5)}{8\pi^{5}}\right\}\varepsilon^{3}K(\log K)^{2}.

Note that
J(PQ_{A}+\phi_{A})=C_{q}+\Psi(A_{0})+\Psi(A)-\Psi(A_{0})+O(\varepsilon^{3}K(\log K)^{2})
. Choosing
\delta
small and
K
large, the above analysis implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{4}(0,0)<\mathcal{J}_{4}(\alpha_{w},\hat{\alpha}_{b}),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,|\alpha_{w}|\text{ or }|\hat{\alpha}_{b}|=\delta^{-1/2}K^{-1}\log K.

Now combining the previous (1)-(4) parts, we know that the infimum of
J(PQ_{A}+\phi_{A})
must be achieved when the parameters
\varepsilon,\xi,a,d,\alpha_{b},\alpha_{w}
are in the interior of the constraint set. Hence we finish the whole proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Theorem
7.2
, it follows that
\inf_{A\in\mathscr{C}}J(PQ_{A}+\phi_{A})
is attained in the interior of
\mathscr{C}
. By Remark
4.6
and Proposition
6.2
,
J(PQ_{A}+\phi_{A})
is at least
C^{1}
on the parameters of
A
. Then the partial derivatives of
J(PQ_{A}+\phi_{A})
with respect to the six parameters of
A
are zero at a minimum point in the interior of
\mathscr{C}
. Then using Lemma
7.1
, we find a nontrivial solution to (
1.12
) and it proves Theorem
1.2
.
∎

In this section, we present essential estimates for
\gamma
and
H_{0}^{e}
, which are necessary for the analyses discussed in the preceding sections. These two functions exhibit certain computational similarities. We provide a detailed computation for
\gamma
and outline the corresponding steps for
H_{0}^{e}
.

8.1.
Estimates on Type I

We estimate
\gamma(z,p)
in this subsection. Recall

(8.1)

\displaystyle\gamma(z,p):=\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{2i\theta_{0}}-p|}-\sum_{j=1}^{K/2-1}\left(\frac{1}{|ze^{4ji\theta_{0}}-p|}-\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{(4j+2)i\theta_{0}}-p|}\right).

The following lemma gives a rough bound of
\gamma
and its derivatives.

Lemma 8.1
.

For any
z,b\in\Sigma_{K}
with
b=(|b|e^{i\alpha_{b}},0)^{T}
and
|b|>\frac{1}{2}
, one has

\displaystyle|\nabla^{m}\gamma(z,p)|_{p=b}|\leq CK^{m+1},\hskip 10.00002ptm=0,1,2,3,4.

Proof.

Let us first prove the case
m=0
.
Suppose
z=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=(re^{i\alpha_{z}},z_{3})
. We recall (
3.10
) and (
3.11
).
Since
|\alpha_{z}\pm\alpha_{b}|<2\theta_{0}
, one can see that

2j\theta_{0}+\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{z}-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{b}\in[(2j-1)\theta_{0},(2j+1)\theta_{0}],

(2j+1)\theta_{0}-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{z}-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{b}\in[2j\theta_{0},(2j+2)\theta_{0}].

Moreover, they both belong to
[0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]
when
1\leq j\leq j_{0}=\lfloor K/4-1\rfloor
, the largest integer less than or equal to
K/4-1
. Thus, (
3.10
), (
3.11
) and the monotonicity of Sine function on
[0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]
imply that

\displaystyle|\bar{z}e^{2i\theta_{0}}-b|<|ze^{4i\theta_{0}}-b|<|\bar{z}e^{6i\theta_{0}}-b|<\cdots<|\bar{z}e^{(4j_{0}+2)i\theta_{0}}-b|.

Therefore

(8.2)

\displaystyle 0<\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{|ze^{4ji\theta_{0}}-b|}-\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{(4j+2)i\theta_{0}}-b|}\right)<\frac{1}{|ze^{4i\theta_{0}}-b|}-\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{(4j_{0}+2)i\theta_{0}}-b|}.

When
j\geq j_{1}=\lceil K/4+1/2\rceil
, the least integer greater than or equal to
K/4+1/2
. In this case, we have

2j\theta_{0}+\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{z}-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{b}
and
(2j+1)\theta_{0}-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{z}-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{b}
belong to
[\tfrac{\pi}{2},\pi]
. Thus (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) and the monotonicity of Sine function on
[\tfrac{\pi}{2},\pi]
imply that

\displaystyle|ze^{4j_{1}i\theta_{0}}-b|>|\bar{z}e^{(4j_{1}+2)i\theta_{0}}-b|>\cdots>|\bar{z}e^{2(K-1)i\theta_{0}}-b|.

Therefore

(8.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|ze^{4j_{1}i\theta_{0}}-b|}-\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{2(K-1)i\theta_{0}}-b|}<\sum_{j=j_{1}}^{K/2-1}\left(\frac{1}{|ze^{4ji\theta_{0}}-b|}-\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{(4j+2)i\theta_{0}}-b|}\right)<0.

For
j_{0}\leq j\leq j_{1}
, one has that
ze^{4ji\theta_{0}}
and
\bar{z}e^{(4j+2)i\theta_{0}}
are far from
b
. More precisely,
|ze^{4ji\theta_{0}}-b|\geq|b|
, and
|ze^{(4j+2)i\theta_{0}}-b|\geq|b|
.
Since
j_{1}-j_{0}\leq 3
, then

\left|\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{j_{1}-1}\left(\frac{1}{|ze^{4ji\theta_{0}}-b|}-\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{(4j+2)i\theta_{0}}-b|}\right)\right|\leq\frac{6}{|b|}.

Combining the above with (
8.2
) and (
8.3
), we get

(8.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma(z,b)&\leq\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{2i\theta_{0}}-b|}+\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{2(K-1)i\theta_{0}}-b|}-\frac{1}{|ze^{4j_{1}i\theta_{0}}-b|}+\frac{6}{|b|},\\
\gamma(z,b)&\geq\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{2i\theta_{0}}-b|}-\frac{1}{|ze^{4i\theta_{0}}-b|}+\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{(4j_{0}+2)i\theta_{0}}-b|}-\frac{6}{|b|}.\end{split}

Note that for any
z,b\in\Sigma_{K}
, one has
1/(2K)\leq|ze^{4ji\theta_{0}}-b|\leq 2
for any
j\geq 1
. This also holds for
|\bar{z}e^{2(K-1)i\theta_{0}}-b|
and
|ze^{4i\theta_{0}}-b|
. Thus, the above inequalities imply that

|\gamma(z,b)|\leq CK.

For the derivatives of
\gamma
, using the estimates like (
3.14
), it is easy to show that for
m\geq 1

\displaystyle|\nabla^{m}\gamma(z,b)|\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{(4j+2)i\theta_{0}}-b|^{m+1}}+C\sum_{j=1}^{K/2-1}\frac{1}{|ze^{4ji\theta_{0}}-b|^{m+1}}.

Invoking (
3.9
), one has

|\nabla^{m}\gamma(z,b)|\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\csc^{m+1}j\theta_{0}\leq CK^{m+1}.

Hence we finish the proof.
∎

In this particular case
z=b
, we have a more precise estimate.

Lemma 8.2
.

Suppose that
b=(|b|e^{i\alpha_{b}},0)^{T}
satisfies
|b|>\tfrac{1}{2}
and
|\alpha_{b}|<\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle\gamma(b,b)=\frac{1}{2|b|}\left[\hat{S}_{1}(K)+O(K^{3}\alpha_{b}^{2})\right].

where
\hat{S}_{1}(K)
is defined in Lemma
9.1
.

Proof.

Using (
3.10
), (
3.11
) and
|\alpha_{b}|<\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{0}
, we obtain
|be^{4ji\theta_{0}}-b|=2|b|\sin 2j\theta_{0}
and
|\bar{b}e^{(4j+2)i\theta_{0}}-b|=2|b|\sin((2j+1)\theta_{0}-\alpha_{b})
. Then

\displaystyle\gamma(b,b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2|b|}\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\frac{1}{\sin((2j+1)\theta_{0}-\alpha_{b})}-\frac{1}{2|b|}\sum_{j=1}^{K/2-1}\sin(2j\theta_{0}).

The following expansion holds uniformly for any
\theta\in(0,\pi)
and
|\alpha_{b}|<\min\{\tfrac{\theta}{2},\pi-\tfrac{\theta}{2}\}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sin(\theta-\alpha_{b})}=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}+\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin^{2}\theta}\alpha_{b}+O\left(\frac{\alpha_{b}^{2}}{\sin^{3}\theta}\right).

Replacing
\theta=(2j+1)\theta_{0}
in the above and summing on
j
from 0 to
K/2-1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\frac{1}{\sin((2j+1)\theta_{0}-\alpha_{b})}=\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\frac{1}{\sin(2j+1)\theta_{0}}+O(K^{3}\alpha_{b}^{2}).

Here the odd term on
\alpha_{b}
vanishes because
\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\csc((2j+1)\theta_{0}-\alpha_{b})
is an even function on
\alpha_{b}
. One can see this by changing
j
to
K/2-1-j
in the summation.
Consequently

\displaystyle\gamma(b,b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2|b|}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{\sin(j\theta_{0})}+O(K^{3}\alpha_{b}^{2})\right)=\frac{1}{2|b|}\left[\hat{S}_{1}(K)+O(K^{3}\alpha_{b}^{2})\right],

where we have used the notations in Lemma
9.1
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

Suppose that
w=(|w|e^{i\alpha_{w}},0)^{T}
and
b=(|b|e^{i\alpha_{b}},0)^{T}
satisfy
|b|>\frac{1}{2}
and
|\alpha_{b}|<\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle w\cdot\nabla_{z}\gamma(b,b)=\frac{|w|}{4|b|^{2}}\left[-\hat{S}_{1}(K)+O(K(\log K)\alpha_{w}^{2}+K^{3}|\alpha_{w}\alpha_{b}|+K^{3}\alpha_{b}^{2})\right]

and the same expansion applies to
w\cdot\nabla_{p}\gamma(b,b)
.

Proof.

Step 1
: It is straightforward to verify that for any
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}|ze^{i\theta}-p|^{-1}=-|ze^{i\theta}-p|^{-3}(z-pe^{-i\theta}).

Thus

(8.5)

\displaystyle w\cdot\nabla_{z}|ze^{i\theta}-p|^{-1}\big|_{z=b,p=b}=\frac{w\cdot(be^{-i\theta}-b)}{|be^{i\theta}-b|^{3}}=-\frac{|w|}{(2|b|)^{2}}\frac{\sin(\tfrac{\theta}{2}+\alpha_{w}-\alpha_{b})}{\sin^{2}\tfrac{\theta}{2}},

where we have used
|be^{i\theta}-b|=2|b|\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}
and

(8.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}w\cdot(be^{-i\theta}-b)&=|w||b|(\cos(\alpha_{b}-\theta-\alpha_{w})-\cos(\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{w}))\\
&=2|w||b|\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\sin(\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{w}-\tfrac{\theta}{2}).\end{split}

Step 2
: It is easy to get that for any
\theta\in[0,2\pi]

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}|\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p|^{-1}=-|\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p|^{-3}(z-\bar{p}e^{i\theta}).

Thus when
|\alpha_{b}|<\frac{1}{2}\theta

(8.7)

\displaystyle w\cdot\nabla|\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p|^{-1}\big|_{z=b,p=b}=\frac{w\cdot(\bar{b}e^{i\theta}-b)}{|\bar{b}e^{i\theta}-b|^{3}}=-\frac{|w|}{(2|b|)^{2}}\frac{\sin(\tfrac{\theta}{2}-\alpha_{w})}{\sin^{2}(\tfrac{\theta}{2}-\alpha_{b})},

where we have used
|\bar{b}e^{i\theta}-b|=|b|\sin(\frac{\theta}{2}-\alpha_{b})
and

(8.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}w\cdot(\bar{b}e^{i\theta}-b)&=|w||b|(\cos(\theta-\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{w})-\cos(\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{w}))\\
&=2|w||b|\sin(\alpha_{b}-\tfrac{\theta}{2})\sin(\tfrac{\theta}{2}-\alpha_{w}).\end{split}

Step 3
: Plugging in
\theta=4j\theta_{0}
in (
8.5
) and
\theta=(4j+2)\theta_{0}
in (
8.7
), taking sum on
j
, we obtain

(8.9)

\displaystyle w\cdot\nabla_{z}\gamma(z,p)\big|_{z=b,p=b}=\frac{|w|}{(2|b|)^{2}}[I_{1}-I_{2}],

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{K/2-1}\frac{\sin(2j\theta_{0}+\alpha_{w}-\alpha_{b})}{\sin^{2}2j\theta_{0}},\hskip 10.00002ptI_{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\frac{\sin((2j+1)\theta_{0}-\alpha_{w})}{\sin^{2}((2j+1)\theta_{0}-\alpha_{b})}.

Think of
I_{1}=I_{1}(\alpha_{w},\alpha_{b})
and
I_{2}=I_{2}(\alpha_{w},\alpha_{b})
as a function of
\alpha_{w}
and
\alpha_{b}
. We want to do the Taylor expansion of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
for
\alpha_{b},\alpha_{w}
. Changing
j
to
K/2-j
, we find that
I_{1}(\alpha_{w},\alpha_{b})=I_{1}(-\alpha_{w},-\alpha_{b})
. Changing
j
to
K/2-1-j
in
I_{2}
, we find that
I_{2}(\alpha_{w},\alpha_{b})=I_{2}(-\alpha_{w},-\alpha_{b})
. In fact, using the estimates in Lemma
9.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{K/2-1}\csc 2j\theta_{0}+O((K\log K)(\alpha_{w}^{2}+\alpha_{b}^{2})),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\csc(2j+1)\theta_{0}+O(K(\log K)\alpha_{w}^{2}+K^{3}|\alpha_{w}\alpha_{b}|+K^{3}\alpha_{b}^{2}).

Therefore, using
\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}(-1)^{j}\csc j\theta_{0}=-\hat{S}_{1}(K)
from Lemma
9.1
,

\displaystyle I_{1}-I_{2}

\displaystyle=-\hat{S}_{1}(K)+O(K(\log K)\alpha_{w}^{2}+K^{3}\alpha_{w}\alpha_{b}+K^{3}\alpha_{b}^{2}).

Inserting this back to (
8.9
), we get the expansion of
w\cdot\nabla_{z}\gamma(b,b)
.

For
w\cdot\nabla_{p}\gamma(b,b)
, the proof goes like the previous one by using the following two identities

\displaystyle\nabla_{p}|ze^{i\theta}-p|^{-1}=|ze^{i\theta}-p|^{-3}(ze^{i\theta}-p),

\displaystyle\nabla_{p}|\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p|^{-1}=|\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p|^{-3}(\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p).

∎

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
w=(|w|e^{i\alpha_{w}},0)^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
b=(|b|e^{i\alpha_{b}},0)^{T}
. If
\alpha_{w}\leq 1
and
|\alpha_{b}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle w^{T}\nabla^{2}_{z,p}\gamma(b,b)w=\frac{|w|^{2}}{8|b|^{3}}\left[\hat{S}_{1}(K)+\hat{S}_{3}(K)+(\alpha_{w},\alpha_{b})\mathcal{A}^{\gamma}(\alpha_{w},\alpha_{b})^{T}+O(\alpha_{w}^{4}K^{3}+\alpha_{b}^{4}K^{7})\right],

where we used the notations in the Lemma
9.1
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}S_{3}^{o}(K)-2S_{1}^{o}(K)+3\hat{S}_{3}(K)&-3\hat{S}_{3}(K)+3S_{1}^{o}(K)\\
-3\hat{S}_{3}(K)+3S_{1}^{o}(K)&\frac{3}{2}[4S_{5}^{o}(K)+S_{3}^{o}(K)-3S_{1}^{o}(K)]+3\hat{S}_{3}(K)\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

Step 1
: Recall

\displaystyle\partial_{z_{\ell},p_{j}}^{2}|z-p|^{-1}=\frac{1}{|z-p|^{3}}\left(\delta_{\ell j}-3\frac{(z-p)_{\ell}(z-p)_{j}}{|z-p|^{2}}\right).

Using
ze^{i\theta}=R_{\theta}z
,
it is easy to verify that

(\nabla^{2}_{z,p}|ze^{i\theta}-p|^{-1})=|ze^{i\theta}-p|^{-3}R_{-\theta}\left(\delta_{\ell j}-3\frac{(ze^{i\theta}-p)_{\ell}(ze^{i\theta}-p)_{j}}{|ze^{i\theta}-p|^{2}}\right).

For any vector
w=(|w|e^{i\alpha_{w}},0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
z=b
with
p=b
,

\displaystyle w^{T}(\nabla^{2}_{z,p}|ze^{i\theta}-p|^{-1})w|_{z=b,p=b}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|be^{i\theta}-b|^{3}}\left[|w|^{2}\cos\theta+3\frac{[w\cdot(be^{i\theta}-b)][w\cdot(be^{-i\theta}-b)]}{|be^{i\theta}-b|^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{|w|^{2}}{|be^{i\theta}-b|^{3}}\left[\cos\theta-3\sin(\tfrac{\theta}{2}+\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{w})\sin(-\tfrac{\theta}{2}+\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{w})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{|w|^{2}}{|be^{i\theta}-b|^{3}}\left[\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\cos(\theta-2\alpha_{w})\right],

where we have used (
8.6
) in the second equal sign.
When
\theta=4j\theta_{0}
, we have

(8.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&w^{T}(\nabla^{2}_{z,p}|ze^{4ji\theta_{0}}-p|^{-1})w|_{z=b,p=b}=\frac{|w|^{2}}{(2|b|)^{3}}\frac{\left[-\cos 4j\theta_{0}+3\cos 2(\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{w})\right]}{2(\sin 2j\theta_{0})^{3}}.\end{split}

Step 2
: Similarly, we can compute the other one. Recall that

\bar{z}e^{i\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta&0\\
\sin\theta&-\cos\theta&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}\\
z_{2}\\
z_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle(\nabla_{z,p}^{2}|\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p|^{-1})=\frac{1}{|\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p|^{3}}\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta&0\\
\sin\theta&-\cos\theta&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\left(\delta_{\ell j}-3\frac{(\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p)_{\ell}(\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p)_{j}}{|\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p|^{2}}\right).

For any
w=(|w|e^{i\alpha_{w}},0)^{T}
, and
z=b
with
p=b

\displaystyle w^{T}(\nabla^{2}_{z,p}|\bar{z}e^{i\theta}-p|^{-1})w|_{z=b,p=b}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\bar{b}e^{i\theta}-b|^{3}}\left[|w|^{2}\cos(\theta-2\alpha_{w})+3\frac{[w\cdot(\bar{b}e^{i\theta}-b)]^{2}}{|\bar{b}e^{i\theta}-b|^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{|w|^{2}}{|\bar{b}e^{i\theta}-b|^{3}}\left[\cos(\theta-2\alpha_{w})+3|\sin(\tfrac{\theta}{2}-\alpha_{w})|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{|w|^{2}}{|\bar{b}e^{i\theta}-b|^{3}}\left[\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\cos(\theta-2\alpha_{w})\right],

where we have used (
8.8
).
When
\theta=(4j+2)\theta_{0}
, we have
|\bar{b}e^{(4j+2)i\theta_{0}}-b|=2|b|\sin(2j+1)\theta_{0}-\alpha_{b})
and

(8.11)

\displaystyle w^{T}(\nabla^{2}_{z,p}|\bar{z}e^{(4j+2)i\theta_{0}}-p|^{-1})w|_{z=b,p=b}=\frac{|w|^{2}}{(2|b|)^{3}}\frac{3-\cos((4j+2)\theta_{0}-2\alpha_{w})}{2(\sin((2j+1)\theta_{0}-\alpha_{b}))^{3}}.

Step 3
: Inserting the above formula (
8.10
) and (
8.11
) to (
8.1
), we have

(8.12)

\displaystyle w^{T}(\nabla_{z,p}^{2}\gamma(z,p))w|_{z=b,p=b}=\frac{|w|^{2}}{(2|b|)^{3}}[I_{1}-I_{2}],

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\frac{3-\cos((4j+2)\theta_{0}-2\alpha_{w}))}{2(\sin((2j+1)\theta_{0}-\alpha_{b}))^{3}},\hskip 10.00002ptI_{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{K/2-1}\frac{-\cos 4j\theta_{0}+3\cos 2(\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{w})}{2(\sin 2j\theta_{0})^{3}}.

Think of
I_{1}=I_{1}(\alpha_{w},\alpha_{b})
and
I_{2}=I_{2}(\alpha_{w},\alpha_{b})
as a function of
\alpha_{w}
and
\alpha_{b}
. It is easy to know

and
I_{1}(\alpha_{w},\alpha_{b})=I_{1}(-\alpha_{w},-\alpha_{b})
.

For
I_{1}
, the following expansion holds uniformly for
K
when
|\alpha_{w}|\leq 1
and
|\alpha_{b}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}
,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\frac{3-\cos(4j+2)\theta_{0}}{2(\sin(2j+1)\theta_{0})^{3}}+[A_{11}\alpha_{w}^{2}+2A_{12}\alpha_{w}\alpha_{b}+A_{22}\alpha_{b}^{2}]+O(\alpha_{w}^{4}K^{3}+\alpha_{b}^{4}K^{7}),

where

\displaystyle A_{11}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\frac{\cos(4j+2)\theta_{0}}{(\sin(2j+1)\theta_{0})^{3}},\hskip 10.00002ptA_{12}=\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\frac{-3(\cos((2j+1)\theta_{0}))^{2}}{(\sin(2j+1)\theta_{0})^{3}},

\displaystyle A_{22}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\frac{3[4+(\sin(2j+1)\theta_{0})^{2}-3(\sin(2j+1)\theta_{0})^{4}]}{2(\sin(2j+1)\theta_{0})^{5}}.

For
I_{2}
, the following expansion holds uniformly for
K
when
|\alpha_{w}|\leq 1
and
|\alpha_{b}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}
,

\displaystyle I_{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{K/2-1}\frac{3-\cos 4j\theta_{0}}{2(\sin 2j\theta_{0})^{3}}+[B_{11}\alpha_{w}^{2}+2B_{12}\alpha_{w}\alpha_{b}+B_{22}\alpha_{b}^{2}]+O(\alpha_{w}^{4}K^{3}+\alpha_{b}^{4}K^{3}),

where

\displaystyle B_{11}=\sum_{j=1}^{K/2-1}\frac{-3}{(\sin 2j\theta_{0})^{3}}\hskip 10.00002ptB_{12}=-B_{11},\hskip 10.00002ptB_{22}=B_{11}.

Combining the leading terms of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{K/2-1}\frac{1+(\sin(2j+1)\theta_{0})^{2}}{(\sin(2j+1)\theta_{0})^{3}}-\sum_{j=1}^{K/2-1}\frac{1+(\sin 2j\theta_{0})^{2}}{(\sin 2j\theta_{0})^{3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{(\sin(j\theta_{0}))^{3}}+\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{(\sin(j\theta_{0}))}

\displaystyle=\hat{S}_{3}(K)+\hat{S}_{1}(K).

Here we have used the notations in Lemma
9.1
.
For the quadratic terms, using some trigonometric identities, we can rewrite them as

A_{11}=S_{3}^{o}(K)-2S_{1}^{o}(K)
,
A_{12}=-3(S_{3}^{o}(K)-S_{1}^{o}(K))
,

one can derive that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}\csc\left(\phi+\tfrac{2j\pi}{n}\right)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\frac{\phi}{\pi}}}{x(1+x)}\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}x^{\frac{2j}{n}}dx=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\frac{\phi}{\pi}}(x-1)}{x(1+x)(x^{\frac{2}{n}}-1)}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\frac{n\phi y}{2\pi}}(e^{\frac{ny}{2}}-1)}{(1+e^{\frac{ny}{2}})(e^{y}-1)}dy=\frac{n}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{[e^{\frac{n\phi y}{2\pi}}+e^{y-\frac{n\phi y}{2\pi}}](e^{\frac{yn}{2}}-1)}{(1+e^{\frac{yn}{2}})(e^{y}-1)}dy,

where we make a change of variable
x=e^{\frac{ny}{2}}
, split the integral and change the integral on
(-\infty,0)
to
(0,\infty)
. Furthermore, if we set
y=2t
, then

(9.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}\csc\left(\phi+\tfrac{2j\pi}{n}\right)=\frac{n}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\tanh\frac{nt}{2}}{\sinh t}\cosh\left(\tfrac{n\phi}{\pi}t-t\right)dt.

Setting
\phi=\pi/n
, we have

(9.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}\csc\tfrac{(2j+1)\pi}{n}=\frac{n}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\tanh\frac{n}{2}t}{\sinh t}dt.

Taking derivative of (
9.5
) on
\phi
twice and using
(\csc x)^{\prime\prime}=2\csc^{3}x-\csc x
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}2\csc^{3}\left(\phi+\tfrac{2j\pi}{n}\right)-\csc\left(\phi+\tfrac{2j\pi}{n}\right)=\left(\frac{n}{\pi}\right)^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2}\tanh\frac{nt}{2}}{\sinh t}\cosh\left(\tfrac{n\phi}{\pi}t-t\right)dt.

Setting
\phi=\pi/n
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}\csc^{3}\tfrac{(2j+1)\pi}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}\csc\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{n}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{\pi}\right)^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2}\tanh\frac{n}{2}t}{\sinh t}dt.

To compute its leading order. First, it is easy to show
\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}\csc\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{n}=O(n\log n)
. Second,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2}\tanh\frac{n}{2}t}{\sinh t}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2}}{\sinh t}\left(1+\frac{-2}{e^{nt}+1}\right)dt.

For the first part,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2}}{\sinh t}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2t^{2}e^{-t}}{1-e^{-2t}}dt=2\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-(2j+1)t}dt=4\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2j+1)^{-3}=\frac{7}{2}\zeta(3),

where
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function.
For the second part, using
\sinh t\geq t
for
t>0

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2}}{\sinh t}\frac{1}{1+e^{nt}}dt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}te^{-nt}dt=n^{-2}.

Summarizing the above analysis,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}\csc^{3}\tfrac{(2j+1)\pi}{n}=\frac{7\zeta(3)}{4\pi^{3}}n^{3}+O(n\log n).

Differentiating (
9.5
) five times on
\phi
and
using
\frac{d^{4}}{dx^{4}}\csc x=(24-20(\sin x)^{2}+(\sin x)^{4})(\csc x)^{5}
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}\csc^{5}\tfrac{(2j+1)\pi}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}\left(\frac{n}{\pi}\right)^{5}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{4}\tanh\frac{n}{2}t}{\sinh t}dt+O(n^{3})=\frac{93\zeta(5)}{48\pi^{5}}n^{5}+O(n^{3}).

This finishes the odd sum. For the even sum, we can compute similarly.

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n/2-1}\csc\left(\phi+\tfrac{2j\pi}{n}\right)=\frac{n}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh[(\frac{n}{2}-1)t]}{(\sinh t)(\cosh\frac{nt}{2})}\cosh\left(\frac{n\phi}{\pi}t\right)dt.

Letting
\phi=0
, we have

(9.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n/2-1}\csc\tfrac{2j\pi}{n}=\frac{n}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh[(\frac{n}{2}-1)t]}{(\sinh t)(\cosh\frac{n}{2}t)}dt.

Taking derivative on
\phi
twice, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n/2-1}2\csc^{3}\left(\phi+\tfrac{2j\pi}{n}\right)-\csc\left(\phi+\tfrac{2j\pi}{n}\right)=\left(\frac{n}{\pi}\right)^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2}\sinh[(\frac{n}{2}-1)t]}{(\sinh t)(\cosh\frac{nt}{2})}\cosh\left(\frac{n\phi}{\pi}t\right)dt.

Setting
\phi=0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n/2-1}\csc^{3}\tfrac{2j\pi}{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n/2-1}\csc\tfrac{2j\pi}{n}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{n^{3}}{\pi^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2}\sinh[(\tfrac{n}{2}-1)t]}{(\sinh t)(\cosh\tfrac{n}{2}t)}dt.

Compute the leading order of them, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n/2-1}\csc^{3}\tfrac{2j\pi}{n}=\frac{\zeta(3)}{4\pi^{3}}n^{3}+O(n\log n).

Using (
9.6
) and (
9.7
), one has

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j+1}\csc\frac{j\pi}{n}=\frac{n}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh\frac{n}{2}t-\sinh(\frac{n}{2}-1)t}{\sinh t\cosh\frac{n}{2}t}dt.

It is easy to verify the following identity

\displaystyle\sinh\tfrac{n}{2}t-\sinh(\tfrac{n}{2}-1)t=(\cosh\tfrac{n}{2}t)(1-e^{-t})+\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{n}{2}t}(e^{t}+e^{-t}-2).

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{n}{2}t}(e^{t}+e^{-t}-2)}{\sinh t\cosh\frac{n}{2}t}dt=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{t}-1}{(1+e^{nt})(e^{t}+1)}dt=O(n^{-1}).

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j+1}\csc\tfrac{j\pi}{n}=\frac{n}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-e^{-t}}{\sinh t}dt+O(1)=\frac{n}{\pi}\log 4+O(1).

∎

Lemma 9.2
.

When
x\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle S^{o}_{k}(n,x)+S^{e}_{k}(n,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(x^{2}+\sin^{2}\tfrac{j\pi}{n})^{-\frac{k}{2}}\leq C\begin{cases}n|\log x|&\text{if }k=1,\\
nx^{1-k}&\text{if }k\geq 2.\end{cases}

Proof.

It is easy to see the following

\displaystyle S^{o}_{k}(n,x)+S^{e}_{k}(n,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(x^{2}+\sin^{2}\tfrac{j\pi}{n})^{-\frac{k}{2}}=2\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}(x^{2}+\sin^{2}\tfrac{j\pi}{n})^{-\frac{k}{2}}.

We divide the sum into two parts. Let
j_{0}=\lfloor nx\rfloor
, the greatest integer less than or equal to
nx
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}(x^{2}+\sin^{2}\tfrac{j\pi}{n})^{-\frac{k}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq x^{-k}nx=Cnx^{1-k},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{n-1}(x^{2}+\sin^{2}\tfrac{j\pi}{n})^{-\frac{k}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{n-1}\csc^{k}\tfrac{j\pi}{n}\leq Cn^{k}\sum_{j_{0}}^{n-1}j^{-k}\leq C\begin{cases}n|\log x|&\text{if }k=1,\\
nx^{1-k}&\text{if }k\geq 2.\end{cases}

∎

and

for all

\csc(nz)

Lemma 9.4
.

Suppose that
n
is even and
x=(1+o_{n}(1))\frac{\log n}{n}
as
n\to\infty
. Then

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,x)=\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}}n(nx)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-nx}(1+O((nx)^{-1})),

\displaystyle S_{3}(n,x)=\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}}n^{3}(nx)^{-\frac{3}{2}}e^{-nx}(1+O((nx)^{-1})),

\displaystyle S_{5}(n,x)=\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}}n^{5}(nx)^{-\frac{5}{2}}e^{-nx}(1+O((nx)^{-1})).

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
9.3
that

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}(x^{2}+(\sin\frac{j\pi}{n})^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{2n}{\pi}\int_{\arcsinh x}^{\infty}\frac{\csch n\phi}{\sqrt{\sinh^{2}\phi-x^{2}}}d\phi.

Making a change of variable
\sinh\phi=x\cosh u
to the above formula, one has

(9.8)

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,x)=\frac{2n}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du.

First, the integral is very small when
u
is away from
0
. In fact, since
\arcsinh t\sim t
as
t\to 0
and
\cosh(2)>3
, then
\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))\leq Ce^{-3nx}
if
u>2
and
n
is large enough. Thus

(9.9)

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du\leq Ce^{-3nx}\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du.

Making a change of variable
\tanh u=t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1-t^{2})(1+x^{2}-t^{2})}}dt=\frac{\mbox{EllipticK}((1+x^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}})}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}},

where

(9.10)

\displaystyle\mbox{EllipticK}(\sigma)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{(1-t^{2})(1-\sigma^{2}t^{2})}}.

Using
[
7
, Formula 900.05]
we have

(9.11)

\mbox{EllipticK}(\sigma)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\left(\begin{matrix}-\frac{1}{2}\\
\ell\end{matrix}\right)^{2}\left[\log\frac{4}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}-b_{\ell}\right](1-m^{2})^{\ell},

with

b_{0}=0,\hskip 10.00002ptb_{\ell}=2\sum_{j=1}^{2\ell}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{j}=b_{\ell-1}+\frac{2}{2\ell(2\ell-1)}.

Using (
9.11
), we have

(9.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{EllipticK}((1+x^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}})}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}=-\log x+\log 4+O(x^{2})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }x\to 0.

Insert the above estimates back to (
9.9
),

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du\leq Ce^{-3nx}\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du\leq C|\log x|e^{-3nx}.

Second, for
u\in[0,2]
, we recall the Taylor expansion

(9.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\arcsinh(t)&=t-\frac{t^{3}}{6}+O(t^{5})\quad\text{as }t\to 0,\\
\csch t&=2e^{-t}+2e^{-3t}+O(e^{-5t}),\quad\text{as }t\to\infty,\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}&=1-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}+O(t^{4}),\quad\text{as }t\to 0.\end{split}

Then we have the following expansion as
n\to\infty

\displaystyle\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+(x\cosh u)^{2}}}

\displaystyle=(2+O(nx^{3}))e^{-nx\cosh u}

holds uniformly for
u\in[0,2]
.
Using this expansion, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du

\displaystyle=(2+O(nx^{3}))\int_{0}^{2}e^{-nx\cosh u}du

\displaystyle=(2+O(nx^{3}))\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nx\cosh u}du+O(e^{-3nx})

\displaystyle=(2+O(nx^{3}))\mbox{BesselK}_{0}(nx)+O\left(e^{-3nx}\right),

where
\mbox{BesselK}_{\alpha}(t)
is the modified Bessel function.

\displaystyle\mbox{BesselK}_{\alpha}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\cosh u}\cosh(\alpha u)du.

It is well known that

\mbox{BesselK}_{0}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-t}(1+\frac{a_{1}}{t}+\frac{a_{2}}{t^{2}}+O(t^{-3})),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }t\to\infty.

where
a_{1},a_{2}
are some constants.
Since
nx\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\pi}(nx)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-nx}(1+O((nx)^{-1})).

Combining the two integral estimates and using
|\log x|e^{-2nx}=O((nx)^{-3/2})
, we get the asymptotic of
S_{1}
.

To get the asymptotic of
S_{3}
, we
take the derivative of (
9.8
) on
x

(9.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{3}(n,x)&=-\frac{1}{x}\frac{d}{dx}S_{1}(n,x)=\frac{2n}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{-1}{x}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du.\end{split}

It is straightforward to get

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-1}{x}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}=\frac{n}{x}\cdot\frac{f_{1}(n,x,u)}{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}+\frac{f_{2}(n,x,u)}{(1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u)^{3/2}},

where

f_{1}(n,x,u)=\coth(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))\cosh u,

f_{2}(n,x,u)=\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))\cosh^{2}u.

We shall study the right-hand side of (
9.14
) as we did for
S_{1}
. First, if
u>2

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-1}{x}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-3nx}\left(\frac{n}{x}\frac{\cosh u}{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}+\frac{\cosh^{2}u}{(1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u)^{3/2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-3nx}\frac{n}{x}\frac{\cosh u}{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}.

Making a change of variable
\cosh u=t^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\cosh u}{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}du=\int_{0}^{1/\cosh 2}\frac{1}{(x^{2}+t^{2})\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}dt\leq C\int_{0}^{1/\cosh 2}\frac{1}{x^{2}+t^{2}}dt\leq\frac{C}{x}.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{-1}{x}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du\leq Cnx^{-2}e^{-3nx}.

Second, consider
u\in[0,2]
. Using the expansion (
9.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-1}{x}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}=2nx^{-1}e^{-nx\cosh u}\cosh u(1+O(nx^{3})),

holds uniformly for
u\in[0,2]
. Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{-1}{x}\frac{d}{dx}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du=2nx^{-1}(1+O(nx^{3}))\int_{0}^{2}e^{-nx\cosh u}\cosh udu

\displaystyle=2nx^{-1}(1+O(nx^{3}))\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nx\cosh u}\cosh udu+O(nx^{-1}e^{-3nx})

\displaystyle=-2nx^{-1}(1+O(nx^{3}))\mbox{BesselK}^{\prime}_{0}(nx)+O(nx^{-1}e^{-3nx}),

where
\mbox{BesselK}^{\prime}_{\alpha}(t)=\tfrac{d}{dt}\mbox{BesselK}_{\alpha}(t)
. It follows from the expansion of
\mbox{BesselK}_{0}(t)
that

\displaystyle\mbox{BesselK}^{\prime}_{0}(t)=-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-t}(1+O(t^{-1})),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }t\to\infty.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{-1}{x}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\frac{\csch(n\arcsinh(x\cosh u))}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}\cosh^{2}u}}du=\sqrt{2\pi}nx^{-1}(nx)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-nx}(1+O((nx)^{-1})).

Combining the above estimates with the one in
(2,\infty)
, we get the asymptotic of
S_{3}
.

The proof of
S_{5}
can be obtained similarly to
S_{3}
. We omit the details.
∎

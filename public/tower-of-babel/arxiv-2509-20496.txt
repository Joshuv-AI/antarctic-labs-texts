Kernel Radon-Nikodym Derivatives for Random Matrix Ensembles
James Tian

Abstract
. We study random operator tuples and random matrix ensembles through positive definite kernels, conditional expectations, and dilation theory. A Radon-Nikodym theorem for kernels is established, showing how domination can be expressed by explicit density operators. For shifted kernels this reduces to simple moment-ratio tests. In bi-unitarily invariant and block-invariant ensembles, these criteria align with classical asymptotics such as the Marchenko-Pastur law, offering a clear and efficient way to detect threshold phenomena.

1.
Introduction

The interplay between operator-algebraic structure and random matrix
phenomena has long been a source of deep insight, especially through
the lens of free probability and operator-valued functional calculus.
Yet many classical proofs in random matrix theory rely heavily on
combinatorial expansions (e.g. noncrossing partitions, Weingarten
calculus) or analytic methods (resolvent estimates, Stieltjes transforms).
In contrast, this paper proposes a conceptual framework grounded in
positive kernels, conditional expectations, dilation theory, and a
Radon-Nikodym theorem for kernels. Our goal is to translate operator-theoretic
positivity constraints into concrete, testable inequalities for random
matrix ensembles, often with minimal combinatorial overhead.

The core idea is simple in outline but rich in implications. Given
a random operator tuple, one can form an operator-valued moment kernel.
By applying a conditional expectation onto a subalgebra (for instance,
diagonal or block-diagonal), one obtains a localized kernel that retains
positivity. A dilation theorem then allows us to lift inequalities
in this localized context back to the full kernel. To compare two
such kernels (or a shifted kernel and the original), we introduce
a Radon-Nikodym theorem at the kernel level: when one kernel dominates
another, the difference can be expressed via a unique positive operator
in a minimal Kolmogorov decomposition. In favorable cases, that operator
(the “density”) is explicit and diagonalizes nicely, making the positivity
condition equivalent to a simple monotonicity or ratio test on normalized
moments.

This scheme builds a bridge from abstract positivity to explicit moment
inequalities, and brings operator-algebraic structure to bear on random
matrix thresholds.

Background and Related Work.
Conditional expectations as
norm-one projections onto subalgebras date to Tomiyama, who proved
foundational existence and uniqueness theorems in
C^{*}
-algebra
settings
[
Tom57
,
Tom58
,
Tom59
]
. Takesaki extended that
to (normal) conditional expectations in the von Neumann algebra context

[
Tak72
,
Tak02
]
. These maps preserve positivity and have
bimodularity properties that make them ideal for “restricting” operator-valued
data without losing structural control. In random matrix contexts,
this corresponds to isolating coordinate or block information from
global operator kernels.

Classical dilation theory (Sz.-Nagy-Foiaş) shows that a contraction
can be extended to a unitary on a larger space, thereby enabling a
functional calculus via compression
[
SNFBK10
]
. In the noncommutative
or multivariable setting, Frazho-Bunce-Popescu’s dilation theorems
provide isometric dilations for tuples (row contractions) acting on
free semigroup algebras, realizing noncommutative von Neumann inequalities

[
Fra84
,
Bun84
,
Pop91
]
. Popescu’s foundational paper

[
Pop89
]
established a Wold-type decomposition and minimal
dilation for noncommuting tuples. Subsequent expositions place these
results in a broader context and tie them to noncommutative analytic
function theory and model theory
[
Sha21
]
. Also relevant
are operator-theoretic treatments of constrained dilations and noncommutative
varieties (e.g. Popescu’s work on operator theory on noncommutative
varieties) where kernel methods and commutant lifting interplay
[
Pop06
]
.

The classical Radon-Nikodym theorem for measures has analogues in
operator algebras via comparisons of positive functionals or completely
positive (CP) maps. Arveson introduced a Radon-Nikodym derivative
for CP maps (in the context of
*
-algebras and Stinespring dilations),
giving structural criteria for when one CP map is dominated by another

[
Arv72
]
. Belavkin and Staszewski gave a Radon-Nikodym theorem
for CP maps in 1986, expressing domination via a positive operator
in the commutant of a Stinespring representation
[
BS86
]
.
Raginsky’s “Radon-Nikodym derivatives of quantum operations” reviews
these formulations and provides alternate perspectives on the derivative
operator and its uniqueness
[
Rag03
]
. However, the idea of
comparing kernels by embedding a density operator in a Kolmogorov
decomposition is, to our knowledge, not widely known.

Random Matrix Theory, Moment Asymptotics, and Inequality Thresholds.

In random matrix theory, moment asymptotics are a central tool: for
example, the Marchenko-Pastur law describes the limiting eigenvalue
distribution of sample covariance matrices
[
MP67
]
, and cumulant
or moment methods (free probability, combinatorial expansions) are
used to derive fine bounds (see
[
BS10
,
AGZ10
]
). These
techniques often require intricate combinatorial control or resolvent
estimates. More recent refinements, such as those by Alexeev, Götze,
and Tikhomirov, offer precise estimates for higher moments and fluctuations,
especially for powers of random matrices
[
AGT10
]
.

Our approach is complementary to those methods: once moment limits
or estimates are known, one can simply plug them into our positivity
criteria (via the kernel Radon-Nikodym method) to test whether dilation
inequalities hold. In many ensembles, the density operator arising
in the Radon-Nikodym decomposition becomes diagonal with respect to
a canonical basis, and the requirement that it be a contraction reduces
exactly to a monotonicity condition on consecutive normalized moments
(i.e.
c_{m+1}\leq c_{m}
). Thus our method transforms combinatorial
or analytic work into a positivity test that uses known moment asymptotics
with structural clarity.

Recent Developments and Context.
In the past decade the field
of random matrix theory has shifted strongly toward universality,
local laws, and dynamical approaches, rather than relying purely on
combinatorial expansions. Erdős, Yau, and collaborators developed
the local semicircle law, eigenvalue rigidity, and the Dyson Brownian
motion method, as collected in
[
EY17
]
. The universality
proof in
[
ESY11
]
is a landmark in this area. Tao and Vu
demonstrated that local eigenvalue statistics depend only on the first
four moments of the entries
[
TV14
]
. They extended this
to eigenvectors as well
[
TV12b
]
. Work on universality for
covariance matrices, especially in the i.i.d. setting, has also progressed
(e.g. Random covariance matrices: Universality of local statistics
by Tao-Vu)
[
TV12a
]
. More recently, the matrix Dyson equation
(MDE) approach, which handles non-identically distributed and correlated
entries, has been developed by Erdős et al.
[
Erd19
]
.

Contributions.
In this work we propose a kernel-level Radon-Nikodym
theory, extending the ideas of Arveson and Belavkin-Staszewski from
CP maps to positive operator-valued kernels. When one kernel is dominated
by another, our theorem produces a unique positive operator in a minimal
Kolmogorov decomposition implementing the domination. In key settings,
especially the shifted kernel on the free semigroup, this operator
is explicit and often diagonalizes in a natural basis.

Coupled with localization via conditional expectations, this setting
allows us to reduce global positivity to blockwise or entrywise testing.
One composes the conditional expectation with dilation machinery to
obtain inequalities that can be checked on subalgebras. This localization
is essential in random matrix contexts, especially when checking blockwise
behavior or coordinate-level constraints.

Perhaps the most striking payoff is that in many random matrix ensembles
(e.g. bi-unitarily invariant or block-Ginibre models), the Radon-Nikodym
density becomes a simple sum of shift adjoint operators, and its contraction
condition is exactly a monotonicity or ratio test on normalized moments.
In practice, that means one can verify a dilation or von Neumann-type
inequality by checking whether
c_{m+1}\leq c_{m}
, a condition often
readily deduced from known asymptotic moment formulas (e.g. the Marchenko-Pastur
scaling). This avoids re-deriving combinatorial expansions for each
case, and instead embeds the test within the structural positivity.

Beyond its efficiency, the approach offers conceptual clarity: when
a density operator fails to be contractive, the violation pinpoints
a structural obstruction in the ensemble. Thus the method not only
recovers known thresholds but gives insight into why they arise, and
suggests how they might generalize to block or anisotropic settings
that are hard to analyze by combinatorial means.

Organization.
In Section
2
we define localized moment
kernels and develop their dilation theory under conditional expectations.
Section
3
proves the Radon-Nikodym theorem for operator-valued
kernels. Section
4
specializes to the shifted kernel on the
free semigroup and gives explicit formulas for the density operator.
Section
5
includes applications to random matrix ensembles,
both scalar and block models, translating positivity constraints into
moment-ratio tests and comparing results to classical thresholds.

2.
Localized Moment Kernels and Dilation Theory

This section lays out the basic objects: moment kernels arising from
random operator tuples, conditional expectations onto subalgebras,
and the associated dilation and von Neumann inequalities. These will
serve as the foundation for the computations in later sections, where
specific choices of subalgebras
\mathcal{B}
(such as diagonal or
block-diagonal algebras) lead to concrete inequalities for random
matrices.

Settings.
Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathbb{F}{}^{+}_{d}

be the free semigroup on generators
\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}
,
with neutral element
\emptyset
. For a word
\alpha=i_{1}\dots i_{k}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
,
write
\tilde{\alpha}=i_{k}\cdots i_{1}
for the reversed word. Given
any
d
-tuple of operators
S=\left(S_{1},\dots,S_{d}\right)
on
a Hilbert space
H
, define

S^{\alpha}\coloneqq S_{i_{1}}\cdots S_{i_{k}},\quad S^{\emptyset}\coloneqq I_{H}.

Then for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
,

\left(S^{\alpha}\right)^{*}=\left(S^{*}\right)^{\tilde{\alpha}},

where
S^{*}=\left(S^{*}_{1},\dots,S^{*}_{d}\right)
.

Let
\left(\Omega,\mathbb{P}\right)
be a probability space and
A=\left(A_{1},\dots,A_{d}\right)

a
d
-tuple of (strongly measurable) random operators
A_{i}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)

on a Hilbert space
H
, and assume

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|A^{\alpha}\right\|^{2}\right]<\infty,\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}.

Define the moment kernel
K\colon\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\times\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)

by

(2.1)

K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[A^{\alpha}\left(A^{\beta}\right)^{*}\right],\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}.

The kernel
K
is positive definite (p.d.), since for every finite

\left\{\left(\alpha_{j},u_{j}\right)\right\}^{N}_{j=1}\subset\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\times H
,

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits^{N}_{i,j=1}\left\langle u_{i},K\left(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right)u_{j}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits^{N}_{i,j=1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle u_{i},A^{\alpha_{i}}\left(A^{\alpha_{j}}\right)^{*}u_{j}\right\rangle_{H}\right]

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum\nolimits_{i}\left(A^{\alpha_{i}}\right)^{*}u_{i}\right\|^{2}_{H}\right]\geq 0.

To formulate subalgebra-valued versions of this construction, we recall
the notion of conditional expectations in operator algebras, following
Tomiyama.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)
be a unital
C^{*}
-subalgebra.
A map
E_{\mathcal{B}}\colon\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)

is a conditional expectation (in the sense of Tomiyama) if it is a
unital, completely positive, idempotent projection and is
\mathcal{B}
-bimodular:

E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(b_{1}Tb_{2}\right)=b_{1}E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(T\right)b_{2},\quad b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathcal{B},\>T\in\mathcal{L}\left(H\right).

Remark 2.2
.

Tomiyama showed that norm-one projections onto
C^{*}
-subalgebras
are exactly such conditional expectations (automatic complete positivity
and bimodularity); see
[
Tom57
,
Tom58
,
Tom59
]
. In the von
Neumann setting, Takesaki’s theorem yields normal, faithful conditional
expectations with the same properties (
[
Tak72
,
Tak02
]
).

Given
E_{\mathcal{B}}
, let
K_{\mathcal{B}}
be the
\mathcal{B}
-valued
moment kernel, defined by

(2.3)

K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\coloneqq E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\right),\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},

and set

(2.4)

\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\coloneqq\sum^{d}_{i=1}K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha i,\beta i\right).

The first step is to check that these
\mathcal{B}
-valued kernels
inherit positivity. This ensures that the dilation framework remains
valid after applying the conditional expectation.

Lemma 2.3
.

K_{\mathcal{B}}
is positive definite, so is
\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}
.

Proof.

Since
K
is p.d., for any finite
\left\{\left(\alpha_{j},u_{j}\right)\right\}^{N}_{j=1}

the block operator

\left[K\left(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right)\right]^{N}_{i,j=1}\in M_{N}\left(\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)\right)

is positive. By the complete positivity of
E_{\mathcal{B}}
,

\left(\mathrm{id}_{M_{N}}\otimes E_{\mathcal{B}}\right)\left(\left[K\left(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right)\right]\right)=\left[K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right)\right]_{i,j}

is also positive. Hence for all
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\in H
,

\sum^{N}_{i,j=1}\left\langle u_{i},K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right)u_{j}\right\rangle_{H}\geq 0,

which proves that
K_{\mathcal{B}}
is p.d.

Since each kernel
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha i,\beta i\right)

is positive definite by reindexing, and the class of positive definite
kernels is closed under finite sums,
\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}

is also p.d.
∎

With positivity in hand, we can now state the operator-valued version
of the moment dilation theorem. This result parallels
[
Tia25
]

but is formulated relative to a subalgebra
\mathcal{B}
.

Theorem 2.4

(Operator-valued moment dilation)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)

be a unital
C^{*}
-subalgebra and
E_{\mathcal{B}}\colon\mathcal{L}(H)\to\mathcal{B}

a Tomiyama conditional expectation. With
K_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}

as above, the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, a Cuntz family
S=\left(S_{1},\dots,S_{d}\right)\subset\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{K}\right)

(i.e.
S^{*}_{i}S_{j}=\delta_{ij}I
and
\sum_{i}S_{i}S^{*}_{i}=I
),
a projection
P\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{K}\right)
, and an isometry

W\colon H\to\mathcal{K}
such that

K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=W^{*}S^{\alpha}P\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}W,\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},

and

\sum^{d}_{i=1}S_{i}PS^{*}_{i}\leq P\quad\text{on }M\coloneqq\overline{span}\left\{\left(S^{\gamma}\right)^{*}WH:\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\right\}.

(2)

\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}
in the
positive-definite kernel order; i.e.
K_{\mathcal{B}}-\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}

is p.d.

Moreover,
\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}=K_{\mathcal{B}}

iff on the cyclic subspace generated by
\{\left(S_{\beta}\right)^{*}WH\}

one has the “no-defect” realization

K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=W^{*}S^{\alpha}\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}W,\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of
[
Tia25
, Theorem 1]

after replacing
K
by
K_{\mathcal{B}}
. We omit the details here.
∎

An immediate consequence of Theorem
2.4
is a mean-square von
Neumann inequality, which bounds the
\mathcal{B}
-valued second
moment of noncommutative polynomials in the random operators by the
norm of the same polynomial evaluated at the free creation operators.

Corollary 2.5

(Operator-valued mean-square von Neumann inequality)
.

Assume
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}
.
Then for any noncommutative polynomial
f\left(Z\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}c_{\alpha}Z^{\alpha}
,

E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(A\right)f\left(A\right){}^{*}\right]\right)\leq\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}I_{H}\in\mathcal{B},

where
L=\left(L_{1},\dots,L_{d}\right)
denotes the left creation
operators on the full Fock space
l^{2}\left(\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\right)
.

Proof.

Repeat the proof of
[
Tia25
, Cor. 4]
with
K
replaced
by
K_{\mathcal{B}}
, or simply apply
E_{\mathcal{B}}
to the operator
inequality there:

\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(A\right)f\left(A\right)^{*}\right]\leq\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}I_{H}.

Since
E_{\mathcal{B}}
is positive and unital, the claim follows.
∎

The abstract inequality can be sharpened by localizing it to positive
elements of
\mathcal{B}
. This yields stronger blockwise and fiberwise
estimates that will be especially useful in applications to random
matrices.

Corollary 2.6

(Localized von Neumann inequality)
.

Under the assumptions of Corollary
2.5
, for
every
f(Z)=\sum c_{\alpha}Z^{\alpha}
and set
X\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(A\right)f\left(A\right)^{*}\right]\in\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)
.
Then for every positive
Y\in\mathcal{B}
,

E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(Y^{1/2}XY^{1/2}\right)\leq\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}Y.

Proof.

From Corollary
2.5
we have
E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(X\right)\leq\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}I_{H}.
By

\mathcal{B}
-bimodularity and complete positivity of
E_{\mathcal{B}}
,

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(Y^{1/2}XY^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=Y^{1/2}E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(X\right)Y^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}Y^{1/2}IY^{1/2}=\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}Y.

∎

Remark 2.7
.

Two particularly useful specializations are immediate.

(1)

Projection (block/diagonal) form. If
Q\in\mathcal{B}
is a projection,
then

E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(QXQ\right)\leq\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}Q.

Since
E_{\mathcal{B}}
is
\mathcal{B}
-bimodular,
E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(QXQ\right)=QE_{\mathcal{B}}\left(X\right)Q
.
Thus in any block decomposition carried by
\mathcal{B}
, each diagonal
block satisfies the same
\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}

bound.

(2)

Vector form. If
\mathcal{B}
contains a rank-one projection
Q=\left|v\left\rangle\right\langle v\right|

(Dirac notation), then

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|f\left(A\right)^{*}v\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}\left\|v\right\|^{2}.

This is the sharp “pointwise” mean-square bound along the fiber spanned
by
v
.

Example 2.8

(Diagonal localization)
.

Let
H=\bigoplus_{r\in R}H_{r}
and
\mathcal{B}=\bigoplus_{r\in R}\mathcal{L}(H_{r})

with central projections
P_{r}
. Then for each
r
,

P_{r}E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(X\right)P_{r}=E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(P_{r}XP_{r}\right)\leq\left\|f(L)\right\|^{2}P_{r}.

If
\dim H_{r}=1
(true diagonal case), writing
e_{r}
for a unit
vector in
H_{r}
gives

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|f\left(A\right)^{*}e_{r}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\left\|f(L)\right\|^{2}.

So every coordinate (or site) separately obeys the same von Neumann
bound.

Example 2.9

(Block von Neumann inequality)
.

With the same
\mathcal{B}=\bigoplus_{r}\mathcal{L}(H_{r})
, define
the block Gram operators

G_{r}\coloneqq P_{r}\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(A\right)f\left(A\right)^{*}\right]P_{r}\in\mathcal{L}(H_{r}).

Then

E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(G_{r}\right)=G_{r}\leq\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}I_{H_{r}},

i.e. each diagonal block is dominated by the same universal constant

\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}
.

Although the right-hand side involves
\left\|f(L)\right\|
,
which is independent of the ensemble, the force of the inequality
is that it localizes a universal constant across blocks and coordinates.
This makes the inequality testable in practice. One only needs to
check whether the left-hand side stays below this benchmark in the
chosen ensemble. To show that this is already concrete in basic cases,
we give two examples where
\left\|f(L)\right\|
is explicit,
leading to variance bounds before any ensemble-specific computation.

Example 2.10

(Monomial test case)
.

Let
f(Z)=Z^{\alpha}
for a word
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
.
Then
f(L)=L^{\alpha}
is an isometry on
\ell^{2}\left(\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\right)
,
hence
\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|=1
. Consequently, for
every positive
Y\in\mathcal{B}
,

E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(Y^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[A^{\alpha}\left(A^{\alpha}\right)^{*}\right]Y^{1/2}\right)\leq Y.

Proof.

Write
\alpha=i_{1}\cdots i_{k}
. The left creation operators satisfy

L^{*}_{j}L_{i}=\delta_{ij}I
for all
i,j
. Therefore

\left(L^{\alpha}\right)^{*}L^{\alpha}=(L^{*}_{i_{k}}\cdots L^{*}_{i_{1}})(L_{i_{1}}\cdots L_{i_{k}})=\underbrace{L^{*}_{i_{k}}L_{i_{k}}}_{=I}\cdots\underbrace{L^{*}_{i_{1}}L_{i_{1}}}_{=I}=I,

so
L^{\alpha}
is an isometry and
\left\|L^{\alpha}\right\|=1
.
Applying Corollary
2.6
with
f\left(Z\right)=Z^{\alpha}
gives
the desired inequality.
∎

Example 2.11

(Linear polynomials)
.

Let
f(Z)=\sum^{d}_{i=1}c_{i}Z_{i}
with
c=\left(c_{1},\dots,c_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
Then
f\left(L\right)=\sum^{d}_{i=1}c_{i}L_{i}
and
\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|=\left\|c\right\|_{2}
.
Consequently, for every positive
Y\in\mathcal{B}
,

E_{\mathcal{B}}\left(Y^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum\nolimits_{i}c_{i}A_{i}\right)\left(\sum\nolimits_{j}c_{j}A_{j}\right)^{*}\right]Y^{1/2}\right)\leq\left\|c\right\|^{2}_{2}Y.

Proof.

Set
T\coloneqq\sum_{i}c_{i}L_{i}
. For any
x
in
\ell^{2}\left(\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\right)
,

\displaystyle\left\|Tx\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum\nolimits_{i}c_{i}L_{i}x,\sum\nolimits_{j}c_{j}L_{j}x\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i,j}\overline{c_{i}}c_{j}\left\langle L_{i}x,L_{j}x\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i,j}\overline{c_{i}}c_{j}\left\langle x,L^{*}_{i}L_{j}x\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i}|c_{i}|^{2}\left\|x\right\|^{2}=\left\|c\right\|^{2}_{2}\left\|x\right\|^{2},

using
L^{*}_{i}L_{j}=\delta_{ij}I
. Hence
\left\|T\right\|=\left\|c\right\|_{2}
.
The assertion follows from this and Corollary
2.6
.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

When
\mathcal{B}
is the diagonal algebra, Example
2.10

gives coordinatewise bounds for the variances of the diagonal entries
of
A^{\alpha}
, while Example
2.11
gives coordinatewise
bounds for
\sum_{i}c_{i}A_{i}
. In the block-diagonal case, the
same formulas yield uniform blockwise bounds with the identical constants

1
and
\left\|c\right\|^{2}
, respectively.

The present section closely parallels the abstract theory developed
in
[
Tia25
]
. However, here these results serve a
different purpose: they provide the technical foundation for concrete
random matrix computations. The dilation theorem and its von Neumann
inequalities are the exact tools that allow us to translate operator-algebraic
positivity into explicit scalar and block inequalities once we specialize
the conditional expectation
E_{\mathcal{B}}
to natural subalgebras
such as diagonal or block-diagonal algebras. This localization is
what makes the results computable and testable for specific ensembles
(e.g., Ginibre). The abstract machinery turns out to be useful to
detect fine structural properties of random matrix models when pushed
into concrete settings.

In Section
5
, we give a concrete analysis, computing the
kernels
K_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}

for standard random matrix ensembles and testing whether the inequality

\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}
holds
in practice.

3.
Radon-Nikodym Derivatives of Kernels

This section develops a Radon-Nikodym theorem for kernels. The idea
is to compare two positive definite kernels whenever one is dominated
by the other, and to describe their relationship through a density
operator that plays the role of a Radon-Nikodym derivative. The result
is modeled on the classical Radon-Nikodym theorem for measures and
its analogue for completely positive maps. By moving to the kernel
level, we obtain a structural tool that is both abstract and flexible,
and that will later allow us to identify explicit densities in random
matrix examples. The main theorem shows that domination of kernels
can be expressed by inserting a unique positive contraction into a
minimal Kolmogorov decomposition, and in the equivariant setting this
contraction lies in the commutant of the underlying representation.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a set,
H
a Hilbert space, and
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)

a unital
C^{*}
-subalgebra. Let
K,L\colon X\times X\to\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)

be operator-valued positive definite kernels with
K\leq L
in the
usual sense, i.e.,
L-K
is positive definite.

Assume
L
admits a minimal Kolmogorov decomposition

L\left(s,t\right)=V_{L}\left(s\right)^{*}V_{L}\left(t\right),\quad V_{L}\left(t\right)\colon H\to\mathcal{K}_{L},

with
\overline{span}\left\{V_{L}\left(t\right)h:t\in X,h\in H\right\}=\mathcal{K}_{L}
.
Then there exists a unique operator
A\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{K}_{L}\right)

such that

(i)
0\leq A\leq I
, and

(ii)
K\left(s,t\right)=V_{L}\left(s\right)^{*}AV_{L}\left(t\right)
,

\forall s,t\in X
.

If, in addition, there is a nondegenerate
*
-representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{K}_{L}\right)

with

V_{L}\left(t\right)\left(bh\right)=\rho\left(b\right)V_{L}\left(t\right)h,\quad b\in\mathcal{B},\>t\in X,\>h\in H,

and
K
arises from the same
\rho
in the sense that

K\left(s,t\right)=V_{K}\left(s\right)^{*}V_{K}\left(t\right)

for some
V_{K}\left(t\right)\colon H\to\mathcal{K}_{L}
satisfying

V_{K}\left(t\right)\left(bh\right)=\rho\left(b\right)V_{K}\left(t\right)h,

then the operator
A
belongs to the commutant
\rho\left(\mathcal{B}\right)^{\prime}
.

Equivalently, there is a unique contraction
W\colon\mathcal{K}_{L}\to\mathcal{K}_{K}

with
V_{K}\left(\cdot\right)=WV_{L}\left(\cdot\right)
and
A=W^{*}W
;
in the
\mathcal{B}
-equivariant situation,
W
intertwines
\rho

(so
W\rho\left(b\right)=\rho\left(b\right)W
, hence
A\in\rho\left(\mathcal{B}\right)^{\prime}
).

Proof.

Define the algebraic subspace

\mathcal{D}_{L}\coloneqq span\left\{V_{L}\left(t\right)h:t\in X,\>h\in H\right\}\subset\mathcal{K}_{L}.

By minimality,
\overline{\mathcal{D}_{L}}=\mathcal{K}_{L}
. Choose
any (fixed) minimal Kolmogorov decomposition for
K
(see e.g.,
[
Aro50
,
Sai05
]
),

K\left(s,t\right)=\widetilde{V}_{K}\left(s\right)^{*}\widetilde{V}_{K}\left(t\right),\quad\widetilde{V}_{K}\left(t\right)\colon H\to\mathcal{K}_{K},

and set

\mathcal{D}_{K}\coloneqq span\left\{\widetilde{V}_{K}\left(t\right)h:t\in X,\>h\in H\right\}\subset\mathcal{K}_{K}.

Define a linear map
T_{0}\colon\mathcal{D}_{L}\to\mathcal{K}_{K}

on generators by

T_{0}\left(\sum\nolimits^{n}_{i=1}V_{L}\left(t_{i}\right)h_{i}\right)\coloneqq\sum\nolimits^{n}_{i=1}\widetilde{V}_{K}\left(t_{i}\right)h_{i},

and extend by linearity. To see that
T_{0}
is well-defined, suppose

\sum_{i}V_{L}\left(t_{i}\right)h_{i}=0
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\nolimits_{i}\widetilde{V}_{K}(t_{i})h_{i}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{K}}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i,j}\left\langle h_{i},K\left(t_{i},t_{j}\right)h_{j}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\nolimits_{i,j}\left\langle h_{i},L(t_{i},t_{j})h_{j}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum\nolimits_{i}V_{L}\left(t_{i}\right)h_{i}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{L}}=0,

where the inequality uses the hypothesis
K\leq L
. Hence
\sum_{i}\widetilde{V}_{K}\left(t_{i}\right)h_{i}=0
,
so
T_{0}
is well-defined.

The same computation shows
T_{0}
is contractive on
\mathcal{D}_{L}
:
for
\xi=\sum_{i}V_{L}\left(t_{i}\right)h_{i}
,

\displaystyle\left\|T_{0}\xi\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{K}}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i,j}\left\langle h_{i},K(t_{i},t_{j})h_{j}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\nolimits_{i,j}\left\langle h_{i},L(t_{i},t_{j})h_{j}\right\rangle_{H}=\left\|\xi\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{L}}.

Therefore
T_{0}
extends uniquely by continuity to a contraction

W\colon\mathcal{K}_{L}\to\mathcal{K}_{K}
with
W|_{\mathcal{D}_{L}}=T_{0}
.
In particular,

WV_{L}\left(t\right)h=\widetilde{V}_{K}\left(t\right)h\quad\forall t\in X,\>h\in H.

Define
A\coloneqq W^{*}W\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{K}_{L}\right)
,
then
A\geq 0
and
\left\|A\right\|\leq\left\|W\right\|^{2}\leq 1
,
hence
0\leq A\leq I
. For
s,t\in X
and
g,h\in H
,

\displaystyle\left\langle g,K\left(s,t\right)h\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\widetilde{V}_{K}\left(s\right)g,\widetilde{V}_{K}\left(t\right)h\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}_{K}}=\left\langle WV_{L}\left(s\right)g,\ WV_{L}(t)h\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}_{K}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle V_{L}\left(s\right)g,W^{*}WV_{L}\left(t\right)h\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}_{L}}=\left\langle g,V_{L}\left(s\right)^{*}AV_{L}\left(t\right)h\right\rangle_{H}.

Since
g,h
are arbitrary,
K\left(s,t\right)=V_{L}\left(s\right)^{*}AV_{L}\left(t\right)

for all
s,t\in X
.

To see uniqueness, suppose
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{K}_{L}\right)

satisfy

V_{L}\left(s\right)^{*}A_{1}V_{L}\left(t\right)=V_{L}\left(s\right)^{*}A_{2}V_{L}\left(t\right)

for all
s,t\in X
. Then

\langle V_{L}(s)g,\left(A_{1}-A_{2}\right)V_{L}(t)h\rangle_{\mathcal{K}_{L}}=0

for all
s,t\in X
, and
g,h\in H
. By density of
\mathcal{D}_{L}
,
this forces
A_{1}=A_{2}
.

Assume there exists a nondegenerate
*
-representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{K}_{L}\right)

such that

V_{L}\left(t\right)\left(bh\right)=\rho\left(b\right)V_{L}\left(t\right)h

and similarly

V_{K}\left(t\right)\left(bh\right)=\rho\left(b\right)V_{K}\left(t\right)h

once
K
is realized in
\mathcal{K}_{L}
. Define
T_{0}
as above
but now with range in
\mathcal{K}_{L}
, i.e.,

T_{0}\left(\sum V_{L}\left(t_{i}\right)h_{i}\right)\coloneqq\sum V_{K}\left(t_{i}\right)h_{i}\in\mathcal{K}_{L}.

Then, for
\xi=\sum V_{L}\left(t_{i}\right)h_{i}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}
,

\displaystyle T_{0}\rho\left(b\right)\xi

\displaystyle=T_{0}\left(\sum V_{L}\left(t_{i}\right)\left(bh_{i}\right)\right)=\sum V_{K}\left(t_{i}\right)\left(bh_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\left(b\right)\sum V_{K}\left(t_{i}\right)h_{i}=\rho\left(b\right)T_{0}\xi.

Thus
T_{0}
intertwines
\rho
on
\mathcal{D}_{L}
, and the extension

W
satisfies
W\rho\left(b\right)=\rho\left(b\right)W
on
\mathcal{K}_{L}
.
Consequently
A=W^{*}W
commutes with
\rho\left(b\right)
for all

b\in\mathcal{B}
, i.e.,
A\in\rho\left(\mathcal{B}\right)^{\prime}
.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

We refer to the unique operator
A
in Theorem
3.1
as the
Radon-Nikodym derivative of
K
with respect to
L
. We denote it
by

\frac{dK}{dL}\coloneqq A\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{K}_{L}\right),

so that

K\left(s,t\right)=V_{L}\left(s\right)^{*}\frac{dK}{dL}V_{L}\left(t\right),\quad s,t\in X.

Remark 3.3

(Comparison with Belavkin-Staszewski

[
BS86
]
)
.

The argument is the kernel-level analogue of the Radon-Nikodym theorem
for completely positive (CP) maps: if
\Phi,\Psi:\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)

are CP with
\Phi\leq\Psi
, then in a Stinespring dilation
\Psi\left(a\right)=V^{*}\pi\left(a\right)V

one constructs a contraction
W
intertwining the two dilations and
obtains a unique
D=W^{*}W\in\pi\left(\mathfrak{A}\right)^{\prime}
such
that
\Phi\left(a\right)=V^{*}\pi\left(a\right)DV
. Here, the role
of Stinespring space is played by the Kolmogorov space
\mathcal{K}_{L}
,
the role of
\pi
by the representation
\rho
of
\mathcal{B}

(when present), and the domination
K\leq L
gives the same contractive
extension
W
. The density
A=W^{*}W
is thus the exact kernel-theoretic
counterpart of the Belavkin-Staszewski “derivative”
D
, including
the commutant membership when a fixed symmetry/representation is present.

4.
RN derivative of the shifted kernel on
\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}

We now apply the Radon-Nikodym theorem (Theorem
3.1
) to our
main object of study.

Let
X=\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
. For a given positive definite kernel

K_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\times\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\to\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)
,
we define its shifted kernel by

\big(K_{\mathcal{B}}\big)_{\Sigma}\left(\alpha,\beta\right):=\sum^{d}_{i=1}K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha i,\beta i\right).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
K_{\mathcal{B}}
be a p.d. kernel a minimal Kolmogorov
decomposition
\left(V,\mathcal{K}\right)
, i.e.,

K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=V\left(\alpha\right)^{*}V\left(\beta\right),\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},

with
V\colon H\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
. On the dense span
\mathcal{D}=span\left\{V\left(\alpha\right)u:\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},\>u\in H\right\}\subset\mathcal{K}
,
define the shift operators
B_{i}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}

by

B_{i}V\left(\alpha\right)u:=V\left(\alpha i\right)u,\quad i=1,\dots,d,

and extend by linearity. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}
;

(2)

the column map
B\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{K}^{\oplus d}
,
x\mapsto\left(B_{1}x,\dots,B_{d}x\right)
,
is contractive. In this case, each
B_{i}
extends by continuity
to a bounded operator on
\mathcal{K}
and

\sum^{d}_{i=1}B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\leq I_{\mathcal{K}}.

Proof.

For
x=\sum_{\alpha}V\left(\alpha\right)u_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}\left\|B_{i}x\right\|{}^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha,\beta}\left\langle u_{\alpha},\left(\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha i,\beta i\right)\right)u_{\beta}\right\rangle_{H},

\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|{}^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha,\beta}\left\langle u_{\alpha},K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)u_{\beta}\right\rangle_{H}.

These identities are immediate from the Kolmogorov representation.
Thus
\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}

iff
\sum_{i}\left\|B_{i}x\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}\leq\left\|x\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}

for all
x\in\mathcal{D}
, i.e., the column map
B
is a contraction
on a dense domain. Hence it extends to a contraction
\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}^{\oplus d}
.
Its adjoint
B^{*}:\mathcal{K}^{\oplus d}\to\mathcal{K}
has the
form

B^{*}\left(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d}\right)=\sum_{i}B^{*}_{i}\xi_{i},

so
B^{*}B=\sum_{i}B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\leq I
.

Conversely, if
\sum_{i}B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\leq I
, then

\sum\left\|B_{i}x\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}=\left\langle x,\left(\sum B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\right)x\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}\leq\left\|x\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}

for all
x\in\mathcal{D}
, which is equivalent to
\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Under the hypotheses of Lemma
4.1
, the RN
derivative of
\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}
with respect
to
K_{\mathcal{B}}
in the Kolmogorov space
\mathcal{K}
is

\frac{d\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}}{dK_{\mathcal{B}}}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{K}\right),\quad 0\leq\sum_{i}B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\leq I_{\mathcal{K}}.

In particular,

\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}\Longleftrightarrow\sum^{d}_{i=1}B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\leq I_{\mathcal{K}}.

Proof.

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
and
u,v\in H
,

\displaystyle\left\langle u,\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)v\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\sum^{d}_{i=1}\left\langle u,K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha i,\beta i\right)v\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\sum^{d}_{i=1}\left\langle V\left(\alpha i\right)u,V\left(\beta i\right)v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle=\sum^{d}_{i=1}\left\langle B_{i}V\left(\alpha\right)u,B_{i}V\left(\beta\right)v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle B_{i}V\left(\alpha\right)u,\left(\sum B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\right)V\left(\beta\right)v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}.

Thus

\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=V\left(\alpha\right)^{*}\left(\sum B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\right)V\left(\beta\right).

By Theorem
3.1
, the RN derivative
A=d\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right){}_{\Sigma}/dK_{\mathcal{B}}

in the given Kolmogorov space is the unique positive operator with
this property, so
A=\sum B^{*}_{i}B_{i}
. The equivalence with
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}

is Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Assume
K_{B}
has a minimal Kolmogorov decomposition
\left(V,\mathcal{K}\right)
,
with

K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=V\left(\alpha\right)^{*}V\left(\beta\right),\quad,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}.

The condition
\sum B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\leq I_{\mathcal{K}}
on the abstract
shift operators in the Kolmogorov space is equivalent to the condition

\sum S_{i}PS^{*}_{i}\leq P
on the cyclic subspace

M\coloneqq\overline{span}\left\{\left(S^{\gamma}\right)^{*}WH:\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\right\}

in the dilation space
H_{dil}
; see Theorem
2.4
. The latter
is simply a concrete realization of the former, as established by
the Frazho-Bunce-Popescu dilation theorem. A detailed proof can be
found in
[
Tia25
]
.

5.
Application to Random Matrix Ensembles

In this section we apply the RN-derivative viewpoint to random matrix
models. We fix
d=1
(one variable) to simplify exposition; words
in
\mathbb{F}^{+}_{1}
are identified with nonnegative integers.
Let
A
be an
N\times N
random matrix with
\mathbb{E}\left\|A\right\|^{2}<\infty

for all
m
. We set

\displaystyle K\left(m,n\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[A^{m}(A^{n})^{*}\right]\in M_{N}\left(\mathbb{C}\right),

\displaystyle K_{\Sigma}\left(m,n\right)

\displaystyle:=K\left(m+1,n+1\right),

and, for a chosen unital subalgebra
\mathcal{B}\subseteq M_{N}

with conditional expectation
E_{\mathcal{B}}
, we write

K_{\mathcal{B}}\coloneqq E_{\mathcal{B}}\circ K,\quad\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}\coloneqq E_{\mathcal{B}}\circ K_{\Sigma}.

By Theorem
4.2
, if
K_{\mathcal{B}}
has Kolmogorov decomposition

K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(m,n\right)=V\left(m\right)^{*}V\left(n\right)

with shift

B\colon V\left(m\right)u\mapsto V\left(m+1\right)u,

then the RN derivative of
\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}

with respect to
K_{\mathcal{B}}
is

\frac{d\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}}{dK_{\mathcal{B}}}=B^{*}B,

and

\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}\Longleftrightarrow B^{*}B\leq I\Longleftrightarrow\text{the defect }\,I-B^{*}B\,\text{ is positive.}

We now derive exact criteria in natural symmetry classes, and work
out concrete consequences for standard ensembles.

5.1.
Bi-unitarily invariant ensembles (diagonal algebra)

Assume the law of
A
is bi-unitarily invariant:
UAV\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}A

for all
U,V\in U\left(N\right)
. Define

(5.1)

c_{m}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\mathop{Tr}\mathbb{E}\big[A^{m}\left(A^{m}\right)^{*}\big]\geq 0,\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.

Lemma 5.1

(Diagonalization of
K
in the bi-unitary case)
.

For all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

K\left(m,n\right)=\delta_{mn}c_{m}I_{N}.

Proof.

For any
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
we have
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}e^{i\theta}A
,
so

\mathbb{E}\left[A^{m}\left(A^{n}\right)^{*}\right]=e^{i\left(m-n\right)\theta}\mathbb{E}\left[A^{m}\left(A^{n}\right)^{*}\right].

Averaging in
\theta
gives
K\left(m,n\right)=0
if
m\neq n
.
For fixed
m
, left invariance yields
UK\left(m,m\right)U^{*}=K\left(m,m\right)

for all
U\in U(N)
. Hence
K\left(m,m\right)=c_{m}I_{N}
by Schur’s
lemma, with
c_{m}
given by (
5.1
).

Since
K\left(m,n\right)
is already a scalar multiple of
I_{N}
,
any conditional expectation
E_{\mathcal{B}}
fixes it, so
K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(m,n\right)=K\left(m,n\right)=\delta_{mn}c_{m}I_{N}
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
A
be bi-unitarily invariant and
K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(m,n\right)=\delta_{mn}c_{m}I_{N}

as above. Choose the Kolmogorov vectors
k_{m}:=K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\cdot,m\right)\in\mathcal{K}=

the RKHS of
K_{\mathcal{B}}
. Then the shift
B
satisfies

Bk_{m}=k_{m+1},\quad\frac{d\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}}{dK_{\mathcal{B}}}=B^{*}B=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{c_{m+1}}{c_{m}}\frac{\left|k_{m}\left\rangle\right\langle k_{m}\right|}{\left\|k_{m}\right\|^{2}}.

Consequently,

\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}\Longleftrightarrow\frac{c_{m+1}}{c_{m}}\leq 1,\quad\forall m\geq 0.

Proof.

Orthogonality:
\left\langle k_{m},k_{n}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=K\left(m,n\right)=\delta_{mn}c_{m}I_{N}
.
Normalizing
e_{m}:=k_{m}/\left\|k_{m}\right\|
gives an
orthonormal basis with

Be_{m}=\sqrt{c_{m+1}/c_{m}}e_{m+1}.

Hence

B^{*}B=\sum_{m}\frac{c_{m+1}}{c_{m}}\left|e_{m}\left\rangle\right\langle e_{m}\right|.

The operator inequality
B^{*}B\leq I
is equivalent to
c_{m+1}\leq c_{m}

for all
m
.
∎

Example 5.3

(Necessary Condition from the Base Case (
m=0
))
.

Since

c_{0}=\tfrac{1}{N}TrI_{N}=1,\quad c_{1}=\tfrac{1}{N}Tr\mathbb{E}\left[A^{*}A\right],

we obtain
\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}\Rightarrow c_{1}\leq c_{0}
,
i.e.,

\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathop{Tr}\left(A^{*}A\right)\right]\leq 1.

For a complex Ginibre ensemble where the entries have variance
\sigma^{2}
,
we have
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathop{Tr}\left(A^{*}A\right)\right]=N^{2}\sigma^{2}
.
So the base inequality forces

\sigma^{2}\leq 1/N.

This provides a fundamental constraint on the total variance of the
matrix.

Example 5.4

(Haar unitary)
.

If
U
is Haar unitary, then
K\left(m,n\right)=\delta_{mn}I_{N}
,
so
c_{m}\equiv 1
,
B^{*}B=I
, and
\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}=K_{\mathcal{B}}

with equality (extreme case of the criterion).

Example 5.5

(Large
N
Ginibre at scale
\sigma^{2}_{N}=\tau/N
)
.

Let
A_{N}
be a complex Ginibre matrix with i.i.d.
entries
a_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}\left(0,\sigma^{2}_{N}\right)
,
where
\sigma^{2}_{N}=\tau/N
and
\tau>0
is fixed. Let

c^{\left(N\right)}_{m}=\frac{1}{N}\mathop{Tr}\mathbb{E}\big[A^{m}_{N}\left(A^{m}_{N}\right)^{*}\big],\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.

Then

(5.2)

c^{(N)}_{m}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\tau^{m}.

Consequently,

c^{\left(N\right)}_{m+1}/c^{\left(N\right)}_{m}\xrightarrow[N\rightarrow\infty]{}\tau,\;\forall m\in\mathbb{N},

and by Theorem
5.2
this yields the sharp asymptotic criterion

\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}\Longleftrightarrow\tau\leq 1\;\left(\text{in the large $N$ regime}\right).

Proof.

(Justification of (
5.2
)) Write the singular values
of
A^{m}_{N}
as
s_{1},\dots,s_{N}\geq 0
. Then

c^{\left(N\right)}_{m}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\nolimits^{N}_{i=1}s^{2}_{i}\right],

i.e.,
c^{\left(N\right)}_{m}
is the first moment of the empirical
law of the squared singular values of
A^{m}_{N}
. For complex Ginibre,
the empirical spectral distribution of
A^{m}_{N}\left(A^{m}_{N}\right)^{*}

converges, as
N\rightarrow\infty
, to the Fuss-Catalan (Raney) distribution
of order
m+1
. Its
p
-th moment is

M^{\left(m\right)}_{p}=\frac{1}{mp+1}\binom{\left(m+1\right)p}{p},\quad p\in\mathbb{N},

see
[
AGT10
]
, Theorem 1.1 and eq. (1.3). Specializing to

p=1
gives
M^{\left(m\right)}_{1}=1
for the unit-variance model.
Under our variance scaling
\sigma^{2}_{N}=\tau/N
, each factor of

A_{N}
contributes a multiplicative
\tau
to squared singular
values, hence the mean scales as
\tau^{m}
. Therefore
c^{(N)}_{m}\rightarrow\tau^{m}
.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Set

d^{\left(N\right)}_{m}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathop{Tr}\left(\left(A^{*}_{N}A_{N}\right)^{m}\right)\right].

Note that in general
c^{\left(N\right)}_{m}\neq d^{\left(N\right)}_{m}

(even for bi-unitarily invariant ensembles).

Let
A_{N}
be a complex Ginibre matrix as in Example
5.5
.
By the Marchenko-Pastur law (parameter
1
) for the sample covariance

A^{*}_{N}A_{N}
at scale
\sigma^{2}_{N}=\tau/N
,

(5.3)

d^{\left(N\right)}_{m}\rightarrow\tau^{m}C_{m},

where
C_{m}=\frac{1}{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}
are the Catalan numbers.

In fact, we may set
X=\sqrt{N}A_{N}
. Then the entries of
X
are
i.i.d. with variance
\tau
, and

S_{N}:=\frac{1}{N}XX^{*}=A_{N}A^{*}_{N}.

Write

\beta_{m}\left(S_{N}\right)=\frac{1}{N}\mathop{Tr}\left(S^{m}_{N}\right)=\frac{1}{N}\mathop{Tr}\left(\left(A_{N}A^{*}_{N}\right)^{m}\right)=\frac{1}{N}\mathop{Tr}\left(\left(A^{*}_{N}A_{N}\right)^{m}\right).

Equations (3.1.6)–(3.1.7) from
[
BS10
]
give

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\beta_{k}\left(S_{n}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum^{k-1}_{r=0}\frac{1}{r+1}\binom{k}{r}\binom{k-1}{r}y^{r}+O\left(n^{-1}\right),

\displaystyle Var\left(\beta_{k}\left(S_{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(n^{-2}\right),

with
y=p/n
(here
p=n=N\Rightarrow y=1
). They also record the
explicit M-P moments
\beta_{k}\left(y,\sigma^{2}\right)=\sigma^{2k}\beta_{k}\left(y,1\right)

(scale
\sigma^{2}
) and, for the standard case,

\beta_{k}\left(y,1\right)=\sum^{k-1}_{r=0}\frac{1}{r+1}\binom{k}{r}\binom{k-1}{r}y^{r}.

Applying these with
y=1
and
\sigma^{2}=\tau
yields
\beta_{m}\left(1,\tau\right)=\tau^{m}\beta_{m}\left(1,1\right)=\tau^{m}C_{m}
.
Hence

d^{\left(N\right)}_{m}=\mathbb{E}\left[\beta_{m}\left(S_{N}\right)\right]\rightarrow\tau^{m}C_{m}.

By contrast,
c^{\left(N\right)}_{m}\rightarrow\tau^{m}
(Example
5.5
),
so

c^{\left(N\right)}_{m}-d^{\left(N\right)}_{m}\rightarrow\tau^{m}\left(1-C_{m}\right),\quad m\geq 2.

5.2.
Block-bi-unitarily invariant ensembles (block-diagonal algebra)

Let
H=\bigoplus^{R}_{r=1}\mathbb{C}^{k_{r}}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{N}

with orthogonal block projections
P_{1},\dots,P_{R}
and
\sum_{r}P_{r}=I
.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\bigoplus^{R}_{r=1}M_{k_{r}}
be the block-diagonal
algebra and
E_{\mathcal{B}}(T):=\bigoplus_{r}P_{r}TP_{r}
the conditional
expectation.

Assume the law of
A\in M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
is block-bi-unitarily
invariant: for all block-diagonal unitaries
U=\bigoplus_{r}U_{r}
,

V=\bigoplus_{r}V_{r}
one has
UAV\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}A
. Set

K\left(m,n\right)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[A^{m}(A^{n})^{*}\right],\quad K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(m,n\right):=E_{\mathcal{B}}K\left(m,n\right).

Lemma 5.7

(Blockwise diagonalization)
.

For all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(m,n\right)=\delta_{mn}\bigoplus^{R}_{r=1}c^{(r)}_{m}I_{k_{r}},\quad c^{(r)}_{m}:=\frac{1}{k_{r}}\mathop{Tr}\mathbb{E}\left[P_{r}A^{m}\left(A^{m}\right)^{*}P_{r}\right]\geq 0.

Proof.

Phase invariance
A\mapsto e^{i\theta}A
gives
K(m,n)=0
for
m\neq n

as in Lemma
5.1
. For fixed
m
, block left invariance yields

P_{r}K\left(m,m\right)P_{r}=P_{r}UK\left(m,m\right)U^{*}P_{r}=U_{r}\left(P_{r}K\left(m,m\right)P_{r}\right)U^{*}_{r},\quad\forall U_{r}\in U\left(k_{r}\right),

hence
P_{r}K(m,m)P_{r}=c^{(r)}_{m}I_{k_{r}}
by Schur’s lemma. Applying

E_{\mathcal{B}}
gives the claim and reads off

c^{(r)}_{m}=\frac{1}{k_{r}}\mathop{Tr}P_{r}K\left(m,m\right)P_{r}

∎

Theorem 5.8

(RN criterion, blockwise)
.

Let
K_{\mathcal{B}}
be as in Lemma
5.7

and let
(\mathcal{K},V)
be a Kolmogorov (minimal) decomposition

K_{\mathcal{B}}(m,n)=V(m)^{*}V(n)
. For each block
r
and
u\in\mathrm{Ran}P_{r}
,
write
k^{(r)}_{m,u}:=V(m)u\in\mathcal{K}
, and let
P^{(r)}_{m}

be the orthogonal projection onto
\overline{\{k^{(r)}_{m,u}:u\in\mathrm{Ran}P_{r}\}}
.
Then the shift
B\,k^{(r)}_{m,u}=k^{(r)}_{m+1,u}
is well-defined
on the Kolmogorov span and

\frac{d\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}}{dK_{\mathcal{B}}}=B^{*}B=\bigoplus^{R}_{r=1}\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{c^{(r)}_{m+1}}{c^{(r)}_{m}}P^{(r)}_{m}.

Consequently,

\left(K_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}\Longleftrightarrow\frac{c^{(r)}_{m+1}}{c^{(r)}_{m}}\leq 1,\quad\forall m\geq 0,\ \forall r=1,\dots,R.

Proof.

For
u,v\in\mathrm{Ran}P_{r}
,

\left\langle k^{(r)}_{m,u},k^{(s)}_{n,v}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=\left\langle u,K_{\mathcal{B}}\left(m,n\right)v\right\rangle=\delta_{mn}\delta_{rs}c^{(r)}_{m}\left\langle u,v\right\rangle,

so levels
\left(m,r\right)
are pairwise orthogonal and

\left\|k^{(r)}_{m,u}\right\|^{2}=c^{(r)}_{m}\left\|u\right\|^{2}.

Choose orthonormal bases
\{e^{(r)}_{j}\}^{k_{r}}_{j=1}
in each
block and set

e^{(r)}_{m,j}:=k^{(r)}_{m,e^{(r)}_{j}}/\sqrt{c^{(r)}_{m}}.

Then
\{e^{(r)}_{m,j}\}_{m\geq 0,\ 1\leq j\leq k_{r},\ 1\leq r\leq R}

is orthonormal in
\mathcal{K}
and

B\,e^{(r)}_{m,j}=\sqrt{c^{(r)}_{m+1}/c^{(r)}_{m}}\,e^{(r)}_{m+1,j}

Hence

B^{*}B=\bigoplus_{r}\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{c^{\left(r\right)}_{m+1}}{c^{\left(r\right)}_{m}}\sum^{k_{r}}_{j=1}\left|e^{\left(r\right)}_{m,j}\left\rangle\right\langle e^{\left(r\right)}_{m,j}\right|=\bigoplus_{r}\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{c^{\left(r\right)}_{m+1}}{c^{\left(r\right)}_{m}}P^{\left(r\right)}_{m}.

The Loewner inequality
B^{*}B\leq I
is therefore equivalent to the
stated blockwise ratios
\leq 1
.
∎

Example 5.9

(Base obstruction per block (finite
N
))
.

Since
c^{\left(r\right)}_{0}=1
and
c^{\left(r\right)}_{1}=\frac{1}{k_{r}}\mathop{Tr}\mathbb{E}\left[P_{r}A^{*}AP_{r}\right]
,
Theorem
5.8
forces
c^{(r)}_{1}\leq 1
for every block
r
.
For i.i.d. entries with variance
\sigma^{2}
,

\mathbb{E}\mathop{Tr}\left(P_{r}A^{*}AP_{r}\right)=Nk_{r}\sigma^{2}

giving the necessary global scaling
\sigma^{2}\leq 1/N
.

Example 5.10

(Fully i.i.d. Ginibre)
.

If
A
has i.i.d. entries (complex Ginibre), then by row-column
permutation symmetry

c^{\left(1\right)}_{m}=\cdots=c^{\left(R\right)}_{m}=:c_{m}\quad\text{for all }m,

so the blockwise RN test reduces to the single sequence
\{c_{m}\}

from Section
5.1
. Thus
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{\Sigma}\leq K_{\mathcal{B}}

holds iff
c_{m+1}\leq c_{m}
for all
m
(equivalently, the same
moment-ratio criterion as in the diagonal case), but now verified
blockwise as well.

Example 5.11

(Heterogeneous blocks)
.

When blocks have different second moments (e.g. ensembles with block-dependent
variances or correlations), Theorem
5.8
detects the worst
block. In particular, if some block
r
fails
c^{(r)}_{1}\leq 1

(or more generally
c^{(r)}_{m+1}\leq c^{(r)}_{m}
for some
m
),
the global inequality fails.

Remark 5.12

(Large
N
asymptotics)
.

In i.i.d. Ginibre scaling
\sigma^{2}_{N}=\tau/N
, the block averages

c^{(r,N)}_{m}
are independent of
r
by symmetry and share the
same large
N
limits as in Section
5.1
. Hence the blockwise
thresholds coincide with the diagonal ones. In ensembles with genuine
block anisotropy, one can analyze each sequence
c^{(r,N)}_{m}
separately
(e.g. by Wick/Weingarten expansions restricted to indices in block

r
) to obtain block-specific asymptotic criteria.

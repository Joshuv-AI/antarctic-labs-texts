Factors associated with severe obstetric anal sphincter injury following vaginal delivery
Authors: Aharon Dick, Yarden Flamer, Tal Zilberman, Gilad Karavani, David Shveiky, Henry H Chill
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimObstetric anal sphincter injury (OASI) is a major complication of vaginal delivery. Tear severity can be categorized according to the structures involved. To date, little is known regarding risk factors for severe OASI tears. We aimed to evaluate the association between known risk factors for OASI and tear severity.MethodsThis was a retrospective cohort study, performed at a tertiary university hospital. The study population included women aged 18–50 years who underwent a vaginal delivery and were diagnosed with OASI. The study cohort was divided into two groups based on OASI severity according to OASI grade: low-grade (3A and 3B) and high-grade (3C and 4). A comparison was performed between the two groups regarding demographic, obstetric and labor related parameters. Quantitative independent variables were compared using Student’s t or Mann–Whitney tests and presented as either mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range). The association between categorical variables was tested using either the Chi-squared test or Fisher's exact test, as appropriate. A multivariate regression model was applied to obtain adjusted odds ratios (aOR) for parameters found to be associated with high-grade OASI tear in the univariate model.ResultsTwo hundred and sixteen patients within the study group and 405 within the control group were included in the final analysis. Patients with high-grade OASI had increased mean parity (0.56 ± 1.0 vs. 0.39 ± 0.7, p = 0.04) and were less likely to be nulliparous (66.2% vs. 74%, p = 0.03). Operative vaginal delivery (37.0% vs. 28.8%, p = 0.03) was associated with an increased risk of high-grade OASI while artificial rupture of membranes (AROM) was more common among the low-grade group (50.3% vs. 41.2%, P = 0.03).Multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted for the dependent parameters of high grade OASI. Operative vaginal delivery (OR 1.72, CI 1.16–2.55, p < 0.01) and higher parity (OR 1.23, CI 1.00–1.51, p = 0.04) were associated with high-grade OASI. AROM (OR 1.49, CI 1.04–2.14, p = 0.02) was more common among the low grade OASI tears.ConclusionOperative vaginal delivery and higher parity are factors associated with more severe OASI, while artificial rupture of membranes was related to lower tear severity.Keywords: Fourth degree tears, Obstetric anal sphincter injury, Operative vaginal delivery, Parity, Perineal laceration, Third degree tears

Introduction

Obstetric anal sphincter injury (OASI) is a major complication of vaginal delivery, associated with both short- and long-term morbidity. Previous studies have found OASI prevalence to be as high as 11% while more recent studies, have found it in 1–2% of vaginal deliveries [1, 2].

OASI is subclassified to 4 categories according to extent of injury to the anal sphincter [3]: Grade 3A– Less than 50% of the external anal sphincter; 3B – More than 50% of the external anal sphincter, but the internal anal sphincter remains intact; 3 C – Both the external and internal anal sphincters are torn; 4th degree – The tear extends to the rectal mucosa or anal epithelium.

Obstetric anal sphincter injuries (OASI) are associated with significant morbidity, including postpartum fecal and anal incontinence [4, 5]. Furthermore, previous literature has shown that the severity of the tear impacts symptom development later in life: rates of fecal and anal incontinence after third-degree tears have been reported to be approximately 14% and 41%, respectively, while rates after fourth-degree tears increase to 30% and 59%, respectively [6]. Other complications associated with OASI include urinary retention [7], bowel fistulas [8] and sexual dysfunction. [9]

Several risk factors for OASI have been identified, including nulliparity, advanced maternal age, induction of labor, epidural anesthesia, fetal macrosomia, fetal occipito-posterior position, prolonged second stage of labor, operative vaginal delivery, and shoulder dystocia [10–12]. Moreover, several studies have focused on unique patient populations which have been shown to have specific risk factors associated with OASI [13–15]. However, data on risk factors leading to more severe OASI are limited. In one previous study, prolonged second stage of labor was shown to increase risk of OASI severity in parous patients [13]. Hence, we hypothesized that prolonged second stage of labor may increase risk for more severe OASI in other patient populations.

The aim of the current study was to identify potential risk factors for severe OASI and to assess their contribution to the occurrence of severe OASI during delivery.

Materials & methods

This was a retrospective cohort study, performed at a tertiary university teaching hospital between 2015–2024. The study population included women aged 18–50 years who underwent a normal or operative vaginal delivery and were diagnosed with OASI following delivery. We excluded women who delivered via cesarean section and those with insufficient medical records confirming an OASI diagnosis as well as patients for whom OASI grade was not available. The study cohort was divided into two groups based on OASI severity according to OASI grade: low-grade (3A and B) and high-grade (3C and 4).

Data were obtained from the delivery room management software used in our medical center. Among parameters collected were age, parity, previous cesarean delivery, comorbidities, gestational age at delivery, method of labor initiation, epidural analgesia, duration of the second stage of labor, occipito-posterior position, mode of delivery, perineal lacerations, postpartum hemorrhage, need for blood transfusion, maternal fever or infection and duration of hospitalization. Neonatal factors included birthweight, estimated fetal weight (clinical and sonographic), low Apgar score and newborn head circumference.

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (approval number: HMO- 0586–22). Given the retrospective nature of the study, informed consent was waived.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS software (version 27). Quantitative independent variables were compared using Student’s t or Mann–Whitney tests and presented as either mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range). The association between categorical variables was tested using either the Chi-squared test or Fisher's exact test, as appropriate.

A multivariate regression model was applied. We report adjusted odds ratios (aOR), 95% confidence interval (CI) for parameters included in the final multivariate analysis. A two-sided p value with a value < 0.05 were considered significant.

Results

A total of 621 patients with OASI were included in the study, of whom 405 had grade 3 A and 3B tears (low-grade) and 216 had tears grade 3 C and 4 (high-grade). Mean age of the entire cohort was 26.5 ± 4.6 years, mean gestational week was 39.7 ± 1.2, and 444 women (71.5%) were primiparous at time of delivery.

Patients with low-grade OASI were compared to those with high-grade OASI. Demographic characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Patients with high-grade OASI had increased mean parity (0.56 ± 1.0 vs. 0.39 ± 0.7, p = 0.04) and were less likely to be nulliparous (66.2% vs. 74%, p = 0.03). Maternal age and related comorbidities did not differ between the groups.

Multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted for the dependent parameters of high grade OASI (Table 3). Operative vaginal delivery (OR 1.72, CI 1.16–2.55, p < 0.01) and higher parity (OR 1.23, CI 1.00–1.51, p = 0.04) were associated with high-grade OASI. In contrast, AROM (OR 1.49, CI 1.04–2.14, p = 0.02) was more common among the low grade OASI tears.

A subgroup analysis was performed in which only nulliparous women were included. This revealed that induction of labor (OR1.80, C.I. 1.17–2.77, P < 0.01) and operative vaginal delivery (OR 1.54, C.I. 1.02–2.34, p = 0.04)) were more common among the high grade OASI tears.

A second subgroup analysis was done comparing women with 4th degree tear only to those with 3 A and 3B degree tears. Higher parity (OR 1.26, C.I. 1.03–1.55, p = 0.02) was associated with a 4th degree tear, while AROM (OR 1.42, C.I. 1.04–2.09, p = 0.02) was found to be associated with less severe tears.

Discussion

In this study, we aimed to identify which factors previously associated with OASI are predictive not only of the risk of sustaining a tear, but also of its severity. We found that operative vaginal delivery and higher parity to be associated with more severe OASI while AROM was associated with lower-grade OASI. Similarly, when including women with 4th degree tear only, higher parity was found to be a risk factor, while AROM was associated with lower grade tears.

Over the past few decades numerous studies have described possible risk factors for OASI. These include both maternal and fetal characteristics which may impact the risk of sphincter involvement. However, few studies have addressed the risk of more severe tears within the OASI spectrum. These include both 3 C and 4th degree tears which previous studies have shown are correlated with increased fecal and anal incontinence compared to 3 A and 3B tears [6]. In theory, knowledge regarding such characteristics could enable the development of strategies to prevent these more impactful tears.

We found that operative vaginal delivery was an independent factor associated with increased OASI severity. This aligns with the findings of Baruch et al., who reported a 1.7-fold increased risk of severe tears with operative vaginal delivery compared to spontaneous vaginal delivery, along with a higher overall incidence of OASI [16].

Previous studies have shown a connection between prolonged second stage of labor and OASI occurrence [17]. In our previous study focusing on parous women with a previous vaginal delivery, we found prolonged second stage to be a risk factor for more severe OASI [13]. In the current study we did not find an association between prolonged second stage and more severe OASI with almost identical results between groups. This may be due to differences in study population, with the majority of patients in the current study being primiparous. This highlights the possibility that different patient populations may have unique risk factors for OASI and OASI severity.

Few studies have previously addressed the question of risk factors for OASI severity. In their study from 2010, Roos et al. reported on 531 patients who sustained OASI out of which 429 had minor OASI compared to 92 with severe OASI. They found epidural analgesia to be the only independent risk factor predicting major OASI [18]. In another retrospective study, data on 520 patients with OASI was presented comparing minor (3A and 3B) vs. major (3C and 4th degree) tears. They found birth weight over 3.5 KG to be associated with major anal sphincter tear [19]. A third study originating from India, reported that shoulder dystocia was associated with severe OASI while episiotomy was a protective factor.

We found parity to be associated with severe OASI. While primiparity is a known risk factor for OASI, once sustained, parity may contribute to the development of more severe tears. This may be attributed to previous perineal trauma resulting in scar tissue formation. Such tissue often lacks the necessary elasticity to withstand the high shear forces experienced during labor, which may contribute to an increased risk of severe perineal tears [20].

In the current study, a trend was observed suggesting that epidural analgesia may be associated with a reduced risk of severe OASI; however, this association did not reach statistical significance (Table 3). Previous studies have reported conflicting data on the impact of epidural analgesia on OASI risk, however, data on its association with OASI severity is limited. In one study epidural analgesia was shown to be a protective factor with relation to OASI [21], while in a recent population-based cohort study, epidural analgesia was shown to be an independent risk factor for OASI [22]. In a recent meta-analysis, focusing on primiparous patients, epidural analgesia was not associated with increased risk of OASI [23]. These findings highlight the need for large scale studies focusing on epidural analgesia in different patient populations and its impact on OASI severity.

Limitations of the study include its retrospective case control design and limited sample size. While our sample size did not allow us to focus on specific patient populations within our cohort, granularity of the data including tear grade, allowed for evaluation of the current research question. Other limitations include lack of data regarding perineal protection techniques and experience of the caregiver managing the delivery.

In summary, within our cohort, prolonged second stage was not associated with severe OASI. We did however manage to define independent risk factors for severe OASI including operative vaginal delivery and increased parity while AROM was shown to be correlated with less severe OASI. These findings need to be confirmed on a larger scale study which would enable subgroup analyses of specific patient populations.

Directed and Undirected Vertex Connectivity Problems are Equivalent for Dense Graphs
Yonggang Jiang, Sagnik Mukhopadhyay, Sorrachai Yingchareonthawornchai

Abstract
Vertex connectivity and its variants are among the most fundamental problems in graph theory, with decades of extensive study and numerous algorithmic advances. The directed variants of vertex connectivity are usually solved by manually extending fast algorithms for undirected graphs, which has required considerable effort. In this paper, we present a simple, black-box randomized reduction from directed to undirected vertex connectivity for dense graphs. As immediate corollaries, we largely simplify the proof for directed vertex connectivity in n 2 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) n^{2+o(1)} time [ LNP+25 ] , and obtain a parallel vertex connectivity algorithm for directed graphs with n ω + o ⁡ ( 1 ) n^{\omega+o(1)} work and n o ⁡ ( 1 ) n^{o(1)} depth, via the undirected vertex connectivity algorithm of [ BJMY25 ] . Our reduction further extends to the weighted version of the problem. By combining our reduction with the recent subcubic-time algorithm for undirected weighted vertex cuts [ CT25 ] , we obtain the first subcubic-time algorithm for weighted directed vertex connectivity, improving upon a three-decade-old bound [ HRG00 ] for dense graphs.

Graphs.

3
A Reduction from Directed to Undirected Vertex Connectivity

We state our main result.

3.1
.

Suppose there is an algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
solving the minimum vertex cut on undirected graphs. In that case, there is a randomized algorithm solving minimum vertex cut on directed graphs w.h.p., making calls to
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
with cumulative
O(n^{2}\log n)
many edges and additional
O(n^{2}\log n)
time. Moreover, the reduction holds even when we restrict both inputs to unweighted graphs.

To prove
Theorem

3.1
, the reduction can be done in three steps as follows.

1.

We reduce directed vertex connectivity (Directed VC) to directed single-source vertex connectivity (Directed SSVC) in
Section

3.1
.

2.

Then, we reduce directed single-source vertex connectivity (Directed SSVC) to undirected single-source vertex connectivity (Undirected SSVC) in
Section

3.2
.

3.

Finally, we reduce undirected single-source vertex connectivity (Undirected SSVC) to undirected vertex connectivity (Undirected VC) in
Section

3.3
.

The constructions in
Sections

3.1
and
3.3
are folklore. The key novelty is the reduction in
Section

3.2
.

3.1
A Reduction from Directed VC to Directed SSVC

3.2
.

If there is an algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
solving single-source minimum vertex cut on directed graphs, then there is a randomized algorithm solving minimum vertex cut on directed graphs w.h.p., making calls to
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
with cumulative inputs
O(m\log n)
edges and additional
O(n^{2})
time. Moreover, the reduction holds even when we restrict both input graphs to be unweighted or undirected.

Proof.

We start by describing the algorithm. We assume the graph is not a complete graph as otherwise there is no vertex cut. Let the minimum weighted-degree of the input graph be
\delta
, and let
W=\sum_{v\in V}w(v)
. The algorithm samples
O(\log n\cdot\frac{W}{W-\delta})
many nodes independently, where each node
u
get sampled with probability
w(u)/W
. For each sampled node
u
, define
G_{u}
as the graph obtained from deleting every edge
(v_{1},v_{2})
with
v_{1},v_{2}\in N_{G}(u)
from
G
. The algorithm calls
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
on
G_{u}
with source
u
for every sampled node
u
, and returns the smallest vertex cut among all returns. We repeat the same algorithm for
G^{T}
and take the smaller vertex cut returned.

Correctness.

Let
(L,S,R)
be one of the smallest vertex cut. Suppose without loss of generality that
w(L)\geq w(R)
(one of
G
or
G^{T}
must satisfy this condition). Notice that
\delta\geq w(S)
because deleting vertices of weight
\delta
disconnects the graph, and
S
should be the smallest weight vertex set whose deletion disconnect the graph. Thus, we get
w(L)\geq(W-\delta)/2
. According to Chernoff’s bound, w.h.p. there is a sampled node
u\in L
, whose single-source minimum vertex cut has weight at most
w(S)
since
S
is clearly a single-source vertex cut in
G_{u}
(since we only delete edges from
G
to get
G_{u}
). To see that the returned cut in
G_{u}
must be a cut in
G
, let
(L^{\prime},S^{\prime},R^{\prime})
be a vertex cut in
G_{u}
with
u\in L^{\prime}
. Then we have
N_{G}(u)\subseteq L^{\prime}\cup S^{\prime}
since all edges adjacent to
u
is present in
G_{u}
. Thus, we only delete edges between
L^{\prime}\cup S^{\prime}
from
G
to get
G_{u}
, which means
(L^{\prime},S^{\prime},R^{\prime})
is still a cut in
G
.

Complexity.

We will analyze the expected size of the summation of all the graphs input to
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
. Let
u
be a sampled node. Fix an edge
e\in E
, let
I_{e}
be the event that
e\in G_{u}
. Notice that if
I_{e}
happens, then one of
e
’s end point must not be a neighbor of
u
. In other words, let
e=(v_{1},v_{2})
, then
u
must be in either
V-N^{in}_{G}(v_{1})
or
V-N^{in}_{G}(v_{2})
. Moreover, we have
w(V-N^{in}_{G}(v_{1})),w(V-N^{in}_{G}(v_{2}))\leq W-\delta
. Thus, the probability that
I_{u}
happens is at most
2(W-\delta)/W
. Notice that the size of
G_{u}
is at most
\sum_{e\in E}I_{e}
, which means the expectation of the size of
G_{u}
can be upper bounded by
m\cdot 2(W-\delta)/W
. Recall that we sampled
O(\log n\cdot\frac{W}{W-\delta})
many nodes, so the total number of edges among all
G_{u}
has expectation
O(m\log n)
. Notice that building all the graphs
G_{u}
can be done by brute force in
O(n^{2})
time.

Notice that when
G
is unweighted or undirected, the graph
G_{u}
becomes unweighted or undirected as well.
∎

\displaystyle=E_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup E_{3}\mbox{ where }

A Reduction from Undirected SSVC to Undirected VC

3.7
.

If there is an algorithm
A_{0}
solving minimum vertex cut on undirected graphs, then there is an algorithm solving single-source minimum vertex cut on undirected graphs making one call to
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
with input
O(m)
edges and additional
O(m)
time. Moreover, the reduction holds even when we restrict both inputs to unweighted graphs.

Proof.

Given a single-source minimum vertex cut instance
G,s
, suppose
G
is a weighted graph, we replace
s
by a node with weight
W=\sum_{v\in V}w(v)
, then run
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
on
G

If

s
by a clique of size

(the minimum degree of

. Let
(L,S,R)
be a minimum vertex cut of

cannot contain all vertices in

, as otherwise

is larger than

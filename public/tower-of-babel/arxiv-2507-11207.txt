On maximal curves of n n -correct sets
H. Hakopian, G. Vardanyan, N. Vardanyan

Abstract
Suppose 𝒳 \mathcal{X} is an n n -correct set of nodes in the plane, that is, it admits a unisolvent interpolation with bivariate polynomials of total degree less than or equal to n . n. Then an algebraic curve q q of degree k ≤ n k\leq n can pass through at most d ⁡ ( n , k ) d(n,k) nodes of 𝒳 , \mathcal{X}, where d ⁡ ( n , k ) = ( n + 2 2 ) − ( n + 2 − k 2 ) . d(n,k)={{n+2}\choose{2}}-{{n+2-k}\choose{2}}. A curve q q of degree k ≤ n k\leq n is called maximal if it passes through exactly d ⁡ ( n , k ) d(n,k) nodes of 𝒳 . \mathcal{X}. In particular, a maximal line is a line passing through d ⁡ ( n , 1 ) = n + 1 d(n,1)=n+1 nodes of 𝒳 . \mathcal{X}. Maximal curves are an important tool for the study of n n -correct sets. We present new properties of maximal curves, as well as extensions of known properties.

Definition 5.3

A finite set

In view of Proposition

(m-2+\delta)
-correct set in
f\setminus\mathcal{I}
and
an
(k-2+\delta)
-correct set in
g\setminus\mathcal{I},
denoted by
\mathcal{C}(f)
and
\mathcal{C}(g),
respectively.

For the set of nodes

\mathcal{X}:=\mathcal{I}(f,g)\cup\mathcal{C}(f)\cup\mathcal{C}(g)

we have the following proposition (for the first statement see
[
7
]
, pp.78-84).

Proposition 5.5
.

The set
\mathcal{X}
is
n
-correct and
f
and
g
are maximal curves.

Proof.

First let us check that
\#\mathcal{X}={n+2\choose 2}.
Indeed, we have that

\#\mathcal{X}=\#\mathcal{I}(f,g)+\#\mathcal{C}(f)+\#\mathcal{C}(g)=mk+{m+\delta\choose 2}+{k+\delta\choose 2}={m+k+\delta\choose 2}.

Let us then check that
\mathcal{X}
is
n
-correct. To do this, let us assume, in view of Proposition
1.3
, that
p\in\Pi_{n},\ p|_{\mathcal{X}}=0
and show that
p=0.

Indeed, according to Max-Noether fundamental theorem we have that

p=Af+Bg,\ \hbox{where}\ A\in\Pi_{k-2+\delta},\ B\in\Pi_{m-2+\delta}.

From here we easily obtain that
A|_{\mathcal{C}(g)}=0
and
B|_{\mathcal{C}(f)}=0.
Since the sets
\mathcal{C}(f)
and
\mathcal{C}(g)
are respectively
(m-2+\delta)
and
(k-2+\delta)
-correct, we obtain that
A=B=0
and therefore
p=0.

Finally, we have that

\#(f\cap\mathcal{X})=\#\mathcal{I}+N_{m-2+\delta}=d(n,m),
and similarly
\#(g\cap\mathcal{X})=\#\mathcal{I}+N_{k-2+\delta}=d(n,k).
Therefore
f
and
g
are maximal curves of degrees
m
and
k,
respectively.
∎

Non-decaying solutions to the 2D dissipative quasi-geostrophic equations
David M. Ambrose, Ryan Aschoff, Elaine Cozzi, James P. Kelliher

Abstract
. We consider the surface quasi-geostrophic equation in two spatial dimensions, with subcritical diffusion (i.e. with fractional diffusion of order 2 ​ α 2\alpha for α > 1 2 \alpha>\frac{1}{2} .) We establish existence of solutions without assuming either decay at spatial infinity or spatial periodicity. One obstacle is that for L ∞ L^{\infty} data, the constitutive law may not be applicable, as Riesz transforms are unbounded. However, for L ∞ L^{\infty} initial data for which the constitutive law does converge, we demonstrate that there exists a unique solution locally in time, and that the constitutive law continues to hold at positive times. In the case that α ∈ ( 1 2 , 1 ] \alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1] and that the initial data has some smoothness (specifically, if the data is in C 2 C^{2} ), we demonstrate a maximum principle and show that this unique solution is actually classical and global in time. Then, a density argument allows us to show that mild solutions with only L ∞ L^{\infty} data are also global in time, and also possess this maximum principle. Finally, we introduce a related problem in which we replace the usual constitutive law for the surface quasi-geostrophic equation with a generalization of Sertfati type, and prove the same results for this relaxed model.

2.1.
Littlewood-Paley operators

We begin with an overview of the Littlewood-Paley operators and some of their properties. There exist two functions
{\chi},{\varphi}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with supp
\widehat{\chi}\subset\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:|\xi|\leq 1\}
and supp
\widehat{\varphi}\subset\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{3}{2}\}
, such that, setting
\varphi_{j}(x)=2^{jd}\varphi(2^{j}x)
for all

,

\begin{split}&\widehat{\chi}+\sum_{j\geq 0}\widehat{\varphi}_{j}=\widehat{\chi}+\sum_{j\geq 0}\widehat{\varphi}(2^{-j}\cdot)\equiv 1.\end{split}

For
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, define
{\chi}_{n}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
in terms of its Fourier transform
{\widehat{\chi}}_{n}
, where
{\widehat{\chi}}_{n}
satisfies

{\widehat{\chi}}_{n}(\xi)=\widehat{\chi}(\xi)+\sum_{j=0}^{n}\widehat{\varphi}_{j}(\xi)

for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. For
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, define the operator
S_{n}
by

S_{n}f={{\chi}}_{n}\ast f.

Finally, for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

, define the inhomogeneous Littlewood-Paley operators
{\Delta}_{j}
by

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}&\Delta_{j}f=\left\{\begin{matrix}0,\qquad j<-1\\
\chi\ast f,\qquad j=-1\\
\varphi_{j}\ast f,\qquad j\geq 0,\end{matrix}\right.\end{matrix}

and, for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, define the homogeneous Littlewood-Paley operators
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
by

\dot{\Delta}_{j}f={\varphi}_{j}\ast f.

Note that
\dot{\Delta}_{j}f={\Delta}_{j}f
when
j\geq 0
.

2.2.
Kernels for the fractional heat equation

We first introduce the fractional Laplacian,

In most of this work, we will use the operator

where

We define the domain

to be the set of functions

for which the above limit exists and is finite for almost every

We now recall that the fractional heat kernel

is the solution to

(2.3)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\nu\Lambda^{2\alpha})g_{\alpha}=0

Often in what follows, we omit the spatial argument for notational convenience and write

While it is known that

2.3.
The constitutive law

For

is not well-defined as a convolution. Hence, we cannot obtain a constitutive law in the form

For the first modification, we let

denote the annulus centered at the origin with inner radius
\varepsilon
and outer radius

Given
\theta\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

1.1

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\PV K*\theta(x)&:=\lim_{(\varepsilon,R)\to(0,\infty)}(\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\varepsilon,R}(0)}K)*\theta(x)\\
&=\lim_{(\varepsilon,R)\to(0,\infty)}\int_{\varepsilon<|x-y|<R}K(x-y)\theta(y)\,dy\end{split}

for any

.

Definition 2.1

We say that

converges
uniformly over annuli

\displaystyle\sup_{(r,R)\in(0,\infty)^{2}}\left\|(\mathbbm{1}_{A_{r,R}(0)}K)*f\right\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

Proof of

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(K*\nabla g_{\alpha}),\varphi\right\rangle

When proving the pointwise regularity of the solution, we must establish that the divergence-free condition on

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
f\in(L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}))^{2}
with
\dv f(t)=0
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for all

\psi\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\dv\int_{0}^{t}(f*\psi)(s,x)\,ds=0\text{ in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

Proof.

For fixed
t\in[0,T]
, let
I(x):=\int_{0}^{t}(f*\psi)(s,x)\,ds
. Note that
I
is in
(L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))^{2}
. Then for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
we can apply the Tonelli-Fubini theorem to give,

\displaystyle(\dv I,\varphi)

\displaystyle=-(I,\nabla\varphi)=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(f*\psi)(s,x)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(s,x-y)\psi(s,y)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\,dy\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(s,x-y)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\,\psi(s,y)\,dx\,dy\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\dv f)(s,x-y)\varphi(x)\psi(s,y)dx\,dy\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(\dv f(s)*\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{\psi}(s))\,ds=0.\qed

Here,
\tilde{\varphi}
and
\tilde{\psi}
are reflected versions of
\phi
and

\psi,
namely
\tilde{\varphi}(x)=\phi(-x)
and
\tilde{\psi}(y)=\psi(-y).

3.1.

We turn now, in

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
(\theta,u)

⁢

Q

Lemma 3.3

Let

for some
\gamma\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\left\|f*g\right\|_{C^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\widetilde{C}^{\gamma}}

Let

Lemma 3.4

Let

For any distinct

\displaystyle\frac{\left|F_{r}(x-z)-F_{r}(y-z)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{\gamma}}\,dz=r^{2a}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\left|F(r^{a}(x-z))-F(r^{a}(y-z))\right|}{\left|r^{a}x-r^{a}y\right|^{\gamma}}r^{a\gamma}\,dz

\displaystyle=r^{2a+a\gamma}r^{-2a}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\left|F(r^{a}x-w)-F(r^{a}y-w)\right|}{\left|r^{a}x-r^{a}y\right|^{\gamma}}\,dw\leq r^{a\gamma}\left\|F\right\|_{\widetilde{C}^{\gamma}}.\qed

Lemma 3.5
.

If

then for all

Proof.

Assume first that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left|\int_{0}^{\left|x-y\right|}\nabla f(x+sh-z)\cdot h\,ds\right|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\int_{0}^{\left|x-y\right|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left|\nabla f(x+sh-z)\right|\,dz\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\int_{0}^{\left|x-y\right|}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{1}}\,ds=\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{1}},

where we used the translation invariance of the

If

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\left|f(x-z)-f(y-z)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{\gamma}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left|f(x-z)-f(y-z)\right|\,dz\leq 2\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}},

which completes the proof.

As before, integrating in time and applying the

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
and select
\theta_{0}\in C_{b}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

satisfying

. Let

be the mild solution given by

1.2
which exists up to time

is continuous in

t>0
for

Proof.

We first aim to bound the size of
\partial_{x_{i}}\theta(b,x)-\partial_{x_{i}}\theta(a,x)
uniformly in

a,b>0

We can write

(5.15)

\begin{split}&\|\partial_{x_{i}}\theta(b,x)-\partial_{x_{i}}\theta(a,x)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\left\|\left(G_{\alpha}(b)-G_{\alpha}(a)\right)\partial_{x_{i}}\theta_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\\
&+\left\|\int_{0}^{b}\nabla G_{\alpha}(b-s)\cdot(\partial_{x_{i}}\theta u+\theta\partial_{x_{i}}u)(s,x)\,ds-\int_{0}^{a}\nabla G_{\alpha}(a-s)\cdot(\partial_{x_{i}}\theta u+\theta\partial_{x_{i}}u))(s,x)\,ds\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\\
&\leq\left\|\left(G_{\alpha}(b)-G_{\alpha}(a)\right)\partial_{x_{i}}\theta_{0}(x)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\left\|\int_{a}^{b}\nabla G_{\alpha}(b-s)\cdot(\partial_{x_{i}}\theta u+\theta\partial_{x_{i}}u)(s,x)\,ds\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left\|\int_{0}^{a}(\nabla G_{\alpha}(b-s)-\nabla G_{\alpha}(a-s))\cdot(\partial_{x_{i}}\theta u+\theta\partial_{x_{i}}u)\,ds\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.\end{split}

For the second term on the right hand side of (

), applying Young’s inequality and

2.9
for

gives the bound,

\begin{split}&\left\|\int_{a}^{b}\nabla G_{\alpha}(b-s)\cdot(\partial_{x_{i}}\theta u+\theta\partial_{x_{i}}u)(s,x)\,ds\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq C\int_{a}^{b}(b-s)^{-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\|(\partial_{x_{i}}\theta u+\theta\partial_{x_{i}}u)(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\,ds\\

The argument then follows similarly to that after line (

6.1.

Having established the short time existence and regularity of

, we now extend the solution to all

. In order to achieve an extension, we must establish the appropriate

bounds for

To begin, we restate a convolution-type Grönwall inequality from
[

]
.

Lemma 6.1

(Volterra-Grönwall Inequality (Theorem 3.2 of
[
27
]
))
.

Suppose
v(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,T])
with
v(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,T]
, and let
a\geq 0,b>0
, and
0<\gamma<1
be constants. If

satisfies the inequality

v(t)\leq a+b\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\gamma}v(s)\,ds\text{ for a.e }t\in[0,T],

then

v(t)\leq\frac{a}{1-\gamma}\exp\left(\frac{b}{1-\gamma}\left(\frac{\gamma}{bB_{0}}\right)^{-\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}t\right)\text{ for a.e }t\in[0,T].

Here
B_{0}=B(1-\gamma,1)
where
B(x,y)
is the Beta function.

From here, we are able to improve the

bound on

.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let

. Suppose

is a mild solution to (

⁢
S
Q
G

) on

with initial data

where

(6.1)

\mu=\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-1},\quad B_{0}=B(\mu^{-1},1),\quad\text{and}\quad C_{\alpha}=\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-1}C(2\alpha B_{0})^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-1}}.

Proof.

Applying the

norm to

and using the fact that

is a probability measure, we obtain,

(6.2)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}K\ast\nabla G_{\alpha}(t-s)\cdot(\theta u)(s,x)\,ds\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.

Next, bringing the

in

yields

\begin{split}&\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\int_{0}^{t}C(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\|\theta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\,ds\\
&\qquad\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t];{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\int_{0}^{t}C(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\,ds.\end{split}

To reach the conclusion, we employ

∎

\theta
of (

S
Q
G

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right].

Suppose that
(\theta,u)\in(L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{2}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})))^{3}
is a mild solution to (

⁢
S
Q
G

) on
[0,T]
for some

with initial data
(\theta_{0},u_{0})
. Then

obeys the maximum principle

\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leq\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.

Proof.

With the hypotheses of the proposition,
by
3.10
, (
\theta,u
) is a
C^{2}
-solution to

(6.3)

\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=-\nu\Lambda^{2\alpha}\theta

on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}

Let
\phi
be a smooth, compactly supported bump function, with

identically one on

and
\supp\phi

. For each

and

We multiply (

(6.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\phi_{R}\theta)+\phi_{R}u\cdot\nabla\theta=-\nu\phi_{R}\Lambda^{2\alpha}\theta.

Using the product rule, we have

u\cdot\nabla(\phi_{R}\theta)=\theta u\cdot\nabla\phi_{R}+\phi_{R}u\cdot\nabla\theta.

Making the above substitution and adding and subtracting

, one has

(6.5)

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\phi_{R}\theta)+u\cdot\nabla(\phi_{R}\theta)=\theta u\cdot\nabla\phi_{R}-\nu\phi_{R}\Lambda^{2\alpha}\theta+\nu\Lambda^{2\alpha}(\phi_{R}\theta)-\nu\Lambda^{2\alpha}(\phi_{R}\theta)\\
&\qquad=-\nu\Lambda^{2\alpha}(\phi_{R}\theta)+I+II,\end{split}

Now, for

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}p|\phi_{R}\theta|^{p-2}\phi_{R}\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\phi_{R}\theta)\,dx=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}p|\phi_{R}\theta|^{p-2}\phi_{R}\theta u\cdot\nabla(\phi_{R}\theta)\,dx\\

Using a weak derivative formulation, we apply the identity

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}p|\phi_{R}\theta|^{p-2}\phi_{R}\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\phi_{R}\theta)dx&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\phi_{R}\theta|^{p}dx=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\phi_{R}\theta|^{p}dx.\end{split}

Therefore, we have

(6.6)

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}}^{p}=-p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\phi_{R}\theta|^{p-2}\phi_{R}\theta u\cdot\nabla(\phi_{R}\theta)\,dx\\
&\qquad-\nu p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\phi_{R}\theta|^{p-2}\phi_{R}\theta\Lambda^{2\alpha}(\phi_{R}\theta)\,dx+p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\phi_{R}\theta|^{p-2}\phi_{R}\theta(I+II)\,dx.\end{split}

With the divergence free condition on

&=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\phi_{R}\theta)^{p}\operatorname{div}u\,dx=0.\end{split}

Moreover, by Lemma 2.5 of
[

]
,

-p\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\phi_{R}\theta|^{p-2}(\phi_{R}\theta)\Lambda^{2\alpha}(\phi_{R}\theta)\,dx\leq 0.

Thus,

(6.8)

p\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p-1}\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\leq p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\phi_{R}\theta|^{p-2}\phi_{R}\theta(I+II)\,dx.

We divide both sides of (
6.8
) by
p\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p-1}
. We conclude that

(6.9)

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\leq p\left(\frac{1}{p\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p-1}}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\phi_{R}\theta|^{p-2}\phi_{R}\theta(I+II)\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq\|I+II\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\frac{\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p-1}_{x}}^{p-1}}{\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p-1}}=\|I+II\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left(\frac{\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p-1}_{x}}}{\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}_{x}}}\right)^{p-1}.\end{split}

We now bound the second term in the product. By the generalized Hölder inequality and the compactness of the support of
\phi
,

\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p-1}_{x}}=\|\phi_{2R}\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p-1}_{x}}\leq\|\phi_{2R}\|_{L^{p(p-1)}_{x}}\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\leq(2\pi R^{2})^{\frac{1}{p(p-1)}}\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}_{x}}.

This implies

\left(\frac{\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p-1}_{x}}}{\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}_{x}}}\right)^{p-1}\leq(2\pi R^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Substituting the above bound into (
6.9
), we find that for any
p\in[1,\infty)
and any fixed
R<\infty
,

\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi_{R}\theta\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\leq(2\pi R^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}\|I(s)+II(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.

Integrating the above expression over time, we get

\|\phi_{R}\theta(t)\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\leq\|\phi_{R}\theta_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{x}}+(2\pi R^{2})^{\frac{2}{p}}\int_{0}^{t}\|I(s)+II(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\,ds.

We take the limit as
p\to\infty
to produce the bound

(6.10)

\|\phi_{R}\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\|\phi_{R}\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|I(s)+II(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\,ds.

We claim that
\|I(t)+II(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}<\infty
for all
t
and moreover,

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\|I(t)+II(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}=0.

Clearly we have

(6.11)

\|I\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\frac{1}{R}\left\|(\theta u)(s,x)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.

We now estimate the commutator term
II
. We consider two cases separately:
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
and
\alpha=1
. First assume
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
. We expand the fractional Laplacian using the singular integral definition (
2.2
), since
\theta\in C^{2}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. We then simplify the commutator as follows:

\begin{split}[\Lambda^{2\alpha},\phi_{R}]\theta(x)&=\phi_{R}(x)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\theta(x)-\theta(y)}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\phi_{R}(x)\theta(x)-\phi_{R}(y)\theta(y)}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\phi_{R}(y)\theta(y)-\phi_{R}(x)\theta(y)}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy.\end{split}

We now split up the above integral into two parts,

\begin{split}[\Lambda^{2\alpha},\phi_{R}]\theta(x)&=III+IV,\end{split}

where

\begin{split}&III=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi(x-y)\frac{\phi_{R}(y)\theta(y)-\phi_{R}(x)\theta(y)}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy,\\
&IV=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1-\phi)(x-y)\frac{\phi_{R}(y)\theta(y)-\phi_{R}(x)\theta(y)}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy.\end{split}

We estimate
III
and
IV
separately. Starting with
III
, write

\begin{split}&III=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\phi(x-y)\frac{\phi_{R}(y)\theta(y)-\phi_{R}(x)\theta(y)}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\phi(x-y)\frac{(\phi_{R}(y)-\phi_{R}(x))(\theta(y)-\theta(x))+\phi_{R}(y)\theta(x)-\phi_{R}(x)\theta(x)}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\phi(x-y)\frac{(\phi_{R}(y)-\phi_{R}(x))(\theta(y)-\theta(x))}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy+\theta(x)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\phi(x-y)\frac{(\phi_{R}(y)-\phi_{R}(x))}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy.\end{split}

We invoke the Lipschitz bounds on
\phi_{R}
and
\theta
, as well as the singular integral definition of
\Lambda^{2\alpha}
, and find that

(6.12)

\begin{split}&III\leq\|\nabla\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\phi(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2\alpha}}+\left|\theta(x)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\phi(x-y)\frac{(\phi_{R}(y)-\phi_{R}(x))}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy\right|\\
&\qquad\leq C\|\nabla\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\left|\theta(x)\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi_{R}(x)\right|\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left|\theta(x)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(1-\phi)(x-y)\frac{(\phi_{R}(y)-\phi_{R}(x))}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy\right|\\
&\qquad\leq C\|\nabla\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+C\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.\end{split}

Moving on to
IV
, we use the Lipschitz bound of
\phi_{R}
to write

(6.13)

\begin{split}&\qquad IV\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(1-\phi)(x-y)\frac{(\phi_{R}(y)-\phi_{R}(x))}{|x-y|^{2+2\alpha}}|\theta(y)|\,dy\\
&\leq\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{(1-\phi)(x-y)}{|x-y|^{1+2\alpha}}\,dy\leq C\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.\end{split}

Combining (
6.12
) and (
6.13
), we conclude that

\|[\Lambda^{2\alpha},\phi_{R}]\theta(x)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}=\|III+IV\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\tilde{C}\|\theta\|_{C^{1}_{b}}(\|\nabla\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\|\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}).

It remains to show that as
R\to\infty
, the above quantities vanish in the limit. Clearly we have

(6.14)

\|\nabla\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\frac{1}{R}\left\|(\nabla\phi)\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}=\frac{1}{R}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.

Lastly, we have to prove
\|\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\to 0
as
R
approaches infinity. To see why this is the case, write

(6.15)

\begin{split}\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi_{R}(x)&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\phi_{R}(y)-\phi_{R}(x)}{\left|x-y\right|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy=\frac{1}{R^{2+2\alpha}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\phi(y/R)-\phi(x/R)}{\left|(x/R)-(y/R)\right|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dy\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{R^{2\alpha}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\phi(z)-\phi(x/R)}{\left|(x/R)-z\right|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dz=\frac{1}{R^{2\alpha}}(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi)(x/R).\end{split}

Therefore,

(6.16)

\begin{split}\|\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}=\frac{1}{R^{2\alpha}}\|(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi)(\cdot/R)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}=\frac{1}{R^{2\alpha}}\|\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.\end{split}

Gathering (
6.14
) and (
6.16
), we obtain the following commutator estimate:

(6.17)

\|[\Lambda^{2\alpha},\phi_{R}]\theta(x)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\tilde{C}\|\theta\|_{C^{1}_{b}}\left(\frac{1}{R}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\frac{1}{R^{2\alpha}}\|\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\right).

Substituting (
6.17
) and the estimate for
I
in (
6.11
) into the
L^{\infty}
bound for the cutoff of
\theta
in (
6.10
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{R}\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{R}\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\int_{0}^{t}\Bigg(\frac{1}{R}\|(u\theta)(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle+\tilde{C}\nu\|\theta(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}C^{1}_{b}}\left(\frac{1}{R}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\frac{1}{R^{2\alpha}}\|\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\right)\Bigg)\,ds.

Thus, given our hypotheses,

\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{1}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})),
we can rewrite the above expression as

(6.18)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{R}\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{R}\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+t\Bigg(\frac{1}{R}\|\theta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle+\tilde{C}\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}C^{1}_{b}}\left(\frac{1}{R}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\frac{1}{R^{2\alpha}}\|\Lambda^{2\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\right)\Bigg).

Taking the limit
R\to\infty
and invoking the weak-
\star
convergence of
\phi_{R}\theta\to\theta
and the uniform boundedness principle yields the desired conclusion,

(6.19)

\|\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}},

for all
t\in[0,\tau)
, when
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
.

Now suppose that
\alpha=1
. We again fix
R>0
and multiply (
6.3
) by the radial function
\phi_{R}
, which gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\phi_{R}\theta)+\phi_{R}u\cdot\nabla\theta=\nu\phi_{R}\Delta\theta.

We expand the Laplacian term as

\displaystyle\phi_{R}\Delta\theta

\displaystyle=\Delta(\phi_{R}\theta)-\theta\Delta\phi_{R}-2\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\phi_{R}.

Proceeding as before, we multiply by
p\left|\phi_{R}\theta\right|^{p-2}\phi_{R}\theta
and integrate over
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, leading to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\phi_{R}\theta\right\|_{L^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left|\phi_{R}\theta\right|^{p-2}\phi_{R}\theta(I+II^{\prime})\,dx,

where

\displaystyle II^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\nu\theta\Delta\phi_{R}-2\nu\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\phi_{R}.

For the new term, we have

(6.20)

\begin{split}\left\|II^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq\nu\left\|\Delta\phi_{R}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+2\nu\left\|\nabla\phi_{R}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq\frac{\tilde{C}^{\prime}\nu}{R^{2}}\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{\tilde{C}^{\prime}\nu}{R}\left\|\nabla\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\end{split}

Repeating the argument up to (
6.10
), we get

\|\phi_{R}\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\|\phi_{R}\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|I(s)+II^{\prime}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\,ds.

By repeating an analogous argument as above, we arrive at the same conclusion. That is, for
\alpha=1
,

(6.21)

\|\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
∎

Appendix A

We conclude by showing the equivalence of the definitions of
\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{I}
on
C^{2}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
f\in C^{2}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, then for
\alpha\in(1/2,1),

\Lambda^{2\alpha}f\equiv\Lambda^{2\alpha}_{I}f.

Proof.

As a consequence of Lemma 3.10 of
[
14
]
or Lemma 1 Section
V
of
[
25
]
, it suffices to show that
\Lambda^{2\alpha}_{I}f
is well defined as
\text{Dom}(\Lambda^{2\alpha}_{I};L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))\subset\text{Dom}(\Lambda^{2\alpha};L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
. To that end, we first break the integral into two pieces,

\begin{split}\Lambda^{2\alpha}_{I}f&=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{r<|h|<1}\underbrace{\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{|h|^{2+2\alpha}}dh}_{=:I(x)}+\underbrace{\int_{|h|>1}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{|h|^{2+2\alpha}}dh.}_{=:II(x)}\end{split}

We first work with
I(x)
by using the differentiability of
f
. Rewrite
I(x)
as

\begin{split}I(x)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{r<|h|<1}\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{|h|^{2+2\alpha}}dh.\end{split}

Since
f\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, by the Mean Value Theorem there exists some
\xi
on the line segment from
x
to
x+h
such that

f(x+h)-f(x)=\nabla f(x+\xi h)\cdot h.

Similarly, there exists some
\zeta
on the segment from
x-h
to
x
such that

f(x)-f(x-h)=\nabla f(x-\zeta h)\cdot h.

Subtracting the second equality from the first, we obtain

f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)=\nabla f(x+\xi h)\cdot h-\nabla f(x-\zeta h)\cdot h.

Now, we apply the Mean Value Theorem once more to the difference
\nabla f(x+\xi h)-\nabla f(x-\zeta h)
. Since
f\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
implies that
\nabla f
is Lipschitz, there exists some point
\eta
(lying on the line segment connecting
x+\xi h
and
x-\zeta h
) such that

\nabla f(x+\xi h)-\nabla f(x-\zeta h)=D^{2}f(\eta)\bigl[(x+\xi h)-(x-\zeta h)=\bigr]=D^{2}f(\eta)\bigl[(\xi+\zeta)h\bigr].

Noting that
\xi,\zeta\in[0,1]
so that
\xi+\zeta\leq 2
, we deduce

\left|f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)\right|\leq\|D^{2}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\,(\xi+\zeta)\,|h|^{2}\leq 2\,\|D^{2}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\,|h|^{2}.

Thus, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
h\neq 0
we have

\frac{|f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)|}{|h|^{2+2\alpha}}\leq 2\,\|D^{2}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\frac{|h|^{2}}{|h|^{2+2\alpha}}=2\,\|D^{2}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\frac{1}{|h|^{2\alpha}}.

Thus, since
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
,

|I(x)|\leq 2\|D^{2}f\|_{L\infty}\int_{|h|\leq 1}\frac{dh}{|h|^{2\alpha}}<\infty.

Turning to estimate
II
, we have

\begin{split}|II(x)|\leq\int_{|h|>1}\frac{|f(x+h)-f(x)|}{|h|^{2+2\alpha}}&\leq\int_{|h|>1}\frac{|f(x+h)-f(x)|}{|h|^{2+2\alpha}}\\
&\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|h|>1}\frac{1}{|h|^{2+2\alpha}}dh<\infty.\end{split}

Hence, the principal-value integral

I(x)=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{|h|>r}\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{|h|^{2+2\alpha}}\,dh

converges absolutely for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and so the singular integral definition of the fractional Laplacian is well defined for
f\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

∎

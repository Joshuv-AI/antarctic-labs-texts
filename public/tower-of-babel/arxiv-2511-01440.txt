Lie Algebra Decomposition Classes for Reductive Algebraic Groups in Arbitrary Characteristic
Joel Summerfield

Abstract
. Decomposition classes provide a way of partitioning the Lie algebras of an algebraic group into equivalence classes based on the Jordan decomposition. In this paper, we investigate the decomposition classes of the Lie algebras of connected reductive algebraic groups, over algebraically closed fields of arbitrary characteristic. We extend some results previously proved under restrictions on the characteristic, and introduce Levi-type decomposition classes to account for some of the difficulties encountered in bad characteristic. We also establish properties of Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction of non-nilpotent orbits, extending the known results for nilpotent orbits.



1.
Introduction

In
[
BK79
, §5.2]
, Borho and Kraft introduced
Zerlegungsklassen
(decomposition classes) as a tool for studying
Schichten der Lie-Algebra
(sheets of a Lie algebra).
They considered a (connected) semisimple algebraic group
G
of adjoint type, over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
, acting via the adjoint action on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie G
.
For an arbitrary element
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, with Jordan decomposition
x=x_{\mathrm{s}}+x_{\mathrm{n}}
, define
\C_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{g\in G\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}=x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right\}
.
Another element
y\in\mathfrak{g}
then has
ähnliche Jordanzerlegung
(similar Jordan decomposition) if there exists
g\in G
such that the Jordan decomposition of
g\cdot y=y^{\prime}_{\mathrm{s}}+y^{\prime}_{\mathrm{n}}
satisfies
\C_{G}y^{\prime}_{\mathrm{s}}=\C_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}
and
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\C_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)\cdot y^{\prime}_{\mathrm{n}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\C_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}
.
This yields an equivalence relation on
\mathfrak{g}
, whose corresponding equivalence classes are decomposition classes.
Useful properties of these decomposition classes were then established in later parts of
[
BK79
]
and
[
Bor81
]
.

In
[
Spa82
, §1.2]
, Spaltenstein extended the idea as follows.
They considered a connected reductive algebraic group
G
, over an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic, again acting via the adjoint action on
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie G
.
Elements
x=x_{\mathrm{s}}+x_{\mathrm{n}}
and
y=y_{\mathrm{s}}+\penalty y_{\mathrm{n}}\in\mathfrak{g}
were said to be equivalent in
\mathfrak{g}
if there exists
g\in G
such that
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y_{\mathrm{s}}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{z\in\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[y_{\mathrm{s}},z}}\right]=0}}\right\}
and
g\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}=y_{\mathrm{n}}
.
This again yields an equivalence relation on
\mathfrak{g}
, whose corresponding equivalence classes were named
packets
by Spaltenstein.

This definition generalises the concept of decomposition classes introduced in
[
BK79
]
, and coincides with them in the characteristic
0
case.
Spaltenstein then established properties of
packets
in
[
Spa82
]
and
[
Spa84
]
.
As with Borho and Kraft,
packets
were introduced by Spaltenstein to study the maximal irreducible subsets of
\mathfrak{g}
consisting of equal-dimension orbits, known as sheets.
Spaltenstein demonstrated in
[
Spa82
]
that some of the properties from
[
BK79
]
generalised immediately to good characteristic, using the fact that connected stabilisers of semisimple elements of
\mathfrak{g}
are Levi subgroups of
G
.
Moreover, certain properties related to nilpotent orbits were also shown in
[
Spa82
]
to hold in the classical cases in bad characteristic (see §
6.7
for details).

In
[
Bro98
, §3]
, Broer also considered decomposition classes, working under the assumption that
G
is the adjoint group of a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, over an algebraically closed field.
They primarily used the additional assumption that the characteristic is very good, and generalised further results from
[
BK79
]
relating to the closures of decomposition classes, as well as establishing that decomposition classes are smooth.
Further results on decomposition classes can be found in
[
Bro98a
]
and
[
TY05
]
(both assuming characteristic
0
),
[
PS18
]
(assuming the Standard Hypotheses), and
[
Amb25
]
(partly assuming good characteristic), amongst other places.

This paper first defines decomposition classes for an arbitrary algebraic group
G
, over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of arbitrary characteristic, acting on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie G
via the adjoint action.
For any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, we define its connected stabiliser
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x\subseteq G
to be the identity component of
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{g\in G\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ g\cdot x=x}}\right\}
, and let
x=x_{\mathrm{s}}+x_{\mathrm{n}}
denote its Jordan decomposition.

Decomposition classes
are then the equivalence classes of
\mathfrak{g}
under the relation
x\sim y
, which holds if and only if there exists
g\in G
such that
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y_{\mathrm{s}}
and
g\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}=y_{\mathrm{n}}
.
The decomposition class containing
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is denoted
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x
, and we prove that each decomposition class has constant stabiliser dimension, and constant centraliser dimension.

From §
2.2
onwards, we assume that
G
is a connected reductive algebraic group, and establish that our definition of decomposition classes coincides with the definition of packets used by Spaltenstein.
We prove some initial properties of decomposition classes in
Theorem

2.10
, including that they are
G
-stable,
\mathbb{K}^{\times}
-stable, irreducible, and constructible sets which form a finite partition of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Then we turn our attention to decomposition varieties, which are defined as the Zariski-closures of decomposition classes.
We equip the set of decomposition classes
\mathfrak{D}[G]
with the closure order, where
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\preceq\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.
In §
3
, we explore how the structure of decomposition classes are affected by central surjections, which leads us to the following preservation result.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
\varphi\colon G\rightarrow H
is a separable central surjection of connected reductive algebraic groups.
For any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, let
\check{x}\coloneqq\mathrm{d}\varphi(x)\in\mathfrak{h}
.
Then
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\mapsto\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{H}\check{x}
defines a bijection
\mathfrak{D}[G]\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}[H]
, with the following properties
:

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathrm{d}\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
restricts to a surjection
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{H}\check{x}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

Preservation of closure
:

\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}}}\right)=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.

\mathrm{(iii)}

Preservation of the partial order
:

\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\preceq\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{H}\check{x}\preceq\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{H}\check{y}
.

\mathrm{(iv)}

\dim\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x=\dim\ker\mathrm{d}\varphi+\dim\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{H}\check{x}
.

Note that (within the generality in which we are working) the stabiliser dimension
\dim\C_{G}x
and the centraliser dimension
\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x
do not necessarily coincide for arbitrary
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.
The fibres of the stabiliser dimension map
\dim\C_{G}\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
are referred to as
stabiliser level sets
, and the irreducible components of non-empty stabiliser level sets are called
stabiliser sheets
.
Analogously, the fibres of the centraliser dimension map
\dim\C_{G}\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
are referred to as
centraliser level sets
, and the irreducible components of non-empty centraliser level sets are called
centraliser sheets
.
We then use
level set
to refer to a subset of
\mathfrak{g}
which is (at least one of) a stabiliser level set or a centraliser level set, and
sheet
to refer to a subset of
\mathfrak{g}
which is (at least one of) a stabiliser sheet or a centraliser sheet.
This is an important departure from the literature (see §
4
for more details) which allows us to uniformly prove results regarding both situations.

Since decomposition classes have constant stabiliser dimension, they are each contained in a unique stabiliser level set; analogously, each decomposition class is contained in a unique centraliser level set.
Given a level set
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, we let
\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathfrak{J}\in\mathfrak{D}[G]\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \mathfrak{J}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}}}\right\}
denote the set of decomposition classes contained in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is a level set of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\mathfrak{J}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
.

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathfrak{J}
is a dense subset of an irreducible component of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
if and only if
\mathfrak{J}
is maximal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}

(
with respect to the closure order
)
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

The irreducible components of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
are in bijection with the decomposition classes which are maximal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}

(
with respect to the closure order
)
, via
\overline{\mathfrak{J}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\mapsto$}}\mathfrak{J}
.

\mathrm{(iii)}

If
\mathfrak{J}
coincides with an irreducible component of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, then
\mathfrak{J}
is isolated in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}

(
with respect to the closure order
)
.

We next look at generalising Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction to arbitrary orbits, building upon the work in
[
Spa82
]
.
For any Levi subgroup
L\subseteq G
, we consider the set of
L
-orbits in
\mathfrak{l}
under the adjoint action, denoted
{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathfrak{l}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$L$}}
.
We then use
[
Spa82
, §2.2]
to establish the existence of an induction map
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\colon{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathfrak{l}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$L$}}\rightarrow{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathfrak{g}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$G$}}
, which generalises the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction of nilpotent orbits.
After covering the properties of this induction established in
[
Spa82
]
, we prove the following results, generalising the corresponding known results about the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction of nilpotent orbits.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{O}\in{\raisebox{0.81778pt}{$\mathfrak{l}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.81778pt}{$L$}}
is an arbitrary
L
-orbit.

\mathrm{(i)}

The induced orbit
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}
is independent of the choice of parabolic used in its construction.

\mathrm{(ii)}

Induction is transitive
:

\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}=\Ind_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{O}
, for nested Levi subgroups
L\subseteq\penalty M\subseteq\penalty G
.

\mathrm{(iii)}

\dim\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}=\dim\mathcal{O}+(\dim G-\dim L)
.

\mathrm{(iv)}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}}}\right)\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
is a single
P
-orbit, where
P\subseteq G
is any parabolic subgroup for which
L
is a Levi factor, and
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Lie\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{R_{u}}(P)}}\right)
.

Having worked in full generality up to this point, we narrow our scope in §
6
to
Levi-type
decomposition classes; these are defined to be the decomposition classes of elements
x\in\mathfrak{g}
such that the connected stabiliser of their semisimple part
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}\subseteq G
is a Levi subgroup.
These are introduced as a tool to avoid some of the complications that arise in bad characteristic, and will allow us to prove the main result of this paper, which extends prior results of
[
Bor81
]
,
[
Bro98
]
, and
[
Amb25
]
.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is a Levi-type decomposition class.
Let
P\subseteq G
be a parabolic with Levi factor
L
, and unipotent radical
U_{P}=\mathrm{R_{u}}(P)
.

\mathrm{(i)}

\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
is a union of decomposition classes.

\mathrm{(iii)}

\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=\bigcup\limits_{z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})}\overline{\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}L\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(z+e_{0}}}\right)}
.

\mathrm{(iv)}

{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}}^{\mathrm{reg}}=\bigcup\limits_{z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})}\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}L\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(z+e_{0}}}\right)
.

This paper concludes by considering a conjecture of Spaltenstein (see

) regarding stabiliser sheets and nilpotent orbits.
In particular, we show that (regardless of characteristic) every Levi-type stabiliser sheet contains a unique nilpotent orbit.

Notation

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic

, with non-zero elements
\mathbb{K}^{\times}
.
All varieties and vector spaces will be over

, and all spaces are equipped with the Zariski topology.
All algebraic groups are assumed to be affine and linear, and the Lie algebras of algebraic groups will be denoted by the corresponding lowercase fraktur letter (for example,
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie G

If

\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}

For any homomorphism of algebraic groups

.
Let
[-,-]\colon\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{g}
.

We denote the set of all non-negative integers by
\mathbb{N}
, and the set of strictly positive integers by
\mathbb{N}^{+}
.
For each

, we let
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
denote the group of

Suppose

is an arbitrary subset.
The connected component of
H

, and referred to as its

.
The centre of
H
is denoted
\mathcal{Z}_{H}
, and its identity component is also denoted
\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{H}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{Z}_{H}}}\right)^{\circ}
.

The adjoint action of

is denoted

, for any

, and the corresponding

-

is

.
The set of all adjoint

-orbits in
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted
{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathfrak{g}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$H$}}
.
More generally,

denotes the

-

of

, and we say that

is

(equivalently,

).

We define the

of

\fc_{\mathfrak{h}}x\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{y\in\mathfrak{h}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ [x,y]=0}}\right\}=\mathfrak{h}\cap\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x
.

\C_{H}X\coloneqq\bigcap_{x\in X}\C_{H}x
and
\fc_{\mathfrak{h}}X\coloneqq\bigcap_{x\in X}\fc_{\mathfrak{h}}x
.
We also let
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})\coloneqq\fc_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathfrak{h}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x\in\mathfrak{h}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ [x,y]=0,\text{for\penalty\ all\penalty\ }y\in\mathfrak{h}}}\right\}

.
When there is no ambiguity, we refer to the

The

2.
Decomposition Classes

For any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, let
x=x_{\mathrm{s}}+x_{\mathrm{n}}
be the (
additive
)
Jordan decomposition
, as explained in
[
Spr98
, §4.4.19]
.
Then
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is
semisimple
if and only if
x=x_{\mathrm{s}}
, and
y\in\mathfrak{g}
is
nilpotent
if and only if
y=y_{\mathrm{n}}
.
This version of the Jordan decomposition is constructed by considering
x
as a locally finite linear endomorphism of the coordinate algebra
\mathbb{K}[G]
.
However, we shall now demonstrate an alternative (but equivalent) way of defining semisimple and nilpotent elements of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Following
[
Bor91
, Example 1.6(8)]
, an
immersive representation
of
G
is any injective homomorphism of algebraic groups
\rho\colon G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}
(for some
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
) such that
\rho
induces an isomorphism of algebraic groups
G\cong\rho(G)
.
Using
[
Spr98
, Theorem 2.3.7(i)]
, every algebraic group has at least one immersive representation, so fix such a
\rho\colon G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.
We then say that
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is
semisimple
if there exists a basis of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
consisting of eigenvectors of
\mathrm{d}\rho(x)\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, and
y\in\mathfrak{g}
is
nilpotent
if
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{d}\rho(y)}}\right)^{n}=0
is the zero-matrix.
It follows from
[
Spr98
, Theorem 4.4.20]
that these definitions are independent of the chosen immersive representation
\rho\colon G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, and coincide with the definitions used throughout
[
Spr98
]
.

We will make use of these alternative definitions in the proofs of
Lemma

2.2
(iii) and
Proposition

6.9
(ii), where we can use an immersive representation to assume (without loss of generality) that
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}
is a closed subgroup for some
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.

The set of all nilpotent elements of
\mathfrak{g}
(the
nilpotent cone
) is denoted
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
, and is a closed subset of
\mathfrak{g}
.
The set of nilpotent
G
-orbits is then denoted
{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$G$}}
.

Two elements
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
are
Jordan equivalent
, written
x\sim y
, if there exists
g\in G
such that
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y_{\mathrm{s}}
and
g\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}=y_{\mathrm{n}}
.
Then
\sim
is an equivalence relation on
\mathfrak{g}
, and thus we may consider its equivalence classes.

Definition 2.1
.

The
G
-decomposition class
of
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is defined as its equivalence class with respect to
\sim
, and is denoted
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{y\in\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ x\sim y}}\right\}
.

We let
\mathfrak{D}[G]
denote the set of
G
-decomposition classes, and note that this definition is different from the definition of packets found in
[
Spa82
, §1.2]
.
We shall prove in

that (assuming
G
is connected reductive) the two definitions coincide.

2.1.
Stabilisers and Centralisers

Observe that, if
\rho\colon G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}
is an immersive representation with
H=\rho(G)\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, then
\mathrm{d}\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
preserves the Jordan decomposition and restricts to a bijection
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y\rightarrow\fc_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathrm{d}\rho(y)
, for any
y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

\mathrm{(i)}

\C_{G}x=\C_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}\cap\C_{G}x_{\mathrm{n}}=\C_{\C_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}}x_{\mathrm{n}}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}\cap\C_{G}x_{\mathrm{n}}}}\right)^{\circ}=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}}x_{\mathrm{n}}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}\cap\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{n}}=\fc_{\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}}x_{\mathrm{n}}
.

Proof.

Since the adjoint action preserves the Jordan decomposition, we have that
g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}=x_{\mathrm{s}}
and
g\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}=x_{\mathrm{n}}
(for any
g\in\C_{G}x
), from which (i) follows.

For any closed subgroups
H,K\subseteq G
, we observe that
H^{\circ}\cap K
is a finite index closed subgroup of
H\cap K
, and thus
(H\cap K)^{\circ}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H^{\circ}\cap K}}\right)^{\circ}
.
Applying this to
H=\C_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}
and
K=\C_{G}x_{\mathrm{n}}
shows that the first equality in (ii) follows from the first equality in (i), whereas the second equality is just notation.

For (iii), we can use a suitable immersive representation to assume (without loss of generality) that
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}
is a closed subgroup, and consequently regard
\mathfrak{g}
as a Lie subalgebra of
\mathrm{End}(V)
, where
V=\mathbb{K}^{n}
.
Following
[
Bor91
, Proposition 4.2(2)]
, there exists a univariate polynomial
q(z)\in z\mathbb{K}[z]
(with no constant term) such that
x_{\mathrm{s}}=q\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)
.
If
y\in\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x
, then
y\circ x=x\circ y
as maps
V\rightarrow V
, from which it follows that
y\circ q(x)=q(x)\circ y
, hence
y\in\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{s}
.
Then
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[x_{\mathrm{n}},y}}\right]=[x,y]-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[x_{\mathrm{s}},y}}\right]
implies that
y\in\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{n}}
.
Therefore,
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x\subseteq\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}\cap\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{n}}
which (since the converse is immediate) proves that
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}\cap\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{n}}
.
∎

We define the
stabiliser dimension map

\dim\C_{G}\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
via
x\mapsto\dim\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\C_{G}x}}\right)\in\mathbb{N}
; likewise for the
centraliser dimension map

\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
.
We immediately observe that both of these maps are constant on each
G
-orbit.
Using the version of Chevalley’s Semi-Continuity Theorem from
[
Bor91
, Corollary AG10.3]
, we can establish the following lemma, in which a map
f\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
(from an arbitrary topological space
X
) is
upper semi-continuous
if
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x\in X\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ f(x)\geq n}}\right\}
is closed for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Both the stabiliser and centraliser dimension maps
\dim\C_{G}\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and
\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
are upper semi-continuous.

We then define the
stabiliser level sets
of
\mathfrak{g}
as the fibres of the stabiliser dimension map, and denote them
\mathfrak{g}_{(m)}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \dim\C_{G}x=m}}\right\}
, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Analogously, we define the
centraliser level sets
of
\mathfrak{g}
as the fibres of the centraliser dimension map, and denote them
\mathfrak{g}_{[m]}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=m}}\right\}
, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
This coincides with the notation introduced in
[
PS18
, Remark 2.1]
.

We shall use the term
level set
of
\mathfrak{g}
to refer collectively to any subset of
\mathfrak{g}
which is (at least one of) a stabiliser level set or a centraliser level set, and denote a generic level set by
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
Since
\C_{G}\lambda x=\C_{G}x
and
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}\lambda x=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x
(for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
), level sets are
\mathbb{K}^{\times}
-stable.
It follows from
Lemma

2.3
that each level set is locally closed in
\mathfrak{g}
.
More generally, for any subspace
V\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, we define
V_{(m)}\coloneqq V\cap\mathfrak{g}_{(m)}
and
V_{[m]}\coloneqq V\cap\mathfrak{g}_{[m]}
, and observe that
V_{(m)}
and
V_{[m]}
are also locally closed in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be an arbitrary subset.
We define the set of
G
-
regular elements
of
X
to be
X^{G\mhyphen\mathrm{reg}}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x\in X\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \dim\C_{G}x\leq\dim\C_{G}y,\text{for\penalty\ all\penalty\ }y\in X}}\right\}
, the set of elements of
X
with minimal stabiliser dimension.
Whenever the underlying group is unambiguous, we shall denote this set
X^{\mathrm{reg}}
instead.
Analogously, we define the set of
\mathfrak{g}
-
regular elements
of
X
to be
X^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x\in X\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x\leq\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y,\text{for\penalty\ all\penalty\ }y\in X}}\right\}
, the set of elements of
X
with minimal centraliser dimension.
Since
X^{\mathrm{reg}}=X\cap\mathfrak{g}_{(m)}
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
is minimal such that this intersection is non-empty, it follows that
X^{\mathrm{reg}}
is open in
X
; a similar argument holds for
X^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}
.

If
V\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a subsapce, then it follows that both
V^{\mathrm{reg}}
and
V^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}
are open dense irreducible subsets of
V
, and are thus both irreducible and locally closed in
\mathfrak{g}
.
In particular, since
\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}x\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a subspace (for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
), we know that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}x}}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{y\in\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x}}\right\}
is irreducible and locally closed in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is a level set and
Y\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

\mathrm{(a)}

If
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\mathfrak{g}_{(m)}
is a stabiliser level set, then
Y\subseteq{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{Y}}^{\mathrm{reg}}=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{Y}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

\mathrm{(b)}

If
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\mathfrak{g}_{[m]}
is a centraliser level set, then
Y\subseteq{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{Y}}^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{Y}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

Proof.

We shall prove (a), observing that an almost-identical proof works for (b), so suppose that
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\mathfrak{g}_{(m)}
.
Recall that
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{Y}
denotes the closure of
Y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Using
Lemma

2.3
, we know that
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{Y}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}\vphantom{Y}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{(\geq m)}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{n\geq m}\mathfrak{g}_{(n)}
.
Therefore, the minimal
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{Y}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{(k)}\neq\emptyset
must be
m
, and hence
{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{Y}}^{\mathrm{reg}}=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{Y}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
The inclusion
Y\subseteq{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{Y}}^{\mathrm{reg}}
is then immediate since
Y\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{Y}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
∎

For some fixed
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, let
\sigma_{x}\colon G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
denote the orbit map
g\mapsto g\cdot x
.
By considering its differential, we have the inclusion
\Lie\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\C_{G}x}}\right)\subseteq\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x
; however, we do not have equality in general.
Using
[
Bor91
, §9.1]
,
\Lie\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\C_{G}x}}\right)=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x
if and only if
\sigma_{x}\colon G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a separable morphism of affine varieties, if and only if
\dim\C_{G}x=\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x
.

There are many extra conditions we could impose on
x
and
G
to force equality here, such as the Standard Hypotheses (see
[
Jan04
, §2.9]
for an explanation of these).
Other suitable conditions can be found throughout the literature, including (for example)
[
Jan04
, §2]
,
[
Let05
, Lemma 2.6.2]
, and
[
Tay16
, Proposition 3.10]
, the latter of which uses results from
[
Her10
]
.

However, the most important condition for us follows from
[
Bor91
, Proposition 9.1(2)]
: if
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, then
\Lie\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\C_{G}x}}\right)=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x
.
Consequently, if
X\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
only consists of semisimple elements, then
X^{\mathrm{reg}}=X^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
satisfy
x\sim y
.

\mathrm{(a)}

\dim\C_{G}x=\dim\C_{G}y
.

\mathrm{(b)}

\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y
.

Proof.

Let
g\in G
be such that
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y_{\mathrm{s}}
and
g\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}=y_{\mathrm{n}}
.
It then follows from
Lemma

2.2
(ii) that
g\cdot\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)\cap\C_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}}}\right)}}\right)^{\circ}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y_{\mathrm{s}}\cap\C_{G}y_{\mathrm{n}}}}\right)^{\circ}=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y
.
Therefore,
\dim\C_{G}x=\dim\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x=\dim\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y=\dim\C_{G}y
, which proves (a).

Since
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)=\Lie\C_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)=\Lie\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)=\Lie\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y_{\mathrm{s}}=\Lie\C_{G}y_{\mathrm{s}}=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y_{\mathrm{s}}
, it follows from
Lemma

2.2
(iii) that
g\cdot\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)\cap\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}}}\right)=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y_{\mathrm{s}}\cap\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y_{\mathrm{n}}=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y
.
Therefore,
\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y
, which proves (b).
∎

It follows from
Proposition

2.5
(a) that each decomposition class lies in a unique stabiliser level set, and therefore each stabiliser level set is the finite disjoint union of the decomposition classes it contains.
Similarly, using
Proposition

2.5
(b), each decomposition class lies in a unique centraliser level set, and so each centraliser level set is the finite disjoint union of the decomposition classes it contains.

Given a level set
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, let
\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathfrak{J}\in\mathfrak{D}[G]\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \mathfrak{J}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}}}\right\}
denote the set of decomposition classes contained in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
It follows from
Proposition

2.5
that
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\bigsqcup_{\mathfrak{J}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]}\mathfrak{J}
.
If
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\mathfrak{g}_{(m)}
is a stabiliser level set, then we shall also use
\mathfrak{D}_{(m)}[G]
to denote
\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
.
Similarly, if
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\mathfrak{g}_{[m]}
is a centraliser level set, then we shall also use
\mathfrak{D}_{[m]}[G]
to denote
\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
.

2.2.
Connected Reductive Algebraic Groups

For the remainder of the paper, we always assume that
G
is a connected reductive algebraic group.
Following
[
Ste75
, §2]
, we say that a connected reductive subgroup
H\subseteq G
is
regular
if it contains a maximal torus of
G
.

By a
Levi subgroup
we mean a Levi factor of a parabolic subgroup, and observe that all Levi subgroups are regular connected reductive subgroups.
If
L\subseteq G
is a Levi subgroup, then we let
\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
denote the (finite) set of all parabolic subgroups of
G
for which
L
is a Levi factor.
Given any parabolic subgroup
P\subseteq G
, we let
U_{P}\coloneqq\mathrm{R_{u}}(P)
denote its unipotent radical, with corresponding Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
.

For the remainder of the section, fix a choice of maximal torus
T\subseteq G
, and let
\Phi=\penalty\Phi(G,T)
denote the corresponding
root system
.
For each
\alpha\in\Phi
, we let
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{U}_{\alpha}
denote the corresponding
root subspace
and
root subgroup
, respectively.
For any subset of roots
\Psi\subseteq\Phi
, let
G(\Psi)\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\mathrm{U}_{\alpha}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \alpha\in\Psi}}\right\rangle
denote the subgroup of
G
generated by the corresponding root subgroups, and let
G_{T}(\Psi)\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle T,\mathrm{U}_{\alpha}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \alpha\in\Psi}}\right\rangle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle T,G(\Psi)}}\right\rangle
denote the subgroup additionally generated by
T
.
We note that, without assumptions on the subset
\Psi\subseteq\Phi
, it may well be the case that
\mathrm{U}_{\beta}\subseteq G(\Psi)
for some
\beta\notin\Psi
.
Moreover, we let
\mathfrak{g}_{\Psi}
be shorthand for
\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Psi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.

Observe that
\mathfrak{P}(G,T)
is precisely the set of Borel subgroups of
G
which contain
T
.
For any
B\in\mathfrak{P}(G,T)
, let
\Phi^{+}_{B}\subseteq\Phi
denote the corresponding set of positive roots.
It follows that
U_{B}=G\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Phi^{+}_{B}}}\right)
and
B=G_{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Phi^{+}_{B}}}\right)
, with corresponding Lie algebras
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi^{+}_{B}}
and
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}
.
As proved in
[
Jan04
, §2.7]
, we have
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})=G\cdot\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}
.
Therefore, for any system of positive roots
\Phi^{+}\subseteq\Phi
, we have
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})=G\cdot\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi^{+}}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
H\subseteq G
is a regular connected reductive subgroup, with
T\subseteq H
.

\mathrm{(i)}

\Phi(H,T)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\alpha\in\Phi\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \mathrm{U}_{\alpha}\subseteq H}}\right\}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\alpha\in\Phi\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}}}\right\}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

If
\Phi^{+}
is a system of positive roots in
\Phi
, then
\Phi^{+}\cap\Phi(H,T)
is a system of positive roots in
\Phi(H,T)
.

\mathrm{(iii)}

If
X\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is such that
H\cdot X\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi^{+}}
, then
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{h})+X\subseteq\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
.

\mathrm{(iv)}

If
P\subseteq G
is a parabolic subgroup with Levi factor
L\subseteq P
and unipotent radical
U=\mathrm{R_{u}}(P)
, then
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{l})+\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proof.

(i) is a consequence of the proof of
[
Bor91
, Proposition 13.20]
, and (ii) is evident from
[
Spr98
, §7.4.5]
.
If
\Phi^{+}_{H}=\Phi^{+}\cap\Phi(H,T)
, then
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{h})=H\cdot\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi^{+}_{H}}
.
For any
y\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{h})
, there exists
h\in H
such that
h\cdot y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi^{+}_{H}}
.
Suppose that
X\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is such that
H\cdot X\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi^{+}}
.
Then, for any
x\in X
, we have that
h\cdot(y+x)\in\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi^{+}}\subseteq\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Therefore,
y+x\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
, as required for (iii).

Since all Levi subgroups of
G
are regular connected reductive subgroups, and
\mathfrak{u}
is
L
-stable, (iv) follows from (iii) and the fact that
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
for some suitable system of positive roots
\Phi^{+}\subseteq\Phi
.
∎

For any
y\in\mathfrak{t}
, let
\Phi_{y}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\alpha\in\Phi\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\alpha(y)=0}}\right\}
denote the set of roots
\alpha\colon T\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
whose differential
\mathrm{d}\alpha\colon\mathfrak{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
has kernel containing
y
.
Since each semisimple element of
\mathfrak{g}
lies in
G\cdot\mathfrak{t}
, and is contained in the Lie algebra of some maximal torus of
G
, the following lemma describes the connected stabiliser and centraliser of any semisimple element of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
y\in\mathfrak{t}
.

\mathrm{(i)}

\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y=G_{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Phi_{y}}}\right)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle T,\mathrm{U}_{\alpha}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \alpha\in\Phi\colon\mathrm{d}\alpha(y)=0}}\right\rangle
is a regular connected reductive algebraic group with root system
\Phi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y,T}}\right)=\Phi_{y}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

\Lie\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y}}\right)=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi_{y}}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{y}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.

\mathrm{(iii)}

There are only finitely many nilpotent
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y
-orbits in
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y
.

\mathrm{(iv)}

If
\Phi^{+}\subseteq\Phi
is any system of positive roots, then
\mathcal{N}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y\cdot\mathfrak{g}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Phi^{+}\cap\Phi_{y}}}\right)}
.

Proof.

See
[
Ste75
, Lemma 3.7]
for (i).
Since
H=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y\subseteq G
is a closed subgroup containing
T
, (ii) follows from
[
Bor91
, Proposition 13.20]
.
Then (iii) is a consequence of the fact that a connected reductive group has only finitely many nilpotent orbits in its Lie algebra (see
[
Jan04
, §2.8, Theorem 1]
, for example).
Finally, (iv) follows from the fact that
\Phi^{+}\cap\Phi_{y}
is a system of positive roots in
\Phi_{y}
, as proved in
Lemma

2.6
(ii).
∎

For each
\alpha\in\Phi
, we consider the subgroup
G_{\alpha}\coloneqq G(\{\alpha,-\alpha\})=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\mathrm{U}_{\alpha},\mathrm{U}_{-\alpha}}}\right\rangle
.
Using the results in
[
MT11
, §8.3]
, we can show that
G_{\alpha}\subseteq G
is a semisimple subgroup of rank
1
, and thus
[
Spr98
, Theorem 7.2.4]
shows that it is isomorphic (as an algebraic group) to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
.
Moreover,
[
MT11
, §8.3]
demonstrates that there exists an isomorphism of algebraic groups
\varphi\colon H\rightarrow G_{\alpha}
, where
H
is either
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
, such that the image of the standard maximal torus coincides with
T\cap G_{\alpha}
, and the differential maps the two root spaces of
\mathfrak{h}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose
y\in\mathfrak{t}
, and
\alpha\in\Phi
.

\mathrm{(i)}

There exists
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
such that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[x,x^{\prime}}}\right]\neq 0
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y\subseteq\mathfrak{t}
, and thus
\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y
consists only of semisimple elements.

\mathrm{(iii)}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)^{\mathrm{reg}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{z\in\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}z}}\right\}
.

\mathrm{(iv)}

\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{z\in\mathfrak{t}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \Phi_{y}\subseteq\Phi_{z}}}\right\}
and
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)^{\mathrm{reg}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{z\in\mathfrak{t}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \Phi_{y}=\Phi_{z}}}\right\}
.

\mathrm{(v)}

If
z\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)^{\mathrm{reg}}
, then
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}z
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\colon H\rightarrow G_{\alpha}
be an isomorphism of algebraic groups as described above, where
H
is either
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
.
If
H=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
, take
x=\mathrm{d}\varphi\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
x^{\prime}=\mathrm{d}\varphi\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
.
Otherwise, for
H=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
, let
\pi\colon\mathrm{GL}_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
denote the canonical quotient homomorphism, and take
x=\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{d}\pi\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}}}\right)\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
x^{\prime}=\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{d}\pi\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}}}\right)\in\mathfrak{t}
.
Simple calculations in either case then show that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[x,x^{\prime}}}\right]\neq 0
, as required for (i).

Using
Lemma

2.7
(ii), we have that
\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y\subseteq\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi_{y}}
.
Since
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y
is
T
-stable, it follows that
\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)
is also
T
-stable.
Thus, in order to establish the first part of (ii), it suffices to prove that
\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=0
(for each
\alpha\in\Phi_{y}
).
Fix
\alpha\in\Phi_{y}
, and – using (i) – let
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\subseteq\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y
and
x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\subseteq\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y
be such that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[x,x^{\prime}}}\right]\neq 0
.
It follows that
x\notin\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x^{\prime}
, and thus
x\notin\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)=\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y
; therefore,
\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is a proper subspace of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
Since
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=1
, we have that
\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=0
, as required.

The second part of (ii) is then immediate since
\mathfrak{t}

Theorem 2.10

Suppose

2.3.
Decomposition Varieties

The closure of a
G
-decomposition class is referred to as a
G
-decomposition variety
.
It follows from
Theorem

2.10
that each decomposition variety is
G
-stable, irreducible, and
\mathbb{K}^{\times}
-stable; in fact, they are stable under arbitrary scalar multiplication: if
z\in\overline{\mathfrak{J}}
, then
\mathbb{K}^{\times}z\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{J}}
, and thus
\mathbb{K}z=\overline{\mathbb{K}^{\times}z}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{J}}
.

We note that (in general) decomposition varieties do not have constant stabiliser dimension, or constant centraliser dimension.
However,
Lemma

2.4
(a) and
Proposition

2.5
(a) imply that
\mathfrak{J}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{\mathrm{reg}}
; analogously,
Lemma

2.4
(b) and
Proposition

2.5
(b) imply that
\mathfrak{J}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}
.

Since
0\in\overline{\mathfrak{J}}
, we have that
\overline{\mathfrak{J}}\cap\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})\neq\emptyset
.
Moreover, it follows from
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}0=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
that
\mathfrak{J}=\overline{\mathfrak{J}}
if and only if
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
, which was stated in
[
Amb25
, §3.1]
(for good characteristic).
We define a relation
\preceq
on
\mathfrak{D}[G]
by
\mathfrak{J}\preceq\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathfrak{J}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}}
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Suppose
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

\mathrm{(i)}

There exists a maximal subset
U_{x}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x
which is open and dense in
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}

\mathrm{(ii)}

If
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
, then
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y
.

\mathrm{(iii)}

\preceq
is a partial order on
\mathfrak{D}[G]
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x
is constructible by
Theorem

2.10
(iii),
[
An12
, Lemma 2.1]
implies that there exists a subset of
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x
which is open and dense in
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.
Taking
U_{x}
to be the union of all such subsets yields (i).

Let
U_{x}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x
and
U_{y}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y
be the subsets described in (i).
If
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
, then

and

are both open and dense in
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}

Since distinct decomposition classes are disjoint, it follows that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y
.

Reflexivity and transitivity of
\preceq
are immediate from its definition, so it remains to prove antisymmetry.
If
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x
and
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y
are such that

and

\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}

∎

Therefore, a decomposition class is uniquely determined by its corresponding decomposition variety.
We note that the proof in

2.11

We refer to

as the

on
\mathfrak{D}[G]

If we let
\prec
denote the corresponding strict partial order, then

implies that
\mathfrak{J}\prec\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}
if and only if
\overline{\mathfrak{J}}\subsetneq\overline{\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}}
.

We say that

\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x
is

\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\prec\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x,\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y}}\right\}\subseteq\mathfrak{D}[G]

\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y
.

Corollary 2.12
.

Suppose
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
are such that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\prec\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y
.
Then
\dim\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x<\dim\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y

Proof.

As observed above, we have
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}\subsetneq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}

Theorem

2.10
(iii) implies that

A useful way to visually represent the poset structure on

is with a Hasse diagram (see

, p. 279]

•

•

•

2.4.
Further Closure Results

In order to establish more properties about decomposition varieties, we need some general topological results about closures.
Suppose that

Each

\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{U}\subseteq V_{i}

Suppose

(which means that

Lemma 2.15

Proposition 2.16

Suppose

\mathrm{(i)}

\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}+\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}

\mathrm{(ii)}

\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})+\overline{G\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}}

\mathrm{(iii)}

Proof.

Using

2.10

\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x+\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x

Fix

\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x
is stable.
Since it is a homeomorphism of topological spaces,
\eta\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
preserves closures, and thus
\eta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}}}\right)=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.
Therefore,
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}+z=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
, from which (i) follows.

For (ii), first use
Theorem

2.10
(v) to get
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x_{\mathrm{n}}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})+G\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}
.
If
T\subseteq G
is any maximal torus, then
Proposition

2.8
(ii) implies that
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=\operatorname{\mathfrak{d}}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathfrak{g}}0\subseteq\mathfrak{t}
.
Therefore,
Lemma

2.15
implies that
\overline{\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})+G\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}}=\overline{\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})}+\overline{G\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}\vphantom{\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})}}
.
Hence, (ii) follows from the fact that
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is closed, and (iii) follows from (ii) and the uniqueness of the Jordan decomposition.
∎

3.
Preservation of Decomposition Classes

In this section we shall explore how decomposition classes interact with direct products, central surjections, and separable central surjections.
Suppose throughout that
H
is also a connected reductive algebraic group, just as
G
is.

The direct product
G\times H
is also a connected reductive algebraic group, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
, whose structure is easily determined by the structures of
G
and
H
.
Suppose
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
y\in\mathfrak{h}
, and consider the decomposition class of
x+y\in\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
.
It follows readily from the definitions that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G\times H}(x+y)=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x+\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{H}y
; consequently,
\mathfrak{D}[G\times H]=\mathfrak{D}[G]\times\mathfrak{D}[H]
as sets, where the closure order on
\mathfrak{D}[G\times H]
coincides with the product order induced from
\mathfrak{D}[G]
and
\mathfrak{D}[H]
.
By induction, this extends to arbitrary finite direct products of connected reductive algebraic groups.

3.1.
Preservation by Central Surjections

A surjective homomorphism of algebraic groups
\varphi\colon G\rightarrow H
is said to be
central
if
\ker\varphi\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{G}
and
\ker\mathrm{d}\varphi\subseteq\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Fix a central surjection
\varphi\colon G\rightarrow H
, and note that the differential
\mathrm{d}\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is not necessarily surjective (see §

).

Suppose
T\subseteq G
is a maximal torus, and
B\in\mathfrak{P}(G,T)
.
Then it follows from
[
Jan04
, §2.7]
that
\check{T}\coloneqq\varphi(T)\subseteq H
is a maximal torus,
\check{B}\coloneqq\varphi(B)\in\mathfrak{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H,\check{T}}}\right)
, and
U_{\check{B}}=\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{B}}}\right)
.
The induced comorphism
\varphi^{*}\colon\mathbb{K}[H]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[G]
restricts to a homomorphism of character groups
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varphi\mathord{\downarrow}_{T}}}\right)^{*}\colon\mathrm{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\check{T}}}\right)\rightarrow\mathrm{X}(T)
.
Then

, Proposition 22.4]
implies that this further restricts to a bijection of root systems
\check{\Phi}=\Phi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H,\check{T}}}\right)\rightarrow\Phi=\Phi(G,T)
.
Given any

, let
\check{x}\coloneqq\mathrm{d}\varphi(x)\in\mathfrak{h}
denote its image under the differential
\mathrm{d}\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
, and let
\check{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq\mathrm{d}\varphi(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
.

Lemma 3.1

(

, Proposition 2.7(a)]
)
.

The restriction of

to

has the following properties, for each

\mathrm{(i)}

It is a bijection
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{h})
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

It induces a bijection
{\raisebox{0.81778pt}{$\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})$}\big/\raisebox{-0.81778pt}{$G$}}\rightarrow{\raisebox{0.81778pt}{$\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{h})$}\big/\raisebox{-0.81778pt}{$H$}}

\mathrm{(iii)}

It restricts to a bijection

.

\mathrm{(iv)}

\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\C_{G}x}}\right)=\C_{H}\check{x}

Therefore, the structure of the nilpotent cone is completely preserved by central surjections.
We note that

(iv) is not necessarily true (in general) for non-nilpotent elements.
Since the Jordan decomposition is preserved by differentials of algebraic group homomorphisms, we have that
\check{x}_{\mathrm{s}}=\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)
and
\check{x}_{\mathrm{n}}=\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x_{\mathrm{n}}}}\right)
, for any

.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose

is semisimple.

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)=\check{\mathfrak{g}}\cap\fc_{\mathfrak{h}}\check{y}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)=\check{\mathfrak{g}}\cap\fd_{\mathfrak{h}}\check{y}

\mathrm{(iii)}

\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathfrak{d}}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)=\check{\mathfrak{g}}\cap\operatorname{\mathfrak{d}}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathfrak{h}}\check{y}

Proof.

Fix a maximal torus

such that

Since
\mathrm{d}\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism, the inclusion
\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)\subseteq\check{\mathfrak{g}}\cap\fc_{\mathfrak{h}}\check{y}

For the converse, suppose

Hence

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[y,x_{\alpha}}}\right]=\mathrm{d}\alpha(y)x_{\alpha}

Therefore,

2.7
(ii) implies (i).

Suppose now that

\Phi_{y}\subseteq\Phi_{x}
by

2.8
(iv).
Since

is a bijection
\check{\Phi}\rightarrow\Phi

\check{\Phi}_{\check{z}}\rightarrow\Phi_{z}
, for any

.
Therefore,
\check{\Phi}_{\check{y}}\subseteq\check{\Phi}_{\check{x}}

Conversely, suppose that

For (iii), suppose that

\operatorname{\mathfrak{d}}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathfrak{g}}y=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{z\in\mathfrak{t}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \Phi_{y}=\Phi_{x}}}\right\}

(iv).
Thus
\check{\Phi}_{\check{y}}=\check{\Phi}_{\check{x}}

\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathfrak{d}}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathfrak{g}}y}}\right)\subseteq\check{\mathfrak{g}}\cap\operatorname{\mathfrak{d}}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathfrak{h}}\check{y}

Conversely, suppose

Using (ii), we know that

Therefore,
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose

.

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x}}\right)=\check{\mathfrak{g}}\cap\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{H}\check{x}

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}}}\right)=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}

\mathrm{(iii)}

\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}

Proof.

Using

(i) in conjunction with

The forward direction of (iii) is immediate, so suppose that

3.2.

Suppose still that

.
It then follows from

.

\mathrm{(i)}

restricts to a surjection
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{H}\check{x}

\mathrm{(ii)}

Preservation of closure

\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}}}\right)=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}

\mathrm{(iv)}

Using

(i), we know that
\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x}}\right)=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{H}\check{x}\in\mathfrak{D}[H]

Its surjectivity follows immediately from the surjectivity of

For injectivity, suppose that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{H}\check{x}=\penalty\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{H}\check{y}

\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x+\ker\mathrm{d}\varphi=(\mathrm{d}\varphi)^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x}}\right)}}\right)=(\mathrm{d}\varphi)^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y}}\right)}}\right)=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y+\ker\mathrm{d}\varphi=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}y

Using

(ii) and (iii), respectively.

Let

Then (iv) follows from

∎

This proves
Theorem

from the introduction.
It follows that the Hasse diagrams of

In particular, suppose we already have the Hasse diagram for

.
To get the Hasse diagram for

we replace each decomposition class label with its image under
\mathrm{d}\varphi

from each dimension label.

Let

denote the adjoint group corresponding to the semisimple algebraic group

4.
Sheets

As explained in the introduction, decomposition classes were originally introduced in
[
BK79
]
as a tool to study the sheets of
\mathfrak{g}
.
In the existing literature, these are the maximal irreducible subsets of
\mathfrak{g}
consisting of equal-dimension orbits.
However, we will make a departure with the following definitions.

Definition 4.1
.

•

An irreducible component
S
of a non-empty level set is called a
sheet
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

•

S
is a
stabiliser sheet
if it is an irreducible component of a stabiliser level set.

•

S
is a
centraliser sheet
if it is an irreducible component of a centraliser level set.

Since each level set of
\mathfrak{g}
is (at least one of) a stabiliser level set or a centraliser level set, each sheet is (at least one of) a stabiliser sheet or a centraliser sheet.
With these definitions, it is stabiliser sheets that have been studied so far in the literature.
The change in nomenclature will allow us to uniformly state certain results about both types of sheet, whilst also highlighting differences (see §
6.7
).

Each stabiliser sheet lies in a unique stabiliser level set, and similarly each centraliser sheet lies in a unique centraliser level set.
If
\Lie\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\C_{G}x}}\right)=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, then
\mathfrak{g}_{(m)}=\mathfrak{g}_{[m]}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and thus stabiliser sheets and centraliser sheets coincide; see §
2.1
for a discussion on when this separability condition holds.

Given a (non-empty) level set
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, we say that
S
is a
sheet of

\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
if
S\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is an irreducible component of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
We note that it is possible for a sheet to be a subset of a level set, without being a sheet of that level set.
For example, if
G=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
then
S=\mathfrak{g}_{[2]}
is a centraliser sheet, and
S\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{(1)}
, but
S
is not an irreducible component of
\mathfrak{g}_{(1)}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
d=\dim G
, then
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}_{(d)}=\mathfrak{g}_{[d]}
.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
is both a stabiliser sheet and a centraliser sheet.

Proof.

Firstly,
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{[d]}
if and only if
\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=\dim\mathfrak{g}
, if and only if
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=\mathfrak{g}
, if and only if
x\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
; and thus
\mathfrak{g}_{[d]}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
.

If
x\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
, then
x
is semisimple, and so
\dim\C_{G}x=\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=\dim\mathfrak{g}=d
; therefore,
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{(d)}
.
Conversely, suppose that
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{(d)}
.
Since
\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x\geq\dim\C_{G}x=d
, we have that
\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=d
.
Therefore,
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=\mathfrak{g}
, and so
x\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Finally,
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}_{(d)}=\mathfrak{g}_{[d]}
is its own irreducible component, from which it follows that
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
is both a stabiliser sheet and a centraliser sheet.
∎

4.1.
Properties of Sheets

Many of the results here have already been established for stabiliser sheets, sometimes with additional assumptions on the characteristic (see
[
BK79
]
,
[
Bor81
]
, and
[
Spa82
]
, for example).
However, we can now extend these to arbitrary sheets in all characteristics.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
S
is a sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}

\mathrm{(i)}

S=\mathbb{K}^{\times}S=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\overline{\mathbb{K}^{\times}S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\overline{\mathbb{K}S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}

\mathrm{(ii)}

is locally closed and
G

\mathrm{(iii)}

\mathrm{(a)}

If
S
is a stabiliser sheet, then
S={\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{S}}^{\mathrm{reg}}=\overline{\mathbb{K}^{\times}S}^{\mathrm{reg}}=\overline{\mathbb{K}S}^{\mathrm{reg}}
.

\mathrm{(b)}

If
S
is a centraliser sheet, then
S={\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{S}}^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}=\overline{\mathbb{K}^{\times}S}^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}=\overline{\mathbb{K}S}^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}

Proof.

Consider the scalar multiplication map
\mathbb{K}^{\times}\times S\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, which is a morphism of affine varieties.
Both
\mathbb{K}^{\times}
and
S
are irreducible, and thus so is their image
\mathbb{K}^{\times}S
.
Recall that
\mathbb{K}^{\times}\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, and thus
\mathbb{K}^{\times}S\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
; hence
S=\mathbb{K}^{\times}S
by maximality.
Since
S
is necessarily closed in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, we have that
S=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\overline{\mathbb{K}^{\times}S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

For (i), it remains to prove the last equality.
Observe that
\mathbb{K}S=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{K}^{\times}S
, and so
\overline{\mathbb{K}S}=\{0\}\cup\overline{\mathbb{K}^{\times}S}
.
If
0\notin\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
then
\overline{\mathbb{K}S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\overline{\mathbb{K}^{\times}S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
Otherwise,
0\in\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, and so
Proposition

4.2
implies that
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=S=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
, and hence
\overline{\mathbb{K}S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=\overline{\mathbb{K}^{\times}S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

For (ii), since
S=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is locally closed, it follows that
S
is also locally closed.
Since
\overline{G\cdot S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is also irreducible in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, maximality implies that
S=\overline{G\cdot S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, from which
G\cdot S=S
follows.

If
S
is a stabiliser sheet, then
Lemma

2.4
(a) implies that
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}={\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{S}}^{\mathrm{reg}}
, and so the first two equalities in (iii)(a) follow from (i).
A similar argument using
Lemma

2.4
(b) shows the same for (iii)(b).
The final equality in both cases follows from the fact that
\overline{\mathbb{K}S}=\{0\}\cup\overline{\mathbb{K}^{\times}S}
.
∎

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is a level set, and
\mathfrak{J}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is a decomposition class.
Since
\mathfrak{J}
is irreducible, there must exist some (not necessarily unique) sheet
S
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
such that
\mathfrak{J}\subseteq S
.
Therefore, each decomposition class lies in at least one stabiliser sheet, and at least one centraliser sheet.

Proposition 4.4
.

Each sheet contains a unique dense decomposition class.

Proof.

Suppose that
S
is a sheet of the level set
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
Let
\mathfrak{J}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{J}_{r}
be the (finitely many) decomposition classes such that
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\bigsqcup\mathfrak{J}_{i}
.
Since
S=\bigsqcup\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S\cap\mathfrak{J}_{i}}}\right)
is closed in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, it follows that
S=\bigcup\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\overline{S\cap\mathfrak{J}_{i}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}}}\right)
, which is a finite union of closed subsets of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
Since
S
is irreducible, there exists some
1\leq j\leq r
such that
S=\overline{S\cap\mathfrak{J}_{j}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{S}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=S\cap\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}
, where the final equality uses
Lemma

4.3
(i).
Then
S\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}
, and so
\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is an irreducible subset of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
containing
S
; then the maximality of
S
forces
S=\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
It follows that
\mathfrak{J}_{j}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=S\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}
, and hence
\mathfrak{J}_{j}
is dense in
S
.

For uniqueness, suppose
\mathfrak{J}_{i}
is also dense in
S
, for some
1\leq i\leq r
.
By
Proposition

2.11
(i) there exist maximal subsets
U_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{J}_{i}
and
U_{j}\subseteq\mathfrak{J}_{j}
which are open and dense in
\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{i}}=\overline{S}=\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}
.
Therefore,
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\neq\emptyset
and thus
\mathfrak{J}_{i}\cap\mathfrak{J}_{j}\neq\emptyset
, which shows that
\mathfrak{J}_{i}=\mathfrak{J}_{j}
.
∎

Given a sheet
S
, let
\mathfrak{D}_{S}
denote its (unique) dense decomposition class.
It is clear from the proof of
Proposition

4.4
that, if
S
is a sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, then
\mathfrak{D}_{S}
is the unique element
\mathfrak{J}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
such that
S=\overline{\mathfrak{J}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
Since
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{S}=\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{S}}
, the following corollary is immediate from
Lemma

4.3
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Suppose
S
is a sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

\mathrm{(i)}

S=\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{S}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathrm{(a)}

If
S
is a stabiliser sheet, then
S=\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{S}}^{\mathrm{reg}}
.

\mathrm{(b)}

If
S
is a centraliser sheet, then
S=\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{S}}^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}
.

4.2.
The Closure Order in Level Sets

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is a level set, and consider the restriction of the closure order
\preceq
to
\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
.
The
Hasse diagram
of
\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
is defined to be the subgraph of the Hasse diagram of
\mathfrak{D}[G]
induced by
\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
.

Definition 4.6
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is a level set, and
\mathfrak{J},\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
.

•

\mathfrak{J}
is
maximal in

\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
if
\mathfrak{J}\preceq\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}
always implies that
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}
.

•

\mathfrak{J}
is
minimal in

\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
if
\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}\preceq\mathfrak{J}
always implies that
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}
.

•

\mathfrak{J}
is
isolated in

\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
if it is both maximal and minimal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

By looking at the Hasse diagram of
\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
, we can determine visually if a decomposition class
\mathfrak{J}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is maximal/minimal or isolated in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
:
\mathfrak{J}
is maximal/minimal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
if and only if there are no edges whose lower/upper end point is
\mathfrak{J}
, and
\mathfrak{J}
is isolated in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
if and only if there are no edges for which
\mathfrak{J}
is an end point.

Theorem 4.7
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is a level set, and
\mathfrak{J}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
.

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathfrak{J}
is maximal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
if and only if
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{D}_{S}

(
for a sheet
S
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle})
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

The sheets of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
are in bijection with the maximal decomposition classes in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

\mathrm{(iii)}

If
\mathfrak{J}
coincides with a sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
then it is isolated in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{D}_{S}
for some sheet
S
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, and that
\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
satisfies
\mathfrak{D}_{S}\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.
Let
S^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
be a sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
such that
\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}\subseteq S^{\prime}
.
Then
Corollary

4.5
(i) and
Lemma

4.3
(i) imply that
S=\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{S}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{S}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=S^{\prime}
.
Since both are irreducible components of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, we have that
S=S^{\prime}
, and so the above inclusions imply that
S=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{D}_{S}
, and so
\mathfrak{D}_{S}
is maximal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

Conversely, suppose
\mathfrak{J}
is maximal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, and let
S
be a sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
such that
\mathfrak{J}\subseteq S
.
Then
Corollary

4.5
(i) implies that
\mathfrak{J}\subseteq S=\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{S}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{S}}
.
Since
\mathfrak{D}_{S}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, the maximality of
\mathfrak{J}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
implies that
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{D}_{S}
, which proves (i).

The bijection for (ii) is given by the map
S\mapsto\mathfrak{D}_{S}
, which sends sheets of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
to maximal decomposition classes in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
; it is well-defined and surjective by (i), and injective by
Corollary

4.5
(i).

Finally, for (iii), if
\mathfrak{J}=S
is itself a sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
then
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{D}_{S}
, and so (i) implies that
\mathfrak{J}
is maximal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
Moreover, if
\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
satisfies
\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{J}}
, then
Lemma

4.3
(i) implies that
\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{J}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}=\mathfrak{J}
, and thus
\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{J}
.
Since
\mathfrak{J}=S
is both maximal and minimal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, it is isolated in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
by definition.
∎

This proves
Theorem

2
from the introduction.
It is currently an open question as to whether the converse of
Theorem

4.7
(iii) holds in all cases.
However, if we make an additional assumption about the sheets of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
, then we can prove the converse to be true.

Theorem 4.8
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is a level set, and assume that every sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is a union of decomposition classes.
Then a decomposition class
\mathfrak{J}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
coincides with a sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
if and only if it is isolated in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

Proof.

The forward direction is covered by
Theorem

4.7
(iii), so suppose that
\mathfrak{J}
is isolated in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
Since
\mathfrak{J}
is maximal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
,
Theorem

4.7
(i) implies there exists a sheet
S
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
such that
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{D}_{S}
; it is therefore sufficient to prove that
\mathfrak{D}_{S}=S
.

It follows from our assumption that
S=\bigsqcup\mathfrak{J}_{i}
, for some collection of decomposition classes
\mathfrak{J}_{i}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
.
Since
\mathfrak{J}_{i}\subseteq S\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{D}_{S}}
, we have that
\mathfrak{J}_{i}\preceq\mathfrak{D}_{S}
, and so the minimality of
\mathfrak{D}_{S}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
implies that
\mathfrak{J}_{i}=\mathfrak{D}_{S}
.
Therefore,
S=\mathfrak{D}_{S}
, as required.
∎

We come back to the assumption required for
Theorem

4.8
in
Corollary

6.25
.
In particular, it always holds if the characteristic is good for
G
.

5.
Lusztig-Spaltenstein Induction

The Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction of nilpotent orbits is already well-studied for connected reductive algebraic groups; see, for example,
[
CM93
, §7]
(over
\mathbb{C}
) and
[
Spa82
, §2.1]
.
However, as demonstrated in
[
Spa82
, §2.2]
, we do not have to limit ourselves to considering only nilpotent orbits.

We shall first cover the results regarding the induction of arbitrary adjoint orbits that Spaltenstein proved in
[
Spa82
]
, and then establish some further useful properties, in line with the known properties of nilpotent Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction.
We note that some of these properties were established (for characteristic
0
) in
[
Bor81
, §2]
.

It is important to note that
[
Spa82
, §2.2]
is carried out under the assumption that all centralisers of semisimple elements of
\mathfrak{g}
have only finitely many nilpotent orbits.
However, as already observed in
Lemma

2.7
(iii), this has since been shown to always be true for any connected reductive
G
; therefore, this assumption imposes no restriction on us.

5.1.
Construction and Initial Properties

Fix a Levi subgroup
L\subseteq G
, and consider the
L
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\coloneqq L\cdot x\in{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathfrak{l}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$L$}}
, for some
x\in\mathfrak{l}
.
Let
P\in\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
be any parabolic subgroup of
G
for which
L
is a Levi factor, with unipotent radical
U_{P}=\mathrm{R_{u}}(P)
.
Then
[
Spa82
, §2.2]
demonstrates that there exists a dense
G
-orbit in
G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
, which we denote
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}=\penalty\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}L\cdot x
.

By construction, this coincides with the usual nilpotent Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction, when it is restricted to nilpotent orbits.
The following lemma covers the properties of this induction map
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\colon{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathfrak{l}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$L$}}\rightarrow{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathfrak{g}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$G$}}
that can either be found explicitly in
[
Spa82
, §2.2]
, or follow readily as consequences.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{\mathcal{O}}=\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}=\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}L\cdot x
.

\mathrm{(i)}

G\cdot\tilde{x}
is the unique dense
G
-orbit in
G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)}}\right)^{\mathrm{reg}}
.

\mathrm{(iii)}

\dim\C_{G}\tilde{x}=\dim\C_{L}x
.

\mathrm{(iv)}

\C^{\,\circ}_{L}x_{\mathrm{s}}
is a Levi subgroup of
\C^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}
, and thus
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\coloneqq\Ind_{\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{l}}x_{\mathrm{s}}}^{\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{C}^{\,\circ}_{L}x_{\mathrm{s}}\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}}}\right)
is a well-defined nilpotent
\C^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}
-orbit.

\mathrm{(v)}

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}=G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x_{\mathrm{s}}+\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}}\right);
moreover, the Jordan decomposition of
\tilde{x}
is
(
up to
G
-conjugacy
)
equal to
x_{\mathrm{s}}+\tilde{n}
for some
\tilde{n}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x_{\mathrm{n}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)\cap\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
.

\mathrm{(vi)}

If
L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}
, then
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}=G\cdot x
.

Proof.

If
y\in\mathfrak{g}
is such that
G\cdot y
is dense in
G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
then
G\cdot\tilde{x}
and
G\cdot y
are
G
-orbits with the same closure
\overline{G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)}
.
Since
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{0}}^{\mathrm{reg}}=\mathcal{O}_{0}
for any
G
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}
, both (i) and (ii) are immediate.
We note that, although (ii) is not explicitly stated in
[
Spa82
]
, it is implied by the notation towards the end of
[
Spa82
, §2.2]
.

On the other hand, (iii) is stated explicitly in
[
Spa82
, §2.2]
, as is the first part of (iv); the second part of which is immediate from the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction of nilpotent orbits (see
[
Spa82
, §2.1]
, for example).

As a consequence of Spaltenstein’s construction of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
in
[
Spa82
, §2.2]
, we have that (up to
G
-conjugacy)
\tilde{x}=x_{\mathrm{s}}+\tilde{n}
for some
\tilde{n}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x_{\mathrm{n}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)\cap\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
.
Since
x_{\mathrm{n}}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{l})
, it follows from
Lemma

2.6
(iv) that
x_{\mathrm{n}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
, and thus
\tilde{n}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}
.
Therefore,
\tilde{x}=x_{\mathrm{s}}+\tilde{n}
is the Jordan decomposition of
\tilde{x}\in\mathfrak{g}
, and so (v) follows from (iv).

Finally, for (vi), suppose that
L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}
.
Then
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{L}x_{\mathrm{s}}=L
, and
Lemma

2.7
(ii) implies that
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}=\fc_{\mathfrak{l}}x_{\mathrm{s}}=\mathfrak{l}
.
Therefore, it follows from (v) that
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}=G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x_{\mathrm{s}}+\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{l}}L\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}}}\right)=G\cdot\penalty\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x_{\mathrm{s}}+L\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}}}\right)=G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L\cdot x}}\right)=G\cdot x
.
∎

Lemma

5.1
(v) is very important because it describes the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction of an arbitrary orbit in terms of the induction of a nilpotent orbit; this will allow us to generalise many of the well-known properties of nilpotent induction to hold for arbitrary orbits.

The following result is not stated anywhere in
[
Spa82
]
, but is implicit from the notation.
We note that
[
Spa82
, §2.1]
does establish this result for nilpotent orbits.

Corollary 5.2
.

The induced orbit
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}=\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}
is independent of the choice of parabolic used in its construction.
Therefore, for any
P,Q\in\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
, we have
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)}}\right)^{\mathrm{reg}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}}}}\right)}}\right)^{\mathrm{reg}}
.

Proof.

Recall from
Lemma

5.1
(iv) that
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{L}x_{\mathrm{s}}
is a Levi subgroup
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}
.
It follows from
[
Spa82
, §2.2]
that
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{P}x_{\mathrm{s}}
and
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{Q}x_{\mathrm{s}}
are both elements of
\mathfrak{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}},\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{L}x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)
.
As noted above,
[
Spa82
, §2.1]
shows that the nilpotent
\C^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=\Ind_{\fc_{\mathfrak{l}}x_{\mathrm{s}}}^{\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}}\C^{\,\circ}_{L}x_{\mathrm{s}}\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}
is independent of the choice of element of
\mathfrak{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}},\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{L}x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)
.
Therefore,
Lemma

5.1
(v) implies that
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}=G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x_{\mathrm{s}}+\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}}\right)
is independent of the choice of element of
\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
.
The statement that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)}}\right)^{\mathrm{reg}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}}}}\right)}}\right)^{\mathrm{reg}}
is then an immediate consequence of
Lemma

5.1
(ii).
∎

This justifies the fact that our notation for the induced orbit
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}L\cdot x
makes no reference to the choice of parabolic
P\in\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
.
In subsequent results regarding Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction, we will implicitly use
Corollary

5.2
without mention.
We also have the following consequence of the fact that Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction preserves stabiliser dimension, which allows us to calculate the dimension of the induced orbit directly from the dimension of the original orbit.

Corollary 5.3
.

For any
P\in\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
, we have
\dim\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}=\dim\mathcal{O}+\penalty\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dim G-\dim L}}\right)=\dim\mathcal{O}+2\dim\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x\in\mathcal{O}
and
\tilde{x}\in\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}
.
Then
Lemma

5.1
(iii) implies that
\dim\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}=\dim G-\penalty\dim\C_{G}\tilde{x}=\dim G-\dim\C_{L}x=(\dim G-\dim L)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dim L-\dim\C_{L}x}}\right)
from which the first equality follows.
The second equality subsequently follows from the observation that
\dim\mathfrak{g}=\dim\mathfrak{l}+2\dim\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which can be seen by considering the root subspaces with respect to some maximal torus
T\subseteq L
.
∎

5.2.
Inducing Unions of Orbits

The following result demonstrates that we can extend induction to unions of equal-dimension orbits.

Corollary 5.4
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{O}\subseteq{\raisebox{0.81778pt}{$\mathfrak{l}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.81778pt}{$L$}}
is a collection of equal-dimension
L
-orbits, with union
X\coloneqq\bigcup_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{O}}\mathcal{O}
, and let
P\in\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
.

\mathrm{(i)}

If
d=\mathrm{codim}_{\mathfrak{l}}\,\mathcal{O}=\dim L-\dim\mathcal{O}
, for any
\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{O}
, then
X\subseteq\mathfrak{l}_{(d)}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)}}\right)^{\mathrm{reg}}=\bigcup\limits_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{O}}\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{(d)}
.

\mathrm{(iii)}

If
X
is closed in
\mathfrak{l}_{(d)}
, then
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)}}\right)^{\mathrm{reg}}
is closed in
\mathfrak{g}_{(d)}
.

Proof.

Since the orbits in
X
have the same dimension, they also have the same codimension
d=\mathrm{codim}_{\mathfrak{l}}\,\mathcal{O}=\dim L-\dim\mathcal{O}
.
Then the (unique)
L
-stabiliser level set containing
X
is precisely
\mathfrak{l}_{(d)}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x\in\mathfrak{l}\,\middle|\,\dim\C_{L}x=d}}\right\}
, which proves (i).

It follows from
Lemma

5.1
(iii) that all of the corresponding
G
-orbits
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}
are also of the same codimension
d
.
Therefore,
\bigcup_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{O}}\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{(d)}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{y\in\mathfrak{g}\,\middle|\,\dim\C_{G}y=d}}\right\}
.
The arguments at the end of
[
Spa82
, §2.2]
then complete the proofs of parts (ii) and (iii).
∎

If
X
is a union of equal-dimension
L
-orbits, then we define
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}X\coloneqq\bigcup\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}
, where the union is taken over all
L
-orbits
\mathcal{O}\in{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathfrak{l}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$L$}}
such that
\mathcal{O}\subseteq X
.
Using
Corollary

5.4
(ii), this is equivalent to the definition given in
[
Spa82
, §2.2]
.

5.3.
Transitivity of Induction

The following property (colloquially known as transitivity of induction) is already well-known for nilpotent orbits under certain assumptions (see
[
PS18
, §2.5]
for a proof assuming the Standard Hypotheses).
However, the proof we present here does not require the transitivity of nilpotent induction as a prerequisite, and so also serves as a proof of that result in arbitrary characteristic.

Theorem 5.5
.

If
L\subseteq M\subseteq G
are nested Levi subgroups of
G
, then
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}=\Ind_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{O}
for any
L
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\in{\raisebox{0.81778pt}{$\mathfrak{l}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.81778pt}{$L$}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
Q_{1}\in\mathfrak{P}(M,L)
and
Q_{2}\in\mathfrak{P}(G,M)
.
Then
Q_{1}=U_{Q_{1}}\rtimes L
and
Q_{2}=U_{Q_{2}}\rtimes M
.
By considering root subgroups (with respect to a choice of maximal torus
T\subseteq L
) we have that
P\coloneqq U_{Q_{2}}\rtimes Q_{1}\in\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
.
Moreover,
U_{P}=U_{Q_{2}}\rtimes U_{Q_{1}}
, and thus
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}
.

It follows from
Lemma

5.1
(i) that there exists
y_{1}\in\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{O}\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}}}\right)
, and there exists
y_{2}\in\Ind_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{O}\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}}}\right)
.
Since
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{O}=M\cdot y_{1}
, and
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}
is
M
-stable, there exists
h\in M
such that
h\cdot y_{2}\in y_{1}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}=\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Therefore,
G\cdot y_{2}=\Ind_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{O}\subseteq G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
.

We have that
\dim\Ind_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{O}=\dim\mathcal{O}+2\dim\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}+\penalty 2\dim\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}=\dim\mathcal{O}+2\dim\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\dim\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}
, where we have used
Corollary

5.3
thrice.
Therefore,
\Ind_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{O}
and
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}
are both equal-dimension
G
-orbits contained in
G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
, and so
Lemma

5.1
(i) implies that
\Ind_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{O}=\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}
.
∎

Our final result of the section is the generalisation of a fact about nilpotent Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction regarding the intersection of
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose that
P\in\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
and
\mathcal{O}\in{\raisebox{0.81778pt}{$\mathfrak{l}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.81778pt}{$L$}}
.

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
P
-stable.

\mathrm{(ii)}

If
y\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}}}\right)\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
, then
P\cdot y
is open and dense in
\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

\mathrm{(iii)}

The intersection
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}}}\right)\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
is a single
P
-orbit.

Proof.

If
x\in\mathcal{O}
, then
[
Let05
, Lemma 2.6.6]
implies that
U_{P}\cdot x\subseteq x+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and therefore
P\cdot x\subseteq L\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
.
Since
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
P
-stable (and thus also
L
-stable), it follows that
P\cdot x\subseteq\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and thus
P\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which proves (i).

If
y\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}}}\right)\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
, then
Lemma

5.1
(iii) implies that
\dim\C_{G}y=\dim L-\dim\mathcal{O}=\dim P-\dim U_{P}-\dim\mathcal{O}
.
Moreover, (i) implies that
P\cdot y\subseteq\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and thus
\dim P\cdot\penalty y\leq\dim\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)=\dim\mathcal{O}+\dim U_{P}
.
Then we have that
\dim\C_{P}y\geq\dim P-\penalty\dim U_{P}-\penalty\dim\mathcal{O}=\dim\C_{G}y
, and so
\C_{P}y\subseteq\C_{G}y
implies that
\dim\C_{P}y=\dim\C_{G}y
.
Therefore,
\dim P\cdot y=\dim U_{P}+\dim\mathcal{O}=\dim\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
.
Since
P\cdot y
has the same dimension as the irreducible variety
\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we have that
P\cdot y
is dense in
\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and thus (using the fact it is locally closed) must also be open in
\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Finally, if
y,z\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}}}\right)\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
, then (ii) shows that
P\cdot y
and
P\cdot z
are both open and dense in
\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Thus
P\cdot y\cap P\cdot z\neq\emptyset
, and so (iii) follows from the fact that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{O}}}\right)\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
is
P
-closed.
∎

Therefore, we have proved all of the properties of the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction map
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\colon{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathfrak{l}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$L$}}\rightarrow{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathfrak{g}$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$G$}}
that were claimed in
Theorem

3
.

6.
Levi-Type Decomposition Classes

As already mentioned, much of the existing literature on decomposition classes has been developed under the assumption that
p\geq 0
is (at least) good for the connected reductive group
G
.
It is well-known (see
[
Spa82
, §1.2, Remark 1]
, for example) that this is equivalent to the assertion that
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y\subseteq G
is a Levi subgroup, for each semisimple
y\in\mathfrak{g}
(or equivalently
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a Levi subalgebra, for each semisimple
y\in\mathfrak{g}
).
Even outside of good characteristic, there exist semisimple
x\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x\subseteq G
is a Levi subgroup, and we shall see that the decomposition classes of such elements have certain nice properties.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose
y\in\mathfrak{g}
and let
L\subseteq G
be a Levi subgroup.

\mathrm{(i)}

If
L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y
, then
y
is semisimple.

\mathrm{(ii)}

L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y
if and only if
\mathfrak{l}=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y
, if and only if
y\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{[\dim\mathfrak{l}]}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y
.
If
T\subseteq L
is a maximal torus, then there exist
y_{0}\in\mathfrak{t}
and
y_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
(for each
\alpha\in\Phi
) such that
y=y_{0}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi}y_{\alpha}
.
Since
T\subseteq\C_{G}y
, and
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is a
T
-stable
direct sum decomposition, we have that
y_{\alpha}=t\cdot y_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in\Phi
and
t\in T
.
For each fixed
\alpha\in\Phi
, pick
t\in T\setminus\ker\alpha
, from which
y_{\alpha}=\alpha(t)y_{\alpha}
implies that
y_{\alpha}=0
.
Hence
y=y_{0}\in\mathfrak{t}
, which proves (i).
Moreover,
Lemma

2.7
(ii) implies that
\mathfrak{l}=\Lie\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y}}\right)=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathfrak{l}=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y
.
This implies that
y\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})\subseteq\mathfrak{t}
, and so
y
is semisimple.
Using
Lemma

2.7
(ii), we know that
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{y}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, and hence
L=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle T,\mathrm{U}_{\alpha}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \alpha\in\Phi_{y}}}\right\rangle
.
Then
Lemma

2.7
(i) implies that
L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y
, which proves the first equivalence in (ii).

For each
z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
, we have that
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}z
, and thus
\dim\mathfrak{l}\leq\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}z
; moreover,
\mathfrak{l}=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}z
if and only if
\dim\mathfrak{l}=\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}z
.
The second equivalence in (ii) then follows from the definition of
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{[\dim\mathfrak{l}]}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}z=\dim\mathfrak{l}}}\right\}
.
∎

Suppose
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is such that
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}\subseteq\mathfrak{l}
is a Levi subalgebra, and let
y\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x
.
Then there exists
g\in G
such that
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y_{\mathrm{s}}=g\cdot\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}
, and thus
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y_{\mathrm{s}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is also a Levi subalgebra.
It follows from
Lemma

6.1
(ii) that for any
x\sim y
, we have that
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}
is a Levi subgroup if and only if
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y_{\mathrm{s}}
is a Levi subgroup.
This leads us to the following important definition.

Definition 6.2
.

An element
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is called
Levi-type
if
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}\subseteq G
is a Levi subgroup.
A decomposition class is called
Levi-type
if any
(
equivalently, all
)
of its elements are Levi-type.
A decomposition variety is called
Levi-type
if it is the closure of a Levi-type decomposition class.

By
Lemma

6.1
(ii),
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is Levi-type if and only if
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a Levi subalgebra.
Moreover, it follows from
Lemma

5.1
(vi) that, if
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is Levi-type with
L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}
, then
\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}L\cdot x=G\cdot x
.
We observe that
p\geq 0
is good for
G
if and only if every element of
\mathfrak{g}
is Levi-type, if and only if every
G
-decomposition class is Levi-type, if and only if every
G
-decomposition variety is Levi-type.

6.1.
Stabiliser-Type Levi Subgroups

Clearly, a decomposition class is Levi-type if and only if any (equivalently, all) of its decomposition data are of the form
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
, where
L\subseteq G
is a Levi subgroup and
e_{0}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{l})
.
However, (in general) not every pair
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
consisting of a Levi subgroup and a nilpotent element
e_{0}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{l})
is a decomposition datum for a decomposition class.

Definition 6.3
.

A Levi subgroup
L\subseteq G
is called
stabiliser-type
if there exists
y\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y
.

It follows from
Lemma

6.1
that such
y\in\mathfrak{g}
are necessarily elements of
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{[\dim\mathfrak{l}]}
; moreover,
L
is stabiliser-type if and only if
\mathfrak{l}=\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y
for some
y\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{[\dim\mathfrak{l}]}
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Suppose
L\subseteq G
is a Levi subgroup, and
e_{0}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{l})
.

\mathrm{(i)}

L
is stabiliser-type if and only if
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{[\dim\mathfrak{l}]}\neq\emptyset
, if and only if
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{[\dim\mathfrak{l}]}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{G\mhyphen\mathrm{reg}}
, if and only if
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{[\dim\mathfrak{l}]}\subseteq\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
is open and dense.

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is a decomposition datum for some decomposition class if and only if
L
is stabiliser-type.

\mathrm{(iii)}

If
L
is stabiliser-type, then
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{G\mhyphen\mathrm{reg}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{y\in\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y=\mathfrak{l}}}\right\}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{y\in\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y=L}}\right\}
.

Proof.

Observe that the first equivalence in (i) is immediate from
Lemma

6.1
(ii).
Since
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
consists entirely of semisimple elements, we know that
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{G\mhyphen\mathrm{reg}}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}
, which equals
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{y\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}y\leq\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}z,\text{for\penalty\ all\penalty\ }z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})}}\right\}
.
Therefore, the second equivalence in (i) follows from the proof of
Lemma

6.1
(ii).
The final equivalence in (i) is then deduced from the fact that
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{G\mhyphen\mathrm{reg}}\subseteq\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
is an open and dense subset.

If
y\in\mathfrak{g}
is such that
L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}y
, then
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is a decomposition datum of the decomposition class
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(y+e_{0}}}\right)
.
Conversely, suppose that
\mathfrak{J}
is a decomposition class such that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is a decomposition datum of
\mathfrak{J}
.
For an arbitrary
x\in\mathfrak{J}
, consider the decomposition datum
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}};x_{\mathrm{n}}}}\right)
of
\mathfrak{J}
.
Then there exists
g\in G
such that
g\cdot\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}=L
and
g\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}=e_{0}
.
Therefore,
L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)\subseteq G
is stabiliser-type, which proves (ii).
Finally (iii) follows immediately from (i) alongside
Lemma

6.1
(ii).
∎

We shall use
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}
to mean
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{G\mhyphen\mathrm{reg}}
, whenever
L\subseteq G
is a Levi subgroup.
A natural question to ask is what conditions on
G
and the characteristic
p\geq 0
guarantee that every Levi subgroup
L\subseteq G
is stabiliser-type.

Lemma 6.5

(
[
Let05
, Lemma 2.6.13(i)]
)
.

Suppose
L\subseteq G
is a Levi subgroup, and
T\subseteq L
is a maximal torus with corresponding root system
\Phi
.
Assume
p\geq 0
is good for
G
and that
p
does not divide
\lvert\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\raisebox{0.81778pt}{$\mathrm{X}(T)$}\big/\raisebox{-0.81778pt}{$\mathbb{Z}\Phi$}}}}\right)_{\mathrm{tor}}\rvert
.
Then
L\subseteq G
is stabiliser-type.

Proof.

Following
[
Let05
, Definition 2.6.10]
, we see that Letellier refers to the elements
x\in\mathfrak{g}
with
L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x
as “
L
-regular elements in
\mathfrak{g}
” (we remark that we do not use this terminology in this paper, in order to not cause confusion with our definitions of
G
-regular and
\mathfrak{g}
-regular elements from §
2.1
).
Then
Lemma

6.1
(ii) implies that
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{[\dim\mathfrak{l}]}
is precisely the set of such elements, and so this result is a rephrasing of
[
Let05
, Lemma 2.6.13(i)]
.
∎

We note that the conditions in
Lemma

6.5
are not necessary for a given Levi subgroup to be stabiliser-type; for example,
G=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}0
is always a stabiliser-type Levi subgroup of itself, regardless of the characteristic
p\geq 0
.
An example of a Levi subgroup which is not stabiliser-type is
L=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&{a^{-1}}\end{pmatrix}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ a\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}}}\right\}\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{2}=G
, with
p=2
; here we have
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{[3]}
, and so
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{[\dim\mathfrak{l}]}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})_{[1]}=\emptyset
.
The complete classification of stabiliser-type Levi subgroups for simple type
\mathrm{A}
algebraic groups will be included in the author’s next paper.

We refer to a pair
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
consisting of a stabiliser-type Levi subgroup
L\subseteq G
, and a nilpotent element
e_{0}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{l})
, as a
Levi-type

G
-decomposition datum
.
It is clear that a decomposition class
\mathfrak{J}
is Levi-type if and only if any (equivalently, all) of its decomposition data are Levi-type.
If
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is a Levi-type decomposition datum, then it follows from
Theorem

2.10
(i) and
Lemma

6.4
(iii) that the corresponding Levi-type decomposition class can be written as
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)=G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)
.

6.2.
Nilpotent Decomposition Classes

Suppose
e_{0}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
is an arbitrary nilpotent element.
Recall from
Theorem

2.10
(v) and
Proposition

2.16
(ii), respectively, that its decomposition class is given by
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}e_{0}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})+G\cdot e_{0}
, and its decomposition variety is given by
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})+\overline{G\cdot e_{0}}
.

Definition 6.6
.

A
G
-decomposition class is called
nilpotent
if it is of the form
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}e_{0}
, for some nilpotent
e_{0}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Similarly, the closure of a nilpotent
G
-decomposition class is called a
nilpotent

G
-decomposition variety.

Let
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{N}}[G]
denote the set of all nilpotent
G
-decomposition classes.
Observe that nilpotent decomposition classes coincide with nilpotent orbits if and only if
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=0
.
Since
G=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}0
, any decomposition datum of a nilpotent decomposition class is of the form
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G;e_{0}}}\right)
, which immediately proves that all nilpotent decomposition classes are Levi-type.

The set of nilpotent orbits
{\raisebox{0.80002pt}{$\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})$}\big/\raisebox{-0.80002pt}{$G$}}
is often equipped with the closure order, defined such that
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\preceq\mathcal{O}
if and only if
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.
The following proposition demonstrates that this essentially coincides with the restriction of the closure order on
\mathfrak{D}[G]
to
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{N}}[G]
.

Proposition 6.7
.

Suppose
x,y\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Then
G\cdot x\subseteq\overline{G\cdot y}
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.

Proof.

If
G\cdot x\subseteq\overline{G\cdot y}
, then
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})+G\cdot x\subseteq\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})+\overline{G\cdot y}=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.
For the converse, suppose that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.
Since
x=x_{\mathrm{n}}\in\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
,
Proposition

2.16
(iii) implies that
x=x_{\mathrm{n}}\in\overline{G\cdot y}
, and so the result follows from the
G
-stability of
\overline{G\cdot y}
.
∎

Corollary 6.8
.

Suppose
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, and
y\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
.
If
x\in\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
, then
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x_{\mathrm{n}}
is a nilpotent decomposition class, and
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.

Proof.

Using
Proposition

2.16
(iii), we know that
x_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
, and
x_{\mathrm{n}}\in\overline{G\cdot y}
.
It follows from
Theorem

2.10
that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x_{\mathrm{n}}
, and hence is a nilpotent decomposition class.
Moreover,
x_{\mathrm{n}}\in\overline{G\cdot y}
implies that
G\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}\subseteq\overline{G\cdot y}
, and so the last part follows from
Proposition

6.7
.
∎

Since closures of nilpotent orbits are a finite union of nilpotent orbits, it follows from
Proposition

6.7
and
Corollary

6.8
that nilpotent decomposition varieties are finite unions of nilpotent decomposition classes.
We shall see in
Theorem

6.15
that (more generally) any Levi-type decomposition variety is a finite union of decomposition classes.

6.3.
Levi-Type Decomposition Varieties

We shall now build towards a description of Levi-type decomposition varieties that was previously only proved under stricter assumptions: that
G
is semisimple, and either
p=0

[
BK79
]
, or
G
is adjoint and
p\geq 0
is very good
[
Bro98
]
.
We note that the proof has a similar structure to the one found in
[
Bro98
, Lemma 3.5.1]
.

It follows from §
6.1
that each Levi-type decomposition variety can be expressed in the form
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=\penalty\overline{G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)}
, where
L\subseteq G
is a stabiliser-type Levi subgroup and
e_{0}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{l})
.
The following result, using
[
Let05
, Lemma 2.6.6]
, allows us to obtain a generalisation of
[
Bro98
, Lemma 3.5.1(i)]
to arbitrary characteristic.

Proposition 6.9
.

Suppose
P\subseteq G
is a parabolic subgroup,
L\subseteq P
is a stabiliser-type Levi factor, and
e_{0}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{l})
.
Let
\mu\colon U_{P}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
denote the restriction of the adjoint action
G\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
.

\mathrm{(i)}

\mu\colon U_{P}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)\rightarrow\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a bijective morphism of varieties.

\mathrm{(ii)}

Suppose we let
U_{P}
act on
U_{P}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)
via
h\cdot(g,z)\coloneqq(hg,z)
, and on
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
via the adjoint action.
Then
\mu\colon U_{P}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)\rightarrow\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an isomorphism of
U_{P}
-varieties.

Proof.

If
z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{l}
, then
z_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}
and
z_{\mathrm{n}}=e_{0}
, hence
Lemma

6.4
(iii) implies that
\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}z_{\mathrm{s}}=L
.
Therefore,
[
Let05
, Lemma 2.6.6]
shows that
\mu\mathord{\downarrow}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{P}\times\{z\}}}\right)}\colon U_{P}\times\{z\}\rightarrow z+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an isomorphism of varieties, and so the image of
\mu
is equal to
\bigcup_{z_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(z_{\mathrm{s}}+e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+\penalty e_{0}+\penalty\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Each
w\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
uniquely decomposes as
w=w_{\mathfrak{l}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(w-w_{\mathfrak{l}}}}\right)
with
w_{\mathfrak{l}}\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}
and
w-w_{\mathfrak{l}}\in\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Thus, the injectivity of
\mu\colon U_{P}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)\rightarrow\penalty\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
follows from the injectivity of
\mu\mathord{\downarrow}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{P}\times\{w_{\mathfrak{l}}\}}}\right)}\colon U_{P}\times\{w_{\mathfrak{l}}\}\rightarrow w_{\mathfrak{l}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since it is the restriction of the adjoint action, we have that
\mu\colon U_{P}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)\rightarrow\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a bijective morphism of varieties, completing the proof of (i).

With the
U_{P}
-actions described in the statement of (ii), both
U_{P}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)
and
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+\penalty e_{0}+\penalty\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
are
U_{P}
-varieties.
Moreover,
\mu\colon U_{P}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)\rightarrow\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is clearly
U_{P}
-equivariant, and thus it remains to prove that it is an isomorphism of varieties.
Using (i) and
[
Spr98
, Theorem 5.3.2(iii)]
, it suffices to prove that, for some
(g,z)\in U_{P}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)
, the corresponding differential
\mathrm{d}\mu_{(g,z)}\colon\mathcal{T}_{(g,z)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{P}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)}}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{\mu(g,z)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
is a bijection between the relevant tangent spaces.

Fix an arbitrary
z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}
.
By considering a suitable immersive representation of
G
, the differential
\mathrm{d}\mu_{(1,z)}
can be interpreted as the map
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})\rightarrow\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
defined by
(x,y)\mapsto y+[x,z]
.
Therefore, it remains to show that
x\mapsto[x,z]
is a bijection
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
However, this is just the differential (at the identity) of the
U_{P}
-orbit map
U_{P}\rightarrow U_{P}\cdot z
.
Since
z_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}
,
[
Let05
, Lemma 2.6.6]
shows the
U_{P}
-orbit map is an isomorphism, and thus its differential is bijective.
∎

Suppose
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is a Levi-type decomposition datum, and
P\in\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
.
It follows from
Proposition

6.9
(i) that
U_{P}\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since
P=U_{P}\rtimes L
, we have that
P\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)=L\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
.
Then, using the fact that both
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}
and
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
are
L
-stable, it follows that
P\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+L\cdot e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Lemma 6.10
.

Suppose
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is a Levi-type decomposition datum, and
P\in\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
.
Then
\overline{P\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a direct sum of vector spaces, and
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+L\cdot e_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{l}
, we know that
\overline{\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+L\cdot e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\overline{\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+L\cdot e_{0}}+\overline{\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\vphantom{\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}}}
.
Then
Lemma

2.15
implies that
\overline{\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+L\cdot e_{0}}=\overline{\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}}+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}\vphantom{\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}}}
.
Since
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is closed, and
\overline{\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
, then the result follows from
P\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+L\cdot e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
∎

Therefore,
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is closed and
P
-stable.
We can thus prove the following generalisation of
[
Bro98
, Lemma 3.5.1(ii)]
, which provides a description of Levi-type decomposition varieties in arbitrary characteristic.

Theorem 6.11
.

Suppose
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is a Levi-type decomposition datum, and
P\in\mathfrak{P}(G,L)
.
Then
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
.

Proof.

We have seen in
Lemma

6.10
that
\overline{P\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is closed and
P
-stable.
Therefore,
Lemma

2.14
implies that
G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is also closed.
Since
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)=G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)\subseteq G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
, it follows that
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}\subseteq G\cdot\penalty\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
.

Conversely, we have that
P\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)\subseteq\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
, and thus
G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\overline{P\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{\mathrm{reg}}+e_{0}}}\right)}}}\right)\subseteq G\cdot\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.
Therefore,
G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
, which proves the required equality.
∎

This proves
Theorem

4
(i) from the introduction.
The following result is just a rephrasing of
Theorem

6.11
, and thus follows immediately.

Corollary 6.12
.

Suppose
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is Levi-type, and
P\in\mathfrak{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G,\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)
.
Then
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=G\cdot\penalty\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{\mathrm{s}}+\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)
.

6.4.
Decomposition Varieties as Unions of Decomposition Classes

Now that we have the description of Levi-type decomposition varieties provided by
Theorem

6.11
, we shall prove that they are unions of decomposition classes.
Firstly, we need some results regarding conjugacy and the Jordan decomposition.

Lemma 6.13
.

Suppose
T\subseteq G
is a maximal torus, and
B\in\mathfrak{P}(G,T)
.
If
y\in\mathfrak{t}
and
x\in y+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}
, then
x_{\mathrm{s}}\in B\cdot y
.

Proof.

Firstly,
[
Let05
, Lemma 2.6.6]
implies that
U_{B}\cdot y\subseteq y+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}
, and observe that
B=U_{B}\rtimes T
.
Since
T
acts trivially on
\mathfrak{t}
, and
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is
T
-stable, we have that
B\cdot y=U_{B}\cdot y\subseteq y+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}
, and consequently
y+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is
B
-stable.

Since
x_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathfrak{b}
is semisimple, there exists
b\in B
such that
b\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathfrak{t}
.
Then
b\cdot x\in b\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(y+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}}}\right)=y+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}
.
Finally, since
b\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{b})=\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}
, it follows from the uniqueness of the direct sum decomposition (and
b\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}+b\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}\in y+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}
) that
b\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}=y
.
∎

Corollary 6.14
.

Suppose
P\subseteq G
is a parabolic subgroup, with Levi factor
L\subseteq P
, and let
y\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
.
If
x\in y+\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{l})+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then
x_{\mathrm{s}}\in P\cdot y
.

Proof.

Fix a maximal torus
T\subseteq L
, and a Borel subgroup
B^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{P}(L,T)
.
Then
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{l})=L\cdot\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}
, and so there exists
h\in L
such that
h\cdot x\in y+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Observe that
B=B^{\prime}U_{P}\in\mathfrak{P}(G,T)
, and that
\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since
y\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})\subseteq\mathfrak{t}
, and
h\cdot x\in y+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{b}}
,
Lemma

6.13
implies that
h\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}\in B\cdot y
.
The result then follows from the fact that
h\in L\subseteq P
and
B\subseteq P
.
∎

Theorem 6.15
.

Suppose
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)\in\mathfrak{D}[G]
is a Levi-type decomposition class, and let
x\in\penalty\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.
Then
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}{x}\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.

Proof.

Since decomposition classes (and decomposition varieties) are
G
-stable, we can (by
Theorem

6.11
) assume (without loss of generality) that
x\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Using
Corollary

6.14
, there exists
h\in P
such that
h\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
.
Since
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
P
-stable (by
Lemma

6.10
),
h\cdot x\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and thus
h\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}=h\cdot x-h\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Observe that
h\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
implies that
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(h\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)
, and hence
\fd_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(h\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)\subseteq\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{\mathfrak{d}}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(h\cdot x_{\mathrm{s}}}}\right)+h\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}\subseteq\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})+\overline{L\cdot e_{0}}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
, and so
Theorem

2.10
(i) implies that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(h\cdot x}}\right)\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.
∎

Therefore, a Levi-type decomposition variety coincides with the union of the decomposition classes that it intersects, which proves
Theorem

4
(ii).
It follows immediately that, for a Levi-type decomposition class
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
, both
{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}}^{\mathrm{reg}}
and
{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}}^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}
are also unions of decomposition classes – where we have used
Lemma

2.4
(iv), alongside
Proposition

2.5
.
This generalises statements found in
[
Bor81
, §3.5]
(for characteristic
0
) and
[
Amb25
, §3.1]
(for good characteristic).

6.5.
Strongly-Levi-Type Decomposition Classes

If
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is a Levi-type decomposition datum, we define
\mathfrak{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[G,L;e_{0}}}\right]\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathfrak{J}\in\mathfrak{D}[G]\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \mathfrak{J}\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}}}\right\}
.
Then
Theorem

6.15
implies that
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
is the finite disjoint union of the decomposition classes in
\mathfrak{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[G,L;e_{0}}}\right]
.
It follows from
Corollary

2.12
that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is the unique decomposition class in
\mathfrak{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[G,L;e_{0}}}\right]
of maximal dimension.

Similarly, if
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is Levi-type, then we define
\mathfrak{D}[G,x]\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathfrak{J}\in\mathfrak{D}[G]\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \mathfrak{J}\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}}}\right\}
, and observe that
\mathfrak{D}[G,x]=\mathfrak{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[G,\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}};x_{\mathrm{n}}}}\right]
.
We can now introduce the following strengthening of
Definition

6.2
.

Definition 6.16
.

An element
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is
strongly-Levi-type
if each
y\in\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
is Levi-type; we then also refer to
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x
and
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
as
strongly-Levi-type
.

In other words, a Levi-type decomposition class
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is strongly-Levi-type if
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
is a (finite disjoint) union of Levi-type decomposition classes.
Once again,
p\geq 0
is good for
G
if and only if every element of
\mathfrak{g}
is strongly-Levi-type, if and only if every
G
-decomposition class is strongly-Levi-type, if and only if every
G
-decomposition variety is strongly-Levi-type.

Proposition 6.17
.

Suppose
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is a strongly-Levi-type decomposition class, and
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M;e_{1}}}\right)\in\mathfrak{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[G,L;e_{0}}}\right]
.

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathfrak{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[G,M;e_{1}}}\right]\subseteq\mathfrak{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[G,L;e_{0}}}\right]
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M;e_{1}}}\right)
is also strongly-Levi-type.

\mathrm{(iii)}

Every nilpotent decomposition class is strongly-Levi-type.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{J}\in\mathfrak{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[G,M;e_{1}}}\right]
, then
\mathfrak{J}\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
.
Since
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M;e_{1}}}\right)\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
, we have that
\mathfrak{J}\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
, from which (i) follows.
Then (ii) is immediate from (i) and
Definition

6.16
.

Now suppose that
x=x_{\mathrm{n}}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Then
Corollary

6.8
implies that
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
is a finite union of nilpotent decomposition classes, and thus (iii) follows from the fact that all nilpotent decomposition classes are Levi-type.
∎

We can also rephrase
Proposition

6.17
(ii) as follows: if
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is strongly-Levi-type and
y\in\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
, then
y\in\mathfrak{g}
is also strongly-Levi-type.

Theorem 6.18
.

If
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)
is a strongly-Levi-type decomposition class, then
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is locally closed.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{D}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[G,L;e_{0}}}\right]=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right),\mathfrak{J}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{J}_{r}}}\right\}
is a labelling of the distinct decomposition classes contained in
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
, and observe that
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)\cup\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq r}\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}
.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that there exists
x\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)\cap\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}
, for some
1\leq j\leq r
.
Since
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x
is strongly-Levi-type, we know that
\mathfrak{J}_{j}
is Levi-type, and thus
Theorem

6.15
implies that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}x\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}
.
However,
\mathfrak{J}_{j}\subseteq\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}
, so
Proposition

2.11
(ii) implies that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)=\mathfrak{J}_{j}
, which is a contradiction.

Therefore, if we let
Y=\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq r}\overline{\mathfrak{J}_{j}}
, we have
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}=\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)\sqcup Y
.
It follows that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathfrak{g}\setminus Y}}\right)
is the intersection of a closed set and an open set, which means
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is locally closed.
∎

Theorem

2.10
(iii) established that every decomposition class is constructible, and hence a finite union of locally closed sets.
Therefore,
Theorem

6.18
strengthens this result, for the case of strongly-Levi-type decomposition classes.
By the above discussion, we have the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 6.19
.

If
p\geq 0
is good for
G
, then every
G
-decomposition class is locally closed.

This result is already known, but the only proofs we were able to find in the literature required characteristic
0
.
We note that it is currently an open problem as to whether non-strongly-Levi-type decomposition classes are locally closed.

We next generalise the results found in

Fix a Levi subgroup

Theorem 6.20
.

If

\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{z}\subseteq\overline{G\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(z+L\cdot e_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\right)\vphantom{\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}L\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(z+e_{0}}}\right)}}=\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}

This proves

(iii) from the introduction, which was first stated (for characteristic

Theorem 6.21
.

If
\operatorname{\mathfrak{J}}_{G}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L;e_{0}}}\right)\in\mathfrak{D}[G]

{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}}^{\mathrm{reg}}=\bigcup\limits_{z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})}\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}L\cdot\penalty\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(z+e_{0}}}\right)

Fix a parabolic

6.20

This proves the final part of

from the introduction, which was first stated (for characteristic

6.7.
Levi-Type Sheets

We can now link some of the results from this section to sheets, as introduced in
Definition

4.1
.
Recall from
Proposition

4.4
that each sheet
S\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
contains a unique dense decomposition class
\mathfrak{D}_{S}
.

Definition 6.23
.

A sheet
S
is called
Levi-type
if
\mathfrak{D}_{S}
is a Levi-type decomposition class.

It follows from our earlier observations that, if we assume good characteristic, then every sheet is Levi-type.
Recall from
Lemma

4.3
(ii) that every sheet is
G
-stable.
Therefore, for any sheet
S
, we have that
S\cap\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
is a finite (possibly empty) union of nilpotent orbits.

Corollary 6.24
.

Suppose
S\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a Levi-type sheet.

\mathrm{(i)}

S
is a union of decomposition classes.

\mathrm{(ii)}

If
S
is a stabiliser sheet, then
S
contains a unique nilpotent orbit.

Proof.

As previously observed,
Theorem

6.15
implies that (for any Levi-type decomposition class
\mathfrak{J}
), both
\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{\mathrm{reg}}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathrm{reg}}
are unions of decomposition classes.
Applying this to
\mathfrak{D}_{S}
shows that (i) follows from
Corollary

4.5
(ii).

Now suppose that
S
is a Levi-type stabiliser sheet, and let
x\in\mathfrak{D}_{S}
, with
L=\operatorname{\mathrm{C}}^{\,\circ}_{G}x_{\mathrm{s}}
.
Then
Corollary

4.5
(ii)(a) implies that
S={\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}}^{\mathrm{reg}}
.
Therefore,
Corollary

6.22
(i) shows that
S\cap\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})=\Ind_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}L\cdot x_{\mathrm{n}}
is a single (nilpotent)

-orbit, as required.
∎

Recall that, in order to prove
Theorem

4.8
, we required an additional assumption on the sheets of a level set
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.
In particular, we required that every sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
was a union of decomposition classes.
Since

6.24
(i) shows that this holds if all the sheets of

are Levi-type sheets, we can restate a version of

4.8
as follows.

Corollary 6.25
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is a level set, and assume that every sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
is Levi-type.
Then a decomposition class
\mathfrak{J}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\langle m\rangle}[G]
coincides with a sheet of
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
if and only if it is isolated in
\mathfrak{g}_{\langle m\rangle}
.

Moreover, if the characteristic is good for
G
, then this assumption always holds (since in that case, every sheet is Levi-type).
We shall conclude this paper by drawing attention to connections between
Corollary

6.24
(ii) and the following conjecture of Spaltenstein, which we have reworded slightly in line with the new terminology introduced in
Definition

4.1
.

Conjecture 6.26

(
[
Spa82
, §1.2]
)
.

For any connected reductive algebraic group
G

(
over an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic
)
, every stabiliser sheet of
\mathfrak{g}
contains exactly one nilpotent orbit.

In
[
Spa82
, §1.2(c)]
, Spaltenstein establishes that every stabiliser sheet contains at least one nilpotent orbit, and observe that
[
BK79
]
(although working in characteristic
0
) essentially prove
Conjecture

6.26
when the characteristic
p\geq 0
is good for
G
.
Moreover,
Spaltenstein
proves in
[
Spa82
, Theorem 2.8]
that
Conjecture

6.26
holds when
G
has no simple components of exceptional type.
In later work, they prove that
Conjecture

6.26
is also true when
G
is a simple algebraic group of either type
\mathrm{E}_{6}

[
Spa83
, §7, Corollary 3]
, or type
\mathrm{F}_{4}
when

[
Spa84
, §5, Theorem]
.

However, it is noted in
[
PS18
, §3.1]
that

6.26
remains open for certain bad characteristics.
It follows from

6.24
(ii) that

6.26
is at least true for Levi-type stabiliser sheets (regardless of characteristic).

We remark that
Conjecture

6.26
is false (in general) for centraliser sheets, and it suffices to show there exist non-empty centraliser level sets that contain no nilpotent orbits.

For example, consider
G=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
with
p=2
, and let
\pi\colon\mathrm{GL}_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
be the canonical quotient homomorphism.
We claim that
\mathfrak{g}_{[1]}\neq\emptyset
contains no nilpotent orbits.

\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
only consists of two nilpotent
G
-orbits: the zero orbit, and
G\cdot x
, where
x=\mathrm{d}\pi\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
.
Simple computation reveals that
\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathrm{d}\pi\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&a\end{pmatrix}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ a,b,c\in\mathbb{K}}}\right\}
, and thus
\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}x=2
, whereas
\dim\fc_{\mathfrak{g}}0=3
.
Similar computation shows that
\mathrm{d}\pi\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{g}_{[1]}
, thus proving our claim.

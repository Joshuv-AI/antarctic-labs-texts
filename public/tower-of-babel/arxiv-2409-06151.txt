Mathematical foundations of phonons in incommensurate materials
Michael Hott, Alexander B. Watson, Mitchell Luskin

Abstract
The physical observation that it is energetically favorable for interacting particles to arrange in a periodic fashion remains a largely open problem, referred to as the crystallization conjecture . In specific models, e.g., coming from atomic pair potentials, periodic configurations can be shown to be stable under, both, global ℓ 2 \ell^{2} or local perturbations. The situation changes when one studies aperiodic media. The specific class of aperiodic media we are interested in arise from taking two 2D periodic crystals and stacking them parallel at a relative twist. We study different notions of stability for such systems. The goal of our analysis is to provide phonons in such systems with meaning. In periodic media, phonons are generalized eigenvectors for a stability operator acting on ℓ 2 \ell^{2} , coming from a mechanical energy. Using ideas rigorously established for the 1D Frenkel-Kontorova model, we assume that we can parametrize minimizing lattice deformations w.r.t. local perturbations via continuous stacking-periodic functions, see [ 9 ] , for which we previously derived a continuous energy density functional [ 18 ] . Such (continuous) energy densities are analytically and computationally much better accessible compared to discrete energy functionals. In order to pass to an ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -based energy functional, we also study the offset energy w.r.t. given lattice deformations, under ℓ 1 \ell^{1} -perturbations. Our findings show that, in the case of an undeformed bilayer heterostructure, while the energy density can be shown to be stable under the assumption of stability of individual layers, the offset energy fails to be stable in the case of twisted bilayer graphene. We then establish conditions for stability and instability of the offset energy w.r.t. the relaxed lattice. Finally, we show that, in the case of incommensurate bilayer homostructures, i.e., two equal layers, if we choose minimizing deformations according to the global energy density above, the offset energy is stable in the limit of zero twist angle. Consequently, in this case, one can then define phonons as generalized eigenvectors w.r.t. the stability operator associated with the offset energy.

Introduction

Generally, phonons are understood as generalized eigenvectors of a stability operator acting on
\ell^{2}
, associated with a mechanical energy. More precisely, expanding a mechanical energy functional w.r.t. equilibrium positions
\{R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{j}\}_{j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
, perturbed by lattice deformations
\delta u:\{R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{j}\}_{j\in{\mathcal{J}}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle E(\{R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{j}+\delta u(R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{j})\}_{j\in{\mathcal{J}}})\,=\,E(\{R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{j}\}_{j\in{\mathcal{J}}})\,+\,\delta^{2}E(\{R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{j}\}_{j\in{\mathcal{J}}})[\delta u,\delta u]\,+\,\ldots\,,

we define phonons as generalized eigenvectors of the stability operator
\delta^{2}E(\{R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{j}\}_{j\in{\mathcal{J}}})
acting on
\ell^{2}
. Crucially, this requires to define an energy functional yielding a well-defined stability operator acting on
\ell^{2}
. Notice that
\delta^{2}E(\{R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{j}\}_{j\in{\mathcal{J}}})
is necessarily positive (semi-)definite.

In the case of periodic media described by a (multi)lattice, the unperturbed lattice positions can be shown to be stable under global
\ell^{2}
or local perturbations. An energy functional, in the simplest case, is given by the sum of harmonic springs. Then the associated phonon Hamiltonian can be computed as the root of the second variation of the mechanical energy. This is due to the fact that, in this case, we model lattice vibrations via harmonic oscillators at every lattice position. Due to the lattice periodicity, Bloch-Floquet theory enables us to analyze the spectral properties of the associated phonons. More precisely, in this case, we define the
phonon Hamiltonian
as the (square root of) the stability operator associated with the energy functional.

In general aperiodic media, however, the story is not as straight-forward. First, we need to study a mechanical energy to obtain a relaxed lattice configuration. This relaxed lattice configuration will generally not be periodic, so Bloch-Floquet theory is not applicable. Moreover, while it is possible to again model the mechanical energy via harmonic springs, analytically or computationally determining the associated energy minimum is generally challenging. Our goal is thus twofold:

1.

Determine an analytically/computationally accessible mechanical energy to compute an energy minimizer.

2.

Find an appropriate phonon model that allows us to treat phonons as
\ell^{2}
-waves.

In order to analyze this question, we shall turn to incommensurate heterostructures. These arise when two or more two-dimensional crystal layers are stacked on top of each other at a relative twist, forming a quasiperiodic medium. Due to the periodicity of the individual layers, quasiperiodic macroscopic patterns, referred to as
moiré pattern
, emerge. Such systems have gained a lot of attention, when it turned out that, at certain small twist angles, known as
magic angles
, special electronic properties are unlocked. More precisely, Bistritzer and MacDonald
[
5
]
derived a reduced model for twisted bilayer graphene that predicted the occurrence of nearly flat electronic bands at a twist angle of
\sim 1.1^{\circ}
, which in turn yields a highly localized density of states. As a consequence of the flat dispersion, the interaction between electrons become dominant and various behavior typical for strongly correlated systems has been observed to emerge, see
[
6
]
. One of the mysterious phases is the superconducting phase, for it occurs at a very low electron density compared to the transition temperature, which is rather typical for unconventional superconductors. However, the mechanism underlying the superconducting phase remains a puzzle.

Spectroscopic measurements
[
31
]
seem to hint at unconventional superconductivity, while electronic transport
[
24
]
shows independence of the critical temperature w.r.t. Coulomb screening. This, in turn, suggests that phonons may play a critical role in understanding superconductivity.

One of the main obstacles in understanding the role of phonons, is the large number of – even intersecting – phonon bands. Physicists have thus come up with certain selection rules to pick only a few bands and projected them onto a low-energy regime, similar as previously done for electrons by Bistritzer and MacDonald, see
[
22
,
25
]
. In addition, these computations cutoff the phonon energy at zero and ignore possible phonon bands with negative dispersion, see, e.g.,
[
26
,
39
]
. Our goal is to provide a mathematical foundation for phonons in incommensurate multilayer structures, and to study whether or not this negative dispersion is a model relic or if it could be avoided by adjusting the model.

In order to find an energy functional, we can generally study (at least) two models

1.

Minimize a
global
energy w.r.t.
\ell^{2}
-perturbations.

2.

Minimize a
local
energy in any finite subset of the medium.

The first approach has the advantage that we would like to model phonons as
\ell^{2}
-normalized waves propagating through an aperiodic medium. However, the main disadvantage here is that, in order to construct a relaxed configuration, one typically constrains the problem to a supercell, corresponding, e.g., to a moiré cell, and chooses, e.g., periodic boundary conditions, see
[
13
,
14
,
38
]
. This is due to the fact that displacement functions are parametrized by their discrete lattice positions and continuum approximations that are commonly used for periodic systems, such as the Cauchy-Born approximation, are not accessible to the generic aperiodicity. Computations over large supercells are challenging, as they scale with
O_{\theta\to 0}(\frac{1}{\theta^{2}})
for twist angles
\theta
.

In order to minimize local energies in a quasiperiodic medium, Aubry and Le Daeron
[
1
]
studied the Frenkel-Kontorova model. In this model, lattice displacements on
{\mathbb{Z}}
are modeled via harmonic springs, and an external incommensurate potential is added. Then, under the assumption of an additonal recurrence condition, it was shown that a minimizer can be parametrized by a periodic function with the same periodicity as the external potential. This
hull
function, see also
[
3
,
4
,
2
]
, is either continuous, when the external potential is very weak, or discontinuous w.r.t. a Cantorus when the external potential is very strong. Analogous results were obtained for 1D rippling chains
[
10
]
by applying Aubry-Mather theory.

Motivated by these 1D results, Cazeaux, Massatt and one of the authors
[
9
]
, see also
[
7
]
, developed an energy density for continuous functions that are periodic w.r.t. to the local
stacking configuration
. We shall note that establishing a two- and higher-dimensional Aubry-Mather theory remains an open problem. In particular, this approach introduces a
third
model for an energy functional:

1.

Minimize an energy density w.r.t.
stacking-periodic
displacements.

From the previous discussion, we have that such a model replaces the second model above in the case of the 1D Frenkel-Kontorova model, while, in 2D, it is a distinct model.

The main advantage of this approach is that, instead of sampling over supercells, it suffices to sample over stacking configurations, making the minimization problem analytically and computationally more accessible; the predicted results are in agreement with the experimental observations
[
8
]
. However, since this energy functional is derived for energy minimizers only, and since these are assumed to be periodic, and thus non-decaying in
\ell^{2}
, we cannot treat phonons in the same framework.

In order to pass from a model based on stacking-periodic perturbations to
\ell^{2}
-perturbations, we employ an idea pursued in
[
17
]
. There, a theory is developed to study energy minimizers for interacting Dirac particles. They start by constructing a
translation-invariant
minimizer in the absence of an electric background field for which the energy
density
is stable w.r.t.
translation-invariant perturbations
. Using this minimizer as a reference state, they then consider the
offset energy
and minimize it w.r.t. Hilbert-Schmidt perturbations. These two minimizers are shown to coincide.

In this work, we adopt these ideas in the following way: We start by recalling results established
[
18
]
, where we generalized the ideas in
[
9
]
to obtain an atomistic energy based on displacements that are
periodic
w.r.t. the local stacking configuration. Crucially, this energy functional is obtained via a thermodynamic limit, analogously to
[
17
]

(1.2)

e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}({\bf u})\,:=\,\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{E\big|_{B_{r}}({\bf u})}{|B_{r}|}\,.

We also establish conditions on the many-body potentials that allow us to employ the results in
[
18
]
. For more details, we refer to Section
2.3
for the assumptions on the potentials, and to Section
3
to show well-definedness of the energy functional.

In Section
4
, we continue to prove the existence a locally unique minimizer
{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}
of
e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
which is stable w.r.t. perturbations that are periodic w.r.t. the local environment. This minimizer lets us compute the
offset energy
w.r.t. the total energy of
{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}

(1.3)

\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}({\bm{\delta}{\bf u}})\,:=\,\lim_{r\to\infty}\Big(E\big|_{B_{r}}({\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}+{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}})-E\big|_{B_{r}}({\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}})\Big)\,.

Definition of phonon Hamiltonian

A natural question to ask is whether
{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}
is still a minimizer for the offset energy, i.e., whether
0
is a minimizer of
\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}
. Assuming sufficient regularity of the involved potentials, expanding in orders of
{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}}
yields that an appropriate space to study
\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
is given by
\ell^{1}
, see
2.5
below.

The goal of this work is thus to study when the minimization problem associated with
e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
is consistent with that associated with
\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}
. When it is, we can rigorously pass from the configuration space framework to the
\ell^{2}
-phonon framework, and the phonon Hamiltonian is defined as

(1.4)

H_{\mathrm{ph}}:=\sqrt{\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}({\bf 0})}:\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}\,.

As we will see, we cannot employ standard perturbative methods and, instead, will provide sufficient criteria to ensure or exclude phonon stability. These criteria need to be numerically verified.

We prove in Section
2.5
that

(1.5)

\delta^{\bar{W}_{\mathrm{per}}^{1,2}}e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}({\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}})\,=\,0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\delta^{\ell^{1}}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}({\bf 0})\,=\,0\,,

i.e., that a critical point of
e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}
under moiré-periodic perturbations remains a critical point of
\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
under
\ell^{1}
-perturbations. In particular, this means that
\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}({\bf 0}):(\ell^{2})^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is well-defined. Equivalently, by duality,
\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}({\bf 0})\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}
is well-defined. We refer to the Section
2
for the chosen notation, and to the respective sections for the precise statements.

The next question thus is whether stability of
e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
is inherited by
\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}
. To answer this question, we start in Section
5
by proving that, for certain choices of interlayer pair potentials

(1.6)

\delta^{2}e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}({\bf 0})\,\geq\,\kappa\,>\,0\quad\wedge\quad\delta^{2}\big|_{{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}}={\bf 0}}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{\bf 0}({\bm{\delta}{\bf u}})\,<\,0\,.

This means that the undeformed union of lattices is stable under periodic perturbations, while it is not under
\ell^{2}
-perturbations. It matches the intuition that we expect the undeformed lattice not to be stable. The condition
\delta^{2}e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}({\bf 0})\geq\kappa
is typically referred to as
stability of the lattice
. In the case of a single layer, this has been shown to be a necessary condition for stability of minimizers, see
[
33
, Appendix C]
. Including the relaxed lattice positions, we prove that, in the case of pair interlayer potentials
v
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\inf_{\|{\bm{\delta}{\bf v}}\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\delta^{2}\big|_{{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}}={\bf 0}}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}({\bm{\delta}{\bf u}})[{\bm{\delta}{\bf v}},{\bm{\delta}{\bf v}}]\,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\inf_{a\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\ \partial_{a}^{2}v\big(x+u^{\mathrm{(eq)}}_{2}(A_{\mathcal{M}}A_{1}^{-1}x)-u^{\mathrm{(eq)}}_{1}(A_{\mathcal{M}}A_{2}^{-1}x)\big)\,.

Thus, a sufficient condition for instability is a negative r.h.s. This instability criterion stems from constructing an approximate minimizer for which the monolayer phonon energy vanishes while the interlayer phonon energy is minimized.

Conversely, a sufficient condition for phonon stability is given by

(1.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{R_{1}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{1}}D^{2}v(x-R_{1}+u^{\mathrm{(eq)}}_{2}(A_{\mathcal{M}}A_{1}^{-1}x)-u^{\mathrm{(eq)}}_{1}(R_{1}+A_{\mathcal{M}}A_{2}^{-1}x))\,\geq\,0

for all
x\in\Gamma_{1}
, in the sense of quadratic forms. Here,
{\mathcal{R}}_{1}
refers to the lattice of layer 1, and
\Gamma_{1}
is the corresponding unit cell. More precisely, we show in Section
5
that this condition implies positive (semi-)definiteness of
\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}
. While we explain the ideas only for interlayer pair potentials, we believe that one can generalize them to, more general, empirical many-body potentials which have become computationally more accessible and which promise to yield yield highly accurate calculations, thanks to the advances made in machine-learned empirical many-body, see, e.g.,
[
11
,
28
,
36
]
.

Relating the present energy functional to an appropriate Allen-Cahn functional, we argue in Section
6
that, for a purely twisted bilayer system for which the layer coupling is mediated through a pair potential
v
, at (twist angles)
\to 0
, the Allen-Cahn minimizers converges to the potential wells of the misfit energy
\Phi_{0}(x):=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\frac{v(A(x-n))}{\det A}
. For the Allen-Cahn theory to be applicable, we need to require that at the potential wells
x_{\mathrm{AB}}
,
x_{\mathrm{BA}}
, we have that

(1.10)

\displaystyle D^{2}\Phi_{0}(x_{\mathrm{BA}/\mathrm{AB}})\,>\,0\,.

This, in turn, means that we need to establish strict inequality in (
1.9

1.10
) for 2D pair potentials for graphene, at the equilibrium distance
d_{\mathrm{BG}}=3.35\mbox{\ \AA}
between the layers. Our choices of pair potentials are Lennard-Jones, Morse and Kolmogorov-Crespi potentials

, see (

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}(u_{j})\,:=\,\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{j}|}\sum_{\alpha_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{j}}\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\hskip-4.30554pt\mathrm{d}x\ V^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\big(\Delta_{j}[u_{j}](x,\alpha_{j})\big)\,,

which we will prove to coincide with
\lim_{N\to\infty}e^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j,N,\gamma_{j}}(u_{j})
.
In this context, it is useful to also abbreviate the site potential density

(2.24)

\displaystyle\phi^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}[u_{j}]:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}},\,x\mapsto\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{j}|}\sum_{\alpha_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{j}}V^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\big(\Delta_{j}[u_{j}](x,\alpha_{j})\big)\,,

which, in turn, allows us to write

(2.25)

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}(u_{j})\,=\,\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\hskip-4.30554pt\mathrm{d}x\ \phi^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}[u_{j}](x)\,.

Similarly, we define the offset site potential

(2.26)

\displaystyle\Delta\Phi^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}[\delta u_{j};u_{j}]\,:=\,|\Gamma_{j}|\big(\phi^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}[u_{j}+\delta u_{j}]-\phi^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}[u_{j}]\big)

(2.27)

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{\alpha_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{j}}\Big(V^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\big(\Delta_{j}[u_{j}+\delta u_{j}](\cdot,\alpha_{j})\big)-V^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\big(\Delta_{j}[u_{j}](\cdot,\alpha_{j})\big)\Big)\,,

and the
offset energy

(2.28)

\displaystyle\Delta E^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j;u_{j}}(\delta u_{j})\,:=\,\sum_{R_{j}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{j}}\Delta\Phi^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}[\delta u_{j};u_{j}](R_{j})\,.

2.2.2
Interlayer coupling

Definition 2.2
.

A function

(2.29)

V\,:\,({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{\Omega_{j}}\to{\mathbb{R}}

is
translation-invariant
iff for any
(a_{\omega_{j}})_{\omega_{j}\in\Omega_{j}}\in({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{\Omega_{j}}
and any
R_{j}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{j}
, we have that

(2.30)

V\big((a_{\omega_{j}+R_{j}})_{\omega_{j}\in\Omega_{j}}\big)\,=\,V\big((a_{\omega_{j}})_{\omega_{j}\in\Omega_{j}}\big)\,.

In addition,
V
is
even
iff
V(x)=V(-x)
.

Let

(2.31)

V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\,:\,({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{\Omega_{3-j}}\to{\mathbb{R}}\,,

with
\alpha_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{j}
be even and translation-invariant. As a special instance, let
v_{{\bm{\alpha}}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, with
\alpha_{k}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
,
k=1,2
,
{\bm{\alpha}}:=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
, denote an even pair potential, and consider

(2.32)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\big((a_{\omega_{3-j}})_{\omega_{3-j}\in\Omega_{3-j}}\big)\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\omega_{3-j}\in\Omega_{3-j}}v_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(a_{\omega_{3-j}})\,.\end{split}

Such interatomic pair potentials
v_{\bm{\alpha}}
have been constructed using DFT, e.g., in
[
23
,
27
,
35
]
.

The fact that we are using the ordered index
{\bm{\alpha}}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
to label pair potentials, as opposed to, e.g.,
(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{3-j})
, is to indicate that pair interactions only depend on the pair of involved atoms, as opposed to the order of the pair. Whenever there is a
\alpha_{j}
or
\alpha_{3-j}
on the LHS of an equation, and
\alpha_{1}
or
\alpha_{2}
on the RHS, the corresponding equation is to be read individually for
j=1
or
j=2
, to avoid confusion.

We abbreviate the
(total) finite difference stencils

(2.33)

\displaystyle\Delta_{j,3-j}[{\bf u}](x,\alpha_{j})\,

\displaystyle:=\,\big(Y_{3-j}(\omega_{3-j}+A_{3-j}A_{j}^{-1}x+\gamma_{3-j})-Y_{j}(x+\gamma_{j},\alpha_{j})\big)_{\omega_{3-j}\in\Omega_{3-j}}\,.

With that, we define the truncated interlayer coupling energy density of the
j
-th layer

(2.34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,N,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bf u})\,:=\,\frac{|{\mathcal{A}}_{j}|}{|\Gamma_{j}||\Omega_{j}(N)|}\sum_{\omega_{j}\in\Omega_{j}(N)}V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\big(\Delta_{j,3-j}[{\bf u}](\omega_{j})\big),\end{split}

and its formal limit

(2.35)

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bf u})\,:=\,\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{j}|}\sum_{\alpha_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{j}}\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\hskip-4.30554pt\mathrm{d}x\ V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\big(\Delta_{j,3-j}[{\bf u}](x,\alpha_{j})\big)\,.

Again, we define the site potential density

(2.36)

\displaystyle\phi^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,{\bm{\gamma}}}[{\bf u}]:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}},\,x\mapsto

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{j}|}\sum_{\alpha_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{j}}V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\big(\Delta_{j,3-j}[{\bf u}](x,\alpha_{j})\big)\,,

and the offset site potential

(2.37)

\displaystyle\Delta\Phi^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,{\bm{\gamma}}}[{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}};{\bf u}]\,:=\,|\Gamma_{j}|\big(\phi^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,{\bm{\gamma}}}[{\bf u}+{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}}]-\phi^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,{\bm{\gamma}}}[{\bf u}]\big)

(2.38)

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{\alpha_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{j}}\Big(V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\big(\Delta_{j,3-j}[{\bf u}+{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}}](\cdot,\alpha_{j})\big)-V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\big(\Delta_{j,3-j}[{\bf u}](\cdot,\alpha_{j})\big)\Big)\,.

This allows us to define the interlayer offset energy

(2.39)

\displaystyle\Delta E^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,{\bm{\gamma}};{\bf u}}({\bm{\delta}{\bf u}})\,

\displaystyle:=\,\sum_{R_{j}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{j}}\Delta\Phi^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,{\bm{\gamma}}}[{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}};{\bf u}](R_{j})\,.

2.2.3
Total energy

We abbreviate the truncated total energy density

(2.40)

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{N,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bf u})\,:=\,\sum_{j=1}^{2}\big(e^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j,N,\gamma_{j}}(u_{j})+e^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,N,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bf u})\big)\,,

the limit energy

(2.41)

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{\bm{\gamma}}({\bf u})\,:=\,\sum_{j=1}^{2}\big(e^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}(u_{j})+e^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bf u})\big)\,.

as well as the total offset energy

(2.42)

\displaystyle\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bm{\gamma}};{\bf u}}({\bm{\delta}{\bf u}})\,:=\,\sum_{j=1}^{2}\big(\Delta E^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j;u_{j}}(\delta u_{j})+\Delta E^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,{\bm{\gamma}};{\bf u}}({\bm{\delta}{\bf u}})\big)\,.

Below, we will show the convergence of
\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}}({\bm{\delta}{\bf u}})
and of

(2.43)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{N,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bf u})\,=\,e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{\bm{\gamma}}({\bf u})

for displacements
{\bf u}
and
{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}}
in appropriate function spaces, and sufficiently regular potentials
V^{\mathrm{(mono)}}
,
V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}
.

Below, if the explicit
{\bm{\gamma}}
-dependence is not important, we will also omit it in the notation of
e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
,
\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
, etc.

2.3.1
Displacements

We start by defining the appropriate function spaces for the energy minimizers. Observe that
e^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}(u_{j})
is invariant under the shift
u_{j}
, and that shifting

with

in

amounts to redefining the origins
\gamma_{j}\to\left\{\gamma_{j}+t_{j}\right\}_{3-j}
. Consequently, we assume that

(2.44)

\displaystyle\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\hskip-4.30554pt\mathrm{d}x\ u_{j}(x)\,=\,0\,.

Let

(2.45)

\displaystyle\bar{W}_{\mathrm{per}}^{1,2}:=\bar{W}_{\mathrm{per}}^{1,2}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}};({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}})\,:=

(2.46)

\displaystyle\quad\Big\{{\bf u}\big|_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\mid{\bf u}\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}),

(2.47)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad{\bf u}(x)={\bf u}(x+R_{\mathcal{M}})\quad\forall R_{\mathcal{M}}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{M}},\,\mathrm{a.e.\ }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

(2.48)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad{\bf u}\big|_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\in W^{1,2}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}};({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}),\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\hskip-4.30554pt\mathrm{d}x\ {\bf u}(x)\,=\,0\Big\}\,.

We endow
\bar{W}_{\mathrm{per}}^{k,p}
with the
\dot{W}^{k,p}
-topology, i.e.,

(2.49)

\|{\bf u}\|_{\bar{W}_{\mathrm{per}}^{k,p}}\,:=\,\|\nabla^{k}{\bf u}\|_{p}\,.

Analogously, we define
\bar{W}_{\mathrm{per}}^{1,2}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}};({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{{\mathcal{A}}_{j}})
,
j=1,2
, as well as
\bar{W}_{\mathrm{per}}^{n,p}
, etc. Next, let

(2.50)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\kappa}\,:=

\displaystyle\,\Big\{{\bf u}=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\bar{W}_{\mathrm{per}}^{1,2}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}};({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}})\mid\forall j=1,2:\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\kappa,

(2.51)

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{R_{j}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{j}}\|u_{j}(\cdot+R_{j})-u_{j}\|_{\infty},\sup_{R_{j}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{j}}\|u_{j}(A_{j}A_{3-j}^{-1}\cdot+R_{j})-u_{3-j}\|_{\infty}\leq\kappa\Big\}\,.

2.3.2
Interpolants

When discussing the Cauchy-Born approximation, we will restrict ourselves to a single sublattice degree of freedom per layer to simplify the analysis. This will allow us to immediately adapt ideas from
[
33
]
. To extent the ideas to multilattices, we refer to
[
32
]
.

Let
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
be such that
\operatorname{supp}\zeta\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}=\{0\}
,
\zeta(0)=1
,
\zeta\geq 0
,
\zeta(x)=\zeta(-x)
, and satisfying

(2.52)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\zeta(x-n)(a+b\cdot n)\,=\,a+b\cdot x\quad\forall{a\in{\mathbb{R}},\,b,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\,.

In particular, we have that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\ \zeta(x)=1
. We also abbreviate
\zeta_{j}:=\zeta(A_{j}^{-1}\cdot)
.

Given a function
v_{j}:{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we construct the
first order interpolant

(2.53)

v_{j}(x)\,:=\,\sum_{R_{j}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{j}}\zeta_{j}(x-R_{j})v_{j}(R_{j})\,,

as well as the quasi-interpolant

(2.54)

\widetilde{v}_{j}\,:=\,\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{j}|}\zeta_{j}*v_{j}\,.

A useful property of convolving with
\zeta_{j}
is that finite differences can be rewritten as convolution with a directional gradient

(2.55)

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{j}(x+R_{j})-\tilde{v}_{j}(x)\,

\displaystyle=\,\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}t\ \nabla_{R_{j}}\tilde{v}_{j}(x+tR_{j})

(2.56)

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{j}|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\ \int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}t\ \zeta_{j}(x+tR_{j}-y)\nabla_{R_{j}}v(y)\,.

2.3.3
Potentials

Smoothness and decay

We define the spaces for finite difference stencils

(2.57)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{j}^{\kappa}:=\Big\{{\bf a}=(a_{\omega_{j}})_{\omega_{j}\in\Omega_{j}}\mid a_{\omega_{j}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\,\|{\bf a}\|_{\mathfrak{D}_{j}}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega_{j}\in\Omega_{j},\\
\omega_{j}\neq(0,\alpha_{j})\end{subarray}}\frac{|a_{\omega_{j}}|}{|R_{j}|}\leq\kappa\Big\}\,.

Let
\mathbf{V}^{m}=({\bf v}^{(1)},\ldots,{\bf v}^{(m)})
and
{\bf v}^{(\ell)}=(v^{(\ell)}_{1},v^{(\ell)}_{2})
, where
v^{(\ell)}_{j}\in\bar{W}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}};({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{{\mathcal{A}}_{j}})
. We abbreviate the interlayer difference stencils

(2.58)

\displaystyle(\Delta^{(j,3-j)}_{\omega_{3-j}}{\bf v}^{(\ell)})(x,\alpha_{j})\,

\displaystyle:=\,v^{(\ell)}_{3-j}(\omega_{3-j}+\gamma_{3-j}+A_{3-j}A_{j}^{-1}x)-v^{(\ell)}_{j}(x+\gamma_{j},\alpha_{j})\,,

(2.59)

\displaystyle(\Delta^{(j,3-j)}_{{\bm{\omega}}_{3-j}^{m}}\mathbf{V}^{m})(x,\alpha_{j})\,

\displaystyle:=\,\big((\Delta^{(j,3-j)}_{\omega_{3-j}^{(\ell)}}{\bf v}^{(\ell)})(x,\alpha_{j})\big)_{\ell=1}^{m}\,.

Similarly, denoting
\mathbf{V}^{m}_{j}:=(v^{(1)}_{j},\ldots,v^{(m)}_{j})
, we introduce the monolayer difference stencils

(2.60)

\displaystyle(\Delta^{(j)}_{\omega_{j}^{\prime}}v^{(\ell)}_{j})(x,\alpha_{j})\,

\displaystyle:=\,v^{(\ell)}_{j}(\omega_{j}^{\prime}+x)-v^{(\ell)}_{j}(x,\alpha_{j})\,,

(2.61)

\displaystyle(\Delta^{(j)}_{{\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{m}}\mathbf{V}^{m}_{j})(x,\alpha_{j})\,

\displaystyle:=\,\big((\Delta^{(j)}_{\omega_{j}^{(\ell)}}v^{(\ell)}_{j})(x,\alpha_{j})\big)_{\ell=1}^{m}\,.

In addition, we abbreviate the partial derivatives

(2.62)

\displaystyle V^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j,\alpha_{j};{\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{m}}({\bf a})\,

\displaystyle:=\,(D_{{\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{m}}V^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}})({\bf a})\,:=\,\frac{\partial^{m}V^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}({\bf a})}{\partial a_{\omega_{j}^{(1)}}\ldots\partial a_{\omega_{j}^{(m)}}}\,,

(2.63)

\displaystyle V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,\alpha_{j};{\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{m}}({\bf a})\,

\displaystyle:=\,(D_{{\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{m}}V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}})({\bf a})\,:=\,\frac{\partial^{m}V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}({\bf a})}{\partial a_{\omega_{j}^{(1)}}\ldots\partial a_{\omega_{j}^{(m)}}}\,.

With that, we introduce the intralayer site potential norm

(2.64)

\displaystyle m^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{\ell,j}({\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell})\,

\displaystyle:=\,\Big(\prod_{n=1}^{\ell}|R_{j}^{(n)}|\Big)\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf h}^{(\ell)}\in({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{\ell},|h_{n}^{\ell}|=1,\\
{\bf a}\in\mathfrak{D}_{j}^{\kappa}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{j}}|V^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j,\alpha_{j};{\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell}}({\bf a})[{\bf h}^{(\ell)}]|\,,

(2.65)

\displaystyle m^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{\ell,3-j}({\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell})\,

\displaystyle:=\,\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf h}^{(\ell)}\in({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{\ell},|h_{n}^{\ell}|=1,\\
{\bf a}\in\mathfrak{D}_{j}^{\kappa},x\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{3-j}\\
\in{\mathcal{A}}_{3-j}\end{subarray}}|V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{3-j,\alpha_{3-j};{\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell}}\big(\Delta_{3-j,j}[{\bm{0}}](x,\alpha_{3-j})+{\bf a}\big)[{\bf h}^{(\ell)}]|\,.

Assumption 2

(Decay assumption)
.

We assume that there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\geq 5}
such that for all
j\in\{1,2\}
and
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we have that

(2.66)

\displaystyle M^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{\ell,j}:=\sum_{{\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell}\in\Omega_{j}^{\ell}}m^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{\ell,j}({\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell})\,<\,\infty\,,\quad M^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{\ell,3-j}:=\sum_{{\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell}\in\Omega_{j}^{\ell}}m^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{\ell,3-j}({\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell})\,<\,\infty\,.

We also abbreviate
M_{\ell}^{(m)}:=\sum_{j=1}^{2}M_{\ell,j}^{(m)}
for
m\in\{\mathrm{mono},\mathrm{inter}\}
. In addition, we assume that

(2.67)

V^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}({\bf 0})\,=\,0,\quad\sup_{x\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\sup_{{\bf a}\in\mathfrak{D}_{j}^{\kappa}}|V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{3-j,\alpha_{3-j}}\big(\Delta_{3-j,j}[{\bm{0}}](x,\alpha_{3-j})+{\bf a}\big)|<\infty\,.

As we will see, this decay assumption suffices to establish the well-posedness of the relaxation problem in the
Cauchy-Born approximation
. In order to ensure the well-posedness for the relaxation problem associated with the general atomistic energy density, we will impose the following condition.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
,
2\leq\ell\leq m
, and
{\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell}\in\Omega_{j}^{\ell}
, where
\omega_{j}^{(n)}=(R_{j}^{(n)},\alpha_{j}^{(n)})\in\Omega_{j}
, we define

(2.68)

\displaystyle m^{\mathrm{(mono,at)}}_{\ell,p,j}({\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell})\,:=

(2.69)

\displaystyle m^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{\ell,j}({\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell})\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\ell}|R_{j}^{(n)}|\Big)^{2}\Big(\sum_{n=2}^{\ell}\big(|R_{j}^{(1)}\times R_{j}^{(n)}|+|R_{j}^{(1)}|+|R_{j}^{(n)}|\big)\Big)^{\frac{1}{(\ell-1)p}}\,.

Assumption 3

(Sufficient decay for modelling error)
.

We assume that there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\geq 3}
such that for all
j\in\{1,2\}
and
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we have that

(2.70)

\displaystyle M^{\mathrm{(mono,at)}}_{\ell,p,j}\,:=\,\sum_{{\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell}\in\Omega_{j}^{\ell}}m^{\mathrm{(mono,at)}}_{\ell,p,j}({\bm{\omega}}_{j}^{\ell})\,<\,\infty\,,

and we abbreviate
M^{\mathrm{(mono,at)}}_{\ell,p}:=\sum_{j=1}^{2}M^{\mathrm{(mono,at)}}_{\ell,p,j}
.

Stability

As part of our assumptions, we will impose a lattice stability condition. This condition suffices to show the existence of a locally unique minimizer of
e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
.

Assumption 4

(Stacking-based stability of lattice)
.

We assume that

(2.71)

\displaystyle\nu_{\mathrm{Stack}}\,:=\,\min_{j=1,2}\inf_{\|\nabla v\|_{2}=1}\delta^{2}e^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}(0)[v,v]\,>\,0\,.

One could state an analogous assumption in terms
\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}(0)
. Crucially, such a condition in addition to smallness of
\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j}(0)
does not suffice to prove existence of a locally unique minimizer, nor its stability.

2.4
Convergence of energy functionals

We now address the convergence of the total energy density

{\bf u}
will be uniformly non-decaying, think

2.4.1
Energy density

We start by recalling some results established in
[
18
]
. A useful tool to prove the existence of the thermodynamic limit
e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
is the following
Birkhoff ergodic theorem
, see
[
20
]
and also
[
18
, Proposition 3.3]
for this formulation.

Proposition 2.3

(Ergodic Theorem)
.

Let
f\in L_{\mathrm{per}}^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}};L_{\mathrm{per}}^{1}(\Gamma_{j}))
. Then

(2.72)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R_{j}\in\\
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(N)\end{subarray}}\frac{f(R_{j}+\omega_{\mathcal{M}},R_{j}+\omega_{3-j})}{(2N+1)^{2}}=\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\hskip-4.30554pt\mathrm{d}x\ f\big(x+\omega_{\mathcal{M}},(I-A_{3-j}A_{j}^{-1})x+\omega_{3-j}\big)\end{split}

converges for almost all
(\omega_{\mathcal{M}},\omega_{3-j})\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\times\Gamma_{3-j}
and also in
L^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\times\Gamma_{3-j})
.

In order to employ Proposition
2.3
to
(e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{N,{\bm{\gamma}}})_{N}
, we also introduce the following generalized site potential

(2.73)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Psi^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j}[{\bf u}](x,y)\,:=\\
&\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{j}|}\sum_{\alpha_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{j}}V^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,\alpha_{j}}\Big(\big(Y_{3-j}(x-y+R_{3-j},\alpha_{3-j})-Y_{j}(x,\alpha_{j})\big)_{\omega_{3-j}\in\Omega_{3-j}}\Big)\,.\end{split}

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
{\bf u}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\kappa}
. Assume that
\phi^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}[u_{j}]\in L^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}})
, and that
\Psi^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j}[{\bf u}]\in L^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\times\Gamma_{3-j})
and for any
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
that
\phi^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j,{\bm{\gamma}}}[{\bf u}]\in L^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}})
, respectively.
We then have for almost all
{\bm{\gamma}}\in\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
that

(2.74)

where, in the last step, we used Poincaré’s inequality. Plugging in the expression (
4.21
), we thus obtain

(4.23)

\|T({\bf 0})\|_{Y}\,\lesssim\,\max_{j=1,2}\|D_{j\to 3-j}\|_{2}M^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{2}\,.

By assumption, we thus can choose
\|T({\bf 0})\|_{Y}
small enough.

2.

Stability estimate:
Proposition

implies that

, e.g., by the Lax-Milgrams theorem. We adapt the proof of

, Theorem 4.3]
to the present case to prove that even

.

For that, let

. Due to the stability estimate described above, there then exists a unique solution

) yields

\displaystyle\|{\bf w}\|_{W^{1,2}}\,\lesssim\,\max_{j=1,2}\|D_{j\to 3-j}\|_{2}^{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}M^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{\ell}\|{\bf u}\|_{W^{1,2}}\,.

solves the elliptic problem with constant coefficients

\sum_{j=1}^{2}\delta^{2}e^{\mathrm{(CB)}}_{j}({\bf 0})[{\bf u},\cdot]\,=\,{\bf v}+{\bf w}\,.

Elliptic regularity theory as explained, e.g., in

{\bf u}\in\in X

\displaystyle\|\delta T(0)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(Y,X)}\,\lesssim\,\Big(1+\nu_{\mathrm{Stack}}^{-1}\max_{j=1,2}\|D_{j\to 3-j}\|_{2}^{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}M^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{\ell}\Big)\,.

3.

Lipschitz bound:
A lengthy but straight-forward calculation, employing the Sobolev embedding

\displaystyle\|\delta^{2}e^{\mathrm{(CB)}}_{j}({\bf u})[{\bf v},\cdot]-\delta^{2}e^{\mathrm{(CB)}}_{j}({\bf u}^{\prime})[{\bf v},\cdot]\|_{W^{1,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\sum_{\ell=3}^{5}M^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{\ell,j}\|{\bf u}-{\bf u}^{\prime}\|_{W^{3,2}}\|{\bf v}\|_{W^{3,2}}\,.

Analogously, one proves that

\displaystyle\|\delta^{2}e^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j}({\bf u})[{\bf v},\cdot]-\delta^{2}e^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j}({\bf u}^{\prime})[{\bf v},\cdot]\|_{W^{1,2}}

With a computation analogous to

and similar to those proving (

\displaystyle\|\delta T({\bf u})-\delta T({\bf u}^{\prime})\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)}\,\lesssim\,\Big(\sum_{\ell=3}^{5}M^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{\ell}+\sum_{\ell=3}^{4}M^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{\ell}\Big)\|{\bf u}-{\bf u}^{\prime}\|_{W^{3,2}}\,.

Consequently, Lemma

5
Phonon stability

As we have previously seen, a critical point for the stacking-based energy density
e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
yields a critical point for the offset energy
\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
. A natural question that we then need to answer is whether stability is also inherited. Recall that an important property of
e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}
is that it is stable in a neighborhood of zero displacement
{\bf u}={\bf 0}
, which allowed us to obtain a locally unique minimizer.

As we shall see, stability is not necessarily inherited in the following sense: First, we will see that
\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{\bf 0}(0)<0
despite
\delta^{2}e^{\mathrm{(tot)}}(0)>0
. After developing a stability criterion, we then show that for purely twisted bilayer systems, at twist angle tending to
0
, the minimizer
{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}_{\theta\to 0}
does indeed satisfy
\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(tot)}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}_{\theta\to 0}}(0)>0
.

For the next assumption, recall the interpolants (
2.53
)–(
2.54
) of functions defined on the lattices
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}
.

Assumption 5

(Phonon stability of monolayer)
.

We assume that

\nu_{\mathrm{Phonon}}\,:=\,\inf_{\|\nabla v\|_{2}=1}\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j}[0][v,v]\,>\,0\,.

In particular, we have that

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(mono)}}_{j;\delta u_{j}}(\delta u_{j})\,\geq\,\nu\|\nabla\delta u_{j}\|_{2}^{2}\,.

For the interlayer potential, we consider the simplified problem for interatomic pair potentials
v
, and with only one sublattice degree of freedom. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j;{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}[{\bm{0}}][{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}},{\bm{\delta}{\bf v}}]\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{2}\sum_{R_{1},R_{2}}(D^{2}v)(R_{1}+u^{\mathrm{(eq)}}_{1}(R_{1})-R_{2}-u^{\mathrm{(eq)}}_{2}(R_{2}))

\displaystyle\qquad[\delta u_{1}(R_{1})-\delta u_{2}(R_{2}),\delta v_{1}(R_{1})-\delta v_{2}(R_{2})]\,.

5.1
Continuum approximation

We now want to study the continuum approximation, in order to find an appropriate test function to approximate the equilibrium energy.

We start by
excluding
the relaxation and fixing the the equilibrium distance
d
. We focus on the interlayer energy in the case of pair potentials

(5.1)

\sum_{R_{1},R_{2}}(v_{1}(R_{1})-v_{2}(R_{2}))^{T}D^{2}v(R_{1}-R_{2},d)(v_{1}(R_{1})-v_{2}(R_{2}))\,.

Observe that we can rewrite

\displaystyle\sum_{R_{1},R_{2}}h(R_{1},R_{2})\,=\,\int\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\ \sum_{R_{1}}\delta(x-R_{1})\sum_{R_{2}}\delta(y-R_{2})h(x,y)\,.

Notice that we can approximate
\delta(x-R_{j})
by
\zeta_{j}(x-R_{j})
. Abbreviating the approximate lattice restrictions

(5.2)

\chi_{{\mathcal{R}}_{j}}(x)\,:=\,\sum_{R_{j}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{j}}\zeta_{j}(x-R_{j})\,,

and the approximate interaction potential

A(x,y)\,:=\,\chi_{{\mathcal{R}}_{1}}(x)\chi_{{\mathcal{R}}_{2}}(y)(D^{2}v)(x-y,d)

we thus approximate
\delta^{2}\Delta E^{\mathrm{(inter)}}_{j;{\bf u}^{\mathrm{(eq)}}}[{\bm{0}}][{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}},{\bm{\delta}{\bf u}}]
by

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\ (\delta u_{1}(x)-\delta u_{2}(y))^{T}A(x,y)(\delta u_{1}(x)-\delta u_{2}(y))\,.

For simplicity, we even consider

A(x,y)\,\leftarrow\,D^{2}v(x-y,d)\,.

Formally, we can justify this simplification by rescaling the interlayer energy by the unit cell volume. In particular, we rescale and take the continuum limit

(5.3)

\chi_{{\mathcal{R}}_{j}}(x)\,\rightarrow\,a_{0}^{2}\sum_{R_{j}\in a_{0}{\mathcal{R}}_{j}}\zeta_{j}(x-R_{j})\xrightarrow{a_{0}\to 0}\int\mathrm{d}y\ \zeta_{j}(x-y)\,=\,1\,,

by the normalization condition on
\zeta_{j}
.

We expand the interlayer energy according to

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\ (v_{1}(x)-v_{2}(y))^{T}D^{2}v(x-y,d)(v_{1}(x)-v_{2}(y))\,=

(5.5)

\displaystyle\quad\int\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\ (v_{1}(x)-v_{1}(y))^{T}D^{2}v(x-y,d)(v_{1}(x)-v_{1}(y))

(5.6)

\displaystyle\quad+\int\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\ (v_{1}(y)-v_{2}(y))^{T}D^{2}v(x-y,d)(v_{1}(y)-v_{2}(y))

(5.7)

\displaystyle\quad+2\int\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\ (v_{1}(x)-v_{1}(y))^{T}D^{2}v(x-y,d)(v_{1}(y)-v_{2}(y))\,.

This allows us to make the following observations:

1.

(
5.5
) corresponds to a
monolayer
contribution in layer 1. In particular, if
v
satisfies some relative compactness property w.r.t. to the monolayer energy, we can treat this contribution perturbatively.

2.

For (
5.6
), we notice that
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are evaluated at the
same
position, which allows us to
average
over
D^{2}v
. Negative eigenvalues of this average could lead to negative total energies, dependent on the eigenfunctions.

3.

(
5.7
) can be controlled via Cauchy-Schwarz’ inequality by distributing it as corrections to the two previous contributions coming from (
5.5
) and (
5.6
).

As an interlayer potential, we fix a potential that is radial in the horizontal direction, i.e.,

(5.8)

v(x,z)\,=\,w(|x|,z)\,,

where
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. A straight-forward calculation yields

(5.9)

\displaystyle D^{2}v(x,z)\,=\,\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial_{\rho}w(\rho,z)}{\rho}Q_{x}\,+\,\partial_{\rho}^{2}w(\rho,z)P_{x}&\partial_{\rho z}^{2}w(\rho,z)\frac{x}{\rho}\\
\partial_{\rho z}^{2}w(\rho,z)\frac{x^{T}}{\rho}&\partial_{z}^{2}w(\rho,z)\end{pmatrix}\,,

where
P_{x}(y^{T},z)^{T}=\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{|x|}
and
Q_{x}=I-P_{x}
.
Another straight-forward computation implies that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\ D^{2}v(x,z)\,

\displaystyle=\,\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\begin{pmatrix}\big(\rho\partial_{\rho}^{2}w(\rho,z)+\partial_{\rho}w(\rho,z)\big)I_{2}&0\\
0&2\rho\partial_{z}^{2}w(\rho,z)\end{pmatrix}

(5.11)

\displaystyle=\,\pi\begin{pmatrix}\rho\partial_{\rho}w(\rho,z)\Big|_{\rho=0}^{\rho=\infty}&0\\
0&2\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\ \rho\partial_{z}^{2}w(\rho,z)\end{pmatrix}

(5.12)

\displaystyle=:\,\operatorname{diag}(0,0,m(z))\,,

where, in the second line, we integrated by parts and used the fact that
w(\rho,\cdot)\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho^{2+}}
for
\rho\to\infty
and
\|\partial\rho w(\cdot,z)\|_{\infty}<\infty
for
|z|\neq 0
.
We now consider two special cases.

1.

w(\rho,z)=f_{1}(\rho)f_{2}(z)
: Then
m(z)=2\pi\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\ \rho f_{1}(\rho)\Big)f_{2}^{\prime\prime}(z)
.

2.

w(\rho,z)=g(r)
, where
r=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}
: A straight-forward calculation yields

(5.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}^{2}w(\rho,z)\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\rho^{2}}{r^{3}}g^{\prime}(r)+\Big(\frac{z}{r}\Big)^{2}g^{\prime\prime}(r)\,=\,\frac{g^{\prime}(r)}{r}+g^{\prime\prime}(r)-\partial_{\rho}^{2}w(\rho,z)\,,

(5.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\rho}{r}g^{\prime}(r)\,

\displaystyle=\,\partial_{\rho}w(\rho,z)\,,\quad\rho g^{\prime\prime}(r)\,=\,\frac{z^{2}}{\rho}\partial_{\rho}^{2}w(\rho,z)-\frac{z^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\partial_{\rho}w(\rho,z)\,.

Consequently, we obtain that

\end{pmatrix},\quad a_{0}\,=\,\sqrt{3}\cdot 1.42\mathrm{\ nm}\,\approx\,2.46\mathrm{\ nm}\,,\quad d_{\mathrm{BG}}\,\approx\,3.35\mbox{\ \AA}\,,

where
d_{\mathrm{BG}}
is the equilibrium distance and
a_{0}/\sqrt{3}
is the lattice constant.
For a radial potential
v^{(3D)}(x,z)=f(|({\bf x},z)|)
, the restricted potential is then given by

(6.18)

v^{(2D)}(x)\,=\,f(\sqrt{|x|^{2}+d_{\mathrm{BG}}^{2}})\,=:\,g(|x|)\quad\Rightarrow\quad V(x)\,=\,\frac{v^{(2D)}(Ax)}{|\det A|}\,.

Observe that we have that

(6.19)

\displaystyle D^{2}v^{(2D)}(x)\,=\,g^{\prime\prime}(r)e_{r}\otimes e_{r}\,+\,\frac{g^{\prime}(r)}{r}e_{\varphi}\otimes e_{\varphi},\quad D^{2}V(x)\,=\,\frac{A^{T}D^{2}v^{(2D)}(Ax)A}{|\det A|}\,,

where
x=re_{r}
,
e_{r}=(\cos{\varphi},\sin{\varphi})^{T}
,
e_{\varphi}=(-\sin{\varphi},\cos{\varphi})^{T}
. To approximate
\Phi_{0}
, we truncate the summation at some
N
, i.e., we compute

(6.20)

(D^{2}\Phi_{0})(AB)\,\approx\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\\
\|n\|_{\infty}\leq N\end{subarray}}D^{2}V(AB)\,.

\displaystyle D^{2}\Phi^{\mathrm{(GSFE)}}(AB)\,\geq\,117\mbox{\ meV},

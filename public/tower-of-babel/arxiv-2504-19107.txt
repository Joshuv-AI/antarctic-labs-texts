Slicing method for nonlinear integral inequalities related to critical nonlinear wave equations
Takiko Sasaki, Kerun Shao, Hiroyuki Takamura

Abstract
The so-called “slicing method” is one of the simple and powerful tools to show the blow-up, as well as optimal upper bound of the lifespan, of solutions to critical nonlinear wave equations by iteration with the logarithmic term. It has made strong advantages in various works on nonlinear hyperbolic PDEs. In this paper, we establish one more example as a short and simple proof of the blow-up theorem for the wave equation with power-type nonlinearities of spatial derivatives of unknown functions. This method may help us to extend the blow-up result for a single nonlinear wave equation to the one for weakly coupled systems.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we are focusing on the following system of integral inequalities
for unknown functions
H\in C([R,T))
;

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle H(t)\geq At^{a}(\log t)^{-b}\left(\log\frac{t}{R}\right)^{c},&\quad t\in[R,T),\\
\displaystyle H(t)\geq B(\log t)^{x}\int_{R}^{t}ds\int_{R}^{s}r^{y}\left(\log\frac{r}{R}\right)^{z}|H(r)|^{p}dr,&\quad t\in[R,T),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where all
A>0
,
B>0
and
T>R>1
are constants.
We assume that exponents
a,b,c,x,y,z
satisfy

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle p>1,\ a\leq 1,\ b\geq\max\left\{0,\frac{x}{p-1}\right\},\\
\displaystyle y+pa=-1,\ z+cp>-1,\ z+cp\geq c-1.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

Note that (
1.1
) with

B=1,\ a=1,\ b=0,c=1,\ x=-p,\ y=-p-1,z=1

can be found as Lemma 3.1 in Shao, Takamura and Wang
[
7
]

which leads to the blow-up, as well as the optimal upper bound of the lifespan,
of the solution of the wave equation with the critical power nonlinearity of derivative-type;

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{tt}-\Delta_{x}u=|\nabla_{x}u|^{(n+1)/(n-1)}&\mbox{in}\ {\bf R}^{n}\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=\varepsilon f(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)=\varepsilon g(x),&x\in{\bf R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where
n\geq 2
,
f,g\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n})
and
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
.
See Introduction of
[
7
]
for the background of the problem (
1.3
) and all the references.
We note that the lifespan is defined as the maximal existence time of the solution.
See also Sasaki, Takamatsu and Takamura
[
6
]
for one dimensional case of (
1.3
),
and Haruyama and Takamura
[
2
]
for its application to quasilinear version of (
1.3
).

The purpose of this paper is to give a short and simple proof of Lemma 3.1 in
[
7
]

for more general setting on the exponents as in (
1.1
) by making use of the so-called “slicing method”,
which enable us to prove the blow-up of the solution for the critical case
by iteration argument with the logarithmic term.
The iteration argument was first introduced to the point-wise estimate of the solution
of nonlinear wave equations by John
[
3
]
.
And it was first combined with the functional method by Lai and Takamura
[
5
]
.
Both papers cannot handle the critical case.
We also note that the proof of Lemma 3.1 in
[
7
]
has 5 pages with relatively complicated arguments.
But one can see below that we have 2 pages with simple computations even for more general case.

We remark that the slicing method was first introduced by Agemi, Kurokawa and Takamura
[
1
]

to obtain the optimal upper bound of the lifespan of the solution of weakly coupled system
of nonlinear wave equations in three space dimensions.
Later, this method applied to various equations.
For example, see Wakasa and Yordanov
[
8
]
for variable coefficient case,
and Kitamura, Takamura and Wakasa
[
4
]
for weighted nonlinearities in one dimension.
Therefore one can expect to extend our result to more various nonlinearities than (
1.3
),
and also to weakly coupled systems easily.

This paper is organized as the theorem and its proof appear in the next section.
This work was carried out when the first author had employed in a cross-appointment system
between Tohoku Univ. and Musashino Univ. on Apr. 2020 - Mar. 2025,
and during the second author’ s stay in Tohoku Univ. on Nov. 2024 - Apr. 2025.

2
Theorem and proof

We shall prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
H\in C([R,T)
be a solution of (
1.1
).
Then,
T
has to satisfies

T\leq\exp\left(\max\left\{2\log R_{\infty},(A^{-1}D)^{\frac{p-1}{x+z+1+(c-b)(p-1)}}\right\}\right),

(2.1)

where

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle R_{\infty}:=R\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+2^{-k}),\\
\displaystyle D:=2^{\left(c+\frac{p+(z+1)(p-1)}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)}p^{\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{B}\max\left\{c+\frac{z+1}{p-1},c+\frac{z+1}{p}\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}>0.\end{array}

Remark 2.1
.

When
B=1
,
a=1
,
b=0
,
c=1
,
x=-p
,
y=-p-1
,
z=1
,
the estimate (
2.1
) yields the result in Lemma 3.1 in
[
7
]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Substituting
H
in the right side of the second line into right side of the first line in (
1.1
),
we have that

\begin{array}[]{ll}H(t)&\displaystyle\geq A^{p}B(\log t)^{x}\int_{R}^{t}ds\int_{R}^{s}r^{y+pa}(\log r)^{-pb}\left(\log\frac{r}{R}\right)^{pc+z}dr\\
&\displaystyle\geq A^{p}B(\log t)^{-(pb-x)}\int_{R}^{t}ds\int_{R}^{s}r^{-1}\left(\log\frac{r}{R}\right)^{pc+z}dr\\
&\displaystyle=\frac{A^{p}B}{pc+z+1}(\log t)^{-(pb-x)}\int_{R}^{t}\left(\log\frac{s}{R}\right)^{pc+z+1}ds\end{array}

for
t\geq R
because of
b\geq 0
,
y+pa=-1
and
pc+z+1>0
by assumption (
1.2
).
Now, let’s take
\delta>0
and restrict
t
as
t\geq(1+\delta)R(>1)
.
Then
H(t)
can be estimated from below as

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{A^{p}B}{pc+z+1}(\log t)^{-(pb-x)}\int_{\frac{t}{1+\delta}}^{t}\left(\log\frac{s}{R}\right)^{pc+z+1}ds

(2.2)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta A^{p}B}{(1+\delta)(pc+z+1)}t(\log t)^{-(pb-x)}\left(\log\frac{t}{(1+\delta)R}\right)^{pc+z+1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta A^{p}B}{(1+\delta)(pc+z+1)}t^{a}(\log t)^{-(pb-x)}\left(\log\frac{t}{(1+\delta)R}\right)^{pc+z+1}

for
t\geq(1+\delta)R
because of
a\leq 1
.

Now, let’s set
\delta_{j}:=2^{-j}
for
j\in{\bf N}

and define sequences
\{b_{j}\}
,
\{c_{j}\}
,
\{R_{j}\}
and
\{A_{j}\}
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}b_{j+1}=pb_{j}-x,&b_{0}=b,\\
c_{j+1}=pc_{j}+z+1,&c_{0}=c,\\
R_{j+1}=(1+\delta_{j})R_{j},&R_{0}=R,\\
A_{j+1}=\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{j}A_{j}^{p}B}{(1+\delta_{j})(pc_{j}+z+1)},&A_{0}=A.\end{array}\right.

Then, it follows for
j\in{\bf N}
from direct calculations that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle b_{j}=p^{j}\left(b-\frac{x}{p-1}\right)+\frac{x}{p-1},\quad c_{j}=p^{j}\left(c+\frac{z+1}{p-1}\right)-\frac{z+1}{p-1},\\
\displaystyle R_{j+1}=R\prod_{k=0}^{j}(1+2^{-k}),\end{array}

and

\displaystyle\log A_{j+1}

\displaystyle=p\log A_{j}+\log B-\log(1+2^{j})-\log c_{j+1}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\geq p\log A_{j}-(j+1)\log(2p)-\log C,

where

C:=\frac{1}{B}\max\left\{c+\frac{z+1}{p-1},c+\frac{z+1}{p}\right\}>0.

We note that the inequality (
2.3
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\log A_{j+1}-(j+1)\frac{\log(2p)}{p-1}-\frac{\log\left((2p)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}C\right)}{p-1}

\displaystyle\geq p\left(\log A_{j}-j\frac{\log(2p)}{p-1}-\frac{\log\left((2p)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}C\right)}{p-1}\right).

Hence we obtain that

\log A_{j}\geq p^{j}\log\left(\frac{A}{(2p)^{\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}}C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\right)+j\frac{\log(2p)}{p-1}+\log\left((2p)^{\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}}C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)

for
j\in{\bf N}
.
Due to the calculation in (
2.2
),

H(t)\geq A_{j}t^{a}(\log t)^{-b_{j}}\left(\log\frac{t}{R_{j}}\right)^{c_{j}}\quad\mbox{for}\ t\geq R_{j}

implies

H(t)\geq A_{j+1}t^{a}(\log t)^{-b_{j+1}}\left(\log\frac{t}{R_{j+1}}\right)^{c_{j+1}}\quad\mbox{for}\ t\geq R_{j+1}

as far as
b_{j}\geq 0
and
c_{j+1}>0
for all
j\in{\bf N}
.
This condition is guaranteed by

b\geq\max\left\{0,\frac{x}{p-1}\right\},\ z+cp>-1\ \mbox{and}\ z+cp\geq c-1

in (
1.2
).
Therefore, denoting
\displaystyle R\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+2^{-k})
by
R_{\infty}
,
we have that

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle\geq A_{j}t^{a}(\log t)^{-b_{j}}\left(\log\frac{t}{R_{j}}\right)^{c_{j}}

\displaystyle\geq(2p)^{\frac{p+(p-1)j}{(p-1)^{2}}}C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\frac{A}{(2p)^{\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}}C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\right)^{p^{j}}t^{a}(\log t)^{-b_{j}}\left(\log\frac{t}{R_{\infty}}\right)^{c_{j}}

for
t\geq R_{\infty}
and
j\in{\bf N}
.
This inequality implies that

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle\geq(2p)^{\frac{p+(p-1)j}{(p-1)^{2}}}C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\frac{A}{(2p)^{\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}}C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\right)^{p^{j}}t^{a}(\log t)^{-b_{j}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\log t\right)^{c_{j}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{z+1}{p-1}}(2p)^{\frac{p+(p-1)j}{(p-1)^{2}}}C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}t^{a}\left(\log t\right)^{-\frac{x+z+1}{p-1}}\left(\frac{A(\log t)^{\left(c-b+\frac{x+z+1}{p-1}\right)}}{2^{\left(c+\frac{z+1}{p-1}\right)}(2p)^{\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}}C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\right)^{p^{j}}

for
t\geq R_{\infty}^{2}
and
j\in{\bf N}
.

If
T
satisfies

\frac{A(\log T)^{\left(c-b+\frac{x+z+1}{p-1}\right)}}{2^{\left(c+\frac{z+1}{p-1}\right)}(2p)^{\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}}C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}>1,

H(T)
cannot be finite as
j\rightarrow\infty
.
Therefore,
T
should satisfy

T\leq\exp\left(\max\left\{2\log R_{\infty},(A^{-1}D)^{\frac{p-1}{x+z+1+(c-b)(p-1)}}\right\}\right),

where we set

D:=2^{\left(c+\frac{p+(z+1)(p-1)}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)}p^{\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}}C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}>0.

The proof is now completed.

\Box

General Airy-type equations, heat-type equations and pseudo-processes
Fabrizio Cinque, Enzo Orsingher Department of Statistical Sciences, Sapienza University of Rome, Italy

Abstract
We present a systematic study of higher-order Airy-type differential equations providing the explicit form of the solutions, deriving their power series expansions and a probabilistic interpretation. Under suitable convergence hypotheses, we compute their integral on the real line and, by means of complex integration, we provide alternative explicit forms. We then focus on the differential equations governing their derivatives, their products, their convolutions and higher-order Scorer type equations. Then, we study higher-order heat-type fractional Cauchy, showing that their fundamental solutions can be expressed in terms of Airy-type functions and their convolutions, recovering as special cases several results of appeared in previous papers. Furthermore, pseudo-processes theory permits us to give nice interpretations of the results, extending them in the case of equations involving different fractional operators and study the moments of the solutions.



Keywords:
Scorer Equation; Partial Differential Equations; Stable Subordinators and Inverses; Fractional Operators

Introduction

The physical origin of Airy functions is the mathematical description of waves propagating through a hole. Airy
[
1
]
showed that the intensity of light on the line (caustic) separating the shaded zone from the light one for a monocromatic light at point
P
with distance
R
from the hole can be approximated, by means of geometric assumptions, with the function of kind

u_{P}\sim\int_{I}e^{-ik\bigl(aw+bw^{3}\bigr)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w,

(1.1)

with suitable parameters
a,b,I
(see Vallée-Soares
[
27
]
page 115).
Inspired by the Airy integral, in this paper we study suitable generalization of this function by solving the higher-order Airy-type ordinary differential equations and by increasing the order of approximation given in (
1.1
), i.e. by considering functions of the form

u(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ixw+\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_{k}\frac{w^{2k+1}}{2k+1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w.

(1.2)

We prove that function (
1.2
) can be expressed as the convolution of higher-order Airy functions (also known as hyper-Airy functions) and can be related to suitable pseudo-processes.

Hyper-Airy functions recently appeared in several papers such as
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
19
]
. In particular, hyper-Airy functions emerge in the study of higher-order heat-type equations of real order such as, with
\alpha>1
,

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)=D^{\alpha}_{x}u(t,x),\ \ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=f(x),\ \ \forall\ x,\\
\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}u(t,x)=0,\ \ \forall\ t,\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
D^{\alpha}_{x}
is the Riesz space fractional operator and it is implicitly defined by means of the Fourier transform, that is, for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{F}\bigl(D^{\alpha}_{x}f\bigr)(\gamma)=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{l l}-i\,\text{sgn}(\gamma)|\gamma|^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}f(\gamma),&\alpha\not\in\mathbb{N}\\
(-i\gamma)^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}f(\gamma),&\alpha\in\mathbb{N},\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.4)

meaning that for integer values of
\alpha
the Riesz operator coincides with the classical derivative.

In the paper
[
19
]
, the authors show that the solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.3
) with
u(0,x)=f(x)=\delta(x)
, the Dirac delta function centered in
0

u_{\alpha}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(xw+tw^{\alpha})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w=\frac{1}{\sqrt[\alpha]{\alpha t}}A_{\alpha}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\sqrt[\alpha]{\alpha t}}\Bigr),

(1.5)

where

A_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos\Bigl(xw+\frac{w^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\Bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w,\ \ x\in\mathbb{C},\ \alpha>1,

(1.6)

is the hyper-Airy function of order
\alpha
(in the sense given in the article
[
19
]
).
If
\alpha=3
, formula (
1.6
) yields the classical Airy function.

In the paper
[
19
]
the authors proved that the solution

can be written as the expectation of a suitable function of a random variable, such as

u_{\alpha}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\pi x}\mathbb{E}\biggl[e^{-b_{\alpha}xG_{\alpha}(1/t)}\sin\Bigl(a_{\alpha}xG_{\alpha}(1/t)\Bigr)\biggr],

(1.7)

with

having probability density function
f_{G}(w;\alpha,\tau)=\frac{\alpha w^{\alpha-1}}{\tau}e^{-w^{\alpha}/\tau}
,

a_{\alpha}=\cos\bigl(\pi/(2\alpha)\bigr)
and
b_{\alpha}=\sin\bigl(\pi/(2\alpha)\bigr)
.
This result was already presented for the odd case (
\alpha=2n+1
) in
[
20
]
. Below, we provide a similar representation for the functions we deal with.

In the case of
\alpha=2n+1
, the starting point of the research on Airy-type functions, in analogy to the well-known third-order Airy functions, can be found in the
2n
-th-order ordinary differential equation

y^{(2n)}+(-1)^{n}xy=0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{C},\ n\in\mathbb{N},

Furthemore, we show that the two terms of (

In accordance with the probabilistic interpretation (

with

From the structure of the differential equation (

) we derive the non-homogeneous ordinary differential equation satisfied by the derivatives of the solutions to (

) as well as the higher-order equations governing their product. We conclude Section 2 investigating the ordinary differential equation solved by the convolution (and its derivatives) of the solutions to equations of type (
1.9
). By assuming

solving (

) with

, function
y=\mathop{\scalebox{2.8}{\raisebox{-0.77498pt}{$\ast$}}}_{k=1}^{N}f_{n_{k}}

\sum_{k=1}^{N}y^{(n_{k}-1)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}h=1\\
h\not=k\end{subarray}}^{N}c_{n_{h}}+xy\prod_{k=1}^{N}c_{n_{k}}=0.

(1.12)

In Section 3 we study a generalization of problem (

) with more general differential equations, with real
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{N}\not=0
and suitable real

,

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}D_{x}^{\alpha_{k}}u(t,x),\ \ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},

showing that the solution is of the type of function (

) and that it can be expressed as the convolution of the hyper-Airy functions (

). We also state in detail the relationship between the higher-order heat-type equations and the Airy-type ones, proving that the solution to (

Finally, we study the pseudo-processes related to problem (

In the integer case, we also compute the moments of the pseudo-processes.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{n}}f(t),&\ \text{if}\ \nu=n,\end{array}\end{cases}

(we refer to

\mathcal{F}D^{\alpha\theta,\theta}_{x}f(\gamma)=-|\gamma|^{\alpha\theta}e^{i\,\text{sgn}(\gamma)\frac{\pi\theta}{2}}\mathcal{F}f(\gamma),\ \ \ \gamma\in\mathbb{R}.

\nu>0

u(0,x)=\delta(x),\ \ \forall\ x,\\
\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}u(t,x)=0,\ \ \forall\ t,\end{cases}

is the pseudo-density of the position of a sum of independent time-changed pseudo-processes

Remark 3.3

(Moments)
.

It is interesting to study the moments of pseudo-processes. Here we show a method to derive all the moments of the odd pseudo-process
X_{2m+1}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
, having pseudo-density (
3.2
).
By keeping in mind formula (
3.8
) and by setting
N=1,\ a_{1}=a\beta_{1}=a(-1)^{m},
with
a\not=0
(set
a=1
for the classical density of the pseudo-process) since
\alpha_{1}=2n+1
, we have the
x
-Fourier transform of (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}u_{2m+1}(t,\gamma)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\gamma x}u(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=e^{-iat\gamma^{2m+1}}

(3.18)

which yields

\displaystyle\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\gamma)^{h}}{h!}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{h}u_{2m+1}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\bigl(-iat\gamma^{2m+1}\bigr)^{k}}{k!}.

For the identity principle of polynomials it must hold that for each
h\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\frac{(i\gamma)^{h}}{h!}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{h}u(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{l l}\frac{(-iat)^{k}\gamma^{(2m+1)k}}{k!},&\ \exists\ k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ s.t. }h=(2m+1)k,\\
0,&\ h\not=(2m+1)k,\ \forall\ k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.\end{array}\end{cases}

(3.19)

Therefore, the non-zero moments are

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{(2m+1)k}u_{2m+1}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=(-1)^{(m+1)k}\,(at)^{k}\frac{\bigl((2m+1)k\bigr)!}{k!},\ \ \ k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.20)

Note that according to
m
and
a
the moments may change sign cyclically.

In light of Theorem
3.1
and the above results, we can express the moments of the functions of type (
3.9
) with odd
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{N}
in terms of those in (
3.20
). Indeed, with

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{h}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos\Biggl(xw+t\sum_{k=1}^{N}w^{\alpha_{k}}\Biggr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\dots,m_{N}=1\\
m_{1}+\dots+m_{N}=h\end{subarray}}^{h}\binom{h}{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{m_{k}}u_{\alpha_{k}}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

(3.21)

where we also used the fact that the first term of (
3.21
) is the pseudo-density of the sum of independent pseudo-processes.

We point out that in the even case,
\alpha_{1}=2n
, with
a_{1}=(-1)^{m+1}
, non-zero moments are given by

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{2mk}u_{2m}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=(-1)^{(m+1)k}\,t^{k}\frac{\bigl(2mk\bigr)!}{k!},\ \ \ k\in\mathbb{N},

which is equivalent to (
3.20
) and it follows by means of (
3.11
) and the above arguments. Note that for
m
even the moments change sign cyclically.

\diamond

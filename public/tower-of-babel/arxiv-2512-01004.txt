Abstract
. Convolution of valuations was introduced by the first named author and Fu for linear spaces, and later by Alesker and the first named author for compact Lie groups. In this paper we study the convolution of invariant valuations on Lie groups. First, we obtain an explicit formula for the convolution of left-invariant valuations on compact groups in terms of differential forms. Independently, we show that a connected Lie group admits smooth bi-invariant valuations beyond the Euler characteristic and the Haar measure if and only if the group is the product of a compact group and a linear space. Finally, we use these two results to define the convolution of bi-invariant smooth valuations on an arbitrary unimodular Lie group, thus unifying both previously defined convolution operations.



1.
Introduction

One of the most fundamental concepts in convex geometry is Minkowski addition of convex bodies. The interplay between Minkowski sum and volume gives rise to the classical Brunn–Minkowski theory, pioneered in the first half of the twentieth century by Hilbert, Minkowski, Alexandrov, and others. In its core lies the Brunn–Minkowski inequality and, more generally, the notion of mixed volume and the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality.

Much later on, a remarkable algebraic perspective on the classical geometric inequalities was discovered which in particular led to their far-reaching generalizations. Namely, it turns out that Minkowski sum restricted to a suitable class of convex bodies induces a natural algebra structure where the inequalities then arise as special cases of the (mixed) Hodge–Riemann relations. This was first carried out by P. McMullen for simple, strongly isomorphic polytopes
[
32
]
. Recently, the first and third named authors jointly with Wannerer accomplished the analogous program for smooth convex bodies in the realm of Alesker’s algebraic theory of valuations
[
17
]
.

Here a valuation is a function
\phi
on the space of convex bodies satisfying

\displaystyle\phi(K\cup L)+\phi(K\cap L)=\phi(K)+\phi(L)

for any convex bodies
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
K\cup L
is convex. The graded Banach space
\Val(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of all continuous, translation-invariant valuations contains a natural dense subspace
\Val^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of smooth, translation-invariant valuations. By a recent result of Knoerr
[
30
]
, the space
\Val^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is spanned by valuations of the form
K\mapsto\vol(K+L)
, where
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is any convex body with smooth boundary and positive curvature. The Bernig–Fu convolution on
\Val^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
was defined in
[
14
]
as

\displaystyle\vol(\bullet+K)*\vol(\bullet+L)=\vol(\bullet+K+L).

(1)

If
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is replaced by a general vector space
V
without a canonical choice of the Lebesgue measure, (
1
) defines a product on
\Val^{\infty}(V)\otimes\Dens(V^{*})
, where
\Dens(V^{*})
is the (one-dimensional) space of Lebesgue measures on
V^{*}
. Equipped with the Bernig–Fu convolution,
\Val^{\infty}(V)\otimes\Dens(V^{*})
is a commutative, associative, graded algebra with unit
\vol\otimes\vol^{*}
. Denoting the vector addition on
V
by
m
, the convolution can be equivalently defined as

\displaystyle\phi*\psi=m_{*}(\phi\boxtimes\psi),

(2)

where
\boxtimes
denotes the exterior product of valuations and
m_{*}
the push-forward under
m
, see Section
2.4
. Besides the convolution, the space
\Val^{\infty}(V)
carries the so-called Alesker product
[
6
]
.
Those two algebraic structures, which are in some precise sense dual to each other
[
10
,
26
]
, were key in much of the recent progress in integral geometry
[
19
,
20
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
31
,
38
,
39
]
.

The power of the algebraic theory of valuations relies on a deep result of Alesker
[
5
]
which implies in particular that the elements of
\Val^{\infty}(V)
can be represented by smooth differential forms. In these terms, the formula for the Bernig–Fu convolution becomes particularly simple, boiling down, essentially, to the wedge product.

Moreover, the language of differential forms provides a natural framework to extend the notion of smooth valuations to smooth manifolds
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
3
]
. The filtered space of smooth valuations on a manifold
X
is denoted by
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)
. Basic examples of smooth valuations are the Euler characteristic
\chi
or any smooth measure on
X
.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{P}_{X}\to X
denote the cosphere bundle. By definition, each
\phi\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)
is represented by a pair
(\mu_{\phi},\omega_{\phi})\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(X)\times\Omega_{\ori}^{n-1}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
,
where
\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(X)
are the smooth measures on
X
, and
\Omega_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
denotes the space of complex-valued differential forms on
\mathbb{P}_{X}
twisted by the pull-back by
\pi
of the orientation line bundle of
X
. Alternatively, we can represent
\phi
by a pair
(c_{\phi},\tau_{\phi})\in C^{\infty}(X)\times\Omega_{\ori}^{n}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
. We write
\phi=[[\mu_{\phi},\omega_{\phi}]]=\{c_{\phi},\tau_{\phi}\}
. Precise definitions and more details are given in Section
2.3
.

While the Alesker product exists on any manifold
X
, in order to define the convolution, one needs, loosely speaking, a replacement for the Minkowski sum. In this regard, Alesker and the first named author
[
2
]
proved that the formula (
2
) defines an associative product on
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)
, called the convolution, if
X=G
is a compact Lie group with multiplication
m:G\times G\to G
.

Due to the aforementioned importance of the translation-invariant case, it is a natural question whether the Alesker–Bernig convolution of valuations on compact Lie groups admits an analogous simple description in terms of differential forms. Our first main result answers this question affirmatively by establishing such a formula for invariant valuations.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}
denote the subspaces of left- and bi-invariant valuations, respectively. Similar notation will be used for spaces of differential forms. We have a natural identification

\displaystyle\Omega^{p}_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}\cong\bigoplus_{k}\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\ori(\mathfrak{g})),\quad\tau\mapsto\sum_{k}\tau_{k}.

Using the Haar probability measure on
G
, we further identify

\displaystyle\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\ori(\mathfrak{g})\cong\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-p+k}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}{\otimes\ori(\mathfrak{g})}\cong\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-p+k}\mathfrak{g},

(3)

and, given a form
\tau_{k}
with values in
\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\ori(\mathfrak{g})
, write
\tilde{\tau}_{k}
for the corresponding form with values in
\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-p+k}\mathfrak{g}
. Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
,
e_{1}^{*},\ldots,e_{n}^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the dual basis, and let
e_{1}^{\sharp},\dots,e_{n}^{\sharp}
denote the induced vector fields on
\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. For
\tilde{\tau},\tilde{\zeta}\in\Omega(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g})
and
r\geq 0
we set

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{r}(\tilde{\tau}\otimes\tilde{\zeta})=\sum_{|K|=r}\iota_{e_{K}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\tau}\wedge\iota_{e_{K}^{*}}\tilde{\zeta},

where the sum runs over all sets
K=\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
and we define
\iota_{e_{K}^{\sharp}}=\iota_{e^{\sharp}_{k_{1}}}\circ\cdots\circ\iota_{e^{\sharp}_{i_{r}}}
and similarly for
\iota_{e^{*}_{K}}
.

We define the convolution product
\tau*\zeta\in\Omega^{p+q-n}_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}
of differential forms
\tau\in\Omega^{p}_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{G\times G}
and
\zeta\in\Omega^{q}_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{(\tau*\zeta)}_{j}=\sum_{k+l\geq j}\epsilon^{p,q}_{k,l,j}\hat{S}_{k+l-j}(\tilde{\tau}_{k}\otimes\tilde{\zeta}_{l}),

(4)

where

\displaystyle\epsilon_{k,l,j}^{p,q}=(-1)^{(n+q)(n+p+l+j)+k(l+j+1)}.

Observe that the lowest term in (
4
) is (up to a sign) the wedge product of
\tilde{\tau}_{k}
and
\tilde{\zeta}_{l}
. We may thus think of this definition as a deformation of the usual wedge product. We prove in Section
4
that
\tau*\zeta\in\Omega_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{G\times G}
if
\zeta
is bi-invariant, and that the convolution is associative.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group. Let
\phi=\{c_{\phi},\tau_{\phi}\}\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}
and
\psi=\{c_{\psi},\tau_{\psi}\}=[[\mu_{\psi},\omega_{\psi}]]\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}
with
\omega_{\psi}
left-invariant and
d\omega_{\psi}
vertical. Denote
\mu(\psi)=\int_{G}\mu_{\psi}=\psi(G)\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

\displaystyle\phi*\psi=\{c_{\phi}\mu(\psi)+\pi_{*}(\tau_{\phi}*\omega_{\psi}),\tau_{\phi}*\tau_{\psi}\}.

(5)

We remark that vertical refers here to the canonical contact structure on
\mathbb{P}_{G}
and that such
\omega_{\psi}
always exists, see Sections
2.1
and
2.3
, respectively.
Formula (
5
) can in fact be extended to the case when also
\phi
is only left-invariant. Indeed, it is easily seen that in such a case one has

\displaystyle\phi*\psi=\int_{G}\mathbf{Ad}_{g}^{*}\phi dg\ *\psi,

where
\mathbf{Ad}_{g}^{*}
is the pull-back under the conjugation by
g\in G
, see Section
5
.

Equation (
5
) suggests that one might be able to define the convolution of bi-invariant valuations for non-compact Lie groups. We will see that this is indeed the case and that the convolution can be defined in such generality that it unifies the Alesker–Bernig convolution on compact Lie groups with the Bernig–Fu convolution on linear spaces.

To this end, we first study which Lie groups have an interesting space of bi-invariant smooth valuations. On any Lie group, the Euler characteristic is a bi-invariant valuation. Furthermore, many Lie groups, such as semi-simple or nilpotent groups, are unimodular, i.e., possess a bi-invariant Haar measure. Our second main result classifies the connected Lie groups that admit smooth bi-invariant valuations beyond those two examples.
Henceforth, smooth valuations
\phi\notin\Span\{\chi,\vol\}
will be called
non-obvious
.

Theorem B
.

A connected Lie group
G
of dimension at least 2 admits a smooth non-obvious bi-invariant valuation if and only if
G
admits a bi-invariant riemannian metric. In this case, the space
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}
is infinite-dimensional except for
G=\mathrm{SO}(3)
and
G=S^{3}
.

In particular, such groups are unimodular. For an equivalent explicit description, recall that by a result of Milnor
[
33
]
, a connected Lie group
G
admits a bi-invariant riemannian metric if and only if
G
is the cartesian product of a compact group and a linear space.

In the two exceptional cases
G=S^{3},\,\mathrm{SO}(3)
, the space of bi-invariant valuations coincides with the space of intrinsic volumes for the bi-invariant round metric, see for example
[
12
]
. We compute the full convolution table for
S^{3}
in Section
8
.

We now turn to defining the convolution of smooth bi-invariant valuations on general unimodular groups. As there is no canonical choice of a Haar measure to play the role of unit element, it is natural to introduce a twist by the one-dimensional space
\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
of dual densities on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
or, equivalently, the dual to the space of Haar measures on
G
.

It is not hard to see that the Haar measures on
G
naturally form a direct summand in
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}
, see Proposition
7.1
. We denote the Haar measure component by
\mu(\bullet)
and retain the same notation also for spaces of valuations twisted by dual densities, so that for
\phi\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, we have
\mu(\phi)\in\mathbb{C}
, as in Theorem
A
. Further, replacing (
3
) by

\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}{\otimes\ori(\mathfrak{g})}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\cong\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-p+k}\mathfrak{g},

\tilde{\tau}_{k}
will denote the form with values in
\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-p+k}\mathfrak{g}
corresponding to a form
\tau_{k}\in\Omega^{k}_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*})\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. Formula (
4
) then defines an associative convolution product on
\Omega_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, and turns the space
\Omega_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}
into a left module over the algebra
\Omega_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a unimodular Lie group. The convolution of valuations
\phi\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and
\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}
is defined as

\phi*\psi=\{c_{\phi}\mu(\psi)+\pi_{*}(\tau_{\phi}*\omega_{\psi}),\tau_{\phi}*\tau_{\psi}\},

(6)

where
\phi=\{c_{\phi},\tau_{\phi}\}
and
\psi=\{c_{\psi},\tau_{\psi}\}=[[\mu_{\psi},\omega_{\psi}]]
with
\omega_{\psi}
left-invariant and
d\omega_{\psi}
vertical.

The validity of Definition
1.1
is not evident; namely, one has to verify that a form
\omega_{\psi}
with the required properties exists, the definition does not depend on its choice, and that the right-hand side of (
6
) indeed corresponds to a valuation. Doing this, together with proving the main properties of the so-defined convolution product, is our third main result.

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a unimodular Lie group. Then the convolution of Definition
1.1
is well defined and continuous, gives rise to an associative product on
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, and turns the space
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}
into a module over the convolution algebra
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. Moreover, it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\vol\otimes\vol^{*}\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is the unit element.

(ii)

The convolution is compatible with the canonical filtration
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)=\mathcal{W}_{0}\supset\mathcal{W}_{1}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{W}_{n}=\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
in the sense that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{W}_{k}^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\right)*\mathcal{W}_{l}^{L_{G}}\subset\mathcal{W}_{k+l-n}^{L_{G}}.

(iii)

For any
\phi\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and
\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}
it holds that
(\chi\otimes\vol^{*})*\psi=(\mu(\psi)\vol^{*})\chi
and
\phi\ast\chi=\mu(\phi)\chi
. In particular,
\Span\{\chi\otimes\vol^{*}\}\subset\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is a two-sided nilpotent ideal.

(iv)

Alesker–Poincaré duality.
If
G
is connected, then the
\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
-valued pairing on
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
,
(\phi,\psi)\mapsto(\phi*\psi)(e)
is perfect. Moreover, one has
(\phi*\psi)(e)=\mu(\phi\cdot\inv^{*}\psi)
where the dot denotes the Alesker product and
\inv:G\to G
the inverse map.

The proof of Theorem
C
we give in Section
7
takes the following indirect approach. We first define an a-priori distinct convolution operation on groups of the form
G=K\times V
, with
K
compact and
V
linear, exploiting the fact that for
\Gamma\subset V
a lattice, the quotient

G\to K\times V/\Gamma
induces an isomorphism on the corresponding spaces of bi-invariant valuations. We then proceed to show that it coincides with the convolution of Definition
1.1
. Finally, the classification established in Theorem
B
allows us to complete the proof of Theorem
C
essentially by reduction to the compact case.

We note without a proof that it can be shown directly that (
6
) gives rise to a well-defined convolution on
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
for any unimodular group
G
, without relying on Theorem
B
.
The advantage of such an a-priori approach is that, under some further conditions such as compatibility of wavefront sets, it applies also to non-smooth bi-invariant valuations, which exist for a larger class of Lie groups. For instance, on semi-simple Lie groups one has a bi-invariant pseudo-riemannian structure, with corresponding intrinsic volumes which are generalized valuations
[
13
]
. In the smooth case, however, this task is significantly simplified by making use of Theorem
B
.

Finally, we establish the compatibility of Definition
1.1
with the previously defined convolutions. Besides proving that it extends the Alesker–Bernig convolution on compact Lie groups and the Bernig–Fu convolution on flat spaces, we also show that the infinitesimal version of our convolution agrees with the Bernig–Fu convolution of
\Ad_{G}
-invariant valuations on the corresponding Lie algebra and that a certain natural averaging map is a homomorphism of convolution algebras.

Observe that a Lie group
G
that is isomorphic to the cartesian product of a compact group and a linear space, has a unique maximal compact subgroup
K\subset G
. We then define
A:\mathcal{V}^{\infty}_{c}(G)^{K\times K}\to\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
by
A(\phi)=\int_{G}L_{g}^{*}\phi d\sigma(g)\otimes\sigma^{*}
for an arbitrary Haar measure
\sigma\in\Dens(\mathfrak{g})
. Here
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}_{c}
denotes the subspace of compactly supported valuations,
L_{g}^{*}
the pull-back under the left translation by
g\in G
, and
\sigma^{*}
the corresponding dual density.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a unimodular Lie group. Consider the convolution on
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
of Definition
1.1
.

(i)

If
G
is compact, then the map
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\to\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
given by
\phi\mapsto\phi\otimes\vol(G)\vol^{*}
is an isomorphism of algebras.

(ii)

If
G=V
is a vector space, then the convolution on

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(V)^{V\times V}\otimes\Dens(V^{*})=\Val^{\infty}(V)\otimes\Dens(V^{*})

agrees with the Bernig–Fu convolution.

(iii)

There exists an isomorphism of graded algebras

\displaystyle\gr\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\cong\Val^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})^{\Ad G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*}).

(iv)

Let
G
be isomorphic to the product of a compact Lie group and a linear space, and let
K\subset G
be the maximal compact subgroup. Then for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}_{c}(G)^{K\times K}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
it holds that

A(\phi\ast\psi)=A\phi\ast\psi=\phi*A\psi=A\phi\ast A\psi

and
\phi\ast\eta=A\phi\ast\eta
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

For a real vector space
V
, let
\mathbb{P}(V)
be the projective space consisting of lines in
V
, and
\mathbb{P}_{+}(V)
its double cover of oriented lines. Thus
\mathbb{P}_{+}(V)
is the set of equivalence classes of vectors
v\in V\setminus\{0\}
with respect to the equivalence relation
v\cong\lambda v,\lambda>0
. The equivalence class of
v
will be denoted by
[v]
. For a smooth manifold
X
, we let
\mathbb{P}_{X}
denote the cosphere bundle, consisting of all pairs
(x,[\xi])
with
x\in X,\xi\in\mathbb{P}_{+}(T_{x}^{*}X)
. It is naturally a contact manifold, with the contact distribution given at the point
(x,[\xi])
by
d\pi|_{(x,[\xi])}^{-1}(\ker\xi)
, where
\pi:\mathbb{P}_{X}\to X
is the projection.

We will use the convention for the push-forward of differential forms as in
[
1
,
2
]
. More precisely, if
\pi:M\to A
is a smooth fiber bundle with compact fibers and
M,A
oriented manifolds, then

\int_{A}\alpha\wedge\pi_{*}\omega=\int_{M}\pi^{*}\alpha\wedge\omega

for
\alpha\in\Omega(A)
compactly supported and
\omega\in\Omega(M)
. Note that the degree of
\pi_{*}\omega
equals the degree of
\omega
minus the degree of the fiber. With this convention, we have
d\circ\pi=\pi\circ d
and the formula

\pi_{*}(\pi^{*}\alpha\wedge\omega)=\alpha\wedge\pi_{*}\omega.

To compute
\pi_{*}
explicitly, assume
\pi_{*}\omega\in\Omega^{k}(M)
and take a point
a\in A
and tangent vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in T_{a}A
. For a point in the fiber of
a
, take lifts
\tilde{v}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{v}_{k}
. We then have

\pi_{*}\mu(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})=\int_{\pi^{-1}(a)}\mu(\tilde{v}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{v}_{k},\bullet),

where the fiber is oriented in the usual way such that, locally, the orientation of
M
is the product orientation corresponding to
A\times\pi^{-1}(a)
.

Note that all scalar-valued (differential) forms considered throughout the paper take values in
\mathbb{C}
. Similarly, all tensor products are over
\mathbb{C}
.

2.2.
Lie groups

Let
G
be an
n
-dimensional real Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra. Take
g,h\in G
. We use the notation as in
[
25
]

L_{h}g=hg
,
R_{h}g=gh
, and

\mathbf{Ad}_{h}(g)=hgh^{-1}
. We set

L_{h}^{*}:=(dL_{h^{-1}})^{*}:T_{g}^{*}G\to T_{hg}^{*}G,\quad R_{h}^{*}:=(dR_{h^{-1}})^{*}:T_{g}^{*}G\to T_{gh}^{*}G,

and

\Ad_{h}^{*}:=(d\mathbf{Ad}_{h^{-1}})^{*}:T_{g}^{*}G\to T_{hgh^{-1}}^{*}G.

Similarly, we denote the adjoint action
\Ad_{h}=d\mathbf{Ad}_{h}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
. We then have
\Ad_{h}^{*}=(\Ad_{h^{-1}})^{*}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. For
x\in\mathfrak{g}
we set
\ad_{x}=\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\Ad_{\exp(tx)}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
and
\ad_{x}^{*}:=\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\Ad_{\exp(tx)}^{*}=(-\ad_{x})^{*}
, and let
x^{\sharp}
be the vector field on
\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
defined by

x^{\sharp}|_{[\xi]}=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}[\Ad_{\exp(tx)}^{*}\xi]=[\ad^{*}_{x}(\xi)].

By
\ori(\mathfrak{g})
we denote the line of orientations on
\mathfrak{g}
. The connected components of
\ori(\mathfrak{g})\setminus\{0\}
correspond to orientations of
\mathfrak{g}
, which can be identified with the left-invariant orientations on
G
using left translation. Thus
\ori(\mathfrak{g})\simeq\ori(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\simeq\ori(\mathfrak{g})^{*}
is a real 1-dimensional module over
G
, such that
g\in G
acts by the scalar
\sgn\det(\Ad_{g}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g})
. There is a natural identification
\Dens(\mathfrak{g})=\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{or}(\mathfrak{g})
. The group
G
is called
unimodular
if it has a bi-invariant Haar measure, or equivalently if
|\det\Ad_{g}|=1
for all
g\in G
. It then holds that

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\ad_{X})=0,\quad X\in\mathfrak{g},

(7)

and the inverse map
\inv:G\to G
preserves any given Haar measure. In particular, compact groups are unimodular.

Let
*:\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{k}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be the Hodge star isomorphism given by

\langle*(X\otimes\det),Y\rangle=\det(X\wedge Y),\quad Y\in\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-k}\mathfrak{g}.

Using that
\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathbb{C}
, we thus have the isomorphism

\displaystyle*^{-1}:\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}\to\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-k}\mathfrak{g},

(8)

which will be frequently used in the paper. Observe that if
\tau\in\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
and
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}
, then

\tau(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})=*^{-1}\tau\wedge X_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge X_{k}.

(9)

By naturality of the Hodge star we have

\displaystyle\Ad_{h}^{*}\circ\,*=*\circ\Ad_{h}.

(10)

Differentiating, we infer that

\displaystyle\ad_{x}^{*}\circ*=*\circ\ad_{x},\quad x\in\mathfrak{g}.

(11)

Finally, the Koszul boundary operator
\partial:\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}\to\wedge^{k-1}\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

\partial(x_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge x_{k})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(-1)^{i+j+1}[x_{i},x_{j}]\wedge x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widecheck{x}_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widecheck{x}_{j}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{k},

where
\widecheck{x}_{i}
means that
x_{i}
is omitted. It satisfies
\partial^{2}=0
.

2.3.
Smooth valuations

We refer to
[
7
,
9
,
4
,
3
]
for the theory of smooth valuations.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold and
\mathbb{P}_{X}=\mathbb{P}_{+}(T^{*}X)
its cosphere bundle. Denote by
\pi:\mathbb{P}_{X}\to X
the projection. Let
\mathcal{P}(X)
denote the class of compact differentiable polyhedra in
X
. Each
P\in\mathcal{P}(X)
admits a normal cycle
N(P)
which is a naturally oriented Legendrian
(n-1)
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of
\mathbb{P}_{X}
, see
[
3
]
for details.

We denote by
\ori(X)
the orientation line bundle on
X
. The line bundles underlying smooth measures and top differential forms on
X
are related by
\Dens(TX)=\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}T^{*}X\otimes\ori(X)
. By
\Omega_{\ori}(X)
, resp.
\Omega_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
, we denote the space of complex-valued differential forms on X, resp. on
\mathbb{P}_{X}
, twisted by
\ori(X)
, resp. by
\pi^{*}\ori(X)
. Note that
\Omega^{n}_{\ori}(X)=\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(X)
.

A functional
\phi:\mathcal{P}(X)\to\mathbb{C}
is called a smooth valuation if there exist
\mu\in\Omega^{n}_{\ori}(X)
and
\omega\in\Omega^{n-1}_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
such that

\displaystyle\phi(P)=\int_{P}\mu+\int_{N(P)}\omega,\quad P\in\mathcal{P}(X).

In this case we denote
\phi=[[\mu,\omega]]
.

Clearly, any measure on
X
is a smooth valuation. Another example is the Euler characteristic
\chi
for which the corresponding forms were constructed by Chern
[
24
]
. If
X
is endowed with a riemannian metric, then the intrinsic volumes
\mu_{k},k=0,\ldots,n
, are smooth valuations on
X
. The Fréchet space of smooth valuations on
X
is denoted by
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)
. It admits a canonical filtration by closed subspaces

\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)=\mathcal{W}_{0}\supset\mathcal{W}_{1}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{W}_{n}=\Omega^{n}_{\ori}(X),

such that the associated graded space
\gr(\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X))=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{k}/\mathcal{W}_{k+1}
is canonically isomorphic to the space of smooth sections of the vector bundle

\Val^{\infty}(TX)\to X

whose fiber over
x\in X
is the space
\Val^{\infty}(T_{x}X)
of smooth and translation-invariant valuations, cf. Section
2.5
below.

By definition, the map
\Omega^{n}_{\ori}(X)\times\Omega_{\ori}^{n-1}(\mathbb{P}_{X})\to\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X),(\mu,\omega)\mapsto[[\mu,\omega]]
is surjective. Let us describe the kernel, following
[
11
]
. A differential form on
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is called vertical if its restriction to the contact distribution vanishes. Rumin has constructed a differential operator
D:\Omega^{n-1}(\mathbb{P}_{X})\to\Omega^{n}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
of order
2
such that
D\omega=d(\omega+\xi)
, where
\xi
is the unique vertical
(n-1)
-form such that
d(\omega+\xi)
is vertical
[
35
]
. We then have
[[\mu,\omega]]=0
if and only if
D\omega+\pi^{*}\mu=0
and
\pi_{*}\omega=0
, where
\pi:\mathbb{P}_{X}\to X
is the bundle projection. Any smooth valuation
[[\mu,\omega]]
can thus be equivalently described by the pair
(\pi_{*}\omega,D\omega+\pi^{*}\mu)\in C^{\infty}(X)\times\Omega^{n}_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
. Note that
(f,\tau)\in C^{\infty}(X)\times\Omega^{n}_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
corresponds to a (unique) smooth valuation if and only if
\tau
is vertical and exact, and
\pi_{*}\tau=df
. In this case we write
\{f,\tau\}
for the valuation defined by this pair. With this notation, we have

\displaystyle[[\mu,\omega]]=\{\pi_{*}\omega,D\omega+\pi^{*}\mu\}.

2.4.
Operations on smooth valuations

The space
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)
carries a natural bilinear structure, the Alesker product, which turns it into a commutative associative filtered algebra with unit
\chi
, see
[
6
,
1
,
3
]
for its construction. A fundamental property of the Alesker product is the following version of Poincaré duality. Let
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}_{c}(X)\subset\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)
denote the ideal of compactly supported valuations, i.e.,
\phi\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\infty}(X)
if it vanishes outside of a compact set
P_{0}
. In this case
\int_{X}\phi:=\phi(P)
does not depend on
P\in\mathcal{P}(X)
if
P\supset P_{0}
. Then the bilinear map

\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)\times\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\infty}(X)\to\mathbb{C},\quad(\phi,\psi)\mapsto\int_{X}\phi\cdot\psi

is a perfect pairing. Consequently,
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)
embeds densely in the space

\mathcal{V}^{-\infty}(X):=\left(\mathcal{V}^{\infty}_{c}(X)\right)^{*}

(12)

of generalized valuations.

Given two smooth manifolds
X,Y
, there exists a natural bilinear map
\boxtimes:\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)\times\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(Y)\to\mathcal{V}^{-\infty}(X\times Y)
, called exterior product. Note that the image is not a smooth valuation, but merely a generalized valuation in the sense of (
12
). For a smooth map
f:X\to Y
, there are further partially defined maps
f_{*}:\mathcal{V}^{-\infty}(X)\dashedrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{-\infty}(Y)
and
f^{*}:\mathcal{V}^{-\infty}(Y)\dashedrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{-\infty}(X)
, called push-forward and pull-back, respectively, under
f
.

The product of smooth valuations on a manifold
X
is the pull-back of the exterior product under the diagonal map
X\hookrightarrow X\times X,x\mapsto(x,x)
, and it is again a smooth valuation on
X
. If a Lie group
G
acts transitively on a manifold
X
, then the push-forward of the exterior product of a smooth valuation on
G
and a smooth valuation on
X
under the map
G\times X\to X
is called the convolution, and it is again a smooth valuation on
X
.

The Euler–Verdier involution
\sigma:\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)\to\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(X)
is defined by

\sigma[[\mu,\omega]]:=(-1)^{n}[[\mu,a^{*}\omega]],

where
n=\dim X
and
a:\mathbb{P}_{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{X}
is the antipodal map. It is compatible with the filtration and the Alesker product and it extends to an involution on
\mathcal{V}^{-\infty}(X)
. The intrinsic volumes satisfy
\sigma\mu_{k}=(-1)^{k}\mu_{k}
.

2.5.
Translation-invariant valuations

Let
X=V
be a real vector space and let
\mathcal{K}(V)
denote the set of convex bodies, i.e., compact convex subsets of
V
. According to a deep result of Alesker, the subspace
\Val^{\infty}(V)\subset\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(V)
of translation-invariant valuations coincides with the dense subspace of
\mathrm{GL}(V)
-smooth vectors in the Banach space
\Val(V)
of translation-invariant
continuous
valuations on
\mathcal{K}(V)
. In fact, by a recent result of Knoerr
[
30
]
,

\Val^{\infty}(V)=\Span\{K\mapsto\vol(K+A)\mid A\in\mathcal{K}^{\infty}_{+}(V)\},

where
\vol\in\Dens(V)
is a Lebesgue measure and
\mathcal{K}^{\infty}_{+}(V)\subset\mathcal{K}(V)
consists of convex bodies with smooth boundary and positive curvature.
Observe that one has the so-called McMullen grading

\Val^{\infty}(V)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\Val^{\infty}_{k}(V),

where
\Val_{k}^{\infty}(V)\subset\Val^{\infty}
is the subspace of
k
-homogeneous valuations, i.e.,
\phi\in\Val_{k}^{\infty}
if
\phi(\lambda K)=\lambda^{k}\phi(K)
for any
\lambda>0
and
K\in\mathcal{K}(V)
.

The space
\Val^{\infty}(V)
carries besides the Alesker product another algebraic structure. More precisely, the Bernig–Fu convolution is the commutative associative bilinear product on
\Val^{\infty}(V)\otimes\Dens(V^{*})
given by

\left[\vol_{n}(\cdot+A_{1})\otimes\mu_{1}^{*}\right]*\left[\vol_{n}(\cdot+A_{2})\otimes\mu_{2}^{*}\right]=\mu_{1}^{*}(\vol_{n})\vol_{n}(\cdot+A_{1}+A_{2})\otimes\mu_{2}^{*}.

It satisfies

[\Val^{\infty}_{k}(V)\otimes\Dens(V^{*})]*[\Val^{\infty}_{l}(V)\otimes\Dens(V^{*})]\subset\Val^{\infty}_{k+l-n}(V)\otimes\Dens(V^{*}).

3.
Invariant differential forms

3.1.
Left-invariant and bi-invariant forms

Let
G
be a unimodular Lie group of dimension
n
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. In this section we will study invariant differential forms on the cosphere bundle
\mathbb{P}_{G}=\mathbb{P}_{+}(T^{*}G)
over
G
.

We will be mostly utilizing the two actions of
G
on itself given by left translation and conjugation, which induce corresponding actions on
\mathbb{P}_{G}
in the natural way:

\displaystyle L_{h}((g,[\xi]))=(hg,[L_{h}^{*}\xi])\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Ad}_{h}((g,[\xi]))=(\mathbf{Ad}_{h}(g),[\Ad_{h}^{*}\xi]).

A differential form on
\mathbb{P}_{G}
is called left-, resp.
\Ad
-invariant, if it is invariant under left translation, resp.
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}
. A form is bi-invariant if it is invariant under both left and right translation, or equivalently if it is both left- and
\Ad_{G}
-invariant.
Using the diffeomorphism
G\times\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\to\mathbb{P}_{G}
,
(g,[\xi])\mapsto(g,[L_{g}^{*}\xi])
, the corresponding induced actions on
G\times\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
are
L_{h}\times\mathrm{id}
and
\mathbf{Ad}_{h}\times\Ad_{h}^{*}
.

Observe that we then have the following identifications:

\displaystyle\Omega^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{k}\Omega^{p-k,k}(G\times\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))^{L_{G}}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{k}\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))\otimes\Omega^{p-k}(G)^{L_{G}}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{k}\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}).

For
\tau\in\Omega^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}
, we decompose accordingly
\tau=\sum_{k}\tau_{k}
, where

\tau_{k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})(x_{1},\dots,x_{p-k})=\tau(u_{1},\dots,u_{k},x_{1},\dots,x_{p-k}).

(13)

Here
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
are vector fields on
\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{p-k}
left-invariant vector fields on
G
, which we identify with vector fields on
\mathbb{P}_{G}\cong G\times\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
.

Considered as an action on
\Omega(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))\otimes\Omega(G)^{L_{G}}
, the adjoint action
(\mathbf{Ad}_{h}\times\Ad_{h}^{*})^{*}
takes the pull-back of the form under the diffeomorphism
\Ad_{h}^{*}
on
\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and applies
(\mathbf{Ad}_{h})^{*}
to the value. Differentiating in the second factor then induces the action
(\Ad_{h}^{*})^{*}\otimes\Ad_{h^{-1}}^{*}
on
\Omega(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. A form
\tau\in\Omega(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}
is thus bi-invariant if and only if the corresponding form
\tau\in\Omega(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is
\Ad
-invariant, that is if for any
h\in G
,

\displaystyle(\Ad_{h}^{*})^{*}\otimes\Ad_{h^{-1}}^{*}\tau=\tau.

(14)

Finally, take

\tau\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\ori(\mathfrak{g}))\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})=\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*})\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}.

The following notation will be used throughout the paper, see (
8
):

\tilde{\tau}=*^{-1}\tau\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-p+k}\mathfrak{g}).

(15)

3.2.
The differential on invariant forms

In the following we will consider the decomposition of left-invariant forms defined by (
13
). As usual we set
\tau_{k}=0
for
k<0
.

Observe that under the identification of
\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*})
with a subspace of
\Omega^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}
, the de Rham differential on the former space differs from the restriction of the de Rham differential on the latter. To avoid any ambiguity, we will adhere to the following convention in the rest of the paper: When we write
\tau\in\Omega^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}
, then
d\tau
refers to the differential of the (complex-valued)
p
-form on
\mathbb{P}_{G}
, whereas for
\tau\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),V)
, where
V
is a vector space,
d\tau
denotes the differential of the
V
-valued
k
-form on
\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0\leq p\leq 2n-1
,
\tau\in\Omega^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}
and
\tau_{k}\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*})
as in (
13
). For
0\leq k\leq p
we have

(d\tau)_{k}=d\tau_{k-1}+(-1)^{k+1}\partial^{*}\tau_{k}.

(16)

Proof.

Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{p-k+1}
be left-invariant vector fields on
G
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
commuting vector fields on some open subset
U\subset\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, which we identify with left-invariant vector fields near
\{e\}\times U\subset G\times\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\cong\mathbb{P}_{G}
. Since
\tau
is left-invariant, it holds for all
i
that

x_{i}\tau(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k},x_{1},\dots,\widecheck{x}_{i},\dots,x_{p-k+1})=0,

where
\widecheck{x}_{i}
means that
x_{i}
is omitted. Note also that
[u_{i},x_{j}]=0
for all
i_{,}j
. Therefore,

\displaystyle d\tau(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k},x_{1},\ldots,x_{p-k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i+1}u_{i}\tau(u_{1},\dots,\widecheck{u}_{i},\dots,u_{k},x_{1},\dots,x_{p-k+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j+k}\tau(u_{1},\dots,u_{k},[x_{i},x_{j}],x_{1}\dots,\widecheck{x}_{i},\dots,\widecheck{x}_{j},\dots,x_{p-k+1})

\displaystyle=d\tau_{k-1}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p-k+1})

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{k+1}\partial^{*}\tau_{k+1}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p-k+1}).

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
X\in\wedge^{k+1}\mathfrak{g}
,
Y\in\wedge^{n-k}\mathfrak{g}
one has

\displaystyle\partial X\wedge Y=(-1)^{k+1}X\wedge\partial Y.

(17)

Proof.

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. By linearity we may assume that both
X
and
Y
are wedge products of basis elements. We will distinguish three cases.
First, if
X,Y
have three or more common factors, then both sides of (
17
) vanish.
Second, if
X,Y
have two factors in common, say
X=e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{k+1}
and
Y=e_{k}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{n-1}
, then the only possibly non-zero summand on both sides of (
17
) is

[e_{k},e_{k+1}]\wedge e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{k-1}\wedge e_{k}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{n-1}.

It remains to consider the case when
X,Y
have exactly one factor in common, say
X=e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{k+1}
and
Y=e_{k+1}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\partial X\wedge Y

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i-1}\wedge[e_{k+1},e_{i}]\wedge e_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n},

\displaystyle X\wedge\partial Y

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=k+2}^{n}e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i-1}\wedge[e_{k+1},e_{i}]\wedge e_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n},

so that

\displaystyle\partial X\wedge Y+(-1)^{k}X\wedge\partial Y

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i-1}\wedge[e_{k+1},e_{i}]\wedge e_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{k}\tr(\ad_{e_{k+1}}),

which vanishes by (
7
) since
G
is unimodular.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
0\leq p\leq 2n-1
and
\tau\in\Omega^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
. For
0\leq k\leq p
we have

\displaystyle(\widetilde{d\tau})_{k}

\displaystyle=d(\tilde{\tau}_{k-1})+(-1)^{n-p+1}\partial\tilde{\tau}_{k}.

(18)

In particular,
\tau\in\Omega^{n}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
is closed if and only if

\displaystyle d\tilde{\tau}_{k-1}=\partial\tilde{\tau}_{k}

(19)

for all
k
.

Proof.

We claim that

\displaystyle\partial^{*}\tau_{k}=(-1)^{n-p+k}*\partial*^{-1}\tau_{k}.

(20)

Applying both sides of (
20
) to
Y\in\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k+1}\mathfrak{g}
, the left-hand side becomes

\langle\partial^{*}\tau_{k},Y\rangle=\langle\tau_{k},\partial Y\rangle.

For the right-hand side we use Lemma
3.2
and compute

\langle(-1)^{n-p+k}*\partial*^{-1}\tau_{k},Y\rangle=(-1)^{n-p+k}\partial*^{-1}\tau_{k}\wedge Y=*^{-1}\tau_{k}\wedge\partial Y=\langle\tau_{k},\partial Y\rangle,

proving the claim. (
18
) then follows from (
20
) by applying
*^{-1}
to (
16
).
∎

4.
Convolution of invariant differential forms

We will now define a formal convolution product of invariant differential forms which will later be used to write an explicit formula for the convolution of smooth valuations on a general Lie group. Throughout this section,
G
will be a unimodular Lie group of dimension
n
,
\vol
some Haar measure,
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra,
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
,
e_{1}^{*},\ldots,e_{n}^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the dual basis, and
e_{1}^{\sharp},\dots,e_{n}^{\sharp}
the induced vector fields on
\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. Recall that
\Dens(\mathfrak{g})=\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\ori(\mathfrak{g})
.

Definition 4.1
.

For
\tilde{\tau},\tilde{\zeta}\in\Omega(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g})
and
r\geq 0
we set

\hat{S}_{r}(\tilde{\tau}\otimes\tilde{\zeta})=\sum_{|K|=r}\iota_{e_{K}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\tau}\wedge\iota_{e_{K}^{*}}\tilde{\zeta},

where the sum runs over all sets
K=\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
and we define
\iota_{e_{K}^{\sharp}}=\iota_{e^{\sharp}_{k_{1}}}\circ\cdots\circ\iota_{e^{\sharp}_{i_{r}}}
and similarly for
\iota_{e^{*}_{K}}
.

Observe that the definition of
\hat{S}_{r}
depends neither on the ordering of the elements in
K
nor on the choice of a basis.

Definition 4.2
.

The
convolution product

\tau*\zeta\in\Omega^{p+q-n}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
of (twisted) differential forms
\tau\in\Omega^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{G\times G}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
and
\zeta\in\Omega^{q}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

\widetilde{(\tau*\zeta)}_{j}=\sum_{k+l\geq j}\epsilon^{p,q}_{k,l,j}\hat{S}_{k+l-j}(\tilde{\tau}_{k}\otimes\tilde{\zeta}_{l}),

where

\displaystyle\epsilon_{k,l,j}^{p,q}=(-1)^{(n+q)(n+p+l+j)+k(l+j+1)}.

Fixing an orientation on
G
, we can identify the Haar measure
\vol
with a top differential form on
G
, which in turn can be both restricted to
T_{e}G=\mathfrak{g}
or pulled-back to
\mathbb{P}_{G}
. We then obtain an element

\vol\otimes\vol^{*}\in\Omega^{n}(\mathbb{P}_{G})\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
, which depends neither on the choice of orientation, nor on the normalization of
\vol
. Explicitly,

\vol\otimes\vol^{*}|_{(g,[\xi])}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})=d\pi(v_{1})\wedge\ldots\wedge d\pi(v_{n})\in\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}T_{g}G\cong\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}.

We then have

(\vol\otimes\vol^{*})*\zeta=\zeta\quad\text{and}\quad\tau*(\vol\otimes\vol^{*})=\tau.

(21)

Proposition 4.3
.

If
\tau,\zeta\in\Omega(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{G\times G}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
are bi-invariant, then so is
\tau*\zeta
.

Proof.

By (
14
) and (
10
), it suffices to show

\left((\Ad_{h^{-1}}^{*})^{*}\otimes\Ad_{h}\right)\hat{S}_{r}(\tilde{\tau}\otimes\tilde{\zeta})=\hat{S}_{r}(\tilde{\tau}\otimes\tilde{\zeta}),\quad r\geq 0.

To this end, observe first that for any index subset
K
we have

\displaystyle(\Ad_{h^{-1}}^{*})^{*}\circ\iota_{e_{K}^{\sharp}}=\iota_{(\Ad_{h}e_{K})^{\sharp}}\circ(\Ad_{h^{-1}}^{*})^{*}

and

\displaystyle\Ad_{h}\circ\iota_{e_{K}^{*}}=\iota_{(\Ad_{h}e_{K})^{*}}\circ\Ad_{h}.

Then the claim follows easily from the invariance of
\tau
and
\zeta
and the fact that the definition of
\hat{S}_{r}
is independent of the basis of
\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

Now we will show that the product defined in Definition
4.2
is associative. For the proof, the following notation will be used: Let
K=\{k_{1},\dots,k_{a}\}
and
L=\{l_{1},\dots,l_{b}\}
be strictly increasing sequences. We define

\displaystyle\epsilon(K,L)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }(k_{1},\dots,k_{a},l_{1},\dots,l_{b})\text{ is an even permutation of }K\cup L,\\
0,&\text{if }K\cap L\neq\emptyset,\\
-1,&\text{if }(k_{1},\dots,k_{a},l_{1},\dots,l_{b})\text{ is an odd permutation of }K\cup L.\end{cases}

This notation will also be used in Section
5
.

Proposition 4.4
.

The convolution product of forms over
\mathbb{P}_{G}
is associative.

Proof.

Let
\tau\in\Omega^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
,
\zeta\in\Omega^{q}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, and
\kappa\in\Omega^{r}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. Using the definition, we compute

\displaystyle\widetilde{(\tau*\zeta)*\kappa}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l,m}\sum_{J,K}\epsilon_{1}\iota_{e_{J}^{\sharp}}(\iota_{e_{K}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\tau}_{k}\wedge\iota_{e_{K}^{*}}\tilde{\zeta}_{l})\wedge\iota_{e_{J}^{*}}\tilde{\kappa}_{m}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k,l,m}\sum_{J,K}\sum_{L\subset J}\epsilon_{2}\iota_{e_{L\cup K}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\tau}_{k}\wedge\iota_{e_{J\setminus L}^{\sharp}}\iota_{e_{K}^{*}}\tilde{\zeta}_{l}\wedge\iota_{e_{J}^{*}}\tilde{\kappa}_{m}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k,l,m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K,L,M\\
L\cap M=\emptyset\\
L\cap K=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\epsilon_{3}\iota_{e_{L\cup K}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\tau}_{k}\wedge\iota_{e_{M}^{\sharp}}\iota_{e_{K}^{*}}\tilde{\zeta}_{l}\wedge\iota_{e_{L\cup M}^{*}}\tilde{\kappa}_{m},

where

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{k,l,k+l-|K|}^{p,q}\epsilon_{k+l-|K|,m,k+l+m-|K|-|J|}^{p+q-n,r}

\displaystyle\epsilon_{2}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{1}\cdot(-1)^{(k-|K|)|J\setminus L|}\epsilon(L,J\setminus L)\epsilon(L,K)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{3}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{k,l,k+l-|K|}^{p,q}\epsilon_{k+l-|K|,m,k+l+m-|K|-|L|-|M|}^{p+q-n,r}\cdot(-1)^{(k-|K|)|M|}\epsilon(L,M)\epsilon(L,K).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tau*(\zeta*\kappa)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l,m}\sum_{J,M}\epsilon^{\prime}_{1}\iota_{e_{J}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\tau}_{k}\wedge\iota_{e_{J}^{*}}(\iota_{e_{M}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\zeta}_{l}\wedge\iota_{e_{M}^{*}}\tilde{\kappa}_{m})

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k,l,m}\sum_{J,M}\sum_{K\subset J}\epsilon^{\prime}_{2}\iota_{e_{J}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\tau}_{k}\wedge\iota_{e^{*}_{K}}\iota_{e_{M}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\zeta}_{l}\wedge\iota_{e^{*}_{J\setminus K\cup M}}\tilde{\kappa}_{m}

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{k,l,m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K,L,M\\
L\cap K=\emptyset\\
L\cap M=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\epsilon_{3}^{\prime}\iota_{e_{K\cup L}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\tau}_{k}\wedge\iota_{e^{*}_{K}}\iota_{e_{M}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\zeta}_{l}\wedge\iota_{e^{*}_{L\cup M}}\tilde{\kappa}_{m},

where

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{k,l+m-|M|,k+l+m-|M|-|J|}^{p,q+r-n}\epsilon_{l,m,l+m-|M|}^{q,r}

\displaystyle\epsilon_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{1}^{\prime}\cdot(-1)^{(n-q+l)|J\setminus K|}\epsilon(K,J\setminus K)\epsilon(J\setminus K,M)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{k,l+m-|M|,k+l+m-|M|-|K|-|L|}^{p,q+r-n}\epsilon_{l,m,l+m-|M|}^{q,r}\cdot(-1)^{(n-q+l)|L|}\epsilon(K,L)\epsilon(L,M).

Using
\epsilon(K,L)\epsilon(L,K)=(-1)^{|K|\cdot|L|}
it follows that
\epsilon_{3}=\epsilon_{3}^{\prime}
.
∎

5.
Compact Lie groups

In this section, we prove the explicit formula for the convolution of left-invariant, smooth valuations on a compact Lie group in terms of differential forms given in Theorem
A
. Throughout the section,
G
will denote an
n
-dimensional compact Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

We will consider the Haar probability measure on
G
. This choice identifies
\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
with
\mathbb{C}
. The isomorphism (
8

*^{-1}:\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\ori(\mathfrak{g})\to\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-k}\mathfrak{g}
and the convolution of invariant differential forms of Definition

is a map
\Omega_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{G\times G}\times\Omega_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}\to\Omega_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}
.

We will need some maps defined in
[
2

. Consider

\mathcal{M}:=\{(g_{1},g_{2},[\xi_{1}:0]\}\cup\{(g_{1},g_{2},[0:\xi_{2}])\}\subset\mathbb{P}_{G\times G}

and let
F:\hat{\mathbb{P}}\to\mathbb{P}_{G\times G}
be the oriented blow-up along
\mathcal{M}
. Let
(G\times G)\times_{m,\pi}\mathbb{P}_{G}
be the preimage of the diagonal under the map
m\times\pi:(G\times G)\times\mathbb{P}_{G}\to G\times G
, endowed with the preimage orientation, see
[
28
]
. We then have a diffeomorphism

\displaystyle\begin{split}(G\times G)_{m,\pi}\times\mathbb{P}_{G}&\cong G\times G\times\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\\
(g,h,gh,[\xi])&\mapsto(g,h,[dL^{*}_{(gh)^{-1}}\xi]).\end{split}

(22)

If we endow the space on the right-hand side with the product orientation, then this diffeomorphism is orientation preserving if and only if
n
is even.

The differential of the multiplication map
m:G\times G\to G
is a map

dm^{*}:(G\times G)\times_{m,\pi}\mathbb{P}_{G}\to\mathbb{P}_{G\times G}

whose image is disjoint from
\mathcal{M}
by

. Let

be the map given outside

by
(g_{1},g_{2},[\xi_{1}:\xi_{2}])\mapsto((g_{1},[\xi_{1}]),(g_{2},[\xi_{2}]))
. Since
F
is a local diffeomorphism outside
\mathcal{M}
, we may define the map
r:=\Phi\circ F^{-1}\circ dm^{*}:(G\times G)\times_{m,\pi}\mathbb{P}_{G}\to\mathbb{P}_{G}\times\mathbb{P}_{G}
. Explicitly,

r(g,h,[\xi])=((g,[R_{h^{-1}}^{*}\xi]),(h,[L_{g^{-1}}^{*}\xi)])).

Finally, we will consider the obvious projections
p_{1},p_{2}:\mathbb{P}_{G}\times\mathbb{P}_{G}\to\mathbb{P}_{G}
,
\tilde{p}_{1},\tilde{p}_{2}:G\times G\to G
,
\hat{p}_{1}:\mathbb{P}_{G}\times G\to\mathbb{P}_{G}
, and
\hat{p}_{2}:\mathbb{P}_{G}\times G\to G
. We thus have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}_{G}\times\mathbb{P}_{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}_{G\times G}}

The right translation by
h
is a diffeomorphism
R_{h}:G\to G
. It induces a diffeomorphism
R_{h}(g,[\xi]):=(gh,[R_{h}^{*}\xi])
on
\mathbb{P}_{G}
. For differential forms
\rho\in\Omega(G)
and
\tau\in\Omega(\mathbb{P}_{G})
and a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(G)
, the convolution from the right is defined in the usual way:

\rho*\mu:=\int_{G}R_{g^{-1}}^{*}\rho d\mu(g)\quad\text{and}\quad\tau*\mu:=\int_{G}R_{g^{-1}}^{*}\tau d\mu(g).

We will identify the orientations on

with left-invariant orientations on

is the push-forward of

under the multiplication

under the induced map

D(\tau*\mu)=D\tau*\mu
and

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\phi=\{c_{\phi},\tau_{\phi}\}=[[\mu_{\phi},\omega_{\phi}]]\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)
with
d\omega_{\phi}=D\omega_{\phi}
and
\psi=\{c_{\psi},\tau_{\psi}\}=[[\mu_{\psi},\omega_{\psi}]]\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)
with
d\omega_{\psi}=D\omega_{\psi}
be smooth valuations, not necessarily left- or right-invariant.
Then

\displaystyle\tau_{\phi*\psi}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}p_{*}r^{*}(p_{1}^{*}\tau_{\phi}\wedge p_{2}^{*}\tau_{\psi})

\displaystyle\phi*\psi

\displaystyle=[[\mu_{\phi}*\mu_{\psi},\omega_{\phi}*\mu_{\psi}+p_{*}r^{*}(p_{1}^{*}\tau_{\phi}\wedge p_{2}^{*}\omega_{\psi})]].

Proof.

By
[
2
, Proposition 5.5]
, we have
a^{*}\tau_{\phi*\psi}=p_{*}r^{*}(p_{1}^{*}a^{*}\tau_{\phi}\wedge p_{2}^{*}a^{*}\tau_{\psi})
, where
a:\mathbb{P}_{G}\to\mathbb{P}_{G}
is the antipodal map. We can define antipodal maps on
\mathbb{P}_{G}\times\mathbb{P}_{G}
and
(G\times G)\times_{m,\pi}\mathbb{P}_{G}
in the natural way, and then
a
commutes with
p_{1}\times p_{2},r,p
. Since the push-forward depends on the orientation of the fiber, which is of dimension
n
, we have
p_{*}a^{*}=(-1)^{n}a^{*}p_{*}
and the first equation follows.

For the second equation, we suppose first that
\mu_{\psi}=0
and use an idea from
[
2
, Proposition 5.7]
. Let
\eta:=\phi*\psi
and
\eta^{\prime}:=[[0,p_{*}r^{*}(p_{1}^{*}\tau_{\phi}\wedge p_{2}^{*}\omega_{\psi})]]
. We want to prove that
\eta=\eta^{\prime}
. We have

\tau_{\eta^{\prime}}=dp_{*}r^{*}(p_{1}^{*}\tau_{\phi}\wedge p_{2}^{*}\omega_{\psi})=(-1)^{n}p_{*}r^{*}(p_{1}^{*}\tau_{\phi}\wedge p_{2}^{*}\tau_{\psi})=\tau_{\eta}.

Hence
\tau_{\eta-\eta^{\prime}}=0
, which means that
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
is a multiple of the Euler characteristic. The support of
\eta
is contained in
m(\spt\phi\times\spt\psi)
by
[
2
, Proposition 5.5]
, and inspecting the definition of
\eta^{\prime}
we see that also
\spt\eta^{\prime}\subset m(\spt\phi\times\spt\psi)
. If
m(\spt\phi\times\spt\psi)\neq G
, then we conclude that
\eta=\eta^{\prime}
. The general case follows by using a partition of unity argument to reduce to the previous case.

Let us finally consider the case
\mu_{\psi}\neq 0,\omega_{\psi}=0
. Then
\tau_{\psi}=\pi^{*}\mu_{\psi}
. The map

(\mathrm{id}\times\pi)\circ r:(G\times G)\times_{m,\pi}\mathbb{P}_{G}\to\mathbb{P}_{G}\times G

is a diffeomorphism whose inverse is given by
(g,[\xi],h)\mapsto(g,h,[R_{h}^{*}\xi])
. It is orientation preserving if and only if
n
is even, see (
22
), hence the pull-back and the push-forward under the inverse map differ by a factor of
(-1)^{n}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\tau_{\phi*\psi}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}p_{*}r^{*}(p_{1}^{*}\tau_{\phi}\wedge p_{2}^{*}\pi^{*}\mu_{\psi})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}p_{*}((\mathrm{id}\times\pi)\circ r)^{*}(\hat{p}_{1}^{*}\tau_{\phi}\wedge\hat{p}_{2}^{*}\mu_{\psi})

\displaystyle=(p\circ((\mathrm{id}\times\pi)\circ r)^{-1})_{*}(\hat{p}_{1}^{*}\tau_{\phi}\wedge\hat{p}_{2}^{*}\mu_{\psi}).

Note that
p\circ((\mathrm{id}\times\pi)\circ r)^{-1}(g,[\xi],h)=\tilde{R}_{h}(g,[\xi])
, hence
\tau_{\phi*\psi}=\tau_{\phi}*\mu_{\psi}
. Now we argue as above. We let
\eta=\phi*\psi
and
\eta^{\prime}:=[[\mu_{\phi}*\mu_{\psi},\omega_{\phi}*\mu_{\psi}]]
. Then
\tau_{\eta^{\prime}}=(\pi^{*}\mu_{\phi}+D\omega_{\phi})*\mu_{\psi}=\tau_{\phi}*\mu_{\psi}=\tau_{\eta}
. Looking at the support of
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
as above shows that
\eta=\eta^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\tau\in\Omega^{p}_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}},\zeta\in\Omega_{\ori}^{q}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}
, and

\theta:=p_{*}r^{*}(p_{1}^{*}\tau\wedge p_{2}^{*}\zeta)\in\Omega_{\ori}^{p+q-n}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}.

\tilde{\theta}_{j}=(-1)^{nq}\int_{G}\Ad_{g}^{*}\tau dg*\zeta.

We assume for simplicity that
G
admits a bi-invariant orientation, which we fix to trivialize
\ori(\mathfrak{g})

\tau

(G\times G)\times_{m,\pi}\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})

We need to prove that

(23)

) in a point, say
(e,[\xi])

Let us fix a basis

of
\mathfrak{g}

(recall that
\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
is identified with

via

). We have

and claim that

Here, similarly as before, the tangent space of

in a point

Fix

\displaystyle\tilde{\theta}^{k,l}_{j}=\epsilon_{6}\sum_{K}\iota_{e_{K}^{\sharp}}\tilde{\tau}_{k}\wedge i_{e_{K}^{*}}\tilde{\zeta}_{l}=\epsilon_{6}\hat{S}_{k+l-j}(\tilde{\tau}_{k}\otimes\tilde{\zeta}_{l}).

Using
\epsilon(I,I^{c})\epsilon(I^{c},I)=(-1)^{(q-l)(n-q+l)}

\epsilon(J,J^{c})\epsilon(J^{c},J)=(-1)^{l(j-l)}

\epsilon_{6}=(-1)^{nq}\epsilon_{k,l,j}^{p,q}
. Summing over
k,l
and observing that
\tilde{\theta}^{k,l}_{j}=0

The general case follows by averaging over

, noting that for left-invariant forms
\tau,\zeta
we have
(\Ad_{g}^{*}\tau)*\zeta=\tau*\zeta
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A
.

Let
\phi=\{c_{\phi},\tau_{\phi}\}\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}
and
\psi=\{c_{\psi},\tau_{\psi}\}=[[\mu_{\psi},\omega_{\psi}]]\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}
with

. By compactness, we may assume that
\omega_{\psi}
is left-invariant. Combining Proposition
5.1
and Proposition
5.2
with

we find

Similarly, using Proposition
5.1

, where

is the Haar probability measure, gives

\displaystyle c_{\phi*\psi}

\displaystyle=\pi_{*}(\omega_{\phi*\psi})

\displaystyle=\pi_{*}(\omega_{\phi}*\mu_{\psi}+p_{*}r^{*}(p_{1}^{*}\tau_{\phi}\wedge p_{2}^{*}\omega_{\psi}))

\displaystyle=c_{\phi}\mu(\psi)+\pi_{*}(\tau_{\phi}*\omega_{\psi}).

∎

Let us finish this section with a basic relation between the two natural pairings induced by the product and convolution.

Lemma 5.3
.

For
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)
one has

\int_{G}(\phi\cdot\psi)=\phi\ast\mathrm{Inv}^{*}\psi(e).

Proof.

Let
\Delta:G\to G\times G
denote the diagonal embedding. Then

\displaystyle\phi\ast\mathrm{Inv}^{*}\psi(e)

\displaystyle=(\phi\boxtimes\mathrm{Inv}^{*}\psi)(m^{-1}(e))

\displaystyle=(\phi\boxtimes\mathrm{Inv}^{*}\psi)((\id\times\mathrm{Inv})\circ\Delta(G))

\displaystyle=(\phi\boxtimes\psi)(\Delta(G))

∎

6.
Existence of bi-invariant valuations

A well-known result of Milnor
[
33
, Lemma 7.5]
asserts that a connected Lie group admits a bi-invariant riemannian metric if and only if it is the cartesian product of a compact Lie group and a vector space. The intrinsic volumes (or Lipschitz–Killing valuations) of such a metric then constitute examples of smooth bi-invariant valuations, as is the case for the coefficients
\phi_{k}
of the Taylor series for the volume of an
\epsilon
-tube of
X\in\mathcal{P}(G)
,
\vol(X_{\epsilon})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}(X)\epsilon^{k}
. We will now show that in fact there are no other groups admitting a bi-invariant smooth valuation distinct from linear combinations of the Euler characteristic and the Haar measure.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
G
be any real Lie group and
V
a finite-dimensional representation of
G
. If
v\in V
is non-zero and
Gv
has
0
as its limit point, then
Gv
is unbounded.

Proof.

Let
Z\subset V
be the smallest
G
-invariant affine subspace containing
v
. As
0
is a limit point of
Gv
,
Z
is linear. Assuming
Gv
is bounded, we conclude that the closure of the convex hull of
Gv
, denoted
K
, is a compact convex body in
Z
, which by construction has full dimension in
Z
. The John ellipsoid of the convex hull of
K\cup(-K)
then defines a
G
-invariant euclidean norm
x\mapsto|x|
in
Z
. Since
|v|\neq 0
, it cannot happen that
g_{j}v\to 0
for a sequence
g_{j}\in G
, as
|g_{j}v|=|v|>0
. This contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
G
be any real Lie group and
V
a finite-dimensional representation of
G
. If
V
has no invariant euclidean structure, then there is an open dense set of vectors
v\in V
with
Gv
unbounded.

Proof.

Take a
G
-invariant centrally symmetric convex body
K
of maximal dimension. Then
\dim K<\dim V
, since otherwise the John ellipsoid of
K
would define a
G
-invariant euclidean structure on
V
. Suppose that a vector
v\in V\setminus\mathrm{span}K
has a bounded orbit. Then the convex hull of the closure of
Gv\cup K\cup(-Gv)
is a centrally symmetric,
G
-invariant convex body of dimension strictly larger than
\dim K
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
G
be a connected real Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra. Then
\Ad_{G}v
does not contain
0
in its closure for some open dense set
U
of vectors
v\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

Choose a maximal proper ideal
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, so that
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
is either simple or abelian and one-dimensional. Let
\pi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{a}
be the projection.

In case
\mathfrak{a}=\mathbb{R}
, it holds for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
that
[u+\mathfrak{h},v+\mathfrak{h}]=0
, so that
[u,v]\in\mathfrak{h}
. Since
G
is connected, we have
\pi(\Ad_{g}v)=\pi(v)
for all
v\in\mathfrak{g}
,
g\in G
, and so we may take
U=\{v\mid\pi(v)\neq 0\}=\mathfrak{g}\setminus\mathfrak{h}
.

In case
\mathfrak{a}
is simple, let
B
be its Killing form, which is non-degenerate. Since
\pi([x,v])=[\pi(x),\pi(v)]
for all
x,v\in\mathfrak{g}
, it holds that

B(\pi(\Ad_{g}v))=B(\Ad_{g}(\pi(v)))=B(\pi(v))

for all
g\in G
,
v\in\mathfrak{g}
, and therefore we may take
U=\{v\mid B(\pi(v))\neq 0\}
.
∎

We can now proceed to prove the first part of Theorem
B
. In what follows,
\vol_{G}
denotes some non-zero left-invariant Haar measure on
G
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
G
be a connected real Lie group of dimension at least
2
. Then
G
admits a smooth bi-invariant valuation
\phi\notin\Span\{\chi,\vol_{G}\}
if and only if
G
admits a bi-invariant riemannian metric.

Proof.

If
G
admits a bi-invariant metric, its first intrinsic volume
\mu_{1}
is a bi-invariant smooth valuation as desired.

Assume now that
G
does not admit a bi-invariant metric, and let
\phi\notin\Span\{\chi,\vol_{G}\}
be a non-zero bi-invariant valuation. Assume first that
G
is connected. We proceed to arrive at a contradiction.

Subtracting a multiple of
\chi
, and of
\vol_{G}
if
G
is unimodular, we may assume
\phi\in\mathcal{W}_{j}(G)\setminus\mathcal{W}_{j+1}(G)
for some
1\leq j\leq n-1
. Any fixed choice of orientation on
G
is evidently invariant under the adjoint action, yielding an equivariant identification
\Dens(\mathfrak{g})=\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The principal symbol of
\phi
is an invariant element of
\Gamma(G,\Val^{\infty}_{j}(TG))
, and its value at the unit element defines a non-zero valuation
\psi\in\Val_{j}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
invariant under the adjoint action of
G
. Applying the Alesker–Fourier isomorphism, we get a non-zero element

\mathbb{F}\psi\in\Val_{n-j}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g})=\Val_{n-j}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

The
n
-form
\tau=\tau_{\mathbb{F}\psi}
of
\mathbb{F}\psi
is a non-zero
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}
-invariant element of

\displaystyle\Omega^{j}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}),\wedge^{n-j}\mathfrak{g})\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Gamma(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}),\wedge^{j}(\xi^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}/\xi)^{*}\otimes\wedge^{n-j}\mathfrak{g})\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Gamma(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}),\wedge^{j}(\xi\otimes\xi^{\perp})\otimes\wedge^{n-j}\mathfrak{g})\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Gamma(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}),\xi^{\otimes j}\otimes\wedge^{j}\xi^{\perp}\otimes\wedge^{j}\mathfrak{g}^{*}),

where in the last step we used the Hodge star isomorphism
\ast:\wedge^{n-j}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\wedge^{j}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Since
\tau
is closed and vertical we have in fact

\tau\in\Gamma(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}),\xi^{\otimes j}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\wedge^{j}\xi^{\perp})),

see
[
26
, Lemma 4.8]
.

Let
\eta\in\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g})
be a line such that
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}v
is unbounded and
0\notin\overline{\mathrm{Ad}_{G}v}
for any non-zero

. Those assumptions hold by Lemmas
6.2
and

6.3
for a dense set of lines
\eta
.

Write
\tau_{\eta}=c(\eta)v^{\otimes j}\otimes Q
, where
v\in\eta,v\neq 0
,
Q\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\wedge^{j}\eta^{\perp})
,
Q\neq 0
, and
c(\eta)\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume
c(\eta)\neq 0
.

Take a sequence
g_{i}\in G
such that
\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{i}}v\to\infty
. By the smoothness and
\Ad_{G}
-invariance of
\tau
, it must hold that
\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{i}}Q\to 0
.

By Lemma
6.1
, there is a sequence
h_{i}\in G
such that
\mathrm{Ad}_{h_{i}}Q\to\infty
in
\Sym^{2}(\wedge^{j}\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. Again since
\tau
is smooth,
\mathrm{Ad}_{h_{i}}v\to 0
, a contradiction.
It follows that
c(\eta)=0
for a dense set of
\eta
, and so
\tau=0
. This contradiction concludes the proof for connected
G
.
∎

To complete the proof of Theorem
B
, we will need two more auxiliary statements.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
G
be a connected compact Lie group of dimension at least
2
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\dim\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}<\infty
.

(ii)

The coadjoint action of
G
on
\mathbb{P}_{*}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is transitive.

(iii)

G
is isomorphic to either
S^{3}
or
\mathrm{SO}(3)
.

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii): Suppose that the coadjoint action is not transitive on
\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. By
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
the space of
\Val_{1}(\mathfrak{g})^{\Ad G}
is infinite-dimensional. Using the results and the notation from
[

, Section 1.5]
, we can extend an element of this space as a bi-invariant section of

. We thus find a smooth valuation in

whose image in
\underline{\Val}_{1}(TG)
is the given section. By averaging with respect to the left and right translations on

, we finally get a bi-invariant valuation in
W_{1}(G)
whose image in
\Val_{1}(\mathfrak{g})
is the element we started with. Hence the subspace

is infinite-dimensional.

(ii)
\implies
(i): Under the assumption (ii),
G
admits a bi-invariant
riemannian metric such that

acts transitively on the unit tangent bundle. It is well known that the space of smooth invariant valuations is finite-dimensional in this case, see for instance
[
1
, Section 10]
.

(iii)
\implies
(ii): This is clear.

(ii)
\implies
(iii): As above, there exists a bi-invariant metric on
G
. By assumption, the adjoint action of

is transitive on the unit sphere in

. Let

be the kernel of the adjoint action. Then we obtain an effective and transitive action of

on the unit sphere in

.

The connected groups

that act transitively and effectively on the unit sphere in some euclidean vector space

are known
[
22
,
34
]
. The possible pairs

are

\displaystyle(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathrm{SO}(n)),(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathrm{U}(n)),(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathrm{SU}(n)),

\displaystyle(\mathbb{H}^{n},\mathrm{Sp}(n)),(\mathbb{H}^{n},\mathrm{Sp}(n)\cdot\mathrm{U}(1)),(\mathbb{H}^{n},\mathrm{Sp}(n)\cdot\mathrm{Sp}(1)),

for

, and the three exceptional cases

(\mathbb{R}^{7},\mathrm{G}^{2}),(\mathbb{R}^{8},\mathrm{Spin}(7)),(\mathbb{R}^{16},\mathrm{Spin}(9)).

In our situation, we have
\dim V=\dim G\geq\dim G^{\prime}=\dim G-\dim H
and
\dim G\geq 2
. Noting that
\mathrm{Sp}(1)\cdot\mathrm{U}(1)\cong\mathrm{U}(2)
and
\mathrm{SU}(2)\cong\mathrm{Sp}(1)
, the only pairs from the list satisfying these dimension bounds are

(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathrm{SO}(3)),(\mathbb{C}^{2},\mathrm{U}(2)),(\mathbb{C}^{2},\mathrm{SU}(2)).

In the case
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathrm{SO}(3))
,

is a finite cover of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
. Since
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{SO}(3))=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, there are only the two possibilities

and

.

It remains to exclude the two other cases. The kernel of the action of

on

is the diagonal embedding of

, hence

acts transitively and effectively on the unit tangent bundle, i.e.,

is a compact isotropic manifold of dimension

Isotropic manifolds

are classified in

,

. The only compact examples of dimension

are

, and in all three cases

is the identity component of the respective isometry group. Since

is non-orientable, it cannot be diffeomorphic to a Lie group. On a compact Lie group

, the vanishing of the first cohomology implies the vanishing of the second cohomology and the non-vanishing of the third cohomology group
[

, hence

and
\mathbb{HP}^{1}\cong S^{4}
cannot be diffeomorphic to Lie groups either.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

The first statement is just Theorem

. For the second statement, let us assume that
G
admits a bi-invariant riemannian metric. By Milnor’s result
[

]
we have

, where

is a compact Lie group and

. If

, then we may apply Proposition

to deduce that

if and only if

.
If

, consider the covering map

, and note that the pull-back map

∎

7.
Convolution of smooth bi-invariant valuations on Lie groups

In this section we define the convolution of bi-invariant smooth valuations on a general unimodular Lie group, thus unifying the Alesker–Bernig convolution on compact Lie groups with the Bernig–Fu convolution of translation-invariant valuations on a linear space.

7.1.
Haar measures and forms

We first verify that for a unimodular Lie group
G
, the line
\mathcal{M}(G)^{G}
of Haar measures is naturally a direct summand of the space of left-invariant valuations. To this end, we define the space of
measure-free
valuations
\mathcal{V}_{mf}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
to consist of all valuations
\{c,\tau\}{\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})}
for which
\tau_{0}=0
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
G
be a unimodular Lie group. Then

\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{mf}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*}).

Proof.

Consider
\tau_{0}\in\Omega^{0}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\ori(\mathfrak{g}))\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\cong C^{\infty}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))
for any left-invariant valuation
\phi=\{c,\tau\}
. By Corollary
3.3
,
d\widetilde{\tau}_{0}=\partial\widetilde{\tau}_{1}=0
. Thus
\tau_{0}
is a constant which will be denoted by
\mu(\phi)
. Depending on the context, we can think of
\mu(\phi)
as an element of
\mathbb{C}
or of
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. Writing
\phi=\mu(\phi)+(\phi-\mu(\phi))
concludes the proof.
∎

We remark that when restricted to bi-invariant valuations, the projection
\mu:\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\to\mathbb{C}
is a character with respect to the convolution defined below, i.e., it satisfies
\mu(\phi*\psi)=\mu(\phi)\mu(\psi)
, see Proposition
7.9
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
G
be a unimodular Lie group. If
\tau\in\Omega^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
satisfies
d\tau=0
and
\pi_{*}\tau=0
, then there exists
\omega\in\Omega^{p-1}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
such that

d\omega=\tau-\tau_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{*}\omega=0.

Proof.

We will inductively construct forms
\tilde{\omega}_{k}\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-p+k+1}\mathfrak{g})
such that
\omega=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}*\widetilde{\omega}_{k}
has the desired properties. We have
\tau=\sum_{k}\tau_{k}
with
\tau_{k}\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{p-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*})\otimes\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n}\mathfrak{g}
and
\tilde{\tau}_{k}\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-p+k}\mathfrak{g})
. By Corollary
3.3
, the equation
d\omega=\tau-\tau_{0}
is equivalent to the system of equations

d\tilde{\omega}_{k}+(-1)^{n-p}\partial\tilde{\omega}_{k+1}=\tilde{\tau}_{k+1},\quad 0\leq k\leq n-1.

First, for
k=n-1
we set
\tilde{\omega}_{n-1}=0
. Now suppose that we have already constructed
\tilde{\omega}_{k+1}
for some
k\leq n-2
. Since
d\tau=0
, Corollary
3.3
implies

\displaystyle d(\tilde{\tau}_{k+1}-(-1)^{n-p}\partial\tilde{\omega}_{k+1})

\displaystyle=d\tilde{\tau}_{k+1}-(-1)^{n-p}\partial(\tilde{\tau}_{k+2}-(-1)^{n-p}\partial\tilde{\omega}_{k+2})

\displaystyle=d\tilde{\tau}_{k+1}+(-1)^{n-p+1}\partial\tilde{\tau}_{k+2}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{d\tau})_{k+2}

\displaystyle=0.

We claim that the closed form
\tilde{\tau}_{k+1}-(-1)^{n-p}\partial\tilde{\omega}_{k+1}\in\Omega^{k+1}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-p+k+1}\mathfrak{g})
is actually exact. Indeed, if
k<n-2
, then
H^{k+1}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))=H^{k+1}(S^{n-1})=0
. If
k=n-2
, then
H^{n-1}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))=H^{n-1}(S^{n-1})=\mathbb{R}
, but we moreover have

\int_{\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})}(\tilde{\tau}_{n-1}-(-1)^{n-p}\partial\tilde{\omega}_{n-1})=\int_{\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})}\tilde{\tau}_{n-1}=0

since
\pi_{*}\tau=0
and
\tilde{\omega}_{n-1}=0
. It follows that
\tilde{\tau}_{k+1}-(-1)^{n-p}\partial\tilde{\omega}_{k+1}=d\tilde{\omega}_{k}
for some
\tilde{\omega}_{k}\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{P}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{n-p+k+1}\mathfrak{g})
which finishes the induction step. We thus have
d\omega=\tau-\tau_{0}
. Since
\tilde{\omega}_{n-1}=0
, we also get
\pi_{*}\omega=0
.
∎

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
G
be a unimodular Lie group. Every left-invariant valuation
\phi\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
can be represented as
\phi=[[\sigma,\omega]]
with
\sigma\in\Omega^{n}_{\ori}(G)^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and
\omega\in\Omega^{n-1}_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
such that
d\omega=D\omega
. Moreover, for each such choice of
\sigma,\omega
, it holds that
\mu(\phi)=\sigma
.

Proof.

Let
\phi=\{\zeta,\tau\}
with
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(G)^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\tau\in\Omega^{n}_{\ori}(\mathbb{P}_{G})^{L_{G}}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. The Euler characteristic
\chi
can be written as
[[\sigma^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}]]
with
\sigma^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}
left invariant (just use Chern’s construction with a left-invariant riemannian metric). Then
D\omega^{\prime}+\pi^{*}\sigma^{\prime}=0,\pi_{*}\omega^{\prime}=1
. Subtracting a multiple of
\chi
we may thus assume that
\zeta=0
. We take
\omega
as in Lemma
7.2
with
d\omega=\tau-\tau_{0}
, and
\sigma
such that
\pi^{*}\sigma=\tau_{0}
. Then
d\omega=D\omega
,
D\omega+\pi^{*}\sigma=\tau
, and
\pi_{*}\omega=0
, hence
\phi=[[\sigma,\omega]]
.

For the last part observe that by Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, we have
(d\omega)_{0}=0
, and so
\pi^{*}\mu(\phi)=\tau_{0}
must equal
\pi^{*}\sigma
.
∎

7.2.
From convolution on compact Lie groups to general convolution

By Theorem

Lemma 7.4

\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})

Q_{\Gamma}:=q_{\Gamma}^{*}\otimes\sigma_{\Gamma}:\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G_{\Gamma})^{T_{\Gamma}}\to\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{V}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*}).

Then
\ast_{\Gamma}
on
\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}\otimes\Dens(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is independent of
\Gamma
. Similarly, the induced product

is independent of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}
be lattices. For the first statement we ought to show that

is an isomorphism of convolution algebras. Denoting

and
q=(q_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{*})^{-1}\circ q_{\Gamma}^{*}:\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G_{\Gamma})\to\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G_{\Gamma^{\prime}})
, we have
Q=\delta q
.

Under the natural identifications, we have
\tau_{q\phi}=\tau_{\phi},\omega_{q\phi}=\omega_{\phi},c_{q\phi}=c_{\phi}
and similarly for
\psi
. However, the isomorphism (
3
) makes use of the Haar measure. Since

maps the Haar probability measure
\nu_{\Gamma}
to
\delta^{-1}\nu_{\Gamma^{\prime}}

\tilde{\tau}_{q\phi}=\delta^{-1}\tilde{\tau}_{\phi},\tilde{\omega}_{q\phi}=\delta^{-1}\tilde{\omega}_{\phi}
etc. By Definition

we have

\tau_{q\phi}*\tau_{q\psi}=\delta^{-1}\tau_{\phi}*\tau_{\psi},\quad\tau_{q\phi}*\omega_{q\psi}=\delta^{-1}\tau_{\phi}*\omega_{\psi}.

Clearly

\mu(q\psi)=\int_{G_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}\psi=\delta^{-1}\int_{G_{\Gamma}}\psi=\delta^{-1}\mu(\psi).

Theorem

as required.

This completes the proof of the first statement. The proof of the second statement is completely analogous.
∎

is independent of the choice of lattice
\Gamma
. To prove the independence of the decomposition into the compact and linear part, we first observe that the formula in terms of differential forms established by Theorem

It is straightforward to extend the convolution of Lemma

Lemma 7.7

Let

If the restriction of a bi-invariant valuation to

For

is right-invariant if and only if

is
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}

∎

It is easy to construct explicit examples where the restriction is not surjective. For instance, if

, where

acts on

Let

Finally, suppose that

(iv) First note that since

It is well known that the space

Lemma 8.1
.

The convolution on

Proof.

Since the group inverse

Let us denote
\chi_{X}(Y)=\chi(X\cap Y)

\displaystyle\nu_{0}

\displaystyle=\chi,

\displaystyle\nu_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gr_{2}(S^{3})}\chi_{E}dE,

\displaystyle\nu_{2}

Lemma 8.2
.

One has

\displaystyle\mu_{0}

\displaystyle=\nu_{0},

(32)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=\pi\nu_{1}+\pi\nu_{3},

(33)

\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=2\pi\nu_{2},

(34)

\displaystyle\mu_{3}

\displaystyle=2\pi^{2}\nu_{3}.

In particular, the Crofton valuations

Proof.

First, (

are eigenvectors of the Euler–Verdier involution with eigenvalue 1 and

\displaystyle\nu_{1}

\displaystyle=a\mu_{1}+b\mu_{3},

\displaystyle\nu_{2}

We will use the template method to determine the constants. To this end, recall that for any

\displaystyle\chi(S^{i})

\displaystyle=1+(-1)^{i},

\displaystyle\mu_{i}(S^{i})

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group. If
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}(G)^{G\times G}
are eigenvectors of the Euler–Verdier involution with eigenvalues
\epsilon_{\phi},\epsilon_{\psi}
, respectively, then
\phi*\psi
is an eigenvector with eigenvalue
(-1)^{\dim G}\epsilon_{\phi}\epsilon_{\psi}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\phi\boxtimes\psi
has eigenvalue
\epsilon_{\phi}\epsilon_{\psi}
. Under the push-forward by the multiplication map
m:G\times G\to G
, the Euler–Verdier eigenvalue shifts by
(-1)^{\dim(G\times G)-\dim G}=(-1)^{\dim G}
.
∎

Uniform Anisotropic Regularity and Low Mach Number Limit of Non-isentropic Ideal MHD Equations with a Perfectly Conducting Boundary
Qiangchang JU Jiawei WANG Junyan ZHANG

Abstract
We prove the low Mach number limit of non-isentropic ideal magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations with general initial data in the half-space whose boundary satisfies the perfectly conducting wall condition. By observing a special structure contributed by Lorentz force in vorticity analysis, we establish uniform estimates in suitable anisotropic Sobolev spaces with weights of Mach number determined by the number of material derivatives. We also observe that the entropy has the enhanced regularity in the direction of the magnetic field. These two observations help us get rid of the loss of derivatives and weights of Mach number in vorticity analysis caused by the simultaneous appearance of entropy, general initial data and the magnetic field, which is one of the major difficulties that do not appear in Euler equations or the isentropic problems. By utilizing the technique of Alinhac good unknowns, the anti-symmetric structure is preserved in the tangential estimates for the system differenetiated by high-order material derivatives.



Keywords
: Compressible ideal MHD, Low Mach number limit, Perfectly conducting wall.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the scaled non-isentropic compressible ideal magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations

1.1
An overview of previous results

As is well-known in fluid dynamics, see for example in Majda’s book
[
22
, Chapter 2.4]
, we can formally derive the incompressible fluid equations from the compressible ones, which corresponds to passing to the limit in certain dimensionless form as the Mach number goes to zero. In particular, for inviscid fluids, taking low Mach number limit is a singular limit process of a hyperbolic system with large parameter, for example the coefficients
\varepsilon^{-1}
in (
1.8
). To study such singular limit, we shall classify the initial data to be two types.

•

Well-prepared initial data:
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=O(\varepsilon^{k}),~\nabla q_{0}=O(\varepsilon^{k})
for
k\geq 1
. In such case, the compressible data is exactly a slight perturbation of a given incompressible data. Uniform estimates in low Mach regime immedately give the strong convergence thanks to the uniform boundedness of first-order time derivatives.

•

General initial data:
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=O(1),~\nabla q_{0}=O(1)
. In such case, the compressible data includes a large perturbation which is actually a highly oscillatory acoustic wave that propagates with a speed of
O(1/\varepsilon)
. One has to filter such acoustic wave and find suitable function spaces for the
strong
convergence.

The low Mach number limit of Euler equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{T}^{d}
or a fixed domain has been studied extensively.

The aim of this paper is to rigorously justify the singular limit in low Mach number regime for ideal MHD with the perfectly conducting wall condition
in the non-isentropic case with general initial data
. The framework in this paper can cover the existing works and does not require extra boundary constraints as in
[

The proof is based on the combination of several key observations and techniques: a special structure in vorticity analysis that illustrates the “mismatch” between the anisotropic norms and the standard Sobolev norms, enhanced regularity of the entropy in the direction of the magnetic field, the usage of material derivative

instead of

when defining the energy functional and the application of “modified Alinhac good unknowns” to overcome the difficulty brought by the anisotropy of the function spaces.

2
Difficulties and strategies

Before going to the detailed proofs, we briefly discuss the main difficulties in this problem and our strategies to overcome these difficulties. In particular, we focus on the difficulties that do not appear in neither of Euler equations, isentropic MHD equations or the case of well-prepared initial data.

The system of compressible ideal MHD with the perfectly conducting wall conditions is a first-order hyperbolic system with characteristic boundary conditions of constant multiplicity, for which there is a potential of normal derivative loss. This also happens for Euler equations and elastodynamics inside a rigid wall with the slip boundary condition, but the vorticity for those two equations can be controlled in standard Sobolev spaces (see Schochet
[
28
]
and the third author
[
42
]
), which could compensate the loss of normal derivative. However, for compressible ideal MHD, one encounters a loss of normal derivative in vorticity analysis caused by the simultaneous appearance of compressibility and the magnetic field. That is exactly one has to use the anisotropic Sobolev spaces to prove the local existence.

2.1
Motivation to define
E(t)
: a special structure in vorticity analysis

We are inspired by a special structure of Lorentz force in vorticity analysis to define the energy functional
E(t)
as in (
1.13
). Let us take the estimate of
\|\nabla\times u\|_{3}^{2}
in the control of
\|u\|_{4}^{2}
for example.

2.1.1
Substitution of the coefficient

In the case of non-isentropic problem with general initial data, one has to replace the coefficient
\rho(\varepsilon q,S)
by
\rho_{0}:=\rho(0,S)
to derive the evolution equation of the vorticity and the current, namely,

D_{t}(\rho_{0}u)-\rho_{0}(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)B=-\underbrace{\nabla(\varepsilon^{-1}q+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2})}_{\text{curl-free}}+\underbrace{\frac{\rho-\rho_{0}}{\rho}}_{=O(\varepsilon)}\nabla(\varepsilon^{-1}q+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}),

as stated in Métivier-Schochet
[
23
]
. Or else, there must be terms like
\partial^{\alpha}\rho(\nabla\times D_{t}u)
whose coefficient
\partial^{\alpha}\rho=O(\varepsilon)\partial^{\alpha}q+O(1)\partial^{\alpha}S
is
O(1)
size and so this term is not uniformly bounded. The advantage of such substituiton is that the unbounded terms are avoided and the extra-generated term has small coefficient and its leading-order part is curl-free. Then it is easy to reproduce the control of
\nabla\times(u,B)
from that of
\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u,\rho_{0}B)
because
\rho_{0}
only depends on
S
whose estimate is straightforward.

2.1.2
A weighted anisotropic structure contributed by the Lorentz force

In the analysis of
\|\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\|_{3}^{2}
, we must encounter a fifth-order term

\mathcal{K}:=-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\nabla\cdot u)\,\mathrm{d}x,

which is uncontrollable in the setting of
H^{4}
. But if we insert the continuity equation
\nabla\cdot u=-\varepsilon aD_{t}q
, commute
\nabla\times
with
D_{t}
and insert the momentum equation
-\nabla q=\varepsilon(\rho D_{t}u+B\times(\nabla\times B))
, we can see that

\displaystyle\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\nabla\cdot u)=-\varepsilon^{2}a\rho^{-1}B\times\left[B\times D_{t}(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\right]+\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{0}aB\times(\partial^{3}D_{t}^{2}u)+\text{low-order terms}.

On the right side, the first term contributes to an energy term
-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}a\rho^{-1}|\varepsilon B\times(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
that gives the regularity of the Lorentz force, while the second term exhibits a “weighted” anisotropic structure that indicates us to trade one normal derivative (in the curl operator
\nabla\times
) for the second-order tangential derivative
\varepsilon^{2}D_{t}^{2}
. As
\varepsilon\to 0
, this anisotropic part converges to 0, and so the incompressible problem can be studied in standard Sobolev spaces. For details, we refer to Section
3.4.1
.

2.1.3
The whole reduction procedures

Apart from the vorticity estimates, we also need to consider the divergence estimates. The continuity equation indicates us to trade one normal derivative in
\nabla\cdot u
for one tangential derivative
\varepsilon D_{t}q
. Then for the pressure
q
, we can always invoke the momentum equation
-\nabla q=\varepsilon(\rho D_{t}u+B\times(\nabla\times B))
to convert a spatial derivative to
\varepsilon D_{t}u
and the estimate of current
\nabla\times B
until there is no spatial derivative falling on
q
and no spatial derivative falling on
u
. The full details are referred to Section
3.4.2
.

It should be remarked that all tangential derivatives generated in the above reduction procedures are material derivatives, and that explains why we write
D_{t}
instead of
\partial_{t}
when defining
E(t)
. In Section
2.3
, we will see using
D_{t}
instead of
\partial_{t}
is necessary for the non-isentropic problem with general initial data, as opposed to the case of well-prepared data or the isentropic problem in
[
16
,
37
,
35
]
.

2.2
Enhanced “directional” regularity of the entropy

One may notice that we require the directional derivative
(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)S
has the same regularity as the other variables. This is in general incorrect for a first-order hyperbolic system, but it can really be achieved for compressible ideal MHD as long as we assume the initial data of
(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)S
has the corresponding regularity. This fact is easy to prove, as
we observe that
D_{t}
commutes with
(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)
which then leads to
D_{t}((\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)S)=0
and the desired enhanced regularity. In fact, this is even easier to be observed if we study the free-boundary MHD in Lagrangian coordinates, e.g.,
[
19
]
, in which the material derivative becomes
\partial_{t}
and
(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)
becomes
time-independent
!

Such enhanced directional regularity is necessary to control the vorticity and the current. For example, in the analysis of
\|\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\|_{3}
, we must encounter the following two terms

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{3}\nabla\times\left(\underline{(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla\rho_{0})}B\right)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{3}\nabla\times\left(\underline{(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla\rho_{0})}u\right)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\,\mathrm{d}x,

in which the underlined terms essentially require the
H^{4}
regularity of
(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)S
. Such difficulty never appears in the study of Euler equations, isentropic problems, or non-isentropic problems with well-prepared initial data. On the other hand, our observation exactly resolves this issue. Similar argument also applies to the recent work by the second author and the third author
[
38
]
about neo-Hookean elastodynamics.

2.3
Usage of material derivatives and “modified” Alinhac good unknowns

Now, we turn to explain why we shall use the variable-coefficient derivative
D_{t}
instead of the simpler one
\partial_{t}
to define
E(t)
. In fact, this is due to the unboundedness of the first-order time derivatives in the case of general initial data. If we decompose the material derivative
D_{t}
into
\partial_{t}
, tangential spatial derivative
\overline{\partial}{}=\partial_{1}
or
\partial_{2}
and the co-normal part
\omega(x_{3})\partial_{3}
, then we must separately do the tangential estimates for
\mathcal{T}=\partial_{t},\overline{\partial}{},\omega(x_{3})\partial_{3}
as in the case of well-prepared data
[
37
]
. However, this will again produce singular terms such as
((\varepsilon\overline{\partial}{})^{8}\rho)(D_{t}u)
and we cannot substitute
\rho
by
\rho_{0}
as in the vorticity control. To overcome this difficulty, we must ensure every tangential derivative to be the material derivative
D_{t}
such that we could completely eliminate the
O(1)
-size terms
\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}\rho\approx O(\varepsilon)\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}q+O(1)\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}S
by using
D_{t}S=0
.

Under our setting, a new difficulty appears, that is,
D_{t}
does not commute with the
\nabla
. In Lemma
A.3
, we compute the concrete form of
[\partial,D_{t}^{k}]f
and find that each term in this commutator must be a monomial of
(\partial D_{t}^{j}f)
and
(\partial D_{t}^{m}u)
for some
j,m\in\mathbb{Z}
. What’s more difficult is that the top-order commutator, namely
[\partial,D_{t}^{8}]f
, contains
(\partial D_{t}^{7}u)(\partial f)
and
8(\partial u)(\partial D_{t}^{7}f)
whose
L^{2}(\Omega)
norms cannot be directly controlled when
\partial=\partial_{3}
. To overcome this difficulty, we adopt the idea of Alinhac good unknowns
[
2
]
, which reveals that the essential leading-order part in
D_{t}^{8}\partial_{i}f
is not simply
\partial_{i}D_{t}^{8}f
but actually the
\partial_{i}
derivative of the Alinhac good unknowns (denoted by
\mathbf{F}^{8}
)

D_{t}^{8}\partial_{i}f\neq\partial_{i}D_{t}^{8}f+L^{2}(\Omega)\text{-controllable terms}\xRightarrow{~~~~~~}D_{t}^{8}\partial_{i}f=\partial_{i}\mathbf{F}^{8}+L^{2}(\Omega)\text{-controllable terms}.

In other words, the core idea of using Alinhac good unknowns is to rewrite the uncontrollable terms to be the form
\partial_{i}(L^{2}\text{-controllable})
terms and merge the terms in the parenthesis into the leading-order term, such that the covariance of the system is still preserved after being differentiated by variable-coefficient derivatives. In view of the singular limit problems, this step is used to preserve the anti-symmetric structure
E_{0}(U)\partial_{t}U+\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla U=f
for the differentiated system.

Under the setting of standard Sobolev spaces,
\mathbf{F}^{8}
is defined to be
D_{t}^{8}f-D_{t}^{7}u\cdot\nabla f
, see for example
[
9
]
. Under the setting of anisotropic Sobolev spaces, the third author
[
19
]
observed that the merged terms must be modified according as the variable
f
. After careful and delicate calculations, the “modified” Alinhac good unknowns used in this paper are (see also
[
19
, Section 5.5]
)

\mathbf{U}_{i}^{8}:=D_{t}^{8}u_{i}-9D_{t}^{7}u\cdot\nabla u_{i},\quad\quad\mathbf{Q}^{8}:=D_{t}^{8}\check{q}-D_{t}^{7}u\cdot\nabla\check{q},~~\check{q}:=q+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|B|^{2}.

Such difference is due to the fact that we must preserve the divergence structure for the velocity but there is no such structure for the pressure. We refer the details to Section
3.3
.

3
Uniform energy estimates

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which will be split into several parts:
L^{2}
estimates, entropy estimates, tangential estimates for material derivatives and the reduction of normal derivatives via div-curl analysis.

3.1
L^{2}
estimate

First, we establish
L^{2}
-energy estimate for (
1.8
).

Proposition 3.1
.

We define the
L^{2}
energy
E_{0}(t)
by

E_{0}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}aq^{2}+\rho|u|^{2}+|B|^{2}+S^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Then it satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}E_{0}(t)}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\leq P(E_{4}(t)).

(3.1)

Invoking the momentum equation and integrate by parts, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}aq^{2}+\rho|u|^{2}+|B|^{2}+S^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}a+\nabla\cdot(au))q^{2}+\nabla\cdot u(|B|^{2}+S^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x-\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot(qu)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}(B\cdot\nabla B)\cdot u+(B\cdot\nabla u)\cdot B\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\nabla|B|^{2}\cdot u+(B\cdot B)\nabla\cdot u\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\varepsilon\partial_{q}aD_{t}q+a\nabla\cdot u)q^{2}+\nabla\cdot u(|B|^{2}+S^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x,

(3.2)

which give the desired energy estimate. This also implies that

\displaystyle\|(q,u,B,S)\|_{0}^{2}\lesssim E_{4}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}P(E_{4}(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.3)

3.2
Enhanced regularity of the entropy

We now verify that the directional derivative
\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla
commutes with the material derivative
D_{t}
. This property leads to the enhanced regularity of the entropy in the direction of the magnetic field.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(q,u,B,S)
be a solution to system (
1.8
), then the following equality holds:

[\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla,D_{t}]=0.

(3.4)

Proof.

By using equations of
\rho
and
B
, we have that

\displaystyle D_{t}(\rho^{-1}B_{i})=

\displaystyle~\rho^{-1}D_{t}B_{i}+B_{i}D_{t}\rho^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\rho^{-1}(B\cdot\nabla u_{i}-B_{i}\nabla\cdot u)-\rho^{-2}B_{i}D_{t}\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\rho^{-1}(B\cdot\nabla u_{i}-B_{i}\nabla\cdot u)+\rho^{-1}B_{i}\nabla\cdot u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,d.

(3.5)

For any function
f\in C([0,T];H^{2})\cap C^{1}([0,T];H^{1})
, we have that

\displaystyle D_{t}(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla f)=

\displaystyle~\sum_{k,j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{t}(\rho^{-1}B_{k}\partial_{k}f)+u_{j}\partial_{j}(\rho^{-1}B_{k}\partial_{k}f)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{k,j=1}^{d}\left(\rho^{-1}B_{k}\partial_{k}(\partial_{t}f+u_{j}\partial_{j}f)+\partial_{t}(\rho^{-1}B_{k})\partial_{k}f-\rho^{-1}B_{k}\partial_{k}u_{j}\partial_{j}f+u_{j}\partial_{j}(\rho^{-1}B_{k})\partial_{k}f\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)D_{t}f+\sum_{k=1}^{d}(D_{t}(\rho^{-1}B_{k})-\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u_{k})\partial_{k}f=(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)D_{t}f.

(3.6)

∎

Since
D_{t}S=0
and
D_{t}(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla S)=0
, we can easily prove the estimates for
(S,\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla S)
. The proof does not involve any boundary term because we do not integrate by parts, so we omit the details.

Corollary 3.3
.

The entropy
S
in (
1.8
) satisfies the following estimates:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{l=0}^{4}\sum_{k=0}^{4-l}\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}(S,\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla S)\right\|_{4-k-l}^{2}\leq P(E(t)).

(3.7)

3.3
Tangential energy estimates

In this section, we will derive the energy estimates for tangential derivatives of solutions. It is more convenient to use the following equations derived from (
1.8
):

\begin{cases}aD_{t}\check{q}-\varepsilon aB\cdot D_{t}B+\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\cdot u=0~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
\rho D_{t}u+\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\check{q}-B\cdot\nabla B=0~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
D_{t}B-B\cdot\nabla u-\varepsilon aB(D_{t}\check{q}-\varepsilon B\cdot D_{t}B)=0~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot B=0~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
D_{t}S=0~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
a=a(\varepsilon\check{q}-\varepsilon^{2}|B|^{2}/2,S)>0,\quad\rho=\rho(\varepsilon\check{q}-\varepsilon^{2}|B|^{2}/2,S)>0~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\bar{\Omega},\end{cases}

(3.8)

where
\check{q}=q+\varepsilon|B|^{2}/2
is the total pressure.

Proposition 3.4
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{8}\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}(q,u,B)\right\|_{0}^{2}\leq P(E(0))+E(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.9)

The proof of this proposition is divided into two parts: When
1\leq k\leq 7
, one can prove the tangential estimates by straightforward calculations; when
k=8
, the anisotropy of the function spaces leads to potential loss of regularity in the commutator
[\nabla,D_{t}^{8}]
as the terms having the form
\nabla D_{t}^{7}f~(f=u,q)
must appear.

3.3.1
Low-order estimates for
\leq 7
material derivatives

For
1\leq k\leq 7
, we take
D_{t}^{k}
to the equations (
3.8
) to obtain that

\displaystyle aD_{t}^{k+1}\check{q}-\varepsilon aB\cdot D_{t}^{k+1}B+\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\cdot D_{t}^{k}u=\mathcal{R}_{q}^{k},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\rho D_{t}^{k+1}u+\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla D_{t}^{k}q-B\cdot\nabla D_{t}^{k}B=\mathcal{R}_{u}^{k},

\displaystyle D_{t}^{k+1}B-B\cdot\nabla D_{t}^{k}u-\varepsilon aB(D_{t}^{k+1}\check{q}-\varepsilon B\cdot D_{t}^{k+1}B)=\mathcal{R}_{B}^{k},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{q}^{k}=

\displaystyle[a,D_{t}^{k}]D_{t}\check{q}-\varepsilon[aB,D_{t}^{k}]\cdot D_{t}B+\varepsilon^{-1}[\partial_{j},D_{t}^{k}]u_{j},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{u}^{k}=

\displaystyle[\rho,D_{t}^{k}]D_{t}u+\varepsilon^{-1}[\nabla,D_{t}^{k}]q-[B\cdot\nabla,D_{t}^{k}]B,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{B}^{k}=

\displaystyle-[B\cdot\nabla,D_{t}^{k}]u-\varepsilon[aB,D_{t}^{k}]D_{t}\check{q}+\varepsilon^{2}[aBB_{j},D_{t}^{k}]D_{t}B_{j}.

Multiplying (
3.10
) by
\varepsilon^{2k}D_{t}^{k}(\check{q},u,B)
, integrating over
\Omega
and integrating by parts, one sees that

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{2k}}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\left(a|D_{t}^{k}\check{q}-B\cdot D_{t}^{k}B|^{2}+\rho|D_{t}^{k}u|^{2}+|D_{t}^{k}B|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{\varepsilon^{2k}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left((\partial_{t}a+\nabla\cdot(au))|D_{t}^{k}\check{q}-B\cdot D_{t}^{k}B|^{2}+\nabla\cdot u|D_{t}^{k}B|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle~-\varepsilon^{2k}\int_{\Omega}a(\varepsilon D_{t}B\cdot D_{t}^{k}B)(D_{t}^{k}\check{q}-B\cdot D_{t}^{k}B)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle~+\varepsilon^{2k}\int_{\Omega}((B\cdot\nabla)D_{t}^{k}B)\cdot D_{t}^{k}u+((B\cdot\nabla)D_{t}^{k}u)\cdot D_{t}^{k}B\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle~+\varepsilon^{2k}\int_{\Omega}\left(D_{t}^{k}\check{q}\mathcal{R}_{q}^{k}+D_{t}^{k}u\cdot\mathcal{R}_{u}^{k}+D_{t}^{k}B\cdot\mathcal{R}_{B}^{k}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle~\mathcal{G}_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{2}+\mathcal{G}_{3}+\mathcal{G}_{4},

(3.11)

where the equation
\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0
and the boundary condition
u_{3}=B_{3}=0
on
\Sigma
are used. We now have to control each term on the right-hand side of (
3.11
). Since
a
is smooth funtions of
\varepsilon q
and
S
, we employ the Sobolev inequality to deduce that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}a\|_{\infty}=\|\varepsilon\partial_{q}\rho\partial_{t}q-\partial_{S}\rho(u\cdot\nabla)S\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle~P(E(t)),

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot(au)\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle~P(E(t)).

Thus, it is easy to find that
\mathcal{G}_{1}
can be bounded from above by

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{1}|\lesssim\|\partial_{t}a+\nabla\cdot(au)\|_{\infty}\|\varepsilon^{k}(D_{t}^{k}\check{q}-B\cdot D_{t}^{k}B)\|_{0}^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{\infty}\|\varepsilon^{k}D_{t}^{k}B\|_{0}^{2}\leq P(E(t)).

(3.12)

Similarly, we control
\mathcal{G}_{2}
as follows:

|\mathcal{G}_{2}|\lesssim\|\varepsilon D_{t}B\|_{\infty}\|\varepsilon^{k}D_{t}^{k}B\|_{0}\|\varepsilon^{k}(D_{t}^{k}\check{q}-B\cdot D_{t}^{k}B)\|_{0}\leq P(E(t)).

(3.13)

For
\mathcal{G}_{3}
, integrating by parts and using the boundary conditions
u_{3}=B_{3}=0
on
\Sigma
and
\nabla\cdot B=0
, one obtains that

\mathcal{G}_{3}=\varepsilon^{2k}\int_{\Omega}-((B\cdot\nabla)D_{t}^{k}u)\cdot D_{t}^{k}B+((B\cdot\nabla)D_{t}^{k}u)\cdot D_{t}^{k}B\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(3.14)

Recall that Lemma
A.3
indicates that the highest-order terms in the commutator
\varepsilon^{k}[\nabla,D_{t}^{k}]f
have the form of either
(\varepsilon^{k-1}\partial D_{t}^{k-1}u)(\varepsilon\partial f)
or
(\varepsilon\partial u)(\varepsilon^{k-1}\partial D_{t}^{k-1}f)
whose
L^{2}(\Omega)
norms can be directly controlled by
P(E(t))
thanks to
k\leq 7
. By standard product estimates, it is straightforward to see that the
L^{2}
-norm of terms in
\mathcal{G}_{4}
can be controlled by
P(E(t))
, i.e.

\mathcal{G}_{4}\leq P(E(t)).

(3.15)

Substituting the estimates for
\mathcal{G}_{1}
-
\mathcal{G}_{4}
into (
3.11
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\|\varepsilon^{k}D_{t}^{k}(\check{q},u,B)\right\|_{0}^{2}\leq P(E(t)),\quad 1\leq k\leq 7.

(3.16)

By the definition of
\check{q}
, it is easy to verify that

\|\varepsilon^{k}D_{t}^{k}q\|_{0}^{2}\leq P(E(0))+\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad 1\leq k\leq 7.

(3.17)

3.3.2
Estimates of 8 material derivatives

Now we consider the estimates of the rest of highest order tangential derivatives
D_{t}^{8}
. In such case, the commutator
\varepsilon^{8}[\nabla,D_{t}^{8}]f
contains the terms
(\varepsilon^{7}\partial D_{t}^{7}u)(\varepsilon\partial f)
and
(\varepsilon\partial u)(\varepsilon^{7}\partial D_{t}^{7}f)
in which the
\partial D_{t}^{7}(\cdot)
part is not controllable in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Such possible loss of regularity is completely caused by the anisotropy of the function spaces, as the
\partial
may be a normal derivative which is considered to be second-order. To overcome this difficulty, we shall introduce the “modified Alinhac good unknowns” in
[
19
, Section 5.5]
by the third author. To be more precise, let

\mathbf{Q}^{8}=D_{t}^{8}\check{q}-D_{t}^{7}u_{i}\partial_{i}\check{q},\quad\mathbf{U}^{8}=D_{t}^{8}u-9D_{t}^{7}u_{i}\partial_{i}u,

(3.18)

respectively be the Alinhac good unknowns of
q
and
u
. Therefore, by Lemma
A.3
and Proposition
3.2
, we have that

\displaystyle D_{t}^{8}\nabla\check{q}=

\displaystyle~\nabla\mathbf{Q}^{8}-8\nabla u_{i}\partial_{i}D_{t}^{7}\check{q}-Z^{8}\check{q},

(3.19)

\displaystyle D_{t}^{8}\nabla\cdot u=

\displaystyle~\nabla\cdot D_{t}^{8}u-\nabla\cdot(D_{t}^{7}u_{i}\partial_{i}u)-8\partial_{j}u_{i}\partial_{i}D_{t}^{7}u_{j}-Z_{j}^{8}u_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\nabla\cdot\mathbf{U}^{8}+8\partial_{j}(\nabla\cdot u)D_{t}^{7}u_{j}-Z^{8}_{j}u_{j},

(3.20)

\displaystyle D_{t}^{8}(B\cdot\nabla B)=D_{t}^{8}(\rho\times\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla B)=

\displaystyle~[D_{t}^{8},\rho](\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla B)+(B\cdot\nabla)(D_{t}^{8}B),

(3.21)

\displaystyle D_{t}^{8}(B\cdot\nabla u)=D_{t}^{8}(\rho\times\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u)=

\displaystyle~[D_{t}^{8},\rho](\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u)+B\cdot\nabla\mathbf{U}^{8}+9(B\cdot\nabla)(D_{t}^{7}u_{i}\partial_{i}u),

(3.22)

where
Z^{k}=(Z_{1}^{k},Z_{2}^{k},Z_{3}^{k})^{\mathrm{T}}
represents the low-order terms in the commutator and its precise definition is recorded in Lemma
A.3
.
We take
D_{t}^{8}
to the equations (
3.8
) to obtain that

\displaystyle aD_{t}\mathbf{Q}^{8}-\varepsilon aB\cdot D_{t}^{9}B+\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{U}^{8}=\mathcal{R}_{q}^{8},

(3.23)

\displaystyle\rho D_{t}\mathbf{U}^{8}+\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\mathbf{Q}^{8}-(B\cdot\nabla)(D_{t}^{8}B)=\varepsilon^{-1}8\nabla u_{i}\partial_{i}D_{t}^{7}\check{q}+\mathcal{R}_{u}^{8},

\displaystyle D_{t}^{9}B-B\cdot\nabla\mathbf{U}^{8}-\varepsilon aB(D_{t}\mathbf{Q}^{8}-\varepsilon B\cdot D_{t}^{9}B)=-9(B\cdot\nabla)(D_{t}^{7}u_{i}\partial_{i}u)+\mathcal{R}_{B}^{8},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{q}^{8}=

\displaystyle[a,D_{t}^{8}]D_{t}\check{q}-aD_{t}(D_{t}^{7}u_{i}\partial_{i}\check{q})-\varepsilon[aB,D_{t}^{8}]\cdot D_{t}B+\varepsilon^{-1}(Z_{j}^{8}u_{j}-8\partial_{j}(\nabla\cdot u)D_{t}^{7}u_{j}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{u}^{8}=

\displaystyle[\rho,D_{t}^{8}]D_{t}u-9\rho D_{t}(D_{t}^{7}u_{i}\partial_{i}u)+\varepsilon^{-1}Z^{8}\check{q}-[D_{t}^{8},\rho](\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla B),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{B}^{8}=

\displaystyle-[D_{t}^{8},\rho](\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u)-\varepsilon[aB,D_{t}^{8}]D_{t}\check{q}+\varepsilon aBD_{t}(D_{t}^{7}u_{i}\partial_{i}\check{q})+\varepsilon^{2}[aBB_{j},D_{t}^{8}]D_{t}B_{j}.

Multiplying (
3.10
) by
\varepsilon^{16}(\mathbf{Q}^{8},\mathbf{U}^{8},D_{t}^{8}B)
, integrating over
\Omega
and integrating by parts, one can see that

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{16}}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\left(a|\mathbf{Q}^{8}-B\cdot D_{t}^{8}B|^{2}+\rho|\mathbf{U}^{8}|^{2}+|D_{t}^{8}B|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{\varepsilon^{16}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left((\partial_{t}a+\nabla\cdot(au))|\mathbf{Q}^{8}-B\cdot D_{t}^{8}B|^{2}+\nabla\cdot u|D_{t}^{8}B|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle~-\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}a(\varepsilon D_{t}B\cdot D_{t}^{8}B)(\mathbf{Q}^{8}-B\cdot D_{t}^{8}B)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle~+\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}((B\cdot\nabla)D_{t}^{8}B)\cdot\mathbf{U}^{8}+((B\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}^{8})\cdot D_{t}^{8}B\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle~+\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{Q}^{8}\mathcal{R}_{q}^{8}+\mathbf{U}^{8}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{u}^{8}+D_{t}^{8}B\cdot\mathcal{R}_{B}^{8}\cdot D_{t}^{8}B\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle~+8\varepsilon^{15}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{U}^{8}\cdot\nabla u_{i}\,\partial_{i}D_{t}^{7}\check{q}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle~-9\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}(B\cdot\nabla)(D_{t}^{7}u_{i}\partial_{i}u)\cdot D_{t}^{8}B\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle~\mathcal{G}_{1}^{\prime}+\mathcal{G}_{2}^{\prime}+\mathcal{G}_{3}^{\prime}+\mathcal{G}_{4}^{\prime}+\mathcal{G}_{5}^{\prime}+\mathcal{G}_{6}^{\prime}.

(3.24)

Since the estimates for

) – (

), we omit the details of the proof and directly present the following conclusions:

\mathcal{G}_{3}^{\prime}=0,\quad\mathcal{G}_{1}^{\prime}+\mathcal{G}_{2}^{\prime}+\mathcal{G}_{4}^{\prime}\leq P(E(t)).

Next, we control the terms
\mathcal{G}_{5}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{G}_{6}^{\prime}

P(E(t))
due to the appearance of
\partial D_{t}^{7}

-\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\check{q}=\rho D_{t}u-B\cdot\nabla B,

and the magnetic field equation

D_{t}(\rho^{-1}B)=\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u,

\mathcal{G}_{5}^{\prime}
is rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{5}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle~8\varepsilon^{15}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{U}^{8}\cdot\nabla u_{i}\,D_{t}^{7}\partial_{i}\check{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\underbrace{+8\varepsilon^{15}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{U}^{8}\cdot\nabla u_{i}\,[\partial_{i},D_{t}^{7}]\check{q}\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{G}_{51}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~8\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{U}^{8}\cdot\nabla u_{i}\,D_{t}^{7}(B\cdot\nabla B_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x\underbrace{-8\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{U}^{8}\cdot\nabla u_{i}\,D_{t}^{7}(\rho D_{t}u_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{G}_{52}^{\prime}}+\mathcal{G}_{51}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~8\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{U}^{8}\cdot\nabla u_{i}\,(B\cdot\nabla)(D_{t}^{7}B_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x\underbrace{+8\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{U}^{8}\cdot\nabla u_{i}\,[D_{t}^{7},\rho](\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla B_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{G}_{53}^{\prime}}+\mathcal{G}_{51}^{\prime}+\mathcal{G}_{52}^{\prime}

(3.25)

Then we integrate by parts in
(B\cdot\nabla)
, insert the concrete for of the Alinhac good unknown
\mathbf{U}^{8}
, use

and

to get

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{5}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle~-8\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}(B\cdot\nabla)(D_{t}^{8}u_{j}-9D_{t}^{7}u_{k}\partial_{k}u_{j})\partial_{j}u_{i}D_{t}^{7}B_{i}\,\mathrm{d}x\underbrace{-8\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{U}^{8}_{j}(B\cdot\nabla)(\partial_{j}u_{i})D_{t}^{7}B_{i}\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{G}_{54}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle~+\mathcal{G}_{51}^{\prime}+\mathcal{G}_{52}^{\prime}+\mathcal{G}_{53}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-8\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\rho D_{t}^{8}(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u_{j})\partial_{j}u_{i}D_{t}^{7}B_{i}\mathrm{d}x+72\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\rho D_{t}^{7}(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u_{k})\partial_{k}u_{j}\partial_{j}u_{i}D_{t}^{7}B_{i}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle~\underbrace{+72\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}(B\cdot\nabla)(\partial_{k}u_{j})D_{t}^{7}u_{k}\partial_{j}u_{i}D_{t}^{7}B_{i}\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{G}_{55}^{\prime}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}\mathcal{G}_{5\ell}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-8\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}u_{i}\,D_{t}^{9}(\rho^{-1}B_{j})\,D_{t}^{7}B_{i}\mathrm{d}x+\underbrace{72\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\rho\partial_{k}u_{j}\,\partial_{j}u_{i}\,D_{t}^{8}(\rho^{-1}B_{k})D_{t}^{7}B_{i}\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{G}_{56}^{\prime}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{5}\mathcal{G}_{5\ell}^{\prime}.

(3.26)

We then integrate
D_{t}
by parts to get

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{5}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle~\underbrace{-8\varepsilon^{16}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}u_{i}\,D_{t}^{8}(\rho^{-1}B_{j})\,D_{t}^{7}B_{i}\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{G}_{5}^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{+8\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}u_{i}\,D_{t}^{8}(\rho^{-1}B_{j})\,D_{t}^{8}B_{i}\mathrm{d}x+8\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}D_{t}(\partial_{j}u_{i})\,D_{t}^{8}(\rho^{-1}B_{j})\,D_{t}^{7}B_{i}\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{G}_{57}^{\prime}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{6}\mathcal{G}_{5\ell}^{\prime}.

(3.27)

By utilizing standard product estimates, it is straightforward to check that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{7}\mathcal{G}_{5\ell}^{\prime}\leq P(E(t)),

(3.28)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{5}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau=

\displaystyle~-\left(8\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}u_{i}D_{t}^{8}(\rho^{-1}B_{j})D_{t}^{7}B_{i}\mathrm{d}x\right)\Big|_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~P(E(0))+\eta\|\varepsilon^{8}D_{t}^{8}(\rho^{-1}B_{j})\|_{0}^{2}+C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{2}\|\partial_{j}u_{i}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\varepsilon^{7}D_{t}^{7}B_{i}\|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~P(E(0))+\eta\|\varepsilon^{8}D_{t}^{8}(\rho^{-1}B)\|_{0}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|u\|_{3}^{2}\|\varepsilon^{7}D_{t}^{7}B\|_{0}^{2},\quad\eta<1/2.

(3.29)

The last term is controlled with the help of Jensen’s inequality and the Reynolds transport formula (Lemma
A.2
)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\|u\|_{3}^{2}\left\|\varepsilon^{7}D_{t}^{7}B\right\|_{0}^{2}

(3.30)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{3}^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\varepsilon^{7}D_{t}^{7}B(0,\cdot)\right\|_{0}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\left(\varepsilon D_{t}\right)^{8}B(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{0}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\|u\|_{3}^{2}E_{7}(0)+E_{4}(t)\int_{0}^{t}P\left(E_{7}(\tau)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\text{. }

\displaystyle\lesssim E_{7}(0)\left(\varepsilon^{2}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{3}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\varepsilon D_{t}u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{3}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)+E_{4}(t)\int_{0}^{t}P\left(E_{7}(\tau)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

Also the term
\|\varepsilon^{8}D_{t}^{8}(\rho^{-1}B)\|_{0}^{2}

plus controllable terms

\|\varepsilon^{8}D_{t}^{8}(\rho^{-1}B)\|_{0}^{2}\lesssim\|\varepsilon^{8}D_{t}^{8}B\|_{0}^{2}+P(E(0))+E(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

In fact, the leading-order term in

. Since

(recall

Therefore,

\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{5}^{\prime}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\eta\|\varepsilon^{8}D_{t}^{8}B\|_{0}^{2}+P(E(0))+E(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad\eta<1/2.

(3.31)

Similarly, one obtains that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{6}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle~-9\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\rho D_{t}^{7}(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u_{i})\partial_{i}u\cdot D_{t}^{8}B\mathrm{d}x-9\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\left((B\cdot\nabla)(\partial_{i}u)D_{t}^{7}u_{i}+[\rho,D_{t}^{7}](\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u_{i})\partial_{i}u\right)\cdot D_{t}^{8}B\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~-9\varepsilon^{16}\int_{\Omega}\rho D_{t}^{8}(\rho^{-1}B_{i})\partial_{i}u\cdot D_{t}^{8}B\mathrm{d}x+P(E(t))\leq P(E(t)).

(3.32)

Substituting the estimates for
\mathcal{G}_{1}^{\prime}
-
\mathcal{G}_{6}^{\prime}
into (

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon^{8}(\mathbf{Q}^{8},\mathbf{U}^{8},D_{t}^{8}B)\|_{0}^{2}\leq\eta\|\varepsilon^{8}D_{t}^{8}B\|_{0}^{2}+P(E(0))+E(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.33)

The control of
(\mathbf{Q}^{8},\mathbf{U}^{8})
immediately leads to the control of
(D_{t}^{8}q,D_{t}^{8}u)
. For example, we have

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon^{8}D_{t}^{8}q\|_{0}^{2}\lesssim\|\varepsilon^{8}\mathbf{Q}^{8}\|_{0}^{2}+\|(\varepsilon^{7}D_{t}^{7}u)(\varepsilon\partial q)\|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|\varepsilon^{8}\mathbf{Q}^{8}\|_{0}^{2}+\|\varepsilon^{7}D_{t}^{7}u\|_{0}^{2}\left(\|\varepsilon q_{0}\|_{3}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\varepsilon D_{t}q(\tau,\cdot)\|_{3}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)

\displaystyle~P(E(0))+\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau,

where we note that the control of

\|\varepsilon^{8}D_{t}^{8}(q,u,B)\|_{0}^{2}\lesssim\eta\|\varepsilon^{8}D_{t}^{8}B\|_{0}^{2}+P(E(0))+E(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad\quad\forall\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).

Choosing

3.4.1
Estimates of vorticity and current

We start with the control of vorticity
\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\times u\|_{3-k-l}^{2}
and current
\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\times B\|_{3-k-l}^{2}

Lemma 3.5
.

Define
\rho_{0}(S):=\rho(0,S)
. Then under the assumption of Theorem
1.1
, for each
l\in\{0,1,2,3\}
and
0\leq k\leq 3-l
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\left\|\varepsilon B\times\left((\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~P\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}E_{4+k}(t)\right)+E_{4+l+1}(t).

(3.39)

Remark 3.1
.

It should be noted that the loss of Mach number weights occurs if we analyze the curl part via the evolution equation of vorticity
\nabla\times u
, because the spatial derivative of
\rho
is still of
O(1)
size with respect to the Mach number
\varepsilon
and
D_{t}u
has
O(1/\varepsilon)
size. Motivated by
[
23
]
, we rewrite the curl part as

\nabla\times u=\rho_{0}^{-1}(\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)-\nabla\rho_{0}\times u),\quad\nabla\times B=\rho_{0}^{-1}(\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)-\nabla\rho_{0}\times B),\quad\rho_{0}:=\rho(0,S).

(3.40)

In Lemma
3.9
, we reproduce the estimates for
\nabla\times u
and
\nabla\times B
by combining the above equality and Lemma
3.5
. The advantange of this substitution is that
\rho_{0}
commutes with
D_{t}
and so we no longer have the singular term. In fact, the momentum equation
\rho D_{t}u+\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla q+B\times(\nabla\times B)=0
can be written as

D_{t}(\rho_{0}u)-\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla B=-\nabla(\varepsilon^{-1}q+|B|^{2}/2)+\frac{\rho-\rho_{0}}{\rho}\nabla(\varepsilon^{-1}q+|B|^{2}/2).

(3.41)

There exists a smooth function
g
such that

\frac{\rho-\rho_{0}}{\rho}=\varepsilon g(\varepsilon q,S),\quad\sum_{k=0}^{4}\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}g\|_{4-k}\leq P(E_{4}(t)).

First, we take
\nabla\times
in (
3.41
) to get the evolution equation

\displaystyle D_{t}(\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u))-\nabla\times\left(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla B\right)=[u\cdot\nabla,\nabla\times](\rho_{0}u)+\nabla g\times\nabla(q+\varepsilon|B|^{2}/2),

(3.42)

where we notice that the right side only contains first-order derivatives and does not lose
\varepsilon
weights.

We first prove the case when
k=0
. Indeed, the cases for
k=1,2,3
follow in the same manner and the analysis will be recorded in Lemma
3.7
and Lemma
3.8
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{3}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\|_{3-k}^{2}+\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\|_{3-k}^{2}+\left\|\varepsilon B\times\left((\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\right\|_{3-k}^{2}\right)\lesssim P(E_{4}(t))+E_{5}(t).

(3.43)

Proof.

We first treat the case
k=0
. Taking
\partial^{3}
in (
3.42
), we get

\displaystyle D_{t}(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u))-\partial^{3}\nabla\times\left(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla B\right)=\underbrace{\partial^{3}(\text{RHS of }\eqref{curlv eq})+[u\cdot\nabla,\partial^{3}](\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u))}_{:=\mathcal{R}_{1}},

(3.44)

where the order of derivatives on the right side does not exceed 4. We start with the estimates of vorticity by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}|\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u))\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}(u\cdot\nabla)\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{1}}.

(3.45)

Then invoking (
3.44
) gives us

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{\Omega}\partial^{3}\nabla\times\left(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla B\right)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x\underbrace{+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{R}_{1}\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho^{-1}(B\cdot\nabla)\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x+\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\underbrace{+\int_{\Omega}\left(\partial^{3}(\nabla(\rho^{-1}B_{i})\times\partial_{i}(\rho_{0}B))+[\partial^{3},\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla]\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{3}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{3}\nabla\times\left((\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla\rho_{0})B\right)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{4}}.

(3.46)

Integrating by parts in
\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho^{-1}(B\cdot\nabla)\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho^{-1}(B\cdot\nabla)\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\overset{B\cdot\nabla}{=}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times((\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)(\rho_{0}u))\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\cdot\left([\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla,\partial^{3}]\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)-\partial^{3}(\nabla(\rho^{-1}B_{i})\times\partial_{i}(\rho_{0}u))+\nabla\cdot(\rho^{-1}B)\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{5}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x+\mathcal{I}_{5}

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times\left((\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla\rho_{0})u\right)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{6}}.

(3.47)

Inserting the evolution equation of
B
to substitute
B\cdot\nabla u
, we get

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}D_{t}B)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\nabla\cdot u)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{-1}|\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\underbrace{+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(D_{t}\rho^{-1}+\rho^{-1}\nabla\cdot u)|\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{7}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\left(\nabla\times([\partial^{3},\rho^{-1}D_{t}](\rho_{0}B))+\nabla\rho^{-1}\times\partial_{t}\partial^{3}(\rho_{0}B)+\nabla(\rho^{-1}u_{i})\partial_{i}\partial^{3}(\rho_{0}B)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{8}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{+\int_{\Omega}\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aBD_{t}q)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{K}}.

(3.48)

Based on the concrete forms of the commutators in Lemma
A.3
, a straightforward product estimate for
\mathcal{I}_{j}

(j=1,2,3,5,7)
gives us

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}+\mathcal{I}_{3}+\mathcal{I}_{5}+\mathcal{I}_{7}\leq P(E_{4}(t)).

(3.49)

In
\mathcal{I}_{4}
and
\mathcal{I}_{6}
, we find that there are fourth-order derivatives falling on
\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla\rho_{0}
. Since
\rho_{0}=\rho(0,S)
only depends on
S
, we can use the enhanced “directional” regularity of
S
(Corollary
3.3
) to control such terms

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{4}+\mathcal{I}_{6}\leq P(E_{4}(t)).

(3.50)

For the terms in
\mathcal{I}_{8}
, there exhibits a time derivative without
\varepsilon
weight. In fact, this time derivative can be replaced with spatial derivatives by using the equations for the magnetic field and entropy. For example, we obtain that

\displaystyle~\|\nabla\times([\partial^{3},\rho^{-1}D_{t}](\rho_{0}B))\|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|\partial_{j}\rho^{-1}\partial_{t}(\rho_{0}B)\|_{3}+\|\partial_{j}(\rho^{-1}u_{k})\partial_{k}(\rho_{0}B)\|_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|(\varepsilon\partial_{q}\rho^{-1}\partial_{j}q+\partial_{S}\rho^{-1}\partial_{j}S)\cdot(\rho_{0}^{\prime}B\partial_{t}S+\rho_{0}\partial_{t}B)\|_{3}+P(E_{4}(t))

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|\rho_{0}^{\prime}B\partial_{S}\rho^{-1}\partial_{j}S(u\cdot\nabla S)\|_{3}+\|\rho_{0}\partial_{S}\rho^{-1}\partial_{j}S(-u\cdot\nabla B+B\cdot\nabla u-B\nabla\cdot u)\|_{3}+P(E_{4}(t))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~P(E_{4}(t)),

(3.51)

which leads to

\mathcal{I}_{8}\leq P(E_{4}(t)).

(3.52)

The crucial term is
\mathcal{K}
in which the highest-order term has 5-th order derivative and thus cannot be controlled by
E_{4}(t)
. We first pick up the highest-order part in
\mathcal{K}
:

\displaystyle\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aBD_{t}q)=

\displaystyle~\varepsilon\partial^{3}((\nabla D_{t}q)\times(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB)+D_{t}q\nabla\times(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times(\partial^{3}\nabla D_{t}q)\underbrace{-\varepsilon[\partial^{3},\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB]\times(\nabla D_{t}q)+\varepsilon\partial^{3}(D_{t}q\nabla\times(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB))}_{:=\mathcal{R}_{2}},

where straightforward calculation shows that
\|\mathcal{R}_{2}\|_{0}\leq P(E_{4}(t))
. For the fifth-order part, the vector identity
(B\cdot\nabla)B-\frac{1}{2}\nabla|B|^{2}=-B\times(\nabla\times B)
motivates us to rewrite the momentum equation as

\rho D_{t}u+B\times(\nabla\times B)=-\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla q.

So, we commute
D_{t}
with
\nabla
and plug this equation into
-\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times(\partial^{3}\nabla D_{t}q)
to get

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times(\partial^{3}\nabla D_{t}q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times(\partial^{3}D_{t}\nabla q)\underbrace{-\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times(\partial^{3}(\nabla u_{j}\partial_{j}q))}_{:=\mathcal{R}_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times\partial^{3}D_{t}(\rho D_{t}u)+\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times\partial^{3}D_{t}(B\times(\nabla\times B))+\mathcal{R}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{0}aB\times(\partial^{3}D_{t}^{2}u)+\underbrace{\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}aB\times D_{t}(B\times\partial^{3}(\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)))}_{=\mathcal{K}_{1}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{+\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times\left([\partial^{3}D_{t},\rho]D_{t}u+[\partial^{3},D_{t}](\nabla\times B)+D_{t}\left([\partial^{3},B]\times(\nabla\times B)\right)+D_{t}\left([\rho_{0},\partial^{3}\nabla]\times B\right)\right)}_{:=\mathcal{R}_{4}}+\mathcal{R}_{3},

(3.53)

where
\|\mathcal{R}_{3}+\mathcal{R}_{4}\|_{0}\leq P(E_{4}(t))
can be proved by using the concrete forms of commutators because the order of derivatives does not exceed 4. It should be noted that the time derivative in
D_{t}\left([\partial^{3},B\times](\nabla\times B)\right)
does not lead to any loss of
\varepsilon
weights because there is already an
\varepsilon
factor in
\mathcal{R}_{3}
. Similar argument also applies to the control of
\mathcal{R}_{4}
. Now, we analyze the contribution of
\mathcal{K}_{1}
in the integral
\mathcal{K}
, presented as below

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}a(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\cdot\left(B\times D_{t}\left(B\times\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(3.54)

We set
\mathbf{u}=B
,
\mathbf{v}=D_{t}\left(B\times\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)
and
\mathbf{w}=\partial^{3}(\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))
in the vector identity
(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v})\cdot\mathbf{w}=-(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{w})\cdot\mathbf{v}
to get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}a(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\cdot\left(B\times D_{t}\left(B\times\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}aD_{t}\left(B\times(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\right)\cdot\left(B\times(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}a\left|B\times(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}(\rho^{-1}a\nabla\cdot u+D_{t}(\rho^{-1}a))\left|B\times(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{R}_{5}}.

(3.55)

The term
\mathcal{R}_{5}
is directly controlled by
P(E_{4}(t))
. Therefore, we get the uniform control for
\mathcal{K}
by using both
E_{4}
and
E_{5}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}a\left|B\times(\partial^{3}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\|B\|_{4}\left(P(E_{4}(t))+\|\rho_{0}aB\|_{\infty}\|\varepsilon^{2}D_{t}^{2}u\|_{3}\right)\leq P(E_{4}(t))+E_{5}(t),

(3.56)

which gives us the energy estimate for the case of
k=0

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\|_{3}^{2}+\|\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\|_{3}^{2}+\left\|\varepsilon B\times(\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\right\|_{3}^{2}\right)\leq P(E_{4}(t))+E_{5}(t).

(3.57)

Similarly, we can prove the same conclusion for
\partial^{\alpha}D_{t}^{k}
with
k+|\alpha|=3
by replacing
\partial^{3}
with
\partial^{\alpha}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}
. Indeed, the highest order of derivatives in the above commutators does not exceed 4-th order, and there is no loss of Mach number weight because none of the above steps creates negative power of Mach number. The fifth-order term can also be analyzed in the same way:

\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times(\partial^{\alpha}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}\nabla D_{t}q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L}}{{=}}-\rho_{0}aB\times\partial^{\alpha}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2}u-\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}aB\times D_{t}\left(B\times(\partial^{\alpha}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\right),

where the
L^{2}
norms of the omitted lower-order terms are controlled by
P(E_{4}(t))
. The contribution of the last term in the above equality is again the energy term
-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}a\left|B\times(\partial^{\alpha}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}\nabla\times B)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
if we replace
\partial^{3}
above by
\partial^{\alpha}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}
. Hence, we obtain the following estimates for vorticity and current in
E_{4}(t)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{3}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\right\|_{3-k}^{2}+\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right\|_{3-k}^{2}+\left\|\varepsilon B\times\left((\varepsilon D_{t})^{k}\nabla\times B\right)\right\|_{3-k}^{2}\right)\leq P(E_{4}(t))+E_{5}(t).

(3.58)

∎

Next, we turn to prove Lemma
3.5
for
1\leq l\leq 3
. The proof is parallel to Lemma
3.6
and we mostly focus on the case of
l=1
.

Lemma 3.7
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\right\|_{2-k}^{2}+\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right\|_{2-k}^{2}+\left\|\varepsilon B\times\left((\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\right\|_{2-k}^{2}\right)

(3.59)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P(E_{4}(t),E_{5}(t))+E_{6}(t).

Proof.

For the case of
k=0
, we take
\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}
in (
3.42
) to get

\displaystyle D_{t}(\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u))-\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times\left(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla B\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}(\text{RHS of }\eqref{curlv eq})+[u\cdot\nabla,\partial^{2}]((\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u))}_{:=\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\prime}}.

(3.60)

Now, standard
L^{2}
-type estimate yields that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}|\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u))\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}(u\cdot\nabla)\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{1}^{\prime}}.

(3.61)

Then invoking (
3.60
) give us the following

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times\left(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla B\right)\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x\underbrace{+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\prime}\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\left((\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times\left((\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla\rho_{0})B\right)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{3}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{+\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\left(\nabla(\rho^{-1}B_{i})\times\partial_{i}(\rho_{0}B)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{4}^{\prime}}+\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)(\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\underbrace{+\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot[\partial^{2},\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla]((\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{5}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot\underbrace{\partial^{2}\left([(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2},\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla]\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)}_{=0}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{j=1}^{4}\mathcal{I}_{j}^{\prime},

(3.62)

where we have used the fact
[\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla,D_{t}]=0
. Then we integrate
B\cdot\nabla
by parts and invoke the evolution equation of
B
to get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\cdot\left[(\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)(\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))\right]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\left[\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times((\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla)(\rho_{0}u))\right]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\left([\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla,\partial^{2}]((\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B))-\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\left(\nabla(\rho^{-1}B_{i})\times\partial_{i}(\rho_{0}u)\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{5}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\underbrace{\partial^{2}\left([\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla,(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}]\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\right)}_{=0}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\left(\nabla\cdot(\rho^{-1}B)\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{6}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x+\mathcal{I}_{5}^{\prime}+\mathcal{I}_{6}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times\left((\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla\rho_{0})u\right)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{7}^{\prime}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{-1}\left|\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\underbrace{+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\rho^{-1}\nabla\cdot u+D_{t}\rho^{-1})\left|\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{8}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\left(\nabla\rho^{-1}\times\partial_{t}(\rho_{0}B)+\nabla(\rho^{-1}u_{i})\times\partial_{i}(\rho_{0}B)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{9}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot[\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2},\rho^{-1}D_{t}]\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{I}_{10}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{+\int_{\Omega}\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\cdot\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aBD_{t}q)\,\mathrm{d}x}_{:=\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}

(3.63)

A straightforward product estimate for
\mathcal{I}_{j}^{\prime}

(1\leq j\leq 10)
gives us

\sum_{j=1}^{10}\mathcal{I}_{j}^{\prime}\leq P(E_{4}(t),E_{5}(t)),

(3.64)

where the control of
\mathcal{I}_{3}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{I}_{7}^{\prime}
again requires the enhanced regularity of
S
in the direction of
B/\rho
as shown in Corollary
3.3
.

For
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
, we shall control
\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aBD_{t}q)
. We have

\displaystyle\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aBD_{t}q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times(\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla D_{t}q)\underbrace{-\varepsilon[\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2},\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB]\times(\nabla D_{t}q)+\varepsilon\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}(D_{t}q\nabla\times(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB))}_{:=\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times(\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{3}\nabla q)\underbrace{-\varepsilon\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times(\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}(\nabla u_{j}\partial_{j}q))}_{:=\mathcal{R}_{3}^{\prime}}+\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{3}(\varepsilon\rho D_{t}u)+\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{3}(\varepsilon B\times(\nabla\times B))++\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\prime}+\mathcal{R}_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\rho_{0}aB\times(\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{4}u)+\underbrace{\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}aB\times D_{t}(B\times\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}(\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)))}_{=\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{+\varepsilon^{4}\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}aB\times\left([\partial^{2}D_{t}^{3},\rho]D_{t}u+[\partial^{2},D_{t}](D_{t}^{2}\nabla\times B)+D_{t}\left([\partial^{2}D_{t}^{2},B]\times(\nabla\times B)\right)+D_{t}\left([\rho_{0},\partial^{2}D_{t}^{2}\nabla\times]B\right)\right)}_{:=\mathcal{R}_{4}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\prime}+\mathcal{R}_{3}^{\prime}.

Similarly as in the proof of Lemma
3.6
, we have

\|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\prime}+\mathcal{R}_{3}^{\prime}+\mathcal{R}_{4}^{\prime}\|_{0}\leq P(E_{4}(t),E_{5}(t)).

The analysis of
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}
is also parallel to the analysis of
\mathcal{K}_{1}
.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\cdot\left(\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}aB\times D_{t}(B\times\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}(\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)))\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}a\left|B\times\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\rho^{-1}a\nabla\cdot u+D_{t}(\rho^{-1}u))\left|B\times\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}\rho^{-1}a\left|B\times\partial^{2}(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+P(E_{4}(t),E_{5}(t)),

(3.65)

and thus

3.4.2
Reduction of pressure and divergence

We show how to reduce the control of the pressure to that of the velocity and magnetic field when there is at least one spatial derivative on the fluid pressure
q
. This follows from using the momentum equation

\displaystyle-\nabla q=\varepsilon\rho D_{t}u+\varepsilon B\times(\nabla\times B)

(3.74)

and the continuity equation and the divergence constraint

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u=-\varepsilon aD_{t}q,~~\nabla\cdot B=0.

(3.75)

These two equations show that a
spatial
derivative of
q
is reduced to a
material
derivative of
u
and the curl part of
B
and the divergence of velocity is converted to the material derivative of
q
.

For the differentiated version, we aim to prove the following reduction

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
k,l
be integers satisfying
0\leq l\leq 3
and
0\leq k\leq 3-l
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}q\|_{4-k-l}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|\rho(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l+1}u\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\|\varepsilon B\times(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}(\nabla\times B)\|_{3-k-l}^{2}

(3.76)

\displaystyle+P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}u\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|a(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l+1}q\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(3.77)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}B\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle~P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.78)

Proof.

We start with the reduction of
\nabla q
.

\displaystyle\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}q\|_{4-k-l}^{2}\lesssim\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}q\|_{0}^{2}+\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla q\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\|[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},\nabla]q\|_{3-k-l}^{2},

(3.79)

where the first term on the right side has been controlled in Proposition
3.4
. This inequality together with (
3.35
)-(
3.38
) shows that we shall control the following terms

•

\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla q\|_{3-k-l}^{2}
and
\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\cdot u\|_{3-k-l}^{2}
.

•

\|[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},\nabla]f\|_{3-k-l}^{2}
for
f=u,B,q
.

For the commutator part, it is summarized in Lemma
A.4
and we just record the result here

\displaystyle\left\|[\partial,(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}]f\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\leq P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.80)

Next, we control
\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla q\|_{3-k-l}^{2}
and
\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\cdot u\|_{3-k-l}^{2}
. Invoking the equations (
3.74
)-(
3.75
), we get

\displaystyle\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\cdot u\|_{3-k-l}^{2}=\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}(a\varepsilon D_{t}q)\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\leq\|a(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l+1}q\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\|[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},a]\varepsilon D_{t}q\|_{3-k-l}^{2},

(3.81)

where the last term can be directly controlled and we postpone the analysis to Section
3.4.3
. For the pressure gradient, we have

\displaystyle-(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla q=

\displaystyle~(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}(\rho\varepsilon D_{t}u+\varepsilon B\times(\nabla\times B))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\rho(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l+1}u+\varepsilon B\times(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}(\nabla\times B)

\displaystyle+[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},\rho](\varepsilon D_{t}u)+[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},\varepsilon B\times](\nabla\times B),

and so

\displaystyle\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla q\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle~\|\rho(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l+1}u\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\|\varepsilon B\times(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}(\nabla\times B)\|_{3-k-l}^{2}

(3.82)

\displaystyle+\|[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},\rho](\varepsilon D_{t}u)\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\|[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},\varepsilon B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{3-k-l}^{2}

In Section
3.4.3
, we will prove that these commutators terms can be directly controlled

\displaystyle\|[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},a]\varepsilon D_{t}q\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\|[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},\rho](\varepsilon D_{t}u)\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\|[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},\varepsilon B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{3-k-l}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.83)

Therefore, we conclude that the reduction of pressure and divergence actually converts a spatial derivative to a material derivative
(\varepsilon D_{t})
and lower-order curl estimate that has been analyzed in Section
3.4.1
. We get

\displaystyle\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}q\|_{4-k-l}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|\rho(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l+1}u\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\|\varepsilon B\times(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}(\nabla\times B)\|_{3-k-l}^{2}

(3.84)

\displaystyle+P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}u\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|a(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l+1}q\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}B\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle~P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.85)

The proof is completed.
∎

Remark 3.3

Closing the uniform estimates

Now we can turn to prove Theorem

Control of the entropy.

Control of the velocity

, the magnetic field
B
and the pressure

This part is divided into several steps.

1.

When

, there is no normal derivative in

3.4
by using the technique of modified Alinhac good unknowns.

2.

When

, recall that (

)-(
3.38
) show that the norms

are reduced to the control of

and

. The commutators generated in this process can be directly controlled as shown in Lemma
3.11
.

3.

For the curl part, we first show in Lemma
3.5
that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\left\|\varepsilon B\times\left((\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}B)\right)\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\right)\leq P(E(t)).

(3.89)

Then we remove the coefficient
\rho_{0}
to reproduce the desired curl estimates

\displaystyle\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\times u\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}\nabla\times B\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~P\left(E(0)\right)+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\sum_{k_{0}\leq k\atop l_{0}\leq l}\left(\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k_{0}+2l_{0}}u\|_{3-k_{0}-l_{0}}^{2}+\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k_{0}+2l_{0}}B\|_{3-k_{0}-l_{0}}^{2}\right),

(3.90)

which are recorded in Lemma
3.9
.

4.

For the divergence part, we combine the momentum equation and the continuity equation to reduce
\nabla q
to
\varepsilon D_{t}u
and
\varepsilon\nabla\times B
and reduce
\nabla\cdot u
to
\varepsilon D_{t}q
:

\displaystyle\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}q\|_{4-k-l}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle+P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}u\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|a(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l+1}q\|_{3-k-l}^{2}+P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}B\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle~P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.92)

as recorded in Lemma
3.10
.

5.

It should be noted that the extra terms generated on the right side of curl estimates are of lower order. If

We repeat step 3 and 4 until there is no spatial derivatives falling on

4

The last section is devoted to the proof of

to the incompressible inhomogeneous MHD system (

4.1
The strong convergence of vorticity, magnetic field and entropy

The uniform bounds imply that, up to a subsequence, we have

\displaystyle(q,u,B,S)\to(q^{0},u^{0},B^{0},S^{0})

\displaystyle\text{ weakly-* in }L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{4}(\Omega)),

(4.1)

\displaystyle(B,S)\to(B^{0},S^{0})

\displaystyle\text{ strongly in }C([0,T];H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\nabla\times(\rho_{0}(S)u)\to\nabla\times(\rho_{0}(S^{0})u^{0})

\displaystyle\text{ strongly in }C([0,T];H^{3-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)),

(4.3)

with
\rho_{0}(S)=\rho(0,S)
. Similarly, we use
a_{0}(S)
to denote
a(0,S)
. Here we note that the strong convergence of the vorticity is due to the uniform boundness of
D_{t}\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u)
which satisfies

D_{t}(\nabla\times(\rho_{0}u))=\nabla\times\left(\rho_{0}\rho^{-1}B\cdot\nabla B\right)+[u\cdot\nabla,\nabla\times](\rho_{0}u)+\nabla g\times\nabla(q+\varepsilon|B|^{2}/2),

where
g(\varepsilon q,S)=(\varepsilon\rho)^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{0})
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon
.

4.2
The strong convergence of pressure and divergence

We first prove that the expected limit functions satisfy
q^{0}=0
and
\nabla\cdot u^{0}=0
. The first and second equation of (
1.8
) are written to be

E(\varepsilon q,S)D_{t}U+\varepsilon^{-1}LU=J,

(4.4)

with

E(\varepsilon q,S)=\begin{pmatrix}a(\varepsilon q,S)&0\\
0&\rho(\varepsilon q,S)I_{3}\end{pmatrix},\quad L=\begin{pmatrix}0&\nabla\cdot\\
\nabla&0\end{pmatrix},\quad U=\begin{pmatrix}q\\
u\end{pmatrix},\quad J=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
B\times(\nabla\times B)\end{pmatrix}.

First notice that

\varepsilon E(\varepsilon q,S)\partial_{t}U+LU=\varepsilon E(\varepsilon q,S)u\cdot\nabla U-\varepsilon J.

(4.5)

Using the uniform bounds and
E(\varepsilon q,S)-E_{0}(S)=O(\varepsilon)
, we obtain

\varepsilon E_{0}(S)\partial_{t}U+LU=\varepsilon f,

(4.6)

where
E_{0}(S)=E(0,S)
and
\{f\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is a bounded family in
C([0,T];H^{3}(\Omega))
. Passing to the weak limit shows that
\nabla q^{0}=0
and
\nabla\cdot u^{0}
=0. Since
q^{0}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{4}(\Omega))
and
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
, we infer
q^{0}=0
.

Proposition 4.1
.

It holds that

\displaystyle q\to

\displaystyle 0\text{ strongly in }L^{2}([0,T];H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)),

(4.7)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u\to

\displaystyle 0\text{ strongly in }L^{2}([0,T];H^{3-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)).

(4.8)

This theorem is a slight variant of
[
1
, Prop. 3.1]
. For the reader’s convenience, we outline the proof detailed in
[
1

[

Proof.

Step 1: Wave-packet transform of the variable-coefficient system.

One first extends the functions to
t\in\mathbb{R}
by

\tilde{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{q}\\
\tilde{u}\end{pmatrix}=\chi_{\varepsilon}U=\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{\varepsilon}q\\
\chi_{\varepsilon}u\end{pmatrix},

(4.9)

where
\chi_{\varepsilon}\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T))
be a family of functions such that
\chi_{\varepsilon}(t)=1
for
t\in[\varepsilon^{1/2},T-\varepsilon^{1/2}]
and
\|\varepsilon\partial_{t}\chi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\varepsilon^{1/2}
, and choose extensions
\tilde{S}
of
S
, supported in
t\in[-1,T+1]

C(\mathbb{R};H^{4}(\Omega))

\tilde{S}^{0}
in
C(\mathbb{R};H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega))
. According to (

\tilde{U}
satisfies

\varepsilon E_{0}(\tilde{S})\partial_{t}\tilde{U}+L\tilde{U}=\varepsilon\tilde{f},

(4.10)

where

is a bounded family in
C(\mathbb{R};H^{3}(\Omega))
.

The above extension to

is necessary, as we want to apply the wave-packet transform to the variable

. Given
\varepsilon>0
, we define the following wave packet transform:

W^{\varepsilon}v(t,\tau,x)=(2\pi^{3})^{-1/4}\varepsilon^{-3/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{(\mathrm{i}(t-s)\tau-(t-s)^{2})/\varepsilon}v(s,x)\mathrm{d}s,

(4.11)

where

,

extends as an isometry from

to

Now, system (

) can be written in

where

\displaystyle\bm{F}^{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle~(F^{\varepsilon}_{1},\bm{F}_{2}^{\varepsilon})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{t,\tau};H^{1}(\Omega))\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{t,\tau};(H^{1}(\Omega))^{d}).

Following the arguments in

, one shows that

(4.13)

Step 2: Strong convergence of
q
via the techniques of microlocal defect measures.

Now we turn to prove the convergence of

. Since

is an isometry, it suffices to prove the strong convergence of

in certain function spaces on
\mathbb{R}_{t,\tau}^{2}\times\Omega

\displaystyle P^{\varepsilon}(t,\tau,\nabla)(\cdot):=

\displaystyle~a_{0}(\tilde{S})\tau^{2}(\cdot)+\nabla\cdot(\rho_{0}^{-1}(\tilde{S})\nabla(\cdot)),

(4.14)

\displaystyle P^{0}(t,\tau,\nabla)(\cdot):=

\displaystyle~a_{0}(\tilde{S}^{0})\tau^{2}(\cdot)+\nabla\cdot(\rho_{0}^{-1}(\tilde{S}^{0})\nabla(\cdot)),

(4.15)

\displaystyle\Theta^{\varepsilon}:=

Since this is a boundary-value problem, we shall decompose

into its interior part and boundary part. Following

(4.18)

f=(1-\Delta_{N})^{-1}g\text{ if and only if }(1-\Delta)f=g\text{ in }\Omega,\text{ and }\partial_{N}f=0\text{ on }\Sigma;

To prove the strong convergence of

Lemma 4.2

(Métivier-Schochet
[

)

For all uniformly bounded family

Here

(

Lemma 4.3

(Métivier-Schochet

)
.

The operator

is a 1-1 mapping for any

\displaystyle\ker_{L^{2}(\Omega)}(P^{0}(t,\tau,\nabla)(1-\Delta_{N})^{-1})=0,\quad\forall(t,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

(4.19)

.

where

denotes the set of compact operators on

.

We now set
\Phi(t,\tau)=\varphi(t,\tau)K^{*}K
for

and
K\in\mathcal{K}
in the above convergence result to get

\varphi K\Theta^{\varepsilon}\to 0\text{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{t,\tau}\times\Omega)

4.3
The strong convergence to the limit system

We continue our proof of Theorem
1.2
. Recall that
\mathcal{P}
be the projection onto
H_{\sigma}
and
\mathcal{Q}=I_{3}-\mathcal{P}
, where
H_{\sigma}=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\nabla\phi,\ \forall\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\}
and
G_{\sigma}=\{\nabla\psi:\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\}
give the orthogonal decomposition
L^{2}(\Omega)=H_{\sigma}\oplus G_{\sigma}
.

From the strong convergence results (
4.3
) and (
4.8
), we know that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\rho_{0}(S)u)\to\mathcal{P}(\rho_{0}(S^{0})u^{0})

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T];H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)),

(4.28)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}u\to\mathcal{Q}u^{0}=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T];H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)).

(4.29)

The previous two properties yields further that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\rho_{0}(S)\mathcal{P}u)\to\mathcal{P}(\rho_{0}(S^{0})\mathcal{P}u^{0})

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T];H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)),

(4.30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\rho_{0}(S)\mathcal{Q}u)\to\mathcal{P}(\rho_{0}(S^{0})\mathcal{Q}u^{0})=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T];H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)),

(4.31)

which, combined with the fact
S\to S^{0}
in
C([0,T];H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega))
, imply that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\rho_{0}(S^{0})\mathcal{P}(u-u^{0}))=

\displaystyle~\mathcal{P}(\rho_{0}(S^{0})[(u-u^{0})-\mathcal{Q}u])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathcal{P}\left(\rho_{0}(S)(u-u^{0})+(\rho_{0}(S^{0})-\rho_{0}(S))(u-u^{0})-\rho_{0}(S)\mathcal{Q}u+(\rho_{0}(S^{0})-\rho_{0}(S))\mathcal{Q}u\right)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle~0\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T];H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)).

Consequently, we find that, by noticing
\rho_{0}(S^{0})
positive in
[0,T]\times\Omega
,
\mathcal{P}u\to\mathcal{P}u^{0}=u^{0}
in
L^{2}([0,T];H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega))
, which, combined with (
4.29
), implies that

u\to u^{0}\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T];H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)).

(4.32)

By (
4.2
) and (
4.32
), we obtain

\displaystyle\rho(\varepsilon q,S)\to\rho_{0}(S^{0})

\displaystyle\text{ in }C([0,T];H^{4-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\nabla u\to\nabla u^{0}

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T];H^{3-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\nabla B\to\nabla B^{0}

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T];H^{3-\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)).

Passing to the limit in the equations for
S
and
B
, we see that the limits
S^{0}
and
B^{0}
satisfy

(\partial_{t}+u^{0}\cdot\nabla)S^{0}=0,\quad(\partial_{t}+u^{0}\cdot\nabla)B^{0}=B^{0}\cdot\nabla u^{0},\quad\nabla\cdot B^{0}=0

in the sense of distributions. Since
\rho(\varepsilon q,S)-\rho_{0}(S)=O(\varepsilon)
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(\varepsilon q,S)D_{t}u=

\displaystyle~(\rho(\varepsilon q,S)-\rho_{0}(S))D_{t}u+\partial_{t}(\rho_{0}(S)u)+(u\cdot\nabla)(\rho_{0}(S)u)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle~\rho_{0}(S^{0})((\partial_{t}+u^{0}\cdot\nabla)u^{0})

in the sense of distributions. Applying the operator
\mathcal{P}
to the momentum equations
\rho D_{t}u+\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla q+B\times(\nabla\times B)=0
and then taking to the limit, we conclude that

\mathcal{P}\left[\rho_{0}(S^{0})((\partial_{t}+u^{0}\cdot\nabla)u^{0}+B^{0}\times(\nabla\times B^{0}))\right]=0.

Therefore,
(u^{0},B^{0},S^{0})\in C([0,T];H^{4}(\Omega))
solves the incompressible MHD equations together with a transport equation

\begin{cases}\varrho(\partial_{t}u^{0}+u^{0}\cdot\nabla u^{0})-B^{0}\cdot\nabla B^{0}+\nabla(\pi+\frac{1}{2}|B^{0}|^{2})=0&~~~\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}B^{0}+u^{0}\cdot\nabla B^{0}-B^{0}\cdot\nabla u^{0}=0&~~~\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot u^{0}=\nabla\cdot B^{0}=0&~~~\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}S^{0}+u^{0}\cdot\nabla S^{0}=0&~~~\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
u_{d}^{0}=B_{d}^{0}=0&~~~\text{on}~[0,T]\times\Sigma,\end{cases}

(4.33)

for a suitable fluid pressure function
\pi
satisfying
\nabla\pi\in C([0,T];H^{3}(\Omega))
. Here
\varrho
satisfies
\partial_{t}\varrho+u^{0}\cdot\nabla\varrho=0,
with initial data
\varrho_{0}:=\rho(0,S_{0}^{0})
. Using the same arguments as in the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 1.5]
, we find that

(u^{0},B^{0},S^{0})|_{t=0}=(w_{0},B^{0}_{0},S^{0}_{0}),

where
w_{0}\in H^{4}(\Omega)
is determined by

w_{0d}|_{\Sigma}=0,\quad\nabla\cdot w_{0}=0,\quad\nabla\times(\rho_{0}(S_{0}^{0})w_{0})=\nabla\times(\rho_{0}(S_{0}^{0})u_{0}^{0}).

Moreover, the uniqueness of the limit function implies that the convergence holds as
\varepsilon\to 0
without restricting to a subsequence. Theorem
1.2
is then proven.

Appendix A
Preliminary lemmas and commutator estimates

In this section, we recall some basic identities and estimates. First, we record the Hodge-type elliptic estimates that are used in the div-curl analysis.

Lemma A.1

(Hodge elliptic estimates)
.

For any sufficiently smooth vector field
X\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and any real number
s\geq 1
, one has

\|X\|_{s}^{2}\lesssim\|X\|_{0}^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot X\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|\nabla\times X\|_{s-1}^{2}+|X\cdot N|_{s-1/2}^{2}.

(A.1)

When
X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we shall replace
\nabla\times X
by
\nabla^{\perp}\cdot X
with
\nabla^{\perp}:=(-\partial_{2},\partial_{1})^{\mathrm{T}}.

Lemma A.2

(Reynolds transport formula)
.

Under the setting of Theorem
1.1
, for each
f(t,x)
satisfying
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
D_{t}f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the Reynolds transport formula holds

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\rho|f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\rho(D_{t}f)f\,\mathrm{d}x.

(A.2)

Moreover, for
0\leq t\leq T
with
T>0
a given number, we have

\displaystyle\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}\lesssim\|f(0,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|D_{t}f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

(A.3)

Proof.

The Reynolds transport formula is proved by direct calculation and using the continuity equation
D_{t}\rho+\rho\nabla\cdot u=0
. For the second formula, we use the non-degeneracy of
\rho
(
1\lesssim\rho\lesssim 1
) to compute that

\displaystyle\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle~\int_{\Omega}\rho|f(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\rho|f(0,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}2\rho(D_{t}f(\tau,x))f(\tau,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\|f(0,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|D_{t}f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}\|f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}\|\rho\|_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|f(0,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|D_{t}f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}\|f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

Using Young’s inequality,
\|D_{t}f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}\|f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}\leq\delta\|f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}+(4\delta)^{-1}\|D_{t}f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}
. Therefore, we get for

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}\lesssim\delta\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}+\|f(0,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|D_{t}f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Pick
\delta>0
suitably small such that the
\delta
-term is absorbed by the left side. Then we get

\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}\lesssim\|f(0,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|D_{t}f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

∎

The next lemma reveals the structure of the commutator between higher-order material derivatives and spatial derivatives.

Lemma A.3

(
[
20

)
.

Let
[\partial,D_{t}]=(\partial u)\tilde{\cdot}\partial

\displaystyle[\partial,D_{t}^{k}]=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~(\partial D_{t}^{k-1}u)\tilde{\cdot}\partial+k(\partial u)\tilde{\cdot}(\partial D_{t}^{k-1})

\displaystyle~+\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=k-1\atop l_{1},l_{2}>0}c_{l_{1},l_{2}}(\partial D_{t}^{l_{1}}u)\tilde{\cdot}(\partial D_{t}^{l_{2}})+\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{n}=k-n+1\atop n\geq 3}d_{l_{1},\cdots,l_{n}}(\partial D_{t}^{l_{1}}u)\cdots(\partial D_{t}^{l_{n-1}}u)(\partial D_{t}^{l_{n}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\partial(D_{t}^{k-1}u_{i}\partial_{i}(\cdot))+k\partial u_{i}\partial_{i}D_{t}^{k-1}(\cdot)

\displaystyle~\underbrace{-D_{t}^{k-1}u_{i}\partial_{i}\partial+\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=k-1\atop l_{1},l_{2}>0}c_{l_{1},l_{2}}(\partial D_{t}^{l_{1}}u)\tilde{\cdot}(\partial D_{t}^{l_{2}})+\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{n}=k-n+1\atop n\geq 3}d_{l_{1},\cdots,l_{n}}(\partial D_{t}^{l_{1}}u)\cdots(\partial D_{t}^{l_{n-1}}u)(\partial D_{t}^{l_{n}})}_{:=Z^{k}},

(A.4)

\displaystyle~(\varepsilon\partial(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k-1}u)\tilde{\cdot}\partial+k(\partial u)\tilde{\cdot}(\varepsilon\partial(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k-1})

(A.5)

Lemma A.4
.

Given integers
k,l
satisfying

and

\displaystyle\left\|[\partial,(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}]f\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\leq P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(\varepsilon\partial(\varepsilon D_{t})^{3}u)(\partial(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}u)
for an example. We have

\displaystyle\|(\varepsilon\partial(\varepsilon D_{t})^{3}u)(\partial(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}u)\|_{0}^{2}\leq\|\varepsilon^{4}\partial D_{t}^{3}u\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\|\partial(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\leq\|\varepsilon^{4}D_{t}^{3}u\|_{2}^{2}\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\varepsilon^{2}\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{3}u(0,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}u\|_{2}^{2}+\underbrace{\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}u\|_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{4}u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau}_{\leq E_{4}(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(E_{6}(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau},

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|\varepsilon(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}u\|_{2}^{4}+\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{3}u(0,\cdot)\|_{0}^{4}+E_{4}(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(E_{6}(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau

and then apply the same argument to get

\|\varepsilon(\varepsilon D_{t})^{2}u\|_{2}^{4}\leq P(E_{4}(0))+\int_{0}^{t}P(E_{5}(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

We can also apply such method to all the other terms in the commutators. Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|[\partial,(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l}]f\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\leq P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(A.7)

for
f=u,B,q,S
.
∎

Lemma A.5
.

Given integers
k,l
satisfying
0\leq l\leq 3
and
0\leq k\leq 3-l
, under the setting of Theorem
1.1
, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},g]f\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}\leq P(E(0))+P(E(t))\int_{0}^{t}P(E(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(A.8)

for
(f,g)=(\varepsilon D_{t}q,a),(\varepsilon D_{t}u,\rho),(\varepsilon(\nabla\times B),B)
.

Proof.

We only consider the estimates for
\left\|[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{k+2l},\varepsilon B\times](\nabla\times B)\right\|_{3-k-l}^{2}
with
k=0,l=3
, which is also the most difficult case, in which it contains the highest number of material derivatives and
\nabla\times B
is a normal derivative instead of a tangential derivative.

In
[(\varepsilon D_{t})^{6},\varepsilon B\times](\nabla\times B)
, the leading-order part gives us two terms (with certain coefficients)

(\varepsilon^{6}D_{t}^{6}B)\times(\varepsilon\nabla\times B)\text{ and }(\varepsilon D_{t}B)\times(\varepsilon^{6}D_{t}^{5}\nabla\times B).

These terms are controlled in the same way as in Lemma
A.4
. For example, the first term is controlled by

\displaystyle\|(\varepsilon^{6}D_{t}^{6}B)\times(\varepsilon\nabla\times B)\|_{0}^{2}\lesssim\|\varepsilon^{6}D_{t}^{6}B\|_{0}^{2}\|\varepsilon B\|_{3}^{2},

where
\|\varepsilon^{6}D_{t}^{6}B\|_{0}^{2}
has been controlled in Proposition
3.4
and Lemma
A.2
gives

\|\varepsilon B\|_{3}^{2}\lesssim\|\varepsilon B_{0}\|_{3}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\varepsilon D_{t}B(\tau,\cdot)\|_{3}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

The second term is controlled in a similar manner

\displaystyle\left\|(\varepsilon D_{t}B)\times(\varepsilon^{6}D_{t}^{5}\nabla\times B)\right\|_{0}^{2}\lesssim\|\varepsilon D_{t}B\|_{2}^{2}\|\varepsilon^{6}D_{t}^{5}\nabla\times B\|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|\varepsilon D_{t}B\|_{2}^{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\|\varepsilon^{5}D_{t}^{5}\nabla\times B(0,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{6}\nabla\times B(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|\varepsilon^{2}D_{t}B\|_{2}^{4}+\|\varepsilon^{5}D_{t}^{5}\nabla\times B(0,\cdot)\|_{0}^{4}+\underbrace{\|\varepsilon D_{t}B\|_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|(\varepsilon D_{t})^{6}\nabla\times B(\tau,\cdot)\|_{0}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau}_{\leq E_{4}(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(E_{7}(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~P(E_{4}(0))+\int_{0}^{t}P(E_{4}(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau+P(E_{6}(0))+E_{4}(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(E_{7}(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

The other terms can be controlled in the same way and we skip the details.
∎

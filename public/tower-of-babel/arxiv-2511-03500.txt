A New Model for Compactly Generated Derived Categories of the Second Kind and Curved Koszul Triality
Yannick Hoyer and Kristoffer Rank Rasmussen

Abstract
For any curved differential graded algebra A A , we define a new model structure on the category of curved differential graded A A -modules, called the injective Guan-Lazarev model structure. We prove that the category of CDG A A -modules with this model structure is Quillen equivalent to the category of curved differential graded contramodules over the extended bar-construction of A A , equipped with the contraderived model structure. This result can be seen as bridging the gap between Positselski’s theory of conilpotent Koszul triality and Guan-Lazarev’s work on non-conilpotent Koszul duality. As an application, we use the injective Guan-Lazarev model structure to show that the tensor product is a Quillen bifunctor with respect to these model structures of the second kind.



Introduction

Koszul duality for differential graded algebras (DG-algebras) is a fundamental phenomenon in homological algebra and representation theory dating back to the work of Beilinson, Ginzburg and Soergel
[
2
]
. Originally thought of as a relationship between graded algebras inducing an equivalence between bounded (derived) module categories, it was observed by Positselski
[
12
,
14
]
that Koszul duality can be interpreted more naturally as a relationship between DG-algebras and curved differential graded coalgebras (CDG-coalgebras). In this more modern setting, Koszul duality identifies the derived category of any DG-algebra
A
with a certain exotic derived category of CDG-comodules over a CDG-coalgebra
BA
, called the bar construction of
A
. This exotic derived category, called the coderived category of CDG
BA
-comodules, arises as the homotopy category of a certain model category structure on the category of CDG
BA
-comodules, and is denoted
D^{\text{co}}(BA\mathchar 45\relax comod)
. We will refer to this model category structure as the coderived model structure. This approach allows for quite general versions of Koszul duality, however, two restrictions remain. Firstly, on the algebra side, curvature cannot be permitted or else the derived category of
A
is not defined. Secondly,
BA
will always be a conilpotent CDG-coalgebra. The first point already indicates that, in order to lift these restrictions, it is necessary to consider a different kind of derived category on the algebra side, which does not depend on the notion of cohomology.

To solve this problem, Guan and Lazarev introduce a model structure
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{co}^{\text{II}}
on the category
A\mathchar 45\relax mod
of CDG
A
-modules over any CDG-algebra
A
, whose homotopy category is denoted
D^{\text{II}}_{c}(A\mathchar 45\relax mod)
(see
[
6
, Theorem 3.7]
) and which can be seen as an analogue to the usual projective model structure on the category of DG-modules over a DG-algebra. It is constructed by first considering a larger, possibly non-conilpotent, version of the classical bar construction, called the extended bar construction of
A
, denoted
\check{B}A
. There is a natural homogeneous
k
-linear map
\tau:\check{B}A\rightarrow A
of degree 1 corresponding to a Maurer-Cartan element in the convolution algebra
Hom_{k}(\check{B}A,A)
. This gives rise to the adjunction

\check{B}A\otimes^{\tau}-:A\mathchar 45\relax mod\rightleftarrows\check{B}A\mathchar 45\relax comod:A\otimes^{\tau}-,

where the twisted tensor product
\otimes^{\tau}
is the usual tensor product but equipped with a differential induced by
\tau
.

Then the model structure on the category of CDG
A
-modules is obtained by transferring the coderived model structure on the category of CDG-comodules over the extended bar construction along the functor
\check{B}A\otimes^{\tau}-
. This transferred model structure automatically gives them a Quillen adjunction to the coderived model structure on the category of CDG
\check{B}A
-comodules, which is then shown to be a Quillen equivalence. This proves an instance of Koszul duality for CDG-algebras and non-conilpotent CDG-coalgebras.

It is well known that, for any CDG-coalgebra
C
, one can consider the corresponding category of (left) CDG
C
-comodules. However, there is also the lesser known notion of (left) curved differential graded contramodule over
C
(CDG
C
-contramodule), which is obtained by inverting the arrows in the characterization of CDG-modules via hom spaces. Contramodules were studied extensively by Positselski
[
12
,
13
]
. Just like in the case of CDG-comodules, the category of CDG-contramodules over
C
can be endowed with a model structure, whose homotopy category is called the contraderived category of CDG
C
-contramodules (see
[
12
, Section 8.2]
), and which is denoted
D^{\text{ctr}}(C\mathchar 45\relax contra)
. We will refer to this model category structure as the contraderived model structure. It was shown by Positselski in
[
12
, Section 5.2]
that there is a natural Quillen equivalence between the coderived model structure and the contraderived model structure for any CDG-coalgebra
C
. This phenomenon is known as comodule-contramodule correspondence. Moreover, as was proven by Positselski in
[
12
, Section 6.3]
, for any DG-algebra
A
, a contraderived version of Koszul duality can be established, identifying the derived category of
A
with the contraderived category of CDG
BA
-contramodules. It is also proven in
[
12
, Section 6.5]
that both Koszul duality and comodule-contramodule correspondence are compatible with one another in the sense that, for any DG-algebra
A
, there is a commutative triangle of equivalences of triangulated categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{co}(BA\mathchar 45\relax comod)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(A\mathchar 45\relax mod)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{ctr}(BA\mathchar 45\relax contra)\ .}

This compatibility is commonly referred to as Koszul triality. It is one of the main goals of this paper to establish a curved, non-conilpotent version of Koszul triality in the framework established by Guan and Lazarev.

Let us point out our main results. Replacing CDG-comodules by CDG-contramodules in the setting of
[
6
]

\tau:\check{B}A\to A
gives rise to an adjunction

Hom^{\tau}(A,-):\check{B}A\mathchar 45\relax contra\rightleftarrows A\mathchar 45\relax mod:Hom^{\tau}(\check{B}A,-).

We prove that the model structure on
C\mathchar 45\relax contra
can be transferred to
A\mathchar 45\relax mod
along the functor

, giving rise to a model structure on the category of CDG

Preliminaries

1.1
Notations and conventions

Throughout this paper we will work over a fixed ground field

All graded objects will be cohomologically graded over a fixed abelian group

Basic theory of model categories and triangulated categories will be assumed.

1.2
The compactly generated derived category following Guan-Lazarev

In this section, we recall the Guan-Lazarev model structure on the category of curved differential graded modules over a curved differential graded algebra and the associated homotopy category called the compactly generated derived category of the second kind. The connection to non-conilpotent Koszul duality will be discussed briefly.

There are no original contributions present in this section. The main reference is
[
6
]

Definition 1.2.1
.

A curved differential graded algebra (CDG-algebra) is a triple
A=(A,d,h)
, consisting of the following data.

1.

A graded
k
-algebra
A=\bigoplus_{i\in\Gamma}A^{i}
.

2.

A homogeneous degree 1
k
-linear map
d:A\rightarrow A
satisfying the graded Leibniz rule

d(ab)=d(a)b+(-1)^{|a|}ad(b)

for any homogeneous element
a\in A
of degree
|a|
and
b\in A
. We will refer to
d
as the differential of
A
.

3.

A cocycle
h\in A^{2}
satisfying
d^{2}(a)=ha-ah
, called the curvature element of
A
.

A differential graded algebra (DG-algebra) is a CDG-algebra
(A,d,h)
with
h=0
.

Example 1.2.2

(
[
12
, Page 133]
)
.

CDG-algebras appear naturally in different contexts such as geometry, matrix factorizations and deformation theory. A CDG-algebra with non-trivial curvature of geometric origin can be constructed as follows:

•

Let
E
be some vector bundle over a smooth affine variety
X
, together with a connection
\nabla_{E}:E\rightarrow E\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\Omega_{X}^{1}
i.e. a morphism of sheaves satisfying the Leibniz rule.

•

Take the endomorphism bundle
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
with connection
\nabla
induced by
\nabla_{E}
.

•

The algebraic de Rham algebra with coefficients in
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
, differential
\nabla
and curvature
\nabla_{E}^{2}
is a CDG-algebra.

Given a graded coalgebra
C
, we denote by
\Delta
the comultiplication and
C^{\ast}=Hom_{k}(C,k)
the
k
-linear dual algebra. We will use abbreviated sweedler notation
\Delta(c)=c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}
for all

. For any graded left
C
-comodule

, there is a natural left

-action on

given by

-comodule comes equipped with a natural right

-action.

Definition 1.2.3
.

A curved differential graded coalgebra (CDG-coalgebra) is a triple
(C,d,h)
, consisting of the following data.

1.

A graded
k
-coalgebra
C=\bigoplus_{i\in\Gamma}C^{i}

2.

A homogeneous

A\otimes^{\tau}-:\check{B}A\mathchar 45\relax comod\rightarrow A\mathchar 45\relax mod.

Theorem 1.2.23

(
[

Let

1.3
Koszul Triality

This section is meant to collect the basic notions needed to formulate Koszul triality in the sense of Positselski and contains no original contributions. The standard reference for this topic is
[
12
]
.

We start by giving a short introduction to curved differential graded contramodules. For a more thorough introduction to contramodules, we recommend the reference
[
13
]
.

Definition 1.3.1
.

Let
C
be a graded coalgebra over
k
. A graded (left) contramodule
P
over
C
is a graded
k
-vector space
P=\bigoplus_{i\in\Gamma}P^{i}
, endowed with a homogeneous
k
-linear map
\alpha:Hom_{k}(C,P)\to P
of degree zero, satisfying the following contraassociativity and counity conditions.

1.

The two maps

Hom_{k}(C\otimes C,P)\to Hom_{k}(C,P)\xrightarrow{\alpha}P

induced by the comultiplication of
C
and

Hom_{k}(C\otimes C,P)\cong Hom_{k}(C,Hom_{k}(C,P))\xrightarrow{\alpha^{*}}Hom_{k}(C,P)\xrightarrow{\alpha}P

must be equal.

2.

The map

P\cong Hom_{k}(k,P)\to Hom_{k}(C,P)\to P

induced by the counit of
C
must be equal to the identity on
P
.

Given a graded coalgebra
C
and a graded
C
-contramodule
P
, there is a natural left
C^{\ast}
-action on
P
, given by

Hom_{k}(C,k)\otimes P\to Hom_{k}(C,P)\to P.

Definition 1.3.2
.

Let
C
be a CDG-coalgebra. A curved differential graded contramodules over
C
(CDG
C
-contramodule)
P
is a graded
C
-contramodule, endowed with a homogeneous
k
-linear map
d:P\to P
of degree 1, such that the contraaction map
\alpha:Hom_{k}(C,P)\to P
commutes with the differentials on
Hom_{k}(C,P)
and
P
, respectively, and such that
d^{2}(p)=hp
for all
p\in P
.

CDG
C
-contramodules form a locally presentable abelian DG-category in the sense of
[
10
]
, which will be denoted
\text{DG}(C\mathchar 45\relax contra)
. The associated abelian category of CDG
C
-contramodules with closed morphisms between them will be denoted
C\mathchar 45\relax contra
and the associated homotopy category will be denoted
H(C\mathchar 45\relax contra)
.

Definition 1.3.3
.

Let
C
be a CDG-coalgebra. A CDG
C
-contramodule
P
is called contraacyclic, if it belongs to the smallest colocalizing subcategory of
H(C\mathchar 45\relax contra)
containing all totalizations of short exact sequences of CDG
C
-contramodules.

Theorem 1.3.4

(
[
12
, Theorem 8.2]
)
.

Let
C
be a CDG-coalgebra. There exists a cofibrantly generated model category structure on the category of CDG
C
-contramodules with closed morhpisms between them, satisfying the following conditions.

1.

A morphism is a weak equivalence if and only if it has a contraacyclic mapping cone.

2.

The class of fibrations is given by the class of surjections of CDG
C
-contramodules.

3.

The class of cofibrations is given by the class of injections of CDG
C
-contramodules whose cokernel is a graded projective CDG
C
-contramodule.

The homotopy category associated to this model category structure is called the contraderived category of CDG
C
-contramodules and will be denoted
D^{ctr}(C\mathchar 45\relax contra)
.

From now on, the abelian category
C\mathchar 45\relax contra
will always be assumed to be equipped with the model category structure described in Theorem
1.3.4
.

Definition 1.3.5
.

Let
C
be a CDG-coalgebra.

1.

For any right CDG
C
-comodule
N
and any left CDG
C
-contramodule

as the coequalizer of the two maps

which will be denoted

2.

For any left CDG

has a natural structure of CDG

-contramodule via the contraaction map

Hom_{k}(C,Hom_{C}(C,N))\to Hom_{k}(C,Hom_{k}(C,N))\cong Hom_{k}(C\otimes C,N)\to Hom_{k}(C,N)

The following theorem by Positselski is known as the comodule-contramodule correspondence.

Theorem 1.3.6

(
[

Let

2.1
Construction

Let

\check{B}A

\displaystyle Hom^{\tau}(\check{B}(A),-):A\mathchar 45\relax mod\rightleftarrows\check{B}A\mathchar 45\relax contra:Hom^{\tau}(A,-)

constructed as follows. For any CDG
A
-module

be the graded
\check{B}A
-contramodule
Hom_{k}(\check{B}A,M)

and

\check{B}A
-contramodule
P
with contraaction map
\alpha:Hom_{k}(C,P)\to P
, let
Hom^{\tau}(A,P)
be the graded
A
-module
Hom_{k}(A,P)
with differential

d(f)(a)=d_{P}\circ f(a)-(-1)^{|f|}f\circ d_{A}(a)+\alpha(c\mapsto(-1)^{|f|+1+|c||a|}f(\tau(c)a))

for any two homogeneous elements

and

of degree

and

Theorem 2.1.1
.

Let
A
be a CDG-algebra over a field
k
and denote by
\check{B}A
its extended bar construction. There exists a cofibrantly generated model category structure on the category of CDG
A
-modules with closed morhpisms between them, satisfying the following conditions.

1.

An
A
-module homomorphism
f:M\rightarrow N
is a weak equivalences if and only if

f^{*}:Hom_{A}(N,V)\rightarrow Hom_{A}(M,V),

is a quasi-isomorphism for all
A
-modules of the form

for some some finite dimensional
\check{B}A
-contramodule
W
.

2.

The class of cofibrations is given by the class of injections of CDG
A
-modules. In particular, every CDG
A
-module is cofibrant with respect to this model structure.

3.

The class of fibrations is given by the maps having the right lifting property with respect to trivial cofibrations.

The model structure defined above will be referred to as the injective Guan-Lazarev (GL) model structure, and the associated model category will be denoted
A\mathchar 45\relax mod)_{ctr}^{\text{II}}
.

Moreover, the pair of functors

\displaystyle Hom_{k}^{\tau}(\check{B}A,-):A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{ctr}^{\text{II}}\rightleftarrows\check{B}A\mathchar 45\relax contra:Hom_{k}^{\tau}(A,-).

is a Quillen adjunction.

Definition 2.1.2
.

The model structure constructed in
2.1.1
will be referred to as the injective GL model structure and is denoted

.

The proof of Theorem

will be achieved via the following transfer theorem for model structures.

Theorem 2.1.3

(
[
7
, Proposition 2.2.1]
)
.

Consider an adjunction between locally presentable categories

\displaystyle L:\mathcal{C}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{M}:R

where
\mathcal{M}
is a cofibrantly generated model category. Suppose that the following conditions are satisfied.

1.

for every object
X\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a morphism
\epsilon_{X}:QX\rightarrow X
such that
V(\epsilon_{X})
is weak equivalence and
V(QX)
is cofibrant.

2.

For each morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
in
\mathcal{C}
there exists a morphism
Qf:QX\rightarrow QY
satisfying
\epsilon_{Y}\circ Qf=f\circ\epsilon_{X}
.

3.

For every object
X\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a factorisation

\displaystyle QX\coprod QX\xrightarrow{j}Cyl(QX)\xrightarrow{p}QX,

of the fold map
q:QX\coprod QX\rightarrow QX
such that

is a weak equivalence.

Then there exists a cofibrantly generated model structure on
\mathcal{C}
satisfying the following conditions.

1.

A morphism

in
\mathcal{C}
is a weak equivalence if and only if
Lf
is a weak equivalence in
M
.

2.

A morphism
f
in
\mathcal{C}
is a cofibration if and only if
Lf
is a cofibration in
M
.

3.

The class of fibrations in
\mathcal{C}
is given by the class of morphisms having the right lifting property with respect to trivial cofibrations.

This model structure is called the left-induced model structure on
\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 2.1.4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}
is a triangulated category which is generated, as a colocalizing subcategory of itself, by a class of objects
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
. Whenever
X\in\mathcal{T}
is an object satisfying
Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(X,S)=0
, then
X
must be zero. In other words,
\mathcal{T}
is cogenerated by the objects in
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

Consider the full subcategory

\{X\}^{\perp}:=\{Y\in\mathcal{T}|Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)=0\}\subseteq\mathcal{T}.

This subcategory is colocalizing (see for instance
[
1
, Definition 2.5]
) and hence must contain the smallest colocalizing subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
generated by
\mathcal{S}
. But by assumption, this is precisely
\mathcal{T}
. In particular,
X\in\{X\}^{\perp}
, so
X=0
.
∎

Corollary 2.1.5

Let

be a CDG-coalgebra. The triangulated category

is cogenerated by all finite dimensional CDG

-contramodules.

Proof.

This follows directly from Lemma
2.1.4
and the fact that

is generated, as a colocalizing subcategory of itself, by all finite dimensional CDG

-contramodules, see
[

∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Hom_{\check{B}(A)}(F(N),W)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Hom_{A}(N,G(W))}

The categories

Lemma 2.2.1

Proposition 2.2.3
.

The following statements hold for any CDG-algebra

1.

The model structure

In other words,

is a weak equivalence (fibration, respectively) in

.

2.

The model structure
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{ctr}^{\text{II}}
is left induced by the functor

A\mathchar 45\relax mod\xrightarrow{Hom^{\tau}(C,-)}C\mathchar 45\relax contra.

In other words,

is a weak equivalence (cofibration) in

, if and only if

is a weak equivalence (cofibration, respectively) in

.

is right induced by the functor

is left induced by the functor

Proof.

The second and third statements hold by construction.

We will prove assertion 1. The proof for 4 is analogous. Let us denote

,

and

. By Lemma

, there is a natural isomorphism

is right induced by

.

Let

be a weak equivalence in

. It is clear that
R^{\prime}R
preserves trivial fibrations and, therefore, by Ken Brown’s Lemma, weak equivalences between fibrant objects. Since every object is fibrant in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{co}^{\text{II}}
,
R^{\prime}R
preserves all weak equivalences. Hence
R^{\prime}Rf
is a weak equivalence.

Suppose
R^{\prime}Rf:R^{\prime}RM\to R^{\prime}RN
is a weak equivalence in
C\mathchar 45\relax contra
. This is equivalent to the adjoint morphism
L^{\prime}R^{\prime}RM\to RN
being a weak equivalence in
C\mathchar 45\relax comod
, since
R^{\prime}RM
is cofibrant and
RN
is fibrant. It follows that the counit of the Quillen equivalence
(L^{\prime},R^{\prime})
provides a weak equivalence
L^{\prime}R^{\prime}RM\to RM
and we have a commutative triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{\prime}R^{\prime}RM}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces RN}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces RM}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Rf}

We conclude that
Rf
is a weak equivalence by the two-out-of-three property for weak equivalences, hence so is
f
.

If
f
is a fibration in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{co}^{\text{II}}
, then so is
R^{\prime}Rf
. Suppose
R^{\prime}Rf
is a fibration in
C\mathchar 45\relax contra
. In other words,
R^{\prime}Rf
is a surjection. By Lemma
2.2.1
,
R^{\prime}R
is naturally isomorphic to the functor
Hom^{\tau}(C,-)
. Since the underlying graded vector spae of
Hom^{\tau}(C,f)
is
Hom_{k}(C,f)
,
f
is a surjection whenever
Hom^{\tau}(C,f)
is.
∎

Theorem 2.2.4
.

The classes of weak equivalences in both model category structures
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{co}^{\text{II}}
and
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{ctr}^{\text{II}}
coincide. In particular, the identity on
A\mathchar 45\relax mod
induces a Quillen equivalence
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{co}^{\text{II}}\rightleftarrows A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{ctr}^{\text{II}}
.

Proof.

The first statement is immediate from the preceding Proposition
2.2.3
as the functor
C\otimes^{\tau}-
both left induces
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{ctr}^{\text{II}}
and right induces
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{co}^{\text{II}}
.

For the second assertion, note that
id:A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{co}\to A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{ctr}
obviously preserves cofibrations and trivial cofibrations, so we have a Quillen pair. The first part of the theorem implies that this Quillen pair is a Quillen equivalence.
∎

Corollary 2.2.5
.

The homotopy category of the injective GL model structure is Quillen equivalent to the compactly generated derived category of the second kind
D^{\text{II}}_{c}(A)
defined in
[
6
, Theorem 3.7]
.

Proof.

As was shown in Theorem
2.2.4
, the identity on
A\mathchar 45\relax mod
induces a Quillen equivalence between
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{co}
and
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{ctr}^{\text{II}}
, which in turn induces an equivalence on the level of homotopy categories.
∎

Theorem 2.2.6
.

The Quillen adjunction

Hom^{\tau}(C,-):A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{ctr}^{\text{II}}\rightleftarrows C\mathchar 45\relax contra:Hom^{\tau}(A,-)

provided by Theorem
2.1.1
is a Quillen equivalence.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2.1
, the functor
Hom^{\tau}(C,-)
factors as

so

is the right adjoint of a Quillen equivalence between
C\mathchar 45\relax contra
and
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{co}^{\text{II}}
. In particular, the right derived functor

is an equivalence of triangulated categories
D_{c}^{\text{II}}(A\mathchar 45\relax mod)\simeq D^{ctr}(C\mathchar 45\relax contra)
. Since every object in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{co}^{\text{II}}
is fibrant, we have
\mathbb{R}Hom^{\tau}(C,M)=Hom^{\tau}(C,M)
for any CDG
A
-module

. Now it follows from Theorem
2.2.4
, and the fact that every object in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{ctr}^{\text{II}}
is cofibrant, that

induces an equivalence of homotopy categories when viewed as a left Quillen functor
A\mathchar 45\relax mod_{ctr}^{\text{II}}\to C\mathchar 45\relax contra
.
∎

Corollary 2.2.7
.

The compactly generated derived category of the second kind
D^{\text{II}}_{c}(A)
is cogenerated by all CDG
A
-modules of the form

for some finitely generated CDG

-contramodule

. Moreover, any fibrant object in

can be obtained as a retract of an inverse limit of these cogenerators.

Proof.

It was proven in Lemma

that the contraderived category

is cogenerated by all finite dimensional CDG

-contramodules. By Theorem

, the image of these objects under the derived functor

provides a class of cogenerators of

. Now it remains to notice that
\mathbb{R}Hom^{\tau}(A,P)=Hom^{\tau}(A,P)
for any CDG
C
-contramodule

since every object in

For the second assertion let

, a fibrant replacement for

), so

∎

Corollary 2.2.8
.

Any fibrant object in

∎

2.3
Abelian model structures

The goal of this section is to prove that both model structures
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{co}
and
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{ctr}
are abelian, thus obtaining an explicit description of the cofibrations in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{co}
and the fibrations in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{ctr}
, respectively.

We recall the notion of abelian model structures.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category. We say that a model structure on
\mathcal{A}
is abelian, if the following two conditions are satisfied.

1.

A morphism is a cofibration if and only if it is an injection with cofibrant cokernel.

2.

A morphism is a fibration if and only if it is a surjection with fibrant kernel.

Lemma 2.3.2
.

Let
A
be a CDG-algebra.

1.

Any cofibration in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{co}
is an injection with cofibrant cokernel.

2.

Any fibration in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{ctr}
is a surjection with fibrant kernel.

Proof.

We only prove the second statement. The proof for the first statement is analogous. Let
f:M\to N
be a fibration in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{ctr}
. It follows from Theorem
2.2.4
that any fibration in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{ctr}
is also a fibration in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{co}
, hence a surjection.

We claim that
ker(f)
is fibrant. Let
p:X\to Y
be a trivial cofibration and consider the following lifting problem.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ker(f)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

Denote by
\iota:ker(f)\to M
the canonical inclusion and consider the following commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ker(f)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

As
f
is a fibration, there exists a lift
h^{\prime}:Y\to M
, making the outer diagram commute. In particular,
f\circ h^{\prime}=0
, so
h^{\prime}
factors through
ker(f)
, providing the desired lift
h:Y\to ker(f)
.
∎

To show the converse inclusions, we will use the following lifting criterion for injections and surjections in abelian categories.

Lemma 2.3.3

(
[
11
, Lemma 9.1.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category,
i:X\to Y
an injection in
\mathcal{A}
and
p:M\to N
a surjection in
\mathcal{A}
. If
Ext^{1}(coker(i),ker(p))=0
, then any lifting problem of the following form admits a solution.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Lemma 2.3.4
.

Let
M\in A\mathchar 45\relax mod
be a CDG
A
-module whose image in
D_{c}^{\text{II}}(A\mathchar 45\relax mod)
is zero.

1.

For any cofibrant object
X
in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{co}
we have
Ext^{1}(X,M)=0
.

2.

For any fibrant object
Y
in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{ctr}
we have
Ext^{1}(M,Y)=0
.

Proof.

We only prove the second statement. The first assertion is proven dually.

Suppose first that
Y
is of the form
Hom^{\tau}(A,P)
for some finite dimensional CDG
C
-contramodule
P
. Suppose we are given a short exact sequence of CDG
A
-modules

0\to Y\xrightarrow{f}X\xrightarrow{g}M\to 0.

As
Y
is graded injective, applying the DG-functor
Hom_{A}(-,Y)
to the above short exact sequence yields a short exact sequence of complexes of vector spaces

0\to Hom_{A}(M,Y)\xrightarrow{g^{\ast}}Hom_{A}(X,Y)\xrightarrow{f^{\ast}}Hom_{A}(Y,Y)\to 0.

By assumption,
Hom_{A}(M,Y)
is acyclic, so
f^{\ast}
must be a quasi-isomorphism by the long exact sequence in cohomology. In particular, there exist morphisms of CDG
A
-modules
\phi:X\to Y
and
\psi:Y\to Y
of degree
0
and
1
, respectively, such that
id_{Y}=\phi\circ f+d(\psi)
. As
f^{\ast}
is surjective, we can choose some morphism of CDG
A
-modules
\psi^{\prime}:X\to Y
satisfying
\psi^{\prime}\circ f=\psi
. A straightforward calculation shows that
(\phi-d(\psi^{\prime}))\circ f=id_{Y}
, hence the original short exact sequence splits.

The general case now follows from Corollary
2.2.7
, together with the observation that any direct limit of acyclic complexes of vector spaces is acyclic. Indeed, if we replace
Y
in the above argument by a direct limit of CDG
A
-modules of the form
Hom^{\tau}(A,P)
for some finite dimensional CDG
C
-contramodule
P
, then
Hom_{A}(M,Y)
is a direct limit of acyclic complexes of vector spaces, hence acyclic.
∎

Theorem 2.3.5
.

Both model structures
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{co}
and
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{ctr}
are abelian.

Proof.

We only prove the statement for
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{ctr}
. The other case is proven dually.

It is clear that the class of cofibrations in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{ctr}
equals the class of injections of CDG
A
-modules with cofibrant cokernel.

By Lemma
2.3.2
, any fibration in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod^{\text{II}}_{ctr}
is a surjection with fibrant kernel. Conversely, let
f:M\to N
be a surjection of CDG
A
-modules with fibrant kernel and consider a lifting problem of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

with
i:X\to Y
an acyclic cofibration. We claim that the image of
coker(i)
in
D_{c}^{\text{II}}(A\mathchar 45\relax mod)
is zero. Indeed, let
Q
be a CDG
A
-module of the form
Hom^{\tau}(A,P)
for some finite dimensional CDG
C
-contramodule
P
. Applying the functor
Hom_{A}(-,Q)
to the short exact sequence

0\to X\xrightarrow{i}Y\to coker(i)\to 0

yields an exact sequence of complexes of vector spaces

0\to Hom_{A}(coker(i),Q)\to Hom_{A}(X,Q)\xrightarrow{i^{\ast}}Hom_{A}(Y,Q)\to 0.

As
i
is a weak equivalence,
i^{\ast}
is a quasi-isomorphism, so
Hom_{A}(coker(i),Q)
is acyclic by the long exact sequence in cohomology.

By Lemma
2.3.4
we have
Ext^{1}(coker(i),Q)=0
, so the above lifting problem admits a solution by Lemma
2.3.3
.
∎

2.4
Derived GL-Tensor-Hom Adjunction

As an application of the injective GL model structure, we establish a tensor hom adjunction for the compactly generated derived category of the second kind and provide an example that illustrates the necessity of two distinct model structures for this to work.

We recall the notion of Quillen adjunction in two variables.

Definition 2.4.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{E}
be categories. An adjunction in two variables is given by a tuple
(\boxtimes,Hom_{r},Hom_{l},\varphi_{r},\varphi_{l})

for

,

and

.

The natural isomorphisms

and

Definition 2.4.2
.

Let

and

be CDG-algebras. A CDG

-module. We will denote by

and

we have the two model structures

and

constructed in

.

,

and

-bimodule and

or

is a trivial cofibration in
\mathcal{C}

.

Lemma 2.4.5

(
[

, Theorem 4.6]
,
[

, Remark 4.19]
)
.

Let

and

be CDG-algebras. The projective GL model structure on

.

Let

and

be CDG-algebras. Any generating trivial cofibration in

is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

The finitely generated twisted modules are all fibrant-cofibrant objects in the projective GL model structure. Weak equivalences between fibrant-cofibrant objects are homotopy equivalences.
∎

Lemma 2.4.7
.

Let
A
,

and

be CDG-algebras,

a map of CDG

-bimodules and

-bimodules. Consider the following pushout diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U\otimes_{B}W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U\otimes_{B}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\otimes_{B}W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces id_{U}\otimes_{B}g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\otimes_{B}id_{W}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner}

There is a natural isomorphism of CDG

-bimodules

\displaystyle\mapsto i_{1}(a)+i_{2}(b)

Observation 2.4.8
.

Lemma 2.4.9

Let

,

and

be CDG-algebras. Let
f,g:M\rightarrow N
be maps of
A\mathchar 45\relax B
-bimodules and assume that
f
and
g
are homotopic as
A\mathchar 45\relax B
-bimodule maps. Let
K
be a
B\mathchar 45\relax D
-bimodule, then
f\otimes_{B}K
and
g\otimes_{B}K
are homotopic as
A\mathchar 45\relax D
-bimodule maps.

Proof.

Let
h:M\rightarrow N
be homotopy between
f
and
g
, i.e.
h
a degree 1 map satisfying

\displaystyle f-g=d_{N}h+hd_{M}.

It can easily be verified that
h\otimes_{B}\text{id}_{K}
is a homotopy between
f\otimes_{B}\text{id}_{K}
and
g\otimes_{B}\text{id}_{K}
.
∎

Theorem 2.4.10
.

Let
A
,
B
and
D
be CDG-algebras and consider the three model categories
A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax B_{co}^{\text{II}}
,
B\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D_{co}^{\text{II}}
and
A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D_{ctr}^{\text{II}}
. The functors

\displaystyle Hom_{A}

\displaystyle:(A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax B_{co}^{\text{II}})^{op}\times(A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D_{ctr}^{\text{II}})\rightarrow B\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D_{co}^{\text{II}},

\displaystyle Hom_{D}

\displaystyle:(B\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D_{co}^{\text{II}})^{op}\times(A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D_{ctr}^{\text{II}})\rightarrow A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax B_{co}^{\text{II}},

\displaystyle\otimes_{B}

\displaystyle:(A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax B_{co}^{\text{II}})\times(B\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D_{co}^{\text{II}})\rightarrow A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D_{ctr}^{\text{II}}

constitute a Quillen adjunction of two variables. In particular, the corresponding derived functors consitute an adjunction in two variables

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}Hom_{A}^{\text{II}}

\displaystyle:D_{c}^{\text{II}}(A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax B)^{op}\times D_{c}^{\text{II}}(A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D)\rightarrow D_{c}^{\text{II}}(B\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D),

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}Hom_{C}^{\text{II}}

\displaystyle:D_{c}^{\text{II}}(B\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D)^{op}\times D_{c}^{\text{II}}(A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D)\rightarrow D_{c}^{\text{II}}(A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax B),

\displaystyle\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}

\displaystyle:D_{c}^{\text{II}}(A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax B)\times D_{c}^{\text{II}}(B\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D)\rightarrow D_{c}^{\text{II}}(A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D).

Proof.

We have to show the two criteria listed in Definition
2.4.4
are fulfilled. Let
f:U\rightarrow V
and
g:W\rightarrow X
be cofibrations in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax B_{co}^{\text{II}}
and
B\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D_{co}^{\text{II}}
, respectively. The projective GL model structure is cofibrantly generated, so according to
[
9
, Lemma 4.2.4]
, we can assume that
f
and
g
are generating cofibrations.

1.

Finitely generated twisted
A\mathchar 45\relax B
-bimodules are free right
B
-modules and similarly finitely generated twisted
B\mathchar 45\relax C
-bimodules are free left
B
-modules, thus
f\otimes_{B}W
and
U\otimes_{B}g
are injections. Define

\displaystyle\phi:(V\otimes_{B}W)\oplus(U\otimes_{B}X)

\displaystyle\rightarrow V\otimes X

\displaystyle(v\otimes_{B}w,u\otimes_{B}x)

\displaystyle\mapsto v\otimes_{B}g(w)+f(u)\otimes_{B}x

then
(V\otimes_{B}W\oplus U\otimes_{B}X)/\text{ker}(\phi)\cong(V\otimes_{B}W)\oplus(U\otimes_{B}X)/\sim
. We conclude that
f\ \square\ g
is an injection and, in particular, a cofibration in
A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D_{ctr}^{\text{II}}
.

2.

Assume that
f
is a generating trivial cofibration, then, according to Lemma
2.4.5
,
f
is a homotopy equivalence. Let
f^{\prime}
denote the homotopy inverse. We claim that
f\ \square\ g
is a homotopy equivalence, with homotopy inverse given by the map

\displaystyle\psi:V\otimes X

\displaystyle\rightarrow(V\otimes_{B}W)\oplus(U\otimes_{B}X)/\sim

\displaystyle v\otimes x

\displaystyle\mapsto(0,f^{\prime}(v)\otimes_{B}x).

Indeed, the composition
(f\ \square\ g)\circ\psi
is given by
v\otimes_{B}x\mapsto f\circ f^{\prime}(v)\otimes_{B}x
, thus
(f\ \square\ g)\circ\psi
is homotopic to
\text{id}_{V\otimes_{B}X}
. Conversely, the composition
\psi\circ(f\ \square\ g)
is given by

\displaystyle(v\otimes_{B}w,u\otimes_{B}x)

\displaystyle\mapsto(0,f^{\prime}(v)\otimes_{B}g(w)+f^{\prime}\circ f(u)\otimes_{B}x)

\displaystyle=(f\circ f^{\prime}(v)\otimes_{B}w,f^{\prime}\circ f(u)\otimes_{B}x),

thus
\psi\circ(f\ \square\ g)
is homotopic to
\text{id}_{(V\otimes_{B}W)\oplus(U\otimes_{B}X)/\sim}
.

∎

The injective GL model structure remedies the standing problem that

\displaystyle\otimes_{B}

\displaystyle:A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax B^{\text{II}}_{co}\times B\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D^{\text{II}}_{co}\rightarrow A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D^{\text{II}}_{co}

is generally not a Quillen bifunctor. This is evident from the example below.

Example 2.4.11
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field,
A=k[x]
with
|x|=0
and
B=k\langle\epsilon\rangle
with
|\epsilon|=1
.

Consider the
B\mathchar 45\relax A
-bimodule
X=(k\langle\epsilon\rangle\otimes k[x],d_{X})
with differential given by
d(1\otimes 1)=\epsilon\otimes x
. Clearly,
A
is cofibrant as an
A
-module and
X
is cofibrant as an
A\mathchar 45\relax B
-bimodule. We want to show that
X\otimes_{A}A
is not cofibrant as a
B
-module. Let
{{}_{B}X}
denote
X
restricted to its
B
-module structure and assume that
X\otimes_{A}A\cong{{}_{B}X}
is cofibrant. The closed map
\psi:k[-1]\rightarrow{{}_{B}X}
defined by
1\mapsto\epsilon\otimes 1
is coacyclic
[
12
, page 32]
and therefore is a weak equivalence
[
5
, Proposition 4.9]
. The trivial
B
-module
k
can be cofibrantly replaced

\displaystyle\varphi:\bigoplus_{\lambda\in k}\left(...\xrightarrow{\epsilon}k\langle\epsilon\rangle_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}k\langle\epsilon\rangle_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}k\langle\epsilon\rangle_{\lambda}\right)[-1]\rightarrow k[-1]

where
k\langle\epsilon\rangle_{\lambda}
is the free rank one CDG
B
-module with differential given by multiplication by
\lambda\epsilon
. The map
\varphi
is defined component-wise as the projection

(...,b_{3}\epsilon+a_{3},b_{2}\epsilon+a_{2},b_{1}\epsilon+a_{1})\mapsto a_{1},

where
a_{i},b_{i}\in k
. It is a straightforward computation to verify that
\varphi
is a weak equivalence using the fact that
D^{\text{II}}_{c}(B)
is compactly generated by the finitely generated twisted rank one modules
B_{\lambda}
(see
[
6
, Example 3.12]
).

The map
\psi\circ\varphi
is a weak equivalence of cofibrant objects, thus it is a homotopy equivalence. Let
\rho:{{}_{B}X}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\lambda\in k}\left(...\xrightarrow{\epsilon}k\langle\epsilon\rangle_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}k\langle\epsilon\rangle_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}k\langle\epsilon\rangle_{\lambda}\right)[-1]
be the homotopy inverse of
\psi\circ\varphi
. Every generator of
X
is in degree 0, while
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in k}\left(...\xrightarrow{\epsilon}k\langle\epsilon\rangle_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}k\langle\epsilon\rangle_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}k\langle\epsilon\rangle_{\lambda}\right)[-1]
is concentrated in positive degrees, so
\rho=0
. Hence
\rho
cannot be a homotopy equivalence. This contradicts the assumption that
{}_{B}X
is cofibrant.

Remark 2.4.12
.

Example
2.4.11
implies that whenever the target
A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D
of
\otimes_{B}
is equipped with the projective GL-model structure, then
\otimes_{B}
is not a Quillen bifunctor in general. The authors do not expect

\displaystyle\otimes_{B}

\displaystyle:A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax B^{\text{II}}_{ctr}\times B\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D^{\text{II}}_{co}\rightarrow A\mathchar 45\relax mod\mathchar 45\relax D^{\text{II}}_{ctr}

to be a Quillen bifunctor either, yet are unaware of a counterexample.

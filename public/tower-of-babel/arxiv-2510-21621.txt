Abstract
. We prove Nash’s G G bound for the Kolmogorov equation with rough coefficients. Our proof is inspired by the treatment of the parabolic problem by Nash (1958) and Fabes and Stroock (1986). To transfer their ideas to the kinetic setting, we employ critical kinetic trajectories. From Nash’s G G bound, we recover the sharp lower bound on the fundamental solution and thus provide an alternative proof of the Harnack inequality for the Kolmogorov equation.



1.
Introduction

We are concerned with weak solutions
f\in\operatorname{L}^{2}_{t,x}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\operatorname{H}_{v}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
satisfying the Kolmogorov equation

(1.1)

(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x})f-\nabla_{v}\cdot(\mathfrak{a}\nabla_{v}f)=0

in the distributional sense, where
T>0
. The matrix-valued function
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}(t,x,v)\in\operatorname{L}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is a rough diffusion coefficient satisfying the ellipticity bounds

(
H1
)

0<\lambda:=\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
(t,x,v)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}\end{subarray}}\frac{\langle\mathfrak{a}(t,x,v)\xi,\xi\rangle}{\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}},

(
H2
)

\Lambda:=\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
(t,x,v)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\lvert\mathfrak{a}(t,x,v)\xi\right\rvert^{2}}{\langle\mathfrak{a}(t,x,v)\xi,\xi\rangle}<\infty.

We refer to
[
4
,
8
]
for more details on the definition of weak solutions and how to work with them.

The kinetic De Giorgi–Nash–Moser theory is concerned with proving a priori
Hölder continuity of weak solutions to (
1.1
). It is inspired by
the treatment of De Giorgi
[
6
]
, Nash

[
20
]
, and Moser

[
17
,
16
,
19
,
18
]

of the elliptic and parabolic problems. The Hölder continuity was first
obtained in
[
24
]
and strengthened later by a proof of the
stronger Harnack inequality in the influential paper
[
10
]
.
We mention only the subsequent improvements

[
11
,
12
,
7
,
8
]
and refer to the introduction of

[
8
]
for more details. The existing literature mainly
follows the ideas of De Giorgi and Moser.

In this article, we explain how to transfer Nash’s ideas to the kinetic setting.
Let us first comment on his treatment of the parabolic problem. Nash’s toolbox
consisted of an
\operatorname{L}^{\infty}
bound, a bound on the first spatial moment, and the
so-called
G
bound, i.e. a universal lower bound for the spatial integral of
the logarithm of the fundamental solution. The
G
bound prevents the kernel
from being too small on large sets and, combined with the moment control, yields
a quantitative overlap of kernels started from nearby points. This overlap
gives scale-invariant decay of oscillation, from which local Hölder continuity
of weak solutions follows.

Still in the parabolic setting, Aronson later established two-sided Gaussian
bounds for the fundamental solutions to the parabolic problem with rough
coefficients
[
2
]
based on the work of Nash. Moreover, he rigorously
established the existence of the fundamental solution
[
3
]
and gave
an alternative proof of the lower bound based on the iteration of Moser’s
parabolic Harnack inequality. Nash’s proof was revisited and simplified by
Fabes and Stroock in
[
9
]
, where they also demonstrated how to obtain
sharp bounds on the fundamental solution directly from the
G
bound. Together
with the representation formula for weak solutions, one may derive Moser’s
parabolic Harnack inequality and deduce Hölder continuity as a corollary.

Let us now recall the state-of-the-art in the kinetic setting.
The existence of the fundamental solution to the Kolmogorov equation (
1.1
) with rough coefficients has been proven in
[
5
]
.
There exists a measurable kernel
\Gamma(t,x,v,s,y,w)
, defined for
s<t
, which is a fundamental solution to the problem (
1.1
).
Weak solutions, in the above sense, are represented by this kernel.
We refer to
[
5
]
for precise statements.

The fundamental solution satisfies the pointwise upper bound

(1.2)

\Gamma(t,x,v,s,y,w)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{(t-s)^{2d}}\exp\left(-C_{1}\left(\frac{|x-y-(t-s)w|^{2}}{(t-s)^{3}}+\frac{|v-w|^{2}}{t-s}\right)\right)

with
C_{0}=C_{0}(d,\lambda,\Lambda)>0
and
C_{1}=C_{1}(\lambda,\Lambda)>0
, see
[
5
, Theorem
4.12]
. Actually, in
[
5
]

the estimate was proven only for
\left\lvert\Gamma\right\rvert
, but
\Gamma\geq 0
by the maximum principle
for weak solutions. We emphasise that the proof in

[
5
]
of the upper bound relies on the

\operatorname{L}^{2}
–
\operatorname{L}^{\infty}
estimate proven in the context of kinetic De Giorgi–Nash–Moser
theory, see
[
23
,
10
,
8
]
,
and in turn on the kinetic Sobolev inequality. Using the Harnack inequality,

[
10
, Theorem 1.6]
or

[
8
, Theorem 7.5]
, a lower bound on
\Gamma
in terms of the
profile in (
1.2
) can be deduced via so-called Harnack chains for
kinetic cylinders, see
[
15
]
.

In this article, we provide an alternative approach to prove the lower bound by following the ideas of Nash.
We make precise connections between Nash’s and Moser’s work and give a trajectorial proof which does not rely on a spatial Poincaré inequality with Gaussian weight as is used in
[
20
,
9
]
.
Our main contribution is the following kinetic version of Nash’s
G
bound.

Lemma 1.1
.

There exists a constant
C=C(d,\lambda,\Lambda)<\infty
such that for all
\left\lvert(x,v)\right\rvert\leq 1
we have

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})}\log\Gamma(1,y,w,0,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\geq-C.

Let us lay out our strategy to prove it. We use critical kinetic trajectories
(Section
2
) developed in
[
8
]
, see also

[
12
,
21
,
1
,
22
]
,
to prove an
\operatorname{L}^{1}
-estimate for the difference of the logarithm of the
fundamental solution and the integral on the left-hand side of (
1.3

Together with moment bounds (supplied by the upper bound (
1.2
)) we
deduce the universal
G
bound in Section
3
.

From the kinetic
G
bound, we infer the following sharp lower bound on the fundamental solution in Section
4
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exist positive constants
c_{0}=c_{0}(d,\lambda,\Lambda)
and
c_{1}=c_{1}(d,\lambda,\Lambda)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{(t-s)^{2d}}\exp\left(-c_{1}\left(\frac{|x-y-(t-s)w|^{2}}{(t-s)^{3}}+\frac{|v-w|^{2}}{t-s}\right)\right)\leq\Gamma(t,x,v,s,y,w)

for almost all
s<t
and
x,y,v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The representation of weak solutions in terms of the fundamental solution
together with the lower and upper bounds readily implies the kinetic Harnack
inequality. See
[
10
, Theorem 1.6]
or

[
8

for precise statements which we do not
make here.

2.
Critical kinetic trajectories

We recall the notion of kinetic trajectories and the special subclass of
critical ones, which were introduced and developed in
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})
and
(t_{1},x_{1},v_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{1+2d}
with
t_{0}\neq t_{1}
.

A family of kinetic trajectories is a map

\gamma=\gamma(r)=\gamma(r;(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0}),(t_{1},x_{1},v_{1}))=(\gamma_{t}(r),\gamma_{x}(r),\gamma_{v}(r))\in\mathbb{R}^{1+2d}

defined for
r\in[0,1]
, that is

•

continuous over
r\in[0,1]
(and in particular bounded),

•

differentiable over
r\in(0,1)
,

•

with endpoints
\gamma(0)=(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})
and
\gamma(1)=(t_{1},x_{1},v_{1})
,

•

satisfying the constraint (kinetic relation)

\dot{\gamma}_{x}(r)=\dot{\gamma}_{t}(r)\gamma_{v}(r)
for
r\in(0,1)
.

A family of kinetic trajectories is called
critical
if it
additionally satisfies

\det\left(\nabla_{(t_{1},x_{1},v_{1})}\gamma(r;(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0}),(t_{1},x_{1},v_{1}))\right)\sim\left\lvert\frac{\gamma_{t}(r)-t_{0}}{t_{1}-t_{0}}\right\rvert^{{2+4d}}\quad\text{ as }r\to 0^{+}

and

(2.1)

\left|\dot{\gamma}_{t}(r)\right|\left|\left(\left[\nabla_{(x_{1},v_{1})}\gamma_{x,v}(r;(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0}),(t_{1},x_{1},v_{1}))\right]^{-1}\right)_{\cdot;2}\right|\sim|\dot{\gamma}_{v}(r)|\quad\text{ as }r\to 0^{+}.

Note that in practice we take
\dot{\gamma}_{t}
constant.

For our purposes, we only need the outcome of the construction given in
[
8
]
: there exists a family of critical kinetic trajectories, explicitly built from forcings with desynchronised logarithmic oscillations, which interpolate between any two endpoints.
These kinetic trajectories are of the form

\gamma(r)=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{t}(r)\\
\gamma_{x}(r)\\
\gamma_{v}(r)\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}t_{0}+(t_{1}-t_{0})r\\
\mathcal{A}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}(r)\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
v_{1}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{B}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}(r)\begin{pmatrix}x_{0}\\
v_{0}\end{pmatrix}\end{pmatrix},

with matrices
\mathcal{A}_{t_{1}-t_{0}},\mathcal{B}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2d\times 2d}
, and
satisfy the following properties:

(1)

The map
\gamma
forms a family of
critical kinetic trajectories in the sense of Definition

2.1
with constant
\dot{\gamma}_{t}=t_{1}-t_{0}
.

(2)

\mathcal{A}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2d\times 2d}
satisfies

(a)

\mathcal{A}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}(0)=0
,
\mathcal{A}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}(1)=\mathrm{Id}_{2d}
,

(b)

\det\mathcal{A}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}(r)=r^{2d}
,

(c)

\left\lvert(\mathcal{A}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}(r)^{-1})_{i;2}\right\rvert\lesssim(1+\left\lvert t_{1}-t_{0}\right\rvert)r^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for
i=1,2
and
r\in(0,1]
,

(3)

\mathcal{B}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2d\times 2d}
satisfies

(a)

\mathcal{B}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}(0)=\mathrm{Id}_{2d}
,
\mathcal{B}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}(1)=0
,

(b)

\det\mathcal{B}_{t_{1}-t_{0}}(r)\gtrsim 1
near
r=0
.

(4)

The trajectory satisfies the following bounds for some universal constants

\begin{cases}\left\lvert\gamma_{x}(r)-x_{0}-r(t_{1}-t_{0})v_{0}\right\rvert\lesssim\left(\left\lvert x_{0}\right\rvert+\left\lvert x_{1}\right\rvert\right)r^{\frac{3}{2}}+\left\lvert t_{1}-t_{0}\right\rvert r^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\left\lvert v_{0}\right\rvert+\left\lvert v_{1}\right\rvert\right),\\[5.69054pt]
\left\lvert\gamma_{v}(r)-v_{0}\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert t_{1}-t_{0}\right\rvert^{-1}\left(\left\lvert x_{0}\right\rvert+\left\lvert x_{1}\right\rvert\right)r^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\left\lvert v_{0}\right\rvert+\left\lvert v_{1}\right\rvert\right)r^{\frac{1}{2}},\\[5.69054pt]
\left\lvert\dot{\gamma}_{v}(r)\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lvert t_{1}-t_{0}\right\rvert^{-1}\left(\left\lvert x_{0}\right\rvert+\left\lvert x_{1}\right\rvert\right)r^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\left\lvert v_{0}\right\rvert+\left\lvert v_{1}\right\rvert\right)r^{-\frac{1}{2}}.\end{cases}

These features are exactly those needed for our analysis, in particular the criticality condition
(2c)
.

3.
Nash’s
G
bound for the Kolmogorov equation

To prove Nash’s
G
bound, we need first a weak
\operatorname{L}^{1}
estimate for the
logarithm of weak supersolutions, which is reminiscent of the one of Moser, but
where the comparison is with a logarithmic mean on the full space with Gaussian
weight. The estimate is reminiscent of
[
8
, Theorem 6.1]
,
and we follow the proof closely.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be a bounded set. Then, for any positive weak supersolution
f>0
of the Kolmogorov equation (
1.1
) in
\left(\frac{1}{4},1\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, we have

s\left\lvert\left\{(t,x,v)\in\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\right)\times E:\log f(t,x,v)-c(f)>s\right\}\right\rvert\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\Lambda\right)

for all
s>0
with

c(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\log f(1,y,w)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})}\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)

and some universal constant
C=C(d,E)>0
.

Proof.

We may assume that
f\geq\varepsilon

\varepsilon>0

\varepsilon\to 0^{+}

In view of
[
8

The fact that

(3.1)

(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x})\log f\geq\nabla_{v}\cdot(\mathfrak{a}\nabla_{v}\log f)+\langle\mathfrak{a}\nabla_{v}\log f,\nabla_{v}\log f\rangle,

which we can use to absorb any appearing gradient term.

Moreover, we introduce

\varphi(y,w):=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\pi}{2}(\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})}

\operatorname{L}^{1}

(t,x,v)\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})\times E

\gamma

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\frac{1}{1-r}\left\lvert\det\mathcal{B}(r)\right\rvert^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}(\tilde{x},\tilde{v})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\,\varphi(y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{1}\int_{\tilde{B}}\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\left(Mr^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\lvert\nabla_{v}\log f\right\rvert_{\mathfrak{a}}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x},\tilde{v})\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}(y,w)-\left\lvert\nabla_{v}\log f\right\rvert_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x},\tilde{v})\varphi(y,w)\right)_{+}

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\sup_{s\in(0,r_{0})}\frac{\left\lvert\det\mathcal{B}(s)\right\rvert^{-1}}{1-s}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}(\tilde{x},\tilde{v})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\,\varphi(y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w).

We introduce
p:=p(\tilde{t},\tilde{x},\tilde{v},y,w):=\left\lvert\nabla_{v}\log f\right\rvert_{\mathfrak{a}}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x},\tilde{v})\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}(y,w)
, which does not depend on
r
.
With this notation, we can estimate the inner integral as

\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\left(Mr^{-\frac{1}{2}}p-p^{2}\right)_{+}\,\mathrm{d}r=2\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}Mp-\theta p^{2}\leq M^{2}\leq C(d)\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\Lambda\right),

where
\theta=\min\{r_{0},M^{2}/p^{2}\}
.
We have proven the universal bound for the first term as the
\varphi
weight allows us to integrate the indicator function of
\tilde{B}
.

For the integral for large
r\in(r_{0},1)
, we employ Young’s inequality as

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\int_{E}\int_{r_{0}}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(Mr^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\lvert\nabla_{v}\log f\right\rvert_{\mathfrak{a}}(\gamma(r))\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}(y,w)-\left\lvert\nabla_{v}\log f\right\rvert_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}(\gamma(r))\varphi(y,w)\right)_{+}

\displaystyle\hskip 312.9803pt\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\int_{E}\int_{r_{0}}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{1}{4}M^{2}r^{-1}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C(d,\left\lvert E\right\rvert)M^{2}\leq C(d,\left\lvert E\right\rvert)\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\Lambda\right).\qed

We now prove Nash’s
G
bound.

Proof of Lemma
1.1
.

Fix
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
with
\lvert(x,v)\rvert\leq 1
and set

f(t,y,w):=\Gamma(t,y,w,0,x,v),\qquad t>0,\,(y,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

Then,
f
is a nonnegative weak solution of the Kolmogorov equation

(\partial_{t}+w\cdot\nabla_{y})f-\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathfrak{a}\,\nabla_{w}f)=0,

on
(\frac{1}{4},1)\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.

Furthermore, we recall that the mass conservation

(3.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f(t,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)=1

for all
t>0
was proven in
[
5
, Theorem 4.14 (ii)]
.
We may replace
f
by
f+\varepsilon
to ensure positivity and let
\varepsilon\to 0
in the end.
In particular, we may apply Lemma
3.1
on
(\frac{1}{4},1)\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.

We use the upper bound (
1.2
) and deduce that

M(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left\lvert(y,w)\right\rvert f(t,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\leq C_{0}

for all
t\in[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}]
and some dimensional constants
c_{0},C_{0}
depending also on the ellipticity constants. We deduce

\int_{\left\lvert(y,w)\right\rvert\geq 2C_{0}}f(t,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\leq\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\int_{\left\lvert(y,w)\right\rvert\geq 2C_{0}}\left\lvert(y,w)\right\rvert f(t,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\leq\frac{1}{2}.

From (
3.2
) we deduce

\int_{\left\lvert(y,w)\right\rvert\leq 2C_{0}}f(t,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\geq\frac{1}{2}

for all
t\in[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}]
.
In particular,

\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\int_{\left\lvert(y,w)\right\rvert\leq 2C_{0}}f(t,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\geq\frac{1}{4}

The upper bound (
1.2
) also gives an
\operatorname{L}^{\infty}
bound on
Q:=\left[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\right]\times B_{2C_{0}}(0)
, i.e.
\left\lvert f(t,y,w)\right\rvert\leq C_{1}
for some
C_{1}=C_{1}(d,\lambda,\Lambda)
and any
(t,y,w)\in Q
. Let
\eta>0
, and
\Omega=\{(t,y,w)\in Q:f(t,y,w)>\eta\}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q}f(t,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(t,y,w)=\int_{\Omega\cap Q}f(t,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(t,y,w)+\int_{\Omega^{c}\cap Q}f(t,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(t,y,w)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left\lvert\Omega\right\rvert+\eta\left\lvert Q\right\rvert.

For
\eta
small enough, we can absorb the last term and obtain
\left\lvert\Omega\right\rvert\geq C_{2}
for some constant
C_{2}
depending on all the other previous constants.

Now, we apply Lemma
3.1
, the
\operatorname{L}^{1}
estimate for the logarithmic level sets on
Q
(note
\Omega\subset Q
and that
Q
is strictly separated from
\{1\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
) to obtain

(3.3)

\left\lvert\{(t,y,w)\in\Omega:\log f(t,y,w)-G(1)>s\}\right\rvert\leq C_{3}s^{-1},\;s>0,

where

G(1)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\log f(1,y,w)\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})}\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)

and
C_{3}>0
is a universal constant depending on
d,\lambda,\Lambda
.

For a constant
S
large enough, we thus obtain that
\left\lvert\Omega_{S}\right\rvert\geq\frac{\left\lvert\Omega\right\rvert}{2}
, where

\Omega_{S}:=\{(t,y,w)\in\Omega:\log f(t,y,w)-G(1)\leq S\}.

Together with the good bound, we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lvert\Omega\right\rvert}{2}\log\eta

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\Omega_{S}\right\rvert\log\eta\leq\int_{\Omega_{S}}\log\eta\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\leq\int_{\Omega_{S}}\log f(t,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{S}}\big(G(1)+S\big)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\leq(G(1)+S)\left\lvert\Omega_{S}\right\rvert

from which we deduce Nash’s
G
bound as

G(1)\geq\frac{\left\lvert\Omega\right\rvert}{2\left\lvert\Omega_{S}\right\rvert}\log\eta-S.\qed

Remark 3.2
.

This also makes precise the analogy of Moser’s weak
\operatorname{L}^{1}
estimate for the logarithm of supersolutions and Nash’s
G
bound.
Moser’s estimate, together with a bound on the first moments and the
\operatorname{L}^{\infty}
bound, yields Nash’s
G
bound.
We emphasise, however, that the original formulation of Moser’s estimate is written for a weight
\varphi
which has compact support.

The good set, i.e. a set of positive measure where the function is bounded away from zero, is also key in De Giorgi’s approach. In Moser’s argumentation, this is somewhat hidden in the lemma of Bombieri–Giusti

[
8
]
.

Remark 3.3
.

One could prove the moment bound also directly by an application of the kinetic Nash inequality. We refer to
[
8
]
and
[
13
]
.

We need the following similar statement, where the integration is in the last variables of
\Gamma
.
This is immediate in the parabolic setting due to symmetry, but slightly more complicated in the kinetic setting.

Corollary 3.4
.

There exists a constant
C=C(d,\lambda,\Lambda)<\infty
such that for all
\left\lvert(x,v)\right\rvert\leq 1
we have

(3.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{2})}\log\Gamma(2,x,v,1,y,w)\,\mathrm{d}(y,w)\geq-C.

Proof.

Let
t>0
and
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. We consider the shifted diffusion coefficient
\mathfrak{a}_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)=\mathfrak{a}(t-\cdot,\cdot)
and the fundamental solution
\Gamma^{\sharp}_{\mathfrak{a}_{t}}(\sigma,y,w,0,x,v)
to the Kolmogorov equation

(\partial_{\sigma}-y\cdot\nabla_{w})f-\nabla_{w}\cdot(\mathfrak{a}_{t}\nabla_{w}f)=0.

(This equation is equivalent to (
1.1
) via a coordinate change
in the position variable.) Using the defining properties and uniqueness of
the fundamental solution,
[
5
, Definition 3.3 and
Lemma 3.4]
, and the observation that

(s,y,w)\mapsto\Gamma(t,x,v,s,y,w)
is the fundamental solution to the adjoint
problem (
[
5
, Theorem 4.14 (ii)]
) we deduce

\Gamma(t,x,v,t-\sigma,y,w)=\Gamma^{\sharp}_{\mathfrak{a}_{t}}(\sigma,y,w,0,x,v).

Setting
t=2
,
\sigma=1
, and applying Lemma
1.1
to
\Gamma^{\sharp}_{\mathfrak{a}_{t}}
(note that the universal constant depends only on the ellipticity bounds and dimension) gives the desired bound.
∎

4.
The lower bound on the fundamental solution

In this section, we follow
[
9
, Lemma 2.6 and Theorem 2.7]
closely.
We first derive a near-diagonal lower bound and then conclude the sharp lower bound.
We recall some important facts on scaling and Galilean invariance for the Kolmogorov equation with rough diffusion coefficient.
For
(t_{1},x_{1},v_{1}),(t_{2},x_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{1+2d}
we define

(t_{1},x_{1},v_{1})\circ(t_{2},x_{2},v_{2}):=(t_{1}+t_{2},x_{1}+x_{2}+t_{2}v_{1},v_{1}+v_{2}).

The pair
(\mathbb{R}^{1+2d},\circ)
is a non-commutative group.
Every
(t,x,v)
admits a right inverse calculated as
(t,x,v)^{-1}=(-t,-x+tv,-v)
.
For
r>0
, we set the scaling relation

[\delta_{r}f](t,x,v)=f(r^{2}t,r^{3}x,rv).

The Kolmogorov equation (
1.1
) is structurally invariant with respect to the translation along
\circ
and the scaling
\delta_{r}
.
To be more precise if
f
is a weak solution to (
1.1
), then
(t,x,v)\mapsto f((t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})\circ(t,x,v))
and
(t,x,v)\mapsto[\delta_{r}f](t,x,v)
are also weak solutions to (
1.1
) with respective diffusion matrices
\mathfrak{a}((t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})\circ\cdot)
and
\delta_{r}\mathfrak{a}
, which both satisfy (
H1
)–(
H2
) with the same constants
\lambda
and
\Lambda
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists
c_{0}=c_{0}(d,\lambda,\Lambda)>0
and a universal
\rho_{0}\in(0,1]
such that

\Gamma(t,x,v,s,y,w)\geq\frac{c_{0}}{(t-s)^{2d}}

whenever
t>s
and for any
(x,v),(y,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
with

|v-w|\leq\rho_{0}\sqrt{t-s},\qquad|x-y-(t-s)w|\leq\rho_{0}(t-s)^{3/2}.

Proof.

By scaling and Galilean invariance, it suffices to prove the bound for

(s,y,w)=(0,0,0)
,
t=2
,
\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq\rho_{0}
and
\left\lvert v\right\rvert\leq\rho_{0}
. Note that
this rescaling will change
\mathfrak{a}
and hence
\Gamma
but not the ellipticity
bounds, which are the only quantities appearing in the estimates. Let
\varphi(\xi,\eta)=\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})}
, so
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\xi,\eta)=1
and
\varphi\leq 1
.

We fix such
(x,v)
and then write via the semigroup-property (proven in

[
5
, Theorem 4.14 (i)]
) at the midpoint equal to

1
:

\displaystyle\Gamma(2,x,v,0,0,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Gamma(2,x,v,1,\xi,\eta)\,\Gamma(1,\xi,\eta,0,0,0)\,\mathrm{d}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Gamma(2,x,v,1,\xi,\eta)\,\Gamma(1,\xi,\eta,0,0,0)\varphi(\xi,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}(\xi,\eta)

By Jensen’s inequality for the logarithm and the measure
\varphi(\xi,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}(\xi,\eta)
we deduce

\displaystyle\log\Gamma(2,x,v,0,0,0)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\log\Gamma(2,x,v,1,\xi,\eta)\varphi(\xi,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\log\Gamma(1,\xi,\eta,0,0,0)\varphi(\xi,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}(\xi,\eta).

The lower bound is now an immediate consequence of Corollary
3.4
(as
\left\lvert(x,v)\right\rvert\leq 1
) for the first term and Lemma
1.1
for the second term.
∎

We iterate this bound to obtain the sharp decay.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We first reduce to a normalised statement. Left-translate by
(s,y,w)^{-1}
so that
(s,y,w)\mapsto(0,0,0)
and
(t,x,v)\mapsto(\tau,X,V)
with

\tau=t-s,\qquad X:=x-y-\tau w,\qquad V:=v-w.

We dilate by
r=\tau^{-1/2}
so that the time gap becomes
1
. By the scaling identity of
\Gamma
,

\Gamma(t,x,v,s,y,w)=\tau^{-2d}\widetilde{\Gamma}(1,\bar{X},\bar{V},0,0,0),

where
(\bar{X},\bar{V})=(X/\tau^{3/2},V/\tau^{1/2})
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is the kernel for the scaled field
\delta_{\tau}\mathfrak{a}
, still satisfying (
H1
) and (
H2
).

It suffices to prove

\widetilde{\Gamma}(1,\bar{X},\bar{V},0,0,0)\ \gtrsim\ \exp\big[{-}C_{0}(|\bar{X}|^{2}+|\bar{V}|^{2})\big],

with constants depending only on
(d,\lambda,\Lambda)
; this will yield the claimed estimate after undoing the normalisation.

Fix
(\bar{X},\bar{V})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. Choose the smallest integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
with

k\ \geq\ k_{0}\Big(|\bar{V}|^{2}+|\bar{X}|^{2}\Big),

for
k_{0}=k_{0}(\lambda,\Lambda)
to be set large enough below, and partition time by
t_{j}=j/k
,
j=0,\ldots,k
.

We build reference centres
\{(x_{j},v_{j})\}_{j=0}^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
satisfying

(x_{0},v_{0})=(0,0),\qquad(x_{k},v_{k})=(\bar{X},\bar{V}),

(4.1)

x_{j}-x_{j-1}=\Delta tv_{j-1},\;1\leq j\leq k,\qquad\Delta t:=1/k,

and the small-increment condition

(4.2)

|v_{j}-v_{j-1}|\leq\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\sqrt{\Delta t},\;1\leq j\leq k

where
\rho_{0}
is the constant from Lemma
4.1
.

One concrete way to produce such a sequence is: take the linear interpolation
v_{j}^{\rm lin}=\frac{j}{k}\bar{V}
, and correct it by a quadratic profile
\varphi_{j}
with
\varphi_{0}=\varphi_{k}=0
so that
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(v_{j}^{\rm lin}+\varphi_{j})=\bar{X}/\Delta t
.
The increments of a quadratic profile satisfy
|\varphi_{j}-\varphi_{j-1}|\lesssim|\bar{X}|/k^{2}
.
Hence for
k
larger than a dimensional multiple of
|\bar{V}|^{2}+|\bar{X}|^{2}
, the bound (
4.2
) holds, while (
4.1
) then determines
\{x_{j}\}
uniquely with
x_{0}=0
and
x_{k}=\bar{X}
.

For a small parameter
\eta\in(0,\rho_{0}/4]
, define the product set

\mathcal{S}_{\eta}:=\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\Big(B(x_{j},\ \eta\Delta t^{3/2})\times B(v_{j},\ \eta\Delta t^{1/2})\Big)\subset(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{k-1}.

Let
((\xi_{1},\eta_{1}),\dots,(\xi_{k-1},\eta_{k-1}))\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}
and set
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})=(0,0)
and
(\xi_{k},\eta_{k})=(\bar{X},\bar{V})
, then for each step
j=1,\dots,k
the near-diagonal hypotheses of Lemma
4.1
hold between the points
(t_{j-1},\xi_{j-1},\eta_{j-1})
and
(t_{j},\xi_{j},\eta_{j})
. Indeed,

|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}|\leq|\eta_{j}-v_{j}|+|v_{j}-v_{j-1}|+|v_{j-1}-\eta_{j-1}|\leq\eta\sqrt{\Delta t}+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\sqrt{\Delta t}+\eta\sqrt{\Delta t}\leq\rho_{0}\sqrt{\Delta t},

and, using (
4.1
),

\displaystyle|\xi_{j}-\xi_{j-1}-\Delta t\eta_{j-1}|

\displaystyle\leq|\xi_{j}-x_{j}|+|x_{j}-x_{j-1}-\Delta tv_{j-1}|+\Delta t|\eta_{j-1}-v_{j-1}|

\displaystyle\leq\eta(\Delta t)^{3/2}+0+\eta(\Delta t)^{3/2}\leq\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}(\Delta t)^{3/2}.

Therefore, by Lemma
4.1
,

\Gamma(t_{j},\xi_{j},\eta_{j},t_{j-1},\xi_{j-1},\eta_{j-1})\geq\ \frac{c_{0}}{(\Delta t)^{2d}},\qquad j=1,\dots,k.

Applying the semigroup property
(k-1)
-times and restricting the integration to
\mathcal{S}_{\eta}
,

\widetilde{\Gamma}(1,\bar{X},\bar{V},0,0,0)\geq\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\eta}}\ \prod_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma(t_{j},\xi_{j},\eta_{j},t_{j-1},\xi_{j-1},\eta_{j-1})\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\,\mathrm{d}(\xi_{j},\eta_{j})\ \geq\ (c_{0}(\Delta t)^{-2d})^{k}|\mathcal{S}_{\eta}|.

We have

|\mathcal{S}_{\eta}|=\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\big(c_{d}\eta^{2d}(\Delta t)^{2d}\big)=\big(c_{d}\eta^{2d}\big)^{k-1}(\Delta t)^{2d(k-1)},

where
c_{d}>0
is a dimensional constant.
Hence

\widetilde{\Gamma}(1,\bar{X},\bar{V},0,0,0)\geq(\Delta t)^{-2d}\big(c_{0}c_{d}\eta^{2d}\big)^{k}c_{d}^{-1}\eta^{-2d}\geq\ C_{1}\big(\alpha\big)^{k},

with
C_{1}=C_{1}(d,\lambda,\Lambda)>0
and
\alpha:=c_{0}c_{d}\eta^{2d}\in(0,1)
for
\eta
sufficiently small.
Moreover, we dropped the factor
(\Delta t)^{-2d}=k^{2d}
.
Finally, with
k\asymp|\bar{V}|^{2}+|\bar{X}|^{2}
, this gives

\widetilde{\Gamma}(1,\bar{X},\bar{V},0,0,0)\geq C_{1}\exp\big[{-}C_{2}(|\bar{V}|^{2}+|\bar{X}|^{2})\big],

for some
C_{2}=C_{2}(d,\lambda,\Lambda)>0
.
∎

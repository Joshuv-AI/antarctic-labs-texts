BusterX++: Towards Unified Cross-Modal AI-Generated Content Detection and Explanation with MLLM
Haiquan Wen, Tianxiao Li, Zhenglin Huang, Yiwei He, Guangliang Cheng

Abstract
Recent advances in generative AI have dramatically improved image and video synthesis capabilities, significantly increasing the risk of misinformation through sophisticated fake content. In response, detection methods have evolved from traditional approaches to multimodal large language models (MLLMs), offering enhanced transparency and interpretability in identifying synthetic media. However, current detection systems remain fundamentally limited by their single-modality design. These approaches analyze images or videos separately, making them ineffective against synthetic content that combines multiple media formats. To address these challenges, we introduce BusterX++ , a novel framework designed specifically for cross-modal detection and explanation of synthetic media. Our approach incorporates an advanced reinforcement learning (RL) post-training strategy that eliminates cold-start. Through Multi-stage Training, Thinking Reward, and Hybrid Reasoning, BusterX++ achieves stable and substantial performance improvements. To enable comprehensive evaluation, we also present GenBuster++ , a cross-modal benchmark leveraging state-of-the-art image and video generation techniques. This benchmark comprises 4,000 images and video clips, meticulously curated by human experts using a novel filtering methodology to ensure high quality, diversity, and real-world applicability. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of our approach.

3.2
Benchmark Construction

Our benchmark leverages state-of-the-art image and video generation techniques and consists of 4,000 images and video clips. It has two parts: real images/videos from real-world scenarios, and synthetic images/videos that simulate real-world conditions. The details are as follows:

Data Sources:
We first employ the following methods to construct a data pool for subsequent filtering.

Real Images/Videos.
We source a large number of real images and videos from OpenVid-1M HD
[
33
]
, a dataset that covers a diverse range of real-world scenarios. These samples are carefully pre-filtered to ensure they come from a wide variety of scenes.

Fake Images/Videos.
We source a large number of fake images and videos from multiple sources. This includes samples from MagicArena (high-rated sample). In addition, we construct the following pipeline for generation: we use Reddit’s official API to obtain social media images that cover a wide range of real-world scenarios. Then, we employ Qwen-2.5-VL
[
5
]
to generate detailed captions that describe the content of these source images. These captions then serve as prompts for various diffusion models to generate synthetic content. Finally, there are models such as FLUX
[
29
]
and GPT-4o
[
36
]
for image generation and Seedance 1.0
[
17
]
, SkyReels V1
[
43
]
for video generation in the pool.

Data Filtering:
At this stage, we introduce a rigorous filtering strategy to ensure that the final samples are of high-quality, diverse in content, and closely aligned with real-world scenarios.

Real Images/Videos.
We first filter out a large number of low-quality samples based on resolution, video frame rate, and video bitrate. Next, we eliminate duplicate content from the same origin clip. Finally, we manually check each sample one by one, removing those with extensive watermarks, anime content, or an obviously synthetic background. This process results in a final set of 1,000 real images and 1,000 fake videos.

Fake Images/Videos.
To ensure the high quality and realism of fake samples, we implement a novel two-stage filtering pipeline. First, we create a mixed pool that contains both real and fake samples from the previous data pool. Experts carefully review this pool to identify samples that appeared real. In the second stage, these identified samples are re-examined to separate those that were actually synthetic. This process results in a final set of 1,000 fake images and 1,000 fake videos.

Post-processing.
We employ the same way as BusterX to post-process the samples. For image, we standardize the resolution to 1024×1024. For video clip, we standardize the resolution to 1920×1080, the duration to 5 seconds, and the frame rate to 24 FPS. Figure
3
shows some samples. This unified post-processing approach offers several benefits:
1) Elimination of Encoding Biases
: By using HEVC encoding with x265, we eliminate potential biases from underlying different encoding preferences.
2) Consistency Across Sources
: Standardization ensures consistency across videos or images generated by different models, which may have varying original resolutions and frame rates.

4.1
Challenges of Cold-Start

Existing MLLM + RL methods
[
59
,
24
]
mostly depend on a SFT cold-start stage before RL. However, despite accelerating training, the performance improvement in the RL stage is often restricted.
We conjecture that this limitation may result from the reasoning quality of CoT data used in the cold-start phase, which could undermine the model’s reasoning ability.
Human judgments about the "fakeness" of images or videos are often based on subtle, intuitive, and multi-dimensional cues such as unnatural reflections, inconsistent lighting, slight motion artifacts, or uncanny valley effects. Given the non-linear and non-linguistic nature of cross-modal preferences in our task, it is extremely challenging to precisely elaborate a linear thinking chain for "why it is fake."
[
25
]

Thus, generating CoT data from MLLMs with prompt engineering is not only difficult to guarantee quality but also potentially degrade the model’s reasoning performance. Consequently, we abandon the cold start and achieve promising results.

4.2
Multi-Stage Training

We employ multi-stage training strategy, setting different learning objectives for each stage to stabilize the training process. As BusterX, we adopt Dynamic sAmpling Policy Optimization (DAPO)
[
65
]
as our reinforcement learning strategy. DAPO samples a group of outputs
\{o_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}
for each question
q
from the old policy
\pi_{\theta_{old}}
. The reward model is then used to score the outputs, yielding
\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}
correspondingly. Then DAPO optimizes the policy model
\pi_{\theta}
by maximizing the following objective:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\text{DAPO}}(\theta)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(q,a)\sim\mathcal{D},\{o_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\cdot\mid q)}

(1)

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{G}|o_{i}|}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\sum_{t=1}^{|o_{i}|}\min\Big(r_{i,t}(\theta)\hat{A}_{i,t},

\displaystyle\text{clip}\Big(r_{i,t}(\theta),1-{\varepsilon_{\text{low}}},1+{\varepsilon_{\text{high}}}\Big)\hat{A}_{i,t}\Big)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle 0<\Big|\{o_{i}\mid\text{is\_equivalent}(a,o_{i})\}\Big|<G,

where
\varepsilon_{\text{low}}
and
\varepsilon_{\text{high}}
are hyper-parameters, and
\hat{A}_{i,t}
is the advantage:

r_{i,t}(\theta)=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}

(2)

\hskip 10.00002pt\hat{A}_{i,t}=\frac{r_{i}-\text{mean}(\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G})}{\text{std}(\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G})}

(3)

Stage-1 Foundation RL:

In this stage, we focus on building the fundamental capabilities of the model. We use RL to train the model on basic classification tasks. The model learns to distinguish between real and AI-generated content by receiving rewards based on the accuracy. This helps the model quickly grasp the basic characteristics and features of different types of data, laying a solid foundation for subsequent training.

Stage-3 Advanced RL:

In the final stage, we further enhance the model’s response quality using advanced RL techniques. The model continues to receive rewards for accurate classifications and well-formatted responses.
We employ a mix of /think and /no_think prompts to train, thereby enhancing the model’s ability to switch in two modes.
Additionally, we introduce a Thinking Reward provided by an external model. This reward evaluates the quality of the model’s thinking process, replacing the Length Reward used in BusterX, and the CoT Length also increases naturally with this approach (Figure
5
). It encourages the model to produce more reasonable and comprehensive interpretations. However, we find that applying the Thinking Reward too early in Stage-1 can destabilize training, which underscores the importance of our multi-stage approach.

6
Conclusion and Limitations

In this paper, we present BusterX++, a novel framework for unified cross-modal AI-generated content detection and explanation. To support evaluation and application expansion, we introduce GenBuster++, a cross-modal benchmark that leverages state-of-the-art image and video generation techniques. By leveraging Multi-stage Training, Thinking Reward, and Hybrid Reasoning, BusterX++ sets new state-of-the-art results in GenBuster++ and other latest benchmarks. Despite promising progress, we recognize certain limitations and aim to address them through future optimization.

1) Generative Technology Adaptation.
While our framework demonstrates effectiveness in detecting fake samples from GenBuster-200K, we observe that samples generated with the latest technologies in GenBuster++ present a more significant challenge. This indicates that the rapid evolution of generative methods necessitates continuous adaptation and enhancement of our detection framework to maintain its efficacy.

2) Potential Bottleneck in Post-training.
Post-training phase may be approaching a performance bottleneck in our task. To further advance the capabilities of MLLM, future work should explore other training stages.

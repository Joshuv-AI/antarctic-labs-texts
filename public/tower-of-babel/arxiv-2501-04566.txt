Recursive Least Squares with Fading Regularization for Finite-Time Convergence without Persistent Excitation
Brian Lai, Dimitra Panagou, Dennis S. Bernstein

Abstract
This paper extends recursive least squares (RLS) to include time-varying regularization. This extension provides flexibility for updating the least squares regularization term in real time. Existing results with constant regularization imply that the parameter-estimation error dynamics of RLS are globally attractive to zero if and only the regressor is weakly persistently exciting. This work shows that, by extending classical RLS to include a time-varying (fading) regularization term that converges to zero, the parameter-estimation error dynamics are globally attractive to zero without weakly persistent excitation. Moreover, if the fading regularization term converges to zero in finite time, then the parameter estimation error also converges to zero in finite time. Finally, we propose rank-1 fading regularization (R1FR) RLS, a time-varying regularization algorithm with fading regularization that converges to zero, and which runs in the same computational complexity as classical RLS. Numerical examples are presented to validate theoretical guarantees and to show how R1FR-RLS can protect against over-regularization.



I
Introduction

Classical recursive least squares (RLS) regularizes the parameter estimate with a fixed weighting selected by the user
[
1
]
.
Regularization provides numerical stability during startup and reduces the variance of the estimator, at the cost of a bias in the estimate
[
2
]
, known as the bias-variance tradeoff within the context of batch least squares
[
3
, pp. 223-224]
.
However, as more data are collected, it may be desirable to update the regularization weighting based on new external knowledge. It may also be desirable to remove regularization entirely in order to remove the regularization-induced bias.
This can be accomplished using time-varying regularization.

Versions of RLS with time-varying regularization are considered in
[
4
,
5
]
.
The method in
[
5
]
uses a recursion to minimize the least squares cost with time-varying regularization.
This method uses a first-order Taylor series approximation of the covariance update, which results in an approximate solution.
Moreover, computing the resulting quadratic term of the expansion requires
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
complexity, whereas classical RLS is run in
\mathcal{O}(pn^{2})
complexity, where
n
is the number of parameters,
p
is the number of measurements per step, and typically
n\gg p
.
A higher order expansion may achieve better approximation at the expense of computational complexity.
The method developed in
[
4
]
uses time-varying regularization with a sliding window of data, that is, a fixed number of data points.
This method has the same computational complexity as classical RLS, and conditions are given for convergence of parameter estimation error to zero.
However, a major assumption the analysis of
[
4
]
is that the data are persistently exciting.

Persistent excitation is a key condition in stability analysis of parameter-estimation methods, and significant effort has been put toward weakening the PE condition
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
In particular,
[
10
]
presents
weakly persistent excitation
as a necessary and sufficient condition for global attractivity of parameter estimation error to zero in RLS .
This present paper shows, however, that weakly persistent excitation is only necessary for overcoming of effect of regularization-induced bias.

This work shows that, by extending classical RLS to include a time-varying regularization term that converges to zero, the parameter-estimation error dynamics are globally attractive to zero without weakly persistent excitation.
In particular, it is sufficient that the data collected attains full rank.
Moreover, if the regularization term is designed to converge to zero in finite time, then the parameter estimation error also converges to zero in finite time.
Finally, we show that, if the difference between regularization matrices in successive steps is of rank 1, then RLS with time-varying regularization can be implemented with the same computational complexity as classical RLS.
We present an algorithm, called
rank-1 fading-regularization RLS
(R1FR-RLS), which satisfies the aforementioned conditions.

Thus, (
11
) is globally attractive to zero.

Next, suppose there exists
k_{\rm cut}\geq k_{\rm rank}
such that, for all
k\geq k_{\rm cut}
,
R_{k}=0_{n\times n}
.
It follows from (
16
) that, for all
k\geq k_{\rm cut}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\phi_{i}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{i}\phi_{i}\succeq\sum_{i=0}^{k_{\rm rank}}\phi_{i}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{i}\phi_{i}\succ 0_{n\times n},

and hence
\sum_{i=0}^{k}\phi_{i}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{i}\phi_{i}
is nonsingular.
Therefore, it follows from (
17
) that, for all
k\geq k_{\rm cut}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\theta}_{k+1}=(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\phi_{i}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{i}\phi_{i})^{-1}0_{n\times n}(\theta_{{\rm reg},k}-\theta)=0_{n\times 1}.

Thus (
11
) is globally finite-time attractive to zero.
∎

V
Fading-Regularization (FR) RLS

This section proposes
fading-regularization RLS
(FR-RLS), a simple algorithm that satisfies the conditions of Theorem
3
sufficient for global finite-time attractivity to zero.
To begin, let
k_{\rm cut}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
, let
\mu\in(0,1)
, let
R_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be positive definite and, for all
k\geq 1
, define

\displaystyle R_{k}\triangleq\begin{cases}\mu^{k}R_{0},&k<k_{\rm cut},\\
0_{n\times n},&k\geq k_{\rm cut}.\end{cases}

(22)

Similarly to classical RLS, the user selects a positive-definite regularization matrix
R_{0}
.
In fading-regularization RLS, the user additional selects the
fading factor

\mu
which controls how quickly regularization decays, and the
regularization cutoff step

k_{\rm cut}
, after which regularization is set to zero.

It follows from (
7
) that, for all
k\geq 0
,
P_{k+1}^{-1}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{k+1}^{-1}=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}R_{k}+\phi_{k}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{k}\phi_{k},&k=0,\\
P_{k}^{-1}+\phi_{k}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{k}\phi_{k}-\mu^{k-1}(1-\mu)R_{0},&0<k<k_{\rm cut},\\
P_{k}^{-1}+\phi_{k}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{k}\phi_{k}-\mu^{k-1}R_{0},&k=k_{\rm cut},\\
P_{k}^{-1}+\phi_{k}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{k}\phi_{k},&k>k_{\rm cut}.\end{cases}

(23)

Moreover,
\theta_{1}
is still computed using (
6
) and, for all
k\geq 1
,
\theta_{k+1}
is computed using (
8
).
Note, however, that to compute
\theta_{k+1}
by (
8
), it is necessary to first compute
P_{k+1}
.
Computing
P_{k+1}
by directly inverting
P_{k+1}^{-1}
is an
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
operation, which may be computationally expensive if
n\gg p
. It may be computationally faster in practice to compute the product
P_{k+1}[\cdots]
in (
8
) using a Cholesky solver or LU solver instead of direct inversion of
P_{k+1}^{-1}
. However, this is still an
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
operation.

Directly inverting
P_{k+1}^{-1}
can be avoided for
k>k_{\rm cut}
by using the matrix inversion lemma, given by Lemma
A.2
.
It follows from Lemma
A.2
for all
k\geq k_{\rm cut}
,
P_{k+1}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle P_{k+1}=P_{k}-P_{k}\phi_{k}^{\rm T}(\Gamma_{k}^{-1}+\phi_{k}P_{k}\phi_{k}^{\rm T})^{-1}\phi_{k}P_{k}.

(24)

Note that this is the same as the classical RLS update equation for
P_{k+1}
since there is no time-varying regularization for
k>k_{\rm cut}
.
Using (
8
) and (
24
), it follows that, for all
k>k_{\rm cut}
,
\theta_{k+1}
can be computed recursively in
\mathcal{O}(pn^{2})
complexity.
However, for all
0<k\leq k_{\rm cut}
, matrix inversion lemma cannot improve computational complexity to compute
P_{k+1}
since
\rank(R_{k}-R_{k-1})=n
.
Hence, for all
0<k\leq k_{\rm cut}
,
\theta_{k+1}
must be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
complexity, which may be significantly slower than
\mathcal{O}(pn^{2})
if
n\gg p
.

VI
Rank-1 Fading-Regularization (R1FR) RLS

The
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
computational complexity of FR-RLS motivates
rank-1 fading-regularization RLS
(R1FR-RLS), an algorithm that satisfies the conditions of Theorem
3
sufficient for global finite-time attractivity to zero, but which runs in the same time complexity as classical RLS.
The key idea behind this algorithm is to design the regularization matrix such that, for all
k\geq 1
,
\rank(R_{k}-R_{k-1})\leq 1
.

To begin, let
j_{\rm cut}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
, let
\mu\in(0,1)
, and let
R_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be positive definite.
Let
d_{0,1},\ldots,d_{0,n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
v_{0,1},\ldots,v_{0,n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times 1}
respectively be eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
R_{0}
satisfying

\displaystyle R_{0}=d_{0,1}v_{0,1}v_{0,1}^{\rm T}+\ldots+d_{0,n}v_{0,n}v_{0,n}^{\rm T}.

(25)

Next, for all
j\geq 0
and for all
l=0,\ldots,n-1
, define

\displaystyle R_{jn+l}\triangleq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mu^{jn}(\mu\sum_{i=1}^{l}d_{0,i}v_{0,i}v_{0,i}^{\rm T}+\sum_{i=l+1}^{n}d_{0,i}v_{0,i}v_{0,i}^{\rm T}),&j<j_{\rm cut},\\
\mu^{jn}\sum_{i=l+1}^{n}d_{0,i}v_{0,i}v_{0,i}^{\rm T},&j=j_{\rm cut},\\
0_{n\times n},&j>j_{\rm cut}.\end{cases}

(26)

For all
k\geq 0
, since
R_{k}
is a linear combination of
\{v_{0,i}v_{0,i}^{\rm T}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, it follows that
R_{k}
is positive semidefinite.
Furthermore, note that, for all
0\leq j\leq j_{\rm cut}
,

\displaystyle R_{jn}=\mu^{jn}R_{0}.

(27)

Hence, the regularization term in rank-1 fading-regularization is the same as that of fading regularization every
n
steps.
Next, note that, for all
k\geq 1
,
R_{k}
can be computed recursively as

\displaystyle R_{k}=\begin{cases}R_{k-1}-c_{k}v_{0,l}v_{0,l}^{\rm T},&k\leq(j_{\rm cut}+1)n,\\
0_{n\times n},&k>(j_{\rm cut}+1)n,\end{cases}

(28)

where

\displaystyle c_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mu^{jn}(1-\mu^{n})d_{0,l},&0<k\leq Mn,\\
\mu^{jn}d_{0,l},&Mn<k\leq(j_{\rm cut}+1)n,\end{cases}

(29)

and where
j\triangleq\lfloor\frac{k-1}{n}\rfloor
and
l\triangleq(k-1)\,\mathrm{mod}\,n
.
It is evident from (
28
) that, for all
k\geq 1
,
R_{k}-R_{k-1}
is a scalar multiple of
v_{0,l}v_{0,l}^{\rm T}
.
Thus, for all
k\geq 1
,
\rank(R_{k}-R_{k-1})\leq 1
.
Next, it follows from (
5
), (
7
), and (
28
) that, for all
k\geq 0
,
P_{k+1}^{-1}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{k+1}^{-1}=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}R_{0}+\phi_{0}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{0}\phi_{0},&k=0,\\
P_{k}^{-1}+\phi_{k}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{k}\phi_{k}-c_{k}v_{0,l}v_{0,l}^{\rm T},&0<k\leq(j_{\rm cut}+1)n,\\
P_{k}^{-1}+\phi_{k}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{k}\phi_{k},&k>(j_{\rm cut}+1)n.\end{cases}

(30)

Furthermore, note that, for all
0<k\leq(j_{\rm cut}+1)n
,

\displaystyle\phi_{k}^{\rm T}\Gamma_{k}\phi_{k}-c_{k}v_{0,l}v_{0,l}^{\rm T}=\begin{bmatrix}\phi_{k}^{\rm T}&v_{0,l}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\Gamma_{k}&0_{p\times 1}\\
0_{1\times p}&-c_{k}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\phi_{k}\\
v_{0,l}^{\rm T}\end{bmatrix}.

(31)

Thus, it follows from Lemma
A.2
that, for all
k\geq 0
,
P_{k+1}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{k+1}=P_{k}-P_{k}\bar{\phi}_{k}^{\rm T}(\bar{\Gamma}_{k}^{-1}+\bar{\phi}_{k}P_{k}\bar{\phi}_{k}^{\rm T})^{-1}\bar{\phi}_{k}P_{k},

(32)

where
\bar{\phi}_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times n}\cup{\mathbb{R}}^{(p+1)\times n}
is defined

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}_{k}\triangleq\begin{cases}\phi_{k},&k=0,\\
\begin{bmatrix}\phi_{k}\\
v_{0,l}^{\rm T}\end{bmatrix},&0<k\leq(j_{\rm cut}+1)n,\\
\phi_{k},&k\geq(j_{\rm cut}+1)n,\end{cases}

(33)

where
\bar{\Gamma}_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times p}\cup{\mathbb{R}}^{(p+1)\times{p+1}}
is defined

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}_{k}\triangleq\begin{cases}\Gamma_{k},&k=0,\\
\begin{bmatrix}\Gamma_{k},&0_{p\times 1}\\
0_{1\times p}&-c_{k}\end{bmatrix},&0<k\leq(j_{\rm cut}+1)n,\\
\Gamma_{k},&k\geq(j_{\rm cut}+1)n,\end{cases}

(34)

and where
P_{0}\triangleq R_{0}^{-1}
.
The computational complexity to compute
P_{k+1}
using (
24
) is
\mathcal{O}((p+1)n^{2})
when
0<k\leq(j_{\rm cut}+1)n
and
\mathcal{O}(pn^{2})
otherwise.
Therefore, for all
k\geq 0
, using (
6
), (
8
), and (
32
),
\theta_{k+1}
can be computed recursively in at most
\mathcal{O}((p+1)n^{2})
complexity.

VIII
Conclusion

This work presented RLS with time-varying regularization, sufficient conditions for global finite-time attractivity of parameter estimation error to zero, and two algorithms that satisfy these sufficient conditions: fading regularization RLS and rank-1 fading regularization RLS.
Numerical examples validate these theoretical guarantees and show how fading regularization can protect against over-regularization.
While this is one potential use case, this work also opens up the potential to new time-varying regularization designs.
Another area of future interest is augmenting time-varying regularization with the existing literature of RLS forgetting algorithms.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be positive definite, let
b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and define
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
by

.
Then,
f
has a unique global minimizer given by

Lemma A.2

(Matrix Inversion Lemma)
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
,
U\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times p}
,
C\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times p}
,
V\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times n}
. Assume
A
,
C
, and
A+UCV
are nonsingular. Then,
(A+UCV)^{-1}=A^{-1}-A^{-1}U(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U)^{-1}VA^{-1}
.

An early warning model for predicting major adverse kidney events within 30 days in acute pancreatitis patients
Authors: Qiulong Wang, Juantao Lv, Dongdong Chen, Xiaojun Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRenal impairment is a common outcome of acute pancreatitis. Nevertheless, research on predictive models for major adverse kidney events within 30 days (MAKE30) in acute pancreatitis (AP) has been scarce.MethodsA retrospective study was conducted at Gansu Provincial Hospital, involving 391 patients with acute pancreatitis who were categorized into non-MAKE30 (320 cases) and MAKE30 (71 cases) groups. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to determine the independent risk factors for MAKE30 in the aforementioned patient cohort. The nomogram was developed utilizing findings from a multivariate logistic regression analysis. Subsequent evaluation of the nomogram involved the use of receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis. Additionally, subgroup analyses were performed to different AP etiology and assess secondary outcomes.ResultsGender, respiratory rate (RR), creatinine (Cr), interleukin 6 (IL-6), prothrombin time (PT), and cardiovascular disease (CVD) were identified as associated predictors of Major Adverse Kidney Events within 30 days (MAKE30) in patients with acute pancreatitis. A nomogram model was developed based on these predictors. Evaluation using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, calibration curve, and decision curve analyses (DCA) demonstrated that the nomogram model exhibited significant discrimination (AUC = 0.842) > the SOFA score (AUC = 0.809), excellent calibration, and substantial clinical utility. Subgroup analysis showed the nomogram model provided good predictive value for both secondary outcomes and various etiologies.ConclusionThis model shows promise in efficiently and accurately evaluating the risk of developing MAKE30 in acute pancreatitis patients within the first 24 h of hospitalization.Keywords: Acute pancreatitis, major adverse kidney events within 30 days, nomogram model, associated predictors

Introduction

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a common complication of acute pancreatitis (AP) with a poor prognosis, low survival rate, and a high frequency of subsequent organ failure [1,2]. Previous studies have reported an incidence of AKI in AP ranging from 7.9% to 21.6% [3,4], with fatality rates in critically ill patients reaching as high as 26% to 75%, despite regional variations in incidence [1,3]. Prolonged renal replacement therapy has the potential to facilitate the gradual improvement of acute pancreatitis complicated by acute kidney injury. However, in cases of milder disease severity, the impact on patient outcomes may be irreversible and lead to significant adverse events [5].

Moreover, there is consensus in the academic community regarding the utility of elevated serum creatinine levels (SCr) or reduced urine output (UO) for evaluating the staging and renal function of acute kidney injury (AKI). However, certain studies have indicated that the diagnostic efficacy of SCr in early AKI cases may be limited, and the intricate relationship between urine output, glomerular filtration rate (GFR), and other biomarkers with renal function remains complex. AKI is characterized not as a straightforward renal disorder, but rather as a constellation of diverse conditions including sepsis, cardiac dysfunction, and other syndromes [6–8]. In 2012, the Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) Clinical Trial Working Group of the National Institute of Diabetes, Digestive, and kidney diseases proposed the utilization of major adverse renal events within 30 days (MAKE30) as a patient-centered endpoint in clinical trials. This composite endpoint is designed to encompass both short- and long-term consequences of AKI by incorporating outcomes such as mortality, new renal replacement therapy (RRT), or persistent renal dysfunction (PRD) [9]. Moreover, MAKE30 serves as a comprehensive clinical outcome indicator that can provide a more objective assessment of renal outcomes compared to AKI with a single complication. Therefore, early identification of MAKE30 development in patients and implementation of targeted management strategies have the potential to enhance clinical outcomes.

Several clinical trials, such as SALT, SMART, and pediatric sepsis, have utilized MAKE30 as a test endpoint [10–12]. Patients with AKI frequently experience hyperchloremia, and there may be a dose-dependent correlation between the severity of AKI and serum Cl-content [13]. Recent research indicates that treating SAP patients with balanced multielectrolyte solutions (BMES) can raise their serum chloride levels, which will be beneficial [14]. Moreover, prior research suggests that APACHE II score, SOFA scores and hyperchloremia may serve as distinct risk factors for predicting MAKE30 in acute pancreatitis patients [15,16]. However, a reliable predictive algorithm for determining MAKE30 in this patient population is currently lacking. The construction of a nomogram model holds potential clinical significance for predicting MAKE30, contingent upon the availability of baseline data and biochemical indicators within 24 h of admission. This study sought to establish an early prediction model for identifying high-risk populations and examining potential risk factors associated with MAKE30. Additionally, the model serves to explore the feasibility of utilizing MAKE30 as an endpoint in future studies on acute pancreatitis.

Materials and methods

Study design

A retrospective cohort analysis involving 391 patients with acute pancreatitis was conducted at a single center between April 2019 and April 2022. Anonymized clinical data were retrieved from the Lian Zhong digital medical record system at Gansu Provincial Hospital in Lanzhou, China. The study protocol were approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Gansu Provincial Hospital, and the ethics approval number is 2024-029.

Patients

Patients with a preliminary diagnosis of acute pancreatitis within 48 h of onset were evaluated for eligibility to participate in clinical trials. The new Atlanta categorization criteria were utilized for the diagnosis of acute pancreatitis, requiring a combination of transabdominal ultrasonography, MRI, or CECT confirmation, serum lipase or amylase activity at least three times higher than the upper limit of normal, and the presence of abdominal pain associated with acute pancreatitis [17].

The exclusion criteria include: (1) individuals hospitalized for less than 24 h; (2) individuals aged below eighteen or above seventy-five; (3) pregnant patients with AP positivity; (4) individuals with chronic renal disease including CKD with serum creatinine clearance rate < 40 mL/min; (5) individuals with kidney cancer or who have undergone kidney transplantation; (6) individuals receiving CRRT prior to admission; (7) individuals with hematological disorders; (8) individuals with severe organ dysfunction; (9) individuals with unknown conditions, such as Lack of laboratory test findings, crucial sickness not tested in time, or missing information.

The subjects were categorized into two groups, MAKE30 and non-MAKE30.Any one of the following criteria can be used to diagnose MAKE30 composite endpoints: (1) death, (2) initial RRT treatment, (3) PRD (defined as a final inpatient serum creatinine result at least 200% higher than baseline), all data were evaluated either 30 days following enrollment or at the time of discharge. Furthermore, baseline serum creatinine was determined as follows: (1) If available, the lowest value was measured between 12 months and 24 h prior to hospitalization; (2) When measured values were unavailable, an estimate was made using the formula previously described [creatinine(mmol/l) = 88.4 × (0.74 − 0.2 + 0.003 × age) in females, and creatinine (mmol/l) = 88.4 × (0.74 + 0.003 × age) in males] [18,19]. A composite endpoint for diagnosing MAKE30 can be formed by combining any of the aforementioned criteria. These three elements were observed 30 days after their introduction or upon hospital discharge, whichever occurred first.

Data collection

Data were collected from the Gansu Provincial Hospital, including demographic information and clinical parameters obtained within 24 h of patient admission. Basic information encompassed age, gender, heart rate (HR), respiratory rate (RR), temperature, Systolic blood pressure (SBP), Diastolic blood pressure (DBP), Mean arterial pressure (MAP), Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA), Acute kidney Injury (AKI), Sepsis, etiology, disease severity, and comorbidities. Additionally, laboratory tests conducted within the same timeframe comprised White Blood Cells (WBC), Neutrophil Percentage (NEUT%), Lymphocyte, Monocyte, Platelet(PLT), Procalcitonin (PCT), Serum creatinine (SCr), Total Bilirubin (TBiL), Albumin (ALB), Globulin (GLB), Total Cholesterol (TC), Glucose, Uric Acid (UA), Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN), Interleukin6 (IL-6), Procalcitonin (PCT), Thromboplastin Time (APTT), Prothrombin Time (PT), Thrombin Time (TT), Prothrombin Time Activity (PTA), Fibrinogen (FIB), and International Normalized Ratio (INR).

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables with a normal distribution were summarized using the mean ± standard deviation, whereas non-normally distributed variables were summarized using the median (interquartile range). Categorical variables were presented as percentages. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression models were employed to ascertain the independent factors influencing MAKE30. In order to enhance the interpretability of the prediction model’s outcomes, logistic regression analysis results were utilized to construct a corresponding nomogram, which was subsequently developed through the utilization of the ‘DynNom’ software application. The efficacy of the MAKE30 nomogram was assessed through the examination of the receiver operating characteristics (ROC) curve, while the accuracy of the prediction model was further evaluated by contrasting the MAKE30 independent predictor with the SOFA model. A calibration curve was employed to evaluate the concordance between the observed and predicted probabilities. The clinical utility of the prediction model was assessed through Decision Curve Analysis (DCA), and the predictive performance of the nomogram for 30-day mortality, PRD, and RRT in subgroup analyses was evaluated using the ROC curve.

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 27.0 and R 4.3.1, with significance set at p < 0.05.

Results

Basic characteristics

A total of 391 individuals diagnosed with acute pancreatitis were included in the study, of which 71 (18.2%) experienced an MAKE30 event during their hospitalization (Figure 1). The Venn diagram (Figure 2A) illustrates the distribution of MAKE30 components, with 38 (9.7%) representing mortality, 43 (11.1%) requiring renal replacement therapy (RRT), and 32 (8.2%) meeting the criteria for persistent renal dysfunction (PRD). The study population consisted of 64.2% male participants, with a median age of 49 years (range 36 to 63). There was a minimal age difference observed between MAKE30 and non-MAKE30 patients, as indicated in Table 1. However, significant disparities were noted in SOFA ratings (p < 0.001) between the two groups, with MAKE30 patients exhibiting higher scores (6.5–8) compared to non-MAKE30 patients (3.3–5). Additionally, significant variations were observed in respiratory rate (RR) (21.20–27 vs. 20.18–21; p < 0.001), heart rate (HR), gender distribution, and illness severity between the MAKE30 and non-MAKE30 cohorts. Conversely, differences in age, temperature, mean arterial pressure (MAP), and etiology were not found to be statistically significant (p < 0.05).

Associated predictors of MAKE30 and nomogram development

The Univariate analysis revealed significant variables in Tables 1 and 2, including gender, heart rate (HR), respiratory rate (RR), hyperlipidemia (HTN), cardiovascular disease (CVD), creatinine (Cr), glucose, uric acid (UA), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), interleukin-6 (IL-6), procalcitonin (PCT), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), prothrombin time (PT), international normalized ratio (INR), platelet count (PLT), albumin, and prothrombin time activity. The variables with statistical significance (p < 0.05) in the above Univariate analysis results were included in further Multivariate logistic regression analyses. Further investigation identified predictors associated with acute pancreatitis patients, including gender (OR 2.181, 95% CI 1.035–4.598), RR (OR 1.073, 95% CI 1.010–1.140), serum creatinine (OR 1.012, 95% CI 1.007–1.016), IL-6 (OR 1.001, 95% CI 1.000–1.001), PT (OR 1.159, 95% CI 1.054–1.274), and CVD (OR 3.225, 95% CI 1.481–7.020) (Table 3). Utilizing the identified variables, a nomogram model was constructed for the prediction of MAKE30 in individuals afflicted with acute pancreatitis. Subsequently, an online version of this nomogram was generated through the utilization of the ‘DynNom’ software application to facilitate its practical application during the inter imperiod.

Verification of the prediction model

The nomogram model of MAKE30 in patients with acute pancreatitis demonstrated diagnostic value as evidenced by the ROC curve analysis. The predictive power of the model for MAKE30 (AUC = 0.842) was found to be superior to that of the SOFA score (AUC = 0.809), as illustrated in Figure 3A and B. Furthermore, a satisfactory calibration was indicated by the predicted probabilities derived from the calibration curve matching the observation data (Figure 4A). Additionally, within a broad and useful range of threshold probabilities, the nomogram was found to have a greater overall net benefit in DCA, suggesting a significant potential for clinical application (Figure 4B). Thus, it can be deduced that the nomogram has potential as a reliable predictive model of MAKE30 in patients with acute pancreatitis.

Discussion

A simplistic nomogram model was developed to forecast MAKE30 in individuals diagnosed with acute pancreatitis, incorporating variables such as gender, RR, SCr, IL-6, PT, and CVD within the initial 24 h of hospital admission. The combined outcome of MAKE30 (18.2%) observed among the 391 acute pancreatitis patients in the study was similar to the prevalence reported in previous studies (16%–23%). In the development of a nomogram model, a broader array of laboratory markers, including standard blood tests, metabolic testing, and coagulation tests, were collected within 24 h of admission, surpassing the scope of previous research efforts. Because the SOFA score, APACHE II score, and other traditional score good predictors of AP and can be used to assess the severity of AP patients, using them as an independent risk factor to develop a predictive model is not persuasive [15,16,20]. We build a new nomogram model with more data, more dependable laboratory metrics, and more baseline data in comparison to the previous nomogram model with MAKE30 (AUC = 0.846) for patients with AP. Our work will be more useful for clinical decision making because it differs from standard prediction methods. Nomogram model’s prediction performance (AUC = 0.842) is compared to SOFA score (AUC = 0.809), indicating that the model performs well and has real-world application. Additionally, it is essential for healthcare professionals to accurately predict MAKE30 and promptly identify individuals at heightened risk for nephrological complications within the initial 24 h of hospital admission. The proactive implementation of therapies aimed at enhancing patient outcomes and reducing mortality rates in cases of acute pancreatitis can be effectively supported by this approach.

A substantial body of literature suggests that sex differences play a significant role in the progression of acute kidney injury to chronic renal impairment [21]. Traditional beliefs indicate that males are more susceptible to acquiring acute kidney injury compared to females [22]. However, recent studies have shown that female patients with severe acute kidney injury and sepsis requiring renal replacement therapy have significantly higher survival rates [23]. Additionally, research indicates that estrogen may have a protective effect on the kidneys. The histone deacetylase SIRT1 has been identified as a mediator of estrogen’s preventative effects in acute kidney injury by inhibiting kidney cell death and inflammation [21,23]. Endoplasmic reticulum stress, lipid peroxidation, and renal cell death have been identified as factors that may disproportionately affect male kidneys compared to female kidneys [24].

Additionally, acute respiratory failure (ARF) is recognized as a significant risk factor for in-hospital mortality in AP patients, with the combination of ARF and AKI further increasing the likelihood of death [25,26]. A cohort study focusing on acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) revealed a correlation between low arterial oxygen partial pressure (PaO2) levels and an elevated risk of mortality due to severe renal complications, aligning with the present study’s findings [27] Administering adequate oxygen therapy to patients with acute pancreatitis can greatly reduce the likelihood of adverse renal outcomes. Respiratory rate, in addition to arterial PaO2, can serve as a useful indicator of hypoxia in patients and is a significant predictor of the occurrence of MAKE30.

The SCr level has traditionally been regarded as the primary marker for renal insufficiency, with AKI diagnosed when there is a decrease in glomerular filtration rate (GFR) of more than 25% within a seven-day period or when SCr levels exceed 1.5 times the baseline value. Despite numerous research efforts to identify and validate new biomarkers for AKI in recent years, SCr continues to play a crucial role in the clinical classification of AKI [28]. Acute kidney injury, characterized by reduced glomerular filtration function and elevated serum creatinine due to impaired filtration and reabsorption through damaged proximal renal tubule cells, is widely recognized as a primary factor contributing to elevated serum creatinine levels. The elevation of serum creatinine is strongly associated with acute kidney injury, the need for renal replacement therapy, and increased mortality rates in critically ill adult patients [29]. Additionally, SCr levels can serve as a valuable tool in forecasting the prognosis and likelihood of kidney injury in individuals with severe burns, critically ill neonates, and cirrhosis [30–32]. Therefore, prompt assessment of SCr fluctuations upon hospital admission is imperative for early detection of renal impairment and prevention of further deterioration.

Inflammatory biomarkers such as procalcitonin (PCT) and C-reactive protein (CRP) are strong predictors of the prognosis of persons with AP-related kidney injury and can be utilized to treat AP development early on [33,34]. The pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-6 has been associated with the severity of organ failure and exhibits rapid elevation in plasma following organ injury. Studies have shown that kidney damage is implicated in various disorders, with IL-6 playing a role in promoting renal fibrosis post-AKI, making it a significant biomarker for chronic kidney disease [35]. Our research indicates that IL-6 serves as an independent predictor of MAKE30. Furthermore, emerging evidence suggests a robust association between IL-6 levels and short-term renal function as well as long-term recovery in AKI patients [36]. Additionally, the involvement of IL-6 in kidney injury may be linked to its influence on the TLR4 receptor in leukocytes or activation of the JAK-STAT3 pathway [37,38]. Moreover, PT is defined as the duration required for prothrombin to convert to thrombin and induce plasma clotting when combined with calcium ions in platelet-deficient plasma. The onset of acute renal responses may stem from tubular cell damage and microcirculation disruption in individuals experiencing deteriorating renal function as a result of heightened thrombin production [39]. Previous studies have established that PT is a significant risk factor for AKI in critically ill patients with sepsis [40]. A novel approach known as thromboinflammatory prognostic scoring (TIPS) has recently been developed, with its components PT and IL-6 showing promise in predicting adverse outcomes in AP patients over a 28-day period [41]. Additionally, survival in AP patients with AKI is impacted by thrombotic indicators such as platelet count (PLT) and D-dimer [2,42]. A prospective study is warranted to determine the potential for expanding a scoring system based on thrombotic and inflammatory biomarkers to assess the severity and prognosis of AP.

Research indicates that individuals with acute and chronic kidney disease are most likely to succumb to cardiovascular disease (CVD), leading to the development of cardio-renal syndrome [43]. Recent studies suggest that renal complications can elevate the likelihood of congestive heart failure by 58%, acute myocardial infarction by 40%, and stroke by 15% [44]. Our study provides evidence that cardiovascular disease (CVD) acts as a valuable tool in managing high-risk individuals by serving as an independent predictor of significant renal complications within 30 days in patients with acute pancreatitis. Furthermore, hypertriglyceridemia (HTG) poses additional risks for atherosclerosis and pancreatitis [45] Further investigation is needed to assess the potential benefits of early screening for hyperlipidemia in acute pancreatitis patients to prevent MAKE30.

In this research study, data obtained within the first 24 h of admission (including Gender, RR, SCr, IL-6, PT, CVD) was utilized to construct a rudimentary column chart model for the prediction of MAKE30 in individuals diagnosed with acute pancreatitis. The efficacy of the nomogram was evaluated in relation to its clinical utility, discriminative ability, and calibration. Furthermore, the nomogram model offers valuable insights to aid in the selection of appropriate therapeutic interventions for patients at elevated risk of experiencing MAKE30.The following example is presented to illustrate the application of the histogram model: Consider a male patient with acute pancreatitis and comorbid cardiovascular disease, presenting with a prothrombin time (PT) of 15 s, a respiratory rate (RR) of 30 breaths per minute, a serum creatinine (SCr) level of 235 μmol/L, and an interleukin-6 (IL-6) concentration of 190 pg/mL. Utilize the data provided in Figure 2B to determine the score for each parameter along the ‘point’ axis. Subsequently, sum the scores assigned to each parameter to calculate the total score, which amounts to 45 (5 for gender, 10 for RR, 16 for SCr, 2 for IL-6, 5 for PT, and 7 for cardiovascular disease). Based on the obtained score, there is an 85% probability of MAKE30 occurring. Alternatively, utilizing the online version can yield similar results with ease (see Figure 2C). Furthermore, our findings indicate that the nomogram significantly predicts 30-day mortality (AUC = 0.872), new renal replacement therapy (AUC = 0.847), and persistent renal dysfunction (AUC = 0.855) in individuals diagnosed with acute pancreatitis (refer to Figure 5). In addition, nomogram models demonstrated good sensitivity in patients with acute pancreatitis of various etiologies: biliary AP (AUC = 0.892), hyperlipidemic AP (AUC = 0.766), and alcoholic AP (AUC = 0.938) had considerable predictive power (refer to Figure 6).

Limitations

This study is subject to several limitations. Firstly, there is a possibility of selection bias impacting the results.Secondly, as a three-year retrospective analysis, significant advancements in management decision-making may have influenced the development of acute pancreatitis. Finally, because the model was built from a training group with a small number of test samples, our findings are best interpreted as findings of association, and additional multicenter prospective trials, as well as more relevant metrics, are required to assess their potential and confirm the results with a large cohort size.

Conclusion

In summary, our research suggests that gender, RR, SCr, IL-6, PT, and CVD levels assessed within the first 24 h of admission serve as significant prognostic indicators for the occurrence of MAKE30 in AP patients. Additionally, the nomogram model utilizing these variables has demonstrated strong performance in predicting MAKE30, exhibiting appropriate calibration and practical utility for clinicians in guiding timely and personalized treatment strategies.

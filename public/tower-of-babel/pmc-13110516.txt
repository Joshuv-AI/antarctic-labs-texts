Adjuvant short-course radiotherapy combined with chemotherapy and a PD-1 inhibitor for resected mucosal melanoma: study protocol for a single-arm, prospective, phase II trial

Introduction

Mucosal melanoma (MM) is a biologically distinct subtype of melanoma that arises from mucosal sites (eg, sinonasal and gastrointestinal tracts) and carries a substantially worse prognosis than cutaneous disease. In China, overall melanoma incidence has been reported to increase by approximately 3%–5% per year.1 MM accounts for <2% of melanomas in Western populations but for 16.0%–33.3% in Asian populations, with a large Chinese series showing that MM comprised 21.6% (113/522) of all melanomas.2 In a nationwide cohort of 706 patients with MM, the median overall survival (OS) was 3.58 years and the 5-year OS was 26.8%.3 In multivariable analyses, depth of invasion, regional lymph-node involvement and distant metastasis were the principal determinants of prognosis; after adjustment for these factors, survival was broadly similar across mucosal primary sites.3 Despite surgery being the cornerstone for localised disease, postoperative relapse remains frequent and optimal adjuvant strategies are not yet established.

Prospective randomised data from China indicate that adjuvant chemotherapy can improve outcomes in resected MM. In a randomised phase II trial comparing high-dose interferon-α2b (HDI) versus temozolomide plus cisplatin (TMZ/DDP), median relapse-free survival (RFS) was 5.4 months in the observation arm, 9.4 months with HDI and 20.8 months with TMZ/DDP; median OS was 21.2, 40.4 and 48.7 months, respectively, with significantly better RFS (p<0.001) and OS (p<0.01) for TMZ/DDP than for either HDI or observation.4 In advanced melanoma, a multicentre retrospective analysis reported that pembrolizumab plus TMZ achieved an objective response rate of 40.0% (8/20) and a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 9.8 months (95% CI 1.7 to 17.9), superior to TMZ-based chemotherapy (median PFS 4.2 months; HR 0.415, 95% CI 0.185 to 0.931; p=0.033) and numerically longer than pembrolizumab monotherapy (median PFS 6.2 months; HR 0.647, 95% CI 0.334 to 1.252; p=0.196).5 These data support chemotherapy as a rational backbone to partner with programmed death 1 (PD-1) inhibition. In the adjuvant setting for MM, a phase II trial comparing chemotherapy plus a PD-1 inhibitor versus chemotherapy alone is ongoing (NCT04462965).

Beyond direct cytotoxicity, radiotherapy (RT) can potentiate antitumour immunity through multiple mechanisms—including induction of immunogenic cell death with the release of damage-associated molecular patterns, enhanced major histocompatibility complex class I expression and activation of the cGAS–STING pathway—providing a biologic rationale to integrate RT with PD-1 blockade in high-risk MM.69 In head-and-neck MM, retrospective analysis indicated that adjuvant radiation therapy increased local control,10 and adding immunotherapy to RT might improve OS compared with RT alone.11 12

Based on the radiobiological rationale for RT, the potential synergy between RT and PD-1 blockade, and the established feasibility of short-course RT in other solid tumours, we designed this single-arm, prospective, phase II study to evaluate adjuvant chemo-radio-immunotherapy in patients with completely resected (R0/R1) MM. Patients will receive two cycles of TMZ-DDP and PD-1 inhibitor therapy, followed by sequential RT, and then four additional cycles of the same systemic regimen.

Melanoma has traditionally been considered relatively sensitive to larger fraction sizes, and hypofractionated RT may therefore be more effective than prolonged conventional fractionation in this disease.12 Increasing the dose per fraction with hypofractionated schedules may therefore improve the effectiveness of RT.12 Moreover, short-course hypofractionated RT (SCRT) confers immunological advantages compared with conventional fractionated radiotherapy. Preclinical and translational data indicate that short-course regimens can mitigate treatment-related lymphopenia, suppress recruitment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells with concomitant decreases in PD-L1 expression and even elicit abscopal effects when paired with immune checkpoint inhibitors.1315 In rectal cancer, the 25 Gy in five fractions SCRT regimen has been widely used and has demonstrated practical feasibility within multimodality treatment strategies, especially when combined with immunotherapy.16 17 In addition, a phase II trial (NCT06455124) evaluating SCRT combined with chemotherapy and immunotherapy after local excision for high-risk early rectal cancer is recruiting.18 In the adjuvant setting for head and neck MM, a clinical trial of hypofractionated RT (6 Gy×5 fractions) combined with pembrolizumab is ongoing (NCT04318717). Considering the aforementioned factors, we selected a SCRT schedule of 25 Gy in five fractions in the present study. In addition, RT was scheduled after the initial two cycles of systemic therapy to allow adequate postoperative recovery, provide early treatment for possible micrometastatic disease and facilitate protocol delivery without substantially delaying systemic therapy.

Methods and analysis

Patient and public involvement

Neither patients nor the public have been involved in the design, recruitment and conduct of this study. The results will be disseminated via scientific journals and academic conferences.

Study design

This is an investigator-initiated, single-arm, prospective, phase II trial evaluating adjuvant SCRT combined with TMZ/DDP and the PD-1 inhibitor pucotenlimab in patients with completely resected (R0/R1) MM. Treatment comprises two 3-week cycles of pucotenlimab plus TMZ/DDP with sequential SCRT (25 Gy in five fractions to the tumour bed and regional lymphatic drainage), followed by four additional cycles of the same systemic regimen. The primary endpoint is 1-year recurrence-free survival (RFS); secondary endpoints include locoregional recurrence-free survival (LRFS), distant metastasis-free survival (DMFS), OS and safety. Trial registration

This trial is registered at https://www.chictr.org.cn (Identifier: ChiCTR2400093001; registered on 27 November 2024).

Protocol version and trial status

Protocol version V.1.0, dated 27 June 2024. Recruitment at Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Centre: recruiting. The study period is from 1 December 2024 to 1 December 2029, and participant recruitment is planned from 1 December 2024 to 1 December 2027.

Randomisation and blinding

This is a single-arm, open-label study; no randomisation or blinding is performed.

Trial organisation and oversight

This investigator-initiated trial is led by the Department of Musculoskeletal Surgery and the Department of Radiation Oncology at Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Centre (FUSCC). The study is conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice. Ethics approval and written informed consent are required at enrolment. Pucotenlimab is supplied at no cost for prespecified cycles; insurance is arranged for participants per institutional policy.

Study population

Eligibility was defined according to prespecified criteria related to tumour status, patient characteristics, prior treatment and laboratory parameters in the registered protocol. Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in online supplemental table 1. Reasons for withdrawal include new distant metastasis confirmed after surgery, receipt of non-protocol anticancer therapy or the participant’s decision to withdraw at any time.

Treatments

Overall regimen and schedule

All eligible patients receive six 3-week cycles of systemic therapy. Patients receive two cycles of pucotenlimab plus TMZ and DDP, followed by SCRT, and then four additional cycles of the same systemic regimen. Systemic therapy consists of pucotenlimab 200 mg IV on day 1, TMZ 200 mg/m² orally on days 1–5 and DDP 25 mg/m² IV on days 1–3 of each 3-week cycle.

Radiotherapy

Patients receive SCRT 25 Gy in five fractions to the tumour bed and regional lymphatic drainage using IMRT/VMAT. Target volumes follow ICRU-50 (GTV/CTV/PTV), and treatment is delivered on five consecutive weekdays with daily image guidance.

Chemotherapy

Each 3-week cycle includes TMZ 200 mg/m² orally on days 1–5 and DDP 25 mg/m² intravenously on days 1–3 with hydration and antiemetic prophylaxis according to institutional standards. Laboratory tests are reviewed prior to each cycle; treatment day sequencing follows local practice, and supportive care (eg, growth-factor use, analgesia, antibiotics and VTE prophylaxis) is permitted as clinically indicated.

Immunotherapy

Pucotenlimab 200 mg is administered intravenously on day 1 of each cycle. No dose-reduction schema is permitted; management is by temporary interruption or permanent discontinuation according to the protocol’s immune-related adverse-event (irAE) algorithms. Re-initiation requires resolution of the irAE to grade ≤1 (or baseline) and tapering of systemic corticosteroids to ≤10 mg prednisolone-equivalent/day, with recovery to ECOG 0–1.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions

Systemic therapy may be delayed for unresolved grade ≥2 toxicities and dose-reduced for chemotherapy-related AEs; DDP may be adjusted for renal impairment. Pucotenlimab is withheld for protocol-defined irAEs and permanently discontinued for severe or recurrent events. Any component stops for disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, pregnancy, consent withdrawal or investigator decision; an end-of-treatment assessment is then performed, followed by protocolled follow-up.

Study endpoints

The primary endpoint is 1-year RFS, measured from first study treatment (Cycle 1 day 1) to the earliest of locoregional or distant recurrence, or death from any cause; patients without an event are censored at the last disease assessment. Secondary endpoints include LRFS, DMFS and OS, all measured from Cycle 1 day 1; death is counted as an event for LRFS/DMFS. Safety endpoints comprise the incidence and severity of AEs/serious AEs (SAEs) and irAEs.

Assessments and follow-up

Tumour assessments follow RECIST V.1.1 (iRECIST recorded when applicable); safety is graded by CTCAE V.5.0. Imaging and clinical evaluations are performed every 12 weeks during treatment and follow-up in year 1 (thereafter per protocol). Laboratory tests and AE monitoring occur each cycle and at follow-up visits.

Laboratory testing and biospecimen collection

Exploratory correlative studies are planned to evaluate treatment-related biomarker and immune changes during therapy and to explore their potential associations with clinical outcomes. Routine safety laboratories are performed at baseline and before each cycle and graded according to CTCAE V.5.0 (full blood count, electrolytes, renal and liver function, fasting glucose and thyroid function); a serum pregnancy test (women of childbearing potential) and screening for hepatitis B/C and HIV are obtained before cycle 1 day 1 (C1D1) according to institutional policy. For exploratory analyses (optional with separate consent), up to 30 mL of peripheral blood is collected at baseline (≤7 days before C1D1), before C3D1 (after SCRT), at the end of treatment (after cycle 6) and at first relapse or the 12-month visit. Plasma/serum and peripheral blood mononuclear cells are processed within 2 hours, aliquoted and stored at −80°C or in vapour-phase liquid nitrogen under coded identifiers at the FUSCC central laboratory. Participants may decline optional sampling without affecting trial participation.

Sample size calculation and statistical analysis

The null 1-year RFS rate of 55% was selected as a slightly conservative benchmark based on previously published adjuvant data in resected MM. In a previous randomised multicentre phase III study, the 1-year RFS was 57.3% in patients receiving adjuvant TMZ plus DDP.19 Because the present study includes higher-risk patients, including those with R1 resection, 55% was considered a reasonable null assumption for sample size estimation. Assuming that the investigational regimen would improve the 1-year RFS from 55% to 70%, 44 evaluable patients were required with a one-sided α of 0.10 and 80% power. To allow for approximately 5% attrition, the target sample size was set at 47 patients. The primary analysis will estimate 12-month RFS from the date of first study treatment using the Kaplan–Meier method with 95% CIs and will compare the observed 12-month RFS with the prespecified null value.

No formal prespecified subgroup analysis or stratified sample size design by primary anatomical site was included in this study. Although anatomical site may represent an important source of clinical heterogeneity in MM, the present trial is a single-arm phase II study with a relatively small overall sample size and the number of patients ultimately enrolled from each anatomical site is difficult to predict. Therefore, the study is not powered for site-specific comparisons. This should be considered a limitation of the current trial.

Missing data will be handled according to endpoint type. For time-to-event outcomes, including RFS, LRFS, DMFS and OS, patients without a documented event at the time of analysis will be censored at the date of their last disease assessment. Patients who withdraw early or are lost to follow-up without a documented event will be censored at the date of their last adequate disease assessment; if no post-baseline disease assessment is available, they will be censored at the date of first study treatment. No formal imputation is planned for the primary time-to-event analysis. Safety analyses will be based on all available data from participants who receive at least one dose of systemic treatment or one fraction of RT. Reasons for treatment discontinuation, withdrawal and loss to follow-up will be recorded and summarised descriptively.

Data collection, management, monitoring and safety oversight

Study data will be recorded in pseudonymised electronic case-report forms and stored on secure institutional servers with access control and audit trails. Source data verification and periodic monitoring will be performed in accordance with the study protocol and applicable institutional and regulatory requirements. Major protocol deviations, AEs, SAEs and data quality metrics will be reviewed at predefined intervals. An independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC), independent of the investigators and study team, has been established for this study to oversee patient safety and overall study conduct. The DMC will periodically review accumulating safety data during the trial and may make recommendations regarding the continuation, modification, temporary suspension or termination of the study if necessary. SAEs will be reported promptly to the institutional ethics committee in accordance with local requirements.

Harms and safety reporting

AEs/SAEs will be collected from informed consent through the end of protocol follow-up; SAEs will be reported within 24 hours per local regulations; toxicities are graded by CTCAE V.5.0. Post-trial SAEs known to investigators will be reported to the sponsor until regulatory review is complete.

Confidentiality and data access

Participant data will be coded with unique study identifiers and handled in accordance with institutional policies and applicable data-protection regulations. Identifiable information will remain confidential and will not be disclosed; no individual-level data will be publicly released.

Dissemination policy

A clinical study report will be prepared by the investigator-initiator; peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations are planned.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Centre. Any protocol amendments will be submitted to the Ethics Committee before implementation and updated in the trial registry. Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations.

Data availability statement

No datasets have been generated or analysed at the time of publication of this study protocol. De-identified participant-level data and relevant supporting documents will be considered for sharing after study completion, subject to ethics approval, institutional policy and applicable regulations. The data sharing plan is available in the trial registry record.

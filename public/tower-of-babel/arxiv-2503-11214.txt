Reformulation of q q -middle convolution and applications
Yumi Arai, Kouichi Takemura

Abstract
. We reformulate the q q -convolution and the q q -middle convolution introduced by Sakai and Yamaguchi, and we introduce q q -deformations of the addition which is related to the gauge-transformation. A merit of the reformulation is the additivity on composition of two q q -middle convolutions. We obtain sufficient conditions that the Jackson integrals associated with the q q -convolution converge and satisfy the q q -difference equation associated with the q q -convolution. We present several third-order linear q q -difference equations and solutions of them by using the q q -middle convolution and the q q -deformations of the addition.

1.
Introduction

Differential equations and difference equations play important roles in investigating special functions, and the middle convolution has been applied for the study of special functions and differential equations.
Katz introduced the middle convolution in his book
[
10
]
to study rigid local systems.
Dettweiler and Reiter
[
3
,
4
]
reformulated the middle convolution for the Fuchsian equations in terms of the linear algebra.
The Fuchsian equations is the system of linear differential equations written as

(1.1)

\frac{dY}{dx}=\left(\frac{A_{0}}{x-t_{0}}+\frac{A_{1}}{x-t_{1}}+\dots+\frac{A_{N}}{x-t_{N}}\right)Y,

where
Y
is a column vector with
m
entries and
A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{N}
are constant square matrices of size
m
.
We review briefly the definition of the convolution and the middle convolution for equation (
1.1
) (or the tuple of the matrices
(A_{0},\dots,A_{N})
).
Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
F_{i}

(i=0,\dots,N)
be the square matrix of size
(N+1)m
of the form

(1.2)

F_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}c@{\,}ccc@{\,}}A_{0}+\lambda I_{m}&A_{1}&\cdots&A_{N}\\
0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0\end{array}\right),\;F_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}c@{\,}c@{\,}cc@{\,}}0&0&\cdots&0\\
A_{0}&A_{1}+\lambda I_{m}&\cdots&A_{N}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0\end{array}\right),\dots,

where
I_{m}
is the identity matrix of size
m
.
Then, the correspondence of the tuple of matrices
(A_{0},\dots,A_{N})\mapsto(F_{0},\dots,F_{N})
(or the correspondence of the associated Fuchsian system) is called the convolution.
It is shown that the following subspaces
\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}
of
\mathbb{C}^{(N+1)m}
are preserved by the action of
F_{i}

(i=0,\dots,r)
.

(1.3)

\mathcal{K}=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}c@{\,}}\mathrm{ker}A_{0}\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{ker}A_{N}\end{array}\right),\quad\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{ker}(F_{0}+F_{1}+\cdots+F_{N}).

We denote the linear transformation induced from the action of
F_{i}
on the quotient space
\mathbb{C}^{(N+1)m}/(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})
by
\overline{F}_{i}
.
The correspondence of the tuple of matrices
(A_{0},\dots,A_{N})\mapsto(\overline{F}_{0},\dots,\overline{F}_{N})
(or the correspondence of the associated Fuchsian system) is called the middle convolution, and we denote it by
mc_{\lambda}
.
See also Haraoka
[
7
, §7.5]
for a detailed description.

It is known that the middle convolution has additivity.
Namely,
mc_{0}
is an identity map and we have

(1.4)

mc_{\lambda}\circ mc_{\mu}=mc_{\lambda+\mu},

under some conditions (see the conditions
(*)
and
(**)
in Definition
5.1
).

A theory of the
q
-deformation of the middle convolution was constructed by Yamaguchi and Sakai.
A tentative theory was presented in master’s thesis of Yamaguchi
[
17
]
which was supervised by Sakai, and a full paper was published later by their joint paper
[
12
]
.
They handled the system of
q
-difference equations described as

(1.5)

Y(qx)=\left(B_{\infty}+\frac{B_{1}}{1-x/b_{1}}+\dots+\frac{B_{N}}{1-x/b_{N}}\right)Y(x),

where
Y(x)
is a column vector with
m
entries and
B_{\infty},B_{1},\dots,B_{N}
are square matrices of size
(N+1)m
.
The construction of the
q
-middle convolution is similar to the case of the Fuchsian system of differential equations.
They investigated several properties of the
q
-middle convolution and related topics.
On the composition of the middle
q
-middle convolution, they obtained

(1.6)

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}_{\mu}\circ\overline{\Psi}_{\lambda}\simeq\overline{\Psi}_{\log(q^{\lambda}+q^{\mu}-1)/\log q}.

See the appendix for the notation.

The theory by Sakai and Yamaguchi was applied to studies of some
q
-difference equations in
[
13
,
2
]
.
Recall that the convolution in equation (
1.2
) is related with Euler’s integral transformation.
Let
Y(x)
be a solution of equation (
1.1
).
Set

(1.7)

W_{j}(x)=\frac{Y(x)}{x-t_{j}},\quad W(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}c@{\,}}W_{0}(x)\\
\vdots\\
W_{N}(x)\end{array}\right).

We apply Euler’s integral transformation for each entry of
W(x)
, i.e. we set

(1.8)

\widetilde{Y}(x)=\int_{\Delta}W(s)(x-s)^{\lambda}ds,

where
\Delta
is an appropriate cycle in
\mathbb{C}
with the variable
s
.
Dettweiler and Reiter
[
3
]
established that the function
\widetilde{Y}(x)
satisfies the following Fuchsian system of differential equation

(1.9)

\frac{d\widetilde{Y}}{dx}=\left(\frac{F_{0}}{x-t_{0}}+\frac{F_{1}}{x-t_{1}}+\dots+\frac{F_{N}}{x-t_{N}}\right)\widetilde{Y},

where
F_{0},\dots,F_{N}
were defined in equation (
1.2
).
Sakai and Yamaguchi
[
12
]
introduced the
q
-deformation of the convolution by considering a
q
-deformation of the integral transformation of Dettweiler and Reiter.
See the appendix for a review of them.
In
[
13
]
, the theory of Sakai and Yamaguchi was applied to the
q
-Heun equation.
Consequently, a
q
-integral transformation of the
q
-Heun equation was suggested.
Note that it was discussed in
[
16
]
from another approach based on the kernel function identity, and the
q
-integral transformation was extended to the variants of the
q
-Heun equations.
In our previous paper
[
2
]
, the
q
-convolution of Sakai and Yamaguchi was reconsidered.
Namely, one parameter which corresponds to the integral cycle was added to the
q
-integral transformation of Sakai and Yamaguchi, and convergence of the
q
-integral transformation was considered.
As an application,
q
-integral representations of solutions to the
q
-hypergeometric equation and its variants were obtained.

In this paper, we reformulate the
q
-convolution of Sakai and Yamaguchi.
Our definition of the
q
-convolution is simpler than that of Sakai and Yamaguchi, and our discussion takes care of convergence.
We rewrite equation (
1.5
) to

(1.10)

\frac{Y(qx)-Y(x)}{-x}=\Big[\sum^{N}_{i=0}\frac{B_{i}}{x-b_{i}}\Big]Y(x),

where
b_{0}=0
, and define the
q
-convolution and the
q
-middle convolution for the tuple
(B_{0},B_{1},\dots,B_{N})
.
A merit of our definition is the additivity of two
q
-middle convolutions.
Namely, we have

(1.11)

mc^{q}_{\lambda}\circ mc^{q}_{\mu}=mc^{q}_{\lambda+\mu},

instead of equation (
1.6
) (see section
3
for the definition).
The
q
-convolution in this paper is also related to a
q
-integral transformation, and the
q
-middle convolution induces the
q
-integral transformation for solutions to
q
-difference equations.

We pay attention to a
q
-analogue of the addition for the Fuchsian equations, which is induced from a gauge-transformation of solutions.
Let
Y
be a solution to the system of differential equation given in equation (
1.1
), and let
(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{N})
be a tuple of complex numbers.
Then, the function
Z=(x-t_{0})^{a_{0}}(x-t_{1})^{a_{1}}\dots(x-t_{N})^{a_{N}}Y
satisfies the following system of differential equations;

(1.12)

\frac{dZ}{dx}=\left(\frac{A_{0}+a_{0}I_{m}}{x-t_{0}}+\frac{A_{1}+a_{1}I_{m}}{x-t_{1}}+\dots+\frac{A_{N}+a_{N}I_{m}}{x-t_{N}}\right)Z.

The correspondence of the tuple of the matrices
(A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{N})\mapsto(A_{0}+a_{0}I_{m},A_{1}+a_{1}I_{m},\dots,A_{N}+a_{N}I_{m})
is called the addition.

We introduce an analogue of the addition for systems of
q
-difference equations.
In particular, we investigate the transformations of the tuples
(B_{0},B_{1},\dots,B_{N})
induced from the transformations of the solutions by

(1.13)

Y(x)\mapsto x^{\mu}Y(x)\mbox{ and }Y(x)\mapsto\frac{(x/\gamma;q)_{\infty}}{(x/\delta;q)_{\infty}}Y(x),

where

(1.14)

(\alpha;q)_{\infty}=(1-\alpha)(1-q\alpha)(1-q^{2}\alpha)\cdots.

See section
2
for details of the
q
-analogue of addition.

In our previous paper
[
2
]
, we obtained
q
-integral representations of solutions to the
q
-hypergeometric equation and the variants of the
q
-hypergeometric equation by applying the
q
-middle convolution.
A standard form of the
q
-hypergeometric equation is given as

(1.15)

(x-q)g(x/q)+(abx-c)g(qx)-\{(a+b)x-q-c\}g(x)=0.

In this paper, we obtain several
q
-difference equations by applying
q
-deformations of the addition and the reformulated
q
-middle convolution repeatedly.
Consequently, we have formal
q
-integral representations of solutions.
The reformulated
q
-middle convolution would be better for discussing a sequence of
q
-difference equations.
We discuss sufficient conditions that the
q
-integral representations converge and they satisfy the attached
q
-difference equation, which is used to generate actual solutions.
The generalized
q
-hypergeometric equation of order
3
is written in the form

(1.16)

(a_{3}x-b_{3})g(q^{3}x)+(a_{2}x-b_{2})g(q^{2}x)+(a_{1}x-b_{1})g(qx)+(a_{0}x-b_{0})g(x)=0,\;a_{3}b_{3}a_{0}b_{0}\neq 0,

and we obtain double
q
-integral representations of solutions of this equation.
We also obtain other
q
-difference equations of order
3
, which seems to be novel.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we discuss
q
-deformations of the addition.
In Section
3
, we reformulate the
q
-convolution and discuss convergence of the
q
-integral transformation which is associated with the
q
-convolution.
In Section

, we obtain the
q
-hypergeometric equation and some generalizations by applying the
q
-middle convolutions and the
q
-deformations of the addition.
We obtain
q
-integral representations of solutions formally, and discuss convergence in some cases.
In Section
5
, we discuss composition of the
q
-middle convolutions and establish the formula in equation (
1.11
).
In Section
6
, we give concluding remarks.
In the appendix, we review the
q
-convolution and the
q
-middle convolution of Sakai and Yamaguch.

Throughout this paper, we assume that the complex number
q
satisfies
0<|q|<1
.

2.
q
-deformations of addition

To reformulate the
q
-convolution in a simpler form, we replace the expression of the linear
q
-difference equation which is associated with the
q
-convolution.
Sakai and Yamaguchi used the linear
q
-difference equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle Y(qx)=\Big(B_{\infty}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{B_{i}}{1-x/b_{i}}\Big)Y(x).

Set
B_{0}=I_{m}-B_{\infty}-B_{1}-\dots-B_{N}
and
b_{0}=0
.
Then, equation (
2.1
) is equivalent to

(2.2)

\frac{Y(qx)-Y(x)}{-x}=\Big[\frac{B_{0}}{x}+\sum^{N}_{i=1}\frac{B_{i}}{x-b_{i}}\Big]Y(x)=\Big[\sum^{N}_{i=0}\frac{B_{i}}{x-b_{i}}\Big]Y(x).

Note that we can regard the left hand side of equation (
2.2
) as the
q
-derivative of
Y(x)
by dividing by
1-q
.

We introduce a
q
-deformation of the addition.
If the function
Y(x)\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
satisfies
Y(qx)=B(x)Y(x)
, then the function

(2.3)

Z(x)=x^{\mu}Y(x)

satisfies
Z(qx)=q^{\mu}B(x)Z(x)
.
In particular, if
Y(x)
is a solution to equation (
2.2
), then the function
Z(x)
in equation (
2.3
) satisfies

(2.4)

\frac{Z(qx)-Z(x)}{-x}=\Big[\frac{(1-q^{\mu})I_{m}+q^{\mu}B_{0}}{x}+\sum^{N}_{i=1}\frac{q^{\mu}B_{i}}{x-b_{i}}\Big]Z(x).

Thus, we define the addition
add_{\mu}
by

(2.5)

add_{\mu}:(B_{0},B_{1},\ldots,B_{N})\mapsto((1-q^{\mu})I_{m}+q^{\mu}B_{0},q^{\mu}B_{1},\ldots,q^{\mu}B_{N}).

We introduce another
q
-deformation of the addition which is induced by the gauge-transformation

(2.6)

Y_{g}(x)=\frac{(x/b^{\prime}_{1};q)_{\infty}}{(x/b_{1};q)_{\infty}}Y(x).

We have

(2.7)

Y_{g}(qx)=\frac{(qx/b^{\prime}_{1};q)_{\infty}}{(qx/b_{1};q)_{\infty}}Y(qx)=\frac{(1-x/b_{1})}{(1-x/b^{\prime}_{1})}\frac{(x/b^{\prime}_{1};q)_{\infty}}{(x/b_{1};q)_{\infty}}Y(qx).

If the function
Y(x)\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
satisfies
Y(qx)=B(x)Y(x)
, then the function
Y_{g}(x)
satisfies

(2.8)

Y_{g}(qx)=\frac{(1-x/b_{1})}{(1-x/b^{\prime}_{1})}\frac{(x/b^{\prime}_{1};q)_{\infty}}{(x/b_{1};q)_{\infty}}B(x)Y(x)=\frac{(1-x/b_{1})}{(1-x/b^{\prime}_{1})}B(x)Y_{g}(x).

Note that equation (
2.2
) is equivalent to

(2.9)

Y(qx)=B(x)Y(x),\quad B(x)=\Big[I-B_{0}+\sum^{N}_{i=1}B_{i}\frac{-x}{x-b_{i}}\Big].

By rewriting
B(x)(1-x/b_{1})/(1-x/b^{\prime}_{1})
as the form of the second equation in (
2.9
), we obtain

(2.10)

\displaystyle Y_{g}(qx)=\Big[I-B_{0}+\Big\{\Big(1-\frac{b^{\prime}_{1}}{b_{1}}\Big)(I-B_{0})+\frac{b^{\prime}_{1}}{b_{1}}B_{1}-\sum^{N}_{i=2}\frac{1-b^{\prime}_{1}/b_{1}}{1-b_{i}/b^{\prime}_{1}}B_{i}\Big\}\frac{-x}{x-b^{\prime}_{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum^{N}_{i=2}\frac{1-b_{i}/b_{1}}{1-b_{i}/b^{\prime}_{1}}B_{i}\frac{-x}{x-b_{i}}\Big]Y_{g}(x).

Therefore, the gauge-transformation (
2.6
) induces the change of the poles
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{N})\mapsto(b^{\prime}_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{N})
and the tuple of the matrices
(B_{0},B_{1},\dots,B_{N})\mapsto(B^{\prime}_{0},B^{\prime}_{1},\dots,B^{\prime}_{N})
where

(2.11)

\displaystyle B^{\prime}_{0}=B_{0},\;B^{\prime}_{1}=\Big(1-\frac{b^{\prime}_{1}}{b_{1}}\Big)(I-B_{0})+\frac{b^{\prime}_{1}}{b_{1}}B_{1}-\sum^{N}_{i=2}\frac{1-b^{\prime}_{1}/b_{1}}{1-b_{i}/b^{\prime}_{1}}B_{i},

\displaystyle B^{\prime}_{j}=\frac{1-b_{j}/b_{1}}{1-b_{j}/b^{\prime}_{1}}B_{j}\;(j=2,\dots,N).

We may also regard this correspondence as a
q
-deformation of addition.

3.
q
-middle convolution and convergence

We reformulate the
q
-convolution by modifying the definition of Sakai and Yamaguchi.
See the appendix for the
q
-convolution of Sakai and Yamaguchi.

Definition 3.1

(Reformulation of
q
-convolution)
.

Let
(B_{0},B_{1},\dots,B_{N})
be the tuple of
m\times m
matrices and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.
We define the tuple
(G_{0},G_{1},\dots,G_{N})
of
(N+1)m\times(N+1)m
matrices as follows.

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}q^{-\lambda}B_{0}+(1-q^{-\lambda})I_{m}&q^{-\lambda}B_{1}&\cdots&\cdots&q^{-\lambda}B_{N}\\[5.0pt]
0&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&&\vdots\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle G_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&&O&&\\[5.0pt]
q^{-\lambda}B_{0}&\cdots&q^{-\lambda}B_{i}+(1-q^{-\lambda})I_{m}&\cdots&q^{-\lambda}B_{N}\\[5.0pt]
&&O&&\end{array}\right){\scriptstyle(i+1)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle G_{N}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\vdots&&&\vdots&\vdots\\[5.0pt]
0&\cdots&\cdots&0&0\\[5.0pt]
q^{-\lambda}B_{0}&\cdots&\cdots&q^{-\lambda}B_{N-1}&q^{-\lambda}B_{N}+(1-q^{-\lambda})I_{m}\end{array}\right).

The correspondence
c^{q}_{\lambda}:(B_{0},B_{1},\dots,B_{N})\mapsto(G_{0},G_{1},\dots,G_{N})
is called the
q
-convolution.

The
q
-convolution in Definition
3.1
is related with a
q
-integral transformation on the linear
q
-difference equations in the form of equation (
2.2
).
Let
\xi\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.
The Jackson integral on the open interval
(0,\infty)
is defined as the infinite sum

(3.10)

\int^{\xi\infty}_{0}f(s)\,d_{q}s=(1-q)\sum^{\infty}_{n=-\infty}q^{n}\xi f(q^{n}\xi).

Let
K_{\lambda}(x,s)
be a function which satisfies

(3.11)

q^{\lambda}K_{\lambda}(qx,s)=K_{\lambda}(x,s/q)=\frac{x-q^{\lambda}s}{x-s}K_{\lambda}(x,s).

Then, the functions

(3.12)

K^{(1)}_{\lambda}(x,s)=x^{-\lambda}\frac{(q^{\lambda+1}s/x;q)_{\infty}}{(qs/x;q)_{\infty}},\quad K^{(2)}_{\lambda}(x,s)=s^{-\lambda}\frac{(x/s;q)_{\infty}}{(q^{-\lambda}x/s;q)_{\infty}}

satisfy equation (
3.11
).
The functions

(3.13)

\frac{(q^{\lambda+1}s/x,bx,q/(bx);q)_{\infty}}{(qs/x,q^{-\lambda}bx,q^{\lambda+1}/(bx);q)_{\infty}},\quad\frac{(x/s,bs,q/(bs);q)_{\infty}}{(q^{-\lambda}x/s,q^{-\lambda}bs,q^{\lambda+1}/(bs);q)_{\infty}}

also satisfy equation (
3.11
) for any
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

The following theorem indicates a correspondence of the solutions to
q
-difference equations associated with the
q
-convolution.

Theorem 3.2
.

(
c.f.
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
2
, Corollary 2.3]
)

Set
b_{0}=0
.
Let
Y(x)
be a solution to

(3.14)

\frac{Y(qx)-Y(x)}{-x}=\Big[\sum^{N}_{i=0}\frac{B_{i}}{x-b_{i}}\Big]Y(x),

and set

(3.15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}_{i}(x)=\int^{\xi\infty}_{0}\frac{K_{\lambda}(x,s)}{s-b_{i}}Y(s)\,d_{q}s,\;(i=0,\dots,N),\;\widetilde{Y}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{Y}_{0}(x)\\
\vdots\\
\widetilde{Y}_{N}(x)\end{pmatrix}.

Assume that the variable
\xi
is independent of the variable
x
or it is proportional to
x
(i.e.
\xi=Ax
where
A
is independent of
x
).
If every element of
\widetilde{Y}_{i}(x)
converges for
i=0,1,\dots,N
and

(3.16)

\lim_{K\to-\infty}K_{\lambda}(x,q^{K-1}\xi)Y(q^{K}\xi)=0,\;\lim_{L\to+\infty}K_{\lambda}(x,q^{L}\xi)Y(q^{L+1}\xi)=0,

then the function
\widetilde{Y}(x)
satisfies

(3.17)

\frac{\widetilde{Y}(qx)-\widetilde{Y}(x)}{-x}=\Big[\sum^{N}_{i=0}\frac{G_{i}}{x-b_{i}}\Big]\widetilde{Y}(x).

To prove the theorem, we define the function
\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{[K,L]}(x)
by

(3.18)

\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{[K,L]}(x)=(1-q)\sum_{n=K}^{L}\frac{K_{\lambda}(x,s)}{s-b_{i}}sY(s)|_{s=q^{n}\xi}.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
Y(x)
be a solution to equation (
3.14
) and assume that the variable
\xi
is independent of the variable
x
.
For
i=0,1,\dots,N
, we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{[K,L]}(qx)-\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{[K,L]}(x)}{-x}=\frac{1-q^{-\lambda}}{x-b_{i}}\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{[K,L]}(x)+\frac{1}{x-b_{i}}\sum^{N}_{j=0}q^{-\lambda}B_{j}\widetilde{Y}_{j}^{[K,L]}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{(1-q)q^{-\lambda}}{x-b_{i}}\{K_{\lambda}(x,q^{K-1}\xi)Y(q^{K}\xi)-K_{\lambda}(x,q^{L}\xi)Y(q^{L+1}\xi)\}.

Proof.

It follows from equation (
3.11
) that

(3.20)

\displaystyle\frac{K_{\lambda}(qx,s)-K_{\lambda}(x,s)}{-x}=\frac{1-q^{-\lambda}}{x-s}K_{\lambda}(x,s),\quad\frac{K_{\lambda}(x,s/q)-K_{\lambda}(x,s)}{s}=\frac{1-q^{\lambda}}{x-s}K_{\lambda}(x,s).

Since

(3.21)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(x-s)(s-b_{i})}=\frac{1}{(s-b_{i})(x-b_{i})}+\frac{1}{(x-s)(x-b_{i})},

we have

(3.22)

\displaystyle\frac{K_{\lambda}(qx,s)-K_{\lambda}(x,s)}{-x(s-b_{i})}=\frac{1-q^{-\lambda}}{(s-b_{i})(x-b_{i})}K_{\lambda}(x,s)+\frac{1-q^{-\lambda}}{(x-s)(x-b_{i})}K_{\lambda}(x,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-q^{-\lambda}}{(s-b_{i})(x-b_{i})}K_{\lambda}(x,s)-\frac{q^{-\lambda}}{x-b_{i}}\frac{K_{\lambda}(x,s/q)-K_{\lambda}(x,s)}{s}.

By multiplying by
(1-q)sY(s)
and taking summation, we have

(3.23)

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{[K,L]}(qx)-\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{[K,L]}(x)}{-x}=\frac{1-q^{-\lambda}}{x-b_{i}}\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{[K,L]}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{q^{-\lambda}(1-q)}{x-b_{i}}\sum_{n=K}^{L}(K_{\lambda}(x,s/q)-K_{\lambda}(x,s))Y(s)|_{s=q^{n}\xi}.

It follows from equation (
3.14
) that

(3.24)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=K}^{L}(K_{\lambda}(x,s/q)-K_{\lambda}(x,s))Y(s)|_{s=q^{n}\xi}

\displaystyle=K_{\lambda}(x,q^{K-1}\xi)Y(q^{K}\xi)-K_{\lambda}(x,q^{L}\xi)Y(q^{L+1}\xi)+\sum_{n=K}^{L}K_{\lambda}(x,s)(Y(qs)-Y(s))|_{s=q^{n}\xi}

\displaystyle=K_{\lambda}(x,q^{K-1}\xi)Y(q^{K}\xi)-K_{\lambda}(x,q^{L}\xi)Y(q^{L+1}\xi)-\sum_{n=K}^{L}\sum^{N}_{j=0}\frac{sK_{\lambda}(x,s)}{s-b_{j}}B_{j}Y(s)|_{s=q^{n}\xi}

\displaystyle=K_{\lambda}(x,q^{K-1}\xi)Y(q^{K}\xi)-K_{\lambda}(x,q^{L}\xi)Y(q^{L+1}\xi)-\frac{1}{1-q}\sum^{N}_{j=0}B_{j}\widetilde{Y}_{j}^{[K,L]}(x).

Therefore, we obtain equation (
3.19
).
∎

The following corollary follows from the definition of the matrices
G_{i}

(i=0,1,\dots,N)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
Y(x)
be a solution to equation (

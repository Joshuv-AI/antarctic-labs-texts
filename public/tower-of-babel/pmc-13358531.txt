Thoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor: a systematic review and meta-analysis of individual patient data with cross-cohort survival comparison
Authors: Zhuoying Su, Guiyang Chen, Fengmei Zhong, Bin Liu, Shuhong Wu, Fu Rong, Yanhua Lv, Xi Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor (SMARCA4-UT) is a rare, highly virulent neoplasm lacking a standardized therapeutic protocol. Because current evidence is scarce, systemic management guidelines are not well-defined. We aimed to address this lack of data by synthesizing individual patient-level data from case reports and retrospective series. Our goal was to characterize treatment patterns and survival, focusing on combination strategies using immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs).MethodsA systematic literature search was performed in PubMed, Embase, and Web of Science from database inception to November 30, 2024, in accordance with PRISMA guidelines. Individual patient data were extracted from eligible published case reports and case series and pooled for unified survival analysis. Aggregated survival data from retrospective cohort studies were summarized for cross-study comparison. Overall survival (OS) was analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method, and treatment-specific outcomes were visualized using swimmer plots and forest plots.ResultsA total of 32 studies comprising 239 patients with thoracic SMARCA4-UT were included, consisting of 26 patients with individual-level data and 8 retrospective cohort studies. Patients were mostly male heavy smokers, and most cohort studies consisted of advanced-stage disease. In the pooled individual patient dataset, 46.2% of patients received first-line chemotherapy plus immunotherapy (Chemo-IO). The median OS (mOS) of pooled cases was 15.0 months, exceeding that reported in most retrospective cohorts treated predominantly with chemotherapy or immunotherapy monotherapy (mOS range, 4.0–7.7 months). Long-term survival was observed almost exclusively in patients receiving Chemo-IO or multimodal treatment. Clinical benefit from Chemo-IO was also observed in patients with negative PD-L1 expression.ConclusionsOur analysis suggests that combination therapy based on ICIs correlates with better survival in thoracic SMARCA4-UT, regardless of PD-L1 expression. Data support using immunotherapy-based regimens early as a first-line treatment for this aggressive disease. These real-world findings offer guidance for clinical decision-making while prospective trials are unavailable.

Highlight box

Key findings

• In this systematic review and meta-analysis of 239 patients with thoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor (SMARCA4-UT), first-line chemo-immunotherapy (Chemo-IO) was associated with a median overall survival of 15.0 months (95% confidence interval: 9.0 months to not reached), which compares favorably with 4.0–7.7 months in historical cohorts treated predominantly with chemotherapy or immunotherapy alone. Clinical benefit from Chemo-IO was observed regardless of PD-L1 expression status.

What is known and what is new?

• Thoracic SMARCA4-UT is a rare, highly aggressive malignancy newly classified by the World Health Organization in 2021, with no established standard of care and a median survival typically under one year.

• This study provides the first pooled individual patient-level survival analysis comparing treatment strategies, demonstrating that early integration of immune checkpoint inhibitors with chemotherapy may be associated with substantially prolonged survival compared with conventional approaches.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Although interpretation is limited by the retrospective, case-based nature of the data, the findings suggest that Chemo-IO could be considered a potential first-line option for eligible patients with thoracic SMARCA4-UT. Clinicians should maintain a high index of suspicion for this entity in patients with poorly differentiated thoracic tumors and prioritize rapid BRG1 immunohistochemistry to expedite diagnosis and timely initiation of chemo-immunotherapy, while awaiting confirmation from prospective studies.

Introduction

Thoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor (SMARCA4-UT) is a rare and highly aggressive tumor entity newly incorporated into the pulmonary tumor classification by the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2021 (1). This tumor is characterized by loss of SMARCA4 protein expression (also known as BRG1), with histological features of undifferentiated or rhabdoid morphology (1). The diagnosis of SMARCA4-UT relies on comprehensive evaluation: clinically presenting as a thoracic mass lesion, histologically demonstrating poorly differentiated small cell, epithelioid, or rhabdoid appearance, and immunohistochemically showing significant reduction or complete loss of BRG1 protein expression (2,3).

While thoracic SMARCA4-UT is increasingly recognized, evidence remains fragmented across isolated case reports and small cohorts. Most studies describe an extremely aggressive disease, with median overall survival (mOS) typically ranging from a few months to 1 year (4). However, few studies have evaluated survival outcomes or therapeutic efficacy quantitatively at the individual patient level. In practice, treatments vary widely—ranging from surgery to chemotherapy plus immunotherapy (Chemo-IO)—and patient responses are inconsistent (5). Consequently, the effectiveness of current strategies is unclear. Furthermore, the prognostic factors driving disease progression are not well understood, making it difficult to select evidence-based treatments or optimize patient management. To further illustrate the fulminant nature of this disease, we present a rapid and fatal case from The Eighth Affiliated Hospital of Southern Medical University (The First People’s Hospital of Shunde) before delving into the systematic literature analysis. We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0405/rc).

Methods

Study design

This study adopts a study design with systematic review and meta-analysis, integrating published case reports with individual patient data extracted from case series, and combining aggregated data from retrospective studies to conduct cross-cohort comparisons. Given the extreme rarity of thoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumors, this study employs a literature-based strategy for individual patient data analysis to achieve a uniform survival analysis and assess outcome differences across different treatment regimens. Because direct survival comparison between pooled case reports and unselected retrospective cohorts is methodologically subject to bias, cohort data were used primarily for contextualization rather than direct statistical merging.

Literature search and study selection

A systematic literature search was conducted in PubMed, Embase, and Web of Science from database inception to November 30, 2024, following the PRISMA guidelines. The search strategy incorporated controlled vocabulary terms and free-text keywords related to “SMARCA4”, “BRG1”, “thoracic tumor”, and “undifferentiated carcinoma”. Eligible studies included published case reports, case series, and retrospective cohort studies reporting thoracic SMARCA4-UT with available clinical, treatment, or survival data (Figure 1). The primary objective of the literature search was to enable extraction and re-analysis of individual patient-level survival data rather than narrative synthesis. The study protocol was prospectively registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251124451).

Literature inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (I) the study subjects were humans with a definite diagnosis of thoracic SMARCA4-UT [diagnosed based on the WHO criteria: undifferentiated or rhabdoid morphology with complete or near-complete loss of SMARCA4/BRG1 expression on immunohistochemistry (IHC)]; (II) clinical, pathological, imaging, genetic testing, treatment or prognostic results were reported; and (III) the type of the literature was an original study (case report, case series, cohort study, etc.).

Exclusion criteria: (I) SMARCA4-deficient tumors of non-thoracic origin; (II) non-original studies such as reviews, editorials, conference abstracts (without full text), animal experiments, etc.; (III) incomplete information and full text is not available; and (IV) non-Chinese and non-English literature.

Literature screening and deduplication process

All search results were imported into Zotero software for consolidation and deduplication. The full search strategy for all databases is provided in Table S1. Subsequently, two independent reviewers screened titles and abstracts, followed by full-text assessment. Any discrepancies were resolved through discussion or adjudication by a third reviewer.

Data extraction and quality assessment

A standardized data extraction form was used to collect baseline characteristics of study subjects, including sample size, patient demographics, tumor characteristics, genetic testing results, treatment regimens, and prognostic indicators. Bias was assessed from the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Tools cases and case series. Attrition was also impaired by incomplete outcome data. Cases with less missing data, such as those with no endpoint or survival time, were excluded from statistical analysis. Baseline information was retained as descriptive information. Traditional statistical evaluation of publication bias (e.g., funnel plots) could not be formally conducted due to the nature of case reports.

Statistical analysis

Data management and statistical analysis were carried out with Python 3.10 (Python Software Foundation, https://python.org) with Pandas (v2.0.3), Lifelines (v0.27.7), and Matplotlib libraries. For pooled individual patient data obtained from case reports, continuous variables (e.g., age, tumor mutational burden) were expressed as median with interquartile range (IQR) due to non-normal distribution. Categorical variables (such as sex, smoking status, PD-L1 expression level, treatment procedure) were represented as frequencies and percentages (n, %). Fisher’s exact test to measure difference of baseline properties of categorical variables within subgroups was used, while the Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables was used. Survival curves were calculated using Kaplan-Meier approach and median survival time with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) was calculated. Swimmer plots were used to illustrate individual case survival times and treatment responses. Compared data extracted from eight retrospective cohorts were used to contextualize survival across studies. Statistical heterogeneity was measured with Cochran’s Q test and quantified with the I2 statistic. The I2 value was <50%, indicating low to moderate heterogeneity. Forest plots were utilized to compare mOS between pooled individual case reports and published cohort studies. P<0.05 was significant.

Results

Clinical characteristics, treatment, and outcome of the case reported by The Eighth Affiliated Hospital of Southern Medical University (The First People’s Hospital of Shunde)

The patient was a 66-year-old male heavy smoker with a history of chronic alcohol consumption (approximately 200 mL per day). He presented to The Eighth Affiliated Hospital of Southern Medical University with a 3-month history of recurrent cough, blood-tinged sputum, and exertional dyspnea. He had no prior history of malignancy, surgery, or other chronic diseases, and no family history of hereditary disorders. Physical examination upon admission revealed scanty moist rales in the bilateral lower lung fields; otherwise, findings were unremarkable. During hospitalization, the patient developed recurrent high-grade fever accompanied by episodes of hypotension, and his dyspnea progressively worsened. Chest computed tomography (CT) revealed a mass-like opacity with increased density in the right lower lobe, measuring approximately 93 mm × 72 mm, which showed heterogeneous mild enhancement on contrast-enhanced scans. Multiple fibrotic and calcified nodules were observed in both lungs, some containing cavities (Figure 2).

Case illustration. (A,B) Chest CT showing a mass with increased density in the right lower lobe, with heterogeneous mild enhancement on contrast-enhanced imaging and multiple small vessels traversing the lesion. CT, computed tomography.

On the third day of hospitalization, bronchoscopy was performed, revealing a polypoid neoplasm obstructing the orifice of the dorsal segment of the right lower lobe. A biopsy was obtained. Histopathological analysis combined with IHC confirmed the diagnosis of SMARCA4-UT. The immunohistochemical profile of the atypical cells was as follows: SMARCA4 loss (−), synaptophysin (+), pan-cytokeratin (pan-CK) (partially +), TTF-1 (−), Napsin A (−), CK7 (−), p40 (−), CK5/6 (−), p53 (approximately 90%, strongly +), Ki-67 (proliferation index approximately 90%), c-MET (SP44) (−), ALK (D5F3) (−), PD-L1 (22C3) [tumor proportion score (TPS) approximately 10%], CgA (−), CD56 (−), p16 (−), and vimentin (+).

While awaiting the pathology report, the patient’s clinical condition deteriorated rapidly; consequently, comprehensive systemic staging and anti-tumor therapy could not be initiated. He rapidly developed severe pneumonia, septic shock, cardiac insufficiency, and coagulation abnormalities. Despite resuscitative efforts, the patient died on the eighth day of hospitalization.

All procedures performed in this study were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional and/or national research committee(s) and with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Written informed consent for publication of this case report and accompanying images was not obtained from the patient or the relatives after all possible attempts were made.

Descriptive analysis of literature cases

Through systematic literature search, we ultimately included 32 studies comprising 24 case reports or small case series (yielding 26 patients with individual-level data) and 8 retrospective cohort studies (cohort series), encompassing a total of 239 patients with thoracic SMARCA4-UT. The baseline characteristics of pooled cases and literature cohorts are summarized in Table 1. The median age of the overall population ranged from 55 to 68 years, with the pooled case series demonstrating a median age of 55.0 years, whereas the cohort series patients were relatively older (weighted average median age approximately 63.0 years). Among the 26 pooled cases, 24 (92.3%) were male. A smoking history was present in 19 patients (73.1%). Regarding clinical staging, the vast majority of cases in cohort studies presented with stage IV metastatic disease (e.g., 100% stage IV in both the Cooper and Lin cohorts) (30,31), while the pooled case series included a higher proportion of locally advanced cases, with only 13 patients (50.0%) classified as stage IV and 13 (50%) as stages II–III. This substantial difference in stage distribution reflects a potential publication bias favoring case reports of patients with surgical opportunities or earlier intervention. Regarding first-line treatment, 12 patients (46.2%) received Chemo-IO, 5 (19.2%) received chemotherapy only, 3 (11.5%) underwent multi-modal therapy including surgery, and 6 (23.1%) received other regimens or best supportive care. In our cross-cohort comparison, earlier or unselected cohorts exhibited lower utilization of first-line Chemo-IO (e.g., Gantzer 2022) (32), whereas more recent cohorts demonstrated increased adoption of combination strategies (e.g., Lin 2023) (31). Differences in survival outcomes corroborate these distinct treatment patterns. The pooled case series achieved a mOS of 15.0 months (95% CI: 9.0 months to not reached), which was superior to most literature cohorts predominantly receiving palliative treatment [mOS typically ranging from 4.0 to 7.7 months, such as 7.3 months reported by Cooper et al. (30) and 4.0 months by Gantzer et al. (32)]. Approximately 31.2% of patients in the pooled case series survived beyond 15 months, predominantly concentrated in the Chemo-IO subgroup. Regarding PD-L1 expression, 30.8% of cases in the pooled case series were negative (<1%), whereas the proportion of negativity in cohort cases was generally higher (approximately 44–70%, such as 70% in the Wang cohort) (33). Although both groups exhibited high rates of PD-L1 negativity, survival analysis suggests that low PD-L1 expression did not significantly impede patients from deriving benefit from combination immunotherapy.

Treatment patterns and survival outcomes

Among the pooled case series, we found that systemic therapy incorporating immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) was the predominant treatment strategy for SMARCA4-UT. Specifically, 46.2% (12/26) of patients received platinum-based Chemo-IO as first-line therapy, 19.2% (5/26) received chemotherapy alone, and 11.5% (3/26) underwent multimodal treatment including surgery followed by adjuvant Chemo-IO. The utilization rate of ICI-based combination regimens was markedly higher than that reported in early retrospective cohorts [such as Gantzer et al. (32) and Cooper et al. (30)], where chemotherapy alone or palliative care was more prevalent. The mOS of the pooled case series was 15.0 months (95% CI: 9.0–not reached). This survival outcome was superior to the eight literature cohorts included in this systematic pooled analysis, where mOS ranged from 4.0 [Gantzer et al. (32)] to 7.7 [Patnaik et al. (34)] and 16.5 months [Zhou et al. (5)] (Table 2). The advantage of ICI-based combination regimens is illustrated in the swimmer plot (Figure 3). The plot shows a long-term survival of patients in Chemo-IO (red bars), while the longest survival time was found only in Chemo-IO or multimodal treatment. For example, the patients from Nagata et al. (9) and Duan et al. (24) had OS above 54 and 43 months, respectively, and they were alive at last follow-up. The patients with chemotherapy alone (blue bars) often have shorter survival, with disease evolution or death occurring within 6 months [e.g., Chan et al. (12), Iguchi et al. (15)]. The swimmer plot highlights that ICI-based combination therapy provided sustained clinical advantage to PD-L1-negative (<1%) patients. For example, the patient reported by Wei et al. (23), despite negative PD-L1 expression and SMARCA4 deficiency, achieved a survival of 22 months with Chemo-IO treatment. The forest plot (Figure 4) further emphasizes the survival advantage of ICI-containing regimens. Cohorts with higher proportions of patients receiving Chemo-IO [such as Lin et al. (31) and Zhou et al. (5)] consistently reported superior mOS compared to cohorts predominantly treated with chemotherapy alone or ICI monotherapy [such as Gantzer et al. (32), mOS: 4.0 months]. This cross-study comparison strongly supports the critical role of Chemo-IO regimens in overcoming the “immune desert” microenvironment characteristic of SMARCA4-UT.

Discussion

Thoracic SMARCA4-UT is an uncommon but very aggressive malignancy. Our analysis confirms the poor survival outcomes associated with this disease. Demographics in our dataset were consistent: mostly male patients with a heavy smoking history. A critical finding is the narrow therapeutic window. Some patients, including the case from The Eighth Affiliated Hospital of Southern Medical University (The First People’s Hospital of Shunde), deteriorated rapidly and suffered multiorgan failure before systemic treatment could start. This indicates that diagnostic delays can effectively eliminate chances for disease control. By combining individual data with cohort analyses, we provide quantitative support for early diagnosis and immediate systemic therapy.

Early clinical recognition: fulminant progression and a limited therapeutic window

A consistently narrow window for effective intervention emerged from the pooled analysis, driven by rapid disease escalation in a subset of patients. This pattern was clearly illustrated by the institutional case included in The Eighth Affiliated Hospital of Southern Medical University (The First People’s Hospital of Shunde), in which explosive tumor progression resulted in multiorgan failure prior to the initiation of systemic therapy. Comparable trajectories of abrupt clinical deterioration were observed among other pooled cases, reinforcing the notion that delayed recognition substantially restricts treatment feasibility. These data collectively highlight the necessity of heightened clinical vigilance and expedited pathological confirmation, particularly when patients present with poorly differentiated thoracic tumors accompanied by aggressive radiographic features. Similar patterns of sudden clinical worsening were noted in other combined instances, emphasizing the impact of delayed identification on treatment options. These findings underscore the importance of increased clinical awareness and prompt confirmation of diagnosis, especially in cases of undifferentiated chest tumors with aggressive imaging characteristics. From a pragmatic perspective, our findings advocate for the early application of SMARCA4/BRG1 immunohistochemical assessment in undifferentiated thoracic malignancies, rather than postponing such testing until completion of standard non–small cell lung cancer molecular profiling [Li et al. (37), 2024].

Survival benefit associated with ICI-based combination therapy

One of the central observations of this pooled analysis is the survival advantage associated with ICI-based combination therapy. Within the individual patient dataset, those receiving first-line Chemo-IO exhibited longer OS compared with patients treated with chemotherapy alone or immunotherapy monotherapy. Durable survival was observed almost exclusively among patients exposed to Chemo-IO or multimodal treatment strategies, a finding that was consistently replicated across retrospective cohorts in which combination regimens were more frequently employed.

These results challenge the applicability of conventional sequential treatment paradigms derived from non–small cell lung cancer, wherein immunotherapy is often deferred until disease progression. Instead, the data support early integration of immunotherapy into first-line systemic treatment when clinical circumstances permit. Such an approach appears particularly pertinent for SMARCA4-UT, a disease in which delayed or insufficient therapy may rapidly erode any remaining therapeutic opportunity.

PD-L1 expression: an unreliable predictive marker

The potential survival advantage of Chemo-IO may stem from specific biological effects of SMARCA4 deficiency. Previous studies have shown that alterations in SMARCA4-related epigenetics play a crucial role in increased responsiveness to immunotherapy (38,39). Specifically, loss of SMARCA4 alters chromatin accessibility at enhancer regions, thereby modulating the expression of innate immune pathway genes, including STING and IL-1β (40). Such epigenetic reprogramming may foster immune activation within the tumor microenvironment and confer responsiveness to ICIs through pathways largely independent of PD-L1 expression (41).

Consistent with this mechanistic framework, our analysis indicates that clinical benefit from Chemo-IO is not confined to patients with elevated PD-L1 expression. Several individuals with PD-L1-negative tumors achieved prolonged survival following combination therapy, suggesting the limited predictive value of PD-L1 status in SMARCA4-UT. This observation aligns with accumulating evidence that SMARCA4-driven epigenetic remodeling can promote antitumor immune responses via PD-L1-independent mechanisms, thereby supporting the use of immunotherapy irrespective of PD-L1 expression levels (5,42).

Impact of co-mutations and molecular heterogeneity

While Chemo-IO often led to extended survival, its benefits were not uniformly seen in all SMARCA4-UT patients. The significant molecular diversity of this cancer type plays a crucial role in this disparity. Specifically, simultaneous genetic changes, primarily mutations in STK11 or KEAP1, have been found to reduce the effectiveness of immunotherapy and are commonly linked to worse clinical outcomes (42). While comprehensive genomic profiling was not uniformly available among the aggregated cases, evidence derived from existing patient cohorts indicates that such co-mutations foster an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, thereby diminishing the effectiveness of immune checkpoint blockade (43).

The crucial importance of thorough molecular profiling in clinical care is underscored by these findings. Incorporating next-generation sequencing into regular diagnostic processes could enhance prognostic accuracy and improve treatment decision-making. Patients with adverse molecular patterns may not benefit from standard treatments; hence, exploring alternative approaches or intensifying treatment, possibly by enrolling in trials assessing innovative combination therapies, is warranted.

Limitations

Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, directly comparing pooled case reports with unselected retrospective cohorts introduces bias. Second, case reports inherently suffer from selection and publication bias; favorable clinical courses and long-term survivors are overrepresented, which likely overestimates the true prognosis of SMARCA4-UT. Furthermore, due to the retrospective nature of the published case reports, certain critical baseline characteristics, such as Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, were completely unreported, precluding a comprehensive evaluation of patient fitness. In addition, substantial variability in therapeutic regimens, duration of follow-up, and reporting quality across included studies limits the strength of causal inferences. Moreover, the lack of complete patient-level genomic data restricted the feasibility of conducting detailed subgroup analyses based on specific molecular alterations. Lastly, owing to the extreme rarity of SMARCA4-UT, the implementation of prospective randomized clinical trials remains unrealistic at present, leaving pooled observational analyses as the primary evidence base for guiding clinical decision-making.

Conclusions

This pooled analysis confirms that thoracic SMARCA4-UT is an aggressive malignancy with rapid progression and poor outcomes under standard therapies. We found quantitative evidence linking ICI-based combination therapy to improved survival. Therefore, this approach may be associated with improved survival and could be considered as a potential first-line option for eligible patients. However, given the limitations inherent in retrospective and case-based data, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Prospective studies are urgently needed to definitively establish the optimal first-line therapy for thoracic SMARCA4-UT.

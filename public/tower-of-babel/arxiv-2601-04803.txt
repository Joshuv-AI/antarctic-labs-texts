Abstract
. In this paper, we establish an operator-valued Fourier multiplier theorem in weighted Lebesgue spaces, Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, assuming the multiplier has ℛ \mathcal{R} -bounded range and satisfies an ℓ r \ell^{r} -summability condition on its bounded s s -variation seminorms over dyadic intervals. The exponents r r and s s reflect the relationship between the geometric properties of the underlying Banach spaces (type and cotype) and the boundedness of Fourier multiplier operators. As our main tool we prove a weighted vector-valued variational Carleson inequality and deduce an estimate of Littlewood–Paley–Rubio de Francia type.

1.
Introduction

Fourier multiplier operators are a central tool in harmonic analysis, with far-reaching applications to partial differential equations, function space theory, numerical analysis and analytic number theory. In this paper we are concerned with an operator-valued version of this theory. Before turning to this setting, we briefly recall some classical results from the scalar-valued case.

In the scalar-valued case, a Fourier multiplier operator
T_{m}
for
m\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is given by

T_{m}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m\cdot\mathcal{F}(f)),\quad f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}),

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform. Sufficient conditions
for the boundedness of a Fourier multiplier operator
T_{m}
on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
are provided by the classical Fourier multiplier theorems due to Marcinkiewicz
[
51
]
, Mihlin
[
54
,
55
]
and Hörmander
[
31
]
.
For the current paper the formulation of Marcinkiewicz’ multiplier theorem is the most natural starting point. Here and below
\Delta
is the dyadic interval partition of
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Marcinkiewicz
[
51
]
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
m\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that for
J\in\Delta
,
m|_{J}\in V^{1}(J)
and

\displaystyle\lVert m\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}(V^{1}(\Delta))}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{J\in\Delta}\lVert m|_{J}\rVert_{V^{1}(J)}<\infty,

where
V^{1}(J)
is the space of functions of bounded variation. Then
T_{m}
is bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
.

In
[
15
]
, Coifman, Rubio de Francia and Semmes relaxed the assumption of bounded variation in Theorem
1.1
to bounded
s
-variation for some
s>1
(see Section
2.5
below). Bounded
s
-variation is weaker for larger
s
and is implied by
\frac{1}{s}
-Hölder smoothness. Coifman et al. proved their result for the boundedness of
T_{m}
on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
for
m\in\ell^{\infty}(V^{s}(\Delta))
in three steps:

(1)

First, they considered the case
p\in[2,\infty)
and
s\in[1,2)
, using the extension of the Littlewood–Paley inequality of Rubio de Francia to arbitrary intervals of
[
18
]
:

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}|S_{I}f|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})},

(1.1)

where
p\in[2,\infty)
,
\mathcal{I}
is a family of disjoint intervals in
{\mathbb{R}}
and
S_{I}=T_{m}
for
m:={{\bf 1}}_{I}
.

(2)

Secondly, the case
p\in(1,2)
and
s\in[1,2)
follows by duality.

(3)

Finally, note that the map
(m,f)\mapsto T_{m}f
is bounded from
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
by the Plancherel theorem. Hence, using bilinear interpolation they concluded that
T_{m}
is bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
if
m\in\ell^{\infty}(V^{s}(\Delta))
with
\frac{1}{s}>\lvert\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\rvert
for
p,s\in(1,\infty)
.

Related multiplier results have been studied in
[
7
,
44
,
45
]
.

1.1.
Fourier multipliers in the vector-valued setting

Now let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces.
An operator-valued Fourier multiplier operator
T_{m}
is of the form

T_{m}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m\cdot\mathcal{F}(f)),\quad f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}};X),

where
m\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, the space
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}};X)
denotes the
X
-valued Schwartz functions and
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform.

The classical multiplier theorems due to Marcinkiewicz and Mihlin were extended to
\UMD
spaces in two stages. The case
X=Y
with scalar-valued multipliers
m
was first addressed in the 1980s by McConnell
[
52
]
and Bourgain
[
9
]
, and extended by Zimmermann
[
71
]
(see also
[
37
]
).
Subsequently, in the early 2000s, the more general setting with
X\neq Y
and operator-valued multipliers
m
was developed and was motivated by applications to evolution equations such as maximal
L^{p}
-regularity and stability of semigroups (see
[
68
]
).
An operator-valued analogue of the Mihlin multiplier theorem was proven by Weis
[
69
]
and applied to maximal
L^{p}
-regularity, and shortly after an analogue of the Marcinkiewicz multiplier theorem by Štrkalj and Weis
[
64
]
. These theorems require a so-called
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness condition (see
[
39
, Chapter 8]
) on the range of
m
, a strengthening of uniform boundedness that was shown to be necessary by Clément and Prüss
[
14
]
. The reader is referred to the Notes of Chapter 8 in
[
39
]
for further references.

This naturally leads to the question whether the aforementioned result of Coifman et al.
[
15
]
admits an extension to the operator-valued setting. This programme was initiated by Hytönen and Potapov
[
35
]
, where the three main steps mentioned above are followed.

(1)

The first step in the operator-valued setting builds on two hypotheses: the range of
m
is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded and the availability of an
X
-valued version of the Littlewood–Paley–Rubio de Francia estimate (
1.1
), introduced by Berkson, Gillespie and Torrea
[
6
]
and called the
\LPR_{p}
-property of
X
. This estimate was further studied in
[
3
,
26
,
42
,
58
]
.
At present, the
\LPR_{p}
-property is only known to hold for Banach
function
spaces satisfying additional geometric requirements, such as the requirement that the
2
-concavification
X^{2}
is UMD (see
[
3
,
58
]
). It is an open problem whether, e.g., the Schatten class
S^{t}
with
t\in[2,\infty)
has the
\LPR_{p}
-property.

(2)

In the second step one can still employ a duality argument.

(3)

In the third step, Hytönen and Potapov
[
35
]
additionally require that
X
is a complex interpolation space between a Banach space
X_{0}
with the
\LPR_{p}
-property and a Hilbert space
H
. Furthermore, the
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness condition has to be replaced by a more intricate condition: the existence of an
\mathcal{R}
-bounded family
\mathcal{T}\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}(X_{0}\cap H)
such that
\Ran(m)\subseteq\mathrm{span}(\mathcal{T})
and
m
has bounded
s
-variation in the Minkowski functional with respect to
\mathcal{T}
. This requirement can be quite delicate to check.

A further generalization of this approach for Banach function spaces was obtained in
[
2
]
.

1.2.
Special case of our main result

The goal of this paper is to prove a weighted operator-valued Fourier multiplier theorem for symbols
m
of bounded
s
-variation with
s>1
, where
s
is related to the geometry of the underlying Banach spaces. We aim for assumptions on the underlying Banach spaces that can be verified beyond the class of Banach function spaces and, at the same time, avoid the intricate operator-theoretic conditions arising from the interpolation argument sketched above.
To do so, we will introduce a different type of Rubio de Francia property.

A simplified version of our main result reads as follows.
A space
X
is called
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
if it can be written as
X=[X_{0},H]_{\theta}
for some
\theta\in(0,1]
. We will use the class
A_{p}
of Muckenhoupt weights for
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
,
t\in(1,2]
,
q\in[2,\infty)
and set
\frac{1}{r}:=\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{q}
. Let
X
be a
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
Banach space with cotype
q
and
Y
be a
\UMD
Banach space with type
t
. Let
s\in[1,\frac{2}{2-\theta})
and suppose that
m\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
has
\mathcal{R}
-bounded range and

\|m\|_{\ell^{r}(\dot{V}^{s}(\Delta;\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))}:=\Bigl(\sum_{J\in\Delta}[m|_{J}]_{V^{s}(J;\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}^{r}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r}}<\infty.

Then
T_{m}
is bounded from
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
to
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y)
for all
p\in(s,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p/s}
.

Moreover, if, additionally,
Y
is a
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
Banach space, then
T_{m}
is bounded from
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)
to
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};Y)

for all
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Theorem
1.2
is established in the main text as Theorem
5.2
. One can assume without loss of generality that
r>s
. Moreover, the result is applicable to
m(\xi)=\rm{sign}(\xi)
, so the UMD property of
X
and
Y
is necessary in general (see
[
38
, Chapter 5]
).

Our result is the first of its kind on general
\theta
-intermediate UMD spaces. As discussed at the end of Subsection
1.1
, previous results in the operator-valued setting either only apply to Banach function spaces or include assumptions on
m
in the interpolation endpoints of
X=[X_{0},H]_{\theta}
.

Compared to the operator-valued analogue of Theorem
1.1
(see
[
30
,
64
]
and
[
39
, Theorem 8.3.9]
), we note that to weaken the
V^{1}
-boundedness to
V^{s}
-boundedness we need to impose a decay condition at zero and infinity in terms of
\ell^{r}
-summability unless
r=\infty
. On the other hand, we do not need to use any Minkowski functionals related to an
\mathcal{R}
-bounded family
\mathcal{T}
containing the range of
m
in its linear span.

We do not know if the decay condition in Theorem
1.2
is necessary. It can, for example, be avoided if

X
has cotype
2
and
Y
has type
2
. It can also be omitted if the
L^{p}
-spaces are replaced by homogeneous Besov spaces
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}
, in which case the condition on
m
takes the simpler form

\sup_{J\in\Delta}\|m\|_{V^{s}(J;\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}<\infty.

Note that in this case type and cotype no longer play a role in the assumptions on
m
. A similar result holds for the inhomogeneous Besov spaces (see Theorem
5.3
) and in the Triebel–Lizorkin scale (see Theorem
5.4
).

Theorem
1.2
yields various Fourier multipliers that are not covered by the Marcinkiewicz or Mihlin multiplier theorems. Indeed, for
s>1
, the space
V^{s}
is substantially larger than
V^{1}
. There is also an
L^{1}
-approach to construct multipliers outside the Marcinkiewicz–Mihlin class, based on the
L^{1}
-integrability of
{\mathscr{F}}^{-1}m
or
{\mathscr{F}}^{-1}({{\bf 1}}_{J}m)
in the strong operator topology. The
L^{1}
-approach plays a central role in
[
27
,
28
]
, where Fourier multiplier theorems on Besov and Lebesgue–Bochner spaces are obtained under the assumption that
X
and
Y
have Fourier type. One of the main building blocks there is a Steklin type multiplier theorem:

m\in B^{1/p}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{-1}mx\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}};Y)\text{ and }\mathcal{F}^{-1}my^{*}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}};X^{*}),\quad x\in X,y^{*}\in Y^{*}.

Since
B^{1/p}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
embeds into the space of continuous functions (
[
40
, Corollary 14.4.27]
), the multipliers satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, being not necessarily continuous, do not fit into this
L^{1}
-approach.

On the other hand, in the next example we show that Theorem
1.2
does not fully cover all multipliers satisfying the assumptions in the operator-valued Mihlin or Marcinkiewicz multiplier theorems. This is due to the decay condition at infinity in case
r<\infty
in Theorem
1.2
.

Example 1.3
.

Let
X=\ell^{p}
with
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
(e_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be the canonical basis of
\ell^{p}
. Let
A
be a multiplication operator on
\ell^{p}
given by
Ae_{n}=2^{n}e_{n}
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with its natural domain
D(A)=\{x\in\ell^{p}:Ax\in\ell^{p}\}
. Define

m(\xi)=A(i\xi+A)^{-1},\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Then we have for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\|m(2^{n+1})-m(2^{n})\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\ell^{p})}

\displaystyle\geq\|m(2^{n+1})e_{n}-m(2^{n})e_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle=|2^{n}(i2^{n+1}+2^{n})^{-1}-2^{n}(i2^{n}+2^{n})^{-1}|

\displaystyle=|(2i+1)^{-1}-(i+1)^{-1}|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}.

Since
m
is continuous, we find that
[m]_{V^{s}(J;{\mathcal{L}}(\ell^{p}))}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}
for every dyadic interval
J\in\Delta
. Therefore,
m\notin\ell^{r}(\dot{V}^{s}(\Delta;\mathcal{L}(\ell^{p})))
for
r<\infty
. However, it is known that
m
satisfies the operator-valued Mihlin or Marcinkiewicz multiplier theorem conditions, see, for instance,
[
43
, Proposition 6.1.3]
.

We conclude this subsection with an example that does not seem to be covered by any existing result. The closest statement we are aware of is
[
2
, Example 5.19]
, but there the hypotheses on
m
are of a different nature. In the scalar-valued case, the result from
[
2
]
is considerably stronger.

Example 1.4
.

Fix
t\in(1,2)
and let
X=S^{t}
be the Schatten class on
\ell^{2}
. Then
X
has type
t
and cotype
2
(see
[
39
, Proposition 7.1.11]
), and it is
\theta
-intermediate UMD for all
\theta\in(0,2-\frac{2}{t})
(see
[
38
, Propositions 5.4.2 and D.3.1]
). Set
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{2}
and choose
s\in(1,t)
. Theorem
1.2
then yields that if
m\in\ell^{r}(\dot{V}^{s}(\Delta;\mathcal{L}(S^{t})))
and
m
has
\mathcal{R}
-bounded range, then
T_{m}
is bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};S^{t})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
.
An analogous statement holds for
t\in(2,\infty)
and follows either by a similar argument or by duality.

1.3.
Methodology

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we establish a new vector-valued estimate of Littlewood–Paley–Rubio de Francia type, which in some sense is weaker and thus easier to check than the
\LPR_{p}
-property, as will be explained below Proposition
4.3
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
,
q\in(2/\theta,\infty)
,
p\in(q^{\prime},\infty)
and let
w\in A_{p/q^{\prime}}
. Let
X
be a
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
Banach space and let
\mathcal{I}
be a family of disjoint intervals of
{\mathbb{R}}
. Then there exists an increasing function
\phi_{X,p,q}:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\|S_{I}f\|^{q}_{X}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}\leq\phi_{X,p,q}([w]_{A_{p/q^{\prime}}})\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}.

(1.2)

The excluded endpoint
q=2
when
\theta=1
(i.e.
X
is a Hilbert space) can be found in
[
3
, Proposition 6.6]
, which is a weighted vector-valued extension of (
1.1
). Theorem
1.5
in the scalar case with
w=1
can be found in
[
18
]
and weights were added in
[
44
]
.
The limiting case
p=q^{\prime}
was shown to be false in
[
16
]
.

We will establish Theorem
1.5
as a special case of the weighted boundedness of the variational Carleson operator, see Theorem
4.2
. Compared to (
1.2
), the variational Carleson operator has an additional supremum over all
\mathcal{I}
inside the
L^{p}
-norm. The analogue for
q=\infty
is usually called Carleson’s inequality, and is the main tool to establish almost everywhere convergence of Fourier series
[
13
,
32
]
.
The (weighted) boundedness of the variational Carleson operator in the scalar-valued case was established in
[
20
,
21
,
56
]
.
The unweighted vector-valued boundedness of the Carleson operator (i.e. Theorem
1.5
with

q=\infty
) was established in
[
34
]
for intermediate UMD spaces. The unweighted vector-valued boundedness of the variational Carleson operator was studied in
[
4
]
, which in particular implies (
1.2
) in the case
w=1
.

We will establish the weighted boundedness of the variational Carleson operator by extending
[
34
]
to the weighted setting through
[
49
]
, and afterwards interpolating the result with weighted bounds for the variational Carleson operator on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};H)
for Hilbert spaces
H
from
[
3
,
20
]
(see also
[
50
]
).
We note that the following variant of (
1.2
) was developed in
[
19
, Section 2.2]
in the periodic setting:

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\|S_{I}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)}.

(1.3)

By Minkowski’s inequality, either (
1.2
) or (
1.3
) is stronger, depending on the relative size of
p
and
q
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.5
, we will show that for
m\in V^{s}({\mathbb{R}};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
with
s\in[1,\frac{2}{2-\theta})
, one has that
T_{m}
is bounded from
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
to
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y)
for all
p\in(s,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p/s}
in Theorem
5.1
. In the proof of this result,
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of the range of
m
does not play any role. This is rather surprising, since it is known that the
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness is necessary for the boundedness of
T_{m}
(see
[
14
]
and
[
38
, Theorem 5.3.15]
). In Theorems
1.2
and
5.2
,
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness enters in an indirect way by a decomposition of
m
, where one part in the decomposition is constant on dyadic intervals.

In Proposition
3.2
, we will show that in the setting of Theorem
1.2
, one actually has that any
m\in\ell^{r}(R^{r}(\mathcal{J};\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))
has
\mathcal{R}
-bounded range, where
\mathcal{J}
is a collection of disjoint intervals in
{\mathbb{R}}
. This is a new result on
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness which complements the work of
[
36
]
and came as a surprise to the authors. Here
R^{r}
is the space used in
[
15
]
defined via certain atoms, for which one has
R^{s}\hookrightarrow V^{s}\hookrightarrow R^{r}
with
s<r
. Motivated by the above, we will prove a general embedding result for Besov spaces and
V^{r}
and
R^{r}
in Lemma
3.3
.

1.4.
Organization

In Section
2
we present some preliminaries. Moreover, we introduce

and

, and prove some new results for them. Then in Section
3
we introduce

-boundedness and give a new sufficient condition for it in terms of the

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

The space of bounded linear operators between complex Banach spaces
X
and
Y
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, and
\mathcal{L}(X):=\mathcal{L}(X,X)
. We write
X\hookrightarrow Y
if
X
embeds in
Y
continuously. The space of
X
-valued tempered distributions is denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}};X)
.
The Fourier transform of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R};X)
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}f
or
\widehat{f}
. If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};X)
, then

\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2\pi i\xi t}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
\Delta
denote the dyadic partition of
\mathbb{R}
, i.e.

\Delta:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\pm[2^{k},2^{k+1}).
The restriction of a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to X
to
J\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by
f|_{J}
. The range of
f
is denoted by
\Ran(f)
.

Constants depending on parameters
a,b,\ldots
are denoted by
C_{a,b,\ldots}
, and their values may vary from line to line. We will often suppress the constant
C_{a,b,\ldots}
by writing
\lesssim_{a,b,\ldots}
. We write
\phi_{a,b,\ldots}\colon[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
to denote a non-decreasing function
which depends only on the parameters
a,b,\ldots
and which may also
change from line to line.

2.2.
Weights

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, we define the
Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
-class
as the class of all locally integrable
w\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to(0,\infty)
such that

[w]_{A_{p}}:=\underset{J}{\sup}\Bigl(\frac{1}{|J|}\int_{J}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{1}{|J|}\int_{J}w(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigr)^{p-1}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all bounded intervals
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. The Muckenhoupt classes are increasing in
p
, i.e. if
p_{0}<p_{1}
, then

A_{p_{0}}\subseteq A_{p_{1}}
with
[w]_{A_{p_{1}}}\leq[w]_{A_{p_{0}}}
.
Let
A_{\infty}=\bigcup_{p>1}A_{p}
.

We have the following self-improvement lemma (see
[
41
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
29
, Corollary 7.2.6]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
, then for any
w\in A_{p}
we have

[w]_{A_{p-\varepsilon}}\lesssim_{p}[w]_{A_{p}},\quad 0\leq\varepsilon\leq\frac{p-1}{1+C[w]_{A_{p}}^{(p-1)^{-1}}}.

For a Banach space
X
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}
we define
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
as the space of all strongly measurable
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to X
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}:=\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\|f(x)\|_{X}^{p}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/p}<\infty.

Recall from
[
23
, Lemma 3.3]
that
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}};X)
is dense in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
.

2.3.
UMD spaces

For an introduction to
\UMD
Banach spaces the reader is referred to
[
38
, Chapters 4 and 5]
and
[
57
]
. We briefly recall some basics.
Let
X
be a Banach space. We denote by
\mathcal{H}
the Hilbert transform, for
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}};X)
given by the principal value integral

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}f(x):=\pv\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}y,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(2.1)

A Banach space is said to have the
\UMD
property
if for some (equivalently all)
p\in(1,\infty)
one has that the Hilbert transform extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)
. By the seminal works of Burkholder
[
12
]
and Bourgain
[
8
]
, this is equivalent to the original definition of the
\UMD
property in terms of the Unconditionality of Martingale Differences.

All reflexive Lebesgue, Sobolev, Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces are
\UMD
Banach spaces. The
\UMD
property implies reflexivity, so
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
are not
\UMD
Banach spaces.

Recall that for an interval
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
,
S_{I}
is defined as the Fourier multiplier operator with symbol
m={{\bf 1}}_{I}
. The vector-valued analogue of the Littlewood–Paley inequality for
\UMD
spaces was obtained in
[
9
]
(see also
[
38
, Chapter 5]
). The following weighted extension can be found in
[
23
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space,
(\varepsilon_{J})_{J\in\Delta}
be a Rademacher sequence,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}
. Then we have for all
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\|\sum_{J\in\Delta}\varepsilon_{J}S_{J}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w;X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{X,p}[w]^{2\max\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\}}_{A_{p}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w;X)},

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w;X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{X,p}[w]^{2\max\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\}}_{A_{p}}\mathbb{E}\big\|\sum_{J\in\Delta}\varepsilon_{J}S_{J}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w;X)}.

In the next definition we will use the complex interpolation method
[X,Y]_{\theta}
, for which we refer to
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
. A Banach space
X
is called a
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
Banach space
if it can be written as
X=[Z,H]_{\theta}
for a
\UMD
Banach space
Z
and a Hilbert space
H
.

If
\theta=1
we have
X=H
, i.e.
X
is a Hilbert space. If
X
is
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
, then its dual is also
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
(see
[
5
, Corollary 4.5.2]
). Since the
\UMD
property is stable under complex interpolation, any intermediate
\UMD
Banach space is a
\UMD
Banach space. Conversely, all
\UMD
Banach
function
spaces are intermediate
\UMD
Banach function spaces by a result of Rubio de Francia
[
60
]
. For general Banach spaces this is an open problem.

2.4.
Type and cotype

Let
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the Rademacher sequence.
A Banach space
X
is said to have
type
t\in[1,2]
if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that for all finite sequences
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in
X
, we have

\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}\bigr\|^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\,\Bigl(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|x_{n}\|^{t}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{t}}.

A Banach space
X
is said to have
cotype
{q}\in[2,\infty]
if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that for all finite sequences
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in
X
, we have

\Bigl(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|x_{n}\|^{{q}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{{q}}}\leq C\,\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}\bigr\|^{{2}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}},

with the usual modification for
{q}=\infty
.
Every Banach space has type 1 and cotype
\infty
. The space
X
has type 2 and cotype 2 if and only if
X
is isomorphic to a Hilbert space (
[
46
, Proposition 3.1]
).
For
p\in[1,\infty)
the (noncommutative)
L^{p}
-spaces are known to have type
p\wedge 2
and cotype
p\vee 2
, see
[
22
,
39
]
.

Finally, we note that for a
\sigma
-finite measure space
(S,\mu)
, if
X
has type
t
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, then
L^{p}(S;X)
has type
t\wedge p
. Moreover, if
X
has cotype
q
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, then
L^{p}(S;X)
has cotype
p\vee q
. In particular, the space
L^{2}(S;X)
has the same type and cotype as
X
(see
[
39
, Proposition 7.1.4]
).

2.5.
The function spaces
R^{s}
,
V^{s}
and
C^{\alpha}

In this subsection we introduce the spaces of bounded
s
-variation
V^{s}
for
s\in[1,\infty)
, which will play a central role in the main result of the paper.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,
s\in[1,\infty)
. For
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to X
define

\left[f\right]_{V^{s}(J;X)}:=\sup_{t_{0}<\cdots<t_{N}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left\|f(t_{i+1})-f(t_{i})\right\|_{X}^{s}\right)^{1/s}.

(2.2)

We write
f\in\dot{V}^{s}(J;X)
if (
2.2
) is finite.
We say that
f
has
bounded
s
-variation for
s\in[1,\infty)
, denoted by
f\in V^{s}(J;X)
, if

\left\|{f}\right\|_{V^{s}(J;X)}:=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(J;X)}+\left[f\right]_{V^{s}(J;X)}<\infty.

(2.3)

Furthermore, we use the convention
V^{\infty}(J;X):=L^{\infty}(J;X)
. Note that
V^{s}(J;X)
is a Banach space.

Given a collection of disjoint sets
\mathcal{J}
in
{\mathbb{R}}
and
r\in[1,\infty]
, let
\ell^{r}(V^{s}(\mathcal{J};X))
and
\ell^{r}(\dot{V}^{s}(\mathcal{J};X))
denote the space of all
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};X)
such that

\lVert f\rVert_{\ell^{r}(V^{s}(\mathcal{J};X))}:=\Bigl(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\lVert f|_{J}\rVert_{V^{s}(J;X)}^{r}\Bigr)^{1/r}<\infty,

and

\lVert f\rVert_{\ell^{r}(\dot{V}^{s}(\mathcal{J};X))}:=\Bigl(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}[{f|_{J}}]_{V^{s}(J;X)}^{r}\Bigr)^{1/r}<\infty,

with the usual modifications for
r=\infty
.

Now suppose that
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is an
interval
. We say that a function
a\colon J\to X
is an
R^{s}(J;X)
-atom
, written as
a\in R^{s}_{\mathrm{at}}(J;X)
, if there exists a set
\mathcal{I}
of mutually disjoint subintervals of
J
and a set of vectors
(c_{I})_{I\in\mathcal{I}}
in
X
such that

a=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}c_{I}{{\bf 1}}_{I}\quad\text{and}\quad\Bigl(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\lVert c_{I}\rVert_{X}^{s}\Bigr)^{1/s}\leq 1.

Define
R^{s}(J;X)
by

R^{s}(J;X):=\Bigl\{f\in L^{\infty}(J;X):f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k},(\lambda_{k})_{k\geq 1}\in\ell^{1},(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subseteq R_{{\mathrm{at}}}^{s}(J;X)\Bigr\},

with norm

\left\|{f}\right\|_{R^{s}(J;X)}:=\inf\bigl\{\left\|(\lambda_{k})_{k\geq 1}\right\|_{\ell^{1}}:\text{$f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k}$ as above}\bigr\}.

Again we let
R^{\infty}(J;X)=L^{\infty}(J;X)
. Note that
R^{s}(J;X)
is a Banach space.

For a collection of disjoint intervals
\mathcal{J}
in
{\mathbb{R}}
and
r\in[1,\infty]
, the space
\ell^{r}(R^{s}(\mathcal{J};X))
consists of all
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};X)
such that

\left\|{f}\right\|_{\ell^{r}(R^{s}(\mathcal{J};X))}:=\Bigl(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\lVert f|_{J}\rVert_{R^{s}(J;X)}^{r}\Bigr)^{1/r}<\infty,

with the usual modification for
r=\infty
.

For
\alpha\in(0,1]
we define the space of

\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions

C^{\alpha}(J;X)
as the space of all
f\colon J\to X
with

\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}(J;X)}:=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(J;X)}+[f]_{C^{\alpha}(J;X)},

where

[f]_{C^{\alpha}(J;X)}:=\sup_{t_{1},t_{2}\in J,t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\frac{\|f(t_{1})-f(t_{2})\|_{X}}{|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\alpha}}.

It is standard to check that
C^{\alpha}(J;X)
is a Banach space.

The following embedding results can be found in
[
2
, Lemma 4.3]
and
[
15
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, let
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an interval, and
s\in[1,\infty)
. Then

(1)

R^{s}(J;X)\hookrightarrow V^{s}(J;X)
and for all
f\in R^{s}(J;X)
,

\|f\|_{V^{s}(J;X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{R^{s}(J;X)}.

(2)

V^{s}(J;X)\hookrightarrow R^{t}(J;X)
and for all
f\in V^{s}(J;X)
and
t>s
,

\|f\|_{R^{t}(J;X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{V^{s}(J;X)}.

(3)

C^{\frac{1}{s}}(J;X)\hookrightarrow V^{s}(J;X)
and for all
f\in C^{\frac{1}{s}}(J;X)
we have

\|f\|_{V^{s}(J;X)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(J;X)}+|J|^{\frac{1}{s}}[f]_{C^{1/s}(J;X)}.

The next lemma gives an interpolation inclusion for the
V^{s}
spaces. A result in the converse direction for both
R^{s}
and
V^{s}
can be found in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X_{0},X_{1}
be Banach spaces and
J\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\theta\in[0,1]
,
s_{0},s_{1}\geq 1
and set
\frac{1}{s_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{s_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{s_{1}}
. Then

[V^{s_{0}}(J;X_{0}),V^{s_{1}}(J;X_{1})]_{\theta}\hookrightarrow V^{s_{\theta}}(J;[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}).

Proof.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
s\in[1,\infty]
. Let
P:=(t_{0},\cdots,t_{N})
with
t_{0},\ldots,t_{N}\in J
such that
t_{0}<\cdots<t_{N}
.
Define the operator
\Phi_{P}:V^{s}(J;X)\to\ell^{s}(X)
as

\Phi_{P}f:=\left(f(t_{0}),f(t_{1})-f(t_{0}),\cdots,f(t_{N})-f(t_{N-1})\right).

Then

\left\|\Phi_{P}f\right\|_{\ell^{s}(X)}=\Bigl(\left\|f(t_{0})\right\|^{s}_{X}+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left\|f(t_{i+1})-f(t_{i})\right\|^{s}_{X}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{s}},

so taking the supremum over all such
P
, we have the following equivalence for the norm (
2.3
) of
V^{s}(J;X)
:

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{V^{s}(J;X)}\eqsim_{s}\sup_{P}\left\|\Phi_{P}f\right\|_{\ell^{s}(X)}.

(2.4)

Now note that
\Phi_{P}
is a bounded linear operator from
V^{s_{0}}(J;X_{0})
to
\ell^{s_{0}}(X_{0})
and from
V^{s_{1}}(J;X_{1})
to
\ell^{s_{1}}(X_{1})
. Therefore, by complex interpolation and
[
38
, Theorem 2.2.6]
,

\Phi_{P}
is a bounded linear operator from
[V^{s_{0}}(J;X_{0}),V^{s_{1}}(J;X_{1})]_{\theta}
to

[\ell^{s_{0}}(X_{0}),\ell^{s_{1}}(X_{1})]_{\theta}=\ell^{s_{\theta}}([X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}).

This combined with (
2.4
) yields

\left\|f\right\|_{V^{s_{\theta}}(J;[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta})}\lesssim\sup_{P}\left\|\Phi_{P}f\right\|_{\ell^{s_{\theta}}([X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta})}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{[V^{s_{0}}(J;X_{0}),V^{s_{1}}(J;X_{1})]_{\theta}},

finishing the proof.
∎

2.6.
Besov spaces and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces

Below we recall the definitions of the vector-valued Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces with
A_{\infty}
-weights.
For further details on Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, the reader is referred to
[
65
,
66
]
. For the unweighted case in the vector-valued setting, see
[
1
,
40
,
63
,
67
]
. The weighted scalar-valued case was studied extensively in
[
11
]
, while the weighted vector-valued case can be found in
[
53
]
.

Fix
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})
such that the Fourier transform
\widehat{\varphi}
of
\varphi
satisfies

0\leq\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\leq 1,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}},\qquad\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)=1\ \text{ if }\ |\xi|\leq 1,\qquad\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)=0\ \text{ if }\ |\xi|\geq\frac{3}{2}.

Let
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})
be defined by

\widehat{\varphi}_{0}=\widehat{\varphi},\qquad\widehat{\varphi}_{1}=\widehat{\varphi}(\cdot/2)-\widehat{\varphi},\qquad\widehat{\varphi}_{k}=\widehat{\varphi}_{1}(2^{-k+1}\cdot),\quad k\geq 2.

Given
p\in(1,\infty)
,
q\in[1,\infty]
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
w\in A_{\infty}
, for
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}};X)
we set

\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}=\Big\|\big(2^{ks}\varphi_{k}*f\big)_{k\geq 0}\Big\|_{\ell^{q}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X))},

\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}=\Big\|\big(2^{ks}\varphi_{k}*f\big)_{k\geq 0}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;\ell^{q}(X))}.

The spaces of tempered distributions for which the above norms are finite are denoted by
B_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
and the
F_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
, respectively, which are Banach spaces. Any other
\widetilde{\varphi}
satisfying the above leads to an equivalent norm.

3.
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness

In this section, we briefly introduce the basic properties of
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness, which play a key role in our main result. For details we refer to
[
39
, Chapter 8]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces and
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a Rademacher
sequence. We say that a family of operators
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X,Y)

is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all finite sequences
(T_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in
\mathcal{T}
and
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in
X
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n}T_{n}x_{n}\Big\|_{Y}\leq C\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}\Big\|_{X}.

The least admissible constant in the inequality is called the
\mathcal{R}
-bound, denoted by
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T})
.

In the above one can replace the
L^{1}
-moment with any
L^{p}
-moment for
p\in[1,\infty)
by the Kahane-Khintchine inequalities. By
[
39
, Propositions 8.1.21 and 8.1.22]
, if
\mathcal{T}
is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded in
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, then the closure of the convex hull of
\mathcal{T}
, denoted as
\overline{\conv}(\mathcal{T})
, is also
\mathcal{R}
-bounded with

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T})=\mathcal{R}(\overline{\conv}(\mathcal{T})).

(3.1)

In the next result, we show that for any interval
J\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
, functions in
R^{r}(J;\mathcal{L}(X,Y))
and
V^{r}(J;\mathcal{L}(X,Y))
have
\mathcal{R}
-bounded range under suitable conditions on
X
and
Y
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
,
Y
be Banach spaces and
\mathcal{J}
be a collection of disjoint intervals in
{\mathbb{R}}
. Suppose that
X
has cotype
q\in[2,\infty]
and
Y
has type
t\in[1,2]
. Let
\frac{1}{r}:=\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{q}
. Then every
f\in\ell^{r}(R^{r}(\mathcal{J};\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))
has
\mathcal{R}
-bounded range with

\mathcal{R}(\Ran(f))\lesssim_{X,Y,q,t}\|f\|_{\ell^{r}(R^{r}(\mathcal{J};\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))}.

Moreover, for
s\in[1,r)
we have that every
f\in\ell^{r}(V^{s}(\mathcal{J};\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))
has
\mathcal{R}
-bounded range with

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\Ran(f))

\displaystyle\lesssim_{X,Y,q,s,t}\|f\|_{\ell^{r}(V^{s}(\mathcal{J};\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))},

Proof.

If
r=\infty
, then
t=q=2
, so every uniformly bounded family is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded (see
[
39
, Theorem 8.1.3]
). Now suppose that
r\in[1,\infty)
. Let
J\in\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{I}
be a family of mutually disjoint subintervals of
J
. For
a\in R^{r}_{\mathrm{at}}(J;\mathcal{L}(X,Y))
write
a=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}c_{I}{{\bf 1}}_{I}
, where

\{c_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
. By
[
25
]
(see also
[
39
, Proposition 8.1.20]
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\Ran(a))=\mathcal{R}(\{c_{I}:{I\in\mathcal{I}}\})\lesssim\Bigl(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\|c_{I}\|^{r}\Bigr)^{1/r}\leq 1.

Now take
f\in\ell^{r}(R^{r}(\mathcal{J};\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))
. Then
f|_{J}\in R^{r}(J;\mathcal{L}(X,Y))
and thus
there exist sequences
(\lambda_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
and
(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subseteq R^{r}_{\mathrm{at}}(J;\mathcal{L}(X,Y))
such that
f|_{J}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k}
. From the triangle inequality one sees that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\Ran(f|_{J}))\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|\cdot\mathcal{R}(\Ran(a_{k}))\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|.

and hence, taking the infimum over
(\lambda_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and employing
[
25
]
once more on
\Ran(f)=\cup_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\Ran(f|_{J})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\Ran(f))\lesssim\Big(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\mathcal{R}\bigl(\Ran(f|_{J})\bigr)^{r}\Big)^{1/r}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{r}(R^{r}(\mathcal{J};\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))},

finishing the proof for
R^{r}
. The result for
V^{s}
now follows by Lemma
2.4
.
∎

Note that the result also holds if
\mathcal{J}
consists of only one interval. Moreover, that interval could be
{\mathbb{R}}
, in which case one obtains that functions in
V^{s}({\mathbb{R}};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))
for
s\in[1,r)
have
\mathcal{R}
-bounded range.

In
[
36
]
sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness are given in terms of Besov and Hölder regularity under the assumption that
X
has cotype
q
and
Y
has type
t
. The space used there is for instance
B^{d/r}_{r,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
and the latter embeds into the bounded continuous functions with values
{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
. Since the functions in
R^{r}
and
V^{s}
are not necessarily continuous, Proposition
3.2
does not follow from
[
36
]
.

In the converse direction for
d=1
, this raises the natural question whether
B^{1/r}_{r,1}({\mathbb{R}};E)
embeds into
R^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)
, which we will show in Lemma
3.3
below. As a consequence, the result of Proposition
3.2
implies the result in
[
36
]
for
d=1
. Our proof can be extended to
d\geq 2
, which we leave to the interested reader.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
E
be a Banach space and let
r\in[1,\infty)
. Then for
r>1
we have

B^{1/r}_{r,1}({\mathbb{R}};E)\hookrightarrow R^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)\cap L^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)\hookrightarrow V^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)\cap L^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)\hookrightarrow B^{1/r}_{r,\infty}({\mathbb{R}};E)

and for
r=1
we have

B^{1}_{1,1}({\mathbb{R}};E)\hookrightarrow V^{1}({\mathbb{R}};E)\cap L^{1}({\mathbb{R}};E)\hookrightarrow B^{1}_{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}};E)

Note that one does not have
B^{1}_{1,1}({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow R^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Indeed, functions in
R^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
can only take countably many values.

Proof.

Since trivially
B^{1/r}_{r,1}({\mathbb{R}};E)\hookrightarrow L^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)
, for the first embedding in the case
r>1
we only need to show
B^{1/r}_{r,1}({\mathbb{R}};E)\hookrightarrow R^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)
. We will use the atomic decomposition of Besov spaces (see
[
62
, Section 3]
). Indeed, any
f\in B^{1/r}_{r,1}({\mathbb{R}};E)
can be written as

f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mu_{j,k}a_{j,k},

(3.2)

where the
\mu_{j,k}
satisfy

\|f\|_{B^{1/r}_{r,1}({\mathbb{R}};E)}\eqsim_{r}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\mu_{j,k}|^{r}\Big)^{1/r},

and the atoms
a_{j,k}
are supported on a fixed dilate of the interval around
k
with length
2^{-j}
. Furthermore, they satisfy
\lVert a_{j,k}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};E)}\leq 1
and
\lVert a_{j,k}^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};E)}\leq 2^{j}
.
Note that

\lVert f+g\rVert_{R^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)}^{r}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{R^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)}^{r}+\lVert g\rVert_{R^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)}^{r}

for disjointly supported
f,g\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to E
. Therefore, since for fixed
j\geq 0
the supports of
a_{j,k}
have bounded overlap, we can estimate

\displaystyle\|f\|_{R^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)}\lesssim_{r}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|a_{j,k}\mu_{j,k}\|_{R^{r}({\mathbb{R}},E)}^{r}\big)}^{1/r}

Hence, it remains to show that
\|a_{j,k}\|_{R^{r}({\mathbb{R}},E)}\lesssim 1
independent of
j,k
. Denoting the support of
a_{j,k}
by
I
, this is a direct consequence of (see Lemma
2.4
)

\|a_{j,k}\|_{R^{r}({\mathbb{R}},E)}\lesssim\|a_{j,k}\|_{V^{1}({\mathbb{R}},E)}\lesssim\|a_{j,k}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;E)}+|I|[a_{j,k}]_{C^{1}(I;E)}\lesssim 1.

This finishes the proof of
B^{1/r}_{r,1}({\mathbb{R}};E)\hookrightarrow R^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)
. The proof of
B^{1}_{1,1}({\mathbb{R}};E)\hookrightarrow V^{1}({\mathbb{R}};E)
is identical.

The second embedding in the first claim follows from Lemma
2.4
. To prove the final embedding of both claims, take
r\in[1,\infty)
. Using the well-known difference norm of the Besov space (see
[
65
, Section 2.5.12]
) it suffices to show that for all
h>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\|f(x+h)-f(x)\|^{r}\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}x\leq h[f]_{V^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)}^{r}.

To check this, note that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\|f(x+h)-f(x)\|^{r}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{jh}^{(j+1)h}\|f(x+h)-f(x)\|^{r}\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{0}^{h}\|f(y+(j+1)h)-f(y+jh)\|^{r}\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{h}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|f(y+(j+1)h)-f(y+jh)\|^{r}\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{h}[f]_{V^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)}^{r}\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}y=h[f]_{V^{r}({\mathbb{R}};E)}^{r},

finishing the proof of the second embedding.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

From the fact that the Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces contain unbounded functions (see

\displaystyle B^{1/r}_{r,q}({\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle\not\hookrightarrow V^{r}({\mathbb{R}})\ \ \text{for all $r\in[1,\infty)$ and $q\in(1,\infty]$},

\displaystyle F^{1/r}_{r,q}({\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle\not\hookrightarrow V^{r}({\mathbb{R}})\ \ \text{for all $r\in(1,\infty)$ and $q\in[1,\infty]$}.

This proves the sharpness of the

The embeddings into

Indeed, one has that

4.
Weighted, vector-valued variational Carleson estimates

In this section, we prepare for our main result by establishing a weighted version of the vector-valued variational Carleson estimate. We start by extending the boundedness of the vector-valued Carleson operator from
[
34
]
to the weighted setting.
For
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}};X)
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
define

\mathcal{C}_{a}f(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{a}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi i\xi x}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The
Carleson maximal operator
is defined as

\mathcal{C}_{*}f(x):=\sup_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}\,\left\|\mathcal{C}_{a}f(x)\right\|_{X},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
, let
X
be a
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
Banach space and let
p\in(1,\infty)
. There exists a non-decreasing function
\phi_{X,p}:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for all
w\in A_{p}
and
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
we have

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{C}_{*}f\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}\leq\phi_{X,p}([w]_{A_{p}})\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}.

Proof.

To avoid strong measurability issues, let
(q_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a dense sequence in
{\mathbb{R}}
, and set
Q_{n}=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\}
for
n\geq 1
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{a}f=f-S_{[a,\infty)}f
, by density, monotone convergence and continuity, it suffices to prove that there exists a non-decreasing function
\phi_{X,p}\colon[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for all
n\geq 1
,
w\in A_{p}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}};X)
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sup_{a\in Q_{n}}\|S_{[a,\infty)}f\|_{X}\Bigr\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}\leq\phi_{X,p}([w]_{A_{p}})\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}.

(4.1)

Define
\mathcal{M}^{a}f:=x\mapsto e^{-2\pi ixa}f(x)
and note that

\left\|S_{[0,\infty)}\mathcal{M}^{a}f\right\|_{X}=\left\|\mathcal{M}^{a}S_{[a,\infty)}f\right\|_{X}=\left\|S_{[a,\infty)}f\right\|_{X}.

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the Hilbert transform. Since
i\mathcal{H}+I=2S_{[0,\infty)}
, it is equivalent to show that there exists a non-decreasing function
\phi_{X,p}:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for all
n\geq 1
,
w\in A_{p}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}};X)
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sup_{a\in Q_{n}}\left\|\mathcal{H}\mathcal{M}^{a}f\right\|_{X}\Bigr\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}\leq\phi_{X,p}([w]_{A_{p}})\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}.

(4.2)

Fix
n\geq 1
. To prove (
4.2
), we want to apply the sparse domination result in
[
49
]
. To do so, let
p_{0}\in(1,\infty)
and define
T:L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}};X)\to L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}};\ell^{\infty}(Q_{n};X))
by

Tf(x,a):=\mathcal{H}\mathcal{M}^{a}f(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},\,a\in Q_{n}.

If
w\equiv 1
,
[
34
, Theorem 1.1]
shows that (
4.1
) holds and therefore (
4.2
) also holds, which implies that
T
is well-defined and bounded by density. For
f\in L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}};X)
we furthermore define

M^{\#}_{T}f(x):=\sup_{I\ni x}\esssup_{x_{1},x_{2}\in I}\left\|T(f{{\bf 1}}_{{\mathbb{R}}\backslash 5I})(x_{1})-T(f{{\bf 1}}_{{\mathbb{R}}\backslash 5I})(x_{2})\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(Q_{n};X)},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

where the supremum is taken over all intervals
I
containing
x
.

Fix
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and an interval
I
containing
x
. Take
x_{1},x_{2}\in I
and denote the length of
I
by
\varepsilon
. Note that for
y\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash 5I
,

|x-y|\eqsim|x_{1}-y|\eqsim|x_{2}-y|\gtrsim 2\varepsilon.

Then by (
2.1
), we have for
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}};X)
that

\displaystyle\bigl\|T(f{{\bf 1}}_{{\mathbb{R}}\backslash 5I})(x_{1})

\displaystyle-T(f{{\bf 1}}_{{\mathbb{R}}\backslash 5I})(x_{2})\bigr\|_{\ell^{\infty}(Q_{n};X)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{a\in Q_{n}}\frac{1}{\pi}\,\Bigl\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\backslash 5I}\Bigl(\frac{1}{x_{1}-y}-\frac{1}{x_{2}-y}\Bigr)e^{-2\pi iya}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigr\|_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\backslash 5I}\frac{|x_{1}-x_{2}|}{|x_{1}-y||x_{2}-y|}\left\|f(y)\right\|_{X}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\backslash 5I}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}\left\|f(y)\right\|_{X}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k}\varepsilon<|x-y|\leq 2^{k+1}\varepsilon}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}\left\|f(y)\right\|_{X}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\frac{2}{2^{k+1}\varepsilon}\int_{|x-y|\leq 2^{k+1}\varepsilon}\left\|f(y)\right\|_{X}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\cdot M\bigl(\left\|f\right\|_{X}\bigr)(x)\lesssim M\bigl(\left\|f\right\|_{X}\bigr)(x),

where
M
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator. Therefore we obtain

M^{\#}_{T}f(x)\lesssim M(\left\|f\right\|_{X})(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Since
M
is weak
L^{1}
-bounded by
[
38
, Theorem 2.3.2]
,
M^{\#}_{T}
is weak
L^{1}
-bounded as well.
We have now checked all assumptions of
[
49
, Theorem 3.2]
with
p_{1}=p_{0}
,
p_{2}=1
,
r=1
and
(S,d,\mu)
is
{\mathbb{R}}
with the Euclidean metric and the Lebesgue measure. By
[
49
, Corollary 1.2]
, we conclude for all
p\in(p_{0},\infty)
,
w\in A_{{p}/{p_{0}}}
and
f\in{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sup_{{a\in Q_{n}}}\bigl\|\mathcal{H}\mathcal{M}^{a}f\bigr\|_{X}\Bigr\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}\lesssim{\frac{p^{2}}{p-p_{0}}}\cdot C_{X,p_{0}}\cdot[w]_{A_{{{p}/{p_{0}}}}}^{\max\{\frac{1}{p-p_{0}},1\}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)},

(4.3)

where the factor
{\frac{p^{2}}{p-p_{0}}}
arises from the proof of
[
49
, Proposition 4.1]
and

C_{X,p_{0}}
the constant in (
4.2
) for the case
w\equiv 1
, i.e. the constant from
[
34
, Theorem 1.1]
. By inspection of the proof, it is clear that
C_{X,p_{0}}\to\infty
as
p_{0}\downarrow 1
.

Now, for fixed
w\in A_{p}
, let
p_{0}\in(1,p)
be such that
\frac{p}{p_{0}}=p-\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon>0
satisfying the conditions in Lemma
2.1
. Combining this with (
4.3
), we conclude that there is a non-decreasing function
\phi_{X,p}\colon[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that (
4.2
) holds, finishing the proof.
∎

We will use Proposition
4.1
to extend the boundedness of the vector-valued variational Carleson operator from
[
4
]
to the weighted setting. Note that we only rely on Proposition
4.1
(and thus the main result of
[
34
]
) in the proof. Hence, we also provide an alternative way to prove the unweighted boundedness of the vector-valued variational Carleson operator, originally proven in

.

For

and
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}};X)
we define the
variational Carleson operator
as

\mathcal{C}_{*}^{q}f(x):=\left[a\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{a}f(x)\right]_{V^{q}({\mathbb{R}};X)}=\underset{\mathcal{I}}{\sup}\Big(\underset{I\in\mathcal{I}}{\sum}\|S_{I}f(x)\|_{X}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

where the supremum is taken over all finite families of disjoint intervals
\mathcal{I}
in
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X
be a
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
Banach space for
\theta\in(0,1]
. Let
q\in(2/{\theta},\infty)
and
p\in(q^{\prime},\infty)
. Then there exists a non-decreasing function
\phi_{X,p,q}:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for all
w\in A_{{p}/{q^{\prime}}}
and
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}_{*}^{q}f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}\leq\phi_{X,p,q}([w]_{A_{{p}/{q^{\prime}}}})\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}.

(4.4)

In case
X={\mathbb{C}}
(and thus
\theta=1
) such results can be found in
[
20
,
21
,
47
,
56
]
.

Proof.

By density it suffices to prove the result for
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}};X)
. Let
(Q_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
J_{n}:=[-n,n]\cap Q_{n}
and

\mathcal{C}_{*,n}^{q}f(x):=\left[a\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{a}f(x)\right]_{V^{q}(J_{n};X)},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

By the monotone convergence theorem and continuity, it suffices to prove the statement for
\mathcal{C}_{*,n}^{q}f
.

Fix
u\in(2,q)
. We start by proving an estimate on a Hilbert space
H
. Note that
H
is isomorphic to
L^{2}
, so the
u^{\prime}
-concavification
H^{u^{\prime}}
is a
\UMD
Banach function space since
L^{2/{u^{\prime}}}
is a
\UMD
Banach function space. For any
p_{0}>u^{\prime}
, by
[
3
, Theorem 5.2]
and Proposition
4.1
, there exists a non-decreasing function
\phi_{H,p_{0},u}:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for all
v\in A_{{p_{0}}/{u^{\prime}}}
and
f\in L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R},v;H)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}_{*,n}^{u}f\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R},v)}\leq\phi_{H,p_{0},u}([v]_{A_{{p_{0}}/{u^{\prime}}}})\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R},v;H)},

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\left\|(x,a)\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{a}f(x)\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}},v;V^{u}(J_{n};H))}&\leq\left\|\left\|a\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{a}f(\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(J_{n};H)}\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}},v)}\\
&\qquad+\left\|\left[a\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{a}f(\cdot)\right]_{V^{u}(J_{n};H)}\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R},v)}\\
&\leq\phi_{H,p_{0},u}([v]_{A_{{p_{0}}/{u^{\prime}}}})\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R},v;H)}.\end{array}

Let
X_{0}
be a
\UMD
Banach space and let
H
be a Hilbert space such that
X:=[X_{0},H]_{\theta}
. Set
\theta_{0}:=\tfrac{u}{q}
.
Then by reiteration, there exists an
\eta\in(0,1)
small enough such that
X_{1}:=[X_{0},H]_{\eta}
and

X=[X_{0},H]_{\theta}=[X_{1},H]_{\theta_{0}}.

Proposition
4.1
yields for all
p_{1}\in(1,\infty)
, there exists a non-decreasing function
\phi_{X_{1},p_{1}}:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for
v\in A_{p_{1}}
,
f\in L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}},v;X_{1})
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\left\|(x,a)\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{a}f(x)\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R},v;\ell^{\infty}(J_{n};X_{1}))}\leq\phi_{X_{1},p_{1}}([v]_{A_{p_{1}}})\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}},v;X_{1})}.\end{array}

Fix
w\in A_{{p/q^{\prime}}}
, choose
p_{0}:=\tfrac{p}{q^{\prime}}u^{\prime}>u^{\prime}
and

p_{1}:=\tfrac{p}{q^{\prime}}>1.

Then

\tfrac{1-\theta_{0}}{p_{1}}+\tfrac{\theta_{0}}{p_{0}}=\tfrac{1}{p},\quad\text{and}\quad\tfrac{\theta_{0}}{u}=\tfrac{1}{q},

so by Lemma
2.5
and
[
38

we have

\displaystyle[L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R},w;\ell^{\infty}(J_{n};X_{1})),L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R},w;V^{u}(J_{n};H))]_{\theta_{0}}\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w;V^{q}(J_{n};X)).

Hence, using complex interpolation between (
4
) and (
4
), we conclude that there is a non-decreasing function
\phi_{X,p,q}:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for all
f\in{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}
,

\left\|{(x,a)\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{a}f(x)}\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;V^{q}(J_{n};X))}\leq\phi_{X,p,q}([w]_{A_{p/q^{\prime}}})\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}

finishing the proof.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.2
, we obtain Theorem
1.5
. That is, we also can conclude that for any family of disjoint intervals
\mathcal{I}
in
{\mathbb{R}}
and
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
,

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\|S_{I}f\|^{q}_{X}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}\leq\phi_{X,p,q}([w]_{A_{p/q^{\prime}}})\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}.

(4.9)

Conversely, one could ask what geometric properties of
X
are implied by (
4.9
). In the next proposition, we show that it implies cotype
q
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
w
be a weight. Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
q\in[2,\infty)
. Suppose that
there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
and all families of disjoint intervals
\mathcal{I}
in
{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\|S_{I}f\|^{q}_{X}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}.

Then
X
has cotype
q
.

Proof.

Take a finite sequence
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in
X
. Let
(e_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be the trigonometric system on
[0,1]
and extend it periodically to
{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of complex Rademacher (or Steinhaus) random variables on
\Omega
(see
[
39
]
). Define
f:=\varphi\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{N}e_{3n}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}
, where
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})
is nonzero and
\mathrm{supp}\,(\widehat{\varphi})\subseteq[0,1]
. Let
I_{n}=[3n-1,3n+1]
. Then
S_{I_{n}}f=\varphi\cdot e_{3n}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}
pointwise in
\Omega
and thus

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|S_{I_{n}}f\|^{q}_{X}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}=\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|x_{n}\|^{q}_{X}\Big)^{1/q}.

Therefore, applying (
4.9
) and taking
p
-th moments on both sides we find that

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|x_{n}\|^{q}_{X}\Big)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl({\mathbb{E}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl({\mathbb{E}}\,\Big\|\varphi\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{N}e_{3n}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl({\mathbb{E}}\,\Big\|\varphi\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w)}{\mathbb{E}}\,\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}\Big\|_{X},

where we used that for fixed
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
(\varepsilon_{n}e_{3n}(t))_{n=1}^{N}
has the same distribution as
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
due to the unimodular invariance. Therefore,
X
has cotype
q
.
∎

The
\LPR_{p}
property introduced in

asserts that for any

and any family of disjoint intervals
\mathcal{I}
, a Rademacher sequence

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\,\Big\|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\varepsilon_{I}S_{I}f\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)}\leq C_{X,p}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)}.

(4.10)

]

) implies type

. In contrast, our vector-valued Littlewood–Paley–Rubio de Francia type estimate (

.

for any family of disjoint intervals
\mathcal{I}
in
{\mathbb{R}}
. In a Banach function space, the result of

.
Indeed, if
X=[X_{0},L^{2}(S)]_{\theta}
with

a UMD Banach function space over a measure space

, then

is
q
-concave and

-convex for

. Furthermore, the

-concavification of

By self-improvement of the
\UMD

X_{0}
(see
[
60
, Theorem 4]
), one can replace
L^{1}(S)
by
L^{1+\varepsilon}(S)
for some
\varepsilon>0
and therefore
X^{q^{\prime}}
has
\UMD
. Hence, the assumptions of Theorem
1.5
imply the assumptions of
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
. Moreover, by
q
-concavity we note that

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\|S_{I}f\|^{q}_{X}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\Big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}|S_{I}f|^{q}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)},

so the conclusion of
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
implies the conclusion of Theorem
1.5
. We refer to
[
4
, Section 5]
for a comparison of Theorem
4.2
and the Banach function space case in
[
3
, Theorem 5.2]
.

5.
Main Result

5.1.
Fourier multiplier on
L^{p}
-spaces

Having shown the weighted, vector-valued variational Carleson estimate, and as a consequence the required Rubio de Francia type inequality, we now turn to our first main result on Fourier multipliers.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
. Let
X
be a
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
Banach space and let
Y
be a Banach space. Let
s\in[1,\frac{2}{2-\theta})
,
p\in(s,\infty)
and let
m\in V^{s}({\mathbb{R}};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))
. There exists a non-decreasing function
\phi_{X,Y,p,s}\colon[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for all
w\in A_{p/s}
,

\|T_{m}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X),L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y))}\leq\phi_{X,p,s}([w]_{A_{p/s}})\cdot\|m\|_{V^{s}({\mathbb{R}};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}.

Note that the range of
m
as in Theorem
5.1
is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded by Proposition
3.2
. However, we will not use this fact in the proof.

Proof.

We first prove the result for
m\in R^{s}({\mathbb{R}};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))
. Write

m=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k},\quad a_{k}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}c_{I}{{\bf 1}}_{I},

where for
c_{I}\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
,
\mathcal{I}_{k}
a family of mutually disjoint subintervals
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}\|c_{I}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}^{s}\leq 1
and

\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}|\lambda_{k}|\leq\|m\|_{R^{s}({\mathbb{R}};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}.

Then we have for
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
that

\displaystyle\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w;Y)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}c_{I}S_{I}f\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w;Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|\Big\|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}c_{I}S_{I}f\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w;Y)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|m\|_{R^{s}({\mathbb{R}};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}\cdot\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Big\|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}c_{I}S_{I}f\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w;Y)}

For the latter we can estimate for fixed
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
by Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}c_{I}S_{I}f\Big\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}\|c_{I}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\|S_{I}f\|_{X}\leq\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}\|S_{I}f\|_{X}^{s^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/s^{\prime}}.

Therefore, applying Theorem
1.5
we can conclude that

\displaystyle\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w;Y)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|m\|_{R^{s}({\mathbb{R}};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}\cdot\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}\|S_{I}f\|_{X}^{s^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/s^{\prime}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w)}

\displaystyle\leq\|m\|_{R^{s}({\mathbb{R}};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}\cdot\phi_{X,p,s}([w]_{A_{{p/s}}})\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w;X)},

concluding the proof for
R^{s}
.

To obtain the result for
V^{s}
, note that by Lemma
2.1
we can choose
s_{1}\in(s,\frac{2}{2-\theta}\wedge p)
depending on
p,s
and
[w]_{A_{{p/s}}}
such that
w\in A_{{p/s_{1}}}
and
[w]_{A_{{p/s_{1}}}}\lesssim_{p,s}[w]_{A_{{p/s}}}.
Since
V^{s}\hookrightarrow R^{s_{1}}
by Lemma
2.4
, the result follows from the previous case applied with
s_{1}
instead of
s
.
∎

Combining Theorem
5.1
with the Littlewood–Paley theorem we obtain the following result for multipliers with some decay at zero and infinity (measured using an
\ell^{r}
-norm over
\Delta
). This is a quantified version of Theorem
1.2
. Note that the second statement in Theorem
1.2
follows by duality.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
,
t\in(1,2]
,
q\in[2,\infty)
and set
\frac{1}{r}:=\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{q}
. Let
X
be a
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
Banach space with cotype
q
and
Y
be a
\UMD
Banach space with type
t
.
Let
s\in[1,\frac{2}{2-\theta})
and suppose that
m\in\ell^{r}(\dot{V}^{s}(\Delta;\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))
has
\mathcal{R}
-bounded range.
Then for all
p\in(s,\infty)
, there exists a non-decreasing function
\phi_{X,Y,p,q,s,t}\colon[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for all
w\in A_{p/s}
,

\displaystyle\|T_{m}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X),L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y))}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{X,Y,p,q,s,t}([w]_{A_{p/s}})\big(\|m\|_{\ell^{r}(\dot{V}^{s}(\Delta;\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))}+\mathcal{R}(\Ran(m))\big).

Note that the assumptions in Theorem
5.2
imply that
r>s
. Indeed, by complex interpolation we know that
X
has cotype
q=\frac{2}{\theta}
(see
[
39
, Proposition 7.1.3]
) and therefore
\frac{1}{r}\leq 1-\frac{\theta}{2}<\frac{1}{s}
.

Proof.

By rescaled Rubio de Francia extrapolation (see
[
17
, Corollary 3.14]
) it suffices to consider
p=2
. We will first prove the result for
V^{s}
instead of
\dot{V}^{s}
.

Take
m\in\ell^{r}(V^{s}(\Delta;\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))
, for
J\in\Delta
define
k_{J}:=\|{{\bf 1}}_{J}m\|_{V^{s}(J;\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}
and set

C_{m}:=\|(k_{J})_{J}\|_{\ell^{r}}=\|m\|_{\ell^{r}(V^{s}(\Delta;\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))}.

Take
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
such that
\widehat{f}
has compact support in
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
, so that below the sum over
\Delta
is finite.

By Proposition
2.2
and type
t
of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y)
we can write

\displaystyle\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{Y}([w]_{A_{2}})\mathbb{E}\,\Big\|\sum_{J\in\Delta}\varepsilon_{J}T_{{{\bf 1}}_{J}m}S_{J}f\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},w;Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{Y,t}([w]_{A_{2}})\Big(\sum_{J\in\Delta}\|T_{{{\bf 1}}_{J}m}S_{J}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},w;Y)}^{t}\Big)^{1/t}

Using Theorem
5.1
we deduce

\|T_{{{\bf 1}}_{J}m}S_{J}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},w;Y)}\leq\phi_{X,Y,s}([w]_{A_{2/s}})\cdot k_{J}\cdot\|S_{J}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}.

Therefore, using Hölder’s inequality,
A_{2/s}\subseteq A_{2}
and cotype
q
of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{X,Y,s,t}([w]_{A_{2/s}})\Big(\sum_{J\in\Delta}k_{J}^{t}\cdot\|S_{J}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}^{t}\Big)^{1/t}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{X,Y,s,t}([w]_{A_{2/s}})\cdot\|(k_{J})_{J}\|_{\ell^{r}}\cdot\Big(\sum_{J\in\Delta}\|S_{J}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}^{q}\Big)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{X,Y,q,s,t}([w]_{A_{2/s}})\cdot C_{m}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\,\Big\|\sum_{J\in\Delta}\varepsilon_{J}S_{J}f\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{X,Y,q,s,t}([w]_{A_{2/s}})\cdot C_{m}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}.

By density (see
[
23
, Lemma 3.3]
), this finishes the proof for
V^{s}
.

Now take
m\in\ell^{r}(\dot{V}^{s}(\Delta;\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))
and define

\widetilde{m}=\sum_{J\in\Delta}{{\bf 1}}_{J}(m-m_{J}),

where
m_{J}=|J|^{-1}\int_{J}m(y)\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}y
for
J\in\Delta
. Then
[\widetilde{m}]_{V^{s}(J;{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))}=[m]_{V^{s}(J;{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))}
for all
J\in\Delta
and

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(J;{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|J|}\int_{J}\|x\mapsto m(x)-m(y)\|_{L^{\infty}(J;{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))}\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq[m]_{V^{s}(J;{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))}.

Therefore,
\widetilde{m}\in\ell^{r}(V^{s}(\Delta;\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))

and

\|T_{\widetilde{m}}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y)}\leq\phi_{X,Y,q,s,t}([w]_{A_{2/s}})\cdot\|m\|_{\ell^{r}(\dot{V}^{s}(\Delta;\mathcal{L}(X,Y)))}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}.

To complete the proof it suffices to show boundedness of
T_{\bar{m}}
with
\bar{m}:=\sum_{J\in\Delta}{{\bf 1}}_{J}m_{J}
. Since
\Ran(m)
is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded, by (
3.1
) we know that

\overline{\conv}(\Ran(m))
is also
\mathcal{R}
-bounded. Since
m_{J}\in\overline{\conv}(\Ran(m))
(
[
38
, Proposition 1.2.12]
),
by Proposition
2.2
we have that

\displaystyle\|T_{\bar{m}}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{Y}([w]_{A_{2}})\cdot\mathbb{E}\,\Bigl\|\sum_{J\in\Delta}\varepsilon_{J}m_{J}S_{J}f\Bigr\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{Y}([w]_{A_{2}})\cdot\mathcal{R}(\Ran(m))\cdot\mathbb{E}\,\Bigl\|\sum_{J\in\Delta}\varepsilon_{J}S_{J}f\Bigr\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{X,Y}([w]_{A_{2}})\cdot\mathcal{R}(\Ran(m))\cdot\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)},

finishing the proof.
∎

5.2.
Fourier multipliers on Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces

If
L^{p}
is replaced by a Besov space or Triebel–Lizorkin space, our multiplier theorem simplifies. Indeed, type, cotype and
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness do not play any role anymore.
Moreover, no decay of
m
is required. We state the result for the inhomogeneous Besov space
B^{\alpha}_{p,q}
. The reader is referred to the text below the theorem for the necessary changes in the homogeneous setting. We define

\displaystyle J_{0}

\displaystyle:=(-1,1),

\displaystyle J_{n}

\displaystyle:=\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}:2^{n-1}\leq|\xi|<2^{n}\},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Theorem 5.3

(Fourier multipliers on Besov spaces)
.

Fix
\theta\in(0,1]
. Let
X
be a
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
Banach space and
Y
be a
\UMD
Banach space. Let
s\in[1,\frac{2}{2-\theta})
and assume that
m\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
satisfies

C_{m}:=\sup_{n\geq 0}\|m\|_{V^{s}(J_{n};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}<\infty.

Let
p\in(s,\infty)
and
q\in[1,\infty]
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then there exists a non-decreasing function
\phi_{X,Y,p,q,s}\colon[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for all
w\in A_{p/s}
,

\displaystyle\|T_{m}\|_{\mathcal{L}(B^{\alpha}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w;X),B^{\alpha}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y))}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{X,Y,p,q,s}([w]_{A_{p/s}})\cdot C_{m}.

Proof.

Let
f\in B^{\alpha}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
. Write
f_{n}=\varphi_{n}*f
and
m_{n}=\widehat{\varphi}_{n}m
, where
(\varphi_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is an inhomogeneous Littlewood–Paley sequence as in
[
40
, Chapter 14]
. Then by the properties of the
(\varphi_{n})_{n\geq 0}
we can write

\displaystyle\|T_{m}f\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y))}\leq\sum_{j=-1}^{1}\Big\|\Big(2^{\alpha k}\|T_{m_{n+j}}f_{n}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y)}\Big)_{n\geq 0}\Big\|_{\ell^{q}},

where we set
m_{-1}=0
.
Therefore, it suffices to show that for any
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|T_{m_{n}}g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y)}\leq\phi_{X,Y,p,s}([w]_{A_{p/s}})\cdot C_{m}\|g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)},\ \ \ g\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X).

By Theorem
5.1
and the support conditions of
\widehat{\varphi}_{n}
, it suffices to estimate
\|m_{n}\|_{V^{s}(\widetilde{J}_{n};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))}
, where
\widetilde{J}_{n}:=J_{n}\cup J_{n+1}
, and we note that
\mathrm{supp}\,(\widehat{\varphi}_{n})\subseteq\widetilde{J}_{n}
.
Since
|\widehat{\varphi}_{n}|\leq 1
, it is clear that

\|m_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{J}_{n};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}\leq\|m\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}\leq C_{m}.

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle[m_{n}]_{V^{s}(\widetilde{J}_{n};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))}\leq\|\widehat{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{J}_{n})}\|m\|_{V^{s}(\widetilde{J}_{n};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))}+\|\widehat{\varphi}_{n}\|_{V^{s}(\widetilde{J}_{n})}\|m\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{J}_{n};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))}\lesssim C_{m},

finishing the proof.
∎

A similar result holds for the homogeneous Besov space if one uses
\Delta
instead of
(J_{n})_{n\geq 0}
. A result related to Theorem
5.3
under a Fourier type
s
condition and using
m\in B^{1/s}_{s,1}(J;{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
was obtained in
[
27
]
. Note that the space
V^{s}(J;{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
is larger, as shown in Lemma
3.3
. It, in particular, contains non-continuous multipliers.

Next, we move to the case of Triebel–Lizorkin spaces. Multiplier theorems in that setting can be found in
[
65
]
for the scalar case, and in
[
10
]
for the vector-valued setting. We obtain our result from the Besov space case using Rubio de Francia extrapolation.

Theorem 5.4

(Fourier multipliers on Triebel–Lizorkin spaces)
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
. Let
X
be a
\theta
-intermediate
\UMD
Banach space and
Y
be a
\UMD
Banach space. Let
s\in[1,\frac{2}{2-\theta})
and assume that
m\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
satisfies

C_{m}:=\sup_{n\geq 0}\|m\|_{V^{s}(J_{n};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))}<\infty.

Let
p,q\in(s,\infty)
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then there exists a non-decreasing function
\phi_{X,Y,p,q,s}\colon[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for all
w\in A_{p/s}
,

\displaystyle\|T_{m}\|_{\mathcal{L}(F^{\alpha}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w;X),F^{\alpha}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w;Y))}\leq\phi_{X,Y,p,q,s}([w]_{A_{p/s}})\cdot C_{m}.

Proof.

In the notation of the proof of Theorem
5.3
, we have to show that

\displaystyle\|(\varphi_{n}*T_{m}f)_{n\geq 0}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;\ell^{q}(Y))}\leq\phi_{X,Y,p,q,s}([w]_{A_{p/s}})\cdot C_{m}\|(\varphi_{n}*f)_{n\geq 0}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;\ell^{q}(X))}.

Since
q\in(s,\infty)
by rescaled Rubio de Francia extrapolation (see
[
17
, Corollary 3.14]
), it suffices to consider
p=q
. This case follows directly from Theorem
5.3
.
∎

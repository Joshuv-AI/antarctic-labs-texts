Abstract
. We present a probabilistic interpretation of several functional isoperimetric inequalities within the class of p p -concave functions, building on random models for such functions introduced by P. Pivovarov and J. Rebollo-Bueno. First, we establish a stochastic isoperimetric inequality for a functional extension of the classical quermassintegrals, which yields a Sobolev-type inequality in this random setting as a particular case. Motivated by the latter, we further show that Zhang’s affine Sobolev inequality holds in expectation when dealing with these random models of p p -concave functions. Finally, we confirm that our results recover both their geometric analogues and deterministic counterparts. As a consequence of the latter, we establish a generalization of Zhang’s affine Sobolev inequality restricted to p p -concave functions in the context of convex measures.

1.
Introduction

A fundamental isoperimetric principle in convex geometry states that, among all convex bodies (compact convex sets with non-empty interior) of a given volume, Euclidean balls have the smallest
i
-th quermassintegral for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. More precisely, if
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body,

(1.1)

W_{i}(K)\geq W_{i}(B_{K}),

where
W_{i}(\cdot)
denotes the
i
-th quermassintegral (see Section
2
for a precise definition) and
B_{K}
is an Euclidean ball with the same volume as
K
. When
i=1
, (
1.1
) is nothing but the classical isoperimetric inequaliy for convex bodies. For context and background the reader may check the excellent books by R. Gardner
[
16
]
, P. Gruber
[
19
]
and R. Schneider
[
41
]
.

Inspired by the work of H. Groemer
[
18
]
on the expected volume of random polytopes, the isoperimetric-type inequality (
1.1
) was shown to hold, by M. Hartzoulaki and G. Paouris in
[
22
]
, also in expectation when dealing with random convex sets. In a more detailed manner, for any collection of independent random vectors
\{X_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
uniformly distributed on
K
, they proved that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[W_{i}\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{X_{1},\dots,X_{N}\}\bigr)\Bigl]\geq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[W_{i}\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{X^{*}_{1},\dots,X^{*}_{N}\}\bigr)\Bigl]

for every
i\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
, where
\{X_{k}^{*}\}_{k=1}^{N}
are independent random vectors uniformly distributed on
B_{K}
. Note that (
1.2
) recovers (
1.1
) as
N\to+\infty
.

A systematic study of stochastic dominance phenomena in isoperimetric-type inequalities was initiated in
[
31
]
(and further developed in
[
32
,
13
,
33
,
1
,
14
]
) where, among other findings, it was proven that convexity (or concavity) properties of certain functionals lead to an stochastic dominance in the associated inequalities. More recently, a similar program focused on stochastic analogues of functional inequalities in the realm
p
-concave functions was started by P. Pivovarov and J. Rebollo-Bueno in
[
36
,
37
]
. The aim of this paper is to continue their program by presenting several stochastic analogues of functional isoperimetric inequalities for
p
-concave functions.

In this regard, we will consider a generalization of the classical quermassintegrals to the setting of
p
-concave functions, independently introduced by S. Bobkov, A. Colesanti, and I. Fragalà
[
4
]
, and by V. Milman and L. Rotem
[
29
]
(see Section
2
). Among other related results, the following functional extension of (
1.1
) was proved in
[
4
,
29
]
.

Theorem A
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an integrable
p
-concave function, with
p\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
. Then, for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
,

(1.3)

W_{i}(f)\geq W_{i}(f^{*}),

where
f^{*}
denotes the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
f
.

In light of (
1.2
), it is natural to wonder about a stochastic analogue of (
1.3
). Our first main result provides such an analogue. To state it precisely, we first introduce the stochastic framework developed by P. Pivovarov and J. Rebollo-Bueno. Let
N\geq n+1
. Given an integrable
p
-concave function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
p\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, and
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
independent random vectors uniformly distributed w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure on the hypograph of
f
, i.e., uniformly sampled on

\text{hyp}(f)=\{(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}:f(x)\geq z\}

(for the sake of simplicity, in the following we will simply say distributed w.r.t.
f
). The so-called
stochastic model
of
f
is build upon the random sets
E_{N,p},H_{N,p}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by

(1.4)

E_{N,p}=\begin{cases}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{R_{Z_{1}^{p}}(X_{1}),\dots,R_{Z_{N}^{p}}(X_{N})\}&\mathrm{if}\quad p<0,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{R_{-\log Z_{1}}(X_{1}),\dots,R_{-\log Z_{N}}(X_{N})\}&\mathrm{if}\quad p=0\end{cases}

and

(1.5)

H_{N,p}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{\widetilde{R}_{Z_{1}^{p}}(X_{1}),\dots,\widetilde{R}_{Z_{N}^{p}}(X_{N})\}

if
p>0
, where for any given
(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
the sets
R_{z}(x)
and
\widetilde{R}_{z}(x)
are defined as

R_{z}(x)=\{(x,r):r\geq z\}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\widetilde{R}_{z}(x)=\{(x,z):r\leq z\}.

Specifically, the stochastic model of
f
constructed from the random vectors
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
, denoted as
\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}
, is the
p
-concave function defined via epigraphs and hypographs as follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{epi}\bigl((\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})})^{p}\bigr)=E_{N,p}\quad\mathrm{if}\quad p<0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{epi}(-\log\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})})=E_{N,p}\quad\mathrm{if}\quad p=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{hyp}\bigl((\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})})^{p}\bigr)=H_{N,p}\quad\mathrm{if}\quad p>0.

Equivalently, defining

(1.6)

e_{N,p}=\begin{cases}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{(X_{1},Z_{1}^{p}),\dots,(X_{N},Z_{N}^{p})\}&\mathrm{if}\quad p<0,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{(X_{1},-\log Z_{1}),\dots,(X_{N},-\log Z_{N})\}&\mathrm{if}\quad p=0,\end{cases}

and

(1.7)

h_{N,p}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{(X_{1},Z_{1}^{p}),\dots,(X_{N},Z_{N}^{p})\}

when
p>0
, we have that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}(x)=\begin{cases}\inf\{z^{1/p}:(x,z)\in e_{N,p}\},&\mathrm{if}\quad p<0\\
\sup\{e^{z}:(x,z)\in e_{N,p}\},&\mathrm{if}\quad p=0\\
\sup\{z^{1/p}:(x,z)\in h_{N,p}\}&\mathrm{if}\quad p>0.\end{cases}

Our first main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an integrable
p
-concave function and
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors distributed w.r.t.
f
. Then, for every
i\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}

\mathbb{E}\left[W_{i}\Bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\Bigr)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[W_{i}\Bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X^{*}_{k},Z^{*}_{k})}\Bigr)\right],

where
\Phi^{N}_{(\cdot,\cdot)}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a stochastic model and
\{(X_{k}^{*},Z_{k}^{*})\}_{k=1}^{N}
are independent random vectors distributed w.r.t. the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
f
.

The case of
i=1
in Theorem
A
is precisely the Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for functions of bounded variation (see e.g.
[
3
, Chapter 3]
for background), which is known to hold without the
p
-concavity assumption. In more detail, under the hypotheses of Theorem
A
, we have that

(1.9)

\mathrm{Per}(f)\geq\mathrm{Per}(f^{*}),

where
\mathrm{Per}(f)=nW_{1}(f)
. Thus, the case of
i=1
in Theorem
1.1
yields a stochastic form of the classical Sobolev inequality in the class of
p
-concave functions.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an integrable
p
-concave function and
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors distributed w.r.t.
f
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Per}\Bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\Bigr)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Per}\Bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X^{*}_{k},Z^{*}_{k})}\Bigr)\right],

where
\Phi^{N}_{(\cdot,\cdot)}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a stochastic model and
\{(X_{k}^{*},Z_{k}^{*})\}_{k=1}^{N}
are independent random vectors distributed w.r.t. the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
f
.

An affine strengthening of (
1.9
) is Zhang’s affine Sobolev inequality, established by G. Zhang in
[
44
]
for
C^{1}
functions and later extended to functions of bounded variation by T. Wang in
[
43
]
. G. Zhang proved that this inequality is equivalent to the Petty projection inequality
[
35
]
for convex bodies:

(1.10)

|\Pi^{\circ}K|\leq|\Pi^{\circ}B_{K}|,

where
\Pi^{\circ}
denotes the polar projection body operator and
|\cdot|
stands for the usual Lebesgue measure (or
volume
). Very recently, G. Paouris, P. Pivovarov and K. Tatarko
[
34
]
have shown that Petty’s inequality (
1.10
) also holds in a stochastic form. This result, combined with Corollary
1.1
, motivates our second main objective: to establish that, within the class of
p
-concave functions, Zhang’s affine Sobolev inequality holds in expectation for the stochastic models defined in (
1.8
).

To achieve this goal, we employ the notion of functional polar projection bodies, which originates from ideas of E. Lutwak, D. Yang and G. Zhang
[
27
,
26
]
(see also
[
25
,
21
,
20
]
). This concept was also formally introduced in
[
2
]
following
[
23
]
(See Section
2
for a precise definition). Within this framework, Zhang’s affine Sobolev inequality takes the following form. Note that, as aforementioned, this inequality is known to hold without the
p
-concavity assumption.

Theorem B
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an integrable
p
-concave function. Then

|\Pi^{\circ}f|\leq|\Pi^{\circ}f^{*}|,

where
f^{*}
denotes the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
f
.

Our second main result reads as follows. Note that rather than the Lebesgue measure we consider any rotationally invariant
convex measure
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e., an absolutely continuous measure with (rotationally invariant)
(-1/n)
-concave density.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an integrable
p
-concave function and
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors distributed w.r.t.
f
. Then, for any rotationally invariant convex measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X^{*}_{k},Z^{*}_{k})}\bigr)\right],

where
\Phi^{N}_{(\cdot,\cdot)}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a stochastic model and
\{(X_{k}^{*},Z_{k}^{*})\}_{k=1}^{N}
are independent random vectors distributed w.r.t. the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
f
.

We would like to remark that the proof of Theorem
1.2
follows the approach of
[
34
]
, which itself builds upon the ideas presented in
[
28
, Section 8.2]
. For our purposes, we employ an interpretation of the functional polar projection bodies using the functional mixed volumes of V. Milman and L. Rotem
[
29
]
. Furthermore, both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
recover their geometric counterparts established by M. Hartzoulaki and G. Paouris
[
22
]
, and by G. Paouris, P. Pivovarov, and K. Tatarko
[
34
]
(see Remark
3.2
), respectively. Given these connections, it is natural to ask whether Theorems
A
and
B
can also be recovered from their stochastic versions. We conclude by addressing this question.

As a consequence of the latter investigation, we obtain the following generalization of Theorem
B
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an integrable
p
-concave function. Then, for any rotationally invariant convex measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(1.11)

\nu(\Pi^{\circ}f)\leq\nu(\Pi^{\circ}f^{*}),

where
f^{*}
denotes the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
f
.

Although a proof can be given using similar ideas to those of Theorem
1.2
, inequality (
1.11
) is, to the best of our knowledge, new.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we collect some preliminaries, background material and several tools the we will use later on, while Section
3
is devoted to the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. Finally, we show in Section
4
how to recover Theorems
A
and
B
from their stochastic versions.

2.
Preliminaries

We work in the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with the standard inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
. We write
e_{i}
for the
i
-th canonical unit vector, and
x_{i}
is used for the
i
-th coordinate of a vector in such a space. We denote by
B^{n}_{2}
the
n
-dimensional Euclidean (closed) unit ball and by
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
its boundary. We use
+
for the Minkowski sum, i.e.,
A+B=\{a+b:\,a\in A,\,b\in B\}
for any non-empty sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Given any set
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\chi_{{}_{M}}
denotes its characteristic function. Moreover, for any unit direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
u^{\perp}
,
P_{u^{\perp}}
and
R_{u}
are used for a hiperplane with normal vector
u
, the orthogonal projection onto it, and the reflection map about it, respectively. Along the paper, the
k
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of a measurable set is denoted by
|\cdot|_{k}
and we will omit the index
k
when it is equal to the dimension of the ambient space; furthermore, as usual, integrating
\mathrm{d}x
will stand the integration with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex set, its support function
h_{K}
and polar body
K^{\circ}
are given by

h_{K}(y)=\sup_{x\in K}\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\quad\mathrm{and}\quad K^{\circ}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h_{K}(x)\leq 1\}.

If
K
contains the origin in its interior, its Minkowski functional is defined as
\|x\|_{K}=\inf\{\lambda>0:x\in\lambda K\}
. Note that, in this case,
K=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|x\|_{K}\leq 1\}
and
\|\cdot\|_{K^{\circ}}=h_{K}(\cdot)
. We will also recall some definitions; the projection body,
\Pi K
, of
K
is the centrally symmetric convex body whose support function is given by
h_{\Pi K}(u)=|P_{u^{\perp}}K|_{n-1}
where, for any given direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
[0,u]
is a segment joining the origin and
u
. Thus the polar projection body, denoted as
\Pi^{\circ}K
, is merely
(\Pi K)^{\circ}
. For convex sets
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
the Hausdorff distance between them, denoted as
\delta^{H}(K,L)
is

\delta^{H}(K,L)=\inf\{\varepsilon>0:M\subset L+\varepsilon B_{2}^{n},L\subset M+\varepsilon B_{2}^{n}\}.

Equivalently,

\delta^{H}(K,L)=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|h_{K}(u)-h_{L}(u)|.

The following remark on the interplay between polarity and Hausdorff distance is in order; let
\{K_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a sequence of centrally symmetric convex bodies such that

K_{N}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}K

as
N\to+\infty
, where
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a centrally symmetric convex body. Then

K^{\circ}_{N}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}K^{\circ}

as
N\to+\infty
.

Let
K_{1},...,K_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be convex bodies and
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\geq 0
. The volume of their Minkowski sum is given by

|\lambda_{1}K_{1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\lambda_{m}K_{m}|=\sum_{1\leq i_{1},....,i_{n}\leq m}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\lambda_{i_{n}}V(K_{i_{1}},...,K_{i_{n}}),

where the coefficient
V(K_{i_{1}},...,K_{i_{n}})
is the so-called mixed volume of
n
-tuple
(K_{i_{1}},...,K_{i_{n}})
(see
[
41
, Section 5]
for background and properties). We will use the standard abbreviation
V(K[n-k],L[k])
when the sets appear, respectively,
n-k
and
k
times in the mixed volume with
k\in\{1,...,n-1\}
. In addition, for any
i\in\{1,...,n-1\}
, the
i
-th quermassintegral of
K
, is denoted by
W_{i}(K)=V(K[n-i],B_{2}^{n}[i])
. Note that, with this terminology,
\|u\|_{\Pi^{\circ}K}=nV(K[n-1],[0,u])
for every
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Moreover, we will use that mixed volumes are continuous w.r.t. the Hausdorff distance.

We will also use the
M
-sum of sets originally introduced by R. Gardner, D. Hug and W. Weil
[
17
]
. Let
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
K_{1},\dots,K_{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, their
M
-combination is defined as

\oplus_{M}(K_{1},\dots,K_{N})=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}x_{i}:x_{i}\in K_{i},(m_{1},\dots,m_{N})\in M\right\}.

It was proved in
[
17
, Theorem 6.1]
that, if
K_{1},\dots,K_{N}
are convex and
M
is a convex body contained in the positive orthant or centrally symmetric, then
\oplus_{M}(K_{1},\dots,K_{N})
is convex.

We remark that the use of the
M
-addition allow us to recover certain random sets that have already been use in the literature when proving stochastic forms of isoperimetric inequalities (see e.g.
[
31
]
and
[
34
]
). In this regard, note that if
[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
is the
n\times N
matrix with columns
x_{i}
then

\oplus_{C}(\{x_{1}\},\dots,\{x_{N}\})=[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]C.

Moreover, taking
C=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{e_{1},\dots,e_{N}\}
one gets

\oplus_{C}(\{x_{1}\},\dots,\{x_{N}\})=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\}.

2.1.
Linear parameter systems

We will use the notion of a
linear parameter system
introduced by C. A. Rogers and G. C. Shepard in
[
40
]
(see also
[
41
, Section 10.4]
). Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body, and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
be a unit direction. A linear parameter system is a family of convex bodies
\{K(t)\}_{t\in I}
, where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an interval, that can be represented as

(2.1)

K(t)=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{x_{j}+\lambda_{j}tu:j\in\mathcal{J}\}.

Here
\{x_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
and
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
are bounded sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}
respectively, and
\mathcal{J}
is an arbitrary index set. We will also make use of the case in which the index set is a convex body
K
, i.e,

K(t)=\text{conv}\{x+t\alpha(x)u:x\in K\},

where
\alpha:K\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded function. An example of this construction is the following one; let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Then
\{K_{u}(t)\}_{t\in[-1,1]}
is the linear parameter system given by

(2.2)

K_{u}(t)=\{y+su:y\in P_{u^{\perp}}K,\;s\in[f_{u}^{t}(y),-g_{u}^{t}(y)]\},

with

f_{u}^{t}(y)=\frac{(1+t)f_{u}(y)+(1-t)g_{u}(y)}{2}

and

g_{u}^{t}(y)=\frac{(1-t)f_{u}(y)+(1+t)g_{u}(y)}{2},

where
f_{u},g_{u}:P_{u^{\perp}}K\to\mathbb{R}
are convex functions such that

K=\{y+su:y\in P_{u^{\perp}}K,\;s\in[f_{u}(y),-g_{u}(y)]\}.

Note that
\{K_{u}(t)\}_{t\in[-1,1]}
interpolates continuously between
K_{u}(1)=K
,
K_{u}(-1)=R_{u}K
and
K_{u}(0)=S_{u^{\perp}}K
, where
S_{u^{\perp}}K
is the
Steiner symmetral
of
K
in the direction
u
.

A fundamental property of linear parameter systems is their convexity under mixed volumes: an elegant proof can be found in
[
41
, Theorem 10.4.1]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{K_{i}(t)\}_{t\in I}
, with
i=1,...,n
, be linear parameter systems in the direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Then
t\mapsto V\bigl(K_{1}(t),...,\,K_{n}(t)\bigr)
is convex.

As a corollary one has the following.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
\{K(t)\}_{t\in I}
a linear parameter system. Then for every
i=0,\dots,n-1

t\mapsto W_{i}\bigl(K(t)\bigr)
is convex.

2.2.
Functional setting

We recall that a function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is

p
-concave, for
p\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
, if

\varphi\bigl((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\bigr)\geq\bigl((1-\lambda)\varphi(x)^{p}+\lambda\varphi(y)^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\varphi(x)\varphi(y)>0
and any
\lambda\in(0,1)
, where the cases
p=0
,
p=\infty
and
p=-\infty
must be understood as the corresponding expressions that are obtained by continuity, namely, the geometric mean, the maximum and the minimum (of
\varphi(x)
and
\varphi(y)
), respectively. A
0
-concave function is usually called
log-concave
whereas a
(-\infty)
-concave function is referred to as
quasi-concave
.
Moreover, Jensen’s inequality implies that a
q
-concave function is also
p
-concave, whenever
q>p
.

In the following we will assume that
p
-concave functions are upper semicontinuous. Indeed, otherwise we may replace the function by its upper closure, which is determined via the closure of the superlevel sets (see
[
39
, Theorem 1.6]
), and thus their Lebesgue measure is preserved. In particular, this will imply that the maximum of such a function is attained. Additionally, we will always consider integrable
p
-concave functions. The latter, together with upper semicontinuity, imply that in our framework the superlevel sets of a
p
-concave function are convex bodies.

Let
s\in[-\infty,1]
and
\nu
be a Borel measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\nu
is
s
-concave if

\nu\bigl((1-\lambda)A+\lambda B\bigr)\geq\bigl((1-\lambda)\nu(A)^{s}+\lambda\nu(B)^{s}\bigr)^{1/s}

When
s=-\infty
the measure is usually referred to as
convex
. Following Borell’s characterization
[
5
]
, an absolutely continuous measure
\nu
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with density
\varphi
is
s
-concave if and only if
\varphi
is
p
-concave with
p=s/(1-ns)
(Note that Jensen’s inequality implies that convex measures are the largest class among
s
-concave ones). The latter can be deduced from the following result, originally proved in

[
5
]
and
[
6
]
(see also
[
15
]
for a detailed presentation).

Theorem 2.2

(The Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality)
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Let
-1/n\leq p\leq\infty
and let
f,g,h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be measurable functions, with positive integrals, such that

h\bigl((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\bigr)\geq\bigl((1-\lambda)f(x)^{p}+\lambda g(y)^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f(x)g(y)>0
. Then

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\left((1-\lambda)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q}+\lambda\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q}\right)^{1/q},

where
q=p/(np+1)
.

Another consequence of the Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality is the following.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
p
-concave function, with
p\geq-1/n
. Then

F(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x

is
p/(np+1)
-concave.

We will also recall the notion of symmetric decreasing rearrangement. We essentially follow
[
8
]
(see also
[
24
, Chapter 3]
). Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a measurable set with finite volume. Its symmetric rearrangement
A^{*}
is an Euclidean open ball with the same volume as
A
. Let now
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an integrable function. Using its
layer-cake representation

f(x)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\chi_{{}_{\{f>t\}}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t,

the
symmetric decreasing rearrangement
of
f
, denoted as
f^{*}
, is given by

f^{*}(x)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\chi_{{}_{\{f>t\}^{*}}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Note that, for any given
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\{f\geq t\}
is used to denote the superlevel set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)\geq t\}
. We also emphasize that
f^{*}
is radially symmetric and decreasing. In addition, it preserves the volume of the superlevel sets, i.e.,
|\{f>t\}|=|\{f^{*}>t\}|
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Moreover, one has
\|f^{*}\|_{p}=\|f\|_{p}
for each
1\leq p\leq+\infty
, where
\|\cdot\|_{p}
is the usual
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-norm.

For any given
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
the Steiner symmetral of
f
with respect to
u^{\perp}
, denoted as
f^{u}
is the function given by

f^{u}(x)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\chi_{{}_{\bigl\{S_{u^{\perp}}\{f>t\}\bigr\}}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Equivalently,
f^{u}
is obtained rearranging
f
along every line parallel to
u
, i.e., for every
y\in u^{\perp}
, taking
h(t)=f(y+tu)
, we have that
f^{u}(y+tu)=h^{*}(t)
. It is proved in
[
7
]
(see also
[
42
, Chapter 14]
) that for every measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with compact support there exists a sequence of the form
f_{0}=f
and
f_{n+1}=f_{n}^{u}
for some
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, which converges in the
L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-norm to
f^{*}
.

A key result involving symmetric decreasing rearrangements, which will be central to this work, is Christ’s version
[
9
]
of the Rogers-Brascamp-Lieb-Luttinger inequality. As shown in
[
31
]
, this theorem is a powerful tool for proving stochastic isoperimetric inequalities. We state it here for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
f_{1},...,f_{N}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be integrable functions and let
F:(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Suppose that
F
satisfies that, for any
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
y=(y_{1},...,y_{N})\in(u^{\perp})^{N}
, the function
F_{u,y}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by
F_{u,y}(t_{1},...,t_{N})=F(y_{1}+t_{1}u,...,y_{N}+t_{N}u)
is even and quasi-concave. Then

(2.4)

\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F(x_{1},...,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F(x_{1},...,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{N}f^{*}_{i}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\mathrm{d}x
stands for
\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{N}
.

Indeed, under the assumptions of Theorem
2.3
one can check that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F(x_{1},...,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F(x_{1},...,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}^{u}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x,

which implies (
2.4
) after a sequence of Steiner symmetrizations with respect to suitable directions. We refer the reader to
[
31
, Proposition 3.2]
for a detailed exposition of the latter. Note that, if
F_{u,y}
is quasi-convex for any
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
y=(y_{1},...,y_{N})\in u^{\perp}
, the inequalities (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) are reversed.

2.3.
Functional mixed volumes

The notion of mixed volumes was extended to a functional setting by V. Milman and L. Rotem
[
29
]
and S. Bobkov, A. Colesanti and I. Fragalà
[
4
]
independently. Although we essentially work in the class of
p
-concave functions, with
p\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, we will introduce this concept for the larger class of quasi-concave functions. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be quasi-concave functions. Their mixed integral or functional mixed volume is defined as

V(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})=\int_{0}^{+\infty}V\bigr(\{f_{1}\geq t\},\dots,\{f_{n}\geq t\}\bigl)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Following the spirit of the geometrical case, for any given quasi-concave function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, its
i
-th quermassintegral is

W_{i}(f)=V(f[n-i],\chi_{{}_{B_{2}^{n}}}[i])=\int_{0}^{+\infty}W_{i}\bigl(\{f\geq t\}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
i\in\{0,1,...,n-1\}
.

Furthermore, for any
p
-concave function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
its polar projection body is the centrally symmetric convex set, denoted as
\Pi^{\circ}f
, whose Minkowski functional is given by

\|u\|_{\Pi^{\circ}f}=nV(f[n-1],\chi_{{}_{[0,u]}}).

Note that

\|u\|_{\Pi^{\circ}f}=n\int_{0}^{+\infty}V(\{f\geq t\}[n-1],[0,u]\})\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{+\infty}|P_{u^{\perp}}\{f\geq t\}|_{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Hence, when
f\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, one obtains using co-areas’s formula that

\|u\|_{\Pi^{\circ}f}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\left\langle\nabla f(x),u\right\rangle|\,\mathrm{d}x.

The latter makes readily verifiable that Zhang’s affine Sobolev inequality can be restated as

|\Pi^{\circ}f|\leq|\Pi^{\circ}f^{*}|.

3.
Main results

Given an integrable
p
-concave function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
p\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, and
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors uniformly distributed w.r.t.
f
. As pointed out in
[
36
]
, it is possible to consider more general forms of the stochastic model introduced in (
1.8
) by using the
M
-sum. Specifically, let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a convex body contained in the positive orthant. We can define the random convex sets
E_{N,p},H_{N,p}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
as

(3.1)

\displaystyle E_{N,p}=\begin{cases}\oplus_{C}\bigl(R_{Z_{1}^{p}}(X_{1}),\dots,R_{Z_{N}^{p}}(X_{N})\bigr)&\mathrm{if}\quad p<0\\
\oplus_{C}\bigl(R_{-\log Z_{1}}(X_{1}),\dots,R_{-\log Z_{N}}(X_{N})\bigr)&\mathrm{if}\quad p=0\end{cases}

and

(3.2)

\displaystyle H_{N,p}=\oplus_{C}\bigl(\widetilde{R}_{Z_{1}^{p}}(X_{1}),\dots,\widetilde{R}_{Z_{N}^{p}}(X_{N})\bigr)

when
p>0
.
Note that the sets defined in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) (and hence the stochastic model build upon them) can be recovered by setting
C=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{e_{1},\dots,e_{N}\}
.

The proofs of both Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
essentially rely on checking that the corresponding functionals satisfy the conditions of Theorem
2.3
. In this regard, for readers convenience, we split the first part of Theorem
1.1
proof’s in two lemmas. We start by adapting
[
36
, Proposition 5.1]
to the context of level sets.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N\geq n+1
,
\{(x_{i},z_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
and
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}
. Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a convex body contained in the positive orthant,

\rho_{i}(p)=\begin{cases}z_{i}^{p}&\mathrm{if}\quad p\neq 0,\\
-\log z_{i}&\mathrm{if}\quad p=0\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle E_{N,p}(t)=\oplus_{C}\bigl(\{R_{\rho_{i}(p)}(x_{i}+\lambda_{i}t\theta)\}_{i=1}^{N}\bigr)\quad(p\leq 0),

\displaystyle H_{N,p}(t)=\oplus_{C}\bigl(\{\widetilde{R}_{\rho_{i}(p)}(x_{i}+\lambda_{i}t\theta)\}_{i=1}^{N}\bigr)\quad(p>0).

Then, for any
z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, both
\{P_{e_{n+1}^{\perp}}(E_{p,N}(t)\cap\pi_{z})\}_{t\in I}
and
\{P_{e_{n+1}^{\perp}}(H_{p,N}(t)\cap\pi_{z})\}_{t\in I}
, where
\pi_{z}=e_{n+1}^{\perp}+ze_{n+1}
and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
an interval, are linear parameter systems.

Proof.

We provide a sketch of the proof of the case
p\leq 0
as the one of
p>0
is analogous. First, note that

\displaystyle E_{N,p}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}(x_{i}+r_{i}e_{n+1})+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\lambda_{i}\right)t\theta:c\in C,\rho_{i}(p)\leq r_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,r):x=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}(x_{i}+\lambda_{i}t\theta),r\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\rho_{i}(p),c\in C\right\}

Let now
(r_{1},\dots,r_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
g:C\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function given by
g(c)=\left\langle(r_{1},\dots r_{N}),c\right\rangle
. Fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}
, the set
\widetilde{C}=g^{-1}(\{z\})
is a compact convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover,

E_{p,N}(t)\cap\pi_{z}=\{x_{\tilde{c}}+ze_{n+1}+\mu_{\tilde{c}}t\theta:\tilde{c}\in\widetilde{C}\}

for all
z\geq\min_{c\in C}\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\rho_{i}(p)
.

Thus, for any
t\in I
and
z\geq\min_{c\in C}\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\rho_{i}(p)
,
P_{e_{n+1}^{\perp}}(E_{p,N}(t)\cap\pi_{z})=\{x_{\tilde{c}}+\mu_{\tilde{c}}t\theta:\tilde{c}\in\widetilde{C}\}
is a linear parameter system indexed in
\widetilde{C}
.
∎

The latter together with Corollary
2.1
yields the following.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
N\geq n+1
and
\{(x_{i},z_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
. Then, fixed
z_{1},\dots z_{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the function
F_{u,y};\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by

F_{u,y}(s_{1},\dots,s_{N})=\int_{0}^{+\infty}W_{i}(\{\Phi^{N}_{(y_{k}+s_{k}u,z_{k})}\geq t\})\,\mathrm{d}t

is convex and even for all
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{N})\in(u^{\perp})^{N}
.

Proof.

For the reader convenience we will use the notation
\bar{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{N})
. Let
\bar{s},\bar{s}^{\prime}
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. As before, we only give a proof of the case
p\leq 0
;

\displaystyle F_{u,y}\bigl((1-\lambda)\bar{s}+\lambda\bar{s}^{\prime}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}W_{i}(\{\Phi^{N}_{(y_{k}+((1-\lambda)s_{k}+\lambda s_{k}^{\prime})u,z_{k})}\geq t\})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}W_{i}\bigl(P_{e_{n+1}^{\perp}}(E_{p,N}(\lambda)\cap\pi_{t})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t,

where

(3.3)

\displaystyle E_{p,N}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\oplus_{C}\bigl(\{R_{\rho_{i}(p)}\bigl(y_{i}+(1-\lambda)s_{i}+\lambda s_{i}^{\prime}\bigr)\theta\bigr)\}_{i=1}^{N}\bigr)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\oplus_{C}\bigl(\{R_{\rho_{i}(p)}\bigl(y_{i}+s_{i}u+\lambda(s_{i}^{\prime}-s_{i})u\bigr)\}_{i=1}^{N}\bigr)

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\oplus_{C}\bigl(\{R_{\rho_{i}(p)}(x_{i}+\mu_{i}\lambda u)\}_{i=1}^{N}\bigr),

and
\mu_{i}=s_{i}^{\prime}-s_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,N
. Hence, using Lemma
3.2
we get that
\{P_{e_{n+1}^{\perp}}(E_{p,N}(\lambda)\cap\pi_{t})\}_{\lambda\in(0,1)}
is a linear parameter system. Thus, Corollary
2.1
imply that
\lambda\mapsto F_{u,y}\bigl((1-\lambda)\bar{s}+\lambda\bar{s}^{\prime}\bigr)
is convex proving that
F_{u,y}(\bar{s})
is so. In addition, taking into account that

\oplus_{C}\bigl(\{R_{\rho_{i}(p)}(y_{i}-s_{i}u)\}_{i=1}^{N}\bigr)=R_{u}\Bigl(\oplus_{C}\bigl(\{R_{\rho_{i}(s)}(y_{i}+s_{i}u)\}_{i=1}^{N}\bigr)\Bigr)

for every
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, it is straightforward to check that

\{\Phi^{N}_{(y_{k}-s_{k}u,z_{k})}\geq t\}=R_{u}\{\Phi^{N}_{(y_{k}+s_{k}u,z_{k})}\geq t\}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Therefore,
F_{u,y}(-\bar{s})=F_{u,y}(\bar{s})
for all
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
y\in(u^{\perp})^{N}
.
∎

We can now finish the proof of our first main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We will use for short the notation
\bar{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
. Let
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors distributed w.r.t.
f
. Note that using Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Bigl[W_{i}\Bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\Bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\left(\int f\right)^{-1}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}}W_{i}\Bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(x_{k},z_{k})}\Bigr)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{{}_{\{f\geq z_{i}\}}}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\left(\int f\right)^{-1}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}W_{i}(\{\Phi^{N}_{(x_{k},z_{k})}\geq t\})\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{{}_{\{f\geq z_{i}\}}}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\bar{z}

\displaystyle=\left(\int f\right)^{-1}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\bar{z},

where, fixed
z\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
,
F_{\bar{z}}:(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the function given by

F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=\int_{0}^{+\infty}W_{i}(\{\Phi^{N}_{(x_{k},z_{k})}\geq t\})\,\mathrm{d}t

and
g_{i}(x_{i})=\chi_{{}_{\{f\geq z_{i}\}}}(x_{i})
for all
i=1,\dots,N
. Hence, Lemma
3.2
implies that
F_{\bar{z}}
is in the conditions of Theorem
2.3
for all
\bar{z}\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}\bar{x}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{n}g^{*}_{i}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\chi_{{}_{\{f\geq z_{i}\}^{*}}}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x,

for any
\bar{z}\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
. Putting all together,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[W_{i}\Bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\Bigr)\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\int f\right)^{-1}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}}F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{{}_{\{f\geq z_{i}\}}}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\left(\int f^{*}\right)^{-1}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}}F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{{}_{\{f\geq z_{i}\}}}^{*}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[W_{i}\Bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X^{*}_{k},Z^{*}_{k})}\Bigr)\right]

as we wanted to prove.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a family of
p
-concave functions, with
p\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
. It was shown in
[
29
]
that

V(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\geq V(f^{*}_{1},\dots,f_{n}^{*})

generalizing, therefore, Theorem
A
. A stochastic analogue of the latter can be deduced by using the argument of Theorem
1.1
’s proof.

We now give a proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We assume, for the sake of simplicity, that
\|f\|_{\infty}=1
. In addition, we use the notation
\bar{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
for short. First note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\bigl[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\left(\int f\right)^{-N}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}}\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(x_{k},z_{k})}\bigr)\prod_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{{}_{\{f\geq z_{i}\}}}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\left(\int f\right)^{-N}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\bar{z},

where, for any fixed
\bar{z}\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
,
F_{\bar{z}}:(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the function given by

\displaystyle F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(x_{k},z_{k})}\bigr),

and
g_{i}(x_{i})=\chi_{{}_{\{f\geq z_{i}\}}}(x_{i})
for all
i=1,\dots,N
. Hence, fixing
\bar{z}\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

\displaystyle F_{\bar{z},y,u}(\bar{s})

\displaystyle=\int_{u^{\perp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{{}_{\{\|w+ru\|_{\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(y_{i}+s_{i}u_{i},z_{i})}}\leq 1\}}}\psi(w+ru)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\int_{u^{\perp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{{}_{\{nV(\Phi^{N}_{(y_{i}+s_{i}u,z_{i})}[n-1],\chi_{{}_{[0,w+ru]}})\leq 1\}}}\psi(w+ru)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\int_{u^{\perp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{{}_{\{g(\bar{s},r)\leq 1\}}}\psi(w+ru)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}w,

\displaystyle=\int_{u^{\perp}}\varphi_{w}(\bar{s})\,\mathrm{d}w,

where
\varphi_{w}:u^{\perp}\times(\mathbb{R})^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the function given by

\varphi_{w}(\bar{s})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{{}_{\{g(\bar{s},r)\leq 1\}}}\psi(w+ru)\,\mathrm{d}r

and
g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined as
g(\bar{s},r)=nV(\Phi^{N}_{(y_{i}+s_{i}u,z_{i})}[n-1],\chi_{{}_{[0,w+ru]}})
.

Secondly, for any given
\lambda\in(0,1)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle(\Phi^{N}_{(y_{i}+((1-\lambda)s_{i}+\lambda s_{i}^{\prime})u,z_{i})}[n-1],\chi_{{}_{[0,w+((1-\lambda)r+\lambda r^{\prime})}})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}V\bigl(P_{e_{n+1}^{\perp}}\bigl(E_{p,N}(\lambda)\cap\pi_{t}\bigr)[n-1],[0,w+ru+\lambda(r^{\prime}-r)u]\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Moreover, note that
\{[0,w+\bigl((1-\lambda)r+\lambda r^{\prime}\bigr)u]\}_{\lambda\in(0,1)}
is a linear parameter system in the direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Hence, Lemma
3.1
together with Theorem
2.1
, imply that
(\bar{s},r)\to g(\bar{s},r)
is jointly convex. Thus, we have for every
w\in u^{\perp}
that

(\bar{s},r)\mapsto\chi_{{}_{\{g(\bar{s},r)\leq 1\}}}\psi(w+ru)

is
(-1/n)
-concave. As a consequence, Corollary
2.2
implies that
\bar{s}\mapsto\varphi_{w}(\bar{s})
is
\alpha
-concave, with
\alpha=-1/(n-1)
(and, in particular, quasi-concave), for every
w\in u^{\perp}
. Furthermore, taking into account that

\{\Phi^{N}_{(y_{i}-s_{i}u,z_{i})}\geq t\}=R_{u}\{\Phi^{N}_{(y_{i}+s_{i}u,z_{i})}\geq t\}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we have that
g(-\bar{s},-r)=g(\bar{s},r)
. The latter, together with the rotational invariance of
\psi
, yield that
\varphi_{w}(\bar{s})=\varphi_{w}(-\bar{s})
for any
w\in u^{\perp}
.

Now, using Fubini’s

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\bigl[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\left(\int f\right)^{-N}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}}\int_{(u^{\perp})^{N+1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi_{w}(\bar{s})\prod_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}(s_{i})\,\mathrm{d}\bar{s}\right)\,\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\bar{z},

where
h_{i}(s_{i})=g_{i}(y_{i}+s_{i}u)
for all
i=1,\dots,N
. Thus, using (
2.5
) we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi_{w}(\bar{s})\prod_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}(s_{i})\,\mathrm{d}\bar{s}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi_{w}(\bar{s})\prod_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}^{*}(s_{i})\,\mathrm{d}\bar{s}

for every
w\in u^{\perp}
and
\bar{z}\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
. Therefore, rolling back

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\bigl[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int f\right)^{-N}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}^{u}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\bar{z}

\displaystyle=\left(\int f^{u}\right)^{-N}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}^{u}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\bar{z}.

As mentioned in Section
2
, for every measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with compact support there exists a sequence of the form
f_{0}=f
and
f_{n+1}=f_{n}^{u}
for some
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, which converges in the
L_{1}
norm to
f^{*}
. Therefore, proceeding as in
[
7
]
, one gets that

\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}^{u}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}}F_{\bar{z}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}^{*}(x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x.

for every
\bar{z}\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}
. The latter yields that

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\Bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X^{*}_{k},Z^{*}_{k})}\bigr)\Bigl],

as we wanted to see.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We finish this section by showing how Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
recovers their geometrical counterparts. Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{i=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors uniformly distributed w.r.t.
\text{hyp}(\chi_{{}_{K}})=K\times[0,1]
. Since
\chi_{{}_{K}}
is a
(+\infty)
-concave function, its stochastic model is given by

\displaystyle\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}(x)=\sup\{\lim_{p\to+\infty}z^{1/p}:(x,z)\in h_{N,+\infty}\}=1

for all
x\in h_{N,+\infty}=\oplus_{C}\bigl(\{X_{1}\},\dots,\{X_{N}\}\bigr)=[X_{1},\dots,X_{N}]C
and zero otherwise. In other words,
\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}=\chi_{{}_{[X_{1},\dots,X_{N}]C}}
. Thus, on the one hand, applying Theorem
1.1
to
\chi_{{}_{K}}
we get that for every
i=1,\dots,n-1

(3.6)

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[W_{i}\bigl([X_{1},\dots,X_{N}]C\bigr)\Bigr]\geq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[W_{i}\bigl([X^{*}_{1},\dots,X^{*}_{N}]C\bigr)\Bigr].

Note that (
3.6
) recovers (
1.2
) when
C=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{e_{1},\dots,e_{N}\}
. Although not explicitly proved, this generalization of (
1.2
) was pointed out in
[
31
, Remark 4.4]
.

On the other hand, using Theorem
1.2
one recovers that

(3.7)

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}[X_{1},\dots,X_{N}]C\bigr)\Bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}[X^{*}_{1},\dots,X^{*}_{N}]C\bigl)\Bigr],

which was proved in
[
34
, Theorem 1.2]
.

4.
Recovering deterministic results

This section is devoted to study how to derive results for deterministic functions from their stochastic models. In this regard, it is enough for our purposes to consider the notion of
epi-convergence
.

Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of functions. We say that
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
epi-converges to
f
or simply
e-\lim_{n\to+\infty}f_{n}=f
if and only if

\mathrm{epi}(f_{n})\overset{\mathrm{P-K}}{\to}\mathrm{epi}(f),

where P-K denotes convergence in the usual Painlevé-Kuratowski sense (see e.g.
[
38
, Chapter 4]
). Analogously, we say that
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

hypo-converges
to
f
(or
h-\lim_{n}f_{n}=f
) if and only if

\mathrm{hyp}(f_{n})\overset{\mathrm{P-K}}{\to}\mathrm{hyp}(f).

It is worth mentioning that convergence in the Hausdorff distance is stronger than convergence in the P-K sense and, when dealing with compact sets, both notions are equivalent (see e.g.
[
38
, p. 117]
). In a more detailed manner, let
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of sets. If

A_{n}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}A,

then
A_{n}\overset{\mathrm{P-K}}{\to}\,\overline{A}
, where
\overline{\cdot}
denotes the closure of a given set. Since we work with closed epigraphs and hypographs convergence in the Hausdorff distance will always imply convergence in the P-K sense.

We would like to remark that epi-convergence has been widely use in the literature, specially in the study of valuations on convex functions (see e.g.
[
10
,
11
,
30
,
12
]
and the references therein). We will use the following lemma originally proved in
[
11
, Lemma 5]
:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a lower semi-continuous convex function such that
\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=+\infty
. If
u_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is sequence of lower semi-continuous convex functions, with
\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u_{n}(x)=+\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfying that
e-\lim_{n}u_{n}=u
. Then

\{u_{n}\leq t\}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}\{u\leq t\}

for all
t\neq\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x)
.

Lemma
4.1
immediately gives the following.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an integrable
p
-concave function, with
p\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of functions within the same class.

i)

If
p\geq 0
and
h-\lim_{n}f^{p}_{n}=f^{p}
, then

\{f_{n}\geq t\}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}\{f\geq t\}

for all
t\neq\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)
.

ii)

If
p\leq 0
and
e-\lim_{n}f^{p}_{n}=f^{p}
, then

\{f_{n}\geq t\}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}\{f\geq t\}

for all
t\neq\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)
.

iii)

If
p=0
and
e-\lim_{n}(-\log f_{n})=-\log f
, then

\{f_{n}\geq t\}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}\{f\geq t\}

for all
t\neq\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)
.

We will use the stochastic models presented in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) to see how Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
recover their deterministic counterparts. Let
N\geq n+1
,
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an integrable
p
-concave function and
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors distributed w.r.t. f. Recall that the stochastic model of
f
is build upon the random sets
E_{N,p},H_{N,p}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by

(4.1)

E_{N,p}=\begin{cases}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{R_{Z_{1}^{p}}(X_{1}),\dots,R_{Z_{N}^{p}}(X_{N})\}&\mathrm{if}\quad p<0,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{R_{-\log Z_{1}}(X_{1}),\dots,R_{-\log Z_{N}}(X_{N})\}&\mathrm{if}\quad p=0\end{cases}

and

(4.2)

H_{N,p}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{conv}\nolimits\{\widetilde{R}_{Z_{1}^{p}}(X_{1}),\dots,\widetilde{R}_{Z_{N}^{p}}(X_{N})\}

if
p>0
.

Moreover, for a given integrable
p
-concave function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we will consider the truncated function
f_{\varepsilon}(x)=f(x)\chi_{{}_{\{f\geq\varepsilon\}}}(x)
. Note that
f_{\varepsilon}\leq f
and dominated convergence, together with the continuity of the classical quermassintegrals in Hausdorff distance, imply that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}W_{i}(f_{\varepsilon})=W_{i}(f)

for every
i\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
. In addition, since any rotational invariant convex measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is continuous in Hausdorff distance (see e.g.
[
13
, Lemma 5.2]
), one has that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\nu(\Pi^{\circ}f_{\varepsilon})=\nu(\Pi^{\circ}f).

Therefore it is enough to prove our claims for
f_{\varepsilon}
.

Furthermore, let
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors distributed w.r.t.
f_{\varepsilon}
. It is straightforward to check that the random sets
E_{N,p}
and
H_{N,p}
are convex bodies. Thus, using for instance Kolmogorov
0-1
law and Borel-
Cantelli lemma, one can check that

\displaystyle E_{N,p}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}epi(f_{\varepsilon}^{p})\quad\mathrm{if}\quad p<0,

\displaystyle E_{N,p}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}epi(-\log f_{\varepsilon})\quad\mathrm{if}\quad p=0\quad\mathrm{and}

\displaystyle H_{N,p}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}epi(f_{\varepsilon}^{p})\quad\mathrm{if}\quad p>0

almost surely as
N\to+\infty
.

We start by proving that almost sure (a. s. for short) convergence holds when considering the functional quermassintegral of an stochastic model. Although not explicitly described, we work in an underlying probability space.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an integrable
p
-concave function and
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors distributed w.r.t.
f
. Then

\lim_{N\to\infty}W_{i}\bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)=W_{i}(f)

a. s. for every
i\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
.

Proof.

We will focus on the case of
p=0
as the rest is analogous. Let
N\geq n+1
and
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{i=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors distributed w.r.t
f_{\varepsilon}
. We have that
E_{N,p}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a convex body and that

E_{N,p}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}epi(-\log f_{\varepsilon})

a. s. when
N\to+\infty
. The latter, together with Lemma
4.2
, yield that

\{\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\geq t\}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}\{f_{\varepsilon}\geq t\}

a. s. for all
t\neq\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)
as
N\to+\infty
. Furthermore, the continuity of the classical quermassintegrals in Hausdorff distance implies that for all
t\neq\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)
and
i\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}

\lim_{N\to+\infty}W_{i}\bigl(\{\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\geq t\}\bigr)=W_{i}\bigl(\{f_{\varepsilon}\geq t\}\bigr)

a. s. Finally, taking into account that
\{\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\geq t\}\subset\{f_{\varepsilon}\geq t\}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, dominated convergence ensure that

\lim_{N\to+\infty}W_{i}\bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)=W_{i}(f_{\varepsilon})

a. s.
∎

We can now see how Theorem
1.1
recovers Theorem
A
. First, Proposition
4.1
and dominated convergence ensure that

\displaystyle W_{i}(f_{\varepsilon})=\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Bigr[\lim_{N\to+\infty}W_{i}\bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\Bigl]

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[W_{i}\bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\Bigr]

The latter, together with Theorem
1.1
, imply that

\displaystyle W_{i}(f_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[W_{i}\bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[W_{i}\bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X^{*}_{k},Z^{*}_{k})}\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigr[\lim_{N\to+\infty}W_{i}\bigl(\Phi^{N}_{(X^{*}_{k},Z^{*}_{k})}\bigr)\Bigl]=W_{i}(f_{\varepsilon}^{*}).

Finally, as aforementioned, we recover Theorem
A
by taking limits as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

We now move on seeing how Theorem
1.2
recovers its determinist counterpart. First we prove the following.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an integrable
p
-concave function and
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors distributed w.r.t.
f
. Then

\Pi^{\circ}\Phi_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}^{N}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}\Pi^{\circ}f

a. s. when
N\to+\infty
.

Proof.

Let
N\geq n+1
and
\{(X_{k},Z_{k})\}_{i=1}^{N}
be independent random vectors distributed w.r.t
f_{\varepsilon}
. As before, we will focus on the case of
p=0
. Moreover, since polarity is continuous w.r.t. the Hausdorff distance, the claim is equivalent to check that

\Pi\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}\Pi f

a. s. when
N\to+\infty
. Recall that

h_{\Pi f}(u)=n\int_{0}^{+\infty}V\bigl(\{f\geq t\}[n-1],[0,u]\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t.

We have, for all
t\neq\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)
, that

\{\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\geq t\}\overset{\delta^{H}}{\to}\{f_{\varepsilon}\geq t\}

a. s. when
N\to+\infty
. Hence, taking into account that
\{\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\geq t\}\subset\{f_{\varepsilon}\geq t\}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the continuity of mixed volumes in Hausdorff distance and dominated convergence yield that

\lim_{N\to+\infty}||h_{\Pi f_{\varepsilon}}-h_{\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}}\|_{\infty}=0

a. s., which is equivalent to the claim.
∎

We finish as follows; Proposition
4.2
and dominated convergence theorem ensure that

\displaystyle\nu(\Pi^{\circ}f_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\nu\bigl(\lim_{N\to+\infty}\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\lim_{N\to+\infty}\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\Bigl]

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\Bigl].

Hence, we deduce from Theorem
1.2
that

\displaystyle\nu(\Pi^{\circ}f_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X_{k},Z_{k})}\bigr)\Bigl]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\nu\bigl(\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X^{*}_{k},Z^{*}_{k})}\bigr)\Bigl]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\nu\bigl(\lim_{N\to+\infty}\Pi^{\circ}\Phi^{N}_{(X^{*}_{k},Z^{*}_{k})}\bigr)\Bigr]=\nu(\Pi^{\circ}f_{\varepsilon}^{*}),

which implies the claim after taking limits as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

The safety profile of trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole regarding concomitant other drugs: a comparative analysis of adverse effects in a tertiary level adult intensive care unit
Authors: Mefküre Durmuş, Davut Aydın, Sena Ustaömer, Sena Güzel Karahan, Ahmet Oğuzhan Küçük, Mehtap Pehlivanlar Küçük
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTrimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) has been used clinically against many gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria, Pneumocystis, and certain protozoa. Beside its beneficial effects, it has many adverse reactions such as hyperkalemia, aplastic anemia, fulminant hepatic necrosis, hematological and neurological toxicity. Evaluation of TMP-SMX-related adverse reactions in intensive care unit (ICU) patients who generally with polipharmacy is lacking. We aimed to identify TMP-SMX-related adverse effects associated with mortality, and to determine independent predictors of this outcome, and factors associated with adverse reactions in critically ill patients who generally with polypharmacy undergoing TMP-SMX therapy.MethodsThis retrospective observational cohort study was conducted in a 16-bed adult medical ICU in Türkiye. To assess the impact of various factors on TMP-SMX-related adverse reactions, patients were categorized into two independent groups based on the presence or absence of adverse effects. Additionally, to analyze changes in laboratory parameters associated with TMP-SMX therapy, paired comparisons were made between pre-therapy and post-therapy values within the same patients. Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 23.0.ResultsSerum creatinin (sCr) elevation, AST elevation, hyperbilirubinemia, thrombocytopenia, and metabolic acidosis were significantly associated with mortality, also sCr elevation was found to be an independent risk factor for mortality. Any of drugs or diseases were increased the risk of hyperkalemia concomitant TMP-SMX. ALT elevation were significantly associated with concomitantly teicoplanin. Endocrinologic disease or respiratory causes of ICU admission were found independent risk factors for AST elevation or acidosis respectively. Thrombocytopenia was associated with concomitantly colistin-echinocandin combination. Cardiovascular diseases concomitant TMP-SMX were found to be an independent risk factor for serum creatinin (sCr) elevation. There was no relationship between sCr elevation and TMP-SMX concomitantly colistimethate.ConclusionsPhysicians should pay attention when prescribing TMP-SMX to patients with cardiovascular diseases. Introduction

Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) has been used clinically since 1968 against many gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria, Pneumocystis, and certain protozoa [1, 2].

Over time, the indications for TMP-SMX have expanded to include the treatment of Pneumocystis carinii pneumonia (Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia, PJP), shigellosis, otitis media, chronic bronchitis, urinary tract infections, and several Escherichia coli infections, as well as the preventive use of TMP-SMX for PJP prophylaxis [2]. The mechanism of action involves blocking bacterial folic acid synthesis by inhibiting thymidine and purine synthesis [3, 4].

TMP-SMX has gastrointestinal adverse reactions, skin reactions, hematological and neurological toxicity, and some drug-drug interactions with oral anticoagulants, sulfonylureas, phenytoin, and methotrexate [1, 4]. Other adverse reactions of this therapy include hyperkalemia, serum creatinine elevation, electrolyte abnormalities, aplastic anemia, elevated liver enzyme levels, and fulminant hepatic necrosis [5, 6].

TMP-SMX is frequently administered to patients with acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) for the treatment and prevention of PJP. The incidence of adverse reactions to TMP-SMX in AIDS patients ranges from 40% to 80%, significantly higher than the 3% to 5% observed in the general population [6]. For instance, among patients with rheumatic and musculoskeletal diseases, 18.7% experienced adverse reactions to TMP-SMX, including allergic reactions (liver dysfunction, skin rash, fever, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia) and non-allergic reactions (hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, and elevated serum creatinine levels) [5]. Adverse reactions to TMP-SMX have been evaluated in various populations, including patients with AIDS, geriatric patients, outpatients, individuals with melioidosis, and those with severe methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) infections [7–12]. Notably, a unique study in the literature evaluated TMP-SMX-related hyperkalemia in patients hospitalized in the intensive care unit (ICU) [13].

Evaluation of TMP-SMX-related adverse reactions in ICU patients is lacking. Additionally, in the literature, drug-drug interactions between TMP-SMX and other medications are primarily limited to non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), anticoagulants, phenytoin, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs), angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), and spironolactone.

The primary objective of this study is to identify TMP-SMX related adverse effects associated with mortality, and to determine independent predictors of this outcome in critically ill patients who generally with polypharmacy undergoing TMP-SMX therapy. The secondary objectives are to identify factors associated TMP-SMX-related adverse reactions and to present epidemiological data on these reactions, including their severity grading and time of onset in this patient population.

Material and method

This retrospective observational cohort study was conducted in a 16-bed adult medical ICU at a tertiary care hospital in Türkiye. To mitigate potential biases inherent in retrospective designs, we meticulously examined all study data. We did not predetermine the sample size; instead, we evaluated all patients who met the inclusion criteria within the specified timeframe. “TMP-SMX therapy” was defined as the administration of intravenous and/or oral TMP-SMX at any dosage for a minimum duration of 48 h.

Study population

Patient electronic medical records from January 2020 to June 2024 were reviewed. All patients (patients ≥ 18 years) who received TMP-SMX therapy for at least 48 h were included in the analysis. Inclusion criteria encompassed patients administered TMP-SMX, while exclusion criteria were:

1)Patients who received TMP-SMX therapy for less than 48 h.

2)Patients who were on prophylactic TMP-SMX therapy prior to admission.

3)Patients who had used any chemotherapeutic agent within the three months preceding the initiation of TMP-SMX therapy.

To assess the impact of various factors on TMP-SMX-related adverse reactions, patients were categorized into two independent groups based on the presence or absence of adverse effects. Additionally, to analyze changes in laboratory parameters associated with TMP-SMX therapy, paired comparisons were made between pre-therapy and post-therapy values within the same patients.

Data collection

The demographics (sex and age), clinical features (mortality, comorbidities, and ICU admission cause, ICU scores), and laboratory data -including white blood cell count, neutrophil (NEU), lymphocyte (LYM), and platelet counts, serum creatinine (sCr), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), total bilirubin, serum sodium, serum potassium levels, C-reactive protein (CRP), and blood gas parameters- were recorded. Additionally, the administration of therapeutic dosages of single drugs and combinations of two drugs that were frequently prescribed together was documented if they were administered for at least 48 h during TMP-SMX therapy. Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) values were calculated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) formula. Mortality was defined as occurring if death took place 48 h after the initiation of TMP-SMX therapy and up to 48 h after its completion. Adverse reactions that occurred during treatment were noted. Laboratory values from up to 24 h before initiating TMP-SMX therapy, as well as the first observed laboratory values outside the reference range after starting or up to 24 h after completing TMP-SMX therapy, were recorded. Previously identified adverse effects associated with TMP-SMX therapy include hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, hyperbilirubinemia, elevated ALT/AST, increased sCr, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, anemia, and metabolic acidosis [1, 9, 10]. Grade changes of adverse effects were noted according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0. If adverse reactions grade changes existed, adverse effects were noted to be present. The existing days and the grade changes of adverse effects were also noted. Adverse events were recorded irrespective of causality as possible/probable/definite; severity was graded according to CTCAE v5.0. The relationship between adverse effects and other medications administered concurrently for at least 48 h during TMP-SMX therapy was investigated. Additionally, the association between any-grade adverse effects and clinical features was analyzed. Acute Physiologic and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II), Sequential (sepsis-related) Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) and Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) scores were calculated daily in the ICU. Pre and post treatment ICU scores (APACHE II, SOFA and GCS) were noted to asses the patients’ clinical status. Pulse corticosteroid therapy was defined as the administration of at least 250 mg of methylprednisolone or an equivalent dose in a single dose. TMP-SMX dosages were categorized based on the trimethoprim (TMP) component: “low” for doses less than 5 mg/kg/day, “moderate” for doses between 5 and 8 mg/kg/day and and “high” for doses exceeding 8 mg/kg/day.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 23.0 (Statistical Package for Social Sciences). The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test assessed the normality of data distributions. Quantitative data with normal distributions are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed data are presented as medians. Demographic characteristics, comorbidities, admission causes, medications, and drug combinations are reported as frequencies and percentages.

To evaluate differences in categorical variables, Pearson’s chi-squared test, Yates’s continuity correction, or Fisher’s exact test were applied as appropriate. The multivariate analysis was performed using binary logistic regression analysis. The variance explained by the model was shown with the Nagelkerke R2 value. The Hosmer-Lemeshow test was used to show the data fit the model well. The risk factors were explained with the odds ratio. Comparisons between dependent groups (pre-treatment and post-treatment) utilized either the paired t-test or the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, depending on data distribution. All analyses included data from 120 patients. Continuous variables were recorded with two decimal places. Cases with missing values were excluded from analyses involving those specific variables. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

TMP-SMX was administered to 181 patients during the study period. A total of 56 patients who did not receive TMP-SMX for at least 48 h and 5 patients who had received TMP-SMX prophylactically before admission were excluded. Thus, 120 patients were included in the study (Fig. 1). There were 20 patients with missing bilirubine levels.

Flowchart of the study population

Among these 120 patients, 72 (60%) were men. The overall median age was 59 years (IQR: 44–70). The mean age of women was 54.16 ± 20.07 years, while the median age of men was 62 years (IQR: 46.25–70). A significant association was found between age and hyperkalemia (p = 0.03). In the hyperkalemia group, the median age was 64 years (IQR: 49.25–72), whereas in patients without hyperkalemia, the median age was 55 years (IQR: 38.5–66.75). No significant association was found between age and other adverse reactions (p > 0.05). The median duration of TMP-SMX therapy was 9 days (IQR: 5–13.75). At least one adverse reaction occurred in 106 patients (88.33%). There was no significant association between TMP-SMX therapy duration or dosage range and the occurrence of at least one adverse reaction. TMP-SMX was most commonly administered at high doses (trimethoprim > 8 mg/kg/day) in 82.5% of patients. The primary indication for TMP-SMX therapy was respiratory infections (50%) (Table 1).

Hyperkalemia was associated with male sex, cardiovascular diseases, and kidney disease although any of them were found to be an independent risk factor. Hyponatremia was significantly associated with piperacillin/tazobactam and acyclovir. Also piperacillin/tazobactam (OR: 4.324, CI: 1.5–12) or acyclovir (OR: 7.93, CI: 1.4–44.1) concomitant TMP-SMX were found to be independent risk factors for hyponatremia. Serum creatinine elevation was related to cardiovascular disease (OR: 2.518, CI: 1.16–5.4). ALT and AST elevation were significantly associated with teicoplanin and the teicoplanin-carbapenem combination. However only teicoplanin (OR: 3.11, CI: 1.31–7.38) was found to be an independent risk factor for ALT elevation. Hematologic diseases (OR:6.824, CI:1.7–27.5), teicoplanin-azole antifungals (OR: 5.235, CI: 1.1–25.1), and pulse corticosteroids (OR:9.058, 1.4–58.8) were found to be independent risk factors for hyperbilirubinemia. Colistimethate-echinocandin (OR: 8.125, CI: 1.5–43.8) was found to be an independent risk factor for thrombocytopenia. Anemia was significantly associated with COVID-19 as an ICU admission cause and colistimethate therapy. Metabolic acidosis was associated with respiratory causes of ICU admission (OR: 2.834, 1.25–6.4) (Table 5). The overall mortality rate was 56.66%. sCr elevation, AST elevation, hyperbilirubinemia, thrombocytopenia, and metabolic acidosis were significantly associated with mortality (p < 0.05). sCr elevation was found to be a significant risk factor for mortality (OR: 8.37, CI: 2.334–30.034) (Table 6). Age, sex, comorbidities or concomitant drugs were not found to be associated with mortality.

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated the adverse reactions associated with TMP-SMX in ICU patients. To our knowledge, this is the first study to comprehensively assess TMP-SMX-related adverse reactions in the ICU setting. We identified factors contributing to these adverse reactions and observed significant increases in serum potassium, liver enzymes, sCr, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), eGFR, partial pressure of carbon dioxide (pCO₂), and lactate levels. Additionally, significant decreases were noted in thrombocyte count, hemoglobin, bicarbonate (HCO₃), and pH values. These findings align with previously reported adverse reactions of TMP-SMX in the literature [5].

We found that older age, male sex, cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, and pulse-dose corticosteroid use were associated with an increased risk of hyperkalemia, whereas clarithromycin appeared to reduce this effect. This may be explained by the significant concomitant administration of clarithromycin with piperacillin-tazobactam, as previous studies have linked piperacillin-tazobactam with severe hypokalemia [14, 15]. However, we suspect that the small number of patients receiving clarithromycin may have influenced this finding. Additionally, any of thoso factors were found to be independent risk factors for hyperkalemia. Further studies are needed to investigate the potential impact of piperacillin-tazobactam on TMP-SMX-induced hyperkalemia.

Hiramoto et al. reported that older age, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease (CKD), and the use of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs) or angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) were significantly associated with hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, and sCr elevation [5]. Our findings regarding hyperkalemia and sCr elevation were consistent with their study, as we also found associations between hyperkalemia or sCr elevation and older age, cardiovascular disease as well as CKD.

Previous studies have linked non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), ARBs/ACEIs, and spironolactone to TMP-SMX-induced hyperkalemia [9, 12, 16]. However, in our study, no significant association was found between these drugs and hyperkalemia. We attribute this to the relatively small number of patients receiving these medications.

Hirai et al. reported that high-dose TMP-SMX did not alter hyperkalemic effects when administered concomitantly with glucocorticoids, while low-dose TMP-SMX combined with prednisolone significantly reduced hyperkalemia [17]. In our study, most patients received high-dose TMP-SMX, and we observed glucocorticoids did not affect the hyperkalemic effect of TMP-SMX. Similarly, a study by Mengi et al. found no relationship between hyperkalemia and TMP-SMX when used concomitantly with corticosteroids [13]. Conversely, Mohan et al. reported that co-administration of prednisolone and TMP-SMX increased the risk of hyperkalemia, likely due to the catabolic effects of glucocorticoid therapy [18]. In our study, pulse-dose corticosteroids combined with TMP-SMX significantly increased hyperkalemia. Additionally this effect wasn’t found as an independent risk factor. Further randomized controlled trials are needed to clarify this issue.

Polderman and Girbes highlighted renal complications associated with piperacillin-tazobactam in ICU patients, including severe electrolyte imbalances. They noted that preexisting tubular dysfunction could be exacerbated by piperacillin-tazobactam, leading to electrolyte disturbances even in patients with normal baseline creatinine levels [19]. Our data revealed a significant hyponatremic effect with piperacillin-tazobactam or acyclovir. Although piperacillin-tazobactam has been previously associated with hypernatremia [15], we hypothesize that its renal side effects, when combined with TMP-SMX, may contribute to significant electrolyte disturbances.

An analysis of adverse events related to piperacillin-tazobactam using the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) identified associations with abnormal liver function, hepatocellular injury, drug-induced liver injury, cholestasis, and hepatic failure [20]. However, in our study, piperacillin-tazobactam was associated with a significant decrease in ALT/AST levels. Wu et al. investigated the clinical efficacy of piperacillin-tazobactam in cirrhotic patients with spontaneous bacterial peritonitis and similarly reported a significant reduction in ALT/AST levels [21]. We hypothesize that this effect may be attributed to the therapeutic action of piperacillin-tazobactam in treating underlying infections. We found, hyperbilirubinemia has been linked to hematological diseases, the use of teicoplanin-azole antifungals, and pulse-dose corticosteroids. Patients with hematological conditions are inherently more susceptible to hyperbilirubinemia [22]. Therefore, we suspect that trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX)-induced hyperbilirubinemia may be exacerbated in these individuals. However, due to the limited number of cases, the observed associations may be coincidental. It should be noted that elevated bilirubin levels observed in critically ill patients may not solely reflect the pharmacologic effects of TMP-SMX. These abnormalities can also result from sepsis-related hepatic dysfunction.

We were surprised to find that neither vancomycin nor colistimethate were associated with an increase in sCr levels, suggesting that these drugs do not enhance the nephrotoxic effects of TMP-SMX. Additionally, while piperacillin-tazobactam, teicoplanin, and TMP-SMX have been reported to cause thrombocytopenia [23], our study did not observe any potentiating effects when these drugs were used concomitantly TMP-SMX. However, the combination of colistin-echinocandins with TMP-SMX was associated with thrombocytopenia as an independent risk factor. An increased risk of thrombocytopenia has been reported with colistimethate, direct myelosupression and immun-mediated platelet destruction have been considered as the underlying mechanisms [24]. Echinocandins, on the other hand, have been linked to thrombocytopenia and coagulation dysfunction [25]. We hypothesize that combining these drugs with TMP-SMX may increase the risk of thrombocytopenia. Further studies are needed to evaluate these drug combinations in relation to thrombocytopenia. Moreover, anemia was significantly associated with COVID-19 and colistimethate in patients receiving TMP-SMX, while these factors weren’t found as independent risk factors for anemia. While aplastic anemia and hemolytic anemia have been reported with TMP-SMX [1, 2], we found that this adverse reaction was more frequent in patients with COVID-19, consistent with reports of hemolytic anemia in COVID-19 patients [26]. Additionally cytopenias observed in critically ill patients may not solely reflect the pharmacologic effects of these drugs, can also result from bone marrow suppression due to critical illness.

Hiramoto et al. investigated adverse reactions associated with prophylactic TMP-SMX in patients with musculoskeletal and rheumatic disorders, reporting an adverse reaction rate of 18.7%. Affected patients were older, had chronic kidney disease, lower lymphocyte and platelet counts, reduced albumin levels, decreased glomerular filtration rates, and elevated AST and ferritin levels [5]. In our study, focusing on an ICU patient population receiving higher doses of TMP-SMX, these characteristics were more prevalent, resulting in a higher AR rate of 88.33%. Both studies categorized adverse reactions by severity. Hiramoto et al. observed liver dysfunction (29.3%), thrombocytopenia (19.5%), serum creatinine elevation (15.9%), hyperkalemia (14.6%), and hyponatremia (11%), with grade 3 or higher adverse reactions including liver dysfunction, thrombocytopenia, and hyperkalemia [5]. Our study reported higher rates of these adverse reactions, likely due to the ICU setting and the administration of higher TMP-SMX doses.

Fraser et al. reportedthat TMP-SMX therapy duration, dose, age, and sex were not associated with acute kidney injury, which aligns with our findings. However, they reported that hypertension, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease were linked to acute kidney injury [27]. In our study, we observed a significant association between cardiovascular disease and sCr elevation in patients receiving TMP-SMX therapy. Additionally sCr elevation was found to be an independent risk factor for mortality. In a study by Fraser et al., acute kidney injury (AKI) was found to be resolve after the discontinuation of TMP-SMX therapy [27]. In contrast, in our study AKI was identified as an independent risk factor for mortality among patients receiving TMP-SMX therapy. Moreover Antoniou et al. reported that hyperkalemia in older patients treated TMP-SMX was associated with an increased risk of mortality [9]. However, in our study, no significant association was found between mortality and TMP-SMX related-hyperkalemia. This differences may have stemmed from variations in study desing and patient populations.

In our study, NSAIDs (1.7%) and ACE inhibitors or ARBs (4.2%) were rarely administered, and no patients received spironolactone, eliminating the possibility of interactions with these drugs. Previous studies examining the interaction between TMP-SMX and these medications reported a higher incidence of hyperkalemia [8, 9, 16, 28].

The most frequently co-administered drugs with TMP-SMX in our study were corticosteroids, low-molecular-weight heparins, carbapenems, and teicoplanin. No significant associations were found between corticosteroids, low-molecular-weight heparins, or carbapenems and adverse reactions, suggesting that these drugs can be safely used alongside TMP-SMX. However, we observed a correlation between teicoplanin and ALT/AST elevation, indicating the need for hepatic function monitoring when these drugs are used together. In previous studies the hepatotoxicity of teicoplanin was demonstrated; however, the exact mechanism remains unknown [29, 30].

In a study conducted in a transplantation unit, TMP-SMX prophylaxis was frequently administered. Researchers investigated the interaction between cyclosporine and TMP-SMX and found that this combination was safe [31]. Similarly, in our study, no significant interaction was observed between calcineurin inhibitors and TMP-SMX in terms of adverse reactions, aligning with the findings of the aforementioned study.

The interactions between TMP-SMX and several drugs, including ACE inhibitors, potassium-sparing diuretics, phenytoin, warfarin, and methotrexate, have been reviewed in the literature, emphasizing the importance of assessing the benefit-risk ratio when these drugs are co-administered with TMP-SMX [32]. In our study, we analyzed multiple drug combinations with TMP-SMX. This data provides new insights, as interactions related to TMP-SMX adverse reactions in ICU settings have not been extensively studied before.

In an ICU study conducted over two years, 51 patients were analyzed for TMP-SMX-related hyperkalemia, with an incidence rate of 49% occurring at an average of 6.2 days. In our study, the incidence was 39.9%, with a median onset of 9 days. Among ICU patients receiving TMP-SMX therapy, the primary causes of admission were respiratory conditions (72.5%) and infections (9.8%), whereas in our study, these rates were 59.2% and 17.5%, respectively. The indication rate for Stenotrophomonas maltophilia infection was 27.5% in the previous study, compared to 22.5% in our study. The average duration of therapy was 10.9 days in the previous study and 9 days in our study. Similar to our findings, age was significantly higher in patients with hyperkalemia. However, a key difference was that in the previous study, hyperkalemia was associated with mortality but not with cardiovascular or renal diseases, whereas in our study, the opposite was observed. Additionally, the reported mortality rate in that study was 59%, whereas we found it to be 56.66% [13].

Although the adverse reaction profile of TMP-SMX is well known, there is no comprehensive study included intensive care patients. This is the first comprehensive analysis of 5 years of data of intensive care medicine on patients with TMP-SMX therapy. The adverse effects of TMP-SMX were evaluated in the context of its interactions with other intensive care medications and diseases.

In conclusion, sCr elevation should be taken into account because of the risk of mortality in patietns with TMP-SMX therapy in ICU. Cardiovascular disease, hematological diseases, pulse dose corticosteroids, teicoplanin, acyclovir, piperacillin-tazobactam or colistimethate sodium/echinocandin carry risk regarding adverse reaction profile of TMP-SMX in ICU patients. Physicians and pharmacists should pay attention to patients on TMP-SMX therapy with those risk factors. In the future, the studies regarding drug interactions with TMP-SMX should be widened beyond renin-angiotensin system inhibitors and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists.

Our study has certain limitations, including its single-center, retrospective design and the absence of a control group for adverse reaction comparison. Many other factors may also affect the occurrence of these adverse events, which may not have been taken into consideration. For example renal replacement therapy, septic shock, vasopressor use, body weight, treatment discontinuation or switching to alternative agents were not included due to conflicting data in the retrospective electronic records. However, it also has notable strengths. We noted pre-and post- ICU scores (GCS, APACHE, SOFA) and analyzed their relationship with adverse effects. We comprehensively evaluated various adverse reactions associated with TMP-SMX and assessed these reactions in conjunction with other medications. To our knowledge, this is the first study to provide an extensive analysis of TMP-SMX-related adverse reactions in an ICU setting.

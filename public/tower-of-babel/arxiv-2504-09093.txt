Boundary measures of holomorphic functions on the imaginary domain
Shigeru Yamagami

Abstract
. In connection with the Herglotz-Nevanlinna integral representation of so-called Pick functions, we introduce the notion of boundary measure of holomorphic functions on the imaginary domain and elucidate some of basic properties.

Introduction

In our previous paper
[
4
]
, we investigated supplementary results to the classical integral representation theorem of Herglotz, Riesz
and Nevanlinna on holomorphic endofunctions of the upper half plane.
As an integral transformation, it is closely related to the Cauchy-Stieltjes transform and we know a satisfactory characterization of
the transform of a positive measure as a holomorphic function on the upper half plane.
Although the transform itself is applicable to complex measures, its description as a holomorphic function seems somewhat awkward.
We have however a pragmatic characterization in terms of existence of boundary measures if its support is not full (
[
9
]
),
which was one of motivations in
[
4
]
.

Here we shall extend it to boundary measures of any support under the assumption of some estimates on the boundary behaviour of holomorphic functions, which is in fact necessary for the existence of boundary measure.

To deal with the case of full support, we need to work on several (at least two) local coordinates to describe boundary limits.
The main problem there is the compatibility of locally defined boundary measures and we here impose localized boundary estimates
to check the compatibility of measures arising from local coordinates.
We then restrict ourselves to the most simple (and seemingly most interesting) choice of local coordinates, i.e.,
the original one
z
and its inversion
\widetilde{z}=-1/z
,
and show that the associated local measures are related to each other under the inversion
and the joined measure on the whole boundary turns out to fulfill the integral representation.

During its formulation, it turns out that the assumption on boundary behavior enables us to capture boundary limits
as distributions of mild singularity (such as Cauchy’s principal value) and
the problem of coordinate transformation is soloved first within boundary distributions.
Boundary measures are then worked out as a result of relevant facts on boundary distributions.

The author is grateful to M. Nagisa and M. Uchiyama for fruitful conversations, which were big impetuses to the present work.

1.
Representing Measures

Let
\overline{\text{\ym C}}=\text{\ym C}\sqcup\{\infty\}
be the Riemann sphere and
\overline{\text{\ym R}}=\text{\ym R}\sqcup\{\infty\}

be the extended real line in
\overline{\text{\ym C}}
.
For a holomorphic function
\varphi(w)
of
w\in\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
,
a complex Radon measure
\lambda
on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
is called the
representing measure
of
\varphi
if it satisfies

\varphi(w)=\frac{\varphi(i)+\varphi(-i)}{2}+\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\frac{1+sw}{s-w}\,\lambda(ds)\quad(w\in\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}).

The representing measure, if any exists, is unique
because
\{(1+sw)/(s-w);w\in\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}\}
is total in the space
C(\overline{\text{\ym R}})
of continuous functions on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
.
Related to the representing measure, given a holomorphic function
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}\setminus(\text{\ym R}\sqcup K)

with
K
a compact subset of
\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
,
the
boundary support

[\varphi]
of
\varphi
is defined to be the complement in
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
of an open subset

\{c\in\overline{\text{\ym R}};\text{$\varphi(z)$ is continuously extended to a neighborhood of $c$ in $\overline{\text{\ym C}}$}\}.

Note that
[\varphi]
is a support in the sense of hyperfunction, which turns out to be the support of the representing measure.

The existence of representing measure is a strong condition on holomorphic functions.

Example 1.1
.

A holomorphic function
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
is extended to to an entire function on
ℂ
if and only if
[\varphi]=\{\infty\}
.
As for an entire function, the representing measure exists if and only if it is an affine function.

By the holomorphic change-of-variable

z=\frac{i-w}{i+w},\quad w=i\frac{1-z}{1+z}

of
\overline{\text{\ym C}}
,
the upper half plane
\text{\ym C}_{+}=\{w\in\text{\ym C};\text{Im}\,w>0\}
is bijectively mapped onto the unit disk

and, under the correspondence
\phi(z)=-i\varphi(w)
, we have the disk version of representing measure:
Let
\text{\ym T}=\{z\in\text{\ym C};|z|=1\}
be the boundary of

. A holomorphic function

on
\overline{\text{\ym C}}\setminus\text{\ym T}

admits the representing measure if there exists a complex Radon measure

on
𝕋

\phi(z)=\frac{\phi(0)+\phi(\infty)}{2}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\text{\ym T}}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}\,\mu(d\zeta).

In view of the Jordan decomposition on signed measures and the Herglotz-Riesz integral representation,
functions having representing measures are exactly linear combinations of positive ones (so-called Pick functions on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
).

\text{Im}\,\phi(0)=0\iff\phi(0)+\phi(\infty)=0

In that case (i.e.,

\phi_{\pm}^{*}=\phi_{\pm}

\text{Re}\,\phi(z)\leq 0

Moreover, the representing measure

\oint f(t)\,\mu_{\pm}(dt)=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\oint f(t)\text{Re}\,\phi_{\pm}(re^{it})\,dt

for each

𝕋

\mu_{r}(dt)=\Bigl(\text{Re}\,\phi_{+}(re^{it})-\text{Re}\,\phi_{-}(re^{it})\Bigr)\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\phi(re^{it})-\phi(r^{-1}e^{it})\Bigr)\,dt

\mu
as

.

2.
Boundary Limits by Circles

Now reverse the process and suppose that the signed measure
\mu_{r}
on
𝕋
associated with
\phi=\phi^{*}
converges
as a linear functional on
C(\text{\ym T})
to a signed Radon measure
\mu
when
r\uparrow 1
.

Then
\mu
is the representing measure of
\phi
;
letting
\phi_{\mu}=\phi_{+}-\phi_{-}
, we need to show
\phi=\phi_{\mu}
.
Since
2\mu(dt)=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\bigl(\phi_{\mu}(re^{it})-\phi_{\mu}(r^{-1}e^{it})\bigr)\,dt
as observed above,
replacing
\phi
with
\phi-\phi_{\mu}
, we may assume that
\mu=0
and the problem is to show that
\phi=0

under the extra assumption
\text{Im}\,\phi(0)=0
.

Though this can be deduced easily from analogous facts on harmonic functions (see
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
for example),
we shall here present a direct proof by modifying arguments in
[
9
, Appendix A]
.

Regard
\mu_{r}
as a bounded linear functional on
C(\text{\ym T})
so that the assumption
\mu=0
implies

\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\mu_{r}(f)=0
for
f\in C(\text{\ym T})
. Since
\mu_{r}(f)
is continuous in
0<r<1
, this implies that

\mu_{r}(f)
is extended to a continuous function of
0<r\leq 1
so that
\mu_{1}(f)=0
.
By the Banach-Steinhaus theorem, the functional norm
\|\mu_{r}\|
is then bounded in
0<r<1
.
Notice also that

\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\phi(re^{it})=\phi(0),\quad\lim_{r\uparrow\infty}\phi(re^{it})=-\lim_{r\uparrow\infty}\overline{\phi(r^{-1}e^{it})}=-\overline{\phi(0)}.

Given
f\in C(\text{\ym T})
, the rotational average (convolution) defined by a periodic integral

\phi_{f}(z)=\oint_{0}^{2\pi}f(t)\phi(ze^{it})\,dt

is a holomorphic function of
z\in\overline{\text{\ym C}}\setminus\text{\ym T}
satisfying
\phi_{f}^{*}=\phi_{\overline{f}}
.
By using translation
(T_{\theta}f)(t)=f(t-\theta)
and the polar form
z=re^{i\theta}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{r}(T_{\theta}f)

\displaystyle=\oint f(t-\theta)\Bigl(\phi(re^{it})-\phi(r^{-1}e^{it})\Bigr)\,dt

\displaystyle=\oint f(t)\Bigl(\phi(re^{i\theta}e^{it})-\phi(r^{-1}e^{i\theta}e^{it})\Bigr)\,dt

\displaystyle=\phi_{f}(z)-\phi_{f}(1/\overline{z}).

Since
T_{\theta}f
is norm-continuous in
\theta
and
\phi_{f}(z)
is continuous in
z\in\overline{\text{\ym C}}\setminus\text{\ym T}
,
the norm boundedness of
\mu_{r}
(
0<r<1
) implies that
\mu_{r}(T_{\theta}f)
is extended to a continuous function of
r\in(0,1]

and
\theta\in\text{\ym R}
in such a way that
\mu_{1}(T_{\theta}f)=0
(
\theta\in\text{\ym R}
).

Thus the harmonic function
\phi_{f}(z)-\phi_{f}(1/\overline{z})
of
z\in\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym T}
is continuously extended to
\overline{\text{\ym C}}

so that it vanishes on
𝕋
, whence it vanishes identically by the maximum principle on its real and imaginary parts.

By choosing approximate delta functions for
f
and taking its limit,
we have
\phi(z)=\phi(1/\overline{z})
, which together with
\phi=\phi^{*}
implies that
\phi(z)
is an imaginary constant,
i.e.,
\phi(z)=\text{Im}\,f(0)=0
.

In this way the representing measure of
\phi
is expressed as the limit of an approximating measure
\text{Re}\,\phi(re^{it})\,dt

associated with an inner circle
|z|=r<1
in
D
(the inversion formula of disk version).
In other words, the representing measure of
\phi=\phi^{*}
exists if and only if
the functional limit
\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\text{Re}\phi(re^{it})\,dt
exists as a signed Radon measure on
C(\text{\ym T})
with respect to
the angle parameter
e^{it}
of
𝕋
.

By the decomposition
\phi=(\phi+\phi^{*})/2+i(\phi-\phi^{*})/2i
, the following is now in our hands.

Theorem 2.1
.

A holomorphic function
\phi
on
\overline{\text{\ym C}}\setminus\text{\ym T}

admits a representing measure on
𝕋
if and only if

\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\frac{1}{2}(\phi(re^{it})-\phi(e^{it}/r))\,dt

exists as a weak* limit of complex Radon measures on
C(\text{\ym T})
.

Moreover, if this is the case, the representing measure of
\phi
is given by the above limit.

4.
Boundary Distributions

To begin with, let the inner circle
z=re^{it}
(
-\pi\leq t\leq\pi

\pm\pi
identified
in accordance with

in

\frac{1-r}{1+r}\leq\text{Im}\,w_{r}(t)\leq\frac{1+r}{1-r}

is located at

\frac{i}{2}\left(\frac{1-r}{1+r}+\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)=i\frac{1+r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}-\frac{1-r}{1+r}\right)=\frac{2r}{1-r^{2}}.

In terms of a new variable

we have

Remark that
e^{it}=(1+is)/(1-is)

Here the notation

indicates that it is an

) traces the circle

so that

Lemma 4.6

(i) For

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d\varphi_{t}(h)}{dt}\right|_{t=\delta}

exist as distributions. Then we have

\int_{\text{\ym R}}f(\tan\frac{t}{2})\phi(e^{it\mp 0})\,dt=-i\int_{\text{\ym R}}f(s)\varphi(s\pm i0)\frac{2}{1+s^{2}}\,ds

for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\text{\ym R})
.

This corollary identifies the most part
-\pi<t<\pi
of the periodic distribution
\phi(e^{it\mp 0})
on
t\in\text{\ym R}
as
a distribution
\varphi(s\pm i0)
on
s\in\text{\ym R}
.

To recover the exceptional point
\pi+2\pi\text{\ym Z}
, we notice that a rotation of the
z
-plane by an angle
\theta
is described in the
w
-plane by

\displaystyle i\frac{1-e^{i\theta}z}{1+e^{i\theta}z}

\displaystyle=i\frac{i+w-e^{i\theta}(i+w)z}{i+w+e^{i\theta}(i+w)z}=i\frac{i+w-e^{i\theta}(i-w)}{i+w+e^{i\theta}(i-w)}

\displaystyle=i\frac{(e^{-i\theta/2}+e^{i\theta/2})w+i(e^{-i\theta/2}-e^{i\theta/2})}{(e^{-i\theta/2}-e^{i\theta/2})w+i(e^{-i\theta/2}+e^{i\theta/2})}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\theta/2)&\sin(\theta/2)\\
-\sin(\theta/2)&\cos(\theta/2)\end{pmatrix}.w,

i.e., the Möbius transform by the rotation matrix of angle
-\theta/2
.
Here the dot indicates the matric action on
w\in\overline{\text{\ym C}}
by the Möbius transformation.
We shall return to this in a later section.

For the choice
\theta=\pm\pi
, the involutive map
z\mapsto-z
in the
z
-plane is then transferred to the inversion

w\mapsto-1/w
in the
w
-plane and we have

\phi(-z)=-i\varphi(-1/w)
so that
the distribution
\phi(e^{i(t+\pi)\mp 0})
shifted by the half period
\pi
is related to the inversion

\widetilde{\varphi}(w)\equiv\varphi(-1/w)
by

\lim_{r\to 1\mp 0}\int_{-\beta}^{\beta}f(\tan\frac{t}{2})\phi(re^{i(t+\pi)})\,dt=-i\lim_{y\to\pm 0}\int_{-b}^{b}f(s)\widetilde{\varphi}(s+iy)\frac{2}{1+s^{2}}\,ds

for
b>0
and
f\in C_{0}^{2}[-b,b]
with
0<\beta\equiv 2\arctan b<\pi
.

If we rewrite the left hand side as

\lim_{r\to 1\mp 0}\int_{\pi-\beta}^{\pi+\beta}f(-\cot t)\phi(re^{it})\,dt

and notice the fact that
-\cot t
is the inversion of
\tan t
,
the inversion of
\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+s^{2}}\widetilde{\varphi}(s\pm i0)
of
-b<s<b
,
which is a distribution on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}\setminus[-1/b,1/b]
, turns out to describe
\phi(e^{it\mp 0})
for
\pi-\beta<t<\pi+\beta
.

In particular, when
ab>1
, on the overlapped region
(-a,a)\setminus[-1/b,1/b]
,
both
\frac{2}{1+s^{2}}\varphi(s\pm i0)
and the inversion of
\frac{2}{1+s^{2}}\widetilde{\varphi}(s\pm i0)

give rise to the same distribution
i\phi(e^{it\mp 0})
on
(\beta-\pi,\pi-\beta)\setminus[-\alpha,\alpha]
, whence

\frac{2}{1+s^{2}}\varphi(s\pm i0)
coincides with the inversion of
\frac{2}{1+s^{2}}\widetilde{\varphi}(s\pm i0)

on the region
(-a,a)\setminus[-1/b,1/b]
.

Now rather long discussions are finally summarized as follows:

Theorem 4.11
.

Under the assumption of Corollary
4.10
, the following hold:

(i)

The inversion
\widetilde{\varphi}(w)=\varphi(-1/w)
shares the property with
\varphi
.

(ii)

Limits
\varphi(x\pm i0)=\lim_{y\to\pm 0}\varphi(x+iy)
and

exist as distributions on

.

(iii)

On

,
\varphi(x\pm i0)/(1+x^{2})
and

are related by

\int_{\text{\ym R}^{\times}}f(x)\frac{\varphi(x\pm i0)}{1+x^{2}}\,dx=\int_{\text{\ym R}^{\times}}\widetilde{f}(x)\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}(x\pm i0)}{1+x^{2}}\,dx.

The joined distribution on

,
is related with the Möbius transform of the boundary distribution

on

5.
Boundary Measures

In this part, we consider a holomorphic function
\varphi

defined on a boundary neighborhood of an open subset
S
of
\overline{\text{\ym R}}=\text{\ym R}\sqcup\{\infty\}
,
which is assumed to behave simply on
S
and therefore
limits
\varphi(x\pm i0)
exist as distributions on
S
.

We say that
\varphi
admits a boundary measure on
S
if
the distribution

\frac{1}{2\pi i(1+x^{2})}\Bigl(\varphi(x+i0)-\varphi(x-i0)\Bigr)

is given by a complex Radon measure
\lambda
(called the
boundary measure
of
\varphi
) on
S
, i.e.,

\lim_{y\to+0}\int_{S}f(s)\frac{\varphi(s+iy)-\varphi(s-iy)}{2\pi i(1+s^{2})}\,ds=\int_{S}f(s)\,\lambda(ds)

for any
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(S)
.

When
\varphi
admits a boundary measure
\lambda
,
let
\varphi_{\lambda}
be a holomorphic function of
z\in\overline{\text{\ym C}}\setminus\overline{S}\supset\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}

(
\overline{S}
being the closure of
S
in
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
)
defined by

\varphi_{\lambda}(z)=\int_{S}\frac{1+sz}{s-z}\,\lambda(ds).

Then
\varphi_{\lambda}(\pm i)=\pm i\lambda(S)
and
\lambda
is a representing measure of
\varphi_{\lambda}
,
whence
\varphi_{\lambda}
behaves simply on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
and the inversion formula for
\lambda
implies

\varphi(x+i0)-\varphi(x-i0)=\varphi_{\lambda}(x+i0)-\varphi_{\lambda}(x-i0)

as distributions on
S
.
Since each term in this identity has a meaning as a distribution on
S
, we can rewrite it as

\varphi(x+i0)-\varphi_{\lambda}(x+i0)=\varphi(x-i0)-\varphi_{\lambda}(x-i0)

and apply the edge-of-the-wedge theorem (
[
7
, Theorem B]
) to see that
\psi\equiv\varphi-\varphi_{\lambda}
is holomorphic on
S
and satisfies

\psi(x+iy)-\psi(x-iy)\to 0
(
y\to 0
) locally uniformly on
S
.
Consequently, the convergence

\lim_{y\to+0}\frac{\varphi(x+iy)-\varphi(x-iy)}{2\pi i(1+x^{2})}dx=\lim_{y\to+0}\frac{\varphi_{\lambda}(x+iy)-\varphi_{\lambda}(x-iy)}{2\pi i(1+x^{2})}dx

has a meaning as a Radon measure on
S
and results in the initial boundary measure
\lambda
, i.e.,
the boundary measure relation

\lim_{y\to+0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\frac{\varphi(x+iy)-\varphi(x-iy)}{2\pi i(1+x^{2})}dx=\int_{S}f(x)\,\lambda(dx)

remains valid for
f\in C_{c}(S)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

A holomorphic function
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
admits a representing measure if and only if the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

\varphi
behaves simply on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
.

(ii)

We can find an open subset
S
of
ℝ
so that
\text{\ym R}\setminus S
is a finite set
and
\varphi
admits a boundary measure on
S
, i.e., there is a complex Radon measure
\lambda_{S}
in
S
satisfying

\lim_{y\to+0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\frac{\varphi(x+iy)-\varphi(x-iy)}{2\pi i(1+x^{2})}dx=\int_{S}f(x)\,\lambda_{S}(dx)

for any
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(S)
.

Moreover, if this is the case and if we define a holomorphic function
\varphi_{S}
on
\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
by

\varphi_{S}(z)=\int_{S}\frac{1+sz}{s-z}\,\lambda_{S}(ds),

then
\varphi-\varphi_{S}
is holomorphically extended to
(\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R})\sqcup S

with each point in
\Sigma\equiv(\text{\ym R}\setminus S)\sqcup\{\infty\}
being at most a simple pole of
\varphi-\varphi_{S}
,
the representing measure
\lambda
of
\varphi
coincides with
\lambda_{S}
on
S
and
the atomic mass of
\lambda
at
\sigma\in\Sigma
is given by

\lambda[\sigma]=\begin{cases}-\rho(\sigma)/(1+\sigma^{2})&(\sigma\in\text{\ym R})\\
\rho(\sigma)&(\sigma=\infty)\end{cases},

where
\rho(\sigma)
is the residue of
\varphi-\varphi_{S}
at
\sigma\in\Sigma
, i.e.,

\varphi(z)-\varphi_{S}(z)-\frac{\rho(\sigma)}{z-\sigma}

is holomorphic near
z=\sigma
if
\sigma\in\text{\ym R}\setminus S
, whereas

\varphi(1/z)-\varphi_{S}(1/z)-\frac{\rho(\sigma)}{z}

is holomorphic near
z=0
if
\sigma=\infty
.

Proof.

Clearly the listed conditions are necessary.

To see the reverse implication, we recall that
\varphi-\varphi_{S}
is holomorphic on
S
, whence
\varphi-\varphi_{S}

has at most finitely many singularities in
\Sigma
as a function on
\overline{\text{\ym C}}
.

Since both
\varphi
and
\varphi_{S}
behaves simply on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
,
each point
\sigma\in\Sigma
must be at most a simple pole of
\varphi-\varphi_{S}
and

\varphi(z)-\varphi_{S}(z)-\sum_{\sigma\in\text{\ym R}\setminus S}\frac{\rho(\sigma)}{z-\sigma}-\rho(\infty)z

is constant as a holomorphic function on the whole sphere
\overline{\text{\ym C}}
.
Thus, in terms of the measure
\lambda
on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
described in the theorem, we obtain

\varphi(z)=\text{const}+\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\frac{1+sz}{s-z}\,\lambda(ds)

and
\lambda
is a representing measure of
\varphi
.
∎

Remark 2
.

Under the existence of a representing measure, the convergence to the boundary measure
\lambda_{S}
in (ii) holds for any
continuous function
f
on
S
vanishing on the boundary of
S
(see Appendix C).

6.
Möbius Transform of Representing Measures

In accordance with the previous article
[
4
]
,
a holomorphic map of
\text{\ym C}_{+}
into itself is called an
endofunction
on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
,
though it is customary to be named after Carathéodory, Herglotz, Nevanlinna, Pick, Riesz and Schur.
An endofunction is obviously extended to a holomorphic function
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
so that
\varphi=\varphi^{*}

(reflection-symmetric extension)
and the classic theorem of G. Herglotz and F. Riesz says that
the reflection-symmetric extension of an endofunction admits a positive representing measure
(simply referred to as the representing measure of an endofunction).
Möbius transforms of positive representing measures are investigated in
[
4
]
.
Here we shall describe the results for complex representing measures.

Start with some notation. Given an invertible matrix

A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

of complex entries, the associated Möbius transform of
z\in\overline{\text{\ym C}}
is denoted by

A.z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}.

An endofunction is called an
autofunction
if it maps
\text{\ym C}_{+}
bijectively onto
\text{\ym C}_{+}
.
Let
\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
be the set of endofunctions and
{\rm Aut}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
be the set of autofunctions.
Remark that
\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
is a unital semigroup by composition and
{\rm Aut}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
is a subgroup of
\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
.
Compared to
\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
,
{\rm Aut}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
is very restrictive and in fact realized by the Möbius transform of matrices in
\text{SL}(2,\text{\ym R})
.
Turning attention to
\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
instead of
\text{\ym C}_{+}
, the Möbius transform is extended to
\text{GL}(2,\text{\ym R})
:
Half planes
\text{\ym C}_{\pm}
are preserved individually or switched according to the sign of determinant.

Given a Radon measure
\lambda
on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
and a matrix
A
in
\text{GL}(2,\text{\ym R})
,
let
\lambda^{A}
be the measure on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
defined by

\lambda^{A}(dt)=\frac{1}{\det(A)}\frac{(at+b)^{2}+(ct+d)^{2}}{1+t^{2}}\lambda(A.dt).

Here
\lambda(A.dt)
denotes the transferred measure of
\lambda(ds)
under the change of variables
s=A.t
.

Now we add the following formula for a record, which is an immediate modification of the one in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

For
A\in\text{GL}(2,\text{\ym R})
,
a holomorphic function
\varphi(z)
of
z\in\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
admits a representing measure
\lambda

if and only if so does the holomorphic function
\varphi(A.z)
of
z\in\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
.

Moreover, if this is the case, the representing measure of
\varphi(A.z)
is given by
\lambda^{A}
.

Notice that, for a positive
\lambda
,
\lambda^{A}
is positive or negative according to the sign of
\det(A)
.

7.
Examples

In this section, we illustrate the results in the previous section by some of simple examples.
At the outset, recall the following fact.

Lemma 7.1
.

If a holomorphic function
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
admits a representing measure
\lambda
on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
,
then

i(1+x^{2})\lambda[x]=\lim_{y\to 0}y\varphi(x+iy)

for
x\in\text{\ym R}
and

i\lambda[\infty]=\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(iy)}{y}.

Here
\lambda[x]
denotes the atomic mass
\lambda(\{x\})
at
x\in\overline{\text{\ym R}}
.

Proof.

This is well-known for endofunctions (and their reflection-symmetric extensions) (cf.
[
4
, Appendix A]
,
[
6
, §1.12]
)
and the general case follows from this special one as a linear combination of endofunctions.

In fact, from the formula

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to+0}y\frac{\varphi(x+iy)+\varphi^{*}(x+iy)}{2}

\displaystyle=i(1+x^{2})\frac{\lambda+\overline{\lambda}}{2}[x],

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to+0}y\frac{\varphi(x+iy)-\varphi^{*}(x+iy)}{2i}

\displaystyle=i(1+x^{2})\frac{\lambda-\overline{\lambda}}{2i}[x]

for reflection-symmetric and antisymmetric parts of
\varphi
, one sees

\lim_{y\to+0}y\varphi(x+iy)=i(1+x^{2})\lambda[x],\quad\lim_{y\to+0}y\varphi(x-iy)=-i(1+x^{2})\lambda[x],

which are combined into the single formula

\lim_{y\to 0}y\varphi(x+iy)=i(1+x^{2})\lambda[x].

Similarly for the atomic mass
\lambda[\infty]
at
\infty
.
∎

Example 7.2
.

Recall that an endofunction
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
gives another endofunction
-1/\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
.
Consequently
1/\varphi
admits a representing measure, simply the negative of the representing measure for
-1/\varphi
.
If this observation is applied to
\varphi(z)=\tan z
, we see that

\tan z+\cot z=\frac{2}{\sin(2z)}

has a representing measure.

In fact,

\tan(x+iy)=\frac{1}{i}\frac{e^{-y+ix}-e^{y-ix}}{e^{-y+ix}+e^{y-ix}}=\frac{2\sin(2x)+i(e^{2y}-e^{-2y})}{(e^{y}-e^{-y})^{2}+4\cos^{2}x}

reveals that
\tan z
is an endofunction.
From
\tan i=i(e-e^{-1})/(e+e^{-1})
, the real constant for
\tan z
is zero.
The representing measure
\lambda
of tangent function is then atomic in view of

[\varphi]=\{\pi n/2;n\in 1+2\text{\ym Z}\}\sqcup\{\infty\}
. The atomic mass at
\infty
is zero because of

\lim_{y\to+\infty}(\tan(iy))/y=0
, whereas

\lim_{z\to\pi n/2}(\pi n/2-z)\tan z=1
shows that the atomic mass at
\pi n/2
is
1/(1+(\pi n/2)^{2})
.
Consequently we have an absolutely convergent series expansion

\tan z=\sum_{n\in 1+2\text{\ym Z}}\frac{1+\pi nz/2}{\pi n/2-z}\frac{1}{1+(\pi n/2)^{2}}

for
z\in\text{\ym C}\setminus(\pi/2)(1+2\text{\ym Z})
.

Similarly the boundary support of
\cot z
is
(\pi\text{\ym Z})\sqcup\{\infty\}
and
the atomic mass at
n\pi
given by
-1/(1+(\pi n)^{2})
with no atomic mass at
\infty
.

Thus the boundary support of
1/\sin(2z)
is
(\pi/2)\text{\ym Z}\sqcup\{\infty\}
with the atomic mass at
\pi n/2
equal to

(-1)^{n+1}/(1+(\pi n/2)^{2})
: We have an absolutely convergent expression

\frac{2}{\sin(2z)}=\sum_{n\in\text{\ym Z}}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{1+(\pi n/2)^{2}}\frac{1+\pi nz/2}{\pi n/2-z}

for
z\not\in(\pi/2)\text{\ym Z}
.

By scaling and then shifting, both
1/\sin z
and
1/\cos z
have representing measures.
As a conclusion, trigonometric functions admit representing measures other than entire ones,
\cos z
and
\sin z
.

Example 7.3

(cf.
[
2
,
5
]
)
.

Consider a rational function
\varphi
which is holomorphic on
\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
, i.e., all poles of
\varphi
are real
(including
\infty
). If
\varphi
has a representing measure, it behaves simply at any point of the boundary
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
,
whence all poles of
\varphi
must be simple. Then the partial fraction decomposition of

takes the form

\varphi(z)=az+b+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{c_{j}}{s_{j}-z},

where

is the set of real poles (excluding
\infty
) and
a
,
b
,
c_{j}\not=0
are complex numbers.
If we compare this with the representing measure relation

\varphi(z)=\frac{\varphi(i)+\varphi(-i)}{2}+\lambda[\infty]z+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1+s_{j}z}{s_{j}-z}\lambda[s_{j}],

we have

c_{j}=(1+s_{j}^{2})\lambda[s_{j}],\quad a=\lambda[\infty],\quad b=\frac{\varphi(i)+\varphi(-i)}{2}-\sum_{j}s_{j}\lambda[s_{j}],

which in turn determines
\lambda[s_{j}]
,

and
(\varphi(i)+\varphi(-i))/2
uniquely from

,

and
(c_{j})
so that

In that case,
\deg\varphi\in\{-1,0,1\}
and

\deg\varphi=\begin{cases}1&(\lambda[\infty]\not=0)\\
0&\text{($\lambda[\infty]=0$ and $b\not=0$)}\\
-1&\text{($\lambda[\infty]=0$ and $b=0$)}\end{cases}.

In the following examples, the principal branch of logarithm is used and denoted by
\log z
:

\log(re^{i\theta})=\log r+i\theta
for

and
-\pi<\theta<\pi
.
For

, the
p

is then defined by

(

).
The logarithm is real in the sense that
\log^{*}=\log
and
the power function satisfies
(z^{p})^{*}=z^{\overline{p}}
, i.e.,
\overline{z^{p}}=(\overline{z})^{\overline{p}}
.
For the reciprocal,
\log(1/z)=-\log z
and
(1/z)^{p}=1/z^{p}
.
For the inversion
-1/z
, the boundary support is changed from

to
[0,\infty]

\log(-1/z)=\begin{cases}i\pi-\log z&(0<\theta<\pi)\\
-i\pi-\log z&(-\pi<\theta<0)\end{cases}

Example 7.4
.

z^{p}
(
p\in\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym Z}
).
The boundary support is
[-\infty,0]
and the power function

behaves simply except for

.
In view of

yz^{p}=r^{1+p}e^{ip\theta}\sin\theta\quad(z=x+iy),

it behaves simply at

y(-1/z)^{p}=e^{\pm i\pi p}r^{1-p}e^{-ip\theta}\sin\theta

according to
\pm y>0

.

if and only if
-1\leq\text{Re}\,p\leq 1
.

.

\frac{e^{iy}}{y}=r^{iq}e^{-\pi q/2}

circulates on the circle of radius

,

As a conclusion, the power function

(

.

We reproduce here Vladimirov’s estimate on the boundary behavior of holomorphic endofunctions on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
.
The original estimate is about functions of multiple variables and the single variable case below is therefore considerably simplified.

Theorem A.1

(Vladimirov)
.

For an endofunction
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
,

|\varphi(z)|\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2}\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{\text{Im}\,z}|\varphi(i)|.

Proof.

Let
\phi(z)
be the accompanied function on
D
satisfying
\text{Re}\,\phi(z)>0
(
z\in D
).
Then

\psi=\frac{\phi-i\text{Im}\,\phi(0)-\text{Re}\,\phi(0)}{\phi-i\text{Im}\,\phi(0)+\text{Re}\,\phi(0)}

is a normalized endofunction on
D
and the Schwarz lemma gives
|\psi(z)|<|z|
(
z\in D
).
Thus

\phi-i\text{Im}\,\phi(0)=(\text{Re}\,\phi(0))(1+\psi)/(1-\psi)
satisfies

\text{Re}\,\phi(0)\frac{1-|z|}{1+|z|}\leq|\phi(z)-i\text{Im}\,\phi(0)|\leq\text{Re}\,\phi(0)\frac{1+|\psi(z)|}{1-|\psi(z)|}\leq\text{Re}\,\phi(0)\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}

for
z\in D
and hence

|\phi(z)|\leq|\text{Im}\,\phi(0)|+\text{Re}\,\phi(0)\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}\leq|\phi(0)|\left(1+\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}\right)=\frac{2|\phi(0)|}{1-|z|}.

Consequently, in view of
\varphi(w)=i\phi(z)
and
z=(i-w)/(i+w)
, we have

|\varphi(w)|\leq\frac{2|\varphi(i)|}{1-|\frac{i-w}{i+w}|}

for
w\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
.

The problem is therefore reduced to estimating the denominator. Let
w=u+iv
(
u\in\text{\ym R}
,
v>0
).

1-\left|\frac{i-w}{i+w}\right|=\frac{|i+w|^{2}-|i-w|^{2}}{|i+w|^{2}+|i^{2}-w^{2}|}=\frac{4v}{|i+w|^{2}+|1+w^{2}|}.

In terms of the polar form
w=re^{i\theta}
,

|i+w|^{2}+|1+w^{2}|=1+r^{2}+2r\sin\theta+\sqrt{(1+r^{2})^{2}-4r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}

Since

F(t)=t+\sqrt{(r+r^{-1})^{2}/4-t^{2}}

for
0\leq t\leq(r+r^{-1})/2
is maximized at
t=(r+r^{-1})/2\sqrt{2}
with the maximum

F\left(\frac{r+r^{-1}}{2\sqrt{2}}\right)=\frac{r+r^{-1}}{\sqrt{2}},

we have
|i+w|^{2}+|1+w^{2}|\leq(1+\sqrt{2})(1+r^{2})
.
∎

We shall describe the inversion formula for representing measures on the real line
ℝ
.
Let
\lambda
be the representing measure of a holomorphic function
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}

\varphi(z)=\frac{\varphi(i)+\varphi(-i)}{2}+\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\frac{1+sz}{s-z}\,\lambda(ds).

Then
\lambda

\varphi(z)

for which we clarify its meaning.

In the above integral representation,

\frac{1+sz}{s-z}-\frac{1+s\overline{z}}{s-\overline{z}}=\frac{1+s^{2}}{s-z}-\frac{1+s^{2}}{s-\overline{z}}=(1+s^{2})\frac{z-\overline{z}}{|s-z|^{2}}=\frac{2iy(1+s^{2})}{(s-x)^{2}+y^{2}}

and hence

\displaystyle\varphi(x+iy)-\varphi(x-iy)

\displaystyle=2iy\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\frac{1+s^{2}}{(s-x)^{2}+y^{2}}\,\lambda(ds)

\displaystyle=2iy\lambda[\infty]+2iy\int_{\text{\ym R}}\frac{1+s^{2}}{(s-x)^{2}+y^{2}}\,\lambda(ds)

Thus the functional norm of the approximating measure is bounded by

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{|\varphi(x+iy)-\varphi(x-iy)|}{1+x^{2}}\,dx\\
\leq 2\pi y|\lambda[\infty]|+\int_{{\text{\ym R}}}|\lambda|(ds)\,(1+s^{2})\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2y}{(1+x^{2})((s-x)^{2}+y^{2})}\,dx,

where the inner integral in the second term is residue-calculated as

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2y}{(1+x^{2})((s-x)^{2}+y^{2})}\,dx

\displaystyle=\oint_{z=i}\frac{2y}{(1+z^{2})((s-z)^{2}+y^{2})}\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad+\oint_{z=s+iy}\frac{2y}{(1+z^{2})((s-z)^{2}+y^{2})}\,dz

\displaystyle=\frac{2y}{2i((s-i)^{2}+y^{2})}\oint_{z=i}\frac{dz}{z-i}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2y}{(1+(s+iy)^{2})2iy}\oint_{z=s+iy}\frac{dz}{z-s-iy}

\displaystyle=2\pi\left(\frac{y}{(s-i)^{2}+y^{2}}+\frac{1}{1+(s+iy)^{2}}\right).

\displaystyle\frac{y}{(s-i)^{2}+y^{2}}+\frac{1}{1+(s+iy)^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2i}\left(\frac{1}{s-i-iy}-\frac{1}{s-i+iy}\right)+\frac{1}{2i}\left(\frac{1}{s+iy-i}-\frac{1}{s+iy+i}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\pi(y+1)\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{\text{\ym R}\setminus[-L,L]}\frac{s^{2}+1}{s^{2}+(y+1)^{2}}\,|\lambda|(ds)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\pi(y+1)\|f\|_{\infty}\,|\lambda|(\text{\ym R}\setminus[-L,L])\leq\pi(y+1)\|f\|_{\infty}\epsilon.

To handle the remaining part of the integral, recall that the convolution with the approximate delta functions

\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{y}{x^{2}+y^{2}}

of
x\in\text{\ym R}
approximate the identity operator on
C_{0}(\text{\ym R})
as
y\to+0
, which enables us to
choose
\delta>0
small enough to satisfy

\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(x)}{1+x^{2}}\frac{y}{\pi((s-x)^{2}+y^{2})}\,dx-\frac{f(s)}{1+s^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{1+L^{2}}

for
s\in\text{\ym R}
and
0<y<\delta
. Then

\left|\int_{[-L,L]}\lambda(ds)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\frac{1+s^{2}}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\frac{y}{(s-x)^{2}+y^{2}}\,dx-\int_{[-L,L]}f(s)\,\lambda(ds)\right|\\
\leq\epsilon\int_{[-L,L]}\frac{1+s^{2}}{1+L^{2}}\,|\lambda|(ds)\leq\epsilon|\lambda|([-L,L])\leq\epsilon|\lambda|(\text{\ym R}).

Putting all these together, we have

\left|\int_{\text{\ym R}}\lambda(ds)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\frac{1+s^{2}}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\frac{y}{(s-x)^{2}+y^{2}}\,dx-\int_{\text{\ym R}}f(s)\,\lambda(ds)\right|\\
\leq\|f\|_{\infty}(y+2)\epsilon+|\lambda|(\text{\ym R})\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we have proved the following.

Theorem C.2
.

Under the same assumption in Proposition
C.1
, we have for
f\in C(\overline{\text{\ym R}})

\lim_{y\to+0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\frac{\varphi(x+iy)-\varphi(x-iy)}{2\pi i(1+x^{2})}\,dx=\int_{\text{\ym R}}f(s)\,\lambda(ds).

Notice that the point
\infty
is missing in the right integral and therefore
the atomic mass
\lambda[\infty]
can not be recoverred in this form.

Completing the Picture for the Skolem Problem on Order-4 Linear Recurrence Sequences


Abstract
For almost a century, the decidability of the Skolem Problem, that is, the problem of determining whether a given linear recurrence sequence (LRS) has a zero term, has remained open. A breakthrough in the 1980s established that the Skolem Problem is decidable for algebraic LRS of order at most 3, and real algebraic LRS of order at most 4. However, for general algebraic LRS of order 4 decidability has remained open. Our main contribution in this paper is to prove decidability for this last case, i.e. we show that the Skolem Problem is decidable for all algebraic LRS of order at most 4.

Introduction

Given a ring
R
, a
linear recurrence sequence
over
R
(

-LRS for short) is a sequence
{\bm{u}}=\langle u_{n}\rangle_{n=0}^{\infty}
of elements of
R

\displaystyle u_{n+d}=a_{d-1}u_{n+d-1}+\dots+a_{0}u_{n}

(1)

where
a_{0},\dots,a_{d-1}\in R
and
a_{0}\neq 0
. If

of
{\bm{u}}
. The celebrated Skolem-Mahler-Lech theorem
[

states that the zero set
\{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}:u_{n}=0\}
of an

is an integral domain of characteristic zero.

Unfortunately, all known proofs of the Skolem-Mahler-Lech theorem are ineffective - there is no known method to compute the zeroes of a given algebraic LRS (that is, a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-LRS, where
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
is the field of algebraic numbers). Equivalently there is currently no known method to decide the problem of whether a given arbitrary
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-LRS has a zero. This problem is known as the Skolem Problem.

So far, only special cases are known, and the general problem has remained open for around 90 years. The Skolem Problem is closely connected with many topics in theoretical computer science and other areas, including loop termination
[
18
,
2
]
, formal power series (e.g.
[
6
, Section 6.4]
), matrix semigroups
[
5
]
, stochastic systems
[
1
,
4
]
and control theory
[
9
]
.

Substantial work has been done on the decidability of the Skolem problem. The breakthrough papers
[
22
,
20
]
established decidability for algebraic LRS of order at most 3 and real algebraic LRS of order at most 4. To date, this is the state of the art on the Skolem Problem; even in the simplest case of integer sequences, decidability is not yet known for order-5 LRS.

More recently, decidability has been shown to hold subject to some additional assumptions. Lipton

[

establish decidability for

\mathbb{Z}

, assuming the

Our contribution in this paper is to extend the results of

2.1
Algebraic numbers and heights

Let
K
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, i.e. a number field. Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
denote the ring of algebraic integers in

of

\mathcal{O}_{K}
-submodule of

is an ideal of

. The fractional ideals of

form a group under multiplication. We have a unique factorisation theorem for fractional ideals
[
17
, p. 22]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Unique Factorisation Theorem)
.

Any fractional ideal
I
of
K
has a unique decomposition

\displaystyle I=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{n_{i}}

where
t\in\mathbb{N}
, each
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a prime ideal and
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
we define the
valuation

v_{\mathfrak{p}}:K\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
by
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)=\infty
and for non-zero
a\in K
, we define
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)
to be the exponent of
\mathfrak{p}
in the prime decomposition of the fractional ideal
a\mathcal{O}_{K}
given by Theorem
2.1
. If
p\in\mathbb{Z}
is a prime, we may define the
p
-adic absolute value on
\mathbb{Q}
by
|x|_{p}=p^{-v_{p}(x)}
. By Ostrowski’s theorem, any (non-trivial) absolute value on
\mathbb{Q}
is equivalent to the “usual” absolute value
|\cdot|
or a
p
-adic absolute value
|\cdot|_{p}
for some prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, if
|\cdot|_{v}
is a (non-trivial) absolute value on
K
, then its restriction to
\mathbb{Q}
is either equivalent to the ”usual” absolute value
|\cdot|
(in which case
|\cdot|_{v}
is
Archimedean
) or some
p
-adic absolute value
|\cdot|_{p}
(in which case
|\cdot|_{v}
is

and we write

). We normalise
|\cdot|_{v}
such that, when restricting to
\mathbb{Q}
,
|\cdot|_{v}
coincides with the usual absolute value
|\cdot|
or
|\cdot|_{p}
for some
p
; in the Archimedean case we have
|x|_{v}=x
for all

, and in the non-Archimedean case
|p|_{v}=p^{-1}
. Denote the set of non-trivial absolute values on
K
, normalised as above, by

.

If
v\in M_{K}
is Archimedean, it either corresponds to a real embedding
K\overset{\sigma}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}
or a pair of complex embeddings
K\overset{\sigma,\overline{\sigma}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{C}
. In both cases, under the normalisation above we have
|x|_{v}=|x|_{\sigma}=|\sigma(x)|
. Define the
local degree

d_{v}
of

as
d_{v}=1
or

if

corresponds to a real embedding or a pair of complex conjugate embeddings respectively.

If
v\in M_{K}
is non-Archimedean, and
v\mid p
, then
v
corresponds to a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
dividing
p
. Under the normalisation above, we have
|x|_{v}=|x|_{\mathfrak{p}}=p^{-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)/e_{\mathfrak{p}}}
where
e_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the ramification index; the exponent of
\mathfrak{p}
in the prime ideal decomposition of
p
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Define
d_{v}=[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
where
K_{v},\mathbb{Q}_{p}
are the completions of
K,\mathbb{Q}
with respect to
|\cdot|_{v},|\cdot|_{p}
respectively.

If
x\in K
and
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=D
, we define the
absolute logarithmic height
of
x
(or just

for short) as

\displaystyle h(x):=\frac{1}{D}\sum_{v\in M_{K}}d_{v}\log\max\{|x|_{v},1\}.

It is known that the height depends only on
x
, not the choice of number field
K
. The height satisfies the following properties, found in
[
24
, Sections 3.2, 3.5]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For any algebraic numbers
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
with
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and any absolute value
|\cdot|_{v}
on
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{1})
we have

1.

h(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2})\leq h(\alpha_{1})+h(\alpha_{2})
,

2.

h(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\leq\log 2+h(\alpha_{1})+h(\alpha_{2})
,

3.

h(\alpha_{1}^{n})=|n|h(\alpha_{1})
.

4.

-[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{1}):\mathbb{Q}]h(\alpha_{1})\leq\log|\alpha_{1}|_{v}\leq[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{1}):\mathbb{Q}]h(\alpha_{1})

2.2
Linear recurrence sequences

Consider a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-LRS
{\bm{u}}
of order
d
satisfying (
1
); define its
characteristic polynomial
as
g(X)=X^{d}-a_{d-1}X^{d-1}-\dots-a_{0}
, and call the distinct roots
\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}\}
of

the
characteristic roots
of
{\bm{u}}
. If
\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}
is a root of unity for some

, call
{\bm{u}}
degenerate, otherwise say
{\bm{u}}
is non-degenerate. By
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
, any
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-LRS may be effectively decomposed into a finite number of subsequences which are all either identically zero or non-degenerate. Therefore, the Skolem Problem reduces to the non-degenerate case.

For
K
a number field containing
{\bm{u}}
and its characteristic roots, given any absolute value
|\cdot|_{v}
on
K

for all

then we say

is

. It is well known that

admits an
exponential polynomial
representation

\displaystyle u_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}P_{i}(n)\lambda_{i}^{n}

where each

is a polynomial with degree one less than the multiplicity of
\lambda_{i}
as a root of

.

3.1
Linear forms in logarithms

The fundamental result used in these papers is Baker’s theorem on linear forms in logarithms
[
3
]
, and its analogue for non-Archimedean absolute values. This yields a lower bound on expressions of the form
|\alpha_{1}^{b_{1}}\dots\alpha_{s}^{b_{s}}-1|_{v}
for algebraic numbers
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}
and integers
b_{1},\dots,b_{s}
such that
\alpha_{1}^{b_{1}}\dots\alpha_{s}^{b_{s}}-1\neq 0
. The original inequality, proven for the usual absolute value on
\mathbb{C}
, has since been improved upon, with various forms existing in the literature. For
|\cdot|_{v}
the usual absolute value on
\mathbb{C}
, we present the following formulation by Matveev
[
16
, Corollary 2.3]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Matveev)
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}
be non-zero complex algebraic numbers contained in a number field of degree
D
and
\log\alpha_{1},\dots,\log\alpha_{s}
some determination of their logarithms. Let
b_{1},\dots,b_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that

\displaystyle\Lambda=b_{1}\log\alpha_{1},+\dots+b_{s}\log\alpha_{s}\neq 0\,.

Further, let
A_{1},\dots,A_{s},B
be real numbers such that

\displaystyle A_{j}\geq\max\{Dh(\alpha_{j}),|\log\alpha_{j}|,0.16\}\,\,\,(j=1,\dots,s)

\displaystyle B=\max\{|b_{1}|,\dots,|b_{s}|\}\,.

Then

\displaystyle|\Lambda|\geq\exp\left(-2^{6s+20}D^{2}A_{1}\dots A_{s}(1+\log D)(1+\log B)\right)\,.

For
|\cdot|_{v}
non-Archimedean, we have the following result of Yu
[
25
, Theorem 1]
.

The original papers
[

]
and

]

]

Theorem 3.2

Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}
be non-zero algebraic numbers contained in a number field

3.2
The MSTV class

The crux of the approach in
[
20
,
22
]
is to use Baker’s theory of linear forms in logarithms to prove lower bounds on exponential sums. For an excellent expository note which elaborates on all the details of the results of
[
20
,
22
]
, see
[
7
]
. Here, we intend only to give a brief overview. In particular, the following results are key.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2}
and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be algebraic numbers in a number field
K
of degree
D
such that
\alpha_{1}/\alpha_{2}
is not a root of unity. Let
h=\max\{h(b_{1}),h(b_{2})\}
, and let
|\cdot|_{v}
be any non-trivial absolute value (let the underlying prime be
p
if this absolute value is non-Archimedean). Then there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
depending effectively on
p,D,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
such that for all

we have

\displaystyle|b_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n}+b_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n}|_{v}\geq|\alpha_{1}|_{v}^{n}e^{-C_{2}(h+1)\log n}

(2)

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}
and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
be algebraic numbers in a number field
K
of degree
D
such that no quotient
\alpha_{i}/\alpha_{j}
is a root of unity for

. Let
h=\max\{h(b_{1}),h(b_{2}),h(b_{3})\}
, and let
|\cdot|_{v}
be an Archimedean absolute value. Suppose also that
|\alpha_{1}|_{v}=|\alpha_{2}|_{v}=|\alpha_{3}|_{v}
. Then there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
depending effectively on
p,D,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
such that for all
n>C_{1}(h+1)
we have

\displaystyle|b_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n}+b_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n}+b_{3}\alpha_{3}^{n}|_{v}\geq|\alpha_{1}|_{v}^{n}e^{-C_{2}(h+1)\log n}

(3)

In the case of Archimedean absolute value, Theorem

is
[

Proof 3.5

(Proof of Theorem

.

Let

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}}\right)^{n}=-\frac{b_{1}}{b_{2}}\,.

Let
\alpha=\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1}
and

. Then by Proposition
2.2
item
3
,

\displaystyle n=\frac{h(b)}{h(\alpha)}\,.

(4)

Now, a result of Voutier
[
23

shows that for any algebraic number
\beta
of degree

that is not a root of unity, we have

\displaystyle h(\beta)\geq\frac{2}{D^{\prime}(\log(3D^{\prime}))^{3}}\,.

(5)

Applying (

, and using items

from Proposition
2.2
on

\displaystyle n\leq D(\log(3D))^{3}h\,.

Therefore, for

\displaystyle\Xi=b^{-1}\alpha^{n}-1\neq 0\,.

Note that if
\left|b^{-1}\alpha^{n}\right|_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 1

|\Xi|_{\mathfrak{p}}=1
and (

) follows easily. So we only need to consider when
\left|b^{-1}\alpha^{n}\right|_{\mathfrak{p}}=1

\left|\alpha\right|_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 1

\log|\alpha|_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0

\displaystyle n=\frac{\log\left|b\right|_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\log|\alpha|_{\mathfrak{p}}}\,.

Since

, either
|\alpha|_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq p^{1/e_{\mathfrak{p}}}

, meaning

. Thus, with items

, and

\displaystyle n\leq\frac{e_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\log p}Dh(b)\leq\frac{2e_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\log p}Dh

Otherwise,

\displaystyle v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Xi)

\displaystyle\leq C\max\left\{h(b),\log p\right\}\max\left\{h(\alpha),\log p\right\}\log n

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(h+1)\log n

\displaystyle|b_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n}+b_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n}|_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle\geq|b_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n}|_{\mathfrak{p}}|\Xi|_{\mathfrak{p}}

We exhibit how Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
lead to decidability for a large class of LRS.

Definition 3.6

(MSTV class)
.

The Mignotte-Shorey-Tijdeman-Vereshchagin (MSTV) class consists of all
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-LRS that have at most 3 dominant roots with respect to some Archimedean absolute value, or at most 2 dominant roots with respect to some non-Archimedean absolute value.

Note that this terminology was introduced in
[
14
]
for
\mathbb{Z}
-LRS, our definition subsumes the one given there.

Theorem 3.7
.

The Skolem problem is decidable for all non-degenerate LRS in the MSTV class.

Proof 3.8
.

Let
{\bm{u}}
be a non-degenerate LRS in the MSTV class with distinct characteristic roots
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}
. For some absolute value
|\cdot|_{v}
and for some
1\leq r\leq 3
we have

\displaystyle|\lambda_{1}|_{v}=\dots=|\lambda_{r}|_{v}>|\lambda_{r+1}|_{v}\geq\dots\geq|\lambda_{s}|_{v}\,.

As noted in Section
2.2
,
{\bm{u}}
admits the exponential polynomial representation

\displaystyle u_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}P_{i}(n)\lambda_{i}^{n}

(7)

for polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{s}
with algebraic coefficients. When
|\cdot|_{v}
is any non-trivial absolute value and
r=2
, or when
|\cdot|_{v}
is Archimedean and
r=3
, we apply Theorem
3.3
or
3.4
to
\sum_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}(n)\lambda_{i}^{n}
to get computable constants
C_{1},C_{2}\geq 0
such that for all
n>C_{1}(h+1)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}(n)\lambda_{i}^{n}\right|_{v}\geq|\lambda_{1}|_{v}^{n}e^{-C_{2}\left(\underset{i}{\max}\{h(P_{i}(n))\}+1\right)\log n}\,.

Note also that for
r=1
such an inequality is trivial. Moreover, by repeated application of items
1
,
2
of Proposition
2.2
, we have
\underset{i}{\max}\{h(P_{i}(n))\}\leq C_{3}\log n
for
n\geq 2
and for some effective constant
C_{3}>0
depending on the coefficients of each
P_{i}
. Thus, we have for all
n>C_{1}(h+1)

\displaystyle|u_{n}|\geq\left|\sum_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}(n)\lambda_{i}^{n}\right|_{v}-\left|\sum_{i=r+1}^{s}P_{i}(n)\lambda_{i}^{n}\right|_{v}\geq|\lambda_{1}|_{v}^{n}e^{-C_{4}(\log n)^{2}}-C_{5}n^{C_{6}}|\lambda_{r+1}|_{v}^{n}

(8)

for some effective
C_{4},C_{5},C_{6}>0
. Since
|\lambda_{1}|_{v}>|\lambda_{r+1}|_{v}
, the first term of the right-hand side of (
8
) grows faster than the second and one may therefore compute
C_{7}>0
such that
|u_{n}|>0
for all
n>C_{7}
. Decidability of the Skolem Problem immediately follows as then one only needs to check whether
u_{n}=0
for each
0\leq n\leq C_{7}
.

In
[
14
]
it is written that the Skolem Problem is known to be decidable for
all

\mathbb{Z}
-LRS in the MSTV class. This is not true. This is because Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
only apply to
non-degenerate
LRS in the MSTV class and, while every LRS can be written as the interleaving of non-degenerate subsequences, crucially the MSTV class is not closed under taking subsequences. Indeed, let
{\bm{v}}
be an arbitrary non-degenerate LRS, and let
\lambda
be an algebraic number whose modulus is larger than any characteristic root of
{\bm{v}}
. Defining

\displaystyle u_{n}=\lambda^{n}-(-\lambda)^{n}+v_{n}\,,

we see that
{\bm{u}}
has 2 dominant roots in modulus, yet
u_{2n}=v_{2n}
. Since
{\bm{v}}
was arbitrary, we see
\langle u_{2n}\rangle_{n=0}^{\infty}
need not lie in the MSTV class.

Fortunately, it is easy to see that the main results of
[
14
]
(decidability of low order reversible sequences, and order 5 sequences assuming the Skolem Conjecture) is not affected by this oversight.

4
The Skolem Problem at order 4

Our aim is to prove the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

The Skolem problem is decidable for all algebraic LRS with at most
4
distinct characteristic roots. In particular, decidability holds for all algebraic LRS of order
d\leq 4
.

For this we require the following basic result of Kronecker
[
12
]
:

Theorem 4.2

(Kronecker)
.

Let
\alpha
be a non-zero algebraic integer. If all Galois conjugates of
\alpha
lie in the unit disc
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}
then
\alpha
is a root of unity.

Theorem
4.1
follows immediately from the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Given algebraic numbers
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}
, let
K
be the Galois closure of
\mathbb{Q}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4})
. If
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}
are all dominant with respect to every Archimedean absolute value on
K
, and for every non-Archimedean absolute value
|\cdot|_{v}
at least 3 of the
\lambda_{i}
are dominant with respect to
|\cdot|_{v}
, then for all
i\neq j
, we have
\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}
is a root of unity.

Proof 4.4
.

Since all the
\lambda_{i}
are dominant with respect to every Archimedean absolute value on
K
, we have

\displaystyle|\sigma(\lambda_{1})|=|\sigma(\lambda_{2})|=|\sigma(\lambda_{3})|=|\sigma(\lambda_{4})|

(9)

for all
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
from which it follows (taking
\sigma
to be the identity) that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\overline{\lambda_{1}}=\lambda_{2}\overline{\lambda_{2}}=\lambda_{3}\overline{\lambda_{3}}=\lambda_{4}\overline{\lambda_{4}}

and so for every prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
we have

\displaystyle|\lambda_{1}|_{\mathfrak{p}}|\overline{\lambda_{1}}|_{\mathfrak{p}}=|\lambda_{2}|_{\mathfrak{p}}|\overline{\lambda_{2}}|_{\mathfrak{p}}=|\lambda_{3}|_{\mathfrak{p}}|\overline{\lambda_{3}}|_{\mathfrak{p}}=|\lambda_{4}|_{\mathfrak{p}}|\overline{\lambda_{4}}|_{\mathfrak{p}}.

(10)

For a fixed prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
, by assumption we have (up to relabelling)

\displaystyle|\lambda_{1}|_{\mathfrak{p}}=|\lambda_{2}|_{\mathfrak{p}}=|\lambda_{3}|_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq|\lambda_{4}|_{\mathfrak{p}}.

(11)

Suppose that exactly 3 of the
\lambda_{i}
were dominant with respect to
|\cdot|_{\mathfrak{p}}
, so the inequality in (
11
) was strict. Then together with (
10
) this forces

\displaystyle|\overline{\lambda_{4}}|_{\mathfrak{p}}>|\overline{\lambda_{1}}|_{\mathfrak{p}}=|\overline{\lambda_{2}}|_{\mathfrak{p}}=|\overline{\lambda_{3}}|_{\mathfrak{p}}.

But the absolute value
|\cdot|_{v}
defined by
|x|_{v}=|\overline{x}|_{\mathfrak{p}}
is non-Archimedean, and
\lambda_{4}
is dominant with respect to
|\cdot|_{v}
, contradicting our assumption that at least 3 of the
\lambda_{i}
are dominant with respect to every non-Archimedean absolute value. So in fact the inequality in (
11
) must be an equality, i.e. for every prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
we have

\displaystyle|\lambda_{1}|_{\mathfrak{p}}=|\lambda_{2}|_{\mathfrak{p}}=|\lambda_{3}|_{\mathfrak{p}}=|\lambda_{4}|_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Thus
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda_{i})=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda_{j})
for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
, so by Theorem
2.1
we have equality of fractional ideals
\lambda_{i}\mathcal{O}_{K}=\lambda_{j}\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Therefore
\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}}\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
and so
\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}}
is an algebraic integer (in fact, a unit of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
) for each
i\neq j
. Also, (
9
) implies that
\left|\sigma\left(\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}\right)\right|=1
for all
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
. Therefore, we may apply Kronecker’s Theorem
4.2
to conclude that
\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}
is a root of unity.

Proof 4.5

(Proof of Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
{\bm{u}}
be an algebraic LRS with at most 4 distinct characteristic roots. As noted in Section
2.2
, one can effectively split
{\bm{u}}
into non-degenerate subsequences, and since each subsequence also has at most 4 distinct characteristic roots, we may assume
{\bm{u}}
is non-degenerate. By Lemma
4.3
applied to the characteristic roots of
{\bm{u}}
and by non-degeneracy of
{\bm{u}}
we conclude that
{\bm{u}}
lies in the MSTV class and so has decidable Skolem problem by Theorem
3.7
.

Remark 4.6
.

Explicitly, what we have shown is that every non-degenerate algebraic LRS with at most 4 distinct characteristic roots is in the MSTV class.

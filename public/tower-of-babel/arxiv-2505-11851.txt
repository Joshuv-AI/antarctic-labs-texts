Sajin Vincent A.W, Aniruddha Deshmukh, Vijay Kumar Sohani

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Singular integral operators have played a fundamental role in harmonic analysis, with applications extending to partial differential equations and geometric measure theory (see, for instance
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
13
]
and the references therein). A classical example is the Hilbert transform in one dimension given by

Hf(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\text{ p.v.}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(x-y)}{y}dy.

Analogously, for an appropriate curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Hilbert transform of
f
along
\gamma
is defined as

Tf(x):=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon\to 0\\
N\to\infty\end{subarray}}\int_{\epsilon<|t|<N}f(x-\gamma(t))\frac{dt}{t},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Fabes and Riviére (
[
5
]
) were motivated to study the Hilbert transform along
\gamma
,
in their effort to extend Calderón and Zygmund’s method of rotation. In
[
10
]
, Nagel, Riviere and Wainger proved that
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\infty
, when
\gamma(t)=(|t|^{\alpha_{1}}\text{sgn}\,t,\ldots,|t|^{\alpha_{n}}\text{sgn}\,t),\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

These singular integral operators have, “the order of the singularity” at
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as same as the dimension of the universe. A hypersingular integral is an integral with a strong singularity whose order is higher than the dimension of the set of integration. In various problems related to function theory and integral equations, hypersingular integrals have emerged as a powerful analytical tool. For an exposition of this topic we refer the readers to
[
11
]
. One of the applications was given by E. Stein in
[
12
]
who characterized the space of Bessel potentials of order
0<\alpha<2
in terms of convergence of hypersingular integrals.

As an example, let
\Gamma(t)=(t,|t|^{k})
or
(t,|t|^{k}\text{sgn}\,t),\,t\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\geq 2
. Then the hypersingular integral operator

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}f(x):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\epsilon<|t|<1}f(x-\Gamma(t))\frac{dt}{t|t|^{\alpha}},\quad\alpha>0,

is not bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
due to the worsened singularity at the origin. In order to balance this, one introduces an oscillatory term
e^{-2\pi i|t|^{-\beta}},\,t\in\mathbb{R}
in the kernel of
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
and considers the operator

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}f(x):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\epsilon<|t|<1}f(x-\Gamma(t))e^{-2\pi i|t|^{-\beta}}\frac{dt}{t|t|^{\alpha}},\quad\text{with }\alpha,\beta>0.

The problem of
L^{p}
-boundedness of the operator
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
was taken up by Chandarana in
[
2
]
. In this paper, Chandarana showed that

•

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
if and only if
\beta\geq 3\alpha
, and

•

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
when
\beta>3\alpha
and

1+\frac{3\alpha(\beta+1)}{\beta(\beta+1)+(\beta-3\alpha)}<p<\frac{\beta(\beta+1)+(\beta-3\alpha)}{3\alpha(\beta+1)}+1.

Chandarana’s result was improved and extended to higher dimensions by Chen
et al.
in
[
3
]
, where the authors considered the curve
\Gamma(t)=(\theta_{1}|t|^{p_{1}},\theta_{2}|t|^{p_{2}},\ldots,\theta_{n}|t|^{p_{n}})
or

\text{sgn}(t)(\theta_{1}|t|^{p_{1}},\theta_{2}|t|^{p_{2}},\ldots,\theta_{n}|t|^{p_{n}})
for
t\in\mathbb{R},\,\theta=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p_{i}>0
. They proved the following:

•

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
whenever
\beta>(n+1)\alpha
and
\frac{2\beta}{2\beta-(n+1)\alpha}<p<\frac{2\beta}{(n+1)\alpha}
, and

•

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
when
\beta=(n+1)\alpha
.

In
[
8
]
, Lee
et al.
consider a hypersingular operator along a hypersurface. In particular they consider the surface
\Gamma(t)=(t,|t|^{k})
, where
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
and
k\geq 1
, and define the operator

\mathcal{R}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-\Gamma(t))\frac{e^{-2\pi i|t|^{-\beta}}\Omega(t)}{|t|^{\alpha+n}}dt,

where the kernel
\Omega(t)/|t|^{n}
is homogeneous of degree
-n
, infinitely differentiable except possibly at the origin and
\int_{|t|=1}\Omega(t)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(t)=0.
Then, for
n\geq 2
, the authors of
[
8
]
proved that

•

\mathcal{R}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
if
\beta>2\alpha>0
, and

•

\mathcal{R}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
for
\alpha/\beta<1/p<(\beta-\alpha)/\beta
and
\beta>2\alpha.

Motivated from the above discussion, we aim to extend Lee’s result to more general surfaces of the form
\Gamma(t)=(t,\varphi(t))
, where
\varphi
is a radial
C^{3}
function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, we assume certain growth conditions on the function
\varphi
. Throughout the article, we assume that
\varphi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following:

(1)

\varphi(0)=0
and
\varphi^{\prime}(r)\cdot\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)>0,\forall r\in(0,\infty)
,

(2)

There exist
k_{2}\geq k_{1}>0
such that for all
r\in(0,\infty)
,

(1.1)

k_{1}\leq\frac{r\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\varphi^{\prime}(r)}\leq k_{2},

and

(3)

There exist
k_{3}>0
such that for all
r\in(0,\infty)

(1.2)

\left|\frac{r\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)}{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)}\right|\leq k_{3}.

Remark 1.1
.

The requirement of
\varphi(0)=0
is not necessary since we deal with norm estimates. However, this assumption is convenient and useful in our calculations.

Remark 1.2
.

Apart from the monomials considered by Lee
et al.
in
[
8
]
one non-trivial example for
\varphi
is
\varphi(r)=r^{2}e^{-r}\sinh(r).
Other such examples would be
\varphi(r)=r^{\gamma_{1}}(1-e^{-r})^{\gamma_{2}},
for
\gamma_{1}>1
and
\gamma_{2}\geq 0
;
\varphi(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{l}a_{j}r^{1+\gamma_{j}},\,a_{j}>0,\,\gamma_{j}>0
for
j=1,\ldots,l.

Remark 1.3
.

We observe some properties about the surface
\Gamma(t)
. Initially, the scalar curvature of
\Gamma(t)
is negative (particularly for
|x|<k_{1}
). But it becomes positive for
|x|>k_{3}.
For large values of
|x|
, the scalar curvature,
S
is bounded between

A|x|^{2(k_{1}-1)}\leq S(x)\leq B|x|^{2(k_{2}-1)}.

Our main results are the following theorems.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\varphi
be a radial
C^{3}
function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as described above.
Let
\Gamma(t)=(t,\varphi(t)),
for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and consider the operator

(1.3)

\mathcal{R}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-\Gamma(t))\frac{e^{-2\pi i|t|^{-\beta}}\Omega(t)}{|t|^{\alpha+n}}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where the kernel
\frac{\Omega(t)}{|t|^{n}}
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

It is homogeneous of degree
-n
.

(b)

\left.\Omega\right|_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
is (uniformly) bounded.

(c)

\int_{|t|=1}\Omega(t)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(t)=0
.

Then, for
n\geq 2
, we have the following:

(1)

\mathcal{R}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
if
\beta>2\alpha>0
.

(2)

\mathcal{R}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
for
\frac{\beta}{\beta-\alpha}<p<\frac{\beta}{\alpha},
when
\beta>2\alpha.

Moving a step ahead, we also prove certain Sobolev estimates for the operator
\mathcal{R}.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be as in Theorem
1.4
. Then,

(1)

For
s\leq\frac{(\beta/2)-\alpha}{\beta+k_{3}+2},
the operator
\mathcal{R}
is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
to
L_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}).

(2)

If
0\leq s\leq\frac{\beta/2-\alpha}{\beta+k_{3}+2}=:s_{0}
and

\left(1-\frac{s}{s_{0}}\right)\frac{\alpha}{\beta}+\frac{s}{2s_{0}}<\frac{1}{p}<\left(1-\frac{s}{s_{0}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right)+\frac{s}{2s_{0}}.

Then, the operator
\mathcal{R}
is bounded from
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)
to
L^{p}_{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)
.

This article is organized as follows. In Section 2, we provide certain well-known results that are employed at various instances in our work. Next, in Section 3, we prove a couple of lemmata that are useful in proving our main results. The proofs of Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
are given in Section 4. Finally, we give a few concluding remarks in Section 5 and mention a few future directions that stem from this work.

We deal with the boundedness problem of the operator
\mathcal{R}
in this article. However, we are not much concerned about constants that might appear at various steps in the estimates. We use the symbol ‘

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall several well-known results that will be utilized in the subsequent sections. We begin by stating the Van der corput’s lemma that gives a bound for certain oscillatory integrals.

Theorem 2.1

(Van der corput’s lemma
[
14
]
)
.

Suppose
\varphi
is a real-valued and smooth function in
(a,b)
, and that
|\varphi^{(k)}(x)|\geq 1
for all
x\in(a,b)
. Then,

\left|\int_{a}^{b}e^{i\lambda\varphi(x)}\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq c_{k}\lambda^{-1/k}\left[|\psi(b)|+\int_{a}^{b}|\psi^{\prime}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\right],

holds when:

(1)

k\geq 2,
or

(2)

k=1
and
\varphi^{\prime}(x)
is monotonic.

The bound
c_{k}
is independent of
\varphi
and
\lambda.

Note that if the lower bound for
|\varphi^{(k)}(x)|
is some constant
c>0
, instead of
1
, then we have

(2.1)

\left|\int_{a}^{b}e^{i\lambda\varphi(x)}\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq c_{k}(\lambda c)^{-1/k}\left[|\psi(b)|+\int_{a}^{b}|\psi^{\prime}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\right].

We now state the mean value theorem for vector valued functions on open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Theorem 2.2

(Mean Value Theorem
[
1
]
)
.

Let
S
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and assume that
f:S\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is differentiable at each point of
S
. Let
x,y\in S
and
L(x,y)\subseteq S
, where
L(x,y)
is the line segment joining the points
x
and
y
. Then for every
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
there is a point
z\in L(x,y)
such that

a\cdot\left(f(y)-f(x)\right)=a\cdot\left(Df(z)(y-x)\right),

where
\cdot
denotes the Euclidean inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{m}.

For the integration of function with radial symmetry in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use the following decomposition of the Lebesgue measure into radial and angular components.

Theorem 2.3

(Polar decomposition in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
15
]
)
.

Any point
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
can be uniquely written as
x=r\gamma
, where
\gamma
lies on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
. We also have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r\gamma)r^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\gamma,

where
\mathrm{d}\gamma
denotes the surface measure on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Now we state an analogue of the polar decomposition over the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{C}
be measurable. Then

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(\gamma)d\gamma=\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}f(\sin\theta\omega,\cos\theta)(\sin\theta)^{n-2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}\theta.

Here
\mathrm{d}\omega
denotes the surface measure on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}.

We now state the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem which is a foundational result in harmonic analysis. It provides a powerful tool for proving boundedness of linear operators between
L^{p}
spaces.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
17
]
)
.

Suppose
T
is a linear mapping from
L^{p_{0}}+L^{p_{1}}
to
L^{q_{0}}+L^{q_{1}}
. Assume that
T
is bounded from
L^{p_{0}}
to
L^{q_{0}}
and from
L^{p_{1}}
to
L^{q_{1}}
. That is, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} \|T(f)\|_{L^{q_{0}}}&\leq M_{0}\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}}},\\
\|T(f)\|_{L^{q_{1}}}&\leq M_{1}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then
T
is bounded from
L^{p}
to
L^{q}
, with

\|T(f)\|_{L^{q}}\leq M\|f\|_{L^{p}},

whenever the pair
(p,q)
is given by

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-t}{p_{0}}+\frac{t}{p_{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-t}{q_{0}}+\frac{t}{q_{1}},

for some
0\leq t\leq 1
. Moreover, the bound
M
satisfies
M\leq M_{0}^{1-t}M_{1}^{t}.

We now proceed to introduce
L^{p}
-based Sobolev spaces that we use in Theorem
1.5

Definition 2.6

(Sobolev spaces
[
6
]
)
.

Let
s
be a real number and let
1<p<\infty
. The Sobolev space
L_{s}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as the space of all tempered distributions
u
in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the property that

((1+|\cdot|^{2})^{s/2}\widehat{u})^{\vee}

is an element of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For such distributions

we define the Sobolev norm as

\|u\|_{L_{s}^{p}}=\left\|((1+|\cdot|^{2})^{s/2}\widehat{u})^{\vee}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

|\xi_{n+1}|2^{-3l}|\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}(2^{-l}r)|
, which is a contradiction. Therefore, the result must be true.
∎

At each point in

, Lemma
3.1
gives a lower bound for one of the derivatives of

mentioned in Equations (

)–(
3.5
). In the next lemma, we show that each point in

has a neighborhood on which at least one of the derivatives of

are bounded below. These neighborhoods is lead to a partition of

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be as in Lemma
3.1
and

\epsilon_{1}=\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon}{6\beta(\beta+1)2^{\beta+2}},\frac{\epsilon}{4\beta(\beta+1)(\beta+2)2^{\beta+3}},\frac{\epsilon}{8}\right\}\text{ and }\epsilon_{2}=\frac{\epsilon}{8}.

Suppose
|g_{r}^{\prime}(r_{0},\theta_{0})|\geq\epsilon\lambda
for some
(r_{0},\theta_{0})\in[1/2,2]\times[0,\pi].

Then
|g_{r}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|\geq\frac{\epsilon\lambda}{2},
for all
(r,\theta)
satisfying

|r-r_{0}|<\epsilon_{1}
and
|\theta-\theta_{0}|<\epsilon_{2}.

The same is true for
g_{\theta}^{\prime},g_{rr}^{\prime\prime}
and
g_{\theta\theta}^{\prime\prime}.

Proof.

Suppose
|g_{r}^{\prime}(r_{0},\theta_{0})|\geq\epsilon\lambda.
Using the triangle inequality and Theorem
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle|g_{r}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|

\displaystyle\geq|g_{r}^{\prime}(r_{0},\theta_{0})|-|g_{r}^{\prime}(r,\theta)-g_{r}^{\prime}(r_{0},\theta_{0})|

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon\lambda-\left|g_{rr}^{\prime\prime}(r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})(r-r_{0})+g_{r\theta}^{\prime\prime}(r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})(\theta-\theta_{0})\right|,

for some
(r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
on the line segment joining
(r,\theta)
and
(r_{0},\theta_{0})
. Using the derivatives of
g
, given in Equations (
3.2
)–(
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle|g^{\prime}_{r}(r,\theta)|

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon\lambda-\beta(\beta+1)2^{\beta l}(r^{\prime})^{-\beta-2}|r-r_{0}|-|\xi_{n+1}||\varphi^{\prime\prime}(2^{-l}r)|2^{-2l}|r-r_{0}|

\displaystyle-2^{-l}|\xi^{\prime}||\sin(\theta^{\prime})|\theta-\theta_{0}|

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon\lambda-\beta(\beta+1)\lambda 2^{\beta+2}\epsilon_{1}-\lambda\epsilon_{1}-\lambda\epsilon_{2}\geq\frac{\epsilon\lambda}{2}.

Suppose
g_{rr}^{\prime\prime}(r_{0},\theta_{0})\geq\epsilon\lambda
. Again using the triangle inequality and Theorem
2.2
, we get

(3.15)

\displaystyle\left|g_{rr}^{\prime\prime}(r,\theta)\right|\geq\epsilon\lambda-\left|g_{rrr}^{\prime\prime\prime}(r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})(r-r_{0})+g_{rr\theta}^{\prime\prime\prime}(r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})(\theta-\theta_{0})\right|,

for some
(r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
on the line segment joining
(r,\theta)
and
(r_{0},\theta_{0})
. Note that

(3.16)

\displaystyle g_{rrr}^{\prime\prime\prime}(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=-\beta(\beta+1)(\beta+2)2^{\beta l}r^{-r-3}+2^{-3l}\xi_{n+1}\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}(2^{-l}r),

and
g_{rr\theta}^{\prime\prime\prime}=0
. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\left|g_{rr}^{\prime\prime}(r,\theta)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon\lambda-\beta(\beta+1)(\beta+2)2^{\beta+3}\lambda\epsilon_{1}-\lambda\epsilon_{2}\geq\frac{\epsilon\lambda}{2}

The result can be proved for
g_{\theta}^{\prime}
and
g_{\theta\theta}^{\prime\prime}
in a similar fashion.
∎

We are now in a position to prove our main results stated in Section

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

(1)

To prove
\mathcal{R}
is bounded on

, by Plancherel theorem, it is enough to show that the multiplier of
\mathcal{R}
, given by

m(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-2\pi i\left(\xi^{\prime}\cdot t+\xi_{n+1}\varphi(t)+|t|^{-\beta}\right)}\frac{\Omega(t)}{|t|^{\alpha+n}}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1})
and
\xi^{\prime}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}),
is uniformly bounded. Using polar decomposition on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the multiplier
m
can be written as

m(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi ih(r,\theta)}\Omega(\theta,\sigma)r^{-\alpha-1}\sin^{n-2}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\sigma,

where
h(r,\theta)=|\xi^{\prime}|r\cos(\theta)+\xi_{n+1}\varphi(r)+r^{-\beta}
. Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)
be such that
\text{supp}(\eta)\subseteq[1/2,2]
and

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta(2^{l}r)=1,\quad\text{for all }r\in(0,\infty).

For instance, one may choose

\eta(x)=\zeta(\log_{2}(x)),

where

\zeta(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{e^{\frac{1}{|x|-1}}}{e^{\frac{1}{|x|-1}}+e^{-\frac{1}{|x|}}},&\quad\text{if }|x|<1,\\
&0,&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

We define, for
l\in\mathbb{Z}
,

m_{l}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi ih(r,\theta)}\Omega(\theta,\sigma)\eta(2^{l}r)r^{-\alpha-1}\sin^{n-2}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Then
m(\xi)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}m_{l}(\xi).

Replacing
r
by
2^{-l}r
, in the definition of
m_{l}(\xi)
, we get

(4.1)

m_{l}(\xi)=2^{\alpha l}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{1/2}^{2}e^{-2\pi ig(r,\theta)}\Omega(\theta,\sigma)\sin^{n-2}(\theta)\eta(r)r^{-\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\sigma,

where
g(r,\theta)=2^{-l}|\xi^{\prime}|r\cos(\theta)+\xi_{n+1}\varphi(2^{-l}r)+2^{\beta l}r^{-\beta}
. Since
m(\xi)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}m_{l}(\xi)
, it is enough to find a bound for
m_{l}
, which is summable. We also notice that, in Equation (
4.1
), we have a uniform bound in the
\sigma
variable, namely
\left|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\right|\left\|\Omega\right\|_{\infty}
. Therefore, we focus on the double integral in
r
and
\theta
.

Let
\kappa
be a compactly supported smooth function with supp
(\kappa)\subseteq[-1,1]
and

\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\kappa\left(x+\frac{4}{3}z\right)=1,\quad x\in(-\infty,\infty).

For an example, we may choose

\kappa(x)=\frac{\psi(x)}{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi\left(x+\frac{4}{3}j\right)},\quad\text{where}

\psi(x)=\begin{cases}e^{-\frac{1}{x\left(\frac{4}{3}-x\right)}},&\text{if }x\in(0,\frac{4}{3})\\
0,&\text{otherwise }.\end{cases}

Let
J
be an index set given by
J=\{j=(j_{1},j_{2}):j_{1}\in\epsilon_{1}\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }j_{2}\in\epsilon_{2}\mathbb{Z}\}
. For
j\in J
, define

\chi_{j}(r,\theta)=\kappa\left(\frac{4(r-j_{1})}{3\epsilon_{1}}\right)\kappa\left(\frac{4(\theta-j_{2})}{3\epsilon_{2}}\right).

Then
\text{supp}\,\chi_{j}\subseteq[\frac{-3\epsilon_{1}}{4}+\epsilon_{1}j_{1},\frac{3\epsilon_{1}}{4}+\epsilon_{1}j_{1}]\times[\frac{-3\epsilon_{2}}{4}+\epsilon_{2}j_{2},\frac{3\epsilon_{2}}{4}+\epsilon_{2}j_{2}]
and in particular
\{\chi_{j}\}_{j\in J}
forms a partition of unity in
[1/2,2]\times[0,\pi]
. We remark that the number of
j
s is independent of
l
. We now define,

m_{l,j}(\xi):=2^{\alpha l}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi ig(r,\theta)}\Omega(\theta,\sigma)r^{-\alpha-1}\eta(r)\chi_{j}(r,\theta)\sin^{n-2}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\sigma.

For a fixed
j
, using Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, it is clear that for all
(r,\theta)\in\text{supp}\,(\chi_{j}),
one of
|g_{r}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|,|g_{rr}^{\prime\prime}(r,\theta)|,|g_{\theta}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|
and
|g_{\theta\theta}^{\prime\prime}(r,\theta)|
is at least as much as
\epsilon\lambda/2
. We now consider each of the cases.

Case 1:
When
j
is such that
|g_{r}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|\geq\frac{\epsilon\lambda}{2}
for all
(r,\theta)\in\text{supp}\,\chi_{j},
we use
integration by parts in the variable
r
and write

\displaystyle m_{l,j}(\xi)=\frac{2^{\alpha l}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle e^{-2\pi ig(r,\theta)}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\frac{-1}{ig_{r}^{\prime}(r,\theta)}r^{-\alpha-1}\chi_{j}(r,\theta)\eta(r)\right)\times

\displaystyle\Omega(\theta,\sigma)\sin^{n-2}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Applying the estimate
|g_{r}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|\geq\epsilon\lambda/2
to each term in the derivative, we get

\displaystyle|m_{l,j}(\xi)|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\alpha l}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{1/2}^{2}\left\{\frac{r^{-\alpha-1}}{|g_{r}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|}|\chi_{j}(r,\theta)||\eta^{\prime}(r)|\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{r^{-\alpha-1}}{|g_{r}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\chi_{j}(r,\theta)\right||\eta(r)|+\frac{1}{|g_{r}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|}(\alpha+1)r^{-\alpha-2}|\chi_{j}(r,\theta)||\eta(r)|

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{|g_{r}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|^{2}}|g_{rr}^{\prime\prime}(r,\theta)|r^{-\alpha-1}|\chi_{j}(r,\theta)||\eta(r)|\right\}|\Omega(\theta,\sigma)|\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\sigma.

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\alpha l}}{\lambda}.

Case 2:
When
j
is such that
|g_{\theta}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|\geq\epsilon\lambda/2
for all
(r,\theta)\in\text{supp}\,\chi_{j},
we use integration by parts in the variable

to get

\displaystyle m_{l,j}(\xi)=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\alpha l}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi ig(r,\theta)}r^{-\alpha-1}\eta(r)\times

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\frac{-1}{ig_{\theta}^{\prime}(r,\theta)}\chi_{j}(r,\theta)\Omega(\theta,\sigma)\sin^{n-2}(\theta)\right)\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Again applying the estimate
|g_{\theta}^{\prime}(r,\theta)|\geq\epsilon\lambda/2
to each term in the derivative we get

(4.3)

|m_{l,j}(\xi)|\lesssim\frac{2^{\alpha l}}{\lambda}.

Case 3:
Let
j
be such that
|g_{rr}^{\prime\prime}(r,\theta)|\geq\epsilon\lambda/2
for all
(r,\theta)\in\text{supp}\,\chi_{j}.
Then using Equation (
2.1
), we get

(4.4)

|m_{l.j}(\xi)|\lesssim\frac{2^{\alpha l}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.

Case 4:
Lastly, if
j
is such that
|g_{\theta\theta}^{\prime\prime}(r,\theta)|\geq\epsilon\lambda/2
, for all
(r,\theta)\in\text{supp}\,\chi_{j},
we again use Equation (
2.1
) and obtain

|m_{l.j}(\xi)|\lesssim\frac{2^{\alpha l}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.

We also have the trivial bound

(4.5)

|m_{l,j}(\xi)|\lesssim 2^{\alpha l},\text{ for all }j\in J.

Since
\lambda\geq 2^{\beta l}
, we have the following estimate.

(4.6)

|m_{l,j}(\xi)|\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &2^{(\alpha-\frac{\beta}{2})l},&\text{ if }&l\geq 0,\\
&2^{\alpha l},&\text{ if }&l<0.\end{aligned}\right.

Also, since there are only finitely many
j
’s with their number independent of

, we have

(4.7)

|m_{l}(\xi)|\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &2^{(\alpha-\frac{\beta}{2})l},&\text{ if }&l\geq 0,\\
&2^{\alpha l},&\text{ if }&l<0.\end{aligned}\right.

From Equation (
4.7
) it is clear at once that when

, the multiplier
m
is bounded and hence
\mathcal{R}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
.

(2)

We now proceed to obtain the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the operator
\mathcal{R}
. Write

\mathcal{R}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x-\Gamma(r\omega))\frac{e^{-2\pi ir^{-\beta}}\Omega(r,\omega)}{r^{\alpha+1}}\,\mathrm{d}rd\omega.

Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{l}f(x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(m_{l}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x-\Gamma(r\omega))\frac{e^{-2\pi ir^{\beta}}\Omega(r\omega))}{{r^{\alpha+1}}}\eta(2^{l}r)\,\mathrm{d}rd\omega.

This gives the estimate

(4.8)

\left\|\mathcal{R}_{l}f\right\|_{1}\lesssim 2^{\alpha l}\|f\|_{1}.

Using the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem (see Theorem
2.5
) on Equations (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we get

\left\|\mathcal{R}_{l}\right\|_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &2^{(\alpha-\beta+\beta/p)l},&\text{ if }&l\geq 0,\\
&2^{\alpha l},&\text{ if }&l<0.\end{aligned}\right.

Since
\mathcal{R}=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{R}_{l}
, for
2\geq p>\frac{\beta}{\beta-\alpha},\,\mathcal{R}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}).
Now we use a standard duality argument to show that
\mathcal{R}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for
2\leq p<\beta/\alpha
. The formal dual of
\mathcal{R}
is given by

\mathcal{R}^{*}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x+\Gamma(t))\frac{e^{2\pi i|t|^{-\beta}}\overline{\Omega(t)}}{|t|^{\alpha+n}}.

Since
\Gamma
and
-\Gamma
satisfies the same properties, we have that
\mathcal{R}^{*}
is also bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
for
2\geq p>\frac{\beta}{\beta-\alpha}
. Now,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\left\{\frac{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{R}f(x)g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|}{\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\left\{\frac{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\mathcal{R}^{*}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|}{\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\sup_{g\in L^{p^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{\left\|\mathcal{R}^{*}g\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}}{\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})},

for
2\geq p^{\prime}>\frac{\beta}{\beta-\alpha}
. This implies
\mathcal{R}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
for
2\leq p<\beta/\alpha
.

∎

Next we give the proof of Sobolev estimate for the operator
\mathcal{R}.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

(1)

We start by observing that

\left\|\mathcal{R}f\right\|_{L_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{s}\left|m(\xi)\right|^{2}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2}.

Using the bounds for
m_{l,j}
, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle|m(\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j\in J_{r}\cup J_{\theta}}\frac{2^{\alpha l}}{\lambda}+\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j\in J_{rr}\cup J_{\theta\theta}}\frac{2^{\alpha l}}{\sqrt{\lambda}},

where,
J_{r}
,
J_{\theta}
,
J_{rr}
, and
J_{\theta\theta}
are the collections of those indices for which
|g_{r}(r,\theta)|\geq\frac{\epsilon\lambda}{2}
,
|g_{\theta}(r,\theta)|\geq\frac{\epsilon\lambda}{2}
,
|g_{rr}(r,\theta)|\geq\frac{\epsilon\lambda}{2}
, and
|g_{\theta\theta}(r,\theta)|\geq\frac{\epsilon\lambda}{2}
, respectively.
We split our analysis in two cases.

Case 1:
First let us consider
\left|\xi^{\prime}\right|\geq\left|\xi_{n+1}\right|.

In this case
|\xi|\asymp|\xi^{\prime}|.
Using the definition of
\lambda
, we get

|m(\xi)|\lesssim\left(\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{2^{\alpha l}}{\max\left\{2^{\beta l},2^{-l}\left|\xi^{\prime}\right|\right\}}+\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{2^{\alpha l}}{\left(\max\left\{2^{\beta l},2^{-l}\left|\xi^{\prime}\right|\right\}\right)^{1/2}}\right)

Now, we break each of the above sums in two parts: one over those
l
for which
2^{\beta l}\geq 2^{-l}|\xi^{\prime}|
and the other where
2^{\beta l}\leq 2^{-l}|\xi^{\prime}|
. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle|m(\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{Z}\\
2^{\beta l}\geq 2^{-l}\left|\xi^{\prime}\right|\end{subarray}}2^{(\alpha-\beta/2)l}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{Z}\\
2^{\beta l}\leq 2^{-l}\left|\xi^{\prime}\right|\end{subarray}}2^{(\alpha+1/2)l}\left|\xi^{\prime}\right|^{-1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\left|\xi^{\prime}\right|^{\frac{\alpha-\beta/2}{1+\beta}}+\left|\xi^{\prime}\right|^{\frac{\alpha+1/2}{\beta+1}-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\asymp\left|\xi^{\prime}\right|^{\frac{\alpha-\beta/2}{1+\beta}}\asymp|\xi|^{\frac{\alpha-\beta/2}{1+\beta}}.

Case 2:
Next, we assume
\left|\xi_{n+1}\right|>\left|\xi^{\prime}\right|.
Here, we have
|\xi|\asymp|\xi_{n+1}|
. Again from the definition of
\lambda
, we get

\displaystyle\left|m\left(\xi\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\alpha l}\left(\max\left\{2^{\beta l},2^{-2l}\left|\xi_{n+1}\right|\sup\limits_{\frac{1}{2}<r<2}\left\{\left|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(2^{-l}r\right)\right|\right\}\right\}\right)^{-1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\alpha l}\left(\max\left\{2^{\beta l},2^{-2l}\left|\xi_{n+1}\right|\sup\limits_{\frac{1}{2}<r<2}\left\{\left|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(2^{-l}r\right)\right|\right\}\right\}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right].

From Equation (
1.2
), we have,

-\frac{k_{3}}{x}\leq\left(\ln|\varphi^{\prime\prime}|\right)^{\prime}\left(x\right)\leq\frac{k_{3}}{x}.

Integrating from
1
to
x
in the above inequalities, we get

|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(1\right)|x^{-k_{3}}\leq|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)|\leq|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(1\right)|x^{k_{3}}.

This leads us to

|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(1\right)|2^{k_{3}\left(l-1\right)}\leq\sup\limits_{\frac{1}{2}\leq r\leq 2}\left\{\left|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(2^{-l}r\right)\right|\right\}\leq|\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(1\right)|2^{-k_{3}\left(l-1\right)}.

We remark here that these inequalities are valid for
l\leq 0
(i.e.,
x\geq 1
in the step where we have integrated). For the case when
l>0
, the inequalities are reversed, but are not used in our proof.

Let
P\left(l\right)
be the property that
2^{l}\geq\left|\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(1\right)}{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{\beta+k_{3}+2}}\left|\xi_{n+1}\right|^{\frac{1}{\beta+k_{3}+2}}
. Now, in the estimate for
m\left(\xi\right)
, we split the sum into two parts, and apply the estimates on the terms involving the maximum, to obtain

\displaystyle\left|m\left(\xi\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\left|\xi_{n+1}\right|^{\frac{\alpha-\beta/2}{\beta+k_{3}+2}}+\sum\nolimits^{\prime}2^{\left(\alpha+1\right)l}2^{-\frac{k_{3}}{2}\left(l-1\right)}\left|\xi_{n+1}\right|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right],

where
\sum^{\prime}
denotes the sum over the collection of those integers
l
that do not satisfy property
P\left(l\right)
. It is clear that such a collection is bounded above and unbounded below, thereby forming a geometric sum. Using this fact, we get

\left|m\left(\xi\right)\right|\lesssim\left|\xi_{n+1}\right|^{\frac{\alpha-\beta/2}{\beta+k_{3}+2}}.

Combining the two estimates obtained above, we get (for large
|\xi|
),

\left|m\left(\xi\right)\right|\lesssim\begin{cases}|\xi|^{\frac{\alpha-\beta/2}{\beta+1}},&\text{ if }\,|\xi^{\prime}|\geq\left|\xi_{n+1}\right|,\\
|\xi|^{\frac{\alpha-\beta/2}{\beta+k_{3}+2}},&\text{ if }\,|\xi^{\prime}|\leq\left|\xi_{n+1}\right|.\end{cases}

We now recall that even for an
L^{2}
-estimate, we require
\beta>2\alpha
, and from our assumptions, we have
k_{3}>0
. Hence, the following estimate is true for large values of
|\xi|
.

\left|m\left(\xi\right)\right|\lesssim|\xi|^{\frac{\alpha-\beta/2}{\beta+k_{3}+2}}.

Now, we are in a position to obtain the
L^{2}
-Sobolev estimate.

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}f\|_{L^{2}_{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{s}\left|m\left(\xi\right)\right|^{2}\left|\widehat{f}\left(\xi\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\int\limits_{|\xi|\leq 1}\left|\widehat{f}\left(\xi\right)\right|^{2}+\int\limits_{|\xi|>1}|\xi|^{2\left(s-\frac{\beta/2-\alpha}{\beta+k_{3}+2}\right)}\left|\widehat{f}\left(\xi\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)}^{2}\asymp\|f\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)}^{2}.

In the final step, we have used the fact that
s\leq\frac{\beta/2-\alpha}{\beta+k_{3}+2}
.

(2)

With the
L^{2}
-Sobolev estimate and the
L^{p}
-
L^{p}
boundedness at hand, we may now use the technique of complex interpolation to obtain
L^{p}
-Sobolev estimates.
First, we observe that we have the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the operator
\mathcal{R}
for
\frac{\alpha}{\beta}<\frac{1}{p}\leq 1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}
. We consider the analytic family of operators
\left\{\mathcal{R}_{z}|0\leq\text{Re }z\leq s_{0}\right\}
, given by

\mathcal{R}_{z}f\left(x\right)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1+|\cdot|^{2}\right)^{\frac{z}{2}}m\ \widehat{f}\right]\left(x\right).

Given an
s\in\left(0,s_{0}\right)
and
p
as in the statement of the theorem, let us fix
\frac{1}{p_{0}}=\left(1-\frac{s}{s_{0}}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{2s_{0}}\right)
. Clearly, we have the
L^{p_{0}}
boundedness of the operator
\mathcal{R}
. That is,

\|\mathcal{R}_{0}f\|_{L^{p_{0}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)}.

Due to the
L^{2}
-Sobolev estimate, we also have,

\|\mathcal{R}_{s_{0}}f\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)}.

The result now follows from the complex interpolation (see
[
16
]
).

∎

5.
Conclusion

In this paper, we have established
L^{p}
-
L^{p}
boundedness and Sobolev estimates for the oscillatory hypersingular integral operator

\mathcal{R}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-\Gamma(t))\frac{e^{-2\pi i|t|^{-\beta}}\Omega(t)}{|t|^{\alpha+n}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Our consideration of the radial function
\varphi
, used in defining the surface
\Gamma
, ensures it satisfies several properties reminiscent of monomials. In fact, it is easy to see that, towards
0
and
\infty
, the function
\varphi
can be compared to monomials. These properties allow us to extend and generalize certain existing results in the literature.

We note that when
\beta<2\alpha
, operators of the form (
1.3
) may fail to be bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
(see
[
8
]
). However, the boundary case
\beta=2\alpha
remains open, and requires further investigation.

The range of exponents
p
for which
\mathcal{R}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
depends on the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
. We believe that the range established here can be improved.

Finally, the surface
\Gamma
introduced in this study exhibits interesting geometric features, as highlighted in Remarks
1.2
and
1.3
, and may be of independent interest for further geometric or analytic explorations.

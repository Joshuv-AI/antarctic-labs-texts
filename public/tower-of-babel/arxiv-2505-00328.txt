The spectral characteristics of the Sturm Hamiltonian with eventually periodic type
Jie CAO, Zhenyu Yu

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the spectral characteristics of the Sturm Hamiltonian with eventually periodic type frequencies under large coupling and establish strict inequalities between the optimal Hölder exponent of the density of states measure, the dimension of the density of states measure, the dimension of the spectrum, and the transport exponent by analyzing the thermodynamic pressure function. Also, we provide the large coupling asymptotic properties of the four spectral characteristics.



1.
Introduction

The
Sturmian Hamiltonian
is a discrete Schrödinger operator defined on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
with Sturmian potential:

(H_{\alpha,\lambda,\theta}\psi)_{n}:=\psi_{n+1}+\psi_{n-1}+\lambda\chi_{[1-\alpha,1)}(n\alpha+\theta\pmod{1})\psi_{n},

where
\alpha\in{\mathbb{I}}:=[0,1]\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}
is the
frequency
,
\lambda>0
is the
coupling constant
and
\theta\in[0,1)
is the
phase
(where
\chi_{A}
is the indicator function of the set
A
). It is well-known that the spectrum of
H_{\alpha,\lambda,\theta}
is independent of
\theta
(see
[
5
]
).
We denote the spectrum by
\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}
.

Another spectral object is the so-called
density of states measure (DOS)

\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
supported on
\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}
, which is defined by

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}}g(E)d\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}(E):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle\delta_{0},g(H_{\alpha,\lambda,\theta})\delta_{0}\rangle d\theta,\ \ \ \forall g\in C(\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}).

Since the operator
H_{\alpha,\lambda,\theta}
has purely singular continuous spectrum (see
[
19
]
), the RAGE Theorem (see, e.g.,
[
44
, Theorem XI.115]
) suggests that when studying the Schrödinger time evolution for this Schrödinger
operator, that is,
e^{-itH_{\alpha,\lambda,\theta}}
for some initial state
\psi\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
, one should consider time-averaged quantities (see for example
[
30
,
20
]
). For
p>0
, consider the
p
-th moment of the position operator,

\langle|X|^{p}\rangle(t)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|n|^{p}\langle e^{-tH_{\alpha,\lambda,\theta}}\delta_{0},\delta_{n}\rangle|^{2}.

Then, define the upper and lower transport exponents
\beta^{+}(\lambda,p)
and
\beta^{-}(\lambda,p)
as:

\beta^{+}(\lambda,p):=\limsup\limits_{T\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{2t}{T}}\langle|X|^{p}\rangle(t)dt}{p\log T},\ \beta^{-}(\lambda,p):=\liminf\limits_{T\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{2t}{T}}\langle|X|^{p}\rangle(t)dt}{p\log T}.

The transport exponents
\beta^{\pm}(\lambda,p)
belong to
[0,1]

and are non-decreasing in
p
(see, e.g.,
[
20
]
), and hence the
following limits exist uniformly in
\theta\in[0,1)
:

\mathcal{T}^{+}(\alpha,\lambda):=\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\beta^{+}(p,\lambda),\ \ \ \ \mathcal{T}^{-}(\alpha,\lambda):=\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\beta^{-}(p,\lambda).

Ballistic transport corresponds to transport exponents being equal to one, diffusive
transport corresponds to the value
1/2
, and vanishing transport exponents correspond to dynamical localization. In all other cases, transport is called anomalous.

Assume
\mu
is a finite Borel measure supported on a metric space
X
. We define the
lower
and
upper
local dimensions of
\mu
at
x\in X
as

\underline{d}_{\mu}(x):=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\mu(B(x,r))}{\log r}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \overline{d}_{\mu}(x):=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\mu(B(x,r))}{\log r}.

(1.1)

If
\underline{d}_{\mu}(x)=\overline{d}_{\mu}(x)
, we say that the
local dimension
of
\mu
at
x
exists and denote it by
d_{\mu}(x)
.
The
Hausdorff dimension
of
\mu
is defined as

\dim_{H}\mu:=\sup\{s:\underline{d}_{\mu}(x)\geq s\text{ for }\mu\text{ a.e. }x\in X\}.

(1.2)

If there exists a constant
d
such that
d_{\mu}(x)=d
for
\mu
a.e.
x\in X
, then necessarily
\dim_{H}\mu=d
, and
\mu
is called
exact-dimensional
(see
[
21
, Chapter 10]
for more details).

In this paper, we will consider the frequency of eventually periodic type. For each
\mathbf{a}=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}
, define the eventually periodic frequencies with sequence
\mathbf{a}
as

\mathcal{EP}(\mathbf{a}):=\Big\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{I}}:\alpha=[b_{1},\cdots,b_{m},\,\overline{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k}}],\,b_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}},\,1\leq i\leq m,\,m\in{\mathbb{N}}\Big\},

where the overline notation denotes infinite repetition of the periodic block. In particular, we define
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{a})=\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{I}}:\alpha=[\overline{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k}}]\}
as the set of periodic frequencies. Let
\mathcal{EP}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}}\mathcal{EP}(\mathbf{a})

be the set of frequencies with eventually periodic type.

Denote the optimal Hölder exponent of
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
by

\gamma(\alpha,\lambda):=\inf\{\underline{d}_{\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}}(E):E\in\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}\}.

(1.3)

For any
\alpha\in\mathcal{EP}
, we will study the dimensional properties of
\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
, and the optimal Hölder exponent of
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
,
as well as
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\alpha,\lambda)
for large
coupling constant
\lambda
. Write

\displaystyle D(\alpha,\lambda):=\dim_{H}\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}\ \ \text{and}\ \ d(\alpha,\lambda):=\dim_{H}\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}.

1.1.
Background and previous results

The Sturmian Hamiltonian, a classic model for one-dimensional quasicrystals, has been studied since the 1980s (see
[
9
,
10
]
). Here, we summarize key findings about its spectral properties, focusing on the Fibonacci Hamiltonian—the most studied Sturmian model. This model uses the golden ratio
\alpha_{1}:=(\sqrt{5}-1)/2
as its frequency. First proposed to describe quasicrystals (see
[
5
,
29
,
39
]
), its spectral structure is now fully understood (see
[
18
]
). We restate key results (Theorem A) relevant to our analysis, briefly introducing the trace map dynamics—a key tool for studying Fibonacci Hamiltonian.

Define the
Fibonacci trace
map
\mathbf{T}:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
as
\mathbf{T}(x,y,z):=(2xy-z,x,y).

It is known that for any
\lambda>0
, the map
\mathbf{T}
preserves the cubic surface

S_{\lambda}:=\{(x,y,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-2xyz-1=\lambda^{2}/4\}.

Write
\mathbf{T}_{\lambda}:=\mathbf{T}|_{S_{\lambda}}
and let
\Lambda_{\lambda}
be the set of points in
S_{\lambda}
with bounded
\mathbf{T}_{\lambda}
-orbits. It is known that
\Lambda_{\lambda}
is the non-wandering set of
\mathbf{T}_{\lambda}
and is a locally maximal compact transitive hyperbolic set of
\mathbf{T}_{\lambda}
, see
[
6
,
8
,
12
,
37
]
.

Let
\mu_{\lambda,\max}
be the measure of maximal entropy of
\mathbf{T}_{\lambda}|_{\Lambda_{\lambda}}
and
\mu_{\lambda}
be the equilibrium measure of
\mathbf{T}_{\lambda}|_{\Lambda_{\lambda}}
that corresponds to the potential
-D(\alpha_{1},\lambda)\log\|D\mathbf{T}_{\lambda}|_{E^{u}}\|
. Assume that
{\rm Lyap}^{u}(p)
is the unstable (positive) Lyapunov exponent of the periodic point
p
, and
{\rm Lyap}^{u}\mu_{\lambda}
(or
{\rm Lyap}^{u}\mu_{\lambda,\max}
) is the unstable Lyapunov
exponent of
\mu_{\lambda}
(respectively,
\mu_{\lambda,\max})
.

Theorem A
(
[
6
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
18
,
27
,
40
]
)
Assume that
\lambda>0
. Then we have

(i) The DOS
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha_{1},\lambda}
is exact-dimensional and
d(\alpha_{1},\lambda)
satisfies Young’s formula:

d(\alpha_{1},\lambda)=\frac{-\log\alpha_{1}}{{\rm Lyap}^{u}\mu_{\lambda,\max}}.

(ii) The optimal Hölder exponent of
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha_{1},\lambda}
satisfies

\gamma(\alpha_{1},\lambda)=\frac{-\log\alpha_{1}}{\sup\limits_{p\in Per(\mathbf{T}_{\lambda})}{\rm Lyap}^{u}(p)}.

(iii) The spectrum
\Sigma_{\alpha_{1},\lambda}
satisfies

\dim_{H}\Sigma_{\alpha_{1},\lambda}=\overline{\dim}_{B}\Sigma_{\alpha_{1},\lambda}

and

D(\alpha_{1},\lambda)=\frac{h_{\mu_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{T}_{\lambda}|_{\Lambda_{\lambda}})}{{\rm Lyap}^{u}\mu_{\lambda}}.

(iv)
\mathcal{T}^{-}(\alpha_{1},\lambda)
and
\mathcal{T}^{+}(\alpha_{1},\lambda)
are equal and independent of
\theta\in[0,1)
. Moreover,

\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\alpha_{1},\lambda)=\frac{-\log\alpha_{1}}{\inf\limits_{p\in Per(\mathbf{T}_{\lambda})}{\rm Lyap}^{u}(p)}.

(v) The following inequalities hold:

\gamma(\alpha_{1},\lambda)<d(\alpha_{1},\lambda)<D(\alpha_{1},\lambda)<\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\alpha_{1},\lambda).

(vi) The following asymptotics hold:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}\gamma(\alpha_{1},\lambda)\cdot\log\lambda=-\frac{3}{2}\log\alpha_{1},\ \ \ \ \ \ \lim\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}d(\alpha_{1},\lambda)\cdot\log\lambda=-\frac{5+\sqrt{5}}{4}\log\alpha_{1},

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}D(\alpha_{1},\lambda)\cdot\log\lambda=\log(1+\sqrt{2}),\ \ \ \ \ \lim\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\alpha_{1},\lambda)\cdot\log\lambda=-2\log\alpha_{1}.

There are several works deal with sub-classes of Sturm Hamiltonian.
Girand
[
25
]
and Mei
[
37
]
considered the eventually periodic type
\alpha
, they showed that

\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
is exact-dimensional and
d(\alpha,\lambda)<D(\alpha,\lambda)
for small
\lambda
.
For
\lambda>20
and the eventually constant type
\alpha
, Qu
[
41
]
showed that
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
is exact-dimensional, and obtained similar asymptotic behaviors of
\gamma(\alpha,\lambda)
,
d(\alpha,\lambda)
and
D(\alpha,\lambda)
like Theorem A (vi).
We remark that, for all works mentioned above, the dynamical method is applicable due to the special types of the frequencies. Very recently, Luna
[
35
]
showed that
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}D(\alpha,\lambda)=1
for
\alpha
with bounded-type.

For general frequencies, the dimensional properties of the spectra are more complex. Fix
\alpha\in{\mathbb{I}}
with continued fraction expansion
[a_{1},a_{2},\cdots]
. Bellissard et al.
[
5
]
showed that
\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}
is a Cantor set of Lebesgue measure zero. By using the subordinacy theory,
Damanik, Killip and Lenz
[
19
]
showed that, if
\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}<\infty
, then
D(\alpha,\lambda)>0
. Based
on the analysis of Raymond about the structure of spectrum
[
43
]
, Liu and
Wen
[
33
]
showed that the spectrum
\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}
has a natural covering structure. This makes the fractal
dimensions of the spectrum of Sturm Hamiltonian were extensively studied in
[
7
,
16
,
22
,
32
,
33
]
. Write

K_{\ast}(\alpha):=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\Big)^{1/k}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ K^{\ast}(\alpha):=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\Big)^{1/k}.

Fix
\lambda\geq 24
. Then it is proven in
[
32
,
33
]
that

\begin{cases}\dim_{H}\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}\in(0,1)&\text{ if }\ \ K_{\ast}(\alpha)<\infty\\
\dim_{H}\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}=1&\text{ if }\ \ K_{\ast}(\alpha)=\infty\end{cases}\ \text{ and }\ \begin{cases}\overline{\dim}_{B}\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}\in(0,1)&\text{ if }\ \ K^{\ast}(\alpha)<\infty\\
\overline{\dim}_{B}\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}=1&\text{ if }\ \ K^{\ast}(\alpha)=\infty\end{cases}.

Later, Damanik and Gorodetski
[
16
]
proved that for Lebesgue a.e.
\alpha\in{\mathbb{I}}
, both
\dim_{H}\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}
and

\overline{\dim}_{B}\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}
remain constant. In a very recent work
[
7
]
, Cao and Qu found a set
of full Lebesgue measure
\tilde{{\mathbb{I}}}\subset{\mathbb{I}}
(independent of
\lambda
), such that for each
(\alpha,\lambda)\in\tilde{{\mathbb{I}}}\times[24,\infty)
,

\dim_{H}\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}=\overline{\dim}_{B}\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}.

The properties of
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
are less studied for general frequencies. In
[
42
]
, for any
\lambda>20
and
\alpha
with bounded continued fraction expansion, Qu constructed certain
\alpha
such that
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
is not exact-dimensional. Jitomirskaya and Zhang
[
26
]
also constructed Liouvillian frequency
\alpha
such that for any
\lambda>0
, the related
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
is also not exact-dimensional. Recently, Cao and Qu
[
7
]
showed that for any
(\alpha,\lambda)\in\tilde{{\mathbb{I}}}\times[24,\infty)
, the DOS
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
is exact-dimensional and its Hausdorff dimension satisfies Young’s formula.

1.2.
Main results

In this paper, we establish the following principal result: For all
\lambda>20
, the spectral characteristics of Sturmian Hamiltonian with eventually periodic type frequencies exhibit striking parallels with those of the Fibonacci Hamiltonian (see Theorem A).

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\lambda>20
,
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{EP}(\mathbf{a})
. Then we have

(i)

The DOS
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
is exact-dimensional and
d(\alpha,\lambda)
satisfies

d(\alpha,\lambda)=-\frac{\mathbf{P}(0)}{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(0)},

where
\mathbf{P}(s)
is the topological pressure (see (
4.13
)) for potential
s\Psi^{\mathbf{a}}
(see (
4.6
)) .

(ii)

The optimal Hölder exponent of
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}
satisfies

\gamma(\alpha,\lambda)=\inf\{\underline{d}_{\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}}(x):x\in\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}\}=-\frac{\mathbf{P}(0)}{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(-\infty)}.

(iii)

The spectrum
\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}
satisfies

\dim_{H}\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}=\overline{\dim}_{B}\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}
, and there exists
0<D<D(\alpha,\lambda)
such that

D(\alpha,\lambda)=-\frac{\mathbf{P}(0)}{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(D)}.

(iv)

\mathcal{T}^{-}(\alpha,\lambda)
and
\mathcal{T}^{+}(\alpha,\lambda)
are equal and independent of
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
. Moreover,

\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\alpha,\lambda)=\sup\{\overline{d}_{\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,\lambda}}(x):x\in\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}\}=-\frac{\mathbf{P}(0)}{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\infty)}.

(1.4)

(v)

The following inequalities hold:

\gamma(\alpha,\lambda)<d(\alpha,\lambda)<D(\alpha,\lambda)<\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\alpha,\lambda).

(1.5)

(vi)

There exist constants
0<\rho_{\gamma}(\mathbf{a})\leq\rho_{d}(\mathbf{a})\leq\rho_{D}(\mathbf{a})\leq\rho_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbf{a})
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\gamma(\alpha,\lambda)\cdot\log\lambda=\rho_{\gamma}(\mathbf{a}),\ \ \ \ \ \ \lim_{\lambda\to\infty}d(\alpha,\lambda)\cdot\log\lambda=\rho_{d}(\mathbf{a}),

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}D(\alpha,\lambda)\cdot\log\lambda=\rho_{D}(\mathbf{a}),\ \ \ \ \ \lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\alpha,\lambda)\cdot\log\lambda=\rho_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbf{a}).

(1.7)

(vii)

The following “tail properties” hold: that is for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{EP}(\mathbf{a})
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma(\alpha,\lambda)=\gamma(\beta,\lambda),\ d(\alpha,\lambda)=d(\beta,\lambda),\ D(\alpha,\lambda)=D(\beta,\lambda),\ \mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\alpha,\lambda)=\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\beta,\lambda).

(1.8)

Remark 1.2
.

(1)

If
k=1
, the frequency
\alpha\in\bigcup_{\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{EP}(\kappa)
is called eventually constant type, this class of frequencies are considered in
[
41
]
. By developing a systematic framework that intrinsically integrates dynamical systems theory with thermodynamic formalism, our works significantly extend prior spectral analysis to Strumian Hamiltonian with eventually periodic type frequencies.

(2)

The derivative of the pressure function
\mathbf{P}
exhibits profound connections to the interrelations between
\gamma(\alpha,\lambda),d(\alpha,\lambda),D(\alpha,\lambda)
and
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\alpha,\lambda)
. The pressure function
\mathbf{P}
is
C^{1}
and strictly convex on
{\mathbb{R}}
(see Proposition

-\infty<\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(-\infty)<\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(0)<\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(D)<\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\infty)<0,

and we can immediately establish (

) from the above inequalities. The inequality

establishes a conjecture of Barry Simon, which was made based on an analogy with work of Makarov and Volberg
[
36
,
45
]
. Note that there exists exactly one point of intersection of the tangent line with the
t
-axis, at the point (
t
,
\mathbf{P}(t)
). In particular, due to statement (i),

is given by the point of intersection of the
tangent line to the graph of
\mathbf{P}(t)
at the point
(0,\mathbf{P}(0))
with the t-axis. Also,
the line
\mathbf{P}(0)+t\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(-\infty)
intersects the
t
-axis at the point
\gamma(\alpha,\lambda)
by statement (ii),
and the line
\mathbf{P}(0)+t\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\infty)
intersects the
t
-axis at the point
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\alpha,\lambda)
by statement (iv). Finally, by Corollary
2.2
(3), the graph of
\mathbf{P}(t)
intersects the
t
-axis at the point
D(\alpha,\lambda)
. These observations are illustrated in Figure

.

The statements (i) and (ii) are new, but (iii) is known (see
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
). We state them here for comparison. Our method gives a new expression for
D(\alpha,\lambda)
.

(4)

Damanik et. al.
[

]
obtained the lower and upper bound for all time-averaged transport exponents for any

, and the part (iv) was presented in

The equalities (

1.3.
Ideas of the proof

Let us explain the idea of our proof. By viewing the spectrum as a kind of subshift of finite type, we successfully transfer the spectral problem to a dynamical problem. Then we combine the tools from the thermodynamical formalism and multifractal analysis, derive the desired result.

Recall that for each
\alpha\in{\mathbb{I}}
and
\lambda>4
, Raymond
[
43
]
constructed a family
\{{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{\alpha}:n\geq 0\}
of decreasing coverings of
\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}
(see Sect.
3.1
).
Later in
[
32
,
41
,
42
]
, a coding
\Omega^{\alpha}
for
\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}
was given based on Raymond’s construction (see Sect.
3.2
). In this way, one can define a coding map

\pi^{\alpha}_{\lambda}:\Omega^{\alpha}\to\Sigma_{\alpha,\lambda}.

Through structural analysis of symbolic space
\Omega^{\alpha}
, we reveal a fundamental deficiency: the absence of an amenable dynamical system framework. Inspired by the works
[
7
,
41
]
, for any periodic frequency
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{a})
with
\mathbf{a}=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}
, we can construct the new symbolic space
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}
as the subshift of finite type to code the spectrum
\Sigma_{\check{\alpha},\lambda}
by coding map
\pi_{\mathbf{a},\lambda}
(see Sect.
4.1
), where
\check{\alpha}=[1,\overline{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k}}]
. To study the spectral characteristics, we need to introduce an appropriate metric
d_{\mathbf{a}}
on
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}
such that the coding map

\pi_{\mathbf{a},\lambda}:(\Omega_{\mathbf{a}},d_{\mathbf{a}})\to(\Sigma_{\check{\alpha},\lambda},|\cdot|)

is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism (see Proposition
4.6
).
The definition is quite simple: for any
\mathbf{v}
,
\mathbf{w}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}
, define

d_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}):=|B^{\check{\alpha}}_{w}|,\ \ \ \text{where}\ w=\pi_{\mathbf{a},\lambda}(\mathbf{v})|_{nk+1}\ \text{if}\ |\mathbf{v}\wedge\mathbf{w}|=n.

(1.9)

In this case, the DOS
\mathcal{N}_{\check{\alpha},\lambda}
is related to the maximal entropy measure
\mu_{\mathbf{a}}
on
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}
under the coding map
\pi_{\mathbf{a},\lambda}
(see Proposition
4.7
), that is

\mu_{\mathbf{a}}\circ\pi^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},\lambda}\asymp\mathcal{N}_{\check{\alpha},\lambda}.

(1.10)

Roughly speaking, the properties (exact-dimensional property or optimal Hölder exponent) of
\mu_{\mathbf{a}}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\check{\alpha},\lambda}
are same.

Let us return back to
(\Omega_{\mathbf{a}},d_{\mathbf{a}})
and consider the measure
\mu_{\mathbf{a}}
. We will introduce how the derivative of the pressure function
\mathbf{P}
exhibits profound connections to the interrelations between
\gamma(\alpha,\lambda),d(\alpha,\lambda),D(\alpha,\lambda)
and
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\alpha,\lambda)
.
At first, we consider the symbolic space
(\Omega_{\mathbf{a}},d_{\mathbf{a}})
, then the metric
d_{\mathbf{a}}
is related to the potential
\Psi^{\mathbf{a}}=\{\psi^{\mathbf{a}}_{n}:n\geq 1\}
, where
\psi^{\mathbf{a}}_{n}(\mathbf{v})=\log|B^{\check{\alpha}}_{w}|
. This kind of metric is also called a weak-Gibbs metric on
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}
, which has been used in
[
41
,
42
]
.
It is natural to define the pressure function

\mathbf{P}(t):=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{|\mathbf{v}|=n}\exp(\sup_{x\in[\mathbf{v}]}\psi^{\mathbf{a}}_{n}(x)).

Then we prove that the crucial result:
\mathbf{P}
is
C^{1}
and strictly convex on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Consequently, the following limits exist

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathbf{P}^{\prime}(-\infty)&:=\lim\limits_{t\to-\infty}\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(t)=\inf\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}}\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\psi^{\mathbf{a}}_{n}(\mathbf{v});\\
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(0)&=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\psi^{\mathbf{a}}_{n}d\mu_{\mathbf{a}};\\
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\infty)&:=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(t)=\sup\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\psi^{\mathbf{a}}_{n}(\mathbf{v}).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.11)

We focus on the maximal entropy measure
\mu_{\mathbf{a}}
, and it is regarded as a Gibbs measure for potential
\Phi=\{-n\mathbf{P}(0),n\geq 1\}
. To study the specturm

by (

), for any

\underline{d}_{\mu_{\mathbf{a}}}(\mathbf{v})=-\frac{\mathbf{P}(0)}{\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\psi^{\mathbf{a}}_{n}(\mathbf{v})/n};\ \ \ \ \ \overline{d}_{\mu_{\mathbf{a}}}(\mathbf{v})=-\frac{\mathbf{P}(0)}{\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\psi^{\mathbf{a}}_{n}(\mathbf{v})/n}.

(1.12)

Now by (

are strongly
equivalent and

is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism. By (

\gamma(\check{\alpha},\lambda)=-\frac{\mathbf{P}(0)}{\mathbf{P}(-\infty)},\ d(\check{\alpha},\lambda)=-\frac{\mathbf{P}(0)}{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(0)},\ D(\check{\alpha},\lambda)=-\frac{\mathbf{P}(0)}{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(D)},\ \mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\check{\alpha},\lambda)=-\frac{\mathbf{P}(0)}{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\infty)}.

2.
Recall on Thermodynamical Formalism and Multifractal Analysis

Let us begin with some notations and background. We say that
(X,T)
is a
topological dynamical system (TDS) if
X
is a compact metric space and
T
is a
continuous mapping from
X
to itself. Denote the set of all
T
-invariant probability measures supported on
X
by
\mathcal{M}(X,T)
.

Assume
\Phi=\{\phi_{n},n\geq 1\}\subset C(X,{\mathbb{R}})
. We call
\Phi
a potential on
X
. We say that
\Phi
is
almost additive
(denote by
C_{aa}(X,T)
), if there exists a constant
C_{aa}(\Phi)\geq 0
such that

|\phi_{n+m}(x)-\phi_{n}(x)-\phi_{m}(T^{n}x)|\leq C_{aa}(\Phi),\ \ \ \forall\ n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In particular, if there exists a constant
c>0
such that
\phi_{n}(x)\leq-cn
and
\phi_{n}
is decreasing for
n
, then we say
\Phi

is negative and write
\Phi\in C_{aa}^{-}(X,T)
. In this case, there exist two constants
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}
such that

-c_{2}n\leq\phi_{n}(x)\leq-c_{1}n.

(2.1)

Given
\Phi\in C_{aa}(X,T)
, by subadditivity,
\Phi_{*}(\mu):=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{X}\phi_{n}d\mu
exists for every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
. Note that if
\Phi
is negative, then
\Phi_{*}(\mu)<0
.

Let
(\Sigma_{A},T)
be a topologically mixing subshift of finite type.
We say
\Phi\in C_{aa}(\Sigma_{A},T)
has bounded variation property, if there
exists constant
C_{bv}(\Phi)\geq 0
such that

\sup\left\{|\phi_{n}(x)-\phi_{n}(y)|:\ x|_{n}=y|_{n}\right\}\leq C_{bv}(\Phi),\ \ \ \forall\ n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We denote by
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{A},T)
and
\mathcal{F}^{-}(\Sigma_{A},T)
the sets of almost additive potentials and negative almost additive potentials with bounded variation, respectively.

Fix
\Psi=\{\psi_{n}:n\geq 1\}\in\mathcal{F}^{-}(\Sigma_{A},T)
, define
a weak Gibbs metric
d_{\Psi}
on
(\Sigma_{A},T)
as

d_{\Psi}(x,y)=\sup_{z\in[x\wedge y]}\exp(\psi_{|x\wedge y|}(z)).

(2.2)

This kind of metric is considered in
[
1
,
24
,
28
,
41
]
. In this section, we always endow
(\Sigma_{A},T)
with the metric
d_{\Psi}
without further mention.

2.1.
Thermodynamical Formalism

Theorem 2.1

(
[
2
,
38
]
)
.

Assume
\Phi\in\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{A},T)
, then the following results hold:

(1) The limit

P(\Phi):=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{|w|=n}\exp(\sup_{x\in[w]}\phi_{n}(x))

exists, and is called the topological pressure of
\Phi
. Moreover, the variational principle holds:

P(\Phi)=\sup\{h_{T}(\mu)+\Phi_{*}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{A},T)\}.

(2.3)

(2) There exists an ergodic measure
\mu_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{A},T)
such that

C^{-1}\leq\frac{\mu_{\Phi}([w])}{\exp(-nP(\Phi)+\phi_{n}(x))}\leq C,\ \ (\forall\ x\in[w]).

\mu_{\Phi}
is called the Gibbs measure related to
\Phi
. Moreover,
\mu_{\Phi}
is the unique invariant measure which attains the supremum of (
2.3
).

If
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{A},T)
satisfies
P(\Phi)=h_{T}(\mu)+\Phi_{*}(\mu)
, then
\mu
is called an equilibrium state of
\Phi
. Furthermore, this equilibrium state is unique.

The following corollary is a standard result directly verifiable through the variational principle; its proof is consequently omitted (see for example
[
4
]
).

Corollary 2.2
.

(1) Fix
\Phi\in\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{A},T)
, the function
s\mapsto P(\Phi+s\Psi)
is convex, strictly decreasing on
{\mathbb{R}}
and

\lim\limits_{s\to-\infty}P(\Phi+s\Psi)=\infty\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \lim\limits_{s\to\infty}P(\Phi+s\Psi)=-\infty.

Thus
P(\Phi+s\Psi)=0
has a unique solution.

(2) In particular, if we let
\Phi=\Psi=\mathbf{0}
, then
h_{top}(T)=P(\mathbf{0})
and
\mathbf{0}
admits a Gibbs measure
\mu_{\max}
with
\mu_{\max}([x|_{n}])\sim\exp(-nP(\mathbf{0}))
for all
x\in\Sigma_{A}
. Moreover,
h_{T}(\mu_{\max})=\sup\{h_{T}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{A},T)\}=h_{top}(T)
, and is called the maximal entropy measure.

(3) The Bowen’s formula holds:
\dim_{H}\Sigma_{A}=s_{\Psi}
where
s_{\Psi}
is the zero of
P(s\Psi)=0
.

2.2.
Analysis of the pressure function

Let
Q(s)=P(s\Psi)
and
\mu_{s}
be the Gibbs measure related to the potential
s\Psi
. In this subsection, we will show that the pressure function
Q
is
C^{1}
and strictly convex on
{\mathbb{R}}
. This property plays a crucial role in the subsequent analysis of this paper.

The following proposition is an analog of
[
3
, Theorem 2]
:

Proposition 2.3
.

(1)

Q
is
C^{1}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
, and
there exist
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}
such that

-c_{2}\leq Q^{\prime}(s)=\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s})\leq-c_{1}\ \ \ \text{for all}\ s\in{\mathbb{R}},

(2.4)

(2)

Q^{\prime}
is strictly increasing on
{\mathbb{R}}
. Consequently
Q
is strictly convex on
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

(1) Since
\Psi\in\mathcal{F}^{-}(\Sigma_{A},T)
and by (
2.1
), there exist constants
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}
such that

-c_{2}\leq\Psi_{*}(\mu)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\psi_{n}d\mu\leq-c_{1},\ \ \ \forall\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{A},T).

(2.5)

Assume that
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
(
s\neq t
). Using variational principle (see (
2.3
)) and noting that
\mu_{t}
is the equilibrium state of
t\Psi
by Theorem
2.1
(2), we have

\displaystyle Q(s)-Q(t)\geq h_{T}(\mu_{t})+(s\Psi)_{*}(\mu_{t})-Q(t)=(s-t)\Psi_{*}(\mu_{t}).

By interchanging
s
and
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle Q(s)-Q(t)\leq(s-t)\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s}).

This yields the following inequalities

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Psi_{*}(\mu_{s})&\leq\frac{Q(s)-Q(t)}{s-t}\leq\Psi_{*}(\mu_{t}),\ \ \text{if }\ s<t,\\
\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s})&\geq\frac{Q(s)-Q(t)}{s-t}\geq\Psi_{*}(\mu_{t}),\ \ \text{if }\ s>t.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

Claim :
Fix any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, if
\mu_{t_{n}}\to\mu
for
some sequence
t_{n}\to s
, then
\mu\equiv\mu_{s}
.

\lhd
If
\mu_{t_{n}}\to\mu
for some sequence
t_{n}\to s
, then by variational principle,

\displaystyle Q(s)\geq h_{T}(\mu)+s\Psi_{*}(\mu).

(2.7)

On the other hand, note that the entropy function
\mu\mapsto h_{T}(\mu)
is upper semicontinuous (see for example
[
46
]
) and the function
\mu\mapsto\Psi_{*}(\mu)
is continuous (see
[
2
, equation (36)]
). Then the function

\mu\mapsto h_{T}(\mu)+s\Psi_{*}(\mu)

is also upper semicontinuous. By Theorem
2.1
(2), for any
t_{n}\to s
, we have

\displaystyle h_{T}(\mu)+s\Psi_{*}(\mu)

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Big(h_{T}(\mu_{t_{n}})+s\Psi_{*}(\mu_{t_{n}})\Big)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Big(Q(t_{n})+(s-t_{n})\Psi_{*}(\mu_{t_{n}})\Big).

(2.8)

By Corollary
2.2
(1),
Q
is continuous and hence
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}Q(t_{n})=Q(s)
. Combining (
2.2
) and (
2.7
) yields the identity

\displaystyle h_{T}(\mu)+s\Psi_{*}(\mu)=Q(s).

Hence
\mu
must be an equilibrium measure of
s\Psi
. By the uniqueness of the equilibrium measure, hence
\mu\equiv\mu_{s}
, this establishes the statement.

\rhd

By equations (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and the Claim as above, we have

-c_{2}\leq Q^{\prime}(s)=\lim_{t\to s}\frac{Q(s)-Q(t)}{s-t}=\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s})\leq-c_{1}.

Thus
Q
is differentiable on
{\mathbb{R}}
and (
2.4
) holds. By Corollary
2.2
(1), it is convex on
{\mathbb{R}}
, we conclude that it is
C^{1}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
and
Q^{\prime}
is increasing.

(2) Now we prove that
Q^{\prime}
is strictly increasing on
{\mathbb{R}}
. Otherwise, there exist
s_{1}<s_{2}
such that
Q^{\prime}(s_{1})=Q^{\prime}(s_{2})
,
and hence we have

Q^{\prime}(s_{1})=Q^{\prime}(s_{2})=Q^{\prime}(s),\ \forall\ s\in(s_{1},s_{2}).

By (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s_{1}})=\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s_{2}})=\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s}),\ \forall\ s\in(s_{1},s_{2}).

(2.9)

Together with Theorem
2.1
(2), we have

\displaystyle Q(s_{1})

\displaystyle=h_{T}(\mu_{s_{1}})+s_{1}\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s_{1}}).

(2.10)

\displaystyle Q(s_{2})

\displaystyle=h_{T}(\mu_{s_{2}})+s_{2}\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s_{2}})=h_{T}(\mu_{s_{2}})+s_{2}\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s_{1}}).

(2.11)

Combining (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we get

\frac{Q(s_{1})-Q(s_{2})}{s_{1}-s_{2}}=\frac{h_{T}(\mu_{s_{1}})-h_{T}(\mu_{s_{2}})}{s_{1}-s_{2}}+\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s_{1}}).

(2.12)

Since
Q
is
C^{1}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
, there exists
\xi\in(s_{1},s_{2})
, such
that

\frac{Q(s_{1})-Q(s_{2})}{s_{1}-s_{2}}=Q^{\prime}(\xi)=\Psi_{*}(\mu_{\xi}).

(2.13)

By equations (
2.9
), (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), we conclude that
h_{T}(\mu_{s_{1}})=h_{T}(\mu_{s_{2}})
. By (
2.11
),

Q(s_{2})=h_{T}(\mu_{s_{2}})+s_{2}\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s_{1}})=h_{T}(\mu_{s_{1}})+s_{2}\Psi_{*}(\mu_{s_{1}}).

Specifically,
\mu_{s_{1}}
constitutes an equilibrium state of
s_{2}\Psi
; however, this directly contradicts Theorem
2.1
(2), which asserts that
Q^{\prime}
is
strictly increasing. Consequently
Q
is strictly convex on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

By Proposition
2.3
, the following two limits exist:

Q^{\prime}(-\infty):=\lim\limits_{s\to-\infty}Q^{\prime}(s),\ \ \ \ \ Q^{\prime}(\infty):=\lim\limits_{s\to+\infty}Q^{\prime}(s).

Corollary 2.4
.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Q^{\prime}(-\infty)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)=\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\psi_{n}(x),\\
&Q^{\prime}(\infty)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)=\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\psi_{n}(x).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.14)

Proof.

We only need to prove the first equation of (
2.14
), and the second one is similar.

Claim :

\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\inf\limits_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\inf\limits_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)=\inf\limits_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\psi_{n}(x).

\lhd

Since
\Psi\in\mathcal{F}^{-}(\Sigma_{A},T)
, then for any
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n+m}(x)\geq\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)+\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{m}(x)-C_{aa}(\Psi).

Thus
(C_{aa}(\Psi)-\inf\limits_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x))_{n\geq 1}
form a sub-additive sequence. Then it is well
known that the limit
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\inf\limits_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)
exists, and the first equality of this Claim holds.

Note that for any
y\in\Sigma_{A}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have
\inf\limits_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)\leq\psi_{n}(y)
, so dividing by
n
, and taking the lower limit and infimum for
y\in\Sigma_{A}
, we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)\leq\inf_{y\in\Sigma_{A}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\psi_{n}(y).

If the second equality of the Claim is not satisfied, the above inequality implies that there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, such that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Big(\psi_{n}(y)-\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)\Big)\geq\varepsilon_{0}\ \ \ \text{for all}\ y\in\Sigma_{A}.

We conclude that there exists some
C>0
such that for all
y\in\Sigma_{A}
and large
n
,

\psi_{n}(y)-\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)\geq C.

This clearly constitutes a contradiction.

\rhd

Since
\Psi
is negative, then for any
s<0
, we have

s\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)\leq\log\sum_{|w|=n}\exp(\sup_{x\in[w]}s\psi_{n}(x))\leq\log\#\{w:|w|=n\}+s\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x).

Dividing by
n
and taking the lower limit as
n\to\infty
, we conclude that

s\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)\leq Q(s)\leq\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\#\{w:|w|=n\}}{n}+s\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x).

Note that
\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\#\{w:|w|=n\}<\infty
. Now by Proposition
2.3
(1), we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\inf_{x\in\Sigma_{A}}\psi_{n}(x)=\lim\limits_{s\to-\infty}\frac{Q(s)}{s}=\lim\limits_{s\to-\infty}Q^{\prime}(s)=Q^{\prime}(-\infty).

This combines with Claim, thus showing the first equality of (
2.14
).
∎

2.3.

Recall that

Proposition 2.8

(1) Define

(2) Define

Proof.

(1) By

Now combine with Corollary

\displaystyle\log\tau_{2}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int S_{n}fd\mu^{\mathbf{a}}_{s}-3\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log a_{i}\leq\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\psi^{\mathbf{a}}_{n}d\mu^{\mathbf{a}}_{s}\leq\log\tau_{1}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int S_{n}fd\mu^{\mathbf{a}}_{s}.

Combining this with Proposition
4.8
, we obtain from (
4.17
) that

\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(s)}{\log\lambda}=\lim\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\Psi^{\mathbf{a}}_{*}(\mu^{\mathbf{a}}_{s})}{\log\lambda}=\int fd\mu^{\mathbf{a}}_{s}.

Note that
S_{n+m}f(\mathbf{v})=S_{n}f(\mathbf{v})+S_{m}f(T^{n}\mathbf{v})
, then

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}}S_{n+m}f(\mathbf{v})\geq\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}}S_{n}f(\mathbf{v})+\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}}S_{m}f(\mathbf{v}),

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}}S_{n+m}f(\mathbf{v})\leq\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}}S_{n}f(\mathbf{v})+\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}}S_{m}f(\mathbf{v}),

which implies that the limits in (
4.16
) exist. Since
\inf\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}}\psi^{\mathbf{a}}_{n}(\mathbf{v})=|B^{\check{\alpha}}_{nk+1,\min}|
, using Lemma
4.5
again, we see that

\displaystyle\log\tau_{2}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}}S_{n}f(\mathbf{v})-3\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log a_{i}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}|B^{\check{\alpha}}_{nk+1,\min}|\leq\log\tau_{1}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}}S_{n}f(\mathbf{v}).

Together with Proposition
4.8
and (

\lim\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(-\infty)}{\log\lambda}=\underline{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{a}).

A similar proof shows that the last equality in (

) holds. Now the result follows.

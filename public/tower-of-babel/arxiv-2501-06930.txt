Tightness criteria for random compact sets of cadlag paths
Nic Freeman, Jan M. Swart

Abstract
We give tightness criteria for random variables taking values in the space of all compact sets of cadlag real-valued paths, in terms of both the Skorohod J1 and M1 topologies. This extends earlier work motivated by the study of the Brownian web that was concerned only with continuous paths. In the M1 case, we give a natural extension of our tightness criteria which ensures that non-crossing systems of paths have weak limit points that are also non-crossing. This last result is exemplified through a rescaling of heavy tailed Poisson trees and a more general application to weaves.

1.1
Introduction

A central theme in probability theory is the weak convergence in law of stochastic processes,
the canonical example of which is the rescaling of random walks to Brownian motion and, more generally, to
\alpha
-stable processes.
The modern perspective is to treat a stochastic process as a single random variable,
a random path,
and to consider convergence in terms of probability laws on the space of all possible paths of the process.
Paths are usually assumed to be
cadlag
i.e. right-continuous with left limits,
with time domain
[0,\infty)
and values in some Polish space
M
.
The space
D_{[0,\infty)}(M)
of such paths is often equipped with Skorohod’s J1 topology, which is commonly known as ‘the’ Skorohod topology.
For real valued processes, the slightly weaker Skorohod M1 topology is sometimes required instead e.g.
[
Whi02
,
DGM24
]
.
Proofs of weak convergence typically involve establishing that
(1) a sequence of probability laws on the space of all paths is tight and
(2) has a unique weak cluster point;
the classical works
[
EK86
]
and
[
Bil99
]
are standard references for this approach.
For this reason, tightness criteria are of central importance.

The introduction of the Brownian web
[
FINR04
]
started interest in random variables that are not a single random path, but rather a random compact
set of
paths.
The framework for doing so established by
[
FINR04
]

for the Brownian web
(often known as ‘the Brownian web topology’)
treats continuous paths.
More recently random compact sets whose elements are paths with jumps have been considered
[
MRV19
,
FS24
]
,
along with surprising tightness failures for continuous paths that are related to the appearance of a small fraction of jumps in the limit
[
BMRV06
,
SSY21
]
.
This naturally asks for tightness criteria for random sets of cadlag paths,
which is the focus of the present article.

We consider systems in which different paths are typically defined on different time domains.
The Brownian web, for example,
covers space-time
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
with continuous paths
\pi
for which the time domains are of the form
[s,\infty)
,
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
is called the starting time of
\pi
.
This necessitates an underlying topology (on individual paths) that allows for the convergence of a sequence of paths that may all have different time domains.
In the work of
[
FINR04
]
, for continuous paths,
such convergence can roughly be described as locally uniform convergence of functions plus convergence of the starting times.
In
[
FS23
]
we generalised this principle by introducing a space of cadlag paths for which the time domains are
arbitrary
closed subsets of the real line.
Although our focus is on random sets of paths,
we note that this generalization is natural in even the most basic examples
e.g. we may then express the rescaling of discrete time random walks to continuous time processes
without involving (linear or otherwise) interpolation in between discrete times of the walk.

In
[
FS23
]
we showed that the space introduced therein of cadlag paths (with arbitrary closed subsets as time domains)
was Polish, for both the J1 and M1 cases.
Our present paper builds on this work by deriving tightness criteria for random compact subsets of this space,
in the case where paths are real valued.
The main result is Theorem
1.2
below.
It provides a robust criterion that involves checking a single property,
based on how frequently paths cross small intervals of
\mathbb{R}
.

When working with cadlag paths on varying time domains, one complication is that the property
of two paths not crossing each other
is not automatically preserved by taking limits.
This complication arises from jumps that may form at the starting times of limiting paths;
we remark that our state space permits this possibility,
because it is necessarily a feature of treating random sets of cadlag paths as compact sets.
The theory that has developed around the Brownian web has shown that systems of non-crossing paths
are substantially easier to work with than systems with crossing paths.
With this in mind, in Theorem
1.3
we note a natural analogue of our main result
which implies not only tightness, but also that all weak limit points are non-crossing.

We give the proofs in Section
2
followed by two applications of our results in Section
3
.
Sections
2
and
3
may be read independently of one another.
In Section
3.1
we give tightness criteria for weaves –
a
weave
, introduced in
[
FS24
]
,
is a random compact subset
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Pi^{\uparrow}

that is non-crossing and for which,
with probability one, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
there exists
\pi\in\mathcal{A}
such that
z\in\overline{\pi}

i.e. the paths cover space-time.
We show that, for weaves,
tightness essentially comes down to
tightness of the motion of a single particle.
In Section
3.2
we exemplify this result on a sequence of rescaled Poisson trees
in which the limiting motion of a single path is an
\alpha
-stable process.

1.2
Path space

We begin by recalling the necessary definitions and related background from
[
FS23
]
.
Let
\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=[-\infty,\infty]
. There exists a unique metrisable topology on

\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\rm c}:=(\overline{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R})\cup\big\{(\ast,-\infty),(\ast,+\infty)\big\}

(1.1)

such that
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\rm c}
is compact and a sequence
(x_{n},t_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\rm c}
converges to a limit
(x,t)
if and only if

(i)

t_{n}\rightarrow t
in the topology on
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,

(ii)

if
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then
x_{n}\rightarrow x
in the topology on
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
.

The space
R^{2}_{\rm c}
has earlier been introduced in
[
FINR04
]
. We can think of
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\rm c}
as being obtained from the space
\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
after squeezing the sets
\overline{\mathbb{R}}\times\{\pm\infty\}
into the single points
(\ast,\pm\infty)
. For this reason, we call
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\rm c}
the
squeezed space
. We let
d_{\rm sqz}
denote any metric generating the topology on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\rm c}
.

Let
I
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}
, let

I^{-}:=\big\{t\in I:(t-\varepsilon,t)\cap I\neq\emptyset\ \forall\varepsilon>0\big\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad I^{+}:=\big\{t\in I:(t,t+\varepsilon)\cap I\neq\emptyset\ \forall\varepsilon>0\big\}

(1.2)

denote the sets of points in
I
that can be approximated from the right or left, respectively, and let
I\ni t\mapsto\pi(t\pm)\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be right- and left-continuous functions such that

\pi(t-)=\lim_{s\uparrow t}\pi(s+)\quad(t\in I^{-})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\pi(t+)=\lim_{s\downarrow t}\pi(s-)\quad(t\in I^{+}).

(1.3)

We call the triple consisting of
I
and the functions
t\mapsto\pi(t-)
and
t\mapsto\pi(t+)
a
path
and we say that
I
is the
domain
of the path. We let
\Pi
denote the set of all paths. For
x,z\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
we write
[x,z]:=\{y\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}:x\wedge z\leq y\leq x\vee z\}
, and we set

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle\pi&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,t):t\in I,\ x\in\{\pi(t-),\pi(t+)\}\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\overline{\pi}&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,t):t\in I,\ x\in[\pi(t-),\pi(t+)]\big\}.\end{array}

(1.4)

We call
\pi
the
closed graph
and
\overline{\pi}
the
filled graph
of
\pi
. We equip these sets with a total order
\preceq
such that two elements
(x,s),(y,t)\in\pi,\overline{\pi}
are strictly ordered
(x,s)\prec(y,t)
if and only if either
s<t
, or
s=t
and
x
lies closer to
\pi(t-)
than
y
. Note that the set
I
as well as the right- and left-continuous functions
t\mapsto\pi(t+)
and
t\mapsto\pi(t-)
can be read off from the closed or filled graph together with the total order
\preceq
. For this reason, we often identify a path with its closed graph and denote a path simply as
\pi
and its domain by
I_{\pi}:=I
. We set

\pi^{\ast}:=\pi\cup\big\{(\ast,-\infty),(\ast,+\infty)\big\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{\pi}^{\ast}:=\overline{\pi}\cup\big\{(\ast,-\infty),(\ast,+\infty)\big\}.

(1.5)

By
[
FS23
, Lemma 3.1]

\pi^{\ast}
and
\overline{\pi}^{\ast}
are compact subsets of the squeezed space
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\rm c}
. We extend the total order
\preceq
to
\pi^{\ast}
and
\overline{\pi}^{\ast}
in such a way that
(-\infty,\ast)
is the minimal element and
(\infty,\ast)
the maximal element.

A
correspondence
between two sets
A,B
is a set
C\subset A\times B
such that

\forall a\in A\ \exists b\in B\mbox{ s.t.\ }(a,b)\in C\quad\mbox{and}\quad\forall b\in B\ \exists a\in A\mbox{ s.t.\ }(a,b)\in C.

(1.6)

We let
{\rm Corr}(A,B)
denote the set of all correspondences between
A
and
B
. If
{\cal X}
is a metrisable topological space, then we let
{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X})
denote the space of nonempty compact subsets of
{\cal X}
. If
d
is a metric generating the topology on
{\cal X}
, then the corresponding
Hausdorff metric
on
{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X})
is defined as

d_{\rm H}(K_{1},K_{2}):=\inf_{z_{1}\in K_{1}}d(z_{1},K_{2})\vee\inf_{z_{2}\in K_{2}}d(z_{2},K_{1})=\inf_{C\in{\rm Corr}(K_{1},K_{2})}\sup_{(z_{1},z_{2})\in C}d(z_{1},z_{2}),

(1.7)

where as usual
d(z,K):=\inf_{z^{\prime}\in K}d(z,z^{\prime})
denotes the distance of a point to a set. It follows from
[
SSS14
, Lemma B.1]
that the topology on
{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X})
only depends on the topology on
{\cal X}
and not on the choice of the metric. We call this the
Hausdorff topology
on
{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X})
. We note that
[
SSS14
, Lemmas B.2 and B3]
show that
{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X})
is Polish if
{\cal X}
is Polish, and compact if
{\cal X}
is compact.

For paths
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi
, we let
{\rm Corr}_{+}(\pi^{\ast}_{1},\pi^{\ast}_{2})
denote the set of correspondences
C
between
\pi^{\ast}_{1}
and
\pi^{\ast}_{2}
that are
monotone
in the sense that

\mbox{there are no $(z_{1},z_{2}),(z^{\prime}_{1},z^{\prime}_{2})\in C$ such that $z_{1}\prec_{1}z^{\prime}_{1}$ and $z^{\prime}_{2}\prec_{2}z_{2}$}.

(1.8)

If
d_{\rm sqz}
is a metric generating the topology on the squeezed space
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\rm c}
, then we define a corresponding metric
d_{\rm J1}
on the path space
\Pi
by

d_{\rm J1}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}):=\inf_{C\in{\rm Corr}_{+}(\pi^{\ast}_{1},\pi^{\ast}_{2})}\sup_{(z_{1},z_{2})\in C}d_{\rm sqz}(z_{1},z_{2})\qquad\big(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi\big).

(1.9)

We define
d_{\rm M1}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
in the same way, but with
{\rm Corr}_{+}(\pi^{\ast}_{1},\pi^{\ast}_{2})
replaced by
{\rm Corr}_{+}(\overline{\pi}^{\ast}_{1},\overline{\pi}^{\ast}_{2})
, the set of monotone correspondences between the compactified filled graphs
\overline{\pi}^{\ast}_{1}
and
\overline{\pi}^{\ast}_{2}
. It follows from
[
FS23
, Thm 2.10]
that the topologies generated by these metrics do not depend on the choice of the metric
d_{\rm sqz}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\rm c}
. By
[
FS23
, Prop 3.3]
, the space
\Pi
, equipped with either topology, is Polish.

When we say that
\Pi
is Polish we mean that
\Pi
is separable and there exists a complete metric
d
generating the topology on
\Pi
. This does not imply that the metrics
d_{\rm J1}
and
d_{\rm M1}
are complete, which, indeed, they are not.
As explained in
[

, Subsection 3.4]
, the topology generated by

corresponds to Skorohod’s J1-topology while

generates Skorohod’s M1-topology. Or rather, these are generalisations of these two classical topologies that allow for convergence of sequences of paths that need not all have the same domain.

We let
\Pi_{\rm c}
and
\Pi^{|}
denote the subspaces of
\Pi
consisting of
continuous
and

paths, respectively, defined as

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm c}&:=&\displaystyle\big\{\pi\in\Pi:\pi(t-)=\pi(t+)\ \forall t\in I_{\pi}\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\Pi^{|}&:=&\displaystyle\big\{\pi\in\Pi:I\mbox{ is an interval}\big\},\end{array}

(1.10)

and we naturally write
\Pi^{|}_{\rm c}:=\Pi_{\rm c}\cap\Pi^{|}
. We also write
\Pi^{\uparrow}
and
\Pi^{\downarrow}
for the subsets of

is the classical path space of the Brownian web

]
. For paths

we write

(t\in I_{\pi})
. As a result of

, the metrics
d_{\rm J1}
and

generate the same topology on
\Pi_{\rm c}
. It follows from

that
\Pi^{|}_{\rm c}
is closed as a subset of

in the J1-topology. We note that paths in
\Pi^{\uparrow}
can make a jump at their starting time. This is different from the usual conventions for the space of cadlag functions defined on
{[0,\infty)}
but will be crucial for Theorem

below as well as in future applications we have in mind.

1.3
Tightness for random compact sets of paths

Let
\Pi
be the path space introduced in the previous subsection, equipped with either the J1 or M1 topology, and let
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi)
be the space of nonempty compact subsets of
\Pi
, equipped with the Hausdorff topology. We note that since
\Pi
is Polish under both the J1 and M1 topologies, by
[
FS23
, Lemma 2.7]
the resulting topology on
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi)
is also Polish in either case. We will be interested in weak convergence of probability measures on
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi)
with respect to both the J1 and M1 topologies on
\Pi
. General topology tells us
2
2

2

In any metrisable space, a sequence
x_{n}
converges to a limit
x
if and only if the set
\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is precompact (i.e., its closure is compact) and
x
is its only cluster point (i.e., subsequential limit point).
that a sequence of probability measures
\mu_{n}
on
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi)
converges weakly to a limit if and only if the set
\{\mu_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is precompact and
\mu
is its only cluster point. Since
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi)
is Polish, Prohorov’s theorem tells us that a family
\{\mu_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
of probability measures on
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi)
is precompact if and only if it is
tight
, i.e., for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a compact
{\cal C}\subset\Pi
such that

\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mu_{\gamma}\big(\Pi\backslash{\cal C}\big)\leq\varepsilon.

(1.11)

We are therefore naturally interested in tightness criteria for families of probability measures on
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi)
, with respect to the J1 and M1 topologies on
\Pi
. As a warm-up, we discuss tightness on
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi_{\rm c})
, where
\Pi_{\rm c}
, defined in (
1.10
), is the space of continuous paths. As already mentioned, the metrics
d_{\rm J1}
and
d_{\rm M1}
generate the same topology on
\Pi_{\rm c}
.

For each
\pi\in\Pi
and real
T,\delta>0
, we set

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle\Delta^{2}_{T,\delta}(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,y):\exists-T\leq s\leq t\leq T\mbox{ s.t.\ }t-s\leq\delta\\
&&\displaystyle\phantom{\big\{(x,y):}\mbox{and }(x,s),(y,t)\in\pi,\ (x,s)\preceq(y,t)\big\}.\end{array}

(1.12)

For each
T,\delta,\varepsilon>0
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we define sets of paths by

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle{\cal S}^{+}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}&:=&\big\{\pi\in\Pi:\exists(x,y)\in\Delta^{2}_{T,\delta}(\pi)\mbox{ s.t.\ }x\leq r,\ r+\varepsilon\leq y\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle{\cal S}^{-}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}&:=&\big\{\pi\in\Pi:\exists(x,y)\in\Delta^{2}_{T,\delta}(\pi)\mbox{ s.t.\ }y\leq r,\ r+\varepsilon\leq x\big\},\end{array}

(1.13)

and we set
{\cal S}^{2}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}:={\cal S}^{+}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cup{\cal S}^{-}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}
.
The set
\displaystyle{\cal S}^{+}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}
comprises paths that cross
[r,r+\varepsilon]

from left to right within time
[-T,T]
,
with the condition that once the crossing has begun it takes time less than
\delta
to complete.
The set
\displaystyle{\cal S}^{-}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}
corresponds to crossings from right to left.
Our first result is an extension of earlier work
[
FINR04
, Prop B1]
which therein is restricted to the space
\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\rm c}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Tightness criterion for sets of continuous paths)

Let
({\cal A}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of random variables with values in
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi_{\rm c})
. Then the laws
(\mu_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
with
\mu_{\gamma}:=\mathbb{P}[{\cal A}_{\gamma}\in\,\cdot\,]
are tight with respect to the topology on
\Pi_{\rm c}
if and only if

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{P}\big[{\cal S}^{2}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cap{\cal A}_{\gamma}\neq\emptyset\big]=0\quad\forall T,\varepsilon>0,\ r\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.14)

We now turn our attention to paths with jumps. For each
\pi\in\Pi
and real
T,\delta>0
, we set

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle\Delta^{3}_{T,\delta}(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,y,z):\exists-T\leq s\leq t\leq u\leq T\mbox{ s.t.\ }u-s\leq\delta\\
&&\displaystyle\phantom{\big\{(x,y,z):}\mbox{and }(x,s),(y,t),(z,u)\in\pi,\ (x,s)\preceq(y,t)\preceq(z,u)\big\}.\end{array}

(1.15)

For each
T,\delta,\varepsilon>0
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we define sets of paths by

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle{\cal S}^{+-}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}&:=&\big\{\pi\in\Pi:\exists(x,y,z)\in\Delta^{3}_{T,\delta}(\pi)\mbox{ s.t.\ }x,z\leq r,\ r+\varepsilon\leq y\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle{\cal S}^{-+}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}&:=&\big\{\pi\in\Pi:\exists(x,y,z)\in\Delta^{3}_{T,\delta}(\pi)\mbox{ s.t.\ }y\leq r,\ r+\varepsilon\leq x,z\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle{\cal S}^{++}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}&:=&\big\{\pi\in\Pi:\exists(x,y,z)\in\Delta^{3}_{T,\delta}(\pi)\mbox{ s.t.\ }x\leq r,\ r+\varepsilon\leq y\leq r+2\varepsilon,\ r+3\varepsilon\leq z\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle{\cal S}^{--}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}&:=&\big\{\pi\in\Pi:\exists(x,y,z)\in\Delta^{3}_{T,\delta}(\pi)\mbox{ s.t.\ }z\leq r,\ r+\varepsilon\leq y\leq r+2\varepsilon,\ r+3\varepsilon\leq x\big\},\end{array}

(1.16)

and we set

{\cal S}^{\rm J}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}:={\cal S}^{++}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cup{\cal S}^{--}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\cal S}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}:={\cal S}^{+-}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cup{\cal S}^{-+}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}.

(1.17)

The set
\displaystyle{\cal S}^{+-}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}
comprises paths that cross
[r,r+\varepsilon]

from left to right, and then back again from right to left, during time
[-T,T]

with the whole exercise taking time less that
\delta
;
whilst
\displaystyle{\cal S}^{-+}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}
corresponds to similar movement from right to left.
The set
\displaystyle{\cal S}^{++}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}

comprises paths that cross
[r,r+\varepsilon]
from left to right,
then take a value in
[r+\varepsilon,r+2\varepsilon]
,
then cross
[r+2\varepsilon,r+3\varepsilon]
from left to right, during time
[-T,T]

with the whole exercise taking time less that
\delta
;
whilst
\displaystyle{\cal S}^{--}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}
corresponds to similar movement in opposite directions.

Our main result is the following theorem. Roughly, this says that the laws of
({\cal A}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
are tight with respect to the J1 topology on
\Pi
if the sets
{\cal A}_{\gamma}
do not contain paths that make two jumps in an arbitrarily brief time after each other. For the M1 topology, it suffices to look only at two jumps in opposite directions.

Theorem 1.2

(Tightness criteria for sets of cadlag paths)

Let
({\cal A}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of random variables with values in
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi)
. Then the laws
(\mu_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
with
\mu_{\gamma}:=\mathbb{P}[{\cal A}_{\gamma}\in\,\cdot\,]
are tight with respect to the J1 topology on
\Pi
if and only if

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ }l}{\rm(i)}&\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{P}\big[{\cal S}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cap{\cal A}_{\gamma}\neq\emptyset\big]=0\quad\forall T,\varepsilon>0,\ r\in\mathbb{R},\\[5.0pt]
{\rm(ii)}&\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{P}\big[{\cal S}^{\rm J}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cap{\cal A}_{\gamma}\neq\emptyset\big]=0\quad\forall T,\varepsilon>0,\ r\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}

(1.18)

The laws
(\mu_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
with
\mu_{\gamma}:=\mathbb{P}[{\cal A}_{\gamma}\in\,\cdot\,]
are tight with respect to the M1 topology on
\Pi
if and only if (
1.18
) (i) holds.

Note that if the laws of the
{\cal A}_{\gamma}
’s are invariant under translations then it suffices to check (
1.18
) (i) and (ii) for
r=0
. If the laws are invariant under reflection then we can replace
{\cal S}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}
by
{\cal S}^{+-}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}
and
{\cal S}^{\rm J}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}
by
{\cal S}^{++}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}
.

1.4
Noncrossing sets of paths

Recall from (
1.10
) that
\Pi^{|}
is the space of “connected” paths, whose domain is an interval, and
\Pi^{\updownarrow}
is the space of bi-infinite paths. Note that
\pi\in\Pi^{|}
if and only if its filled graph
\overline{\pi}
as defined in (
1.4
) is connected in the topological sense. We say that a path
\pi^{\prime}

extends
a path
\pi
if
\overline{\pi}\subset\overline{\pi}^{\prime}
. Following
[
FS23
]
, for
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{|}
, we write
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}
if
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
can be extended to bi-infinite paths
\pi^{\prime}_{1},\pi^{\prime}_{2}\in\Pi^{\updownarrow}
such that
\pi^{\prime}_{1}(t\pm)\leq\pi^{\prime}_{2}(t\pm)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We note that in spite of the suggestive notation, this relation is not transitive, i.e.,
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}\vartriangleleft\pi_{3}
does not imply
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{3}
. We say that a set
{\cal A}\subset\Pi^{|}
is
noncrossing
if each
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in{\cal A}
satisfy
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}
or
\pi_{2}\vartriangleleft\pi_{1}
(or both). Throughout the present subsection, we equip
\Pi^{|}
with the M1 topology and we equip

{\cal K}_{\rm nc}(\Pi^{|}):=\big\{{\cal A}\in{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi^{|}):{\cal A}\mbox{ is noncrossing}\big\}

(1.19)

with the corresponding Hausdorff topology. We will prove a tightness criterion for random variables with values in
{\cal K}_{\rm nc}(\Pi^{|})
. We note that when we say that a collection of probability measures is
tight on

{\cal K}_{\rm nc}(\Pi^{|})
, we mean that the compact subsets that occur in the definition of tightness (see (
1.11
)) are subsets of
{\cal K}_{\rm nc}(\Pi^{|})
(and not of some larger space). In particular, this implies that each weak cluster point is concentrated on
{\cal K}_{\rm nc}(\Pi^{|})
, which would not follow from the tightness criterion with respect to the M1 topology of Theorem
1.2
.

Nevertheless, the tightness criterion for noncrossing sets of paths turns out to be very similar to condition (
1.18
) (i) (though a bit stronger). To formulate it, for each
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi
and real
T,\delta>0
, we set

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle\Delta^{2}_{T,\delta}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2}):\exists-T\leq s_{i}\leq t_{i}\leq T\mbox{ s.t.\ }(x_{i},s_{i}),(y_{i},t_{i})\in\pi_{i},\\
&&\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\ (x_{i},s_{i})\preceq(y_{i},t_{i})\ (i=1,2)\mbox{ and }(t_{1}\vee t_{2})-(s_{1}\wedge s_{2})\leq\delta\big\},\end{array}

(1.20)

and we define

\displaystyle{\cal C}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}:=\big\{(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in\Pi^{2}:\exists(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})\in\Delta^{2}_{T,\delta}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\mbox{ s.t.\ }x_{1},y_{2}\leq r,\ r+\varepsilon\leq y_{1},x_{2}\big\}.

(1.21)

Theorem 1.3

(Tightness criterion for noncrossing sets of paths)

Equip
\Pi^{|}
with the M1 topology and
{\cal K}_{\rm nc}(\Pi^{|})
with the corresponding Hausdorff topology. Let
({\cal A}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of random variables with values in
{\cal K}_{\rm nc}(\Pi^{|})
. Then the laws
(\mu_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
with
\mu_{\gamma}:=\mathbb{P}[{\cal A}_{\gamma}\in\,\cdot\,]
are tight on
{\cal K}_{\rm nc}(\Pi^{|})
if and only if

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{P}\big[{\cal C}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cap({\cal A}_{\gamma}\times{\cal A}_{\gamma})\neq\emptyset\big]=0\quad\forall T,\varepsilon>0,\ r\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.22)

In words, the event
{\cal C}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cap({\cal A}_{\gamma}\times{\cal A}_{\gamma})\neq 0
occurs
when
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}
contains a path that crosses
[r,r+\varepsilon]
in one direction,
and another (perhaps different) path that crosses
[r,r+\varepsilon]
in the opposite direction,
with both events taking place within a single time window of length
\delta
between times
-T
and
T
.
As already discussed at the end of Section
1.1
, in Section
3
we give some applications of Theorem
1.3
.

It is easy to see that (
1.22
) implies (
1.18
) (i) (see Lemma
2.18
below), but the converse implication does not hold in general. For sets of bi-infinite paths, the two conditions are equivalent. This is a consequence of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
and the following lemma.

Lemma 1.4

(Sets of bi-infinite paths)

Let
\Pi
be equipped with the M1 topology and
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi)
with the corresponding Hausdorff topology. Then
{\cal K}_{\rm nc}(\Pi^{\updownarrow})
is a closed subset of
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi)
.

2.1
Convergence criteria

Let
{\cal X}
be a metrisable space, let
d
be any metric generating the topology, and let
{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X})
be the space of nonempty compact subsets of
{\cal X}
, equipped with the Hausdorff topology. We recall that by
[
SSS14
, Lemma B.3]
, if

is compact, then so is

. We cite the following lemma from
[
SSS14
, Lemma B.1]

Lemma 2.1

(Convergence in the Hausdorff topology)

Let
K_{n},K\in{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X})
. Then
K_{n}\rightarrow K
in the Hausdorff topology if and only if there exists a
C\in{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X})
such that

for all

and

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}K&\subset&\displaystyle\big\{x\in{\cal X}:\exists x_{n}\in K_{n}\mbox{ s.t.\ }x_{n}\rightarrow x\big\}\\[5.0pt]
&\subset&\displaystyle\big\{x\in{\cal X}:\exists x_{n}\in K_{n}\mbox{ s.t.\ $x$ is a cluster point of }(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big\}\subset K.\end{array}

(2.1)

Assume that
{\cal X}
is separable and locally compact. Let
{\cal X}_{\infty}:={\cal X}\cup\{\infty\}
denote its one-point compactification. It is well-known that
{\cal X}_{\infty}
is metrisable. We observe that
{\cal C}:=\{A\in{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X}_{\infty}):\infty\in A\}
is a closed subset of
{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X}_{\infty})
and hence compact. The map
A\mapsto A_{\infty}:=A\cup\{\infty\}
is a bijection from the space
{\rm Cl}({\cal X})
of closed subsets of
{\cal X}
to
{\cal C}
. We use this to equip
{\rm Cl}({\cal X})
with the metric
d_{\rm c}
defined as

d_{\rm c}(A,B):=d_{\rm H}\big(A_{\infty},B_{\infty}\big)\qquad\big(A,B\in{\rm Cl}({\cal X})\big),

(2.2)

where
d_{\rm H}
is the Hausdorff metric associated with the metric
d
on
{\cal X}
. By our previous observation,
\big({\rm Cl}({\cal X}),d_{\rm c}\big)
is compact. We call the topology generated by

the
local Hausdorff topology
. The following lemma shows that this topology does not depend on the choice of the metric
d
on
{\cal X}
.

Lemma 2.2

(The local Hausdorff topology)

Let
A_{n},A\in{\rm Cl}({\cal X})
. Then
A_{n}\rightarrow A
in the local Hausdorff topology if and only if

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}A&\subset&\displaystyle\big\{x\in{\cal X}:\exists x_{n}\in A_{n,\infty}\mbox{ s.t.\ }x_{n}\rightarrow x\big\}\\[5.0pt]
&\subset&\displaystyle\big\{x\in{\cal X}:\exists x_{n}\in A_{n,\infty}\mbox{ s.t.\ $x$ is a cluster point of }(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big\}\subset A.\end{array}

(2.3)

Proof
If (
2.3
) holds, then

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}A_{\infty}&\subset&\displaystyle\big\{x\in{\cal X}_{\infty}:\exists x_{n}\in A_{n,\infty}\mbox{ s.t.\ }x_{n}\rightarrow x\big\}\\[5.0pt]
&\subset&\displaystyle\big\{x\in{\cal X}_{\infty}:\exists x_{n}\in A_{n,\infty}\mbox{ s.t.\ $x$ is a cluster point of }(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big\}\subset A_{\infty}\end{array}

(2.4)

and conversely (
2.4

and the definition of

. Note that in (

Let

(2.5)

2.2
Compactness criteria

Let
d_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
be any metric generating the topology on the extended real line
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
d_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}(x,A):=\inf_{y\in A}d(x,y)
denote the distance of a point
x
to a subset
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. For any path
\pi\in\Pi
, we define moduli of continuity by

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle m_{T,\delta}(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\sup\big\{d_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}(x,y):(x,y)\in\Delta^{2}_{T,\delta}(\pi)\big\},\\[5.0pt]

\displaystyle m^{\rm M}_{T,\delta}(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\sup\big\{d_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}(y,[x,z]):(x,y,z)\in\Delta^{3}_{T,\delta}(\pi)\big\}.\end{array}

(2.10)

Then
[
FS23
, Thms 3.6 and 3.7]
tell us the following.

Theorem 2.5

(Compactness criteria)

A set
{\cal A}\subset\Pi_{\rm c}
is precompact if and only if

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\pi\in{\cal A}}m_{T,\delta}(\pi)=0\quad\forall T>0.

(2.11)

A set
{\cal A}\subset\Pi
is precompact with respect to the J1 topology if and only if

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\pi\in{\cal A}}m^{\rm J}_{T,\delta}(\pi)=0\quad\forall T>0.

(2.12)

A set
{\cal A}\subset\Pi
is precompact with respect to the M1 topology if and only if

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\pi\in{\cal A}}m^{\rm M}_{T,\delta}(\pi)=0\quad\forall T>0.

(2.13)

We moreover mention
[
FS23
, Lemma 2.8]
which says the following.

Lemma 2.6

(Compactness in the Hausdorff topology)

Let
{\cal X}
be a metrisable topological space and let
{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X})
be the space of nonempty compact subsets of
{\cal X}
, equipped with the Hausdorff topology. Then a set
{\cal A}\subset{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X})
is precompact if and only if there exists a
C\in{\cal K}_{+}({\cal X})
such that
K\subset C
for all
K\in{\cal A}
.

2.5
and Lemma

allow us to characterise the precompact subsets of

, respectively of

Theorem 2.7

(Tightness criterion for sets of continuous paths)

Let
({\cal A}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of random variables with values in
{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi_{\rm c})
. Then the laws
(\mu_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
with
\mu_{\gamma}:=\mathbb{P}[{\cal A}_{\gamma}\in\,\cdot\,]

if and only if

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{P}\big[\sup_{\pi\in{\cal A}_{\gamma}}m_{T,\delta}(\pi)>\varepsilon\big]=0\quad\forall T,\varepsilon>0.

(2.14)

Proof
Assume that the laws
(\mu_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
are tight. Then for each
\eta>0
, there exists a compact
{\mathbf{C}}_{\eta}\subset{\cal K}_{+}(\Pi_{\rm c})
such that

\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{P}\big[{\cal A}_{\gamma}\in{\mathbf{C}}_{\eta}\big]\geq 1-\eta.

(2.15)

By Lemma
2.6
, there exists a compact

implies
{\cal A}\subset{\cal C}_{\eta}

\eta>0

(2.16)

By (

) of Theorem

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}w_{T,\eta}(\delta)=0\quad(T>0,\ \gamma\in\Gamma)\quad\mbox{with}\quad w_{T,\eta}(\delta):=\sup_{\pi\in{\cal C}_{\eta}}m_{T,\delta}(\pi)\quad(T,\delta>0,\ \gamma\in\Gamma).

Fix
T,\varepsilon>0
. It follows that for each
\eta>0
, there exists a

such that

and hence, by (

) holds. Formula (

(2.19)

Summing over
T
and

\mathbb{P}\big[\exists T,n\geq 1\mbox{ s.t.\ }\sup_{\pi\in{\cal A}_{\gamma}}m_{T,\delta_{n}(T,\eta)}(\pi)>n^{-1}\big]\leq\eta\qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma).

(2.20)

Setting

(2.21)

our previous formula says that

\mathbb{P}\big[{\cal A}_{\gamma}\subset{\cal C}_{\eta}\big]\geq 1-\eta\qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma).

(2.22)

We observe that
\delta\leq\delta_{n}(T,\eta)
implies

and hence by the definition of

It follows that

\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\pi\in{\cal C}_{\eta}}m_{T,\delta}(\pi)\leq n^{-1}\quad\forall T,n\geq 1,

which by (

By Lemma

, this implies that the laws

Lemma 2.9

(Alternative tightness criteria)

Condition (

) is equivalent to

(2.32)

Condition (
2.26
) with

replaced by

is equivalent to

(2.33)

Proof
We have
\sup_{\pi\in{\cal A}_{\gamma}}m_{T,\delta}(\pi)>\eta
if and only if there exist

and

, so (

so (
2.26

In what follows, it will be convenient to make a concrete choice for the metric

Lemma 2.10

(Jumping over intervals)

For each
\varepsilon>0
and

and

For each

Proof
Fix
\varepsilon>0

x,y\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
satisfy
x<y

and hence the set

k<k_{-}(\varepsilon)
, then also
k_{-}(\varepsilon)\in K
. Indeed
k\in K
and

imply
x\leq k\varepsilon<k_{-}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon
while the observation that

d_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}(-\infty,y)\geq d_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}(x,y)>2\varepsilon>d_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}\big(-\infty,k_{-}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon\big)+\varepsilon>d_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}\big(-\infty,(k_{-}(\varepsilon)+1)\varepsilon\big)

implies

The proof of the second claim goes a bit differently. Assume that

satisfy
x<y

. The same argument gives
(k+3)\varepsilon\leq z

2.4
Noncrossing paths

In this subsection we prepare for the proof of Theorem
1.3
. For
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{|}
, we say that
\pi_{1}

crosses

\pi_{2}
if
\pi_{1}\not\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}
and
\pi_{2}\not\vartriangleleft\pi_{1}
, i.e., if the set
\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\}
is not noncrossing in the sense defined in Subsection
1.4
. We say that
\pi_{1}

collides
with
\pi_{2}

at time

t
if
t\in I_{\pi_{1}}\cap I_{\pi_{2}}
and
\pi_{1}(t\pm),\pi_{2}(t\mp)<\pi_{1}(t\mp),\pi_{2}(t\pm)
, where the sign
\pm
can be either
+
or
-
and
\mp
is the opposite sign. In words, this says that at time
t
the paths
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
jump over some interval in opposite directions. It is not hard to see that if
\pi_{1}
collides with
\pi_{2}
at some time
t
, then
\pi_{1}
crosses
\pi_{2}
. We let
I^{\circ}
denote the interior of a closed real interval and let
\partial I:=I\backslash I^{\circ}
denote the set of its finite boundary points. We will prove the following result. Note that this shows in particular that for bi-infinite paths, the noncrossing property is preserved under limits.

Proposition 2.12

(Crossing in the limit)

Assume that
\pi^{n}_{1},\pi^{n}_{2}\in\Pi^{|}
satisfy
\pi^{n}_{i}\rightarrow\pi_{i}
as
n\rightarrow\infty

(i=1,2)
in the M1 topology for some
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{|}
, and that
\pi^{n}_{1}
does not cross
\pi^{n}_{2}
for any
n
. Then precisely one of the following statements must hold:

(i)

\pi_{1}
does not cross
\pi_{2}
,

(ii)

\pi_{1}
collides with
\pi_{2}
at some time
t\in\partial I_{\pi_{1}}\cup\partial I_{\pi_{2}}
.

Proposition
2.12
extends
[
FS24
, Lemma 3.4.9]
. It is not hard to guess the statement of Proposition
2.12
after scribbling a few figures on a piece of paper, but giving a precise proof is a bit more work. Our proof follows a clear strategy but is nevertheless quite long, so we challenge the reader to find a shorter one.

To prepare for the proof of Proposition
2.12
, we start by giving a more direct characterisation of the relation
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}
. The
split real line
is the set
\mathbb{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}
consisting of all words of the form
t\star
with
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\star\in\{-,+\}
.
For each
\pi\in\Pi^{|}
, we define
I^{\mathfrak{s}}_{\pi}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}
by

I^{\mathfrak{s}}_{\pi}:=\big\{t\star:t\in I_{\pi},\ \star\in\{-,+\},\ t\star\neq s-,u+\big\},

(2.50)

and letting
s:=\inf I_{\pi}
and
u:=\sup I_{\pi}
denote the starting time and final time of
\pi
, which may be
\pm\infty
, we define

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle I^{\rm l}_{\pi}&:=&\displaystyle I^{\mathfrak{s}}_{\pi}\cup\big\{s-:s\in\mathbb{R},\ \pi(s+)<\pi(s-)\big\}\cup\big\{u+:u\in\mathbb{R},\ \pi(u-)<\pi(u+)\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle I^{\rm r}_{\pi}&:=&\displaystyle I^{\mathfrak{s}}_{\pi}\cup\big\{s-:s\in\mathbb{R},\ \pi(s-)<\pi(s+)\big\}\cup\big\{u+:u\in\mathbb{R},\ \pi(u+)<\pi(u-)\big\},\end{array}

(2.51)

i.e., we include
s-
in
I^{\rm l}_{\pi}
only if
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\pi(s+)<\pi(s-)
, and so on. Finally, we set

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle L(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,t\pm)\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}\times I^{\rm l}_{\pi}:x<\pi(t\pm)\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle R(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,t\pm)\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}\times I^{\rm r}_{\pi}:\pi(t\pm)<x\big\}.\end{array}

(2.52)

Note that these definitions simplify a lot for bi-infinite paths. The following lemma gives a more direct characterisation of the relation
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}
.

Lemma 2.13

(Ordering of paths)

Two paths
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{|}
satisfy
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}
if and only if
L(\pi_{1})\cap R(\pi_{2})=\emptyset
.

Proof
This is only a slight extension of
[
FS23
, Lemma 3.3.2]
but for completeness we give the proof. For bi-infinite paths
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{\updownarrow}
the statement is trivial. It is also straightforward to check that if
\pi^{\prime}
extends
\pi
, then
L(\pi)\subset L(\pi^{\prime})
and
R(\pi)\subset R(\pi^{\prime})
. In view of this, the condition
L(\pi_{1})\cap R(\pi_{2})=\emptyset
is clearly necessary for
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}
. To prove sufficiency, we will prove the following statement:

(!)

Assume that
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{|}
satisfy
L(\pi_{1})\cap R(\pi_{2})=\emptyset
. Then there exist extensions
\pi^{\prime}_{i}
of
\pi_{i}

(i=1,2)
such that
\pi^{\prime}_{1}\in\Pi^{\uparrow}
and
L(\pi^{\prime}_{1})\cap R(\pi^{\prime}_{2})=\emptyset
.

By symmetry, it then follows that we can also exted
\pi_{2}
to
+\infty
and similarly both paths to
-\infty
while preserving the property that
L(\pi_{1})\cap R(\pi_{2})=\emptyset
, which for bi-infinite paths trivially implies
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}
. It therefore remains to prove (!). Let
s_{i}:=\inf I_{\pi_{i}}
and
u_{i}:=\sup I_{\pi_{i}}
. The cases
u_{1}=\pm\infty
are trivial so without loss of generality we assume that
u_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
. The cases
u_{1}<s_{2}
and
u_{1}>u_{2}
are trivial so without loss of generality we can assume that
s_{2}\leq u_{1}\leq u_{2}
. If
\pi_{1}(u_{1}+)\leq\pi_{1}(u_{1}-)
, then we can simply extend
\pi_{1}
to the path that jumps to
-\infty
at time
u_{1}
and stays there, so we are done. If
\pi_{1}(u_{1}-)<\pi_{1}(u_{1}+)
, then
u_{1}+\in I^{\rm l}_{\pi}
and hence
(x,u_{1}+)\in L(\pi)
for all
x<\pi_{1}(u_{1}+)
. We now distinguish two cases: I.
u_{1}<u_{2}
, and II.
u_{1}=u_{2}=:u
. In case I, the condition
L(\pi_{1})\cap R(\pi_{2})=\emptyset
implies
\pi_{1}(u_{1}+)\leq\pi_{2}(u_{1}+)
, so we can extend
\pi_{1}
to the path that jumps to
\pi_{2}(u_{1}+)
at time
u_{1}
and from then on stays equal to
\pi_{2}
. Case II is a bit more tricky. If
\pi_{1}(u+)\leq\pi_{2}(u+)
we can proceed as in case I but it may also happen that
\pi_{2}(u+)<\pi_{1}(u+)
. If this happens, however, then the condition
L(\pi_{1})\cap R(\pi_{2})=\emptyset
forces
\pi_{2}(u-)\leq\pi_{2}(u+)
so in this case we can first extend
\pi_{2}
to a path
\pi^{\prime}_{2}
satisfying
\pi_{1}(u+)\leq\pi_{2}(u+)
and then again proceed as in case I.

We can now also give a more direct characterisation of when two paths cross each other.

Lemma 2.14

(Crossing of paths)

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{|}
. Then
\pi_{1}
crosses
\pi_{2}
if and only if
L(\pi_{1})\cap R(\pi_{2})\neq\emptyset
and
L(\pi_{2})\cap R(\pi_{1})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof
Immediate from Lemma
2.13
.

We next start to investigate how crossing behaves under limits. For any
\pi\in\Pi^{|}
, we let
\pi^{\circ}
denote the path whose domain is the closure of
I^{\circ}_{\pi}
and that is equal to
\pi
on
I^{\circ}_{\pi}
and does not jump at its finite boundary points. In other words, if
I_{\pi}
is an interval of positive length, then
\pi^{\circ}
is obtained from
\pi
by removing jumps at finite boundary points, and if
I_{\pi}
consists of a single point or is empty, then
\pi^{\circ}
is the trivial path with
I_{\pi^{\circ}}=\emptyset
. Recall from (
2.5
) that
\mathbb{S}:=\overline{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}
. For any
\pi\in\Pi^{|}
we write

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle L^{\circ}(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}:t\in I^{\circ}_{\pi},\ x<\pi(t-)\wedge\pi(t+)\big\},\quad L^{\rm c}(\pi):=\mathbb{S}\backslash L^{\circ}(\pi),\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\overline{L}(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}:t\in I_{\pi},\ x\leq\pi(t-)\vee\pi(t+)\big\}.\end{array}

(2.53)

We define
R^{\circ}(\pi),R^{\rm c}(\pi)
, and
\overline{R}(\pi)
analogously, with
x<\pi(t-)\wedge\pi(t+)
replaced by
\pi(t-)\vee\pi(t+)<x
etc. These sets look a lot like the sets
L(\pi)
and
R(\pi)
from (
2.52
), but they are better behaved under limits. Note that
L^{\circ}(\pi)
is open while
L^{\rm c}(\pi)
and
\overline{L}(\pi)
are closed. Note also that
L^{\circ}(\pi)=L^{\circ}(\pi^{\circ})
and
\overline{L}(\pi^{\circ})
is the closure of
L^{\circ}(\pi)
.

Lemma 2.15

(Areas left of a path)

Assume that
\pi_{n}\in\Pi^{|}
satisfy
\pi_{n}\rightarrow\pi
as
n\rightarrow\infty
in the M1 topology for some
\pi\in\Pi^{|}
. Then
\overline{L}(\pi_{n})\rightarrow\overline{L}(\pi)
and
L^{\rm c}(\pi_{n})\rightarrow L^{\rm c}(\pi)
in the local Hausdorff topology on
{\rm Cl}(\mathbb{S})
.

Proof
Assume that
\pi_{n}\rightarrow\pi
in the M1 topology and the filled graphs
\overline{\pi}_{n}
are not empty. Then by Lemma
2.4

\overline{\pi}_{n}\rightarrow\overline{\pi}
in the local Hausdorff topology on
{\rm Cl}(\mathbb{S})
, which by Lemma
2.2
means that:

(i)

for each
z\in\overline{\pi}
there exist
z_{n}\in\overline{\pi}_{n}
such that
z_{n}\rightarrow z
,

(ii)

if
z_{n}\in\overline{\pi}_{n}
have a cluster point
z
, then
z\in\overline{\pi}
.

It is not hard to see that moreover
I_{\pi_{n}}\rightarrow I_{\pi}
in the local Hausdorff topology on
{\rm Cl}(\mathbb{R})
.

We will only prove that
L^{\rm c}(\pi_{n})\rightarrow L^{\rm c}(\pi)
. The proof that
\overline{L}(\pi_{n})\rightarrow\overline{L}(\pi)
is similar, but easier. We assume without loss of generality that
\overline{\pi}_{n}\neq\emptyset
for all
n
. We note that

L^{\rm c}(\pi)=\big\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}:t\not\in I^{\circ}_{\pi}\big\}\cup\big\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}:t\in I^{\circ}_{\pi}\mbox{ and }\pi(t-)\wedge\pi(t+)\leq x\big\}.

(2.54)

By Lemma
2.2
, we have to show that:

(i)’

for each
z\in L^{\rm c}(\pi)
there exist
z_{n}\in L^{\rm c}(\pi_{n})
such that
z_{n}\rightarrow z
,

(ii)’

if
z_{n}\in L^{\rm c}(\pi_{n})
have a cluster point
z
, then
z\in L^{\rm c}(\pi)
.

To prove (i)’, assume that
(x,t)\in L^{\rm c}(\pi)
. Then either 1.
t\not\in I^{\circ}_{\pi}
or 2.
t\in I^{\circ}_{\pi}
and
\pi(t-)\wedge\pi(t+)\leq x
. In case 1, we can use that
I_{\pi_{n}}\rightarrow I_{\pi}
to choose
t_{n}\not\in I^{\circ}_{\pi_{n}}
such that
t_{n}\rightarrow t
. Now
(x,t_{n})\in L^{\rm c}(\pi_{n})
satisfy
(x,t_{n})\rightarrow(x,t)
. In case 2, we set
y:=\pi(t-)\wedge\pi(t+)
. Then
(y,t)\in\overline{\pi}
and hence by (i) there exist
(y_{n},t_{n})\in\overline{\pi}_{n}
such that
(y_{n},t_{n})\rightarrow(y,t)
. Since
y\leq x
it follows that
L^{\rm c}(\pi)\ni(y_{n}\vee x,t_{n})\rightarrow(x,t)
.

To prove (ii)’, assume that
(x_{n},t_{n})\in L^{\rm c}(\pi_{n})
have a cluster point
(x,t)
. By going to a subsequence, we may assume that
(x_{n},t_{n})\rightarrow(x,t)
and either 1.
t_{n}\not\in I^{\circ}_{\pi_{n}}
for all
n
or 2.
t_{n}\in I^{\circ}_{\pi_{n}}
and
\pi_{n}(t_{n}-)\wedge\pi_{n}(t_{n}+)\leq x_{n}
for all
n
. In case 1 by the fact that
I_{\pi_{n}}\rightarrow I_{\pi}
we have
t=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}t_{n}\not\in I^{\circ}_{\pi}
so
(x,t)\in L^{\rm c}(\pi)
and we are done. Case 2 is trivial if
t\not\in I^{\circ}_{\pi}
so without loss of generality we assume
t\in I^{\circ}_{\pi}
. Then
t_{n}\in I^{\circ}_{\pi}
for
n
large enough so by going to a further subsequence if necessary, we can assume that
t_{n}\in I^{\circ}_{\pi}
for all
n
and
y_{n}:=\pi_{n}(t_{n}-)\wedge\pi_{n}(t_{n}+)
converge to a limit
y\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
\overline{\pi}_{n}\ni(y_{n},t_{n})\rightarrow(y,t)
so by (i)
(y,t)\in\overline{\pi}
and hence
\pi(t-)\wedge\pi(t+)\leq y
. Since
y_{n}\leq x_{n}
we have
y\leq x
so we conclude that
(x,t)\in L^{\rm c}(\pi)
and we are done.

We next need a lemma that characterises
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}
in terms of the sets
L^{\circ},\overline{L},R^{\circ}
, and
\overline{R}
. Below, for any real interval
I
with
s:=\inf I
and
u:=\sup I
, we set

\partial^{-}I:=\{s\}\cap\mathbb{R}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial^{+}I:=\{u\}\cap\mathbb{R},

(2.55)

i.e.,
\partial^{-}I
and
\partial^{+}I
are the sets of finite lower and upper boundary points of

.

Lemma 2.16

(Ordering of non-colliding paths)

Assume that
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{|}
. Then one has
\pi_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap\overline{R}(\pi_{2})=\emptyset
,

(ii)

\overline{L}(\pi_{1})\cap R^{\circ}(\pi_{2})=\emptyset
,

(iii)

there does not exist a
t\in\partial^{\pm}I_{\pi_{1}}\cup\partial^{\mp}I_{\pi_{2}}
with
t\in I_{\pi_{1}}\cap I_{\pi_{2}}
such that
\pi_{1}(t\pm),\pi_{2}(t\mp)<\pi_{1}(t\mp),\pi_{2}(t\pm)
.

Proof
Recall that
\pi^{\circ}
is the path
\pi
with jumps at finite boundary points of the domain removed. We start by proving that the following conditions are equivalent:

{\rm(a)}\ \pi^{\circ}_{1}\not\vartriangleleft\pi^{\circ}_{2},\quad{\rm(b)}\ L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap R^{\circ}(\pi_{2})\neq\emptyset\quad{\rm(c)}\ L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap\overline{R}(\pi^{\circ}_{2})\neq\emptyset.

(2.56)

By Lemma
2.13
and the definitions of
\pi^{\circ},L(\pi),L^{\circ}(\pi),\overline{L}(\pi)
etcetera, we see that (a)–(c) are equivalent to, respectively,

(a)’

\pi_{2}(t\star)<\pi_{1}(t\star)\mbox{ for some }t\star\in I^{\mathfrak{s}}_{\pi_{1}}\cap I^{\mathfrak{s}}_{\pi_{2}}
,

(b)’

\pi_{2}(t-)\wedge\pi_{2}(t+)<\pi_{1}(t-)\vee\pi_{1}(t+)\mbox{ for some }t\in I^{\circ}_{\pi_{1}}\cap I^{\circ}_{\pi_{2}}
,

(c)’

\pi_{2}(t-)\wedge\pi_{2}(t+)<\pi_{1}(t-)\wedge\pi_{1}(t+)\mbox{ for some }t\in I^{\circ}_{\pi_{1}}\cap I^{\circ}_{\pi_{2}}
.

The implication (c)’
\Rightarrow
(a)’ is trivial and the implication (a)’
\Rightarrow
(b)’ follows from the observation that each
t-\in I^{\mathfrak{s}}_{\pi_{1}}\cap I^{\mathfrak{s}}_{\pi_{2}}
can be approximated from below by
t_{n}\in I^{\circ}_{\pi_{1}}\cap I^{\circ}_{\pi_{2}}
and likewise each
t+\in I^{\mathfrak{s}}_{\pi_{1}}\cap I^{\mathfrak{s}}_{\pi_{2}}
can be approximated from above. The implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(c), finally, follows from the fact that
\overline{R}(\pi^{\circ}_{2})
is the closure of
R^{\circ}(\pi_{2})
, so all three conditions are equivalent.

For any
\pi\in\Pi^{|}
with initial and final time
s:=\inf I_{\pi}
and
u:=\sup I_{\pi}
, let us set (compare (
2.51
))

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle L^{-}(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,s-):s\in\mathbb{R},\ \pi(s+)<\pi(s-)\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle L^{+}(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,u+):s\in\mathbb{R},\ \pi(u-)<\pi(u+)\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle R^{-}(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,s-):s\in\mathbb{R},\ \pi(s-)<\pi(s+)\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle R^{+}(\pi)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{(x,u+):s\in\mathbb{R},\ \pi(u+)<\pi(u-)\big\}.\end{array}

(2.57)

Then, by Lemma
2.13
,
\pi_{1}\not\vartriangleleft\pi_{2}
is equivalent to

\big(L^{-}(\pi)\cup L(\pi^{\circ})\cup L^{+}(\pi)\big)\cap\big(R^{-}(\pi)\cup R(\pi^{\circ})\cup R^{+}(\pi)\big)\neq\emptyset.

(2.58)

We observe that (
2.58
) holds precisely if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

I.

L(\pi^{\circ}_{1})\cap R(\pi^{\circ}_{2})\neq\emptyset
,

II.

L(\pi^{\circ}_{1})\cap R^{\star}(\pi_{2})\neq\emptyset
for some
\star\in\{-,+\}
,

III.

L^{\star}(\pi_{1})\cap R(\pi^{\circ}_{2})\neq\emptyset
for some
\star\in\{-,+\}
,

IV.

L^{\star}(\pi_{1})\cap R^{\ast}(\pi_{2})\neq\emptyset
for some
\star,\ast\in\{-,+\}
.

For any
\pi\in\Pi^{|}
and
t\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, let
\pi^{t}
denote the path with domain
I_{\pi^{t}}:=I_{\pi}\cap\{t\}
defined by
\pi^{t}(t\pm):=\pi(t\pm)
if
t\in I_{\pi}
. We claim that the conditions I–IV are equivalent, respectively, to:

I’.

L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap R^{\circ}(\pi_{2})\neq\emptyset
,

II’.

\exists t\in I^{\circ}_{\pi_{1}}\cap\partial^{\mp}I_{\pi_{2}}\mbox{ s.t.\ }L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap\overline{R}(\pi^{t}_{2})\neq\emptyset
or
\pi_{1}(t\pm),\pi_{2}(t\mp)<\pi_{1}(t\mp),\pi_{2}(t\pm)
,

III’.

\exists t\in\partial^{\pm}I_{\pi_{1}}\cap I^{\circ}_{\pi_{2}}\mbox{ s.t.\ }\overline{L}(\pi^{t}_{1})\cap R^{\circ}(\pi_{2})\neq\emptyset
or
\pi_{1}(t\pm),\pi_{2}(t\mp)<\pi_{1}(t\mp),\pi_{2}(t\pm)
,

IV’.

\exists t\in\partial^{\pm}I_{\pi_{1}}\cap\partial^{\mp}I_{\pi_{2}}\mbox{ s.t.\ }\pi_{1}(t\pm),\pi_{2}(t\mp)<\pi_{1}(t\mp),\pi_{2}(t\pm)
.

The equivalence of I and I’ has already been proved. Let us set
s_{i}:=\inf I_{\pi_{i}}
and
u_{i}:=\sup I_{\pi_{i}}

(i=1,2)
. We observe that
L(\pi^{\circ}_{1})\cap R^{-}(\pi_{2})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
s_{2}\in I^{\circ}_{\pi_{1}}
,
\pi_{2}(s_{2}-)<\pi_{2}(s_{2}+)
, and
\pi_{2}(s_{2}-)<\pi_{1}(s_{2}-)
. There are now two possibilities: if
\pi_{2}(s_{2}-)<\pi_{1}(s_{2}+)
, then
L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap\overline{R}(\pi^{s_{2}}_{2})\neq\emptyset
, and if
\pi_{1}(s_{2}+)\leq\pi_{2}(s_{2}-)
, then
\pi_{1}(s_{2}+),\pi_{2}(s_{2}-)<\pi_{1}(s_{2}-),\pi_{2}(s_{2}+)
. The argument is the same if
L(\pi^{\circ}_{1})\cap R^{-}(\pi_{2})\neq\emptyset
, except that now all signs are reversed so we arrive at the condition that
L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap\overline{R}(\pi^{u_{2}}_{2})\neq\emptyset
or
\pi_{1}(u_{2}-),\pi_{2}(u_{2}+)<\pi_{1}(u_{2}+),\pi_{2}(u_{2}-)
. This proves the equivalence of II and II’. The equivalence of III and III’ follows from the same argument, again with all signs reversed, and the proof of the equivalence of IV and IV’ is similar.

To complete the proof, it now suffices to observe that
L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap\overline{R}(\pi_{2})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap R^{\circ}(\pi_{2})\neq\emptyset
or there exists a
t\in I^{\circ}_{\pi_{1}}\cap\partial I_{\pi_{2}}
such that
L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap\overline{R}(\pi^{t}_{2})\neq\emptyset
, and the condition
L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap\overline{R}(\pi_{2})\neq\emptyset
can be rewritten similarly.

The final ingredient in the proof of Proposition
2.12
is the following lemma.

Lemma 2.17

(Limits of ordered paths)

Assume that
\pi^{n}_{1},\pi^{n}_{2}\in\Pi^{|}
satisfy
\pi^{n}_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi^{n}_{2}
for all
n
and that
\pi^{n}_{i}\rightarrow\pi_{i}
as

(i=1,2)

. Then

and
\overline{L}(\pi_{1})\cap R^{\circ}(\pi_{2})=\emptyset
.

Proof
By symmetry, it suffices to prove that
L^{\circ}(\pi_{1})\cap\overline{R}(\pi_{2})=\emptyset
or equivalently
\overline{R}(\pi_{2})\subset L^{\rm c}(\pi_{1})
. Since
\pi^{n}_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi^{n}_{2}
for all
n
, Lemma
2.16
tells us that
\overline{R}(\pi^{n}_{2})\subset L^{\rm c}(\pi^{n}_{1})
for all

2.15
, using Lemma
2.2
.

Proof of Proposition
2.12

Assume that
\pi^{n}_{1},\pi^{n}_{2}\in\Pi^{|}
satisfy
\pi^{n}_{i}\rightarrow\pi_{i}
as
n\rightarrow\infty

(i=1,2)
in the M1 topology for some
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{|}
, and that
\pi^{n}_{1}
does not cross
\pi^{n}_{2}
for any
n
. It is clear from Lemma
2.14
that (i) and (ii) cannot hold simultaneously, so it suffices to prove that if
\pi_{1}
does not collide with
\pi_{2}
at any time

, then
\pi_{1}
does not cross
\pi_{2}
.

Since
\pi^{n}_{1}
does not cross
\pi^{n}_{2}
for any
n
, by going to a subsequence, we can assume that either
\pi^{n}_{1}\vartriangleleft\pi^{n}_{2}
for all

or
\pi^{n}_{2}\vartriangleleft\pi^{n}_{1}
for all

tells us that

Lemma 2.18

(Jumps in opposite directions)

Let
{\cal A}
be a compact subset of
\Pi^{|}
, let

{\cal S}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cap{\cal A}_{\gamma}\neq\emptyset\quad\mbox{implies}\quad{\cal C}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cap({\cal A}_{\gamma}\times{\cal A}_{\gamma})\neq\emptyset.

Proof

{\cal S}^{+-}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cap{\cal A}_{\gamma}\neq\emptyset
if and only if:

(S)

There exists a
\pi\in{\cal A}
and

{\cal C}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cap({\cal A}_{\gamma}\times{\cal A}_{\gamma})\neq\emptyset

Lemma 2.19

(Crossing in the limit)

\Pi^{|}

\forall T,\varepsilon>0,\ r\in\mathbb{R}\ \exists\delta>0,\ m\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ s.t.\ }{\cal C}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cap({\cal A}_{n}\times{\cal A}_{n})=\emptyset\ \forall n\geq m.

\exists T,\varepsilon>0,\ r\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ s.t.\ }\forall\delta>0\ \exists m\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ s.t.\ }{\cal C}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,\varepsilon,r}\cap({\cal A}_{n}\times{\cal A}_{n})\neq\emptyset\ \forall n\geq m.

Throughout Section
3
we will use a piece of notation that is in common usage for the Brownian web, and related objects,
but that we have not so far introduced.
For
\pi\in\Pi^{\uparrow}
we write
\sigma_{\pi}
for the initial time of
\pi

i.e. if
\pi
has time domain
[t,\infty)
then
\sigma_{\pi}=t
.

3.1
Weaves

In this section we prove a result that specialises Theorem
1.3
.
A
weave
, introduced and studied in
[
FS24
]
,
is a random compact subset
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Pi^{\uparrow}

that is non-crossing and for which,
with probability one, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
there exists
\pi\in\mathcal{A}
such that
z\in\overline{\pi}

i.e. the paths cover space-time.
We consider weaves with a law that is invariant under deterministic translations of space-time.
In this setting, we show that tightness of the motion of a single particle in the sense of the
classical
Skorohod space
D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})
(as defined in e.g.
[
EK86
,
Bil99
,
Whi02
]
) essentially controls tightness of the whole system in
\mathcal{K}(\Pi^{\uparrow})
.
We show also that translation invariance can be replaced by a more technical condition that is uniform in space and time.

Before stating the result we must note a subtle technical point.
Let
(X_{n})
be a sequence of
D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})
valued random variables.
Although
D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})
is a subspace of
\Pi^{\uparrow}
,
tightness of
(X_{n})
in
\Pi^{\uparrow}
(in the sense of Theorem
1.2
)
does not imply tightness of
(X_{n})
in
D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})
(in the classical sense).
The distinction arises because for
\pi\in D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})
we must have
\pi(0-)=\pi(0+)

whereas
\Pi
permits jumps at time
0
,
more precisely

D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})=\big\{\pi\in\Pi^{\uparrow}:\sigma_{\pi}=0,\ \pi(0-)=\pi(0+)\big\}.

(3.1)

Tightness conditions for
D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})

(as found in
[
EK86
,
Bil99
,
Whi02
]
and suchlike)
must therefore enforce that limit points do not jump at time
0
,
whereas tightness conditions (such as Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
)
for the larger space
\Pi^{\uparrow}
need not do so. For single paths the precise connection,
which is a straightforward consequence of
[
FS23
, Thm 3.10]
, is as follows.

Lemma 3.1

(Tightness on
D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})
)

Let
(X_{n})
be a sequence of random variables with values in
D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})
. Then their laws are tight on
D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})
under J1 (resp. M1) if and only if these laws are tight on
\Pi
under J1 (resp. M1) and additionally,
for all
\varepsilon>0
we have

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\big[\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}|X_{n}(t)|\geq\varepsilon\big]=0.

We now recount some important facts from
[
FS24
, Section 2.4]
concerning weaves.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a weave.
Then, except for
z
within a (deterministic) null set
R_{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
for each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
there exists an almost surely unique path
\pi_{z}\in\Pi^{\uparrow}
that begins at
z
and does not cross
\mathcal{A}
.
We say that
\pi_{z}
is the
one-particle motion
of
\mathcal{A}
from
z
.

We say that a weave
\mathcal{A}
is
homogeneous
if the law of
\mathcal{A}
is invariant under deterministic translations of space and time. In this case the set
R_{\mathcal{A}}
is empty:
for each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
there exists an almost surely unique random path
\pi_{z}\in\Pi^{\uparrow}
that begins at
z

and does not cross
\mathcal{A}
.
Moreover the law of the path

t\mapsto\pi_{(x,s)}(s+t)-x\qquad\text{ for }t\in[0,\infty)

(3.2)

does not depend on
z=(x,s)
.
The law of this process might include a jump at time
t=0
,
but if it does not do so then (
3.2
) is a
D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})
valued random variable.
We refer to this law as the one-particle motion of
\mathcal{A}
.

For a weave
\mathcal{A}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be the
\sigma
-field generated by
\{\pi(s)\,;\,\pi\in\mathcal{A},\,\sigma_{\pi}\leq s\leq t\}
.
We say that
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is the generated filtration of
\mathcal{A}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Tightness criteria for homogeneous weaves)

Let
(\mathcal{A}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of weaves,
with generated filtrations
(\mathcal{F}^{n}_{t})
.
Assume the following:

(i)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is homogeneous.

(ii)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
X_{n}
be the one-particle motion of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
from the origin.
Suppose that
X_{n}
takes values in
D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})

and is strongly Markov with respect to the filtration
(\mathcal{F}^{n}_{t})_{t\in[0,\infty)}
.

(iii)

The laws of
(X_{n})
are tight on
D_{[0,\infty)}(\mathbb{R})
, under J1 or M1.

Then the laws of
(\mathcal{A}_{n})
are tight on
\mathcal{K}(\Pi^{\uparrow})
under M1
and all weak limit points are non-crossing.

As a corollary to the proof of Theorem
3.2
we will obtain the following more general, but more technical result.

Corollary 3.3

(Tightness criteria for inhomogeneous weaves)

In the setting of Theorem
3.2
,
the same conclusion can be drawn if instead of conditions (i)-(iii) we have that
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
there exists a random path
\pi^{n}_{z}\in\Pi^{\uparrow}
,
which does not cross
\mathcal{A}_{n}
, such that:

(iv)

\pi^{n}_{z}
is strongly Markov with respect to
(\mathcal{F}^{n}_{t})
.

(v)

For all
\varepsilon>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
T\in(0,\infty)
,

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{z\in\{x\}\times[-T,T]}\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,\delta]}\left|\pi^{n}_{z}(s^{n}_{z}+s)-\pi^{n}_{z}(s^{n}_{z})\right|\geq\varepsilon\right]=0.

(3.3)

Proof
The proof of Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.3
,
which we give together,
will take up the remainder of Section
3.1
.
Let us first handle the connection between the two results,
namely we show that (i)-(iii) implies both (iv) and (v).
To see this
take
\pi^{n}_{z}
to be the one-particle motion of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
from
z
,
which by (i) and our general comments above is well defined for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
with a distribution that does not depend on

.
Condition (iv) then follows from (ii) and translation invariance.
Condition (v) follows from (iii) and Lemma

.
It therefore suffices to establish the conclusion of Theorem
3.2

under conditions (iv) and (v), which we assume hereon.

Let
T,\delta\in(0,\infty)
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
.
For each

we define a sequence of paths
\pi^{n}_{m}

and stopping times
\tau^{n}_{m}
.
Set
\tau^{n}_{0}=-T
and then for

\displaystyle\pi_{m}^{n}

\displaystyle=\pi_{(r+\varepsilon,\tau_{m-1})}^{n},\qquad

\displaystyle\tau_{m}^{n}=\inf\{t\geq\tau_{m-1}^{n}\,;\,\pi_{m}^{n}\leq r\text{ or }\pi_{m}^{n}\geq r+2\varepsilon\}.

In words, the path
\pi^{n}_{m}
begins at time

at spatial location

.
The time
\tau^{n}_{m}
occurs when
\pi^{n}_{m}
exits the region
[r,r+2\varepsilon]
,
upon which
\pi^{n}_{m+1}
is born at space-time location
(r+\varepsilon,\tau^{n}_{m})
.
If
\tau^{n}_{m}
is infinite,
then the inductive definition terminates
and the sequences defined are taken to be finite.

Define also the events

E_{\delta,m}^{n}=\left\{\tau^{n}_{m}>\tau^{n}_{m-1}+\delta\right\},\qquad E_{\delta}^{n}=\bigcap_{m=1}^{M_{n}}E_{\delta,m}^{n}

where
{M_{n}}=\inf\{m\in\mathbb{N}\,;\,\tau_{m}\geq T\}
.
We claim that, in the notation of (
1.22

\left\{\mathcal{C}^{\rm M}_{T,\delta,2\varepsilon,r}\cap(\mathcal{A}_{n}\times\mathcal{A}_{n})\neq\emptyset\right\}\;\subseteq\;\Omega\setminus E_{\delta}^{n}.

(3.4)

The reasoning behind equation (

) is explained in Figure

,
which depicts the sequences

and
(\pi^{n}_{m})

In order to check (

occurs with high probability, in a sense matching the limits appearing in (

By (

which implies that, for all

EFS17
]
and others,
in which
the motion of a single particle is within the natural domain of attraction of an
\alpha
-stable process, where
\alpha\in(0,2)
.
For brevity we show only tightness. We do not attempt to characterize the limit,
which will be a system of highly correlated non-crossing
\alpha
-stable processes.
In the case
\alpha=2
,
after a diffusive rescaling the particles become coalescing Brownian motions that are independent before coalescence;
under suitable conditions the limit in this case is known to be the Brownian web.
However in the
\alpha
-stable case one should expect that particles in the limit are dependent even before coalescence.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
let
\mu
be a finite measure on
(0,\infty)

such that

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}R^{\alpha}\int_{R}^{\infty}r\,\mu(dr)\;\in(0,\infty),

(3.7)

for example
\mu(dr)=(1\wedge r^{-\alpha-2})\,dr
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}

let
P_{n}
denote a Poisson point process in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
with intensity measure

n^{1/\alpha}\,dx\otimes n\,dt\otimes\mu_{n}(dr)

(3.8)

where
\mu_{n}(A)=\mu\left(n^{1/\alpha}A\right)
.

Given a point
z=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we define a cadlag path
\pi^{n}_{z}
with time domain
[t,\infty)

and initial point
(x,t)
,
by specifying that
\pi^{n}_{z}
remains constant except
at values of
t
for which
\pi^{n}_{z}(t-)\in[x-r,x+r]
for some
(x,t,r)\in P_{n}
;
at such times the path jumps and
\pi^{n}_{z}(t+)=x
.
We will show below that

\pi_{z}^{n}
is a compound Poisson process,
which by symmetry has zero mean.

We define

W_{n}=\{\pi^{n}_{z}\,;\,z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\}

and let
\mathcal{W}_{n}
be the closure of
W_{n}
in
\Pi^{\uparrow}
.
See Figure
3.1
for a graphical depiction of
W_{n}
and
P_{n}
.
Equation (
3.8
) corresponds to a space-time rescaling in which, at stage
n
,
we speed up time by a factor
n
and compress space by a factor
n^{1/\alpha}
.
In particular,
for fixed but arbitrary
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
for
s\in(0,\infty)
the processes

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}&s\mapsto\pi^{n}_{z}(s)\text{ and }s\mapsto n^{-1/\alpha}\pi^{1}_{z}(ns)\text{ have the same law.}\end{array}

(3.9)

The remainder of the present section will apply Theorem
3.2
to the sequence
(\mathcal{W}_{n})
.
We must first show that
\mathcal{W}_{n}
is a weave.
Then we must check conditions (i)-(iii) of Theorem
3.2
;
conditions (i) and (ii) are essentially immediate and condition (iii) will come from showing
that the limiting one-particle motion is
\alpha
-stable.

Remark 3.4

For arbitrary
Z_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}

we have that
A_{n}=\{\pi^{n}_{z}\,;\,z\in Z_{n}\}\subseteq W_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{n}
.
Setting
\mathcal{A}_{n}
to be the closure of
A_{n}
,
tightness of the sequence
(\mathcal{A}_{n})

follows immediately from that of
(\mathcal{W}_{n})
.
For example, we might use paths begun on rescalings of the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{n}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,;\,n^{1/\alpha}x\in\mathbb{Z},\;nt\in\mathbb{Z}\}

as depicted in Figure
3.1
.

We begin with the one-particle motion.
More precisely, we will show that the
process
X_{n}(s)=\pi^{(n)}_{(0,0)}(s)
is a compound Poisson process.
In view of (
3.9
) it suffices to consider
n=1
.
Let us note at the outset that condition (
3.7
)
combined with the requirement that
\mu
be a finite measure
implies that
\int_{0}^{\infty}r\,\mu(dr)<\infty
.

We refer to each
(x,t,r)\in P_{n}
as an event and to the space-time region
[x-r,x+r]\times\{t\}
as
being affected by the event. We similarly say that when
X_{n}(t-)\in[x-r,x+r]
,
the path
X_{n}
is affected by the event
(x,t,r)
.
Taking
n=1
,
if the current location of
X_{1}=\pi^{(1)}_{(0,0)}
is
y\in\mathbb{R}
then it is affected by events at rate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{\{y\in[x-r,x+r]\}}\,dx\,\mu(dr)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}r\,\mu(dr)

(3.10)

which is finite.
Upon being affected by an event,
the resulting jump changes the spatial location of
X_{n}
by (addition of) a random variable
J
for which

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[J\geq R]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{\{y\in[x-r,x-R]\}}\,dx\,\mu(dr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\int_{0}^{\infty}(r-R)\vee 0\;\mu(dr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\int_{R}^{\infty}r\,\mu(dr),

(3.11)

where
R\in(0,\infty)
and
K=2\int_{0}^{\infty}r\,\mu(dr)
.
Note that by symmetry for
R\geq 0
we have
\mathbb{P}[J\geq R]=\mathbb{P}[J\leq-R]
,
so (
3.11
) characterizes the distribution of
J
.

Proposition 3.5

(Tightness of heavy tailed Poisson trees)

The laws of
(\mathcal{W}_{n})
are tight on
\mathcal{K}(\Pi^{\uparrow})
under M1
and all weak limit points are non-crossing.

Proof
We first justify that
\mathcal{W}_{n}
is a weave, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
With
n\in\mathbb{N}
fixed, it is clear that
\mathcal{W}_{n}
is a subset of
\Pi^{\uparrow}

and that each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfies
z\in\overline{\pi}
for some
\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
.
To see that
\mathcal{W}_{n}
is a weave, it remains only to show that
\mathcal{W}_{n}
is almost surely compact and non-crossing, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

A similar calculation to (
3.10
)
shows that for each
L,T\in(0,\infty)
there exists, almost surely,
a random
\delta>0
(depending on
L
,
T
and
n
) such that the regions
[x-r,x+r]\times[t-\delta,t+\delta]

are disjoint for all
(x,t,r)\in P_{n}
with
(x,t)\in[-L,L]\times[-T,T]
.
Note that paths in
W_{n}
remain constant (in space) outside of such regions.
With this in hand it is easily seen via Theorem
2.5
that
\mathcal{W}_{n}=\overline{W_{n}}
is almost surely compact,
under both J1 and M1.
Moreover, noting that
W_{n}
is non-crossing,
Proposition
2.12
gives that if two paths
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
were to cross, then they must collide
at time
\sigma_{\pi}\vee\sigma_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
However, this eventuality cannot occur due to
\delta>0
.
Hence
\mathcal{W}_{n}
is almost surely non-crossing.

To complete the proof we will apply Theorem
3.2
to the sequence
(\mathcal{W}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
for which we must check conditions (i)-(iii) of that theorem.
Conditions (i) and (ii) follow immediately from standard independence properties and translation-invariance of Poisson point processes, so it remains only to check condition (iii).

We have seen above that the one-particle motion of
\mathcal{W}_{n}

from the origin is a compound Poisson process with jump rate (
3.10
)
and jump distribution
J
characterized by (
3.11
).
Theorem 4.5.2 of
[
Whi02
]
gives that
a
discrete
time random walk with i.i.d. increments having distribution
J
is within the normal domain of attraction of an
\alpha
-stable process, with time rescaled by a factor
n
and space rescaled by
n^{-1/\alpha}
.
Theorem 4.5.3 of
[
Whi02
]

gives that such a walk converges in law, as a cadlag process under the J1 (and hence also M1) topology, to an
\alpha
-stable process.
For brevity we do not calculate the scale parameter of the limiting
\alpha
-stable process here
but it may be found via formulae therein; by symmetry the skewness and shift parameters are both zero.
Noting (
3.10
),
we are in fact concerned with a random walk in continuous time i.e. a compound Poisson process.
The two cases differ only by a strictly increasing piecewise linear time change,
a time change which in the limit converges almost surely, in the locally uniform sense, to a linear time change.
Thus, by Theorem 13.2.2 of
[
Whi02
]
,
the same results apply to the sequence
n\mapsto X_{n}(\cdot)
.
This establishes condition (iii) of Theorem
3.2
,
which completes the proof.

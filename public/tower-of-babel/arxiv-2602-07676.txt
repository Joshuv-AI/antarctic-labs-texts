Existence of Ground State and Excited Spinning Q Q -Vortex Solitons on Finite Domains
Caroline Brumelot, Luciano Medina

Abstract
We establish the existence of spinning Q Q -vortex solitons in a complex scalar field theory with a sextic potential on a finite domain. By reducing the governing equation to a nonlinear boundary value problem, we use variational methods to prove the existence of at least two distinct types of solutions: a ground state solution obtained via constrained minimization and an excited state of the saddle-point type obtained via the Mountain Pass Theorem. We derive bounds for the angular frequency ω \omega , the wave amplitude, and the domain size P P , and provide explicit estimates for the exponential decay of the solutions. Furthermore, we implement a spectral-Galerkin formulation to numerically compute the profiles of fundamental Q Q -vortices, illustrating the saturation behavior of the soliton’s amplitude and the asymptotic dependence of the frequency on a prescribed reduced norm and vortex winding number, as well as verifying the theoretical results and visualizing the topological phase structure of the solutions.

Introduction

In the context of field theoretic models of high energy physics, solitons are localized finite energy solutions of nonlinear field equations and realizations of elementary particles. Solitons, in most nonlinear field theories, possess strong regularity properties but typically lack a nonzero angular momentum, which is a desired characteristic of elementary particles
[
11
,
22
,
27
]
. There are two classes of solitons: topological, having a conserved topological charge, and nontopological, having a conserved Noether charge
[
10
,
13
,
15
,
26
]
. Examples of topological and nontopological solitons include monopoles, vortices, domain walls, soliton stars, boson stars, hadron structures, and of our particular interest,
Q
-balls
[
2
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
20
]
.

From a mathematical perspective,
Q
-balls are examples of the class of hylomorphic solitons and their existence has been well studied by Benci and others
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. While the work of Volkov and Wöhnert
[
27
]
presented the first numerical evidence of static spinning
Q
-balls, the field has seen significant recent activity. Notably, Almumin et al.
[
1
]
recently investigated slowly rotating
Q
-balls, confirming the existence of metastable states with small angular momentum. Furthermore, studies by Zhang et al.
[
28
]
have explored superradiance in rotating solitons, linking the “spinning” nature of these solutions to wider stability phenomena. Beyond the global
U(1)
symmetry, significant progress has also been made in the study of gauged or electrically charged
Q
-balls. Recent works have established the existence of such charged solutions in multi-scalar theories
[
7
]
, proven their existence in coupled Abelian-Higgs models
[
25
]
, and explored their complex dynamical interactions, where electromagnetic repulsion plays a critical role
[
16
]
.

Our aim is to establish a rigorous mathematical existence theory for spinning
Q
-vortices-generalizations of
Q
-balls in
2+1
dimensions analyzed numerically by Volkov and Wöhnert
[
27
]
and to use our theory to study their profiles. To this end, following the formulation in
[
27
]
, consider the theory of a complex scalar field in 3+1 dimensional flat Minkowski spacetime defined by the Lagrangian density

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi^{*}-U(|\Phi|).

(1.1)

The global
U(1)
symmetry of the Lagrangian under
\Phi\to e^{i\alpha}\Phi
leads to the conserved Noether charge

\displaystyle Q=\frac{1}{i}\int(\Phi^{*}\dot{\Phi}-\Phi\dot{\Phi}^{*})d^{3}x.

(1.2)

The variation of the action with respect to the field
\Phi^{*}
yields the general Euler-Lagrange equation of motion:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}\Phi+\frac{\partial U}{\partial\Phi^{*}}=0.

(1.3)

Assuming cylindrical symmetry where the field is independent of
z
, and employing polar coordinates
(t,\rho,z,\theta)
, we seek spinning
Q
-vortices via the ansatz

\displaystyle\Phi=\phi(\rho)e^{i\omega t+iN\theta},

(1.4)

where
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
is the wave frequency and
N\in\mathbb{Z}
is the rotational quantum number or vortex number.

The energy per unit vortex length is

\displaystyle E=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\left(\omega^{2}\phi^{2}+\phi_{\rho}^{2}+\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\phi^{2}+U(\phi)\right)d\rho,

(1.5)

and the angular momentum per unit length is given by

\displaystyle J=4\pi\omega N\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\phi^{2}d\rho\equiv NQ.

(1.6)

When
N\neq 0
, the vortex rotates around the
z
-axis with angular momentum
J
.

From (
1.5
), we see that finite energy solutions on the unbounded domain require
\phi(0)=0
and decay
\phi(\rho)\rightarrow 0
as
\rho\rightarrow\infty
. To rigorously establish existence and facilitate numerical computation, we study the problem on a finite domain
D_{P}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|<P\}
, imposing the Dirichlet boundary condition
\phi(P)=0
for some large
P>0
. This formulation serves dual purposes: it models physical confinement and acts as a computational proxy for the free-space solution, consistent with the numerical approach of Volkov and Wöhnert
[
27
]
. We establish a priori bounds and decay estimates in Section 2 to quantify the behavior of these solutions as
\rho\to P
.

Under the explicit potential

\displaystyle U(\phi)=\lambda\left(\phi^{6}-a\phi^{4}+b\phi^{2}\right),\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda,a,b>0,\hskip 20.00003ptb>a^{2}/4,

(1.7)

the existence of
Q
-vortices reduces to the two-point boundary value problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&\phi_{\rho\rho}+\dfrac{1}{\rho}\phi_{\rho}-\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\phi-\lambda(6\phi^{5}-4a\phi^{3}+2b\phi)+\omega^{2}\phi=0,\quad\rho\in(0,P),\\
&\phi(0)=0=\phi(P).\end{array}\right.

In our mathematical analysis, it is convenient to distinguish between the reduced norm functional
\tilde{Q}(\phi)
, defined by:

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}(\phi):=4\pi\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi^{2}d\rho,

(1.10)

and the constraint equation
\tilde{Q}(\phi)=\tilde{Q}_{0}
. We use the term prescribed norm for the constant
\tilde{Q}_{0}
, which serves as the constraint value in our minimization problem. In the context of nonlinear optics, this quantity is often referred to as the beam power, while in particle physics it represents the static geometric contribution to the Noether charge. This distinction aligns with recent rigorous studies on the existence of optical vortices
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
, where the beam power is prescribed rather than the frequency.

We now state our main analytical results governing solutions to (
1
), followed by a summary of our numerical findings.

Theorem 1.1

.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}[0,P]\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(0,P)
be a nontrivial classical solution of (
1
). A necessary condition for existence is

2\lambda\left(b-\frac{a^{2}}{3}\right)+\frac{N^{2}}{P^{2}}<\omega^{2}.

(1.11)

Furthermore, if
\omega^{2}<2\lambda b+\frac{N^{2}}{P^{2}}
, the solution is uniformly bounded by

\phi^{2}(\rho)<\frac{2a}{3}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\rho\in[0,P].

(1.12)

Additionally, under the same condition on
\omega
, the solution decays exponentially fast near the boundary:

\phi^{2}(\rho)\leq\frac{2a}{3}\exp(-\sigma(\rho-P_{0}))\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for every $\rho\in[P_{0},P]$,}

(1.13)

with
P_{0}\in(0,P)
sufficiently large and decay rate
\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{N^{2}}{P^{2}}+2\lambda b-\omega^{2}}
.

Theorem 1.2

.

Let
|N|\geq 1
and
\lambda,a,b>0
such that
b>a^{2}/4
. For any
\omega
satisfying

2\lambda\left(b-\dfrac{1}{4}a^{2}\right)<\omega^{2}<2\lambda b

(1.14)

and
P
sufficiently large (specifically
P>P^{*}
as defined in Lemma 3.2), there exist at least two different types of positive solutions,
\phi(\rho)>0
for every
\rho\in(0,P)
, satisfying (
1
). One solution is a minimum and the other a saddle point of the corresponding action functional. There also exist infinitely many pairs of solutions (not necessarily positive) to (
1
).

Theorem 1.3

.

Let
|N|\geq 1
and
\lambda,a,b>0
such that
b>a^{2}/4
. There exists a solution pair
(\phi_{0},\omega_{0})
to (
1
) such that
\phi_{0}(\rho)>0
for all
\rho\in(0,P)
, where
\omega_{0}
appears as the Lagrange multiplier of a constrained minimization problem. Additionally, if
\omega_{0}^{2}<2\lambda b
, the prescribed norm
\tilde{Q}_{0}
must satisfy the lower bound:

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{0}>\frac{\pi|N|}{a\lambda}.

(1.15)

Numerical Implications.
We complement our existence theory with a spectral-Galerkin framework and validate the theoretical bounds and characterize the physical structure of the vortices, revealing three distinct behaviors, in agreement with the numerical work of Volkov and Wöhnert
[
27
]
:

1.

Amplitude Saturation:
Consistent with Theorem 1.1, the field amplitude remains strictly bounded. As the prescribed norm
\tilde{Q}_{0}
increases, the soliton profile transitions from a Gaussian-like shape to a saturated “flat-top” structure, asymptotically approaching the theoretical ceiling
\phi_{max}\approx\sqrt{2a/3}
.

2.

Nonlinear Dispersion:
The eigenfrequency
\omega^{2}
exhibits a monotonic, nonlinear dependence on the prescribed norm. Our computations map the full frequency existence range, showing the frequency descending from the linear limit
\omega_{max}^{2}=2\lambda b
toward the critical existence threshold
\omega_{min}^{2}=2\lambda\left(b-\frac{1}{4}a^{2}\right)
as the vortex grows.

3.

Topological Geometry:
For higher winding numbers (
|N|\geq 2
), the numerical profiles demonstrate the dominant role of the centrifugal barrier (
N^{2}/\rho^{2}
). Increasing the topological charge significantly widens the vortex core, displacing the energy density radially outward and confirming the existence of topologically distinct states with phase winding numbers corresponding to
N
.

Proof of Theorem 1.1

In this section, we establish Theorem 1.1 as a result of the three separate lemmas below. We first establish necessary bounds on the frequency
\omega
and the amplitude
\phi
, and then use these bounds to prove the exponential decay of the solution near the boundary.

Lemma 2.1

.

If
\phi\in\mathcal{C}[0,P]\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(0,P)
is a nontrivial solution of (
1
), then

\displaystyle 2\lambda\left(b-\dfrac{1}{3}a^{2}\right)+\dfrac{N^{2}}{P^{2}}<\omega^{2}.

(2.1)

Proof.
If
\phi
is a solution to (
1
), then
-\phi
is also a solution. Hence, without loss of generality, there exists a point
\rho_{0}\in(0,P)
such that
\phi(\rho_{0})>0
is a local maximum, so
\phi_{\rho}(\rho_{0})=0
and
\phi_{\rho\rho}(\rho_{0})\leq 0
. Substituting these into (
1
), we get

\displaystyle 0\geq\phi_{\rho\rho}(\rho_{0})

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho_{0}^{2}}+\lambda(6\phi^{4}(\rho_{0})-4a\phi^{2}(\rho_{0})+2b)-\omega^{2}\right)\phi(\rho_{0}).

(2.2)

Since
\phi(\rho_{0})>0
, the term in the parenthesis must be non-positive:

\displaystyle\omega^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho_{0}^{2}}+2\lambda(3\phi^{4}(\rho_{0})-2a\phi^{2}(\rho_{0})+b).

(2.3)

The quadratic polynomial
f(u)=3u^{2}-2au+b
(with
u=\phi^{2}
) achieves its global minimum at
u=a/3
. The minimum value is
f(a/3)=b-a^{2}/3
. Using this and the fact that
\rho_{0}<P
(so
1/\rho_{0}^{2}>1/P^{2}
), we obtain the strict inequality:

\displaystyle\omega^{2}

\displaystyle>\dfrac{N^{2}}{P^{2}}+2\lambda\left(b-\dfrac{1}{3}a^{2}\right).

(2.4)

\hskip 20.00003pt\square

Lemma 2.2

.

If
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}[0,P]\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(0,P)
is a solution of (
1
) and
\omega^{2}<2\lambda b+\dfrac{N^{2}}{P^{2}}
, then
\phi^{2}(\rho)<\dfrac{2a}{3}
for all
\rho\in[0,P]
.

Proof.
Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}[0,P]\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(0,P)
be a solution of (
1
) with
\omega^{2}<2\lambda b+\dfrac{N^{2}}{P^{2}}
. As before, assume
\phi
has a positive global maximum at
\rho_{0}\in(0,P)
such that
\phi_{\rho}(\rho_{0})=0
and
\phi_{\rho\rho}(\rho_{0})\leq 0
. Substituting into (
1
):

\displaystyle 0\geq\phi_{\rho\rho}(\rho_{0})

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho_{0}^{2}}+2\lambda b-\omega^{2}\right)\phi(\rho_{0})+6\lambda\phi^{3}(\rho_{0})\left(\phi^{2}(\rho_{0})-\dfrac{2a}{3}\right).

(2.5)

By the hypothesis on
\omega^{2}
, we have
\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho_{0}^{2}}+2\lambda b-\omega^{2}>\dfrac{N^{2}}{P^{2}}+2\lambda b-\omega^{2}>0
. Since
\phi(\rho_{0})>0
, the first term on the right-hand side is strictly positive. For the sum to be non-positive, the second term must be negative:

\displaystyle 6\lambda\phi^{3}(\rho_{0})\left(\phi^{2}(\rho_{0})-\dfrac{2a}{3}\right)<0.

(2.6)

This implies
\phi^{2}(\rho_{0})<\dfrac{2a}{3}
. Since
\phi(\rho_{0})
is the global maximum, it follows that
\phi^{2}(\rho)\leq\phi^{2}(\rho_{0})<\dfrac{2a}{3}
for all
\rho\in[0,P]
.

\hskip 20.00003pt\square

Lemma 2.3

.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}[0,P]\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(0,P)
is a solution of (
1
). If
\omega^{2}
satisfies the inequality

\omega^{2}<2\lambda b+\dfrac{N^{2}}{P^{2}},

(2.7)

then there exist constants
P_{0}\in(0,P)
and
\sigma>0
(depending only on the system parameters), such that

\phi^{2}(\rho)\leq\frac{2a}{3}\exp(-\sigma(\rho-P_{0}))\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for every $\rho\in[P_{0},P]$.}

Proof.
Rewrite (
1
) as:

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=\left(\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho^{2}}-\omega^{2}+\lambda\left(6\phi^{4}-4a\phi^{2}+2b\right)\right)\phi.

(2.8)

We compute the Laplacian of
w=\phi^{2}
:

\displaystyle\Delta w=2\phi\Delta\phi+2|\nabla\phi|^{2}\geq 2\phi\Delta\phi=2\left(\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho^{2}}-\omega^{2}+2\lambda b+\lambda(6\phi^{4}-4a\phi^{2})\right)w.

(2.9)

Since
\phi(P)=0
and
\phi
is continuous, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
P_{0}
sufficiently close to
P
such that the nonlinear term satisfies
6\lambda\phi^{4}-4a\lambda\phi^{2}>-\epsilon
for all
\rho\in[P_{0},P]
.

Let
2\delta=\frac{N^{2}}{P^{2}}+2\lambda b-\omega^{2}
, which is strictly positive by hypothesis. We choose
P_{0}
such that the nonlinear term is small enough, specifically choosing
\epsilon=\delta
. Then for all
\rho\in[P_{0},P]
:

\displaystyle\Delta w\geq 2\left(\frac{N^{2}}{P^{2}}-\omega^{2}+2\lambda b-\delta\right)w=2\delta w.

(2.10)

Now, define the comparison function
\xi(\rho)=\frac{2a}{3}e^{-\sigma(\rho-P_{0})}
, where we set
\sigma=\sqrt{2\delta}
. Computing the Laplacian of
\xi
:

\displaystyle\Delta\xi=\xi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{\rho}\xi^{\prime}=\sigma^{2}\xi-\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\xi<\sigma^{2}\xi=2\delta\xi.

(2.11)

Define
v(\rho)=w(\rho)-\xi(\rho)
. Then:

\displaystyle\Delta v=\Delta w-\Delta\xi>2\delta w-2\delta\xi=2\delta v.

(2.12)

Thus
(\Delta-2\delta)v>0
on
(P_{0},P)
. By the maximum principle, the maximum of
v
must occur on the boundary.
At the inner boundary
\rho=P_{0}
:
v(P_{0})=\phi^{2}(P_{0})-\frac{2a}{3}
. By Lemma 2.2, we know
\phi^{2}(\rho)<\frac{2a}{3}
globally, so
v(P_{0})<0
.
At the outer boundary
\rho=P
:
v(P)=0-\frac{2a}{3}e^{-\sigma(P-P_{0})}<0
.
Since
v<0
on the boundary,
v(\rho)\leq 0
for all
\rho\in[P_{0},P]
.
Therefore,
\phi^{2}(\rho)\leq\frac{2a}{3}e^{-\sqrt{2\delta}(\rho-P_{0})}
.

\hskip 20.00003pt\square

Proof of Theorem 1.2

Fundamental
Q
-vortices are nontrivial positive solutions of (
1
). We first use a direct minimization approach to establish the existence of fundamental
Q
-vortices which arise as minima of a suitable action functional. We then utilize the Mountain Pass Theorem to establish the existence of a second, distinct solution of the saddle point type.

To this end, let the action functional
I_{\omega}:H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be given by

\displaystyle I_{\omega}(\phi)=\int_{0}^{P}\left\{\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2}+\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho}\phi^{2}-\omega^{2}\rho\phi^{2}\right)+\lambda\rho(\phi^{6}-a\phi^{4}+b\phi^{2})\right\}d\rho,

(3.1)

where
H
is the completion of
X=\left\{\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}[0,P]:\phi(0)=0=\phi(P)\right\}
endowed with the inner product

\displaystyle(\phi,\tilde{\phi})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{P}\left\{\rho\phi_{\rho}\tilde{\phi}_{\rho}+\dfrac{1}{\rho}\phi\tilde{\phi}\right\}d\rho,\hskip 10.00002pt\phi,\tilde{\phi}\in H,

(3.2)

and norm
||\phi||^{2}=(\phi,\phi)
.

Consider the direct minimization problem

\displaystyle\eta_{0}=\inf\{I_{\omega}(\phi):\phi\in H\}.

(3.3)

Rewrite the functional (
3.1
) in the form

\displaystyle I_{\omega}(\phi)=\int_{0}^{P}\left\{\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2}+\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho}\phi^{2}\right)+\lambda\rho h(\phi)\right\}d\rho

(3.4)

with

\displaystyle h(\phi)=\phi^{6}-a\phi^{4}+\left(b-\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)\phi^{2}.

(3.5)

Since the coefficient of
\phi^{6}
is positive,
h(\phi)
is bounded from below by some constant
h_{\min}
. It follows that

\displaystyle I_{\omega}(\phi)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}||\phi||^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\lambda h_{\min}P^{2}

(3.6)

and
I_{\omega}
is coercive on
H
. Thus the direct minimization problem (
3.3
) is well-defined.

The following lemma proves that the functional
I_{\omega}
satisfies the Palais-Smale (PS) condition.

Lemma 3.1

.

Let
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
H
such that
|I_{\omega}(\phi_{j})|\leq\alpha
for some constant
\alpha>0
and
I^{\prime}_{\omega}(\phi_{j})\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
. Then there exists a subsequence of
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
which converges strongly to some element
\phi
in
H
.

Proof.
Suppose that
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
H
such that
|I_{\omega}(\phi_{j})|\leq\alpha
for some constant
\alpha>0
. Then the coercive bound (
3.6
) gives us the boundedness of the sequence
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
H
. We may treat each
\phi_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots
, as a real-valued radially symmetric function defined over the disc
D_{P}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x^{2}+y^{2}\leq P^{2}\}
and vanishing on the boundary
\partial D_{P}
. From the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi^{2}d\rho\leq P^{2}\int_{0}^{P}\dfrac{\phi^{2}}{\rho}d\rho,

(3.7)

it follows that
H
is an embedded subspace of the standard Sobolev space
W^{1,2}_{0}(D_{P})
, composed of radially symmetric functions such that each element
\phi
of
H
satisfies
\phi(0)=0
. Hence,
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
W^{1,2}_{0}(D_{P})
. Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
\phi_{j}\rightharpoonup\phi
weakly in
W^{1,2}_{0}(D_{P})
as
j\rightarrow\infty
.
The compact embedding
W^{1,2}(D_{P})\subset\subset L^{p}(D_{P})

(p\geq 1)
then gives the strong convergence of
\phi_{j}\rightarrow\phi
in
L^{p}(D_{P})
as
j\rightarrow\infty
for every
p\geq 1
. Moreover,
\phi
is radially symmetric and we may write
\phi=\phi(\rho)
, which satisfies
\phi(P)=0
.

By hypothesis, we also suppose that
I^{\prime}_{\omega}(\phi_{j})\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
or, equivalently, that there is a sequence
\epsilon_{j}\geq 0
for every
j=1,2,\ldots,
such that

|(I^{\prime}_{\omega}(\phi_{j}),\tilde{\phi})|\leq\epsilon_{j}||\tilde{\phi}||

(3.8)

for every
\tilde{\phi}\in H
and
\epsilon_{j}\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
. We also use the notation
(\cdot,\cdot)
to denote the duality pairing between
H
and its dual space
H^{-1}
.

Letting
j\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.8
), we get

\displaystyle 0=\int_{0}^{P}\left\{\rho\phi_{\rho}\tilde{\phi}_{\rho}+\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho}\phi\tilde{\phi}+\lambda\rho\left(6\phi^{5}\tilde{\phi}-4a\phi^{3}\tilde{\phi}+2\left(b-\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)\phi\tilde{\phi}\right)\right\}d\rho

(3.9)

for every
\tilde{\phi}\in H
. Let
\tilde{\phi}=\phi_{j}-\phi
in (
3.8
) and (
3.9
). Inserting the result of (
3.9
) into the resulting (
3.8
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{P}\left\{\rho(\phi_{j,\rho}-\phi_{\rho})^{2}+\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho}(\phi_{j}-\phi)^{2}+\lambda\rho\left(6(\phi_{j}^{5}-\phi^{5})-4a(\phi_{j}^{3}-\phi^{3})\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.+2\left(b-\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)(\phi_{j}-\phi)\right)(\phi_{j}-\phi)\right\}d\rho\right|\leq\epsilon_{j}||\phi_{j}-\phi||.

(3.10)

Consequently, using the triangle inequality, we isolate the norm
||\phi_{j}-\phi||^{2}
:

\displaystyle||\phi_{j}-\phi||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\int_{0}^{P}\rho\left(6|\phi_{j}^{5}-\phi^{5}|+4a|\phi_{j}^{3}-\phi^{3}|+2\left|b-\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}\right||\phi_{j}-\phi|\right)|\phi_{j}-\phi|d\rho

\displaystyle+\epsilon_{j}||\phi_{j}-\phi||.

(3.11)

To prove strong convergence, we show that the integral term on the right-hand side vanishes as
j\to\infty
. We analyze the highest order term
\int_{0}^{P}\rho|\phi_{j}^{5}-\phi^{5}||\phi_{j}-\phi|d\rho
. By the generalized Hölder inequality with exponents
p=6/5
and
q=6
(where
1/p+1/q=1
), we have:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P}\rho|\phi_{j}^{5}-\phi^{5}||\phi_{j}-\phi|d\rho\leq\left(\int_{0}^{P}\rho|\phi_{j}^{5}-\phi^{5}|^{6/5}d\rho\right)^{5/6}\left(\int_{0}^{P}\rho|\phi_{j}-\phi|^{6}d\rho\right)^{1/6}.

(3.12)

Since
\phi_{j}\to\phi
strongly in
L^{6}(D_{P})
, the term
||\phi_{j}-\phi||_{L^{6}}\to 0
. Furthermore, the convergence
\phi_{j}\to\phi
in
L^{6}
implies
\phi_{j}^{5}\to\phi^{5}
strongly in
L^{6/5}(D_{P})
by the continuity of the Nemytskii operator. Thus, the product converges to zero. Similar arguments apply to the lower-order cubic and linear terms using appropriate Hölder exponents (e.g.,
p=3/2,q=3
or
p=2,q=2
).
Therefore, the entire right-hand side tends to zero as
j\to\infty
, implying
||\phi_{j}-\phi||\to 0
strongly in
H
.

\hskip 20.00003pt\square

Since
I_{\omega}
is bounded below, coercive, and satisfies the PS condition, the global minimum exists. However, we must ensure this minimum is nontrivial (i.e., not the zero solution).

Lemma 3.2

.

Let
a,b,\lambda>0
such that
b>a^{2}/4
. For any
\omega
satisfying

\displaystyle 2\lambda\left(b-\dfrac{1}{4}a^{2}\right)<\omega^{2},

(3.13)

there exists a
\phi_{0}\in H
such that
I_{\omega}(\phi_{0})<0
, provided
P>P^{*}
. The bound
P^{*}
is given explicitly by

\displaystyle P^{*}=\frac{\mathcal{B}+\sqrt{\mathcal{B}^{2}+4\mathcal{A}\mathcal{C}}}{2\mathcal{A}},

(3.14)

where
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}
are positive constants defined in the proof below.

Proof.
We construct a test function
\phi_{0}
to verify the condition
I_{\omega}(\phi_{0})<0
. Let
t>0
be a parameter to be fixed later and define the trapezoidal function:

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(\rho)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}t\rho,&0\leq\rho<1,\\
t,&1\leq\rho\leq P-1,\\
t(P-\rho),&P-1<\rho\leq P.\end{array}\right.

First, we verify that
\phi_{0}\in H
. The function
\phi_{0}
is continuous on
[0,P]
as the piecewise definitions agree at the transition points
\rho=1
and
\rho=P-1
. Furthermore,
\phi_{0}(0)=0
and
\phi_{0}(P)=0
, satisfying the boundary conditions. The weak derivative
\phi_{0,\rho}
exists and is piecewise constant (taking values
t,0,-t
), hence
\phi_{0,\rho}\in L^{2}(0,P)
. Thus,
\phi_{0}\in H
.

We now compute the action
I_{\omega}(\phi_{0})
. The potential terms are dominated by the plateau region
[1,P-1]
. Integrating exactly, we find:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi_{0}^{2}d\rho

\displaystyle=t^{2}\left(\frac{P^{2}}{2}-P+\frac{1}{3}\right),

(3.18)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi_{0}^{4}d\rho

\displaystyle=t^{4}\left(\frac{P^{2}}{2}-P+\frac{1}{5}\right),

(3.19)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi_{0}^{6}d\rho

\displaystyle=t^{6}\left(\frac{P^{2}}{2}-P+\frac{1}{7}\right).

(3.20)

The gradient term evaluates to
\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi_{0,\rho}^{2}d\rho=t^{2}P
. For the centrifugal term, we use the upper bound valid for
P\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P}\frac{\phi_{0}^{2}}{\rho}d\rho\leq\int_{0}^{1}\rho t^{2}d\rho+\int_{1}^{P}\frac{t^{2}}{\rho}d\rho=\frac{t^{2}}{2}+t^{2}\ln P.

(3.21)

Substituting these into
I_{\omega}(\phi_{0})
and grouping by powers of
P
, we obtain:

\displaystyle I_{\omega}(\phi_{0})\leq-\mathcal{A}P^{2}+\mathcal{B}P+\mathcal{C}\ln P,

(3.22)

where the coefficient of the quadratic term is:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=-\frac{\lambda}{2}\left[t^{6}-at^{4}+\left(b-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)t^{2}\right].

(3.23)

To ensure
\mathcal{A}>0
, we choose
t
to minimize the polynomial in brackets. Setting
t^{2}=a/2
, we get:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=-\frac{\lambda(a/2)}{2}\left(\frac{a^{2}}{4}-\frac{a^{2}}{2}+b-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)=\frac{\lambda a}{4}\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}-\left(b-\frac{a^{2}}{4}\right)\right).

(3.24)

By hypothesis (
3.13
),
\mathcal{A}>0
. The linear coefficient
\mathcal{B}
collects the remaining positive terms (gradient and potential corrections):

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\lambda\left[t^{6}-at^{4}+\left(b-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)t^{2}\right]+\lambda\left(\frac{a}{5}t^{4}-\frac{1}{7}t^{6}-\frac{1}{3}\left(b-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)t^{2}\right),

(3.25)

and
\mathcal{C}=\frac{N^{2}t^{2}}{2}
. For large
P
, the term
\mathcal{C}\ln P
is sub-dominant to
\mathcal{B}P
. We can strictly bound
\ln P<P
for
P>1
to simplify the sufficient condition to a quadratic inequality:

\displaystyle-\mathcal{A}P^{2}+(\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{C})P<0\implies P>\frac{\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{A}}.

(3.26)

Thus, for sufficiently large
P
,
I_{\omega}(\phi_{0})<0
.

\hskip 20.00003pt\square

From Lemma 3.2, we conclude that
\eta_{0}=\inf I_{\omega}(\phi)<0
. Since
I_{\omega}(0)=0
, the minimizer
\tilde{\phi}
must be nontrivial.

We now establish the existence of a second, distinct solution using the Mountain Pass Theorem. First, we show that the functional possesses the requisite geometry.

Lemma 3.3

.

There is a
\delta>0
and
C>0
such that for any
\phi\in H
with
0<||\phi||<\delta
, we have
I_{\omega}(\phi)>0
and
I_{\omega}(\phi)\geq C
for
||\phi||=\delta
.

Proof.
Rewrite the functional (
3.1
) in the form

\displaystyle I_{\omega}(\phi)=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{P}\left(\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2}+\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho}\phi^{2}\right)d\rho+\lambda\int_{0}^{P}\rho g(\phi)d\rho

(3.27)

with

\displaystyle g(\phi)=\phi^{6}-a\phi^{4}+\left(b-\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)\phi^{2}.

(3.28)

For
\omega^{2}<2\lambda b
, we have
\lim\limits_{\xi\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{g(\xi)}{\xi^{2}}=b-\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}>0
.
Thus, for any
\epsilon>0
there is a
\xi_{0}>0
such that

g(\xi)\geq\left(b-\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}-\epsilon\right)\xi^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all $|\xi|<\xi_{0}$}.

(3.29)

Let
\delta=\xi_{0}/\sqrt{2}>0
and
\phi\in H
such that
0<||\phi||\leq\delta
. Using the embedding
||\phi||_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2}||\phi||
, we have
|\phi(\rho)|\leq\xi_{0}
.
Taking
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2}\left(b-\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)
, we get

\displaystyle I_{\omega}(\phi)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}||\phi||^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\lambda\left(b-\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi^{2}d\rho\geq\dfrac{1}{2}||\phi||^{2}.

(3.30)

Thus, for
||\phi||=\delta
, we have
I_{\omega}(\phi)\geq\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}:=C>0
.

\hskip 20.00003pt\square

From Lemma 3.2, there exists
\phi_{0}\in H
such that
I_{\omega}(\phi_{0})<0
. We can choose
\delta
small enough such that
||\phi_{0}||>\delta
. Since
I_{\omega}(0)=0
,
I_{\omega}(\phi)\geq C>0
on the sphere
||\phi||=\delta
, and
I_{\omega}(\phi_{0})<0
, the functional exhibits the Mountain Pass geometry.

Define the family of paths
\Gamma=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}([0,1];H):\gamma(0)=0,\gamma(1)=\phi_{0}\}
. The Mountain Pass level is defined as:

\displaystyle c=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}I_{\omega}(\gamma(t)).

(3.31)

Since every path
\gamma
from
0
to
\phi_{0}
must intersect the sphere
||\phi||=\delta
, we have:

\displaystyle c\geq\inf_{||\phi||=\delta}I_{\omega}(\phi)\geq C>0.

(3.32)

By the Mountain Pass Theorem
[
14
,
24
]
, since
I_{\omega}
satisfies the PS condition, there exists a critical point
\phi_{MP}\in H
such that
I_{\omega}(\phi_{MP})=c
and
I^{\prime}_{\omega}(\phi_{MP})=0
.

We conclude that we have found at least two distinct critical points for the functional
I_{\omega}
. To ensure these solutions are physically relevant (positive), we employ a truncation argument. Define the modified nonlinearity
h^{+}(\phi)
by:

h^{+}(\phi)=\begin{cases}\phi^{6}-a\phi^{4}+(b-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2\lambda})\phi^{2}&\text{if }\phi\geq 0,\\
0&\text{if }\phi<0.\end{cases}

(3.33)

Let
I_{\omega}^{+}
denote the action functional associated with this truncated potential. It is straightforward to verify that
I_{\omega}^{+}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition and possesses the same Mountain Pass geometry as the original functional. Consequently, there exist a ground state
\tilde{\phi}
(minimizer) and an excited state
\phi_{MP}
(saddle point) for
I_{\omega}^{+}
.

To prove non-negativity, let
\phi
be a critical point of
I_{\omega}^{+}
and consider the test function
\phi^{-}=\min(0,\phi)
. Testing the Euler-Lagrange equation with
\phi^{-}
yields:

\displaystyle\langle(I_{\omega}^{+})^{\prime}(\phi),\phi^{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{P}\rho\left(\phi_{\rho}\phi^{-}_{\rho}+\frac{N^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\phi\phi^{-}-\omega^{2}\phi\phi^{-}\right)d\rho+\lambda\int_{0}^{P}\rho h^{+}(\phi)\phi^{-}d\rho.

(3.34)

Using the properties
\phi\phi^{-}=(\phi^{-})^{2}
,
\phi_{\rho}\phi^{-}_{\rho}=(\phi^{-}_{\rho})^{2}
, and the fact that
h^{+}(\phi)\phi^{-}\equiv 0
by definition, this reduces to:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P}\rho(\phi^{-}_{\rho})^{2}d\rho+\int_{0}^{P}\rho\left(\frac{N^{2}}{\rho^{2}}-\omega^{2}\right)(\phi^{-})^{2}d\rho=0.

(3.35)

Since
\frac{N^{2}}{\rho^{2}}>\frac{N^{2}}{P^{2}}
and we consider
\omega^{2}
within the spectral gap, the term
(\frac{N^{2}}{\rho^{2}}-\omega^{2})
is bounded from below. The coercivity of the differential operator implies that
||\phi^{-}||=0
, and thus
\phi(\rho)\geq 0
almost everywhere.

Finally, by the Strong Maximum Principle applied to the operator
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta+(\frac{N^{2}}{\rho^{2}}-\omega^{2})
, any non-trivial non-negative solution must be strictly positive in
(0,P)
. Since
\phi(\rho)>0
, we have
h^{+}(\phi)\equiv h(\phi)
, and thus these positive critical points are genuine solutions to the original problem (
1
).

Proof of Theorem 1.3

Consider the action functional
I:W_{0}^{1,2}(0,P)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle I(\phi)=\int_{0}^{P}\left\{\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2}+\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho}\phi^{2}\right)+\lambda\rho(\phi^{6}-a\phi^{4}+b\phi^{2})\right\}d\rho

(4.1)

and the reduced norm functional
\tilde{Q}:W_{0}^{1,2}(0,P)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}(\phi)=4\pi\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi^{2}d\rho.

(4.2)

The field equation (
1
) may be treated as a nonlinear eigenvalue problem such that
\omega^{2}
appears as the Lagrange multiplier of the constrained minimization problem

\displaystyle I_{0}(\tilde{Q}_{0})=\inf\{I(\phi):\phi\in H\text{ and }\tilde{Q}(\phi)=\tilde{Q}_{0}\},

(4.3)

where
\tilde{Q}_{0}>0
is a fixed, prescribed value for the functional.

Using the inequality
\phi^{6}-a\phi^{4}+b\phi^{2}\geq\phi^{2}(b-a^{2}/4)
, valid for all
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
, and the constraint
\tilde{Q}(\phi)=\tilde{Q}_{0}
, we get the coercive bound

\displaystyle I(\phi)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{P}\left(\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2}+\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho}\phi^{2}\right)d\rho+\dfrac{\lambda}{4\pi}\left(b-\dfrac{a^{2}}{4}\right)\tilde{Q}_{0}.

(4.4)

Since
b>a^{2}/4
, the functional is bounded below and the constrained minimization problem (
4.3
) is well defined. Let
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a minimizing sequence of (
4.3
). From the coercive inequality (
4.4
), there is a constant
C>0
depending on
a
,
b
,
\lambda
, and
I_{0}
, but independent of
j
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P}\left(\rho\phi_{j,\rho}^{2}+\dfrac{N^{2}}{\rho}\phi_{j}^{2}\right)d\rho\leq C,

(4.5)

with
\phi_{j,\rho}=\frac{d}{d\rho}\phi_{j}
. Since both functionals
I
and
\tilde{Q}
are even, and recalling that the distributional derivative satisfies
|\frac{d}{d\rho}|\phi(\rho)||\leq|\frac{d}{d\rho}\phi(\rho)|
for any real-valued function defined on
W^{1,2}(0,P)

[
19
]
, we get
I(|\phi_{j}|)\leq I(\phi_{j})
and
\tilde{Q}(|\phi_{j}|)=\tilde{Q}(\phi_{j})
. Hence, we may construct a new sequence, also labeled as
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
, of non-negative valued functions.

We treat each
\phi_{j}
as a radially symmetric function defined over
D_{P}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x^{2}+y^{2}\leq P^{2}\}
vanishing on the boundary. Under the radially symmetric reduced norm
||\cdot||:W^{1,2}_{0}(D_{P})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle||\phi||^{2}=\int_{0}^{P}\left(\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2}+\rho\phi^{2}\right)d\rho,

(4.6)

and the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi^{2}d\rho\leq P^{2}\int_{0}^{P}\frac{\phi^{2}}{\rho}d\rho,

(4.7)

we deduce that
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
W^{1,2}_{0}(D_{P})
. Without loss of generality, we suppose that
\phi_{j}\rightharpoonup\phi_{0}
weakly in
W^{1,2}_{0}(D_{P})
as
j\rightarrow\infty
.
The compact embedding
W^{1,2}(D_{P})\subset\subset L^{p}(D_{P})

(p\geq 1)
gives the strong convergence of
\phi_{j}\rightarrow\phi_{0}
in
L^{p}(D_{P})
as
j\rightarrow\infty
. Furthermore, for any
\epsilon\in(0,P)
, the sequence is bounded in
W^{1,2}(\epsilon,P)
, which embeds compactly into
\mathcal{C}[\epsilon,P]
. Thus,
\phi_{j}\to\phi_{0}
uniformly on compact subsets away from the origin.

To verify the constraint
\tilde{Q}(\phi_{0})=\tilde{Q}_{0}
, we note that strong convergence in
L^{2}(D_{P})
implies

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}(\phi_{0})=4\pi\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi_{0}^{2}d\rho=\lim_{j\to\infty}4\pi\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi_{j}^{2}d\rho=\tilde{Q}_{0}.

(4.8)

The weak lower semicontinuity of the norm and the continuity of the potential terms under strong
L^{p}
convergence imply

\displaystyle I(\phi_{0})\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}I(\phi_{j})=I_{0}.

(4.9)

Thus,
\phi_{0}
is a minimizer. By the Lagrange Multiplier Theorem, there exists
\omega^{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi_{0}
satisfies the weak form of (
1
). Standard elliptic regularity theory implies that
\phi_{0}
is a classical solution
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(0,P)\cap\mathcal{C}[0,P]
.

To prove positivity, suppose there exists
\rho_{0}\in(0,P)
such that
\phi_{0}(\rho_{0})=0
. Since
\phi_{0}\geq 0
, this
\rho_{0}
is a global minimum, implying
\phi_{0,\rho}(\rho_{0})=0
. By the uniqueness theorem for ODEs, this initial condition
\phi_{0}(\rho_{0})=\phi^{\prime}_{0}(\rho_{0})=0
implies
\phi_{0}(\rho)\equiv 0
for all
\rho
, which contradicts the constraint
\tilde{Q}(\phi_{0})=\tilde{Q}_{0}>0
. Therefore,
\phi_{0}(\rho)>0
for all
\rho\in(0,P)
.

Finally, we establish the lower bound on the reduced norm. Multiply (
1
) by
\rho\phi(\rho)
and integrate by parts on
(0,P)
. Using the equation satisfied by the minimizer
(\phi_{0},\omega)
:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{P}\left(-\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2}-\frac{N^{2}}{\rho}\phi^{2}-\lambda\rho(6\phi^{6}-4a\phi^{4}+2b\phi^{2})+\omega^{2}\rho\phi^{2}\right)d\rho

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2}d\rho-N^{2}\int_{0}^{P}\dfrac{\phi^{2}}{\rho}d\rho+4a\lambda\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi^{4}d\rho-(2\lambda b-\omega^{2})\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi^{2}d\rho.

(4.10)

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
\phi(0)=0
:

\displaystyle\phi^{2}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\rho}2\phi(s)\phi_{s}(s)ds\leq 2\left(\int_{0}^{P}s\phi_{s}^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{P}\dfrac{\phi^{2}}{s}ds\right)^{1/2}.

(4.11)

Multiplying by
\rho\phi^{2}(\rho)
and integrating, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi^{4}d\rho\leq\frac{\tilde{Q}_{0}}{2\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2}d\rho\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{P}\dfrac{\phi^{2}}{\rho}d\rho\right)^{1/2}.

(4.12)

Applying Young’s inequality
xy\leq\epsilon x^{2}+\frac{y^{2}}{4\epsilon}
with
x=(\int\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2})^{1/2}
and
y=\frac{\tilde{Q}_{0}}{2\pi}(\int\phi^{2}/\rho)^{1/2}
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi^{4}d\rho\leq\epsilon\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2}d\rho+\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\left(\frac{\tilde{Q}_{0}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{P}\dfrac{\phi^{2}}{\rho}d\rho\right).

(4.13)

Substituting (
4.13
) into (
4.10
), the positive gradient term has coefficient
(4a\lambda\epsilon-1)
:

\displaystyle 0\leq(4a\lambda\epsilon-1)\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi^{2}_{\rho}d\rho+\left(\frac{4a\lambda\tilde{Q}_{0}^{2}}{16\epsilon\pi^{2}}-N^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{P}\dfrac{\phi^{2}}{\rho}d\rho-(2\lambda b-\omega^{2})\frac{\tilde{Q}_{0}}{4\pi}.

(4.14)

We choose
\epsilon=\frac{1}{4a\lambda}
to eliminate the gradient term. The coefficient of the integral
\int(\phi^{2}/\rho)
becomes:

\displaystyle\frac{4a\lambda\tilde{Q}_{0}^{2}}{16\left(\frac{1}{4a\lambda}\right)\pi^{2}}-N^{2}=\frac{16a^{2}\lambda^{2}\tilde{Q}_{0}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}-N^{2}=\frac{a^{2}\lambda^{2}\tilde{Q}_{0}^{2}}{\pi^{2}}-N^{2}.

(4.15)

Thus, the inequality becomes:

\displaystyle 0\leq\left(\frac{a^{2}\lambda^{2}\tilde{Q}_{0}^{2}}{\pi^{2}}-N^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{P}\dfrac{\phi^{2}}{\rho}d\rho-(2\lambda b-\omega^{2})\frac{\tilde{Q}_{0}}{4\pi}.

(4.16)

For a solution to exist with
\omega^{2}<2\lambda b
, the last term is strictly negative. Therefore, for the inequality to hold, the first term must be strictly positive:

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}\lambda^{2}\tilde{Q}_{0}^{2}}{\pi^{2}}-N^{2}>0\implies\tilde{Q}_{0}>\frac{\pi|N|}{a\lambda}.

(4.17)

5
Numerical Results: Profiles of fundamental
Q
-vortices

The constrained minimization method of Section 4 enables the spectral-Galerkin computation of fundamental
Q
-vortex profiles for a prescribed reduced norm
\tilde{Q}_{0}
. Identical to the numerical work of Volkov and Wöhnert
[
27
]
, we adopt the parameter set:
\lambda=1
,
a=2
, and
b=1.1
.

These parameters impose strict theoretical constraints on the existence and geometry of the solutions, which serve as benchmarks for our numerical results:

1.

Amplitude Bound:
Theorem 1.1 establishes a uniform upper bound on the field amplitude, predicting saturation at:

\phi^{2}(\rho)<\frac{2a}{3}\implies|\phi(\rho)|<\sqrt{\frac{4}{3}}\approx 1.1547.

(5.1)

2.

Frequency Existence Range:
Theorem 1.2 guarantees the existence of ground and excited state solutions for angular frequencies within the range:

\omega_{min}^{2}:=2\lambda\left(b-\frac{a^{2}}{4}\right)<\omega^{2}<2\lambda b=:\omega_{max}^{2}.

(5.2)

For our chosen parameters, these critical bounds are
\omega_{min}^{2}=0.2
and
\omega_{max}^{2}=2.2
.

3.

Prescribed Norm Threshold:
Theorem 1.3 predicts that for solutions to exist below the upper frequency bound (
\omega^{2}<2.2
), the prescribed reduced norm must satisfy the lower bound:

\tilde{Q}_{0}>\frac{\pi|N|}{a\lambda}=\frac{\pi|N|}{2}\approx 1.57|N|.

(5.3)

To determine the appropriate domain size for the numerical computation, we refer to the sufficient bound
P^{*}
established in Lemma 3.2. For the chosen parameters, we calculate
P^{*}\approx 12.5
. To ensure our domain is sufficiently large to capture the exponential tail of the solution and minimize boundary effects, we conservatively select
P=20
for all subsequent computations as in
[
27
]
.

Let
V_{m}
be the finite-dimensional subspace spanned by the first
m
orthonormal functions
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(0,P)
, constructed from the sine basis via a modified Gram-Schmidt process. We define the weighted inner product:

(\psi,\tilde{\psi})=4\pi\int_{0}^{P}\rho\psi(\rho)\tilde{\psi}(\rho)\,d\rho,

(5.4)

such that
(\psi_{i},\psi_{j})=\delta_{ij}
. This choice is motivated by the reduced norm functional
\tilde{Q}(\phi)
, allowing the constraint to be expressed simply as the sum of squared coefficients.

We approximate the solution as
\phi(\rho)\approx\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\psi_{j}(\rho)
, where
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Substituting this ansatz into the action functional
I(\phi)
and exploiting the orthonormality of the basis, the potential term
\int\rho b\phi^{2}
reduces to a constant proportional to
\tilde{Q}_{0}
. The infinite-dimensional problem reduces to minimizing the function
F:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle F(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{i}a_{j}\left(K_{ij}+N^{2}C_{ij}\right)+\frac{\lambda b}{8\pi}\tilde{Q}_{0}+\lambda\int_{0}^{P}\rho\left(\phi^{6}(\rho)-a\phi^{4}(\rho)\right)d\rho,

(5.5)

subject to the constraint
\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}^{2}=\tilde{Q}_{0}
. Here,
K_{ij}
and
C_{ij}
are the pre-computed “stiffness” and “centrifugal” matrices, respectively:

K_{ij}=\int_{0}^{P}\rho\psi_{i}^{\prime}(\rho)\psi_{j}^{\prime}(\rho)\,d\rho,\hskip 20.00003ptC_{ij}=\int_{0}^{P}\frac{1}{\rho}\psi_{i}(\rho)\psi_{j}(\rho)\,d\rho.

(5.6)

The minimization of (
5.5
) is performed using Python’s
SciPy
optimization library. Once the minimizer
\phi
is found, the nonlinear frequency shift
\omega^{2}
(the Lagrange multiplier) is recovered by projecting the Euler-Lagrange equation onto the solution itself:

\omega^{2}=\frac{4\pi}{\tilde{Q}_{0}}\left(\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi_{\rho}^{2}\,d\rho+N^{2}\int_{0}^{P}\frac{\phi^{2}}{\rho}\,d\rho+\int_{0}^{P}\rho\phi U^{\prime}(\phi)\,d\rho\right),

(5.7)

where
U^{\prime}(\phi)=\lambda(6\phi^{5}-4a\phi^{3}+2b\phi)
.

To quantify the accuracy of our numerical approximation, we define the
Residual Error
(RE) as the
L^{2}
norm of the residual of the differential equation (
1
), normalized by the domain size,

RE=\frac{1}{P}\left(\int_{0}^{P}\left(\phi_{\rho\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\phi_{\rho}-\frac{N^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\phi+\omega^{2}\phi-\lambda(6\phi^{5}-4a\phi^{3}+2b\phi)\right)^{2}d\rho\right)^{1/2}.

(5.8)

Figure
1
displays the computed profiles for varying prescribed norm
\tilde{Q}_{0}
with fixed
N=1
. The profiles exhibit a characteristic transition from Gaussian-like shapes at low norm to flat-top, saturated structures at high norm. This saturation behavior is predicted by the potential structure, and we observe that the maximum amplitude remains strictly bounded below the theoretical limit
\phi_{max}\approx 1.1547
(dashed line), confirming the uniform bound derived in Theorem 1.1.

Finally, we investigate the effect of the rotational quantum number
N
on the soliton properties. We fix the prescribed norm at
\tilde{Q}_{0}=100
and vary
N
from 1 to 5. As shown in Figure
4
and Table
2
, increasing
N
pushes the vortex core outward. This effect is driven by the
centrifugal barrier
term
N^{2}/\rho^{2}
in the energy functional, which acts as a repulsive potential near the origin. To minimize energy, the field amplitude is forced to vanish at
\rho=0
and the peak density is shifted to larger radii. Consequently, the maximum amplitude
\phi_{max}
decreases significantly, while the eigenfrequency
\omega^{2}
increases, moving closer to the upper spectral bound.

Abstract
. Introduction

Given a reductive group
G
and a “level”
q\in\mathbf{C}^{\times}
, one obtains the category
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(\check{G})
of representations of the quantum group. It can be regarded as a braided monoidal deformation of the category
\mathrm{Rep}(\check{G})
of representations of the Langlands dual group
\check{G}
.

Morever, the category
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(\check{G})
admits a ribbon structure, which allows one to “spread”
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(\check{G})
onto an oriented
2
-manifold
M
, thus defining an
\mathbb{E}_{M}
-category. It is then possible to extract a global invariant

\int_{M}\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(\check{G}),

called its “factorization homology”, which plays a prominent role in the Betti quantum geometric Langlands program. We refer the reader to
[
BZBJ18
,
BZN18
]
for details.

The consideration above makes us suspect that
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(\check{G})
starts life naturally as an
\mathbb{E}_{M}
-category. Indeed, in the context of the quantum geometric Langlands program, the level
q
is not expected to be a complex number in general, but a (suitably categorified) degree-
4
reduced cohomology class of
\textnormal{{B}}G
. Unless
G
is simply connected, a level of this kind can vary along
M
, so we do not expect
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(\check{G})
to come from a single ribbon category.

In this note, we confirm this suspicion for quantum tori: We give a direct definition of
\mathrm{Rep}_{q}(\check{T})
as an
\mathbb{E}_{M}
-category, which accommodates the general notion of levels as well as nonsplit tori. From a classical perspective, one can say that this note explains how quantum tori behave in family.

Given an

Remark 1.3.3
.

2.1.1.

Remark 2.1.2

Remark 3.2.3
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be an algebraic stack with a smooth cover
f:Z\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}

\colim\operatorname{Sing}(Z^{\bullet}_{/\mathscr{Y}})^{\mathrm{top}}\simeq\operatorname{Sing}\mathscr{Y}^{\mathrm{top}},

\colim\operatorname{Sing}(Z^{\bullet}_{/Y})^{\mathrm{top}}\simeq\operatorname{Sing}Y^{\mathrm{top}}.

Lemma 3.2.5
.

The morphism (
3.12
) is an isomorphism.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that (
3.12
) induces an isomorphism on homotopy groups.

The connected components of

T

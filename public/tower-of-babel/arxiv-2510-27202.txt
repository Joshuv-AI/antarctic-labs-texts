Error analysis with exponential decay estimates for a fully discrete approximation of a class of strongly damped wave equations
Krishan Kumar, P. Danumjaya, Anil Kumar, Amiya K. Pani

Abstract
This paper deals with the asymptotic behavior and FEM error analysis of a class of strongly damped wave equations using a semidiscrete finite element method in spatial directions combined with a finite difference scheme in the time variable. For the continuous problem under weakly and strongly damping parameters α \alpha and β , \beta, respectively, a novel approach usually used for linear parabolic problems is employed to derive an exponential decay property with explicit rates, which depend on model parameters and the principal eigenvalue of the associated linear elliptic operator for the different cases of parameters such as ( i ) ​ α , β > 0 (i)\;\alpha,\beta>0 , ( i ​ i ) ​ α > 0 , β ≥ 0 (ii)\;\alpha>0,\beta\geq 0 and ( i ​ i ​ i ) ​ α ≥ 0 , β > 0 (iii)\;\alpha\geq 0,\beta>0 . Subsequently, for a semi-discrete finite element scheme keeping the temporal variable continuous, optimal error estimates are derived that preserve exponential decay behavior. Some generalizations that include forcing terms and spatially as well as time-varying damping parameters are discussed. Moreover, an abstract discrete problem is discussed, and as a consequence, uniform decay estimates for finite difference as well as spectral approximations to the damped system are briefly indicated. A complete discrete scheme is developed and analyzed after applying a finite difference scheme in time, which again preserves the exponential decay property. The given proofs involve several energies with energy-based techniques to derive the consistency between continuous and discrete decay rates, in which the constants involved do not blow up as α → 0 \alpha\to 0 and β → 0 \beta\to 0 . Finally, several numerical experiments are conducted whose results support the theoretical findings, illustrate uniform decay rates, and explore the effects of parameters on stability and accuracy. Keywords: Strongly damped wave equation, uniform decay estimates, Galerkin finite elements, optimal error estimates, numerical experiments. Mathematics Subject Classification. 65M60, 65M15, 35L20.

2
Continuous case

This section discusses the decay of the energy
E(u)(t)={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}+|u|^{2}_{1}\right)
based on a technique used in the exponential decay of the solution of the parabolic initial and boundary value problem.

By setting
u_{\delta}=e^{\delta t}u
, write

u_{\delta}^{\prime}=\delta e^{\delta t}u+e^{\delta t}u^{\prime}=\delta u_{\delta}+e^{\delta t}u^{\prime}

and

u_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}=\delta^{2}u_{\delta}+2\delta e^{\delta t}u^{\prime}+e^{\delta t}u^{\prime\prime}.

Now, rewrite (
1.5
) as

\displaystyle(u^{\prime\prime}_{\delta},v)+\beta~a(u^{\prime}_{\delta},v)+(\alpha-2\delta)(u^{\prime}_{\delta},v)+(1-\beta\delta)\,a(u_{\delta},v)-\delta(\alpha-\delta)(u_{\delta},v)=0,

(2.1)

with
u_{\delta}(0)=u_{0}\;\mbox{and}\;(u_{\delta})^{\prime}(0)=\delta u_{0}+u_{1}\;\mbox{in}\;\Omega.

Theorem 2.1
.

The following decay estimate hold for the energy
E(u)(t)={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}+|u|^{2}_{1}\right)
:

(i)

if
0<\delta<\min\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}{2},\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}\right)
or
0<\delta=\frac{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}{2}
, then

E(u)(t)\leq C(\alpha,\beta,\lambda_{1})e^{-2\delta t}E(u)(0),

(ii)

if
\delta=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}
, then

\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}\leq Ce^{-2\delta t}E(u)(0).

Proof.

A choice of
v=u_{\delta}^{\prime}
in (
2.1
) yields

{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\|u_{\delta}^{\prime}\|^{2}+(1-\beta\delta)|u_{\delta}|^{2}_{1}-((\alpha-\delta)\delta)\|u_{\delta}\|^{2}\right)+(\alpha-2\delta)\|u_{\delta}^{\prime}\|^{2}+\beta|u_{\delta}^{\prime}|^{2}_{1}=0.

A use of the Poincaré inequality (
1.4
) shows

{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\|u_{\delta}^{\prime}\|^{2}+(1-\beta\delta)|u_{\delta}|^{2}_{1}-((\alpha-\delta)\delta)\|u_{\delta}\|^{2}\right)+\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}-2\delta\right)\|u_{\delta}^{\prime}\|^{2}\leq 0.

(2.2)

Setting

\tilde{E}(u_{\delta})(t)={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|u_{\delta}^{\prime}\|^{2}+(1-\beta\delta)|u_{\delta}|^{2}_{1}-((\alpha-\delta)\delta)\|u_{\delta}\|^{2}\right)

(2.3)

an integration of equation (
2.2
) in time with
\delta\leq\frac{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}{2}
reveals

\tilde{E}(u_{\delta})(t)\leq\tilde{E}(u_{\delta})(0)\leq CE(u)(0).

(2.4)

Again, an application of (
1.4
) with
\delta<\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}
and
\gamma_{0}=1-\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\delta
shows

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(u_{\delta})(t)

\displaystyle={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|u_{\delta}^{\prime}\|^{2}+(1-\beta\delta)|u_{\delta}|^{2}_{1}-\alpha\delta\|u_{\delta}\|^{2}+\delta^{2}\|u_{\delta}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|\delta u_{\delta}+e^{\delta t}u^{\prime}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\left(\lambda_{1}-\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)\delta\right)|u_{\delta}|^{2}_{1}\right)+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\|u_{\delta}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{e^{2\delta t}}{2}\min\left(1,\gamma_{0}\right)\left(\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}+|u|^{2}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}e^{2\delta t}E(u)(t).

On substituting in (
2.4
), we conclude the proof of
(i)
.

For
(ii)
, if
\delta=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}
, then a use of (
1.4
) in (
2.3
) yields

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(u_{\delta})(t)

\displaystyle\geq{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|u^{\prime}_{\delta}\|^{2}+\delta^{2}\|u_{\delta}\|^{2}\right)={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|e^{2\delta t}u+\delta u_{\delta}\|^{2}+\delta^{2}\|u_{\delta}\|^{2}\right)

(2.5)

\displaystyle\geq{\frac{1}{2}}e^{2\delta t}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}.

Again, substitute (
2.5

2.4
) to complete the rest of the proof.

3
Semidiscrete Method

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a shape regular triangulation of
\bar{\Omega}
and let
h=\max\mbox{diam}(K)~\forall~K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
be the discretization parameter. Let

\displaystyle\left(u_{h}^{\prime\prime},v\right)+\beta~a_{h}(u_{h}^{\prime},v)

\displaystyle+\alpha\left(u_{h}^{\prime},v\right)+a_{h}(u_{h},v)=0,~\forall~v\in X_{h},

to be defined later.

By defining

\displaystyle\left(u_{h,\delta}^{\prime\prime},v\right)+\beta~a_{h}(u_{h,\delta}^{\prime},v)+(\alpha-2\delta)\left(u_{h,\delta}^{\prime},v\right)

\displaystyle+(1-\beta\delta)~a_{h}(u_{h,\delta},v)

\displaystyle-\delta(\alpha-\delta)\left(u_{h,\delta},v\right)=0,~\forall~v\in X_{h}.

The discrete energy is given by

E_{h}(u_{h})(t)={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|u_{h}^{\prime}\|^{2}+|u_{h}|^{2}_{1}\right).

\displaystyle+\left(1-\vartheta\right)\beta|\partial_{t}U^{n}|^{2}_{1}+\left(1-\vartheta\right)\alpha\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{k}{2}\left(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}+|\partial_{t}U^{n}|^{2}_{1}\right)\leq 0.

This completes the proof of (
4.2
). For the proof of (
4.4
), an application of the Hölder inequality with (
3.3
) and the Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\Big|\left(\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n+1}\right)\Big|\leq\|\partial_{t}U^{n+1}\|\|U^{n+1}\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|\,|U^{n+1}|_{1}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}{2\lambda_{1}}|U^{n+1}|^{2}_{1}.

Using (
4.5
) in the definition of the extended energy (
4.3
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{\delta}^{n}(U)

\displaystyle=E^{n}(U)+\delta\left(\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}E^{n}(U)+\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}\delta\right)\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}{2\lambda_{1}}\delta\right)|U^{n+1}|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}E^{n}(U),\;\;\mbox{provided}\;0<\delta\leq\min\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}{2},\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}\right).

Again from the definition of the extended energy (
4.3
), a use of (
4.5
) shows

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{\delta}^{n}(U)

\displaystyle=E^{n}(U)+\delta\left(\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}E^{n}(U)-\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}\delta\right)\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}{2\lambda_{1}}\delta\right)|U^{n+1}|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}E^{n}(U),\;\;\mbox{provided}\;0<\delta\leq\min\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}{2},\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}\right).

This completes the rest of the proof.
∎

The following theorem deals with uniform exponential decay property of the completely discrete energy.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
0<\delta\leq\min\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}{2},\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}\right)
, the solution
U^{n+1}
for
n\geq 1
of (
4.1
) satisfies

E^{n}(U)\leq 3e^{-\frac{1}{15}\delta t_{n}}E^{0}(U),

where
E^{n}(U)={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}+|U^{n+1}|^{2}_{1}\right)
.

Proof.

From the definition of the extended energy (
4.3
), there holds

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{t}\tilde{E}^{n}_{\delta}(U)

\displaystyle=\bar{\partial}_{t}E^{n}(U)+\delta\bar{\partial}_{t}\left(\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n+1}\right)

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\bar{\partial}_{t}E^{n}(U)+\frac{\delta}{k}\left(\left(\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n+1}\right)-\left(\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n}\right)+\left(\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n}\right)-\left(\partial_{t}U^{n-1},U^{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{\partial}_{t}E^{n}(U)+\delta\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}+\delta\left(\bar{\partial}_{t}\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n}\right).

Setting
\chi=U^{n}
in (
4.6
) with the relation
U^{n}=U^{n+1}-k\partial_{t}U^{n}
, a use of the Hölder inequality with (
3.3
) and the Young’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\left(\bar{\partial}_{t}\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=-\beta a_{h}(\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n})-\alpha\left(\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n}\right)-a_{h}(U^{n+1},U^{n})

\displaystyle=-\beta a_{h}(\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n+1})+\beta k|\partial_{t}U^{n}|^{2}_{1}-\alpha\left(\partial_{t}U^{n},U^{n+1}\right)+\alpha k\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-|U^{n+1}|^{2}_{1}+ka_{h}(U^{n+1},\partial_{t}U^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\delta_{0}}|\partial_{t}U^{n}|^{2}_{1}+\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}|U^{n+1}|^{2}_{1}+\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\delta_{1}}+\frac{k^{2}\delta_{1}}{2}\right)|\partial_{t}U^{n}|^{2}_{1}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\lambda_{1}\delta_{0}}\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}|U^{n+1}|^{2}_{1}+\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\lambda_{1}\delta_{1}}+\frac{k^{2}\lambda_{1}\delta_{1}}{2}\right)\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}-|U^{n+1}|^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}|U^{n+1}|^{2}_{1}+\frac{k^{2}}{2\delta_{2}}|\partial_{t}U^{n}|^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-\left(1-\delta_{0}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}\right)|U^{n+1}|^{2}_{1}+\frac{\beta\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}{2\lambda_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}+\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}\right)|\partial_{t}U^{n}|^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}{2\lambda_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}+\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}\right)\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}+k^{2}\left(\delta_{1}+\frac{1}{2\delta_{2}}\right)|\partial_{t}U^{n}|^{2}_{1}.

A use of the above inequality with (
4.2
) with a choice of
\vartheta=\frac{21}{40},\delta_{0}=\frac{3}{4},\delta_{2}=\frac{2}{5},\delta_{1}=\frac{30}{17}
in (
4.6
) provided
0<\delta\leq\min\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}{2},\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}\right)
and
\delta k\leq\frac{34}{205}
, shows

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{t}\tilde{E}^{n}_{\delta}(U)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\delta}{20}|U^{n+1}|^{2}_{1}-\left(\frac{21}{40}\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)-\delta\right)\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}-\beta\left(\frac{19}{40}-\frac{19}{20}\frac{\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}{\lambda_{1}}\delta\right)\,|\partial_{t}U^{n}|^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle-\alpha\left(\frac{19}{40}-\frac{19}{20}\frac{\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)}{\lambda_{1}}\delta\right)\,\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}-\frac{k}{2}\|\bar{\partial}_{t}\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}-\frac{k}{2}\left(1-\frac{205}{34}\delta k\right)\,|\partial_{t}U^{n}|^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{10}\delta E^{n}(U)-\left(\frac{21}{40}\left(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}\right)-\frac{21}{20}\delta\right)\|\partial_{t}U^{n}\|^{2}-\frac{k}{2}\left(1-\frac{205}{34}\delta k\right)\,|\partial_{t}U^{n}|^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{10}\delta E^{n}(U).

Now, proceed iteratively to arrive at

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{n}_{\delta}(U)\leq\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{15}\delta k}\tilde{E}^{n-1}_{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{1}{15}\delta k}\tilde{E}^{n-1}_{\delta}(U)\leq\dots\leq e^{-\frac{1}{15}\delta t_{n}}\tilde{E}^{0}_{\delta}(U),

and from (
4.4
)

\displaystyle E^{n}(U)\leq 2\tilde{E}_{\delta}^{n}(U)

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-\frac{1}{15}\delta t_{n}}\tilde{E}^{0}_{\delta}(U)\leq 3e^{-\frac{1}{15}\delta t_{n}}E^{0}(U).

This completes the rest of the proof.
∎

5
Some Generalizations

This section focuses on some generalizations such as problems with nonhomogeneous right-hand sides, with space and time varying damping coefficients, and beam equations with a damping term. Finally, an abstract semi-discrete problem is formulated with some assumptions, which provides a uniform decay property, and, as a consequence, finite difference as well as spectral methods are briefly discussed, whose solutions show uniform asymptotic behavior.

5.1
Inhomogeneous Equations

This subsection discusses the strongly damped wave equation with time independent source term such as

u^{\prime\prime}+\beta Au^{\prime}+\alpha u^{\prime}+Au=f\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,\infty)

with initial conditions

u(0)=u_{0},\quad u^{\prime}(0)=u_{1}.

Let
u_{\infty}
be the unique solution of the steady-state equation

Au_{\infty}=f\quad\mbox{with}\quad u_{\infty}=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega.

Then, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
w=u(t)-u_{\infty}.
Then, there holds

(i)

for
0<\delta<\min\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}{2},\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}\right)
or
0<\delta=\frac{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}}{2},

E^{(k)}_{A^{(j)}}(w)(t)\leq C(\alpha,\beta,\lambda_{1})e^{-2\delta t}E^{(k)}_{A^{(j)}}(w)(0)=C(\alpha,\beta,\lambda_{1})e^{-2\delta t}\left(|w^{(k)}(0)|^{2}_{2j-1}+|w^{(k-1)}(0)|^{2}_{2j}\right).

(ii)

For
0<\delta=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\alpha+\beta\lambda_{1}},

|w^{(k)}(t)|^{2}_{2j-1}\leq C(\alpha,\beta,\lambda_{1})e^{-2\delta t}E^{(k)}_{A^{(j)}}(w)(0)=C(\alpha,\beta,\lambda_{1})e^{-2\delta t}\left(|w^{(k)}(0)|^{2}_{2j-1}+|w^{(k-1)}(0)|^{2}_{2j}\right).

Proof.

Note that
w(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle w^{\prime\prime}+\beta Aw^{\prime}+\alpha w^{\prime}+Aw=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,\infty),

(5.1)

\displaystyle w(0)=u_{0}-u_{\infty},\quad w^{\prime}(0)=u_{1}.

Therefore, for problem (
5.1
), a use of techniques similar to the proof of decay properties in Theorems
2.1
-
2.3
, replacing
u
by
w
completes the rest of the proof.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

For
d=2,3
, a use of the Sobolev embedding result shows

\|w(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|w(t)\|_{2}\leq Ce^{-\delta t}\left(\|u_{1}\|_{3}+\|u_{0}-u_{\infty}\|_{3}\right).

This implies
u(t)\to u_{\infty}
as
t\to\infty
with exponential decay rate
\delta.

For the semidiscrete solution
w_{h}(t)=u_{h}(t)-u_{\infty,h}
similar results as in Section
3
hold true and therefore,
\|u_{h}(t)-u_{\infty,h}\|_{L^{\infty}}=O(e^{-\delta t})
.

Remark 5.2
.

If
f(t)=O(e^{-\delta_{0}t})
, then the solution also decays exponentially with the decay rate
\delta^{*}=\min(\delta,\delta_{0}).

Av=-\sum_{l,q=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}(a_{lq}(x)\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{q}})+a(x)v,

where the
d\times d
matrix
[a_{lq}(x)]
is symmetric, uniformly positive definite and
a\geq 0.

Remark 5.4
.

The present analysis can be easily extended to the problem (
1.1
) when
A=\Delta^{2}
with clamped, hinged, or free homogeneous boundary conditions.

5.4
Abstract discrete problem

Given two finite-dimensional spaces
V_{h}
and
H_{h}
with inner products and norms defined, respectively, as
(\cdot,\cdot)_{V_{h}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{H_{h}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{V_{h}},\;\|\cdot\|_{H_{h}},
such that

V_{h}
is continuously imbedded in
H_{h}
and the discrete symmetric bounded, positive definite linear operator
A_{h}:V_{h}\mapsto V_{h}
and related discrete bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot):V_{h}\times V_{h}\mapsto R
defined by
a_{h}(v_{h},w_{h})=(A_{h}v_{h},w_{h})_{H_{h}}=(A^{1/2}_{h}v_{h},A^{1/2}_{h}w_{h})_{H_{h}},
the semidiscrete damped wave equation is to find
u_{h}(t)\in V_{h}
for
t>0
such that

\displaystyle u_{h}^{\prime\prime}+\beta A_{h}u_{h}^{\prime}+\alpha u_{h}^{\prime}+A_{h}u_{h}=0,\;\;t>0,

(5.10)

with
u_{h}(0)=u_{0,h}\in V_{h}
and
u_{h}^{\prime}(0)=u_{1,h}\in H_{h},
where
u_{h}(0)
and
u_{h}^{\prime}(0)
known functions. As long as the following discrete version of Poincare inequality

\|A^{1/2}_{h}v_{h}\|^{2}_{H_{h}}\geq\lambda\|v_{h}\|^{2}_{H_{h}}

is satisfied with
\lambda>0
independent of the discretization parameter
h,
it is possible to prove the decay estimates for the semidiscrete solution
u_{h},
where the decay estimates are uniform with respect to
h.

5.4.1
Finite Difference Schemes

We assume that the domain
\Omega
is the unit square in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
Av=-\Delta v
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition. For the numerical solution of (
1.1
) with
\Omega=(0,1)\times(0,1)
, we select a mesh of width
h=\frac{1}{M}
, where
M
is a positive integer, and cover
\bar{\Omega}=~\Omega\cup\partial\Omega
with a square grid of mesh points
x_{ij}=(ih,jh)
for
i,~j=0,1,\ldots,M
. Let
\Omega_{h}=\{x_{ij}:~x_{ij}\in\partial\Omega\}
. We can cover the whole
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with such a square grid, and will denote it by
\mathbb{R}_{h}^{2}.

For a function
w
defined on
\mathbb{R}_{h}^{2}
, we adopt the following notation: for
x~\in~\mathbb{R}_{h}^{2}
and
l~=~1,2
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} w^{\pm l}=w(x\pm h{\bf e}_{l}),\;w^{+l,-q}=w(x&+h{\bf e}_{l}-h{\bf e}_{q}),\;\nabla_{l}w(x)=\frac{w(x+h{\bf e}_{l})-w(x)}{h}\;\mbox{and}\\
\bar{\nabla}_{l}w(x)&=\frac{w(x)-w(x-h{\bf e}_{l})}{h},\end{aligned}

where
{\bf e}_{l}
is the
l
th unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{h}
denote the mesh functions defined on
\mathbb{R}_{h}^{2}
that vanish outside of
\Omega_{h}
. For
u,~v\in~\mathcal{D}
, we now introduce the discrete
L^{2}
-space, denoted by
L_{h}^{2}
with inner product and norm given, respectively, by

<w,v>=h^{2}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{h}^{2}}w(x)v(x)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\|w\|_{0,h}=<w,w>^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, for
w~\in~{\mathcal{D}}_{h}
, let
H_{h}^{1}
denote the distance analogue of the
H_{0}^{1}
-Sobolev space with norm
\|w\|_{1,h}^{2}=\sum_{l=1}^{2}\|\nabla_{l}w\|_{0,h}^{2}
. We also introduce the discrete
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}
-Sobolev space with norm

\|w\|_{2,h}^{2}=\|w\|_{1,h}^{2}+\sum_{l=1,q}^{2}\|\nabla_{l}\bar{\nabla}_{q}w\|_{0,h}^{2},\,\,\,w\in{\mathcal{D}_{h}}

and denote it by
H_{h}^{2}
. Whenever there is no confusion, we write
\|w\|
and
\|w\|_{j},~j~=~1,2
, instead of
\|w\|_{0,h}
and
\|w\|_{j,h}.

For functions
v
and
w
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{h}
, the following identities are easy consequences of summation by parts

<\nabla_{l}v,w>=-<v,\bar{\nabla}_{l}w>,\,\,\,l=1,2.

(5.11)

Let
A_{h}
denote the finite difference operator approximating
A
, which is defined for
v_{h}\in H^{2}_{h}
by

A_{h}v_{h}=-\Delta_{h}v_{h}=-\sum_{l,q=1}^{d}\bar{\nabla}_{l}(\nabla_{q}v_{h}),

and with
u_{h}(t)\in H_{h}^{1},
the semidiscrete finte difference formulation is given by (
5.10
), where
V_{h}=H^{1}_{h}
and
H_{h}=L^{2}_{h}.

Setting for
v_{h},w_{h}\in H_{h}^{1}
,
a(v_{h},w_{h})=\sum_{l=1}^{d}<\nabla_{l}v_{h},\nabla_{l}w_{h}>=<A_{h}v_{h},w_{h}>
by (
5.11
),
the following properties are valid.

(i)

a(v_{h},v_{h})=<A_{h}v_{h},v_{h}>=\|v_{h}\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{h}}.

(ii)

|a(v_{h},w_{h})|=|<A_{h}(t)V,W>|\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{1,h}\|w_{h}\|_{1,h}.

Since
A_{h}
is symmetric and positive definite,
A^{1/2}
is well defined and

a(v_{h},w_{h})=<A_{h}^{1/2}v_{h},A^{1/2}w_{h}>=<A_{h}v_{h},w_{h}>

and

a(v_{h},v_{h})=\|A^{1/2}v_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{h}}.

From the discrete version of Poincáre inequality it follows that there exists a positive constant
\lambda>0
independent of
h
such that for
v\in H^{1}_{h}
, we have

\lambda\|v_{h}\|^{2}~\leq\sum_{l=1}^{d}\|\nabla_{l}V\|^{2}=\|A^{1/2}v_{h}\|_{0,h}^{2}.

Remark 5.5
.

This analysis in abstract discrete problem can be easily extended to spectral method applied to the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
).

7
Conclusion

This paper deals with a detailed, mathematically rigorous study of semidiscrete and completely discrete approximations for damped wave equations involving weak and strong damping parameters, which preserve uniform exponential decay and optimal error estimates. The analysis involves energy-based techniques to establish consistency between continuous and discrete decay rates and explicit rates that depend on damping parameters and the principal eigenvalue of the associated linear elliptic operator. The rigorous statements and careful estimates significantly improve our results. Including multiple scenarios, such as space, time-dependent damping parameters, and nonhomogeneous source terms, is a major highlight of our approach. The set of numerical experiments, the results of which confirm our theoretical findings, is presented. The generalizations given in the Section
5
include the problem with nonhomogeneous forcing function, with time and space varying damping coefficients, vibration of beam with damping and an abstract discrete problem with application to both finite difference and spectral approximations showing uniform decay behavior.

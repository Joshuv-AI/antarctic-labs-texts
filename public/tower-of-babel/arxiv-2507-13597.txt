Equation of state of spin-polarized nuclear matter in the relativistic Hartree-Fock method
Toi Tachibana, Kouichi Hagino, Kenichi Yoshida, Qiang Zhao

Abstract
We calculate the equation of state (EOS) of spin-polarized nuclear matter in the relativistic Hartree-Fock method. To this end, we employ the relativistic point-coupling model, with which the Fock terms are considerably simplified, reducing them to the same form as the Hartree terms. In analogy to the slope parameter L L of the isospin-symmetry energy for spin-unpolarized matter, we evaluate the spin slope parameter L s L_{s} of the corresponding spin-symmetry energy for spin-polarized matter. We find that the slope parameter L L and the spin slope parameter L s L_{s} have a negative correlation in the case of isoscalar polarization, where neutrons and protons are spin-polarized in the same direction. On the other hand, the spin slope parameter is nearly independent of the slope parameter in the case of isovector polarization, where neutrons are spin-polarized along the opposite direction to protons. We show that these correlations are a natural consequence of the relativistic point coupling model which we employ.



I
INTRODUCTION

The equation of state (EOS) of nuclear matter
plays a crucial role
in a variety of astrophysical phenomena, such as the inner structure of neutron stars and supernovae
Yamamoto et al. 2017
;
Togashi et al. 2017
;
Shen et al. 1998
;
Hempel et al. 2012
;
Sumiyoshi et al. 2005
.
It has been regarded as one of the major goals of nuclear physics to
determine the EOS of nuclear matter with high precision,
using a unified method both for finite nuclei and infinite nuclear matter.

The EOS of symmetric nuclear matter in the vicinity of the saturation density
is characterized by the saturation density, the saturation energy and
the incompressibility, which have
already been known to some extent
Ring and Schuck 1980
;
Bohr and Mottelson 1969
;
Obertelli and Sagawa 2021
;
Fetter and Walecka 1971
;
Shlomo et al. 2006
;
Sharma 2009
;
Garg and Colò 2018
.
However, this information is not sufficient to discuss
the inner structure of neutron stars, as a high isospin asymmetry and a wide range of nucleon density
are realized there.
Therefore, many theoretical and experimental studies have been carried out
Akmal et al. 1998
;
Togashi and Takano 2013
;
Bombaci and Lombardo 1991
;
Lattimer and Lim 2013
;
Berman and Fultz 1975
;
Zenihiro et al. 2010
;
Adhikari et al. 2021
;
Adhikari et al. 2022
;
Tsang et al. 2012
;
Carbone et al. 2010

aiming at extending the EOS into the isospin-asymmetric and a wide density regions.

In particular, it has been expected that
the symmetry energy near the saturation density can be constrained by terrestrial nuclear experiments.
Here, the symmetry energy approximately corresponds to
the difference between the energies of symmetric nuclear matter and pure neutron matter,
characterizing
the EOS of asymmetric nuclear matter.
For instance, it is known that the slope parameter defined as the derivative of the symmetry energy at the saturation
density is linearly correlated with
the neutron skin thickness of heavy nuclei such as
\hphantom{{}^{\text{208}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{208}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{208}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{208}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{208}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Pb}

Roca-Maza et al. 2011
, and thus
measuring the neutron skin thickness of finite nuclei can constrain the slope parameter. Because of this,
numerous experiments
to measure the neutron skin thickness have been carried out so far
Zenihiro et al. 2010
;
Tamii et al. 2011
;
Adhikari et al. 2021
;
Adhikari et al. 2022
.

While
spin saturated nuclear matter is usually considered in such studies,
the EOS of symmetric nuclear matter
can be extended not only to the isospin-asymmetric direction, that is, neutron matter,
but also
to the spin-asymmetric direction, that is, spin polarized matter.
This is to
explore properties of nuclear matter in the two-dimensional
spin-isospin space.
Spin-polarized matter is of interest for several reasons.
Firstly,
the EOS of spin-polarized matter determines
the existence of a ferromagnetic phase of nuclear matter
through its
magnetic susceptibility
Vidaña and Bombaci 2002
;
Niembro et al. 1990
;
Marcos et al. 1991
;
Maruyama and Tatsumi 2001
.
Secondly,
spin polarization in nuclear matter may affect the proton fraction in neutron star matter,
which may enhance neutron star cooling
Tan et al. 2020
.
Recently, observations of the blue kilonova ejecta following the neutron star merger GW170817
Tanaka et al. 2017
;
Abbott et al. 2017
;
Evans et al. 2017

have indicated that a hypermassive neutron star remnant has a strong magnetic field of
(1-3)\times 10^{14}
G at its surface
Metzger et al. 2018
,
and a partial spin polarization could occur there.
Because of this, the EOS of spin-polarized matter has attracted lots of attention in recent years.
In Refs.
Khoa et al. 2022
;
Tan et al. 2020
,
Khoa et al. discussed the EOS of spin-polarized matter using the non-relativistic Hartree-Fock method with the CDM3Yn
interaction and concluded that up to 60-80% of baryons in the neutron star merger may be spin polarized.
They also defined the spin slope parameter as the derivative of the EOS of fully spin-polarized matter
at the saturation density.
Properties of spin polarized nuclear matter were studied also in Ref.
Margueron et al. 2009
;
Chamel and Goriely 2010
using the non-relativistic
Skyrme interactions.

In this paper, we
study the spin-polarized nuclear matter
by using the relativistic framework,
which more naturally treats the spin degree of freedom of nucleons than non-relativistic frameworks.
In addition,
we investigate the correlation between the slope parameter and the spin slope parameter.
If there is a significant correlation between these slopes,
one can indirectly constrain the EOS for spin-polarized matter by measuring the neutron skin thickness or other observables
which are correlated with the slope parameter. Notice that there have been almost no discussions so far on experimental
probes which can constrain the EOS of spin-polarized matter.

To discuss the EOS of spin polarized matter,
it has been pointed out that the Fock exchange terms
play a crucial role
Niembro et al. 1990
;
Maruyama and Tatsumi 2001
.
For this reason, we shall
adopt the relativistic point-coupling model, which simplifies the treatment of
the Fock terms.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we formulate
the relativistic point-coupling model.
In Sec.
III
, we present the numerical results for the spin polarized EOS
for several values of the spin polarization rate.
In Sec.
IV
, we discuss the correlation between the slope parameter and the spin slope parameter,
and propose a possible probe
to constrain the EOS for spin-polarized matter.
Finally, we summarize the paper in Sec.
V
.

II
Relativistic Point-Coupling Model

In order to calculate the EOS of spin-polarized matter, we employ the relativistic
zero-range point-coupling model
Bürvenich et al. 2002
;
Zhao et al. 2010
. This model was originally invented for
Hartree calculations of atomic nuclei, but it has recently been extended to Hartree-Fock
calculations with the exchange terms
Sulaksono et al. 2003
;
Liang et al. 2012
;
Zhao et al. 2022
.
The Lagrangian for this model reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}^{\text{free}}+\mathcal{L}^{\text{4f}}+\mathcal{L}^{\text{der}}+\mathcal{L}^{\text{em}},

(1)

where
\mathcal{L}^{\text{free}}
is the free nucleon term given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\text{free}}=\bar{\psi}(i\gamma_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}-M)\psi.

(2)

Here,
\psi
and
M
represent the nucleon field and the nucleon mass, respectively.

\mathcal{L}^{\text{4f}}
refers to the four fermion term, and is
expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\text{4f}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{(0)}_{S}(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{(0)}_{tS}(\bar{\psi}\vec{\tau}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{(0)}_{V}(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{(0)}_{tV}(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\vec{\tau}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{(0)}_{PV}(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}\psi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{(0)}_{tPV}(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}\vec{\tau}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{(0)}_{PS}(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{5}\psi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{(0)}_{tPS}(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{5}\vec{\tau}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{(0)}_{T}(\bar{\psi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{(0)}_{tT}(\bar{\psi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\vec{\tau}\psi)^{2}.

(3)

Here, the subscript
S
,
V
,
PS
,
PV
and
T
represent the scalar, vector, pseudoscalar, pseudovector and tensor coupling terms, respectively, while the additional subscript “
t
” refers to the isovector
channel.
For uniform matter, the contribution from the pseudoscalar term vanishes due to parity conservation.
Likewise, the derivative coupling term
\mathcal{L}^{\text{der}}
and the electromagnetic term
\mathcal{L}^{\text{em}}

in Eq. (
1
)
do not contribute to the EOS of infinite nuclear matter.

With the no-sea approximation, one can expand the nucleon field
\psi(x)

for uniform matter
by using the basis of annihilation operators of nucleons
\{c_{\bm{p}st}\}
together with
the positive energy plane wave solutions
u(\bm{p},s,t)
as,

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\sum_{\bm{p},s,t}e^{-ip\cdot x}u(\bm{p},s,t)c_{\bm{p}st},

(4)

where
\bm{p}
represents the single-particle momentum and the indices
s
and
t
refer to the spin and the isospin of a nucleon, respectively.

V
represents the volume of the system and we take the infinite volume limit in the following discussion.
In the Hatree-Fock approximation, the ground state wave function is given
as a Slater determinant,

\displaystyle\ket{\Phi_0}=\prod_{\bm{p},s,t}c^{\dagger}_{\bm{p}st}\ket{-},

(5)

where
|-\rangle
is the vacuum state.
Taking the expectation value of the corresponding Hamiltonian in the ground state yields
the energy density of the system,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\frac{\bra{\Phi_0}H\ket{\Phi_0}}{V}=\mathcal{E}_{\text{kin}}+\mathcal{E}_{\text{4f}}.

(6)

In this equation,
the kinetic energy term
\mathcal{E}_{\text{kin}}
is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{kin}}=\sum_{s,t}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}u^{\dagger}(\bm{p},s,t)(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\alpha}+M\beta)u(\bm{p},s,t),

(7)

where
\bm{\alpha}=\gamma^{0}\bm{\gamma}
and
\beta=\gamma^{0}
are Dirac matrices.
The four-fermion interaction energy
\mathcal{E}_{\text{4f}}
is written as a sum of the
Hartree (direct) and the Fock (exchange) terms,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{4f}}=\mathcal{E}^{\text{D}}+\mathcal{E}^{\text{EX}},

(8)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\text{D}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\alpha^{(0)}_{i}\left[\sum_{\xi}\bar{u}(\xi)\left(\mathcal{O}\Gamma\right)_{i}u(\xi)\right]^{2},

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\text{EX}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\alpha^{(0)}_{i}\sum_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}\left[\bar{u}(\xi)\left(\mathcal{O}\Gamma\right)_{i}u(\xi^{\prime})\right]\left[\bar{u}(\xi^{\prime})\left(\mathcal{O}\Gamma\right)_{i}u(\xi)\right].

(10)

Here, we have used a simplified notation, in which
\xi
represents a set of
(\bm{p},s,t)
and the sum
\sum_{\xi}
is defined as

\sum_{\xi}=\sum_{s,t}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}.

(11)

In Eq. (
9
) and (
10
),
the matrices
\mathcal{O}
and
\Gamma
represent the isospin matrix and the gamma matrix, respectively, with

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\in\{1,\tau_{3}\},~~~~\Gamma\in\{1,\gamma_{5},\gamma^{\mu},\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu},\sigma^{\mu\nu}\}.

(12)

Each combination of
\mathcal{O}
and
\Gamma
represents
a coupling channel,
i\in\{S,tS,V,tV,PS,tPS,PV,tPV,T,tT\}
, that is,
\left(\mathcal{O}\Gamma\right)_{i}
.
The Fierz transformation simplifies the Fock terms into

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\text{EX}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\alpha^{(0)}_{i}\Lambda_{ij}\left[\sum_{\xi}\bar{u}(\xi)\left(\mathcal{O}\Gamma\right)_{j}u(\xi)\right]^{2},

(13)

where
\Lambda
refers to the Fierz transformation matrix.
Therefore, one can easily sum up the Hartree and the Fock terms and obtains

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\text{4f}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\alpha^{(0)}_{i}(\delta_{ij}-\Lambda_{ij})\left[\sum_{\xi}\bar{u}(\xi)\left(\mathcal{O}\Gamma\right)_{j}u(\xi)\right]^{2}

(14)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\left[\sum_{\xi}\bar{u}(\xi)\left(\mathcal{O}\Gamma\right)_{j}u(\xi)\right]^{2},

(15)

with
\alpha_{j}\equiv\sum_{i}\alpha^{(0)}_{i}(\delta_{ij}-\Lambda_{ij})
.
As a result, one obtains the four-fermion interaction energy
\mathcal{E}^{\text{4f}}
composed of
the channel densities as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\text{4f}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{S}\rho_{S}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{tS}\rho_{tS}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{V}\rho_{V}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{tV}\rho_{tV}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{PV}\rho_{PV}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{tPV}\rho_{tPV}^{2}+\alpha_{T}\rho_{T}^{2}+\alpha_{tT}\rho_{tT}^{2},

(16)

with

\displaystyle\rho_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi}\bar{u}(\xi)u(\xi),~~~\rho_{tS}=\sum_{\xi}\tau_{3}(\xi)\bar{u}(\xi)u(\xi),

(17a)

\displaystyle\rho_{V}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi}u^{\dagger}(\xi)u(\xi),~~~\rho_{tV}=\sum_{\xi}\tau_{3}(\xi)u^{\dagger}(\xi)u(\xi),

(17b)

\displaystyle\rho_{PV}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi}u^{\dagger}(\xi)\Sigma_{z}u(\xi),~~~\rho_{tPV}=\sum_{\xi}\tau_{3}(\xi)u^{\dagger}(\xi)\Sigma_{z}u(\xi),

(17c)

\displaystyle\rho_{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi}u^{\dagger}(\xi)\beta\Sigma_{z}u(\xi),~~~\rho_{tT}=\sum_{\xi}\tau_{3}(\xi)u^{\dagger}(\xi)\beta\Sigma_{z}u(\xi).

(17d)

Here,
\Sigma_{z}
is the spin operator along the
z
-axis and is expressed as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{z}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sigma_{z}&0\\
0&\sigma_{z}\end{array}\right)

in the Dirac representation.
Notice that not all of the coupling constants in Eq. (
II
) are independent,
since the rank of
1-\Lambda^{T}
is 5,
that is smaller than the dimension of the matrix.
The number of independent parameters in Eq. (
II
) is 5, that is
the same as the rank of the matrix.
In Ref.
Zhao et al. 2022
, those 5 independent parameters are taken to be

\alpha_{S},\alpha_{V},\alpha_{tS},\alpha_{tV}
and
\alpha_{T}
, and the other coupling constants are expressed as
a linear combination of these five coupling constants,

\displaystyle\alpha_{PV}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}(2\alpha_{S}+3\alpha_{tS}+2\alpha_{V}+3\alpha_{tV}+6\alpha_{T})

(20a)

\displaystyle\alpha_{tPV}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{9}(2\alpha_{S}+3\alpha_{tS}+5\alpha_{V}-6\alpha_{tV}+6\alpha_{T})

(20b)

\displaystyle\alpha_{tT}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{18}(-\alpha_{S}+3\alpha_{tS}+2\alpha_{V}-6\alpha_{tV}+6\alpha_{T}).

(20c)

The PCF-PK1 model of Ref.
Zhao et al. 2022
also assumes a density dependence for

\alpha_{S},~\alpha_{V},~\alpha_{tS}
, and
\alpha_{tV}
in a form of

\alpha_{i}(\rho_{B})=\alpha_{i}(\rho_{0})f_{i}(x),~~~i=S,V,tS,{\rm and}~tV,

(21)

where
\rho_{B}
and
\rho_{0}
are the baryon density and the nuclear saturation density, respectively,
and
x
is defined as
x\equiv\rho_{B}/\rho_{0}
.
The function
f_{i}(x)
is defined as

f_{i}(x)=a_{i}\,\frac{1+b_{i}(x+d_{i})^{2}}{1+c_{i}(x+d_{i})^{2}},

(22)

where
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}
, and
d_{i}
are model parameters.

To construct the densities,
one needs to determine the single-particle wave function
u(\bm{p},s,t)
by
solving the Dirac equation obtained by
taking the variation of the the energy density
\mathcal{E}
with respect to

u^{\dagger}(\bm{p},s,t)
,

\displaystyle\left[\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\alpha}+M^{*}\beta-V_{PV}\Sigma_{z}+2V_{T}\beta\Sigma_{z}\right]u(\bm{p},s,t)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{\bm{p},s,t}^{*}u(\bm{p},s,t).

(23)

In this equation, we have defined the effective mass,
M^{*}
, the effective energy,
\epsilon_{\bm{p},s,t}^{*}
, and the self
energy for the
PV
and
T
channels,
V_{PV}
and
V_{T}
, as

\displaystyle M^{*}

\displaystyle=M+\alpha_{S}\rho_{S}+\tau_{3}\alpha_{tS}\rho_{tS},

(24a)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\bm{p},s,t}^{*}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{\bm{p},s,t}-\alpha_{V}\rho_{V}-\tau_{3}\alpha_{tV}\rho_{tV},

(24b)

\displaystyle V_{PV}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{PV}\rho_{PV}+\tau_{3}\alpha_{tPV}\rho_{tPV},

(24c)

\displaystyle V_{T}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{T}\rho_{T}+\tau_{3}\alpha_{tT}\rho_{tT}.

(24d)

The
4\times 4
matrix appearing on the left-hand side of Eq. (
II
) reads

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M^{*}-(V_{PV}-2V_{T})\sigma_{z}&\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma}\\
\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma}&-M^{*}-(V_{PV}+2V_{T})\sigma_{z}\end{array}\right).

When nuclear matter is not spin-polarized, the self-energies
V_{PV}
and
V_{T}
vanish, and
one can solve the Dirac equation (
II
) analytically for given densities.
In contrast, for spin-polarized matter, it is much more difficult to obtain the analytic solution of the
Dirac equation, and we thus numerically diagonalize the 4
\times
4 matrix, (
II
).
The self-consistent solution for a given baryon density
\rho_{B}
is obtained iteratively as follows.
First, we solve the Dirac equation by setting
\rho_{i}=0
for all
i
except for
\rho_{V}
, which is set to be
\rho_{V}=\rho_{B}
,
and then construct the density
\rho_{i}
with the solutions of the the Dirac equation.
This provides the initial value of the density for each coupling channel,
\rho_{i}
.
We then numerically diagonalize
the matrix (
II
) for each momentum satisfying
|\bm{p}|<p_{k_{F},s,t}
, where
p_{k_{F},s,t}
refers to the
Fermi momentum evaluated in the Thomas-Fermi approximation for the nucleons with spin
s
and isospin
t
.
For simplicity, we shall ignore the distortion of the Fermi surface
Maruyama and Tatsumi 2001
;
Maruyama et al. 2018
induced by a spin polarization.
Notice that, since Eq. (
II
) has the rotational symmetry around the
z
-axis, it is sufficient to consider the 2-dimensional momentum space
(p_{\perp},p_{z})
, assuming
p_{x}=p_{y}=p_{\perp}
.
Second, we calculate each density,
\rho_{i}
, using the so obtained solution,
u(\bm{p},s,t)
.
We repeat this procedure until
the densities
\rho_{i}
are converged to certain values.

III
Numerical Results

Figure 1:
(a), (b): The EOS of spin-polarized symmetric nuclear matter
for (a) the isoscalar (IS) and (b) the isovector (IV) spin polarizations
for various values of
\Delta
defined by
Eq. (
29
).
(c), (d): The expectation value of the spin operator
\Sigma_{z}
for the IS and the IV spin polarizations, respectively.

The EOS of spin-polarized matter is characterized by the baryon debsity,
\rho_{B}
, the isospin asymmetry,
\delta
, and the spin polarization rate
\Delta_{t}
for nucleons with isospin
t
.
These are expressed as,

\displaystyle\rho_{B}=\rho_{V},

(27)

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{\rho_{n}-\rho_{p}}{\rho_{B}},

(28)

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}=\frac{\rho_{t,s=1}-\rho_{t,s=-1}}{\rho_{t}},

(29)

where
\rho_{t,s}
and
\rho_{t}~(t=n,p)
are given by

\displaystyle\rho_{t,s}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}u^{\dagger}(\bm{p},s,t)u(\bm{p},s,t),

(30)

\displaystyle\rho_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\rho_{t,s}.

(31)

For simplicity, in this work we assume
|\Delta_{n}|=|\Delta_{p}|\equiv\Delta
.
We consider two cases: one is the isoscalar (IS) spin polarization, where
the spins of neutrons and protons are polarized in the same direction, that is,

\Delta_{n}=\Delta_{p}
, while the other is the isovector (IV) spin polarization, where
the spins of neutrons are polarized in the opposite direction to those of protons, that is,

\Delta_{n}=-\Delta_{p}
.
Notice that only the IV polarization was considered in the previous study by Khoa et al.
Tan et al. 2020
;
Khoa et al. 2022
.

Figs.
1
(a) and (b) show the EOS of spin-polarized symmetric matter calculated by the relativistic point-coupling model.
Fig.
1
(a) shows the result of the IS polarization, while Fig.
1
(b) shows that of the IV polarization.
One can see that
the energy of nuclear matter for a given value of
\Delta
is larger for the IS polarization than for the IV polarization.
This difference is due to the different contribution of the PV and T channels.
That is, for the IS(IV) polarization, the isoscalar (isovector) components of these channels contribute.
In any case, these contribution vanish in the Hartree approximation, indicating an importance
of including the Fock terms.

It should be noted that the spin index,
s
,
in the relativistic wave function
u(\bm{p},s,t)

does not represent the spin along the
z
-axis,
because the single-particle Hamiltonian in Eq. (
II
) does not commute with the spin operator along the
z
-axis,
\Sigma_{z}
, except for
\bm{p}=0
, and thus the wave function
u(\bm{p},s,t)
is not an eigenvector of
\Sigma_{z}
.
That is,

\Delta

should be regarded simply as a convenient parameter for numerical calculations,
and a more consistent treatment is to calculate the expectation value of

\Sigma_{z}
.
Notice that this problem is inherent to the relativistic approach,
as the expectation value of
\Sigma_{z}
coincides to
\Delta

in the non-relativistic framework.
Figs.
1
(c) and (d) show the expectation value of
\Sigma_{z}
,
\expectationvalue{\Sigma_z}
.
One can see that
\expectationvalue{\Sigma_z}
is smaller than
\Delta
especially in the high density region.
In comparison to the results of Khoa et al.
Tan et al. 2020
;
Khoa et al. 2022
for the IV polarization,
we obtain a qualitatively similar EOS as a function of
\Delta
, even though
our EOS is somewhat softer.

The EOS of spin unpolarized matter is often expanded in the isospin asymmetry
\delta
as

\displaystyle\frac{E}{A}(\rho_{B},\delta)=\frac{E}{A}(\rho_{B},\delta=0)+S(\rho_{B})\delta^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{4}),

(32)

in which
S(\rho_{B})
is the symmetry energy expressed as

\displaystyle S(\rho_{B})=\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\delta^{2}}\frac{E}{A}\right|_{\delta=0}.

(33)

The slope parameter
L
is defined as

\displaystyle L=3\rho_{0}\left.\frac{\partial S}{\partial\rho_{B}}\right|_{\rho_{B}=\rho_{0}}.

(34)

It has been well recognized that
the 4th-order or the higher-order terms with respect to
\delta
in Eq. (
32
)
are negligibly small
Khoa et al. 1996
;
Togashi and Takano 2013
.
In other words, one can approximately obtain the symmetry energy
S(\rho_{B})
as

\displaystyle S(\rho_{B})\simeq\frac{E/A(\rho_{B},\delta)-E/A(\rho_{B},\delta=0)}{\delta^{2}},

(35)

instead of Eq. (
33
).
By analogy with the symmetry energy
S(\rho_{B})
, one can
introduce the spin symmetry energy
W(\rho_{B},\delta)

Khoa et al. 2022
.
In the relativistic framework, the original expression for
W(\rho_{B},\delta)
in Ref.
Khoa et al. 2022

can be modified as

\displaystyle W(\rho_{B},\delta)=\frac{E/A(\rho_{B},\delta,\Delta)-E/A(\rho_{B},\delta,\Delta=0)}{\left[\expectationvalue{\Sigma_z}(\rho_{B},\delta,\Delta)\right]^{2}}.

(36)

The definition of the spin symmetry energy
W(\rho_{B},\delta)
differs from that of Ref.
Khoa et al. 2022

in that the numerator of
W(\rho_{B},\delta)
is not
\Delta
but
\expectationvalue{\Sigma_z}
.
From Eq. (
36
), one can define the spin slope parameter
L_{s}
as
Khoa et al. 2022

\displaystyle L_{s}=3\rho_{0}\left.\frac{\partial W}{\partial\rho_{B}}\right|_{\rho_{B}=\rho_{0}}.

(37)

In our calculations, we obtain the slope parameter of
L=79.9
MeV for spin unpolarized matter.
This value is consistent with the empirical value
L=58.7\pm 28.1
MeV
Oertel et al. 2017

In the non-relativistic calculation by Khoa et al.

IV
correlation between the slope parameters

In order to investigate a possible probe to constrain the spin slope parameter,
L_{s}
, we first study
the correlation between the slope parameter
L
and the spin slope parameter
L_{s}
.
Since the measurement of the neutron skin thickness of finite nuclei can constrain the slope parameter
L

Roca-Maza et al. 2011
,
the spin slope parameter can also be constrained if
the spin slope parameter
L_{s}
is significantly correlated with the slope parameter
L
.
For this purpose,
we apply a similar method as that proposed in Ref.
Inakura and Nakada 2015
, in which
the slope parameter is varied by changing the density dependence of the coupling constants.
To this end,
we introduce a new parameter
y
and modify the coupling constant for the isovector-vector channel,
\alpha_{tV}(\rho_{B})
, as

\displaystyle\alpha_{tV}(\rho_{B})\rightarrow(1-y)\alpha_{tV}(\rho_{B})+y\alpha_{tV}(\rho_{0}).

(38)

Notice that this modification keeps
the value of
\alpha_{tV}(\rho_{B})
at the saturation density,
\rho_{0}
.
It therefore modifies
only the slope of the EOS while the EOS at the saturation density is unaffected.
Notice that one could also modify the coupling constant for the isovector-scalar channel,
\alpha_{tS}(\rho_{B})
, instead of

\alpha_{tV}
, but we have confirmed that the results do not significantly change and thus we
only show the results with the modification in
\alpha_{tV}
.

Fig.
2
shows the correlation between the slope parameter
L
and the spin slope parameter
L_{s}
.
The values of the slope parameters obtained with the original value of the coupling constant
\alpha_{tV}

y=0

In the case of the IS spin polarization shown by the red dot-dashed line,
the slope parameter
L

L_{s}

On the other hand, in the case of the IV spin polarization shown by the
blue dashed line, the spin slope parameter
L_{s}
is nearly independent of the slope parameter
L
and remains almost a constant.
This indicates that the spin slope parameter for the IS spin polarization
can be constrained by measuring the neutron skin thickness, whereas that for the IV polarization
cannot be constrained in this way.

According to Eq. (
35
) for the definition of the symmetry energy, the symmetry energy can be obtained from
the EOS of symmetric matter and neutron matter.
For symmetric matter, only
\rho_{S}
and
\rho_{V}
have non-zero values in Eq. (
6
) while
the other densities vanish. On the other hand, for neutron matter,
\rho_{tS}
and
\rho_{tV}
,
in addition to
\rho_{S}
and
\rho_{V}
, are non-zero and the others vanish.
Since, for neutron matter,
\rho_{tS}
and
\rho_{tV}
are approximately the same as
\rho_{B}
at the saturation density,
the symmetry energy
S(\rho_{B})
is approximately calculated as

\displaystyle S(\rho_{B})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{E}{A}(\rho_{B},\delta=1,\Delta=0)-\frac{E}{A}(\rho_{B},\delta=0,\Delta=0)

\displaystyle=\Delta E_{\text{kin}}+\frac{1}{2\rho_{B}}(\alpha_{tS}\rho_{tS}^{2}+\alpha_{tV}\rho_{tV}^{2})

\displaystyle\simeq\Delta E_{\text{kin}}+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{tS}+\alpha_{tV})\rho_{B},

(39)

where
\Delta E_{\text{kin}}
represents the difference between the kinetic energy for symmetric matter and that for
neutron matter.
The slope parameter
L
can be calculated by differentiating the symmetry energy
S(\rho_{B})
with respect to the
baryon density
\rho_{B}
as,

\displaystyle L=L_{\text{kin}}^{(n)}+\frac{3}{2}(\alpha_{tS}+\alpha_{tV})\rho_{0},

(40)

where
L_{\text{kin}}^{(n)}
is the contribution to the slope parameter from
\Delta E_{\text{kin}}
.
On the other hand, the spin symmetry energy for the IS polarization is obtained from the EOS of
unpolarized and fully polarized symmetric matter.
For fully IS-polarized symmetric matter,
\rho_{PV}
and
\rho_{T}
as well as
\rho_{S}
and
\rho_{V}
are finite
and the others vanish.
Following a similar procedure as in
L
,
the spin slope parameter can thus be approximately obtained as

\displaystyle L_{s}^{\text{(IS)}}\simeq L_{s,\text{kin}}^{\text{(IS)}}+\frac{3}{2}(-\alpha_{PV}+2\alpha_{T})\rho_{0}.

(41)

Similarly, the spin slope parameter for the IV polarization is obtained as

\displaystyle L_{s}^{\text{(IV)}}\simeq L_{s,\text{kin}}^{\text{(IV)}}+\frac{3}{2}(-\alpha_{tPV}+2\alpha_{tT})\rho_{0},

(42)

in which
we have used
the fact that only
\rho_{tPV}
and
\rho_{tT}
as well as
\rho_{S}
and
\rho_{V}
have non-zero values
for fully IV-polarized matter.

SUMMARY

We have calculated the EOS of spin-polarized matter in the relativistic point-coupling model.
In this study, we have considered two types of spin polarization: one is the IS polarization and the other is the IV polarization.
For both of these,
we have calculated the spin slope parameter in a similar way to the slope parameter
for spin-unpolarized matter.
To propose a probe to constrain the EOS of spin-polarized matter,
we have investigated the correlation between the slope parameter and the spin slope parameter.
We have found a negative linear correlation between the slope parameter and the spin slope parameter in the case of the IS polarization,
whereas for the IV polarization, the spin slope parameter was found to be nearly independent of the slope parameter and remain
almost a constant.
We have shown that these behaviors of the spin slope parameters can be understood in terms of
the relativistic structure of the model which we employed in this work.

Our finding suggests that the spin slope parameter for the IS polarization can be constrained by measuring the neutron skin
thickness of finite nuclei. Using
the experimental value of the neutron
skin thickness of
\hphantom{{}^{\text{208}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{208}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{208}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{208}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{208}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Pb}
,
we have extracted
the spin slope parameter for the IS polarization of
85 MeV
\lesssim L_{s}\lesssim
135 MeV and
113 MeV
\lesssim L_{s}\lesssim
172 MeV from the
PREX
Adhikari et al. 2021
and the proton elastic scattering
Zenihiro et al. 2010
measurements, respectively.

In this study, we have considered only symmetric nuclear matter.
It would be straightforward to extend the present work to
the EOS for spin polarized matter with an arbitrary isospin asymmetry
\delta
.
In addition, although we have investigated in this paper the correlation between the slope parameter and the spin slope parameter,
it would be more desirable to propose an observable for finite nuclei which is directly correlated with the spin
slope parameter. Moreover,
it is also important to constrain the
W(\rho_{0},\delta)
itself, that is, the spin symmetry energy at the saturation density.
We leave these as interesting future works.

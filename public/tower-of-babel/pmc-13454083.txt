Ferroptosis-related gene EPAS1 suppresses breast cancer progression by inhibiting M2 macrophage polarization
Authors: Zhaoneng Lin, Yongshun Li, Yuzhen Shi, Yaolu Liu, Minli Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the role of the ferroptosis-related gene EPAS1 in breast cancer progression and its impact on macrophage polarization.MethodsBioinformatics and machine learning approaches were employed to identify ferroptosis-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in breast cancer tissues. Functional enrichment analysis was subsequently performed to explore the biological roles of these DEGs. The expression of EPAS1 was further analyzed in relation to macrophage polarization, with a particular focus on M1 and M2 macrophage infiltration. In addition, ROC curve analysis was conducted to evaluate the prognostic value of EPAS1 expression in predicting patient survival.ResultsA total of ferroptosis-related DEGs were identified by integrating GEO and TCGA datasets, among which EPAS1 and ACO1 were selected as key genes using machine learning algorithms. Functional enrichment analysis indicated that these genes were mainly involved in hypoxia response and cancer-related pathways. Survival analysis showed that high EPAS1 expression was significantly associated with improved overall survival, whereas ACO1 exhibited no prognostic significance. ROC curve analysis demonstrated that EPAS1 had moderate prognostic value, with an AUC of 0.837. Risk score analysis further revealed that patients in the high-risk group had poorer survival outcomes and lower EPAS1 expression levels compared to the low-risk group. Moreover, immune infiltration analysis indicated that EPAS1 expression was positively correlated with M1 macrophage infiltration and negatively correlated with M2 macrophage infiltration.ConclusionEPAS1 functions as a key ferroptosis-related gene that regulates macrophage polarization in breast cancer by promoting M1 macrophage infiltration while suppressing M2 polarization. Targeting EPAS1 may represent a promising therapeutic strategy to enhance anti-tumor immunity and improve clinical outcomes in breast cancer patients.Keywords: Ferroptosis, EPAS1, Breast cancer, Macrophage, Machine learning

Introduction

Breast cancer is one of the most prevalent malignancies worldwide and represents a major public health burden [1, 2]. Despite significant advances in screening and treatment, the complex biological heterogeneity of breast cancer continues to challenge accurate prognostic prediction and the development of effective therapeutic strategies [3, 4]. The tumor microenvironment (TME), particularly the interactions between cancer cells and immune cells, plays a critical role in tumor progression and response to therapy [5–7].

Recent studies have identified ferroptosis, a regulated form of cell death characterized by lipid peroxide accumulation, as a critical process in cancer biology [8]. Unlike classical apoptosis, ferroptosis is driven by iron-dependent oxidative stress and has been shown to influence tumor growth, metastasis, and therapeutic response [8, 9]. Emerging evidence further indicates that ferroptosis can reshape the tumor immune landscape, thereby modulating the behavior and function of immune cells, including macrophages [10, 11]. Recent studies have further revealed a reciprocal relationship, where inducing ferroptosis promotes M1 polarization [12, 13], whereas ferroptosis resistance drives M2 polarization [14, 15].

Macrophages are key components of the immune system and exhibit remarkable functional plasticity, primarily classified into M1 (pro-inflammatory) and M2 (anti-inflammatory) phenotypes [16, 17]. M1 macrophages contribute to anti-tumor immunity and can enhance the efficacy of cancer therapies, whereas M2 macrophages are generally associated with tumor progression, angiogenesis, and immune evasion [18, 19]. The dynamic balance between these phenotypes is a critical determinant of cancer outcomes [20, 21].

EPAS1 (endothelial PAS domain protein 1), a central regulator of hypoxia signaling, has been implicated in multiple aspects of cancer biology, including cell proliferation, survival, and angiogenesis [22–24]. Emerging evidence suggests that EPAS1 may also influence macrophage polarization, potentially shifting the tumor microenvironment toward a more immunologically favorable state [25]. However, the specific role of EPAS1 in regulating M1/M2 macrophage dynamics in breast cancer remains largely unclear.

Recent pan-cancer transcriptomic analyses have successfully identified novel prognostic and immune-related biomarkers for breast cancer using integrative bioinformatics approaches. For instance, Wang et al. identified PPME1 as an independent risk factor across multiple cancer types [26], while Gou et al. demonstrated that BEND3 overexpression correlates with poor prognosis and immune infiltration [27]. Conversely, Wang et al. reported that CLIC6 is downregulated in most cancers and may serve a protective role in breast cancer [28]. Similarly, Elihamu et al. found that CORO1A, despite being overexpressed in breast cancer, is associated with favorable prognostic outcomes, suggesting its potential protective function through immune modulation [29]. These studies collectively highlight the value of pan-cancer bioinformatics approaches in identifying clinically relevant biomarkers, yet the role of ferroptosis-related genes such as EPAS1 in this context remains underexplored.

This study aims to investigate the role of EPAS1 as a ferroptosis-related gene in breast cancer progression, with a particular focus on its impact on macrophage polarization. Using integrated bioinformatics and machine learning approaches, we sought to identify ferroptosis-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in breast cancer tissues and to elucidate the association between EPAS1 expression, patient prognosis, and immune cell interactions. Our findings may provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying breast cancer progression and highlight EPAS1 as a potential therapeutic target.

Materias and methods

Data download and preprocessing

The study cohort consisted of transcriptomic data obtained from the GEO database (accession number GSE42568) and breast cancer transcriptomic data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) for differential expression analysis. These datasets included breast tissue samples from both healthy controls and breast cancer patients.

The GSE42568 dataset comprised 121 samples, including 104 breast cancer tumor samples and 17 normal breast tissue samples. The TCGA dataset included 1,081 breast cancer tumor samples and 99 normal tissue samples.To ensure cross-dataset consistency between the GEO (GSE42568) and TCGA (BRCA) transcriptomic data, a multi-step normalization procedure was applied. For the TCGA dataset, the original HTSeq-FPKM (Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads) data were first converted into TPM (Transcripts Per Million) to minimize the influence of gene length and sequencing depth differences. For the GEO dataset, the raw expression matrix was log2-transformed after a baseline adjustment. Subsequently, to eliminate batch effects and enable integrated analysis across the two platforms, the ComBat algorithm from the sva R package was employed. Both datasets were then subjected to Z-score standardization of the expression levels for each gene across all samples to achieve a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1. This combined approach (TPM conversion + ComBat + Z-score) was selected to balance the detection biases of microarray (GEO) and RNA-seq (TCGA) technologies while preserving biological variance. The transcriptomic data were processed as follows: based on gene annotation information from the TCGA database, Ensembl IDs in the datasets were converted into gene symbols. Protein-coding genes were then extracted to construct a new expression matrix. Subsequently, the expression data were normalized and standardized for downstream analysis. A total of 259 ferroptosis-related genes were obtained from the FerrDB database (http://www.zhounan.org/ferrdb/).

Identification of differentially expressed genes associated with ferroptosis

Differential expression analysis of transcriptome data was conducted using the R package “limma.” A fold change threshold of 1.5 and P < 0.05 were applied to identify DEGs between tumor tissues and adjacent normal tissues. False discovery rate (FDR) adjustment was performed to control for multiple comparisons. The threshold of FC > 1.5 was selected because it achieves a reasonable balance between detecting true biological differences and controlling false positive rates in cancer transcriptome studies, and is widely adopted in most breast cancer bioinformatics analyses. To explore the relationship between DEGs and ferroptosis, the identified DEGs were compared with ferroptosis-related genes obtained from the FerrDB database (http://www.zhounan.org/ferrdb). By overlapping these two gene sets, a list of ferroptosis-related DEGs was generated, which may play critical roles in breast cancer.

Functional enrichment analysis

To further investigate the biological functions and pathways associated with ferroptosis, ferroptosis-related DEGs were analyzed using the DAVID database (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/). Gene Ontology (GO) annotation and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses were performed to identify significantly enriched GO terms and KEGG pathways (P < 0.05). The results of the enrichment analyses were visualized using R software. These findings provide insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying ferroptosis in breast cancer.

Identification of key ferroptosis-related genes associated with breast cancer by machine learning

Three complementary machine learning algorithms were employed to identify key ferroptosis-related genes associated with breast cancer occurrence, progression, and prognosis: maximal clique centrality (MCC) within a protein-protein interaction network, random forest, and LASSO regression.

First, a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of the 13 ferroptosis-related DEGs was constructed using the STRING database (http://string-db.org). The MCC algorithm, implemented via the CytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape software, was used to calculate the connectivity of each node in the network. The top 7 genes with the highest MCC scores were selected as candidates.

Second, random forest analysis was performed using the R package “randomForest” with the number of trees (ntree) set to 500 and the number of candidate variables at each split (mtry) set to the square root of the total number of features. Genes were ranked by their importance scores, and the top 7 genes were retained.

Third, LASSO regression was conducted using the R package “glmnet” to identify ferroptosis-related genes significantly associated with patient prognosis. Using clinical data (survival status and survival time) and expression data of ferroptosis-related genes, LASSO applies L1 regularization to shrink irrelevant coefficients to zero, thereby selecting a subset of genes with non-zero coefficients.

Integration of machine learning results: The gene sets selected by each of the three algorithms were intersected using a Venn diagram. Only genes that were simultaneously identified by all three methods (MCC, random forest, and LASSO) were defined as key ferroptosis-related genes for further analysis.

Results

Screening of differentially expressed genes associated with ferroptosis

A total of 1,389 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified from the GEO dataset between tumor (BC group) and adjacent normal tissues (NC group), including 801 upregulated genes and 588 downregulated genes (Fig. 1A). Additionally, a total of 1103 DEGs from TCGA were identified between tumor and adjacent tissues (Fig. 1B). To further explore the relationship between DEGs and ferroptosis, the identified DEGs were intersected with ferroptosis-related genes obtained from the FerrDB database. This analysis identified 23 ferroptosis-related DEGs in the GEO dataset (Fig. 1C) and 15 in the TCGA dataset (Fig. 1D). The overlap between these two gene sets yielded 10 common ferroptosis-related DEGs that may play important roles in breast cancer (Fig. 1E). The expression patterns of these genes are presented in a heatmap (Fig. 1F).

GO enrichment analysis indicated that ferroptosis-related DEGs were mainly involved in transcriptional regulation, hypoxia response, and protein–protein interactions (Fig. 2A). KEGG pathway analysis further revealed that these genes were significantly enriched in several cancer-related pathways, including metabolism in cancer, the HIF-1 signaling pathway, and the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway (Fig. 2B).

Identification of key ferroptosis-related genes in breast cancer

A PPI network was constructed based on the 13 ferroptosis-related DEGs, revealing potential interactions among these genes (Fig. 3A). Using the MCC algorithm, the top 7 genes with the highest connectivity were identified based on network topological analysis (Fig. 3B). LASSO regression analysis further identified 7 ferroptosis-related genes significantly associated with breast cancer prognosis (Fig. 3C). In addition, random forest analysis selected 7 key genes with strong discriminative ability between breast cancer and adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 3D). By integrating the results of these three machine learning approaches, two common genes, ACO1 and EPAS1, were ultimately identified as key ferroptosis-related genes (Fig. 3E).

Upregulation of EPAS1 is associated with a favorable prognosis in breast cancer

All tumor samples were divided into EPAS1 high-expression and low-expression groups. The results indicated that the OS rate of the EPAS1 high-expression group was significantly higher than that of the low-expression group (Fig. 4A). However, the results of the K-M curve analysis showed that ACO1 expression was not significantly associated with the prognosis of breast cancer patients (Fig. 4B). In addition, the ROC curve analysis results showed that EPAS1 has a high diagnostic value for breast cancer patients, with an AUC of 0.837 (Fig. 4C). To further evaluate the prognostic performance of EPAS1, a risk score model was constructed. The risk score distribution with a cutoff value of 0.28 stratified patients into high-risk and low-risk groups (Fig. 4D, upper panel). Analysis of survival status revealed that most deceased patients were concentrated in the high-risk group, while the majority of surviving patients belonged to the low-risk group (Fig. 4D, middle panel). Examination of EPAS1 expression levels showed that low-risk patients exhibited higher EPAS1 expression, whereas high-risk patients showed lower EPAS1 expression (Fig. 4D, lower panel). These findings collectively demonstrate that upregulation of EPAS1 is associated with lower risk scores and improved survival outcomes in breast cancer patients.

EPAS1 inhibits M2 macrophage polarization in breast cancer

In EPAS1 high-expression breast cancer samples, M2 macrophages exhibited lower infiltration compared to EPAS1 low-expression breast cancer samples (Fig. 5A). Further quantitative analysis using CIBERSORT demonstrated that EPAS1 expression was positively correlated with M1 macrophage infiltration (Spearman r = 0.08, P = 0.00583; Fig. 5B) and negatively correlated with M2 macrophage infiltration (Spearman r = -0.09, P = 0.00143; Fig. 5C). This suggests that EPAS1 may inhibit tumor progression by suppressing M2 macrophage polarization.

Discussion

This study identified EPAS1 as a key ferroptosis-related gene in breast cancer, demonstrating its association with macrophage polarization and its potential role in tumor progression. Our results showed that high EPAS1 expression was positively correlated with M1 macrophage infiltration and negatively correlated with M2 macrophage infiltration. These findings are consistent with previous studies highlighting the critical role of macrophage polarization in shaping the TME and influencing clinical outcomes.

Macrophages play a complex and context-dependent role in cancer. M1 macrophages are generally associated with anti-tumor immunity through the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, enhanced antigen presentation, and increased cytotoxic activity against tumor cells [20, 30, 31]. In contrast, M2 macrophages are commonly linked to tumor progression, tissue remodeling, and immunosuppression [32]. In this study, the upregulation of EPAS1 and its positive association with M1 macrophage infiltration suggest that EPAS1 may promote a pro-inflammatory tumor microenvironment that is unfavorable for tumor growth. Notably, macrophage polarization is strongly influenced by hypoxic and metabolic cues, both of which are regulated by EPAS1 [33, 34]. These findings support a potential role for EPAS1 as a key regulator of macrophage dynamics in breast cancer.

Moreover, the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway, a key regulator of innate immunity and inflammation, may also contribute to macrophage polarization [35]. NOD receptors, particularly NOD1 and NOD2, have been shown to activate pro-inflammatory signaling pathways and influence macrophage phenotypic differentiation. Previous studies indicate that NOD signaling promotes M1 polarization while suppressing M2 polarization [36], suggesting a potential interaction between EPAS1 and the NOD pathway in regulating macrophage function. In this context, the interplay between EPAS1 and NOD signaling may represent an important regulatory axis through which breast cancer cells shape the tumor immune microenvironment, favoring M1 over M2 macrophage infiltration. Further studies are needed to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the interaction between EPAS1 and NOD receptors in breast cancer.

Additionally, our findings indicate that elevated EPAS1 expression is associated with favorable prognostic outcomes in breast cancer patients. This observation is particularly relevant in the context of the tumor microenvironment, which plays a critical role in determining cancer prognosis. Previous studies have demonstrated that increased infiltration of M1 macrophages is correlated with improved survival across multiple cancer types, including breast cancer. Consistent with these findings, our results suggest that EPAS1-mediated enhancement of M1 polarization may contribute to improved patient outcomes and could represent a potential therapeutic strategy. Our finding that EPAS1 expression correlates with favorable prognosis and reduced M2 macrophage infiltration is consistent with recent pan-cancer biomarker studies that have identified both oncogenic and tumor-suppressive genes through similar analytical frameworks [26–29]. Notably, while PPME1 and BEND3 function as oncogenic drivers with high expression linked to poor outcomes, CLIC6, CORO1A, and our identified EPAS1 exhibit distinct patterns. CLIC6 is downregulated in breast cancer and may serve a protective role [28]; CORO1A, despite being overexpressed, is associated with better prognosis [29]; and EPAS1 upregulation correlates with reduced M2 macrophage infiltration and improved survival. These contrasting observations suggest that the relationship between gene expression, immune modulation, and prognosis in breast cancer is context-dependent and warrants further mechanistic investigation.

Notably, while our findings position EPAS1 as a tumor-suppressive gene in breast cancer, contradictory reports exist in the literature. Several studies have demonstrated oncogenic roles of EPAS1 in certain contexts. For instance, EPAS1 has been identified as a key driver of tamoxifen resistance in luminal A breast cancer, where its upregulation is associated with poor survival outcomes and an altered tumor microenvironment [37]. Metabolic profiling further revealed that EPAS1 promotes fatty acid uptake via CD36 and enhances glutamine metabolism through SLC1A5, contributing to redox balance and cell survival under tamoxifen stress [38]. Conversely, EPAS1 has been shown to play an unanticipated tumor-suppressive role in papillary thyroid carcinoma, where higher EPAS1 expression is associated with better progression-free survival [39]. In breast cancer, estrogen-dependent downregulation of EPAS1 has been reported, and high nuclear HIF-2α levels correlate with better overall survival in certain breast cancer patient cohorts [40]. These context-dependent discrepancies may be explained by differences in cancer type, molecular subtype, hormone receptor status, tumor microenvironment, and the specific downstream pathways activated. In ER-positive breast cancer, for example, estrogen signaling has been shown to downregulate EPAS1 expression [40], which may partially explain the favorable prognosis associated with EPAS1 upregulation observed in our study. Further investigation is warranted to delineate the precise molecular contexts that determine whether EPAS1 functions as an oncogene or tumor suppressor.

In addition to its role in macrophage polarization, EPAS1 has been implicated in several key pathways associated with cancer progression. Notably, the HIF signaling pathway plays a central role in cellular adaptation to hypoxia and is closely linked to tumor growth and metastasis [41]. As a key hypoxia-responsive factor, EPAS1 can interact with hypoxia-related signaling networks to drive adaptive responses under low oxygen conditions, thereby supporting tumor cell survival and progression [42]. Our findings suggest that EPAS1 may function as a molecular link connecting ferroptosis, hypoxia, and immune regulation, highlighting its potential as a multifaceted therapeutic target in breast cancer.

Despite these promising findings, our study has several limitations that should be addressed in future research. First, although the sample size was relatively large, it may not fully capture the heterogeneity of breast cancer subtypes and their corresponding TMEs. Second, due to limitations in data availability, we were unable to perform stratified analyses of EPAS1 expression and function across different molecular subtypes (such as Luminal, HER2-positive, and triple-negative breast cancer) or clinical stages. Given the well-recognized heterogeneity of breast cancer, the tumor immune microenvironment varies significantly among subtypes and stages, and the regulatory role of EPAS1 in macrophage polarization may be influenced by these factors. Third, the reliance on bioinformatics and machine learning approaches necessitates further validation through in vitro and in vivo experiments to confirm the functional roles of EPAS1 in macrophage polarization and breast cancer progression. Finally, the causal relationship between EPAS1, ferroptosis, and M2 macrophage polarization cannot be established based on transcriptomic data alone. Future studies should incorporate larger, multi-center clinical cohorts and include EPAS1 gain- and loss-of-function experiments, ferroptosis-related assays (e.g., lipid ROS and GPX4), and macrophage co-culture systems to validate the proposed mechanisms and further elucidate the subtype- and stage-specific roles of EPAS1.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study identifies EPAS1 as a ferroptosis-related gene that plays an important role in regulating macrophage polarization and breast cancer progression. By promoting M1 macrophage infiltration and suppressing M2 polarization, EPAS1 may contribute to the establishment of a more favorable tumor microenvironment. These findings enhance our understanding of the interplay between ferroptosis, immune regulation, and breast cancer, and suggest that EPAS1 may serve as a potential therapeutic target for improving clinical outcomes.

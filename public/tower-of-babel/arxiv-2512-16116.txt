Rota-Baxter operators on braces, post-braces and the Yang-Baxter equation
Li Guo, Yan Jiang, Yunhe Sheng, You Wang

Abstract
. Combining the notions of braces and relative Rota-Baxter operators on groups in connection with the Yang-Baxter equation and a factorization theorem of Lie groups from integrable systems, relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces and post-braces are introduced. A relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace naturally induces a post-brace, and conversely, every post-brace determines a relative Rota-Baxter operator on its sub-adjacent brace. Furthermore, a post-brace yields two Drinfel’d-isomorphic solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation. As a special case, enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operators give rise to matched pairs of braces. Focusing on enhanced Rota-Baxter operators on two-sided braces, a corresponding factorization theorem is established. Examples are provided from the two-sided brace associated with the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces are introduced to connect to post-braces and matched pairs of braces, to construct solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation, and to give a factorization theorem for two-sided braces.

1.1.
Braces and set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation

The Yang-Baxter equation first appeared in theoretical physics and statistical mechanics in the works of C. N. Yang
[
40
]
. Independently, G. Baxter employed the Yang-Baxter equation in his solution to the eight-vertex model
[
8
]
. Since then, the Yang-Baxter equation has been extensively studied, leading to broad achievements in various areas of mathematics and mathematical physics.
In
[
17
]
, Drinfel’d proposed to study set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation on a set
X
, which is a bijective map

R:X\times X\to X\times X
satisfying the following equation:

(R\times{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\times R)(R\times{\rm{Id}})=({\rm{Id}}\times R)(R\times{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\times R).

In the seminal work
[
18
]
,
Etingof-Schedler-Soloviev introduced the structure group
G(X,R)
to classify set-theoretical solutions.
Later on, Lu-Yan-Zhu
[
28
]
showed that braided groups give rise to set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. In
[
15
]
, Doikou-Rybolowicz-Stefanelli introduced the notion of Drinfel’d homomorphisms between set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation and showed that
Drinfel’d isomorphic classes of left non-degenerate solutions correspond to isomorphic classes of left
shelves endowed with special endomorphisms.

As a generalization of radical rings, braces were introduced by Rump
[
30
,
31
]
to produce non-degenerate involutive solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. It was proved that braces are equivalent to bijective
1
-cocycles in
[
12
]
. See
[
11
,
13
,
19
,
29
,
33
]
for recent achievements in the study of braces and their related set-theoretical solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation.

Afterwards, skew braces were introduced by Guarnieri and Vendramin
[
22
]
as a nonabelian generalization of braces. Then the factorization problem for skew-braces was systematically studied in
[
25
]
. It is remarkable that skew braces are connected with other algebraic structures in numerous areas, such as matched pairs of groups, groups with exact factorizations, rings and near-rings, regular subgroups, bijective
1
-cocycles and Hopf-Galois extensions. These connections led to several new types of solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
[
34
]
. Later on, Brzeziński
[
10
]
introduced the notion of skew trusses, as a common generalization of skew left rings and skew braces. More recently, Trappeniers
[
37
]
studied two-sided skew braces and showed that every two-sided skew brace is an extension of a weakly trivial skew brace by a two-sided brace.

1.2.
Rota-Baxter operators on groups and post-groups

The notion of Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras was introduced by G. Baxter

[
7
]
in his probability study to understand Spitzers identity in fluctuation theory, and found applications in Connes-Kreimer’s algebraic approach to the renormalization in quantum field theory
[
16
]
. See
[
23
]
for more details. The notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators (also called
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator) on Lie algebras was introduced in
[
27
]
, which are closely related to the classical Yang-Baxter equation and naturally induce pre-Lie or post-Lie algebras
[
3
,
9
]
. This connection generalizes to the splitting of operads
[
2
]
.

The notion of Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups was introduced in
[
24
]
, whose differentiation give rise to Rota-Baxter operators on the corresponding Lie algebras.
The authors obtained a factorization theorem of Rota-Baxter Lie groups, realizing the Global Factorization Theorem of Semenov-Tian-Shansky on Lie groups
[
32
]
without going through integration.
Consequently, Goncharov defined Rota-Baxter operators on cocommutative Hopf
algebras in
[
20
]
such that many classical results still hold at the Hopf algebra level. In
[
5
,
6
]
, Bardakov and Gubarev discovered the relation between skew braces and Rota-Baxter groups. In
[
4
]
, the notion of post-groups was introduced, as the derived structure of Rota-Baxter operators on groups. Post-groups are equivalent to skew braces, and lead to non-involutive solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. Recently, the notion of Rota-Baxter type operators on trusses was introduced in
[
14
]
from the perspective of Rota-Baxter operators on rings.

The notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on groups was introduced in
[
26
]
, in order to establish a local Lie theory from the perspective of the local integration and differentiation. It was shown that relative Rota-Baxter operators on groups give rise to matched pairs of groups, a notion critical to the Yang-Baxter equation
[
18
,
28
]
.
See
[
36
]
for further details.

1.3.
Main results and outline of the paper

In this paper, we introduce the notion of
relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces
, generalizing the Rota-Baxter theory from groups to the significantly richer setting of braces that comprise of two compatible group laws. The added complexity of braces necessitates new ideas and constructions to obtain a coherent theory with expected applications to the Yang-Baxter equation.

We develop the new framework in three steps.
First, we introduce the notions of relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces and of post-braces, generalizing the classical correspondence between relative Rota-Baxter operators on groups and post-groups. An interesting phenomenon is that a post-brace produces a pair of Drinfel’d-isomorphic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.
Second, we impose an
enhanced
condition on relative Rota-Baxter operators, and show that it provides the needed restriction to obtain matched pairs of braces that extend matched pairs of groups.
Finally, we apply enhanced Rota-Baxter operators to two-sided braces and establish a new factorization theorem for braces in the sense of
[
25
]
, generalizing the classical factorization theorems for Lie groups from integrable systems
[
24
,
32
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notions of relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces and post-braces, and study their relations.
Moreover, we prove that a post-brace gives rise to two set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation that are Drinfel’d isomorphic (Theorem
2.26
).
In Section
3
, we define enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces and matched pairs of braces, and show that an enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace induces a matched pair of braces (Theorem
3.14
).
In Section
4
, we utilize the notion of (enhanced) Rota-Baxter operators on two-sided braces to obtain a factorization theorem of two-sided braces (Theorem
4.9
).
As an illustration of the general results, in Section
5
, we provide various examples based on the brace induced by the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra.

2.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces and post-braces

In this section, we first recall the semi-trivial action of braces. Then we define the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces by using the semi-trivial action. As the underlying structures of relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces, the notion of post-braces are introduced, which can give rise to set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.

2.1.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces

Let us first recall the notion of semi-trivial actions of braces. Then we introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces, which naturally split the brace structures into post-brace structures.

Definition 2.1
.

(i)

A
skew brace

(G,\cdot,\circ)
consists of a group
(G,\cdot)
and a group
(G,\circ)
such that

(1)

a\circ(b\cdot c)=(a\circ b)\cdot a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ c),\quad\forall a,b,c\in G.

Here
a^{-1}
is the inverse of
a
in the group
(G,\cdot)
.

(ii)

A
brace
is a skew brace in which the group
(G,\cdot)
is abelian. In addition, a brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is called
two-sided
if the following condition also holds

(2)

(a\cdot b)\circ c=(a\circ c)\cdot c^{-1}\cdot(b\circ c),\quad\forall a,b,c\in G.

(iii)

Let
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
be braces. A
homomorphism
of braces from
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
to
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
is a map
\Psi:G\to H
such that

(3)

\Psi(a\cdot_{G}b)=\Psi(a)\cdot_{H}\Psi(b),\quad\Psi(a\circ_{G}b)=\Psi(a)\circ_{H}\Psi(b),\quad\forall a,b\in G.

We denote by
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
the set of automorphisms of the brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(
[
39
]
)

If
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a brace, then we have

(4)

a\circ b^{-1}=a\cdot(a\circ b)^{-1}\cdot a,\quad\forall a,b\in G.

Moreover, if
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a two-sided brace, then we also have

(5)

a^{-1}\circ b=b\cdot(a\circ b)^{-1}\cdot b,\quad\forall a,b\in G.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
34
,
38
]
)
Let
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
be braces. Then a map
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
is called a
semi-trivial action
of the brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
on the brace
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
, if
\Phi(a\circ_{G}b)=\Phi(a)\Phi(b)
for all
a,b\in G
.

Remark 2.4
.

In this case,
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
is an action of the group
(G,\circ_{G})
on the group
(H,\circ_{H})
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
be a semi-trivial action of the brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
on the brace
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
. Then
(H\times G,\cdot_{\rtimes},\circ_{\rtimes})
is a brace, where
\cdot_{\rtimes}
and
\circ_{\rtimes}
are defined by

(6)

\displaystyle(h,a)\cdot_{\rtimes}(k,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h\cdot_{H}k,a\cdot_{G}b);

(7)

\displaystyle(h,a)\circ_{\rtimes}(k,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h\circ_{H}\Phi(a)k,a\circ_{G}b),\quad\forall a,b\in G,h,k\in H.

(H\times G,\cdot_{\rtimes},\circ_{\rtimes})
is called the
semi-direct product

of braces
G
and
H
, and denoted by
H\rtimes_{\Phi}G
.

Proof.

(H\times G,\cdot_{\rtimes})
is actually the direct product of the abelian groups
(G,\cdot_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H})
. Since
\Phi(a\circ_{G}b)=\Phi(a)\Phi(b)
and
\Phi(a)(h\circ_{H}k)=(\Phi(a)h)\circ_{H}(\Phi(a)k)
,
(H\times G,\circ_{\rtimes})
is a group.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle(h,a)\circ_{\rtimes}((k,b)\cdot_{\rtimes}(t,c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h,a)\circ_{\rtimes}(k\cdot_{H}t,b\cdot_{G}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(h\circ_{H}\Phi(a)(k\cdot_{H}t),a\circ_{G}(b\cdot_{G}c)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(h\circ_{H}(\Phi(a)k)\cdot_{H}(\Phi(a)t)),a\circ_{G}(b\cdot_{G}c)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big((h\circ_{H}\Phi(a)k)\cdot_{H}h^{-1}\cdot_{H}(h\circ_{H}\Phi(a)t),(a\circ_{G}b)\cdot_{G}a^{-1}\cdot_{G}(a\circ_{G}c)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h\circ_{H}\Phi(a)k,a\circ_{G}b)\cdot_{\rtimes}(h^{-1},a^{-1})\cdot_{\rtimes}(h\circ_{H}\Phi(a)t,a\circ_{G}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big((h,a)\circ_{\rtimes}(k,b)\big)\cdot_{\rtimes}(h,a)^{\cdot_{\rtimes}-1}\cdot_{\rtimes}\big((h,a)\circ_{\rtimes}(t,c)\big),

which implies that
(H\times G,\cdot_{\rtimes},\circ_{\rtimes})
is a brace.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
be a semi-trivial action of a brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
on a brace
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
. A map
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is called a
relative Rota-Baxter operator
on the brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to the semi-trivial action
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
, if

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(h\cdot_{H}k);

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(h)\circ_{G}\mathcal{B}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))(k)),\quad\forall h,k\in H.

Remark 2.7
.

Equation (
9
) implies that
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the group
(G,\circ_{G})
with respect to the action
\Phi
of
G
on
H
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
be a semi-trivial action of a brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
on a brace
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
. Then
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to
\Phi
if and only if the graph
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}):=\{(h,\mathcal{B}(h))|h\in H\}
is a sub-brace of the semi-direct product
H\rtimes_{\Phi}G
.

Proof.

By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle(h,\mathcal{B}(h))\cdot_{\rtimes}(k,\mathcal{B}(k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(h\cdot_{H}k,\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(k)\Big);

\displaystyle(h,\mathcal{B}(h))\circ_{\rtimes}(k,\mathcal{B}(k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))(k),\mathcal{B}(h)\circ_{G}\mathcal{B}(k)\Big),

for all
h,k\in H
. Then by (
8
) and (
9
), it is obvious that
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to
\Phi
if and only if the graph
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B})
is a sub-brace of the semi-direct product
H\rtimes_{\Phi}G
.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to a semi-trivial action
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
. Then

(H,\cdot_{\mathcal{B}},\circ_{\mathcal{B}})
is a brace, called the
descendent brace
, where
\cdot_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\circ_{\mathcal{B}}
are defined by

(10)

h\cdot_{\mathcal{B}}k=h\cdot_{H}k,\quad h\circ_{\mathcal{B}}k=h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))(k),\quad\forall h,k\in H.

Moreover,
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is a homomorphism of braces from
(H,\cdot_{\mathcal{B}},\circ_{\mathcal{B}})
to
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the group
(G,\circ_{G})
with respect to the action
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
, by
[
26
, Proposition 3.5]
, we deduce that
(H,\circ_{\mathcal{B}})
is a group with the unit
e_{H}
and
\Phi(\overline{\mathcal{B}(h)})(\bar{h})
is the inverse of
h
in
(H,\circ_{\mathcal{B}})
, where
\overline{\mathcal{B}(h)}
and
\bar{h}
are inverse elements of
\mathcal{B}(h)
in the group
(G,\circ_{G})
and
h
in the group
(H,\circ_{H})
respectively. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle h\circ_{\mathcal{B}}(k\cdot_{\mathcal{B}}t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))(k\cdot_{\mathcal{B}}t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\circ_{H}\big((\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))k)\cdot_{H}(\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(h\circ_{H}(\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))k)\big)\cdot_{H}h^{-1}\cdot_{H}\big(h\circ_{H}(\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h\circ_{\mathcal{B}}k)\cdot_{\mathcal{B}}h^{-1}\cdot_{\mathcal{B}}(h\circ_{\mathcal{B}}t),

which implies that
(H,\cdot_{\mathcal{B}},\circ_{\mathcal{B}})
is a brace. It is obvious that
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is a homomorphism of braces from
(H,\cdot_{\mathcal{B}},\circ_{\mathcal{B}})
to
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
in this case.
∎

2.2.
Post-braces

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of post-braces and show that a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace splits the brace to a post-brace. First we recall the notion of post-groups and some basic results.

Definition 2.10
.

(
[
4
]
)
A
post-group
is a group
(G,\circ)
equipped with a multiplication
\rhd:G\times G\to G
such that

(i)

for each
a\in G
, the left multiplication

L^{\rhd}_{a}:G\to G,\quad L^{\rhd}_{a}b=a\rhd b,\quad\forall b\in G,

is an automorphism of the group
(G,\circ)
, that means,

(11)

a\rhd(b\circ c)=(a\rhd b)\circ(a\rhd c),\quad\forall a,b,c\in G;

(ii)

the following “weighted” associativity holds,

(12)

\big(a\circ(a\rhd b)\big)\rhd c=a\rhd(b\rhd c),\quad\forall a,b,c\in G.

Definition 2.11
.

(
[
4
]
)
A
homomorphism
of post-groups from
(G,\circ_{G},\rhd_{G})
to
(H,\circ_{H},\rhd_{H})
is a map
\Psi:G\to H
that preserves the operations
\circ
and
\rhd
:

\Psi(a\circ_{G}b)=\Psi(a)\circ_{H}\Psi(b),\quad\Psi(a\rhd_{G}b)=\Psi(a)\rhd_{H}\Psi(b),\quad\forall a,b\in G.

Remark 2.12
.

In
[
4
, Theorem 3.25]
, the authors proved that the category of post-groups (resp. pre-groups) is isomorphic to the category of skew braces (resp. braces).

Lemma 2.13
.

(
[
4
]
)

Let
e
be the unit of the post-group
(G,\circ,\rhd)
. Then for all
a\in G
, we have

(13)

\displaystyle a\rhd e=e;

(14)

\displaystyle e\rhd a=a.

Theorem 2.14
.

(
[
4
]
)

Let
(G,\circ,\rhd)
be a post-group. Define
\ast:G\times G\to G
by

(15)

\displaystyle a\ast b:=a\circ(a\rhd b),\quad\forall a,b\in G.

(i)

Then
(G,\ast)
is a group with
e
being the unit, and the inverse map
\dagger:G\to G
given by

(16)

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}:=(L^{\rhd}_{a})^{-1}(\bar{a}),\quad\forall a\in G,

where
\bar{a}
is the inverse of
a
with respect to the group
(G,\circ)
. The group
(G,\ast)
is called the
sub-adjacent group
of the post-group
(G,\circ,\rhd)
.

(ii)

The left multiplication
L^{\rhd}:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(G)
is an action of the group
(G,\ast)
on the group
(G,\circ)
.

(iii)

Let
\Psi:(G,\circ_{G},\rhd_{G})\to(H,\circ_{H},\rhd_{H})
be a homomorphism of post-groups. Then
\Psi
is a homomorphism of the sub-adjacent groups from
(G,\ast_{G})
to
(H,\ast_{H})
.

Definition 2.15
.

A
post-brace
is a quadruple
(G,\cdot,\circ,\rhd)
such that

(i)

(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a brace,

(ii)

(G,\circ,\rhd)
is a post-group,

(iii)

\rhd
and
\cdot
satisfy the following compatibility condition:

(17)

a\rhd(b\cdot c)=(a\rhd b)\cdot(a\rhd c),\quad\forall a,b,c\in G.

A
homomorphism of post-braces
from
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G},\rhd_{G})
to
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H},\rhd_{H})
is a map
\Psi:G\to H
that preserves the operations
\cdot,\circ
and
\rhd
:

(18)

\Psi(a\cdot_{G}b)=\Psi(a)\cdot_{H}\Psi(b),\quad\Psi(a\circ_{G}b)=\Psi(a)\circ_{H}\Psi(b),\quad\Psi(a\rhd_{G}b)=\Psi(a)\rhd_{H}\Psi(b),\ \forall a,b\in G.

In addition to the brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
, a post-brace naturally gives rise to another brace on the underlying set.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ,\rhd)
be a post-brace. Define a binary operation
\ast:G\times G\to G
by

(19)

\displaystyle a\ast b

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle a\circ(a\rhd b),\quad\forall a,b\in G.

(i)

Then
(G,\cdot,\ast)
is a brace with
e
being the unit, called the
sub-adjacent brace
of the post-brace
(G,\cdot,\circ,\rhd)
and denoted by
G_{\rhd}
.

(ii)

The left multiplication

L^{\rhd}_{a}:G\to G,\quad L^{\rhd}_{a}b=a\rhd b,\quad\forall a,b\in G,

is a semi-trivial action of the brace
(G,\cdot,\ast)
on the brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
.

(iii)

Let
\Psi:G\to H
be a homomorphism of post-braces from
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G},\rhd_{G})
to
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H},\rhd_{H})
. Then
\Psi
is a homomorphism of the sub-adjacent braces from
(G,\cdot_{G},\ast_{G})
to
(H,\cdot_{H},\ast_{H})
.

Proof.

(i)

(G,\ast)
is a group according to Theorem
2.14
(i). By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle a\ast(b\cdot c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ(a\rhd(b\cdot c))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq-post-brace}}{=}

\displaystyle a\circ((a\rhd b)\cdot(a\rhd c))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq-brace}}{=}

\displaystyle(a\circ(a\rhd b))\cdot a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ(a\rhd c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\ast b)\cdot a^{-1}\cdot(a\ast c),

which implies that
(G,\cdot,\ast)
is a skew brace.

(ii)
By Theorem
2.14
(ii), if
(G,\circ,\rhd)
is a post-group, then the left multiplication
L^{\rhd}:(G,\ast)\to\mathrm{Aut}(G,\circ)
is an action of the group
(G,\ast)
on the group
(G,\circ)
. Combined with (
17
), we deduce that

L^{\rhd}_{a}
is in
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
for all
a\in G
. Thus, the left multiplication

L^{\rhd}:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(G)
is a semi-trivial action of the brace
(G,\cdot,\ast)
on the brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
.

(iii)
By Theorem
2.14
(iii), if
\Psi:G\to H
is a homomorphism of post-groups, then
\Psi
is a homomorphism of the groups from
(G,\ast_{G})
to
(H,\ast_{H})
, which implies that
\Psi
is a homomorphism of the sub-adjacent braces from
(G,\cdot_{G},\ast_{G})
to
(H,\cdot_{H},\ast_{H})
.
∎

Remark 2.17
.

For any post-brace
(G,\cdot,\circ,\rhd)
, there exists another skew brace structure
(G,\circ,\ast)
, where
\ast
is given by (
15
). See
[
4
, Proposition 3.22]
.

Now we establish the close relation between relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces and post-braces.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to a semi-trivial action
\Phi:(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})\to\mathrm{Aut}(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
. Define a multiplication
\rhd_{\mathcal{B}}:H\times H\to H
by

(20)

\displaystyle h\rhd_{\mathcal{B}}k=\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))(k),\quad\forall h,k\in H.

Then
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H},\rhd_{\mathcal{B}})
is a post-brace, whose sub-adjacent brace is the descendant brace
(H,\cdot_{\mathcal{B}},\circ_{\mathcal{B}})
given in Proposition
2.9
.

Conversely, let
(G,\cdot,\circ,\rhd)
be a post-brace. Then the identity map
{\rm{Id}}:G\to G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the sub-adjacent brace
(G,\cdot,\ast)
given in Theorem
2.16
with respect to the semi-trivial action
L^{\rhd}
of the brace
(G,\cdot,\ast)
on the brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
.

Proof.

By
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
, since
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the group
(G,\circ_{G})
with respect to the action
\Phi
of
(G,\circ_{G})
on
(H,\circ_{H})
, then
(H,\circ_{H},\rhd_{\mathcal{B}})
is a post-group. By (
20
), for all
h,k,t\in H
, we have

\displaystyle h\rhd_{\mathcal{B}}(k\cdot_{H}t)=\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))(k\cdot_{H}t)=\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))(k)\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))(t)=(h\rhd_{\mathcal{B}}k)\cdot_{H}(h\rhd_{\mathcal{B}}t),

which implies that
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H},\rhd_{\mathcal{B}})
is a post-brace. Moreover, by (
10
), we have

h\circ_{\mathcal{B}}k=h\circ_{H}(\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))k)=h\circ_{H}(h\rhd_{\mathcal{B}}k),\quad\forall h,k\in H.

Thus,
(H,\cdot_{\mathcal{B}},\circ_{\mathcal{B}})
is the sub-adjacent brace of the post-brace
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H},\rhd_{\mathcal{B}})
.

Now let
(G,\cdot,\circ,\rhd)
be a post-brace. By (
19
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm{Id}}(a)\cdot{\rm{Id}}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm{Id}}(a\cdot b);

\displaystyle{\rm{Id}}(a)\ast{\rm{Id}}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm{Id}}(a\circ(L^{\rhd}_{{\rm{Id}}(a)}b)),\quad\forall a,b\in G,

which implies that
{\rm{Id}}:G\to G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the brace
(G,\cdot,\ast)
with respect to the semi-trivial action
L^{\rhd}
on the brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
.
∎

2.3.
Post-braces and the Yang-Baxter equation

In this subsection, we prove that a post-brace provides two solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation that are Drinfel’d isomorphic. First we recall the notion of set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
X
be a set. A set-theoretical solution of the
Yang-Baxter equation
on

X
is a bijective map
R:X\times X\to X\times X
satisfying:

(21)

\displaystyle R_{12}R_{23}R_{12}=R_{23}R_{12}R_{23},\,\,\,\,\text{where}\,\,\,\,R_{12}=R\times{\rm{Id}}_{X},~R_{23}={\rm{Id}}_{X}\times R.

A set
X
with a set-theoretical solution of
the Yang-Baxter equation on
X
is called a
braided set
and
is denoted by
(X,R)
.

Moreover, we denote by
R(a,b)=(\varphi_{a}(b),\psi_{b}(a))
for all
a,b\in X
.

R
is called
non-degenerate
if for all
a,b\in X
, the maps
\varphi_{a}
and
\psi_{b}
are bijective.
R
is called
involutive
if
R^{2}={\rm{Id}}_{X\times X}
.

Definition 2.20
.

Let
(X,R)
and
(Y,R^{\prime})
be two braided sets. A
homomorphism
of braided sets from
(X,R)
to
(Y,R^{\prime})
is a map
f:X\to Y
such that
(f\times f)R=R^{\prime}(f\times f)
.

If
f
is bijective, then
f
is called an equivalence of braided sets from
(X,R)
to
(Y,R^{\prime})
.

Let us recall the following theorem, which implies that (skew) braces give rise to set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. See
[
18
,
22
,
35
,
39
]
for more information.

Theorem 2.21
.

(
[
31
]
)

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a brace. Then for
a,b\in G
, the map

(22)

\displaystyle R_{G}:G\times G\to G\times G,\quad R_{G}(a,b)=(a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b),\overline{a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b)}\circ a\circ b),

is a non-degenerate involutive solution of the Yang-Baxter equation on the set
G
. Here
a^{-1}
and
\bar{a}
are inverse element of
a
in the groups
(G,\cdot)
and
(G,\circ)
respectively.

Next we recall the notion of derived solutions and Drinfel’d homomorphisms between solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.

Definition 2.22
.

(
[
15
,
35
]
)
Let
R(a,b)=(\varphi_{a}(b),\psi_{b}(a))
be a left non-degenerate solution of the Yang-Baxter equation on the set
X
. Then

(23)

\displaystyle R^{d}:X\times X\to X\times X,\quad R^{d}(a,b)=(\varphi_{a}\psi_{\varphi_{b}^{-1}(a)}(b),a),\quad\forall a,b\in X

is also a solution, which is called the
derived solution
of
R
.

Definition 2.23
.

(
[
15
]
)
Let
(X,R)
and
(Y,R^{\prime})
be two braided sets. A map
\omega:X\times X\to Y\times Y
is called a
Drinfel’d homomorphism
if

\omega~R=R^{\prime}~\omega.

If
\omega
is a bijection,
\omega
is called a
Drinfel’d isomorphism
. In this case,
(X,R)
and
(Y,R^{\prime})
are called
Drinfel’d isomorphic
via
\omega
and denoted by
R\cong_{D}R^{\prime}.

Remark 2.24
.

The notion of Drinfel’d homomorphisms is weaker than the notion of homomorphisms of solutions introduced in Definition
2.20
, in the sense that, if
(X,R)
and
(Y,R^{\prime})
are homomorphic (resp. equivalent) via
f:X\to Y
, then they are Drinfel’d homomorphic (resp. Drinfel’d isomorphic) via
\omega=f\times f
. See
[
15
]
for an example that the notion is strictly weaker as well as more details on Drinfel’d homomorphisms.

Next we consider the derived solution of
R_{G}
, which is induced by a brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
introduced by Theorem
2.21
.

Proposition 2.25
.

(
[
15
]
)

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a brace and
R_{G}
the induced non-degenerate solution of the Yang-Baxter equation on the set
G
introduced by (
22
). Then the derived solution
R_{G}^{d}
of
R_{G}
takes the simple form as follows

R_{G}^{d}(a,b)=(b,a),\quad\forall a,b\in G.

A post-brace
(G,\cdot,\circ,\rhd)
contains two braces by Theorem
2.16
, which gives rise to two non-degenerate solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. Next we show that these two solutions are actually Drinfel’d isomorphic.

Theorem 2.26
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ,\rhd)
be a post-brace. Then for
a,b\in G
,

(24)

\displaystyle R_{1}(a,b)=(a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b),\overline{a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b)}\circ a\circ b),

and

(25)

\displaystyle R_{2}(a,b)=(a^{-1}\cdot(a\ast b),(a^{-1}\cdot(a\ast b))^{\dagger}\ast a\ast b),

are two non-degenerate involutive solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation on the set
G
.
Here
a^{\dagger}
is the inverse element of
a
with respect to the group
(G,\ast)
given by (
16
).
Moreover,
R_{1}
is Drinfel’d isomorphic to
R_{2}
via the map
\overline{\omega}:G\times G\to G\times G
given by

\overline{\omega}(a,b)=\big(a,(L^{\blacktriangleright}_{a})^{-1}(a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b))\big),\quad\forall a,b\in G,

where
\blacktriangleright
is the
\lambda
-map of the brace
(G,\cdot,\ast)
defined by
a\blacktriangleright b=a^{-1}\cdot(a\ast b)
.

Proof.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ,\rhd)
be a post-brace. By Theorem
2.16
,
(G,\cdot,\circ)
and
(G,\cdot,\ast)
are braces. Then by Theorem
2.21
,
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are non-degenerate solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation on the set
G
. By
[
15
, Lemma 2.12]
,

R_{1}
is Drinfel’d isomorphic to its derived solution
\tau
, where
\tau
is the flip map defined by
\tau(a,b)=(b,a)
for all
a,b\in G
. More precisely, there exists a bijective map
\omega_{1}:G\times G\to G\times G
given by
\omega_{1}(a,b)=(a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b),a)
such that
\omega_{1}R_{1}=\tau\omega_{1}
. Applying the same method, there exists another bijective map

\omega_{2}:G\times G\to G\times G
given by
\omega_{2}(a,b)=(a\blacktriangleright b,a)
such that
\omega_{2}R_{2}=\tau\omega_{2}
.

Therefore, there exists a map
\overline{\omega}:=\omega_{2}^{-1}\omega_{1}
such that
\overline{\omega}R_{1}=R_{2}\overline{\omega}
, which implies that
R_{1}
are Drinfel’d isomorphic to
R_{2}
via the map
\overline{\omega}
.
∎

Since a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace naturally induces a post-brace, it naturally provides two Drinfel’d isomorphic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation as follows.

Corollary 2.27
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to a semi-trivial action
\Phi:(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})\to\mathrm{Aut}(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
. Then
R,R^{\mathcal{B}}:H\times H\to H\times H
defined by

R(h,k)=(h^{-1}\cdot_{H}(h\circ_{H}k),\overline{(h^{-1}\cdot_{H}(h\circ_{H}k))}\circ_{H}h\circ_{H}k),\quad\forall h,k\in H,

and

R^{\mathcal{B}}(h,k)=(h^{-1}\cdot_{H}(h\circ_{\mathcal{B}}k),(h^{-1}\cdot_{H}(h\circ_{\mathcal{B}}k))^{\dagger}\circ_{\mathcal{B}}h\circ_{\mathcal{B}}k),\quad\forall h,k\in H,

are two Drinfel’d isomorphic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation on the set
H
. Here
\overline{a}
is the inverse element of
a
with respect to the group
(H,\circ_{H})
, and
a^{\dagger}
is the inverse element of
a
with respect to the group
(H,\circ_{\mathcal{B}})
given by (
10
).

3.
Enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operators and matched pairs of braces

In this section, we introduce the notion of enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces, as a strengthened form of relative Rota-Baxter operators. We show that an enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace give rise to a factorization of brace. Moreover, we also introduce the notion of matched pairs of braces and show that an enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace gives rise to a matched pair of braces.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
be a semi-trivial action of the brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
on the brace
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
. Then a map
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is called an
enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operator
on the brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to the semi-trivial action
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
if

(26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(h\cdot_{H}k);

(27)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)\circ_{G}\mathcal{B}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\Big(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)k\Big)\cdot_{G}a,

for all
a\in G,h,k\in H
.

Remark 3.2
.

When we choose
a
to be the unit
e_{G}
of the brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
, then (
27
) degenerates to (
9
), which implies that an enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operator is a special relative Rota-Baxter operator.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
be an enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to a semi-trivial action
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
. Then for all
a\in G,k\in H
, we have

(28)

a\circ_{G}\mathcal{B}(k)=\mathcal{B}(\Phi(a)k)\cdot_{G}a.

Proof.

By (
26
), we have
\mathcal{B}(e_{H})=e_{G}
. Setting
h
equal to
e_{H}
in (
27
), we have
a\circ_{G}\mathcal{B}(k)=\mathcal{B}(\Phi(a)k)\cdot_{G}a
.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

(
[
25
]
)
A
left ideal
of a skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a subgroup I of
(G,\cdot)
such that
a\rhd I\subseteq I
for all
a\in G
, where
\rhd
is given by
a\rhd b=a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b)
.
In particular, if
(I,\cdot)
is normal in
(G,\cdot)
and
(I,\circ)
is normal in
(G,\circ)
, then I is called an
ideal
of a skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
.

Moreover, we say that
G
admits a factorization through

H
and
K
, if
H,K
are two left ideals of a skew brace
G
and
G=H\cdot K
as the direct product.

Remark 3.5
.

If I is an ideal of a skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
, then we have
a\circ b=a\cdot(a\rhd b)\in I
for all
a,b\in I
, which implies that
(I,\circ)
is a subgroup of
(G,\circ)
. Consequently,
(I,\cdot,\circ)
is a sub-brace of
(G,\cdot,\circ)
.

Since
a\circ b=a\cdot(a\rhd b)
, if
G
admits a factorization through
H
and
K
, we have

G=H\cdot K=H\circ K.

Now we show that enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces give rise to factorizations of braces.
Let
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
be braces and
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
be a map.
For all
h\in H,a\in G
, the map

(29)

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathcal{B}}:H\times G\to H\rtimes_{\Phi}G,\quad\xi_{\mathcal{B}}(h,a)=(h,\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a),

is invertible. In fact, the inverse map
\xi^{-1}:H\rtimes_{\Phi}G\to H\times G
is given by

(30)

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}(h,a)=(h,\mathcal{B}(h)^{-1}\cdot_{G}a),\quad\forall h\in H,~a\in G.

Transporting the brace structure on
(H\rtimes_{\Phi}G,\cdot_{\rtimes},\circ_{\rtimes})
to
H\times G
, we obtain a brace
(H\times G,\bullet,\star)
, where the multiplications
\bullet
and
\star
are given by

(31)

\displaystyle(h,a)\bullet(k,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}\Big(\xi_{\mathcal{B}}(h,a)\cdot_{\rtimes}\xi_{\mathcal{B}}(k,b)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}\Big((h,\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)\cdot_{\rtimes}(k,\mathcal{B}(k)\cdot_{G}b)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}\Big(h\cdot_{H}k,(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)\cdot_{G}(\mathcal{B}(k)\cdot_{G}b)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(h\cdot_{H}k,\mathcal{B}(h\cdot_{H}k)^{-1}\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(k)\cdot_{G}b\Big),

and

(32)

\displaystyle(h,a)\star(k,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}\Big(\xi_{\mathcal{B}}(h,a)\circ_{\rtimes}\xi_{\mathcal{B}}(k,b)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}\Big((h,\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)\circ_{\rtimes}(k,\mathcal{B}(k)\cdot_{G}b)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}\Big(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)(k),(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)\circ_{G}(\mathcal{B}(k)\cdot_{G}b)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)(k),\mathcal{B}(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)k)^{-1}\cdot_{G}((\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)\circ_{G}(\mathcal{B}(k)\cdot_{G}b))\Big).

Moreover, the unit of the brace
(H\times G,\bullet,\star)
is
(e_{H},\mathcal{B}(e_{H})^{-1})
and the inverse elements of
(h,a)
in the groups
(H\times G,\bullet)
and
(H\times G,\star)
are given by

(33)

\displaystyle(h,a)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(h^{-1},\mathcal{B}(h^{-1})^{-1}\cdot_{G}a^{-1}\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h)^{-1}\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(e_{H})\Big);

(34)

\displaystyle(h,a)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)^{-1}(\bar{h}),\mathcal{B}(\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)^{-1}(\bar{h}))^{-1}\cdot_{G}\overline{(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)}\Big).

Theorem 3.6
.

With the above notations,
(H\times G,\bullet,\star)
is a brace factorization into ideals
H\times\{e_{G}\}
and
\{e_{H}\}\times G
if and only if
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is an enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operator on the brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to the semi-trivial action
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
.

Proof.

By (
31
) and (
32
), it is obvious that
\{e_{H}\}\times G
is a left ideal of the brace
(H\times G,\bullet,\star)
by a direct calculation. For all
h,k\in H
, we have

(35)

\displaystyle(h,e_{G})\bullet(k,e_{G})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{bullet}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\Big(h\cdot_{H}k,\mathcal{B}(h\cdot_{H}k)^{-1}\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(k)\Big);

(36)

\displaystyle~~(h,e_{G})\star(k,e_{G})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{star}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\Big(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))(k),\mathcal{B}(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))k)^{-1}\cdot_{G}(\mathcal{B}(h)\circ_{G}\mathcal{B}(k))\Big).

Thus,
H\times\{e_{G}\}
is a sub-brace of
(H\times G,\bullet,\star)
if and only if

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(h\cdot_{H}k)^{-1}\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{G};

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h))k)^{-1}\cdot_{G}(\mathcal{B}(h)\circ_{G}\mathcal{B}(k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{G},

which implies that
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
. Then we have
\mathcal{B}(e_{H})=e_{G}
and (
33
) can be simplified to the following form:

(h,a)^{-1}=(h^{-1},a^{-1}),\quad\forall h\in H,~a\in G.

Moreover, for all
a\in G,h,k\in H
, we have

\displaystyle(h,a)^{-1}\bullet((h,a)\star(k,e_{G}))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{star}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(h^{-1},a^{-1})\bullet\Big(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)(k),\mathcal{B}(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)k)^{-1}\cdot_{G}((\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)\circ_{G}\mathcal{B}(k))\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{bullet}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\Big(h^{-1}\cdot_{H}(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)(k)),a^{-1}\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)k)^{-1}\cdot_{G}((\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)\circ_{G}\mathcal{B}(k))\Big).

Thus,
H\times\{e_{G}\}
is a left ideal of the brace
(H\times G,\bullet,\star)
if and only if

a^{-1}\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h\circ_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)k)^{-1}\cdot_{G}((\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}a)\circ_{G}\mathcal{B}(k))=e_{G},

which implies that
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is an enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operator on the brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to the semi-trivial action
\Phi
.
∎

Next we recall the notion of a matched pair of groups. Then we introduce the notion of a matched pair of braces using matched pairs of groups.

Definition 3.7
.

(
[
36
]
)
A
matched pair of groups
is a triple
(G,H,\sigma)
, where
(G,\cdot_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H})
are groups and

\sigma:G\times H\,\rightarrow\,H\times G,\quad(a,h)\mapsto(a\rightharpoonup h,a\leftharpoonup h),

is a map satisfying the following conditions:

(37)

\displaystyle e_{G}\rightharpoonup h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h,

(38)

\displaystyle a\rightharpoonup(b\rightharpoonup h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{G}b)\rightharpoonup h,

(39)

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{G}b)\leftharpoonup h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(a\leftharpoonup(b\rightharpoonup h)\big)\cdot_{G}(b\leftharpoonup h),

(40)

\displaystyle a\leftharpoonup e_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a,

(41)

\displaystyle(a\leftharpoonup h)\leftharpoonup k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\leftharpoonup(h\cdot_{H}k),

(42)

\displaystyle a\rightharpoonup(h\cdot_{H}k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\rightharpoonup h)\cdot_{H}\big((a\leftharpoonup h)\rightharpoonup k\big),

for all
a,b\in G
,
h,k\in H.

Proposition 3.8
.

(
[
36
]
)

Let
(G,\cdot_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H})
be groups. Then
(G,H,\sigma)
is a matched pair of groups if and only if
(H\times G,\bowtie)
is a group with the unit
(e_{H},e_{G})
, where the multiplication
\bowtie
is given by

(h,a)\bowtie(k,b)=(h\cdot_{H}(a\rightharpoonup k),(a\leftharpoonup k)\cdot_{G}b),\quad\forall a,b\in G,h,k\in H.

(H\times G,\bowtie)
is called the
double group
of the matched pair of groups
(G,H,\sigma)
.

Remark 3.9
.

Let
(G,H,\sigma)
be a matched pair of groups. Then
\sigma
is bijective. Moreover, the triple
(H,G,\sigma^{-1})
is also a matched pair of groups.

Definition 3.10
.

A
matched pair of braces
is a quadruple
(G,H,\sigma,\theta)
, where
G=(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
and
H=(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
are braces and

\displaystyle\sigma:G\times H\,\rightarrow\,H\times G,\quad(a,h)\mapsto(a\rightharpoonup h,a\leftharpoonup h);

\displaystyle\theta:G\times H\,\rightarrow\,H\times G,\quad(a,h)\mapsto(a\rightharpoondown h,a\leftharpoondown h),

are maps such that

(i)

(G,H,\sigma)
is a matched pair of groups
(G,\cdot_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H})
;

(ii)

(G,H,\theta)
is a matched pair of groups
(G,\circ_{G})
and
(H,\circ_{H})
;

(iii)

\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup
and
\rightharpoondown,\leftharpoondown
satisfy the following compatibility conditions:

(43)

\displaystyle(a\leftharpoondown(k\cdot_{H}(b\rightharpoonup t)))\circ_{G}((b\leftharpoonup t)\cdot_{G}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(((((a\leftharpoondown k)\circ_{G}b)\leftharpoonup a^{-1})\cdot_{G}a^{-1})\leftharpoonup(a\rightharpoondown t)\Big)\cdot_{G}((a\leftharpoondown t)\circ_{G}c);

(44)

\displaystyle h\circ_{H}(a\rightharpoondown(k\cdot_{H}(b\rightharpoonup t)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h\circ_{H}(a\rightharpoondown k))\cdot_{H}\Big(((a\leftharpoondown k)\circ_{G}b)\rightharpoonup(a^{-1}\rightharpoonup h^{-1})\Big)\cdot_{H}

\displaystyle\Big((((a\leftharpoondown k)\circ_{G}b)\leftharpoonup(a^{-1}\rightharpoonup h^{-1}))\cdot_{G}(a\leftharpoonup(a^{-1}\rightharpoonup h^{-1}))^{-1}\Big)\rightharpoonup(h\circ_{H}(a\rightharpoondown t)),

for all
a,b,c\in G,h,k,t\in H
.

Remark 3.11
.

Let
(G,H,\sigma,\theta)
be a matched pair of braces. Then
\sigma,\theta
are bijective. Moreover, the quadruple
(H,G,\sigma^{-1},\theta^{-1})
is also a matched pair of braces.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})
be braces. Then
(G,H,\sigma,\theta)
is a matched pair of braces if and only if
(H\times G,\cdot_{\bowtie},\circ_{\bowtie})
is a brace with unit
(e_{H},e_{G})
and multiplications
\cdot_{\bowtie},\circ_{\bowtie}
given by

\displaystyle(h,a)\cdot_{\bowtie}(k,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h\cdot_{H}(a\rightharpoonup k),(a\leftharpoonup k)\cdot_{G}b),

\displaystyle(h,a)\circ_{\bowtie}(k,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h\circ_{H}(a\rightharpoondown k),(a\leftharpoondown k)\circ_{G}b),\quad\forall a,b\in G,h,k\in H.

(H\times G,\cdot_{\bowtie},\circ_{\bowtie})
is called the
double
brace of the matched pair of braces
(G,H,\sigma,\theta)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.8
, we deduce that
(G,H,\sigma)
is a matched pair of groups if and only if
(H\times G,\cdot_{\bowtie})
is a group with unit
(e_{H},e_{G})
. Similarly,
(G,H,\theta)
is a matched pair of groups if and only if
(H\times G,\circ_{\bowtie})
is a group with unit
(e_{H},e_{G})
. Moreover, by a direct calculation, for all
a,b,c\in G,h,k,t\in H
,

(h,a)\circ_{\bowtie}((k,b)\cdot_{\bowtie}(t,c))=(h,a)\circ_{\bowtie}(k,b)\cdot_{\bowtie}(h,a)^{\cdot_{{\bowtie}}-1}\cdot_{\bowtie}(h,a)\circ_{\bowtie}(t,c)

if and only if the equations (
43
) and (
44
) hold. Therefore,

(G,H,\sigma,\theta)
is a matched pair of braces if and only if
(H\times G,\cdot_{\bowtie},\circ_{\bowtie})
is a brace.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

The notion of a matched pair of braces here is very different from the one introduced in
[
1
]
. The latter was used to construct a non-trivial example of finite simple left braces and recover a finite left brace from its Sylow subgroups.

Enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces naturally give rise to matched pairs of braces.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
be an enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to a semi-trivial action
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
. Define
\rightharpoonup,\rightharpoondown:G\times H\to H
and
\leftharpoonup,\leftharpoondown:G\times H\to G
respectively by

\displaystyle a\rightharpoonup h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h,\quad\quad\quad a\leftharpoonup h=a,

\displaystyle a\rightharpoondown h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(a)h,\quad a\leftharpoondown h=a,

for all
a\in G,h\in H
. Then
(G,H,\sigma,\theta)
is a matched pair of braces, where

\sigma(a,h)=(a\rightharpoonup h,a\leftharpoonup h)
and
\theta(a,h)=(a\rightharpoondown h,a\leftharpoondown h).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.6
, if
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is an enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace
(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})
with respect to
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
,
(H\times G,\bullet,\star)
has a brace factorization into ideals
H\times\{e_{G}\}
and
\{e_{H}\}\times G
. By (
31
) and (
32
), for all
h\in H,a\in G
, we have

\displaystyle(e_{H},a)\bullet(h,e_{G})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h,a);

\displaystyle(e_{H},a)\star(h,e_{G})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\Phi(a)h,\mathcal{B}(\Phi(a)h)^{-1}\cdot_{G}(a\circ_{G}\mathcal{B}(h))\big)\overset{\eqref{enhance-property}}{=}(\Phi(a)h,a),

which implies that
a\rightharpoonup h=h,~a\leftharpoonup h=a
and

a\rightharpoondown h=\Phi(a)h,~a\leftharpoondown h=a
by Proposition
3.12
.
It is obvious that
(G,H,\sigma)
is a matched pair of groups
(G,\cdot_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H})
and
(G,H,\theta)
is also a matched pair of groups
(G,\circ_{G})
and
(H,\circ_{H})
. We only need to prove that
\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup,\rightharpoondown,\leftharpoondown
satisfies the compatibility conditions (
43
) and (
44
). For all
a\in G,h,k,t\in H
, we have

\displaystyle h\circ_{H}\Phi(a)(k\cdot_{H}t)=h\circ_{H}(\Phi(a)(k)\cdot_{H}\Phi(a)(t))=(h\circ_{H}\Phi(a)(k))\cdot_{H}h^{-1}\cdot_{H}(h\circ_{H}\Phi(a)(t)),

which implies that Eq. (
44
) holds. Eq. (
43
) holds naturally by (
1
). Thus,
(G,H,\sigma,\theta)
is a matched pair of braces.
∎

4.
Enhanced Rota-Baxter operators on two-sided braces and factorization theorems

In this section, we introduce the notion of Rota-Baxter operators and enhanced Rota-Baxter operators on two-sided braces. An enhanced Rota-Baxter operator on a two-sided brace gives rise to a braided brace and possesses a factorization theorem, generalizing the factorization theorem of Lie groups given by
[
24
, Theorem 3.5]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a two-sided brace. Then the adjoint action
\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}
for the group

(G,\circ)
is a semi-trivial action of the two-sided brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
on itself.

Proof.

We only need to show that
\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{a}
is an automorphism for the group
(G,\cdot)
for all
a\in G
. Actually, for all
a,b,c\in G
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{a}(b\cdot c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ(b\cdot c)\circ\bar{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ b\cdot a^{-1}\cdot a\circ c)\circ\bar{a}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq-two-sided-brace}}{=}

\displaystyle(a\circ b\circ\bar{a})\cdot\bar{a}^{-1}\cdot(a^{-1}\circ\bar{a}^{-1})\cdot\bar{a}^{-1}\cdot(a\circ c\circ\bar{a})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{equality-two-sided-brace}}{=}

\displaystyle(a\circ b\circ\bar{a})\cdot(a\circ\bar{a})\cdot(a\circ c\circ\bar{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{a}(b)\cdot\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{a}(c),

which implies that
\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{a}\in\mathrm{Aut}(G,\cdot)
for all
a\in G
. Thus,
\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}
is a semi-trivial action of the two-sided brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
on itself.
∎

Now we can define (enhanced) Rota-Baxter operators on two-sided braces.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a two-sided brace. Then a map
\mathcal{B}:G\to G
is called a
Rota-Baxter operator
on the two-sided brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
, if

(45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a)\cdot\mathcal{B}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a\cdot b);

(46)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a)\circ\mathcal{B}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a\circ(\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{B}(a)}b)),

for all
a,b\in G
. If in addition,
\mathcal{B}:G\to G
satisfies the equality

(47)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}(a)\cdot x)\circ\mathcal{B}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a\circ(\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{B}(a)\cdot x}b))\cdot x,\quad\forall a,b,x\in G,

then
\mathcal{B}:G\to G
is called an
enhanced Rota-Baxter operator
on the two-sided brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
. An
enhanced Rota-Baxter two-sided brace
is a two-sided brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
together with an enhanced Rota-Baxter operator
\mathcal{B}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:G\to G
be an enhanced Rota-Baxter operator on the two-sided brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
.
Then we have

(48)

\displaystyle b\circ\mathcal{B}(b)=\mathcal{B}(b)\cdot b,\quad\forall b\in G.

Proof.

By (
45
), we have
\mathcal{B}(e_{G})=e_{G}
. Setting
a
equal to
e_{G}
and
x
equal to
b
in (
47
), then we have
b\circ\mathcal{B}(b)=\mathcal{B}(b)\cdot b
for all
b\in G
.
∎

By Proposition
2.9
, we obtain that a Rota-Baxter operator on the two-sided brace induces a descendent brace. However, the descendent brace might no longer be two-sided.

Corollary 4.4
.

If
\mathcal{B}:G\to G
is a Rota-Baxter operator on the two-sided brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
, then

(G,\cdot,\ast)
is a brace, called the
descendent brace
and denoted by
G_{\mathcal{B}}
, where
\ast
is defined by

(49)

a\ast b=a\circ\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{B}(a)}(b),\quad\forall a,b\in G.

Moreover,
\mathcal{B}:(G,\cdot,\ast)\to(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a homomorphism of braces.

Next we prove a factorization theorem of enhanced Rota-Baxter two-sided braces, which generalizes the factorization theorem of Rota-Baxter groups given by
[
24
, Theorem 3.5]
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{B})
be an enhanced Rota-Baxter two-sided brace. Denote by
(G_{\mathcal{B}},\cdot,\ast)
the descendent brace given in Corollary
4.4
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{B})
be an enhanced Rota-Baxter two-sided brace. Define

\mathcal{B}_{+}:G\to G,\quad\mathcal{B}_{+}(a)=a\circ\mathcal{B}(a).

Then
\mathcal{B}_{+}
is a homomorphism of braces from
G_{\mathcal{B}}
to
G
.

Proof.

For all
a,b\in G
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{+}(a\cdot b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\circ\mathcal{B}(a\cdot b)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{enhance-RBO}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a\cdot b)\cdot(a\cdot b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}(a)\cdot a)\cdot(\mathcal{B}(b)\cdot b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{+}(a)\cdot\mathcal{B}_{+}(b),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{+}(a\ast b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ(\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{B}(a)}b)\circ\mathcal{B}(a\circ\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{B}(a)}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ\mathcal{B}(a)\circ b\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(a)}\circ\mathcal{B}(a)\circ\mathcal{B}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ\mathcal{B}(a)\circ b\circ\mathcal{B}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{+}(a)\circ\mathcal{B}_{+}(b).

Thus,
\mathcal{B}_{+}
is a homomorphism of braces from
G_{\mathcal{B}}
to
G
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{B}
be an enhanced Rota-Baxter operator on
G
. Define four subsets of
G
as follows:

G_{+}:=\mathrm{Im}\mathcal{B}_{+},\quad G_{-}:=\mathrm{Im}\mathcal{B},\quad K_{+}:=\mathrm{Ker}\mathcal{B},\quad K_{-}:=\mathrm{Ker}\mathcal{B}_{+}.

Since both
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}_{+}
are homomorphisms of braces, it follows that
G_{+}
and
G_{-}
are sub-braces of G,

K_{+}
and
K_{-}
are ideals of
G_{\mathcal{B}}
and
G_{\pm}\cong G_{\mathcal{B}}/K_{\mp}
as braces.
Moreover, we have the following relations.

Lemma 4.6
.

K_{+}\subset G_{+}
and
K_{-}\subset G_{-}
are ideals of braces.

Proof.

Let
a\in K_{-}
, that is,
\mathcal{B}_{+}(a)=a\circ\mathcal{B}(a)=e
. Then we have
a=\overline{\mathcal{B}(a)}=\mathcal{B}(a^{\dagger})
, where
a^{\dagger}=\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\overline{\mathcal{B}(a)}}\bar{(a)}
is the inverse of
a
in the group
(G_{\mathcal{B}},\ast)
. Thus,
a\in G_{-}
and hence
K_{-}\subset G_{-}
.

For any
a\in K_{-}
and
\mathcal{B}(b)\in G_{-}
, let us check
\mathcal{B}(b)\circ a\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)}\in K_{-}
. In fact, we have

\displaystyle b*a*b^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b\circ(\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{B}(b)}a)\circ\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{B}(b)\mathcal{B}(a)}\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)}}\bar{b}=\mathcal{B}(b)\circ a\circ\mathcal{B}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b\circ\mathcal{B}(b)\circ a\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)}\circ\mathcal{B}(b)\circ\mathcal{B}(a)\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)}\circ\bar{b}\circ\mathcal{B}(b)\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(a)}\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(b)\circ a\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)},

where in the last equation we used the fact that
a\circ\mathcal{B}(a)=e
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(b)\circ a\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)}\circ\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B}(b)\circ a\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(b)\circ a\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)}\circ\mathcal{B}(b\ast a\ast a^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(b)\circ a\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)}\circ\mathcal{B}(b)\circ\mathcal{B}(a)\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)}=e,

which implies that
\mathcal{B}(b)\circ a\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)}
is in
K_{-}
. Since
(G,\cdot)
is an abelian group, we also have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}(b)\cdot a\cdot\mathcal{B}(b)^{-1})\circ\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B}(b)\cdot a\cdot\mathcal{B}(b)^{-1})=a\circ\mathcal{B}(a)=e,

which implies that
\mathcal{B}(b)\cdot a\cdot\mathcal{B}(b)^{-1}
is in
K_{-}
. Since
K_{-}
is an ideal of
G_{\mathcal{B}}
,
K_{-}
is an ideal of
G_{-}
. Likewise we prove that
K_{+}
is an ideal of
G_{+}
.
∎

Based on Lemma
4.6
, we define a map

\Theta:G_{-}/K_{-}\to G_{+}/K_{+},\quad\Theta([\mathcal{B}(a)])=[\mathcal{B}_{+}(a)],\quad\forall a\in G,

where
[\cdot]
denotes the equivalence class in the two quotients. In order to show that
\Theta
is well-defined, consider an arbitrary element
a\in K_{-}
, that is,
a=\overline{\mathcal{B}(a)}=\mathcal{B}(a^{\dagger})
. Note that
a^{\dagger}=\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\overline{\mathcal{B}(a)}}\bar{a}=\bar{a}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\Theta([\mathcal{B}(b)\circ a])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta([\mathcal{B}(b\ast\bar{a})])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{B}_{+}(b\ast\bar{a})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(b\ast\bar{a})\circ\mathcal{B}(b\ast\bar{a})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[b\ast\mathcal{B}(b)\circ a\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(b)}\circ\mathcal{B}(b)\circ\mathcal{B}(a^{\dagger})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta([\mathcal{B}(b)]),

and

\displaystyle\Theta([\mathcal{B}(b)\cdot a])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta([\mathcal{B}(b\cdot a^{\dagger})])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{B}_{+}(b\cdot a^{\dagger})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(b\cdot a^{\dagger})\circ\mathcal{B}(b\cdot a^{\dagger})]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{enhance-RBO}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(b\cdot a^{\dagger})\cdot(b\cdot a^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(b)\cdot\mathcal{B}(a^{\dagger})\cdot b\cdot a^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta([\mathcal{B}(b)]),

which implies that
\Theta
is well-defined.

Proposition 4.7
.

The map
\Theta:G_{-}/K_{-}\to G_{+}/K_{+}
is an isomorphism of braces, called the
Cayley transform
of the enhanced Rota-Baxter operator
\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\Theta
is surjective. To see that it is also injective, take
\mathcal{B}_{+}(a)=a\circ\mathcal{B}(a)\in K_{+}
, that is,
\mathcal{B}(a\circ\mathcal{B}(a))=e
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{+}(\mathcal{B}(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a)\circ\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B}(a))=\mathcal{B}(a\ast\mathcal{B}(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a\circ\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{B}(a)}\mathcal{B}(a))=\mathcal{B}(a\circ\mathcal{B}(a))=e,

which implies that
\mathcal{B}(a)\in K_{-}
. This proves that
\Theta
is injective.

We next show that
\Theta
is a homomorphism of braces, which follows from

\displaystyle\Theta([\mathcal{B}(a)]\cdot[\mathcal{B}(b)])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta([\mathcal{B}(a\cdot b)])=[\mathcal{B}_{+}(a\cdot b)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{B}_{+}(a)\cdot\mathcal{B}_{+}(b)]=\Theta([\mathcal{B}(a)])\cdot\Theta([\mathcal{B}(b)]),

and

\displaystyle\Theta([\mathcal{B}(a)]\circ[\mathcal{B}(b)])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta([\mathcal{B}(a\ast b)])=[\mathcal{B}_{+}(a\ast b)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{B}_{+}(a)\circ\mathcal{B}_{+}(b)]=\Theta([\mathcal{B}(a)])\circ\Theta([\mathcal{B}(b)]),

according to Proposition
4.5
. Therefore,
\Theta
is an isomorphism of braces.
∎

Now we consider the direct product brace
(G_{+}\times G_{-},\cdot_{D},\circ_{D})
, where the multiplications
\cdot_{D}
and
\circ_{D}
are given by

\displaystyle(a_{+},a_{-})\cdot_{D}(b_{+},b_{-})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(a_{+}\cdot b_{+},a_{-}\cdot b_{-}),

\displaystyle(a_{+},a_{-})\circ_{D}(b_{+},b_{-})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(a_{+}\circ b_{+},a_{-}\circ b_{-}),\quad\forall a_{+},b_{+}\in G_{+},a_{-},b_{-}\in G_{-}.

Let
G_{\Theta}\subset G_{+}\times G_{-}
denote the subset

G_{\Theta}:=\{(a_{+},a_{-})\in G_{+}\times G_{-}\mid\Theta([a_{-}])=[a_{+}]\}.

Define a map
\Phi:G\to G_{\Theta}
by

\Phi(a):=(\mathcal{B}_{+}(a),\mathcal{B}(a)).

Lemma 4.8
.

With the above notations,
G_{\Theta}
is a sub-brace of
(G_{+}\times G_{-},\cdot_{D},\circ_{D})
. Moreover, the map
\Phi
is an isomorphism of braces from
(G_{\mathcal{B}},\cdot,\ast)
to
(G_{\Theta},\cdot_{D},\circ_{D})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.7
, for any
(a_{+},a_{-}),(b_{+},b_{-})\in G_{\Theta}
, we have

\displaystyle\Theta([a_{-}\cdot b_{-}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta([a_{-}]\cdot[b_{-}])=\Theta([a_{-}])\cdot\Theta([b_{-}])=[a_{+}]\cdot[b_{+}]=[a_{+}\cdot b_{+}];

\displaystyle\Theta([a_{-}\circ b_{-}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta([a_{-}]\circ[b_{-}])=\Theta([a_{-}])\circ\Theta([b_{-}])=[a_{+}]\circ[b_{+}]=[a_{+}\circ b_{+}],

which implies that
(a_{+}\cdot b_{+},a_{-}\cdot b_{-})\in G_{\Theta}
and
(a_{+}\circ b_{+},a_{-}\circ b_{-})\in G_{\Theta}
. Then
G_{\Theta}
is a sub-brace of
(G_{+}\times G_{-},\cdot_{D},\circ_{D})
.

We next check that
\Phi
is a bijection. Let
a\in G
such that
\Phi(a)=(e,e)
. Then we have
\mathcal{B}(a)=a\circ\mathcal{B}(a)=e
. Thus
a=e
, which implies that
\Phi
is injective. For any
(a_{+},a_{-})\in G_{\Theta}
, we have
\Theta([a_{-}])=[a_{+}]
. Since
a_{-}\in G_{-}
, there exists
a\in G
such that
\mathcal{B}(a)=a_{-}
. Hence we get

\displaystyle\Theta([a_{-}])=\Theta([\mathcal{B}(a)])=[a\circ\mathcal{B}(a)].

Therefore,
[a_{+}]=[a\circ\mathcal{B}(a)]
, which means that there exists
b\in K_{+}
such that

a_{+}=a\circ\mathcal{B}(a)\circ b.

Let
a^{\prime}=a\ast b
. Then we have

\displaystyle\Phi(a^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((a\ast b)\circ\mathcal{B}(a\ast b),\mathcal{B}(a\ast b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{B}(a)}(b)\circ\mathcal{B}(a)\circ\mathcal{B}(b),\mathcal{B}(a)\circ\mathcal{B}(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ\mathcal{B}(a)\circ b,\mathcal{B}(a))=(a_{+},a_{-}).

Therefore,
\Phi
is surjective.

Finally, for any
a,b\in G
, by Proposition
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(a\ast b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}_{+}(a\ast b),\mathcal{B}(a\ast b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}_{+}(a)\circ\mathcal{B}_{+}(b),\mathcal{B}(a)\circ\mathcal{B}(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}_{+}(a),\mathcal{B}(a))\circ_{D}(\mathcal{B}_{+}(b),\mathcal{B}(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(a)\circ_{D}\Phi(b).

Therefore,
\Phi
is an isomorphism of braces from
(G_{\mathcal{B}},\cdot,\ast)
to
(G_{\Theta},\cdot_{D},\circ_{D})
.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

(
Factorization Theorem
of enhanced Rota-Baxter two-sided braces)

Let
(G,\mathcal{B})
be an enhanced Rota-Baxter two-sided brace. Then every element
a\in G
can be uniquely expressed as
a=a_{+}\circ\overline{a_{-}}=a_{-}^{-1}\cdot a_{+}
for
(a_{+},a_{-})\in G_{\Theta}
.

Proof.

For any
a\in G
, we have
a=\mathcal{B}_{+}(a)\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(a)}
which also equal to
\mathcal{B}(a)^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{B}_{+}(a)
by (
48
). To see the uniqueness, if
a=a_{+}\circ\overline{a_{-}}=b_{+}\circ\overline{b_{-}}
, then

\overline{b_{+}}\circ a_{+}=\overline{b_{-}}\circ a_{-}\in G_{+}\cap G_{-},~\mbox{ and~}~\Theta(\overline{b_{-}}\circ a_{-})=\overline{b_{+}}\circ a_{+}.

Suppose
\overline{b_{+}}\circ a_{+}=\overline{b_{-}}\circ a_{-}=\mathcal{B}(s)\in G_{+}\cap G_{-}
for some
s\in G
. Then

\Theta([\mathcal{B}(s)])=[\mathcal{B}_{+}(s)]=[s\circ\mathcal{B}(s)]=[\mathcal{B}(s)],

which implies that
s\circ\mathcal{B}(s)=\mathcal{B}(s)\circ c
for some
c\in K_{+}
. Note that
K_{+}\subset G_{+}
as an ideal. Then we have
s=\mathcal{B}(s)\circ c\circ\overline{\mathcal{B}(s)}\in K_{+}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{B}(s)=e
. Hence we get
b_{+}=a_{+}
and
b_{-}=a_{-}
.
∎

5.
Examples

In this section, we determine Rota-Baxter operators and enhanced Rota-Baxter operators on the two-sided brace induced by the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover, we give the corresponding post-braces and descendent braces. Finally, we present two Drinfel’d isomorphic solutions
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
of the Yang-Baxter equation on
\mathfrak{g}
by applying Theorem
2.26
.

Example 5.1
.

(
[
21
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra over the field of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
with the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
satisfying

[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{3},\quad[e_{1},e_{3}]=[e_{2},e_{3}]=0.

Then
(\mathfrak{g},+,\circ)
is a two-sided brace, where
+
is the natural abelian group structure on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
and the group structure
\circ
is given by

x\circ y:=x+y+\frac{1}{2}[x,y],\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Since a Rota-Baxter operator
\mathcal{B}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
on the brace
(\mathfrak{g},+,\circ)
satisfies
\mathcal{B}(x+y)=\mathcal{B}(x)+\mathcal{B}(y)
, there holds
\mathcal{B}(rx)=r\mathcal{B}(x)
for
r\in\mathbb{Q}
. We further assume that
\mathcal{B}
is continuous. Then we have
\mathcal{B}(rx)=r\mathcal{B}(x)
for
r\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e.
\mathcal{B}
is a linear map. With respect to the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
given above, a linear map
\mathcal{B}
is determined by its matrix which we denote by the same symbol, via

\mathcal{B}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}&B_{13}\\
B_{21}&B_{22}&B_{23}\\
B_{31}&B_{32}&B_{33}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
x_{3}\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall x=x_{1}e_{1}+x_{2}e_{2}+x_{3}e_{3}.

Example 5.2
.

There are three classes of Rota-Baxter operators on the two-sided brace
(\mathfrak{g},+,\circ)
given in Example
5.1
as follows:

(i)

\left\{\left.\mathcal{B}=\begin{pmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}&0\\
B_{21}&B_{22}&0\\
B_{31}&B_{32}&0\end{pmatrix}\,\right|\,\begin{vmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{21}&B_{22}\end{vmatrix}=0,\ B_{31},B_{32}\in\mathbb{R}\right\};

(ii)

\left\{\left.\mathcal{B}=\begin{pmatrix}B_{33}+\sqrt{B_{33}(B_{33}+1)}&0&0\\
0&B_{33}+\sqrt{B_{33}(B_{33}+1)}&0\\
B_{31}&B_{32}&B_{33}\end{pmatrix}\,\right|\,B_{31},B_{32}\in\mathbb{R},B_{33}\in(-\infty,-1)\cup(0,\infty)\right\};

(iii)

\left\{\left.\mathcal{B}=\begin{pmatrix}B_{33}-\sqrt{B_{33}(B_{33}+1)}&0&0\\
0&B_{33}-\sqrt{B_{33}(B_{33}+1)}&0\\
B_{31}&B_{32}&B_{33}\end{pmatrix}\,\right|\,B_{31},B_{32}\in\mathbb{R},B_{33}\in(-\infty,-1)\cup(0,\infty)\right\}.

Moreover, there is only one class of enhanced Rota-Baxter operators on
(\mathfrak{g},+,\circ)
given
as follows:

\left\{\left.\mathcal{B}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
B_{31}&B_{32}&0\end{pmatrix}\,\right|B_{31},B_{32}\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

In fact, by a direct calculation, for all
x=x_{1}e_{1}+x_{2}e_{2}+x_{3}e_{3},y=y_{1}e_{1}+y_{2}e_{2}+y_{3}e_{3},
we have

[x,y]=(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1})e_{3},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

which implies that

x\circ y=x+y+\frac{1}{2}(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1})e_{3}.

Then we have

\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{x}(y)=x\circ y\circ(-x)=y+(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1})e_{3}.

We consider the equation
\mathcal{B}(x)\circ\mathcal{B}(y)=\mathcal{B}(x\circ(\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{B}(x)}y))
and
obtain the following results by a direct and complicated calculation:

B_{13}=B_{23}=0
and

\frac{1}{2}\begin{vmatrix}B_{11}x_{1}+B_{12}x_{2}&B_{11}y_{1}+B_{12}y_{2}\\
B_{21}x_{1}+B_{22}x_{2}&B_{21}y_{1}+B_{22}y_{2}\end{vmatrix}=\frac{1}{2}B_{33}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1}&y_{1}\\
x_{2}&y_{2}\end{vmatrix}+B_{33}\begin{vmatrix}B_{11}x_{1}+B_{12}x_{2}&y_{1}\\
B_{21}x_{1}+B_{22}x_{2}&y_{2}\end{vmatrix},\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

If
B_{33}\neq 0
and
B_{33}\in(-\infty,-1)\cup(0,\infty)
, we have
B_{12}=B_{21}=0
and
B_{11}=B_{22}=B_{33}\pm\sqrt{B_{33}(B_{33}+1)}

by a direct calculation, which are exactly the second and third classes of Rota-Baxter operators.
If
B_{33}=0
, we have

\begin{vmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{21}&B_{22}\end{vmatrix}=0,

which is the first class of Rota-Baxter operators.

Moreover, if we consider enhanced Rota-Baxter operators on the brace
(\mathfrak{g},+,\circ)
satisfying (
47
), that is, the following equation holds:

(\mathcal{B}(x)+z)\circ\mathcal{B}(y)=\mathcal{B}(x\circ(\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{(\mathcal{B}(x)+z)}y))+z,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},

then we have

(\mathcal{B}(x)+z)\circ\mathcal{B}(y)=\mathcal{B}(x)\circ\mathcal{B}(y)+z+\frac{1}{2}(z_{1}\mathcal{B}(y)_{2}-z_{2}\mathcal{B}(y)_{1})e_{3},

and

\mathcal{B}(x\circ(\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{(\mathcal{B}(x)+z)}y))+z=\mathcal{B}(x\circ\mathrm{Ad}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{B}(x)}y)+\begin{vmatrix}z_{1}&y_{1}\\
z_{2}&y_{2}\end{vmatrix}\mathcal{B}(e_{3})+z,

which implies that

\begin{vmatrix}z_{1}&y_{1}\\
z_{2}&y_{2}\end{vmatrix}\mathcal{B}(e_{3})=\frac{1}{2}\begin{vmatrix}z_{1}&\mathcal{B}(y)_{1}\\
z_{2}&\mathcal{B}(y)_{2}\end{vmatrix}e_{3}.
According to the above notation, it is equivalent to the following equation:

\begin{vmatrix}z_{1}&B_{33}y_{1}-\frac{1}{2}(B_{11}y_{1}+B_{12}y_{2})\\
z_{2}&B_{33}y_{2}-\frac{1}{2}(B_{21}y_{1}+B_{22}y_{2})\end{vmatrix}=0,\quad\forall y_{1},y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R},

which implies that
B_{21}=B_{12}=0
and
B_{33}=\frac{1}{2}B_{22}=\frac{1}{2}B_{11}=0
. Therefore, all enhanced Rota-Baxter operators on the brace
(\mathfrak{g},+,\circ)
are of the following form

\left\{\left.\mathcal{B}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
B_{31}&B_{32}&0\end{pmatrix}\,\right|B_{31},B_{32}\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

Example 5.4
.

We consider two non-degenerate involutive solutions
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
of the Yang-Baxter equation given in Theorem
2.26
on the two-sided brace given in Example
5.1
. By a direct calculation, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle-x+(x\circ y)=-x+x+y+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]=y+\frac{1}{2}[x,y],

and

\displaystyle\overline{-x+(x\circ y)}\circ x\circ y=(-y-\frac{1}{2}[x,y])\circ x\circ y=(x-y)\circ y=x+\frac{1}{2}[x,y],

which implies that

R_{1}(x,y)=(y+\frac{1}{2}[x,y],x+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]).

By a direct calculation, we have

x^{\dagger}=-x+[\mathcal{B}(x),x],

as the inverse of
x
in the group
(\mathfrak{g},\ast)
. Then by the fact that
\mathcal{B}([x,y])=0
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle-x+x\ast y=-x+x+y+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]+[\mathcal{B}(x),y]=y+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]+[\mathcal{B}(x),y],

and

\displaystyle(-x+x\ast y)^{\dagger}\ast x\ast y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-y-\frac{1}{2}[x,y]-[\mathcal{B}(x),y]+[\mathcal{B}(y),y])\ast x\ast y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x-y-[\mathcal{B}(x),y]+[\mathcal{B}(y),y]-[\mathcal{B}(y),x])\ast y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]+[x,\mathcal{B}(y)],

which implies that

R_{2}(x,y)=(y+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]+[\mathcal{B}(x),y],x+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]+[x,\mathcal{B}(y)]).

In this case,
R_{2}
can be expressed in terms of
\ast
as follows:

R_{2}(x,y)=(-x+x\ast y,y+(-y)\ast x),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then by Theorem
2.26
, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle(L^{\blacktriangleright}_{x})^{-1}(-x+(x\circ y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{\dagger}\ast(x\circ y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-x+[\mathcal{B}(x),x])\ast(x+y+\frac{1}{2}[x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-x+[\mathcal{B}(x),x]+x+y+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]-\frac{1}{2}[x,y]-[\mathcal{B}(x),x]-[\mathcal{B}(x),y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y-[\mathcal{B}(x),y],

which implies that
R_{1}
is Drinfel’d isomorphic to
R_{2}
via the map
\overline{\omega}:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}
given by

\overline{\omega}(x,y)=(x,y-[\mathcal{B}(x),y]),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Forest Fire Model on ℤ + \mathbb{Z}_{+} with Delays
Satyaki Bhattacharya, Stanislav Volkov

Abstract
We consider a generalization of the forest fire model on ℤ + \mathbb{Z}_{+} with ignition at zero only, studied in [ 15 ] . Unlike that model, we allow delays in the spread of the fires and the non-zero burning time of individual ‘‘trees’’. We obtain some general properties for this model, which cover, among others, the phenomena of an ‘‘infinite fire’’, not present in the original model.

Introduction

The following forest fire model was introduced in
[

Various forest fire models have been abundant, especially in the physics literature, partly for their practical applications and partly for the fact that they exhibit so-called
self-organized criticality
, which is a property of many dynamical systems that naturally evolve into a critical state, and where small changes can, surprisingly, trigger large-scale events. This phenomenon can be useful for explaining why complex systems (e.g., earthquakes, avalanches, stock markets, etc.) exhibit power-law behaviour without the necessity of external fine-tuning. Self-organized criticality is considered as a mechanism by which complexity arises in nature; for a more detailed analysis of this notion, we refer the reader to
[

[

2
Existence of infinite fire

The infinite fire is a phenomenon which cannot happen in the forest fire models studied in either
[
9
]
or
[
15
]
; it arises, however, when there is a delay in the spreading of fire.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}(\Delta>0)>0
. Then a.s. there will be an infinite fire, i.e.
\mathbb{P}(\inf\{k:\ n_{k}=\infty\}<\infty)=1
.

The next auxiliary statement is quite obvious, yet, for the sake of completeness, we present its short proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\Delta
is a non-negative random variable with
\mathbb{P}(\Delta>0)>0
. Then for some constants
\delta_{1}>0
and
\delta_{2}>0

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Delta_{j}^{\prime}\geq n\delta_{1}\text{ \ for all }n\geq 1\right)\geq\delta_{2}

where
\Delta_{j}^{\prime}
are i.i.d. copies of
\Delta
.

Proof.

By the condition of the lemma, there exist
a_{1}>0
and
p_{1}>0
such that
\mathbb{P}(\Delta\geq a_{1})\geq p_{1}
.
Hence
\Delta_{j}^{\prime}
is stochastically larger than

Z_{j}=\begin{cases}a_{1},&\text{with probability }p_{1};\\
0,&\text{with probability }1-p_{1}\end{cases}

and we can assume that
Z_{j}
s are also i.i.d.
Consequently, it suffices to show that

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}\geq n\delta_{1}\text{ for all }n=1,2,\dots\right)\geq\delta_{2}

where
\delta_{1}:=\mathbb{E}Z_{j}/2=a_{1}p_{1}/2>0
.

By the strong law of large numbers
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}\to a_{1}p_{1}
a.s. as
n\to\infty
, hence, since
\delta_{1}<\mathbb{E}Z_{J}
, a.s. there exists a (random)

for all

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}\geq n\delta_{1}\ \forall n>n_{0}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}\geq n\delta_{1}\ \forall n>n_{0}\mid Z_{1}=Z_{2}=\dots=Z_{n_{0}}=a_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}\geq n\delta_{1}\ \forall n>n_{0}\text{ and }Z_{1}=Z_{2}=\dots=Z_{n_{0}}=a_{1}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{1}=Z_{2}=\dots=Z_{n_{0}}=a_{1}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}\geq n\delta_{1}\ \forall n\geq 1\text{ and }Z_{1}=Z_{2}=\dots=Z_{n_{0}}=a_{1}\right)}{p_{1}^{n_{0}}}\leq\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}\geq n\delta_{1}\ \forall n\geq 1\right)}{p_{1}^{n_{0}}}

3
Instant burning

In this Section, we will consider the case when
\theta=0
, and
\mathbb{E}\Delta_{x}>0
that is, the trees are burnt instantly but the expected time for the fire to spread from site

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
\theta\equiv 0
,
0<\mathbb{E}\Delta<\infty
and
\mathrm{Var}(\Delta)>0
. Then there is only one infinite fire.

Remark 3.2
.

The non-random case
\Delta\equiv\mathrm{const}>0
will be covered later in Theorem
3.3

Proof.

The existence of infinite fire follows from Theorem
2.1

\mathbb{E}\Delta>0

\mathbb{P}(\Delta>0)>0
, so we only need to show that there cannot be
a second infinite fire

For each site

let

be
the index of the fire for this site
, which turned out to be the first infinite fire (hence there will be an

such that for all

).

Assume now that there is a second infinite fire. Since for any

is a.s. finite, there will be a (random) site

such that each site with

was not burnt by any

between the times when it was burnt by the first and the second infinite fire. Hence, for each

, the first (the second resp.) fire to burn

is, in fact, the first (the second resp.) infinite fire. Since burning is immediate (

), for

\displaystyle f_{x+1,1}=f_{x,1}+\Delta_{x,1}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{x+1,2}=f_{x,2}+\Delta_{x,2}.

Let us define

where
\xi_{k}=\Delta_{k,2}-\Delta_{k,1}
are i.i.d. mean zero random variables. Then

Proposition 3.4

Let

Suppose the fire reaches some site

. Conditioned on this time

We have

As a result,

as

, yielding

.

Now assume
c>1
.
We will consider only the harder case (d). Let
\Delta_{x}^{(i)}
be the (random) spread time from site
x
to site

, for the fire number

, and let

\displaystyle D_{n,i}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}\Delta_{x}^{(i)}<(c-\delta)\log n\right\},

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle:=\{\text{the first fire which reaches the $n-$th tree does not spread to the $(n+1)^{st}$ tree}\}

Note that in these notations

units of time from its start

.

We have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(A_{n})&=\mathbb{P}(A_{n}\cap D_{n,\zeta_{n}}^{c})+\mathbb{P}(A_{n}\cap D_{n,\zeta_{n}})\leq\mathbb{P}\left(A_{n}\mid D_{n,\zeta_{n}}^{c}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(D_{n,\zeta_{n}}\right)\\

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(D_{n,i}\mid\zeta_{n}=i\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(D_{n,i}\right).

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle:=\{\text{the first $i-1$ fires do not reach site }n\},

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle:=\{\text{the $i$-th fire reaches site }n\}.

Theorem 4.1

Suppose that

Proof.

Let
L_{i,1}
be the length of the maximum stretch of sites to the right of
m_{i}
burned at the same time as
m_{i}+1
, so that
f_{m_{i}+y,1}=f_{m_{i}+1,1}
for
y=2,3,\dots,L_{i,1}
. After the fire reaches site
m_{i}+L_{i,1}
, and
m_{i}+L_{i,1}+1
is empty at that time (since we know that the fire did not immediately go further) there are two possibilities: either with probability
e^{-\theta}
within the next
\theta
units of time no tree appears at the site
m_{i}+L_{i,1}+1
, or such a tree does appear.

In the first case, we set
N_{i}=1
and stop. In the latter case, we say that the fire has
a jump
at position
m_{i}+L_{i,1}+1
, meaning that the fire, whilst burning both
m_{i}+L_{i,1}
and
m_{i}+L_{i,1}+1
, reached the latter site with a delay (up to
\theta
;
0<f_{m_{i}+L_{i,1}+1,1}-f_{m_{i}+L_{i,1},1}<\theta
). In case of such a jump, the fire will immediately spread to the additional
L_{i,2}
sites to the right of
m_{i}+L_{i,1}
, and thus
f_{m_{i}+L_{i,1}+y,1}=f_{m_{i}+L_{i,1}+1,1}
for
y=2,3,\dots,L_{i,2}
, but there is no tree at the site
m_{i}+L_{i,1}+L_{i,2}+1
at this time.

Similarly, we can define
L_{i,3},L_{i,4},\dots,L_{i,N_{i}}
, where
N_{i}
is the number of continuous stretches of sites that were burnt at the same time (before the fire eventually stopped completely);
N_{i}
is the number of such jumps. As a result,
N_{i}
is a geometric random variable with rate
e^{-\theta}
, and the site
\left(m_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}L_{i,j}\right)+1
is burnt slightly later than the site
m_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}L_{i,j}
for each
k=1,2,\dots,N_{i}-1
, with the delay of up to
\theta
.

From the construction,

\displaystyle m_{i+1}-m_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}L_{i,j}.

(4.6)

Note also that
N_{i}
is typically not very large, formally, for some sufficiently large
c_{1}>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(C_{i}^{c})\leq\left(1-e^{-\theta}\right)^{\lfloor c_{1}\log i\rfloor}\leq\frac{1}{i^{2}}\quad\text{ where }C_{i}:=\{N_{i}\leq c_{1}\log i\}

(4.7)

which implies by the Borel-Cantelli lemma that a.s.
N_{i}\leq c_{1}\log i
for all but finitely many
i
s.

Our goal now is to find an upper bound on how far the fire, that passed beyond
m_{i}
for the first time, can travel (i.e., the first fire to burn
m_{i}+1
). Conjecture
4.4
postulates that the time to reach
m_{i}
for
the second time
is at most
c\log m_{i}
for all
i\geq\kappa
, and for these values of
i
each
L_{i,j}
can be coupled with
\xi_{i,j}
,
j=1,2,\dots
, where
\xi_{i,j}
are independent geometric random variables with rate
e^{-(\theta(j-1)+c\log m_{i})}=m_{i}^{-c}e^{-\theta(j-1)}
, respectively, such that
L_{i,j}\leq\xi_{i,j}
. This coupling results from the fact that under this conjecture, the time to reach
m_{i}
for the second time
f_{m_{i},2}\leq c\log m_{i}
and then after
j-1
jumps the time is at most
\Delta:=f_{m_{i},2}+\theta(j-1)\leq c\log m_{i}+\theta(j-1)
, and the probability of not having a single tree by this time is
e^{-\Delta}
.

Together with (
4.6
) this yields

\displaystyle\left(m_{i+1}-m_{i}\right)1_{i\geq\kappa}\preccurlyeq\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}\xi_{i,j}

(4.8)

where
X\preccurlyeq Y
means that
Y
stochastically dominates
X
.
Let

\mathcal{B}_{i}:=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}\xi_{i,j}\geq m_{i}^{c+\epsilon/2}\times c_{1}\log i\right\}\supseteq\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}\xi_{i,j}\geq m_{i}^{c+\epsilon}-m_{i}\right\}

for large
i
(the inclusion follows from part (a)).
Then, using (
4.7
), for
i\geq\kappa

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{B}_{i}\mid\mathcal{F}_{f_{m_{i}+1,1}}\right)&\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{B}_{i}\mid N_{i}\leq c_{1}\log i\mid\mathcal{F}_{f_{m_{i}+1,1}}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(N_{i}>c_{1}\log i\mid\mathcal{F}_{f_{m_{i}+1,1}}\right)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{i,j}\geq m_{i}^{c+\epsilon/2}\text{ for some }j=1,2,\dots,\lfloor c_{1}\log i\rfloor\right)+\frac{1}{i^{2}}\\
&\leq c_{1}\log i\times\max_{j\leq c_{1}\log i}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{i,j}\geq m_{i}^{c+\epsilon/2}\right)+\frac{1}{i^{2}}\end{split}

(4.9)

Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{i,j}>m_{i}^{c+\epsilon/2}\right)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{m_{i}^{c}e^{\theta(j-1)}}\right)^{m_{i}^{c+\epsilon/2}}\leq\exp\left(-e^{-\theta(j-1)}\,m_{i}^{\epsilon/2}\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{m_{i}^{\epsilon/2}}{i^{c_{1}\theta}}\right)

as
j\leq c_{1}\log i
, and by part (a) of the lemma
m_{i}
grows faster than
i^{r}
for any
r>0
, the RHS of (
4.9
) is summable over
i
, and thus the events
\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}\xi_{i,j}\geq m_{i}^{c+\epsilon}-m_{i}\right\}
happen finitely often by the Borel-Cantelli lemma. As a result, from (
4.8
)

m_{i+1}\leq m_{i}^{c+\epsilon}

for all large
i
a.s.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

Fix some
\epsilon>0
. Lemma
4.6
implies that
\log m_{i+1}\leq(1+\epsilon)\log m_{i}
for all large
i
, a.s. On the other hand, by Lemma
4.2
,
f_{m_{i+1},1}\leq(1+\epsilon)\log m_{i+1}
. Hence, for all
n\in\{m_{i},\dots,m_{i+1}\}
we have

f_{n,1}\leq f_{m_{i+1},1}\leq(1+\epsilon)^{2}\log{m_{i}}\leq(1+\epsilon)^{2}\log{n}.

Together with Lemma
4.3
, this implies
f_{n,1}=\mathcal{O}(\log n)
.
∎

The next statement strengthens part (a) of Lemma
4.6
(
without
using Conjecture
4.4
).

Theorem 4.7
.

There exists an
\alpha>0
such that
m_{i}\geq\exp(\exp(\alpha i))
for all large
i
a.s.

Proof.

Fix some
\epsilon>0
, and denote
\mathbb{P}_{*}(\cdot)=\mathbb{P}\left(\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{f_{m_{i},1}}\right)
, the conditional probability measure based on the information available until the first time
m_{i}
was burnt; thus
\mathcal{F}_{f_{m_{i},1}}
is a
stopped sigma-algebra
.

First, we want to estimate the time difference between the second and the first fires which burnt the next maximum site
m_{i+1}
. Define

A_{i}=\{f_{m_{i+1},2}-f_{m_{i+1},1}\geq 2\epsilon\log m_{i}\}

and recall the definitions of
F_{n}
from Lemma
4.3
,
B_{i}
from (
4.4
) and
C_{i}
from (
4.7
); the event
C_{i}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
, while the event
F_{m_{i}}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable.

On
F_{m_{i}}
, we have
f_{m_{i+1},1}>f_{m_{i},1}\geq(1-\epsilon)\log m_{i}
. Let us now estimate
\mathbb{P}_{*}\left(A_{i}^{c}\mid F_{m_{i}}\right)
. The fire which reached
m_{i+1}
for the first time is also the one which reached sites
x\in(m_{i},m_{i+1}]
for the first time. Recall that
N_{i}
denotes the number of jumps of this fire on these positions; from the proof of part (b) of Lemma
4.6
it follows that with probability
\geq 1-1/i^{2}
the event
C_{i}=\{N_{i}\leq\lfloor c\log i\rfloor\}\subseteq\{e^{\theta N_{i}}\leq i^{c\theta}\}
occurs (we are putting an upper bound on the number of jumps in a fire and this fact did not require Conjecture
4.4
).

For
f_{m_{i+1},2}-f_{m_{i+1},1}
to be smaller than
2\epsilon\log m_{i}
, at least one tree must appear during the time interval
[f_{m_{i+1},1}-\theta N_{i},f_{m_{i+1},1}+2\epsilon\log m_{i}]
at each location
x\in[m_{i},m_{i+1})
(the term
(-\theta N_{i})
corresponds to the fact that there are up to
N_{i}
jumps among these positions), and the probability of this event is at most
\left(1-\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2\epsilon}\,e^{N_{i}\theta}}\right)
for each of these sites, independently of the others. Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{*}(A_{i}^{c}\mid F_{m_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{*}(\text{tree appears at all $x$ for }m_{i}<x\leq m_{i+1}\mid F_{m_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{*}(\text{tree appears at all $x$ for $m_{i}<x\leq m_{i+1}$}\mid B_{i},F_{m_{i}})+\mathbb{P}_{*}(B_{i}^{c}\mid F_{m_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{*}(\text{tree appears at all $x$ for $m_{i}<x\leq m_{i+1}$}\mid B_{i},C_{i},F_{m_{i}})+\mathbb{P}(C_{i}^{c}\mid F_{m_{i}})+\mathbb{P}_{*}(B_{i}^{c}\mid F_{m_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2\epsilon}\,e^{\theta c\log i}}\right)^{m_{i}^{1-\epsilon}}+\frac{1}{i^{2}}+(1-c^{\prime})=\left(1-\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2\epsilon}\,i^{c\theta}}\right)^{m_{i}^{1-\epsilon}}+\frac{1}{i^{2}}+1-c^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{m_{i}^{1-3\epsilon}}{i^{c\theta}}\right\}+1-2c^{\prime\prime}\qquad\text{for large }i

for some
c^{\prime\prime}>0
, since on
B_{i}
we have
m_{i+1}-m_{i}\geq m_{i}^{1-\epsilon}
, and using (
4.5
).

The longer the time interval between the first and second fire at
m_{i+1}
(as implied by the event
A_{i}
), the greater the probability that it will burn at least
m_{i}^{1+\epsilon/2}
additional sites. Hence, if both
F_{m_{i}}
and
A_{i}
occur, the probability that
the second fire to arrive at
m_{i+1}
will also stop before immediately burning additional
m_{i}^{1+\epsilon/2}
sites equals

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{*}(f_{m_{i+1}+k,1}>f_{m_{i+1},2}\text{ for some }k\in[1,m_{i}^{1+\epsilon/2}]\mid F_{m_{i}},A_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{*}(f_{m_{i+1}+1,1}>f_{m_{i+1},2}\mid F_{m_{i}},A_{i})+\sum_{k=2}^{m_{i}^{1+\epsilon/2}}\mathbb{P}_{*}(f_{m_{i+1}+k,1}>f_{m_{i+1},2}\mid F_{m_{i}},A_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{e^{-\theta}}{m_{i}^{2\epsilon}}+\frac{m_{i}^{1+\epsilon/2}}{m_{i}^{1+\epsilon}}\leq\frac{2}{m_{i}^{\epsilon/2}}

(4.10)

(when
m_{i}
is large). The first term in the above inequality corresponds to the probability that a tree appeared at position
m_{i+1}+1
during the time interval

[f_{m_{i+1},1}+\theta,f_{m_{i+1},2}]\supseteq[f_{m_{i+1},1}+\theta,f_{m_{i+1},1}+2\epsilon\log m_{i}],

while the second term corresponds to the event that
at least one
of the locations
m_{i+1}+2,m_{i+1}+3,\dots,m_{i+1}+m_{i}^{1+\epsilon/2}
remains vacant, and we know that the probability of such a site to remain vacant is
\exp\{-(1+\epsilon)\log m_{i}\}=1/m_{i}^{1+\epsilon}
. Since
F_{m_{i}}
is
\mathbb{P}_{*}
-measurable, using (
4.10
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{*}(m_{i+2}-m_{i}\geq m_{i}^{1+\epsilon/2})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{*}(m_{i+2}-m_{i}\geq m_{i}^{1+\epsilon/2},A_{i},F_{m_{i}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{*}(m_{i+2}-m_{i}\geq m_{i}^{1+\epsilon/2}\mid A_{i},F_{m_{i}})\,\mathbb{P}_{*}(A_{i}\mid F_{m_{i}})\,\mathbb{P}_{*}(F_{m_{i}})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{2}{m_{i}^{\epsilon/2}}\right)\cdot\left(2c^{\prime\prime}-\exp\left\{-\frac{m_{i}^{1-3\epsilon}}{2\,i^{c\theta}}\right\}\right)1_{F_{m_{i}}}\geq c^{\prime\prime}\cdot 1_{F_{m_{i}}}\cdot 1_{m_{i}>i^{r}}

for some fixed non-random
r>1
and all large
i
. Now the inequality
m_{i+2}-m_{i}\geq m_{i}^{1+\epsilon/2}
implies

\displaystyle\log m_{i+2}\geq\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\log m_{i}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\log\log m_{i+2}-\log\log m_{i}\geq\log\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)=:\epsilon^{\prime}>0.

(4.11)

Setting
i=2j
,
j=1,2,\dots
, we conclude that we can construct an i.i.d. sequence of Bernoulli(
c^{\prime\prime}
) random variables
\xi_{j}
such that

\log\log m_{2(j+1)}-\log\log{m_{2j}}\geq\epsilon^{\prime}\xi_{j}\cdot 1_{F_{m_{2j}}}\cdot 1_{m_{2j}>(2j)^{r}}.

By Lemma
4.6

m_{i}>i^{r}
for any fixed
r
and all sufficiently large
i
; we also know that the events
F_{m_{i}}^{c}
occur only finitely often a.s. yielding

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\log m_{2k}}{k}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\log\log m_{2j+2}-\log\log m_{2j}\right)}{k}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\epsilon^{\prime}\xi_{j}\cdot 1_{F_{m_{2j}}}\cdot 1_{m_{2j}>(2j)^{r}}}{k}=\epsilon^{\prime}\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\xi_{j}}{k}=\epsilon^{\prime}c^{\prime\prime}>0\qquad\text{a.s.}

(please compare this with the proof of part (a) of Lemma
4.6
) from which it follows that for some
\alpha>0
we have
m_{i}\geq\exp\left\{e^{\alpha i}\right\}
for all sufficiently large
i
a.s.
∎

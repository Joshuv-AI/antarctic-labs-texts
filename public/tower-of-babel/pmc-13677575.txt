Type I interferon upregulates IL-18RAP in neutrophils to enhance chemotaxis and bactericidal activity in autoimmune liver diseases
Authors: Wenwen Jin, Xi Su, Lizhi Liu, Shilin Du, Wenting Xiong, Zifei Zhu, Cunyan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveGiven the established association of interleukin-18 (IL-18) and IL-18 receptor accessory protein (IL-18RAP) with autoimmune liver diseases (AILD), while their specific mechanism remains unclear, this study aimed to investigate the expression and functional significance of IL-18RAP in neutrophils from patients with AILD.MethodsThe expression levels of IL-18RAP were determined by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) and Western blot. Serum type I interferon (IFN) concentrations were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). In vitro cell experiments were conducted to assess chemotactic phagocytosis and to analyze the regulatory effect of IL-18RAP on neutrophil function. In vitro IFN stimulation experiments were performed to analyze the relationship between IL-18RAP expression and type I IFN.ResultsIL-18RAP mRNA and protein expression levels were increased in patients with AILD. After IL-18 stimulation, the chemotaxis index and nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) reduction rate of neutrophils in the AILD group increased (P < 0.05). Serum type I IFN concentrations were elevated in AILD patients (Z = −2.14, P = 0.032). IL-18RAP mRNA expression was upregulated in the group stimulated with high-concentration patient serum and in the human recombinant IFN-α stimulation group, indicating that high concentrations of type I IFN can upregulate IL-18RAP expression in neutrophils.ConclusionElevated IL-18RAP expression in AILD patients enhanced the chemotactic, phagocytic, and bactericidal functions of neutrophils. Higher serum type I IFN levels in AILD patients upregulated IL-18RAP expression in neutrophils, suggesting that type I IFN contributes to the pathogenesis of AILD by upregulating IL-18RAP.

Introduction

Autoimmune liver diseases (AILD) are a group of chronic inflammatory hepatobiliary disorders mediated by abnormal autoimmunity, mainly including primary biliary cholangitis (PBC), autoimmune hepatitis (AIH), primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC), and overlapping syndromes [1]. Neutrophils can cause extensive tissue damage, are continuously recruited to sites of chronic inflammation, and release serine proteases, form neutrophil extracellular traps, and activate other immune cells, thereby promoting the progression of many chronic inflammatory diseases, including atherosclerosis, diabetes, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and autoimmune diseases [2]. Studies have shown that neutrophils are involved in autoimmune liver disease [[3], [4], [5], [6]]: in the early stages of the disease, neutrophils may accumulate and infiltrate the liver, and autoantibodies against neutrophil intracellular proteins are commonly present; the number of infiltrating neutrophils in the bile duct is positively correlated with disease progression and cholestasis. Our previous study found that serum interferon (IFN)-α expression was upregulated in patients with PBC [7]. It has been reported that type I IFN can activate neutrophils, thereby exacerbating chronic inflammation [8]. We speculate that the high expression of type I IFN in patients with AILD contributes to AILD pathogenesis by regulating neutrophils; however, the specific mechanisms remain to be elucidated.

Interleukin-18 (IL-18) is a pro-inflammatory cytokine [9]. IL-18 enhances the expression of adhesion molecules, respiratory burst, and chemokine production in peripheral blood neutrophils through IL-18 receptors (IL-18 R) [10]. The IL-18 R consists of IL-18 Rα and IL-18 Rβ; the latter is also known as Interleukin-18 receptors accessory protein (IL-18RAP). IL-18RAP cannot bind to IL-18, but it is essential for IL-18 signal transduction [9]. IL-18 R is expressed on blood cells and also on non-immune cells such as epithelial cells and nerve cells, participating in cell survival and differentiation. IL-18RAP is mainly expressed on immune cells, including neutrophils [[11], [12], [13]]. Dysregulation of IL-18 levels may contribute to autoimmune and inflammatory diseases and has been associated with conditions such as cancer, allergy, and arthritis [12,14]. Studies have shown that IL-18 and IL-18RAP play important roles in immune regulation and contribute to the development of autoimmune diseases, including inflammatory bowel disease, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, and systemic lupus erythematosus [15,16]. In these conditions, elevated serum IL-18 levels activate immune cells and trigger inflammatory responses that drive disease progression. IL-18 has likewise been found to be upregulated in patients with PBC [17]. In patients with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), upregulated IL-18RAP expression in neutrophils is associated with disease activity and renal involvement, and this upregulation exerts significant pathological effects on lupus progression by mediating reactive oxygen species (ROS) production [18]. This drew our attention, and we believe that IL-18 and IL-18RAP are also involved in the pathogenesis of AILD, which is likewise characterized by neutrophil infiltration.

Neutrophils are involved in the pathogenesis of AILD [3,4,19]. Elevated levels of type I IFN and IL-18 have been detected in patients with PBC [7] [17], and both can activate neutrophils [8] [18]. However, the mechanisms by which these cytokines regulate neutrophil function in AILD remain unclear. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate IL-18RAP expression in neutrophils from patients with AILD, evaluate its impact on neutrophil function, and determine whether type I IFN regulates IL-18RAP expression, thereby providing new insights into the pathogenesis of AILD and a theoretical basis for its diagnosis and treatment.

Materials and methods

Research object

Serum and EDTA-anticoagulated whole blood samples were collected from AILD patients hospitalized at Hunan Provincial People's Hospital between August 2022 and December 2023. Inclusion criteria: AILD patients meet the descriptive diagnostic criteria for AILD, and comply with the Guidelines on the diagnosis and management of autoimmune hepatitis (2021) [20]or the Guidelines on the Diagnosis and Management of Primary Biliary Cholangitis (2021) [21]or the Guidelines on the diagnosis and management of primary sclerosing cholangitis (2021) [22]formulated by the Hepatology Branch of the Chinese Medical Association. Exclusion criteria: (1) Patients with acute and chronic infectious diseases or various immune system diseases such as SLE and Sjogren's syndrome. (2) Diagnosed as AILD combined with alcoholic or non-alcoholic liver disease, liver virus infection. (3) Pregnant or lactating women. (4) Patients who are not first-time hospitalized or treated.

Inclusion criteria for the healthy control (HC) group: age- and sex-matched healthy individuals randomly selected from the Physical Examination Center of Hunan Provincial People's Hospital; those with abnormal physical examination results were excluded. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Declaration of Istanbul, and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Hunan Provincial People's Hospital, which granted a waiver of informed consent.

Neutrophil separation and culture

EDTA-anticoagulated whole blood was layered onto Polymorphprep™ at a 1:1 ratio and centrifuged (500–550 × g, 30 min, 18–22°C). The polymorphonuclear layer was collected, washed with PBS, and treated with RBC lysis buffer if needed. Neutrophils were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% FBS at 37°C under 5% CO2.

Quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from neutrophils using RNAiso Plus. RNA concentration and purity were assessed by A260 and the A260/A280 ratio. Reverse transcription was performed with TaKaRa RR047A, including gDNA Eraser treatment. qPCR was performed using TaKaRa RR820A and TB Green Premix Ex TaqII.

Primers (5′–3′) were: IL-18RAP F, TGAAGAACACTTGGCCCTGA; IL-18RAP R, GCAAGATTCACTGCTGCTTGTT; IFIT1 F, GCAGGCTGTCGCTGCTAAATC; IFIT1 R, CCACAGAGCCTTTTCTTTCGG; GAPDH F, TTCACCACCATGGAGAAGGC; GAPDH R, GATGGCATGGACTGTGGTCA.

The qPCR program was 95°C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 5 s and 60°C for 30 s, with melting curve analysis. GAPDH served as the internal control, and relative expression was calculated by the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Western blot

Neutrophils were lysed in RIPA buffer supplemented with PMSF. Protein concentration was determined by absorbance at 280 nm, and protein purity was assessed by SDS-PAGE. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE, transferred to PVDF membranes, blocked with 5% BSA, and incubated with rabbit anti-human IL-18RAP and anti-GAPDH primary antibodies, followed by HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG. Bands were visualized with ECL and quantified using ImageJ, with GAPDH as the loading control.

Neutrophil transwell chemotaxis assay

Neutrophils (2 × 105) in serum-free RPMI-1640 were added to the upper chambers of 24-well Transwell plates (5-μm pores). The lower chambers contained RPMI-1640 with 20% FBS alone or supplemented with IL-18 (100 ng/mL) or fMLP (100 nM). After incubation at 37°C for 2–3 h, migrated cells were stained with 0.1% crystal violet and counted in five random fields. The chemotaxis index (CI) was calculated as migrated cells toward the test sample divided by migrated cells toward the negative control.

Neutrophil nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) reduction test

Neutrophils (2 × 105/well) were seeded in six-well plates and treated with IL-18 (100 ng/mL, 2 h) or fMLP (100 nM, 1.5 h followed by 30 min). Cells were then incubated with NBT reagent at 37°C in the dark for 30–90 min. The reaction was stopped with ice-cold PBS, and smears were examined microscopically. One hundred neutrophils were counted, and the NBT reduction rate was calculated as the percentage of NBT-positive cells.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Serum samples from AILD patients and healthy controls were collected, and type I IFN concentrations were measured using a commercial ELISA kit according to the manufacturer's instructions. Standards were serially diluted two-fold (starting at 10 pg/mL), and blank, standard, and sample wells were prepared. Samples were diluted 5-fold (40 μL diluent + 10 μL serum). After adding standards or samples, plates were incubated at 37°C in the dark for 30 min and washed. Enzyme conjugate was added, followed by another 37°C incubation and washing. Color was developed with reagents A and B at 37°C for 10 min, stopped with stop solution, and absorbance was read at 450 nm using an ELx808 microplate reader. Type I IFN concentrations were calculated from the standard curve.

In vitro cell stimulation experiment

Healthy neutrophils were stimulated for 6 h with HC serum, low-concentration patient serum (type I IFN < 100 ng/L), high-concentration patient serum (type I IFN ≥ 200 ng/L), or recombinant human IFN-α (200 ng/L). IL-18RAP mRNA expression was then measured by qRT-PCR.

Statistical analysis

Data were processed using SPSS 20.0 statistical software and plotted using Graph Prism 8.3.0. The data were tested for normal distribution, and the measurement data with normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x‾±s). The comparison between two groups was performed using t-tests, and the comparison between multiple groups was performed using analysis of variance. The data with skewed distribution were expressed as median (P25, P75). Mann-Whitney U test was used for comparisons between two groups, and Kruskal-Wallis test was used for comparisons among multiple groups. For categorical data, the chi-square test was used for comparisons between two groups. Spearman correlation analysis was performed, and P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

AILD comprises a group of chronic inflammatory liver diseases with unmet therapeutic needs, and its pathogenesis remains incompletely understood. In this study, we found that both mRNA and protein levels of IL-18RAP were significantly upregulated in neutrophils from AILD patients compared with HCs, suggesting a potential role for IL-18RAP in disease pathogenesis. Previous studies have reported elevated IL-18 levels in PBC patients, which correlate with disease severity [[23], [24], [25]]. As IL-18RAP is indispensable for IL-18 signal transduction [26,27], its upregulation may amplify IL-18 signaling in neutrophils. Mechanistically, IL-18 binding to IL-18 Rα recruits IL-18RAP to form a high-affinity heterotrimeric complex, which activates nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) and activator protein (AP)-1, thereby inducing the production of inflammatory mediators such as adhesion molecules, chemokines, and Fas ligands [13,28]. Additionally, IL-18 can activate the p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway in neutrophils [29,30]. Therefore, elevated IL-18RAP expression in AILD neutrophils may enhance IL-18-driven inflammatory responses and contribute to disease progression.

To assess the functional consequences, we examined neutrophil chemotaxis and bactericidal activity. fMLP, a well-characterized chemoattractant, was used as a positive control [31]. Both the AILD and HC groups exhibited increased chemotaxis and NBT reduction after fMLP stimulation, confirming that neutrophils from both sources were functionally competent. Notably, IL-18 stimulation significantly increased the chemotactic index and NBT reduction rate exclusively in neutrophils from AILD patients, whereas no significant changes were observed in the HC group. These findings indicate that IL-18 specifically enhances the chemotactic, phagocytic, and oxidative burst functions of AILD neutrophils, likely due to their elevated IL-18RAP expression. The NBT reduction assay reflects NADPH oxidase activity and the generation of reactive oxygen species, which are critical for microbial killing but also contribute to tissue damage when produced excessively. In AILD, immune cells including neutrophils infiltrate around bile ducts, and neutrophil-derived reactive oxygen species and proteases may propagate biliary injury [32,33]. Thus, IL-18RAP-mediated hyper-responsiveness of neutrophils to IL-18 may promote the recruitment and activation of these cells within the liver, fueling local inflammation and tissue destruction.

Our results further demonstrated that serum type I IFN concentrations were significantly higher in AILD patients than in healthy controls, consistent with previous reports [34]. IFIT1, a prototypical IFN-stimulated gene [35,36], was also significantly upregulated in AILD neutrophils, and its expression was positively correlated with IL-18RAP mRNA levels. In vitro experiments confirmed that both high-concentration patient serum (type I IFN ≥ 200 ng/L) and recombinant human IFN-α directly upregulated IL-18RAP expression in healthy neutrophils. These data support the hypothesis that elevated type I IFN in AILD patients drives the upregulation of IL-18RAP on neutrophils. It was known that upregulation of type I IFN could activate NF-κB, which in turn induced the release of inflammatory cytokines and thereby led to autoimmunity [37,38]. It could also activate innate immune cells and regulate adaptive immunity. In immune-mediated inflammatory diseases, it promoted the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps and sustains the inflammatory cycle [[39], [40], [41]]. We therefore propose a model in which increased type I IFN in AILD upregulates IL-18RAP on neutrophils, thereby sensitizing them to IL-18. Given that IL-18 levels are also elevated in these patients, the combination of high IL-18 and high IL-18RAP may synergistically enhance neutrophil effector functions, contributing to the vicious cycle of inflammation and autoimmunity characteristic of AILD.

This study has limitations. The mechanistic link between type I IFN, IL-18RAP, and neutrophil function was inferred from correlation and in vitro stimulation experiments, and direct in vivo evidence is lacking; serum IL-18 was not measured, although previous reports support its elevation in AILD. In addition, eligible AILD samples were rare and difficult to collect, and the residual blood after routine clinical testing yielded too few neutrophils to perform both protein and nucleic acid assays (e.g., Western blot and qRT-PCR) in the same sample. Moreover, no diagnostic cutoff value or ROC curve analysis for IL-18RAP in AILD was established in this study. Larger multicenter studies with sufficient cell yields and functional blockade of IL-18RAP or type I IFN signaling are needed to confirm causality.

In conclusion, to the best of our knowledge, this work provides the first mechanistic evidence linking elevated type I IFN, increased IL-18RAP expression, and augmented neutrophil effector functions in AILD. The data indicate that elevated serum type I IFN in AILD promotes IL-18RAP upregulation on neutrophils; in the presence of high IL-18, this upregulation further strengthens chemotaxis, phagocytosis, and oxidative burst, thereby facilitating neutrophil recruitment and tissue inflammation. This previously unappreciated crosstalk between type I IFN signaling and the IL-18/IL-18RAP pathway offers new insight into AILD immunopathogenesis and nominates the type I IFN/IL-18RAP axis as a candidate for future diagnostic and therapeutic development.

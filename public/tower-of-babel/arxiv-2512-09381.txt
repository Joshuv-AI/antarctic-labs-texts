Finite axiomatization of GL × S5 \textbf{GL}\times\textbf{S5} and Grz × S5 \textbf{Grz}\times\textbf{S5}
G. Bezhanishvili, M. Khan

Abstract
. We prove that 𝐆𝐋 × 𝐒𝟓 \mathbf{GL}\times\mathbf{S5} is product matching, and that 𝐆𝐫𝐳 × 𝐒𝟓 \mathbf{Grz}\times\mathbf{S5} is axiomatizable by adding to [ 𝐆𝐫𝐳 , 𝐒𝟓 ] [\mathbf{Grz},\mathbf{S5}] the Gödel translation of the monadic Casari formula. This settles the question of finite axiomatization for two of the product logics listed in [ 14 , p. 137] .

1.
Introduction

The notion of fibring or combining logics arises naturally in a range of applications such as temporalization
[
11
]
, modalization
[
10
]
, formal representation of practical reasoning
[
15
]
, software specification
[
8
]
, etc.
A prominent example of combining logics is the
product
of modal logics (see, e.g.,
[
12
, p. 126]
), which we refer to as
product logics
.
Since these are defined semantically, it is a nontrivial matter to axiomatize them, and some product logics are not even recursively enumerable (see, e.g.,
[
12
, Cor. 7.14,7.16]
). In spite of this, product logics have received considerable attention in the literature in both pure and applied contexts (see, e.g.,

[
12
]
and the references therein).

Our interest in product logics stems from their connection to predicate modal logic. For a propositional modal logic
L
, let
QLB
denote the least predicate extension of
L
with the
Barcan formula
. In some cases, the product logc
\textbf{L}\times\textbf{S5}
axiomatizes the one-variable fragment of
QLB
(see, e.g.,
[
14
, Thm. 13.8]
or
[
12
, Thm. 3.21]
).
Because of this, we restrict our attention to products
of the form
\textbf{L}\times\textbf{S5}
, where
L
is a Kripke complete propositional modal logic.
Products of this form are relatively well-behaved.
For example, if
L
is
Horn axiomatizable
,
there is a convenient finite axiomatization of
\textbf{L}\times\textbf{S5}
(see, e.g.,
[
12
, Thm. 5.9]
).
Prominent examples of modal logics that are
not
Horn axiomatizable are the Gödel-Löb logic
GL
and the Grzegorczyk logic
Grz
,
the frames of which are not even first-order definable (see, e.g.,
[
7
, Thm. 6.7, 6.8]
). It is natural to seek a finite axiomatization for
\textbf{GL}\times\textbf{S5}
and
\textbf{Grz}\times\textbf{S5}
. Indeed, this was left as an open problem in
[
14
, p. 137]
.

A convenient framework to study products with
S5
, and more generally expanding relativized products with
S5
(see
[
12
, p. 432]
),
is the formalism of
monadic modal logic
, which we refer to as
mm-logics
(see, e.g.,
[
2
, Def. 2.13]
). We can view product and expanding product logics as special mm-logics, with product logics characterized in particular by the presence of the
monadic Barcan formula
. In some cases, the minimal monadic extension
ML
of a propositional modal logic
L
and the monadic Barcan formula may suffice to axiomatize
\textbf{L}\times\textbf{S5}
(for example, when
L
is Horn axiomatizable). When this occurs, the pair
(\textbf{L},\textbf{S5})
is said to be
product matching
(see, e.g.,
[
12
, p. 223]
).
Thus, letting
MLB
denote the monadic extension of
L
together with the monadic Barcan formula, the pair
(\textbf{L},\textbf{S5})
is product matching iff
\textbf{MLB}=\textbf{L}\times\textbf{S5}
.

It is well known that
(\textbf{Grz},\textbf{S5})
is not product matching
[
12
, Thm. 5.17]
. In contrast, we prove that
{\textbf{GL}\times\textbf{S5}}
is product matching, while
\textbf{Grz}\times\textbf{S5}
can be axiomatized by adding to
MGrzB
the Gödel translation of the monadic Casari formula (in analogy with
[
2
, p. 443]
, we denote the resulting system by
\textbf{M}^{+}\textbf{GrzB}
).
We establish these results by showing that the systems
MGLB
and
\textbf{M}^{+}\textbf{GrzB}
have the finite model property (fmp for short). This, in turn, is done by first establishing the fmp for
MGrzB
, and then adjusting the proof accordingly for
\textbf{M}^{+}\textbf{GrzB}
and
MGLB
. The fmp for
MGrzB
is obtained by an appropriate modification of the selective filtration strategy developed in
[
3
]
(which in turn draws on

[
16
,
12
,
2
]
) to prove the fmp for
MGrz
.
The additional requirements imposed by the monadic Barcan formula may force the selection process to produce an infinite refutation frame. However, our key observation is that its depth remains bounded. In light of local tabularity of the logics
\textbf{MGrz}[n]
and
\textbf{MGL}[n]
of finite depth
(see
[
5
, Sec. 4.10, 4.11]
), this is sufficient to establish the fmp for
MGrzB
(as well as for
\textbf{M}^{+}\textbf{GrzB}
and
MGLB
), thus settling the questions raised at the end of
[
3
, Sec. 8]
.

Consequently, we obtain that

\textbf{MGLB}=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\textbf{MGLB}[n]

and
\textbf{M}^{+}\textbf{GrzB}=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\textbf{M}^{+}\textbf{GrzB}[n].

In addition, we observe that
\textbf{MGLB}[n]=\textbf{GL}[n]\times\textbf{S5}
and
\textbf{M}^{+}\textbf{GrzB}[n]=\textbf{Grz}[n]\times\textbf{S5}
for each
n\geq 1
. The latter is shown by proving that the
embedding
\textbf{Grz}[n]\hookrightarrow\textbf{GL}[n]
lifts to an embedding
\textbf{M}^{+}\textbf{GrzB}[n]\hookrightarrow\textbf{MGLB}[n]
.
This, together with the above, yields that the embedding
\textbf{Grz}\hookrightarrow\textbf{GL}
also lifts to an embedding
\textbf{M}^{+}\textbf{GrzB}\hookrightarrow\textbf{MGLB}
. As a consequence, we obtain that
{\textbf{GL}\times\textbf{S5}}=\textbf{MGLB}
and
\textbf{Grz}\times\textbf{S5}=\textbf{M}^{+}\textbf{GrzB}
.

In

.

[

For a Kripke complete propositional modal logic

[

For a propositional modal logic

[

Recalling the

\Diamond\exists xP(x)\to\exists x\Diamond P(x)

, and

In general, for a Kripke complete propositional modal logic

that

[

Definition 3.7
.

[

A Kripke complete unimodal logic

\psi(x,y,\vec{z})

The following

Theorem 3.8
.

[

If
\textbf{L}_{1}

\textbf{L}\times\textbf{S5}

Remark 3.9

3.8

Numerical investigation of MHD stratified flow over an inclined cylinder with Brownian motion, thermophoresis and waste discharge concentration effects
Authors: Khadija Rafique, Zafar Mahmood, Assmaa Abd-Elmonem, Nagat A A Suoliman, Ioan-Lucian Popa, Abhinav Kumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractComprehending the interaction of transport phenomena across inclined cylinder is essential for enhancing engineering systems such as heat exchangers, pollution dispersion mechanisms, and bioreactors, where fluid flow, heat transfer, and mass transport are interconnected. This study examines flow, heat, and mass transfer with entropy generation in bioconvection nanofluid flow over an inclined cylinder, considering the influences of thermal radiation, mass suction, magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), Joule heating, viscous dissipation, heat absorption, Brownian motion, thermophoresis, discharge concentration, and stratification phenomena. The system of partial differential equations is rehabilitated into system of non-linear ordinary differential equations by sufficient transformations. Keller box technique is therefore an implicit finite difference strategy used numerically to solve similarity equations. This research carefully examines the impact of several dimensionless factors on velocity, temperature, concentration, entropy production, skin friction, Nusselt number, Sherwood number, and microbiological density profiles. A detailed parametric study demonstrates that inclined geometry intensifies axial gravity forces and alters boundary layer dynamics, improving velocity profiles at steeper angles () and facilitating magnetic forces (). Thermal profiles demonstrate dual dependencies: viscous dissipation (Ec) and radiation (Rd) increase temperatures, whilst stratification (δ) and heat absorption (H) decrease them. The interactions of nanoparticles underscore a trade-off: Brownian motion (Nb) disperses particles, reducing concentration, while enhancing thermal conductivity and increasing temperature; conversely, thermophoresis (Nt) concentrates particles and heat at the surface. The number of microbes increases by about 327.3% when the Péclet number goes from 0.5 to 2.0. The Sherwood number goes up by around 436.3% and the Nusselt number goes up by about 51.7% as the Brownian motion parameter goes from 0.2 to 0.8. When the thermophoresis parameter goes from 0.1 to 0.4, the Sherwood number goes down by 57.4% and the Nusselt number goes down by 2.1%.Keywords: Nanoliquid, Stratification, Heat and mass transfer, Bioconvection, Entropy generation, Thermal radiation, Finite difference techniqueSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Researchers and investigators have recently given nanotechnology a lot of consideration. The chemical, microelectronic, engineering, hybrid-powered engine, and biological processes that include it are the reasons for this. To make regular liquids conduct heat better, nanomaterials are basically tiny particles (between 1 and 100 nm) evenly mixed in a regular liquid1. Nanoliquids possess distinct features that make them more suitable as materials. Nanomaterials possess novel properties that facilitate heat transmission. Buongiorno2 provided a theoretical framework for improving the heat transfer rate of standard liquids. He emphasized that the primary methods for improving thermal transportation are solely random and thermophoresis diffusions. Nanomaterials play a crucial role in enhancing the thermal efficiency of hybrid power engines, electronic gadgets, nuclear system chillers, residential refrigerators, and several other applications. The interaction of nanoparticles with fluids is garnering significant attention in nanotechnology research. The objective of Ramesh et al.3 study is to investigate the performance of a ternary nanofluid on a slippery surface. The energy equation elucidates the heat source/sink phenomenon. The essay introduces suction, slide effects, and convective boundary conditions at the wall as unique features. Obalalu and others4 examine how a magnetic dipole affects the changing flow of nanofluid with both uniform and varied reactions, driven by a curved stretched sheet under specific conditions of slip and melting heat. The primary aim of the work by Abbas et al.5 is to examine the effect of nonlinear thermal radiation on hybrid nanoparticles on a flat plate, influenced by an angled magnetic field and viscous dissipation.

In environmental and industrial systems, stratification, which is marked by spatial differences in temperature, concentration, or density, significantly affects natural convection and transport processes. Density changes in thermally stratified media help control vertical fluid motion, which in turn alters heat and mass transfer rates. Solutal stratification, caused by concentration gradients, similarly modulates species transport and reaction kinetics. Haq et al.6 want to evaluate the efficacy of fluids to enhance the efficiency of contemporary equipment. They construct the momentum equation by incorporating the effects of Darcy-Forchheimer flow, permeability, and magnetic fields. The thermal field equation incorporates thermal radiation, intermolecular friction forces, and Joule heating effects. The impact of thermal and solutal stratifications at the cylinder’s boundary is also taken into account. In their study, Liang et al.7 used a two-dimensional axisymmetric computational fluid dynamics (CFD) model to examine how geysers and icing affect the thermal stratification inside the tank. The rocket’s fuel tank and its vertical tunnel tube are both chosen. Sankari et al.8 conducted an examination of the impact of magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) on drift, heat, and mass transport in a Jeffery nanoparticle liquid. The research incorporates transfer equations that account for the effects of thermophoresis and Brownian motion on particle deposition. The research investigates the influence of a nanofluid on a porous, exponentially elongating sheet, emphasizing the double-stratification effects on thermal and mass transmission. Using a Maxwell nanofluid flowing across an inclined stretching sheet, Khan et al.9 investigated the effects of nonlinear stratification and nonlinear mixed convection. There were quadratic mixed convection effects, a magnetic field that was inclined, a radiative heat flow, and convective boundary conditions.

Heat and mass transport processes are fundamental to energy systems, chemical reactors, and biological activities. In nanoliquid flows, these processes are intensified by nanoparticle migration, Brownian motion, and thermophoresis, which improve heat diffusion and species transfer. Mahabaleshwara et al.14 analyze ternary nanofluid flow with heat and mass transfer over a Riga plate, including the effects of Newtonian heating. The heat source increases the rate of heat transfer between a solid surface and fluid flow, whereas the reverse tendency occurs with a heat sink. Increasing values of the chemical reaction parameter enhance the mass transfer rate. Effect of curvature and inclined angle parameters

The greater centrifugal forces caused by the surface geometry cause the radial pressure gradient to be more pronounced as the curvature parameter rises. The flow encounters increased radial acceleration compressing the boundary layer towards the surface as the curvature gets more pronounced, that is, with decreasing cylinder radius. This compression results in a diminished boundary layer thickness, yielding a more pronounced velocity gradient near the wall. As a result, the wall shear stress escalates, leading to an augmentation in the magnitude of the skin friction coefficient. The findings unequivocally demonstrate that increased values of enhance the contact between the surface and the fluid, thereby augmenting the skin friction. The axial component of gravity acting in the flow direction becomes increasingly important as the cylinder’s inclination angle rises from to . Gravitational help speeds the fluid close to the surface, therefore improving momentum transfer in the boundary layer. At the wall, the resultant velocity profile becomes sharper, which raises the velocity gradient and hence the wall shear stress. The findings show that growing increases the streamwise flow momentum, which causes a steady increase in the size of the skin friction coefficient.

Effect of magnetic and mass suction parameters

The application of a magnetic field produces a Lorentz force that counteracts the movement of the electrically conductive fluid. This resistive force not only attenuates the overall fluid velocity but also alters the momentum equilibrium near the wall. The fluid reacts by creating more defined velocity profiles inside the boundary layer to maintain flow continuity and momentum transfer. The augmentation of the velocity gradient at the wall elevates the wall shear stress. The data indicate a distinct pattern of escalating skin friction magnitude with enhanced magnetic field intensity, demonstrating the significant shear produced to counteract magnetic resistance. On the other hand, mass suction through the cylinder surface draws fluid from the boundary layer, so thinning it and eliminating low-momentum fluid next to the wall. This thinning sharpens the velocity profile at the surface, hence increasing the velocity gradient. Suction causes an increase in skin friction as wall shear stress is exactly proportional to this gradient. The findings verify that when rises, the flow clings more closely to the surface with lower boundary layer thickness, hence generating a significant increase in the size of the skin friction coefficient.

Effect of thermal stratification () and Eckert number () parameters

Thermodynamic stratification reduces the upward forces that help heat move through the fluid by forming layers of different temperatures. The suppression of vertical mixing, which decreases the gradients in the thermal boundary layer, causes the thermal resistance to rise as increases. The result is a lower Nusselt number due to a smaller wall temperature differential.

The Eckert number measures the impact of viscous dissipation in fluid flow. Higher Eckert number values show that more kinetic energy is being changed into internal energy due to viscous heating, which raises the temperature of the fluid. This process diminishes the temperature differential between the surface and the fluid, attenuating the thermal gradient at the wall and decreasing the conductive heat transfer rate, which leads to a reduced Nusselt number.

Effect of Grashof number () and inclination angle () parameters

The Grashof number quantifies buoyancy effects resulting from heat gradients. While buoyancy often enhances convective transport, in this scenario, a heightened Grashof number thickens the thermal boundary layer and displaces the temperature gradients from the wall. This lowers the local heat transfer rate from the surface, leading to a reduction in the Nusselt number despite augmented buoyant forces. The component of buoyancy-driven flow changes with surface inclination in relation to gravity. The gravitational component along the surface becomes stronger as the inclination angle rises, thereby improving natural convection. This leads to quicker heat transfer and a higher Nusselt number by making the thermal boundary layer gradient near the surface steeper and encouraging more flow circulation.

Effect of heat absorption () and curvature () parameters

A negative heat absorption coefficient indicates internal heat creation in the fluid. As the intensity of this negative value increases, more thermal energy is introduced to the system, enhancing the local temperature gradient next to the wall. This process amplifies the temperature gradient and enhances convective heat transport, leading to a higher Nusselt number. The curvature parameter illustrates the geometric impact of the cylinder arrangement. Increasing increases the centrifugal effects and radial stretching, which hasten the flow and thin the thermal boundary layer. This flow intensification close to the wall sharpens the temperature gradient, hence improving heat transfer and raising the Nusselt number.

Effect of Brownian motion (), thermophoresis () and thermal radiation parameters

The Brownian motion parameter refers to the small random movements of nanoparticles, which improve thermal conductivity by increasing thermal diffusion. As increases, the better energy transfer from particle collisions helps mix the heat more effectively, increasing the temperature difference at the wall and raising the Nusselt number. Thermophoresis, in contrast, is a process that moves nanoparticles from warmer to cooler areas. Raising encourages particles to migrate, which in turn may redirect heat away from the hot surface. Because the wall’s thermal gradient is now less steep, the local heat flow is lower, and the Nusselt number is lower as a result. Thermal radiation increases the total heat transfer capability of the medium, particularly in flows that participate in radiation. As rises, the effective thermal conductivity of the fluid increases due to radiative energy transfer. This increases the temperature gradient next to the wall, resulting in improved conductive heat transfer and an elevated Nusselt number.

Conclusions

This study quantitatively examines the boundary layer flow and entropy production of magnetohydrodynamic bio-convection nanofluid around an inclined cylinder, including discharge concentration, thermal radiation, and stratification effects. The impact of several critical factors, including the dimensionless mass suction parameter, Brownian motion parameter, thermophoresis parameter, and Schmidt number on the momentum, temperature, and concentration boundary layers, is evaluated. Additionally, the impact of the local Nusselt number, local Sherwood number, microorganism density number and skin friction coefficient next to the cylinder’s surface is assessed both visually and statistically.

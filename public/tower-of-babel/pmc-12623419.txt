Computational analysis on the influence of pressure and temperature on drug solubility in supercritical CO2 with machine learning and optimizer
Authors: Ahmad J. Obaidullah, Wael A. Mahdi, Adel Alhowyan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Machine learning models can be applied for estimation of continuous manufacturing parameters in pharmaceutical processing of oral-solid formulations. Development of Quality by Design (QbD) has motivated the pharmaceutical sector to move towards continuous manufacturing by developing advanced computational models as well as analytical techniques. Despite the application of conventional methods, supercritical fluids (SCFs) have offered a new way for particle generation for nanonization and advanced manufacturing. In this work, the solubility of Letrozole was evaluated using temperature and pressure correlated to machine learning. KNN (K-Nearest Neighbors) and two boosted versions employing AdaBoost and bagging ensemble models are used. Golden eagle optimizer (GEOA) was applied as optimizer and tweaking hyper-parameters. Once the models were optimized, they were assessed using various performance metrics. The R-squared scores achieved were 0.9907 for KNN, 0.9945 for AdaBoost-KNN, and 0.9938 for Bagging-KNN. Among these, the AdaBoost-KNN model demonstrated the highest accuracy.

Introduction

Significant developments in improving the solubility/ bioavailability of therapeutic medicines with poor solubility play a momentous role in pharmaceutical industry because of complicated problems associated with pharmaceutical solubility1,2. The oral administration of therapeutic drugs is of great attraction compared to intravenous, intramuscular and subcutaneous ways due to its different advantages and safety3. By the oral administration of a specific drug, it needs to be dissolved in the gastrointestinal fluid to facilitate the penetration through the bloodstream4. As such, solubility is the limiting step and enhancement of solubility would improve drugs bioavailability.

Over the recent decades, supercritical fluids (SCFs) have been introduced as an innovative approach for the generation of particles in both micro and nano scales. Thus, disparate scientific efforts are recently being conducted to use the SCFs to increase the bioavailability of therapeutic drugs possessing low aqueous solubility3,5. SCFs (especially CO2-SCF) have attracted the attentions of many researchers in the scientific scope of pharmacology owing to eco-friendliness, low toxicity, inflammability, affordability and the generation of low waste during operation6,7. The method of SCFs can operate via different techniques such as rapid expansion which produces drug particles with nanosized and improved solubility because of higher surface energy8,9. The operation of rapid expansion is based on dissolution of drug in the solvent (supercritical state) and then releasing the pressure during the process to simply remove the solvent and isolate the drug particles. As such, the first step which is the limiting step is dissolving of drug in the solvent, while drugs with poor solubility in SCFs cannot be processed by this technique.

Letrozole (LET) is an aromatase inhibitor medication, orally administered for the treatment of patients suffering from early-stage breast cancer in women who have experienced menopause10,11. This promising medication, which was approved in 1996 is the 257th prevalently prescribed drug in the United States12. Due to its poor water solubility, this medication can be assessed for operation by SCF to enhance its solubility by reducing the size of LET particles.

In recent years, significant cost of experimental investigations has encouraged the researchers to employ various predictive mathematical models to evaluate the solubilities of orally-administered drugs based on artificial intelligence (AI). Empirical, semi-empirical and equations of state are known as the most commonly applied predictive models in recent years13,14. These mathematical-based approaches possess great potential of application to predict the experimental outcomes of drugs solubility in CO2-SCF over a wide range of temperature and pressure15,16.

In various scientific fields, traditional computing methods are being gradually phased out and machine learning (ML) methods are taking their place. Neighbor-based models, ensemble models, Tree-based models, and kernel-based models are some of the problem-solving strategies that are included here. Models of ML can now be applied to the investigation of a wide variety of problems, each of which has several input attributes and several output values. These methods allow for the derivation of some relationships between the inputs and the outcomes.

Among the most used ML methods are k-nearest neighbors and its different versions. The key concept behind k-nearest neighbors, also known as KNN model, is that it makes use of the similarity between the attributes of the input data in order to generate predictions based on other points that are most comparable to the initial point. To be more specific, it remembers all of the training data even when it’s in the testing phase17,18. In this study we used KNN and also their improved versions using bagging and boosting methods.

Some useful methods for enhancing regression models are bagging and boosting. Breiman19, introduced a simple ensemble method called Bagging, which shows remarkable performance. The bootstrap approach, which iteratively creates and duplicates small sections of training data, increases the algorithm’s diversity. The majority-vote approach is used to build the final prediction results from the subsets after each one is used to fit a unique basic estimator19,20.

Boosting is another ensemble model that builds on the research of Freund and Schapire21. It’s different from Bagging in that it produces a more heterogeneous group of novice learners by gradually reweighting the training data. In the next training step, we’ll give more weight to the samples that the previous estimator underestimated. As a result, training instances with higher prediction errors are more frequently selected in later iterations, enabling the model to effectively reduce bias. In the final Boosting model, each base estimator is assigned a weight based on its predictive performance22.

This paper aims to mathematically estimate the solubility of LET in an extensive range of temperature and pressure through CO2-SCF system. To reach this goal, KNN and two upgraded versions (AdaBoost and bagging ensemble) models were employed.

Methodology

The dataset used in this study, originally described in the subsection 2 − 1, consists of 45 data points detailing Letrozole solubility under varying temperature and pressure, and serves as the basis for all model training and evaluation. In this research, a general framework for testing, optimizing and evaluating the mentioned models, i.e., KNN and two ensemble models based on it, has been considered. Figure 1 shows the general architecture of this framework. In the normalization step, the data are normalized with the help of the min-max scalar so that the larger change interval of one of the two inputs does not have a greater and abnormal effect on the final result.

Then, in order to remove the outliers, Isolation Forest method was used, which will be examined in the following. As the last pre-processing, the data is divided into two parts, training and testing. In this study, a ratio of 8 to 2 was chosen for this division.

Finally, the selected models were fine-tuned using the Golden Eagle Optimizer (GEOA), which was employed to optimize their hyperparameters. The resulting models were then evaluated on test data. This optimization approach represents a key novelty of the present study.

All computational analyses and machine learning experiments were implemented in Python 3.11. Several well-established scientific libraries were employed to facilitate modeling and visualization: scikit-learn (sklearn) for machine learning algorithms and performance evaluation, NumPy for numerical operations, and Matplotlib for data visualization and graphical representations. These software packages were selected for their stability, widespread adoption in scientific research, and strong support for reproducibility.

Within the methodology, the dataset was first pre-processed using NumPy for array manipulation and normalization procedures. Outlier detection (Isolation Forest) and machine learning regression models (KNN, Bagging-KNN, AdaBoost-KNN) were implemented using scikit-learn, ensuring a standardized framework for model training, hyperparameter optimization, and evaluation. Performance metrics including R², RMSE, MAE, and Max Error were also calculated through scikit-learn’s built-in modules. Finally, Matplotlib was used to generate the comparative plots and 2D/3D visualizations of predicted versus experimental solubility values, allowing for graphical interpretation of model performance and trends.

This combination of open-source, peer-reviewed software tools provides transparency and reproducibility in the modeling process, enabling independent verification and potential extension of the results in future studies.

Golden eagle optimizer (GEOA)

Golden eagle optimizer (GEOA)24 is used in this study for hyper-parameter tuning. GEOA is a nature inspired meta-heuristic algorithm with advantages over traditional methods. The process begins with an initial randomly created group and emulates the predatory tactics of golden eagles to improve the population’s overall fitness and find the best solution. Drawing inspiration from the eagle’s natural ability to glide, spiral, and remember prey positions, GEOA represents this conduct using mathematical formulas that mimic both the soaring and strike trajectories, efficiently balancing exploration and exploitation during optimization challenges24,25.

The GEOA was chosen over other metaheuristic algorithms for its effective balance of exploration and exploitation, mimicking golden eagle hunting behavior to efficiently navigate complex optimization spaces. Compared to traditional optimizers like Genetic Algorithms or Particle Swarm Optimization, GEOA offers faster convergence and higher-quality solutions for hyper-parameter tuning of the models like KNN in this study. Its dynamic search strategy, driven by cruise and attack phases, enhances robustness and adaptability. The GEOA configuration used includes a population size of 47, maximum iterations of 95, cruise factor (α) of 0.53, attack factor (β) of 0.32, exploration weight of 0.68, exploitation weight of 0.32, and a mutation rate of 0.1, providing a balanced framework for optimizing model performance.

Bagging

Individual estimators (regressors) are commonly used machine learning methods such as KNN that are formed by procedures from whole training data. Single estimators, have their own limits and bottlenecks. In order to achieve superior results, Bagging Ensemble Regression combines the estimates from several individual estimators28.

This is due to the fact that the calculations used by the Bagging Ensemble Regression model include an application of the idea of variance-covariance-bias decomposition. The square error is broken down into three different terms using this particular form of decomposition. In the intervening period, the square error is decreased by taking advantage of the covariance term in conjunction with multiple separate learners, which results in a lower overall error rate29,30. In the Bagging approach, the individual learners are referred to as the basis learners. The process of the Bagging method can be simplified into this three steps30,31:

The ultimate result is obtained by averaging the results of all base estimators.

Figure 2 illustrates the architecture of the bagging ensemble method, emphasizing its parallel nature. In this approach, multiple KNN models are trained independently on distinct subsets of the training data, generated through bootstrap sampling. Each model operates simultaneously, learning from its unique data subset. The last prediction is derived by aggregating the outputs of these individual models, via averaging, which reduces variance and enhances accuracy. This parallel training structure leverages computational efficiency and model diversity to improve overall performance.

Boosting

In boosting, every single dataset point is used for base estimators. To start, the model gives equal importance (weight) to each sample of the dataset. Then, on each iteration, more weight is assigned to the data points with inaccurate predictions. The final solution is the weighted average of all base estimators32. In this work we used AdaBoost for boosting KNN model.

AdaBoost, by changing the weight distribution, functions as a boosting ensemble method for amplifying the accuracy of base estimators33,34. Due to its capabilities, this technique has grown in popularity35,36.

This strategy begins with a weak model (the weak learner), and then gradually and consistently combines the weak learner and the other models to generate a trustworthy system capable of dealing with complicated scenarios37,38. These stages outline the AdaBoost algorithm in general39–41:

Isolation forest

The Isolation Forest (IF) method is used to detect outliers by assigning an isolation score to each data point. The model constructs tree structures by recursively applying random splits on selected features, effectively isolating anomalous instances from the rest of the data45.

The manner in which the Isolation Forest algorithm handles information is the key contributor to its usefulness. Instead of performing an analysis on the complete dataset, it employs a decision tree to find abnormalities in the data, hence reducing the amount of time and storage space required46. The model is grid-divided so that it may be cut up into the required number of parts for the subsampling that the Isolation Forest technique performs47.

Figures 7, 8, and 9 render three-dimensional (3D) illustrations to compare the performance of KNN, Adaboost KNN and Bagging KNN to determine the roles of pressure and temperature on the LET solubility inside CO2-SCF. Moreover, Figs. 10 and 11 show the two-dimensional (2D) schemes, which individually evaluate the influence of the above-mentioned operational factors (temperature and pressure) on the solubility of LET to optimize the operational conditions. All 2D and 3D figures show a very similar trend in LET solubility variations which is attributed to the small and narrow dataset. The variations of LET solubility with P & T as demonstrated by 2D and 3D plots show agreement with the analysis of drug solubility by ML reported in other studies which can be used as benchmark for confirmation of the outcomes achieved in this research48–51. Indeed, both P and T have sharp effects on increasing the drug solubility in SCF.

As might be expected, increase in the values of pressure led to a major increment in the LET solubility owing to enhancing the solvent density and therefore, the solvating strength in the CO2-SCF system16. The effect of temperature on the solubility of LET is more complicated. In one side, by increasing temperature, the LET sublimation pressure increases significantly, which eventuates in improving the solubility in the system. In another side, increase in the temperature decreases the density and thereby declines the solubility of LET in the CO2-SCF system. Therefore, optimization of the process temperature can be an appropriate technique to reach the maximum solubility of drugs in the CO2-SCF system. The analysis revealed that the cross-over pressure (COP) plays a key role in determining whether temperature has a positive or negative influence on Letrozole solubility52. When pressure exceeds the COP, the increase in sublimation pressure with temperature outweighs the decrease in solvent density, leading to higher solubility. Conversely, at pressures below the COP, the reduction in solvent density dominates, causing solubility to decrease as temperature rises in the CO₂-SCF system. It should be pointed out that in CO₂-SCF processing, the best operational point is not at the highest P & T as increasing pressure and temperature would enhance the energy cost and consequently the processing and maintenance costs. Thus, economic analysis is required to find the best operating conditions to minimize the processing cost and maximize LET solubility in the solvent.

Conclusion

The solubility of Letrozole in supercritical CO₂ was modeled using temperature and pressure as input variables, employing KNN and its enhanced variants—AdaBoost and Bagging ensemble models. The models were optimized using the GEOA for hyperparameter tuning. Among the models tested, the AdaBoost-enhanced KNN (ADA-KNN) model showed the highest accuracy within the scope of this dataset, achieving an R-squared value of 0.9945 and error rates of 0.0606 (MAE), 0.0698 (RMSE), and 0.0997 (Max Error). The KNN and Bagging-enhanced KNN (BAG-KNN) models also performed well, with R-squared values of 0.9907 and 0.9938, respectively. However, given the limited dataset size, these results should be interpreted with caution and are specific to the conditions of this study. With only 45 data points, the models may not fully capture the broader variability of the system, and small fluctuations in the data can disproportionately affect the performance metrics. Therefore, further validation on larger and more diverse datasets is necessary to confirm the generalizability of the findings. While the ADA-KNN model was the best-performing among those tested, further validation with larger datasets would be necessary to assess its broader applicability. Nonetheless, the findings highlight the potential of ensemble methods, particularly AdaBoost, in enhancing predictive accuracy for solubility estimation in supercritical CO₂ systems.

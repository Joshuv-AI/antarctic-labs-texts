Entity relationship extraction method based on dependency parsing and graph neural networks
Authors: Fupeng Wei, Xing Liu, Limin Pan, Wen Zhao, Ge Shi, Zhaohui Zeng, Yibo Jiao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo support campus security governance, especially campus traffic safety management, many ternary extraction techniques in knowledge graphs rely on character-level text analysis; however, the differences between word semantics and overall word meanings often lead to ambiguity and overlap in ternary extraction. Traditional methods are difficult to effectively manage overlapping relationships in text, which seriously damages the flexibility and extraction accuracy of the dataset. In addition, entity separation significantly affects entity relationship triplet extraction, and weak associations between remote entities often blur entity boundaries and reduce recall rates. This study offers the MGRel entity relationship extraction model, which integrates dependent syntactic analysis with a graph neural network to address the issues above and enhance knowledge acquisition for campus security scenarios. Firstly, by incorporating the dependent syntactic parser alongside the dual analysis mechanism of global semantic dependency and syntactic dependency, it effectively captures long-distance semantic associations and enhances entity relationship recognition accuracy; secondly, it devises the architecture of a hierarchical semantic graph convolutional neural network to facilitate the fine-grained extraction of deep implied semantic features among entities; finally, the attention-driven multi-feature fusion module is presented to improve the discriminative capacity of the ternary classifier via a noise filtering approach. The experimental results on three core general-purpose benchmark datasets—NYT, WebNLG, and DuIE—show that the F1 score of this model increases by 1.3, 0.4 and 3.2%, respectively, compared with the current optimal model, demonstrating a considerable advantage over the comparative techniques and potential value for campus security–oriented campus traffic safety applications.Keywords: Entity Relationship extraction, Smart campus security, Dependency parsing, Graph neural networks, Triple classificationSubject terms: Computational science, Computer science

Introduction

Campus transportation safety pertains primarily to the security and protection of students traversing campus pathways and utilizing public transit services on campus. National case studies indicate that the dangers associated with campus transportation safety have markedly escalated alongside the growth in the number of schools and universities and their student populations1. At present, the potential for minor accidents to result in severe repercussions, including academic expulsion or life-threatening injuries (e.g., the common instance of a motor vehicle accident leading to fatal outcomes2–4), has heightened the apprehension of student families regarding safety and security on campuses. Simultaneously, in light of pressing issues such as the increase in student enrollment, deteriorating infrastructure, inadequate road capacity, and the overall absence of traffic signal systems and management strategies at intersections5–7, the development of a comprehensive solution has become imperative for ensuring campus traffic safety.

Currently, research primarily concentrates on the scientific decision-making model employed by emergency professionals in response to real-time crises at incident sites8. Traditional decision-making models frequently encounter difficulties, including inadequate systematization of the decision-making knowledge base, delayed response times, and undue reliance on individual emergency response experience. Due to the abrupt nature and significant interdependence of campus traffic safety incidents, conventional decision-making approaches are inadequate for emergency management. Consequently, the development of an intelligent emergency management system has become a necessary solution to overcome the existing challenges.

In recent years, researchers have undertaken a series of studies on the development of knowledge graphs and the potential association analysis of campus emergency transportation incidents. Liu et al.9 introduced a multi-dimensional knowledge graph construction technique utilizing the BiLSTM-CRF model, which facilitates the visual association modeling of accidents and potential risks by examining the influence mechanisms of risk variables and propagation modes on railroad safety, thereby offering quantitative support for the development of safety strategies. Consequently, Saffet Erdogan et al.10 developed a framework to identify the distributional characteristics of high-risk locations using spatial statistical analysis of Turkey’s traffic accident data utilizing geographic information system (GIS) technology. Farman Ali et al.11 integrated ontology methods, latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA), and bi-directional long- and short-term memory networks (Bi-LSTM) to create a real-time monitoring system capable of dynamically detecting traffic accidents and projecting the evolution of circumstances. Ma et al.12 introduced a stacked sparse self-encoder (SSAE) to develop a deep learning system aimed at accurately predicting traffic accident injury levels via the merging of multi-dimensional causal data. Lin et al.13 subsequently integrated various machine learning algorithms to develop a predictive model for intersection accident risk, utilizing a multi-source traffic accident database, thereby offering technical assistance to traffic management authorities in formulating targeted prevention and control strategies.

Contemporary research emphasizes knowledge graph construction and complementation tasks14–16, enhancing the traditional emergency decision-making model through the integration of advanced language models and graph neural network techniques. However, the entity association method, reliant on relational extraction, continues to confront the fundamental challenge of ternary group overlap. At the entity identification level, the boundary ambiguity issue primarily manifests in two ways: first, the disproportionate distribution of categories results in insufficient associations among entities; second, the intertwining of multiple relationships among entities tends to obfuscate classifier decision-making. It is important to highlight that current research predominantly relies on public English datasets for model validation and optimization, and there is an absence of rigorous evaluation standards for Entity Relationship extraction models in Chinese contexts.

This study proposes a bimodal parsing framework to address the aforementioned issues: it utilizes semantic dependency analysis to transcend surface syntactic constraints and establish explicit semantic connections between linguistic units via dependency arcs; subsequently, it integrates phrase constituent parsing with hierarchical syntactic relationship modeling to facilitate multi-granularity characterization of linguistic units. This study specifically creates a Chinese scenario dataset to improve the model’s generalization capability for cross-language generalization ability comparison trials. Related work

Campus traffic safety

Campus traffic safety, a critical component in the development of contemporary smart cities, primarily aims to ensure the personal safety of students who walk or utilize public transportation on campus and adjacent roadways. The existing traffic accident prevention system predominantly depends on advanced technologies, including data mining, machine learning, cloud computing, and the Internet of Things, to develop intelligent solutions17–19. In the domain of accident response and treatment strategy research, scholars frequently employ a synthesis of experience-based and data-oriented methodologies. Ma et al.12 proposed a complete analytical framework utilizing a deep learning-based stacked sparse autoencoder (SSAE) to forecast the severity of injuries in traffic accidents through the construction of multi-dimensional influencing factor models. This research’s innovative methodological system is primarily evident in two aspects: first, the integration of traffic flow data, environmental perception data, individual behavior data, and other multi-source heterogeneous information into a cohesive analytical framework; second, the implementation of a differentiated machine learning algorithm pipeline facilitates the nuanced processing of complex data features and the extraction of profound insights.

Campus event knowledge graph

The knowledge graph offers essential technical assistance for integrating multi-source heterogeneous data related to campus transportation occurrences, fundamentally aiming to provide a structured representation framework using semantic networks to elucidate the associative linkages among elements. Technology has emerged as a significant research focus for researchers globally, aimed at constructing a knowledge system for safety events by the systematic documentation of event information, including entity descriptions and intricate relationships. Utilizing this technology, researchers can examine probable correlations of security events, forecast risk scenarios, and develop decision support models, with substantial advancements achieved in related domains. Nonetheless, a research vacuum persists in the methods for developing a knowledge network of transportation-related safety occurrences inside campus contexts. Recent research has predominantly employed methodologies including pattern matching, machine learning, deep learning, and graph convolutional neural networks to develop event knowledge graphs20–23. For instance, Simon Gottschalk et al.24 identified through empirical research that conventional knowledge graphs exhibit issues such as redundant entity representations and inadequate modeling of interactions between events and temporal information, subsequently proposing a multi-language event-oriented temporal knowledge graph architecture. Conversely, the ”Lean” framework established by Deng et al.25 utilizes the event knowledge graph to extract historical temporal information, emphasizing the investigation of the association modeling mechanism between vocabulary and semantics within specific contexts.

Event information entity relationship extraction

Entity recognition and relationship extraction serve as fundamental components of event information extraction. The conventional pipelined methodology frequently results in feature degradation across subtasks, alongside issues like entity redundancy and incomplete relationship extraction, stemming from the challenges of capturing intricate long-distance associations among entities. This section addresses the joint entity and relationship extraction technique for deep learning26–32, which successfully mitigates the intrinsic limitations of the conventional phased-processing model via end-to-end modeling.

In parallel, recent NER studies have further strengthened entity boundary modeling and feature fusion. Li et al. propose AGNER33, which introduces a unified entity modeling method and leverages diffusion-based denoising to progressively refine entity boundaries under continual-learning settings. Hou et al. present HiNER34, a hierarchical Chinese NER framework that integrates character, lexical, and syntactic information and enhances relation features via Triformer and an attention fusion module.

Currently, the seq2rel end-to-end sequence model developed by John Giorgi et al.35 adeptly addresses the intricate issues of cross-sentence relations, coreference, and n-tuple relations through an innovative linearization model. This model employs a joint learning mechanism to amalgamate the tasks of entity extraction, coreference disambiguation, and relation extraction, thereby avoiding the labeling dependency and error propagation inherent in pipelined approaches. The MT-DNN framework, developed by Vincenzo Moscato et al.36, amalgamates multi-task learning with knowledge distillation techniques, facilitates information sharing via multi-task co-training, and improves model performance through a distillation strategy, effectively addressing the issue of limited labeled data for relationship extraction in the biomedical field. Furthermore, the pioneering research on joint extraction modeling encompasses37–39 and additional sets of findings.

The utilization of graph convolutional neural network (GCN) technology in knowledge graph building demonstrates multidimensional advancement40–43. Qiu et al.44 integrated text mining and deep learning to automate the extraction of geological entity relationships in iron ore deposits, created a structured map comprising six types of entities and twenty-four types of relationships, and developed a prototype of an intelligent question-and-answer system based on this map. Xiyu Wang et al.45 suggested a relationship extraction approach utilizing the CNN model and pre-training vectors to autonomously extract safety requirement relationships from building construction safety texts and to construct a knowledge graph that facilitates compliance verification. Zhong Zhang et al. proposed and designed a hierarchical few-sample learning model (HFKT) that integrates hierarchical class-structured knowledge via a tree-structured knowledge graph, effectively addressing the issue of sample insufficiency and demonstrating superior performance across multiple benchmark datasets. Furthermore, Yong Shi et al.21 devise a GCN-LSTM integration approach to facilitate stock time-series feature extraction and trend prediction through the construction of a multi-relational knowledge graph.

The aforementioned research and technology advancements have systematically facilitated the methodological progression in knowledge graph development. This study enhances the existing model utilizing graph convolutional neural networks and empirically demonstrates significant improvements in the performance metrics of three critical tasks: entity recognition, relationship extraction, and atlas construction, ultimately achieving a systematic development of the knowledge atlas for campus traffic safety events.

Event extraction model framework

Many current techniques for ternary extraction in knowledge graphs rely on character-level text analysis; nevertheless, the disparity between single-word semantics and overall word semantics often results in ambiguity and overlap in ternary extraction. Traditional approaches struggle to efficiently manage overlapping relationships in text, which significantly impairs dataset flexibility and extraction accuracy. Moreover, entity separation significantly influences entity relationship triad extraction, and the tenuous association between remote entities tends to obscure entity boundaries and diminish the recall rate. The OneRel model is a novel method for extracting joint entities and relationships, which completes the extraction task in a single module in one step, which is of great significance in knowledge graph construction. Traditional methods typically decompose this task into multiple modules or steps, but this approach ignores the interdependent and inseparable nature of entities and relationships, leading to issues of joint errors and information redundancy. The OneRel model effectively addresses these issues by treating joint extraction as a fine-grained triplet classification problem. This paper offers a multilayer graphic convolutional neural network entity relationship extraction model (MGRel) for a campus traffic safety event knowledge graph, based on the OneRel model46, as seen in Fig. 2. It encodes the input sentence and incorporates dependency parsing to construct syntactic and semantic dependency graphs; the resulting graph-aware features are propagated through MultiGCN to obtain graph embeddings, fused with textual representations, and finally fed into an attention-driven multi-feature fusion module for triplet classification, with predictions produced by a softmax layer. Initially, the Chinese and English public datasets are incorporated into the MGRel model, wherein, based on the OneRel model, the global dependency semantics and syntactic graph embedding representation of sentences are amalgamated during the initial vector generation phase47. This integration enhances the efficacy of relationship extraction by assimilating cross-sentence syntactic structural features and accommodating the Chinese-specific word segmentation mechanism. Secondly, we develop a multilayer semantic graph convolutional network (MultiGCN) to acquire global semantic and syntactic graph embedding representations via multilayer information propagation, and to extract the profound implicit semantics of entity relationships. The derived graph embedding semantic vectors are integrated with the original text. utilizes vectors to produce new sequence vectors , while the graph hybrid pooling layer captures the global associations of the semantic information. A multi-feature fusion attention approach is subsequently implemented to optimize triad classification accuracy48 by augmenting the weight assignment of candidate entities, hence significantly boosting the recognition accuracy of distant entities. Ultimately, ternary boundary optimization is executed via a softmax layer to mitigate the issues of ternary overlap and entity boundary ambiguity throughout the extraction process, thereby achieving high-precision ternary recognition.

Application of the knowledge graph for campus traffic safety events

Using the universities in Henan Province as a case study, the swift enlargement of enrollment numbers has led to a continuous rise in campus population density, hence causing a substantial increase in campus traffic flow. Currently, the primary issues about traffic safety on college campuses are as follows: (1) Limited by objective circumstances, campus construction planning exhibits a lack of foresight, leading to inadequate road widths, traffic congestion, and the mingling of pedestrians and vehicles; (2) an absence of specific laws and regulations to deter and penalize illegal vehicles on campus; (3) a deficient awareness of traffic safety among students and faculty, evidenced by behaviors such as indiscriminate parking, cellphone usage at intersections, and neglecting to reduce speed while traversing roadways. Traffic safety on college campuses remains a critical focus of online public opinion monitoring, with its event ontology constituting the fundamental component of the Knowledge Graph’s top-level design. This framework possesses the capacity for comprehensive proactive problem identification and early warning through the analysis of dynamic case data about traffic safety incidents in provincial colleges and universities. For instance, in the pre-assessment of campus traffic safety incidents, utilizing historical event data from various colleges and universities, the integrated application of knowledge graph reasoning algorithms facilitates the monitoring, early warning, and efficient management of campus traffic safety events, thereby safeguarding the well-being of faculty and students. Conclusion

The paper proposes MGRel, a dependency graph-based model for extraction, designed to address the requirements of online public opinion monitoring and the development of smart campus security. The model adeptly addresses the constraints of ternary overlapping processing in relation extraction, resolves the issue of ambiguous entity boundaries, and markedly enhances the precision of relation identification by incorporating dual mechanisms of cross-utterance syntactic structure feature extraction and deep semantic information mining. The experimental results indicate that the MGRel model attains performance enhancements of 1.3%, 0.4%, and 3.2% in F1 value across the three benchmark datasets: NYT, WebNLG, and DuIE, respectively. Future studies will concentrate on the iterative refinement of the model architecture to address multi-tiered, complicated relational scenarios, alongside a comprehensive investigation of the technical implementation pathway for the interpretable entity relationship extraction model.

Effective policies on prevention and control of cardiovascular diseases in different countries: A realistic review
Authors: Aliasghar Valipour, Ali Mohammad Mosadegh Rad, Mohammad Mahboubi, Mohammad Arab
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are responsible for 60% of deaths from noncommunicable diseases worldwide. Iran’s CVD control and prevention program faces challenges that have limited its effectiveness and efficiency. This study aimed to identify effective programs, policies, and interventions for the prevention and control of CVDs in Iran. The realistic review method was used to conduct this research. Original peer-reviewed studies published in the field of policies and interventions for the prevention and control of CVDs between 2000 and July 22, 2024, were collected in PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Persian databases such as Iranmedex, MagIran, irandoc, and Scientific Information Database (SID). Articles that met the inclusion criteria were analyzed using the framework analysis using MAXQDA 10 software. A total of 61 effective interventions and policies were identified for the CVD control and prevention program. The most important effective policies included primary and secondary prevention interventions, nutritional interventions, lifestyle modification, tobacco control policies, and blood pressure control. A total of 56 contextual factors were identified in this program, the most important of which were the availability of appropriate primary care, access to health centers, doctors and medicines, the private sector, insurance funds, and subsidized services. A total of 36 intervention mechanisms were identified, the most important of which were public education through mass media, social participation, participation of experts, and nongovernmental organizations. Finally, 22 outcomes were identified in this study, the most important of which included reducing disease risk factors, improving nutritional habits, and reducing disease prevalence. This review shows that effective programs and interventions for the prevention and control of CVDs can be implemented in each country with an appropriate mechanism to reduce the burden of the disease, taking into account the political, social, economic, and sociocultural context, and based on the experiences of other countries, interventions that are more compatible with our country’s conditions can be introduced to policymakers and decision-makers in the country’s health system for selection and implementation.

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are responsible for 60% of deaths from noncommunicable diseases worldwide, and in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), 58% of deaths under the age of 60 are due to these diseases.[1] According to the World Health Organization (WHO), over the past few decades, CVDs have been the most important cause of death in different age groups in countries around the world, especially in developing countries.[2] At the beginning of the 20th century, CVDs were responsible for 10% of all deaths in the world, but in the 21st century, they are responsible for 50% of deaths in developed countries and 25% of deaths in developing countries.[3] According to estimates by the WHO, CVDs are expected to cause 23.6 million deaths in 2030.[4] It is also predicted that between 2011 and 2030, about $47 trillion will be lost worldwide due to noncommunicable diseases, and about $30 trillion of it will be due to four diseases: diabetes, cancers, chronic lung diseases, and CVDs. CVDs are one of the most important causes of death in the world and incur high costs.[5] Existing studies indicate that in Eastern Mediterranean and Middle Eastern (EMME) countries, including Iran, CVDs are major health problems and their consequences are increasing rapidly.[6] The prevalence of noncommunicable diseases is also increasing in Iran.[7] It is also predicted that the burden of CVDs will increase by 2025 due to the aging population in Iran.[8] The mortality rate from CVDs will reach 44.8% by 2030.[910] The major contribution to the increasing burden of CVDs in developing countries is the increasing prevalence of risk factors and the difficulties in designing appropriate interventions. On the other hand, because LMICs face limited resources for managing CVDs, designing cost-effective preventive interventions is very useful and valuable.[11]

Effective interventions to reduce the burden of CVDs include broad interventions to reduce risk factors, individualized approaches to modifying risk factors for high-risk individuals (such as those with diabetes), and treatment of CVD events.[12] However, existing studies show that in the EMME countries, including Iran, despite having a national document for the prevention and control of noncommunicable diseases and considering the main goal of reducing premature mortality from heart and coronary artery diseases (CADs) by 25% by 2025,[13] appropriate and effective management has not yet been applied to the current situation.[14] However, by taking the necessary measures, these diseases and their consequences can be prevented and controlled.[15] Therefore, by conducting this study, effective programs, policies, and interventions for prevention and control of CVDs in the world were reviewed and investigated to assist policymakers and managers by getting a deeper understanding of the subject and providing appropriate knowledge to identify the underlying issues and problems and the mechanisms of how to implement these policies and interventions, and taking into account the indigenous and cultural characteristics of Iran so that they can make better decisions and take more effective actions when choosing and modifying policies, programs, and developments.

Materials and Methods

Approach

This study is a realistic review conducted in 2024. It focuses on a relatively new research strategy that goes beyond making judgments. This type of review explains the mechanisms of complex program functioning or the reasons for their failure and compare different studies in the context. It seeks to answer the question, “For which people and in what environments this program is effective?” This study is a prospective and descriptive evaluation, with a focus on realism and methodological orientation. The realist study aims to uncover fundamental theories and explain how mechanisms and strategies interact in different contexts, comparisons, and outcomes.

In this research, the seven-step realistic review protocol of Mosadeghrad et al.[16] was used, which includes the following steps: determining research questions; clarifying basic theory; developing search strategies; collecting, evaluating, and selecting evidence; synthesizing evidence; modifying the initial theory of the program; and presenting the proposal.

Search strategy

To conduct this study, we searched scientific databases such as PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science for relevant studies published between 2000 and July 2024. Our search strategy involved using a combination of medical subject headings (MeSH), terms, and keywords related to “health policy” and “cardiovascular diseases”. These keywords were identified from the title, abstract, and keywords of relevant studies found during the initial review. We used Boolean operators “OR” and “AND” to combine similar or different concepts, respectively. Additionally, we manually searched the reference lists of included studies and other studies on “prevention and control policies of CVD” for further relevant studies. We also retrieved Persian publications from Persian databases such as Iranmedex, MagIran, irandoc, and Scientific Information Database (SID). Selection and evaluation

In the article identification and selection phase, articles were screened to determine their relevance to answering our research question. The inclusion criteria for the study were limited in terms of time due to the WHO’s attention to the category of noncommunicable diseases, including CVDs (studies after the beginning of 2000 to July 22, 2024) and in terms of language (both Persian and English). In terms of study type, all original peer-reviewed publications including cohort, cross-sectional, quantitative, or qualitative studies, mixed-methods, randomized controlled trials, and pilot studies were included. All interventions or programs that aimed to control CVDs were considered, regardless of content, duration, setting, or delivery method. The exclusion criteria for the study also were study designs and protocols, case reports, commentaries, books, blog posts, conference abstracts, editorials, and review articles, studies published in languages other than Persian and English, studies published after the end of July 22, 2024, and scientific references without full text. After obtaining final approval for the articles in the previous steps, AV and MM each read the full text of each article to extract the required information. This information was then documented in a standardized data extraction form. AV and MM then reviewed and confirmed the extracted information independently. If any discrepancies arose, the research team members discussed and reached a consensus to make a final decision. A data extraction form including sections on authors’ characteristics, year of publication of the article, country of study, plan, context, mechanism, and outcome was used to extract data.

Data analysis

Ritchie and Spencer’s five-step framework analysis method, including familiarization with the data, identifying a thematic framework, indexing, drawing tables, mapping, and interpretation, was used to analyze the data. The data analysis was performed using Version 10 of the MAXQDA software.

Ethical considerations

All ethical considerations were observed in review studies in this study. Receiving the code of ethics from the research deputy of Tehran University of Medical Sciences and Health Services and noninterference of personal opinions of researchers in the round-up stages, analysis and reporting of information were ethical considerations of this research. The goal of this stage was to gather the most data and combine information from different countries, so a quality evaluation checklist was not necessary.

Results

Study description

A total of 1885 articles were extracted from various databases. After removing duplicates and articles without full text, 1011 studies remained for title and abstract review. During this process, 486 articles were excluded based on the title and abstract analysis. At this stage, 194 articles relevant to the prevention and control of CVDs were identified. Upon a thorough screening of the remaining articles, 160 were excluded due to a lack of applicable findings in the domain of CVD prevention and control. Additionally, four articles were identified through citation reviews. Ultimately, 38 research papers were included in this study [Figure 1].

Characteristics of included studies

From 2000 to 2010, there were a few studies focused on interventions and policies for the prevention and control of CVDs. However, there has been a significant increase in publications since 2011, with approximately 60.5% of the articles published during this period. About 55% of studies were conducted in Asia, with notable contributions from countries such as Iran, Singapore, Vietnam, China, and India. Among Asian nations, Iran had the highest number of studies, totaling ten. In Europe, 21% of the studies were conducted in countries like Italy, Russia, Turkey, Sweden, and the Netherlands. Approximately 13% of the research was carried out in Africa, with Kenya, Ghana, Nigeria, Ethiopia, and Tunisia participating. In the Americas, about 11% of the studies were conducted in Brazil, the United States, and Colombia, including governance and leadership, financial resources, human resources, equipment and medicines, information systems, and health service delivery. Theory modification

The first theory regarding the prevention and control program for CVDs in this study is as follows: If the CVD prevention and control program is well written and implemented with the cooperation and participation of all stakeholders (plan), and if there is a comprehensive, high-quality, and continuous disease prevention and control program for CVDs that includes intersectoral participation from all stakeholders (context), then this will lead to increased knowledge and changes in attitudes among the public and patients. This change will foster trust in the program, encouraging individuals to seek assistance from the health system and other relevant organizations for various services (mechanism). Ultimately, this will result in a reduction in the burden of disease and an improvement in the health of individuals and society as a whole (outcome).

This study, by identifying interventions, contextual factors, and mechanisms affecting interventions for the prevention and control of CVD, revised the initial theory of the CVD prevention and control program. The final theory of the CVD prevention and control program is: “Policies and interventions for the prevention and control of CVDs in line with the goals of the WHO, development goals and the document on non-communicable diseases, having a strategic and operational plan, having a disease surveillance system and appropriate strategies, sufficient and sustainable resources, combined, participatory, community-based and cost-effective and evidence-based interventions, interventions based on disease risk factors, team-based care and access to necessary and appropriate medicines and technologies, active participation of stakeholders, intra- and inter-sectoral cooperation (plan), If governance and leadership are strengthened, risk factor reduction, health service and care delivery, follow-up, monitoring and evaluation of the program in the health system and across sectors, compatible with the political, economic, cultural, social and technological environment (context), with a step-by-step approach, active participation of stakeholders and the private sector, non-governmental organizations and health volunteers, through mass media, campaigns, behavioral counseling, trust, patient and community participation, commitment, accountability and responsibility of health system managers and employees (mechanism) and ultimately, disease risk factors reduction, mortality and disease burden reduction, maintenance and enhancement of health promotion in the community (outcome) will be achieved.”

Suggestion

CVD prevention and control program is one of the most important priorities of the country’s health system. Therefore, having a consistent and strong CVD prevention and control program is a prerequisite for implementing effective policies and interventions in this field. Iran’s CVD prevention and control program, with revision, modification of programs and policies, strategic and operational planning according to the prevalence of risk factors in each province or city, proper management and leadership, strengthening intersectoral cooperation, obliging stakeholder organizations to implement relevant agreements, providing necessities, and its implementation mechanisms can improve the health of the community and patients. An effective, efficient, and implementable cardiovascular disease prevention and control program through the implementation of evidence-based and community-based, cost-effective, beneficial, and comprehensive stakeholder participation policies leads to a reduction in risk factors, a reduction in the incidence and prevalence of the disease, a decrease in mortality and disease burden, and an increase in life expectancy. Achieving such results requires political commitment and a multisectoral approach at a high level, organizational commitment, and accountability of all elements and levels of the health system, and the participation of all stakeholders in the implementation of program interventions and policies, public trust and participation in screening programs, and their satisfaction with the continued provision of comprehensive and quality care and services. Numerous internal and external contextual factors affect the outcomes and mechanisms of cardiovascular disease prevention and control policies. In the domestic dimension of governance and leadership, prevention and risk factor reduction programs, health care, and monitoring and surveillance of interventions need to be strengthened. The policy of preventing and controlling CVD reduces the risk of disease in individuals by adopting effective and enforceable strategies, legislation, and political actions, pursuing healthy lifestyle patterns that ensure the health of all members of society, and promoting effective interventions and reducing the population’s exposure to common and modifiable risk factors for disease, such as tobacco use, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, and harmful alcohol consumption. Providing health services and care to people with CVD according to evidence-based standards and guidelines and utilizing the expertise of health system staff to meet patients’ needs will help in effective disease management. In the external dimension, political/legal, economic, and social/cultural factors with cross-sectoral participation affect the success of the cardiovascular disease prevention and control program, which should be considered by policymakers, managers, and all stakeholders in the health system.

Discussion

This study was conducted to identify effective policies and interventions for the prevention and control of CVDs in Iran. Implementation of practical, scalable, cost-effective interventions with participation and cooperation within the health system and interdepartment of other ministries plays a significant role in reducing health system costs and patients’ out-of-pocket payments, reducing disease risk factors, and reducing morbidity, mortality, and disability. Caused by disease, it reduces the burden of disease improves health and increases the life expectancy of people in the society. Another consideration regarding the effectiveness of an evidence-based policy or intervention is the method of implementation and the context in which it is implemented, not the policy itself. Implementing a policy may have very different results in local, national, and international locations and contexts. Interactions between a policy with other related policies and environmental factors may explain the success or failure of a policy implemented in different regions.

In this study, 56 effective interventions in the field of prevention and control of CVDs were identified, which were grouped into six blocks of the WHO, including governance and leadership, financial resources, human resources, equipment and medicines, information systems, and health service delivery. The health system governance process includes components of the organization; intersectoral communication and cooperation; policy-making and planning; inheritance and leadership; and monitoring and evaluation.[59]

In this study, most interventions in the field of governance were related to primary health care interventions,[32444850] food labeling,[18233042] and tobacco control policies.[26303248] However, in some studies, salt reduction strategies,[3342] secondary prevention interventions,[4857] and community-based interventions[1756] were effective. It seems that in the world, in the field of governance, preventive and healthcare interventions are more effective than therapeutic and secondary strategies, and environmental and regulatory interventions have been less effective.

In the field of financing, most interventions were related to health insurance organizations and funds,[2934415357] but some studies confirm government-subsidized services and coupons[4553] and show that they are much more effective than external funding interventions.[20] In the field of human resources, most interventions are related to specialist physicians, nutritionists, psychologists,[20354553] health workers, experts,[225257] experienced workers, and health workers[3141]. The results show that interventions in the field of human resources related to the level of education and at the level of specialist physicians and experienced experts are more effective than interventions in general practitioners and trained nurses[53] and trained health care workers.[26]

In the field of equipment and medicine, most interventions are related to drug treatment,[34454653] but some studies confirm drug purchase, medication adherence,[52] and upgrading medical equipment and ambulance services,[48] indicating that drug treatment is more effective than other interventions in the field of equipment and medicine.

In the field of information systems, the most common educational interventions are advertisements on state and local television and radio and newspaper articles[232428333854] and are shown to be better and more effective than videos in studies, health screenings,[26] and the use of population databases[47] due to their extent and large audience.

The results show that in the field of health care and services, most interventions are related to focusing on a healthy diet,[2324252628414346474950545658] quit smoking campaigns,[23283337384143464950] increasing physical activity,[23242837384143464954] and lifestyle modification strategies.[243435373948525358] However, in other studies, the focus is on controlling risk factors for CVD such as controlling hypertension,[283343454653] dietary salt reduction intervention,[2226333751] high cholesterol control,[334346] and diabetes control,[284653] which have been increasingly emphasized as effective interventions in the prevention and control of cardiovascular diseases, indicating that countries can contribute to the health of their people by achieving appropriate models for community-based interventions to improve the lifestyle of their communities and control disease risk factors.

Numerous internal and external contextual factors influence the outcomes and mechanisms of cardiovascular disease prevention and control policies. The internal dimension is divided into factors (governance and leadership, financial resources, human resources, equipment and medicine, information systems, and health service provision).

In the field of governance and leadership, the most influential contextual factors include the existence of an appropriate primary care system.[1822414954] Other studies have identified the existence of community-based programs[242957] and the existence of health and medical centers[3441] as contextual factors, but some studies have mentioned the existence of a strong health system,[31] the existence of a cardiovascular disease prevention program,[50] the existence of a diabetes and hypertension care program,[56] and free medical care,[48] which indicates that the existence of an appropriate primary care system is more effective than other contextual factors, even having a free medical care program, in successfully implementing cardiovascular disease interventions.

The most effective contextual factors in the field of financing were related to the existence of health insurance organizations and funds,[2934415357] government subsidized services and coupons,[4553] and the provision of external assistance.[20] These studies show that successful implementation of interventions always requires financial support, especially the existence of health insurance funds.

In our study, the most important contextual factors in the field of human resources are specialist physicians, nutritionists, and psychologists.[20354553] However, other studies have emphasized the role of health workers and experts.[2231415257] The results show that in the field of human resources, in addition to specialist physicians and experienced experts, human resources such as general practitioners and trained nurses[53] and trained health care workers[26] are needed to effectively implement interventions.

The contextual factors affecting equipment and medicine are different. The results of the studies emphasize factors such as having a low-cost drug program,[34] drug access,[46] the presence of medical equipment, and ambulance services,[48] However, it seems that contextual factors are more effective in the field of drugs than in the field of equipment.

Among the effective contextual factors in the field of information systems, the most common factor was related to advertisements on state and local television and radio, and newspaper articles.[232428333854] However, other studies have also used educational videos, health demonstration presentations,[26] and the use of population databases[47] as effective contextual factors. This study shows that the use of mass media such as state and local television and radio is very effective due to its extent and large audience.

One of the most important contextual factors in the field of health care and services is access to health centers,[252646] but other studies emphasized the existence of diabetes and hypertension care programs[5056] and access to primary health care.[2945] However, some studies have mentioned the coverage of preventive and therapeutic interventions,[44] access to a doctor,[46] evaluation of the prevention program,[50] and surveillance.[32]

The external dimension (political-legal, economic, social and cultural, technological and environmental factors) is also influential and should be considered by policymakers, managers, and all stakeholders in the health system. Among the political-legal context factors, the most important factor is the requirement for inter-sectoral cooperation,[183038] but some studies have pointed to the existence of intrasectoral cooperation and partnership,[30] the existence of the global SDG program, universal health coverage (UHC) global agenda,[41] and legal communities.[32]

Among economic factors, the most effective factors were the cooperation of insurance organizations,[2934415357] but in some studies, subsidized and coupon services,[4553] affordable care,[3258] having a moderate income,[3542] and social work organizations[2343] have been identified as contextual economic factors.

Contextual factors in the social and cultural filed are very different, the most important of which are the presence of social welfare organizations,[2343] cultural fit,[2427] and the presence of nongovernmental organizations.[2735] However, many studies have mentioned various contextual factors such as foreign aid,[20] social and religious centers,[22] comprehensive education,[24] low addiction rates,[37] healthy eating culture,[48] mass media,[33] social and cultural diversity,[26] schools and public places,[25] use of industry,[26] high education,[47] support from local hospitals and doctors,[43] and the presence of local restaurants.[54]

Among external factors, environmental factors[3456] and technology[47] were the least mentioned factors in this study, which seem to have been used less often due to high costs and lack of infrastructure.

In our study, a variety of mechanisms affecting cardiovascular disease interventions were identified, including intrasectoral mechanisms, intersectoral mechanisms, and collaborative mechanisms. In the intrasectoral mechanisms, different types of implementation mechanisms were identified. Many studies have proposed the use of health workers,[22315257] and continuity of care[37454653] as mechanisms for implementing interventions. However, in some other studies, health system policies,[283249] World Health Guidelines,[2944] drug treatment methods,[34454653] health education and treatment,[172048] and participation of professionals in health care[20354553] were used as methods of interventions, which are among the most important mechanisms in the field of health, and it is one of the most important mechanisms for screening patients and the general population for blood pressure, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia and plays an important role in developing policies and programs to prevent CVD. In intersectoral mechanisms, most studies were related to cooperation between nongovernmental organizations.[22242835] However, other studies introduced private sector cooperation,[52] women’s committees,[24] multilateral campaigns,[45] and corporate social responsibility for health[50] as important intersectoral mechanisms. It seems that in the intersectoral mechanisms of the health system, as the health care provider, in intersectoral cooperation with various organizations, they develop and provide prevention programs for CVD and, through healthy lifestyle education, can play a significant role in reducing risk factors and mortality from CVD.

In some studies, the intervention mechanism was a combination of internal and external health organizations, which has been effective. In the participatory mechanism, various studies have proposed the use of social participation,[232838394356] the participation of state and local mass media,[242628333854] and face-to-face education[25263843] as the most important mechanisms for implementing interventions, but other studies have also identified industry participation,[2643] creating healthy food buffets in schools, competitions in schools,[2538] and dynamic group interactions[2526] in this mechanism. Therefore, developing a participatory process to support a lifestyle modification program seems to be a feasible and effective strategy for promoting cardiovascular health programs.

The results of effective interventions and policies in CVD are divided into two groups: main objectives and subobjectives. In our study, there are various outcomes in the main objectives section, such as reducing cardiovascular disease risk factors, reducing hospitalizations, and reducing disease prevalence and mortality, which are the most common outcomes of various studies in this review. However, other studies have addressed subobjectives, such as the effectiveness of community-based programs, cost-effectiveness of interventions, development of cardiovascular programs, and development of insurance services.

Conclusions

To address the complex and multifaceted challenges of cardiovascular disease and optimally manage its risk reduction worldwide, effective interventions and strategies are needed that address a wide range of preventive and clinical contexts involving populations, patients, and primary care providers. One consideration in the effectiveness of a policy or intervention is the way it is implemented and the context in which it is implemented, not the policy itself. The implementation of a policy may have very different outcomes in local, national, and international settings and contexts. Interactions between a policy with other relevant policies and possible environmental factors can explain. By identifying effective programs and interventions in different countries, a realistic review can redesign these policies and interventions according to the political, economic, and social/cultural conditions (context) with an appropriate mechanism to achieve disease burden reduction and help policymakers and decision-makers in the health system to make better decisions and take more effective actions regarding the selection and modification of policies and programs or their development.

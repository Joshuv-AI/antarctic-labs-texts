Damping optimization of discrete mechanical systems – rod/string model
Ninoslav Truhar School of Applied Mathematics and Informatics, Josip Juraj Strossmayer University of Osijek, Trg Ljudevita Gaja 6, 31000 Osijek, Croatia, Krešimir Veselić Fernuniversität Hagen, Fakultät für Mathematik und Informatik, Postfach 940, D-58084 Hagen, Germany

Abstract
This paper investigates two optimization criteria for damping optimization in a multi-body oscillator system with arbitrary degrees of freedom ( n n ), resembling string/rod free vibrations. The total average energy over all possible initial data and the total average displacement over all possible initial data. Our first result shows that both criteria are equivalent to the trace minimization of the solution of the Lyapunov equation with different right-hand sides. As the second result, we prove that in the case of damping with one damper, for the discrete system, the minimal trace for each criterion can be expressed as a linear or cubic function of the dimension n n . Consequently, the optimal damping position is determined solely by the number of dominant eigenfrequencies and the optimal viscosity, independent of the dimension n n , offering efficient damping optimization in discrete systems. The paper concludes with numerical examples illustrating the presented theoretical framework and results.



Keywords:
String model, Rod model, Damping optimization, Optimal position of a damper, Lyapunov equation

\displaystyle K=U_{K}\Lambda_{K}U_{K}^{T}\,,

The quantity to be minimized is the mean displacement

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathbf{x}(t)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{y}_{1}(t)^{T}\Omega^{-1}\Phi^{T}\Phi\Omega^{-1}\mathbf{y}_{1}(t)\mathrm{d}t

The unit sphere average

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(\hat{X}P)

Example 2.1
.

Consider the one dimensional oscillator

m\ddot{x}+c\dot{x}+kx=0\,,

and the Lyapunov equation

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\sqrt{k/m}\\
\sqrt{k/m}&-c/m\\
\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}x_{11}&x_{12}\\
x_{12}&x_{22}\\
\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}x_{11}&x_{12}\\
x_{12}&x_{22}\\
\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\sqrt{k/m}\\
-\sqrt{k/m}&-c/m\\
\end{array}\right]

=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-a&0\\
0&-b\\
\end{array}\right]

with
a,b\geq 0,\ a+b>0
.
This gives the equations

-2\sqrt{k/m}x_{12}=-a\qquad\sqrt{k/m}(x_{11}-x_{22})-x_{12}c/m=0

x_{12}\sqrt{k/m}-x_{22}c/m+x_{12}\sqrt{k/m}-x_{22}c/m=-b\,.

Hence

x_{12}=\frac{a}{2\sqrt{k/m}}=\frac{a}{2}\sqrt{m/k}\,,

(x_{22}-x_{11})\sqrt{k/m}+\frac{ac}{2\sqrt{km}}=0\,,

x_{22}=\left(\frac{a}{2}+\frac{b}{2}\right)m/c\,,

x_{11}=x_{22}+\frac{ac}{2k}=\frac{a+b}{2}m/c+\frac{ac}{2k}.

For
a=b=1
we obtain the trace for the average energy criterion

\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X_{1})=2m/c+\frac{c}{2k}\,,

with the known minimum at the critical damping

.
In the case of the average displacement criterion we have
a=1/k,\ b=0
giving the trace

\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X_{2})=m/(kc)+c/(2k^{2})\,,

again with a unique minimum

obviously different from the above one.

This shows that further study of the properties of both criteria
will be an interesting issue, especially a comparison between them in the sense of quality of the solution and complexity of the calculation. However, in this paper, we will not further consider the optimization criteria, this will be a subject of our future studies.
In the rest of the paper, we will concentrate only on the position
optimization for both criteria for the vibrations of the structured models.

3
Vibrations of rod/string and discrete mass–spring model

Our main result concerns string or rod vibrations described with the following partial differential equation:

\displaystyle\rho(x)u(x,t)_{tt}+c(x)u(x,t)_{t}-(k(x)u(x,t)_{x})_{x}=0\,,

\displaystyle u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0\,,\quad x\in(0,1)\,,

The advantage of the equation (

\displaystyle u_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin{k\pi x}\,.

In the weak formulation of the formal equation (
25
) the delta damper is represented by the quadratic form

\displaystyle\omega(u,v)=du(y)v(y)\,,d\geq 0\,,

to which there corresponds the matrix
C(y)
in the basis
u_{k}
, given as

\displaystyle C_{kj}=\frac{d}{2}\sin{(k\pi y)}\sin{(j\pi x)}\,.

Now a discretization consists in cutting a finite matrix out of these.

This leads to the finite dimensional system with

\displaystyle K=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\omega_{1}^{2},\ldots,\omega_{n}^{2})\,,M=I_{n}\,,C=C(y)=dc(y)c(y)^{T}\,,

with

\displaystyle c(y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}\sin{(\pi y)}&\sin{(2\pi y)}&\ldots&\sin{(n\pi y)}\end{bmatrix}^{T}\,.

(26)

More details about the properties of the vibrating system, described by the partial differential equation (
25
) can be found in
(
12
, 4.1 The Wave Equation)
.

If
\rho
and
k
are not constant we may still use the Fourier decomposition above and by common integrals compute the coefficients of the matrices
M
and
K
as follows

\displaystyle M_{kj}=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\sin{(k\pi x\rho(x))}\sin{(j\pi x)}dx\,,

\displaystyle K_{kj}=\frac{kj\pi^{2}}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\cos{(k\pi xk(x))}\cos{(j\pi x)}dx\,,

and
C
as above. The only difference is that now the matrices
M
and
K
are not diagonal anymore.
Note that choosing other boundary conditions would lead to other trigonometric functions.
Thus, we end up with minimizing the Lyapunov trace for one-dimensional damping as described in
[
26
]
which is given by an explicit formula as a function of
y
. This can again be
minimised either analytically or numerically or just by plotting.

To do this we must first normalise
c(y)
to a constant norm that is independent of
y
.
We have

\displaystyle\|c(y)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\sin^{2}{(k\pi y)}=\frac{n}{4}-\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\cos{(2k\pi y)}=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{4}-\frac{1}{4}\emph{Re}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}e^{2k\pi\imath y}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{4}-\frac{1}{4}\emph{Re}\frac{e^{2\pi\imath y}(1-e^{2\pi\imath ny})}{1-e^{2\pi\imath ny}}\,,0<y<1\,.

(27)

The same approach can be applied to the rod model

\displaystyle\rho(x)u(x,t)_{tt}+c(x)u(x,t)_{t}-(k(x)u(x,t)_{x})_{x}+(a(x)u(x,t)_{xx})_{xx}=0\,,

(28)

\displaystyle u(0,t)=u(x,t)_{xx}(0,t)=u(1,t)=u(x,t)_{xx}(1,t)=0\,.

If the material is homogeneous, say,
\sigma(x)=\sigma_{0}>0
,
\rho(x)=1
,
k(x)=k_{0}
and
a(x)=a_{0}
then the stationary undamped equation

\displaystyle-\omega^{2}u-k_{0}u(x,t)_{xx}+a_{0}u(x,t)_{xxxx}=0

(29)

has again the eigenfunctions

\displaystyle u_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin{k\pi x}\,,

with the eigenfrequencies

\displaystyle\omega_{k}=k\pi\sqrt{k^{2}\pi^{2}a_{0}+k_{0}}\,.

This was the spectral discretisation of the continuous system. Another way is to start from the finite dimensional case as in (
2
) and
(
3
), that is

M=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}),\quad m_{i}>0\,,

K=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}k_{1}+k_{2}&-k_{2}&&&\\
-k_{2}&k_{2}+k_{3}&-k_{3}&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&-k_{n-1}&k_{n-1}+k_{n}&-k_{n}\\
&&&-k_{n}&k_{n}+k_{n+1}\\
\end{array}\right).

For
k_{i}=m_{i}=1
the eigenfrequencies are

\displaystyle\omega_{l}=2\sin\frac{l\pi}{2(n+1)}\,,l=1,\ldots,n\,.

and the eigenvector matrix
Q=(q_{rl})
given as

\displaystyle q_{rl}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n+1}}\sin\frac{rl\pi}{n+1}\,.

That is, the
k
-th eigenvector is given by

\displaystyle u_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n+1}}\begin{bmatrix}\sin{\frac{1k\pi}{n+1}}&\sin{\frac{2k\pi}{n+1}}&\ldots&\sin{\frac{nk\pi}{n+1}}\end{bmatrix}^{T}

(30)

This case is interesting in itself and also because it can be understood as a discretisation of the continuous string. Recall that we have denoted by
Q=(q_{rl})
the eigenvector matrix. This matrix happens also to be symmetric, so its
k
-th column, that is, the vector
u_{k}
from (
30
) is at the same time the representation of the canonical unit vector
e_{k}
in the orthonormal basis
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
. Our
damped system with the damping matrix
C=ve_{k}e_{k}^{T}
is now equivalent with the system

\displaystyle M=I_{n}\,,K=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\omega_{1}^{2},\ldots,\omega_{n}^{2})\,,C=vu_{k}u_{k}^{T}\,,

which describes the damper at the position
k
. We now make the discrete variable
k
continuous by setting

\displaystyle C(z)=vc(z)c(z)^{T}\,,

with

\displaystyle c(z)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n+1}}\begin{bmatrix}\sin{\frac{1zn\pi}{n+1}}&\sin{\frac{2zn\pi}{n+1}}&\ldots&\sin{\frac{nzn\pi}{n+1}}\end{bmatrix}^{T}\,,0<z\leq 1\,,

(31)

such that

\displaystyle c\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)=u_{k}\,.

Now, this is strikingly analogous to the spectral discretisation we made previously. Moreover, for large
n
the vectors
c(z)
here and there coincide. The normalization formulae here are quite analogous to those in (
27
). Here too, this construction has started from the special stiffness matrix
K
above but it can serve with any 1D vibrational system where the boundary conditions should better be
taken accordingly.

3.1
Optimal dampers’ position – discrete mass spring mechanical system

In this section, we will present the main result of the optimal position of one damper for the discrete mass spring mechanical system.

We will show, that optimal position is a simple function of the dimension
n
, which means that we can calculate the optimal position for one damper on some smaller dimensions and then estimate it for a general case.

We focus on a single damper due to the fact that only for
one damper there exists an explicit expression for the trace of the solution to the corresponding Lyapunov equation.

The initial criterion to be applied is the total average energy criterion. As outlined in section
2
, we will consider the Lyapunov equation

\displaystyle AX+XA^{T}=-Z\,,

where
A
is defined in (
16
) and

\displaystyle Z_{1}=Z_{s}\oplus Z_{s}\,\qquad Z_{s}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{s}&\\
&0_{(n-s)}\end{bmatrix}\,.

(32)

Our goal is to find the minimal
tr(X)
, which corresponds with the minimization of the total average energy.
Further,

\displaystyle\Omega=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}{(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})}\,,\omega_{j}=2\sin{\frac{j\pi}{2(n+1)}}\,.

For a given position
p_{k}
, the damping matrix
C
is defined as

\displaystyle C=cc^{T}\,,

(33)

where for
p_{k}\in\{\frac{1}{n},\frac{2}{n},\ldots,1\}

\displaystyle c(p_{k},n)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n+1}}\begin{bmatrix}\sin{\frac{1p_{k}n\pi}{n+1}}&\sin{\frac{2p_{k}n\pi}{n+1}}&\ldots&\sin{\frac{np_{k}n\pi}{n+1}}\end{bmatrix}^{T}\,.

Note, that
p_{k}=k/n
is a discrete set of points defined by continuous variable
0<z\leq 1
from (
31
).

Since,

\displaystyle\omega_{j}=\omega_{j}(n)\,,

we see that the
\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X)
is a function of dimension
n
positions
p_{k}
, and viscosity
v
, that is

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X)=\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(p_{k},v,n))\,.

We will show that for a fixed position
\mathbf{p}
(which does not depend on
n
) defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}\in(0,1)\,,

(34)

the optimal
\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p}_{\rm{opt}},v_{\rm{opt}}))

is a simple function (linear for “energy criterion” or cubic
for “displacement” criterion) of dimension
n
.
Here the optimal
\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p}_{\rm{opt}},v_{\rm{opt}},n))
is defined with optimal position and corresponding viscosity

\displaystyle(\mathbf{p}_{\rm{opt}},v_{\rm{opt}})=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\mathbf{p},v}\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p},v))\,.

3.2
Optimal position of one damper for the discrete mass–spring model

As previously highlighted, determining the optimal placement of several dampers poses a significant challenge. As we will illustrate below, even in the case of the rod/string model, where explicit expressions for all undamped eigenfrequencies and eigenvectors are available, positioning multiple dampers remains an unresolved issue.

Thus, in the subsequent section, we will present our main results regarding the properties of the trace function of the solution to the Lyapunov equation in the case of one damper. These findings will enable us to identify the optimal position for a single damper in the discrete mass–spring model, applicable across all dimensions.

The main tool will be a modified formula obtained by
Veselić
in
[
26
]
in the form documented in
[
7
]
for the trace of the Lyapunov equation

A^{T}X+XA=-I_{2n},

(35)

where
A
is defined as

A=\begin{bmatrix}0&\Omega\\
-\Omega&-v\mathbf{c}\mathbf{c}^{T}\end{bmatrix}\,,

(36)

and
v>0
is the viscosity parameter which has to be optimized
and for a given position
\mathbf{c}=\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}&c_{2}&\ldots&c_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
,
c_{i}\neq 0
.

The trace of the solution
X
multiplied by diagonal matrix
Z_{\Delta}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(Z_{\Delta}X)

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{v}+bv\,,

(37)

where

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2z_{k}}{c_{k}^{2}}\;,

(38)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z_{k}c_{k}^{2}}{2\omega_{k}^{2}}+z_{k}\left(\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\frac{3\omega_{k}^{2}c_{j}^{2}+\omega_{k}^{2}c_{k}^{2}+\omega_{j}^{2}c_{j}^{2}+\omega_{j}^{2}c_{k}^{2}}{(\omega_{k}^{2}-\omega_{j}^{2})^{2}}+2\frac{\omega_{k}^{2}}{c_{k}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\frac{c_{j}^{2}}{\omega_{k}^{2}-\omega_{j}^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\,,

(39)

and
Z_{\Delta}
is any diagonal matrix, i.e.

Z_{\Delta}=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\,.

(40)

From (
37
) we get optimal viscosity “for free”

\displaystyle v_{\textrm{opt}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}}\,.

(41)

This means that the optimal trace for a damper in a fixed position is

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X_{\rm{opt}})

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{ab}\,.

(42)

In what follows we will show that the optimal trace (
42
)
for position defined as in (
34
), (
\mathbf{p}=k/n
) is asymptotically a linear function of the dimension
n
, that is

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X_{\rm{opt}})=\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X_{\rm{opt}}(\mathbf{p},n))\sim\mathcal{O}(n)\,.

More precisely, we will show that for the optimal trace (
42
) at position
\mathbf{p}
, corresponding optimal coefficients
a
and
b
are linear functions of
the dimension
n
, i.e. we will show that for
n
large enough (
n\rightarrow\infty
)

\displaystyle a(n)\sim\alpha_{1}n+\alpha_{0}\,,b(n)\sim\beta_{1}n+\beta_{0}\,,

for some functions
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}
and
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}
which does not depend on
n
.

3.3
Optimal trace for the average total energy

As we have mentioned above using expression
(
42
)

and the structure of
a
and
b
from (
38
) and (
39
), respectively, we will prove that for rod or string model for
n
large enough the optimal trace is asymptotically
a linear function of
n
. This means that for
n
large enough the optimal trace divided by
n
is a function that does not depend on
n
.

More precisely we will show that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n}=\alpha_{1}\,,\quad\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b(n)}{n}=\beta_{1}\,,\quad

where
\alpha_{1}
and
\beta_{1}
does not depend on
n
, thus

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p},n)\right)

\displaystyle=\eta(\mathbf{p},n)\sim\eta_{1}n+\eta_{0}\,,

where
\eta_{1}
, and
\eta_{0}
are some functions that are independent of
n
.

For the criterion of the average total energy, frequency-cut projection
is defined as in (
32
):

\displaystyle Z_{1}=Z_{s}\oplus Z_{s}\,,\qquad Z_{s}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{s}&\\
&0_{(n-s)}\end{bmatrix}\,,

which means that all
z_{k}=1
from (
40
),
for
k=1,\ldots,s
, while
z_{k}=0
, for
k=s+1,\ldots,n
.

Recall that
\omega_{j}
and
c_{j}(\mathbf{p},n)
for string/rod model
are given as

\displaystyle\omega_{j}(n)=2\sin{\frac{j\pi}{2(n+1)}}\,,

and

\displaystyle c_{j}(\mathbf{p},n)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n+1}}\sin{\frac{j\mathbf{p}n\pi}{n+1}}\,.

If we insert these into
a
and
b
from (
38
) and (
39
), respectively,
we get

\displaystyle a(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{(n+1)}{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi kn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)}\,,

(43)

\displaystyle b(n)

\displaystyle=b_{1}(n)+b_{2}(n)+b_{3}(n)\,,

(44)

\displaystyle b_{1}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{\csc^{2}\left(\frac{\pi k}{2(n+1)}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi kn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)}{4(n+1)}\,,

(45)

\displaystyle b_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\frac{\omega_{k}^{2}(3c_{j}^{2}+c_{k}^{2})+\omega_{j}^{2}(c_{j}^{2}+c_{k}^{2})}{(\omega_{k}^{2}-\omega_{j}^{2})^{2}}\,,

(46)

\displaystyle b_{3}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}2\frac{\omega_{k}^{2}}{c_{k}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\frac{c_{j}^{2}}{\omega_{k}^{2}-\omega_{j}^{2}}\right)^{2}\,,

(47)

where, after additional simplification

\displaystyle b_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\frac{2\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi j}{2n+2}\right)\left(\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi jn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)+\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi kn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)\right)}{(n+1)\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi j}{n+1}\right)-\cos\left(\frac{\pi k}{n+1}\right)\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi k}{2n+2}\right)\left(3\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi jn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)+2\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi kn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)\right)}{(n+1)\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi j}{n+1}\right)-\cos\left(\frac{\pi k}{n+1}\right)\right)^{2}}

(48)

\displaystyle b_{3}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}4\cdot(n+1)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi k}{2n+2}\right)\csc^{2}\left(\frac{\pi kn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\frac{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi jn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)}{(n+1)\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi j}{n+1}\right)-\cos\left(\frac{\pi k}{n+1}\right)\right)}\right)^{2}\,,

(49)

Thus, we proceed with the calculation of the limits of
functions
a(n)/n
and
b(n)/n
.

For the first limit, note that

\displaystyle\frac{a(n)}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{(n+1)}{n\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi kn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)}\,,

which implies that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\csc^{2}(\pi k\mathbf{p})\,.

This shows, that
a(n)
has oblique asymptote with slope

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\csc^{2}(\pi k\mathbf{p})\,.

(50)

Further, note that

\displaystyle\frac{b(n)}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{1}(n)}{n}+\frac{b_{2}(n)}{n}+\frac{b_{3}(n)}{n}\,.

The ratio
\displaystyle{\frac{b_{1}(n)}{n}}
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{b_{1}(n)}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{\csc^{2}\left(\frac{\pi k}{2(n+1)}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi kn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)}{4n(n+1)}\,,

thus, the limit of
\displaystyle{\frac{b_{1}(n)}{n}}
is

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{1}(n)}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{2\sin^{2}(\pi k\mathbf{p})}{\pi^{2}k^{2}}\,.

(51)

Further,

\displaystyle\frac{b_{2}(n)}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\Gamma(j,k,\mathbf{p})\,,

where

\displaystyle\Gamma(j,k,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi j}{2n+2}\right)\left(\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi jn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)+\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi kn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)\right)}{n(n+1)\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi j}{n+1}\right)-\cos\left(\frac{\pi k}{n+1}\right)\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi k}{2n+2}\right)\left(3\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi jn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)+2\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi kn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)\right)}{n(n+1)\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi j}{n+1}\right)-\cos\left(\frac{\pi k}{n+1}\right)\right)^{2}}\,.

Using simple properties of the limit of trigonometric functions and some trigonometric identities we can see that
\Gamma(j,k,\mathbf{p})
has the
upper bound

\displaystyle 0<\Gamma(j,k,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\left(2(j\pi)^{2}+4(k\pi)^{2}\right)}{n(n+1)(2n+2)^{2}\left(\sin\left(\frac{(j+k)\pi}{2(n+1)}\right)\sin\left(\frac{(j-k)\pi}{2(n+1)}\right)\right)^{2}}\,,

which further implies

\displaystyle 0<\Gamma(j,k,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4(n+1)\left(j^{2}+2k^{2}\right)}{\pi^{2}n(j-k)^{2}(j+k)^{2}}\,.

For a fixed
k\in\mathds{N}
, the sum

\displaystyle S(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\,\sum_{j\neq k}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)\left(j^{2}+2k^{2}\right)}{n(j-k)^{2}(j+k)^{2}}\,

(52)

is convergent. Indeed note that for fixed
k

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq k}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)\left(j^{2}+2k^{2}\right)}{n(j-k)^{2}(j+k)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq k}^{\infty}\frac{\left(j^{2}+2k^{2}\right)}{(j^{2}-k^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq k}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(j^{2}-k^{2})}+3\sum_{j\neq k}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}}{(j^{2}-k^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{(j^{2}-k^{2})}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(j^{2}-k^{2})}+3\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{k^{2}}{(j^{2}-k^{2})^{2}}+3\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}}{(j^{2}-k^{2})^{2}}\,.

Since, for
j>k
we have

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}}{(j^{2}-k^{2})^{2}}\leq\frac{k^{2}}{j^{2}-k^{2}}\,,

using

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\frac{1}{j^{2}-k^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k}H_{2k}\,,

where
H_{2k}
are generalized harmonic numbers ( see
(
2
, Theorem 3.4)
)
we can conclude that the sum
S(k)
from (
52
) is convergent.

For example (for
n\rightarrow\infty
)

\displaystyle S(1)=0.468064\,,S(2)=0.867021\,,S(3)=0.940901\,,S(4)=0.96676\,,\ldots

\displaystyle S(10)=0.994687\,,\ldots,S(100)=0.999953\,,\ldots,S(n)=1\,.

Thus, the limit of
\displaystyle{\frac{b_{2}(n)}{n}}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{2}(n)}{n}

\displaystyle\leq s\cdot\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\frac{4(n+1)\left(j^{2}+2k^{2}\right)}{\pi^{2}n(j-k)^{2}(j+k)^{2}}\leq s\,.

(53)

Note that the above limit depends only on
s
and it is independent on
\mathbf{p}

(position of the damper).

For the last limit, consider

\displaystyle\frac{b_{3}(n)}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{4}{n(n+1)}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi k}{2n+2}\right)\csc^{2}\left(\frac{\pi kn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\frac{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi jn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)}{\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi j}{n+1}\right)-\cos\left(\frac{\pi k}{n+1}\right)\right)}\right)^{2}\,.

Note that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{3}(n)}{n}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{\left(\pi^{2}k^{2}\right)\csc^{2}\left(\frac{\pi kn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)}{n(n+1)}\left(\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\frac{2\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi jn\mathbf{p}}{n+1}\right)}{\pi^{2}\left(k^{2}-j^{2}\right)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\csc^{2}(\pi k\mathbf{p})\left(\sum_{j\neq k}^{\infty}\frac{2k\sin^{2}(\pi j\mathbf{p})}{\pi\left(k^{2}-j^{2}\right)}\right)^{2}\,.

Note, for fixed
k

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\sin^{2}(\pi j\mathbf{p})}{\left(k^{2}-j^{2}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{1}{j^{2}-k^{2}}\right|\,,

which using

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j^{2}-k^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k}H_{2k}\,,

where
H_{2k}
are generalized harmonic number,
implies that the sums

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{s}\csc^{2}(\pi k\mathbf{p})\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2k\sin^{2}(\pi j\mathbf{p})}{\pi\left(k^{2}-j^{2}\right)}\right)^{2}\,,

is convergent. We have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{3}(n)}{n}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{s}\csc^{2}(\pi k\mathbf{p})T(k,\mathbf{p})\,,

(54)

where

\displaystyle T(k,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{k^{2}-j^{2}}+\frac{1}{2k}H_{2k}\,.

(55)

Note that the limit in (
54
) depends only on
s
and
\mathbf{p}
.

We have showed that sums from (
51
), (
53
) and (
54
) converge, respectively, which implies

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b(n)}{n}=\beta_{1}(s,\mathbf{p})\,,\quad

(56)

and from (
50
) follows

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n}=\alpha_{1}(s,\mathbf{p})\,.\quad

(57)

To summarize, if one is interested in the calculation of the
the best damping, that is the best position and corresponding optimal viscosity using the average total energy criterion, is equivalent to the minimization

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p},v,s))\rightarrow\min\,,

where
X(\mathbf{p},v,s)
is the solution of the Lyapunov equation

AX(\mathbf{p},v,s)+X(\mathbf{p},v,s)A^{T}=-Z,

(58)

where
A
is defined in (
36
)
and
v>0
is the viscosity parameter which has to be optimized
for a damper
\mathbf{c}=c(\mathbf{p})
in given position

\mathbf{p}=k/n
.

If one tries to calm down the first
s
undamped eigenfrequencies
\omega_{1}<\ldots<\omega_{s}
, the matrix
Z
is defined as

Z=Z_{s}\oplus Z_{s}\,,Z_{s}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{s}&0\\
0&0_{n-s}\end{bmatrix}\,.

Thus, let

\displaystyle v_{\rm{opt}}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{v}\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p},v,s))\,,

(59)

be the optimal viscosity obtained for the position
\mathbf{p}
and given
s
.

Based on the preceding calculation, we can state the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X(\mathbf{p},v_{\rm{opt}},s)
be the solution of the Lyapunov equation (
58
), with optimal viscosity

v_{\rm{opt}}
as in (
59
). Then it holds

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p},v_{\rm{opt}},s))}{n}

\displaystyle=\eta_{1}(\mathbf{p},s)\,,

(60)

where
\eta_{1}(\mathbf{p},s)
, is a function that is independent of
n
.

Proof 3.3
.

Using (
42
), one can see that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p},v_{\rm{opt}},s))}{n}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\frac{a(n)}{n}\frac{b(n)}{n}}\,.

Now, from (
57
) and (
56
) follows

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p},v_{\rm{opt}},s))}{n}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(s,\mathbf{p})\beta_{1}(s,\mathbf{p})}\doteq\eta_{1}(\mathbf{p},s)\,,

which completes the proof.

The main benefit from the result of Theorem
3.2
is
that, for a given set of dominant eigenfrequencies which we try to calm,
that is for a given matrix
Z
, one can calculate the best position
with the corresponding optimal viscosity for some modest dimension
n
,
which then holds for general (large)
n
. Note, that the optimal position
\mathbf{p}
will change if we change the number of dominant eigenfrequencies
s
which we try to calm.

All this will be illustrated in the section with numerical examples.

3.4
Optimal trace for the total average displacement

To minimize the total average displacement within the rod or string model, it is necessary to minimize the trace of the solution to the Lyapunov equation. This involves using the same system matrix but with a distinct right-hand side.

Recall, from (
18
) and (
2.2
) follows that the right-hand side of the Lyapunov equation contains

\displaystyle\widehat{K}^{-1}=\Omega^{-1}\Phi^{T}\Phi\Omega^{-1}=\Omega^{-2}\,.

Here we have used that
\Phi
is an orthogonal matrix.

Thus, the projection matrix
Z_{\Delta}
from (
37
) and
(
2.2
) is diagonal given as

\displaystyle Z_{\Delta}=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\Omega_{s}^{-2},0_{n-s})\oplus 0_{n}\,,

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{s}=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{s})\,.

This configuration enables us (once again) a usage of
a
and
b
from (

) and (

) for determining the optimal trace.

Note, if we are interested in calming down
s
dominant eigenfrequencies

\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{s}
, the non-zero diagonal entries of the matrix
are given as

\displaystyle z_{k}=\frac{1}{\omega_{k}^{2}}\,,\qquad k=1,\ldots,s\,.

Since, for the string/rod model
\omega_{k}
is given as

\displaystyle\omega_{k}=2\sin\frac{\pi k}{2n+2}\,,

we see that
\omega_{k}^{2}
has the same rate of
convergence as
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{n^{2}}}
i.e.

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{2}\omega_{k}^{2}=k^{2}\pi^{2}\,.

(61)

Thus, in the case of the minimization of the total average displacement,
for
n
large enough the optimal trace function divided
by
n^{3}
is a function which does not depend on
n
.

More precisely, from (
42
) follows

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p},v_{\rm{opt}},s))

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{a_{K}(n)b_{K}(n)}\,,

(62)

where

\displaystyle a_{K}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{2}{\omega_{k}^{2}c_{k}^{2}}\;,

(63)

\displaystyle b_{K}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{c_{k}^{2}}{2\omega_{k}^{4}}

(64)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\omega_{k}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\frac{2\omega_{k}^{2}c_{j}^{2}+\omega_{k}^{2}c_{k}^{2}}{(\omega_{k}^{2}-\omega_{j}^{2})^{2}}+\frac{\omega_{k}^{2}}{c_{k}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j\neq k}^{n}\frac{c_{j}^{2}}{\omega_{k}^{2}-\omega_{j}^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\,.

Now, using (
50
) and (

) it is easy to show

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{a_{K}(n)}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{1}{k^{2}\pi^{2}}\csc^{2}(\pi k\mathbf{p})\,.

(65)

Further let
b_{K1}
,

and

and
b_{3}

such that

,

.

Now again using (
61
) and (
51
) it is easy to get

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{K1}(n)}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{2\sin^{2}(\pi k\mathbf{p})}{\pi^{4}k^{4}}\,.

(66)

Similarly, using (

) and (

)
we get

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{K2}(n)}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{s}\frac{1}{k^{2}\pi^{2}}\,,

and using (

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{K3}(n)}{n^{3}}

(68)

where

\displaystyle T(k,\mathbf{p})

(69)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{K}(n)}{n^{3}}=\gamma_{2}(s,\mathbf{p})\,.\quad

(70)

undamped eigenfrequencies
\omega_{1}<\ldots<\omega_{s}

\displaystyle Z=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\Omega_{s}^{-2},0_{n-s})\oplus 0_{n}\,,

\displaystyle\Omega_{s}=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{s})\,.

\displaystyle v_{\rm{opt}}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{v}\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p},v,s))\,,

Let

.

Proof 3.5
.

Using (

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p},v_{\rm{opt}},s))}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\frac{a_{K}(n)}{n^{3}}\frac{b_{K}(n)}{n^{3}}}\,.

Now, from (

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}(X(\mathbf{p},v_{\rm{opt}},s))}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\gamma_{1}(s,\mathbf{p})\gamma_{2}(s,\mathbf{p})}\doteq\xi(\mathbf{p},s)\,,

which completes the proof.

Remark 3.6
.

It is important to note that in the case of string or rod oscillations,
the eigenfrequencies and corresponding eigenvectors do not depend on the dimension
n
.

For the string, which is explicitly diagonalized with the undamped frequencies

\omega_{k}=k\pi

the orthonormal eigenvectors are given by

u_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin(k\pi x).

For the rod, the eigenvectors are also

u_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin(k\pi x),

but the eigenfrequencies are

\omega_{k}=k\pi\sqrt{k^{2}\pi^{2}a_{0}+k_{0}}.

Since the summations in equations (
38
) and (
39
) run from
k=1
to
k=s
for a fixed
s
,
the quantities
a
and
b
remain equal for any
n>s
.
This is due to the fact that the first
s
eigenpairs are independent of the total dimension
n
.

Remark 3.7
.

Regarding the relationship between the discretization size
s
and the accuracy of the numerical solution, a classical engineering example presented in Reddy
[
18
]
demonstrates that for linear finite elements applied to the one-dimensional wave equation with fixed ends, the
L^{2}
-norm error of the solution converges with order
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
, where
h=1/n
is the uniform mesh size. This result is based on the comparison between the analytical eigenmode solution and the finite element approximation using a consistent mass matrix.

Therefore, if we aim for an accuracy of approximately
\varepsilon=10^{-8}
, it is sufficient to choose the number of elements such that
n>10000
, under the assumption that
s<n
. This ensures that the number of retained modes remains below the spatial resolution limit imposed by the mesh, maintaining both stability and accuracy of the FEM approximation.

5
Conclusion and outlook

The paper’s main contribution is the study of two distinct optimization criteria for damping optimization in a multi-body oscillator system with arbitrary degrees of freedom (
n
),
which corresponds with the model of string/rod free vibrations. As the first result, we have shown that both criteria are equivalent with the trace minimization of the solution of the
Lyapunov equation with different right-hand sides. The second result we prove that the minimal trace for each criterion can be expressed as a simple (linear or cubic) function of
dimension
n
. In other words, the optimal position solely depends on the number of dominant eigenfrequencies and the optimal viscosity and does not depend on dimension
n
.
This highlights a simplified approach to damping optimization in such systems.

Conjectures and future work:

1.
Numerical experiments show the similar results
about the optimal positions of several dampers hold as well. Namely, the optimal positions of
r\geq 2
dampers depend only on the number of dominant eigenfrequencies
s
and their location
i
(
\omega_{i+1}<\ldots<\omega_{i+s}
) and does not depend on dimension
n
. It is true for both criteria.

We need to prove this, and we intend to tackle it in the near future.

2.
Study, the system of ODEs of the more general structure
like the vibration chain of masses and springs shown on Figure
1
with general
m_{i}
and
k_{i}
.

3.
Some general theoretical results about the stability of infinite dimensional systems with bounded spectrum. For example a deeper understanding of the spectral properties with one or two or more dimensional dimensional damping.

4.
Further study of the properties of both optimization criteria:
i)
The total average energy over all possible initial data

and ii)
The total average displacement over all possible initial data.
Especially a comparison between them in the sense of the quality of the solution and the complexity of the calculation.

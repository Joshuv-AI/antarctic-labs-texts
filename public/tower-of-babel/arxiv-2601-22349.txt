Forward-KL Convergence of Time-Inhomogeneous Langevin Diffusions
Andreas Habring, Martin Zach

Abstract
Many practical samplers rely on time-dependent drifts—often induced by annealing or tempering schedules—to improve exploration and stability. This motivates a unified non-asymptotic analysis of the corresponding Langevin diffusions and their discretizations. We provide a convergence analysis that includes non-asymptotic bounds for the continuous-time diffusion and its Euler–Maruyama discretization in the forward-Kullback–Leibler divergence under a single set of abstract conditions on the time-dependent drift. The results apply to many practically-relevant annealing schemes, including geometric tempering and annealed Langevin sampling. In addition, we provide numerical experiments comparing the annealing schemes covered by our theory in low- and high-dimensional settings.

A subclass of those MC-based methods is derived from discretizations of stochastic differential equations (SDEs).

Despite their empirical success, the theoretical analysis of the time-inhomogeneous Langevin diffusion remains fragmented.
Existing guarantees typically specialize to a particular path (
e.g.
, geometric tempering or convolutional paths) and require smoothness assumptions that implicitly assume strong solutions of the Fokker–Planck equation.
In addition, most existing guarantees control the backward-Kullback–Leibler divergence
\KL(\pi\,|\,\mu_{t})
instead of the forward-Kullback–Leibler divergence
\KL(\mu_{t}\,|\,\pi)

However, the latter is the natural notion of convergence for the Langevin diffusion since it is the Lyapunov functional of the Fokker–Planck equation and directly controls entropy dissipation.
As a result, practitioners lack a single theorem that (a) certifies forward-KL convergence for broad families of annealing paths and (b) exposes how schedule choice trades off mixing and tracking error.
We provide such a theorem for
Equation

4
and its Euler–Maruyama discretization in this article.

Convergence results under the assumption of a uniform bound on the ratios

The time-inhomogeneous Langevin diffusion is also closely related to diffusion models
(

)
.
Consider the SDE

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=f(X_{t},t)\mathrm{d}t+g(X_{t},t)\mathrm{d}W_{t},

where

.
Under suitable choices for

, and one could sample from

by simulating the SDE

)

Indeed, both variants have been considered:
Methods that rely on discretizations of

are typically called

, whereas the time-inhomogeneous Langevin diffusion on the forward-process densities is called

(

)

Hybrid variants have also been considered:
The predictor–corrector algorithm due to

In this context, our results are relevant because they endow annealed Langevin sampling and related schemes with convergence results.

3

4.3.5
Posteriors in Bayesian Inverse Problems

As an important special case, we consider the posterior distributions that arise in the context of Bayesian inverse problems.
The analysis in this section is different to that in

In Bayesian inverse problems, one is interested in sampling from posterior distributions

where

In many relevant settings, such as medical imaging, microscopy, and astronomy, the relationship between the data

\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})

In this case, the

admits the density

and we will show in the following proposition that, it is possible to prescribe a path

for the

3

Proposition 4.15

Assume the prior path

3

.
Define for fixed

Section

3
.

increases by at most

Proof.

We have
U_{\tau}(x)=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}|Ax-y|^{2}+U_{X}^{\tau}(x)+\text{const}

\nabla U_{\tau}(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}A^{\top}(Ax-y)+\nabla U_{X}^{\tau}(x).

(32)

The first term is globally Lipschitz with constant

Dissipativity is preserved (and typically strengthened) since

Finally,

6
Conclusion

In this article we proved the convergence of time-inhomogeneous Langevin diffusions and their discretizations in the forward-Kullback–Leibler divergence.
The proofs build on an abstract set of assumptions on the drift of the diffusion covering many popular annealing schemes such as geometric tempering and annealed Langevin sampling.
Compared to the literature, the presented proofs rigorously take care of potential non-smoothness arising from the fact that solutions of the Fokker–Planck equation are generally guaranteed only in the weak sense.

The convergence results as well as the numerical experiments clearly suggest that annealing paths should be designed in a way that ensures that initially the target density is mostly convex and well-conditioned (
i.e.
, small log-Sobolev constant, small Lipschitz constant of the gradient of the potential). In particular, in such cases we find a clear benefit of the time-inhomogeneous diffusion over basic ULAin terms of convergence speed and mode coverage.

Future work

For future work, we plan to derive explicit bounds on the log-Sobolev constants of probability paths
(p_{\tau})_{\tau}
in order to estimate optimal annealing schemes
\tau
as well as optimal convergence rates.

Impact statement

This paper presents work whose goal is to advance the field of machine learning.
There are many potential societal consequences of our work, none of which we feel must be specifically highlighted here.

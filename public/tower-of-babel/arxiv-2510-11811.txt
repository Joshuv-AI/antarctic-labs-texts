Note on Energy index and first eigenvalue of minimal surfaces in spheres
Matilde Gianocca

Abstract
. A minimal immersion from a surface to S 3 S^{3} can be viewed both as a critical point of the area and of the energy. Although no difference appears at first order, looking at the respective second variations unveils significant differences. It is well known that whenever the first eigenvalue satisfies λ 1 ​ ( Σ ) ≥ 2 \lambda_{1}(\Sigma)\geq 2 , the index is ind E ​ ( Σ ) ≤ 4 \mathrm{ind}_{E}(\Sigma)\leq 4 . The converse implication is much more subtle. We prove that whenever λ 1 ​ ( Σ ) < 1 6 \lambda_{1}(\Sigma)<\frac{1}{6} , there exists a vector field X X , orthogonal to the four Möbius vector fields, with negative second variation. We also prove an arbitrary codimension version of this statement: any immersed minimal surface Σ ⊂ S n \Sigma\subset S^{n} with first eigenvalue λ 1 ​ ( Σ ) < n − 2 2 ​ n \lambda_{1}(\Sigma)<\frac{n-2}{2n} admits a vector field X X orthogonal to the n + 1 n+1 Möbius fields with negative second variation.



Introduction

A surface in
S^{n}
for which the first variation of area vanishes is said to be a minimal surface and has zero mean curvature. The Morse index of a minimal surface is defined as the maximal dimension of a subspace of normal variations on which the second variation of area is negative definite. When looking at minimal surfaces in
S^{3}
, the index can be reduced to the scalar operator

(0.1)

J_{A}(f)=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}-2f^{2}-|A|^{2}f^{2}d\mu.

Urbano
[
11
]
proved the classification of low index minimal surfaces in
S^{3}
: if the minimal surface has index at most five, then it is either a totally geodesic two-sphere, which has index
1
, or a Clifford torus, which has index
5
. For minimal surfaces in higher codimension, even in the case of spheres, the index is poorly understood. For codimension higher than one, the second variation operator cannot be reduced to an operator acting on functions, and many properties of the index change. Kusner–Wang recently studied the case of minimal
2
-tori in
S^{4}
in
[
6
]
. For ambient spaces with positive isotropic curvature, lower bounds for minimal spheres have been obtained by Micallef–Moore
[
7
]
. Many of the results regarding index of minimal surfaces in higher codimension rely on work of Ejiri–Micallef
[
2
]
, relating the area index of a minimal surface, to the index of its immersion as a harmonic map.

In this note, we study the relation between the first eigenvalue of a minimal surface in
S^{n}
and its energy index. The energy index is the maximal dimension of a subspace of vector fields tangent to the sphere, such that the operator

(0.2)

D^{2}E(X)=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{S^{n}}X|^{2}-2|X^{N}|^{2}-|X^{T}|^{2}d\mu

is negative definite.
The operator can be rewritten as

(0.3)

D^{2}E(X)=\sum\limits_{i}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla X^{i}|^{2}-2|X^{i}|^{2}.

For any surface
\Sigma
which is not contained in an equatorial
S^{2}
, there is an explicit four-dimensional subspace on which
D^{2}E
is negative definite, which is the space of Möbius vector fields

(0.4)

\xi_{i}=\nabla^{S^{n}}x_{i}=e_{i}-\langle e_{i},x\rangle x.

Any vector field
X
orthogonal to the Möbius fields satisfies

(0.5)

\int_{\Sigma}X^{i}=\int_{\Sigma}X\cdot\xi_{i}=0.

Hence, by looking at the second variation of energy on the orthogonal complement of the Möbius vector fields, one observes that (see also
[
5
]
)

(0.6)

\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)\geq 2\quad\text{implies }\quad D^{2}E(X)\geq 0,\text{ for all }X\perp\{\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n+1}\}.

Whether the converse holds is much more difficult to answer since the existence of a single function
f
with

(0.7)

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}-2f^{2}<0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Sigma}f=0

does not a priori imply the existence of a zero-average vector field tangent to
S^{n}
with

(0.8)

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla X|^{2}-2|X|^{2}<0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Sigma}X=0.

We prove in this note that one can construct such a vector field assuming
\lambda_{1}<\frac{n-2}{2n}
.

Yau conjectured that for any embedded minimal surface
\Sigma\subset S^{3}
the first eigenvalue is given by
\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)=2
. It would therefore be interesting to understand if

Question:
for a minimal immersed surface
\Sigma\subset S^{3}
, does
\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)<2
imply that the energy index is at least
5
?

Our proof relies on identities which are independent of the codimension of the surface in
S^{n}
, which allow us to prove:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Sigma\subset S^{n}
be a minimal immersed surface with
\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)<\frac{n-2}{2n}
. Then,

(0.9)

\text{there exists }X\perp\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n+1}\,\,\,\text{with }D^{2}E(X)<0.

Equivalently, there exists a vector field
X
on the surface
\Sigma
,
\int_{\Sigma}X=0
and
X(x)\cdot x\equiv 0
,

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla X^{i}|^{2}-2|X^{i}|^{2}<0.

1.
Preliminaries

We start by introducing the notation used for minimal immersions, as well as the definitions of index for both the energy and area functionals.
Let

be an isometrically immersed minimal surface (closed, orientable). Then, (see Theorem 1.2 in
[

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\bigg|_{t=0}A(u_{t})\leq\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\bigg|_{t=0}E(u_{t}).

1.1.
Index comparison

Given a minimal surface
\Sigma\subset S^{n}
, the two indices
\mathrm{ind}_{A}
and
\mathrm{ind}_{E}
are typically very different. For example, for the Lawson surfaces
\xi_{1,g}

[
4

\mathrm{ind}_{E}(\xi_{1,g})=4
. As we explain in this note, it appears that the energy index is more closely related to the first eigenvalue of the minimal surface. The precise relation between the two indices, is given by the following:

Theorem 3

(Ejiri–Micallef,
[
2
]
)
.

Let
u:\Sigma\to N
be a branched minimal immersion. Then,

(1.6)

\mathrm{ind}_{E}(u)\leq\mathrm{ind}_{A}(u)\leq\mathrm{ind}_{E}(u)+r

for

(1.7)

if }b\leq 2g-3,\\
4g-2+2\big[\frac{-b}{2}\big]\text{ if }2g-2\leq b\leq 4g-4,\\
0\text{
if }b\geq 4g-3.\end{cases}

2.1.
Computations with Möbius fields

Consider the sections given by

(2.1)

Computing the covariant derivatives, for all

(2.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}^{S^{n}}\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{v}^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(\langle e_{i},x\rangle x)+\langle\nabla_{v}^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(\langle e_{i},x\rangle x),x\rangle x

\displaystyle=-\partial_{v}\langle e_{i},x\rangle x-\langle e_{i},x\rangle v+\partial_{v}\langle e_{i},x\rangle x

(2.4)

\displaystyle=-\langle e_{i},x\rangle v

(2.5)

\displaystyle=-x_{i}v.

Computing the second variation of energy in the direction

(see
[

D^{2}E_{u}(\xi_{i})=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{S^{n}}_{u}\xi_{i}|^{2}-2|\xi_{i}|^{2}+|\xi_{i}^{T}|^{2}=\int_{\Sigma}2x_{i}^{2}-2|\xi_{i}|^{2}+|\xi_{i}^{T}|^{2}

The second term is given by

(2.8)

The second variation is therefore given by

(2.9)

,

(2.57)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}D^{2}E(f\xi_{i})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{n\lambda-2n+4}{n-2}\int_{\Sigma}|f\xi_{i}^{N}|^{2}.

In particular, there exists
i_{0}\in\{1,...,n+1\}
with

(2.58)

D^{2}E(f\xi_{i})<\frac{n\lambda-2n+4}{n-2}\int_{\Sigma}|f\xi_{i}^{N}|^{2}

and it suffices to verify

(2.59)

\frac{n\lambda-2n+4}{n-2}<-\frac{3}{2}\iff 2n\lambda-4n+8<-3n+6\iff 2n\lambda+2<n\iff\lambda<\frac{n-2}{2n}.

This concludes the proof of the main Theorem.

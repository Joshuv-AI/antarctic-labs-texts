Idempotent & Nilpotent Operators and Matrices in Bicomplex Space
Anjali, Akhil Prakash, Amita, Neeraj Kumar Tomar

Abstract
. This paper explores idempotent and nilpotent operators in bicomplex spaces, focusing on their properties and behavior. We define idempotent and nilpotent matrices in this framework and derive related results. Several theorems are presented to establish conditions for the existence and behavior of bicomplex idempotent and nilpotent operators and bicomplex idempotent matrices.

Preliminaries and Notations

This section provides an introduction to bicomplex numbers and explores their key properties. It highlights several essential findings related to bicomplex numbers.

Bicomplex numbers:

Bicomplex numbers are an extension of complex numbers, defined as:

\xi={u}_{1}+{i}_{1}{u}_{2}+{i}_{2}{u}_{3}+{i}_{1}{i}_{2}{u}_{4},

where
\displaystyle u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
and
\displaystyle u_{4}
are real numbers with

\displaystyle{i}_{1}{i}_{2}\ =\ {i}_{2}{i}_{1},\;\;{i}_{1}^{2}={i}_{2}^{2}=-1.

The collection of all bicomplex numbers is represented by
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{2}
and is referred to as the bicomplex space.
For simplicity,
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{1}
stands for the set of complex numbers, and
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{0}
indicates the set of real numbers. The bicomplex space
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{2}
can be characterized in two distinct ways:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\{{u}_{1}+{i}_{1}{u}_{2}+{i}_{2}{u}_{3}+{i}_{1}{i}_{2}{u}_{4}\;:\;{u}_{1},{u}_{2},{u}_{3},{u}_{4}\in\mathbb{C}_{0}\},and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\{{z}_{1}+{i}_{2}{z}_{2}\;:\;{z}_{1},{z}_{2}\in\mathbb{C}_{1}\}.

The set
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{2}
contains zero-divisors, which makes it an algebra over
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{1}
rather than a field. Within
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{2}
, there are exactly four idempotent elements:
\displaystyle 0,1,e_{1},e_{2}
, where
\displaystyle e_{1}
and
\displaystyle e_{2}
are two nontrivial idempotent elements, specified as follows:

e_{1}\coloneqq\frac{(1+i_{1}i_{2})}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad e_{2}\coloneqq\frac{(1-i_{1}i_{2})}{2}.

These elements stand out due to their orthogonality
\displaystyle(e_{1}e_{2}=e_{2}e_{1}=0)
and the fact that they add up to 1
\displaystyle(e_{1}+e_{2}=1)
.

(1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mbox{Also,}\quad e_{1}^{n}=e_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad e_{2}^{n}=e_{2};n\in\mathbb{N}.

Idempotent Representation and Equality Condition of Bicomplex Numbers:

Every bicomplex number
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{C}_{2}
has a unique idempotent representation as a complex combination of
\displaystyle e_{1}
and
\displaystyle e_{2}
as follows:

\xi=(z_{1}-i_{1}z_{2})e_{1}+(z_{1}+i_{1}z_{2})e_{2},

The complex numbers
\displaystyle(z_{1}-i_{1}z_{2})
and
\displaystyle(z_{1}+i_{1}z_{2})
are called the idempotent component of
\displaystyle\xi
and are denoted by
\displaystyle\xi^{-}
and
\displaystyle\xi^{+}
, respectively (cf. Srivastava [11]). Thus, the bicomplex number can be written as:

\displaystyle\xi=\xi^{-}e_{1}+\xi^{+}e_{2}
, where
\displaystyle\xi^{-}=z_{1}-i_{1}z_{2}
and
\displaystyle\xi^{+}=z_{1}+i_{1}z_{2}
.

Furthermore, for two bicomplex numbers
\displaystyle\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{C}_{2}
,

\xi=\eta\Leftrightarrow\xi^{-}=\eta^{-},\xi^{+}=\eta^{+}

That is, the bicomplex numbers are equal if and only if their corresponding idempotent components coincide.

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{2}

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle T_{2}

Suppose
\displaystyle T=e_{1}T_{1}+e_{2}T_{2}\in L_{1}^{n}\times_{e}L_{1}^{n}

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{2}
-idempotent linear operator. We use Definition

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 34.14322ptT^{2}=T

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle e_{1}^{2}T_{1}^{2}+e_{2}^{2}T_{2}^{2}+2e_{1}e_{2}T_{1}T_{2}=e_{1}T_{1}+e_{2}T_{2}

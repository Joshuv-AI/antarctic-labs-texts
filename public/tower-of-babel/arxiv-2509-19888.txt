An Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers for Topology Optimization
Harsh Choudhary, Sven Leyffer, Dominic Yang

Abstract
We consider a class of integer-constrained optimization problems governed by partial differential equation (PDE) constraints and regularized via total variation (TV) in the context of topology optimization. The presence of discrete design variables, nonsmooth regularization, and non-convex objective renders the problem computationally challenging. To address this, we adopt the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) framework, which enables a decomposition of the original problem into simpler subproblems that can be solved efficiently. The augmented Lagrangian formulation ensures consistency across variable updates while facilitating convergence under appropriate conditions.

Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Optimization, PDE Constrained Optimization, Topology Optimization

Problem Statement

Topology optimization (TO) is a method for producing optimal design patterns within a given domain, subject to specific constraints. TO finds applications in fields like engineering, material science, thermodynamics etc. In practice, TO problems are ill-posed due to rapidly oscillating material distributions or fine-scale structures that are not physically realizable
Clason et al. 2018
so we usually add a TV regularizer term in our optimization objective.
The specific problem we are interested in is the design of an optimal heat sink where we have two materials of given conductivity and the total conductivity of the material is given by a linear combination of these. We seek an optimal distribution of a discrete design variable
w(x)
where
x
in some domain
\Omega
so as to minimize the objective in (
1
), which consists of two terms, a compliance and a total-variation regularizer. The constraints include the PDE, boundary condition, and a volume constraint that limits the volume of
w(x)
in
\Omega
to a volume fraction
V_{\max}
:

\displaystyle\inf_{u,w}\quad

\displaystyle J(w)=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}\cdot u(x,w(x))\,dx+\alpha\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w(x)|\,dx

(1.a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(k(w(x))\nabla u(x))+\mathcal{F}(x)=0,\quad\text{in }\Omega

(1.b)

\displaystyle-k(w(x))\nabla u(x)\cdot n=0,\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega_{N}

(1.c)

\displaystyle k(w(x))=k_{0}(1-w(x))+k_{1}w(x)

(1.d)

\displaystyle u(x)=0,\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega_{D}

(1.e)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w(x)\,dx\leq V_{\max};\quad w(x)\in\{0,1\},\quad\forall x\in\Omega,

(1.f)

where
\partial\Omega_{D}
and
\partial\Omega_{D}
denote Dirichlet and Neumann boundaries, respectively.

We discretize (
1

E

denote the value of

on element

denotes the nodal values of

e\in E
and
\mathcal{N}(e),e\in E
denotes the set of adjacent elements of

To make the TV norm independent of the type of discretization, we introduce edge-weights,
s_{ee^{\prime}}
, for all adjacent elements.
To distinguish the resulting finite-dimension problem from (
1

(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})
to denote the discretized states,

and controls,

.

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{w}}{\text{min}}

\displaystyle J(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})=\sum_{e\in E}f_{e}^{T}u_{e}+\alpha\sum_{e\in E}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}(e)}|w_{e}-w_{e^{\prime}}|\cdot s_{ee^{\prime}}

(2)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle A(\mathbf{w})\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{f}=0,\quad\text{including B.C.}

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}w_{e}\leq V_{\max}\cdot|E|,\qquad\mathbf{w}\in\{0,1\}^{|E|}

where we have combined the PDE and boundary conditions, (

The discretized problem is a mixed integer nonlinear optimization problem and is particularly hard to solve because the feasible set is nonconvex, and it contains integer variables, hence we cannot directly use any gradient-based methods. Another challenge is the non-smoothness of the second term. Third is that the compliance term is nonconvex as a function of
w

2
Solution Methodology

To solve (
2
), we propose an alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM). We first introduce an auxiliary variable
v\in[0,1]
, a continuous relaxation of the discrete control
w
and obtain an equivalent problem (
3
):

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}}{\text{min}}{}

\displaystyle J(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})=\sum_{e\in E}f_{e}^{T}u_{e}+\alpha\sum_{e\in E}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}(e)}|w_{e}-w_{e^{\prime}}|\cdot s_{ee^{\prime}}

(3)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle A(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{f}=0,

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}=0

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}w_{e}\leq V_{\max}\cdot|E|\ ;\quad\mathbf{w}\in\{0,1\}^{|E|}

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}v_{e}\leq V_{\max}\cdot|E|\ ;\quad\mathbf{v}\in[0,1]^{|E|},

where we also use the SIMP formulation of conductivity
Krishna et al. 2017
, because it promotes the integrality of
v
:
k(v)=\delta+(1-\delta)v^{p}
.

The bold symbols in (
3
) and subsequent equations represent vectors.
Note that we have also duplicated the volume constraint. This problem decouples into two independent subproblems if not for the copy constraint
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{w}
. To remove the copy constraint, we dualize the constraint and construct the augmented Lagrangian.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\mathbf{f}^{T}\mathbf{u}+\alpha\sum\limits_{e\in E}\sum\limits_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}(e)}|w_{e}-w_{e^{\prime}}|\cdot s_{ee^{\prime}}+\boldsymbol{\lambda}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{2}

(4)

ADMM alternates between two subproblems: a continuous one in
\mathbf{v}
and a discrete one in
\mathbf{w}
. The first subproblem (
5
) is a nonlinear (discretized) PDE-constrained optimization problem for the continuous control
\mathbf{v}
.

\texttt{Cont}(\mathbf{v};\overline{\mathbf{w}},\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\;\triangleq\;\left\{\begin{aligned} \underset{\textbf{u, v}}{\text{min}}{}\quad&\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v};\overline{\mathbf{w}},\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}):=\mathbf{f}^{T}\mathbf{u}+\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\overline{\mathbf{w}}-\mathbf{v})+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\overline{\mathbf{w}}-\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{2}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad&A(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{f}=0,\quad\\
&\sum_{e\in E}v_{e}\leq V_{\max}\cdot|E|;\quad\mathbf{v}\in[0,1]^{|E|}\end{aligned}\right.

(5)

In practice, we solve (
5
) by a reduced-space approach that eliminates the discretized states,
\mathbf{u}
using the controls
\mathbf{v}
in the discretized PDE. We solve this problem using some gradient-based constraint optimization method like IPOPT
Wächter and Biegler 2006
.

The second subproblem (
6
) is interesting in the sense that we have a TV regularizer along with the quadratic penalty term and a budget constraint. This resembles a class of problems presented in
Yang 2025
, which finds applications in image denoising, topology optimization, etc. These types of problems can be solved using an augmentation scheme where a solution is incremented or decremented on connected subregions. We follow the discussion in
Yang 2025
to randomize these moves and attain an approximating heuristic algorithm for this subproblem.

\texttt{Disc}(\mathbf{w},\overline{\mathbf{v}},\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\;\triangleq\;\left\{\begin{aligned} \underset{\mathbf{w}}{\text{min}}\quad&\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\mathbf{w};\overline{\mathbf{v}},\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}):=\alpha\sum\limits_{e\in E}\sum\limits_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}(e)}|w_{e}-w_{e^{\prime}}|\cdot s_{ee^{\prime}}+\boldsymbol{\lambda}(\mathbf{w}-\overline{\mathbf{v}})+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\mathbf{w}-\overline{\mathbf{v}}\|_{2}^{2}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad&\sum_{e\in E}w_{e}\leq V_{\max}\cdot|E|;\quad\mathbf{w}\in\{0,1\}^{|E|}\end{aligned}\right.

(6)

where
s_{ee^{\prime}}\in\{1,\sqrt{2}\}
are the edge weights. We can now define our ADMM algorithm.

Initialize:

\mathbf{v}^{(0)},\mathbf{w}^{(0)},\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(0)},j=0
, set
\tau_{0}=\max(1,\|\mathbf{w}^{(0)}-\mathbf{v}^{(0)}\|^{2}_{2})\,\gamma

while

\|\mathbf{w}^{(j)}-\mathbf{v}^{(j)}\|_{2}^{2}>\delta

do

\mathbf{v}^{*}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathbf{v}}\texttt{Cont}(\mathbf{v};{\mathbf{w}^{(j)}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(j)}})
;

//
cont. subprob.

\mathbf{w}^{*}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathbf{w}}\texttt{Disc}(\mathbf{w};{\mathbf{v}^{(j+1)}},\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(j)})
;

//
discr. subprob.

if

\|\mathbf{w}^{(j+1)}-\mathbf{v}^{(j+1)}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\beta\,\tau^{(j)}

then

\tau^{(j+1)}\leftarrow(1-\zeta)\tau^{(j)}+\zeta(\|\mathbf{w}^{(j+1)}-\mathbf{v}^{(j+1)}\|^{2}_{2})
;

\mathbf{v}^{(j+1)}\leftarrow\mathbf{v}^{*};\quad\mathbf{w}^{(j+1)}\leftarrow\mathbf{w}^{*}
;

//
accept step

\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(j+1)}\leftarrow\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(j)}+\rho^{(j)}(\mathbf{w}^{(j+1)}-\mathbf{v}^{(j+1)})
;

//
update multiplier

else

\mathbf{v}^{(j+1)}\leftarrow\mathbf{v}^{(j)};\quad\mathbf{w}^{(j+1)}\leftarrow\mathbf{w}^{(j)}
;

//
reject step

\rho^{(j+1)}\leftarrow c\,\rho^{(j)}
;

//
update penalty

j\leftarrow j+1
;

Algorithm 1

ADMM for Topology Optimization

Unlike traditional ADMM methods that use a fixed penalty parameter, we adopt the funnel-based strategy from
Kiessling et al. 2024
to adaptively increase the penalty when constraint infeasibility is high. Let the tolerance threshold be
\beta\tau^{(j)}
, with
\tau^{(0)}=\max(1,\|\mathbf{w}^{(0)}-\mathbf{v}^{(0)}\|^{2}_{2})\gamma
and some constant
\gamma>1
. If the computed step is such that
\|\mathbf{w}^{(j+1)}-\mathbf{v}^{(j+1)}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\beta\tau^{(j)}
, the step is accepted and the threshold is tightened via
\tau^{(j+1)}\leftarrow(1-\zeta)\tau^{(j)}+\zeta(\|\mathbf{w}^{(j+1)}-\mathbf{v}^{(j+1)}\|^{2}_{2})
, for
0<\zeta<1
. Otherwise, the step is rejected and the penalty parameter
\rho
is increased by a factor
c>1
.

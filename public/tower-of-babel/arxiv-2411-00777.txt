Besselian Schauder Frames and the Structure of Banach Spaces
Rafik Karkri

Abstract
Schauder bases are fundamental tools for analyzing the structure of Banach spaces. In this work, we show that Besselian Schauder frames (BSF) play a similar role in certain contexts. BSF are a new class of Schauder frames, lying between unconditional and general frames. We first prove that every unconditional Schauder frame (USF) is BSF, but the reverse implication is false. Specifically, we extend several well-known results of Karlin and James to Banach spaces with BSF, particularly to those with USF. We prove that many classical Banach spaces do not admit BSF, and in particular, do not admit USF. Before establishing these results, for every Banach space E E with a finite dimensional decomposition, we provide an explicit method to construct a Schauder frame for E E . In particular, Szarek’s Banach space has a Schauder frame, which famously lacks a Schauder basis. This finding provides strong motivation for extending classical Schauder basis theory to the framework of Schauder frames.

Introduction

In 1932, Banach and Mazur
[
3
, theorem 9, p. 185]

proved that every separable Banach space is isometrically
embeddable into
C([0,1])
(the Banach space of continuous functions on the closed
interval
[0,1]
).
The existence of a Schauder basis for
C([0,1])

[
21
, example 4.1.11, p. 352]
led Banach to ask whether every separable Banach space has a Schauder basis
[
3
, p. 111]
.

Recall that a sequence

\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
in a Banach space

E
is called a Schauder basis (in short, basis) for
E
if, for every
x\in E
, there exists a unique sequence of scalars
\left(\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

such that

x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}x_{n}
. If, in addition, the series

\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}x_{n}
converges unconditionally to
x
for every

x\in E
, the sequence is called an unconditional Schauder basis (in short, unconditional basis) for
E
.

This problem (known as the basis problem) remained open for a long time and was solved in 1973 in the
negative by P. Enflo
[
11
]
.

In 1987, S. J. Szarek
[
25
]
constructed a complemented subspace without a Schauder basis in a space that does possess a Schauder basis, thereby solving a fifty-year-old problem. This result established that the Schauder basis property is not inherited by complemented subspaces.

In 1948, Karlin
[
17
]

used the concept of unconditional bases to investigate the relationships between the structures of a Banach space
E
, its dual
E^{*}
and its bidual
E^{**}
.
Among other results, he studied reflexivity and weak sequential completeness, proving the following:

Theorem (Karlin):

Let
E
be a Banach space with an unconditional basis. If
E^{*}
is separable, then:

1.

E^{*}
is weakly sequentially complete;

2.

E^{*}
has an unconditional basis.

In the same paper, Karlin posed the following question:

Question (Karlin):

If
E^{*}
has a basis, does
E
itself have a basis?

In 1971, Johnson, Rosenthal, and Zippin

[
14
]
provided an affirmative answer to this question, thereby resolving one of the fundamental problems in basis theory.

In
1950
, James
[
13
]
used the concepts of shrinking and boundedly complete
basis as tools in the structure theory of Banach spaces.

The concept of frames (generalizing the concept of bases) for Hilbert spaces was introduced in
1952
by Duffin and Schaeffer
[
10
]
. A frame for a Hilbert space

\left(\mathcal{H},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle\right)
is a sequence

\left(h_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
in
\mathcal{H}
for which there exist constants
A,B>0
such that:

A\left\|h\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\left|\left\langle h,h_{n}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq B\left\|h\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\;\;(h\in\mathcal{H}).

(1)

This condition allows each

x\in\mathcal{H}
to be
written as:

x=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\left\langle x,h_{n}\right\rangle h_{n},

but the linear independence between the elements of

\left(h_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is not required.
If the upper inequality in (
1
) holds,

\left(h_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is called
a Bessel sequence with bound
B
.

The concept of frames (as a generalization
of Schauder bases and Hilbert frames) for Banach spaces was
introduced in
2008
by
Casazza, Dilworth, Odell, Schlumprecht, and Zsak
[
7
]
.
A Schauder frame for a given Banach space
E
is a sequence

\left((a_{n},b_{n}^{*})\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\subset E\times E^{*}

such that, for each
x
in
E
, we have:

x=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}b_{n}^{*}\left(x\right)a_{n}.

In 2021, a separate group of authors
[
16
]
introduced the concept of Besselian Schauder frames for Banach spaces by adding a Besselian property to this definition. Specifically:

Definition.

A sequence
\mathcal{F}:=\left(\left(a_{n},b_{n}^{*}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
in
E\times E^{*}
is called a Besselian Schauder frame (BSF) for
E
if the following conditions hold:

1.

\mathcal{F}
is a Schauder frame for
E
;

2.

There exists a constant
B>0
such that:

\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{n}^{*}\left(x\right)\right|\left|y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)\right|\leq B\left\|x\right\|_{E}\left\|y^{*}\right\|_{E^{*}},\;\;\;(x\in E,y^{*}\in E^{*}).

The second condition generalizes the upper inequality in (
1
)(but, in contrast to the Hilbert space case, it does not generally imply unconditional convergence of the frame) and reveals the dual space structure that is not apparent in the definition of frames for Hilbert spaces
(since
\mathcal{H}^{*}\cong\mathcal{H}
).

In 2009, R. Liu
[
20
]
extended James’s results on shrinking and boundedly complete bases to unconditional Schauder frames by introducing the concepts of locally shrinking frames and locally boundedly complete frames.

In the same year, a separate group of authors—Carando and Lassalle
[
4
]
, and D. Carando , S. Lassalle and P. Schmidberg
[
5
]
—extended these results to Banach spaces with unconditional atomic decompositions, and hence to Banach spaces with unconditional Schauder frames, by introducing a more general definition of a shrinking frame.

In this paper, we generalize these results (using a new and general definition of shrinking frame, which coincides with the natural definition in the basis setting) to the large class of Banach spaces with Besselian Schauder frames, and in particular to Banach spaces with unconditional Schauder frames. We prove the following theorem:

Theorem A.

Let
\mathcal{F}:=\left(\left(a_{n},b_{n}^{*}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be a Besselian Schauder frame for a Banach space
E
. Then:

1.

\mathcal{F}

is shrinking if and only if there is no subspace of
E
isomorphic to

\ell_{1}
.

2.

\mathcal{F}
is boundedly complete for
E

if and only if

E
is weakly sequentially complete
if and only if
there is no subspace of
E
isomorphic to
c_{0}
.

3.

E
is reflexive
if and only if
E
contains no isomorphic copies of
\ell_{1}
or
c_{0}
.

On the other hand, we generalize some well-known results of Karlin
[
17
]
to Banach spaces with Besselian Schauder frames (and, in particular, to those with unconditional Schauder frames); see
theorem B
in section (
2
).

It is well-known that:

1.

Every Banach space with an unconditional Schauder basis possesses Pełczyński’s property (u)
[
2
, proposition 3.5.3, p. 67]
.

2.

The classical Banach spaces such as

C(\omega^{\omega})
,
C([0,1])
,

L_{1}([0,1])
, the James space
\mathcal{J}
,

K(\ell_{p},\ell_{q})
,

\ell_{p}\otimes_{\pi}\ell_{q^{*}}
and
\ell_{p^{*}}\otimes_{\varepsilon}\ell_{q}
(for
p\geq q>1
) do not admit unconditional Schauder bases.

In this paper, we extend these results to Banach spaces with Besselian Schauder frames (and, in particular, to those with unconditional Schauder frames).

Finally, for every Banach space
E
with a finite dimensional decomposition,
we provide an explicit method to construct a Schauder frame for
E
. As a result, Szarek’s famous example
[
25
]
serves as an illustration of a Banach space with a Schauder frame that does not admit a Schauder basis. This example underscores the importance of generalizing classical results from Banach spaces with Schauder bases to those with Schauder frames.

3
Schauder frames

The purpose of this section is to examine the relationships between the following concepts:

1.

Schauder frames (and bases),

2.

Besselian Schauder frames (and bases), and

3.

unconditional Schauder frames (and bases).

Additionally, we establish several characterizations of Besselian Schauder frames.

Definition 1
.

[
7
, definition 2.2, p. 68]

A sequence
\mathcal{F}
is called a Schauder frame (in short, frame) (resp.
unconditional Schauder frame (in short, unconditional frame) ) for
E
if for all
x\in E
, the series
\sum_{n}b_{n}^{*}\left(x\right)a_{n}
is convergent (resp. unconditionally
convergent) in
E
to
x
.

It is an open problem whether every complemented subspace of a space with
unconditional basis has an unconditional basis

[
6
, problem 1.8, p. 279]
.
The example (
1
) shows (without any difficulty)
that a frame passes to complemented subspaces.

Example 1
.

Every complemented subspace of a Banach space with a frame (resp. an unconditional frame) has a frame (resp. an unconditional frame).

Proof.

Let
E
be a Banach space with an unconditional frame

\mathcal{F}:=\left(\left(a_{n},b_{n}^{*}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\subset E\times E^{*}
,
and let
F
be a complemented subspace of
E
with corresponding continuous projection
P:E\rightarrow F
. For any permutation
\sigma
of
\mathbb{N}^{*}
and any
x\in F
, we have:

x=P\left(x\right)=\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}b_{\sigma(n)}^{*}(x)P\left(a_{\sigma(n)}\right).

It follows that

\left(\left(P\left(a_{n}\right),b_{n\left|F\right.}^{*}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

is an unconditional frame for
F

(where
b_{n\left|F\right.}^{*}

is the restriction of
b_{n}^{*}
to
F
).
If
\mathcal{F}
is just a frame, it suffices to put (
\sigma=Id
). ∎

In
[
6
, proposition 6.5, p. 300]
, it is proved that if
(E_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a FDD of
E
and each

E_{n}
has a basis
((e_{n,i},e^{*}_{n,i}))_{i=1}^{d_{n}}
with basis constant
K_{n}

such that
\underset{n}{\sup}K_{n}<\infty
then, the sequence

(((e_{n,i},e^{*}_{n,i}))_{i=1}^{d_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

is a basis for
E
.

In the frames setting (proposition (
1
)),
we provide an explicit method to construct a frame for
E
from the
bases of each
E_{n}
without requiring

\underset{n}{\sup}K_{n}<\infty
.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that
E
has an FDD. Then
E
has a frame.

Proof.

Let
(E_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be an FDD of
E
. Suppose that for each

n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the sequence

\left((e_{n,i},e^{*}_{n,i})\right)_{i=1}^{d_{n}}
is a basis for
E_{n}
with

\|e^{*}_{n,i}\|=\|e_{n,i}\|=1
.
Define a sequence
((a_{n,j},b^{*}_{n,j}))_{j=1}^{d_{n}^{2}}
in
E_{n}
by:

a_{n,kd_{n}+r}=d_{n}^{-1}e_{n,r}\;\;\text{and}\;\;b^{*}_{n,kd_{n}+r}=e^{*}_{n,r},

for each

1\leq r\leq d_{n}
and
0\leq k\leq d_{n}-1
. Then

((a_{n,j},b^{*}_{n,j}))_{j=1}^{d_{n}^{2}}
is a frame for

E_{n}
. We claim that the sequence

\left(\left((a_{n,j},b^{*}_{n,j}\circ P_{n})\right)_{j=1}^{d_{n}^{2}}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

is a frame for
E
.
To verify this, let

x\in E
and
\varepsilon>0
. By the properties of the FDD, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that for all
n\geq N
, we have

\left\|\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}P_{k}(x)-x\right\|_{E}\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\left\|P_{n}(x)\right\|_{E}\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence it suffices to prove that for each
1\leq m<d_{n}^{2}
we have

\left\|S_{n-1,m}(x)-x\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon,

where

S_{n-1,m}(x):=\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\overset{d_{k}^{2}}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}b^{*}_{k,j}(P_{k}(x))a_{k,j}+\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}b^{*}_{n,i}(P_{n}(x))a_{n,i}.

For each
1\leq m<d_{n}^{2}
, there exists
0\leq\alpha\leq d_{n}-1
such that:

\alpha d_{n}<m\leq(\alpha+1)d_{n}.

We then decompose
A:=\left\|\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}b^{*}_{n,i}(P_{n}(x))a_{n,i}\right\|_{E}
as follows:

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\left\|\overset{\alpha d_{n}}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}b^{*}_{n,i}(P_{n}(x))a_{n,i}+\overset{m}{\underset{i=\alpha d_{n}+1}{\sum}}b^{*}_{n,i}(P_{n}(x))a_{n,i}\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{d_{n}-1}\sum_{r=1}^{d_{n}}b^{*}_{n,kd_{n}+r}(P_{n}(x))a_{n,kd_{n}+r}+\overset{m-\alpha d_{n}}{\underset{r=1}{\sum}}b^{*}_{n,\alpha d_{n}+r}(P_{n}(x))a_{n,\alpha d_{n}+r}\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{d_{n}-1}\sum_{r=1}^{d_{n}}d_{n}^{-1}e^{*}_{n,r}(P_{n}(x))e_{n,r}+\overset{m-\alpha d_{n}}{\underset{r=1}{\sum}}d_{n}^{-1}e^{*}_{n,r}(P_{n}(x))e_{n,r}\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{d_{n}-1}d_{n}^{-1}P_{n}(x)+\overset{m-\alpha d_{n}}{\underset{r=1}{\sum}}d_{n}^{-1}e^{*}_{n,r}(P_{n}(x))e_{n,r}\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|P_{n}(x)\right\|_{E}+\frac{m-\alpha d_{n}}{d_{n}}\left\|P_{n}(x)\right\|_{E}.

Consequently

\left\|S_{n-1,m}(x)-x\right\|_{E}\leq\left\|\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}P_{k}(x)-x\right\|_{E}+\left\|\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}b^{*}_{n,i}(P_{n}(x))a_{n,i}\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon,

which finishes the proof.∎

Lemma 1
.

Let
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}z_{m}
be a series in a Banach space
E
. The following statements are equivalent:

1.

The series converges unconditionally.

2.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that for all finite sets
A\subseteq\{M+1,M+2,\ldots\}
and all scalars
\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{K}
with
|\lambda_{m}|\leq 1
:

\left\|\sum_{m\in A}\lambda_{m}z_{m}\right\|_{E}<\varepsilon.

Proof.

The implication
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
is classical. Indeed, if condition (2) holds, then in particular (by taking
\lambda_{m}=1
for all
m
) the series satisfies the Cauchy criterion and is therefore convergent. The unconditional convergence then follows from a standard characterization
[
24
, Theorem 16.1, p. 461]
.

Now, assume that the series converges unconditionally. Suppose, for contradiction, that condition (2) fails. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
M\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, there exist a finite set
A_{M}\subseteq\{M+1,M+2,\ldots\}
and scalars
(\lambda_{m}^{M})_{m\in A_{M}}
with
|\lambda_{m}^{M}|\leq 1
satisfying:

\left\|\sum_{m\in A_{M}}\lambda_{m}^{M}z_{m}\right\|_{E}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

We now construct a bounded sequence
(\lambda_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
such that the series
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}z_{m}
diverges, contradicting the classical characterization of unconditional convergence.

1.

Let
M_{1}=1
. Choose a finite set
A_{1}\subseteq\{M_{1}+1,M_{1}+2,\ldots\}
and scalars
(\lambda_{m}^{1})_{m\in A_{1}}
with
|\lambda_{m}^{1}|\leq 1
such that

\left\|\sum_{m\in A_{1}}\lambda_{m}^{1}z_{m}\right\|_{E}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

2.

Set
M_{2}=\max A_{1}+1
. Choose a finite set
A_{2}\subseteq\{M_{2}+1,M_{2}+2,\ldots\}
and scalars
(\lambda_{m}^{2})_{m\in A_{2}}
with
|\lambda_{m}^{2}|\leq 1
such that

\left\|\sum_{m\in A_{2}}\lambda_{m}^{2}z_{m}\right\|_{E}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

3.

Proceed inductively: having defined
M_{k}
and
A_{k}
, set
M_{k+1}=\max A_{k}+1
, and choose a finite set
A_{k+1}\subseteq\{M_{k+1}+1,M_{k+1}+2,\ldots\}
and scalars
(\lambda_{m}^{k+1})_{m\in A_{k+1}}
with
|\lambda_{m}^{k+1}|\leq 1
such that

\left\|\sum_{m\in A_{k+1}}\lambda_{m}^{k+1}z_{m}\right\|_{E}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

Define the sequence
(\lambda_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
by:

\lambda_{m}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{m}^{k}&\text{if }m\in A_{k}\text{ for some }k,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By construction,
|\lambda_{m}|\leq 1
for all
m
, so
(\lambda_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is bounded. Now consider the partial sums over the finite sets
S_{k}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}
. The sets
(A_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
are finite, disjoint, and their union is increasing. For each
k\geq 2
, we have:

\left\|\sum_{m\in S_{k}}\lambda_{m}z_{m}-\sum_{m\in S_{k-1}}\lambda_{m}z_{m}\right\|_{E}=\left\|\sum_{m\in A_{k}}\lambda_{m}^{k}z_{m}\right\|_{E}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

This shows that the sequence of partial sums
\left(\sum_{m\in S_{k}}\lambda_{m}z_{m}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is not Cauchy. Therefore, the series
\sum\lambda_{m}z_{m}
diverges, contradicting the assumption of unconditional convergence.∎

The following theorem is inspired by
[
1
, p. 32-33]
.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is an unconditional frame for
E
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is a Besselian frame for
E
.

Proof
.
For each
M\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and

(s_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in\mathcal{S}
, the linear operator

T_{M,s}:E\rightarrow E,\;x\mapsto\sum_{m=1}^{M}s_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}

is bounded. By unconditionality of
\mathcal{F}
and the Banach-Steinhaus theorem, the linear operator

T_{s}:E\rightarrow E,\;x\mapsto\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}s_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}

is bounded.
We now show that for each
x\in E
, the mapping

s\mapsto T_{s}(x)
is continuous on the compact metric space

\mathcal{S}
.

Fix
x\in E
and
\varepsilon>0
. By lemma (
1
), there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that for all finite sets

A\subseteq\{M+1,M+2,\dots\}
and all scalars
\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{K}
with

|\lambda_{m}|\leq 1
:

\left\|\sum_{m\in A}\lambda_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}\right\|_{E}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Now, for
s,t\in\mathcal{S}
, we estimate the difference:

\|T_{s}(x)-T_{t}(x)\|_{E}\leq\underbrace{\left\|\sum_{m=1}^{M}(s_{m}-t_{m})b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}\right\|_{E}}_{\text{(I)}}+\underbrace{\left\|\sum_{m=M+1}^{\infty}(s_{m}-t_{m})b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}\right\|_{E}}_{\text{(II)}}.

Estimate (II): Since
|s_{m}-t_{m}|\leq 2
, define
\lambda_{m}=\frac{s_{m}-t_{m}}{2}
(
|\lambda_{m}|\leq 1
). Then,

\text{(II)}\leq 2\sup_{A\subseteq\{M+1,\dots\}}\left\|\sum_{m\in A}\lambda_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}\right\|_{E}\leq 2\frac{\varepsilon}{4}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Estimate (I): Let
C=\max_{1\leq m\leq M}\|b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}\|_{E}
. Choose

\delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{M+1}MC}.

If
d(s,t)<\delta
, then for each
m\leq M
,

|s_{m}-t_{m}|<2^{m}d(s,t)<2^{m}\delta\leq 2^{M}\delta.

Hence,

\text{(I)}\leq\sum_{m=1}^{M}|s_{m}-t_{m}|C<M2^{M}\delta C=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Combining (I) and (II), we conclude that:

\|T_{s}(x)-T_{t}(x)\|_{E}<\varepsilon

whenever
d(s,t)<\delta
. Thus,
s\mapsto T_{s}(x)
is continuous on

\mathcal{S}
.

Since
\mathcal{S}
is compact, the set

\left\{\|T_{s}(x)\|_{E}:s\in\mathcal{S}\right\}
is bounded.
By the uniform boundedness principle, there is a constant
\mathcal{D}>0
such that

\left\|\underset{m=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}s_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}\right\|_{E}\leq\mathcal{D}\|x\|_{E},\;\;\;\left(x\in E,\;(s_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in\mathcal{S}\right).

Now fix
x\in E
and
y^{*}\in E^{*}
. Choose a sequence of signs

s=\left(s_{m}\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
such that

|b_{m}^{*}(x)y^{*}(a_{m})|=s_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)y^{*}(a_{m}),\;\;\;(m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}).

(with
s_{m}=1
if
b_{m}^{*}(x)y^{*}(a_{m})=0
) Then:

\displaystyle\underset{m=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{m}^{*}(x)y^{*}(a_{m})\right|

\displaystyle=\overset{\infty}{\underset{m=1}{\sum}}s_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)y^{*}(a_{m})=\left|y^{*}\left(\overset{\infty}{\underset{m=1}{\sum}}s_{m}b_{m}^{*}(x)a_{m}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|y^{*}\left(T_{s}(x)\right)\right|\leq\|y^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\left\|T_{s}(x)\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\|y^{*}\|_{E^{*}}\left\|T_{s}\right\|_{L(E)}\cdot\left\|x\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{D}\left\|x\right\|_{E}\|y^{*}\|_{E^{*}}.

Thus
\mathcal{F}
is a Besselian Schauder frame for
E
.∎

Lemma 3
.

There exists a series
\sum x_{n}
in
c_{0}
such that:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|y^{*}(x_{n})|\leq 2\|y^{*}\|_{\ell_{1}},\;\;(y^{*}\in c_{0}^{*})

and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}
converge conditionally (i.e., it converges but not unconditionally) in
c_{0}
to
0
.

Proof.

We construct a series
\sum x_{n}
in
c_{0}
as follows. For each
k\geq 1
, define a block of
2k
terms, all affecting only the
k
-th coordinate.
For
j=1,\dots,k
:

x_{N_{k}+2j-1}=\dfrac{1}{k}e_{k},

x_{N_{k}+2j}=-\dfrac{1}{k}e_{k},

where
N_{k}=2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}i=k(k-1)
(with
N_{1}=0
), and
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is the standard unit vector basis of
c_{0}
. The terms are ordered by completing each block consecutively.

1.

Convergence in
c_{0}
:

The partial sum after each complete block is the zero vector. Within block
k
, the sup-norm of any partial sum is at most
1/k
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, choose
K
such that
1/K<\varepsilon
. For all
n>N_{K}
, the partial sum
S_{n}
involves only blocks with index
\geq K+1
or an incomplete blocks of index
\leq K
. In either case
\|S_{n}\|_{c_{0}}\leq 1/K<\varepsilon
. Hence
\sum x_{n}
converges to
0
in
c_{0}
.

2.

Weakly unconditionally Cauchy (WUC):

The dual space
c_{0}^{*}
is isometrically isomorphic to
\ell_{1}
.
Every
y^{*}\in c_{0}^{*}
corresponds to a sequence
(y^{*}_{k})\in\ell_{1}
with
\|y^{*}\|_{\ell_{1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|y^{*}_{k}|
.
For each
k
, the block contributes
2k
terms, each of absolute value
|y^{*}(x_{n})|=|y^{*}_{k}|/k
.
Therefore,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|y^{*}(x_{n})|=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2k\cdot\frac{|y^{*}_{k}|}{k}=2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|y^{*}_{k}|=2\|y^{*}\|_{\ell_{1}}<\infty.

Thus the series is WUC.

3.

Not unconditionally convergent:

A series
\sum x_{n}
in a Banach space converges unconditionally if and only if
\sum\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}
converges for every choice of signs
\varepsilon_{n}\in\{\pm 1\}
.
Choose
\varepsilon_{n}
as follows:

\varepsilon_{n}=\begin{cases}+1&\text{if }x_{n}=\frac{1}{k}e_{k},\\
-1&\text{if }x_{n}=-\frac{1}{k}e_{k}.\end{cases}

Hence,

\sum\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2k\text{ terms of }\frac{1}{k}e_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2e_{k}.

The partial sums are
S_{K}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}2e_{k}
, and

\|S_{K+1}-S_{K}\|_{c_{0}}=\|2e_{K+1}\|_{c_{0}}=2,

which does not tend to
0
. Thus the series
\sum\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}
does not converge in
c_{0}
.
By the sign criterion, the original series
\sum x_{n}
is not unconditionally convergent.

Thus,
\sum x_{n}
is conditionally convergent (not unconditionally) and weakly unconditionally Cauchy in
c_{0}
.

Theorem 3
.

There exists a Besselian frame which is not unconditional.

Proof

We construct a frame
(a_{n},b^{*}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
for
c_{0}
using the series from lemma (
3
). Let
\sum x_{n}
be the series in
c_{0}
constructed in lemma (
3
), which converges conditionally to
0
and is weakly unconditionally Cauchy (WUC). Let
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
denote the standard unit vector basis of
c_{0}
and
(e^{*}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
the corresponding coordinate functionals. Fix a nonzero functional
\varphi\in c_{0}^{*}
; for instance, take
\varphi=e_{1}^{*}
.

Define the frame by interleaving terms as follows. For each

k=1,2,3,\dots
:

a_{2k-1}=e_{k},\quad b_{2k-1}^{*}=e_{k}^{*}.

a_{2k}=x_{k},\quad b_{2k}^{*}=\varphi.

We verify the required properties:

1.

Frame property:

For any
x\in c_{0}
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}^{*}(x)a_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e_{k}^{*}(x)e_{k}+\varphi(x)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}=x+\varphi(x)\cdot 0=x,

since
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}=0
by lemma (
3
). Thus
(a_{n},b_{n}^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a frame for
c_{0}
.

2.

Besselian Condition:

For any
x\in c_{0}
and
y^{*}\in c_{0}^{*}\cong\ell^{1}
, using example (
4
) and lemma(
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}^{*}(x)y^{*}(a_{n})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|e_{k}^{*}(x)y^{*}(e_{k})|+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\varphi(x)y^{*}(x_{k})|

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|_{c_{0}}\|y^{*}\|_{\ell_{1}}+\|x\|_{c_{0}}\|\varphi\|_{\ell_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|y^{*}(x_{k})|

\displaystyle=3\|x\|_{c_{0}}\|y^{*}\|_{\ell_{1}}

which establishes that
(a_{n},b_{n}^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a Besselian frame with constant
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\leq 3
.

3.

Conditional convergence for some
x
:

Take
x=e_{1}
. Then
\varphi(e_{1})=1
, and the frame expansion becomes

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}^{*}(e_{1})a_{n}=e_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}.

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}=0
, this series converges to
e_{1}
in the given ordering.
However, because
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}
converges conditionally to
0
, there exists a rearrangement
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{\sigma(k)}
that does not converge to
0
(or converges to a different limit). Consequently, rearranging the even-indexed terms of the frame expansion accordingly yields a series that does not converge to
e_{1}
. Therefore, the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}^{*}(e_{1})a_{n}
does not converge unconditionally to
e_{1}
, i.e., it converges conditionally.

Thus, we have constructed a Besselian frame for
c_{0}
and an element
e_{1}\in c_{0}
such that the frame expansion converges conditionally to
e_{1}
.

Theorem 4
.

\mathcal{F}
is a Besselian sequence of
E
if and only if
for every
x\in E
and
y^{*}\in E^{*}
, the following condition holds:

\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{n}^{*}\left(x\right)\right|\left|y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)\right|<\infty.

(2)

Proof.

Suppose that the inequality (
2
) holds
for each
x\in E
and
y^{*}\in E^{*}
.
Let
y^{*}\in E^{*}
and

(s_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in\mathcal{S}
,
an argument analogous to theorem (
2
) yields a constant

\mathcal{D}_{y^{*}}>0
such that:

\left|\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}s_{n}b_{n}^{*}(x)y^{*}(a_{n})\right|\leq\mathcal{D}_{y^{*}}\|x\|_{E},\;\;\;\left(x\in E,\;(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in\mathcal{S}\right).

Consequently, we obtain:

\displaystyle\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{n}^{*}(x)y^{*}(a_{n})\right|\leq\mathcal{D}_{y^{*}}\left\|x\right\|_{E},\;\;\;(x\in E,y^{*}\in E^{*}).

(3)

Similarly, for each
x\in E
there is a constant

\mathcal{C}_{x}>0
such that

\left|\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}s_{n}b_{n}^{*}(x)y^{*}(a_{n})\right|\leq\mathcal{C}_{x}\|y^{*}\|_{E^{*}},\;\;\;\left(y^{*}\in E^{*},\;(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in\mathcal{S}\right),

therefore we obtain:

\displaystyle\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{n}^{*}(x)y^{*}(a_{n})\right|\leq\mathcal{C}_{x}\left\|y^{*}\right\|_{E^{*}},\;\;\;(x\in E,y^{*}\in E^{*}).

(4)

Finally, combining inequalities (
4
) and
(
3
) with the method of
[
15
, theorem 3.6, p. 232]
we conclude that
\mathcal{F}
is Besselian. The reverse implication is immediate from the definition.∎

The existence of Banach spaces with unconditional frames that lack unconditional bases remains an open problem. In other words, is the inclusion

\left\{\text{Banach spaces with}\;USB\right\}\subsetneq\{\text{Banach spaces with}\;USF\}

strict? (see
[
8
, remark 5.8, p. 170]
).

Problem.
Does there exist a Banach space
E
that admits a Besselian frame but no unconditional frame? In other words, is the inclusion

\left\{\text{Banach spaces with}\;USF\right\}\subsetneq\{\text{Banach spaces with}\;BSF\}

strict? A positive answer, together with corollary (
5
) would show that the class of spaces with Besselian frames lies strictly between the class of spaces with unconditional frames and the class of spaces with frames. These two problems form a natural hierarchy of questions about the strictness of the inclusions between classes of Banach spaces defined by increasingly general frame properties.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a Besselian frame for
E
,
\left(x_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a weakly Cauchy sequence
in
E
and
y^{*}\in E^{*}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}y^{*}(x_{r})=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right).

(5)

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is Besselian, the first equality follows immediately from the frame representation

x_{r}=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})a_{n},\;\;\left(r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\right).

Consider the operator:

U:E\rightarrow\ell_{1};\;\;x\mapsto\left(b_{n}^{*}(x)y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}.

The Besselian property ensures that
U
is bounded. Thus,

\left(U(x_{r})\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a weakly Cauchy sequence in
\ell_{1}
. By the Schur property of
\ell_{1}
, this implies that
\left(U(x_{r})\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a Cauchy sequence.
Let
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
r_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that:

\underset{n=m}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\left|b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)-b_{n}^{*}(x_{r_{0}})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)\right|\leq\varepsilon,\;\;(r\geq r_{0},\;\;m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}).

Since the series
\sum_{n}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r_{0}})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)

is convergent, there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
for each
m\geq N_{0}
we have

\left|\sum_{m}^{\infty}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r_{0}})y_{k}^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)\right|\leq\varepsilon
. Hence

\displaystyle\left|\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)-\underset{n=1}{\overset{m}{\sum}}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon,\;\;\;(r\geq r_{0},\;m\geq N_{0}).

Then

\left|\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)-\underset{n=1}{\overset{m}{\sum}}\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)\right|\leq 2\varepsilon,\;\;\;(m\geq N_{0}),

and consequently we have

\displaystyle\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right).

(6)

Which finishes the proof.∎

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a Besselian frame for
E
,
\left(y_{k}^{*}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a weakly Cauchy sequence
in
E^{*}
and
z^{**}\in E^{**}
. If the limit
of
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{**}(b_{n}^{*})y_{k}^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)
exists as
k
tends to infinity, then:

\displaystyle\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}z^{**}(b_{n}^{*})y_{k}^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}z^{**}(b_{n}^{*})y_{k}^{*}\left(a_{n}\right).

(7)

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is Besselian, its dual sequence

\mathcal{F}^{*}
is also Besselian. Consider the operator:

V:E^{*}\rightarrow\ell_{1};\;\;y^{*}\mapsto\left(z^{**}(b_{n}^{*})y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}.

The Besselian property ensures that
V
is bounded. Thus,

\left(V(y_{k}^{*})\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a weakly Cauchy sequence, and hence
is a Cauchy sequence.
The conclusion now follows by an argument analogous to that in lemma (

Lemma 6

Let
\mathcal{G}:=\left(\left(b_{n}^{*},\phi_{n}^{**}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

\left(x_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{***}(\phi_{n}^{**})b_{n}^{*}(x_{r}),

exists as

\displaystyle\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}z^{***}(\phi_{n}^{**})b_{n}^{*}\left(x_{r}\right)=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}z^{***}(\phi_{n}^{**})b_{n}^{*}\left(x_{r}\right).

(8)

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a Besselian frame for
E
and

is separable. Then

is weakly sequentially complete.

Proof.

Let
\left(y_{k}^{*}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be a weakly Cauchy
sequence in
E^{*}
.
Then, for each
\phi\in E^{**}
the limit of
\phi(y_{k}^{*})
exists as
k
tends to infinity.
In particular, there exists an element
y_{0}^{*}\in E^{*}
such that
y_{k}^{*}(x)\rightarrow y_{0}^{*}(x)

for each
x\in E
. To prove the theorem, it suffices to show that

\phi(y_{k}^{*})\rightarrow\phi(y_{0}^{*})
for every
\phi\in E^{**}
.
Indeed, let
\phi\in E^{**}
. Since
E^{*}
is separable, Gelfand theorem (see
[
12
]
) guarantees the existence of a sequence

\left(x_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
in
E
such that:

\displaystyle\phi(y^{*})=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}y^{*}(x_{r}),\;\;\;(y^{*}\in E^{*}).

(9)

Consequently, by lemmas (
4

5

\displaystyle\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\phi(y_{k}^{*})

\displaystyle=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}y_{k}^{*}(x_{r})=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y_{k}^{*}(a_{n})

\displaystyle=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\phi(b_{n}^{*})y_{k}^{*}(a_{n})=\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\phi(b_{n}^{*})y_{0}^{*}(a_{n})

\displaystyle=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y_{0}^{*}(a_{n})=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})y_{k}^{*}(a_{n})

\displaystyle=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}y_{k}^{*}(x_{r})=\phi(y_{0}^{*}).

Therefore,
E
is weakly sequentially complete.∎

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}:=\left(\left(b_{n}^{*},\phi_{n}^{**}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

is WSC.

Proof.

Let
\left(y_{k}^{*}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be a weakly Cauchy sequence in

such that
y_{k}^{*}(x)\rightarrow y_{0}^{*}(x)

\phi(y_{k}^{*})\rightarrow\phi(y_{0}^{*})

\phi\in E^{**}

Let
\phi\in E^{**}

E^{*}

[
12
]

\phi(y^{*})=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}y^{*}(x_{r}),\;\;\;(y^{*}\in E^{*}).

. For each

\displaystyle\left|\underset{k=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\beta_{k}\alpha_{k}\right|

\displaystyle=\underset{i\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\left|\underset{k=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\beta_{k}\phi_{k}^{**}(y_{i}^{*})\right|=\underset{i\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\left|\left(\underset{k=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\beta_{k}\phi_{k}^{**}\right)(y_{i}^{*})\right|

\displaystyle=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\phi_{n}^{**}(y_{k}^{*})b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})=y_{0}^{*}(x_{r}),

\displaystyle\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\phi(y_{k}^{*})

\displaystyle=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}y_{k}^{*}(x_{r})=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\phi_{n}^{**}(y_{k}^{*})b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})

\displaystyle=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\phi_{n}^{**}(y_{k}^{*})\phi(b_{n}^{*})=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\alpha_{n}\phi(b_{n}^{*})

\displaystyle=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}z^{***}(\phi_{n}^{**})\phi(b_{n}^{*})=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}z^{***}(\phi_{n}^{**})b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})

\displaystyle=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\phi_{n}^{**}(y_{k}^{*})J_{E}(x_{r})(b_{n}^{*})=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\phi_{n}^{**}(y_{k}^{*})b_{n}^{*}(x_{r})

\displaystyle=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}y_{k}^{*}(x_{r})=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}y_{0}^{*}(x_{r})=\phi(y_{0}^{*}).

This completes the proof.∎

Theorem 11

\mathcal{F}

Proof.

1.

(1)\Longrightarrow(2)

\alpha_{n}=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}b_{n}^{*}(x_{r}).

Since
\mathcal{F}
is Besselian, it follows
by lemma (
4

\displaystyle\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}y^{*}(x_{r})=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\alpha_{n}y^{*}\left(a_{n}\right),\;\;\;(y^{*}\in E^{*}).

(10)

If the series
\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}a_{n}
is convergent, then

\left(x_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
converges weakly to

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}a_{n}
. To verify convergence, observe that for any

and

\displaystyle\left|\underset{k=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\beta_{k}\alpha_{k}\right|

\displaystyle=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\left|\underset{k=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\beta_{k}b_{k}^{*}(x_{r})\right|=\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\left|\left(\underset{k=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\beta_{k}b_{k}^{*}\right)(x_{r})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\underset{r}{\sup}\|x_{r}\|_{E}\right)\left\|\underset{k=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\beta_{k}b_{k}^{*}\right\|_{E^{*}}.

By Helly’s theorem
[
3

Thus,
\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}a_{n}=\sum_{n}z^{**}(b_{n}^{*})a_{n}
, which converges by the bounded completeness of
\mathcal{F}
. Hence,

\left(x_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}a_{n}

is WSC.

2.

(2)\Longrightarrow(1)

3.

Construction of a novel copper-induced-cell-death-related gene signature for prognosis in colon cancer, with focus on KIF7
Authors: Hua Li, Jingying Chen, Zhengxian Liu, Lujuan Pan, Xiaoling Lan, Lihe Jiang, Fuda Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundColon cancer (CC) is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Accurate prognostic markers are essential for patient risk stratification and personalized treatment. Copper-induced cell-death-related genes (CRG) have emerged as potential players in cancer prognosis, yet their role in CC remains unclear.MethodsThis study aimed to comprehensively evaluate the expression of CRG and their roles in CC using gene expression and clinical data from TCGA and GEO databases. Univariate Cox regression and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) analyses identified prognostic genes, leading to the construction of a CRG prognostic signature. The signature’s predictive accuracy was validated using Kaplan-Meier survival curves, Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves, and a nomogram model. Additionally, we conducted experiments including immunofluorescence staining and cellular assays to validate the key genes’ biological functions.ResultsA 12-gene CRG signature was significantly associated with overall survival in CC patients. The high-risk group, as classified by median risk score, exhibited significantly shorter survival times compared to the low-risk group. The signature’s predictive accuracy was further confirmed with Area Under the Curve (AUC) scores exceeding 0.75 in TCGA and GSE17536 cohorts. Notably, the risk score was significantly correlated with immune checkpoints, chemotherapy sensitivity, and tumor microenvironment. Furthermore, the risk score showed a strong association with immunotherapy response in patients from GSE78220 and GSE39688 cohorts. Bioinformatics analysis of KIF7, a key gene within the signature, revealed its upregulation in CC and significant associations with tumor mutation burden, microsatellite instability, and immune cell infiltration across various cancers. Experiments confirmed that KIF7 was upregulated in CC and its knockdown reduced cell proliferation, migration, and invasion.ConclusionThe CRG prognostic signature can effectively predict overall survival, immune microenvironment and chemotherapy response in CC. KIF7, as a potential prognostic marker, has significant potential for the prediction and treatment of CC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-024-13315-1.Keywords: Copper-induced cell death, Cuproptosis, Prognostic signature, Immunotherapy, Tumor microenvironment, KIF7, Colon cancer

Introduction

Colon cancer (CC), a highly prevalent malignancy worldwide and ranks as the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1]. Recent epidemiological data indicate that over 1.9 million new cases of colorectal cancer (CRC) were diagnosed in 2020, with approximately 0.9 million fatalities reported during the same period [2]. Despite significant advancements in the detection and management of CC, the associated morbidity continues to rise, and the 5-year survival rate remains unacceptably low [3]. Although several prognostic indicators, such as disease stage and carcinoembryonic antigen levels, are available, accurately predicting the survival duration of CC patients remains a complex challenge. Therefore, a reliable prognostic evaluation model is essential for predicting clinical outcomes in CC patients and for enhancing therapeutic strategies.

Copper (Cu) is an essential trace element for both humans and microorganisms. However, it acts as a double-edged sword: while it is critical for enzyme production, even moderate intracellular concentrations can be toxic, resulting in cellular apoptosis [4]. In cancer patients, copper levels are significantly elevated in both serum and tumor tissues [4, 5]. Increased serum Cu concentrations are associated with disease stage and progression, particularly in colorectal and breast cancers [6]. Copper oxide nanoparticles have been shown to induce apoptosis in HT-29 human CRC cells by inhibiting the expression of Bcl-2 and Bcl-xl proteins [7]. Additionally, copper demonstrates anti-colorectal cancer properties, with mechanisms linked to the enhancement of immunogenic cancer cell death [8]. Diethyldithiocarbamate-copper complexes have been found to inhibit CRC progression by modulating the aerobic glycolysis pathway [9]. Stepien et al. discovered that copper plays a crucial role in inflammatory and oxidative pathways; higher copper levels are correlated with an increased risk of CC, particularly evident in the two years leading up to diagnosis [10].

Cuproptosis is a newly defined form of copper-dependent cellular demise that is distinct from established regulated cell death mechanisms, including apoptosis, ferroptosis, pyroptosis, and necroptosis [11]. It is dependent on mitochondrial respiration and is driven by the direct interaction of copper with lipoylated elements of the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle [11]. Recent research has increasingly focused on cuproptosis, recognizing its potential as a key therapeutic approach in cancer treatment [12]. Wang et al. investigated the role of cuproptosis in enhancing the sensitivity of prostate cancer cells to docetaxel, demonstrating that cuproptosis—mediated through DLAT and the mTOR signaling pathway—improves chemotherapeutic efficacy and may help overcome drug resistance in advanced prostate cancer [13]. Furthermore, recent studies indicate that excessive copper accumulation can promote CC by influencing cellular metabolism and increasing oxidative stress [14, 15]. Elevated copper concentrations are crucial for both the progression of CRC and its metastasis, especially to the liver. Recent study has demonstrated that copper levels in liver metastases from CRC are markedly higher compared to those in normal liver tissue [16]. Targeting copper ions presents a promising new strategy for CC treatment. Nevertheless, it remains uncertain whether the expression patterns of genes associated with copper-induced-cell-death-related genes (CRG) are related to the clinical outcomes of individuals with CC.

The objective of this investigation is to construct a novel CRG signature with the purpose of improving prognosis prediction in CC. By employing a thorough examination of CRG expression patterns and clinical data, we sought to pinpoint crucial prognostic markers and develop a robust predictive model. The methodology comprises the integration of gene expression data from multiple sources, the performance of survival analyses, and the evaluation of the functional significance of the identified CRG. This approach not only broadens our comprehension of cuproptosis’s role in CC but also offers valuable insights into potential therapeutic targets. The CRG prognostic signature, particularly the role of KIF7, holds significant promise for refining patient risk stratification and guiding personalized treatment strategies. Ultimately, this research could contribute to more effective management of CC and improved patient outcomes.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition

RNA-seq transcriptome data and relevant clinical records of individuals with CC were procured from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) databases (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo). We analyzed these data using R (version 4.3.0). Before conducting a comprehensive analysis, we standardized the expression matrix and eliminated duplicates and entries lacking clinical details. A total of 430 COAD specimens with gene expression profiles and clinical data were procured from the TCGA database, and 562 samples from the GSE39582 and 177 samples from GSE17536 (Supplement Table S1). GSE78220 and GSE39688 were used as independent immunotherapy cohorts. A sum of 1495 CRG were retrieved from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/) by filtering with a relevance score beyond seven using “copper induces cell death” as the search term (Supplement Table S2).

Construction and validation of the prognostic CRG signature in CC

We performed univariate Cox regression and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) analyses to select optimum prognostic genes with P < 0.01 using “glmnet” and “survival” packages. The “ggforest” package was applied to visualize the results of candidate prognostic genes. Thus, CC patients in each dataset were classified into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. To evaluate the predictive accuracy of the CRG signature, we performed survival analysis comparing high- and low-risk groups, utilizing the “survival” and “survminer” R packages. Furthermore, a time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was plotted via the “survivalROC” package.

Development and validation of the nomogram model

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify whether risk scores and relevant clinical features could be predictors of overall survival (OS) in patients with CC. GSE17536 was not included in the validation process for our nomogram model due to the absence of essential clinical pathological features, such as TNM stage, which are vital for the construction and validation of the nomogram. Subsequently, we constructed a nomogram using the “rms” package, incorporating all independent prognostic factors with P < 0.05. The nomogram model was estimated with a calibration plot. We calculated the total points of each CC patient according to the nomogram and categorized them into three groups: low-, intermediate-, and high-point groups. The predictive ability of the nomogram model for CC patients was assessed using Kaplan–Meier, ROC, and Decision curve analysis (DCA).

Differential expression analysis and functional enrichment analysis

The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between high- and low-risk groups were filtered by “limma” package in TCGA cohort. DEGs meeting |log2FC| > 0.5 and FDR < 0.05 were considered significantly differentially expressed. Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) [17] were used to identify the potential functions of DEGs. The enriched GO terms primarily fall into molecular function (MF) ontologies, cellular components (CC), and biological processes (BP). The “clusterProfiler” [18] package was applied to perform function analyses and visualize the results.

Risk score and its implications for immunity, drug sensitivity, and immunotherapy

We examined the immune checkpoint expression in patients in two groups. The sensitivity of different anticancer drugs was forecast utilizing the “pRRophetic” package. The “ssGSEA” package was utilized to compute Immune.Score, ESTIMATE.Score, stromal.Score, and EMT.Score. We also analyzed the tumor immune dysfunction and exclusion (TIDE) score, microsatellite instability (MSI), T cell exclusion and dysfunction scores in high- and low-risk groups via the TIDE database (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/). Participants in immunotherapy cohorts were classified into responder and non-responder groups. Based on the median risk score, these individuals were categorized into high- and low-risk subsets.

Integrated analysis of KIF7 based on TCGA dataset

In pan-cancer analysis, the hazard ratios of KIF7 in various cancers were extracted from TISCH2 database (http://tisch.comp-genomics.org/home/). The “TCGAplot” package was employed to assess the link between KIF7 and the tumor mutation burden (TMB) and MSI. The “ssGSEA” algorithm was applied to examine the link between KIF7 and multiple immune cell infiltrations, and the outcomes were presented with the “ggplot2” package. The link between risk score and clinicopathological parameters like tumor and pathologic TNM stage was also investigated.

Acquisition of clinical samples

Five tumor samples and their adjacent normal tissues used for immunofluorescence staining were obtained from patients diagnosed with CC who underwent surgical intervention at the Affiliated Hospital of Youjiang Medical University for Nationalities. All subjects provided written informed consent, and the study received ethical clearance from the hospital’s Ethics Committee.

Immunofluorescence staining

Clinical specimens were preserved in 4% paraformaldehyde and sectioned to 6 μm thickness. Sections were incubated with 5% BSA for 1 h to prevent non-specific binding. Afterward, sections were kept overnight at 4℃ with diluted KIF7 primary antibody. Following three washes with PBS, sections were treated with an appropriate fluorescently labeled secondary antibody for 1 h at ambient temperature. Nuclei were stained with DAPI; sections were affixed utilizing a mounting medium. Fluorescent signals were observed using fluorescence microscopy.

Cell culture and transfection

The human CRC cell line (LOVO) was purchased from IMMOCELL (Xiamen, China). LOVO were subsequently cultivated in a humid environment at 37 °C and 5% CO2 concentration in Dulbecco’s modified essential medium (DMEM) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Excell, Shanghai, China). KIF7 small interfering RNA (siRNA) were transfected into cells using Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The sequences for si-KIF7: sense GCUAUCAACAUCCGCAUGATT, antisense UCAUGCGGAUGUUGAUAGCTT. The sequences for negative control: sense UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT, antisense ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAATT.

RNA isolation and RT-qPCR

The total RNA was extracted employing TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) and converted to cDNA utilizing a HiScript® II Q RT SuperMix Kit (Nanjing, China). RT-qPCR was executed utilizing a SYBR Green Master Mix (Vazyme, China). Quantification of gene expression was performed utilizing the 2−ΔΔCt technique, and the findings were then standardized to GAPDH. Primers designed for genes in our study are as follows: KIF7 forward: GGATGGAAACCTGACTGCCT and reverse: AGGGGGACTGCTCTGCG; GAPDH forward: ATGGGTGTGAACCACGAGA and reverse: CAGGGATGATGTTCTGGGCA.

Wound healing assessment

To assess cell migration ability, a linear wound was induced using a 200 µl pipette tip. Following the creation of the wound, the cells underwent three rinses with PBS to eliminate debris. Subsequently, the cells were kept in DMEM medium (Procell, Wuhan, China) at 37 °C for a period of 12 h. Images of the wound area were captured at 0 h, 24 h, and 48 h using an optical microscope. The wound closure rate was then calculated based on these measurements.

Cell counting kit (CCK)-8 assays

CCK-8 assay (Dojindo, Japan) was performed to measure the cellular viability. In brief, after LOVO cells had been incubated for 24, 48, and 72 h in 96-well plates, CCK-8 reagent (10 µl) was introduced for 2 h. Thereafter, a microplate reader was used to determine each well’s absorbance at 450 nm (BioTek, VT, USA).

Transwell evaluation

The abilities of cell migration and invasion were assessed using 24-well transwell inserts and Matrigel. For invasion assessment, the upper compartments were layered with Matrigel, whereas for locomotion, they remained uncoated. Cells were suspended in serum-deprived medium and introduced to the upper compartments (200 µl), while the lower compartments contained DMEM medium supplemented with 10% FBS. Following a 24-hour incubation at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 environment, non-invading or migrating cells on the upper membrane surface were eliminated, and cells on the lower surface were fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde and colored with 0.5% crystal violet. The quantity of stained cells was quantified by capturing images and counting in five random fields of view using ImageJ software.

Statistical analysis

All data were statistically analyzed using the R software (version 4.3.0). The “survivalROC” package facilitated the generation of time-dependent ROC curves and the computation of the area under the curve (AUC). Correlation analyses were executed employing Pearson’s coefficient. The above experimental assays were executed in triplicate, and the outcomes were denoted as mean ± standard deviations (SD). Wilcoxon-ranked sum test was or Student’s t test employed to investigate the differences between two groups. Statistical significance was denoted by * P < 0.05, ** P < 0.01, *** P < 0.001, **** P < 0.0001.

Results

Construction and validation of the prognostic CRG signature in CC

In order to develop a signature that could be applicable to all cases, we initially identified 1,495 CRG in 430 samples within the TCGA-COAD cohort. Univariate Cox regression analysis demonstrated that 43 CRG were associated with overall survival in patients with CC. Subsequently, we conducted LASSO logistic regression to screen the 43 genes and reduce the number of candidate genes, leaving 29 genes (Fig. 1A-B). Subsequently, the 29 genes were subjected to multivariate Cox regression analysis. Subsequently, 16 CRG were identified and presented in a forest plot (Fig. 1C). The prognostic significance of these 16 CRG was then evaluated using the xiantao platform (https://www.xiantaozi.com/). Twelve genes demonstrated significant correlations with overall survival and were selected to construct the most optimal prognosis model, comprising GSR, NGF, ULK1, SERPINA1, GSTM1, CTSD, KIF7, MAN1B1, PTPN6, NOG, CYP19A1, and LRP2 (Fig. 2). Among these 12 genes, CC patients with high expression of GSR and SERPINA1 have a longer survival time. Based on the coefficients derived from the multivariate Cox regression analysis, a CRG signature was devised to predict the prognosis of CC patients.

Survival analysis of twelve genes with significantly prognostic significance in the TCGA-COAD dataset

To verify the accuracy of the CRG signature, all patients in TCGA-COAD, GSE39582 and GSE17536, were respectively divided into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. The Kaplan–Meier survival curves showed that the high-risk groups were less likely to survive than the low-risk group in TCGA cohort, GSE39582, and GSE17536 (Fig. 3A-C). ROC curves at 1, 3, and 5 years showed the average AUC scores in these cohorts were all greater than 0.6 (Fig. 3D-F). AUC scores in TCGA cohort and GSE17536 exceeded 0.75, indicating a high degree of predictive accuracy. For further verification, risk score curves were plotted. The risk score scatter plot revealed that patients in the low-risk groups were more likely to survive longer and have lower mortality rates than those in the high-risk groups (Fig. 3G-I).

Development and validation of the nomogram model

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were applied to test whether risk core and other clinical parameters were independent prognostic factors for OS. Our findings indicated that risk score, age, and pathologic.M were notably linked to the prognosis, and they served as independent prognostic factors in the TCGA cohort (Fig. 4A-B). A nomogram model included three indices: risk score, age, and pathologic.M. Subsequently, the aggregate of these three points was utilized to forecast the survival probabilities (Fig. 4C). The calibration curve of the nomogram suggested strong coherence between the prediction and actual observations (Fig. 4D).

(C) Establishing a signature-based prognostic nomogram predicting OS in CC patients. (D) Calibration curve for the possibility of 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival in a nomogram

To validate the nomogram model, we computed the total points for each individual with CC. All patients in the TCGA cohort and GSE39582 were categorized into three subcohorts: low-, intermediate-, and high-point subcohorts. Kaplan–Meier curves indicated that the low-point subcohort exhibited a more favorable prognosis compared to the other two subcohorts (Fig. 5A and D). ROC curves at 1, 3, and 5 years showed that the average AUC scores were greater than 0.7 (Fig. 5B and E). DCA cures demonstrated that the net benefit of the nomogram model was advantageous, especially in 5 years (Fig. 5C and F).

Differential expression analysis and functional enrichment analysis

Differential expression analysis between high-risk group and low-risk group in TCGA-COAD was performed via the “limma” package. A sum of 232 DEGs was ascertained, encompassing 145 upregulated and 87 downregulated genes. The volcano plot and heatmap plot are shown in Fig. 6A-B. To explore the biological roles and pathways of the 232 DEGs, GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were conducted using the “clusterProfiler” package. These DEGs were predominantly enriched in extracellular structure organization and extracellular matrix organization (Fig. 6C), extracellular matrix structural constituents and glycosaminoglycan binding (Fig. 6D), endoplasmic reticulum lumen, and collagen-containing extracellular matrix (Fig. 6E). In the KEGG enrichment analysis, DEGs were mainly involved in protein digestion, absorption, and focal adhesion (Fig. 6F).

Risk score and its implications for immunity, drug sensitivity, and immunotherapy

We conducted a comprehensive examination to explore disparities in tumor biology between high- and low-risk groups. Notably, we identified differential expression of 20 immune checkpoint genes (Fig. 7A). Most of these genes, including CTLA4, CD70, CD276, and CD27, exhibited elevated expression levels in the low-risk group. In contrast, HHLA2 and CD44 were more highly expressed in the high-risk group. These observations suggest that the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) may vary significantly between risk groups, indicating a potential need for tailored immunotherapeutic strategies based on risk stratification. A lower IC50 value signifies higher drug potency. Figure 7B illustrates that individuals in the high-risk group may be more sensitive to gefitinib and dasatinib, while those in the low-risk group appear to respond better to gemcitabine and metformin. More drugs with differences in drug sensitivity between high- and low-risk groups are shown in Supplementary Table S4. Pearson correlation analysis revealed a positive correlation between the risk score and immune score, ESTIMATE score, stromal score, and EMT score (Fig. 7C). We utilized the TIDE website to assess the potential clinical effectiveness of ICIs. Results indicated that the TIDE score, along with T cell exclusion and dysfunction scores, was lower in the low-risk subgroup compared to the high-risk subgroup. Conversely, the MSI score was higher in the low-risk group, suggesting that individuals in the low-risk group may derive greater benefit from ICI treatment relative to those in the high-risk group (Fig. 7D). To evaluate the predictive capability of risk scores for patient responses to immunotherapy, we examined the distribution of treatment responders and non-responders in two datasets, GSE78220 and GSE39688. Results demonstrated a higher proportion of responders in the low-risk group compared to the high-risk group (Fig. 7E).

Verifying the expression of KIF7 via online database and clinical samples

We validated the expression of KIF7 in CC using the UALCAN database (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/index.html), confirming that KIF7 is highly expressed in tumor tissues (Fig. 9A). Additionally, we downloaded immunohistochemical images of KIF7 from the HPA database (https://www.proteinatlas.org/, Fig. 9B). Finally, we downloaded the expression profile of KIF7 across various colorectal cancer cell lines from the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) database (https://sites.broadinstitute.org/ccle/). KIF7 exhibited the highest expression in the LOVO cell line; therefore, we selected the LOVO cell line for subsequent loss-of-function experiments (Fig. 9C). Clinical samples were collected for immunofluorescence staining to detect KIF7 expression. The results demonstrated that KIF7 is markedly overexpressed in tumor tissues (Fig. 9D).

Investigating the biological function of KIF7 through cellular experiments

The role of KIF7 in CC cells remains unclear. We aimed to elucidate its function by knocking down KIF7 in LOVO cells. The result of RT-qPCR confirmed the successful downregulation of KIF7 in LOVO cells (Fig. 9E). The results of the CCK-8 assay indicated that increased levels of KIF7 inhibited cell proliferation (Fig. 9F). In the wound healing assay, a reduction in KIF7 expression led to a decreased wound closure rate compared to si-NC group at both 24 and 48 h (Fig. 9G). Additionally, transwell experiments demonstrated that the migration and invasion capabilities were markedly inhibited following KIF7 silencing (Fig. 9H).

Discussion

Despite recent advancements in surgical techniques and chemotherapeutic agents, the prognosis for individuals with late-stage and disseminated CC remains unsatisfactory [19, 20]. Thus, novel and reliable biomarkers for individualized prognosis of CC urgently need to be identified. The associations between copper and malignancies have been observed for over a hundred years. Elucidating our present comprehension of the link between copper and cancer presents challenges. The prognostic landscape of CC is constantly shifting as researchers uncover new biological indicators and potential treatment objectives. Our study introduces a CRG signature that holds significant promise in risk stratification and prognosis prediction for CC patients.

Cellular death induced by copper is orchestrated through an age-old mechanism involving the lipoylation of proteins [11]. Several recent investigations have illuminated copper’s roles as a non-structural intracellular messenger in signaling, directly linking it to pathway components that regulate cell division and lipid metabolism [21–23]. Elevated copper levels have been observed in various malignancies compared to healthy tissues [24]. On the other hand, copper is beneficial for tumors. Increased copper concentrations have been observed in tumors or serum from animal models and individuals with numerous cancer types, encompassing breast, lung, intestine, gall bladder and prostate cancers [4]. The accumulation of copper has been linked to enhanced proliferation, growth, angiogenesis, and metastasis [25]. Cuproptosis represents a distinct mode of cellular demise that is dependent on copper, setting it apart from traditional apoptosis. Cuproptosis serves a crucial function in tumor biology by influencing cancer cell survival and proliferation through copper-dependent mechanisms [26, 27]. Clearly, copper dyshomeostasis and cuproptosis are significant factors in cancer progression. However, the precise role of the CRG signature in patients with CC remains unclear and requires further investigation.

In our study, we have developed a novel gene signature related to copper-induced cell death to forecast the outcome of individuals with CC. The prognostic indicator comprises 12 genes: GSR, NGF, ULK1, SERPINA1, GSTM1, CTSD, KIF7, MAN1B1, PTPN6, NOG, CYP19A1, and LRP2. Based on the risk score, the patients were categorized into high- and low-risk groups. The 12-CRG signature serves as a dependable prognostic evaluation instrument as it prevents the exclusion of prognostic data and eliminates redundant prognostic information. In this investigation, the Kaplan–Meier plots revealed a statistically notable survival likelihood between the elevated and reduced risk groups in both TCGA and GEO cohorts, initially confirming the signature’s efficacy in predicting CC risk. Moreover, the ROC curve substantiated the precision and reliability of this signature for patient prognosis. The AUC values at 1, 3, and 5 years were 0.814, 0.811, and 0.808, respectively, in the TCGA cohort, further demonstrating the signature’s accuracy. Risk score emerged as an autonomous prognostic factor for CC. Our findings indicate that the CRG signature is markedly linked to OS in individuals with CC, underscoring its potential as a predictive instrument for clinical outcomes. Recent studies have highlighted the significance of cuproptosis-related genes in the prognostic prediction of colon cancer. Dongming Li et al. developed a cuproptosis-associated lncRNA signature based on TCGA data, with AUC values of 0.707, 0.742, and 0.744 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year predictions, respectively. However, no experiments were conducted to validate the differences in expression and biological functions of lncRNAs [28]. Similarly, Yong Su et al. developed a gene signature with AUC values of 0.737, 0.646, and 0.633 over the same time frame, yet the experimental validation was absent [29]. Guangzhan Liang et al. developed a prognostic model comprising nine cuproptosis-associated lncRNAs, which demonstrated robust predictive capacity and was partially validated using cell lines and tissues [30]. Our study is noteworthy for its use of multiple datasets for validation and its experimental confirmation of the role of key genes in colon cancer cells. This approach increases the reliability of the findings and their potential clinical applicability.

Furthermore, after constructing the CRG signature, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of gene expression profiles and clinical data from the TCGA and GEO databases. The findings indicated that the risk score procured from the CRG signature was markedly associated with immune checkpoints, chemotherapy sensitivity, and the tumor microenvironment (TME), offering novel perspectives on the role of copper-induced cell death in CC. The TME is a critical determinant of cancer progression and response to therapy [31, 32]. Our analysis showed that the risk score was markedly linked to immune cell infiltration, indicating that CRGs may influence the immunological landscape within tumors. This aligns with the notion that the TME serves a multifaceted function in tumor proliferation, immune system suppression, and clinical treatment efficacy [33]. The risk score was positively linked to immune checkpoint genes, implying that patients with higher risk scores may respond differently to ICIs. Current treatments for CC include surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy; however, chemotherapy remains a primary option in the clinic, particularly for inoperable stages and metastasis [34]. Drug resistance markedly impairs the clinical effectiveness of therapeutic agents in individuals with CC [35]. In the present study, we investigated the chemotherapeutic sensitivity of high-risk and low-risk groups and identified four drugs (metformin, gefitinib, gemcitabine, and dasatinib) showing different drug sensitivities. Non-tumor drugs familiar to clinicians may also possess active antitumor properties; for instance, metformin, used for diabetes, displays efficacy in inhibiting pancreatic cancer, lung cancer, and ovarian cancer [9]. Future goals of new therapeutics should aim to increase selectivity, reduce side effects, and overcome cell resistance [24]. The risk score was linked to the sensitivity to certain chemotherapeutic drugs and the response to immunotherapy, suggesting that CRGs could potentially serve as a guide for personalized chemotherapy regime [36]. This aligns with the growing appreciation of the function of TME in drug resistance and the development of targeted therapies [37]. The ability to predict which patients are more likely to respond to immunotherapy could greatly enhance treatment outcomes and reduce unnecessary side effects for non-responders.

To elucidate our model’s role, we conducted GO and KEGG enrichment analyses. Interestingly, DEGs between high- and low-risk groups primarily pertained to extracellular matrix (ECM). ECM modifications are regarded as major external factors driving tumor advancement, with ECM rigidity promoting cancer cell multiplication and movement [38]. Copper is required for ECM stabilization via lysyl oxidase (copper-dependent monoamine oxidase) [39]. Furthermore, 232 DEGs were mainly associated with abdominal aortic aneurysm and vascular diseases. Cu Furthermore, 232 DEGs were mainly associated with abdominal aortic aneurysm and vascular diseases. Cu has been identified as a constraining element for various facets of cancer advancement, encompassing growth, angiogenesis, and metastasis [25]. Cu serves a crucial function in metastasis by modulating angiogenesis, which constitutes an essential mechanism necessary for metastatic capability [6].

Notably, our study also focused on the role of KIF7, a kinesin family protein linked to Hedgehog signaling and cancer development [40]. KIF7 is a molecular motor protein belonging to the Kinesin family, which is mainly responsible for intracellular substance transport and signaling, and plays an important role in cell polarity formation and tissue pattern formation [41]. Recent study has demonstrated that KIF7 not only plays a role in the activation of the Hedgehog signaling pathway but also exerts influence over a range of biological processes, including cell growth, migration, and differentiation, through interactions with other signaling molecules [42]. KIF7’s role in regulating ciliary microtubules and affecting Hedgehog signaling suggests its influence on tumorigenesis [43, 44]. The abnormal expression of KIF7 is closely related to the occurrence and development of various diseases. For example, in epithelial ovarian cancer, the low expression of KIF7 is associated with poor prognosis in patients, suggesting that KIF7 may serve as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target [45]. The available evidence suggests that the expression of KIF7 is closely associated with the prognosis of breast cancer. In particular, its overexpression has been linked to a reduction in survival rates among patients. Consequently, the development of targeted drugs or therapeutic strategies against KIF7 may offer novel avenues for the treatment of breast cancer and other tumors [46]. In our study, KIF7 was found to be associated with hazard ratio, TMB, microsatellite instability, immune cell infiltration and tumor stage. Figure 9 shows our findings that KIF7 is overexpressed in tumor tissues, which is consistent with previous studies [47, 48]. Knockdown of KIF7 in colon cancer cells significantly reduced cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, as assessed by CCK-8, wound healing, and Transwell assays. In a word, our study provides fresh insights into KIF7’s multifaceted role in colon cancer, emphasizing the need for further research into its therapeutic potential.

Nevertheless, this investigation faced certain constraints. Firstly, to determine the prognostic accuracy of CRG-related signatures associated with CC, we used the TCGA-COAD cohort and two other GEO datasets. It will be helpful to increase the sample size for further external validation; thus, it is necessary to improve the detection power by integrating multiple individual datasets in a future study. Secondly, further experimental evidence is required to fully elucidate the role of CRG-related signatures and the underlying mechanisms of CC.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study presents strong support for the prognostic significance of the CRG signature in CC and underscores the potential role of KIF7 as a key factor in the disease. Furthermore, we offer new insights into the link between the CRGs signature, the immune microenvironment, and chemotherapy response in CC. These findings contribute to the expanding body of research aimed at enhancing the predictive accuracy of clinical outcomes for CC patients and suggest novel avenues for therapeutic intervention.

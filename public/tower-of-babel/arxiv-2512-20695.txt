On the decay of a light spinless particle into two photons Preprint: TTP25-057, P3H-25-118
Ferruccio Feruglio, Gabriele Levati, Robert Ziegler

Abstract
We analyze the effective couplings of a light, spinless, gauge-singlet particle ϕ \phi to on-shell photons. Starting from the most general theory at the electroweak scale, which allows for CP-violating interactions suppressed by inverse powers of an ultraviolet scale Λ \Lambda , we derive the corresponding low-energy effective theory valid below the GeV scale. Within this framework, we systematically expand the effective couplings of ϕ \phi to on-shell photons in powers of small parameters. Working at the one-loop level, we retain terms at first order in 1 / Λ 1/\Lambda . We incorporate both isospin-breaking effects and η \eta – η ′ \eta^{\prime} mixing, and provide explicit expressions for the couplings up to first order in m ϕ 2 / m η 2 m_{\phi}^{2}/m_{\eta}^{2} and m π 2 / m η 2 m_{\pi}^{2}/m_{\eta}^{2} . As applications, we compute the decay rate of ϕ \phi into two photons and illustrate our results in several physically motivated scenarios.

Introduction

The absence of any unambiguous evidence for heavy new physics (NP) at the LHC has motivated growing interest – both theoretical and experimental – towards alternative scenarios featuring new light particles with masses much lighter than a proton. As the low-energy remnants of a possibly rich dynamics above the electroweak scale, light NP candidates would offer the unique opportunity to probe new interactions beyond the SM ones.
A prominent example in this direction is given by light spinless particles, whose mass can be naturally protected against quantum corrections if they are pseudo Nambu-Goldstone bosons (pNGBs) of a spontaneously broken global symmetry. In this case, the mass of such particles can be naturally lighter than the scale at which the corresponding symmetry is broken.

5
Conclusions

We have evaluated the effective couplings of a light spinless gauge-singlet particle
\phi
to on-shell photons.
We considered the most general case of CP-violating microscopic interactions, allowing for both
scalar and pseudoscalar elementary couplings. By conventionally assigning
\phi
to the CP-even sector,
we have formally distinguished between CP-invariant and CP-violating interactions. We focused on a light, weakly coupled particle with
m_{\phi}\ll{\rm GeV}
, whose interactions with matter,
the Higgs boson, and gauge fields are suppressed by inverse powers of a heavy scale
\Lambda
.

In specific models, such a particle may represent a viable dark matter candidate.
Stability on cosmological scales requires its mass to lie below the threshold for decay into an electron–positron pair, unless the couplings to electrons is extremely suppressed.
Moreover, the decay rate into two photons must be sufficiently small to evade stringent constraints,
such as those arising from stellar cooling and from indirect X-ray and gamma-ray line searches.
In general, a comprehensive assessment of the viable parameter space cannot be achieved without precise knowledge
of the dependence of the relevant physical observables on all particle couplings.
For instance, in specific scenarios, the decay rate into two photons can be entirely determined by the microscopic couplings of the decaying particle to quarks, leptons, and gluons.

Starting from the most general theory that includes a spinless gauge-singlet particle at the electroweak scale,
we derived the corresponding low-energy effective theory below the GeV scale, where light quarks
and gluons are replaced by a set of pseudoscalar mesons.
Using this effective theory, we systematically expanded the effective couplings of
\phi
to on-shell photons
in powers of small parameters. Working at the one-loop level, we retained terms at first order in
1/\Lambda
.
We accounted for both isospin-breaking effects and
\eta
–
\eta^{\prime}
mixing, providing explicit expressions
for the couplings up to first order in
m_{\phi}^{2}/m_{\eta}^{2}
and
m_{\pi}^{2}/m_{\eta}^{2}
.

The coupling of light scalars and pseudoscalars to photons at low energies has been extensively studied in the literature.
Nevertheless, we have extended existing analyses in several important aspects.
The decay of a hypothetical light Higgs particle — with a mass below 1 GeV — into two photons was investigated
in Ref.
79
, where the gluonic contribution was first evaluated using the QCD trace anomaly
and current algebra techniques.
Building on a similar theoretical framework, we have extended the analysis of Ref.
79

to fully general CP-invariant couplings, including the possibility of mixing between
\phi
and the Higgs boson as
a particular case.
Our treatment extends the recent analysis of Ref.
57
— which also focuses on the CP-invariant sector —
by fully incorporating the contribution arising from the
\phi
–gluon interaction.

In the CP-violating sector, we have comprehensively accounted for the effects of light pseudoscalar mesons,
including isospin-breaking effects and
\eta
–
\eta^{\prime}
mixing.
A crucial consistency check of our computation is provided by the independence of the final result under
the most general change of basis — with three active light flavors,
u
,
d
, and
s
— designed to eliminate
the CP-violating
\phi
–gluon interaction.
In light of a discrepancy between our results and the analogous analysis of Ref.
16
,
we explicitly demonstrate this independence using two distinct expansions, which are presented in the Appendix.
Our general results can be specialized to any physically motivated scenario involving a light spinless particle,
and we discuss several well-known examples in the final part of this work, pointing out various discrepancies with the existing literature.

Appendix A
Operators involving
\phi
and the Higgs boson

Here we show that it is possible to choose a field basis where all derivative operators involving
\phi
and the Higgs boson
are absent.
Operators containing derivatives have at least dimension five:

\displaystyle O_{1}=

\displaystyle~\Box\phi\,|H|^{2}\,,

\displaystyle O_{2}=

\displaystyle~\phi\,\Box|H|^{2}

\displaystyle O_{3}=

\displaystyle~\phi H^{\dagger}D^{2}H\,,

\displaystyle O_{4}=

\displaystyle~\phi D^{2}H^{\dagger}\,H

\displaystyle O_{5}=

\displaystyle~\phi DH^{\dagger}DH\,,

\displaystyle O_{6}=

\displaystyle~\partial\phi DH^{\dagger}\,H

\displaystyle O_{7}=

\displaystyle~\partial\phi H^{\dagger}\,DH\,.

(A.1)

Via integration by parts, they are related by the equations

\displaystyle O_{1}=

\displaystyle~O_{2}\,,

\displaystyle O_{2}=

\displaystyle~O_{3}+O_{4}+2O_{5}

\displaystyle-O_{6}=

\displaystyle~O_{3}+O_{5}\,,

\displaystyle-O_{7}=

\displaystyle~O_{4}+O_{5}\,,

that can be used to eliminate the operators
O_{2}
,
O_{5}
,
O_{6}
and
O_{7}
.
The operators
O_{3,4}
can be removed by making use of the equations of motion of the Higgs doublet. In terms of the two combinations
O_{\pm}=O_{3}\pm O_{4}
(
O_{+}
is hermitian, while
O_{-}
is antihermitian) we obtain:

\displaystyle O_{+}=

\displaystyle~2\phi(\lambda v^{2}|H|^{2}-\lambda|H|^{4})-\phi(\bar{q}_{L}Y_{d}Hd_{R}+\bar{l}_{L}Y_{e}He_{R}+\bar{q}_{L}Y_{u}\tilde{H}u_{R}+h.c.)

\displaystyle O_{-}=

\displaystyle~\phi(\bar{q}_{L}Y_{d}Hd_{R}+\bar{l}_{L}Y_{e}He_{R}-\bar{q}_{L}Y_{u}\tilde{H}u_{R}-h.c.)\,.

Our effective Lagrangian already includes the operators (we use
{\cal Y}
to distinguish these couplings from the Yukawas
Y

of the SM):

\displaystyle-\phi(\bar{q}_{L}{\cal Y}_{d}Hd_{R}+\bar{l}_{L}{\cal Y}_{e}He_{R}+\bar{q}_{L}{\cal Y}_{u}\tilde{H}u_{R}+h.c.)\,.

The combination
c_{+}O_{+}+ic_{-}O_{-}
produces a shift of the couplings
{\cal Y}
:

\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{d,e}\to{\cal Y}_{d,e}+(c_{+}-ic_{-})Y_{d,e}\,,~~~~~~~~{\cal Y}_{u}\to{\cal Y}_{u}+(c_{+}+ic_{-})Y_{u}\,.

We conclude that
O_{3,4}
can be eliminated in favour of the nonderivative operators

\phi|H|^{2}\,,~~~\phi|H|^{4}\,.

We are left with a single derivative operator, that can be chosen as
O_{1}
. To show that also
O_{1}
is redundant,
consider the energy density

\displaystyle V=

\displaystyle~V_{H}+V_{\phi}+V_{\phi H}\,.

We first discuss
V_{\phi}
, the limit of
V
when the Higgs doublet is set to zero.
We assume the dependence of
V_{\phi}
on
\phi
is only through the combination

(\phi/\Lambda)
:

\displaystyle V_{\phi}=

\displaystyle~\mu^{2}\Lambda^{2}f\left(\frac{\phi}{\Lambda}\right)=\mu^{2}\Lambda^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}f^{(n)}(0)\frac{\phi^{n}}{\Lambda^{n}}\,.

The function
f(x)
is dimensionless and the overall scale
\mu^{2}\Lambda^{2}

has been choosen to deliver a mass term for
\phi
proportional to
\mu^{2}
, a free parameter. When
\mu^{2}=0
, the
potential is flat, reproducing the case of a Goldstone boson. When
\mu^{2}=\Lambda_{QCD}^{4}/\Lambda^{2}
,
an axion-like mass is reproduced. We have in mind an application where
\mu
is below the electron-positron
energy threshold, so that
\phi
can only decay into two photons or two neutrinos.
Neglecting all the
1/\Lambda
terms, the leading equations of motion of
\phi
read

\displaystyle\Box\phi=~-f^{\prime}(0)\mu^{2}\Lambda-f^{\prime\prime}(0)\mu^{2}\phi+...

Replacing this in
O_{1}
we obtain

\displaystyle O_{1}=-f^{\prime}(0)\mu^{2}\Lambda|H|^{2}-f^{\prime\prime}(0)\mu^{2}\phi|H|^{2}+...

showing that
O_{1}
can be absorbed by redefining
V_{H}+V_{\phi H}
. We write

\displaystyle V_{H}=

\displaystyle~-\lambda v^{2}|H|^{2}+\lambda|H|^{4}

\displaystyle V_{\phi H}=

\displaystyle~\beta_{1}\frac{\phi}{\Lambda}v^{2}|H|^{2}+\beta_{2}\frac{\phi}{\Lambda}|H|^{4}+{\cal O}(\phi^{2}/\Lambda^{2})\,,

understanding that
the parameters
v^{2}
and
\beta_{1}
include the effect of
O_{1}
, respectively. Here again
we have assumed that the interaction of
\phi
with the SM fields occurs via the combination

(\phi/\Lambda)^{k}
and we have kept only the first order
k=1
. In
V_{H}+V_{\phi H}
we retained
operators up to dimension 5.
It is not restrictive to choose a field basis where
\langle\phi\rangle=0
(otherwise we conveniently shift
\phi
).
This imposes a relation on the minimum conditions. Setting
|H|=\rho/\sqrt{2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V}{\partial\rho}=

\displaystyle~-\lambda v^{2}\rho+\lambda\rho^{3}+\beta_{1}\frac{\phi}{\Lambda}v^{2}\rho+\beta_{2}\frac{\phi}{\Lambda}\rho^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\lambda\rho(v^{2}-\rho^{2})=0

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi}=

\displaystyle~\mu^{2}\Lambda\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}f^{(n+1)}(0)\frac{\phi^{n}}{\Lambda^{n}}+\beta_{1}\frac{1}{2\Lambda}v^{2}\rho^{2}+\beta_{2}\frac{1}{4\Lambda}\rho^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~f^{\prime}(0)\mu^{2}\Lambda+\beta_{1}\frac{1}{2\Lambda}v^{2}\rho^{2}+\beta_{2}\frac{1}{4\Lambda}\rho^{4}=0\,.

These are solved by

\displaystyle\langle H\rangle=

\displaystyle~\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(0)\mu^{2}=

\displaystyle~-(2\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})\frac{v^{4}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\,.

Evaluating the second derivatives at the minimum we find
the mass matrix

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2\lambda v^{2}&(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})\frac{v^{3}}{\Lambda}\\
(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})\frac{v^{3}}{\Lambda}&f^{\prime\prime}(0)\mu^{2}\end{array}\right)\,.

To first order in
v/\Lambda
and in the limit
f^{\prime\prime}(0)\mu^{2}\ll\lambda v^{2}
, the mass eigenstates are:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{H}\\
\hat{\phi}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\xi\displaystyle\frac{v}{\Lambda}\\
-\xi\displaystyle\frac{v}{\Lambda}&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}H\\
\phi\end{array}\right)\,,

where
\xi=\frac{(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})}{2\lambda}
is the mixing parameter used in the text.

In summary, all derivative
\phi-H
interactions can be eliminated in favour of non-derivatives ones. At order
1/\Lambda
,
these are
\phi|H|^{2}
and
\phi|H|^{4}
. Expanding the energy density around the minimum, the main effect
is represented by a mixing between
\phi
and
H
, of order
v/\Lambda
, contributing to the

\phi\to\gamma\gamma
amplitude.

Appendix B
\phi
-meson mixing

In the basis
P=(\phi,\pi^{0},\eta_{8},\eta_{0})
, the quadratic part of the
\phi
-meson Lagrangian reads

L_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}P)^{T}K_{P}(\partial^{\mu}P)-\frac{1}{2}P^{T}M_{P}^{2}P\,,

where, setting
\epsilon=f_{\pi}/\Lambda
, the matrices
K_{P}
and
M_{P}^{2}
are given by

\displaystyle K_{P}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&c_{1}\epsilon&c_{2}\epsilon&c_{3}\epsilon\\
c_{1}\epsilon&1&0&0\\
c_{2}\epsilon&0&1&0\\
c_{3}\epsilon&0&0&1\end{array}\right)\,,

\displaystyle M_{P}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}m_{\phi}^{2}&-m_{12}^{2}\epsilon&-m_{13}^{2}\epsilon&-m_{14}^{2}\epsilon\\
-m_{12}^{2}\epsilon&B_{0}(m_{u}+m_{d})&\frac{\displaystyle B_{0}}{\sqrt{3}}(m_{u}-m_{d})&B_{0}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}(m_{u}-m_{d})\\
-m_{13}^{2}\epsilon&\frac{\displaystyle B_{0}}{\sqrt{3}}(m_{u}-m_{d})&m_{\eta_{8}}^{2}&\delta m^{2}\\
-m_{14}^{2}\epsilon&B_{0}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}(m_{u}-m_{d})&\delta m^{2}&m_{\eta_{0}}^{2}\\
\end{array}\right)\,,

with

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{y}_{u}-\hat{y}_{d}\right)-2\tilde{C}_{G}(Q_{u}-Q_{d})\,,

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{y}_{u}+\hat{y}_{d}-2\hat{y}_{s}\right)-2\tilde{C}_{G}(Q_{u}+Q_{d}-2Q_{s})\right]\,,

\displaystyle c_{3}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{y}_{u}+\hat{y}_{d}+\hat{y}_{s}\right)-2\tilde{C}_{G}(Q_{u}+Q_{d}+Q_{s})\right]\,,

\displaystyle m_{12}^{2}

\displaystyle=4\tilde{C}_{G}B_{0}(m_{u}Q_{u}-m_{d}Q_{d})\,,

\displaystyle m_{\eta_{8}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{0}}{3}(m_{u}+m_{d}+4m_{s})\,,

\displaystyle m_{13}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}\tilde{C}_{G}B_{0}(m_{u}Q_{u}+m_{d}Q_{d}-2m_{s}Q_{s})\,,

\displaystyle m_{\eta_{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2B_{0}}{3}(m_{u}+m_{d}+m_{s})+M_{0}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle m_{14}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\tilde{C}_{G}B_{0}(m_{u}Q_{u}+m_{d}Q_{d}+m_{s}Q_{s})\,,

\displaystyle\delta m^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}B_{0}(m_{u}+m_{d}-2m_{s})\,,

(B.9)

where we recall that in this normalization
Q_{u}+Q_{d}+Q_{s}={\rm tr}\,Q_{A}=1/2
.

One could in principle perform an exact diagonalization of these matrices to identify the canonically normalized mass eigenstates
(\phi_{\rm phys},\pi^{0}_{\rm phys},\eta_{\rm phys},\eta_{\rm phys}^{\prime})
. However, it is well known that
\chi PT
at leading order is not adequate to describe
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mixing
62
;
63
, and
corrections to mixing angles
and masses arise at NLO
81
;
22
;
10
. Here we ignore these corrections, which justifies to perform a diagonalization only at leading order in the small parameters
w\equiv m_{u}/m_{s}\sim 0.02
and
\delta\equiv(m_{d}-m_{u})/(m_{d}+m_{u})\sim 0.3
(and
\epsilon=f_{\pi}/\Lambda
).

We begin by moving from the
(\eta_{8},\eta_{0})
basis to the
(\eta,\eta^{\prime})
basis, through the rotation

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\eta_{8}\\
\eta_{0}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\alpha&\sin\alpha\\
-\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\eta\\
\eta^{\prime}\end{array}\right)\,.

This diagonalizes the 3-4 block of the mass matrix, with diagonal entries that to the order we are interested in (up to multiplicative corrections of order
{\cal O}(\delta^{2}w^{2})
) can be identified with the physical masses
m_{\eta}^{2}
and
m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2}
. These masses and the mixing angle
\alpha
are related to the entries in the 3-4 block (
\delta m^{2},m_{\eta_{8}},m_{\eta_{0}}
) by the three equations

\displaystyle\tan 2\alpha

\displaystyle=\frac{2\delta m^{2}}{m_{\eta_{0}}^{2}-m_{\eta_{8}}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\cos 2\alpha

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\eta_{8}}^{2}-m_{\eta_{0}}^{2}}{m_{\eta}^{2}-m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle m_{\eta}^{2}+m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{\eta_{8}}^{2}+m_{\eta_{0}}^{2}\,.

(B.16)

In turn these relations allow us to express
M_{0}^{2}
and
m_{s}
in terms of the mixing angle
\alpha
and
m_{\eta}^{2}
, giving (we introduce the shorthand
c\equiv\cos\alpha,s\equiv\sin\alpha
)

\displaystyle M_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=2B_{0}\left(\frac{m_{u}}{1-\delta}-m_{s}\right)\frac{\sqrt{2}(c^{2}-s^{2})-cs}{3cs}\,,

(B.17)

\displaystyle B_{0}m_{s}

\displaystyle=m_{\eta}^{2}\frac{3c}{2(2c+\sqrt{2}s)}+B_{0}m_{u}\frac{c-\sqrt{2}s}{(-1+\delta)(2c+\sqrt{2}s)}\,,

(B.18)

along with the relation

\displaystyle m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{\eta}^{2}\frac{2cs-\sqrt{2}c^{2}}{s(2c+\sqrt{2}s)}-B_{0}m_{u}\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{(-1+\delta)s(2c+\sqrt{2}s)}\,.

(B.19)

Finally we can express
B_{0}m_{u}
through the mass of the neutral pion
m_{\pi}^{2}
. The leading order relation is given by

\displaystyle B_{0}m_{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\delta}{2}m_{\pi}^{2}\,,

(B.20)

and receives multiplicative corrections of order
\delta^{2}w
. Therefore the LO expressions is sufficient to express
all heavy scales through physical masses as (isospin breaking effects only enter at higher order)

\displaystyle M_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(m_{\eta}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}\right)\frac{\sqrt{2}(s^{2}-c^{2})+cs}{s(2c+\sqrt{2}s)}\,,

(B.21)

\displaystyle B_{0}m_{s}

\displaystyle=m_{\eta}^{2}\frac{3c}{4c+2\sqrt{2}s}+m_{\pi}^{2}\frac{\sqrt{2}s-c}{4c+2\sqrt{2}s}\,,

(B.22)

\displaystyle m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{\eta}^{2}\frac{c(2s-\sqrt{2}c)}{s(2c+\sqrt{2}s)}+m_{\pi}^{2}\frac{1}{s(\sqrt{2}c+s)}\,.

(B.23)

As anticipated, the last relation does not allow for realistic
\eta
- and
\eta^{\prime}
masses, which require NLO
\chi
PT. Here we follow Refs.
8
;
36
and make the choice
s=-1/3,c=2\sqrt{2}/3
, which gives the simple expressions

\displaystyle M_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=3\left(m_{\eta}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle B_{0}m_{s}

\displaystyle=m_{\eta}^{2}-\frac{m_{\pi}^{2}}{2}\,,

\displaystyle m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle=4m_{\eta}^{2}-3m_{\pi}^{2}\,.

(B.24)

Taking the expressions in Eqs. (
B.24
,
B.20
) along with
s=-1/3,c=2\sqrt{2}/3
, one can calculate the mixing angles
\theta_{P\phi}
of
\phi
with the meson mass eigenstate
P
, which is the first column of the orthogonal matrix
O
that diagonalizes the mass matrix in the basis where kinetic terms are diagonal, i.e.

\displaystyle\theta_{P\phi}

\displaystyle=O_{P1}\,,

\displaystyle O^{T}R^{T}M^{2}_{P}RO

\displaystyle=M^{2}_{\rm diag}\,,

\displaystyle R^{T}K_{P}R

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{4\times 4}\,.

(B.25)

Plugging these mixing angles into the
Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) term
104
;
107
:

{\cal L}_{WZW}\supset-\frac{\alpha}{4\pi f_{\pi}}\left(\pi^{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\eta_{8}+2\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\eta_{0}\right)F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\,,

(B.26)

or equivalently in the
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
basis for the chosen value of
\alpha

one obtains a contribution to the effective
\phi

given by

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{C}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_{P=\pi^{0},\eta,\eta^{\prime}}C_{P}\theta_{P\phi}\,,

(B.28)

with

. This adds to the one controlled by

\tilde{C}_{\gamma}=\tilde{c}_{\gamma}+\sum_{i=\mathrm{all}}N_{C}^{i}Q_{i}^{2}\,\hat{y}_{i}-4\tilde{C}_{G}\sum_{i=u,d,s}N_{C}^{i}Q_{i}^{2}Q_{Aii}\,,

Abstract
. This paper proposes a new hybrid high-order discretization for the biharmonic problem and the corresponding eigenvalue problem. The discrete ansatz space includes degrees of freedom in n − 2 n-2 dimensional submanifolds (e.g., nodal values in 2D and edge values in 3D), in addition to the typical degrees of freedom in the mesh and on the hyperfaces in the HHO literature. This approach enables the characteristic commuting property of the hybrid high-order methodology in any space dimension. The main results are guaranteed lower eigenvalue bounds of higher order. Furthermore, we derive quasi-best approximation estimates as well as reliable and efficient a posteriori error estimators under minimal regularity assumptions on the exact solution. The latter motivates an adaptive mesh-refining algorithm that empirically recovers optimal convergence rates for singular solutions.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\in\{2,3\}
, be a polyhedral Lipschitz domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
and unit outer normal vector
\nu_{\partial\Omega}
.
The main focus of this paper is the approximation of the eigenpairs
(\lambda,u)
of the biharmonic eigenvalue problem

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}u

\displaystyle=\lambda u\quad\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle\partial_{n}u

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Here and throughout this paper, the subscripts

and

are associated with differential operators in the normal and tangential directions, respectively. The variational formulation of (

) seeks
(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times V
with

such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{2}v)_{\Omega}=\lambda(u,v)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for any }v\in V.

For the source problem, there are numerous finite element methods for the approximation of (
1.2
). Conforming methods include the piecewise quintic Argyris element in 2D
[
2
]
or

conforming elements in a subtriangulation, also known as Hsieh-Clough-Tocher splits in 2D
[
20
,
37
]
and Worsey–Farin splits in 3D
[
47
,
37
]
. Due to the complicated nature of

conforming elements in the implementation, in particular in 3D, many nonconforming methods have been developed. On simplicial meshes, we mention the lowest-order Morley element
[
41
]
as well as its higher-order generalization in 2D
[
7
]
.
Finite elements of arbitrary order on general meshes include the virtual elements (VE) of
[
10
,
51
,
1
]
in 2D, the weak Galerkin (WG) methods of
[
43
,
49
]
, the hybrid high-order (HHO) methods of
[

,

,
30
]
, and the discontinuous Galerkin (dG) methods of
[

,
36
]
.
The degrees of freedom of the aforementioned methods are different and lead to varying computational efficiency. In structured meshes, the performance of all methods is fairly comparable; cf.
[
29
]
for a comparison between some HHO, WG, dG and the Morley finite element methods.

The focus of this paper is the HHO methodology introduced in
[
26
,
27
]
; we refer to the monographs
[
28
,
21
]
for an overview of applications.
The HHO methodology is a class of hybridizable methods that can be defined for arbitrary polytopal meshes and polynomial degree – a flexibility shared by many nonconforming methods from above. In fact, close relationship between hybridizable methods such as HDG, HHO, and WG exists, cf.
[
23
,
22
,
21
]
for further discussions. In certain applications, hybridizable methods allow for additional benefits. We mention guaranteed error control in convex minimization problems by duality techniques, where HHO methods provide a cheap post-processing of the dual variable
[
18
,
44
]
.

For eigenvalue problems, upper bounds can be obtained by conforming methods. However, applications, e.g., in safety analysis require guaranteed lower eigenvalue bounds (LEB), which is accessible by hybridizable methods
[
19
,
15
,
45
]
. Here, direct lower bounds can be obtained with a fine-tuned stabilization, i.e., the discrete eigenvalue is a lower bound of the exact one. This leads to
higher-order convergence rates and provides access to adaptive mesh-refining algorithm. Thus, the HHO eigensolver of this paper can be considered as a higher-order generalization of
[
17
]
.
It turns out that these benefits are related to the design of the reconstruction operator, which satisfies the following commuting property.
Let
V_{h}
denote the discrete ansatz space and
I_{h}:V\to V_{h}
is a canonical interpolation of
V
onto
V_{h}
.
The Hessian of a continuous function is approximated by the piecewise Hessian of a reconstruction operator
R_{h}:V_{h}\to P_{k+2}(\mathcal{T})
, where
k\geq 0
is the degree of approximation and
\mathcal{T}
is the underlying mesh, such that the
L^{2}
orthogonality

(1.3)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(v-R_{h}I_{h}v)\perp\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}P_{k+2}(\mathcal{T})\quad\text{for any }v\in V

holds.
This property is available in 2D for
[
29
]
and in arbitrary space dimensions for
[
8
]
, although the design of
[
29
]
appears more sophisticated as it utilizes less (non-hybridizable) degrees of freedom. To achieve (
1.3
) in any space dimensions and derive LEB for (
1.2
), we utilize an ansatz similar to
[
39
]
. The point of departure is the local integration by parts formula

(1.4)

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}v,\nabla^{2}p)_{T}=(v,\Delta^{2}p)_{T}-(v,\partial_{n}\Delta p)_{\partial T}+(\partial_{n}v,\partial_{nn}p)_{\partial T}+(\partial_{t}v,\partial_{nt}p)_{\partial T}

for any
v\in H^{2}(T)
and
p\in P_{k+2}(T)
in a mesh element
T

from
[
29
]
.
For the Poisson equation, lowest-order hybridizable and Crouzeix-Raviart methods are closely related.
Thus, considering the degrees of freedom of Morley finite elements motivates another integration by parts for the term
(\partial_{t}v,\partial_{nt}p)_{\partial T}
along the
n-1
dimensional hyperfaces
F
of
T
with outer normal vector
\nu_{\partial F}
. We arrive at

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\nabla^{2}v,\nabla^{2}p)_{T}&=(v,\Delta^{2}p)_{T}-(v,\partial_{n}\Delta p)_{\partial T}+(\partial_{n}v,\partial_{nn}p)_{\partial T}\\
&\quad-(v,\Delta_{t}\partial_{n}p)_{\partial T}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}(v,\partial_{tn}p\cdot\nu_{\partial F})_{\partial F},\end{aligned}

where
\partial F
denotes the
n-2
dimensional boundary of the face
F
.
(In 2D, integrals over vertices are understood as the point evaluation.)
This formula leads to a discrete ansatz space that also involves degrees of freedom to approximate the restriction of
v
along
\partial F
similar to
[
39
]
and the virtual elements
[
10
,
51
,
1
]
.
These additional degrees of freedom ensure that the resulting reconstruction operator

satisfies (

and, thus, higher computational cost is expected, cf.

In combination with a new stabilization, we derive the first LEB with higher convergence rates for the biharmonic problem in the current literature with the arguments of
[

,
45
]
.
To be precise,
we establish that

\displaystyle\mathrm{LEB}(j)\coloneqq\min\{1,1/(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{h}(j))\}\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\lambda(j)

is a lower bound for the

. Notably,

In particular, if

In addition to LEB, we provide a full error analysis of the source and eigenvalue problem under minimal

regularity assumptions of the exact solution.
For the source problem associated with (

), we prove the Cea’s type estimate

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}+|u_{h}|_{s_{h}}\lesssim\min_{p\in P_{k+2}(\mathcal{T})}\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-p)\|_{\Omega}+\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T})

with the data oscillation
\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T})
of a given right-hand side
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
This is to be contrasted with the error analysis of
[
29
]
, where

regularity of the solution with

is required. A reduction of the smoothness assumption can be achieved by employing

ansatz spaces for the cell variable on regular simplicial meshes, cf.
[

]
.

An important aspect of (

) is the efficiency
|u_{h}|_{s_{h}}\lesssim
of the stabilization
|u_{h}|_{s_{h}}
of
u_{h}
, which contributes to a reliable and efficient error control

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\eta\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}+\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T})

with the error estimator
\eta
defined in (
3.21
) below. Thus, this paper provides an extension of the analysis of
[
33
,
5
,
12
]
from second-order to fourth-order problems.
The error analysis of the source problem can be extended to the eigenvalue problem using the arguments of
[
35
,
15
]
.

For the sake of brevity, we only consider conforming simplicial meshes but mention that an extension to general polytopal meshes is straight-forward, cf.
Remark

.
Finally, we note that parts of our error analysis are restricted to
n\in\{2,3\}
. The reason is the lack of conforming finite element spaces in higher space dimensions, whose degrees of freedom do not depend on second or higher derivatives – a technical difficulty that may be solved in the future.

Discretization

In this section, we provide details on the numerical scheme for the source problem associated with (
1.2
), which seeks, for a given right-hand side
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the solution
u\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{2}v)_{\Omega}=(f,v)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for any }v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

The HHO method for the eigenvalue problem is presented in
Section

4
, where the stabilization is slightly modified for explicit constants in the LEB (
1.6
).

2.1.
Triangulation

A
d
-simplex,
0\leq d\leq n
, is the convex hull of
d+1
points with positive
d
dimensional Lebesgue measure.
A
d
dimensional side of a simplex
T
is the convex combination of
d+1
vertices of
T
.
The set of
n-1
(resp.
n-2
) dimensional sides of
T
, called faces (resp. edges) of
T
, is denoted by
\mathcal{F}(T)
(resp.
\mathcal{E}(T)
).
Let
\mathcal{T}
be a regular triangulation of
\Omega
into closed nonempty simplices so that
\cup\mathcal{T}=\overline{\Omega}
and the intersection of two distinct simplices is either empty or exactly one lower-dimensional side.
The sets
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\cup_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\mathcal{F}(T)
and
\mathcal{E}\coloneqq\cup_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\mathcal{E}(T)
consist of all
n-1
and
n-2
dimensional sides in
\mathcal{T}
.
Interior sides are collected in
\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
, while
\mathcal{F}(\partial\Omega)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{E}(\partial\Omega)\coloneqq\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
are the set of boundary sides.

For any face
F\in\mathcal{F}
, the direction of its unit outer normal vector is fixed so that
\nu_{F}\coloneqq\nu_{\partial\Omega}|_{F}
for each
F\in\mathcal{F}(\partial\Omega)
. The neighbors of an interior face
F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
are denoted by
T_{\pm}
with the convention
\nu_{\partial T_{+}}|_{F}=\nu_{F}
. The jump of a piecewise function
v\in W^{1,1}(\mathrm{int}(T_{\pm}))
reads
[v]_{F}\coloneqq v|_{T_{+}}-v|_{T_{-}}\in L^{1}(F)
. On boundary faces, the jump of any function is defined as its trace.

Given a
d
-simplex
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of diameter
h_{M}\coloneqq\mathrm{diam}(M)
, the set
P_{k}(M)
consists of all restrictions of polynomials of degree at most
k
onto
M
and the map
\Pi_{M}^{k}:L^{1}(M)\to P_{k}(M)
denotes the
L^{2}
orthogonal projection onto
P_{k}(M)
. In the special case
d=0
(i.e.,
M
is a vertex),
\Pi_{M}^{k}
is the point evaluation.
The piecewise version of this for the triangulation
\mathcal{T}
reads
\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{k}:L^{1}(\Omega)\to P_{k}(\mathcal{T})
, where
(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{k}v)|_{T}\coloneqq\Pi_{T}^{k}v|_{T}
for any
T\in\mathcal{T}
.
We define the projections
\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{k}
and
\Pi_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}
analogously.
The mesh size function
h_{\mathcal{T}}
of the triangulation
\mathcal{T}
is defined locally by
h_{\mathcal{\mathcal{T}}}|_{T}\coloneqq h_{T}
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}
.
Given
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the data oscillation of
f
of degree
\ell\geq 0
reads
\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T})\coloneqq\|h_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})f\|_{\Omega}
.
The shape regularity
\varrho(T)
of a simplex
T
is the ratio of its diameter to the radius of the largest inscribed ball. This gives rise to the shape regularity
\varrho(\mathcal{T})\coloneqq\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\varrho(T)
of the triangulation
\mathcal{T}
.

The differential operators
\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}
,
\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}
, and
\Delta^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}
denote the piecewise
version of
\nabla^{2}
,
\nabla
and
\Delta
without explicit reference to
\mathcal{T}
.

2.2.
Local ansatz space and reconstructions

Given
k\geq 0
and a simplex
T\in\mathcal{T}
, the local HHO ansatz space reads

V_{h}(T)\coloneqq P_{\ell}(T)\times P_{m}(\mathcal{F}(T))\times P_{k}(\mathcal{F}(T))\times P_{k}(\mathcal{E}(T)),

with
\ell\geq\max\{k-2,1\}
,
m=\max\{k-1,0\}
, and the canonical interpolation
I_{T}:W^{2,1}(\mathrm{int}(T))\to V_{h}(T)
, defined by

\displaystyle v\mapsto(\Pi_{T}^{\ell}v,\Pi_{\mathcal{F}(T)}^{m}v,\Pi_{\mathcal{F}(T)}^{k}\partial_{n}v,\Pi_{\mathcal{E}(T)}^{k}v)\in V_{h}(T).

We note that the choice
\ell\coloneqq k+2
is relevant for computational LEB in
Section

4
below, while
\ell\geq 1
is required for the approximation of the right-hand side.
The local reconstruction operator
R_{T}:V_{h}(T)\to P_{k+2}(T)
maps
v_{h}=(v_{T},v_{\mathcal{F}(T)},\beta_{\mathcal{F}(T)},v_{\mathcal{E}(T)})\in V_{h}(T)
onto
R_{T}v_{h}\in P_{k+2}(T)
with

(2.2)

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}R_{T}v_{h},\nabla^{2}p)_{T}

\displaystyle=(v_{T},\Delta^{2}p)_{T}-(v_{\mathcal{F}(T)},\partial_{n}\Delta p)_{\partial T}+(\beta_{\mathcal{F}(T)},\partial_{nn}p)_{\partial T}

\displaystyle-(v_{\mathcal{F}(T)},\Delta_{t}\partial_{n}p)_{\partial T}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F(T)}}(v_{\mathcal{E}(T)},\partial_{tn}p\cdot\nu_{\partial F})_{\partial F}

for any
p\in P_{k+2}(T)
. This uniquely defines
R_{T}v_{h}
up to the degrees of freedom associated with
P_{1}(T)
, the kernel of
\nabla^{2}
. The latter are fixed by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{T}\nabla R_{T}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial T}v_{\mathcal{F}(T)}\nu_{\partial T}\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{T}R_{T}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{T}v_{T}\,\mathrm{d}x.

By construction of
R_{T}
, the following property holds.

Lemma 2.1

(commuting)
.

Any
v\in H^{2}(T)
satisfies the
L^{2}
orthogonality
\nabla^{2}(v-R_{T}I_{T}v)\perp\nabla^{2}P_{k+2}(T)
. In particular,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}(v-R_{T}I_{T}v)\|_{T}=\min_{p\in P_{k+2}(T)}\|\nabla^{2}(v-p)\|_{T}.

Proof.

The
L^{2}
orthogonality follows immediately from (
1.5
) and (
2.2
). This implies the best approximation property (
2.4
).
∎

The local stabilization
s_{T}:V_{h}(T)\times V_{h}(T)\to\mathbb{R}
of this paper reads, for any
u_{h}=(u_{T},u_{\mathcal{F}(T)},\alpha_{\mathcal{F}(T)},u_{\mathcal{E}(T)}),v_{h}=(v_{T},v_{\mathcal{F}(T)},\beta_{\mathcal{F}(T)},v_{\mathcal{E}(T)})\in V_{h}(T)
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} s_{T}(u_{h},v_{h})\coloneqq&h_{T}^{-4}(\Pi_{T}^{\ell}(u_{T}-R_{T}u_{h}),\Pi_{T}^{\ell}(v_{T}-R_{T}v_{h}))_{T}\\
&\quad+h_{T}^{-3}(\Pi_{\mathcal{F}(T)}^{m}(u_{\mathcal{F}(T)}-R_{T}u_{h}),\Pi_{\mathcal{F}(T)}^{m}(v_{\mathcal{F}(T)}-R_{T}v_{h}))_{\partial T}\\
&\quad+h_{T}^{-1}(\Pi_{\mathcal{F}(T)}^{k}(\alpha_{\mathcal{F}(T)}-\partial_{n}R_{T}u_{h}),\Pi_{\mathcal{F}(T)}^{k}(\beta_{\mathcal{F}(T)}-\partial_{n}R_{T}v_{h})_{\partial T}\\
&\quad+h_{T}^{-2}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}(T)}(\Pi_{E}^{k}(u_{\mathcal{E}(T)}-R_{T}u_{h}),\Pi_{E}^{k}(v_{\mathcal{E}(T)}-R_{T}v_{h}))_{E}.\end{aligned}

The stabilization is quasi-optimal in the following sense.

Lemma 2.2

(optimality of
s_{T}
)
.

Let a simplex
T
be given. Any
v\in H^{2}(T)
satisfies

\displaystyle\sqrt{s_{T}(I_{T}v,I_{T}v)}\lesssim\min_{p\in P_{k+2}(\mathcal{T})}\|\nabla^{2}(v-p)\|_{T}.

Proof.

The constraints (
2.3
) give rise to

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{T}R_{T}I_{T}v\,\text{d}x&=\int_{T}\Pi_{T}^{\ell}v\,\text{d}x=\int_{T}v\,\text{d}x,\\
\int_{T}\nabla R_{T}I_{T}v\,\text{d}x&=\int_{\partial T}\Pi_{\mathcal{F}(T)}^{m}v\nu_{\partial T}\,\text{d}s=\int_{\partial T}v\nu_{\partial T}\,\text{d}s=\int_{T}\nabla v\,\text{d}x.\end{aligned}

Hence, the Poincaré inequality imply

(2.7)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-2}\|v-R_{T}I_{T}v\|_{T}+h_{T}^{-1}\|\nabla(v-R_{T}I_{T}v)\|_{T}\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}(v-R_{T}I_{T}v)\|_{T}.

From this, the best approximation property of
L^{2}
projections, the trace inequality, we infer

\displaystyle s_{T}(I_{T}v,I_{T}v)\leq h_{T}^{-4}\|v-R_{T}I_{T}v\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{-3}\|v-R_{T}I_{T}v\|_{\partial T}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+h_{T}^{-1}\|\nabla(v-R_{T}I_{T}v)\|_{\partial T}^{2}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}(T)}h_{T}^{-2}\|v-R_{T}I_{T}v\|_{E}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}(v-R_{T}I_{T}v)\|_{T}^{2}.

This and (
2.4
) conclude the proof.
∎

2.3.
Discrete problem

The discrete ansatz space of
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
reads

V_{h}\coloneqq P_{\ell}(\mathcal{T})\times P_{m}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega))\times P_{k}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega))\times P_{k}(\mathcal{E}(\Omega))

with the local restriction

\displaystyle v_{h}|_{T}\coloneqq(v_{\mathcal{T}}|_{T},v_{\mathcal{F}}|_{\partial T},((\nu_{F}\cdot\nu_{\partial T}|_{F})\beta_{\mathcal{F}}|_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)},(v_{\mathcal{E}}|_{E})_{E\in\mathcal{E}(T)})\in V_{h}(T)

in
T\in\mathcal{T}

for any
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{F}},\beta_{\mathcal{F}},v_{\mathcal{E}})\in V_{h}
.
Here,
P_{m}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega))
(resp.
P_{k}(\mathcal{E}(\Omega))
) is the set of all functions
v_{\mathcal{F}}\in P_{m}(\mathcal{F})
(resp.
v_{\mathcal{E}}\in P_{k}(\mathcal{E})
) vanishing along boundary faces
v_{\mathcal{F}}|_{F}=0
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}(\partial\Omega)
(resp. boundary edges
v_{\mathcal{E}}|_{E}=0
for all
E\in\mathcal{E}(\partial\Omega)
) to model homogeneous boundary conditions. The global interpolation
I_{h}:H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow V_{h}
is defined by

\displaystyle v\mapsto(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell}v,\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{m}v,\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{k}(\nu_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot\nabla v),\Pi_{\mathcal{E}}^{k}v)\in V_{h}.

We define the reconstructions
R_{h}
and the stabilization
s_{h}
locally by

(2.8)

\displaystyle(R_{h}v_{h})|_{T}=R_{T}(v_{h}|_{T})\quad\text{in }T\in\mathcal{T}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})\coloneqq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}s_{T}(u_{h}|_{T},v_{h}|_{T})

for any
u_{h},v_{h}\in V_{h}
. The discrete problem seeks the unique solution
u_{h}\in V_{h}
to

(2.9)

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=(f,v_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}\quad\text{for any }v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{F}},\beta_{\mathcal{F}},v_{\mathcal{E}})\in V_{h}

with the bilinear form

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})\coloneqq(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}R_{h}u_{h},\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}R_{h}v_{h})_{\Omega}+s_{h}(u_{h},v_{h}),

which induce the seminorms
\|\bullet\|_{h}\coloneqq\sqrt{a_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)}
and
|\bullet|_{s_{h}}\coloneqq\sqrt{s_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)}
in
V_{h}
.

Remark 2.3

(relation to WG method of
[
39
]
)
.

If
\ell=k+2
, the discrete Hessian
\partial^{2}_{w}
in
[
39
]
is reconstructed in the space
P_{k}(\mathcal{T})^{n\times n}
of piecewise polynomials of degree at most
k
using a similar formula to (
2.2
). It can be shown that the
L^{2}
projection of the discrete Hessian
\partial_{w}^{2}v_{h}
from
[
39
]
onto
\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}P_{k+2}(\mathcal{T})
is equal to
\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}R_{h}v_{h}
for any
v_{h}\in V_{h}

Remark 3.2

(extension to other hybridizable methods)

and

Proof.

For the sake of brevity, we only provide details in three space dimensions because the 2D case is similar but simpler.

Construction.
Averaging techniques in
C^{1}
finite element spaces on the Worsey-Farin split of
\mathcal{T}

[
47
,
37
]
lead to a linear operator
\mathcal{A}_{h}:V_{h}\to V
with
the typical local approximation property

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|h_{T}^{-2}\delta\|^{2}_{T}\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F},F\cap T\neq\emptyset}(h_{F}^{-3}\|[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|^{2}_{F}+h_{F}^{-1}\|[\partial_{n}R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|^{2}_{F})

for any

and
\delta\coloneqq R_{h}v_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}v_{h}
, cf.
[
34
]
for further details.
To ensure the right-inverse property, we consider a correction operator
S_{h}

by setting appropriate degrees of freedom of
C^{1}
conforming elements as an alternative approach to bubble function techniques.
Let
r=\max\{\ell,m,k\}+10
.
For any
w=(w_{\mathcal{T}},w_{\mathcal{F}},\gamma_{\mathcal{F}},w_{\mathcal{E}})
in the product space
L^{2}(\mathcal{T})\times L^{2}(\cup\mathcal{F})\times L^{2}(\cup\mathcal{F})\times L^{2}(\cup\mathcal{E})
, there exists a function
S_{h}w\in P_{r}(\mathcal{T})\cap H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
with

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{E}S_{h}wp\,\text{d}t=\int_{E}w_{\mathcal{E}}p\,\text{d}t\quad\text{for any }p\in P_{r-10}(E)\text{ along every edge }E\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega),\\
&\int_{F}S_{h}wp\,\text{d}s=\int_{F}w_{\mathcal{F}}p\,\text{d}s\quad\text{for any }p\in P_{r-9}(F)\text{ on every face }F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega),\\
&\int_{F}\partial_{n}S_{h}wp\,\text{d}s=\int_{F}\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}p\,\text{d}s\quad\text{for any }p\in P_{r-7}(F)\text{ on every face }F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega),\\
&\int_{T}S_{h}wp\,\text{d}x=\int_{T}w_{\mathcal{T}}p\,\text{d}x\quad\text{for any }p\in P_{r-8}(T)\text{ in every }T\in\mathcal{T}.\end{aligned}

In fact, these weights are a subset of the degrees of freedom of the
C^{1}
conforming finite element
P_{r}(\mathcal{T})\cap H^{2}(\Omega)

[
50
]
, cf.
Appendix

A
for further details. By setting the remaining degrees of freedom to zero, we obtain
S_{h}w\in V
and, from equivalence of norms in finite dimensional spaces and scaling arguments,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &h_{T}^{-4}\|S_{h}w\|_{T}^{2}\lesssim h_{T}^{-4}\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{T}^{2}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}(h_{F}^{-3}\|w_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{F}^{2}+h_{F}^{-1}\|\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}\|^{2}_{F})+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}(T)}h_{E}^{-2}\|w_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{E}^{2}.\end{aligned}

Define
J_{h}:V_{h}\to V
for any
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{F}},\beta_{\mathcal{F}},v_{\mathcal{E}})\in V_{h}
by

\displaystyle J_{h}v_{h}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{h}v_{h}+S_{h}w\in V

with the choice
w=v_{h}-I_{h}\mathcal{A}_{h}v_{h}
.
By construction of
S_{h}
in (
3.2
),
I_{h}J_{h}=\mathrm{Id}
in
V_{h}
.

Approximation property of
J_{h}
.

Given a simplex
T\in\mathcal{T}
, (
3.3
), the best approximation of
L^{2}
projections, trace, and inverse inequalities imply

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-4}\|S_{h}w\|_{T}^{2}\lesssim s_{T}(v_{h},v_{h})+h_{T}^{-4}\|\Pi_{T}^{\ell}\delta\|_{T}^{2}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}(T)}h_{E}^{-2}\|\Pi_{F}^{k}\delta|_{T}\|_{E}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}(h_{F}^{-3}\|\Pi_{F}^{m}\delta|_{T}\|_{F}^{2}+h_{F}^{-1}\|\Pi_{F}^{k}\partial_{n}\delta|_{T}\|_{F}^{2})\lesssim s_{T}(v_{h},v_{h})+h_{T}^{-4}\|\delta\|_{T}^{2}.

This,
\|(J_{h}-R_{h})v_{h}\|_{T}\leq\|\delta\|_{T}+\|S_{h}w\|_{T}
from a triangle inequality, (
3.1
), and the sum over all simplices establish

\displaystyle\|h_{\mathcal{T}}^{-2}(J_{h}-R_{h})v_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}\lesssim s_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})

(3.4)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}(h_{F}^{-3}\|[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|^{2}_{F}+h_{F}^{-1}\|[\partial_{n}R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|^{2}_{F}).

Continuity of
J_{h}
.

For any
F\in\mathcal{F}
, the triangle and Poincaré inequality show

\displaystyle h_{F}^{-3/2}

\displaystyle\|[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}\lesssim h_{F}^{-3/2}\|\Pi_{F}^{0}[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}+h_{F}^{-1/2}\|[\partial_{t}R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{F}^{-3/2}\|\Pi_{F}^{0}[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}+h_{F}^{-1/2}\|\Pi_{F}^{0}[\partial_{t}R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}+h_{F}^{1/2}\|[\partial_{tt}R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}.

An integration by parts on the (two-dimensional) face
F
, the Cauchy, and discrete trace inequality establish, for any
p\in P_{0}(F)^{2}
, that

\displaystyle\int_{F}\Pi_{F}^{0}[\partial_{t}R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}p\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{F}[\partial_{t}R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}p\,\text{d}s=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}(F)}\int_{E}[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}p\cdot\nu_{\partial F}|_{E}\,\text{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}(F)}h_{E}^{-1}\|\Pi_{E}^{k}[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{E}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\|p\|_{F},

whence
h_{F}^{-1}\|\Pi_{F}^{0}[\partial_{t}R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}(F)}h_{E}^{-2}\|\Pi_{E}^{k}[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{E}^{2}
. If

is an interior face with the neighbors

, then
\|\Pi_{E}^{k}[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{E}\leq\|\Pi_{E}^{k}(R_{h}v_{h}|_{T_{+}}-v_{\mathcal{E}})\|_{E}+\|\Pi_{E}^{k}(R_{h}v_{h}|_{T_{-}}-v_{\mathcal{E}})\|_{E}
. If

, then

and so,
\|\Pi_{E}^{k}[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{E}=\|\Pi_{E}^{k}(R_{h}v_{h}-v_{\mathcal{E}})\|_{E}
from homogeneous boundary conditions. Thus,

(3.6)

\displaystyle h_{F}^{-1}\|\Pi_{F}^{0}\partial_{t}[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T},F\subset K}s_{K}(v_{h},v_{h}).

It is straight-forward to verify

and so, the best approximation property of

projections
and the triangle inequality provide

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{F}^{0}[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\|\Pi_{F}^{m}[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T},F\subset K}\|\Pi_{F}^{m}(R_{h}v_{h}|_{K}-v_{\mathcal{F}})\|_{F}^{2}.

The combination of (

3.7

\displaystyle h_{F}^{-3}\|[R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T},F\subset K}(s_{K}(v_{h},v_{h})+\|\nabla^{2}R_{h}v_{h}\|_{K}^{2})

We proceed as above to bound the normal gradient jump of

by

\displaystyle h_{F}^{-1}\|[\partial_{n}R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{F}^{-1}\|\Pi_{F}^{0}[\partial_{n}R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}^{2}+h_{F}\|[\partial_{tn}R_{h}v_{h}]_{F}\|_{F}^{2}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T},F\subset K}(s_{K}(v_{h},v_{h})+\|\nabla^{2}R_{h}v_{h}\|_{K}^{2}).

This, (
3.4
), (
3.8
), and inverse estimates conclude the continuity of

(3.18)

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{2}z,\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}R_{h}e_{h})_{\Omega}+(\nabla^{2}z,\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(J_{h}e_{h}-R_{h}e_{h}))_{\Omega}.

The

orthogonality (

), a Cauchy inequality, and the continuity of

prove

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}z,\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(J_{h}e_{h}-R_{h}e_{h}))_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(z-G_{h}z),\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}J_{h}e_{h})_{\Omega}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(z-G_{h}z)\|_{\Omega}\|e_{h}\|_{h}.

Linear algebra shows the split

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}z,\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}

\displaystyle R_{h}e_{h})_{\Omega}=(\nabla^{2}z,\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}G_{h}u)_{\Omega}-(\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}G_{h}z,\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}R_{h}u_{h})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{2}z,\nabla^{2}u)_{\Omega}-(\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}G_{h}z,\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}R_{h}u_{h})_{\Omega}-(\nabla^{2}z,\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-G_{h}u))_{\Omega}.

The first term on the right-hand side is equal to

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(z-G_{h}z),\nabla^{2}u)_{\Omega}+(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(G_{h}z-J_{h}I_{h}z),\nabla^{2}u)_{\Omega}+(\nabla^{2}J_{h}I_{h}z,\nabla^{2}u)_{\Omega}.

This, the previously displayed formula, the variational formulations (
2.1
) and (
2.9
), and
(\nabla^{2}z,\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-G_{h}u))_{\Omega}=(\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(z-G_{h}z),\nabla^{2}u)_{\Omega}
from (

) provide

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}z,\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}R_{h}e_{h})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(f,J_{h}I_{h}z-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell}z)_{\Omega}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\qquad+s(I_{h}z,u_{h})+(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(G_{h}z-J_{h}I_{h}z),\nabla^{2}u)_{\Omega}.

The
L^{2}
orthogonality
J_{h}I_{h}z-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell}z\perp P_{\ell}(\mathcal{T})
from
I_{h}\circ J_{h}=\mathrm{Id}
in
V_{h}
and (
3.11
) imply

\displaystyle(f,J_{h}I_{h}z-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell}z)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=((1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})f,J_{h}I_{h}z-z)_{\Omega}+((1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})f,(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})z)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T})(\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(z-G_{h}z)\|_{\Omega}+\|h_{\mathcal{T}}^{-2}(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})z\|_{\Omega}).

Hence, (
3.20
), the
L^{2}
orthogonality
\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(G_{h}z-J_{h}I_{h}z)\perp\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}P_{k+2}(\mathcal{T})
from
Lemma

2.1
, the Cauchy inequality,
Lemma

2.2
, and (
3.10
) show

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}z,\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}R_{h}e_{h})_{\Omega}\lesssim(\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T})+|u_{h}|_{s_{h}}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-G_{h}u)\|_{\Omega})

\displaystyle\times(\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(z-G_{h}z)\|_{\Omega}+\|h_{\mathcal{T}}^{-2}(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})z\|_{\Omega})

.

The combination of this with (
3.18
)–(
3.19
) results in

\displaystyle\|e_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{\Omega}\lesssim(\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T})+\|e_{h}\|_{h}+|u_{h}|_{s_{h}}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-G_{h}u)\|_{\Omega})

\displaystyle\times(\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(z-G_{h}z)\|_{\Omega}+\|h_{\mathcal{T}}^{-2}(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})z\|_{\Omega})

.

The assertion then follows from
Theorem

3.5
and the elliptic regularity (

).
∎

Remark 3.9

To extend the analysis of this paper to polytopal meshes, let us consider a partition

or

.

Recall that (

\displaystyle 0<\lambda(1)<\lambda(2)\leq\lambda(3)\leq\dots

4.1.
Discrete eigenvalue problem

Higher-order computational LEB require a fine-tuning of the stabilization and explicit constants in

2.2

with a face

and an edge

\displaystyle\ell_{T}(F)\coloneqq h_{T}^{2}|F|/|T|\quad\text{and}\quad\ell_{T}(E,F)\coloneqq\begin{cases}h_{T}^{2}|E|/|F|&\mbox{if }n=3,\\
h_{T}^{2}/|F|&\mbox{if }n=2.\end{cases}

Given a simplex

and a positive parameter

in (

\displaystyle s_{T}(u_{h},v_{h})\coloneqq\sigma h_{T}^{-4}(\Pi_{T}^{\ell}(u_{T}-R_{T}u_{h}),\Pi_{T}^{\ell}(v_{T}-R_{T}v_{h}))_{T}

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma h_{T}^{-2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}\ell_{T}^{-1}(F)(\Pi_{F}^{m}(u_{\mathcal{F}(T)}-R_{T}u_{h}),\Pi_{F}^{m}(v_{\mathcal{F}(T)}-R_{T}v_{h}))_{F}

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}\ell_{T}^{-1}(F)(\Pi_{F}^{k}(\alpha_{\mathcal{F}(T)}-\partial_{n}R_{T}u_{h}),\Pi_{F}^{k}(\beta_{\mathcal{F}(T)}-\partial_{n}R_{T}v_{h}))_{F}

We mention that the weights

and

are, up to multiplicative constants depending on the shape of

for any

of (

Apart from this modification for the stabilization, we adopt the remaining notation from

.
Let

so that the discrete ansatz space reads

uniquely.
We define the bilinear form

\displaystyle a_{\mathcal{T}}(u_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{T}})\coloneqq a_{h}((u_{\mathcal{T}},Z_{h}u_{\mathcal{T}}),(v_{\mathcal{T}},Z_{h}v_{\mathcal{T}}))\quad\text{for any }u_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{\mathcal{T}}

and introduce the solution operator
\hat{T}_{h}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to V_{\mathcal{T}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
with

\displaystyle a_{\mathcal{T}}(\hat{T}_{h}\phi,v_{\mathcal{T}})=(\phi,v_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}\quad\text{for any }\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega)\text{ and }v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V_{\mathcal{T}}.

The face and edge variables are eliminated by the operator
Z_{h}
and so,

(4.5)

\displaystyle T_{h}\phi=(\hat{T}_{h}\phi,Z_{h}\hat{T}_{h}\phi)\quad\text{for any }\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega),

cf.
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
for a precise proof of this. Thus,
\lambda_{h}
is a discrete eigenvalue of (
4.2
) if and only if
\lambda_{h}^{-1}
is an eigenvalue of
\hat{T}_{h}
.
The convergence of
\hat{T}_{h}
to
T
can be obtained from the error estimates of
Section

3
.
Recall the index
s
of elliptic regularity from (
3.17
).

Theorem 4.4

(Convergence of solution operators)
.

It holds

\displaystyle\|T-\hat{T}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\Omega);L^{2}(\Omega))}\lesssim h^{s}.

In particular, for any eigenvalue
\mu
of
T
with algebraic multiplicity
r
, there exist
r
eigenvalues
\mu_{h,1},\dots,\mu_{h,r}
of
\hat{T}_{h}
with
\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\{\mu-\mu_{h,r}\}\lesssim h^{s}
.

Proof.

Given any
L^{2}
functions
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, (
4.4
)–(
4.5
) imply

\displaystyle(T\phi-\hat{T}_{h}\phi,\psi)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=((1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})T\phi,\psi)_{\Omega}+(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell}T\phi-\hat{T}_{h}\phi,\psi)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=((1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})T\phi,\psi)_{\Omega}+a_{h}(I_{h}T\phi-T_{h}\phi,T_{h}\psi)

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})T\phi\|_{\Omega}\|\psi\|_{\Omega}+\|I_{h}T\phi-T_{h}\phi\|_{h}\|T_{h}\psi\|_{h}.

Theorem

3.5
and the elliptic regularity of
T\phi\in H^{2+s}(\Omega)
provide
\|I_{h}T\phi-T_{h}\phi\|_{h}\lesssim\mathrm{osc}(\phi,\mathcal{T})+\min_{p\in P_{k+2}(\mathcal{T})}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(T\phi-p)\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h^{s}\|\phi\|_{\Omega}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})T\phi\|_{\Omega}+\|I_{h}T\phi-T_{h}\phi\|_{h}\lesssim h^{2}\|\nabla^{2}T\phi\|_{\Omega}+h^{s}\|\phi\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h^{s}\|\phi\|_{\Omega}

from a Poincaré inequality and the stability
\|\nabla^{2}T\phi\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{\Omega}
of the solution operator
T
.
A triangle inequality and
Theorem

3.5
prove

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{h}\psi\|_{h}\leq\|T_{h}\psi-I_{h}T\psi\|_{h}+\|I_{h}T\psi\|_{h}\lesssim\mathrm{osc}(\psi,\mathcal{T})+\|\nabla^{2}T\psi\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{\Omega}.

The combination of the three previously displayed formula concludes the proof.
∎

We mention that the convergence rates given in
Theorem

4.4
are suboptimal, but can be recovered using the arguments of
[
11
]
. However, the sole goal was to establish the spectral correctness of the method as a detailed error analysis (that lead to optimal convergence rates) is presented below.
Furthermore, the spectral correctness also applies to any
\ell\geq\max\{k-2,1\}
(not just
\ell=k+2
).

4.4.
A priori

In the remaining parts of this section, we extend the error analysis of the source problem in
Section

3
to the eigenvalue problem (
1.2
) using the arguments of
[
14
,
15
]
. For the sake of brevity, the analysis is only carried out for simple eigenvalues. However, we mention that an extension to eigenvalue clusters is possible following
[

,

]
.

Let
\lambda=\lambda(j)
be a simple eigenvalue with the associated eigenvector
u=u(j)
. For
1\leq j\leq\dim(P_{\ell}(\mathcal{T}))
(with
\ell=k+2
), the discrete problem (
4.2
) provides an approximation
\lambda_{h}=\lambda_{h}(j)
of
\lambda
with the discrete eigenvector
u_{h}=(u_{\mathcal{T}},u_{\mathcal{F}},\alpha_{\mathcal{F}},u_{\mathcal{E}})=u_{h}(j)\in V_{h}
.
The sign of the eigenvectors can be chosen so that
(u,u_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}\geq 0
.
Recall the index
2\geq s>0
of elliptic regularity from (
3.17
) and
t\coloneqq\min\{s,\ell-1\}
from
Theorem

3.6
.

Theorem 4.5

(a priori)
.

Assume that the mesh size
h
is sufficiently small. Then

\displaystyle|\lambda-\lambda_{h}|+\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{h}^{2}+h^{-2t}\|u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}^{2}\lesssim\min_{p\in P_{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-p)\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof departs from the auxiliary problem that seeks the discrete solution
\widetilde{u}_{h}=(\widetilde{u}_{\mathcal{T}},\widetilde{u}_{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{\alpha}_{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{u}_{\mathcal{E}})\in V_{h}
to (
2.9
) with the right-hand side
f=\lambda u
, i.e.,

\displaystyle a_{h}(\widetilde{u}_{h},v_{h})=\lambda(u,v_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}\quad\text{for any }v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{F}},\beta_{\mathcal{F}},v_{\mathcal{E}})\in V_{h}.

For sufficiently small mesh size
h
,
Theorem

4.4
shows that

C\coloneqq\sqrt{2}(1+\max_{k\in\{1,\cdots,N\}\setminus\{j\}}|\lambda/(\lambda-\lambda_{h}(k))|)\lesssim 1

is uniformly bounded. It is known from
[
14
, Lemma 2.4]
that

\|u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}\leq C\|u-\widetilde{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}
.
Hence, a triangle inequality, the
L^{2}
estimate of
Theorem

3.6
, and (
2.11
)–(
2.12
) imply

\displaystyle\|u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|u-\widetilde{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}\leq\|(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})u\|_{\Omega}+\|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell}u-\widetilde{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-G_{h})u\|_{\Omega}+\|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell}u-\widetilde{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h^{t}\min_{p\in P_{k+2}(\mathcal{T})}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-p)\|_{\Omega}+h^{t}\mathrm{osc}(\lambda u,\mathcal{T}).

Notice that, for
\ell=k+2
,
\mathrm{osc}(\lambda u,\mathcal{T})\leq\|h_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}\lambda(u-G_{h}u)\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h^{4}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-G_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}
from (
2.12
) and with (
2.11
),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h^{t}\min_{p\in P_{k+2}(\mathcal{T})}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-p)\|_{\Omega}.

With the abbreviation
e_{h}=I_{h}u-u_{h}
, it remains to establish

\displaystyle|\lambda-\lambda_{h}|+\|e_{h}\|_{h}^{2}\lesssim\min_{p\in P_{k+2}(\mathcal{T})}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-p)\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

Elementary algebra with the normalization
\|u\|_{\Omega}=1=\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}
leads to
2\lambda=\lambda\|u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+2\lambda(u,u_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}
. This and
\|e_{h}\|_{h}^{2}-\lambda_{h}=\|I_{h}u\|_{h}^{2}-2a_{h}(I_{h}u,u_{h})
from
\lambda_{h}=\|u_{h}\|_{h}^{2}
show the split

(4.7)

\displaystyle\lambda-\lambda_{h}+\|e_{h}\|_{h}^{2}=\lambda\|u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\|I_{h}u\|_{h}^{2}-\lambda+2(\lambda(u,u_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}-a_{h}(I_{h}u,u_{h})).

The individual terms on the right-hand side are bounded as follows.

Lemma

2.1
proves
\lambda=\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{\Omega}^{2}\geq\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}G_{h}u\|_{\Omega}^{2}
and so,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|I_{h}u\|_{h}^{2}-\lambda=\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}G_{h}u\|_{\Omega}^{2}+|I_{h}u|_{s_{h}}^{2}-\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq|I_{h}u|_{s_{h}}^{2}.

The variational formulation (
1.2
) proves

\displaystyle\lambda(u,u_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\lambda(u,J_{h}u_{h})_{\Omega}-\lambda((1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})u,J_{h}u_{h})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{2}J_{h}u_{h})_{\Omega}-\lambda((1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})u,J_{h}u_{h})_{\Omega}.

This and the identity
a_{h}(I_{h}u,u_{h})=(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}G_{h}u,\nabla^{2}J_{h}u_{h})_{\Omega}+s_{h}(I_{h}u,u_{h})
from
Lemma

2.1
provide

\displaystyle\lambda(u,u_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}-a_{h}(I_{h}u,u_{h})

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-G_{h}u),\nabla^{2}J_{h}u_{h})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda((1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})u,J_{h}u_{h})_{\Omega}-s_{h}(I_{h}u,u_{h}).

From (
2.12
)–(
2.13
) and the orthogonality (
2.10
), we deduce

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-G_{h}u),\nabla^{2}J_{h}u_{h})_{\Omega}-\lambda((1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell})u,J_{h}u_{h})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-G_{h}u)\|_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(J_{h}u_{h}-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}

.

The combination of the two previously displayed formula results in

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\lambda(u,u_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}-a_{h}(I_{h}u,u_{h})\\
&\qquad\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-G_{h}u)\|_{\Omega}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(J_{h}u_{h}-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}-s_{h}(I_{h}u,u_{h}).\end{aligned}

Recall the estimate
\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(J_{h}-R_{h})I_{h}u\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-G_{h}u)\|_{\Omega}
from (
3.10
).
The triangle inequality and continuity of
J_{h}
in
Lemma

3.1
imply

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(J_{h}u_{h}-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla^{2}J_{h}e_{h}\|_{\Omega}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}R_{h}e_{h}\|_{\Omega}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(J_{h}-R_{h})I_{h}u\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|e_{h}\|_{h}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-G_{h}u)\|_{\Omega}.

This, (
4.9
),
-s_{h}(I_{h}u,u_{h})\leq|I_{h}u|^{2}_{s_{h}}+|I_{h}u|_{s_{h}}|e_{h}|_{s_{h}}
, and
Lemma

2.2
provide

\displaystyle\lambda(u,u_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}-a_{h}(I_{h}u,u_{h})\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-G_{h}u)\|_{\Omega}(\|e_{h}\|_{h}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-G_{h}u)\|_{\Omega}).

In combination with (
4.6
)–(
4.8
) and
Lemma

2.2
, we conclude

\displaystyle\lambda-\lambda_{h}+\|e_{h}\|_{h}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-G_{h}u)\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

If
\sigma
is chosen sufficiently small so that
\lambda_{h}\leq\lambda
for sufficiently small mesh sizes
h
from
Remark

4.2
, then the proof is complete. Otherwise, we need to bound of
\lambda_{h}-\lambda+\|e_{h}\|_{h}^{2}
. Elementary algebra with
\|u\|_{\Omega}=1=\|u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}
and (
4.2
) lead to
2\lambda_{h}=\lambda_{h}\|u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{\Omega}+2\lambda_{h}(u,u_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}=\lambda_{h}\|u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{\Omega}+2a_{h}(u_{h},I_{h}u)
. This,
\lambda=\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{\Omega}^{2}\geq\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}G_{h}u\|_{\Omega}^{2}
from (
2.10
), and
\lambda_{h}=\|u_{h}\|_{h}^{2}
yield

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}-\lambda+\|e_{h}\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{h}\|u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{\Omega}-\lambda-\lambda_{h}+2a_{h}(u_{h},I_{h}u)+\|e_{h}\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{h}\|u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{\Omega}-\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}G_{h}u\|_{\Omega}^{2}-\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{h}+2a_{h}(u_{h},I_{h}u)+\|e_{h}\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{h}\|u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{\Omega}+|I_{h}u|_{s_{h}}^{2},

where
\|e_{h}\|_{h}^{2}=\|u_{h}\|_{h}^{2}+\|I_{h}u\|^{2}_{h}-2a_{h}(u_{h},I_{h}u)
is utilized in the last step.
The assertion
\lambda_{h}-\lambda+\|e_{h}\|_{h}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-G_{h}u)\|^{2}_{\Omega}
follows from (
4.6
) and
Lemma

2.2
.
∎

4.5.
A posteriori

The error control of
Theorem

3.7
motivates the error estimator

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}^{2}\coloneqq\mu^{2}+|u_{h}|_{s_{h}}^{2}

for the biharmonic eigenvalue problem, where
\mu
is defined in (

).

Theorem 4.6

(a posteriori)
.

For sufficiently small mesh sizes
h
, it holds

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}\approx\widehat{\eta}.

Proof.

The proof departs from the auxiliary problem that seeks
\widetilde{u}\in V
such that

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}\widetilde{u},\nabla^{2}v)_{\Omega}=\lambda_{h}(u_{\mathcal{T}},v)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for any }v\in V.

In other words,
\widetilde{u}
is the unique solution to the biharmonic problem with right-hand side
\lambda_{h}u_{\mathcal{T}}
and so, the a posteriori error control from
Theorem

3.7
provides

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\widetilde{u}-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\widehat{\eta},

where the data oscillation
\mathrm{osc}(\lambda_{h}u_{\mathcal{T}},\mathcal{T})=0
vanishes.
The error between
u
and
\widetilde{u}
is bounded by the error of the right-hand side

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}(u-\widetilde{u})\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|\lambda u-\lambda_{h}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}.

Hence, the triangle inequality implies

(4.11)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\widehat{\eta}+\|\lambda u-\lambda_{h}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}.

The inequality (6.7) of
[
15
]
applies and provides

(4.12)

\displaystyle\|\lambda u-\lambda_{h}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h^{s}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-G_{h}u)\|_{\Omega}

from (
4.6
).
Hence, for sufficient small mesh sizes
h
, the reliability
\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\widehat{\eta}
follows from (
4.11
).
Recall that
\widehat{\eta}
is efficient in the sense

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(\widetilde{u}-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}.

This, a triangle inequality, (
4.10
), and (
4.12
) conclude the efficiency

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|\nabla^{2}(u-\widetilde{u})\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|\lambda u-\lambda_{h}u_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-R_{h}u_{h})\|_{\Omega}.\qed

A.1.

- finite element in 2D

The

- finite element in 2D is known as the Argyris element (cf.
[
20
, Section 6]
). For each

with

, the local degrees of freedom are given by:

(1)

u(x)
,
\nabla u(x)
,
\nabla^{2}u(x)
at each vertex
x
of
T
,

(2)

\int_{e}uq\,\text{d}s
for any

and edge

(if

for any

and edge

of

with unit normal vector

,

(4)

\int_{T}uq\,\text{d}x
for each

(if

Theorem A.1

(
C^{1}
- finite element in 2D)
.

Any
u\in P_{k}(T)
with
k\geq 5
is uniquely determined by the degrees of freedom from above.

Proof.

The number of degrees of freedom is

\displaystyle 6\times 3+(2k-9)\times 3+\tfrac{(k-5)(k-4)}{2}=\tfrac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}=\operatorname{dim}P_{k}(T)

It suffices to prove that
u=0
if it vanishes at all the degrees of freedom.
From the first set of degrees of freedom, the derivatives of
u
of order
\leq 2
vanish at all the vertices, which indicates that
u|_{e}=(\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k})^{3}p
for some
p\in P_{k-6}(e)
on an edge
e=\mathrm{conv}\{x_{j},x_{k}\}
. Then the second set of degrees of freedom implies that
u|_{e}=0
. Similarly, it holds that
\tfrac{\partial u}{\partial n_{e}}|_{e}=(\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k})^{2}w
for some
w\in P_{k-5}(e)
and the third set of degrees of freedom provides
\tfrac{\partial u}{\partial n_{e}}|_{e}=0
. In summary,
u
and
\nabla u
vanishes on the boundary of
T
and so,
u=(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3})^{2}v
for some
v\in P_{k-6}(T)
. The fourth set of degrees of freedom concludes
u=0
.
∎

- finite element in 3D

with

at each vertex

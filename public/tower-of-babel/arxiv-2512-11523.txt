Quantization for Semipositive Adjoint Line Bundles
Yu-Chi Hou

Abstract
. Given a big and semipositive line bundle in the adjoint setting, we show that Donaldson’s quantized Monge–Ampère energy associated with any bounded plurisubharmonic weight converges to the Monge–Ampère energy. Our proof refines an argument of Berman and Freixas i Montplet in the ample case by employing a pointwise semi-classical Ohsawa–Takegoshi type extension theorem. As an application, we obtain the weak convergence of adjoint Bergman measures associated with bounded plurisubharmic weights toward the corresponding non-pluripolar measures



1.
Introduction

Given an ample line bundle
L
over a complex projective manifold
X
, endowed with the Kähler form
\omega
polarized by
L
, a central theme in Kähler geometry—first articulated by Yau
[
65
, p. 139]
—is the approximation of transcendental geometric objects on
(X,\omega)
by asymptotic algebraic data coming from the powers
L^{k}:=L^{\otimes k}
. This is the philosophy of
Kähler quantization
.

The initial proposal is to approximate the infinite-dimensional space

\mathcal{H}_{\omega}:=\{\,\phi\in C^{\infty}(X):\omega+dd^{c}\phi>0\,\}

of Kähler potentials by the finite-dimensional spaces
\mathcal{H}_{k}
of positive Hermitian forms on
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
. Via the Kodaira embedding, elements of
\mathcal{H}_{k}
correspond to Fubini–Study metrics and thus form a distinguished subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
. This was established in the seminal works of Tian
[
63
]
, Bouche
[
17
]
, Ruan
[
60
]
, Catlin
[
20
]
, Zelditch
[
66
]
, Lu
[
48
]
, among many others, using asymptotics of the Bergman kernel (cf. Theorem
4.6
).

Donaldson later proposed that not only the metrics but also the
geometry
of
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
should be captured by the finite-dimensional geometries of
\mathcal{H}_{k}

[
36
, p. 483]
. This principle has since been realized in various settings and has had deep impacts on problems concerning canonical metrics and stability; see, for example,
[
57
,
62
,
21
,
16
,
26
,
36
,
37
,
61
,
67
]
among a rapidly expanding body of literature. We refer to
[
49
]
for a detailed exposition of some classical developments in this direction.

A natural question is whether this quantization picture extends beyond the ample case. This has become increasingly relevant in view of recent progress on canonical metrics in degenerate or singular settings
[
6
,
46
,
47
,
30
,
32
,
64
,
53
,
51
,
52
]
, where the positivity of
L
only holds in a weak sense. The present note fits into this broader direction, establishing a quantization result for Monge–Ampère energy in big and semipositive setting.

Statement of Main Result

We now describe our results, referring to Section
2
and Section
3
for details. Let
L
be holomorphic line bundle over a complex projective manifold
X
of dimension
n
. We assume that
L
is big and semipositive. Let
h_{0}^{L}
be a smooth metric on
L
and let
\theta:=c_{1}(L,h_{0}^{L})
denote the Chern curvature of
h_{0}^{L}
so that
\theta
is a smooth semipositive
(1,1)
-form.

One of the difficulties in the degenerate setting is the lack of smooth potentials. Instead, we work with the set of
\theta
-plurisubharmonic functions on
X
, denoted by
\PSH(X,\theta)
. This parametrizes singular hermitian metrics on
L
whose curvature
\theta_{u}:=\theta+dd^{c}u
is positive in the current sense. Following the pioneering work of Boucksom et al.
[
18
]
, for
u\in\PSH(X,\theta)
, one can define the
non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure

\theta_{u}^{n}
, which is a Borel measure on
X
that puts no mass on pluripolar sets. Moreover, we denote by

E_{\theta}:\PSH(X,\theta)\to[-\infty,\infty)

the
Monge–Ampère energy
or
Aubin–Yau energy
. It is the primitive of the Monge–Ampère operator and plays an important role in the variational approach to canonical Kähler metrics.

On the other hand, let
K_{X}:=\Omega^{n,0}_{X}
be the canonical bundle on
X
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the adjoint line bundle
L^{k}\otimes K_{X}
. For global sections
s_{1},s_{2}\in H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X})
, locally written as

s_{j}=\sigma_{j}\,dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n},\quad j=1,2,

where
\sigma_{j}
are local holomorphic sections of
L^{k}
, we set

\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}:=\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}\left(\frac{i}{2}\right)^{n}dz_{1}\wedge d\bar{z}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}\wedge d\bar{z}_{n},

yielding a smooth volume form on
X
. For a function
u:X\to[-\infty,\infty)
, we define the
Hilbert norm

(1.1)

(s_{1},s_{2})_{Hilb_{k}(u)}:=\int_{X}\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

for sections
s_{1},s_{2}\in H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X})
such that (
1.1
) is finite. We say
u
is
admissible
if
Hilb_{k}(u)
is finite on the whole space
H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X})
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Clearly, any bounded function is admissible. For an admissible function
u
, we define the
quantized Monge–Ampère energy
by

(1.2)

E_{k}(u):=-\frac{1}{kN_{k}}\log\left(\frac{\det Hilb_{k}(u)}{\det Hilb_{k}(0)}\right),

which is first introduced by Donaldson
[
37
]
(see also Berman–Boucksom
[
3
]
).

Our main result is the quantization of Monge–Ampère energy in adjoint twisting.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
L
is a big and semipositive line bundle on
X
, then for any
u\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}E_{k}(u)=E_{\theta}(u).

A result such as Theorem
1.1
was raised as an open problem in Berman–Freixas i Montplet
[
7
, Remark 3.6]
, and our theorem provides a partial answer in the big and semipositive setting.
When
L
is ample and no adjoint twisting is present, Donaldson
[
37
]
first established the convergence for smooth Kähler potentials; this was later extended to continuous potentials by Berman–Boucksom
[
3
, Theorem A]
and to all finite-energy potentials by Darvas–Lu–Rubinstein
[
26
]
.
In the adjoint setting, Berman–Freixas i Montplet
[
7
, Theorem 3.5]
proved the quantization of energy for finite-energy potentials, and subsequently Darvas–Xia
[
28
]
obtained the result for arbitrary twisting.
In the big, untwisted case, Berman–Boucksom
[
3
, Theorem A]
showed convergence for all continuous metrics.
More recently, Darvas–Xia
[
29
]
proved a “partial quantization” theorem for continuous metrics under arbitrary twisting on a pseudoeffective line bundle.

Theorem
1.1
has an immediate application to quantization of non-pluripolar measure. For an admissible
u
, we fix a basis
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{N_{k}}\}
of
H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X})
with
N_{k}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X})
. Then we define
Bergman measure
and its normalization by

(1.3)

B_{L^{k},u}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k}}|S_{j}(x)|^{2}_{(h_{u}^{L})^{k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k}}|S_{j}(x)|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku(x)},\quad\beta_{L^{k},u}=\frac{1}{N_{k}}B_{L^{k},u}.

Coupling with a standard trick in Berman–Boucksom–Witt Nyström
[
2
]
, Theorem
1.1
yields the following weak convergence of normalized Bergman measures to non-pluripolar measures

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
u\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
. Then
\beta_{L^{k},u}
converges weakly to non-pluripolar measure
\frac{1}{\vol L}\theta_{u}^{n}
as
k\to\infty
, where
\vol L
is the volume of the line bundle
L
, see (
2.7
).

Our approach refines the arguments in
[
7
]
as follows. Fix an ample line bundle
A
on
X
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
. We prove a comparison Theorem for Bergman measure to
L
and to small ample twist
L\otimes A^{\varepsilon}
with almost optimal asymptotic bound (Theorem
4.1
). This allows us to apply Berman’s local Morse inequality
[
7
, Proposition 2.5]
to the twisted bundle with explicit dependence on
\varepsilon
, see (
4.9
). Combining this with the standard variational relations between the Monge–Ampère energy and non-pluripolar measures, the quantized energy and Bergman measures, and the convexity of the quantized Monge–Ampère energy along weak geodesics, we can carry out the proof of Theorem
1.1
for bounded potentials following standard argument as in
[
3
, Theorem A]
and
[
7
, Theorem 3.5]
. However, as we will explain in Remark
5.4
, our method does not presently extend to arbitrary finite-energy potentials, due to integrability issues for initial tangent vectors of weak geodesics.

As this work was being finalized, Siarhei Finski kindly informed us of his related work
[
40
]
, in which he quantizes the Darvas–Mabuchi
d_{p}
distances on big line bundles and for potentials of the type
P_{\theta}(f)
(defined below
2.4
), where
f
is continuous. In particular, for
p=1
and
\theta
semipositive, his result implies the version of Theorem
1.1
for such potentials, a subset of the class
\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}
. Our setting and approach differ from his in two main aspects. His quantization is formulated using the untwisted section spaces
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
, whereas we work in the adjoint setting. This allows us to apply the Berndtsson–Păun positivity theorem directly. Additionally, he reduces the problem to the ample case via passing to certain birational models over
X
, while our reduction is by twisting
L^{k}\otimes K_{X}
by an ample line bundle on
X
as explained above.

Organization

We briefly describe the structure of the paper.

In Section 2 we collect the necessary background from pluripotential theory, following
[
18
,
5
,
25
,
24
]
. We also recall the basic variational formulas for
E_{\theta}
.

Section 3 is devoted to Bergman measures and quantized Monge–Ampère energies in the adjoint setting. We prove basic properties of
B_{L^{k},u}
and

and discuss their variational formula. Next, we recall the positivity of direct images à la Berndtsson–Păun
[

,
13
,
11
,

]
and the relation with convexity of

along weak geodesics. Finally, we recall Berman’s local holomorphic Morse inequalities and the convergence of Bergman measures at equilibrium, adapting the arguments of
[

,
3
]
to our adjoint framework.

Section 4 is the new input in our argument. We prove the the comparison Theorem (Theorem
4.1
). This follows from choosing peak sections with asymptotically optimal
L^{\infty}
-control from a pointwise semi-classical Ohsawa–Takegoshi type extension theorem for powers of an ample line bundle (Theorem
4.2
). We also discuss the relation of Theorem
4.2
with recent work of Finski
[
38
, Theorem 1.10]
. We also present a local Morse inequality in ample twisting setting.

Finally, in Section 5 we combine these ingredients to prove Theorems
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
and conclude with obstacles to aribtrary finite-energy potentials in Remark
5.4
.

Recap on Pluripotential Theory

Let
(X^{n},\omega)
be a compact (connected) Kähler manifold of (complex) dimension
n
with Kähler form
\omega
. A function

is quasi-plurisubharmonic (or qpsh for short) if it can be locally written as a sum of plurisubharmonic (psh) functions and a smooth function. Given a closed, real
(1,1)
-form

\{\theta\}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})

\PSH(X,\theta):=\{u:X\to[-\infty,\infty):u:\text{qpsh},\quad\theta_{u}:=\theta+dd^{c}u\geq 0,\quad u\not\equiv-\infty\},

where
\theta_{u}\geq 0
in the sense of current. Here, we choose the normalization

d^{c}:=\frac{i}{4\pi}(\bar{\partial}-\partial)\quad\text{so that}\quad dd^{c}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}.

For a real
(1,1)

\theta\in\{\alpha\}

(i)

Kähler if there exists a Kähler form in
\alpha

(ii)

(1,1)
-form in
\alpha

(iii)

\psi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(X)

(iv)

(v)

If

Finite Energy Space and Weak Geodesics

Let
\theta
be a closed, smooth
(1,1)
-form so that
\{\theta\}
is big. If
u\in\PSH(X,\theta)
has minimal singularity type, then we define
Monge–Ampère energy
by

(2.8)

E_{\theta}(u):=\frac{1}{(n+1)\vol(\{\theta\})}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\int_{X}(u-V_{\theta})\theta_{u}^{j}\wedge\theta^{n-j}_{V_{\theta}}

which is monotonic, see
[
18
, §2.2]
. Following
[
18

(with different sign), we can extend
E_{\theta}
to any

by

E_{\theta}(u):=\inf\{E_{\theta}(v):v\in\PSH(X,\theta),\text{ minimal singular, }u\leq v\}

and define
finite energy space
by

(2.9)

\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta):=\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):E_{\theta}(u)>-\infty\}.

We summarize some properties on
E_{\theta}
and
\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)

Theorem 2.2

(
[

]

[

]

[
25
]
, Proposition 2.13)

If
\theta
is a closed, smooth
(1,1)
-form representing a big class, then we have

(i)

If

has full Monge–Ampère mass and

\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}=\vol(\{\theta\}),\quad\int_{X}|u|\theta_{u}^{n}<\infty.

(ii)

(iii)

is concave along affine curves. Particularly, for

\frac{1}{\vol(\{\theta\})}\int_{X}(u-v)\theta_{u}^{n}\leq E_{\theta}(u)-E_{\theta}(v)\leq\frac{1}{\vol(\{\theta\})}\int_{X}(u-v)\theta_{v}^{n}.

Bergman Measure and Quantized Monge–Ampère Energy

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional complex vector space of dimension
N
. We denote
\mathcal{H}_{V}
by the space of positive-definite hermitian inner products on
V
. For
H,H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{V}
, there exists an
H
-hermitian endomorphism
A\in\operatorname{End}(V)
such that

(3.1)

(s_{1},s_{2})_{H^{\prime}}=(e^{A}s_{1},s_{2})_{H},\quad s_{1},s_{2}\in V.

For a fixed
H_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{V}
, we define a partial order on the space of
H_{0}
-hermitian endomorphisms by:

A_{1}\geq A_{2},\quad\text{ if $A_{1}-A_{2}$ is $H_{0}$-positive semidefinite.}

Let
X
be a compact projective manifold of dimension
n
with a given Kähler form
\omega
,
L
be holomorphic line bundles over
X
,
K_{X}
be the canonical bundle on
X
. We assume that
L
is big and nef and fix a smooth hermitian metric
h_{0}^{L}
on
L
such that its Chern curvature
\theta:=c_{1}(L,h_{0}^{L})
. We consider the space of global sections

V_{k}:=H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X}),\quad N_{k}:=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X}),

and we denote
\mathcal{H}_{k}:=\mathcal{H}_{V_{k}}
. Recall that in the introduction, for a function
u:X\to[-\infty,\infty)
, we define Hilbert norm as in (
1.1
):

(s_{1},s_{2})_{Hilb_{k}(u)}:=\int_{X}\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku},

and we say
u
is admissible if
Hilb_{k}(u)
is finite on whole
V_{k}
. An immediate consequence of Theorem
2.3
(ii) gives the following affirmative answer to the question raised by Berman–Freixas i Montplet in
[
7
, Remark 2.2]
.

Corollary 3.1

(Admissibility of Finite Energy Potentials)
.

If
L
is a big and nef line bundle,
\theta=c_{1}(L,h_{0}^{L})
for some smooth hermitian metric
h_{0}^{L}
on
L
, then any
u\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)
is admissible. That is,
Hilb_{k}(u)
as defined in (
1.1
) is a well-defined hermitian inner product on
H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X})
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

For two hermitian inner products
H,H^{\prime}
on
V_{k}
, the quantized Monge–Ampère energy is given by

(3.2)

E_{k}(H,H^{\prime}):=-\frac{1}{kN_{k}}\log\left(\frac{\det H}{\det H^{\prime}}\right)=\frac{1}{kN_{k}}\mathrm{tr}A

where
A
is the endomorphism in (
3.1
). It satisfies cocycle condition:

(3.3)

E_{k}(H)-E_{k}(H^{\prime})=E_{k}(H;H^{\prime}).

We fix
H_{0}:=Hilb_{k}(0)
as our reference hermitian form and set
E_{k}(H):=E_{k}(H;H_{0})
.
We collect some easy consequences of the definition.

Lemma 3.2
.

For each fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
Hilb_{k}
and
E_{k}
satisfy:

(i)

Hilb_{k}(u+C)=e^{-kC}Hilb_{k}(u)
and
E_{k}(u+C)=E_{k}(u)+C
for any
C\in\mathbb{R}
and any admissible
u
;

(ii)

if
u_{1},u_{2}
are admissible and
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
, then
Hilb_{k}(u_{1})\geq Hilb_{k}(u_{2})
and
E_{k}(u_{1})\leq E_{k}(u_{2})
;

(iii)

if
(u_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a monotone sequence of admissible functions decreasing a.e. to an admissible
u
, then

\lim_{j\to\infty}E_{k}(u_{j})=E_{k}(u).

Proof.

For (i), the first statement follows directly from definition (
1.1
). By (
3.3
), we have

E_{k}(u+C)-E_{k}(u)=E_{k}(u+C;u)=\frac{-1}{kN_{k}}\log\frac{\det(e^{-k(u+C)}Hilb_{k}(u))}{\det(Hilb_{k}(u))}=C.

For (ii), we claim that
Hilb_{k}(u_{1})\geq Hilb_{k}(u_{2})
with respect to
H_{0}
if
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
. With respect to a fixed
H_{0}
-orthonormal basis
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{N_{k}}\}
of
V_{k}
, the endomrohpsim
A(u_{l})
from
H_{0}
to
Hilb(u_{l})
is given by

[A(u_{l})]_{ij}:=\log(S_{l},S_{j})_{Hilb_{k}(u_{l})}=\log\int_{X}\langle S_{i},S_{j}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku_{l}},\quad l=1,2.

Hence, we see that
A_{1}-A_{2}\geq 0
with respect to
H_{0}
since

((A_{1}-A_{2})S_{j},S_{j})_{H_{0}}=\log\frac{\int_{X}|S_{j}|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku_{1}}}{\int_{X}|S_{j}|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku_{2}}}\geq 0.

Particularly, this proves (ii) since
E_{k}(u_{2})-E_{k}(u_{1})=\frac{1}{kN_{k}}(\mathrm{tr}(A_{1})-\mathrm{tr}(A_{2}))\geq 0
. For (iii), if
u_{j}\downarrow u
a.e. on
X
, then
e^{-ku_{j}}\to e^{-ku}
a.e. on
X
. Since
u
is admissible, we can apply dominated convergence theorem to conclude that
Hilb_{k}(u_{j})\to Hilb_{k}(u)
with respect to a fixed
H_{0}
-orthonormal basis of
V_{k}
. The case
u_{j}\uparrow u
is similar.
∎

Recall that we define Bergman measure and normalized Bergman measure in (
1.3
). It is easy to see that (

Lemma 3.3
.

If
u\in L^{\infty}(X)
or
u\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)
, then we have the following extremal characterization:

(3.4)

B_{L^{k},u}(x)=\sup_{s\in V_{k}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|s(x)|^{2}_{(h_{u}^{L})^{k}}}{\|s\|^{2}_{Hilb_{k}(u)}}=\sup_{s\in V_{k}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|s(x)|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku(x)}}{\|s\|^{2}_{Hilb_{k}(u)}}\text{ as $(n,n)$-forms.}

Proof.

We choose a local holomorphic frame
\eta_{k}
for
L^{k}\otimes K_{X}
on an open set
U\subset X
and set

\Lambda:=\langle\eta_{k},\eta_{k}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku},

which is a measurable
(n,n)
-form on
U
. For an orthonormal basis
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{N_{k}}\}
of
V_{k}
and
s\in V_{k}
, we write
S_{j}=f_{j}\eta_{k}
and
s=f\eta_{k}
for some holomorphic functions
f_{j},f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
. Then

\langle S_{j},S_{j}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku}=|f_{j}|^{2}\Lambda,\quad\langle s,s\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku}=|f|^{2}\Lambda.

Now, if we write
s=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k}}a_{j}S_{j}
, then
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k}}|a_{j}|^{2}=\|s\|_{Hilb_{k}(u)}^{2}
. If
u\in L^{\infty}(X)
, then for any

,

\frac{|s(x)|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku(x)}}{(s,s)_{Hilb_{k}(u)}}=\frac{|\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k}}a_{j}f_{j}(x)|^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k}}|a_{j}|^{2}}\Lambda(x)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k}}|f_{j}(x)|^{2}\Lambda(x)=B_{L^{k},u}(x)

by Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the inequality holds if we choose
a_{j}=\overline{f_{j}(x)}
. If
u\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)
, then above argument holds for a.e.
x\in U
so that
\Lambda(x)<\infty
(this is true if
x\notin\{u=-\infty\}
which is a pluripolar set). Moreover, it is clear that both sides of (
3.4
) is independent of the choice of local holomorphic frame
\eta
. Hence, two
(n,n)
-form coincide (a.e.) on
X
.
∎

We also need the quantized version of
2.13
for quantized Monge–Ampère energy. The proof is inspired by
[

and

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u_{0},u_{1}
be
\theta
-psh functions with minimal singularity type,
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto u_{t}
be the weak geodesic joining them. Then

(3.5)

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0+}E_{k}(u_{t})=\int_{X}\dot{u}_{0}\beta_{L^{k},u_{0}}.

Proof.

We take an orthonormal basis
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{N_{k}}\}
of
V_{k}
with respect to
Hilb_{k}(u_{0})
. Then for
t>0
,
H_{k}(t):=Hilb_{k}(u_{t})
is given by the Gram matrix

H_{k}(t)_{ij}:=(S_{i},S_{j})_{Hilb_{k}(u_{t})}=\int_{X}\langle S_{i},S_{j}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku_{t}}.

First, we show that
E_{k}(u_{t})
is differentiable at
t=0+
. Now, by Lemma
2.4
(iii), there exists
C>0
such that
\sup_{X}|u_{t}-u_{0}|\leq C
for any
t\in[0,1]
. For each
i,j
,

\frac{H_{k}(t)_{ij}-H_{k}(0)_{ij}}{t}=\int_{X}\langle S_{i},S_{j}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku_{0}}\frac{e^{-k(u_{t}-u_{0})}-1}{t}

Now, we see that the integrant is dominated since
\langle S_{i},S_{j}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku_{0}}\in L^{1}(X)
and

\left|\frac{e^{-k(u_{t}-u_{0})}-1}{t}\right|\leq ke^{k|u_{t}-u_{0}|}\left|\frac{u_{t}-u_{0}}{t}\right|\leq kCe^{kC}.

Since
t\mapsto u_{t}
is Lipschitz,
|u_{t}(x)-u_{0}(x)|\leq tC
. Hence, by mean-value theorem,

\frac{e^{-k(u_{t}(x)-u_{0}(x))}-1}{t}=-ke^{-k\xi(t,x)}\frac{u_{t}(x)-u_{0}(x)}{t},

for some
\xi(t,x)
between

. Thus, as
|\xi(t,x)|\leq|u_{t}(x)-u_{0}(x)|\leq tC\to 0
as

,

\lim_{t\to 0+}\frac{e^{-k(u_{t}(x)-u_{0}(x))}-1}{t}=-k\dot{u}_{0}(x),

We can apply dominated convergence Theorem to get:

\lim_{t\to 0+}\frac{H_{k}(t)_{ij}-H_{k}(0)_{ij}}{t}=-k\int_{X}\dot{u}_{0}\langle S_{i},S_{j}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-u_{0}}.

Since
H_{k}(0)=I_{N_{k}}
, we can calculate

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\log\det(H(t))=\mathrm{tr}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0+}H_{k}(t)=-k\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k}}\int_{X}\dot{u}_{0}|S_{j}|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}e^{-ku_{0}}=-kN_{k}\int_{X}\dot{u}_{0}\beta_{L^{k},u_{0}}.

By (

,

, later generalized by Berndtsson–Păun
[

]
and Păun–Takayama
[

]
in the singular setting. We refer to the survey of Păun
[
55
,
56
]
and Hacon–Popa–Schnell
[
44
]
for more comprehensive overview of the direction.

We now consider the following set-up. Let
X
be a projective manifold,
T:=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}:1<|\tau|<e\}
be the closed annulus. We denote
\mathcal{X}:=X\times T
by the product manifold and
p:\mathcal{X}\to T
by the projection onto the second factor. Since
X
is projective,
p
is a projective morphism.

Suppose
F
is a pseudoeffective line bundle over
\mathcal{X}
with Chern curvature
\eta:=c_{1}(F,h_{0}^{F})
for some smooth reference metric
h_{0}^{F}
. For
u\in\PSH(X,\eta)
, we denote
h^{F}:=h_{0}^{F}e^{-u}
by the singular hermitian metric on
F
and
u_{\tau}:=u|_{X\times\{\tau\}}
. Clearly, the direct image

E:=p_{*}(K_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes F)

is a trivial bundle over
T
with fibers over
\tau\in T
given by

p_{*}(K_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes F)_{\tau}=H^{0}(X,K_{X}\otimes F|_{X\times\{\tau\}}).

For
\tau\in T
, we consider the following Hilbert norm on
H^{0}(X,K_{X}\times F|_{X\times\{\tau\}})
by:

(3.7)

g_{\mathcal{X}/T,\tau}(s,s^{\prime})=\int_{X}h_{0}^{F}|_{X\times\{\tau\}}(s,s^{\prime})e^{-u_{\tau}}\leq\infty,\quad s,s^{\prime}\in H^{0}(X,K_{X}\otimes F|_{X\times\{\tau\}}).

We record the Berndtsson–Păun theorem on positivity of direct images, specialized to our context.

Theorem 3.6

(
[
11
]
, Theorem 3.5)
.

In the set-up as above, the fiberwise
g_{\mathcal{X}/Y,y}
defines a singular hermitian metric
g_{\mathcal{X}/Y}
on
E
with positive curvature.

Here, we follow the definition of positively curved singular hermitian metric on a vector bundle given in
[
54
]
(see also
[
56
]
and
[
44
]
): let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a complex manifold
X
. A singular hermitian metric on
E
is a function
h
which associates each point
x\in X
with a possibly unbounded quadratic form
h_{x}:E_{x}\to[0,\infty]
such that

(i)

h_{x}
is a positive-definite quadratic form outside a measure zero set of
X
;

(ii)

for any local section
s\in H^{0}(U,E)
over some open set
U\subset X
,
h(s,s):U\to[0,\infty]
is a measurable function.

With this definition, it is easy to prove that (c.f.
[
44
, Lemma 18.2]
) if
h
is a singular hermitian metric on
E
, then it induces a singular hermitian metric
h^{*}
on
E^{*}
by

|f|_{h^{*}}=\sup\left\{\frac{|f(v)|}{|v|_{h}}:v\in E_{x},\quad|v|_{h}\neq 0\right\},\quad\forall f\in E_{x}^{*}.

Following
[
11
]
, we say a singular hermitian metric

on

is
Griffiths semipositive
if for any open set
U\subset X
and
f\in H^{0}(U,E^{*})
,
\log|f|_{h^{*}}\in\PSH(U)
. Then we have

Proposition 3.7

(
[
54
]
, Lemma 2.2.6, 2.3.3;
[
58
]
Proposition 1.3 (ii))
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank

. If
E
admits a Griffiths semipositive singular metric
h
in above sense, then
-\log\det h
is a positive singular hermitian metric on
\det E:=\Lambda^{r}E
.

Remark 3.8
.

If
h
is a smooth hermitian metric on

, then the above definition coincides with the usual definition of Griffiths semipositivity. The advantage of the above definition is that one can bypass curvature to define positivity of a vector bundle. In fact, an example of Raufi
[

shows that the curvature tensor does not make sense in general.

Now, let
L
be a big line bundle over a projective manifold

for each

. Based on the idea of

is a trivial vector bundle over
T
with fiber
(E_{k})_{\tau}=H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X})
for
\tau\in T
. (
3.7
) becomes

|s|^{2}_{g_{\mathcal{X}/Y,\tau}}=\int_{X}|s|_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}^{2}e^{-ku_{\tau}}=Hilb_{k}(u_{\tau}),\quad s\in(E_{k})_{\tau}\cong H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X}).

This implies a fact that is the key in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 3.9
.

For a finite energy geodesic
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto u_{t}\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)
,
t\mapsto E_{k}(u_{t})
is convex on
[0,1]
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.6
and Proposition
3.7
,
-\log\det(Hilb_{k}(u_{\tau}))
is a positive hermitian metric on the trivial line bundle
\det(E_{k})
on
T
. Hence,
-d_{\tau}d^{c}_{\tau}\log\det(Hilb_{k}(u_{\tau})\geq 0
. As
\log\det(Hilb_{k}(u_{\tau}))
is
S^{1}
-invariant, this shows that
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto E_{k}(u_{t})=-\frac{1}{kN_{k}}\log\det(Hilb_{k}(u_{t}))
is convex.
∎

The above corollary is also proved in
[
7
, Proposition 3.4 (ii)]
.

Local Morse Inequalities and Equilibrium Measures

In this subsection we recall Berman’s local Morse inequalities and the resulting convergence of Bergman measures. None of the results are new: they are due to Berman
[
8
]
and Berman–Boucksom
[
3
]
, and we only adapt the arguments to our adjoint setting
H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X})
. We include the proofs for completeness.

Theorem 3.10

(Local Morse inequalities, after Berman
[
8
]
)
.

Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
, endowed with a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}^{L}
with curvature
\theta:=c_{1}(L,h_{0}^{L})
. Fix a smooth Hermitian form
\omega
on
X
. Then for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}(X)
, writing

\theta_{\phi}:=\theta+dd^{c}\phi
and
B_{L^{k},\phi}
for the adjoint Bergman measure of
L^{k}\otimes K_{X}
with weight
\phi
, we have:

(i)

There exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
k
and
\phi
, such that

\frac{n!}{k^{n}}B_{L^{k},\phi}\;\leq\;C\,\omega^{n}\quad\text{on }X.

(ii)

Pointwise on
X
,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{n!}{k^{n}}B_{L^{k},\phi}(x)\;\leq\;\mathbb{1}_{X_{+}(\phi)}(x)\,\theta_{\phi}^{n}(x),

where
X_{+}(\phi):=\{x\in X:\theta_{\phi}(x)>0\}
.

Sketch of proof.

We follow the strategy of Berman
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
, indicating only the adjustments for the adjoint case.
Fix
x\in X
. Choose holomorphic coordinates and a local frame of
L
near
x
so that the weight of
h_{\phi}^{L}
has Taylor expansion

\Phi(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}|z_{j}|^{2}+O(|z|^{3}),

with
\lambda_{j}
the eigenvalues of
\theta_{\phi}(x)
with respect to
\omega(x)
.
A local section of
L^{k}\otimes K_{X}
can be written as

s=f(z)\,e_{L}^{\otimes k}\,dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n},

and its pointwise norm is
|s(z)|^{2}_{(h_{\phi}^{L})^{k}}=|f(z)|^{2}e^{-k\Phi(z)}
.

Restricting the Hilbert norm
\|\cdot\|_{Hilb_{k}(\phi)}
to a shrinking polydisc
D(0,\log/\sqrt{k})
and rescaling by
w=\sqrt{k}z
, one obtains the usual comparison

k\Phi\bigl(w/\sqrt{k}\bigr)=\Phi^{0}(w)+O\bigl(k^{-1/2}(\log k)^{3}\bigr),

uniformly on the polydisc of radius
\log k
.
Thus
e^{-k\Phi(w/\sqrt{k})}
is uniformly comparable, up to a multiplicative error
e^{\rho_{k}}
with
\rho_{k}\to 0
, to the Gaussian model
e^{-\Phi^{0}(w)}
.

Applying the plurisubharmonic submean inequality to
|f|^{2}
on tori of radii
r_{j}\in[0,\log k]
and integrating in polar coordinates, one arrives at the classical bound

(3.8)

\frac{n!\,|s|^{2}(x)}{k^{n}\|s\|^{2}_{Hilb_{k}(\phi)}}\leq e^{\rho_{k}}\frac{n!\,dV}{(2\pi)^{n}\prod_{j}\int_{0}^{\log k}e^{-\lambda_{j}r^{2}}r\,dr}.

Since
-C\omega\leq\theta_{\phi}\leq C\omega
by compactness of
X
, all eigenvalues satisfy
|\lambda_{j}|\leq C
.
A direct computation of the integrals in (
3.8
) then gives a uniform upper bound, and taking the supremum over all sections via the extremal characterization of the Bergman kernel yields (i).

For (ii), if some
\lambda_{j}(x)\leq 0
, then the denominator in (
3.8
) diverges and the right-hand side tends to
0
as
k\to\infty
.
If instead all
\lambda_{j}(x)>0
, then

\int_{0}^{\log k}e^{-\lambda_{j}r^{2}}r\,dr=\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_{j}(\log k)^{2}}}{2\lambda_{j}},

and rewriting the expression in (
3.8
) shows that the right-hand side converges to

\frac{n!}{\pi^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,dV=\theta_{\phi}^{n}(x).

Again using the extremal characterization of
B_{L^{k},\phi}
gives (ii).
∎

We now record the standard consequence of the local Morse inequalities.
Again, the proof is a direct adaptation of
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
; we only insert the adjoint notation.

Proposition 3.11

(Global domination and weak convergence)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.10
, and for any fixed smooth volume form
\nu
on
X
, we have

(3.9)

\frac{B_{L^{k},\phi}}{\nu}\;\leq\;e^{k(P_{\theta}(\phi)-\phi)}\,\sup_{X}\frac{B_{L^{k},\phi}}{\nu}\quad\text{on }X.

Moreover, if
L
is big, then

\beta_{L^{k},\phi}\;\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{w}\;\frac{1}{\vol(L)}\,\theta_{P_{\theta}(\phi)}^{n}.

Sketch of proof.

We follow the argument of
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
, indicating only the adjoint modifications.
In a local trivialization of
L^{k}\otimes K_{X}
with
h_{0}^{L}=e^{-\Phi^{0}}
, an

Hilb_{k}(\phi)
-orthonormal basis has the form

S_{j}=f_{j}e_{L}^{\otimes k}\otimes dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}
, and the Bergman
density relative to
\nu
is

b_{L^{k},\phi}=\frac{B_{L^{k},\phi}}{\nu}=e^{-k(\Phi^{0}+\phi)}\sum_{j}|f_{j}|^{2}.

Let
C_{k}:=\sup_{X}b_{L^{k},\phi}
and define

u_{k}:=\phi+\tfrac{1}{k}\log(b_{L^{k},\phi}/C_{k})
.
Locally,

u_{k}+\Phi^{0}=\tfrac{1}{k}\log(\sum|f_{j}|^{2}/C_{k})
is psh, hence
u_{k}\in\PSH(X,\theta)
,
and since
b_{L^{k},\phi}\leq C_{k}
, one has
u_{k}\leq\phi
.
By maximality of
P_{\theta}(\phi)
, this implies

u_{k}\leq P_{\theta}(\phi)
, which is equivalent to the global domination formula
(
3.9
).

For the convergence, let
D:=\{\phi=P_{\theta}(\phi)\}
.
From (
3.9
) and Theorem
3.10
(i),
one gets the estimate

b_{L^{k},\phi}\leq e^{-k\varepsilon}\frac{Ck^{n}}{n!}\quad\text{on }F_{\varepsilon}:=\{P_{\theta}(\phi)+\varepsilon\leq\phi\},

hence
\int_{F_{\varepsilon}}\beta_{L^{k},\phi}\to 0
.
Any weak limit
\beta
of
\beta_{L^{k},\phi}
is therefore supported on
D
.

For any nonnegative
\psi\in C^{0}(X)
, reverse Fatou’s lemma and
Theorem
3.10
(ii) give

\int_{X}\psi\,\beta\leq\frac{1}{\vol(L)}\int_{X}\psi\,\mathbf{1}_{X_{+}(\phi)}\theta_{\phi}^{n}.

By Proposition
2.1
and (
2.6
), we have
\theta_{P_{\theta}(\phi)}^{n}=\mathbf{1}_{D}\theta_{\phi}^{n}=\mathbf{1}_{X_{+}(\phi)}\theta_{\phi}^{n}
. This yields

\int_{X}\psi\,\beta\leq\int_{X}\psi\,\frac{1}{\vol(L)}\theta_{P_{\theta}(\phi)}^{n}.

Thus
\beta\leq\vol(L)^{-1}\theta_{P_{\theta}(\phi)}^{n}
, and since both are probability
measures, equality holds.
Hence the whole sequence
\beta_{L^{k},\phi}
converges weakly to

\vol(L)^{-1}\theta_{P_{\theta}(\phi)}^{n}
.
∎

Finally, we recall the well-known implication, proved in
[
3
, Theorem A]
.
The argument carries over verbatim to the adjoint setting (compare also
[
29
, Prop. 6.4]
).

Corollary 3.12
.

If
L
is big and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(X)
, then

\lim_{k\to\infty}E_{k}(\phi)=E_{\theta}\big(P_{\theta}(\phi)\big).

Proof.

Applying Lemma
3.5
to the
affine path
t\mapsto t\phi
, we obtain

E_{k}(\phi)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}E_{k}(t\phi)\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}\!\int_{X}\phi\,\beta_{L^{k},t\phi}\,dt.

By Proposition
3.11
, for each fixed
t\in[0,1]
,
\beta_{L^{k},t\phi}
converges weakly to
\frac{1}{\vol(L)}\theta_{P_{\theta}(t\phi)}^{n}
as
k\to\infty
. Hence

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\phi\,\beta_{L^{k},t\phi}=\frac{1}{\vol(L)}\int_{X}\phi\,\theta_{P_{\theta}(t\phi)}^{n}=\frac{d}{dt}E_{\theta}\!\big(P_{\theta}(t\phi)\big),

where we use Lemma
2.8
in the last identity. Since each
\beta_{L^{k},t\phi}
is a probability measure and

|\int_{X}\phi\,\beta_{L^{k},t\phi}|\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}

uniformly in
k,t
. The result follows from Dominated convergence.
∎

4.
Peak Section and Bergman Kernel Comparison

Let
A
be an ample line bundle over a complex projective manifold
X
with a given smooth hermitian metric
h_{0}^{A}
such that
\omega:=c_{1}(A,h_{0}^{A})
is a Kähler form. Fix
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
and consider sufficiently divisible
k\gg 0
such that
k\varepsilon\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote

V_{k,\varepsilon}:=H^{0}(X,(L\otimes A^{\varepsilon})^{k}\otimes K_{X}),\quad N_{k,\varepsilon}:=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(X,(L\otimes A^{\varepsilon})^{k}\otimes K_{X}).

We can extend (
1.1
) to
\varepsilon
-twisted case by: for
u\in L^{\infty}(X)
or
\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)
,

(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})_{Hilb_{k,\varepsilon}(u)}:=\int_{X}\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}\otimes(h^{A}_{0})^{k\varepsilon}}e^{-ku},\quad\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in V_{k,F}

Similarly, we can extend (
1.3
) to this case by:

(4.1)

B_{(L\otimes A^{\varepsilon})^{k},u}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k,\varepsilon}}|\sigma_{j}(x)|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}\otimes(h_{0}^{A})^{\varepsilon k}}e^{-ku(x)},

where
\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{N_{k,\varepsilon}}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
V_{k,\varepsilon}
with respect to
Hilb_{k,\varepsilon}(u)
. Similar to Lemma
3.3
,

(4.2)

B_{(L\otimes A^{\varepsilon})^{k},u}=\sup_{\sigma\in V_{k,\varepsilon}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|\sigma(x)|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}\otimes(h_{0}^{A})^{k\varepsilon}}e^{-ku(x)}}{(s,s)_{Hilb_{k,\varepsilon}(u)}}

The main result of this section is the following comparison theorem between Bergman kernels which is crucial in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
and admissible
u
, and for sufficiently divisible
k\gg 1
, we have

B_{L^{k},u}\leq\left(1+\frac{C}{\varepsilon k}\right)^{2}B_{(L\otimes A^{\varepsilon})^{k},u},

where
C=C(n,X,\omega)
depends only on dimension
n
, manifold
X
, and Kähler form
\omega=c_{1}(A,h_{0}^{A})
.

Theorem
4.1
relies on the following pointwise semi-classical Ohsawa–Takegoshi type result.

Theorem 4.2
.

There exists
C>0
and
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
p\geq p_{1}
, any
x\in X
, and any
v\in(A^{p})_{x}
, one can find a global holomorphic section
a=a_{p,x,v}\in H^{0}(X,A^{p})
with

(4.3)

a(x)=v,\quad|v|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}\leq\sup_{X}|a|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}\leq\left(1+\frac{C}{p}\right)|v|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}},

where
C=C(n,X,\omega)
depends only on dimension
n
, manifold
X
, and Kähler form
\omega=c_{1}(A,h_{0}^{A})
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By Theorem
4.2
, there exists
C_{0}>0
and
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
x\in X
, any
k\geq p_{0}/\varepsilon_{0}
, there exists a section
a=a_{\varepsilon k,x}\in H^{0}(X,\varepsilon kA)
such that

|a(x)|_{(h^{A}_{0})^{\varepsilon k}}=1,\quad\sup_{X}|a|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{\varepsilon k}}\leq 1+\frac{C}{\varepsilon k}.

Now, for any
s\in V_{k}:=H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes K_{X})
, we define
t:=s\otimes a\in V_{k,\varepsilon}=H^{0}(X,(L\otimes A^{\varepsilon})^{k}+K_{X})
. Then

(t,t)_{Hilb_{k,\varepsilon}(u)}=\int_{X}|t|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}\otimes(h_{0}^{A})^{k\varepsilon}}e^{-ku}=\int_{X}|s|_{(h_{0}^{L})^{k}}|a|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{\varepsilon k}}^{2}e^{-ku}\leq\left(\sup_{X}|a|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{\varepsilon k}}\right)^{2}(s,s)_{Hilb_{k}(u)}.

Hence, if
(s,s)_{Hilb_{k}(u)}\leq 1
, then
(t,t)_{Hilb_{k,\varepsilon}(u)}\leq\left(1+\frac{C}{\varepsilon k}\right)^{2}
. On the other hand, for a.e.
x\in X
, we have

\displaystyle|s|_{(h_{u}^{L})^{k}}^{2}(x)=|s|_{(h_{u}^{L})^{k}}^{2}(x)|a|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{\varepsilon k}}^{2}(x)=|t|_{(h_{u}^{L})^{k}\otimes(h_{0}^{A})^{k\varepsilon}}^{2}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{C}{\varepsilon k}\right)^{2}\sup_{\sigma\in V_{k,\varepsilon}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|\sigma(x)|^{2}_{(h_{u}^{L})^{k}\otimes(h_{0}^{A})^{\varepsilon k}}e^{-ku(x)}}{(\sigma,\sigma)_{Hilb_{k,\varepsilon}(u)}}=\left(1+\frac{C}{\varepsilon k}\right)^{2}B_{(L\otimes A^{\varepsilon})^{k},u}(x),

where the last identity follows from (
4.2
).
Then by (
3.4
), we obtain the result.
∎

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
The statement is likely familiar to experts: it should follow from Hörmander’s
L^{2}
-estimates in the
spirit of Tian’s construction of peaked sections
[
63
]
, although we have not been able to locate an
explicit reference. A stronger assertion appears in Ruan
[
60
, Theorem 7.2]
, which states that for all
sufficiently large
p
and any
x\in X
, there exists
a_{p,x}\in H^{0}(X,A^{p})
such that

\sup_{X}|a_{p,x}|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}=|a_{p,x}(x)|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}.

For completeness, we include a proof relying only on the Catlin–Zelditch on-diagonal asymptotics
for the Bergman kernel. Theorem
4.2
also follows from the far-diagonal
exponential decay of Ma–Marinescu
[
50
]
, combined with the near-diagonal asymptotics of
Dai–Liu–Ma
[
23
, Theorem 4.1.8]
. Recently, Finski
[
38
,
39
]
has proved a powerful
semi-classical Ohsawa–Takegoshi theorem in much greater generality; see Remark
4.5
for the
relation to Theorem
4.2
.

Before proceeding to prove Theorem
4.2
, we recall the standard Bergman kernel set-up. For each
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the
L^{2}
-inner product on
H^{0}(X,pA)
by

(4.4)

(s,t)_{p}:=\int_{X}\langle s,t\rangle_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

We denote
d_{p}:=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(X,pA)
. Given an orthonormal basis
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{d_{k}}\}
of
H^{0}(X,pA)
with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{p}
above, we define full Bergman kernel by

(4.5)

K_{p}(y,x):=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k}}S_{j}(y)\otimes S_{j}^{*}(x)\in A^{p}_{y}\otimes(A^{p}_{x})^{*},

where
S_{j}^{*}(x)
is the dual vector of
S_{j}(x)
acting on
v\in A^{p}_{x}
by
S_{j}^{*}(x)\cdot v=\langle S_{j}(x),v\rangle_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}
. It is clear that
K_{p}(y,x)
is independent of the choice of orthonormal basis and
K_{p}(x,y)
is the Schwartz kernel of the orthogonal projection
\Pi_{p}:L^{2}(X,A^{p})\to H^{0}(X,A^{p})
, i.e.,

\Pi_{p}s(y):=\int_{X}K_{p}(y,x)\cdot s(x)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}(x),\quad\forall s\in L^{2}(X,A^{p}).

The Bergman kernel (or Bergman density) by the restriction on the diagonal:

(4.6)

K_{p}(x):=K_{p}(x,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}|S_{j}(x)|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}.

For any
x,y\in X
, we define pointwise operator norm of
K_{p}(y,x):(A^{p}_{x})\to(A^{p}_{y})
as:

|K_{p}(y,x)|_{op}=\sup_{v\in(A^{p})_{x}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|K_{p}(y,x)\cdot v|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}}{|v|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}}.

Then we have the following elementary Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for Bergman kernel.

Lemma 4.3
.

|K_{p}(y,x)|_{op}\leq K_{p}(x)^{1/2}K_{p}(y)^{1/2}
.

Proof.

For a fixed orthonormal basis
\{S_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d_{p}}
of
H^{0}(X,A^{p})
with respect to (
4.4
). Then for
v\in(A^{p})_{x}
,

K_{p}(y,x)\cdot v=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}\langle v,S_{j}(x)\rangle_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}S_{j}(y).

Let
c_{j}=\langle v,S_{j}(x)\rangle_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}
. Since
(A^{p}_{x})\cong\mathbb{C}
, we can find a generator
e_{x}
for
(A^{p}_{x})
with
|e_{x}|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}=1
so that
S_{j}(x)=\beta_{j}e_{x}
and
v=\nu e_{x}
. Then

\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}|c_{j}|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}|\nu\overline{\beta_{j}}|^{2}=|\nu|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{k}}|\beta_{j}|^{2}=|v|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{k}}|S_{j}(x)|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}=|v|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}^{2}K_{p}(x).

On the other hand, by triangle inequality and usual Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

\left|\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}c_{j}S_{j}(y)\right|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}|c_{j}S_{j}(y)|_{(h_{0}^{k})^{p}}\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}|c_{j}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}|S_{j}|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq K_{p}(x)^{1/2}K_{p}(y)^{1/2}.

Taking the supremum over all
v
, we obtain the required inequality.
∎

We recall the following fundamental asymptotic expansion of Bergman kernel due to Tian
[
63
]
, Bouche
[
17
]
Ruan
[
60
]
, Catlin
[
20
]
, Zelditch
[
66
]
, and Lu
[
48
]
:

Theorem 4.4
.

There exists
b_{j}\in C^{\infty}(X)
such that for
l,r\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C=C(l,r,X,\omega)>0
satisfying

(4.7)

\left\|K_{p}(x)-\sum_{j=0}^{l}p^{n-j}b_{j}(x)\right\|_{C^{r}(X)}\leq Cp^{n-(l+1)},\quad\forall p\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover,
b_{0}=1
and
b_{1}=S_{\omega}
, the scalar curvature, and
b_{j}
are derivatives of curvatures.

With this, we are now ready to prove Theorem
4.2
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

First, since
A
is ample, there exists
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that if
p\geq p_{0}
,
A^{p}
is basepoint-free, i.e., for any
x\in X
, there exists
a^{\prime}\in H^{0}(X,A^{p})
so that
a^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
. For
x\in X
, consider

W_{x}:=H^{0}(X,pL\otimes\mathscr{I}_{x})=\{a\in H^{0}(X,pL):a(x)=0\},

where
\mathscr{I}_{x}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
is the ideal sheaf of
\{x\}
. For
p\geq p_{0}
,
W_{x}
is a codimension
1
subspace of
H^{0}(X,A^{p})
and thus we can find
peak section

P_{p,x}\in H^{0}(X,A^{p})
(c.f.
[
49
, Definition 5.1.7]
) so that
\|P_{p,x}\|_{k}^{2}=1
and
P_{p,x}\perp W_{x}
. Since (
4.5
) is independent of the choice of orthonormal bases, we can choose an orthonormal basis
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{d_{k}}\}
with
S_{1}=P_{p,x}
. In this case, (
4.5
) is given by:

K_{p}(y,x)=P_{p,x}(y)\otimes P_{p,x}(x)^{*},\quad K_{p}(x)=|P_{p,x}(x)|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}.

Moreover, since
P_{p,x}(x)\neq 0
, it is a holomorphic local frame of
A^{p}
near
x
and thus

v=\frac{\langle v,P_{p,x}(x)\rangle_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}}{|P_{p,x}(x)|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}}P_{p,x}(x)=\frac{K_{p}(x,x)\cdot v}{K_{p}(x)}

Hence, we define the extended section by

(4.8)

a_{p,x,v}(y)=\frac{K_{p}(y,x)\cdot v}{K_{p}(x)}=\frac{\langle v,P_{p,x}(x)\rangle}{K_{p}(x)}P_{p,x}(y)\in H^{0}(X,A^{p}).

Then for any
y\in X
, by Lemma
4.3
,

|a_{p,x,v}(y)|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}=\frac{|K_{p}(y,x)\cdot v|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}}{K_{p}(x)}\leq\frac{|K_{p}(y,x)|_{op}|v|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}}{K_{p}(x)}\leq\left(\frac{K_{p}(y)}{K_{p}(x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}|v|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}.

Hence, taking supremum over
y\in X
, we reduce to estimate
\left(\frac{\sup_{X}K_{p}}{K_{p}(x)}\right)^{1/2}
. Now, by Theorem
4.4
for
r=0
and
l=1
, for any
y\in X
, there exists
C>0
such that

K_{p}(y)=p^{n}\left(1+\frac{S_{\omega}}{p}+R_{p}(y)\right),\quad|R_{p}(y)|\leq\frac{C}{p^{2}}.

Then the ratio can be estimate by

\frac{\sup_{X}K_{p}}{K_{p}(x)}\leq\frac{1+\sup_{X}S_{\omega}/p+C/p^{2}}{1+S_{\omega}(x)/p-C/p^{2}}=1+\frac{\sup_{X}S_{\omega}-\inf_{X}S_{\omega}(x)}{p}+O(p^{-2}).

Taking the square roots, we see that

\left(\frac{\sup_{X}K_{p}}{K_{p}(x)}\right)^{1/2}\leq 1+\frac{\sup_{X}S_{\omega}-\inf_{X}S_{\omega}}{2p}+O(1/p^{2}).

Hence, for sufficiently large
p\geq p_{1}\geq p_{0}
, we get
|a_{p,x,v}(y)|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}\leq(1+C/p)|v|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

We now briefly explain the relation between Theorem
4.2
and the work of
Finski
[
38
]
.
The extended section
a_{p,x,v}
constructed in (
4.8
) is in fact the

L^{2}
-minimal holomorphic extension of
v
.
Indeed, any
\widetilde{a}\in H^{0}(X,A^{p})
with
\widetilde{a}(x)=v
decomposes uniquely as

\widetilde{a}=\alpha P_{p,x}+a^{\prime}
with
a^{\prime}\in W_{x}
, and

\alpha=\frac{\langle v,P_{p,x}(x)\rangle}{|P_{p,x}(x)|^{2}_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}}=\frac{\langle v,P_{p,x}(x)\rangle}{K_{p}(x)}.

Thus
\widetilde{a}=a_{p,x,v}+a^{\prime}
, and orthogonality gives

\|\widetilde{a}\|_{p}^{2}=\|a_{p,x,v}\|_{p}^{2}+\|a^{\prime}\|_{p}^{2}\geq\|a_{p,x,v}\|_{p}^{2}
.

In
[
38
]
, for a complex submanifold
\iota:Y\hookrightarrow X
, Finski introduces the

optimal extension operator

E_{p}^{Y}:L^{2}(Y,\iota^{*}A^{p})\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,A^{p}),

which sends a datum on
Y
to its
L^{2}
-minimal extension.
Using precise asymptotics of its Schwarz kernel,
[
38
, Theorem 1.10]
shows that for some

C>0
and all
p\gg 1
,

\sup_{X}|E_{p}^{Y}s|_{(h_{0}^{A})^{p}}\leq\Bigl(1+\frac{C}{\sqrt{p}}\Bigr)\sup_{Y}|s|_{(\iota^{*}h_{0}^{A})^{p}},\qquad s\in H^{0}(Y,\iota^{*}A^{p}).

In the special case
Y=\{x\}
, the preceding discussion identifies the kernel explicitly as

E_{p}^{x}(y,x)=\frac{K_{p}(y,x)}{K_{p}(x)}

(see also
[
41
, §2.5]
).
This yields another proof of Theorem
4.2
, with the refined bound

1+C/\sqrt{p}
.

We will also need an
\varepsilon
-twisted analogue of the local Morse inequality. Fix a positive line bundle
(A,h_{0}^{A})
with curvature
\omega:=c_{1}(A,h_{0}^{A})
and let
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
. For sufficiently divisible
k
, we denote
B_{k,\varepsilon}:=B_{(L\otimes A^{\varepsilon})^{k},0}
by the
\varepsilon
-twisted Bergman measure defined in (
4.1
).

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth semi-positively curved metric
h_{0}^{L}
, i.e.,
\theta:=c_{1}(L,h_{0}^{L})\geq 0
. Then we have the following upper bound estimates on
X
:

(4.9)

\frac{n!}{k^{n}}B_{k,\varepsilon}\leq\frac{e^{\rho_{k}+\varepsilon\widetilde{\rho}_{k}}}{(1-e^{-\varepsilon(\log k)^{2}})^{n}}(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)^{n},

where
\rho_{k},\widetilde{\rho}_{k}\to 0
uniformly on
X
as
k\to\infty

Proof.

We follow the proof of Theorem
3.10
. Fix
x\in X
and choose coordinates and local frames
e_{L},e_{A}
as before so that

|e_{L}|_{h_{0}^{L}}^{2}=e^{-\Phi_{L}},\qquad|e_{A}|_{h_{0}^{A}}^{2}=e^{-\Phi_{A}},\qquad\omega(x)=\frac{i}{2}\sum dz_{j}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j},

and the weight of
(L\otimes A^{\varepsilon})^{k}
is given by
e^{-k\Phi_{\varepsilon}}
, where
\Phi_{\varepsilon}:=\Phi_{L}+\varepsilon\Phi_{A}
has Taylor expansion

\Phi_{\varepsilon}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon)|z_{j}|^{2}+O(|z|^{3}),

where
\lambda_{j}
are the eigenvalues of
\theta(x)
relative to
\omega(x)
.
Thus the quadratic model is given by

\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{0}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon)|z_{j}|^{2},\qquad\text{and hence }\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon>0.

Rescaling
w=\sqrt{k}z
on the standard shrinking polydisc gives the uniform approximation

k\Phi_{\varepsilon}(w/\sqrt{k})=\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{0}(w)+\rho_{k}+\varepsilon\widetilde{\rho}_{k},\qquad\rho_{k},\widetilde{\rho}_{k}\to 0.

Repeating the submean-value argument used for Theorem
3.10
then yields

\frac{n!|s|^{2}(x)}{k^{n}\|s\|^{2}}\leq e^{\rho_{k}+\varepsilon\widetilde{\rho}_{k}}\frac{n!dV_{n}}{(2\pi)^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\log k}e^{-(\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon)r^{2}}r\,dr}.

Since the quadratic model satisfies
\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon>0
, the Gaussian integrals in the denominator behave exactly as in the proof of Theorem
3.10
, with
\lambda_{j}
replaced by
\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon
.
Using the identity

(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)^{n}=\frac{n!}{\pi^{n}}\!\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon)\right)dV_{n},

the same computation as in the untwisted case yields

\frac{n!|s|^{2}(x)}{k^{n}\|s\|^{2}}\;\leq\;e^{\rho_{k}+\varepsilon\widetilde{\rho}_{k}}\Biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-e^{-(\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon)(\log k)^{2}}}\Biggr)(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)^{n}(x)\leq{(1-e^{-\varepsilon(\log k)^{2}})^{n}}(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)^{n}(x),

since
\lambda_{j}(x)+\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon
for all
j
and
x\in X
. (
4.9
) then follows from extremal characterization (
4.2
).
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2

Recall that
X
is a compact projective manifold of dimension
n
,
L
be a big and semipositive line bundle over
X
with fixed smooth metric
h_{0}^{L}
on
L
with Chern curvature form
\theta:=c_{1}(L,h^{L}_{0})\geq 0
. Let us first deduce Corollary
1.2
from Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

For
f\in C^{0}(X)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
u+tf
is bounded and thus is admissible. We set

f_{k}(t):=E_{k}(u+tf),\quad g(t):=E_{\theta}(P_{\theta}(u+tf)).

Notice that
u+tf\geq P_{\theta}(u+tf)\geq m:=\inf_{X}(u+tf)>-\infty
by definition of envelope and thus,
P_{\theta}(u+tf)\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}
. By Theorem
1.1
, we obtain that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(t)\geq\lim E_{k}(P_{\theta}(u+tf))=g(t),\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(0)=\lim_{k\to\infty}E_{k}(u)=E_{\theta}(u)=g(0).

By Lemma
3.5
,
f_{k}(t)
is concave and is differentiable at
t=0
:

f_{k}^{\prime}(0)=\int_{X}f\beta_{L^{k},u}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.8
,
g(t)
is differentiable at
t=0
and

g^{\prime}(0)=\int_{X}f\frac{\theta_{u}^{n}}{\vol(\{\theta\})}.

The result follows from the elementary lemma proved in
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

In fact, the arguments in Theorem
5.2
below shows that
\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(t)=g(t)
.

We now begin with the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
which holds for any finite-energy potentials.

Theorem 5.2
.

For
u\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)
,
\limsup_{k\to\infty}E_{k}(u)\leq E_{\theta}(u)
.

Proof.

The proof goes exactly as in
[
7
, Theorem 3.5]
and
[
3
, Theorem A]
. Since
u
is upper semi-continuous, we can find a sequence
\phi_{j}\in C^{\infty}(X)
such that
\phi_{j}\downarrow u
. Clearly,
\phi_{j}
is admissible and
E_{k}(u)\leq E_{k}(\phi_{j})
by Lemma
3.2
(ii). By Corollary
3.12
,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}E_{k}(u)\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}E_{k}(\phi_{j})=E_{\theta}(P_{\theta}(\phi_{j})),\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}.

On the other hand, by definition of envelope,
u\leq P_{\theta}(\phi_{j})\leq\phi_{j}
and thus
P_{\theta}(\phi_{j})\downarrow u
. Then

\lim_{k\to\infty}E_{k}(u)\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}E_{\theta}(P_{\theta}(\phi_{j}))=E_{\theta}(u),

by continuity of Monge–Ampère energy along decreasing sequences.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The proof actually shows that Theorem
5.2
holds for any big and nef line bundle
L
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

It remains to prove that for any
u\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}
, we have

(5.1)

\liminf_{{k\to\infty}}E_{k}(u)\geq E_{\theta}(u).

Following
[
7
]
, we consider the following functional on
\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)
:

\mathcal{F}_{k}(u):=E_{k}(u)-E_{\theta}(u),\quad u\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta).

By Lemma
2.2
(iii) and Lemma
3.2
(ii),
\mathcal{F}_{k}
is continuous along decreasing sequences in
\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)
. Also, by definition (
2.8
) and Lemma
3.2
(iii),
\mathcal{F}_{k}(u+C)=\mathcal{F}_{k}(u)
for
C\in\mathbb{R}
. For fixed
u\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}
, we may assume that
u\leq 0
by addition of a constant. By Lemma
2.4
(iii),
\dot{u}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(X)
.

By Corollary
3.9
, which is a consequence of Berndtsson–Păun positivity of direct image,
E_{k}(u_{t})
is convex. Also, Theorem
2.6
shows that
t\mapsto E_{\theta}(u_{t})
is affine. Thus,
t\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{k}(u_{t})
is convex and

\mathcal{F}_{k}(u)\geq\lim_{t\to 0+}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{k}(u_{t})}{t-0}=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0+}E_{k}(u_{t})-\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}E_{\theta}(u_{t})\geq\int_{X}\dot{u}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{N_{k}}B_{L^{k},0}-\frac{1}{\vol L}\theta^{n}\right),

where we make use of Lemma
2.13
and Lemma
3.4
in the last inequality. Notice that
\dot{u}_{0}
is integrable with respect to
\frac{1}{N_{k}}B_{L^{k},0}
since it is bounded.

Now, for fixed
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
and an positive line bundle
(A,h_{0}^{A})
with curvature
\omega
, we set
\omega_{\varepsilon}:=\theta+\varepsilon\omega
. By Theorem
4.1
and (
4.9
),

\frac{1}{N_{k}}B_{L^{k},0}\leq N_{k}^{-1}\left(1+\frac{C}{\varepsilon k}\right)^{2}B_{k,\varepsilon}\leq\frac{k^{n}/n!}{N_{k}}\frac{e^{\rho_{k}+\varepsilon\widetilde{\rho}_{k}}}{(1-e^{-\varepsilon(\log k)^{2}})^{n}}\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}

As
\dot{u}_{0}\leq 0
, we obtain

(5.2)

\mathcal{F}_{k}(u)\geq\int_{X}\dot{u}_{0}\left(\left(1+\frac{C}{\varepsilon k}\right)^{2}\frac{k^{n}/n!}{N_{k}}\frac{e^{\rho_{k}+\varepsilon\widetilde{\rho}_{k}}}{(1-e^{-\varepsilon(\log k)^{2}})^{n}}\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}-\frac{1}{\vol L}\theta^{n}\right).

Taking
k\to\infty
, we see that for fixed
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
, since
\rho_{k},\widetilde{\rho}_{k}\to 0
and from (
2.7
) (since
L
is big):

1+\frac{C}{\varepsilon k}\to 1,\quad\frac{k^{n}/n!}{N_{k}}\to\frac{1}{\vol L},\quad\frac{e^{\rho_{k}+\varepsilon\widetilde{\rho}_{k}}}{(1-e^{-\varepsilon(\log k)^{2}})^{n}}\to 1.

As a result, we obtain for fixed
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(u)\geq\frac{1}{\vol L}\int_{X}\dot{u}_{0}\left(\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}-\theta^{n}\right)=\frac{1}{\vol L}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{X}\dot{u}_{0}\theta^{n-j}\wedge(\varepsilon\omega)^{j}.

Taking
\varepsilon\to 0+
, we obtain the result.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The argument proving (
5.1
) fails for general

u\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)
, since one cannot in general guarantee that the
initial tangent
\dot{u}_{0}
of the weak geodesic
t\mapsto u_{t}
in

\PSH(X,\theta)
is integrable with respect to
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}
in
(
5.2
), even for arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
. One obstruction comes from a counterexample of Di Nezza

[
33
, Example 4.5]
, which shows that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)\not\subset\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\omega_{\varepsilon}),

Indeed, let
u\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)\setminus\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\omega_{\varepsilon})
with
u\leq 0
. From

\PSH(X,\theta)\subset\PSH(X,\omega_{\varepsilon})
and envelope definition of the weak geodesic, the weak geodesic

v_{t}
joining
0
and
u
in
\PSH(X,\omega_{\varepsilon})
satisfies

v_{t}\leq u_{t},\qquad t\in[0,1],

by the envelope construction. Since
u\notin\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\omega_{\varepsilon})
, one can shows that

\int_{X}\dot{v}_{0}\,\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}=-\infty.

As
u_{t}
and
v_{t}
agree at the endpoints,
we get
\dot{u}_{0}\leq\dot{v}_{0}\leq 0
. This implies that
\dot{u}_{0}\notin L^{1}(X,\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n})
since

\int_{X}\dot{u}_{0}\,\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}\;\leq\;\int_{X}\dot{v}_{0}\,\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}\;=\;-\infty,

Thus, a necessary condition for the integrability of
\dot{u}_{0}
is that

u\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\omega_{\varepsilon})
. To author’s current knowledge, we do not aware of any general sufficient condition.

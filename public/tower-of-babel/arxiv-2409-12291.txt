The operator of relative complementation
Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
By the operator of relative complementation is meant a mapping assigning to every element x x of an interval [ a , b ] [a,b] of a lattice 𝐋 \mathbf{L} the set x a ​ b x^{ab} of all relative complements of x x in [ a , b ] [a,b] . Of course, if 𝐋 \mathbf{L} is relatively complemented then x a ​ b x^{ab} is non-empty for each interval [ a , b ] [a,b] and every element x x belonging to it. We study the question under what condition a complement of x x in 𝐋 \mathbf{L} induces a relative complement of x x in [ a , b ] [a,b] It is well-known that this is the case provided 𝐋 \mathbf{L} is modular and complemented. However, we present a more general result. Further, we investigate properties of the operator of relative complementation, in particular in the case when the interval [ a , b ] [a,b] is a modular sublattice of 𝐋 \mathbf{L} or if it is finite. Moreover, we characterize when the operator of relative complementation is involutive and we show a class of lattices where this identity holds. Finally, we establish sufficient conditions under which two different complements of a given element x x of [ a , b ] [a,b] induce the same relative complement of x x in this interval.

Proposition 2.2

\displaystyle(u\vee x)\wedge z

\displaystyle=z\wedge\big((u\vee x)\wedge y\big)=z\wedge v=x,

\displaystyle(u\wedge y)\vee z

\displaystyle=z\vee\big((u\wedge y)\vee x\big)=z\vee v=y,

\displaystyle=z\vee\big((u\wedge y)\vee x\big)=(u\wedge y)\vee z=y,

\displaystyle z\wedge v

\displaystyle=z\wedge\big((u\vee x)\wedge y\big)=(u\vee x)\wedge z=x,

Properties of the operator
ab

Properties of the operator of relative complementation are in certain sense similar to those of the operator of complementation already investigated in by the authors in
[
3
]
. Hence, also the proofs of the following results are written in a similar way, in fact in some cases we only rename the operators. However, for the reader’s convenience, the full proofs are presented.

Throughout this section let
(L,\vee,\wedge)
be a lattice and
a,b\in L
with
a<b
and assume
[a,b]
to be complemented.

The pair
(^{ab},^{ab})
is the Galois connection between
(2^{[a,b]},\subseteq)
and
(2^{[a,b]},\subseteq)
induced by the relation

\{(x,y)\in[a,b]^{2}\mid x\vee y=b\text{ and }x\wedge y=a\}.

From this we conclude

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\subseteq(A^{ab})^{ab},

\displaystyle A\subseteq B

\displaystyle\Rightarrow B^{ab}\subseteq A^{ab},

\displaystyle\big((A^{ab})^{ab}\big)^{ab}

\displaystyle=A^{ab},

\displaystyle A\subseteq B^{ab}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow B\subseteq A^{ab}

for all
A,B\subseteq[a,b]
. Since
A\subseteq(A^{ab})^{ab}
we have that
(A^{ab})^{ab}\neq\emptyset
whenever
\emptyset\neq A\subseteq[a,b]
. A
subset

A
of
[a,b]
is called
closed
if
(A^{ab})^{ab}=A
. Let
\Cl([a,b])
denote the set of all closed subsets of
[a,b]
. Then clearly
\Cl([a,b])=\{A^{ab}\mid A\subseteq[a,b]\}
. If
a<b
then because of
A^{ab}\cap(A^{ab})^{ab}=\emptyset
for all

we have that
\big(\Cl([a,b]),\subseteq,{}^{ab},\emptyset,[a,b]\big)
forms a complete ortholattice with

\displaystyle\bigvee_{i\in I}A_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i}\right)^{ab}\right)^{ab},

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{i\in I}A_{i}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}

for all families
(A_{i};i\in I)
of closed subsets of
[a,b]
.

Next we describe the basic properties of the operator
ab
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
c\in[a,b]
. Then the following holds:

(i)

c\in(c^{ab})^{ab}
and
\big((c^{ab})^{ab}\big)^{ab}=c^{ab}
,

(ii)

(x^{ab},\leq)
is an antichain for every
x\in[a,b]
if and only if
[a,b]
does not contain a sublattice isomorphic to
\mathbf{N}_{5}
containing
a
and
b
,

(iii)

(c^{ab},\leq)
is convex,

(iv)

if the mapping
x\mapsto(x^{ab})^{ab}
from
[a,b]
to
2^{[a,b]}
is not injective then the identity
(x^{ab})^{ab}\approx x
is not satisfied in the interval
[a,b]
.

Proof.

(i)

follows directly from above.

(ii)

First assume there exists some
d\in[a,b]
such that
(d^{ab},\leq)
is not an antichain. Then
d\notin\{a,b\}
. Now there exist
e,f\in d^{ab}
with
e<f
. Since

and

we have

. Because of

we have

. Hence the elements

,
d
,
e
,

and

are pairwise distinct and form an

containing

and

. If, conversely,

contains a sublattice

isomorphic to

and containing

and

and hence

is not an antichain.

(iii)

If
d,e\in c^{ab}
,

and

then

and

.

(iv)

If the mapping

is not injective then there exist

and

does not satisfy the identity

.

∎

Let

and the partial order relation on

is illuminated in the following result.

Theorem 3.4
.

Consider the following statements:

(i)

x^{ab}\vee y^{ab}\leq_{1}(x\wedge y)^{ab}
for all
x,y\in[a,b]
,

(ii)

for all
x,y\in[a,b]
,
x\leq y

,

(iii)

(x\vee y)^{ab}\leq_{1}x^{ab}\wedge y^{ab}
for all
x,y\in[a,b]
,

(iv)

x^{ab}\vee y^{ab}\subseteq(x\wedge y)^{ab}
for all
x,y\in[a,b]
,

(v)

x^{ab}\wedge y^{ab}\subseteq(x\vee y)^{ab}
for all
x,y\in[a,b]
,

(vi)

(x\vee y)^{ab}=_{1}x^{ab}\wedge y^{ab}
for all

Then
(i)

(ii)
,
(ii)

(iv)
and

Proof.

Let

.

(i) implies (ii):

c\leq d
implies

.

(ii) implies (iii):

Because of

we have
(c\vee d)^{ab}\leq_{1}c^{ab},d^{ab}
which implies
(c\vee d)^{ab}\leq_{1}c^{ab}\wedge d^{ab}

(iii) implies (ii):

c\leq d

(iv) implies (i) – (iii):

We have (i) and hence also (ii) and (iii) are satisfied.

(iv) and (v) together imply (vi):

We have seen that (iv) implies (iii). Now

4
Modular relatively complemented lattices

Our next task is to characterize the property that
[a,b]
satisfies the identity
(x^{ab})^{ab}\approx x
.

As mentioned in Example
2.4
and in the previous section, this task has a deeper sense only if the interval
[a,b]
in question is assumed to be a modular sublattice of
\mathbf{L}
.

Again, we can modify our results from
[
3
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge)
be a lattice,
a,b\in L
with
a\leq b
and assume
[a,b]
to be a modular sublattice of
\mathbf{L}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

[a,b]
satisfies the identity
(x^{ab})^{ab}\approx x
,

(ii)

for every
x\in[a,b]
and each
y\in(x^{ab})^{ab}
there exists some
z\in y^{ab}
satisfying either
(x\vee y)\wedge z=a
or
(x\wedge y)\vee z=b
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii):

If
c\in[a,b]
,
d\in(c^{ab})^{ab}
and
e\in d^{ab}
then
d=c
and
(c\vee c)\wedge e=d\wedge e=0
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i):

Suppose
[a,b]
not to satisfy the identity
(x^{ab})^{ab}\approx x
. Then there exists some
c\in[a,b]
with
(c^{ab})^{ab}\neq c
. Let
\in(c^{ab})^{ab}\setminus\{c\}
. According to (ii) there exists some

satisfying either
(c\vee d)\wedge e=a
or
(c\wedge d)\vee e=b
. Since
c
and

are different elements of
(c^{ab})^{ab}
and
\big((c^{ab})^{ab},\leq\big)
is an antichain according to Corollary
3.2
, we conclude

. Now
(c\vee d)\wedge e=a
would imply

c\parallel d

again contradicting

. This shows that
[a,b]
satisfies the identity
(x^{ab})^{ab}\approx x
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathbf{L}
be the direct product of a Boolean algebra and an arbitrary number of lattices

(ii)

Proof.

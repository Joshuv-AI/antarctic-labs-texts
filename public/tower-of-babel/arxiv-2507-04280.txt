Periapsis shift in the Zipoy-Voorhees spacetime Preprint: RUP-25-15
Akihito Katsumata, Tomohiro Harada

Abstract
We study the periapsis shift of timelike bound orbits in the Zipoy-Voorhees spacetime, which is an exact, static, axisymmetric, and vacuum solution characterized by the deformation parameter γ \gamma , including the Schwarzschild spacetime as γ = 1 \gamma=1 . We derive both the exact formula for the periapsis shift of a quasi-circular orbit and the formula for the periapsis shift by the post-Newtonian (PN) expansion. We show that the periapsis shift in the Zipoy-Voorhees spacetime for 1 / 11 < γ ≤ 1 / 5 1/11<\gamma\leq 1/5 is the same as in the Schwarzschild spacetime to the 2PN order if e = ( γ − 1 − 5 ) / 6 e=\sqrt{(\gamma^{-1}-5)/6} , where e e is the eccentricity of the orbit. Furthermore, we show that the parameter γ \gamma and the corresponding quadrupole moment M ~ 2 \tilde{M}_{2} of the supermassive compact object at Sagittarius A* are constrained to γ ≳ 1.7 × 10 − 2 \gamma\gtrsim 1.7\times 10^{-2} and M ~ 2 ≲ 1.2 × 10 3 \tilde{M}_{2}\lesssim 1.2\times 10^{3} , respectively, from observational data on S2 using the obtained PN expansion formula. Finally, we derive a new series representation for the periapsis shift in the Zipoy-Voorhees spacetime using a recently proposed prescription, which shows fast convergence not only in the weak-field regime but also for small eccentricity.

One of the important relativistic effects in orbital motions is the periapsis shift, a phenomenon in which the major axis of the orbit rotates and the periapsis is shifted. The most famous example of this phenomenon is Einstein’s successful explanation of Mercury’s perihelion shift

Recently, a formula for the periapsis shift in the ZV spacetime has been derived in the approximation that the eccentricity of the orbit,

Chakrabarty:2022fbd

Motivated by the above, in this paper, we first derive an exact formula for the periapsis shift of a quasi-circular orbit in the ZV spacetime by another simple method. Then, we derive the PN expansion formula for the periapsis shift in the ZV spacetime with arbitrary eccentricity and compare it with that in the Schwarzschild spacetime. We also give constraints on the deformation parameter
\gamma

For the analysis of the periapsis shift in the S2-Sgr A* system, the PN expansion formula for arbitrary eccentricity is sufficiently accurate. However, a star orbiting closer to Sgr A* than S2 has been reported recently

Therefore, in the last part of this paper, we derive a new series representation for the periapsis shift in the ZV spacetime using a recently proposed prescription

The obtained formula allows us to easily and very accurately calculate the periapsis shift in strong gravitational fields where the convergence of the PN expansion formula is not guaranteed, and will also be useful for verifying numerical calculations.

II
Geodesic motion of massive test particles

In this section, we consider the geodesic motion of massive test particles in the ZV spacetime, including the circular orbits, as a preliminary for calculating the periapsis shift.

II.1
Motion of massive test particles

The ZV spacetime, also known as the
\gamma
-metric,
\delta
-metric, and
q
-metric, is an exact, static, axisymmetric, asymptotically flat, and vacuum solution of Einstein’s field equations. In the Erez-Rosen coordinates
Erez:1959
, the line element is given by

\displaystyle\differential{s}^{2}=-f^{\gamma}\differential{t}^{2}+f^{\gamma(\gamma-1)}g^{1-\gamma^{2}}\left(f^{-1}\differential{r}^{2}+r^{2}\differential{\theta}^{2}\right)+f^{1-\gamma}r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\differential{\phi}^{2},

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle f=1-\frac{2M}{\gamma r},\quad g=1-\frac{2M}{\gamma r}+\frac{M^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\gamma^{2}r^{2}}.

(2.2)

The parameter
M
denotes the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner (ADM) mass
Arnowitt:1960es
. The non-dimensional parameter
\gamma
is often called the deformation parameter, which is related to the mass multipole moments and characterizes the deformation of the source from spherical symmetry. Now, we can assume
\gamma>0
without loss of generality (see Appendix
D
). As for
\gamma>1
(
0<\gamma<1
), the spacetime is oblate (prolate). The spacetime is spherically symmetric for
\gamma=1
, and it recovers the Schwarzschild spacetime
Sch1916
. Note that the spacetime also recovers the CC spacetime
Chazy:1924
;
Curzon:1925
in spherical coordinates in the limit of
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
. A brief review of the ZV spacetime and a calculation of multipole moments in the spacetime are given in Appendices
D
and
E
, respectively.

Let us consider the geodesic motion of a test particle with mass
\mu
on the equatorial plane (i.e.,
\theta=\pi/2
). The Lagrangian of the particle is written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\mu\left(-f^{\gamma}\dot{t}^{2}+f^{\gamma^{2}-\gamma-1}g^{1-\gamma^{2}}\dot{r}^{2}+f^{1-\gamma}r^{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}\right),

(2.3)

where the dot denotes the derivative with respect to the proper time of the particle. Due to the spacetime symmetry, the energy and the angular momentum of the particle

\displaystyle E:=-\partialderivative{\mathcal{L}}{\dot{t}},\quad L:=\partialderivative{\mathcal{L}}{\dot{\phi}},

(2.4)

are conserved. Evaluating these derivatives from Eq. (
2.3
) and solving the two equations for
\dot{t}
and
\dot{\phi}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{t}=\frac{\tilde{E}}{f^{\gamma}},\quad\dot{\phi}=\frac{\tilde{L}}{r^{2}f^{1-\gamma}},

(2.5)

where
\tilde{E}:=E/\mu
and
\tilde{L}:=L/\mu
. Substituting Eq. (
2.5
) into the normalization condition
g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}=-1
, we obtain after some simplification

\displaystyle\dot{r}^{2}=\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)^{1-\gamma^{2}}\left[\tilde{E}^{2}-f^{\gamma}\left(1+f^{\gamma-1}\frac{\tilde{L}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\right].

(2.6)

IV.1

VI
New series representation for the periapsis shift

In the future, with improvements in observational technology, we may observe the periapsis shift of stars in strong gravitational fields, where the PN expansion formula is not sufficiently accurate. Motivated by this, in this section, we derive a new series representation for the periapsis shift in the ZV spacetime using the recently proposed prescription
Katsumata:2024qzv

VI.1
Derivation

The following derivation follows the prescription proposed in
Katsumata:2024qzv
. First, expanding
F(\chi)
(
4.9
) in powers of the eccentricity
e
, we obtain

\displaystyle F(\chi)=\sqrt{\frac{\left[1-M/(\gamma p)\right]^{2(1-\gamma^{2})}\left[1-2M/(\gamma p)\right]^{\gamma^{2}}}{1-2(3+1/\gamma)M/p+2(1+1/\gamma)(2+1/\gamma)M^{2}/p^{2}}}+\mathcal{O}(e).

(6.1)

Factoring out the zeroth order term in this expression, Eq. (
4.8
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\delta\phi=2\sqrt{\frac{\left[1-M/(\gamma p)\right]^{2(1-\gamma^{2})}\left[1-2M/(\gamma p)\right]^{\gamma^{2}}}{1-2(3+1/\gamma)M/p+2(1+1/\gamma)(2+1/\gamma)M^{2}/p^{2}}}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}G(\chi)\differential{\chi},

(6.2)

where

\displaystyle G(\chi):=\sqrt{\frac{1-2(3+1/\gamma)M/p+2(1+1/\gamma)(2+1/\gamma)M^{2}/p^{2}}{\left[1-M/(\gamma p)\right]^{2(1-\gamma^{2})}\left[1-2M/(\gamma p)\right]^{\gamma^{2}}}}F(\chi).

(6.3)

Then, expanding the integrand
G(\chi)
in powers of
e
, we can obtain

\displaystyle G(\chi)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Y_{n}(\chi)\varepsilon^{n}\quad\text{(}N=1,2,3,\ldots\ \ \text{and}\ \ n=0,1,2,\ldots\text{)},

(6.4)

where we have defined the new expansion parameter as

\displaystyle\varepsilon:=\frac{eM/p}{\left[1-M/(\gamma p)\right]\left[1-2M/(\gamma p)\right]\left[1-2(3+1/\gamma)M/p+2\left(1+1/\gamma\right)\left(2+1/\gamma\right)(M/p)^{2}\right]}.

(6.5)

Note that this expansion parameter
\varepsilon
recovers that in the CC case (see Eq. (3.28) in
Katsumata:2024qzv
) in the limit of
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
. The concrete forms of the coefficients
Y_{n}(\chi)
are omitted here for their length and presented in Appendix
C
. Then, from Eqs. (
6.2
) and (
6.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\delta\phi=2\sqrt{\frac{\left[1-M/(\gamma p)\right]^{2(1-\gamma^{2})}\left[1-2M/(\gamma p)\right]^{\gamma^{2}}}{1-2(3+1/\gamma)M/p+2(1+1/\gamma)(2+1/\gamma)M^{2}/p^{2}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\varepsilon^{n}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}Y_{n}(\chi)\differential{\chi}.

(6.6)

Finally, let us evaluate the integral in Eq. (
6.6
) and obtain a new series representation for the periapsis shift. We can confirm that
Y_{n}
with odd
n
up to at least the seventh order (i.e.,
Y_{1}
,
Y_{3}
,
Y_{5}
, and
Y_{7}
) are odd functions in terms of
\chi
, and therefore these coefficients do not contribute to the integral. Then, Eq. (
6.6

\displaystyle\delta\phi=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\left[1-M/(\gamma p)\right]^{2(1-\gamma^{2})}\left[1-2M/(\gamma p)\right]^{\gamma^{2}}}{1-2(3+1/\gamma)M/p+2(1+1/\gamma)(2+1/\gamma)M^{2}/p^{2}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Z_{2n}\varepsilon^{2n},

(6.7)

where

\displaystyle Z_{0}

\displaystyle:=1,

(6.8)

\displaystyle Z_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{12}\Bigl[-9(3-4/\gamma)+6(41-19/\gamma-35/\gamma^{2}-2/\gamma^{3})M/p

\displaystyle\quad-3(163+218/\gamma-290/\gamma^{2}-194/\gamma^{3}-20/\gamma^{4})(M/p)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+6\left(74+206/\gamma+37/\gamma^{2}-276/\gamma^{3}-153/\gamma^{4}-20/\gamma^{5}\right)(M/p)^{3}

\displaystyle\quad-2(1+1/\gamma)(86+292/\gamma+90/\gamma^{2}-345/\gamma^{3}-299/\gamma^{4}-58/\gamma^{5})(M/p)^{4}

\displaystyle\quad-8(1+1/\gamma)(2+1/\gamma)(3-14/\gamma+6/\gamma^{2}+6/\gamma^{3}+6/\gamma^{4}+5/\gamma^{5})(M/p)^{5}

\displaystyle\quad+4(1+1/\gamma)^{2}(2+1/\gamma)^{2}(3-6/\gamma+8/\gamma^{2}-3/\gamma^{3}-1/\gamma^{4})(M/p)^{6}\Bigr],

(6.9)

and so forth. The concrete forms of the higher order coefficients,
Z_{4},Z_{6},\ldots
, are omitted here because of their length. In principle, it is also possible to obtain any higher order coefficients.

Then, we finally obtain a new series representation for the periapsis shift per round in the ZV spacetime as

\displaystyle\Delta\phi_{\text{ZV}}(N)=2\pi\left[\sqrt{\frac{\left[1-M/(\gamma p)\right]^{2(1-\gamma^{2})}\left[1-2M/(\gamma p)\right]^{\gamma^{2}}}{1-2(3+1/\gamma)M/p+2(1+1/\gamma)(2+1/\gamma)M^{2}/p^{2}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Z_{2n}\varepsilon^{2n}-1\right].

VII
Summary and conclusions

In this paper, we have discussed the periapsis shift in the ZV spacetime. We have derived the exact formula for the periapsis shift of a quasi-circular orbit (
3.5
) by considering the radial frequency and the orbital angular velocity from the equation of motion, and also obtained the PN expansion formula for arbitrary eccentricity (
4.10
). The contribution of the deformation parameter
\gamma
appears from the 2PN order term. The obtained PN expansion formula recovers those in the Schwarzschild spacetime for
\gamma=1
and the CC spacetime for
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
. We have investigated the deviation of the obtained PN expansion formula from that in the Schwarzschild spacetime. The deviation
\delta_{\text{ZV-S}}
(
4.14
) is the quadratic function of
1/\gamma
and therefore has the maximum value. We have found the non-trivial value of
\gamma
for which the deviation vanishes. This means that the periapsis shift looks as if it is in the Schwarzschild spacetime, even if it is in the ZV spacetime.

Furthermore, we have discussed the constraining of the deformation parameter
\gamma
and the corresponding quadrupole moment
\tilde{M}_{2}
of Sgr A* using the obtained PN expansion formula and the observational data on the periapsis shift of S2
GRAVITY:2020gka
. We have found that
f_{\text{SP}}
has the maximum value
f_{\text{SP,max}}\simeq 1.00147
for
\gamma\simeq 0.18699
(
\tilde{M}_{2}\simeq 9.1997
). Therefore, if future observations find
f_{\text{SP}}\gtrsim 1.00147
within error, the possibility that the spacetime around Sgr A* is the ZV spacetime can be excluded. For example, if the best fit value remains at
f_{\text{SP}}\simeq 1.1
and the error in the observation becomes less than half of the current value, the exclusion can be achieved. Furthermore, from the observational constraint
f_{\text{SP}}\gtrsim 0.91
, we have obtained the lower bound of
\gamma
as
\gamma\gtrsim 1.7\times 10^{-2}
, which is interpreted as
\tilde{M}_{2}\lesssim 1.2\times 10^{3}
. We should note that, although the present analysis considers the ZV spacetime (static and vacuum), the effects of the rotation of the source or matter distribution should also contribute to the periapsis shift. Therefore, for a more realistic analysis, the discussion should be conducted using stationary spacetimes such as the Tomimatsu-Sato or the
\delta
-Kerr spacetimes. It would also be interesting to consider the effects of matter distributions or to remove the assumption of being on the equatorial plane. These are our future works.

Finally, we have derived the new series representation for the periapsis shift in the ZV spacetime using the recently proposed prescription
Katsumata:2024qzv
. According to the prescription, the expansion parameter
\varepsilon
is defined as the eccentricity divided by the non-dimensional quantity that vanishes in the ISCO limit. This means that the parameter
\varepsilon
denotes both the eccentricity of the orbit and its proximity to the ISCO. We have examined the behaviors of the expansion parameter
\varepsilon
and the relative error of the truncated series representation. The parameter
\varepsilon
decreases as
e
is decreased. At
e\rightarrow 0
, although the PN expansion parameter approaches a nonzero value
M/d
,
\varepsilon
approaches zero. It can also be seen that the larger
d/M
and
\gamma
also lead to the smaller
\varepsilon
. The relative error of the truncated new formula also has a similar dependence to that of
\varepsilon
: the relative error of the truncated new formula approaches zero in the case of
e\rightarrow 0
, while the error of the truncated PN expansion formula approaches a non-zero value. The larger
d/M
also leads to a smaller error of the truncated new formula. Also, for
\gamma\lesssim 1
, the error decreases as
\gamma
increases.

Qualitatively, the smaller the eccentricity, the higher the accuracy of the truncated new formula. If the eccentricity is sufficiently small, the truncated new formula has higher accuracy than the truncated PN expansion formula, even in strong gravitational fields where the convergence of the PN expansion formula is not guaranteed. In addition, even if the orbit is highly eccentric, the truncated new formula has comparable or higher accuracy than the truncated PN expansion formula if the semi-major axis is sufficiently large. The simple and highly accurate formula obtained by truncating the new series representation is practical and useful, for example, for discussing the periapsis shifts in strong gravitational fields and for verifying numerical calculations. Further applications of the new series expansion for the periapsis shift to other spacetimes are to be investigated.

Appendix D
Brief review of the Zipoy-Voorhees spacetime

In this appendix, we briefly review the ZV spacetime. Axisymmetric, static, and vacuum solutions of Einstein’s field equations can be described by the Weyl metric
Weyl:1917
;
Weyl:2012
(see also
Griffiths:2009dfa
). Using cylindrical coordinates
(t,\rho,z,\phi)
with
-\infty<t<\infty
,
\rho\geq 0
,
-\infty<z<\infty
, and
0\leq\phi<2\pi
, the line element is written as

\displaystyle\differential{s}^{2}=-\mathrm{e}^{2\Phi}\differential{t}^{2}+\mathrm{e}^{2(\Lambda-\Phi)}(\differential{\rho}^{2}+\differential{z}^{2})+\rho^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2\Phi}\differential{\phi}^{2},

(D.1)

where the metric functions
\Phi
and
\Lambda
depend on
\rho
and
z
. The vacuum Einstein’s field equations imply

\displaystyle\partialderivative[2]{\Phi}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\partialderivative{\Phi}{\rho}+\partialderivative[2]{\Phi}{z}=0,\quad\partialderivative{\Lambda}{\rho}-\rho\left[\left(\partialderivative{\Phi}{\rho}\right)^{2}-\left(\partialderivative{\Phi}{z}\right)^{2}\right]=0,\quad\partialderivative{\Lambda}{z}-2\rho\partialderivative{\Phi}{\rho}\partialderivative{\Phi}{z}=0.

(D.2)

The first equation can be recognized as the simple three-dimensional Laplace equation in cylindrical coordinates. Therefore,
\Phi
can be regarded as the analogy of the Newtonian potential. Once we find a solution for
\Phi
, we can also find a solution for
\Lambda
by integrating the second and third equations. Note that the Minkowski spacetime is obviously obtained when
\Phi=0
and
\Lambda=0
.

The asymptotically flat solutions of the first equation for
\Phi
can be expressed by multipole expansions (see e.g.
Griffiths:2009dfa
):

\displaystyle\Phi=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{(\rho^{2}+z^{2})^{(n+1)/2}}P_{n}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}}\right),

(D.3)

where
P_{n}
are Legendre polynomials. It is noteworthy that the coefficients
a_{n}
in Eq. (
D.3
) do not directly correspond to the actual mass multipole moments of a physical source. A calculation of the relativistic multipole moments
M_{n}
is given in Appendix
E
. The expression for
\Lambda
corresponding to the solution (
D.3
) is given as (e.g.
Griffiths:2009dfa
)

\displaystyle\Lambda=-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{i}a_{j}\frac{(i+1)(j+1)}{i+j+2}\frac{P_{i}P_{j}-P_{i+1}P_{j+1}}{(\rho^{2}+z^{2})^{(i+j+2)/2}}.

(D.4)

The simplest asymptotically flat Weyl solution is generated by the spherically symmetric Newtonian potential of the point mass with mass
M
:

\displaystyle\Phi=-\frac{M}{R},\quad\Lambda=-\frac{M^{2}\rho^{2}}{2R^{4}},\quad R:=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}.

(D.5)

This is the CC solution
Chazy:1924
;
Curzon:1925
, which corresponds to putting the coefficients
a_{n}
as
a_{0}=M
and
a_{n}=0
(for
n\geq 1
). Note that although the Newtonian potential that generates this spacetime is spherically symmetric, the spacetime itself is not spherically symmetric.

The ZV solution
Bach:1922
;
Darmois:1927
;
Zipoy:1966btu
;
Voorhees:1970ywo
is generated by a Newtonian potential sourced by a rod with the length
2l
and the mass
M
, located at
\rho=0
and
-l<z<l
. The corresponding potential is given by

\displaystyle\Phi=\sigma\log\left(\frac{R_{-}+z-l}{R_{+}+z+l}\right),\quad R_{\pm}:=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+(z\pm l)^{2}},

(D.6)

where we have introduced the mass linear density
\sigma=M/(2l)
. Using the parameters
M
and
l
, the solution for the metric (
D.1
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{2\Phi}=\left(\frac{R_{+}+R_{-}-2l}{R_{+}+R_{-}+2l}\right)^{\gamma},\quad\mathrm{e}^{2\Lambda}=\left[\frac{(R_{+}+R_{-})^{2}-4l^{2}}{4R_{+}R_{-}}\right]^{\gamma^{2}},

(D.7)

where we have introduced a non-dimensional parameter
\gamma
, known as the deformation parameter:

\displaystyle\gamma:=\frac{M}{l}=2\sigma.

(D.8)

Note that the Minkowski spacetime is recovered when
\gamma\rightarrow 0
(i.e.
M\rightarrow 0
or
l\rightarrow\infty
). It is also noteworthy that the solution (
D.7
) is invariant under the transformation
l\rightarrow-l
. By virtue of this, we can assume
l>0
(therefore
\gamma>0
) without loss of generality. As we will see in Appendix
E
, this parameter
\gamma
is related to the mass quadrupole and characterizes the deformation of the source from spherical symmetry. As for
\gamma>1
(
0<\gamma<1
), the spacetime is oblate (prolate). Note that
\gamma
is often denoted as
\delta
, and then the ZV spacetime is called the
\delta
-metric in some literature. The coefficients
a_{n}
corresponding to the ZV solution are given by
a_{n}=0
(for odd
n
) and
a_{n}=Ml^{n}/(n+1)
(for even
n
). When the mass linear density of the rod is
\sigma=1/2
(i.e.
\gamma=1
), Eq. (
D.7
) recovers the Schwarzschild solution. It also recovers the CC solution (
D.5
) in the limit
l\rightarrow 0
(i.e.
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
).

Finally, we introduce the metric of the ZV spacetime written in other coordinates. In terms of prolate spheroidal coordinates (
x
,
y
) such that

\displaystyle\rho=l\sqrt{(x^{2}-1)(1-y^{2})},\quad z=lxy,

(D.9)

the metric functions of the ZV spacetime (
D.7
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{2\Phi}=\left(\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right)^{\gamma},\quad\mathrm{e}^{2\Lambda}=\left(\frac{x^{2}-1}{x^{2}-y^{2}}\right)^{\gamma^{2}},

(D.10)

and the metric (
D.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\differential{s}^{2}=-\mathrm{e}^{2\Phi}\differential{t}^{2}+\Sigma^{2}\left(\frac{\differential{x}^{2}}{x^{2}-1}+\frac{\differential{y}^{2}}{1-y^{2}}\right)+R^{2}\differential{\phi}^{2},

(D.11)

where

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}

\displaystyle=l^{2}(x+1)^{\gamma(\gamma+1)}(x-1)^{\gamma(\gamma-1)}(x^{2}-y^{2})^{1-\gamma^{2}},

(D.12)

\displaystyle R^{2}

\displaystyle=l^{2}(x+1)^{1+\gamma}(x-1)^{1-\gamma}(1-y^{2}).

(D.13)

Furthermore, by introducing the Erez-Rosen coordinates (
r
,
\theta
)
Erez:1959

\displaystyle x=\frac{r}{l}-1,\quad y=\cos\theta,

(D.14)

the metric of the ZV spacetime can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\differential{s}^{2}=-f^{\gamma}\differential{t}^{2}+f^{\gamma(\gamma-1)}g^{1-\gamma^{2}}\left(f^{-1}\differential{r}^{2}+r^{2}\differential{\theta}^{2}\right)+f^{1-\gamma}r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\differential{\phi}^{2},

(D.15)

where

\displaystyle f=1-\frac{2M}{\gamma r},\quad g=1-\frac{2M}{\gamma r}+\frac{M^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\gamma^{2}r^{2}}.

(D.16)

The ZV metric written as in Eqs. (
D.15
) and (
D.16
) is often called the
\gamma
-metric or the
\delta
-metric. Notice that it is clear that this metric recovers the Schwarzschild one for
\gamma=1
. We can also confirm that the metric (
D.15
) recovers the CC one in the spherical coordinates when
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
:

\displaystyle\differential{s}^{2}=-\mathrm{e}^{2\Phi}\differential{t}^{2}+\mathrm{e}^{2(\Lambda-\Phi)}\left(\differential{r}^{2}+r^{2}\differential{\theta}^{2}\right)+\mathrm{e}^{-2\Phi}r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\differential{\phi}^{2},

(D.17)

where

\displaystyle\Phi=-\frac{M}{r},\quad\Lambda=-\frac{M^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{2r^{2}}.

(D.18)

Note that this form of the CC solution can also be obtained by rewriting the CC solution written in Eqs. (
D.1
) and (
D.5
) using variable transformation
\rho=r\sin\theta
and
z=\rho\cos\theta
. In addition, focusing the deviation from
\gamma=1
and introducing the parameter
q
as
\gamma=1+q
, Eq. (
D.15
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\differential{s}^{2}=-f^{1+q}\differential{t}^{2}+f^{-q}\left[\left(1+\frac{M^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\gamma^{2}r^{2}}f^{-1}\right)^{-q(2+q)}\left(f^{-1}\differential{r}^{2}+r^{2}\differential{\theta}^{2}\right)+r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\differential{\phi}^{2}\right].

(D.19)

This form of the ZV metric is usually called the
q
-metric. Although the solution (
D.7
) is now generally called the ZV metric,
\gamma
-metric,
q
-metric, and
\delta
-metric, it has been initially discovered by Bach and Weyl
Bach:1922
, and Darmois
Darmois:1927
. Therefore, to be more precise, we might call this spacetime the Bach-Weyl-Darmois-Zipoy-Voorhees (BWDZV) spacetime.

Appendix E
Multipole moments in the Zipoy-Voorhees spacetime

The relativistic and coordinate-invariant definitions of multipole moments in general relativity have been proposed by Geroch
Geroch:1970cd
, Hansen
Hansen:1974zz
, Thorne
Thorne:1980ru
, and Beig and Simon
Beig:1981
. Algorithms to calculate the multipole moments have been developed, for example, by Fodor, Hoenselaers, and Perjés
Fodor:1989
. In this appendix, we present the calculation of the multipole moments in the ZV spacetime using a method proposed by Hoenselaers
Hoenselaers:1986gcr
and summarized more practically by Quevedo
Quevedo:1989rfm
.

Let us start by considering the Ernst potentials
Ernst:1967by
,
E
and
\xi
, in the ZV spacetime:

\displaystyle E=\mathrm{e}^{2\Phi}=\left(\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right)^{\gamma},\quad\xi=\frac{1-E}{1+E},

(E.1)

where we have used the prolate spheroidal coordinates. Introducing the inverse Weyl coordinate
\tilde{z}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{z}=\frac{1}{z}=\frac{1}{lxy},

(E.2)

the Ernst potentials are rewritten as

\displaystyle E(\tilde{z},y)=\left(\frac{1-ly\tilde{z}}{1+ly\tilde{z}}\right)^{\gamma},\quad\xi(\tilde{z},y)=\frac{1-E(\tilde{z},y)}{1+E(\tilde{z},y)}.

(E.3)

Hoenselaers showed that, particularly in the static case, the mass multipole moments
M_{n}
can be calculated by

\displaystyle M_{n}=m_{n}+d_{n}\quad(n=0,1,2,\ldots),

(E.4)

where the first term
m_{n}
can be obtained

\displaystyle m_{n}=\left.-\frac{h_{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\right|_{y=1,\tilde{z}=0},

(E.5)

with the simple recurrence formula for
h_{n}
:

\displaystyle h_{1}=\frac{1}{2E}\derivative{E}{\tilde{z}},\quad h_{n}=\derivative{h_{n-1}}{\tilde{z}}+2\xi h_{1}h_{n-1}\ (\text{for}\ n\geq 2),

(E.6)

while the second term
d_{n}
in Eq. (
E.4
) is determined by comparing Eq. (
E.4
) with the original Geroch-Hansen definition (see also
Fodor:1989
). It can be written in terms of
m_{n}
, for example

\displaystyle d_{0}

\displaystyle=d_{1}=d_{2}=d_{3}=0,\quad d_{4}=-\frac{1}{7}A_{2,0}m_{0},\quad d_{5}=-\frac{1}{21}A_{2,0}m_{1}-\frac{1}{3}A_{3,0}m_{0},

Large Language Models as Innovators: A Framework to Leverage Latent Space Exploration for Novelty Discovery
Mateusz Bystroński, Mikołaj Hołysz, Grzegorz Piotrowski, Nitesh V. Chawla, Tomasz Kajdanowicz

Abstract
Innovative idea generation remains a core challenge in AI, as large language models (LLMs) often struggle to produce outputs that are both novel and relevant. Despite their fluency, LLMs tend to replicate patterns seen during training, limiting their ability to diverge creatively without extensive prompt engineering. Prior work has addressed this through domain-specific heuristics and structured prompting pipelines, but such solutions are brittle and difficult to generalize. In this paper, we propose a model-agnostic latent-space ideation framework that enables controlled, scalable creativity by navigating the continuous embedding space of ideas. Unlike prior methods, our framework requires no handcrafted rules and adapts easily to different domains, input formats, and creative tasks. This paper introduces an early-stage prototype of our method, outlining the conceptual framework and preliminary results highlighting its potential as a general-purpose co-ideator for human-AI collaboration.



1
Introduction

Creative idea generation is a cornerstone of innovation across domains, from product design and writing to scientific research. Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have opened new possibilities for AI-assisted brainstorming, as these models can produce fluent and contextually relevant ideas on demand. However, LLMs often struggle to generate truly original or imaginative outputs. Because they are trained on vast repositories of existing data, LLMs tend to follow familiar patterns, leading to repetitive or “safe” ideas that lack genuine novelty
Mizrahi et al. 2025
.

Simply increasing a model’s randomness (e.g., the temperature setting) yields only modest gains in originality
Mizrahi et al. 2025
, and instructing an LLM to “be creative” can backfire by producing invalid or hallucinated results
Mizrahi et al. 2025
. In short, despite their fluency, current LLMs often fall short in creative divergence, necessitating new approaches to unlock their ideation potential.

One promising direction is to augment LLMs with heuristic seeding and structured idea transformations. Recent studies have shown that providing an LLM with carefully crafted seeds or combining existing concepts in novel ways can markedly improve the creativity of its outputs. For example, Mizrahi et al. (2024) introduce a pipeline that parses textual inputs into structured representations and recombines them to generate creative outcomes
Mizrahi et al. 2025
. In the culinary domain, their system merges disparate recipes—e.g., combining a pizza preparation method with the flavors of alfajores cookies yields a novel “alfajores pizza” concept
Mizrahi et al. 2025
. Complementing this line of work, Holysz et al. (2025) propose a multi-stage LLM pipeline for synthesizing realistic medical datasets: the system first seeds generation with domain-specific “chief complaints,” then incrementally expands them into full patient profiles and simulated clinical dialogues, using tightly crafted schemas to preserve medical plausibility
Holysz et al. 2025
. Such heuristic-seeding approaches—though effective—illustrate the cost of hand-tailoring rules and representations for each domain to unlock higher-level creativity.

However, these methods rely on hand-crafted rules and domain-specific representations. In the above example, bespoke steps (parsing recipes into ingredient trees, using a specialized recombination algorithm, etc.) were tailored to the cooking domain. Applying this technique to a different domain (e.g., product design or story writing) would require designing new rules or structured schemas from scratch, limiting the scalability of structured prompting solutions. This highlights a need for a more general and automated creativity enhancement framework that does not depend on extensive prompt engineering or domain-specific heuristics.

In this work, we propose a novel solution: a model-agnostic
latent-space ideation framework
that automatically explores variations of seed ideas in a continuous latent “idea space.” The key insight is that modern generative models implicitly organize concepts in a high-dimensional latent space, where semantic relationships and potential combinations are encoded as vector operations. By navigating this space, we can uncover imaginative combinations that would be hard to reach through direct prompting. The latent space connecting different concepts can serve as a wellspring of creativity: blending disparate ideas along latent dimensions produces new concepts that are both novel and coherent.

By harnessing latent-space exploration, our method synthesizes high-quality ideas without extensive human prompt engineering, instead letting the model’s learned conceptual manifold drive the creative generation.

Our framework is designed to act as a versatile “co-ideator” for humans, adapting to different domains and input formats with minimal effort. It exhibits strong adaptability and compositionality: the ideation process can begin from a short textual brief (e.g., a problem description or theme) or from a handful of seed ideas provided by the user. In either case, the system encodes the inputs and then recursively explores their latent neighbors to expand the idea space. This means the system can start with a simple concept and iteratively build more complex, unexpected ideas by combining and varying elements in latent space.

However, our method shows, that textual outputs can be opitimized in semantic latent space, which can be generalized to various NLP related tasks.

2
Related Work

Inspired by human brainstorming, recent work explores using multiple LLMs in tandem to enhance creativity. LLM Discussion (Lu et al., 2024) proposes a multi-agent discussion framework in which several LLM instances assume diverse roles and collectively tackle open-ended prompts
Lu et al. 2024
. The approach employs a three-phase interaction (divergent idea generation, debate, and convergence) and a role-playing setup (e.g., one model acts as a “visionary” and another as a “devil’s advocate”) to mimic a group brainstorming session
Lu et al. 2024
.

This diversity of perspectives mitigates the homogeneous thinking of a single model and leads to more original responses. Empirical evaluations on classic divergent thinking tasks—e.g., Alternative Uses Tests and other creativity benchmarks—show that the role-play discussion framework produces significantly more original and elaborated answers than both single-LLM baselines and prior multi-LLM strategies
Lu et al. 2024
. Notably, it outperformed simpler multi-LLM baselines like parallel brainstorming with voting or LLM debate on metrics such as originality and flexibility
Lu et al. 2024
. These findings align with those of Zhao et al. (2025), who also observed that collaboration among multiple LLMs can boost creative output, with especially marked gains in originality (novelty) of ideas
Zhao et al. 2025
.

Multi-agent setups have also been used to evaluate creativity in interactive settings. Hejabi et al. (2024) introduce a simulation based on the Balderdash game to assess LLMs’ creative deception abilities
Hejabi et al. 2024
. In this game, several LLM “players” invent plausible-sounding fake definitions for obscure terms to fool a guesser, while one provides the true definition. A referee LLM judges the similarity of definitions to detect the truth
Hejabi et al. 2024
.

Through this framework, the authors measure metrics like the
True Definition Ratio
(how often the model finds the real meaning) and
Deception Ratio
(how often others are misled by the fake definition)
Hejabi et al. 2024
. The results yield insights into LLMs’ capacity for creative deception and strategy, highlighting strengths and failure modes (e.g., models struggled when confronted with very rare vocabulary). This multi-agent evaluation underscores that even when each model individually is a large pretrained LLM, engaging them in adversarial or cooperative roles can reveal and potentially enhance creative behaviors that would not manifest in a single-turn setting.

Measuring “creativity” in language generation is inherently complex, so recent studies have adopted multi-dimensional evaluation protocols. A common approach is to borrow criteria from divergent thinking tests in psychology. For example, the Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking define axes such as
Originality
(novelty of ideas),
Fluency
(quantity of ideas),
Flexibility
(diversity of idea categories), and
Elaboration
(level of detail)
Lu et al. 2024
.

Lu et al. 2024
evaluate their LLM Discussion method on exactly these metrics, using both LLM-based scoring and human judges, and report consistent gains especially in Originality and Elaboration over baseline methods
Lu et al. 2024
. Standard tasks like the Alternative Uses Test (thinking of unusual uses for common objects), Instances and Similarities tests (from the Wallach–Kogan creativity battery) have been adopted as benchmarks for LLM-generated creativity
Lu et al. 2024
.

3
Method

Figure 1:
Overview of the latent-space ideation framework. Seed ideas from user, or gathered using LLM, are then encoded into embeddings via an encoder. The latent space formed by these embeddings is explored to produce new candidate vectors. These are projected into the token embedding space of a decoder LLM using an xRAG-style projector, enabling generation of novel ideas. The generated outputs are evaluated by an LLM-based judge and can optionally feed back into the prompt or embedding space, supporting iterative refinement.

We propose a
latent-space ideation framework
consisting of a sequence of modular components that transform an initial problem description into diverse, expanded ideas. The architecture is model-agnostic and task-agnostic, meaning each module can leverage different underlying models without altering the overall pipeline. The core components include:

1.

A semantic encoder that converts ideas or prompts into latent embeddings,

2.

A latent explorer that generates new candidate idea embeddings via interpolation, extrapolation, or noise-based perturbations,

3.

A cross-modal projector (xRAG-style modality bridge) that maps latent vectors into the input space of a text decoder,

4.

A decoder LLM that generates natural-language idea descriptions from the projected embeddings,

5.

An evaluator LLM that scores or filters the generated ideas against a rubric of creativity criteria.

The pipeline is designed to be general-purpose: for example, any sentence embedding model can serve as the encoder and any large language model as the decoder.

Figure
1
illustrates the end-to-end workflow, which proceeds through the following stages:

1. Problem Comprehension & Seed Generation (Optional)

If initial ideas or prompts are not provided, the framework begins by interpreting the task context and producing seed concepts. A large language model (LLM) receives the creative brief and returns a set of diverse, high-level idea snippets that serve as anchors for subsequent latent-space exploration. Crucially,
any
upstream ideation pipeline can occupy this slot—ranging from classic brainstorming heuristics and analogical reasoning tools to multi-agent LLM discussion frameworks or domain-specific prompt chains. Our method does not replace these generators; instead, it augments them by treating their outputs as entry points into a continuous latent geometry where systematic interpolation, extrapolation, and refinement can occur. When the user already supplies seed ideas, this stage can be skipped entirely, and the provided concepts flow directly into the encoding step.

2. Latent Encoding

Each seed idea is encoded into a fixed-dimensional latent vector using a text encoder. For an idea text
x_{i}
, the encoder yields:

e_{i}=\text{Enc}(x_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

a dense vector in a semantic latent space. The encoder is treated as a frozen, domain-agnostic module.

3. Embedding-Space Exploration

To generate novel candidate embeddings, we consider several strategies for exploring the latent space around the known set
\{e_{i}\}
. These may include:

•

Interpolation:
For a pair of embeddings
e_{i},e_{j}
, we sample
\lambda\in[0,1]
and compute:

e_{\text{new}}=\lambda e_{i}+(1-\lambda)e_{j}.

This method is used in our evaluation as it allows smooth traversal between existing representations while remaining within the convex hull of known concepts.

•

Extrapolation:
Extending beyond the known embedding space by setting
\lambda\notin[0,1]
, enabling exploration of novel semantic directions.

•

Noise-based perturbation:
Introducing isotropic Gaussian noise:

e_{\text{new}}=e_{i}+\epsilon,\quad\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}),

which allows stochastic diversification while retaining proximity to the original concept.

While we restrict our empirical evaluation to interpolation, the general framework admits any continuous metaheuristic or sampling scheme over the embedding space, enabling future extensions with richer exploration strategies.

4. Cross-Modal Projection

To decode the new vectors, we map each
e_{\text{new}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
into the token embedding space of a decoder LLM. This is done using a learned projector:

W_{p}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m},

where
m
matches the LLM’s token embedding dimension. The output

h_{X}=W_{p}(e_{\text{new}})

is inserted as a special token embedding (denoted
[X]
) into the input sequence. This acts as a latent-conditioned prompt, akin to continuous prefix-tuning
Cheng et al. 2024
;
Bystroński et al. 2025
.

5. Latent-to-Text Decoding

With the embedding
h_{X}
now in context, the decoder LLM generates a textual description:

y_{\text{new}}=\text{Dec}(h_{X}),

where
Dec
is a decoder LLM prompted to paraphrase
h_{X}

Cheng et al. 2024
. The model treats
h_{X}
as a learned virtual token and expands it into coherent text, yielding candidate idea descriptions.

6. Evaluation

Each
y_{\text{new}}
is scored against a creativity. Then, best and valid candidates are selected. As measuring creativity is an open research, in this work we use LLM as a Judge as evaluator against main part of creativity rubric introduced in
Lu et al. 2024
, particulary:

•

Originality:
Is the idea original or unexpected?

•

Relevancy:
Is the idea relevant to the objective (derived from problem description or input ideas)?

More sophisticated creativity metrics will be explored in future works.

7. Feedback Loop

To enable iterative refinement, high-scoring ideas extend known valid ideas manifold, allowing more accurate space exploration. The exploration strategy can be adjusted (e.g., focusing on promising latent regions) or the problem context updated. This diverge-evaluate-converge cycle mirrors human brainstorming
Lu et al. 2024
, and continues until a stopping condition is reached. Over multiple iterations, the framework converges to a set of high-quality, diverse ideas aligned with the original task.

5
Discussion and Conclusion

We introduced a novel
latent-space ideation framework
that significantly advances AI-assisted creative idea generation by moving beyond traditional prompt engineering and heuristic-based approaches. By encoding ideas into a continuous latent space and systematically exploring this manifold through operations like interpolation, our framework enables the generation of
highly original
and
fluent
ideas. This approach leverages the inherent conceptual organization within modern generative models, allowing for the discovery of novel combinations that are difficult to achieve with direct textual prompting alone.

Our initial experiments demonstrate that this latent-space exploration consistently enhances the originality and fluency of generated ideas across diverse creative tasks, offering a new paradigm for augmenting human ideation. While the current gains are modest due to a conservative filtering strategy and the limitations of interpolation, our work serves as a proof-of-concept, revealing the untapped potential of continuous latent spaces for fostering computational creativity in language domain.

One strength of our framework lies in its compositionality and adaptability. It can initiate the ideation process from a concise textual brief or a few user-provided seed ideas, recursively exploring their latent neighbors to expand the idea space. This enables the system to iteratively construct complex and unexpected ideas by blending and varying elements within the latent space. Our empirical evaluation demonstrates promising results, particularly in boosting the originality and fluency of generated ideas across various creativity benchmarks, including the Alternative Uses Test (AUT), Instances, Similarities, and Scientific ideation tasks.

Another strength of our framework is its capacity for infinite exploration. As high-quality ideas are generated and validated, they can be fed back into the latent space, expanding the effective "known" manifold of valid concepts. This iterative, self-improving loop allows the system to continually uncover novel and relevant ideas, enhancing its creative capacity over time.

Ultimately, our latent-space ideation framework unlocks the creative potential of LLMs and provides a promising foundation for developing versatile and powerful AI co-ideators.

6
Future Works

Looking ahead, several avenues for future research emerge. Developing more sophisticated latent space exploration strategies, such as swarm-based optimization algorithms, will be crucial to increase the efficiency of idea generation and improve
flexibility
. Furthermore, integrating more advanced human-in-the-loop feedback mechanisms and dynamic adjustment of exploration parameters could further refine the iterative ideation process. We also recognize the need for more efficient and nuanced scoring functions to evaluate generated ideas, moving beyond reliance on GPT-4o as a judge. Exploring specialized, potentially lightweight, evaluators or incorporating more objective, domain-specific metrics could significantly streamline the feedback loop.

The complexity of matroid homomorphism reconfiguration
Cheolwon Heo, Mark Siggers

Abstract
. We consider a reconfiguration version of the homomorphism problem Hom 𝕄 ​ ( N ) {\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{M}}(N) for binary matroids N N . This reconfiguration problem, Recol 𝕄 ​ ( N ) {\rm Recol}_{\mathbb{M}}(N) , asks, for two homomorphisms ϕ \phi and ψ \psi of a matroid M M to N N , if there is a path of homomorphism from ϕ \phi to ψ \psi such that consecutive homomorphism in the path differ on a single cocircuit of N N . We show that this problem is trivial in the case that N N is, or dismantles to, the graphic matroid M ⁡ ( K 2 ) M(K_{2}) , and that the problem is PSPACE \PSPACE -complete when N N is the graphic matroid M ⁡ ( K 3 ) M(K_{3}) , M ⁡ ( K 4 ) M(K_{4}) , or any graphic matroid containing M ⁡ ( K 5 ) M(K_{5}) .

Introduction

All matroids in this paper are binary matroids, meaning that they have a representation by a matrix over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

A homomorphism from a matroid

More precisely, where the point space

Retraction and list variations of the problem
\Hom_{M}(N)
were also considered in
[
8
]
; for these variations, while the decision graph was still the main tool, there were several differences between the graph and matroid versions of the problem. In this paper we look a reconfiguration version of
\Hom_{M}(H)

In a reconfiguration version of a decision problem, one does not look to decide if there is a solution to an instance, but looks to decide if there is series of small changes one can make to get between a given pair of solutions. One well known reconfiguration version of the graph colouring problem
\gHom(H)
is the graph recolouring problem
\gRecol(H)
. Where the colouring graph
\gCol(G,H)
is the graph on he set of
H
-colourings of
G
in which two are adjacent if they differ on a single vertex, one must decide, for given maps
\phi
and
\psi
, if there is a path between
\phi
and
\psi
in
\gCol(G,H)
.

The colouring graph generally has order exponential in the order of the instance
G
, so deciding if such a path exists can take exponential time. It is not hard to show that the problem
\gRecol(H)
is in the complexity class
\PSPACE
.
It was shown in
[
5
]
that the problem
\gRecol(K_{n})

is in
\Poly
if
n\leq 3
and in
[
2
]
that it is
\PSPACE
-complete if
n\geq 4
.
Here is our reconfiguration graph.

Definition 1.4
.

The
matroid

-colouring graph
\mCol(M,N)
of
M
for binary matroids
M
and

, is the graph whose vertex set is the set of matroid homomorphisms from

to

, in which
two vertices
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
are adjacent if for some cocircuit
C
of
M
,
\tau(e)\neq\tau^{\prime}(e)
if and only if

.

Example 1.5

2.
Technical Defintions

Recall that all matroids in this paper are simple binary matroids.
A
loop
in a matroid is a circuit consisting of a singular point.
A matroid with a loop is called
looped
. In Sections
2
and
3
, results and definitions are for matroids which may have loops. From Section
4

we consider only loopless matroids.

Any simple binary matroid
N
can be representated by an
m\times|E(N)|
matrix
A
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with distinct columns. The value
m
can be taken to be the rank of
N
, but sometimes it is useful to use a greater
m
.
The columns of
A
are the points of
N
, and as they are vectors in space
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
they can be added together. Their span in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
is the
point space

P(N)
of
N
. A subset of columns is a cycle if it sums, over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
to
0
.

The requirement, for a map
\tau:E(N)\to E(M)
, that
\tau(Z)
is a disjoint union of circuits for any circuit
Z
becomes the requirement that if
\sum_{x\in Z}x=0
, then
\sum_{x\in Z}\tau(x)=0
; that is, it becomes the requirement that
\tau
is a linear map of
P(N)
to
P(M)
. Though the sum of two elements depends on the representation
A
, the fact that a set of points sums to zero does not depend on
A
, and so the following equivalent definitions of a matroid homomorphisms, which use binary representations of the matroids, are independent of the actual representations used.

Fact 2.1
.

For binary matroids
M
and
N
a map
\tau:E(M)\to E(N)
is a matroid homomorphism if and only if it defines a linear map of point spaces, if and only if for all circuits
Z
of
M

\tau(Z):=\sum_{e\in Z}\tau(e)=0.

Recall from
Definition

1.4
that two homomorphisms in
\mCol(M,N)

are adjacent if and only if they differ on a cocircuit of
C
of
M
. The following shows that if this happens, they must differ on that cocircuit on a constant. We note that Proposition 4.1 of
[
6
]
gives the following two facts in the case that the matroid
N
is a vector space.

Fact 2.2
.

Let
\tau,\tau^{\prime}:M\to N
be matroid homomorphisms for binary matroids
M,N
, and let
C
be a cocircuit of
M
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

For
e\in E(M)
,
\tau(e)\neq\tau^{\prime}(e)
if and only if
e\in C
.

(2)

There exists a non-zero vector
c\in P(N)
such that

\tau^{\prime}=\tau+c\chi_{C}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tau(e)+c&\mbox{ if }e\in C\\
\tau(e)&\mbox{ otherwise .}\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof.

It is easy to see that (2) implies (1) as
c
is a non-zero vector.
For the converse, let us assume (1).
Let
\tau:E(M)\to P(N)
be given by
\tau(e):=\tau(e)-\tau^{\prime}(e)
.
Then (1) implies that
\tau(e)\neq 0
if and only if
e\in C
.
Now, let us assume that there exist points
e_{1},e_{2}\in C
such that
\tau(e_{1})\neq\tau(e_{2})
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume the first entries of
\tau(e_{1})
, and
\tau(e_{2})
as columns in
P(N)
are
1
and
0
, respectively.
Let
X
be the set of all

such that the first entry of
\tau(e)
is
1
.
As
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
are matroid homomorphisms from
M
to
N
, for any circuit
Z
of
M
,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{e\in C\cap Z}\tau(e)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{e\in Z}\tau(e)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{e\in Z}(\tau(e)-\tau^{\prime}(e))

\displaystyle=\tau(Z)-\tau^{\prime}(Z)=0-0

\displaystyle=0

This implies that
|X\cap Z|
is even. Our choice of
Z
is arbitrary, so
X
is a disjoint union of cocircuits of
M
.
Since
X
is non-empty and
X\subseteq C
, we have

, contradicting the fact that
\tau(e_{2})\in C-X
.
∎

So two maps are adjacent in
\mCol(M,N)
if and only if they differ by a constant on a cocircuit.
The following complements this, saying that we can get a new homomorphism from an old one by adding a constant on a cocircuit as long as doing so is well defined.

Fact 2.3
.

Let

be a binary matroid,

be such that

.

Proof.

This is trivial as for every circuit

of

is even.
∎

Again, the

depends on the representation

of

, but the fact that there is such a

does not. We say that we get

from

3.
Induced homomorphsims, retractions, and dismantling

Recall that a problem
A

polynomially reduces
to a problem
B
if for an instance
I_{A}
of
A
, we can construct an instance
I_{B}
of
B
that is of size polynomial in that of
I_{A}
, such that
I_{A}
is a YES instance of
A
if and only if
I_{B}
is a YES instance of
B
. In this case we write
A\leq_{\Poly}B
. We say, informally, that
B
is at least as hard as
A
, which means, in particular, that
A
is polynomially time solvable if
B
is, and
B
is
\PSPACE
-hard if
A
is. If
A\leq_{\Poly}B\leq_{\Poly}A
then the problems are
polynomially equivalent
and we write
A=_{\Poly}B
.

A basic fact in the theory of graph recolourings which comes from
[
3
]
is the fact that a homomorphism
f:H\to H^{\prime}
induces a homomorphism
f^{*}:\gCol(G,H)\to\gCol(G,H^{\prime})
. If the homorphism is a retraction, it induces a polynomial reduction
\gRecol(H)\leq_{\Poly}\gRecol(H^{\prime})
, and if it is a dismantling retraction it induces a polynomial equivalence of
\gRecol(H)=_{\Poly}\gRecol(H^{\prime})
. We observe here that the same holds for matroid homomorphisms.

For matroid homomorphisms
\alpha:L\to M
and
\beta:M\to N
we get, of course, that
\beta\circ\alpha:L\to N
is a matroid homomorphism.
This extends naturally to walks in the colouring graphs in a couple of ways.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha:L\to M
and
\beta:M\to N
be matroid homomorphisms.

(1)

If there is a walk
A
between
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
in
\mCol(L,M)
, then there is a walk between
\beta\circ\alpha
and
\beta\circ\alpha^{\prime}
in
\mCol(L,N)
.
We call this walk
\beta\circ A
.

(2)

If there is a walk
B
between
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
in
\mCol(M,N)
, then there is a walk between
\beta\circ\alpha
and
\beta^{\prime}\circ\alpha
in
\mCol(L,N)
. We call this walk
B\circ\alpha
.

Proof.

For the first statement, it is enough to consider the case that
\alpha\sim\alpha^{\prime}
. We write

\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha+c\chi_{C}
for some constant
c\in E(M)
and some cocircuit
C
of
L
.
To be pedantic, we write this as

\alpha^{\prime}(e)=\alpha(e)+\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c&\mbox{ if }e\in C\\
0&\mbox{ if }e\not\in C\\
\end{array}\right..

By the linearity of
\beta
we have

[\beta\circ\alpha^{\prime}](e)=[\beta\circ\alpha](e)+\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\beta(c)&\mbox{ if }e\in C\\
0&\mbox{ if }e\not\in C\\
\end{array}\right.,

which we can write as
\beta\circ\alpha^{\prime}=\beta\circ\alpha+\beta(c)\chi_{C}
. Thus
\beta\circ\alpha\sim\beta\circ\alpha^{\prime}
in
\mCol(L,N)
as needed.

For the second statement, we write
\beta^{\prime}=\beta+c\chi_{C}
, which allows us to write

[\beta^{\prime}\circ\alpha](e)=[\beta\circ\alpha](e)+\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c&\mbox{ if }\alpha(e)\in C\\
0&\mbox{ if }\alpha(e)\not\in C\\
\end{array}\right.,

and so
\beta^{\prime}\circ\alpha=(\beta\circ\alpha)+c\chi_{\alpha^{-1}(C)}
. As the pre-image
\alpha^{-1}(C)
of a cocircuit
C
is a cocycle, so a disjoint union of cocircuits,
we get a path from
\beta^{\prime}\circ\alpha
to
\beta\circ\alpha
by recolouring one cocircuit at at time, as needed.
∎

In particular, part (1) not only takes paths to paths, but takes edges to edges, so gives us the following.

Fact 3.2
.

For a matroid homomorphism
f:N\to N^{\prime}
, and a matroid
M
, the induced map
f^{*}:\mCol(M,N)\to\mCol(M,N^{\prime})
defined by
f^{*}(\tau)=f\circ\tau

is a graph homomorphism.

A surjective homomorphism
r:M\to M^{\prime}
is a
retraction
if there is a homomorphism
c:M^{\prime}\to M
such that
r\circ c
is the identity on
M^{\prime}
. If this exists then
c
is an isomorphism, and identifying
M^{\prime}
with
c(M^{\prime})
we can view
M^{\prime}
as a submatroid of
M
that is fixed by
r
. A matroid
M^{\prime}
is a
retract
of
M
if there is a retraction
r:M\to M^{\prime}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

If a matroid
N^{\prime}
is a retract of
N
, then there is a path between two vertices of
\mCol(M,N^{\prime})
if and only if there is a path between them in
\mCol(M,N)
. In particular
\mRecol(N^{\prime})\leq_{\Poly}\mRecol(N)
.

Proof.

A path between vertices of
\mCol(M,N^{\prime})
is a path between them in
\mCol(M,N)
. On the other hand, by
Fact

3.2
, a path between them in
\mCol(M,N)
maps, by
r^{*}
where
r:N\to N^{\prime}
is the retraction, to a path between them in
\mCol(M,N^{\prime})
.
∎

A retraction
r\in\mCol(N,N)
is a
dismantling retraction
if it is adjacent to the identity map in
\mCol(N,N)
.
Recall that a dismantling retraction
r:H\to H
of graphs is any retraction whose image
H^{\prime}
has one fewer vertices than
H
. These induce dismantling retractions on the graphic matroids, so our definition can be viewed as a generalisation of the definition of a dismantling retraction for graphs.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
r:N\to N^{\prime}
is a dismantling retraction, then there is a path between two vertices
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
of
\mCol(M,N)
if and only if there is a path between
r\circ\tau
and
r\circ\tau^{\prime}
in
\mCol(M,N^{\prime})
. In particular,
\mRecol(N^{\prime})\equiv_{\Poly}\mRecol(N)
.

Proof.

By
Fact

3.2
, we have that a path between
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
in
\mCol(M,N)
maps by
r^{*}
to a path between
r\circ\tau
and
r\circ\tau^{\prime}
in
\mCol(M,N^{\prime})
.

To see that a path between
r\circ\tau
and
r\circ\tau^{\prime}

gives a path between
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
it is enough to show, for any
\tau\in\mCol(M,N)
, that there is a path from
\tau
to
r\circ\tau
in
\mCol(M,N)
. But this is immediate from part (2) of
Lemma

3.1
from the fact that
\id\sim r
in
\mCol(N,N)
.
∎

If there is a series of dismantling retractions whose composition takes a matroid
N
to a retract
N^{\prime}
then we say that
N
dismantles to
N^{\prime}
.

Corollary 3.5
.

If
N
dismantles to the loop
M^{\ell}(K_{1})
or the edge
M(K_{2})
then

\mRecol(N)
is trivial.

Proof.

By
Proposition

3.4
it is enough to show

\mRecol(M^{\ell}(K_{1}))
and
\mRecol(M(K_{2}))
are trivial, and the latter is done in
Fact

1.7
. For the former, observe that for any instance
M
the graph
\mCol(M,M^{\ell}(K_{1}))
is a single vertex, and so all instances of
\mRecol(M^{\ell}(K_{1}))
are YES instances.
∎

In the graph case, if follows by a result of
[
3
]
that
\gRecol(H)
is trivial if and only if

is dismantlable. We wonder if an analogue holds for matroids.

Conjecture 3.6
.

\mRecol(N)

dismantles to the loop

or the edge

.

Now we turn our attention to the hardness part of

4.
Decision Graphs

A
decision
graph for a matroid
N
is a graph
D
such that for any graph
G
,

G\to D\iff M(G)\to N.

These were constructed for all non-bipartite binary matroids in
[
8
]
and used to show that
\mHom(N)
is hard for all
N
with girth
3
.

Our construction did not use the ideas of Tutte’s duality, which we recalled in the introduction, but a much simpler construction using these ideas was (essentially) given in Proposition 3.4 of
[
6
]
. To translate the construction to matroids, one requires a representation, and different representations can give different (though homomorphically equivalent) graphs. We give this construction without a representation, for all binary matroids, and then observe that it is exactly the construction from
[
6
]
in the case that the representing matrix is an
m\times n
matrix where
m
is the rank of
N
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
N
be a binary matroid of rank
m
and size
n
, and let
B
be a basis of
N
. The
decision graph

D(N,B)
is a graph on the power set of
B
. Again viewing the power-set as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-module, two sets
V,W\subset B
are adjacent if
|V\triangle W|=1
or there exists
e\in E-B
such that
(V\triangle W)\cup\{e\}
is a circuit.

This is implicit to the matroid, but using a representation, has a form that is generally easier to use. Letting
N
be represented by a matrix
A
where the elements
B
of the basis are the columns with a single non-zero entry, the graph defined in the following definition is clearly
D(N,B)
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
A
be an matrix representing
N
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.The decision graph
D(N,A)
is the graph on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
such that columns
v
and
w
of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
are adjacent if
v+w
is a column of
A
.

If
N
is a matrix of rank
r
but is represented by a matrix with more than
r
rows, then
D(N,A)
has more vertices than
D(N,B)
.
But if
A
has
r
rows, and the columns corresponding to the elements of
B
make an identity matrix, then these are the same graph, independent of representation.

Indeed, it follows by the unique representability of binary matroids, proved in
[
4
]
, that any two representations
of a rank
r
binary matroid
N
by matrices over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with
r
rows differ by elementary row operations, so where one is
A
, the other is
A^{\prime}A
for some invertible
r\times r
matrix
A^{\prime}
.
The vertex map
\phi:D(N,A)\to D(N,A^{\prime}A)
defined by
\phi(v)=A^{\prime}v
is a graph isomorphism, as the invertibility of
A^{\prime}
implies that
v+w=e
if and only if
A^{\prime}v+A^{\prime}w=A^{\prime}e
.

Fact 4.3
.

For a binary matroid
N
of rank
r
and size
n
,5 the decision graph
D(N,B)
, for any basis
B
, is isomorphic to
D(N,A)
for any representation of
N
by an
n
-by-
r
matrix
A

over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

It is shown in Proposition 3.4 of
[
6
]
that the graphs
D(N,A)
for representations
A
of
D
are decision graphs. As
D(N,B)
is the smallest of these, we now denote it by
D_{u}(N)
and call it
the
decision graph of
N
. We mentioned before that
K_{4}
is the decision graph for
M(K_{3})
. The details of this are given in the following example, as
M(K_{3})
is the projective geometry
\PG(1,2)
.

Example 4.4
.

The projective geometry matroid
PG(t-1,2)
has representation as the matrix of all non-zero columns of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{t}
. Its universal decision graph is
K_{2^{t}}
.

The matroid clique
M(K_{n})
has representation as the matrix of columns of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n-1}
with weight
1
or
2
. Its universal decision graph is the
n
-dimensional half-cube
\frac{1}{2}Q_{n}
– the vertices are the columns of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n-1}
and two are adjacent if they differ in one or two coordinates.

4.1.
The decision graph and the induced Tutte connection

The proof from Proposition 3.4 of
[
6
]
that
D_{u}(N)
is a decision graph uses the Tutte construction that we saw in
Example

1.3
to get an element of
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N)
for every element of
\mCol(M(G),N)
, and vice versa.

Definition 4.5

(The Tutte Connection)
.

For a graph
G
, fix a vertex
v_{0}
and a spanning subtree
T
of
G
rooted at
v_{0}
. This defines a partial ordering of
V(G)
by saying that
u\leq v
if
u
is in the path in
T
from
v
to
v_{0}
. Let
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1}
be an
ordering of
V(G)
that refines this partial order. Let
b
be an vertex of
D_{u}(N)
.

•

For any
\tau\in\mCol(M(G),N)
let
\phi_{\tau,b}:V(G)\to V(D_{u}(N))
be defined by setting

(1)

\phi_{\tau,b}(v_{0})=b,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\phi_{\tau,b}(v_{j})=\phi_{\tau,b}(v_{i})+\tau(v_{i}v_{j})

for
j=1,\dots,n-1
, where
v_{i}
is the neighbour of
v_{j}
in
T
of lower index.

•

For a graph homomorphism
\phi:G\to D_{u}(N)
, define the map
\tau_{\phi}:M(G)\to N
by

(2)

\tau_{\phi}(uv)=\phi(u)+\phi(v).

The standard proof of Tutte shows that
\phi_{\tau,b}:G\to D_{u}(N)
and
\tau_{\phi}:M(G)\to N
are homomorphisms (see Proposition 3.4 of
[
6
]
)
and that
\tau_{\phi_{\tau,b}}=\tau
. Thus on the level of vertices, the decision graph induces maps between
\mCol(M(G),N)
and
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N))
.
Letting
\Phi_{b}(\tau)=\phi_{\tau,e}
and
\Phi^{-1}(\phi)=\tau_{\phi}
, we have that for each
b\in V(D_{u}(N))
,

\Phi_{b}:V(\mCol(M(G),N))\to V(\gCol(G,D_{u}(N)))

is an injection with inverse
\Phi^{-1}
.
Let
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N);v_{0},b)
be the subgraph of
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N))
induced on the the vertices
\phi
for which
\phi(v_{0})=b
.

Fact 4.6
.

For a matroid
N
, and a graph
G
with designated vertex
v_{0}
of
G
, the map

\Phi_{b}:V(\mCol(M(G),N))\to V(\gCol(G,D_{u}(N);v_{0},b))

is a bijection with inverse
\Phi^{-1}
.

As
b
runs over the vertices of
D_{u}(N)
, the graphs
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N);v_{0},b)
partition
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N))
. Moreover, as
D_{u}(N)
is vertex transitive, they induce isomorphic subgraphs. Because of this we generally drop the subscript
b
, assuming it is the
0
vector in
V(D_{u}(N))
and let
\Phi=\Phi_{0}
. Similarily, the choice of
T
in
Definition

4.5
is unimportant, and changing
v_{0}
only changes
b
in
\phi_{\tau,b}
to another constant, so we generally remove
v_{0}
from the notation and write

\gCol(G,D_{u}(N);0)
for
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N);v_{0},b)
.

Now while
\Phi
and
\Phi^{-1}
give a nice
1
-to-
|D_{u}(N)|
correspondence between the vertex sets of
\mCol(M(G),N)
and
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N))
, neither of them induce a homomorphism. Indeed, the following example shows that
\Phi
preserves no connectivity at all.

Example 4.7
.

The decision graph of
M(K_{3})
is
D_{u}(M(K_{3}))=K_{4}
.
While the recolouring graph
\gRecol(K_{4},K_{4})
is easily seen to consist of
24
independent vertices,
\gRecol(M(K_{4}),M(K_{3}))
is a
K_{3,3}
– from a given colouring, we can recolour by switching the colours on any two colour classes.

On the other hand, one can show that
\Phi^{-1}
maps components of
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N))
to components of
\mCol(M(G),N)
.
We make this into a bijection of components by replacing
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N))
by a super-graph that we get by considering Kempe recolourings.

4.2.
Matroid recolouring to Kempe recolouring

Inspired by Kempe’s flawed proof of the four-colour theorem, the notion of a (vertex) Kempe recolouring of a graph has appeared
in many contexts, and indeed the notion of edge Kempe recolourings, which one gets via the Tutte connection, have also appeared.
In
[
1
]
the reconfiguration problem was considered for Kempe recolourings.
Given a map
\phi\in\gCol(G,K_{n})
a component of the subgraph induced on the set
\phi^{-1}(a,b)
of vertices of two colours
a
and
b
is
an
(a,b)
-component of
G
. A Kempe recolouring of
G
is when one toggles the colours
a
and
b
on an
(a,b)
-component.

The following was proved in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 4.8
.

The problem
\kRecol(K_{n})
of deciding, for two
n
-colourings of a graph
G
, if there is a path between them in
\kCol(G,K_{n})
, is
\PSPACE
-complete for
n\geq 4
.

Replacing
K_{n}
with any decision graph, we can give the following reframing of this definition, which allows us to ‘add’ colours.
It is clear that this coincides with the above notion of ‘toggling’ when
D
is a clique
K_{2^{t}}=D_{u}(PG(t-1,2))
.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
N
be a matroid and
D=D_{u}(N)
be its decision graph.
For a graph
G
, maps
\phi
and
\psi
in
\gCol(G,D)
are
Kempe-adjacent
, or, adjacent in
\kCol(G,D)
if

\psi=\phi+b\chi_{U}

for some
b,b^{\prime}
in
[n]
and some component
U
of
\phi^{-1}(b^{\prime},b^{\prime}+b)
in
G
.

For an edge
\phi\sim\phi^{\prime}
of
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N))
,
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi
differ on only one vertex
v
, and where
b=\phi^{\prime}(v)-\phi(v)
the vertex
v
is a component of
\phi^{-1}(\phi^{\prime}(v),\phi^{\prime}(v)+b)
as no neighbour of
v
can have either of these colours. So
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N))
is a subgraph of
\kCol(G,D_{u}(N))
.
The following says that
\Phi^{-1}
maps components of
\kCol(G,D_{u}(N))
to components of
\mCol(M(G),N)
. Clearly it does the
same for the components of the subgraph
\gCol(G,D_{u}(N))
.

Lemma 4.10
.

For any matroid
N
and graph
G
, if
\phi\sim\phi^{\prime}
in
\kCol(G,D_{u}(N))
, then there is a path from
\tau_{\phi}=\Phi^{-1}(\phi)
to
\tau_{\phi^{\prime}}=\Phi^{-1}(\phi^{\prime})
in
\mCol(M(G),N)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\phi\sim\psi\in\kCol(G,K_{n})
.
So, by definition,
\psi=\phi+b\chi_{U}
where
U
is a
(b,b+b^{\prime})
-component of
G
for some
b^{\prime}
. Let
E=\delta(U)
be the edges of
G
from
U
to
\bar{U}
. This is a cutset of
G
so is a disjoint union of cocycles
C_{1},\dots,C_{d}
. We have that

\tau_{\phi^{\prime}}=\tau_{\phi}+b\chi_{C}
, and so we get a path from
\tau_{\phi}
to
\tau_{\phi^{\prime}}
in
\mCol(M(G),N)
by adding

to the cocycles

, one at a time.

∎

In general
\Phi=\Phi_{0}
does not even take edges of
\mCol(M(G),N)
to paths of
\kCol(G,D_{u}(N))
. But it does when
we restrict our attention to the case that

is a clique
K_{2^{t}}=D_{u}(PG(t-1,2))
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
N=PG(t-1,2)
for

so
D_{u}(N)=K_{n}
with

. There is path from
\tau_{\phi}
to
\tau_{\psi}
in
\mCol(M(G),N)
if and only if there is a path from
\phi=\Phi_{0}(\tau_{\phi})
to
\psi=\Phi_{0}(\tau_{\psi})
in
\kCol(G,K_{n})
.

Proof.

Lemma

4.10
gives us the easy direction. For the other direction, let
\tau_{\phi}
and
\tau_{\psi}
be adjacent.
We show that there is, in fact, a path from
\phi
to
\psi
in
\kCol(G,K_{n};0)
.
We have
\tau_{\psi}=\tau_{\phi}+b\chi_{C}
for some cocycle

of

be such that

, and

.
We claim that for any vertex

of

.
∎

Thus we can solve

5.
Matroid
n
-recolouring is PSPACE-complete

In this section, we prove
Theorem

5.2
which extends
Corollary

4.14
to
K_{n}
for
n\geq 5
. With
Fact

1.7
, this gives
Theorem

1.6
.

Recall that the set
V_{n}=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
of standard normal vectors in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
is a basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
. The
support
of a vector in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
is the set of elements in this basis used to represent it, its
weight
is the number of vectors in its support. Let
E_{n}
be the set of weight two vectors of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
.

The standard
graphic representation
of a graphic matriod
M(G)
is the representation by the matrix
A_{G}
with columns
E(M(G))=E_{G}=\{v_{i}+v_{j}\mid v_{i}\sim_{G}v_{j}\}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
where the vertex set
V=V(G)=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
is viewed as the set
V_{n}
.
In the case of the matroid
M(K_{n})
,
E_{K_{n}}=\{v_{i}+v_{j}\mid v_{i}\neq v_{j}\in V\}
; we often write this as
2V
.

We start with a simple and believable fact.

Fact 5.1
.

For
n\geq 5
and any loopless graph
G
, any homomorphism
\tau:M(K_{n})\to M(G)
maps
M(K_{n})
isomorphically to a copy of
M(K_{n})
in
M(G)
.

Proof.

Let
M(K_{n})
have a graphic representation with
V(K_{n})=\{u_{0},\dots,u_{n-1}\}
, and let
M(G)
have a graphic representation by
A_{G}
with
V(G)=\{v_{0},\dots,v_{m-1}\}
.

Any homomorphism
\tau:M(K_{n})\to M(G)
is
\Phi^{-1}(\phi)
for some homomorphism
\phi:K_{n}\to D
of
K_{n}
to the decision graph
D=D(M(G),A_{G})
for which
\phi(u_{0})=0
. As adjacent vertices in
D
differ by columns in
A_{G}
, which have weight
2
over
V(G)
,
a clique of size
n\geq 5
in
D
containing the vertex
0
can be assumed to be

\{0,v_{0}+v_{1},v_{0}+v_{2},\dots,v_{0}+v_{n-1}\}
by permuting the labels in
V(G)
. So we may assume that
\phi(u_{i})=v_{0}+v_{i}
for all
i
. It follows that
\tau(u_{i}+u_{j})=v_{i}+v_{j}
for all
i,j\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
and so
\tau
maps
M(K_{n})
isomorphically to a copy of
M(K_{n})
in
M(G)
, as needed. Indeed
\tau(u_{0}+u_{j})=0+u_{0}+u_{j}=u_{0}+u_{j}
, and when
i,j\neq 0
we have

\tau(u_{0}+u_{j})=\phi(u_{i})+\phi(u_{j})=v_{0}+v_{i}+v_{0}+v_{j}=v_{i}+v_{j}.

∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
N
be a loopless graphic matroid containing a copy of
M(K_{n})
for
n\geq 5
. The problem
\mRecol(N)
is
\PSPACE
-complete.

Proof.

Let
N
be a graphic matroid containing a copy of
M(K_{5})
. Let
n\geq 5
be the largest
n
such that

N
contains a copy of
M(K_{n})
. We use the graphic representation of
N
over
V_{N}=\{v_{1},\dots v_{N}\}
.
We assume that the span of
V_{n}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
induces a copy
N_{K}
of
M(K_{n})
, and let
N_{K_{4}}

be the copy of
M(K_{4})
induced by the span of
V_{4}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{4}\}
. Let
N_{Z}
be the
4
-star

\{v_{1}+v_{n},v_{2}+v_{n},v_{3}+v_{n},v_{4}+v_{n}\}
in
N_{K}
.

We use a gadget construction to reduce
\mRecol(M(K_{4}))
to
\mRecol(N)
, showing that the latter is hard.

Construction 5.3
.

For a matroid
M
, construct the matroid
M^{*}
as follows.
Let
M_{K}
, graphically represented over
U_{n}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
, be a copy of
N_{K}
. Let
M_{K_{4}}
be the copy of
N_{K_{4}}
on
U_{4}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
and
M_{Z}=\{u_{1}+u_{n},u_{2}+u_{n},u_{3}+u_{n},u_{4}+u_{n}\}
be the copy of
N_{Z}
.
Construct
M^{*}
from
M
and
M_{K}
by adding, for each
e\in E(M)
, the new point
e^{\prime}
and the circuit

\{e,e^{\prime}\}\cup M_{Z}
. Where
E
is the point set of
M
let
E^{\prime}=\{e^{\prime}\mid e\in E\}
; so
M^{*}
has point set
E\cup E^{\prime}\cup 2U_{n}
.

Observe that there is a basis of
M^{*}
consisting of a basis of
M
and the star

\{u_{i}+u_{n}\mid u_{i}\in U_{n}\setminus\{u_{n}\}\}
which contains
M_{Z}
. With respect to this basis, the fundamental cycle containing a point
e^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
is

C_{e^{\prime}}=\{e,e^{\prime},\}\cup M_{Z}
.
Every homomorphism
\tau\in\mCol(M,M(K_{4}))
can be viewed as a homomorphism
\tau:M\to N_{K_{4}}\subset N
. We extend such a homomorphism
\tau
to a map
s(\tau)
of
M^{*}
to
N
by letting it take
M_{K}
identically to
N_{K}
; that is, by setting
s(\tau)(u_{i}+u_{j})=v_{i}+v_{j}
.
As this defines
s(\tau)
on our basis of
M^{*}
, and so it extends linearly to a map
s(\tau)
from
E(M^{*})
to
N_{K}\subset N
.

Claim 5.4
.

For a homomorphism
\tau:M\to M(K_{4})
, the map
s(\tau):M^{*}\to N
is a homomorphism.

Proof.

The map
s(\tau)
is homomorphism on the submatroids
M
and
M_{K}
of
M^{*}
, which contain a basis of
M^{*}
, so extending linearly, this is a homomorphism as long as for any
e^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
the linear extension
s(\tau)(e^{\prime})=s(\tau)(e)+s(\tau)(N_{Z})=s(\tau)+v_{1}+v_{2}+v_{3}+v_{4}
is indeed an edge of
N
.
But
s(\tau)(e)
is in
2V_{4}
and so this is also in
2V_{4}
, and so is an edge of
N_{K_{4}}\subset N
.
∎

On the other hand, we have the following.

Claim 5.5
.

For a homomorphism
\sigma:M^{*}\to N
, the restriction
\sigma_{M}
of
\sigma
to
M
is a homomorphsim of
M
to some copy of
M(K_{4})
in
N

Proof.

By definition
M
contains a copy
M_{K}
of

which by

5.1
maps isomorphically to a copy of
M(K_{n})
in
N
,
and so we may assume that
\sigma(u_{i}+u_{j})=v_{i}+v_{j}
for all
u_{i}+u_{j}\in 2U_{n}=E(M_{K})
. Thus
\sigma(M_{Z})=v_{1}+v_{2}+v_{3}+v_{4}
. As
0=\sigma(C_{e^{\prime}})=\sigma(e)+\sigma(e^{\prime})+\sigma(M_{Z})
we get that
\sigma(e)+\sigma(e^{\prime})=v_{1}+v_{2}+v_{3}+v_{4}
, and so
\sigma(e)
and
\sigma(e^{\prime})
, being in
2V_{N}
, are both in
2V_{4}\subset E(N)
, as needed.

∎

To finish the proof we show that there is a path from
\tau
to
\tau^{\prime}
in
\mCol(M,M(K_{4}))
if and only if there is a path from
s(\tau)
to
s(\tau^{\prime})
in
\mCol(M^{*},N)
. As
\mRecol(M(K_{4}))
is
\PSPACE
-complete, this is enough to show that
\mRecol(N)
is too.

We use the graphic representation of

over

. The first direction is quite easy.

Lemma 5.6
.

If
\tau\sim\tau^{\prime}
in
\mCol(M,M(K_{4}))
then there is a path from

s(\tau)
to
s(\tau^{\prime})
in
\mCol(M^{*},N)

Proof.

Assume that
\tau\sim\tau
in
\mCol(M,M(K_{4}))
. So
\tau^{\prime}=\tau+(e_{i}+e_{j})\chi_{C}
for some

and some cocircuit

of

. We claim that

where

is a cocycle of

. Certainly it has even intersection with every cycle of

, and empty, so even, intersection with every cycle of
M_{K}
. Its intersection with the fundamental cycles

are also even, and so as we have verified it on a basis of the cycle space, its intersection with every cycle is even. Thus

is indeed a cocycle of

. It is then the disjoint union of cocircuits

and so by

to these cocircuits, one at a time.
∎

The other direction is a bit more work.

Claim 1
.

We may assume one of the following.

(1)

In proving

Question 6.1
.

Is
\mRecol(M(G))

\PSPACE
-complete for any graph

containing a

We have not done any thing for binary matroids

Is

Is

tractable? Is

Ideals generated by corner-interval minors
Marie Amalore Nambi

Abstract
. In this article, we study binomial ideals generated by an arbitrary collection of corner-interval 2 2 -minors of a generic matrix. We determine the minimal prime ideals of such ideals and characterize their radicality in the special case of corner minors. Moreover, we discuss connectivity properties of contingency tables in algebraic statistics. We compute the Hilbert-Poincaré polynomial of the ideal generated by the set of all corner-interval minors and we derive the formula for the regularity in the case of corner minors.

1.
Introduction

Let
M=(x_{ij})_{i=1,\ldots,m\atop j=1,\ldots,n}
be an
m\times n
matrix of indeterminates over a field
K
. The ideals generated by all
r
-minors of
M
, known as determinantal ideals of
M
, have been extensively studied from various perspectives, see the lecture notes and survey
[
5
,
4
]
. In recent past, motivated by applications in algebraic statistics, more specifically in the study of conditional independence ideals and the connectedness of contingency tables, researchers have been interested in the study of ideals generated by arbitrary subsets of
2
-minors of
M
, see
[
7
,
13
]
.

In combinatorial commutative algebra, when
2
-minors are associated with combinatorial objects, researchers are interested in studying the algebraic properties of the corresponding ideals through their underlying combinatorial structures. In this context, Herzog et al.
[
13
]
and independently Ohtani
[
18
]
introduced the notion of binomial edge ideal corresponding to a finite simple graph. The binomial edge ideal of a graph can be visualized as the ideal generated by some set of
2
-minors of a
2\times n
matrix. The authors showed that the binomial edge ideals of graphs are radical. Moreover, the authors characterized its primary decomposition. However, even when the matrix is extended to size
3\times n
, our understanding is limited, even a simple example shows that there are not radical.

In this paper, we focus on the ideal generated by
2
-minors that arise from an arbitrary subset of corner-interval minors. A corner-interval minor of
M
is a
2
-minor of the form
[a_{1},a_{2}|1,b_{2}]
, where
a_{1},a_{2}\in[m]
and
b_{2}\in[n]
. This includes corner minors, a class of
2
-minors introduced by Diaconis, Eisenbud, and Sturmfels in
[
7
]
. This class of ideals can be viewed as a natural generalization of the generalized binomial edge ideal of a star graph.

Rauh
[
21
]
introduced the generalized binomial edge ideal of graphs, while Ene et al.
[
10
]
extended it by introducing the binomial edge ideal of a pair of graphs. Both of these classes of ideals are natural extensions of binomial edge ideals, generalizing the setting from a
2\times n
matrix to an arbitrary
m\times n
matrix. Moreover, the authors studied Gröbner basis and characterzied radical and minimal primes. The significance of studying the generalized binomial edge ideal of graphs lies in its applications to algebraic statistics, see
[
21
]
. The ideal generated by adjacent
2
-minors of a matix
M
was introduced in
[
7
]
and their algebraic properties such as prime, radical and minimal prime ideals are studied in
[
15
,
14
]
. The ideal generated by all adjacent
2
-minors of
M
forms a lattice basis ideal. Moreover, the lattice ideal associated with this basis is the ideal generated by all
2
-minors of
M
(cf.
[
9
]
). The class of ideals of diagonal
2
-minors of a
n\times n
matrix is studied in
[
11
]
. Qureshi
[
19
]
associated a combinatorial object known as a polyomino with the ideal generated by its inner
2
-minors, referring to the resulting ideal as a polyomino ideal. The author proved that the ideals associated with convex polyominoes are prime. The characterization of the primality and radicality of the polyomino ideals is still open. The primary objective of this article is to study the minimal prime ideals and radicality of ideals generated by arbitrary collections of corner-interval minors of
M
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an arbitrary subset of corner-interval minors. The ideal

I(\mathcal{C})=(x_{a_{1}b_{1}}x_{a_{2}b_{2}}-x_{a_{1}b_{2}}x_{a_{2}b_{1}}\mid\text{ for every }[a_{1},a_{2}|b_{1},b_{2}]\in\mathcal{C})\subset S=K[x_{ij}\mid x_{ij}\in V(\mathcal{C})]

is called the binomial ideal of
\mathcal{C}
. One effective approach to studying the primality of the ideal
I(\mathcal{C})
is through its relationship with a toric ideal. In Section
3
, we construct a bipartite graph associated with
\mathcal{C}
and compare the ideal
I(\mathcal{C})
with the toric ideal of the edge ring of this graph. It turns out that the toric ideal is a minimal prime of
I(\mathcal{C})
that does not contain any variables, see Lemma
3.8
. Moreover, we give an explicit description of the minimal prime ideals of
I(\mathcal{C})
by introducing admissible sets, a purely combinatorial way, see Theorem
3.17
. In Section
4
, we study the special case where
\mathcal{C}
is a collection of arbitrary corner minors. We provide a characterization of the radicality of the ideal
I(\mathcal{C})
, see Corollary
4.6
. Moreover, we obtain a decomposition of the ideal
I(\mathcal{C})
, as shown in Proposition
4.7
. In the case where the ideal is radical, we further describe its primary decomposition. As an application, we describe when contingency tables are connected via corner minors of
\mathcal{C}
.

The second part of this article is dedicated to the study of the Hilbert-Poincaré series of the generalized binomial edge ideal of star graphs, as well as the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of the binomial edge ideal of a pair of star graphs. Several authors have found exact formulas or bounds for the regularity of the generalized binomial edge ideal of graphs, see
[
2
,
6
,
16
,
23
,
22
]
. However, the regularity of the binomial edge ideals of pair of graphs are not that much explored. In Section
5
, we obtain the regularity of the binomial ideal generated by the set of all corner minors of
M
, see Theorem
5.5
. In addition, we compute the Hilbert-Poincaré polynomial of the generalized binomial edge ideal of a star graph, see Theorem
5.3
. Finally, we obtain a lower bound for the regularity of powers of the ideal generated by an arbitrary set of
2
-minors of corner-intervals of
M
in terms of its subcorner-interval minors.

is called the star graph. We recall that the edge ideal of

2.2.
Commutative algebra

Let

The

of

\reg(M)\coloneqq\max\{j-i\mid\beta_{i,j}(M)\neq 0\}.

We recall the definition of Hilbert series as follows (cf.
[
8
]
). Let
H(M,i)
be the Hilbert function of
M
. The Hilbert-Poincare series of
S
-module

is

H_{M}(z)=\sum_{i\geq 0}H(M,i)z^{i}.

By the Hilbert–Serre theorem, the Hilbert-Poincare series of

is a rational function, we have

H_{M}(z)=\hp_{M}(z)/(1-z)^{d},

where

is the Krull dimension of

-module

and has the form
\hp_{M}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\sum_{j=0}^{p+r}(-1)^{i}\beta_{i,j}(M)z^{j}
, where

is the projective dimension of

.

In the following remark we recall a basic property of the Hilbert-poincaré polynomial and the regularity of an

-module.

I(\mathcal{C})+x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})+H^{2}+V^{2}:x_{11}=H+V+I_{2}(M_{2,2})
. It is clear that
H+V+I_{2}(M_{2,2})\subset I(\mathcal{C})+x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})+H^{2}+V^{2}:x_{11}
. For other inclusion, first we establish that the minimal generating set of
I(\mathcal{C})+x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})+H^{2}+V^{2}
form a Gröbner basis with respect to term order defined in Remark
5.2
.
From Remark
2.4
it follows that the minimal generating set of
H+V+I_{2}(M_{2,2})
form a Gröbner basis. Then, using [Proposition 15.12]
[
8
]
we get that
\ini_{<}(I(\mathcal{C})+x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})+H^{2}+V^{2}:x_{11})=\ini_{<}(H+V+I_{2}(M_{2,2}))
, and thus are equal. Thus, it is reduce to show that
S(f,g)
reduce to
0
for any
f,g
in the minimal generating set of
I(\mathcal{C})+x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})+H^{2}+V^{2}
. It is known that
S(f,g)
reduce to
0
if
\ini_{<}f
and
\ini_{<}g
are coprime or
f
and
g
are monomials. In the following we will show the remaining cases:

(1)

S(x_{i1}x_{1j}-x_{11}x_{ij},x_{i1}x_{1j^{\prime}}-x_{11}x_{ij^{\prime}})=x_{11}x_{ij^{\prime}}x_{1j}-x_{11}x_{ij}x_{1j^{\prime}}\in H^{2}
,

(2)

S(x_{i1}x_{1j}-x_{11}x_{ij},x_{i1}x_{j^{\prime}1})=x_{11}x_{ij}x_{j^{\prime}1}\in V^{2}
,

for any
i\in[m]
, and
j,j^{\prime}\in[n]
. Other pairs can be treated similarly. Note that the
S
-pair
S(x_{11}(x_{il}x_{kj}-x_{kl}x_{ij}),x_{11}(x_{i^{\prime}l^{\prime}}x_{k^{\prime}j^{\prime}}-x_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}x_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}))
reduce to
0
, since the minimal generating set
x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})
form a Gröbner basis (see Remark
2.4
). Likewise, the
S
-pair
S(x_{i1}x_{1j}-x_{11}x_{ij},x_{11}(x_{i^{\prime}l^{\prime}}x_{k^{\prime}j^{\prime}}-x_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}x_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}))
reduce to
0
, because the initial terms are coprime, as desired.

Then, from
[
16
, Proposition 3.3]
it follows that
\reg(S/(H+V+I_{2}(M_{2,2})))=\reg(S/(I(\mathcal{C})+x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})+H^{2}+V^{2}):x_{11})=\min\{m-2,n-2\}
.

Since
I(C)+x_{11}=(H\setminus x_{11})(V\setminus x_{11})
, it is easy to verify that
I(\mathcal{C})+x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})+H^{2}+V^{2}+x_{11}=x_{11}+(H\setminus x_{11}+V\setminus x_{11})^{2}
. Polarization of the ideal
(H\setminus x_{11})^{2}+(V\setminus x_{11})^{2}
is the edge ideal of graph obtained by attaching a whisker to each vertex of a complete graph on vertex set
\{H\setminus\{x_{11}\},V\setminus\{x_{11}\}\}
. Then from Remark
2.7
it follows that
\reg(S/(x_{11}+(H\setminus x_{11}+V\setminus x_{11})^{2}))=1
. Applying Remark
2.5
to the following short exact sequence

\begin{split}0\longrightarrow\frac{S}{I(\mathcal{C})+x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})+H^{2}+V^{2}:x_{11}}(-1)\longrightarrow\frac{S}{I(\mathcal{C})+x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})+H^{2}+V^{2}}\\
\longrightarrow\frac{S}{I(\mathcal{C})+x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})+H^{2}+V^{2}+x_{11}}\longrightarrow 0,\end{split}

yields
\reg(S/I(\mathcal{C})+x_{11}I_{2}(M_{2,2})+H^{2}+V^{2})=\min\{m-1,n-1\}
. Since we obtain the regularity of the middle and the last modules of Equation (
5.3
), again applying Remark
2.5
to Equation (
5.3
) one yields
\reg(S/I(\mathcal{C}))=\min\{m,n\}
, as desired.
∎

In the following proposition, we obtain a bound for the regularity of powers of the ideal generated by binomials that corresponds to an arbitrary collection of corner-interval minors in terms of its subcorner-interval minors.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an arbitrary set of corner-interval minors and let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}
be the maximal vertical interval of
\mathcal{C}
. If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be a subcorner-interval minors obtained by removing
H_{k}
from
\mathcal{C}
, for some
k
, then

\beta_{ij}(S/I(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})^{t})\leq\beta_{ij}(S/I(\mathcal{C})^{t})

for all
i,j\geq 0
and
t\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be a subcorner-interval minors of
\mathcal{C}
and
S_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}=K[x_{ij}\mid x_{ij}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}]
be a polynomial ring. First, we claim that
I(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})^{t}=I(\mathcal{C})^{t}\cap S_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
, for all
t\geq 1
, where
I(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
is the binomial ideal of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
in the ring
S_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
. Since generators of the ideal
I(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})^{t}
contained in the ideal
I(\mathcal{C})^{t}
, it follows that
I(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})^{t}\subseteq I(\mathcal{C})^{t}\cap S_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
. For the other inclusion, consider the map
\psi:S\rightarrow S_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
by setting
\psi(x_{ij})=0
if
x_{ij}\notin V(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
and
\psi(x_{ij})=x_{ij}
if
x_{ij}\in V(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
. Let
g=\sum_{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{t}\in\mathcal{C}}a_{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{t}}f_{\delta_{1}}\cdots f_{\delta_{t}}
be an element in
I(\mathcal{C})
, where
a_{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{t}}\in S
. Then, we have

\begin{split}\psi(g)&=\sum_{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{t}\in\mathcal{C}}\psi(a_{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{t}})\psi(f_{\delta_{1}}\cdots f_{\delta_{t}})\\
&=\sum_{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{t}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\psi(a_{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{t}})f_{\delta_{1}}\cdots f_{\delta_{t}}\in I(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}).\end{split}

Therefore, we have
I(\mathcal{C})\cap S_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\subseteq I(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
. Then the statement follows from
[
17
, Corollary 2.5]
that if
S_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}/I(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})^{t}
is an algebra retract of
{S}/{I(\mathcal{C})^{t}}
.
Consider,
{S_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}}/{I(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})^{t}}\xhookrightarrow{i}{S}/{I(\mathcal{C})^{t}}\xrightarrow{\bar{\psi}}{S_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}}/{I(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})^{t}}
, where
\bar{\psi}
is an induced by the map
\phi
. Then one can see that
\bar{\psi}\circ i
is identity on
{S_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}}/{I(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})^{t}}
as desired.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

The above result also holds when maximal vertical intervals are considered in place of maximal horizontal intervals. In this case, a lower bound can be obtained by applying Theorem
5.5
after removing the appropriate intervals.

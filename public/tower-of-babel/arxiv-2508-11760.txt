Semi-analytic studies of accretion disk and magnetic field geometry in M 87*
Saurabh ,, Maciek Wielgus ,, Arman Tursunov, Andrei P. Lobanov, Razieh Emami

Abstract
Context. Magnetic fields play a pivotal role in dynamics of black hole accretion flows and formation of relativistic jets. Observations by the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) provided unprecedented insights into accretion structures near black holes. Interpreting these observations requires a theoretical framework linking polarized emission to underlying system properties and magnetic field geometries. Aims. We investigate how system properties, particularly magnetic field geometry in the event horizon scale region, influence the structure of the observable synchrotron emission in M 87*. Specifically, we aim to quantify the sensitivity of observables used by the EHT to black hole spin, plasma dynamics, accretion disk thickness, and magnetic field geometry. Methods. We adopt a semi-analytic radiatively inefficient accretion flow model in Kerr spacetime. We vary magnetic field geometry, black hole spin, accretion disk dynamics, and geometric thickness of the disk. We perform general relativistic ray tracing with a full polarized radiative transfer to obtain synthetic images of M 87*. We extract EHT observables, such as disk diameter, asymmetry, and polarimetric metrics from synthetic models. We also consider a number of general relativistic magnetohydrodynamics simulations and compare them with the semi-analytical models. Results. We see limited impact of the disk thickness on observables. On the other hand, toroidal field dominated and poloidal field dominated magnetic configurations can be distinguished reliably. The flow dynamics, in particular presence of radial inflow, also significantly impacts the EHT observables. Conclusions. The M 87* system is most consistent with a poloidal magnetic field dominated flow with partially radial inflow. While the spin remain elusive, moderate or large positive values are preferred.

Introduction

How do magnetic fields govern the behavior of matter near black holes (BHs), and what role do they play in the formation of powerful jets and accretion flows in these extreme environments? This question remains central to our understanding of BH astrophysics. The interplay between magnetic fields and accretion flows significantly impacts the immediate vicinity of BHs and extends to broader implications for galaxy dynamics and the evolution of supermassive BHs in Active Galactic Nuclei
(
Blandford & Znajek 1977
;
Fabian 2012
, AGNs;)
.
At the event horizon scale, magnetic fields are thought to be crucial for angular momentum transport and energy dissipation within accretion flows, thereby influencing the accretion rate and playing a key role in launching and collimating relativistic jets
(
Kovalev et al. 2005
;
Beckwith et al. 2008
)
. These processes are governed by magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) principles
(
Balbus & Hawley 1991
;
McKinney et al. 2012
)
, with the structure and evolution of magnetic fields being key to deciphering the observed properties of BH systems, including their polarized emission.

It was not until the recent radio observations of the centers of galaxies Messier 87 (M 87*) and the Milky Way, Sagittarius A* (Sgr A*), by the Event Horizon Telescope
(
EHTC et al. 2019a
;
EHTC et al. 2019b
;
EHTC et al. 2022a
;
EHTC et al. 2022b
, EHT;)
and the near-infrared (NIR) observations by the GRAVITY instrument
(
GRAVITY Collaboration et al. 2018
)
that new avenues were opened to probe horizon scale properties of BHs. EHT observations of M 87* and Sgr A* revealed the presence of bright ring-like structures with substantial central-brightness depression, providing compelling evidence for the existence of supermassive compact objects in their cores
(
EHTC et al. 2019a
;
EHTC et al. 2022a
)
.
Subsequent analyses of the EHT observations yielded further insights into the physics of these systems, particularly through the study of polarized emission structures, constraining magnetic field geometry in the core region
(
EHTC et al. 2021a
;
EHTC et al. 2021b
;
EHTC et al. 2024a
;
EHTC et al. 2024b
)
. These observations revealed the presence of dynamically important magnetic fields that
may
plays a crucial role in launching and collimating relativistic jets.

In order to explain the observations and model the behavior of magnetized plasma in the strong gravitational field near BHs, General Relativistic Magnetohydrodynamics (GRMHD) simulations are employed
(
Gammie et al. 2003
;
McKinney & Gammie 2004
)
. These simulations have become an indispensable tool for investigating the intricate interplay between magnetic fields
(
McKinney et al. 2012
;
Gold et al. 2017
;
Ripperda et al. 2022
)
, accretion flows
(
Porth et al. 2019
;
Narayan et al. 2022
)
, and spacetime geometry
(
Mizuno et al. 2018
;
Olivares-Sánchez et al. 2024
;
Chatterjee et al. 2023a
;
Chatterjee et al. 2023b
)
, as well as their combined effects
(
EHTC et al. 2019a
;
Chatterjee et al. 2020
;
Nathanail et al. 2022
)
.

While GRMHD simulations offer the most physically self-consistent framework to study BH accretion, the inference based on these models remains contentious
(
Gralla 2021
;
Wielgus 2021
, e.g.,)
. Despite its successes as an effective model, GRMHD does solve a set of equations inadequate to describe collisionless plasma, assuming highly uncertain initial conditions. Furthermore, most of the results are obtained within the ideal MHD framework while the important role of non-ideal MHD effects, such as magnetic reconnection, in turbulent accretion flows near the event horizon is recognized
(
Ripperda et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. Global stationary semi-analytic approaches offer a complementary perspective by enabling parametric studies of accretion flows and magnetic field geometries. They allow for investigation of how the prescribed disk properties and magnetic field’s geometry impact observable properties, including polarization of the emitted radiation
(
Narayan & Yi 1995
;
Pu & Broderick 2018
)
. Such an approach constitutes a bridge between toy models useful for building intuition
(
Narayan et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
and computationally exhaustive GRMHD, where parameters of the accretion disk and magnetic field follow the evolution of the MHD equations and can not be modified explicitly.

A low luminosity AGN system of M 87* falls into a category of radiatively inefficient accretion flows
(
Yuan et al. 2003
, RIAFs;)
with a geometrically thick and optically thin accretion disk. Unlike thin disks, which efficiently cools down by blackbody radiation
(
Shakura & Sunyaev 1973
)
, RIAFs are characterized by low number densities and extremely high temperature ions, decoupled from that of the radiating electrons. Thus, radiative cooling is inefficient, and excess energy is removed from the system through advection. A stationary semi-analytic RIAF model framework for M 87* has been proposed
(
Broderick & Loeb 2009
;
Vincent et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
. In this paper, we systematically study the impact of the magnetic field configuration, BH spin, as well as geometric thickness and dynamics of the accretion disk on the observational appearance of RIAF models. We compare the RIAF results with GRMHD simulations, as well as with the characteristic observables extracted from the EHT data, including polarimetric quantities.

2.1
RIAF setup

We adopt a simple semi-analytic RIAF model of a Radiatively Inefficient Accretion Flow
(
Broderick et al. 2016
;
Pu & Broderick 2018
;
Vos et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. In this framework, rather than evolving conservative equations of MHD, we prescribe a stationary model of the accretion flow. In particular, we assume the number density of electrons
n_{\rm e}
and electron temperature
T_{\rm e}
to behave as power laws of the spherical radius,

\displaystyle n_{\rm e}

\displaystyle=n_{\rm e,0}\left(\frac{r}{r_{g}}\right)^{-\delta}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}(H\tan\theta)^{-2}\right],

(1)

\displaystyle T_{\rm e}

\displaystyle=T_{\rm e,0}\left(\frac{r}{r_{g}}\right)^{-\gamma},

(2)

for a gravitational radius
r_{g}=GM_{\rm BH}/c^{2}
and we use standard Boyer-Lindquist coordinate system
(t,r,\theta,\phi)
in Kerr spacetime of a rotating BH, parametrized with a dimensionless spin parameter
a_{*}

(
Kerr 1963
)
. Parameter
H
controls the geometric thickness of the disk
(
Pu & Broderick 2018
;
Vincent et al. 2022
)
. We take
H=0.5
as a fiducial geometrically thick model, and test additionally
H=0.1
and
H=0.3
. There are some observational handles on
\gamma
in RIAF sources based on radiative spectra
(
Broderick et al. 2011
, e.g.,)
and VLBI brightness temperatures
(
Wielgus et al. 2024
)
of Sgr A*, and hence we adopt a value of
\gamma=0.84
for our fiducial model, which is consistent with observational constraints. Our choices for all fiducial model parameters are given in Table
1
.

To model the four-velocity of the accretion flow, we follow
Pu et al. 2016
,
Tiede et al. 2020
and many others, interpolating between Keplerian orbital motion and geodesic free fall, hence

\displaystyle u^{r}

\displaystyle=u^{r}_{\rm K}+\kappa_{\rm ff}(u^{r}_{\rm ff}-u^{r}_{\rm K}),

(3)

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\rm K}+(1-\kappa_{\rm K})(\Omega_{\rm ff}-\Omega_{\rm K}),

(4)

where (
u^{r}_{\rm K},\Omega_{\rm K}
) and
(u^{r}_{\rm ff},\Omega_{\rm ff})
represent the radial four-velocity component
u^{r}
and angular velocity
\Omega=u^{\phi}/u^{t}
for Keplerian and for free fall motion with zero angular momentum, respectively. In the Keplerian case, fluid motion inside the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) is modeled as radial inspiral, following from the conservation of the ISCO values of energy
E=-u_{t}
and angular momentum
L=u_{\phi}
. We put
u^{\theta}=0
and obtain
u^{t}
from the four-velocity normalization. We assume
u^{r}<0
, that is, we do not allow for outflows (that is, no emission from the jets). Under the assumed parametrization, Keplerian motion corresponds to
(\kappa_{\rm ff},\kappa_{\rm K})=(0,1)
and free fall corresponds to
(\kappa_{\rm ff},\kappa_{\rm K})=(1,0)
. Fractional values of these parameters result in a mixed non-geodesic prescription, more appropriate for a model of a realistic astrophysical flow in M 87*, where sub-Keplerian azimuthal velocity profile, and presence of advection are expected
(
Takahashi & Mineshige 2011
, e.g.,)
. We consider
(\kappa_{\rm ff},\kappa_{\rm K})=(0,1)
,
(1,0)
and
(0.5,0.5)
, with the latter used as the fiducial RIAF model.

2.2
Magnetic field geometry

We consider several geometries of the magnetic field, embedded in the accretion flow described in Section
2.1
. These are typically defined through vector potential
\mathbf{A}
, thus enforcing the vanishing divergence of the magnetic field
\nabla_{i}B^{i}=0
. In our convention, Latin indices correspond to three spatial dimensions, while Greek ones span over all four spacetime dimensions.

We assume axisymmetric configuration, with the only non-zero component
A_{\phi}
, that is,
A_{r}=A_{\theta}=0
. The frame–dragging effect twists magnetic field lines and inevitably induces an electric field. As shown by
Komissarov 2004
;
Komissarov 2022
, such an induced electric field cannot be completely screened in the vicinity of a rotating BH — in particular, it is unscreened within the ergosphere. This is a general feature of this class of semi-analytic models, and we only focus on the magnetic field’s impact on the observables here. We consider the following seven geometries, illustrated in Figure
1
:

\star

Radial
(
Blandford & Znajek 1977
, split monopole, e.g.,)

A_{\phi,R}=1-|\cos{\theta}|,

(5)

\star

Vertical
(
Vos et al. 2022
, e.g.,)

A_{\phi,V}=r\sin{\theta},

(6)

\star

Dipole
(
McKinney & Blandford 2009
)

A_{\phi,D}=\frac{1}{2}((r+r_{0})^{\nu}\mathcal{F}_{-}+2\mathcal{F}_{+}(1-\ln(\mathcal{F}_{+}))),

(7)

where
\mathcal{F}_{-}=1-\cos^{\mu}{\theta}
,
\mathcal{F}_{+}=1+\cos^{\mu}{\theta}
,
\nu=3/4
,
\mu=4
, and
r_{0}=4r_{g}
,

\star

Quadrupole
(
McKinney & Blandford 2009
)

A_{\phi,Q}=A_{\phi,D}\cos{\theta},

(8)

\star

Parabolic
(

A_{\phi,P}=r^{k}(1-|\cos{\theta}|)=r^{k}A_{\phi,R},

(9)

where we set
k=0.75
and for
k=0
, this expression reduces to the radial magnetic field (split monopole),

\star

Combined field
(
Kenzhebayeva et al. 2024
)
. This solution combines two fundamental field configurations, namely the vertical homogeneous solution of
Wald 1974
and the radial (split-monopole), resulting in a paraboloidal field that is a a self-consistent solution to the Maxwell equations in Kerr spacetime,

A_{\phi,C}=g_{\phi\phi}+2a_{*}g_{t\phi}+p_{0}\frac{(r^{2}+a_{*}^{2})|\,\cos{\theta}\,|}{r^{2}+a_{*}^{2}\cos^{2}{\theta}},

(10)

where
p_{0}
is the ratio of the radial to vertical magnetic field components.

\star

Finally, for a toroidal configuration, it is straightforward to define a divergence-free magnetic field directly as

\ B^{r}=B^{\theta}=B^{t}=0,\ B^{\phi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\ ,

(11)

where
g
is the Kerr metric determinant.

For the configurations defined with a vector potential, with
A_{r}=A_{\theta}=0
, the lab-frame (static observer’s frame) magnetic field components can be defined simply as

B^{r}=\frac{\partial_{\theta}A_{\phi}}{\sqrt{-g}}\ \ ,\ \ B^{\theta}=-\frac{\partial_{r}A_{\phi}}{\sqrt{-g}}\ ,\ \ B^{\phi}=B^{t}=0\ .

(12)

We observe the asymptotic behavior of the magnetic field strength following these prescriptions to be
|B|\propto r^{-1}
for the vertical and toroidal configurations,
|B|\propto r^{k-2}
for the parabolic field, and
|B|\propto r^{-2}
for the case of radial field, dipole and quadrupole. The combined field asymptotes to a constant vertical magnetic field. Furthermore, dipole field is predominantly vertical in the equatorial plane, and quadrupole is predominantly radial in the equatorial plane, which is relevant for signatures of a geometrically thin disk.

Subsequently, we renormalize the magnetic fields by a constant factor, so that the magnetic field strength at the equatorial ring
(r=3r_{g},\theta=\pi/2)
is equal to
B_{0}=5
G, comparable to the EHT estimates
(
EHTC et al. 2021b
)
. In ideal magnetohydrodynamics the lab-frame magnetic field
B^{\mu}
is related to the fluid-frame magnetic field fourvector
b^{\mu}
as
(
Gammie et al. 2003
)

b^{t}=B^{i}u_{i}\ \ ,\ \ b^{i}=\frac{B^{i}+b^{t}u^{i}}{u^{t}}\ ,

(13)

for the fluid fourvelocity
u^{\mu}
, which we have defined in Section
2.1
. The Magnitude of
b^{\mu}
and its angle with respect to the momentum of the emitted photon
k^{\mu}
are then used to compute the intrinsic synchrotron emission, along with other radiative transfer parameters.

We notice that while the magnetic fields that we define are divergence-free by construction, we do not enforce the resulting configuration of
(b^{\mu},u^{\mu})
to be a stationary solution of the full induction equation in Kerr metric.

2.3
Emission and synthetic images

In order to perform the radiative transfer and ray trace the synthetic images of the source, we follow the numerical setup described in
Mościbrodzka & Gammie 2018
. At radio frequencies the emission from RIAF systems is dominated by the synchrotron radiation produced by hot electrons gyrating in the magnetic field
(
Yuan & Narayan 2014
)
. We assume a relativistic thermal (Maxwell-Jüttner) energy distribution of electrons and follow the fitting formulas of
Pandya et al. 2016
to compute the necessary coefficients of the fully polarized radiative transfer, including emission, absorption, and Faraday effects.

For the mass and distance of M 87* we assumed
M_{\rm BH}=6.4\times 10^{9}\rm{M}_{\odot}
and
D_{s}=16.9\,\rm{Mpc}
, respectively
(
Gebhardt et al. 2011
;
EHTC et al. 2019d
;
EHTC et al. 2025
)
. We set the observing frequency to
\nu_{\rm obs}=230\,
GHz, within the observing frequency band of the EHT
(
EHTC et al. 2019e
;
EHTC et al. 2019f
)
. We adopt an inclination angle
\theta_{i}
, calculated with respect to the angular momentum vector of the accretion disk rather than that of the BH, of
163^{\circ}
for the BH spins
a_{*}\geq 0
and inclination angle of
17^{\circ}
for
a_{*}<0
, thus the BH spin vector always points into the screen, away from the observer, see for example the left panel of Figure
2
. The position angle of the BH spin vector projected on the observer’s screen is subsequently aligned to
108^{\circ}
east of north (right panel of Figure
2
), ensuring the forward jet observed position angle of
\theta_{\mathrm{PA}}=288^{\circ}
east of north, consistent with the radio observations
(
Walker et al. 2018
)
. The dependence of the choice of the inclination on the BH spin follows the arguments of
EHTC et al. 2019c
, informed by the EHT image asymmetry.

We ray trace images in the field-of-view (FOV) =
200\,\mu
as, corresponding to about
40\,r_{g}/D_{s}
. In our idealized RIAF disk model there is negligible amount of emission in the wider FOV, similarly as in the images reconstructed by the EHT
(
EHTC et al. 2019b
;
EHTC et al. 2024c
)
. For each considered magnetic field model, accretion flow parameters
\kappa_{\rm ff}
,
\kappa_{\rm K}
, and disk thickness parameter
H
, we tune the number density scale
n_{\rm e,0}
and temperature scale
T_{\rm e,0}
to match the typically observed compact scale emission in M 87* of
\mathcal{I}_{\rm tot}=
0.5 Jy at 230 GHz
(
EHTC et al. 2019b
;
Wielgus et al. 2020
;
EHTC et al. 2024c
)
within 10% for the model with dimensionless spin
a_{*}=-0.94
. We keep the same normalization for other spin values, leading to flux density deviation from 0.5 Jy of up to a factor of 2. In all cases, our
n_{\rm e,0}
is within an order of magnitude from
10^{4}
cm
-3
, and
T_{\rm e,0}
is within an order of magnitude from
10^{11}
K, reasonably consistent with the EHT estimates
(
EHTC et al. 2021b
)
.

2.4
GRMHD images

For comparison purposes, we also consider time-averaged GRMHD images from the EHT 230 GHz images library
(
EHTC et al. 2021b
;
Wong et al. 2022
;
Dhruv et al. 2025
)
. In these simulations, plasma and magnetic field co-evolve following the conservative MHD equations in a curved Kerr spacetime, reaching a quasi-stationary turbulent state of accretion. We consider 10 simulations, computed for 5 different values of BH spin, and for two different states of magnetization, magnetically arrested disk (MAD), characterized by strong, coherent magnetic fields
(
Narayan et al. 2003
)
and weak, turbulent magnetic field solution, ‘standard and normal evolution’
(
Narayan et al. 2012
, SANE; )
. Each of these two states predict distinct dynamical outcomes and observable phenomena
(
Tchekhovskoy et al. 2011
;
Narayan et al. 2012
;
EHTC et al. 2019c
)
. The models differ primarily in the strength and configuration of the magnetic fields threading the accretion flow, leading to distinct predictions for accretion rates, jet power, and observable emission characteristics. In the MAD regime, the magnetic flux threading the BH accumulates until it reaches a saturation point, which results in a magnetically-dominated inner accretion flow. The accretion flow becomes highly non-axisymmetric, with low-density magnetic flux bundles alternating with high-density streams of gas
(
Igumenshchev 2008
)
and magnetic field lines exhibit a characteristic split-monopole configuration in the inner regions
(
Tchekhovskoy et al. 2011
)
. In contrast, the SANE model represents a weakly magnetized accretion flow where the accretion flow maintains a more axisymmetric structure compared to MAD
(
Narayan et al. 2012
)
. In this case the magnetic field lines exhibit a more tangled and turbulent configuration
(
Sądowski et al. 2014
)
.

For all models that we consider, the electron energy distribution is relativistic thermal, which is a common assumption for modeling emission at frequencies
\nu_{\rm obs}
relevant for this work
(
EHTC et al. 2019c
)
. However, while the electron temperature
T_{\rm e}
in our RIAF models is governed by a radial power law given by Eq.
2
, the GRMHD models prescribe
T_{\rm e}
as a function of the ion temperature
T_{\rm i}
and the plasma
\beta_{\rm p}
parameter, a local ratio of gas pressure to magnetic pressure. Both quantities are constrained within a GRMHD simulation, and the ion to electron temperature ratio is then computed with a parameter
R_{\rm h}
, following
Mościbrodzka et al. 2016

\frac{T_{\rm i}}{T_{\rm e}}=\frac{R_{\rm h}\beta_{\rm p}^{2}+1}{1+\beta_{\rm p}^{2}}\ .

(14)

Results of
EHTC et al. 2021b
,
EHTC et al. 2023
and
Joshi et al. 2024
hint at a preference for a large value of
R_{\rm h}
, hence we only consider a fixed value of
R_{\rm h}=160
, resulting in relatively cold electrons in the accretion disk region and, as a consequence, in stronger Faraday effects
(
Quataert & Gruzinov 2000
)
.

Methodology

We aim to make comparisons between our synthetic images of RIAF model and the 2017 M 87* observations of the EHT
(
EHTC et al. 2019a
;
EHTC et al. 2021a
)
. We discuss the angular diameter of the ring and its brightness asymmetry as total intensity image metrics. Furthermore, we consider polarimetric consistency metrics following the methodology of
EHTC et al. 2021b
. The summary of the EHT constraints that we consider is given in Table

.

3.1
Ring angular diameter

We extract the size of the ring from the images using the
REx
module in the
eht-imaging
2
2

2

(

EHTC et al. 2019b

REx

is estimated by the radial distance of the pixel with the peak intensity in the images. We blur the synthetic RIAF images with a 15

3.3
Polarimetric observables

We follow
EHTC et al. 2021a
;
EHTC et al. 2021b
to define the metrics quantifying polarization properties in both resolved and unresolved images. We denote flux densities integrated over the field of view solid angle
A
as
\mathcal{I}_{\rm tot}
for total intensity,

\mathcal{I}_{\rm tot}=\int_{A}\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}dA\\

(17)

and
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tot}
for the linear polarization,

\mathcal{P}_{\rm tot}=\int_{A}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}dA=\int_{A}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}+i\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\right)dA\ .

(18)

\mathcal{I}_{\rm tot}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tot}
are measured in jansky (Jy) while quantities with tilde refer to flux density per solid angle (brightness).
\mathcal{I}
,
\mathcal{Q}
,
\mathcal{U}
,
\mathcal{V}
are the four Stokes parameters, describing the observed state of polarized emission.
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tot}
is a complex number defining strength and orientation of the polarized emission. The net polarization fraction, corresponding to a coherent average of
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}
over the field of view and thus measurable from unresolved observations, is defined as

\lvert m\rvert_{\rm net}=\frac{\left|\mathcal{P}_{\rm tot}\right|}{\mathcal{I}_{\rm tot}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{I}_{\rm tot}}\left|\int_{A}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}dA\right|\ .

(19)

For resolved EHT images, we also define the incoherently averaged linear polarization fraction

\displaystyle\langle\lvert m\rvert\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{I}_{\rm tot}}\int_{A}\left|\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\right|dA\ .

(20)

This metric depends on the effective image resolution, and following
EHTC et al. 2021a
we compute it on images blurred with a 15
\mu
as circular Gaussian, to mimic the effective EHT resolution. Furthermore, we consider the second mode of the azimuthal (in the
\phi
angle direction) Fourier decomposition of the polarized image, denoted as the
\beta_{2}

(
Palumbo et al. 2020
)
,

\beta_{2}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{I}_{\rm tot}}\int_{A}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\,e^{-2i\phi}\,dA\ .

(21)

The
\beta_{2}
metric exploits the approximate polar symmetry of the BH image observed at low inclination
\theta_{i}
. The argument of this complex number characterizes the angle between the EVPA pattern and the BH image ring, while its absolute value informs about how rotationally symmetric the EVPA pattern is.

4
Results

We present a grid of RIAF model images in Figure
3
. These are calculated for the fiducial parameters given in Table
1
and incorporate various magnetic field geometries discussed in Section
2.2
and different values of BH spin
a_{*}
. All images exhibit a general ring morphology, with a prominent presence of a narrow photon ring
(
Johnson et al. 2020
;
Paugnat et al. 2022
)
, formed by photons executing at least half loop around the BH on their way to the observer’s screen. The reconstructed EHT images, achieving an effective resolution of
\sim 15\,\mu
as, generally blend this feature with the diffused direct emission, see Figure
2
. The morphology of RIAF images is very similar to that found in time-averaged GRMHD images shown in Figure
4
. One outstanding feature is the second smaller ring that can be seen in SANE
R_{\rm h}=160
,
a_{*}=0.94
, which is the model for which the jet base emission dominates over the disk emission
(
EHTC et al. 2019c
)
. Similar feature appears in the RIAF models for a dipole magnetic field configuration, where it is connected to the enhanced emission in the off-equatorial region of high density of magnetic field lines at intermediate values of
\theta
, see panel (c) of Figure
1
. Another apparent feature in the GRMHD images is the depolarization of the north-east part of the ring seen in many of the images. This effect has been attributed to the Faraday rotation scrambling the polarization along lightrays that spend a longer time in the accreting plasma
(
EHTC et al. 2021b
)
. The depolarization does not affect our fiducial RIAF images, which generally remain Faraday-thin. This is discussed further in Section
5
.

EHTC et al. 2021b
and
Narayan et al. 2021
discussed the relationship between the magnetic field geometry and the observable EVPA pattern of the accretion system modeled as a thin emission ring. Following a simple argument of perpendicularity between polarization vector
\mathbf{P}
, magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
and wave vector
\mathbf{k}
for synchrotron emission, expressed with a
\mathbf{P}\propto\mathbf{k}\times\mathbf{B}
relation, they concluded that toroidal magnetic fields produce radial EVPA pattern and poloidal magnetic fields generally produce azimuthal EVPA patterns. With images shown in Figure
3
we confirm that these conclusions hold well for a more realistic spatially extended global emission models and geometries, as long as the Faraday depth is low.

Ring angular diameter

The ring size
\alpha

\alpha

r_{g}/D_{s}
, which are of order of 10%. There is a weak dependence of the ring diameter on the BH spin seen both in RIAF and in GRMHD simulations, with low spin models forming slightly larger rings. The effect is related to both BH event horizon and ‘BH shadow’, in the sense of a trace of the photon shell
(
Bardeen et al. 1972
, e.g.,)
on the distant observer’s screen
(
Bardeen 1973
, the apparent boundary of)
, decreasing with the increase of
|a_{*}|
. It is notable that the sign of
a_{*}
only plays a secondary role for the image size. The magnetic field configuration, on the other hand, does not significantly impact the measured diameter for the fiducial RIAF model. The high spin SANE GRMHD model result in significantly lower measurement of the diameter as a consequence of the jet base emission. Thus, we conclude that the jet emission dominated models are discouraged under
r_{g}/D_{s}
priors and given the EHT observations
(
EHTC et al. 2019c
;
EHTC et al. 2021b
)
. Furthermore, in the first row of Figure
7
, we inspect the impact of the accretion flow velocity model on the normalized diameter
\alpha
. The Keplerian velocity models with a large positive spin show significantly decreased ring size. This is related to the size of the ISCO monotonically decreasing with BH spin in Kerr metric. In our Keplerian RIAF models the accretion flow velocity remains purely azimuthal above the ISCO, while below ISCO the developing radial inflow velocity component results in beaming emitted radiation toward the BH, hence the size of the image is sensitive to the location of the transition between the azimuthal and inflowing velocity regions. This effect persists for all considered magnetic field geometries and has been recognized before, by
Vincent et al. 2022
, among others. As a consequence, high positive spin models can only be consistent with the
r_{g}/D_{s}
prior with presence of inflow already above the ISCO. Finally, the geometric thickness of the disk does not impact the diameter strongly, as demonstrated in Figure
8
.

4.2
Brightness asymmetry

Figure 8:
Same as Figure
7
but comparing three values of the geometric thickness
H
, as defined in Section

.

The emission forming the 230 GHz M 87* ring is expected to originate from the extremely compact region near the BH event horizon. The EHT observations and their interpretation confirm that most of the observed intensity originates at around 4-5
r_{g}

(
EHTC et al. 2019c
)
. In this region, the impact of BH spin on photon trajectories is significant. Hence, brightness asymmetry
\mathcal{I}_{r}
of the M 87* image essentially originates from the interplay of two effects, special relativistic Doppler boosting associated with the fluid motion and general relativistic effects related to BH spin. These effects deform the null geodesics and affect the length of the path that is within the emission region
EHTC et al. 2019c
pointed out that for the GRMHD models in the EHT library it is the BH spin position that controls the image asymmetry direction, which is the origin of the convention for the image inclination dependent on the BH spin, see Section
2.3
. Following that convention, if the
\mathcal{I}_{r}
estimates presented in the right panel of Figure
5
are smaller than
\mathcal{I}_{r}=1
, then the brighter side of the image is the bottom one (
\mathcal{I}_{1}
defined in Figure
2
), consistent with the EHT observations. In particular, for a negative BH spin
\mathcal{I}_{r}<1
implies that the location of the brighter side is decided by the BH spin and is opposite to what would be observed if the BH was not spinning (in which case the Doppler boost would be the only cause of the brightness asymmetry). Indeed, for majority of our RIAF models we find
\mathcal{I}_{r}<1
for a negative BH spin. Nonetheless, some Keplerian velocity models with negative BH spin indicate
\mathcal{I}_{r}>1
and could be brought into consistency with the observations by the image position angle rotation, see Figure
7
. The Keplerian models are also generally most asymmetric, and their brightness asymmetry is driven predominantly by the Doppler effect. Many of these Keplerian models can be rejected based on excessive amount of asymmetry in comparison to the EHT images. On the other hand, we see far less asymmetry in RIAF images corresponding to radial infall, for which there is no Doppler effect associated with the azimuthal motion ( Figure
7
). As a consequence, the zero angular momentum radial inflow RIAF models are too symmetric and thus inconsistent with the observations for all magnetic field configurations and all BH spins.

With the exception of the negative spin MAD models, GRMHD images appear more asymmetric than the RIAF ones with the fiducial sub-Keplerian velocity pattern, see Figure
5
. To obtain the amount of asymmetry similar to the positive spin GRMHD within RIAF framework, more azimuthal velocity pattern and a more azimuthally aligned velocity pattern in conjunction with a toroidal magnetic field is required Figure
7
. A mixture of toroidal and poloidal magnetic fields are present in the GRMHD models, while the RIAF models that we consider in this work are either purely toroidal or purely poloidal.

Inspecting the dependence of
\mathcal{I}_{r}
on the BH spin
a_{*}
in Figures
5
-
7
, the brightness asymmetry generally increases with growing positive spin, as Doppler and spin effects act in the same direction in that case. For
a_{*}<0
the effects related to BH spin and the Doppler boosting act against one another, in most cases producing a too symmetric image, inconsistent with the observational constraints. Hence, the brightness asymmetry is constraining BH spin in a model-dependent way, giving a preference toward large positive values of
a_{*}
in fiducial RIAF models, and moderate positive values for GRMHD. The models with a small negative BH spin are disfavored in both frameworks. Similarly as in case of the ring diameter, the accretion disk thickness
H
has a very limited impact on the brightness asymmetry, Figure
8
.

4.3
Polarimetric quantities

For a low inclination angle
\theta_{i}
, a polarization pattern from an axisymmetric magnetic field averages out to a low value of
|m|_{\rm net}
. Many models, both GRMHD and RIAF, survive this observational constraint, see the first panel of Figure
6
. On the other hand, the resolved polarization fraction
\langle|m|\rangle
is always too large in our RIAF models, significantly larger than both observations and GRMHD models. Many SANE models are actually too depolarized, and only some of the MAD models for low or mildly negative BH spin match the observational constraints. The reason for the large
\langle|m|\rangle
in our RIAF models is likely low contribution from Faraday effects, causing significant depolarization both in GRMHD models and, presumably, in the EHT observations
(
EHTC et al. 2021b
)
. For similar physical reasons the absolute value of
\beta_{2}
is large for the circularly symmetric patterns in RIAF models, but much smaller in GRMHD images as well as in the EHT observations. Keplerian models, generally producing the least symmetric images (Section
4.2
), yield the lowest
|\beta_{2}|
. Nonetheless, the argument of
\beta_{2}
appears to be a powerful test of the magnetic field geometry, particularly for a distinction between the dominance of the azimuthal magnetic field component, yielding
\arg\beta_{2}\approx 0^{\circ}
, and various poloidal magnetic field configurations, for which
\arg\beta_{2}\approx 180^{\circ}
. These findings are also in agreement with the results obtained in
Emami et al. 2023
. GRMHD models reach values in between, which is expected from a mixed magnetic field configuration, see the fourth panel of Figure
6
. Keplerian RIAF models with poloidal magnetic fields exhibit a somewhat similar behavior, Figure
7
. Unlike GRMHD, these RIAF models do not obey the fluid four-velocity and magnetic field relation, constrained by the ideal MHD induction equation. The observed
\beta_{2}
angle is also in the intermediate range
(-163^{\circ},-127^{\circ})
, indicating either a mixed magnetic field geometry or an impact from Faraday rotation. For vertical magnetic field configurations we recover the trends discussed by
Palumbo 2025
and proposed as a test of a BH spin. However, while trends are there, the values obtained with a global RIAF model differ from predictions of a thin ring model used by
Palumbo 2025
, and a different magnetic field configuration may shift the values further or even reverse the trend direction. Finally, net EVPA, another quantity sensitive to the magnetic field geometry, was only weakly constrained in
EHTC et al. 2021a
, but broadly corresponds to the direction parallel to the jet (
\theta_{\rm PA}=-72^{\circ}
). We reproduce the observed EVPA with a number of RIAF models of various magnetic field configurations. However, GRMHD models that we consider, producing small positive net EVPA, are not consistent with the observations. In a very simplistic (although commonly used) framework vertical magnetic fields (parallel to the jet axis), would produce a perpendicular net EVPA at about 18
∘
, while azimuthal magnetic fields (perpendicular to the jet axis) would produce net EVPA around -72
∘
. This heuristic works reasonably well for infalling and sub-Keplerian RIAF models with azimuthal or vertical magnetic field structure (last panel of Figure
6
), however, both larger azimuthal velocity and more complicated magnetic field structure affect this simple picture in a non-trivial way (last row of Figure
7
). Furthermore, we ignored the potential impact of Faraday rotation. For M 87* rotation measure
\sim\,10^{5}
rad m
-2

(
Goddi et al. 2021
)
the argument of
\beta_{2}
can be affected by up to about 20
∘
, and EVPA by up to about 10
∘
.

4.4
Impact of geometric thickness of the disk

RIAF flows are expected to be very hot and as a consequence geometrically thick. Models often make a simplifying assumption of equatorial emission
(
Gralla et al. 2019
;
Paugnat et al. 2022
;
Urso et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. Here we test how much geometric thickness matters for a number of observables, summarizing results in Figure
8
. In most cases the geometric thickness does not impact results significantly. Hence, as long as the emission is disk-dominated (and not dominated by the off-equatorial jet base, for instance), the equatorial emission assumption provides a reasonable approximation of a more realistic thick disk. One exception is the dipolar magnetic field configuration, for which the geometrically thin disk is mostly threaded by a vertical magnetic field, but as the thickness increases, the emission region extends more into the off-equatorial radial magnetic field region, see Figure
1
. The effect is particularly large for polarimetric observables, which are more sensitive to the magnetic field geometry.

5
Discussion and Conclusions

We used a semi-analytic geometric RIAF disk model of black hole accretion to study the impact of the system parameters on the seven observables, polarimetric ones in particular, used for evaluating theoretical models of M 87*
(
EHTC et al. 2019c
;
EHTC et al. 2021b
)
. We considered five values of BH spin, seven distinct models of magnetic field geometry, three models of the plasma velocity pattern, and three values of the geometric thickness of the accretion flow. We compared our ray traced synthetic images with the EHT observations as well as with a subset of GRMHD simulations from the EHT M 87* library computed at the observing frequency of 230 GHz.

We aimed to verify what simplifying assumptions matter for the observables, and to what degree results obtained under simplifying assumptions can be interpreted quantitatively. We observed that the heuristic of radial polarization pattern implying azimuthal magnetic field structure and azimuthal polarization pattern implying poloidal magnetic fields generally holds for global accretion models, particularly for the flows with non-negligible inflow, as expected in RIAFs. The geometric thickness of the disk does not impact observables significantly in most cases, which could be an argument for the validity of conclusions obtained with a simplifying assumption of the equatorial emission. On the other hand, magnetic field geometry, plasma velocity profile, and BH spin all leave an important imprint on the resulting images.

While we adjusted parameters to match the observed M 87* flux density of
\sim
0.5 Jy, our models remain Faraday thin, which is not the case for GRMHD simulations. The observations also indicate appreciable Faraday depth through partial depolarization
(
EHTC et al. 2021a
;
EHTC et al. 2021b
)
. We further inspected this discrepancy, comparing our RIAF model setup to the GRMHD results in the EHT library, discussed by
Dhruv et al. 2025
. We found significant differences between the assumed RIAF structure discussed in Section
2.1
and predictions of GRMHD, such as a much steeper decrease in the gas temperature with radius seen in GRMHD. As a consequence, lower temperatures of the electrons in the emitting region could be a reason for the more pronounced Faraday effects seen in GRMHD
(
Quataert & Gruzinov 2000
)
. However, attempts to reproduce the appreciable Faraday depth by roughly matching the RIAF model properties to those of the averaged GRMHD were unsuccessful, which led us to a tentative conclusion that either non-axisymmetric turbulent flow structure or off-equatorial jet-base region plasma is responsible for the observed depolarization.

As a consequence of lack of depolarization, our RIAF models are all very symmetric, resulting in large amplitude of
\beta_{2}
and resolved polarization degree
\langle|m|\rangle
, inconsistent with the observations. Considering other remaining observables, majority of RIAF models violate at least some of those. Argument of
\beta_{2}
appears to be particularly constraining. One model that fares reasonably well against these constraints is the sub-Keplerian flow with a dipolar magnetic field configuration and spin of
a_{*}=0.5
. However, that is the solution visually disagreeing with the EHT image reconstruction with a presence of a second ring, see the discussion in Section
4
. More broadly, our results (particularly the argument of
\beta_{2}
) indicate dominance of poloidal magnetic fields in M 87*, and the relatively large image brightness asymmetry discourages zero or mildly negative BH spins. It is apparent that the EHT observations have a lot of constraining power over the accretion models. The model most consistent with the data would necessarily combine poloidal and toroidal magnetic field components, as well as azimuthal and radial velocity patterns.

We discussed impact of RIAF model parameters on the EHT observables. Some of the limitations of this work include fixed assumptions on the power law structure of the flow, and lack of mixed toroidal-poloidal magnetic field configurations. Fitting a more general RIAF model directly to the EHT data would constitute an interesting follow-up work. Feasibility of a similar approach was recently demonstrated by
SaraerToosi & Broderick 2025
.
Within the RIAF framework one could also test accretion disk configurations in non-Kerr spacetimes without resorting to computationally exhaustive covariant MHD numerical simulations, see
Vincent et al. 2021
or the recent developments from
Yin et al. 2025
.

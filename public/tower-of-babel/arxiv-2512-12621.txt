Existence and nonexistence results for a nonlocal isoperimetric problem on ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n}
Haizhong Li, Bo Yang

Abstract
. In Euclidean space ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , the minimization problem of a nonlocal isoperimetric functional with a competition between perimeter and a nonlocal term derived from the negative power of the distance function, has been extensively studied. In this paper, we investigate this nonlocal isoperimetric problem in hyperbolic space ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} , we prove that the geodesic balls are unique minimizers (up to hyperbolic isometries) for small volumes m m and obtain nonexistence results for large volumes m m under certain ranges of the exponent in the nonlocal term.

Introduction

Let
n\geq 2
,
0<\alpha<n
and
\gamma>0
. For any measurable set
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we consider the following functional:

(1.1)

\mathcal{E}(F)=P(F)+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(F),

where

(1.2)

P(F)=\sup\left\{\int_{F}\mathrm{div}_{g}X\,dV_{g}:X\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\mathrm{T}\mathbb{H}^{n}),|X|_{g}\leq 1\right\},

denotes the perimeter of
F
in the sense of De Giorgi and the second term
NL_{\alpha}(F)
is defined as

(1.3)

NL_{\alpha}(F)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}{\frac{\chi_{F}(x)\chi_{F}(y)}{{d_{g}(x,y)}^{\alpha}}}\,dV_{g}(x)dV_{g}(y),

where
\chi_{F}
denotes the characteristic function of
F
. Furthermore, in both (
1.2
) and (
1.3
),
g
denotes the metric of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and in this paper, all quantities with subscript
g
indicate that they are considered in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. In the following, we investigate the minimization problem

(1.4)

E(m)=\inf_{|F|_{g}=m}\mathcal{E}(F).

The minimization problem (
1.4
) of functional (
1.1
) has a physical background in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and has been extensively studied. The case where
\alpha=1
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is particularly significant, recalling Gamow’s liquid drop model for atomic nuclei. In this model, the nucleus is viewed as a region
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
densely packed with nucleons (protons and neutrons) at constant density, implying the nucleon count is proportional to the volume of
\Omega
. The perimeter term in the energy functional corresponds to the surface tension binding the nucleus and the second term represents the Coulomb repulsion between protons (here all physical constants have been normalized to 1 for simplicity). Then the minimization problem is to ask that whether stable atomic nuclei at arbitrarily high atomic numbers can exist in the framework of the classical liquid drop model of nuclear matter.

In the case of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the minimization problem of functional (
1.1
) is highly nontrivial since by the isoperimetric inequality, the perimeter term attains its minimum on balls for fixed volume, while the second term attains its maximum on balls by the Riesz’s rearrangement inequality (cf.
[
Leib-Loss01
, Theorem 3.7]
). For set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
measurable with volume
|F|=m
, we scale
F
by
F\to m^{\frac{1}{n}}U
, then
|U|=1
and using the scaling properties of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(1.5)

\mathcal{E}(F)=m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(P(U)+\gamma m^{\frac{n+1-\alpha}{n}}NL_{\alpha}(U)\right),

which suggests that the perimeter term is dominant if volume
m
is small and the nonlocal term
NL_{\alpha}(\cdot)
is dominant if volume
m
is large. Hence, we expect that there exist minimizers for small
m
and there exist no minimizers for large
m
. Indeed, we define the critical volume
m_{*}
to be the volume such that the value of the functional
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
of the ball with volume
m_{*}
equals that of two balls with both volume
m_{*}/2
, spaced infinitely far apart, i.e.,

\mathcal{E}\left(\left(\frac{m_{*}}{b_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}B\right)=2\mathcal{E}\left(\left(\frac{m_{*}}{2b_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}B\right),

where
B
denotes the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
b_{n}=|B|
. In the follwing of paper, we also denote
\omega_{n-1}:=|\partial B|
, which is the area of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that by coarea formula, we have
\omega_{n-1}=nb_{n}
. A direct calculation shows

(1.6)

m_{*}=\left(\frac{2^{\frac{1}{n}}-1}{1-2^{\frac{\alpha-n}{n}}}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{\gamma NL_{\alpha}(B)}\right)^{\frac{n}{n+1-\alpha}}b_{n}.

The significance of

is that for
m>m_{*}
, the value of the functional
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
of two balls with both volume
m/2
, spaced infinitely far apart is less than that of a ball with volume
m
. Moreover, it is conjectured that

Now we turn back to the case of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. In this situation, the perimeter term also attains its minimum on geodesic balls with fixed volume by the isoperimetric inequality, and the second term attains its maximum on geodesic balls with fixed volume by the Symmetrization Lemma (cf.
[

, Page 225]
). Hence the minimization problem (
1.4
) in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is also highly nontrivial and we expect that there are similar results in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
compared with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Before stating our main results, we make some measure-theoretic notations. We say that a function
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
has bounded variation, i.e.
u\in BV(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, if there holds

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}{|\nabla u|_{g}}\,dV_{g}:=\sup\left\{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}u\,\mathrm{div}_{g}X\,dV_{g}:X\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\mathrm{T}\mathbb{H}^{n}),|X|_{g}\leq 1\right\}<\infty.

For any measurable set
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we say that
F
has finite perimeter if its characteristic function
\chi_{F}\in BV(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and then its perimeter can be denoted as
P(F):=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}{|\nabla\chi_{F}|}\,dV_{g}
. The
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure with
k\in[0,n]
is denoted as
H^{k}(F)
.

For any
H^{n}
-measurable set
F
, its upper and lower densities at a point
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
are defined respectively by

(1.7)

\overline{D}(F,x):=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{|F\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}}{|B_{r}(x)|_{g}},\quad\underline{D}(F,x):=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{|F\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}}{|B_{r}(x)|_{g}},

where
B_{r}(x)\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the open geodesic ball with center
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and radius
r
. If the upper and lower densities are equal, their common value will be called the density of
F
at
x
, and it will be denoted by
D(F,x)
.

The essential interior
\mathring{F}^{M}
of
F
is then defined as the set of all
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
for which
D(F,x)=1
, while the essential closure
\bar{F}^{M}
of
F
is defined as the sets of all points where
\overline{D}(F,x)>0
. The essential boundary
\partial^{M}F
of F is defined as the set of points where
\overline{D}(F,x)>0
and
\overline{D}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus F,x)>0
. For any two sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we denote
A\Delta B:=(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)
as the symmetric difference of
A
and
B
, then by the Lebesgue–Besicovitch Differentiation Theorem (cf.
[
Evans15
, Theorem 1.32]
), we have

|F\Delta\mathring{F}^{M}|_{g}=|F\Delta\bar{F}^{M}|_{g}=0.

We also have that (cf.
[
Ambrosio01
, Page 46]
)

(1.8)

\partial^{M}F=\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus(\mathring{F}^{M}\cup(\mathring{F}^{c})^{M}).

The reduced boundary
\partial^{*}F
of a set of finite perimeter F is defined as a set of points
x\in\partial^{M}F
such that the measure-theoretic normal exists at
x
, i.e., if the following limit exists:

\nu_{F}(x):=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\int_{B_{r}(x)}{\nabla{\chi_{F}}\,dV_{g}}}{\int_{B_{r}(x)}{|\nabla{\chi_{F}}|_{g}\,dV_{g}}}\quad\text{and}\quad|\nu_{F}(x)|_{g}=1,

where
\nabla\chi_{F}
is the vector-valued Radon measure associated with the distributional derivative of
\chi_{F}
and
|\nabla{\chi_{F}}|_{g}
coincides with the
H^{n-1}
measure restricted to
\partial^{M}F
. If
F
is a set of finite perimeter, then by De Giorgi’s structure theorem (cf.
[
Ambrosio-Fusco-Pallara00
, Theorem 3.59]
,
[
Maggi-12
, Theorem 15.9]
), we have
P(F)=H^{n-1}(\partial^{*}F)
. Also, by a result of Federer (cf.
[
Ambrosio-Fusco-Pallara00
, Theorem 3.61]
), we have
\partial^{*}F\subset\partial^{M}F
and
H^{n-1}(\partial^{M}F\setminus\partial^{*}F)
=0.

Remark 1
.

For any set
A\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we can define the relative perimeter of
F
in

as
P(F;A):=|\nabla{\chi_{F}}|(A)=H^{n-1}(\partial^{*}F\cap A)
. We say that

is a set of locally finite perimeter, if for every compact set

, we have
P(F;K)<\infty
.

We say that a set

of finite perimeter is essentially bounded, if its essential closure
\bar{F}^{M}
is bounded. Also, we say
F
is decomposable, if there exists a partition
(A,B)
of

with both

and

, such that
P(F)=P(A)+P(B)
. Otherwise, we say that
F
is indecomposable.

All the measure-theoretic notations mentioned above suit the case of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Remark 4

Through a more refined analysis of the constants appearing in the estimates, we can further show that if

for two fixed

The proof of this theorem in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
differs significantly from that in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, scaling transformations possess particularly convenient geometric properties. In particular, they allow one to rescale the volume to an arbitrary prescribed value while preserving the shape of the underlying set, and geodesic balls are mapped to geodesic balls under such transformations. Consequently, the analysis of the shape of minimizers in the small-volume regime can be reduced to the study of the corresponding minimization problem for a fixed reference volume
b_{n}
, as presented in
[
Bonacini-Cristoferi14
,
Knupfer-Muratov13
,
Knupfer-Muratov14
,
Figalli15
]
.

However, such transformations are lacking in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Fortunately, after comparing several common models of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we have adopted the
\Phi_{\lambda}
-transformation (see §
2.1
for details) in the upper half-space model of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which has the advantage that by adjusting the value of parameter
\lambda
, the volume of any set in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
can be scaled to a given multiple. By means of the
\Phi_{\lambda}
-transformation, we can establish several fundamental properties of the minimizers, including their regularity, essential boundedness and indecomposability, and we can further deduce the existence of minimizers for all sufficiently small volumes
m\leq m_{0}
for some positive

depending only on
n,\alpha
and
\gamma
.

When proving that geodesic balls are the unique minimizers for small volumes, we encounter a new difficulty. Since our
\Phi_{\lambda}
-transformation cannot map geodesic balls to geodesic balls, the argument for a fixed volume of
b_{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is inapplicable. A key observation is that if the minimizer
E
satisfies
|E|_{g}=|B_{r}|_{g}=m
for

and some positive

E
is contained in a geodesic ball of radius

for some uniform constant

can be viewed as a radial graph over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with the radial function close to

provided the volume

is small enough. Then by means of proof by contradiction and Fuglede’s type estiamtes for the perimeter and the nonlocal term, we are able to prove that for small volumes
m
, geodesic balls are unique minimizers up to hyperbolic isometries.

On the other hand, by adopting similar ideas from
[
Knupfer-Muratov14
]
and leveraging the
\Phi_{\lambda}
-transformation, we can prove that the functional
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
does not admit a minimizer for large volume

with an extra assumption that

For all
n\geq 2
,

and

, there exists a positive constant

depending on

2.1.
Upper half-space model of
\mathbb{H}^{n}

The
n
-dimensional hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
can be viewed as
\mathbb{H}^{n}=(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},g)
, where
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
is the upper half-space and the metric
g=\frac{1}{x_{n}^{2}}\delta
. Here
\delta
denotes the canonical metric of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Usually, we denote this model as
U^{n}
, and call it the upper half-space model of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
Ratcliffe19
, Theorem 4.6.1]

For any two points
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in U^{n}
, the metric
d_{g}
on
U^{n}
satisfies

(2.1)

\cosh d_{g}(x,y)=1+\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2x_{n}y_{n}},

where
|x-y|
denotes the Euclidean distance between
x
and
y
.

For any fixed
\lambda>0
, we define the transformation
\Phi_{\lambda}
which maps
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
to
x^{\prime}:=\Phi_{\lambda}(x)=(x_{1}^{\prime},\dots,x_{n}^{\prime})
as follows:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}:U^{n}

\displaystyle\to U^{n},

(2.2)

\displaystyle(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n})

\displaystyle\mapsto(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},\lambda x_{n}).

Obviously,
\Phi_{\lambda}
is bijective with
\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}=\Phi_{{\lambda}^{-1}}
. In the rest of this subsection, we show some basic estimates about the transformation
\Phi_{\lambda}
which will be used in this paper.

Proposition 2.2
.

Given fixed
\lambda>0
and
x,y\in U^{n}
, there holds

(2.3)

\min\{{\lambda}^{-2},1\}d_{g}(x,y)\leq d_{g}(\Phi_{\lambda}(x),\Phi_{\lambda}(y))\leq\max\{{\lambda}^{-2},1\}d_{g}(x,y).

Proof.

Denote
x^{\prime}:=\Phi_{\lambda}(x)=(x_{1}^{\prime},\dots,x_{n}^{\prime})
and
y^{\prime}:=\Phi_{\lambda}(y)=(y_{1}^{\prime},\dots,y_{n}^{\prime})
. Then we have that
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
x_{n}^{\prime}=\lambda x_{n}
, so does

. Hence by (

), there holds

(2.4)

\cosh d_{g}(\Phi_{\lambda}(x),\Phi_{\lambda}(y))=\cosh d_{g}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=1+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(x_{i}-y_{i})^{2}+{\lambda}^{2}(x_{n}-y_{n})^{2}}{2{\lambda}^{2}x_{n}y_{n}}.

\bullet\,\,\bf{Case\,1.}
If
\lambda\geq 1
, then by (
2.4
), we obviously have

(2.5)

\cosh d_{g}(\Phi_{\lambda}(x),\Phi_{\lambda}(y))\leq 1+\frac{{\lambda}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(x_{i}-y_{i})^{2}+{\lambda}^{2}(x_{n}-y_{n})^{2}}{2{\lambda}^{2}x_{n}y_{n}}=\cosh d_{g}(x,y).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\cosh d_{g}(\Phi_{\lambda}(x),\Phi_{\lambda}(y))

\displaystyle\geq 1+\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2{\lambda}^{2}x_{n}y_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\lambda}^{2}}\cosh d_{g}(x,y)+1-\frac{1}{{\lambda}^{2}}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\geq\cosh\left(\frac{1}{{\lambda}^{2}}d_{g}(x,y)\right).

Indeed, if we let
a=\frac{1}{{\lambda}^{2}}\in(0,1]
and consider the function
f(t)=a\cosh t+1-a-\cosh(at)

) in the case of

\bullet\,\,\bf{Case\,2.}
If
0<\lambda<1
, we can argue as
Case 1
to get the estimate

d_{g}(x,y)\leq d_{g}(\Phi_{\lambda}(x),\Phi_{\lambda}(y))\leq\frac{1}{{\lambda}^{2}}d_{g}(x,y).

Combining the above two cases gives the proof of this proposition.

. Then the volume element of
U^{n}
at
x
satisfies

(2.9)

dV_{g}(x)=\frac{1}{x_{n}^{n}}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n},

where
dx:=dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}
is the volume element on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence using (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle|\Phi_{\lambda}(F)|_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Phi_{\lambda}(F)}{dV_{g}(x^{\prime})}=\int_{\Phi_{\lambda}(F)}{\frac{1}{(x_{n}^{\prime})^{n}}}\,dx_{1}^{\prime}\cdots dx_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1-n}\int_{F}{\frac{1}{x_{n}^{n}}}\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1-n}|F|_{g},

which gives (
2.7
).

To get (
2.8
), we firstly prove that
\partial^{M}\Phi_{\lambda}(F)=\Phi_{\lambda}(\partial^{M}F)
. For a general set
E\subset U^{n}
, it is obviously that
\Phi_{\lambda}(E)\cap B_{r}(x)=\Phi_{\lambda}(E\cap\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(B_{r}(x))
for any
x\in U^{n}
, which gives

(2.10)

\frac{|\Phi_{\lambda}(E)\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}}{|B_{r}(x)|_{g}}=\frac{|\Phi_{\lambda}(E\cap\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(B_{r}(x)))|_{g}}{|B_{r}(x)|_{g}}=\frac{|E\cap\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(B_{r}(x))|_{g}}{|\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(B_{r}(x))|_{g}}.

By
[
Ratcliffe19
, Theorem 4.6.4; Exercise 4.6.6]
, we see that the geodesic ball
B_{r}(x)
in
U^{n}
is actually an Euclidean ball contained in the upper half space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
with center
x(r)=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n}\cosh r)
and radius
x_{n}\sinh r
. Hence
\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(B_{r}(x))
is indeed an Euclidean ellipsoid which shrinks to the point
\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)
as
r
tends to 0. Since the (upper) density of a point keeps invariant regardless of the shape of the sets used to shrink to the point, as long as they are bounded and have bounded eccentricity, we deduce from (
2.10
) that the (upper) density of
\Phi_{\lambda}(E)
at the point
x
equals to that of
E
at the point
\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)
. If we take
E=F
and
E=U^{n}\setminus F
respectively, and notice that
\Phi_{\lambda}(U^{n}\setminus F)=U^{n}\setminus{\Phi_{\lambda}(F)}
, then using (
1.7
) and (
2.10
) gives
\partial^{M}\Phi_{\lambda}(F)=\Phi_{\lambda}(\partial^{M}F)
.

Finally, by Proposition
2.2
,
\Phi_{\lambda}
is a Lipschitz map with Lipschitz constant
\mathrm{Lip}\,\Phi_{\lambda}\leq\max\{{\lambda}^{-2},1\}
and since
F
is of finite perimeter, we have

\displaystyle P(\Phi_{\lambda}(F))

\displaystyle=H^{n-1}(\partial^{M}\Phi_{\lambda}(F))=H^{n-1}(\Phi_{\lambda}(\partial^{M}F))

\displaystyle\leq(\mathrm{Lip}\,\Phi_{\lambda})^{n-1}H^{n-1}(\partial^{M}F)

\displaystyle\leq\max\{{\lambda}^{2-2n},1\}P(F).

The left hand side of inequality (
2.8
) also holds since
F=\Phi_{{\lambda}^{-1}}(\Phi_{\lambda}(F))
and
\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}
is a Lipschitz map of
U^{n}
with Lipschitz constant
\mathrm{Lip}_{\lambda^{-1}}\leq\max\{\lambda^{2},1\}
by Proposition
2.2
. This completes of the proof of inequality (
2.8
).
∎

2.2.
Isoperimetric-type inequalities in
\mathbb{H}^{n}

In this subsection, we give some results concerning the isoperimetric-type inequalities in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, including the relative isoperimetric inequality, the quantitative isoperimetric inequality and an Euclidean-like isoperimetric inequality for measurable sets in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We begin with a simple property of concave functions which will be used in the proof of the relative isoperimetric inequality.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
\phi(x):[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is a
C^{2}
, concave function. Then for any
a,b>0
, we have

(2.11)

\phi(a)+\phi(b)\geq\phi(a+b)+\phi(0).

Moreover, if
\phi
is strictly concave, the inequality is strict.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
0<a\leq b
, then by Langrange’s mean value theorem, there exist
t_{1}\in(0,a)
and
t_{2}\in(b,a+b)
such that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\phi(a)-\phi(0)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\prime}(t_{1})a,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\phi(a+b)-\phi(b)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\prime}(t_{2})a.

Since
\phi
is
C^{2}
and concave, we have
\phi^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
and hence
\phi^{\prime}(t_{1})\geq\phi^{\prime}(t_{2})
, since
t_{1}<t_{2}
. This gives (
2.11
) obviously. Moreover, if
\phi
is strictly concave, then
\phi^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is not identically 0 on any interval of
[0,+\infty)
, which implies that
\phi^{\prime}(t_{1})>\phi^{\prime}(t_{2})
and hence the inequality in (
2.11
) is strict.
∎

In the rest of this paper, we denote
B_{r}
as the geodesic ball in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with radius
r
, if there is no need to mention where the center is. Let
\xi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be an increasing function (which we usually call the isoperimetric function) such that

(2.14)

P(B_{r})=\xi(|B_{r}|_{g}).

If we denote
g(r)=|B_{r}|_{g}
, then by the coarea formula, we have

(2.15)

g(r)=|B_{r}|_{g}=\int_{0}^{r}{P(B_{s})}\,ds=\omega_{n-1}\int_{0}^{r}{\sinh^{n-1}s}\,ds,

hence
g^{\prime}(r)=\omega_{n-1}\sinh^{n-1}r
. If we view
|B_{r}|_{g}
as a new variable
z
, since
g^{\prime}>0
, then the function
g
has an inverse
g^{-1}
with
r=g^{-1}(z)
. Moreover,

\frac{dg^{-1}(z)}{dz}=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}\sinh^{n-1}(g^{-1}(z))}

and

\xi(z)=P(B_{g^{-1}(z)})=\omega_{n-1}\sinh^{n-1}(g^{-1}(z)).

Combining the above two equations gives

(2.16)

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=(n-1)\omega_{n-1}\sinh^{n-2}(g^{-1}(z))\cosh(g^{-1}(z))\frac{dg^{-1}(z)}{dz}=(n-1)\coth(g^{-1}(z)),

(2.17)

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-1}{\sinh^{2}(g^{-1}(z))}\frac{dg^{-1}(z)}{dz}=-\frac{n-1}{\omega_{n-1}\sinh^{n+1}(g^{-1}(z))}<0,

from which we have the folllowing proposition:

Proposition 2.5
.

The isoperimetric function
\xi
defined in (
2.14
) is an increasing, strictly concave function on
[0,+\infty)
.

Remark 7
.

For any measurable set
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we have the isoperimetric inequality:
P(F)\geq\xi(|F|_{g})
, and equality holds if and only if
F
is a geodesic ball.

In
[
Maggi-12
, Proposition 12.37; Remark 12.38]
, Maggi proved the following relative isoperimetric inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
on an Euclidean ball:

Proposition 2.6

(Local perimeter bound on volume)
.

If
n\geq 2
,
t\in(0,1)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
, then there exists a positive constant
c(n,t)
such that

(2.18)

P(F;B_{r}(x))\geq c(n,t)|F\cap B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

for every set of locally finite perimeter
F
such that
|F\cap B_{r}(x)|\leq t|B_{r}(x)|
. Moreover, there exists a constant
c(n)
such that

(2.19)

P(F;B_{r}(x))\geq c(n)\min\{|F\cap B_{r}(x)|,|B_{r}(x)\setminus F|\}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

The two constants appeared in Proposition
2.6
do not depend on
r
, since the classical isoperimetric inequalitiy for sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has a nice explicit expression. However, the isoperimetric function
\xi
defined by (
2.14
) in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
can not be expressed explicitly in general, hence we can only prove an analogue result in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with constant depending on the radius
r
of the geodesic ball.

Proposition 2.7
.

If
n\geq 2
,
t\in(0,1)
,
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
0<r\leq r_{0}
for some
r_{0}>0
. Then there exists a positive constant
c(n,t,r_{0})
such that

(2.20)

P(F;B_{r}(x))\geq c(n,t,r_{0})\xi(|F\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}),

for every set of locally finite perimeter
F
such that
|F\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}\leq t|B_{r}(x)|_{g}
. Moreover, there exists a constant
c(n,r_{0})
such that

(2.21)

P(F;B_{r}(x))\geq c(n,r_{0})\xi(\min\{|F\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g},|B_{r}(x)\setminus F|_{g}\}).

Proof.

Since this proof is independent of the choice of
x
, hence we omit it in the following. By
[
Maggi-12
, Lemma 15.12]
, we have for a.e.
s>0
, there holds

\displaystyle P(F\cap B_{s})

\displaystyle=H^{n-1}(F\cap\partial B_{s})+P(F;B_{s}),

\displaystyle P(B_{s}\setminus F)

\displaystyle=H^{n-1}(\partial B_{s}\setminus F)+P(F;B_{s}).

If we denote
p(s):=P(F;B_{s})
and
h(s)=|F\cap B_{s}|_{g}
for simplicity, which are both continuous functions in
s
, by Remark
7
we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle\xi(h(s))\leq P(F\cap B_{s})

\displaystyle=H^{n-1}(F\cap\partial B_{s})+p(s),

(2.23)

\displaystyle\xi(|B_{s}|_{g}-h(s))\leq P(B_{s}\setminus F)

\displaystyle=H^{n-1}(\partial B_{s}\setminus F)+p(s).

If we add (
2.22
) and (
2.23
) together, and notice that
H^{n-1}(F\cap\partial B_{s})+H^{n-1}(\partial B_{s}\setminus F)=P(B_{s})=\xi(|B_{s}|_{g})
, then we have that

(2.24)

\xi(h(s))+\xi(|B_{s}|_{g}-h(s))\leq 2p(s)+\xi(|B_{s}|_{g})

holds for a.e.
s>0
. Since both sides of the inequality (
2.24
) are continous in
s
, so (
2.24
) actually holds for all
s>0
, and particularly it holds for
s=r
. If we set
h(r)=\beta|B_{r}|_{g}
, and hence
|B_{r}|_{g}-h(r)=(1-\beta)|B_{r}|_{g}
, then (
2.24
) now becomes (taking
s=r
)

(2.25)

2p(r)\geq\xi(\beta|B_{r}|_{g})+\xi((1-\beta)|B_{r}|_{g})-\xi(|B_{r}|_{g}),

where
0<\beta\leq t<1
and
0<r\leq r_{0}
. Define the function

(2.26)

\Psi(\beta,r)=\frac{\xi(\beta|B_{r}|_{g})+\xi((1-\beta)|B_{r}|_{g})-\xi(|B_{r}|_{g})}{\xi(\beta|B_{r}|_{g})}=1-\frac{\xi(|B_{r}|_{g})-\xi((1-\beta)|B_{r}|_{g})}{\xi(\beta|B_{r}|_{g})}.

By Lemma
2.4
and Proposition
2.5
, we have
\Psi(\beta,r)>0
. We next show that

(i)

For any
r_{*}>0
,
\lim_{(\beta,r)\to(0,r_{*}),\beta>0,r>0}\Psi(\beta,r)=1
;

(ii)

For any
\beta_{*}>0
,
\lim_{(\beta,r)\to(\beta_{*},0),\beta>0,r>0}\Psi(\beta,r)=1-\beta_{*}^{-\frac{n-1}{n}}[1-(1-\beta_{*})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}]
, and if we denote the right hand side of the limit as
k(\beta_{*})
, then
\lim_{\beta_{*}\to 0}k(\beta_{*})=1
and
k(\beta_{*})\geq 1-t^{-\frac{n-1}{n}}[1-(1-t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}]>0
for
\beta_{*}\in(0,t]
.

(iii)

\lim_{(\beta,r)\to(0,0),\beta>0,r>0}\Psi(\beta,r)=1
.

If we can prove the above three properties, then the function
\overline{\Psi}(\beta,r)
defined by

(2.27)

\overline{\Psi}(\beta,r)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Psi(\beta,r),\beta>0,r>0,\\
&k(\beta),\beta>0,r=0,\\
&1,\beta=0,r\geq 0\end{aligned}\right.

is continous for
0\leq\beta\leq t
and
0\leq r\leq r_{0}
, which in turn yields the existence of a constant
c_{0}(n,t,r_{0})
, such that
\overline{\Psi}(\beta,r)\geq c_{0}(n,t,r_{0})
and hence (
2.20
) holds with
c(n,t,r_{0})=\frac{1}{2}c_{0}(n,t,r_{0})
.

To prove (i), for any sequence
(\beta_{i},r_{i})\to(0,r_{*})
as
i\to\infty
, we have
\beta_{i}|B_{r_{i}}|_{g}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
, assume that
s_{i}
satisfies
|B_{s_{i}}|_{g}=\beta_{i}|B_{r_{i}}|_{g}
, then
s_{i}\to 0
and
|B_{s_{i}}|_{g}\sim b_{n}s_{i}^{n}
and
P(B_{s_{i}})\sim nb_{n}s_{i}^{n-1}
. Moreover,
s_{i}\sim\left(\frac{\beta_{i}}{b_{n}}|B_{r_{i}}|_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}
and hence

(2.28)

\xi(\beta_{i}|B_{r_{i}}|_{g})=P(B_{s_{i}})\sim nb_{n}s_{i}^{n-1}\sim nb_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(\beta_{i}|B_{r_{i}}|_{g}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

On the other hand, by the Taylor expansion, there holds

\displaystyle\xi((1-\beta_{i})|B_{r_{i}}|_{g})

\displaystyle=\xi(|B_{r_{i}}|_{g})-\beta_{i}|B_{r_{i}}|_{g}\xi^{\prime}(|B_{r_{i}}|_{g})+O(\beta_{i}^{2}|B_{r_{i}}|_{g}^{2})

(2.29)

\displaystyle=\xi(|B_{r_{i}}|_{g})-(n-1)\beta_{i}|B_{r_{i}}|_{g}\coth{r_{i}}+O(\beta_{i}^{2}|B_{r_{i}}|_{g}^{2}).

Combining (
2.26
) with (
2.28
) and (
2.29
) gives
\lim_{i\to\infty}\Psi(\beta_{i},r_{i})=1
.

To prove (ii), for any sequence
(\beta_{i},r_{i})\to(\beta_{*},0)
, we see that
|B_{r_{i}}|_{g}\sim b_{n}r_{i}^{n}
and
P(B_{r_{i}})\sim nb_{n}r_{i}^{n-1}
. Assume that
\beta_{i}|B_{r_{i}}|_{g}=|B_{s_{i}^{1}}|_{g}
and
(1-\beta_{i})|B_{r_{i}}|_{g}=|B_{s_{i}^{2}}|_{g}
, then as
i\to\infty
, we have
s_{i}^{1}\sim\beta_{i}^{\frac{1}{n}}r_{i}
and
s_{i}^{2}\sim(1-\beta_{i})^{\frac{1}{n}}r_{i}
. Hence

(2.30)

\displaystyle\xi(\beta_{i}|B_{r_{i}}|_{g})=P(B_{s_{i}^{1}})

\displaystyle\sim nb_{n}(s_{i}^{1})^{n-1}\sim nb_{n}\beta_{i}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}r_{i}^{n-1},

(2.31)

\displaystyle\xi((1-\beta_{i})|B_{r_{i}}|_{g})=P(B_{s_{i}^{2}})

\displaystyle\sim nb_{n}(s_{i}^{2})^{n-1}\sim nb_{n}(1-\beta_{i})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}r_{i}^{n-1}.

Combining (
2.26
) with (
2.30
) and (
2.31
) gives

(2.32)

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\Psi(\beta_{i},r_{i})

\displaystyle=1-\lim_{i\to\infty}\beta_{i}^{-\frac{n-1}{n}}[1-(1-\beta_{i})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}]

(2.33)

\displaystyle=1-\beta_{*}^{-\frac{n-1}{n}}[1-(1-\beta_{*})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}].

Using L’Hospital’s rule and after a direct calculation, we see that

(2.34)

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta_{*}\to 0}k(\beta_{*})

\displaystyle=1-\lim_{\beta_{*}\to 0}\left(\frac{\beta_{*}}{1-\beta_{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}=1,

(2.35)

\displaystyle k^{\prime}(\beta_{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n}\beta_{*}^{-2+\frac{1}{n}}[1-(1-\beta_{*})^{-\frac{1}{n}}]<0.

Hence
k(\beta_{*})
is a decreasing function in
\beta_{*}
, which implies that
k(\beta_{*})\geq k(t)>k(1)=0
. This completes the proof of (ii).

The proof of (iii) follows directly from (
2.32
) and (
2.34
).

Finally, to prove (
2.21
), we take
t=\frac{1}{2}
and notice that for any measurable set
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, either
|F\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}\leq\frac{1}{2}|B_{r}(x)|_{g}
or
|F^{c}\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}\leq\frac{1}{2}|B_{r}(x)|_{g}
. Then applying (
2.20
) to
F
or
F^{c}
with
t=\frac{1}{2}
gives (
2.21
).
∎

Next we introduce a quantitative isoperimetric inequality proved by Bögelein, Duzaar and Scheven
[
Bogelein15
]
. Given a measurable set
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
B_{r}
a geodesic ball with the same volume of
F
, i.e.,
|F|_{g}=|B_{r}|_{g}
. We define the isoperimetric gap
\mathbf{D}(F)
to be
\mathbf{D}(F):=P(F)-P(B_{r})
, which is nonnegative by the isoperimetric inequality. Also, we define the
hyperbolic Fraenkel asymmetry index
\bm{\upalpha}(F)
as follows:

(2.36)

\bm{\upalpha}(F)=\min_{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}|F\Delta B_{r}(x)|_{g}.

Theorem 2.8
.

For any
r_{0}>0
, there exists a constant
c^{\prime}(n,r_{0})
such that for any set
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
of finite perimeter with volume
|F|_{g}=|B_{r}|_{g}
for some
r\in(0,r_{0}]
. Then the following inequality holds:

(2.37)

\mathbf{D}(F):=P(F)-P(B_{r})\geq\frac{c^{\prime}(n,r_{0})}{\sinh^{n+1}r}\bm{\upalpha}^{2}(F).

Proof.

The proof of Theorem
2.8
follows directly from combining
[
Bogelein15
, Theorem 1.1]
with
[
Bogelein15
, Equation (1.6)]
.
∎

Remark 8
.

Indeed,
[
Bogelein15
, Theorem 1.1]
gives the inequality between
\mathbf{D}(F)
and the
L^{2}
-oscillation index
\bm{\beta}(F)
of the set
F
. Since inequality (
2.37
) is enough for our use, we omit the explicit definition of
\bm{\beta}(F)
here and readers can refer to
[
Bogelein15
, §2.6]
for further details.

In the end of this subsection, we give an Euclidean-like isoperimetric inequality for sets in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which is proved in
[
Bogelein15
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
r_{0}>0
. For any radius
r\in(0,r_{0}]
, we have the estimates

(2.38)

nb_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}|B_{r}|_{g}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq P(B_{r})\leq nb_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\left[\cosh{r_{0}}|B_{r}|_{g}\right]^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Remark 9
.

By Lemma
2.9
, the isoperimetric function
\xi
defined in (
2.14
) satsifies
\xi(z)\geq nb_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}z^{\frac{n-1}{n}}
. Combined with Remark
7
, there holds

(2.39)

P(F)\geq\xi(|F|_{g})=nb_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}|F|_{g}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

for any measurable set
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Also, under the same assumption of Proposition
2.7
, we can deduce from (
2.20
) and (
2.21
) respectively that

(2.40)

\displaystyle P(F;B_{r}(x))

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}(n,t,r_{0})|F\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

(2.41)

\displaystyle P(F;B_{r}(x))

\displaystyle\geq c_{2}(n,r_{0})\min\{|F\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g},|B_{r}(x)\setminus F|_{g}\}^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

where
c_{1}(n,t,r_{0})=nb_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}c(n,t,r_{0})
and
c_{2}(n,r_{0})=nb_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}c(n,r_{0})
.

2.3.
Quasiminimizer of the perimeter

In this subsection, we introduce two notions of quasiminimizer of the perimeter.

Definition 2.10
.

A set
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
is said to be an
(\omega,r_{0})
-minimizer for the perimeter, with
\omega>0
and
r_{0}>0
, if for every geodesic ball
B_{r}(x)
with
r\leq r_{0}
and for every set of finite perimeter
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that
E\Delta F\subset\subset B_{r}(x)
, we have

(2.42)

P(E)\leq P(F)+\omega|E\Delta F|_{g}.

Definition 2.11
.

A set
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
of finite perimeter is said to be a perimeter
(K,r_{1})
-quasiminimizer, with
r_{1}>0
and
K\geq 1
, if for every geodesic ball
B_{r}(x)
with
r\leq r_{1}
and for every set of finite perimeter
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that
E\Delta F\subset\subset B_{r}(x)
, we have

(2.43)

P(E;B_{r}(x))\leq KP(F;B_{r}(x)).

The following result was proved in
[
Kin13
, Theorem 5.2]
in the context of metric spaces, and the hyperbolic version was stated in
[
Bogelein15
, Theorem 5.1]
, hence we include it here for the convenience of readers.

Theorem 2.12
.

Suppose that
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a perimeter
(K,r_{1})
-quasiminimizer. Then, up to modifying
E
in a set of measure zero, the topological boundary of
E
coincides with the reduced boundary, i.e.,
\partial E=\partial^{*}E
. Moreover, both
E
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus E
are locally porous in the sense that there exists a constant
C_{0}>1
, depending only on
n
and
K
, such that for every
x\in\partial E
and
0<r<r_{1}
, there are points
y,z\in B_{r}(x)
for which

B_{r/C_{0}}(y)\subset E\,\,\text{and}\,\,B_{r/C_{0}}(z)\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus E

holds true.

For
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
measurable, we define the function
v_{F,\alpha}(\cdot)
to be

(2.44)

v_{F,\alpha}(x)=\int_{F}{\frac{1}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}}\,dV_{g}(y),

and hence
NL_{\alpha}(F)=\int_{F}{v_{F,\alpha}(x)}\,dV_{g}(x)
. We firstly prove two useful lemmas concerning
v_{F,\alpha}(\cdot)
and
NL_{\alpha}(\cdot)
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Given
0<\bar{m}<\infty
. If
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a measurable set with
|F|_{g}\leq\bar{m}
. Then for
\forall 0<\alpha<n
, there exists a constant
c_{3}
depending on
n,\bar{m}
and
\alpha
, such that
||v_{F,\alpha}(x)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq c_{3}
.

Proof.

By the Bathtub principle (cf.
[
Leib-Loss01
, Theorem 1.14]
), assume that
|F|_{g}=|B_{r}|_{g}
for some
r>0
, then

\displaystyle v_{F,\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{r}(x)}{\frac{1}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}}\,dV_{g}(y)

\displaystyle=nb_{n}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\sinh^{n-1}s}{s^{\alpha}}\,ds

(2.45)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{nb_{n}}{n-\alpha}\cosh^{n-1}(b_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n}}\bar{m}^{\frac{1}{n}})r^{n-\alpha}.

The second inequality is due to the fact that since
b_{n}r^{n}\leq|B_{r}|_{g}\leq\bar{m}
, we have
\sinh s\leq\cosh(b_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n}}\bar{m}^{\frac{1}{n}})\cdot s
for
0\leq s\leq r
. Also, (
2.45
) gives

(2.46)

v_{F,\alpha}(x)\leq\frac{nb_{n}^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{n-\alpha}\cosh^{n-1}(b_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n}}\bar{m}^{\frac{1}{n}}){\bar{m}}^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}:=c_{3}.

∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Given
0<\bar{m}<\infty
, if
E,F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
are two measurable sets with
|E|_{g},|F|_{g}\leq\bar{m}
, then for
\forall 0<\alpha<n
, there holds

(2.47)

|NL_{\alpha}(E)-NL_{\alpha}(F)|\leq 2c_{3}|E\Delta F|_{g},

where
c_{3}:=c_{3}(n,\bar{m},\alpha)
is the constant given in Lemma
2.13
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle NL_{\alpha}(E)-NL_{\alpha}(F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}{\left(\frac{\chi_{E}(x)(\chi_{E}(y)-\chi_{F}(y))}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}+\frac{\chi_{F}(y)(\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{F}(x))}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}\right)}dV_{g}(x)dV_{g}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{E\setminus F}(v_{E,\alpha}(x)+v_{F,\alpha}(x))\,dV_{g}(x)-\int_{F\setminus E}(v_{E,\alpha}(x)+v_{F,\alpha}(x))\,dV_{g}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E\Delta F}(v_{E,\alpha}(x)+v_{F,\alpha}(x))\,dV_{g}(x)

(2.48)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2c_{3}|E\Delta F|_{g},

which implies this lemma directly.
∎

Then we turn to prove that a minimizer of the minimization problem (
1.4
) is indeed an
(\omega,r_{0})
-minimizer for a suitable choice of
(\omega,r_{0})
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Assume that
E
is a minimizer to the minimization problem (
1.4
) with
|E|_{g}=m
. Then for any given
r_{0}>0
, there exists a suitable
\omega>0
such that
E
is an
(\omega,r_{0})
-minimizer for the perimeter.

Proof.

We firstly prove that there exists
\Lambda>0
such that
E
is a solution to the penalized problem

(2.49)

\min\{\mathcal{E}(F)+\Lambda\left||F|_{g}-m\right|:F\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\}.

Then it is sufficient to show that there exists
\Lambda>0
such that if
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
satisfies

\mathcal{E}(F)+\Lambda\left||F|_{g}-m\right|\leq\mathcal{E}(E),

then
|F|_{g}=m
. Assume by contradiction that there exists
\Lambda_{h}\to\infty
and
F_{h}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that

(2.50)

\mathcal{E}(F_{h})+\Lambda_{h}\left||F_{h}|_{g}-m\right|\leq\mathcal{E}(E)\quad\text{and}\quad|F_{h}|_{g}\neq m.

Using the upper-half space model
U^{n}
and the transformation
\Phi_{\lambda}
as described in §
2.1
, we construct a family of new sets
\{E_{h}\}
as follows:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda_{h}}:U^{n}

\displaystyle\to U^{n},

\displaystyle(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n})

\displaystyle\mapsto(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},\lambda_{h}x_{n}).

The constants
\{\lambda_{h}\}
are chosen to satsify
|E_{h}|_{g}=m
, hence by Proposition
2.3
, we have

\lambda_{h}=\left(\frac{|F_{h}|_{g}}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\neq 1.

Note that since
\Lambda_{h}\to\infty
, we have
\lambda_{h}\to 1
by (
2.50
). Next we consider the following two cases.

\bullet\,\,\bf{Case\,1}.
There exists a subsequence
\{\lambda_{h_{k}}\}\subset\{\lambda_{h}\}
with countably many elements such that
\lambda_{h_{k}}>1
and
\lambda_{h_{k}}\to 1
as
k\to\infty
. In this case, note that

(2.51)

\mathcal{E}(E_{h_{k}})=P(E_{h_{k}})+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(E_{h_{k}}).

By (
2.3
) and (
2.8
), we have the following estimates:

(2.52)

\displaystyle P(E_{h_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq P(F_{h_{k}}),

\displaystyle NL_{\alpha}(E_{{h_{k}}})

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{h_{k}}}\int_{E_{h_{k}}}{\frac{1}{(x_{n}^{\prime})^{n}}\frac{1}{(y_{n}^{\prime})^{n}}\frac{1}{d_{g}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})^{\alpha}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}}

(2.53)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{h_{k}}^{-2(n-\alpha-1)}NL_{\alpha}(F_{h_{k}}).

Combining (
2.51
) with (
2.52
) and (
2.53
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(E_{h_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq P(F_{h_{k}})+\gamma\lambda_{h_{k}}^{-2(n-\alpha-1)}NL_{\alpha}(F_{h_{k}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(F_{h_{k}})+\gamma(\lambda_{h_{k}}^{-2(n-\alpha-1)}-1)NL_{\alpha}(F_{h_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(E)+\gamma(\lambda_{h_{k}}^{-2(n-\alpha-1)}-1)NL_{\alpha}(F_{h_{k}})-\Lambda_{h_{k}}\left||F_{h_{k}}|_{g}-m\right|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(E)+\gamma(\lambda_{h_{k}}^{-2(n-\alpha-1)}-1)NL_{\alpha}(F_{h_{k}})-\Lambda_{h_{k}}(1-\lambda_{h_{k}}^{1-n})|F_{h_{k}}|_{g}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(E)+(1-\lambda_{h_{k}}^{1-n})|F_{h_{k}}|_{g}\left[\gamma\frac{\lambda_{h_{k}}^{-2(n-\alpha-1)}-1}{1-\lambda_{h_{k}}^{1-n}}\frac{NL_{\alpha}(F_{h_{k}})}{|F_{h_{k}}|_{g}}-\Lambda_{h_{k}}\right]

(2.54)

\displaystyle<\mathcal{E}(E),\quad\text{if $k$ is sufficiently large},

since

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}{\frac{\lambda_{h_{k}}^{-2(n-\alpha-1)}-1}{1-\lambda_{h_{k}}^{1-n}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(n-\alpha-1)}{n-1},

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}NL_{\alpha}(F_{h_{k}})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{F_{h_{k}}}{v_{F_{h_{k}},\alpha}}\,dV_{g}\leq c_{3}m,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}|F_{h_{k}}|_{g}

\displaystyle=m,\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\Lambda_{h_{k}}=\infty,

where the inequality is due to Lemma
2.13
. Then we get a contradiction with the assumption that
E
is a minimizer to the minimization problem (
1.4
) and hence that there exists
\Lambda>0
such that
E
is a solution to the penalized problem (
2.49
).

\bullet\,\,\bf{Case\,2.}
There exists a subsequence
\{\lambda_{h_{k}}\}\subset\{\lambda_{h}\}
with countably many elements such that
\lambda_{h_{k}}<1
and
\lambda_{h_{k}}\to 1
as
k\to\infty
. In this case, we can argue as
\bf{Case\,1}
to deduce that
E
is a solution to the penalized problem (
2.49
).

Combining the two cases above, given any
r_{0}>0
and for every geodesic ball
B_{r}(x)
with
r\leq r_{0}
and every set of finite perimeter
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that
E\Delta F\subset\subset B_{r}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle P(E)

\displaystyle\leq P(F)+\gamma(NL_{\alpha}(F)-NL_{\alpha}(E))+\Lambda\left||F|_{g}-m\right|

(2.55)

\displaystyle\leq P(F)+(2\gamma c_{3}+\Lambda)|E\Delta F|_{g},

where we have used the fact that
||F|_{g}-m|\leq|E\Delta F|_{g}
and Lemma
2.14
with
\bar{m}=m+|B_{r_{0}}|_{g}
. This completes the proof with
\omega=2\gamma c_{3}+\Lambda
for any given positive
r_{0}
.
∎

2.4.
Fuglede’s type estimates in
\mathbb{H}^{n}

In this subsection, we give Fuglede’s type estimates of
P(E)
and
NL_{\alpha}(E)
for a nearly spherical set
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which are crucial in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 2.16
.

For any
n\geq 2
,
0<\alpha<n
and
R_{0}>0
, there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
depending only on
n
and
R_{0}
, such that if
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a nearly spherical set with
|E|_{g}=|B_{r}|_{g}
for some
r\in(0,R_{0}]
, whose boundary is given by

(2.56)

\partial E=\{(x,r(1+u(x))):x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}

under the geodesic polar coordinate, where
u:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{1}
function, with
||u||_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. Then we have the estimate

(2.57)

NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})-NL_{\alpha}(E)\leq L_{0}{\sinh}^{2n-\alpha}r\left([u]^{2}_{\frac{\alpha+1-n}{2}}+||u||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\right)

for some constant
L_{0}>0
depending only on
n,\alpha
and
R_{0}
. Here
[u]^{2}_{\frac{\alpha+1-n}{2}}
is defined by

[u]^{2}_{\frac{\alpha+1-n}{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\,d\sigma_{x}\,d\sigma_{y}.

Proof.

We slightly change the notation, we assume that
|E_{t}|_{g}=|B_{r}|_{g}
, where

\partial E_{t}=\{(x,r(1+tu(x))):x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\},

which satisfies
||u||_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
t\in(0,2\varepsilon_{0}]
. Then for any two points
\tilde{x}=(x,\rho)
and
\tilde{y}=(y,s)
under the geodesic polar coordinate, the distance
d_{g}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
satisfies

(2.58)

\cosh d_{g}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=\cosh\rho\cosh s-\sinh\rho\sinh s\cos\beta,

where we have used the hyperbolic cosine law and
\beta
is the angle between
x
and
y
,
which satisfies

(2.59)

\cos\beta=\frac{2-|x-y|^{2}}{2}.

Combining (
2.58
) with (
2.59
), we have

\displaystyle\cosh d_{g}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})

\displaystyle=\cosh\rho\cosh s-\sinh\rho\sinh s+\sinh\rho\sinh s(1-\cos\beta)

\displaystyle=\cosh(\rho-s)+\frac{\sinh\rho\sinh s}{2}|x-y|^{2},

which gives

d_{g}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=\mathrm{arccosh}\left[\cosh(\rho-s)+\frac{\sinh\rho\sinh s}{2}|x-y|^{2}\right].

If we define the function
f_{\theta}(\rho,s)
as

(2.60)

f_{\theta}(\rho,s)=\frac{\sinh^{n-1}\rho\sinh^{n-1}s}{\mathrm{arccosh}^{\alpha}\left[\cosh(\rho-s)+\frac{\sinh\rho\sinh s}{2}{\theta}^{2}\right]}.

Then
NL_{\alpha}(E_{t})
can be expressed by

(2.61)

NL_{\alpha}(E_{t})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r(1+tu(x))}\int_{0}^{r(1+tu(y))}{f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)}\,d\rho\,ds.

By exploiting the identity

2\int_{0}^{a}\int_{0}^{b}=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{0}^{a}+\int_{0}^{b}\int_{0}^{b}-\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{b},

we deduce that

\displaystyle NL_{\alpha}(E_{t})=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r(1+tu(x))}\int_{0}^{r(1+tu(x))}{f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)}\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{r(1+tu(y))}^{r(1+tu(x))}\int_{r(1+tu(y))}^{r(1+tu(x))}{f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)}\,d\rho\,ds

(2.62)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I-II.

By taking
u=0
in (
2.62
), we have

(2.63)

NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}{f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)}\,d\rho\,ds.

Combining (
2.62
) with (
2.63
) gives

(2.64)

NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})-NL_{\alpha}(E_{t})=NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})-I+\frac{t^{2}r^{2}}{2}h(t),

where
h(t)
is defined as

(2.65)

h(t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{u(y)}^{u(x)}\int_{u(y)}^{u(x)}{f_{|x-y|}(r(1+t\rho),r(1+ts))}\,d\rho\,ds.

By Lemma
A.2
and
A.3
, there exists two constants
L^{\prime}
and
L^{\prime\prime}
depending on
n,\alpha
and
R_{0}
, such that

(2.66)

\displaystyle NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})-I\leq t^{2}L^{\prime}\sinh^{2n-\alpha}r||u||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})},

(2.67)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial f_{|x-y|}(r(1+\tau\rho),r(1+\tau s))}{\partial\tau}\right|\leq L^{\prime\prime}f_{|x-y|}(r,r),\,\,\forall\tau\in(0,t).

From (
2.67
) we have

(2.68)

|h^{\prime}(\tau)|\leq L^{\prime\prime}h(0),\,\,\forall\tau\in(0,t).

Next we estimate
h(0)
. Let
w=\mathrm{arccosh}\left[1+\frac{\sinh^{2}r}{2}|x-y|^{2}\right]
, i.e.
\cosh w=1+\frac{\sinh^{2}r}{2}|x-y|^{2}
. Since
|x-y|\leq 2
,
r\leq R_{0}
, there exists
L^{\prime\prime\prime}(R_{0})
, such that
\cosh w\leq 1+\frac{L^{\prime\prime\prime}}{2}w^{2}
, which implies

(2.69)

w\geq\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{L^{\prime\prime\prime}}}|x-y|.

Combining (
2.65
)-(
2.69
), we get

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+tL^{\prime\prime}\right)h(0)

\displaystyle=(1+tL^{\prime\prime})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{u(y)}^{u(x)}\int_{u(y)}^{u(x)}{f_{|x-y|}(r,r)}\,d\rho\,ds

(2.70)

\displaystyle\leq(1+tL^{\prime\prime})(L^{\prime\prime\prime})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\sinh^{2n-2-\alpha}r[u]^{2}_{\frac{\alpha+1-n}{2}}.

Then combining (
2.64
), (
2.66
) and (
2.70
) gives

(2.71)

NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})-NL_{\alpha}(E_{t})\leq t^{2}\sinh^{2n-\alpha}r\left[L^{\prime}||u||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}+\frac{1+tL^{\prime\prime}}{2}(L^{\prime\prime\prime})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}[u]^{2}_{\frac{\alpha+1-n}{2}}\right],

which obviously implies the announced result.
∎

.

, hence (

) also implies that there exists a constant
L_{1}
, depending only on

,

and

, such that

(2.72)

NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})-NL_{\alpha}(E)\leq L_{1}\sinh^{2n-\alpha}r||u||^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.

.

(2.73)

.

3.
Basic properties of minimizers

Assume that
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a minimizer solving the minimization problem (
1.4
) with
|E|_{g}=m
. In this section, we prove that
\partial^{*}E
is
C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}
,
E
is essentially bounded, indecomposable and admits a uniform lower density bound.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a minimizer solving the minimization problem (
1.4
) with
|E|_{g}=m
. Then the reduced boundary
\partial^{*}E
is a
C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}
manifold and the Hausdorff dimension of the singular set satisfies
\text{dim}_{H}(\partial E\setminus\partial^{*}E)\leq n-8
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.15
, we see that
E
is an
(\omega,r_{0})
-minimizer for the perimeter for two positive finite constants
\omega
and
r_{0}
. Then we can apply the result of
[
Tamanini82
, Thorem 1]
to get the desired regularity and the Hausdorff dimension of the singular set.
∎

It is well-known that the
(\omega,r_{0})
-minimizer satisfies the upper and lower density estimates. We state it in the following theorem, whose proof is a direct adaptation of
[
Caraballo11
, Theorem 6,(i),(iii)]
and
[
Maggi-12
, Theorem 21.11]
from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{H}^{n}
case due to the local nature.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
is an
(\omega,r_{0})
-minimizer for the perimeter in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, then there exists a constant
r^{\prime}\in(0,r_{0})
, such that for any
0<r\leq r^{\prime}
and
x\in\partial F
, there holds

(3.1)

0<c_{4}\leq\frac{|F\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}}{|B_{r}(x)|_{g}}\leq 1-c_{4}<1

for some constant
c_{4}\in(0,1)
independent of
x
. Moreover, both
\mathring{F}^{M}
and
(\mathring{F}^{c})^{M}
are open.

We also present the relationship between
\partial^{M}F
,
\partial\mathring{F}^{M}
,
\partial^{*}F
and
\partial F
for a set of finite perimeter
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter, then we have

(3.2)

\partial^{*}F\subset\partial^{M}F\subset\partial\mathring{F}^{M}=\overline{\partial^{*}F}\subset\partial F.

Moreover, if
F
satisfies the density estimate (
3.1
), then

(3.3)

\partial^{*}F\subset\partial^{M}F=\partial\mathring{F}^{M}=\overline{\partial^{*}F}\subset\partial F.

Proof.

The proof of
\partial^{*}F\subset\partial^{M}F
can be found in
[
Ambrosio-Fusco-Pallara00
, Theorem 3.61]
, and
\partial\mathring{F}^{M}=\overline{\partial^{*}F}
can be found in
[
Caraballo11
, Theorem 10]
. By the definition of
\partial^{M}F
, we see that for
\forall x\in\partial^{M}F
and
\forall r>0
,

\displaystyle|B_{r}(x)\cap F|_{g}

\displaystyle=|B_{r}(x)\cap\mathring{F}^{M}|_{g}>0,

\displaystyle|B_{r}(x)\cap F^{c}|_{g}

\displaystyle=|B_{r}(x)\cap(\mathring{F}^{c})^{M}|_{g}>0,

which implies that
x\in\partial\mathring{F}^{M}
and hence
\partial^{M}F\subset\partial\mathring{F}^{M}
.
\overline{\partial^{*}F}\subset\partial F
follows from the fact that
\partial^{*}F\subset\partial F
and
\partial F
is closed. This completes the proof of (
3.2
).

If
F
satisfies the density estimate (
3.1
), then by (
1.8
) and Theorem
3.2
, the set
\partial^{M}F
is closed. Hence we have
\overline{\partial^{*}F}\subset\partial^{M}F
, which gives (
3.3
).
∎

Then we can prove that a minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
is essentially bounded and indecomposable.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
solves the minimization problem (
1.4
) with
|E|_{g}=m
. Then
E
is essentially bounded and indecomposable.

Proof.

Firstly, we prove that
E
is essentially bounded. By Proposition
2.15
and Theorem
3.2
, we find that there exists
r>0
such that for
\forall x\in\partial E
, we have

(3.4)

|E\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}\geq c_{4}|B_{r}(x)|_{g}:=c_{4}|B_{r}|_{g}

for some constant
c_{4}
independent of
x
. Then we argue by contradiction. Assume that there exists a sequence of points
\{x_{n}\}\subset\bar{E}^{M}
, such that
d_{g}(x_{n},O)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, where
O\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a fixed point. Without loss of generality, we assume that
d_{g}(x_{n},x_{m})>4r
and hence
B_{r}(x_{n})\cap B_{r}(x_{m})=\emptyset
for
n\neq m
. By the definition of
\partial^{M}E
, we see that for any
n
,

(3.5)

|B_{r}(x_{n})\cap\bar{E}^{M}|_{g}>0,\quad\text{and}\quad|B_{r}(x_{n})\setminus\bar{E}^{M}|_{g}>0.

This implies that there exists
y_{n}\in\partial^{M}E\cap B_{r}(x_{n})
for any
n
, since otherwise by (
1.8
), we have

B_{r}(x_{n})=\left(B_{r}(x_{n})\cap\mathring{E}^{M}\right)\cup\left(B_{r}(x_{n})\cap(\mathring{E}^{c})^{M}\right),

which says that the geodesic ball
B_{r}(x_{n})
is the union of two non-empty disjoint open sets by Proposition
2.15
and Theorem
3.2
, contradicting the connectedness of
B_{r}(x_{n})
. By Lemma
3.3
, we have
\partial^{M}E=\partial\mathring{E}^{M}=\overline{\partial^{*}E}\subset\partial E
, hence the points
\{y_{n}\}
satisfy the lower density bound (
3.4
), and then we have

m=|E|_{g}\geq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|E\cap B_{r}(y_{n})|_{g}\geq c_{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|B_{r}|_{g}=\infty,

which is a contradiction and the essentially boundedness of
E
is proved.

Next we prove that
E
is indecomposable. We assume by contradiction that if
E
is decomposable, i.e. there exists a partition
(E_{1},E_{2})
of
E
with both
|E_{1}|_{g}>0
and
|E_{2}|_{g}>0
, such that
P(E)=P(E_{1})+P(E_{2})
. Take
\{\tau_{h}\}
be a family of isometries in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which satisfies

(3.6)

d_{g}(E_{1},\tau_{h}E_{2})\to\infty,\quad\text{as}\quad h\to\infty.

Since

is essentially bounded, hence
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are also essentially bounded. Take

, then for

sufficiently large, we have

\displaystyle P(\bar{E}_{h})

\displaystyle=P(E_{1})+P(\tau_{h}E_{2})=P(E_{1})+P(E_{2})=P(E),

\displaystyle|\bar{E}_{h}|_{g}

\displaystyle=|E_{1}|_{g}+|\tau_{h}E_{2}|_{g}=|E_{1}|_{g}+|E_{2}|_{g}=|E|_{g},

and hence

\displaystyle\liminf_{h\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(\bar{E}_{h})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{h\to\infty}(P(\bar{E}_{h})+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(\bar{E}_{h}))

\displaystyle=P(E)+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(E_{1})+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(E_{2})

\displaystyle<P(E)+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(E_{1})+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(E_{2})+2\gamma\int_{E_{1}}\int_{E_{2}}{\frac{1}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}\,dV_{g}(x)\,dV_{g}(y)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(E),

which implies that there exists some sufficiently large

, such that

|\bar{E}_{h_{0}}|_{g}=|E|_{g},\quad{\mathcal{E}}(\bar{E}_{h_{0}})<\mathcal{E}(E).

This contradicts the fact that
E
is a minimizer of the functonal
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
with volume

, hence
E
is indecomposable.
∎

Let
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter with
|F|_{g}=m
. Denote
\bar{r}=\bar{r}(n)
to be radius such that

. Then for

to be the collection of

(cf. §

). The number

is chosen to be the smallest integer for which the volume of each ball does not exceed 1 and such that

by (

), and hence the

are disjoint from each other.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter with
|F|_{g}=m
. Then we have

(3.8)

\limsup_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{E}({\widehat{F}_{R}})\leq c_{5}\max\{m,m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\},

where

is a constant depending on
n,\alpha
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

Denote the geodesic ball centered at
x_{k}
as
B^{k}
respectively, then for
1\leq i\neq j\leq N
,

(3.9)

d_{g}(B^{i},B^{j})\to\infty,\quad\text{as $R\to\infty$}.

We firstly estimate the perimeter term
P(\widehat{F}_{R})
.

(i) If
m\leq 1
, then
\widehat{F}_{R}
is precisely a geodesic ball

with radius

(3.10)

(ii) If

Combining (

(3.13)

Lemma 3.6

(Nonoptimality Criterion)
.

Let
F\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter with
|F|_{g}=m
, suppose that there exist two disjoint sets of finite perimeter
F_{1},F_{2}
with positive measures such that
F=F_{1}\cup F_{2}
and

(3.17)

\Sigma:=P(F_{1})+P(F_{2})-P(F)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(F_{2}).

Then there is an
\varepsilon>0
depending only
n,\alpha
and
\gamma
, such that if

(3.18)

|F_{2}|_{g}\leq\varepsilon\min\{1,|F_{1}|_{g}\},

there exists a set
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
|G|_{g}=|F|_{g}
and
\mathcal{E}(G)<\mathcal{E}(F)
.

Proof.

Let
m_{1}=|F_{1}|_{g}
and
m_{2}=|F_{2}|_{g}
. Using the upper-half space model
U^{n}
and let
\Phi_{\lambda}
be the transformation defined in (
2.2
), which maps
F_{1}
to the set
\tilde{F}
. We choose
\lambda
such that
|\tilde{F}|_{g}=|F|_{g}=m
, then by (
2.7
), we deduce that
\lambda^{1-n}=1+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}
. Let
\beta:=\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\leq\varepsilon
by (
3.18
). Then we have

(3.19)

\lambda=(1+\beta)^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}<1.

We will compare
\mathcal{E}(F)
with
\mathcal{E}(\tilde{F})
and
\mathcal{E}({\widehat{F}_{R}})
to get suitable choice of
G
. Firstly, if
\limsup_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{E}({\widehat{F}_{R}})<\mathcal{E}(F)
, then there exists
R_{0}
sufficiently large, such that
G:=\widehat{F}_{R_{0}}
satisfies the requirements of this lemma. Otherwise, we assume that

(3.20)

\mathcal{E}(F)\leq\limsup_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{E}({\widehat{F}_{R}})\leq c_{5}\max\{m,m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\}.

Then it remains to show that there is a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
depending on
n,\alpha
and
\gamma
, such that if the condition (
3.18
) holds, then
\mathcal{E}(\tilde{F})<\mathcal{E}(F)
. By (
2.3
), (
2.8
) and (
3.19
), we see that

(3.21)

\displaystyle P(\tilde{F})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2-2n}P(F_{1}),

\displaystyle NL_{\alpha}(\tilde{F})

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{F}}\int_{\tilde{F}}\frac{1}{(x_{n}^{\prime})^{n}}\frac{1}{(y_{n}^{\prime})^{n}}\frac{1}{d_{g}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})^{\alpha}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}

(3.22)

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda}^{2-2n}NL_{\alpha}(F_{1}).

Combining (
3.21
) with (
3.22
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\tilde{F})

\displaystyle=P(\tilde{F})+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(\tilde{F})

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda}^{2-2n}\mathcal{E}(F_{1})

(3.23)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(F_{1})+({\lambda}^{2-2n}-1)\mathcal{E}(F_{1}).

If we choose
\varepsilon\leq 1
, then

1\leq\lambda^{-1}=(1+\beta)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

Hence, there exists a constant
K
depending on
n
such that

(3.24)

\lambda^{2-2n}-1\leq K(\lambda^{-1}-1)=K[(1+\beta)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}-1]\leq K\beta,

where the first inequality is due to Langrange’s mean value theorem. Combining (
3.23
) with (
3.24
) gives

(3.25)

\mathcal{E}(\tilde{F})\leq(1+K\beta)\mathcal{E}(F_{1}).

By assumption (
3.17
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(F)=P(F)+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(F)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle P(F)+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(F_{1})+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(F_{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle P(F_{1})+P(F_{2})-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(F_{2})+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(F_{1})+\gamma NL_{\alpha}(F_{2})

(3.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(F_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(F_{2}).

From (
3.25
) and (
3.26
), we get

(3.27)

\mathcal{E}(\tilde{F})-\mathcal{E}(F)\leq-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(F_{2})+K\beta\mathcal{E}(F_{1}).

By (
2.39
), we have

(3.28)

\mathcal{E}(F_{2})>P(F_{2})\geq nb_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}>0.

Also, by (
3.20
) and (
3.26
),

(3.29)

\mathcal{E}(F_{1})<\mathcal{E}(F)\leq c_{5}\max\{m,m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\}.

Combining (
3.27
)-(
3.29
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\tilde{F})-\mathcal{E}(F)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}nb_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+K\beta c_{5}\max\{m,m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\}

(3.30)

\displaystyle:=-d_{1}(n)m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+d_{2}(n,\alpha,\gamma)\beta\max\{m,m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\}.

Since by (
3.18
),
\beta,m_{2}\leq\varepsilon
, then

\beta m=\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}(m_{1}+m_{2})=(1+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}})m_{2}\leq(1+\varepsilon)m_{2}\leq 2m_{2}.

Then (
3.30
) now becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\tilde{F})-\mathcal{E}(F)

\displaystyle\leq-d_{1}(n)m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+d_{2}(n,\alpha,\gamma)\max\{2m_{2},{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{n}}(2m_{2})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\}

\displaystyle=-d_{1}(n)m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+2^{\frac{n-1}{n}}d_{2}(n,\alpha,\gamma)m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\max\{(2m_{2})^{\frac{1}{n}},\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\}

\displaystyle=-d_{1}(n)m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+2d_{2}(n,\alpha,\gamma)\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

(3.31)

\displaystyle=(-d_{1}(n)+2d_{2}(n,\alpha,\gamma)\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}})m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

If we take

(3.32)

\varepsilon=\min\left\{1,\left[\frac{d_{1}(n)}{4d_{2}(n,\alpha,\gamma)}\right]^{n}\right\},

then we have
\mathcal{E}(\tilde{F})<\mathcal{E}(F)
.
∎

In the end of this section, we improve the lower density result of Theorem

to a bound which independent of the choice of set.

Lemma 3.7

(Uniform lower density bound)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter, which solves the minimization problem (
1.4
) with
|E|_{g}=m
. Then for every
x\in\bar{E}^{M}
, we have for some constant

depending on
n,\alpha
and
\gamma
, there holds

(3.33)

|E\cap B_{1}(x)|_{g}\geq c_{6}\min\{1,m\}.

Proof.

For given
r>0
and

, define the sets
F_{1}^{r}=E\setminus B_{r}(x)
and
F_{2}^{r}=E\cap B_{r}(x)
. Hence by
P(B_{r})=nb_{n}\sinh^{n-1}r
and (

|F_{2}^{r}|_{g}\leq|B_{r}(x)|_{g}\leq n^{-\frac{n}{n-1}}b_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}P(B_{r}(x))^{\frac{n}{n-1}}=b_{n}\sinh^{n}r.

If we take

where
\varepsilon
is the constant appeared in Lemma
3.6
(we can choose
\varepsilon
as in (
3.32
)). Then condition (
3.18
) holds for any

by Langrange’s mean value theorem, which implies that

(3.36)

|F_{2}^{r}|_{g}\leq b_{n}\sinh^{n}r\leq 2b_{n}r^{n}\leq 2b_{n}r_{s}^{n}=2b_{n}d_{3}^{n}\min\{1,m\}.

If
m\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle|F_{2}^{r}|_{g}

\displaystyle\leq 2b_{n}d_{3}^{n}\leq\varepsilon(1-2b_{n}d_{3}^{n})<\varepsilon,

\displaystyle|F_{2}^{r}|_{g}

\displaystyle\leq 2b_{n}d_{3}^{n}\leq\varepsilon(1-2b_{n}d_{3}^{n})\leq\varepsilon(m-2b_{n}d_{3}^{n})\leq\varepsilon|F_{1}^{r}|_{g},

which satisfies the condition (

If

, then

\displaystyle|F_{2}^{r}|_{g}

\displaystyle\leq 2b_{n}d_{3}^{n}m\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}m<\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}<\varepsilon,

\displaystyle|F_{2}^{r}|_{g}

\displaystyle\leq 2b_{n}d_{3}^{n}m\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}m\leq\varepsilon|F_{1}^{r}|_{g},

which also satisfies the condition (
3.18
). However, since
E
is a minimizer, then (
3.17
) cannot hold for any

, i.e., for

, we have

(3.37)

\Sigma^{r}:=P(F_{1}^{r})+P(F_{2}^{r})-P(E)>\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(F_{2}^{r})>\frac{1}{2}P(F_{2}^{r}).

On the other hand, by
[
Ambrosio01
, Proposition 1]
and
[
Ambrosio-Fusco-Pallara00
, Theorem 3.61]
, we have

(3.38)

\Sigma^{r}=2H^{n-1}(\partial^{*}F_{1}^{r}\cap\partial^{*}F_{2}^{r}),

In fact, since all the points belonging to
\partial^{*}F_{1}^{r}
and
\partial^{*}F_{2}^{r}
are supported on
\partial B_{r}(x)
and have density
\frac{1}{2}
by
[
Ambrosio-Fusco-Pallara00
, Theorem 3.61]
, we have
\partial^{*}F_{1}^{r}\cap\partial^{*}F_{2}^{r}=\mathring{E}^{M}\cap\partial B_{r}(x)
. Combining this with (
3.37
) and (
3.38
), we have

\displaystyle 2H^{n-1}(\mathring{E}^{M}\cap\partial B_{r}(x))>\frac{1}{2}\left[H^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap B_{r}(x))+H^{n-1}(\mathring{E}^{M}\cap\partial B_{r}(x))\right],

which gives

(3.39)

H^{n-1}(\mathring{E}^{M}\cap\partial B_{r}(x))>\frac{1}{3}H^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap B_{r}(x)).

Define

(3.40)

r_{t}=\sup\left\{0\leq r\leq r_{s}:|E\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}\geq\frac{1}{2}|B_{r}(x)|_{g}\right\},

and we consider the following two cases.

(i) If
r_{t}=r_{s}<1

(3.41)

|E\cap B_{1}(x)|_{g}\geq|E\cap B_{r_{s}}(x)|\geq\frac{1}{2}|B_{r_{s}}(x)|_{g}\geq\frac{b_{n}}{2}r_{s}^{n}=\frac{b_{n}d_{3}^{n}}{2}\min\{1,m\}.

(ii)If

(3.42)

P(E;B_{r}(x))=H^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap B_{r}(x))\geq c_{1}|E\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

where
c_{1}
is some constant depending only on
n
. Denote
U(r)=|E\cap B_{r}(x)|_{g}
, then combining the coarea formula with (
3.39
) and (
3.42
), we have for a.e.

(3.43)

\frac{dU(r)}{dr}\geq\frac{c_{1}}{3}U(r)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

for all

\displaystyle U^{\frac{1}{n}}(r)

\displaystyle\geq U^{\frac{1}{n}}(r_{t})+\frac{c_{1}}{3n}(r-r_{t})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}|B_{r_{t}}(x)|_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}+\frac{c_{1}}{3n}(r-r_{t})

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{b_{n}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}r_{t}+\frac{c_{1}}{3n}(r-r_{t})

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{\left(\frac{b_{n}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}},\frac{c_{1}}{3n}\right\}r,\,\,\forall r_{t}\leq r\leq r_{s}.

In particular, we have

(3.44)

|E\cap B_{1}(x)|_{g}=U(1)\geq U(r_{s})\geq\min\left\{\frac{b_{n}}{2},\left(\frac{c_{1}}{3n}\right)^{n}\right\}d_{3}^{n}\min\{1,m\}.

Then combining (
3.41

∎

4.
Existence of minimizers

In this section, we prove that the functional
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
admits a minimizer for small volumes. Inspired by
[
Figalli15
, Lemma 4.5]
, we firstly prove the following technical lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
r_{0}>0
and
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, if
|E\setminus B_{r_{0}}|_{g}\leq\eta
for some
\eta>0
, then there exists
r_{0}\leq r_{E}\leq r_{0}+C_{1}(n)\eta^{\frac{1}{n}}
, such that

(4.1)

P(E\cap B_{r_{E}})\leq P(E)-\frac{|E\setminus B_{r_{E}}|_{g}}{C_{2}(n)\eta^{\frac{1}{n}}},

where
C_{1}(n)=4b_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n}}
and
C_{2}(n)=2n^{-1}b_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n}}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we consider a set
E
with
|E\setminus B_{r_{0}}|_{g}\leq\eta
and
|E\setminus B_{r_{0}+C_{1}\eta^{\frac{1}{n}}}|_{g}>0
. Correspondingly, if we set
u(r)=|E\setminus B_{r}|_{g}
with
r>0
, then
u
is a decreasing function with

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &[0,r_{0}+C_{1}\eta^{\frac{1}{n}}]\subset\text{spt}\,u,\\
&u(r_{0})\leq\eta,\\
&u^{\prime}(r)=-H^{n-1}(\mathring{E}^{M}\cap\partial B_{r})\,\,\text{for a.e.}\,\,r>0.\end{aligned}\right.

Arguing by contradiction, we now assume that

(4.3)

P(E)\leq P(E\cap B_{r})+\frac{u(r)}{C_{2}\eta^{\frac{1}{n}}},\quad\forall r\in[r_{0},r_{0}+C_{1}\eta^{\frac{1}{n}}].

We decompose
E
as
E=(E\cap B_{r})\cup(E\setminus B_{r})
, then we have

(4.4)

P(E)=P(E\cap B_{r})+P(E\setminus B_{r})-2H^{n-1}(\mathring{E}^{M}\cap\partial B_{r}).

Combining (
2.39
) (taking
F=E\setminus B_{r}
) with (
4.2
)-(
4.4
) and the fact that
u(r)\leq\eta
, we see that for a.e.
r\in[r_{0},r_{0}+C_{1}\eta^{\frac{1}{n}}]
, there holds

(4.5)

u^{\prime}(r)\leq-\frac{nb_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}{4}u(r)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

which gives

(4.6)

u^{\frac{1}{n}}(r_{0}+C_{1}\eta^{\frac{1}{n}})\leq u^{\frac{1}{n}}(r_{0})-\frac{b_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}{4}C_{1}\eta^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq 0,

where we have used the fact that
u(r_{0})\leq\eta
and the definition of
C_{1}
in the last inequality. It contradicts the assumption that
u(r_{0}+C_{1}\eta^{\frac{1}{n}})>0
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

There exists
m_{0}>0
depending on
n,\alpha
and
\gamma
, such that for every
m\leq m_{0}
and every set of finite perimeter
F
with
|F|_{g}=|B_{r}|_{g}=m
, there exists a set of finite perimeter
G
and a positive constant
C_{3}
depending only on
n
,
\alpha
and
\gamma
, such that

is contained in a geodesic ball of radius
(1+C_{3}r^{\frac{1}{2n}})r
, which satsifies
|G|_{g}=m
and
\mathcal{E}(G)\leq\mathcal{E}(F)
. In particular, for
m\leq m_{0}
, the minimization problem (
1.4
) admits a minimizer
E
with volume
m
which is contained in a geodesic ball of radius
(1+C_{3}r^{\frac{1}{2n}})r
.

Proof.

Firstly, we prove the existence of such set
G
. In the following of the proof, we always assume that
m\leq|B_{1}|_{g}
. Then directly we have
r\leq 1
and hence
\sinh r\leq\cosh 1\cdot r
. Combined with (

B_{(1-2\delta)r_{k}}(O)\subset E_{k}\subset B_{(1+2\delta)r_{k}}(O),

where

is the origin of

and is the barycenter of all

[

, §6.6.2]

which converges to 0 in

for any

is a radial graph with respect to

\partial E_{k}=\{(x,r_{k}(1+u_{k}(x))):x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}.

Since we assume that

are not geodesic balls, hence we have

By Theorem

In this section, we prove that the functional
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
does not admit minimizers for large volumes. Firstly, we prove an interpolation estimate which was firstly derived in

in the case of

, but also holds in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
through some minor modifications in its proof.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any nonnegative
u\in BV(\mathbb{H}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, there exists a constant
c_{7}
depending only on
n
and
\alpha
, such that

(6.1)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}{u^{2}}\,dV_{g}\leq c_{7}||u||^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n+1-\alpha}}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}{|\nabla u|_{g}}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n+1-\alpha}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}\,dV_{g}(x)\,dV_{g}(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1-\alpha}}.

Proof.

Take
R>0
to be determined, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}\,dV_{g}(x)\,dV_{g}(y)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{B_{2R}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)}\frac{u(x)[u(x)+u(y)-u(x)]}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}\,dV_{g}(y)\,dV_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{B_{2R}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)}\frac{u^{2}(x)}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}\,dV_{g}(y)\,dV_{g}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{B_{2R}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)}\frac{u(x)[u(y)-u(x)]}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}\,dV_{g}(y)\,dV_{g}(x)

(6.2)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I+II.

Term
I
can be estimated as follows.

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{B_{2R}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)}\frac{u^{2}(x)}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}\,dV_{g}(y)\,dV_{g}(x)

\displaystyle\geq(2R)^{-\alpha}|B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}|_{g}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}{u^{2}(x)}\,dV_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=(2R)^{-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{R}^{2R}\sinh^{n-1}r\,dr\,d\sigma\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}{u^{2}(x)}\,dV_{g}(x)

(6.3)

\displaystyle=nb_{n}(2R)^{-\alpha}\int_{R}^{2R}\sinh^{n-1}r\,dr\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}{u^{2}(x)}\,dV_{g}(x).

Next we estimate term
II
.
Given
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
y\in B_{2R}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)
, denote
\gamma_{x,y}(t)
as the geodesic connecting
x
and
y
with
\gamma_{x,y}(0)=x
and
\gamma_{x,y}(1)=y
. Then we have
|\gamma_{x,y}^{\prime}(t)|_{g}\equiv d_{g}(x,y)
for
t\in[0,1]
and

\displaystyle|u(y)-u(x)|

\displaystyle=|u(\gamma_{x,y}(1))-u(\gamma_{x,y}(0))|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}{(u\circ\gamma_{x,y})^{\prime}(t)}\,dt\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}{\nabla u(\gamma_{x,y}(t))\cdot\gamma_{x,y}^{\prime}(t)}\,dt\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}{\big|\nabla u(\gamma_{x,y}(t))\big|_{g}\cdot\big|\gamma_{x,y}^{\prime}(t)}\big|_{g}\,dt

\displaystyle=d_{g}(x,y)\int_{0}^{1}{\big|\nabla u(\gamma_{x,y}(t))\big|_{g}}\,dt,

which implies that for any
y\in B_{2R}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{u(x)[u(y)-u(x)]}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq R^{-\alpha}|u(x)||u(y)-u(x)|

(6.4)

\displaystyle\leq 2R^{1-\alpha}||u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\int_{0}^{1}{\big|\nabla u(\gamma_{x,y}(t))\big|_{g}}\,dt.

Combining (
6.2
) with (
6.4
) gives

(6.5)

II\geq-2R^{1-\alpha}||u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{B_{2R}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)}\int_{0}^{1}{\big|\nabla u(\gamma_{x,y}(t))\big|_{g}}\,dt\,dV_{g}(y)\,dV_{g}(x).

We claim that for any fixed

, there holds

(6.6)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{B_{2R}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)}\big|\nabla u(\gamma_{x,y}(t))\big|_{g}\,dV_{g}(y)\,dV_{g}(x)=|B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}|_{g}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla u|_{g}\,dV_{g}.

Note that (
6.6
) is obviously true for

, hence we only consider
t\in(0,1]
. Indeed, the volume element in geodesic polar coordinates is

Then the map

takes

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{B_{2R}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)}\big|\nabla u(\gamma_{x,y}(t)\big|_{g}\,dV_{g}(y)\,dV_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{B_{2tR}(x)\setminus B_{tR}(x)}\frac{|\nabla u(z)|_{g}}{t}\left(\frac{\sinh(r_{z}/t)}{\sinh r_{z}}\right)^{n-1}dV_{g}(z)\,dV_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla u(z)|_{g}\int_{\{x:z\in B_{2tR}(x)\setminus B_{tR}(x)\}}\frac{1}{t}\left(\frac{\sinh(r_{z}/t)}{\sinh r_{z}}\right)^{n-1}dV_{g}(x)\,dV_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla u(z)|_{g}\int_{B_{2tR}(z)\setminus B_{tR}(z)}\frac{1}{t}\left(\frac{\sinh(r_{x}/t)}{\sinh r_{x}}\right)^{n-1}dV_{g}(x)\,dV_{g}(z),

where

(6.10)

\int_{B_{2tR}(z)\setminus B_{tR}(z)}\frac{1}{t}\left(\frac{\sinh(r_{x}/t)}{\sinh r_{x}}\right)^{n-1}dV_{g}(x)=\int_{B_{2R}(z)\setminus B_{R}(z)}dV_{g}=|B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}|_{g},

where we have used (

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\geq-2R^{1-\alpha}||u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}|B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}|_{g}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla u|_{g}\,dV_{g}

(6.11)

\displaystyle=-2nb_{n}R^{1-\alpha}||u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\int_{R}^{2R}\sinh^{n-1}r\,dr\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla u|_{g}\,dV_{g}.

Combining (

) and (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}u^{2}\,dV_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2R)^{\alpha}}{nb_{n}\int_{R}^{2R}\sinh^{n-1}r\,dr}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}\,dV_{g}(x)\,dV_{g}(y)+2^{\alpha+1}R||u||_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla u|_{g}\,dV_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\alpha}R^{\alpha-n}}{(2^{n}-1)b_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\alpha}}\,dV_{g}(x)\,dV_{g}(y)+2^{\alpha+1}R||u||_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla u|_{g}\,dV_{g}

(6.12)

\displaystyle:=aR^{\alpha-n}+bR,

where we have used

in the second inequality.
For the function

|E_{t}|_{g}=|B_{r}|_{g}
for some
0<r\leq R_{0}
, whose boundary is given by

\partial E_{t}=\{(x,r(1+tu(x))):x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}.

By the previous arguments in Theorem
2.16
, we already have

(A.1)

NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})-NL_{\alpha}(E_{t})=NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})-I+\frac{t^{2}r^{2}}{2}h(t),

where

(A.2)

\displaystyle NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}{f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)}\,d\rho\,ds,

(A.3)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r(1+tu(x))}\int_{0}^{r(1+tu(x))}{f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)}\,d\rho\,ds,

(A.4)

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{u(y)}^{u(x)}\int_{u(y)}^{u(x)}{f_{|x-y|}(r(1+t\rho),r(1+ts))}\,d\rho\,ds.

Here the function
f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)
is defined as

f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)=\frac{\sinh^{n-1}\rho\sinh^{n-1}s}{\mathrm{arccosh}^{\alpha}\left[\cosh(\rho-s)+\frac{\sinh\rho\sinh s}{2}{|x-y|}^{2}\right]}.

Then by change of variables, we see that

(A.5)

I=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(1+tu(x))^{2}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}{f_{|x-y|}\left((1+tu(x))\rho,(1+tu(x))s\right)}\,d\rho\,ds.

Lemma A.1
.

There exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
depending on
n
and
R_{0}
, such that for
0<t\leq 2\varepsilon_{0}
, we have

(A.6)

f_{|x-y|}\left((1+tu(x))\rho,(1+tu(x))s\right)\geq f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)\left(1+tuG_{|x-y|}(\alpha,\rho,s)+t^{2}u^{2}D\right)

for some function
G_{|x-y|}(\alpha,\rho,s)
and a positive constant
D
depending on
n,\alpha
and
R_{0}
. Moreover, there exists a constant
D^{\prime}
depending on
n,\alpha
and
R_{0}
, such that
\big|G_{|x-y|}(\alpha,\rho,s)\big|\leq D^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Firstly, by Taylor expansion, we have

\displaystyle\sinh^{n-1}\left[(1+tu)\rho\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sinh^{n-1}\rho\left[1+(n-1)tu\rho\coth\rho\right],

\displaystyle\sinh^{n-1}\left[(1+tu)s\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sinh^{n-1}s\left[1+(n-1)tus\coth s\right]

and hence

(A.7)

\sinh^{n-1}\left[(1+tu)\rho\right]\sinh^{n-1}\left[(1+tu)s\right]\geq\sinh^{n-1}\rho\sinh^{n-1}s(1+tua_{1}(\rho,s)+t^{2}u^{2}a_{2}(\rho,s)),

where

(A.8)

\displaystyle a_{1}(\rho,s)

\displaystyle=(n-1)(\rho\coth\rho+s\coth s),

(A.9)

\displaystyle a_{2}(\rho,s)

\displaystyle=(n-1)^{2}\rho s\coth\rho\coth s,

and
a_{1}(\rho,s),a_{2}(\rho,s)
are all uniformly bounded from above by a constant depending only on
n
and
R_{0}
. Secondly, also by a direct Taylor expansion, we find

\displaystyle\cosh\left[(1+tu)(\rho-s)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\cosh(\rho-s)+tu(\rho-s)\sinh(\rho-s)+\frac{t^{2}u^{2}}{2}\cosh\left(\frac{3}{2}R_{0}\right)(\rho-s)^{2},

\displaystyle\sinh\left[(1+tu)\rho\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sinh\rho+tu\rho\cosh\rho+\frac{t^{2}u^{2}}{2}\rho^{2}\sinh\left(\frac{3}{2}\rho\right),

\displaystyle\sinh\left[(1+tu)s\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sinh s+tus\cosh s+\frac{t^{2}u^{2}}{2}s^{2}\sinh\left(\frac{3}{2}s\right),

which gives

\displaystyle\cosh\left[(1+tu)(\rho-s)\right]+\frac{\sinh\left[(1+tu)\rho\right]\sinh\left[(1+tu)s\right]}{2}|x-y|^{2}

(A.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\cosh(\rho-s)+\frac{\sinh\rho\sinh s}{2}|x-y|^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{4}{t^{k}u^{k}}b^{k}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s),

where the functions
b^{k}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)(1\leq k\leq 4)
are defined as

\displaystyle b^{1}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)

\displaystyle=(\rho-s)\sinh(\rho-s)+\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2}(s\cosh s\sinh\rho+\rho\cosh\rho\sinh s),

\displaystyle b^{2}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cosh\left(\frac{3}{2}R_{0}\right)(\rho-s)^{2}+\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4}\left[s^{2}\sinh\left(\frac{3}{2}s\right)\sinh\rho+2\rho s\cosh\rho\cosh s+\rho^{2}\sinh\left(\frac{3}{2}\rho\right)\sinh s\right],

\displaystyle b^{3}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4}\rho s\left[s\cosh\rho\sinh\left(\frac{3}{2}s\right)+\rho\cosh s\sinh\left(\frac{3}{2}\rho\right)\right],

\displaystyle b^{4}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{8}\rho^{2}s^{2}\sinh\left(\frac{3}{2}\rho\right)\sinh\left(\frac{3}{2}s\right).

Denote the right hand side of (
A.10
) as
F(t)
, then we expand
\mathrm{arccosh}(F(t))
at the point
\mathrm{arccosh}(F(0))
. We get

(A.11)

\mathrm{arccosh}(F(t))=\mathrm{arccosh}(F(0))+tu\frac{b^{1}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)}{\left[F^{2}(0)-1\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(\tau)}{2}t^{2}

for some point
\tau\in(0,t)
. Since it can be directly checked that for any
\tau\in(0,t)
,

F^{2}(\tau)-1=O(F^{2}(0)-1),

and for all
1\leq k\leq 4
,

\displaystyle b^{k}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)

\displaystyle=O(F^{2}(0)-1),

\displaystyle b^{k}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)

\displaystyle=O(\mathrm{arccosh}^{2}(F(0))),

hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{arccosh}\left\{\cosh\left[(1+tu)(\rho-s)\right]+\frac{\sinh\left[(1+tu)\rho\right]\sinh\left[(1+tu)s\right]}{2}|x-y|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{arccosh}(F(t))

(A.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{arccosh}(F(0))\left[1+tua^{3}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)+t^{2}u^{2}a^{4}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)\right].

Here
a^{3}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s),a^{4}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)
are two functions which are all uniformly bounded from above by a constant depending on
R_{0}
. Combining (
A.7
) and (
A.12
) gives

(A.13)

f_{|x-y|}\left((1+tu(x))\rho,(1+tu(x))s\right)\geq f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)\frac{1+tua_{1}(\rho,s)+t^{2}u^{2}a_{2}(\rho,s)}{\left(1+tua^{3}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)+t^{2}u^{2}a^{4}_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)\right)^{\alpha}}.

Then the conclusion of this lemma follows from Taylor expansion of the right hand side of (
A.13
) once more.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Under the choice of
\varepsilon_{0}
in Lemma
A.1
, there exists a constant
L^{\prime}
depending on
n,\alpha
and
R_{0}
, such that

(A.14)

NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})-I\leq t^{2}L^{\prime}\sinh^{2n-\alpha}r||u||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.

Proof.

In the absence of ambiguity, the function
G_{|x-y|}(\alpha,\rho,s)
is abbreviated as
G
in the following of this proof. By Lemma
A.1
, we see that

\displaystyle NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})-I\leq

\displaystyle-t\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}(G+2)f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle-t^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u^{2}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}(D+2G+1)f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle-t^{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u^{3}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}(2D+G)f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)\,d\rho\,ds

(A.15)

\displaystyle-Dt^{4}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u^{4}\,d\sigma_{x}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)\,d\rho\,ds

Note that we have the following two observations. Firstly, since
|E_{t}|_{g}=|B_{r}|_{g}
, we have (cf.
[
Bogelein15
, Equation (4.5)]
)

(A.16)

\left|t\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u\,d\sigma_{x}\right|\leq D_{1}t^{2}||u||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1]})},

for some constant
D_{1}
depending on
n
and
R_{0}
. Secondly, for any fixed

, the integral
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)\,d\rho\,ds

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}Gf_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)\,d\rho\,ds
are only dependent on
|x|
, hence we have

(A.17)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,d\sigma_{y}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}f_{|x-y|}(\rho,s)\,d\rho\,ds=\frac{NL_{\alpha}(B_{r})}{\omega_{n-1}}\leq D_{2}\sinh^{2n-\alpha}r

(A.18)

Lemma A.3
.

Assume that
x
and
y
are two arbitrary points in
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the values of
\rho,s
are taken between
u(x)
and
u(y)
and
0<\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then for any
\tau\in(0,2\varepsilon_{0})
and
r\in(0,R_{0}]
, we have

(A.19)

\left|\frac{\partial f_{|x-y|}(r(1+\tau\rho),r(1+\tau s))}{\partial\tau}\right|\leq L^{\prime\prime}(n,\alpha,R_{0})f_{|x-y|}(r,r)

for some positive constant
L^{\prime\prime}
depending on
n,\alpha
and
R_{0}
.

Proof.

Define
A=r(1+\tau\rho)
,
B=r(1+\tau s)
and denote
\theta:=|x-y|
for simplicity, then

(A.20)

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(A,B)

\displaystyle=\sinh^{n-1}A\sinh^{n-1}B\,\text{arccosh}^{-\alpha}\left[\cosh(A-B)+\frac{\sinh A\sinh B}{2}\theta^{2}\right],

(A.21)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(A,B)}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial f_{\theta}}{\partial A}\frac{\partial A}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\partial f_{\theta}}{\partial B}\frac{\partial B}{\partial\tau}=r\left[\rho\frac{\partial f_{\theta}}{\partial A}+s\frac{\partial f_{\theta}}{\partial B}\right],

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{\theta}}{\partial A}=

\displaystyle(n-1)\sinh^{n-2}A\sinh^{n-1}B\cosh A\,\text{arccosh}^{-\alpha}\left[\cosh(A-B)+\frac{\sinh A\sinh B}{2}\theta^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\alpha\sinh^{n-1}A\sinh^{n-1}B\,\text{arccosh}^{-\alpha-1}\left[\cosh(A-B)+\frac{\sinh A\sinh B}{2}\theta^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left\{\left[\cosh(A-B)+\frac{\sinh A\sinh B}{2}\theta^{2}\right]^{2}-1\right\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sinh(A-B)+\frac{\cosh A\sinh B}{2}\theta^{2}\right)

(A.22)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\text{I}_{A}-\text{II}_{A},

and by symmetry

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{\theta}}{\partial B}=

\displaystyle(n-1)\sinh^{n-2}B\sinh^{n-1}A\cosh B\,\text{arccosh}^{-\alpha}\left[\cosh(A-B)+\frac{\sinh A\sinh B}{2}\theta^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\alpha\sinh^{n-1}A\sinh^{n-1}B\,\text{arccosh}^{-\alpha-1}\left[\cosh(A-B)+\frac{\sinh A\sinh B}{2}\theta^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left\{\left[\cosh(A-B)+\frac{\sinh A\sinh B}{2}\theta^{2}\right]^{2}-1\right\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sinh(B-A)+\frac{\cosh B\sinh A}{2}\theta^{2}\right)

(A.23)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\text{I}_{B}-\text{II}_{B}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial f_{\theta}}{\partial\tau}\right|

\displaystyle=r\left|(\rho\text{I}_{A}+s\text{I}_{B})-(\rho\text{II}_{A}+s\text{II}_{B})\right|

(A.24)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{2}\left(\text{I}_{A}+\text{I}_{B}\right)+r|\rho\text{II}_{A}+s\text{II}_{B}|.

In the following, we prove that
r\text{I}_{A}
(then by symmety,
r\text{I}_{B}
) and
r|\rho\text{II}_{A}+s\text{II}_{B}|
can be controlled by
f_{\theta}(r,r)
respectively. Firstly, it is obvious that there exists two constant
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
depending on
R_{0}
, such that
r\leq\sinh r\leq K_{1}r
and
\sinh r\leq\sinh\frac{3}{2}r\leq K_{2}\sinh r
for
0\leq r\leq R_{0}
. Hence we know that
\sinh A\leq\sinh\frac{3}{2}r\leq K_{2}\sinh r
and also
\sinh B\leq K_{2}\sinh r
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{arccosh}\left[\cosh(A-B)+\frac{\sinh A\sinh B}{2}\theta^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{arccosh}\left[1+\frac{\sinh^{2}(\frac{r}{2})}{2}\theta^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{arccosh}\left(1+\frac{r^{2}\theta^{2}}{8}\right)

\displaystyle\geq K_{3}\mathrm{arccosh}\left(1+\frac{K_{1}^{2}}{2}r^{2}\theta^{2}\right)

(A.25)

\displaystyle\geq K_{3}\mathrm{arccosh}\left(1+\frac{\sinh^{2}r}{2}\theta^{2}\right)

for some constant
K_{3}
depending only on
R_{0}
. The third inequality is due to
0\leq\xi:=\theta r\leq 2R_{0}
and

\lim_{\xi\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{arccosh}\left(1+\frac{\xi^{2}}{8}\right)}{\mathrm{arccosh}\left(1+\frac{K_{1}^{2}}{2}\xi^{2}\right)}=\frac{1}{2K_{1}}.

From the above argument, we arrive at

(A.26)

\frac{r}{2}\left(\text{I}_{A}+\text{I}_{B}\right)\leq(n-1)K_{2}^{2n-3}K_{3}^{-\alpha}\cosh\left(\frac{3}{2}R_{0}\right)f_{\theta}(r,r).

For the term
|\rho\text{II}_{A}+s\text{II}_{B}|
, we do further estimates: Note that
0\leq\sinh r\cdot\theta\leq 2\sinh R_{0}
, then there exists a constant
K_{4}>1
, depending only on
R_{0}
, such that

(A.27)

\mathrm{arccosh}\left(1+\frac{\sinh^{2}r}{2}\theta^{2}\right)\geq\frac{\sinh r\cdot\theta}{K_{4}}\geq\frac{r\theta}{K_{4}}.

Also, we have

\displaystyle\left[\cosh(A-B)+\frac{\sinh A\sinh B}{2}\theta^{2}\right]^{2}-1

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\sinh A\sinh B}{2}\theta^{2}\right)^{2}-1

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sinh A\sinh B\cdot\theta^{2}

(A.28)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)\cdot\theta^{2}\geq\frac{r^{2}\theta^{2}}{4}.

Then combining (
A.25
), (
A.27
) and (
A.28
), we have

(A.29)

r|\rho\text{II}_{A}+s\text{II}_{B}|\leq 2\alpha K_{1}K_{2}^{2n-2}K_{3}^{-\alpha-1}K_{4}\left[\frac{\left(u(x)-u(y)\right)^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}\cosh\left(\frac{3}{2}R_{0}\right)\right]f_{\theta}(r,r).

Since it can be easily checked that the geodesic distance
d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(x,y)
between
x
and
y
in
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
satisfies
|x-y|\leq d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(x,y)\leq\frac{\pi}{2}|x-y|

, and also observe that
||u||_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then there exists a constant
K_{5}
, depending only on the upper bound of the
C^{1}
norm of
u
, such that

(A.30)

\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|}\leq K_{5}.

Combining (
A.24
), (
A.26
), (
A.29
) and (
A.30
) yields the assertion of this lemma.
∎

What is a Polynomial-Time Computable L2-Function?
Aras Bacho 1, Svetlana Selivanova 2, Martin Ziegler 3

Abstract
We give two natural definitions of polynomial-time computability for L 2 \mathrm{L}^{2} functions; and we show them incomparable (unless #P 1 ⊆ FP 1 \text{\rm{\#P}}_{1}\subseteq\text{\rm{FP}}_{1} ).

Introduction

The computational cost of an algorithm
\mathcal{A}
receiving binary strings as input
is commonly measured
in dependence on the length
n
of said input, more precisely:
When
t_{\mathcal{A}}(x)<\infty
denotes the number of steps that
\mathcal{A}
makes
on input
x\in\{\textup{{0}},\textup{{1}}\}^{*}
, one usually considers its worst-case runtime

t_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=\max\big\{t_{\mathcal{A}}(x):|x|\leq n\big\}
: which is obviously well-defined (namely pointwise finite) and non-decreasing a function of type
\mathbb{N}\nearrow\mathbb{N}
,
its asymptotic growth being the core subject of classical complexity theory.
This well-definition of worst-case cost

t_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\;=\;\max\big\{t_{\mathcal{A}}(x):x\in X_{n}\big\}

(1)

holds equally trivially for computations over any countable domain
X
,
when equipped with a monotone cover
X_{n}
by finite subsets of
X
:
for instance
X_{n}=\big\{\text{graph }G=(V,E)\text{ with }|V|\leq n\text{ vertices}\big\}
.

Perhaps less known, but folklore at least in Computable Analysis
[
30
, Theorems 7.1.5 +7.2.7]
,
is that Equation (
1
) remains well-defined even when generalizing ‘finite’ to ‘compact’;
specifically when the domain
X=\bigcup_{n}X_{n}
is a suitably encoded
[
25
]

\sigma
-compact topological space with compact cover
X_{n}\subseteq X_{n+1}
.
For instance
t_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=\max\big\{t_{\mathcal{A}}(x,n):0\leq x\leq 1\big\}
is still well-defined
when
\mathcal{A}
denotes an algorithm computing
a continuous total function
f:[0;1]\to\mathbb{C}
in the sense of
[
18
, Definition 2.11]
or
[
30
, Example 7.2.14]
,
namely pointwise approximating
x\mapsto f(x)
up to error
2^{-n}
.
This well-definition is crucial to the rich complexity theory of real (and complex) continuous functions
[
18
,
8
]
,
including investigations on the complexity blow-up

[
10
,
14
,
15
,
5
,
19
,
6
,
4
]

incurred by popular operators and functionals on suitable spaces of (uniformly) continuous functions.

Indeed, Computable Analysis has long focused on continuous functions only
[
11
]
:
because any terminating computation
\mathcal{A}(x)
by itself depends continuously on its input
x
,
namely
\mathcal{A}
can read only a finite initial part of the infinite information conveyed by
x\in X
.
Integrable functions generalize continuous ones.
They arise naturally in Measure Theory and as (weak derivatives of) solutions to PDEs.
A
p
-integrable function
f:[0;1]\to\mathbb{C}
is technically an equivalence
class of mappings which differ on some subset of
[0;1]
of measure zero.
One cannot reasonably define computing such generalized
f
in terms
of algorithmic evaluation
x\mapsto f(x)
. Better definitions of
qualitative computability are well-known, and equivalent
[

,
30
,

,

]
.
The present work continues previous explorations of

,

1.2
Computability and Complexity of Continuous Functions

An equivalent definition of computability for continuous functions
f\in\mathcal{C}[0;1]
,
based on the Weierstraß approximation theorem, has been generalized successfully:
Algorithmic output only, of (the coefficient vectors of) a sequence of Gaussian dyadic rational polynomials
p_{n}

approximating
f
in the
\infty
-norm up to error
2^{-n}

[
24
, §I.0.3]
.
Recall that Gaussian dyadic rationals are of the form
(x+iy)/2^{n}
where
x,y,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Indeed, the set of such polynomials is countable,
and dense in
\mathcal{C}[0;1]
by Stone-Weierstraß.
This suggests
[
30
, Definition 8.1.2]

defining computability of a point
x
in a fixed separable metric space
(X,d)

in terms of computably approximating
x
up to error
2^{-n}
by a suitable sequence
of elements from some fixed countable dense subset of
X
.
Unfortunately already for
\mathcal{C}[0;1]

this alternative definition, although qualitatively equivalent,
can differ exponentially in quantitative computational cost
[
18
, §8]
.
This leaves us kind of in the dark when trying to define, say,
polynomial-time computability for square-integrable functions
f:[0;1]\to\mathbb{C}

[
21
]
.
The following alternative characterization of polynomial-time computation for
\mathcal{C}[0;1]

from
[
18
, Theorem 2.22]
does offer some guidance:

Definition 1

Let
\mathcal{C}[0;1]
denote the set of total continuous complex functions
f:[0;1]\to\mathbb{C}
.
Call such
f

computable in polynomial time
iff
there exists a Gaussian integer double sequence
\bar{v}=(v_{n,L,\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}+i\mathbb{Z}
,
indexed by
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq\ell<L\in\mathbb{N}
such that it holds (i) and (ii) and (iii):

i)

\big|f(\ell/L)-v_{n,L,\ell}/2^{n}\big|\leq 2^{-n}

ii)

Both
(n,L,\ell)\mapsto\operatorname{Re}v_{n,L,\ell},\operatorname{Im}v_{n,L,\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}
are computable in time polynomial in
n+\log L

iii)

f
has a polynomial modulus of continuity
[
3
, Fact 3]
:

|t-t^{\prime}|\leq 2^{-\operatorname{poly}(n)}\;\Rightarrow\;|f(t)-f(t^{\prime})|\leq 2^{-n}\kern 5.0pt.

(2)

Call
f
computable in
#P
if it holds (i) and (iii) and (ii’):

ii’)

Both
\{\textup{{0}},\textup{{1}}\}^{*}\>\ni\>\textup{{1}}^{n}\,\textup{{0}}\,\mathrm{bin}(L)\,\mathrm{bin}(\ell)\mapsto\operatorname{Re}v_{n,L,\ell},\operatorname{Im}v_{n,L,\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}
are in
GapP
.
∎

Recall that
\text{\rm{\#P}}\supseteq\text{\rm{FP}}
denotes the class of polynomial witness counting problems

\varphi(\vec{v})\;=\;\operatorname{Card}\big\{\vec{w}\in\{\textup{{0}},\textup{{1}}\}^{*}:|\vec{w}|\leq\operatorname{poly}(|\vec{v}|),\vec{w}\in P\big\}\;\geq 0,\quad P\in\text{\rm{P}}

(3)

and
\text{\rm{GapP}}\supseteq\text{\rm{\#P}}
its closure under negation/subtraction
[
12
]
.
Moreover
GapP
is closed under polynomial products and under exponential sums
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
.
Other than
[
19
, Definitions 13+14]
, we here do not allow (repeated and adaptive and post-processable)
oracle access to
#P
: for sharper (pun) complexity claims
without climbing up the Counting Hierarchy, such as in Theorem
3.1
.
Note that
\mathcal{O}(n+\log|\ell|)
is the input length in (ii’), which thus generalizes (ii) meaning that
mappings
\textup{{1}}^{n}\,\textup{{0}}\,\mathrm{bin}(L)\,\mathrm{bin}(\ell)\mapsto\operatorname{Re}v_{n,L,\ell},\operatorname{Im}v_{n,L,\ell}
belong to
FP
.

2
What is a Polynomial-Time Computable L2 Function?

Let us consider the separable space
\mathrm{L}^{2}[0;1]
of
complex-valued square-integrable functions on
[0;1]
.
Recall that this set comes equipped with the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle=\int\nolimits_{0}^{1}f(t)\bar{g}(t)\,dt

and induced norm
\|f\|_{2}=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle}
,
which makes
\mathrm{L}^{2}[0;1]
a Hilbert space.

\mathrm{L}^{2}[0;1]
is a special case of the
hierarchy
\mathrm{L}^{p}[0;1]
of Banach spaces,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
which generalize and contain the space
\mathcal{C}[0;1]

with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}

from Subsection
1.2
.

Calculus knows (at least) two common choices of countable dense subsets of
\mathrm{L}^{2}[0;1]
:
a) Fourier series with Gaussian dyadic rational coefficients
and
b) piecewise constant functions comprised of equidistant blocks of Gaussian dyadic rational heights.
This suggests the following:

Definition 2

a)

Call
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}[0;1]

Fourier-
computable in polynomial time iff
there exists a Gaussian integer double sequence
\bar{c}=(c_{k,n})\in\mathbb{Z}+i\mathbb{Z}
, indexed by
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, such that it holds (a i) and (a ii) and (a iii):

a i)

\big|\tilde{f}_{k}-c_{k,n}/2^{n}\big|\leq 2^{-n}

a ii)

Both
(n,k)\mapsto\operatorname{Re}c_{k,n},\operatorname{Im}c_{k,n}\in\mathbb{Z}
are computable in time polynomial in
n+\log|k|

a iii)

It holds
\big\|f-\mathcal{F}_{K}(f)\big\|_{2}\leq 2^{-m}
for
K=2^{\operatorname{poly}(m)}
,

where
\mathcal{F}_{K}(f,t):=\sum_{|k|\leq K}\tilde{f}_{k}\cdot\exp(2\pi ikt)
denotes the first
2K+1
terms
of the Fourier series with coefficients
\tilde{f}_{k}=\int_{0}^{1}\exp(-2\pi ikt)\cdot f(t)\,dt
.

a’)

Call
f
Fourier-computable in
#P
iff it holds (a i) and (a iii) and (a’ ii):

a’ ii)

Both
\{\textup{{0}},\textup{{1}}\}^{*}\>\ni\>\textup{{1}}^{n}\,\textup{{0}}\,\mathrm{bin}(k)\mapsto\operatorname{Re}c_{k,n},\operatorname{Im}c_{k,n}\in\mathbb{Z}
belong to
GapP
.

b)

Call
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}[0;1]

Step-
computable in polynomial time iff
there exists a Gaussian integer multi sequence
\bar{h}=(h_{S,s,n})
, indexed by
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq s<S\in\mathbb{N}
, such that it holds (b i) and (b ii) and (b iii):

b i)

\big|\hat{f}_{S,s}-h_{S,s,n}/2^{n}\big|\leq 2^{-n}

b ii)

Both
(S,s,n)\mapsto\operatorname{Re}h_{S,s,n},\operatorname{Im}h_{S,s,n}\in\mathbb{Z}
are computable in time polynomial in
n+\log|S|

b iii)

It holds
\big\|f-\mathcal{S}_{S}(f)\big\|_{2}\leq 2^{-m}
for
S=2^{\operatorname{poly}(m)}
,

where
\mathcal{S}_{S}(f,t):=\hat{f}_{S,s}
for
\tfrac{s}{S}\leq t<\tfrac{s+1}{S}

denotes the piecewise constant function with
S
equidistant steps of complex heights

\hat{f}_{S,s}:=S\cdot\int_{s/S}^{(s+1)/S}f(t)\,dt
.

b’)

Call
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}[0;1]
Step-computable in
#P
iff it holds (b i)+(b iii)+(b’ ii):

b’ ii)

Both
\{\textup{{0}},\textup{{1}}\}^{*}\>\ni\>\textup{{1}}^{n}\,\textup{{0}}\,\mathrm{bin}(S)\,\mathrm{bin}(s)\mapsto\operatorname{Re}h_{S,s,n},\operatorname{Im}h_{S,s,n}\in\mathbb{Z}
belong to
GapP
.

c)

Call
f
Step-computable in polynomial time in
mean
iff
there exists a Gaussian integer multi sequence
\bar{h}=(h_{S,s,n})
such that (c i) and (b ii) and (b iii):

c i)

\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{S(n)-1}\big|\hat{f}_{S(n),s}-h_{S(n),s,n}/2^{n}\big|^{2}/S(n)}\leq 2^{-n}

for
S(n)=2^{\operatorname{poly}(n)}
.

c’)

Call
f
Step-computable in
#P
in
mean
iff
if holds (c i)+(b’ ii)+(b iii).

The sequence
(\tilde{f}_{k})
of Fourier coefficients
constitutes the well-known Parseval isometry between
\mathrm{L}^{2}[0;1]
and
\ell^{2}
.
And (a iii) means that said Fourier series converges no slower than exponential-polynomially;
similarly, (b iii) means that piecewise averaged step functions converge no slower than exponential-polynomially.
In fact (a iii) and (b iii) are equivalent according to
[
3
, Theorem 17c+a]
.
Note that
\tilde{f}_{0}=\hat{f}_{1,0}
.

\mathcal{A}

4.1
Heat Equation

Consider an initial-value problem for the heat equation on the unit interval with periodic boundary conditions:

\begin{cases}u_{t}=u_{xx},\ x\in[0,1)\text{ mod }1,\ t>0,\\
u(x,0)=f(x),\end{cases}

(12)

where
f\in L^{2}[0,1]
. As is well known, this problem has a unique solution
u
, and for any fixed
t>0
,
u(\cdot,t)\in C^{\infty}[0,1]
(hence also in
H^{s}[0,1]
for all
s>0
). Recall that
r\in\mathbb{R}
is
polynomial-time computable
if there is an algorithm that, given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, outputs, within time polynomial in
n
, a sequence of
r_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
|r-r_{n}/2^{n}|\leq 2^{-n}
.

Theorem 4.1

Let
t>0
be polynomial-time computable real numbers.

If the initial function
f\in L^{2}[0,1]
is Fourier-computable in polynomial time, then the solution
u(\cdot,t)
at time
t
is also Fourier-computable in polynomial time.

Proof

The solution of (
12
) is given by

u(x,t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{f}_{k}e^{-4\pi^{2}k^{2}t}e^{2\pi ikx},

thus, the Fourier coefficients of
u(\cdot,t)
are
\tilde{u}_{k}(t)=\tilde{f}_{k}e^{-4\pi^{2}k^{2}t}
.

Since
f
is Fourier-computable in polynomial time, there exist a polynomial
p
and a double sequence
(c_{k,n})
of Gaussian integers, such that ai), aii) of Definition
2
hold, and
\big\|f-\mathcal{F}_{K}(f)\big\|_{2}\leq 2^{-n}
with
K=2^{p(n)}
.

By Parseval’s identity,

\big\|u(\cdot,t)-\mathcal{F}_{K}(u(\cdot,t))\big\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{|k|>K}|\tilde{u}_{k}(t)|^{2}=\sum_{|k|>K}|\tilde{f}_{k}|^{2}e^{-8\pi^{2}k^{2}t}\leq\sum_{|k|>K}|\tilde{f}_{k}|^{2}\leq 2^{-2n},

hence

\big\|u(\cdot,t)-\mathcal{F}_{K}(u(\cdot,t))\big\|_{2}\leq 2^{-n},

therefore aiii) for
u(\cdot,t)
holds for the same
K=2^{p(n)}
.

Now we need to construct a double sequence
(d_{k,n})
of Gaussian integers, such that ai), aii) hold for
u(\cdot,t)
.
Since
t
is polynomial-time computable, we can compute, within polynomial time, sequence
|t-t_{m}/2^{m}|\leq 2^{-m}
. It is possible to compute, within polynomial time, an integer sequence
b_{k,m}
(i.e., a sequence of Gaussian integers with zero imaginary part) such that

\bigl|e^{-4\pi^{2}k^{2}t}-b_{k,m}/2^{m}\bigr|\leq 2^{-m}
,
since the exponential function is polynomial-time computable, as well as the argument
4\pi^{2}k^{2}t
. We also have
\big|\tilde{f}_{k}-c_{k,m}/2^{m}\big|\leq 2^{-m}
.

From aiii) with
n=0
we have
K_{0}=2^{p(0)}
such that
\big\|f-\mathcal{F}_{K_{0}}(f)\big\|_{2}\leq 1
, hence

\big\|f\big\|_{2}\leq\big|\mathcal{F}_{K_{0}}(f)\big|_{2}+1
. Since
K_{0}
is a constant, we can compute a rational number
M
with
\big\|f\big\|_{2}\leq M
in constant time (by approximating the finitely many coefficients
\tilde{f}_{k}
for
|k|\leq K_{0}
using the given
c_{k,1}
).

Define

\alpha_{n}=\Bigl\lceil\sqrt{\frac{\ln M+n\ln 2}{4\pi^{2}t}}\Bigr\rceil.

For
|k|\geq\alpha_{n}
we have
|\tilde{u}_{k}(t)|\leq Me^{-4\pi^{2}k^{2}t}\leq 2^{-n}
; set
d_{k,n}=0
for these
k
.

For
|k|<\alpha_{n}
choose an auxiliary precision parameter

m=n+\lceil\log_{2}(M+2)\rceil+1
.

For each such
k
, let
P_{k,m}=c_{k,m}\cdot b_{k,m}\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
and define
d_{k,n}
by rounding
P_{k,m}/2^{2m-n}
to the nearest Gaussian integer:

d_{k,n}=\Bigl\lfloor\frac{P_{k,m}}{2^{2m-n}}\Bigr\rceil.

Then

\bigl|\tilde{u}_{k}(t)-d_{k,n}/2^{n}\bigr|\leq 2^{-n}.

Finally, let us check the condition aii) for
u
.
For
|k|\geq\alpha_{n}
,
d_{k,n}=0
. For
|k|<\alpha_{n}
, note that
\alpha_{n}=O(\sqrt{n})
, hence
\log|k|=O(\log n)
.
Computing
c_{k,m}
takes time polynomial in
m+\log|k|=\text{poly}(n)
.
Computing
b_{k,m}
takes time polynomial in
m
. The multiplication and rounding are polynomial in the bit-length of
P_{k,m}
, which is
O(m)
.
Thus
(n,k)\mapsto d_{k,n}
is computable in time polynomial in
n+\log|k|
.∎

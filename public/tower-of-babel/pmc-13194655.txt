Bridging laboratory findings and artificial intelligence for the design of TlInTe2 crystals
Authors: Muhammad A O Ahmed, Hessah Alotaibi, F Gami, Mohamed M Fangary
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn our laboratory a specially designed Bridgman technique was utilized to pre- pare single crystals of TlInTe2. The structure of TlInTe2 in powder form was examined by X-ray diffraction, revealing the lattice parameters of a = 8.494 Å and c = 7.181 Å. The optical properties of single-layered crystals of TlInTe2 were studied using spectrophotometric transmittance and reflectance measure- ments in the wavelength range of 200–2500 nm. The dispersion curve of the refractive index showed anomalous dispersion in the absorption region and nor- mal dispersion in the transmitted region. The direct band gap was determined to be 2.08 eV. Micro-Raman measurements were carried out at room temperature using a Dilor XY800 triple monochromator with a CCD camera and a Kr+ laser (λexc = 647.1 nm). The laser power was reduced to 1–8 mW to prevent rapid changes in the Raman spectrum, allowing observation of photoinduced spec- tral dynamics. Micro-Raman spectroscopy provides a powerful non-destructive method to investigate the vibrational properties of TlInTe2, offering valuable insights into its structural properties for specific applications in photonics and optoelectronic devices. By careful analysis and comparison with theoretical mod- els and other materials in the same class, we gain a deep understanding of TlInTe2’s potential and limitations. Furthermore, this study integrates machine learning (ML) techniques to improve understanding and prediction of TlInTe2’s. Optical properties. Using synthetic data, Random Forest models were trained to predict optical parameters such as refractive index and absorption coeffi- cient with near-perfect accuracy. These ML models significantly reduce the need for extensive experimental measurements and offer a powerful tool for optimiz- ing the performance of the material in optoelectronic applications. The findings underscore the growing importance of combining ML with materials science to accelerate discoveries and advances in semiconductors such as TlInTe2.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-44965-9.Keywords: Optical properties, Raman spec, Troscopy, Chalcogenides, Machine learning, Synthetic data, Exploratory data analysisSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

Chalcogenide semiconductors of the general form TlMeX2 (with Me = In or Ga and X = Se or Te) exhibit unique electronic and optoelectronic properties that are attrac- tive for infrared photonics and thermophotovoltaics1,2. Among them, TlInTe2 features a layered tetragonal lattice and a direct band gap, giving rise to strong optical absorption and efficient electron–photon conversion. Previous studies have reported photoconductivity, near-band-edge absorption and defect-related phenomena in TlInTe23,4. Its direct gap (approximately 2 eV) and large absorption coeffi- cient in the visible and near-infrared region make it suitable for photovoltaic cells, photodetectors and lasers5,6. Raman spectroscopy complements optical measure- ments by probing vibrational modes and revealing lattice dynamics and defect states. In parallel, machine‐learning algorithms are increasingly employed to predict optical and electrical properties of semiconductors and accelerate materials discovery. This study builds on these foundations by combining crystal growth, spectroscopic charac- terization and data-driven modelling to elucidate and predict the optical properties of TlInTe2.

Raman spectroscopy is a powerful technique used for the investigation of vibra- tional properties, which are becoming increasingly important for the lattice dynamics of material7,8. For the particular case of TlInTe2, Raman analysis will feature phonon modes of atoms in its lattice. Vibrations play an important role in under- standing the thermal and mechanical characteristics, electron–phonon interaction, and electrical conduction and optical properties of the material. Raman spectroscopy can be applied to the identification of defects and disorder in the crystal structure of TlInTe29. These defects in the material drastically affect the performance of the devices by altering their electrical and optical behaviors.

Knowledge of optical and vibrational modes of the layered single crystals of TlInTe2 in the wavelength range of 200–2500 nm is important not only for solid state physics but also for understanding how TlInTe2 interacts with light and heat, so that scientists and engineers can more productively realize novel uses of its prop- erties. The optical properties and vibrational modes of the layered single crystals of TlInTe2 were investigated at wavelengths of 200 to 2500 nm. This paper will focus on the synthesis of information related to the optical characteristics of TlInTe2 crystals and an exploratory data analysis activity conducted to make inferences. TlInTe2 is a very interesting material with great potential for optoelectronic applications due to its outstanding optical characteristics. In our case, we used artificial data to simulate and understand how these materials behave over a wide range of wavelengths to help design and optimize them for certain applications.

Related work

Machine learning (ML) has emerged as a powerful tool for predicting and optimising materials properties. Our previous work demonstrated that Random Forest and Gradi- ent Boosting models can successfully predict the AC conductivity of TlInTe2 based on experimental data, highlighting the benefits of coupling ML with traditional materials characterization10. In the optical domain, neural networks, support vector machines and convolutional neural networks have been used to estimate refractive indices, extinction coefficients and band-gap energies from experimental or first-principles spectra, reducing the need for extensive measurements11–14. Random Forest mod- els have also been applied to chalcogenide compounds to predict absorption coefficients and refractive indices, demonstrating high accuracy and accelerating the discovery of optoelectronic materials15. A recent study on an organic phenylalaninato lead nitrate complex integrated quantum chemical descriptors, topological parameters and spectroscopic data to train ML models for optical‑limiting applications16. While that work focused on a metal–organic complex and third-order nonlinearity, our present study targets a chalcogenide semiconductor and uses synthetic optical spec- tra to predict linear optical constants. Detailed surveys of ML methods and extended comparisons with other techniques are provided in the Supplementary Information.

Laboratory experimental technique

To cultivate high-quality TlInTe2 single crystals, we utilized a refined version of the Bridgman-Stockbarger technique. We carefully measured and combined the pure materials required: 24.9095 g of thallium (35.585%, sourced from Aldrich, with 99.9999% purity), 13.9902 g of indium (19.986%, also from Aldrich, with 99.9999% purity), and 31.1003 g of tellurium (44.429%, from Aldrich, 99.9999% purity). The materials were then placed in an ampoule, which was then sealed within a silica tube under a vacuum pressure of 10 − 5 Torr. The tellurium employed in the synthesis also had 99.9999% purity and was obtained from the same supplier (Aldrich).

Throughout the growth process, the ampoule remained in the furnace’s hot zone at 1145 K for approximately 24 h to ensure the melt was thoroughly homogenized. Periodic shaking of the melt during heating was performed to expedite the diffusion of the components. Using a mechanical system, the ampoule was carefully drawn from one zone to another at a controlled pace of about 1.6 mm per hour. The crystal was grown at a constant temperature in the middle zone of 1050 K, which corresponds to the phase diagram indicating the crystallization temperature of TlInTe217. The whole process of one crystal growth took about 12 days. Photographs of the as‑grown ingot, a cleaved section and the pressed pellet are shown in Fig. 1, illustrating the quality of the single crystals used in this study. The crystalline structure of the powdered TlInTe2 was examined using X-ray diffraction (XRD) at room temperature. These XRD measurements were performed with a Philips X’Pert diffractometer, employing monochromatic Cu Kα radiation, operating at 40 kV and 25 mA.

The transmittance T (λ) and reflectance R(λ) of the TlInTe2 layered single crys- tals, each with a thickness of 0.23 mm, were measured at normal incidence over the spectral range of 200 to 2500 nm using a double-beam spectrophotometer (JASCO model V−570 UV-VIS-NIR). These measurements were taken at room temperature, with a relative uncertainty of 1% for both transmittance and reflectance. Micro- Raman measurements were made at room temperature using a Dilor XY800 triple monochromator, which is equipped with a CCD camera and a Kr+ laser (λexc = 647.1 nm).

Raman modes noted in TlInTe2 are used to probe different features of its structure and vibrations. The A1g vibration mode at 150 cm−1 is very sensitive to the bonding environment of the Te atoms. The study has noted that the mode softens with an increase in temperature, associated with a weakening of the Te-Tl-Te bond. The temperature also shifts the In-Te and Tl-Te bond stretching modes to red at 170 and 250 cm−1, respectively. Defects/impurities within the mate- rial could be identified through the Raman spectra taken from the TlInTe2 material. For example, oxygen impurities tend to have additional peaks in the spectrum, as found near 500 cm−1. Raman spectroscopy is an important tool for investigation of not only TlInTe2 but most ternary semiconductor materials. It can yield information about the structure, vibration, and electronic properties of the materials and can find any defects and impurities easily; therefore, such knowledge may be very useful for performance optimization of the material in electronic and optoelectronic devices.

Discussion and conclusions

TlInTe2 in powdered form, made from cleaved single crystals, was identified as having a tetragonal structure at room temperature. Structural parameters such as crystal- lite size (D), dislocation density (δ), and microstrain (ε) were evaluated using XRD spectra. The optical properties of TlInTe2 layered single crystals were also studied. Spectrophotometric measurements of transmittance and reflectance in the wavelength range of 200–2500 nm were used to determine the spectral and optical parameters. The dispersion curve of the refractive index showed anomalous dispersion in the absorp- tion region and normal dispersion in the transmitted region. The direct band gap was found to be 2.08 eV. Raman spectroscopy is a powerful method for examining the structural and vibrational properties of TlInTe2 and other ternary semiconductor materials. The Raman spectra for these materials contain a series of intense peaks that can be put to good use in the investigation of different features related to the environment of the bonding, phonon dynamics, and defects. This technique is very.

useful for getting insight into the fundamental properties of these materials to improve their performance in electronics and optoelectronics devices. The synthetic data gen- erated enabled further extended EDA, which helped to bring out deeper insights into the optical properties of TlInTe2 crystals. The effectiveness of the Random Forest model in property prediction presents it as a valued tool in material design and opti- mization. It can, therefore, be future work related to working with these models on real-world applications, as well as further sensitivity exploration and material prop- erty optimization. It makes possible the powerful approach of combining ML with synthetic data for use in material science research to predict critical optical proper- ties that allow a better understanding of the possible applications and limitations of TlInTe2 and related materials.The investigations on optical properties in the TlInTe2 crystals, coupled with the generation method of the synthetic data and the ML mod- els, is an effective approach. The proposed model offers an opportunity to perform a comprehensive analysis over a wide wavelength range, including information that is difficult to obtain using other approaches. The fact that models based on random forest achieve high accuracy in the prediction of optical properties, such as the refrac- tive index, absorption coefficient, and dielectric constants, shows the great potential for approaches based on machine learning in material science. Such models save time, resources, and open up new possibilities for the rapid screening and optimization of material in some specific application.

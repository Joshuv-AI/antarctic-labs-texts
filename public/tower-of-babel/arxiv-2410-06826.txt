Comparing direct limit and inverse limit of even K K -groups in non-commutative p p -adic Lie extensions
Meng Fai Lim

Abstract
In a previous paper of the author, we establish a duality for the direct limit and the inverse limit of higher even K K -groups over a ℤ p d \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d} -extension. In this paper, we shall establish such a duality over certain non-commutative p p -adic Lie extensions. Keywords and Phrases : Even K K -groups, non-commutative p p -adic Lie extension. Mathematics Subject Classification 2020 : 11R23, 11R70, 11S25.

Introduction

In this paper,
p
will always denote an odd prime. We shall also fix an integer
i\geq 2
. Let
F
be a number field and
F_{\infty}
a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-extension of
F
. For each finite intermediate extension
L
of
F_{\infty}/F
, the works of Quillen
[
23
]
and Borel
[
1
]
tell us that the even
K
-groups
K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
are finite. Now, for two finite subextensions
L\subseteq L^{\prime}
, the inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L^{\prime}}
induces a map

\jmath_{L/L^{\prime}}:K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L^{\prime}})[p^{\infty}]

by functoriality. (Note that this induced map may not be injective in general.) In the other direction, there is the norm map (also called the transfer map)

\mathrm{Tr}_{L^{\prime}/L}:K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L^{\prime}})[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}].

We then consider the following direct limit and inverse limit

\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}],

whose transition maps are given by the maps
\jmath_{L/L^{\prime}}
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{L^{\prime}/L}
respectively. These limit modules come equipped with natural
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module structures, where
G=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
. In
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
, the author proved the following.

Theorem

(
[
14
]
)
.

Retain the notation as above. Then there is a pseudo-isomorphism

\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\right)^{\iota}\sim\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\right)^{\vee}

of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-modules.

Here, for a given
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
, the module
M^{\iota}
is defined to be the same underlying
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module
M
but with a
\Gamma
-action given by

\gamma\cdot_{\iota}x=\gamma^{-1}x,\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma\in\Gamma,x\in M,

and
(-)^{\vee}
is the Pontryagin dual.
Since
K_{0}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]
can be identified with the Sylow
p
-subgroup of the class group of
\mathcal{O}
, the above result may be thought as a generalization of the previous results on class groups (see the works of Nekovář
[
16
]
, Vauclair
[
24
]
and, more recently, that of Lai and Tan
[
11
]
) to the higher even
K
-groups.

The goal of this paper is to extend the preceding theorem to certain non-commutative
p
-adic Lie
extensions. As before,
F
will always denote a number field. We shall, once and for all, fix a finite set
S
of primes of
F
which is always assumed to contain those above
p
and the infinite primes. Let
S^{\prime}
denote the set of finite primes of
S
. Write
F_{S}
for the maximal algebraic extension of
F
unramified outside
S
. For each finite extension
L
of
F
contained in
F_{S}
, denote by
S(L)
(resp.,
S^{\prime}(L)
) the set of primes of
L
above
S
(resp., above
S^{\prime}
). For ease of notation, we shall write
\mathcal{O}_{L,S}
(instead of
\mathcal{O}_{L,S^{\prime}}
) for the ring of
S^{\prime}(L)
-integers of
L
. This notational convention will be in force throughout the remainder of the paper without further mention.

For two finite subextensions
L\subseteq L^{\prime}
, we have a map
\jmath_{L/L^{\prime}}:K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L,S})[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L^{\prime},S})[p^{\infty}]
induced by the inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{L,S}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L^{\prime},S}
and a norm map
\mathrm{Tr}_{L^{\prime}/L}:K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L^{\prime},S})[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L,S})[p^{\infty}]
. Similarly, we consider the following direct limit and inverse limit

\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L,S})[p^{\infty}]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L,S})[p^{\infty}],

whose transition maps are given by the maps
\jmath_{L/L^{\prime}}
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{L^{\prime}/L}
respectively.

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
i\geq 2
and let
F_{\infty}
be a
p
-adic Lie extension of
F
which is contained in
F_{S}
. Assume that
G=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)
has no
p
-torsion. Suppose that at least one of the following statements holds.

(a)

The integer
i
is even and
F_{\infty}
is a totally real field.

(b)

F_{\infty}=F(\mu_{p^{\infty}},a_{1}^{p^{-\infty}},...,a_{r}^{p^{-\infty}})
.

Then there is an isomorphism

\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L,S})[p^{\infty}]\right)^{\vee}\cong\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L,S})[p^{\infty}],\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket\right)

of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-modules.

To see how our theorem compares with that in
[
14
]
, we first return to the situation when
G=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
. In this context, it follows from
[
19
, Proposition 8]
that
\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket)
is pseudo-isomorphic to
M^{\iota}
. Furthermore,
[
7

tells us that
F_{\infty}\subseteq F_{S_{p\infty}}
, where
S_{p\infty}
is the set of primes consisting precisely of those above
p
and the infinite primes.
On the other hand, by an application of the localization sequence of Soulé (see
[

or
[
21

for every

. In view of these observations, the main result in
[

can be restated as the following isomorphism

\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L,S_{p}})[p^{\infty}]\right)^{\vee}\cong\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L,S_{p}})[p^{\infty}],\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket\right).

2
Iwasawa modules

As before, we let
p
denote a fixed odd prime. The Iwasawa algebra of a compact
p
-adic Lie group
G
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is defined by

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{U}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U],

where
U
runs over the open normal subgroups of
G
and the transition maps in the inverse
limit are given by the canonical projection maps.

In the event that the group
G
is pro-
p
with no
p
-torsion, it is known that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
is
a Noetherian Auslander regular ring (cf.
[
25
, Theorem 3.26]
) with no zero divisors (cf.
[
18
]
). As a consequence, it admits a skew field
Q(G)
which is flat
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
(see
[
5
, Chapters 6 and 10]
or
[
12
, Chapter
4, §9 and §10]
). This in turn enables us to define the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-rank of a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
, which is given by

\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M)=\dim_{Q(G)}(Q(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}M).

The module
M
is then said to be
torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
if
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M)=0
. We also recall a useful equivalent formulation of a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module:
\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket)=0
(cf.
[
25
, Remark 3.7]
). If the torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
also satisfies
\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket)=0
, we shall say that
M
is a pseudo-null
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module.

We now extend the notion of torsion modules and pseudo-null modules to the case when
G
is a general compact
p
-adic Lie group. A well-known theorem of Lazard asserts that
G
contains an open normal subgroup
G_{0}
which is pro-
p
with no
p
-torsion (cf.
[
3
, Theorem 8.32]
). By
[
17
, Proposition 5.4.17]
, we have

\Ext^{i}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket)\cong\Ext^{i}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G_{0}\rrbracket}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G_{0}\rrbracket)

for every finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
. In view of this identification, we shall say that
M
is a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module (resp., psuedo-null
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module) if
\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket)=0
(resp.,
\Ext^{i}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket)=0
for
i=0,1
). Equivalently, this is saying that
M
is a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module (resp., pseudo-null
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module), whenever
M
is a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G_{0}\rrbracket
-module (resp., pseudo-null
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G_{0}\rrbracket
-module) in the sense of the preceding paragraph. (Also, compare with
[
25
, Discussion after Definition 2.6]
.)

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module. For an open subgroup
U
of
G
, we write
M_{U}
for the largest quotient
of
M
on which
U
acts trivially. We shall say that the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
is
systematically coinvariant-finite
if
M_{U}
is finite for every open subgroup
U
of
G
. Note that
\{M_{U}\}_{U}
forms a direct system of finite modules with transition maps given by the norm maps

N_{U/V}:M_{U}\longrightarrow M_{V}

for
V\subseteq U
. In particular,
\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}M_{U}
is a discrete
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module if
M
is systematically coinvariant-finite.

Lemma 2.2
.

A systematically coinvariant-finite
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
is finitely generated torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
.

Proof.

Replacing
G
by a smaller subgroup, we may assume that
G
is pro-
p
with no
p
-torsion. Since
M_{G}
is finite, Nakayama lemma tells us that
M
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
. It then follows from a formula of Harris
[
6
, Theorem 1.10]
that

\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M_{G_{n}})=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M)p^{dn}+O(p^{d(n-1)}),

where
d
is the dimension of
G
(in the sense of
[
3
, Theorem 8.36]
) and
\{G_{n}\}
is a certain prescribed decreasing sequence of open subgroups with
\cap_{n\geq 1}G_{n}=\{1\}
(see
[
6
, P. 105]
for its precise definition).
In view of the systematically coinvariant-finiteness assumption, we have
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M_{G_{n}})=0
for every
n
. This in turn forces
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M)=0
in the above equality which proves the lemma.
∎

The next short exact sequence will be a key component for our subsequent discussion.

Proposition 2.3
.

For every systematically coinvariant-finite
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
, we have the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}M_{U}\Big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket)\longrightarrow\Big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}H_{1}(U,M)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\Big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow 0,

where the transition maps in the limit is given by norm maps.

Proof.

By a result of Jannsen (see
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
or
[
17
, Theorem 5.4.13]
), there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}(M_{U}[p^{\infty}])\Big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket)\longrightarrow\Big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}H_{1}(U,M)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\Big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow 0.

On the other hand, the systematically coinvariant-finite property implies that the leftmost term in the above exact sequence is
\Big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}M_{U}\Big)^{\vee}
.
∎

3
K
-groups and Galois cohomology

Retain the notation from Section
1
. We now recall the following deep calculations of Qullien
[
22
]
and Borel
[
1
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
i\geq 2
. Then the group
K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{F,S})
is finite and

\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(K_{2i-1}(\mathcal{O}_{F,S})\big)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r_{1}(F)+r_{2}(F),&\hbox{if $i$ is odd;}\\
r_{2}(F),&\hbox{if $i$ is even.}\end{array}\right.

Here
r_{1}(F)
(resp.,
r_{2}(F)
) is the number of real embeddings (resp., number of
pairs of complex embeddings) of
L
.

Denote by
\mu_{p^{\infty}}
the group of all
p
-power root of unity. The natural action of
G_{S}(F)
on
\mu_{p^{\infty}}
induces the cyclotomic character

\chi:G_{S}(F)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}.

For a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G_{S}(F)]
-module
X
, we denote by
X(i)
the
i
-fold Tate twist of
X
. More precisely,
X(i)
is the
G_{S}(F)
-module which is
X
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module but with a
G_{S}(F)
-action given by

\sigma\cdot x=\chi(\sigma)^{i}\sigma x,

where the action on the right is the original action of
G_{S}(F)
on
X
.
Plainly, we have
X(0)=X
and
\mu_{p^{\infty}}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)
.
In
[
21
]
, Soulé connected the higher
K
-groups with (continuous) Galois cohomology groups via the
p
-adic Chern class maps

\mathrm{ch}_{i,k}^{(p)}:K_{2i-k}(\mathcal{O}_{F,S})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow H^{k}(G_{S}(F),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i))

for
i\geq 2
and
k=1,2
. (For the precise definition of these maps, we refer readers to loc. cit.) By the deep work of Rost and Voevodsky
[
26
]
(also see
[
27
]
), these
p
-adic Chern class maps are known to be isomorphisms. As a consequence of these, we have the following identification between the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
K
-groups and Galois cohomology.

Proposition 3.2
.

For
i\geq 2
, one has the following isomorphisms

K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{F,S})[p^{\infty}]\cong H^{2}\big(G_{S}(F),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)

of finite abelian groups. Furthermore, if
F
is totally real and
i
is even, then one has the following isomorphisms

K_{2i-1}(\mathcal{O}_{F,S})[p^{\infty}]\cong H^{1}\big(G_{S}(F),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)

of finite abelian groups.

We now introduce the Iwasawa cohomology groups which will be an important object for our study. For every extension
L
of
F
contained in
F_{S}
, write
G_{S}(L)
for the Galois group
\Gal(F_{S}/L)
.
For every extension
\mathcal{L}
of
F
contained in
F_{S}
, the Iwasawa cohomology groups are defined by

H^{k}_{\mathrm{Iw},S}\big(\mathcal{L}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big):=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}H^{k}\big(G_{S}(L),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big),

where
L
runs through all finite extensions of
F
contained in
\mathcal{L}
and the transition maps are given by the corestriction maps. For ease of notation,
we will drop the ‘
S
’ in the Iwasawa cohomology groups.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that if
\mathcal{L}/F
is a finite extension, then one has

H^{k}_{\mathrm{Iw},S}\big(\mathcal{L}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)=H^{k}\big(G_{S}(\mathcal{L}),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)
.

Let
F_{\infty}
be a
p
-adic Lie extension of
F
contained in
F_{S}
. In other words,
F_{\infty}
is a Galois extension of
F
, whose Galois group
G=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)
is a compact
p
-adic Lie group. The groups
H^{k}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)
come naturally equipped with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module structures and can be shown to be finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
(see
[
15
, Proposition 4.1.3]
).
The following version of Tate’s descent spectral sequence for Iwasawa cohomology will be useful in our subsequent discussion.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
F_{\infty}
be a
p
-adic Lie extension of
F
contained in
F_{S}
. Suppose that
i\geq 2
.
Let
U
be a closed normal subgroup of
G=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)
and write
\mathcal{L}
for the fixed field of

. Then we have a homological spectral sequence

H_{r}\big(U,H^{2-s}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i))\big)\Longrightarrow H^{2-r-s}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(\mathcal{L}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)

and an isomorphism

H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)_{U}\cong H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(\mathcal{L}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big).

Furthermore, if
U
is an open subgroup of
G
, then
\mathcal{L}
is a finite extension of
F
and we have

H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)_{U}\cong H^{2}\big(G_{S}(\mathcal{L}),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big).

Proof.

When
G
is a commutative group, the spectral sequence is a result of Nekovář
[
16
, Proposition 4.2.3]
. For a noncommutative group
G
, this follows from
[
4
, Proposition 1.6.5]
or
[
15
, Theorem 3.1.8]
. The isomorphisms of the proposition follow from reading off the initial term of the spectral sequence. Finally, in the event that

is an open subgroup of
G
, then
\mathcal{L}
is a finite extension of
F
, and so
H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(L/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i))
identifies with
H^{2}(G_{S}(L),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i))
as noted in Remark
3.3
.
∎

Let

, Lemma 3.6]
, one has the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\scriptstyle{N_{U/V}}
\textstyle{H^{2}\big(G_{S}(F_{\infty}^{U}),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{res}}
\textstyle{H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)_{V}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{H^{2}\big(G_{S}(F_{\infty}^{V}),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)}

for every open normal subgroups
V\subseteq U
of
G
, where
F_{\infty}^{U}
(resp.,
F_{\infty}^{V}
) denotes the fixed field of
F_{\infty}
under
U
(resp., under
V
). Upon combining this with (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U,N_{U/V}}H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)_{U}\cong\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L,S})[p^{\infty}].

Putting all the above observations together into Proposition
3.3
, we obtain a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L,S})[p^{\infty}]\Big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}\Big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}K_{2i-2}(\mathcal{O}_{L,S})[p^{\infty}],\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket\Big)\\
\longrightarrow\Big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{U}H_{1}\big(U,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\Big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow 0.

(3.3)

Therefore, it remains to show that
H_{1}\big(U,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)
is finite for every open subgroup
U
of
G
. Without loss of generality, upon relabelling, it is enough to show that
H_{1}\big(G,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)
is finite.

We now give a proof of this in the totally real context.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
under assumption (a).

From the low degree terms of the homological spectral sequence

H_{r}\big(G,H^{2-s}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i))\big)\Longrightarrow H^{2-r-s}\big(G_{S}(F),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big),

we have a surjection

H^{1}\big(G_{S}(F),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\longrightarrow H_{1}\big(G,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i))\big)\longrightarrow 0.

Since
F
is totally real and
i
is even, it follows from Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
that the group
H^{1}\big(G_{S}(F),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)
is finite. Hence it follows that

H_{1}\big(G,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)
is also finite which is what we want to show.
∎

4
Multi-false Tate extension

The goal of this final section of the paper is to prove Theorem
1.1
under assumption (b). Set
F_{\infty}=F(\mu_{p^{\infty}},a_{1}^{p^{-\infty}},...,a_{r}^{p^{-\infty}})
, where
a_{1},...,a_{r}\in F^{\times}
whose image in
F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{p}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. (This is usually coined as the multi-false Tate extension.) We then write
G=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)
and
H=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F(\mu_{p^{\infty}}))
, where Kummer theory tells us that
H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}
. As seen in the discussion in the previous section, it remains to show that
H_{1}\big(G,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)
is finite. Now, set
G_{0}=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F(\mu_{p}))
. Clearly,
G_{0}
is an open normal subgroup of
G
with index coprime to
p
. Therefore, one has an identification

H_{1}\big(G_{0},H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)_{G/G_{0}}\cong H_{1}\big(G,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big).

In view of this isomorphism, for the verification of the finiteness of
H_{1}\big(G,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)
, one is reduced to showing that
H_{1}\big(G_{0},H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)
is finite. Therefore, replacing
F
by
F(\mu_{p})
, we may assume that the group
\Gamma:=G/H=\Gal(F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/F)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
which we will do for the remainder of the section.

We shall require a further technical result. Before stating this said result, we make a remark on notation.
For a given character
\kappa:G/H\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
and a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G/H\rrbracket
-module
T
, we let

T(\kappa)
denote the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module
T
with the new commuting

G/H
-action given by the twist of the original by
\kappa
. With this notation in hand, we can state the required technical result.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a compact
p
-adic Lie group and
H

a closed normal subgroup of
G
. Assume that we are given a
finite family of closed normal subgroups
H_{j}

(0\leq j\leq r)
of

G
such that
1=H_{0}\subseteq H_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq H_{r}=H
with

H_{j}/H_{j-1}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for
1\leq j\leq r
, and such that the action
of
G
on
H_{j}/H_{j-1}
by inner automorphism is given by a
homomorphism
\chi_{j}:G/H\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

For a given finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
, there exists a
finite family
(S_{k})_{1\leq k\leq t}
of

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G/H\rrbracket
-submodules of
H_{1}(H,M)
satisfying all of the following
properties.

(i)

0=S_{0}\subseteq S_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq S_{t}=H_{1}(H,M)
.

(ii)

For each
k

(1\leq k\leq t)
, there is a

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G/H\rrbracket
-subquotient
T
of
M_{H}
and a family

(s(k))_{1\leq k\leq r}
of nonnegative
integers such that
|\{k|s(k)>0\}|\geq 1
and such that

S_{k}/S_{k-1}
is isomorphic to the twist
T(\prod_{1\leq i\leq r}\chi_{k}^{s(k)})
of
T
.

Proof.

This is a special case of a result of Kato
[
9
, Proposition 4.2]
, and we refer the readers to loc. cit for the proof. ∎

We return to our multi-false Tate extension situation. Kummer theory tells us that the action of
\Gamma
on
H
by inner automorphism coincides with the cyclotomic character. Therefore, in this context, each
S_{k}/S_{k-1}
is isomorphic to
T(n_{k})
for some positive integer
n_{k}
.

On the other hand, from the spectral sequence

H_{r}\big(\Gamma,H_{s}\big(H,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)\big)\Longrightarrow H_{r+s}\big(G,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big),

we obtain a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow H_{1}\big(H,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)_{\Gamma}\longrightarrow H_{1}\big(G,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)\longrightarrow H_{1}\big(\Gamma,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)_{H}\big)\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore, we are now reduced to showing the leftmost and rightmost terms of the short exact sequence is finite. By Proposition
3.4
, we have

H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)_{H}\cong H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)

which is a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-module. Now by Proposition
3.4
again, we have

H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)_{\Gamma}\cong H^{2}\big(G_{S}(F),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)

which is finite. In view of this latter finiteness observation, it follows from the structure theory of finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-module (for instances, see
[
2
, Proposition A.1.7]
) that
H_{1}\big(\Gamma,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)
is also finite.

It therefore remains to show that
H_{1}\big(H,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)_{\Gamma}
is finite. For this, we require a preliminary lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

0\longrightarrow M^{\prime}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow 0

be a short exact sequence of finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-modules. Then

M_{\Gamma}
is finite if and only if
M^{\prime}_{\Gamma}
and
M^{\prime\prime}_{\Gamma}
are finite.

In particular, if
M
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-module with
M_{\Gamma}
being finite, then
N_{\Gamma}
is finite for every subquotient
N
of
M
.

Proof.

Indeed, one has the following exact sequence

H_{1}(\Gamma,M^{\prime\prime})\longrightarrow M^{\prime}_{\Gamma}\longrightarrow M_{\Gamma}\longrightarrow M^{\prime\prime}_{\Gamma}\longrightarrow 0.

Clearly, if
M^{\prime}_{\Gamma}
and
M^{\prime\prime}_{\Gamma}
are finite, then so is
M_{\Gamma}
. Now suppose that
M_{\Gamma}
is finite, then so is
M^{\prime\prime}_{\Gamma}
. By
[
2
, Proposition A.1.7]
, the latter implies that
H_{1}(\Gamma,M^{\prime\prime})
is also finite. Hence it follows from this and the above exact sequence that
M^{\prime}_{\Gamma}
is finite.

The second assertion is immediate from the first.
∎

We turn back to continue with the proof of our theorem. Applying Proposition
4.1
, we see that there is a

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-subquotient
T
of

H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)_{H}\cong H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)

such that
H_{1}\big(H,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)
is a successive extension of
T(n_{k})
for some
n_{k}\geq 1
with
1\leq k\leq r
. In view of this and taking the first assertion of Lemma
4.2
into account, the verification of the finiteness of
H_{1}\big(H,H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)\big)_{\Gamma}
is reduced to showing that
T(k)_{\Gamma}
is finite for every
k\geq 1
. Via the second assertion of Lemma
4.2
, the latter is reduced to showing that

\Big(H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)_{H}(k)\Big)_{\Gamma}

is finite for every
k\geq 1
. Now observe that

\Big(H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F_{\infty}/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)_{H}(k)\Big)_{\Gamma}\cong\Big(H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)\big)(k)\Big)_{\Gamma}\cong\Big(H^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\big(F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/F,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i+k)\big)\Big)_{\Gamma},

where we note that the second isomorphism follows from an application of
[
20
, Lemma 2.5.1(c)]
. By virtue of Propositions
3.2
and
3.4
, the latter is precisely

H^{2}\big(G_{S}(F),\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i+k)\big)\cong K_{2(i+k)-2}(\mathcal{O}_{F,S})

which is finite as required. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
under assumption (b) which also concludes the paper.

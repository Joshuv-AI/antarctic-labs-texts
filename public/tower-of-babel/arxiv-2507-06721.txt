3
Overview

This section provides an overview of our construction algorithms and the key new tools we develop. To clarify the context of our contribution, we begin by describing the framework underlying both construction algorithms of Wulff-Nilsen
[
Wul12
]
. This framework consists of the following three steps:

(Step 1)

Sample a set
S
and create an oracle that upon a query on
u,v\in V
produces
\hat{d}_{G_{S}}(u,v)
.

(Step 2)

Compute a spanner
H
.

(Step 3)

Create an oracle that upon a query on
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
produces
\hat{d}_{H}(s_{1},s_{2})
.

The query of a distance oracle produced by this framework returns
\min(\hat{d}_{G_{S}}(u,v),h_{S}(u)+h_{S}(v)+\hat{d}_{H}(p(u),p(v)))
.
Wulff-Nilsen uses this framework to obtain his

O(\sqrt{k}m+kn^{1+\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{k}}+O(k^{-1})})
construction time algorithm, as follows. In
(Step 1)

\mbox{{ADO}}(G_{S},k)
is constructed. In
(Step 2)
a
(2k^{\prime}-1)
-spanner
H
from
Lemma

5
is computed. In
(Step 3)

\mbox{{ADO}}(H,k^{\prime\prime})
is constructed.

Next, we discuss our new main techniques that allow us to improve
(Step 1)
.
Notice that there is a tradeoff between
|S|
and
|E(G_{S})|
.
When
|E(G_{S})|
is small
(Step 1)
becomes faster and
|S|
increases, resulting in a slower
(Step 3)
.
Given this tradeoff, the key to achieving faster construction algorithms is the following optimization problem:

Problem 4
.

For every
0\leq x_{0}\leq 1
, what is the densest graph for which one can construct a
(2k-1)
-stretch distance oracle in
O(n^{1+x_{0}+\frac{1}{k}})
time?

The construction algorithm of Thorup and Zwick
[
TZ05
]
solves
4
for graphs with
O(n^{1+x_{0}})
edges. Wulff-Nilsen uses the construction algorithm of
[
TZ05
]
in
(Step 1)
.
We improve the construction algorithm of
[
TZ05
]
, for every
x_{0}>\frac{1}{k}
and solve
4
for graphs with
O(n^{1+\frac{k-1}{k-2}x_{0}-\frac{1}{k(k-2)}})
edges.

To obtain our improved construction algorithm, we develop a new hierarchy of parameterized distance oracles, which we denote with
hADO
(see
Theorem

1
).
The hierarchical distance oracle uses our new parameterized distance oracle, which we denote with
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}
(see
Lemma

10
).

Let
S\subseteq V
. Roditty, Thorup, and Zwick
[
RTZ05
]
presented an
O(m|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
construction time algorithm for constructing a
(2k-1)
-stretch distance oracle that uses
O(n|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
space and supports queries for vertex pairs
\langle s,v\rangle\in S\times V
.
Our new parameterized distance oracle,
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}
, is a generalization of the parameterized distance oracle of
[
RTZ05
]
.
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}
is
constructed in the same time, uses the same space and query time, and supports queries for vertex pairs
\langle u,v\rangle\in V\times V
rather than
\langle s,v\rangle\in S\times V
.
We prove that
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}(G,k,S)\mbox{{.Query}}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(2k-1)d(u,v)
.
Notice that if
u\in S
then
h_{S}(u)=0
and
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}(G,k,S)\mbox{{.Query}}(u,v)\leq(2k-1)d(u,v)
, as in
[
RTZ05
]
. (For more details see
Section

4.2
.)

The hierarchical distance oracle
hADO
works as follows.
Let
S_{0}
be a set of size
n^{1-x_{0}}
, and construct
\mbox{{ADO}}(G_{S_{0}},k)
.
Let
t=\lceil\log\log{n}\rceil
.
For every
1\leq i\leq t
, construct
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}(G_{S_{i}},k-1,S_{i-1})
, where
S_{i}
is a random set of size
n^{1-x_{i}}
and
x_{i}=x_{0}-\frac{1}{k(k-1)}+\frac{x_{i-1}}{k-1}
. The query of
hADO
returns
\min(\mbox{{ADO}}(G_{S_{0}},k)\mbox{{.Query}}(u,v),\min_{1\leq i\leq t}(\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}(G_{S_{i}},k-1,S_{i-1})\mbox{{.Query}}(u,v)))
.

The construction time of
hADO
is
\tilde{O}(n^{1+x_{0}+\frac{1}{k}})
.
To see this notice that constructing
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}(G_{S_{i}},k-1,S_{i-1})
takes
O(|E(G_{S_{i}})|\times|S_{i-1}|^{\frac{1}{k-1}})=O(kn^{1+x_{i}+\frac{1-x_{i-1}}{k-1}})
time, and since
x_{i}=x_{0}-\frac{1}{k(k-1)}+\frac{x_{i-1}}{k-1}
we get

O(kn^{1+x_{0}-\frac{1}{k(k-1)}+\frac{x_{i-1}}{k-1}+\frac{1-x_{i-1}}{k-1}})=O(kn^{1+x_{0}+\frac{1}{k}})
.

\mbox{{hADO}}\mbox{{.Query}}(u,v)\leq(2k-1)d(u,v)
for every
u,v
such that
P(u,v)\subseteq G_{S_{t}}
.
To see that, let
j
be the first index for which
P(u,v)\subseteq G_{S_{j}}
. Since
P(u,v)\not\subseteq G_{S_{j-1}}
, it follows from
Lemma

4
that

\max(h_{S_{j-1}}(u),h_{S_{j-1}}(v))\leq d(u,v)
,
thus,
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}(G_{S_{j}},k-1,S_{j-1})\mbox{{.Query}}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{S_{j-1}}(u),h_{S_{j-1}}(v))+(2(k-1)-1)d(u,v)\leq(2k-1)d(u,v)
. (See
Lemma

14
for a complete proof.)

Our new hierarchical distance oracle,
hADO
, obtains a truly subquadratic construction algorithm for every
k\geq 3
even without using the three-step framework presented above.
Roughly speaking, the construction algorithm works as follows.
Let
x_{0}
be the solution for the equation
n^{1+\frac{k-1}{k-2}x_{0}-\frac{1}{k(k-2)}}=m
. One can verify that
x_{0}=\frac{\log_{n}{m}k^{2}-2\log_{n}{m}k-k^{2}+2k+1}{k(k-1)}
.

We construct
\mbox{{hADO}}(G,k,x_{0})
.
In the hierarchy, we have that
|E(G_{S_{t}})|=n^{1+\frac{k-1}{k-2}x_{0}-\frac{1}{k(k-2)}}=m
. Since
|E(G_{S_{t}})|=m
we can replace
G_{S_{t}}
with
G
without affecting the construction time.
Since
P(u,v)\subseteq G
for every
u,v\in V
, we obtain a
(2k-1)
-stretch distance oracle.
The construction time of this distance oracle is
\tilde{O}(n^{1+x_{0}+\frac{1}{k}})=\tilde{O}(m^{1-\frac{1}{k-1}}n^{\frac{2}{k-1}})
(See
Lemma

16
).
We remark that this is truly subquadratic since for
m=n^{2-\varepsilon}
we get a running time of
\tilde{O}(n^{(2-\varepsilon)(1-\frac{1}{k-1})}n^{\frac{2}{k-1}})=\tilde{O}(n^{2-\varepsilon(1-\frac{1}{k-1})})
, which is truly subquadratic for every
k>2
.

Next, we overview our improvement in the regime of linear construction time algorithms.
To improve the previous results, we use the framework described above and improve each step of the three-step framework as follows:
In
(Step 1)
we use
\mbox{{hADO}}(G,k,x_{0})
as opposed to
\mbox{{ADO}}(G,k)
as in
[
Wul12
]
.
This enables us to estimate distances in
G_{S_{t}}
instead of
G_{S}
.
In
(Step 2)
we
compute a
(2k^{\prime}-1)
-spanner
H
as in
[
Wul12
]
. However, we augment
H
with edges between
u
and
p(u)
of weight
h(u)
for every
u\in V
.
This enables us to bound
d_{H}(p(u),p(v))
with
h(u)+h(v)+(2k^{\prime}-1)d(u,v)
instead of
(2k^{\prime}-1)(h(u)+h(v)+d(u,v))
as in
[
Wul12
]
.

For
(Step 3)
we develop an additional parameterized distance oracle,
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P^{\prime}}
(see
Lemma

11
), that is constructed in
O(m|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
time, the same running time as
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}
, but uses
O(|S|^{1+\frac{1}{k}})
space which is significantly less space than the
O(n|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
space required by
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}
. The space reduction comes at a cost of supporting queries only for vertex pairs in
S\times S
.
In
(Step 3)
we use
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P^{\prime}}(H,k-1,{S_{t}})
instead of
\mbox{{ADO}}(H,k)
as in
[
Wul12
]
.

These improvements allows us to reduce the
O(\sqrt{k}m+kn^{1+\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{k}+O(k^{-1})})
construction time of
[
Wul12
]
to
O(\sqrt{k}m+kn^{1+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{k}})
and the
\tilde{O}(km+kn^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{2}{k}+O(k^{-2})})
construction time of
[
Wul12
]
to
\tilde{O}(km+kn^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{3}{4k-6}})
. (See
Sections

5.3
and
5.2
.)

By applying our new techniques to unweighted graphs and allowing a small additive error, we can further improve the construction time.
In particular, among our results for unweighted graphs, we obtain
a construction algorithm that in
O(km+kn^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{0.75}{k}+O(k^{-2})})
time constructs a
(2k-1,2)
-approximation distance oracle, improving the
O(km+kn^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{k}+O(k^{-2})})
of Baswana, Gaur, Sen, and Upadhyay
[
BGSU08
]
, for every
k>2
.

We remark that all of our construction algorithms can be de-randomized using the methods presented by Roditty, Thorup, and Zwick
[
RTZ05
]
at a logarithmic cost.

Properties of the classical distance oracle of

In this section, we discuss the internals and some properties of the Thorup and Zwick distance oracle

The following lemma appears explicitly in

Lemma 7

(
[
TZ05
]
)
.

Given an integer parameter
k\geq 2
, we can compute in
O(n)

A_{1},\ldots,A_{k-1}
, such that
|A_{i}|=O(n^{1-i/k})
for every
i\in[1,k-1]
.
For every

the size of

The cost of computing

Next, for completeness, we describe the query of
[
TZ05
]
(
ADO.Query
). Later, we generalize its correctness proofs, and this generalization helps us to achieve the oracles of
Section

4.2
.

The procedure
ADO.Query
gets as an input
u,v\in V
, and using their bunches gives an estimation
\hat{d}(u,v)
.
For
i\in[k]
, if
p_{i}(u)\in B_{i}(v)
return
h_{i}(u)+d(p_{i}(u),v)
, otherwise swap
u
and

and continue iterating the for loop.
A pseudo-code for
ADO.Query
is presented in
Algorithm

1
.

for

i\in[k]

1

then

h_{i}(u)+d(p_{i}(u),v)
(u,v)\leftarrow(v,u)

Algorithm 1

\mbox{{ADO.Query}}(u,v)

Let
\hat{d}(u,v)
be
\min(\mbox{{ADO.Query}}(u,v),\mbox{{ADO.Query}}(v,u))
.
The following lemmas prove properties of
\hat{d}(u,v)
, which generalizes the proof of
[
TZ05
]
.

Lemma 8
.

\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{k-1}(u),h_{k-1}(v))+d(u,v)

Proof.

Since
A_{k}=\emptyset
, we have that
p_{k-1}(u)\in B_{k-1}(v)
.
Thus,
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq h_{k-1}(u)+d(p_{k-1}(u),v)\leq 2h_{k-1}(u)+d(u,v)
, where the last inequality follows from the triangle inequality.
Using symmetrical arguments, we have that
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2h_{k-1}(v)+d(u,v)
, as required.
∎

Lemma 9
.

For every integer
1\leq i\leq k
, one of the following holds.

•

\min(h_{i}(u),h_{i}(v))\leq\min(h_{i-1}(u),h_{i-1}(v))+d(u,v)

•

\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{i-1}(u),h_{i-1}(v))+d(u,v)

Proof.

Wlog (without loss of generality), assume that
h_{i-1}(u)\leq h_{i-1}(v)
.
We divide the proof into two cases.
The case where
p_{i-1}(u)\in B_{i}(v)
, and the case where
p_{i-1}(u)\notin B_{i}(v)
.
Consider the case where
p_{i-1}(u)\in B_{i}(v)
. In this case,
the value of
d(p_{i-1}(u),v)
is saved in the distance oracle. From the triangle inequality it follows that
d(p_{i-1}(u),v)\leq h_{i-1}(u)+d(u,v)
. Therefore, we have that
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq h_{i-1}(u)+d(p_{i-1}(u),v)\leq 2h_{i-1}(u)+d(u,v)
.
Since
h_{i-1}(u)\leq h_{i-1}(v)
we get that
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{i-1}(u),h_{i-1}(v))+d(u,v)
,
as required.

Consider now the case that
p_{i-1}(u)\notin B_{i}(v)
. From the definition of
B_{i}(v)
it follows that
h_{i}(v)\leq d(v,p_{i-1}(u))\leq d(u,v)+h_{i-1}(u)
. Since
h_{i-1}(u)\leq h_{i-1}(v)
, we get that
h_{i}(v)\leq\min(h_{i-1}(u),h_{i-1}(v))+d(u,v)
, and thus
\min(h_{i}(u),h_{i}(v))\leq\min(h_{i-1}(u),h_{i-1}(v))+d(u,v)
, as required.
∎

4.2
Parameterized distance oracles

A
parameterized distance oracle
is constructed with respect to a special input set
S\subseteq V
.
Roditty et al.
[
RTZ05
]
presented an algorithm that constructs such a
(2k-1)
-distance oracle in

O(m|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
time. The distance oracle uses
O(n|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
space and answers distance queries only for vertex pairs
\langle s,v\rangle\in S\times V
in
O(k)
time.

In this section, we present two new parameterized distance oracles used in our fast constructions.
In the first parameterized distance oracle, the query supports vertex pairs from
V\times V
. In the second parameterized distance oracle, the query supports vertex pairs only from
S\times S
.

First, we generalize the parameterized distance oracle of
[
RTZ05
]
, and extend the query to support vertex pairs from
V\times V
, as follows.
For every
u,v\in V
the query returns an estimation
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(2k-1)d(u,v)
.
Notice that when
u\in S
we have
h_{S}(u)=0
thus
\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))=0
, and
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq(2k-1)d(u,v)
, as in the distance oracle of
[
RTZ05
]
.
Formally:

Lemma 10
.

Let
k\geq 1
and let
S\subseteq V
. There is an
O(n|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
-space distance oracle that given two vertices
u,v\in V
, returns in
O(k)
time an estimation
\hat{d}(u,v)
such that
d(u,v)\leq\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(2k-1)d(u,v)
. The distance oracle is constructed in
O(m|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
time.

Proof.

The distance oracle is constructed as follows.
Let
A_{0}=V
,
A_{1}=S
,
A_{k+1}=\emptyset
.
For every
2\leq i\leq k
the set
A_{i}\subseteq A_{i-1}
is a random set such that
|A_{i}|=|A_{i-1}|\cdot|S|^{-i/k}
.
We let
B_{0}(u)=\{u\}
for every
u\in V
.
As presented in
[
RTZ05
]
, we compute
C(u)
for every
u\in S
in
O(m|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
time in order to save for every
u\in V
and
1\leq i\leq k
the sets
B_{i}(u)
.
From
Lemma

7
, we have that
\sum_{u\in V}\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}{|B_{i}(u)|}\leq O(n|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
, thus, the distance oracle uses
O(n|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
space.

The query of the distance oracle returns
\min(\mbox{{ADO.Query}}(u,v),\mbox{{ADO.Query}}(v,u))
. To prove its correctness, we first prove by induction for every
i\in[1,k+1]
that either
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(2i-3)d(u,v)
or
\min(h_{i}(u),h_{i}(v))\leq\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(i-1)\cdot d(u,v)
.

For
i=1
, since
h_{1}(\cdot)=h_{S}(\cdot)
we get that
\min(h_{1}(u),h_{1}(v))=\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))
and the claim holds.
Next, we assume the claim holds for
i-1
and prove the claim for
i
.
From the fact that the claim holds for
i-1
, we get that either
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(2i-5)d(u,v)
or
\min(h_{i-1}(u),h_{i-1}(v))\leq\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(i-2)\cdot d(u,v)
. If
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(2i-5)d(u,v)
then
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(2i-3)d(u,v)
and the claim holds. Otherwise, we have that
\min(h_{i-1}(u),h_{i-1}(v))\leq\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(i-2)\cdot d(u,v)
.
From
Lemma

9
it follows that either

\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{i-1}(u),h_{i-1}(v))+d(u,v)
or
\min(h_{i}(u),h_{i}(v))\leq d(u,v)+\min(h_{i-1}(u),h_{i-1}(v))
.
Since
\min(h_{i-1}(u),h_{i-1}(v))\leq\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(i-2)\cdot d(u,v)
we get that either
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(2i-3)\cdot d(u,v)
or that
\min(h_{i}(u),h_{i}(v))\leq\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(i-2)\cdot d(u,v)+d(u,v)=\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(i-1)\cdot d(u,v)
, as wanted.

Next, we show that for
i=k+1
we have that
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq 2\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(2k-1)\cdot d(u,v)
since
\min(h_{k+1}(u),h_{k+1}(v))\leq\min(h_{S}(u),h_{S}(v))+(i-1)d(u,v)
does not hold.
∎

Next, we present the second parameterized distance oracle
that supports queries only between vertices from
S
.
Notice that this oracle uses much less space than the first oracle.

Lemma 11
.

Let
k\geq 1
and let
S\subseteq V
. There is an
O(|S|^{1+\frac{1}{k}})
-space distance oracle that given two vertices
u,v\in S
, returns in
O(k)
time an estimation
\hat{d}(u,v)
such that
d(u,v)\leq\hat{d}(u,v)\leq(2k-1)d(u,v)
. The distance oracle is constructed in
O(m|S|^{\frac{1}{k}})
time.

Proof.

The construction and the query are the same as in the distance oracle from
Lemma

10
. However, instead of saving
B_{i}(u)
, for every
u\in V
and
0\leq i\leq k
, we save
B_{i}(u)
only for every
s\in S
. Thus, the space is
O(|S|^{1+\frac{1}{k}})
.
The correctness of the query follows from the fact that
\mbox{{ADO.Query}}(u,v)
only uses the bunches of
u
and
v
. Thus, if
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
then we have the information needed in
\mbox{{ADO.Query}}(s_{1},s_{2})
.
∎

Since the distance oracle of
Lemma

10
and the distance oracle of
Lemma

11
differ only by the supported vertex pairs, we refer to the oracle of
Lemma

10
as
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P}(G,k,S)
and to the oracle of
Lemma

11
as
\mbox{{ADO}}_{P^{\prime}}(G,k,S)
.

4.3
A new hierarchy of distance oracles

In this section, we present a new distance oracle composed of a hierarchy of
t=\lceil\log\log{n}\rceil
distance oracles. The distance oracle computes a set
S_{t}
. The query of the distance oracle guarantees a
(2k-1)
-stretch for distances in
G_{S_{t}}
.

The distance oracle is constructed as follows. We get as an input a graph and two parameters
k
and
x_{0}
, where
k\geq 3
and
\frac{1}{k}\leq x_{0}<1
.

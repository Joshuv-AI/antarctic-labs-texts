Probing the existence of a new charged vector boson decaying into heavy neutral leptons using ultra-peripheral heavy ion collisions at ATLAS
Y.M. Oviedo-Torres 1,2, Sebastian Tapia 3,4, J. Zamora-Saa 1,2

Abstract
In this paper we explore the potential of Ultra-peripheral Collisions in the LHC to investigate new physics, focusing specifically on the production of new charged vector bosons V ± {V}^{\pm} that decay into heavy neutral leptons N L N_{L} in the context of the Vector Scotogenic Model. We show that the ATLAS experiment searching for dilepton+met final states through UPCs of lead ions, can prove the existence of new charged vector bosons in the mass range 5–100 GeV, a region not explored by the LEP-II experiment. Our analysis identifies regions in the parameter space where the signal can be distinguished from the background with high statistical significance. Within the mass range 5 GeV < M V ± , M N L < <M_{V^{\pm}},M_{N_{L}}< 100 GeV, ATLAS can exclude scenarios with 95% of C.L. for specific mass scenarios such as (30 GeV, 20 GeV), (30 GeV, 10 GeV) and (20 GeV, 10 GeV). In a discovery context, ATLAS could reach a significance of 5 σ \sigma only for (20 GeV, 10 GeV). Furthermore, we show that in the absence of new physics signals at ATLAS, the High Luminosity LHC with UPCs of protons could explore further into larger mass ranges, specifically 100 GeV < M V ± < <M_{V^{\pm}}< 200 GeV and 5 GeV < M N L < <M_{N_{L}}< 200 GeV. We find that the HL-LHC can exclude this mass range with 95% of C.L., and most scenarios can achieve a discovery significance of 5 σ \sigma .

We know that protons are particles composed by quarks and gluons that interact through the strong nuclear force. This necessarily implies that conventional proton-proton collisions generate a significant amount of irreducible hadronic background, making it difficult the identification of many interesting processes that could reveal signals of new physics, especially those that occur at energies below the available partonic center-of-mass energy. For this reason, it is essential to mitigate the impact of the background contamination on relevant signals using advanced data detection and analysis techniques, as well as improvements in reconstruction and event selection algorithms, in order to properly separate the signals of interest from the background. A strategy that has been little explored in the literature which can help minimize this hadronic background is the search for new physics through Ultra-Peripheral Collisions (UPCs) of heavy ions. The main characteristic of this type of collision is that the beam components (such as lead ions in the LHC experiment, for example

These fields are very intense due to the high electric charge of heavy ion nuclei (for example, the lead nucleus has an electric charge of +82e, in contrast to the proton, whose electric charge is only +1e). This intensity allows for the generation of interesting processes without hadronic contamination, in which new particles can be produced, particularly charged vector bosons and Heavy Neutral Leptons (HNLs), such as those studied in this work.

Motivated by the reduced background produced in UPC collisions compared to conventional proton-proton collisions, this paper studies the feasibility of producing new charged vector bosons that decay into HNLs, predicted in the Vector Scotogenic Model (VSM), through UPCs for the current stage of the ATLAS Experiment and future high-luminosity configuration of the LHC (HL-LHC). II
Probing new physics with Ultra-peripheral Collisions of Heavy Ions

It is well-established that conventional proton-proton collisions, such as those taking place most of the time at the LHC, represent one of the primary avenues for testing phenomena predicted by the SM and for directly or indirectly constraining many BSM theories. This is largely due to the nature of the proton itself. As a hadron, most of the interactions in these collisions are dominated by the strong interaction between its partonic components. These partonic interactions give rise to a rich phenomenology that can be used to constrain, for example, the properties of hypothetical particles. However, these types of collisions also present a significant challenge: the enormous amount of hadronic background generated, which can obscure the signals of new physics.

An alternative and relatively underexplored approach that significantly reduces the QCD background and creates an ideal environment for searching for new particles is the use of Ultra-peripheral Collisions (UPCs) of Heavy Ions. In UPCs, the electromagnetic field of one nucleus induces inelastic transitions in the other, resulting in processes that share the same transition matrix elements as those induced by real photons
Bertulani et al. 2005
;
Bertulani 2023
. This implies that the majority of interactions in UPCs are governed by electromagnetic interactions rather than strong interactions. Furthermore, in most UPC processes, there is no fragmentation of the beam constituents. These two characteristics lead to cleaner final states, which can result in improved signal-to-background ratios for new physics searches compared to conventional proton-proton collisions.

Motivated by the advantages that ultra-peripheral collisions offer for the search for new particles and considering the higher energy per particle achievable in proton beams compared to lead beams, the following section extends this analysis to the high-luminosity configuration of the LHC (HL-LHC) using ultra-peripheral proton collisions.

In contrast, in proton-proton collisions, the absence of these intense electromagnetic fields further suppresses UPC interactions. However, the expected luminosity of the LHC for pp-UPC is significantly higher than the current luminosity achieved by ATLAS with lead ion UPCs.

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we have investigated the potential of Ultra-peripheral Collisions at the LHC to prove new physics beyond the Standard Model, focusing on the production of new charged vector bosons that decay into heavy neutral leptons predicted by the Vector Scotogenic Model. In the first part of this work, we demonstrated that the ATLAS Experiment with Ultra-peripheral collisions of Lead Ions can search for a new charged vector boson in a mass region where the LEP-II Experiment did not search, particularly in the range of 5 to 50 GeV. We identified regions of parameter space where the signal can be distinguished from the background with high statistical significance. Our results showed that, within the analyzed mass range (5 GeV
<{M}_{{V}^{\pm}},{M}_{{N}_{L}}<
100 GeV), the ATLAS experiment could exclude with a 95% C.L. the scenarios corresponding to the masses (
{M}_{{V}^{\pm}},{M}_{{N}_{L}}
): (30 GeV, 20 GeV), (30 GeV, 10 GeV) and (20 GeV, 10 GeV). In a discovery context, ATLAS could reach a significance level of 5
\sigma
for the mass scenario (20 GeV, 10 GeV). This demonstrates the potential of the ATLAS experiment with lead UPC collisions to search for new physics, particularly in lower mass scenarios where the QCD background in conventional proton-proton collisions would obscure the signal. Furthermore, in a scenario where the hypothetical ATLAS search using UPCs of lead ions reveals no signals of new physics, we show that the HL-LHC, with UPCs of protons, has the potential to exclude or discover scenarios within the mass ranges 100 GeV
<{M}_{{V}^{\pm}}<
200 GeV and 5 GeV
<{M}_{{N}_{L}}<
200 GeV, for the same production channel. For this high-luminosity stage of the LHC, we find that all mass scenarios can be excluded with a 95% C.L., or in a discovery context, 5
\sigma
can be achieved in most mass scenarios.

Emotion correction for weibo users via weakly supervised learning and semantic understanding
Authors: Ganglong Duan, Zhengjuan Chen, Yanying Shang, Hongquan Xue
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryWith the fast pace of modern society, individuals are facing increasing psychological stress and emotional challenges. As an important platform for public emotional expression, Weibo contains extensive textual data reflecting users’ emotional states. This paper proposes an emotion correction approach for Weibo users that integrates weakly supervised learning with semantic understanding. The approach combines emotion recognition, emotion-cause classification, and personalized content recommendation, where the emotional polarity of Weibo texts is first identified, negative posts are then classified into corresponding emotion-cause categories, and positively oriented content related to users’ emotion causes is subsequently recommended to provide targeted emotional support. Experiments on a real-world Weibo dataset show that the proposed approach achieves accuracies of 96.2% and 83.5% in emotion recognition and emotion-cause classification, respectively, while also delivering strong recommendation performance. These results demonstrate the potential of the proposed model for supporting users’ emotional correction and contributing to mental health.

Introduction

In today’s fast-paced society, psychological stress and emotional problems are becoming increasingly prevalent, with a growing number of people experiencing negative emotions such as anxiety, depression, and loneliness. Traditional psychological counseling services are limited by manpower, geographic constraints, and social stigma associated with mental illness, making large-scale preventive screening and real-time intervention difficult to achieve.1 The widespread use of social media has provided the public with diverse channels for emotional expression, among which Weibo has become an important platform for individuals to voluntarily share their emotions.2

In recent years, natural language processing (NLP) has achieved significant progress and has been widely applied in various domains, such as sentiment analysis,3 personality detection,4 and content generation.5 These technologies enable machines to understand and analyze human language at scale, providing powerful tools for the large-scale analysis of user-generated textual data. Among these applications, sentiment analysis has attracted considerable attention due to its ability to identify emotional polarity and understand public opinion in online environments. Weibo posts contain rich emotional information, and performing sentiment analysis on such data is of great research value.

Emotions are closely related to human health and play a crucial role in psychological well-being. Studies have shown that persistent negative emotional states and difficulties in emotion regulation are closely associated with various psychological problems, such as depression and anxiety.6 Medical studies have further demonstrated that individuals with depression exhibit significant differences in emotional dynamics compared to healthy populations, and emotional features can serve as an important basis for automatic depression detection.7 From a broader perspective, the widespread diffusion of negative emotions on social platforms, often characterized as emotional contagion, may also have significant social impacts, influencing public opinion and shaping the overall online environment.8 Therefore, addressing public emotional issues is of great importance for both individual well-being and social stability. However, existing studies in sentiment analysis have primarily focused on emotional polarity recognition, with limited attention to the underlying causes of negative emotions, thereby making it difficult to address users’ emotional issues at their root.

Meanwhile, in this era of information overload, recommender systems have emerged as effective tools for personalized information filtering, widely applied in personalized recommendation domains such as e-commerce and news. Yet, conventional recommender systems typically focus on user interests while neglecting their latent emotional needs. If sentiment analysis and recommendation technologies could be integrated—so that users exhibiting negative emotions receive personalized recommendations of emotionally corrective content—this would offer a novel perspective for psychological health intervention.

To address the aforementioned challenges, this study proposes an “emotion-corrective recommendation” framework featuring a three-stage architecture: (1) recognition—identifying negative users through a sentiment analysis model; (2) classification—extracting the causes behind users’ negative emotions using an emotion-cause classification model; and (3) recommendation—performing content matching based on the identified causes to achieve targeted emotional recommendations. The framework follows the logical progression of “emotion recognition-cause analysis-emotional intervention,” thereby forming a closed-loop process from emotion detection to regulation.

The specific research content mainly consists of four aspects: data construction, emotion recognition, emotion-cause modeling, and the design of an emotion-corrective recommendation mechanism. First, user-generated textual data are collected and preprocessed from the Weibo platform to construct an experimental dataset for sentiment analysis and emotion-cause research. Second, an emotion recognition model is developed to identify the emotional tendencies of user posts and to filter texts exhibiting prominent negative emotional characteristics. Subsequently, from the perspective of the causes of negative emotions, weakly supervised signals and semantic representation methods are introduced to model and classify emotion causes in Weibo texts, and the dominant emotion causes are further summarized and analyzed at the user level. Finally, based on the results of emotion recognition and emotion-cause analysis, an emotion-corrective content recommendation mechanism is constructed. By matching users’ emotion causes with positively oriented content, targeted emotional interventions for users with negative emotions are achieved, forming a complete research workflow from emotion detection and cause analysis to emotional regulation.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows.(1)We construct large-scale and diverse Weibo text datasets with corresponding data collection and preprocessing strategies, providing a reusable data foundation for social media sentiment analysis and emotion-cause modeling.(2)We develop and implement an emotion recognition model tailored to Weibo texts, enabling accurate identification of users with negative emotions and supporting subsequent emotion-cause analysis and corrective recommendation.(3)We propose an emotion-cause modeling method by integrating weakly supervised signals with semantic representations, effectively improving the generalization ability of fine-grained emotion-cause recognition under limited annotation conditions.(4)We design a content recommendation mechanism that integrates emotional polarity and emotion-cause consistency, transforming emotion analysis results into actionable positive content recommendations and establishing a closed-loop framework for emotion correction.

Results

Dataset

Data collection

In sentiment analysis and emotion correction tasks, the selection of datasets directly affects model performance and the reliability of research conclusions. Existing Weibo datasets are mostly constructed at the post level—each user corresponds to only one Weibo post—which fails to capture users’ overall emotional states over extended periods. The objective of this study is to identify users’ overall emotional tendencies and further achieve emotion correction by analyzing the causes of their emotions. Therefore, the data must be user-centered, covering multiple Weibo posts from each user over a certain period, to more accurately capture their long-term emotional characteristics. Based on this requirement, existing public datasets are insufficient; therefore, self-constructed datasets were built.

This study constructs three datasets in total.(1)Weibo User Dataset: This dataset serves as the core data foundation of the study and is used across experiments in all three modules. It consists of Weibo posts published by users over approximately one year and is used to construct users’ overall emotional profiles. The final dataset includes 651,949 posts from 11,128 Weibo users collected between January and October 2025, with each user contributing at least 10 posts.(2)Emotion-Cause Lexicon Corpus Dataset: This dataset is used for emotion-cause modeling in the second module and provides corpus support for constructing the emotion-cause lexicon and generating weak supervision signals. It is organized around multiple representative emotion-related themes, with each theme containing approximately 50,000 related Weibo posts, resulting in a total size of about 400,000 posts.(3)Recommendation Candidate Dataset: This dataset is used to construct candidate content for the emotion-corrective recommendation module and serves as the source corpus for delivering positive emotional content to users. It consists of 2,545 Weibo posts collected based on different thematic keywords.

Data preprocessing

After data collection, the raw Weibo text must be normalized and cleaned to ensure input quality and consistency for the models. The preprocessing pipeline includes the following steps.(1)Data Cleaning

(1)Data Cleaning

Data Cleaning

Weibo posts often contain a large amount of noise irrelevant to semantics, such as HTML tags, emojis, URLs, and special symbols. Using regular expressions and Python’s built-in string processing functions, such irrelevant characters were systematically removed.(2)Chinese Word Segmentation

(2)Chinese Word Segmentation

Chinese Word Segmentation

Since Chinese text is not naturally space-delimited, the Jieba segmentation tool (precise mode) was used to segment cleaned Weibo posts into word tokens, ensuring accurate semantic segmentation and improved embedding learning.(3)Stopword Removal

(3)Stopword Removal

Stopword Removal

Functional words contribute little to semantic representation. Based on the Harbin Institute of Technology (HIT) stopword list, these high-frequency but semantically weak tokens were removed to enhance the model’s ability to capture core information.

For the Emotion-Cause Lexicon Corpus Dataset, two additional processing steps were performed: deduplication and class balancing. Duplicate posts generated during multi-page scraping were removed using the pandas.drop_duplicates() function to ensure data uniqueness. To mitigate bias caused by class imbalance, 50,000 samples were randomly selected from each cause category, resulting in a lexicon corpus with a uniform class distribution.

Metrics for recommendation module

To evaluate the performance of the recommendation module, we adopt three ranking-oriented metrics, including Precision@k, normalized discounted cumulative gain (NDCG@k), and mean average precision (MAP@k). To assess recommendation quality, a three-point rating scale (0–2) is adopted to evaluate each recommended Weibo post. The scoring criteria are defined as follows.0 (Irrelevant/Unhelpful): The content is completely unrelated to the user’s emotional cause, or although related, it expresses negativity that may intensify the user’s negative emotions.1 (Partially Relevant, Limited Help): The content has some connection to the user’s emotional cause, but it is not sufficiently positive, or its effect on emotional regulation is limited.2 (Relevant and Positive, Effective): The content is clearly related to the user’s emotional cause, and it conveys a positive, constructive message that can effectively alleviate the negative emotions associated with that cause.(1)Precision@k

We analyze their parameter size (Params), floating-point operations (FLOPs), and actual training time. The BiLSTM model contains approximately 2.23M parameters with about 23M FLOPs per sample. Considering both classification performance and computational efficiency, the BiLSTM model provides the best balance between effectiveness and efficiency.

Negative emotion cause classification module

In the second module, the goal is to classify the negative Weibo posts of the previously identified users into eight emotion cause categories. A hybrid method combining lexicon-based weak supervision and BERT modeling was used.(1)Baseline Model ComparisonTo evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed hybrid approach, a set of comparative experiments was conducted. As shown in the results, the pure lexicon-based method can achieve reasonable performance without manual annotation; however, due to its reliance on keyword matching and lack of contextual semantic modeling, its discriminative ability is limited. When neural models such as TextCNN and BiLSTM are introduced and trained on lexicon-generated pseudo labels, semantic modeling capability is enhanced, but performance does not consistently surpass the pure lexicon method, likely due to noise in the pseudo labels.

The pure BERT method, which relies solely on a small annotated dataset, also shows limited performance improvement because of insufficient training data. In contrast, the proposed Lexicon + BERT approach achieves the best performance across all evaluation metrics. Discussion

This study addresses the problem of identifying and intervening in negative emotions among social media users by proposing an integrated emotion-corrective recommendation framework. To address the limitations of existing studies, the proposed approach combines sentiment analysis, emotion-cause modeling, and recommendation strategies into a unified pipeline. Specifically, the framework follows a three-stage process of “recognition-classification-recommendation,” forming a closed-loop mechanism from emotion perception to emotional intervention. The results demonstrate that the proposed framework can effectively capture users’ emotional tendencies and reveal their underlying causes, thereby enabling more targeted and positive emotional guidance.

Comparison with related work

Emotion correction

From a psychological perspective, emotion correction refers to helping individuals adjust maladaptive emotional response patterns through specific interventions, thereby establishing healthier emotional experiences and modes of expression. At present, systematic research on emotion correction remains limited. Miu et al.12 conducted a meta-analysis and found that individuals with childhood trauma are more prone to difficulties in emotion regulation, tend to suppress or ruminate on negative emotions, and consequently face a higher risk of psychological disorders. Troy et al.13 proposed an integrative theoretical framework of emotion regulation, suggesting that psychological resilience results from the interaction among emotion regulation strategies, adversity characteristics, and cultural factors, and emphasizing the central role of emotion regulation in coping with adversity. Stellern et al.14 also demonstrated through meta-analysis that individuals with substance use disorders experience greater difficulties in emotional control—particularly in impulse management and the use of regulation strategies—than healthy individuals. Overall, existing studies have been primarily situated within the field of clinical psychology, focusing on theoretical models of self-emotion regulation or analyses of variable relationships.

In addition, within research that applies computational techniques to mental health analysis, many studies focus on detecting or identifying specific psychological problems from user data. For example, Xu et al.15 proposed ABAFnet, an attention-driven acoustic feature fusion network that integrates four types of acoustic features and incorporates a type-adaptive CNN, an LSTM-Attention mechanism, and a dynamic weight fusion strategy to improve depression detection performance. Zhu et al.16 introduced a multimodal model based on image captioning and cross-attention mechanisms, which enhances visual representations through semantic image descriptions and OCR information and fuses textual and visual features via multi-head cross-attention to accomplish anxiety recognition. However, these studies remain at the detection stage and offer limited exploration of how to provide effective and automated follow-up interventions for identified users. Therefore, whether in theory-oriented psychological research or computational approaches focused on mental-state recognition, studies on concrete emotion correction strategies that can be implemented in specific contexts are still relatively scarce.

In clinical psychology, cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is recognized as one of the most effective forms of psychological intervention. Proposed by Beck,17,18 CBT is grounded in the principle of identifying and modifying irrational cognitive patterns and negative automatic thoughts to alter emotional responses and behavioral outcomes. This provides an important theoretical foundation for the present study. Building on the psychological concept of emotion correction, this study extends and applies it by constructing a computational model based on social media data. Unlike previous research relying on users’ self-regulation, the proposed RCR closed-loop system proactively delivers positively oriented content. This approach transforms emotion correction from a theoretical construct into a computational and automated intervention, offering a novel pathway for psychological health support in non-clinical settings.

Text sentiment analysis

Text sentiment analysis is a key NLP task that aims to determine the emotional polarity of text through automated analysis of lexical, syntactic, and semantic information. Current mainstream approaches are based on deep learning, using neural networks to automatically extract semantic features, capture affective signals, and model contextual dependencies for emotion classification. Recent research has continuously sought to improve the accuracy of emotional polarity classification. For example, Onan19 proposed a convolutional recurrent neural network architecture combining BiLSTM and GRU with an intra-group enhancement mechanism to optimize feature extraction. Talaat20 integrated pre-trained BERT models with BiLSTM and BiGRU structures for sentiment classification, demonstrating that models incorporating BiGRU significantly outperform traditional and single BERT baselines. Ouyang et al.21 introduced the ASHGAT model based on a hypergraph attention mechanism, which constructs word-level hypergraphs that integrate syntactic and semantic relations, thereby enhancing the modeling of associations between aspect words and sentiment words. Meng et al.22 proposed a multi-message passing framework based on heterogeneous graphs, which integrates multimodal features including text, audio, and visual signals and models interactions among speakers through graph-based message passing, for emotion category identification in conversational contexts.

Although deep learning-based sentiment analysis has achieved remarkable progress in emotion classification, user-generated content contains not only emotional categories but also the underlying causes that trigger these emotions. In real-world applications, understanding the underlying causes and contextual events behind emotions is of greater practical significance, particularly for emotion regulation and psychological intervention. Nevertheless, research in this area remains relatively limited. In recent years, with the continuous advancement of information extraction tasks, emotion cause extraction (ECE) has gradually gained increasing attention.23 The task of ECE was first introduced by Lee et al.,24 whose goal was to identify the underlying causes behind emotions expressed in text. They constructed the first Chinese emotion cause corpus and proposed a rule-based detection method that relied on linguistic cues. However, their approach required prior emotion annotation before cause extraction, thereby overlooking the interdependence between emotions and their causes. To address this issue, Xia et al.25 proposed the emotion-cause pair extraction (ECPE) task, which enables simultaneous extraction of emotions and corresponding causes from documents without pre-labeling. ECPE is typically divided into two stages: individual extraction of emotional and causal clauses via multi-task learning, followed by emotion-cause pairing and filtering.

While ECPE has substantially advanced research in emotion cause analysis, several challenges remain. First, ECPE is inherently a joint extraction task with high modeling complexity, making unified representation difficult. Second, the task requires simultaneous annotation of emotional and causal clauses, which is highly labor-intensive; moreover, public datasets remain limited in scale and diversity. Third, many state-of-the-art ECPE models depend on complex model architectures and training strategies, which impose high computational costs during training and deployment. Even the best-performing ECPE models on public datasets still exhibit considerable room for improvement in accuracy and robustness, particularly when dealing with complex or implicit emotional-causal relationships.

This study not only extends the focus of sentiment analysis from traditional polarity classification to the exploration of the underlying causes of negative emotions, but also proposes a lightweight and practical classification approach that reduces dependence on large-scale manual annotation.

Pre-trained language models and weakly supervised learning

In recent years, PLMs have represented a major breakthrough in NLP. Trained through self-supervised learning on large-scale corpora, these models capture rich semantic and syntactic representations. Through fine-tuning, knowledge from PLMs can be effectively transferred to downstream tasks, enabling rapid adaptation to diverse application domains. BERT, proposed by Devlin et al.,26 adopts a bidirectional Transformer architecture and performs deep semantic modeling through masked language modeling and next sentence prediction. It has been widely applied in various NLP tasks, such as sentiment analysis, named entity recognition (NER), and dialogue generation. For instance, Wan et al.27 proposed ECR-BERT, which integrates the OCC emotion model’s cognitive rules with BERT for sentiment analysis of emergency-related social media texts. Li et al.28 proposed a self-distillation-based multi-level semantic enhancement BERT model for Chinese NER, which introduced multi-granularity semantic features and a reweighted distillation mechanism to improve recognition performance. Lv et al.29 proposed a transformer-based dialogue generation model for Chinese text, which incorporated representations of the large-scale pre-trained Chinese BERT model NEZHA (a Chinese BERT-based model) into character-level embeddings via a word-phrase fusion embedding mechanism to enhance semantic understanding, and additionally optimized sparse Softmax to boost generation capabilities, thus effectively elevating the quality of Chinese dialogue generation. Despite their success, pre-trained models still face challenges such as high annotation costs and limited domain adaptability in practical applications.

To alleviate these issues, weakly supervised learning has been proposed as a viable solution. This paradigm aims to exploit incomplete, inexact, or limited supervision—often through rules, lexicons, pseudo-labels, or teacher models—to generate indirect training signals and reduce manual labeling costs.30 A growing body of research has explored neural model training under weakly or semi-supervised conditions. For example, Lien et al.31 proposed a generalized weak supervision (GWS) framework for neural information retrieval, which iteratively re-labels data to improve ranking performance without manual annotations. Xie et al.32 introduced a class-aware pseudo-labeling (CAP) method for weakly supervised multi-label image classification, which adaptively sets class-specific thresholds to generate pseudo-labels and jointly trains with true labels, improving classification accuracy. You et al.33 developed a semi-supervised contrastive learning framework for medical image segmentation, which enhances model stability and performance under limited annotation conditions through variance reduction and stratified sampling.

The combination of deep learning and weak supervision has become an important direction for addressing data scarcity. Compared with image data, where visual features and augmentation techniques are more intuitive, textual data pose greater challenges due to semantic complexity and label noise sensitivity.34 However, integrating PLMs within a weakly supervised framework leverages their deep semantic understanding to mitigate pseudo-label noise. For example, Wallaart et al.35 proposed a weakly supervised Sentence-based Aspect Category and Sentiment Classification (SB-ASC) method, which utilizes Sentence-BERT to generate high-quality pseudo-labels and jointly trains them with a BERT model for aspect and sentiment prediction. Building on this idea, the present study integrates weakly supervised learning with pre-trained language models to address the dual challenges of limited annotation and textual noise, and develops a negative emotion-cause classification model that achieves strong semantic representation and domain adaptability even under low-resource conditions.

Recommendation algorithms

Recommender systems were originally developed to help users efficiently locate content matching their needs. Their core principle is to predict user preferences for unseen items by analyzing users’ historical behaviors, interests, and item attributes. Such systems have been widely applied across e-commerce, social media, and other online platforms, attracting considerable attention from researchers in recent years. With the deepening of research, scholars have gradually begun integrating emotional factors into recommendation systems. Abbasi-Moud et al.36 proposed a semantic clustering-based sentiment analysis approach for tourism recommendation, which analyzes emotional tendencies in user reviews before generating personalized recommendations that combine contextual and destination features. Choudhary et al.37 developed SARWAS, a deep ensemble learning method that fuses sentiment analysis outputs with user ratings and models them using multilayer neural networks to achieve more accurate and personalized product recommendations. Darraz et al.38 combined lexicon-based and deep learning approaches by integrating TextBlob and MLP models with DeepMF collaborative filtering to improve hotel recommendation accuracy.

Although such emotion-aware recommender systems can align recommendations with users’ emotional tendencies, they primarily capture attitudinal preferences toward content or products rather than addressing users’ underlying psychological and emotional states. In essence, these systems remain interest-driven. More critically, consistently recommending content that matches users’ current negative emotions may exacerbate these emotions—a phenomenon termed the “emotion amplification” effect. Mechanistically, this is similar to the “preference amplification” effect, where users mainly consume recommended content, leading to a narrowing of interests, reinforcement of preferences, content homogenization, and the formation of “filter bubbles.”39 For instance, when a user experiences heartbreak, continuously recommending sad quotes or breakup-related content may deepen negative emotions rather than alleviate them. Hence, emotionally congruent recommendations are not necessarily emotionally beneficial.

To address this issue, Liu et al.40 proposed a novel emotion-based personalized music recommendation (EPMR) framework built on LSTM networks. The framework incorporates a “care factor” as a bias parameter, ensuring that recommended music is slightly more positive than the user’s current mood, thereby promoting emotional uplift. Inspired by this work, the present study introduces the concept of emotion-corrective recommendation, aiming to achieve emotion correction through Weibo content recommendation. However, in contrast to the EPMR framework, this study advances emotion modeling by not only identifying users’ emotional states but also extracting the underlying causes of their negative emotions from textual data. This enables genuinely targeted interventions, allowing the system to “treat the cause rather than the symptom” by recommending positive Weibo content tailored to each user’s specific emotional cause.

Overall, although existing studies have made substantial progress in emotion-related research from multiple perspectives, several limitations remain. First, research on emotion correction is still largely confined to theoretical frameworks in psychology, with the limited exploration of computational and automated intervention methods in real-world scenarios. Second, most sentiment analysis studies focus primarily on emotion polarity recognition, while the underlying causes of emotions are often overlooked, limiting their practical value in emotional intervention. Third, although pre-trained language models and weakly supervised learning have shown strong potential in addressing data scarcity and improving semantic representation, their integration for fine-grained emotion-cause analysis remains insufficiently explored. Finally, existing emotion-aware recommender systems mainly emphasize aligning recommendations with user preferences or emotional tendencies, but lack mechanisms to actively guide users toward positive emotional states, and may even lead to emotion amplification effects. Therefore, there remains a research gap in developing an integrated framework that can simultaneously identify users’ emotional states, uncover the underlying causes of negative emotions, and provide targeted emotion-corrective recommendations.

In the emotion recognition stage, the model achieves high classification accuracy (96.2%), suggesting that the combination of Word2Vec embeddings and BiLSTM architecture is capable of capturing contextual emotional features in semantically diverse, noisy, and context-dependent Weibo texts. This provides a reliable foundation for subsequent modules. In the emotion-cause classification stage, the hybrid approach integrating lexicon-based weak supervision and BERT demonstrates clear advantages over both purely rule-based and fully supervised methods, particularly in balancing semantic representation with limited annotated data. As emotion-cause identification is still relatively challenging and less explored, the performance of this module suggests that incorporating lexicon-based weak supervision with BERT-based contextual modeling provides an effective way to capture fine-grained emotional triggers. In the recommendation stage, the proposed hybrid strategy that combines sentiment filtering with similarity-based matching significantly outperforms baseline methods in Precision@k, NDCG@k, and MAP@k, and both qualitative case analysis and quantitative evaluation confirm its ability to provide users with targeted recommendations of emotionally corrective Weibo content that is closely related to their underlying emotional causes. Taken together, these findings suggest that the three modules function not only as independent components but also as an integrated pipeline, in which the robustness and reliability of upstream results directly support the effectiveness of downstream emotional intervention.

The proposed research demonstrates significant theoretical and practical value. From a theoretical perspective, this study extends traditional sentiment analysis from emotion polarity recognition to emotion-cause modeling and intervention-oriented applications, offering a perspective that connects emotion understanding with emotional regulation. It also moves beyond conventional recommender systems that focus primarily on user preferences by incorporating emotion-cause information into the recommendation process, thereby introducing an emotion-aware and correction-oriented modeling paradigm. In addition, by integrating methods from NLP, deep learning, and recommender systems, this study illustrates how interdisciplinary approaches can be effectively combined to address complex social and behavioral problems, providing a useful reference for related cross-domain research.

From a practical perspective, the proposed method enables a non-intrusive and low-cost intervention mechanism by leveraging social media data, allowing emotional guidance to be embedded into users’ everyday information consumption. In practice, many users are reluctant to actively seek psychological support, while the proposed framework can identify potential negative emotional states without requiring explicit user participation and deliver emotionally corrective content through recommendation. By modeling the underlying causes of users’ emotional expressions, the system provides cause-aware positive content in a more natural and less intrusive manner, helping to reduce psychological resistance. Furthermore, by mitigating the causes of negative emotional content, the proposed approach can help limit the spread of negative emotions in social networks and contribute to a healthier online environment, with potential applications in mental health support and public health services.

Limitations of the study

Despite these contributions, several limitations remain. The ultimate effectiveness of the recommendations—i.e., whether users’ emotional states improve—requires actual user feedback, which has not yet been collected, and thus the real-world impact of the emotion correction intervention has not been directly evaluated. In addition, factors such as individual differences in emotional expression, linguistic metaphors, and contextual dependency may affect the model’s generalization ability. Furthermore, the current framework mainly relies on textual data and does not fully incorporate multimodal information, which may limit its applicability in more complex social media environments.

Future research can be conducted in several directions. Online experiments or emotion-scale questionnaires can be designed to collect users’ emotional changes before and after receiving recommendations, thereby enabling real-world validation of the model’s effectiveness in emotion correction. Moreover, integrating multimodal information such as images, emojis, and comments may further improve the model’s ability to recognize complex emotional states and enhance the precision of intervention strategies. Finally, the proposed correction strategy can be combined with dialogue systems to enable real-time interaction and dynamic adjustment of recommendations based on user feedback, ultimatelyachieving a personalized emotion correction mechanism characterized by “dynamic understanding-instant intervention.”

Method details

Overall architecture

To effectively achieve precise emotional intervention and positive guidance for Weibo users, this study constructs an integrated emotion regulation framework—namely, a three-stage RCR(Recognition–Classification–Recommendation) architecture. As illustrated in Figure 1, the proposed emotion correction model consists of three core modules. The leftmost module performs emotion recognition through a hierarchical deep learning model; the middle module focuses on emotion cause classification, built upon a self-constructed emotion cause lexicon and a pre-trained language model; and the rightmost module implements recommendation, which relies on the results of the first two modules. Additionally, the recommendation module reuses parts of the model constructed in the emotion recognition stage for implementation. The modules are tightly connected in both data flow and functional objectives, forming a progressive mechanism that moves from recognition to classification to recommendation.

User sentiment analysis model based on Word2Vec and BiLSTM

The ultimate objective of this study is to achieve emotion correction through content recommendation. However, such recommendation is not intended for all users but specifically for those exhibiting emotional distress. Therefore, it is necessary to first identify the target users with negative emotions through sentiment analysis. Accordingly, sentiment analysis serves as a critical foundation for the subsequent stages and must demonstrate high reliability. Considering the semantic diversity and contextual dependence of Weibo posts, traditional machine-learning approaches struggle to capture deep semantic relationships. Therefore, we adopt a well-established deep learning approach with strong performance in sentiment analysis to perform polarity classification, constructing a Word2Vec+BiLSTM model. During model training, the embedding layer is initialized with pre-trained Word2Vec embeddings to enhance semantic representation capability. User negative emotion-cause classification model based on lexicon-based weak supervision and BERT

In the emotion recognition module, the target users of this study have already been identified. However, merely determining that a user’s emotional tendency is negative is not sufficient to support personalized emotion correction. It is also essential to understand why users experience negative emotions, which is the key to realizing individualized emotion correction. Firstly, considering that different groups face diverse emotional problems41 and that intervention strategies vary across emotional issue types, it is necessary to perform fine-grained classification of negative emotions and categorize users more precisely according to their emotional causes. Secondly, when expressing certain types of distress, users often use specific keywords that not only reflect the thematic content of the text but also reveal the underlying emotional causes. These keywords thus serve as crucial cues for classification. Meanwhile, the classification results should be interpretable and maintainable to ensure transparency and scalability in content recommendation.

To simultaneously meet the requirements for fine-grained classification, feature–cause association, and interpretability, this study develops a method combining lexicon-based weak supervision and BERT modeling. Lexicon-based approaches are commonly used in sentiment polarity classification due to their simplicity in design, high computational efficiency, and flexibility in lexicon maintenance and expansion.42

Building on the fundamental concept of lexicon-based sentiment analysis, this study introduces innovations and extensions in both lexicon construction and application. First, instead of using the traditional “positive–negative sentiment lexicon”, this study constructs a dedicated negative emotion-cause lexicon based on users’emotional sources. Specifically, common causes of negative emotions are categorized into eight major classes, and corresponding cause-specific lexicons are constructed for each category to support the preliminary classification of users’emotional causes.

Second, relying solely on lexicon matching has inherent limitations, such as difficulties in handling semantic ambiguity and capturing contextual dependencies. Therefore, this study integrates the lexicon-based method with a pre-trained language model. Specifically, lexicon matching is first used to generate initial pseudo-labels for large-scale data, which are then used as weak supervision signals to train a BERT model, followed by fine-tuning on a small set of manually annotated data. In this framework, the lexicon is not used as the final classifier. Instead, it serves as a weak supervision mechanism to generate large-scale pseudo-labeled data for training the pre-trained language model. After classifying each negative Weibo post, results are aggregated at the user level. For each user, the frequency of each cause category is counted and sorted in descending order, forming a cause list such as [“Social Issues,” “Appearance Anxiety,” “Health Problems,” “Other”].

To ensure the accuracy of subsequent recommendations, users whose emotional causes are exclusively classified as “Other” are filtered out. Since their posts do not explicitly match any predefined negative emotion keywords, it is difficult to determine their specific emotional triggers; recommendations for these users would be based solely on interests, which falls outside the scope of this emotion-corrective recommendation study. Removing such users ensures that the emotional classification and intervention strategies remain targeted and prevents potentially ineffective or inefficient interventions, thereby improving the overall efficiency of the recommendation model.

Emotion-corrective recommendation based on weibo Users’Negative emotion causes

The goal of this stage is to achieve final emotion correction based on the work of the previous modules. Drawing inspiration from the core concept of cognitive restructuring in cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), this study designs a lightweight intervention strategy. Cognitive restructuring aims to help individuals identify and modify negative thinking patterns that lead to emotional distress.18 Accordingly, the core task of this module is to recommend Weibo content that is related to users’negative emotion causes but presented from a more positive perspective, gently guiding users to view their difficulties from new angles, thereby stimulating positive emotions and alleviating negative moods. To achieve this, the recommendations must satisfy both content relevance and positive sentiment. Therefore, this study constructs a recommendation model integrating sentiment filtering and content filtering. In the content filtering stage, a hybrid semantic similarity method is designed, combining TF-IDF feature similarity and Sentence-BERT semantic similarity to balance the matching of emotional causes with user-level semantic similarity.

Quantification and statistical analysis

All computations were performed using the Python programming language, and all figures reporting model performance were generated using standard Python libraries. No statistical hypothesis tests (e.g., t-tests or ANOVA) were conducted, as the comparisons between models are based on deterministic evaluation metrics computed on fixed test sets. Model performance is evaluated using standard quantitative metrics, including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score for classification tasks, as well as Precision@k, NDCG@k, and MAP@k for the recommendation task, as defined in Section “Evaluation Metrics”.

For the sentiment analysis module, evaluation is conducted on a held-out test set comprising 15% of the full dataset. For the emotion-cause classification module, the test set consists of n = 520 manually annotated Weibo posts, with 65 samples in each of the eight emotion-cause categories. For the recommendation module, evaluation is performed on n = 50 users, where each user is associated with a recommendation list of length 10 and corresponding relevance judgments.

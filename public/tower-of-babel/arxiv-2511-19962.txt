d_{1}-(a_{X}+n+1)\leq-d_{1}+1
.
Therefore
N^{*}
maps on to a minimal generator
\eta
of
M^{*}
degree

d_{1}-(a_{X}+n+1)
and that the remaining minimal generators of

have degree
\geq-d_{1}+2
.
It follows that

, so

satisfies
(S_{1})
.

(
b
):

N^{*}=R(a_{X}+n+1-d_{1})
, by the above argument.

(
c
):
By (
a
),
\height J\geq 2
.
Let
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass R/J
.
Since
\depth M^{*}\geq 2
and
\depth R\geq 3
,
we see that
{\mathfrak{p}}\neq R_{+}
.
Hence we have a free resolution

0\longrightarrow N_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\longrightarrow M_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\longrightarrow R_{\mathfrak{p}}\longrightarrow 0

of
(R/J)_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Since
\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R/J)_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=0

, we conclude that

\height{\mathfrak{p}}=2
. Hence

is a unmixed height-two ideal.
Further for all
{\mathfrak{p}}\neq R_{+}

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that
J
is not reduced and that
J
contains three
linearly independent quadratic polynomials.
Then
R/J
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
n-1
.

Proof.

We claim that the multiplicity
e(R/J)\leq 3
. Assume the claim for now.
If
|\Min(R/J)|=3
, then using the additivity formula of multiplicity
(cf., e.g.,
[
Eis95
, Exercise 12.11]
), we see that

\displaystyle J=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Min(R/J)}{\mathfrak{p}}
, contradicting the
hypothesis that
J
is not reduced.

Assume that
|\Min(R/J)|=2
.
Write
\Min(R/J)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}\}
.
If
e(R/{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})>1
for some
1\leq i\leq 2
,
then, again, using the additivity formula, we obtain a
contradiction to the hypothesis that
J
is not reduced. Hence, without
loss of generality,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}=(x_{0},x_{1})
,

{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}=(y_{2},y_{3})
for some linear forms
y_{2},y_{3}

and
J=(x_{0},x_{1}^{2})\cap(y_{2},y_{3})
.
If
\height(x_{0},x_{1},y_{2},y_{3})=4
, then
J
would not have three
linearly independent quadratic polynomials, so, without loss of
generality,
y_{2}\in(x_{0},x_{1})
and
y_{3}\not\in(x_{0},x_{1})
.
Now consider the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow R/J\longrightarrow\begin{matrix}R/(x_{0},x_{1}^{2})\\
\oplus\\
R/(y_{2},y_{3})\end{matrix}\longrightarrow R/(x_{0},x_{1}^{2},y_{2},y_{3})\longrightarrow 0.

Note that
(x_{0},x_{1}^{2},y_{2},y_{3})
is generated by a regular sequence of
length
3
. Hence

\depth R/(x_{0},x_{1}^{2},y_{2},y_{3})=\dim R/(x_{0},x_{1}^{2},y_{2},y_{3})=n-2.

Therefore
\depth R/J=\dim R/J=n-1
.

Finally assume that
|\Min(R/J)|=1
.
Write
\Min(R/J)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}\}
.
Note that
e(R/{\mathfrak{p}})\neq 2
. If
e(R/{\mathfrak{p}})=3
, then we obtain
a contradiction to the hypothesis that
J
is reduced.
Hence, without loss of generality,
{\mathfrak{p}}=(x_{0},x_{1})
and
J={\mathfrak{p}}^{2}
.
Therefore, again,
\depth R/J=\dim R/J=n-1
.

Now to prove the claim:
Let
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
be linearly independent quadratic polynomials in
J
.
If two of them say, without loss of generality,
f_{1},f_{2}
,
form a regular sequence, then the multiplicity
e(R/(f_{1},f_{2}))=4
;
since
J
is unmixed and
J\neq(f_{1},f_{2})
,
e(R/J)\leq 3
.

Otherwise, none of
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
is irreducible.
There cannot exist a linear form
l
that divides
f_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 3
; for, otherwise, the height three ideal

\displaystyle\left(\frac{f_{1}}{l},\frac{f_{2}}{l},\frac{f_{3}}{l}\right)
would be subset of an associated prime ideal of

R/J
, a contradiction of the fact that
J
is a height-two unmixed ideal.
Therefore there exist pairwise linearly independent linear forms
l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}
such that

(f_{1},f_{2})\subseteq(l_{1})
,
(f_{2},f_{3})\subseteq(l_{2})
and

(f_{1},f_{3})\subseteq(l_{3})
.
Then, without loss of generality,
f_{1}=l_{1}l_{3}
,
f_{2}=l_{1}l_{2}
,
f_{3}=l_{2}l_{3}
.
If
l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}
are linearly independent, then

(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})=(l_{1},l_{2})\cap(l_{2},l_{3})\cap(l_{1},l_{3}).

Note that

\Ass(R/J)=\Min(R/J)\subseteq\Min(R/(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}))=\{(l_{1},l_{2}),(l_{2},l_{3}),(l_{1},l_{3})\}

and
\lambda_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/JR_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq\lambda_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})R_{\mathfrak{p}})

for every
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(R/J)
. This would imply that
J
is reduced.
Therefore we conclude that

l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}
span a two-dimensional vector-space
and
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})=(l_{1},l_{2})^{2}
. Since
\dim R/J=\dim R/(l_{1},l_{2})^{2}
, we again see that
e(R/J)\leq 3
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By way of contradiction,

Let

From (

\displaystyle a(R/J)

\displaystyle=\max\{j\mid\homology^{n-1}_{R_{+}}(R/J)_{j}\neq 0\}

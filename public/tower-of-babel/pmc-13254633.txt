Beyond cylindrical stents: does a tapered-and-flared design improve biliary drainage?
Authors: Woo Hyun Paik
Published: 2026

The use of endoscopic transpapillary biliary drainage using self-expandable metal stents (SEMS) is currently the standard palliative treatment for malignant distal biliary obstruction (MDBO). Over the past two decades, fully covered SEMS (FCSEMS) have largely replaced uncovered SEMS because of their removability and ability to prevent tumor ingrowth. Nevertheless, recurrent biliary obstruction (RBO) remains a major limitation of endoscopic biliary drainage. Stent migration and occlusion due to biliary sludge or debris are the most common mechanisms of stent dysfunction; improving stent patency remains an ongoing challenge.1-3

Murabayashi et al.4 reported clinical outcomes of a novel tapered-and-flared FCSEMS (TF-FCSEMS) in patients with unresectable MDBO. In this multicenter pilot study involving 40 patients, the authors reported a median time to RBO (TRBO) of 539 days, with relatively low rates of symptomatic migration and acceptable adverse events. Although the study was limited by its retrospective design and modest sample size, the results are noteworthy because they draw attention to an often-underappreciated determinant of biliary stent performance: geometry.

Most currently available biliary SEMS possess a cylindrical configuration that has remained largely unchanged since the introduction of metal stents. However, the mechanical forces acting on biliary stents are complex. Tumor compression, papillary resistance, duodenal peristalsis, and bile flow interact dynamically and influence stent patency and migration. Therefore, conventional cylindrical stents may not be able to optimally address these competing forces. The TF-FCSEMS evaluated in this study has been designed to address this limitation by incorporating two structural modifications: a tapered distal segment and a flared distal end. Theoretically, the tapered portion generates a pressure gradient that facilitates bile flow and reduces biliary sludge formation, whereas the flared distal end serves as an anchor at the papilla and reduces proximal migration.

Attempts to improve the stent performance through structural modifications are not new. Various anti-migration features, including anchoring fins, flared ends, and anti-migration flaps, have been introduced in recent years. However, these innovations have accompanying tradeoffs. Stents designed to improve anchoring may increase mucosal injury or reflux cholangitis, whereas designs aimed at improving bile flow may predispose to migration.5 By combining a tapered lumen with a distal flare, the TF-FCSEMS represents an attempt to balance these competing mechanical considerations.

The clinical outcomes reported by Murabayashi et al.4 are promising. The median TRBO reported in this pilot study compares favorably with the previously reported outcomes of conventional FCSEMS for MDBO. Moreover, symptomatic stent migration occurs in only a small proportion of patients. These findings suggest that the combined tapered-and-flared design may influence stent behavior in clinically meaningful ways.

However, several important factors should be considered when interpreting these results. First, the benefit of the TF-FCSEMS appears to depend strongly on the anatomical relationship between the malignant stricture and the tapered portion of the stent. In the present study, strictures located outside the tapered segment were identified as significant risk factors of RBO. This observation suggests that the mechanical advantages of the tapered design may only be realized when the malignant stricture lies within approximately 3 cm of the papilla. In practice, this means that the TF-FCSEMS may be the most effective for true distal biliary strictures, whereas its advantage may be less pronounced in more proximal lesions.

Second, the effect of antitumor therapy on stent behavior warrants careful consideration. With advances in systemic therapy for pancreaticobiliary malignancies, tumor shrinkage following chemotherapy or chemoradiotherapy has become more common. Such changes may alter the radial forces acting on the stent and predispose it to migration. Indeed, in the present study, several cases of stent migration occurred after tumor regression. Therefore, future studies evaluating new stent designs should consider incorporating oncological treatment variables and tumor responses into their analyses.

Another important issue is the coexistence of a malignant duodenal obstruction. Duodenal stenosis frequently accompanies advanced pancreaticobiliary cancer and may alter bile flow and stent function. Although the present study did not demonstrate a clear association between the flared distal end and food impaction, the possibility of increased duodenobiliary reflux remains a concern. Evaluating stent outcomes according to the established classifications of malignant duodenal obstruction may help clarify this issue in future studies.

The risk of cystic duct obstruction is another consideration when fully covered stents are placed across the papillae. Although the tapered design of the TF-FCSEMS may reduce the mechanical compression of the cystic duct compared with some conventional designs, the potential risk of cholecystitis remains an important clinical consideration. Therefore, careful patient selection and consideration of prophylactic gallbladder drainage may therefore are warranted.

Despite these limitations, the study by Murabayashi et al.4 highlighted an important direction for innovation in the design of biliary stents. As the survival of patients with pancreaticobiliary malignancies has improved with antitumor therapy, maintaining durable biliary drainage has become increasingly important. Incremental improvements in stent engineering, including the optimization of stent geometry, may translate into meaningful clinical benefits.

Ultimately, the key question raised by this study is not simply whether the TF-FCSEMS is feasible, but whether its design truly improves outcomes when compared with the existing FCSEMS. Answering this question will require prospective comparative studies and randomized trials that account for tumor characteristics, oncological treatment, and duodenal obstruction.

The TF-FCSEMS represents a thoughtful attempt to reconsider a device whose fundamental design has remained largely unchanged for several decades. Whether modifying the stent geometry can meaningfully improve long-term biliary patency remains to be determined; however, this concept merits further investigation.

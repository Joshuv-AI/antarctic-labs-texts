A Composition-Based Approach to EKR Problems
Javad B. Ebrahimi and Ali Taherkhani

Abstract
. Let ℱ \F be a family of subsets of a finite set X X . A subfamily of ℱ \F is intersecting if any two members of ℱ \F has nonempty intersection. We say ℱ \F has the Erdős–Ko–Rado property (EKR property), if, for any fixed element of X X the subfamily of all sets in ℱ \mathcal{F} that contain x x is an intersecting subfamily of ℱ \mathcal{F} of possible maximum size. We say ℱ \F has the strong Erdős–Ko–Rado property (the strong EKR property), if every intersecting subfamily of ℱ \mathcal{F} of maximum size is of the form consisting of all members that contain a fixed element of X X . In this paper, we introduce a general compositional framework for proving the EKR property and the strong EKR property for certain set systems. Our approach is based on a composition lemma, which we develop and explain in detail. To demonstrate the effectiveness of this tool, we present simple proofs of several previously known results in the field. As an application, we show that for every fixed r r -uniform hypergraph H H and all sufficiently large integers n n , the family of all subhypergraphs of the complete r r -uniform hypergraph on n n vertices that are isomorphic to H H satisfies the strong EKR property, where two copies of H H are considered intersecting if they share at least one common hyperedge. This result provides a notable generalization of the earlier work of Meagher and Moura, Kamat and Misra, and of Borg and Meagher on the strong EKR property for perfect machings and k k -matchings in complete graphs. We establish an explicit linear bound in k k on the number of vertices of complete and complete bipartite graphs such that the family of k k -cycles in K n K_{n} and K n , n K_{n,n} satisfies the EKR and strong EKR properties.

Introduction

The Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem is one of the most well-known and fundamental results in the area of extremal combinations.
This theorem was proved in 1938 and published in 1961 by Erdős, Ko, and Rado
[

Two permutations

An intersecting family of permutations is a subset of permutations such that every pair of its elements intersect.
In 1977, Deza and Frankl showed that the maximum size of an intersecting family of permutations on

It worth noting that a permutation on

K_{n,n}

We say two matchings in a graph are intersecting if they have at least one common edge.
Consequently, every intersecting family of permutations corresponds to
an intersecting family of perfect matchings in the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}

Meager and Moura proved that in the complete graph on an even number of vertices, the largest size

In 2017, Godsil and Meagher presented an algebraic proof of this result

We say that two cycles in a graph are intersecting if they have at least one common edge.
More generally, we say two subhypergraphs

4.
Composition Framework

In this section, we develop a composition framework for deriving EKR-type results. This section consists of two subsections, each of which describes a lemma: namely,
the composition lemma and the
G
-balanced lemma. These lemmas are used to establish EKR-type
results in the subsequent section.

Each subsection begins with essential definitions, followed by the formal statement of the lemma.
After introducing each lemma, we present illustrative examples demonstrating its application.
These examples provide new proofs of previously established results, some of which are notably
shorter and more elementary. The proofs of the lemmas are postponed to Section
6
.

4.1.
Composition Lemma

Composing mathematical structures in a suitable way is a well-established approach in mathematics, often employed to construct new objects that satisfy desired properties. In this work, we present a method for composing two EKR families to form a new family, and we show, in what we call the composition lemma, that this family retains the EKR property.
In order to present the composition lemma, we first need some definitions.

Definition 3
.

Let
\ell\leq m\leq n
be positive integers.
Let
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{M}

i)

Regular relation.

A relation

Let

be a finite set of indices. If for every

\mathcal{L}
to
\mathcal{M}
, then we say

\sim_{{}_{\mathcal{I}}}\mbox{$\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\ $}\{\sim_{i}|i\in\mathcal{I}\}

to
\mathcal{M}
.

iii)

Let
\sim_{\I}
be a family of regular relations from
\mathcal{L}
to

For every element

and

\mathcal{M}^{{}^{(i)}}_{{}_{L}}\mbox{$\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\ $}\{M\in\mathcal{M}|\,\,L\sim_{i}M\}.

Let
\sim_{\I}
be a family of regular relations from
\mathcal{L}
to
\mathcal{M}
.
For every element

\mathcal{L}^{{}^{(i)}}_{{}_{M}}\mbox{$\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\ $}\{L\in\mathcal{L}|\,\,L\sim_{i}M\}.

(EKR Chain and Special EKR Chain)

Corollary 1

(Erdős-Ko-Rado Theorem
[
8
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 2k
be two positive integer numbers. Then, the family of all
k
-subsets of an n-element set
X
is an EKR family.

Proof.

Let
X=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
,
\LL={X\choose k}
,
\M=\{X\}
, and
\I=\mathring{S}_{n}
, where
\mathring{S}_{n}
is the set of all cyclic orders of elements of
X
.
For a
\pi\in\mathring{S}_{n}
and an element
i\in X
, we define a cyclic interval
A_{\pi,i}
by

A_{\pi,i}=\{\pi(i),\pi(i+1),\ldots,\pi(i+k-1)\},

where addition is modulo
n
.

For every element
\pi\in\mathring{S}_{n}
, define the relation
\sim_{\pi}
as follows. A
k
-subset
L
of
X
satisfies
L\sim_{\pi}X
if for some
i\in X

we have
L=A_{\pi,i}
.

We show that the triple
(\LL,\M,\sim_{\mathring{S}_{n}})
is an EKR chain. To see this, first note that for every fixed
\pi\in\mathring{S}_{n}

and
M=X
, we have
\LL^{{}^{(\pi)}}_{{}_{M}}=\{L|\,L\sim_{\pi}M\}=\{A_{\pi,i}|\,i\in X\}
is an EKR family. This claim is simply because when
n\geq 2k
, the maximum cardinality of an intersecting family among all

k
-subsets of
X
that are a cyclic interval with respect to
\pi
is equal to
k
, i.e., the number of cyclic intervals containing a fixed element of
X
.
Finally, the family
\M
is an EKR family because it consists of a single set, and obviously it satisfies the EKR property.
It is obvious that for any two elements
\pi,\sigma\in\mathring{S}_{n}
, we have
|\LL^{{}^{(\pi)}}_{{}_{M}}|=|\LL^{{}^{(\sigma)}}_{{}_{M}}|=n
.
Also, for any
k
-subset
L
and any
\pi\in\mathring{S}_{n}
, if there exists an

such that
L=A_{\pi,i}
, then
|\mathcal{M}^{{}^{(\pi)}}_{L}|=1

Remark 1

The above argument is essentially a translation of the elegant argument of Katona, known as Katona’s cycle method, into our composition framework.
Katona considers the case that

Definition 5

Corollary 2

(

]
)

Let

be a positive integer number and let

Proof.

5.
Applications

In this section, we present some of the applications of the composition framework developed in the previous section. Each subsection of this part is devoted to one such application.

5.1.
Generalized Katona Cycle Method

Borg and Meagher in
[
3
]
presented an elegant framework for deriving EKR-type results for set systems under certain symmetry assumptions. Their result is a natural generalization of celebrated Katona’s cycle method for proving the original Erdős-Ko-Rado Theorem. The following result is in the heart of their framework, but in a slightly different notation.

Theorem D

consecutive elements of the ordering form an element of the family
\mathcal{V}
.

Then, the family
\mathcal{V}
has the EKR property.

We show that Theorem

follows directly from Lemma
2

Proof of Theorem

We show that the assumptions of Theorem

imply those of Lemma
2
. The first assumption of Lemma
2
regarding the existence of

is precisely the first assumption of the proposition. The

-balanced assumption is also satisfied by assumption

to be all the consecutive

guarantees that the family

has the EKR property.
∎

An ordering of the elements of

which satisfies Condition

In
[
3
]
, Borg and Meagher showed that if

is the set of all the edges of

-matchings of the edges of

[

Although Theorem

-matchings), then there is no such admissible ordering.

Therefore, their method does not resolve the question of

In the next subsection, we show that the composition lemma can handle this question.

Rademacher-type exact formula and higher order Turán inequalities for r r -colored ℓ \ell -regular partitions Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 11P82, 11N37; Secondary 11F20. Keywords and phrases. Circle method, Rademacher-type exact formula, r r -colored partitions, ℓ \ell -regular partitions, Higher order Turán inequalities.
Archit Agarwal, Meghali Garg, Bibekananda Maji

Abstract
. In 1937, Rademacher refined the circle method of Hardy and Ramanujan to derive an exact convergent series for the partition function p ⁡ ( n ) p(n) . In 1942, Hua derived an exact formula for the distinct part partition function, and in 1971, Hagis generalized this result to the case of ℓ \ell -regular partitions. More recently, Iskander, Jain, and Talvola established a Rademacher-type exact formula for the r r -colored partition function. In this paper, we employ the circle method to obtain a Rademacher-type exact formula for r r -colored ℓ \ell -regular partitions for any r ∈ ℕ r\in\mathbb{N} and ℓ ≥ 2 \ell\geq 2 . As an application, we derive higher order Turán inequalities for the r r -colored ℓ \ell -regular partition function using a result of Griffin, Ono, Rolen, and Zagier. Furthermore, as additional consequences, we establish Rademacher-type exact formulas and higher order Turán inequalities for the r r -colored distinct part partition function and for the sum of minimal excludants over ordinary partitions and overpartitions.



1.
Introduction

In the theory of partitions, determining an exact value for the partition function
p(n)
for large
n
has been considered as one of the challenging problems for quite some time until
Hardy and Ramanujan
[
23
]
made a breakthrough in 1918 by developing the circle method and obtaining an asymptotic formula for
p(n)
which enabled them to find values of partition function for large
n
.
Mainly, they showed that, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

p(n)=\frac{e^{\pi\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda_{n}}{3}}}}{4\sqrt{3}\lambda_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda_{n}}{3}}}\right)+O\!\left(\frac{e^{\pi\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\sqrt{n/2}}}{n}\right),

where
\lambda_{n}=n-1/24
.
In particular, one can see that,

\displaystyle p(n)\thicksim\frac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}}e^{\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}}\quad\mathrm{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

The circle method has emerged as one of the most powerful and indispensable techniques in the field of analytic number theory. This technique was further improved by Rademacher
[
33
,
34
]
, who derived the following exact formula for

\displaystyle p(n)=2\pi\left(\frac{1}{6\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}\left(n-\frac{1}{24}\right)}}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{k}(n)}{k}I_{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{\pi}{k}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}\left(n-\frac{1}{24}\right)}\right),

(1.1)

where

\displaystyle A_{k}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h~\mathrm{mod}~k\\
\gcd(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}e^{i\pi s(h,k)-2\pi in\frac{h}{k}},

and
I_{\nu}
denotes the modified Bessel function of the first kind and
s(h,k)
denotes the Dedekind sum defined as

\displaystyle s(h,k):=\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\frac{r}{k}\left(\frac{hr}{k}-\left\lfloor\frac{hr}{k}\right\rfloor-\frac{1}{2}\right).

(1.2)

A detailed discussion on Radmacher’s proof of (
1.1
) can be found in
[
2
,
4
,
35
]
.

\displaystyle p^{(r)}(n)=2\pi\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{m=0}^{\lfloor{\frac{r}{24}}\rfloor}p^{(r)}(m)A(n)I_{1+\frac{r}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi}{k}\sqrt{\left(\frac{r}{24}-m\right)\left(n-\frac{r}{24}\right)}\right)\left(\frac{\frac{r}{24}-m}{n-\frac{r}{24}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{r}{4}},

1.1.
Higher order Turán inequalities

In recent years, considerable attention has been devoted to the study of Turán inequalities and their higher order generalizations for combinatorial and number-theoretic sequences. A sequence
\{t(n)\}
of real numbers is said to be log-concave if it satisfies the classical Turán inequality:

\displaystyle t(n)^{2}\geq t(n-1)t(n+1),\quad\text{for}~\text{all}\quad n\geq 1.

The investigation of log-concavity and Turán inequalities is deeply intertwined with the theory of real entire functions in the Laguerre–Pólya class and with analytic aspects related to the Riemann Hypothesis
[
12
,
17
,
20
,
37
]
.
These properties also frequently arise in combinatorics, where many classical sequences such as the binomial coefficients, Stirling numbers, and Bessel numbers are known to exhibit log-concavity
[
36
]
. For the partition function
p(n)
, Nicolas
[
28
]
first proved that

\displaystyle p(n)^{2}>p(n-1)p(n+1),

for all
n>25
, which was later established again by DeSalvo and Pak
[
15
]
using Lehmer’s refinement of the error term in the formula for
p(n)
. Beyond classical log-concavity, the notion of higher order Turán inequalities offer a broader framework for studying real-rootedness phenomena via Jensen polynomials. For a real sequence
\{t(n)\}
, the Jensen polynomial of degree
d
and shift
n
is defined by

\displaystyle J_{t}^{d,n}(X):=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{d}{i}t(n+i)\,X^{i}.

The sequence
\{t(n)\}
is said to satisfy the degree
d
Turán inequality at
n
if the polynomial
J_{t}^{d,n-1}(X)
is hyperbolic, i.e., all its roots are real.
The study of Jensen polynomials has proven remarkably powerful in understanding the asymptotic behavior and analytic structure of arithmetic functions. For instance, Chen, Jia, and Wang
[
10
]
established the hyperbolicity of the cubic Jensen polynomial
J_{p}^{3,n-1}(X)
associated to the partition function
p(n)
for all
n\geq 94
and further conjectured that for every integer
d\geq 1
, there exists an integer
N_{p}(d)
such that
J_{p}^{d,n-1}(X)
is hyperbolic for all
n\geq N_{p}(d)
.
This conjecture was subsequently proved by Griffin, Ono, Rolen, and Zagier
[
20
, Theorem 5]
. They not only proved the result for the partition function but also established the hyperbolicity of Jensen polynomials associated with the Fourier coefficients of weakly holomorphic modular forms on the full modular group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Their result revealed a striking connection between Jensen polynomials and the Hermite polynomials
H_{d}(X)
.
They proved that, under mild analytic assumptions on a positive sequence
\{t(n)\}
, properly scaled Jensen polynomials converge to Hermite polynomials as
n\to\infty
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
20
, Theorem 3 and 8]

Let
\{t(n)\},\{A(n)\},\{\delta(n)\}
be sequences of positive real numbers, with
\delta(n)
tending to
0
.
For integers
j\geq 0
,
d\geq 1
, suppose that there are real numbers
g_{3}(n),g_{4}(n),\ldots,g_{d}(n)
, for which

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{t(n+j)}{t(n)}\right)=A(n)j-\delta(n)^{2}j^{2}+\sum_{i=3}^{d}g_{i}(n)j^{i}+o(\delta(n)^{d}),\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

with
g_{i}(n)=o(\delta(n)^{i})
for each
3\leq i\leq d
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\delta(n)^{-d}}{t(n)}J_{t}^{d,n}\!\left(\frac{\delta(n)X-1}{\exp(A(n))}\right)\right)=H_{d}(X).

Since Hermite polynomials have distinct real zeros, and real-rootedness is preserved under linear transformations, this implies that Jensen polynomials have distinct real roots as well and consequently, the higher order Turán inequalities hold for large
n
. Over time, many mathematicians have investigated log-concavity and higher order Turán inequalities for various partition functions, interested readers can see
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
17
,
27
,
29
,
30
]
. In 2021, Crig and Pun
[
11
]
examined the higher order Turán inequalities associated with the
\ell
-regular partition function. In 2024, Dong and Ji
[
18
]
also studied the same for the partition function into distinct parts.
In the present paper, we utilize the above result of Griffin, Ono, Rolen and Zagier to obtain higher order Turán inequalities for
r
-colored
\ell
-regular partitions.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present the main results of this paper. Section
3
collects the necessary preliminaries required for the proofs, including a transformation formula for
f(q^{\ell})
, where
q=e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{h}{k}+\frac{iz}{k^{2}}\right)}
,

\operatorname{Re}(z)>0

Preliminaries

In this section, we present several key results that are crucial for deriving the main results of the current paper. We first state an important result related to Dedekind sums.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
h_{1}
is an integer such that
hh_{1}\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}~k)
, then
s(h,k)=s(h_{1},k)
.

A proof of this result can be found in

.
∎The next result gives Weil’s bound for Kloosterman’s sum. This bound plays a crucial role in proving the convergence of an infinite series.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a,b,c
be integers with
c>0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{x\bmod c}e^{2\pi i\frac{ax+b\bar{x}}{c}}=\mathcal{O}(c^{\frac{1}{2}}d(c)\gcd(a,b,c)^{\frac{1}{2}}),

\bar{x}

We refer
[
26

for the proof of this result.
∎Hardy and Ramanujan
[
23

\displaystyle f\left(e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{h}{k}+\frac{iz}{k^{2}}\right)}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{z}{k}}e^{i\pi s(h,k)}e^{\frac{\pi}{12k}\left(\frac{k}{z}-\frac{z}{k}\right)}f\left(e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{h^{\prime}}{k}+\frac{i}{z}\right)}\right),

It is well known that the Dedekind eta function

\displaystyle\eta(\tau)=e^{\frac{i\pi\tau}{12}}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{2\pi im\tau})=\frac{e^{\frac{i\pi\tau}{12}}}{f(e^{2\pi i\tau})}.

For a divisor

\displaystyle f\left(e^{2\pi i\frac{\ell}{Q}\left(\frac{h}{k}+\frac{iz}{k}\right)}\right)=e^{\frac{i\pi\ell}{12Q}\left(\frac{h}{k}+\frac{iz}{k}\right)}\eta^{-1}\left(\frac{\ell}{Q}\left(\frac{h}{k}+\frac{iz}{k}\right)\right).

(3.3)

We now recall a transformation formula for
\eta(\tau)

\tau^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}

\displaystyle\eta\left(\frac{a\tau^{\prime}+b}{c\tau^{\prime}+d}\right)=\epsilon(a,b,c,d)\sqrt{\frac{c\tau^{\prime}+d}{i}}\eta(\tau^{\prime}),\quad\forall\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),

. Note that

.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

and
\ell\geq 2
be positive integers and

. Let

\displaystyle R\left(e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{h}{k}+\frac{iz}{k^{2}}\right)}\right)=\left(\frac{Q}{\ell}\right)^{\frac{r}{2}}e^{i\pi r\left(s(h,k)-s\left(\frac{\ell h}{Q},\frac{k}{Q}\right)\right)+\frac{\pi r}{12z}\left(1-\frac{Q^{2}}{\ell}\right)+\frac{\pi rz}{12k^{2}}(\ell-1)}\frac{F\left(e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{h^{\prime}}{k}+\frac{i}{z}\right)}\right)}{F\left(e^{2\pi iQ\left(\frac{h_{Q}}{k}+\frac{iQ}{\ell z}\right)}\right)},

Now we state a result, from the theory of Bessel functions, which will be useful to prove our main result.

Lemma 3.4
.

[

For

an integral representation of the modified Bessel function of the first kind is given by:

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(z)=\frac{(z/2)^{\nu}}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty}^{c+i\infty}t^{-\nu-1}e^{t+\frac{z^{2}}{4t}}\mathrm{d}t.

(3.8)

Prior to stating the next result, we introduce several notations that will be used in the subsequent discussion. First, we define

\displaystyle\omega(h,k)=e^{\pi is(h,k)}.

\displaystyle\omega(h,k,\ell,r)=\left[\frac{\omega(h,k)}{\omega\left(\frac{\ell h}{Q},\frac{k}{Q}\right)}\right]^{r},

Our objective is to estimate a sum involving

\displaystyle g=\begin{cases}\gcd(3,k)&\textrm{if}~k~\textrm{odd},\\
8\gcd(3,k)&\textrm{if}~k~\textrm{even},\end{cases}

Here

\displaystyle g=\gcd(3,k),

\displaystyle~hh^{\prime}\equiv-1\pmod{gk},~

\displaystyle fp\equiv 1\pmod{gk},~

\displaystyle f=24/g,

\displaystyle\quad Thh^{*}\equiv-1\pmod{GK}

(3.14)

\displaystyle FP\equiv 1(\bmod~{GK}),

\displaystyle F=\frac{24}{G},

Let us consider

\displaystyle\omega(h,k,\ell,r)=\left[\frac{\left(\frac{2^{\alpha}}{h}\right)\left(\frac{h}{Q^{*}}\right)}{\left(\frac{K}{T}\right)}\right]^{r}(-1)^{\frac{r(h-1)(Q^{*}-1)}{4}}i^{\frac{rb}{2}\{(k+1)-T^{\prime}(K+1)\}}e^{\frac{2\pi ipr(Uh+Vh^{\prime})}{gk}},

\displaystyle g=8\gcd(3,k),

and

We are now prepared to state a bound for a Kloosterman-type sum that will be employed in the subsequent analysis.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\gcd(k,\ell)=Q
, and suppose
h\equiv a\pmod{Q}
,
\gcd(a,Q)=1
. Assume further that
hh^{\prime}\equiv-1\pmod{k}
and that
s_{1}\leq h^{\prime}\pmod{k}<s_{2}
for integers satisfying
0\leq s_{1}<s_{2}\leq k
. Let
t\leq n
; and
M
is a fixed integer, then the exponential sum

\displaystyle X:=W_{\ell,r,M}(n,k)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{h\bmod{k}}\omega(h,k,\ell,r)e^{-\frac{2\pi i(hn-h^{\prime}M)}{k}}

admits the estimate

\displaystyle X:=W_{\ell,r,M}(n,k)=\mathcal{O}\left(n^{1/2}k^{1/2+\epsilon}\right),

where the implied constant in the
\mathcal{O}
-term depends only on
\ell
. Here, the symbol
\sum^{\prime}
indicates that the summation is restricted to a reduced residue system modulo
k
, possibly subject to additional stated conditions.

The proof of this result follows the same line of reasoning as that given by Hagis
[
22
, p. 378]
. However, for the sake of completeness and clarity, we provide a brief outline below. Using a property of the Dedekind sum, one can see that the function
\omega(h,k,\ell,r)
has period
k
when viewed as a function of
h
. If we change the sum over
\bmod~k
in
X
to
\bmod~gk
and select
h^{\prime}
so that
hh^{\prime}\equiv-1(\bmod~gk)
, then employing Proposition
3.6
, we obtain

X=g^{-1}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{h\bmod gk}A(h,k,\ell,r)e^{\frac{2\pi if(h)}{gk}},

where
f(h)=(qrU-gn)h+(qrV+gM)h^{\prime}.

If
k
is even, we split
X
into four parts,
X_{1}
,
X_{3}
,
X_{5}
,
X_{7}
, so that in
X_{d}
we have
h\equiv d\pmod{8}
as well as
h\equiv a\pmod{Q}
. Therefore,
X=X_{1}+X_{3}+X_{5}+X_{7}
, where

\displaystyle X_{d}=A_{d}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{h\bmod gk}e^{\frac{2\pi if(h)}{gk}}

(3.15)

with
|A_{d}|=g^{-1}\leq 1
.
If
k
is odd, then (
3.15
) holds if we identify
X
with
X_{d}
and ignore the restriction
h\equiv d\pmod{8}
.
For two fixed integers
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
, let us define a function
\beta(s)
as follows:

\displaystyle\beta(s)=\begin{cases}1,&\textrm{if}~s_{1}\leq s~(\bmod~{k})<s_{2},\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that
\beta(s)
is a periodic function with period
k
. Thus,
\beta(s)
can be written as finite Fourier series,

\beta(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}e^{\frac{2\pi isj}{k}},\quad\textrm{where}\quad\beta_{j}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\beta(s)e^{\frac{-2\pi isj}{k}}.

One can prove that

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}=\mathcal{O}(k^{\varepsilon})
for any
\varepsilon>0
.
We can now drop the restriction
s_{1}\leq h^{\prime}\pmod{k}<s_{2}
and write

\displaystyle X_{d}

\displaystyle=A_{d}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{h\bmod gk}\beta(h^{\prime})e^{\frac{2\pi if(h)}{gk}}

\displaystyle=A_{d}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{h\bmod gk}e^{\frac{2\pi i\left((qrU-gn)h+(qrV+gM+gj)h^{\prime}\right)}{gk}}.

If
k
is odd, then
\sum^{\prime}
represents a Kloosterman sum.
When
k
is even, we write
Q=2^{\alpha}Q^{*}
, where
\alpha\geq 0
and
Q^{*}
is odd. It is straightforward to verify that if
\alpha\leq 1
, the two congruence conditions

(I)
h\equiv a\pmod{Q}\quad
and   (II)
h\equiv d\pmod{8}

are jointly equivalent to a single condition of the form

h\equiv a^{*}\pmod{8Q^{*}}.

If
\alpha=2
and
a\not\equiv d\pmod{4}
, then the sum
\sum^{\prime}
is empty. However, if
a\equiv d\pmod{4}
, conditions (I) and (II) again combine into a single congruence

h\equiv a^{*}\pmod{8Q^{*}}.

For
\alpha\geq 3
, if
a\not\equiv d\pmod{8}
, the sum
\sum^{\prime}
is empty;
whereas if
a\equiv d\pmod{8}
, conditions (I) and (II) reduce to

h\equiv a\pmod{Q}.

Hence, in all cases, either
\sum^{\prime}
is empty or it is a Kloosterman sum.
Finally, applying Weil’s bound for Kloosterman sums i.e., Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle X=\mathcal{O}(n^{1/2}k^{1/2+\epsilon}).

∎

Thus, considering the path of integration as the union of arcs

, the integral (

\gcd(k,\ell)=Q\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{i}{k^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{m,s=0}^{\left\lfloor{\frac{r}{24}\left(\frac{\ell}{Q^{2}}-1\right)}\right\rfloor}p^{(r)}(m)a_{r}(s)\mathcal{C}(n)

\displaystyle\times\int_{1-i\infty}^{1+i\infty}\frac{-1}{\omega^{2}}e^{2\pi\omega\left(\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{Q^{2}}{\ell}\right)-\left(m+\frac{Q^{2}s}{\ell}\right)\right)+\frac{2\pi}{\omega k^{2}}\left(n+\frac{r}{24}(\ell-1)\right)}\mathrm{d}\omega,

Substituting
\left(\frac{\pi r}{12}\left(1-\frac{Q^{2}}{\ell}\right)-2\pi\left(m+\frac{Q^{2}s}{\ell}\right)\right)\omega=t
and comparing it with (

), we get

\displaystyle b_{\ell}^{(r)}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\mid\ell\\
Q^{2}<\ell\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{Q}{\ell}\right)^{\frac{r}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
\gcd(k,\ell)=Q\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{2\pi}{k^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{m,s=0}^{\left\lfloor{\frac{r}{24}\left(\frac{\ell}{Q^{2}}-1\right)}\right\rfloor}p^{(r)}(m)a_{r}(s){C}(n)\sqrt{\frac{\frac{\pi r}{12}\left(1-\frac{Q^{2}}{\ell}\right)-2\pi\left(m+\frac{Q^{2}s}{\ell}\right)}{\frac{\pi r}{12k^{2}}(\ell-1)+\frac{2n\pi}{k^{2}}}}

\displaystyle\times I_{1}\left(\frac{4\pi}{k}\sqrt{\left(\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{Q^{2}}{\ell}\right)-\left(m+\frac{Q^{2}s}{\ell}\right)\right)\left(n+\frac{r}{24}(\ell-1)\right)}\right).

(4.12)

Further, utilizing

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(z)=\frac{1}{i^{\nu}}J_{\nu}(iz),\quad J_{1}(z)=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}J_{0}(z),

we get the desired result (

).

We first separate the main term

corresponding to

) and denote the contribution of the remaining terms by

. This yields

\displaystyle b_{\ell}^{(r)}(n)

\displaystyle=M(n)+E(n),

\displaystyle M(n)=\left(\frac{1}{\ell}\right)^{\frac{r}{2}}2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}{\frac{r}{24}(\ell-1)+n}}I_{1}\left(4\pi\sqrt{\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)\left(n+\frac{r}{24}(\ell-1)\right)}\right).

(4.13)

Next, we employ the classical asymptotic expansion for the modified Bessel function of the first kind,

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{e^{x}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}\quad\text{as}\quad x\to\infty.

Making use of this asymptotic expansion into (
4.13
) gives

\displaystyle M(n)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\ell}\right)^{\frac{r}{2}}\left(\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\frac{1}{n+\frac{r}{24}(\ell-1)}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}e^{4\pi\sqrt{\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)\left(n+\frac{r}{24}(\ell-1)\right)}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\ell}\right)^{\frac{r}{2}}\left(\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}e^{4\pi\sqrt{\frac{nr}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}}

as
n\to\infty
.
Using the same asymptotic expansion, one can see that, for any
k\geq 2
, the modified Bessel function present in
E(n)
has the following asymptotic expansion:

\displaystyle I_{1}\left(\frac{4\pi}{k}\sqrt{\delta\left(n+\frac{r}{24}(\ell-1)\right)}\right)\sim\frac{e^{\frac{4\pi}{k}\sqrt{\delta\left(n+\frac{r}{24}(\ell-1)\right)}}}{\sqrt{\frac{8\pi^{2}}{k}\sqrt{\delta\left(n+\frac{r}{24}(\ell-1)\right)}}}\sim\frac{e^{\frac{4\pi}{k}\sqrt{\delta n}}}{\sqrt{\frac{8\pi^{2}}{k}\sqrt{\delta n}}},

where

\displaystyle\delta=\left(\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{Q^{2}}{\ell}\right)-\left(m+\frac{Q^{2}s}{\ell}\right)\right).

Moreover, one can easily check that, for any
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{e^{\frac{4\pi}{k}\sqrt{\delta n}}}{e^{4\pi\sqrt{\frac{nr}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}}}=0.

Here we have used the fact that
\sqrt{\delta}<\sqrt{\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}
as
(m,s)\neq(0,0)
.
This completes the proof of Corollary
2.2
.

From (

), for any non-negative integer

, we have

\displaystyle\frac{b_{\ell}^{(r)}(n+j)}{b_{\ell}^{(r)}(n)}\sim\left(\frac{n}{n+j}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}e^{4\pi\sqrt{\frac{r(n+j)}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}-4\pi\sqrt{\frac{rn}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}}.

Taking natural logarithm on both sides, we have

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{b_{\ell}^{(r)}(n+j)}{b_{\ell}^{(r)}(n)}\right)\sim 4\pi\sqrt{\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}\left(\sqrt{n+j}-\sqrt{n}\right)-\frac{3}{4}\log\left(\frac{n+j}{n}\right).

The above right side expression can be simplified as follows:

\displaystyle 4\pi\sqrt{\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}\left(\sqrt{n+j}-\sqrt{n}\right)-\frac{3}{4}\log\left(\frac{n+j}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=4\pi\sqrt{\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{1/2\choose i}\frac{j^{i}}{n^{i-1/2}}+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{i}j^{i}}{in^{i}}

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r}{24n}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}-\frac{3}{4n}\right)j-\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{r}{24n^{3}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}-\frac{3}{8n^{2}}\right)j^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=3}^{\infty}\left(4\pi\sqrt{\frac{r}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}{1/2\choose i}\frac{1}{n^{i-1/2}}+\frac{3}{4}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{in^{i}}\right)j^{i}.

Now, let
A(n)=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r}{24n}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}-\frac{3}{4n},~\delta(n)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{r}{24n^{3}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)}-\frac{3}{8n^{2}}}
and

for all

. A simple calculation shows that, for

, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{g_{i}(n)}{(\delta(n))^{i}}=0.

We can further check that

for all
i\geq d+1
. Hence, it follows that the sequences

and
\{g_{i}(n)\}

1.1
. Therefore, the Jensen polynomials corresponding to the

. This concludes the proof of Theorem
2.6
.

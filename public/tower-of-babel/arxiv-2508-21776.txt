Akram Alishahi, Eugene Gorsky, Beibei Liu

Abstract
. Inspired by the S n S^{n} colored version of Khovanov and Khovanov-Rozansky homology, we define a colored version of knot Floer homology by studying the colimit of a directed system of link Floer homology with infinite full twists. Specifically, our n n -colored knot Floer homology of a knot K K is then defined as the colimit of the link Floer homology of ( n , m ​ n ) (n,mn) -cables of K K by fixing n n and letting m m goes to infinity. We show that the colimit of the infinite full twists is a module over the colored knot Floer homology of the unknot. In addition, we give an explicit description of colored Heegaard Floer homology for L-space knots, and maps for colored knot Floer homology of crossing changes.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation: colored invariants via cables

One of the central ideas in quantum topology is the notion of colored knot invariants
[
33
]
. Given a knot
K\subset S^{3}
, a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and its representation
V
, one can define the Laurent polynomial
P_{\mathfrak{g},V}(K)\in\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
which is a topological invariant of
K
. For example, for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and
V=\mathbb{C}^{2}
one gets Jones polynomial, while
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and
V=S^{n}\mathbb{C}^{2}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
corresponds to the so-called colored Jones polynomial
[
26
]
. More generally, one refers to
P_{\mathfrak{g},V}(K)
as the invariant of
K
colored by the representation
V
.

For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
Rosso and Jones
[
34
]
proved that the invariants of any cable
K_{n,p}
of the knot
K
are related to colored invariants of
K
. In particular, one has the following identity

(1)

P_{\mathfrak{sl}_{N},S^{n}\mathbb{C}^{N}}(K)=\lim_{p\to\infty}P_{\mathfrak{sl}_{N},\mathbb{C}^{N}}(K_{n,p}).

On the left hand side of (
1
) we get
S^{n}\mathbb{C}^{N}
-colored (or simply
S^{n}
-colored) invariant of
K
, while on the right hand side we get the limit of uncolored invariants of cables
K_{n,p}
normalized by a certain monomial in
q
which we omit. The limit means that for all
i
and sufficiently large
p
the coefficient at
q^{i}
in the right hand side stabilizes.

In recent decades, there was a lot of work
[
7
,
10
,
18
,
19
,
35
]
towards categorifying (
1
) in the context of Khovanov and Khovanov-Rozansky homology
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
. In particular, for
N=2
the (uncolored) Jones polynomial is categorified by Khovanov homology
\mathrm{Kh}(K)
and one can
define
the
S^{n}
-colored Khovanov homology as the colimit

(2)

\mathrm{Kh}_{S^{n}}(K):=\varinjlim\left[\mathrm{Kh}(K_{n,0})\xrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}\mathrm{Kh}(K_{n,n})\xrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}\mathrm{Kh}(K_{n,2n})\xrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}\cdots\right]

The terms on the right hand side differ by adding one full twist, and the colimit is often referred to as “infinite full twist”
[
35
]
.

The key novel feature of (
2
) are the
connecting maps

\alpha_{n}:\mathrm{Kh}\left(K_{n,mn}\right)\to\mathrm{Kh}\left(K_{n,(m+1)n}\right)
which are required for the definition of colimit. The colimit on the right hand side of (
2
) is a bigraded vector space which is typically infinite-dimensional, but one can prove that it is finite-dimensional in each bidegree. See
[
10
,
18
,
19
,
35
]
and references therein for more details and an explicit description of the maps
\alpha_{n}
, and related computations in Khovanov-Rozansky homology. See Section
1.7
for some computations of colored homology.

In this paper, we define and study some analogues of (
1
) and (
2
) in the context of Alexander polynomial and Heegaard Floer homology. In fact, the analogue of
(
1
) is not too interesting, as the Alexander polynomials for cables of
K
are proportional to the Alexander polynomial of
K
evaluated at
t_{1}\cdots t_{n}
(see Section
2.4
for more details). However, an analogue of (
2
), which we are about to define, has a rich and interesting structure.

1.2.
Colored knot Floer homology

Analogously to (
2
), we define a colimit invariant under full twists by using link Floer homology. Let
L\subset S^{3}
be an oriented
n
-component link and
M
be an unknot bounding a disk
D
that intersects every component positively at exactly one point. Let
L_{m}
denote the link obtained by inserting
m
full twists in
L
. Then we can define a colimit of the following directed system of
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})
:

(3)

\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)=\varinjlim\left[\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L)\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{1})\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{2})\to\cdots\right]

where the connecting map
\phi_{0}
is a cobordism map induced by blowing down the
(-1)
-framed unknot
M
in some
\text{Spin}^{c}
-structure (see Proposition
2.7
), and we use the “full” version of link Floer homology developed in
[
38
,
39
]
. In particular,
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})
is a module over
\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\ldots,U_{n},V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}]
.

In our first main result, we prove that
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
is well defined.

Theorem 1.1
.

The
\mathbb{F}
-vector space
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
has well defined Alexander
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-grading, and Maslov
\mathbb{Z}
-grading. The homogeneous component of each
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
-degree is finite-dimensional.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we normalize the Alexander degrees such that they are preserved by the maps
\phi_{0}
. More precisely, we define

(4)

\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(L_{m};\overline{\mathbf{s}}):=\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m},\mathbf{s})

where

\overline{\mathbf{s}}=\mathbf{s}-(\mathbf{c}_{m},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{m})
and
\mathbf{c}_{m}=m(n-1)/2
. We then prove that for any fixed normalized Alexander degree
\overline{\mathbf{s}}
the dimension of
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(L_{m};\overline{\mathbf{s}})
stabilizes for sufficiently large
m
:

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
\overline{\mathbf{s}}=(\overline{s}_{1},\overline{s}_{2},\cdots,\overline{s}_{n})
with
\overline{s}_{i}\geq C-m
for all
i
we have

\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(L_{m},\overline{\mathbf{s}})\cong\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(L_{m+1},\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)

Here,
C
is some constant depending on
L
but independent of
m
.

The isomorphism in Theorem
1.2
is obtained by
analyzing certain special Heegaard diagrams of
L_{m}
and
L_{m+1}
, constructing an explicit bijection on generators and verifying that it is a chain map, see Theorem
3.2
.
Theorem
1.2
gives an upper bound for the dimension of the limit (see Lemma
2.2
) for each Maslov grading
j
:

(5)

\dim\mathcal{H}_{D,j}(L;\overline{\mathbf{s}})\leq\dim\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}_{j}(L_{m};\overline{\mathbf{s}}),\quad m\gg 0.

In fact, we conjecture a stronger statement.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Given a normalized Alexander degree
\overline{\mathbf{s}}
, the maps

\phi_{0}:\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(L_{m};\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)\to\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(L_{m+1};\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)

are isomorphisms for sufficiently large
m
. As a consequence,
\dim\mathcal{H}_{D,j}(L;\overline{\mathbf{s}})=\dim\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}_{j}\left(L_{m};\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)
for
m\gg 0
.

Remark 1.4
.

Based on Theorem
1.2
, one might be tempted to define a “naive” version of
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
by simply computing
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(L_{m};\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)
for
m\gg 0
. However, it is unclear whether the isomorphisms constructed in the proof of Theorem
1.2
have any nice topological properties.

On the other hand, the map
\phi_{0}
does interact well with the cobordism maps in Heegaard Floer homology which yields nice properties of the colimit (
3
). See, in particular, Theorems
1.13
and
1.15
below.

Remark 1.5
.

Throughout this paper, for simplicity, we focus on the case that every component of the link intersects the disk
D
positively in one point. However, an analogous version of Theorem
1.2
holds in general. More precisely, suppose there exists a subset
I\subset\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
such that every component of
L_{I}=\amalg_{i\in I}L_{i}
(resp.
L_{\bar{I}}=\amalg_{i\in\bar{I}}L_{i}
) intersects
D
negatively (resp. positively) at one point, where
\bar{I}=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}\setminus I
. Let
|I|=l
. If we use
\phi_{-l}
to define
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
instead of
\phi_{0}
then there is a constant
C
independent of
m
such that for any
\bar{\mathbf{s}}
with
\bar{s}_{i}\leq-(C-m)
for
i\in I
and
\bar{s}_{i}\geq C-m
for
i\in\bar{I}
we have
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(L_{m};\bar{\mathbf{s}})\cong\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(L_{m+1};\bar{\mathbf{s}})
. Here, Alexander grading is renormalized such that
\bar{s}_{i}=s_{i}+\mathbf{c}_{m}
for
i\in I
and
\bar{s}_{i}=s_{i}-\mathbf{c}_{m}
for
i\in\bar{I}
. This is a direct consequence of Theorem
1.2
and the symmetry of link Floer homology, that is
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L,\mathbf{s})\cong\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L^{\prime};\mathbf{s}^{\prime})
where
L^{\prime}=(-L_{I})\amalg L_{\bar{I}}
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
is obtained from
\mathbf{s}
by multiplying every
i
-th entry with
i\in I
by
-1
. Consequently, we can define
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
with connecting homomorphisms
\phi_{-l}
and after proper renormalization of the Maslov grading, the limit is graded by Maslov and Alexander grading and is finite dimensional in each degree.

Given a knot
K\subset S^{3}
and an integer
n>0
we consider the family of
n
-component cables
K_{n,mn}
for
m\geq 0
and their link Floer homology
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,mn})
. All components of
K_{n,mn}
are isotopic to
K
and their pairwise linking numbers are all equal to
m
. Applying the colimit definition to the link
K_{n,mn}
, we define the
n
-colored knot Floer homology of
K
.

Definition 1.6
.

We define the
n
-colored knot Floer homology of
K
as the colimit of the directed system

(6)

\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)=\varinjlim\left[\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,0})\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,n})\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,2n})\to\cdots\right].

This is a special case of
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
where
L=K_{n,0}
and the disk
D
bounds the meridian of
K
.

For
n=1
all cables
K_{1,m}
coincide with
K
and we get
\mathcal{H}_{1}(K)=\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K)
. In Lemma
6.1
we prove that Conjecture
1.3
holds whenever
K
is an L-space knot. Assuming Conjecture
1.3
, the inequality (
5
) becomes an equality, and we get the ranks of
\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)
for all (normalized) Alexander degrees, as well as its Euler characteristic.

Theorem 1.7
.

Assuming Conjecture
1.3
, the Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)
is given by

\chi(\mathcal{H}_{n}(K))=\lim_{m\to\infty}(t_{1}\cdots t_{n})^{-m(n-1)/2}\chi_{K_{n,mn}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=(t_{1}\cdots t_{n})^{1/2}\chi_{K}(t_{1}\cdots t_{n})

where
\chi_{K}(t)=\frac{\Delta_{K}(t)}{1-t^{-1}}
is the Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K)
.

Remark 1.8
.

We can choose some framing
f
on the knot
K
and consider the
n
-component link
L_{f}=K_{n,fn}
whose components are pushoffs of
K
along the framing. Adding a full twist changes
f
to
f+1
, and we obtain the colimit

\mathcal{H}_{D}(L_{f})=\varinjlim\left[\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,fn})\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,(f+1)n})\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,(f+2)n})\to\cdots\right].

It is easy to see that as a vector space this does not depend on
f
and is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)
. The Maslov grading does not depend on
f
, but the normalized Alexander grading is shifted by
\left(\frac{f(n-1)}{2},\ldots,\frac{f(n-1)}{2}\right)
.

Remark 1.9
.

One can define a colimit using different choices of connecting maps
\phi_{k}
corresponding to different
\text{Spin}^{c}
structures in the surgery cobordism. We expect that other
\phi_{k}
correspond to different “colors” of the homology. For simplicity of computation, we focus on the choice of
\phi_{0}
throughout the paper. By conjugation, the colimit for the choice of
\phi_{n-1}
is the same as the choice of
\phi_{0}
up to some regrading and interchanging
U_{i}
with
V_{i}
.

Remark 1.10
.

One can define a similar colimit when components of the cables are oriented differently, see Remark
1.5
.

Instead of considering the family of
(n,mn)
cables of
K
with
n

(n,mn+r)
-cables of
K
for fixed remainder
r
. These links have

(7)

\varinjlim\left[\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,r})\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,n+r})\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,2n+r})\to\cdots\right].

The following conjecture, if true, would imply that the colimit of (

, one can define the normalized Alexander grading

which generalizes

and is preserved by

.

Conjecture 1.11
.

a) For a given normalized Alexander degree
\widetilde{\mathbf{s}}
, the dimension of
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(K_{n,mn+r},\widetilde{\mathbf{s}})

b) The maps

\phi_{0}:\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(K_{n,mn+r};\widetilde{\mathbf{s}}\right)\to\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(K_{n,(m+1)n+r};\widetilde{\mathbf{s}}\right)

.

At the moment, we cannot generalize our proof of Theorem
1.2
to the case
r\neq 0
. However, for
r=1
Hedden proved Conjecture

(a) in
[

for minus and hat versions of Heegaard Floer homology, but part (b) of the conjecture appears to be open in these cases as well.

Remark 1.12
.

Assuming Conjecture
1.11
(a), it appears that many results of the paper can be applied to (
7
) for arbitrary

, with some small modifications. We plan to investigate the properties of more general colored homology (
7
) in future work.

Next, we study the module structure of
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
. Since the connecting maps
\phi_{0}
commute with the action of
U_{i},V_{i}
, the limit is clearly a module over
\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\ldots,U_{n},V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}]
. However,
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
has a richer module structure over a commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
, which is a localization of the cable algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}
defined in subsection
1.5
.

Theorem 1.13
.

Consider the commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
with generators
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n},V_{1},\ldots,V_{n},\mathsf{A}
and relations

(8)

U_{i}=\mathsf{A}\prod_{j\neq i}V_{j},\quad U_{i}V_{i}=U_{i^{\prime}}V_{i^{\prime}}\quad i,i^{\prime}=1,\ldots,n.

Then for an arbitrary
n
-component link
L
there is an action of
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
on
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
(in particular, on
\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)
for any knot
K
).
The generator
\mathsf{A}
changes Alexander degree by
(-1,\ldots,-1)
and Maslov degree by
-2
.

Problem 1.14
.

Is
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
a finitely generated module over
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
?

1.3.
Colored knot Floer homology and crossing changes

Let
K^{+},K^{-}
denote two knot diagrams that differ at a single crossing, and
K^{+}
represents the one with a positive crossing,
K^{-}
represents the one with a negative crossing. There are cobordism maps between
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K^{+})
and
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K^{-})
induced by blowing down a
(-1)
-framed unknot. We study the colored knot Floer homology of
K^{+},K^{-}
respectively, and find analogous cobordism maps between them.

Theorem 1.15
.

For any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, there are maps

G_{j}^{\mathrm{col}}:\mathcal{H}_{n}(K^{-})\to\mathcal{H}_{n}(K^{+}),\quad\ F_{j}^{\mathrm{col}}:\mathcal{H}_{n}(K^{+})\to\mathcal{H}_{n}(K^{-})

with

\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(G_{j}^{\mathrm{col}})=\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(F_{j}^{\mathrm{col}})=-j^{2}-j,\quad A_{i}(G_{j}^{\mathrm{col}})=-2j+1,\quad A_{i}(F_{j}^{\mathrm{col}})=n-1.

All maps
G_{j}^{\mathrm{col}},F_{j}^{\mathrm{col}}
commute with the action of the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
.

1.4.
Examples

We compute a lot of examples of colored homology, starting with the unknot. In this case,
K_{n,mn}=T(n,mn)
is a torus link, and its “full” link Floer homology was computed in
[
5
]
. This computation can be used to prove the following:

Theorem 1.16
.

If
K=O_{1}
is the unknot then
\mathcal{H}_{n}(O_{1})
is a free rank one module over
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
. In particular, it is isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
as an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space.

More generally, we can compute the colored homology when
K
is an L-space knot. Recall that an L-space is a 3-manifold with minimal possible rank of Heegaard Floer homology
[
29
]
. A knot (resp. link)
K
is called an L-space knot (resp. L-space link) if all sufficiently large Dehn surgeries of
S^{3}
along
K
yield L-spaces
[
25
]
. If
K
is an L-space knot then by
[
13
]
for
m\gg 0
the cable
K_{n,mn}
is an L-space link, and
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,mn})
is determined by
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K)
and, in fact, by the Alexander polynomial of
K
. This allows us to prove the following.

Theorem 1.17
.

Consider the homomorphism
\varepsilon_{n}:\mathbb{F}[U,V]\to\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
defined by

\varepsilon_{n}(U)=\mathsf{A},\varepsilon_{n}(V)=V_{1}\cdots V_{n}.

Assume
K
is an L-space knot, then

\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)\simeq\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K)\bigotimes_{\mathbb{F}[U,V]}\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}

where we regard
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
as a module over
\mathbb{F}[U,V]
via the homomorphism
\varepsilon_{n}
.

See Section
6
for more details. In particular, we give an explicit description of
\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)
by generators and relations in Theorem
6.2
.

1.5.
Cabled homology

In order to prove Theorem
1.13
, we need to study the relation between
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})
for different
m
in more detail.

Definition 1.18
.

The
n
-strand
cable algebra

\mathcal{A}_{n}
is defined as a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
graded algebra over
\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\ldots,U_{n},V_{1},\ldots,V_{n},\mathbf{U}]
with commuting generators
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}
and the following relations:

•

U_{i}V_{i}=\mathbf{U},\ i=1,\ldots,n

•

Linear relations

(9)

U_{I}a_{k-1}=V_{\overline{I}}a_{k}

where
I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
is an arbitrary subset with
|I|=k
and
\overline{I}=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus I
. Here,
U_{I}=\prod_{i\in I}U_{i}
and
V_{\overline{I}}=\prod_{i\notin I}V_{i}
.

•

Quadratic relations

(10)

a_{i}a_{j}=\mathbf{U}^{k\ell-ij}a_{k}a_{\ell}

whenever
i+j=k+\ell
and
i\leq k\leq\ell\leq j
.

The gradings correspond to the Alexander (
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
) and Maslov (
\mathbb{Z}
) gradings, along with an additional
\mathbb{Z}
“twist grading”. The generator
a_{k}
has Alexander grading

A(a_{k})=\left(\frac{n-1}{2}-k,\ldots,\frac{n-1}{2}-k\right),

Maslov grading
\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(a_{k})=-k^{2}-k
and twist grading
\mathbf{tw}(a_{k})=1
.

Theorem 1.19
.

Consider the direct sum

\mathcal{TW}_{D}(L)=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})

with its Alexander and Maslov grading together with additional “twist” grading given by
m
. Then
\mathcal{TW}_{D}(L)
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
-graded module over
\mathcal{A}_{n}
where the generators
a_{k}
act by certain cobordism maps

\phi_{k}:\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})\to\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m+1}).

The maps
\phi_{k}
are defined geometrically. Actually they are the cobordism maps induced from blowing down the
(-1)
-framed unknot corresponding to different
\text{Spin}^{c}
structures, see Proposition
2.7
. In order to
prove Theorem
1.19
one needs to verify the relations (
9
) and (
10
) for them. We first verify these equations in Theorem
4.6
when
L=O_{n}
is the
n
-component unlink, and prove that

\mathcal{TW}_{D}(O_{n})=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,mn))

is a free rank one module over
\mathcal{A}_{n}.

We then use naturality properties of link Floer homology
[
38
]
to complete the proof for general
K
in Theorem
5.1
.

Theorem
1.13
can then be deduced from Theorem
1.19
and the following result which we prove as Theorem
5.2
.

Theorem 1.20
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
is isomorphic to (graded) localization
\mathcal{A}_{n}[a_{0}^{-1}]
. Under this isomorphism, the generator
\mathsf{A}
corresponds to
a_{1}/a_{0}
.

Consider the

2.1.
Colimits

We recall some useful definitions and properties of colimits that will be used throughout the paper.

Let
\{V_{i}\ :\ i\geq 1\}
be a collection of vector spaces, and
f_{i}:V_{i}\to V_{i+1}
are some linear maps (called connecting maps). We call this data a directed system and draw it as follows:

(12)

(V_{\bullet},f_{\bullet}):=\left[V_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}V_{2}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}\cdots\right].

Definition 2.1
.

The colimit
\varinjlim(V_{\bullet},f_{\bullet})
of the directed system
(V_{\bullet},f_{\bullet})
is defined as the vector space spanned by pairs
(v,i)
where
v\in V_{i}
modulo the equivalence relation
(v,i)\sim(f_{i}(v),i+1)
.

The following easy observation will be useful.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{V_{i}\}
be a sequence of vector spaces together with some connecting maps
f_{i}:V_{i}\to V_{i+1}
. Let
L=\varinjlim(V_{i},f_{i})
be the colimit of this directed system.

If
\dim V_{i}\leq D
for sufficiently large
i
then
\dim L\leq D
(in particular,
L
is finite-dimensional).

Proof.

Recall that
L
is spanned by the equivalence classes of vectors
v_{i}\in V_{i}
modulo relation
v_{i}\sim f_{i}(v_{i})
. We will abbreviate
f_{i}
to
f
when it is clear from context, so
v_{i}\sim f(v_{i})
and
v_{i}\sim f^{k}(v_{i})
for all
k>0
.

Assume that
v_{i_{1}}\in V_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{D+1}}\in V_{i_{D+1}}
represent equivalence classes of some vectors in
L
. Furthermore, assume that for
i>N
we have
\dim V_{i}\leq D
, pick
n=\max(i_{1},\ldots,i_{D+1},N+1)
. Then the vectors

f^{n-i_{1}}(v_{i_{1}}),\ldots,f^{n-i_{D+1}}(v_{i_{D+1}})

all belong to
V_{n}
and
\dim V_{n}\leq D
. Therefore these vectors are linearly dependent and the respective vectors in
L
(represented by
v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{D+1}}
) are linearly dependent in
L
.

We conclude that any
D+1
vectors in
L
are linearly dependent, so
\dim L\leq D
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
(V_{\bullet},f_{\bullet})
and
(W_{\bullet},g_{\bullet})
are two directed systems, and
h_{i}:V_{i}\to W_{i+s}
is a collection of maps such that
g_{i+s}\circ h_{i}=h_{i+1}\circ f_{i}
. Then there is a well defined map

h:\varinjlim(V_{\bullet},f_{\bullet})\to\varinjlim(W_{\bullet},g_{\bullet}).

Proof.

We define
h(v,i)=(h_{i}(v),i+s)
for
v\in V_{i}
. By our assumption,
h
preserves the equivalence relation and hence defines a map of colimits.
∎

2.2.
Heegaard Floer homology

In this subsection, we review the necessary background for link Floer homology. We assume some familiarity with the basic Heegaard Floer homology and its refinement to knots, see
[
28
,
27
,
30
,
31
]
for details. Given a link
\mathcal{L}
with
n
components in a 3-manifold
Y
, one can define the “full” version of Heegaard Floer complex
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(Y,L)
from the Heegaard diagram by using some version of Lagrangian Floer complexes. The multi-pointed Heegaard link diagram
(\Sigma,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta},{\bf{w}},{\bf{z}})
for such pair
(Y,L)
is defined as follows:

(1)

\Sigma
is a closed oriented genus
g
surface;

(2)

\bm{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{g+n-1}\}
, and
\bm{\beta}=\{\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{g+n-1}\}
are collections of simple closed curves on
\Sigma
such that
\bm{\alpha}
and
\bm{\beta}
each span a
g
-dimensional subspace of
H_{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})
and curves in
\bm{\alpha}
(and
\bm{\beta}
) are pairwise disjoint.

(3)

{\bf{w}}=\{w_{1},\cdots,w_{n}\}
and
{\bf{z}}=\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\}
denote sequences of basepoints on
\Sigma
such that each component of
\Sigma\setminus\bm{\alpha}
(respectively of
\Sigma\setminus\bm{\beta}
) contains a single point of
{\bf{w}}
and a single point of
{\bf{z}}
.

The generators of the link Floer chain complex
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(Y,L)
are tuples of intersection points
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}
of the Lagrangian tori

\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}=\alpha_{1}\times\cdots\times\alpha_{g+n-1},\quad\mathbb{T}_{\beta}=\beta_{1}\times\cdots\times\beta_{g+n-1}

in the symmetric product
\textup{Sym}^{g+n-1}(\Sigma)
.

We work over the coefficient field
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
in the paper. The marked points
z_{i}
and
w_{i}
are associated with formal variables
V_{i}
and
U_{i}
, and the chain complex
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(Y,L)
is a module over the polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\cdots,U_{n},V_{1},\cdots,V_{n}]
. The differential in
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(Y,L)
is obtained by counting pseudo-holomorphic disks in
\textup{Sym}^{g+n-1}(\Sigma)
via:

(13)

\partial\mathbf{x}=\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}}\sum_{\varphi\in\pi_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),\mu(\varphi)=1}(\#\mathcal{M}(\varphi)/\mathbb{R})U_{1}^{n_{w_{1}}(\varphi)}\cdots U_{n}^{n_{w_{n}}(\varphi)}V_{1}^{n_{z_{1}}(\varphi)}\cdots V_{n}^{n_{z_{n}}(\varphi)}\mathbf{y}.

Here the sum is taken over all holomorphic disks from
\mathbf{x}
to
\mathbf{y}
in
\pi_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
representing the homotopy classes of maps
\varphi
from the unit disk
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
to
\textup{Sym}^{g+n-1}(\Sigma)
satisfying some boundary condition. The Maslov index of
\varphi
is denoted by
\mu(\varphi)
. For any point
x\in\Sigma\setminus(\bm{\alpha}\cup\bm{\beta})
,
n_{x}(\varphi)
denotes the intersection number of the divisor
\{x\}\times\textup{Sym}^{g+n-2}(\Sigma)\subset\textup{Sym}^{g+n-1}(\Sigma)
with
\varphi(\mathbb{D})
. The moduli space
\mathcal{M}
consists of all pseudo-holomorphic curves representing
\varphi
for a generic 1-parameter family of almost complex structures on
\textup{Sym}^{g+n-1}(\Sigma)
. For more details, see
[
28
]
.

We denote the homology of the link Floer chain complex
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(Y,L)
by
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(Y,L)
, which is an invariant of the pair
(Y,L)
. The actions of
U_{i}V_{i}
on the complex
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(L)
are pairwise homotopic, so we introduce the following relation:

U_{1}V_{1}=\cdots=U_{n}V_{n}=\mathbf{U},

and let
R_{UV}
denote the ring generated by
U_{1},\cdots,U_{n},V_{1},\cdots,V_{n}
modulo this relation. Then the action of
R
on
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(Y,L)
factors through
R_{UV}
. We get different versions of Heegaard Floer homology by setting extra constraints on the variables
U_{i},V_{i}
. For example,
H_{\ast}(\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(Y,L)/(V_{1}-1,\cdots,V_{n}-1))
is isomorphic to
\mathit{HF}^{-}(Y)
, the minus version of Heegaard Floer homology of the 3-manifold
Y
.

In this paper, we focus on links
L=L_{1}\cup\ldots\cup L_{n}
in the three-sphere and, for simplicity, we write the complex as
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(L)
. The complex
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(L)
and its homology
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L)
have several gradings. Firstly, there is the
\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}
-valued Maslov bigrading, denoted by
(\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}},\textup{gr}_{{\bf{z}}})
, as well as the Alexander multi-grading
A=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
valued in the lattice

\mathbb{H}_{L}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}+\dfrac{1}{2}(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n})

where
\ell_{i}
is the linking number of
L_{i}
with the rest of the components, i.e.,
\ell_{i}=\sum_{j\neq i}lk(L_{i},L_{j})
. These gradings are relatively determined by the following equations:

(14)

\begin{split}&\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(\mathbf{x})-\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(\mathbf{y})=\mu(\varphi)-2n_{{\bf{w}}}(\varphi)\\
&\textup{gr}_{{\bf{z}}}(\mathbf{x})-\textup{gr}_{{\bf{z}}}(\mathbf{y})=\mu(\varphi)-2n_{{\bf{z}}}(\varphi)\\
&A_{i}(\mathbf{x})-A_{i}(\mathbf{y})=n_{z_{i}}(\varphi)-n_{w_{i}}(\varphi)\end{split}

where
\varphi\in\pi_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
and
n_{{\bf{w}}}(\varphi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}n_{w_{i}}(\varphi)
whereas
n_{{\bf{z}}}(\varphi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}n_{z_{i}}(\varphi)
. Furthermore, the actions of
U_{i},V_{i}
are homogeneous with respect to these gradings with weights

(15)

(\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}},\textup{gr}_{{\bf{z}}})(U_{i})=(-2,0),\quad(\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}},\textup{gr}_{{\bf{z}}})(V_{i})=(0,-2),\quad\text{and }A(V_{i})=-A(U_{i})=\mathbf{e}_{i}

where
\mathbf{e}_{i}
is the standard
i
-th coordinate vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The differential on
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(L)
preserves the Alexander grading, while dropping both
\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}
and
\textup{gr}_{{\bf{z}}}
by one. In fact, these gradings satisfy the relation

A_{1}+\ldots+A_{n}=\frac{1}{2}(\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}-\textup{gr}_{{\bf{z}}}).

For any
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{H}_{L}
, let
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(L,\mathbf{s})
denote the subcomplex of
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(L)
generated by all elements
x
such that
A(x)=\mathbf{s}
. By the grading formula (
15
), it is straightforward to see that the product
U_{i}V_{i}
preserves the Alexander gradings, and
\mathcal{C\!F\!L}(L,\mathbf{s})
is a module over the subring
\mathbb{F}[U_{1}V_{1},U_{2}V_{2},\cdots,U_{n}V_{n}]
, and so
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L,\mathbf{s})
is an
\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{U}]
-module. In particular, for any link
L
we have

\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L)=\oplus_{s\in\mathbb{H}_{L}}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L,\mathbf{s}).

and
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L,\mathbf{s})
is the direct sum of one copy of
\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{U}]
with some
\mathbf{U}
-torsion.

Recall that a rational homology sphere
M
is an
L-space
if for each
\text{Spin}^{c}
-structure
\mathfrak{s}
,
HF^{-}(M,\mathfrak{s})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{U}]
. A link
L
in the three-sphere
S^{3}
is an
L-space link
if the Dehn surgery
S^{3}_{\mathbf{d}}(L)
is an L-space for all
\mathbf{d}\gg 0
. Another way to characterize the L-space links is the condition that
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L,\mathbf{s})\cong\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{U}]
for every lattice point
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{H}_{L}
. For any link
L
in the three sphere, we define the link invariant, known as the
h
-function.

Definition 2.5
.

The
h
-function
h:\mathbb{H}_{L}\to\mathbb{Z}
of a link
L
in the three-sphere is defined so that for a given
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{H}_{L}
the value
h(\mathbf{s})
is
-\dfrac{1}{2}\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}
for the maximal Maslov grading of non-torsion elements in
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L,\mathbf{s})
.

For L-space links
L
, its
h
-function can be computed from the Alexander polynomials of the link and its sublinks, see
[
4
]
. Moreover, for such links,
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L)
is determined by its
h
-function (see
[
5
]
), which is determined by these Alexander polynomials.

Recall that for an

2.4.
Alexander polynomials of cable links

The link Floer homology
\mathit{HFL}^{-}(L)
is the categorification of the multi-variable Alexander polynomials of links
L
in the three sphere. That is,

\chi_{L}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{H}_{L}}\chi(\mathit{HFL}^{-}(L,\mathbf{s}))t_{1}^{s_{1}}t_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots t_{n}^{s_{n}}.

Here
\chi_{L}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})
is a normalization of Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})
of
L
as follows.

If
L
is a knot, then

\chi_{L}(t)=\frac{\Delta_{L}(t)}{1-t^{-1}}.

It is a polynomial in
t
and an infinite series in
t^{-1}
. For a link
L
with
n>1
components, we get

\chi_{L}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=(t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{n})^{1/2}\Delta_{L}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}).

Given a knot
K
, recall that the multivariable Alexander polynomial of the
(n,mn)
cable
K_{n,mn}
(with
n
components) is given by the equation:

\Delta_{K_{n,mn}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=\Delta_{K}(\mathbf{t})\Delta_{T(n,mn)}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}),

\chi_{K_{n,mn}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=(1-\mathbf{t}^{-1})\chi_{K}(\mathbf{t})\chi_{T(n,mn)}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}).

where
\mathbf{t}=t_{1}\cdots t_{n}
. Furthermore,

\chi_{T(n,mn)}=\frac{(\mathbf{t}^{m/2}-\mathbf{t}^{-m/2})^{n-1}}{(\mathbf{t}^{1/2}-\mathbf{t}^{-1/2})},

so we conclude

(17)

\chi_{K_{n,mn}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=\mathbf{t}^{-1/2}\chi_{K}(\mathbf{t})(\mathbf{t}^{m/2}-\mathbf{t}^{-m/2})^{n-1}.

This immediately implies the following:

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
K
be a knot of Seifert genus
g(K)
, and
m\gg 1
, define
\mathbf{c}_{m}=\frac{m(n-1)}{2}
. Then

\mathbf{t}^{-\mathbf{c}_{m}}\chi_{K_{n,mn}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=\mathbf{t}^{-1/2}\chi_{K}(\mathbf{t})\mod\mathbf{t}^{-m}\mathbb{Z}[[\mathbf{t}^{-1}]].

In particular,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathbf{t}^{-\mathbf{c}_{m}}\chi_{K_{n,mn}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=\mathbf{t}^{-1/2}\chi_{K}(\mathbf{t}).

Furthermore, the normalized Euler characteristic
t^{-\mathbf{c}_{m}}\chi_{K_{n,mn}}(t)
stabilizes in the region

\{s\preceq g(K)(1,\ldots,1)\}\setminus\{s\preceq(g(K)-m)(1,\ldots,1)\}

and vanishes for
s\not\preceq g(K)(1,\ldots,1)
.

Here two vectors
\mathrm{v}\preceq\mathrm{w}
means that each component
v_{i}\leq w_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,n
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The homology
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,mn))
as a module over

is generated by
Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{m(n-1)}
with Alexander and Maslov degrees

A(Y_{i})=(\mathbf{c}-i,\ldots,\mathbf{c}-i),\ \textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(Y_{i})=-(q+1)(qm+2r),\ \ i=qm+r,\ 0\leq r\leq m-1.

Furthermore, the relations between

and

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 7.3]

[
5

, to write down the generators we need to compute the

-x&\mathrm{if}\ x<0\end{cases}

In particular, for

we get

. Suppose that

, and so
\min(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})=s_{1}
.

(a) If
s_{1}\geq\mathbf{c}
, then
s_{i}-\mathbf{c}+(i-1)m\geq 0
for all
i\geq 1
and so by Equation (
19
)
h(s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{n})=0
. Therefore, the generator of
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T_{n,mn},\mathbf{s})
has Maslov grading equal to
0
. On the other hand,

\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(V^{s_{1}-\mathbf{c}}_{1}\cdots V^{s_{n}-\mathbf{c}}_{n}Y_{0})=\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(Y_{0})=0,\ A(V^{s_{1}-\mathbf{c}}_{1}\cdots V^{s_{n}-\mathbf{c}}_{n}Y_{0})=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}),

so

V^{s_{1}-\mathbf{c}}_{1}\cdots V^{s_{n}-\mathbf{c}}_{n}Y_{0}
generates
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T_{n,mn},\mathbf{s})
.

(b) If
\mathbf{c}-m\leq s_{1}\leq\mathbf{c}
, then
s_{1}-\mathbf{c}\leq 0
, while
s_{i}-\mathbf{c}+(i-1)m\geq 0
for
i>1
. Therefore, by Equation (
19
)
h(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})=\mathbf{c}-s_{1}=k
and so the generator of
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T_{n,mn},\mathbf{s})
has Maslov grading
-2k
.
On the other hand, for
k\leq m
we get

\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(V^{s_{1}-\mathbf{c}+k}_{1}\cdots V^{s_{n}-\mathbf{c}+k}_{n}Y_{k})=\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(Y_{k})=-2k,\ A(V^{s_{1}-\mathbf{c}+k}_{1}\cdots V^{s_{n}-\mathbf{c}+k}_{n}Y_{k})=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}),

so
V^{s_{1}-\mathbf{c}+k}_{1}\cdots V^{s_{n}-\mathbf{c}+k}_{n}Y_{k}
generates
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T_{n,mn},\mathbf{s})
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
\overline{\mathbf{s}}=(\overline{s}_{1},\overline{s}_{2},\cdots,\overline{s}_{n})
with
\min(\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})\geq-m
, the map

\phi_{0}:\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(T(n,mn),\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)\to\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(T(n,(m+1)n),\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The map
\phi_{0}
has Alexander degree
\left(\frac{n-1}{2},\ldots,\frac{n-1}{2}\right)
and Maslov degree 0. Since
T(n,mn)
and
T(n,(m+1)n)
are both L-space links and
\phi_{0}
is injective, it is sufficient to check that the Maslov degrees of the generators of the two
\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{U}]
-towers
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(T(n,mn),\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)
and
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(T(n,(m+1)n),\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)
match.

If
\min(\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})\geq 0
, then by part (a) of Lemma
4.2
the generators of
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(T(n,mn),\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)
and
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(T(n,(m+1)n),\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)
have Maslov degree
0
. If
\min(\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})\geq-k
for some
0<k\leq m
, by part (b) of Lemma
4.2
, the generators of
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(T(n,mn),\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)
and
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}\left(T(n,(m+1)n),\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)
have Maslov degree
-2k
. So
\phi_{0}
is an isomorphism in both cases.

∎

Corollary 4.4
.

For any
\overline{\mathbf{s}}=(\overline{s}_{1},\overline{s}_{2},\cdots,\overline{s}_{n})
with
\min(\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})\geq-m

\mathcal{H}_{n}(O,\overline{\mathbf{s}})\cong\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(T(n,mn),\overline{\mathbf{s}}).

Proof.

It follows from the definition of the colimit: suppose that
Y\in\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(T(n,m^{\prime}n),\overline{\mathbf{s}})
for some
m^{\prime}
. Choose
m
such that
m\geq m^{\prime}
and
\min(\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})\geq-m
. Then,
Y
is equivalent to
\left(\phi_{0}\right)^{m-m^{\prime}}(Y)
in the colimit which is in
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(T(n,mn),\overline{\mathbf{s}})
.
∎

4.2.
Cable algebra and cobordism maps

Next, we explicitly describe the cobordism maps
\phi_{k}
corresponding to adding full twists for
T(n,mn)
. In order to do that, we consider the direct sum of homology for all
n
-strand cables of the unknot with nonnegative integer slopes:

(21)

\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O):=\mathcal{TW}(O_{n})=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,nm))

where
O
is the unknot and
O_{n}
is the
n
-component unlink.

Each summand is
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
graded by Alexander multi-grading (
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
) and Maslov grading (
\mathbb{Z}
). Moreover,
\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O)
has an additional
\mathbb{Z}
grading given by the cabling slope
m
, we call it
twist grading
and denote it by
\mathbf{tw}
. Note that cobordism maps
\phi_{k}
, corresponding to adding a full twist, act naturally on
\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O)
with twist grading
1
i.e. they map
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,mn))
to
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,(m+1)n))
.

Definition 4.5
.

The
n
-strand
cable algebra

\mathcal{A}_{n}
is defined as a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
graded algebra over
R_{UV}
with commuting generators
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}
and the following relations:

•

Linear relations

(22)

U_{I}a_{k-1}=V_{\overline{I}}a_{k}

where
I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
is an arbitrary subset with
|I|=k
and
\overline{I}=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus I.

•

Quadratic relations

(23)

a_{i}a_{j}=\mathbf{U}^{k\ell-ij}a_{k}a_{\ell}

whenever
i+j=k+\ell
and
i\leq k\leq\ell\leq j
.

As above, the gradings correspond to the Alexander (
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
) and Maslov (
\mathbb{Z}
) gradings, along with an additional
\mathbb{Z}

twist grading
. The generator
a_{k}
has Alexander grading

A(a_{k})=\left(\frac{n-1}{2}-k,\ldots,\frac{n-1}{2}-k\right),

Maslov grading
\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(a_{k})=-k^{2}-k
and twist grading
\mathbf{tw}(a_{k})=1
.

It is easy to check that the relations are homogeneous with respect to all three gradings.

Theorem 4.6
.

The homology
\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O)
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
graded module over the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}
where the generators
a_{k}
act by cobordism maps
\phi_{k}
. Furthermore,
\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O)
is a free
\mathcal{A}_{n}
-module generated by a single class
1\in\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,0))
.

Proof.

We proceed in several steps.

Step 1:
We show that there is a well-defined action of algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}
on
\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O)
, where
a_{k}
acts by the cobordism map
\phi_{k}
. First, we prove that the actions of
\phi_{k}
and
\phi_{k^{\prime}}
on
\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O)
commute. By
[
13
]
all links
T(n,nm)
are L-space links, so
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,mn))
is free of rank 1 over
\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{U}]
in each Alexander degree. The maps
\phi_{k}
and
\phi_{k^{\prime}}
correspond to negative definite cobordisms, so they are both injective
[
1
]
, and hence determined by their Alexander and Maslov degrees. Now we observe that both compositions
\phi_{k}\circ\phi_{k^{\prime}}
and
\phi_{k^{\prime}}\circ\phi_{k}

have the same Alexander and Maslov degrees by Proposition
2.7
, and hence coincide.

Next, we check that these cobordism maps satisfy relations (
22
) and (
23
). Note that
a_{k}
has the same Alexander, Maslov and twist grading as
\phi_{k}
, thus relations (
22
) and (
23
) being homogeneous, implies that the cobordism maps satisfy these relations as well and the action is well-defined.

Step 2:
We prove that
\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O)
is generated by a single class
1\in\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,0))
under the action of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
.
We follow Theorem
4.1
and claim that the generator
Y_{i}\in\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,mn))
can be identified with the monomial
a_{q}^{m-r}a_{q+1}^{r}
(where
i=mq+r
) applied to
1
.
By the above, the element
a_{q}^{m-r}a_{q+1}^{r}(1)
is nonzero, and it is sufficient to verify that it has the same Alexander and Maslov degree as
Y_{i}
. Indeed, the Alexander grading equals

A_{j}(a_{q}^{m-r}a_{q+1}^{r})=(m-r)\left(\frac{n-1}{2}-q\right)+r\left(\frac{n-1}{2}-q-1\right)=m\frac{n-1}{2}-mq-r=\mathbf{c}-i

and the Maslov grading equals

\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(a_{q}^{m-r}a_{q+1}^{r})=-(m-r)q(q+1)-r(q+1)(q+2)=-(q+1)(mq+2r).

To sum up, we have a surjective map of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
modules from
\mathcal{A}_{n}
to
\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O)
.

Step 3:
Finally, we need to prove that this map is an isomorphism and
\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O)\simeq\mathcal{A}_{n}
. For this, we need to find the generators of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
over
R_{UV}
, relations between them and compare these with Theorem
4.1
.

Define
\widetilde{Y_{i}}=a_{q}^{m-r}a_{q+1}^{r}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
where
i=mq+r
. Let us prove that
\widetilde{Y_{i}}
generate
\mathcal{A}_{n}
over
R_{UV}
. Indeed, consider a monomial
M=a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{m}}
with
i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\cdots\leq i_{m}
. If
i_{m}-i_{1}\leq 1
then
M=\widetilde{Y}_{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{m}}
by the above. Otherwise we can use (
23
) to write

a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{m}}=\mathbf{U}^{i_{m}-i_{1}-1}a_{i_{1}+1}a_{i_{m}-1}.

since
(i_{1}+1)(i_{m}-1)-i_{1}i_{m}=i_{m}-i_{1}-1
.
This decreases the power of
a_{i_{1}}
and we can proceed by induction.

Finally, we need to verify that the relations from Theorem
4.1
hold for
\widetilde{Y_{i}}
. This is clear since the relations between

\widetilde{Y}_{i}=a_{q}^{m-r}a_{q+1}^{r}
and
\widetilde{Y}_{i+1}=a_{q}^{m-r-1}a_{q+1}^{r+1}
agree with the relations between
a_{q}
and
a_{q+1}
,
up to multiplication by
a_{q}^{m-r-1}a_{q+1}^{r}
.
∎

Example 4.7
.

The homology of
T(2,2m)
is generated by
a_{0}^{m},a_{0}^{m-1}a_{1},\ldots,a_{1}^{m}
modulo relations
U_{1}a_{0}=V_{2}a_{1}
and
U_{2}a_{0}=V_{1}a_{1}
, times a degree
(m-1)
monomial.

Example 4.8
.

The homology of
T(3,6)
is generated by six degree 2 monomials in
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}
, but in fact we have a relation
a_{0}a_{2}=\mathbf{U}a_{1}^{2}
since
a_{0}a_{2}
and
a_{1}^{2}
have the same Alexander degree. So
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(3,6))
is generated by
a_{0}^{2},a_{0}a_{1},a_{1}^{2},a_{1}a_{2},a_{2}^{2}
.

Example 4.9
.

Similarly, the homology of
T(3,3m)
is generated by
2m+1
monomials

a_{0}^{m},a_{0}^{m-1}a_{1},\ldots,a_{0}a_{1}^{m-1},a_{1}^{m},a_{1}^{m-1}a_{2},\ldots,a_{1}a_{2}^{m-1},a_{2}^{m}.

4.3.
Colored knot Floer homology of the unknot

Finally, we are ready to compute the colored homology
\mathcal{H}_{n}(O)
. Recall that this invariant is defined as the limit of the directed system of
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,mn))
along the directed system of maps

\phi_{0}
. We define the algebraic analogue of this as follows:

Definition 4.10
.

Let the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}

be the (graded) localization of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
in
a_{0}
:

\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}:=\mathcal{A}_{n}[a_{0}^{-1}]:=\mathrm{span}\left\{\frac{Y}{a_{0}^{m}}:\mathbf{tw}(Y)=m\right\}\subset\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{A}_{n}).

Note that since
\phi_{0}:\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O)\to\mathrm{Cab}_{n}(O)
is injective, by Theorem
4.6
the multiplication by
a_{0}
in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is injective as well. Therefore
a_{0}
is not a zero divisor in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
, and
\mathcal{A}_{n}[a_{0}^{-1}]
embeds into
\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{A}_{n}).

Lemma 4.11
.

The colored homology of the unknot
\mathcal{H}_{n}(O)
is a free rank 1 module over
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
.

Proof.

This is an easy consequence of Theorem
4.6
. We identify an element
Y\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
such that
\mathbf{tw}(Y)=m
with a class
Y(1)\in\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,mn))
. By Theorem
4.6
this yields an isomorphism
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,mn))\simeq\mathrm{span}\left\{Y\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\mid\mathbf{tw}(Y)=m\right\}
.

The colored homology
\mathcal{H}_{n}(O)
is spanned by classes
Y(1)\in\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,mn))
for all
m
modulo relations
Y(1)\sim\phi_{0}(Y(1))
, equivalently, by the elements
Y\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
modulo relations
Y\sim a_{0}Y
. Given such an element
Y
, we can associate to it the fraction
\frac{Y}{a_{0}^{m}}\in\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{A}_{n})
. This agrees with our equivalence relation since

\frac{Y}{a_{0}^{m}}=\frac{a_{0}Y}{a_{0}^{m+1}}.

and the result follows.
∎

Next, we would like to have some concrete description of
\mathcal{A}_{n}[a_{0}^{-1}]
and
\mathcal{H}_{n}(O)
.

Theorem 4.12
.

The colored homology of unknot is isomorphic to

(24)

\mathcal{H}_{n}(O)\simeq\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}\simeq\frac{\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\ldots,U_{n},V_{1},\ldots,V_{n},\mathsf{A}]}{(U_{i}=V_{\overline{i}}\mathsf{A})}.

where
\mathsf{A}=a_{1}/a_{0}
. The generator
\mathsf{A}
has Maslov grading
\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}=-2
and renormalized Alexander grading
(-1,\ldots,-1)
.

Proof.

The first isomorphism follows from the Lemma
4.11
, so we focus on
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}=\mathcal{A}_{n}[a_{0}^{-1}]
.
The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is generated by
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}
, so
\mathcal{A}_{n}[a_{0}^{-1}]
is generated by
a_{k}/a_{0}
. We claim that

(25)

\frac{a_{k}}{a_{0}}=\mathbf{U}^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\left(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{0}}\right)^{k}=\mathbf{U}^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\mathsf{A}^{k}.

This is equivalent to the identity
a_{k}a_{0}^{k-1}=\mathbf{U}^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}a_{1}^{k}
which can be proved by repeated application of (
23
) and induction in
k
:

a_{k}a_{0}^{k-1}=(a_{k}a_{0})a_{0}^{k-2}=(\mathbf{U}^{k-1}a_{k-1}a_{1})a_{0}^{k-2}=\mathbf{U}^{k-1}a_{1}(a_{k-1}a_{0}^{k-2}).

Therefore
\mathcal{A}_{n}[a_{0}^{-1}]
is generated by the powers of
\mathsf{A}
over
R_{UV}
.

The relations (
22
) for
k=1
have the form
U_{i}a_{0}=V_{\overline{i}}a_{1}
and imply
U_{i}=V_{\overline{i}}\mathsf{A}
. Let us check that these relations in turn imply all other relations in
\mathcal{A}_{n}[a_{0}^{-1}]
. First note that

U_{i}V_{i}=V_{1}\cdots V_{k}\mathsf{A}

does not depend on
i
, and we do not need to add this as an additional relation.

Let
|I|=k>1
. We claim that

(26)

U_{I}=\mathbf{U}^{k-1}V_{\overline{I}}\mathsf{A}.

Indeed, if
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
then

\mathbf{U}^{k-1}V_{\overline{I}}=U_{i_{2}}\cdots U_{i_{k}}V_{i_{2}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}V_{\overline{I}}=U_{i_{2}}\cdots U_{i_{k}}V_{\overline{i_{1}}}

hence

\mathbf{U}^{k-1}V_{\overline{I}}\mathsf{A}=U_{i_{2}}\cdots U_{i_{k}}(V_{\overline{i_{1}}}\mathsf{A})=U_{i_{2}}\cdots U_{i_{k}}U_{i_{1}}=U_{I}.

Now (
26
) implies (after multiplication by
\mathbf{U}^{\frac{(k-1)(k-2)}{2}}\mathsf{A}^{k-1}
):

\mathbf{U}^{\frac{(k-1)(k-2)}{2}}U_{I}\mathsf{A}^{k-1}=\mathbf{U}^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}V_{\overline{I}}\mathsf{A}^{k}

By applying (
25
) this can be rewritten as
U_{I}\frac{a_{k-1}}{a_{0}}=V_{\overline{I}}\frac{a_{k}}{a_{0}}
, and after multiplication by
a_{0}
we get (
22
).

To prove (
23
), we divide both sides by
a_{0}^{2}
and get

\left(\frac{a_{i}}{a_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{j}}{a_{0}}\right)=\mathbf{U}^{k\ell-ij}\left(\frac{a_{k}}{a_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{\ell}}{a_{0}}\right)

and this is immediate from (
25
).

∎

Remark 4.13
.

We can also directly check the isomorphism (
24
) using Lemma
4.2
and Corollary
4.4
. By Lemma
4.2
, we have two cases. If
\min(\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})\geq 0
then
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(T(n,mn),\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})
is generated by

V_{1}^{\overline{s}_{1}}\cdots V_{n}^{\overline{s}_{n}}Y_{0}=V_{1}^{\overline{s}_{1}}\cdots V_{n}^{\overline{s}_{n}}a_{0}^{m}\sim V_{1}^{\overline{s}_{1}}\cdots V_{n}^{\overline{s}_{n}}.

If
-m\leq\min(\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})\leq 0
then
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(T(n,mn),\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})
is generated by

V_{1}^{\overline{s}_{1}+k}\cdots V_{n}^{\overline{s}_{n}+k}Y_{k}=V_{1}^{\overline{s}_{1}+k}\cdots V_{n}^{\overline{s}_{n}+k}a_{0}^{m-k}a_{1}^{k}\sim V_{1}^{\overline{s}_{1}+k}\cdots V_{n}^{\overline{s}_{n}+k}\mathsf{A}^{k}.

Therefore the limiting homology is indeed isomorphic to

\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\ldots,U_{n},V_{1},\ldots,V_{n},\mathsf{A}]/(U_{i}=V_{\hat{i}}\mathsf{A})\simeq\mathbb{F}[V_{1},\ldots,V_{n},\mathsf{A}]

as a graded vector space.

5.
Module structures of Colored knot Floer homology

In this section, we use the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and its localization to study the module structure for cabled and colored knot Floer homology.

5.1.
Action of cobordism maps: general case

Recall that for an
n
-component link
L
in
S^{3}
such that every component intersects a disk
D
positively exactly once,

\mathcal{TW}_{D}(L)=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m}).

As before, this is a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
graded vector space with Alexander, Maslov and twist gradings.

Theorem 5.1
.

The space
\mathcal{TW}_{D}(L)
is a triply graded module over the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

Proof.

We have cobordism maps
\phi_{k}:\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})\to\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m+1})
. We construct the action of the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}
such that the generators
a_{k}
act on
\mathcal{TW}_{D}(L)
via the maps
\phi_{k}
. For this to make sense, we need to verify that the maps
\phi_{k}
pairwise commute and satisfy the relations (
22
) and (
23
).
We closely follow the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 3.13]
.

We would like to compare the cobordism maps from
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})
to
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m+1})
and the similar maps from
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,mn))
to
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(T(n,(m+1)n))
. To distinguish them, we denote them respectively by
\phi_{k}^{L}
and
\phi_{k}^{O}
.

Let us denote by
L^{\prime}_{m}
the link
L_{m}
with an extra pair of basepoints
w^{\prime}_{i},z^{\prime}_{i}
per component. We extend the coloring
\sigma
on
L_{m}
to
L^{\prime}_{m}
so that its codomain is
P^{\prime}=P\sqcup\{p^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,p^{\prime}_{n}\}

and
\sigma^{\prime}(w^{\prime}_{i})=p^{\prime}_{i},\sigma^{\prime}(z^{\prime}_{i})=\sigma(z_{i})
.
Then
\sigma^{\prime}
restricts to a coloring of
L_{m}
with codomain
P^{\prime}
, and for simplicity, we still denote it by
\sigma^{\prime}
. The ring isomorphism
\mathcal{R}_{P}^{-}\cong\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\cdots,U_{n},V_{1},\cdots V_{n}]
extends to an isomorphism

\mathcal{R}_{P^{\prime}}^{-}\cong\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\cdots,U_{n},V_{1},\cdots,V_{n},U^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,U^{\prime}_{n}]

by sending formal variables
X_{p^{\prime}_{i}}
to
U^{\prime}_{i}
. Then, we have the following isomorphisms

\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m}^{\sigma^{\prime}})\cong\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}\mathbb{F}[U^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,U^{\prime}_{n}]

and

\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m}^{{}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}})\cong\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m}^{\sigma^{\prime}})/\langle U_{1}-U^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,U_{n}-U^{\prime}_{n}\rangle\cong\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m}).

Let
\mathcal{S}_{m}
be the quasi-stabilization cobordism from
L_{m}
to
L^{\prime}_{m}
. Then, the composition of the induced cobordism map from
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})
to
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L^{\prime}_{m})
with the latter isomorphism is identity.

Observe that
L_{m}
can be obtained as band connected sum of
L
and
T(n,mn)
where we add one band per link component. Consider the following cobordisms:

•

\mathcal{C}_{m}
is the decorated cobordism from
L_{m}
to
L_{m+1}
obtained from the
(-1)
-surgery on an unknot bounding a disk intersecting each component of
L_{m}
positively at exactly one point,

•

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{m}
is the induced decorated cobordism from
L^{\prime}_{m}
to
L^{\prime}_{m+1}
similar to
\mathcal{C}_{m}
,

•

\mathcal{B}_{m}
from
L_{m}
to

•

from

to

,

•

\mathcal{C}_{O_{n},m}
is the cobordism from
L_{m}\sqcup O_{n}
to
L_{m}\sqcup T(n,n)
given by the 2-handle attachment along the (-1)-framed unknot for inserting a full twist in

.

Define

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{m}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{m}\circ\mathcal{S}_{m}=\mathcal{S}_{m+1}\circ\mathcal{C}_{m}.

Under the aforementioned isomorphism
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m+1}^{{}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}})\cong\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m+1})
, the homomorphism induced by the cobordism map
F_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{m},\mathfrak{s}_{k}}

from
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})
to
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m+1})
is equal to
\phi^{L}_{k}
. Note that
\mathcal{S}_{m}
can be decomposed as the cobordism
\mathcal{B}_{m}
containing
n
births from
L_{m}
to

followed by
n
band attachments cobordism
\mathcal{C}_{b,m}
.

By arguing as in
[
1

(see also Proposition 3.8 in
[
1
]
) we can isotope the attaching circle of the 2-handle in
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{m}
so that we can
change the order of 2-handle attachment and band attachments, which shows that the composition

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{m}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{m}\circ\mathcal{C}_{b,m}\circ\mathcal{B}_{m}

\mathcal{C}_{b,m,1}\circ\mathcal{C}_{O_{n},m}\circ\mathcal{B}_{m}.

Therefore by naturality (
[

\phi_{k}^{L}=F_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{m},\mathfrak{s}_{k}}=F_{\mathcal{C}_{b,m,1}}\circ F_{\mathcal{C}_{O_{n},m},\mathfrak{s}_{k}}\circ F_{\mathcal{B}_{m}}=F_{\mathcal{C}_{b,m,1}}\circ\left(\mathrm{Id}\otimes\phi_{k}^{O}\right)\circ F_{\mathcal{B}_{m}}.

Here,

denotes the full twist cobordism map. Since all the maps are

-linear and

22

Next, we need to study the compositions of two maps
\phi_{\ell}^{L}:\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})\rightarrow\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m+1})
and
\phi_{k}^{L}:\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m+1})\rightarrow\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m+2})

\phi_{k}^{L}\circ\phi_{\ell}^{L}=\phi_{k}^{L}\circ F_{\mathcal{C}_{b,m,1}}\circ\left(\mathrm{Id}\otimes\phi_{k}^{O}\right)\circ F_{\mathcal{B}_{m}}.

By isotopying the attaching circle of the second
2
-handle, changing the order of band attachments and the second 2-handle attachment, and using the naturality of link Floer homology we have

\phi_{k}^{L}\circ\phi_{\ell}^{L}=F_{\mathcal{C}_{b,m,2}}\circ\left(\mathrm{Id}\otimes\left(\phi_{k}^{O}\circ\phi_{\ell}^{O}\right)\right)\circ F_{\mathcal{B}_{m}}.

Consequently, since (

∎

5.2.
Colored homology: general case

Next, we turn to the limit
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
for a link
L
with the decorated disk
D
.

Theorem 5.2
.

The colored homology
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
is a module over the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of Theorem
5.1
. Consider an element
b=\frac{a}{a_{0}^{t}}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}[a_{0}^{-1}]
with
\mathbf{tw}(a)=t
, and a representative in an equivalence class
Y\in\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})
. We define
b(Y):=[a(Y)]
where the action of
a
is given by Theorem
5.1
.

We need to check that this is well-defined under the equivalence relations for
b
and
Y
. First, we can write

b=\frac{a}{a_{0}^{t}}=\frac{aa_{0}}{a_{0}^{t+1}}.

Then

aa_{0}(Y)=a_{0}(a(Y))=\phi_{0}(a(Y))\sim a(Y).

Second, we have
Y\sim\phi_{0}(Y)
and

a(\phi_{0}(Y))=a(a_{0}(Y))=a_{0}(a(Y))=\phi_{0}(a(Y))\sim a(Y).

Note that in both cases we need to use the fact that the actions of
a
and
\phi_{0}
commute. This follows from the fact (proven in Theorem
5.1
) that the actions of
\phi_{k}
and
\phi_{0}
commute. In fact, by Lemma
2.3
this is enough to ensure the action of
a
on the colimit, as in the second case.
∎

5.3.
Braid group action

The homology of cables has a natural braid group action by cobordisms that swap different components. We summarize its properties in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.3
.

The braid group
\mathrm{Br}_{n}
acts on
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})
for all
m
. Given a braid
\beta\in\mathrm{Br}_{n}
and the corresponding permutation
w_{\beta}\in S_{n}
we have

\beta\circ U_{i}=U_{w_{\beta}(i)}\beta,\ \beta\circ V_{i}=V_{w_{\beta}(i)}\beta.

Furthermore, the action of
\mathrm{Br}_{n}
commutes with the maps
\phi_{k}:\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m})\to\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(L_{m+1})
:

\beta\circ\phi_{k}=\phi_{k}\circ\beta.

Proof.

All statements follow from the naturality of cobordism maps in link Floer homology
[
38
]
. For the last equation, note that
\phi_{k}
corresponds to blowing down the meridian which commutes (up to isotopy) with permuting the components of the link.

∎

Corollary 5.4
.

There is a natural
S_{n}
action on the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}
which permutes
U_{i},V_{i}
and fixes
\mathbf{U}
and
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}
. Furthermore, the action of
\mathrm{Br}_{n}
on
\mathcal{TW}_{D}(L)
and the action of
S_{n}
on
\mathcal{A}_{n}
are compatible via the action of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
on
\mathcal{TW}_{D}(L)
from Theorem
5.1
.

Note that the relations (
22
) and (
23
) are clearly invariant under the
S_{n}
action. We can apply these results to colored homology.

Proposition 5.5
.

There is a natural action of
\mathrm{Br}_{n}
on the colored homology
\mathcal{H}_{D}(L)
. It is compatible with the action of
S_{n}
on
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{col}}=\mathcal{A}_{n}[a_{0}^{-1}]
which permutes
U_{i},V_{i}
and fixes
\mathsf{A}
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that braid group action fixes
a_{0}
, and Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Examples: L-space knots

As in (

),
we define
\mathbf{c}_{m}=m(n-1)/2
and

. Similarly, we define
h^{\mathrm{stab}}_{n,mn}(\overline{\mathbf{s}})=h_{n,mn}(\mathbf{s})
. If

is an L-space link then

\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(K_{n,mn},\overline{\mathbf{s}})\simeq\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{U}]\left[-2h_{n,mn}^{\mathrm{stab}}(\overline{\mathbf{s}})\right].

6.1.
Colored homology: description

We study cables
K_{n,mn}
with
n
components. Each of these components is isotopic to
K_{1,m}=K
. In this section, we assume that
K
is an L-space knot and
m
is large, so that
K_{n,mn}
is an
L
-space link by
[
13
]
.
First, we can describe the colored homology as a graded
\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{U}]
-module, one Alexander grading at a time.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let us fix the renormalized Alexander grading
\overline{\mathbf{s}}=(\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})
. Then for
m
large enough we have

\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(K_{n,mn},\overline{\mathbf{s}})\simeq\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{U}][-2h_{K}(\min(\overline{\mathbf{s}}))]

where
\min(\overline{\mathbf{s}})=\min\{\overline{s}_{1},\cdots,\overline{s}_{n}\}
, and the map
\phi_{0}:\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(K_{n,mn},\overline{\mathbf{s}})\to\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(K_{n,(m+1)n},\overline{\mathbf{s}})
is an isomorphism. In particular, for
m
large enough we get

\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(K_{n,mn},\overline{\mathbf{s}})\simeq\mathcal{H}_{n}(K,\overline{\mathbf{s}}).

Proof.

As in Theorem
4.6
, we argue that the map
\phi_{0}:\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(K_{n,mn})\to\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(K_{n,(m+1)n})
is nonzero, has (stable) Alexander degree zero and its action on the tower
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}^{\mathrm{stab}}(K_{n,mn},\overline{\mathbf{s}})\simeq\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{U}]
is determined by its Maslov degree.

Note that the
h
-function is symmetric, and by
[
13
, Theorem 4.3]
we can write it for
K_{n,mn}
as

h_{n,mn}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})=h_{K}(s_{1}-\mathbf{c}_{m})+h_{K}(s_{2}-\mathbf{c}_{m}+m)+\ldots+h_{K}(s_{n}-\mathbf{c}_{m}+(n-1)m).

where
s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\ldots\leq s_{n}
and
h_{K}
denotes the
h
-function of
K
. So,

h_{n,mn}^{\mathrm{stab}}(\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})=h_{K}(\overline{s}_{1})+h_{K}(\overline{s}_{2}+m)+\ldots+h_{K}(\overline{s}_{n}+(n-1)m).

We can choose
m
large enough so that
\overline{s}_{i}>g(K)-m
for all
i
, then
h_{K}(\overline{s_{k}}+(k-1)m)=0
for
k>1
. We conclude that for large
m

h_{n,mn}^{\mathrm{stab}}(\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{n})=h_{K}(\overline{s}_{1})=h_{K}(\min(\overline{\mathbf{s}})).

and the result follows.
∎

Next, we describe the module structure of this homology over
\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\ldots,U_{n},V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}]
.
First, we need to introduce some notations.
Following
[
29
]
we have

\chi_{K}(t)=\frac{\Delta_{K}(t)}{1-t^{-1}}=\sum_{\sigma\in S}t^{\sigma}=t^{\sigma_{1}}+t^{\sigma_{2}}+\ldots

where
S=\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots\}
is some bounded above subset of
\mathbb{Z}
and we assume
\sigma_{1}>\sigma_{2}>\ldots
where
\sigma_{i}=1-i
for
i\geq g+1
. We can describe
\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K)
by generators and relations:

•

The generators are
z_{\sigma_{i}}
for
\sigma_{i}\in S
, with Alexander degree
\sigma_{i}
and homological degree
-2h_{K}(\sigma_{i})=-2(i-1)
.

•

The relations are

(27)

Uz_{\sigma_{i}}=V^{\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{i+1}-1}z_{\sigma_{i+1}}.

Indeed, both
Uz_{\sigma_{i}}
and
V^{\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{i+1}-1}z_{\sigma_{i+1}}
have Alexander degree
\sigma_{i}-1
and homological degree
-2i=-2(i-1)-2
. Note that when
\sigma_{i+1}=\sigma_{i}-1
we have
Uz_{\sigma_{i}}=z_{\sigma_{i+1}}
, so we can in principle eliminate
z_{\sigma_{i+1}}
. In particular, for
i\geq g+1
we can write
z_{\sigma_{i}}=U^{i-g-1}z_{-g}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

The colored homology
\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)
has generators

\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i}}
of Alexander degree

A\left(\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i}}\right)=(\sigma_{i},\ldots,\sigma_{i}),

and Maslov degree
-2(i-1)
. The relations are given by

(28)

U_{j}\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i}}=V^{(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{i+1})\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i+1}},1\leq j\leq n,

where
\mathbf{e}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{e}_{j}
, and
\mathbf{e}_{j}
is the unit vector with
j
-th entry equal one. Here,

V^{(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{i+1})\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}=(V_{1}\cdots V_{n})^{\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{i+1}-1}\left(\prod_{l\neq j}V_{l}\right).

Proof.

Let
z(\overline{\mathbf{s}})
denote the generator of the
\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{U}]
tower in
\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)
of renormalized Alexander degree
\overline{\mathbf{s}}
. By Lemma
6.1
the Maslov degree of
z(\overline{\mathbf{s}})
equals
-2h_{K}(\min(\overline{\mathbf{s}}))
.

We can describe the action of
U_{i}
and
V_{i}
on generators
z(\overline{\mathbf{s}})
as follows:

Case 1:

\min(\overline{\mathbf{s}})=\min(\overline{\mathbf{s}}+\mathbf{e}_{i})=\sigma
. In this case

(29)

V_{i}z(\overline{\mathbf{s}})=z(\overline{\mathbf{s}}+\mathbf{e}_{i}),\ U_{i}z(\overline{\mathbf{s}}+\mathbf{e}_{i})=\mathbf{U}z(\overline{\mathbf{s}}).

Note that this implies

z(\overline{\mathbf{s}})=V_{1}^{\overline{s}_{1}-\sigma}\cdots V_{n}^{\overline{s}_{n}-\sigma}z(\sigma,\ldots,\sigma)

and the diagonal elements generate
\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)
under the action of
V_{i}
.

Case 2:

\min(\overline{\mathbf{s}})=\sigma,\min(\overline{\mathbf{s}}+\mathbf{e}_{i})=\sigma+1
. In this case

(30)

V_{i}z(\overline{\mathbf{s}})=\mathbf{U}^{h_{K}(\sigma)-h_{K}(\sigma+1)}z(\overline{\mathbf{s}}+\mathbf{e}_{i}),\ U_{i}z(\overline{\mathbf{s}}+\mathbf{e}_{i})=\mathbf{U}^{1-h_{K}(\sigma)+h_{K}(\sigma+1)}z(\overline{\mathbf{s}}).

By combining (
29
) and (
30
) we get

V_{1}\cdots V_{n}z(\sigma,\ldots,\sigma)=\mathbf{U}^{h_{K}(\sigma)-h_{K}(\sigma+1)}z(\sigma+1,\ldots,\sigma+1).

In particular,
V_{1}\cdots V_{n}z(\sigma,\ldots,\sigma)=z(\sigma+1,\ldots,\sigma+1)
whenever
h_{K}(\sigma)=h_{K}(\sigma+1)
, so the homology is generated by

\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i}}:=z(\sigma_{i},\ldots,\sigma_{i}).

The relations (
28
) follow directly from grading computation and by a similar argument as Theorem
4.1
, the relations (
28
) generate all relations among the generators.

∎

Remark 6.3
.

We get an infinite chain of generators
\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i}}
for
i\geq g+1
, these have Alexander degrees
A(\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i}})=(1-i,\ldots,1-i)
and Maslov degrees
\textup{gr}_{{\bf{w}}}(\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i}})=-2(i-1)
. The relations between these generators are nontrivial and given by

U_{i}\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i}}=V_{\overline{i}}\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i+1}}.

In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)
is infinitely generated over
\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\ldots,U_{n},V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}]
.

6.2.
Colored homology: module structure

In this subsection we determine the module structure of
\mathcal{H}_{n}(K)
over the cabled algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}[a_{0}^{-1}]
assuming that
K
is an L-space knot.

Lemma 6.4
.

In the notations of Theorem
6.2
the action of
\mathsf{A}=a_{1}/a_{0}
is given by

\mathsf{A}\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i}}=(V_{1}\cdots V_{n})^{\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{i+1}-1}\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i+1}}.

Proof.

The action of
\phi_{1}:\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,mn})\to\mathcal{H\!F\!L}(K_{n,(m+1)n})
is nontrivial and sends an

The element

has (renormalized) Alexander degree
(\sigma_{i}-1,\ldots,\sigma_{i}-1)
and Maslov degree
-2i
which agrees with the Alexander and Maslov degrees of

∎

Remark 6.5
.

For
i\geq g+1
we have

\mathsf{A}\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i}}=\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i+1}},

so we can compactly write

\widetilde{z}_{\sigma_{i}}=\mathsf{A}^{i-g-1}\widetilde{z}_{-g}.

is finitely generated over

Theorem 6.6
.

Assume

as a

in the left hand side. Indeed,

over

(31)

z_{\sigma_{i}}\otimes\mathsf{A}f=z_{\sigma_{i}}\otimes\varepsilon_{n}(U)f=(Uz_{\sigma_{i}})\otimes f=V^{\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{i+1}-1}z_{\sigma_{i+1}}\otimes f=\\

7.
Application: crossing change maps

Suppose two knot diagrams
K^{+}
and
K^{-}
differ at a single crossing which is positive in
K^{+}
and negative in
K^{-}
. We would like to find a relation between their colored homologies, but first we relate the homology of cables.

The blackboard framings for both
K^{+}
and
K^{-}
correspond to the writhe of their diagrams. Note that the writhe of
K^{+}
is 2 more than the writhe of
K^{-}
. The
n
-component cables of
K^{+}
and
K^{-}
are related by
n^{2}
crossing changes. More precisely, changing
n^{2}
negative crossings in
K^{-}_{n,mn}
to positive results in
K^{+}_{n,(m+2)n}
since the linking number between each pair of components increases by 2.

Remark 7.4
.

The cables
K^{+}_{n,(m+2)n}
and
K^{-}_{n,mn}
are related by a sequence of

Let

of Maslov degree zero and Alexander degree

It is easy to see that, as before,

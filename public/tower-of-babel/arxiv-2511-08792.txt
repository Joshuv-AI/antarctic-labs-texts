Higher Nash Blow-ups and Nobile’s Theorem
Shravan Saoji

Abstract
. We study the higher Nash blow-ups introduced by T. Yasuda and investigate the higher version of the classical Nobile’s theorem. In particular, we give a characteristic free proof of the higher Nobile’s theorem for the graded case. We also give a proof for the 2nd order Nash blow-ups in characteristic zero.

1.
Introduction

The higher Nash blow-up was first introduced by T. Yasuda
[
8
]
which can be thought of as a higher version of the Nash blow-up of a variety. The usual Nash blow-up of a variety replaces each smooth point by the limit of nearby tangent spaces in a Grassmannian. The higher Nash blow-up of a variety instead replaces each smooth point by the limit of nearby infinitesimal neighborhoods in a Hilbert scheme of points. The Nash blow-up and later higher Nash blow-up were introduced as a method for resolution of singularities. An important step towards this problem is to prove that Nash and higher Nash blow-ups modify singular varieties. In the case of the Nash blow-up, there is a classical theorem by A. Nobile
[
6
]
for varieties over
\mathbb{C}
. The theorem says that the Nash blow-up of a variety is isomorphic to the variety if and only if the variety is non-singular. The same theorem was proved by D. Duarte and L. Núñez-Betancourt
[
3
]
for normal varieties over a field of positive characteristic. We investigate the higher version of Nobiles’s theorem for the graded case. The higher Nobile’s theorem has been investigated for curves in
[
8
]
, and for normal hypersurfaces in
[
4
]
, and
[
3
]
. We prove the following theorems.

Theorem A

(see
10.1
)
.

Let
X
be a graded algebraic variety over an algebraically closed field
k
. Suppose
\mathrm{Nash}_{n}(X)\cong X
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then

(1)

if
\operatorname{char}(k)=0
, then
X
is a non-singular variety.

(2)

if
X
is normal and
\operatorname{char}(k)>0
, then
X
is a non-singular variety.

We also give the higher version of Nobile’s theorem for the 2nd order Nash
blow-ups.

Theorem B

(see
10.2
)
.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety over an algebraically closed field
k
with
\operatorname{char}(k)=0
. Suppose
\mathrm{Nash}_{2}(X)\cong X
, then
X
is a non-singular variety.

We show that theorems A and B are achieved as corollaries to the following theorem.

Theorem C

(see
3.3
)
.

Assume the hypothesis of theorem A or theorem B, then
\mathrm{Nash}_{1}(X)\cong X
.

We can say that the Nobiles’s theorem implies the higher Nobile’s theorem in
these cases.

Terminology and assumptions

Throughout the paper, we will assume
k
to be an algebraically closed field of
arbitrary characteristic unless specified. All our varieties will be reduced and irreducible
separated schemes of finite type over
k
. We say an affine variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{s}
is graded if the defining ideal of
X
is generated by homogeneous elements.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give the definition, some properties, and facts of the module of principal parts of order
n
. Further, we introduce the notion of higher Nash blow-up.

The module of principal parts

The module of principal parts is a generalized version of the module of Kähler differentials.

Definition 2.1
.

[
5
]

Let
X
be a Noetherian
k
scheme. Consider the diagonal morphism
\Delta:X\longrightarrow X\times_{k}X
and
\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}
be the ideal sheaf defining the diagonal
\Delta(X)\subset X\times_{k}X
. We define the sheaf of principal parts of order
n
, denoted
\mathcal{P}_{X/k}^{n}
, as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{X/k}^{n}:=\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}X}/\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}^{n+1}.

Similarly, define
\mathcal{P}_{X/k,+}^{n}:=\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}/\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}^{n+1}
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{P}_{X/k,+}^{1}\cong\Omega_{X/k}
.
Now, let’s define the module of principal parts of order
n
for a
k
-algebra. When
X=\operatorname{Spec}R
for a
k
-algebra
R
, the construction is as follows. Consider the multiplication map

\varphi:R\otimes R\longrightarrow R,\quad a\otimes b\longmapsto ab.

Then
\mathcal{P}_{X/k}^{n}
corresponds to

P_{R/k}^{n}:={R\otimes R}/{I_{R}^{n+1}}.

where
I_{R}:=\text{ker}(\varphi)
. Similarly, define the
R
-module
P_{R/k,+}^{n}:={I_{R}}/{I_{R}^{n+1}}
and, we have the following isomorphism

P_{R/k}^{n}\cong R\oplus P_{R/k,+}^{n}.

Notice that when
n=1
,
P_{R/k,+}^{1}\cong\Omega_{R/k}
. Consider the canonical derivation of order
n

d_{R}^{n}:R\longrightarrow P_{R/k,+}^{n},\quad x\longmapsto(1\otimes x-x\otimes 1)+I_{R}^{n+1}.

The
R
-module
P_{R/k,+}^{n}
is generated by the image of the map
d_{R}^{n}
.

To lighten the notation, we will write
\mathcal{P}_{X}^{n}=\mathcal{P}_{X/k}^{n}
, and
P_{R,+}^{n}=P_{R,+}^{n}
when the base field is understood from the context. Similarly, we will write
d^{n}=d_{R}^{n}
when
R
is clear from the context. We now discuss a fact about canonical derivation, and the functoriality of the module of principal parts.

Remark 2.2
.

[
1
]

Let
x,y\in R
, then a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle{d^{n}}(xy)={d^{n}}(x){d^{n}}(y)+y{d^{n}}(x)+x{d^{n}}(y).

Remark 2.3
.

Let
R
and
S
be
k
-algebras and
f:R\longrightarrow S
be a
k
-algebra homomorphism. We see
S
as a
R
-module, then we have the following
R
-module homomorphisms

\displaystyle P_{R}^{n}\longrightarrow P_{S}^{n}

and

\displaystyle P_{R,+}^{n}\longrightarrow P_{S,+}^{n}.

Similarly, we have the following
S
-module homomorphisms

\displaystyle P_{R}^{n}\otimes_{R}S\longrightarrow P_{S}^{n}

and

\displaystyle P_{R,+}^{n}\otimes_{R}S\longrightarrow P_{S,+}^{n}.

Higher Nash blow-ups

We will now define the notion of higher Nash blow-up which is a higher version of the Nash blow-up of a variety.

Definition 2.4
.

(Hilbert scheme of points)
Let
X
be a variety over
k
. The Hilbert scheme of points on
X
is defined as

\mathrm{Hilb}_{l}(X)=\{[Y]\mid Y\subset X,\ \dim(Y)=0\ \text{and}\ \text{length}(Y)=l\}.

where length(
Y)=\sum_{y\in Y}{\dim_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{Y,y})}
.

Definition 2.5
.

[
8
]

Let
X
be a variety of dimension
d
over
k
. For a point
x\in X
, the
n
-th infinitesimal neighborhood of
x
is
x^{(n)}:=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/m_{x}^{n+1}
. If
x
is a smooth point, then
[x^{(n)}]\in\mathrm{Hilb}_{\binom{n+d}{d}}(X)
. This gives us a morphism

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}:X_{\operatorname{sm}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hilb}_{\binom{n+d}{d}}(X),\quad x\longmapsto[x^{(n)}].

The Nash blow-up of order
n
of
X
, denoted

\mathrm{Nash}_{n}(X)
, is defined to be the closure of the graph

\Gamma_{\sigma_{n}}
inside
X\times_{k}\mathrm{Hilb}_{\binom{n+d}{d}}(X)
. It comes equipped with the natural projection

\displaystyle\pi_{n}:\mathrm{Nash}_{n}(X)\longrightarrow X

Higher Nash blow-ups and modules of principal parts are related via the following classical construction.

Definition 2.6
.

[
5
]

Let
X
be reduced Noetherian scheme and
\mathcal{M}
a coherent

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module locally free of constant rank
r
on an open dense
subscheme
U\subseteq X
. The Nash blowup of
X
associated
to
\mathcal{M}
, denoted
\mathrm{Nash}(X,\mathcal{M})
, is defined to be the closure of the graph of the natural map
U\longrightarrow\mathrm{Grass}_{r}(\mathcal{M})
.

The next result relates the above two definitions.

Proposition 2.7
.

[
8
]

Let
X
be a variety of dimension
d
, then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathrm{Nash}_{n}(X)\cong\mathrm{Nash}(X,P_{X}^{n})\cong\mathrm{Nash}(X,P_{X,+}^{n})

Notice that when
n=1
,
\mathrm{Nash}_{1}(X)
is isomorphic to the classical Nash blow-up of
X
.

Definition 2.8

(Torsion submodule)
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and
M
be a
R
-module. We define torsion submodule of
M
, denoted
\mathrm{tors}_{R}(M)
, as

\displaystyle\mathrm{tors}_{R}(M)=\{m\in M\mid\exists\ \text{a non-zero divisor}\ r\in R\ \text{such that}\ rm=0\}.

From now on, we will denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}=M/\mathrm{tors}_{R}(M).

This definition extend naturally to the global case. For a quasi-coherent module
\mathcal{M}
on
X
, we write

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{M}/\mathrm{tors}_{R}(\mathcal{M}).

We now give the universal property of higher Nash blow-up.

Proposition 2.9
.

[
8
,
7
]

Let
X
be a variety of dimension
d
over
k
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and we have the natural projection map

\displaystyle\pi_{n}:\mathrm{Nash}_{n}(X)\longrightarrow X.

Suppose we also have a morphism
f:Y\longrightarrow X
such that
\widetilde{f^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{X,+}^{n})}
is locally free, then there exists a unique morphism
g:Y\longrightarrow X
such that
\pi_{n}\circ g=f
. Equivalently, one can use
\widetilde{f^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{X,+}^{n})}
instead of
f^{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{X,+}^{n}})
.

3.
Reducing higher Nobile’s theorem to classical Nobile’s theorem

We will use the following setup for the rest of this paper.

Setup 3.1
.

We use the following notations.

•

We fix an affine algebraic vaiety
X\subset\mathbb{A}^{\text{s}}
of dimension
d
over the base field
k
.

•

We write
\mathbb{A}^{\text{s}}=\operatorname{Spec}S
and
X=\operatorname{Spec}B
where
B=S/I
.

•

We fix a closed point
x\in X
, and set
R:=B_{m_{x}}=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
where
m_{x}
is the maximal ideal corresponding to
x
.

•

Let
K
be the field of fractions of
R
.

To prove the generalized Nobile’s theorem we need a key property. We define this property as follows:

Definition 3.2

(
\mathrm{HN}_{n}
)
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra,
m_{R}
be a maximal ideal and
\dim R=d
. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
R
satisfies the
\text{HN}_{n}
property if there exists algebraically independent elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\in m_{R}
, corresponding to a ring homomorphism
A:=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\longrightarrow R
, such that the induced map

\displaystyle P_{A,+}^{n}\otimes_{A}R/m_{R}\longrightarrow\widetilde{P}_{R,+}^{n}\otimes_{R}R/m_{R}

is injective. Notice that this is equivalent to saying that

\displaystyle P_{A}^{n}\otimes_{A}R/m_{R}\longrightarrow\widetilde{P}_{R}^{n}\otimes_{R}R/m_{R}

is injective.

We now see how higher Nobile’s theorem reduces down to the classical Nobile’s theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be an affine algebraic variety, and
R
be as in Setup
3.1
. Suppose
R
satisfies the
Definition 3.2

(
\mathrm{HN}_{n}
)
.
property, and
\mathrm{Nash}_{n}(X)\cong X
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\mathrm{Nash}_{1}(X)\cong X

Proof.

We assume
\mathrm{Nash}_{n}(X)\cong X
. Then by the definition of
n
-th Nash blow-up we have that
\widetilde{\pi_{n}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{X,+}^{n})}
is locally free of rank
D-1:=\binom{n+d}{d}-1
. But
\pi_{n}
is an isomorphism, so
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{X,+}^{n}
is locally free of rank
D-1
which implies
\widetilde{P}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x},+}^{n}
is free of rank
D-1
for all
x\in X
. Let
R:=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
. By Theorem
6.1
, we have
A=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
such that

\displaystyle{P_{A}^{n}}\otimes_{A}R/m_{R}\longrightarrow{\widetilde{P}_{R}^{n}}\otimes_{R}R/m_{R},

is injective. It is equivalent to say that

\displaystyle{P_{A,+}^{n}}\otimes_{A}R/m_{R}\longrightarrow{\widetilde{P}_{R,+}^{n}}\otimes_{R}R/m_{R},

is injective. But
\dim(P_{A,+}^{n}\otimes_{A}R/m_{R})=\dim(\widetilde{P}_{R,+}^{n}\otimes_{R}R/m_{R})=D-1
, and so the above map is an isomorphism. Thus,
\widetilde{P}_{R,+}^{n}\otimes_{R}R/m_{R}
is generated by
\big\{[{\overline{{d^{n}}({x_{1}})}]^{\alpha_{1}}}\cdots[{\overline{{d^{n}}({x_{d}})}]^{\alpha_{d}}}\mid 1\leq|\alpha|\leq n\big\}
as
R/m_{R}
vector space. Consider the surjective map

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\widetilde{P}_{R}^{n}\otimes_{R}R/m_{R}\cong\frac{\widetilde{P}_{R}^{n}}{m_{R}\widetilde{P}_{R}^{n}}.

Further, it follows from Nakayama’s lemma that the
R
-module
\widetilde{P}_{R,+}^{n}
is freely generated by
\big\{{\overline{{d^{n}}({x_{1}})}^{\alpha_{1}}}\cdots{\overline{{d^{n}}({x_{d}})}^{\alpha_{d}}}\mid 1\leq|\alpha|\leq n\big\}
. Furthermore, we have a surjective
R
-module homomorphism

\displaystyle P_{R,+}^{n}=I_{R}/I_{R}^{n+1}\longrightarrow\Omega_{R}=I_{R}/I_{R}^{2}.

This induces the following surjective map

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{R,+}^{n}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\Omega}_{R}.

It follows that the
R
-module
\widetilde{\Omega}_{R}
is generated by
\big\{\overline{d({x_{1}})},\cdots,\overline{d({x_{d}})}\big\}
. But
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde{\Omega}_{R})=\dim R=d
. Thus,
\widetilde{\Omega}_{R}=\Omega_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}/\text{tors}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}(\Omega_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}})
is free of rank
d
. This is equivalent to
\widetilde{\Omega}_{X}
being locally free of rank
d
. So, by the universal property
2.9
of Nash blow-up, there exists a unique map
\varphi:X\longrightarrow\mathrm{Nash}_{1}(X)
. Thus,
\pi:\text{Nash}_{1}(X)\longrightarrow X
is an isomorphism.
∎

4.
Arc lemma

In this section, we construct an arc and observe some of its important properties.

Remark 4.1
.

We can assume
x\in X
the origin of
\mathbb{A^{\text{s}}}
. Now we consider linear projections of the form
\mathbb{A^{\text{s}}}\longrightarrow Y
, where
Y
is an affine subspace of
\mathbb{A^{\text{s}}}
of dimension
d
containing

. For each such projection we can choose affine coordinates

in
\mathbb{A^{\text{s}}}
centered at

and the projection is given by
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})\in\mathbb{A^{\text{s}}}\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in Y
(notice that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
give coordinates in

). Let

be the subspace of
\mathbb{A^{\text{s}}}
given by
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}=0
. This

is a finite integral extension. Then the proof of the Noether normalization lemma shows that the subset

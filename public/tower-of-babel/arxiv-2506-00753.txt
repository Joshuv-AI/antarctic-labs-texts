Abstract
. We determine all groups which occur as torsion subgroups of ℚ \mathbb{Q} -curves defined over number fields of degrees 3 3 , 5 5 and 7 7 . In particular, we prove that every torsion subgroup of a ℚ \mathbb{Q} -curve defined over a number field of degree 3 , 5 3,5 or 7 7 already occurs as a torsion subgroup of an elliptic curve with rational j j -invariant. As the quadratic case has been solved by Le Fourn and Najman, and the case of extensions of prime degree greater than 7 7 has been solved by Cremona and Najman, this paper completes the classification of torsion of ℚ \mathbb{Q} -curves over number fields of prime degree. To solve our problem, we describe how images of Galois representations of elliptic curves change upon applying an isogeny. Since Cremona and Najman proved that ℚ \mathbb{Q} -curves over an odd degree number field K K are exactly those which are K K -isogenous to an elliptic curve with rational j j -invariant, we can rely on known results about images of Galois representations of rational elliptic curves. As a corollary of our results, we establish that the torsion subgroup an elliptic curve over a number field K K of prime degree which is isogenous to an elliptic curve with rational j j -invariant is equal to the torsion subgroup of some elliptic curve defined over a degree p p number field with rational j j -invariant.



1.
Introduction

Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve, where
K
is a number field. By the Mordell-Weil theorem, the group
E(K)
is finitely generated and hence of the form
T\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}
for some
r\geq 0
, where
T
is a finite Abelian group.

A typical question one might ask is to determine all possible torsion subgroups occuring in a given class of elliptic curves. For elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
, we have the following famous result by Mazur.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
15
]

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. Then
E(\mathbb{Q})_{tors}
is isomorphic to one of the following
15
groups:

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/N_{1}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\text{ with }N_{1}=1,\ldots,10,12,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2N_{2}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\text{ with }N_{2}=1,\ldots,4.

There exist infinitely many
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-isomorphism classes for each such torsion subgroup.

An elliptic curve
E
over a number field
K
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-curve
if it is isogenous over
\overline{K}
to all its Galois conjugates.

For example, all elliptic curves isogenous to an elliptic curve with rational
j
-invariant are
\mathbb{Q}
-curves. Indeed, if
E
is isogenous to an elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
with rational
j
-invariant, then there is a twist
E^{\prime\prime}
of
E^{\prime}
which is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and an isogeny
\varphi:E\to E^{\prime\prime}
. Then there is also an isogeny
\varphi^{\sigma}:E^{\sigma}\to(E^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma}
, but
(E^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma}=E^{\prime\prime}
, and the composition of
\varphi
and the dual of
\varphi^{\sigma}
is an isogeny from
E
to
E^{\sigma}
.

Over number fields of odd degree, Cremona and Najman have proven that all
\mathbb{Q}
-curves arise in this way, with the isogeny even being defined over the base field of the
\mathbb{Q}
-curve.

Proposition 1.2
.

[
4
, Theorem 2.7.]

If
E
is a non-CM
\mathbb{Q}
-curve defined over an odd degree number field
K
, then
E
is
K
-isogenous to an elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
with rational
j
-invariant.

Remark 1.3
.

As any isogeny is a composition of a cyclic isogeny and multiplication by
m
for some integer
m
, we may assume that the isogeny from the above proposition is cyclic.

The goal of this article is to find all possible groups which arise as torsion subgroups of
\mathbb{Q}
-curves over cubic, quintic and septic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
.

The classification of torsion of base changes of rational elliptic curves, and more generally of elliptic curves with rational
j
-invariant, is known for cubic, quintic and septic fields.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
16
, Theorem 1.]

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a rational elliptic curve, and let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a cubic extension. Then
E(K)_{tors}
is isomorphic to one of the following
20
groups:

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/N_{1}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\text{ with }N_{1}=1,\ldots,10,12,13,14,18,21,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2N_{2}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\text{ with }N_{2}=1,2,3,4,7.

There exist infinitely many
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-isomorphism classes for each such torsion subgroup except for
\mathbb{Z}/21\mathbb{Z}
, for which there is only one elliptic curve with this torsion, which is defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{9})^{+}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

[
6
, Theorem 1]

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a rational elliptic curve, and let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a quintic extension. Then
E(K)_{tors}
is isomorphic to one of the following
17
groups:

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/N_{1}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\text{ with }N_{1}=1,\ldots,10,11,12,25

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2N_{2}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\text{ with }N_{2}=1,2,3,4.

There exist infinitely many
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-isomorphism classes for each such torsion subgroup except for
\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}
, for which there are three elliptic curves with this torsion over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{11})^{+}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
7
, follows from Proposition 7.7]

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a rational elliptic curve, and let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a septic extension. Then
E(K)_{tors}
is isomorphic to one of the
15
groups from
Theorem

1.1
.

Remark 1.7
.

In fact, Gužvić proved in
[

that elliptic curves with rational
j
-invariant over an odd prime degree number field have the same list of possible torsion subgroups as base changes of rational elliptic curves.

Over quadratic number fields, there is one exception. Namely, the group
\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z}
appears among torsion subgroups of elliptic curves with rational
j
-invariant, and does not appear among torsion subgroups of rational elliptic curves.

Corollary 1.12
.

Let

be an odd prime number. If a group
T
appears as the torsion subgroup of some

-curve over a number field of degree

, then
T
also appears as the torsion subgroup of some elliptic curve over a number field of degree

-invariant.

The above corollary is false for

. However, a similar statement holds if we replace the class of
\mathbb{Q}
-curves by the class of elliptic curves which are isogenous to an elliptic curve with rational

-invariant.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
p
be a prime number. If a group
T
appears as the torsion subgroup of some elliptic curve over a number field of degree

which is
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-isogenous to an elliptic curve with rational

-invariant, then

also appears as the torsion subgroup of some elliptic curve over a number field of degree

with rational

-invariant.

Remark 1.14
.

Under our definition, CM elliptic curves are also
\mathbb{Q}
-curves. Some authors exclude CM curves from the definition of
\mathbb{Q}
-curves, but we will not do that. In any case, the main results of this paper stay the same regardless of the chosen convention. In
[
3
]
, Clark, Corn, Rice and Stankewicz classified torsion of CM elliptic curves over number fields of degree at most

. They proved that groups which occur as torsion subgroups of CM curves over cubic fields are those from

\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}/14\mathbb{Z}
. Over quintic fields, the only new group which appears is

. Over septic fields, no new groups appear. In the rest of this article, we only focus on non-CM elliptic curves.

Remark 1.15
.

The paper is structured as follows. In

In

We do this case by case, eliminating each group which occurs for elliptic curves over cubic extensions and does not occur for rational elliptic curves over cubic extensions. 2.
General results

In this section, we recall some helpful results which will be used throughout the paper.

It is a standard fact that every non-CM elliptic curve with rational
j
-invariant is a quadratic twist of an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. The following well-known result describes Galois representations of quadratic twists.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
K
. Let
E^{\prime}
be a quadratic twist of
E
by
d\in K^{\times}
. Let
\psi:G_{K}\to\{-1,1\}
be the quadratic character defined by
\sigma(\sqrt{d})=\psi(\sqrt{d})\sqrt{d}
. Denote by
\rho_{E,N}
and
\rho_{E^{\prime},N}
the mod
N
Galois representations of
E
and
E^{\prime}
respectively. Then, for an appropriate choice of bases of
E[N]
and
E^{\prime}[N]
, we have

\rho_{E,N}(\sigma)=\psi(\sigma)\rho_{E^{\prime}N}(\sigma).

The proof can be found in
[
19
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Since
\mathbb{Q}
-curves are isogenous to elliptic curves with rational
j
-invariants, it is very useful to describe what happens to the image of Galois representations after applying isogenies.

We start with the following lemma which describes what happens with a point of prime order on the starting elliptic curve.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
2
, Lemma 4.2.]

Let
E_{1}/F
be an elliptic curve with a point
P\in E_{1}(F)
of prime order
p
and let
\varphi:E_{1}\to E_{2}
be an isogeny defined over
F
. Then either
E_{2}
has a point of order
p
or for some basis of
E_{2}[p]

\rho_{E_{2},p}(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}\chi_{p}(\sigma)&y\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\ \text{for all }\sigma\in G_{F}.

To avoid referring to bases, we may rephrase
Lemma

2.2
as follows.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
E_{1}/F
be an elliptic curve with a point
P\in E_{1}(F)
of prime order
p
and let
\varphi:E_{1}\to E_{2}
be an isogeny defined over
F
. Then every
\alpha\in\rho_{E_{2},p}(G_{F})
has eigenvalue
1
.

Combining
Lemma

2.1
and
Lemma

2.2
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
E
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-curve over an odd degree number field
K
with a point of order
p
defined over
K
, where
p
is a prime number. Let
\phi:E\to E^{\prime}
be an isogeny, where
E^{\prime}
is a curve with rational
j
-invariant, and let
E^{\prime\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
be a quadratic twist of
E^{\prime}.
Then
E^{\prime\prime}
has a
p
-isogeny defined over
K
and any element of
\rho_{E^{\prime\prime},p}(G_{K})
has eigenvalue
1
or
-1
.

The following proposition, which strengthens
[
2
, Proposition 4.4.]
, describes what happens in the composite case.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
E_{1}/F
be an elliptic curve with a point
P
of order
N
such that
\langle P\rangle
is fixed by
G_{F}
. Denote by
H\leq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
the subgroup
\{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\mid\sigma(P)=kP\text{ for some }\sigma\in G_{F}\}
.

Let
\phi:E_{1}\to E_{2}
be a cyclic isogeny defined over
F
. Let
\alpha
be the order of
\phi(P)
in
E_{2}
. Then the image of the mod
N
representation
\rho_{E_{2},N}(G_{F})
is conjugate to a subgroup of the group of all matrices of the form

\begin{bmatrix}h&\frac{N}{\alpha}u\\
\alpha v&z\end{bmatrix},

where
h\in H
.

Proof.

Let
\{P,Q\}
be a basis for
E_{1}[N]
. Denote by
\alpha
and
\beta
respectively the order of
\phi(P)
and
\phi(Q)
. Let
R
be a generator of
E_{1}[N]\cap\ker\phi
. Then
\alpha P=xR
and
\beta Q=yR
for some
x,y
. This means that
\alpha\cdot\frac{y}{\gcd(x,y)}P=\beta\cdot\frac{x}{\gcd(x,y)}Q
, so
N
divides both
\alpha\cdot\frac{y}{\gcd(x,y)}
and
\beta\cdot\frac{x}{\gcd(x,y)}
. From here, it follows that
N\mid\lcm(\alpha,\beta)
. Namely, assume that
p^{k}
divides
N
and doesn’t divide
\alpha
nor
\beta
. Then
p
divides both
\frac{y}{\gcd(x,y)}
and
\frac{x}{\gcd(x,y)}
, a contradiction.

Now let
\{R,S\}
be a basis for
E_{2}[N]
, chosen so that
\frac{N}{\alpha}R=\phi(P)
and
\frac{N}{\beta}S=\phi(Q)
. We now compute the action of
\sigma\in G_{F}
on both elements of this basis.

We know that

\frac{N}{\alpha}\sigma(R)=\sigma(\phi(P))=\phi(\sigma(P))=h\phi(P)=h\frac{N}{\alpha}R

for some
h\in H
, so

\sigma(R)=hR+\alpha(mR+nS)=(h+m\alpha)R+n\alpha S

for some coefficients
m,n
.

Furthermore,
\frac{N}{\beta}\sigma(S)=\sigma(\phi(Q))=\phi(\sigma(Q))
. If we set
\sigma(Q)=uP+h^{-1}\chi_{N}(\sigma)Q
, we get

\frac{N}{\beta}\sigma(S)=u\phi(P)+h^{-1}\chi_{N}(\sigma)\phi(Q)=\frac{uN}{\alpha}R+h^{-1}\frac{\chi_{N}(\sigma)N}{\beta}S.

Since the left hand side belongs to
E_{1}[\beta]
, so does the right hand side. Thus,
\frac{N}{\beta}\mid\frac{uN}{\alpha}
, or equivalently
\alpha\mid u\beta
. Furthermore, we have

\sigma(S)=\frac{u\beta}{\alpha}R+h^{-1}\chi_{N}(\sigma)S+\beta(xR+yS)=\left(\frac{u\beta}{\alpha}+x\beta\right)R+(\chi_{N}(\sigma)+y\beta)S

for some coefficients
x,y
.

Written as a matrix, we have

\rho_{E_{2},N}(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}h+m\alpha&\frac{u\beta}{\alpha}+x\beta\\
n\alpha&h^{-1}\chi_{N}(\sigma)+y\beta\end{bmatrix}.

Taking the determinant (viewed in
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
), we obtain

\chi_{N}(\sigma)=\chi_{N}(\sigma)+hy\beta+h^{-1}m\alpha\chi_{N}(\sigma)+my\alpha\beta-nu\beta.

However,
u\beta
is divisible by both
\alpha
and
\beta
, so
u\beta=0
as
\lcm(\alpha,\beta)=N
. Similarly,
my\alpha\beta=0
. Simplifying the equation with respect to these conclusions, we obtain

hy\beta+h^{-1}m\alpha\chi_{N}(\sigma)=0.

From here, we see that
\beta
and
\alpha
both divide
m\alpha
, so
m\alpha=0
, and
y\beta=0
follows. In conclusion, we have

\rho_{E_{2},N}(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}h&\frac{u\beta}{\alpha}+x\beta\\
n\alpha&h^{-1}\chi_{N}(\sigma)\end{bmatrix}.

Finally, since
u\beta
and
\alpha x\beta
are both divisible by
N
, we see that
\frac{u\beta}{\alpha}
and
x\beta
are both divisible by
\frac{N}{\alpha}
, so the top right entry is always divisible by
\frac{N}{\alpha}
, which proves the lemma.

\Box

In other words, if
E_{1}
has an
N
-isogeny over
K
for some
N
and we apply some other cyclic isogeny
\phi:E_{1}\to E_{2}
, the
N
-isogeny turns into an
\alpha
-isogeny and an
\frac{N}{\alpha}
-isogeny on
E_{2}
, and those two are independent.

We also spell out the special case when
E_{1}
has a point of order
N
over
K
, not just an
N
-isogeny.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
E_{1}/F
be an elliptic curve with a point
P
of order
N
defined over
F
. Let
\phi:E_{1}\to E_{2}
be a cyclic isogeny defined over
F
. Denote by
\alpha
the order of
\phi(P)
in
E_{2}
. Then the image of the mod
N
Galois representation
\rho_{E_{1},N}(G_{F})
is conjugate to a subgroup of the group of all matrices of the form

\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac{N}{\alpha}u\\
\alpha v&z\end{bmatrix}.

The following result is well-known, but we reprove it for the sake of completion.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
K
be a number field, and let
M
and
N
be positive integers. Suppose that for some elliptic curve
E/K
, there are generators
P
and
Q
of
E[M^{2}N]
such that
[M]P
and
[MN]Q
lie in
K
. Then
E
is
K
-isogenous to an elliptic curve
E_{1}/K
such that
\rho_{E_{1},M^{2}N}(G_{K})
is conjugate to a subgroup of the group of matrices of the form

\begin{bmatrix}1+MN\cdot k&*\\
0&*\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

Take an
M
-isogeny
\phi:E\to E_{1}
whose kernel is
[MN]Q
. Take a basis of the form
\{\phi(P),S\}
of
E_{1}[M^{2}N]
. In this basis,
\rho_{E_{1},M^{2}N}(G_{K})
has the required shape.

\Box

3.
Torsion over cubic number fields

The list of all possible torsion subgroups of eliptic curves over cubic fields is known.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
5
, Theorem A]

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a cubic extension and
E/K
be an elliptic curve. Then
E(K)_{tors}
is isomorphic to one of the following
26
groups:

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/N_{1}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\text{ with }N_{1}=1,\ldots,16,18,20,21,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2N_{2}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\text{ with }N_{2}=1,\ldots,7.

There exist infinitely many
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-isomorphism classes for each such torsion subgroup except for
\mathbb{Z}/21\mathbb{Z}
, for which there is only one elliptic curve with this torsion, which is defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{9})^{+}
.

To prove
Theorem

1.8
, it remains to prove that none of the following six groups appears as torsion of a
\mathbb{Q}
-curve over a cubic field.

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/N_{1}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\text{ with }N_{1}=11,15,16,20

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2N_{2}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\text{ with }N_{2}=5,6.

3.1.
Eliminating
\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}

Proposition 3.2
.

The group
\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}
does not occur as a torsion subgroup of a
\mathbb{Q}
-curve over a cubic number field.

Proof.

Suppose that some
\mathbb{Q}
-curve
E
over a cubic field
K
has a point
P
of order
11
.
We have the following situation:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E/K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}/K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime\prime}/\mathbb{Q}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Here
E
is our starting
\mathbb{Q}
-curve,
\phi
is a cyclic isogeny defined over
K
,
E^{\prime}/K
is an eliptic curve with rational
j
-invariant,
E^{\prime\prime}
is an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\sim
is an isomorphism from
E^{\prime}
to
E^{\prime\prime}
, i.e.
E^{\prime\prime}
is a quadratic twist of
E^{\prime}
.

By Corollary
2.4
, we know that
E^{\prime\prime}
has an
11
-isogeny over
K
. However, by Proposition
2.8
,
E^{\prime\prime}
also has an
11
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
.

There are two rational
j
-invariants for which the corresponding elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
has an
11
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
. For a fixed
j
-invariant, any two rational elliptic curves with this
j
-invariant are quadratic twists. This means that we can pick any rational elliptic curve with this
j
-invariant and check that its Galois image doesn’t have the properties it would need to have by Corollary
2.4
.

The
j
-invariants for which there is an
11
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
are
-11^{2}
and
-11\cdot 131^{3}
. Examples of elliptic curves with these
j
-invariant are 1089.c1 and 1089.c2 from LMFDB.

For
E^{\prime\prime}
being any of these curves, the image of the mod 11 Galois representation
\rho_{E^{\prime\prime},11}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
has
220
elements. Since
3\nmid 220
, we have
\rho_{E^{\prime\prime},11}(G_{K})=\rho_{E^{\prime\prime},11}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})

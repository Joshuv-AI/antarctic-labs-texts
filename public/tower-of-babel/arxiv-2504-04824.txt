Abstract
. We establish the optimal regularity of viscosity solutions to u t − x n γ ​ Δ ​ u = f , u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u=f, which arises in the regularity theory for the porous medium equation. Specifically, we prove that under the zero Dirichlet boundary condition on { x n = 0 } \{x_{n}=0\} , the optimal regularity of u u up to the flat boundary { x n = 0 } \{x_{n}=0\} is C 1 , 1 − γ C^{1,1-\gamma} . Moreover, for the homogeneous equations, we establish that the optimal regularity of u u is C 2 , 1 − γ C^{2,1-\gamma} in the spatial variables, and that x n − γ ​ u x_{n}^{-\gamma}u is smooth in the variables x ′ x^{\prime} and t t .

1.
Introduction

The main goal of this paper is to derive the optimal regularity of viscosity solutions to the following degenerate parabolic equations:

(1.1)

u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u=f,

where
0<\gamma<1
and
f
is bounded and continuous.

Equation (
1.1
) is closely related to the following Cauchy–Dirichlet problem for the porous medium equation:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} v_{t}-\Delta v^{m}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
v&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
v&=v_{0}&&\text{on }\Omega\times\{t=0\},\end{aligned}\right.

where
m>1
,
v_{0}>0
in
\Omega
, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain with a smooth boundary. By applying appropriate transformations such as the hodograph transform, the change of variables that flattens
\partial\Omega
, and linearization, the problem (
1.2
) is transformed into the following Dirichlet problem:

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u&=f&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\coloneqq Q_{1}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}\\
u&=0&&\text{on }S_{1}\coloneqq Q_{1}\cap\{x_{n}=0\},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\gamma=1-1/m\in(0,1)
. The regularity results for the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) can be applied to the Cauchy–Dirichlet problem (
1.2
) for the porous medium equation in bounded domains, we refer to
[
19
,
20
]
. For the unbounded domain, specifically
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we refer to
[
6
]
.

1.1.
Main results

The global
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
) was recently established by Lee and Yun
[
25
, Corollary 1.3]
. Here, the exact value of
\alpha
is unknown, but it cannot exceed
1-\gamma
.
To be precise, a direct computation shows that for any linear function
l(x)
,

\varphi(x,t)=l(x)+2t+\frac{x_{n}^{2-\gamma}}{(2-\gamma)(1-\gamma)}

is a solution to

u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u=1.

Therefore, solutions to (
1.1
) cannot be expected to be more regular than
C^{1,1-\gamma}
up to the flat boundary
S_{1}
. The essence of our main results is that, under sufficiently smooth boundary data, the simple solution
\varphi
actually represents a worst-case scenario in terms of boundary regularity.

The boundary regularity of solutions to (
1.1
) is influenced by factors such as the geometry of the boundary and the Dirichlet boundary condition. Since the flat boundary
S_{1}
is smooth, it does not present a significant obstacle to attaining optimal regularity of solutions to (
1.1
). However, the boundary regularity of solutions cannot exceed that of the boundary data, making the boundary condition a critical factor in determining the optimal regularity.

Our first main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
f\in C(Q_{1}^{+})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})
and
g\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
. Let
u
be a viscosity solution to

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u&=f&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u&=g&&\text{on }S_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then
u\in C^{1,\bar{\alpha}}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})
with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{1,\bar{\alpha}}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})}),

where
\bar{\alpha}=\min\{\beta,1-\gamma\}
and
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\beta
and
\gamma
.

Remark 1.2
.

If the Dirichlet boundary data
g
is sufficiently smooth, for example,
g\in C^{1,1-\gamma}(\overline{S_{1}})
, then the optimal regularity of viscosity solutions to (
1.4
) is
C^{1,1-\gamma}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose
f\in C(Q_{1}^{+})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})
and let
u
be a viscosity solution to (
1.3
). Then

u\in C^{1,1-\gamma}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})
with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{1,1-\gamma}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
and
\gamma
.

In the case of homogeneous equations, higher regularity can be expected. The global
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions to

(1.5)

u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u=0\quad\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}

was recently proved by Lee and Yun
[
25
, Theorem 1.7]
. A direct calculation shows that, for any linear function
l(x)
,

\psi(x,t)=l(x)+tx_{n}+\frac{x_{n}^{3-\gamma}}{(3-\gamma)(2-\gamma)}

is a solution to (
1.5
). Therefore, solutions to (
1.5
) cannot be expected to possess more than
C^{2,1-\gamma}
up to the flat boundary
\{x_{n}=0\}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Let
u\in C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
be a solution to (
1.5
) with boundary data
u=g
on
S_{1}
. Note that

|u_{t}(x,t)|\leq x_{n}^{\gamma}|\Delta u(x,t)|\leq\|u\|_{C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})}x_{n}^{\gamma}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}},

which implies that

(1.6)

g_{t}=0\quad\text{on }S_{1}.

Hence, condition (
1.6
) is a sufficient condition for the existence of a
C^{2}
-solution.

Our second main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
\beta\in(0,1)
and
g\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})
satisfies (
1.6
). Let
u
be a viscosity solution to

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u&=0&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u&=g&&\text{on }S_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then

u(\cdot,t)\in C^{2,\bar{\alpha}}(\overline{B_{1/2}^{+}})\quad\text{for all }t\in[-1/4,0]\qquad\text{and}\qquad x_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}\in C^{1-\gamma}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})

with the uniform estimates

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{2,\bar{\alpha}}(\overline{B_{1/2}^{+}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})})

and

\|x_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}\|_{C^{1-\gamma}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})}),

where
\bar{\alpha}=\min\{\beta,1-\gamma\}
and
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\beta
and
\gamma
.

By setting
u=m^{1/\gamma}v^{m}
, the Cauchy–Dirichlet problems (
1.2
) can be reformulated as follows:

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-u^{\gamma}\Delta u&=0&&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
u&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
u&=u_{0}&&\text{on }\Omega\times\{t=0\},\end{aligned}\right.

where
u_{0}\coloneqq m^{1/\gamma}v_{0}^{m}
. The optimal regularity of solutions to (
1.8
) was recently established as
C^{2,1-\gamma}
by Jin, Ros-Oton, and Xiong
[
17
]
in the sense described below:

(1.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u(\cdot,t)&\in C^{2,1-\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})&&\text{for all }t>T^{*}\\
u(x,\cdot)&\in C^{\infty}(T^{*},\infty)&&\text{uniformly in }x\in\overline{\Omega},\end{aligned}\right.

where the time
T^{*}>0
satisfying

(1.10)

u(\cdot,t)\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})\quad\text{for all }t>T^{*}.

When
\Omega=B_{1}^{+}
, the global Lipschitz regularity (
1.10
) can be interpreted as

u\approx x_{n}\quad\text{near }S_{1}.

Consequently, as in the
[
29
, Section 4]
, the operator in (
1.1
) can be understood as follows:

(1.11)

u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u\approx u_{t}-u^{\gamma}\Delta u\quad\text{near }S_{1}.

Based on the approximation (
1.11
), it is reasonable to expect that the optimal regularity results in (
1.9
) may also hold for (
1.1
).

The following corollary, in the case
\kappa=0
, is analogous to the optimal regularity results in
[
17
]
, and due to the structure of the equation, it indicates that all higher-order tangential derivatives, excluding those in the
x_{n}
-direction, are smooth.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
u
be a viscosity solution to

(1.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u&=0&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u&=0&&\text{on }S_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then

D_{(x^{\prime},t)}^{\kappa}u(\cdot,t)\in C^{2,1-\gamma}(\overline{B_{1/2}^{+}})\quad\text{for all $\kappa\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}$ and $t\in[-1/4,0]$}

with the uniform estimate

\|D_{(x^{\prime},t)}^{\kappa}u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{2,1-\gamma}(\overline{B_{1/2}^{+}})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
and
\gamma
.

Moreover,

x_{n}^{-\gamma}D_{(x^{\prime},t)}^{\kappa}u\in C^{1-\gamma}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})\quad\text{for all $\kappa\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}$}

with the uniform estimate

\|x_{n}^{-\gamma}D_{(x^{\prime},t)}^{\kappa}u\|_{C^{1-\gamma}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}.

1.2.
Related works

Equations exhibiting degeneracy on
S_{1}
arise in various applied fields, including the porous medium equation, Gauss curvature flow, the Monge–Ampère equation, nonlocal equations, and financial mathematics. For instance, the porous medium equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\infty)
can be linearized as a degenerate parabolic equation

(1.13)

u_{t}-x_{2}(u_{x_{1}x_{1}}+u_{x_{2}x_{2}})-\nu u_{x_{2}}=f

with
\nu>0
. Using the smoothness of solutions to (
1.13
) in weighted Hölder space, Daskalopoulos and Hamilton
[
6
]
established the smoothness of both the solution and the free boundary of the porous medium equation.

For the porous medium equation in bounded domains, Kim and Lee
[
19
]
established regularity results by applying
C_{s}^{2+\alpha}
-estimates in weighted Hölder spaces for solutions to (
1.1
).
The results of
[
6
]
suggest that solutions to the porous medium equation in bounded domains may also be smooth in weighted Hölder spaces.
However, there are limitations to analyzing the higher regularity of solutions using these weighted Hölder spaces. This conjecture was completely resolved by Kim, Lee, and Yun
[
20
]
. They established regularity results for solutions to the porous medium equation in bounded domains by proving that solutions to (
1.1
) admit infinite order fractional expansions by using the generalized Schauder theory
[
22
]
for fractional order expansions.

Research on nonlinear equations using the technique of linearizing them into degenerate equations has been actively pursued over the past two decades. For the Gauss curvature flow, see
[
7
,
18
]
; for the Monge–Ampère equation, see
[
9
,
23
]
; for nonlocal equations, see
[
5
]
; and for financial mathematics, see
[
21
]
.

Alongside the study of nonlinear equations, the regularity theory for equations with degeneracy or singularity on
S_{1}
has advanced significantly over the past two decades. Daskalopoulos and Lee
[
8
]
established the Alexandroff–Bakelman–Pucci estimates, Harnack inequality, and the Hölder continuity of solutions to

u_{t}-a^{11}u_{x_{1}x_{1}}-x_{2}a^{22}u_{x_{2}x_{2}}-2\sqrt{x_{2}}a^{12}u_{x_{1}x_{2}}-b^{1}u_{x_{1}}-b^{2}u_{x_{2}}=f.

Within the framework of regularity theory utilizing weighted Hölder spaces
C_{s}^{k+\alpha}
associated with the Riemannian metric
ds
introduced in
[
6
]
, Song and Wang
[
28
]
established
C_{s}^{2+\alpha}
-estimates of solutions to

u_{x_{1}x_{1}}+x_{2}au_{x_{2}x_{2}}-bu_{x_{2}}=f.

Additionally, Feehan and Pop
[
15
,
16
]
proved
C_{s}^{2+\alpha}
-estimates of solutions to

u_{t}-x_{n}a^{ij}D_{ij}u-b^{i}D_{i}u-cu=f

and a priori
C_{s}^{k+2+\alpha}
-estimates of solutions to

x_{n}a^{ij}D_{ij}u+b^{i}D_{i}u-cu=f.

In the study of regularity theory based on the weighted Sobolev spaces, Dong and Phan
[
10
,
11
]
established
W^{1,p}
-estimates of solutions to

x_{n}^{\gamma}(u_{t}+\lambda u)-D_{i}[x_{n}^{\gamma}(a^{ij}D_{j}u-F_{i})]=\sqrt{\lambda}x_{n}^{\gamma}f.

Furthermore, Dong, Phan, and Tran
[
12
,
13
]
established
W^{1,p}
-estimates of solutions to

u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}D_{i}(a^{ij}D_{j}u)+\lambda cu=x_{n}^{\gamma}DF+f

and
W^{2,p}
-estimates of solutions to

u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u+\lambda u=x_{n}^{\gamma}f.

In the context of weighted Sobolev spaces with mixed norms, Dong and Ryu
[
14
]
proved
W^{1,p,q}
-estimates of solutions to

a_{0}u_{t}-x_{n}^{2}D_{i}(a^{ij}D_{j}u)+x_{n}b^{i}D_{i}u+x_{n}D_{i}(\tilde{b}^{i}u)+cu+\lambda c_{0}u=D_{i}F_{i}+f.

Kim, Lee, and Yun
[
22
]
introduced a new framework for analyzing the regularity of solutions to

u_{t}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}a^{ij}D_{ij}u-2x_{n}^{\gamma/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a^{in}D_{in}u-x_{n}^{\gamma}a^{nn}D_{nn}u-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b^{i}D_{i}u-x_{n}^{\gamma/2}b^{n}D_{n}u-cu=f

by employing fractional order expansions and developing a generalized Schauder theory to support this approach.

Over the past decade, active research on optimal regularity has employed the compactness argument. For the fully nonlinear equations, we refer to
[
1
,
2
]
, and for the
p
-Laplace equations, we refer to
[
3
,
4
]
.

In the case of optimal regularity of solutions to (
1.8
), the approach differs from those previously mentioned. Specifically, Jin, Ros-Oton, and Xiong
[
17
]
proved that

\frac{u}{\textnormal{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)}\text{ is globally H\"{o}lder continuous}

and by applying Schauder estimates, they obtained the optimal regularity results.

A key technique in this study is to interpret the parabolic equation as an elliptic one by leveraging the Hölder continuity of the time derivative. This approach can also be found in the study of the parabolic
p
-Laplace equation, we refer to
[
26
]
for details.

1.3.
Strategy of the proof

The proof of the optimal regularity is divided into two main parts: the inhomogeneous case and the homogeneous case. Each part relies on a combination of compactness arguments and refined regularity theory.

(i)

Optimal regularity for the inhomogeneous equations: The key idea is to consider a distance function that preserves the parabolic scaling on
S_{1}
. This distance function enables us to capture the correct behavior of the solution along the normal direction. The strategy proceeds as follows:

•

Introduce a distance function that preserves the parabolic scaling structure for (
1.1
).

•

We employ the boundary
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity for the homogeneous equations from
[
25
]
as a foundational estimate..

•

We exploit interior
C^{1,1^{-}}
-regularity to obtain control away from the boundary.

•

A compactness argument, combined with boundary blow-up argument, leads to the optimal regularity up to the boundary.

(ii)

Optimal regularity for the homogeneous equations: This part of the analysis is based on differentiating the equation in time and exploiting the special structure of
u_{t}
near the boundary:

•

Noting that
u_{t}\approx x_{n}
near
S_{1}
, we analyze the weighted time derivative
x_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}
, which turns out to be Hölder continuous.

•

This structure allows us to reduce the parabolic equation to an elliptic one in suitable coordinates, and we apply classical elliptic regularity theory to conclude.

•

When the boundary data vanishes, we apply a bootstrap argument to show that all higher-order tangential derivatives (excluding the
x_{n}
-direction) satisfy optimal regularity estimates.

1.4.
Organization of the paper

The paper is organized as follows.

•

Section

2
provides preliminary material necessary for our analysis. We introduce notations, define the appropriate Hölder spaces, and recall the notion of viscosity solutions. We also present known regularity results that serve as key tools throughout the paper.

•

Section

3
is devoted to establishing the main results on optimal regularity. We divide the analysis into two parts:

–

In
Section

3.1
, we prove the optimal
C^{1,1-\gamma}
-regularity of viscosity solutions to the inhomogeneous equation up to the flat boundary under regularity assumptions on the data. The argument combines compactness methods with a boundary blow-up argument.

–

In

2.2.
Hölder spaces

We define the Hölder spaces used throughout the paper.

Definition 2.1

(Hölder spaces)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a domain and let
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

(i)

The space
C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
consists of all functions
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying the following condition holds: there exists
C>0
such that

|u(x,t)-u(y,s)|\leq C(|x-y|+\sqrt{|t-s|})^{\alpha}

for all
(x,t),(y,s)\in\Omega
.

(ii)

The space
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
consists of all functions
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying the following condition holds: for each
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\overline{\Omega}
, there exist
C>0
,
r>0
and a linear function
L
such that

|u(x,t)-L(x)|\leq C(|x-x_{0}|+\sqrt{|t-t_{0}|})^{1+\alpha}

for all
(x,t)\in\Omega\cap Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
.

(iii)

The space
C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
consists of all functions
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying the following condition holds: for each
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\overline{\Omega}
, there exist
C>0
,
r>0
and a quadratic polynomial
P
such that

|u(x,t)-P(x,t)|\leq C(|x-x_{0}|+\sqrt{|t-t_{0}|})^{2+\alpha}

for all
(x,t)\in\Omega\cap Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
.

Unlike interior Hölder continuity, Hölder continuity at the boundary points requires considering a metric that is invariant under the scaling of (
1.1
), we refer to
[
25
, Section 2.1]
.

Definition 2.2

(Pointwise Hölder continuity at the flat boundary)
.

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in S_{1}
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i)

We say that
g
is
C^{k,\alpha}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
, denoted by
g\in C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
, if there exist constant
C>0
,
r>0
and a polynomial
P(x,t)
with
\deg P\leq k
such that

|g(x,t)-P(x,t)|\leq C(|x-x_{0}|+|t-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{k+\alpha}

for all
(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{+}\cap Q_{r}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})
.

(ii)

The space
C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{S_{1}})
consists of all functions
g\in C(\overline{S_{1}})
such that

g\in C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})\quad\text{for all }(x_{0},t_{0})\in\overline{S_{1}}.

Remark 2.3
.

Since
0<\gamma<1
, the metric

d[(x,t),(y,s)]\coloneqq|x-y|+|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}}

in
Definition

2.2
has a higher-order in the time distance compared to the standard parabolic metric. Thus, the global regularity is ultimately governed by the regularity with respect to the standard parabolic metric.

2.3.
Viscosity solutions

We introduce the concept of solutions to (
1.1
). Since the main equation (
1.1
) has a nondivergence structure, we adopt the notion of viscosity solutions.

Definition 2.4

(Viscosity solutions)
.

Suppose that
f
is a continuous function in
Q_{1}^{+}
.

(i)

Let
u
be a upper semicontinuous function in

of (

(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{+}
and each function

,

attains a local maximum at

, then

\varphi_{t}(x,t)-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta\varphi(x,t)\leq f(x,t).

(ii)

Let
u
be a lower semicontinuous function in
Q_{1}^{+}
. We say that
u
is a
viscosity supersoution
of (
1.1
) when the following condition holds: if for each
(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{+}
and each function

3.1.
Optimal regularity for inhomogeneous equations

In this subsection, we address the optimal regularity of solutions to (
1.4

\eta
appearing in

and

cannot be precisely determined in the general case, we will choose the exponent of
\eta
in these lemmas to be optimal.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
\delta>0
depending only on
n
and
\gamma
such that if
u
is a viscosity solution to (
1.4
) satisfying

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1,\quad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(S_{1})}\leq\delta,

then there exists a constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\|u-ax_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta}^{+})}\leq\eta^{2-\gamma}\quad\text{and}\quad|a|\leq C_{1},

where
\eta\in(0,1)
is a constant depending only on
n
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

Let us assume that the conclusion is false, that is, there exists a sequence of functions
\{(u^{k},f^{k},g^{k})\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that

\|u^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1,\quad\|f^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq\frac{1}{k},\quad\|g^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(S_{1})}\leq\frac{1}{k}

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u^{k}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u^{k}&=f^{k}&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u^{k}&=g^{k}&&\text{on }S_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, the following estimates hold:

(3.1)

\|u^{k}-ax_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta}^{+})}>\eta^{2-\gamma}\quad\text{for all }a\in\mathbb{R}\text{ with }|a|\leq C_{1},

where
\eta\in(0,1)
will be determined later.

Since
\|u^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows from the interior Hölder regularity for uniformly parabolic equations that
\{u^{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equicontinuous in any compact set
K\subset Q_{1}^{+}
. So, by Arzela-Ascoli theorem, there exist a subsequence
\{u^{k_{j}}\}
of
\{u^{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and a function
u^{\infty}\in C(Q_{1}^{+})
such that

u^{k_{j}}\to u^{\infty}
uniformly in any compact set
K\subset Q_{1}^{+}
as
j\to\infty
.

Since
\|f^{k_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1/k_{j}
and
\|g^{k_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(S_{1})}\leq 1/k_{j}
, we can see that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|f^{k_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}\|g^{k_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(S_{1})}=0.

So,
Lemma

2.8
yields that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u^{\infty}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u^{\infty}&=0&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u^{\infty}&=0&&\text{on }S_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then, by
Lemma

2.7
, there exists
P(x)=a_{0}x_{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}x_{n}
such that

\displaystyle|u^{\infty}(x,t)-a_{0}x_{n}|\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}x_{i}|+C_{1}|x|^{1+\sigma}\right)x_{n}\quad\text{for all $(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}}$}

and
a_{0}\leq\|P\|\leq C_{1}
.

Finally, by taking
\eta<(\frac{1}{4C_{1}})^{1/\gamma}
, we have

(3.2)

\|u^{\infty}-a_{0}x_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta}^{+})}\leq(\|P\|\eta+C_{1}\eta^{1+\sigma})\eta\\
\leq 2C_{1}\eta^{2}\\
<\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2-\gamma}.

On the other hand, letting
k=k_{j}\to\infty
in (
3.1
) for

yields

\displaystyle\|u^{\infty}-a_{0}x_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta}^{+})}\geq\eta^{2-\gamma},

which contradicts (
3.2
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\delta>0
and
\eta>0
be as in

3.1

and let

Then there exists a sequence

\displaystyle|a_{k}-a_{k-1}|\leq C_{1}\eta^{(k-1)\bar{\alpha}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|u-a_{k}x_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta^{k}}^{+})}\leq\eta^{k(1+\bar{\alpha})}.

Proof.

We prove it by induction.

(i)

In the case

(ii)

\hskip 34.14322pt|\widetilde{g}(y,s)|=\frac{|g(ry,r^{2-\gamma}s)|}{r^{1+\bar{\alpha}}}\leq\frac{2^{-1-\beta}}{r^{1+\bar{\alpha}}}\cdot\delta\cdot(2r)^{1+\beta}\leq\delta\quad\text{for all }(y,s)\in\{y_{n}=0\}.

\displaystyle|a_{k+1}-a_{k}|=r^{\bar{\alpha}}|\tilde{a}|\leq C_{1}\eta^{k\bar{\alpha}}.

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-a_{k+1}x_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq r^{1+\bar{\alpha}}\left|v\left(\frac{x}{r},\frac{t}{r^{2-\gamma}}\right)-\tilde{a}\cdot\frac{x_{n}}{r}\right|

for all

Therefore, by induction, (

.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Optimal boundary

-estimates)
.

Let

and

n

\beta
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
(x_{0},t_{0})
is the origin. First, let us show that the assumptions of
Lemma

3.2
hold through an appropriate reduction argument.

Since
g\in C^{1,\beta}(0,0)

such that the following estimate holds:

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} v_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta v&=f&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\

For

Then we have

\displaystyle|a_{i}-a_{j}|

which implies that

.

Finally, reverting the reduction to

|u(x,t)-L(x)|\leq CN(|x|+|t|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{1+\bar{\alpha}}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{+}

and

|Du(0,0)|\leq|a_{\infty}|N+|DL_{0}|\leq CN.

∎

Lemma 3.4

Since (

x_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}\in C^{1-\gamma}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})
with the uniform estimate

\|x_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}\|_{C^{1-\gamma}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})}),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\beta
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
(x,t),(y,s)\in Q_{1/2}^{+}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x_{n}\leq y_{n}\eqqcolon r
. Since the equation in (
1.7
) is uniformly parabolic in any
\Omega\subset\joinrel\subset Q_{1}^{+}
,
u\in C_{\textnormal{loc}}^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})
. Moreover, it follows from (
1.6
) that
u_{t}
is a solution to (
1.12
).
Then, combining
Remark

1.4
,
Lemma

2.6
and
[
25
, Theorem 1.5 and Theorem 1.7]
gives

(3.14)

\displaystyle|u_{t}(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{3/4}^{+})}x_{n}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{C^{2}(\overline{Q_{3/4}^{+}})}x_{n}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})})x_{n}

for all
(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}}
. This implies that

(3.15)

|x_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}(x,t)|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})})(|x|+|t|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{1-\gamma}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}}.

Case 1:
|x-y|+|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}}\leq r/2.

Let
v(x,t)=x_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}(x,t)
. From (
3.15
), we have

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}(y,s))}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})})r^{1-\gamma}.

Furthermore, by interior Hölder estimates for uniformly parabolic equations, we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle|x_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}(x,t)-y_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}(y,s)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}(y,s))}}{r^{1-\gamma}}(|x-y|+\sqrt{|t-s|})^{1-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})})(|x-y|+\sqrt{|t-s|})^{1-\gamma}.

Case 2:
|x-y|+|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}}>r/2.

From (
3.14
), we obtain

\displaystyle|x_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}(x,t)-y_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}(y,s)|

\displaystyle\leq|x_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}(x,t)|+|y_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}(y,s)|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})})(x_{n}^{1-\gamma}+y_{n}^{1-\gamma})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})})r^{1-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})})(|x-y|+|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{1-\gamma}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})})(|x-y|+\sqrt{|t-s|})^{1-\gamma}.

Combining (
3.16
) and (
3.17
), we obtain the conclusion.
∎

Time derivative estimates
Lemma

3.5
transforms the homogeneous parabolic equation into an elliptic equation with a Hölder source term on a fixed time slice, which is the key idea in establishing the optimal regularity of solutions to (
1.7
). Based on this idea, we present the proof of
Theorem

1.5
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.5
.

For fixed
t\in[-1/4,0]
,
v(x)\coloneqq u(x,t)
is a solution to the following elliptic problem:

(3.18)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta v&=\tilde{f}&&\text{in }B_{1}^{+}\\
v&=\tilde{g}&&\text{on }S_{1}\end{aligned}\right.

where
\tilde{f}\coloneqq x_{n}^{-\gamma}u_{t}(\cdot,t)
and
\tilde{g}\coloneqq g(\cdot,t)
. By
Lemma

3.5
, we know that
\tilde{f}\in C^{1-\gamma}(\overline{B_{3/4}^{+}})
and

(3.19)

\|\tilde{f}\|_{C^{1-\gamma}(\overline{B_{3/4}^{+}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})}).

Thus, by (
3.19
) and the Schauder estimates for elliptic equations, we have
v\in C^{2,\bar{\alpha}}(\overline{B_{1/2}^{+}})
with the uniform estimate

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{2,\bar{\alpha}}(\overline{B_{1/2}^{+}})}

\displaystyle=\|v\|_{C^{2,\bar{\alpha}}(\overline{B_{1/2}^{+}})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|\tilde{f}\|_{C^{1-\gamma}(\overline{B_{3/4}^{+}})}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{S_{1}})}).

∎

1.
Introduction

A
Brascamp-Lieb datum
is a tuple

{\mathscr{D}}=(H,(H_{j})_{j\in J},(\ell_{j})_{j\in J},(q_{j})_{j\in J})

where
J
is a finite index set,

are finite dimensional real Hilbert spaces (a.k.a. Euclidean spaces),
\ell_{j}:H\to H_{j}
are
linear maps from
H
to
H_{j}
, and

are positive real numbers. Below, we simply write

{\mathscr{D}}=((\ell_{j})_{j\in J},(q_{j})_{j\in J})

and keep implicit the notation
H,H_{j}
.

The
Brascamp-Lieb constant of
{\mathscr{D}}
is the smallest constant
\BL({\mathscr{D}})\in[0,+\infty]
such that

(1)

\int_{H}\prod_{j\in J}(f_{j}\circ\ell_{j})^{q_{j}}\leq\BL({\mathscr{D}})\prod_{j\in J}\left(\int_{H_{j}}f_{j}\right)^{q_{j}},

for all collections
(f_{j})_{j\in J}
of measurable functions
f_{j}\colon H_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

The Brascamp-Lieb inequality (
1
) unifies and generalises a number of classical results (corresponding to specific data

) such as the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on

, Hölder’s inequality, Young’s inequality, the Loomis-Whitney inequality.
The study of (

One of the cornerstones in the study of Brascamp-Lieb inequalities is the finiteness criterion, due to Barthe

1.1.
Bounds for Brascamp-Lieb constants

Let
{\mathscr{D}}=((\ell_{j})_{j},(q_{j})_{j})
be a Brascamp-Lieb datum.
We present explicit upper and lower bounds for
\BL({\mathscr{D}})
that refine several known qualitative results, such as the finiteness criterion of Bennett-Carbery-Christ-Tao
[
7
,
8
]
, and the local-boundedness result of Bennett-Bez-Flock-Lee
[
6
]
.

We start by introducing the quantities that play a role in the bounds below.
First, we refine the notion of rank for a linear map between Euclidean spaces, taking the metric into account.

Definition

(Essential rank)
.

Let
H,H^{\prime}
be Euclidean spaces, let
\ell:H\rightarrow H^{\prime}
be a linear map, let
\alpha\geq 0
.
The
\alpha
-essential rank
of
\ell
is the number (counted with multiplicity) of singular values of
\ell
that are strictly greater than
\alpha
. We denote it by
\rk_{\alpha}(\ell)
.

Clearly,
\rk_{0}(\ell)=\rk(\ell)
is nothing more than the rank of
\ell
.
Several characterisations of
\rk_{\alpha}
will be given in
Section

2
.
For a given subspace
W\subseteq H
, we write

\rk_{\alpha}(\ell\mid W)=\rk_{\alpha}(\ell_{\mid W})

for the
\alpha
-essential rank of
\ell
restricted to
W
.

Definition

(Essential acuity)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{j})_{j\in J}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{J}
, and
W\subseteq H
a subspace. The
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-essential acuity of
{\mathscr{D}}
within
W
is

\mathscr{A}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}({\mathscr{D}}\mid W)=\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\rk_{\alpha_{j}}(\ell_{j}\mid W).

The algebraic perceptivity condition (
3
) amounts to
\mathscr{A}_{0}({\mathscr{D}}\mid W)\geq\dim W
for all
W
. The next definition can be seen as a quantified version of (
3
) involving the metric.

Definition

(Metric perceptivity)
.

We say
{\mathscr{D}}
is
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-perceptive
if for every subspace
W\subseteq H
, we have

(4)

\mathscr{A}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}({\mathscr{D}}\mid W)\geq\dim W.

See §
2.2
for alternative characterisations in the context where
\ell_{j}
are orthogonal projectors.

We will also make use of the constant

(5)

\mathscr{E}({\mathscr{D}})=\prod_{j\in J}q_{j}^{-q_{j}\dim H_{j}/2}.

\mathscr{E}({\mathscr{D}})
can be interpreted as some weighted
exponential entropy
for the vector
(q_{j})_{j\in J}
seen as a measure on
J
.
It is dominated by

\mathscr{E}({\mathscr{D}})\leq\exp\left({\textstyle\frac{1}{2e}\sum_{j\in J}\dim H_{j}}\right).

Theorem 1.1

(Upper bound)
.

Let
{\mathscr{D}}=\bigl((\ell_{j})_{j\in J},(q_{j})_{j\in J}\bigr)
be a Brascamp-Lieb datum. Assume
{\mathscr{D}}
globally critical (i.e. (
2
) holds) and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-perceptive for some
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{J}
. Then writing
d=\dim H
, we have

\BL({\mathscr{D}})\leq d^{\frac{d}{2}}\mathscr{E}({\mathscr{D}})\prod_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{-q_{j}\dim H_{j}}.

To complete this result, we record a lower bound. Below, we write
\|\ell_{j}\|
for the operator norm of
\ell_{j}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Lower bound)
.

Let
{\mathscr{D}}=\bigl((\ell_{j})_{j\in J},(q_{j})_{j\in J}\bigr)
be a Brascamp-Lieb datum. Set
C:=1+\sup_{j\in J}\|\ell_{j}\|
.
Then for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
and all
W\in\Gr(H)
,

\BL({\mathscr{D}})\geq\left(C^{2}\sum\nolimits_{j\in J}q_{j}\right)^{-\dim H/2}\alpha^{\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\rk_{\alpha}(\ell_{j}\mid W)-\dim W}.

As an illustration, let us compute those bounds in the context of Young’s inequality.

Example
.

(Young’s inequality)
Consider the Brascamp-Lieb datum
{\mathscr{D}}=\bigl((\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}),(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\bigr)
with
H=\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
H_{1}=H_{2}=H_{3}=\mathbb{R}
,
\ell_{1}(x,y)=x
,
\ell_{2}(x,y)=y
,
\ell_{3}(x,y)=x-y
.

Then the condition of
1.1
is satisfied for a vector
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{3}
if

q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3}=2
,
0<q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}\leq 1
, and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha_{1}^{2}+(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3})^{2}<1\\
\alpha_{2}^{2}+(\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3})^{2}<1.\end{array}\right.

In particular, it is satisfied for all
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}
.

1.1
gives
\BL({\mathscr{D}})\leq\frac{10}{q_{1}^{q_{1}/2}q_{2}^{q_{2}/2}q_{3}^{q_{3}/2}}
.
On the other hand,
1.2
(applied with
\alpha=1
) gives
\BL({\mathscr{D}})\geq\frac{1}{18}
.
We can compare these estimates to the actual value of

\BL({\mathscr{D}})=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{3}\frac{(1-q_{j})^{1-q_{j}}}{q_{j}^{q_{j}}}\right)^{1/2}
.

\BL({\mathscr{D}})=\Upsilon({\mathscr{D}})\BL({\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{proj}})\,\,\,\text{ where }\,\,\,\Upsilon({\mathscr{D}})=\prod_{j\in J}\lvert\det\nolimits_{\ker(\ell_{j})^{\perp}\to H_{j}}(\ell_{j})\rvert^{-q_{j}},

Qualitative consequences.

From Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, we derive a few qualitative corollaries.

Finiteness.

Our approach consists in making effective the proof of the aforementioned finiteness criterion from
[
7
]
. Therefore, it is not surprising that Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
imply the latter.

To recover the criterion, note on the one hand that if (
2
) fails, then by scaling consideration,
\BL({\mathscr{D}})=+\infty
.
If (
3
) fails for some subspace
W\subseteq H
, then we have for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}({\mathscr{D}}\mid W)-\dim W\leq\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\dim\ell_{j}(W)-\dim W<0.

Letting
\alpha\to 0
in
1.2
, we obtain
\BL({\mathscr{D}})=+\infty
.

On the other hand, assume
{\mathscr{D}}
satisfies (
2
) and (
3
). Note that given
W
, we have
\mathscr{A}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}({\mathscr{D}}|W^{\prime})=\mathscr{A}_{0}({\mathscr{D}}|W^{\prime})
for
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{J}
close enough to zero and
W^{\prime}
close enough to
W
. By compactness of the Grassmanian
\Gr(H)
, we deduce that
{\mathscr{D}}
is
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-perceptive for
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{J}
in a neighorhood of
0
, whence
\BL({\mathscr{D}})<+\infty
by
1.1
.

Local boundedness.
The condition that
{\mathscr{D}}
is
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-perceptive is stable under small perturbation of the
\ell_{j}
.
Thus,
1.1
implies the following stability result, originally due to Bennett-Bez-Flock-Lee
[
6
]
: if
\BL({\mathscr{D}})<+\infty
for some Brascamp-Lieb datum
{\mathscr{D}}=(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{q})
, then fixing
\mathbf{q}
, there is a constant
C>0
such that
\BL(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{q})<C
holds uniformly for
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
ranging in a small neighborhood of
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\prod_{j\in J}\Ell(H,H_{j})
.

Here
\Ell(H,H_{j})
denotes the set of linear maps from
H
to
H_{j}
.
Let us mention that continuity and differentiability of
\BL(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{q})
in the variable
\boldsymbol{\ell}
are investigated by Valdimarsson
[
20
]
, Bennett-Bez-Cowling-Flock
[
5
]
and Garg-Gurvits-Oliveira-Wigderson
[
15
]
.

Joint local boundedness for simple data.

The following joint local boundedness seems to be new.
Recall that a globally critical Brascamp-Lieb datum is said to be
simple
if (
3
) holds as a strict inequality for any proper nonzero subspace
W\in\Gr(H)
.

Corollary 1.3

(Joint local boundedness in
(\ell_{j})_{j}
and
(q_{j})_{j}
near simple data)
.

Let
{\mathscr{D}}
be a simple Brascamp-Lieb datum.
Then there exists a constant
C>0
and a neighborhood
U
of
{\mathscr{D}}
in
\prod_{j\in J}\Ell(H,H_{j})\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{J}
such that for any
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}\in U
that is globally critical, we have
\BL({\mathscr{D}}^{\prime})<C
.

1.2.
Bounds for localised regularised Brascamp-Lieb constants.

We present effective
regularised
and
localised
Brascamp-Lieb inequalities.
They imply Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and have the advantage to be meaningful for Brascamp-Lieb data that do not necessarily satisfy (
2
) or (
3
).
Those estimates strengthen former quantitative bounds due to Maldague
[
19
]
.

We first introduce the corresponding definitions.
Given a Euclidean space
H
, a positive definite symmetric endomorphism
R\in\End(H)
, and
x\in H
, set

(8)

\chi_{R}(x)=e^{-\pi\langle x,Rx\rangle}\hskip 11.74988pt\text{ and }\hskip 11.74988pt\mathscr{N}_{R}(x)=(\det R)^{1/2}\chi_{R}(x).

We interprete
\chi_{R}
as a Gaussian truncation function, while
\mathscr{N}_{R}
is the centered normal probability density of covariance
(2\pi R)^{-1}
.
A function
f:H\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is said to be of
type
R
if it takes the form of a convolution
f=\mathscr{N}_{R}*\mu
for some finite Borel measure
\mu
on
H
.
In essence, being of type
R
expresses that a function comes from the mollification of a positive measure at a scale given by
R
(say
\mathscr{N}_{R}^{-1}[1,+\infty)
). As
R
gets bigger, the scale gets smaller, so the condition becomes less restrictive.

A localised regularised Brascamp-Lieb datum is a triple
({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)
where

{\mathscr{D}}=((\ell_{j})_{j\in J},(q_{j})_{j\in J})
is a Brascamp-Lieb datum,

{\bf{R}}=(R_{j})_{j\in J}
is a collection such that each
R_{j}
is a positive definite symmetric endomorphism of
H_{j}

and
T
is a positive definite symmetric endomorphism of
H
.

The localised regularised Brascamp-Lieb constant of
({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)
is the smallest number
\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)\in[0,+\infty]
such that

\int_{H}\chi_{T}\prod_{j\in J}(f_{j}\circ\ell_{j})^{q_{j}}\leq\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)\prod_{j\in J}\left(\int_{H_{j}}f_{j}\right)^{q_{j}},

for all collections
(f_{j})_{j\in J}
such that
f_{j}:H_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is of type
R_{j}
for each
j\in J
. In sum,
the parameter
{\bf{R}}
imposes a certain class of regular functions, while the parameter
T
yields a gradual truncation along the integral.

This definition (with
T=\Id_{H}
) is extracted from
[
7
, Section 8]
.

Remark.

A variant of
\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)
, asking for a straight regularisation (inputs
f_{j}
tile-wise constant) and a straight truncation (
\chi_{T}
replaced by
\mathbbm{1}_{B_{1}^{H}}
) is used in
[

. Upper bound estimates for this variant variant follow from counterparts on

using suitable mollification, as in the proof of

We give an explicit upper bound on
\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)
.
In situations where (
2
) is not satisfied, we use the
total acuity

\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})=\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\dim H_{j}.

For situations where (
3
) is violated, we need the following notion.

Definition

(Metric perceptivity 2)
.

Given
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{J}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we say
{\mathscr{D}}
is
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta)
-perceptive
if for every subspace
W\subseteq H
, we have

(9)

\mathscr{A}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}({\mathscr{D}}\mid W)\geq\dim W-\beta.

We also use the norm

N({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)=\big\|T+\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\ell^{*}_{j}\,R_{j}\,\ell_{j}\big\|

where
\ell^{*}_{j}:H_{j}\rightarrow H
stands for the adjoint of
\ell_{j}
. Finally, we recall the quantity
\mathscr{E}({\mathscr{D}})
has been defined in (

).

Theorem 1.4

(Upper bound)
.

Let
({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)
be a localised regularised Brascamp-Lieb datum, let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{J}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Assume
{\mathscr{D}}
is
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta)
-perceptive and
\rk_{\alpha_{j}}(\ell_{j})=\dim H_{j}
for each
j\in J
.
Then, writing

, we have

(10)

\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)\leq d^{\frac{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})}{2}}\mathscr{E}({\mathscr{D}})\prod_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{-q_{j}\dim H_{j}}N({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)^{\frac{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})-d+\beta}{2}}\lVert T^{-1}\rVert^{\frac{\beta}{2}}.

Remark.

The condition
\rk_{\alpha_{j}}(\ell_{j})=\dim H_{j}
can be interpreted as a quantitative strengthening of the condition that
\ell_{j}\colon H\to H_{j}
is surjective.
The latter is often required in the form of a non-degeneracy condition.

Remark.
In the context of

\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})=d
and
\beta=0
, so
{\bf{R}}
and

The upper bound from

(see

In fact, upper bounds on other variants of the Brascamp-Lieb inequalities can be deduced similarly. For instance, assuming the relation
\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})-d+\beta=0
, we see

plays no role in the above upper bound, whence we may pass to the limit to bound in the same manner the
localised
Brascamp-Lieb constant
\BL({\mathscr{D}},\infty,T)

Note the appearance of the condition

In other words,

Another limiting upper bound is that of
\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},0)
under the assumption

Remark.

The exponents

We also record the following lower bound for localised regularised Brascamp-Lieb constants. For simplicity, we assume

for each

Theorem 1.5

(Lower bound)
.

Let
{\mathscr{D}}=\bigl((\ell_{j})_{j},(q_{j})_{j}\bigr)
be a Brascamp-Lieb datum. Set

.
Let

\BL({\mathscr{D}},\mathbf{R},T)\geq\left((C/\alpha)^{2}t+C^{2}\sum\nolimits_{j\in J}q_{j}\right)^{-\dim H/2}\alpha^{\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\rk_{\alpha}(\ell_{j}\mid W)-\dim W}.

Remark.

Theorems

strengthen former quantitative bounds due to Maldague

Finally, we discuss some consequences of

Visual inequality.

Let
H
be a Euclidean space.
Given a subset

the
\delta
-covering number of

\delta

Consider a collection of lines

in

and

with

If

and

, then it is well-known that for every

and
\delta>0

{\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}(A)\ll_{d}\alpha^{-d}{\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}(\pi_{L_{1}}A)\dotsm{\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}(\pi_{L_{d}}A).

As a corollary of the effective localised regularised Brascamp-Lieb estimate from
1.4
, we can generalise the above inequality to arbitrary configurations of subspaces.

Theorem 1.6

(Visual inequality)
.

Let
{\mathscr{D}}=((\pi_{H_{j}})_{j\in J},(q_{j})_{j\in J})
be a Brascamp-Lieb datum made of orthogonal projectors
\pi_{H_{j}}:H\to H_{j}
where

.
Set

.
Assume

is

-perceptive for some

,

.
Then for every
\delta\in(0,1)
, every subset

, we have

(12)

{\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}(A)\leq C\delta^{-\beta}\prod_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{-q_{j}\dim H_{j}}\prod_{j\in J}{\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}\bigl(\pi_{H_{j}}A\bigr)^{q_{j}}

where
0<C\ll_{d}O_{d}(1)^{\sum_{j}q_{j}}(1+\sum_{j\in J}q_{j})^{\frac{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})-d+\beta}{2}}\mathscr{E}({\mathscr{D}})
.

In the above,

refers to the closed centered unit ball in
H
.

Remark.

The inequality (
11
) is covered by
1.6
, taking

and
\ell_{j}=\pi_{L_{j}}
,
q_{j}=1
for

the

\alpha

is

-perceptive (so

), and

,

is

.

Remark.

The lack of perceptivity is incarnated by

. Its role in the visual inequality can be understood as follows.
If in (

), we wish to remove the first

projectors (

), we may only guarantee

as can be seen by taking

with

This observation is reflected by the fact that the datum

-perceptive.

Perturbed Brascamp-Lieb theorem and multilinear Kakeya inequalities.

The upper bound of
1.4
can be put into Zorin-Kranich’s machine
[
23
, Theorem 1.3]
to derive effective perturbed Brascamp-Lieb inequalities and consequently multilinear Kakeya inequalities.
The effectiveness of
1.4
allows us to control quantities appearing in the statements of such inequalities that were previously known to depend on the Brascamp-Lieb datum.
More precisely, previously, these quantities would depend obscurely on the Brascamp-Lieb datum, while now we know how to control them in terms of the parameters
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta)
of perceptivity.
Such quantities include

(1)

the constant
\delta
in Zhang’s endpoint perturbed Brascamp-Lieb inequality
[
21
, Theorem 1.11]
(let us mention here that this theorem generalises the celebrated multilinear Kakeya estimates of Bennett-Carbery-Tao
[

and of Guth
[
16
, Theorem 1.3]
).

(2)

the constant
\nu
and the constant

in Maldague’s generalised multilinear Kakeya inequality

.

On the terminology of perceptivity.

In the literature, condition (
3
) usually does not have a name.
We propose the terminology of
algebraic perceptivity
, and later on of
metric perceptivity
for its quantified version. “Algebraic” refers to linear algebra, while “metric” refers to the additional involvement of the Euclidean structure. The term perceptivity is motivated by the intuition that for a Brascamp-Lieb datum
{\mathscr{D}}
to reflect features of
H
without loss, we need at least
\sum q_{j}\dim\ell_{j}(H)\geq\dim H
.
Here, condition (
3
) requires that this also holds in restriction to subspaces, in other words,
{\mathscr{D}}
perceives all subspaces.
In
1.6
, the visual lexical field arises naturally: in order to perceive the size of a set, we only need to know its projection via a Brascamp-Lieb datum satisfying a perceptivity condition in the sense defined previously.

2.
Essential rank and perceptivity

In this section, we clarify the notions of essential rank and perceptivity by presenting several properties and characterisations.

2.1.
Essential rank

We let
H,H^{\prime}
denote Euclidean spaces. Recall
\Gr(H)
denotes the Grassmanian of
H
, and
\Ell(H,H^{\prime})
is the set of linear maps from
H
to
H^{\prime}
.
We first record a straightforward continuity result for the essential rank.

Lemma 2.1
.

The map

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Ell(H,H^{\prime})\times\Gr(H)&\to&\mathbb{N},\\
(\alpha,\ell,W)&\mapsto&\rk_{\alpha}(\ell\mid W)\end{array}

is lower semi-continuous.
That is, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the subset of triples
(\alpha,\ell,W)
such that
\rk_{\alpha}(\ell\mid W)\geq k
is open.

Next, we give an equivalent definition of the essential rank.
For a Euclidean space
W
and a radius
\rho\geq 0
, we denote by
B_{\rho}^{W}
the
closed
centered ball of radius
\rho
in
W
.
In particular,
B^{W}_{0}=\{0\}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
\ell\in\Ell(H,H^{\prime})
,
W\in\Gr(H)
, we have

\rk_{\alpha}(\ell\mid W)=\min\Bigl\{\,\dim E\,:\,E\in\Gr(H^{\prime})\text{ with }\ell\bigl(B^{W}_{1}\bigr)\subseteq B^{H^{\prime}}_{\alpha}+E\,\Bigr\}.

Remark.

We see from the proof that the above minimum is realized by some
E
of the form
E=\ell(V)
where
V\in\Gr(W)
satisfies
\ell_{|W}(V^{\perp})\subseteq E^{\perp}
and
\|\ell_{|W\cap V^{\perp}}\|\leq\alpha
.

Proof.

We need to show that
r=\widetilde{r}
where

r:=\rk_{\alpha}(\ell_{|W})\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988pt\widetilde{r}:=\min\Bigl\{\,\dim E\,:\,E\in\Gr(H^{\prime})\text{ with }\ell\bigl(B^{W}_{1}\bigr)\subseteq B^{H^{\prime}}_{\alpha}+E\,\Bigr\}.

By the singular decomposition of
\ell_{|W}:H\to H^{\prime}
, there is an orthonormal basis
(w_{1},\dotsc,w_{k})
of
W
such that the
(\ell w_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
are pairwise orthogonal and

\sigma_{1}:=\lVert\ell w_{1}\rVert\geq\dots\geq\sigma_{k}:=\lVert\ell w_{k}\rVert

are the singular values of
\ell_{|w}
, decreasingly ordered.
By definition of the essential rank
r
, we have
\sigma_{i}>\alpha
for
1\leq i\leq r
while
\sigma_{i}\leq\alpha
for
r+1\leq i\leq k
.

On the one hand, for
E_{0}:=\Span\{\ell w_{1},\dotsc,\ell w_{r}\}
, we have

\ell(B_{1}^{W})\subseteq B^{H^{\prime}}_{\alpha}+E_{0},

leading to
\widetilde{r}\leq\dim E_{0}=r
.

On the other hand, we observe that

\ell(B^{W}_{1})\supseteq\ell(B^{\Span(w_{1},\dots,w_{r})}_{1})\supseteq B^{E_{0}}_{\sigma_{r}}.

As
\sigma_{r}>\alpha
, we obtain that
\ell(B^{W}_{1})
cannot be included in
B_{\alpha}^{H^{\prime}}+E
for some
E\in\Gr(H^{\prime})
with dimension
\dim E<\dim E_{0}
. Therefore
\widetilde{r}\geq r
.
∎

The case of orthogonal projectors.

We present further characterisations of the essential rank in the setting of orthogonal projectors.
The next lemma provides a hands-on description in the rank-one case.

Lemma 2.3
.

Consider a line
L=\mathbb{R}u\in\Gr(H)
with
\|u\|=1
. For all
W\in\Gr(H)
, we have

\rk_{\alpha}(\pi_{L}\mid W)=\begin{cases}1\hskip 11.74988pt\text{if $\lVert\pi_{W}(u)\rVert>\alpha$,}\\
0\hskip 11.74988pt\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Note
\rk_{\alpha}(\pi_{L}\mid W)\in\{0,1\}
and is equal to
1
if and only if
\|(\pi_{L})_{|W}\|>\alpha
, i.e. there exists
w\in W
with
\|w\|\leq 1
such that
|\langle u,w\rangle|>\alpha
. But
\sup_{w\in B^{W}_{1}}|\langle u,w\rangle|=\|\pi_{W}(u)\|
, whence the result.
∎

We now turn to the case of projectors of arbitrary rank.
Fix a Riemannian metric on
\Gr(H)
which is invariant by the group of isometries of
H
.
We let
\dist(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the corresponding distance on
\Gr(H)
, and for
r\geq 0,W\in\Gr(H)
, we write
B_{r}(W)
for the closed ball or radius
r
and center
W
.
By convention, if
V,W\in\Gr(H)
have different dimensions, then
\dist(V,W)=+\infty
.

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists a constant
C>1
, depending only on the choice of metric on
\Gr(H)
, such that for all
V,W\in\Gr(H)
,
\alpha\in[0,C^{-1})
,

(13)

\min_{W^{\prime}\in B_{C\alpha}(W)}\dim\pi_{V}(W^{\prime})\leq\rk_{\alpha}(\pi_{V}\mid W)\leq\min_{W^{\prime}\in B_{\alpha/C}(W)}\dim\pi_{V}(W^{\prime}).

Proof.

We use the shorthand
\ell:=\pi_{V}
and
r=\rk_{\alpha}(\ell_{|W})
.

We start with the inequality on the left. Recall the basis
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
of
W
from the proof of
Lemma

2.2
.
Consider

W^{\prime}_{0}=\Span\{w_{1},\dotsc,w_{r},w_{r+1}-\ell w_{r+1},\dotsc,w_{k}-\ell w_{k}\}.

Using that
\ell
is a projector, we have
\dim\ell(W^{\prime}_{0})\leq r
.
To bound
\dist(W^{\prime}_{0},W)
, observe that the map from
H^{k-r}
to
\mathbb{R}
given by

(v_{r+1},\dotsc,v_{k})\mapsto\dist(\Span\{w_{1},\dotsc,w_{r},w_{r+1}+v_{r+1},\dotsc,w_{k}+v_{k}\},W)

is
C
-Lipschitz continuous on the ball of radius
C^{-1}
around
0\in H^{k-r}
for some constant
C>1
.
Moreover, since
\dist
is invariant under the group of isometries and the latter acts transitively on
\Gr(H,k)
, the constant
C
is uniform in
W\in\Gr(H,k)
.
As
\max_{i>r}\|\ell w_{i}\|\leq\alpha
by definition, we get
\dist(W^{\prime}_{0},W)\leq Ck\alpha
provided
k\alpha<C^{-1}
. This shows the inequality on the left (with
C\dim H
in the place of
C
).

For the inequality on the right, observe
4
4

4

Use a local chart around
W^{\prime}
and then obtain uniformity using the group of isometries.
that there is a constant
C>1
such that
for all
W,W^{\prime}\in\Gr(H)
,

\sup_{w\in B_{1}^{W}}\dist(w,W^{\prime})<C\dist(W,W^{\prime}).

Then for every
W^{\prime}\in\Gr(H)
such that
\dist(W,W^{\prime})\leq\alpha/C
, using that
\ell
is
1
-Lipschitz, we have

\ell(B_{1}^{W})\subseteq\ell(W^{\prime})+B^{H}_{\alpha}.

In view of
Lemma

2.2
, this implies

r\leq\dim\ell(W^{\prime}),

finishing the proof of the second inequality.
∎

2.2.
Perceptivity

The characterisations of essential rank discussed above immediately provide further characterisations of perceptivity.

Example
.

As a corollary of
Lemma

2.3
, if the tuple
(\ell_{j})_{j\in J}
in the datum
{\mathscr{D}}
consists of orthogonal projectors of rank one, then for
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{J}
, the
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-essential acuity within
W\in\Gr(H)
is given by

\mathscr{A}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}({\mathscr{D}}\mid W)=\sum\{q_{j}\,:\,j\in J\text{ such that }\lVert\pi_{W}(u_{j})\rVert>\alpha_{j}\},

where
u_{j}
is a unit vector in the line
\ell_{j}(H)
.
Thus,
\alpha^{\otimes J}
-perceptivity means in essence that for every
W\in\Gr(H)
, the sum of weights of lines forming an angle greater than
\alpha
with
W^{\perp}
is at least
\dim W
.
This geometric criterion is simple to visualise.

For orthogonal projectors of arbitrary rank, the next lemma provides useful additional insight on perceptivity. This characterisation will be exploited in
[
4
]
to derive a subcritical projection theorem from the present article.

Lemma 2.5
.

There exists a constant
C>1
depending only on the choice of metric on
\Gr(H)
such that the following holds.
Let
{\mathscr{D}}=((\pi_{H_{j}})_{j\in J},(q_{j})_{j\in J})
be a globally critical Brascamp-Lieb datum made of orthogonal projectors
\pi_{H_{j}}:H\to H_{j}
where
H_{j}\in\Gr(H)
.
Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{j})_{j\in J}\in[0,C^{-1})^{J}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

If for every
W\in\Gr(H)
,

\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\max_{W^{\prime}\in B_{C\alpha_{j}}(W)}\frac{\dim H^{\perp}_{j}\cap W^{\prime}}{\dim W}-\frac{\beta}{\dim W}\,\leq\,\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\frac{\dim H^{\perp}_{j}}{\dim H},

then
{\mathscr{D}}
is
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta)
-perceptive.

Conversely, if
{\mathscr{D}}
is
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta)
-perceptive, then the above holds with the condition
W^{\prime}\in B_{C\alpha_{j}}(W)
replaced by
W^{\prime}\in B_{\alpha_{j}/C}(W)
.

Taking
\beta=0
, we see perceptivity means in essence that the proportion of kernel
H^{\perp}_{j}
intersecting any given subspace
W
is smaller (in average) than the proportion of
H^{\perp}_{j}
in the whole space
H
.

Proof of
Lemma

2.5
.

Let us show the sufficient condition.
By (the first bound in)
Lemma

2.4
, we know that in order to show that
{\mathscr{D}}
is
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta)
-perceptive, it suffices to check:
\forall W\in\Gr(H)
,

(14)

\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\Bigl(\dim W-\max_{W^{\prime}\in B_{C\alpha_{j}}(W)}\dim(H^{\perp}_{j}\cap W^{\prime})\Bigr)\geq\dim W-\beta.

Equation (
14
) can be rewritten as

(15)

\Bigl(\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}-1\Bigr)\dim W\geq\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\max_{W^{\prime}\in B_{C\alpha_{j}}(W)}\dim(H^{\perp}_{j}\cap W^{\prime})-\beta.

On the other hand, the assumption of global criticality guarantees

(16)

\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\frac{\dim H^{\perp}_{j}}{\dim H}=\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}-1

and the desired inequality follows by plugging (
16
) into (
15
) and dividing by
\dim W
.

The proof of the necessary condition is similar, using this time the second bound from
Lemma

2.4
.
∎

3.
Upper bounds for localised regularised data

In this section we prove
1.4
.

3.1.
Lieb’s theorem

A key ingredient is Lieb’s theorem
[
17
, Theorem 6.2]
, which constitutes a central result in the theory of Brascamp–Lieb inequalities.
It states that there is no loss in restricting the inputs
(f_{j})_{j\in J}
in the definition of the Brascamp-Lieb constant to Gaussian inputs
f_{j}=\chi_{A_{j}}
, with positive definite symmetric
A_{j}\in\End(H_{j})
.

We are going to use the following generalisation, which concerns regularised and localised data. It is extracted from
[
7
, Corollary 8.15]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Generalised Lieb’s theorem
[
7
]
)
.

Let
({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)
be a localised regularised Brascamp-Lieb datum with
{\mathscr{D}}=((\ell_{j})_{j\in J},(q_{j})_{j\in J})
and
{\bf{R}}=(R_{j})_{j\in J}
.
Then

(17)

\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)=\sup_{0<A_{j}\leq R_{j}}\left(\frac{\prod_{j}(\det A_{j})^{q_{j}}}{\det(T+\sum_{j}q_{j}\ell_{j}^{*}A_{j}\ell_{j})}\right)^{1/2},

where
0<A_{j}\leq R_{j}
means
A_{j}
is ranging through all positive definite symmetric endomorphisms of
H_{j}
such that
R_{j}-A_{j}
is positive semi-definite, for each
j\in J
.

3.2.
Proof of
1.4

The proof of
1.4
is inspired by
[
7
, Section 5]
. The strategy is to use the generalised Lieb theorem to restrict the estimate of
\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)
to Gaussian inputs, and then perform explicit computations to bound the latter.

Given
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
with
a\leq b
, we use the notation
\llbracket a,b\rrbracket
to denote the set of integers between
a
and
b
, that is,
\llbracket a,b\rrbracket=\mathbb{Z}\cap[a,b]
.

Proof of
1.4
.

We first reduce to the case where
\alpha_{j}=1
for all
j\in J
. Indeed, note that for each
j\in J
and every
W\in\Gr(H)
, we have

\rk_{1}(\alpha_{j}^{-1}\ell_{j}\mid W)=\rk_{\alpha_{j}}(\ell_{j}\mid W)
. Therefore the datum
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}:=\bigl((\alpha_{j}^{-1}\ell_{j})_{j},(q_{j})_{j}\bigr)
is
(1^{\otimes J},\beta)
-perceptive and satisfies
\rk_{1}(\alpha_{j}^{-1}\ell_{j})=\dim H_{j}
for all
j
. By a simple change of variables, we also have

\BL({\mathscr{D}},\mathbf{R},T)=\prod_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{-q_{j}\dim H_{j}}\BL({\mathscr{D}}^{\prime},\mathbf{R}^{\prime},T)

where
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}=(\alpha_{j}^{2}R_{j})_{j\in J}
, and
N({\mathscr{D}},\mathbf{R},T)=N({\mathscr{D}}^{\prime},\mathbf{R}^{\prime},T)
.
It is thus sufficient to prove the theorem for
{\mathscr{D}}={\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}
, whence the reduction to
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=1^{\otimes J}
.

Now, in view of Lieb’s theorem
3.1
, it suffices to show

(18)

\frac{\prod_{j\in J}(\det A_{j})^{q_{j}}}{\det(T+\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\ell_{j}^{*}A_{j}\ell_{j})}\leq d^{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})}\mathscr{E}({\mathscr{D}})^{2}N({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)^{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})-d+\beta}\lVert T^{-1}\rVert^{\beta}

for all positive definite symmetric endomorphisms
A_{j}\in\End(H_{j})
satisfying
A_{j}\leq R_{j}
.

Let
M:=T+\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\,\ell^{*}_{j}A_{j}\ell_{j}\in\End(H)
.
It is symmetric and positive definite.
Let
(e_{1},\dotsc,e_{d})
be an orthonormal basis of
H
in which the matrix of
M
is diagonal, with diagonal entries
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{d}>0
.
For a set of indices
I\subseteq\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
, we write

V_{I}=\Span\{\,e_{i}:i\in I\,\}.

For
k\in\llbracket 0,d-1\rrbracket
, we abbreviate

V_{>k}=V_{\llbracket k+1,d\rrbracket}.

For each

, we construct an index subset
I_{j}\subseteq\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
satisfying

(19)

\forall i\in I_{j},\hskip 11.74988pt\dist(\ell_{j}e_{i},\ell_{j}V_{I_{j}\cap(i,d]})\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}},

(20)

\forall i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket,\hskip 11.74988pt\dist(\ell_{j}e_{i},\ell_{j}V_{I_{j}\cap[i,d]})<\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}.

This construction is done inductively.
First, we put

in

if and only if
\lVert\ell_{j}e_{d}\rVert\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}
.
This determines

.
Then for each integer

is determined.
We put

if (

) holds, and we skip

This procedure terminates when

satisfying (
19
), (
20
).

We derive further properties of the sets

. First, we claim that for all

\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\lvert I_{j}\cap(k,d]\rvert\geq d-k-\beta.

To see that, note that by (
20
), we have

\ell_{j}B_{1}^{V_{>k}}\subseteq\ell_{j}V_{I_{j}\cap(k,d]}+B^{H}_{1}.

In view of
Lemma

2.2
, this implies

(22)

\rk_{1}(\ell_{j}\mid V_{>k})\leq\dim V_{I_{j}\cap(k,d]}=\lvert I_{j}\cap(k,d]\rvert.

Summing over
j\in J
and using the assumption of perceptivity, the claim (
21
) follows.

We also see that
|I_{j}|=\dim H_{j}
. Indeed, taking
k=0
in (
22
) and recalling
\rk_{1}(\ell_{j})=\dim H_{j}
by hypothesis, we obtain
\dim H_{j}\leq\lvert I_{j}\rvert
.
By (
19
), we have

(23)

\lVert\wedge_{i\in I_{j}}\ell_{j}e_{i}\rVert=\prod_{i\in I_{j}}\dist\bigl(\ell_{j}e_{i},\ell_{j}V_{I_{j}\cap(i,d]}\bigr)\geq d^{-\frac{\lvert I_{j}\rvert}{2}},

and in particular
\lvert I_{j}\rvert\leq\dim H_{j}
.
Therefore
\lvert I_{j}\rvert=\dim H_{j}
. Note the argument shows that
(\ell_{j}e_{i})_{i\in I_{j}}
is a basis of
H_{j}
. By definition of
\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})
, we also have

(24)

\sum_{j}q_{j}\lvert I_{j}\rvert=\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}}).

We deduce upper bounds on the determinants of the

.
Given

, and

, we have

(25)

{\langle A_{j}\ell_{j}e_{i},\ell_{j}e_{i}\rangle}={\langle\ell_{j}^{*}A_{j}\ell_{j}e_{i},e_{i}\rangle}\leq\frac{1}{q_{j}}{\langle Me_{i},e_{i}\rangle}\leq\frac{\lambda_{i}}{q_{j}}.

Employing

3.2
below, then (

\displaystyle{\det A_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\wedge_{i\in I_{j}}\ell_{j}e_{i}\rVert^{-2}\prod_{i\in I_{j}}{\langle A_{j}\ell_{j}e_{i},\ell_{j}e_{i}\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\frac{d}{q_{j}}\Bigr)^{\lvert I_{j}\rvert}\prod_{i\in I_{j}}\lambda_{i}.

Taking power
q_{j}
, then the product over
j
, and using (

), we get

(26)

\prod_{j\in J}(\det A_{j})^{q_{j}}\leq d^{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})}\mathscr{E}({\mathscr{D}})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\lambda_{i}}^{\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\lvert I_{j}\cap\{i\}\rvert}.

We now bound the product on the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
.
Telescoping, then using the properties (
21
) and (
24
) of
I_{j}
, then telescoping again, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\lambda_{i}}^{\sum_{j}q_{j}\lvert I_{j}\cap\{i\}\rvert}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}^{\sum_{j}q_{j}\lvert I_{j}\rvert}\prod_{k=1}^{d-1}\left(\frac{\lambda_{k+1}}{\lambda_{k}}\right)^{\sum_{j}q_{j}\lvert I_{j}\cap(k,d]\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}^{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})}\prod_{k=1}^{d-1}\left(\frac{\lambda_{k+1}}{\lambda_{k}}\right)^{d-k-\beta}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}^{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})-d+1+\beta}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}\dotsm\lambda_{d-1}\lambda_{d}^{1-\beta}

Equations (

\frac{\prod_{j\in J}(\det A_{j})^{q_{j}}}{{\det M}}\leq d^{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})}\mathscr{E}({\mathscr{D}})^{2}\lambda_{1}^{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})-d+\beta}\lambda_{d}^{-\beta}.

Now since the Gaussian input
(A_{j})_{j}
is dominated by

we have
\lambda_{1}\leq N({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)
.
On the other hand,
\lambda_{d}\geq\bigl\lVert T^{-1}\bigr\rVert^{-1}
.
This concludes the proof of (

∎

In the above proof, we used the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
H
be a Euclidean space, let
Q\in\End(H)
be a positive semi-definite symmetric endomorphism, let
(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{d})
be a basis of
H
.
Then

\det Q\leq\|\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\varepsilon_{d}\|^{-2}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\langle Q\varepsilon_{k},\varepsilon_{k}\rangle.

Proof.

If
(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{d})
is an orthonormal basis, then
\|\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\varepsilon_{d}\|=1
and the result follows by the Choleski decomposition, which states that
Q=F^{*}F
where
F
is upper triangular in the basis
(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{d})
. For the general case, we write
(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{d})=A(\varepsilon^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\varepsilon^{\prime}_{d})
where
A\in\GL_{d}(\mathbb{R})
and the
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{i}
form an orthonormal basis. We then have

\det Q=(\det A)^{-2}\det A^{*}QA\leq\|\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\varepsilon_{d}\|^{-2}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\langle A^{*}QA\varepsilon^{\prime}_{k},\varepsilon^{\prime}_{k}\rangle,

hence the result.
∎

4.
Lower bound for localised regularised data

In this section we show
1.5
, which states a lower bound for localised regularised Brascamp-Lieb constants.

Proof of
1.5
.

Consider a collection
(A_{j})_{j\in J}
of positive definite symmetric endomorphisms
A_{j}\in\End(H_{j})
with
A_{j}\leq\id_{H_{j}}
. The localised regularised Brascamp-Lieb inequality with test functions
f_{j}=\chi_{A_{j}}
(definition in (
8
)) yields by direct computation

(28)

\BL({\mathscr{D}},\mathbf{R},T)\geq\left(\frac{\prod_{j\in J}(\det A_{j})^{q_{j}}}{\det\left(T+\sum\nolimits_{j\in J}q_{j}\ell_{j}^{*}A_{j}\ell_{j}\right)}\right)^{1/2}.

The strategy is to choose the
A_{j}
so that the right-hand side of (
28
) has the desired lower bound.

By
Lemma

2.2
, for each
j\in J
, there exists
E_{j}\in\Gr(H_{j})
of dimension
\dim E_{j}=\rk_{\alpha}(\ell_{j}\mid W)
such that

\ell_{j}B^{W}_{1}\subseteq B^{H_{j}}_{\alpha}+E_{j}.

Let
A_{j}\in\End(H_{j})
be the positive definite symmetric endomorphism whose square root is

A_{j}^{1/2}=\alpha\id_{E_{j}}\oplus\id_{E_{j}^{\perp}}.

Observe that
A_{j}\leq\id_{H_{j}}
and
\det(A_{j})^{1/2}=\alpha^{\rk_{\alpha}(\ell_{j}\mid W)}
. It remains to bound from above the determinant of
T+\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\ell_{j}^{*}A_{j}\ell_{j}
. We assume from the start that
E_{j}
has the property described in the remark after
Lemma

2.2
.
In particular, for any unit vector
w\in W
, we can decompose
w=w^{\prime}+w^{\prime\prime}
into orthogonal vectors satisfying
\ell_{j}w^{\prime}\in E_{j}
,
\ell_{j}w^{\prime\prime}\in E_{j}^{\perp}
,
\lVert\ell_{j}w^{\prime}\rVert\leq C\lVert w^{\prime}\rVert
and
\lVert\ell_{j}w^{\prime\prime}\rVert\leq\alpha\lVert w^{\prime\prime}\rVert
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\langle\ell_{j}^{*}A_{j}\ell_{j}w,w\rangle

\displaystyle=\lVert A_{j}^{1/2}\ell_{j}w\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert\alpha\ell_{j}w^{\prime}+\ell_{j}w^{\prime\prime}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\lVert\ell_{j}w^{\prime}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\ell_{j}w^{\prime\prime}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{2}C^{2}\lVert w^{\prime}\rVert^{2}+\alpha^{2}\lVert w^{\prime\prime}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{2}C^{2}

On the other hand, for every unit vector
w_{\perp}\in W^{\perp}
, we have

\langle\ell_{j}^{*}A_{j}\ell_{j}w_{\perp},w_{\perp}\rangle=\lVert A_{j}^{1/2}\ell_{j}w_{\perp}\rVert^{2}\leq C^{2}.

Consider now an orthonormal basis
(e_{1},\dotsc,e_{d})
of
H
, obtain by joining an orthonormal basis of
W
with one of
W^{\perp}
.
Using
Lemma

3.2
, the above inequalities, then
C\geq 1\geq\alpha
, we find

\displaystyle\det\left(T+\sum\nolimits_{j\in J}q_{j}\ell_{j}^{*}A_{j}\ell_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left\langle Te_{i}+\sum\nolimits_{j\in J}q_{j}\ell_{j}^{*}A_{j}\ell_{j}e_{i},\,e_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\Big(t+\sum\nolimits_{j\in J}q_{j}\alpha^{2}C^{2}\Bigr)^{\dim W}\left(t+\sum\nolimits_{j\in J}q_{j}C^{2}\right)^{\dim W^{\perp}}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{2\dim W}\left((C/\alpha)^{2}t+C^{2}\sum\nolimits_{j\in J}q_{j}\right)^{d}.

By combination with (
28
), this yields the announced lower bound.
∎

5.
Bounds on Brascamp-Lieb constants

We derive the estimates on
\BL({\mathscr{D}})
from their counterparts for
\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)
.

5.1.
Brascamp-Lieb constants as limits

It is well known that
\BL({\mathscr{D}})
can be expressed as a limit of localised regularised Brascamp-Lieb constants. We recall this standard fact.

First, we see how to pass to the limit to remove the truncation by
T
. Let
{\mathscr{D}}=((\ell_{j})_{j\in J},(q_{j})_{j\in J})
be a Brascamp-Lieb datum, and
{\bf{R}}=(R_{j})_{j\in J}
a collection such that each
R_{j}
is a positive definite symmetric endomorphism of
H_{j}
.
Define the regularised Brascamp-Lieb constant
\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}})
to be
\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}})=\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},0)
. More formally, it is the smallest number
\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}})
such that

\int_{H}\prod_{j\in J}(f_{j}\circ\ell_{j})^{q_{j}}\leq\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}})\prod_{j\in J}\left(\int_{H_{j}}f_{j}\right)^{q_{j}},

for all inputs
(f_{j})_{j\in J}
of functions satisfying that
f_{j}
is of type
R_{j}
for each
j\in J
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Given a regularised Brascamp-Lieb datum
({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}})
, we have

\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}})=\lim_{T\to 0}\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T),

where
T\to 0
means that
T
converges to
0
in
\End(H)
while staying symmetric and positive definite.

Proof.

This is a simple application of Fatou’s Lemma. ∎

Next, we get rid of the regularisation.

Lemma 5.2

(
[
7
, Eq. (44)]
)
.

Given a regularised Brascamp-Lieb datum
({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}})
, we have

\BL({\mathscr{D}})=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\BL({\mathscr{D}},t{\bf{R}}),

where
t{\bf{R}}
is the coordinate-wise scalar multiplication of
{\bf{R}}
by
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

5.2.
Proof of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2

We can now justify the upper bound
1.1
and the lower bound
1.2
on Brascamp-Lieb constants

Proof of
1.1
.

Note that being globally critical and

-perceptive implies in particular that

hence

for every

.
This allows us to apply

with

and for any regularisation

and localisation

.
As

and

.

, we see this inequality also holds for

Proof of

It follows from

,

6.
Visual inequality

We prove the visual inequality announced in the introduction.

Proof of
1.6
.

We may assume that
A
is a union of balls of radius
\delta
.
Let
\mathbf{R}=(R_{j})_{j\in J}
by given by
R_{j}=\delta^{-2}\Id_{H_{j}}
, and
T=\Id_{H}
. Set
f_{j}=\mathbbm{1}_{\pi_{H_{j}}(A)}*\mathscr{N}_{R_{j}}
(it can be seen as a mollification of the projection
\pi_{H_{j}}(A)
at scale
\delta
).
Observe that

\mathbbm{1}_{A}\ll O_{d}(1)^{\sum_{j}q_{j}}\prod_{j\in J}(f_{j}\circ\pi_{H_{j}})^{q_{j}}\,\chi_{T}.

Integrating over
H
, then using the definition of
\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)
and that each
f_{j}
is of type

, we obtain

(29)

{\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}(A)\delta^{d}\ll_{d}O_{d}(1)^{\sum_{j}q_{j}}\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)\prod_{j\in J}({\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}(\pi_{H_{j}}A)\delta^{\dim H_{j}})^{q_{j}}.

Using
1.4
, we may further bound

(30)

\BL({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)\leq d^{\frac{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})}{2}}\mathscr{E}({\mathscr{D}})\prod_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{-q_{j}\dim H_{j}}N({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)^{\frac{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})-d+\beta}{2}}.

and

, the last term in (

) is dominated by

(31)

N({\mathscr{D}},{\bf{R}},T)^{\frac{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})-d+\beta}{2}}\leq\Bigl(1+\sum\nolimits_{j\in J}q_{j}\Bigr)^{\frac{\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})-d+\beta}{2}}\delta^{-\mathscr{A}({\mathscr{D}})+d-\beta}.

Combining (

), (
30
), (
31
), and cancelling out the terms

, we obtain the inequality announced in

.
∎

Remark.

It follows from this proof that the exponent

is optimal. Indeed, if

were to hold with a smaller exponent, say

, then the above argument would yield (

) with the term

replaced by

, but such an inequality clearly fails as noticed earlier in §

.

Remark.

Instead of considering the

-covering number of a bounded subset of

, we may zoom out and reduce to the

-covering number of a dilated set, that is,

.

using this time a fixed resolution

Appendix A
On separating geometry and distortion

We provide an example where the variant (
7
) of
1.1
mentioned in the introduction is more efficient than the original upper bound in
1.1
.

Example
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1]
be a parameter.
Consider the Brascamp-Lieb datum
{\mathscr{D}}_{\lambda}=\bigl((\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}),(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\bigr)
where the
\ell_{j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
are given by:
\forall x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\displaystyle\ell_{1}(x)=(x_{2},x_{3})\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988pt\ell_{2}(x)=(x_{1},x_{3})\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988pt\ell_{3}(x)=(x_{1},\lambda x_{2}).

For
\lambda=1
,
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}
is the Loomis-Whitney datum from
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
It is known that
\BL({\mathscr{D}}_{1})=1
(see e.g.
[
7
, Example 1.6]
).
For general
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, a simple change of variables shows that

\BL({\mathscr{D}}_{\lambda})=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\BL({\mathscr{D}}_{1})=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Note that the configuration of the kernels
\Ker\ell_{j}
from
{\mathscr{D}}_{\lambda}
is independent of
\lambda
, being that of a Loomis-Whitney scenario.
The variation of
\BL({\mathscr{D}}_{\lambda})
in
\lambda
is only due to the distortion of
\ell_{3}
.

We can apply the variant (
7
) to
{\mathscr{D}}_{\lambda}
.
The distortion term
\Upsilon({\mathscr{D}}_{\lambda})
is precisely
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
Other terms in the upper bound (
7
) are independent of
\lambda
. In fact
({\mathscr{D}}_{\lambda})_{\mathrm{proj}}
is

(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
-perceptive if and only if
\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{3}^{2}<1
.
The variant (
7
) then gives
\BL({\mathscr{D}}_{\lambda})\leq 3^{3}2^{\frac{3}{2}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Although not sharp, it captures the correct asymptotic in
\lambda
.

On the contrary, if we use
1.1
directly instead, we obtain the wrong asymptotic.
Indeed, testing the requirement (
4
) with
W=\mathbb{R}(0,1,0)
, we see that for
{\mathscr{D}}_{\lambda}
to be
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
-perceptive, we need at least
\alpha_{3}<\lambda
.
This results in the upper bound of
1.1
having the term
\alpha_{3}^{-q_{3}\dim H_{3}}>\lambda^{-1}
. Hence the claim.

In conclusion, the upper bound variant (
7
) for Brascamp-Lieb constants can be more relevant than the original
1.1
when dealing with linear maps which are far from being projectors.

EKR-Type Theorems for Pendant Graph Constructions
Michael Carrion, Melissa M. Fuentes, Zaphenath Joseph, Alexander Nappo

Abstract
The classical Erdős–Ko–Rado (EKR) theorem characterizes the maximum size of intersecting families of r r -element subsets of an n n -element set. We study EKR-type questions for independent r r -sets in pendant graph constructions, obtained by attaching to each base vertex a clique of prescribed size. Our contributions are threefold. We give an alternate and purely combinatorial proof (via shifting and shadows) that the pendant complete graph K n ∗ K_{n}^{*} is r r -EKR for n ≥ 2 ​ r n\geq 2r , and strictly so for n > 2 ​ r n>2r , recovering a result of De Silva, Dionne, Dunkelberg, and Harris. We extend this to generalized pendant complete graphs , where every base vertex in the clique supports a clique of arbitrary size, proving that that generalized pendant complete graphs are r r -EKR whenever n ≥ 2 ​ r n\geq 2r . For pendant paths P n ∗ P_{n}^{*} , we provide elementary constructions showing that P n ∗ P_{n}^{*} is not ( n − k ) (n-k) -EKR when n ≥ 3 ​ k + 2 n\geq 3k+2 for k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , not ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -EKR for n ≥ 6 n\geq 6 , and not n n -EKR for n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 . These results fit naturally into the Holroyd–Talbot perspective relating r r -EKR thresholds to independence parameters and supply tools for further pendant constructions.



1
Introduction

The classical Erdős–Ko–Rado (EKR) theorem is a cornerstone of extremal combinatorics, describing the structure of intersecting families of uniform sets. In graph-theoretic extensions, one studies independent
r
-sets of a graph
G
and asks when stars are extremal. Following Holroyd, Spencer, and Talbot
[
5
]
, we say that
G
has the
r
-EKR property
if every largest intersecting subfamily of independent
r
-sets is an
r
-star (that is, all members contain a common vertex).

Throughout, graphs are finite and simple. For a graph
G
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{I}^{(r)}(G)
denote the family of independent
r
-subsets of
V(G)
. A subfamily
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{(r)}(G)
is
intersecting
if every two members have nonempty intersection. For
v\in V(G)
, we write
\mathcal{I}^{(r)}_{v}(G):=\{A\in\mathcal{I}^{(r)}(G):v\in A\}
and refer to this as the
r
-star centered at
v
. We also write
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
.

In this work we focus on
pendant
constructions. Given a graph
G
with vertex set
V(G)=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
, the
pendant graph

G^{*}
is obtained by adjoining a new pendant vertex
p_{i}
to each
v_{i}
:

V(G^{*})\;=\;V(G)\;\sqcup\;\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\},\qquad E(G^{*})\;=\;E(G)\;\sqcup\;\{\,v_{i}p_{i}:\;i\in[n]\,\}.

We call
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
the
pendant vertices
of
G^{*}
. More generally, for a sequence
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
of positive integers, the
generalized pendant graph

G^{\mathbf{s}}
is formed by attaching to
v_{i}
a clique
K_{s_{i}}
for each
i\in[n]
with vertex set
V(K_{s_{i}})=\{v^{i}_{1},\dots,v^{i}_{s_{i}}\}
, each adjacent to
v_{i}
and to no other base vertex:

V(G^{\mathbf{s}})\;=\;V(G)\;\sqcup\;\bigl(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}V(K_{s_{i}})\bigr),\qquad E(G^{\mathbf{s}})\;=\;E(G)\;\sqcup\;\bigl(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}E(K_{s_{i}})\bigr)\;\sqcup\;\{\,v_{i}v^{i}_{j}:\;i\in[n],\,1\leq j\leq s_{i}\,\}.

Let
\mathbf{1}=(1,\ldots,1)
be of length
n
. For
m\geq 1
let
m\mathbf{1}=(m,\dots,m)
and write
G^{m}:=G^{m\mathbf{1}}
; in particular
G^{*}=G^{\mathbf{1}}
.

Two benchmark families already illustrate the range of behaviors we study. For the pendant complete graph
K_{n}^{*}
, De Silva, Dionne, Dunkelberg, and Harris proved that
K_{n}^{*}
is
r
-EKR when
n\geq 2r
, and strictly so when
n>2r

[
2
, Theorem 4]
. We provide an alternative proof via shifting and shadow arguments. For pendant paths
P_{n}^{*}
, the largest
r
-stars are centered at
p_{2}
and
p_{n-1}
for all
1\leq r\leq n
, and
P_{n}^{*}
is not
n
-EKR for
n\geq 4

[
2
, Theorem 5 and Lemma 9]
. Building on these foundations, we extend the
K_{n}^{*}
analysis to generalized pendant complete graphs
K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}}
and also give explicit non-EKR constructions for
P_{n}^{*}
at high values of
r
.

Organization.

Section
2
gives a short, purely combinatorial proof that
K_{n}^{*}
is
r
-EKR for
n\geq 2r
(with strictness for
n>2r
). Section
3
extends this to generalized pendant complete graphs
K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}}
, proving
r
-EKR under the same threshold. Section
4
turns to pendant paths
P_{n}^{*}
and presents elementary constructions showing that
P_{n}^{*}
is not
(n-k)
-EKR when
n\geq 3k+2
for
k\geq 2
, not
(n-1)
-EKR for
n\geq 6
, and not
n
-EKR for
n\geq 4
. Section
5
outlines directions for further work, including pendant cycles and powers of paths or cycles.

2
Complete Pendant Graphs

The EKR property for the pendant complete graph
K_{n}^{*}
was established by De Silva, Dionne, Dunkelberg, and Harris
[
2
]
. Here we present an alternative proof using classical shifting and shadow techniques, inspired by Katona’s shadow bound
[
6
]
and a short proof of classical EKR due to Frankl and Füredi
[
4
]
. The approach is purely combinatorial.

Given a family
\mathcal{A}
of
r
-element sets and an integer
s
, the
s
-shadow
of
\mathcal{A}
is

\partial_{s}\mathcal{A}\;=\;\{\,S:\ |S|=s\text{ and }S\subseteq A\text{ for some }A\in\mathcal{A}\,\}.

Shadows can be used to bound intersecting families via the following result of Katona.

Theorem 1

(Katona,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
a,b
be integers with
0\leq b\leq a
, and let
\mathcal{A}
be a family of
a
-element sets such that

|A\cap A^{\prime}|\geq b
for all
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
. Then

|\mathcal{A}|\ \leq\ |\partial_{\,a-b}\mathcal{A}|\,.

Theorem 2
.

For all
n\geq 2r
, the pendant complete graph
K_{n}^{*}
is
r
-EKR. Moreover, when
n>2r
, equality holds only for an
r
-star centered at a pendant vertex.

Proof.

Write
V(K_{n}^{*})=V(K_{n})\sqcup P
with
V(K_{n})=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
and
P=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
, where
p_{i}
is pendant to
v_{i}
. Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{(r)}(K_{n}^{*})
be intersecting. We prove

|\mathcal{A}|\ \leq\ r\binom{n-1}{r-1},

with the indicated equality condition for
n>2r
.

For
i\in[n]
define the shift

S_{i}(A)\;=\;\begin{cases}(A\setminus\{v_{i}\})\cup\{p_{i}\},&\text{if }v_{i}\in A\text{ and }(A\setminus\{v_{i}\})\cup\{p_{i}\}\notin\mathcal{A},\\
A,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Iterating
S_{1},\dots,S_{n}
we may assume
\mathcal{A}
is
shift-stable
, i.e.,
S_{i}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}
for all
i
.

Since
K_{n}
is a clique, any independent
r
-set contains at most one base vertex
v_{i}
. Partition
\mathcal{A}
into sets

\mathcal{A}_{0}\;=\;\{A\in\mathcal{A}:\ A\cap V(K_{n})=\emptyset\}\text{ and }\mathcal{A}_{1}\;=\;\{A\in\mathcal{A}:\ |A\cap V(K_{n})|=1\}.

The classical EKR Theorem (
[
3
]
) yields

|\mathcal{A}_{0}|\ \leq\ \binom{n-1}{r-1}

(1)

for
n\geq 2r
.

Each
A\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
has the form
A=A^{\prime}\cup\{v_{i}\}
with
A^{\prime}\subseteq P
and
|A^{\prime}|=r-1
. Define

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}\;=\;\{\,A\setminus\{v\}:\ A\in\mathcal{A}_{1},\ v\in A\cap V(K_{n})\,\}.

Claim 1
.

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
is intersecting.

Proof.

Suppose
A,B\in\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
with
A\cap B=\emptyset
. Then
A\cup\{v_{i}\},\,B\cup\{v_{j}\}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
for some
i,j
. If
i\neq j
, then
(A\cup\{v_{i}\})\cap(B\cup\{v_{j}\})=\emptyset
, contradicting that
\mathcal{A}_{1}
is intersecting. Hence
i=j
. By shift-stability,
(B\cup\{v_{i}\}\setminus\{v_{i}\})\cup\{p_{i}\}=B\cup\{p_{i}\}\in\mathcal{A}
. However,

(A\cup\{v_{i}\})\cap(B\cup\{p_{i}\})=\emptyset

since
A\cap B=\emptyset
,
v_{i}\notin B\cup\{p_{i}\}
, and
p_{i}\notin A\cup\{v_{i}\}
, again a contradiction. Thus
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
is intersecting.
∎

Fix
p_{1}\in P
and put
P^{\prime}=P\setminus\{p_{1}\}
. Form the partition

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}\;=\;\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}\ \cup\ \overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}},

where

\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}=\{X\in\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}:\,p_{1}\in X\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}}=\{X\in\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}:\,p_{1}\notin X\}.

Define

\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\{X\setminus\{p_{1}\}:X\in\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}\}\,\text{ and }\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}}=\{\,P^{\prime}\setminus X:\ X\in\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}}\,\}.

Note that sets in
\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}
are of size
r-2
and sets in
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
are of size
n-r
. Clearly,
|\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}|=|\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}|
and
|\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}}|=|\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}}|
.

Claim 2
.

Every two sets of
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
have intersection size at least
b:=n-2r+2
.

Proof.

Take
A,B\in\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
and let
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}}
correspond so that
A=P^{\prime}\setminus A^{\prime}
and
B=P^{\prime}\setminus B^{\prime}
. Then

|A\cap B|=|P^{\prime}\setminus(A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime})|=|P^{\prime}|-|A^{\prime}|-|B^{\prime}|+|A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime}|=(n-1)-2(r-1)+|A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime}|.

By Claim
1
,
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}}
is intersecting, so
|A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime}|\geq 1
. Hence
|A\cap B|\geq n-2r+2
, as claimed.
∎

Applying Theorem
1
to the
a
-uniform family
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
with
a=n-r
and
b=n-2r+2
(so
a-b=r-2
) gives

|\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}}|\ \leq\ |\partial_{\,r-2}(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}})|.

Claim 3
.

\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}\,\cap\,\partial_{\,r-2}(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Suppose
X\in\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}
and
U\in\partial_{\,r-2}(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}})
. Then
X\subseteq P^{\prime}\setminus B^{\prime}
for some
B^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}}
, so
X\cap B^{\prime}=\emptyset
. However,
X\cup\{p_{1}\}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
, with
(X\cup\{p_{1}\})\cap B^{\prime}=\emptyset
, contradicting Claim
1
.
∎

Both families
\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}
and
\partial_{\,r-2}(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}})
consist of
(r-2)
-subsets of
P^{\prime}
, and by Claim
3
, they are disjoint. Therefore,

|\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}|\;+\;|\partial_{\,r-2}(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}})|\ \leq\ \binom{n-1}{r-2}.

Thus,

|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}|\;=\;|\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}|+|\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}}|\;=\;|\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}|+|\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}}|\ \leq\ |\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}|+|\partial_{\,r-2}(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime\prime}})|\ \leq\ \binom{n-1}{r-2}.

Each
(r-1)
-set in
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
forbids its corresponding
r-1
base vertices in
V(K_{n})
, so it extends to an independent
r
-set in at most
n-r+1
ways by adding one base vertex. Hence,

|\mathcal{A}_{1}|\ \leq\ (n-r+1)\,|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}|\ \leq\ (n-r+1)\binom{n-1}{r-2}.

Finally,

|\mathcal{A}|\;=\;|\mathcal{A}_{0}|+|\mathcal{A}_{1}|\ \leq\ \binom{n-1}{r-1}+(n-r+1)\binom{n-1}{r-2}\;=\;r\binom{n-1}{r-1}.

If
n>2r
, the EKR inequalities used for
|\mathcal{A}_{0}|
in (
1
) and for the shadow-bound component are strict unless the respective families are stars; tracing back through the equalities forces
\mathcal{A}
to be exactly an
r
-star centered at a pendant vertex. When
n=2r
, non-star extremals can occur (as in classical EKR), so we do not claim strictness in that boundary case. This matches the behavior described in
[
2
]
in Theorem 4.
∎

3
Generalized Pendant Complete Graphs

In this section, we extend the
K_{n}^{*}
result to pendant constructions over a complete base with larger pendant cliques.
Given
K_{n}
and a sequence
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
of positive integers, the
generalized pendant complete graph

K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}}
is formed by attaching to each
v_{i}\in V(K_{n})
a clique
K_{s_{i}}
; the vertices of
K_{s_{i}}
are adjacent to
v_{i}
and to no other base vertex. The special cases are
K_{n}^{*}=K_{n}^{\mathbf{1}}
and
K_{n}^{m}:=K_{n}^{m\mathbf{1}}
.

We will use the following result of Bollobás and Leader for the disjoint union of
n
complete graphs
K_{m}
, which we denote by
nK_{m}
.

Theorem 3

(Bollobás–Leader
[
1
]
)
.

Let
m\geq 2
and
1\leq r\leq n
. If
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{(r)}(nK_{m})
is intersecting, then

|\mathcal{A}|\ \leq\ m^{\,r-1}\binom{n-1}{r-1}\,.

Theorem 4
.

Let
m\geq 2
. Then the generalized pendant complete graph
K_{n}^{m}
is
r
-EKR for all
n\geq 2r
.
In particular, the case
m=1
is
K_{n}^{*}
and follows from Theorem
2
.

Proof.

Label the vertices of the pendant clique at
v_{i}
by
V(K_{m})=\{v_{1}^{i},\dots,v_{m}^{i}\}
. Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{(r)}(K_{n}^{m})
be intersecting.

For each base vertex
v_{i}
, define a shift

S_{i}(A)=\begin{cases}(A\setminus\{v_{i}\})\cup\{v_{1}^{i}\},&\text{if }v_{i}\in A\text{ and }(A\setminus\{v_{i}\})\cup\{v_{1}^{i}\}\notin\mathcal{A},\\
A,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

After iterating
S_{1},\dots,S_{n}
, assume
\mathcal{A}
is shift-stable. Partition
\mathcal{A}
into two sets,

\mathcal{A}_{0}=\{A\in\mathcal{A}:\ A\cap V(K_{n})=\emptyset\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{A}_{1}=\{A\in\mathcal{A}:\ |A\cap V(K_{n})|=1\}.

On the pendant layer
nK_{m}
, Theorem
3
gives

|\mathcal{A}_{0}|\ \leq\ m^{\,r-1}\binom{n-1}{r-1}.

For each
A\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
remove its unique base vertex to obtain an
(r-1)
-set in
nK_{m}
:

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}=\{A\setminus\{v\}:\ A\in\mathcal{A}_{1},\ v\in A\cap V(K_{n})\}\ \subseteq\mathcal{I}^{(r-1)}(nK_{m}).

Claim 4
.

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
is intersecting.

Proof.

If
A,B\in\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
were disjoint, there exist
i,j
with
A\cup\{v_{i}\},B\cup\{v_{j}\}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
. If
i\neq j
these are disjoint, contradicting that
\mathcal{A}
is intersecting. If
i=j
, shift-stability implies
A\cup\{v_{1}^{i}\}\in\mathcal{A}
. Then

(A\cup\{v_{1}^{i}\})\cap(B\cup\{v_{i}\})=\emptyset,

which is a contradiction.
∎

Applying Theorem
3
to
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{(r-1)}(nK_{m})
yields

|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}|\ \leq\ m^{\,r-2}\binom{n-1}{r-2}.

Each
A\in\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
forbids exactly the
r-1
base vertices paired with its chosen pendants, so
A
extends to a member of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
in at most
n-r+1
ways. Hence

|\mathcal{A}_{1}|\ \leq\ (n-r+1)\,|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}|\ \leq\ (n-r+1)\,m^{\,r-2}\binom{n-1}{r-2}.

Summing gives

|\mathcal{A}|\ =\ |\mathcal{A}_{0}|+|\mathcal{A}_{1}|\ \leq\ m^{\,r-1}\binom{n-1}{r-1}+(n-r+1)\,m^{\,r-2}\binom{n-1}{r-2}=m^{\,r-2}(m+r-1)\binom{n-1}{r-1}.

Consider the
r
-star at a canonical pendant, say
v_{1}^{1}
. By partitioning according to whether or not a set contains a base vertex, we have

|\mathcal{I}^{(r)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n}^{m})|=m^{\,r-1}\binom{n-1}{r-1}\;+\;(n-1)\,m^{\,r-2}\binom{n-2}{r-2}\;=\;m^{\,r-2}(m+r-1)\binom{n-1}{r-1},

so the bound is tight and
K_{n}^{m}
is
r
-EKR for
n\geq 2r
.
∎

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
be a sequence of positive integers and
n\geq 2r
. Then
K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}}
is
r
-EKR.

Proof.

By relabeling the base vertices, assume
s_{1}\leq\cdots\leq s_{n}
, and within the largest pendant clique
K_{s_{n}}
fix distinct vertices
v_{1}^{n},v_{2}^{n}
(if all
s_{i}
are equal, then
K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}}=K_{n}^{m}
and the result follows from Theorem
4
together with the
m=1
case from Theorem
2
). Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{(r)}(K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}})
be intersecting.

Define a
local
shift
T
on sets in
\mathcal{A}
by

T(A)=\begin{cases}(A\setminus\{v_{1}^{n}\})\cup\{v_{2}^{n}\},&\text{if }v_{1}^{n}\in A\text{ and }(A\setminus\{v_{1}^{n}\})\cup\{v_{2}^{n}\}\notin\mathcal{A},\\
A,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Replacing
\mathcal{A}
by
T(\mathcal{A})
if necessary, we may assume
T(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}
(shift-stable within
K_{s_{n}}
).

Partition
\mathcal{A}
by the presence of
v_{1}^{n}
:

\mathcal{A}_{0}=\{A\in\mathcal{A}:\ v_{1}^{n}\notin A\},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{1}=\{A\in\mathcal{A}:\ v_{1}^{n}\in A\},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}=\{A\setminus\{v_{1}^{n}\}:\ A\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\}.

Let
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n}-1)
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n-1})
.

Claim 5
.

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{(r-1)}\!\bigl(K_{n-1}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}}\bigr)
is intersecting.

Proof.

Each
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
is an
(r-1)
-set in
K_{n-1}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}}
since we delete the single vertex
v_{1}^{n}
.
If
A_{1}^{\prime},A_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
were disjoint, let
A_{i}=A_{i}^{\prime}\cup\{v_{1}^{n}\}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
(
i=1,2
). By
T
-stability,
A_{1}^{\prime}\cup\{v_{2}^{n}\}\in\mathcal{A}
. But then

\bigl(A_{1}^{\prime}\cup\{v_{2}^{n}\}\bigr)\cap\bigl(A_{2}^{\prime}\cup\{v_{1}^{n}\}\bigr)=\emptyset,

contradicting that
\mathcal{A}
is intersecting.
∎

Claim 6
.

For any pendant
v_{1}^{1}
,

\bigl|\mathcal{I}^{(r)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}})\bigr|\;=\;\bigl|\mathcal{I}^{(r)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}})\bigr|\;+\;\bigl|\mathcal{I}^{(r-1)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n-1}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}})\bigr|.

Proof.

Partition
\mathcal{I}^{(r)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}})
by whether
v_{1}^{n}
is present. Removing
v_{1}^{n}
gives a bijection from the subfamily containing
v_{1}^{n}
to
\mathcal{I}^{(r-1)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n-1}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}})
, while the subfamily avoiding
v_{1}^{n}
is precisely
\mathcal{I}^{(r)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}})
.
∎

We now argue by double induction on
(r,\sum_{i}s_{i})
(lexicographic order). The case
r=1
is trivial. If
s_{1}=\cdots=s_{n}
, the result follows from Theorem
4
(and Theorem
2
when
s_{i}=1
for every
i
). Otherwise
s_{n}\geq 2
and we apply the local shift
T
above.

Since
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is an intersecting family of
r
-sets in
K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}
is an intersecting family of
(r-1)
-sets in
K_{n-1}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}}
by Claim
5
, the inductive hypothesis gives

|\mathcal{A}_{0}|\ \leq\ \bigl|\mathcal{I}^{(r)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}})\bigr|\,,\qquad|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}|\ \leq\ \bigl|\mathcal{I}^{(r-1)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n-1}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}})\bigr|.

Since
|\mathcal{A}|=|\mathcal{A}_{0}|+|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime}|
, Claim
6
yields

|\mathcal{A}|\ \leq\ \bigl|\mathcal{I}^{(r)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}})\bigr|+\bigl|\mathcal{I}^{(r-1)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n-1}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}})\bigr|\ =\ \bigl|\mathcal{I}^{(r)}_{v_{1}^{1}}(K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}})\bigr|,

and hence,
K_{n}^{\mathbf{s}}
is
r
-EKR.
∎

4
Counterexample for Pendant Path Graphs

In contrast to the EKR behavior observed in pendant complete graphs and their generalizations, the EKR property does not hold universally for all pendant constructions. In this section, we demonstrate that the pendant path graph
P_{n}^{*}
fails to be
(n-k)
-EKR when
n\geq 3k+2
and
k\geq 2
(we treat the
k=0
and
k=1
cases separately using subtly different constructions). Specifically, we construct an intersecting family of independent sets of size
n-k
whose size exceeds that of the star family centered at
p_{2}
in
P_{n}^{*}
, which De Silva et al. (
[
2
]
) proved is the largest star center.

We acknowledge De Silva et al. had previously proven the pendant path graph fails to be
n
-EKR. However, we provide an alternate construction in Theorem
8
. Our approach involves partitioning based on the value of
k
, and in each case, we exhibit explicit families that augment a standard leaf-centered star into a strictly larger intersecting family.

Theorem 6
.

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
. If
n\geq 3k+2
, then the pendant path graph
P_{n}^{*}
is
not

(n-k)
‑EKR.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{2}:=\mathcal{I}^{(n-k)}_{p_{2}}(P_{n}^{*})
, which is a largest
(n-k)
‑star by
[
2
, Theorem 5]
. Define the single
(n-k)
‑set

T_{k}\ :=\ \{p_{k+1},p_{k+2},\dots,p_{n}\}\ \in\ \mathcal{I}^{(n-k)}(P_{n}^{*}).

We claim that every
S\in\mathcal{L}_{2}
intersects
T_{k}
when
n\geq 3k+2
. Suppose not. Then
S\cap T_{k}=\emptyset
, so
S
uses no pendants among indices
k+1,\dots,n
. Consequently, all vertices of
S
with indices in
\{k+1,\dots,n\}
must lie in the base set
\{x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.

The subgraph induced by
\{x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n}\}
is a path on
n-k
vertices, whose independence number is
\alpha=\lceil(n-k)/2\rceil
. From indices
1,\dots,k
, the set
S
contains
p_{2}
and may include at most
k-1
other pendants (one from each remaining base–pendant pair). Hence,

|S|\ \leq\ 1+(k-1)+\Big\lceil\frac{n-k}{2}\Big\rceil\ =\ k+\Big\lceil\frac{n-k}{2}\Big\rceil.

Under
n\geq 3k+2
we have
\lceil(n-k)/2\rceil\leq(n-k)/2
and therefore

k+\Big\lceil\frac{n-k}{2}\Big\rceil\ \leq\ k+\frac{n-k}{2}\ <\ n-k,

contradicting that
|S|=n-k
. Thus
S\cap T_{k}\neq\emptyset
for every
S\in\mathcal{L}_{2}
.

It follows that
\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{L}_{2}\cup\{T_{k}\}
is intersecting and strictly larger than the star
\mathcal{L}_{2}
(since
T_{k}\notin\mathcal{L}_{2}
). Hence,
P_{n}^{*}
is not
(n-k)
‑EKR for
n\geq 3k+2
,
k\geq 2
.
∎

We next treat the case
k=1
.

Theorem 7
.

For
n\geq 6
, the pendant path graph
P_{n}^{*}
is
not

(n-1)
‑EKR.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{2}:=\mathcal{I}^{(n-1)}_{p_{2}}(P_{n}^{*})
be a largest
(n-1)
‑star by
[
2
, Thm. 5]
. Define the single
(n-1)
‑set

T^{\prime}\ :=\ \{p_{1},p_{3},p_{4},\dots,p_{n}\}\ \in\ \mathcal{I}^{(n-1)}(P_{n}^{*}).

We claim that
S\cap T^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
for every
S\in\mathcal{L}_{2}
when
n\geq 6
. If
S\cap T^{\prime}=\emptyset
, then
S
contains
p_{2}
and
no other
pendant, so all remaining
n-2
vertices of
S
would have to lie in the base set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
. Since a path on
n
vertices has independence number
\lceil n/2\rceil
, we have

|S|\ \leq\ 1+\Big\lceil\frac{n}{2}\Big\rceil\ <\ n-1,

for all
n\geq 6
, a contradiction. Therefore
\mathcal{L}_{2}\cup\{T^{\prime}\}
is intersecting and strictly larger than the star, and
P_{n}^{*}
is not
(n-1)
‑EKR for
n\geq 6
.
∎

Finally we address the case
k=0
(i.e.,
r=n
).

Theorem 8
.

For all
n\geq 4
, the pendant path graph
P_{n}^{*}
is
not

n
‑EKR.

Proof.

Define

A\ :=\ \{x_{i}:\ i\ \text{odd}\}\ \cup\ \{p_{i}:\ i\ \text{even}\}\ \in\ \mathcal{I}^{(n)}(P_{n}^{*}).

Then
A
is independent: among the base vertices we select only
x_{i}
with odd
i
, which are pairwise nonadjacent in the path
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
, and each
p_{i}
is adjacent only to
x_{i}
. Let
A^{c}
be the set obtained by swapping the choice in each pair
\{x_{i},p_{i}\}
; explicitly,

A^{c}\ =\ \{x_{i}:\ i\ \text{even}\}\ \cup\ \{p_{i}:\ i\ \text{odd}\}\ \in\ \mathcal{I}^{(n)}(P_{n}^{*}).

Thus
A
and
A^{c}
are two
disjoint
independent
n
-sets.

We claim that if
S,T\in\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(P_{n}^{*})
are disjoint, then
T
is the
pairwise complement
of
S
(i.e., for each
i
,
S
contains exactly one of
\{x_{i},p_{i}\}
and
T
contains the other). Indeed, disjointness and
|S|=|T|=n
force
S\cup T
to contain exactly one vertex from each pair
\{x_{i},p_{i}\}
twice, hence one in
S
and the other in
T
. In particular, both
S
and
S^{c}
must be independent. On a path, this happens if and only if the set of indices where
S
chooses base vertices is an
alternating
(parity) subset (so that no two consecutive base vertices are chosen in either
S
or
S^{c}
). Consequently, up to swapping parity, the only disjoint pair within
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(P_{n}^{*})
is
\{A,A^{c}\}
.

Consider now the family

\mathcal{F}\ :=\ \mathcal{I}^{(n)}(P_{n}^{*})\ \setminus\ \{A^{c}\}.

By the preceding paragraph,
\mathcal{F}
is intersecting: any two disjoint members of
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(P_{n}^{*})
would have to be
A
and
A^{c}
, but
A^{c}\notin\mathcal{F}
.

Finally, compare
|\mathcal{F}|
with the size of an
n
‑star. With
\mathcal{L}_{2}:=\mathcal{I}^{(n)}_{p_{2}}(P_{n}^{*})
, note that
\mathcal{L}_{2}
contains only
n
‑sets that include
p_{2}
, whereas there are
at least two
independent
n
‑sets that avoid
p_{2}
, namely
A^{c}
and

C\ :=\ \{x_{2},x_{4}\}\ \cup\ \{p_{i}:\ i\notin\{2,4\}\}\ \in\ \mathcal{I}^{(n)}(P_{n}^{*})\qquad(n\geq 4),

since
x_{2}
and
x_{4}
are nonadjacent and each
p_{i}
is adjacent only to
x_{i}
. Hence

|\mathcal{L}_{2}|\ \leq\ \bigl|\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(P_{n}^{*})\bigr|\ -\ 2\quad\text{while}\quad|\mathcal{F}|\ =\ \bigl|\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(P_{n}^{*})\bigr|\ -\ 1,

so
|\mathcal{F}|>|\mathcal{L}_{2}|
. Therefore the star
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is not maximum among intersecting families of independent
n
‑sets, and
P_{n}^{*}
is not
n
‑EKR for all
n\geq 4
.
∎

5
Future Directions

This paper established the Erdős–Ko–Rado (EKR) property for pendant constructions over complete graphs and their generalizations, and demonstrated its failure in certain cases for pendant path graphs. Several natural directions for future research arise from these results.

One compelling question is whether the pendant path graph
P_{n}^{*}
satisfies the EKR property exactly for all
r\leq\mu(P_{n}^{*})/2=n/2
and fails as soon as
r>n/2
. In fact, we show that whenever
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 3k+2
, one can build an intersecting family larger than the star at
p_{2}
, proving that
P_{n}^{*}
is not
(n-k)
-EKR (including the cases
r=n
for
n\geq 4
,
r=n-1
for
n\geq 6
, and more generally
r=n-k\geq\lceil(2n+2)/3\rceil
). These explicit constructions refine and extend De Silva’s alternate counterexample and confirm that, while EKR holds for all
r\leq n/2
, it breaks down immediately above that half-independence threshold—fully aligning with the Holroyd–Talbot conjecture.

Beyond paths, it is natural to ask whether other pendant constructions satisfy similar extremal properties. One such example is the
pendant cycle graph

C_{n}^{*}
, formed by attaching a pendant vertex to each vertex of the cycle
C_{n}
. Determining for which values of
r
the graph
C_{n}^{*}
is
r
-EKR remains an open question and may require new ideas beyond those used for pendant paths and cliques.

Another direction involves studying powers of paths and cycles. The
k
-th power of a graph
G
, denoted
G^{k}
, connects each vertex to all others within distance
k
in the original graph. Holroyd, Spencer, and Talbot
[
5
]
proved that for certain values of
r
, the
k
-th powers of paths and cycles are
r
-EKR, particularly when
r\leq\alpha(G)
, where
\alpha(G)
is the independence number. However, a complete understanding of which values of
r
yield extremal star families in these powers is still lacking.

While these families share structural similarities, their EKR behavior appears to diverge in subtle but significant ways. The challenge ahead lies in identifying the precise graph features—such as symmetry, neighborhood overlap, and independence number—that govern whether stars are extremal. Developing techniques that can handle these nuances across broader classes of graphs remains a central problem in the study of EKR-type problems for families of independent sets in graphs.

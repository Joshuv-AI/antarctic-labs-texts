A lightweight cryptographic algorithm incorporating path coloring of cartesian product of graphs
Authors: P Shivapriya, K N Meera, Yuqing Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLabeling or coloring the vertices of a graph is called vertex labeling or coloring. The path coloring of a graph is one on which vertices at distances and 3 on a path are labeled with a minimum label difference of and 1 respectively and one such path exists between all pairs of vertices. The connection number, , is the minimum value of the greatest integer used in any viable path coloring of the graph. Finding the of a graph is highly non-trivial and the primary objective of this work is to determine the for the Cartesian product of any two graphs. An efficient and computationally simpler cryptographic algorithm is developed by using these concepts in cryptography. This work aims to implement this cryptographic algorithm to support devices with constrained storage and energy capacities. With these ideas, the article attempts to improve the scope of application of graph labeling in cryptographic algorithms. Progressing further, the work evaluates parameters such as key strength, key randomness and security of the encryption algorithm through four statistical tests conducted at a high level of confidence. The tests proved that the proposed method is best suited for applications requiring shorter but stronger keys, compared to the existing graph labeling methods, which are algorithmically complex.Keywords: Cartesian product; path coloring; Graph labeling; Lightweight cryptographySubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Applied mathematics

Introduction

Graph theory is a significant subject with diverse practical applications, of which graph labeling is an intriguing and relevant part. To gain a better understanding of the basic concepts of graph theory, one can refer to the reference1. The graphs deliberated upon in this article involve simple and undirected ones. Graph labeling can be described as assigning positive integers to vertices, edges, or both in a graph, while adhering to certain mathematical constraints or conditions. This article explicitly focuses on the distance-constrained labeling, the path coloring of graphs. It is a variant of the labeling, which was introduced to solve the Frequency Assignment Problem (FAP). The aim of FAP was the efficient allocation of frequencies to radio stations to minimize the interference between them and simultaneously reduce the usage of bandwidth. The initial model of this notion in graph theory was proposed in2. Most of the articles on labeling focus on obtaining results on the minimum span, where span is the highest label used, known as the labeling number. Later, the idea of interference occurring at more than two levels, prompted the development of new forms of the labeling, such as the labeling and labeling of graphs. The labeling for particular graph classes became the subject of research by various authors since its definition, whereas limited resources about labeling are present. Later, the relevance of a path meeting the labeling criteria for secure communication in security networks led to the formulation of the path coloring in3, which motivated the development of the path coloring in4 and path coloring in5. The major focus of these works was also to reduce the span and obtain the respective connection numbers and for a graph G. Similar results on the bounds of the span, called as radio mean labeling number of graphs, can be observed in the works6–8.

Although the primary intention of developing variants of the path coloring was to address issues in security networks, they also possess practical applications in cryptography, another pertinent subject in communication. One of the primary objectives in cryptography is to guarantee secure communication. The graph labeling approach is mostly credited with its ability to employ number sequences in disguise as graph elements, which is essential in different encryption techniques. Several ongoing research studies are being conducted regarding this particular combination. A few insights on implementing the path coloring in cryptography are provided in the works4,9. Other instances include10,11. The authors in these works utilize the path coloring to generate keys for the Triple DES encryption technique. Additionally, a test is provided to determine the strength of the key generated, using Entropy analysis in11. This test determines the strength of the key based on its randomness. Further, several graph theory researchers have presented different encryption techniques incorporating different graph labeling techniques. The authors have presented the combination of a digraph substitution cipher with the Even-odd harmonious labeling technique, where the substitution is performed using a digraph matrix in12. An encryption technique that combines symmetric and asymmetric cryptosystems is presented in13. Two matrices utilizing the Antimagic and Felicitous labeling are developed to produce numerous complex cipher-texts, enhancing the security during data transfer in14. A similar approach utilizing the Antimagic labeling technique with different encryption methods to enhance security is provided in15. Further, a key generation method incorporating the Super-H Antimagic Total Labeling(SHATL), where the authors focus on generating strong keys for the Vigenere Cipher, is explained in16. Moreover, the authors have claimed that the keys generated are strong as their keys are of larger lengths, constituting random integers. These works focused on including various graph labeling techniques in cryptographic algorithms, but have left the discussion on the efficacy of their methods unaddressed.

One common observation in the collaboration of graph labeling with cryptography is the impact of the complex nature of several algorithms on devices with limited computational and energy resources, such as IoT. Although a few lightweight cryptographic approaches in IoT can be observed in17,18, the issue is still unaddressed. Hence, to overcome such shortcomings, this work proposes a computationally simpler yet strong technique by leveraging the path coloring and value of the Cartesian product of graphs. The method creates effective keys with greater uncertainty or randomness by using only the integers 1, 3, 6 and 8, which is proved in “The lightweight cryptographic algorithm” section. Moreover, a cryptanalysis is conducted using a cipher-only attack on the proposed approach to strengthen the claim. Though several techniques exist, a filtering was conducted based on the closeness in the key and strength of the cipher-text generated, leaving only two methods, namely the Radio mean labeling and SHATL, to be compared with the proposed method. Four different hypothesis tests were conducted to prove the efficacy of the proposed method and are detailed in “Statistical analysis” section.

The article is structured into six sections, commencing with the Introduction in “Introduction” section. “Preliminaries” section includes all preliminary results and definitions for easy comprehension of the work. The mathematical findings on the path coloring of the Cartesian product are discussed in “Mathematical results” section. “Objective” section defines the research objective of the proposed work following which the system model is presented. A novel, lightweight cryptographic approach to overcome the challenges faced by certain devices when using traditional cryptographic methods is presented in “The lightweight cryptographic algorithm” section by leveraging the results of the path coloring obtained in “Mathematical results” section. The statistical analysis of the proposed method can also be found in “5” section. The conclusion and future scope follow this in “Conclusion” section.

Objective

The literature indicated multiple applications of the path coloring, mostly emphasizing cryptography. One of the unaddressed issues in the literature is the key strength, which is crucial in cryptography. The primary objective of the paper is to integrate the labeling technique into a cryptographic algorithm while addressing challenges related to efficient key management, key strength, and computational complexity by employing the path coloring.

The lightweight cryptographic algorithm

Cryptography has become an essential and indelible part of human life. Researchers primarily conduct studies to present complex and robust algorithms that tackle threats from adversaries or third parties. The demand for effective cryptographic algorithms for devices with constrained computing and energy resources has been overshadowed by the requirement for further improvement of complex algorithms. This claim is supported by research in the literature that employs graph labeling in cryptography to highlight concepts for strengthening complex cryptosystems. Moreover, a lightweight algorithm with efficient key management and low computational and energy requirements needs to be introduced.

The presented work suggests a cryptographic approach incorporating the idea of path coloring of the Cartesian product of graphs to meet the conditions above. Cryptanalysis

The study of examining and deciphering cryptographic systems, protocols and algorithms to reveal confidential data such as plain-text or an encryption key without authorization is known as cryptanalysis. It involves the identification of potential weaknesses in cryptographic algorithms and exploiting them to decode the encrypted data. A cryptographic algorithm becomes efficient if it fails to fall prey to certain cryptanalysis techniques. In this section, hypothesis testing is conducted to test whether the proposed algorithm can be broken under the conventional cryptanalysis, the cipher-only attack. This test is considered in particular due to the possibility of third-party interference during communication. Moreover, it is necessary to show that the cipher-texts thus obtained are not easy to break.

The strength of the cipher-text generated in the proposed approach is determined by two measures for conducting hypothesis testing, namely the Index of Coincidence(IoC) and Entropy analysis. The cipher-text can be easily hacked if a pattern is observed to repeat. Therefore, the IoC test is conducted, where a higher IoC value indicates some pattern repetition of characters. A lower value for IoC indicates less repetition. Similarly, Entropy analysis is a test usually conducted to check some random behavior of sequences, which is also used to test the uncertainty of the cipher-text.

This work prioritizes the communication of smaller texts requiring high security. Though several graph labeling approaches are applied in cryptography, a handful are available for comparison. The labeling technique having a higher similarity with the proposed approach is considered for comparison, namely, the SHATL in the Vigenere cipher16.

The testing is initially conducted on a sample of 30 words of length 6, which is extended up to length 10 and the results are provided in the Test Results. The IoC and Entropy values of the two methods are obtained and the results are provided in “Statistical analysis” section.

Conclusion

The work initially provided results on the path coloring of the Cartesian product of graphs giving . The Algorithm 1 assigns the same integers to the vertices irrespective of the graphs chosen by the sender and the receiver and this is an added advantage. As opposed to 32 or 64 bits for key storage, the proposed approach requires 2 or 4 bits, reducing the space requirement most suited for short-length keys. Also, the cryptanalysis is more difficult because the proposed work eliminates patterns and promotes unpredictability. Since the IoC of the proposed method is less than 0.1, attacks based on the frequency of letters are less successful. Moreover, a higher entropy of 0.9 ensures greater unpredictability. This combination of low IoC and high entropy makes cryptanalysis difficult. Furthermore, the unaddressed issues such as efficient key management, key strength and computational complexity are addressed in this work. Extending the above concept to other graph products like strong products, tensor products and so on is an open problem of great mathematical and cryptographic interest.

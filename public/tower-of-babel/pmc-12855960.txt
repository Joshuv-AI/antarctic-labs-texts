Synergistic comparison of ANN and ANFIS predictive modeling with experimental electrochemical performance data in CO₂ conversion
Authors: Anil Kumar Deepati, Movva Naga Swapna sri, Anusha Peyyala, Itha Veeranjaneyulu, Amaleswari Rajulapati, Hari Prasadarao Pydi, S Revathi, M Ramya
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis work systematically evaluated CO₂ conversion performance metrics across the recent high-impact literature by focusing on five key inputs; applied potential (AP), catalyst loading (CL), composition (CO), support (SU) and electrolyte (EL) and two outputs; Faradaic efficiency (FE) and current density (CD). Dataset Sixty-four series were evaluated to generate predictive models for FE and CD. Residual plots showed a good fit along the fitted axis with deviations from normality being quite small, confirming linear relationships between input and output. Histograms have shown most of the errors were on the ranges − 0.8 to 0.8 for FE and − 4 to 4 for CD, thus confirming that the error was normally distributed and improving prediction reliability. Optimal electrode with reference to SN ratio was determined to be AP2-CL1-CO1-SU 2-E12 (–1.29 V, 0.426 mg/cm², 10% of Cu-In, carbon papers support and 0.1 M KOH) as optimal for increasing Faradic efficiency and AP1-CL1-CO1-SU1-EL1 (–1.29 V, 0.426 mg/cm², 10% of Cu-In, graphene papers and 0.1 M KHCO3) as optimal for improving Current Density. ANOVA results showed that the catalyst loading and applied potential are two major factors influencing FE and CD.ANN (5-10-2) exhibited good prediction performance (overall R² = 0.93726). From the performance metrics, ANFIS exhibited low error values RMSE (0.0725 & 0.0525) and MAE (0.1411 & 0.0811) for both FE and CD when compared with ANN and also the corresponding ΔR² confirmed that ANFIS delivered the better predictive accuracy. This comparison demonstrated the strong predictive power of the ANFIS with respect to ANN, verifying the reliability of the modelling approach based on experimental results.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-34138-5.Keywords: Electrochemical process, Carbon dioxide, Sustainable production, Current density, Artificial neural network, ANFISSubject terms: Chemistry, Energy science and technology

Introduction

The growing emissions of greenhouse gases, including CO2 and CH4, intensify the greenhouse effect and contribute to global warming. The elevation of CO2 content significantly influences the increase in worldwide temperatures. Due to its significant eco-friendly effects, dropping carbon emissions without disturbing economic development has become the most urgent problem1,2.The electrochemical conversion of CO2 into valuable chemicals has gathered important consideration due to favourable features, including the usage of environmentally friendly electrolytes, the capacity to fine-tune product creation and the integration of renewable energy sources. Nevertheless, CO2RR faces many challenges, such as the existence of many concurrent reactions, more energy barriers and the struggle from the hydrogen evolution reaction3,4.CO2 is reduced electrochemically in different electrons and protons, with some molecules formed. However, the catalysts are far from being practical systems despite their impressive selectivity for each of the compounds5,6. The electrochemical parameters include the applied potential that dictates whether CO 2 is reduced to CO, methane or other products competing with the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER)7–10. CO₂RR is an environmentally sustainable process which mimics artificial photosynthesis for utilising renewable sources of energy to convert CO 2 into fuels and chemicals. In this system, CO₂ is fed into the cathode through a gas diffusion electrode (GDE), where it reacts with both electrons supplied by a renewable energy source and hydroxide ions (OH⁻) in the electrolyte to produce carbon-based products11,12. At the same time, water is oxidised to protons, electrons and oxygen (O₂), thus maintaining charge neutrality and enabling for continued operation of the system at the anode (Fig. 1). The electrolyte layer facilitates OH⁻ transport between the cathode and anode, preserving ionic conductivity and system stability13,14. Liu et al.15introduced a simple anodic corrosion technique for the synthesis of oxygen-rich CuO nanoplate arrays that develop during electrocatalysis, resulting in the formation of Cu/Cu2O interfaces. The catalyst exhibited outstanding performance, achieving Faradaic efficiency (84.5%) for the generation of C2H4, 27.6% ethylene energy efficiency and the co-electrolysis of CO2 with minimal O2 concentrations produces surface hydroxyl species that significantly augment the activity of copper-catalyzed CO2 electroreduction. Saad Sarfraz et al.16 have reported an efficient and stable electrocatalyst composed of Cu and Sn for CO2 reduction to CO. The bimetallic electrode was prepared by electrodepositing tin species onto the oxide-derived copper (OD-Cu) surface. An optimum amount of addition of Sn achieved Faradaic efficiency of 90% and current density of 1.0 mA cm–2 toward carbon monoxide (CO).In study17–19, For cost-competitive CO2 electrolysis, a high current density was required with increased selectivity, reduced overpotential and longer working time. Current density is a vital parameter used to analyse the performance of catalyst as higher current densities show elevated reaction rate. However, to achieve higher current density, better electrolytes and operation conditions are necessary. Cu2O has been extensively investigated as an effective catalyst for the CO2-RR. Through diverse techniques for refining morphology and surface modification, enhanced selectivity and activity of various products can be attained. They presented a unique Cu2O nanostructure design achieved through facet-controlled etching, resulting in a conversion of catalyst structure hollow dice-like shape and also demonstrated a significant Faradic efficiency for formic acid, attaining 75.1% at a potential of −1.0 V20. Apurv Saxena et al.21used a binary CoTe electrocatalyst for CO2 reduction in an aqueous medium under ambient circumstances at neutral pH. CoTe exhibited a Faradaic efficiency of 86.83% for acetic acid at a minimal potential of − 0.25 V vs. RHE. CoTe exhibited superior CO2RR activity relative to Co3O4, attributable to the augmented electrochemical activity of the catalytically active transition metal centre and enhanced intermediate adsorption on the catalyst surface and also demonstrates consistent CO2RR catalytic activity over 50 h with a low Tafel slope of 50.3 mV dec–1. Nor Hafizah Yasin et al.22examined the electroreduction effectiveness of Cu- GDL for the conversion of CO₂ to ethylene in a liquid flow cell setup. The examination of the surface morphology and crystallinity of the Cu-based GDL prior to and during CO₂RR indicated substantial structural transformations, evolving from microscale particles to a floral structure and transitioning from pure Cu to a composite of Cu and Cu₂O. Optimisation of essential parameters was conducted to improve CO₂ reduction efficiency. From their result, Ethylene synthesis was optimal at elevated half-cell potentials, attaining FE of 60.95% and CD of 94.74 mA/cm². Xinyi Chen et al.23introduced Cu–polyamine hybrid catalyst, created via co-electroplating, which significantly enhances the selectivity for ethylene synthesis. The Faradaic efficiency for ethylene synthesis was 87% at − 0.47 V and energy efficiency attains around 50%. Raman test demonstrated that the polyamine associated with the Cu electrode leads to elevated surface pH, increased CO content, and enhanced stabilisation of intermediates in comparison to the incorporation of additives with minimal or no amine activity. In study24, the utilisation of Cu oxides/ZnO-based electrocatalytic surfaces for the gas- phase electroreduction of CO2 to ethylene within a filter-press electrochemical cell. The FE (91.1%) and ethylene production rate (487.9 µmol m− 2 s− 1) were attained, when a current density of 7.5 mAcm− 2 was applied. Jingjie Wu et al.25discussed the impact of the microstructure of the Sn catalyst for formate production. It was observed that the electrode with 17 to 20 wt% Nafion had the maximum current density for formate production when the average particle size of Sn catalysts varied between from 100 nm to 1.5 μm. Siyu Zhong et al.26reported that the energy efficiency of the CO2RR system was improved, achieving 52.6% at a current of 1.2 A under alkaline circumstances and 49.4% under neutral conditions, characterized by low voltage and good Faradaic efficiency. The optimisation of the reaction microenvironment significantly reduces the precipitation problem, enabling the system to function reliably for more than 100 h, achieving a Faradaic efficiency of 90% for CO generation. G.D.Sainz et al.27 utilised a filter press reactor in continuous mode and employing a Sustainion anion exchange membrane and operating at a current density of up to 600 mA cm − 2. The configuration with identical electrocatalysts demonstrates performance akin to the GDE design with liquid electrolyte, although it benefits from functioning solely with vapour input to the cathode, hence eliminating the necessity for a liquid catholyte. Despite resulting in a more diluted product, superior combinations of Faradaic Efficiency (73.7%), energy usage (342 kWh·kmol−1), and product rates (22.9 mmol m− 2·s−1) were attained at elevated current densities. In another study28, the concurrent nanoscale manipulation of oxygen vacancies and shape in Cu2O catalysts for the electrochemical reduction of CO2 to liquid products, specifically formate and ethanol. Through a comparative analysis of the performance of cube- and tetrakaidecahedron-shaped Cu2O catalysts, they have established that the flower-like Cu2O catalyst, characterised by an abundance of oxygen vacancy defects, displayed enhanced efficacy in the reduction of CO2 to liquid products. The maximum FE for liquid products was 95.5%, with a notable ethanol FE of 52.6% and a generation rate of 23.8 µmol h − 1 cm − 2 in an H-cell. According to Yaoyu Yin studies29, the variations in Nafion configurations on the catalyst surface subsequently influence the binding of ∗CO and ∗H intermediates, consequently enabling the modulation of product distribution. They utilised a Cu nanosheet catalyst at 800 mA cm − 2, the Faradaic efficiency for multi carbon compounds rises from 67.5% to 90.5% merely by altering the dispersion solvent from low-ε dimethyl sulfoxide to moderate-ε isopropanol. Many research works have used optimization techniques for prediction the output responses. for example, Bashir Rahmanian et al.30described the application of RSM to optimize MEUF for the removal of lead ions from aqueous solution samples. Due to inherent complexity and nonlinear character of membrane-based separation processes, ANN and ANFIS have been applied. The results from the ANN and ANFIS models show strong correlation R² =0.92, R² = 0.97 respectively. Morteza Esfandyari et al.31investigated the efficiency of 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium tetrachloroaluminate ionic liquid as a treatment additive towards diesel fuel with thiophene. Influence of operational parameters including volumetric ratio of ionic liquid to model fuel was investigated, along with extraction temperature and time. ANFIS and particle swarm optimisation (PSO) have been developed to predict sulphur removal from diesel by EDS process using BMIM. The results were satisfactorily correlated with the experimental data and model optimised using PSO predicts the data better than ANFIS. Mahdi Niknam Shahrak et al.32 predicted the water vapour adsorption capacity in metal-organic framework (MOF) materials and through ANN and ANFIS methods. The comparison between their result and with experimental data exhibited that both the ANFIS and ANN model had remarkable prediction results of water vapour space adsorption capacity in MOFs, which were required less mean square error (MSE = 0.005 and 0.002 respectively). This is a clear indication that considerable potential exists for the use of both ANN and ANFIS techniques to effectively sift through MOFs suitable for water vapour adsorption. In another study33, The ANN model was formulated based on the extensive experimental data with Nansulate coating. Comparison of the ANN results with those obtained from experiments found the qualitative and quantitative agreement to be better between values predicted using the ANN model and experimental obtained data. In addition, the ANN model developed is proved to perform better predictability over a wide range of operational conditions and also found the maximum relative error to be 3% between the experimental results and ANN calculations. In study34, The Co (III)/Al2O3 catalyst was tested in Fischer-Tropsch synthesis (FTS) over a wide range of synthesis gas conversions and compared with those from ANN simulations and predicted the composition of CH4, CO2 and CO during Fischer-Tropsch Process of Natural Gas based on Neural Network model. The input vector, a four-dimensional array that includes variables of pressure, temperature, and time on CO/H2 ratio from a set of 70 different experiments. The results showed that the predicted values of model were in good agreement with the experimental data.

Data collection

Finding an active and stable catalyst for the electrochemical reduction of CO2 to valuable chemical compounds is especially important with regard to efforts in reducing the level of atmospheric CO2. These catalysts showed high catalytic activity, which was improved due to the composite with ZIF-8 that decreased CO binding36, The present challenges of the catalysts, supports, electrolytes and incomplete selectivity, as well as intermediates during CO2 reduction transformations, require serious research advancement. In addition, the activation of *CO and selective C2 + products is enhanced by La doping. For this study, data on CO₂RR performance were systematically collected from recent high-impact literature35–40 focusing on five key input parameters and two performance outputs. The inputs included the applied potential (AP), which drives CO₂ reduction and is typically reported against reference electrodes, catalyst loading (CL)which influences activity and mass transport, composition (CO), describing the elemental or molecular ratios that define catalyst structure and synergy, support (SU), a descriptive categorical factor such as carbon paper, Cu foam, that governs conductivity and stability and electrolyte (EL) generally impact ion transport, local pH, and selectivity. The two outputs such as Faradaic efficiency (FE), which measures product selectivity by quantifying the fraction of charge contributing to a specific CO₂RR product, and current density (CD), which reflects catalytic activity by reporting the rate of electron transfer per electrode area.The extent of CO₂ adsorption and activation is controlled by the applied potential that increases local surface electron density at more negative potentials (favouring formation of the reactive CO₂•⁻ intermediate and tele sequestration of H* by subsequent COOH/CHO species) thus enhancing faradaic efficiency (FE) and current density (CD). The support effect adjusts electron transfer and intermediate stabilization; conductive or defect-rich supports modify local electronic states and modulate the adsorption energies of COOH, CO* and other intermediates. Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy inference system (ANFIS)

The modelling processes through input-output data have received significant research attention. The primary elements of soft computing, including fuzzy logic and ANN have demonstrated significant effectiveness in dealing with complex nonlinear system recognition and control challenges. The ANFIS model was a hybrid predictive framework that integrates the learning abilities of ANNs with the reasoning functionalities of fuzzy logic. It employs input-output sets and a collection of if-then fuzzy rules to integrate the human-like reasoning approach characteristic of FISs42,43, and this model was particularly proficient in diverse engineering applications characterised by inconsistent or nonlinear data, when traditional methods are inadequate or overly complex to implement. An ANFIS structure typically comprises five layers, each composed of several nodes. The outputs from the preceding layer served as input nodes for the next layer. Result and discussion

The interaction plots for FE and CD were shown in Figs. 3 and 4, respectively. From Fig. 3, the Faradaic efficiency increased significantly when the input parameters were maintained at an applied potential of − 1.09 V with a catalyst loading of 0.426 mg/cm², using a Cu–In alloy containing 10% indium supported on carbon nanotubes in a 0.5 M NaHCO₃ electrolyte, where the combined effects of Cu-In incorporation, the high conductivity of CNTs, and the bicarbonate medium enhanced charge transfer and improved selectivity toward the desired CO₂ reduction products. Lower catalyst loading enhanced Faradaic efficiency because a thinner catalyst layer improves mass transport by reducing diffusion barriers, allowing CO₂ molecules and electrolyte ions to reach active sites more efficiently45,46. This also ensures better utilisation of active sites, as nearly all of them remain electrochemically accessible, unlike in thicker layers, where many sites become buried or inactive. From Fig. 4, a significant increase in Current density was observed when the input parameters were maintained at an applied potential of − 1.29 V, catalyst loading of 0.426 mg/cm², 10% of Cu–In composition, carbon nanotube support and 0.1 M NaHCO₃ electrolyte. Lower applied potential (more negative voltage) increases current density because the stronger driving force accelerates electron transfer kinetics and enhances the overall reaction rate. At more negative potentials, a larger number of electrons are available to participate in the reduction process, which lowers the activation energy barrier for CO₂ conversion and increases the rate of intermediate formation on the catalyst surface. This results in higher turnover of reactant molecules, greater utilisation of catalytic sites, and consequently, an increase in the measured current density.

Utilisation of ANN for result prediction, MATLAB R2016a was employed for predicting the output responses such as FE and CD, through the Neural Network tool. The collection of data served as the principal input-output source for training the neural network. The dataset was divided into input and output matrices. Numerous network configurations were examined using various training algorithms to identify the optimal design. The experimental dataset, consisting of 64 data sets, was erratically divided into three segments: 70% (44 datasets) for training, 15% (10 datasets) for testing, and 15% (10 datasets) for validation. This division technique employed 19 data sets for training, 4 for testing, and 4 for validation, thereby mitigating network overfitting. The ANN model (5–10–2) feed-forward backpropagation architecture, which indicates 5 nodes of the input layer, 10 nodes of the hidden layer, and 3 nodes of the output layer and Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) algorithm was selected for its fast convergence and robustness for training the experimental datasets. ANN employs two essential metrics: R² and MSE for evaluating the precision and overall efficacy of the created model. Supplementary Fig. 2 depicts an error histogram that quantifies the deviation between predicted values and goal values for 20 bins. The histogram is segmented into bins, each corresponding to distinct ranges of error. From observation, the majority of samples have an inaccuracy of 0.7528, which was located at the central bin. Supplementary Fig. 3 illustrates the training progress of a neural network over 9 epochs, showing that the gradient steadily decreased from a high initial value to about 744.29, indicating that learning and weight adjustments were taking place, while the damping factor (µ) rose early for stability and then stabilized at 1, reflecting a balanced optimization process between fast convergence and stability. The peak validation performance occurs at epoch 3, as demonstrated in Supplementary Fig. 4, which depicts the Mean Squared Error (MSE) over 9 epochs for all datasets, with the ideal validation performance recorded at 2776.81 at epoch 2. The ANN architecture 5–10–3 was seen in the detailed regression graph presented in Supplementary Fig. 5. The correlation coefficients identified for the training, validation, and testing sets were 0.96338, 0.9144, and 0.94419, respectively. The correlation coefficient for all datasets was determined to be 0.93726.

To evaluate the prediction of the ANFIS model, Supplementary Fig. 6 and Supplementary Fig. 7 illustrated how they can be utilised in predicting the Faradic efficiency and current density. ANFIS was developed by Gaussian membership functions with fuzzy logic framework with an adaptive neural network architecture, thereby combining the comprehension of fuzzy reasoning with the self-learning capabilities of neural networks and The fuzzy system was trained by the neural network 100 epochs. The fuzzy inference mechanism was managed by a rule base consisting of 27 fuzzy logic rules and the model’s value was demonstrated through a collection of experimental validations. These settings established a conducive environment for charge transfer and intermediate stabilisation, resulting in improved electrochemical activity. The precise Cu–In ratio amplifies synergistic effects, enhancing selectivity, whilst the graphene support delivers increased conductivity and stability, and the NaHCO₃ electrolyte supplies a buffered medium that preserves ionic conductivity and pH equilibrium throughout the reaction. Conclusion

In this work, the electrochemical reduction of CO₂ was analyzed by experimental datasets with five input parameters such as applied potential, composition, catalyst loading, support, and electrolyte for predicting Faradaic efficiency and current density. Statistical modelling approaches such as SN ratio, ANOVA and machine learning frameworks (ANN and ANFIS) were employed for determining the best parameters and predicting accuracy of FE and CD. The following conclusions were made based on the findings below.

Residual analysis verified that errors had a normal distribution with negligible deviations, thus enhancing confidence in the predictive models. The examination of the signal-to-noise ratio identified optimal parameter combinations for maximising Faradaic efficiency (AP = − 1.29 V, CL = 0.426 mg/cm², CO = 10% Cu–In, SU = CNT, EL = 0.1 M KOH) and current density (AP = − 1.29 V, CL = 0.426 mg/cm², CO = 10% Cu–In, SU = CNT, EL = 0.1 M KHCO₃).

The examination of the signal-to-noise ratio identified optimal parameter combinations for maximising Faradaic efficiency (AP = − 1.29 V, CL = 0.426 mg/cm², CO = 10% Cu–In, SU = CNT, EL = 0.1 M KOH) and current density (AP = − 1.29 V, CL = 0.426 mg/cm², CO = 10% Cu–In, SU = CNT, EL = 0.1 M KHCO₃).

ANOVA further indicated that catalyst loading was the most significant factor for Faradaic Efficiency (FE), whereas applied voltage predominantly influenced current density (CD) performance, with the electrolyte also exerting considerable impact in both cases. Regression analyses revealed the significant adverse effect of increasingly negative potentials on FE, attributed to competitive hydrogen development, while CL, CO, and EL presented secondary influences.

ANFIS precisely forecasted a Faradic efficiency of 72.9% and a Current Density of 542 mA cm⁻².

From the performance metrics, ANFIS exhibited low error values RMSE (0.0725 & 0.0525) and MAE (0.1411 & 0.0811) for both FE and CD when compared with ANN and also the corresponding ΔR² confirmed that ANFIS delivered the better predictive accuracy. The findings emphasised the necessity of optimising catalyst loading and application potential by utilising advanced hybrid models for guiding the systematic design of effective CO₂ reduction systems.

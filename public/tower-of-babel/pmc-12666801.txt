Chronic sternal pain relief after a pecto-intercostal fascial plane block: A case report
Authors: Harlyn Naika Veloso-Evans, Kyle Wentz, Neville Campbell
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChronic sternal pain is relatively common in adults and particularly in patients after surgery involving the chest wall such as with cardiac surgery or mastectomy. Treatment options for patients with chronic sternal pain have largely consisted of physical therapy and oral medications. The pecto-intercostal fascial plane block, while having proven efficacy in managing pain in the perioperative setting, has very limited evidence demonstrating its use within the outpatient setting.Case reportThis is a case of a 53-year-old female with chronic underlying sternal pain presumably from costochondritis who underwent radical bilateral mastectomy and radiation therapy for breast cancer, which significantly worsened her sternal pain. She failed conservative measures. A pecto-intercostal fascial plane block was ordered and administered. This brought 80–100 % pain relief and significant functional improvements for the patient which lasted for over three months.ConclusionThis is a novel case where a patient with chronic sternal pain which worsened after bilateral mastectomy received a pecto-intercostal fascial plane block and had almost complete pain relief for over a three month period. Additional research is needed to further establish this procedure and its efficacy in managing patients in an outpatient setting with chronic sternal pain.

Introduction

Anterior chest wall pain in adults is relatively common. Among patients who present to the emergency department with chest pain, costochondritis or other musculoskeletal etiology is often found as the primary pain generator with an incidence reported of 12–36 % [[1], [2], [3]]. In patients after chest wall surgical procedures, chronic chest wall pain can occur with a prevalence thought to be 20–50 % in patients after mastectomy [4,5], and 20–28 % [6,7] in patients after cardiac surgery with sternotomy. The exact mechanism of chronic chest wall pain symptoms in both surgical and non-surgical patients is not entirely understood. For patients after chest wall surgery with either mastectomy or cardiac surgery involving sternotomy, it is thought that chronic sternal pain can develop due to injury to the nerves and tissues of the chest wall during surgery. The treatment options for chronic sternal or chest wall pain are often limited to physical therapy and oral medications. Although the evidence is limited, there is evidence demonstrating efficacy of manual therapies for sternal pain [8,9] with one study demonstrating sustained results at 3 month follow-up [9].

Within the perioperative setting during a mastectomy or cardiac surgery with sternotomy, interventions can be utilized to manage acute sternal pain. A pecto-intercostal fascial plane block administers medication to the fascial plane between the pectoralis major and external intercostal muscles targeting the anterior cutaneous branches of the second through sixth intercostal nerves to provide analgesia both during and after mastectomy [10]. This procedure is also utilized for perioperative pain management for cardiac surgeries involving sternotomy. Several studies demonstrate efficacy in utilization of pecto-intercostal nerve blocks for perioperative pain after cardiac surgery involving sternotomy [[11], [12], [13]] and mastectomy [14].

The pecto-intercostal fascial plane block has largely been limited to the perioperative setting. This is a novel case where a patient with underlying chronic sternal pain which severely worsened after a bilateral mastectomy sustained almost complete pain relief for over three months after a pecto-intercostal fascial plane block was administered in an outpatient setting.

Case presentation

A 53-year-old female presented with chronic mid/low sternal pain which started in 2015. She was diagnosed with breast cancer that was HLA-B27 positive in July of 2019. She underwent bilateral mastectomy in August 2019. She had radiation therapy from October 2019 until December 2019. She then underwent bilateral breast reconstruction in 2020. Throughout this treatment course from 2019 to 2020 she noted significant worsening of her symptoms localized to the middle to lower sternum.

Four years after her breast reconstruction, she established care with our outpatient pain management clinic. At her initial outpatient clinic visit for pain management she was found to have pain that was 5/10 in severity. She described the pain as pressure and squeezing. Her pain was intermittent and worse with activity. She reported being unable to lift more than 5 pounds, clean her house, or raise the arms above the head without significant pain that increased to 7/10 in severity with these activities. She managed her symptoms with cyclobenzaprine which she took sparingly, and felt this provided moderate temporary relief. She denied taking any other medications for her condition. She completed a course of chiropractics after symptom onset which was ineffective. An MRI of the chest with and without contrast demonstrated no abnormalities of her sternum but did demonstrate bone marrow edema within the left clavicular head. Upon exam, she experienced significant tenderness with palpation along the middle to lower sternum. A bilateral pecto-intercostal fascial plane block (PIFB) was ordered for management of her chronic sternal pain, which was subsequently performed at two months since her first outpatient pain management clinic visit.

Procedure technique

A verbal and written informed consent were obtained prior to the procedure, which was performed bilaterally. With the patient in a supine position, the patient's skin along the chest wall was prepped in sterile fashion with chlorhexidine. A 22-gauge 3.5-inch needle was placed 2–3 cm lateral from the sternum from a cephalad to caudal direction until the needle tip was found between the pectoralis major and external intercostal muscle at the right 4th-5th rib interspace confirmed with ultrasound guidance [Fig. 1]. After negative aspiration, 2 cc was injected of a mixture containing 3.5 cc 1 % lidocaine, 3.5 cc .25 % bupivacaine, and 1 cc 10mg/ml Dexamethasone. The procedure was similarly performed at the left 4th-5th rib interspace and bilateral 5th-6th rib interspaces [Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4]. After each injection, the needles were then restyletted and removed.

Outcomes

On the day of the procedure her VAS was 4/10 pre-procedure and 0/10 post-procedure. Two weeks later in clinic she reported 80–100 % pain relief from the procedure. She admitted to improvements in function and noted that she could lift weights without pain which was not the case before the injection as even lifting five pounds had caused significant pain. She noticed improvements in cleaning the house and raising the arms overhead without pain, which had been present before the injection with these activities.

At three-month follow-up she reported the procedure was “life changing” and allowed her to lift weights, and remain active without pain. She reported that her pain remained minimal at three-month follow-up. At four-month follow-up she admitted the pain had returned similar to that prior to the procedure. The decision was made to repeat the pecto-intercostal fascial plane block given the significant results she experienced with the first injection. The repeat injection was performed 5 months after the initial procedure. At two week follow-up she had reported 70 % pain relief from the repeat injection with similar improvements in function as she had reported after the initial injection.

Discussion

While often utilized as a tool for management of perioperative or acute sternal pain, to our knowledge, this is the first case reported of a patient receiving a pecto-intercostal fascial plane block for management of chronic sternal pain which worsened after a mastectomy. There is one case reported by Sahoo et al. of a patient who was treated with a pecto-intercostal fascial plane block for chronic sternal pain after cardiac surgery with sternotomy [15]. Other types of injections for non-cardiac chest pain have been reported including trigger point injections [16], transversus thoracis muscle plane blocks [17], and sternoclavicular joint injections [18].

The transversus thoracis muscle plane block has proven efficacy in the perioperative period, but it was not pursued in our case as it requires a deeper needle placement than the pecto-intercostal fascial plane block, with injectate administered between the transversus thoracis and internal intercostal muscle layers [17]. However, this option could be reasonably considered if the more superficial pecto-intercostal fascial plane block was not pursued or found to be unsuccessful. Trigger point injections also have demonstrated proven efficacy for non-cardiac chest pain, however, our patient's pain was localized more midline than where trigger point injections are typically administered along the musculature of the chest wall [16]. Given this location of pain within the middle to lower sternum, the sternoclavicular joint injection was not pursued as her pain was not localized to that region.

It is worth noting the relevance of our patient's history of worsening sternal pain after bilateral mastectomy. Given the efficacy demonstrated of the pecto-intercostal fascial plane block within the perioperative period after mastectomy it was decided to pursue an application of this procedure in the outpatient setting [14]. If the patient only had costochondritis without surgery history, this option may not have been as readily pursued given the lack of evidence of this injection being applied for costochondritis in any setting. Again, while there is paucity of evidence of this injection being applied for chronic pain, the authors noted the perioperative studies in the decision-making process [14].

Oral medications utilized in this patient population often include chronic narcotic pain management although the exact prevalence of opioid use for patients with chronic post-surgical sternal pain after sternotomy or mastectomy is unknown [[19], [20], [21]]. Chronic narcotic pain medication for treatment of musculoskeletal pain is controversial and carries significant risks and potential complications [22]. Appropriate application of the pecto-intercostal fascial plane block procedure could further provide additional treatment options for patients with chronic sternal pain.

This case represents an additional treatment option utilized in the outpatient setting for chronic sternal pain. This patient sustained near complete pain relief for over three months after her injection, and found “life-changing” functional improvements as well. In this patient, her similar pain symptoms did eventually return at four months after the procedure. For our patient, it was decided to repeat the pecto-intercostal fascial plane block with steroid given the amount of relief she had sustained with the first injection. However, it is worth noting and well known that continued steroid exposure poses various health risks [23], and so caution should be considered with ongoing repeat injections when symptoms return. Additional research could be performed to look at efficacy when the procedure is performed with anesthetic only, without the use of steroid.

Further research could also investigate the efficacy of medication administration at various levels and injection sites compared to what was performed for this particular case. Sahoo et al. reported administration of the injectate at the 2nd-3rd intercostal space and 4th-5th intercostal space [12] in their case while our patient received treatment at the 4th-5th intercostal space and 5th-6th intercostal space which was decided based on the location of her pain. Four injections were administered with each having a volume of 2 cc. For fascial plane injections there is limited evidence demonstrating superiority of multiple injections with smaller volumes versus one injection with a large volume. Again, further research on this topic could be beneficial to guide proceduralists performing fascial plane blocks.

Ultimately, further research is needed to establish this procedure as a viable tool for managing patients with chronic sternal pain after mastectomy, after cardiac surgery with sternotomy, and for patients with costochondritis.

Conclusion

This is a novel case where a patient with chronic sternal pain which worsened after bilateral mastectomy received a pecto-intercostal fascial plane block (PIFB) and had almost complete pain relief for over three months. Additional research is needed to further establish this procedure and its efficacy in managing patients with chronic sternal pain.

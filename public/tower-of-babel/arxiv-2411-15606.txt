Abstract
.

Extending Verdier’s deformation space to the normal cone of a closed subscheme and Rost’s double deformation space of a pair of nested closed subschemes, we introduce a notion of deformation spaces attached to chains of immersions of arbitrary lengths
n
. One main result, which builds on the formalism of multi-centered dilatations of schemes, is the existence of so-called panelization isomorphisms, which produce under suitable regularity conditions several canonical isomorphisms between a given deformation space of length
n
and some deformation spaces of smaller lengths. Having these panelization isomorphisms also allows to give geometric descriptions of the strata –certain restrictions of special interest– of deformation spaces.

Introduction

The purpose of this article is to introduce and study a notion deformation space for chains of immersions of closed subschemes of arbitrary lengths, which recovers as special cases the deformation space to the normal cone of a closed subscheme as defined by Verdier
[
16
]
and Rost double deformation space of a pair of nested closed subschemes
[
14
]
.

For a closed subscheme
Y
of a scheme
X
with defining ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
, the deformation space
\mathrm{Def}(Y,X)
is a scheme over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{X}

defined as the relative spectrum of the
\mathcal{O}_{X}[t]
-subalgebra

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{I}^{n}t^{-n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}[t^{\pm 1}],

where, by convention,
\mathcal{I}^{n}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
for
n<0
. The structure morphism
\sigma:\mathrm{Def}(Y,X)\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{X}
expresses
\mathrm{Def}(Y,X)
as the dilatation (also called affine blow-up or affine modification
[
3
,
11
]
) of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{X}
with center consisting of the closed subscheme
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{Y}\subset\mathbb{A}^{1}_{X}
and the divisor
D
equal to the image of the zero section
\{0\}:X\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{X}
, see e.g.
[
1
]
. The fact that
\mathrm{Def}(Y,X)
fits into a pair of cartesian diagrams

\textstyle{C_{Y/X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Def}(Y,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{G}_{m,X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\{0\}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{X}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{G}_{m,X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where
C_{Y/X}=\mathrm{Spec}(\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}\mathcal{I}^{n}/\mathcal{I}^{n+1})
is the normal cone of
Y
in
X
, plays a central role in the construction of Gysin homomorphisms for regular closed immersions in several cohomology theories, and in particular in the construction of intersection theory within Chow groups. The double deformation space
\mathrm{Def}(Z,Y,X)
of a pair of nested closed immersions
Z\subset Y\subset X
was introduced later on by Rost
[
14
]
as scheme over
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{X}
whose purpose was primarily to serve as a tool to verify the associativity of certain intersection operations on Chow groups.

It was observed in
[
10
]
that in the same way as
\mathrm{Def}(Y,X)
can be interpreted as a mono-centered dilatation of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{X}
, Rost’s double deformation space
\mathrm{Def}(Z,Y,X)
can be interpreted in a natural way as a double-centered dilatation of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{X}
. Building on this observation, it becomes natural to define a notion of deformation space attached to a chain of nested closed immersions of arbitrary length within the formalism of multi-centered dilatations of schemes of
[
10
]
. As a result, the first part of this paper proposes a general notion of
multi-centered deformation space of arbitrary length and study its basic properties. Building on ideas and techniques inspired by iterated multi-centered dilatations of schemes we derive from universal properties of multi-centered dilatations
[
11
,
10
]
the existence of canonical morphisms, called
panelization morphisms
, which allow, under appropriate regularity assumptions, to identify a given multi-centered deformation space with several other multi-centered deformation spaces of smaller length. These panelization morphisms and isomorphisms allow an inductive view on the construction and the study of the properties of multi-centered deformations spaces. We use in turn these different complementary presentations of multi-centered deformation spaces to study the geometry of their
strata
, which are particular closed subschemes analogue in our setting to the restrictions of the deformations space and the double deformation space to the coordinate hyperplanes and their intersections.

The article is organized as follows. Section
1

provides a quick recollection on (multi-centered) dilatations of rings and schemes following
[
10
]
. Section
2
presents the definition of our general multi-centered deformations spaces and collects basic results on these. Section
3
is devoted to the construction of the panelization morphisms of multi-centered deformations spaces (Proposition
3.2
) and the identification of suitable regularity assumptions under which these morphisms are isomorphisms (Theorem
3.7
and Theorem
3.10
).
Section
4
discusses the notion of strata of multi-centred deformation spaces, the main results there being Proposition
4.2

and Proposition
4.3
which provide, again under appropriate regularity assumptions, complementary descriptions of these strata in the form of multi-centred deformation spaces of smaller lengths. Section
5
provides a summary and some applications of the results of the previous sections in the special situation of the “Verdier-Rost deformation space”
\mathrm{Def}(Z_{n},\ldots,Z_{0})
of a collection of closed immersions
Z_{n}\subset Z_{n-1}\subset\cdots\subset Z_{1}\subset Z_{0}
between schemes smooth over a locally Noetherian base scheme.

The article is completed by three appendices: Appendix
A
examines from the viewpoint of multi-centered dilatations some symmetric variants of multiple deformation spaces appearing in
[
14
,
6
,
13
]
. Appendix
B
provides constructions of vector bundles by means of dilatations which are in particular used to clarify the existence of non-canonical isomorphism between certain strata of multi-centered deformations spaces. Appendix
C
, joint with Sylvain Brochard, is devoted to establishing several results of independent interest concerning products, intersections and sums of ideals generated by monomials in a regular sequence, which play an important role in the proof of Theorem
3.10
but for which we were unable to find a convenient reference.

Preliminaries

1.1
Closed subschemes, divisors and vector bundles

The set of all closed subschemes of a scheme
X
is denoted
Clo(X)
. If
Y_{1},Y_{2}
belong to
Clo(X)
, we write
Y_{1}\subset Y_{2}
if
Y_{1}
is a closed subscheme of
Y_{2}
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
be the quasi-coherent ideals of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
such that
Y_{1}=V({\mathcal{M}}_{1})
, and
Y_{2}=V({\mathcal{M}}_{2})
.Then
Y_{1}+Y_{2}
is defined as
V({\mathcal{M}}_{1}{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
,
Y_{1}\cup Y_{2}
is defined as
V({\mathcal{M}}_{1}\cap{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
and
Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}
is defined as
V({\mathcal{M}}_{1}+{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
.

An element
D
of
Clo(X)
is called locally principal if its ideal sheaf is locally generated by a single element and an effective Cartier divisor if its ideal sheaf is locally generated by a non-zero-divisor.

By a vector bundle over a scheme
X
, we mean the relative spectrum
p:\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})\to X
of the symmetric algebra
\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}\mathcal{E}
of a coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{E}
.

1.2
Multi-centered dilatations of schemes

We assume the formalism of multi-centered dilatations of schemes as defined and studied in
[
10
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we briefly recall some essential definitions and results.

A multi-center
\{[Y_{i},D_{i}]\}_{i\in I}

on a scheme
X
is a set of pairs of closed subschemes of
X
such that each
D_{i}
,
i\in I
, is locally principal. Following
[
10
, Definition 3.4]
for a collection
D_{I}=(D_{i})_{i\in I}
of locally principal closed subschemes of
X
, we denote by
{\operatorname{Sch}}_{X}^{\{D_{i}\}_{i\in I}\text{-reg}}
the full subcategory of schemes over
X
whose objects are morphisms
f:T\to X
such that
f^{-1}(D_{i})
is an effective Cartier divisor in
T
for all
i
. Building on
[
10
, Proposition 3.17]
, a multi-centered dilatation of a scheme
X
can be shortly defined for our purpose through its universal property:

Theorem-Definition 1.1

The dilatation of a scheme
X
with multi-center
\{[Y_{i},D_{i}]\}_{i\in I}
is the
X
-scheme
\sigma:{\operatorname{Bl}}\left\{{}^{D_{i}}_{Y_{i}}\right\}_{i\in I}X\to X
representing the functor
{\operatorname{Sch}}_{X}^{\{D_{i}\}_{i\in I}\text{-reg}}\to Set
defined by

(f:T\to X)\;\longmapsto\;\begin{cases}\left\{*\right\},\;\text{if $f^{-1}(D_{i})\subset f^{-1}\left(Y_{i}\right)$ in $Clo\left(T\right)$ for every $i\in I$};\\
\varnothing,\;\text{else.}\end{cases}

(1.1)

If
\#I=0
, then
{\operatorname{Bl}}\left\{{}^{D_{i}}_{Y_{i}}\right\}_{i\in I}X=X
. If
I=\{1\}
is a singleton, then
[Y_{1},D_{1}]
is called a mono-center and
{\operatorname{Bl}}\left\{{}^{D_{1}}_{Y_{1}}\right\}X
is called a mono-centered dilatation.

We briefly summarize from

in the local affine setting. Here,

for some ring

for a collection of ideals

for some elements

. Putting
L_{i}=(M_{i}+(a_{i}))

(\ell,a^{\nu})

\ell\in\textstyle\prod_{i\in I}L_{i}^{\nu_{i}}
and
a^{\nu}=\prod_{i\in I}a_{i}^{\nu_{i}}
for some multi-index

of natural numbers, with the equivalence relation defined by
(\ell,a^{\nu})\sim(\ell^{\prime},a^{\nu^{\prime}})
if
(\ell a^{\nu^{\prime}}-\ell^{\prime}a^{\nu})a^{\beta}=0
in

for some multi-index
\beta\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0})^{I}
. We let
\tfrac{\ell}{a^{\nu}}
be the equivalence class of
(\ell,a^{\nu})

the set of all equivalence classes. The formulas

\frac{\ell}{a^{\nu}}+\frac{\ell^{\prime}}{a^{\nu^{\prime}}}=\frac{\ell a^{\nu^{\prime}}+\ell^{\prime}a^{\nu}}{a^{\nu+\nu^{\prime}}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{\ell}{a^{\nu}}\times\frac{\ell^{\prime}}{a^{\nu^{\prime}}}=\frac{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{a^{\nu+\nu^{\prime}}}

which make it into a commutative unital ring. The natural map
\sigma^{*}:A\to A\left[\left\{\tfrac{M_{i}}{a_{i}}\right\}_{i\in I}\right]

is a ring homomorphism, called the dilatation homomorphism. By
[
10

the so-defined

-algebra

identifies with the sub-algebra of of the localization
A[\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}^{-1}]
of

, obtained by adjoining formal inverses
a_{i}^{-1}
for all

, generated by
\left\{\tfrac{M_{i}}{a_{i}}\right\}_{i\in I}
.
The

with multi-center

is then the spectrum
{\operatorname{Bl}}\left\{{}^{D_{i}}_{Y_{i}}\right\}_{i\in I}X

and the morphism
\sigma:{\operatorname{Bl}}\left\{{}^{D_{i}}_{Y_{i}}\right\}_{i\in I}X\to X
corresponding to
\sigma^{*}
is called the dilatation morphism.

2
Multi-centered deformation spaces

Let
(I,\leqslant)
be a finite totally ordered set. Every subset
J
of
I
is considered as endowed with the total ordering induced from
I
. Moreover, for every element
i\in I
, we let

J_{\geqslant i}=\{j\in J|i\leqslant j\}\textrm{ and }J_{>i}=\{j\in J|i<j\}.

2.1
Definition and universal property

Definition 2.1
.

A
deformation datum
on a scheme
X
indexed by a finite totally ordered set
(I,\leqslant)
is a pair
\left({}^{D_{I}}_{X_{I}}\right)=\left({}^{D_{i}}_{X_{i}}\right)_{i\in I}

consisting of an order reversing map

X_{I}:(I,\leqslant)\to Clo(X,\subset):i\mapsto X_{I}(i)=X_{i}\quad\textrm{(if $i\geqslant i^{\prime}$, then $X_{i}\subset X_{i^{\prime}}$)}

and a collection
D_{I}=\{D_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of locally principal closed subschemes of
X
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
multi-centered deformation space
of a deformation datum
\left({}^{D_{I}}_{X_{I}}\right)
on a scheme
X
is the
X
-scheme

\sigma_{I}:{\mathbb{D}}_{I}={\mathbb{D}}\left(\left({}^{D_{I}}_{X_{I}}\right){}_{X}\right)={\mathbb{D}}\left(\left({}^{D_{i}}_{X_{i}}\right)_{i\in I}{}_{X}\right):={\operatorname{Bl}}\left\{{}^{\sum_{j\in I_{\geqslant i}}D_{j}}_{X_{i}}\right\}_{i\in I}X\to X,

(2.1)

that is, the dilatation of
X
with multi-center
\{[X_{i},\sum_{j\in I_{\geqslant i}}D_{j}]\}_{i\in I}
.

Having an abstract index set
I
will be convenient to state and prove the main results, but in practice, there is no loss of generality to identify
I
with the totally ordered set
\{n\geqslant i\geqslant 1\}
where
n=\#I
. A deformation datum is then a chain
X_{n}\subset\ldots\subset X_{1}\subset X
of closed subschemes together with locally principal closed subschemes
D_{n},\ldots,D_{1}
of
X
.
In this case, we will denote the associated multi-centered deformation space by

{\mathbb{D}}\big(^{D_{n},\ldots,D_{1}}_{X_{n},\ldots,X_{1}~X}\big)={\operatorname{Bl}}\left\{{}_{X_{i}}^{\sum_{j=i}^{n}D_{j}}\right\}_{n\geqslant i\geqslant 1}X.

(2.2)

Proposition 2.3

(Universal property of deformation spaces)
The multi-centered deformation space
\sigma_{I}:{\mathbb{D}}\left(\left({}^{D_{I}}_{X_{I}}\right){}_{X}\right)\to X
represents the contravariant functor

\delta:{\operatorname{Sch}}_{X}^{\{D_{i}\}_{i\in I}\text{-reg}}\to Sets
defined by

(f:T\to X)\;\longmapsto\;\begin{cases}\left\{*\right\},\;\text{if $f^{-1}\left(\sum_{s\in I_{\geqslant i}}D_{s}\right)\subset f^{-1}\left(X_{i}\right)$ in $Clo\left(T\right)$ for every $i\in I$};\\
\varnothing,\;\text{else.}\end{cases}

(2.3)

Proof.

The categories
{\operatorname{Sch}}_{X}^{\{D_{i}\}_{i\in I}\text{-reg}}
and
{\operatorname{Sch}}_{X}^{\left\{\sum_{j\in I_{\geqslant i}}D_{j}\right\}_{i\in I}\text{-reg}}
being equal by
[
10
, Fact 3.5]
, the assertion is an immediate consequence of Definition
2.1
and
[
10
, Proposition 3.17]
.
∎

Corollary 2.4

If
T\to X
is an object in the category
{\operatorname{Sch}}_{X}^{\{D_{i}\}_{i\in I}\text{-reg}}
, we have

\#{\operatorname{Hom}}_{X}\left(T,{\mathbb{D}}\left(\left({}^{D_{I}}_{X_{I}}\right){}_{X}\right)\right)\in\left\{0,1\right\}.

Proof.

Immediate from Proposition
2.3
.
∎

Corollary 2.5

With the notation above, the open subset
{\mathbb{D}}_{I}\setminus\sigma_{I}^{-1}(\sum_{i\in I}D_{i})
of
{\mathbb{D}}_{I}
is Zariski dense and the restriction
\sigma_{I}:{\mathbb{D}}_{I}\setminus\sigma_{I}^{-1}(\textstyle\sum_{i\in I}D_{i})\to X\setminus(\textstyle\sum_{i\in I}D_{i})

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Put
U_{I}=X\setminus(\textstyle\sum_{i\in I}D_{i})
. The property that
\sigma_{I}^{-1}(U_{I})={\mathbb{D}}_{I}\setminus\sigma_{I}^{-1}(\textstyle\sum_{i\in I}D_{i})
is Zariski
dense follows from the fact that
\sigma_{I}^{-1}(\sum_{i\in I}D_{i})
is an effective Cartier divisor on
{\mathbb{D}}_{I}
. Since the inclusion morphism
f:U_{I}\to X
belongs to
{\operatorname{Sch}}_{X}^{D_{I}\text{-reg}}
and since
f^{-1}(\sum_{j\in I_{\geqslant i}}D_{j})=\emptyset\subset f^{-1}(X_{i})
for every
i\in I
, Proposition
2.3

implies the existence of a unique
X
-morphism
\hat{f}:U_{I}\to{\mathbb{D}}_{I}
, which is then necessarily an isomorphism onto its image
\sigma_{I}^{-1}(U_{I})
.
∎

2.2
Base change and restriction of index sets

Let
\left({}^{D_{I}}_{X_{I}}\right)
be a deformation datum on a scheme
X

f:X^{\prime}\to X
be an
X
-scheme. We then have an induced order reversing map

X^{\prime}_{I}:(I,\leqslant)\to Clo(X^{\prime},\subset),i\mapsto X^{\prime}_{I}(i):=X_{i}\times_{X}X^{\prime}.

On the other hand,

Definition 2.6
.

The deformation datum

Lemma 2.7

\left({}^{D_{I}}_{X_{I}}\right)
be a deformation datum on a scheme
X

be an
X
-scheme. Then there exists a unique morphism of

-schemes

\mathrm{Sch}_{X^{\prime}}^{\{D_{i}|_{X^{\prime}}\}_{i\in I}\textrm{-reg}}

-schemes

{\mathbb{D}}\left(\left({}^{D_{I}}_{X_{I}}\right)|_{X^{\prime}}{~}{}_{X^{\prime}}\right)\to{\mathbb{D}}\left(\left({}^{D_{I}}_{X_{I}}\right){~}_{X}\right)\times_{X}X^{\prime}

The composition

the existence and uniqueness

Proposition 2.10

With the notation above, the following hold:

(2.7)

3.2
Panelization isomorphisms

We continue with the notation and assumptions of the previous subsections. Example

illustrates that the panelization morphisms

of Proposition

need not be isomorphisms in general. Our aim in this subsection is to provide sufficient conditions on a subset of indices

under which

will be an isomorphism.

As it will appear more clearly below, such conditions can be interpreted as certain regularity properties on the collections of closed subschemes and locally principal divisors in a deformation datum on the scheme

.
We proceed in two steps: first, in subsection

we identify conditions under which for a given subset

of Proposition

.

A.1
Definition and universal property

Definition A.1
.

A

on a scheme

Proof.

This is a consequence of Definition
A.2
and

Appendix C
Intersections, products, and sums of ideals generated by monomials in a regular sequence

by Sylvain Brochard, Adrien Dubouloz and Arnaud Mayeux

In this appendix, we prove that ideals generated by monomials in a regular sequence behave like monomial ideals in a polynomial ring with respect to intersections, sums and products. This might be well-known to experts, but we were unable to find a convenient reference. Before proceeding, let us fix some notations.

Let
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be a sequence of elements in a ring
A
. For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
, say
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
, we denote by
x^{\alpha}
the element

x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
.
The elements
x^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
are called the
x
-monomials of
A
, or simply the monomials if the sequence
x
is understood. We denote by
\Mon_{x}(A)
, or just
\Mon(A)
, the set of monomials of
A
. In the remainder of this section, we fix a sequence
x
, and all monomials are monomials relatively to this sequence. We denote by
(x)
the ideal
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.

If
I
is an ideal of
A
, we denote by
\Mon(I)
the set of monomials that belong to
I
. In other words,

\Mon(I)=I\cap\Mon(A)
. We denote by
\Delta_{I}
the set of elements
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
such that
x^{\alpha}\in I
.

Definition C.1
.

An ideal
I
of
A
is a
monomial ideal
if it is generated by monomials.

There is a (partial) order on
{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
given by the relation
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\leqslant(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})
if and only if
\alpha_{i}\leqslant\beta_{i}
for all
i
. If
\Delta
is a subset of
{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
, we denote by
\Delta_{\textrm{min}}
the set of minimal elements of
\Delta
, and by
\langle\Delta\rangle
the ideal of
{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
generated by
\Delta
.

\langle\Delta\rangle=\{\alpha+\beta\ |\ \alpha\in\Delta\textrm{ and }\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\}.

It is well-known that
\Delta_{\textrm{min}}
is a finite set, and that for any
\alpha\in\Delta
, there is an element
\beta\in\Delta_{\textrm{min}}
such that
\beta\leqslant\alpha
.
We have the obvious relations
\langle\Delta_{\textrm{min}}\rangle=\langle\Delta\rangle
and
\langle\Delta\rangle_{\textrm{min}}=\Delta_{\textrm{min}}
.

Proposition C.2

We have the following properties for monomial ideals
I,J
of
A
.

1.

I
is generated by
\Mon(I)
.

2.

I
is generated by the elements
x^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(\Delta_{I})_{\textrm{\rm min}}
.

3.

I=J
if and only if
\Mon(I)=\Mon(J)
.

4.

The ideals
I+J
and
IJ
are monomial.

Proof.

Let
S
be a set of monomials that generate
I
. The inclusion
S\subset\Mon(I)
implies (1). Assertions (2) and (3) follow immediately. (4) is obvious.
∎

Definition C.3
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
A
. The

of
I
is the set
\supp(I)
of indices
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
for some
\alpha\in(\Delta_{I})_{\textrm{\rm min}}
.

Lemma C.4

Assume that the sequence
x
is quasi-regular. Let
f\in A
be an element of the form

f=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}f_{i}
,
where
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
are monomials of
A
that are distinct to each other, and the
a_{i}
are elements of
A\smallsetminus(x)
. Let
g_{1},\dots,g_{\ell}
be other monomials. Assume that
f
belongs to the ideal
(g_{1},\dots,g_{\ell})
. Then each element
f_{i}
is a multiple of some
g_{j}
.

Proof.

Let
d
be the minimal degree of the monomials
f_{i}
. Then
f\in(x)^{d}
. We can write
f=\sum b_{j}g_{j}
for some
b_{j}\in A
. Let
t
be the minimal degree of the monomials

. If

, we have
f\in(x)^{d}\subset(x)^{t+1}
, hence the relation

projects to
\sum\overline{b_{j}}\overline{g_{j}}=0
in

. Since the monomials of degree

form an

-basis, it follows that

. Hence, replacing the

by some multiples if necessary, we may assume that

\sum_{i}\overline{a_{i}}\overline{f_{i}}=\sum_{j}\overline{b_{j}}\overline{g_{j}}

Any monomial

of degree

on the left-hand side has a nonzero coefficient

(because

), hence it must appear on the right-hand side too. This means that each monomial

of degree

∎

Corollary C.5

Assume that the sequence
x
is quasi-regular. Let
I,J
be monomial ideals of
A
. Then:

1.

\Mon(I+J)=\Mon(I)\cup\Mon(J)

2.

\Mon(IJ)=\Mon(I).\Mon(J)

Proof.

The inclusion
\Mon(I)\cup\Mon(J)\subset\Mon(I+J)
is obvious. Conversely, let

. Since

and monomials of

, it is a multiple of one of them. Hence

and this proves (1). The proof of (2) is very similar: the inclusion
\Mon(I)\Mon(J)\subset\Mon(IJ)
is obvious. Conversely, let

. Since

, it is a linear combination of monomials of the form

, where

and

. By

, it is a multiple of one of these products. Hence

.
∎

Lemma C.6

Assume that

Proof.

If

is a free resolution of
A/I_{n}
and hence, we have
\mathrm{Tor}_{k}^{A}(A/J,A/I_{n})=H_{k}(K_{\bullet}(A,s_{n})\otimes_{A}A/J)
for all
k
. Since the subsequence
s_{n}
is disjoint from the subsequences
s_{j}
generating the ideals
I_{j}
,
1\leqslant j\leqslant n-1
, the image
\overline{s}_{n}
of
s_{n}
by the quotient morphism
A\to A/J
is a regular sequence in
A/J
. This implies
that
K_{\bullet}(A,s_{n})\otimes_{A}A/J
is isomorphic to the Koszul complex

K_{\bullet}(A/J,\overline{s}_{n})
and that the latter is exact. It follows that
\mathrm{Tor}_{k}^{A}(A/J,A/I_{n})=0
for every
k>0
, whence that
\mathrm{Ker}(\mu_{1})=\mathrm{Tor}_{2}^{A}(A/J,A/I_{n})=0
. Now assume by induction that
\mathrm{Ker}(\mu_{m})=0
for every
1\leqslant m<m_{n}
. Tensoring the exact sequence
0\to I_{n}^{m_{n}-1}/I_{n}^{m_{n}}\to A/I_{n}^{m_{n}}\to A/I_{n}^{m_{n}-1}\to 0
by
A/J
gives the long exact sequence

\cdots\to\mathrm{Tor}_{2}^{A}(A/J,I_{n}^{m_{n}-1}/I_{n}^{m_{n}})\to\mathrm{Tor}_{2}(A/J,A/I_{n}^{m_{n}})\to\mathrm{Tor}_{2}(A/J,A/I_{n}^{m_{n}-1})\to\cdots

Since
I_{n}
is generated by the regular sequence
s_{n}
,
I_{n}^{m_{n}-1}/I_{n}^{m_{n}}
is a free
A/I_{n}
-module, isomorphic to the
(m_{n}-1)
-th symmetric module of
I_{n}/I_{n}^{2}
. Since
\mathrm{Tor}_{2}^{A}(A/J,A/I_{n})=0
by the previous observation, this implies that
\mathrm{Tor}_{2}^{A}(A/J,I_{n}^{m_{n}-1}/I_{n}^{m_{n}})=0
as well. Combined with the induction hypothesis that
\mathrm{Ker}(\mu_{m_{n}-1})=\mathrm{Tor}_{2}^{A}(A/J,A/I_{n}^{m_{n}-1})=0
, this implies that
\mathrm{Tor}_{2}^{A}(A/J,A/I_{n}^{m_{n}})=\mathrm{Ker}(\mu_{m_{n}})=0
and completes the proof.
∎

We conclude this appendix with a result that can provide formulas like
I\cap J=IJ
in other situations. This variant is not used in the main part of the article. In particular it contains a slight generalization of
[
5
, (16.9.13.5)]
: in
[
5
]
it is proved that
I^{\ell}\cap J=I^{\ell}J
for any integer
\ell\geqslant 0
, under the assumption that
I
is generated by a sequence that is regular in
A/J
, with
A
Noetherian and
I
contained in the Jacobson radical. Recall that, following Meng
[
12
]
, an ideal
I
in a ring
A
is called strongly Lech-independent if
I^{i}/I^{i+1}
is free over
A/I
for any
i\geqslant 1
. It is clear on the definition that a quasi-regular sequence generates a strongly Lech-independent ideal. But the latter condition is actually significantly weaker. For example, any maximal ideal is clearly strongly Lech-independent, but is not always generated by a quasi-regular sequence.

Proposition C.14

Let
A
be a ring. Let
p\leqslant q
be two natural integers. Let
(f_{1},\dots,f_{q})
be a sequence of elements of
A
. Consider the ideals
I=(f_{1},\dots,f_{p})
and
J=(f_{p+1},\dots,f_{q})
.

(1)

If the sequence
(f_{1},\dots,f_{q})
is
H_{1}
-regular, then the sequence
(\overline{f}_{1},\dots,\overline{f}_{p})
in
A/J
is
H_{1}
-regular.
[
15
,
Tag 068L
]

(2)

If the sequence
(\overline{f}_{1},\dots,\overline{f}_{p})
in
A/J
is
H_{1}
-regular, then
I\cap J=IJ
.
[
15
,
Tag 0665
]

(3)

If
I
is strongly Lech-independent, then for any ideal
K
such that
I\cap K=IK
, we have
I^{\ell}\cap K=I^{\ell}K
for any integer
\ell\geqslant 0
.

(4)

If both
I
and
J
are strongly Lech-independent, and if
I\cap J=IJ
, then for all
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}
:

I^{\alpha}\cap J^{\beta}=I^{\alpha}J^{\beta}.

Proof.

(1) Let
\sum_{i=1}^{p}\overline{\lambda}_{i}\overline{f}_{i}=0
be a relation in
A/J
. It can be lifted to a relation
\sum_{i=1}^{q}\lambda_{i}f_{i}=0
in
A
. Since the sequence
(f_{1},\dots,f_{q})
is
H_{1}
-regular, there exists a family of elements
(\mu_{j,k})
indexed by the pairs
(j,k)
with
1\leqslant j<k\leqslant q
, such that for all
i
,

\lambda_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}f_{j}\mu_{j,i}-\sum_{k=i+1}^{q}f_{k}\mu_{i,k}.

By projecting this relation in
A/J
, we obtain the desired result.

(2) Let
x\in I\cap J
. Since
x\in I
, there exist elements
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{p}
such that

x=\sum_{i=1}^{p}f_{i}\lambda_{i}
. By projecting this relation in
A/J
, we get
\sum\overline{f}_{i}\overline{\lambda}_{i}=0
, in other words the family
(\overline{\lambda}_{1},\dots,\overline{\lambda}_{p})
is in the kernel of the differential
d
of the Koszul complex. Since the sequence
(\overline{f}_{1},\dots,\overline{f}_{p})
is
H_{1}
-regular,
(\overline{\lambda}_{1},\dots,\overline{\lambda}_{p})
is in the image of
d
, so there exists a family of elements
(\mu_{j,k})
indexed by the pairs
(j,k)
with
j<k
, and elements
y_{i}\in J
, such that for all
i
,

\lambda_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}f_{j}\mu_{j,i}-\sum_{k=i+1}^{p}f_{k}\mu_{i,k}+y_{i}.

We deduce that

\displaystyle x=\sum_{i=1}^{p}f_{i}\lambda_{i}=\sum_{1\leqslant j<i\leqslant p}f_{i}f_{j}\mu_{j,i}-\sum_{1\leqslant i<k\leqslant p}f_{i}f_{k}\mu_{i,k}+\sum_{i=1}^{p}f_{i}y_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}f_{i}y_{i}\in IJ.

(3) The equality is proved by induction on
\ell
. It is obvious for
\ell=0
and it is our assumption for
\ell=1
. Suppose
I^{\ell}\cap K=I^{\ell}K
for some integer
\ell\geqslant 0
. Let
x
be an element of
I^{\ell+1}\cap K
. By the inductive hypothesis, we know that
x\in I^{\ell}K
. Let
(g_{i})
be a family of elements that form an
A/I
-basis of
I^{\ell}/I^{\ell+1}
. Then there exist a finite number of elements
\lambda_{i}\in K
, and a
y\in I^{\ell+1}K
such that

x=y+\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}g_{i}.

Moreover, since
x\in I^{\ell+1}
, we have
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}g_{i}=0
in
I^{\ell}/I^{\ell+1}
. By the strong Lech-independence of
I
, this implies that
\lambda_{i}\in I
for all
i
. We deduce that
\lambda_{i}\in I\cap K=IK
, which proves that
x\in I^{\ell+1}K
.

(4) From (3) applied with
K=J
, we deduce that
I^{\alpha}\cap J=I^{\alpha}J
for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Applying (3) again with the strongly independent ideal
J
and
K=I^{\alpha}
, we deduce the desired result.
∎

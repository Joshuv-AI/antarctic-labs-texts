Systemic blood pressure changes following coronary revascularization: towards a coronary baroreflex?
Authors: Pierre Lantelme, Sophie Lampert, Brahim Harbaoui, Pierre-Yves Courand, Salvatore De Rosa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Systemic blood pressure (BP) regulation is essential for maintaining cardiovascular homeostasis. Classical mechanisms involve carotid and aortic baroreceptors, as well as the autonomic nervous system, which dynamically adjust vascular tone, heart rate (HR), and cardiac contractility. While a renal afferent feedback loop has recently been described, the potential role of the heart—particularly the coronary circulation—as a source of neural feedback influencing systemic BP remains poorly characterized. From a physiological perspective, this pathway would be highly relevant, as perfusion of the coronary, cerebral, and renal circulations critically depends on systemic BP. This hypothesis is supported by animal data suggesting that coronary baroreceptors may modulate systemic BP.1–3 Furthermore, we and other have repeatedly observed easier BP control following coronary revascularization.4 Yet, convincing human evidence is scarce.

As a first step in this hypothesis-generating study, we assessed acute changes in systemic BP following coronary angioplasty (CA) compared with diagnostic angiography (DA), to investigate whether myocardial ischaemia may contribute to short-term BP regulation. We postulated that relief of significant myocardial ischaemia through revascularization might be associated with an acute BP reduction. To ensure broadly comparable cardiovascular risk profiles while maximizing differences in ischaemic burden, we compared patients undergoing CA for critically ischaemic proximal coronary lesions with patients undergoing DA for angiographically intermediate but functionally non-ischaemic lesions.

We conducted a single-centre, retrospective study including 141 stable patients undergoing coronary angiography at Croix-Rousse University Hospital (Lyon, France). The primary objective was to compare BP variations at time points remote from the invasive procedure, in order to minimize the influence of peri-procedural stress and medications. To this end, inclusion required a hospital stay of at least 3 days. Blood pressure measurements were performed by trained nursing staff using validated sphygmomanometers, in accordance with routine institutional practice. For the present analysis, the systolic BP (SBP), diastolic BP (DBP), and HR measurements temporally most distant from the procedure were extracted from medical records. Owing to the organization of hospital stays, these measurements consistently corresponded to admission and discharge values obtained ≥12 h before and after the procedure, respectively.

Regarding procedural characteristics, 96% of procedures were performed via radial access. Intracoronary nitrates were systematically administered prior to angioplasty and fractional flow reserve (FFR) measurements, while low-dose midazolam (1 mg) was administered at the operator’s discretion.

The CA group (n = 76) had critical left main/proximal LAD lesions (>90% stenosis) with documented ischaemia, while the DA group (n = 65) had intermediate non-ischaemic proximal/mid-LAD lesions (50–70% stenosis, FFR >0.80 for all). Exclusion criteria were acute coronary syndrome, haemodynamic instability, atrial fibrillation, missing BP data, and outpatient procedures. Antihypertensive therapy was quantified using a Treatment Intensity Score (TIS). To calculate TIS, the prescribed daily dose of each medication was expressed relative to its maximum approved daily dose. Individual TIS values for each antihypertensive agent were calculated and summed to obtain a composite TIS score, reflecting the cumulative proportional dose across drug classes (0 = none; 1 = full maintenance dose).5 Continuous variables were reported as medians (interquartile range) and compared using analysis of variance; categorical variables were compared with the χ2 test. Analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was also performed with adjustment for TIS, age, and baseline BP. Significance was defined as P < 0.05. In accordance with French law, the study was approved by the regional ethics committee (MR-004 n°25-5411).

The CA group was older [76.3 (62.6; 83.4) vs. 69.7 (66.5; 75.6) years, P = 0.018] and had a lower prevalence of diabetes (26.3 vs. 49.2%, P = 0.005). Sex distribution, other risk factors, and comorbidities did not differ. Baseline BP was slightly higher in the CA group [systolic: 134 (121; 157) vs. 130 (116; 140) mmHg, P = 0.055; diastolic: 74 (65; 85) vs. 68 (64; 79) mmHg, P = 0.04]. The majority of patients (85%) received at least one antihypertensive medication, most commonly ACE inhibitors/angiotensin-2 receptor blockers (61%) and beta-blockers (55%), with a similar distribution between groups (data not shown). Treatment Intensity Score was also similar between groups [0.75 (0.25; 1.69) vs. 1.0 (0.5; 1.5) in the CA and DA groups, respectively; P = 0.64].

Angiographically, the CA group included 39% single-vessel, 39% double-vessel, and 22% triple-vessel disease, with percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) targeting the left main in 7 patients (9%) and proximal LAD in 69 (91%). The DA group included predominantly single-vessel disease (81%).

Following PCI, SBP decreased by −7 (−19, 4) mmHg and DBP by −4 (−11; 3) mmHg in the CA group, whereas changes were minimal in the DA group [+1 (−13; 19) and +1 (−7; 8) mmHg for SBP and DBP, respectively; both P = 0.008]. A greater proportion of patients exhibited a BP decrease in the CA group (Figure 1). Heart rate remained unchanged between groups [−2 (−8; +5) vs. −2 (−7; +2) b.p.m. in the CA and DA groups, respectively; P = 0.8].

Overall, antihypertensive treatment intensity remained stable after PCI in both groups [ΔTIS (post–pre): 0.0 (0.0; 0.0) in the CA group vs. 0.0 (0.0; 0.1) in the DA group; P = 0.4]. As illustrated in Figure 1, ΔTIS was equal to zero in most patients, and when present, treatment changes were infrequent and showed no consistent direction. To further exclude the potential impact of treatment modifications not fully captured by ΔTIS, we performed a sensitivity analysis restricted to patients with completely unchanged antihypertensive therapy (ΔTIS = 0, with no change in either drug class or dosage; n = 47 in the CA group and n = 42 in the DA group). Results were consistent with the main analysis, with a greater reduction in BP in the CA group [SBP: −5.0 (−18.0; 5.0) vs. +1.5 (−12.5; 19.5) mmHg, P = 0.057; DBP: −3.0 (−12.0; 3.0) vs. +2.0 (−6.3; 8.0) mmHg in the CA and DA groups, respectively; P = 0.018].

In ANCOVA models performed in the overall population and adjusted for age, baseline BP, and ΔTIS, the association remained significant for SBP (P = 0.037) but not for DBP (P = 0.183). Adjustment for HR did not change the results (data not shown). Additional adjustment for diabetes attenuated these associations, although similar trends persisted (SBP: P = 0.078; DBP: P = 0.145).

In this report, relief of myocardial ischaemia through coronary revascularization was associated with an acute reduction in systemic BP. Several measures were taken to limit confounding inherent to this retrospective observational design, including the use of a control group with broadly comparable clinical profiles, equivalent peri-procedural management (including sedation and nitrate use), and BP assessment ≥12 h after the procedure to minimize transient effects. Baseline cardiovascular medications were well balanced, and no systematic treatment changes occurred between BP assessments. Individual changes in TIS were minimal, and sensitivity analyses restricted to patients with unchanged therapy confirmed the robustness of the findings. Although residual confounding cannot be excluded, these results reasonably support an effect of ischaemia relief on systemic BP. The occurrence of this response following the relief of chronic myocardial ischaemia argues against a purely transient or procedural effect and instead supports the involvement of a regulated mechanism that may extend beyond the immediate post-procedural phase.

Experimental data suggest that coronary pressure-dependent reflexes may influence systemic BP, with slower responses than classical baroreflexes.1–3 In humans, changes in coronary perfusion during cardiac surgery have been associated with reciprocal BP variations, supporting the existence of a potential reflex pathway.6 Evidence in the PCI setting remains limited, with only one non-controlled study reporting delayed BP reduction after revascularization.4 In this context, the magnitude of BP reduction observed in our study was comparable with that reported in second-generation renal denervation trials, supporting a potential sympatho-modulatory component.7 Consistent with our findings, renal denervation trials have generally reported minimal effects on HR, in line with the absence of significant HR variation observed in our study.8

Alternative mechanisms should also be considered. Transient ischaemia during balloon inflation may reduce myocardial workload,9 and incomplete recovery at the time of BP assessment may have contributed to the observed BP difference. Stress-related effects are also possible, although the absence of significant HR changes argues against a major role. Endothelial vasodilation mediated by nitric oxide (NO) represents another plausible mechanism, although its contribution to systemic haemodynamic effects after coronary revascularization remains uncertain. Experimental data suggest dissociation between NO availability and systemic BP, as sustained NO elevation after revascularization does not consistently translate into persistent BP reduction in experimental models.10

If confirmed, these findings may have clinical implications for post-revascularization care, suggesting that antihypertensive therapy may warrant reassessment after relief of significant coronary stenosis to avoid potential overtreatment in patients experiencing secondary BP reduction. They also raise the hypothesis that myocardial ischaemia could represent a modifiable contributor to recent-onset or worsening hypertension, although this remains to be demonstrated.

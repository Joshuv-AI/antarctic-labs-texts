2
Massless vector field in FLRW

The photon propagator in power-law inflation is computed here as a mode sum.
In order to derive this mode sum it is necessary to have the canonical quantization rules,
and to solve for the field operators.
Since canonical quantization in multiplier gauges such as (
1.1
)
requires indefinite metric space of states, it is generally not directly possible to identify
creation/annihilation operators as one would do for e.g. scalar fields. This is due to the
presence of first-class constraints that need to be quantized as conditions on the space
of states. This section is devoted to recapping this canonical quantization procedure
that laid out in detail in
[
45
]
. It relies on the canonical formulation
of the classical theory that is given here after first introducing the basic definitions
of cosmological spaces and power-law inflation in particular.

2.1
FLRW and power-law inflation

Spatially flat cosmological backgrounds are characterized by the
Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) line element,

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)d\vec{x}^{\,2}=a^{2}(\eta)\bigl(-d\eta^{2}+d\vec{x}^{\,2}\bigr)\,,

(2.1)

where
t
is the physical time, and
\eta
the conformal time, the two being related
by
dt\!=\!ad\eta
, and where equal time slices are
(D\!-\!1)
-dimensional Euclidean
spaces. The scale factor
a
encodes the evolution of the expansion.
The expansion rate is captured either by the physical Hubble rate,
or by the conformal Hubble rate, defined respectively by

H=\frac{1}{a}\frac{da}{dt}\,,\qquad\quad\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{a}\frac{da}{d\eta}\,.

(2.2)

The two Hubble rates are related as
\mathcal{H}\!=\!Ha
.
Acceleration is captured by the principal slow-roll parameter,

\epsilon=-\frac{1}{H^{2}}\frac{dH}{dt}=1-\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\frac{d\mathcal{H}}{d\eta}\,,

(2.3)

where
\epsilon\!<\!1
corresponds to accelerated expansion, and
\epsilon\!>\!1

to decelerated expansion. Sometimes the deceleration
parameter
q\!=\!1\!-\!\epsilon
is used instead of the slow-roll parameter.
Power-law inflation is charaterized by a constant principal slow-roll
parameter,
0\!<\!\epsilon\!=\!{\tt const.}\!<\!1
. In this case the scale factor and the Hubble rate
take simple closed form expressions,

\mathcal{H}=\frac{H_{0}}{1-(1\!-\!\epsilon)H_{0}(\eta\!-\!\eta_{0})}\,,\qquad\quad a=\Bigl(\frac{\mathcal{H}}{H_{0}}\Bigr)^{\!\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}}\,.

(2.4)

where
\eta_{0}
is some initial time for which
a(\eta_{0})\!=\!1

and
\mathcal{H}(\eta_{0})\!=\!H_{0}\!=\!H(\eta_{0})
. The de Sitter space limit is
obtained by
\epsilon\!\to\!0
.

When considering the two-point functions in later sections it will be convenient to express
them in terms of bi-local scalar variables. The first of these is the distance function,

y(x;x^{\prime})=(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\Bigl[\|\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime}\|^{2}-(\eta\!-\!\eta^{\prime})^{2}\Bigr]\,,

(2.5)

that is related to the geodesic distance. In the de Sitter limit this quantity is invariant
under all de Sitter symmetries. Cosmological backgrounds, including power-law inflation,
admits only the cosmological symmetries, and two more variables bi-local variables are necessary,

u(x;x^{\prime})=(1\!-\!\epsilon)\ln(aa^{\prime})\,,\qquad\quad v(x;x^{\prime})=(1\!-\!\epsilon)\ln(a/a^{\prime})\,.

(2.6)

Here and henceforth primes on quantities denote them to be
related to the primed coordinate in bi-local objects (e.g.
a^{\prime}\!=\!a(\eta^{\prime})
),
while unprimed quantities are related to the unprimed coordinate.

2.2
Classical gauge-invariant canonical formulation

The first step towards canonical quantization of the photon field
A_{\mu}
,
defined by the covariant Maxwell action,

S[A_{\mu}]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\biggl[-\frac{1}{4}g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}\biggr]\,,

(2.7)

where
F_{\mu\nu}\!=\!\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}\!-\!\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
is the vector field strength tensor,
is to derive the classical canonical formulation. For that it is useful to first specialize the
action above to FLRW backgrounds and to decompose the indices into temporal and spatial ones,

S[A_{\mu}]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,a^{D-4}\,\biggl[\frac{1}{2}F_{0i}F_{0i}-\frac{1}{4}F_{ij}F_{ij}\biggr]\,.

(2.8)

Henceforth all decomposed indices are written as subscripts, and repeated spatial indices
are assumed to be summed over.
The intermediate step in deriving the canonical action is constructing the
extended action
[
63
]
,
4
4

4

This step is equivalent to the standard
Legendre transform, but is much better adapted to off-shell applications, and to theories
with constraints.

obtained by promoting time derivatives to independent velocity fields,

\partial_{0}A_{0}\longrightarrow V_{0}\,,\qquad\quad F_{0i}\longrightarrow V_{i}\,,

(2.9)

and introducing accompanying Lagrange multiplier fields,
\pi_{0}
and
\pi_{i}
,
to ensure on-shell equivalence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\bigl[A_{0},V_{0},\Pi_{0},A_{i},V_{i},\Pi_{i}\bigr]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,\biggl\{a^{D-4}\biggl[\frac{1}{2}V_{i}V_{i}-\frac{1}{4}F_{ij}F_{ij}\biggr]

(2.10)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\Pi_{0}\Bigl(\partial_{0}A_{0}-V_{0}\Bigr)+\Pi_{i}\Bigl(\partial_{0}A_{i}-\partial_{i}A_{0}-V_{i}\Bigr)\biggr\}\,.

Then solving on-shell for as many velocity fields as possible, which in this case is only
V_{i}
,

\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}}{\delta V_{i}}\approx 0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad V_{i}\approx\overline{V}_{i}=a^{4-D}\Pi_{i}\,,

(2.11)

produces the desired canonical action upon plugging the solutions back into
the extended action,

\mathscr{S}\bigl[A_{0},\Pi_{0},A_{i},\Pi_{i},\ell\bigr]\equiv\mathcal{S}\bigl[A_{0},V_{0}\to\ell,\Pi_{0},A_{i},\overline{V}_{i},\Pi_{i}\bigr]=\!\!\int\!d^{D\!}x\,\Bigl[\Pi_{0}\partial_{0}A_{0}+\Pi_{i}\partial_{0}A_{i}-\mathscr{H}-\ell\Psi_{1}\Bigr]\,,

(2.12)

where the canonical Hamiltonian is

\mathscr{H}=\frac{1}{2}a^{4-D}\Pi_{i}\Pi_{i}+\Pi_{i}\partial_{i}A_{0}+\frac{1}{4}a^{D-4}F_{ij}F_{ij}\,.

(2.13)

Note that the field
V_{0}
was relabeled to
\ell
in order to emphasize its
interpretation as the Lagrange multiplier
generating the primary constraint
\Psi_{1}\!=\!\Pi_{0}
. Also note that
Dirac’s notation for on-shell (weak) equalities “
\approx
” is used,
to distinguish them from the off-shell (strong) equalities denoted by “
=
”,
that apply at the level of the action.

Canonical equations of motion follow from varying the canonical action (
2.12
)
with respect to dynamical fields,

\partial_{0}A_{0}\approx\ell\,,\qquad\partial_{0}\Pi_{0}\approx\partial_{i}\Pi_{i}\,,\qquad\partial_{0}A_{i}\approx a^{4-D}\Pi_{0}+\partial_{i}A_{0}\,,\qquad\partial_{0}\Pi_{i}\approx a^{D-4}\partial_{j}F_{ji}\,,

(2.14)

They can be put in the form of Hamilton’s equations using
the Poisson brackets that follow from the symplectic part of the canonical
action,

\bigl\{A_{0}(\eta,\vec{x}),\Pi_{0}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr\}=\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,,\qquad\bigl\{A_{i}(\eta,\vec{x}),\Pi_{j}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr\}=\delta_{ij}\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,,

(2.15)

with the remaining brackets vanishing. Varying the canonical action with respect to the
Lagrange multiplier
\ell
yields the primary constraint,

\Psi_{1}=\Pi_{0}\approx 0\,,

(2.16)

while its conservation generates a secondary constraint,

\partial_{0}\Psi_{1}\approx\partial_{i}\Pi_{i}\equiv\Psi_{2}\approx 0\,.

(2.17)

The two constraints form a complete set of first-class constraints,
meaning that their Poisson brackets vanish on-shell,

\bigl\{\Psi_{I}(\eta,\vec{x}),\Psi_{J}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr\}\approx 0\,,\qquad I,J=1,2\,,

(2.18)

and hence no further constraints are generated by the conservation of the
secondary constraint.

2.3
Classical gauge-fixed canonical formulation

Imposing a multiplier gauge
[
64
]
(also called average gauge)
amounts to fixing
\ell
off-shell in the canonical action (
2.12
)
to be a function of canonical variables.
This procedure is shortcutted by adding the gauge-fixing term
S_{\rm gf}[A_{\mu}]

to the gauge-invariant action (
2.7
), thus defining the gauge-fixed action,

S_{\star}[A_{\mu}]=S[A_{\mu}]+S_{\rm gf}[A_{\mu}]\,.

(2.19)

The gauge-fixing term in (
1.1
) considered here preserves cosmological
symmetries, and evaluated on the FLRW background reads

S_{\rm gf}[A_{\mu}]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,a^{D-4}\,\biggl[-\frac{1}{2\xi}\Bigl(\partial_{0}A_{0}+(D\!-\!2\!-\!2\zeta)\mathcal{H}A_{0}-\partial_{i}A_{i}\Bigr)^{\!2}\,\biggr]\,.

(2.20)

The canonical formulation of this action is derived
following the same procedure as in Sec.
2.2
.
The difference is that the gauge-fixed action is not longer gauge-invariant, so that
no constraints are generated. The resulting canonical gauge-fixed action takes the form

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{\star}[A_{0},\Pi_{0},A_{i},\Pi_{i}]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,\Bigl[\Pi_{0}\partial_{0}A_{0}+\Pi_{i}\partial_{0}A_{i}-\mathscr{H}_{\star}\Bigr]\,,

(2.21)

where the gauge-fixed Hamiltonian is
[
45
]

\mathscr{H}_{\star}=\frac{a^{4-D}}{2}\Bigl(\Pi_{i}\Pi_{i}-\xi\Pi_{0}\Pi_{0}\Bigr)+\Pi_{i}\partial_{i}A_{0}+\Pi_{0}\partial_{i}A_{i}-(D\!-\!2\!-\!2\zeta)\mathcal{H}\Pi_{0}A_{0}+\frac{a^{D-4}}{4}F_{ij}F_{ij}\,.

(2.22)

It can now be seen that this corresponds to the following choice for the Lagrange multiplier in (
2.12
),

\ell\longrightarrow-\frac{\xi a^{4-D}}{2}\Pi_{0}+\partial_{i}A_{i}-(D\!-\!2\!-\!2\zeta)\mathcal{H}A_{0}\,.

(2.23)

The gauge-fixed equations of motion now follow from varying the gauge-fixed canonical action
with respect to the dynamical fields

\displaystyle\partial_{0}A_{0}\approx{}

\displaystyle-\xi a^{4-D}\Pi_{0}+\partial_{i}A_{i}-(D\!-\!2\!-\!2\zeta)\mathcal{H}A_{0}\,,

(2.24a)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\Pi_{0}\approx{}

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\Pi_{i}+(D\!-\!2\!-\!2\zeta)\mathcal{H}\Pi_{0}\,,

(2.24b)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}A_{i}\approx{}

\displaystyle a^{4-D}\Pi_{i}+\partial_{i}A_{0}\,,

(2.24c)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\Pi_{i}\approx{}

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\Pi_{0}+a^{D-4}\partial_{j}F_{ji}\,,

(2.24d)

and they can be written as Hamiton’s equations utilizing
the same Poisson brackets (
2.15
).

Note that the gauge-fixed action encodes for the gauge-fixed dynamics,
but does not encode the first-class constraints in (
2.16
) and (
2.17
).
These have to be demanded separately, in addition to the gauge-fixed action,
at the level of initial conditions given at some initial value surface,

\Psi_{1}(\eta_{0},\vec{x})=\Pi_{0}(\eta_{0},\vec{x})\approx 0\,,\qquad\Psi_{2}(\eta_{0},\vec{x})=\partial_{i}\Pi_{i}(\eta_{0},\vec{x})\approx 0\,.

(2.25)

Then the gauge-fixed dynamics ensures that these are conserved in time.

2.4
Quantizing photon in FLRW

Quantizing the photon field in multiplier gauges requires two ingredients:
quantizing the dynamics, and quantizing the constraints. The former is straightforward
and follows the rules of canonical quantization for theories without constraints. This
entails promoting the dynamical fields to field operators,

A_{\mu}(x)\longrightarrow\hat{A}_{\mu}(x)\,,\qquad\quad\Pi_{\mu}(x)\longrightarrow\hat{\Pi}_{\mu}(x)\,,

(2.26)

and their Poisson brackets (
2.15
) to commutators,

\bigl[\hat{A}_{0}(\eta,\vec{x}),\hat{\Pi}_{0}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=i\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,,\qquad\bigl[\hat{A}_{i}(\eta,\vec{x}),\hat{\Pi}_{j}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\delta_{ij}\,i\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,.

(2.27)

Equations of motion for these field operators are the same as the classical
gauge-fixed equations (
2.24
).
Constraints (
2.25
) are quantized as conditions on the
indefinite metric space of states.
Before implementing the these, it is advantageous to first
decompose the field operators into different sectors, and to introduce their
momentum space formulation.

Helmholtz decomposition and momentum space.

It is convenient to break up the spatial components of field operators into
transverse and longitudinal parts,

\hat{A}_{i}=\hat{A}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}+\hat{A}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\Pi}_{i}=\hat{\Pi}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}+\hat{\Pi}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\,,

(2.28)

such that the individual parts,

\hat{A}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\hat{A}_{j}\,,\qquad\hat{A}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\hat{A}_{j}\,,\qquad\hat{\Pi}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\hat{\Pi}_{j}\,,\qquad\hat{\Pi}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\hat{\Pi}_{j}\,,

(2.29)

are defined in terms of transverse ad longitudinal projection operators,

\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}=\delta_{ij}-\frac{\partial_{i}\partial_{j}}{\nabla^{2}}\,,\qquad\quad\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}=\frac{\partial_{i}\partial_{j}}{\nabla^{2}}\,,

(2.30)

These projectors are idempotent,
\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\mathbb{P}_{jk}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\!=\!\mathbb{P}_{ik}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}
,

\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\mathbb{P}_{jk}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\!=\!\mathbb{P}_{ik}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}
,
and mutually
orthogonal,
\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\mathbb{P}_{jk}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\!=\!\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\mathbb{P}_{jk}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\!=\!0
.
The utility of the Helmholtz decomposition is in decoupling the transverse sector from the
scalar sector (containing longitudinal and temporal components).

The transverse sector field operators have the following momentum space expansion

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}_{i}(\eta,\vec{x})=a^{-\frac{D-4}{2}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{D-2}\varepsilon_{i}(\sigma,\vec{k})\,\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(2.31a)

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}_{i}(\eta,\vec{x})=a^{\frac{D-4}{2}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{D-2}\varepsilon_{i}(\sigma,\vec{k})\,\hat{\pi}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(2.31b)

where the transverse polarization tensor satisfies

\displaystyle k_{i}\,\varepsilon_{i}(\sigma,\vec{k})=0\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}^{*}(\sigma,\vec{k})=\varepsilon_{i}(\sigma,-\vec{k})\,,

(2.32a)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}^{*}(\sigma,\vec{k})\varepsilon_{i}(\sigma^{\prime},\vec{k})=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\qquad

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma=1}^{D-2}\varepsilon_{i}^{*}(\sigma,\vec{k})\varepsilon_{j}(\sigma,\vec{k})=\delta_{ij}-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}\,.

(2.32b)

Note that powers of the scale factor have been extracted for later convenience
from the Fourier transform in (
2.31
).
In momentum space Hermiticity takes a different form,

\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}^{\dagger}(\vec{k})=\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}(-\vec{k})\,,\qquad\qquad\hat{\pi}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}^{\dagger}(\vec{k})=\hat{\pi}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}(-\vec{k})\,,

(2.33)

while the non-vanishing canonical commutation relations read

\bigl[\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k}),\hat{\pi}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma^{\prime}}(\eta,\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\,i\delta^{D-1}(\vec{k}\!+\!\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\,.

(2.34)

The transverse sector equations of motion, decoupled from the scalar sector, are

\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}=\hat{\pi}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!4)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}\,,\qquad\partial_{0}\hat{\pi}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}=-k^{2}\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!4)\mathcal{H}\hat{\pi}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}\,.

(2.35)

The momentum sapce expansions of the scalar sector field operator read

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{0}(\eta,\vec{x})=a^{-\frac{D-2-2\zeta}{2}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\,\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{0}^{\dagger}(\vec{k})=\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(-\vec{k})\,,

(2.36a)

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{0}(\eta,\vec{x})=a^{\frac{D-2-2\zeta}{2}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\,\hat{\pi}_{0}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{0}^{\dagger}(\vec{k})=\hat{\pi}_{0}(-\vec{k})\,,

(2.36b)

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}_{i}(\eta,\vec{x})=a^{-\frac{D-2-2\zeta}{2}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\Bigl(-\frac{ik_{i}}{k}\Bigr)\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}^{\dagger}(\vec{k})=\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(-\vec{k})\,,

(2.36c)

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}_{i}(\eta,\vec{x})=a^{\frac{D-2-2\zeta}{2}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\Bigl(-\frac{ik_{i}}{k}\Bigr)\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}^{\dagger}(\vec{k})=\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(-\vec{k})\,.

(2.36d)

where different powers of scale factors are extracted from the Fourier transform
in order to obtain simpler equations of motion.
The non-vanishing canonical commutation relations of the scalar sector are

\bigl[\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\eta,\vec{k}),\hat{\pi}_{0}(\eta,\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\bigl[\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm L}(\eta,\vec{k}),\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm L}(\eta,\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=i\delta^{D-1}(\vec{k}\!+\!\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\,.

(2.37)

while the momentum space equations of motion read

(2.38)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\pi}_{0}=k\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm L}+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2\!-\!2\zeta)\mathcal{H}\hat{\pi}_{0}\,,

(2.39)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm L}=a^{2-2\zeta}\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm L}-k\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2\!-\!2\zeta)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm L}\,,

(2.40)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm L}=-k\hat{\pi}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2\!-\!2\zeta)\mathcal{H}\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm L}\,.

(2.41)

Quantized constraints.

The primary constraints (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) are functions
of the field operators, and are consequently quantized when those fields are promoted
to field operators. The Fourier space expansion of these two Hermitian constraint operators reads

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}_{1}(\eta,\vec{x})={}

\displaystyle a^{\frac{D-2-2\zeta}{2}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{k}}\hat{\psi}_{1}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(2.42a)

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}_{2}(\eta,\vec{x})={}

\displaystyle a^{\frac{D-2-2\zeta}{2}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{k}}\,k\,\hat{\psi}_{2}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(2.42b)

where,

\hat{\psi}_{1}=\hat{\pi}_{0}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\psi}_{2}=\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}\,.

(2.43)

In the quantized theory the constraints are implemented
as conditions on the space of states. These conditions define the subspace
of physical states, and have to be implemented in the form of a non-Hermitian
linear combination of Hermitian constraint operators
[
45
]
,

\hat{\mathcal{K}}(\vec{k})=c_{1}(\eta,k)\hat{\psi}_{1}(\eta,\vec{k})+c_{2}(\eta,k)\hat{\psi}_{2}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(2.44)

annihilating the physical state,

\hat{\mathcal{K}}(\vec{k})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,.\qquad\quad\forall\vec{k}\,.

(2.45)

This condition guarantees that any matrix element of the product of Hermitian
constraints vanishes. The time-independence of the non-Hermitian subsidiary constraint
operator implies equations of motion for the coefficient functions in (
2.44
),

\partial_{0}c_{1}=kc_{2}-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2\!-\!2\zeta)\mathcal{H}c_{1}\,,\qquad\partial_{0}c_{2}=-kc_{1}+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2\!-\!2\zeta)\mathcal{H}c_{2}\,.

(2.46)

3
Scalar mode functions and two-point functions

The computation of photon mode functions and photon two-point functions
in subsequent sections greatly benefits from recalling properties of scalar mode
functions and scalar two-point functions in power-law inflation, that are collected here.

3.1
Scalar mode functions

The rescaled mode function of a nonminimally coupled scalar field in power-law
inflation satisfies the following ubiquitous equation of motion,

\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\lambda^{2}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\mathscr{U}_{\lambda}(\eta,k)=0\,,

(3.1)

where the constant
\lambda
parametrized the connection to the nonminnimal coupling.
The general solution to this equation,

\mathscr{U}_{\lambda}(\eta,\vec{k})=\alpha(\vec{k})\,U_{\lambda}(\eta,k)+\beta(\vec{k})\,U_{\lambda}^{*}(\eta,k)\,,

(3.2)

is a linear combination of the fundamental positive-frequency mode function,

U_{\lambda}(\eta,k)=e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}(2\lambda+1)}e^{\frac{-ik}{(1-\epsilon)H_{0}}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{4(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}}}\,H^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{\lambda}\biggl(\frac{k}{(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}}\biggr)\,,

(3.3)

and its complex conjugate, where
H^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{\lambda}
is the Hankel function of the
first kind.
This is the power-law inflation generalization of the Chernikov-Tagirov-Bunch-Davies
mode function in de Sitter space
[
65
,
66
]
,
and its normalization is given by the Wronskian,

U_{\lambda}(\eta,k)\partial_{0}U_{\lambda}^{*}(\eta,k)-U_{\lambda}^{*}(\eta,k)\partial_{0}U_{\lambda}(\eta,k)=i\,.

(3.4)

The flat space limit of (
3.3
) is obtained by taking
H_{0}\!\to\!0
, where first two
subleading orders will be needed in later sections,

\displaystyle U_{\lambda}(\eta,k)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-ik(\eta-\eta_{0})}}{\sqrt{2k}}\biggl\{1+\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\lambda^{2}\Bigr)\Bigl[\frac{(1\!-\!\epsilon)H_{0}}{ik}\Bigr]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8}\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\lambda^{2}\Bigr)\biggl[\Bigl(\frac{9}{4}\!-\!\lambda^{2}\Bigr)+4ik(\eta\!-\!\eta_{0})\biggr]\Bigl[\frac{(1\!-\!\epsilon)H_{0}}{ik}\Bigr]^{2}+\dots\biggr\}\,.

(3.5)

There are two useful recurrence relations for raising and lowering the mode function index,

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!+\!\lambda\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}\biggr]U_{\lambda}=-ikU_{\lambda+1}\,,\qquad\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\lambda\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}\biggr]U_{\lambda}=-ikU_{\lambda-1}\,,

(3.6)

that follow from recurrence relations for Hankel functions
[
67
,
68
]
,
and allow the Wronskian (
3.4
) to be written in a compact form,

{\rm Re}\Bigl[U_{\lambda}(\eta,k)U_{\lambda-1}^{*}(\eta,k)\Bigr]=\frac{1}{2k}\,.

(3.7)

Further identities that are used in later sections
follow from the mode equation (
3.1
) and the
two recurrence relations (
3.6
):

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}\!+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\lambda^{2}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\Bigl(\frac{U_{\lambda-1}}{\mathcal{H}}\Bigr)=2(1\!-\!\epsilon)ikU_{\lambda}\,,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}\!+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\lambda^{2}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\bigl(\mathcal{H}U_{\lambda-1}\bigr)\!=\!-2(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}^{2}\Bigl[ikU_{\lambda}\!+\!2(\lambda\!-\!1)(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}U_{\lambda-1}\Bigr],\,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}\!+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\lambda^{2}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\frac{\partial U_{\lambda}}{\partial\lambda}=2\lambda(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}U_{\lambda}\,.

(3.10)

The first two follow from simply acting the derivatives on the left-hand side,
and recognizing the equation of motion and the index-raising recurrence relation,
while the third one follows from a parametric derivative of the equation of motion.

\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{-\bigl[(1\!-\!\epsilon)H_{0}\bigr]^{D-2}\,\Gamma(\lambda)\,\Gamma(2\lambda)}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}\,\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!+\!\lambda\bigr)\,\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D-1}{2}\bigr)\,\bigl(\frac{D-1}{2}\!-\!\lambda\bigr)}\Bigl[\frac{k_{0}^{2}e^{-u}}{(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}\Bigr]^{\frac{D-1}{2}-\lambda}\,.

(3.26)

4
Dynamics in power-law inflation

In this section the equations of motion for the photon field operators are solved
for two particularly simple choices for the gauge-fixing parameter
\zeta
.
This is accomplished by making use of the results for the scalar mode functions collected
in Sec.
3.1
. Transverse and scalar sectors are considered
separately.

4.1
Dynamics of the transverse sector

The two first order equations of motion for the transverse
sector (
2.35
) combine into a second order one,

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\nu^{2}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}={}

\displaystyle 0\,,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}={}

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\nu\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}\,,

(4.2)

where the index of the equation is

\nu=\frac{D\!-\!3\!-\!\epsilon}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)}\,.

(4.3)

Making use of the fundamental solution of the scalar mode equation (
3.3
),
and the index-lowering recurrence relation (
3.6
), the solutions
for the field operators follow

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})=U_{\nu}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\sigma,\vec{k})+U_{\nu}^{*}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{\dagger}(\sigma,-\vec{k})\,,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})=-ikU_{\nu-1}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\sigma,\vec{k})+ikU_{\nu-1}^{*}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{\dagger}(\sigma,-\vec{k})\,.

(4.5)

The operator
\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}
and its conjugate are the annihilation and creation
operators satisfying canonical commutation relations,

\bigl[\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\sigma,\vec{k}),\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{\dagger}(\sigma^{\prime},\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\,\delta^{D-1}(\vec{k}\!-\!\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\,,

(4.6)

that can be computed with the help of the Wronskian (
3.7
).
Then it is natural to consider the vacuum state of the transverse sector defined
by

\hat{b}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T},\sigma}(\vec{k})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\quad\forall\vec{k},\sigma\,.

(4.7)

4.2
Dynamics of the scalar sector

Equations of motion (
2.39
)
and (
2.41
) for canonical momenta
decouple from the other two equations of the scalar sector.
They can be combined into a second order equation,

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\nu_{\zeta}^{2}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\hat{\pi}_{0}={}

\displaystyle 0\,,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\nu_{\zeta}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}\biggr]\hat{\pi}_{0}\,,

(4.9)

where the index of the equation is

\nu_{\zeta}=\frac{D\!-\!1\!-\!\epsilon\!-\!2\zeta}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)}=\nu+\frac{1\!-\!\zeta}{1\!-\!\epsilon}\,,

(4.10)

with
\nu
defined in (
4.3
).
The solutions for canonical momenta immediatly follow

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{0}(\eta,\vec{k})=ikU_{\nu_{\zeta}}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\vec{k})-ikU_{\nu_{\zeta}}^{*}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})\,,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(\eta,\vec{k})=kU_{\nu_{\zeta}-1}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\vec{k})+kU_{\nu_{\zeta}-1}^{*}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})\,,

(4.12)

where operators
\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\vec{k})
and its conjugate account for the initial
constants of integration, and where the overall normalization was chosen for convenience
to correspond to the one used in
[
45
]
.

The remaining two equations (
2.38
)
and (
2.40
)
for vector potential field operators can be combined into an
inhomogeneous second order equation,

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\nu_{\zeta}^{2}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}={}

\displaystyle a^{2-2\zeta}\Bigl[2(1\!-\!\zeta)\mathcal{H}\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}-(1\!-\!\xi)k\hat{\pi}_{0}\Bigr]\,,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{0}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\nu_{\zeta}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}+\frac{a^{2-2\zeta}}{k}\hat{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}\,.

(4.14)

Using the results from Sec.
3.1
, the solutions can
be written as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(\eta,\vec{k})={}

\displaystyle-iU_{\nu_{\zeta}}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}(\vec{k})+iU_{\nu_{\zeta}}^{*}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})-iv_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\vec{k})+iv_{\scriptscriptstyle L}^{*}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})\,,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\eta,\vec{k})={}

\displaystyle U_{\nu_{\zeta}-1}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}(\vec{k})+U_{\nu_{\zeta}-1}^{*}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})+v_{0}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\vec{k})+v_{0}^{*}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})\,,

(4.16)

where the particular mode functions satisfy,

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\nu_{\zeta}^{2}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}={}

\displaystyle a^{2-2\zeta}\Bigl[2(1\!-\!\zeta)ik\mathcal{H}U_{\nu_{\zeta}-1}+(1\!-\!\xi)k^{2}U_{\nu_{\zeta}}\Bigr],

(4.17)

\displaystyle v_{0}={}

\displaystyle\frac{i}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\nu_{\zeta}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}\biggr]v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}+a^{2-2\zeta}U_{\nu_{\zeta}-1}\,.

(4.18)

and where
\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}(\vec{k})
and its conjugate account for constants of
integration. Solving these equations is somewhat more involved, and is postponed until
Sec.
4.3
. However, the normalization
of particular mode functions can be derived straightforwardly.
The equation of motion (
4.17
) for the longitudinal particular mode function, and the
Wronskian (
3.7
) imply the following relation,

\partial_{0}\,{\rm Im}\Bigl[v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(\eta,k)\,\partial_{0}U_{\nu_{\zeta}}^{*}(\eta,k)-\partial_{0}v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(\eta,k)\,U_{\nu_{\zeta}}^{*}(\eta,k)\Bigr]=-(1\!-\!\zeta)a^{2-2\zeta}\mathcal{H}\,.

(4.19)

Integrating this expression now gives,

{\rm Im}\Bigl[v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(\eta,k)\,\partial_{0}U_{\nu_{\zeta}}^{*}(\eta,k)-\partial_{0}v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(\eta,k)\,U_{\nu_{\zeta}}^{*}(\eta,k)\Bigr]=-\frac{a^{2-2\zeta}}{2}\,.

(4.20)

where the constant of integration is fixed according to the conventions
of
[
45
]
. By using Eq. (
4.18
)
and the recurrence relation for scalar mode function (
3.6
)
this condition can be rewritten in the form of a Wronskian-like relation,

{\rm Re}\Bigl[v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(\eta,k)U_{\nu_{\zeta}-1}^{*}(\eta,k)+v_{0}(\eta,k)U_{\nu_{\zeta}}^{*}(\eta,k)\Bigr]=0\,.

(4.21)

These conditions are then used to infer the commutators between the
constant momentum space operators, where the nonvanishing ones are

\bigl[\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}(\vec{k}),\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}^{\dagger}(\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\bigl[\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\vec{k}),\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}^{\dagger}(\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=-\delta^{D-1}(\vec{k}\!-\!\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\,.

(4.22)

While these operators can be canonicalized to satisfy the canonical relations for
creation/annihilation operators, it is not necessary to do so
[
45
]
.

4.3
Solving for particular mode functions

Equations of motion for the particular mode functions (
4.17
) and (
4.17
)
can be solved for arbitrary values of
\xi
and
\zeta
. However, their solution
is rather unwieldy, and involves
{}_{3}F_{2}
generalized hypergeometric functions
(cf. Appendix A of
[
51
]
). However, for discreet choices fo
\zeta

the solutions simplify considerably, and are expressible in terms of the CTBD scalar mode
functions (
3.3
), and a finite number of their derivatives. For this to be the case,
the time-dependent factor
a^{2-2\zeta}
multiplying the source term in (
4.17
)
has to be proportional to an even power (either positive or negative) of the conformal
Hubble rate,

a^{2-2\zeta}=\Bigl(\frac{\mathcal{H}}{H_{0}}\Bigr)^{\!2n}\,,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(4.23)

This puts a condition on the values of the second gauge-fixing parameter
\zeta

that lead to practical and simple solutions for the photon mode functions,

\zeta=1-n(1\!-\!\epsilon)\,.

(4.24)

Henceforth, when referring to
\zeta
we assume that it takes discreet values
given by the relation above, unles stated otherwise explicitly.
This imples that the index defined in (
4.10
)
also takes discreet values,

\nu_{\zeta}=\nu+n\,.

(4.25)

In such discreet
\zeta
gauges the equations of motion for particular mode functions read

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!(\nu\!+\!n)^{2}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}=\Bigl(\frac{\mathcal{H}}{H_{0}}\Bigr)^{\!2n}\Bigl[2n(1\!-\!\epsilon)ik\mathcal{H}U_{\nu+n-1}+(1\!-\!\xi)k^{2}U_{\nu+n}\Bigr]\,,

(4.26)

\displaystyle v_{0}={}

\displaystyle\frac{i}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!(\nu\!+\!n)\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}\biggr]v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}+\Bigl(\frac{\mathcal{H}}{H_{0}}\Bigr)^{\!2n}U_{\nu+n-1}\,.

(4.27)

These equations can be solved for any
n
, which is done in
Appendix
A
. However,
almost all cases end up being impractical when constructing the two-point function
in Sec.
5
. That is why here only two simple choices are
considered: the conformal gauge for which
n\!=\!0
, and
the deceleration gauge for which
n\!=\!1
. The solutions for
particular mode functions in these two one-parameter families of gauges are worked
out in the remainder of this section, in both cases normalized by the requirement
that in flat space they reduce to

\displaystyle v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}\xrightarrow{H_{0}\to 0}\frac{1}{4}\Bigl[-(1\!+\!\xi)+2(1\!-\!\xi)ik(\eta\!-\!\eta_{0})\Bigr]\frac{e^{-ik(\eta-\eta_{0})}}{\sqrt{2k}}\,.

(4.28)

\displaystyle v_{0}\xrightarrow{H_{0}\to 0}\frac{1}{4}\Bigl[(1\!+\!\xi)+2(1\!-\!\xi)ik(\eta\!-\!\eta_{0})\Bigr]\frac{e^{-ik(\eta-\eta_{0})}}{\sqrt{2k}}\,,

(4.29)

Henceforth we label the first gauge-fixing parameter differently in each
gauge:
\xi\!=\!\alpha
for the conformal gauge, and
\xi\!=\!\beta
for the deceleration
gauge, in order to distinguish between them more easily, while
\zeta
still stands for the
general case.

Conformal gauge (
\boldsymbol{n\!=\!0}
).

The equations of motion for particular mode functions in this case read

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\nu^{2}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}={}

\displaystyle(1\!-\!\alpha)k^{2}U_{\nu}\,,

(4.30)

\displaystyle v_{0}={}

\displaystyle\frac{i}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\nu\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}\biggr]v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}+U_{\nu-1}\,,

(4.31)

and are solved by,

\displaystyle v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}=-\frac{(1\!-\!\alpha)ik}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)}\biggl[\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}U_{\nu-1}-\frac{1}{H_{0}}U_{\nu}\biggr]-\frac{1\!-\!(1\!-\!\alpha)\nu}{2}U_{\nu}\,,

(4.32)

\displaystyle v_{0}=-\frac{(1\!-\!\alpha)ik}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)}\biggl[\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}U_{\nu}-\frac{1}{H_{0}}U_{\nu-1}\biggr]+\frac{1\!-\!(1\!-\!\alpha)\nu}{2}U_{\nu-1}\,.

(4.33)

Checking that these indeed solve (
4.30
)
and (
4.31
)
involves using the equation of motion (
3.1
)
for the scalar mode function and the identity (
3.8
).
It is clear that
\alpha\!=\!1
is the simplest choice as it turns
particular solutions into homogeneous ones, which justifies referring to
this choice as the
simple conformal gauge
.
This gauge choice is known to be simplest choice in the de Sitter limit
[
52
]
.
In a sense this gauge can rightfully be considered the generalization of the
Feynman gauge in flat space to power-law inflation.

Deceleration gauge (
\boldsymbol{n\!=\!1}
).

The particular mode functions
in this case satisfy,

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!(\nu\!+\!1)^{2}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}={}

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{\mathcal{H}}{H_{0}}\Bigr)^{\!2}\biggl[2(1\!-\!\epsilon)ik\mathcal{H}U_{\nu}+(1\!-\!\beta)k^{2}U_{\nu+1}\biggr]\,,

(4.34)

\displaystyle v_{0}={}

\displaystyle\frac{i}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!(\nu\!+\!1)\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}\biggr]v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}+\Bigl(\frac{\mathcal{H}}{H_{0}}\Bigr)^{\!2}U_{\nu}\,.

(4.35)

Making use of identities (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) it can be seen
that solutions are,

\displaystyle v_{\scriptscriptstyle L}=\frac{-ik}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)H_{0}}\biggl\{\frac{\beta}{\nu\!+\!1}\biggl[\frac{\mathcal{H}}{H_{0}}U_{\nu}-U_{\nu+1}\biggr]+\Bigl(1\!-\!\frac{\beta}{\beta_{s}}\Bigr)\biggl[\frac{ik}{\nu(1\!-\!\epsilon)H_{0}}\frac{\partial U_{\nu+1}}{\partial\nu}+\frac{\mathcal{H}}{\nu H_{0}}U_{\nu}+U_{\nu+1}\biggr]\biggr\}\,,

(4.36)

\displaystyle v_{0}=\frac{-ik}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)H_{0}}\biggl\{\frac{\beta}{\nu\!+\!1}\biggl[\frac{\mathcal{H}}{H_{0}}U_{\nu+1}-U_{\nu}\biggr]+\Bigl(1\!-\!\frac{\beta}{\beta_{s}}\Bigr)\biggl[\frac{ik}{\nu(1\!-\!\epsilon)H_{0}}\frac{\partial U_{\nu}}{\partial\nu}+U_{\nu}\biggr]\biggr\}\,.

(4.37)

where parameter another parameter is introduced,

\beta_{s}=\frac{\nu\!+\!1}{\nu}=\frac{D\!-\!1\!-\!3\epsilon}{D\!-\!3\!-\!\epsilon}\,.

(4.38)

Showing that these solutions indeed satisfy Eqs. (
4.34
) and (
4.35
)
requires the use of scalar mode equation of motion (
3.1
),
the related identity (
3.9
),
and the recurrence relations (
3.6
).
The special case
\beta\!=\!\beta_{s}
gives the simplest result for the particular
mode functions, and it is thus referred to as the
simple deceleration gauge
.

Dynamics of constraints

Equations of motion for the coefficient functions of constraints (

can likewise be combined into a homogeneous second order equation,

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\nu_{\zeta}^{2}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]c_{2}={}

\displaystyle 0\,,

(4.39)

\displaystyle c_{1}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\nu_{\zeta}\Bigr)(1\!-\!\epsilon)\mathcal{H}\biggr]c_{2}\,.

(4.40)

The solutions are chosen to be consistent with the Lorentz
invariance
[
59
,
60
]

for

in both gauges, and the de Sitter invariance
[
46

for

in the deceleration gauge,

c_{1}(\eta,k)=-iU_{\nu_{\zeta}-1}^{*}(\eta,k)\,,\qquad\quad c_{2}(\eta,k)=U_{\nu_{\zeta}}^{*}(\eta,k)\,.

The non-Hermitian constraint operator (
2.44

\hat{\mathcal{K}}(\vec{k})=\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\vec{k})\,,

(4.42)

from where it follows that the scalar sector of the state must be defined by

\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\vec{k})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\quad\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}(\vec{k})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\quad\forall\vec{k}\,.

(4.43)

5
Two-point functions

The solutions for field operators, and the definitions of the state determined in the
preceding section are sufficient to compute the photon two-point functions in
the conformal gauge and in the deceleration gauge. These two-point functions have
to satisfy both their respective equations of motion and the respective Ward-Takahashi
identities, that are recalled first. This is followed by computing the momentum space
representation of the two-point functions in terms of mode sums. These mode sums are
then evaluated to obtain the position space two-point functions, that are then written in
the covariantized form.

i\bigl[\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-9.25307pt{\tt a}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt b}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-9.25307pt{\tt a}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt b}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-6.03194pt{\tt a}\kern 4.08334pt{\tt b}}}_{{\kern-6.30338pt{\mu}\kern 4.08334pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-4.86526pt{\tt a}\kern 2.91667pt{\tt b}}}_{{\kern-5.1367pt{\mu}\kern 2.91667pt{\nu}}}}\bigr](x;x^{\prime})=i\bigl[\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-9.25307pt{\tt a}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt b}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-9.25307pt{\tt a}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt b}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-6.03194pt{\tt a}\kern 4.08334pt{\tt b}}}_{{\kern-6.30338pt{\mu}\kern 4.08334pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-4.86526pt{\tt a}\kern 2.91667pt{\tt b}}}_{{\kern-5.1367pt{\mu}\kern 2.91667pt{\nu}}}}\bigr](x;x^{\prime})\Bigr|_{\beta=\beta_{s}}-(\beta_{s}\!-\!\beta)\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\prime}_{\nu}\biggl[\frac{e^{-\frac{(D-4)\epsilon}{2(1-\epsilon)}u}}{2(\nu\!+\!1)(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}_{\nu+1}}{\partial\nu}\biggr]\,.

(5.44)

6
Various limits

In order to establish connection with the existing literature and known results it is useful
to examine several available limits that are considered in this section.

6.1
De Sitter limit

The de Sitter limit is obtained by taking the limit
\epsilon\!\to\!0
in which
the Hubble parameter is constant
H\!=\!H_{0}
. The tensor structure functions
retain their form, with the bi-scalars defined in (

taking their de Sitter limits, and the index defined in (

(6.1)

Conformal gauge.

Taking the limit
\epsilon\!\to\!0

of the scalar structure functions (
5.28
)–(
5.29
) for the conformal gauge
gives,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}\xrightarrow{\epsilon\to 0}{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2H_{0}^{2}}\biggl[-1+\frac{(1\!-\!\alpha)}{2}\biggr]\mathcal{F}_{\nu_{0}}(y)\,,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}\xrightarrow{\epsilon\to 0}{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2H_{0}^{2}}\frac{(1\!-\!\alpha)}{2}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}_{\nu_{0}}(y)}{\partial y}\,,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}\xrightarrow{\epsilon\to 0}{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2H_{0}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\nu_{0}}(y)\,,

where the last one is recognized to be a constant using the
power-series representation (

For the special case

It is curious that the latter two structure functions do not depend on the

Deceleration gauge.

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{3}\xrightarrow{\epsilon\to 0}{}

\displaystyle 0\,,

(6.9)

for
\beta\!\neq\!0

6.2
Flat space limit

In the limit of flat space
H_{0}\!\to\!0
, both gauge-fixing terms reduce to the Lorentz
invariant one, and consequently both propagators must reduce to the same Lorentz
invariant propagator.
The tensor structures from (
5.24
) in this limit reduce to:

\displaystyle\bigl(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}^{\prime}y\bigr)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle\!-2(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}\,,

(6.11)

\displaystyle\bigl(\partial_{\mu}y\bigr)\bigl(\partial_{\nu}^{\prime}y\bigr)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle\!-4(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{4}H_{0}^{4}\Delta x_{\mu}\Delta x_{\nu}\,,

(6.12)

\displaystyle\Bigl[\bigl(\partial_{\mu}y\bigr)\bigl(\partial_{\nu}^{\prime}u\bigr)\!+\!\bigl(\partial_{\mu}u\bigr)\bigl(\partial_{\nu}^{\prime}y\bigr)\Bigr]\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle 2(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{3}H_{0}^{3}\bigl(\Delta x_{\mu}\delta_{\nu}^{0}-\delta_{\mu}^{0}\Delta x_{\nu}\bigr)\,,

(6.13)

\displaystyle\bigl(\partial_{\mu}u\bigr)\bigl(\partial_{\nu}^{\prime}u\bigr)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}\delta_{\mu}^{0}\delta_{\nu}^{0}\,.

(6.14)

The reduced propagator functions reduce to flat space scalar propagators,

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y)\xrightarrow{H_{0}\to 0}\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D-2}{2}\bigr)}{4\pi^{\frac{D}{2}}\bigl(\Delta x^{2}\bigr)^{\!\frac{D-2}{2}}}\equiv i\Delta(x\!-\!x^{\prime})\,,

(6.15)

derivatives with respect to distance functions become,

\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ \frac{1}{(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial(\Delta x^{2})}\,,

(6.16)

and parametric derivatives give,

\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ \frac{\lambda(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}\Delta x^{2}}{D\!-\!4}i\Delta(x\!-\!x^{\prime})\,,\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}-\frac{\lambda}{2}i\Delta(x\!-\!x^{\prime})\,.

(6.17)

For the conformal gauge the flat space limits of the structure functions are:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}(y,u)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}\biggl[-1+\frac{(1\!-\!\alpha)}{2}\biggr]i\Delta(x\!-\!x^{\prime})\,,

(6.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}(y,u)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{4}H_{0}^{4}}\biggl[-\frac{(1\!-\!\alpha)(D\!-\!2)}{4\Delta x^{2}}\biggr]i\Delta(x\!-\!x^{\prime})\,,

(6.19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}(y,u)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}\biggl[1-\frac{(1\!-\!\alpha)}{2}\frac{(D\!-\!4)\epsilon}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)}\biggr]i\Delta(x\!-\!x^{\prime})\,,

(6.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{4}(y,u)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}\times 0\,,

(6.21)

while for the deceleration gauge they reduce to:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}(y,u)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}\biggl[-1+\frac{(1\!-\!\beta)}{2}\biggr]i\Delta(x\!-\!x^{\prime})\,,

(6.22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}(y,u)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{4}H_{0}^{4}}\biggl[-\frac{(1\!-\!\beta)(D\!-\!2)}{4\Delta x^{2}}\biggr]i\Delta(x\!-\!x^{\prime})\,,

(6.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{3}(y,u)\ \overset{H_{0}\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ {}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\nu(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}\times 0\,.

It follows that both gauges produce the same flat space limit,

i\bigl[\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-5.44861pt{\tt a\!}\kern 4.08334pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-6.30338pt{\mu}\kern 4.08334pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-4.28194pt{\tt a\!}\kern 2.91667pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-5.1367pt{\mu}\kern 2.91667pt{\nu}}}}\bigr](x;x^{\prime})=\biggl[\eta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{(1\!-\!\xi)}{2}\biggl(\eta_{\mu\nu}-(D\!-\!2)\frac{\Delta x_{\mu}\Delta x_{\nu}}{\Delta x^{2}}\biggr)\biggr]i\Delta(x\!-\!x^{\prime})\,,

(6.26)

(6.28)

Conformal gauge.

The limits in (

5.28

5.29

\mathcal{A}_{1}\xrightarrow{D\to 4}-\frac{(1\!+\!\alpha)}{16\pi^{2}y}\,,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{2}\xrightarrow{D\to 4}-\frac{(1\!-\!\alpha)}{16\pi^{2}y^{2}}\,,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{3}\xrightarrow{D\to 4}\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}y}\,,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{4}\xrightarrow{D\to 4}-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\,.

Plugging these into the covariantized representation (

and acting the derivatives in the basis tensors explicitly

i\bigl[\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-5.44861pt{\tt a\!}\kern 4.08334pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-6.30338pt{\mu}\kern 4.08334pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-4.28194pt{\tt a\!}\kern 2.91667pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-5.1367pt{\mu}\kern 2.91667pt{\nu}}}}\bigr](x;x^{\prime})\xrightarrow{D\to 4}\biggl[\eta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{(1\!-\!\alpha)}{2}\biggl(\eta_{\mu\nu}-2\frac{\Delta x_{\mu}\Delta x_{\nu}}{\Delta x^{2}}\biggr)\biggr]\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\Delta x^{2}}\,,

The reason behind this is that the gauge-fixing functional becomes exactly

Deceleration gauge.

Apart from the the four-dimensional limit of the rescaled propagator functions

\frac{(D\!-\!4)\epsilon}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)}\mathcal{F}_{\nu+1}\xrightarrow{D\to 4}-\frac{(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\,,\qquad\Bigl[\frac{(D\!-\!4)\epsilon}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)}\Bigr]^{\!2}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}_{\nu+1}}{\partial\nu}\xrightarrow{D\to 4}\frac{(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\,,

Using these, and the fact that the simple deceleration gauge reduces to
\beta_{s}\!\xrightarrow{D\to 4}\!3

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}\xrightarrow{D\to 4}\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\biggl\{-\frac{1}{y}-\Bigl(1\!-\!\frac{\beta}{3}\Bigr)\biggl[\frac{6\!-\!y}{2(4\!-\!y)^{2}}\ln\Bigl(\frac{y}{4}\Bigr)-\frac{3\!-\!y}{4y\!-\!y^{2}}\biggr]\biggr\}\,,

(6.33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}\xrightarrow{D\to 4}\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\biggl\{\frac{1}{2y^{2}}-\Bigl(1\!-\!\frac{\beta}{3}\Bigr)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\biggl[\frac{6\!-\!y}{2(4\!-\!y)^{2}}\ln\Bigl(\frac{y}{4}\Bigr)-\frac{3\!-\!y}{4y\!-\!y^{2}}\biggr]\biggr\}\,,

(6.34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{3}\xrightarrow{D\to 4}0\,,

(6.35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{4}\xrightarrow{D\to 4}\frac{\beta}{24\pi^{2}}\,.

(6.36)

The alternate form (

i\bigl[\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-7.26392pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-7.26392pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-4.77779pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 4.08334pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-6.30338pt{\mu}\kern 4.08334pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-3.61111pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 2.91667pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-5.1367pt{\mu}\kern 2.91667pt{\nu}}}}\bigr](x;x^{\prime})\xrightarrow{D\to 4}i\bigl[\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-7.26392pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-7.26392pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-9.63307pt{\mu}\kern 6.56947pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-4.77779pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 4.08334pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-6.30338pt{\mu}\kern 4.08334pt{\nu}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\mu}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-3.61111pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 2.91667pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-5.1367pt{\mu}\kern 2.91667pt{\nu}}}}\bigr](x;x^{\prime})\Bigr|_{\beta=3}+\frac{(3\!-\!\beta)}{24\pi^{2}}\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\prime}_{\nu}\biggl[\frac{2(3\!-\!y)}{4\!-\!y}\ln\Bigl(\frac{y}{4}\Bigr)-{\rm Li}_{2}\Bigl(1\!-\!\frac{y}{4}\Bigr)-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\biggr]\,,

7
Simple observables

Computing simple observables provides provides another way of checking the
two-point functions worked out in Sec.
5
,
besides examining special limits worked out in the preceding section.
The first of the two simple observables considered here is the tree-level correlator
of the vector field strength, expressible in terms of derivatives acting on the two-point
function,

\bigl\langle\Omega\bigr|\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}(x)\,\hat{F}_{\rho\sigma}(x^{\prime})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=4\bigl(\delta^{\alpha}_{[\mu}\partial_{\nu]}\bigr)\bigl(\delta^{\beta}_{[\rho}\partial^{\prime}_{\sigma]}\bigr)i\bigl[\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\alpha}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-7.26392pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-9.81512pt{\alpha}\kern 6.56947pt{\beta}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\alpha}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-7.26392pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-9.81512pt{\alpha}\kern 6.56947pt{\beta}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\alpha}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-4.77779pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 4.08334pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-6.43341pt{\alpha}\kern 4.08334pt{\beta}}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}}}_{{{\alpha}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-3.61111pt{\scriptscriptstyle-\!}\kern 2.91667pt{\scriptscriptstyle\!+}}}_{{\kern-5.26674pt{\alpha}\kern 2.91667pt{\beta}}}}\bigr](x;x^{\prime})\,.

(7.1)

It is clear that the dependence on the arbitrary gauge-fixing parameters drops out
from this correlator when two-point functions written in
forms (
5.42
) and (
5.44
) are used.
Thus, only parts corresponding to the simple conformal gauge and the simple
deceleration gauge contribute.
The correlator (
7.1
) can be expanded in an appropriate tensor basis,

\displaystyle\bigl\langle\Omega\bigr|\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}(x)\hat{F}_{\rho\sigma}(x^{\prime})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=\bigl(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\prime}_{[\rho}y\bigr)\bigl(\partial^{\prime}_{\sigma]}\partial_{\nu}y\bigr)\,\mathcal{G}_{1}(y,u)+\bigl(\partial_{[\mu}y\bigr)\bigl(\partial_{\nu]}\partial^{\prime}_{[\sigma}y\bigr)\bigl(\partial^{\prime}_{\rho]}y\bigr)\,\mathcal{G}_{2}(y,u)

\displaystyle+\Bigl[\bigl(\partial_{[\mu}y\bigr)\bigl(\partial_{\nu]}\partial^{\prime}_{[\sigma}y\bigr)\bigl(\partial^{\prime}_{\rho]}u\bigr)+\bigl(\partial_{[\mu}u\bigr)\bigl(\partial_{\nu]}\partial^{\prime}_{[\sigma}y\bigr)\bigl(\partial^{\prime}_{\rho]}y\bigr)\Bigr]\,\mathcal{G}_{3}(y,u)

\displaystyle+\bigl(\partial_{[\mu}u\bigr)\bigl(\partial_{\nu]}\partial^{\prime}_{[\sigma}y\bigr)\bigl(\partial_{\rho]}^{\prime}u\bigr)\,\mathcal{G}_{4}(y,u)+\bigl(\partial_{[\mu}y\bigr)\bigl(\partial_{\nu]}u\bigr)\bigl(\partial^{\prime}_{[\rho}y\bigr)\bigl(\partial^{\prime}_{\sigma]}u\bigr)\,\mathcal{G}_{5}(y,u)\,,

(7.2)

where the structure functions of this expansion
are related to the structure functions of the two-point functions in (
5.24
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}=4\Bigl(\frac{\partial\mathcal{C}_{1}}{\partial y}-\mathcal{C}_{2}\Bigr)\,,\qquad\quad\mathcal{G}_{2}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{1}}{\partial y}\,,\qquad\quad\mathcal{G}_{3}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{1}}{\partial u}\,,

(7.3a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{4}=4\Bigl(\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{C}_{1}}{\partial u^{2}}-2\frac{\partial\mathcal{C}_{3}}{\partial u}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{C}_{4}}{\partial y}\Bigr)\,,\qquad\quad\mathcal{G}_{5}=-\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{G}_{1}}{\partial u^{2}}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{4}}{\partial y}\,.

(7.3b)

Plugging in the scalar structure functions
in the conformal gauge (
5.28
)–(
5.31
), or in the
deceleration gauge (
5.32
)–(
5.35
) produces the same answer
for the two independent structure functions,

\mathcal{G}_{1}=\frac{2\,e^{-\frac{(D-4)\epsilon}{2(1-\epsilon)}u}}{(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}\biggl[-\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\mathcal{F}_{\nu}\biggr]\,,\qquad\mathcal{G}_{4}=\frac{2\,e^{-\frac{(D-4)\epsilon}{2(1-\epsilon)}u}}{(1\!-\!\epsilon)^{2}H_{0}^{2}}\biggl[\frac{(D\!-\!4)\epsilon}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)}\biggl(1-\frac{(D\!-\!4)\epsilon}{2(1\!-\!\epsilon)}\biggr)\mathcal{F}_{\nu}\biggr]\,,

(7.4)

and consequently for the remaining three.
These are precisely the structure functions found in
[
51
]
,
computed using the two-point function in the simple covariant gauge.
Therefore, it follows immediately that the
D\!\to\!4
limit of the vector field correlator
reproduces the flat space result,

\bigl\langle\Omega\bigr|\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}(x)\,\hat{F}_{\rho\sigma}(x^{\prime})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle\xrightarrow{D\to 4}\frac{2}{\pi^{2}(\Delta x^{2})^{2}}\biggl[\eta_{\mu[\rho}\eta_{\sigma]\nu}-4\eta_{\alpha[\mu}\eta_{\nu][\sigma}\eta_{\rho]\beta}\frac{\Delta x^{\alpha}\Delta x^{\beta}}{\Delta x^{2}}\biggr]\,,

(7.5)

as it should since the gauge-invariant photon is conformally coupled to gravity in
four spacetime dimensions.

The other simple observable is the one-loop energy-momentum tensor,

\bigl\langle\Omega\bigr|\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}(x)\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=\Bigl(\delta_{(\mu}^{\rho}\delta_{\nu)}^{\sigma}\!-\!\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr)g^{\alpha\beta}\bigl\langle\Omega\bigr|\hat{F}_{\rho\alpha}(x)\hat{F}_{\sigma\beta}(x)\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle\,.

(7.6)

The energy-momentum tensor above is defined as a variation of the gauge-invariant
Maxwell action (
2.7

gauge-fixed action (
2.19

at the expectation value level, as the difference is guaranteed to vanish
identically

45

(see also
[
73
,
74
]
for the case of the Stueckelberg
field). Since the structure functions (

in
[
51
]
, the energy-momentum tensor also matches,

\bigl\langle\Omega\bigr|\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}(x)\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,

(7.7)

consistent with the computation in the simple covariant gauge
[
51
]
.
It should be noted that the conformal anomaly (e.g.
[
75
]
)
does not appear when computing just this diagram. The entire one-loop effective action
should be renormalized for the finite conformal anomaly to appear in the
energy-momentum tensor.

8
Discussion

Proton propagators have been constructed for power-law inflation in two different
one-parameter families of noncovariant gauges (
1.1
), using canonical
quantization methods
[
45
]
. This is a considerable
improvement compared to much more complicated propagator in the simple covariant gauge
worked out previously in
[
51
]
. The two new propagators worked out
here are far more tractable, as they are expressed in terms of scalar propagators and their
derivatives, multiplying simple tensor structures. This makes practically feasible to explore
the dynamical symmetry breaking effects found for scalar electrodynamics in de Sitter
space
[
22
]
, in more realistic inflationary spacetimes
with the non-vanishing principal slow-roll parameter. It also allows to explore the vicinity
of the symmetric phase of the Abelian Higgs model
[
77
]
, for which the
unitary gauge propagator
[
76
]
is not appropriate. The presence of
arbitrary gauge-fixing parameters provides another useful feature of being able to test for
gauge dependence of computed observables.

The biggest technical simplification compared to the general covariant
gauge
[
51
]

comes from dispensing with the need to explicitly evaluate the inverse Laplace operator
acting on a scalar two-point function. This requirement was used to identify
discreet choices for the second gauge-fixing parameter
\zeta

that lead to simple solutions for mode functions. Two of these choices, dubbed the
conformal gauge (
\zeta\!=\!1
), and the deceleration gauge (
\zeta\!=\!\epsilon
),
in addition lead to tractable position space photon
propagators, that are expressed in terms of a finite number of derivatives acting on scalar
propagators. These propagators are expressed in the covariantized
form (
5.24
), with the structure functions given
in (
5.28
)–(
5.31
) for the conformal gauge,
and in (
5.32
)–(
5.35
) for the deceleration gauge.
Both propagators satisfy both the respective equations of motion, and
the respective Ward-Takahashi identity.
The conformal gauge propagator also represents a new propagator for de Sitter space,
that is a one-parameter generalization of the simple photon gauge propagator
[
52
]
,
while in the deceleration gauge the propagator correctly reduces to the general
covariant gauge propagator in the de Sitter limit
[
46
,
47
]
, including the de Sitter breaking term.

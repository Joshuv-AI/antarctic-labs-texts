The Sharp Measure Upper Bound of the Nodal Sets of Neumann Laplace Eigenfunctions on C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} Domains
Xiujin Chen, Xiaoping Yang

Abstract
1 1 1 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35B05. Let Ω \Omega be a bounded domain in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} with C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} boundary and let u λ u_{\lambda} be a Neumann Laplace eigenfunction in Ω \Omega with eigenvalue λ \lambda . We show that the ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -dimensional Hausdorff measure of the zero set of u λ u_{\lambda} does not exceed C ​ λ C\sqrt{\lambda} .



1
Introduction

In this paper, our goal is to obtain the sharp upper bound for the area of the nodal sets of Neumann Laplace eigenfunctions in
C^{1,1}
domains. The nodal set of
u
is denoted by
Z(u)=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)=0\}
.

Throughout this paper, we always use the notation
\nu
to indicate the unit outer normal vector field on the boundary in the context. With this convention, our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain and let
u_{\lambda}
satisfy
\Delta u_{\lambda}+\lambda u_{\lambda}=0
in
\Omega
and
\frac{\partial u_{\lambda}}{\partial\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. Then

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(u_{\lambda}))\leq C\sqrt{\lambda},

where
C
depends only on
\Omega
.

The argument we adopt mainly follows the spirit of Logunov, Malinnikova, Nadirashvili and Nazarov’s work
[
6
]
, where they studied the nodal sets of Dirichlet eigenfunctions. But for the Neumann problem, the obstacles are very different especially near the boundary. We also improve the argument in
[
6
]
when we consider a boundary neighborhood with the large doubling index. Beside that, the main novelty of this paper is in Section
4
, where we construct an approximation for the Neumann problem by delicately applying the maximum principle.

We briefly introduce the history of the study on the nodal sets of Laplace eigenfunctions. Let
\Delta_{M}
be the Laplace operator on a

n
-dimensional smooth compact manifold
M
and
u_{\lambda}
be a corresponding eigenfunction,
\Delta_{M}u_{\lambda}+\lambda u_{\lambda}=0
on
M
. S. T. Yau
[
9
]
conjectured that the surface area of the nodal set of
u_{\lambda}
satisfies

c(M)\sqrt{\lambda}\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(u_{\lambda}))\leq C(M)\sqrt{\lambda}.

(1.1)

Donelly and Fefferman
[
5
]
proved the conjecture for manifolds with analytic metrics. For smooth manifolds, the lower bound and a polynomial upper bound were obtained by Logunov in
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
respectively. For other related works, see the references in
[
13
,
12
]
.

For boundary value problems, Donelly and Fefferman
[
10
]
also proved the estimate (
1.1
) for manifolds with analytic boundaries assuming that
u_{\lambda}
satisfies either Dirichlet or Neumann boundary condition. Kukavica
[
11
]
generalized their results to eigenfunctions of elliptic operators with real analytic coefficients. Liu, Tian and Yang
[
16
]
obtained a measure upper bound for Robin eigenfunctions when the boundary is piecewise analytic. Lin and Zhu
[
14
]
proved the similar estimates to (
1.1
) for bi-Laplacian eigenfunctions with the assumption that the boundary is real analytic. Tian and Yang
[
17
]
obtained the sharp upper bound for bi-Laplacian eigenfunctions when the boundaries are piecewise analytic.

For domains that are not analytic, there are less results. The polynomial upper bounds for the area of the zero set of Robin Laplace eigenfunctions in smooth domains was obtained by Zhu
[
15
]
. The sharp upper bound for the area of the zero set of Dirichlet Laplace eigenfunctions in
C^{1}
domains or Lipschitz domains with small Lipschitz constants was obtained by Logunov, Malinnikova, Nadirashvili and Narazov
[
6
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the definition of Lipschitz domains with small Lipschitz constants and list several useful lemmas. In Section
3
, we introduce facts about the doubling index, especially the almost monotonicity. In Section
4
, we construct the standard approximation to the Neumann problem near the boundary. In Section
5
, we use the approximation in Section
4
to study the nodal sets near the boundary. In Section
6
, we complete the proof of Theorem
3.3
, which is a measure upper bound of nodal sets for harmonic functions. In Section
7
, we introduce the standard extension method for Laplace eigenfunctions and use Theorem
3.3
to complete the proof of the main theorem.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Lipschitz domains with small Lipschitz constants

Throughout this paper, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use the notation
x=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
where
x^{\prime}
is the first
n-1
coordinates of
x
and
x^{\prime\prime}
is the last coordinate of
x
.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\tau\in(0,1)
, and let
B=B(x,r)
for some
x\in\partial\Omega
. We say that
\partial\Omega\cap B
is
\tau
-Lipschitz if there is an isometry
T
:
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a Lipschitz function
f
:
B^{n-1}(0,r)\to\mathbb{R}
with the Lipschitz constant bounded by
\tau
such that
T(0)=x
,
f(0)=0
and

\Omega\cap B=T\left(\{(y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime})\in B^{n}(0,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}:y^{\prime\prime}>f(y^{\prime})\}\right).

In this case we write
\partial\Omega\cap B\in Lip(\tau)
.

Remark 2.1
.

In this paper, most of the arguments are local. When considering the part of the boundary
\partial\Omega\cap B(x,r)
, we choose the local coordinate system in
B(x,r)
such that
x=0
and
e_{n}=-\nu(x)
, where
e_{n}
is the unit vector in the direction of the last coordinate.

Definition 2
.

We say that
\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain with local Lipschitz constant
\tau
if there exists
r>0
such that
\partial\Omega\cap B(x,r)\in Lip(\tau)
for any
x\in\partial\Omega
.

Remark 2.2
.

Clearly, any bounded
C^{1}
domain is a Lipschitz domain with local Lipschitz constant for any positive
\tau
. Of course so is any bounded
C^{1,1}
domain.

2.2
Auxiliary lemmas

We list several useful lemmas that will be repeatedly used in the following sections. The first one is the local boundedness of solutions to the Neumann problem.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
h\in C(\overline{\Omega})
,
\Delta u=0
in
\Omega
, and
\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. Then there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
0<r<r_{0}
,

\sup_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}h^{2}\leq\frac{C}{|B(x,2r)|}\int_{B(x,2r)\cap\Omega}h^{2},

where
C
depends only on the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

The standard Moser method. See, e.g., Lemma 3.1 in
[
4
]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain,
h\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\Delta u=0
in
\Omega
, and
\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. Then there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
0<r<r_{0}
,

\sup_{B(x,r)\cap\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla h|\leq\frac{C}{r}\sup_{B(x,2r)\cap\Omega}|h|,

where
C
depends only on the
C^{1,1}
character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

By a standard
C^{1,1}
coordinate transformation
\Psi
(See e.g.
[
8
]
), the Neumann problem in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
can be transformed to the Neumann problem in a unit half ball
B_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|<1,x^{\prime\prime}>0\}
. Let
v(x)=u\circ\Psi(x)
, we have
-\mathrm{div}(A\nabla v)=0
in
B_{+}
and
\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0
on the flat boundary, where
A=\mathrm{det}J\Psi J\Psi^{-T}J\Psi^{-1}
and
J\Psi
denotes the Jacobi matrix of
\Psi
. The fact that
\Psi
is a
C^{1,1}
transformation implies
A
is Lipschitz. By extending
v
to the whole ball symmetrically and the interior gradient estimates, we have for sufficiently small
r
,

\sup_{\Psi^{-1}(B(x,r)\cap\overline{\Omega})}|\nabla v|\leq\frac{C}{r}\sup_{\Psi^{-1}(B(x,2r)\cap\Omega)}|v|,

where
C
depends on the transformation
\Psi
and thus on the
C^{1,1}
character of
\partial\Omega
. Back to the original domain, since
\Psi
is non-degenerate, we obtain the required estimate.
∎

The following smallness propagation lemma is from
[
1
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
B_{+}
be the half-ball,
B_{+}=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}:|x^{\prime}|^{2}+|x^{\prime\prime}|^{2}<1,x^{\prime\prime}>0\}
, and
\Gamma
be the flat part of
\partial B_{+}
,
\Gamma=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\overline{B_{+}}:x^{\prime\prime}=0\}
. There exist
\gamma=\gamma(n)\in(0,1)
and
C=C(n)>0
such that if
h
is harmonic in
B_{+}
,
h\in C^{1}(\overline{B_{+}})
and satisfies the inequalities

|h|\leq 1,\ |\nabla h|\leq 1\ \text{in }B_{+},\quad|h|\leq\varepsilon,\ |\partial_{n}h|\leq\varepsilon\ \text{on }\Gamma,

then

|h(x)|\leq C\varepsilon^{\gamma}\ \text{in }\frac{1}{3}B_{+},

where
\frac{1}{3}B_{+}=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}):|x^{\prime}|^{2}+|x^{\prime\prime}|^{2}=\frac{1}{9},x^{\prime\prime}>0\}
.

3
Doubling Index

Let
h\in C(\overline{\Omega})
be a non-zero harmonic function in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
r>0
, let

H_{h}(x,r)=\int_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}h^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{h}(x,r)=\log\frac{H_{h}(x,2r)}{H_{h}(x,r)}.

The quantity
N_{h}(x,r)
is called the doubling index of
h
in
B(x,r)
. For
B(x,2R)\subset\Omega
, it is a well-known fact that for any
0<r<R
,

N_{h}(x,r)\leq N_{h}(x,R).

(3.1)

For an elementary proof, see, e.g.,
[
7
]
.

For a cube
Q
with diameter
l
such that
Q\cap\Omega\neq\varnothing
, define the maximal doubling index of
h
in
Q
by

N^{*}_{h}(Q)=\sup_{x\in Q\cap\overline{\Omega},0<r\leq l}N_{h}(x,r).

By this definition, we have for any smaller cube
q\subset Q
, there holds
N^{*}_{h}(q)\leq N^{*}_{h}(Q)
.

The following lemma is called the almost monotonicity of the doubling index. Comparing to (
3.1
), in the following statement, the doubling index near the boundary is considered.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{1,1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
B=B(x_{0},R)
for some
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,
\Delta h=0
in
\Omega\cap B
and
\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B
, then

N_{h}(x,r_{1})\leq C(N_{h}(x,r_{2})+1),

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}\cap\frac{1}{4}B
and
0<2r_{1}\leq r_{2}<R/16
, where
C
depends on the
C^{1,1}
character of
\partial\Omega\cap B
.

For the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we refer the readers to
[
2
]
, where the more general Robin problem is considered. Also notice that we consider the Laplace equation with Neumann boundary value in this lemma, so better monotonicity of the doubling index can be obtained and Lemma
3.1
follows.

By this kind of almost monotonicity, a simple corollary is the three-ball estimate.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{1,1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
B=B(x_{0},R)
for some
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,
\Delta h=0
in
\Omega\cap B
and
\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B
, then

\sup_{B(x,\frac{3}{2}r)\cap\Omega}|h|\leq C\left(\sup_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}|h|\right)^{1-\delta}\left(\sup_{B(x,4r)\cap\Omega}|h|\right)^{\delta},

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}\cap\frac{1}{4}B
and
0<2r_{1}\leq r_{2}<R/16
, where
C
and
\delta
depend on the
C^{1,1}
character of
\partial\Omega\cap B
.

Proof.

In Lemma
3.1
, take
r_{1}=r
and
r_{2}=2r
and take exponents from both sides, we obtain

H_{h}(2r)\leq CH_{h}(r)^{1-\delta}H_{h}(4r)^{\delta}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we obtain the similar estimate in terms of
\sup|h|
as required.
∎

It is a standard way to extend the Laplace eigenfunction to a harmonic function. See Section
7
for details. In the following sections, we focus on the nodal sets of harmonic functions (with Neumann boundary value). For harmonic functions with Neumann boundary value, we claim the following estimate on the nodal set.

Theorem 3.3
.

There exists absolute constants
\tau_{n}>0
and
C>0
such that the following holds. Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain,
\partial\Omega\cap B(x,128r)\in Lip(\tau)
for some
x\in\partial\Omega
and
\tau<\tau_{n}
. Then

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(h)\cap B(x,r))\leq C(N_{h}(x,4r)+1)r^{n-1},

for any non-zero harmonic function
h\in C(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying
\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B(x,128r)
.

The fact that Theorem
3.3
implies Theorem
1.1
will be shown in Section
7
. Before that, we are devoted to proving Theorem
3.3
. We also need the estimate for the zero set inside the domain, which was proved by Donnelly and Fefferman in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
h
be a non-zero harmonic function in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. There exists
C
such that

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(h)\cap B)\leq C(N_{h}(x,4r)+1)r^{n-1},

for any ball
B=B(x,r)
satisfying
\overline{B(x,8r)}\subset\Omega
.

4
Approximation for the Neumann Problem

In this section, we introduce how to approximate the Neumann problem near the
C^{1,1}
boundary by a mixed boundary value problem in a regular domain, in fact, a cylinder.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use the notation
x=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
where
x^{\prime}
is the first
n-1
coordinates of
x
and
x^{\prime\prime}
is the last coordinate of
x
. We also let
S(x,r,h):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}|<r,0<y^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}<h\}
denote the cylinder with radius
r
and height
h
whose lower surface is centred at
x
.

Definition 3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain,
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
\partial\Omega\cap B(x_{0},10)\in Lip(\tau)
. Choose the coordinate as in Remark
2.1
so
x_{0}=0
. Let
x_{1}=x_{0}-3\tau e_{n}
and
S=S(x_{1},1,1)
. For
\tau<1/100
, we have
\Omega\cap S\neq\varnothing
, the upper surface of
S
is contained in
\Omega
and the lower surface of
S
is contained in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. We call the triple
(x_{1},S,\Omega\cap S)

the standard approximation
near
x_{0}
. We remark that this approximation depends on
\tau
.

We have introduced how to find a cylinder to approximate to a
\tau
-Lipschitz domain in a neighborhood of the boundary. Next we find a solution to the Neumann problem in the cylinder to approximate to the solution to the Neumann problem in the neighborhood.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain,
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
\partial\Omega\cap B(x_{0},10)\in Lip(\tau)
with
\tau<1/100
. Also let
h\in C(\overline{\Omega})
be normalized as
\sup_{\Omega\cap B(x_{0},3)}|h|=1
and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta h=0\ \text{in}\ \Omega\\
&\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\cap B(x_{0},10)\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Consider the standard approximation
(x_{1},S,\Omega\cap S)
near
x_{0}
. Then there exists
g\in C(\overline{S})
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta g=0\ \text{in}\ S\\
&\frac{\partial g}{\partial e_{n}}=0\ \text{on}\ \Gamma_{-}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Gamma_{-}:=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\overline{S(x_{1},1,1)}:x^{\prime\prime}=x_{1}^{\prime\prime}\}
is the lower surface of
S
, and

\sup_{\Omega\cap S}|g-h|<C\tau,\quad\sup_{S}|g|=\sup_{\Omega\cap S}|h|.

To prove this theorem, we need the following quantitative uniqueness of a mixed valued problem in a domain close to a cylinder.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
S=S(0,1,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a cylinder,
\Sigma=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}))\in\partial S:0<x^{\prime\prime}<1\}
be its side surface,
\Gamma_{+}=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}))\in\partial S:x^{\prime\prime}=1\}
be its upper surface,
f:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function such that
0<f(x^{\prime})<1/3
for any
x^{\prime}\in B^{n-1}(0,1)
,
\Omega=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x^{\prime})>x^{\prime\prime}\}
and
w\in C(\overline{S\cap\Omega})
be a solution to the following problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta w=0\ \text{in}\ S\cap\Omega\\
&w=0\ \text{on}\ \Sigma\cap\Omega\\
&\left|\frac{\partial w}{\partial e_{n}}\right|<\delta\ \text{on}\ \Gamma_{+}\cup(\partial\Omega\cap S)\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Then

\sup_{\Omega\cap S}|w|<C(n)\delta.

Proof.

By normalization, it is sufficient to show that for any solution to the following problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta w=0\ \text{in}\ S\cap\Omega\\
&w=0\ \text{on}\ \Sigma\cap\Omega\\
&\sup_{\Omega\cap S}|w|=1\\
\end{aligned}\right.

there holds

\sup_{\Gamma_{+}\cup(\partial\Omega\cap S)}\left|\frac{\partial w}{\partial e_{n}}\right|\geq c(n).

(4.1)

By maximum principle,
\sup_{\Omega\cap S}|w|
can only be attained at some
\overline{x}\in\Gamma_{+}\cup(\partial\Omega\cap S)
. Without loss of generality, we assume
w(\overline{x})>0
and thus
\sup_{\Gamma_{+}\cup(\partial\Omega\cap S)}w=\sup_{\Omega\cap S}|w|=1
.

Let
\varphi(x)
be the harmonic function

\varphi(x)=(n-1)|x^{\prime\prime}-2/3|^{2}-|x^{\prime}|^{2}+1,

then we have
\varphi\geq 0
in
\overline{S}
. Also let
\varphi_{a}(x)=a\cdot\varphi(x)
for
a>0
. Let
a_{0}=\inf\{a>0:\varphi_{a}(x)\geq w(x)\text{ for }x\in\Gamma_{+}\cup(\partial\Omega\cap S)\}
. Since
\inf_{\Gamma_{+}\cup(\partial\Omega\cap S)}\varphi>0
and
\sup_{\Gamma_{+}\cup(\partial\Omega\cap S)}w=1
, the infimum exists. For the maximum point
\overline{x}
, we also have
\varphi_{a_{0}}(\overline{x})\geq w(\overline{x})
, which implies

a_{0}\geq\frac{1}{(n-1)|\overline{x}^{\prime\prime}-2/3|^{2}-|\overline{x}^{\prime}|^{2}+1}.

Since
\overline{x}\in\Gamma_{+}\cup(\partial\Omega\cap S)
, we have
|\overline{x}^{\prime\prime}-2/3|\leq 1
. Therefore
a_{0}\geq c(n)>0
. We have
\varphi_{a_{0}}(x)\geq w(x)
on
\Gamma_{+}\cup(\partial\Omega\cap S)
and
\varphi_{a_{0}}(x)\geq 0=w(x)
on
\Sigma\cap\Omega
, by the maximum principle,
\varphi_{a_{0}}(x)\geq w(x)
in
S\cap\Omega
. By the definition of
a_{0}
, there exists
y\in\Gamma_{+}\cup(\partial\Omega\cap S)
such that
w(y)=\varphi_{a_{0}}(y)
. If
y\in\Gamma_{+}
, then

\frac{\partial w}{\partial e_{n}}(y)\geq\frac{\partial\varphi_{a_{0}}}{\partial e_{n}}(y)=a_{0}\cdot\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial e_{n}}(y)=a_{0}\cdot 2(n-1)(y^{\prime\prime}-2/3)\geq c(n)>0,

since
y^{\prime\prime}-2/3=1/3
for
y\in\Gamma_{+}
. If
y\in\partial\Omega\cap S
, then

\frac{\partial w}{\partial e_{n}}(y)\leq\frac{\partial\varphi_{a_{0}}}{\partial e_{n}}(y)=a_{0}\cdot\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial e_{n}}(y)=a_{0}\cdot 2(n-1)(y^{\prime\prime}-2/3)\leq-c(n)<0,

since
y^{\prime\prime}-2/3\leq-1/3
for
y\in\partial\Omega\cap S
. In both cases, we have

\left|\frac{\partial w}{\partial e_{n}}(y)\right|\geq c(n)>0.

Then (
4.1
) follows.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

For simplicity, we can choose the coordinate such that
x_{1}
is the origin. Firstly, we extend
h
to
\Sigma:=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\partial S:0<x^{\prime\prime}<1\}
continuously in the following way. For any
x\in\Sigma\cap\Omega
, define
\tilde{h}(x):=h(x)
. For any
(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\Sigma\setminus\Omega
, let
y_{x}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
be the number such that
(x^{\prime},y_{x}^{\prime\prime})\in\Sigma\cap\partial\Omega
and define
\tilde{h}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}):=h(x^{\prime},y_{x}^{\prime\prime})
. Now we have
\tilde{h}\in C(\Sigma)
. Let
\Gamma_{+}:=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\partial S:x^{\prime\prime}=1\}
be the upper surface of
S
. Since
\Gamma_{+}\subset\Omega
, we have
h\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma_{+})
. Let
g
be the solution to the following problem (See
[
3
]
for the existence of the solution):

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta g=0\ \text{in}\ S\\
&g=\tilde{h}\ \text{on}\ \Sigma\\
&\frac{\partial g}{\partial e_{n}}=\frac{\partial h}{\partial e_{n}}\ \text{on}\ \Gamma_{+}\\
&\frac{\partial g}{\partial e_{n}}=0\ \text{on}\ \Gamma_{-}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(4.2)

where
\Gamma_{-}:=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\partial S:x^{\prime\prime}=0\}
is the lower surface of
S
. Let
w=g-h\in C(\overline{S\cap\Omega})
, then we have
\Delta w=0
in
S\cap\Omega
,
w=0
on
\Sigma\cap\Omega
and
\frac{\partial w}{\partial e_{n}}=0
on
\Gamma_{+}
.

We want to use Lemma
4.2
to show that
\sup_{\Omega\cap S}|w|<C\tau
. Check the conditions, then it remains to show that
\left|\frac{\partial w}{\partial e_{n}}\right|<C\tau
on
\partial\Omega\cap S
.

Since we have normalized
\sup_{\Omega\cap B(x_{0},3)}|h|=1
,

\sup_{\overline{\Omega}\cap B(0,2)}|\nabla h|\leq\sup_{\overline{\Omega}\cap B(x_{0},2+3\tau)}|\nabla h|\leq\sup_{\overline{\Omega}\cap B(x_{0},5/2)}|\nabla h|\leq C_{1}

by Lemma
2.2
and
\tau<1/100
. On
\partial\Omega\cap B(0,2)
, we have

\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial e_{n}}\right|=|\nabla h\cdot(-e_{n})|\leq|\nabla h\cdot\nu|+|\nabla h\cdot(-e_{n}-\nu)|\leq C|-e_{n}-\nu|\leq C_{2}\tau,

since
\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B(0,2)
and
\partial\Omega\cap B(0,2)\in C^{1,1}(\tau)
thus the unit vectors
-e_{n}
and
\nu
are close in terms of
\tau
.

Let
\psi(x)
be the harmonic function on
B_{2}=B(0,2)
such that
\psi(x)=1
on
\partial B_{2,+}:=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\partial B_{2}:x^{\prime\prime}>0\}
and
\psi(x)=-1
on
\partial B_{2,-}:=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\partial B_{2}:x^{\prime\prime}<0\}
. Obviously,
\psi(x)=0
on
\{x^{\prime\prime}=0\}
. Define the linear function
l(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})=x^{\prime\prime}/2
. By the maximum principle, we have
\psi(x)\geq l(x)
in
B_{2,+}:=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in B_{2}:x^{\prime\prime}>0\}
. For any
(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{2}
, we have
x^{\prime\prime}\geq|x_{0}^{\prime\prime}|-|x^{\prime\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime\prime}|\geq 3\tau-2\tau=\tau
. Therefore
\psi(x)\geq l(x)\geq\tau/2
on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{2}
. Let
\bar{\psi}=2C_{3}\psi
, where
C_{3}=\max\{C_{1},C_{2}\}
. Then we have for any
x\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{2}
,

\bar{\psi}(x)\geq C_{2}\tau\geq\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial e_{n}}(x)\right|.

And for any
x\in\partial B_{2}\cap\Omega
, we have
\psi(x)=1
, then

\bar{\psi}(x)\geq C_{1}\geq\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial e_{n}}(x)\right|.

Note that the function
\frac{\partial h}{\partial e_{n}}
is also harmonic. By maximum principle, we have
\bar{\psi}(x)\geq\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial e_{n}}(x)\right|
for
x\in\Omega\cap B_{2}
. Particularly,
\bar{\psi}(x)\geq\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial e_{n}}(x)\right|
for
x\in\partial S\cap\Omega
.

Recall that
g
is defined by (
4.2
), we have
\left|\frac{\partial g}{\partial e_{n}}(x)\right|=\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial e_{n}}(x)\right|
for
x\in\partial S\cap\Omega
and
\left|\frac{\partial g}{\partial e_{n}}(x)\right|=0
for
x\in\partial S\setminus\Omega
. Then we have for
x\in\partial S
,
\bar{\psi}(x)\geq\left|\frac{\partial g}{\partial e_{n}}(x)\right|
. Note that the function
\frac{\partial g}{\partial e_{n}}(x)
is also harmonic. By the maximum principle, we have
\bar{\psi}(x)\geq\left|\frac{\partial g}{\partial e_{n}}(x)\right|
for
x\in S
. On the other hand, by the Cauchy estimates,
\sup_{S}|\nabla\bar{\psi}|\leq C\sup_{B_{2}}|\bar{\psi}|\leq C
. Note that
\bar{\psi}=0
on
\Gamma_{-}
, we have
\bar{\psi}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\leq Cx^{\prime\prime}
in
S
and thus
\left|\frac{\partial g}{\partial e_{n}}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\right|\leq Cx^{\prime\prime}
in
S
. For
(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\partial\Omega\cap S
, we have
x^{\prime\prime}\leq 4\tau
. Therefore
\left|\frac{\partial g}{\partial e_{n}}(x)\right|\leq C\tau
on
\partial\Omega\cap S
, which is the desired claim.

For the second estimate in the theorem, we note that in our construction,
\frac{\partial g}{\partial e_{n}}=0
on
\Gamma_{-}
. Then by the maximum principle and Hopf’s lemma,
|g|
can only attain its maximum on
\Gamma_{+}\cup\Sigma
. Again by the construction of
g
, we have
g=h
on
\Gamma_{+}
and
g=\tilde{h}
on
\Sigma
. Therefore,

\sup_{S}|g|=\sup_{\Gamma_{+}\cup\Sigma}|g|=\max\{\sup_{\Gamma_{+}}|h|,\sup_{\Sigma}|\tilde{h}|\}=\sup_{\Gamma_{+}\cup(\Sigma\cap\Omega)}|h|=\sup_{\Omega\cap S}|h|,

where we also use the maximum principle and Hopf’s lemma for
h
in the last equality.
∎

5
Nodal Sets near the Boundary

5.1
A Standard Division of Cubes on the Boundary

The following division process of cubes is firstly introduced by Logunov in
[
6
]
. It is worth pointing out that though we argue on
C^{1,1}(\tau)
domains in this paper, the following division also works on Lipschitz domains with small Lipschitz constant.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\partial\Omega\cap B\in C^{1,1}(\tau)
where
B=B(x,r)
for some
x\in\partial\Omega
. Also assume
\tau<(16\sqrt{n})^{-1}
. Choose the local coordinate as in Remark
2.1
. Let
Q
be a cube centred at
x_{Q}\in\partial\Omega\cap B
whose sides are parallel to the axes of the coordinate system and such that
Q\subset B
. Denote the side length of
Q
by
s
.

We cover
\Omega\cap Q
by small cubes in the following way. Firstly, consider the projection
\pi(Q)
of
Q
to
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\{0\}
. Let
k\geq 3
. Divide
\pi(Q)
into
2^{k(n-1)}
equal small cubes
w
with side length
2^{-k}s
in the normal way. For each
(n-1)
-dimensional
w
, there exists a unique
n
-dimensional cube
q
such that
\pi(q)=w
and the center of
q
lies on
\partial\Omega\cap B
. Then we use at most
2^{k}
small cubes
p
with side length
2^{-k}s
to cover
(\pi^{-1}(w)\cap(\Omega\cap Q))\setminus q
such that
p\subset Q
,
\pi(p)=w
,
p
has no inner common points with
q
. Cubes
p
may overlap.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)
denote the set of cubes
q
which intersect the boundary and
\mathcal{I}_{k}(Q)
denote the set of inner cubes
p
. Since
\tau<(16\sqrt{n})^{-1}
, for inner cubes
p
,
dist(p,\partial\Omega)\geq s/4
. The triple
(Q,\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q),\mathcal{I}_{k}(Q))
is called
the standard construction
.

5.2
Cubes with Large Doubling Indexes

Lemma 5.1
.

There exist constants
k_{0}\geq 3
and
N_{0}\geq 1
such that for any integer
k\geq k_{0}
, there exists
\tau(k)>0
for which the following statement holds. Suppose that
\Omega
is a
C^{1,1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\partial\Omega\cap B\in Lip(\tau)
,
\tau<\tau(k)
, and
Q\subset\frac{1}{64}B
is a cube centred on
\partial\Omega
. Then for any
h\in C(\overline{\Omega})
harmonic in
\Omega
, with
\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B
, and
N^{*}_{h}(Q)>N_{0}
, there exists a cube
q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)
such that
N^{*}_{h}(q)\leq N^{*}_{h}(Q)/2
.

Proof.

Let
x_{Q}
and
l
denote the center of
Q
and the diameter of
Q
respectively. Let
B_{1}=B(x_{Q},l)
. Then
B_{1}\subset B
. Denote
M^{2}=\int_{B_{1}\cap\Omega}h^{2}
and
N=N^{*}_{h}(Q)
.

We prove the lemma by contradiction. Suppose
N^{*}_{h}(q)>N/2
for each
q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)
. Then for each
q
, there exists
y_{q}\in q\cap\overline{\Omega}
and
0<r_{q}\leq 2^{-k}l
such that
N_{h}(y_{q},r_{q})>N/2
. With the assumption that
N
is sufficiently large, the almost monotonicity of the doubling index (Lemma
3.1
) implies
N_{h}(y_{q},2^{j}r_{q})>cN
for
1\leq j\leq k
and
c
only depneds on the
C^{1,1}
character of
\partial\Omega
.

Assumeing that
k\geq 20
, apply the estimate of the doubling index
k-4
times and use that
B(y_{q},l/2)\subset B_{1}
to obtain

\begin{split}\int_{B(y_{q},2^{-k+2}l)\cap\Omega}h^{2}&\leq\int_{B(y_{q},8r_{q})\cap\Omega}h^{2}\leq e^{-cN(k-4)}\int_{B(y_{q},2^{k-1}r_{q})\cap\Omega}h^{2}\\
&\leq e^{-cN(k-4)}\int_{B(y_{q},l/2)\cap\Omega}h^{2}\leq e^{-c^{\prime}Nk}M^{2},\end{split}

By the local boundedness near the boundary (Lemma
2.1
), we have

\sup_{B(y_{q},2^{-k+1}l)\cap\Omega}h^{2}\leq C2^{nk}l^{-n}\int_{B(y_{q},2^{-k+2}l)\cap\Omega}h^{2}\leq C2^{nk}l^{-n}e^{-cNk}M^{2}.

(5.1)

As in the standard construction, assume that
\tau<(16\sqrt{n})^{-1}
. For each
q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)
, denote by
q^{+}
its upper quarter, where “up” is in the direction of
e_{n}
. Then
dist(q^{+},\partial\Omega)\geq 2^{-k-3}s
, where
s
denotes the side length of
Q
. Thus we have
l=\sqrt{n}s
. We also have for any
y\in q^{+}
,
B(y,2^{-k-3}s)\subset B(y_{q},2^{-k+1}l)
. By the standard Cauchy estimate,

\sup_{q^{+}}|\nabla h|\leq C2^{k}l^{-1}\sup_{B(y_{q},2^{-k+1}l)\cap\Omega}|h|\leq C2^{k(n+2)/2}l^{-(n+2)/2}e^{-cNk}M.

(5.2)

Let
B_{0}=B(x_{Q}+3\cdot 2^{-k-3}se_{n},s/2)
and let
B_{0,+}=\{(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in B_{0}:x^{\prime\prime}\geq x_{Q}+3\cdot 2^{-k-3}s\}
be the upper half of
B_{0}
. We denote by
\Gamma_{0}
the flat part of the boundary of
B_{0,+}
. Note that
2B_{0}\subset B_{1}
. Assuming that
\tau<2^{-k-3}
, we have
dist(B_{0,+},\partial\Omega)\geq 2^{-k-2}s
. Then by the local boundedness (Lemma
2.1
) and the interior Cauchy estimate, we get

\sup_{B_{0}\cap\Omega}|h|\leq Cl^{-n/2}M,\quad\sup_{B_{0,+}}|\nabla h|\leq C2^{k}l^{-(n+2)/2}M.

We also have
\Gamma_{0}\subset\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)}q^{+}
since
\tau<2^{-k-3}
. Then by (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we have

\sup_{\Gamma_{0}}|h|\leq C2^{kn/2}l^{-n/2}e^{-cNk}M,\quad\sup_{\Gamma_{0}}|\nabla h|\leq C2^{k(n+2)/2}l^{-(n+2)/2}e^{-cNk}M.

Let
\tilde{h}=C^{-1}2^{-k}l^{n/2}M^{-1}h
, then
\tilde{h}
satisfies

\sup_{B_{0}\cap\Omega}|\tilde{h}|\leq 1,\quad\sup_{B_{0,+}}|\nabla\tilde{h}|\leq 1,\quad\sup_{\Gamma_{0}}|\tilde{h}|\leq\varepsilon,\quad\sup_{\Gamma_{0}}|\nabla\tilde{h}|\leq\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon=C2^{kn/2}e^{-cNk}
. Apply Lemma
2.3
to
\tilde{h}
, we have
\sup_{\frac{1}{3}B_{0,+}}|\tilde{h}|\leq C\varepsilon^{\gamma}
, that is

\sup_{\frac{1}{3}B_{0,+}}|h|\leq C2^{\gamma kn/2+k}l^{-n/2}e^{-c\gamma Nk}M.

Let
y_{Q}=x_{Q}+\frac{s}{12}e_{n}
and let
m
be the least interger such that
2^{m}>16\sqrt{n}
. Then
B_{2}=B(y_{Q},2^{-m}l)\subset\frac{1}{3}B_{0,+}
for sufficiently large
k
. With the fact
|B_{2}|\leq Cl^{n}
, we have

\int_{B_{2}}h^{2}\leq|B_{2}|\sup_{B_{2}}h^{2}\leq|B_{2}|\sup_{\frac{1}{3}B_{0,+}}h^{2}\leq C2^{\gamma kn+2k}e^{-c\gamma Nk}M^{2}.

Finally, we estimate the growth of
h
at
y_{Q}
in terms of the doubling index. On the one hand, since
B_{1}\subset B(y_{Q},2l)
,

\log\frac{\int_{B(y_{Q},2l)\cap\Omega}h^{2}}{\int_{B_{2}}h^{2}}\geq\log\frac{\int_{B_{1}\cap\Omega}h^{2}}{\int_{B_{2}}h^{2}}\geq c\gamma Nk-\gamma kn-2k-C,

where we have used
\log 2<1
. On the other hand, by the almost monotonicity of the doubling index (Lemma
3.1
),

\log\frac{\int_{B(y_{Q},2l)\cap\Omega}h^{2}}{\int_{B_{2}}h^{2}}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}N_{h}(y_{Q},2^{-j}l)\leq C(m+1)(N_{h}(y_{Q},l)+1)\leq C^{\prime}(N+1).

Then the inequality above implies

c\gamma Nk-\gamma kn-2k-C\leq C^{\prime}(N+1).

Choose
k
sufficiently large so that
c\gamma k/2>C^{\prime}
, then we have

N\leq\frac{2(\gamma kn+2k+C)}{c\gamma k}\leq C(n+\gamma^{-1}).

Taking
N_{0}=C(n+\gamma^{-1})
, we obtain a contradiction for
N>N_{0}
.
∎

with
y_{j}\in Q
. Then
8\overline{B_{j}}\subset\Omega
and
N_{h}(y_{j},s(Q)/25)\leq N^{*}_{h}(Q)
. By Lemma
3.4
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(h)\cap Q)&\leq\sum_{j}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(h)\cap B_{j})\leq C(N_{h}(y_{j},s(Q)/25)+1)r^{n-1}\\
&\leq C^{\prime}(N^{*}_{h}(Q)+1)r^{n-1}\leq C^{\prime\prime}(N_{h}(x,4r)+1)r^{n-1}.\end{split}

For boundary cubes, we use (
6.1
). Thus for each boundary cube
Q
,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(h)\cap Q)\leq CN^{**}_{h}(Q)s(Q)^{n-1}\leq C^{\prime}(N_{h}(x,4r)+1)r^{n-1}.

(6.2)

Summing these inequalities over all cubes, we obtain the required estimate. It remains to prove (
6.1
).

6.2
Proof of (
6.1
)

We fix a compact set
K\subset\Omega
and prove that

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(h)\cap Q\cap K)\leq C_{0}N^{**}_{h}(Q)s(Q)^{n-1},

(6.3)

where
Q\subset 2B
is a cube as in the standard construction and
C_{0}
is independent of
K
. Then (
6.1
) follows.

For cubes
Q
with sufficiently small side length, (
6.3
) holds since
Q\cap K=\varnothing
. We prove (
6.3
) by induction. Assume it holds for cubes with
s(Q)<s
, we prove it for cubes with
s(Q)<2^{k}s
, where
k\geq k_{2}(n)
as in the beginning of this section.

We consider the standard construction
(Q,\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q),\mathcal{I}_{k}(Q)
. Each inner cube
q\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(Q)
can be covered by balls
b
centred in
q\subset Q
with radii
s(q)/100
and such that
8\bar{b}\subset\Omega
, so that the number of balls is bounded by a dimensional constant. For each such ball
b=B(y,s(q)/100)
, we have
N_{h}(y,s(q)/25)\leq N^{*}_{h}(Q)
. Then by Lemma
3.4
, we have

\sum_{q\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(Q)}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(h)\cap q)\leq C(N^{*}_{h}(Q)+1)s(Q)^{n-1}\leq C_{1}N^{**}_{h}(Q)s(Q)^{n-1},

where
C_{1}
depends on
k
and thus only on
n
.

For boundary cubes
q
, we have
N^{**}_{h}(q)\leq N^{**}_{h}(Q)
since
q\subset Q
. Recall that there exists a cube
q_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)
such that either
N^{**}_{h}(q_{0})\leq N^{**}_{h}(Q)/2
or
Z(h)\cap q_{0}=\varnothing
. We use the induction assumption for every boundary cube and obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}&(Z(h)\cap K\cap(\cup_{q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)}q))\\
&\leq\sum_{q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q),q\neq q_{0}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(h)\cap K\cap q)+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(h)\cap K\cap q_{0})\\
&\leq\sum_{q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q),q\neq q_{0}}C_{0}N^{**}_{h}(q)s(q)^{n-1}+\frac{C_{0}}{2}N^{**}_{h}(Q)s(q_{0})^{n-1}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{2^{k(n-1)}-1}{2^{k(n-1)}}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{k(n-1)}}\right)C_{0}N^{**}_{h}(Q)s(Q)^{n-1}.\end{split}

Finally, we choose
C_{0}
large enough so that

C_{1}+\left(\frac{2^{k(n-1)}-1}{2^{k(n-1)}}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{k(n-1)}}\right)C_{0}<C_{0}.

Note that
C_{0}
does not depend on
K
. Then, summing the interior and boundary estimates, we obtain

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(h)\cap K\cap Q)\leq C_{0}N^{**}_{h}(Q)s(Q)^{n-1}.

This concludes the induction step and the proof of (
6.1
).

7
Neumann Laplace Eigenfunctions

7.1
Harmonic extension and an estimate on the doubling index

For the Neumann eigenfunction
u
satisfying
\Delta u+\lambda u=0
in
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{0}
. Let
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\times\mathbb{R}
and

h(x,t)=e^{\sqrt{\lambda}t}u(x).

(7.1)

Then
\Delta h=0
in
\Omega
and
\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}=0
on
\partial\Omega=\partial\Omega_{0}\times\mathbb{R}
. And the zero set
Z(h)=Z(u)\times\mathbb{R}
. Also, we have the following estimate on the doubling index.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain. There exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(\Omega_{0})
such that the following statement holds. For any
r<r_{0}/16
there exists
C=C(r,\Omega_{0})
such that for any Neumann Laplace eigenfunction
u
in
\Omega_{0}
with eigenvalue
\lambda
, the corresponding harmonic extension
h
satisfies
N_{h}(y,r)\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}
for any
y\in\overline{\Omega}=\overline{\Omega}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
r_{0}
be such that
\partial\Omega\cap B(x,r_{0})
can be represented by a
C^{1,1}
function in local coordinates for any
x\in\partial\Omega
. We have
r_{0}=r_{0}(\Omega)
. For any
y=(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}
, let
y_{0}=(x,0)
. Since
h(x,t+s)=e^{\sqrt{\lambda}t}h(x,s)
, we have
N_{h}(y,r)=N_{h}(y_{0},r)
. So it is sufficient to prove the estimate for
y\in\Omega\times\{0\}
.

Fix
r<r_{0}/16
and let
\mathcal{S}\subset\overline{\Omega}_{0}
be a
r/8
-net for
\overline{\Omega}_{0}
, i.e.,
\overline{\Omega}_{0}\subset\bigcup_{p\in\mathcal{S}}B(p,r/8)
. The number of points in
\mathcal{S}
does not exceed
C(n,r,diam(\Omega_{0}))
. Assume that
|u(x_{0})|=\max_{\Omega_{0}}|u|=1
. For any
y=(y^{\prime},0)\in\overline{\Omega}_{0}\times\{0\}
, we consider a path
\gamma:[0,1]\to\overline{\Omega}_{0}
from
y^{\prime}
to
x_{0}
. such that
\gamma((0,1))\subset\Omega_{0}
. Now we construct an overlapping chain of balls
\{B_{j})\}_{j=0}^{J}
with radii
r/2
. Let
y_{0}=y^{\prime}
. Assuming that
y_{j}
is constructed, we define
s_{j}=\sup\{s\in[0,1]:|\gamma(s)-y_{j}|\leq r/8\}
. If
s_{j}<1
, we have
|\gamma(s_{j})-y_{j}|=r/8
and choose
y_{j+1}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
|\gamma(s_{j})-y_{j+1}|<r/8
. If
s_{j}=1
, we choose
y_{j+1}=y_{J}=x_{0}
and stop the chain. Define
B_{j}=B(y_{j},r/2)
. In this way, we have
|y_{j}-y_{j+1}|<r/4
for
0\leq j\leq J-1
and
B_{j+1}\subset\frac{3}{2}B_{j}
. Also,
J
does not exceed the number elements in
\mathcal{S}
plus two.

Let now
\tilde{B}_{j}=B((y_{j},0),r/2)
be the corresponding ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then we have
\sup_{4\tilde{B}_{j}\cap\Omega}|h|\leq e^{2\sqrt{\lambda}r}
. By Corollary
3.2
, in both cases that
4\tilde{B}_{j}\subset\Omega
and
4\tilde{B}_{j}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\varnothing
, we always have

\sup_{\frac{3}{2}\tilde{B}_{j}\cap\Omega}|h|\leq C\left(\sup_{\tilde{B}_{j}\cap\Omega}|h|\right)^{1-\delta}\left(\sup_{4\tilde{B}_{j}\cap\Omega}|h|\right)^{\delta}\leq Ce^{2\delta\sqrt{\lambda}r}\left(\sup_{\tilde{B}_{j}\cap\Omega}|h|\right)^{1-\delta},

where
C
and
\delta
only depends on
n
and the
C^{1,1}
character of
\Omega
. Therefore we obtain

\sup_{\tilde{B}_{j}\cap\Omega}|h|\geq ce^{-\frac{2\delta}{1-\delta}\sqrt{\lambda}r}\left(\sup_{\frac{3}{2}\tilde{B}_{j}\cap\Omega}|h|\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\delta}}\geq ce^{-\frac{2\delta}{1-\delta}\sqrt{\lambda}r}\left(\sup_{\tilde{B}_{j+1}\cap\Omega}|h|\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\delta}}

Remind that we have
\sup_{\tilde{B}_{J}\cap\Omega}|h|=e^{\sqrt{\lambda}r/2}
. Combining the inequalities above, we obtain

\sup_{\tilde{B}_{0}\cap\Omega}|h|\geq c_{1}e^{-C_{2}\sqrt{\lambda}},

where
c_{1}
and
C_{2}
depend on
r
and
J
but not on
\lambda
. The
r/8
-net
\mathcal{S}
can be chosen so that the number of elements in
\mathcal{S}
depends only on
diam(\Omega_{0})
,
r
and the dimension
n
. Thus
c_{1}
and
C_{2}
depend only on
diam(\Omega_{0})
,
r
and
n
.

Finally, by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\begin{split}N_{h}(y,r)&=\log\frac{\int_{4\tilde{B}_{0}\cap\Omega}h^{2}}{\int_{2\tilde{B}_{0}\cap\Omega}h^{2}}\\
&\leq\log\frac{\sup_{4\tilde{B}_{0}\cap\Omega}h^{2}}{\sup_{\tilde{B}_{0}\cap\Omega}h^{2}}+C\\
&\leq(2r+C_{2})\sqrt{\lambda}+C\leq C\sqrt{\lambda},\end{split}

where
C=C(\Omega_{0},r)
. We also use the fact that
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{0})>0
, where
\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{0})
is the first Neumann Laplace eigenvalue in
\Omega_{0}
.
∎

7.2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain. Let
\tau_{n}
be the constant as in Theorem
3.3
. For this
\tau_{n}
, there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(\Omega_{0})
such that
\partial\Omega_{0}\cap B(x,r_{0})\in Lip(\tau)
for every
x\in\partial\Omega_{0}
. Consider the domain
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and let
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{0}\times[-1,1]
. Firstly, we cover the
2^{-12}r_{0}
-neighborhood of
\partial\Omega\times[-1,1]
by balls centred on
\partial\Omega
with radii
2^{-10}r_{0}
. Denote these balls by
\{B_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
. We have
\partial\Omega\cap 128B_{j}\in Lip(\tau_{n})
. The number
J
depends only on
\partial\Omega_{0}
and
r_{0}
thus only on
\Omega_{0}
. Then we cover
\Omega_{1}\setminus(\cup_{j}B_{j})
by balls with centers in
\Omega_{1}\setminus(\cup_{j}B_{j})
and radii
2^{-15}r_{0}
. Denote these balls by
\{B^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
. We have
8B^{\prime}_{k}\subset\Omega
. The number
K
depends only on
\Omega_{0}
.

Let now
u
be a Neumann Laplace eigenfunction in
\Omega_{0}
:
\Delta u+\lambda u=0
in
\Omega_{0}
and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{0}
. Its harmonic extension
h(x,t)=e^{\sqrt{\lambda}t}u(x)
satisfies
\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}
on
\partial\Omega
. Let

C_{0}=\max\{C(2^{-8}r_{0},\Omega_{0}),C(2^{13}r_{0},\Omega_{0})\},

where

7.1
. Then for
B(x,r)\in\{B_{j}\}\cup\{B^{\prime}_{k}\}

3.3
to the boundary balls

3.4
to the inner balls

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{n}(Z(h)\cap\Omega_{1})&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mathcal{H}^{n}(Z(h)\cap B_{j})+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{H}^{n}(Z(h)\cap B^{\prime}_{k})\\
&\leq C(J,K)(C_{0}\sqrt{\lambda}+1)(r(B_{j})^{n}+r(B^{\prime}_{k})^{n})\leq C_{1}\sqrt{\lambda}.\end{split}

Then
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z(u)\cap\Omega_{0})\leq C_{1}\sqrt{\lambda}

1.1
.

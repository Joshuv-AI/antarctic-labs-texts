A finite dimensional trace formula
Zhao Tianhong

Abstract
We take the trace of Von-Neumann’s ergodic theorem and get a trace formula of a unitary matrix family. It is an extension of Poisson summation formula in higher dimension. We also construct a family of crystalline measure with complex coefficient.

Let
U\left(k\right),k\in\mathbb{R}
be a family of finite-dimensional unitary matrix,
A\left(k\right),k\in\mathbb{R}
be a family of uniform bounded matrix. Suppose
k\mapsto U\left(k\right)
and
k\mapsto A\left(k\right)
are smooth, with some growth condition. Suppose
f\left(k\right)=\det\left(U\left(k\right)-I\right)
only has simple zero. Let
\lambda\left(k\right)
be the eigenvalue of
U\left(k\right)
,
\left|\lambda\left(k\right)\right\rangle
be the eigenvector of
\lambda\left(k\right)
, then we have:

Theorem 1.1
.

\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)A\left(k\right)\right)=\sum_{k_{0}}\frac{\left\langle\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)|A\left(k_{0}\right)|\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)\right\rangle}{\left|\left\langle\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)|U^{{}^{\prime}}\left(k_{0}\right)|\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)\right\rangle\right|}\delta\left(k-k_{0}\right)

where
k_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy:
\det\left(U\left(k_{0}\right)-I\right)=0,\ \ \lambda\left(k_{0}\right)=1.

Let
A\left(k\right)=U^{{}^{\prime}}\left(k\right)=\frac{dU\left(k\right)}{dk}
, suppose
U\left(k\right)
has positive generator, then we have:

Theorem 1.2
.

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)U^{{}^{\prime}}\left(k\right)\right)=\sum_{k_{0}}\delta\left(k-k_{0}\right)

2
Proof

We start at one-dimensional case:

Theorem 2.1
.

(Poisson summation formula)
Let
z\in S^{1}
, we have:

\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{m}=\delta\left(z-1\right).

To get the usual Poisson summation formula, we only need to take
z=e^{2\pi it},t\in\mathbb{R}
and use the change the variable of Dirac
\delta
function:

\delta\left(e^{2\pi it}-1\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m}\delta\left(t-m\right),

then we will get:

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2m\pi it}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta\left(t-m\right).

Now we extend this statement to high-dimensional case.

We want to consider a summation:

\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}U^{m},

where
U
is a unitary matrix.

From Von-Neumann’s ergodic theorem, we know:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{m=-N}^{N}U^{m}=P_{1},

P_{1}
is the projection on invariant subspace:

X_{inv}=\left\{x\in X,Ux=x\right\},

and
X
is the underlying Hilbert space. So it could be better to consider the trace of
\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}U^{m}
and get the information of fixed subspace by
U
. Noted that
P_{1}=0
if and only if
U
does not have eigenvalue
1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
U\left(k\right),\ k\in\mathbb{R}
be a family of unitary matrix,
k\mapsto U\left(k\right)
is smooth. Suppose
f\left(k\right)=\det\left(U\left(k\right)-I\right)
only has simple zero and the eigenvalue
\lambda\left(k\right)
has non-zero speed:
\frac{d\lambda}{dk}\neq 0
and the fourier coefficient
c_{m}\left(g\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)\right)g\left(k\right)dk
satisfy:
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{m}\left(g\right)
converges absolutely, where
g
is Schwartz function.

Then we have:

\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)\right)=\sum_{\lambda}\delta\left(\lambda\left(k\right)-1\right)=\sum_{k_{0}}\frac{\delta\left(k-k_{0}\right).}{\left|\frac{d\lambda}{dk}(k_{0})\right|}

where
\lambda\left(k\right)
is the eigenvalue of
U\left(k\right)
, the middle sum runs over all the eigenvalue of
U\left(k\right)
, the right sum runs over all the
k_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that:

f\left(k_{0}\right)=\det\left(U\left(k_{0}\right)-I\right)=0.

The sum converges in the sense of distribution on Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
-1<t<1
be a parameter,
g\left(k\right)\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, then we have:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{\left|m\right|}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)\right)g\left(k\right)dk=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{\left|m\right|}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)\right)g\left(k\right)dk

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\lambda}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1-t^{2}}{\left|t-\lambda\left(k\right)\right|^{2}}g\left(k\right)dk

First ”=” is dominated convergence theorem. From definition of Dirac
\delta
function:

t\to 1^{-},\ \ \ \frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1-t^{2}}{\left|t-\lambda\left(k\right)\right|^{2}}\to\delta\left(\lambda\left(k\right)-1\right).

Take the limit:
t\to 1^{-}
and we get:

\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)\right)=\sum_{\lambda}\delta\left(\lambda\left(k\right)-1\right).

Suppose
f\left(k\right)=\det\left(U\left(k\right)-I\right)
only has simple zero and the eigenvalue
\lambda\left(k\right)
has non-zero speed:
\frac{d\lambda}{dk}\neq 0
, then from change of variable we get:

\sum_{\lambda}\delta\left(\lambda\left(k\right)-1\right)=\sum_{k_{0}}\frac{\delta\left(k-k_{0}\right).}{\left|\frac{d\lambda}{dk}(k_{0})\right|}

where
\lambda\left(k\right)
is the eigenvalue of
U\left(k\right)
, the left sum runs over all the eigenvalue of
U\left(k\right)
, the right sum runs over all the
k_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that:

f\left(k_{0}\right)=\det\left(U\left(k_{0}\right)-I\right)=0.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

(speed of eigenvalue)
Suppose
f\left(k\right)=\det\left(U\left(k\right)-I\right)
only has simple zero.
We have:

\frac{d\lambda}{dk}\left(k_{0}\right)=\left\langle\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)|U^{{}^{\prime}}\left(k_{0}\right)|\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)\right\rangle.

for all the
k_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

f\left(k_{0}\right)=\det\left(U\left(k_{0}\right)-I\right)=0.

Proof.

Take the differential of:

\lambda(k)=\left\langle\lambda(k)|U(k)|\lambda(k)\right\rangle=tr\left(P_{\lambda}U\right)

and use the smoothness of
U\left(k\right)
,
\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)=1
, we can get the result.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose
A\left(k\right),k\in\mathbb{R}
is a family of uniform bounded matrix, Let
U\left(k\right),\ k\in\mathbb{R}
be a family of unitary matrix. Suppose
k\mapsto U\left(k\right)
and
k\mapsto A\left(k\right)
are smooth. Suppose
f\left(k\right)=\det\left(U\left(k\right)-I\right)
only has simple zero and the eigenvalue
\lambda\left(k\right)
has non-zero speed:
\frac{d\lambda}{dk}\neq 0
and the fourier coefficient
c_{m}\left(g\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)A\left(k\right)\right)g\left(k\right)dk
satisfy:
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{m}\left(g\right)
converges absolutely, where
g
is Schwartz function.

Then we have:

\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)A\left(k\right)\right)=\sum_{k_{0}}\frac{\left\langle\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)|A\left(k_{0}\right)|\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)\right\rangle}{\left|\left\langle\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)|U^{{}^{\prime}}\left(k_{0}\right)|\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)\right\rangle\right|}\delta\left(k-k_{0}\right)

where
k_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy:
\det\left(U\left(k_{0}\right)-I\right)=0,\ \ \lambda\left(k_{0}\right)=1.

The sum converges in the sense of distribution on Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.

Proof.

Similar as Theorem 2.2, we have:
∎

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{\left|m\right|}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)A\left(k\right)\right)g\left(k\right)dk=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{\left|m\right|}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)A\left(k\right)\right)g\left(k\right)dk

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(1-t^{2})\left\langle\lambda\left(k\right)|A\left(k\right)|\lambda\left(k\right)\right\rangle}{\left|t-\lambda\left(k\right)\right|^{2}}g\left(k\right)dk.

Take the limit:
t\to 1^{-}
, we get the result.

Corollary 2.5
.

Suppose the generator of
U\left(k\right)
:

D\left(k\right)=-i\frac{dU(k)}{dk}U^{-1}\left(k\right)

is uniform-bounded positive definite matrix satisfy the condition above, then we have:

Theorem 2.6
.

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)U^{{}^{\prime}}\left(k\right)\right)=\sum_{k_{0}}\delta\left(k-k_{0}\right)

Example 2.1
.

(Crystalline measure)

Suppose
U\left(k\right)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{ik\ell_{1}}&0&..&0\\
0&e^{ik\ell_{2}}&..&0\\
0&0&..&0\\
..&..&..&e^{ik\ell_{n}}\end{pmatrix}S,\

where S is a constant unitary matrix,
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},...,\ell_{n}>0
. Let
A
be a constant unitary matrix. Then the trace formula can be written as:

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n_{1}+n_{2}...+n_{N}=n}c\left(n_{1},n_{2},...n_{N}\right)e^{ik\left(n_{1}\ell_{1}+...+n_{N}\ell_{N}\right)}=\sum_{k_{0}}c^{\prime}\left(n\right)\delta(k-k_{0})

where
k_{0}
is zero of
f\left(k\right)=det\left(I-U\left(k\right)\right)
,
c^{\prime}\left(n\right)=\frac{\left\langle\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)|A\left(k_{0}\right)|\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)\right\rangle}{\left|\left\langle\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)|U^{{}^{\prime}}\left(k_{0}\right)|\lambda\left(k_{0}\right)\right\rangle\right|}
,
n_{1},n_{2}...n_{N}
run over all non-positive numbers or all non-negetive numbers.

3
Visible of this result

There are two ways to draw this result by software. First, we may consider the sum:
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{\left|m\right|}tr\left(U^{m}\left(k\right)\right),-1<t<1
, take
t
near 1.

Second, we can use Newton’s identity. Let
h\left(\lambda,k\right)=\det\left(\lambda I-U\left(k\right)\right)
be the characteristic polynomial of
U\left(k\right)
, from Cayley-Hamilton theorem we have:

h\left(U,k\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}\left(k\right)U^{i}\left(k\right)=0,

multiply
U
and
U^{-1}
and take the trace we will get many linear relationships, known as Newton’s identity
[
4
]
:

\begin{cases}...\\
\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}\left(k\right)tr\left(U^{i+j}\left(k\right)\right)&=0\\
\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}\left(k\right)tr\left(U^{i+j+1}\left(k\right)\right)&=0\\
\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}\left(k\right)tr\left(U^{i+j+2}\left(k\right)\right)&=0\\
...\end{cases}

where
j\in\mathbb{Z}
is an arbitary integer.

Using the Newton’s identity, we have:

\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}\left(k\right)\right)\left(\sum_{j=-N}^{N}tr\left(U^{j}\left(k\right)\right)\right)=R\left(n,N,k\right),

where the reminder
R\left(n,N,k\right)
is uniform bounded on variable
N,k
. Thus we have:

\left(\sum_{j=-N}^{N}tr\left(U^{j}\left(k\right)\right)\right)=\frac{R\left(n,N,k\right)}{h\left(1,k\right)}=\frac{R\left(n,N,k\right)}{\det\left(I-U\left(k\right)\right)}

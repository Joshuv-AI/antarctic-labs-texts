Network meta-analysis of triple immunosuppressive therapies and standard of care for induction of remission of active lupus nephritis
Authors: Gabriel Contreras, Vinodh Mechery, Sanjana Kota, Daniel Del Castillo Rix, Miguel Bandes, Adriana Dejman, Yiqin Zuo, Juan Camilo Duque, Jair Munoz Mendoza
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn patients with active lupus nephritis (LN), 8 different triple immunosuppressive therapies have been compared to the standard of care (SOC) of double immunosuppressive therapy in 12 induction randomized clinical trials (RCTs). The triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+Tacrolimus, SOC+Voclosporin, SOC+Belimumab, and SOC+Obinutuzumab compared to SOC alone demonstrated significantly higher remission of LN without excessive risk of serious adverse events (SAEs) and serious infectious events (SIEs) in RCTs. Direct or indirect comparisons among triple immunosuppressive therapies have not been studied. In this network meta-analysis (NMA), we systematically provide indirect comparisons of the relative efficacy and safety of triple immunosuppressive therapies, ranking them according to efficacy achieving primary renal remission (PRR) of LN and safety reducing the risk of SAEs and SIEs.ResultsThe NMA included predominantly women with LN. In direct pairwise comparisons, SOC+Tacrolimus (benefit ratio 1.91 [95% Credible Interval 1.45 to 2.56]), SOC+Voclosporin (1.69 [1.30 to 2.23]), SOC+Obinutuzumab (1.41 [1.09 to 1.84]), and SOC+Belimumab (1.32 [1.04 to 1.69]) had significantly higher PRR compared to SOC alone. In indirect pairwise comparisons, patients who received SOC+Tacrolimus compared to SOC+Rituximab (2.30 [1.27 to 4.20]), SOC+Anifrolumab (1.98 [1.07 to 3.52]), SOC+Ocrelizumab (1.81 [1.12 to 2.82]), and SOC+Abatacept (1.78 [1.07 to 2.91]) had significantly higher PRR. Patients who received SOC+Voclosporin compared to SOC+Rituximab (2.03 [1.14 to 2.68]) had significantly higher PRR. SOC+Belimumab, SOC+Obinutuzumab, SOC+Voclosporin and SOC+Tacrolimus ranked as effective therapies inducing PRR with SUCRA probability scores between 61.14% and 95.12%. Risk ratios of SAE and SIE were not significantly different among the immunosuppressive therapies.ConclusionIn patients with LN, induction therapies of SOC with calcineurin inhibitors had the highest relative benefit achieving remission of LN.Keywords: lupus nephritis, triple immunosuppressive therapy, renal remission, serious adverse events, serious infectious events

Introduction

Major advances in induction immunosuppressive therapies for patients with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) with severe and active lupus nephritis (LN) have resulted in significant improvement in remission of LN without excessive risk of serious adverse events (SAEs).1–12 In the last 18 years, the use of triple immunosuppressive therapies for induction of LN, combining a calcineurin inhibitor (Tacrolimus1,6 or Voclosporin7,9) or an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody (Rituximab 2 or Ocrelizumab 3 or Obinutuzumab10,12) or a B-lymphocyte stimulator (BlyS) inhibitor (Belimumab 8 ) or a type I interferon (IFN) receptor antagonist (Anifrolumab 11 ) or a soluble Cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein-4 (CTLA-4) analog (Abatacept4,5) with standard of care (SOC) therapy, have been directly compared to SOC therapy of double immunosuppressive therapies (mycophenolate or cyclophosphamide or sequential fixed-dose of cyclophosphamide followed by azathioprine and corticosteroids) in 12 randomized clinical trials (RCTs).

In published RCTs, the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC plus Tacrolimus (SOC+Tacrolimus1,6), SOC plus Voclosporin (SOC+Voclosporin7,9), SOC plus Belimumab (SOC+Belimumab 8 ), and SOC plus Obinutuzumab (SOC+Obinutuzumab10,12) demonstrated significantly higher remission of LN without excessive risk of SAEs compared to SOC therapy alone. However, the use of SOC plus Rituximab (SOC+Rituximab 2 ), SOC plus Ocrelizumab (SOC+Ocrelizumab 3 ), SOC plus Anifrolumab (SOC+Anifrolumab 11 ), and SOC plus Abatacept (SOC+Abatacept4,5) have not demonstrated better efficacy and safety compared to SOC therapy alone.

Currently, there are four available triple immunosuppressive therapies for the treatment of active and severe LN, and considerations for choosing one triple immunosuppressive therapy over the other require discussion between patients and providers of individual study results. There are no studies comparing directly or indirectly the efficacy and safety of triple immunosuppressive therapies among themselves. Therefore, we completed a network meta-analysis (NMA) of triple immunosuppressive therapies that provided systematic evidence of the relative efficacy and safety of triple immunosuppressive therapies for the treatment of active and severe LN (Figure 1).

Network meta-analysis of triple immunosuppressive therapies and standard of care therapy (SOC) for the treatment of active and severe LN.

Methods

Data sources, searches, and included studies

We performed an electronic search for RCTs that compared triple immunosuppressive therapies to SOC therapy for the induction treatment of SLE patients with active and severe LN, using the MEDLINE, EMBASE, PUBMED, and Cochrane Library databases. The literature search was conducted from databases inception through mid-2025, prior to the initial submission of the manuscript. Three investigators (GC, VM and JMM) independently screened titles, abstracts, and full-text articles for eligibility. Any disagreements regarding study inclusion were resolved through discussion and consensus among the investigators. Our search terms included both controlled vocabulary (where available, including MeSH and database-specific indexing terms) and free-text keywords with an example of the search strategy used in the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials included in supplemental table 9. The first author (GC) also manually scanned the references in the relevant articles for cross-reference to other published RCTs. The main eligibility criteria of studies for inclusion in the NMA were the following: (1) RCTs for induction of LN using a study design comparing triple immunosuppressive therapies to SOC or double immunosuppressive therapies of SOC alone, and (2) RCTs published in peer-reviewed journals in English. Reviews, observational studies, and clinical trials that did not clearly define eligibility criteria, randomization process, treatment strategies, outcomes, statistical analyses, and did not use the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT 13 ) were excluded from this NMA (supplemental Figure 1 flow diagram). Six investigators (GC, VM, DDCR, YZ, JD and JMM) independently assessed the quality of RCTs and reached a consensus according to the Risk of Bias-2 (RoB-2) system for assessing quality of RCTs. 14

Assessment of validity of network meta-analysis

The literature search yielded 12 published RCTs that compared 8 triple immunosuppressive therapies, SOC+Tacrolimus, SOC+Voclosporin, SOC+Obinutuzumab, SOC+Belimumab, SOC+Abatacept, SOC+Ocrelizumab, SOC+Anifrolumab, and SOC + Rituximab, to SOC therapy alone for inclusion in the primary network of the NMA (Figure 1). Four RCTs3,4,7,11 had two dose groups of the experimental immunosuppressive drugs that were pooled together as single treatment group in each study. This approach was chosen because the efficacy and safety outcomes reported for the two dose regimens were broadly similar and because pooling avoided double-counting participants in the shared control SOC alone group, consistent with standard meta-analytic methodology. 15 The assumption of homogeneity of the 12 RCTs included in the NMA was fulfilled in terms of similar patients’ characteristics (gender, age, active and severe LN with similar baseline renal function tests), comparator of SOC immunosuppressive therapy, and renal remission outcomes (tables 1 and supplemental table 1). The assumption of connectivity of the 12 RCTs was fulfilled as the control groups received the common comparator of SOC therapy. The assumption of transitivity of the NMA was fulfilled by the similarly measured renal remission outcomes and the consistent treatment effects across the direct pairwise comparisons of triple immunosuppressive therapies to SOC therapy alone for the induction of LN. The assumption of consistency of the NMA was fulfilled by the agreement between direct and indirect pairwise comparisons (supplemental Figures 4 and 5 of the posterior mean deviance of individual data points in the inconsistency model against their posterior mean deviance in the consistency model).

Data extraction and outcomes of efficacy and safety

The following investigators, GC, SK, DDCR, MB, AD, JD, YZ and JMM, extracted and validated the data from the 12 RCTs included in the NMA (tables 1 and supplemental table 1). The primary efficacy outcome of interest was primary renal remission (PRR) as defined in the primary publications of the included RCTs (supplemental table 1). The definition of the secondary efficacy outcome of complete renal remission (CRR) was also included in the supplemental table 1. The primary safety outcome of interest was SAEs, and the secondary safety outcome was serious infectious events (SIEs).

Statistical analysis for the network meta-analysis

The efficacy and safety of triple immunosuppressive therapies for active and severe LN were assessed with a Bayesian NMA using fixed-effect and random-effect models. The fixed-effect model was used for analyses in this paper due to lower effective number of parameters and estimated lower deviance information criterion, indicating a less complex and better-fitting model compared to the random-effect model. The random-effect model yielded similar results, not included in the paper. In supplemental tables 3 and 4, sensitivity analyses were provided according to the mechanism of action of the third immunosuppressive agent in the triple immunosuppressive therapies in a secondary network with 6 groups: SOC+Calcineurin inhibitor (tacrolimus and voclosporin); SOC+Anti-CD20 (Rituximab, Ocrelizumab and Obinutuzumab); SOC+Belimumab, an anti-BLyS; SOC+Anifrolumab, a type I IFN receptor antagonist; SOC+Abatacept, a soluble CTLA-4 analog; and, SOC alone. Additional sensitivity analyses were provided constructing a network excluding the AURA-LV study 7 that used a regimen with the fastest tapering of glucocorticoids (GCs) compared to other RCTs (supplemental tables 5 and 6). Sensitivity analyses were also provided constructing another network excluding the BELONG and NCT00119678 studies3,4 due to concerns of bias in the RoB-2 assessment (supplemental Figure 6, supplemental tables 7 and 8). In the NMA models, all Markov chains were run with 1000 adaptation iterations to adjust parameters for better mixing, 1000 burn-in iterations to allow stabilization, followed by 10000 iterations to estimate the posterior distribution used to estimate benefit ratios (BRs) of efficacy outcomes and risk ratios (RRs) of safety outcomes. Pairwise BRs or RRs with 95% credible intervals (95% CrIs) of PRR, CRR, SAEs and SIEs were illustrated in Forest plots, tables 2 and supplemental tables 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8. BRs and RRs were considered statistically significant if the 95% CrI did not include 1. Immunosuppressive therapies were ranked by their relative efficacy and safety using the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA), with probability scores between 100% (best) and 0% (worse), considering scores of equal or more than 50% as efficacious and/or safe therapies. Statistical analyses were performed using the R statistical software (version 4.1.2; R Core Team 2025) using libraries BUGSnet. Data visualization was carried out using the ggPLOT2 and Forestplot package.16–18

Results

Randomized controlled trials in the network meta-analysis

All 12 combined RCTs included 2797 and 2963 SLE patients with active and severe LN in the efficacy and safety analyses, respectively. Patients baseline charactesitics were similar across all studies (table 1). Supplemental table 1 illustrates frequencies of efficacy and safety outcomes according to therapy groups with definitions of efficacy outcomes and GCs regimes. Figure 1 illustrates the primary network of 9 immunosuppressive therapies with 36 pairwise comparisons. The secondary network of 6 immunosuppressive therapies included 15 pairwise comparisons.

Direct pairwise comparisons of efficacy and safety outcomes of induction immunosuppressive therapies for lupus nephritis

Efficacy outcome

In the primary network, 8 direct pairwise comparisons were performed of each triple immunosuppressive therapy compared to SOC therapy alone for the induction treatment of active and severe LN. The Forest plot in Figure 2(a) illustrates that patients who received SOC+Tacrolimus (BR 1.91 [95% CrI 1.45 to 2.56]), SOC+Voclosporin (1.69 [1.30 to 2.23]), SOC+Obinutuzumab (1.41 [1.09 to 1.84]), and SOC+Belimumab (1.32 [1.04 to 1.69]) had significantly higher BRs of achieving PRR compared to patients who received the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone. Likewise, the Forest plot in supplemental Figure 2(a) illustrates that patients who received SOC+Tacrolimus (1.91 [1.46 to 2.57]), SOC+Voclosporin (1.68 [1.29 to 2.20]), SOC+Belimumab (1.50 [1.08 to 2.10]), and SOC+Obinutuzumab (1.39 [1.06 to 1.84]), also had significantly higher BRs of achieving CRR compared to patients who received the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone. All other direct pairwise comparisons did not demonstrate significant differences in BRs of achieving the primary or secondary efficacy outcome of PRR or CRR, with 95% CrIs including 1.

(a) Benefit Ratio of Primary Renal Remission from Lupus Nephritis Treatment. (b) Risk Ratio of Serious Adverse Events from Lupus Nephritis Treatment.

In the secondary network, 5 direct pairwise comparisons were performed of each triple immunosuppressive therapy compared to SOC therapy alone. Supplemental tables 3 and 4 illustrates that patients who received SOC+Calcineurin inhibitor had significantly higher BR of achieving PRR or CRR (1.79 [1.48 to 2.18]) compared to patients who received the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone. Likewise, patients who received SOC+Belimumab had significantly higher BRs of achieving PRR (1.32 [1.04 to 1.68]) and CRR (1.50 [1.08 to 2.11]) compared to patients who received the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone. All other direct pairwise comparisons did not demonstrate significant differences in the BRs of achieving the primary or secondary efficacy outcome of PRR or CRR, with 95% CrIs including 1.

Safety outcomes

In the primary and secondary networks, direct pairwise comparisons of any of the triple immunosuppressive therapy did not achieve significant differences in the RRs of the primary or secondary safety outcomes of SAEs or SIEs compared to the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone, with 95% CrIs including 1 (Forest plots 2b and supplemental Forest plot 2b; tables 2 and supplemental tables 2, 3, and 4).

Indirect pairwise comparisons of efficacy and safety outcomes of induction triple immunosuppressive therapies for lupus nephritis

Efficacy outcomes

In the primary network, 28 indirect pairwise comparisons were performed between the triple immunosuppressive therapies for the induction treatment of active and severe LN. The lower triangle of table 2 illustrates that patients who received SOC+Tacrolimus compared to SOC+Rituximab (2.30 [1.27 to 4.20]), SOC+Anifrolumab (1.98 [1.07 to 3.52]), SOC+Ocrelizumab (1.81 [1.12 to 2.82]), and SOC+Abatacept (1.78 [1.07 to 2.91]) had significantly higher BRs of achieving PRR. Also, patients who received SOC+Voclosporin compared to SOC+Rituximab (2.03 [1.14 to 2.68]) had significantly higher BR of achieving PRR. All other indirect pairwise comparisons did not achieve significant differences in the BRs of achieving the primary efficacy outcome of PRR, with 95% CrIs including 1.

The lower triangle of supplemental table 2 illustrates that patients who received SOC+Tacrolimus compared to SOC+Rituximab (2.30 [1.27 to 4.23]), SOC+Ocrelizumab (1.82 [1.13 to 2.89]), and SOC+Abatacept (1.79 [1.09 to 2.93]) had significantly higher BRs of achieving CRR. Also, patients who received SOC+Voclosporin compared to SOC+Rituximab (2.01 [1.13 to 3.68]) had a significantly higher BR of achieving CRR. All other indirect pairwise comparisons did not demonstrate significant differences in the BRs of achieving the secondary efficacy outcome of CRR, with 95% CrIs including 1.

In the secondary network, 10 indirect pairwise comparisons were performed between the triple immunosuppressive therapies. The lower triangle of supplemental table 3 illustrates that patients who received SOC+Calcineurin inhibitor compared to SOC+Anti-CD20 (1.49 [1.12 to 1.97]), SOC+Abatacept (1.67 [1.06 to 2.60]) and SOC+Anifrolumab (1.86 [1.02 to 3.21]) had significantly higher BRs of achieving PRR. The lower triangle of supplemental table 4 illustrates that patients who received SOC+Calcineurin inhibitor compared to SOC+Anti-CD20 (1.51 [1.14 to 1.99]) and SOC+Abatacept (1.67 [1.06 to 2.62]) had significantly higher BRs of achieving the secondary efficacy outcome of CRR.

Safety outcomes

Indirect pairwise comparisons between triple immunosuppressive therapies were illustrated in the upper triangles of table 2 and supplemental tables 2, 3 and 4. Patients who received any of the triple immunosuppressive therapy compared to any other triple immunosuppressive therapy did not have significant differences in RRs of SAEs or SIEs, with 95% CrIs including 1 in the primary and secondary networks.

Sensitivity analyses of a network excluding the AURA-LV study and a network excluding BELONG and NCT00119678 studies

As illustrated in supplemental tables 5 and 6, direct and indirect pairwise comparisons of efficacy and safety outcomes between immunosuppressive therapies in the network excluding the AURA-LV 7 study did not substantially differ from comparisons in the primary NMA described above. Likewise as illustrated in supplemental tables 7 and 8, direct and indirect pairwise comparisons of efficacy and safety outcomes between immunosuppressive therapies in the network excluding BELONG and NCT00119678 studies3,4 did not substantially differ from comparisons in the primary NMA.

Relative ranking of the induction immunosuppressive therapies for lupus nephritis according to efficacy and safety using the primary network

Efficacy outcomes

As illustrated in the SUCRA plot in Figure 3(a), the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Tacrolimus (SUCRA = 95.12%) had the highest probability score, ranking this therapy as the best induction immunosuppressive therapy achieving the primary efficacy outcome of PRR, followed by the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+Voclosporin (SUCRA = 82.27%), SOC+Obinutuzumab (SUCRA = 68.42%), and SOC+Belimumab (SUCRA = 61.14%). Other induction immunosuppressive therapies had SUCRA probability scores less than 50% with the lowest score of 14.18% for the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Rituximab, ranking as the worst therapy inducing PRR.

(b) Surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) plot with ranking probability of treatments from the best to the worst preventing serious adverse events.

As illustrated in the SUCRA plot in supplemental Figure 3(a), the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Tacrolimus (SUCRA = 93.81%) had the highest probability score, ranking this therapy as the best induction immunosuppressive therapy achieving the secondary efficacy outcome of CRR, followed by the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+Voclosporin (SUCRA = 82.97%), SOC+Belimumab (SUCRA = 71.28%), and SOC+Obinutuzumab (SUCRA = 63.62%). Other induction immunosuppressive therapies had SUCRA probability scores less than 50% with the lowest score of 13.60% for the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Rituximab, ranking as the worst therapy inducing CRR.

Safety outcomes

As illustrated in the SUCRA plot in Figure 3(b), the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Rituximab (SUCRA = 84.85%) had the highest probability score ranking this therapy as the safest induction immunosuppressive therapy with the lowest risk of complicating with the primary safety outcome of SAEs, followed by the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+Belimumab (SUCRA = 79.78%), SOC+Abatacept (SUCRA = 61.93%), SOC (SUCRA = 57.90%), and SOC+Ocrelizumab (SUCRA = 51.00%). Other induction immunosuppressive therapies had SUCRA probability scores less than 50% with the lowest score of 13.37% for the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Tacrolimus, ranking as the riskiest therapy complicated by SAEs.

As illustrated in the SUCRA plot in supplemental Figure 3(b), the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Belimumab (SUCRA = 70.44%) had the highest probability score ranking this therapy as the safest induction immunosuppresive therapy with the lowest risk of complicating with the secondary safety outcome of SIEs, followed by the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+Rituximab (SUCRA = 70.42%), the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone (SUCRA = 66.94%), and the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Voclosporin (SUCRA = 56.83%). Other induction immunosuppressive therapies had SUCRA probability scores less than 50% with the lowest score of 18.71% for the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Tacrolimus, ranking as the riskiest therapy complicated by SIEs.

Discussion

In the last 18 years, published RCTs of induction immunosuppressive therapies for patients with severe and active LN have demonstrated the superiority of 4 triple immunosuppressive therapies, SOC+Tacrolimus, SOC+Voclosporin, SOC+Belimumab, and SOC+Obinutuzumab, compared to the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone, achieving higher remission of LN without excessive risk of SAE, SIE, and mortality. Our primary NMA consistently confirmed the findings in the RCTs with weighted BRs above 1 and 95% CrIs excluding 1 that the likelihood of achieving PRR was significantly higher of 1.91, 1.69, 1.41, and 1.32 folds in patients treated with SOC+Tacrolimus, SOC+Voclosporin, SOC+Obinutuzumab, and SOC+Belimumab respectively, compared to patients treated with the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone. Similarly, the likelihood of achieving CRR was significantly higher in patients treated with any of these triple immunosuppressive therapies compared to patients treated with the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone, as illustrated in the supplemental Figure 2(a) and supplemental table 2.

In our NMA, we reported new findings that we consider informative to providers caring for patients with active LN. The indirect pairwise comparisons additionally revealed that the likelihood of achieving PRR was significantly higher of 2.30, 1.98, 1.81, and 1.78 folds in patients treated with the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Tacrolimus compared to patients treated with the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+Rituximab, SOC+Anifrolumab, SOC+Ocrelizumab, and SOC+Abatacept, respectively. Similarly, the likelihood of achieving CRR was significantly higher of 2.30, 1.82, and 1.79 folds in patients treated with the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Tacrolimus compared to patients treated with the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+Rituximab, SOC+Ocrelizumab, and SOC+Abatacept. Patients treated with the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Voclosporin also has significantly higher likelihood of achieving PRR of 2.03 folds and of achieving CRR of 2.01 folds compared to patients treated with the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Rituximab. The likelihood of achieving PRR and CRR of patients treated with the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+Obinutuzumab and SOC+Belimumab were not significantly different compared to patients treated with any other triple immunosuppressive therapies, with 95% CrIs including 1.

As illustrated in the primary and secondary network analyses, the most efficacious triple immunosuppressive therapies included a calcineurin inhibitor with the highest BRs and high relative ranking probability scores above 82% of inducing remission of LN. The triple immunosuppressive therapies including a calcineurin inhibitor were the most beneficial likely due to their dual effect of systemic immunosuppression inhibiting synthesis of interleukin-2 and other cytokines resulting in inhibition of T-cell activation 19 and their direct effect in the glomerular basement membrane barrier reducing proteinuria by stabilization of the actin cytoskeleton in kidney podocytes with the blocking of the dephosphorylation of synaptopodin, preventing its degradation by cathepsin. 20 The high CRR of LN with triple immunosuppressive therapies, including a calcineurin inhibitor, was primarily driven by an early achievement of UPCR ≤ 0.5 with a median time of 170 days. 9

The BRs of achieving the efficacy outcomes of the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+Obinutuzumab and SOC+Belimumab were similarly high, with probability scores between 61.14% and 68.42% of inducing PRR and between 63.62% and 71.28% of inducing CRR. Obinutuzumab and Belimumab are B-cell depleting therapies with different mechanisms of action, but similar efficacy for the treatment of LN.21–25 Their high efficacy in achieving remission of LN highlight the importance of B-cell immunity in SLE.

It is important to discuss that the relative benefit of the three triple immunosuppressive therapies including an Anti-CD20 varied substantially, with probability scores ranging from 14.18% to 68.42% of inducing PRR and probability scores ranging from 13.60% to 63.62% of inducing CRR. These differences in efficacy of Anti-CD20s are likely related to lower depletion of B-lymphocytes with the use of type 1 Anti-CD20s such as Rituximab 2 and Ocrelizumab 3 compared to the type 2 Anti-CD20 Obinutuzumab.10,12,21,26 Obinutuzumab, compared to type 1 Anti-CD20s, has greater affinity for the immunoglobulin gamma Fc region receptor III-A on the effector cells minimizing B-lymphocytes internalization of the agent, with greater antibody-mediated cellular cytotoxicity of B-lymphocytes and less dependency on the complement-dependent cytotoxicity.21,26

The relative ranking of efficacy of the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Anifrolumab, inducing remission of LN, was low and comparable to the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone, with probability scores of 27.43% inducing PRR and of 32.65% inducing CRR. Anifrolumab antagonizes type I interferon (IFN) cytokines that are highly expressed in SLE patients, playing a role in inflammation, immunoregulation, and T-cells activation. Anifrolumab is approved for the treatment of patients with moderate to severe extrarenal SLE,27,28 but its role in the treatment of patients with severe LN is uncertain. 29

Risk of SAEs and particularly SIEs is another relevant safety aspect for patients undergoing induction therapy for severe LN. In our NMA, the likelihood of SAEs was higher but not statistically different (95% CrIs including 1) in patients treated with the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+tacrolimus, SOC+Obinutuzumab, SOC+Voclosporin, SOC+Anifrolumab, and SOC+Ocrelizumab compared to patients treated with the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone (table 2). Similarly, the likelihood of SIEs was higher but not statistically different in patients treated with the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+tacrolimus, SOC+Abatacept, SOC+Ocrelizumab, SOC+Anifrolumab, SOC+Obinutuzumab, and SOC+Voclosporin, compared to patients treated with the double immunosuppressive therapy of SOC alone (supplemental table 2). The relative ranking of therapy safety revealed that the triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Rituximab ranked as the safest therapy preventing SAEs (Figure 3(b)). The triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Belimumab ranked as the safest therapy preventing SIEs (supplemental Figure 3(b)). The triple immunosuppressive therapy of SOC+Tacrolimus ranked as the riskiest therapy, complicated by SAEs and SIEs. The SUCRA rankings require a cautionary interpretation because it only provides relative hierarchical estimates of likelihood rather than definitive guarantees of safety. The SUCRA ranking can mask the magnitude and certainty of the efficacy and safety effects, compared to the more definitive estimates of BRs and RRs which were not significantly different with their 95% CrIs including 1.

Our NMA has several limitations. First, the study was an indirect comparative evidence based on aggregate trial-level data and the BRs and RRs of outcomes in the pairwise comparisons were estimated by pooling outcomes reported in the included RCTs. Particularly, the BRs and RRs of the indirect pairwise comparisons were inferred estimates of relative benefit and relative risk without direct pairwise comparisons that can only be performed in head-to-head comparisons of triple immunosuppressive therapies in RCTs. Second, we did not compare other safety outcomes, such as kidney adverse events, due to the lack of uniformly reporting of these adverse events in included studies. Nephrotoxicity is a SAE of particular interest in patients treated with triple immunosuppressive therapies including calcineurin inhibitors. Studies comparing the triple immunosuppressive therapies of SOC+Tacrolimus and SOC+Voclosporin did not uniformly include a kidney biopsy which is the most sensitive and specific diagnostic test to detect calcineurin inhibitor nephrotoxicity. Third, we did not compare the risk of mortality alone due to the low frequency of mortality in patients treated with triple immunosuppressive therapies or SOC therapy alone. Mortality was included in SAEs. Fourth, we did not compare the efficacy of immunosuppressive therapies on extrarenal lupus activity, which was not uniformly reported in the included RCTs that focused on LN outcomes.

Our NMA has several strengths, including the high quality of included RCTs based on the RoB-2 system as illustrated in supplemental Figure 6. Sensitivity analyses of the network excluding two RCTs with some concerns for bias did not substantially differ from analyses of the primary network. Our NMA included a large number of SLE patients with severe LN: 2797 in the efficacy outcomes analyses and 2963 in the safety outcomes analyses. Assumptions of homogeneity, transitivity, connectivity, and consistency of the 12 RCTs included in the study were fulfilled in the NMA.

In conclusion, patients with severe LN treated with SOC+Tacrolimus, SOC+Voclosporin, SOC+Obinutuzumab, and SOC+Belimumab had a significantly higher likelihood of achieving PRR and CRR compared to patients treated with SOC alone, without excessive risk of SAEs and SIEs. Patients treated with SOC+Tacrolimus had a significantly higher likelihood of achieving PRR compared to patients treated with SOC+Rituximab, SOC+Anifrolumab, SOC+Ocrelizumab, and SOC+Abatacept. Patients treated with SOC+Voclosporin had a significantly higher likelihood of achieving PRR and CRR compared to patients treated with SOC+Rituximab.

Analysis of an exponential integrator for stochastic PDE s driven by Riesz noise
Charles-Edouard Bréhier, David Cohen, Lluís Quer-Sardanyons, Johan Ulander

Abstract
. We present and study an explicit exponential integrator for parabolic SPDEs in any dimension driven by a Gaussian noise which is white in time and with spatial correlation given by a Riesz kernel. Under assumptions on the coefficients of the SPDE, we prove strong error bounds and exhibit how the rate of convergence depends on the exponent in the Riesz kernel. Finally, numerical experiments in spatial dimensions 1 1 and 2 2 are provided in order to confirm our convergence results.

1.
Introduction

The analysis of convergence of numerical schemes for stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs)
started with the seminal paper
[
31
]
. In this reference, the authors showed the convergence in
probability of an implicit numerical scheme to the solution of a stochastic heat equation with a nonlinear term
and driven by additive space-time white noise defined on the interval
[0,1]
. The literature on (strong) convergence
of numerical schemes to solutions of SPDEs, in particular of the parabolic type, in dimension
1
is
now well established, see e.g
[
49
,
32
,
54
,
28
,
38
,
35
,
44
,
2
,
62
,
5
,

,
50
,
51
,
59
,
61
,
48
,
23
,
8
,
10
,
30
,
53
,
7
,
29
,
16
,
57
]
and references therein.

Moving beyond the one dimensional case and focusing on the random field setting, convergence results of numerical methods for SPDEs in dimension larger than
1
are currently lacking. Without being exhaustive, we refer the interested reader to
[
13
,
51
,
63
]
. Indeed, the results presented in our paper are closely related to those obtained in
[
51
]
. More precisely, in the latter reference the authors study the speed of convergence of explicit and implicit (semi-) discretization schemes for the following semilinear stochastic heat equation driven by Riesz noise with parameter
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge d)
, where
d\geq 1
is the space dimension:

(1)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+b(t,x,u(t,x))+\sigma(t,x,u(t,x))\dot{F_{\alpha}}(t,x),\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for $x\in(0,1)^{d}$},\end{cases}

with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. Here,
\Delta
is the Laplacian operator on
[0,1]^{d}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions,
\dot{F_{\alpha}}
denotes a Gaussian noise which is white in time and has a spatial correlation given by a Riesz kernel (see Section
2.2
for the precise definition),
u_{0}
is a continuous function on
[0,1]^{d}
and the functions
b,\sigma
satisfy some conditions (see Section
2.4
).

Our main objective is to analyze the rate of strong convergence of an explicit stochastic exponential integrator for the SPDE (
1
).
The main contributions of the present work are twofold:

First, we prove that the strong rate of convergence of the stochastic exponential integrator is
(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})^{-}
(see Theorem
8
for the precise statement). This rate of convergence is optimal, in the sense that it coincides with the time regularity of the solution
u
of equation (
1
) (see Proposition
7
). Moreover, the obtained rate of convergence coincides with those of the explicit and implicit Euler–Maruyama schemes considered in
[
51
, Thm. 3.4. (ii) and (iii)]
. On top of that,
our error estimates are uniform with respect to time and space (see (
35
)) and require only the minimal assumption of the initial condition
u_{0}
being a continuous function. We also point out that the considered stochastic exponential integrator provides two distinct benefits over the numerical schemes studied in
[
51
]
: it avoids the CFL-related step-size restriction of the explicit Euler–Maruyama scheme and it has advantages in implementation over the implicit Euler–Maruyama scheme (see Section
5
for numerical illustrations).

The second important contribution of the present paper is that we numerically confirm the theoretically derived strong convergence rates in dimensions
d=1
and
d=2
(see Section
5
). To the best of our knowledge, no previous references in the literature have shown such numerical results in dimension
2
in the random field setting.

Before elaborating a bit more on the above described contributions, let us mention that
exponential integrators have a long history in the numerical analysis of deterministic ordinary and partial differential equations,
see the review
[
36

The proof of our main result (Theorem
8
) on the strong rate of convergence for the stochastic exponential integrator is carried out using two important ingredients. On the one hand, we apply well-known estimates for stochastic integrals with respect to our Riesz noise. Here, we adapt the theory developed by Dalang in the seminal paper

to our setting, in which we consider our SPDE in the bounded domain

2.1.
Notation

The set of positive integers is denoted by
{\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
. Let
Q=(0,1)^{d}
and
\overline{Q}=[0,1]^{d}
, where
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
is the spatial dimension. The Euclidean norm in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is denoted by
|\cdot|
. Let
\mathcal{B}(Q)
denote the set of Borel subsets of
Q
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{Q})
denote the space of real-valued continuous functions on
\overline{Q}
. For any
v\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{Q})
, set

\|v\|_{\infty}=\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\max}~|v(x)|.

Moreover, let

\mathcal{C}_{0}^{0}(\overline{Q})=\{v\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{Q});\,v(x)=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~x\in\partial Q\}.

Let
L^{2}(Q)
denote the space of square-integrable real-valued functions on
Q
. In addition, let
\mathcal{D}(Q)
denote the space of real-valued smooth functions defined on
Q
.

In this article, we consider the Laplace operator
\Delta
on the
d
-dimensional domain
Q
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\partial Q=\overline{Q}\setminus Q
. It is well-known that this operator can be diagonalized. For all
{\underline{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
, define
|{\underline{k}}|^{2}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}|k_{j}|^{2}
. For all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, set
\varphi_{j}(\cdot)=\sqrt{2}\sin(j\pi\cdot)
. For all
{\underline{k}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
, define

\varphi_{{\underline{k}}}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\varphi_{k_{j}}(x_{j}),\hskip 18.49988pt\forall~x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\overline{Q}.

Then
\bigl(\varphi_{{\underline{k}}}\bigr)_{{\underline{k}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}
is a complete orthonormal system of
L^{2}(Q)
. Moreover, for any
{\underline{k}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
, the function
\varphi_{{\underline{k}}}
is an eigenfunction of the operator
\Delta
, with associated eigenvalue
-|{\underline{k}}|^{2}\pi^{2}
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space. The expectation operator is denoted by
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
.
For any
p\in[1,\infty)
and any real-valued random variable
u
, we define

\vvvert u\vvvert_{p}=\bigl({\mathbb{E}}[|u|^{p}]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\in[0,\infty]
. Let
L^{p}(\Omega)
denote the space of real-valued random variables such that
\vvvert u\vvvert_{p}<\infty
.

In the sequel,
C
denotes a constant that may vary from line to line. Subscripts on
C
may be employed to indicate dependence with respect to parameters.

2.2.
Riesz noise

In this subsection, we introduce the Riesz noise
F_{\alpha}
, depending on the parameter
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge d)
,
and we provide the main tools to define stochastic integrals with respect to the noise
F_{\alpha}
.

For all
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge d)
, define the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha}
and the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
and
\|\cdot\|_{(\alpha)}
as follows: for any smooth functions
\phi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathcal{D}(Q)
, set

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q\times Q}\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{2}(y)|x-y|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q\times Q}\phi(x)\phi(y)|x-y|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q\times Q}|\phi(x)||\phi(y)||x-y|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Let us introduce the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
as the completion of the space
\mathcal{D}(Q)
with respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha}
and the associated norm
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
.
Any function
g:\overline{Q}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}_{(\alpha)}<\infty
belongs to

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
(see, e.g.,
[
25
, Prop. 2.6(a)]
).

Next, define the Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}=L^{2}([0,\infty);\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})
with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}
defined as follows: for all
\varphi,\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}
, set

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle\varphi_{1}(t,\cdot),\varphi_{2}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\varphi(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Given the parameter
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge d)
, the Riesz noise
F_{\alpha}
is defined as a family
F_{\alpha}=\{F_{\alpha}(\varphi);\,\varphi\in\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}\}
of centered Gaussian random variables, characterized by the following covariance structure: for all
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}
, one has

{\mathbb{E}}\left[F_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1})F_{\alpha}(\varphi_{2})\right]=\langle\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}.

For any time
s\geq 0
and any Borel set
A\in\mathcal{B}(Q)
, the function
\varphi_{s,A}:(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times Q\mapsto\mathds{1}_{[0,s]}(t)\mathds{1}_{A}(x)
belongs to
\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}
. Set

F_{\alpha}\left([0,s]\times A\right)=F_{\alpha}\left(\varphi_{s,A}\right).

For any
t\geq 0
, we define
\mathcal{F}_{t}
as the
\sigma
-algebra

\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\left(\left\{F_{\alpha}([0,s]\times A);\,0\leq s\leq t,A\in\mathcal{B}(Q)\right\}\right).

In the sequel, we consider predictable processes with respect to the filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

The stochastic integral with respect to the noise
F_{\alpha}
is understood in the Walsh–Dalang sense
(see e.g.
[
56
, Chapter 2]
,
[
26
, Chapter 2]
or
[
27
,
25
]
).
Namely, if
\{X(s,y),\,s\geq 0,y\in\overline{Q}\}
is any predictable random field
satisfying that
{\mathbb{E}}[\|X\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}]<\infty
, then the stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{Q}X(s,y)F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)
is a well-defined centered random variable, which satisfies the following isometry property:

(2)

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{Q}X(s,y)F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\right|^{2}\right]={\mathbb{E}}[\|X\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}].

If
X
, in addition, is such that
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\|X(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{(\alpha)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right]<\infty
, then one has

(3)

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{Q}X(s,y)F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\right|^{2}\right]\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\|X(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{(\alpha)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right].

For all
\varphi\in\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}
, one has

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{Q}\varphi(s,y)F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)=F_{\alpha}(\varphi).

Finally, let us recall the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality, see e.g.
[
40
, Appendix B]
: for all
p\geq 1
and
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
C_{p}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that for any predictable process
X
such that
\int_{0}^{T}\|X(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{(\alpha)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, the stochastic integral satisfies
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{Q}X(s,y)F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\in L^{2p}(\Omega)
, and one has

(4)

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{Q}X(s,y)F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\right|^{2p}\right]\leq C_{p}(T){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|X(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{(\alpha)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p}\right],

which can be rewritten as

\vvvert\int_{0}^{T}\int_{Q}X(s,y)F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\vvvert_{2p}^{2p}\leq C_{p}(T)\vvvert\int_{0}^{T}\|X(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{(\alpha)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\vvvert_{p}^{p}.

Remark 1
.

The Riesz noise
F_{\alpha}
is indeed well-defined for all
\alpha\in(0,d)
. The restriction
\alpha<2
is due to the fact that we want the following stochastic convolution to be well-defined, for all
T>0
:

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-s,x,y)F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y),\;(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q},

where
G_{d}
is the heat kernel introduced in the next Section
2.3
. More precisely, condition
\alpha<2
is used to prove that
the function
(s,y)\in[0,t]\times\overline{O}\mapsto G_{d}(t-s,x,y)
belongs to
\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}
(see (
12
) in Lemma
4
). This is important in order to define the mild solution to equation (
1
) (see (
3
)).

2.3.
Heat kernel

In this subsection, we introduce the heat kernel
G_{d}
on
Q
with Dirichlet homogeneous boundary conditions, and provide several of its fundamental properties. For all
t\in(0,\infty)
and all
x,y\in\overline{Q}
, set

G_{d}(t,x,y)=\sum_{{\underline{k}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}\exp(-|{\underline{k}}|^{2}\pi^{2}t)\varphi_{{\underline{k}}}(x)\varphi_{{\underline{k}}}(y),

where for all
{\underline{k}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
,
|{\underline{k}}|^{2}
and
\varphi_{{\underline{k}}}
are defined in Subsection
2.1
.

Let us first recall standard properties of the heat kernel
G_{d}
. First, one has

(5)

G_{d}(t,x,y)\geq 0,\hskip 18.49988pt\forall t\in(0,\infty),~x,y\in\overline{Q}.

Moreover, for all
t\in(0,\infty)
and
x\in\overline{Q}
, one has

(6)

\int_{Q}G_{d}(t,x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\in[0,1].

Next, let us provide estimates for the heat kernel and its first order spatial and temporal derivatives.

Lemma 2
.

The heat kernel
G_{d}
, its spatial gradient
\nabla_{x}G_{d}
and its temporal derivative
\partial_{t}G_{d}
satisfy the following upper bounds:
there exist
c_{d},C_{d}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
(t,x,y)\in(0,\infty)\times\overline{Q}\times\overline{Q}
, one has

(7)

\displaystyle G_{d}(t,x,y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{t}},

(8)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}G_{d}(t,x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{t}},

(9)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}G_{d}(t,x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}t^{-\frac{d+2}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{t}}.

We refer for instance to
[
45
, page
274
in Chapter 4]
for a proof of Lemma
2
.
Combining (
7
) and (
9
) yields the following inequality.

Lemma 3
.

There exists
C_{d}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that
t_{1}<t_{2}
, and all
x,y\in\overline{Q}
, one has

(10)

\displaystyle\big|G_{d}(t_{2},x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1},x,y)\big|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\mathds{1}_{\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{t_{1}}<1}\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{t_{1}}t_{1}^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2t_{1}}}

\displaystyle+C_{d}\mathds{1}_{\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{t_{1}}\geq 1}\Bigl(t_{1}^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2t_{1}}}+t_{2}^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2t_{2}}}\Bigr).

In addition, for all
\gamma\in[0,1]
, for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that
t_{1}<t_{2}
, and all
x,y\in\overline{Q}
, one has

(11)

\big|G_{d}(t_{2},x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1},x,y)\big|\leq C_{d}\frac{|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}}{t_{1}^{\gamma}}\Bigl(t_{1}^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2t_{1}}}+t_{2}^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2t_{2}}}\Bigr).

Let us state additional properties of the heat kernel
G_{d}
, which are not standard since they are concerned with the norms
\|\cdot\|_{(\alpha)}
. Some of the estimates below can be found in
[
51
, Appendix A]
. For the sake of completeness, we add the whole proof of the lemma below in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge d)
.
There exists
C_{\alpha}\in(0,\infty)
such that the following inequalities hold:

(12)

\displaystyle\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\|G_{d}(t,x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\leq C_{\alpha}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~t\in(0,\infty),

(13)

\displaystyle\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|G_{d}(t_{2}-t,x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{\alpha}(T)|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~t_{2}\geq t_{1}\geq 0,

(14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|G_{d}(t,x_{2},\cdot)-G_{d}(t,x_{1},\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}dt\leq C_{\alpha}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{2-\alpha},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~x_{1},x_{2}\in\overline{Q}.

Moreover, for all
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
, there exists
C_{\alpha,\gamma}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(15)

\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|G_{d}(t_{2}-t,x,\cdot)-G_{d}(t_{1}-t,x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{\alpha,\gamma}t_{1}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}-2\gamma}|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{2\gamma},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~t_{2}\geq t_{1}\geq 0.

Let us finally provide a technical lemma on estimates for stochastic integrals of the heat kernel
G_{d}
with respect to the noise
F_{\alpha}
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge d)
,
T\in(0,\infty)
, and
p\in[1,\infty)
. There exists
C_{p,\alpha}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that the following holds: if
\kappa\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a mapping such that
\kappa(s)\leq s
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and if
\{X(s,x),\,(s,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}\}
is any square-integrable predictable process taking values in
\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}
, then one has the upper bounds

\displaystyle\underset{0\leq t\leq T}{\sup}~\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa(s),x,y)X(s,y)F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\vvvert_{2p}

(16)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\alpha}(T)\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-\kappa(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert X(s,x)\vvvert_{2p}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(17)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\alpha}(T)\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-\kappa(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|X(s,x)|^{2p}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

(18)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\alpha}(T)\underset{0\leq t\leq T}{\sup}~\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert X(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}.

The proofs of Lemma
3
, of Lemma
4
and of Lemma
5
are postponed to Appendix
A
.

2.4.
Regularity assumptions

We conclude this section by giving the assumptions on the initial value
u_{0}
and on the coefficients
b
and
\sigma
which appear in
the stochastic evolution equation.

Assumption 1
.

The initial value
u_{0}
is a function such that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}_{0}(\overline{Q})
. In other words, it is assumed that
u_{0}\colon\overline{Q}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous, and that
u_{0}(x)=0
for all
x\in\partial Q=\overline{Q}\setminus Q
.

Note that in this article the initial value
u_{0}
is assumed to be deterministic. Considering
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable random initial values, independent of the Riesz noise
F_{\alpha}
, would be possible up to minor standard modifications.

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
denote a fixed time. The mappings
b
and
\sigma
are assumed to satisfy the following conditions.

Assumption 2
.

The mappings
b\colon[0,T]\times\overline{Q}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
\sigma\colon[0,T]\times\overline{Q}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfy the following assumptions: there exist
C\in(0,\infty)
such that,
for all
s,t\in[0,T]
,
x,y\in\overline{Q}
,
u,v\in{\mathbb{R}}
, one has

(L)

\displaystyle|b(s,x,u)-b(t,y,v)|+|\sigma(s,x,u)-\sigma(t,y,v)|\leq C\bigl(|s-t|^{1/2-\alpha/4}+|x-y|^{1-\alpha/2}+|u-v|\bigr),

and such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,
x\in\overline{Q}
,
u\in{\mathbb{R}}
, one has

(LG)

\displaystyle|b(t,x,u)|+|\sigma(t,x,u)|\leq C(1+|u|).

Observe that the condition (
L
) means that the two mappings
u\in{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto b(t,x,u)
and
u\in{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto\sigma(t,x,u)
are globally Lipschitz continuous, uniformly with respect to
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\overline{Q}
. Moreover, the temporal and spatial Hölder regularity exponents

1/2-\alpha/4
and
1-\alpha/2
in the condition (
L
) are related to the optimal temporal and spatial regularity properties of the solutions, see Section
3
and e.g.
[
51
, Prop. 3.2]
.

3.
Well-posedness and properties of the SPDE driven by Riesz noise

The objective of this section is to study the stochastic partial differential equation (
1
) driven by Riesz noise in dimension
d
, employing the tools presented in Section
2
. Proposition
6
provides existence and uniqueness of a mild solution as well as moment bounds. Proposition
7
provides a temporal regularity result.

We consider the stochastic partial differential equation

(19)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+b(t,x,u(t,x))+\sigma(t,x,u(t,x))\frac{\partial^{2}F_{\alpha}}{\partial t\partial x}(t,x),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~t>0,x\in Q,\\
&u(t,x)=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~x\in\partial Q,\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~x\in\overline{Q},\end{aligned}\right.

where the initial value
u_{0}
satisfies Assumption
1
and the mappings
b
and
\sigma
satisfy Assumption
2
. The formulation (
19
) is formal. We recall that a mild solution to (
19
) is an adapted process
\{u(t,x),\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}\}
which satisfies the following identity: almost surely, for any
(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\overline{Q}
, one has

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}G_{d}(t,x,y)u_{0}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-s,x,y)b(s,y,u(s,y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

(20)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-s,x,y)\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y).

The stochastic integral with respect to the Riesz noise
F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)
appearing in (
3
) is understood in the Walsh–Dalang sense, as presented in Subsection
2.2
.

Let us state properties of mild solutions of the parabolic SPDE (
19
).

Proposition 6
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
be satisfied. For all
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists a unique global mild solution
\{u(t,x),\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}\}
to (
19
).

In addition, the mild solution satisfies the following moment bounds: for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and all
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
C_{p}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that one has

(21)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u(t,x)|^{2p}]\leq C_{p}(T)\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2p}\bigr).

Proof.

Existence of the unique mild solution
u(t,x)
to the parabolic SPDE (
19
) can be found in, for example,

[
27
,
12
,
47
,
26
]
.

The moment bounds of
u(t,x)
are established using standard arguments. A detailed proof is given for completeness.

For all
t\in[0,T]
and all
x\in Q
, one has

(22)

{\mathbb{E}}[|u(t,x)|^{2p}]\leq 3^{2p-1}\Bigl(M_{p}^{1}(t,x)+M_{p}^{2}(t,x)+M_{p}^{3}(t,x)\Bigr),

where
M_{p}^{1}(t,x)
,
M_{p}^{2}(t,x)
and
M_{p}^{3}(t,x)
are defined as

\displaystyle M_{p}^{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{Q}G_{d}(t,x,y)u_{0}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right|^{2p}

\displaystyle M_{p}^{2}(t,x)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-s,x,y)b(s,y,u(s,y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle M_{p}^{3}(t,x)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-s,x,y)\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\right|^{2p}\right].

First, owing to the properties (
5
) and (
6
) of the heat kernel
G_{d}
(see Subsection
2.3
), one has

\left|\int_{Q}G_{d}(t,x,y)u_{0}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq\int_{Q}G_{d}(t,x,y)|u_{0}(y)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\underset{y\in Q}{\sup}~|u_{0}(y)|\int_{Q}G_{d}(t,x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}.

As a result, one obtains

(23)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~M_{p}^{1}(t,x)\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2p}.

Let us then consider
M_{p}^{2}(t,x)
. Applying the Hölder inequality and the properties (
5
) and (
6
) of the heat kernel
G_{d}
(see Subsection
2.3
), one obtains

\displaystyle M_{p}^{2}(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-s,x,y){\mathbb{E}}[|b(s,y,u(s,y))|^{2p}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-s,x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|b(s,y,u(s,y))|^{2p}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s~t^{2p-1}.

Owing to (
LG
), the mapping
b
has at most linear growth, therefore one has

\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|b(s,y,u(s,y))|^{2p}]\leq C_{p}\bigl(1+\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u(s,y)|^{2p}]\bigr).

As a result, one obtains

(24)

M_{p}^{2}(t,x)\leq C_{p}(T)\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(1+\underset{{y}\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u(s,y)|^{2p}]\bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Finally, let us consider
M_{p}^{3}(t,x)
. Applying the inequality (
17
) from Lemma
5
(with
\kappa(s)=s
for all
s\geq 0
), one obtains

M_{p}^{3}(t,x)\leq C_{p,\alpha}(T)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\underset{{y}\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{2p}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Owing to (
LG
), the mapping
\sigma
has at most linear growth, therefore one has

\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{2p}]\leq C_{p}\bigl(1+\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u(s,y)|^{2p}]\bigr).

Hence, one gets that

(25)

M_{p}^{3}(t,x)\leq C_{p,\alpha}(T)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\bigl(1+\underset{{y}\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u(s,y)|^{2p}]\bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Combining the upper bounds (
23
), (
24
) and (
25
), one obtains the following inequality: there exists
C_{p,\alpha}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
one has

\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u(t,x)|^{2p}]\leq C_{p,\alpha}(T)\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2p}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+(t-s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)\bigl(1+\underset{{y}\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u(s,y)|^{2p}]\bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right).

Applying a Grönwall inequality (e.g.,
[
27
, Lem. 15]
) then provides the moment bounds (
21
).

This concludes the proof of Proposition
6
.
∎

Let us now state and prove a result on temporal Hölder regularity of the mild solution (
3
) to (
19
). The version presented in Proposition
7
has not previously been obtained in the literature, to the best of our knowledge.

Proposition 7
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
be satisfied. Let
T\in(0,\infty)
and
\{u(t,x),\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}\}
be the unique mild solution to (
19
).
For all
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge d)
,
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
, there exists
C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
0<t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T
one has

(26)

\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\bigl({\mathbb{E}}[|u(t_{2},x)-u(t_{1},x)|^{p}]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)\frac{|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}}{t_{1}^{\gamma}}(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}).

Proof.

Assume that
0<t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T
and let
x\in Q
, then one has the decomposition

u(t_{2},x)-u(t_{1},x)=\delta^{1}(t_{1},t_{2},x)+\delta^{2}(t_{1},t_{2},x)+\delta^{3}(t_{1},t_{2},x)+\delta^{4}(t_{1},t_{2},x)+\delta^{5}(t_{1},t_{2},x),

where the error terms
\delta^{j}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,5\}
are given by

\displaystyle\delta^{1}(t_{1},t_{2},x)

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}\left[G_{d}(t_{2},x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1},x,y)\right]u_{0}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\delta^{2}(t_{1},t_{2},x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{Q}\left[G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\right]b(s,y,u(s,y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\delta^{3}(t_{1},t_{2},x)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)b(s,y,u(s,y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\delta^{4}(t_{1},t_{2},x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{Q}\left[G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\right]\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\delta^{5}(t_{1},t_{2},x)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y).

Let us consider the term
\delta^{1}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
. Assume that
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Applying the inequality (
11
) on the heat kernel (see Subsection
2.3
), one obtains the following upper bounds:

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{Q}\left[G_{d}(t_{2},x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1},x,y)\right]u_{0}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q}\big|G_{d}(t_{2},x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1},x,y)\big||u_{0}(y)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~|u_{0}(y)|\int_{Q}\big|G_{d}(t_{2},x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1},x,y)\big|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}}{t_{1}^{\gamma}}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}.

Therefore one obtains the following upper bound for
\delta^{1}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
: for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
, there exists
C_{\gamma}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(27)

\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~|\delta^{1}(t_{1},t_{2},x)|\leq C_{\gamma}\frac{|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}}{t_{1}^{\gamma}}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~0<t_{1}<t_{2}.

Let us then consider the term
\delta^{2}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
. Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. Applying the Minkowski inequality, one has

\displaystyle\vvvert\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{Q}

\displaystyle\left[G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\right]b(s,y,u(s,y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\vvvert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{Q}\big|G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\big|\vvvert b(s,y,u(s,y))\vvvert_{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert b(s,y,u(s,y))\vvvert_{p}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{Q}\big|G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\big|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Owing to the condition (
LG
) from Assumption
2
, the mapping
b
has at most linear growth. Therefore, applying the moment bounds (
21
) from Proposition (
6
), one obtains the upper bound

\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert b(s,y,u(s,y))\vvvert_{p}\leq C\bigl(1+\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)\vvvert_{p}\bigr)\leq C_{p}(T)\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}\bigr).

Applying the inequality (
11
) on the heat kernel (see Subsection
2.3
), one obtains

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{Q}\big|G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\big|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\frac{|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}}{(t_{1}-s)^{\gamma}}\int_{Q}(t_{1}-s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2(t_{1}-s)}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\frac{|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}}{(t_{1}-s)^{\gamma}}\int_{Q}(t_{2}-s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2(t_{2}-s)}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{Q}\frac{|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}}{(t_{1}-s)^{\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma}(T)|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}.

Therefore one obtains the following upper bound for
\delta^{2}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
: for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, there exists
C_{p,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that

(28)

\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert\delta^{2}(t_{1},t_{2},x)\vvvert_{p}\leq C_{p,\gamma}(T)|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}\bigr),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~0<t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T.

The treatment of the term
\delta^{3}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
is similar: applying the Minkowski inequality and the property (
6
) of the heat kernel (see Subsection
2.3
), one has

\displaystyle\vvvert\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)b(s,y,u(s,y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\vvvert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)\vvvert b(s,y,u(s,y))\vvvert_{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert b(s,y,u(s,y))\vvvert_{p}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(T)\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}\bigr)|t_{2}-t_{1}|.

Therefore one obtains the following upper bound for
\delta^{3}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
: for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, there exists
C_{p,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that

(29)

\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert\delta^{3}(t_{1},t_{2},x)\vvvert_{p}\leq C_{p,\gamma}(T)|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}\bigr),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~0<t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T.

It remains to deal with the terms
\delta^{4}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
and
\delta^{5}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
. Applying the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality (
4
), one obtains the upper bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|\delta^{4}(t_{1},t_{2},x)|^{2p}]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{Q}\left[G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\right]\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\right|^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(T){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|\left[G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,\cdot)-G_{d}(t_{1}-s,x,\cdot)\right]\sigma(s,\cdot,u(s,\cdot))\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\right|^{p}\right].

For all
(t_{1},t_{2},x)\in[0,T]^{2}\times\overline{Q}
(with
t_{1}<t_{2}
), introduce the auxiliary positive measure
\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(1)}
on
[0,t_{1}]\times Q^{2}
, which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, and with Radon–Nikodym derivative given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(1)}(s,y,z)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}=\left|G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\right|\left|G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,z)-G_{d}(t_{1}-s,x,z)\right||y-z|^{-\alpha}.

Making use of the auxiliary measure
\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(1)}
introduced above yields the upper bound

{\mathbb{E}}[|\delta^{4}(t_{1},t_{2},x)|^{2p}]\leq C_{p}(T){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\iint_{Q\times Q}|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))||\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(1)}(s,y,z)\right|^{p}\right].

Applying the Hölder inequality, one then obtains

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|\delta^{4}(t_{1},t_{2},x)|^{2p}]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\iint_{Q\times Q}{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{p}|\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|^{p}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(1)}(s,y,z)~\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(1)}([0,t_{1}]\times Q^{2})^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y,z\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{p}|\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|^{p}]~\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(1)}([0,t_{1}]\times Q^{2})^{p}.

Owing to the condition (
LG
) from Assumption
2
, the mapping
\sigma
has at most linear growth. Therefore, applying the moment bounds (
21
) from Proposition (
6
), one obtains the upper bounds

\displaystyle\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y,z\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{p}|\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|^{p}]

\displaystyle\leq\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{2p}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(T)\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|^{2p}_{\infty}\bigr).

Moreover, applying the inequality (
15
) from Lemma
4
, with the condition
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
, if
0<t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T
, one obtains the upper bound

\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(1)}([0,t_{1}]\times Q^{2})=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,\cdot)-G_{d}(t_{1}-s,x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq C_{\alpha,\mu}(T)|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{2\gamma}.

Gathering the upper bounds, one obtains the following upper bound for
\delta^{4}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
: for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
, there exists
C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that

(30)

\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert\delta^{4}(t_{1},t_{2},x)\vvvert_{2p}\leq C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}\bigr),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~0<t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T.

Finally, the treatment of the term
\delta^{5}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
is similar to the treatment of
\delta^{4}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
above. Applying the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality (
4
), one obtains the upper bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|\delta^{5}(t_{1},t_{2},x)|^{2p}]=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\right|^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(T){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,\cdot)\sigma(s,\cdot,u(s,\cdot))\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\right|^{p}\right].

For all
(t_{1},t_{2},x)\in[0,T]^{2}\times\overline{Q}
(with
t_{1}<t_{2}
), we introduce the auxiliary positive measure
\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(2)}
on
[t_{1},t_{2}]\times Q^{2}
, which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, and with Radon–Nikodym derivative given by

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(2)}(s,y,z)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}=G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,y)G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,z)|y-z|^{-\alpha}.

Making use of the auxiliary measure
\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(2)}
introduced above yields the upper bound

{\mathbb{E}}[|\delta^{5}(t_{1},t_{2},x)|^{2p}]\leq C_{p}(T){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\iint_{Q\times Q}|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))||\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(2)}(s,y,z)\right|^{p}\right].

Applying the Hölder inequality, one then obtains

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\iint_{Q\times Q}|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))||\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(2)}(s,y,z)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\iint_{Q\times Q}{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{p}|\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|^{p}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(2)}(s,y,z)~\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(2)}([t_{1},t_{2}]\times Q^{2})^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y,z\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{p}|\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|^{p}]~\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(2)}([t_{1},t_{2}]\times Q^{2})^{p}.

Owing to the condition (
LG
) from Assumption
2
, the mapping
\sigma
has at most linear growth. Therefore, applying the moment bounds (
21
) from Proposition (
6
), one obtains the upper bounds

\displaystyle\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y,z\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{p}|\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|^{p}]

\displaystyle\leq\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{2p}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(T)\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2p}\bigr).

Moreover, applying the inequality (
15
) from Lemma
4
, if
0<t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T
, one obtains the upper bound

\nu_{t_{1},t_{2},x}^{(2)}([t_{1},t_{2}]\times Q^{2})=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|G_{d}(t_{2}-s,x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq C_{\alpha}(T)|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Gathering the upper bounds, and taking into account the condition
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
, one obtains the following upper bound for
\delta^{5}(t_{1},t_{2},x)
: for all
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, there exists
C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that

(31)

\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert\delta^{5}(t_{1},t_{2},x)\vvvert_{2p}\leq C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma}\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}\bigr),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~0<t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T.

Combining the upper bounds (
27
), (
28
), (
29
),
(
30
) and (
31
) then provides the inequality (
26
). The proof of Proposition
7
is thus completed.
∎

4.
Convergence analysis of the stochastic exponential integrator

In this section, we introduce the stochastic exponential integrator given by (
32
) and (
34
) applied to the stochastic partial differential equation (
19
). We then state Theorem
8
, which is the main result of this article, and provides strong convergence of the integrator when the time-step size
\tau
goes to
0
, with rate
\gamma
arbitrarily close to
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
and given an integer
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
define the time-step size
\tau=\frac{T}{m}
. For all
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
, introduce the grid times
t_{\ell}=\ell\tau
. In addition, let
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denote the integer part, and for all
s\in[0,T]
set

\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)=\tau\left\lfloor\frac{s}{\tau}\right\rfloor=\frac{T}{m}\left\lfloor\frac{ms}{T}\right\rfloor=\sup~\{t_{\ell};~t_{\ell}\leq s\}.

Let us first start by giving a continuous-time mild formulation of the stochastic exponential integrator which is inspired
by
[
2
]
which treats the case of one dimensional SPDE driven by space-time white noise. The random field
\{u^{m}(t,x),\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}\}
is defined as the solution to the following system: for all
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\overline{Q}
,

(32)

\displaystyle u^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}G_{d}(t,x,y)u_{0}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)b(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u^{m}(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\sigma(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u^{m}(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y))\,F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y).

The formulation (
32
) of the stochastic exponential integrator is mainly useful for the theoretical
analysis of the scheme.
For its practical implementation, it is sufficient to consider approximations
\mathcal{U}^{m}_{\ell}=u^{m}(t_{\ell},\cdot)
at the grid times
t_{\ell}
. From the definition (
32
) of the integrator and the semigroup property of the heat kernel
G_{d}
, for all
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
and all
x\in\overline{Q}
, one has

(33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\ell+1}^{m}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}G_{d}(\tau,x,y)\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{m}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\tau\int_{Q}G_{d}(\tau,x,y)b(t_{\ell},y,\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{m}(y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\int_{Q}G_{d}(\tau,x,y)\sigma(t_{\ell},y,\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{m}(y))\,F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y).

Let the operator
e^{\tau\Delta}
be defined such that for all
v\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{0}(\overline{Q})
one has

e^{\tau\Delta}v(x)=\int_{Q}G_{d}(\tau,x,y)v(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~x\in\overline{Q}.

In addition, define Gaussian random variables
\bigl(\delta_{\ell}F_{\alpha}\bigr)_{0\leq\ell\leq m-1}
by

\delta_{\ell}F_{\alpha}(\varphi)=F_{\alpha}\bigl(\mathds{1}_{[t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1})}\otimes\varphi\bigr)=\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t_{\ell+1}}\int_{Q}\varphi(y)\,F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}.

Note that if
\ell_{1}\neq\ell_{2}
then the Gaussian random variables
\delta_{\ell_{1}}F_{\alpha}
and
\delta_{\ell_{2}}F_{\alpha}
are independent. Moreover, for all
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
,
\delta_{\ell}F_{\alpha}
is a centered Gaussian random variable, with covariance structure given by

{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\delta_{\ell}F_{\alpha}(\varphi)\delta_{\ell}F_{\alpha}(\psi)\bigr]=\tau\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\alpha}=\tau\iint_{Q\times Q}\varphi(x)\psi(y)|x-y|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,\hskip 18.49988pt\forall~\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}.

Employing the notation introduced above, the stochastic exponential integrator is written as

(34)

\mathcal{U}^{m}_{\ell+1}=\mathrm{e}^{\tau\Delta}\left(\mathcal{U}^{m}_{\ell}+\tau b(t_{\ell},\cdot,\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{m})+\sigma(t_{\ell},\cdot,\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{m})\delta_{\ell}F_{\alpha}\right),\ \ell=0,\ldots,m-1.

The initial value of the scheme is
\mathcal{U}_{0}^{m}=u_{0}
for any value
\tau=T/m
of the time-step size.

We are now in position to state the main result of this article.

Theorem 8
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
be satisfied. Let
T\in(0,\infty)
. Let
u
denote the unique mild solution given by (
3
) to the stochastic partial differential equation (
19
). For all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
u^{m}
denote the solution to the stochastic exponential integrator (
32
) with time-step size
\tau=T/m
.

For all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
, there exists
C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
one has

(35)

\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[|u^{m}(t,x)-u(t,x)|^{2p}\right]^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C_{p,{\alpha},\gamma}(T)\tau^{\gamma}.

The proof of Theorem
8
is postponed to Section
6
.

Note that one obtains the same rate of convergence, arbitrarily close to
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}
, as for the explicit Euler–Maruyama scheme and the semi-implicit Euler–Maruyama scheme obtained in
[
51
, Theorem 3.4. part (iii)]
. However, in Theorem
8
there is no need to impose regularity conditions on the initial value
u_{0}
other than continuity.

6.
Proof of the main result

This section gives the proof of Theorem
8
.

Proof.

Recall that the random field
u
is the mild solution given by (
3
) to the stochastic partial differential equation (
19
), and that, for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the random field
u^{m}
is the numerical solution given by the stochastic exponential integrator (
32
) with time-step size
\tau=T/m
.

For all
p\in[1,\infty)
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2p}^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\vvvert u(t,x)-u^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}=\bigl({\mathbb{E}}[|u(t,x)-u^{m}(t,x)|^{2p}]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2p}},\quad\forall~(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{E}_{2p}^{m}}(t)

\displaystyle=\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\mathcal{E}_{2p}^{m}(t,x),\quad\forall~t\in[0,T].

We consider the following decomposition of the error
u(t,x)-u^{m}(t,x)
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\overline{Q}
: one has

\displaystyle u(t,x)-u^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=A_{1}^{m}(t,x)+A_{2}^{m}(t,x)+A_{3}^{m}(t,x)+A_{4}^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle+B_{1}^{m}(t,x)+B_{2}^{m}(t,x)+B_{3}^{m}(t,x)+B_{4}^{m}(t,x),

where the error terms
A_{j}^{m}(t,x)
, for
j\in\{1,\ldots,4\}
, are defined as

\displaystyle A_{1}^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}\left[G_{d}(t-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\right]b(s,y,u(s,y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle A_{2}^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\left[b(s,y,u(s,y))-b(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(s,y))\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle A_{3}^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\left[b(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(s,y))-b(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y))\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle A_{4}^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\left[b(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y))-b(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u^{m}(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y))\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

and the error terms
B_{j}^{m}(t,x)
, for
j\in\{1,\ldots,4\}
, are defined as

\displaystyle B_{1}^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}\left[G_{d}(t-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\right]\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y),

\displaystyle B_{2}^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\left[\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))-\sigma(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(s,y))\right]F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y),

\displaystyle B_{3}^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\left[\sigma(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(s,y))-\sigma(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y))\right]F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y),

\displaystyle B_{4}^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\left[\sigma(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y))-\sigma(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u^{m}(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y))\right]F_{\alpha}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y).

For all
j\in\{1,\ldots,4\}
and all
t\in[0,T]
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{j,2p}^{m}(t,x)=\vvvert A_{j}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p},\quad\mathcal{B}_{j,2p}^{m}(t,x)=\vvvert B_{j}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p},\qquad\forall~x\in Q,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{A}_{j,2p}^{m}}(t)=\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\mathcal{A}_{j,2p}^{m}(t,x),\quad\overline{\mathcal{B}_{j,2p}}^{m}(t)=\underset{x\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\mathcal{B}_{j,2p}^{m}(t,x).

Applying the Minkowski inequality, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2p}^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{4}\mathcal{A}_{j,2p}^{m}(t,x)+\sum_{j=1}^{4}\mathcal{B}_{j,2p}^{m}(t,x),\qquad\forall~(t,x)\in[0,T]\times Q,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{2p}^{m}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{4}\overline{\mathcal{A}_{j,2p}^{m}}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{4}\overline{\mathcal{B}_{j,2p}^{m}}(t),\qquad\forall~t\in[0,T].

The proof proceeds by proving upper bounds for each error term
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{j,2p}^{m}}(t)
and
\overline{\mathcal{B}_{j,2p}^{m}}(t)
with
j\in\{1,\ldots,4\}
.

In the sequel, the value of the parameter
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
is fixed.
Note that for all
s\in[0,T]
one has
\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)\leq s
and
|s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)|\leq\tau
.

\bullet
Treatment of the error term
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{1,2p}^{m}}(t)
.

Applying the Minkowski inequality, for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}
, one has

\displaystyle\vvvert A_{1}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}\big|G_{d}(t-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\big|\vvvert b(s,y,u(s,y))\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert b(s,y,u(s,y))\vvvert_{2p}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}\big|G_{d}(t-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\big|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

On the one hand, owing to the condition (
LG
) from Assumption
2
, the mapping
b
has at most linear growth. Applying the moment bounds (
21
) from Proposition
6
gives the upper bounds

\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert b(s,y,u(s,y))\vvvert_{2p}\leq C\bigl(1+\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)\vvvert_{2p}\bigr)\leq C_{p}(T)(1+\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}).

On the other hand, applying the inequality (
11
) on the heat kernel (see Subsection
2.3
), one obtains the upper bounds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}\big|G_{d}(t-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\big|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)|^{\gamma}}{(t-s)^{\gamma}}\int_{Q}(t-s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2(t-s)}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)|^{\gamma}}{(t-s)^{\gamma}}\int_{Q}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\tau^{\gamma}}{(t-s)^{\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma}(T)\tau^{\gamma}.

As a result, for the error term
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{1,2p}^{m}}(t)
, one obtains the following upper bound: there exists
C_{p,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that one has

(37)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\overline{\mathcal{A}_{1,2p}^{m}}(t)\leq C_{p,\gamma}(T)(1+\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty})\tau^{\gamma}.

\bullet
Treatment of the error term
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{2,2p}^{m}}(t)
.

Applying the Minkowski inequality, for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}
, one has

\vvvert A_{2}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\vvvert b(s,y,u(s,y))-b(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(s,y))\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Owing to the condition (
L
) from Assumption
2
and to the assumption
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
, the mapping
s\in[0,T]\mapsto b(s,y,u)
satisfies a Hölder continuity property with exponent
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}
, uniformly with respect to
(y,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Therefore, one has

\displaystyle\vvvert A_{2}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)|s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Applying the property (
6
) of the heat kernel
G_{d}
(see Subsection
2.3
), one obtains

\vvvert A_{2}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}\leq CT\tau^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}.

Recall that
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
. As a result, for the error term
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{2,2p}^{m}}(t)
, one obtains the following upper bound: there exists
C_{p,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that one has

(38)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\overline{\mathcal{A}_{2,2p}^{m}}(t)\leq C_{p,\gamma}(T)\tau^{\gamma}.

\bullet
Treatment of the error term
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{3,2p}^{m}}(t)
.

Applying the Minkowski inequality, for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}
, one has

\vvvert A_{3}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\vvvert b(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(s,y))-b(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y))\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Owing to the condition (
L
) from Assumption
2
, the mapping
u\mapsto b(s,y,u)
satisfies a global Lipschitz continuity property. Therefore, applying the property (
6
) of the heat kernel
G_{d}
(see Subsection
2.3
), one has

\displaystyle\vvvert A_{3}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

To proceed, one needs to apply the regularity property (
26
) from Proposition
7
of the solution
u
. However, since
\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)=0
for all
s\in[0,\tau)
, it is not applicable on the time interval
[0,\min(t,\tau))
. The integral is then decomposed as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\min(t,\tau)}\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\mathds{1}_{t>\tau}\int_{\tau}^{t}\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

On the one hand, note that for all
s\in[0,\min(t,\tau))
one has
u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),\cdot)=u_{0}
, thus applying the Minkowski inequality and the moment bounds (
21
) from Proposition
6
, for the first integral one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\min(t,\tau)}\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\min(t,\tau)}\bigl(\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)\vvvert_{2p}+\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\tau\bigl(1+\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\bigr).

On the other hand, applying the regularity property (
26
) from Proposition
7
, for all
t\in(\tau,\infty)
one has

\int_{\tau}^{t}\underset{y\in Q}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq C_{p,\gamma}(T)\bigl(1+\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\bigr)\int_{\tau}^{t}\frac{|s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)|^{\gamma}}{\left(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)\right)^{\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Using the lower bound
\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)\geq s-\tau
, the upper bound
|s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)|\leq\tau
and a change of variables, one obtains the upper bounds

\int_{\tau}^{t}\frac{|s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)|^{\gamma}}{\left(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)\right)^{\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{\tau}^{t}\frac{\tau^{\gamma}}{(s-\tau)^{\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq{\tau^{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{t-\tau}\frac{1}{s^{\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq C_{\gamma}(T)\tau^{\gamma}.

As a result, for the error term
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{3,2p}^{m}}(t)
, one obtains the following upper bound: there exists
C_{p,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that one has

(39)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\overline{\mathcal{A}_{3,2p}^{m}}(t)\leq C_{p,\gamma}(T)\bigl(1+\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\bigr)\tau^{\gamma}.

\bullet
Treatment of the error term
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{4,2p}^{m}}(t)
.

Applying the Minkowski inequality, for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}
, one has

\vvvert A_{4}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\vvvert b(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y))-b(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y,u^{m}(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y))\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Owing to the condition (
L
) from Assumption
2
, the mapping
u\mapsto b(s,y,u)
satisfies a global Lipschitz continuity property. Therefore, recalling the definition of
\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{2p}^{m}(s)
and applying the property (
6
) of the heat kernel
G_{d}
(see Subsection
2.3
), one has

\displaystyle\vvvert A_{4}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\vvvert u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)-u^{m}(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q}G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{2p}^{m}(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{2p}^{m}(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

As a result, for the error term
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{4,2p}^{m}}(t)
, one obtains the following upper bound: there exists
C(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that one has

(40)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\overline{\mathcal{A}_{4,2p}^{m}}(t)\leq C(T)\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{2p}^{m}(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

This concludes the treatment of the error terms
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{j,2p}^{m}}(t)
with
j\in\{1,\ldots,4\}
. It remains to deal with the error terms
\overline{\mathcal{B}_{j,2p}^{m}}(t)
with
j\in\{1,\ldots,4\}
.

\bullet
Treatment of the error term
\overline{\mathcal{B}_{1,2p}^{m}}(t)
.

Applying the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality (
4
), for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}
, one has

{\mathbb{E}}[|B_{1}^{m}(t,x)|^{2p}]\leq C_{p}(T){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\|[G_{d}(t-s,x,\cdot)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,\cdot)]\sigma(s,\cdot,u(s,\cdot))\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|^{p}\right].

For all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{Q}
, introduce the auxiliary positive measure
\nu_{t,x}^{m}
on
[0,t]\times Q^{2}
, which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, and with Radon–Nikodym derivative given by

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{t,x}^{m}(s,y,z)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}=\left|G_{d}(t-s,x,y)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,y)\right|\left|G_{d}(t-s,x,z)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,z)\right||y-z|^{-\alpha}.

Making use of the auxiliary measure
\nu_{t,x}^{m}
introduced above yields the upper bound

{\mathbb{E}}[|B_{1}^{m}(t,x)|^{2p}]\leq C_{p}(T){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{Q\times Q}|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))||\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{t,x}^{m}(s,y,z)\right|^{p}\right].

Applying the Hölder inequality, one then obtains

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|B_{1}^{m}(t,x)|^{2p}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(T)\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{Q\times Q}{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{p}|\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|^{p}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{t,x}^{m}(s,y,z)\nu_{t,x}^{m}([0,t]\times Q^{2})^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(T)\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y,z\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{p}|\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|^{p}]\nu_{t,x}^{m}([0,t]\times Q^{2})^{p}.

Owing to the condition (
LG
) from Assumption
2
, the mapping
\sigma
has at most linear growth. Therefore, applying the moment bounds (
21
) from Proposition
6
, one obtains the upper bounds

\displaystyle\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y,z\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{p}|\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))|^{p}]

\displaystyle\leq\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))|^{2p}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(T)\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2p}\bigr).

To proceed, one needs to prove an upper bound for

\nu_{t,x}^{m}([0,t]\times Q^{2})=\int_{0}^{t}\|G_{d}(t-s,x,\cdot)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

For all
s\in(0,t)
, let
\delta(s)=(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))-(t-s)=s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)
. In order to apply the inequality (
10
) on the heat kernel from Lemma
3
(see Subsection
2.3
), the cases
\delta(s)<t-s
and
\delta(s)\geq t-s
are treated separately.

On the one hand, if
\delta(s)<t-s
, one obtains

\displaystyle\|G_{d}(t-s,x,\cdot)

\displaystyle-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\delta(s)^{2}}{(t-s)^{2}}\iint_{Q\times Q}(t-s)^{-d}e^{-c\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2(t-s)}}e^{-c\frac{|z-x|^{2}}{2(t-s)}}|y-z|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z.

Under the condition
\delta(s)<t-s
, for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
one has

\frac{\delta(s)^{2}}{(t-s)^{2}}\leq\frac{\delta(s)^{2\gamma}}{(t-s)^{2\gamma}}.

Applying changes of variable
y^{\prime}=y-x
and
z^{\prime}=z-x
, and
y^{\prime\prime}=y^{\prime}/\sqrt{t-s}
and
z^{\prime\prime}=z^{\prime}/\sqrt{t-s}
, one has

\displaystyle\iint_{Q\times Q}(t-s)^{-d}

\displaystyle e^{-c\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2(t-s)}}e^{-c\frac{|z-x|^{2}}{2(t-s)}}|y-z|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(t-s)^{-d}e^{-c\frac{|y^{\prime}|^{2}}{2(t-s)}}e^{-c\frac{|z^{\prime}|^{2}}{2(t-s)}}|y^{\prime}-z^{\prime}|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{-c\frac{|y^{\prime\prime}|^{2}}{2}}e^{-c\frac{|z^{\prime\prime}|^{2}}{2}}|y^{\prime\prime}-z^{\prime\prime}|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y^{\prime\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}(t-s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

On the other hand, if
\delta(s)=s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)\geq t-s
, applying the inequality (
12
) from Lemma
4
, one obtains

\displaystyle\|G_{d}(t-s,x,\cdot)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|G_{d}(t-s,x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\|G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}(t-s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}+C_{\alpha}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}(t-s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Moreover, under the condition
\delta(s)\geq t-s
, for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
one has

1\leq\frac{\delta(s)^{2\gamma}}{(t-s)^{2\gamma}}.

As a result, in both cases one obtains the upper bound

\|G_{d}(t-s,x,\cdot)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\leq C_{\alpha}\frac{(s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{2\gamma}}{(t-s)^{2\gamma+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}.

Therefore, taking into account the condition
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4})
, for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times Q
, one has

\displaystyle\nu_{t,x}^{m}([0,t]\times Q^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\|G_{d}(t-s,x,\cdot)-G_{d}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\tau^{2\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-2\gamma-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq C_{\alpha,\gamma}(T)\tau^{2\gamma}.

Finally, for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times Q
, one has the upper bound

\vvvert B_{1}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}^{2p}={\mathbb{E}}[|B_{1}^{m}(t,x)|^{2p}]\leq C_{\alpha,\gamma}(T)\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2p}\bigr)\tau^{2p\gamma}.

As a result, for the error term
\overline{\mathcal{B}_{1,2p}^{m}}(t)
, one obtains the following upper bound: there exists
C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that one has

(41)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\overline{\mathcal{B}_{1,2p}^{m}}(t)\leq C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}\bigr)\tau^{\gamma}.

\bullet
Treatment of the error term
\overline{\mathcal{B}_{2,2p}^{m}}(t)
.

Applying the inequality (
18
) from Lemma
5
(with
\kappa(s)=\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)

As a result, for the error term
\overline{\mathcal{B}_{2,2p}^{m}}(t)
, one obtains the following upper bound: there exists
C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that one has

(42)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\overline{\mathcal{B}_{2,2p}^{m}}(t)\leq C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)\tau^{\gamma}.

\bullet

) from Lemma

Owing to the condition (

\sigma

\vvvert B_{3}^{m}(t,x)\vvvert_{2p}^{2}\leq C_{p,\alpha}(T)\int_{0}^{t}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\min(t,\tau)}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\mathds{1}_{t>\tau}\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\min(t,\tau)}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\min(t,\tau)}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\bigl(\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)\vvvert_{2p}^{2}+\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

To deal with the integral in the right-hand side above, observe that since

On the other hand, applying the regularity property (
26

7

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle\underset{y\in\overline{Q}}{\sup}~\vvvert u(s,y)-u(\kappa_{m}^{T}(s),y)\vvvert_{2p}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\gamma}(T)\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\frac{|s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)|^{2\gamma}}{\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)^{2\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty})^{2}.

The integral in the right-hand side above can be treated as follows: using the inequalities
s\geq\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)
and
\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)\geq s-\tau
, for all
t>\tau
one has

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\frac{|s-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)|^{2\gamma}}{\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)^{2\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2\gamma}\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-\kappa_{m}^{T}(s))^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}^{T}(s)^{-2\gamma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2\gamma}\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(s-\tau)^{-2\gamma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2\gamma}\int_{0}^{t-\tau}(t-\tau-s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}s^{-2\gamma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2\gamma}(t-\tau)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}-2\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s^{\prime})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(s^{\prime})^{-2\gamma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha,\gamma}(T)\tau^{2\gamma}.

As a result, for the error term
\overline{\mathcal{B}_{3,2p}^{m}}(t)
, one obtains the following upper bound: there exists
C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)\in(0,\infty)
such that one has

(43)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\overline{\mathcal{B}_{3,2p}^{m}}(t)\leq C_{p,\alpha,\gamma}(T)\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}\bigr)\tau^{\gamma}.

17
) from Lemma
5
(with

Owing to the condition (

) from Assumption

\sigma

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|B_{4}^{m}(t,x)|^{2p}]

As a result, for the error term

The objective of this section is to provide the proofs of the properties of the heat kernel stated in Subsection

Proof of Lemma
3
.

Let
t_{1}<t_{2}
. The proof of the inequality (
10
) proceeds by considering two cases.

First, assume that
\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{t_{1}}<1
, i. e. that
t_{1}<t_{2}<2t_{1}
.
Applying the inequality (
9
) on the temporal derivative of the heat kernel
G_{d}
, one obtains

|G_{d}(t_{2},x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1},x,y)|\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}|\partial_{t}G_{d}(t,x,y)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{d}(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{t_{2}}}.

Under the condition
t_{2}<2t_{1}
, one obtains the upper bound

|G_{d}(t_{2},x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1},x,y)|\leq C_{d}\frac{(t_{2}-t_{1})}{t_{1}}t_{1}^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2t_{1}}}.

Secondly, assume that
\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{t_{1}}\geq 1
, i. e. that
t_{2}\geq 2t_{1}
. Applying the triangle inequality and the inequality (
7
) from Lemma
2
, one obtains for all
x,y\in\overline{Q}

\displaystyle|G_{d}(t_{2},x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1},x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq G_{d}(t_{2},x,y)+G_{d}(t_{1},x,y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\bigl(t_{1}^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{t_{1}}}+t_{2}^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{t_{2}}}\bigr).

Combining the results above then provides the inequality (
10
).

The inequality (
11
) is a straightforward consequence of the inequality (
10
). Indeed, one first obtains the inequality

|G_{d}(t_{2},x,y)-G_{d}(t_{1},x,y)|\leq C_{d}\min\left(\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{t_{1}},1\right)\left(t_{1}^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2t_{1}}}+t_{2}^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-c_{d}\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2t_{2}}}\right)

and if
\gamma\in(0,1)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
one has
\min(x,1)\leq x^{\gamma}
.

This concludes the proof of Lemma
3
.
∎

Let us now provide the proof of Lemma
4
. The inequalities (

), (

), (

Proof of the inequality (

Let
t\in(0,\infty)
and

. Applying the inequality (
7
), one has

\displaystyle\|G_{d}(t,x,\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q\times Q}G_{d}(t,x,y)G_{d}(t,x,z)|y-z|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-d}\iint_{Q\times Q}e^{-c\frac{|y-x|^{2}+|z-x|^{2}}{t}}|y-z|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{G}_{t,x}^{1}+\mathcal{G}_{t,x}^{2},

where
\mathcal{G}_{t,x}^{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{t,x}^{2}
are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{t,x}^{1}

\displaystyle=Ct^{-d}\iint_{Q\times Q}\mathds{1}_{|x-y|\geq|y-z|}e^{-c\frac{|y-x|^{2}+|z-x|^{2}}{t}}|y-z|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{t,x}^{2}

\displaystyle=Ct^{-d}\iint_{Q\times Q}\mathds{1}_{|x-y|\leq|y-z|}e^{-c\frac{|y-x|^{2}+|z-x|^{2}}{t}}|y-z|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z.

Concerning
\mathcal{G}_{t,x}^{1}
, taking into account the condition
|x-y|\geq|y-z|
and applying the change of variables

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{t,x}^{1}

\displaystyle=Ct^{-d}\iint_{Q\times Q}\mathds{1}_{|x-y|\geq|y-z|}e^{-c\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{t}}e^{-c\frac{|z-x|^{2}}{t}}|y-z|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z

\mathcal{G}_{t,x}^{2}

|x-y|\leq|y-z|
and applying the change of variables
(y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=(y-x,z-x)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{t,x}^{2}

\displaystyle=Ct^{-d}\iint_{Q\times Q}\mathds{1}_{|x-y|\leq|y-z|}e^{-c\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{t}}e^{-c\frac{|z-x|^{2}}{t}}|y-z|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z

The proof of the inequality (
12

Proof of the inequality (
13

Let us now deal with

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{G}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|\delta x|^{2}}^{\infty}\|G_{d}(t,x_{2},\cdot)-G_{d}(t,x_{1},\cdot)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle|G_{d}(t,x_{2},y)-G_{d}(t,x_{1},y)|=|G_{d}(t,x(1),y)-G_{d}(t,x(0),y)|

Appendix B
Implementation details on the covariance matrix of the noise in dimension
d=1

In this appendix, we provide some details on the implementation of the discretization of the noise in Section
5.1
in
d=1
dimension.
We recall that
\alpha\in(0,1)
and that the covariance matrix
C\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-1)\times(n-1)}
has elements given by

C_{ij}=\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i}+\Delta x}\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j}+\Delta x}|x-y|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,\ \text{for}\ i,j=1,\ldots,n-1,

where we set
\Delta x=\frac{1}{n}
for the mesh size. These integrals can explicitly be computed as seen in the next lemma.

Lemma 9
.

For
i<j
, one has

C_{ij}=C_{ji}=\frac{\Delta x^{2-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha)}\left((j-i+1)^{2-\alpha}-2(j-i)^{2-\alpha}+(j-i-1)^{2-\alpha}\right).

Furthermore, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, one has

C_{ii}=\frac{2\Delta x^{2-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha)},\ \text{for}\ i=1,\ldots,n-1.

Proof.

We first consider the case
i<j
. This means that
x_{i}+\Delta x\leq x_{j}
and that
y\leq x
for all
y\in[x_{i},x_{i}+\Delta x]
and for all
x\in[x_{j},x_{j}+\Delta x]
. One then has

\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j}+\Delta x}|x-y|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j}+\Delta x}(x-y)^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\frac{(x_{j}+\Delta x-y)^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}-\frac{(x_{j}-y)^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}.

Integrating the above with respect to the variable
y
, one thus obtains

C_{ij}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i}+\Delta x}(x_{j}+\Delta x-y)^{1-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y-\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i}+\Delta x}(x_{j}-y)^{1-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

The first integral gives

\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i}+\Delta x}(x_{j}+\Delta x-y)^{1-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\frac{1}{2-\alpha}\left((x_{j}+\Delta x-x_{i})^{2-\alpha}-(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2-\alpha}\right).

The second integral gives

\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i}+\Delta x}(x_{j}-y)^{1-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\frac{1}{2-\alpha}\left((x_{j}-x_{i})^{2-\alpha}-(x_{j}-x_{i}-\Delta x)^{2-\alpha}\right).

Thus, one obtains

C_{ij}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{2-\alpha}\left((x_{j}+\Delta x-x_{i})^{2-\alpha}-2(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2-\alpha}+(x_{j}-x_{i}-\Delta x)^{2-\alpha}\right).

Finally, recalling that
x_{j}=j\Delta x
and
x_{i}=i\Delta x
, one obtains the expression

C_{ij}=\frac{\Delta x^{2-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha)}\left((j-i+1)^{2-\alpha}-2(j-i)^{2-\alpha}+(j-i-1)^{2-\alpha}\right).

To compute
C_{ii}
, we first compute the integral

\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i}+\Delta x}|x-y|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{x_{i}}^{y}(y-x)^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{y}^{x_{i}+\Delta x}(x-y)^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\left((y-x_{i})^{1-\alpha}+(x_{i}+\Delta x-y)^{1-\alpha}\right).

Thus, integrating with respect to the variable
y
gives us that

C_{ii}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i}+\Delta x}(y-x_{i})^{1-\alpha}+(x_{i}+\Delta x-y)^{1-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\frac{2\Delta x^{2-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha)}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

C_{ij}
is of the more general form

(\ast)=\int_{a}^{a+\Delta x}\int_{b}^{b+\Delta x}\int_{c}^{c+\Delta x}\int_{d}^{d+\Delta x}\left((x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}+(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}\right)^{-\alpha/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{2},

for different values of
a,b,c,d\in[0,1]
. Here we integrate over the following:
x_{1}\in[a,a+\Delta x]
,
y_{1}\in[b,b+\Delta x]
,
x_{2}\in[c,c+\Delta x]
and
y_{2}\in[d,d+\Delta x]
.

Let us now introduce new variables
\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}_{2},\tilde{y}_{1}
and
\tilde{y}_{2}
as follows:

x_{1}=a+\tilde{x}_{1}\Delta x,\ \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}=\Delta x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{x}_{1}

x_{2}=a+\tilde{x}_{2}\Delta x,\ \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2}=\Delta x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{x}_{2}

y_{1}=a+\tilde{y}_{1}\Delta x,\ \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{1}=\Delta x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{y}_{1}

y_{2}=a+\tilde{y}_{2}\Delta x,\ \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{2}=\Delta x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{y}_{2}.

Then, for the above
4
dimensional integral we have that

(\ast)=\Delta x^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left((a-c+\Delta x(\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{x}_{2}))^{2}+(b-d+\Delta x(\tilde{y}_{1}-\tilde{y}_{2}))^{2}\right)^{-\alpha/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{x}_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{y}_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{x}_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{y}_{2}.

Relabeling back to
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}
and factorizing out
\Delta x^{\alpha}
, one then obtains

(50)

(\ast)=\Delta x^{4-\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left((A+(x_{1}-x_{2}))^{2}+(B+(y_{1}-y_{2}))^{2}\right)^{-\alpha/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{2},

where
A=\frac{a-c}{\Delta x}
and
B=\frac{b-d}{\Delta x}
.

If
i=j
(meaning that
A=0
and
B=0
), then
(\ast)
reduces to

(\ast)=\Delta x^{4-\alpha}I(\alpha),

where we recall that
I(\alpha)
is defined in the statement of the lemma. We refer to
[
4
]
for more details.

If
i\neq j
(meaning that
A\neq 0
or
B\neq 0
), then we first consider

(\square)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left((A+(x_{1}-x_{2}))^{2}+(B+(y_{1}-y_{2}))^{2}\right)^{-\alpha/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2}.

Instead of integration over
[0,1]\times[0,1]
with respect to the measure
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2}
, we integrate along
r\in[-\sqrt{2}/2,\sqrt{2}/2]
(to match the domain
[0,1]\times[0,1]
) with respect to the measure
2(\sqrt{2}/2-|r|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r

(\square)=\int_{-\sqrt{2}/2}^{\sqrt{2}/2}\left((A+2r/\sqrt{2})^{2}+(B+(y_{1}-y_{2}))^{2}\right)^{-\alpha/2}\times 2(\sqrt{2}/2-|r|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r.

We now change variables according to
q=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}r
and obtain that

(\square)=\int_{-1}^{1}\left((A+q)^{2}+(B+(y_{1}-y_{2}))^{2}\right)^{-\alpha/2}(1-|q|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q.

Repeating the above also for the double integrals involving
y_{1},y_{2}
in (
50
), gives us

(\ast)=\Delta x^{4-\alpha}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}\left((A+q_{1})^{2}+(B+q_{2})^{2}\right)^{-\alpha/2}(1-|q_{1}|)(1-|q_{2}|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q_{2},

where we also added back the factor of
\Delta x^{4-\alpha}
.
∎

In contrast to the dimension
d=1
in Appendix
B
, some integrals in
C_{ij}
cannot be computed exactly.
On the one hand, the diagonal elements
C_{ii}
involve the integral
I(\alpha)
which can be computed
using matlab’s command
hypergeom
to evaluate the hypergeometric function.
On the other hand, the other elements of the covariance matrix need numerical approximations, where
we used the matlab command
integral2
.

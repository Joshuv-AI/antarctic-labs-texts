1.
Introduction

Stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) of parabolic type, and in particular the stochastic heat equation, form a central class of models in probability theory. In the classical Euclidean setting, the stochastic heat equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)+\sigma(t,x,u)\dot{W}(t,x)

has been studied extensively using stochastic convolution techniques and the theory of stochastic evolution equations in Hilbert spaces (see
[
29
]
and
[
11
]
for details). These foundational works establish existence, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions under various assumptions on the nonlinearity and the driving noise. More recently, fine properties of solutions, including intermittency, sample path regularity, and tail behavior, have attracted significant attention (see
[
20
,
16
,
6
,
9
,
19
,
18
,
26
]
).

In many applications, however, the underlying state space naturally carries a nontrivial geometric structure. This motivates the study of stochastic heat equations on Riemannian manifolds. Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional compact smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary. We consider the stochastic heat equation

(1.2)

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{M}u(t,x)+\sigma(t,x,u)\dot{W}(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M,

with deterministic initial condition
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
, where
\Delta_{M}=\mathrm{div}\circ\mathrm{grad}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator. A random field
\{u(t,x)\}_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M}
is said to be a mild solution to (
1.2
) if it satisfies

(1.3)

u(t,x)=\int_{M}P_{t}(x,y)u_{0}(y)dy+\int_{[0,t]\times M}P_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))W(dsdy),

where
P_{t}(x,y)
denotes the heat kernel on
M
. Precise estimates on
P_{t}
can be obtained under geometric assumptions such as lower bounds on the Ricci curvature (see
[
22
]
and
[
14
]
). These analytic properties play a crucial role in the study of SPDEs on manifolds. The stochastic integral in (
1.3
) is understood in the Itô-Walsh sense.

The Itô-Walsh solution theory for (
1.2
) is well developed in spatial dimension
d=1
when
\dot{W}
is space-time white noise. In higher dimensions, however, white noise becomes too singular, and it is well known that no function-valued solution exists for the stochastic heat equation driven by space-time white noise when
d\geq 2
.

To study the equation in higher dimensions, it is therefore natural to regularize the noise in the spatial variable. In the Euclidean setting
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Dalang
[
13
]
extended Walsh’s theory to
d\geq 2
by considering noises that are white in time and colored in space, with homogeneous spatial covariance
G(x,x^{\prime})=G(x-x^{\prime})
. A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a random field solution is

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\hat{G}(d\xi)}{1+|\xi|^{2}}<\infty,

where
\hat{G}
denotes the Fourier transform of
G
. This condition, commonly referred to as Dalang’s condition, can be interpreted as a regularity requirement on the noise.

In contrast to the Euclidean case, where Fourier analysis provides a natural tool via Bochner’s theorem, the construction on a compact manifold relies on the spectral decomposition of the Laplace-Beltrami operator, which plays the role of a Fourier transform in this geometric setting. The statement below is to construct an intrinsic family of Gaussian noises on compact Riemannian manifolds that are smoother than white noise and allow the study of (
1.2
) within the Itô-Walsh framework.

Let
L^{2}(M)
denote the space of square-integrable functions on
M
, equipped with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. Let
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(M)
consisting of eigenfunctions of
-\Delta_{M}
, with corresponding eigenvalues
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots
. Thus,
-\Delta_{M}\phi_{n}=\lambda_{n}\phi_{n}
, and for any
\varphi\in L^{2}(M)
there exists a unique expansion

(1.4)

\varphi(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\phi_{n}(x).

In particular,
a_{0}=m_{0}^{-1/2}\int_{M}\varphi dm
, where
m_{0}
denotes the volume of
M
.

To define a function-valued solution to (
1.2
), we introduce a family of spatial Gaussian noises with covariance structure depending on parameters
\alpha,\rho\geq 0
. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
be a complete probability space and let
W(\varphi)
and
W(\psi)
be centered Gaussian random variables for each
\varphi
and
\psi
in
L^{2}(M)
, with covariance

(1.5)

\mathbb{E}[W(\varphi)W(\psi)]=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\alpha,\rho}:=\rho a_{0}b_{0}+\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{a_{n}b_{n}}{\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}},

where
\{a_{n}\}
and
\{b_{n}\}
are the coefficients of
\varphi
and
\psi
in decomposition (
1.4
), respectively. For
\rho>0
, let
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,\rho}
be the completion of
L^{2}(M)
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha,\rho}
.

Remark 1.1
.

From (
1.5
), we have the continuous embeddings
L^{2}(M)\subset\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,\rho}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\beta,\rho}
for
0\leq\alpha<\beta
. Moreover, the choice
\rho=1
and
\alpha=0
recovers spatial white noise on
M
.

For
\alpha,\rho>0
, the covariance
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha,\rho}
admits an integral kernel representation. Define

G_{\alpha}(x,y):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\left(P_{t}(x,y)-\frac{1}{m_{0}}\right)dt\text{ and }G_{\alpha,\rho}(x,y):=\frac{\rho}{m_{0}}+G_{\alpha}(x,y).

Using the spectral expansion

P_{t}(x,y)=\frac{1}{m_{0}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{n}t}\phi_{n}(x)\phi_{n}(y)

and the identity
\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}e^{-\lambda t}dt=\Gamma(\alpha)\lambda^{-\alpha}
, we obtain

G_{\alpha,\rho}(x,y)=\frac{\rho}{m_{0}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{-\alpha}\phi_{n}(x)\phi_{n}(y).

Consequently,

(1.6)

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\alpha,\rho}=\int_{M^{2}}\varphi(x)G_{\alpha,\rho}(x,y)\psi(y)m(dx)m(dy).

Remark 1.2
.

Throughout the paper, we assume that Dalang’s condition
\alpha>(d-2)/2
holds.

Remark 1.3
.

The kernel
G_{\alpha}
can be viewed as an analogue of the Riesz kernel on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Although
\int_{M}G_{\alpha}(x,y),m(dy)=0
and
G_{\alpha}
is not nonnegative, it was shown in
[
9
]
that
G_{\alpha}
is bounded below on
M
. Hence, by choosing
\rho
sufficiently large, the covariance function
G_{\alpha,\rho}
can be made nonnegative.

Proposition 1.1

(Lemma 2.9 in
[
5
]
)
.

For any
\alpha>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}>0
such that

|G_{\alpha}(x,y)|\leq\begin{cases}C_{\alpha}&\alpha>d/2\\
C_{\alpha}(1+\log^{-}(d(x,y)))&\alpha=d/2\\
C_{\alpha}d(x,y)^{2\alpha-d}&\alpha<d/2\end{cases}

where
\log^{-}(z)=\max(0,-\log z)
and
d(x,y)
denotes the Riemannian distance on
M
.

We now define the noise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M
that is white in time and colored in space.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
and define the following Hilbert space of space-time functions

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\rho}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{H^{\alpha,\rho}}(M))

equipped with
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\rho}}
such that for any
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\rho}
,

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\rho}}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle\varphi(t,\cdot),\psi(t,\cdot)\rangle_{\alpha,\rho}dt.

On a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
, let
W_{\alpha,\rho}
be a centered isonormal Gaussian process over
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\rho}
, i.e.,

\mathbb{E}[W_{\alpha,\rho}(\varphi)W_{\alpha,\rho}(\psi)]=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\rho}}

for each
\varphi,\psi
in
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\rho}
. We refer to
W_{\alpha,\rho}
as colored noise on
M
that is white in time.

We impose the following assumptions on the noise coefficient
\sigma
.

Hypothesis 1.1
.

There exists a constant
\mathcal{D}>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
,
x\in M
and
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
,

(1.7)

|\sigma(t,x,u)-\sigma(t,x,v)|\leq\mathcal{D}|u-v|.

Hypothesis 1.2
.

There exist constants
\mathcal{C}_{1}
,
\mathcal{C}_{2}>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
,
x\in M
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

(1.8)

\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\sigma(t,x,u)\leq\mathcal{C}_{2}.

Remark 1.4
.

The first assumption ensures that
\sigma
is Lipschitz continuous in the solution variable, while the second guarantees uniform nondegeneracy of the noise. Under Hypothesis (
1.7
) and Dalang’s condition, it is well known that equation (
1.2
) admits a unique random field solution.

Small ball problems have been well studied and one can see
[
23
]
for an overview of known results on Gaussian processes and references on other processes. In summary, given a stochastic process
X_{t}
starting at the origin, the small ball problem concerns the asymptotic behavior of

P\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{t}|<\varepsilon\right)\text{ as }\varepsilon\downarrow 0.

In our framework, the solution
u
is generally non-Gaussian due to the dependence of
\sigma
on
u
. Small ball probabilities for stochastic heat equations of this type were first studied in
[
2
]
for space-time white noise. Subsequent extensions include Hölder-type norms
[
15
]
and diffusion coefficients that are uniformly elliptic but only Hölder continuous in
u

[
17
]
. These works, however, are restricted to one-dimensional spatial domains due to the singularity of white noise.

More recently,
[
8
]
adapted the approach of
[
2
]
, combined with Dalang’s framework
[
13
]
, to study small ball probabilities for the fractional stochastic heat equation driven by spatially homogeneous noise on the
d
-dimensional torus. Their analysis relies on Fourier methods to control fluctuations of the noise term and employs a different temporal discretization scheme.

The present work extends the study of small ball probabilities for stochastic heat equations to arbitrary compact smooth Riemannian manifolds. Building on the Itô-Walsh framework and Dalang’s theory of spatially colored noise, we replace Euclidean Fourier analysis with the spectral decomposition of the Laplace–Beltrami operator, yielding an intrinsic and coordinate-free approach that explicitly reflects the underlying geometry. Within this setting and under the conditions on the noise coefficient, we establish small ball probability estimates for the resulting non-Gaussian solutions, thereby extending the one-dimensional theory developed in
[
2
,
15
,
17
]
and the torus-based analysis of
[
8
]
to a broad class of compact manifolds. In particular, our approach allows us to treat the boundary regime
\alpha=d/2
, which is inaccessible in previous works relying on spatial homogeneity or flat geometry. This work can be viewed as an initial step toward a study of small ball probabilities for SPDEs on manifolds.

2.
Main Result

Theorem 2.1
.

Under the hypothesis (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) on the noise coefficient
\sigma
, consider the solution to (
1.2
) with initial condition
u_{0}\equiv 0
. Then there exist positive constants
\textbf{C}_{0},\textbf{C}_{1},\textbf{C}_{2},\textbf{C}_{3},\mathcal{D}_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{0}
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that for all
0<\mathcal{D}<\mathcal{D}_{0},0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
T>1
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{\mathbf{C}_{1}T}{\varepsilon^{\frac{2d+4}{h}}}\right)

\displaystyle<P\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<t\leq T\\
x\in M\end{subarray}}|u(t,x)|<\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle<\begin{cases}\mathbf{C}_{2}\exp\left(-\dfrac{\mathbf{C}_{3}T}{\varepsilon^{\frac{H}{2\alpha+2-d}}}\right)&\max\left(0,\dfrac{d}{2}-1\right)<\alpha<\dfrac{d}{2}\\[8.00003pt]
\mathbf{C}_{2}\exp\left(-\dfrac{\mathbf{C}_{3}T[\ln\ln(1/\varepsilon)]^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)&\alpha=\dfrac{d}{2}\\[8.00003pt]
1&\alpha>\dfrac{d}{2}.\end{cases}

Here
h=\min(1,2\alpha-d+2)
and
H=\min\left(\frac{6d-4\alpha}{d},4\alpha+8-2d\right)
.

Remark 2.1
.

Except in the one-dimensional white noise case
(d=1,\alpha=0)
, the exponent of
\varepsilon
appearing in the upper bound is strictly smaller than that in the lower bound. This discrepancy arises from the fact that the temporal coefficient
c_{0}
, defined in (
3.1
), depends explicitly on
\varepsilon
. Despite this suboptimality, the small ball estimates remain valid in all dimensions for both the upper and lower bounds.

Remark 2.2
.

When
\alpha>d/2
, the driving noise is bounded and continuous, and its spatial correlations decay to a constant as the distance between two points increases. In this regime, the corresponding small ball probability admits only a trivial upper bound within our framework.

We now outline the main ideas underlying the proof of Theorem
2.1
. In
[
2
]
, the authors decomposed a one-dimensional spatial interval into small subintervals. To extend this approach to a
d
-dimensional compact manifold, we instead partition the domain into a collection of nested geodesic balls.

A key technical difficulty concerns the temporal coefficient
c_{0}
introduced in
[
2
]
. In that work,
c_{0}
was shown to be uniformly bounded, relying on the exponential decay of spatial correlations as the distance between points increases. In our setting, however, the spatial correlations decay much more slowly, and a uniform bound on
c_{0}
is no longer available. Instead, we control
c_{0}
in terms of
\varepsilon
, as specified in (
3.1
).

A central ingredient of the analysis is the Markov property of (
1.2
) with respect to the time variable
t
. This allows us to reduce the problem to studying the behavior of the (non-Gaussian) solution over short time intervals. To derive the upper bound in Theorem
2.1
, we employ a perturbative argument that approximates the solution
u
by a Gaussian random field in small space-time regions. This approximation is combined with sharp estimates for an associated Gaussian process, whose behavior depends sensitively on the noise parameter
\alpha
. This dependence explains the specific choice of upper bounds in the sequence of events appearing in the next section.

For the lower bound, we apply the Gaussian correlation inequality together with a change-of-measure argument inspired by
[
2
]
. We further show that the approximation error can be controlled by an appropriate choice of the time intervals over which the coefficients are frozen. A carefully constructed stopping time plays a crucial role here, allowing us to overcome the obstruction that prevents a direct extension of the lower bound argument to dimensions
d>1
.

In particular, it is natural to ask how the present estimates behave as the Riesz kernel parameter approaches its critical value
\alpha=d/2
. This regime is closely related to the weak convergence problem as
\beta\downarrow 0
studied in
[
4
]
, as well as Remark(a) following Theorem 2.1 in
[
7
]
. Our results complement and extend the Euclidean theory of SPDEs and demonstrate that the entire framework can be naturally generalized to compact Riemannian manifolds, thereby covering a broad class of spaces with rich geometric and topological structure.

, we state Proposition

and explain its relationship to Theorem
2.1
. Section
4
collects several auxiliary estimates, and Section
5
contains the proof of Proposition
3.1
, thereby completing the argument.

3.
Key Proposition

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose a maximal
\varepsilon^{2}
-separated set
\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N(\varepsilon)}\subset M
,
that is,
d(x_{j},x_{k})\geq\varepsilon^{2}
for
j\neq k
and
\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{N(\varepsilon)}B_{M}(x_{j},\varepsilon^{2})=M
. Next, we partition the time interval
[0,T]
into subintervals of length
c_{0}\varepsilon^{4}
where
c_{0}=c_{0}(\varepsilon)
is a temporal coefficient satisfying

(3.1)

c_{0}<\begin{cases}\exp\left(C\varepsilon^{-2d}\ln(\varepsilon)\right)&\alpha=d/2\\
C\varepsilon^{\frac{8\alpha}{d-2\alpha}}&\alpha<d/2.\end{cases}

The constant
C>0
defined in (
5.15
) is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Remark 3.1
.

Unlike the space-time white noise case studied in
[
2
]
, the coefficient
c_{0}
must depend on
\varepsilon
due to the strong spatial correlations between distant points. As shown in the Lemma
5.2
, when
\alpha<d/2
the correlation sum decays polynomially; when
\alpha=d/2
, it decays logarithmically; and when
\alpha>d/2
it remains bounded away from zero, in which case the correlation sum cannot be controlled for any choice of
c_{0}
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Nevertheless, in our setting, the precise value of
c_{0}
does not enter either the upper or lower bounds for the small ball probabilities.

We denote
t_{i}=ic_{0}\varepsilon^{4}
and pick a reference point
x_{0}\in M
to define the nested sets for
j=1,2,\dots,J(\varepsilon)
,

(3.2)

R_{i,j}:=\left\{(t_{i},x_{k}):d(x_{0},x_{k})\leq j\varepsilon^{2}\right\}

where
J(\varepsilon)\geq\frac{\text{diam}(M)}{\varepsilon^{2}}>J(\varepsilon)-1
. Thus,
R_{i,j}
consists of all spatial separation points contained in the geodesic ball
B_{M}(x_{0},j\varepsilon^{2})
at time
t_{i}
.

According to Proposition
1.1
, the solution to (
1.2
) exhibits distinct behaviors depending on the value of
\alpha
. We therefore introduce the following function, which will be used throughout the paper:

(3.3)

f(t)=\begin{cases}t&\alpha>d/2\\
t\ln(1/t)&\alpha=d/2\\
t^{\alpha+1-d/2}&\max(0,d/2-1)<\alpha<d/2.\end{cases}

For
n\geq 0
, we define the events

(3.4)

F_{n}=\left\{|u(t,x)|\leq\sqrt{f(t_{1})}\text{ for all }(t,x)\in R_{n,J}\right\},

which capture the boundedness of the solution at time
t_{n}
over all spatial separation points.

In addition, let
E_{-1}=\Omega
and recall
h=\min(1,2\alpha-d+2)
. For

, we define

(3.5)

E_{n}=\left\{|u(t_{n+1},x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{3}t_{1}^{h/4},\,\text{and }|u(t,x)|\leq\mathcal{C}_{3}t_{1}^{h/4}\text{ for all }t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}),x\in M\right\}.

This event enforces a uniform bound on the solution over the entire time interval
[t_{n},t_{n+1})
, with a stricter constraint at time
t_{n+1}
. The constant
\mathcal{C}_{3}>0
is chosen so that

(3.6)

\mathcal{C}_{3}>6\mathcal{C}_{2}\sqrt{\frac{\ln C_{5}}{C_{6}}},

where
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is the uniform bound of
\sigma
in (
1.8
) and
C_{5},C_{6}
are positive constants appearing in Lemma

.

.

Consider the solution to (

) with the initial condition

. There exist positive constants
\textbf{C}_{4},\textbf{C}_{5},\textbf{C}_{6},\textbf{C}_{7},\mathcal{D}_{0}

\textbf{C}_{4}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{5}}{t_{1}^{\min\left(\frac{d-2\alpha}{2d},\frac{2\alpha+2-d}{2}\right)}}\right)&\max(0,d/2-1)<\alpha\leq(d-1)/2,\end{cases}

Proof of Theorem
2.1

Proof.

)

\varepsilon_{2}
, we obtain

t_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\varepsilon^{2+2d}}{\ln(1/\varepsilon)}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\ln(1/\varepsilon_{2})^{-C^{\prime}}}{\ln\ln(1/\varepsilon_{2})}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon_{2}^{2}}{[\ln\ln(1/\varepsilon_{2})]^{2}},

which implies

P\left(\left\{|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon_{2},t\in[0,T],x\in M\right\}\right)\leq\textbf{C}_{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{3}T[\ln\ln(1/\varepsilon_{2})]^{2}}{\varepsilon_{2}^{2}}\right).

We now turn to the lower bound. The event
E_{n}
in
(
3.5
)
deals with the behavior of
u(t,x)
over the entire interval
[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
. Hence

E:=\bigcap_{n=-1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor}E_{n}\subset\left\{|u(t,x)|\leq t_{1}^{h/4},t\in[0,T],x\in M\right\},

and

P(E)=P\left(\bigcap_{n=-1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor}E_{n}\right)=P(E_{-1})\prod_{n=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor}P\left(E_{n}\bigg|\bigcap_{k=-1}^{n-1}E_{k}\right).

With
u_{0}(x)\equiv 0
and
E_{-1}=\Omega
, Proposition
3.1
(b) immediately yields

\begin{split}P\left(\left\{|u(t,x)|\leq\mathcal{C}_{3}t_{1}^{h/4},t\in[0,T],x\in M\right\}\right)&\geq P(E)\geq\left[\textbf{C}_{6}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{7}}{t_{1}^{d/2}}\right)\right]^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor+1}\\
&\geq\textbf{C}_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{1}T}{t_{1}^{d/2+1}}\right).\end{split}

Finally, replacing
t_{1}^{h/4}
with
\varepsilon_{3}
gives

P\left(\left\{|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon_{3},t\in[0,T],x\in M\right\}\right)\geq\textbf{C}_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{1}T}{\varepsilon_{3}^{\frac{2d+4}{h}}}\right).

The proof is complete, and the rest of this paper is devoted to the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

4.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall basic facts on the heat kernel on a compact Riemannian manifold and collect several estimates that will be used throughout the paper.

4.1.
Heat kernel estimates

Let
M
be a compact
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold without boundary. A function
P:(0,\infty)\times M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
is called a fundamental solution (or heat kernel) of (
1.2
) if

(1)

P\in C^{1,2,2}\left((0,\infty)\times M\times M\right)
;

(2)

for all
x,y\in M
and
t>0
,

\partial_{t}P(t,x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{x}P(t,x,y);

(3)

for every
x\in M
and
f\in C(M)
,

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\int_{M}P(t,x,y)f(y)\,dy=f(x).

We write
P_{t}(x,y)=P(t,x,y)
to simplify our notation. A fundamental solution
P
can be constructed in
[
24
]
with the method of parametrix (see also
[
25
]
). We recall the following estimate for small
t
, in
[
24
]
, for all
n\geq 0
, there exists
C>0
such that

(4.1)

P_{t}(x,y)\leq(2\pi t)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{2t}\right)+Ct^{n}\quad x,y\in M,t\leq 1,

and the following estimate for large
t\geq 1
from
[
3
]
: There exist
C,C^{\prime}>0
such that

(4.2)

\sup_{x,y\in M}|P_{t}(x,y)-m_{0}^{-1}|\leq C\exp(-C^{\prime}t).

The next lemma provides
L^{1}
-bounds on spatial and temporal increments of the heat kernel. These estimates extend Lemma 4.1 in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exist positive constants
C,C^{\prime}
depending only on
M
such that, for all
x,y\in M
and all
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
,

(4.3)

\int_{M}|P_{t}(x,w)-P_{t}(y,w)|dw\leq\min\left(\frac{Cd(x,y)}{\sqrt{t}},2\right),

(4.4)

\int_{M}|P_{t}(x,w)-P_{s}(x,w)|dw\leq\min\left(C^{\prime}\log\left(\frac{t}{s}\right),2\right).

Proof.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
be a minimizing geodesic jointing
x
and
y
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
. For each fixed
w\in M
, the map
s\mapsto P_{t}(\gamma(s),w)
is differentiable. By the mean value theorem,

(4.5)

|P_{t}(x,w)-P_{t}(y,w)|\leq d(x,y)\sup_{z\in\text{Im}(\gamma)}|\nabla_{x}P_{t}(z,w)|.

For a compact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature bounded below,
[
22
]
concluded that the heat kernel satisfies Gaussian-type gradient estimates for small
t
:

(4.6)

|\nabla_{x}P_{t}(x,w)|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}V(x,\sqrt{t})}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}d(x,w)^{2}}{t}\right),

where
V(x,r)
denotes the volume of the geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at
x
. Integrating (
4.5
) over
w\in M
and applying (
4.6
) gives

\begin{split}\int_{M}|P_{t}(x,w)-P_{t}(y,w)|dw&\leq d(x,y)\sup_{z\in\text{Im}(\gamma)}\int_{M}|\nabla_{x}P_{t}(z,w)|dw\\
&\leq d(x,y)\sup_{z\in\text{Im}(\gamma)}\int_{M}\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}V(z,\sqrt{t})}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}d(z,w)^{2}}{t}\right)dw.\end{split}

Via the Bishop-Gromov volume comparison theorem,
M
satisfies a local volume doubling property for small
t
, which implies

\int_{M}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}d(z,w)^{2}}{t}\right)dw\leq CV(z,\sqrt{t}).

Hence,

\int_{M}|P_{t}(x,w)-P_{t}(y,w)|dw\leq\frac{Cd(x,y)}{\sqrt{t}}.

Since the integral is trivially bounded by
2
, (
4.3
) follows.

For the temporal bound, since
P_{t}(x,w)
is
C^{1}
in
t
,

\begin{split}\int_{M}|P_{t}(x,w)-P_{s}(x,w)|dw&\leq\int_{M}\int_{s}^{t}|\partial_{r}P_{r}(x,w)|drdw\\
&\leq\int_{s}^{t}\int_{M}\frac{C}{rV(x,\sqrt{r})}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}d(x,w)^{2}}{r}\right)dwdr\\
&\leq C\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{r}dr=C\log\left(\frac{t}{s}\right),\end{split}

where the second inequality follows from equation
(4.1)
in
[
10
]
and Fubini’s theorem. Again, the integral is clearly bounded by
2
, giving (
4.4
).
∎

4.2.
Noise term estimates

We denote the stochastic integral term in (
1.3
) (the noise term) by

(4.7)

N(t,x):=\int_{[0,t]\times M}P_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))W(dsdy),

and we will estimate its regularity in the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.2

(Spatial regularity of the noise term)
.

There exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
in (
1.8
), such that for any
\alpha>\max\left(\frac{d-2}{2},0\right)
,
\xi\in(0,\min\left(2\alpha-d+2,1\right))
,
t\in[0,1]
and
x,y\in M
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,x)-N(t,y))^{2}\right]\leq\begin{cases}C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}d(x,y)^{2\xi}&\alpha>d/2\\
C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}d(x,y)^{\xi}&\alpha\leq d/2.\end{cases}

Proof.

To simplify our notation, we fix
t,x
and y, and define, for
0<s<t
,

K_{s}(w):=P_{t-s}(x,w)-P_{t-s}(y,w).

By Definition
1.1
and (
1.6
), we have

(4.8)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,x)-N(t,y))^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[W^{2}_{\alpha,\rho}(K_{t}(\cdot)\sigma(t,\cdot,u(t,\cdot)))\right]\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M^{2}}\left|K_{s}(z)K_{s}(w)G_{\alpha,\rho}(w,z)\sigma(s,z,u(s,z))\sigma(s,w,u(s,w))\right|dwdzds\\
&\leq\sup_{r,v}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma(r,v,u(r,v))^{2}\right]\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M^{2}}|K_{s}(z)|\cdot|K_{s}(w)|\cdot G_{\alpha,\rho}(w,z)dwdzds.\end{split}

Case 1:
\alpha>d/2

By (
1.8
), Proposition
1.1
, and Lemma
4.1
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,x)-N(t,y))^{2}\right]&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M^{2}}|K_{s}(z)|\cdot|K_{s}(w)|dwdzds\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\min\left(\frac{Cd(x,y)}{\sqrt{t-s}},2\right)^{2}ds\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}d(x,y)^{2\xi}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\xi}ds\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}d(x,y)^{2\xi},\end{split}

where we used
\min(cx,2)\leq\max(c,2)x^{\xi}
for
x>0,0<\xi<1
and
\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\xi}ds\leq\frac{1}{1-\xi}
.

Case 2:
\alpha=d/2

Using Hölder’s inequality and the integrability of the logarithmic singularity on compact manifolds: for
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\|\log^{-}(d(\cdot,z))P_{t}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|P_{t}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}\cdot\|\log^{-}(d(\cdot,z))\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{d}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}.

Applying Proposition
1.1
, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}&\left[(N(t,x)-N(t,y))^{2}\right]\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M^{2}}|K_{s}(z)|\cdot|K_{s}(w)|\cdot(1+\log^{-}(d(w,z)))dwdzds\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\left[d(x,y)^{2\xi}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M}|K_{s}(z)|\left(\int_{M}\log^{-}(d(w,z))[P_{t-s}(x,w)+P_{t-s}(y,w)]dw\right)dzds\right]\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\left(d(x,y)^{2\xi}+d(x,y)^{\xi}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{\xi}{2}-\frac{d}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}ds\right)\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}d(x,y)^{\xi},\end{split}

where the integral
\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{\xi}{2}-\frac{d}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}ds
converges when choosing
p<\frac{d}{d-2+\xi}
.

Case 3:
\alpha<d/2

(4.9)

\begin{split}\int_{M}P_{t-s}(x,w)G_{\alpha,\rho}(w,z)dw&=\int_{M}P_{t-s}(x,w)\left(\frac{\rho}{m_{0}}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}\left(P_{r}(w,z)-\frac{1}{m_{0}}\right)dr\right)dw\\
&=C+C^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}\left(P_{t-s+r}(x,z)-\frac{1}{m_{0}}\right)dr\\
&\leq C+C^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}(t-s+r)^{-d/2}dr\\
&=C+C^{\prime}(t-s)^{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{\alpha-1}(1+u)^{-d/2}du\leq C(t-s)^{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}},\end{split}

where integral interchanging follows the equation
(2.8)
in
[
5
]
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{\alpha-1}(1+u)^{-d/2}du
converges when
0<\alpha<d/2
. Similarly, the same estimate holds for
P_{t-s}(y,w)
. Substituting (
4.9
) into (
4.8
) yields

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,x)-N(t,y))^{2}\right]&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M^{2}}|K_{s}(z)|\cdot[P_{t-s}(x,w)+P_{t-s}(y,w)]G_{\alpha,\rho}(w,z)dwdzds\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M}|K_{s}(z)|(t-s)^{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}dzds\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}d(x,y)^{\xi}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-\frac{d+\xi}{2}}ds\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}d(x,y)^{\xi}\end{split}

valid for
0<\xi<\min(2\alpha-d+2,1)
and Dalang’s condition ensures
2\alpha-d+2>0
.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(Temporal regularity of the noise term)
.

There exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
in (
1.8
), such that for any
\alpha>\max\left(\frac{d-2}{2},0\right)
,
\zeta\in\left(0,\min\left(\alpha+1-\frac{d}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)
,
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
and
x\in M
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,x)-N(s,x))^{2}\right]\leq\begin{cases}C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{2\zeta}&\alpha>d/2\\
C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}&\alpha\leq d/2.\end{cases}

Proof.

As in the simplification used in Lemma
4.2
, for fix
s
,
t
and
x
, we define, for
0<r<s
,

L_{r}(z):=P_{t-r}(x-z)-P_{s-r}(x-z).

By the independence of the noise on disjoint time intervals (Definition
1.1
), we can split the difference:

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[(N(t,x)-N(s,x))^{2}]&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}\int_{M}L_{r}(z)\sigma(r,z,u(r,z))W(dzdr)\right.\right.\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\left.\left.+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{M}P_{t-r}(x-z)\sigma(r,z,u(r,z))W(dzdr)\right)^{2}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}\int_{M}L_{r}(z)\sigma(r,z,u(r,z))W(dzdr)\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{M}P_{t-r}(x-z)\sigma(r,z,u(r,z))W(dzdr)\right)^{2}\right]\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2}\end{split}

where the cross term vanishes.

Estimate of
I_{1}
:

As in Lemma
4.2
,

I_{1}\leq\sup_{r,v}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma(r,v,u(r,v))^{2}\right]\int_{0}^{s}\int_{M^{2}}|L_{r}(z)|\cdot|L_{r}(w)|\cdot G_{\alpha,\rho}(w,z)dwdzdr.

\bullet
Case 1:
\alpha>d/2

Using (
1.8
), Proposition
1.1
and (
4.4
),

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{M^{2}}|L_{r}(z)|\cdot|L_{r}(w)|dwdzdr\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{s}\min\left(C\log\left(\frac{t-r}{s-r}\right),2\right)^{2}dr\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{2\zeta}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\zeta}dr\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{2\zeta},\end{split}

where we used
\min(c\log(1+x),2)\leq\max(c,2)x^{\zeta}
for
x>0,1>\zeta>0
and
\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\zeta}ds<\infty
when
\zeta<\frac{1}{2}
.

\bullet
Case 2:
\alpha=d/2

By Proposition
1.1
and the logarithmic integrability,

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{M^{2}}|L_{r}(z)|\cdot|L_{r}(w)|\cdot(1+\log^{-}(d(w,z)))dwdzdr\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\left[(t-s)^{2\zeta}+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{M}|L_{r}(z)|\int_{M}\log^{-}(d(w,z))[P_{t-r}(x-w)+P_{s-r}(x-w)]dwdzdr\right]\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\left[(t-s)^{2\zeta}+(t-s)^{\zeta}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\zeta-\frac{d}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}ds\right]\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta},\end{split}

with
p<\frac{d}{d-2+2\zeta}
to ensure convergence.

\bullet
Case 3:
\alpha<d/2

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{M}|L_{r}(z)|\int_{M}[P_{t-r}(x-w)+P_{s-r}(x-w)]G_{\alpha,\rho}(w,z)dwdzdr\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\zeta}\left[(t-r)^{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}+(s-r)^{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}\right]dr\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\zeta+\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}dr\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}.\end{split}

Note that the integral
\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{\zeta+\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}dr
converges when
\zeta<\alpha-\frac{d}{2}+1
.

Estimate of
I_{2}
:

The result is straightforward since the heat kernel is always positive.

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq\sup_{r,v}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma(r,v,u(r,v))^{2}\right]\int_{s}^{t}\int_{M^{2}}P_{t-r}(x,z)P_{t-r}(x,w)G_{\alpha,\rho}(w,z)dwdzdr\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{M^{2}}P_{t-r}(x,z)P_{t-r}(x,w)G_{\alpha,\rho}(w,z)dwdzdr.\\
&\leq\begin{cases}C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}1dr=C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{2\zeta}&\alpha>d/2\\
C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\left((t-s)+\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{-\frac{d}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}dr\right)\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}&\alpha=d/2\\
C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}dr\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}+1}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}&\alpha<d/2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Summing the estimates for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
completes the proof.
∎

The regularity estimates for the noise term yield two corresponding upper bounds on its tail probabilities, as detailed below.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}
, independent on
\mathcal{C}_{2}
in (
1.8
), such that, for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
,
x,y\in M
,
\alpha>\max\left(\frac{d-2}{2},0\right)
,
\zeta\in\left(0,\min\left(\alpha+1-\frac{d}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)
and
\xi\in(0,\min\left(2\alpha-d+2,1\right))
, we have

(4.10)

P(|N(t,x)-N(t,y)|>\kappa)\leq\begin{cases}C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\kappa^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}d(x,y)^{2\xi}}\right)&\alpha>d/2\\
C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\kappa^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}d(x,y)^{\xi}}\right)&\alpha\leq d/2,\end{cases}

(4.11)

P(|N(t,x)-N(s,x)|>\kappa)\leq\begin{cases}C_{3}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}\kappa^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{2\zeta}}\right)&\alpha>d/2\\
C_{3}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}\kappa^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}}\right)&\alpha\leq d/2.\end{cases}

Proof.

For a fixed
t
, we define

N_{t}(s,x):=\int_{[0,s]\times M}P_{t-r}(x-y)\sigma(r,y,u(r,y))W(drdy).

Note that
N_{t}(t,x)=N(t,x)
as defined in (
4.7
), and that
N_{t}(s,x)
is a continuous
\mathcal{F}_{s}^{W}
adapted
\mathbb{R}
valued martingale in
s
since the integrand does not depend on
s
. For fixed
t,x
and
y
, let

\mathcal{N}_{s}:=N_{t}(s,x)-N_{t}(s,y)=\int_{[0,s]\times M}K_{r}(z)\sigma(r,z,u(r,z))W(drdz),

so that
\mathcal{N}_{t}=N(t,x)-N(t,y)
. Since
\mathcal{N}_{s}
is a continuous local martingale with
\mathcal{N}_{0}=0
, it can be represented as a time-changed Brownian motion, i.e.,

\mathcal{N}_{t}=B_{\langle\mathcal{N}\rangle_{t}}.

Lemma
4.2
provides bounds for
\langle\mathcal{N}\rangle_{t}
in three regimes, and applying the reflection principle to the Brownian motion
B_{\langle\mathcal{N}\rangle_{t}}
, with
x
and
y
interchanged, yields the result in (
4.10
).

For a fixed
x
, we define

U_{q_{1}}:=\int_{[0,{q_{1}}]\times M}L_{r}(y)\sigma(r,y,u(r,y))W(drdy)

where
0\leq q_{1}\leq s
. Note that
U_{q_{1}}
is a continuous
\mathcal{F}_{q_{1}}^{W}
adapted local martingale with
U_{0}=0
. In addition, we define

V_{q_{2}}:=\int_{[0,{q_{2}}]\times M}P_{t-s-r}(x-y)\sigma(r+s,y,u(r+s,y))W(drdy)

where
0\leq q_{2}\leq t-s
. Note that
V_{q_{2}}
is also a continuous
\mathcal{F}_{q_{2}}^{W}
adapted local martingale with
V_{0}=0
. Thus, both
U_{q_{1}}
and
V_{q_{2}}
admit representations as time-changed Brownian motions, namely,

U_{s}=B_{\langle U\rangle_{s}}\text{~and~}V_{t-s}=B^{\prime}_{\langle V\rangle_{t-s}},

where
B
,
B^{\prime}
are two different Brownian motions. It is clear that
N(t,x)-N(s,x)=U_{s}+V_{t-s}
, which leads to

P(N(t,x)-N(s,x)>\kappa)\leq P(U_{s}>\kappa/2)+P(V_{t-s}>\kappa/2).

Lemma
4.3
provides bounds for
\langle U\rangle_{s}
and
\langle V\rangle_{t-s}
in three regimes, and applying the reflection principle to two Brownian motions, with
s
and
t
interchanged, yields the result in (
4.11
).
∎

Definition 4.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the Euclidean grid

G_{n}:=\left\{\left(\frac{j}{2^{2n}},\frac{k_{1}}{2^{n}},\dots,\frac{k_{d}}{2^{n}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:j,k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Two Euclidean grid points
y,y^{\prime}\in G_{n}
are “nearest neighbors” if they differ by at most one coordinate step:

(1)

Either
y=y^{\prime}
in all but one spatial coordinate and
|y_{i}-y_{i}^{\prime}|=\frac{1}{2^{n}}
for that coordinate, or

(2)

They differ in one step in time index and match in space.

The following lemma employs a generic dyadic chaining argument to estimate a tail probability over a small space-time region, carrying the approach of
[
2
]
. Moreover, the lemma relaxes the exponent of
\varepsilon
, allowing us to obtain exponential bounds on the probability that are valid across different regimes.

Lemma 4.5
.

Given any reference point
p
on
M
, there exist positive constants

, independent of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
in (

) and

, such that for all

,

where

and

.

Proof.

Since
M
is a compact smooth manifold, there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for every

and every
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, the exponential map at

induces a normal coordinate chart

\phi_{p}:=Id\times\exp_{p}^{-1}:\left[0,\gamma\varepsilon^{4}\right]\times B_{M}(p,\varepsilon^{2})\to\left[0,\gamma\varepsilon^{4}\right]\times B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,\varepsilon^{2}),

which is a smooth diffeomorphism. Moreover, the Riemannian distance is uniformly bi-Lipschitz equivalent to the Euclidean distance in normal coordinates: for
x,y\in\left[0,\gamma\varepsilon^{4}\right]\times B_{M}(p,\varepsilon^{2})
,

(4.12)

C|\phi_{p}(x)-\phi_{p}(y)|\leq d(x,y)\leq C^{\prime}|\phi_{p}(x)-\phi_{p}(y)|

with Lipschitz constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
independent of
p
and
\varepsilon
.

Fix
\gamma\geq 1
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the grid

\mathbb{G}_{n}=\left\{\left(\frac{j}{2^{2n}},\frac{k_{1}}{2^{n}},...,\frac{k_{d}}{2^{n}}\right)\in\left[0,\gamma\varepsilon^{4}\right]\times B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,\varepsilon^{2}):j,k_{1},...,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Set
n_{0}=\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(\gamma^{-1/2}\varepsilon^{-2}\right)\right\rceil
, so that

\log_{2}\left(2\gamma^{-1/2}\varepsilon^{-2}\right)>n_{0}\geq\log_{2}\left(\gamma^{-1/2}\varepsilon^{-2}\right)>n_{0}-1.

For
n<n_{0}
,
\mathbb{G}_{n}
contains only the origin. For
n\geq n_{0}
, using (
4.13
), the cardinality of
\mathbb{G}_{n}
is bounded by

\#\mathbb{G}_{n}\leq C(d)\cdot\left(\gamma\varepsilon^{4}2^{2n}+1\right)\cdot\left(2\varepsilon^{2}2^{n}+1\right)^{d}\leq C(d)2^{(2+d)(n-n_{0})}.

We choose parameters
0<\delta_{1}(\alpha,d)<\delta_{2}(\alpha,d)<m/2
, and set
\delta:=\delta_{2}-\delta_{1}
. They are chosen to satisfy the following constraint

(4.14)

2\zeta\wedge\xi=\begin{cases}m-2\delta&\min(0,d/2-1)<\alpha\leq d/2\\
m/2-\delta&\alpha>d/2\end{cases}

for any
\zeta\in\left(0,\min\left(\alpha+1-\frac{d}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)
and
\xi\in(0,\min\left(2\alpha-d+2,1\right))
.

We now focus on the regime
\min(0,d/2-1)<\alpha\leq d/2
. The case
\alpha>d/2
can be treated analogously and follows easily from the argument below. Define

(4.15)

\mathcal{M}=\frac{1-2^{-\delta_{1}}}{(3+d)2^{\delta n_{0}}}

and consider the event

A(n,\kappa)=\left\{|N(s)-N(q)|\leq\kappa\mathcal{M}\varepsilon^{m}2^{-\delta_{1}n}2^{\delta_{2}n_{0}}\text{ for all nearest neighbors $\phi_{p}(s),\phi_{p}(q)\in\mathbb{G}_{n}$}\right\}.

If the pair is nearest in space in the sense of Definition
4.1
case
1
, then (
4.10
) and (
4.12
) imply

P\left(|N(s)-N(q)|>\kappa\mathcal{M}\varepsilon^{m}2^{-\delta_{1}n}2^{\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right)\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\kappa^{2}\mathcal{M}^{2}\varepsilon^{2m}}{2^{-n\xi}\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}}2^{-2\delta_{1}n}2^{2\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right).

If the pair is nearest in time in the sense of Definition
4.1
case
2
, then (
4.11
) implies

P\left(|N(s)-N(q)|>\kappa\mathcal{M}\varepsilon^{m}2^{-\delta_{1}n}2^{\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right)\leq C_{3}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}\kappa^{2}\mathcal{M}^{2}\varepsilon^{2m}}{2^{-2n\zeta}\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}}2^{-2\delta_{1}n}2^{2\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right).

Therefore, a union bound over all nearest-neighbor pairs in
\mathbb{G}_{n}
gives

\displaystyle P(A^{c}(n,\kappa))\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi_{p}(s),\phi_{p}(q)\in\mathbb{G}_{n}\\
\text{nearest neighbors}\end{subarray}}P\left(|N(s)-N(q)|>\kappa\mathcal{M}\varepsilon^{m}2^{-\delta_{1}n}2^{\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C2^{(2+d)(n-n_{0})}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\mathcal{M}^{2}\varepsilon^{2m}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}}2^{n(2\zeta\wedge\xi)}2^{-2\delta_{1}n}2^{2\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right)

where

are positive constants depending only on

. The last inequality follows from that

by the definition of

in (

), and our choices of

and
\delta
in (

). Let

and we can bound

by summing

for all

,

\displaystyle P\left(A^{c}(\kappa)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}P\left(A^{c}(n,\kappa)\right)\leq\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}C2^{(2+d)(n-n_{0})}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\mathcal{M}^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\gamma^{m/2}}2^{(m-2\delta_{2})(n-n_{0})}\right)

From definitions (

) and (

\displaystyle P\left(A^{c}(\kappa)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}\kappa^{2}2^{-2\delta n_{0}}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\gamma^{m/2}}\right)\leq C_{5}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{6}\kappa^{2}(\gamma\varepsilon^{4})^{\delta}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\gamma^{m/2}}\right)

for any

. Taking the infimum over admissible

yields the optimal bound

\displaystyle P\left(A^{c}(\kappa)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\delta}C_{5}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{6}\kappa^{2}(\gamma\varepsilon^{4})^{\delta}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\gamma^{m/2}}\right)\leq C_{5}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{6}\kappa^{2}(\gamma\varepsilon^{4})^{\frac{m-\min(1,2\alpha-d+2)}{2}}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\gamma^{m/2}}\right).

Now for any point
(t,x)
in
\left[0,\gamma\varepsilon^{4}\right]\times B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,\varepsilon^{2})
, whose normal coordinate is in a grid

for some

, a standard chaining argument similar to page 128 of

yields a path from the

such that the normal coordinate of each pair

is the nearest neighbor in some grid

. For each grid, at most

, we obtain

\displaystyle|N(t,x)|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}|N(p_{j})-N(p_{j+1})|\leq(3+d)\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\kappa\mathcal{M}\varepsilon^{m}2^{-\delta_{1}n}2^{\delta_{2}n_{0}}\leq\kappa\varepsilon^{m}.

Points in
\bigcup\limits_{n}\mathbb{G}_{n}
are dense in
[0,\gamma\varepsilon^{4}]\times B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,\varepsilon^{2})
, and we may extend

to a continuous version. Hence, for
\gamma\geq 1
,

P\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq\gamma\varepsilon^{4}\\
x\in B_{M}\left(p,\varepsilon^{2}\right)\end{subarray}}|N(t,x)|>\kappa\varepsilon^{m}\right)\leq P(A^{c}(\kappa))\leq C_{5}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{6}\kappa^{2}(\gamma\varepsilon^{4})^{\frac{m-\min(1,2\alpha-d+2)}{2}}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\gamma^{m/2}}\right).

For
0<\gamma<1
, the time interval
\left[0,\gamma\varepsilon^{4}\right]
is shorter relative to the spatial scale, so a direct application of the previous grid method is not optimal. We therefore cover the geodesic ball
B_{M}(p,\varepsilon^{2})
by
\frac{C}{\sqrt{\gamma^{d}}}
pieces of smaller ball with radius
\sqrt{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2}
. If the supremum over the whole region is large, then it must be large in at least one sub-ball, so a union bound implies that

P\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq\gamma\varepsilon^{4}\\
x\in B_{M}\left(p,\varepsilon^{2}\right)\end{subarray}}|N(t,x)|>\kappa\varepsilon^{m}\right)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\gamma^{d}}}P\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq\gamma\varepsilon^{4}\\
x\in B_{M}\left(p,\sqrt{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2}\right)\end{subarray}}|N(t,x)|>\kappa\varepsilon^{m}\right)

=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma^{d}}}P\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq(\sqrt{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2})^{2}\\
x\in B_{M}\left(p,\sqrt{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2}\right)\end{subarray}}|N(t,x)|>\frac{\kappa}{\gamma^{m/4}}\left(\gamma^{1/4}\varepsilon\right)^{m}\right)\leq\frac{C_{5}}{\sqrt{\gamma^{d}}}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{6}\kappa^{2}(\gamma\varepsilon^{4})^{g(m)}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\gamma^{m/2}}\right).

Combining both cases completes the proof.
∎

\begin{split}\text{Var}[N(t,x)]&=\int_{[0,t]\times M^{2}}P_{t-s}(x,y)P_{t-s}(x,z)\sigma(s,y)\sigma(s,z)G_{\alpha,\rho}(y,z)dydzds\\
&\geq C\mathcal{C}_{1}^{2}\left[t+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}\left(P_{2s+r}(x,x)-\frac{1}{m_{0}}\right)drds\right]\\
&\geq C\mathcal{C}_{1}^{2}\left[t-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1-2s}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}e^{-C(2s+r)}drds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\alpha-1}(2s+r)^{-d/2}drds\right]\\
&\geq C\mathcal{C}_{1}^{2}\left[t+t^{\alpha+1-d/2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2t}}w^{\alpha-1}(2v+w)^{-d/2}dwdv\right].\end{split}

For
\alpha\geq d/2
, the last integral produces a contribution of order
t
(or
t\log(1/t)
when
\alpha=d/2
):

\begin{split}t^{\alpha+1-d/2}&\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2t}}w^{\alpha-1}(2v+w)^{-d/2}dwdv\geq t^{\alpha+1-d/2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2t}}w^{\alpha-1}(2+w)^{-d/2}dwdv\\
&\geq t^{\alpha+1-d/2}\int_{1}^{\frac{1}{2t}}w^{\alpha-1}(2+w)^{-d/2}dw\geq Ct^{\alpha+1-d/2}\int_{1}^{\frac{1}{2t}}w^{\alpha-1-d/2}dw,\end{split}

and the lower bound follows. When
\max(0,d/2-1)<\alpha<d/2
, it converges and yields a contribution of order
t^{\alpha+1-d/2}
:

t^{\alpha+1-d/2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2t}}w^{\alpha-1}(2v+w)^{-d/2}dwdv\geq Ct^{\alpha+1-d/2},

which completes the proof of (
5.6
).

The covariance satisfies

\begin{split}\text{Cov}[N(t,x)N(t,y)]&=\int_{[0,t]\times M^{2}}P_{t-s}(x,w)P_{t-s}(y,z)\sigma(s,w)\sigma(s,z)G_{\alpha,\rho}(w,z)dwdzds\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\left[t+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}\left(P_{2s+r}(x,y)-\frac{1}{m_{0}}\right)drds\right]\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\left[t+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1-2s}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}e^{-C(2s+r)}drds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1-2s}r^{\alpha-1}drds\right.\\
&\quad\left.+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1-2s}r^{\alpha-1}(2s+r)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{4s+2r}\right)drds\right].\end{split}

Denote the last term by

I(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1-2s}r^{\alpha-1}(2s+r)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{4s+2r}\right)drds.

After the change of variables
u=2s+r
and splitting the integration domain, one obtains

\begin{split}I(t)&=C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{2s}^{1}(u-2s)^{\alpha-1}u^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{2u}\right)duds\\
&=C\int_{0}^{1}u^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{2u}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\min(t,u/2)}(u-2s)^{\alpha-1}ds\right)du\\
&\leq C\left[\int_{0}^{4t}u^{\alpha-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{2u}\right)du\right.\\
&\quad\left.+\int_{4t}^{1}u^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{2u}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}(u-2s)^{\alpha-1}ds\right)du\right]\\
&:=I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t).\end{split}

I_{1}
can be bounded by

I_{1}(t)\leq Cd(x,y)^{2\alpha-d+2}\int_{\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{8t}}^{\infty}v^{-\alpha+d/2-2}e^{-v}dv\leq C\frac{t^{\alpha-d/2+2}}{d(x,y)^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{8t}\right)\leq Ct.

Since
u-2t\in(2t,u)\subset(u/2,u)
when
u\in(4t,1)
, by the mean value theorem, we obtain

\int_{0}^{t}(u-2s)^{\alpha-1}ds\leq Ctu^{\alpha-1},

and
I_{2}
can be bounded by

I_{2}(t)\leq Ct\int_{0}^{1}u^{\alpha-1-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{2u}\right)du.

Applying Proposition
1.1
(Lemma 2.9 of
[
5
]
) yields

I_{2}(t)\leq\begin{cases}Ct&\alpha>d/2\\
Ct(1+\log^{-}(d(x,y)))&\alpha=d/2\\
Ctd(x,y)^{2\alpha-d}&\alpha<d/2.\end{cases}

Combining these estimates proves (
5.7
) and completes the proof.
∎

We now turn to computing an upper bound for

By choosing the coefficients to satisfy

&\leq\left(\sum_{(t,x)\in R_{1,j-1}}\left|\eta^{(j)}(t,x)\right|\right)\cdot\sup_{(t,x)\in R_{1,j-1}}\textrm{SD}[N_{(t_{1})}(t,x)]\\
&<\frac{\sqrt{C_{7}}}{2\sqrt{C_{8}}}\cdot\sqrt{C_{8}f(t_{1})}=\frac{\sqrt{C_{7}f(t_{1})}}{2}.\end{split}

Therefore,

\textrm{SD}(Y)\geq\textrm{SD}(N_{(t_{1})}(t,x))-\textrm{SD}(X)>\frac{\sqrt{C_{7}f(t_{1})}}{2}.

Thus, (

\begin{split}P(I_{j}|\mathcal{G}_{j-1})&\leq P\left(|Z^{\prime}|\leq\frac{2\sqrt{f(t_{1})}}{\sqrt{\textrm{Var}[N_{(t_{1})}(t,x)|\mathcal{G}_{j-1}]}}\right)\\

where

d
,
\alpha
, and

P\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{J}A_{j}\right)\leq C^{C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-2}}=\exp\left(-\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right),

d
,
\alpha
, and
M

For a given

Writing (

Lemma 5.3

(The Gaussian correlation inequality)
.

For any convex symmetric sets
K,L
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any centered Gaussian measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mu(K\cap L)\geq\mu(K)\mu(L).

Proof.

See in paper
[
28
]
and
[
21
]
.
∎

By the Markov property of
u(t,\cdot)
, the behavior of the solution on the interval
[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
depends only on
u(t_{n},\cdot)
and on the noise
\dot{W}(t,x)
restricted to
[t_{n},t]\times M
. Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
(a) and recalling
\mathcal{C}_{3}
in (
3.6

(5.16)

P\left(E_{0}\right)\geq\textbf{C}_{6}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{7}}{t_{1}^{d/2}}\right),

where the constants
\textbf{C}_{6}
,
\textbf{C}_{7}
are independent of
u_{0}
, provided that
|u_{0}(x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{3}t_{1}^{h/4}
for all
x\in M
with
h=\min(1,2\alpha-d+2)
.

We prove (
5.16
) in two steps. First, we establish the estimate in the Gaussian case. We then extend the analysis to the non-Gaussian setting and demonstrate that the approximation error between the Gaussian and non-Gaussian cases can be effectively controlled by an appropriate choice of the time intervals over which the coefficients are frozen.

Deterministic
\sigma

We begin with the Gaussian case, where
\sigma(t,x,u)
is deterministic and depends only on
(t,x)
. For
n\geq 0
, we define the events

(5.17)

D_{n}=\left\{|u(t_{n+1},x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{6}t_{1}^{h/4},\text{and}~|u(t,x)|\leq\frac{2\mathcal{C}_{3}}{3}t_{1}^{h/4},~\forall t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}],x\in M\right\}.

Let

P_{t}(u_{0})(x):=P_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)*u_{0}(x)=\int_{M}P_{t}(x,y)u_{0}(y)dy,

then

(5.18)

|P_{t}(u_{0})(x)|\leq\sup_{x\in M}|u_{0}(x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{3}t_{1}^{h/4}.

Suppose
\theta:[0,{t_{1}}]\times\Omega\to\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,\rho}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-progressively measurable process satisfying:

(1)

Integrability:

\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|\theta_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,\rho}}dt<\infty\quad a.s.

(2)

Novikov’s condition:

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|\theta_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,\rho}}dt\right)\right]<\infty.

Then we can define a new probability measure
Q
on
\mathcal{F}_{t}
by

\frac{dQ}{dP}:=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\langle\theta_{t},dW_{t}\rangle_{\alpha,\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|\theta_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,\rho}}dt\right)

and under
Q
, the process

\widetilde{W}_{t}:=W_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}ds

is a centered Gaussian process over
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,\rho}
. Formally, for
x\in M
,

\displaystyle\dot{\widetilde{W}}(t,x)=\dot{W}(t,x)-\theta(t,x),

which remains white in time and colored in space under
Q
. For a deterministic, uniformly bounded
\theta_{t}
with
|\theta_{t}|<B<\infty
, the integrability and Novikov’s condition reduce to verifying

(5.19)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|\theta_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,\rho}}dt&=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{M^{2}}\theta(t,x)G_{\alpha,\rho}(x,y)\theta(t,y)dxdydt\\
&=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\frac{\rho}{m_{0}}\left(\int_{M}\theta(t,x)dx\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}\left(\int_{M}\theta(t,x)\phi_{n}(x)dx\right)^{2}dt\\
&\leq C{t_{1}}B^{2}<+\infty.\end{split}

We choose
\theta(t,x)=-\frac{P_{t}(u_{0})(x)}{t_{1}\sigma(t,x)}
. Then equation (
1.3
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=P_{t}(u_{0})(x)+\int_{[0,t]\times M}P_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y)\left[\widetilde{W}(dsdy)-\frac{P_{s}(u_{0})(y)}{t_{1}\sigma(s,y)}dsdy\right]

\displaystyle=P_{t}(u_{0})(x)-\frac{tP_{t}(u_{0})(x)}{t_{1}}+\int_{[0,t]\times M}P_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y)\widetilde{W}(dsdy)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{t}{t_{1}}\right)P_{t}(u_{0})(x)+\int_{[0,t]\times M}P_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y)\widetilde{W}(dsdy).

The first term vanishes at time
t_{1}
, and by (
5.18
),

(5.20)

\left|\left(1-\frac{t}{t_{1}}\right)P_{t}(u_{0})(x)\right|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{3}t_{1}^{h/4}.

We define

\widetilde{N}(t,x):=\int_{[0,t]\times M}P_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y)\widetilde{W}(dyds).

Although
\widetilde{W}
does not have the same covariance as
W
under
P
, it is Gaussian under
Q
, and Lemma
4.5
applies as

(5.21)

\begin{split}Q\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq c_{0}\varepsilon^{4}\\
x\in B_{M}(p,\sqrt{c_{0}}\varepsilon^{2})\end{subarray}}|\widetilde{N}(t,x)|>\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{6}t_{1}^{h/4}\right)&=Q\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq\left(c_{0}^{1/4}\varepsilon\right)^{4}\\
x\in B_{M}(p,\sqrt{c_{0}}\varepsilon^{2})\end{subarray}}|\widetilde{N}(t,x)|>\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{6}t_{1}^{h/4}\right)\\
&\leq C_{5}\exp\left(-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}^{2}C_{6}}{36\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}}\right)<1,\end{split}

where
\gamma=1
in Lemma
4.5
, and the last inequality follows from the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{3}
in (
3.6
).

We are now in a position to apply the Gaussian correlation lemma (Lemma
5.3
) in order to bound the probability of the event under the measure
Q
. For any
\theta>0
, we define

A_{k}(\theta):=\left\{\omega:\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,t_{1}]\times U_{k}}|\widetilde{N}(t,x)(\omega)|\leq\theta\right\},

where
\{U_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
is a finite cover of
M
. Let
D_{k}\subset[0,t_{1}]\times U_{k}
be countable and dense, and
D_{k,n}\subset D_{k}
is an increasing sequence of finite subsets with
\bigcup\limits_{n}D_{k,n}=D_{k}
. Define another sequence of sets

A^{(n)}_{k}(\theta):=\left\{\omega:\sup_{(t,x)\in D_{k,n}}|\widetilde{N}(t,x)(\omega)|\leq\theta\right\}.

Then
A_{k}^{(n)}(\theta)
is a decreasing sequence of events which converges to
A_{k}(\theta)
almost surely given that
\widetilde{N}
has a.s. continuous paths, and therefore

\lim_{n\to\infty}Q\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{K}A^{(n)}_{k}(\theta)\right)=Q\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcap_{k=1}^{K}A^{(n)}_{k}(\theta)\right)=Q\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{K}\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A^{(n)}_{k}(\theta)\right)=Q\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{K}A_{k}(\theta)\right)

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\prod_{k=1}^{K}Q\left(A_{k}^{(n)}(\theta)\right)=\prod_{k=1}^{K}\lim_{n\to\infty}Q\left(A_{k}^{(n)}(\theta)\right)=\prod_{k=1}^{K}Q\left(A_{k}(\theta)\right),

because
K
is finite and
\prod_{k=1}^{K}x_{k}
is continuous on
[0,1]^{K}
. Each
A_{k}^{(n)}(\theta)
is a convex, symmetric subset of
\mathbb{R}^{|D_{k,n}|}
, so Lemma
5.3
yields

Q\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{K}A^{(n)}_{k}(\theta)\right)\geq\prod_{k=1}^{K}Q\left(A^{(n)}_{k}(\theta)\right).

Taking limits as
n\to\infty
on both sides gives

(5.22)

Q\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{K}A_{k}(\theta)\right)\geq\prod_{k=1}^{K}Q(A_{k}(\theta)).

By (
5.17
), (
5.20
), (
5.21
), and (
5.22
),

(5.23)

\begin{split}Q(D_{0})&\geq Q\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq t_{1}\\
x\in M\end{subarray}}|\widetilde{N}(t,x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{6}t_{1}^{h/4}\right)\\
&\geq Q\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq t_{1}\\
x\in B_{M}(x,\sqrt{c_{0}}\varepsilon^{2})\end{subarray}}|\widetilde{N}(t,x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{6}t_{1}^{h/4}\right)^{\left(\frac{C}{\sqrt{c_{0}}\varepsilon^{2}}\right)^{d}}\\
&\geq\left[1-C_{5}\exp\left(-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}^{2}C_{6}}{36\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}}\right)\right]^{\left(\frac{C}{\sqrt{c_{0}}\varepsilon^{2}}\right)^{d}}.\end{split}

Since
\frac{dQ}{dP}
is a Radon-Nikodym derivative,

(5.24)

1=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{dQ}{dP}\right]

and, for a deterministic
\theta_{t}
, we can estimate the second moment of the Radon-Nikodym derivative by replacing
\theta_{t}
with
2\theta_{t}
,

(5.25)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{dQ}{dP}\right)^{2}\right]=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|\theta_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,\rho}}dt\right)\leq\exp\left(Ct_{1}^{h/2-1}\right)

where the last inequality comes from (
5.19
) and (
5.24
), and by Cauchy-Schwarz,

Q(D_{0})\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{dQ}{dP}\right)^{2}\right]}\cdot\sqrt{P(D_{0})}.

Combining (
5.23
) and (
5.25
), we can bound the probability of event
D_{0}
under measure
P
as

(5.26)

\begin{split}P(D_{0})&\geq\exp\left(-Ct_{1}^{h/2-1}\right)\exp\left(\frac{C^{\prime}}{c_{0}^{d/2}\varepsilon^{2d}}\ln\left[1-C_{5}\exp\left(-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}^{2}C_{6}}{36\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}}\right)\right]\right)\\
&\geq\exp\left(-Ct_{1}^{h/2-1}-C^{\prime}t_{1}^{-d/2}\right)\\
&\geq C\exp\left(-C^{\prime}t_{1}^{-d/2}\right),\end{split}

since
h=\min(2\alpha-d+2,1)
and the term
t_{1}^{-d/2}
dominates as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

Remark 5.1
.

This reflects a metric entropy effect: enforcing a uniform smallness event over space–time incurs a cost proportional to the number of essentially independent spatial blocks. The scaling is sharp for sup-norm events of Gaussian fields with spatial regularity
h/2
.

Non-deterministic
\sigma

We now treat the case of random
\sigma(t,x,u)
via a freezing argument. One can decompose

u(t,x)=u_{g}(t,x)+M(t,x),

where
u_{g}
solves

\partial_{t}u_{g}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{M}u_{g}(t,x)+\sigma(t,x,u_{0}(x))\dot{W}(t,x)

and

M(t,x)=\int_{[0,t]\times M}P_{t-s}(x,y)[\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))-\sigma(s,y,u_{0}(y))]W(dsdy).

The process
u_{g}
is Gaussian, and under the assumption
|u_{0}(x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{3}t_{1}^{h/4}
, we define

\widetilde{D}_{0}=\left\{|u_{g}(t_{1},x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{6}t_{1}^{h/4},\text{and}~|u_{g}(t,x)|\leq\frac{2\mathcal{C}_{3}}{3}t_{1}^{h/4}~\forall t\in[0,t_{1}],x\in M\right\}.

By (
5.26
), we acquire

(5.27)

P\left(\widetilde{D}_{0}\right)\geq C\exp\left(-C^{\prime}t_{1}^{-d/2}\right).

Now we define the stopping time

\tau=\inf\left\{t>0:|u(t,x)-u_{0}(x)|>t_{1}^{d/4}\text{~for some $x\in M$}\right\},

with
\tau=+\infty
if the set is empty, and set

\widetilde{M}(t,x)=\int_{[0,t]\times M}P_{t-s}(x,y)[\sigma(s,y,u(s\wedge\tau,y))-\sigma(s,y,u_{0}(y))]W(dsdy).

On
\{\tau>t_{1}\}
, we have
M(t,x)=\widetilde{M}(t,x)
for all
t\leq t_{1}
. Therefore,

(5.28)

\begin{split}P(E_{0})&\geq P\left(\widetilde{D}_{0}\bigcap\left\{\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq t_{1}\\
x\in M\end{subarray}}|M(t,x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{6}t_{1}^{h/4}\right\}\right)\\
&\geq P\left(\widetilde{D}_{0}\bigcap\left\{\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq t_{1}\\
x\in M\end{subarray}}|M(t,x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{6}t_{1}^{h/4}\right\}\bigcap\{\tau>t_{1}\}\right)\\
&\geq P(\widetilde{D}_{0})-P\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq t_{1}\\
x\in M\end{subarray}}|\widetilde{M}(t,x)|>\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{6}t_{1}^{h/4}\right)\end{split}

with
|u(t,x)-u_{0}(x)|\leq t_{1}^{d/4}
for all
t\in[0,t_{1}]
and
x\in M
. Applying Remark
4.1
to
\widetilde{M}
and using a union bound as in (
5.5
), we obtain

(5.29)

P\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq t_{1}\\
x\in M\end{subarray}}|\widetilde{M}(t,x)|>\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{6}t_{1}^{h/4}\right)\leq\frac{C_{5}}{(c_{0}\varepsilon^{4})^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{6}}{\mathcal{D}^{2}t_{1}^{d/2}}\right).

Combining (
5.27
), (
5.28
), and (
5.29
), we conclude that there exists a
\mathcal{D}_{0}>0
such that for all
0<\mathcal{D}<\mathcal{D}_{0}
,

\begin{split}P(E_{0})&\geq C\exp\left(-C^{\prime}t_{1}^{-d/2}\right)-\frac{C_{5}}{t_{1}^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{6}}{\mathcal{D}^{2}t_{1}^{d/2}}\right)\\
&\geq\textbf{C}_{6}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{7}}{t_{1}^{d/2}}\right).\end{split}

This establishes (
5.16
) and completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
(b).

Abstract
. We study the local behavior of weak solutions, with possible singularities, of nonlocal nonlinear equations. We first prove that sets of capacity zero are removable for weak solutions under certain integrability conditions. We then characterize the asymptotic behavior of singular solutions near an isolated singularity in terms of the fundamental solution.

1.
Introduction

The classical results due to Serrin
[
39
,
40
]
deal with the local behavior of weak solutions of quasilinear equations in divergence form, whose model equation is the
p
-Laplace equation
-\Delta_{p}u=0
for
p>1
. Regarding the regular behavior of solutions, he established the local boundedness, the weak Harnack inequality, the Harnack inequality and Hölder continuity by adapting Moser’s iteration technique
[
35
,
36
]
to the quasilinear setting. These regularity results were then employed to investigate the singularity of solutions, leading to the removable singularity theorem and the isolated singularity theorem. He first showed that a solution
u
in
\Omega\setminus E
can be extended as a solution of the same equation in the entire domain
\Omega
, provided that
E
is negligible in the sense of capacity and that
u
satisfies certain integrability conditions. Furthermore, he proved that singular solutions near an isolated singularity must behave like the fundamental solution of the
p
-Laplace equation.

The goal of this paper is to establish a nonlocal counterpart of the results in celebrated papers
[
39
,
40
]
. In particular, we are interested in the nature of removable singularities of solutions and the behavior of a solution near an isolated singularity in the nonlocal nonlinear framework. It is noteworthy that the local regularity of weak solutions of nonlocal equations has been relatively well studied in various works, including those by Caffarelli–Chan–Vasseur
[
8
]
and Kassmann
[
25
]
for linear equations and by Di Castro–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
19
,
20
]
and Cozzi
[
17
]
for nonlinear equations.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<s<1<p<\infty
and
\Lambda\geq 1
, and assume that
sp\leq n
. Let
\Omega
be a nonempty open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which may be unbounded. We are concerned with weak solutions
u
of the nonlocal nonlinear equation

(1.1)

\mathcal{L}u+b(x,u)=0

in
\Omega
or in a subset of
\Omega
, where
\mathcal{L}
is an integro-differential operator of the form

\mathcal{L}u(x)\coloneqq 2\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

with a measurable kernel
k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
satisfying the symmetry
k(x,y)=k(y,x)
and the ellipticity condition

(1.2)

\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\leq k(x,y)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{n+sp}},

and
b:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function satisfying the structure condition

(1.3)

|b(x,z)|\leq b_{1}(x)|z|^{p-1}+b_{2}(x)

with nonnegative functions
b_{1},b_{2}\in L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(\Omega)
(
0<\varepsilon<sp
). The principal model equation is the fractional
p
-Laplace equation
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=0
.

Our first main theorem on the removable singularity reads as follows. See Section
2
for the definitions of weak solutions and sets of capacity zero.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E
be a relatively closed set in
\Omega
of
(t,q)
-capacity zero, where

q\geq p\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{cases}t=s&\text{if}~q=p,\\
t\in(s,1)&\text{if}~q>p.\end{cases}

If
u
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus E
such that
u
is continuous in
\Omega\setminus E
and
u\in L^{(1+\delta)\theta}(\Omega\setminus E)
for some
\delta>0
, where

\theta=q(p-1)/(q-p),

then
u
has a representative that solves the same equation (
1.1
) in
\Omega
and is continuous in
\Omega
.

A few remarks on Theorem
1.1
are in order. In contrast to the local case,
u
is a priori defined on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, since
E
is the set of measure zero by Lemma
2.14
, the condition
u\in L^{(1+\varepsilon)\theta}(\Omega\setminus E)
in Theorem
1.1
is equivalent to
u\in L^{(1+\varepsilon)\theta}(\Omega)
. When
q=p
, this condition is understood as
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Note, however, that the requirement
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus E)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
u
to be a weak solution cannot be replaced by
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see Definition
2.5
). This makes the problem significantly more challenging.

If
E
is a singleton, then
E
is of
(s,p)
-capacity zero according to Lemma
2.15
. Thus, Theorem
1.1
implies the following removable isolated singularity theorem under a suitable growth condition in the subcritical case.

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume that
sp<n
and that
0\in\Omega
. Let
u
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
such that

u(x)=O\left(|x|^{-(n-sp)/(p-1)+\delta}\right)

near the origin for some
\delta>0
. Then
u
has a representative that solves the same equation (
1.1
) in
\Omega
and is continuous in
\Omega
.

Let us mention that Del Pezzo–Quaas
[
18
]
recently verified that the fundamental solution
\Gamma_{s,p}
for
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
is given by

\Gamma_{s,p}(x)=\begin{cases}|x|^{-(n-sp)/(p-1)}&\text{if $sp<n$},\\
-\log|x|&\text{if $sp=n$},\end{cases}

up to multiplication constants depending only on
n
,
s
and
p
. Thus, Corollary
1.2
states that
u
has a removable singularity at the origin if it blows up slower than the fundamental solution.

Our second main theorem is concerned with the classification of the behavior of solutions near an isolated singularity. We begin with the subcritical case. Here and below,
u\eqsim v
means that
C_{1}\leq u/v\leq C_{2}
for some positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. (
u\lesssim v
and
u\gtrsim v
can be defined in the same way.)

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
sp<n
and that
0\in\Omega
. Let
u
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
such that
u
is continuous and bounded from below in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
. Then either
u
has a removable singularity at the origin or

u\eqsim|x|^{-(n-sp)/(p-1)}

in a neighborhood of the origin.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.3
, an alternative version of Corollary
1.2
is obtained in the subcritical case as follows. We point out that Corollary
1.4
improves Corollary
1.2
in terms of the growth condition, but it requires an additional boundedness assumption from one side to take advantage of Theorem
1.3
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Assume that
sp<n
and that
0\in\Omega
. Let
u
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
such that
u
is bounded from one side in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
and

u(x)=o\left(|x|^{-(n-sp)/(p-1)}\right)

near the origin. Then
u
has a representative that solves the same equation (
1.1
) in
\Omega
and is continuous in
\Omega
.

In the critical case
sp=n
, we are able to achieve the partial classification result in terms of the fundamental solution
\Gamma_{s,p}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
sp=n
and that
0\in\Omega
. Let
u
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
such that
u
is continuous and bounded from below in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
. Then either
u
has a removable singularity at the origin or

u\lesssim-\log|x|

in a neighborhood of the origin.

Let us briefly summarize the historical background. Removable singularity and isolated singularity of solutions of
local
equations have been of great interest; just to name a few, we refer to
[
7
,
12
,
23
]
for linear equations,
[
37
,
39
,
40
]
for quasilinear equations and
[
2
,
30
,
31
]
for fully nonlinear equations. All results on isolated singularities share the essence that if
u
is a solution in the punctured ball, then either
u
has a removable singularity or
u
behaves like a fundamental solution of the associated local operator. We also refer to
[
11
,
13
,
29
]
for the scalar curvature equations and
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
22
,
34
]
for other semilinear equations, where the asymptotic behavior is captured by functions different from fundamental solutions. See also comprehensive monographs
[
38
,
41
]
and references therein.

There has been a growing interest in the study on singularities of solutions of
nonlocal
equations. Li–Wu–Xu
[
33
]
, Li–Liu–Wu–Xu
[
32
]
and Klimsiak
[
28
]
established the Bôcher-type theorems for nonnegative solutions of fractional linear equations, which characterize isolated singularities by Dirac mass
\delta_{0}
in the distribution sense. Moreover, there have been several works focusing on fractional semilinear equations of the form

(1.4)

(-\Delta)^{s}u=u^{\alpha}

for some
\alpha>1
. Caffarelli–Jin–Sire–Xiong
[
9
]
, Chen–Quaas
[
14
]
, Chen–Véron
[
15
]
, Chen–Li–Ou
[
16
]
and Yang–Zou
[
42
,
43
]
investigated the local behavior of solutions of (
1.4
) in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
with zero complement data for different ranges of
\alpha
. In particular, for the critical case
\alpha=(n+2s)/(n-2s)
that corresponds to the fractional Yamabe equation, Jin–de Queiroz–Sire–Xiong
[
24
]
described local behavior of solutions with a singular set of fractional capacity zero. Let us also mention the work by Ao–González–Hyder–Wei
[
1
]
, where a removability result for the equation (
1.4
) was established.

We point out that most of these papers rely heavily on the Caffarelli–Silvestre extension
[
10
]
that guarantees the validity of various tools in the local setting. In fact, the condition that
u
vanishes on the complement of
B_{1}
removes the effect of the nonlocal tail term, which encodes the long-range interaction of
u
.

The novelty of the present paper lies in preserving the nonlocal character of the problem. We impose no restrictions on the behavior of solutions in the complement of
\Omega
nor on the structure of
\mathcal{L}
. To the best of our knowledge, our results are new even in the linear case, i.e.
p=2
. Furthermore, despite the technical difficulties arising from
b
, we take this lower-order term
b
into consideration to potentially extend our results to the semilinear context. In particular, one may further investigate the local behavior of positive singular solutions of nonlocal nonlinear equations of the form
\mathcal{L}u=u^{\alpha}
with
1<\alpha<n(p-1)/(n-sp)
, similar to how
[
4
,
22
]
employed the results of
[
39
,
40
]
in the local semilinear setting.

Let us finally illustrate the strategies to deduce the principal results of the present paper. In contrast to the simple display of our main theorems, their proofs are quite intricate and rely heavily on advanced tools from nonlocal regularity theory. As mentioned earlier, the main difficulty in proving the removability theorem (Theorem
1.1
) arises from the lack of the regularity of
u
in the whole domain
\Omega
. To address this difficulty, we develop the De Giorgi–Nash–Moser theory tailored to our situation. Indeed, in Section
3
, we obtain Moser-type Caccioppoli estimates that contain a cut-off function
\bar{\eta}
vanishing in a neighborhood of
E
, which is introduced to avoid the possible singularity on
E
, and some powers of weak (sub-)solutions
u
. On the one hand, we need a polynomial of
u
with a certain growth rate to use the integrability condition given in Theorem
1.1
. On the other hand, we also need a polynomial of
u
with an arbitrarily large power in order for Moser’s iteration to work. By taking a test function as the minimum of these two polynomials, we arrive at the desired Caccioppoli estimate.

To prove Theorem
1.1
by means of Caccioppoli-type estimates, we now choose
\bar{\eta}=\bar{\eta}_{j}
as a sequence of functions approximating the capacity potential of
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
E
is of capacity zero and
u
satisfies the integrability condition, taking the limit as
j\to\infty
eliminates the effect of
E
(or
\bar{\eta}
) from the integral inequality. Moreover, another limiting argument that will be specified in Section
4
guarantees that the test function can be reduced to a single polynomial with an arbitrarily large power. We conclude by using the standard Moser’s iteration method that
u
belongs to the desired function space and so the set
E
is indeed removable.

The proof of the isolated singularity theorem (Theorem
1.3
or
1.5
) is carried out by combining several steps as follows. If a weak solution
u
has a non-removable isolated singularity at
0
, then

(i)

\lim_{x\to 0}u(x)=\infty
: In the local setting, Lemma
5.1
follows from a relatively simple argument utilizing Theorem
1.1
, the Harnack inequality and the maximum principle. More precisely, the maximum principle plays a crucial role in verifying the desired limiting behavior of
u
in the region between two particular spheres on which
u
becomes very large. However, the maximum principle for nonlocal operators provides weaker information compared to the local case, as it depends not only on the boundary data but also on the complement data. To overcome this challenge, we replace the maximum principle by the weak Harnack inequality (Lemma
5.2
), which encodes the global behavior of
u
into the (finite) nonlocal tail term. In particular, we observe that a positive weak solution
u
of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
satisfies De Giorgi-type Caccioppoli estimates in
\Omega
, while
u
itself may not belong to
W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

u\lesssim\Gamma_{s,p}
near
0
: We begin with introducing an invariant quantity
\overline{K}\coloneqq\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)+\int b(\varphi-1)
, where
\varphi
is contained in a suitable function class, see Lemma
5.3
for the precise description. Then we derive a weaker version of upper bounds for singular solutions in Lemma
5.4
based on a new localization technique. This technique reveals the relationship between the invariant value
\overline{K}
and the capacity of a specific annulus, by clarifying the effect of the nonlocal tail term. Furthermore, we end up with the complete upper bound estimates after solving the difference inequality for the integral of
b
in Lemma
5.5
. We note that the desired upper bound follows from the fact that the capacity of an annulus can be captured by the growth rate of
\Gamma_{s,p}
in view of Lemma
2.12
.

(iii)

u\gtrsim\Gamma_{s,p}
near
0
: In Lemma
5.6
, we define another invariant value
K\coloneqq\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)+\int b\varphi
for
\varphi
in the same function class as before. By developing similar estimates on
K
but in a more careful way together with the results from step (ii), it turns out that
K
has a positive sign in Lemma
5.7
. The lower bound of singular solutions is obtained in Lemma
5.8
by using the positivity of
K
and suggesting an appropriate test function in terms of the
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
-potential. Even though an additional difficulty arises from the maximum principle unlike the local situation, the upper bound of
u
allows us to restrict our attention to the boundary behavior of
u
near spheres. A similar procedure in step (iii) seems to fail in the critical case
sp=n
, where we could only obtain the upper bounds.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we summarize the notation and preliminary results on functional inequalities, weak solutions and capacities. Section
3
consists of local regularity estimates, namely, various Caccioppoli-type estimates, Hölder regularity and the Harnack inequality. The proofs for Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
are presented in Sections
4
and
5
, respectively.

2.1.
Functional inequalities

In this section, we recall definitions of several function spaces and provide inequalities related to these spaces. The fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
consists of measurable functions
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty]
whose fractional Sobolev norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

is finite. By
W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
we denote the space of functions
u
such that
u\in W^{s,p}(G)
for every open
G\Subset\Omega
.

Since we study nonlocal equations, the
tail space

L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\coloneqq\left\{u~\text{measurable}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{(1+|y|)^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y<\infty\right\}

is useful. Note that
u\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if the
nonlocal tail
(or
tail
for short)

\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r)\coloneqq\left(r^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/(p-1)}

is finite for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
.

Some functional inequalities, such as the fractional Sobolev inequality and Hölder’s inequality, are presented here. We begin with fractional Sobolev inequalities. See Di Nezza–Palatucci–Valdinoci
[
21
, Theorems 6.5 and 6.7]
for their proofs.

Theorem 2.1

(Fractional Sobolev inequality)
.

Assume that
sp<n
and let
p^{\ast}_{s}=np/(n-sp)
. Let
B=B_{r}(x_{0})
be a ball. Then there exists
C=C(n,s,p)>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}_{s}}(B)}\leq C\left([u]_{W^{s,p}(B)}+r^{-s}\|u\|_{L^{p}(B)}\right)

for any
u\in W^{s,p}(B)
and that

\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}_{s}}(B)}\leq C[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for any measurable and compactly supported function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Note that Theorem
2.1
provides the embedding
W^{s,p}(B)\subset L^{p^{\ast}_{s}}(B)
. Another useful embedding for fractional Sobolev spaces is
W^{s,p}(B)\subset W^{\tilde{s},\tilde{p}}(B)
, where
\tilde{s}\in(0,s)
and
\tilde{p}\in(1,p)
, which is obtained from the following lemma and the embedding
L^{p}(B)\subset L^{\tilde{p}}(B)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Cozzi
[
17
, Lemma 4.6]
)

Let
\tilde{s}\in(0,s)
,
\tilde{p}\in(1,p)
and
B=B_{r}(x_{0})
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,s,\tilde{s},p,\tilde{p})>0
such that

[u]_{W^{\tilde{s},\tilde{p}}(B)}\leq Cr^{n\frac{p-\tilde{p}}{p\tilde{p}}+s-\tilde{s}}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B)}

for any
u\in W^{s,p}(B)
.

We also need the following lemma, which is a generalized version of Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\tilde{s}\in(0,s)
,
\tilde{p}\in(1,p)
, and let
G
be a bounded open set. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,s,\tilde{s},p,\tilde{p})>0
such that

(2.1)

\int_{G}\int_{G}u(x)\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{\tilde{p}}}{|x-y|^{n+\tilde{s}\tilde{p}}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(\mathrm{diam}\,G)^{(s-\tilde{s})\tilde{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p/(p-\tilde{p})}(G)}[v]_{W^{s,p}(G)}^{\tilde{p}}

for any
u\in L^{p/(p-\tilde{p})}(G)
and
v\in W^{s,p}(G)
.

Note that Lemma
2.3
is also true when
\tilde{s}=s
and
\tilde{p}=p
.

Proof.

Let
L
denote the left-hand side of (
2.1
). It follows from Hölder’s inequality that

L\leq\|u\|_{L^{p/(p-\tilde{p})}(G)}\left(\int_{G}\left(\int_{G}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{\tilde{p}}}{|x-y|^{n+\tilde{s}\tilde{p}}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p/\tilde{p}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\tilde{p}/p}.

Let
\mu(x,\mathrm{d}y)=|x-y|^{-n+(s-\tilde{s})\tilde{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y
and
d=\mathrm{diam}\,G
. Then

\mu(x,G)\leq\int_{B_{d}(x)}|x-y|^{-n+(s-\tilde{s})\tilde{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq Cd^{(s-\tilde{s})\tilde{p}}<\infty

for any
x\in G
. Thus, an application of Jensen’s inequality shows that

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{p/(p-\tilde{p})}(G)}\left(\int_{G}\mu(x,G)^{p/\tilde{p}-1}\int_{G}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{sp}}\mu(x,\mathrm{d}y)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\tilde{p}/p}

\displaystyle\leq Cd^{(s-\tilde{s})\tilde{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p/(p-\tilde{p})}(G)}[v]_{W^{s,p}(G)}^{\tilde{p}},

where
C=C(n,s,\tilde{s},p,\tilde{p})>0
.
∎

We close the section with the following inequalities, which appear several times throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
S,G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be nonempty bounded measurable sets such that
S\Subset\mathrm{int}\,G
, and let
d=\mathrm{dist}(S,G^{c})
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,s,p)>0
such that

(2.2)

\int_{S}\int_{G^{c}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq Cd^{-sp}\|u\|_{L^{p-1}(S)}^{p-1}

for any
u\in L^{p-1}(S)
. Moreover, if
x_{0}\in S
, then

\int_{S}\int_{G^{c}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{|S|}{d^{sp}}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{diam}\,S}{d}\right)^{n+sp}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(u;x_{0},d)

for any
u\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Since for any
x\in S

\int_{G^{c}}|x-y|^{-n-sp}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{d}(x)}|x-y|^{-n-sp}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq Cd^{-sp},

the inequality (
2.2
) follows.

If
x\in S
and
y\in G^{c}
, then
|y-x_{0}|\leq|y-x|+|x-x_{0}|\leq(1+\frac{\mathrm{diam}\,S}{d})|x-y|
, and hence

\displaystyle\int_{S}\int_{G^{c}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{diam}\,S}{d}\right)^{n+sp}\int_{S}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{d}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{|S|}{d^{sp}}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{diam}\,S}{d}\right)^{n+sp}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(u;x_{0},d),

as desired.
∎

2.2.
Weak solution

In this section, we define a weak solution of the equation (
1.1
) and study some properties of weak solutions. For this purpose, we define for measurable functions
u,v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[-\infty,\infty]
a quantity

\mathcal{E}(u,v)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

provided that it is finite.

Definition 2.5
.

A function
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
weak supersolution
(resp.
weak subsolution
) of (
1.1
) in
\Omega
if

(2.3)

\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)+\int_{\Omega}b(x,u(x))\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0~\left(\text{resp.}\leq 0\right)

for all nonnegative
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. A function
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
weak solution
of (
1.1
) in
\Omega
if (
2.3
) holds for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
.

As test functions are often not in
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, it is desirable to consider a larger class of functions than
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in Definition
2.5
.

Proposition 2.6
.

A function
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a weak supersolution (resp. weak subsolution) of (
1.1
) in
\Omega
if and only if (
2.3
) holds for all nonnegative
\varphi\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
with
\supp{\varphi}\Subset\Omega
.

Note that similar results for weak subsolutions and weak solutions also hold.

Proof.

Let us fix an open set
G
such that
\supp{\varphi}\Subset G\Subset\Omega
. By mollification, there exist nonnegative functions
\varphi_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G)
such that
\varphi_{j}\to\varphi
in
W^{s,p}(G)
as
j\to\infty
. The formula (2.9) in Kim–Lee
[
27
]
shows that

(2.4)

|\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi_{j}-\varphi)|\leq C(u)\|\varphi_{j}-\varphi\|_{W^{s,p}(G)}.

On the other hand, by using (
1.3
) we have

\left|\int_{\Omega}b(x,u(x))(\varphi_{j}-\varphi)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\int_{G}(b_{1}|u|^{p-1}+b_{2})|\varphi_{j}-\varphi|\,\mathrm{d}x.

We claim that

(2.5)

\int_{G}(b_{1}|u|^{p-1}+b_{2})|w|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\left(\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(G)}^{p-1}+1\right)\|w\|_{W^{s,p}(G)}

for any
w\in W^{s,p}(G)
, where
C=C(n,s,p,\varepsilon,\|b_{1}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(G)},\|b_{2}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(G)},G)>0
. Indeed, if
sp<n
, then it follows from Hölder’s inequality and the fractional Sobolev inequality that

\int_{G}b_{1}|u|^{p-1}|w|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq|G|^{\frac{\varepsilon}{n}}\|b_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{sp-\varepsilon}}(G)}\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}_{s}}(G)}^{p-1}\|w\|_{L^{p^{\ast}_{s}}(G)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(G)}^{p-1}\|w\|_{W^{s,p}(G)}

and that

\int_{G}b_{2}|w|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq|G|^{1-\frac{sp-\varepsilon}{n}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}_{s}}}\|b_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{sp-\varepsilon}}(G)}\|w\|_{L^{p^{\ast}_{s}}(G)}\leq C\|w\|_{W^{s,p}(G)}.

If
sp=n
, then we let

(2.6)

\tilde{s}\in\left(s\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2n}\right),s\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{p}=\frac{n}{\tilde{s}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2p}}

so that
\tilde{p}<p
and
\tilde{s}\tilde{p}<n
. Then similar computations as above together with Lemma
2.3
(applied with
u\coloneqq 1
and
v\coloneqq u
) and Jensen’s inequality show that

\begin{split}\int_{G}b_{1}|u|^{p-1}|w|\,\mathrm{d}x&\leq|G|^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2n}}\|b_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{sp-\varepsilon}}(G)}\|u\|_{L^{\tilde{p}^{\ast}_{\tilde{s}}}(G)}^{p-1}\|w\|_{L^{\tilde{p}^{\ast}_{\tilde{s}}}(G)}\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{W^{\tilde{s},\tilde{p}}(G)}^{p-1}\|w\|_{W^{\tilde{s},\tilde{p}}(G)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(G)}^{p-1}\|w\|_{W^{s,p}(G)}\end{split}

and that

\int_{G}b_{2}|w|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq|G|^{\frac{\varepsilon}{n}-\frac{1}{\tilde{p}^{\ast}_{\tilde{s}}}}\|b_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{sp-\varepsilon}}(G)}\|w\|_{L^{\tilde{p}^{\ast}_{\tilde{s}}}(G)}\leq C\|w\|_{W^{s,p}(G)}.

In either case, the claim (
2.5
) holds.

Therefore, by using (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) applied with
w=\varphi_{j}-\varphi
, we conclude that

\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi_{j})+\int_{\Omega}b(x,u(x))\varphi_{j}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\to\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)+\int_{\Omega}b(x,u(x))\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

as
j\to\infty
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
2.6
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 2.7
.

A function
u
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega
if and only if
u
is both a weak subsolution and a weak supersolution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, then
\varphi=\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-}
, where
\varphi_{+}\coloneqq\max\{\varphi,0\}
and
\varphi_{-}\coloneqq\max\{-\varphi,0\}
. Since
\varphi_{\pm}
are nonnegative functions such that
\varphi_{\pm}\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and
\supp{\varphi_{\pm}}\Subset\Omega
, the desired result follows from Proposition
2.6
.
∎

Proposition
2.6
is useful especially when we test the equation (
1.1
) by functions of the form
u\eta
, where
\eta
is a cut-off function. The following lemma shows that such a function is a legal test function.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, then
u\eta\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
and
\supp{(u\eta)}\Subset\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
S=\supp{(u\eta)}
, then it is clear that
S\subset\supp{\eta}\Subset\Omega
and that

\|u\eta\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(S)}<\infty.

In order to prove that
[u\eta]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}<\infty
, we fix an open set
G
such that
S\Subset G\Subset\Omega
. Then

[u\eta]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{p}=\int_{G}\int_{G}\frac{|(u\eta)(x)-(u\eta)(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+2\int_{G}\int_{\Omega\setminus G}\frac{|(u\eta)(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2}.

We see that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{G}\int_{G}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}|\eta(y)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{G}|u(x)|^{p}\int_{G}\frac{|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}^{p}[u]_{W^{s,p}(G)}^{p}+C(\mathrm{diam}\,G)^{p(1-s)}\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}^{p}\|u\|_{L^{p}(G)}^{p}

and that

I_{2}\leq 2\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}^{p}\int_{S}|u(x)|^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{d}(x)}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{d^{sp}}\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}^{p}\|u\|_{L^{p}(S)}^{p}

for some
C=C(n,s,p)>0
, where
d=\mathrm{dist}(S,G^{c})
. Since
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, we conclude that
u\eta\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
.
∎

Since we are interested in weak solutions with an isolated singularity, we need to deal with functions in
W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\{x_{0}\})
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
. The following lemma shows that one can regularize such functions by truncating them when they have large values near the singularity.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
and let
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\{x_{0}\})
be such that
u\geq 1
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
. Then
\min\{u,1\}\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that
\varphi\coloneqq\min\{u,1\}\in W^{s,p}(G)
for each open set
G\Subset\Omega
containing
x_{0}
. Take
r>0
sufficiently small so that
B_{r}=B_{r}(x_{0})\Subset G
and
u\geq 1
on
B_{r}
. Then clearly
\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(G)}^{p}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(G\setminus B_{r})}^{p}+|B_{r}|<\infty
. Moreover, it follows from
|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq|u(x)-u(y)|
that

\begin{split}[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(G)}^{p}&\leq[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(G\setminus B_{r/2})}^{p}+2\int_{G\setminus B_{r/2}}\int_{B_{r/2}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq[u]_{W^{s,p}(G\setminus B_{r/2})}^{p}+2\int_{G\setminus B_{r}}\int_{B_{r/2}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-1|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq[u]_{W^{s,p}(G\setminus B_{r/2})}^{p}+Cr^{-sp}\int_{G\setminus B_{r}}(|u(x)|^{p}+1)\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty,\end{split}

which proves that
\varphi\in W^{s,p}(G)
.
∎

Instead of regularizing functions in
W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\{x_{0}\})
, one can use a function that is constant near the singularity.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\{x_{0}\})\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If
\varphi\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\{x_{0}\})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is such that
\mathrm{supp}\,{\varphi}\Subset\Omega
and
\varphi=1
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
, then
\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)
is finite.

Proof.

Let
S=\supp\varphi
and take
r>0
sufficiently small so that
\varphi=1
on
B_{r}=B_{r}(x_{0})
and that
B_{r}\Subset\mathrm{int}\,S
. We fix an open set
G
such that
S\Subset G\Subset\Omega
. Then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\iint_{(G^{c}\times G^{c})^{c}\setminus(B_{r/2}\times B_{r/2})}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\Lambda\int_{G\setminus B_{r/2}}\int_{G\setminus B_{r/2}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+2\Lambda\int_{B_{r/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r/2}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}|1-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+2\Lambda\int_{G\setminus B_{r/2}}\int_{G^{c}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}|\varphi(x)|}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

where
\Lambda
is the ellipticity constant given in (
1.2
).

Since
G\setminus B_{r/2}\Subset\Omega\setminus\{x_{0}\}
, it follows that
u\in W^{s,p}(G\setminus B_{r/2})
. Thus, Hölder’s inequality shows that

I_{1}\leq\Lambda[u]_{W^{s,p}(G\setminus B_{r/2})}^{p-1}[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(G\setminus B_{r/2})}<\infty.

For
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
, we use Lemma
2.4
to see that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|1-\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{B_{r/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}+|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{sp}}\left(1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}\right)\left(\|u\|_{L^{p-1}(B_{r/2})}^{p-1}+r^{n}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(u;x_{0},r/2)\right)

and that

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}\int_{S}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{d}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}+|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{d^{sp}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p-1}(S)}^{p-1}+|S|\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{diam}\,S}{d}\right)^{n+sp}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(u;x_{0},d)\right),

where
d=\mathrm{dist}(S,G^{c})
. Since
u\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, both
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
are finite.
∎

2.3.
(s,p)
-capacity

In this section, we recall the definition of the
(s,p)
-capacity and collect some basic properties of it. Let us begin with the definition.

Definition 2.11
.

The
(s,p)
-capacity
of a compact set
K\subset\Omega
is defined by

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(K,\Omega)=\inf_{u\in W(K,\Omega)}{[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}},

where
W(K,\Omega)=\{u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega):u\geq 1~\text{on}~K\}
. For open sets
G\subset\Omega

(2.7)

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(G,\Omega)\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\text{ compact}\\
K\subset G\end{subarray}}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(K,\Omega),

and for arbitrary sets
E\subset\Omega
,

(2.8)

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E,\Omega)\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\text{ open}\\
E\subset G\subset\Omega\end{subarray}}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(G,\Omega).

As pointed out in Björn–Björn–Kim
[
6
, Section 5]
, (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) do not change the definition of capacities for compact sets.

The following estimates for the
(s,p)
-capacity of balls and annulus will be useful in the sequel.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
0<r\leq R/2
. Then

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2},B_{R})\eqsim\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B}_{r},B_{R})\eqsim\begin{cases}\left(r^{-\frac{n-sp}{p-1}}-R^{-\frac{n-sp}{p-1}}\right)^{1-p}&\text{if }sp<n,\\
(\log(R/r))^{1-p}&\text{if }sp=n,\end{cases}

where the comparable constants depend only on
n
,
s
and
p
.

Proof.

The second comparability is contained in Kim–Lee–Lee
[
26
, Lemma 2.17]
, so it is enough to prove the first comparability. Since the capacity is monotone increasing with respect to the argument, it suffices to prove that

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2},B_{R})\gtrsim\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B}_{r},B_{R}).

Indeed, since there exists a ball
B=B_{r/4}(y_{0})
contained in the annulus
\overline{B}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2}
, we have

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2},B_{R})\geq\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B,B_{R})\gtrsim\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B}_{r},B_{R}).

∎

A set of
(s,p)
-capacity zero plays a key role in the main theorems. Let us provide a precise definition of it.

Definition 2.13
.

A set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is of
(s,p)
-capacity zero
if
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E\cap\Omega,\Omega)=0
for all open
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

A set of
(s,p)
-capacity zero in particular has measure zero.

Lemma 2.14
.

If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is of
(s,p)
-capacity zero, then
|E|=0
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set and let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(E\cap\Omega,\Omega)=0
, there exists an open neighborhood
G\subset\Omega
of
E\cap\Omega
such that
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(G,\Omega)<\varepsilon
. Let
K\subset G
be a compact set and take
\varphi\in W(K,\Omega)
such that
[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\leq\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(K,\Omega)+\varepsilon<2\varepsilon
. Thus, the fractional Poincaré inequality (see e.g. Björn–Björn–Kim
[
6
, (5.6)]
) shows that

|K|\leq\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<C\varepsilon,

which in turn implies that
|G|\leq C\varepsilon
. Therefore, we conclude that
|E|=0
.
∎

In case
E
is a singleton, we have the following result.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\{x_{0}\}
is of
(s,p)
-capacity zero if and only if
sp\leq n
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
6
, Lemma 5.5]
that
sp\leq n
if and only if
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\{x_{0}\},\Omega)=0
for every bounded open
\Omega
containing
x_{0}
. Since
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\{x_{0}\},\cdot)
is decreasing with respect to the set inclusion, the desired result follows.
∎

3.
Regularity estimates

In this section, we provide several regularity estimates such as Caccioppoli-type estimates, Hölder estimates and the Harnack inequality, which play a fundamental role in the analysis of singularities for weak solutions of (
1.1
).

3.1.
Caccioppoli-type estimates

Various Caccioppoli estimates are known in the literature, but we need special types of estimates due to the lack of regularity of weak solutions. Such estimates will be used in a crucial way in the sequel. For instance, if
u
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus E
for some measure zero set
E
, one can regularize
u
under some appropriate assumptions by using Caccioppoli-type estimates so that
u
is a weak solution to the same equation in
\Omega
or has nice regularity properties in
\Omega
.

In order to state the Caccioppoli-type estimates, we fix
\beta\geq\beta_{0}>0
,
\gamma=\beta+p-1
and
\gamma_{0}=\beta_{0}+p-1
. For
l>d>0
, we define the functions
F,G:[d,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
by

(3.1)

F(t)=\begin{cases}t^{\gamma/p}&\text{if}~d\leq t\leq l,\\
l^{\gamma/p}(\frac{\gamma}{\gamma_{0}}(\frac{t}{l})^{\gamma_{0}/p}+\frac{\gamma_{0}-\gamma}{\gamma_{0}})&\text{if}~t>l,\end{cases}

and

G(t)=F(t)F^{\prime}(t)^{p-1}-F(d)F^{\prime}(d)^{p-1}.

Note that
F
is the minimum of two polynomials.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
E
be a relatively closed set in
\Omega
with measure zero and let
\bar{\eta}
be a nonnegative smooth function vanishing in a neighborhood of
E
. Let
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
be a nonnegative function. Assume that

(3.2)

d>\left(R^{\varepsilon}\|b_{2}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\right)^{1/(p-1)}.

If
u
is a weak subsolution of (
1.1
) in
B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus E
, then

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|F(\bar{u}(x))-F(\bar{u}(y))|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\max\{(\eta\bar{\eta})(x),(\eta\bar{\eta})(y)\}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\left(1+\gamma^{p-1}+\left(\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}\right)^{p}\right)\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}F(\bar{u}(x))^{p}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|(\eta\bar{\eta})(x)-(\eta\bar{\eta})(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+C\left(\gamma^{p-1}+\gamma^{2sp(p-1)/\varepsilon}\right)r^{-sp}\|F(\bar{u})\eta\bar{\eta}\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}^{p}\\
&\quad+C\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}\left(\sup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{\bar{u}^{p-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}F(\bar{u})F^{\prime}(\bar{u})^{p-1}(\eta\bar{\eta})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

where
\bar{u}=u_{+}+d
and
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda,\varepsilon,R^{\varepsilon}\|b_{1}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(B_{R}(x_{0}))})>0
.

Before we provide the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we prove the following algebraic inequality as a discrete version of

|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}G(\bar{u})\eta^{p}\right)\geq c\,|\nabla(F(\bar{u})\eta)|^{p}-C\left(1+\left(\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}\right)^{p}\right)|F(\bar{u})|^{p}|\nabla\eta|^{p},

where the constants
c,C>0
depend only on
p
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
A=a_{+}+d,B=b_{+}+d
and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\geq 0
. Then

\begin{split}&\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}|a-b|^{p-2}(a-b)(G(A)\eta_{1}^{p}-G(B)\eta_{2}^{p})\\
&\geq c\left|F(A)-F(B)\right|^{p}\max\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p}-C\left(1+\left(\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}\right)^{p}\right)\max\{F(A),F(B)\}^{p}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p},\end{split}

where
c
and
C
are positive constants depending only on
p
.

Proof.

We may assume that
a>b
. Note that since
a-b\geq A-B
and
(a-b)^{p-1}G(B)=(A-B)^{p-1}G(B)
, we have

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}(a-b)^{p-1}(G(A)\eta_{1}^{p}-G(B)\eta_{2}^{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}(a-b)^{p-1}(G(A)-G(B))\eta_{1}^{p}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}(a-b)^{p-1}G(B)(\eta_{1}^{p}-\eta_{2}^{p})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}(A-B)^{p-1}(G(A)-G(B))\eta_{1}^{p}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}(A-B)^{p-1}G(B)(\eta_{1}^{p}-\eta_{2}^{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}(A-B)^{p-1}(G(A)-G(B))\eta_{2}^{p}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta_{0}}(A-B)^{p-1}G(A)(\eta_{1}^{p}-\eta_{2}^{p}).

A straightforward computation shows that

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=p\beta\gamma^{-1}(F^{\prime})^{p}

\displaystyle\text{if}~t\leq l,

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\geq p\beta_{0}\gamma^{-1}(F^{\prime})^{p}\quad

\displaystyle\text{if}~t\geq l.

It thus follows from Jensen’s inequality that

(F(A)-F(B))^{p}\leq(A-B)^{p}\fint_{B}^{A}(F^{\prime})^{p}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{\gamma}{p\beta_{0}}(A-B)^{p-1}(G(A)-G(B)).

This together with the inequalities
G\leq F(F^{\prime})^{p-1}
and
|\eta_{1}^{p}-\eta_{2}^{p}|\leq p|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|\max\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p-1}
shows that

\displaystyle L

The following form of the Harnack inequality on annuli can be obtained by using a standard covering argument, but we provide a proof for completeness.

Theorem 3.9

We can cover

is independent of

\int_{B_{R/2}}\int_{B_{R/2}}\frac{|\bar{u}^{\gamma_{0}/p}(x)-\bar{u}^{\gamma_{0}/p}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Since

|a-b|^{p}\min\{a^{\gamma_{0}-p},b^{\gamma_{0}-p}\}\leq\left|\int_{b}^{a}t^{\gamma_{0}/p-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\right|^{p}=\left(\frac{p}{\gamma_{0}}\right)^{p}\left|a^{\gamma_{0}/p}-b^{\gamma_{0}/p}\right|^{p}\quad\text{for any }a,b\geq d,

we obtain from (
4.5
) that

(4.6)

\int_{B_{R/2}}\int_{B_{R/2}}\frac{|\bar{u}(x)-\bar{u}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\min\{\bar{u}^{\gamma_{0}-p}(x),\bar{u}^{\gamma_{0}-p}(y)\}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

We may assume that
\delta>0
is sufficiently small so that
\gamma_{0}=(1+\delta)(p-1)\leq p
. Then the estimate (
4.6
) together with the boundedness (
4.4
) yields
[u_{+}]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R/2})}<\infty
. Note that we have only used the fact that
u
is a weak subsolution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus E
. By using that
u
is a weak supersolution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus E
, one can also shows that
[u_{-}]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R/2})}<\infty
. This concludes that
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{R/2})
.

We next prove that
u
is indeed a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
B_{R/4}
. If we let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{R/4})
, then
\varphi\bar{\eta}_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{R/4}\setminus E)
. Since
u
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus E
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi\bar{\eta}_{j})+\int_{B_{R/4}}b(x,u(x))\varphi(x)\bar{\eta}_{j}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R/2}}\int_{B_{R/2}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\bar{\eta}_{j}(x)-\bar{\eta}_{j}(y))\varphi(y)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{R/2}}\int_{B_{R/2}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))\bar{\eta}_{j}(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{B_{R/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R/2}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))\varphi(x)\bar{\eta}_{j}(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{R/4}}b(x,u(x))\varphi(x)\bar{\eta}_{j}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\eqqcolon J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}.

If
q>p
, then it follows from Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.2
that

(4.7)

|J_{1}|\leq CR^{n\frac{q-p}{pq}+t-s}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/4})}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R/2})}^{p-1}[\bar{\eta}_{j}]_{W^{t,q}(B_{R/2})}.

If
q=p
, then (
4.7
) is nothing but Hölder’s inequality. The estimate (
4.7
) together with
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{R/2})
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}[\bar{\eta}_{j}]_{W^{t,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0
implies that
\lim_{j\to\infty}J_{1}=0
.

We observe that the integrands in
J_{2}
and
J_{3}
are bounded by functions

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}+\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}+|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}|\varphi(x)|,

respectively, up to constants depending only on
p
and
\Lambda
. Clearly,
H_{1}\in L^{1}(B_{R/2}\times B_{R/2})
. Moreover, we see by using Lemma
2.4
that

\displaystyle\|H_{2}\|_{L^{1}(B_{R/2}\times B_{R/2}^{c})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/4})}\int_{B_{R/4}}\int_{B_{R/2}^{c}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}+|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/4})}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p-1}(B_{R/4})}^{p-1}+R^{n}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(u;x_{0},R/2)\right)<\infty.

It therefore follows from the dominated convergence theorem that

\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)+\int_{B_{R/4}}b(x,u(x))\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0

as desired.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

We take
q\in(p,n/s)
sufficiently close to
n/s
so that

((n-sp)-\delta(p-1))\frac{q}{q-p}<n.

Then there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that

-\left(\frac{n-sp}{p-1}-\delta\right)(1+\delta_{0})\theta=-(1+\delta_{0})\left((n-sp)-\delta(p-1)\right)\frac{q}{q-p}>-n,

where
\theta=q(p-1)/(q-p)
. This implies that
u\in L^{(1+\delta_{0})\theta}(\Omega)
. We now choose
t\in(s,1)
sufficiently close to
s
so that
tq<n
, then
\{0\}
is of
(t,q)
-capacity zero by Lemma
2.15
. Thus, Theorem
1.1
shows that the origin is a removable singularity.
∎

5.
Isolated singularity

The goal of this section is to describe the asymptotic behavior of a singular solution near an isolated non-removable singularity. Namely, we provide the proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
.
In the rest of the paper, we always assume that
\Omega
contains the origin and that
\Omega
is bounded as Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
focus on the isolated singularity at the origin. Note, however, that these theorems still hold for unbounded
\Omega
. Moreover, we may assume that
u\in C(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\})\cap W^{s,p}(\Omega\setminus B_{\rho})
for all
\rho>0
by recalling Theorem
3.7
and choosing
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
if necessary.
The last assumption allows us to avoid technical issues arising from the regularity of
u
near
\partial\Omega
.

We begin with the following result.

Lemma 5.1

(Non-removable singularity)
.

Let
u
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
such that
u
is positive in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
. If
u
has a non-removable singularity at the origin, then

\lim_{x\to 0}u(x)=\infty.

For local equations, such a limit behavior follows rather easily from the removable singularity theorem, the Harnack inequality and the minimum principle, see Section 7 in Serrin
[
39
]
for instance. However, the (global) minimum principle is not suitable for capturing this phenomenon in the nonlocal case. We thus provide an alternative approach based on the following weak Harnack inequality.

Lemma 5.2

(Weak Harnack inequality)
.

Let
u
be a weak supersolution of (
1.1
) in
B_{R}\setminus\{0\}
such that
u
is nonnegative in
B_{R}\setminus\{0\}
. Then for any
r\leq R/2
,

\left(\fint_{B_{r}}u^{p_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\leq C\inf_{B_{r}}u+C\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R)+C\left(R^{\varepsilon}\|b_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{sp-\varepsilon}}(B_{R})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

where
p_{0}\in(0,1)
and
C>0
depend only on
n
,
s
,
p
,
\Lambda
,
\varepsilon
and
R^{\varepsilon}\|b_{1}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(B_{R})}
.

Proof.

Since
\{0\}
is of
(s,p)
-capacity zero, there exists a sequence of smooth functions
\bar{\eta}_{j}
such that
0\leq\bar{\eta}_{j}\leq 1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}[\bar{\eta}_{j}]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0
,
\bar{\eta}_{j}\to 1
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
j\to\infty
and
\bar{\eta}_{j}
vanishes in a neighborhood of
\{0\}
; see the beginning of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We now set
E=\{0\}
, let
B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})\Subset B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})\subset B_{R}
and take
\bar{\eta}=\bar{\eta}_{j}
in (
3.11
). Since
w_{-}\coloneqq(u-k)_{-}\in L^{\infty}(B_{R})
for all
k\in\mathbb{R}
by assumption, it follows from Fatou’s lemma that

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\frac{|w_{-}(x)-w_{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}w_{-}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{w_{+}^{p-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\|w_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{2}}(x_{0}))}^{p}\lim_{j\to\infty}[\bar{\eta}_{j}]_{W^{s,p}(B_{r_{2}}(x_{0}))}^{p}+Cr_{2}^{-sp}\left(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\right)^{p}\|w_{-}\|_{L^{p}(B_{r_{2}}(x_{0}))}^{p}\\
&\quad+C\left(r_{2}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p-1}}\|b_{2}\|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(B_{R})}+\frac{|k|^{p}}{r_{2}^{\varepsilon}}\right)|A^{-}(k;x_{0},r_{2})|^{1-\frac{sp-\varepsilon}{n}}\\
&\quad+Cr_{2}^{-sp}\left(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\right)^{n+sp}\|w_{-}\|_{L^{1}(B_{r_{2}}(x_{0}))}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(w_{-};x_{0},r_{2}).\end{split}

Since
\lim_{j\to\infty}[\bar{\eta}_{j}]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0
, it implies that

u\in\mathrm{DG}_{-}^{s,p}\left(\Omega;\|b_{2}\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(B_{R})},C,-\infty,\frac{\varepsilon}{n},\frac{\varepsilon}{p-1},\infty\right)

for some
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda,\varepsilon,R^{\varepsilon}\|b_{1}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(B_{R})})>0
, where
\mathrm{DG}_{-}^{s,p}
is the fractional De Giorgi class given in
[
17
, Section 6.1]
. Thus, the desired estimate follows from
[
17
, Proposition 6.8]
.
∎

Let us now prove Lemma
5.1
by using the weak Harnack inequality given in Lemma
5.2
.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

Since
sp\leq n
, it follows from Lemma
2.15
that
\{0\}
is of
(s,p)
-capacity zero. Thus, Theorem
1.1
implies that

(5.1)

\limsup_{x\to 0}u(x)=\infty.

It only remains to prove that
\liminf_{x\to 0}u(x)=\infty
. Suppose by contradiction that

\liminf_{x\to 0}u(x)=L<\infty.

Let
B_{R}=B_{R}(0)\subset\Omega
. Then, for each
M>0
, there exists a radius
r=r_{M}\in(0,R/2)
such that

u\geq M

in
B_{r}\setminus B_{r/2}
for
n\geq 2
and in
(r/2,r)
or in
(-r,-r/2)
for
n=1
, by the Harnack inequality (Theorem
3.9
for
n\geq 2
and Theorem
3.8
for
n=1
) and (
5.1
). By using Lemma
5.2
and the fact that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}\setminus\{0\}
, we obtain that

\left(\fint_{B_{r}}u^{p_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p_{0}}\leq C\inf_{B_{r}}u+C\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R)+C\left(R^{\varepsilon}\|b_{2}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(B_{R})}\right)^{1/(p-1)}

for some
p_{0}\in(0,1)
. By combining all the estimates above and taking sufficiently small
r
, we conclude that
M\leq CL+C
for some
C>0
, which leads to the contradiction.
∎

In order to prove the upper bounds in Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
, we first introduce the following invariant.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\varphi\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
\mathrm{supp}\,{\varphi}\Subset\Omega
and
\varphi=1
in a neighborhood of the origin. If
u
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
, then the value

(5.2)

\overline{K}\coloneqq\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)+\int_{\Omega}b(x,u(x))(\varphi(x)-1)\,\mathrm{d}x

is finite and independent of the particular choice of
\varphi
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.10
that
\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)
is finite. Moreover, (
2.5
) shows that

\left|\int_{\Omega}b(x,u(x))(\varphi(x)-1)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus N}(b_{1}|u|^{p-1}+b_{2})|\varphi-1|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\left(\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega\setminus N)}^{p-1}+1\right)<\infty,

where
N
is a neighborhood of the origin on which
\varphi=1
, and
C
is a constant depending only on
n
,
s
,
p
,
\varepsilon
,
\|b_{1}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(\Omega)}
,
\|b_{2}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(\Omega)}
and
\Omega
.

Let
\varphi
and
\overline{\varphi}
be two functions satisfying the conditions given in the statement. It then follows from Proposition
2.6
and Corollary
2.7
that

\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi-\overline{\varphi})+\int_{\Omega}b(x,u(x))(\varphi(x)-\overline{\varphi}(x))\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

which implies the desired conclusion.
∎

We next provide a weaker version of upper bounds for singular solutions. For this purpose, we fix a ball
B_{4R}=B_{4R}(0)\Subset\Omega
with
R>0
to be determined later in the proof of Lemma
5.7
(see (
5.25
)), depending only on
n
,
s
,
p
,
\Lambda
and
b_{1}
, and define

\mathcal{B}(r)\coloneqq\int_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{r}}|b(x,u(x))|\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for }0<r<4R.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
u
be given as in Lemma
5.1
. Then

(5.3)

u^{p-1}(x)\lesssim\begin{cases}(1+\mathcal{B}(|x|))|x|^{-(n-sp)}&\text{if}~sp<n,\\
(1+\mathcal{B}(|x|))(-\log|x|)^{p-1}&\text{if}~sp=n,\end{cases}

in a neighborhood of the origin.

In fact, (
5.3
) holds in
B_{4R}
since
u
is bounded away from the origin.

Proof.

Assume first that
n\geq 2
. Set

(5.4)

M=M_{r}=\max_{\overline{B}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2}}u\quad\text{and}\quad m=m_{r}=\min_{\overline{B}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2}}u

for
r\leq R
. Then
M\geq m>0
. It is enough to show that for sufficiently small
r\in(0,1)

(5.5)

M^{p-1}\lesssim\begin{cases}(1+\mathcal{B}(r))r^{-(n-sp)}&\text{if}~sp<n,\\
(1+\mathcal{B}(r))(-\log r)&\text{if}~sp=n.\end{cases}

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{3R})
be a cut-off function such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
|\nabla\eta|\leq 2/R
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\eta=1
in
B_{2R}
. Define

v=u\eta\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi=\min\{v/m,1\}.

Since
v/m\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\{0\})
and
\lim_{x\to 0}v(x)/m=\infty
, it follows from Lemma
2.9
that
\varphi\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. Moreover, since
\varphi=\varphi\bar{\eta}
for any
\bar{\eta}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that
\bar{\eta}=1
on
B_{3R}
, Lemma
2.8
shows that
\varphi\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
. It is clear that
\varphi=1
in some neighborhood of the origin and that
\supp{\varphi}\Subset\Omega
, so
\overline{K}
given by (
5.2
) is a constant independent of
\varphi
by Lemma
5.3
.

Since

m^{p-1}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}\leq|v(x)-v(y)|^{p-2}(v(x)-v(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)),

we obtain that

(5.6)

m^{p-1}\mathcal{E}(\varphi,\varphi)\leq\mathcal{E}(v,\varphi)\leq|\mathcal{E}(u\eta,\varphi)-\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)|+\overline{K}+\left|\int_{\Omega}b(x,u(x))(\varphi(x)-1)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|.

We now estimate each term in the right-hand side of (
5.6
). First of all, we see that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\mathcal{E}(u\eta,\varphi)-\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)|&\leq\int_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{R}}|\mathcal{A}(u\eta)-\mathcal{A}(u)||\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+2\int_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{4R}}|\mathcal{A}(u\eta)-\mathcal{A}(u)|\varphi(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+2\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{R}}|\mathcal{A}(u\eta)-\mathcal{A}(u)||\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+2\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}|\mathcal{A}(u\eta)-\mathcal{A}(u)|\varphi(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4},\end{split}

where
\mathcal{A}(u)=\mathcal{A}(u)(x,y)\coloneqq|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))
.

Since

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}(u\eta)-\mathcal{A}(u)|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C|(u\eta)(x)-(u\eta)(y)|^{p}+C|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}+Cu^{p}(x)|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{p}

and

\displaystyle|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq m^{-p}|(u\eta)(x)-(u\eta)(y)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq Cm^{-p}(|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}+u^{p}(x)|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{p}),

it follows from Young’s inequality that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq\frac{C}{m}\int_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|\mathcal{A}(u\eta)-\mathcal{A}(u)|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}+m^{p}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\frac{C}{m}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{4R}\setminus B_{R})}^{p}+\frac{C}{m}R^{-sp}\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{4R}\setminus B_{R})}^{p},\end{split}

where
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda)>0
. On the other hand, since

(5.9)

|\mathcal{A}(u\eta)-\mathcal{A}(u)|\leq C(p)(|u(x)|^{p-1}+|u(y)|^{p-1})

and
|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq 1
, we have by Lemma
2.4
that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq C\int_{B_{3R}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{4R}}\frac{u^{p-1}(x)+u^{p-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p-1}(B_{3R})}^{p-1}+R^{n}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(u;0,R)\right),\end{split}

where
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda)>0
. Similar computations show that

(5.11)

\begin{split}I_{3}+I_{4}&\leq C\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{2R}}\frac{u^{p-1}(x)+u^{p-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{B_{3R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\frac{u^{p-1}(x)+|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p-1}(B_{3R})}^{p-1}+R^{n}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(u;0,R)\right)\end{split}

where
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda)>0
.

Next, we estimate

(5.12)

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}b(x,u(x))(\varphi(x)-1)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|&\leq\int_{\Omega\cap\{v\leq m\}}|b(x,u(x))|\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\mathcal{B}(r)+\int_{B_{r}\cap\{v\leq m\}}|b(x,u(x))|\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{4R}}|b(x,u(x))|\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\eqqcolon\mathcal{B}(r)+J_{1}+J_{2}.\end{split}

By applying Hölder’s inequality and using
r<1
, we can estimate

as

(5.13)

J_{1}\leq\int_{B_{r}}(b_{1}m^{p-1}+b_{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq Cr^{n-sp+\varepsilon}m^{p-1}+C,

where
C>0
depends only on
n
,
s
,
p
,
\varepsilon
,
\|b_{1}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(\Omega)}
and
\|b_{2}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(\Omega)}
. Moreover, (
2.5
) shows that

(5.14)

J_{2}\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{4R}}(b_{1}|u|^{p-1}+b_{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega\setminus B_{4R})}^{p-1}+C,

where
C
depends only on
n
,
s
,
p
,
\varepsilon
,
\|b_{1}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(\Omega)}
,
\|b_{2}\|_{L^{n/(sp-\varepsilon)}(\Omega)}
and
\Omega
.

By combining (
5.6
)–(
5.8
) and (
5.10
)–(
5.14
), we obtain that

(5.15)

m^{p}\mathcal{E}(\varphi,\varphi)\leq C+(C+\overline{K}+\mathcal{B}(r))m+Cr^{n-sp+\varepsilon}m^{p},

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
n
,
s
,
p
,
\Lambda
,
R
,
d
,
\eta
and
u
. Since the function
\varphi
is admissible for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2},B_{3R})
, it follows from Lemma
2.12
that

(5.16)

\Lambda\mathcal{E}(\varphi,\varphi)\geq[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\geq\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B}_{r}\setminus B_{r/2},B_{3R})\eqsim\begin{cases}r^{n-sp}&\text{if}~sp<n,\\
(-\log r)^{1-p}&\text{if}~sp=n,\end{cases}

provided that

is sufficiently small. Thus, by taking small

, we obtain from (

) and (
5.16
) that

m^{p}\lesssim\left(1+(1+\mathcal{B}(r))m\right)\begin{cases}r^{n-sp}&\text{if}~sp<n,\\
(-\log r)^{1-p}&\text{if}~sp=n.\end{cases}

Note that Lemma 2 in Serrin
[

]
implies that

(5.17)

m^{p-1}\lesssim\begin{cases}(1+\mathcal{B}(r))r^{-(n-sp)}&\text{if}~sp<n,\\
(1+\mathcal{B}(r))(-\log r)^{p-1}&\text{if}~sp=n.\end{cases}

Therefore, the desired upper bound (

) for sufficiently small

follows from (

) and the Harnack inequality (Theorem

).

Lemma 5.5

Let

The upper bound of singular solutions is now an immediate consequence of Lemmas

Proofs of the upper bound in Theorems

We may assume, by adding a suitable constant

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{E}(u\eta,\varphi)-\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)|+K+\left|\int_{\Omega}b(x,u(x))\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

We now fix

,

-potential of

in

[\phi]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}=\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B}_{r},B_{\delta R})\eqsim\left(r^{-\frac{n-sp}{p-1}}-R^{-\frac{n-sp}{p-1}}\right)^{1-p}\lesssim r^{n-sp},

\widetilde{M}\gtrsim r^{-(n-sp)/(p-1)}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\widetilde{M}=\max_{\overline{B}_{\overline{c}r}\setminus B_{\delta r}}u,

and so the Harnack inequality (Theorem
3.9

Next, we assume that
n=1

\widetilde{M}=\max_{[\delta r,R]}u\quad\text{or}\quad\widetilde{M}=\max_{[-R,-\delta r]}u.

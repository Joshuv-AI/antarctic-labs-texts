Cofinal families of finite VC-dimension Thanks: The first author would like to thank the Israel Science Foundation for partial support of this research (Grant 1302/23). The second author would like to thank the Israel Science Foundation for partial support of this research (Grant no. 804/22).
Omer Ben-Neria, Itay Kaplan, George Peterzil

Abstract
. Given infinite cardinals θ ≤ κ \theta\leq\kappa , we ask for the minimal VC-dimension of a cofinal family ℱ ⊆ [ κ ] < θ \mathcal{F}\subseteq[\kappa]^{<\theta} . We show that for θ = ω \theta=\omega and κ = ℵ n \kappa=\aleph_{n} it is consistent with ZFC that there exists such a family of VC-dimension n + 1 n+1 , which is known to be the lower bound. For θ > ω \theta>\omega we answer this question completely, demonstrating a strong dichotomy between the case of singular and regular θ \theta . We furthermore answer some relative and generalized versions of the above question for singular θ \theta , and answer a related question which appears in [ BBKS24 ] .

1.
Introduction

Suppose that
X
is an infinite set of size
\kappa
and that
\mathcal{F}
is a family of subsets, each of cardinality at most
\theta
for
\theta\leq\kappa
. Consider two possible properties:

(1)

The family
\mathcal{F}
is cofinal: every set of cardinality
<\theta
is contained in a member of
\mathcal{F}
.

(2)

The family
\mathcal{F}
has finite VC-dimension, meaning that there is some
n<\omega
such that given any subset
s
of
X
of size
n
, there is some
t\subseteq s
such that
t
is not of the form
s\cap A
for some
A\in\mathcal{F}
, see
2.2
.

Observe that (
1
) pulls
\mathcal{F}
towards having many sets while (
2
) pulls
\mathcal{F}
towards having few sets. This precise tension is the center of this paper. Generalizing (
1
) we may introduce a third cardinal
\lambda\leq\theta
and ask that
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-cofinal, meaning that every set of size
<\lambda
is contained in a member of
\mathcal{F}
. This leads us to:

Question 1.1
.

Given cardinals
\lambda\leq\theta\leq\kappa
, what is the minimal VC-dimension of a
\lambda
-cofinal family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq[\kappa]^{<\theta}
(where
[\kappa]^{<\theta}
is the set of all subsets of
\kappa
of cardinality
<\theta
)?

Consider the family of proper initial segments of
\omega
. Every finite subset of the natural numbers
is contained in such an initial segment, and it can be seen to have VC-dimension 1. Thus, we have that for
\lambda=\kappa=\theta=\omega
, the answer to
1.1
is 1.

In
[
BBKS24
]
, Bays, Simon and the first two authors, motivated by questions in model theory, considered a version of
1.1
. Generalizing the example of initial segments of
\omega
, they proved:

Fact 1.2
.

[
BBKS24
, Theorem 3.8]

There is an
\omega
-cofinal family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}
with
\VC(\mathcal{F})=2
.

The following result in their paper demonstrates that the VC-dimension for such families cannot be smaller, and more generally gives a lower bound for a possible answer to
1.1
.

Fact 1.3
.

[
BBKS24
, Proposition 3.3 and Remark 3.7]

For
n<\omega
, a cardinal
\kappa
and a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq[\kappa^{+n}]^{<\kappa}
which is
(n+2)
-cofinal we have
\VC(\mathcal{F})\geq n+1
.

Remark 1.4
.

1.3
does not appear exactly in this form in
[
BBKS24
]
: there, the family is required to cover every finite subset of
\kappa^{+n}
, not just those of size
n+1
. However, the proof there gives this stronger formulation.

In this paper, we attempt to continue this project. In particular, we ask when the bound of
1.3
is optimal. The following is the first result proved.

(

(

Fix infinite cardinals

(1)

If

(2)

.

In light of

,

Question 1.6

(1)

What can we say for

(2)

Regarding those two questions, we get the following consistency results.

(1)

The notion of VC-dimension is important in machine learning, where it corresponds to

In their paper, the following was also proved:

[

Suppose

A bit on the proofs.

The main construction throughout this paper is that of an
n
-ordering system, see
2.8
. The idea is to construct systems of compatible well-orders on a cardinal and its initial segments (and each of their initial segments, etc.) and considering sets which are closed relative to these systems, in a certain precise sense (see
2.13
). This idea originated in
[
BBKS24
, Theorem 3.8]
where this was done for
n=1
. Having this general definition forces us to prove many properties by induction on
n
. Accordingly, we made an effort to find the correct definitions so that the proofs are as simple as possible.

2.
Preliminaries and ordering systems

2.1.
VC dimension and cofinal families of sets

Definition 2.1
.

Given a set
X
and a cardinal
\theta
, write
[X]^{<\theta}
for the collection of subsets of
X
of size less than
\theta
.
Say that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq[X]^{<\theta}
is
\lambda
-cofinal
if every
A\in[X]^{<\lambda}
is contained in a member of
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Fix a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
and a cardinal
\kappa\leq|X|
.

(1)

Say that
A\subseteq X
is
shattered by
\mathcal{F}
if for every
A_{0}\subseteq A
there is some
S_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
A_{0}=A\cap S_{0}
.

(2)

The
VC-dimension of
\mathcal{F}
is the maximal size of a finite subset of
X
which is shattered by
\mathcal{F}
, or
\infty
if this is unbounded. Say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
VC-class
if
\VC(\mathcal{F})<\infty
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given cardinals
\lambda\leq\theta\leq\kappa
, let
\VCcof(\lambda,\theta,\kappa)
be the least number
n\in\omega\cup\{\infty\}
such that there is a
\lambda
-cofinal family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq[\kappa]^{<\theta}
of VC-dimension
n
.

We start by investigating basic properties of this function.

Proposition 2.4
.

Given
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
and
X_{0}\subseteq X
we have that any subfamily
\mathcal{F}_{0}
of the family
\mathcal{F}\restriction X_{0}=\{X_{0}\cap S:S\in\mathcal{F}\}
satisfies
\VC(\mathcal{F}_{0})\leq\VC(\mathcal{F})
. In particular, for every choice of cardinals
\lambda\leq\theta\leq\kappa
and
\lambda_{0}\leq\theta_{0}\leq\kappa_{0}
with
\kappa_{0}\leq\kappa
,
\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda
and
\theta\leq\theta_{0}
, we have that
\VCcof(\lambda_{0},\theta_{0},\kappa_{0})\leq\VCcof(\lambda,\theta,\kappa)
.

Proof.

The first part is trivial. For the second part, let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq[\theta]^{<\kappa}
be
\lambda
-cofinal, and apply the first part with
X=\theta
and
X_{0}=\theta_{0}
. It is clearly still
\lambda
-cofinal, and consists of sets of size less than
\kappa\leq\kappa_{0}
, so in particular
\lambda_{0}
-cofinal, giving us the desired inequality.
∎

Restating
1.3
in these terms, we have:

Fact 2.5
.

[
BBKS24
, Proposition 3.3 and Remark 3.7]

For every infinite cardinal
\kappa
and natural number
n
, we have
\VCcof(n+2,\theta,\theta^{+n})\geq n+1
.

From
2.5
we get the following interesting corollary, which is not used elsewhere in this work. It also follows more directly from
[
PS25
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}\subseteq[\omega]^{<\omega}
has the following “uniform cofinality” property: there is a function
f:\omega\rightarrow\omega
such that given
A\in[\omega]^{n}
there is
B\in[\omega]^{<f(n)}
with
A\subseteq B
and
B\in\mathcal{F}
. Then
\VC(\mathcal{F})=\infty
.

Proof.

Fix such a family
\mathcal{F}
with a corresponding function
f:\omega\rightarrow\omega
. consider the two-sorted structure
\mathcal{M}=(\omega,\mathcal{F},\in)
, where
\mathord{\in}\subseteq\omega\times\mathcal{F}
is the incidence relation. Then for every
n<\omega
, we have that
\mathcal{M}\models\psi_{n}
, where:

\displaystyle\psi_{n}:=\ \forall x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1}\in\omega\ \exists y_{0},\ldots,y_{f(n)-1}\in\omega\ \exists S\in\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\big\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1}\big\}\subseteq S\land\big\{y_{0},\ldots,y_{f(n)-1}\big\}=S

Assume towards a contradiction that
\VC(\mathcal{F})=m<\omega
. Let
\mathcal{N}=(N,\hat{\mathcal{F}})
be an elementary extension of cardinality
\aleph_{m}
. Without loss of generality we have
N=\aleph_{m}
. Now, write
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\fin}=\hat{\mathcal{F}}\cap[\aleph_{m}]^{<\omega}
. Since having VC-dimension
m
is a first-order property, we have
\VC(\mathcal{\hat{F}})=m
, hence
\VC(\mathcal{\hat{F}}_{\fin})\leq m
. Because
\mathcal{N}\models\psi_{n}
for every
n<\omega
, we have that
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\fin}
is cofinal as well, a contradiction to the bound of
2.5
.
∎

The following example is a strong hint towards the ideas to follow.

Examples 2.7
.

(1)

We claim that
\VCcof(\aleph_{0},\aleph_{0},\aleph_{0})=1
. First, the family
\mathcal{F}=\big\{\{0,\ldots,n-1\}:n<\omega\big\}
witnesses
\VCcof(\aleph_{0},\aleph_{0},\aleph_{0})\leq 1
. Indeed, given any finite nonempty
A\subseteq\omega
we have
A\subseteq\sup A+1
, and given any two distinct
n_{1}<n_{2}<\omega
, the set
\{n_{1},n_{2}\}
is not shattered by
\mathcal{F}
, as any
I\in\mathcal{F}
with
n_{2}\in I
has
n_{1}\in I
as well, so
\VC(\mathcal{F})<2
. Since any family with at least two distinct sets has VC-dimension at least 1, we have
\VC(\mathcal{F})=1
.
2.4
and
2.5
imply that
1\leq\VCcof(\aleph_{0},\aleph_{0},\aleph_{0})
as well.

(2)

In fact, given any infinite cardinal
\kappa
, this generalizes to demonstrate that
\VCcof(\cof(\kappa),\kappa,\kappa)=1
, with the upper bound witnessed by the family of proper initial segments of
\kappa
.

(3)

Note that for every natural number
n
and infinite cardinals
\theta\leq\kappa
, the family
[\kappa]^{n+1}
witnesses
\VCcof(n+2,\theta,\kappa)\leq n+1
. In fact, applying
2.5
and monotonicity gives us an equality whenever
\kappa\geq\theta^{+n}
.

Lemma 2.16
.

Suppose
\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}
is an
n
-ordering system on a set
X
and

. Then for every

there is a unique

such that

.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by induction, where the case

s=\emptyset
. For
n>1
and

x_{0}=\max_{\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}_{\emptyset}}s
. By the induction hypothesis there exists

such that
s\setminus(\{x_{0}\}\cup t_{k-1})\subseteq\mathord{\operatorname{dom}}(\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}^{x_{0}}_{t_{k-1}})=\mathord{\operatorname{dom}}(\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}_{t_{k-1}\cup\{x_{0}\}})
, which shows that setting
s_{k}=t_{k-1}\cup\{x_{0}\}
finishes the existence proof. For uniqueness, suppose

both satisfy the conclusion of the lemma. We will first claim that
s_{k},t_{k}
must contain
x_{0}
. Indeed, for any nonempty

we have
x_{0}\notin\mathord{\operatorname{dom}}(\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}_{t})\subseteq X_{\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}_{\emptyset}\max_{\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}_{\emptyset}}t}\subseteq X_{\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}_{\emptyset}x_{0}}
, so the desired inclusion cannot hold otherwise. Write
s_{k}^{\prime}=s_{k}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
and
t_{k}^{\prime}=t_{k}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
. Note that we have
s\setminus\{x_{0}\}\subseteq\mathord{\operatorname{dom}}(\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}_{x_{0}})
, so we have
s\setminus s_{k}\subseteq\mathord{\operatorname{dom}}(\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}^{x_{0}}_{s_{k}^{\prime}})\subseteq X_{\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}_{\emptyset}x_{0}}
and

. Applying the induction hypothesis proves
s_{k}^{\prime}=t_{k}^{\prime}
, which completes the proof of uniqueness.
∎

n

\varepsilon\prec\mathrel{\mkern-5.0mu}\mathrel{\cdot}_{\{\gamma_{1}\}}\gamma_{2}
and we have to show that

. If

. But since

-closed, so

Finally we show (
5

-closed, and

, and hence this follows from the induction hypothesis applied to

.

∎

4.

Recall

In the singular case, we show that in VC-classes the cofinality parameter

\cof(\theta)
. It is not clear what can be said about
\VCcof(\lambda,\theta,\kappa)
when

, and see

for a precise formulation.

However, we do show in

that relative to the existence of a measurable

5.

Given the second result mentioned in

So far, to construct

For

Accelerated Topological Pumping in Photonic Waveguides Based on Global Adiabatic Criteria
Kai-Heng Xiao 1, Jin-Lei Wu 2, Zhi-Yong Hu 1, Jin-Kang Guo 2, Xu-Lin Zhang 1, Jia Li 2, Shi-Lei Su 2, Xiang Ni 3, Qi-Dai Chen 1, Zhen-Nan Tian 1

Abstract
Adiabatic topological pumping promises robust transport of energy and information, but its speed is fundamentally limited by the instantaneous adiabatic condition, which demands prohibitively slow parameter variations. Here we develop a paradigm shift from instantaneous to global adiabaticity. We derive a global adiabatic criterion (GAC), which sets an absolute fidelity bound by controlling the root-mean-square value of nonadiabaticity factor. We further introduce a fluctuation-suppression acceleration criterion, which minimizes spatial inhomogeneity and allows us to safely increase the mean nonadiabaticity. Experimentally, we implement this principle in femtosecond-laser-written photonic Su-Schrieffer-Heeger waveguide arrays via scalable power-law coupling modulation. Our accelerated topological pumping achieves > 0.95 >0.95 fidelity over a fivefold reduced device length compared to the conventional scheme, exhibits the predicted linear scaling with the system size, and maintains robust performance across a > 400 >400 nm bandwidth. This principle of GAC provides a universal design rule for fast, compact, and robust adiabatic devices across quantum and classical topological platforms.

The measured output intensity distributions across the 13 waveguides in each sample are in good agreement with their respective theoretical values. According to both measured and predicted results, all the three samples for our acceleration strategy show almost fully localized light on the 13th waveguide representing the other edge of the waveguide array. On the contrary, for the conventional adiabatic pumping samples, the light intensity on the last waveguide is enhanced with increased sample length. Overall, significant light diffusion is observed in the bulk of the waveguide array of these conventional adiabatic samples, but our accelerated topological pumping sample of the same length exhibits strong light localization on the last waveguide. These results clearly demonstrate that our GAAC enables fast topological pumping for photonic transport with remarkably reduced device length and minimal bulk diffusion.

Conclusion
—We have theoretically formulated and experimentally verified an efficient strategy for accelerating adiabatic topological pumping by developing GAAC, transcending the limitations of conventional instantaneous conditions. By deriving that fidelity is governed by the spatial mean
{\overline{Q}}_{\rm non}
and variance
\sigma^{2}_{Q}
of the nonadiabatic factor, we enable rapid parameter modulation where the topological gap is large, while maintaining adiabaticity globally. Implemented in femtosecond-laser-written photonic lattices via scalable power-law couplings, this approach achieves high-fidelity edge-to-edge transport in device lengths much shorter than the conventional pumping scheme, exhibits the predicted linear scalability (
L\propto N
), and maintains robustness over a broad optical bandwidth (
>400
nm). Moreover, as detailed by Note 6 in
SM

SM

The specific power-law coupling functions presented here serve as a representative example that satisfies our GAAC. In principle, any modulation profile conforming to these criteria is applicable, underscoring the universality of the framework. Moreover, our analytical GAAC can be integrated with numerical optimization methods (e.g., the recently reported adiabaticity control limit by gradient descent method

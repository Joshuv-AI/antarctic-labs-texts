Re: GSJ-26-1985 - Interlaminar Contralateral Endoscopic Foraminotomy Reduces Postoperative Dysesthesia in Patients With Degenerative Lumbar Deformity: A Multicenter Comparative Cohort Study
Authors: Facundo Van Isseldyk
Published: 2026

We thank the authors for their close reading of our manuscript and for raising two points that deserve a direct response.

Point 1. POD onset definition and case classification.

The commentators correctly identify a discordance between our stated POD definition (new-onset burning leg pain occurring 3 to 7 days after surgery) and the reported onset range (2 to 6 days) in the TELF group. We have reviewed the source data and confirm that one patient (Patient 11, TELF group) had a symptom onset documented on postoperative day 2, which falls outside the stated 3 to 7 day window. Even when this patient in the everyday practice would be treated as a POD (in fact, he was), this case should not have been classified as POD under our own definition. We therefore reclassify Patient 11 as a non-POD case. With this correction applied, the primary outcome becomes 7 of 46 TELF patients (15.2%) versus 1 of 54 ICELF patients (1.9%), p=0.023 (Fisher exact), OR=9.51, RRR=87%, NNT=7.5. The onset range in the TELF group is now 3 to 6 days, consistent with the stated definition. The direction, magnitude, and statistical significance of the between-group difference are unchanged, and the clinical conclusion is unaffected.

Point 2. Interpretation of functional outcomes.

The commentators are correct. The between-group comparisons of VAS, ODI, and modified MacNab outcomes are tests of difference, not equivalence, and the study was not powered for a formal equivalence or noninferiority analysis of secondary outcomes. The term “equivalent” overstates what the data support. The appropriate expression of our findings is that “no statistically significant between-group difference in functional outcomes was detected at any time point through 12 months” when talking about secondary outcomes. Both groups showed large, symmetric within-group improvements across all functional measures, and MacNab excellent-or-good rates were 67.4% and 70.4% in the TELF and ICELF groups respectively. The absence of a detected functional difference, alongside a marked reduction in POD, supports the conclusion that ICELF reduces approach-related neural irritation without an apparent cost to decompressive efficacy. A prospective study powered for functional equivalence would be required to confirm this formally.

The core finding of this study, a significant and clinically meaningful reduction in POD with ICELF in patients with degenerative lumbar deformity, is unaffected by either correction. We honestly thank the authors the dedication they put when reading our paper. This truly rewards our efforts writing it.

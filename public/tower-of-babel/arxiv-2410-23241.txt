p p -converse theorems for elliptic curves of potentially good ordinary reduction at Eisenstein primes Thanks: TK was supported by the 2021 MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowship 01064790 – ExplicitRatPoints while working on this article.
Timo Keller, Mulun Yin

Abstract
. Let E / 𝐐 E/\mathbf{Q} be an elliptic curve and p ≥ 3 p\geq 3 be a prime. We prove the p p -converse theorems for elliptic curves of potentially good ordinary reduction at Eisenstein primes (i.e., such that the residual representation E ⁡ [ p ] E[p] is reducible) when the p p -Selmer rank is 0 0 or 1 1 . The key step is to obtain the anticyclotomic Iwasawa Main Conjectures for an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field K K where E E does not have CM similar to those in [ CGLS22 ] and descent to 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} . As an application we get improved proportions for the number of elliptic curves in quadratic twist families having rank 0 0 or 1 1 .

Introduction

0.1.
Statement of the main results

Let
E/\mathbf{Q}
be an elliptic curve. The Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture predicts that
{\operatorname{ord}}_{s=1}L(E,s)
, the order of vanishing of the
L
-function of
E
at
s=1
, should equal
{\operatorname{rk}}_{\mathbf{Z}}E(\mathbf{Q})
, the rank of its Mordell–Weil group. The celebrated theorem of Gross–Zagier and Kolyvagin states that for
r\in\{0,1\}
,

{\operatorname{ord}}_{s=1}L(E,s)=r\Rightarrow{\operatorname{rk}}_{\mathbf{Z}}E(\mathbf{Q})=r.

In fact, they proved a stronger result. Consider the following conditions:

(i)

{\operatorname{ord}}_{s=1}L(E,s)=r
;

(ii)

{\operatorname{rk}}_{\mathbf{Z}}E(\mathbf{Q})=r\text{ and }\#\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q})<\infty
.

What they proved is that (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) if
r\in\{0,1\}
, where
\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q})
is the Tate–Shafarevich group of
E
, which is conjectured to be always finite.

Over the past few years, much progress has been made towards a converse to the above theorem. If
p
is any prime, one can define the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer groups associated to
E
, denoted by
{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbf{Q})
. Then there is a third condition:

(iii)

{\operatorname{corank}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbf{Q})=r
.

(iii) is naturally a consequence of (ii) because the groups fit into an exact sequence

0\to E(\mathbf{Q})\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}\to{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbf{Q})\to\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q})[p^{\infty}]\to 0.

The implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) is usually called the
p
-converse to Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin’s theorem, which can be formulated in the following way.

Conjecture A
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbf{Q}
and let
p
be a prime. Let
r\in\mathbf{Z}
. Then

{\operatorname{corank}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbf{Q})=r\Rightarrow{\operatorname{ord}}_{s=1}L(E,s)=r.

When
r\in\{0,1\}
, many important cases of the
p
-converse theorems have been essentially obtained, most of which only allow
p
to be a prime of ordinary reduction or good supersingular reduction for
E
. For example, when
E[p]
is reducible as a
{\operatorname{Gal}}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q})
-module (the
Eisenstein
case) and does not have the trivial representation as a
{\operatorname{Gal}}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}/\mathbf{Q}_{p})
-subrepresentation, the good ordinary case is treated in
[
CGLS22
]
. The restriction on
E[p]
has later been removed and generalized by the authors in
[
KY24
]
to include the multiplicative case, while
E
cannot have supersingular reduction in the Eisenstein case.

Our first result is the anticyclotomic Iwasawa Main Conjectures for elliptic curves at potentially good ordinary Eisenstein primes
p\geq 3
. In fact, we formulate and prove things in terms of a bit more general ‘Heegner pairs’ See
Theorem

3.5.1
.

Our main results are the following:

Theorem B
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbf{Q}
and let
p>2
be a prime of potentially good ordinary reduction for
E
. Assume
E[p]
is reducible. Let
r\in\{0,1\}
. Then

{\operatorname{corank}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbf{Q})=r\Rightarrow{\operatorname{ord}}_{s=1}L(E,s)=r,

and so
{\operatorname{rk}}_{\mathbf{Z}}E(\mathbf{Q})=r
and
\#\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q})<\infty
.

0.2.
Method of proof and outline of the paper

Let
E/\mathbf{Q}
be an elliptic curve of analytic rank
0
or
1
and
K/\mathbf{Q}
be a
Heegner field
for
E/\mathbf{Q}
, i.e., such that all
p\mid N_{E}
split in
K
and
r_{\operatorname{an}}(E/K)=1
.
A
p
-converse theorem for an elliptic curve
E
is naturally a consequence of a ‘Heegner Point type’ Main Conjecture of Iwasawa theory. More precisely, let
{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)
and
S_{p}(E/K)
be the Selmer groups fitting into the exact sequences

0\to E(K)\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}\to{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)\to\Sha_{p^{\infty}}\to 0,

0\to E(K)\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p}\to S_{p}(E/K)\to\varprojlim_{n}\Sha_{p^{n}}\to 0.

Then it is predicted that
{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}\oplus M\oplus M
for some finite
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-module
M
such that

{\operatorname{length}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}(M)={\operatorname{length}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}(S_{p}(E/K)/\mathbf{Z}_{p}\cdot\kappa_{1})-v_{p}({\operatorname{Tam}}(E/K)),

where
\kappa_{1}\in S_{p}(E/K)
is an element of a ‘Kolyvagin system’ coming from Heegner points and
{\operatorname{Tam}}(E/K)
is the product of Tamagawa numbers for
E
over
K
. The Kolyvagin system argument in
[
How04
]
gives a partial answer to the above conjecture in many cases. Namely, one obtains the desired structure theorem, while being able to show

(0.1)

{\operatorname{length}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}(M)\leq{\operatorname{length}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}(S_{p}(E/K)/\mathbf{Z}_{p}\cdot\kappa_{1}).

Unfortunately, to obtain a
p
-converse theorem, one would ask for an opposite inequality, which often requires new ideas in different cases.

One could also consider a more general ‘
\Lambda
-version’ (in fact, it is necessary to do so) of the above conjecture for
\Lambda\coloneq\mathbf{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
, the anticyclotomic Iwasawa algebra. Namely, if
K_{\infty}/K
is the anticyclotomic
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
with subfields
\{K_{n}\subset K_{\infty}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
with
[K_{n}:K]=p^{n}
, one could consider the ‘limiting Selmer groups’
{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})\coloneq\varinjlim{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{n})
and
S_{p}(E/K_{\infty})\coloneq\varprojlim S_{p}(E/K_{n})
. They will be closely related to certain (dual) Selmer groups denoted by
X\coloneq{\operatorname{Hom}}(\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}}(K,M_{f}),\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p})
and
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}}(K,\mathbf{T})
respectively, and one expects that
X
should be pseudo-isomorphic to
\Lambda\oplus M\oplus M
where
M
is a torsion
\Lambda
-module. Moreover, it is conjectured that

\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(M)=\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}}(K,\mathbf{T})/\Lambda\cdot\kappa_{1}^{{\operatorname{Heeg}}}),

where
\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}
denotes the characteristic
\Lambda
-ideal and
\kappa_{1}^{{\operatorname{Heeg}}}
is again an element of a Kolyvagin system coming from Heegner points. Then in the good ordinary case, the Kolyvagin system argument in
[
How04
]
also proves the structure theorem and shows that
\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(M)\supset\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}}(K,\mathbf{T})/\Lambda\cdot\kappa_{1}^{{\operatorname{Heeg}}})
, a ‘one-side divisibility’ of the Heegner Point Main Conjecture. It was first observed in
[
CGLS22
]
that in the Eisenstein case, one could obtain the reversed divisibility by passing to an equivalent form of the Main Conjecture and comparing the so-called ‘Iwasawa invariants’.

More precisely, in the good ordinary case, there are three steps in establishing the equalities in the Heegner Point Main Conjectures:

I

Proving one divisibility using a Kolyvagin system argument;

II

Constructing a so-called
Perrin-Riou’s regulator map
or
the big logarithm map
that maps a Heegner class to the Bertolini–Darmon–Prasana
p
-adic
L
-function, establishing the equivalence between the Heegner Point Main Conjecture to the
Greenberg’s Main Conjecture
involving Greenberg’s Selmer groups and
p
-adic
L
-functions. This relation is sometimes referred to as
the explicit reciprocity law
;

III

In the setting of Greenberg’s Main Conjecture, comparing the Iwasawa invariants of the algebraic side (for Selmer groups) and the analytic side (for
p
-adic
L
-functions). An equality of the invariants will turn one divisibility coming from the corresponding one in the Heegner Point Main Conjecture into an equality. This in turn implies the equality in Heegner Point Main Conjecture.

Step III above heavily relies on the Eisenstein assumption. By the reducibility of
E[p]
, there is a short exact sequence

(0.2)

0\to\mathbf{F}_{p}(\varphi)\to E[p]\to\mathbf{F}_{p}(\psi)\to 0

for some characters
\varphi
and
\psi
. One can relate the Iwasawa invariants of
f
to those of
\varphi
and
\psi
on both the algebraic side (in terms of Selmer groups) and the analytic side (in term of
p
-adic
L
-functions). Now the Iwasawa Main Conjectures (
[
Rub91
]
&
[
CW78
]
. Also
[
dS87
]
) for the characters bridge the algebraic side and the analytic side. The Heegner Point Main Conjecture, together with Mazur’s control theorem, typically yields the
p
-converse theorem.

In the multiplicative reduction case, one proves the Greenberg’s Main Conjecture using congruence in a Hida family (
[
Ski14
]
). If
f
is a weight
2
newform with
p
dividing its level, there is a Hida family
\{f_{k}\}
of higher weight newforms of good reduction ‘lying above’
f
. By a limiting process, the Greenberg’s Main Conjectures for
f_{k}
’s imply the Greenberg’s Main Conjecture for
f
. One then shows the equivalence between the Greenberg’s Main Conjecture and the Heegner Point Main Conjecture by studying the exceptional behavior of the big logarithm map for
f
(
[
Cas15
]
,
[
KY24
]
).

In this paper, we extend the Kolyvagin system argument to some new cases in the additive reduction setting, and show that, with carefully defined Selmer groups, one could also formulate and prove Heegner Point Main Conjectures as well as control theorems that would suffice to give a
p
-converse theorem. In doing so, one needs to distinguish between three cases (see
section

1
for a quick review of types of additive reduction):

i

the
potentially good ordinary case
;

ii

the
potentially multiplicative case
;

iii

the
potentially supersingular case
.

The first two cases are both considered as
potentially ordinary
and can be studied together. In fact, if
f
is a newform of weight
2
associated to an elliptic curve of potentially ordinary reduction at
p
, then it is a twist of an
p
-ordinary newform with nebentypus, i.e.,
f=\tilde{f}\otimes\varepsilon
for some
p
-ordinary
\tilde{f}
and a finite order character
\varepsilon
, and we will work with a
Heegner pair

(\tilde{f},\chi_{\varepsilon})
that is equivalent to
f
.

In the potentially good ordinary case, we modify the arguments in
[
CGLS22
]
, or rather, a generalization in
[
KY24
]
, to obtain an Iwasawa Main Conjecture over an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field
K
that is not the CM field of
E
(if
E
does not have complex multiplication, we could choose any
K
satisfying certain hypotheses). Roughly speaking, if
\tilde{f}
has good ordinary reduction, then one simply considers everything twisted by
\varepsilon
, and the arguments are not very different from the good ordinary case without twists.

In the potentially multiplicative case, one can hope to work with a twisted Hida family
\{\tilde{f}_{k}\otimes\varepsilon_{k}\}
lying above
f
, where
\{\tilde{f}_{k}\}
is a Hida family lying above
\tilde{f}
. Then
\tilde{f}_{k}\otimes\varepsilon_{k}
are thus automatically twists of good ordinary forms, which makes reasoning analogous to the ordinary case possible. However, due to the lack of tools of studying Perrin-Riou’s regulator map, we will not consider the potentially multiplicative case in this work. Nonetheless, many of our results allow
p
in the level of the ordinary modular forms.

A key step in our arguments is to find a suitable finite extension of
K
where
E
obtains good reduction, and show that climbing up the fields only introduce controllable errors.

The paper is organized as follows:

In
section

1
, we review the reduction types of an elliptic curve, with a focus on different cases of additive reduction. We also discuss reduction types of modular forms for our application.

In
section

2
, we introduce Iwasawa theory, which will be the main tool to prove the main results. We also include a structural but comprehensive proof of the
p
-converse theorem in the good ordinary case.

In
section

3
, we study Iwasawa theory in the potentially good ordinary cases, where we formulate and prove the Heegner Point Main Conjectures, yielding the proofs of the
p
-converse theorems.

0.3.
Relation to previous works

On one hand,
p
-converse theorems are consequences of Iwasawa Main Conjectures. When the analytic, hence algebraic rank of
E/\mathbf{Q}
is
0
or
1
, the Iwasawa Main Conjectures have been studied by several authors in good ordinary case (
[
CGLS22
]
for (residually) reducible,
[
SU14
]
for irreducible) and bad multiplicative case (
[
KY24
]
for reducible,
[
Ski14
]
for irreducible), whether or not a
p
-converse theorem is explicit.

In this work, we prove the
p
-converse theorems for elliptic curves of potentially good ordinary reduction at additive odd primes in the residually reducible case.

On the other hand,
p
-converse theorems see interesting applications in arithmetic statistics, allowing the distribution of certain
Selmer ranks
to control the ranks of the Mordell–Weil groups.

0.4.
Eisenstein primes

Let
f
be the weight
2
newform associated to
E
. In the residually reducible setting, one could take the advantage of the congruence of
f
to an Eisenstein series
G
(by the results of
[
Kri16
]
), and obtain a congruence of the
p
-adic
L
-functions of
f
(constructed in
[
BDP13
]
) to that of
G
. These two
p
-adic
L
-functions
\mathcal{L}_{f}
,
\mathcal{L}_{G}
are carefully studied in
[
CGLS22
]
. The fact that we could compare
\mathcal{L}_{f}
to
\mathcal{L}_{G}
using congruence without the need to compare interpolation properties is what allows us to finish the argument in the bad reduction case. In the non-Eisenstein case, however, one seems unable to argue without comparing interpolation properties directly, which make the situations much more mysterious.

0.5.
Potentially multiplicative reduction

Recall that our proof of the Heegner Point Main Conjectures is divided into three steps:

•

I. Proving one-side divisibility;

•

II. Establishing the explicit reciprocity law;

•

III. Comparing Iwasawa invariants

Steps I and III can be easily extended to the potentially multiplicative case. However, in proving step II we need to use the results from
[
JLZ21
]
, which do no work any more. Indeed, they need to assume
p
is split in the chosen imaginary quadratic field
K
, while if
f=\tilde{f}\otimes\varepsilon
is a potentially multiplicative form with
\varepsilon_{f}=\mathbf{1}=\varepsilon_{\tilde{f}}
,
\varepsilon
must be a quadratic character such that
p\mid{\operatorname{cond}}(\varepsilon)
. Such a character exactly corresponds to imaginary quadratic fields where
p
does not split.

It is mentioned in
[
JLZ21
]
that a analog of their results which allows non-split
p
might be achievable by possible extensions of the work in
[
AI19
]
. If so,
p
-converse theorems for potentially multiplicative reduction should also be within reach, which we hope to examine in future work. For future use, some of our results also cover potentially multiplicative case.

0.6.
Higher weight modular forms

The goal of this paper is to prove the
p
-converse theorems for elliptic curves at primes of potentially good ordinary reduction. However, in the potential use of Hida theory to treat the potentially multiplicative case in weight
2
, it is natural to study twists of good ordinary forms of higher weights (corresponding to ‘Heegner pairs’). It should be mentioned that the ‘twists’ will in general no longer be classical modular forms since these characters will have infinite order. But we can think of these twists as self-dual twist on the level of Galois representations. Some of our arguments still work for general Heegner pairs.

On the other hand, a modular form of weight greater than
2
of multiplicative reduction is no longer ordinary. Therefore we will not consider those situations.

0.7.
Applications.

We would get better proportions of elliptic curves in certain families that satisfy the BSD rank conjecture and provide new evidence towards Goldfeld’s Conjecture. See
Corollary

3.8.1
.

1.
Primes of additive reduction

In this section we review some fundamental results on the reduction types of elliptic curves and modular forms at a prime
p
.

1.1.
Reduction types of elliptic curves

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbf{Q}
and
\tilde{E}
be its reduction modulo
p
. If
\tilde{E}
is non-singular, we say that
E
has good reduction at
p
, otherwise
E
has bad reduction at
p
. Suppose first that
E
has good reduction at
p
. Let
a_{p}=p+1-\#\tilde{E}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
. We say that
E
has
(good) ordinary
reduction at
p
if
p\nmid a_{p}
, and
E
is said to have
(good) supersingular
reduction at
p
otherwise. One knows that if
E
has supersingular reduction at
p
, then
E[p]
must be an irreducible
G_{\mathbf{Q}}
-module.

Now suppose
E
has bad reduction. Then
\tilde{E}
would have a unique singular point
P
. If
P
is nodal, we say
E
has
(bad) multiplicative
reduction at
p
. If
p
is cuspidal, we say
E
has
(bad) additive
reduction at
p
. In the multiplicative case, we have
a_{p}=\pm 1
, so multiplicative reduction is also considered ordinary.

The
L
-function of
E
is formally defined as an Euler product of local factors at every prime:

L(E,s)\coloneq\prod_{p\ \text{good}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{p\ \text{bad}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}

where
a_{p}\in\{0,\pm 1\}
depending on the reduction type modulo bad primes
p\mid N
.

We also defined the conductor
N_{E/\mathbf{Q}}
of
E/\mathbf{Q}
to be

N_{E/\mathbf{Q}}=\prod_{p}p^{f_{p}},

where

f_{p}=\begin{cases}0&\text{$E$ has good reduction at $p$}\\
1&\text{$E$ has multiplicative reduction at $p$}\\
2&\text{$E$ has additive reduction at $p>3$}\\
2+\delta_{p}&\text{$E$ has additive reduction at $p=2,3$},\end{cases}

where
\delta_{p}\geq 0
is a technical constant.

1.2.
Reduction types of modular forms

By modularity theorem, every elliptic curve is associated with a weight
2
newform
f(z)=\sum_{n}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N_{f}))^{{\operatorname{new}}}
where
q=e^{2\pi iz}
in the sense that their
L
functions agree:

L(E,s)=L(f,s)\coloneq\sum_{n}a_{n}n^{-s}.

The conductor
N_{E}
of the elliptic curve agrees with the level
N_{f}
of the newform. We could then decompose
N_{f}
into a product
N_{{\operatorname{mult}}}N_{{\operatorname{add}}}
such that
\ell\mid N_{{\operatorname{mult}}}
implies
\ell
is of multiplicative reduction and
\ell\mid N_{{\operatorname{add}}}
implies
\ell
is of additive reduction. In general, if we have a modular form
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))^{{\operatorname{new}}}
, we say
p
is a good prime for
f
if
p\nmid N
, that
p
is a multiplicative prime for
f
if
p\|N
and that
p
is an additive prime for
f
if
p^{2}\mid N
.

Following
[
Kat04
]
, we can also talk about potential good reduction for a modular form.

Definition 1.2.1

(Remark 12.7 in
[
Kat04
]
)
.

There exists a finite extension
K
of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
having the following properties. For any finite place
v
of
F
which does not lie over
p
, the representation of
{\operatorname{Gal}}(\overline{K}/K)
on
V_{F_{v}}(f)
is unramified. For any finite place
v
of
F
which lies over
p
, the representation of
{\operatorname{Gal}}(\overline{K}/K)
on
V_{F_{v}}(f)
is crystalline.

1.3.
Primes of additive reduction

When
E/\mathbf{Q}
has additive reduction at a prime
p
and is viewed as an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
, one could enlarge the field so that
E
can gain good reduction or multiplicative reduction over some finite extension of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
. For this reason, additive reduction as sometimes referred to as
unstable
reduction. On the other hand, good reduction and multiplicative reduction do not change when extending the ground field, so they are both called
semistable
reduction.

When
E
has complex multiplication, it has potentially good reduction everywhere.

1.4.
Field of semistable reduction

Most of the time, we do not need to make a specific choice of an extension
L_{u}/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
where
E
gains semistable reduction. However, it is convenient to record what can be said about such extensions. We have the following result from
[
Con
]
.

Proposition 1.4.1

(Proposition 6.5 in
[
Con
]
)
.

Pick
N\geq 3
not divisible by
p
. Then
E
acquires semistable reduction at all places of the finite Galois splitting field
\mathbf{Q}(E[N])/\mathbf{Q})
of
A[N]
.

Lemma 1.4.2
.

There is a prime
q\geq 3
of good reduction for
E
such that
E
acquires good reduction over
\mathbf{Q}_{p}(E[q])/\mathbf{Q}_{p}

Proof.

If

, one can choose

and
{\operatorname{Gal}}(\mathbf{Q}(E[q])/\mathbf{Q})
is a subgroup of
{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{3})
, which has order

. In particular, one can assume there is a finite extension

with a place

so that

gains semistable reduction over

with

.

When

, then there is a prime
3<q\not\equiv 1\pmod{3}
of good reduction for
E
such that
E
acquires good reduction over
\mathbf{Q}_{3}(E[q])/\mathbf{Q}_{3}
. Note that this is a solvable extension with Galois group

isomorphic to a subgroup of
{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{q})
. Hence by
[
KS23
, Theorem 2.14]
and since
{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{q})

(a)

contained in a Borel subgroup,

(b)

contained in a normalizer of a (split or non-split) Cartan subgroup, or

(c)

exceptional, i.e., having projective image in

.

We prove that we can find a prime

as above such that
3\nmid\#G
.

(a)

In this case

because of our assumption on
q
.

(b)

The normalizers of a Cartan have order dividing

3

, and

in the non-split case. But if

\overline{\rho}_{3}(G_{\mathbf{Q}_{q}})

: If
E
is not CM, the density of ordinary primes is
1
, so we are good. If
E
has CM by an imaginary quadratic field
K
, then we just need

. But if
K=\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
,

is isogenous to

.

(c)

This cannot happen for

a prime of good reduction according to
[
KS23
, Proposition 2.45]
. ∎

2.
Iwasawa Theory

In this section, we discuss some important tools from Iwasawa theory. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbf{Q}
and
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))^{\operatorname{new}}
be the weight
2
newform associated to
E
. Let
p
be a prime number. Throughout the paper,
K
denotes an imaginary quadratic field satisfying the following hypotheses:

Assumption 2.0.1
.

(i)

p=v\bar{v}
is split in
K
;

(ii)

K
satisfy the
Heegner hypothesis
, i.e., every prime
\ell\mid N
is split in
K
;

(iii)

the discriminant
D_{K}
of
K
is
<-3
and odd.

Let
K_{\infty}/K
be the anticyclotomic
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
and denote by
\Gamma
its Galois group
\Gamma\coloneq{\operatorname{Gal}}(K_{\infty}/K)\cong\mathbf{Z}_{p}
. For each
n\in\mathbf{N}
, let
K_{n}\subset K_{\infty}
be the subfield with
[K_{n}:K]=p^{n}
. The Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda\coloneq\mathbf{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
can be identified with a formal power series ring
\mathbf{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
be sending a topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
to
1+T
.

For a torsion
\Lambda
-module
M
, one knows that there is a
pseudo-isomorphism
(i.e., a
\Lambda
-morphism with finite kernel and cokernel)

M\sim\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(f_{i}^{k_{i}})\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(p^{l_{j}})

where each
f_{i}
is an irreducible
distinguished polynomial
with
f_{i}\equiv T^{\deg(f_{i})}\pmod{p}
. We define the
\lambda,\mu
-invariants of

to be

\lambda(M)\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}\deg(f_{i}),

\mu(M)\coloneq\sum_{j=1}^{t}l_{j}.

The
characteristic ideal

\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(M)
of
M
is the
\Lambda
-ideal generated by the
characteristic polynomial

f_{\Lambda}(M)\coloneq p^{\mu(M)}\prod_{i=1}^{s}f_{i}^{k_{i}}.

Example 2.0.2
.

If
M_{1},M_{2}
are two
\Lambda
-torsion modules with
\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(M_{1})\subset\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(M_{2})
, then
\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(M_{1})=\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(M_{2})
if and only if
\lambda(M_{1})=\lambda(M_{2})
and
\mu(M_{1})=\mu(M_{2})
.

2.1.
Selmer structures and Kolyvagin systems

To state the Iwasawa Main Conjectures from which our
p
-converse theorem follows, we first need to introduce suitable
Selmer groups
. They generalize the usual Selmer groups
{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)
and
S_{p}(E/K)
in the introduction. The following discussions are taken from
[
CGLS22
, section 3.1]
.

2.1.1.
Selmer structures

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete Noetherian local ring with field of fractions of characteristic
0
and with finite residue field of characteristic
p
, and let
M
be a topological
R[G_{K}]
-module such that the
G_{K}
-action is unramified outside a finite set of primes. A
Selmer structure

\mathcal{F}
on
M
is finite set
\Sigma=\Sigma(\mathcal{F})
of places of
K
containing
\infty
, the primes above
p
and the places where
M
is ramified, together with a choice of
R
-submodules (called
local conditions
)
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(K_{w},M)\subset\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},M)
for every
w\in\Sigma
. The associated
Selmer group
is then defined as

\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(K,M)\coloneq\ker\Bigl\{\text{{H}}^{1}(K^{\Sigma}/K,M)\to\prod_{w\in\Sigma}\frac{\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},M)}{\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(K_{w},M)}\Bigr\}.

where
K^{\Sigma}
is the maximal extension of
K
unramified outside
\Sigma
.

Some local conditions we will see frequently are the following:

•

The
strict
(resp.
relaxed
) local condition:
\text{{H}}^{1}_{{\operatorname{str}}}(K_{w},M)\coloneq 0
(resp.
\text{{H}}^{1}_{{\operatorname{rel}}}(K_{w},M)\coloneq\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},M)
);

•

The
unramified
local condition:
\text{{H}}^{1}_{{\operatorname{nr}}}(K_{w},M)\coloneq\ker(\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},M)\to\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w}^{{\operatorname{nr}}},M))
where
K_{w}^{{\operatorname{nr}}}
is the maximal unramified extension of
K_{w}
.

When
M
is unramified at a prime
w\nmid p
, we also called the unramified local condition the
finite
local condition
\text{{H}}^{1}_{f}(K_{w},M)\coloneq\text{{H}}^{1}_{{\operatorname{nr}}}(K_{w},M)
. The
singular quotient
is defined by the exact sequence

0\to\text{{H}}^{1}_{f}(K_{w},M)\to\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},M)\to\text{{H}}^{1}_{s}(K_{w},M)\to 0.

Let
\mathscr{L}_{0}\coloneq\mathscr{L}_{0}(M)
be the set of degree
2
rational primes (that is, inert in
K
) of
\ell\neq p
at which
M
is ramified. For
K[\ell]
the ring class field of conductor
\ell
, we define the
transverse
local condition at
\lambda\mid\ell\in\mathscr{L}_{0}
by

\text{{H}}^{1}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(K_{\lambda},M)\coloneq\ker(\text{{H}}^{1}(K[\ell]_{\lambda},M)\to\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{\lambda^{\prime}},M))

where
K[\ell]_{\lambda^{\prime}}
is the completion of
K[\ell]
at any prime
\lambda
above
\ell
.

Lastly, given a submodule
N
(resp. quotient) of
M
and a local condition
\mathcal{F}
on
M
, we define the
propagated
local condition on
N
, still denoted by
\mathcal{F}
, to be the preimage (resp. image) of
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(K_{v},M)
under the natural map

\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{v},N)\to\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{v},M)

(resp.
\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{v},M)\to\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{v},N)
).

As in
[
How04
]
, we call a
Selmer triple

(M,\mathcal{F},\mathscr{L})
the data of a Selmer structure
\mathcal{F}
on
M
and a subset
\mathscr{L}\subset\mathscr{L}_{0}
with
\mathscr{L}\cap\Sigma(\mathcal{F})=\emptyset
. Given a Selmer triple
(M,\mathcal{F},\mathscr{L})
and given pairwise coprime integers
a,b,c
divisible only by primes in
\mathscr{L}_{0}
, we define the modified Selmer group
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}^{a}_{b}(c)}(K,M)
by choosing
\Sigma(\mathcal{F}^{a}_{b}(c))=\Sigma(\mathcal{F})\cup\{w\mid abc\}
and the local conditions

\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}^{a}_{b}(c)}(K_{\lambda},M)=\begin{cases}\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{\lambda},M)&\text{if }\lambda\mid a\\
0&\text{if }\lambda\mid b\\
\text{{H}}^{1}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(K_{\lambda},M)&\text{if }\lambda\mid c\\
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(K_{\lambda},M)&\text{if }\lambda\nmid abc\\
\end{cases}

Definition 2.1.1
.

Let
{\operatorname{Quot}}(M)
denote the
quotient category
of
M
whose objects are quotients
M/IM
of
M
by ideals
I
of
R
and whose morphisms from
M/IM
to
M/I^{\prime}M
are the maps induced by scalar multiplication
r\in R
with
rI\subset I^{\prime}
.

A local condition functorial over
{\operatorname{Quot}}(M)
is called
Cartesian
if for any injective morphism
N\to M
the local condition
\mathcal{F}
on
N
is the same as the local condition obtained by propagation from
M
to
N
.

Remark 2.1.2
.

By
[
MR04
, Lemma 1.1.9]
, the unramified local condition is Cartesian.

2.1.2.
Kolyvagin systems

From now on let
T
be a compact
R
-module with a continuous linear
G_{K}
-action that is unramified outside a finite set of primes. For each
\lambda\mid\ell\in\mathscr{L}_{0}=\mathscr{L}_{0}(T)
, let
I_{\ell}
be the smallest ideal containing
\ell+1
for which the Frobenius element
{\operatorname{Frob}}_{\lambda}\in G_{K_{\lambda}}
acts trivially on
T/I_{\ell}T
. By class field theory,
\lambda
splits completely in the Hilbert class field of
K
, and the
p
-Sylow subgroups of
G_{\ell}\coloneq{\operatorname{Gal}}(K[\ell]/K[1])
and
\mathbf{k}_{\lambda}^{\times}/\mathbf{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
are identified via the Artin symbol, where
\mathbf{k}_{\lambda}
is the residue field of
\lambda
. Hence by
[
MR04
, Lemma 1.2.1]
, there is a
finite-singular comparison isomorphism

\varphi_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{fs}}\coloneq\text{{H}}^{1}_{f}(K_{\lambda},T/I_{\ell}T)\cong T/I_{\ell}T\cong\text{{H}}^{1}_{s}(K_{\lambda},T/I_{\ell}T)\otimes G_{\ell}

Given a subset
\mathscr{L}\subset\mathscr{L}_{0}
, let
\mathscr{N}
denote the set of square-free products of primes
\ell\in\mathscr{L}
, and for each
n\in\mathscr{N}
, define

I_{n}\coloneq\sum_{\ell\mid n}I_{\ell}\subset R,\ \ \ G_{n}\coloneq\bigotimes_{\ell\mid n}G_{\ell},

with the convention that
1\in\mathscr{N}
,
I_{1}=0
and
G_{1}=\mathbf{Z}
.

Definition 2.1.3
.

A
Kolyvagin system
for a Selmer triple
(T,\mathcal{F},\mathscr{L})
is a collection of classes

\kappa\coloneq\{\kappa_{n}\in\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}(n)}(K,T/I_{n}T)\otimes G_{n}\}_{n\in\mathscr{N}}

such that
(\varphi_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{fs}}\otimes\mathbf{1})({\operatorname{loc}}_{\lambda}(\kappa_{n}))={\operatorname{loc}}_{\lambda}(\kappa_{n\ell})
for all
n\ell\in\mathscr{N}
.

We denote by
\textbf{KS}(T,\mathcal{F},\mathscr{L})
the
R
-module of Kolyvagin systems for
(T,\mathcal{F},\mathscr{L})
.

2.2.
The Iwasawa Main Conjectures

In this section we introduce some Iwasawa Main Conjectures that will be needed in the proof of the
p
-converse theorems. We also discuss some strategies in proving known cases and how they could be adapted to new cases.

2.2.1.
The Heegner Point Main Conjecture

When
E
has good ordinary reduction at a prime
p
, several Iwasawa Main Conjectures have been formulated and proved in e.g.
[
CGLS22
]
. The one that serves as a key ingredient in the proof of
p
-converse theorems is the following Heegner Point Main Conjecture first formulated by Perrin-Riou
[
PR87
]
.

Fix a modular parametrization

\pi\colon X_{0}(N)\to E.

Then the Kummer images of Heegner points on
X_{0}(N)
over ring class fields of
K
of
p
-power conductor give rise to a class
\kappa^{\operatorname{Heeg}}\in S\coloneq\varprojlim S_{p}(E/K_{n})
. The group
S
is naturally a
\Lambda
-module and the class
\kappa^{\operatorname{Heeg}}
is known to be non-
\Lambda
-torsion by results of Cornut and Vatsal
[
Cor02
]
,
[
Vat03
]
. We put
X\coloneq{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}(\varinjlim{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{n}),\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p})
.

Conjecture 2.2.1

(The Heegner Point Main Conjecture)
.

Let
E/\mathbf{Q}
be an elliptic curve and
p>2
be a prime of good ordinary reduction, and let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field satisfying the Heegner hypothesis. Then both
S
and
X
have
\Lambda
-rank one, and

\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(X_{\operatorname{tors}})=\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(S/\Lambda\cdot\kappa^{\operatorname{Heeg}})^{2},

where
X_{\operatorname{tors}}
denote the
\Lambda
-torsion submodule of
X
.

Under
Assumption

2.0.1
, the above conjecture is now a theorem by combined results of
[
CGLS22
]
,
[
CGS23
]
and
[
KY24
]
in the Eisenstein case. The proof uses a variation of Kolyvagin system arguments systematically studied in
[
How04
]
(for the ‘
\supset
’-divisibility) and a comparison of Iwasawa invariants on both sides to turn the divisibility into an equality. To perform the comparison, one needs to go to a different yet equivalent type of Iwasawa Main Conjecture, the
Greenberg’s Iwasawa Main Conjecture
.

2.2.2.
The Greenberg’s Main Conjecture

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{E}
be the modified Selmer group (called the
(Greenberg’s) unramified Selmer group
) obtained from
{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})\coloneq\varinjlim{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{n})
by relaxing (resp. imposing triviality) at places above
v
(resp.
\overline{v}
). Let
\mathfrak{X}_{E}\coloneq{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}(\mathfrak{S}_{E},\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p})
be its Pontryagin dual. From the work of Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna, there is a
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{E}\coloneq\mathcal{L}_{f}\in\Lambda^{\operatorname{ur}}
interpolating the central values of the
L
-function of
f/K
twisted by certain characters of
\Gamma
of infinite order. Here
\Lambda^{\operatorname{nr}}\coloneq\Lambda\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\operatorname{nr}}
where
\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\operatorname{nr}}
is the completion of the ring of integers of the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
.

Conjecture 2.2.2

(Greenberg’s Iwasawa Main Conjecture)
.

Let
E/\mathbf{Q}
be an elliptic curve and
p>2
a prime of good ordinary reduction for
E
. Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field satisfying the Heegner hypothesis where
p
splits. Then
\mathfrak{X}_{E}
is
\Lambda
-torsion, and

\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{X}_{E})\Lambda^{\operatorname{nr}}=(\mathcal{L}_{E})

as ideals in
\Lambda^{\operatorname{nr}}
.

As is already observed in
[
Cas17
, Appendix A]
(see also
[
BCK21
, Theorem 5.2]
), in the good ordinary setting, the Greenberg’s Main Conjecture is equivalent to the Heegner Point Main Conjecture, and is therefore also a theorem now by the results of the aforementioned authors in the Eisenstein case. The proof uses an explicit congruence of
f
to a certain Eisenstein series
G
and compares the algebraic side (the Selmer groups for
f
and those for the two characters appearing in the semisimplification of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
) and the analytic side (the interpolation properties of the
p
-adic
L
-function of
G
(hence that of
f
) to those of the same characters).

2.3.
Prototype: Iwasawa Main Conjectures with good ordinary reduction

2.4.
A control theorem

Another ingredient we need in proving the

2.5.
Proof of the
p
-converse theorems

We now explain how our
p
-converse theorems would follow from the Heegner Point Main Conjecture, at least in the good ordinary case (see
[
CGLS22
, Theorem 5.2.1]
for a reference). In fact, we only need one divisibility. We first recall a theorem of Kolyvagin, which works for any prime.

Theorem 2.5.1

(Kolyvagin)
.

Let
E/\mathbf{Q}
be an elliptic curve and
p
be a prime. Let
r\in\{0,1\}
. Then

{\operatorname{ord}}_{s=1}L(E/\mathbf{Q},s)=r\ \Rightarrow\ {\operatorname{corank}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbf{Q})=r.

Here
{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbf{Q})
is the usual

Selmer group defined similarly as in
section

0.2
with
K
replaced by
\mathbf{Q}
(that definition makes sense for any number field).

The proof of the
p
-converse theorem relies on a choice of an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field

over which

does not have CM and the anticyclotomic Iwasawa Main Conjectures holds.

Proof of the
p
-converse in good ordinary case.

Again let
r\in\{0,1\}
.

When
{\operatorname{corank}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbf{Q})=r
, we could choose a
K
which satisfies
Assumption

2.0.1
such that
{\operatorname{ord}}_{s=1}L(E^{K}/\mathbf{Q},s)=1-r
, where
E^{K}
is the twist of
E
by
K
. We also assume that
E
does not have CM by
K
. Then by
Theorem

2.5.1
,
{\operatorname{corank}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E^{K}/\mathbf{Q})=1-r
. It then follows that
{\operatorname{corank}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)=1
.
Theorem

2.4.1
then implies that
{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})^{\Gamma}
also has
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-corank
1
, or equivalently,
X_{\Gamma}
has
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-rank
1
. Indeed, from the discussion after
[
How04
, Theorem A]
, there is an pseudo-isomorphism from
{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})^{\vee}
to

. On the other hand, from
Theorem

2.3.2
one has

{\operatorname{rk}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}X_{\Gamma}={\operatorname{rk}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\Lambda_{\Gamma}+2{\operatorname{corank}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}M_{\Gamma}=1+2{\operatorname{corank}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}M_{\Gamma},

so

must be finite.

This is equivalent to
T\nmid f_{\Lambda}(M)
, so by
Theorem

2.3.9
(in fact, the divisibility ‘

T\nmid f_{\Lambda}(\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}}(K,\mathbf{T})/\Lambda\kappa_{\infty})
, which in turn means

is finite, so
\kappa_{\infty}
is non-torsion.

There is an injection
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}}(K,\mathbf{T})_{\Gamma}\hookrightarrow S_{p}(E/K)
coming from taking first cohomology of the short exact sequence

It then follows that

has non-torsion image in

.
∎

3.
Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves at primes of potentially ordinary reduction

In this section we treat the case where
E/\mathbf{Q}
is an elliptic curve and
p
is a prime of potentially good ordinary reduction for
E
. We let
L
be a finite extension of
K
where
E
gains good reduction, and
K
is an imaginary quadratic field satisfying
Assumption

2.0.1
as before. Some of our arguments also allow potentially multiplicative reduction.

To obtain a
p
-converse theorem in the potentially ordinary case, one would naturally hope to argue as in the good ordinary case (see
section

2.5
), namely, proving an Iwasawa Main Conjecture and appealing to a control theorem. However, several definitions no longer make sense in the bad reduction case, including
{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}_{w}(T_{p}E)
which was defined as the kernel of reduction of the Tate module at a place

in

. In particular, one needs to find a reasonable substitute of the ordinary Selmer group.

Note that one could on the other hand always define, regardless of the reduction type, the Bloch–Kato Selmer group

with the following local conditions:

\text{{H}}^{1}_{\operatorname{BK}}(F_{w},V)=\begin{cases}\text{{H}}^{1}_{\operatorname{ur}}(F_{w},V)&v\nmid p\infty,\\
\ker(\text{{H}}^{1}(F_{w},V)\to\text{{H}}^{1}(F_{w},V\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{cris}\,}))&w\mid p,\\

where
\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{cris}\,}
is Fontaine’s ring of crystalline periods,
F
is any number field and
w
is a prime of
F
, and
V=T_{p}E\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
. One could also defined the Bloch–Kato Selmer groups for
T\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}T_{p}E
and
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}V/T\cong E[p^{\infty}]
via propagation in terms of the short exact sequence

The Bloch–Kato Selmer groups
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\operatorname{BK}}(K,A)
(resp.
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\operatorname{BK}}(K,T)
) agree with the classical Selmer groups
{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)
(resp.
S_{p}(E/K)
). When
E
has ordinary reduction at
p
, they almost agree with the ordinary Selmer groups
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{ord}}}(K,A)
(resp.
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{ord}}}(K,T)
) we defined in
section

2.3
, which were necessary for our arguments. In fact, when
E
has ordinary reduction at a place
w
of
F
above
p
, the local conditions
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\operatorname{BK}}(F_{w},V)
and
\text{{H}}^{1}_{\operatorname{ord}}(F_{w},V)
agree, where, in the multiplicative case (which is necessarily of weight
2
by
[
Ski14
, Lemma 2.1.2]
), the one dimensional subspace
{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V)
is defined so that

is unramified. It is sometimes inconvenient to work with the Bloch–Kato Selmer group directly. We will therefore define some appropriate Selmer groups that turn out to be the same as the Bloch–Kato ones. Then we formulate and prove the Heegner Point Main Conjecture and a control theorem as in the good ordinary case. The
p
-converse theorem will follow as a consequence. First we need to study Galois representations attached to elliptic curves of potentially ordinary reduction.

3.1.

In this subsection, let

. On the other hand, to study a potentially multiplicative form of weight
2
, one would naturally hope to study a twisted Hida family

consisting of twists of good ordinary forms. As we will see shortly, the correct thing one should consider are ‘Heegner pairs’, where in some sense corresponds to self-dual twists of the representations attached to modular forms with nebentypus.

Recall that our fixed imaginary quadratic field

satisfies the Heegner hypothesis, i.e., every prime

is split in

. Since

, we further assume the prime divisors of

are also split in

, so that every prime
\ell\mid N^{\prime}
is also split in

. Hence there exists an ideal

such that

\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{N}^{\prime}\cong\mathbf{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbf{Z}.

Choose a Hecke character
\chi
of infinity type
(0,0)
whose restriction to
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\times}
is
\varepsilon^{-2}
(equivalently, the restriction to
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\times}
is
\varepsilon^{2}=\varepsilon_{\tilde{f}}^{-1}
. See
[
BDP13
, section 4]
). Such a Hecke character exists thanks to
[
JLZ21
, Remark 2.1.2]
.
Now the pair
(\tilde{f},\chi)
is a
Heegner pair
to which one could associate a Heegner point
z_{(\tilde{f},\chi)}
.

The following results from
op. cit.
will be adapted.

Proposition 3.1.2

(Theorem B in
[
JLZ21
]
)
.

Let
(\tilde{f},\chi)
be a pair of an
p
-ordinary form
\tilde{f}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N^{\prime}),\varepsilon^{-2})
and a Hecke character
\chi
of finite type
(\mathfrak{N}^{\prime},\varepsilon^{-2})
and infinity type

. If

to the BDP

up to a unit.

The above result is a generalization of
Proposition

2.3.6

, and will be revisited in

[

]

also covers modular forms of higher weights, where the Hecke characters will also have nonzero infinity type. We will not consider such Heegner pairs in this work.

With the above definition of Heegner pairs, the natural question to ask is how do they relate to our potentially good ordinary form. In general, Hecke characters with fixed central character is not unique (in fact, they can differ by some finite order anticyclotomic characters). The corresponding

-representations are also different. However, their anticyclotomic theory will not the detect the difference. In what follows, we will specifically construct a Heegner pair whose corresponding
G_{K}
-representation agrees with the one for our potentially good ordinary form.

Proposition 3.1.3
.

Let

be a weight
2

. Then there is a Heegner pair

Consider the following commutative diagram

3.2.

In this subsection, we naturally extend the definitions for a
p

Let

G^{\varphi,\psi,N^{\prime}}
(see
[
Kri16
, Remark 32, Theorem 34]
). In other words,
\tilde{f}
is of partial Eisenstein descent in the sense of
op. cit.
.

Similarly, there is also an exact sequence

0\to\mathbf{F}(\varphi\varepsilon)\to\overline{\rho}_{f}\to\mathbf{F}(\psi\varepsilon)\to 0,

from which we obtain a congruence between the non-constant terms of
\tilde{f}\otimes\varepsilon
, which is another newform, and a certain Eisenstein series
G^{\varphi\varepsilon,\psi\varepsilon,N}
. As in
[
CGLS22
, section 2.2]
, this yields a congruence between the BDP
p
-adic
L
-functions

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\equiv(\mathcal{L}_{G^{\varphi\varepsilon,\psi\varepsilon,N}})^{2}(\text{mod }p\Lambda^{\operatorname{ur}}),

and moreover for the Katz
p
-adic
L
-functions
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\psi\varepsilon}
an equality

\mathcal{L}_{G^{\varphi\varepsilon,\psi\varepsilon,N}}=\mathcal{E}^{\iota}_{\varphi\varepsilon,\psi\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}_{\varphi\varepsilon}

for a certain factor introduced in
loc. cit.
. Since
(\varphi\varepsilon)(\psi\varepsilon)=\mathbf{1}
, the same arguments in
[
CGLS22
, Theorem 2.2.2]
show that the Iwasawa invariants on the analytic side are compatible with those on the algebraic side.

Now as in
[
KY24
]
, using the Iwasawa Main Conjectures for the characters proved by Rubin in
[
Rub91
]
(together with
[
CW78
]
. See also
[
dS87
, III.1.10]
), one obtains the following theorem.

Theorem 3.5.1
.

Assume we are in Case (I) of
section

3.1
, i.e.,
p\nmid N^{\prime}
. Assume that
p=v\overline{v}
splits in
K
. Assume that
\text{{H}}^{0}(K,\overline{\rho_{\tilde{f}}\otimes\varepsilon})=0
. Then

\mu(\mathfrak{X})=\mu(\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon})=0\text{ and }\lambda(\mathfrak{X})=\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}).

Consequently,

\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{X})\Lambda^{\operatorname{ur}}=(\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon})\text{ holds in }\Lambda^{\operatorname{ur}},

or equivalently,

\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{X}_{\operatorname{tors}})=\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(\text{{H}}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}}(K,\mathbf{T})/\Lambda\kappa_{\infty})^{2}\text{ holds in }\Lambda.

Remark 3.5.2
.

•

As in
[
KY24
, Theorem 3.0.8]
, the proof of the Main Conjectures heavily rely on the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariants, especially in the situation where

.

•

As in
[
KY24
, Remark 3.0.9]
, the assumption
\text{{H}}^{0}(K,\overline{\rho_{\tilde{f}}\otimes\varepsilon})=0
can be removed from Ribet’s lemma and Perrin-Riou’s formula.

3.6.
Control theorems for potentially good ordinary reduction

In this subsection, we prove the control theorems for potentially good ordinary reduction. The proof is similar to that of
Theorem

2.4.1
, mainly following
[
Gre99
]
. In particular, we assume
V_{\varepsilon}
corresponds to an elliptic curve of potentially ordinary reduction at
p
, so that
\tilde{f}
is a weight
2
newform. In particular, when
E
is an elliptic curve, we have
T_{\varepsilon}=T_{p}E
,
V_{\varepsilon}=T_{p}E\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
and
A_{\varepsilon}=E[p^{\infty}]
.

We begin by noting that the proof will only be different at places above
p
. More precisely, as in the good ordinary case, it suffices to show the map
r_{w}
has finite kernel for each place
w
of
K
, and for
w\nmid p
the argument is the same as those in
Theorem

2.4.1
. On the other hand, the study of
\ker(r_{v})
and
\ker(r_{\overline{v}})
in
[
Gre99
, Lemma 3.4]
makes use of the kernel of reduction
E[p^{\infty}]\to\tilde{E}[p^{\infty}]
where
\tilde{E}
is the reduction of
E
at
v
and
\overline{v}
respectively, which becomes more mysterious in if
E
has additive reduction at
p
. Luckily, for the
p
-converse theorems, we do not need to understand the kernels completely, and it suffices to show they are finite. In fact, we will choose an extension which trivializes
\varepsilon
as before, so that
V_{\varepsilon}
is identified with
V_{\tilde{f}}
over this extension and one could apply the arguments from the ordinary case. It then remains to show climbing up the field only introduces finite errors.

Theorem 3.6.1

(Control theorem)
.

With notations from
Theorem

2.4.1
, in the potentially good ordinary but not ordinary case,
\ker(r_{v})
and
\ker(r_{\overline{v}})
are finite.

Proof.

In our notation, for
w=v,\overline{v}
, the map
r_{w}
reads as

r_{w}\colon\frac{\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},A_{\varepsilon})}{\text{{H}}^{1}_{\operatorname{BK}}(K_{w},A_{\varepsilon})}\to\frac{\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},M_{\varepsilon})}{\text{{H}}^{1}_{\operatorname{BK}}(K_{w},M_{\varepsilon})}.

As in
section

3.2
, the Bloch–Kato local condition for
A
agrees with the twisted ordinary local condition coming from a Heegner pair
(\tilde{f},\chi)
. Similarly, the Bloch–Kato local condition for
M_{E}
can be identified with a twisted ordinary local condition. Therefore the map
r_{w}
is identified with

\frac{\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},A_{\varepsilon})}{p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(K_{w},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))\to\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},V_{\varepsilon})))}\to\frac{\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},M_{\varepsilon})}{{\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(K_{w},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(M_{\varepsilon})))\to\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},M_{\varepsilon}))}.

where
p_{*}
denotes propagation from
\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},V_{\varepsilon})
to
\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},A_{\varepsilon})
. From now on, we simply write
{\mathrm{im}\,}(\text{{H}}^{1}(-,{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(-)))
for images and ignore the codomain when it is clear from the context.

As before, let
L_{u}/K_{w}
be an extension that trivializes
\varepsilon
. We first assume
\tilde{f}
is of good reduction. Then as in the good ordinary case (see
[
Gre99
, Lemma 3.4]
), for
G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\operatorname{Gal}}(L_{u}/K_{v})
, the ‘restricted’ map

r_{u}\colon\frac{\text{{H}}^{1}(L_{u},A_{\varepsilon})^{G}}{p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(L_{u},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))))^{G}}\to\frac{\text{{H}}^{1}(L_{u},M_{\varepsilon})^{G}}{{\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(L_{u},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(M_{\varepsilon}))))^{G}}

has finite kernel.

From snake lemma applied to the maps defining
\ker(r_{w})
and
\ker(r_{u})
, to get finiteness of
\ker(r_{w})
, it suffices to show finiteness of the map

r\colon\frac{\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},A_{\varepsilon})}{p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(K_{w},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))))}\to\frac{\text{{H}}^{1}(L_{u},A_{\varepsilon})^{G}}{p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(L_{u},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))))^{G}}.

Again from snake lemma applied to the commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(t)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(s)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(r)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(K_{w},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},A_{\varepsilon})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},A_{\varepsilon})}{p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(K_{w},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(L_{u},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))))^{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{{H}}^{1}(L_{u},A_{\varepsilon})^{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{\text{{H}}^{1}(L_{u},A_{\varepsilon})^{G}}{p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(L_{u},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))))^{G}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{coker}\,}(t)}
,
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}

it suffices to show both
\ker(s)
and
{\mathrm{coker}\,}(t)
are finite. Now from the inflation-restriction exact sequence,
\ker(s)=\text{{H}}^{1}({\operatorname{Gal}}(L_{u}/K_{v}),A_{\varepsilon}^{G_{L_{u}}})
, which is finite since
{\operatorname{Gal}}(L_{u}/K_{v})
and
A_{\varepsilon}^{G_{L_{u}}}=E(L_{u})[p^{\infty}]
are both finite (the latter by
[
KY24
, Proposition 1.3.4 (iii), weight
2
case]
. One can replace their
K_{w}
by
L_{u}
).

It remains to study
{\mathrm{coker}\,}(t)
. First note that
\ker(t)
is finite because it injects into
\ker(s)
. We will show that
p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(K_{w},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))))
and
p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(L_{u},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))))^{G}
have the same
\mathbf{Z}_{p}

-corank, from which the finiteness of
{\mathrm{coker}\,}(t)
follows.

Since the restriction map
\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))\to\text{{H}}^{1}(L_{u},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))
is an isomorphism of vector spaces (the composition of restriction and corestriction is a scalar multiplication. See for example
[
NSW08
, Corollary 1.5.7]
), they have the same
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-dimensions. We claim that propagation takes
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-dimensions to
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-coranks, i.e., if a subspace of
\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},V_{\varepsilon})
has
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-dimension
d
, then its propagation into
\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},V_{\varepsilon})
has
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-corank
d
.

Let
V^{\prime}
be a
d
-dimensional subspace of
\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},V_{\varepsilon})
, and let
p_{*}(V^{\prime})
be its propagation to
\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},A_{\varepsilon})
. Then there is an exact sequence

0\to\ker(p_{*})\to V^{\prime}\to p_{*}(V^{\prime})\to 0

where
\ker(p_{*})
is a subspace of
\text{{H}}^{1}(K_{w},T_{\varepsilon})
, and is hence a finitely generated
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-module. Denoting the
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-rank, the
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-dimension and the
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-corank of the three terms in the above exact sequence by
r,d,c
respectively, one sees that there must be an identity
r=d=c
.

The same is true for subspaces of
\text{{H}}^{1}(L_{u},V_{\varepsilon})^{G}
since propagation commutes with invariants. Therefore, from the isomorphism in the previous paragraph, both
p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(K_{w},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))))
and
p_{*}({\mathrm{im}\,}(H^{1}(L_{u},{\operatorname{Fil}}^{+}(V_{\varepsilon}))))^{G}
have
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-corank
d
. Thus
{\mathrm{coker}\,}(t)
is finite.

Note that all we use is that over the extension
L_{u}
, the map
r_{u}
has finite kernel, and that the restriction maps only introduce some finite errors. Thus the same arguments work when
\tilde{f}
has multiplicative reduction, by the discussion of the multiplicative reduction in
[
Gre99
, section 3]
, except that the appeal to
[
KY24
, 1.3.4 (ii)]
is now modified as in
Lemma

3.3.3
to fit the multiplicative case.
∎

3.7.
The
p
-converse theorems for potentially good ordinary reduction

Combining everything above, we arrive at the main theorem in this section.

Theorem 3.7.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbf{Q}
and let
p>2
be a prime of potentially good ordinary reduction for
E
. Assume that
E[p]
is reducible. Let
r\in\{0,1\}
. Then

{\operatorname{corank}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}{\operatorname{Sel}}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbf{Q})=r\Rightarrow{\operatorname{ord}}_{s=1}L(E,s)=r,

and so
{\operatorname{rk}}_{\mathbf{Z}}E(\mathbf{Q})=r
and
\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q})<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
(\tilde{f},\chi_{\varepsilon})
be the Heegner pair associated to
E
. As in the good ordinary case, the proof relies on the choice of an imaginary quadratic field
K
. The only difference is that in addition to the hypotheses
K
should verify, we further require that the prime divisors of
{\operatorname{cond}}(\varepsilon)
are split in
K
. As in the proof of
[
CGLS22
, Theorem 5.2.1]
, this only impose a finite number of congruence conditions on
D_{K}
, so the existence of
K
is still guaranteed. By replacing the appeal to
Theorem

2.4.1
(
resp.
Theorem

2.3.2
,
Theorem

2.3.9
) to
Theorem

3.6.1
(
resp.
Theorem

3.3.6
,
Theorem

3.5.1
), the rest of the proof is exactly the same as that in
section

2.5
.
∎

.

We can obtain better proportions of quadratic twists of (algebraic and analytic) rank

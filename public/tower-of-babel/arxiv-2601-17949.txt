On the area-depth symmetry on Łukasiewicz paths
Wenjie Fang

Abstract
In an effort to further understanding q , t q,t -Catalan statistics, a new statistic on Dyck paths called 0 ​ p ​ t 0pt was proposed in Pappe, Paul and Schilling (2022) and was shown to be jointly equi-distributed with the well-known 𝚊𝚛𝚎𝚊 \mathtt{area} statistics. In a recent preprint, Qu and Zhang (2025) generalized 0 ​ p ​ t 0pt to so-called “ k → \vec{k} -Dyck paths”. They showed that 𝚊𝚛𝚎𝚊 \mathtt{area} and 0 ​ p ​ t 0pt are also jointly equi-distributed over such paths with a fixed multiset of up-steps and a given first up-step, and they conjectured that the same holds when also fixing the last up-step. In this short note, we settle this conjecture on the more general context of Łukasiewicz paths by interpreting 𝚊𝚛𝚎𝚊 \mathtt{area} and 0 ​ p ​ t 0pt under the classical bijection between Łukasiewicz paths and plane trees, through which the symmetry is transparent.



1
Introduction

The study of
q,t
-Catalan polynomials
C(q,t)
started from the two combinatorial formulas of the bi-graded Hilbert series of the subspace of alternants in the diagonal coinvariant space with two sets of variables, both describing Dyck paths of a given size with two jointly equi-distributed statistics: with
\mathtt{area}
and
\mathtt{bounce}
by Haglund
[
Hag03
]
, and with
\mathtt{dinv}
and
\mathtt{area}
by Haiman. The two pairs of statistics are related by the famous zeta map
[
And+02
]
(see also
[
CFM20
]
for a simpler definition). For more details, readers are referred to
[
Hag08
]
.

From the algebraic definition, it is trivial that the
q,t
-Catalan polynomials are symmetric in
q
and
t
. However, on the combinatorial side, it is totally mysterious, with no bijective explanation known. In an effort to better understand combinatorially the related statistics, Pappe, Paul and Schilling proposed in
[
PPS22
]
another statistics
0pt
on Dyck paths and found that it is also jointly equi-distributed with
\mathtt{area}
by exhibiting an involution exchanging
\mathtt{area}
and
0pt
. The
q,t
-polynomial
\widetilde{C}(q,t)
defined with
\mathtt{area}
and
0pt
, which is different from the classical
C(q,t)
, is thus also
q,t
-symmetric.

There are many generalizations of the
q,t
-Catalan polynomials, usually defined on generalizations of Dyck paths, sometimes with an algebraic motivation, but not always. For instance, Xin and Zhang considered in
[
XZ23
]
the so-called
\vec{k}
-Dyck paths
, on which they introduced the three classical statistics
\mathtt{dinv}
,
\mathtt{area}
and
\mathtt{bounce}
, even if without direct algebraic supporting background. They then defined a generalized
q,t
-Catalan polynomial
C_{\lambda}(q,t)
on
\vec{k}
-Dyck paths using these generalized statistics and studied their properties. Unfortunately,
C_{\lambda}(q,t)
is not
q,t
-symmetric in general.

In search for generalizations of the
q,t
-Catalan polynomials defined on
\vec{k}
-Dyck paths with better
q,t
-symmetric properties, in a recent preprint
[
QZ25
]
, Qu and Zhang generalized the
0pt
statistics in
[
PPS22
]
to
\vec{k}
-Dyck paths. They defined a generalization of the
\mathtt{area}
-
0pt

q,t
-polynomial
\widetilde{C}(q,t)
to
\vec{k}
-Dyck paths with a given multiset
M
of up-steps, denoted here by
\widetilde{C}_{M}(q,t)
. They also defined variants of
\widetilde{C}_{M}(q,t)
with extra conditions of the first and/or the last up-step. They then showed bijectively that the variant of
\widetilde{C}_{M}(q,t)
fixing the first up-step is
q,t
-symmetric, and they conjectured that it remains
q,t
-symmetric even when also fixing the last up-step. The main result of this short note is a proof of this conjecture, with precise definitions of notations postponed to later sections.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
QZ25
, Conjecture 1.4]
)
.

We define
\widetilde{C}_{a,M,b}(q,t)
as a sum over Łukasiewicz paths
P
with the first (resp. last) up-step of degree
a
(resp.
b
) and
M
the multiset of the degrees of other up-steps:

\widetilde{C}_{a,M,b}(q,t)=\sum_{P}q^{\mathtt{area}(P)}t^{0pt(P)}.

Then
\widetilde{C}_{a,M,b}(q,t)
is symmetric in
q,t
,
i.e.
,
\widetilde{C}_{a,M,b}(q,t)=\widetilde{C}_{a,M,b}(t,q)
.

Our proof relies essentially on the observation that both statistics
\mathtt{area}
and
\mathtt{bounce}
on Łukasiewicz paths can be interpreted naturally over the classical bijection between Łukasiewicz paths and plane trees.

The rest of this article is organized as follows. We lay down the definitions of Łukasiewicz paths, the statistics
\mathtt{area}
and
0pt
and the related polynomials in
Figure

1
. Then we revisit the classical bijection between Łukasiewicz paths and plane trees in
3.1
, along with how
\mathtt{area}
and
0pt
are transferred to plane trees. Finally, in
4.1
we show how to obtain
q,t
-symmetry results using simple bijections in a transparent way.

2
Łukasiewicz paths and its statistics

Łukasiewicz paths
are lattices paths in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with steps of the form
(1,k)
with
k\geq-1
that go under the
x
-axis only at the last step, which is always
(1,-1)
. We denote by
\mathcal{L}
the set of Łukasiewicz paths.
Figure

1
shows an example of Łukasiewicz paths. For the ease of notation, we denote by
D
the only down-step
(1,-1)
, and
U_{k}
the up-step
(1,k)
for
k\geq 0
, and we say that
U_{k}
is of
degree

k
. The
degree profile
(or simply
profile
) of a Łukasiewicz path
P
, denoted by
\mathtt{Pr}(P)
, is the sequence of degrees of up-steps in
P
from left to right. In this case, the
profile multiset
of
P
, denoted by
\mathtt{M}(P)
is the multiset of entries in
\mathtt{Pr}(P)
. For a finite multiset
M
of natural numbers, we denote by
M_{k}
the multiplicity of
k
in
M
, and we write
M=[0^{M_{0}},1^{M_{1}},2^{M_{2}},\dots]
while only listing elements in
M
. We note that
\vec{k}
-Dyck paths, the family of paths studied in
[
QZ25
]
, first introduced in
[
XZ19
]
, are simply Łukasiewicz paths with
\mathtt{Pr}(P)=\vec{k}
without any
0
, with the last down-step removed.

Figure 1:
Example of a Łukasiewicz path, with its statistics
\mathtt{area}
and
0pt
.

We now introduce the statistics we consider on Łukasiewicz paths, which is a simple generalization of those in
[
QZ25
]
for
\vec{k}
-Dyck paths.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a Łukasiewicz path
P
, its
area vector

\mathtt{Area}(P)
is the vector whose
i
-th entry is the
y
-coordinate of the
i
-th up-step of
P
. The
area
of
P
, denoted by
\mathtt{area}(P)
, is the sum of all entries in
\mathtt{Area}(P)
. See
Figure

1
for an example.

Definition 2.2
.

In a Łukasiewicz path
P
, we associate each down-step
P_{j}=D
except the last one with an up-step
P_{i}=U_{k}
with
k>0
such that, when drawing a horizontal ray from the midpoint of
P_{j}
to the left, the first non-horizontal up-step it hits in the middle is
P_{i}
. In this case, we say that
P_{j}
is
matched
with
P_{i}
. An up-step
P_{j}=U_{k}
has exactly
k
matching down-steps
P_{j_{1}},\dots,P_{j_{k}}
with
j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}
, and we say that
P_{j_{i}}
is the
i
-th matching down-step of
P_{j}
. We then associate to each step
P_{i}
a value
d_{i}
as follows:

•

The first step
P_{1}
, which is always an up-step, has value
d_{1}=0
;

•

If
P_{i}=D
, let
P_{j}
be its matching up-step, and suppose that
P_{i}
is the
\ell
-th matching down-step of
P_{j}
, then we take
d_{i}=d_{j}+\ell
;

•

If
P_{i}=U_{k}
with
i>0
, we take
d_{i}=d_{i-1}
.

The
depth vector
of
P
, denoted by
\mathtt{Depth}(P)
, is the vector formed by the values
d_{i}
for all up-steps
P_{i}
from left to right. The
depth
of
P
, denoted by
0pt(P)
, is the sum of all entries in
\mathtt{Depth}(P)
. See
Figure

1
for an example.

Remark 2.3
.

We note that our definition of
0pt
seems different from the one in
[
QZ25
]
, which used two tableaux called “Filling tableau” and “Ranking tableau”, whose columns represent up-steps in a
\vec{k}
-Dyck path
P
. In a word, the Filling tableau of
P
can be seen as associating
k
down-steps to each up-step
P_{i}=U_{k}
of degree
k
by scanning down-steps from left to right and associate each down-step with the closest up-step before it that still needs new down-steps. This is clearly the same as our notion of matching steps. The Ranking tableau pf
P
is then obtained by labeling the first up-step by
0
, then the
i
-th down-step associated to an up-step with label
\ell
is labeled by
\ell+i
, while a remaining up-step is also labeled
\ell
if its previous step is labeled
\ell
. The depth of
P
is the sum of labels of all up-steps. This also agrees with our definition here.

Now we define the
q,t
-polynomial associated to
\mathtt{area}
and
0pt
. We denote the concatenation of two vectors
P,Q
by
P\cdot Q
, and the union of two multisets
L,M
by
L\uplus M
. We further give four notations of subsets of Łukasiewicz paths:

•

\mathcal{L}_{M}
: the set of Łukasiewicz paths
P
with
\mathtt{M}(P)=M
;

•

\mathcal{L}_{a,M}
: the set of Łukasiewicz paths
P
with
U_{a}
as the first up-step, and
\mathtt{M}(P)=M\uplus\{a\}
;

•

\mathcal{L}_{M,b}
: the set of Łukasiewicz paths
P
with
U_{b}
as the last up-step, and
\mathtt{M}(P)=M\uplus\{b\}
;

•

\mathcal{L}_{a,M,b}
: the set of Łukasiewicz paths
P
with
U_{a}
(resp.
U_{b}
) as the first (resp. last) up-step, and
\mathtt{M}(P)=M\uplus\{a,b\}
, with
\{a,b\}
understood as a multiset.

We then define

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{C}_{M}(q,t)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{L}_{M}}q^{\mathtt{area}(P)}t^{0pt(P)},&\quad\widetilde{C}_{a,M}(q,t)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{L}_{a,M}}q^{\mathtt{area}(P)}t^{0pt(P)},\\
\widetilde{C}_{M,b}(q,t)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{L}_{M,b}}q^{\mathtt{area}(P)}t^{0pt(P)},&\quad\widetilde{C}_{a,M,b}(q,t)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{L}_{a,M,b}}q^{\mathtt{area}(P)}t^{0pt(P)}.\end{split}

(1)

We may also define a more refined version of these polynomials. Given a vector
\mathbf{K}
, we define the following polynomial summing over Łukasiewicz paths with profile
\mathbf{K}
.

\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{K}}(q,t)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{L},\mathtt{Pr}(P)=\mathbf{K}}q^{\mathtt{area}(P)}t^{0pt(P)}.

(2)

In the following, the profile vector will always be given in boldface to avoid confusion between the polynomials.

3
Plane trees and
q,t
-statistics

A
plane tree
is rooted trees in which each
internal node
,
i.e.
, a node with at least one child, comes with a left-to-right order of its children. Nodes that are not internal are called
leaves
. We denote by
\mathcal{T}
the set of plane trees. There is a classical bijection between Łukasiewicz paths and plane trees, see,
e.g.
,
[
FS09
, Section I.5.3]
, that we describe below.

Construction 3.1
.

Given a plane tree
T
, we perform a contour walk from left to right, starting from the root, and transcribe the nodes into steps upon their first encounter:
D
if it is a leaf, and
U_{k}
if it is an internal node of degree
k+1
. The lattice path thus obtained, denoted by
\lambda(T)
, is a Łukasiewicz path.

Conversely, given a Łukasiewicz path
P
, starting with an empty positioning called
bud
, we may construct a tree by reading
P
from left to right: when reading an up-step
U_{k}
(resp. down-step
D
), we replace the leftmost empty bud by an internal node with
k+1
empty buds below (resp. a leaf). The plane tree is denoted by
\tau(P)
. It is clear that
\lambda
and
\tau
are bijections between
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{T}
, and are inverse of each other.

Figure 2:
Example of the bijections
\lambda
and
\tau
.

The statistics
\mathtt{area}
and
0pt
on Łukasiewicz paths are transferred naturally to plane trees. We see in
3.1
that up-steps of a Łukasiewicz path
P
from left to right are converted to internal nodes of
T=\tau(P)
in the contour walk order, with
U_{k}
translated to a node of degree
k+1
. In the following, we denote by
u_{i}
the internal node in
T
associated to the
i
-th up-step of
P
.

Definition 3.2
.

Given a plane tree
T
, for an internal node
u
, an edge
e
is called a
left thorn
(resp.
right thorn
) of
u
if
e
is a descending edge of an ancestor
v\neq u
to the left (resp. right) to the path from
v
to
u
. We denote by
\mathtt{lthorn}(u)
(resp.
\mathtt{rthorn}(u)
) the number of left thorns (resp. right thorns) of
u
, and by
\mathtt{lthorn}(T)
(resp.
\mathtt{rthorn}(T)
) the sum of
\mathtt{lthorn}(u)
(resp.
\mathtt{rthorn}(u)
) for all its internal nodes
u
. See
Figure

3
for an illustration.

Figure 3:
(a) Illustration of
\mathtt{lthorn}
and
\mathtt{rthorn}
on an internal node. (b) Example of the
\mathtt{lthorn}
. (c) Example of
\mathtt{rthorn}
.

Comparing the statistics in
Figures

1
and
3
, it seems that
\mathtt{area}
and
0pt
on Łukasiewicz paths are transferred to
\mathtt{rthorn}
and
\mathtt{lthorn}
on plane trees. This is indeed the case.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
P
be a Łukasiewicz path and
T=\tau(P)
. Then
\mathtt{area}(P)=\mathtt{rthorn}(T)
.

Proof.

We show instead the stronger result
\mathtt{Area}(P)_{i}=\mathtt{rthorn}(u_{i})
, with
u_{i}
the internal node of
T
associated to the
i
-th up-step of
P
in
3.1
. Our original claim comes from summing on both sides. We proceed by induction on the distance from the root to
u_{i}
. For distance
0
, we have
i=1
and
u_{1}
is the root. It is clear that
\mathtt{Area}(P)_{1}=0=\mathtt{rthorn}(u_{1})
. For non-zero distance,
u_{i}
is a child of some internal node
u_{j}
, and by induction hypothesis we have
\mathtt{Area}(P)_{j}=\mathtt{rthorn}(u_{j})
. Suppose that
u_{j}
has
k
children, with
u_{i}
the
\ell
-th one. By
3.2
, we have
\mathtt{rthorn}(u_{i})=\mathtt{rthorn}(u_{j})+k-\ell
. Now, in
3.1
, it is clear that between the ending point of the
j
-th up-step of
P
and the starting point of the
i
-th one is a concatenation of
\ell-1
Łukasiewicz paths, each corresponding to the sub-trees of
T
induced by the
\ell-1
children of
u_{j}
to the left of
u_{i}
. As each Łukasiewicz path introduces a variation of
-1
in the
y
-coordinate, we have
\mathtt{Area}(P)_{i}=\mathtt{Area}(P)_{j}+(k-1)-(\ell-1)
. We thus have
\mathtt{Area}(P)_{i}=\mathtt{rthorn}(u_{i})
, by which we conclude the induction.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
P
be a Łukasiewicz path and
T=\tau(P)
. Then
0pt(P)=\mathtt{lthorn}(T)
.

Proof.

We show instead the stronger result
\mathtt{Depth}(P)_{i}=\mathtt{lthorn}(u_{i})
, with
u_{i}
the internal node of
T
associated to the
i
-th up-step of
P
in
3.1
. Our original claim comes from summing on both sides. We proceed by induction on the root distance of
u_{i}
. When
u_{i}
is the root, we have
i=1
, and it is clear that
\mathtt{Depth}(P)_{1}=0=\mathtt{rthorn}(u_{1})
. In the case when
u_{i}
has a parent
u_{j}
, by induction hypothesis we have
\mathtt{Depth}(P)_{j}=\mathtt{lthorn}(u_{j})
. Suppose that
u_{j}
has
k
children, with
u_{i}
the
\ell
-th one. By
3.2
we have
\mathtt{lthorn}(u_{i})=\mathtt{lthorn}(u_{j})+\ell-1
. Now, as in a Łukasiewicz path, the only unmatched down step is the last one, it is clear that the
j
-th up-step of
P
is matched with the down steps giving the rightmost leaf of sub-trees of
T
induced by children of
u_{j}
, except for the last one. As the step preceding the
i
-th up-step of
P
is the down step giving the rightmost leaf of the sub-tree induced by the
(\ell-1)
-st child of
u_{j}
(or the
j
-th up-step if
\ell=1
), by
2.2
, we have
\mathtt{Depth}(P)_{i}=\mathtt{Depth}(P)_{j}+\ell-1
. We thus have
\mathtt{Depth}(P)_{i}=\mathtt{rthorn}(u_{i})
, by which we conclude the induction.
∎

4
Symmetries in
q,t
through trees

Given a plane tree
T
, we denote by
\operatorname{mir}(T)
its image by left-right reflection. The following proposition is clear from
3.2
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For a plane tree
T
, let
T^{\prime}=\operatorname{mir}(T)
, then
\mathtt{lthorn}(T)=\mathtt{rthorn}(T^{\prime})
and
\mathtt{rthorn}(T)=\mathtt{lthorn}(T^{\prime})
.

Proof.

It is clear that a left thorn
e
of a node
u
is a right thorn of
u
in
\operatorname{mir}(T)
.
∎

Now the
q,t
-symmetry of
\widetilde{C}_{a,M}(q,t)
is transparent.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
QZ25
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

For any
a\in\mathbb{N}
and multiset
M
with elements in
\mathbb{N}
, we have
\widetilde{C}_{a,M}(q,t)=\widetilde{C}_{a,M}(t,q)
.

Proof.

Let
P
be a Łukasiewicz path, and
T=\tau(P)
. From
3.1
,
P\in\mathcal{L}_{a,M}
if and only if
T
has a root of degree
a+1
, and the multiset of the degrees of non-root internal nodes is
M^{\prime}=[1^{M_{0}},2^{M_{1}},\dots]
. Thus
P^{\prime}=\lambda(T^{\prime})
with
T^{\prime}=\operatorname{mir}(T)
is also in
\mathcal{L}_{a,M}
if and only if
P\in\mathcal{L}_{a,M}
, meaning that
\lambda\circ\operatorname{mir}\circ\tau
is an involution on
\mathcal{L}_{a,M}
. We then conclude by
Equations

1
,
3.3
,
3.4
and
4.1
.
∎

To show the
q,t
-symmetry of
\widetilde{C}_{a,M,b}(q,t)
, we need an extra involution.

Construction 4.3
.

Given a plane tree
T
, it
left lodestar
(resp.
right lodestar
) is the leftmost (resp. rightmost) internal node whose children are all leaves. Note that the left (resp. right) lodestar of
T
is not necessarily on its leftmost (resp. rightmost) path, and it is possible that both lodestars are the same. We define the
lodestar swap
of
T
, denoted by
\operatorname{swap}(T)
, to be the tree obtained by swapping the left and right lodestars of
T
. See
Figure

4
for an example of the lodestar swap.

Figure 4:
Example of the lodestar swapping map.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
T
be a plane tree and
T^{\prime}=\operatorname{swap}
its lodestar swap. Then
\mathtt{lthorn}(T)=\mathtt{lthorn}(T^{\prime})
and
\mathtt{rthorn}(T)=\mathtt{rthorn}(T^{\prime})
.

Proof.

By
3.2
, the descendant edges of lodestars are never thorns of any internal node, thus their swapping does not affect
\mathtt{lthorn}
and
\mathtt{rthorn}
of any node.
∎

Now we can show our main result
1.1
.

Proof of
1.1
.

Let
P
be a Łukasiewicz path, and
T=\tau(P)
. It is clear from
3.1
that the right lodestar of
T
corresponds to the last up-step of
P
. From
3.1
,
P\in\mathcal{L}_{a,M,b}
if and only if
T
has a root of degree
a+1
, the multiset of the degrees of non-root internal nodes is
M^{\prime}=[1^{M_{0}},2^{M_{1}},\dots]\uplus\{b+1\}
, and the right lodestar of
T
is of degree
b+1
. We take
P^{\prime}=\lambda(T^{\prime})
with
T^{\prime}=\operatorname{swap}(\operatorname{mir}(T))
. It is clear that the left (resp. right) lodestar of
\operatorname{mir}(T)
is from the right (resp. left) lodestar of
T
. Hence, the right lodestar of
T^{\prime}
is from the right lodestar of
T
. We thus know that
P^{\prime}
is also in
\mathcal{L}_{a,M,b}
if and only if
P\in\mathcal{L}_{a,M,b}
, meaning that
\lambda\circ\operatorname{swap}\circ\operatorname{mir}\circ\tau
is an involution on
\mathcal{L}_{a,M,b}
. We then conclude by
Equations

1
,
3.3
,
3.4
,
4.1
and
4.4
.
∎

We have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.5

(
[
QZ25
, Conjecture 1.4]
)
.

For any
b\in\mathbb{N}
and multiset
M
with elements in
\mathbb{N}
, we have
\widetilde{C}_{M,b}(q,t)=\widetilde{C}_{M,b}(t,q)
.

Proof.

It is clear that we may write
\widetilde{C}_{M,b}
as a sum of
\widetilde{C}_{a,M^{\prime},b}
where
a
runs over elements of
M
and
M^{\prime}=M\setminus\{a\}
. We then conclude by
1.1
.
∎

Using our bijections, we also have a transparent proof of the following results.

Proposition 4.6
.

Given a vector
\mathbf{K}
of natural numbers, we have

1.

\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{K}\cdot(a)}(q,t)=\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{K}\cdot(b)}(q,t)
for any
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
;

2.

\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{K}}(q,t)=\widetilde{C}_{\mathbf{K}}(t,q)
when
\mathbf{K}
is of length
3
.

Proof.

For
1
, we observe from the proof of
4.4
that changing the degree of a lodestar does not change either
\mathtt{lthorn}
or
\mathtt{rthorn}
. We thus conclude by the fact that the right lodestar of
T
is always the rightmost internal node in the contour walk order.

For
2
, we note that a plane tree
T
with
\mathtt{Pr}(T)=\mathbf{K}
has only three internal nodes, one of them the root. There are only two possibilities: either the two remaining internal nodes are siblings, or one is the parent of the other. For both cases, the profile is invariant under
\operatorname{swap}\circ\operatorname{mir}
. We thus conclude in the same way as in the proof of
1.1
.
∎

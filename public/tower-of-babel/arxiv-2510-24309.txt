Pair-breaking as the fundamental limit to persistent-current stabilization in fermionic superfluids
Buğra Tüzemen, Andrea Barresi, Gabriel Wlazłowski, Piotr Magierski, Klejdja Xhani

Abstract
We study the stability of persistent currents in fermionic superfluids with impurities within the BCS regime by using time-dependent density functional theory. Unlike in Bose–Einstein condensates, we find that current stabilization by impurities is intrinsically limited by the pair-breaking threshold. Below the threshold, impurities enhance winding number stability, but pair-breaking continues to drive dissipation of the flow. Above this critical velocity, superflow destabilizes, emitting vortices. Impurities then govern vortex mobility and pinning, exhibiting regimes of collective pinning and hopping. Moreover, pinned vortices do not guarantee dissipationless flow due to ongoing pair-breaking. Our results identify pair breaking as the fundamental mechanism that sets the ultimate limit of persistent-current stability in fermionic superfluids, providing insights relevant to ultracold Fermi gases and neutron-star matter.



Introduction.
—
The flow of a Fermi superfluid through a spatially inhomogeneous medium plays a central role in many physical systems and poses several unresolved theoretical challenges. In such environments, the superfluid fraction is generally reduced below unity, implying that only part of the fluid participates in coherent, collective motion
Leggett 1970
;
Orso and Stringari 2024
;
Saslow 1976
;
Galli et al. 2007
;
Biroli et al. 2011
;
Pérez-Cruz et al. 2025
. The remaining fraction, effectively decoupled from the superflow, is expected to behave as a normal component even at zero temperature, although it may not contribute to entropy production. Whether this decoupling occurs depends sensitively on both the nature of the inhomogeneities and the velocity of the superfluid. A systematic classification of the dynamical regimes arising under these conditions remains an open problem.

Impurities play a crucial role in superfluid dynamics. Much like in classical fluids, they can give rise to dissipative processes. In Bose superfluids, impurities often serve as nucleation sites for quantum vortices
Sasaki et al. 2010
;
Christenhusz et al. 2025
, which are the primary agents coupling the superfluid and normal components and enabling energy transfer between them. For instance, the Hall-Vinen-Bekarevich-Khalatnikov model (a generalization of Landau’s two-fluid theory) requires both a superfluid fraction below unity and the presence of quantized vorticity for dissipation to occur during the dynamics (see
Barenghi et al. 2006
for reviews). Thus, impurities influence superfluid behavior in two fundamental ways: first, their mere presence effectively reduces the superfluid fraction; second, by seeding vortices, they enable the transfer of flow energy into the normal component, which subsequently dissipates it into internal excitations.

Ultracold atomic gases provide a tunable platform for studying superfluid transport. In two-dimensional annular or toroidal traps, persistent currents arise with quantized circulation determined by the winding number
w

Ryu et al. 2007
;
Cai et al. 2022
;
Pecci et al. 2021
;
Del Pace et al. 2022
;
Polo et al. 2025
. In Bose–Einstein condensates (BECs), impurities or barriers can nucleate quantized vortices
Ramanathan et al. 2011
;
Williams et al. 2010
;
Wright et al. 2013
;
Kwon et al. 2015
;
Gan et al. 2025
;
Pezzè et al. 2024
;
Xhani et al. 2023
;
Del Pace et al. 2022
;
Moulder et al. 2012
;
Kumar et al. 2017
, leading to phase slips and the decay of superflow. Interestingly, recent studies have shown that introducing a controlled number of weak links (Josephson junctions) can, paradoxically, stabilize the superflow by increasing the critical current required for decay
Pezzè et al. 2024
. This effect arises due to the reduction of phase jumps or maximum velocity at the weak-links with increasing junction number. Hence, engineered weak links can suppress phase slips and enhance the metastability of persistent currents.

An open question is whether this behavior is a universal feature of all superfluids or specific to BECs. Fermi superfluids constitute a distinct class of systems characterized by additional complexity. Unlike BECs, superfluidity in these systems arises from the formation of Cooper pairs, which become fragile as one approaches the so-called BCS regime: a regime of weak interactions. Pair-breaking processes reduce the superfluid fraction and can trigger vortex nucleation, leading to dynamics that differ markedly from those of BECs
Xhani et al. 2025a
;
Pisani et al. 2024
;
Chen et al. 2025
. In this work, we show that pair breaking imposes a fundamental limit on the stability of persistent currents: beyond a critical velocity associated with the pair breaking, stability cannot be enhanced by introducing impurities, unlike in the case of BECs
Xhani et al. 2025b
;
Pezzè et al. 2024
. This mechanism is analogous to the depairing current known from superconductivity
Kunchur 2019
;
MacManus-Driscoll et al. 2004
;
Miura et al. 2022
;
Miura et al. 2024
, which sets the intrinsic upper bound on supercurrent achievable in superconductors. Here, we identify its counterpart for superfluids and analyze how stability depends on impurity size and spacing, as well as on their influence on vortex mobility in Fermi superfluid rings within the BCS regime.

Theoretical model.
—

We study the dynamics of persistent currents in a weakly attractive fermionic superfluid within the Bardeen–Cooper–Schrieffer (BCS) regime. The evolution is described using time-dependent density functional theory for superfluid Fermi gases
Bulgac 2007
;
Bulgac and Forbes 2008
;
Bulgac et al. 2012
, in the form of the extended Superfluid Local Density Approximation (SLDAE)
Boulet et al. 2022
. This framework has been successfully applied to Josephson dynamics
Wlazłowski et al. 2023
, persistent currents
Xhani et al. 2025a
, vortex dynamics
Barresi et al. 2023
;
Bulgac et al. 2011
;
Wlazłowski et al. 2018
, Higgs modes
Bulgac and Yoon 2009
, spin-imbalanced systems
Bulgac et al. 2006
;
Bulgac and Forbes 2008
;
Kopyciński et al. 2021
, and quantum turbulence
Wlazłowski et al. 2015
;
Hossain et al. 2022
.

The time-dependent SLDAE equations are formally equivalent to the time-dependent Bogoliubov–de Gennes (BdG) equations (with
\hbar=m=1
):

\begin{pmatrix}h(\vec{r},t)&\Delta(\vec{r},t)\\
\Delta^{*}(\vec{r},t)&-h^{*}(\vec{r},t)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u_{n}(\vec{r},t)\\
v_{n}(\vec{r},t)\end{pmatrix}=i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\begin{pmatrix}u_{n}(\vec{r},t)\\
v_{n}(\vec{r},t)\end{pmatrix},

(1)

where
(u_{n},v_{n})^{T}
are the Bogoliubov amplitudes.
Unlike the conventional BdG formalism valid only for weak coupling, SLDAE accurately captures the entire BCS–BEC crossover. Here we focus on
a_{s}k_{F}=-1
, with
k_{F}=(3\pi^{2}\rho_{0})^{1/3}
defined by the bulk density
\rho_{0}
, and
a_{s}
being the scattering length.
The normal, anomalous, and current densities are expressed as

\rho=2\sum_{n}\lvert v_{n}\rvert^{2},\,\,\,\nu=\sum_{n}u_{n}v_{n}^{*},\,\,\,\vec{j}=2\sum_{n}\textrm{Im}\left(v_{n}\vec{\nabla}v_{n}^{*}\right)

(2)

where the sums run over quasiparticle states with
E_{n}\in(0,E_{c})
, with the cutoff
E_{c}
that regularizes ultraviolet divergences
Bulgac and Yu 2002
;
Bulgac 2007
.
The single-particle Hamiltonian and pairing field are

h=-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+U+V_{\textrm{ext}}(\vec{r},t),\qquad\Delta=-\frac{C}{\rho^{1/3}}\nu,

(3)

with
U
the mean-field potential,
V_{\textrm{ext}}
the external potential (trap and impurity potentials), and
C
a regularized pairing constant. Their explicit expressions are provided in Ref.
Boulet et al. 2022
and assure proper reproduction of the quantum Monte Carlo equation of state and pairing gap.
For a uniform system at a considered coupling constant, SLDAE gives
\Delta/\varepsilon_{F}\approx 0.134
, where
\varepsilon_{F}=k_{F}^{2}/2
is the Fermi energy, and the corresponding coherence length is
\xi=\varepsilon_{F}/k_{F}\Delta\approx 7.5\,k_{F}^{-1}
.

The superfluid is confined in a two-dimensional ring potential with inner and outer radii
R_{\mathrm{in}}=30k_{F}^{-1}
and
R_{\mathrm{out}}=60k_{F}^{-1}
, respectively. Trapping is applied only in the
x
–
y
plane, while translational symmetry is imposed along the
z
direction; thus, the system is effectively three-dimensional at the microscopic level.
A controllable superflow is generated using the phase-imprinting technique. Specifically, a phase
\phi=w_{0}\arctan(x,y)
is imprinted onto the order parameter,
\Delta(x,y)=|\Delta(x,y)|e^{i\phi(x,y)}
, and the corresponding ground state is obtained from stationary simulations [
i\partial/\partial t\rightarrow E_{n}
in Eq. (
1
)] in the absence of impurities. This procedure initializes a rotating superfluid with a total phase winding of
2\pi w_{0}
, corresponding to a persistent-current state with winding number
w_{0}
.
A key distinction between BEC and BCS superfluids arises already at this stage: in the latter, the winding number cannot exceed the pair-breaking threshold
w_{\mathrm{pb}}\approx 2R_{\mathrm{in}}v_{\textrm{pb}}
, where
v_{\textrm{pb}}=\sqrt{\sqrt{\mu^{2}+\Delta^{2}}-\mu}
and
\mu
is the chemical potential
Xhani et al. 2025a
. Above this limit, pair breaking suppresses the order parameter near the inner radius, which may lead to vortex emission and subsequent decay of the current. For the present setup, the estimated threshold is
w_{\mathrm{pb}}=5
.

Similar to the experimental procedure
Pezzè et al. 2024
, impurities are introduced dynamically during the simulation. Each impurity is modeled by a Gaussian potential symmetrically distributed along a ring (forming a circular lattice):

\displaystyle V_{\textrm{imp}}(\vec{r},t)

\displaystyle=V_{0}(t)\sum_{i=1}^{N_{d}}\exp\!\left[\frac{(x-x_{i})^{2}+(y-y_{i})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right],

(4)

where
V_{0}(t)
is the time-dependent amplitude, ramped from zero to its final value (
\approx 2\varepsilon_{F}
) within the time interval
(0\text{\textendash}25)\varepsilon_{F}^{-1}
. The impurity centers are positioned at
x_{i}=R_{0}\cos(2\pi i/N_{d})
and
y_{i}=R_{0}\sin(2\pi i/N_{d})
, with
R_{0}=(R_{\mathrm{in}}+R_{\mathrm{out}})/2
. We explore the system’s response as a function of two control parameters: the number of impurities
N_{d}
and their size
s
, defined as the full width at half maximum,
s\approx 2.355\sigma
. Two representative regimes are considered: impurities larger than the coherence length (
s_{1}=1.33\xi
) and smaller impurities (
s_{2}=0.67\xi
). In our simulations, we vary
2\leq N_{d}\leq 16
.

The equations of motion (
1
) are solved numerically on a computational grid of size
128\times 128
, with lattice spacing
dxk_{F}=1
, and plane waves along the third direction are assumed. All simulations are performed using the
W-SLDA Toolkit

WSL

Results
—

Representative snapshots from the time-dependent simulations are shown in Fig.
1
(a) for the case
w_{0}=7
, i.e., above the pair-breaking threshold, and for two configurations: without impurities (
N_{d}=0
) and with
N_{d}=6
impurities. As expected, for the case of no impurities, the persistent current is unstable. Vortices are spontaneously nucleated (at the inner edge for
N_{d}=0
), which leads to the decay of the current. Systematic simulations confirm that, for
w_{0}>w_{\mathrm{pb}}

Xhani et al. 2025b
;
Pezzè et al. 2024
in the BEC regime, where the addition of impurities or Josephson junctions can enhance the stability of the persistent current. This fundamental difference between bosonic and fermionic superfluids constitutes one of the main conclusions of this work.

To make this statement quantitative, we introduce an energy-based diagnostic by computing the flow energy, which measures the total current in the system:

E_{\mathrm{flow}}(t)=\int\frac{\vec{j}^{2}(\vec{r},t)}{2\rho(\vec{r},t)}\,d^{3}\vec{r}.

(5)

This quantity includes contributions from both the superfluid and normal components, since within the DFT framework
\vec{j}\neq\rho\,(\hbar/2)\nabla\phi
(the factor
2
corresponds to the Cooper pair mass). This differs from, for example, the Gross–Pitaevskii approach, where the equality holds exactly. Consequently,
E_{\mathrm{flow}}
captures both vortex dynamics and normal-fluid dissipation
Xhani et al. 2025a
.
We also monitor the condensation energy,

E_{\mathrm{cond}}(t)=\frac{3}{8}\int\frac{|\Delta(\vec{r},t)|^{2}}{\varepsilon_{F}(\vec{r},t)}\rho(\vec{r},t)\,d^{3}\vec{r},

(6)

where
\varepsilon_{F}(\vec{r})
is the local Fermi energy
Wlazłowski et al. 2023
. This quantity measures the pairing strength and provides a direct probe of pair-breaking processes during the evolution.
To quantify dissipation, the temporal profile of the flow energy,
E_{\mathrm{flow}}(t)
, is fitted with an exponential function,
f(t)=A\exp\left[-(t-t_{0})/\tau\right]+B
where
A
and
B
are related to initial and final values,
\tau
the decay time, and
t_{0}=25\,\varepsilon_{F}^{-1}
denotes the time when the impurities are fully ramped on. To characterize the topological stability of the current, we also define the critical winding number
w_{c}
as the largest initial winding
w_{0}
that remains stable over the time interval, precisely for
t\varepsilon_{F}\leq 1000
.

To illustrate this behavior, we focus, for example, on the case

Conclusions
—
We have investigated the stability of persistent currents in fermionic superfluids within the BCS regime using time-dependent density functional theory. Our results demonstrate that the stability of supercurrents is fundamentally limited by a pair-breaking threshold, in sharp contrast to Bose–Einstein condensates, where impurity-induced stabilization is possible
Xhani et al. 2025b
;
Pezzè et al. 2024
.

Two distinct channels of energy dissipation emerge in fermionic systems: (i) topological relaxation, associated with winding-number decay and vortex proliferation above the critical winding
w_{c}
, and (ii) microscopic pair-breaking processes, which operate even below this threshold. The latter channel is primarily governed by impurity size, as larger impurities enhance the local suppression of the order parameter and accelerate energy loss without necessarily inducing vortex nucleation.
In the vortex-nucleation regime, the size and spacing of impurities strongly influence vortex mobility. Larger impurities reduce vortex mobility, indicating a transition to a pinning regime, while closely spaced impurities allow for vortex hopping between pinning sites. These findings provide a microscopic picture of how disorder and geometry jointly control the interplay between dissipation and stability in fermionic superflows.

The results are directly relevant to astrophysical systems such as neutron stars. Although neutron matter is strongly interacting, its pairing gap and coherence length correspond to a weak-coupling BCS regime
Carlson et al. 2012
. The impurity spacings explored here are comparable to the expected distances between nuclear clusters in the neutron-star crust
Negele and Vautherin 1973
;
Chamel 2012
. Hence, the mechanisms identified (pair-breaking dissipation and impurity-mediated pinning) offer insight into vortex dynamics in neutron-star superfluids and their role in phenomena such as pulsar glitches
Chamel and Haensel 2008
;
Haskell and Melatos 2015
;
Antonopoulou et al. 2022
.

The F-pure threshold versus the a -invariant for standard graded rings
Suchitra Pande

Abstract
. Hirose, Watanabe and Yoshida conjectured a criterion for a standard graded strongly F F -regular ring to be Gorenstein in terms of the F F -pure threshold. We complete the proof of this conjecture. We also prove natural extensions of the conjecture to section rings of normal, F F -split projective varieties with respect to globally generated ample divisors. Our proof exploits the geometry of the Proj of the graded ring.



1.
Introduction

The
Frobenius
map provides a powerful tool to capture the singularity properties of prime characteristic rings. Using its properties, Hochster and Huneke introduced the class of
strongly
F
-regular
rings (
2.2
) as a class of mild singularities that are particularly well-behaved
[
HH89
]
. For a strongly
F
-regular local (or
\mathbb{N}
-graded) ring
(S,\mathfrak{m})
of prime characteristic
p>0
, Takagi and Watanabe introduced a numerical invariant of
S
called the
F
-pure threshold
(
2.4
) measuring asymptotic properties of the Frobenius splittings of
S
(
[
TW04
]
). The
F
-pure threshold is a positive characteristic analog of the log canonical threshold for complex singularities and which plays a central role in the minimal model program (
[
HY03
]
).

The singularity notions defined via the Frobenius map interact in interesting ways with classical notions such as normal, Cohen-Macaulay, Gorenstein, etc. For instance, strongly
F
-regular rings are always normal and Cohen-Macaulay
[
HH89
]
. While they are not always Gorenstein, Hirose, Watanabe and Yoshida proposed a conjecture to detect when a strongly
F
-regular standard graded ring
S
is Gorenstein using the
F
-pure threshold
[
HWY14
]
. This conjecture involves comparing the value of the
F
-pure threshold with the classical
a
-invariant of Goto and Watanabe:

Definition 1.1
.

[
GW78
]

Let
S
be a
d
-dimensional
\mathbb{N}
-graded domain with homogeneous maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Then, the
a
-invariant of
S
is defined to be

\text{a}(S)=\min\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\,|\,[H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)]_{-n}\neq 0\}.

Here,
H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}[H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)]_{n}
denotes the
d^{\text{th}}
local cohomology module of
S
with support in the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, which we view as a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded module over
S
.

Then the conjecture of Hirose, Watanabe and Yoshida states:

Conjecture 1.2
.

(
[
HWY14
, Conjecture 1.1]
)
Let
k
be an
F
-finite field and
S
a standard graded algebra over
k
. Assume that
S
is strongly
F
-regular. Then,

(a)

we always have
\text{fpt}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)\leq-\text{a}(S)
, where
\text{fpt}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)
denotes the
F
-pure threshold of
S
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
(
2.4
).

(b)

Moreover,
S
is Gorenstein if and only if
\text{fpt}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)=-\text{a}(S)
.

The purpose of this paper is to complete the proof of this conjecture.

Main Theorem
.

(
3.1
)
Let
S
be a standard graded, strongly
F
-regular ring over an
F
-finite field of characteristic
p>0
. Then,
S
is Gorenstein if and only if the negative of the a-invariant of
S
is equal to the
F
-pure threshold of
S
.

In fact, our result holds more generally for
\mathbb{N}
-graded section rings of normal,
F
-split projective varieties with respect to globally generated ample divisors (see
3.1
).

Earlier works towards this include:

•

The case when
S
is a toric ring was proved in the original paper
[
HWY14
]
which inspired the statement of the conjecture.

•

The case when
S
is a Hibi ring was proved by Chiba and Matsuda
[
CM15
]
.

•

Part (a) and the only if part of Part (b) of
1.2
were both proved by De Stefani and Núñez-Betancourt
[
DN15
]
. Their results (with the natural generalizations) also hold for the more general class of standard graded, normal and
F
-pure rings (instead of strongly
F
-regular).

•

The remaining part of
1.2
, which is the if part of Part (b), was proved conditionally by Singh, Takagi and Varbaro in
[
STV17
]
, under the additional assumption that the anti-canonical ring of
S
is finitely generated. We note that this condition is expected to be true for all strongly
F
-regular rings (
[
DST24
]
), and is known for certain classes such as determinantal rings, strongly
F
-regular rings of dimension at most
3
, and
4
-dimensional strongly
F
-regular rings of characteristic
p>5
. The purpose of this paper is to prove
1.2
unconditionally.

Our proof is geometric, and proceeds by reinterpreting the
F
-pure threshold and the
a
-invariant in terms of divisor classes on the projective variety
X=\Proj(S)
. This translation is inspired the works of Smith
[
Smi97
]
and
[
Smi00
]
. The next key idea, following
[
Sch09
]
, is to use duality for the Frobenius map to associate a Weil-divisor
D_{\varphi}
on
X
to any homogeneous
p^{-e}
-linear map
\varphi:F^{e}_{*}S\to S
. With this association, the proof of
3.1
reduces to the observation that on a projective variety over a field
k
, the only divisor class
E
that is at once effective and a limit of anti-effective classes is the trivial class (
3.4
).

It is natural to seek generalizations of
1.2
to other settings such as non-standard graded rings, or local rings. Note that the assumption on normality of
S
can not be dropped as shown in
[
DN15
, Example 5.3]
. A compelling generalization of
1.2
to all normal and
F
-pure local rings was introduced in
[
STV17
]
using an invariant called the
F
-injective
threshold to replace the
a
-invariant. Since our techniques involve the projective geometry of
\Proj(S)
, they do not seem to extend directly to this setting.

2.
Preliminaries

Notation 2.1
.

Unless stated otherwise, all rings considered will be commutative, Noetherian and with a unit. Throughout,
k
will denote an
F
-finite field of characteristic
p>0
(which means that we have
[k:k^{p}]<\infty
).

2.1.
The Frobenius map in characteristic
p>0
.

Let
R
be any ring of prime characteristic
p
. Then for any
e\geq 1
, let
F^{e}:R\to R
sending
r\mapsto r^{p^{e}}
be the
e^{\text{th}}
-iterate of the
Frobenius morphism
. Since
R
has characteristic
p
,
F^{e}
defines a ring homomorphism, allowing us to define a new
R
-module for each
e\geq 1
obtained via restriction of scalars along
F^{e}
. We denote this new
R
-module by
F_{*}^{e}R
and its elements by
F_{*}^{e}r
(where
r
is an element of
R
). Concretely,
F_{*}^{e}R
is the same as
R
as an abelian group, but the
R
-module action is given by:

r\cdot F_{*}^{e}s:=F_{*}^{e}(r^{p^{e}}s)\textrm{\quad for $r\in R$ and $F_{*}^{e}s\in F_{*}^{e}R$}.

The ring
R
is called
F
-finite
if the Frobenius map
F
on
R
is a finite map.

2.2.
F-purity and F-regularity

Definition 2.2
.

[
HH89
]

Let
R
be a Noetherian
F
-finite ring of characteristic
p
.
R
is said to be
F
-pure
if the map
R\to F_{*}R
splits as a map of
R
-modules. And
R
is said to be
strongly
F
-regular
if for any element
c\in R
that is not contained in any minimal prime of
R
, there exists an integer
e\gg 0
, such that, the following map

\begin{split}R\to F^{e}_{*}R\\
1\mapsto F^{e}_{*}c\end{split}

splits as a map of
R
-modules.

Remark 2.3
.

By
[
HH94
]
, a strongly
F
-regular ring is always, normal and Cohen-Macaulay.

2.3.
The F-pure threshold

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an
F
-finite local domain over a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
TW04
]
)
.

Assume that
R
is
F
-pure. For each
e\geq 1
, let

\nu_{e}(\mathfrak{m})=\max\{n\,|\,\exists\,x\in\mathfrak{m}^{n}\,\text{such that the map }R\to F^{e}_{*}R,\,1\mapsto F^{e}_{*}x\,\text{ splits}\}.

The
F
-pure threshold of
(R,\mathfrak{m})
with respect to the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
is defined to be the limit

\text{fpt}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R):=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{e}(\mathfrak{m})}{p^{e}}.

2.4.
Section rings and modules

Definition 2.5
.

Let
k
be a field and
X
be a projective scheme over
k
. Given an ample invertible sheaf
\mathscr{L}
on
X
, the
\mathbb{N}
-graded ring
S
defined by

S=S(X,\mathscr{L}):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}H^{0}(X,\mathscr{L}^{n})

is called the
section ring
of
X
with respect to
\mathscr{L}
. The affine scheme
\Spec(S)
is called the (affine)
cone over
X
with respect to
\mathscr{L}
.

Similarly, given a coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
X
, the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded module defined by

M(X,\mathscr{L}):=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{0}(X,\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{L}^{n})

is called the
section module
of
\mathscr{F}
with respect to
\mathscr{L}
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a normal, projective subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k}
. Then, the section ring of
X
with respect to the very ample line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
is the normalization of the homogeneous coordinate ring
k[x_{0},\dots,x_{N}]/I_{X}
(where the
I_{X}
is the homogeneous radical ideal corresponding to
X
). Note that even though the homogeneous coordinate ring is always standard graded (i.e., generated by degree one elements), the corresponding section ring, being the normalization, might not be standard graded.

In this paper, we will consider a slightly more general situation than the above example, namely with respect to globally generated invertible sheaves. Recall that an ample invertible sheaf
\mathscr{L}
is globally generated if and only if the corresponding section ring
S(X,\mathscr{L})
admits a homogeneous system of parameters consisting of degree
1
elements (see
[
Smi97
, Section 1]
).

Definition 2.7
.

Suppose
S
is a section ring over an
F
-finite field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. Assume that
X=\Proj(S)
is normal. Let
L
denote an ample divisor corresponding to
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
. Let
I_{e}(mL)
denote the
e^{th}
-splitting subspace
of
S_{m}
on
X
defined as:

I_{e}(mL)=\{x\in S_{m}\,|\,\varphi(F^{e}_{*}x)=0\,\forall\,\varphi\in\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mL),\mathcal{O}_{X})\}.

Recall that
I_{e}(S)=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}I_{e}(mL)
is the splitting ideal of
S
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose
S
is the section ring of a normal projective variety
X
(over
k
) with respect to a globally generated ample invertible sheaf
\mathscr{L}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}\subset S
denote the homogeneous maximal ideal, then the
F
-pure threshold of
S
can be computed as follows:
let

\nu^{\prime}_{e}(S):=\max\{r\,|\,S_{\geq r}\not\subset I_{e}(S)\}=\max\{r\,|\,I_{e}(\mathscr{L}^{r})\neq S_{r}\}.

Then,

\text{fpt}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)=\lim_{e\to\infty}\,\frac{\nu^{\prime}_{e}(S)}{p^{e}-1}.

Proof.

When
S
is standard graded, this is just a restatement of
[
STV17
, Proposition 2.5]
, since
\mathfrak{m}^{r}=S_{\geq r}
. In the more general case, since
S
is
F
-pure, we know that the projective variety
X
is globally
F
-split. Then, as
\mathscr{L}
is ample we have the following cohomology vanishings by
[
MR85
, Proposition 1]
:

H^{i}(X,\mathscr{L}^{m})=0,\quad\forall\,i>0,\,m>0.

In particular, since
\mathscr{L}
is globally generated, the sheaf
\mathscr{L}^{d}
is
0
-regular with respect to
\mathscr{L}
, where
d=\dim(S)=\dim(X)+1
. In particular, the multiplication maps

(2.1)

S_{r}\times S_{m}\twoheadrightarrow S_{m+r}

are surjective whenever
r\geq 0
and
m\geq d
. Therefore, for all
r\geq d
, we have inclusions

\mathfrak{m}^{r}\subset S_{\geq r}\subset\mathfrak{m}^{r-d}.

We refer to
[
Laz04
, Section 1.8]
for details regarding the use of Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity.

With notation as in
2.4
, this shows that we have

\nu_{e}\leq\nu^{\prime}_{e}\leq\nu_{e}+d
for all
e
. Dividing by
p^{e}-1
and taking a limit as
e\to\infty
, we obtain
\text{fpt}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{\prime}_{e}}{p^{e}-1}
.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
S
be as in
2.8
. Then, for any
n\geq 1
we have

\text{fpt}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S^{(n)})=\frac{\text{fpt}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)}{n},

where
S^{(n)}
denotes the
n^{\rm{th}}
-Veronese subring of
S
.

Proof.

With notation as in
2.8
, for any
e\geq 1
, we claim that

\nu^{\prime}_{e}(S^{(n)})+1+\frac{d}{n}\geq\frac{\nu^{\prime}_{e}(S)}{n}\geq\nu^{\prime}_{e}(S^{(n)}).

The right-inequality follows immediately from
2.7
. For the left inequality, we note that by the definition of
\nu^{\prime}_{e}
, for all
m\geq\nu_{e}^{\prime}+1
we have
I_{e}(\mathscr{L}^{mn})=S_{mn}
. Suppose
I_{e}(\mathscr{L}^{r})\neq S_{r}
for some
r\geq n(\nu_{e}^{\prime}+1)+d
. Let
f\in S_{r}\setminus I_{e}(\mathscr{L}^{r})
. Then, by the surjection
Equation

2.1
, we may write
f=\sum g_{i}h_{i}
, where
g_{i}\in S_{n(\nu_{e}^{\prime}+1)}
. But this is a contradiction since
I_{e}(\mathscr{L}^{n(\nu_{e}^{\prime}+1)})=S_{n(\nu_{e}^{\prime}+1)}
and
I_{e}
is an ideal of
S
. This proves the claim. The Corollary now follows by diving by
p^{e}
and taking a limit as
e\to\infty
.
∎

3.
Proof of Main Theorem

In this section, we will prove the main theorem of this paper in
3.1
as conjectured in
[
HWY14
]
. In fact, we will prove a slightly more general result that works for normal,
F
-pure section rings that admit a system of parameters by elements of degree
1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S
be an
\mathbb{N}
-graded section ring over an
F
-finite field
k
of characteristic
p>0
(
2.5
). Assume that
S
is normal,
F
-pure and admits a homogeneous system of parameters of degree one elements. Also suppose that the dimension
d+1
of
S
is at least two and set
a:=-\text{a}(S)
. Then, we have

\text{fpt}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)\leq a
and equality holds if and only if
S
is quasi-Gorenstein.

Definition 3.2
.

Recall that the
a
-invariant of an
\mathbb{N}
-graded ring
S
(with homogeneous maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
) is defined by

-\text{a}(S)=\min\{r\,|\,[H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)]_{-r}\neq 0\}

where
d+1
is the dimension of
S
.

Definition 3.3
.

A normal local ring
R
with a canonical module
\omega_{R}
is called quasi-Gorenstein if
\omega_{R}\cong R.

Our proof of
3.1
uses the projective geometry of
\Proj(S)
. We first review some constructions that are helpful for translating between the algebraic and geometric interpretations.

3.1.
Graded canonical modules.

For an
\mathbb{N}
-graded section ring
S
of a normal projective variety
X
with respect to ample invertible sheaf
\mathscr{L}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
(
2.5
), there is a natural choice of a graded module isomorphic to a canonical module of
S
.
Since
X
is normal, we may consider the canonical divisor
K_{X}
of
X
by extending from the regular locus of
X
. Moreover, the reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})
is isomorphic to the canonical sheaf of
X
. Then the section module
M(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}),\mathscr{L})
(
2.5
) is a reflexive module of rank
1
over
S
that is isomorphic to a canonical module of
S
. See
[
Smi97
, Section 1]
for a detailed discussion regarding the graded canonical module of a section ring.

Since any graded module that is isomorphic to
S_{\mathfrak{m}}
after localization at
\mathfrak{m}
must be isomorphic to a shift
S(r)
for some
r>0
,
S
being quasi-Gorenstein is thus equivalent to the condition that
K_{X}\sim r\mathscr{L}
as Weil-divisors on
X
, for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

3.2.
Homogeneous Splittings:

We will use the following notion of “homogeneous splittings" over
S
: Since
S
is an
\mathbb{N}
-graded ring, for each
e\geq 1
, the
S
-algebra
F^{e}_{*}S
is naturally an
\frac{1}{p^{e}}\mathbb{N}
-graded such that
F^{e}
is a degree-preserving map. Moreover, for any
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
S
-module
N
, the module
\Hom_{S}(F^{e}_{*}S,N)
is
\frac{1}{p^{e}}\mathbb{Z}
-graded with a decomposition given as

(3.1)

\Hom_{S}(F^{e}_{*}S,N)=\bigoplus_{\frac{n}{p^{e}}\in\frac{1}{p^{e}}\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{S}^{\text{gr}}\left(F^{e}_{*}S\left[\frac{-n}{p^{e}}\right],N\right)

where
F^{e}_{*}S\left[\frac{-n}{p^{e}}\right]
is the module whose
\frac{m}{p^{e}}
degree component (for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
) is
(F^{e}_{*}S)_{\frac{m-n}{p^{e}}}
. Here,
\Hom_{S}^{\text{gr}}\left(F^{e}_{*}S\left[\frac{-n}{p^{e}}\right],N\right)
is the space of degree-shifting maps
F^{e}_{*}S\to N
that send degree
\frac{-n+mp^{e}}{p^{e}}
elements of
F^{e}_{*}S
to degree
m
elements of
N
and are zero on the remaining degrees.

This perspective is useful for geometric interpretations as follows: note that the
S
-module

M_{e,n}:=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(F^{e}_{*}S)_{\frac{n+mp^{e}}{p^{e}}}

is the section module (
2.5
) of the sheaf
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL)
, where
X=\Proj(S)
with
L
corresponding to the invertible sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
. Thus, if we have a homogeneous element
f\in S_{n}
of degree
n
such that
f\notin I_{e}(S)
(where
I_{e}
is as in
2.7
), then there exists a homogeneous splitting
\varphi:F^{e}_{*}S\to S
such that
\varphi(F^{e}_{*}f)=1
, which corresponds to an element
\varphi\in\Hom_{S}^{\text{gr}}\left(F^{e}_{*}S\left[\frac{n}{p^{e}}\right],S\right)
. This homogeneous then induces a splitting
\tilde{\varphi}:F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{f})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
, where
D_{f}
denotes the divisor of zeroes of
f
. Thus, homogeneous splittings exactly correspond to splittings coming from sheaf maps on
X
. For a detailed discussion regarding this, we refer to
[
LP24
, Section 2]
.

Proof of
3.1
.

First, we may reduce to the case of an algebraically closed base field
k
by taking a flat base change
k\to\overline{k}.
The
a
-invariant is preserved by flat base change for local cohomology, and the
F
-pure threshold of
S
is preserved since the base change map
S\to S\otimes_{k}\overline{k}
has regular fibers (see
[
Yao06
, Theorem 5.6]
). So we assume that
k=\overline{k}
is algebraically closed.

Now, we reinterpret all the terms involved in terms of
X:=\Proj(S)
and
L
be a Cartier divisor corresponding to
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
. Note that since
S
is assumed to be normal,
X
is normal. Recall that the local cohomology module
H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)
is isomorphic to the graded module (since
d\geq 1
)

H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)=\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{d}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(rL)).

Therefore, the negative
a
-invariant of
S
can be reinterpreted as
a=\min\{r\,|\,[H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)]_{-r}=H^{d}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-rL))\neq 0\}
.

Furthermore, we consider the canonical divisor
K_{X}
(by extending from the regular locus) of
X
, which gives us the corresponding reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})
isomorphic to the canonical sheaf of
X
. In particular, by duality for the canonical sheaf (see
[
Smi97
, Section 1]
for a detailed discussion), we may rewrite:
H^{d}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-rL))\cong H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+rL)).
Therefore, the negative
a
-invariant is reformulated as

a=\min\{r\,|\,H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+rL))\neq 0\}.

Therefore, by definition, we have
H^{0}(X,K_{X}+aL)\neq 0
, which defines an effective Weil-divisor
D\geq 0
such that

(3.2)

D\sim K_{X}+aL.

Thus, by the discussion in
Section

3.1
,
S
will be quasi-Gorenstein if we can show that
D=0
as a Weil divisor.

Now, suppose we have a homogeneous element
f\in S
of degree
m
and let
D_{f}
denote the Cartier divisor of zeroes corresponding to the section
f
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)
. Then by the above discussion on homogeneous splittings, whenever
f\notin I_{e}(nL)
(
2.7
) we obtain a homogeneous splitting
\varphi
of
f
giving us a global splitting

\tilde{\varphi}:F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{f})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

as
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules.
By the duality isomorphism

\mathscr{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{f}),\mathcal{O}_{X})\cong F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}((1-p^{e})K_{X}-D_{f}),

for the Frobenius map,
\tilde{\varphi}
in turn gives us a dual effective Weil divisor
D_{\varphi}\sim(1-p^{e})K_{X}-D_{f}
.
See
[
SS10
, Section 4.1]
for a detailed discussion regarding duality for the Frobenius map.

Then, as
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisors, we have the linear equivalence

\frac{1}{p^{e}-1}D_{\varphi}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-K_{X}-\frac{n}{p^{e}-1}L,

since
D_{f}\sim nL
.
Combining with
Equation

3.2
, we obtain

D+\frac{1}{p^{e}-1}D_{\varphi}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}(a-\frac{n}{p^{e}-1})L.

Now, since both
D
and
D_{\varphi}
are effective and
L
is ample, this implies that

a-\frac{n}{p^{e}-1}\geq 0\implies\frac{n}{p^{e}-1}\leq a.

Therefore, in the notation of
2.8
, we must have

\frac{\nu^{\prime}_{e}(S)}{p^{e}-1}\leq a

for each
e
(since by definition, for each
e
, there is a non-zero element
f_{e}
of degree
\nu^{\prime}_{e}
in
S
such that
f_{e}\notin I_{e}(S)
). Hence, by
2.8
we must have

\text{fpt}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)\leq a.

Now, suppose
\text{fpt}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)=a
. Then, we may pick a sequence of homogeneous elements
f_{e}\notin I_{e}(n_{e}L)
of degree
n_{e}
of
S
such that
\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{n_{e}}{p^{e}-1}=a
. Then, by the previous discussion, we can construct effective Weil-divisors
D_{e}
(corresponding to the splittings
\varphi_{e}
of
D_{f_{e}}
) such that

(3.3)

D+\frac{1}{p^{e}-1}D_{e}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}(a-\frac{n_{e}}{p^{e}-1})L.

We now claim that this forces
D
to be the zero divisor. Assuming this claim for the moment, we note that we are done since this forces
-K_{X}\sim aL
and in turn,
S
is quasi-Gorenstein.

Thus, it remains to show that
Equation

3.3
forces
D
to be the zero divisor. First, we assume that
K_{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. Note that this implies that
D
and
D_{e}
are all
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors. Now, we consider the classes of
D
and
D_{e}
in the Néron-Severi space
N^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
, denoted by
[D]
and
[D_{e}]
.
Equation

3.3
implies that in
N^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
,

\lim_{e\to\infty}[D+D_{e}]=\lim_{e\to\infty}[D]+[D_{e}]=\lim_{e\to\infty}(a-\frac{m_{e}}{p^{e}-1})[L]=0.

Thus, we see that the class
[D]
is the limit of
-[D_{e}]
, thus,
-[D]
is pseudo-effective. But,
D
was effective by assumption. Since there are no lines contained in the pseudo-effective cone of
X
,
[D]
is forced to be zero (see
[
CHMS14
, Lemma 2.3]
).
Now, let
r\geq 0
be such that
rD
is Cartier. Then, since
[rD]=r[D]
is zero, we must have that
rD
is the zero divisor, because if not, for a general curve
C\subset X
that intersects
D
but is not contained in
D
, we must have that the degree of
rD|_{C}
is positive, since it is effective. This completes the proof if
K_{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.

In the general case, we need a more careful version of the above argument, which is presented in
3.4
and shows that in this case again,
D
is forced to be zero which concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

The following lemma was used in the proof above:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and
L
be an ample divisor on
X
. Suppose we have an effective Weil-divisor
D
, a sequence of effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisors
(D_{e})_{e\geq 1}
and a sequence of non-negative rational numbers
\lambda_{e}
such that

(3.4)

D+D_{e}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\lambda_{e}L

and

(3.5)

\lim_{e\to\infty}\lambda_{e}=0.

Then, we necessarily have
D=0
.

To prove this, we will use intersection theory of divisors.

Numerical Intersection Theory

We review Kleiman’s numerical intersection theory of divisors as developed in
[
Kle66
]
. We only state the definition and main facts that we need here.

Let
Y
be a proper scheme over a field
K
of dimension
d
. Let
\mathscr{L}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{L}_{r}
be line bundles on
Y
,
D
an effective Cartier divisor and
\mathscr{F}
a coherent sheaf on
Y
. Then we have the following theorem due to Snapper.

Theorem 3.5
.

[
Kle66
]

Consider the function
f(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})=\chi(\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{1}^{m_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathscr{L}_{r}^{m_{r}})
for
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\chi
denotes the Euler characteristic (over
K
). Then there is a polynomial
P(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
and of total degree
\leq s=\dim\mathrm{Supp}(\mathscr{F})
such that
P(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})=f(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
for all
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 3.6
.

[
Kle66
, Section 2, Definition 1]

Suppose that
\dim Y\leq r
, then we define the intersection number
(\mathscr{F};\mathscr{L}_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathscr{L}_{r})
to be the coefficient of the term
x_{1}\cdots x_{r}
in the polynomial
P(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})
as in Theorem
3.5
.

For any coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
with
\dim(\mathrm{Supp}(\mathscr{F}))\leq r
, this defines an integer valued multi-linear form on Pic
(Y)^{r}
. If a line bundle
\mathscr{L}
is defined by a Cartier divisor
D
, then we sometimes write
D
instead of
\mathscr{L}
in the intersection form. We need the following properties of intersection numbers:

Proposition 3.7
.

(1)

(
[
Kle66
, Section 2, Proposition 4]
) If
\mathscr{F}
is a locally free sheaf, and
D
is a Cartier divisor on
X
, then

(\mathscr{F};\mathscr{L}_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathscr{L}_{d-1}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D))=(\mathscr{F}|_{D};\mathscr{L}_{1}|_{D}\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathscr{L}_{d-1}|_{D})

(2)

(
[
Kle66
, Section 2, Proposition 5, Corollary 1,2]
) Let
V=\mathrm{Supp}(\mathscr{F})
and
V_{1},\dots,V_{s}
its irreducible components. Let
l_{i}=\mathrm{length}(\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{V_{i}})
where
\mathcal{O}_{V_{i}}
is the stalk of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
at the generic point of
V_{i}
, then, if
r\geq\dim(V)

(\mathscr{F};\mathscr{L}_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathscr{L}_{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}l_{i}\times(\mathscr{L}_{1}|_{V_{i}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathscr{L}_{r}|_{V_{i}})

In particular, if
\mathscr{F}
is an invertible sheaf, then
(\mathscr{F};\mathscr{L}_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathscr{L}_{d})=(\mathscr{L}_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathscr{L}_{d})
.

Proof of
3.4
.

First we note that if
\lambda_{e}=0
for any
e
then
D+D_{e}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
. Since both
D
and
D_{e}
are effective and
X
is projective, this implies that
D=D_{e}=0
as required. Therefore, we may assume that
\lambda_{e}
is positive for all
e
.

For each
e\geq 1
, write
\lambda_{e}=\frac{a_{e}}{b_{e}}
for postive integers
a_{e}
and
b_{e}
. Moreover, by replacing both
a_{e}
and
b_{e}
by a positive multiple, we may assume that
b_{e}D
and
b_{e}D_{e}
are both
\mathbb{Z}
-Weil divisors. Then, since
D+D_{e}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\lambda_{e}L
, we may assume that
b_{e}(D+D_{e})\sim a_{e}L
. In particular,
Z_{e}:=b_{e}(D+D_{e})
is an effective Cartier divisor on
X
. Now, using Part (1) of
3.7
we see that

(3.6)

a_{e}(L\cdot\ldots\cdot L\cdot L)=(L\cdot\ldots\cdot L\cdot\mathcal{O}_{Y}(Z_{e}))=(L|_{Z_{e}}\cdot\ldots\cdot L|_{Z_{e}}).

Now suppose
D\neq 0
, then let
E
be a non-zero prime divisor in the support of
D
. Then, we have
Z_{e}\geq b_{e}E
for each
e\geq 1
. Moreover, since
L
is ample, the restriction of
L
to any prime divisor of
X
has a positive intersection number. Using this observation and Part (2) of
3.7
, we have

(3.7)

(L|_{Z_{e}}\cdot\ldots\cdot L|_{Z_{e}})\geq b_{e}(L|_{E}\cdot\ldots\cdot L|_{E}).

Now,
Equation

3.6
and
Equation

3.7
together imply that
\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{a_{e}}{b_{e}}\geq\frac{(L|_{E}\cdot\ldots\cdot L|_{E})}{(L\cdot\ldots\cdot L\cdot L)}>0
, which is a contradiction to the assumption that
\lim_{e\to\infty}\lambda_{e}=0
. Thus we must have
D=0
, which completes the proof.
∎

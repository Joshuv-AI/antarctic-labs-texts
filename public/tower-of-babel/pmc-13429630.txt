FL-trafficNet: a hybrid federated transformer-GNN framework with dual attention for smart routing in IoV and VANET environments
Authors: Pallati Narsimhulu, Rashmi Sahay
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOne of the major problems smart cities face is how to efficiently route traffic, especially when connected vehicles and sensors produce a very large amount of real, time data. This heavy traffic load results in delays, inefficient routing, and excessive processing of central units. Hence, this article presents FL-TrafficNet, a hybrid routing framework that enhances traffic management in the Internet of Vehicles (IoV) and Vehicular Ad Hoc Networks (VANETs) scenarios. FL-TrafficNet combines Transformer models, and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to capture not only the time-dependent traffic variation but also the layout of the road network. A dual-attention component enables the model to pinpoint the most significant features in both spatial and temporal domains. To avoid uploading all the raw data to the cloud, the solution employs Federated Learning (FL), whereby vehicles and roadside units (RSUs), train their models on the spot and only share the resultant updates. This ensures data confidentiality and significantly reduces the network traffic. A reinforcement component embedded in the model dynamically makes path decisions by analyzing real-time traffic updates and feedback. The model works continuously by learning from nearby changes in traffic, weather, or road status. Simulation results show that FL-TrafficNet reduces errors in prediction (Mean Absolute Error (MAE): 1.95, Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE): 2.87, Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE): 3.12), improves data privacy (97.8% privacy score), and increases traffic delivery rate (TDR) to 38.4%, a clear improvement over existing recent methods. These results make it suitable for real-time, privacy-aware routing in urban traffic networks.Keywords: Federated learning, GNNs, Transformer models, Spatio-temporal attention, Intelligent traffic routingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Smart city traffic systems use many connected devices like vehicles, sensors, and RSUs that share large amounts of data every second. This constant flow of live traffic information creates possibilities for smarter routing in VANETs and IoV-based road networks. Existing routing methods1–25 that follow fixed maps or slow-updating models struggle to keep up with sudden changes on the vehicle traffic (Ref. Figure 1). For busy city roads, there is a need for routing that can understand both time-based traffic trends and the links between moving vehicles, routes, and signal systems.

Conceptual pipeline for smart routing in IoV/VANET environments.

FL-TrafficNet is built to work with real-time traffic updates and shifting road conditions using a smart mix of learning models. It brings together GNNs and Transformer encoders to understand traffic patterns in both space and time. GNNs analyze the connectivity of roads, vehicles, and signals in a traffic network, whereas Transformers identify the useful patterns in traffic over different time scales. To protect the privacy of personal data and also minimize the transfer of large data over networks, FL-TrafficNet adopts FL. This technique enables local edge devices and RSUs to perform model training on the respective raw data. Thus, without exchanging it, only updated learning patterns are sent to a central server. The model is assisted by a dual attention system in focusing its attention on the most significant aspects of traffic data, even if it is complex or unclear. A reinforcement unit uses real-time feedback to re-route dynamically. Thus, continuously adjusting the routes based on what’s going on at the roads.

Novelty and motivation

Vehicles in VANET and IoV supported networks rely on routing updates from their surroundings to help them avoid slow routes and reach their destination on time. Some of the current solutions either cannot detect deeper traffic patterns or they are too focused on the central servers, which poses privacy issues and results in server congestion. Basically, if the systems do not have efficient tools to analyze spatial and temporal traffic trends along with privacy preservation techniques, these systems remain ineffective. A system which can learn locally from the traffic at each location, has instant reaction to changes, and is capable of providing route guidance in a decentralized manner in real-time is highly desirable. Despite the growing interest in hybrid intelligent transportation models that combine Fl, GNN, and transformer architectures, existing approaches largely treat these components in a loosely coupled manner without achieving a unified optimization strategy. In particular, existing studies often lack an integrated mechanism that simultaneously captures spatial dependencies, temporal dynamics, and adaptive decision-making in a privacy-preserving distributed environment.

To address these limitations, we propose FL-TrafficNet, a unified framework that tightly integrates FL with GNN and Transformer models for efficient spatiotemporal traffic modeling. Unlike conventional approaches, the proposed framework enables joint optimization across distributed clients, ensuring collaborative model learning while preserving data privacy at edge nodes such as RSUs. In continution, FL-TrafficNet incorporates a reinforcement learning (RL)-based adaptive routing mechanism, which dynamically adjusts traffic signal control policies based on real-time traffic conditions. This additional decision-making layer distinguishes the proposed model from existing hybrid architectures by enabling adaptive and context-aware traffic management. As a result, the framework not only improves prediction accuracy but also enhances traffic signal decision-making efficiency in intelligent transportation systems (ITS).

Problem statement

Smart city roads are loaded with data from various sources such as cars, signals, cameras, and sensors. This data can be used to anticipate traffic jams, detect the problems at an early stage, and facilitate the route selection. However, much of this data is either not utilized or stored in separate systems. This strategy not only ensures the accuracy but also the rapidity of traffic control. The goal is to employ a routing technique that is efficient in real-life situations, flexible to changes, and compliant with data privacy and communication requirements of the future smart cities.

Literature review

Traffic systems have been incredibly dynamic and large-data operations in the recent days. Researchers have been working on advanced prediction, routing, and optimization models that utilize spatio-temporal data, FL, and smart edge computing to cater to the increasing needs of intelligent transport networks. This paper highlights the main points of the recent studies in this area.

Graph-based models have become a favorite for dealing with spatio, temporal patterns. Deep attention layers were used in an attention network designed by Remmouche et al.1 for long-term forecasting only. On one hand, there is the high precision and high computation cost. To deal with extremely precise trends in traffic data and its granularity. Ju et al.2 introduced COOL, which integrated spatial and temporal learning through GNNs. The model was very successful; however, it demanded a large amount of data and a very careful adjustment of parameters. Song et al.3 made a model for mobile edge computing in vehicular networks to lower the latency by moving the calculation nearer the vehicles although privacy was not well protected in that case.

Privacy-preserving approaches are increasingly essential in traffic systems, particularly when handling sensitive mobility data. Feng and Qian4 came up with PPSTSL, a method that combines FL and split processing. It allowed them to protect the privacy of their raw data. However, the training time was compromised. Following this idea, Huang et al.5 looked into FL with local spatio-temporal data, thus improving privacy of location and reducing network load. But different local datasets sometimes caused the model performance to be inconsistent. Zhu et al.6 enhanced further by adding blockchain to FL which made the resultant model more trustworthy and communication-efficient. However, the blockchain part made the framework heavier.

Adaptive learning methods in distributed environments have been the subject of various investigations. A framework by Yaqub et al.7 enabled asynchronous updates in federated environments with the use of GNNs. Such an update scheme allowed for flexible training delays across devices but the model was less accurate under sparse updates. Al-Huthaifi et al.8 integrated graph differential equations with the FL concept in their FedGODE model, which reflected data flows continuously, yet the system was susceptible to noisy inputs. Piccialli et al.9 introduced the GRAPHITE generative model that treated traffic forecasting as a task of generating the next states. Their approach was able to cope with the scenarios of high complexity; however, it was a difficult task to understand the model’s decisions.

Lightweight and real-time models mostly concentrate on fast routing changes. Gupta and Lee10 came up with a congestion prediction model that was effective for urban areas with limited infrastructure, thus performing well in less complex road networks. Ma et al.11 incorporated real-time visual data with deep Q-learning (DQL) for adaptive routing based on video feeds. Even though model enhanced the performance, depended on the quality of the videos and the stability of the connections. Bairi et al.12 presented a VANET-based signal control system that prioritized emergency vehicles, which was quite effective, but it needed the deployment of many sensors.

On the side of secured routing, Akilan et al.13 developed a VANET-based traffic management system with optimized path selection and security features. The model was not very successful during low vehicle density scenarios. Khan et al.14 proposed RADAR, a rapid route discovery algorithm for SDN-based IoV systems. Their approach increased the speed of route discovery but failed to be robust in a dynamic traffic environment. Ji et al.15 employed blockchain smart contracts for transparent routing decisions, which increased the complexity and slowed down the updates.

Spatio-temporal learning has been used with RL techniques as well. Jamal et al.16 proposed QV2X combining GNNs and RL for real-time traffic adaptation. The method need a large-trained dataset but managed to deliver the results dynamically. Gonzalez-Cancelas et al.17 saw the benefits of IoV and blockchain for traffic management in port terminals, thereby improving the local logistics. However, scaling remained a challenge. Ajaz et al.18 conceived CLORP using lion optimization and clustering for route selection, which was great under normal circumstances but tough to handle frequent speed changes.

Devarajan et al.19 solved rerouting and load balancing issues by utilizing FL combined blockchain for traffic management and task offloading support. Blockchain brought trust but was at the cost of longer processing time. Balamurugan et al.20 developed a forecasting framework that performed with minimal error under normal scenarios but was not quick enough to respond to abrupt changes. Yang et al.21 used whale optimization for multi-vehicle routing; thus enabling vehicle-to-vehicle cooperation. However, the model’s trustworthiness was affected by the unstable traffic flows. Lastly, Liu et al.22 devised a multipath routing model that made use of real, time traffic information to allocate vehicle loads efficiently. However, network instability caused the reduction of the effectiveness of the model during disruptions23–25.

In contrast, the proposed FL-TrafficNet framework introduces a tightly coupled architecture that unifies these paradigms within a single learning framework. Moreover, existing works generally do not incorporate adaptive decision-making mechanisms such as RL in conjunction with federated optimization. Proposed methodology

FL-TrafficNet is a smart routing system designed to address traffic problems in rapidly growing cities. The system combines Transformer models, GNNs, and FL to provide fast and accurate routing suggestions, particularly when the traffic conditions are dynamic. The existing methods2,4–6,8,10,12,14,16,17,20,22 that depend on central servers often face issues with data privacy and system overloads. In order to accomplish that, FL-TrafficNet utilizes the method of sharing the learning among local traffic points such as RSUs and vehicles to ensure that sensitive data remains close to the source.

The system put forward aims at differentiating the time-based and space-based traffic changes in an ever-evolving network environment. Time-based traffic describes the way the communication or mobility pattern changes with time, whereas space-based traffic depicts the physical network structure and spatial relationships between nodes. Considering both time and space makes the model capable of taking routing decisions more accurately as it gets the changes in traffic behavior and the fixed or moving device positions simultaneously.

The model tackles time-based traffic variations through transformer layers that trace patterns in time. For example, rapidly changing traffic load situations such as emergencies or network congestions. On the other hand, the model employs GNN layers for spatial comprehension. These layers examine the node location, the closeness of the nodes, and the flow of data between them. Such a spatial perspective is helpful in determining the optimal route concerning the node location, energy level, and link condition.

A dual-attention system is employed to prioritize the most essential features that vary from case-to-case. For example, in a densely packed high traffic or when the layout of the nodes is very significant, spatial-attention would be the main one. While temporal attention would be the main feature when the traffic is changing rapidly. This trade-off between time and space makes the system versatile and precise even if the network is unstable or unpredictable. Such a model results in better route choices which, reduces the waiting time, conserves energy, and elevates the rate of successful delivery in IoT environments.

Instead of one location receiving all the traffic data, each traffic device creates its own mini model. These gadgets send only the learning updates to a hub, which makes the whole system light and secure. It also facilitates situations where the network connection is not very stable. Devices can come in their own time because the system accommodates the updates by which it is not necessary for all to happen simultaneously.

In order to make routing more flexible and aware of the context, a RL module has been integrated into the system. This module reacts to the road situation dynamics and the vehicle movements to improve routing decision, making. After making each decision, the module evaluates the result like the speed of message delivery or the energy consumption and revises its policy accordingly. Eventually, this study enables the system to pick routes that adapt well to both immediate traffic changes and vehicle flow patterns over time.

Simulation Setup

The simulation took place on a machine with an Intel Core i9 processor, 64 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. Python 3.9 was the main platform for the development. PyTorch and Deep Graph Library (DGL) were the tools to create the main learning modules. For FL, the Flower framework was chosen. Flower is a versatile framework that enables distributed ML on various devices or data centers without the need to share raw data26. It is an approach to collaborate in model training while data remains at its source location. RL functionalities have been created with the help of the Stable-Baselines3 library. The datasets selected for the evaluation of the performance are METR-LA27, PeMSD728, and TaxiBJ29. Conclusion and future scope

This paper introduces a hybrid intelligent routing framework named FL-TrafficNet, built for smart traffic networks. The system integrates Transformer encoders, GNNs, and FL to effectively handle spatio-temporal traffic data and provide fast, privacy-aware routing decisions. The dual-attention mechanism enables the model to capture the most relevant spatial and temporal patterns, while the FL paradigm ensures that traffic nodes perform local model training and share only updated weights, thereby preserving data privacy and reducing communication overhead. The RL module leverages real-time vehicle feedback to support adaptive decision-making under dynamic traffic conditions. The framework has been evaluated in a simulation-based environment, demonstrating significant improvements in delay reduction, prediction accuracy, and responsiveness to sudden traffic pattern changes. While the current study focuses on simulation-based validation, real-world deployment aspects are considered as part of future work. In this context, FL-TrafficNet can be extended to IoV scenarios, where large-scale interactions among vehicles, RSUs, and heterogeneous devices introduce challenges such as dynamic mobility, intermittent connectivity, and resource constraints. The framework can be further enhanced through the incorporation of digital twin platforms for large-scale urban simulations, including emerging transportation domains such as drone and waterway systems. Additionally, FL can be strengthened with advanced privacy-preserving techniques such as differential privacy and homomorphic encryption to improve trust among participating entities. To support deployment on low-power devices, adaptive model pruning, lightweight architectures, and energy-efficient update strategies can be explored. Finally, validating the framework across multiple real-world traffic systems and cities will further establish its robustness, scalability, and practical applicability in next-generation ITS.

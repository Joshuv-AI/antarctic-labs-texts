Hydrodynamic limit and large deviations for run-and-tumble particles with mean-field switching rates
Elena Pulvirenti, Frank Redig, Hidde van Wiechen

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we study run-and-tumble particles moving on two copies of the discrete torus (referred to as layers), where the switching rate between layers depends on a mean-field interaction among the particles. We derive the hydrodynamic limit of this model, as well as the large deviations from the hydrodynamic limit. Our main tool is the introduction of a weakly perturbed version of the system, whose hydrodynamic equations precisely characterize the trajectories associated with large deviations.

1
Introduction

Run-and-tumble particles serve as simple models of active matter
[
1
]
. Systems of active particles constitute an important class of non-equilibrium systems where at the microscopic scale energy is dissipated in order to produce directed motion. They show a rich phenomenology such as clustering and long-range order
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
.

In the mathematics literature on interacting particle systems, results on active particles are not very abundant (in contrast with the physics literature). To our knowledge, the first rigorous result on hydrodynamic limits for active particles is
[
4
]
, where a system of locally interacting active particles was studied. In
[
5
]
,
[
4
]
, the authors prove the hydrodynamic limit (using the non-gradient method) and identify
a motility-induced phase separation and
a transition to collective motion from the equations obtained in the hydrodynamic limit. See also e.g.

[
6
]
for recent results in the physics literature on locally interacting active particles and collective effects therein.

In this paper we consider a simpler model where the interaction between the run-and-tumble particles is of mean-field type, i.e., via their empirical distribution. For this model we can then both prove the hydrodynamic limit and the large deviations from the hydrodynamic limit.
In our model, the particles move on the one-dimensional torus, and have an internal state which takes values
\pm 1
and determines the direction of motion.
The interaction between the particles arises implicitly via the flip rate at which particles flip their internal state which depends on the magnetization.

First, we start by deriving the hydrodynamic limit, which is a coupled system of partial differential equations for the densities of particles
with internal state
\pm 1
, and an ordinary differential equation for the “magnetization”.
Second, we consider a weakly perturbed model where the influence of an external field which weakly depends on time and (microscopic) space is added. This field influences the rate at which particles flip their internal state and the rate at which they jump in the direction of their internal state.
This is reminiscent of the weakly asymmetric exclusion process
[
7
]
which is an essential tool to study the large deviations for the trajectory of the density in the symmetric exclusion process (SEP). Contrary to the situation of the SEP, in our model, the perturbation does not act on the direction of the motion, which is always in the direction of the internal state.

We prove the hydrodynamic limit of this weakly perturbed model and use it to prove a large deviation principle for the trajectory of the densities in the original model. The technique of proof is based on a change of measure between the original and the weakly perturbed model and the associated exponential martingale (the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the perturbed model w.r.t. the original model). Due to the mean-field character of the interaction, no super-exponential replacement lemmas are needed, i.e., the quantities appearing in the Radon-Nikodym derivative between the perturbed and unperturbed model are a function of the empirical densities and the magnetization.

The rest of our paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the model, the weakly perturbed model and state the hydrodynamic limit of both. In Section 3 we prove large deviations for the trajectory of the densities. In Section
4
we provide the proof of the hydrodynamic limits stated in Section 2.

2
Run-and-tumble particles with mean-field switching rates

In this section we describe the run-and-tumble particle model with mean-field switching rates. Later on we will also define a weakly perturbed version of this model which we will need for the large deviations. For both models, we will consider particles on the two-layered torus
V_{N}:=\mathbb{T}_{N}\times S
, where
\mathbb{T}_{N}=\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
is the discrete torus, and
S=\{-1,1\}
which we will call the internal state space. We then say that a particle
(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}
has position
x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
and internal state
\sigma\in S
. The parameter
N
is a scaling parameter, and we will be interested in the limiting dynamics when
N\to\infty
.

We will consider processes of particle configurations
\{\eta^{N}_{t}:t\geq 0\}
on the state space
\Omega_{N}=\mathbb{N}^{V_{N}}
. We denote by
\eta^{N}_{t}(x,\sigma)
the number of particles at site
(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}
at time
t\geq 0
. The Markovian dynamics is then described as follows:

i)

Active jump:
with rate
N
a particle jumps from
(x,\sigma)
to
(x+\sigma,\sigma)
.

ii)

Internal state flip: a particle jumps from
(x,\sigma)
to
(x,-\sigma)
, with a mean-field rate denoted by
c(\sigma,m_{N}(\eta_{t}^{N}))

Here by “mean-field” we mean that the flip rate

c(\sigma,m_{N}(\eta_{t}^{N}))
depends on the whole configuration
\eta
only via its magnetization
m_{N}(\eta_{t}^{N})
.
For
\eta\in\Omega_{N}
, this magnetization is defined via

m_{N}(\eta):=\frac{1}{|\eta|}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\big(\eta(x,1)-\eta(x,-1)\big),

(2.1)

where
|\eta|:=\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)
denotes the total number of particles in the configuration
\eta
. Here we also use the convention that if
|\eta|=0
then
m_{N}(\eta)=0
. We assume
c(\sigma,\cdot)
to be bounded from above and below (away from zero) and Lipschitz-continuous in the second variable, i.e., there exists an
L>0
such that for
\sigma\in\{-1,1\}
and
m_{1},m_{2}\in[-1,1]
we have that

|c(\sigma,m_{1})-c(\sigma,m_{2})|\leq L|m_{1}-m_{2}|.

(2.2)

The generator of the process is described above,
working on functions
f:\Omega_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, is defined as follows.

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{N}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=N\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)\big[f(\eta^{(x,\sigma)\to(x+\sigma,\sigma)})-f(\eta)\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}c(\sigma,m_{N}(\eta))\eta(x,\sigma)\big[f(\eta^{(x,\sigma)\to(x,-\sigma)})-f(\eta)\big],

(2.3)

where
\eta^{(x,\sigma)\to(y,\sigma^{\prime})}
denotes the configuration
\eta
where a single particle has jumped from
(x,\sigma)
to
(y,\sigma^{\prime})
, if possible.

Remark 2.1
.

If we choose the rates
c(\sigma,m)\equiv 1
, then the particles do not interact with each other, and we recover the run-and-tumble particle process, studied, for instance, in
[
1
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. An actual example of mean-field rates where particles do interact with one another is, for instance, given by the Curie-Weiss Glauber rates
c(\sigma,m)=e^{-\sigma\beta m}
with
\beta>0
.

2.1
Hydrodynamic limit

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the empirical measure of a configuration
\eta\in\Omega_{N}
by

\pi^{N}(\eta):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)\delta_{(\frac{x}{N},\sigma)},

(2.4)

where
\delta
denotes the Dirac measure. For a given
\eta
,
\pi^{N}(\eta)
is a positive Radon measure on the macroscopic space
V:=\mathbb{T}\times S
, where
\mathbb{T}=[0,1]
is the torus. We denote the space of positive Radon measures on
V
by
\mathcal{M}_{V}
.
Given
t\geq 0
, we further denote the empirical measure of the configuration
\eta^{N}_{t}
as

\pi^{N}_{t}:=\pi^{N}(\eta_{t}^{N})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta_{t}^{N}(x,\sigma)\delta_{(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma)},

(2.5)

For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, this produces a process

\{\pi^{N}_{t}:t\in[0,T]\}
with trajectories in the Skorokhod space
D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
. The corresponding space of test functions is given by,

C^{\infty}(V):=\{\phi:V\to\mathbb{R}\ \big|\ \phi(\cdot,\sigma)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})\text{ for all $\sigma\in S$}\}.

(2.6)

For
\phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
we now denote the pairing

\left<\pi^{N}_{t},\phi\right>=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta_{t}^{N}(x,\sigma)\phi(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma).

(2.7)

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
and smooth
\varrho(x,\sigma):V\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
we define the product Poisson measures

\mu_{N}^{\varrho}=\bigotimes_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\mathrm{Pois}(\varrho\left(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma\right)),

(2.8)

which is the local equilibrium measure for the non-interacting (
c(\sigma,m)\equiv 1
) case (see
[
8
]
). We assume that the process
\{\eta^{N}_{t}:t\geq 0\}
starts at
t=0
from the configuration
\eta^{N}=\eta^{N}_{0}
distributed according to
\mu_{N}^{\varrho}
, which fixes the initial density profile. More precisely,
\pi^{N}_{0}
converges as
N\to\infty
to the positive measure with density
\varrho(x,\sigma):V\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, i.e., we have that for every
\phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
and
\varepsilon>0

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho}\left(\left|\langle\pi^{N}_{0},\phi\rangle-\sum_{\sigma\in S}\int\phi(x,\sigma)\varrho(x,\sigma)\mathrm{d}x\right|>\varepsilon\right)=0.

(2.9)

Here
\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho}
denotes the path-space measure of the process with initial distribution
\mu_{N}^{\varrho}
, where we omit the dependence on the terminal time
T
since we assume that this is fixed.

The question of the hydrodynamic limit is to find the limiting PDE for
\pi^{N}_{t}
as
N\to\infty
.
We start with the following preliminary computation

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{N}\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=N\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)\left(\big\langle\pi^{N}\big(\eta^{(x,\sigma)\to(x+\sigma,\sigma)}\big),\phi\big\rangle-\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),\phi\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)c(\sigma,m_{N}(\eta))\left(\big\langle\pi^{N}\big(\eta^{(x,\sigma)\to(x,-\sigma)}\big),\phi\big\rangle-\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),\phi\rangle\right)

(2.10)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)\left[N\big(\phi(\tfrac{x+\sigma}{N},\sigma)-\phi(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma)\big)+c(\sigma,m_{N}(\eta))\big(\phi(x,-\sigma)-\phi(x,\sigma)\big)\right].

From this computation, we observe that the evolution of the empirical measure depends on the evolution of the magnetization
m_{N}(\eta^{N}_{t})
. Note however that the magnetization, defined in (
2.1
), can be expressed in terms of the empirical measure as follows,

m_{N}(\eta)=m(\pi^{N}(\eta)):=\frac{\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),\mathds{1}_{\sigma=1}-\mathds{1}_{\sigma=-1}\rangle}{\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),1\rangle}.

(2.11)

This representation of the magnetization motivates the following definition of the magnetization corresponding to a density
\varrho(x,\sigma):V\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
as

m(\varrho):=\frac{\langle\varrho,\mathds{1}_{\sigma=1}-\mathds{1}_{\sigma=-1}\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}}{\langle\varrho,1\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}},

(2.12)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}
denotes the inner product of
L^{2}
-functions on the space
V
, given by

\langle\psi,\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}=\sum_{\sigma\in S}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(x,\sigma)\phi(x,\sigma)\mathrm{d}x,

(2.13)

for any
\psi,\phi\in L^{2}(V)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\cdot)\to\delta_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
is the trajectory of measures with density
\varrho_{t}(x,\sigma)

which solves the following equation,

\dot{\varrho}_{t}(x,\sigma)=-\sigma\partial_{x}\varrho_{t}(x,\sigma)+c(-\sigma,m(\varrho_{t}))\varrho_{t}(x,-\sigma)-c(\sigma,m(\varrho_{t}))\varrho_{t}(x,\sigma),

(2.14)

with initial condition
\varrho_{0}(x,\sigma)=\varrho(x,\sigma)
.

This theorem will be a consequence of a more general hydrodynamic limit of a weakly perturbed modification of the model, which we will introduce in Section
2.2
. Apart from the hydrodynamic limit of the empirical measure, we can also find a limiting equation of the magnetization.

Corollary 2.2
.

\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}(m(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\in\cdot)\to\delta_{m(\varrho_{\cdot})}
, where
\varrho_{t}
solves (
2.14
). Furthermore,
m_{t}:=m(\varrho_{t})
solves the following equation,

\dot{m}_{t}=c(-1,m_{t})\cdot(1-m_{t})-c(1,m_{t})\cdot(1+m_{t}),

(2.15)

with initial condition
m_{0}=m(\varrho)
.

Remark 2.2
.

When considering the Curie-Weiss Glauber rates
c(\sigma,m)=e^{-\sigma\beta m}
with inverse temperature
\beta>0
, the evolution of the magnetization is given by

\dot{m}_{t}=2\sinh(\beta m_{t})-2m_{t}\cosh(\beta m_{t}).

(2.16)

As
t\to\infty
the process
m_{t}
converges to a solution of the mean-field equation

m^{*}=\tanh(\beta m^{*}).

(2.17)

For
\beta\leq 1
the only solution is
m^{*}=0
. However, for
\beta>1
, there exist two nonzero solutions, and if
m_{0}\neq 0
, the process
m_{t}
converges to either the positive or negative solution, depending on the initial value
m_{0}
. This simulates a type of flocking behavior of the particles, where particles tend to move in the same direction.

2.2
Weakly perturbed model

We furthermore introduce a weakly perturbed version of our model, which will be a key tool for the study of large deviations since it produces the deviating trajectories which have a finite rate function. The weak perturbation will be parametrized by a time-dependent potential
H:[0,T]\times V\to\mathbb{R}
, which we will assume to be differentiable in time and continuous in space, and which we will denote by
H\in C^{1,0}([0,T]\times V)
. The time-dependent generator of this model, acting on functions
f:\Omega_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, is given as follows:

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{H}_{N,t}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=N\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)e^{H_{t}(\frac{x+\sigma}{N},\sigma)-H_{t}(\frac{x}{N},\sigma)}\big[f(\eta^{(x,\sigma)\to(x+\sigma,\sigma)})-f(\eta)\big]

(2.18)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)c(\sigma,m_{N}(\eta))e^{H_{t}(\frac{x}{N},-\sigma)-H_{t}(\frac{x}{N},\sigma)}\big[f(\eta^{(x,\sigma)\to(x,-\sigma)})-f(\eta)\big].

Note that for
H=0
we recover the original model.
We will denote by
\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho,H}
the path-space measure of this process, where
\eta^{N}_{0}
is distributed as
\mu^{\varrho}_{N}
(cf. (
2.8
)). We further abbreviate
\widetilde{H}_{t}(x):=H_{t}(x,1)-H_{t}(x,-1)
. The hydrodynamic limit of this process is then given in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\cdot)\to\delta_{\alpha^{H}}
, where
\alpha^{H}\in D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
is the trajectory of measures with density
\varrho^{H}_{t}(x,\sigma)

which solves the following equation,

\dot{\varrho}^{H}_{t}(x,\sigma)=-\sigma\partial_{x}\varrho^{H}_{t}(x,\sigma)+c(-\sigma,m(\varrho^{H}_{t}))e^{\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)}\varrho^{H}_{t}(x,-\sigma)-c(\sigma,m(\varrho^{H}_{t}))e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)}\varrho^{H}_{t}(x,\sigma),

(2.19)

with initial condition
\varrho^{H}_{0}(x,\sigma)=\varrho(x,\sigma)
.

Note that Theorem
2.1
follows from this Theorem
2.3
by choosing
H\equiv 0
. The proof of Theorem
2.3
will be postponed to Section
4
. Below we give the evolution of the magnetization under the perturbed dynamics.

Corollary 2.4
.

\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}(m(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\in\cdot)\to\delta_{m(\varrho^{H}_{\cdot})}
, where
\varrho^{H}_{t}
solves (
2.19
). Furthermore,
m^{H}_{t}:=m(\varrho^{H}_{t})
solves the following equation,

\dot{m}^{H}_{t}=\frac{1}{\langle\varrho,1\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}}\left\langle\varrho^{H}_{t},-2\sigma e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)}c(\sigma,m^{H}_{t})\right\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}.

(2.20)

with initial condition
m_{0}=m(\varrho)
.

Proof.

Note that if
\alpha_{N}\to\alpha
in
D([0,T]:\mathcal{M}_{V})
then
m(\alpha_{N})\to m(\alpha)
in
D([0,T]:[-1,1])
, hence
m(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\xrightarrow{d}m_{\cdot}
under
\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}
. Therefore, we can find an equation for the evolution of the magnetization from the evolution of
\varrho^{H}_{t}
. First note that
\langle\varrho_{t}^{H},1\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}=\langle\varrho,1\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}
for all
t\geq 0
, which is due to the conservation of particles. Therefore, using the definition (
2.12
), we find

\dot{m}_{t}=\frac{\langle\dot{\varrho}^{H}_{t},\mathds{1}_{\sigma=1}-\mathds{1}_{\sigma=-1}\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}}{\langle\varrho,1\rangle_{L^{2}(V){}}}

(2.21)

By the periodic boundary conditions, we have that

\langle-\sigma\partial_{x}\varrho_{t}^{H},\mathds{1}_{\sigma=1}-\mathds{1}_{\sigma=-1}\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}=0,

(2.22)

hence (
2.20
) follows by filling in (
2.19
) into (
2.21
).
∎

Corollary
2.2
follows from Corollary
2.4
by choosing
H\equiv 0
and using that for any density
\varrho
we have that

\frac{\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varrho(x,\sigma)\mathrm{d}x}{\langle\varrho,1\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\sigma m(\varrho)).

(2.23)

Note that, unlike in the unperturbed model, under the perturbed dynamics the evolution of the magnetization is not a closed equation, but depends on the density
\varrho^{H}_{t}
. This is because under the these dynamics the magnetization process is no longer a Markov process on its own, and additional information on the positions of the particles is required.

3
Large deviations

In this section we will prove a large deviation principle for the run-and-tumble particle process with mean-field switching rates. We start by defining the rate function
\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}:D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})\to[0,\infty]
, which is given in two parts

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha})=h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})+\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})

(3.1)

Here
h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})
is the static part of the large deviation rate function, only depending on the measure at time
t=0
. It can be informally written as

h^{\varrho}_{0}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})=\mu^{\varrho}_{N}(\pi^{N}_{0}\approx\widehat{\alpha}_{0}),

(3.2)

i.e, it corresponds to the large deviation principle of the initial density profile
\pi^{N}_{0}
under the starting distribution
\mu^{\varrho}_{N}
.
Since
\mu_{N}^{\varrho}
is given by a product Poisson measure, the corresponding rate function is known, and given by

h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})=\sup_{\phi}h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0};\phi),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0};\phi)=\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{0},\phi\rangle-\langle\varrho,e^{\phi}-1\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}.

(3.3)

Here the supremum is taken over all
\phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
.

The term
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})
in (
3.1
) is the dynamic part of the rate function, and depends on the whole trajectory
\widehat{\alpha}
. It is given by the following:

\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})=\sup_{G}\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha};G),\qquad\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha};G)=\ell(\widehat{\alpha};G)-\int_{0}^{T}\left<\widehat{\alpha}_{t},c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\left(e^{-\sigma\widetilde{G}_{t}(x)}-1\right)\right>\mathrm{d}t.

(3.4)

Here the supremum is taken over all
G\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times V)
, and we recall the notation
\widetilde{G}_{t}(x)=G_{t}(x,1)-G_{t}(x,-1)
. Furthermore,
\ell(\widehat{\alpha};G)
is a linear functional, defined as follows

\ell(\widehat{\alpha};G):=\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{T},G_{T}\rangle-\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{0},G_{0}\rangle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},(\partial_{t}+\sigma\partial_{x})G_{t}\rangle\mathrm{d}t,

(3.5)

and
m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t})
is the magnetization of the measure, defined as

m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t})=\frac{\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},\mathds{1}_{\sigma=1}-\mathds{1}_{\sigma=-1}\rangle}{\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},1\rangle}.

(3.6)

Remark 3.1
.

In Lemmas
3.7
and
3.8
we derive more explicit expressions of the static part
h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})
and the dynamic part
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})
. Specifically, we identify the functions
\phi
and
H
for which
h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})=h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0};\phi)
and
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})=\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha};H)
.

In order to prove the large deviation principle with rate function
\mathcal{I}
, we need to establish the upper and lower bound.

i.

Upper bound
: For every closed set
\mathcal{C}\subset D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
we have

\uplim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{C})\leq-\inf_{\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha})

(3.7)

ii.

Lower bound
: For every open set
\mathcal{O}\subset D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
we have

\lowlim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{O})\geq-\inf_{\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha})

(3.8)

3.1
Radon-Nikodym derivatives

The goal of this section is to obtain an explicit form for the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the path-space measure of the weakly perturbed process
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}
with respect to the path-space measure of the original process
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}
. This Radon-Nikodym derivative is given by the so-called exponential martingale of the process, and is given in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
T>0
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
H\in C^{\infty}(V)
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Z}^{H}_{N,T}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})

\displaystyle:=\exp\left(N\langle\pi^{N}_{T},H_{T}\rangle-N\langle\pi^{N}_{0},H_{0}\rangle-\int_{0}^{T}e^{-N\langle\pi^{N}_{t},H_{t}\rangle}(\partial_{t}+\mathscr{L}_{N})e^{N\langle\pi^{N}_{t},H_{t}\rangle}\mathrm{d}t\right).

(3.9)

Proof.

By Palmowski and Rolski
[
12
]
, the exponential martingale
\mathcal{Z}^{H}_{N,T}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})
is equal to the Radon-Nikodym derivative
\frac{\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}}
, where
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{N}
is the path-space measure (up to time
T
) of the process corresponding to the time-dependent Markov generator on
\widehat{\varrho}_{N}
given by

\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{N,t}f(\eta)=e^{-N\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),H_{t}\rangle}\left[\mathscr{L}_{N}\big(f(\eta)\cdot e^{N\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),H_{t}\rangle}\big)-f(\eta)\cdot\mathscr{L}_{N}e^{N\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),H_{t}\rangle}\right].

(3.10)

where
\pi^{N}
now denotes the empirical measure as a function of
\eta\in\widehat{\varrho}_{N}
, as defined in (
2.4
). We will prove that
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{N}=\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho,H}
by showing that
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{N,t}
is equal to the generator of the weakly perturbed process
\mathscr{L}_{N,t}^{H}
as defined in (
2.18
). We compute, using (
2
)

\displaystyle e^{-N\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),H_{t}\rangle}

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{N}\big(f(\eta)\cdot e^{N\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),H_{t}\rangle}\big)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle N\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)\big[f(\eta^{(x,\sigma)\to(x+\sigma,\sigma)})e^{H_{t}(\frac{x+\sigma}{N},\sigma)-H_{t}(\frac{x}{N},\sigma)}-f(\eta)\big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}c(\sigma,m(\eta))\eta(x,\sigma)\big[f(\eta^{(x,\sigma)\to(x,-\sigma)})e^{H_{t}(\frac{x}{N},-\sigma)-H_{t}(\frac{x}{N},\sigma)}-f(\eta)\big],

(3.11)

where we used that

\langle X_{N}(\eta^{(x,\sigma)\to(y,\sigma^{\prime})}),H_{t}\rangle-\langle X_{N}(\eta),H_{t}\rangle=H_{t}(\tfrac{y}{N},\sigma^{\prime})-H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma).

(3.12)

Similarly we compute

\displaystyle e^{-N\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),H_{t}\rangle}\mathscr{L}_{N}e^{N\langle\pi^{N}(\eta),H_{t}\rangle}(\eta)=\

\displaystyle N\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)\big[e^{H_{t}(\frac{x+\sigma}{N},\sigma)-H_{t}(\frac{x}{N},\sigma)}-1\big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}c(\sigma,m(\eta))\eta(x,\sigma)\big[e^{H_{t}(\frac{x}{N},-\sigma)-H_{t}(\frac{x}{N},\sigma)}-1\big].

(3.13)

Substituting (
3.1
) and (
3.1
) into (
3.10
), we indeed find that
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{N,t}=\mathscr{L}_{N,t}^{H}
, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

\frac{1}{N}\log\left(\mathcal{Z}_{N,T}^{H}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\right)=\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot};H)+\mathcal{O}(\tfrac{1}{N}).

(3.14)

Proof.

Making use of the following approximation

e^{H_{t}(\frac{x+\sigma}{N},\sigma)-H_{t}(\frac{x}{N},\sigma)}-1=\sigma\partial_{x}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma)+\mathcal{O}(\tfrac{1}{N}),

(3.15)

we are able to write (
3.1
) as

e^{-N\langle\pi^{N}_{t},H_{t}\rangle}\mathscr{L}_{N}e^{N\langle\pi^{N}_{t},H_{t}\rangle}=N\langle\pi^{N}_{t},\sigma\partial_{x}H_{t}\rangle+N\left\langle\pi^{N}_{t},c(\sigma,m(\pi^{N}_{t}))\left(e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)}-1\right)\right\rangle+\mathcal{O}(1).

(3.16)

By now plugging this into (
3.1
), we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N,T}^{H}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(N\langle\pi^{N}_{T},H_{T}\rangle-N\langle\pi^{N}_{0},H_{0}\rangle-N\int_{0}^{T}\langle\pi^{N}_{t},(\partial_{t}+\sigma\partial_{x})H_{t}\rangle\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{O}(1)\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \times\exp\left(-N\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\pi^{N}_{t},c(\sigma,m(\pi^{N}_{t}))\left(e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)}-1\right)\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(N\ell(\pi^{N}_{\cdot};H)-N\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\pi^{N}_{t},c(\sigma,m(\pi^{N}_{t}))\left(e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)}-1\right)\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{O}(1)\right),

(3.17)

finishing the proof.
∎

3.2
Upper bound

In this section we will prove the large deviation upper bound (
3.7
). A crucial ingredient is to prove that the path-space measures of
\pi^{N}_{\cdot}
are exponentially tight, which then reduces the proof of (
3.7
) to compact sets.

Theorem 3.3

(Exponential Tightness)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a compact set
\mathcal{K}_{n}\subset D([0,T],\mathcal{M}_{V})
such that

\uplim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\notin\mathcal{K}_{n})=-n.

(3.18)

Before proving exponential tightness, we first give the proof of the upper bound for compact sets.

Theorem 3.4

(Upper bound for compact sets)
.

For every compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
we have that

\uplim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{K})\leq-\inf_{\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}).

(3.19)

Proof.

We start with the following computation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\varrho}\left[\mathds{1}_{\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{K}}\cdot\frac{e^{Nh_{0}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{0};\phi)}}{e^{Nh_{0}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{0};\phi)}}\cdot\frac{\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{N,T}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})}{\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{N,T}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})}\right]

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq-\inf_{\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}}\frac{1}{N}\log\left[e^{Nh_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0};\phi)}\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{N,T}^{G}(\widehat{\alpha})\right]+\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}^{\varrho}_{N}\left[e^{Nh_{0}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{0};\phi)}\cdot\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{N,T}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\right],

where we recall the definition of
h_{0}^{\varrho}
in (
3.3
) and of
\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{N,T}
in (
3.1
). Since
\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{N,T}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})
is a martingale with
\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{N,0}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})=1
, we actually find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\varrho}_{N}\left[e^{Nh_{0}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{0};\phi)}\cdot\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{N,T}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\varrho}_{N}}\left[e^{Nh_{0}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{0};\phi)}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\varrho}_{N}}\left[e^{N\left(\langle\pi^{N}_{0},\phi\rangle-\langle\varrho,e^{\phi}-1\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}\right)}\right]=1,

(3.21)

where we used that
\mu^{\varrho}_{N}
is a product Poisson distribution. Therefore the second term in (
3.20
) vanishes. For the first term, note that we took
\phi
and
G
arbitrarily, so we have

\uplim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{K})\leq-\sup_{\phi,G}\uplim_{N\to\infty}\inf_{\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}}\frac{1}{N}\log\left[e^{Nh_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0};\phi)}\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{N,T}^{G}(\widehat{\alpha})\right]

(3.22)

using Corollary
3.2
, we find that

\displaystyle-\sup_{\phi,G}\uplim_{N\to\infty}\inf_{\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}}\frac{1}{N}\log\left[e^{Nh_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0};\phi)}\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{N,T}^{G}(\widehat{\alpha})\right]

\displaystyle=-\sup_{\phi,G}\inf_{\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}}h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0};\phi)+\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha};G)

\displaystyle=-\inf_{\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}),

(3.23)

where we were able to interchange the supremum over
\phi
and
G
with the infimum over
\widehat{\alpha}
using the argument of Lemma 11.3 in
[
13
]
, using that
\mathcal{K}
is compact.
∎

In order to prove the exponential tightness, we want to use the method used in
[
14
, pages 271-273]
. However, since we do not know the invariant measure of this system, we first turn to a perturbed model of which we do know the invariant measures, namely the independent run-and-tumble particle system, as defined in
[
9
]
(without diffusive jumps). The generator of this process corresponds to setting
c(\sigma,m)\equiv 1
in the generator
\mathscr{L}_{N}
defined in (
2
), and we will denote it by
\mathscr{L}_{N}^{\mathrm{RTP}}
. For this process, we know that the Product Poisson measures with constant density
\varrho_{c}
are invariant. We denote the path-space measure of this process, started from
\mu^{\varrho}_{N}
, by
\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho}_{N}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho}_{N}}\left[\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}}\right)^{2}\right]\leq e^{CN}.

(3.24)

Proof.

Note that we can split the Radon-Nikodym derivative in the following way

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varrho}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varrho_{c}}_{N}}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho}_{N}}.

(3.25)

The Radon-Nikodym derivative of the Poisson distributions can be bounded in the following way

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varrho}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varrho_{c}}_{N}}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)\log\left(\frac{\varrho(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma)}{\varrho_{c}}\right)-\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}(\varrho(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma)-\varrho_{c})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\log\left(\frac{||\varrho||_{\infty}}{\varrho_{c}}\right)|\eta^{N}|+2\varrho_{c}N\right).

(3.26)

Since the jump rates of the processes corresponding to
\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho}_{N}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}
only differ at the internal state jumps, by the Girsanov formula, we find that the Radon-Nikodym derivative is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho}_{N}}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\int_{0}^{T}\log\big(c(\sigma,m_{N}(t))\big)\mathrm{d}J_{t}^{(x,\sigma)\to(x,-\sigma)}-\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\int_{0}^{T}\eta^{N}_{t}(x,\sigma)\big(c(\sigma,m_{N}(t))-1\big)\mathrm{d}t\right),

(3.27)

where
J_{t}^{(x,\sigma)\to(x,-\sigma)}
is the number of jumps made from
(x,\sigma)
to
(x,-\sigma)
up to time
t
. Since
c(\sigma,m_{N}(t))
is bounded from above and below, we can find constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho}_{N}}\right)^{2}\leq\exp\left(c_{1}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}J_{T}^{(x,\sigma)\to(x,-\sigma)}+c_{2}T|\eta^{N}|\right),

(3.28)

where we recall that
|\eta^{N}|
is the total number of particles in the configuration
\eta^{N}
. Note that
\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}J_{T}^{(x,\sigma)\to(x,-\sigma)}
is the total number of internal state jumps up to time
T
, which under
\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho}_{N}
is a Poisson process with intensity
|\eta^{N}|
. Combining (
3.2
) and (
3.28
), we can find a constant
c_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho}_{N}}\left[\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq e^{4\varrho_{c}N}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho}_{N}}\left[\exp\left(c_{3}T|\eta^{N}|\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(4\varrho_{c}N+2||\varrho||_{\infty}\left(e^{c_{3}T}-1\right)N\right),

(3.29)

hence (
3.24
) holds.
∎

Now we can proceed with the proof of exponential tightness, using the approach from
[
14
]
. We will need the following result.

Lemma 3.6
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and
G\in C^{\infty}(V)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}\left(\sup_{|s-t|<\delta}\left|\left<\pi^{N}_{t},G\right>-\left<\pi^{N}_{s},G\right>\right|\geq\varepsilon\right)=-\infty.

(3.30)

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. First note that we have the following

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}\left(\sup_{|s-t|<\delta}\left|\left<\pi^{N}_{t},G\right>-\left<\pi^{N}_{s},G\right>\right|\geq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}\left(\sup_{|s-t|<\delta}\left<\pi^{N}_{t},G\right>-\left<\pi^{N}_{s},G\right>\geq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}\left(\sup_{|s-t|<\delta}\left<\pi^{N}_{t},-G\right>-\left<\pi^{N}_{s},-G\right>\geq\varepsilon\right).

(3.31)

Since we are considering every
G\in C^{\infty}(V)
, we can neglect the absolute value in (
3.30
).
Furthermore, by Hölder’s inequality we have that for a general event
A

\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}(A)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}}\left[\mathds{1}_{A}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}}\right]\leq\left(\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}(A)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho}_{N}}\left[\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}}\right)^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.32)

Therefore, by Lemma
3.5
, it is enough to prove the result for
\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}
.

Using the following inclusion.

\left\{\sup_{|s-t|<\delta}\langle\pi^{N}_{t},G\rangle-\langle\pi^{N}_{s},G\rangle\geq\varepsilon\right\}\subset\bigcup_{k=0}^{[T\delta^{-1}]}\left\{\sup_{0\leq t<\delta}\langle\pi^{N}_{k\delta+t},G\rangle-\langle\pi^{N}_{k\delta},G\rangle>\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right\},

(3.33)

we are able to find that

\displaystyle\uplim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}\left(\sup_{|s-t|<\delta}\langle\pi^{N}_{t},G\rangle-\langle\pi^{N}_{s},G\rangle\geq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\uplim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\max_{k=0}^{[T\delta^{-1}]}\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}\left(\sup_{0\leq t<\delta}\langle\pi^{N}_{k\delta+t},G\rangle-\langle\pi^{N}_{k\delta},G\rangle\geq\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\uplim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}\left(\sup_{0\leq t<\delta}\langle\pi^{N}_{t},G\rangle-\langle\pi^{N}_{0},G\rangle\geq\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon\right),

(3.34)

where in the last step we used that
\mu^{\varrho_{c}}_{N}
is invariant for the RTP system.
By denoting the exponential martingale corresponding to
\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{RTP}}_{N}
as
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathrm{RTP},G}_{N,t}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})
(recall the definition of the exponential martingale in (
3.1
)), and multiplying both sides by a constant
\lambda>0
, we find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}\left(\sup_{0\leq t<\delta}\langle\pi^{N}_{t},\lambda G\rangle-\langle\pi^{N}_{0},\lambda G\rangle\geq\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}\left(\sup_{0\leq t<\delta}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathcal{Z}^{\mathrm{RTP},\lambda G}_{N,t}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})+\frac{1}{N}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-N\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\lambda G_{s}\rangle}(\partial_{s}+\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{RTP}}_{N})e^{N\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\lambda G_{s}\rangle}\mathrm{d}s\geq\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}\left(\sup_{0\leq t<\delta}\mathcal{Z}^{\mathrm{RTP},\lambda G}_{N,t}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\geq e^{\frac{1}{8}N\lambda\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}\left(\sup_{0\leq t<\delta}\frac{1}{N}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-N\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\lambda G_{s}\rangle}(\partial_{s}+\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{RTP}}_{N})e^{N\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\lambda G_{s}\rangle}\mathrm{d}s\geq\tfrac{1}{8}\lambda\varepsilon\right).

(3.35)

Recalling that
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathrm{RTP},\lambda G}_{N,t}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})
is a non-negative martingale, by Doob’s martingale inequality we can upper bound the first part by

\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}\left(\sup_{0\leq t<\delta}\mathcal{Z}^{\mathrm{RTP},\lambda G}_{N,t}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\geq e^{\frac{1}{8}N\lambda\varepsilon}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}\left[\mathcal{Z}^{\mathrm{RTP},\lambda G}_{N,\delta}\right]\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{8}N\lambda\varepsilon}=e^{-\frac{1}{8}N\lambda\varepsilon}.

(3.36)

For the second part, using (
3.16
), we are able to find the following upper bound for the integrand

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}e^{-N\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\lambda G_{s}\rangle}(\partial_{s}+\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{RTP}}_{N})e^{N\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\lambda G_{s}\rangle}\leq\frac{1}{N}|\eta^{N}|\left(\lambda||\partial_{x}G||_{\infty}+M\left(e^{\lambda||\widetilde{G}||_{\infty}}-1\right)\right).

(3.37)

Therefore, using that
|\eta^{N}|
is Poisson distributed with parameter
N\varrho_{c}
under
\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}
, we find that by the Markov inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}\left(\sup_{0\leq t<\delta}\frac{1}{N}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-N\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\lambda G_{s}\rangle}(\partial_{s}+\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{RTP}}_{N})e^{N\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\lambda G_{s}\rangle}\mathrm{d}s\geq\tfrac{1}{8}\lambda\varepsilon\right)=\mathcal{O}(\delta).

(3.38)

Combining (
3.36
) and (
3.38
), we find that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{RTP},\varrho_{c}}_{N}\left(\sup_{|s-t|<\delta}\left|\left<\pi^{N}_{t},G\right>-\left<\pi^{N}_{s},G\right>\right|\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq-\frac{1}{8}\lambda\varepsilon,

(3.39)

and since we can take
\lambda
arbitrarily large, this concludes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to give a proof of the exponential tightness.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

We start by defining the following set

\mathcal{E}_{K}=\left\{\widehat{\alpha}\in D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V}):\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\widehat{\alpha}_{t}(V)\leq K\right\}.

(3.40)

For this set, by the Chernoff inequality, we find that

\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\notin\mathcal{E}_{K})=\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\pi^{N}_{t}(V)>K\right)\leq e^{-NK}\mathbb{E}^{\varrho}_{N}\left[\exp\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}N\pi^{N}_{t}(V)\right)\right],

(3.41)

where the expectation can be upper bounded in the following way

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\varrho}_{N}\left[\exp\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}N\pi^{N}_{t}(V)\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}^{\varrho}_{N}\left[\exp\left(|\eta^{N}|\right)\right]\leq e^{N||\varrho||_{\infty}(e-1)}.

(3.42)

Combining (
3.41
) and (
3.42
), we can find a sequence of numbers
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

\uplim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\notin\mathcal{E}_{K_{n}})\leq-n

(3.43)

Next we consider a countable uniformly dense family
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(V)
and define for each
\delta>0
and
\varepsilon>0
the following set

\mathcal{C}_{j,\delta,\varepsilon}=\left\{\widehat{\alpha}\in D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V}):\sup_{|t-s|<\delta}\left|\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},\phi_{j}\rangle-\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{s},\phi_{j}\rangle\right|\leq\varepsilon\right\}.

(3.44)

Note that for any choice of the parameters the set
\mathcal{C}_{j,\delta,\varepsilon}
is closed, and by Lemma
3.6
there exists a
\delta=\delta(j,m,n)
such that

\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\notin\mathcal{C}_{j,\delta,1/m})\leq\exp(-Nnmj)

(3.45)

for large enough
N
, and so

\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}\left(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\notin\bigcap_{j\geq 1,m\geq 1}\mathcal{C}_{j,\delta(j,m,n),1/m}\right)\leq\sum_{j\geq 1,m\geq 1}\exp(-Nnmj)\leq C\exp(-Nn),

(3.46)

for some constant
C>0
. By now considering the set

\mathcal{K}_{n}=\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\bigcap_{j\geq 1,m\geq 1}\mathcal{C}_{j,\delta(j,m,n),1/m},

(3.47)

it follows that (
3.18
) holds for this choice of
\mathcal{K}_{n}
. Since we furthermore know that it is closed we only need to show that it is relatively compact, which can be done by proving the following two things
[
14
, Proposition 4.1.2]
:

1.

\{\widehat{\alpha}_{t}:\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{n},t\in[0,T]\}
is relatively compact in
\mathcal{M}_{V}
.

2.

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}w_{\delta}(\widehat{\alpha})=0
, where

w_{\delta}(\widehat{\alpha}):=\sup_{|t-s|\leq\delta}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left(1\wedge|\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},\phi_{j}\rangle-\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{s},\phi_{j}\rangle|\right)=0.

(3.48)

Here item
1
. is satisfied since
\mathcal{K}_{n}\subset\mathcal{E}_{K_{n}}
and closed balls are compact in
\mathcal{M}_{V}
, and
2
. follows from the definition of the sets
C_{j,\delta,\varepsilon}
.
∎

3.3
Lower bound

In this section we will prove the large deviation lower bound, as given in (
3.8
). The main idea is to show that if
\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha})<\infty
, then there exists a function
H
such that

\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha})=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\alpha}_{0}}_{N}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{\alpha}_{0},H}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho}_{N}}\right].

(3.49)

To achieve this, we have to show two things. First we have to show that if
h^{\varrho}_{0}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})<\infty
, then
\widehat{\alpha}_{0}
has a density
\widehat{\varrho}_{0}
, and
h^{\varrho}_{0}
can be written as the relative entropy of product Poisson distributions with the respective densities
\varrho
and
\widehat{\varrho}_{0}
. After that, we have to show that if
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})<\infty
, then there exists a measurable
H:[0,T]\times V\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\widehat{\alpha}
satisfies the hydrodynamic equation of the weakly perturbed model, given in (
2.19
), and that
H
is then a function for which the supremum in the definition of
\mathcal{I}_{tr}
in (
3.4
) is attained.

The first step follows from the following Lemma

Lemma 3.7
.

If
h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})<\infty
, then
\widehat{\alpha}_{0}
has a density
\widehat{\varrho}_{0}:V\to\mathbb{R}
, and

h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0}}_{N}}\left[\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0}}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varrho}_{N}}\right].

(3.50)

Proof.

Assume
h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})<\infty
If
\widehat{\alpha}_{0}
is not absolutely continuous, then there exists a Borel set
A\subset V
such that
\widehat{\alpha}_{0}(A)>0
and
\lambda_{V}(A)=0
, with
\lambda_{V}
the Lebesgue measure on
V
. By the definition of
h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})
in (
3.3
), we have that for
\phi\in C^{\infty}(V)

\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{0},\phi\rangle\leq h_{0}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})+\langle\varrho,e^{\phi}-1\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}.

(3.51)

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
now take a sequence
(\phi^{(n)}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(V)
such that
\phi^{(n)}_{k}\to n\mathds{1}_{A}
pointwise as
k\to\infty
. It then follows that

\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{0},\phi^{(n)}_{k}\rangle\to n\widehat{\alpha}_{0}(A),\qquad\langle\varrho,e^{\phi^{(n)}_{k}}-1\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}\to 0,

(3.52)

as
k\to\infty
. By taking
n
large enough this contradicts (
3.51
),
hence we can conclude that
\widehat{\alpha}_{0}
has a density
\widehat{\varrho}_{0}
.

The rest of the proof of (
3.50
) follows then from calculating the supremum.

\displaystyle h^{\varrho}_{0}(\widehat{\alpha}_{0})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi}\left\{\langle\widehat{\varrho}_{0},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}-\langle\varrho,e^{\phi}-1\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{\varrho}_{0},\log(\tfrac{\widehat{\varrho}_{0}}{\varrho})\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}-\langle\widehat{\varrho}_{0}-\varrho,1\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0}}_{N}}\left[\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0}}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varrho}_{N}}\right].

(3.53)

where the supremum is attained for
\phi=\log(\frac{\widehat{\varrho}_{0}}{\varrho})
.
∎

By a similar argument as in the previous lemma, we can show that if
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})<\infty
then there exists a density
\widehat{\varrho}:[0,T]\times V\to\mathbb{R}
for the whole trajectory
\widehat{\alpha}
. In the following Lemma we prove an alternative formula for the dynamic part of the rate function in the case that
\widehat{\varrho}_{t}(v)>0
for all
t\in[0,T],v\in V
.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})<\infty
and
\widehat{\varrho}>0
then there exists a bounded measurable function
H
such that
\widehat{\alpha}
satisfies the equation (
2.19
) in the weak sense. Furthermore,
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})=\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha};H)
and

\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},\left(e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)}(-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)-1)+1\right)c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t.

(3.54)

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathcal{I}_{tr}
in (
3.4
), we have the following

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times V)\\
||G||_{\infty}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\{\ell(\widehat{\alpha};G)-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\left(e^{-\sigma\widetilde{G}_{t}(x)}-1\right)\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t\right\}\leq\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha}),

(3.55)

and so

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times V)\\
||G||_{\infty}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\ell(\widehat{\alpha};G)\leq\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})+\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\left(e-1\right)\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

(3.56)

Consequently, by the Hahn-Banach theorem, we can extend the linear functional
\ell(\widehat{\alpha};\cdot)
to a bounded linear functional in
C([0,T]\times V)
. Therefore, by the Riesz representation theorem, there exists a signed measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{[0,T]\times V}
such that

\ell(\widehat{\alpha};G)=\langle\nu,G\rangle:=\int_{[0,T]\times V}Gd\nu.

(3.57)

Again, since we assume that
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})<\infty
, this measure
\nu
has a density
g:[0,T]\times V\to\mathbb{R}
. By then plugging in the definition of
\ell(\widehat{\alpha};G)
in (
3.5
), we find that
\widehat{\alpha}
satisfies

\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{T},G_{T}\rangle-\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{0},G_{0}\rangle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},(\partial_{t}+\sigma\partial_{x})G_{t}\rangle\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\langle g_{t},G_{t}\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}\mathrm{d}t,

(3.58)

i.e., it satisfies the following PDE in the weak sense

\dot{\widehat{\alpha}}_{t}(x,\sigma)=-\sigma\partial_{x}\widehat{\alpha}_{t}(x,\sigma)+g_{t}(x,\sigma).

(3.59)

We can now split up
g_{t}(x,\sigma)=\sigma f_{t}(x)+h_{t}(x)
, and we will show that
h\equiv 0
almost everywhere. To see this, note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\sup_{G}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\langle\sigma f_{t},G_{t}\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\langle h_{t},G_{t}\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\left(e^{-\sigma\widetilde{G}_{t}(x)}-1\right)\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\widetilde{G},\overline{G}}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\langle f_{t},\widetilde{G}_{t}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\langle h_{t},\overline{G}_{t}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\left(e^{-\sigma\widetilde{G}_{t}(x)}-1\right)\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t\right\},

(3.60)

where
\overline{G}_{t}(x)=G_{t}(x,1)+G_{t}(x,-1)
. By considering functions where
G_{t}(x,1)=G_{t}(x,-1)
, it follows that if
h\not\equiv 0
almost everywhere then the last supremum is infinite, which contradicts
\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha})<\infty
. Therefore,

\dot{\widehat{\alpha}}_{t}(x,\sigma)=-\sigma\partial_{x}\widehat{\alpha}_{t}(x,\sigma)+\sigma f_{t}(x)

(3.61)

holds weakly, with
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
some bounded measurable function.

Setting
\Psi_{t}(x,\sigma)=e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)}
in (
2.19
), we solve the following equation for
\Psi_{t}
,

\sigma f_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{\Psi_{t}(x,\sigma)}c(-\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\widehat{\varrho}_{t}(x,-\sigma)-\Psi_{t}(x,\sigma)c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\widehat{\varrho}_{t}(x,\sigma)

(3.62)

This is a quadratic equation in
\Psi_{t}
, with the following positive and bounded solution

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}(x,\sigma)=\frac{-\sigma f_{t}(x)+\sqrt{f_{t}(x)^{2}+4\widehat{\varrho}_{t}(x,\sigma)c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\widehat{\varrho}_{t}(x,-\sigma)c(-\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))}}{2\widehat{\varrho}_{t}(x,\sigma)c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))}.

(3.63)

It is a straightforward calculation to show that
\Psi(x,\sigma)\cdot\Psi(x,-\sigma)=1
, and so (
2.19
) holds for
\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)=-\log(\Psi(x,1))
.

Now, assuming that
\widehat{\alpha}
satisfies (
2.19
) we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\sup_{G}\left\{\ell(\widehat{\alpha};G)-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},\left(e^{-\sigma\widetilde{G}_{t}(x)}-1\right)c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\rangle\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{G}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},\left(-e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)}\sigma\widetilde{G}_{t}(x)-e^{-\sigma\widetilde{G}_{t}(x)}+1\right)c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\Big\langle\widehat{\alpha}_{t},\sup_{p\in\mathbb{R}}\left(-e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)}\sigma p-e^{-\sigma p}+1\right)c(\sigma,m(\widehat{\alpha}_{t}))\Big\rangle\mathrm{d}t,

(3.64)

where we can interchange the supremum in the last integral by dominated convergence. This supremum is attained for
p=\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)
, indeed showing that
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})=\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha};H)
. After filling this in, it follows that (
3.54
) holds.
∎

We now have a clear formulation of the rate function when
\widehat{\alpha}
has positive density, however if the density can be zero then the formulation in (
3.63
) is not well-defined. Furthermore, in order for the hydrodynamic limit of the weakly perturbed model to hold in Theorem
2.1
, we need to assume that
H\in C^{1,0}([0,T]\times V)
. We therefore define the following space

D_{0}([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V}):=\left\{\widehat{\alpha}\in D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V}):\widehat{\varrho}>0,\text{ $\widehat{\alpha}$ satisfies \eqref{weak-PDE} with $H\in C^{1,0}([0,T]\times V)$}\right\}

(3.65)

We will now show that the rate function of trajectories outside this set can be approximated by the rate function of trajectories within this set.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\widehat{\alpha}\in D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
such
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})<\infty
, then there exists a sequence
(\widehat{\alpha}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset D_{0}([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
such that
\widehat{\alpha}_{k}\to\widehat{\alpha}
weakly and

\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha}_{k}).

(3.66)

Proof.

We first show that
\widehat{\alpha}\in D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
can be approximated by trajectories with positive density. We define the following measure for any
\varepsilon>0

\widehat{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon)\widehat{\alpha}+\varepsilon 1

(3.67)

where
1
on the right-hand side denotes the measure with constant density equal to 1. It follows that
\widehat{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}
has positive density and that
\widehat{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}\to\widehat{\alpha}
weakly as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Therefore, by convexity and lower semicontinuity of the rate function
\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}
, we then find that

\uplim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})\leq\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})\leq\lowlim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}).

(3.68)

Hence we have indeed found a good approximation.

Now assume that
\widehat{\alpha}\in D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
has density
\widehat{\varrho}>0
and that
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})<\infty
. By Lemma
3.8
, there exists a bounded measurable
H:[0,T]\times V\to\mathbb{R}
such that (
2.19
) holds weakly. Now find a sequence
\widehat{\alpha}_{k}
with densities
\widehat{\varrho}_{k}\in C^{2,1}([0,T]\times V)
such that
\widehat{\varrho}_{k}\to\widehat{\varrho}
pointwise as
k\to\infty
. It then follows that
\widehat{\alpha}_{k}\to\widehat{\alpha}
weakly and, by (
3.61
) and (
3.63
), each
\widehat{\alpha}_{k}
satisfies (
2.19
) for some function
H_{k}\in C^{1,0}([0,T]\times V)
where
H_{k}\to H
pointwise. By the formulation of
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})
in (
3.54
), we can then indeed conclude that (
3.66
) holds.
∎

Theorem 3.10

(Lower bound)
.

Given a
\widehat{\alpha}\in D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
, for every neighborhood
\mathcal{O}\subset D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
of
\widehat{\alpha}
we have that

\lowlim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{O})\geq-\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}).

(3.69)

As a consequence, for every open set
\mathcal{O}\subset D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
we have that

\lowlim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{O})\geq-\inf_{\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}).

(3.70)

Proof.

If
\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha})=\infty
, the result is immediate, therefore we can assume that
\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha})<\infty
. By Lemma
3.9
it is then enough to prove it for
\widehat{\alpha}\in D_{0}([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
, and so by Theorem
2.3
there exists an
H\in C^{1,0}([0,T]\times V)
such that

\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0},H}_{N}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\cdot)\to\delta_{\widehat{\alpha}}.

(3.71)

where
\widehat{\varrho}_{0}
is the density of
\widehat{\alpha}_{0}
. Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle\lowlim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}^{\varrho}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{O})

\displaystyle=-\lowlim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0},H}_{N}\left[\mathds{1}_{\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{O}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0}}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varrho}_{N}}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0},H}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0}}_{N}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq-\lowlim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0},H}_{N}\left[\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0}}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varrho}_{N}}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0},H}_{N}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\widehat{\varrho}_{0}}_{N}}\right]

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{I}^{\varrho}(\widehat{\alpha}),

(3.72)

where we used Lemma
3.7
, Corollary
3.2
and that
\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha})=\mathcal{I}_{tr}(\widehat{\alpha};H)
.
∎

4
Proof of the hydrodynamic limit

In this section we prove the hydrodynamic limit of the weakly perturbed process defined in Section
2.2
. We first prove that the PDE given in (
2.19
) is well-posed, and afterwards we prove Theorem (
2.3
).

4.1
Well-posedness of the PDE

The approach is to define a sequence of densities
\varrho^{(n)}
through the following recursive relation

\partial_{t}\varrho^{(n+1)}_{t}(x,\sigma)=-\sigma\partial_{x}\varrho^{(n+1)}_{t}(x,\sigma)+c(-\sigma,m(\varrho^{(n)}_{t}))e^{\sigma H_{t}(x)}\varrho^{(n+1)}_{t}(x,-\sigma)\\
-c(\sigma,m(\varrho^{(n)}_{t}))e^{-\sigma H_{t}(x)}\varrho^{(n+1)}_{t}(x,\sigma),

(4.1)

where every
\varrho^{(n)}
starts from
\varrho^{(n)}_{0}=\varrho
.
Setting
\mathcal{T}(\varrho^{(n)})=\varrho^{(n+1)}
, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction (up to some finite time
T>0
) in the space
L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{1}(V))
. First note that for every trajectory
\varrho^{(n)}
, the trajectory
\varrho^{(n+1)}
solving (
4.1
) satisfies the conservation of particles, hence for any
t\geq 0
we have that
||\varrho^{(n+1)}_{t}||_{L^{1}(V)}=||\varrho||_{L^{1}(V)}
with
\varrho
the initial profile. Therefore, we indeed have that
\mathcal{T}:L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{1}(V))\to L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{1}(V))
. Now let
\varrho_{1}
,
\varrho_{2}
be any two trajectories of densities, and denote the following

\displaystyle\psi_{1}=\mathcal{T}(\varrho_{1}),

\displaystyle\psi_{2}=\mathcal{T}(\varrho_{2}),

\displaystyle m_{1,t}=m(\varrho_{1,t}),

\displaystyle m_{2,t}=m(\varrho_{2,t}).

(4.2)

Furthermore, denote
\delta=\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}
and
\varepsilon=m_{1}-m_{2}
. For
[m]=\{m_{t}:t\in[0,T]\}
a deterministic trajectory of magnetizations, we define the mapping

Q_{[m]}[\psi](x,\sigma,t)=c(-\sigma,m_{t})e^{\sigma H_{t}(x)}\psi_{t}(x,-\sigma)-c(\sigma,m_{t})e^{-\sigma H_{t}(x)}\psi_{t}(x,\sigma).

(4.3)

Note that this mapping is linear in
\psi
. We then have that

\partial_{t}\delta_{t}(x,\sigma)=-\sigma\partial_{x}\delta_{t}(x,\sigma)+Q_{[m_{1}]}[\psi_{1}](x,\sigma,t)-Q_{[m_{2}]}[\psi_{2}](x,\sigma,t).

(4.4)

We can rewrite the difference of the last two terms in a linear and non-linear part as follow

Q_{[m_{1}]}[\psi_{1}]-Q_{[m_{2}]}[\psi_{2}]=Q_{[m_{1}]}[\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}]+\left(Q_{[m_{1}]}[\psi_{2}]-Q_{[m_{2}]}[\psi_{2}]\right).

(4.5)

For the linear part, we have that

\displaystyle||Q_{[m_{1}]}[\delta](\cdot,\cdot,t)||_{L^{1}(V)}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|c(-\sigma,m_{1,t})e^{\sigma H_{t}(x)}\delta_{t}(x,-\sigma)-c(\sigma,m_{1,t})e^{-\sigma H_{t}(x)}\delta_{t}(x,\sigma)\right|\right|_{L^{1}(V)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}||\delta_{t}||_{L^{1}(V)},

(4.6)

with
C_{1}
some constant depending on the bounded functions
c
and
H
. For the non-linear part, we have that

\displaystyle Q_{[m_{1}]}[\psi_{2}](x,\sigma,t)-Q_{[m_{2}]}[\psi_{2}](x,\sigma,t)

\displaystyle=[c(-\sigma,m_{1,t})-c(-\sigma,m_{2,t})]e^{\sigma H_{t}(x)}\psi_{2,t}(x,-\sigma)-[c(\sigma,m_{1,t})-c(\sigma,m_{2,t})]e^{-\sigma H_{t}(x)}\psi_{2,t}(x,\sigma).

(4.7)

By the Lipschitz-continuity of
c(\sigma,m)
we now have that

||Q_{[m_{1}]}[\psi_{2}](\cdot,\cdot,t)-Q_{[m_{2}]}[\psi_{2}](\cdot,\cdot,t)||_{L^{1}(V)}\leq L|\varepsilon_{t}|\cdot C_{2}||\psi_{2,t}||_{L^{1}(V)},

(4.8)

with
L
the Lipschitz-constant and
C_{2}
some constant depending on
H
. Here
||\psi_{2,t}||_{L^{1}(V)}=||\varrho||_{L^{1}(V)}
by conservation of particles. Furthermore, we have that

\displaystyle|\varepsilon_{t}|=|m_{1,t}-m_{2,t}|=\frac{1}{||\varrho||_{L^{1}(V)}}\cdot||\varrho_{1,t}-\varrho_{2,t}||_{L^{1}(V)}.

(4.9)

From (
4.1
) and (
4.8
), we find that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}||\delta_{t}||_{L^{1}(V)}

\displaystyle\leq||Q_{[m_{1}]}[\delta](\cdot,\cdot,t)||_{L^{1}(V)}+||Q_{[m_{1}]}[\psi_{2}](\cdot,\cdot,t)-Q_{[m_{2}]}[\psi_{2}](\cdot,\cdot,t)||_{L^{1}(V)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}||\delta_{t}||_{L^{1}(V)}+LC_{2}||\varrho_{1,t}-\varrho_{2,t}||_{L^{1}(V)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}||\delta_{t}||_{L^{1}(V)}+LC_{2}||\varrho_{1}-\varrho_{2}||_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{1}(V))}.

(4.10)

Note that the transport term vanishes since
\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}|\delta_{t}(x,\sigma)|dx=0
by periodic boundary conditions. We can rewrite this in integral form as

||\delta_{t}||_{L^{1}(V)}\leq\int_{0}^{t}C_{1}||\delta_{s}||_{L^{1}(V)}\mathrm{d}s+LC_{2}t||\varrho_{1}-\varrho_{2}||_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{1}(V))}.

(4.11)

By Gronwall’s inequality, we find that

||\delta_{t}||_{L^{1}(V)}\leq LC_{2}te^{C_{1}t}||\varrho_{1}-\varrho_{2}||_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{1}(V))},

(4.12)

and so we can conclude that

||\mathcal{T}(\varrho_{1})-\mathcal{T}(\varrho_{2})||_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{1}(V))}\leq LC_{2}Te^{C_{1}T}||\varrho_{1}-\varrho_{2}||_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{1}(V))}.

(4.13)

Taking
T>0
small enough, this is a contraction on
L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{1}(V))
, hence the sequence
\varrho^{(n)}
converges locally to a unique solution of the PDE (
2.19
).

4.2
Proof of Theorem (
2.3
)

Recall that
\alpha^{H}\in D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
denotes the process such that for every
t\in[0,T]
the measure
\alpha^{H}_{t}
has density
\varrho^{H}_{t}(x,\sigma)
, which is the solution the the PDE given by (
2.19
).
In this way,
\alpha^{H}
is the unique trajectory of measures measure such that for every
G\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times V)
and
t\in[0,T]
we have that

\mathscr{M}_{t}^{H,G}(\alpha^{H}):=\left<\alpha^{H}_{t},G_{t}\right>-\left<\alpha^{H}_{0},G_{0}\right>-\int_{0}^{t}\left<\alpha^{H}_{s},\left(\partial_{s}+\left(\!A^{H}_{s,\alpha^{H}}\!\right)^{\!*}\right)G_{s}\right>\mathrm{d}s=0,

(4.14)

where
\left(\!A^{H}_{s,\alpha^{H}}\!\right)^{\!*}
is the differential operator given by

\left(\!A^{H}_{s,\alpha^{H}}\!\right)^{\!*}G_{s}(x,\sigma)=\sigma\partial_{x}G_{s}(x,\sigma)+c(\sigma,m(\alpha^{H}_{s}))e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{s}(x)}\big(G_{s}(x,-\sigma)-G_{s}(x,\sigma)\big),

(4.15)

which is the action of the adjoint of
A^{H}_{s,\alpha^{H}}
on smooth functions, with
A^{H}_{s,\alpha^{H}}
given by

A^{H}_{s,\alpha^{H}}G(x,\sigma)=-\sigma\partial_{x}G(x,\sigma)+c(-\sigma,m(\alpha^{H}_{s}))e^{\sigma\widetilde{H}_{s}(x)}G(x,-\sigma)-c(\sigma,m(\alpha^{H}_{s}))e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{s}(x)}G(x,\sigma).

(4.16)

For a given
G\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times V)
we define the Dynkin Martingale

\mathscr{M}_{N,t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}):=\langle\pi^{N}_{t},G_{t}\rangle-\langle\pi^{N}_{0},G_{0}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{s}+\mathscr{L}^{H}_{N,s})\langle\pi^{N}_{s},G_{s}\rangle\mathrm{d}s.

(4.17)

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
G\in C^{\infty}(V)
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathscr{M}_{N,t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})-\mathscr{M}_{t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\right|\right]=0.

(4.18)

Proof.

Note that

\mathscr{M}_{N,t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})-\mathscr{M}_{t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathscr{L}^{H}_{N,s}\langle\pi^{N}_{s},G_{s}\rangle-\left\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\left(\!A^{H}_{s,\pi^{N}_{\cdot}}\!\right)^{\!*}G_{s}\right\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}s.

(4.19)

So we need to calculate
\mathscr{L}^{H}_{N,s}\langle\pi^{N}_{s},G_{s}\rangle
. In order to do that, we start with the preliminary calculation

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{H}_{N,s}\langle\pi^{N}_{s},G_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle=N\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta^{N}_{s}(x,\sigma)e^{H_{s}(\frac{x+\sigma}{N},\sigma)-H_{s}(\frac{x}{N},\sigma)}\left[\big\langle\pi^{N}\big((\eta^{N}_{s})^{(x,\sigma)\to(x+\sigma,\sigma)}\big),G_{s}\big\rangle-\langle\pi^{N}_{s},G_{s}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta_{s}^{N}(x,\sigma)c(\sigma,m(\pi^{N}_{s}))e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{s}(x)}\left[\big\langle\pi^{N}\big((\eta^{N}_{t})^{(x,\sigma)\to(x,-\sigma)}\big),G_{s}\big\rangle-\langle\pi^{N}_{s},G_{s}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta^{N}_{s}(x,\sigma)e^{H_{s}(\frac{x+\sigma}{N},\sigma)-H_{s}(\frac{x}{N},\sigma)}\big(G_{s}(\tfrac{x+\sigma}{N},\sigma)-G_{s}(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta_{s}^{N}(x,\sigma)c(\sigma,m(\pi^{N}_{s}))e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{s}(x)}\big(G_{s}(x,-\sigma)-G_{s}(x,\sigma)\big),

(4.20)

Using Taylor approximation, we can now write

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{H}_{N,s}\langle\pi^{N}_{s},G_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta^{N}_{s}(x,\sigma)\left[\sigma\partial_{x}G_{s}(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma)+c(\sigma,m(\pi^{N}_{s}))e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{s}(x)}\big(G_{s}(x,-\sigma)-G_{s}(x,\sigma)\big)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+R(N,H,G,s)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\left(\!A^{H}_{s,\pi^{N}_{\cdot}}\!\right)^{\!*}G_{s}\right\rangle+R(N,H,G,s),

(4.21)

where

\displaystyle R(N,H,G,s)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)||\partial_{x}G||_{\infty}\sup_{(y,\sigma^{\prime})\in V_{N}}\big|H_{s}(\tfrac{y+\sigma^{\prime}}{N},\sigma^{\prime})-H_{s}(\tfrac{y}{N},\sigma^{\prime})\big|

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta(x,\sigma)||\partial_{xx}G||_{\infty}e^{2||H||_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}|\eta^{N}|\left(||\partial_{x}G||_{\infty}\sup_{(y,\sigma^{\prime})\in V_{N}}\big|H_{s}(\tfrac{y+\sigma^{\prime}}{N},\sigma^{\prime})-H_{s}(\tfrac{y}{N},\sigma^{\prime})\big|+\frac{1}{N}||\partial_{xx}G||_{\infty}e^{2||H||_{\infty}}\right).

(4.22)

We then conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathscr{M}_{N,t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})-\mathscr{M}_{t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}R(N,H,G,s)\mathrm{d}s\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T}{N}\left(||\partial_{x}G||_{\infty}\sup_{(y,\sigma^{\prime})\in V_{N}}\big|H_{s}(\tfrac{y+\sigma^{\prime}}{N},\sigma^{\prime})-H_{s}(\tfrac{y}{N},\sigma^{\prime})\big|+\frac{1}{N}||\partial_{xx}G||_{\infty}e^{2||H||_{\infty}}\right)\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[|\eta^{N}|\right]\to 0,

(4.23)

where we used that
\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[|\eta^{N}|\right]\leq||\varrho||_{\infty}N
and that
H_{s}
is continuous on
\mathbb{T}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
G\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times V)
we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathscr{M}_{N,t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\right)^{2}\right]=0.

(4.24)

Proof.

By Doob’s maximal inequality we find that

\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathscr{M}_{N,t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\right)^{2}\right]\leq 4\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\left(\mathscr{M}_{N,T}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\right)^{2}\right]=4\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{N,t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{t})\mathrm{d}t\right],

(4.25)

where in the last equality we use that the quadratic variation of the martingale
\mathscr{M}_{N,t}^{H,G}
is given by the integral of the carré du champ operator
\Gamma_{H,t}^{N,G}
defined by

\Gamma_{N,t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{t})=\mathscr{L}^{H}_{N,t}(\langle\pi^{N}_{t},G_{t}\rangle)^{2}-2\langle\pi^{N}_{t},G_{t}\rangle\cdot\mathscr{L}^{H}_{N,t}\langle\pi^{N}_{t},G_{t}\rangle.

(4.26)

For a general jump process generator
Lf(\eta)=\sum_{\eta^{\prime}}r(\eta,\eta^{\prime})(f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta))
we have that

Lf^{2}(\eta)-2f(\eta)\cdot Lf(\eta)=\sum_{\eta^{\prime}}r(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\big(f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta)\big)^{2},

(4.27)

and so we find that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{N,t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta^{N}_{t}(x,\sigma)e^{H_{t}(\frac{x+\sigma}{N},\sigma)-H_{t}(\frac{x}{N},\sigma)}\big(G_{t}(\tfrac{x+\sigma}{N},\sigma)-G_{t}(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma)\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta^{N}_{t}(x,\sigma)c(\sigma,m(\pi^{N}_{t}))e^{-\sigma\widetilde{H}_{t}(x)}\big(G_{t}(x,-\sigma)-G_{t}(x,\sigma)\big)^{2}.

(4.28)

By the mean value theorem and since
c(\sigma,m)
is bounded, we can find an upper bound given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{N,t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\leq\mathcal{O}(\tfrac{1}{N^{2}})\cdot|\eta^{N}|.

(4.29)

Using again that
\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[|\eta^{N}|\right]\leq||\varrho||_{\infty}N
, we then find that

\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathscr{M}_{N,t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\right)^{2}\right]\leq 4T\mathcal{O}(\tfrac{1}{N^{2}})\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[|\eta^{N}|\right]\to 0.

(4.30)

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

\{\pi^{N}_{\cdot}:N\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight in
D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
.

Proof.

By Aldous’ criteria, we have to show the following:

B.1

For all
t\in[0,T]
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact
K(t,\varepsilon)\subset\mathcal{M}_{V}
such that

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\big(\pi^{N}_{t}\notin K(t,\varepsilon)\big)\leq\varepsilon.

(4.31)

B.2

For all
\varepsilon>0

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\big(\omega(\pi^{N}_{\cdot},\delta)\geq\varepsilon\big)=0,

(4.32)

where

\omega(\pi^{N}_{\cdot},\delta)=\sup\{d(\pi^{N}_{t},\pi^{N}_{s})|:s,t\in[0,T],|t-s|<\delta\},

(4.33)

with
d
the metric on
\mathcal{M}_{V}
defined for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{M}_{V}
as

d(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}\Big(1\wedge\big|\left<\alpha,\phi_{j}\right>-\left<\beta,\phi_{j}\right>\big|\Big).

(4.34)

We start with proving
B.1
. For every
C>0
, we have that the set

K_{C}=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{V}:\mu(V)\leq C\right\}

is compact in
\mathcal{M}_{V}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}(\pi^{N}_{t}\notin K_{C})=\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}(\pi^{N}_{t}(V)>C)\leq\frac{1}{C}\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}[\pi^{N}_{t}(V)],

(4.35)

where we used the Markov inequality in the last step. Here

\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}[\pi^{N}_{t}(V)]=\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta_{t}^{N}(x,\sigma)\delta_{(\tfrac{x}{N},\sigma)}(V)\right]=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\big[|\eta^{N}|\big]\leq||\varrho||_{\infty}.

(4.36)

Therefore

\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}(\pi^{N}_{t}\notin K_{C})\leq\frac{1}{C}||\varrho||_{\infty}.

(4.37)

Since we took
C
arbitrarily, we can take
C>||\varrho||_{\infty}\varepsilon^{-1}
, and
B.1
follows.

To prove
B.2
, take
\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon
and note that by the Markov inequality we have that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}(\omega(\pi^{N}_{\cdot},\delta)>\varepsilon)\leq\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\omega(\pi^{N}_{\cdot},\delta)\right]\leq\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\omega(\pi^{N}_{\cdot},\delta)\right].

(4.38)

Now for
\omega(\pi^{N}_{\cdot},\delta)
we have that

\displaystyle\omega(\pi^{N}_{\cdot},\delta)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,T]\\
|t-s|<\delta\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}\Big(1\wedge\big|\langle\pi^{N}_{t},\phi_{j}\rangle-\pi^{N}_{s}(\phi_{j})\big|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-m}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,T]\\
|t-s|<\delta\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big|\langle\pi^{N}_{t},\phi_{j}\rangle-\pi^{N}_{s}(\phi_{j})\big|,

(4.39)

where we took
m\in\mathbb{N}
arbitrarily. Using the martingale
\mathscr{M}_{N,t}^{H,\phi_{j}}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})
, we find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\big|\langle\pi^{N}_{t},\phi_{j}\rangle-\pi^{N}_{s}(\phi_{j})\big|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\left|\mathscr{M}_{N,t}^{H,\phi_{j}}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})-\mathscr{M}_{N,s}^{H,\phi_{j}}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})-\int_{s}^{t}\mathscr{L}^{H}_{N,s}\langle\pi^{N}_{r},\phi_{j}\rangle\mathrm{d}r\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathscr{M}_{N,t}^{H,\phi_{j}}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot})\right|\right]+\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\left|\int_{s}^{t}\mathscr{L}^{H}_{N,s}\langle\pi^{N}_{r},\phi_{j}\rangle\mathrm{d}r\right|\right].

(4.40)

By Lemma
4.2
the first expectation vanishes as
N\to\infty
. By (
4.2
) we can upper bound the second expectation by

\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\left|\int_{s}^{t}\mathscr{L}^{H}_{N,s}\langle\pi^{N}_{r},\phi_{j}\rangle\mathrm{d}r\right|\right]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\left|\int_{s}^{t}\left\langle\pi^{N}_{r},\left(\!A^{H}_{s,\pi^{N}_{\cdot}}\!\right)^{\!*}\phi_{j}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}r\right|\right]+\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\left|\int_{s}^{t}R(N,\phi_{j},r)\mathrm{d}r\right|\right].

(4.41)

From (
4.2
) we see that the second expectation also vanishes as
N\to\infty
(uniformly in
s
and
t
). For the first expectation note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\left|\int_{s}^{t}\left\langle\pi^{N}_{r},\left(\!A^{H}_{s,\pi^{N}_{\cdot}}\!\right)^{\!*}\phi_{j}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}r\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\left|\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,\sigma)\in V_{N}}\eta_{r}^{N}(x,\sigma)\left|\left|\left(\!A^{H}_{s,\pi^{N}_{\cdot}}\!\right)^{\!*}\phi_{j}\right|\right|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}r\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\left|\left|\left(\!A^{H}_{s,\pi^{N}_{\cdot}}\!\right)^{\!*}\phi_{j}\right|\right|_{\infty}|t-s|\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\big[|\eta^{N}|\big]

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left|\left(\!A^{H}_{s,\pi^{N}_{\cdot}}\!\right)^{\!*}\phi_{j}\right|\right|_{\infty}||\varrho||_{\infty}\delta.

(4.42)

Combining all of the above, we find that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}(\omega(\pi^{N}_{\cdot},\delta)>\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}2^{-m}+\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N}\left[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,T]\\
|t-s|<\delta\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big|\langle\pi^{N}_{t},\phi_{j}\rangle-\pi^{N}_{s}(\phi_{j})\big|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}2^{-m}+\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\left|\left|\left(\!A^{H}_{s,\pi^{N}_{\cdot}}\!\right)^{\!*}\phi_{j}\right|\right|_{\infty}||\varrho||_{\infty}\delta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}2^{-m}.

(4.43)

Since we took
m
arbitrarily, we can choose it such that
2^{-m}\leq(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2}
, i.e.,

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N}(\omega(\pi^{N}_{\cdot},\delta)>\varepsilon)<\varepsilon^{\prime},

(4.44)

and since we took
\varepsilon^{\prime}
arbitrarily small, we indeed find that
B.2
holds.
∎

We are now ready to give the proof of the hydrodynamic limit of the weakly perturbed model.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

By Prokhorov’s theorem, the tightness of the sequence
\{\pi^{N}_{\cdot}:N\in\mathbb{N}\}
implies that the sequence is sequentially compact. If we then prove that every convergent subsequence converges to
\delta_{\alpha}
, then the theorem holds.

Take such a convergent subsequence
\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N_{k}}(\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\in\cdot)\to P^{*}
, with
P^{*}
a probability measure on
D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V})
. For a given
\varepsilon>0
and
G\in C^{\infty}(V)
,
define the set

\Xi^{H,G}_{\varepsilon}=\left\{\beta\in D([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{V}):\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathscr{M}^{H,G}_{t}(\beta)\right|\leq\epsilon\right\},

(4.45)

which is closed in the Skorokhod topology. By Portmanteau’s theorem, we now have that

\displaystyle P^{*}(\Xi^{H,G}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N_{k}}(\pi^{N_{k}}_{\cdot}\in\Xi^{H,G}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N_{k}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathscr{M}_{t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N_{k}}_{\cdot})\right|\leq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N_{k}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathscr{M}_{N_{k},t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N_{k}}_{\cdot})\right|\leq\varepsilon\right),

(4.46)

where we used Lemma
4.1
for the last step. Now by using Chebyshev’s inequality together with Lemma
4.2
, we find that

\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N_{k}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathscr{M}_{N_{k},t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N_{k}}_{\cdot})\right|>\varepsilon\right)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\varrho,H}_{N_{k}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathscr{M}_{N_{k},t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N_{k}}_{\cdot})\right|^{2}\right]\to 0,

(4.47)

and so indeed

P^{*}(\Xi^{H,G}_{\varepsilon})\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\varrho,H}_{N_{k}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathscr{M}_{N_{k},t}^{H,G}(\pi^{N_{k}}_{\cdot})\right|\leq\varepsilon\right)=1.

(4.48)

Since this is true for all
\varepsilon>0
and
G\in C^{\infty}(V)
, it follows that
P^{*}=\delta_{\alpha^{H}}
.
∎

In-situ dual-lighting system for HER2 nucleic acid and protein co-localization through simultaneous visual signal amplification in single pathological sections
Authors: Tiantian Wang, Zhiqing Chen, Xuan Fu, Lin Chen, Yao Huang, Ang Liu, Qingling Zhang, Bei Hu, Weiyang Liang, Qunyue Wu, Shan Li, Xin Li, Lei Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe pathological state of human epidermal growth factor receptor-2 (HER2) determines the treatment strategies. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) and in situ hybridization (ISH) are the only two recognized diagnostic methods for HER2-positive (HER2 + ). IHC, although widely used, has limited reliability and often requires further confirmation by ISH for IHC ≤ 2 + . Furthermore, studies have demonstrated that HER2-low patients may still benefit from HER2-targeted therapy, highlighting the need for accurate and sensitive detection methods.MethodsIn this study, we designed the molecular beacon (MB) for HER2 amplification detection. Subsequently, the in-situ dual-lighting system (ISDLS) was established by combining MB-based loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) and tyramide signal amplification (TSA)-based IHC for HER2 in-situ nucleic acid and protein detection in a single sample, and was verified in both cell line slides and human tumor sections. To better elucidate the significance of our study, we compared the test results with clinical reports.ResultsHere, we show that, in a single sample, satisfactory nucleic acid and protein signals are obtained, especially in human tumor sections within one day. The detection rate for clinical IHC ≥ 1+ and fluorescence in situ hybridization amplification (FISH + ) sections reaches 100%, and the detection rate of clinical FISH- sections reaches 77.8%.ConclusionsISDLS enables the spatial in-situ distribution of amplified nucleic acid and protein signals in one section, providing a basis for multidimensional analysis of diseases. ISDLS is also expected to improve the speed, sensitivity, and accuracy of detection compared to other methods.

Introduction

Nucleic acids and proteins are crucial bio-macromolecules, and their differential expression patterns not only underpin the distinct identities and specialized functions of cells, but also serve as pivotal determinants in disease pathogenesis1,2, diagnosis3, therapeutic intervention4,5. Therefore, it is very important to rapidly, precisely, specifically, and sensitively identify cell types and molecular characteristics by integrating nucleic acid and protein levels to capture regions of interest in samples6–8, especially in a single clinical sample9. It can avoid the impact of multiple clinical tests or sample differences on the results, and find abnormalities that cannot be found by single-level testing, so as to improve the accuracy of detection and achieve more accurate personalized medicine10. Therefore, an efficient and accurate one-step imaging method for simultaneous detection of nucleic acids and proteins is urgently required11, especially in clinicopathological sections.

Human epidermal growth factor receptor-2 (HER2) is a recognized mediator of oncogenic processes dysregulated in many solid tumors12,13, including protein overexpression and/or gene amplification14. The marked differential expression of HER2 in normal versus tumor cells makes it an attractive marker for disease diagnosis and targeted therapy15,16, especially in breast cancer17 and gastric cancer18. For HER2-targeted therapy, Enhertu (DS-8201a)19, an antibody-drug conjugate with a topoisomerase I inhibitor, has been approved for treating eligible patients, which also offers new hope for patients with HER2-low breast cancer20,21. However, a positive clinicopathological diagnosis becomes the gold standard for allowing HER2-targeted therapy. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) and in situ hybridization (ISH) are the only two recognized imaging methods for HER2 detection22. In the HER2-positive breast cancer diagnosis, HER2+ patients with an IHC score of 2+ are required further diagnosis by ISH to confirm the result. Recently, a new category called HER2-low23, HER2 IHC 1+, or HER2 IHC 2+ with HER2 ISH negative, was established for novel HER2-targeted therapy24. Therefore, it is necessary to propose a dual imaging detection method for nucleic acid and protein that could enhance detection sensitivity and accuracy, potentially benefiting more patients25,26.

Loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) and tyramide signal amplification (TSA) have garnered significant attention in molecular diagnostics. Cierzniak et al. developed an improved LAMP for detecting genetic variations27. Fukudo et al. successfully applied in-situ LAMP technology to detect gene point mutations in paraffin sections28. Alhamid et al. applied LAMP to the detection of Mycoplasma bovine and improved the detection sensitivity and specificity29. These studies all highlight LAMP’s broad application potential in clinical detection. Regarding TSA, Choi et al. successfully developed a highly sensitive multiplex immunoassay using TSA and encoded hydrogel microparticles30. Kubelka-Sabit et al. established the ISH-TSA method and demonstrated its improved sensitivity for detecting positive results in real-sample31. Alzu’bi et al. reported the feasibility of TSA-based multiple immunolabeling techniques for detecting protein expression in paraffin-embedded human fetal frontal brain sections32. These studies indicated that TSA had great potential in IHC and signal enhancement. Integrating the advantages of LAMP and TSA, we aimed to propose a method for simultaneous and high-sensitivity detection of HER2 nucleic acid and protein in tumor sections within a shorter timeframe. This integration promises to advance current methodologies and provide a more efficient and accurate tool for molecular diagnostics.

Herein, we establish an ingenious and simultaneous dual detection system of nucleic acids and proteins in situ applied to the HER2 diagnosis in one pathological section or single cell, as shown in the Graphical Abstract. This in-situ dual-lighting system (ISDLS) incorporates two fluorescent signal amplification systems: A, specially designed molecular beacon to LAMP (MB-LAMP) for nucleic acids, and B, the TSA to IHC (TSA-IHC) for proteins. The ISDLS significantly improves the detection sensitivity and accuracy for low-abundance nucleic acids or proteins, which can facilitate more patients with HER2 disorders benefiting from personalized medicine and lay a foundation for developing a powerful strategy for the high-plex spatial co-localization detection of both nucleic acids and proteins.

Materials and methods

Design and selection of primers

We retrieved the ERBB2 sequence (NG_007503.1) from the NCBI database and identified the ERBB2-253 and ERBB2-356 conserved regions (Supplementary Sequences 1, 2) as potential targets for LAMP detection. Candidate LAMP Primers designed using the Explorer V5 software (Supplementary Primers 1–4) were screened out for the LAMP amplification system based on their efficiency33, precisely depending on the quantification cycle (Cq) (Refer to “Supplementary materials and methods 1.2” if needed).

Molecular beacon design and MB-LAMP optimization

The corresponding MBs (LFP and LBP) consisted of a loop structure and a six-base stem structure based on the loop primers of the preferred Primer 41 (LF and LB), respectively. A fluorescence chromophoric group (FAM) and a fluorescence quenching group (Dabcyl) were specially designed at the 5’ end and 3’ end of the MBs34, which resulted in the MB fluorescence being controlled by temperature. And the oligonucleotides (including primers and MBs) used in this study were all synthesized by Shanghai Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. Then, the established MB-LAMP system could achieve the purpose of in situ labeling of HER2 targets in human tumor sections. Furthermore, the hybridization temperature and time of the MB-LAMP system were optimized based on the endpoint fluorescence properties of FIh/FIa (FIh: Endpoint fluorescence of hybridization; FIa: Endpoint fluorescence of amplification) according to the working principle of MBs (For detailed optimization processes, refer to the “Supplementary materials and methods 1.4” section in the Supplementary Information).

In-situ MB-LAMP on cell line slides

The SKBR3 and MCF10A cell line (purchased from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shanghai, China, and certified by the vendor as mycoplasma-negative and STR-authenticated prior to delivery) slides were used to evaluate the feasibility of the MB-LAMP system. The sample was fully covered by a fresh MB-LAMP amplification system, and then the amplification was performed under the aforementioned optimal MB-LAMP conditions: 65 °C for 60 min, followed by hybridization at 45 °C for 60 min. Next, the cell line slides were allowed to reach room temperature (25 °C) and the nucleus stained by Hoechst33342 were optional as needed. After all these steps were done, the resulting slides were photographed by a fluorescence microscope.

Optimization of the TSA-IHC system

A 594-Tyr fluorescent dye synthesized in our laboratory was applied to the TSA-IHC system for HER2 protein overexpression detection35. The optimizations were performed on the TSA-IHC system with various critical factors, involving the removal of endogenous oxidase, the concentration of blocking agent, blocking time, HRP labeled anti-HER2 antibody (2 mg/mL, provided by Zhuhai Sanmed Biotech LTD, China) and 594-Tyr concentration and incubating time (For detailed optimization processes, refer to the “Supplementary materials and methods 1.7” section in the Supplementary Information). Then, the resulting slides were photographed by a fluorescence microscope, and the mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) was measured by Image J software as well as plotted output.

Optimization of ISDLS

The TSA-IHC and MB-LAMP implementation sequence was also the most critical factor for the fluorescence imaging effect of ISDLS. Two different experimental sequences were set up with prefabricated SKBR3 cell line slides with the aforementioned optimal conditions. Experimental Sequence 1) the MB-LAMP was performed firstly for HER2 nucleic acid amplification and lighting, and then followed by the TSA-IHC procedure to light HER2 protein in situ. Experimental Sequence 2) the TSA-IHC was performed firstly and followed by the MB-LAMP procedure. After the in-situ dual-lighting steps, the nucleus was stained to assist in localization. Finally, the resulting images were photographed by a fluorescence microscope.

ISDLS on cell line slides

The cell line slides of SKBR3 and MCF10A were further applied to validate the performance of the ISDLS. First of all, the ISDLS (MB-LAMP followed by TSA-IHC) was performed to light as well as amplify the signal of HER2 nucleic acid and protein in situ. (The optimized MB-LAMP procedure involved incubation at 65 °C for 60 min, followed by incubation at 45 °C for 60 min, after which the samples were transferred to room temperature for subsequent operations. For the preferred TSA-IHC process, following the MB-LAMP, the samples were treated with 3% H₂O₂ for 30 min and then blocked with pre-prepared 5% skim milk powder for 30 min; Subsequently, they were incubated with HRP-labeled anti-HER2 antibody (1:4 K) for 30 min and finally treated with 5 μM 594-Tyr for 30 min.) After the in-situ dual-lighting steps, the cell nucleus was also stained. Finally, the resulting images were photographed by a fluorescence microscope. In parallel, HER2-positive gastric cancer cell (NCI-N87, a kind gift from Bio-Thera Solutions, Ltd., Guangzhou, China and certified by the vendor as mycoplasma-negative and STR-authenticated prior to delivery) was tested in the same way for systematic robustness verification.

ISDLS in human tumor sections

All clinical tests were performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and were approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital (Guangzhou, China). The Ethics Approval Number was: KY2024-253-02. Written informed consent was obtained from each patient or their legal guardian prior to sample collection.

Based on the above results, paraffin-embedded human breast cancer tissue sections were pre-processed (Refer to the “Supplementary materials and methods 1.8” section in the Supplementary Information) to evaluate the efficacy of ISDLS. The process was summarized as follows: The target areas were uniformly covered with fresh MB-LAMP reaction solution, and incubated in a wet box at 65 °C for 60 min followed by 60 min at 45 °C. After cooling to room temperature, the sections were washed with PBS to remove the excess components. TSA-IHC was performed according to the optimized protocol (For the preferred TSA-IHC process, following the MB-LAMP, the samples were treated with 3% H₂O₂ for 30 min and then blocked with pre-prepared 5% skim milk powder for 30 min; Subsequently, they were incubated with HRP labeled anti-HER2 antibody (1:4 K) for 30 min and finally treated with 5 μM 594-Tyr for 30 min.), with counterstaining the cell nuclei. Finally, the resulting images were captured using a fluorescence microscope. In addition, 30 human tumor sections with varying HER2 expression levels of different tumor types (including breast, gastric, lung, ovarian, intestinal, uterus, and bladder cancers) were blind-tested, and the results were compared with clinical reports to further assess the reliability and universality of the ISDLS. (All the selected experimental sections were sourced from Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital. The subsequent ISDLS results were compared to the clinical reports.)

Statistics and reproducibility

In our study, we employed rigorous statistical methods and ensured reproducibility through careful experimental design and analysis. All statistical analyses were conducted using OriginPro 2021 or ImageJ-win64. Each critical experiment was performed in triplicate, with replicates defined as repeated measurements of the same sample using the same technique to ensure consistency of the results.

MB screening and MB-LAMP optimization

According to the results in Fig. 1D, if the system went directly to room temperature, MB directly restored the stem-loop structure but did not bind adequately to targets, which led to the general disappearance of the fluorescence and no sign of recovery within 30 min. Therefore, the genomic gene extracted from HER2-positive cells was used as the detection template to optimize the MB-LAMP system, including the hybridization temperature and time after the amplification. As shown in Fig. 1E and F, during the temperature range of 41–50 °C, the FIh/FIa ratios first gradually increased with the increase of hybridization temperature, and then gradually decreased. But, only Primer 41-LFP could achieve 100% fluorescence recovery between 44-48 °C (Fig. 1E), and Primer 41-LBP was unsatisfactory (Fig. 1F). In addition, it was obvious that the FIh/FIa based on Primer 41-LFP gradually approached 100% with the increase of binding time up to 60 min (Fig. 1G). Further, it could be clearly seen from Fig. 1H that the normal MB-LAMP group performed a satisfactory fluorescence signal recovery process during the hybridization period after amplification (the red curve), and the three control groups was not observed. On the whole, Primer 41-LFP was demonstrated clearly superior results throughout the optimization process, and was selected for the MB-LAMP system with incubating at 45 °C for 60 min after amplification.

Feasibility of MB-LAMP on cell line slides

The SKBR3 and MCF10A cell line slides were used to prove the feasibility of the MB-LAMP system. After the MB-LAMP process, the in-situ amplified green fluorescence signal was presented on HER2-positive SKBR3 cell line slides (Fig. 1I, K). However, a similar signal was absent on HER2-negative MCF10A cell line slides (Fig. 1J, L) and the MFI statistics of these images were also intuitively illustrated the differences in fluorescence intensity of HER2-positive and HER2-negative cell slides (Fig. 1M). In addition, according to Supplementary Fig. 2A and B, Primer 41-LFP free groups had no fluorescent signal, and the normal MB-LAMP groups had an amplified green fluorescence signal compared with the “Non-Amplification” groups, which reflected the satisfactory signal amplification effect of the MB-LAMP system. Furthermore, the same conclusion was supported by the MFI results (Supplementary Fig. 3) from these slices.

Optimization of TSA-IHC system

To better label HER2 protein based on the principle of TSA-IHC system (Fig. 2A), the specific conditions of the TSA-IHC system were optimized. According to Fig. 2B and Supplementary Fig. 4, pre-treated samples with 3% H2O2 for 5-30 min resulted in significant endogenous catalase scavenging, with 30 min being more sufficient. Blocking for 30 min (Fig. 2C and Supplementary Fig. 5B) with the pre-prepared 5% skim milk powder (Supplementary Fig. 5A) could effectively block non-specific sites. As for the HRP labeled anti-HER2 antibody, the optimal concentration and incubation time were both essential to improve assay reliability and reduce assay costs. Thus, the best signal display was the antibody diluted at 1:4 K (Fig. 2D and Supplementary Fig. 6A) and incubated for 30 min (Fig. 2E and Supplementary Fig. 6B). ISDLS optimization

The ISDLS operated MB-LAMP followed by TSA-IHC, showed excellent co-localization of the green amplification signal for HER2 nucleic acid and the red amplification signal for HER2 protein (Supplementary Fig. 8A). In particular, the auxiliary localization of nuclear staining further confirmed the consistency of the dual-signal in situ. However, as we could see from Supplementary Fig. 8B, the ISDLS-operated TSA-IHC followed by MB-LAMP, showed a visible unsatisfactory image results compared with the above results in Supplementary Fig. 8A and the MFI in Supplementary Fig. 9 indicated that green amplification signal for HER2 nucleic acid and the red amplification signal for HER2 protein were retained only about 70% (44.7/63.3) and 50% (34.4/67.3). In addition, the preferential MB-LAMP process was more conducive to exhibiting the true localization of HER2 nucleic acid amplification signal, so, suggesting the MB-LAMP followed by TSA-IHC for ISDLS was preferable.

In contrast, the HER2-negative MCF10A cell was almost unmarked (Fig. 3B). Of course, the extremely weak mark was acceptable. Although MCF10A is a non-cancerous cell line, the HER2 gene may also have a certain expression. Besides, in any biological detection technology, background signal like this is completely unavoidable. Furthermore, the gastric cancer-derived HER2-positive NCI-N87 cell also showed outstanding dual-labeling results as displayed in Fig. 3C. More profoundly, these results broaden the application scope of the ISDLS, making it not only suitable for detecting HER2 in breast cancer, but also in gastric cancer and potentially other HER2-related cancers. ISDLS in human tumor sections

The ultimate goal of ISDLS was to achieve simultaneous sensitive and accurate detection of disease-associated nucleic acid and protein targets in one pathological section. Therefore, the optimized ISDLS was perfectly articulated in breast cancer tissue sections, and the nucleus staining further assisted in locating the spatial location of the biomarkers in human tumor sections (Fig. 4A, B). From the imaging results of Tests 1-3, HER2 nucleic acid and protein could be visualized through FAM-labeled green fluorescent signals and 594-Tyr-labeled red fluorescent signals in these HER2-positive human tumor sections. Satisfactory dual-signals were seen in the dual-channel merged plot of MB-LAMP&TSA-IHC from HER2-positive sections in Tests 1-3. And the images in Test 4 showed a satisfactory negative control with negligible noise. Moreover, we could zero in on the double-labeled aim areas in the merged plot of MB-LAMP&TSA-IHC&Nucleus with ease and speed, which would be a significant advantage in observing an unknown area to determine whether it was aimed areas. In particular, the MB-LAMP&TSA-IHC&Nucleus image results in Test 3, showed a significant dual-positive area surrounded by the clear, unlabeled negative cells, effectively proved the high specificity of the ISDLS strategy. Not only that, but the MFI statistical analysis results in Fig. 4C conducted on the images of Test1-4 also further corroborated the aforementioned results in Fig. 4A, B. It was also worth noting that, compared with the current clinical pathological testing procedures36–38, ISDLS could output the detection results of HER2 nucleic acid and protein from only one section within a single day, in contrast, the current clinical pathological testing procedures required a few days and different sections. Discussions

In this paper, we screened Primer 41-LFP out targeting the conserved sequence of HER2-356, and established the MB-LAMP in situ amplification system based on it. A 594-Tyr fluorescent dye synthesized in our laboratory was applied to the TSA-IHC system for HER2 protein overexpression detection with good performance. The ISDLS was ingeniously constructed to accomplish the simultaneous detection of the nucleic acid and protein in one sample on demand, demonstrating the great potential to be applied to pathological diagnosis for human tumor sections.

Several studies also focused on simultaneous nucleic acid and protein detection in single pathology samples. For instance, PGDS integrated chromogenic in situ hybridization (CISH) and IHC for HER2 status assessment in breast cancer39; a gene-protein assay used FISH and CISH for bright-field tricolor visualization of HER2-related elements in breast cancer sections40; and a novel method combined FISH and immunofluorescence (IF) for double detection in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded sections41. However, unlike these methods, ISDLS combined MB-LAMP and TSA-IHC to enable simultaneous amplification of nucleic acid and protein signals in a single sample. To date, there has been little to no literature explicitly reporting such dual signal amplification in one pathology sample. This unique capability gives ISDLS higher sensitivity and accuracy, providing a more precise and comprehensive assessment for pathological diagnosis.

HER2 overexpression is a key indicator for tumor molecular subtyping and serves as one of the criteria for guiding targeted therapy. While current clinical methods could only detect HER2 protein or nucleic acid separately, our ISDLS could simultaneously display the spatial distribution of both HER2 nucleic acid and protein biomarkers in a single pathological section. Furthermore, the ISDLS could be applied to both cell lines and tissue sections with promising results. Importantly, the application of ISDLS was not limited to breast cancer, but extended to other tumors with corresponding targets. The distinct red amplification signal in the TSA-IHC system could serve as an important companion diagnostic biomarker for HER2 IHC ≥ 1+ cancer patients. Meanwhile, the simultaneous detection of both protein and nucleic acid could improve the detection sensitivity for individuals with HER2 IHC < 1+ but high levels of nucleic acid. Of course, it had to be admitted that we did not take into account the evaluation of tumor heterogeneity42 at this stage, and the results provided by the ISDLS proposed in this research were merely semi-quantitative, but it could fully conform to the existing positive clinical detection results. Moreover, the proposed method improved the detection rate of HER2-low significantly. As a proposed clinicopathological approach for HER2 in situ dual diagnosis, it had distinct advantages in terms of sensitivity and accuracy, especially for HER2-low. Currently, the performance of ISDLS is limited to testing on cell lines and 30 histological sections. Although its genuine clinical application still required further validation, it was expected to become a powerful tool for pathological detection in the near future.

Furthermore, multi-omics strategies have been developed to reveal the relationship between the genotypes and phenotypes in single cell, particularly focusing on the spatiotemporal interactions between genomics, transcriptomic, and proteomics43–45. To adequately and accurately characterize cells and tissues, nucleic acids and proteins in the region of interest need to be detected in situ simultaneously in the same tissue sample, which requires to visualize the spatial organization within tissues or cells by simultaneous fluorescence detection of RNA, DNA, and proteins. Therefore, the co-localization feature of the ISDLS enabled multi-dimensional analysis simultaneously at both nucleic acid and protein levels, providing a powerful tool. Additionally, this approach not only promoted the development of next-generation diagnostic pathology but also held significant scientific and clinical value.

Global regularity results for the fractional heat equation and application to a class of non-linear KPZ problem
Boumediene Abdellaoui, Somia Atmani, Kheiredine Biroud, El-Haj Laamri

Abstract
. In the first part of this paper, we prove the global regularity, in an adequate parabolic Bessel-Potential space and then in the corresponding parabolic fractional Sobolev space, of the unique solution to following fractional heat equation w t + ( − Δ ) s ​ w = h ; w ⁡ ( x , t ) = 0 ​ in ​ ( ℝ N ∖ Ω ) × ( 0 , T ) ; w ⁡ ( x , 0 ) = w 0 ​ ( x ) ​ in ​ Ω w_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}w=h\;;\;w(x,t)=0\text{ in }\;(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T)\;;\;w(x,0)=w_{0}(x)\;\text{in}\;\Omega , where Ω \Omega is an open bounded subset of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . The proof is based on a new pointwise estimate on the fractional gradient of the corresponding kernel. Moreover, we establish the compactness of ( w 0 , h ) ↦ w (w_{0},h)\mapsto w . As a majeur application, in the second part , we establish existence and regularity of solutions to a class of Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equations with fractional diffusion and a nonlocal gradient term. Additionally, several auxiliary results of independent interest are obtained.



1.
Introduction

This work is structured in two main parts.

— The
first part
is devoted to a fine study of the regularity of solutions to the following fractional heat equation

(FHE)\qquad\left\{\ \begin{array}[]{lllll}w_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}w&=&h(x,t)&\text{in}&\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T),\\
w(x,t)&=&0&\text{in}&(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
w(x,0)&=&w_{0}(x)&\text{in}&\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
h
and
w_{0}
are measurable functions satisfying some integrability assumptions. We assume here and in the
rest of this paper
that
0<s<1
and
\Omega
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with regular boundary. The operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
denotes the fractional Laplacian introduced par M. Riesz in
[
53
]
and defined for any
\phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by:

(1.1)

(-\Delta)^{s}\phi(x):=a_{N,s}\mbox{ P.V. }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy},\;s\in(0,1),

where P.V. stands for the Cauchy principal value and

(1.2)

a_{N,s}:=\frac{s2^{2s}\Gamma\left(\frac{N+2s}{2}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)}

is a normalization constant chosen
so that
the following pair of identities :

\lim\limits_{s\to 0^{+}}(-\Delta)^{s}\phi=\phi\quad\text{and }\quad\lim\limits_{s\to 1^{-}}(-\Delta)^{s}\phi=-\Delta\phi

holds.
For further details, see,
e.g.
,
[
26
, Proposition 4.4]

or
[
25
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Prior to stating our results, we briefly review
some previous works that are closely related to the present study.

Existence and uniqueness of a weak solution to
(FHE)
, in the
sense of Definition
2.10
, were established
in
[
47
]
for data in
L^{1}
. Moreover, by employing
approximation techniques and suitable test functions, the authors
obtained partial regularity of the solution in certain fractional
parabolic Sobolev spaces
L^{\sigma}(0,T;W^{s,\sigma}_{0}(\Omega))

(defined in Subsection
2.1
), with
\sigma\leq 2
. It is worth
noting, however, that such approximation arguments are not
well-suited for proving higher-order fractional regularity in more
general parabolic fractional spaces. Therefore, a novel approach
is needed to address this limitation.

In
[
14
]
, the authors considered the transformation
u=w\phi
, where
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega\times(0,T))
, and derived local (interior) regularity results in a fractional framework as well as in Besov spaces, depending on the smoothness of the source term
h
. The key idea in
[
14
]
was to analyze the equation satisfied by
u
rather than
w
itself. However, this approach has a purely local nature and does not capture the influence of the initial condition on the global regularity of the solution
w
.

Further refined regularity results were established in

[
33
]
,
[
34
]
, and
[
9
]
for an extended class
of pseudodifferential operators. The approach adopted in these works
combines the calculus of pseudodifferential operators with
interpolation techniques in Triebel-Lizorkin spaces. Under
suitable hypotheses on the data
(h,w_{0})
– in particular, by imposing
w_{0}=0
or prescribing additional regularity
for
u_{0}
–, the authors derive regularity
estimates for solutions of the fractional heat equation.

It is worth noting that in the case
h=0
, the authors of

[
31
]
investigated boundary regularity for solutions in
bounded
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
domains. They showed that if the
initial datum belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega)
, the solution
is Hölder continuous for
t>0
, and satisfies
\dfrac{u(t,\cdot)}{\delta^{s}}\in\mathcal{C}^{s-\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega})
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,s)
,
where
\delta(x)
denotes the distance from
x
to
\partial\Omega
.

Regarding the elliptic case, the first complete global
(up to the boundary) regularity result was recently established in

[
4
]
. The authors used a representation formula along with
precise estimates on the Green’s function to derive optimal
regularity in Bessel-Potential spaces
\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
(see Subsection
2.1
for
the definition). For a comprehensive treatment of regularity
theory in the elliptic setting, we refer the reader to

[
4
]
.

The
first goal
of the present work is to extend these results to the parabolic setting by establishing
global regularity
for the solution
w
. Depending on the regularity of the data
(h,w_{0})
, we show that the solution belongs to appropriate parabolic Bessel-Potential spaces
L^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, as well as to the corresponding parabolic fractional Sobolev spaces
L^{p}(0,T;W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, defined in Subsection
2.1
.

Our methodology extends the recent framework introduced in
[
4
]
. Let
P_{\Omega}
denote the heat kernel associated with the homogeneous exterior Dirichlet problem for the fractional heat operator
\partial_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}
. A central component of our analysis is the derivation of pointwise estimates for the fractional gradient of
P_{\Omega}
. These kernel estimates are then used to establish pointwise bounds for the fractional gradient of the solution to the Dirichlet problem
(\mathrm{FHE})
, even when the forcing term and initial datum are merely integrable with respect to the distance to the boundary.

To the best of our knowledge, these gradient estimates are entirely novel. They lead to refined regularity results formulated within the appropriate functional framework.

Compared to the elliptic case, addressing time
regularity introduces additional challenges, particularly near
t=0
and when
x
approaches the boundary of
\Omega
. As we
will see, another critical difficulty arises as
t\to 0
,
especially when the fractional parameter
s
is small. In
particular, obtaining uniform integral estimates over
\Omega_{T}

requires imposing an additional constraint on
s
.

\bullet
We begin this first part with the following main result :

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\rho\in[s,\max\{1,2s\})
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega

with
x\neq y
and
t>0
,
we have:

1.

If
s\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then

\begin{array}[]{lll}&&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)|\leq\dfrac{C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s+\rho-1}}\\
&\times&\bigg(\frac{\delta^{s-\rho}(x)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{1-s-\rho}}+t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}\log\frac{D}{|x-y|}+t^{\frac{s+\rho-1}{2s}}\delta^{s-\rho}(x)+t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}|\log(\delta(x))|+|x-y|^{2s-1}\bigg).\end{array}

2.

If
s>\frac{1}{2}
, then

\begin{array}[]{lll}&&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)|\leq\dfrac{C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+\rho}}\\
&\times&\bigg(\frac{\delta^{s-\rho}(x)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{s-\rho}}+\frac{t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}}{|x-y|^{2s-1}}+t^{\frac{\rho-s}{2s}}\delta^{s-\rho}(x)+\log\frac{D}{|x-y|}+|\log(\delta(x))|\bigg),\end{array}

where
D>>\text{diam}(\Omega):=\max\{|x-y|\mbox{ ;
}x,y\in\overline{\Omega}\}
and
\delta(x):=\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
.

Remark 1.2
.

It should be noted that the constant
C
in the previous theorem is independent of
x,y
and
t
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is presented
in Section
3
.

As a byproduct, integrating the preceding estimate in time yields the following result, recently established in
[
4
]
, concerning the fractional regularity of the Green function, at least when
s>\frac{1}{4}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assume that
s>\frac{1}{4}
. Then, for any
\rho\in[s,\max\{1,2s\})
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}|(-\Delta)_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{s}(x,y)|&\leq\dfrac{C}{|x-y|)^{N-(2s-\rho)}}\left(\dfrac{\delta^{s-\rho}(x)}{|x-y|^{s-\rho}}+\log(\frac{D}{|x-y|})+|\log(\delta(x))|\right).\end{array}

\bullet
As a significative application of the preceding pointwise
estimate on
|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}P_{\Omega}|
, and using the
representation formula of the solution
w
to Problem
(FHE)
, we obtain a complete and
global regularity

result for
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(.,t)
for any
\rho\in[s,\max\{1,2s\})
.
A key tool in achieving our objective is an
L^{p}
-estimate for a class of hyper-singular
integrals, whose full proof is provided in the Appendix.

To keep the analysis as structured as possible, we treat separately the following two cases:

➢

case :
w_{0}\neq 0
and
h=0
;

➢

case :
w_{0}=0
and
h\neq 0
.

➢ First case:
w_{0}\neq 0
and
h=0
.
Our first result is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\rho\in[s,\max\{1,2s\})
. Let
w
be the unique weak
solution to Problem
(FHE)
with
w_{0}\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
where

\sigma\geq 1
and define
\widehat{\sigma}\leq\min\{\sigma,\frac{1}{\rho-s}\}
. Then

1.

If
2s+\rho\geq 1
, for all
\eta>0
small enough and for all

p>\widehat{\sigma}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}&&||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\widehat{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\times&\bigg(t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(1+\eta)(\rho-s)}+t^{-\frac{\rho-s}{2s}}+t^{-\frac{\rho+\eta-s}{2s}}+t^{-\frac{N\eta}{2sp(1+\eta)}-\frac{\rho-s}{2s}}\bigg)||w_{0}||_{L^{\widehat{\sigma}}(\Omega)}.\end{array}

2.

If
2s+\rho<1
, then setting
\sigma_{0}<\min\{\widehat{\sigma},\frac{N}{1-2s-\rho}\}
, for all

p>\frac{\sigma_{0}N}{N-\sigma_{0}(1-2s-\rho)}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}&&||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\times&\bigg(t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(1+\eta)(\rho-s)}+t^{-\frac{\rho+\eta-s}{2s}}+t^{-\frac{N\eta}{2sp(1+\eta)}-\frac{\rho-s}{2s}}+t^{-\frac{\rho-s}{2s}}\bigg)||w_{0}||_{L^{\widehat{\sigma}}(\Omega)}.\end{array}

Further regularity results in Bessel potential spaces are obtained for fixed
t>0
.

➢ Case:
w_{0}=0
and
h\neq 0
.

In order to establish the
regularity of the solution in
\Omega_{T}
, we need additional
assumptions on
s
and
\rho
. This is a consequence of the
presence of the singular term
t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}
, which naturally appears
in the estimates of heat kernel for small times. More
precisely, we have:

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
s>\frac{1}{4}
and fixed
\rho\in[s,\max\{1,2s\})

such that
\rho-s<\min\{\frac{s}{(N+2s)},\frac{4s-1}{(N+2s-1)}\}
.

Let
h\in L^{m}(\Omega_{T})
where
m\geq 1
and
w

be the
unique solution to Problem (FHE). Then :

1.

Case where
2s+\rho\geq 1
.

– If
1\leq m\leq\frac{N+2s}{2s-\rho}
, then
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w\in{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}
for all
m<r<\overline{\overline{m}}_{s,\rho}:=\frac{m(N+2s)}{(N+2s)(m(\rho-s)+1)-ms}
, moreover, we have

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w||_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}\leq CT^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{r})}(1+T^{-\frac{\rho+\eta-s}{2s}}+T^{-\frac{\rho-s}{2s}})||h||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}.

– If
m>\frac{N+2s}{2s-\rho}
, then

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w\in{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}
for all

r<\frac{1}{\rho-s}
and

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w||_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}\leq CT^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{N+2s}{2sm}}(1+T^{-\frac{\rho+\eta-s}{2s}}+T^{-\frac{\rho-s}{2s}})||h||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}.

2.

Case
2s+\rho<1
.

– If
m\leq\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}
, then
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w\in{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}
for all

m<r<\frac{m(N+2s)}{(N+2s)(m(\rho-s)+1)-m(3s+\rho-1)}
and

\begin{array}[]{lll}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w||_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}&\leq&C(T^{\frac{1-2s-\rho}{2s}}+1)T^{-\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{r})}\\
&\times&\bigg(T^{\frac{1}{2}}+T^{\frac{4s-1-\eta}{2s}}+T^{\frac{3s+\rho-1}{2s}}+T^{\frac{4s-1}{2s}}+T^{\frac{2s-\rho}{2s}}\bigg)||h||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}.\end{array}

– If
m>\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}
, then for
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w\in{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}
for all

r<\frac{1}{\rho-s}
, and for
\eta>0
small enough, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w||_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}&\leq&C(T^{\frac{1-2s-\rho}{2s}}+1)T^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{N+2s}{2sm}}\\
&\times&\bigg(T^{\frac{1}{2}}+T^{\frac{4s-1-\eta}{2s}}+T^{\frac{3s+\rho-1}{2s}}+T^{\frac{4s-1}{2s}}+T^{\frac{2s-\rho}{2s}}\bigg)||h||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}.\end{array}

In any case, we have

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w||_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega,T)||h||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}.

We refer to Section

4
for a complete proof and
other related results.
\square

— In the
second part
, we apply the results obtained in the first part to analyze
a class of Kardar-Parisi-Zhang equations with fractional gradients. More specifically, we consider the following problem :

(KPZ_{f})\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&\big|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\big|^{q}+f&\text{in }\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(x,t)&=&0&\text{in }(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)&=&u_{0}(x)&\text{in }\Omega.\end{array}\right.

where
q\geq 1
,
f
and
u_{0}
are measurable functions satisfying
some integrability assumptions.

It is worth recalling that the fractional gradient, or the so-called “
the
half-s
Laplacian
”, is given by the expression:

(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{\rvert x-y\rvert^{N+s}}dy.

The Kardar–Parisi–Zhang
(KPZ_{f})
problem with fractional
diffusion is a prominent and actively studied model at the
intersection of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs),
statistical physics, and nonlocal analysis. It generalizes the
classical
(KPZ_{f})
equation by replacing the standard Laplacian
(which models ordinary diffusion) with a fractional Laplacian,
thereby allowing the description of anomalous transport and
long-range interactions in interface growth phenomena. This type
of equations arises in various fields such as turbulent flows,
porous media dynamics, mathematical finance, and biological
transport processes. For an in-depth discussion and further
references, we refer the reader to the seminal
works
[
40
,
39
,
41
,
36
]
and the interesting monograph
[
59
]
.

As far as we know, Problem
(KPZ_{f})
, with
fractional gradient, has not yet been investigated in the
literature, and addressing it represents the
second goal
of
the present work. Under suitable integrability assumptions on
f
and
u_{0}
, we establish the existence of a solution to this
Problem, in the sense of Definition

6.1
.

It is worth recalling that the case where the nonlocal term
|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{q}
is replaced by a local gradient term has been studied in
[
20
]
and
[
7
]
, where the existence of solution was obtained under the condition
q<\dfrac{s}{1-s}
, provided the data satisfy appropriate assumptions. We also note that the elliptic counterpart of this problem was recently addressed in
[
5
]
, and further analyzed in
[
60
]
under additional smallness conditions on the data.

Our aim is to extend this result to the largest
possible class of data
(f,u_{0})
and exponents
q
.

The existence results are proved by combining the regularity estimates obtained in the first part of this paper with a
suitable application of the Schauder fixed-point Theorem.

In order to do this, an important tool will be a compactness
result, in a suitable fractional space, for the operator
\Phi

defined on
L^{1}(\Omega_{T})\times L^{1}(\Omega)
by
\Phi(h,w_{0})=w
, where
w
denotes the unique solution to
(FHE)
.
Taking into account the non-local character of the fractional gradient, proving the compactness of the operator
\Phi

(in a parabolic Bessel potential space), especially for
s\leq\frac{1}{2}
, is a delicate matter. It requires a fine analysis that combines
various a priori estimates with a new representation of elements in the Bessel potential space, as stated in Theorem
2.2
.

This compactness result appears to be new and, to the best of our
knowledge, may prove useful in other related problems.

A detailed proof is given in Section

5
.

Finally, related to Problem
(KPZ_{f})
, for the
sake of clarity, we shall present our main results by
distinguishing between the following two prototypical cases:

➢

f\neq 0
and
u_{0}=0
;

➢

f=0
and
u_{0}\neq 0
.

➣ First case:
f\neq 0
and
u_{0}=0
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
f\in L^{m}(\Omega_{T})
with
1\leq m
and
s\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
. Assume that one of the following conditions hold

(i)

s\in(\frac{1}{3},1)
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1\leq m\leq\frac{N+2s}{s}\vskip 5.69046pt\mbox{ and }1<q\leq\frac{N+2s}{N+2s-ms},\\
\mbox{ or }\\
m>\frac{N+2s}{s}\vskip 11.38092pt\mbox{ and }q\in(1,+\infty).\end{array}\right.

(ii)

s\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{3}]
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1\leq m\leq\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}\vskip 11.38092pt\mbox{ and }1<q\leq\frac{N+2s}{N+2s-m(4s-1)},\\
\mbox{ or }\\
m>\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}\vskip 17.07182pt\mbox{ and }q\in(1,+\infty).\end{array}\right.

Then, there exists
0<T^{*}<T
such that Problem
(KPZ_{f})

has a solution
u\in L^{\gamma}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{L}^{s,\gamma}_{0}(\Omega))
with

1.

1<\gamma<\overline{m}:=\frac{m(N+2s)}{N+2s-ms}
if
1\leq m<\frac{N+2s}{s}
, and
1<\gamma<+\infty
if
m\geq\frac{N+2s}{s}
, if
s\in(\frac{1}{3},1)
,

or

2.

1<\gamma<\overline{m}:=\frac{m(N+2s)}{N+2s-m(4s-1)}
if
1\leq m<\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}
, and
1<\gamma<+\infty
if
m\geq\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}
, if
s\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{3}]
.

Remark 1.7
.

As for the unexpected restriction
s>\frac{1}{4}
, it is not clear whether it is only technical,

➣ Second case:
u_{0}\neq 0
and

.

.

2
, we gather several preliminary tools and inequalities that will be used throughout the paper. This section is divided into three subsections. In Subsection

.
Section

pointwise estimate on the “
\rho
-Laplacian” of
P_{\Omega}
. The
proof relies on integral functional estimates and fine properties
of the heat kernel.
Once these estimates have been established, we derive the
main regularity results for the (unique) solution to the fractional heat
equation
(FHE)
, using hyper-singular integrals. For clarity of
the presentation, we consider two cases separately: (i)
h=0

and
w_{0}\neq 0
and (ii)
h\neq 0
and
w_{0}=0
.
It is worth noting that the resulting regularity is global in
nature. These results are presented in
Section
4
.
As a significative application of the regularity theory developed
in Section
4
, we establish
the compactness of the operator
\Phi:L^{1}(\Omega_{T})\times L^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{s,q}_{0}(\Omega)),
for
q<\widehat{\kappa}_{s,\rho}
, where
\widehat{\kappa}_{s,\rho}>1

is defined in (
4.50
). The proof is given in
Section
5

2.1.
Some useful functional spaces and their properties.

Let

,

and

be an open subset of

.

\bullet

Fractional Sobolev
Spaces

— The fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(U)
is defined
as follows

W^{s,p}(U):=\left\{\phi\in L^{p}(U)\,\,;\;\;\iint\limits_{U\times U}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy<\infty\right\}.

and it is a
Banach space endowed with the norm

\|\phi\|_{W^{s,p}(U)}:=\left(\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(U)}^{p}+\iint\limits_{U\times U}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},.

We refer to
[
26
,
25
]
and their references for more
properties of the fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(U)
.

— When
U\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the space
\mathbb{W}_{0}^{s,p}(U)
is defined as

\mathbb{W}_{0}^{s,p}(U):=\bigg\{\phi\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\,\,;\,\,\phi=0\textup{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus U\bigg\}.

If in addition
U
is bounded, then
\mathbb{W}_{0}^{s,p}(U)
is a Banach
space endowed with the norm

\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{W}^{s,p}_{0}(U)}:=\left(\iint\limits_{D_{U}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\right)^{1/p},

with
D_{U}:=(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus(\mathcal{C}U\times\mathcal{C}U).

— If
p=2
, then

–

W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a Hilbert space. Moreover, for
\phi,\psi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

<(-\Delta)^{s}\phi,\psi>=\dfrac{a_{N,s}}{2}\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\dfrac{\left(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\right)\left(\psi(x)-\psi(y)\right)}{||x-y||^{N+2s}}dxdy,

where the constant
a_{N,s}
was introduced via the formula (
1.2
).

–

\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(U):=\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s,2}(U)
. Furthermore, we denote by
\mathbb{H}^{-s}(U)
the dual space of
\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(U)
.

— In the same way, we define the parabolic fractional space

L^{p}(0,T;\,\mathbb{W}^{s,p}_{0}(U))
as the set of functions
\phi\in L^{p}(U\times(0,T)
such that

||\phi||_{L^{p}(0,T;\,\mathbb{W}_{0}^{s,p}(U))}<\infty,

with

||\phi||_{L^{p}(0,T;\,\mathbb{W}_{0}^{s,p}(U))}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\iint\limits_{D_{U}}\frac{|\phi(x,t)-\phi(y,t)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdydt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

\mathcal{M}^{p}(U)

We denote by
\mathcal{M}^{p}(U)
, the

\phi

defined on

such that

(2.1)

\bigg|\bigg\{x\in U:|\phi(x)|>k\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\frac{C}{k^{p}}.

If we set

then

||.||_{\mathcal{M}^{p}(U)}
is a semi-norm.
\square

\bullet

Bessel Potential Spaces
\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

Before proceeding with the definition of Bessel potential spaces, we recall two important nonlocal analogues of the classical gradient that will be relevant in our analysis. For any test function
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define:

(2.2)

\nabla^{s}\phi(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\cdot\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\cdot\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N}},\quad s\in(0,1),

and the scalar-valued quantity

(2.3)

\mathbb{D}_{s}(\phi)(x):=\left(\frac{a_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

These objects provide different ways of capturing
nonlocal variations of smooth functions. For further details, we
refer the reader to
[
56
,
52
]
.

It is known (see e.g.
[
55
,
57
]
) that
\mathbb{D}_{s}
behaves asymptotically like the classical gradient
as
s\to 1^{-}
, in the sense that

(2.4)

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\,\mathbb{D}_{s}^{2}(\phi)(x)=|\nabla\phi(x)|^{2},\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Given the nonlocal character of the fractional Laplacian, it is natural to investigate the regularity of
w
(the weak solution to Problem
(FHE)
, in a functional space that reflects this structural feature.

— The Bessel potential space

\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as the completion of

\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm

\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
defined by

||\phi||_{\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\quad\textup{
and }\quad(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi=\mathcal{F}^{-1}((1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathcal{F}\phi).

According to
[
10
,
56
,
57
,
58
]
, the space

\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
can be endowed with the
equivalent norm

\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

or

\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\nabla^{s}\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

— If
p>\frac{2N}{N+2s}
,

\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
can also be defined as the set of
functions
\phi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\mathbb{D}_{s}(\phi)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, endowed with the equivalent norm

||\phi||_{\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\mathbb{D}_{s}(\phi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

We refer to
[
58
]
for more details.

We can summarize the essential
properties of
\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
in the following
proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that

and
0<s<1
. Then, we have

1.

W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if

and

\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if

.

2.

For all
0<\varepsilon<s<1
and
1<p<+\infty
,

\mathbb{L}^{s+\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset\mathbb{L}^{s-\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

The first assertion follows from
[
58
, Theorem 5 in Chapter V]
, while the second is a consequence of
[
10
, Theorem 7.63]
.

According to
[

]
(see

next beautiful partial characterization of the Bessel potential

Assume that

, then

(2.5)

\bigg\|\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p}+s}}\bigg\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C(p,N)\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=||\phi||_{\mathbb{L}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.\square

2.3.

for all
\theta<\infty
and

\bigg\||\nabla w|\delta^{1-s}\bigg\|_{L^{\theta}(\Omega_{T})}\leq CT^{\gamma_{2}}||h||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})},

where
\gamma_{2}=\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{m})+\frac{2s-1}{2s}
.

Corollary 2.14

(
[
8
, Corollary 3.13]
)
.

By combining Theorems
2.12
and
2.13
and using the fact that
|\nabla\delta|=1
a.e. in
\Omega
, we deduce that
if
w
is the unique
weak solution to Problem
(FHE)
with
w_{0}=0
, then
w\delta^{1-s}\in L^{\theta}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,\theta}_{0}(\Omega))
for all
\theta
such that

\frac{1}{\theta}>\frac{1}{m}-\frac{2s-1}{N+2s}
and

||w\delta^{1-s}||_{L^{\theta}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,\theta}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq C(T^{\gamma_{1}}+T^{\gamma_{2}})||h||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})},

where
\gamma_{1}=\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{m})+\frac{1}{2}
and
\gamma_{2}=\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{m})+\frac{2s-1}{2s}
. Moreover, if
m<\frac{N+2s}{2s-1}
, then the above estimate holds for all
\theta<\frac{m(N+2s)}{N+2s-m(2s-1)}.

In the case where
h=0
and
w_{0}\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
with
\sigma\geq 1
, we
have the next regularity result.

Theorem 2.15

(
[
8
, Proposition 3.20]
)
.

Suppose that
h\equiv 0
and
w_{0}\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
. Let
w
be the unique weak solution to Problem
(FHE)
, then
there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for all

r,\rho\geq\sigma
and for all
t>0
, we have

(2.16)

||w(\cdot,t)||_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{r})}||w_{0}||_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)},

and

(2.17)

\Big\|\dfrac{w(\cdot,t)}{\delta^{s}}\Big\|_{L^{\rho}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{\rho})-\frac{1}{2}}||w_{0}||_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)}.

Moreover,
w\in L^{r}(\Omega_{T})
for all
r<\frac{\sigma(N+2s)}{N}
and
\dfrac{w}{\delta^{s}}\in L^{\rho}(\Omega_{T})
for all

\rho<\frac{\sigma(N+2s)}{N+\sigma s}
.

3.
Estimate on the fractional gradient of the heat kernel.

In this section, we prove the main pointwise estimate on the half-fractional gradient of
P_{\Omega}
, the heat kernel with Dirichlet
boundary condition.
This estimate will be the key tool in establishing the main regularity result for
w
, the unique weak solution of
(FHE)
.

We recall that,
w
to Problem
(FHE)
is given by

(3.1)

w(x,t)=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w_{0}(y)P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)dy+\iint_{\Omega_{t}}h(y,\tau)P_{\Omega}(x,y,t-\tau)dyd\tau,

where
\Omega_{t}=\Omega\times(0,t)
.

In the next lemma, we recall the most useful properties of the heat kernel of the fractional
Laplacian. We refer to
[
8
,
16
,
21
,
43
]
for the proofs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
s\in(0,1)
, then for all
x,y\in\Omega
and for all

0<t<T
, we have

(3.2)

P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)\backsimeq\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta^{s}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\times\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\times\left(t^{\frac{-N}{2s}}\wedge\dfrac{t}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\right)

and

(3.3)

|\nabla_{x}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)|\leq C(N,s,\Omega)\left(\dfrac{1}{\delta(x)\wedge t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\right)P_{\Omega}(x,y,t).

Thus

(3.4)

|\nabla_{x}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Big(1\wedge\dfrac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{t}}{(\delta(x))^{1-s}}\cdot\dfrac{C}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{(N+2s)}}\qquad\mbox{ if }\quad\delta(x)<t^{\frac{1}{2s}},\\
\\
C\Big(1\wedge\dfrac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\cdot\dfrac{t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s}}\qquad\mbox{ if }\quad\delta(x)\geq t^{\frac{1}{2s}}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, if
s>\frac{1}{2}
, then
|\nabla_{x}P_{\Omega}|\in L^{q}(\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T))
for all
q<\dfrac{N+2s}{N+1}
and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}|\nabla_{x}P_{\Omega}|^{q}\,dx\,dy\,dt\leq C(\Omega)(T^{\frac{N+2s-q(N+s)}{2s}}+T^{\frac{N+2s-q(N+1)}{2s}}).

Notice that

t^{\frac{-N}{2s}}\wedge\dfrac{t}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\simeq\dfrac{t}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s}}.

Hence

(3.5)

P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)\backsimeq C\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta^{s}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\times\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\dfrac{t}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s}}\backsimeq C\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta^{s}(x)\delta^{s}(y)}{t}\right)\dfrac{t}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s}}.

In the same way, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)dt=\mathcal{G}_{s}(x,y),

where
\mathcal{G}_{s}(x,y)
is the Green function of the fractional
Laplacian with Dirichlet condition. It is known that

(3.6)

\mathcal{G}_{s}(x,y)\simeq\dfrac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2s}}\bigg(\frac{\delta^{s}(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}\wedge 1\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\wedge 1\bigg).

We refer to
[
22
]
for more details.

Now, we are in position to give the key estimate on the nonlocal gradient of the heat
kernel.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any
\rho\in[s,\min\{1,2s\})
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for a.e.
x,y\in\Omega
with
x\neq y
and for all
t>0
,
we have

(1)

If
s\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

(3.7)

\begin{array}[]{lll}&&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)|\leq\dfrac{C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s+\rho-1}}\\
&\times&\bigg(\frac{\delta^{s-\rho}(x)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{1-s-\rho}}+t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}\log\frac{D}{|x-y|}+t^{\frac{s+\rho-1}{2s}}\delta^{s-\rho}(x)+t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}|\log(\delta(x))|+|x-y|^{2s-1}\bigg).\end{array}

(2)

If
s>\frac{1}{2}
, we have

(3.8)

\begin{array}[]{lll}&&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)|\leq\dfrac{C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+\rho}}\\
&\times&\bigg(\frac{\delta^{s-\rho}(x)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{s-\rho}}+\frac{t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}}{|x-y|^{2s-1}}+t^{\frac{\rho-s}{2s}}\delta^{s-\rho}(x)+\log\frac{D}{|x-y|}+|\log(\delta(x))|\bigg),\end{array}

where
D>>\text{diam}(\Omega):=\max\{|x-y|\mbox{ ;
}x,y\in\overline{\Omega}\}
.

Remark 3.3
.

As mentioned in the introduction, the constant
C
in the previous theorem is independent of
x,y
and
t
.

Proof.

The proof will be a consequence of a more general estimate on

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)|
. Fixed
\rho>0

such that
s\leq\rho<\min\{1,2s\}
. Let
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
with
x\neq y
and
t>0
be fixed. Then, we know that

(3.9)

\begin{array}[]{lll}{(-\Delta)_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2}}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)}&=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)-P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}dz\\
&=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)-P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}dz\vskip 5.69046pt+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)-P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}dz\\
&=I_{1}(x,y,t)+I_{2}(x,y,t).\end{array}

We start by estimating
I_{1}
, since
P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)\equiv 0
when
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
, then

(3.10)

\begin{array}[]{lll}I_{1}(x,y,t)&=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)-P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}dz=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}dz\\
&\leq C(\Omega)\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)}{\delta^{\rho}(x)}.\\
\end{array}

We deal now with
I_{2}
which is more involved. Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
N+\sigma>\max\{2s,1\}
, then

\begin{array}[]{llll}\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}{I}_{2}(x,y,t)&=&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)-\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}dz,\\
&=&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)\dfrac{\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|z-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}-\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}dz,\\
&+&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)-\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|z-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}dz,\\
&=&{I}_{21}(x,y,t)+{I}_{22}(x,y,t).\end{array}

We begin with
{I}_{21}

\begin{array}[]{lll}|{I}_{21}(x,y,t)|&=\bigg|\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)\dfrac{\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|z-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}-\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}dz\bigg|\\
&\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\bigg|\langle z-x,\nabla\left((t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\tau x+(1-\tau)z-y|)^{N+\sigma}\right)\rangle\bigg|d\tau dz\\
&\leq C\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\tau x+(1-\tau)z-y|\right)^{N+\sigma-1}d\tau dz.\end{array}

Notice that, for
0<\tau<1
and for all
x,y,z\in\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\tau x+(1-\tau)z-y|\right)^{N+\sigma-1}\leq

\displaystyle\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+\tau|x-y|+(1-\tau)|z-y|\right)^{N+\sigma-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|z-y|\right)^{N+\sigma-1}+C|x-y|^{N+\sigma-1}.

Hence

\begin{array}[]{lll}|{I}_{21}(x,y,t)|&\leq C\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|z-y|\right)^{N+\sigma-1}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}dz+C\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)\,|x-y|^{N+\sigma-1}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}dz\\
\\
&={I}_{211}(x,y,t)+{I}_{212}(x,y,t).\end{array}

We begin by estimating
{I}_{211}
. Using estimates
(
3.2
), (
3.5
) in Lemma
3.1
and
according to Lemma
2.5
, we obtain that

(3.11)

\begin{array}[]{lll}{I}_{211}(x,y,t)&\leq&C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{1}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}|\,(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|z-y|)^{1-\sigma}}dz\\
&\leq&C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{1}{|x-z|^{N-(1-\rho)}\,|z-y|^{1-\sigma}}dz\\
&\leq&C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\bigg(1+|\log|x-y||)\bigg)&\mbox{ if }\sigma=\rho,\\
\bigg(1+|x-y|^{\sigma-\rho})\bigg)&\mbox{ if }\sigma\neq\rho\end{array}\right.\end{array}

We deal now with
{I}_{212}
. We have

\begin{array}[]{lll}{I}_{212}(x,y,t)&\leq&C|x-y|^{N+\sigma-1}\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}dz\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)|x-y|^{N+\sigma-1}\int\limits_{\Omega}\dfrac{tdz}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|z-y|)^{N+2s}|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)|x-y|^{N+\sigma-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{P_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}dz,\end{array}

where
P_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is the fractional heart kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Setting

W(x,y,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{P_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}dz,

then,
W
solves the equation

(3.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}W_{t}(x,y,t)+(-\Delta)_{y}^{s}W(x,y,t)&=&0&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
W(x,y,0)&=&\dfrac{1}{|x-y|^{N+\rho-1}}&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{array}\right.

see
[
12
]
for more details. Since
\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+\rho-1}}
is a
supersolution to (
3.12
), then
W(x,y,t)\leq\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+\rho-1}}
. Thus

(3.13)

{I}_{212}(x,y,t)\leq C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)|x-y|^{\sigma-\rho}.

Hence, from (
3.11
) and (
3.13
), we get

(3.14)

|{I}_{21}(x,y,t)|\leq C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\bigg(1+|\log|x-y||)\bigg)&\mbox{ if }\sigma=\rho,\\
\bigg(1+|x-y|^{\sigma-\rho})\bigg)&\mbox{ if }\sigma\neq\rho\end{array}\right.

Now, going back to
{I}_{22}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}{I}_{22}(x,y,t)&=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)-\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|z-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}P_{\Omega}(z,y,t)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}dz\\
&=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\Theta_{(y,t)}(x)-\Theta_{(y,t)}(z)}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}dz,\end{array}

where, for
t>0,y\in\Omega
fixed,
\Theta
is defined by

\Theta_{(y,t)}(x)=(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+\sigma}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t).

Notice that, using (
3.2
) and (
3.5
), it holds that

\Theta_{(y,t)}(x)=0
if
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
for a.e.
y\in\Omega
and
t>0
. Moreover, we find that

\Theta_{(y,t)}(x)\leq C\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta^{s}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\times\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\times\dfrac{t}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{2s-\sigma}}.

Notice that

\nabla_{x}\Theta_{(y,t)}(x)=\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}\nabla_{x}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)+P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)\nabla_{x}\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}.

Then, using now (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), it follows that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\Theta_{(y,t)}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+\sigma}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta(x)\wedge t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\bigg)+CP_{\Omega}(x,y,t)\left(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+\sigma-1}.

Notice that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\Theta_{(y,t)}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{C}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{2s-\sigma}}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{(\delta(x))^{1-s}}\chi_{\{\delta(x)<t^{\frac{1}{2s}}\}}+t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}\chi_{\{\delta(x)\geq t^{\frac{1}{2s}}\}}\bigg)+\frac{C\delta^{s}(x)\delta^{s}(y)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{2s-\sigma+1}}.

Thus, for
(y,t)
fixed, we have
\Theta_{(y,t)}\in\mathbb{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
p<\frac{1}{1-s}
, for a.e.
y\in\Omega
and
t>0
.
Therefore, using Lemma 3.1 in
[
4
]
, we obtain that, for
(y,t)
fixed and for a.e.
x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\Theta_{(y,t)}(x)-\Theta_{(y,t)}(z)=\int_{0}^{1}\langle x-z,\nabla\Theta_{(y,t)}(\tau x+(1-\tau)z)\rangle d\tau.

Hence, for
t>0
and for a.e.
x,y\in\Omega
, we have

{I}_{22}(x,y,t)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\langle x-z,\nabla\Theta_{(y,t)}(\tau x+(1-\tau)z)\rangle d\tau\bigg)\frac{dz}{|x-z|^{N+\rho}}.

Setting
\xi_{\tau}=\tau x+(1-\tau)z
and define
I_{\Omega}=\{\tau\in[0,1]:\xi_{\tau}\in\Omega\}
, then

\begin{array}[]{lll}|{I}_{22}(x,y,t)|&\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{|\nabla\Theta_{(y,t)}(\tau x+(1-\tau)z)|}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}d\tau dz\\
&\leq C(\Omega)\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\tau x+(1-\tau)z-y|)^{N+\sigma-1}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}P_{\Omega}(\tau x+(1-\tau)z,y,t)d\tau dz\\
&+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\tau x+(1-\tau)z-y|)^{N+\sigma}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}|\nabla P_{\Omega}(\tau x+(1-\tau)z,y,t)|d\tau dz\\
&\leq C(\Omega)\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{I_{\Omega}}\dfrac{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\xi_{\tau}-y|)^{N+\sigma-1}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}P_{\Omega}(\xi_{\tau},y,t)d\tau dz+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{I_{\Omega}}\dfrac{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\xi_{\tau}-y|)^{N+\sigma}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}|\nabla P_{\Omega}(\xi_{\tau},y,t)|d\tau dz\\
&={I}_{221}(x,y,t)+{I}_{222}(x,y,t).\end{array}

Since
\Omega
is a bounded domain, we get the existence of
R>>1
such that
\Omega\subset B_{R}(0)
and
\xi_{\tau}\in B_{R}(0)
for all
\tau\in[0,1]
. Hence, using
(
3.2
) and (
3.5
), we get

\begin{array}[]{lll}{I}_{221}(x,y,t)&\leq\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\xi_{\tau}-y|)^{N+\sigma-1}P_{\Omega}(\xi_{\tau},y,t)d\tau\bigg)\frac{dz}{|z-x|^{N+\rho-1}}\\
&\leq(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\dfrac{1}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\xi_{\tau}-y|)^{1-\sigma}|z-x|^{N+\rho-1}}dz\bigg)d\tau\\
&\leq(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\dfrac{1}{|\xi_{\tau}-y|^{1-\sigma}|z-x|^{N+\rho-1}}dz\bigg)d\tau\\
&\leq(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\dfrac{1}{|z-(1-\tau)^{-1}(y-\tau x)|^{1-\sigma}|z-x|^{N+\rho-1}}dz\bigg)\frac{d\tau}{(1-\tau)^{1-\sigma}}\\
&\leq C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\tau}{(1-\tau)^{1-\sigma}}(1+|\ln(\frac{|x-y|}{1-\tau}|)\mbox{ if }\sigma=\rho,\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\tau}{(1-\tau)^{1-\rho}}(1+|x-y|^{\sigma-\rho})\mbox{ if }\sigma\neq\rho,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\\
&\leq C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(1+|\ln|x-y||)\mbox{ if }\sigma=\rho,\\
(1+|x-y|^{\sigma-\rho})\mbox{ if }\sigma\neq\rho.\\
\end{array}\right.\end{array}

Hence

(3.15)

I_{221}\leq C(\frac{\delta^{s}(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge 1)\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(1+|\ln|x-y||)\mbox{ if }\sigma=\rho,\\
(1+|x-y|^{\sigma-\rho})\mbox{ if }\sigma\neq\rho.\\
\end{array}\right.

We estimate now
I_{222}
. Using (
3.3
), it follows
that

\begin{array}[]{lll}{I}_{222}(x,y,t)&=&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{I_{\Omega}}\dfrac{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\xi_{\tau}-y|)^{N+\sigma}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}|\nabla P_{\Omega}(\xi_{\tau},y,t)|d\tau dz\\
&\leq&C\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{I_{\Omega}}\dfrac{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\xi_{\tau}-y|)^{N+\sigma}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\delta(\xi_{\tau})\wedge t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\right)P_{\Omega}(\xi_{\tau},y,t)d\tau dz\\
&\leq&C\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{I_{\Omega}}\dfrac{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\xi_{\tau}-y|)^{N+\sigma}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(\xi_{\tau},y,t)}{\delta(\xi_{\tau})}\chi_{\{\delta(\xi_{\tau})<t^{\frac{1}{2s}}\}}d\tau dz\\
&+&C\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{I_{\Omega}}\dfrac{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|\xi_{\tau}-y|)^{N+\sigma}}{|x-z|^{N+\rho-1}}\dfrac{P_{\Omega}(\xi_{\tau},y,t)}{t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\chi_{\{\delta(\xi_{\tau})\geq t^{\frac{1}{2s}}\}}d\tau dz\end{array}

Now, using (
3.2
) and (
3.5

\begin{array}[]{llll}&&|(-\Delta)_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{s}(x,y)|\leq C\displaystyle\delta^{s-\rho}(x)\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{dt}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+s}}dt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{t^{\frac{s+\rho-1}{2s}}dt}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s+\rho-1}}dt\bigg)\\
&+&\displaystyle(|\log(\delta(x))|+\log\frac{D}{|x-y|})\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}dt}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s+\rho-1}}dt\\
&+&\displaystyle|x-y|^{2s-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{dt}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s+\rho-1}}dt.\end{array}

Since
s>\frac{1}{4}
, then the above integrals are converging near
the zero. Now, setting
\hat{t}=\frac{t}{|x-y|^{2s}}
, it holds
that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{dt}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+s}}dt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{t^{\frac{s+\rho-1}{2s}}dt}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s+\rho-1}}\leq\frac{C(\Omega)}{|x-y|^{N-s}},

\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}dt}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s+\rho-1}}dt\leq\frac{C(\Omega)}{|x-y|^{N-(2s-\rho)}},

and

|x-y|^{2s-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{dt}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s+\rho-1}}dt\leq\frac{C(\Omega)}{|x-y|^{N-(2s-\rho)}}.

Thus,

\begin{array}[]{lll}|(-\Delta)_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{s}(x,y)|&\leq&\displaystyle\dfrac{C}{|x-y|^{N-(2s-\rho)}}\bigg(\frac{\delta^{s-\rho}(x)}{|x-y|^{s-\rho}}+\log\frac{D}{|x-y|}+|\log(\delta(x)|\bigg).\end{array}

∎

4.
Application to the regularity of the fractional heat
equation.

The main goal of this section is to establish the regularity of the solution
w
to Problem
(FHE)
in suitable Bessel potential and fractional Sobolev spaces, depending on the regularity of the data
h
and
w_{0}
. This will be achieved by means of the representation formula and the sharp estimate previously obtained for the heat kernel.

To simplify our presentation, we will consider separately the two cases:

\bullet
Case:
h=0
,
w_{0}\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
;

\bullet
Case:
h\in L^{m}(\Omega_{T})
,
w_{0}=0
.

4.1.
Case:
w_{0}\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
and
h=0
.

In this case, the problem
(FHE)
takes the form

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}w_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}w&=&0&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\\
w(x,t)&=&0&\text{in}\quad(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
w(x,0)&=&w_{0}(x)&\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Before going further, we state in the next theorem, a regularity result related to the
regularity of a class of hyper-singular integrals. This result will be used
systematically throughout this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
g\in L^{m}(\Omega)
with
m\geq 1
. For
x\in\Omega
and
t>0
, we define

G_{\lambda}(x,t)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{g(y)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+\lambda}}dy,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, there exists a positive
constant depending on
\Omega
and the data and it is
independent of
t
and
g
such that:

\bullet
If
N+\lambda\leq 0
, then
G_{\lambda}(x,t)\leq C(\Omega)(t^{-\frac{N+\lambda}{2s}}+1)||g||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
a.e. for
t>0
and

x\in\Omega
.

\bullet
If
\lambda\geq 0
, then for
p>m
, we have

(4.2)

||G_{\lambda}(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{\frac{-\lambda}{2s}-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{\ell})}||g||_{L^{m}(\Omega)}.

\bullet
If
-N<\lambda<0
, then considering
m_{0}
such that
1\leq m_{0}<\min\{m,-\frac{N}{\lambda}\}
, then for all
p>m_{0}
and for all

\nu>0
, we have

(4.3)

||G_{\lambda}(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}t^{\frac{-\lambda}{2s}-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{m_{0}}-\frac{1}{p})}||g||_{L^{m}(\Omega)}&\mbox{ if
}p>\frac{m_{0}N}{N+m_{0}\lambda},\\
t^{-\nu\frac{N}{2s}}||g||_{L^{m}(\Omega)}&\mbox{ if
}p\leq\frac{m_{0}N}{N+m_{0}\lambda}.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, in the case where
\lambda<0
, then for all
p>m
, we have

(4.4)

||G_{\lambda}(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(t^{-\frac{\lambda}{2s}}+1)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{m})}||g||_{L^{m}(\Omega)}.

We refer the reader to the Appendix for the proof.

Now, we are ready to state our first regularity result about the fractional regularity of the solution to Problem (
4.1
).
In order to clarify the structure of the proof, we proceed by distinguishing two separate cases :

\bullet

Inside regularity:
x\in\Omega
and
t\in(0,T)
;

\bullet

Outside regularity:
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
and
t\in(0,T)
.

Theorem 4.2

Assume that

(1)

\begin{array}[]{lll}&&||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\widehat{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\times&\bigg(t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(1+\eta)(\rho-s)}+t^{-\frac{\rho-s}{2s}}+t^{-\frac{\rho+\eta-s}{2s}}+t^{-\frac{N\eta}{2sp(1+\eta)}-\frac{\rho-s}{2s}}\bigg)||w_{0}||_{L^{\widehat{\sigma}}(\Omega)}.\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{lll}&&||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{2}}\\

(4.5)

Using Theorem

&\times&\bigg(t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(1+\eta)(\rho-s)}+t^{-\frac{\rho+\eta-s}{2s}}+t^{-\frac{N\eta}{2sp(1+\eta)}-\frac{\rho-s}{2s}}+t^{-\frac{\rho-s}{2s}}\bigg)||w_{0}||_{L^{\widehat{\sigma}}(\Omega)}.\end{array}

(4.24)

\begin{array}[]{lll}&&||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\widehat{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(1+\eta)(\rho-s)}+t^{-\frac{\rho-s}{2s}}\bigg)||w_{0}||_{L^{\widehat{\sigma}}(\Omega)}\\
&+&C(t^{\frac{1-2s-\rho}{2s}}+1)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\widehat{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{p})}\bigg(t^{\frac{2s-1-\eta}{2s}}+t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(1+\eta)(\rho-s)+\frac{s+\rho-1}{2s}}+t^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}\bigg)||w_{0}||_{L^{\widehat{\sigma}}(\Omega)}\end{array}

Notice that estimate (
4.24
) is equivalent to estimate
(
4.23
) for
t>>1
.

As a consequence, we have the next result.

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
4.2
are
all satisfied, and let
w
be the unique weak solution to Problem
(
4.1
). Then

(1)

If
s\leq\rho<s+\frac{s}{N}
and
\sigma=1
, then

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w\in L^{p}(\Omega_{T})
for all

p<\frac{N+2s}{N+s+N(\rho-s)}
and

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w||_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega_{T})||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

where
C(\Omega_{T})\to 0
as
T\to 0
.

(2)

Assume that
\rho=s
, define

\widehat{p_{s}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\sigma(N+2s)}{N+\sigma s}\mbox{ if }\>\>3s>1\\
\\
\frac{\sigma_{0}(N+2s)}{N+\sigma_{0}s}\mbox{ if }\>\>3s<1,\end{array}\right.

where
\sigma_{0}<\min\{\sigma,\frac{N}{1-3s}\}
. Then for all
p<\widehat{p_{s}}
, there exists a positive constant
C=C(\Omega)

such that

(4.25)

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}w||_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega)\bigg(T^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}}+T^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{p(1+\eta)})-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}}+T^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{s+\eta}{2s}+\frac{1}{p}}\bigg)||w_{0}||_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)},

where
\eta>0
is small enough and chosen such that

p<\min\bigg\{\frac{\sigma(N+2s)}{N+\sigma(s+\eta)},\frac{\sigma(\frac{N}{1+\eta}+2s)}{N+\sigma s}\bigg\}
.

In particular, if
\sigma=1
, then for all
p<\frac{N+2s}{N+s}
, we have
||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}w||_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega_{T})||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
where

C(\Omega_{T})\to 0
as
T\to+\infty
.

To complete our global regularity result in this case, we have to
estimate the fractional gradient of
w
outside
\Omega
. More
precisely, we have the next proposition :

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
4.2
are satisfied. Let
w
be the unique weak solution to Problem
(
4.1
). Assume that
s\leq\rho<\max\{2s,1\}
,
p<\frac{1}{\rho-s}
and fix
\alpha
small enough
such that
p<\frac{1}{\rho-s+\alpha}
.
Let
p_{0}\geq p
be such that
p_{0}>\frac{Np}{N+\alpha p-pN(\rho-s+\alpha)}
, then, there exists a
positive constant
C
depends only on
\Omega
and the data and
it is independent of
t
, such that

(4.26)

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega){\bigg\|\dfrac{w}{\delta^{s}}(.,t)\bigg\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)}}\leq C(\Omega)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{p_{0}})-\frac{1}{2}}\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)}.

Moreover, for
\rho=s
, then
|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}w|\in{L^{p}((\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)\times(0,T))}
for all

p<\widehat{p_{s}}:=\dfrac{\sigma(N+2s)}{N+\sigma s}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
w_{0}\gneqq 0
. Thus,
w\gneqq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Hence,
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega
, we have

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(x,t)|=\bigg|\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{-w(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+\rho}}dy\bigg|=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{w(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+\rho}}dy.

Notice that, for
y\in\Omega
, we have
|x-y|\geq\delta(y)
. Let
p\geq\sigma
be fixed, we consider the exterior set
\Omega_{1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega;\,\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>>1\}
, then
|x-y|\geq\frac{|x|+1}{2}
for all
y\in\Omega
. Thus, for
x\in\Omega_{1}
, we get

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(x,t)|\leq\frac{2}{(|x|+1)^{N+\rho}}\int_{\Omega}|w(y,t)|dy\leq\frac{C}{(|x|+1)^{N+\rho}}\bigg\|\frac{w(.,t)}{\delta^{s}}\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, we conclude that
|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(.,t)|\in L^{1}(\Omega_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
and, for all
\theta\geq 1
, for all
r>\sigma
, we have

(4.27)

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(,t)||_{L^{\theta}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C(\Omega,r)\bigg\|\frac{w(.,t)}{\delta^{s}}\bigg\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega,r)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{r})-\frac{1}{2}}||w_{0}||_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)}.

Now, we treat the integral in the set
\Omega_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega)
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
0<\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq 2
for all
x\in\Omega_{2}
.

So, for
\alpha>0
small enough to be chosen later and for
x\in\Omega_{2}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(x,t)|&=&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{w(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+\rho}}dy\\
&\leq&\dfrac{1}{\delta^{\rho-s+\alpha}(x)}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{w(y,t)}{\delta^{s}(y)}\dfrac{1}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy=\frac{R(x,t)}{(\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega))^{\rho-s+\alpha}},\end{array}

where

R(x,t)=\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{w(y,t)}{\delta^{s}(y)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dydt.

Since
w_{0}\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
with
\sigma\geq 1
, then using Proposition
2.15
, we obtain that

\dfrac{w(y,t)}{\delta^{s}(y)}\in L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
for all
p_{0}\geq\sigma
, to be chosen later, and

\bigg\|\dfrac{w}{\delta^{s}}(.,t)\bigg\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{p_{0}})-\frac{1}{2}}||w_{0}||_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)}.

For
t\in(0,T)
fixed, since
\Omega
is bounded, by using Theorem
2.8
, it follows that
R(.,t)\in L^{\frac{p_{0}N}{N-p_{0}\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and

||R(.,t)||_{L^{\frac{p_{0}N}{N-p_{0}\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C(\Omega)\bigg\|\dfrac{w(.,t)}{\delta^{s}}\bigg\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)}.

Fixed now
p_{0}
such that

p_{0}>\frac{Np}{N+\alpha p-pN(\rho-s+\alpha)}
, then by the generalized Hölder inequality, we deduce
that
|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(,t)|\in L^{a}(\Omega_{2})

with
a=\frac{Np_{0}}{(\rho-s+\alpha)Np_{0}+N-\alpha p_{0}}

and

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{2}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(x,t)|^{a}dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{a}}&\leq&\displaystyle C\bigg\|\frac{w(.,t)}{\delta^{s}}\bigg\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{p_{0}})-\frac{1}{2}}\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)}.\end{array}

Notice that, taking into consideration the value of
p_{0}
, we
deduce that
p\leq a
. Hence, choosing
\theta=p
and
r=p_{0}
in
(
4.27
), we deduce that

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(x,t)|\chi_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega\}}\in{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)}
and

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)\bigg\|\dfrac{w}{\delta^{s}}(.,t)\bigg\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{p_{0}})-\frac{1}{2}}||w_{0}||_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)}.

Now, if
\rho=s
, then
\frac{Np}{N+\alpha p-pN(\rho-s+\alpha)}=\frac{Np}{N-\alpha p(N-1)}\to p
as
\alpha\to 0
.
Hence,
we deduce that for all
p>\sigma
and for all
\eta>0
, we have

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)\bigg\|\dfrac{w}{\delta^{s}}(.,t)\bigg\|_{L^{p+\eta}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{p+\eta})-\frac{1}{2}}||w_{0}||_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, if
p<\widehat{p_{s}}:=\dfrac{\sigma(N+2s)}{N+\sigma s}
, then

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}w|\in{L^{p}((\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)\times(0,T))}
and

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{p_{0}})-\frac{1}{2}}\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)},

and

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w||_{L^{p}((\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)\times(0,T))}\leq C(\Omega_{T})||w_{0}||_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)}.

∎

By combining Theorem

Theorem 4.9

||G_{\alpha,\lambda}||_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega)T^{\frac{2s(\alpha+1)-\lambda}{2s}-\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{m_{0}}-\frac{1}{r})}||g||_{L^{m_{0}}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega)T^{\frac{1}{m_{0}}-\frac{1}{m}+\frac{2s(\alpha+1)-\lambda}{2s}-\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{m_{0}}-\frac{1}{r})}||g||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}.

Theorem 4.10
.

s>\frac{1}{4}
and fixed

\rho-s<\min\{\frac{s}{(N+2s)},\frac{4s-1}{(N+2s-1)}\}

\eta>0
small enough, we
have:

(1)

If

, then,

\bullet
If

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w\in{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}

\bullet

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w||_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega,T)||h||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality and using the linearity of the problem,

According to the sign of

Case:

and

Let

4.28
),

(4.29)

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(x,t)|\leq\iint_{\Omega_{t}}h(y,\tau)|(-\Delta)_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2}}P_{\Omega}(x,y,t-\tau)|dyd\tau.\vskip 5.69046pt\\

Using estimate (
3.7
), we reach that

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}w(x,t)|\leq C\left(K_{1}(x,t)+K_{2}(x,t)+K_{3}(x,t)+K_{4}(x,t)+K_{5}(x,t)\right),

K_{3}(x,t):=\delta^{s-\rho}(x)\iint_{\Omega_{t}}\dfrac{h(y,\tau)(t-\tau)^{\frac{s+\rho-1}{2s}}}{\left((t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+2s+\rho-1}}d\tau dy,

K_{4}(x,t):=|\log(\delta(x))|\iint_{\Omega_{t}}\dfrac{h(y,\tau)(t-\tau)^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}}{\left((t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|\right)^{N+2s+\rho-1}}d\tau dy,

and

K_{5}(x,t):=\iint_{\Omega_{t}}\dfrac{h(y,\tau)}{|x-y|^{1-2s}((t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+2s+\rho-1}}d\tau dy,

Since
s>\frac{1}{4}
, then
\frac{2s-1}{2s},\frac{s+\rho-1}{2s}>-1
. To estimate
K_{1}
, we use Theorem
4.9

with
\alpha=0
and
\lambda=s
, then

. Thus,

K_{1}\in L^{r}(\Omega_{T})
for all

m<r<\overline{m}_{s}:=\frac{m(N+2s)}{N+2s-ms}
and

||K_{1}||_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}\leq T^{\gamma_{1}+\frac{1}{a}}||h||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})},

where
\gamma_{1}=\frac{N}{2sa}-\frac{N+s}{2s}
and

a=\frac{m^{\prime}r}{m^{\prime}+r}
. Hence

(4.30)

||K_{1}||_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{r})}||h||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})},

We now consider the term
K_{2}
. Notice that for all
0<\eta<<1
,
there exists a positive constant

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
(h,w_{0})\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})\times L^{1}(\Omega)
, let
w
be the unique weak
solution to
Problem
(FHE)
, then for all
\varrho>0
, for all
r<\frac{N+2s}{N}
and for all

1<\alpha<2s
, we have

(5.1)

{\varrho\,}\bigg|\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|\nabla w(x,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|^{\frac{r+\alpha-1}{\alpha r}}\leq C\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}||w||^{\frac{(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}.

Proof.

Since
s>\frac{1}{2}
, then using Proposition 3.7 in
[
8
]
, for
all
1<\alpha<2s
and for all
k>0
, we have

||T_{k}(w)||_{L^{\alpha}(0,T;{\mathbb{W}^{1,\alpha}_{0}(\Omega)})}\leq C(\Omega,T)k^{\alpha-1}(||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}).

Let
\varrho>0
, then

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|\nabla w(x,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\subset\\
&\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|\nabla w(x,t)|\geq\varrho\mbox{ and
}|w(x,t)|\leq k\bigg\}\cup\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|w(x,t)|\geq k\bigg\}.\end{array}

Hence

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\bigg|\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|\nabla w(x,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\\
\\
&\bigg|\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|\nabla T_{k}(w(x,t))|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|+\bigg|\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|w(x,t)|\geq k\bigg\}\bigg|.\end{array}

Notice that

\bigg|\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|\nabla T_{k}(w(x,t))|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\frac{|\nabla T_{k}(w(x,t))|^{\alpha}}{\varrho^{\alpha}}dxdt\leq\frac{C(\Omega_{T})k^{\alpha-1}}{\varrho^{\alpha}}\bigg(||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}\bigg).

On the other hand, since
w\in L^{r}(\Omega_{T})
, we have

\bigg|\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|w(x,t)|\geq k\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\frac{||w||^{r}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}}{k^{r}}.

Thus

\bigg|\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|\nabla w(x,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\frac{C(\Omega_{T})k^{\alpha-1}}{\varrho^{\alpha}}\bigg(||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}\bigg)+\frac{||w||^{r}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}}{k^{r}}.

Minimizing the second expression in
k
, we reach that

\bigg|\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|\nabla w(x,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\frac{C(\Omega_{T},\alpha)}{\varrho^{\frac{\alpha r}{r+\alpha-1}}}||w||^{\frac{r(\alpha-1)}{r+\alpha-1}}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}\bigg(||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}\bigg)^{\frac{r}{r+\alpha-1}}.

Hence, we conclude.
∎

We consider now the case

Fix

have the next estimate.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
\frac{1}{4}<s\leq\frac{1}{2}
and fix

,

. Then, we have

(5.2)

\varrho\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|^{\frac{2r+q}{2qr}}\leq C\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}||w||^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha>0
, then

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\subset\\
&\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\mbox{ and
}|w(x,t)|\geq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\}\\
&\cup\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\mbox{ and
}|w(y,t)|\geq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\}\\
&\cup\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\mbox{ and
}|w(x,t)|\leq k|x-y|^{\theta},|w(y,t)|\leq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\},\end{array}

where
k>0
and
\theta>0
is chosen such that
\theta\frac{N+2s}{N}<<N
. Then

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\\
\\
&\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\mbox{ and
}|w(x,t)|\geq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\}\bigg|\\
&+\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\mbox{ and
}|w(y,t)|\geq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\}\bigg|\\
\\
&+\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\mbox{ and
}|w(x,t)|\leq k|x-y|^{\theta},|w(y,t)|\leq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\}\bigg|.\end{array}

Notice that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|w(x,t)|\geq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\}\bigg|&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{r}}\int_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|w(x,t)|^{r}}{|x-y|^{r\theta}}dxdydt\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{r}}\int_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}|w(x,t)|^{r}\bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{r\theta}}\bigg)dxdt\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{r}}\int_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}|w(x,t)|^{r}\bigg(\int_{B_{R}(x)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{r\theta}}\bigg)dxdt\\
\end{array}

where
R>0
is chosen such that
\Omega\subset\subset B_{R}(x)
.

Using the fact that
r\theta<<N
, then
\int_{B_{R}(x)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{r\theta}}\leq C(\Omega)
. Hence, we deduce that

(5.3)

\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|w(x,t)|\geq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\}\bigg|\leq C(\Omega)\frac{||w||^{r}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}}{k^{r}}.

In the same way we obtain that

\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|w(y,t)|\geq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\}\bigg|\leq C(\Omega)\frac{||w||^{r}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}}{k^{r}}.

We estimate now the term

\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\mbox{ and
}|w(x,t)|\leq k|x-y|^{\theta},|w(y,t)|\leq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\}\bigg|.

Since
\Omega
is a bounded domain, then for all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega
, we have
|x-y|^{\theta}\leq C(\Omega)
. Thus

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\mbox{ and
}|w(x,t)|\leq k|x-y|^{\theta},|w(y,t)|\leq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\}\subset\\
&\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\mbox{ and
}|w(y,t)|\leq C(\Omega)k,|w(y,t)|\leq C(\Omega)k\bigg\}.\end{array}

Let
k_{1}=C(\Omega)k
, then, under the condition
|w(x,t)|\leq C(\Omega)k,|w(y,t)|\leq C(\Omega)k
, we have

W(x,y,t)=\frac{T_{k_{1}}(w(x,t))-T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{q}+s_{1}}}.

Thus

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\mbox{ and
}|w(x,t)|\leq k|x-y|^{\theta},|w(y,t)|\leq k|x-y|^{\theta}\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\\
\\
&\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }\bigg|\dfrac{T_{k_{1}}(w(x,t))-T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{q}+s}}\bigg|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|.\end{array}

Notice that for
t\in(0,T)
fixed, according to Theorem
2.2

(see Theorem 1.2 in
[
35
]
), we reach that

\begin{array}[]{lll}H_{\varrho}(t)&:=&\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega\mbox{ with }\bigg|\dfrac{T_{k_{1}}(w(x,t))-T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{q}+s}}\bigg|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|\\
\\
&\leq&\dfrac{C}{\varrho^{q}}\bigg\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))\bigg\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\simeq\dfrac{C}{\varrho^{q}}\bigg\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))\bigg\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.\end{array}

Integrating in time, we reach that

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }\bigg|\dfrac{T_{k_{1}}(w(x,t))-T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{q}+s}}\bigg|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|\\
&=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}H_{\varrho}(t)dt\leq\dfrac{C}{\varrho^{q}}\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))||^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}dt\\
\\
&\leq\dfrac{C}{\varrho^{q}}\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))||^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}dt+\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))||^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)}dt\bigg).\end{array}

Using Hölder’s inequality, it holds that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))||^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}dt&\leq&\displaystyle C(\Omega)\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))||^{q}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}dt\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C(\Omega_{T})\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))||^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}dt\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C(\Omega_{T})\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}dt\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}}.\end{array}

Using the fact that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}dt&\simeq&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|T_{k_{1}}(w(x,t))-T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdydt\\
&\leq&k_{1}\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)})\bigg),\end{array}

we deduce that

\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))||^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}dt\leq C(\Omega_{T})k^{\frac{q}{2}}_{1}\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}}.

To estimate the term
\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))||^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)}dt
, we follow closely the computations used in the proof of
Proposition
4.5
.

Let
\Omega_{1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega;\,\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>>1\}
, then
|x-y|\geq\frac{|x|+1}{2}
for all
x\in\Omega_{1}
and
y\in\Omega
. Hence, using Hölder’s inequality, we
have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t)||^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega_{1})}dt&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\frac{2}{(|x|+1)^{q(N+s)}}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}|T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))|dy\bigg)^{q}dxdt\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C(\Omega_{1})\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}|T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))|dy\bigg)^{q}dt\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C(\Omega)\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))|^{2}}{\delta^{2s}(y)}dydt\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}}.\end{array}

Let now
\Omega_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega)
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
0<\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq 2
for all
x\in\Omega_{2}
.
For
\alpha>0
small enough, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t)||^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega_{2})}dt&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))}{|x-y|^{N+s}}dy\bigg)^{q}dxdt\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\frac{dx}{\delta^{q\alpha}(x)}\bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))|}{\delta^{s}(y)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy\bigg)^{q}dxdt\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\frac{R^{q}(x,t)}{\delta^{q\alpha}(x)}dxdt,\\
\end{array}

where
R(x,t)=\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))|}{\delta^{s}(y)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy
.
Notice that
\dfrac{T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))}{\delta^{s}(y)}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, then by
using Theorem
2.8
, it holds that
R(.,t)\in L^{\frac{2N}{N-2\alpha}}(\Omega_{2})
and

||R(.,t)||_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-2\alpha}}(\Omega_{2})}\leq C\bigg\|\frac{T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))}{\delta^{s}(.)}\bigg\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Hence, for

q<\frac{2N}{N-2\alpha}
, using Hölder’s inequality, choosing
\alpha>0

such that
q\alpha\frac{2N}{2N-q(N-2\alpha)}<<1
, we get

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))||^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega_{2})}dt&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\bigg(R^{\frac{2N}{N-2\alpha}}(x,t)dx\bigg)^{\frac{q(N-2\alpha)}{2N}}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{2}}\frac{dx}{(\delta(x))^{q\alpha\frac{2N}{2N-q(N-2\alpha)}}}\bigg)^{\frac{2N-q(N-2\alpha)}{2N}}dt\\
\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C(\Omega_{2})\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\bigg(R^{\frac{2N}{N-2\alpha}}(x,t)dx\bigg)^{\frac{q(N-2\alpha)}{2N}}dx\\
\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C(\Omega)\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\|\frac{T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t))}{\delta^{s}(.)}\bigg\|^{q}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}dt\leq\displaystyle C(\Omega_{T})\bigg\|\frac{T_{k_{1}}w}{\delta^{s}}\bigg\|^{q}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}.\end{array}

Thus, combining the above estimates, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}T_{k_{1}}(w(.,t)||^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega)}dt\leq C(\Omega_{T})\bigg\|\frac{T_{k_{1}}w}{\delta^{s}}\bigg\|^{q}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}.

Since
T_{k_{1}}(w)\in L^{2}(0,T,{\mathbb{W}^{s,2}_{0}(\Omega)})
, then using the
Hardy’s
inequality in Theorem
2.4
, we reach that

\bigg\|\frac{T_{k_{1}}(w)}{\delta^{s}}\bigg\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega)\int_{0}^{T}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|T_{k_{1}}(w(x,t))-T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdydt\leq k_{1}\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg).

In conclusion, we find that

(5.4)

\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }\bigg|\frac{T_{k_{1}}(w(x,t))-T_{k_{1}}(w(y,t))}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{q}+s}}\bigg|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\frac{C(\Omega_{T})k^{\frac{q}{2}}_{1}}{\varrho^{q}}\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}}.

Recall that
k_{1}=C(\Omega)k
, then, combining estimates
(
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we conclude that

\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\frac{C(\Omega_{T})k^{\frac{q}{2}}}{\varrho^{q}}\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}}+C(\Omega)\frac{||w||^{r}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}}{k^{r}}.

Now minimizing in
k>0
, it holds that

\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\frac{C(\Omega_{T})}{\sigma^{\frac{2qr}{2r+q}}}\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{\frac{qr}{2r+q}}||w||^{\frac{qr}{2r+q}}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}.

Hence, the result follows.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

\bullet
If
s>\frac{1}{2}
, according to (
5.1
), we
obtain that

\sup_{\varrho>0}\sigma\bigg|\bigg\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\mbox{ with }|\nabla w(x,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|^{\frac{r+\alpha-1}{\alpha r}}:=M\leq C\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}||w||^{\frac{(\alpha-1)}{2}}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}.

Thus,
|\nabla w|\in\mathcal{M}^{\frac{\alpha r}{r+\alpha-1}}(\Omega_{T})
, the Marcinkiewicz space. Since
\Omega_{T}
is a
bounded domain, we deduce that if
\frac{\alpha r}{r+\alpha-1}>1
, then
for all
1\leq b<\frac{\alpha r}{r+\alpha-1}
, we have

||\nabla w||_{L^{b}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega_{T},r,b)M\leq C(\Omega_{T},r,b)\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}||w||^{\frac{(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}.

Taking into consideration that the above estimate holds for all

\alpha<2s
and for all
r<\frac{N+2s}{N}
, then we deduce that for
all
b<\frac{N+2s}{N+1}
, we have

(5.5)

||\nabla w||_{L^{b}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega_{T},r,b)M\leq C(\Omega_{T},r,b)\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}||w||^{\frac{(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}.

\bullet
If
\frac{1}{4}<s\leq\frac{1}{2}
, according to
(
5.2
), we get

\sup_{\varrho>0}\varrho\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{
with }|W(x,y,t)|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|^{\frac{2r+q}{2qr}}:=M\leq C\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}||w||^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}.

Hence,
w\in\mathcal{M}^{\frac{2qr}{2r+q}}(\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T))
. Since
\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)
is bounded, we
obtain that, if
\frac{2qr}{2r+q}>1
, then
W\in L^{a}(\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T))
for all
a<\frac{2qr}{2r+q}
and

(5.6)

||W||_{L^{a}(\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T))}\leq C(\Omega_{T},r,q)M\leq C(\Omega_{T},r,p)\bigg(||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}||w||^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}.

We are now in position to show that
\Gamma
is a compact operator.

Theorem 5.4
.

Consider
\Phi
, the operator defined previously, then

\Phi
is compact.

Proof.

Let
\{h_{n}\}_{n}\subset L^{1}(\Omega_{T}),\{w_{0n}\}_{n}\subset L^{1}(\Omega)
be
such that

||h_{n}||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0n}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\hat{C}
. Define
w_{n}=\Phi(h_{n},w_{0n})
, then

w_{n}
solves the Problem:

(5.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}(w_{n})_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}w_{n}&=&h_{n}&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\\
w_{n}(x,t)&=&0&\text{in}\quad(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
w_{n}(x,0)&=&w_{0n}&\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{array}\right.

According to Corollary
4.19
and the previous regularity
results, for
\rho
fixed such that
s\leq\rho<s+\min\{\frac{s}{(N+2s)},\frac{4s-1}{(N+2s-1)}\}
, then for
all
a<\widehat{\kappa}_{s,\rho}
, we have

||w_{n}||_{L^{a}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{0}^{\rho,a}(\Omega))}\leq C(\Omega_{T})\bigg(||h_{n}||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+||w_{0n}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\bigg).

Let
{q}<\min\{\frac{N+2s}{N+s},\frac{N+2s}{N+1-2s}\}
, then we get
the existence of
\rho>s
close to
s
such that

q<\widehat{\kappa}_{s,\rho}
and
||w_{n}||_{{L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{0}^{{\rho,q}}(\Omega))}}\leq C(\Omega_{T})
. Hence, we get the
existence of
w\in L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{0}^{\rho,q}(\Omega))
such
that, up to a subsequence, we have
w_{n}\rightharpoonup w
weakly in

L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{0}^{s,q}(\Omega))
and
w_{n}\to w
strongly
in
L^{a}(\Omega_{T})
for all
a<\frac{N+2s}{N}
and
w_{n}\to w
a.e. in

\Omega_{T}
. Moreover, we have also that
\dfrac{w_{n}}{\delta^{s}}\to\dfrac{w}{\delta^{s}}
strongly in
L^{a_{1}}(\Omega_{T})
for all

a_{1}<\frac{N+2s}{N+s}
.

For
n\geq m
, we set
\mathcal{W}_{n,m}=w_{n}-w_{m}
, then

\mathcal{W}_{n,m}
solves the problem

(5.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}(\mathcal{W}_{n,m})_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}\mathcal{W}_{n,m}&=&h_{n}-h_{m}&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\\
\mathcal{W}_{n,m}&=&0&\text{in}\quad(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
\mathcal{W}_{n,m}(x,0)&=&0&\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Notice that
\mathcal{W}_{n,m}\to 0
a.e. in
\Omega_{T}
and

\mathcal{W}_{n,m}\to 0
strongly in
L^{a}(\Omega_{T})
for all

a<\frac{N+2s}{N}
as
n,m\to\infty
.

We spilt the proof into two cases according to the value of
s
.

Case:
s>\frac{1}{2}
.
In this case we have

\frac{N+2s}{N+s}<\frac{N+2s}{N+1-2s}
. Fixed

q<\frac{N+2s}{N+s}
. Let
a<\frac{N+2s}{N+1}
to be chosen later,
then we get the
existence of
1<\alpha<2s
such that
a<\frac{N+2s}{N+\frac{2s}{\alpha}}
.
Hence, there exists
r<\frac{N+2s}{N}
such that
q<\frac{\alpha r}{r+\alpha-1}
. Going back to estimate (
5.5
), it
holds that

||\nabla\mathcal{W}_{n,m}||_{L^{a}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega_{T},r,a,\hat{C})||\mathcal{W}_{n,m}||^{\frac{(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}\to 0\mbox{
as }n,m\to\infty.

Therefore, we conclude that
\{w_{n}\}_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in the space

{L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,a}_{0}(\Omega))}
for all
a<\frac{N+2s}{N+1}
.
Thus,

w_{n}\to w
strongly in
{L^{a}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,a}_{0}(\Omega))}
. Hence
w_{n}\to w
strongly in
L^{a}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,a}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
. Using an
interpolation result, see
[
46
]
, it results that, for

q<\frac{N+2s}{N+s}
fixed, we can get the existence of

a<\frac{N+2s}{N+1}
and
r<\frac{N+2s}{N}
such that

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-s}{r}+\frac{s}{a}
with

||w_{n}(.,t)-w(.,t)||_{\mathbb{L}_{0}^{s,q}(\Omega)}\leq||w_{n}(.,t)-w(.,t)||^{1-s}_{L^{r}(\Omega)}||w_{n}(.,t)-w(.,t)||^{s}_{\mathbb{W}^{1,a}_{0}(\Omega)}\,a.e.\mbox{ for }t\in(0,T).

Integrating in time, we get

||w_{n}-w||_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{0}^{s,q}(\Omega))}\leq||w_{n}-w||^{1-s}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}||w_{n}-w||^{s}_{{L^{a}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,a}_{0}(\Omega))}}.

Therefore, we conclude that, for
q<\frac{N+2s}{N+s}
,

||w_{n}-w||_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{0}^{s,q}(\Omega))}\to 0\mbox{ as }n\to\infty,

that is the desired result for
s>\frac{1}{2}
.

Case:
s\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
Recall that

q<\min\{\frac{N+2s}{N+s},\frac{N+2s}{{N+1-2s}}\}
.

Let
\varrho>0
, then applying estimate (
5.2
) to the
function
\mathcal{W}_{n,m}
, it holds that

\bigg|\bigg\{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)\mbox{ with }\bigg|\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n,m}(x,t)-\mathcal{W}_{n,m}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{q}+s}}\bigg|\geq\varrho\bigg\}\bigg|\leq\frac{C(\Omega_{T})}{\varrho^{\frac{2qr}{2r+q}}}\bigg\|\mathcal{W}_{n,m}\bigg\|^{\frac{qr}{2r+q}}_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}\to 0\mbox{ as }n,m\to\infty.

Thus,

\bigg\{\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n,m}(x,t)-\mathcal{W}_{n,m}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{q}+s}}\bigg\}_{n,m}

is a Cauchy sequence in the sense of measure. Hence, up to a
subsequence, for a.e.
(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,T)
, we
have,

\frac{|w_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(y,t)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs}}\to\frac{|w(x,t)-w(y,t)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs}}\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Since
q<\frac{N+2s}{N+s}
, then

\int_{0}^{T}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|w_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(y,t)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs}}dxdydt\leq C.

Therefore, by Vitali’s Lemma, we deduce that

(5.9)

\int_{0}^{T}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|w_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(y,t)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs}}dxdydt\to\int_{0}^{T}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|w(x,t)-w(y,t)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs}}dxdydt\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Recall that
\dfrac{w_{n}}{\delta^{s}}\to\dfrac{w}{\delta^{s}}
strongly in

L^{\beta}(\Omega_{T})
for all
\beta<\frac{N+2s}{N+s}
. Hence, using
(
5.9
), we deduce that
w_{n}\to w
strongly in

L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{s,q}_{0}(\Omega))
. Now, by Proposition
2.1
,
since
\frac{N+2s}{N+s}<2
, we obtain that
w_{n}\to w
strongly in

L^{a}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{0}^{s,a}(\Omega))
for all

a<\min\{\frac{N+2s}{N+s},\frac{N+2s}{N+1-2s}\}
. Hence, the result
follows.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

In the case where
s\in[\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
, then

\frac{N+2s}{N+s}\leq\frac{N+2s}{N+1-2s}
. Hence, the compactness
result holds for all
a<\frac{N+2s}{N+s}
.

Remark 5.6
.

The arguments used in the case
s\leq\frac{1}{2}
are valid also for the case
s>\frac{1}{2}
. However,
we have include the alternative proof in the case
s>\frac{1}{2}
which is based on the regularity of the local gradient.

6.
Fractional Kardar-Parisi-Zhang Problem with nonlocal gradient term.

The purpose of this section is to apply the previous
regularity and compactness results to analyze the existence of solution
u
to the
Problem

{(KPZ_{f})}\quad\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{q}+f&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\\
u(x,t)&=&0&\text{in}\quad(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)&=&u_{0}(x)&\text{in }\quad\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Here
(f,u_{0})\in L^{m}(\Omega_{T})\times L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
with
(m,\sigma)\in[1,+\infty)^{2}
,
\frac{1}{3}<s<1
and
q\leq 1
. To simplify the
presentation of our results, we will consider separately two
cases:

i)
Case:
f\neq 0
and
u_{0}=0
;

ii)
Case:
f=0
and
u_{0}\neq 0
.

First, in the next definition, we specify the sense of the weak solution to Problem
{(KPZ_{f})}
.

Definition 6.1
.

Suppose
(f,u_{0})\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})\times L^{1}(\Omega)
. We say that
u
is a weak solution to
(KPZ_{f})

if
u,|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{q}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
with
u\equiv 0
a.e. in
(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T)
, and for all

\phi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{s}}
, we have

(6.1)

\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\,u\big(-\phi_{t}\,+(-\Delta)^{s}\phi\big)\,dx\,dt=\\
\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\,(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{q}+f)\phi\,dxdt+\int_{\Omega}{u_{0}(x)\phi(x,0)\,dx},

with
\phi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{s}}
, given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{s}}:=\left\{\phi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;;\;\phi(x,t)=0\;\;\,\text{in}\;\;(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T)\;\;\text{and}\;\;(-\Delta)^{s}\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})\right\}.

6.1.
Case:
f\neq 0

and
u_{0}=0
.

In this case, we treat mainly the Problem

(6.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{q}+f&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\\
u(x,t)&=&0&\text{in}\quad(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)&=&0&\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Our existence result is given in the next theorem.

Theorem 6.2
.

Suppose
f\in L^{m}(\Omega_{T})
with
1\leq m
and
s\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
. Assume that one of the following conditions hold

(1)

s\in(\frac{1}{3},1)
and

(6.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1\leq m\leq\frac{N+2s}{s}\vskip 5.69046pt\mbox{ and }1<q\leq\frac{N+2s}{N+2s-ms},\\
\mbox{ or }\\
m>\frac{N+2s}{s}\vskip 11.38092pt\mbox{ and }q\in(1,\infty).\end{array}\right.

(2)

s\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{3}]
and

(6.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1\leq m\leq\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}\vskip 11.38092pt\mbox{ and }1<q\leq\frac{N+2s}{N+2s-m(4s-1)},\\
\mbox{ or }\\
m>\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}\vskip 17.07182pt\mbox{ and }q\in(1,\infty).\end{array}\right.

Then, there exists
0<T^{*}<T
such that Problem (
6.2
)
has a solution
u\in L^{\gamma}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{L}^{s,\gamma}_{0}(\Omega))
with

(1)

1<\gamma<\overline{m}:=\frac{m(N+2s)}{N+2s-ms}
if
1\leq m<\frac{N+2s}{s}
, and
1<\gamma<\infty
if
m\geq\frac{N+2s}{s}
, if
s\in(\frac{1}{3},1)
,

or

(2)

1<\gamma<\overline{m}:=\frac{m(N+2s)}{N+2s-m(4s-1)}
if
1\leq m<\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}
, and
1<\gamma<\infty
if
m\geq\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}
, if
s\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{3}]
.

Remark 6.3
.

Taking into consideration the relation between
\mathbb{W}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

and
\mathbb{L}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we deduce that the solution to
Problem (
6.2
) obtained in the previous theorem
satisfies:

(1)

If
s\in(\frac{1}{3},1)
, then

i)

u\in L^{\gamma}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{W}^{s,\gamma}_{0}(\Omega))
for
all
1<\gamma<\frac{m(N+2s)}{N+2s-ms}
if
1\leq m<\frac{N+2s}{s}
,

ii)

u\in L^{\gamma}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{W}^{s,\gamma}_{0}(\Omega))

for all
1<\gamma<\infty
if
m\geq\frac{N+2s}{s}
.

(2)

If
s\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{3}]
, then

i)

u\in L^{\gamma}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{W}^{s,\gamma}_{0}(\Omega))
for
all
1<\gamma<\frac{m(N+2s)}{N+2s-m(4s-1)}
if
1\leq m<\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}
,

ii)

u\in L^{\gamma}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{W}^{s,\gamma}_{0}(\Omega))

for all
1<\gamma<\infty
if
m\geq\frac{N+2s}{4s-1}
.

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

We give a complete proof under the first set of conditions, the other cases follow in the same
way using the corresponding regularity tools..

Suppose that
f\in L^{m}(\Omega_{T})
with
1\leq m\leq\frac{N+2s}{s}
, let
q>1
be such that
1<q\leq\frac{N+2s}{N+2s-ms}
, then
qm<\overline{m}_{s}=\frac{m(N+2s)}{N+2s-ms}
. Hence, we get the existence of
r>1
such that

qm<r<\overline{m}_{s}
.

Fixed
r
as above, then we define the set

E:=\left\{\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;\,\,\mathbb{L}^{s,1+\xi}_{0}(\Omega))\,\,;\,\,||\varphi||_{L^{r}(0,T;\,\,\mathbb{L}^{s,r}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq\ell^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\},

with
0<\xi<q-1
. It is clear that
E
is a convex and closed set. Now, we consider the operator
\hat{\Phi}
defined by

\begin{array}[]{lll}\hat{\Phi}&:&E\to L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{s,1+\xi}_{0}(\Omega))\\
&&\varphi\to{\hat{\Gamma}}(\varphi)=u,\end{array}

with
u
being the unique weak solution of the Problem

(6.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi|^{q}+f&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\\
u(x,t)&=&0&\text{in}\quad(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)&=&0&\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{array}\right.

To obtain our existence result, we just need to prove that

\hat{\Phi}
has a fixed point. The main idea is to the Schauder’s fixed point
Theorem in a suitable set. The proof will be given in
several steps.

Step 1.
We show that
\hat{\Phi}
is well-defined.

Indeed,
since
\varphi\in E
and
qm<r
, then

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi|^{q}+f\in L^{m}(\Omega_{T})\subset L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
. Thus, according to Theorem
2.11
and the regularity result in Corollary
4.19
, we get
the existence and the uniqueness of a weak solution
u
to Problem (
6.5
) such
that
u\in L^{\sigma}(0,T;\,\,\mathbb{L}^{s,\sigma}_{0}(\Omega))
for all
\sigma<\frac{N+2s}{N+s}
. .
Hence,
\hat{\Phi}
is well defined.

Step 2
. We show that
\hat{\Phi}(E)\subset E
.

Let
h=|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi|^{q}+f,
then
h\in L^{m}(\Omega_{T})
, using Theorem
4.16
, we get

\begin{array}[]{llll}||u||_{L^{\nu}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{0}^{s,\nu}(\Omega))}&\leq&C(\Omega,T)||h||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}\\
&\leq&C(\Omega,T)\left(||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi||_{L^{qm}(\Omega_{T})}^{q}+||f||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}\right)\\
&\leq&C(\Omega,T)\left(||\varphi||_{L^{r}(0,T;\,\,\mathbb{L}^{s,r}_{0}(\Omega))}^{q}+||f||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}\right)\\
&\leq&C(\Omega,T)\left(\ell+||f||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}\right),\end{array}

for all
\nu<\overline{m}_{s}
. Therefore, using the fact that

C(\Omega,T)\to 0
as
T\to 0
, we get the existence of
T^{*}>0
such
that for
T\leq T^{*}
fixed, there exists
\ell>0
with

C(\Omega,T)\left(\ell+||f||_{L^{m}(\Omega_{T})}\right)\leq\ell^{\frac{1}{q}}
. Hence

||u||_{L^{\nu}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{0}^{s,\nu}(\Omega))}\leq\ell^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Choosing
\nu=r
, we reach that
u\in E
. Thus,
\hat{\Phi}(E)\subset E
.

Step 3
. Compactness and continuity of
\hat{\Phi}
.

Let us begin by proving that
\hat{\Phi}
is continuous. Consider

\left\{\varphi_{n}\right\}_{n}\subset E
such that

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
strongly in
L^{1}(0,T;\,\,\mathbb{L}^{s,1+\xi}_{0}(\Omega))
.

Setting
u_{n}:=\hat{\Phi}(\varphi_{n})
and
u:=\hat{\Phi}(\varphi)
.
Then,
(u_{n},u)
satisfies

(6.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lclll}\partial_{t}u_{n}+(-\Delta)^{s}u_{n}&=&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi_{n}|^{q}+f&\text{ in }&\Omega_{T},\\
\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi|^{q}+f&\text{ in }&\Omega_{T},\\
u(x,t)=u_{n}(x,t)&=&0&\hbox{ in }&(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{n}(x,0)&=&0&\hbox{ in }&\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Setting
w_{n}=u_{n}-u
, then
w_{n}
solves the Problem

(6.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lclll}\partial_{t}w_{n}+(-\Delta)^{s}w_{n}&=&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi_{n}|^{q}-|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi|^{q}&\text{ in }&\Omega_{T},\\
w_{n}(x,t)&=&0&\hbox{ in }&(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
w_{n}(x,0)&=&0&\hbox{ in }&\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Hence, using Corollary
4.19
, it holds that

(6.8)

\bigg\||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{n}|-|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|\bigg\|_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega_{T})}\leq||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}w_{n}||_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C\bigg\||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi_{n}|^{q}-|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi|^{q}\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}{\color[rgb]{1,0,1},}

for all
\alpha<\frac{N+2s}{N+s}
. On the other hand, since
\left\{\varphi_{n}\right\}_{n}\subset E
,
then, for
r
fixed as above, we have

||\varphi_{n}||_{L^{r}(0,T;\,\,\mathbb{L}^{s,r}_{0}(\Omega))},||\varphi||_{L^{r}(0,T;\,\,\mathbb{L}^{s,r}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq C\mbox{ for all }n.

Now, by interpolation, for
\theta\in(0,1)
with
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{\theta}{1+\zeta}+\frac{1-\theta}{r}
, it follows that

\begin{array}[]{lll}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi)||_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))}&\leq&C||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi)||^{\theta}_{L^{1+\zeta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi)||^{1-\theta}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))}\\
&\leq&C||(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi)||^{\theta}_{L^{1+\zeta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))}\to 0\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.\end{array}

Thus,
|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi)|\to 0
strongly in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)))
. In particular,
|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi_{n}|\to|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi|
strongly in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
.
Therefore,
(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{n}\to(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u
strongly in
L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
.
Then,
(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{n}\to(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u
strongly in
L^{1+\zeta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
. Hence,
\hat{\Gamma}
is
continuous.

We show now the Compactness of
\hat{\Phi}
. Let

\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n}\subset E
be such that

\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\,\,\mathbb{L}^{s,1+\xi}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq C
, with
C
is a constant independent of
n
and
u_{n}=\hat{\Gamma}(\varphi_{n})
.
Since
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n}\subset E
, then for
r
fixed such that
mq<r<\overline{m}_{s}
, we have
||\varphi_{n}||_{L^{r}(0,T;\,\,\mathbb{L}^{s,r}_{0}(\Omega))}<C
for all

n
.

Thus,
h_{n}=|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi_{n}|^{q}+f
is bounded in
L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
, thanks to the compactness results in
Theorem
5.4
and Remark
5.5
, we get the existence of a subsequence denoted
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
such that
u_{n}\to u

strongly in
L^{\alpha}(0,\,T;\,\mathbb{L}^{s,\alpha}_{0}(\Omega))
for all
\alpha<\frac{N+2s}{N+s}
.

In particular,
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
L^{1}(0,\,T;\,\mathbb{L}^{1,1+\zeta}_{0}(\Omega))
. Thus, the compactness of
\hat{\Phi}
follows.

Therefore, since all the conditions of Schauder’s fixed point
Theorem are satisfied, we get the existence of a function
u\in E
such that
\hat{\Phi}(u)=u
. It is clear that
u
is a weak solution to
Problem (
6.2
).

6.2.
Case:
f=0
and
u_{0}\neq 0
.

In this subsection, we deal with the case
f=0
and
u_{0}\neq 0
. Notice that the existence result that we are going to present in this situation is not optimal and takes advantage of the existence
result proved in Theorem
6.2
, we refer to the remark below for further comments.

Consider the Problem

(6.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{q}&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\\
u(x,t)&=&0&\text{in}\quad(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)&=&u_{0}(x)&\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{array}\right.

We have the next existence result.

Theorem 6.4
.

Suppose
u_{0}\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
with
1\leq\sigma<\frac{N}{(1-3s)_{+}}
. Assume that
1\leq q<\frac{N+2s\sigma}{N+\sigma s}
. Then, there exists
T^{*}>0

such that Problem (
6.9
) has a weak solution
u
such that

(1)

if
s>\frac{1}{3}
, then
u\in L^{\theta_{1}}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{L}^{s,\theta_{1}}_{0}(\Omega))
for all

\theta_{1}<\min\left\{\frac{q(N+\sigma s)}{q(N+\sigma s)-\sigma s},\frac{\sigma(N+2s)}{N+\sigma s}\right\}
.

(2)

if
s\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{3}]
, then
u\in L^{\theta_{1}}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{L}^{s,\theta_{1}}_{0}(\Omega))

with
\theta_{1}<\min\left\{\frac{q(N+\sigma s)}{q(N+\sigma s)-\sigma(4s-1)},\frac{\sigma(N+2s)}{N+\sigma s}\right\}
.

Proof.

To simplify our presentation, we give the proof in the case
s\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{3})
. Let
\phi
be the solution to the Problem

(6.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}\phi_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}\phi&=&0&\text{ in }\quad\Omega_{T},\\
\phi(x,t)&=&0&\hbox{ in }\quad(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
\phi(x,0)&=&u_{0}(x)&\hbox{ in}\quad\,\,\Omega.\\
\end{array}\right.

According to Theorem
4.6
and Corollary
4.8
, we have

\phi\in L^{p}(0,T;\,\,\mathbb{L}_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega))
for all

p\leq\widehat{p_{s}}:=\frac{\sigma(N+2s)}{N+\sigma s}
.

Let
\widetilde{u}=u-\phi
, then
\widetilde{u}
solves the
Problem

(6.11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcllll}{\widetilde{u}}_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}\widetilde{u}&=&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}{\widetilde{u}}+(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi|^{q}&\text{ in }&\Omega_{T},\\
\widetilde{u}(x,t)&=&0&\hbox{ in }&(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
\widetilde{u}(x,0)&=&0&\hbox{ in}&\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Thus, to prove the existence of solutions to Problem
(
6.9
), it is enough to show that the problem
(
6.11
) has a positive solution

\widetilde{u}
.

Let
1<r<\frac{{\sigma}(N+2s)}{N+{\sigma}s}
be fixed, close to

\frac{{\sigma}(N+2s)}{N+{\sigma}s}
, we define the set

F:=\Big\{\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{s,1+\eta}_{0}(\Omega))\,;\,\varphi\in L^{r}(0,\,T;\,\mathbb{L}^{s,r}_{0}(\Omega))\mbox{ and }||\varphi||_{L^{r}(0,\,T;\,\mathbb{L}^{s,r}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq\Lambda\Big\},

with
0<\eta<q-1
. Consider the operator

\mathcal{L}:F\longrightarrow L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{s,1+\eta}_{0}(\Omega))\ \

\varphi\longmapsto\mathcal{L}(\varphi)=\widetilde{u},

with
\widetilde{u}
is the unique solution of the Problem

(6.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcllll}{\widetilde{u}}_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}\widetilde{u}&=&|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}{\varphi}+(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi|^{q}&\text{ in }&{\Omega_{T}},\\
\widetilde{u}(x,t)&=&0&\hbox{ in }&(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
\widetilde{u}(x,0)&=&0&\hbox{ in }&\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Notice that

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}{\varphi}+(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi|^{q}\leq C(q)(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}{\varphi}|^{q}+|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi|^{q}).

Define
f=C(q)|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}{\phi}|^{q}
, then
f\in L^{m}(\Omega_{T})
for all

m<\frac{{\sigma}(N+2s)}{q(N+{\sigma}s)}
.

Going back to Theorem
6.2
, using the fact that
\frac{{\sigma}(N+2s)}{q(N+{\sigma}s)}<\frac{(N+2s)}{s}
, then,
according to Theorem
6.2
, we get the existence of a positive solution
\tilde{u}
to Problem (
6.12
) such that

\tilde{u}\in L^{\gamma}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{L}^{s,\gamma}_{0}(\Omega))
for all
1<\gamma<\frac{q(N+\sigma s)}{q(N+\sigma s)-\sigma s}
. Since
u=\widetilde{u}+\phi
, then
u\in L^{\theta_{1}}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{L}^{s,\theta_{1}}_{0}(\Omega))

with
\theta_{1}<\min\left\{\frac{q(N+\sigma s)}{q(N+\sigma s)-\sigma s},\frac{\sigma(N+2s)}{N+\sigma s}\right\}
. It is clear that
u
solves the Problem (
6.9
). Hence, we conclude.
∎

7.
Extensions and further results

In this section, we give some extensions of our
regularity-existence results to other nonlinear problem with
different structures.

\bullet
In the case where
s>\frac{1}{2}
, the above
regularity result can be extended to the case where the fractional
Laplacian is perturbed by a drift term. More precisely, we define
the operator

L(w)=(-\Delta)^{s}w+B(x,t).\nabla w,

where
B
is a vector fields
satisfies the condition

(7.1)

\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}|B(x,t)|^{\theta}dx\bigg)^{\rho}{\theta}dt<\infty,

with
1\leq\theta,\rho\leq\infty
, verified

\frac{N}{2s}\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\rho}<1-\frac{1}{2s}
, see
example 3, page 335 in
[
37
]
. We refer also to Definition 1
in
[
37
]
for more general condition related to Kato class of
functions.

Notice that if
|B|\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega_{T})
with
\sigma>\frac{N+2s}{2s-1}
,
then the condition (
7.1
) holds.

Denote by
\widetilde{P}_{\Omega}
, the Dirichlet heart kernel
associate to
L
, then according to
[
16
]
,
[
22
]
and

[
37
]
, we obtain that
\widetilde{P}_{\Omega}\simeq P_{\Omega}
and
|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{P}_{\Omega}(x,y,t)|\leq C\frac{P_{\Omega}(x,y,t)}{\delta(x)\wedge t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}
. Thus, if
\widetilde{w}
is the solution to
the Problem

(7.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcllll}\widetilde{w}_{t}+L(\widetilde{w})&=h&\text{ in }&\Omega_{T},\\
\widetilde{w}(x,t)&=&0&\hbox{ in }&(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
\widetilde{w}(x,0)&=&w_{0}(x)&\hbox{ in }&\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Then, the regularity of
\widetilde{w}
is the same as the
regularity of
w
, the solution to Problem
(FHE)
obtained in
Section
4
.

\bullet
Related to the Problem
{(KPZ_{f})}
, we can replace the term

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{q}
by
|\nabla^{s}u|^{q}
defined in
(
2.2
). Namely, the same existence result holds for the
Problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&|\nabla^{s}u|^{q}+f&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\\
u(x,t)&=&0&\text{in}\quad(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)&=&u_{0}(x)&\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{array}\right.

under the same conditions on the parameters.

\bullet
We can also use the same approach to treat the case
where the fractional gradient term appears as an absorption
term. Namely, we can consider the Problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u+|(-\Delta)^{s}u|^{q}&=&f&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\\
u(x,t)&=&0&\text{in}\quad(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)&=&u_{0}(x)&\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{array}\right.

The
existence is proved for small time values.

Notice that, as observed in
[
6
]
, in the case of the
gradient appears as an absorption term, it is possible to show the
existence for
L^{1}
data if
q<2s
. The question of existence of a solution for
L^{1}
datum is still open
for
q>2s
including for the local case.

\bullet
The question of uniqueness seems to be more difficult.
In the case where the fractional gradient is substituted by the
local gradient and
s>\frac{1}{2}
, the uniqueness of the good solution is proved in

[
7
]
. The situation is more delicate in our case, since the
comparison principle for the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcllll}v_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}v&=&C|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v|&\text{ in }&{\Omega_{T}},\\
v(x,t)&=&0&\hbox{ in }&(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
v(x,0)&=&0&\hbox{ in }&\Omega,\end{array}\right.

seems to be not true.

Appendix

In this section, we prove some technical tools that we have used
previously throughout the paper.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Recall that

G_{\lambda}(x,t)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{g(y)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+\lambda}}dy.

To get the regularity of
G_{\lambda}
, we will use a duality
argument. Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
,
then for
p\geq 1
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}||G_{\lambda}(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}&=&\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\{||\phi||_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq 1\}}{\int_{\Omega}}G_{\lambda}(x,t)\phi(x)dx\leq\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\{||\phi||_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq 1\}}\int_{\Omega}|\phi(x)|\int_{\Omega}|g(y)|{H}(x-y,t)dydx,\\
\end{array}

where
H(x,t):=\dfrac{1}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+\rvert x\rvert)^{N+\lambda}}
. Using Young’s inequality, it holds that

(8.1)

\begin{array}[]{lll}||G_{\lambda}(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}&\leq&\sup\limits_{\{||\phi||_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\leq 1}\}}||\phi||_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}||g||_{L^{m}(\Omega)}||H(.,t)||_{L^{a}(\Omega)},\end{array}

where
\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\dfrac{1}{m}=2
.
Then by a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}H^{a}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\dfrac{1}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+\rvert x\rvert)^{(N+\lambda)a}}dx=t^{-a\frac{N+\lambda}{2s}}\int\limits_{\Omega}\dfrac{1}{(1+\frac{|x|}{t^{\frac{1}{2s}}})^{(N+\lambda)a}}dx.

Setting
z=\frac{x}{t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}
, it holds that

\int\limits_{\Omega}H^{a}(x,t)dx\leq t^{\frac{N}{2s}-a\frac{N+\lambda}{2s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{1}{(1+|z|)^{(N+\lambda)a}}dz.

The last integral is finite if and only if
(N+\lambda)a>N
. Thus
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{m}<\frac{\lambda}{N}
.

Now, if
\lambda\geq 0
, then
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{m}<\frac{\lambda}{N}

for all
p>m
. Thus from (
8.1
), we get

||G_{\lambda}(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{\frac{-\lambda}{2s}-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p})}||g||_{L^{m}(\Omega)}.

Suppose that
\lambda<0
, then

G_{\lambda}(x,t)\leq C(t^{-\frac{\lambda}{2s}}+1)\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{g(y)}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N}}dy.

Now, repeating the same duality argument as above for the last
term, it holds that for all
p>m
, we have

(8.2)

||G_{\lambda}(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(t^{-\frac{\lambda}{2s}}+1)t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p)}}||g||_{L^{m}(\Omega)}.

Notice that, if
-N<\lambda<0
, we can improve estimate (
8.2
).
Namely for
m_{0}
fixed such that
1\leq m_{0}<\min\{m,-\frac{N}{\lambda}\}
, then
g\in L^{m_{0}}(\Omega)
and

-\frac{1}{m_{0}}<\frac{\lambda}{N}
. Thus, fixed
p
such that

p>\frac{m_{0}N}{N+m_{0}\lambda}
, then
p>m_{0}
and

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{m_{0}}<\frac{\lambda}{N}
. Repeating the same
computations as in the case
\lambda>0
, with
m_{0}
instead of

m
, we obtain that

||G_{\lambda}(.,t)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{\frac{-\lambda}{2s}-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{m_{0}}-\frac{1}{p})}||g||_{L^{m_{0}}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega)t^{\frac{-\lambda}{2s}-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{m_{0}}-\frac{1}{p})}||g||_{L^{m}(\Omega)}.

Proof of Theorem
4.9
.

Recall that

G_{\alpha,\lambda}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{g(y,\tau)(t-\tau)^{\alpha}}{((t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+\lambda}}dyd\tau.

We give a detailed proof in the case where
p\leq\frac{N+2s}{2s(\alpha+1)-\lambda}
. The other cases follow in the same way.

Recall that
\lambda<2s(\alpha+1)
. We will use a duality argument.
Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{T})
, then

\begin{array}[]{lll}||G_{\alpha,\lambda}||_{L^{\ell}(\Omega_{T})}&=&\displaystyle\sup\limits_{{||\phi||_{L^{\ell^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})}\leq 1}}{\iint_{\Omega_{T}}}G_{\alpha,\lambda}(x,t)\phi(x,t)dxdt,\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\sup\limits_{{||\phi||_{L^{\ell^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})}\leq 1}}\iint_{\Omega_{T}}|\phi(x,t)|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|g(y,\tau)|{H}(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau dxdt,\\
\end{array}

where

{H}(x,t):=\dfrac{t^{\alpha}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2s}}+\rvert x\rvert)^{N+\lambda}}.

Using Young’s inequality, it holds that

(8.3)

\begin{array}[]{lll}||G_{\alpha,\lambda}||_{L^{\ell}(\Omega_{T})}&\leq&\sup\limits_{{||\phi||_{L^{\ell^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})\leq 1}}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}||\phi(.,t)||_{L^{\ell^{\prime}}(\Omega)}||g(.,\tau)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}||H(.,t-\tau)||_{L^{a}(\Omega)}d\tau dt,\end{array}

where
\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{1}{\ell^{\prime}}=2
.

Notice that

||H(.,t)||_{L^{a}(\Omega)}\leq t^{\alpha+\frac{N}{2sa}-\frac{N+\lambda}{2s}}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{dz}{(1+|z|)^{(N+\lambda)a}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{a}}.

The last integral is finite if
a(N+\lambda)>N
which implies that

(8.4)

\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{1}{p}<\frac{\lambda}{N}.

Hence
assuming the above hypothesis, we obtain that

(8.5)

||H(.,t)||_{L^{a}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{\alpha+\frac{N}{2sa}-\frac{N+\lambda}{2s}}.

If
\lambda\geq 0
, then (
8.4
) holds for all
\ell>p
. Let

\gamma=\alpha+\frac{N}{2sa}-\frac{N+\lambda}{2s}
. Since
2s\alpha<N+\lambda
, then

\gamma<0
. Going back to (
8.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle||G_{\alpha,\lambda}||_{L^{\ell}(\Omega_{T})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{{||\phi||_{L^{\ell^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})\leq 1}}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}||\phi(.,t)||_{L^{\ell^{\prime}}(\Omega)}||g(.,\tau)||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}|t-\tau|^{\gamma}d\tau dt.

Using again Young’s inequality in the time, we reach that

(8.6)

\begin{array}[]{lll}||G_{\alpha,\lambda}||_{L^{\ell}(\Omega_{T})}&\leq&C\sup\limits_{{||\phi||_{L^{\ell^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})\leq 1}}}||g||_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}||\phi||_{L^{\ell^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}t^{\bar{a}\gamma}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{a}}},\\
\end{array}

with
\dfrac{1}{\bar{a}}+\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{1}{\ell^{\prime}}=2
. It is clear that

\bar{a}=a=\frac{p^{\prime}\ell}{p^{\prime}+\ell}
and
\gamma=\frac{2s\alpha-\lambda}{2s}-\frac{N}{2s}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\ell})
.

Now by (
8.6
), it follows that the last integral is finite if

a|\gamma|<1
. Taking into consideration the values of
a
and
\gamma
,
the above condition holds if

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\ell}<\frac{2s(\alpha+1)-\lambda}{N+2s}
. Hence for
all
\ell
such that

p<\ell<\frac{p(N+2s)}{(N+2s-p(2s(\alpha+1)-\lambda))_{+}}
, we have

(8.7)

||G_{\alpha,\lambda}||_{L^{\ell}(\Omega_{T})}\leq CT^{\gamma+\frac{1}{a}}||g||_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}=CT^{\frac{2s(\alpha+1)-\lambda}{2s}-\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\ell})}||g||_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}.

Now, if
\lambda<0
, then as in the proof of Theorem

4.1
, since
\Omega
is a bounded domain,
we get

G_{\alpha,\lambda}(x,t)\leq C(T^{-\frac{\lambda}{2s}}+1)\int_{0}^{t}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{g(y,\tau)(t-\tau)^{\alpha}}{((t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N}}dyd\tau.

Following the same argument as in the case
\lambda\geq 0
,
we deduce that for all
\ell
such that

p<\ell<\frac{p(N+2s)}{(N+2s-p(2s(\alpha+1)))_{+}}
, we have

(8.8)

||G_{\alpha,\lambda}||_{L^{\ell}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(T^{-\frac{\lambda}{2s}}+1)T^{\alpha+1-\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\ell})}||g||_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}.

In the case where
\lambda<0
, we can improve estimate (
8.8
)
under additional hypothesis. More precisely, assume that
\lambda<0

and
N(\alpha+1)>|\lambda|
. We fix
p_{0}
such that
1\leq p_{0}<\min\{p,-\frac{N}{\lambda}\}
, then
g\in L^{p_{0}}(\Omega_{T})
. Since

-\frac{1}{p_{0}}<\frac{\lambda}{N}
, then for

\ell>\frac{p_{0}N}{N+p_{0}\lambda}
, the condition (
8.4
) is verified
for the pair
(p_{0},\ell)
.

Notice that

\frac{p_{0}N}{N+p_{0}\lambda}<\frac{p_{0}(N+2s)}{(N+2s-p_{0}(2s(\alpha+1)-\lambda))_{+}}
.
Thus, for all
\ell
such that

\frac{p_{0}N}{N+p_{0}\lambda}<\ell<\frac{p_{0}(N+2s)}{(N+2s-p_{0}(2s(\alpha+1)-\lambda))_{+}}
,
we have

||G_{\alpha,\lambda}||_{L^{\ell}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega)T^{\frac{2s(\alpha+1)-\lambda}{2s}-\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{\ell})}||g||_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega)T^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{2s(\alpha+1)-\lambda}{2s}-\frac{N+2s}{2s}(\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{\ell})}||g||_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}.

If
N(\alpha+1)\leq|\lambda|
, then
-1<\alpha<0
. Choosing
a>0
such that

a(N+\lambda)<N
, then
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\ell}<-\frac{\lambda}{N}
.
Hence

\int\limits_{\Omega}H^{a}(x,t)dx\leq t^{\alpha a}\int\limits_{\Omega}\dfrac{1}{|x|^{(N+\lambda)a}}dx\leq C(\Omega)t^{\alpha a}.

Thus the integral in time converges if

\alpha>\-\frac{1}{a}
. Hence
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\ell}<1+\alpha
. Then
for
\ell
fixed such that
\ell<\min\{\frac{pN}{N+\lambda p},\frac{p}{(1-p(\alpha+1))_{+}}\}
, we have

||G_{\alpha,\lambda}||_{L^{\ell}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C(\Omega)T^{(\alpha+1)-(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\ell})}||g||_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}.

Remark 8.1
.

If
\lambda>2s(\alpha+1)
, then
G_{\alpha,\lambda}
is not well defined in the
sense that the integral
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{g(y,\tau)(t-\tau)^{\alpha}}{((t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+\lambda}}dyd\tau
can diverge including for regular data. To see that, we
consider the case where
g=C
, then choosing
x\in\Omega
such that

B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle G_{\alpha,\lambda}(x,t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{r}(x)}\frac{(t-\tau)^{\alpha}}{((t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+\lambda}}dyd\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{r}(x)}\frac{\sigma^{\alpha}}{(\sigma^{\frac{1}{2s}}+|x-y|)^{N+\lambda}}dyd\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\alpha-\frac{N+\lambda}{2s}}\int_{B_{r}(x)}\frac{dy}{(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\sigma^{\frac{1}{2s}}})^{N+\lambda}}dyd\sigma.

Setting
z=\frac{(x-y)}{\sigma^{\frac{1}{2s}}}
, then

\displaystyle G_{\alpha,\lambda}(x,t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\alpha-\frac{N+\lambda}{2s}+\frac{N}{2s}}\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\sigma^{\frac{1}{2s}}}}(0)}\frac{dz}{(1+|z|)^{N+\lambda}}dzd\sigma.

Now, for
t<<1
, we have
\sigma\leq t<<1
, then

\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\sigma^{\frac{1}{2s}}}}(0)}\frac{dz}{(1+|z|)^{N+\lambda}}dz\geq\int_{B_{r}(0)}\frac{dz}{(1+|z|)^{N+\lambda}}dz=C.

Thus

G_{\alpha,\lambda}(x,t)\geq C\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\alpha-\frac{N+\lambda}{2s}+\frac{N}{2s}}d\sigma=\infty.

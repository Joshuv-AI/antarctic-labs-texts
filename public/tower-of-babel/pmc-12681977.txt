Elucidating VOC dynamics and molecular mechanisms in nonanal-treated postharvest tomatoes infected with Botrytis cinerea
Authors: Hongdou Gao, Jinnian Li, Chengxin Wu, Huilan Ye, Qi Jia, Yang Xu, Youliang Jin, Shuang Liu, Miaohong Ruan, Weiqun Huang, Jie Pang, Fenglin Zhong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, an 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal treatment decreased the incidence rate of gray mold on tomato and enhanced the defense capacity by altering the activities of the main defense-related enzymes, including NADPH-cytochrome c reductase, aminopyrine-n-demethylase, aniline-4-hydroxylase, and erythromycin N-demethylase. Defense mechanisms are associated with cell degradation. PUFAs are closely related to the maintenance of cell integrity. The enzymes related to PUFAs showed weak activities until 36 h after the 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal treatment, afterwards, their activities increased. The 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal treatment decreased the energy wasted by decreasing NAD+, NADH, and ATP levels. These changes may respond to nonanal application, further regulating the defense capacity of tomato. The nonanal treatment also decreased the contents of key substances required for the synthesis of PUFAs [volatile aldehydes and alcohols (VAAs)]. The most important substances for disease resistance (hexanal, 2-hexanal, and citral) also significantly decreased after the 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal treatment. The transcriptome data revealed FAD2, OPRs, Alpha-DOX1, and KCS related to PUFAs, Of these, 14 were down-regulated and 5 were up-regulated after the 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal treatment compared with the control. and the genes in the plant–pathogen interaction pathway were up-regulated in the control group. This indicated that the nonanal treatment increased the postharvest tomato plant's resistance capability and helped maintain cell integrity, which meant that less genes and substances were required to fight the Botrytis cinerea infection.Keywords: Nonanal, Botrytis cinerea, Tomato, PUFAs, Metabolomics and transcriptome

Introduction

Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum L.) is an important vegetable crop in China, and its planting area and yield are among the top in the world. According to the China Agricultural Statistical Yearbook, the planting area and output of tomatoes have increased annually, and tomato has become an important income source for farmers (Zhang & Wang, 2020). Processed tomato products, such as paste, sauce, and juice, are strongly competitive on the international market (Li & Chen, 2019), and their export volumes increase annually, making them important contributors to China's agricultural exports. However, tomato is frequently infected by various kinds of pathogens, which greatly affects the yield and quality.

Globally, gray mold causes economic losses of up to $100 billion per year (Chen et al., 2022). Botrytis cinerea, as an airborne plant pathogen, with a necrotizing lifestyle, invades plants, causing gray mold disease (Williamson et al., 2007). Botrytis cinerea is characterized by high genetic variability, a large reproductive capacity, short life cycle, and high resistance to fungicides, making it a main cause of heavy tomato losses (Chaouachi et al., 2021). Current control strategies for tomato gray mold have significant limitations. The progress in breeding disease-resistant varieties is slow, and chemical control methods face the problem of pathogen resistance. Environmental factors and cultivation measures also affect control measures (Williamson et al., 2007).

The plant immune system serves as a crucial defense mechanism against pathogen invasion, involving complex molecular mechanisms and signaling pathways. In recent years, VOCs in plants have gained significant attention as potential immune regulators (Ma et al., 2022). Among these, Nonanal is produced by fatty acid peroxidation (Schulz & Dickschat, 2007), and, as one of the most important VOCs, it is present in many species, such as rapeseed, soybean and Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Inouye et al., 2001; Yayli et al., 2006). Nonanal has demonstrated remarkable potential in directly inhibiting pathogen growth and inducing plant immune responses (Zhang, Zeng, et al., 2017). Nonanal can significantly inhibit hyphal growth and destroy cell membrane integrity, leading to the leakage of cellular components (Zhang, Sun, et al., 2017a), It can also be used as a plant immune inducer to activate systemic resistance. Although the broad-spectrum antifungal activity of nonanal (including against Alternaria, Curvularia, Candida albicans, Staphylococcus aureus, Fusarium oxysporum and others) has been confirmed (Inouye et al., 2001; Sharma et al., 2014; Yayli et al., 2006). The molecular mechanisms behind its inhibition of pathogens and its role in plant immunity have not been fully elucidated.

More than 400 kinds of VOCs were found in different tomato tissues by Martina et al. (2021). They protect plants from abiotic stresses, herbivores, and pathogens/competing plants (Kaiser, 2006; Silva et al., 2018). VOCs act as signaling molecules against bacterial infections and herbivorous pests (Hammerbacher et al., 2019; Lopez et al., 2012). They can directly disrupt the membrane integrity and permeability of pathogenic bacteria, leading to potential reduction, ion leakage, and disrupted ATP synthesis, which leads confounding changes in pH and inorganic ions, as well as accelerating pathogenic microbial death (Hammerbacher et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2018). The roles and mechanisms of PUFA-associated VOCs in disease resistance are not known. Therefore, the molecular mechanisms of such VOCs in response to Botrytis cinerea may form a theoretical basis for the genetic improvement of disease resistance in tomato. This study explored the effects of nonanal treatment on VOCs, cell membrane integrity and PUFA biosynthesis in postharvest tomato infected with Botrytis cinerea.

Materials and methods

Sample processing

‘Hongyan 601’ tomatoes that showed consistent maturity, no mechanical damage, and no pests were collected. The cell concentration of the Botrytis cinerea suspension was 109 CFU·L−1. The control group received 5 μL 109 CFU·L−1 Botrytis cinerea + 10 μL sterile water. The treatment group received 5 μL 109 CFU·L−1 Botrytis cinerea + 10 μL 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal. The tomato samples treated with each suspension were placed in independent sealed boxes. The sampling points were 0, 12, 24, 36, 48, and 72 h after treatment. The sample were selected at the site of onset 3–5 mm. The applied nonanal concentration was screened in a pre-experiment. 12 tomato fruits per group were elected and analyzed. Each experiment was performed in triplicate and had three biological replicates.

Botrytis cinerea inhibition experiment

Botrytis cinerea (B05.10) was incubated at 22 °C for 72 h on PDA plates. Then, 1 mm3 of Botrytis cinerea mycelia was picked and transferred to the center of each PDA plate (90 mm diameter), containing 0, 0.017, 0.020, or 0.027 mol·L−1 nonanal (control medium = sterile water: glycerin = 1: 1, nonanal was added control medium to reach 1.2 mL, then the mixed was added PDA to reach 50 mL). Plates were then incubated at 22 °C. Images were taken every 24 h until the entire PDA plate was covered with Botrytis cinerea. The experiment was repeated three times.

Measurement of tomato physiological properties

NAD+, NADH, ATP, and Cytochrome b5 contents were determined using the appropriate kits from Suzhou Comin Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The activities of alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH), lipoxygenase (LOX), NADPH-cytochrome c reductase (NCR), aminopyrine-N-demethylase (AND), aniline-4-hydroxylase (AH), erythromycin N-demethylase (ERND), and acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC) were also determined using the appropriate kits from Suzhou Comin Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

Transcriptomic analysis

Transcriptomic analyses for control (24 h) and 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal (24 h) were performed by BioMarker Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). The total RNA was extracted using an RNAprep Pure Plant Kit (Tiangen, Beijing, China). Sequencing libraries were constructed using Hieff NGS Ultima Dual-mode mRNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina [Yeasen Biotechnology (Shanghai) Co., Ltd.]. RNA concentration and purity were tested using a NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Wilmington, DE, USA). RNA integrity was evaluated using the RNA Nano 6000 Assay Kit on an Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). The raw reads were processed using the bioinformatic pipeline tool BMKCloud (www.biocloud.net) online platform. Tomato reference genome sequences were used to map the filtered reads (https://solgenomics.net/organism/Solanum_lycopersicum/genome). The quantification of gene expression levels was expressed as FPKM. Genes with an adjusted P-value <0.01 and Fold Change ≥2 as determined by DESeq2 were assigned as differentially expressed. The KEGG and GO enrichment analyses for all DEGs were calculated based on the hypergeometric test (Gao et al., 2023).

Real-time PCR analysis

Total RNAs of different tomato samples were extracted using an FastPure® Plant Total RNA Isolation Kit (Vazyme, China). The cDNAs were constructed using StarScript III All-in-one RT Mix with gDNA Remover (GenStar, China). The gene-specific PCR primers were designed using Primer 5.0, and those used in the RT-qPCR analyses are listed in Supplemental File 1. The reaction system and procedure were as previously reported (Gao et al., 2024).

Metabolome analysis

A metabolome analysis was conducted by BioMarker Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Freeze-dried control (24 h) and 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal (24 h) tomato samples were extracted with 0.16 mL methanol and 0.08 mL dH2O. Afterwards, 10 μL of adonitol, as internal standard, was added. Samples were mixed for 30 s, homogenized at 45 Hz for 4 min using a ball mill, and treated with ultrasound for 5 min. They were then centrifuged for 15 min at 13,000 ×g and 4 °C. Then, 0.18 mL of each supernatant was placed into a 2 mL glass vial. A 25 μL sample was taken, and samples were pooled to form the QC sample. After drying in a vacuum concentrator, 30 μL Methoxy amination hydrochloride was added, and samples were maintained at 80 °C for 30 min. Afterwards, 50 μL of the BSTFA reagent was added to each sample aliquot and allowed to sit for 1.5 h at 70 °C. Then, the samples were maintained at 25 °C. For the QC sample, 5 μL of a standard mixture of fatty acid methyl esters was added. Samples were analyzed using an Agilent 7890 gas chromatography system coupled with a Pegasus HT time-of-flight mass spectrometer. A DB-5MS capillary column, coated with 5 % diphenyl cross-linked with 95 % dimethylpolysiloxane, was used (30 m × 250 μm inner diameter, 0.25 μm film thickness, J&W Scientific, Folsom, CA, USA), and the gas flow rate was 166 μL s−1. Helium was the carrier gas (50 μL s−1). The temperature profile was as follows: 50 °C for 1 min, followed by an increase to 310 °C at 0.16 °C s−1, and then held for 8 min. The temperatures were as follows: 280 °C (injection), 270 °C (transfer line), and 220 °C (ion source). The energy was −70 eV.

Statistical analyses

Heatmaps and figures were constructed using Excel and Origin 2022. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for statistical analysis, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation.

Results

The defense capacity of Botrytis cinerea

The defense capacity of nonanal against Botrytis cinerea is shown in Fig. 1. The different concentrations of nonanal had different defense capacities against Botrytis cinerea (Fig. 1A). At 0.017 mol·L−1, nonanal had a significant defense capacity against Botrytis cinerea for 72 h. At 0.020 and 0.027 mol·L−1, nonanal inhibited the growth of Botrytis cinerea for 72 h. Disease spot diameters on tomato increased from 48 to 84 h (Fig. 1B). However, the treated group exhibited a slower increase in disease spot diameter size compared with the control group. The disease spot diameter for the control was 1.96 times greater than that of the treated group by 84 h. The disease incidence rate on tomato was higher in the control group than the treated group from 48 to 84 h (Fig. 1C). The control group reached 100 % incidence at 72 h, whereas the treated group did not reach this level until 84 h. Tomatoes in the control group began to develop gray mold at 48 h (Fig. 1D), whereas the 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal-treated group did not display significant signs of infection at 48 h. From 48 h to 84 h, members of the control group had more obvious infections compared with the treated group.

The physiology related to PUFA biosynthesis

The LOX activity level was not obviously different between control and treated groups, and showed a steady trend at approximately 30 U g−1 for 72 h (Fig. 2A). The ADH activity was higher in the control than in the treated group from 0 to 36 h. At 48 and 72 h, the ADH activity in the treated group was 2.54 and 1.87 times that of the control (Fig. 2B). The ERND activity peak of was 0.0151 nmol s−1 kg−1 occurred at 24 h in the control group, whereas the ERND activity peak of 0.0095 nmol s−1 kg−1 occurred at 36 h in the treated group (Fig. 2C). The ACC activity was higher in the control than in the treated group from 0 to 36 h, but it was lower in the control than the treated group from 48 to 72 h (Fig. 2D). The AH activity was higher in the treated than in the control group from 0 to 36 h. Then, the AH activity peaked at 48 h, with values of 0.1318 and 0.1027 nmol s−1 g−1 in the control and treated groups, respectively (Fig. 2E). The AND activity also showed an increasing trend, peaking at 36 h and then decreased (Fig. 2F). The NCR activity increased from 0 to 24 h and then decreased from 36 to 72 h (Fig. 2G). Additionally, the NCR activity was lower in the control group than in the treated group after 24 h. ATP content decreased in the control group, whereas in the treated group it first declined and then increased from 0 to 72 h (Fig. 2H). The NADH content increased from 0 to 12 h and then decreased (Fig. 2I). The NADH content was greater in the control group than the treated group. The NAD+ content increased, peaking at 24 h in the control group and at 48 h in the treated group, and then decreased (Fig. 2J). The cytochrome b5 content increased, peaking at 48 h with a value of 0.15 nmol·L−1, in the control group and at 36 h with a value of 0.26 nmol·L−1, in the treated group, before decreasing (Fig. 2K).

The transcriptomic analysis

RNA-seq was performed to investigate key DEGs that responded to nonanal treatment in tomato infected with Botrytis cinerea. In the 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal treatment (24 h) vs. control (24 h) comparison, 505 DEGs were identified, including 255 down-regulated DEGs and 250 up-regulated DEGs (Fig. 3A). The DEGs are more directly represented using a heatmap (Fig. 3B). Plant hormone signal transduction (ko04075) occupied 10.31 %, Plant–pathogen interaction (ko04626) occupied 9.79 % and most of the DEGs mapped to the ‘metabolism’ class (Fig. 3C). The pathways related to PUFA biosynthesis accounted for 15.99 %. The Clusters of Orthologous Groups (COG) functional classification of consensus sequence results revealed 19 functional classes commonly represented in the transcriptomic analysis (Fig. 3D). DEGs were significantly enriched in ‘secondary metabolites biosynthesis, transport and catabolism’, ‘general function prediction only’, and ‘defense mechanisms’, as well as ‘posttranslational modification, protein turnover, chaperones’, and ‘carbohydrate transport and metabolism’ (Fig. 3D). The Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis annotated 390 DEGs, which were classified into ‘biological process’, ‘cellular component’, and ‘molecular function’ categories. The most-enriched GO terms were ‘catalytic activity’, ‘cellular anatomical entity’, ‘binding’, and ‘cellular process’ (Fig. 3E). There were 19 DEGs related to biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids (Fig. 3F).

The metabolic analysis

The metabolic analysis revealed differences in VOCs between 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal-treated (24 h) and control (24 h) tomato groups infected with Botrytis cinerea. A total of 259 VOCs were detected in the nonanal-treated (24 h) and control (24 h) groups, and 32 differential expression (DE) VOCs between control and nonanal-treated infected Botrytis cinerea had |log2FC| > 1 and VIP > 1. Among them, 27 DE VOCs were decreased and 5 DE VOCs were increased in the nonanal-treated group (Fig. 8A–C). The PCA revealed that nonanal-treated and control groups differed (Fig. 8D). The former was positively correlated with PC1, whereas the latter was negatively correlated with PC1. There were 20 significant VOC metabolites, including 9 aromatics, 2 esters, and 4 ketones (Fig. 8E). The aromatics, nitrogen compounds, and esters had the highest relative contents, occupying 33 %, 16 %, and 15 %, respectively, in the control group and 35 %, 15 %, and 16 %, respectively, in the treated group (Fig. 8F). The other VOCs showed similar trends in the control and treated groups. There were no significant differences between control and treated groups in the peak areas of aldehydes and alcohols. The aldehydes contained eight different classes, heptanal, capraldehyde, hexanal, nonanal, octanal, undecanal, dodecanal, and hexadecenal. Additionally, the aldehyde classes with the largest numbers of members were capraldehyde and hexanal in both the control (representing 47.8 % and 34.8 % of the total, respectively) and nonanal (representing 39.6 % and 39.3 % of the total, respectively) groups (Fig. 8G). Among the aldehydes, hexanal (22 %) and trans-4,5-epoxy-(E)-2-decenal (21 %) had the greatest accumulations in the control, whereas hexanal (22 %) and nonanal (19 %) had the greatest accumulations in the nonanal group (Fig. 8H). In the control vs. nonanal-treated group comparison, the aldehyde showing the greatest difference was nonanal, which had a 5.28-fold higher peak area in the treated group than in the control. The others had higher levels in the control than in the treated group. The alcohols contained seven different classes, butanol, decyl alcohol, nonanol, dodecanol, pentadecyl alcohol, pentanol, and octanol. Pentanol and nonanol were the classes with the most members in both the control (representing 43 % and 45 % of the total, respectively) and nonanal (representing 44.8 % and 43.2 % of the total, respectively) groups (Fig. 8I). Alcohol levels were higher in the treated group than in the control (Fig. 8J). Among them, [2H4]-n-nonyl alcohol and 1H-imidazole-2-methanol, 1-methyl- were the most accumulated alcohols in the control and nonanal groups.

Discussion

Nonanal has gained considerable interest as a VOC for use in defending against plant diseases and insect pests due to its bio-compatibility, accessibility, and practicality (Martínez et al., 2024; Zhang, Sun, et al., 2017b). Seeds of nonanal-exposed plants contain more phenolic compounds, have increased antioxidant activity, and cause resistance-gene priming (Martínez et al., 2024). Furthermore, nonanal enhances the destruction of cell wall integrity and the accumulation of ROS in Aspergillus flavus (Zhang et al., 2023), and it could kill pathogenic bacteria. Nonanal plays a key role in plant-to-plant interactions by inducing the up-regulation of HDA2 and TPL (defense-related genes) and by down-regulating O-methyltransferase in barley (Alessandro et al., 2022). In this study, the capacity of nonanal to cause changes in the expression of genes related to the biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids was shown. The changes in aldehyde and alcohol contents were associated with the enhanced resistance capacity of tomato against Botrytis cinerea.

During the Botrytis cinerea infection process, the nonanal treatment decreased the associated disease incidence rate on postharvest tomato (Fig. 1BCD), which was similar to the results of Zhang, Sun, et al. (2017b). The gray mold of tomato is closely related to defense mechanisms and cell degradation. CYP enzymes (including ERND, AH, AND, and NCR) play important roles in plant defense through their involvement in the production of different fatty acid conjugates (Xu et al., 2015). Compared with in the 0.054 mol·L−1 nonanal-treated group, the activities of ERND and AND were higher in the control up to 36 h after treatment, and then, their activities were lower in the control from 36 to 72 h (Fig. 2 CF). This indicated that nonanal had a better capacity to maintain the production of defense-related substances from 0 to 36 h. After 36 h, nonanal still induced defense enzymes in postharvest tomato. The content of cytochrome b5 was higher in the nonanal-treated group (Fig. 2K), which also indicated that nonanal can increase the resistance of tomato to Botrytis cinerea.

Fatty acid is an important component of the cell membrane. It plays an important role in energy metabolism, signal transduction, and aroma formation (Dai et al., 2021). Fatty acid metabolism involves biosynthesis, extension, and degradation, which are mainly regulated by ADH and ACC (Li et al., 2020). The nonanal treatment decreased the ADH and ACC activities before 36 h (Fig. 2 BD). Energy homeostasis plays an important role in delaying postharvest disease. Due to the vital role of ATP in fatty acid synthesis and membrane repair, energy deficiency has been associated with membrane damage (Wang et al., 2018). The treated group required less energy to maintain postharvest tomato resistance against Botrytis cinerea, as shown by decreases in ATP, NADH, and NAD+(Fig. 2HIJ). This indicated that nonanal enhanced the production of PUFAs and decreased the energy required to maintain cell integrity. This is consistent with the energy changes reported by Ding et al. (2025). The results indicate that nonanal enhanced cell wall integrity by increasing PUFAs and CYP-related enzymes and substances. Thus, it reduced cell wall degradation to reduce the infective capability of gray mold.

Transcription factors play key roles in plant defense against Botrytis cinerea, and many transcription factors participate in regulating the process in tomato by regulating the PUFAs and defense-related enzymes (Fig. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 6). Oletic acid dehydrogenase (FAD2) is an omega-6 fatty acid desaturase. FAD2 encodes oleic acid dehydrogenase, which plays an important role in the oleic acid to linoleic acid ratio in plants (Salimonti et al., 2011). In our study, the expression of FAD2 was significantly inhibited by the nonanal treatment. This may enhance the oleic acid content in tomato, which could protect cell integrity. Patel et al. also found that inhibited FAD2 expression significantly increases the oleic acid contents in maize and peanut (Patel et al., 2004). The 12-oxo-phytodienoic acid reductases (OPRs) have very high substrate specificity and aid in converting linolenic acid to the phytohormone jasmonic acid (JA) (Zhang et al., 2005), and JA enhances defense responses in plants (Juergen et al., 2004). The nonanal treatment increased the expression of OPRs, and this may enhance the defense capacity of tomato. Alpha-DOX1 has alpha-dioxygenase activity and is implicated in plant defenses. In this study, the transcription level of Alpha-DOX1 was down-regulated by the nonanal treatment. This may be related to nonanal increasing the plant's defense capacity against Botrytis cinerea. Two genes related to arachidonic acid metabolism exhibited down-regulation. This occurred in response to nonanal increasing the plant's defense capacity. Phospholipid: diacylglycerol acyltransferase also increases the content of arachidonic acid (Pan et al., 2013). 3-Ketoacyl-CoA synthase (KCS) is a rate-limiting enzyme for the synthesis of long-chain fatty acids, and KCS and long-chain acyl-CoA synthetase are involved in the synthesis of cuticular wax and cork constituents in plants (Lee & Suh, 2013; Wu et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023). Additionally, KCS enhances the response to stress. KCS6, KCS10, and KCS20 were down-regulated in the nonanal-treated group.

Some genes are associated with only PUFA synthesis, whereas others are also associated with the defense capacity against Botrytis cinerea infection. Nonanal decreases the degradation of cuticular wax and cork constituents, and it maintains the plant's defense capacity (Xu et al., 2015; Zhang et al., 2023). The nonanal-treated group did not need to up-regulate more genes to maintain the cell integrity in response to Botrytis cinerea infection. The nonanal treatment decreased the degradation of PUFAs, which enhanced the defense capacity against Botrytis cinerea. The PUFA synthesis-related genes Solyc12g044950.1, Solyc12g049030.1, Solyc12g100250.1, Solyc12g100240.1, and Solyc12g100260.1 were down-regulated during the nonanal treatment (Fig. 7). The down-regulated genes were negatively regulated during Botrytis cinerea infection.

Interestingly, KCS also appeared in the plant–pathogen interaction pathway. Peng et al. (2021) found that PUFAs in apples may be involved in disease resistance. In the present study in the nonanal-treated group, compared with the control group, there were 13 down-regulated genes and 6 up-regulated genes in the plant–pathogen interaction pathway. This indicated that nonanal could increase the resistance of tomato against Botrytis cinerea. The lipoxygenase (LOX) pathway converts PUFA (such as linoleic acid and linolenic acid) into VAAs (such as cis-3-hexenoic acid, hexanal), which may affect the regulation and related biological functions of PUFA by affecting the fatty acid metabolic cycle. VAAs act as signaling molecules in the interaction between plants and the environment (Narisawa et al., 2024).

VOCs, as signaling molecules, are also closely related to the disease resistance capacity. There was a significant down-regulation of VOCs in postharvest Botrytis cinerea-infected tomato treated with nonanal (Fig. 8). In particular, there was a marked reduction in VAAs compared with in the control group. VAAs are involved in PUFA biosynthesis, which affects cell synthesis that is beneficial for enhancing plant defense capacities (Xu et al., 2015; Zhang et al., 2023). VAAs are increased in the Botrytis cinerea defense process in tomato, and nonanal spraying increases the defense capacity, which affects the VAA content of plants (Zhang, Sun, et al., 2017a). This suggests a dynamic interaction between host defense mechanisms and fungal metabolic adaptation, which highlights the potential role of aldehydes and alcohols in modulating pathogen virulence and host resistance.

Aldehydes, such as nonanal, disrupt fungal membrane integrity by altering membrane permeability, thereby inhibiting hyphal growth and spore germination (Chen et al., 2023; Dubey et al., 2020; Zhang, Sun, et al., 2017a). Here, the hexanal, 2-hexanal, and citral contents were lower in the nonanal-treated group than the control group during Botrytis cinerea infection. This indicates that nonanal reduces FAD2 expression, resulting in lower levels of hexanal and 2-hexanal, which similar to Peng et al. (2023). This also indicated that nonanal helps to improve tomato's antibacterial functions, which extends its storage life. Alcohols, particularly those derived from fatty acid oxidation, can serve as signaling molecules to activate plant systemic resistance or act as carbon sources for microbial antagonists (Chen et al., 2023). Slight elevations in alcohols levels may indicate fungal stress adaptation.

VAAs enhance defense responses mainly by regulating stress-response hormones, including JA and salicylic acid (SA). In addition, these stress-response hormones have closely relationships with PUFAs. VAAs induce the production of large amounts of JA, which is a defense-related substance (Juergen et al., 2004). Additionally, VAAs mediate the signaling of SA. Plant reinfection by pathogens causes the expression of pathogenesis-related genes PR-1b and PI-I (regulated by the SA signaling pathway) to be greatly enhanced (Zhang et al., 2019). The SA signaling can also antagonize JA signaling in many plants (Thaler et al., 2012). SA and JA are important plant defense hormones in the resistance against gray mold disease. The above findings suggest that VOCs (especially VAAs) not only play important roles in cell synthesis, they also act as signals to regulate the stress-response hormones. This may be the main reason for the difference in the VAA content between uninfected and Botrytis cinerea-infected tomato.

Conclusion

In summary, tomato's response to Botrytis cinerea infection involves many genes. Here, integrated metabolomic and transcriptomic analyses provided insights into tomato postharvest storage and the reduction in the rate of gray mold disease by nonanal. Metabolomic data showed that VAAs, such as hexanal, 2-hexanal, and citral, are the most important substances for disease resistance. Moreover, the transcriptomic analysis revealed that FAD2, OPRs, Alpha-DOX1, and KCS encoded enzymes related to PUFA biosynthesis in tomato. These major DEGs may respond to nonanal application, further regulating the defense capacity of tomato. Overall, these findings provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms of nonanal resistance in tomato against Botrytis cinerea.

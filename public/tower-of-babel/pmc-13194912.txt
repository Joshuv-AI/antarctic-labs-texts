Nationwide prevalence of impacted teeth in Saudi Arabia: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Bassam Alalola, Fathimah Farook, Ali Asiri, Ghadah Aldakheel, Farraj Albalawi, Suliman Alsaeed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTooth impaction is a common developmental dental anomaly that may lead to significant clinical complications. Although several studies have investigated the prevalence of impacted teeth in Saudi Arabia, the available evidence is heterogeneous and varies considerably according to study design and population characteristics. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to estimate the pooled prevalence of impacted permanent teeth in Saudi Arabia, evaluate tooth-specific impaction rates, and compare prevalence estimates between general and orthodontic populations. A systematic search of MEDLINE, Scopus, and EMBASE databases was conducted up to January 1st, 2025, following Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. Descriptive cross-sectional studies reporting the prevalence of impacted permanent teeth in Saudi Arabia were included. Study selection, data extraction, and methodological quality assessment were performed independently by two reviewers using the Joanna Briggs Institute Critical Appraisal Checklist. A random-effects meta-analysis was used to estimate pooled prevalence, and subgroup analyses were conducted to explore sources of heterogeneity. Twenty studies encompassing a pooled sample of 80,029 patients were included. The estimated pooled prevalence of a permanent tooth impaction was 7% (95% confidence interval: 4%–11%), with considerable heterogeneity across studies. Mandibular third molars were the most frequently impacted teeth (15.9%), followed by maxillary third molars (6.2%) and maxillary canines (3.2%). Subgroup analysis showed no significant difference in the overall prevalence of tooth impaction between general and orthodontic populations. However, maxillary canine impaction was significantly more prevalent among orthodontic patients compared to the general population (p < 0.05). Permanent tooth impaction affects approximately one in 14 patients attending dental clinics in Saudi Arabia, with third molars and maxillary canines being most commonly involved. Introduction

Tooth impaction is a common dental problem that affects millions of people worldwide. Tooth impaction occurs when normal tooth eruption is impeded by another tooth, bone, soft tissues, or other pathology beyond the predictable chronological eruption period (Littlewood and Mitchell 2019). Impacted teeth represent one of the most challenging conditions encountered by orthodontists (Allareddy et al. 2020). Environmental factors and genetic influences contribute to developmental disturbances such as tooth impaction. A wide range of systemic and local factors has been associated with impacted teeth (Kaczor-Urbanowicz et al. 2016). Systemic factors implicated include endocrine deficiencies (hypothyroidism), cleidocranial dysplasia, and craniofacial dysostosis syndromes. Local factors include abnormal tooth size, arch length discrepancies, primary teeth root resorption failure, early loss of primary teeth with subsequent space loss, presence of supernumerary teeth, and trauma (Kaczor-Urbanowicz et al. 2016).

Several complications may be associated with tooth impaction; therefore, early detection remains critical. Examples of relatively common complications include hyperplastic follicular space, dentigerous cyst formation, and odontogenic keratocyst development (Bishara and Ortho. 1992; Chu et al. 2003; Ferguson and Pitt 2004; Gonzalez et al. 2011). More severe complications may involve malignant transformation of the cystic wall into squamous cell carcinoma or mucoepidermoid carcinoma (Kaczor-Urbanowicz et al. 2016). Tooth impaction is influenced by several factors, including age, sex, ethnicity, eruption timing, and available space. Numerous studies have examined the prevalence of impacted teeth across different ethnicities, cultures, and geographic regions (Fardi et al. 2011; Topkara and Sari 2012; Patil and Maheshwari 2014; Arabion et al. 2017; Alamri et al. 2024).

The existing literature on the prevalence of impacted teeth in Saudi Arabia is extensive, although substantial heterogeneity exists, often reflecting inconsistencies in methodology and reporting. Recent evidence suggests that this variability may be partly explained by differences in the populations studied (Alalola et al. 2023). Studies assessing impacted canines in specialized patient groups, such as those attending orthodontic or oral and maxillofacial surgery (OMFS) clinics (Alkadhi et al. 2017; Fawzan et al. 2017; Alalola et al. 2023), frequently report higher prevalence estimates because of referral-related selection patterns. In contrast, studies conducted in general population samples report considerably lower prevalence values (Mustafa 2014; El-Khateeb et al. 2015), underscoring the influence of sample selection on reported outcomes.

Accordingly, this systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the current literature on the prevalence of impacted permanent teeth in Saudi Arabia. An additional objective involved establishing a comprehensive nationwide reference to provide insights into the prevalence and distribution of impacted teeth across the country.

Methodology

This systematic review and meta-analysis, registered in PROSPERO (registration ID: CRD42025413244), aimed to investigate the prevalence of tooth impaction in Saudi Arabia. The review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. An electronic search of MEDLINE, Scopus, and EMBASE databases was conducted up to January 1st, 2025. The search strategy incorporated synonyms and related terms for the keywords “prevalence,” “impaction,” “tooth,” and “Saudi Arabia,” with full details provided in Supplement 1. Gray literature sources were also reviewed, including unpublished research from King Saud bin Abdulaziz University for Health Sciences (KSAU-HS) and relevant governmental websites. Bibliographies of included studies and Google Scholar were manually searched to identify additional eligible studies. No restrictions were applied regarding language or publication date.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria encompassed descriptive cross-sectional studies reporting the prevalence of any impacted teeth in any region of Saudi Arabia, with impaction diagnosed through radiographic and/or clinical examinations. Exclusion criteria included studies reporting partial impaction, retained roots, patients with craniofacial abnormalities or developmental disorders affecting tooth eruption, review articles, survey-based studies, and studies reporting prevalence based on the number of teeth examined rather than the number of individuals.

Screening and quality assessment

The screening process was managed using COVIDENCE™ systematic review software. Methodological quality was assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Checklist for prevalence studies. The checklist includes nine items, of which two were not applicable to all included studies. The remaining seven items were evaluated using responses of “yes,” “no,” or “unclear” for each study.

Study selection followed a three-step process within COVIDENCE™. Two independent researchers (A.A. and G.D.) screened titles, abstracts, and full texts according to the predefined eligibility criteria. Disagreements were resolved through discussion and consensus, and unresolved discrepancies were referred to a third researcher (B.A.). Following final study selection, data extraction was independently performed by A.A. and G.D., followed by joint review to finalize the extracted dataset. Methodological quality assessment was also independently conducted by A.A. and G.D., with disagreements resolved in consultation with B.A.

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

A random-effects model was applied to estimate the pooled prevalence of tooth impaction. Statistical heterogeneity across studies was assessed using the I2 index. Subgroup analyses were conducted to explore potential sources of heterogeneity. All statistical analyses were performed using R software, version 4.2.2.

Results

Study selection

The systematic search identified 79 records from MEDLINE, Scopus, and EMBASE databases. After duplicate removal, 42 records remained. Title and abstract screening excluded 27 records, which reduced the number of full-text articles assessed for eligibility to 15.

Besides database searching, 24 records were identified from alternative sources, including Google Scholar, hand-searched reference lists of relevant publications, and gray literature obtained from the KSAU-HS website. A total of 39 records underwent further evaluation. Among these records, 19 studies were excluded after applying the eligibility criteria. Exclusion occurred for the following reasons: seven studies failed to report adequate prevalence data for impacted teeth, population size, or relevant estimates; six studies lacked clear information regarding study design; and six studies did not use suitable study designs for prevalence estimates. A final total of 20 studies met the inclusion criteria for qualitative and quantitative synthesis. Figure 1 presents the PRISMA flow diagram summarizing the study selection process.

Critical appraisal

The risk of bias (RoB) assessment for the included studies was conducted using the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist with no numerical scoring system applied. Based on this assessment, none of the included studies met the criteria for classification as having a low RoB. 13 studies were categorized as having a moderate or unclear RoB (Alrwuili et al. 2016; El-Khateeb et al. 2015; Haralur et al. 2017; Alalola et al. 2023; Mahjoub et al. 2024; Jan et al. 2014; Alhammadi et al. 2018; Almarhoumi et al. 2022; Afify and Zawawi 2012; Melha et al. 2017; Patil et al. 2018; Alyami et al. 2020; Zaman et al. 2021).

Conversely, 7 studies demonstrated a high RoB (Baidas et al. 2022; Alfergani et al. 2017; Alkadhi et al. 2017; Alassiry 2020; Fawzan et al. 2017; Mustafa 2014, 2015), indicating significant concerns regarding methodological rigor.

The major sources of bias across several studies emerged within the sampling and outcome assessment domains. Particularly regarding sampling exclusively from specialized populations, and diagnostic criteria and methods used for identifying impacted teeth. Multiple studies demonstrated inconsistencies in sample selection and the absence of standardized assessment protocols, factors that may influence the reliability of reported outcomes. The distribution of moderate or unclear (yellow) and high (red) RoB ratings in Fig. 2 further reflects these methodological concerns.

Synthesis of results

From a pooled sample of 80,029 patients, random-effects meta-analysis demonstrated an estimated pooled prevalence of a permanent tooth impaction of 7% (95% confidence interval: 4–11%), with considerable heterogeneity observed across included studies (Fig. 3). Mandibular third molars showed the highest prevalence of impaction (15.9%), followed by maxillary third molars (6.2%) and maxillary canines (3.2%) (Table 2).

Forest plot showing the overall prevalence of permanent tooth impaction with subgroup analysis comparing general and orthodontic populations

The subgroup analysis indicated no significant difference in the overall prevalence of tooth impaction between general and orthodontic populations. However, analysis by tooth type demonstrated a significantly higher prevalence of maxillary canine impaction among orthodontic patients compared to the general population. All observed differences reached statistical significance (p < 0.05) (Fig. 4).

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis provided the first comprehensive evaluation of the literature addressing the prevalence of all impacted teeth in Saudi Arabia and was designed to serve as a nationwide epidemiological reference. Previous investigations from the western, southern, northern, and central regions have examined this topic. A summary of the key characteristics and principal findings of the 20 included studies appears in Table 1. The present analysis pursued three objectives: determination of the overall prevalence of impacted teeth, clarification of variations reported across studies, and comparison of national estimates with global prevalence rates.

After pooling data from 80,029 patients attending dental clinics across multiple regions of Saudi Arabia, the overall prevalence of at least one impacted tooth reached 7% (Fig. 3). This estimate aligns with reports from smaller population-based studies conducted in Sweden (8.3%) and Canada (6.9%) (Ahlqwist and Grondahl 1991; Shah et al. 1978). Conversely, higher prevalence rates ranging from 28% to 44% have been reported in several international studies; however, such reports frequently originated from single cities or centers rather than nationally representative samples (Chu et al. 2003; Arabion et al. 2017).

Mandibular third molars represent the most frequently impacted teeth in the global literature, followed by maxillary third molars and maxillary canines (Ahlqwist and Grondahl 1991; Gisakis et al. 2011; Alalola et al. 2023; Chu et al. 2003), a pattern reaffirmed by the present findings (Table 2). In this study, the prevalence of impacted mandibular and maxillary third molars reached 15.9% and 6.2% respectively. Other investigations have reported variable estimates (Hasan et al. 2016; Hassan 2010; Chu et al. 2003; Shakib et al. 2011), findings largely attributable to heterogeneity in study designs, sampling strategies, and demographic characteristics of investigated populations (Adeola et al. 2023; Al-Dajani et al. 2017; Pinto et al. 2024). More specifically, prevalence demonstrates substantial regional variation, with higher rates documented in Asia and the Middle East and lower rates reported in Europe, suggesting contributions from geographic, environmental, and potential genetic factors to the risk of tooth impaction (Pinto et al. 2024).

After pooling data from 51,588 patients across multiple regions of Saudi Arabia, the prevalence of maxillary canine impaction reached 3.2%, whereas mandibular canine impaction was markedly lower at 0.36% (Table 2; Fig. 4). This pronounced difference between maxillary and mandibular canine impaction rates has been consistently documented in the literature (Alamri et al. 2024; Bishara and Ortho. 1992). Interpretation of published evidence remains challenging because substantial heterogeneity exists among studies conducted in similar regions, with reported prevalence estimates for maxillary canine impaction ranging from 1.3% to 10.1% (Mustafa 2014; Alkadhi et al. 2017). One major source of this variability relates to the characteristics of the study population. Investigations conducted in specialized clinical cohorts, such as patients attending orthodontic or OMFS clinics, are inherently affected by selection bias, as these populations are more likely to include individuals with impacted teeth (Alalola et al. 2023). Accordingly, the subgroup analysis demonstrated a significantly higher prevalence of maxillary canine impaction among orthodontic patients compared to the general population (Fig. 4). The persistently high heterogeneity likely reflects genuine epidemiological diversity arising from regional sampling, referral bias, age distribution, and diagnostic criteria rather than solely on methodological shortcomings.

Impacted premolars are relatively uncommon. Only four studies (Mustafa 2015; Alalola et al. 2023; Mahjoub et al. 2024; Afify and Zawawi 2012) reported the prevalence of premolar impaction, yielding rates of 0.66% for mandibular premolars and 0.47% for maxillary premolars. These estimates were considerably lower than internationally reported data from Turkey, India and Greece, which documented prevalence ranges between 0.81% and 4.3% (Ezirganli et al. 2014; Şimşek-Kaya et al. 2011; S.; Patil and Maheshwari 2014; Fardi et al. 2011). Such variation likely reflects differences in study design, sampling strategies, and demographic characteristics of the populations investigated.

Certain teeth, particularly permanent first and second molars, remain markedly under-investigated in the literature, with an even greater evidence gap within studies from Saudi Arabia. Within the present analysis, only one study reported prevalence estimates for impacted first and second molars (Alalola et al. 2023), documenting rates of 0.02% and 0.18%, respectively (Table 2). These findings align with international reports from Turkey, India, and the United States (Grover and Lorton 1985; Srinivasan et al. 2016; Bereket et al. 2011), reinforcing both the rarity of molar impaction and the tendency for second molars to be affected more frequently than first molars.

Limitations

This review had several limitations. Firstly, reliance on retrospective data introduced an inherent dependence on pre-existing clinical records, increasing susceptibility to selection bias and variability arising from the absence of standardized diagnostic criteria. Future investigations would benefit from multicenter, nationwide, standardized cross-sectional designs capable of reducing these sources of bias and providing more representative prevalence estimates. Secondly, limitations related to population characterization were identified. Although most included studies explicitly reported samples restricted to Saudi nationals, several studies did not clearly specify participant nationality. Consequently, the pooled estimates more accurately reflect the prevalence of impacted teeth among patients attending dental clinics in Saudi Arabia rather than prevalence among Saudi nationals exclusively.

Conclusions

Based on pooled data from 80,029 patients, this systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrated that permanent tooth impaction affects 1 in 14 patients attending dental clinics in Saudi Arabia and constitutes a clinically relevant finding, with an overall estimated prevalence of 7%. Mandibular third molars were the most frequently impacted teeth, followed by maxillary third molars and maxillary canines. Although the overall prevalence of tooth impaction did not differ significantly between general and orthodontic populations, orthodontic patients exhibited a notably higher prevalence of maxillary canine impaction. These findings emphasize the importance of early detection and targeted clinical assessment, particularly for maxillary canines, and provide a valuable epidemiological reference for clinicians and policymakers in planning preventive and interceptive orthodontic care.

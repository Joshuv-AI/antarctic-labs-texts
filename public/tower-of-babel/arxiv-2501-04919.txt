Arveson’s version of the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula for Hilbert modules over the polynomial rings Thanks: This work is supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China : (12271298 and 11871308).
Penghui Wang, Ruoyu Zhang and Zeyou Zhu

Abstract
In this paper, we complete the framework of Arveson’s version of the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula by proving that Arveson’s version of the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula holding true for the quotient module in the Drury-Arveson module is equivalent to the associated submodule being locally algebraic. Moreover, we establish the asymptotic Arveson’s curvature invariant and the asymptotic Euler characteristic for contractive Hilbert modules over the polynomial ring in infinitely many variables, and obtain the infinitely-many-variables analogue of Arveson’s version of Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula. Finally, we solve the finite defect problem for submodules of the Drury-Arveson module H 2 H^{2} in infinitely many variables by proving that H 2 H^{2} has no nontrivial submodules of finite rank.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{B}_{d}
be the open unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Recall the Drury-Arveson space
H_{d}^{2}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on

\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}(z)={1\over 1-\langle z,\lambda\rangle}.

It can be viewed as a Hilbert module
[
8

]
on the polynomial ring

, equipped with a natural module action defined by the multiplication by polynomials.
To simplify the notations, write
\mathcal{P}_{d}=\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\cdots,z_{d}].
In
[
3

, Arveson introduced the curvature invariant for quotient modules in
H_{d}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}(N\in\mathbb{N}^{+})
. Let
\mathcal{M}\subset H_{d}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a submodule and
\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
be the corresponding quotient module. Let

S_{z_{i}}f=P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}(M_{z_{i}}\otimes I_{N})f,\,\forall f\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}.

For

Then for all

are positive operators on the range of

. In what follows, the range of a bounded operator

will be denoted by

[
3
]
that the radial limits

\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}
relative to

on
\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}
, and Arveson’s curvature invariant is defined by

K(\mathcal{M}^{\perp})=\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}}\lim\limits_{r\to 1^{-}}(1-r^{2})\operatorname{trace}(F(r\xi))d\sigma_{d}(\xi),

where
\sigma_{d}
is the rotationally invariant probability measure on

. Such an invariant was deeply studied by

Next, we will introduce the Euler characteristic.
Let

has finite free resolutions in the category of finitely generated

-modules

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow F_{n}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow F_{2}\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}\rightarrow 0,

In

In the beginning of

For which quotient modules of

\mathcal{M}

E_{\lambda}\mathcal{M}=\{f(\lambda):f\in\mathcal{M}\}.

The fiber dimension of

fd(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{sup}\{\operatorname{dim}E_{\lambda}\mathcal{M}:\lambda\in\mathbb{B}_{d}\}.

Definition 1.1

A submodule

Remark 1.1

Recall that
\mathcal{M}

Theorem 1.1
.

For a submodule
\mathcal{M}\subseteq H_{d}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}

\mathcal{M}^{\perp}

In
[
3
]
, Arveson proved that the quotient modules of

have universal properties. To illustrate it, let
(T_{1},T_{2},\cdots,T_{d})

In this case, the Hilbert space

with the module action given by

Obviously, Drury-Arveson’s module
H_{d}^{2}
is a

-contractive Hilbert module and Arveson
[

]
proved that any pure

-contractive Hilbert module is unitarily equivalent to a quotient module of
H_{d}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}

.

Remark 1.2
.

(1)

When

Theorem
1.1
recovers Arveson’s result

if and only if
\mathcal{M}
contains a nonzero polynomial.

(2)

[
15
, Theorem 18]
demonstrates that Arveson’s version of the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula holds when the submodules are generated by polynomials. Furthermore, as indicated by Remark
1.1
, this can be regarded as a corollary of Theorem
1.1
.

(3)

For
N\geq 2,
by
[
3
, Theorem B]
and Theorem
1.1
, graded submodules in
H^{2}_{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
are locally algebraic, and hence they have enough polynomials. It is noteworthy that, to the best of our knowledge, it was previously unknown whether a graded submodule in Arveson’s framework contains a nonzero polynomial.

Our next goal is to extend Arveson’s version of the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula to Drury-Arveson modules in infinitely many variables.
To do this, we need to define the asymptotic curvature invariant and the asymptotic Euler characteristic for the so-called finite rank
\omega
-contractive Hilbert modules over
the polynomial ring
in infinitely many variables.

A sequence of commuting operators

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}=(T_{1},\cdots,T_{m},\cdots)
is called an
\omega
-contraction
, if

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}T_{i}T_{i}^{*}\leq I,~\forall~m\geq 1.

(1.7)

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{m}
be
the polynomial ring in infinitely many variables
.
Any
\omega
-contraction
\mathbb{T}_{\omega}
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
gives rise to a
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-module structure on
\mathcal{H}

in the natural way, as follows:

\displaystyle p\cdot\zeta=p(T_{1},\cdots,T_{m},\cdots)\zeta,\quad p\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty},\quad\zeta\in\mathcal{H}.

(1.8)

In this case,
\mathcal{H}
is called an
\omega
-contractive Hilbert module
.
Obviously, if
\mathbb{T}_{\omega}
is an
\omega
-contraction, then
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}T_{i}T_{i}^{*}
converges in the strong operator topology as
m
tends to infinity. The defect operator
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
is defined by

\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}=\Big(I-\text{SOT-}\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}T_{i}T_{i}^{*}\Big)^{1/2},

and

\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{ran}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
is called the rank of
\mathcal{H}.

Moreover, the
\omega
-contraction
\mathbb{T}_{\omega}
gives rise to a completely positive map
\phi
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
by way of

\phi(A)=\text{SOT-}\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}T_{i}AT_{i}^{*},\quad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

Obviously,
\phi^{n}(I)
is decreasing. An
\omega
-contractive Hilbert module is pure if

\text{SOT-}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\phi^{n}(I)=0.

As an example of
\omega
-contractive Hilbert modules, Arveson’s module of infinitely many variables will be reviewd in Section 3 and denoted by
H^{2}.
Analogous to the finitely-many-variables case, it will be proved in Section 4, any pure
\omega
-contractive Hilbert module of finite rank is unitarily equivalent to a quotient module
\cal M^{\perp}
of
H^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}

for some
N\geq 1.

As a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}
in
\mathbb{\ell}^{2}
, with the reproducing kernel

{\cal K}_{\lambda}(z)={1\over 1-\langle z,\lambda\rangle},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{B},

H^{2}
was introduced by Agler and M
{}^{\text{c}}
Carthy
[
2
]
to study interpolation problems.

As in the finitely-many-variables case, for any submodule
\cal M
in
H^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
, the compression of
M_{z_{i}}\otimes I_{N}
on
\cal M^{\perp}
is defined by

S_{z_{i}}f=P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}(M_{z_{i}}\otimes I_{N})f,\,\forall f\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp},

and

S(z)=\text{SOT-}\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\bar{z}_{i}S_{z_{i}}.

Moreover, for any
z\in\mathbb{B}
,
the operator
I-S(z)
is invertible. Define

F(z)\xi=\Delta_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}\left(I-S(z)^{*}\right)^{-1}(I-S(z))^{-1}\Delta_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}\xi,\quad\xi\in\text{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}.

For any integer

, let

K_{0}^{m}(z^{(m)})=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1}\left(1-r^{2}\right)\operatorname{trace}F(rz_{1},\cdots,rz_{m},0,0,\cdots)

for almost every point

relative to

Set

In Section 4,
we will prove that

K_{m+1}({\mathcal{M}^{\bot}})\leq K_{m}({\mathcal{M}^{\bot}})\leq\text{rank}({\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}).

Hence for
{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}},
we define the asymptotic curvature invariant
by

K({\mathcal{M}^{\bot}})=\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}K_{m}({\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}).

Seemingly, the asymptotic curvature invariant depends on the choice of the orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}.
However the following result will imply that the asymptotic curvature invariant is coordinate-free, which
is an infinitely-many-variables analogue of
[

.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq H^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a submodule, then

K(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})=N-fd(\mathcal{M}).

Here,
E_{\lambda}\mathcal{M}
and

can be defined similarly to
(
1.4
) and (
1.5
). Next, we define the asymptotic Euler characteristic. For an
\omega
-contractive

\mathcal{H}

\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m}=\operatorname{span}\left\{f\cdot\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}\zeta:f\in\mathcal{P}_{m},\zeta\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

Then

is a finitely generated

, and the asymptotic Euler characteristic will be defined as

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{H})=\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\chi_{m}(\mathcal{H}).

(1.9)

The following result solves Problem

in the infinitely-many-variables case.

Theorem 1.3
.

For a submodule
\mathcal{M}\subseteq H^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
, Arveson’s version of the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula holds true for
\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
iff
\mathcal{M}
is locally algebraic.

It is well known that any nontrivial homogeneous submodule
\mathcal{M}
of
H_{d}^{2}

must be generated by polynomials. Consequently, one has

However, in the infinitely-many-variables case, we present an example of a homogeneous submodule in

that does not contain any nonzero polynomial.
By Theorem

, Arveson’s version of the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula
may not hold for homogeneous quotient modules in

.

At last, we will study
\it{the\,finite\,defect\,problem}
for submodules in

Such a problem for submodules in

was raised by Arveson in

Problem 1.2

Is the rank of each nonzero submodule of

that has infinite codimension in

By showing that for

, any nonzero submodule

Theorem 1.4

If
\mathcal{M}
is a nonzero submodule of

In Section 3, fundamental properties of Drury-Arveson’s space of infinitely many variables are introduced.
In Section 4, we define the asymptotic curvature invariant and the asymptotic Euler characteristic for

In Section 5, we present an example of a homogeneous submodule in

2
Arveson’s version of the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula

This section is dedicated to proving our main result, Theorem
1.1
.
The main tool used to deal with Theorem
1.1
is the representation of the Euler characteristic in terms of the rank of finitely generated modules over an integral domain.
Let
\mathfrak{R}
be an integral domain, and
S=\mathfrak{R}\setminus\{0\}.
Then
S^{-1}\mathfrak{R}

is the field of fractions of
\mathfrak{R}
. For a finitely generated module
F
over
\mathfrak{R}
, let
S^{-1}F

denote the module of fractions. It is evident that
S^{-1}F

forms a vector space over
S^{-1}\mathfrak{R}
. We denote the rank of
F
by
\dim_{S^{-1}\mathfrak{R}}(S^{-1}F)
, as introduced in
[
27
, Page 873]
. Obviously, in (
1.1
),
\beta_{k}
is the rank of
F_{k}.

For a
d
-contractive Hilbert module
\mathcal{H},
the defect operator is defined by

\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}=\left(I-T_{1}T_{1}^{*}-\cdots-T_{d}T_{d}^{*}\right)^{1/2}.

The
d
-contractive Hilbert module
\mathcal{H}
is of finite rank if
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
is a finite rank operator. Similar to
(
1.1
), the Euler characteristic can also be defined.
Recall that in
[
3
]
Arveson defined

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}=\operatorname{span}\left\{f\cdot\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}\zeta\mid f\in\mathcal{P}_{d},\zeta\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

(2.1)

By
[
27
, Lemma 10.1]
and
[
1
, Proposition 2.11]
, it can be clearly seen that

\chi(\mathcal{H})=\operatorname{rank}(\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}),

(2.2)

which plays the key role in the study of Arveson’s version of the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula.
Before proving Theorem
1.1
,
we need some lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
submodule in
H_{d}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
such that

\operatorname{dim}~\mathcal{M}\cap(\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N})=n.

Then for an orthonormal basis
\{1\otimes e_{i}\}_{i=n+1}^{N}
of

\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}\ominus(\mathcal{M}\cap(\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N})),

\{P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}(1\otimes e_{i})\}_{i=n+1}^{N}
is a linear basis of
\operatorname{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}},
in particular,
\operatorname{rank}\,\Delta_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}=N-n.

Proof.

At first,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}^{2}

\displaystyle=I_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}S_{z_{i}}S_{z_{i}}^{*}

\displaystyle=I_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}M_{z_{i}}P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{M^{\bot}}|_{M^{\bot}}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}\left(I-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}\right)\bigg|_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(E_{0}\otimes I)P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}|_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}},

where
E_{0}
is the orthogonal projection from
H_{d}^{2}
onto the one-dimensional space of constant functions.
Now, take an orthonormal basis
\{1\otimes e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
\mathcal{M}\cap(\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}),
which extends to an orthonormal basis
\{1\otimes e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
of
\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}.

Next, we claim that both
\{P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{i})\}_{i=n+1}^{N}
and
\{P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(E_{0}\otimes I)P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{i})\}_{i=n+1}^{N}

are linearly independent. Firstly, assume that for
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{i})=0,

and we have
\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}(1\otimes e_{i})\in\mathcal{M}\cap(\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}).

By the choice of
\{1\otimes e_{i}\}_{i=n+1}^{N},

\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}(1\otimes e_{i})\perp\left(\mathcal{M}\cap(\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N})\right).

It follows that
\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}(1\otimes e_{i})=0,
and hence
\mu_{i}=0.

Similarly, assume that
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(E_{0}\otimes I)P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{i})=0.

Then

\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}\left(E_{0}\otimes I\right)P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}(1\otimes e_{i})\in\mathcal{M}.

Obviously,
\operatorname{ran}\,(E_{0}\otimes I)\subseteq\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N},

and hence

\left(E_{0}\otimes I\right)P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}(1\otimes e_{i})\in\mathcal{M}\cap(\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}).

Therefore, by the choice of
\{1\otimes e_{i}\}_{i=n+1}^{N}
again,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(E_{0}\otimes I\right)P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}(1\otimes e_{i}),\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}(1\otimes e_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}(1\otimes e_{i}),\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}(1\otimes e_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}(1\otimes e_{i}),P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}(1\otimes e_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\|P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}\sum\limits_{i=n+1}^{N}\mu_{i}(1\otimes e_{i})\right\|^{2},

and hence
\mu_{i}=0.
The claim is proved. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}\,\{P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(E_{0}\otimes I)P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{i})\}_{i=n+1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{span}\,\{P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{n+1}),\cdots,P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{N})\}

\displaystyle=N-n.

Obviously,

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}\,\{P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(E_{0}\otimes I)P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{i})\}_{i=n+1}^{N}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}\left(E_{0}\otimes I\right)f:f\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{span}\,\{P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{n+1}),\cdots,P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{N})\}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\{P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}\left(E_{0}\otimes I\right)f:f\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}\}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{span}\,\{P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{n+1}),\cdots,P_{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}(1\otimes e_{N})\}.

The proof is completed.
∎

In particular,

\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}=\operatorname{span}\left\{f\cdot P_{\mathcal{M}^{\perp}}(1\otimes e_{i}):f\in\mathcal{P}_{d},n+1\leq i\leq N\right\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
submodule of

3
Drury-Arveson space in infinitely many variables

In this section, we will introduce some elementary properties of
Drury-Arveson space
of infinitely many variables.
For a complex separable Hilbert space
\cal H
, let
\Gamma_{s}^{n}(\cal H)
be the symmetric
n
-fold tensor product of
\cal H
with itself, and the symmetric Fock space

\Gamma_{s}({\cal H})=\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{s}^{n}(\cal H),

where
\Gamma_{0}(\cal H)=\mathbb{C}
is the vacuum space. In the historical literature
[
26
]
, the symmetric Fock space describes the bosonic fields.

By using the framework of Arveson
[
4
]
, for
m=\dim\cal H<\infty
, the symmetric Fock space
\Gamma_{s}(\cal H)
is unitarily equivalent to
H_{m}^{2}
. For
\cal H
being of infinite dimension, similar to the framework of Arveson
[
4
]
,
\Gamma_{s}(H)
can be described as a Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}
in
\ell^{2}
. Recall that a complex-valued function
g
on an open subset
U
of a Banach space
X
is analytic on
U
if
g
is locally bounded, and for each
x_{0}\in U
and any
x\in X
, the function
g\left(x_{0}+zx\right)
depends analytically on the parameter
z
for
x_{0}+zx\in U
.

It is easy to see that
H_{m}^{2}\subset H_{m+1}^{2}
and for any
f\in H_{m}^{2}
,

\|f\|_{H_{m}^{2}}=\|f\|_{H_{m+1}^{2}},

which implies that
\bigcup\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}H_{m}^{2}
can be equipped with a natural norm, and in what follows, we will denote its completion by
H^{2}
. Further, any
f\in\bigcup\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}H_{m}^{2}
can be seen as a holomorphic function on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}
by

\hat{f}(z)=f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})\quad\text{for}~f\in H_{m}^{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{B}.

Next for any
f\in\bigcup\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}H_{m}^{2}
, assume
f\in H_{m_{0}}^{2}
for
m_{0}>0
, and we have for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{B}
,

|\hat{f}(\lambda)|=|f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m_{0}})|\leq\frac{\|f\|_{H^{2}_{m_{0}}}}{\sqrt{1-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{0}}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}}}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{H^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-\|\lambda\|_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\ell^{2}}}^{2}}}.

It follows that for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{B}
, the evaluation functional
E_{\lambda}
on
\bigcup\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}H_{m}^{2}
, defined by
E_{\lambda}(f)=f(\lambda)
, is continuous, which is naturally extended to a continuous functional on
H^{2}
. Therefore, as mentioned in the introduction,
H^{2}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on

\mathbb{B}
, with the reproducing kernel

\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}(z)={1\over 1-\langle z,\lambda\rangle},

which will be called Drury-Arveson space in infinitely many variables. Obviously,
H^{2}
is unitarily equivalent to
\Gamma_{s}(\ell^{2})
.
Let

\mathbb{N}^{(\mathbb{N})}=\left\{(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mid\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},\text{ and }\alpha_{n}=0\text{ for all but finitely many }n\right\}.

For any
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{n},0,0,\cdots)\in\mathbb{N}^{(\mathbb{N})}
, set

|\alpha|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i},\quad\alpha!=\alpha_{1}!\cdots\alpha_{n}!,

and

z^{\alpha}=z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}z_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}},\quad z\in\mathbb{B}.

It is straightforward to verify that the set
\{z^{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{(\mathbb{N})}\}

is an orthogonal basis in
H^{2}
. Additionally, we have

\|z^{\alpha}\|^{2}=\frac{\alpha!}{|\alpha|!}.

As usual, write
M_{i}=M_{z_{i}}
and it is obvious that

M_{i}^{*}z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\alpha_{i}=0,\\

4

In the present section, we will establish the asymptotic curvature invariant and the asymptotic Euler characteristic of finite rank
\omega

For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m+1}\leq\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m}.

Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
\omega
-contractive Hilbert module of finite rank,
then

\chi_{m+1}(\mathcal{H})\leq\chi_{m}(\mathcal{H}).

By Lemma
4.9
,
we define the asymptotic Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{H}

\chi(\mathcal{H})=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{m}(\mathcal{H}).

(4.28)

Remark 4.1
.

It is not difficult to see that the Euler characteristic
\chi(\mathcal{H})
corresponds to
\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}
.
In fact,
suppose that
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{ran}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathfrak{n}.

Let
\left\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{\mathfrak{n}}\right\}

be a linear basis of
\operatorname{ran}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}.
Without loss of generality, suppose that

\left\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{k}\right\}

is a maximal linearly independent set of
\left\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{\mathfrak{n}}\right\}
in the module
\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}.
Obviously, there exists an
m_{0}>0
such that
for
m>m_{0},

\left\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{k}\right\}

is a maximal linearly independent set of
\left\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{\mathfrak{n}}\right\}
in the module
\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m}.

Hence by Lemma
2.3
,

\chi(\mathcal{H})=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{m}(\mathcal{H})=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m}=k=\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Similar to
[
3
, Proposition 4.5]
and the corollary of
[
3
, Proposition 4.9]
,

\left\{m!\frac{\operatorname{dim}\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m,n}}{n^{m}}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

converges and

\chi_{m}(\mathcal{H})=m!\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{dim}\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m,n}}{n^{m}},

(4.29)

where

\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m,n}=\operatorname{span}\left\{f\cdot\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}\zeta:f\in\mathcal{P}_{m},\operatorname{deg}f\leq n,\zeta\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

Then we have the following proposition, whose proof will be
put in Appendix.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
\omega
-contractive Hilbert module of finite rank,
then

\operatorname{dim}\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m,n}=\operatorname{rank}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{m}^{k}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)\right),

and hence

\chi_{m}(\mathcal{H})=m!\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{m}^{k}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)\right)}{n^{m}}.

Recall that
K_{m}(\mathcal{H})
is decreasing if
\mathcal{H}
is pure, and the asymptotic curvature invariant
K(\mathcal{H})=\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}K_{m}(\mathcal{H}),

hence we
have the following inequality.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
\omega
-contractive Hilbert module of finite rank, then

K_{m}(\mathcal{H})\leq\chi_{m}(\mathcal{H}),

In particular, if
\mathcal{H}
is pure,
K(\mathcal{H})\leq\chi(\mathcal{H}).

Proof.

Since

\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{m}^{k}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)\right)=\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{m}^{k}\left(I-\phi(I)\right)\right)\leq\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{m}^{k}\left(I-\phi_{m}(I)\right)\right)=I-\phi_{m}^{n+1}(I)\leq I,

we have

\operatorname{trace}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{m}^{k}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)\right)\leq\operatorname{rank}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{m}^{k}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)\right).

Then by Proposition
4.8
and Proposition
4.10
,

K_{m}(\mathcal{H})\leq\chi_{m}(\mathcal{H}).

Furthermore, if
\mathcal{H}
is pure, then by (

) and (

K(\mathcal{H})=\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}K_{m}(\mathcal{H})\leq\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{m}(\mathcal{H})=\chi(\mathcal{H}).

∎

(2)

(1):

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): Suppose that

Hence by Theorem

5
An
example
of homogeneous submodule containing no nonzero polynomials

It is straightforward to verify that any homogeneous submodule of
H_{d}^{2}

is polynomially generated. Consequently, Arveson’s version of the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula holds for homogeneous quotient modules in
H_{d}^{2}.

In this section, we give an example of homogeneous submodule
\mathcal{M}
of
H^{2},
which contains no nonzero polynomials.
Then by Theorem
1.3
and Proposition
4.11
,

K(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})<\chi(\mathcal{M}^{\bot}).

Let

f=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(i+1)!}z_{i},

To simplify the notation, write
\lambda_{i}=\frac{1}{(i+1)!}.

Consider the homogeneous submodule
\mathcal{M}_{f}
in
H^{2}
generated by
f.
Obviously,
\mathcal{M}_{f}
is homogeneous:

\mathcal{M}_{f}=\mathop{\oplus}_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n},

where

\mathcal{M}_{n}=\overline{\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{\alpha}f:|\alpha|=n-1\right\}},\quad n\geq 1.\\

Lemma 5.1
.

\mathcal{M}_{n}
contains no nonzero ploynomials.

Proof.

Let
H_{n}
be the space of homogeneous functions in
H^{2}
of degree
n.
Given a ploynomial
q\in H_{n}\cap\mathcal{P}_{\infty},
then there exists a
d_{0}>n
such that

q\in\mathcal{P}_{d_{0}}\cap H_{n}.

We claim that for all
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}\cap H_{n-1},

||q-p\cdot f||^{2}\geq\frac{||q||^{2}}{4d_{0}^{3}(d_{0}+1)!(d_{0}+4)!^{2}}.

Indeed, let

P_{d_{0}}
be the orthogonal projection from
H^{2}
onto
H_{d_{0}}^{2}.

Write

p=(I-P_{d_{0}})p+P_{d_{0}}p.

For simplicity, set

p_{1}=(I-P_{d_{0}})\,p,\quad p_{2}=P_{d_{0}}\,p,

and

f_{1}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{0}}\lambda_{i}z_{i},\quad f_{2}=\sum\limits_{i=d_{0}+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}z_{i}.

Since for
|\alpha|=n-2,~i,j>d_{0},~l>d_{0}~\text{and}~z^{\beta}\in\mathcal{P}_{d_{0}},

\langle z_{i}z^{\beta},z_{j}z_{l}z^{\alpha}\rangle=0;
therefore,

\langle p_{2}f_{2},p_{1}f_{2}\rangle=\left\langle p_{2}\sum\limits_{i=d_{0}+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}z_{i},p_{1}\sum\limits_{i=d_{0}+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}z_{i}\right\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=d_{0}+1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{j=d_{0}+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\overline{\lambda_{j}}\langle p_{2}z_{i},p_{1}z_{j}\rangle=0.

By direct calculations,

\langle q,p_{1}f_{2}\rangle=\langle p_{2}f_{1},p_{1}f_{2}\rangle=\langle p_{1}f_{1},p_{1}f_{2}\rangle=\langle q,p_{2}f_{2}\rangle=\langle p_{2}f_{1},p_{2}f_{2}\rangle=\langle q,p_{1}f_{1}\rangle=\langle p_{2}f_{1},p_{1}f_{1}\rangle=0.

It follows that

\langle q-p_{2}f_{1},p_{1}f_{2}\rangle=\langle p_{2}f_{2}+p_{1}f_{1},p_{1}f_{2}\rangle=\langle q-p_{2}f_{1},p_{2}f_{2}+p_{1}f_{1}\rangle=0.

Then

||q-pf||^{2}=||q-p_{2}f_{1}-p_{2}f_{2}-p_{1}f_{1}-p_{1}f_{2}||^{2}=||q-p_{2}f_{1}||^{2}+||p_{2}f_{2}+p_{1}f_{1}||^{2}+||p_{1}f_{2}||^{2}.

Next, we will estimate
dist(q,\mathcal{M}_{n})
in two cases.

Case~1.

||p_{2}||\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{||q||}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{0}}\lambda_{i}}.

Notice that

\left\|p_{2}f_{1}\right\|\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{0}}\lambda_{i}\|p_{2}\|,
then

||q-pf||\geq\left\|q-p_{2}f_{1}\right\|\geq\|q\|-\left\|p_{2}f_{1}\right\|\geq\|q\|-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{0}}\lambda_{i}\|p_{2}\|\geq\frac{\|q\|}{2}.

Case~2.

||p_{2}||>\frac{1}{2}\frac{||q||}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{0}}\lambda_{i}}.

For
|\gamma|=n-1
and
z^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{P}_{d_{0}},

\left\|z^{\gamma}z_{i}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{\|z^{\gamma}\|^{2}}{n},i\geq d_{0}+1.

Then

||p_{2}f_{2}||^{2}=\left\|\sum\limits_{i=d_{0}+1}^{\infty}p_{2}\lambda_{i}z_{i}\right\|^{2}=\sum\limits_{i=d_{0}+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\left\|p_{2}z_{i}\right\|^{2}\geq\sum\limits_{i=d_{0}+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\frac{\|p_{2}\|^{2}}{n}\geq\frac{\|q\|^{2}}{\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{0}}\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=d_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{4n}.

Set

h^{2}=\frac{\|q\|^{2}}{\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{0}}\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=d_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{4n}.

Case~2.1.

||p_{1}||\leq\frac{h}{2||M_{f_{1}}||}.

||q-pf||\geq||p_{2}f_{2}+p_{1}f_{1}||\geq(||p_{2}f_{2}||-||p_{1}f_{1}||)\geq\frac{h}{2}.

Case~2.2.

||p_{1}||>\frac{h}{2||M_{f_{1}}||}.

Notice that

\left\|z^{\gamma^{\prime}}z_{d_{0}+1}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{\|z^{\gamma^{\prime}}\|^{2}}{n}~\text{for}~|\gamma^{\prime}|=n-1,z^{\gamma^{\prime}}\perp\mathcal{P}_{d_{0}},

\frac{\lambda_{d_{0}+1}}{d_{0}}-\sum_{i=d_{0}+2}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}=\frac{1}{d_{0}(d_{0}+1)!}-\sum_{i=d_{0}+2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(i+1)!}\geq\frac{2}{(d_{0}+3)!}>0,

and

||M_{f_{1}}||\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d_{0}}\lambda_{i}\leq d_{0}+4,

so

\displaystyle||q-pf||

\displaystyle\geq\left\|p_{1}f_{2}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=d_{0}+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}z_{i}p_{1}\right\|

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\|\lambda_{d_{0}+1}z_{d_{0}+1}p_{1}\right\|-\sum_{i=d_{0}+2}^{\infty}||\lambda_{i}z_{i}p_{1}\|

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{d_{0}+1}\frac{\left\|p_{1}\right\|}{\sqrt{n}}-\sum_{i=d_{0}+2}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\left\|p_{1}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{d_{0}+1}\frac{\left\|p_{1}\right\|}{d_{0}}-\sum_{i=d_{0}+2}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\left\|p_{1}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|p_{1}\right\|\left(\frac{\lambda_{d_{0}+1}}{d_{0}}-\sum_{i=d_{0}+2}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{h}{2||M_{f_{1}}||}\left(\frac{\lambda_{d_{0}+1}}{d_{0}}-\sum_{i=d_{0}+2}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{h}{(d_{0}+4)!}.

We conclude

||q-pf||^{2}\geq min\left\{\frac{\|q\|^{2}}{4},\frac{h^{2}}{4},\frac{h^{2}}{(d_{0}+4)!^{2}}\right\}=min\left\{\frac{\|q\|^{2}}{4},\frac{h^{2}}{(d_{0}+4)!^{2}}\right\}.

By the definition of
h^{2},
it is easy to see that

h^{2}\geq\frac{\|q\|^{2}}{4d_{0}^{3}(d_{0}+1)!}.

Then

||q-pf||^{2}\geq\frac{||q||^{2}}{4d_{0}^{3}(d_{0}+1)!(d_{0}+4)!^{2}}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{M}_{n}
contains no nonzero polynomials.

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

The homogeneous submodule
\mathcal{M}_{f}
contains no nonzero ploynomials.

Proof.

Suppose that there is a ploynomial
p
such that
p\in\mathcal{M}_{f},
then there is an
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that

It follows that for all

∎

6

The present section is devoted to the finite defect problem of submodule in

The main result is the following theorem.

.

If
\mathcal{M}

, for

\displaystyle=M_{ma+i_{a}+k}^{*}\sum\limits_{t=1}^{n}\lambda_{t}M_{k}^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{(t)}M_{mt+i+k}E_{r}f\right)

Write

\displaystyle M_{ma+i_{a}+k}^{*}

\displaystyle M_{k}^{*}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{t}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{(t)}M_{mt+i+k}E_{r}f

\displaystyle=M_{ma+i_{a}+k}^{*}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{t}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{(t)}M_{k}^{*}M_{mt+i+k}(f_{4})

) and (

),

(6.5)

On the other hand, by (

\displaystyle M_{k}^{*}(f_{4})\neq 0,

(6.6)

and write

P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}
be the projection from
H^{2}\otimes R
onto
H_{m}^{2}\otimes R.

Considering the linear operator
A_{m}:E\rightarrow H^{2}\left(\partial\mathbb{B}_{m}\right)\otimes R
defined by

A_{m}\zeta=b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi(1\otimes\zeta),\quad\zeta\in E,

where
\Phi
is defined in (
4.10
)
and

b_{m}:H_{m}^{2}\otimes R\rightarrow H^{2}\left(\partial\mathbb{B}_{m}\right)\otimes R

is the natural inclusion mapping.
To continue, we need some lemmas.

Lemma 7.4
.

\displaystyle K_{m}(\mathcal{H})=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{H}-\operatorname{trace}\left(A_{m}A_{m}^{*}\right).

Proof.

Let
\hat{\Phi}=P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes E}.
Then for every
\zeta_{1}\in E,\zeta_{2}\in R,z^{(m)}\in\mathbb{B}_{m}
,

\begin{array}[]{rlc}\langle\hat{\Phi}(z^{(m)})\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\rangle&=\langle\hat{\Phi}\left(1\otimes\zeta_{1}\right),\mathcal{K}_{z^{(m)}}\otimes\zeta_{2}\rangle\\
&=\langle P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes E}\left(1\otimes\zeta_{1}\right),\mathcal{K}_{z^{(m)}}\otimes\zeta_{2}\rangle\\
&=\langle\Phi(1\otimes\zeta_{1}),\mathcal{K}_{z^{(m)}}\otimes\zeta_{2}\rangle\\
&=\langle\Phi(z^{(m)})\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\rangle.\end{array}

So

A_{m}\zeta(z^{(m)})=\hat{\Phi}(1\otimes\zeta)(z^{(m)})=\hat{\Phi}(z^{(m)})\zeta=\Phi(z^{(m)})\zeta,

and for almost
z^{(m)}\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{m},

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}(\Phi(z^{(m)})\Phi(z^{(m)})^{*})=\operatorname{trace}(\Phi(z^{(m)})^{*}\Phi(z^{(m)}))=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\Phi(z^{(m)})\mathfrak{e}_{n}\|^{2}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{m}\mathfrak{e}_{n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})\|^{2},

where
\{\mathfrak{e}_{n}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a basis of
E.

Integrating over
\partial\mathbb{B}_{m},
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}_{m}}\operatorname{trace}(\Phi(z^{(m)})\Phi(z^{(m)})^{*})d\sigma_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}_{m}}\left\|A_{m}\mathfrak{e}_{n}(z^{(m)})\right\|^{2}d\sigma_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|A_{m}\mathfrak{e}_{n}\right\|_{H^{2}\left(\partial\mathbb{B}_{m}\right)\otimes R}^{2}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{trace}\left(A_{m}^{*}A_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{trace}\left(A_{m}A_{m}^{*}\right).

Then by (
4.7
), (
4.8
) and (
4.9
),

\displaystyle K_{m}(\mathcal{H})=\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}_{m}}\operatorname{trace}(I_{R}-\Phi(z^{(m)})\Phi(z^{(m)})^{*})d\sigma_{m}=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{H}-\operatorname{trace}\left(A_{m}A_{m}^{*}\right).

The proof is complete.
∎

Now, we define a linear map
\Gamma_{m}:\mathcal{B}(H_{m}^{2}\otimes R)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{m})\otimes R)
as follows:

\Gamma_{m}(X)=b_{m}Xb_{m}^{*}.

Let
Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{m}
be the canonical operators of the Hardy module
H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{m})\otimes R.

The linear map
d\Gamma_{m}:\mathcal{B}(H_{m}^{2}\otimes R)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{m})\otimes R)
is defined
as follows:

d\Gamma_{m}(X)=\Gamma_{m}(X)-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}Z_{k}\Gamma_{m}(X)Z_{k}^{*}.

Lemma 7.5
.

d\Gamma_{m}\left(P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\right)=A_{m}A_{m}^{*}

Proof.

Notice that for
\xi\in H^{2}\left(\partial\mathbb{B}_{m}\right)\otimes R,\,\eta\in E,

\displaystyle\langle 1\otimes A_{m}^{*}\xi,1\otimes\eta\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle A_{m}^{*}\xi,\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\xi,A_{m}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\xi,b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes E}(1\otimes\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes E}\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}b_{m}{}^{*}\xi,1\otimes\eta\rangle.

It follows that

\displaystyle\langle A_{m}A_{m}^{*}\xi,\eta\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle 1\otimes A_{m}^{*}\xi,1\otimes A_{m}^{*}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes E}\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}b_{m}^{*}\xi,1\otimes A_{m}^{*}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Phi^{*}b_{m}^{*}\xi,1\otimes A_{m}^{*}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Phi^{*}b_{m}^{*}\xi,\left(E_{0}\otimes I_{E}\right)\left(1\otimes A_{m}^{*}\eta\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\left(E_{0}\otimes I_{E}\right)\Phi^{*}b_{m}^{*}\xi,1\otimes A_{m}^{*}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\left(E_{0}\otimes I_{E}\right)\Phi^{*}b_{m}^{*}\xi,P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes E}\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}b_{m}^{*}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes E}\left(E_{0}\otimes I_{E}\right)\Phi^{*}b_{m}^{*}\xi,\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\left(E_{0}\otimes I_{E}\right)\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}b_{m}^{*}\xi,\eta\rangle.

Thus, from the fact that
\Phi\in hom(H^{2}\otimes E,H^{2}\otimes R),

we have

\displaystyle A_{m}A_{m}^{*}

\displaystyle=b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\left(E_{0}\otimes I_{E}\right)\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}b_{m}^{*}

\displaystyle=\left(b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\right)\left(I_{H^{2}\otimes E}-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{T}_{i}\mathfrak{T}_{i}^{*}\right)\left(b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\left(b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\right)\left(b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\right)^{*}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\right)\mathfrak{T}_{i}\mathfrak{T}_{i}^{*}\left(b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{m}\left(P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}Z_{i}\left(b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\right)\left(b_{m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\right)^{*}Z_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{m}\left(P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}Z_{i}\Gamma_{m}\left(P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\right)Z_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=d\Gamma_{m}\left(P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\right),

where
\mathfrak{T}_{i}=S_{z_{i}}\otimes I_{E}.

∎

Set
\phi_{m}(X)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}T_{k}XT_{k}^{*}.

Proof~of~Proposition~\ref{2.151}.
By Lemma
7.5
and
[
3
, Theorem 3.10]
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}(A_{m}A_{m}^{*})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{trace}(d\Gamma_{m}(P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rank\,\mathcal{H}}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{trace}\left(P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}E_{n,m}\right)}{\operatorname{trace}\left(E_{n,m}\right)},

where
E_{n,m}
is the projection from
H_{m}^{2}\otimes R
onto its subspace of homogeneous (vector-valued) functions of degree
n
.
Since
L^{*}L+\Phi\Phi^{*}=I,
by Lemma
7.4
,

\displaystyle K_{m}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{H}-\operatorname{trace}\left(A_{m}A_{m}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{H}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(1-\frac{\operatorname{trace}(P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\Phi\Phi^{*}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}E_{n,m})}{\operatorname{trace}\left(E_{n,m}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{H}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{trace}\left(P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}L^{*}LP_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}E_{n,m}\right)}{\operatorname{trace}\left(E_{n,m}\right)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{trace}\left(P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}L^{*}LP_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}E_{n,m}\right)}{q_{m-1}(n)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{trace}\left(LP_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}E_{n,m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}L^{*}\right)}{q_{m-1}(n)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{trace}\left(\phi_{m}^{n}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)\right)}{q_{m-1}(n)}.

The last equation follows from the reasoning below.
For
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, by Theorem
4.1
,

L^{*}\eta=\left(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1},\cdots\right),\quad\zeta_{n}=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots i_{n}=1}^{\infty}e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{n}}\otimes\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}T_{i_{n}}^{*}\cdots T_{i_{1}}^{*}\eta.

Let
\zeta_{m,n}=\sum\limits_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots i_{n}=1}^{m}e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{n}}\otimes\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}T_{i_{n}}^{*}\cdots T_{i_{1}}^{*}\eta,

then

\displaystyle LP_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}E_{n,m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}L^{*}\eta

\displaystyle=LP_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}E_{n,m}P_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\left(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1},\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=LP_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}E_{n,m}\left(\zeta_{m,0},\zeta_{m,1},\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=LP_{H_{m}^{2}\otimes R}\zeta_{m,n}

\displaystyle=L\zeta_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}=1}^{m}T_{i_{1}}\cdots T_{i_{n}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}T_{i_{n}}^{*}\cdots T_{i_{1}}^{*}\eta

\displaystyle=\phi_{m}^{n}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)\eta.

Therefore

K_{m}(\mathcal{H})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{trace}\left(\phi_{m}^{n}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)\right)}{q_{m-1}(n)}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\operatorname{trace}\left(\phi_{m}^{k}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)\right)}{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}q_{m-1}(k)}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{trace}\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{m}^{k}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)\right)}{q_{m}(n)}.

Finally, we will prove Proposition
4.10
.

Proof~of~Proposition~\ref{3.21}.

Obviously,

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m,n}

\displaystyle=span\left\{f\cdot\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}\zeta\mid f\in\mathcal{P}_{m},\operatorname{deg}f\leq n,\zeta\in H\right\}

\displaystyle=span\left\{T_{i_{1}}\cdots T_{i_{k}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}\zeta\mid\zeta\in H,1\leq i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\leq m,k=0,1,\cdots,n\right\}

is a linear submanifold of
\mathcal{H}.

Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m,n}}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left\{\eta\in\mathcal{H}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{m}\cdots\sum_{i_{k}=1}^{m}|\left\langle T_{i_{1}}\cdots T_{i_{k}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}\zeta,\eta\right\rangle\mid=0,\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{H}\right\}\right.

\displaystyle=\left\{\eta\in\mathcal{H}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{m}\cdots\sum_{i_{k}=1}^{m}\|\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}T_{i_{k}}^{*}\cdots T_{i_{1}}^{*}\eta\|^{2}=0\right\}\right.

\displaystyle=\left\{\eta\in\mathcal{H}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left\langle\phi_{m}^{k}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2})\eta,\eta\right\rangle=0\right\}\right.

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ker}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{m}^{k}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2})\right).

Hence by (
4.29
),

\chi_{m}(\mathcal{H})=m!\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{dim}\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{H}}^{m,n}}{n^{m}}=m!\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{ran}\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{m}^{k}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)\right)}{n^{m}}.

A reproducible EEG–ERP methodology for assessing cognitive decline: integrating P300 latency and resting-state EEG frequency☆
Authors: Wojciech Derkowski, Piotr Derkowski
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEarly detection of cognitive decline remains one of the major challenges in contemporary neurology. Although event-related potentials associated with cognitive processes, particularly the P300 component, as well as resting-state EEG analysis, have long been investigated as potential functional biomarkers of dementia, their clinical application is often limited by methodological heterogeneity and insufficient transparency of signal processing procedures. In this article, we present a reproducible EEG–ERP methodology based on proprietary software for stimulus generation, synchronization, and deterministic averaging of visual event-related potentials, combined with quantitative analysis of resting-state EEG frequency. The approach integrates precise stimulus timing, transparent offline synchronization using an audio marker recorded on the ECG channel, and a fully deterministic averaging procedure. Pilot clinical data obtained from patients with mild cognitive impairment and dementia are included to illustrate the feasibility of the proposed workflow.• Software-controlled visual stimulation with fixed inter-stimulus intervals and explicit offline synchronization using an audio marker.• Deterministic ERP averaging enabling reliable estimation of P300 latency in routine clinical EEG recordings.• Integration of resting-state EEG frequency and P300 latency as interpretable electrophysiological markers of cognitive decline.Keywords: Electroencephalography, Event-related potentials, P300, Mild cognitive impairment, Dementia, Mini-mental state examination, Methodology

Background

The progressive aging of the population is associated with a dynamic increase in the prevalence of dementia syndromes, particularly Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and its prodromal stages, such as mild cognitive impairment (MCI). In this context, there is a growing demand for non-invasive, relatively inexpensive, and reproducible functional biomarkers that could support early diagnosis and monitoring of disease progression [[1], [2], [3]]. Event-related potentials and resting-state electroencephalography have long been used in the assessment of cognitive function. However, methodological heterogeneity in stimulus generation, synchronization procedures, and signal processing pipelines often limits reproducibility and comparability across laboratories [4,5].

The present article describes a transparent and reproducible EEG–ERP methodology designed for implementation in routine clinical settings. The emphasis is placed on explicit timing control, deterministic averaging procedures, and clear documentation of each analytical step. The method integrates analysis of resting-state EEG frequency with estimation of P300 latency within a unified framework.

Event-related potentials associated with cognitive processes, recorded in EEG signals, represent one of the best-established neurophysiological tools for the assessment of cognitive functions. Among them, the P300 component—most commonly elicited using oddball paradigms—reflects processes related to attention allocation and working memory updating. Functionally distinct subcomponents, P3a and P3b, can be identified, with the latter (maximal over parietal regions) being particularly relevant in the context of cognitive functions impaired in the early stages of dementia [6,7].

Numerous studies have demonstrated prolonged P300 latency in patients with AD and MCI, forming a continuum between normal aging, the prodromal stage, and overt dementia. Meta-analyses confirm that P300 latency is a stable and reproducible parameter, whereas amplitude measures are characterized by greater methodological variability [[1], [2], [3]].

An important aspect of P300 research is its sensitivity to methodological configuration. The obtained results are influenced by factors such as stimulus modality (auditory vs. visual), target stimulus probability, inter-stimulus interval, the level of subject engagement (active vs. passive tasks), as well as EEG recording parameters, signal filtering, reference selection, and artifact rejection criteria. For this reason, international guidelines emphasize the need for standardization of ERP recording and analysis procedures in clinical research [[8], [9], [10]].

In recent years, increasing attention has also been paid to the application of the P300 component in the diagnosis of early stages of cognitive impairment, including MCI, as well as to its correlations with neuropsychological test results such as the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), a widely used standardized and validated screening tool for global cognitive function, assessing domains such as orientation, memory, attention, language, and visuospatial abilities. It has been shown that prolonged P300 latency correlates with the severity of cognitive deficits and may reflect slowing of central information processing. At the same time, it is emphasized that the diagnostic value of P300 increases when it is analyzed as part of a multidimensional biomarker model rather than as a single isolated parameter [[11], [12], [13]].

In parallel, it has been demonstrated that slowing of resting-state EEG activity correlates with the severity of cognitive deficits and neuropsychological test performance. Despite the extensive body of literature, the routine clinical use of EEG and ERP remains limited, mainly due to the heterogeneity of stimulation protocols, synchronization methods, and signal processing algorithms [14]. Despite decades of research, the P300 component remains a relevant and promising research tool in cognitive neurology. Its advantages include non-invasiveness, relatively low cost, high temporal resolution, and the possibility of repeated measurements. In the context of contemporary trends, such as the integration of EEG data with advanced signal processing techniques and predictive models, the role of P300 as a functional biomarker of dementia may be further strengthened.

Method details

Hardware and recording environment

Electroencephalographic recordings are performed using a digital EEG system (EB Neuro Neurotravel) with a sampling frequency of 1024 Hz. Standard clinical electrode placement is used. The electrocardiography channel is utilized for recording an external audio synchronization marker.

Resting-state EEG is recorded prior to stimulus presentation under standardized conditions.

Stimulus generation

Visual stimuli are generated using a proprietary Windows-based application (Checker.exe) developed in C# (.NET Framework, Windows Forms).

The stimulation protocol includes a total of 200 visual stimuli, consisting of:

•160 rectangular checkerboard stimuli,

160 rectangular checkerboard stimuli,

•40 triangular checkerboard stimuli [15,16];

40 triangular checkerboard stimuli [15,16];

with a fixed inter-stimulus interval of 1000 ms.

Stimulus timing is controlled using an absolute scheduling mechanism to minimize cumulative drift.

At the onset of each stimulus, a brief audio signal is generated and delivered to the EEG amplifier, where it is recorded in the electrocardiography channel as a temporal marker.

No hardware triggers are used.

Synchronization procedure

The onset of the first stimulus is identified visually using the recorded audio marker in the electrocardiography channel.

The corresponding EEG sample is manually selected as the reference point for epoch segmentation.

Subsequent epochs are defined deterministically based on the fixed 1000 ms inter-stimulus interval.

The theoretical temporal uncertainty introduced by manual marking corresponds to one sampling interval (approximately 0.98 ms at 1024 Hz), which is negligible relative to the latency of the P300 component.

ERP averaging

Averaging of event-related potentials is performed offline using a proprietary application (VEP.exe), also developed in the C# programming language (.NET Framework). The onset of the first stimulus is visually identified based on the recorded audio marker on the electrocardiography channel, and the corresponding EEG sample is manually selected as the starting point for epoch segmentation. Subsequent epochs are defined deterministically based on the known stimulation interval [15,16].

Averaging consists of calculating the point-wise mean of consecutive epochs of fixed duration. If digital filtering was enabled in the application, it is applied prior to the averaging process. Baseline correction is performed by subtracting the value of the first sample of the averaged waveform from all signal samples. No automatic artifact rejection, amplitude-based exclusion criteria, or weighted averaging procedures are applied. The algorithm is fully deterministic.

Due to the manual identification of the onset of the first epoch, a small temporal jitter corresponding to the EEG sampling resolution (1/1024 s, approximately 0.98 ms) may be present; however, given the fixed inter-stimulus interval and the relatively long latency of the P300 component (approximately 300 ms), this effect should be considered negligible for the estimation of P300 latency [4]. P300 latency is defined as the time point of the maximal positive peak within the expected latency window.

P300 amplitude was not analyzed in the present methodology, as the focus of the proposed approach is on latency estimation as a more robust and reproducible parameter.

The averaging procedure produces identical outputs when repeated on the same dataset, ensuring full computational reproducibility.

Resting-state EEG frequency analysis

The dominant frequency of the basic EEG rhythm is determined from resting-state recordings.

The method allows integration of background EEG frequency with ERP-derived P300 latency within the same analytical workflow.

Method validation

To illustrate practical implementation, the method is applied in a pilot group of 20 patients diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment or dementia. All participants were born between 1949 and 1955. Diagnoses were established in accordance with current clinical standards and were confirmed by neuroimaging examinations (computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging), neuropsychological assessment (including the Mini-Mental State Examination, MMSE), routine electroencephalography, and visual evoked potentials (VEP). The MMSE is a 30-point questionnaire commonly used to assess global cognitive function, with lower scores indicating greater cognitive impairment. A control group was not included, as the objective of this study was methodological demonstration rather than comparative clinical analysis.

The study protocol received a positive opinion from the Bioethics Committee of the Opole Regional Medical Chamber (resolution OIL/KB/356/25).

Personal Use and Attitude toward Integrative Medicine and Nursing among Employees at a German University Hospital: Results of a Cross-Sectional Study
Authors: Lena Mohr, Lea Raiber, Johanna Thiele, Klaus Kramer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPrevious studies have shown that integrative medicine (IM) is of considerable relevance from the patients’ perspective. The acceptance of healthcare professionals is essential to meet patient needs and integrate IM into routine clinical practice. The aim of this study was to assess personal use and attitudes toward IM, as well as the implementation of integrative nursing (IN), among employees at a German university hospital.MethodsA cross-sectional online study was conducted among employees at University Hospital Ulm in 2022. Univariate analysis of frequencies and cross-tabulations with chi-square tests were performed.ResultsA total of 244 employees participated in the survey, 80.7% of whom were female and 49.3% were nurses. The mean age was 38.3 ± 12.3 years. Most respondents were familiar with IM (87.3%; n = 179). Overall, 63.2% (n = 129) rated its benefit as high. A total of 26.1% (n = 54) indicated that IN had been implemented in their own work settings, while the majority (91.4%; n = 171) supported broader implementation. Interest in training and education on IN was expressed by 52.4% (n = 98). Subgroup analyses revealed statistically significant associations between age and familiarity with IM (p = 0.039), personal use of IM (p = 0.019), and the rating of its benefits (p = 0.009), as well as between work-related patient contact and interest in further IN training (p < 0.001). No significant associations were observed for sex and profession.ConclusionThe findings indicate a high level of personal use and favorable attitudes toward IM, along with notable interest in IN among hospital employees, highlighting potential for further implementation of IM within clinical practice.

Introduction

Integrative medicine (IM) encompass a range of complementary methods, including nutritional (e.g., herbs remedies), psychological (e.g., mindfulness), and physical (e.g., manual therapies) approaches [1]. Additionally, these approaches are often used individually or in combination [1]. Integrative nursing (IN) represents a relevant component of IM [2, 3], defined in the present study as an external, naturopathic nursing intervention involving poultices and compresses, rhythmic embrocations, therapeutic baths, and washes.

Interest in IM is high in Germany and uptake by different patient groups is increasing [4–7]. A systematic review shows that between 18% and 63% of cancer patients in Germany use IM [7]. Recent German studies confirm this variation in utilization of IM methods, with 46.5% of patients with breast or gynecological cancer having used it [8] and 76% of orthopedic and trauma patients reporting current or past use of IM [9]. As demonstrated by Lederer et al. [10] (2021), the importance of IM is evident in German university hospitals, where 48% of patients use IM for their current disease and 48% request counseling on IM. Therefore, it can be assumed that IM is relevant from the patients’ perspective – as well as within academic and inpatient settings.

Preliminary findings suggest that healthcare professionals within university hospitals are also interested in IM [11, 12]. For example, two-thirds of employees at a German university hospital expressed interest in IM in general, while at the same time only 20% felt adequately informed about IM [12]. A recent study conducted at German university hospitals confirms the positive overall attitude among healthcare professionals toward IM [11]. A significant difference was observed between the professions, with nurses, physiotherapists, and midwives demonstrating a more favorable attitude toward IM compared to physicians; respondents rated their knowledge level about IM as moderate [11].

However, it can be assumed that acceptance of IM by healthcare professionals such as nurses and physicians is imperative to meet patient needs and successfully integrate IM into routine clinical practice. Yet there is a paucity of research on the knowledge, attitudes, and use of IM – and in particular of IN – among healthcare professionals within inpatient settings in Germany. This raises the question of the extent to which existing findings can be applied to the specific context of University Hospital Ulm. Therefore, a survey was conducted among employees at the hospital to assess perspectives on IM and implementation of IN. The objective of the study was threefold: first to evaluate the knowledge and personal use of IM; second to determine the attitudes toward IM; and third to examine the implementation of IN in inpatient care settings. The findings were subsequently compared across subgroups defined by age, sex, profession, and work-related patient contact.

Methods

An anonymous, cross-sectional survey with a quantitative, descriptive research design was carried out among employees at University Hospital Ulm, Germany, within a 4-month period between July and October in 2022. The survey was conducted using an online questionnaire.

Participants

The survey encompassed all professional groups employed at the University Hospital Ulm, including nurses, physicians, and administrative staff. Eligibility criteria for participation included being at least 18 years of age, employment at University Hospital Ulm, sufficient language proficiency, and provision of informed consent. These inclusion criteria were verified through screening questions preceding the survey. The study was planned as a total population survey as all employees of the hospital were invited to participate.

Data Collection

Data collection was conducted using the online survey platform Unipark (Tivian XI GmbH, Cologne, Germany). Participants accessed the questionnaire via a survey link or QR code and submitted their responses anonymously. On average, respondents took 4 min to complete the questionnaire.

Employees were invited to participate in the online survey through multiple channels, including internal hospital mailing lists, news posts on the hospital-wide internal network, and printed materials such as posters and flyers. Participation in the study was limited to employees who had access to the intranet or the QR code provided. Initially, interested employees received more detailed information about the study, including information on data protection. Upon providing online consent for the anonymized processing of their data, participants were redirected to the survey. Participation was entirely voluntary and anonymous.

Questionnaire

A self-developed questionnaire was used to assess the use of and attitudes toward IM and IN. During the development of the questionnaire, the topics and items were discussed and further refined in collaboration with experts from the fields of nursing and medicine. The resulting draft was subsequently subjected to cognitive pretesting with five employees using the think-aloud method to ensure the clarity and relevance of the items [13]. Within the questionnaire, IN was defined as external, naturopathic nursing and healthcare applications, such as compresses, rhythmic embrocations, therapeutic washes, and baths.

The questionnaire consisted of four sections. Before beginning the questionnaire, the inclusion criteria were queried, and the survey was terminated if the criteria were not met. Ethical Approval

Approval for the study was provided by the Ulm University Ethics Committee (488/21). Permission to undertake the survey was granted by the hospital board and staff council. All participants were informed about the study and data collection and gave informed consent before inclusion in the study.

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to analyze the data, including absolute and relative frequencies. Group differences in frequency distributions were assessed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. No datasets were excluded from the analysis due to single missing values, and no imputation was performed. Instead, missing data were addressed using pairwise deletion in the inferential analysis. As a result, the number of cases varies between analyses; this is transparently reported in the Results section for each respective analysis. Data analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 28. The significance level was established as 5% (p < 0.05). Responses to the open-ended question were categorized using content analysis.

Results

A total of 255 employees agreed to participate in the study. However, 9 participants did not meet the inclusion criteria, and 2 participants provided no responses throughout the entire questionnaire. Consequently, 244 participants were included in the final analysis. The completion rate was 73.7%.

Demographic Characteristics

The mean age of the participants was 38.3 ± 12.6 years, ranging from 19 to 63 years. The majority identified as female (80.7%; n = 197). Among the participants, 49.3% (n = 104) were nurses, while only 5.2% (n = 11) were physicians. Approximately two-thirds of the participants (68.1%; n = 141) reported having work-related patient contact. The characteristics of the study population are outlined in Table 2.

IM in General

The majority of the participants (87.3%; n = 179) reported being familiar with IM approaches. Among those who indicated familiarity, 82.6% (n = 147) stated that they had personal experience with IM or had used it themselves. Furthermore, 73.6% (n = 131) reported having applied or recommended IM to others. Participants also rated the perceived benefits of IM: 63.2% respondents (n = 129) perceived a high benefit in IM, while 34.8% (n = 71) reported a minor benefit. Only four respondents (2.0%) indicated no perceived benefit. Reasons for this (multiple response possible) included lack of efficacy (n = 4), potential harm to the patient (n = 1), and not evidence based (n = 1).

IM Approaches Already Used

A total of 128 participants provided information on the IM approaches they had previously used, resulting in 391 documented mentions. The number of methods reported per individual ranged from 1 to 9. As illustrated in Figure 1, biologically based therapies – such as herbal ointments, vitamins, and botanicals – emerged as the most prevalent category (39.1%; n = 153), followed by body-based practices, including embrocations, massages, and chiropractic therapy (31.2%; n = 122). Approaches classified under whole medical systems, such as ayurvedic medicine, and homeopathy, were reported by 16.9% (n = 66). In contrast, biofield therapies and mind-body therapies were mentioned only rarely.

IM and IN within the Inpatient Setting

Participants with work-related patient contact were asked to rate their patients’ receptiveness to IM on a scale from 1 (not at all) to 5 (very open-minded). The average rating was 3.6 (SD = 1.1; Mdn = 3.0). Fewer than half of the respondents (40.1%; n = 83) were aware that IN was implemented on some wards at University Hospital Ulm. A quarter of participants (26.1%; n = 54) reported that IN was implemented in their own work setting. Similar proportions stated that IN was not implemented (24.2%; n = 50), that their work setting did not involve patient care (24.6%; n = 51), or that they lacked sufficient knowledge to assess this (25.1%; n = 52).

Looking to the Future

The majority of respondents (91.4%; n = 171) expressed a desire for the routine implementation of IN at the hospital. At the same time, approximately half of the respondents (52.4%; n = 98) indicated interest in receiving education and training on IN. However, 38.0% (n = 71) indicated that this was not relevant to their current work setting.

Subgroup Analysis

First, analyses were conducted to examine whether subgroups defined by age, sex, profession, and work-related patient contact were associated with familiarity with IM, personal use of IM, and application or recommendation of IM to others. A statistically significant, but weak association was identified between age-group and familiarity with IM (χ2(2) = 6.493; p = 0.039; n = 205; Cramer’s V = 0.178). Overall, 80.0% of young employees (18–30), 90.0% of middle-aged employees (31–45), and 93.2% of older employees (46–67) were familiar with IM. A statistically significant relationship was also identified between age-group and personal use of IM (χ2(2) = 7.968; p = 0.019; n = 178), with a moderate effect size (Cramer’s V = 0.212). The proportion of participants reporting previous personal use of IM increased across age-groups: 73.0% in the 18–30 age-group, 83.3% in the 31–45 group, and 92.7% among those aged 46 and above. No statistically significant associations were found between sex, profession, or work-related patient contact and familiarity with IM, personal use of IM, or application or recommendation of IM to others. Discussion

The objective of the presented study was to assess the personal use of and attitudes toward IM, as well as the implementation of IN, among employees at a German university hospital. The growing utilization of IM – particularly among patients – has made this topic an increasingly important consideration for all healthcare professionals.

In the present study, participant age was evenly distributed across all age-groups, with the majority identifying as female, comprising 80% of the sample. This proportion corresponds to the overall distribution among all employees at University Hospital Ulm [14], as well as results regarding other studies conducted in healthcare settings [11, 15, 16]. The distribution of professional categories in the sample showed notable disparities. Nurses accounted for the largest share of the sample, whereas physicians were considerably underrepresented. Although nurses constitute the largest group of healthcare professionals in clinical settings such as hospitals, the proportion of physicians participating in the present survey remains comparatively low. This trend aligns with other studies, representing physicians also to a lesser extent than nurses [11, 15]. The lower participation of physicians may be related to their greater reluctance toward IM [11, 12]. Nonetheless, the number of physicians included in this study remains limited and should be interpreted with caution.

The personal user rate of IM in the present study was high, at approximately 80%. This finding aligns with the results of previous studies, which reported that 76% of employed adults in the US healthcare system use IM personally [17] and 85.7% of healthcare professionals at a hospital in Mexico use IM [18]. Moreover, the utilization rate observed in this study exceeds that reported for the German general population. A representative survey found that 69.6% of Germans had used IM at least once in their lifetime [19]. A substantial body of research has documented a high IM usage among healthcare professionals, particularly nurses [20, 21]. This utilization pattern is characterized by healthcare professionals’ greater frequency of IM use in comparison to the general population [17]. However, variations within the definition of IM across studies pose challenges for direct comparison, and this needs to be taken into account when interpreting the present results in context of the existing literature.

The present results demonstrate a significant relationship between age-group and familiarity with IM, personal use of IM, and rating of its benefits. This finding is consistent with a representative study of the German general population, which also reported an age-related association with IM utilization [19]. However, the frequency of IM use is increasing with age [19]. In contrast, a study focusing on healthcare professionals found no such association between age and IM-related characteristics [22]. One possible explanation for the observed relationship in the present study is that older individuals are more likely to suffer from chronic health conditions and have longer medical treatment histories, which may lead them to use CIM to manage persistent symptoms and improve their quality of life. Another possible explanation is that the duration of exposure increases with age, making older individuals more likely to use IM.

In the present survey, biologically based therapies and body-based practices were identified as the most frequently utilized approaches of IM. This finding aligns with existing literature, which consistently highlights biologically based approaches – including herbal supplements and vitamins – as being the most prevalent among both general populations and cancer patients [6, 23–25]. This trend is further substantiated by a study of Australian nursing staff [20]. However, in contrast to the present results, this study identified a contribution of mind-body therapies [20]. The high prevalence of biologically based therapies in the current sample may be explained by their easy accessibility, broad acceptance, and the possibility of self-administration without professional supervision. Additionally, naturopathy, particularly phytotherapy, has a strong tradition and is widely accepted within the German healthcare context.

Fewer than half of the employees were aware of the existence of IN at the hospital. Additionally, only one-quarter reported that IN has been implemented in their own work settings. In light of the findings, it is recommended that the dissemination of knowledge regarding the existing IN approaches at the hospital should be furthered. The objective is threefold: to promote understanding of IN, to raise awareness, and to support its integration into everyday clinical practice.

Moreover, the survey results indicated a predominant preference among the majority of the participants for the regular implementation of IN. This favorable disposition toward the implementation of IN forms a trend that portends the approval of the future expansion of IN. Hall et al. [26] (2017) conducted a systematic review and found that nurses experience elevated levels of job satisfaction when they have the opportunity to practice IM on patients. This expanded range of actions and therapies has been shown to engender new competencies and appreciation for nursing. Given the absence of widespread implementation of IN at the University Hospital Ulm and the pronounced employee-driven demand for its integration, considerable potential exists for future development in this area.

The present study results indicated a notable interest in training or education related to IN, regarding approximately half of all respondents expressing such interest. Among nursing staff, this proportion increased to three-quarters, indicating a particularly elevated level of interest in this topic. This finding is consistent with previous studies, which reported that healthcare professionals, particularly nurses, have expressed a strong desire for more information on IM in general [11, 26, 27]. A recent study from Australia found that nearly 90% of healthcare professionals at a major cancer center expressed interest in learning more about IM [28]. In addition, healthcare professionals are obligated to possess a sufficient understanding of IM to effectively inform patients with adequate information and provide guidance if necessary [22, 29]. The pursuit of continuing education and training in IM has the potential to broaden one’s skill set, particularly in the domain of IM consulting.

Furthermore, employees with work-related patient contact showed greater interest in training or education on IN, indicating a statistically significant association. This finding appears plausible as expanding competencies in this area is particularly relevant for staff members directly involved in patient care. A nationwide study among oncology providers in Germany showed a high demand for IM-related education, indicating that healthcare professionals value IM knowledge as essential for clinical practice [30].

In addition to the previously described associations of age-group and work-related patient contact, no significant relationship was identified in the present study regarding sex or professional role. The findings suggest that the experience and knowledge of IM are largely independent of sex. This is consistent with results regarding patient-based studies, which also found no statistically significant sex differences interest in IM [5]. However, contradictory findings have been reported in the extant literature. For instance, female employees at a university clinic exhibited a greater interest in IM [12], and a similar trend in attitudes toward IM has been observed among medical and pharmacy students [31, 32]. In another study, skepticism toward IM was reduced among female German healthcare professionals [11]. The increased utilization of IM by women in the general population has been well documented [19, 33]. These discrepancies with the present findings may, however, be influenced by the relatively low number of male participants in the sample, who may represent a subgroup with a more favorable attitude toward IM.

Likewise, no statistically significant associations were identified between profession groups (physicians vs. nurses) and the use, evaluation, experience, or interest in IM. In contrast, previous studies have reported that nurses tend to show stronger support for IM, whereas physicians often exhibit lower levels of interest [12], are less favorable [27] – particularly in the context of German university hospitals [11]. The discrepancy between the present findings and those reported in the existing literature may be explained by the low participation rate of physicians in this survey. It is conceivable that the small number of physicians who did participate were particularly open to IM, thereby introducing a potential selection bias.

Limitations

However, it is important to acknowledge the limitations of this survey when interpreting the results. First, the study was conducted only at University Hospital Ulm, which may limit the generalizability of the results to other institutions and healthcare settings. While a total population survey was initially planned, technical issues prevented email distribution to all employees, leaving the actual reach of the survey unknown. As a result, the response rate could not be accurately calculated, though it is estimated to be below 10%. Since not all employees had an equal opportunity to participate, the sample must be classified as a non-probability convenience sample. A validated questionnaire suitable for the study objectives was not available. Accordingly, a self-developed questionnaire was employed, which was pretested using the think-aloud method. To enhance participation, the questionnaire was kept concise. As a result, this limited the depth of inquiry and prevented a more nuanced exploration of certain topics. Future studies should aim to investigate various IM approaches in greater detail. In order to retain as much information as possible, cases with missing values were not excluded from all analyses. Consequently, sample size and composition varied depending on specific analysis. Moreover, the potential for bias must be acknowledged. For example, employees with a particular interest in IM may have been more likely to participate, introducing a potential selection bias.

Conclusion

This study provides insights into the personal use of and attitudes toward IM, as well as the implementation of IN, among employees at a German university hospital. The study’s findings indicate that personal use of IM is widespread, with biologically based therapies and body-based practices being the most frequently used approaches. A substantial level of interest in IN-related training was also observed, particularly among employees with work-related patient contact. Future research should focus specifically on profession groups, such as nursing staff, and on the practical aspects of IM and IN implementation in routine clinical practice to enable a more comprehensive evaluation.

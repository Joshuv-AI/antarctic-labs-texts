Schauder estimates for germs of distributions on smooth manifolds
Beatrice Costeri, Claudio Dappiaggi, Paolo Rinaldi, Matteo Savasta

Abstract
We discuss germs of distributions on d − d- dimensional smooth Riemannian manifolds and, in particular, we derive multi-level Schauder estimates without making any further assumptions on the underlying geometry. As a preliminary step, we define the notions of coherence and homogeneity for germs of distributions on open subsets of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 . Subsequently, we formulate both the reconstruction theorem , cf. , [ CZ20 ] , and the Schauder estimates, cf. , [ BCZ24 ] , in this setting. Leveraging the properties of the exponential map, we extend these results to Riemannian manifolds. Specifically, we devise a counterpart of the reconstruction theorem previously established in the literature [ RS21 ] , while additionally proving the regularity of the reconstructed distribution in suitable Hölder-Zygmund spaces. Finally, by introducing a novel concept of β \beta -regularizing kernels on Riemannian manifolds, we establish Schauder estimates for coherent and homogeneous germs in this context.

Introduction

A major advance in the solution theory for singular stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, is the
theory of regularity structures
, see
[
Hai14
]
. In this seminal paper, Hairer develops a highly abstract framework which allows to establish local existence and uniqueness of solutions to a broad class of stochastic PDEs, including the Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation,
cf.
,
[
KPZ86
]
. Two cornerstones of Hairer’s regularity structures are the
reconstruction theorem
and the
multi-level Schauder estimates
, see
[
Hai14
, Thms 1.3 & 1.6]
. The latter, in particular, play a crucial rôle in the analysis of elliptic and parabolic PDEs, as they serve as an essential tool in fixed-point arguments.

Yet, despite its high level of generality, the theory of regularity structures does not incorporate the degree of concreteness required for establishing a close connection to several physically inspired models. Most notably, in sharp contrast to quantum field theory,
cf.
,
[
BFD+15
,
BF09
]
, this approach does not provide a concrete mean to construct explicitly the solution and the correlation functions of a nonlinear singular SPDE. This motivated the development of a complementary approach, inspired by algebraic quantum field theory,
cf.
,
[
FR16
]
and designed to perform explicit, perturbative computations even when the underlying background is not flat, see
[
DDR+20
,
BDR23
,
BDR24
,
BCD+24
]
. Yet this novel method suffers of a severe limitation, namely the impossibility of proving convergence of the underlying perturbative series. Hence, it is highly desirable to establish a connection with the theory of regularity structures and, to this end, to develop counterparts of both the reconstruction theorem and Schauder estimates on generic backgrounds.

In order to achieve this goal, it is necessary to translate these two key results in a language better suited to a formulation on manifolds. This issue is naturally addressed by working with germs of distributions. Since these are intrinsically local objects, this formulation yields a framework that can be naturally extended to smooth manifolds.
Specifically, given a
germ of distributions
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, namely, a family
(F_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, one might wonder whether there exists a globally defined distribution
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

f\approx F_{x}\;\;\text{in a neighborhood of}\;\;x,

where the approximation is to be understood in a suitable sense, see
[
CZ20
]
. In this instance, the germ
(F_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is said to be a
local approximation
of
f
. For instance, if
f
is generated by a
C^{r}
function,
r\geq 0
, the associated germ is the Taylor polynomial of degree
r
centered at a point
x
, see Example
2.10
.
However, under milder regularity assumptions on the global distribution
f
, the existence and uniqueness of a reconstruction in terms of
(F_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is far less trivial. Indeed, in
[
CZ20
]
, it was proven that, under the additional requirement that the germ is
coherent
, such a reconstruction exists. The counterpart of this result on smooth manifolds has been already discussed in
[
RS21
]
, yet in this work we shall prove a version of the reconstruction theorem on Riemannian manifolds, see Theorem
2.11
, tailored to a slightly more general notion of germ. This will allow us to assess in addition the continuity of the reconstruction operator in suitable Hölder-Zygmund spaces, see Section
5.3
.

More recently, in
[
BCZ24
]
, also multi-level Schauder estimates have been established within this distributional framework. These stem from the observation that the convolution between a distribution and a kernel that has an integrable singularity at the origin improves the regularity of the resulting object. Examples of kernels abiding by this property include the heat kernel as well as the Green’s functions of several linear differential operators. Most notably, such a regularization property emerges also when working at the level of germs of distributions,
cf.
,
[
Hai14
, Thm. 1.6]

In this context, the main idea of Schauder estimates, is that, given a singular kernel
\mathsf{K}
associated with a differential operator, there exists an integration map
\mathcal{K}
between germs such that, locally, the following approximation property holds true in a suitable sense:

f\approx F_{x}\;\;\text{at}\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\Rightarrow\mathsf{K}*f\approx\mathcal{K}F_{x}\;\;\text{at}\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Expanding on these concepts, we establish the main results of this paper. These are codified in the following two theorems, concerning Schauder estimates for germs of distributions on Riemannian manifolds, see Theorems
6.1
and
6.2
as well as Section

More precisely, exploiting the fact that the exponential map is a local diffeomorphism, one can work on open subsets of the tangent space and, then, pull back to the corresponding geodesically convex sets on the Riemannian manifold. To this avail, we develop novel tools to scale and re-center test functions on Riemannian manifolds, see Appendix
C

[

, we do not make any assumption on the structure of the kernel near the singular points of the manifold,

[

\psi\in\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}_{\gamma}\end{array}$}}}\lvert(F_{x}-F_{y})(\psi_{x}^{D^{U}_{K}})\rvert+\sup_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda}],\hskip 2.21254ptx,y\in K\\
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}\end{array}$}}}\frac{\lvert(F_{x}-F_{y})(\varphi^{\lambda}_{x})\rvert}{\lambda^{\alpha_{K}}(\lambda+\lvert x-y\rvert)^{\gamma-\alpha_{K}}}.

(2.4)

Remark 2.3

As discussed in
[
RS21
]
, the introduction of the parameter
D_{K}^{U}:=\mathrm{dist}(K,\partial U)
is necessary since, after the rescaling of the test-function,

\operatorname{supp}(\varphi^{\lambda}_{x})\subset B(x,\lambda)
. In order for
F_{x}(\varphi_{x}^{\lambda})
to be meaningful we need that

\operatorname{supp}(\varphi^{\lambda}_{x})\subset B(x,\lambda)\subset U
, which is
granted by the above construction.

Remark 2.4

Given a germ
F
as per Definition
2.1
, it is equivalent to
state that it is
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\gamma)
-coherent of order

\boldsymbol{r}
as per Definition
2.2
or that, for every compact set,
K\subset U
,
\bar{\lambda}\in(0,D_{K}^{U})
,

\lvert(F_{x}-F_{y})(\varphi^{\lambda}_{x})\rvert\lesssim_{K}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda^{\alpha_{K}}&\text{if}\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\lambda\leqslant\lvert x-y\rvert\\
\lambda^{\gamma}&\text{if}\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\lambda>\lvert x-y\rvert\end{array}\right.,

(2.5)

uniformly for
x,y\in K
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}
,
\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda}]
.

In addition to coherence it is convenient to introduce a second property of germs which goes under the name of
homogeneity
.

Definition 2.5

(Homogeneity)

In the same setting of Definition
2.1
,
given two families
\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\doteq(\bar{\alpha}_{K})_{K\subset U}\subset\mathbb{R}
, and
\boldsymbol{r}\doteq(r_{K})_{K\subset U}\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
, indexed by compact sets
K\subset U
, we say that
F
is

•

\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}
-homogeneous of order

\boldsymbol{r}
, if for any
K\subset U
, and for any
\bar{\lambda}\in(0,D_{K})
with
D_{K}^{U}:=\mathrm{dist}(K,\partial U)
,

\lvert F_{x}(\varphi^{\lambda}_{x})\rvert\lesssim\lambda^{\bar{\alpha}_{K}},

(2.6)

uniformly over
x\in K
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}
and
\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda}]
. The space of
\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}
-homogeneous
germs of order
\boldsymbol{r}
over
U
is denoted by

\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
and it is
endowed with the family of seminorms

\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\ni F\mapsto\|F\|_{\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}:=\sup_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda}]\\
x\in K\\
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}\end{array}$}}}\frac{\lvert F_{x}(\varphi^{\lambda}_{x})\rvert}{\lambda^{\bar{\alpha}_{K}}},

(2.7)

•

weakly
\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}
-homogeneous of order

\boldsymbol{r}
, denoted by
F\in\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
, if, for
any compact subset
K\subset U
and for any
\bar{\lambda}\in(0,D^{U}_{K})
,

\lvert F_{x}(\psi_{x}^{D^{U}_{K}})\rvert\lesssim 1\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\lvert F_{x}(\varphi^{\lambda}_{x})\rvert\lesssim\lambda^{\bar{\alpha}_{K}},

(2.8)

uniformly over
x\in K
,
\psi\in\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}_{\bar{\alpha}_{K}}
and
\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda}]
.
Here
\psi_{x}^{D^{U}_{K}}
and
\varphi^{\lambda}_{x}
are as per Equation
(
1.1
), while the spaces
\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}
and
\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}_{\bar{\alpha}_{K}}
are as per Equations (
1.3
) and (
1
), respectively. We equip

\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
with the
family of seminorms

\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\ni F\mapsto\|F\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}:=\sup_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}[]{c}\hskip 2.21254ptx\in K\\
\psi\in\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}\end{array}$}}}\lvert F_{x}(\psi_{x}^{D^{U}_{K}})\rvert+\sup_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda}],\hskip 2.21254ptx\in K\\
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}_{\bar{\alpha}_{K}}\end{array}$}}}\frac{\lvert F_{x}(\varphi^{\lambda}_{x})\rvert}{\lambda^{\bar{\alpha}_{K}}}.

(2.9)

Remark 2.6

Note that, under the hypotheses of Definition

2.5
, if
F
has positive homogeneity, that is if
\bar{\alpha}_{K}>0
for all
K\subset U
, then

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lvert F_{x}(\varphi^{\lambda}_{x})\rvert=0.

This scenario is not of interest for the forthcoming construction and,
therefore, we shall focus mainly on the cases where
\bar{\alpha}_{K}\leqslant 0
.

At this point, we can merge together Definitions
2.2
and
2.5
to devise a notion of coherent and homogeneous germs on
U
.

Definition 2.7

(Coherent and homogeneous germs)

Under the hypotheses of
Definition
2.1
, a germ
F
is called

•

(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\gamma)
-coherent with homogeneity

\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}
of order
\boldsymbol{r}
if Equations (
2.1
) and (
2.6
) hold true. The space of such germs
is denoted
\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U):=\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\cap\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
. This space
is endowed with the family of seminorms

\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\ni F\mapsto\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}:=\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}+\|F\|_{\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}.

(2.10)

•

weakly
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\gamma)
-coherent with homogeneity

\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}
of order
\boldsymbol{r}
if Equations (
2.3
) and (
2.8
) hold true. The space of
such germs is denoted
\check{\mathcal{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)=\check{\mathcal{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\cap\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
and it is
endowed with the family of seminorms

\check{\mathcal{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\ni F\mapsto\|F\|_{\check{\mathcal{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}:=\|F\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}+\|F\|_{\check{\mathcal{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}.

(2.11)

Remark 2.8

Observing that the difference between coherence (
resp
. homogeneity) and weak
coherence (
resp
. weak homogeneity) only concerns the classes of test functions
involved, if for all compact sets
K\subset U
, it holds that

\bar{\alpha}_{K}<0
, then

\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)=\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
(
resp
., whenever

\gamma<0
,

\check{\mathcal{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)=\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
). This entails
that the notions of weak homogeneity and weak coherence become relevant only if
\bar{\alpha}_{K}\geqslant 0
and
\gamma\geqslant 0
, respectively.

It is worth discussing two notable examples of germs. Of particular
relevance is the second one since, heuristically speaking, germs could be
thought as the counterpart at the level of distributions of what Taylor series
are for functions of positive regularity.

Example 2.9

(Constant germ)

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set and consider a distribution
f\in\mathcal{Z}^{\bar{\alpha}}(U)
, as per Definition
1.3
. Set
F_{x}:=f
for any
x\in U
. Then, for any
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, the constant germ
F
is
(\alpha,\gamma)
-coherent as per
Definition
2.2
and
\bar{\alpha}
-homogeneous as per
Definition
2.5
.

Example 2.10

(Taylor Polynomials)

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
\gamma>0
. Given a
function
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}(U)
, for all compact sets
K\subset U
,

\lvert f(y)-\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\gamma}f(y)\rvert\lesssim_{K}\lvert x-y\rvert^{\gamma},

where
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\gamma}f
is the Taylor polynomial of
f
at
x
,
i.e.
,

\mathcal{T}^{\gamma}_{x}f(y):=\sum_{0\leqslant\lvert k\rvert<\lfloor\gamma\rfloor}(\partial^{k}f)(x)\frac{(y-x)^{k}}{k!},

(2.12)

where
k=(k_{1}\ldots k_{d})
is a multi-index with
|k|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}
. We prove that
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\gamma}f
is a
(0,\gamma)
-coherent
germ of order
0
and that, for all compact set
K\subset U
,

\|\mathcal{T}^{\gamma}_{x}f\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{0}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{0}}(U),K}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}(U)},

where the seminorms above are defined in Equations (
1.2
) and (
2.2
). Fixing an arbitrary compact set
K\subset U
, for
0\leqslant\lvert k\rvert\leqslant\gamma
,
\partial^{k}f\in\mathcal{C}^{\gamma-\lvert k\rvert}(U)
. If we consider the Taylor
expansion of
\partial^{k}f(x)
on the right hand side of Equation
(
2.12
) around a point
z\in K

\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\gamma}f(y)=\sum_{\lvert k\rvert<\gamma}\left(\sum_{\lvert l\rvert\leqslant\gamma-\lvert k\rvert}\partial^{k+l}f(z)\frac{(x-z)^{l}}{l!}+R^{k}(x,z)\right)\frac{(y-x)^{k}}{k!},

(2.13)

where
R^{k}(x,z)
is the Lagrange remainder. Standard results in calculus
entail that

\partial^{k}f(x)-\mathcal{T}_{z}^{\gamma-\lvert k\rvert}\partial^{k}f(x)=R^{k}(x,z)\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\text{with}\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\lvert R^{k}(x,z)\rvert\leqslant\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}(U)}\lvert x-z\rvert^{\gamma-\lvert k\rvert},

(2.14)

uniformly for
x,z\in K
. Switching from
l
to
k^{\prime}:=k+l
we can
rewrite Equation (
2.13
) as

\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\gamma}f(y)=\sum_{\lvert k^{\prime}\rvert<\gamma}\partial^{k^{\prime}}f(z)\left(\sum_{k\leqslant k^{\prime}}\frac{(x-z)^{k^{\prime}-k}}{(k^{\prime}-k)!}\frac{(y-x)^{k}}{k!}\right)+\\
\sum_{\lvert k\rvert<\gamma}R^{k}(x,z)\frac{(y-x)^{k}}{k!}\\
=\mathcal{T}_{z}^{\gamma}f(y)+\sum_{\lvert k\rvert<\gamma}R^{k}(x,z)\frac{(y-x)^{k}}{k!}.

(2.15)

As a consequence,

\mathcal{T}_{z}^{\gamma}f(y)-\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\gamma}f(y)=-\sum_{\lvert k\rvert<\gamma}R^{k}(x,z)\frac{(y-x)^{k}}{k!},

(2.16)

and, exploiting Equation (
2.14
), it follows that

\lvert\mathcal{T}_{z}^{\gamma}f(y)-\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\gamma}f(y)\rvert\leqslant\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}(U)}\sum_{\lvert k\rvert<\gamma}\lvert y-x\rvert^{\lvert k\rvert}\lvert x-z\rvert^{\gamma-\lvert k\rvert}.

Therefore, for any test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}^{0}
, see Equation
(
1.3
), it descends that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{T}_{z}^{\gamma}f(y)-\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\gamma}f(y))\varphi^{\lambda}_{x}(y)\mathrm{d}y\right|\leqslant\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}(U)}\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}(U)}\sum_{n<\gamma}\lvert z-x\rvert^{\gamma-n}\lambda^{n},

(2.17)

uniformly for
x,z\in K
with
\lambda\in(0,D_{K}^{U})
, see Definition

2.5
. This entails the sought conclusion.

2.1
The reconstruction theorem

Goal of this section is to present one of the main results concerning the theory of germs
of distributions, the
reconstruction theorem
, here specialized to open sets
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
. We refer the reader to
[
CZ20
]
for the statement of this theorem on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geqslant 1
and to
[
RS21
]
for its generalization to Riemannian manifolds.
Henceforth, for the sake of conciseness, we shall omit all proofs which can be found in the aforementioned references.

For later convenience, we give the following preliminary definitions. Given a compact subset
K\subset U
and
\epsilon>0
we call

\epsilon
-enlargement
of
K
the set

K_{\epsilon}:=K+\overline{B(0,\epsilon)},

(2.18)

where
B(0,\epsilon)
is the open ball centered at the origin of radius

\epsilon
. In addition, we set

\mathrm{Diam}(K)=\max_{x,y\in K}|x-y|.

(2.19)

We can now state the crucial result of this section.

Theorem 2.11

(Reconstruction on open sets)

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
be an open set and let
F\in\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
be an
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\gamma)-
coherent germ of order
\boldsymbol{r}
on
U
as per
Definition
2.2
. Given a compact set
K\subset U
, we denote by

\tilde{K}:=K_{3D_{K}^{U}/4},\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004ptm_{K}:=\max\{\lfloor-\alpha_{\tilde{K}}+1\rfloor,r_{\tilde{K}}+d+1\},

(2.20)

where
K_{3D_{K}^{U}/4}
is as per Equation (
2.18
). There
exists
\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U)
, called the

reconstruction of
F
, such that, for every compact set
K\subset U
and for
every
\bar{\lambda}\in(0,D_{K}^{U})
,

\lvert(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F-F_{z})(\varphi^{\lambda}_{z})\rvert\lesssim_{\alpha_{\tilde{K}},r_{\tilde{K}},\bar{\lambda}}\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),\tilde{K}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda^{\gamma}&\text{for}\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\gamma\neq 0\\
\log(1+\frac{1}{\lambda})&\text{for}\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\gamma=0\end{array}\right.

(2.21)

uniformly for
x\in K
,
\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda})
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}^{r_{K}}
, see Equations (
1.1
) and (
1.3
). In addition,

1.

if
\gamma>0
, the reconstruction is the unique distribution in

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U)
, such that

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lvert(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F-F_{z})(\varphi^{\lambda}_{z})\rvert=0.

2.

for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, the
reconstruction can always be chosen in such a way that
\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}:\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U)
is linear.

Barring minor modifications,
the proof of Theorem
2.11
follows slavishly from that of
[
CZ20
, Thm. 5.1]
, hence we omit it.

Remark 2.12

A direct consequence of Theorem
2.11
is that, if
\gamma\neq 0
, on a compact set

K\subset U
, the germ
F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F
is
\gamma
-homogeneous
of order
m_{K}
, see Equation (
2.20
), as well as
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\gamma)
-coherent of order
r_{K}
. On account of
[
BCZ24
, Prop. B.1]
, it
follows that
F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F
is coherent and homogeneous of
order
\bar{r}_{K}:=\lfloor\max\{-\alpha_{K},-\gamma\}+1\rfloor=\lfloor-\alpha_{K}\rfloor
. Comparing Equation (
2.21
) with Equation (
2.6
), it descends that

\|F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F\|_{\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}\lesssim_{\alpha_{\tilde{K}},r_{\tilde{K}},\bar{\lambda}}\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),\tilde{K}},

(2.22)

where
\tilde{K}
is as per Equation (
2.20
). Suppose now that

F
is
\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-homogeneous, with
\bar{\alpha}_{K}\leqslant\gamma
for all compact sets
K\subset U
. Putting together Equations
(
2.6
) and (
2.22
), and using
the triangle inequality, it follows that the constant germ

\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F
satisfies the following homogeneity estimate

\|\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F\|_{\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}}(U),K}\lesssim_{\alpha_{\tilde{K}},r_{\tilde{K}},\bar{\lambda}}\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),\tilde{K}}+\|F\|_{\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}\leqslant\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),\tilde{K}}.

(2.23)

At last, we establish a result on the Hölder regularity
of the reconstruction defined as per Theorem
2.11
.

Corollary 2.13

(Hölder regularity of the reconstruction)

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
F=(F_{x})_{x\in U}
be a germ as per Definition
2.1
.

1.

If
F\in\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
, as per Definition

2.7
, then, fixed a compact set
K\subset U
, for every open set
U_{K}\subset K
, there exists
\tilde{\alpha}_{\tilde{K}}
such that

\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F\in\mathcal{Z}^{\tilde{\alpha}_{\tilde{K}}}(U_{K}),\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\text{and}\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\|\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F\|_{\mathcal{Z}^{\tilde{\alpha}_{\tilde{K}}}_{\tilde{K},\bar{\lambda}}}\leqslant\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),\tilde{K}}+\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),\tilde{K}},

(2.24)

where
\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F
is as per Theorem
2.11
while
\mathcal{Z}^{\tilde{\alpha}_{K}}(U_{K})
is as per
Definition
1.3
.

2.

If
F\in\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
, with
\gamma\leqslant 0
, and
f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U)
are two
reconstructions of
F
, namely Equation (
2.21
) holds
true for
f
and
f^{\prime}
in place of
\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F
, then

f-f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\gamma}(U)\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\text{and}\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{Z}^{\gamma}_{K,\bar{\lambda}}}\leqslant\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),\tilde{K}}.

(2.25)

The proof of the above corollary is a minor adaptation of that on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
discussed in
[
CZ20
, Thm. 12.7 & Rem. 12.9]
, thus we omit it.

3
Schauder Estimates for Germs on Open Subsets of

\mathbb{R}^{d}

In this section, we shall discuss multi-level Schauder estimates for germs of distributions on
open subsets
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
. The crucial result of this part is Theorem
3.6
, whose counterpart on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has been discussed in
[
BCZ24
, Thm. 3.17]
.

As a preliminary step, we need to recall the notion of
regularizing kernel
, which will prove essential for the ensuing investigation.

Definition 3.1

(
\beta-
regularizing kernel)

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geqslant 1
,
m,r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta,\rho>0
. We say that
\mathrm{K}:U\times U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
\beta
-regularizing kernel of order

(m,r)
with range
\rho
if

\mathrm{K}(x,y)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{K}_{n}(x,y),\qquad\operatorname{for}a.e.\quad x,y\in U.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathrm{K}_{n}\in C^{m,r}(U\times U)
,
i.e., it is
m
-times differentiable with respect to the first variable and

r
-times with respect to the second one. In addition each
\mathrm{K}_{n}
is such that, for any compact set

K\subset U
,

1.

\operatorname{supp}(\mathrm{K}_{n})\subset\{(x,y)\in U\times U|\hskip 2.70004pt|x-y|\leqslant\rho 2^{-n}\}
.

2.

for any
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
such
that
|k|\leqslant m
and
|\ell|\leqslant r
and for any
x,y\in K
, there exists
c_{K}>0
such that

|\partial_{1}^{k}\partial_{2}^{\ell}\mathrm{K}_{n}(x,y)|\leqslant c_{K}2^{(d-\beta+|\ell|+|k|)n}

where
\partial_{1},\partial_{2}
denote the partial derivatives with respect to the variables
x

and
y
, respectively.

3.

for all
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
such that

|k|,|\ell|\leqslant r
and for any
x,y\in K
, there exists
c_{K}>0
such that

\left|\int_{U}(y-x)^{\ell}\partial_{2}^{k}\mathrm{K}_{n}(x,y)\mathrm{d}x\right|\leqslant c_{K}2^{-\beta n},

where
(y-x)^{\ell}:=(y_{1}-x_{1})^{\ell_{1}}\ldots(y_{d}-x_{d})^{\ell_{d}}
.

Given a
\beta
-regularizing kernel
\mathrm{K}
as per previous definition, a natural question concerns the
possibility of establishing an interplay between its action and the theory of
germs on open sets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
outlined in Section
2
. Heuristically, let
(F_{x})_{x\in U}
be an
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\gamma)
-coherent germ with
\gamma>0
as per
Definition
2.1
abiding by the hypotheses of Theorem
2.11
. Hence, item
1.
in Theorem
2.11
entails that the reconstruction
\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U)
is
unique. Under the assumption that the convolution
\mathrm{K}\star\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F
is well-defined, one might wonder whether there exists
a corresponding map
\mathcal{K}
acting on the space of germs and yielding a
novel germ of distributions such that
\mathrm{K}\star\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F=\mathcal{R}^{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathcal{K}(F)
. Here we use the symbol
\gamma^{\prime}
to
highlight that,
a priori
, the coherence parameter of the new germ
might change. Yet, the formalization of this intuitive argument within our setting is not as straightforward as one might think.

Remark 3.2

In
[
BCZ24
]
this issue is addressed and solved
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and, under the same assumptions taken by these authors, our
result might appear to be weaker,
cf.

[
BCZ24
, Thm. 3.17]
but this is not entirely true. On the one hand, this is because their proof relies on a large scale
decomposition of test functions, which does not admit a straightforward
counterpart on bounded open sets,
cf.

[
BCZ24
, Thm. 5.6 & Lem.
5.11]
. On the other hand, we shall consider homogeneous and coherent
germs as per Definitions
2.2
and
2.5
,
where we allow the parameters
\alpha_{K}
and
\bar{\alpha}_{K}
to depend on
an underlying compact subset
K
, contrary to
[
BCZ24
]
where the
counterparts are taken to be constant. This allows to bypass the issue
mentioned above with reference to the large scale decomposition.
Furthermore, when working on a Riemannian manifold
M
, the absence of a
global chart leads naturally to considering parameters
\alpha_{K}
and

\bar{\alpha}_{K}
depending on an underlying compact subset and, unless the
manifold
M
is compact itself, we cannot work with
\inf_{K\subset M}\alpha_{K}
or with
\inf_{K\subset M}\bar{\alpha}_{K}
as they might not be
finite.

In view of this remark, we feel that, although the proof
of the following results is similar in many aspects to that given in

[
BCZ24
]
, the differences shall become so relevant in the following
sections that delving into the details is justified. To avoid a lengthy and not
so user friendly statement we shall divide it in several propositions. Furthermore, we set most of the notation and of the conventions before the main
statements. More precisely, for all
\rho>0
and given a bounded open subset

U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we introduce the restriction

U^{\rho}:=\{x\in U\,|\,\text{dist}(x,\partial U)>\rho\}.

(3.1)

With
K
we shall denote instead a compact subset such that
K\subset U^{2\rho}
, while
K^{\prime}
is the smallest convex compact set containing
K
. In addition, let

\tilde{K}^{\prime}:=K^{\prime}_{3D_{K}^{U^{\rho}}/4}
, see Equation
(
2.18
) with

. At last, given
\alpha_{K}\in\mathbb{R}
and

, we define

\bar{\alpha}_{K}:=\min\{\alpha_{K},-r_{K}-d\}
.

We start by
stating two preliminary results which will play a role in the proof of the
main results of this section.

The first of these states that under suitable assumptions the integration of a
coherent (
resp.
homogeneous) germ against a
\beta
-regularising kernel produces a
weakly coherent (
resp.
weakly homogeneous) germ with coherence (
resp.
homeogeneity) increased by

\beta
. As the proof follows the steps of
[
BCZ24
, Thm. 5.4]
we omit it.

Theorem 3.3

(Integration of germs)

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open
set. Consider
\mathsf{K}
, a
\beta
-regularising kernel of order
(m,r)

over
U
with range
\rho
as per Definition
3.1
and let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. For any families
of real numbers
\bar{\alpha}_{K},\alpha_{K}
and
r_{K}
, indexed by the compact
sets
K\subset U
with
\bar{\alpha}_{K},\alpha_{K}\leqslant\gamma\hskip 1.69998pt\hskip 1.69998pt,\forall K\subset U
, it holds that

•

if
m>\bar{\alpha}_{K}+\beta,\hskip 1.69998pt\hskip 1.69998pt\forall K\subset U
, the map
\mathsf{K}:\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\to\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}+\beta}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U^{2\rho})
is continuous, i.e.,

\|\mathrm{K}F\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}+\beta}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U^{2\rho}),K}\lesssim_{K,\rho,\bar{\lambda}}\|F\|_{\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}.

(3.2)

•

if
m>\gamma+\beta
, the map
\mathsf{K}:\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\to\check{\mathcal{G}}^{(\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\beta)_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U^{2\rho})
is continuous, i.e.,

\|\mathrm{K}F\|_{\check{\mathcal{G}}^{(\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\beta)_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U^{2\rho}),K}\lesssim_{K,\rho,\bar{\lambda}}\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}.

(3.3)

In addition, assuming that
r>\max\{\bar{\alpha}_{K},\alpha_{K}\},\forall K\subset U
and that
m>\gamma+\beta
, the map
\mathsf{K}:\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\to\check{\mathcal{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}+\beta,(\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\beta)_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U^{2\rho})
is continuous, i.e.,

\|\mathrm{K}F\|_{\check{\mathcal{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}+\beta,(\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\beta)_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U^{2\rho}),K}\lesssim_{K,\rho,\bar{\lambda}}\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}.

(3.4)

The second result concerns the possibility of turning a weakly homogeneous
germ in a homogeneous one by means of the subtraction of a suitable Taylor
polynomial. This procedure is also known in the literature as

renormalization
, although we shall refrain from adopting this
nomenclature since it carries different meanings both in quantum field theory
and in stochastic partial differential equations. We prefer to avoid
possible sources of confusion. For the proof, see
[
BCZ24
, Thm. 5.10]
.

Theorem 3.4

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let

F\in\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
,
see Definition
2.2
.

•

For any compact
K\subset U
, it holds that

\sup_{x\in K}|D^{k}F_{x}(x)|\lesssim\|F\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K},\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\text{for}\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt0\leqslant|k|<\bar{\alpha}_{K},

(3.5)

where
D^{k}\equiv\partial_{x^{1}}^{k_{1}}\ldots\partial_{x^{n}}^{k_{n}}
with

|k|=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}
.

•

Setting
\mathbb{X}^{k}_{x}(y):=(y-x)^{k}
and assuming that,

\forall K\subset U,\bar{\alpha}_{K}\not\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then

G_{x}:=F_{x}-\mathcal{T}^{\gamma}_{x}(F_{x})=F_{x}-\sum_{0\leqslant\lvert k\rvert<\gamma}D^{k}F_{x}(x)\frac{\mathbb{X}^{k}_{x}}{k!},

(3.6)

is an
\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}
-homogeneous germ of distributions of order

\boldsymbol{r}
,
i.e.
,
G\in\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
, and

\|G\|_{\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}\lesssim\|F\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}.

(3.7)

•

If, for all compact
K\subset U
such that
\bar{\alpha}_{K}\in\mathbb{N}
and for all multi-indices
k
with
\lvert k\rvert=\bar{\alpha}_{K}
,
D^{k}F\in\mathsf{G}^{0}_{r_{K}}(U)
, then
G\in\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
and,

\|G\|_{\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}\lesssim\|F\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}+\sum_{\lvert k\rvert=\bar{\alpha}_{K}}\|D^{k}F\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{0}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}.

(3.8)

As a Corollary of Theorem
3.3
we
establish how an element lying in
\check{\mathcal{G}}^{\gamma,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
, namely a weakly coherent and
weakly homogeneous germs, can be turned into a homogeneous and coherent germ
by subtracting a suitable Taylor polynomial, see
[
BCZ24
, Thm. 5.10]
. In
the following we shall use extensively the notation and the results listed in
Appendix
A
.

Corollary 3.5

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
convex open set and let

, with
\alpha_{K}\neq 0\hskip 1.69998pt,\forall K\subset U
,
\gamma\not\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

k
such that

, the pointwise derivatives
D^{k}F_{x}(x)

G_{x}:=F_{x}-\mathcal{T}^{\gamma}_{x}(F_{x})\equiv F_{x}-\sum_{0\leqslant\lvert k\rvert<\gamma}D^{k}F_{x}(x)\frac{\mathbb{X}^{k}_{x}}{k!},

(3.9)

it holds that

(i)

there exists a family
\{\tilde{\alpha}_{K}\}_{\mathrm{K}\subset U}\subset\mathbb{R}
with

(ii)

the map

is linear, and for all compact sets

, one has that

\displaystyle\|G\|_{\mathsf{G}^{\gamma}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}\lesssim\hskip 2.70004pt\|F\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\gamma}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|G\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}\lesssim\hskip 2.70004pt\|F\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\gamma}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K^{\prime}}.

(3.11)

Proof.

We focus only on Equation (
3.11
)
since all other statements are a direct consequence of
[
BCZ24
, Thm.
5.10]
replacing
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Theorem

3.4
entails that, given
F\in\check{\mathcal{G}}^{\gamma,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)

as per Definition
2.7

G_{x}:=F_{x}-\mathcal{T}^{\gamma}_{x}(F_{x})=F_{x}-\sum_{0\leqslant\lvert k\rvert<\gamma}D^{k}F_{x}(x)\mathbb{X}^{k}_{x}\in\mathsf{G}^{\gamma}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),\qquad\|G\|_{\mathsf{G}^{\gamma}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}\lesssim\|F\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\gamma}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K}.

To prove coherence of

, we use Proposition

, item
(2)

yielding the existence of
\tilde{\alpha}_{K}
,
cf.

(
A.14
), such that

-coherent.
Additionally, again by Proposition

, we have that

where

At this stage, we have all the ingredients to establish the main result of this section, namely
Schauder estimates for germs of distributions on an open convex set.

Theorem 3.6

(Schauder estimates on open convex sets)

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open, convex and
bounded. Let
F\in\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
, as per Definition
2.2
, with
\alpha_{K}\leqslant\gamma
,
\forall K\subset U
and
\gamma\neq 0
. We denote by

\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F
the reconstruction of
F
, as per Theorem
2.11
. Furthermore, let
\beta>0

\alpha_{K}+\beta\neq 0\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\forall K\subset U,\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\gamma+\beta\not\in\mathbb{N},

and consider a
\beta
-regularizing kernel
\mathsf{K}
on
U
with range

\rho
of order
(m,r)
, as per Definition
3.1
,
to be such that

m>\gamma+\beta,\qquad\qquad r>-\alpha_{K},\qquad\qquad\forall K\subset U.

(3.12)

Defining the germ
(\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F)_{x}
as

\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F:=\mathsf{K}F-\mathcal{T}^{\gamma+\beta}(\mathsf{K}\{F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F\})=\mathsf{K}F-\sum_{0\leqslant\lvert k\rvert\leqslant\lfloor\gamma+\beta\rfloor}D^{k}(\mathsf{K}\{F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F\})(x)\frac{\mathbb{X}^{k}}{k!},

(3.13)

where
\mathbb{X}^{k}_{x}(y):=(y-x)^{k}
, it holds that, with
\bar{r}_{K}:=-\alpha_{K}
,

\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F-\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\in\mathcal{G}^{\gamma+\beta,\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}}(U^{2\rho}),

(3.14)

where
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
is as per Corollary
3.5
.
In addition, the following continuity estimate holds true: There exists a
compact
\tilde{K}^{\prime}\subset U
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F-\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\gamma+\beta,\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}}(U^{2\rho}),K}&\lesssim\hskip 1.69998pt\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),\tilde{K}^{\prime}},\end{array}

(3.15)

where here
\lesssim
encodes a proportionality constant depending on
K,\rho,\gamma+\beta,\alpha_{\tilde{K}^{\prime}},\bar{r}_{\tilde{K}^{\prime}},\bar{\lambda}
,
cf.
Definition
2.2
.

Proof.

Given
F\in\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)
as
per hypothesis,
F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F
is both
\gamma
-homogeneous and

(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\gamma)
-coherent of order
\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}=\lfloor-\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rfloor
, as shown in Remark

2.12
. By Theorem
3.3
,
\mathsf{K}(F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\in\check{\mathcal{G}}^{\gamma+\beta,(\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\beta)_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U^{2\rho})
. On account of Equation
(
3.13
), we have

\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F-\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)=\mathsf{K}(F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)-\sum_{0\leqslant\lvert k\rvert\leqslant\lfloor\gamma+\beta\rfloor}D^{k}(\mathsf{K}\{F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F\})(x)\frac{\mathbb{X}^{k}}{k!}.

Applying Corollary
3.5
, it follows that there
exists
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
with
\tilde{\alpha}_{K}\leqslant\min\{0,\gamma+\beta\}
for any
K
, such that it holds that

\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F-\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\in\mathcal{G}^{\gamma+\beta,\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}}(U)
, with

\|\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F-\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}}(U^{2\rho}),K}\lesssim_{K,\gamma+\beta,\bar{r}_{K},d}\|\mathsf{K}(F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\gamma+\beta}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U^{2\rho}),K^{\prime}},

(3.16)

and
K^{\prime}\supset K
is a convex compact set contained in
U
. By Equation
(
3.2
) it descends that, for a suitable enlargement
\tilde{K}^{\prime}

of
K^{\prime}

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|\mathsf{K}(F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\gamma+\beta}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U^{2\rho}),K^{\prime}}&\lesssim_{\rho,\bar{\lambda}}&\|F-\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F\|_{\check{\mathsf{G}}^{\gamma}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),K^{\prime}}\\
&\lesssim_{\alpha_{\tilde{K}^{\prime}},r_{\tilde{K}^{\prime}},d}&\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}}(U^{2\rho}),\tilde{K}^{\prime}}\end{array}

(3.17)

where we used Equation (
2.22
). Equations
(
3.16
) and (

3.15
).

Proposition 3.7

Under the assumptions and notations of
Theorem
3.6
, denoting by
\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime}:=(r_{K^{\prime}})_{K^{\prime}\subset U}
, and assuming
\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U^{2\rho})
, the map

\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}:\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\to\mathcal{G}^{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime}}(U^{2\rho}),\qquad\qquad F\mapsto\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F,

is linear and continuous, i.e.,

\|\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime}}(U^{2\rho}),K}\lesssim\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),\tilde{K}^{\prime}}.

(3.18)

Furthermore

\mathcal{R}^{\gamma+\beta}(\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F)=\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F),

(3.19)

on
U^{2\rho}
, i.e.,
\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)
is a
(\gamma+\beta)-
reconstruction of the germ of distributions
\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F
.

Proof.

If
\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U^{2\rho})
, by
triangular inequality

\|\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime}}(U^{2\rho}),K}\leqslant\|\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}F-\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime}}(U^{2\rho}),K}+\|\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime}}(U^{2\rho}),K}\lesssim\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U),\tilde{K}^{\prime}},

(3.20)

where in the last inequality we used Equation (
3.15
)
together with

\|\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}F)\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime}}(U^{2\rho}),K}=0,

as the coherence norm of a constant germ is vanishing. Note that in the last
step of Equation (
3.20
), the constant of the estimate
depends on
\bar{r}_{\tilde{K}^{\prime}}
and not on

since we can
always set
\bar{r}=\max\{-\alpha_{K},-\gamma\}=-\alpha_{K}\geqslant r
. Equation (
3.19
) follows from Equation
(
3.16
). As a matter of fact, it is sufficient to compare

2.6
) and (
2.21
)
noting that, by assumptions,

.
∎

At last we establish also a bound reading all the germs above as

Proposition 3.8

Under the same assumptions and notations of Theorem

3.6
and of Proposition
3.7

\mathcal{K}^{\gamma,\beta}:\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U)\to\mathcal{G}^{(\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}+\beta\wedge 0),\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\gamma+\beta}}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}}(U^{2\rho}),

(3.21)

Proof.

Under the above assumptions, Equation (
2.23
)

Remark 4.4

We observe that the above definition is well-posed and it yields a smooth

Definition 4.5

(Coherence on Riemannian manifolds)

Let

and consider three

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}:=(\alpha_{K})_{K\subset M}\subset\mathbb{R},\qquad\boldsymbol{r}:=(r_{K})_{K\subset M}\subset\mathbb{N}_{0},\qquad\boldsymbol{R}:=(R_{K})_{K\subset M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+},

F
is
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\gamma)
-coherent of order
\boldsymbol{r}
and range
\boldsymbol{R}

(4.11)

\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda}]
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{p}^{r_{K}}(K)

(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\gamma)
-coherent

\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda}],\hskip 1.39304pt\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{p}^{r_{K}}(K)\end{array}$}}}\hskip 2.70004pt\frac{\lvert(F_{p}-F_{q})(\varphi^{\lambda}_{p})\rvert}{\lambda^{\alpha_{K}}(\lambda+d(p,q))^{\gamma-\alpha_{K}}}.

(4.12)

Definition 4.6

(Homogeneity on Riemannian manifolds)

Let

\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}:=(\bar{\alpha}_{K})_{K\subset M}\subset\mathbb{R},\qquad\boldsymbol{r}:=(r_{K})_{K\subset M}\subset\mathbb{N}_{0},

be two families indexed by compact sets
K\subset M

is

\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}
-homogeneous of order
\boldsymbol{r}

\bar{\lambda}\in(0,\rho_{K})

\lvert F_{p}(\varphi^{\lambda}_{p})\rvert\leqslant C_{K}\lambda^{\bar{\alpha}},

(4.13)

uniformly for
p\in K
,
\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda}]
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{p}^{r_{K}}(K)
. The space of
\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}
-homogeneous
germs of distributions on
M
of order
\boldsymbol{r}
is

is endowed with the family of seminorms

\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(M)\ni F\mapsto\|F\|_{\mathsf{G}^{\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(M),K}:=\sup_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda}],\\
p\in K,\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{p}^{r_{K}}(K)\end{array}$}}}\frac{\lvert F_{p}(\varphi^{\lambda}_{p})\rvert}{\lambda^{\bar{\alpha}_{K}}}.

(4.14)

5
Reconstruction Theorem on Riemannian
manifolds

Having introduced the notion of coherent and homogeneous germs of distributions on Riemannian manifolds, see Section
4
, we shall now state a reconstruction result, tailored to this setting.

In addition to the
existing literature
[
RS21
, Thm. 18]
, we prove the continuity of the
reconstruction operator and we assess the regularity of the reconstructed
distribution in the sense of negative Hölder-Zygmund spaces on Riemannian
manifolds, whose definition we recall here for the reader’s convenience.

Definition 5.1

(Negative Hölder-Zygmund spaces on Riemannian
manifolds)

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold, and let

\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. We define
\mathcal{Z}^{\gamma}(M)
as the set of
distributions
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{Z}^{\gamma}_{K,\bar{\lambda}}}=\sup_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda}],\hskip 2.21254ptp\in K\\
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}^{r}_{p}(K)\text{with}\hskip 2.21254ptr=\lfloor-\gamma+1\rfloor\end{array}$}}}\frac{\lvert f(\varphi^{\lambda}_{p})\rvert}{\lambda^{\gamma}}<+\infty,

(5.1)

for all compact sets
K\subset M
and for all
\bar{\lambda}\in(0,\rho_{K})
, where
\rho_{K}
and the spaces
\mathcal{B}^{r}_{p}(K)
are as per
Equation (
4.9
).

Remark 5.2

We define the Hölder-Zygmund spaces only in the case
\gamma\leqslant 0
as the definition for positive
\gamma
would require additional
structures such as the notion of jets. Nevertheless, since the homogeneity
of the germ corresponds to the Hölder-Zygmund regularity of the
reconstruction, see Theorem
5.3
, and since germs
with positive homogeneity have the identically zero distribution as
reconstruction, see Remark
2.6
, the case
\gamma>0

is not of interest for our goals.

Theorem 5.3

(Reconstruction theorem on Riemannian manifolds)

M
be a Riemannian manifold, and
let

. Consider an

(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\gamma)
-coherent germ

\boldsymbol{r}
and

\boldsymbol{R}

\gamma>0

j\in I

uniformly over
\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda})
,

. Furthermore, it holds true that

1.

The map

is linear,

2.

If

\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-homogeneous, as per

4.6
, for each

, denoting by

Proof.

The strategy of the proof consists in exploiting the analogous results on
open sets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Theorem
2.11
, and
then show that the exponential map yields a reconstruction on the whole

Given a finite good cover
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and taking

,
by Proposition
4.8

\exp_{j\ast}^{-1}F
satisfies a

\exp_{j}^{-1}(p),\exp_{j}^{-1}(q)
such

. We can always assume that
\operatorname{diam}(U_{j})\leqslant R_{K}

\exp_{j\ast}^{-1}F

(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\gamma)
-coherent germ on

, as per Definition
2.2
.

2.11
, there exists a unique

and
\bar{\lambda}\in(0,\operatorname{dist}(K,\partial U_{j}))
, it

\lvert(\exp_{j\ast}^{-1}F_{p}-f_{j})(\varphi^{\lambda}_{\exp_{j}^{-1}(p)})\rvert\lesssim\|\exp_{j\ast}^{-1}F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U^{\prime}_{j}),K^{\prime}_{3D_{K^{\prime}}/4}}\lambda^{\gamma},

,
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}^{m_{K^{\prime}}}

\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda})

. Recall that on account of Proposition
4.8
, we have

\lvert\lvert\exp_{j\ast}^{-1}F\rvert\rvert_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r}}(U^{\prime}_{j}),K^{\prime}_{3D_{K^{\prime}}/4}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{}}}\leqslant\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r},\boldsymbol{R}}(M),\tilde{K}},

where
\tilde{K}:=\exp_{j}(K^{\prime}_{3D_{K^{\prime}}/4})\subset U_{j}
is a compact set. Overall, this implies that

\lvert(\exp_{j\ast}^{-1}F_{p}-f_{j})(\varphi^{\lambda}_{\exp_{j}^{-1}(p)})\rvert\lesssim\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\gamma}}_{\boldsymbol{r},\boldsymbol{R}}(M),\tilde{K}}\lambda^{\gamma}.

(5.4)

We define the reconstruction of
F
restricted to
U_{j}\subset M
as

\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}_{j}F:=\exp_{j\ast}f_{j}.

So far, we have built a family of distributions

such that
\mathcal{R}^{\gamma}_{j}F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U_{j})
for any

. Our next goal is to prove that this family identifies a well

\begin{array}[]{lll}|(f_{j}-\exp_{j}^{\ast}\circ\exp_{\ell\ast}f_{\ell})(\varphi^{\lambda}_{\exp_{\ell}^{-1}(p)})|&\leqslant&|(f_{j}-\exp_{j}^{\ast}F_{p})(\varphi^{\lambda}_{\exp_{\ell}^{-1}(p)})|+\lvert(\exp_{j}^{\ast}F_{p}-\exp_{j}^{\ast}\circ\exp_{\ell\ast}f_{\ell})(\varphi^{\lambda}_{\exp_{\ell}^{-1}(p)})\rvert\\

Being the map

. Therefore, by uniqueness of the reconstruction,

on

and thus the existence of

) entails that the germ of

-homogeneous germ

) holds true.

Linearity of the map

descends from that of

\exp_{j\ast}^{-1}F\mapsto f_{j},\hskip 2.70004pt\hskip 2.70004pt\forall j\in I
. The part of the statement concerning the regularity of the reconstruction follows by invoking locally Corollary
2.13
.

∎

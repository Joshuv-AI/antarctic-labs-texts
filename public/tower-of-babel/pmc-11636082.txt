Protocol for cryopreserving neural organoids and human living brain tissue using MEDY
Authors: Weiwei Xue, Jiacheng Du, Yunteng Li, Zhicheng Shao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryCryopreservation of neural organoids and human living brain tissue faces huge challenges. Here, we present a cryopreservation protocol for the reliable storage of diverse neural organoids and human living brain tissue using methylcellulose, ethylene glycol, DMSO, and Y27632, termed MEDY. We describe steps for preparing cryopreservation solution, preparing cortical organoids, and surgical removal of human brain tissue. We then detail procedures for cryopreservation, resuscitation, and detection assays. This protocol can serve as a model for studying human neurological diseases.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Xue et al.1Subject areas: neuroscience, organoids, stem cells

Before you begin

This protocol describes the preparation of cryopreservation solution and cryo-resuscitation procedure.2,3 It could be used for cryopreservation storage of human living brain tissue and brain organoid.1 Before experiment, high-quality embryonic stem cells are necessary in order to induct diverse nerve organoids.4

Step-by-step method details

Cryopreserve cortical organoids

Timing: 24–30 h

1.Change fresh W4 medium with Y27632 (final concentration 10 μM) for cultured cortical organoids and continue culturing for 1.5–2 h.

Change fresh W4 medium with Y27632 (final concentration 10 μM) for cultured cortical organoids and continue culturing for 1.5–2 h.

2.Add 500 μL freshly prepared MEDY cryopreservation solution to the cryogenic vial first.

Add 500 μL freshly prepared MEDY cryopreservation solution to the cryogenic vial first.

3.Transfer 2 organoids to cryogenic vial with 500 μL MEDY cryopreservation solution and add another 500 μL MEDY cryopreservation solution.

Transfer 2 organoids to cryogenic vial with 500 μL MEDY cryopreservation solution and add another 500 μL MEDY cryopreservation solution.

Note: Avoid much medium into cryopreservation solution during transfer organoid to cryogenic vial. After the organoid is sucked up, vertical pipette for a moment. When the organoid fall to the bottom edge of the tip by gravity, gently stick to the liquid surface of the cryopreservation solution, and the organoid will fall into the cryopreservation solution by the action of liquid tension with little medium to the cryopreservation solution.

4.Stand at 23°C–28°C for 1/6–5 h.

Stand at 23°C–28°C for 1/6–5 h.

Note: Pretreatment allows permeation of Y27632 into organoids, reducing vitrification and increasing osmotic pressure. Based on the diameter of organoids, pretreatment time at 23°C–28°C was extended by 20 min/mm.

5.Place the cryogenic vials containing organoids in a freezing container and freeze at −80°C for 24 h, then transfer to liquid nitrogen for storage.

Place the cryogenic vials containing organoids in a freezing container and freeze at −80°C for 24 h, then transfer to liquid nitrogen for storage.

Expected outcomes

Using this protocol, the thawed cortical organoids could be maintained most structure, cell diversity and neural function. We can observe the axon elongation in the coating Matrigel (Figure 3). For the thawed brain tissue, cell migration should be observed. Neuron and glial cells are surviving. Besides, positive calcium salt deposition staining showed pathological features were retained (Figure 4).

Limitations

In this protocol, although the MEDY cryopreservation technology could enable the maintenance of pathological features of pathological brain tissue derived from epileptic patient, it remains a challenge to cryopreserve the whole brain.

This protocol is more suitable for high quality organoids. So, it is important to culture embryonic stem cells which retain high cellular totipotency.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Methylcellulose is difficult to dissolve during preparing MEDY.

Potential solution

•Store sterilized methylcellulose at 23°C–28°C, and stir methylcellulose as much as possible with the 1 mL tip of a pipet or a fine glass rod before dissolve, which will enable sterilized methylcellulose more dispersed and easier to be dissolved.

Problem 2

It is not convenient to weigh methylcellulose after high temperature sterilization.

Potential solution

•Before sterilization, first weigh the fixed mass of methylcellulose in multiple tubes, and then sterilize. Configure the required amount according to the volume of frozen liquid.

Problem 3

It is difficult or impossible to filter the frozen liquid after preparation.

Potential solution

•Sterilization process was divided to two steps. After the liquid part of the cryopreservation solution is prepared (W4 medium + 10% Ethylene glycol + 10% DMSO + 10 μM Y27632), it is filtered by a 0.22 μm filter membrane, and then the sterilized methylcellulose is dissolved.

Problem 4

Occasionally, it happens that the resuscitated organoid grows poorly.

Potential solution

•Before cryopreservation process, organoid must be cultured in a well growth state, so that it can be well resistant to damage caused by the freezing process.

No signal was detected during MEA detection.

Potential solution

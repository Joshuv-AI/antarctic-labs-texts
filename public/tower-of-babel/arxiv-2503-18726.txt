The Pro-Étale Homotopy Type


Abstract
In this paper we define the pro-étale homotopy type of a scheme and prove some of its expected properties. Our definition is similar to the definition of the étale homotopy type by Michael Artin and Barry Mazur. We prove that for a qcqs scheme the pro-étale homotopy type is profinite, determined by a single split affine wc hypercovering and computes the cohomology of a certain class of sheaves. We show that the pro-étale homotopy type of a w-contractible scheme is trivial and compute the pro-étale homotopy type of the real numbers. Moreover, we prove that a suitable version of \uppi 0 \uppi_{0} composed with the pro-étale homotopy type gives back the space of components of the base scheme. We make some progress towards describing the pro-étale homotopy type of arbitrary fields. Lastly, we give a refined definition of the pro-étale homotopy type using the theory by Ilan Barnea and Tomer M. Schlank and the theory of condensed sets by Dustin Clausen and Peter Scholze. This allows us to define pro-étale homotopy groups associated to pointed qcqs schemes.



Paul Meffle

Chapter 1
Introduction

The definition of the étale homotopy type in
[
AM69
]
by Michael Artin and Barry Mazur relies on the fact that the
étale site is locally connected.
As we show in Example (
4
) the pro-étale site is not locally connected.
As a result, we cannot apply the theory from
[
AM69
]
directly.
To address the issue we use the
space of components
\uppi
of a (topological) space: the set of components endowed
with its natural topology.
We define the
pro-étale homotopy type
of a scheme
S
as the pro-object

\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)\colon\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Hy}(S))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{s}\mathrm{Top}),\;U_{\bullet}\mapsto\uppi(U_{\bullet})

in the homotopy category of simplicial spaces.
Here the homotopy categories are defined by identifying (simplicially) homotopic morphisms.
Our definition works for arbitrary schemes, but we have to work with a considerably more complicated object and
lose the intrinsic characterisation of the set of components of a locally connected site.

In
[
BS14
]
Bhargav Bhatt and Peter Scholze introduce the notion of w-contractible scheme, which are affine schemes
that trivialise pro-étale coverings.
They turn out to be the affine weakly contractible objects in the pro-étale site and in Corollary (
7
)
we show that their pro-étale homotopy type is trivial.
In particular, this applies to an algebraically closed field.

{srthm}

[Corollary (
7
)]
Given a w-contractible scheme
W
, there is a natural isomorphism

\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(W)=\mathrm{c}(\uppi(W))_{\bullet},

where
\mathrm{c}
denotes the constant simplicial object.

Moreover, we prove an adjusted version of the statement hinted at in
[
BS14
, Remark 4.2.9]
: the pro-étale homotopy
type of a qc scheme can be computed from a single split affine weakly contractible hypercovering.

{srthm}

[Theorem (
7
)]
For any split affine weakly contractible hypercovering
W_{\bullet}
in the pro-étale site of a qc scheme
S
there is a
natural isomorphism

\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)=\uppi(W_{\bullet}).

We give some evidence that our definition deserves the name of pro-étale homotopy type.
First, we prove a statement similar to that of Eric M. Friedlander in
[
Fri82
, Proposition 5.2]
, which relates the set
of simplicial components of the étale homotopy type to the set of components of the underlying base scheme.

{srthm}

[Theorem (
5
)]
Given a scheme
S
, the space of simplicial components of the pro-étale homotopy type is given by the space of components
of
S
.
More precisely, we have a natural isomorphism

\uppi\circ\uppi_{0}\circ\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)=\uppi(S),

where
\uppi_{0}
denotes the space of simplicial components of a simplicial space; see Definition (
5
).

Second, we define the cohomology of the pro-étale homotopy type of a qcqs scheme with values in pro-étale sheaves that
come from sheaves on the space of components; this includes all constant sheaves associated to totally disconnected spaces.
Motivated by their defining property we call these sheaves
\uppi
-sheaves
.

{srthm}

[Theorem (
8
)]
For any
\uppi
-sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on the pro-étale site of a qcqs scheme

there is a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{p}(\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S),\mathscr{F})=\mathrm{H}^{p}(S_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}},\mathscr{F}),

where the left hand side denotes the cohomology of the pro-étale homotopy type and the right hand side the cohomology
of the pro-étale site.

In section
9
we make some progress towards describing the pro-étale homotopy type of arbitrary fields.
Although we ultimately fall short of our goal, we use our approach in Example (
9
) to compute the pro-étale
homotopy type of the real numbers.

Lastly, we extend the space of components to pro-objects of simplicial sheaves of the pro-étale site and use the theory
by Ilan Barnea and Tomer M. Schlank introduced in
[
BS16
]
to define a refined pro-étale homotopy type.
The central result is the following.

{srthm}

[Theorem (
10
)]
Given a qcqs scheme

, the extension
\uppi_{\mathrm{s}}
of the space of components to the pro-étale topos together with its
right adjoint

induces a pair

\mathrm{Pro}(\uppi_{\mathrm{s}})\dashv\mathrm{Pro}(\uppi^{\mathrm{s}})\colon\mathrm{Pro}(\mathrm{s}\mathrm{Sh}(S_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}))\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Pro}(\mathrm{sCond})

.

The

is now given by the value of the left derived functor induced by

We prove that this is indeed a refinement of the pro-étale homotopy type defined using hypercoverings.

{srthm}

[Theorem (
10
)]
Let
S
be a qcqs scheme.
There is a natural isomorphism between the refined pro-étale homotopy type and the pro-étale homotopy type
\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)

considered as pro-objects in the homotopy category of simplicial condensed sets.

We also give a definition for the
n
th pro-étale homotopy group of a pointed qcqs scheme.
The question of how this notion relates to the pro-étale fundamental group turned out to be out of the scope of this paper.

Chapter 2
Preliminaries and notation

In this section we mention some preliminary concepts and clarify some notation.
We denote the categories of (topological) spaces by
\mathrm{Top}
, profinite sets by
\mathrm{Prof}
and extremally disconnected sets
by
\mathrm{Extr}
.
We do not pose any general assumptions on the schemes we consider.
We use the abbreviations
qc
and

for quasi-compact and quasi-separated respectively.
A

is a scheme together with a geometric point.

For an introduction to sites and sheaves we recommend
[
Tam94
]
and follow the notation therein.
In particular, a morphism

of sites is given by a functor in the same direction that preserves coverings
and fibre products.
It induces an pair
f_{\mathrm{s}}\dashv f^{\mathrm{s}}\colon\mathrm{Sh}(\mathscr{C})\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Sh}(\mathscr{D})
of adjoint functors between the categories of
sheaves.
We define a
point of a site
\mathscr{C}
that admits finite limits to be a morphism
\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathrm{Set}
of sites that preserves
finite limits and coproducts.

A

morphism of schemes is a flat morphism whose diagonal is flat.
For any scheme
S
its
pro-étale site
S_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}
is defined as the category of weakly étale schemes over
S
together
with families

\{U_{i}\rightarrow X\}_{i\in I}
of morphisms such that any open affine in
X
is mapped onto by an open affine in
\coprod_{i\in I}U_{i}
as coverings.
We use the abbreviation

for coverings in the pro-étale site of a scheme.
We refer the reader to the original source
[
BS14
, Section 4]
or alternatively
[
Stacks
, Tag 0965]
for a detailed treatment.

In general, we use the definitions and notation of
[
GJ09
]
when it comes to simplicial objects in a category
\mathscr{C}
and
homotopy.
Following
[
Stacks
]
we call the natural functor
\mathrm{sk}_{n}\colon\mathrm{s}\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathrm{s}_{n}\mathscr{C}
from the category of simplicial
objects to their
n
-truncated counter parts the
n
-skeleton
and its left adjoint
\mathrm{cosk}_{n}
the
n
-coskeleton
.
By abuse of notation we denote the composite
\mathrm{cosk}_{n}\circ\mathrm{sk}_{n}
again by
\mathrm{cosk}_{n}
.
Apart from section
10
the
homotopy category

\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{s}\mathscr{C})
of simplicial objects in
\mathscr{C}
is the
category with the same objects and equivalence classes of homotopic morphisms.

A

is a simplicial space
X_{\bullet}
and a morphism
\Delta[0]\rightarrow X_{\bullet}
.
Together with morphisms that respect the base points they form a category
\mathrm{s}\mathrm{Top}_{*}
and their homotopy category
is defined using non-pointed homotopies.

Lastly, a
pro-object in a category
\mathscr{C}
is a functor from a variable cofiltered category into

.
See
[
AM69
, Section A.2]
for an introduction including a definition of the morphisms between them.
Recall that a category is cofiltered if for any two objects there is a third object mapping to them and for any two parallel
morphisms there is a third morphism that equalises them; see
[
Mac98
, 211]
.
The most important property of pro-objects is that they do not change (up to natural isomorphism) when precomposed with an
initial functor; see
[
AM69
, Corollary A.2.5]
.
The central example of an initial functor is a functor

between cofiltered categories such that for each
object
i
in
I
there exists an object
j
in
J
and a morphism
f(j)\rightarrow i
.
A precise definition of the dual notion can be found in
[
AM69
, Paragraph A.1.5]
.

In this paper we encounter set-theoretic issues, which, in the literature, are usually fixed by choosing a universe or an
uncountable strong limit cardinal.
We ignore such issues.

We show that affine wc objects behave well with respect to sheafification.

{srlem}

For any presheaf

Chapter 5
Definition of the pro-étale homotopy type

{srsum}

In this section we define the pro-étale homotopy type of a (pointed) scheme and prove a analogue of

[
Fri82
, Proposition 5.2]
.

We recall the notion of pointed simplicial space and pointed hypercovering.

{srrem}

A hypercovering of a scheme
S
is a simplicial object
U_{\bullet}
of its pro-étale site such that the natural morphisms

U_{n+1}\rightarrow\mathrm{cosk}_{n}(U_{\bullet})_{n+1}
as well as
U_{0}\rightarrow S
are coverings.
Together with morphisms of simplicial objects they form a category
\mathrm{Hy}(S)
and their homotopy category obtained by identifying
homotopic morphisms is cofiltered by
[
Gro72b
, Théorème V.7.3.2.1]
.

A geometric point
s
of
S
induces a point
s
of the pro-étale site of
S
by mapping schemes to their geometric points
over
s
.
A
pointed hypercovering
is a hypercovering
U_{\bullet}
of
S
and a morphism
\Delta[0]\rightarrow s(U_{\bullet})
.
Together with morphisms respecting the base points we obtain a category
\mathrm{Hy}(S,s)
of pointed hypercoverings.
As above their homotopy category is defined by identifying homotopic morphisms and cofiltered.
Note that we use non-pointed homotopies since this does not affect the definition of the pro-étale homotopy type.

We are not able to use the definition of the homotopy type introduced in
[
AM69
, 112]
since the pro-étale site is
not locally connected.

{srrem}

As Example (
4
) shows, any scheme admits a weakly étale morphism from a scheme that is not
locally connected.
This is a consequence of weakly étale morphisms not possessing the finiteness condition of étale morphisms.
Therefore we take the natural topology on the set of components into account.

We introduce the central notion in its pointed and non-pointed flavour.

{srdef}

For any pointed scheme
(S,s)
its
(pointed) pro-étale homotopy type
is the pro-object

\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S,s)\colon\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Hy}(S,s))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{s}\mathrm{Top}_{*}),\;(U_{\bullet},u)\mapsto\uppi(U_{\bullet},u)

in the homotopy category of pointed simplicial spaces.
An analogous definition using non-pointed hypercoverings gives rise to the
pro-étale homotopy type
\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)
of
S
.

We prove that both definitions are compatible.

{srlem}

For a pointed scheme
(S,s)
, the natural functor
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Hy}(S,s))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Hy}(S))
is initial.
Moreover, there is a natural isomorphism

\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)=\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S,s)

of pro-objects in
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{s}\mathrm{Top}))
.

Proof.

We conclude the first claim from the fact that geometric points lift along coverings.
The second claim follows from
[
AM69
, Corollary A.2.5]
.
∎

As a first test, we want to prove a version of
[
Fri82
, Proposition 5.2]
: composing the étale homotopy type with the
set of simplicial components gives back the set of components of the base scheme.
First, we introduce an analogue of the set of components for simplicial spaces.

{srdef}

Given a simplicial space
X_{\bullet}
, its
space of (simplicial) components
is the space

\uppi_{0}(X_{\bullet})\coloneqq\mathrm{CoEq}(X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}).

Denote the induced functor by
\uppi_{0}\colon\mathrm{s}\mathrm{Top}\rightarrow\mathrm{Top}
.

We can characterise the component functor of simplicial spaces as an adjoint.

{srlem}

The component functor
\uppi_{0}
of simplicial spaces and the constant simplicial space functor
\mathrm{c}
form a pair

\uppi_{0}\dashv\mathrm{c}\colon\mathrm{s}\mathrm{Top}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Top}

of adjoint functors.

Proof.

Let
\uppi_{0}(X_{\bullet})\rightarrow Y
be a morphism of spaces.
We construct a morphism
X_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathrm{c}(Y)_{\bullet}
of simplicial spaces inductively.
Composition with the natural morphism
X_{0}\rightarrow\uppi_{0}(X_{\bullet})
gives rise to a morphism
X_{0}\rightarrow Y
,
which coequalises the face morphisms
d_{0}
and
d_{1}

Suppose a morphism

that coequalises the

’s of degree
n{+}1
has been constructed.
This induces a morphism

by pre-composing with any of these

’s.
Using the induction hypothesis and the simplicial identities one computes that
f_{n+1}
coequalises the face morphisms
of degree
n{+}2
.

A morphism
X_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathrm{c}(Y)_{\bullet}
of simplicial spaces induces a unique morphism
\uppi_{0}(X_{\bullet})\rightarrow Y
by the universal property of the coequaliser.
These constructions are mutually inverse.
∎

Analogous to the result in
[

, Tag 00GX]
for simplicial sets, we prove that the space of components of a
simplicial space preserves products.

{srlem}

Given simplicial spaces

and

\uppi_{0}(X_{\bullet}\times Y_{\bullet})=\uppi_{0}(X_{\bullet})\times\uppi_{0}(Y_{\bullet}).

Applying the universal property of the coequaliser to

yields
a morphism of spaces
\uppi_{0}(X_{\bullet}\times Y_{\bullet})\rightarrow\uppi_{0}(X_{\bullet})

The same works for

[

, Tag 00GX]

Since the morphism

is surjective and the morphism

[
Bou07
, Proposition I.5.1.1.b]
.
∎

We are now able to conclude that the component functor of simplicial spaces identifies homotopic morphisms.

{srcor}

The space of components induces a functor
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{s}\mathrm{Top})\rightarrow\mathrm{Top}

\uppi_{0}

Proof.

Given a simplicial space
X_{\bullet}

∎

We introduce some notation for pointed simplicial spaces.

{srrem}

The space of components of a pointed simplicial space

Chapter 8
Cohomology of the pro-étale homotopy type

{srsum}

We define a notion of cohomology for the pro-étale homotopy type for a certain class of pro-étale sheaves and then show
that it computes their pro-étale cohomology.

The central tool in this section is the Verdier hypercovering theorem, which allows us to compute the cohomology of
abelian sheaves using the homotopy category of hypercoverings.

{srthm}

[
[
Gro72b
, Théorème V.7.4.1.2]
]
Let
\mathscr{C}
be a site that admits finite limits and finite coproducts.
For any abelian sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
\mathscr{C}
there is a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{p}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{F})=\colim_{U_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Hy})^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{H}^{p}(\mathscr{F}(U_{\bullet}))

for all
p\geqslant 0
.

Recall that the étale homotopy type computes the étale cohomology with values in constant abelian sheaves.

{srrem}

Let
S
be a locally Noetherian scheme and
G
an abelian group.
We have a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{p}(S_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\underline{G})=\colim_{U_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Hy})^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{H}^{p}(\uppi(U_{\bullet}),G)\eqqcolon\mathrm{H}^{p}(\Pi_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S),G).

by
[
AM69
, Corollary 9.3]
.
Here the cohomology groups in the middle are those of the simplicial set
\uppi(U_{\bullet})
.

We generalise this for the pro-étale site of a qcqs scheme using the subcategory of sheaves characterised in

[
BS14
, Lemma 4.2.13]
, which we give a name.

{srdef}

For any qcqs scheme
S
, a
\uppi
-sheaf
is a sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on the site
S_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}
such that for any morphism
X\rightarrow Y
of qcqs objects in
S_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}
that induces an isomorphism between the spaces of components, the morphism

\mathscr{F}(Y)\rightarrow\mathscr{F}(X)

is an isomorphism.
The
\uppi
-sheaves form a full subcategory
\uppi
-
\mathrm{Sh}(S_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}})
of the category of sheaves.
For an
abelian
\uppi
-sheaf
the morphism
\mathscr{F}(Y)\rightarrow\mathscr{F}(X)
is required to be an isomorphism of abelian
groups and the category is denoted by
\uppi
-
\mathrm{AbSh}(S_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}})
.

We have already seen examples of
\uppi
-sheaves.

{srexa}

The constant sheaf associated to a totally disconnected space
T
on the pro-étale site of a qcqs scheme is a

\uppi
-sheaf.
In fact, we have
\underline{T}(X)=\underline{T}(\uppi(X))
for any object
X
in the pro-étale site by
Proposition (
4
).
Similarly we see that a constant sheaf associated to an abelian totally disconnected group is an abelian
\uppi
-sheaf.

By an abstract argument the equivalence of
[
BS14
, Lemma 4.2.13]
of
\uppi
-sheaves with sheaves of the
pro-étale site on the space of components factors through the categories of abelian sheaves.

{srcor}

Assuming
S
is a qcqs scheme, the functors
\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}
and
\alpha^{\mathrm{s}}
induced by the morphism of sites from
Proposition (
4
) give rise to an equivalence

\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}\simeq\alpha^{\mathrm{s}}\colon\mathrm{AbSh}(\uppi(S)_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}})\rightleftarrows\uppi{\text{-}}\mathrm{AbSh}(S_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}})

of categories by restriction of
\alpha^{\mathrm{s}}
.
Moreover, for any abelian
\uppi
-sheaf
\mathscr{F}
and qcqs object
X
in the pro-étale site there is a natural isomorphism

\alpha^{\mathrm{s}}\mathscr{F}(\uppi(X))=\mathscr{F}(X).

Proof.

For any morphism
\varphi\colon\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}
of sites the induced functors
\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}
and
\varphi^{\mathrm{s}}
on
the categories of sheaves factor through the respective categories of abelian sheaves.
On the level of sheaves of sets the equivalence is proved in
[
BS14
, Lemma 4.2.13]
.

We conclude the second claim from Lemma (
4
) and the defining property of abelian
\uppi
-sheaves.
∎

With Corollary (
8
) in place, we naturally extend the definition of the cohomology of the
pro-étale homotopy type to
\uppi
-sheaves.

{srdef}

Given a qcqs scheme
S
and an abelian
\uppi
-sheaf
\mathscr{F}
, the
cohomology of the pro-étale homotopy type
is
defined by

\mathrm{H}^{p}(\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S),\mathscr{F})\coloneqq\colim_{W_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Hy_{awc}})^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{H}^{p}(\alpha^{\mathrm{s}}\mathscr{F}(\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)(W_{\bullet}))).

In fact, by Theorem (
7
) it is enough to consider a single split affine wc hypercovering.

We verify that this generalised definition of the cohomology of the pro-étale homotopy type computes the pro-étale
cohomology.

{srthm}

For any qcqs scheme
S
and abelian
\uppi
-sheaf
\mathscr{F}
there is a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{p}(\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S),\mathscr{F})=\mathrm{H}^{p}(S_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}},\mathscr{F}).

Proof.

By unpacking the definitions and applying Corollary (
8
) we have

\mathrm{H}^{p}(\alpha^{\mathrm{s}}\mathscr{F}(\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)(W_{\bullet})))=\mathrm{H}^{p}(\alpha^{\mathrm{s}}\mathscr{F}(\uppi(W_{\bullet})))=\mathrm{H}^{p}(\mathscr{F}(W_{\bullet}))

for any affine wc hypercovering

.
After taking the colimit over all such hypercoverings we obtain the cohomology of the pro-étale homotopy type of
S

Using Corollary (
6

\colim_{W_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Hy_{awc}})^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{H}^{p}(\mathscr{F}(W_{\bullet}))=\colim_{U_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Hy})^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{H}^{p}(\mathscr{F}(U_{\bullet})).

This equals the pro-étale cohomology of

by Theorem (

∎

Chapter 9
Pro-étale homotopy type of a field

{srsum}

We give some ideas on how to describe the pro-étale homotopy type of a field.
Although we do not succeed in full generality, we compute the pro-étale homotopy type of the real numbers.

{srrem}

Let
k
be a field and
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
a separable closure in an algebraic closure.
Contrary to the étale situation the natural covering
\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k})\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(k)
is an object in the pro-étale site.
Since the pro-étale fundamental group of
k
is given by
\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}/k)
, we expect the pro-étale homotopy type to
be its classifying space.

We first show that the separable closure is an affine wc covering.

{srlem}

Given a field
k
and a separable closure
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
in an algebraic closure, the affine scheme
\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k})
is a wc
object in the pro-étale site of
k
.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{Spec}(R)\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k})
be an affine covering in the pro-étale site of
k
.
Choose a point
p
of
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
.
By
[
Stacks
, Tag 092R]
its residue field
\kappa(p)
is a separable extension of
k
.
Hence there is a
k
-morphism
\kappa(p)\rightarrow\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
.
The composite
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}\rightarrow R\rightarrow\kappa(p)\rightarrow\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
is a
k
-automorphism of
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
and thus
has an inverse.
By composing the morphism
R\rightarrow\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
with this inverse we obtain a section of the morphism
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}\rightarrow R
.
We conclude that
\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k})
is wc.
∎

{srrem}

Let
k
be a field and
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
a separable closure in an algebraic closure.
Our goal is to use the affine wc covering by
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
to construct an affine wc hypercovering of
k
.
We first examine the natural hypercovering
\mathrm{cosk}_{0}(\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}))_{\bullet}
.

We can describe the underlying space of the
0
-coskeleton of a separable closure.

{srthm}

Given a field
k
and a separable closure
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
in an algebraic closure, the space underlying
\mathrm{cosk}_{0}(\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}))_{n}

is naturally isomorphic to the
n
-fold product of
\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}/k)
.

Proof.

We follow a suggestion by Annette Huber-Klawitter.
Since the field
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
is a filtered colimit of the finite Galois-extensions of
k
that are contained in
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
,
we may write

\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}\otimes_{k}\bar{k}\vphantom{k})=\lim_{l/k}\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}\otimes_{k}l)=\lim_{l/k}\coprod_{\mathrm{Gal}(l/k)}\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}).

Any connected subset of
\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}\otimes_{k}\bar{k}\vphantom{k})
gets mapped to a point under the projections of the filtered limit
since the involved spaces are discrete.
Any ideal
\mathfrak{i}
of
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}\otimes_{k}\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
can be written as

\mathfrak{i}=\colim_{l/k}(\mathfrak{i}\cap(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}\otimes_{k}l)).

In particular, this holds for any prime ideal and we conclude that any connected subset of the scheme
\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}\otimes_{k}\bar{k}\vphantom{k})
consists of a single point.
Furthermore, we have
\mathfrak{i}
is contained in
\mathfrak{p}
if and only if
\mathfrak{i}\cap(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}\otimes_{k}l)
is contained in
\mathfrak{p}\cap(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}\otimes_{k}l)
for all finite Galois-extensions
l/k
in
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
.
Hence the space underlying the spectrum of
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}\otimes_{k}\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
is naturally isomorphic to
\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}/k)
.
These calculations work analogously for an
(n{+}1)
-fold tensor product.
∎

At first sight the situation seems promising.

{srrem}

Let
k
be a field and
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
a separable closure in an algebraic closure.
After verifying the simplicial structures agree the space of components of
\mathrm{cosk}_{0}(\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}))_{\bullet}
should be given
by the classifying space of
\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}/k)
.
We run into the problem that
\mathrm{cosk}_{0}(\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}\vphantom{k}))_{\bullet}
need not be wc.
By
[
BS14
, Theorem 1.8]
a scheme is affine wc if and only if its space of components is w-local, its local rings at closed
points are strictly Henselian and its space of components is extremally disconnected.
The first two properties are satisfied in each degree since a profinite set is w-local by
[
BS14
, Example 2.1.2]
and
the local rings are given by
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
.
The problem lies in the space of components.

We give an example that illustrates the problem.

{srexa}

Let
k
be a finite field and
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}
a separable closure.
By
[
Sza09
, Examples 1.3.7.1]
the Galois-group of
\bar{k}\vphantom{k}/k
is
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Z}}}
, the profinite completion of the integers,
which is not extremally disconnected.
Assume the contrary and note that we can write
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Z}}}
as a product over all
p
-adic integers.
Since the projections of a product of spaces are open, we conclude that the
p
-adic integers are extremally disconnected.
This is absurd; see
[
MathOverflow
]
.

{srrem}

Even if the Galois-group were extremally disconnected, it is known that a product of two non-discrete extremally disconnected
groups is not extremally disconnected.
See for example
[
Sch19
, Warning 2.6]
or
[
AT08
, Problem 4.5.A]
.

For discrete groups we do not run into these problems.

{srexa}

The Galois-group of the complex numbers over the real numbers is
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Z}}/2\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Z}}
and thereby discrete.
Hence the
0
-coskeleton of the natural covering by the complex numbers is affine wc.
Furthermore, in this situation the component functor is the one from the étale setting and we conclude that
Theorem (
9
) extends to an isomorphism of simplicial spaces.
By Theorem (
7
) the pro-étale homotopy type of the real numbers is given by the classifying
space of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Z}}/2\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Z}}
.

We have the hope that Lemma (
4
) can be used to construct an affine wc hypercovering in the pro-étale site
of a field.

Chapter 10
Refined pro-étale homotopy type

{srsum}

In the following we define the pro-étale homotopy type as a pro-object of condensed sets using the machinery introduced
by Ilan Barnea and Tomer M. Schlank in
[
BS16
]
.

First, we recall the notion of condensed set.

{srrem}

A condensed set is a sheaf on the pro-étale site of an algebraically closed field and we denote their category by
\mathrm{Cond}
.
Equivalently it is a sheaf on the site of extremally disconnected spaces with finite surjective families as coverings.
The category of condensed sets provides a natural setting to combine topology with homological algebra.
Indeed, there is a faithful functor
\underline{\,\cdot\,}\colon\mathrm{Top}\rightarrow\mathrm{Cond}
that becomes full when restricted to compactly

[

We review the definition of the refined étale homotopy type from
[
BS16

{srrem}

In
[

[
BS16

The weak equivalences and fibrations in this structure are the combinatorial weak equivalences and local fibrations considered
in
[
Jar87

by John F. Jardine.
This weak fibration category induces a model structure on the category of pro-objects.
The authors use this new model structure to define a refinement of the étale homotopy type from
[

The induced functors

Here

In
[

the authors compare their definition to the one from
[

for the étale

With some modifications we can apply the theory from

{srrem}

We consider the full subcategory

The space of components induces a functor

{srlem}

Let
S

Given an affine wc object

\uppi_{\mathrm{s}}\mathrm{h}_{W}=\underline{\uppi(W)}

Let

\uppi^{\mathrm{s}}A(W)=A(\uppi(W))

We conclude that there are natural bijections

We need to verify manually that the right adjoint preserves epimorphisms to conclude that it is a weak right Quillen functor.

{srlem}

For any qcqs scheme

Let

\upgamma\circ\uppi_{\mathrm{s}}\circ j

\uppi_{0}(\mathrm{s}\mathfrak{S}_{\mathrm{fw}})\rightarrow\mathrm{Ho_{w}}(\mathrm{sCond})
.
To obtain
\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)
considered as a pro-object in
\mathrm{Ho_{w}}(\mathrm{sCond})
, we need to restrict to the homotopy category of
hypercoverings.
The inclusion
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Hy}(S_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}))\rightarrow\uppi_{0}(\mathrm{s}\mathfrak{S}_{\mathrm{fw}})
is initial by
[
Jar94
, Lemma 2.2]
.
Here we consider a hypercovering as a constant simplicial sheaf by virtue of the associated representable sheaf.

We have proved that
\upgamma\circ\tilde{\Pi}_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)
is naturally isomorphic to
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Hy}(S_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ho_{w}}(\mathrm{sCond})

as a pro-object.
By Corollary (
6
) we may further restrict to affine wc hypercoverings.
On those the functor
\uppi_{\mathrm{s}}
is given by the space of components followed by
\underline{\,\cdot\,}
by Lemma (
10
).
∎

We also obtain a natural definition for the homotopy groups of the pro-étale homotopy type.

{srrem}

For a pointed qcqs scheme our first version of the pro-étale homotopy type is profinite.
Thus we may use the theory in
[
Qui08
]
by Gereon Quick to define pro-profinite homotopy groups associated to the pro-étale
homotopy type.
But as noted in
[
BS14
, 70]
, the pro-étale fundamental group need not even be pro-discrete.

{srrem}

Given a simplicial presheaf
\mathscr{F}_{\bullet}
, we obtain a notion of homotopy groups fibred over
\mathscr{F}_{0}
as explained in

[
BS16
, 833-834]
.
For any simplicial set
E_{\bullet}
, we consider

\uppi_{n}(E_{\bullet})\coloneqq\coprod_{e\in E_{0}}\uppi_{n}(E_{\bullet},e),

where we take the coproduct over the usual pointed homotopy groups of simplicial sets.
We have a natural map
\uppi_{n}(E_{\bullet})\rightarrow E_{0}
that is functorial in
E_{\bullet}
.
For any simplicial condensed set
A_{\bullet}
we obtain a presheaf
\uppi_{n}\circ A_{\bullet}
of groups fibred over
A_{0}
on
the site of extremally disconnected spaces.
By this we mean that for any extremally disconnected space
P
, the set
\uppi_{n}(A_{\bullet}(P))
is a group fibred over the
set
A_{0}(P)
.
Note that contrary to the general case this is already a sheaf, and thereby a condensed set, since the functor
\uppi_{n}

preserves binary products.
We think of this as the
n
th condensed homotopy group of
A_{\bullet}
fibred over
A_{0}
.

Given a qcqs scheme
S
, we obtain fibred homotopy pro-groups
\uppi_{n}\circ\tilde{\Pi}_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)
associated to the refined
pro-étale homotopy type.
By definition weak equivalences of simplicial sheaves induce isomorphisms on the fibred homotopy groups.
Hence we obtain a natural isomorphism

\uppi_{n}\circ\tilde{\Pi}_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)=\uppi_{n}\circ\underline{\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S)}

by Theorem (
10
).

We unravel the definitions in the pointed setting.

{srrem}

Given a simplicial condensed set
A_{\bullet}
and a point
a
in
A_{0}(*)
, we can describe the fibred homotopy groups of

A_{\bullet}
above
a
.
They are given as the sheaves
E\mapsto\uppi_{n}(A_{\bullet}(E),a_{E})
on the site of extremally disconnected spaces, where

a_{E}
denotes the image of
a
under the natural map
A_{0}(*)\rightarrow A_{0}(E)
.
In particular, any pointed simplicial profinite set induces a pointed simplicial condensed set in the sense above.
Moreover, this construction is functorial in
A_{\bullet}
and the point
a
.

This motivates our definition of the pro-étale homotopy groups of a pointed qcqs scheme.

{srdef}

For a pointed qcqs scheme
(S,s)
the
n
th pro-étale homotopy group
is the pro-object

\uppi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}},n}(S,s)\coloneqq\uppi_{n}\circ\underline{\Pi_{\mathrm{p\acute{e}}}(S,s)}

in the category of condensed groups.
Here the functor
\uppi_{n}
on the right denotes the condensed homotopy group from Remark (
10
).

{srrem}

Our definition raises the question of its relation to the pro-profinite homotopy groups from Remark (
10
)
and, more importantly, to the pro-étale fundamental group.
At this point in time we have not been able to answer these questions.

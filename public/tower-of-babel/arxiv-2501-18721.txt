On non-uniqueness in the option valuation problem
Ekaterina A. Ladykova, Olga S. Rozanova

Abstract
. It is known [ 1 ] that the value of a call option in the case of constant elasticity processes (CEV) with the indicator α \alpha exceeding the critical α = 1 \alpha=1 is determined in a non-unique way. We show how, based on an already existing mathematical theory concerning the correctness of boundary conditions for degenerate parabolic equations on the semi-axis [ 0 , ∞ ) [0,\infty) , this phenomenon can be explained. Namely, for 1 < α ≤ 3 2 1<\alpha\leq\frac{3}{2} the non-uniqueness is due to the fact that the initial data of the call option are outside the Täcklind class, and for α > 3 2 \alpha>\frac{3}{2} it is due to the absence boundary condition for x = ∞ x=\infty .

1. Introduction.

The price of an option with the exercise function
\Phi(x)
in the case where the random process determining the dynamics of the risky asset is a process with constant elasticity (CEV), see (

(1)

\displaystyle U_{\tau}+rxU_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}x^{2\alpha}U_{xx}-rU=0,\quad x>0,\,0\leq\tau\leq T,

(2)

\displaystyle U(T,x)=\Phi(x).

Here

(3)

\displaystyle\Phi(x)=\max(0,x-K),\quad K>0.

The equation (
1

\alpha=1

[

It is one of the most widely used achievements of financial mathematics (e.g.,

[

The simplicity of this formula is due to the fact that by replacing the independent variable

However, in the process of studying real markets it became clear that it is necessary to consider a wider class of random processes than geometric Brownian motion, which is associated with the interest in considering CEV processes with exponents different from one. Formulas for the option price have long been known. They were derived for all cases of the
\alpha

[

For

,

[

The formulas are quite cumbersome and contain the so-called

However, 25 years after the publication of the option price formula for the CEV model in

This caused a surge of interest in the problem

We are interested in explaining the phenomenon of non-uniqueness from the point of view of the theory of linear degenerate partial differential equations.

Some aspects of this theory are not included in the circle of ideas used by specialists in theoretical financial mathematics.

In particular, we note that

In what follows, to avoid unnecessary details, we set

\displaystyle U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}x^{2\alpha}U_{xx},\quad x>0,\,0\leq t\leq T,

(6)

\displaystyle U(0,x)=\Phi(x).

In the problem of the price of a call option, the initial condition obtained in this way has a linear growth at infinity, but for the study of the phenomenon of non-uniqueness we will not limit ourselves to such special initial data.

Thus, in light of the results on the rules for setting boundary conditions in accordance with Fickera’s theory, the following problems arise:

1. On the uniqueness class for

with growth constraint at infinity (Tacklind class);

2. On the uniqueness class for

with growth constraint at zero;

3. On the uniqueness class for

In particular, we will show that the non-uniqueness of the solution in the option pricing problem for

5. On Tikhonov-Täklind classes.

Questions of uniqueness of the solution of the Cauchy problem for linear parabolic equations have been studied since Holmgren’s 1924 work on the heat equation. However, the fundamental work should be considered Tikhonov’s 1935 work

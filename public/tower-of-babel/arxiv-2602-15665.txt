Magnetic Hardy inequalities with singular integral weights
Hynek Kovařík, Pier Cristoforo Rossaro

Abstract
. In this paper we present Hardy type inequalities for magnetic Dirichlet forms with singular integral weights. We analyze the local and global optimality of the integral weight and discuss several examples in details. An application of our results to spectral estimates for magnetic Schrödinger operators is provided as well.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The background

The classical Hardy inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
states that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\,\geq\,\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx,\qquad n\geq 3

(1.1)

for all
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Inequality (
1.1
) is a multidimensional version of the original
one-dimensional bound proved by Hardy in 1920,
[
20
]
.
It is well known that the constant on the right hand side of (
1.1
)
is sharp and that the inequality fails, for any positive integral weight, in dimension one and two. For further reading we refer to the monographs
[
21
,
26
]
and references therein.

Hardy inequality for magnetic Dirichlet forms has much shorter history than its non-magnetic counterpart (
1.1
). Nevertheless, starting from the pioneering
work by Laptev and Weidl
[
24
]
it has produced a considerable amount of literature, see e.g.
[
30
,
29
,
4
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
and the monograph
[
3
]
. The essential contribution of
[
24
]
is the proof of the fact that introducing a magnetic field
B:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, which means replacing
\nabla
in (
1.1
) by a magnetic gradient
i\nabla+A
with
A:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\nabla\times A=B
, leads to a Hardy type inequality even in dimension two;

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(i\nabla+A)u|^{2}\,dx\,\geq\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho\,|u|^{2}\,dx\,.

(1.2)

Such a bound holds under suitable regularity conditions on
B
and
for test functions
u
in the corresponding form domain. By
[
24
,
30
]
,
we have
\rho\ \gneqq 0
as soon as
B\neq 0
. It should be pointed out however that while generically

\rho(x)\sim\,|x|^{-2}
at infinity, in a neighbourhood of zero one has
\rho(x)\sim|x|^{-2}
only in the special case of the so-called
Aharonov-Bohm magnetic field which is concentrated in one point. In fact, in that case we have

\rho(x)=\frac{\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mu-k|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\qquad\text{if}\qquad A(x)=A_{ab}(x)=\mu\,\frac{(-x_{2},x_{1})}{|x|^{2}}\,,\\

(1.3)

where the constant
\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mu-k|^{2}
is sharp, see
[
24
]
. On the other hand, if the magnetic field is sufficiently regular so that
|A|\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with

q>2
, then
\|(i\nabla+A)u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{2}^{2}
is
locally
equivalent to
\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}
, cf. Theorem
2.2
. Hence the domain of the quadratic form in (
1.2
) includes also functions which are constant in the vicinity of zero, and therefore the weight function
\rho
cannot be as singular as in (
1.1
) and (
1.3
). To see this observe that the function
|\cdot|^{-2}
is not locally integrable in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Yet the validity of (
1.2
) for a weight function
\rho
with an integrable singularity in the origin cannot be excluded a priori. Nonetheless, in all the above mentioned papers concerning dimension two, with the exception of
[
10
]
, the integral weight
\rho
is
locally bounded
.

The main object of interest of
[
10
]
are magnetic operators
on Heisenberg group. However, in
[
10
, Rem. 6.6]
the authors observe that their method can be applied also to magnetic Schrödinger operators to show that if
B
is smooth, compactly supported and such that
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\not\in\mathbb{Z}
, then inequality (
1.2
) holds with

\rho(x)\,=\frac{c(B,r_{0})}{r^{2}\big[1+(\log_{+}r_{0}/r)^{2}\big]}\,,\qquad r=|x|.

(1.4)

Here
r_{0}
is large enough so that supp
B\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ |x|<r_{0}\}
, and
\log_{+}
denotes the positive part of the logarithm. The constant in (
1.4
) depends only on the magnetic field and on the choice of
r_{0}
.

In this paper, inspired by
[
10
]
, we address the following questions. First, what is the strongest possible singularity of
\rho
in the Hardy inequality (
1.2
)
generated by a regular magnetic field? Here and below we call a magnetic field
B:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}

regular
if

B\in L^{p}_{\rm\,loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\quad\text{for some}\quad p>1.

(1.5)

Second, what is the relation between the local behavior of
\rho
and
B
in a situation in which the field is not regular but still belongs to
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
? Recall that
that the Aharonov-Bohm magnetic field generated by the vector
A_{ab}
given in (
1.3
) is not locally integrable since in that case we have
B(x)=0
for any
x\neq 0
, while
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\neq 0
.

As for the first question, it turns out that the weight (
1.4
) found in
[
10
]
is the best possible, at least as far as the logarithmic scale is concerned, for
all regular
magnetic fields, see Theorem
1.1
and Remark
1.2
. Moreover, the decay rate of
\rho_{0}
is optimal also at infinity if no further restrictions on
B
are introduced, see Remark
1.3
and Proposition
2.5
.

To answer the second question we consider, for definiteness, magnetic fields
B\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with a single isolated singularity in the origin. The behavior of
\rho
in a neighborhood of zero is then characterized
in terms of the (normalized) flux of
B
through the ball of radius
r
centered in the origin:

\alpha(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|x|<r}B(x)\,dx.

(1.6)

Accordingly we define

\Phi(r)=\frac{\alpha(r)}{r}\,.

(1.7)

Our main result concerning singular fields then implies that if
the singularity of
B
is strong enough, then
\rho(x)\sim\Phi(r)^{2}
as
r\to 0
, see Theorem
1.6
for more details. We also characterize the domain on which the quadratic form
\|(i\nabla+A)u\|_{2}^{2}
is closed, and show how it depends on the asymptotic behavior of the magnetic field around zero. This is done in Corollary
3.4
.

Before stating our main results let us fix some necessary notation.

Notation

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an arbitrary set and
f,g:X\to\mathbb{R}
. In the following, we write

f(x)\lesssim g(x)

if
there exists a constant
c>0
, such that
f(x)\leq c\,g(x)
for all
x\in X
. The symbol
\gtrsim
is defined accordingly,. We also write
f(x)\asymp g(x)
if
f(x)\gtrsim g(x)
and
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
.

By
\mathscr{B}_{R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\,|x|<R\}
we denote the ball of radius
R
centered at the origin.

1.2.
Main results

Throughout the paper we denote by

Q_{A}[u]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(i\nabla+A)u|^{2}\,dx

(1.8)

the quadratic form associated to
A
. The associated graph norm is given by

\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert u\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert_{A}=\big(Q_{A}[u]+\|u\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\,.

(1.9)

Let

d_{0}(Q_{A})=\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\,\rvert\hskip-0.98329pt\rvert\hskip-0.98329pt\rvert\cdot\rvert\hskip-0.98329pt\rvert\hskip-0.98329pt\rvert_{A}}\,.

(1.10)

Our first result shows that the singularity in (
1.4
) is generic for all regular magnetic fields.

Theorem 1.1

(
Hardy inequality for regular magnetic fields).

Let
B\neq 0
satisfy (
1.5
), and let
A:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a vector field such that
\nabla\times A=B
. Then there exists
a constant
C_{B}
such that for all
u\in d_{0}(Q_{A})
we have

Q_{A}[u]\,\geq\,C_{B}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho_{0}\,|u|^{2}dx

(1.11)

where

\rho_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{r^{2}(1+\log^{2}r)}\,.

(1.12)

A couple of comments concerning Theorem
1.1
are in order

Remark 1.2

(
Optimality at zero).

The power of the logarithm on the right hand side of (
1.11
) is optimal locally in the vicinity of zero in the
following sense. Given any magnetic field satisfying (
1.5
) the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{B}_{1/e}}\dfrac{|u|^{2}}{r^{2}\,|\log r|^{b}}\,dx\ \leq\ c\,Q_{A}[u]\qquad u\in d_{0}(Q_{A})

(1.13)

fails for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
and any
b<2
. To see this consider the radial function
u_{\alpha}(r)=|\log_{+}(1/r)|^{\alpha}
. Clearly
u_{\alpha}
is compactly supported and a quick calculation shows that
u_{\alpha}\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
any

\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
.
Hence, by Theorem
2.2
(1), we have
|(i\nabla+A)u_{\alpha}|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. On the other hand, the left hand side of
(
1.13
) with
u=u_{\alpha}
is finite if and only if
b>1+2\alpha
.

Remark 1.3

(
Optimality at infinity).

Without further restrictions on the magnetic field, the power of the logarithm on the right hand side of (
1.11
) is optimal also at infinity.
This follows from Proposition
2.5
below. Note however, that for magnetic fields with finite non-integer flux the logarithmic factor in (
1.11
) can be removed, see
[
24
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

In the last section of the paper we discuss an application of inequality (
1.11
) to spectral inequalities for two-dimensional magnetic Schrödinger operators, see Theorem
4.1
.

In order to state our main result for singular fields we need to introduce some additional notation. We say that a magnetic field
B
is
singular
if it can be decomposed into
a sum

B=B_{s}+B_{r},

(1.14)

where
B_{r}
satisfies (
1.5
), and
B_{s}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
has an isolated singularity in the origin which is strong enough so that

\forall\,\varepsilon>0:\quad\lim_{r\to 0}r^{-\varepsilon}\!\!\int_{\mathscr{B}_{r}}B_{s}\,dx=\pm\infty.

(1.15)

Notice that, in view of the decomposition (
1.14
), this condition contradicts the validity of (
1.5
) for
B
.

Assumption 1.5
.

Suppose that
B
satisfies (
1.14
) and (
1.15
) with
B_{s}
being bounded on compact
subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
.

The domain of the quadratic form
Q_{A}
is then given by

d(Q_{A})=\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})}^{\,\rvert\hskip-0.98329pt\rvert\hskip-0.98329pt\rvert\cdot\rvert\hskip-0.98329pt\rvert\hskip-0.98329pt\rvert_{A}}\,.

(1.16)

In the next result we quantify the local contribution of the flux to the integral weight in the Hardy inequality.

Theorem 1.6

(
Hardy inequality for singular magnetic fields).

Let
B
satisfy Assumption
1.5
. Then there exists
\eta>0
and a constant
c(B,\eta)
such that
for all
u\in d(Q_{A})
we have

Q_{A}[u]\,\geq\,c(B,\eta)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho\,|u|^{2}\,dx\,,

(1.17)

where

\rho(r)=\begin{cases}\rho_{0}(r)+\Phi^{2}(r)&\text{ if}\quad r\leq\eta\\[3.0pt]
\rho_{0}(r)&\text{ if}\quad r>\eta\end{cases}\,.

(1.18)

Recall that
\alpha(\cdot)
is defined in (
1.6
).

Remark 1.7
.

It follows from equation (
1.6
) that the term
\Phi^{2}(r)
can be arbitrarily close to the singularity generated by the Aharonov-Bohm vector potential
A_{ab}
, cf. equation (
1.3
). Hence the weight function covers, by varying
\alpha(\cdot)
, the whole “gap” between
\rho_{0}(\cdot)
and
|\cdot|^{-2}
.
Explicit examples are given in Section
3.1
, see in particular Example (
3.22
).

Remark 1.8
.

Notice also that in the case of the Aharonov-Bohm field, which is not covered by Theorem
1.6
, we have
\alpha(r)=
constant
, see (
1.3
).

Remark 1.9
.

A comparison of equations (
1.18
) and (
1.12
) shows that the contribution from
\Phi
becomes dominant as soon as
\alpha(r)\to 0
slowly enough so that

|\alpha(r)\log r|\to\infty
as
r\to 0
. This defines also the borderline case in which the graph norm
\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\cdot\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert_{A}
is no longer locally equivalent to the
H^{1}-
norm. Indeed,
by Corollary
3.4
we have

d(Q_{A})=\big\{u\in d_{0}(Q_{A_{r}}):\ |\Phi u|\in L_{\rm loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\big\}\,,

(1.19)

where
A_{r}
satisfies
\nabla\times A_{r}=B_{r}
, cf. (
1.14
).

2.
Regular magnetic fields

We start by describing our choice of the gauge. Given a regular magnetic field
B
we consider a distributional solution of the equation

-\Delta h=B\,.

(2.1)

Then the vector field
A:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by

A=(\partial_{x_{2}}h,-\partial_{x_{1}}h)

(2.2)

satisfies
\nabla\times A=B
in the sense of distributions.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
B
satisfies (
1.5
) and let
A
be given by (
2.2
). Then
|A|\in L^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
q>2
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
B\in L^{p}_{\rm\,loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
1<p<2
. By the elliptic regularity we then have
h\in W_{{\rm loc}}^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
see e.g.
[
19
, Thm. 9.15]
. Now the Sobolev imbedding, see e.g.
[
25
, Thm. 8.8]
, implies that
|\nabla h|\in L^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any

q\leq\frac{2p}{2-p}
. Since
\frac{2p}{2-p}>2
by assumption, this proves the claim.
∎

In view of Lemma
2.1
and
[
27
, Theorem 2.2]
it follows that the quadratic form (
1.8
)
is closed on the domain

d_{0}(Q_{A})=\big\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\ |(i\nabla+A)u|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\big\}.

(2.3)

where
\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert u\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert^{2}_{A}
is defined in (
1.9
).

The following theorem, which is due to Weidl, will be important in the proof of our main results.

Theorem 2.2

(Weidl)
.

Suppose that
|A|\in L^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
q>2
.

(1)

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded connected Lipschitz domain and let
\Omega\subseteq U
be a set of positive Lebesgue
measure. Then
for any
u\in H^{1}(U)
,

\|(i\nabla+A)u\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\ \asymp\ \|u\|_{H^{1}(U)}^{2}\,.

(2.4)

(2)

If
B\neq 0
, then for any compact set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of positive Lebesgue measure there exists a constant
c(\Omega,B)
such that

Q_{A}[u]\geq c(\Omega,B)\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,dx

(2.5)

for all
u\in d(Q_{A})
.

For the proof of Theorem
2.2
we refer to
[
30
, Cor. 9.2]
and
[
29
, Lemma 4.1]
.

We now prove a result which plays a crucial role in the proof of inequality (
1.11
).
In order to simplify the notation in the sequel, given a bounded set
\Omega
we introduce the shorthand

\|u\|_{L^{2}_{w}(\Omega)}=\Big\|\dfrac{u}{r\log(r/r_{0})}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

(2.6)

where
r_{0}
is large enough so that
\Omega\Subset\mathscr{B}_{r_{0}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a compact set with
C^{1}
boundary and
suppose that
\Omega
contains an open neighbourhood of the origin.
Let
r_{0}
be such that
\Omega\Subset\mathscr{B}_{r_{0}}
. Then, for any
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{w}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ C\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,.

(2.7)

where the constant
C
depends only on
\Omega
.

Note that
w\notin L^{p}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
p>1
. Hence (
2.7
)
doesn’t follow from standard imbedding theorems.

Proof.

Let
u\in\ H^{1}(\Omega)
.
We consider the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big|\nabla u+\dfrac{fu}{r}x\big|^{2}dx\,\geq\,0,

(2.8)

where
f
is a radial function to be determined later. Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla u+\dfrac{fu}{r}x\Big|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+\dfrac{f}{r}\nabla(u^{2})\cdot x+f^{2}u^{2}\right)dx,

(2.9)

and an integration by parts implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{f}{r}\nabla(u^{2})\cdot xdx=\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{2}\dfrac{f}{r}x\cdot\nu d\sigma-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\nabla\cdot\left(\dfrac{xf}{r}\right)dx\,.

(2.10)

Here
\nu
denotes the external normal unit vector to the boundary. Considering (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\big(f^{\prime}+\dfrac{f}{r}-f^{2}\big)\,dx\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+BT(u),

(2.11)

where we have denoted

BT(u):=\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{2}\,\dfrac{f}{r}\,x\cdot\nu d\sigma

(2.12)

Choose

f=-\dfrac{1}{2r\log(r/r_{0})}

(2.13)

so that

f^{\prime}+\dfrac{f}{r}-f^{2}=\dfrac{1}{4r^{2}(\log(r/r_{0}))^{2}}\,.\\

(2.14)

Next we treat the boundary term (
2.12
).
Since
0\not\in\partial\Omega
by assumptions, we have

|f|_{\partial\Omega}\lesssim 1
. Hence by the boundary trace theorem, see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 5.22]
,
there exists a constant
C=C(\Omega)
such that

BT(u)\ \lesssim\ \displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{2}d\sigma\ \lesssim\ \|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\,.

(2.15)

Inserting this bound together with (
2.14
) into (
2.11
) proves (
2.7
)
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The fact that
\|u\|_{L^{2}_{w}(\Omega)}<\infty
for any
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is known, see e.g.
[
9
]
,
[

]
,
[

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Owing to the gauge invariance of inequality (
1.11
) we may work with the vector
A
given by (
2.2
).
In view of Lemma
2.1
it suffices to prove the claim for any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Without loss of generality we may suppose that
B
is not identically zero in
\mathscr{B}_{3}
. Then by Theorem
2.2
(1)

\|(i\nabla+A)u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\geq C_{0}\,\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathscr{B}_{3})}

(2.16)

This in combination with the diamagnetic inequality
[
25
, Theorem 7.21]

|\nabla|u||\leq|(i\nabla+A)\,u|\qquad\text{a.~e.}

(2.17)

and Lemma
2.3
implies

\|(i\nabla+A)u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\ \gtrsim\ \||u|\|_{H^{1}(\mathscr{B}_{3})}^{2}\ \gtrsim\ \int_{\mathscr{B}_{3}}\dfrac{|u|^{2}}{{r^{2}(1+\log^{2}r)}} dx\,.

(2.18)

It remains to extend inequality (
2.18
) to (
1.11
). We have to show that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(i\nabla+A)\,u|^{2}\,dx\ \geq\ C\int_{\mathscr{B}_{3}^{c}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{r^{2}(\log r)^{2}}\,\,dx,

(2.19)

for some
C
. We follow the argument of
[
23
]
. In view of the diamagnetic inequality (
2.17
), and the fact that
e<3
, it suffices to prove that for all
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}|f^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\,r\,dr+c\int_{0}^{e}|f(r)|^{2}\,r\,dr\geq\,C\int_{e}^{\infty}\frac{|f(r)|^{2}}{r\,(\log r)^{2}}\,dr.

(2.20)

Define the function
\xi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\xi(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad}cr}c&\text{if \,}&0<r\leq 1\\
c(2-r)&\text{if \,}&1<r\leq 2\end{array}\right.,\qquad\xi(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad}cr}c(r-2)&\,\text{if }&2<r\leq e\\
c&\,\text{if }&e<r\end{array}\right..

An integration by parts gives

\int_{0}^{\infty}|(\xi f)^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\,r\,dr+c(1-c)\int_{0}^{e}|f(r)|^{2}\,r\,dr\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|f^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\,r\,dr+c\int_{0}^{e}|f(r)|^{2}\,r\,dr.

Now, since
\xi(2)=0
, we integrate by parts again to get

\int_{2}^{\infty}\left((\xi f)^{\prime}(r)-\frac{(\xi f)(r)}{2r\log r}\right)^{2}r\,dr=\int_{2}^{\infty}|(\xi f)^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\,r\,dr-\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{|(\xi f)(r)|^{2}}{4r\,(\log r)^{2}}\,dr.

The last two equations imply (
2.20
) and hence (
2.19
).
∎

To show that the power of the logarithmic factor on the right hand side of (
1.11
) is optimal at infinity, we need the following improvement of
[
16
, Proposition 2.8]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
B
satisfy (
1.5
) and suppose that

|B(x)|=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-\nu}),\ \quad\nu>2,\quad|x|\to\infty.

(2.21)

Assume moreover that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\,dx=2\pi m,\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2.22)

Suppose that there exists a function
\mathrm{w}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
such that

Q_{A}[u]\,\geq\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{w}\,|u|^{2}\,\,dx\qquad\forall\,u\in d_{0}(Q_{A}).

(2.23)

Then for
any

\alpha<1
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{w}\,(\log_{+}r)^{\alpha}\,\,dx<\infty.

(2.24)

Proof.

To prove (
2.24
) we use the Poincaré gauge

A(x)=(-x_{2},x_{1})\int_{0}^{1}B(tx)\,t\,dt\,.

(2.25)

We then have

Q_{A}[u]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big[\,|\partial_{r}u|^{2}+r^{-2}|(i\partial_{\theta}+ra(r,\theta))u|^{2}\Big]\,r\,drd\theta,

(2.26)

where,

a(r,\theta)=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}B(t,\theta)\,t\,dt

(2.27)

denotes the azimuthal component of the vector potential. We define

\varphi(r,\theta)=\theta(m-\alpha(r))+r\!\!\int_{0}^{\theta}a(r,s)ds\,.

(2.28)

and construct a sequence of test functions
u_{n}
as follows;

u_{n}(r,\theta)=e^{i\varphi(r,\theta)}\,(\log_{+}r)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\ \dfrac{\log_{+}(n/r)}{\log n}

(2.29)

Then
u_{n}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and the support of
u_{n}
is compact. Hence, by Theorem
2.2
(1),
u_{n}\in d(Q_{A})
. Moreover,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}Q_{A}[u_{n}]<\infty.

(2.30)

Indeed, the radial derivative part of
Q_{A}[u_{n}]
gives

\displaystyle|\partial_{r}u_{n}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(\log n)^{2}}\left|\partial_{r}\left[(\log r)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\log(n/r))\right]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2}{(\log n)^{2}}\left((\partial_{r}(\log r)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})^{2}(\log(n/r))^{2}+(\log r)^{\alpha}(\partial_{r}\log(n/r))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2}{(\log n)^{2}\,r^{2}}\left[(\log n)^{2}(\log r)^{\alpha-2}+(\log r)^{\alpha}\right],

see (
2.29
).
Hence, keeping in mind that
\alpha<1
we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{r}u_{n}|^{2}\,rdr

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{1}^{n}|\partial_{r}u_{n}|^{2}\,rdr<\infty.

As for the azimuthal derivative, we compute

|(i\partial_{\theta}+ra(r,\theta))u_{n}|^{2}=(m-\alpha(r))^{2}\,|u_{n}|^{2}\,.

By equation (
2.22
) and assumptions on
B
,

\displaystyle\big|m-\alpha(r)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{r}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{2\pi}|B(t,\theta)|\,t\,dtd\theta\,\leq\,\frac{C}{(1+r)^{\nu-2}}\,

with a constant
C
independent of
n
. Since
\alpha<1
, this gives

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2}|(i\partial_{\theta}+ra(r,\theta))u_{n}|^{2}\,rdr

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{1}^{n}\frac{(\log r)^{\alpha}}{r(1+|\log r|)^{2}}\,dr<\infty\,.

Hence we have proved (
2.30
).
On the other hand, for any
r
and
\theta
we have
|u_{n}(r,\theta)|\leq|u_{n+1}(r,\theta)|
. Moreover,

\lim_{n\to\infty}|u_{n}(r,\theta)|^{2}=(\log_{+}r)^{\alpha}

(2.31)

pointwise in
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,2\pi)
. Equation (
2.24
) now follows from monotone convergence by applying
inequality (
2.23
) to
u=u_{n}
and letting
n\to\infty
.
∎

3.
Singular magnetic fields

The set up

We use again the Poincaré gauge (
2.25
).
The quadratic form (
1.8
) is then closed on the domain (
1.16
). As in the proof of Proposition
2.5
we
write
Q_{A}[u]
in the polar coordinates, see equation (
2.26
). Following
[
24
]
we introduce, for any fixed
r>0
, the self-adjoint operator
K_{r}
acting in
H^{1}([0,2\pi))
as

K_{r}v=i\partial_{\theta}v+rav,

(3.1)

with
a=a(r,\theta)
defined in (
2.27
). As pointed out in
[
24
]
, the eigenvalues of
K_{r}
are given by

\lambda_{m}(r)=m-\alpha(r),\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.2)

Recall that
\alpha
is defined in (
1.6
). Indeed, from the Stokes theorem and (
1.6
) we infer that

\alpha(r)=\frac{r}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}a(r,\theta)d\theta\,.

Moreover, since the spectrum of
K_{r}
is purely discrete, its normalized eigenfunctions, given by

v_{m}(r,\theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\ e^{-i\big(\theta\lambda_{m}(r)-r\!\!\int_{0}^{\theta}a(r,s)ds\big)}\,,\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}

(3.3)

form a complete orthonormal basis of
L^{2}([0,2\pi))
. For a given
u\in d(Q_{A})
, we can thus expand
u(r,\cdot)
into the Fourier series with respect to this basis to
obtain

u(r,\theta)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{m}(r)v_{m}(r,\theta),\qquad u_{m}(r)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(r,\theta)\,\overline{v}_{m}(r,\theta)\,d\theta\,.

(3.4)

The Parseval identity then gives

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u(r,\theta)|^{2}\,d\theta=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|u_{m}(r)|^{2}\qquad\forall\,r>0.

(3.5)

Hence, by (
2.26
),

Q_{A}[u]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\partial_{r}u|^{2}\,r\,drd\theta+\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}r^{-2}\,\lambda^{2}_{m}(r)\,|u_{m}(r)|^{2}\,r\,dr.

(3.6)

Following the decomposition (
1.14
) we now split the vector potential accordingly as follows;

A=A_{s}+A_{r},\qquad A_{s,r}(x)=(-x_{2},x_{1})\int_{0}^{1}B_{s,r}(tx)\,t\,dt\,.

(3.7)

Observe that by Lemma
2.1
, there exits
q>2
such that

A_{r}\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\,.

(3.8)

Since
B\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
we have
\alpha(r)\to 0
as
r\to 0
. Hence there exists

\delta>0
such that

|\alpha(r)|\,\leq\,\frac{1}{4}\qquad\forall\,r\leq 2\delta.

(3.9)

Let
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
be a smooth radial function such that

\chi(r)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if}\quad r\leq\delta\\[3.0pt]
0&\text{ if}\quad r\geq 2\delta\end{cases}

(3.10)

We have

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be given by (
3.7
), and let
\chi
be as above.
Then, for
any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})
,

\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Phi^{2}|\chi u|^{2}\,dx\,\leq\,\|(i\nabla+A)(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}\,\leq\,2\|\nabla(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Phi^{2}|\chi u|^{2}\,dx\,.

(3.11)

Proof.

We expand
\chi u
into the Fourier series with respect to the basis (
3.3
). Since
\chi
is radial, we have
(\chi u)_{m}(r)=\chi(r)u_{m}(r)
. This implies

\|(i\nabla+A)(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\partial_{r}(\chi u)|^{2}\,r\,drd\theta+\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}r^{-2}|\lambda_{m}(r)|^{2}|\chi u_{m}|^{2}\,r\,dr\,.

(3.12)

By (
3.9
) we have
\alpha(r)\leq\frac{1}{4}
on the support of
\chi
. Hence

\lambda^{2}_{m}(r)\,\chi^{2}\,\geq\,\frac{1}{2}\big(m^{2}+\alpha^{2}(r)\big)\chi^{2}\qquad\forall\,r>0,\ \ \forall\,m\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(3.13)

Since

\|\nabla(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\partial_{r}(\chi u)|^{2}\,r\,drd\theta+\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}r^{-2}m^{2}|\chi u_{m}|^{2}\,r\,dr,

again by Parseval, inserting (
3.13
) into (
3.12
) proves the lower bound in (
3.11
). The upper bound follows in the
same way since
\lambda_{m}^{2}(r)\leq 2m^{2}+2\alpha^{2}(r)
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In view of the decomposition (
1.14
) we may assume, without loss of generality, that
B_{s}
is compactly supported.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
B
satisfy Assumption
1.5
and let
A
be as above. Let
\chi
be as in Lemma
3.1
. Then for any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})

\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert u\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert^{2}_{A}\ \asymp\ \|(i\nabla+A_{r})u\|^{2}_{2}+\|\chi\Phi u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|^{2}_{2}\,.

(3.14)

Recall that the
\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert u\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert^{2}_{A}
is defined in (
1.9
).

Proof.

Below we denote by
C
a generic positive number whose value may change
line to line.
Let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})
. We use a version of the IMS localization formula similarly as it was done in the proof of
[
12
, Prop. 1.1]
,
see also
[
8
, Sec. 2.1]
.
Let
\zeta:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]

satisfy
\chi^{2}+\zeta^{2}=1
.
From Assumption
1.5
and Remark
3.2
it follows that

|\zeta A_{s}|\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

(3.15)

Using the fact that
\chi\nabla\chi+\zeta\nabla\zeta=0
we deduce the identity

|(i\nabla+A)u|^{2}=|(i\nabla+A)(\chi u)|^{2}+|(i\nabla+A)(\zeta u)|^{2}-|u\nabla\chi|^{2}-|u\nabla\zeta|^{2}\,.

(3.16)

Since
\chi
and
\zeta
are smooth, this combined with (
3.15
), Lemma
3.1
and the elementary inequality
|a+b|^{2}\leq 2|a|^{2}+2|b|^{2}
implies

\displaystyle\|(i\nabla+A)u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\leq 4\|\nabla(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}+4\|\chi\Phi u\|_{2}^{2}+4\|(i\nabla+A_{r})\zeta u\|_{2}^{2}+4\|\zeta A_{s}u\|_{2}^{2}+C\|u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8\|\chi\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+8\|\chi\Phi u\|_{2}^{2}+4\|\zeta(i\nabla+A_{r})u\|_{2}^{2}+C\|u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8\|\chi\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+4\|\chi\Phi u\|_{2}^{2}+8\|(i\nabla+A_{r})u\|_{2}^{2}+C\|u\|_{2}^{2}\,.

From Theorem
2.2
(1) and (
3.8
) it follows that that

\|\chi\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\,\lesssim\ \|(i\nabla+A_{r})u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{2}^{2}.

This completes the proof of the upper bound in (
3.14
).

To prove the lower bound we use again Lemma
3.1
and the inequality

|a+b|^{2}\geq(1-\eta)|a|^{2}+(1-\eta^{-1})|b|^{2}\qquad\forall\,a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\ \eta\in(0,1).

(3.17)

From (
3.16
) and (
3.15
) we then deduce that

\displaystyle\|(i\nabla+A)u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\geq\,\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\chi\Phi u\|_{2}^{2}+(1-\eta)^{2}\|\zeta(i\nabla+A_{r})u\|_{2}^{2}-C\|u\|_{2}^{2}.

(3.18)

Applying (
3.17
) a couple of more times we further get

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\geq\,(1-\eta)\,\|\chi\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-C\|u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\eta)^{2}\|\chi(i\nabla+A_{r})u\|_{2}^{2}-C\|u\|_{2}^{2}-(1-\eta^{-1})(1-\eta)\|\chi A_{r}u\|^{2}\,.

To control the last term we notice that, by
the compactness of the imbedding
H^{1}(\mathscr{B}_{2\delta})\hookrightarrow L^{s}(\mathscr{B}_{2\delta}),\ s>2
,
for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant

\|\chi u\|^{2}_{L^{s}(\mathscr{B}_{R})}\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|\chi u\|_{2}^{2}

This combined with the Hölder inequality, (

2.2
(1) gives

\displaystyle\|\chi A_{r}u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,\varepsilon\,\|A_{r}\|_{L^{q}(\mathscr{B}_{2\delta})}\,\|\nabla(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\,C\|(i\nabla+A_{r})(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}+\widetilde{C}_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon\,C\|\chi(i\nabla+A_{r})u\|_{2}^{2}+2\varepsilon\,C\|\nabla\chi\|^{2}_{\infty}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\widetilde{C}_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\,.

Taking

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\chi u)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\geq\,c\|\chi(i\nabla+A_{r})u\|_{2}^{2}-C\|u\|_{2}^{2}.

with some

.
In view of (

) this implies

\displaystyle\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert u\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert\hskip-1.19995pt\rvert^{2}_{A}\,\gtrsim\,\|(i\nabla+A_{r})u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Phi\chi u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{2}^{2}

(3.19)

as claimed.
∎

Example 1

Consider the singular magnetic field

B_{s}(x)=B_{s}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad}cr}\displaystyle{\frac{b_{0}}{r^{2}|\log r|^{\gamma}}}&\,\text{if }&r\leq e^{-1}\\[10.0pt]
0&\,\text{if}&e^{-1}<r\end{array}\right.,\qquad\gamma>1

(3.22)

where
b_{0}\neq 0
is a constant. The condition
\gamma>1
assures that
B_{s}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
A short calculation shows that

\int_{\mathscr{B}_{r}}B_{s}\,dx=\frac{2\pi\,b_{0}}{\gamma-1}\,|\log r|^{1-\gamma}\qquad r\leq e^{-1}\,.

Condition (
1.15
) is thus satisfied and Theorem
1.6
gives

\rho(r)\,=\,r^{-2}\Big(|\log r|^{-2}+\frac{b_{0}^{2}}{(\gamma-1)^{2}}|\log r|^{2-2\gamma}\Big),\qquad r\leq e^{-1}

Hence
\rho\asymp\rho_{0}
as long as
\gamma\geq 2
. In fact, by Corollary
3.4
,
we have
d(Q_{A})=d_{0}(Q_{A_{r}})
for all
\gamma\geq 2
.

Notice also that if
\gamma\leq\frac{3}{2}
, then the singularity of
\rho
in the origin is not integrable. Accordingly,
by inequality (
1.17
),

\forall\,u\in d(Q_{A}):\quad\liminf_{x\to 0}|u(x)|=0\qquad\text{for}\quad\gamma\leq\frac{3}{2}.

B_{s}(x)=B_{s}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad}cr}\displaystyle{\frac{b_{0}}{r^{2}\,|\log r|\big(\log|\log r|\big)^{\gamma}}}&\,\text{if }&r\leq e^{-2}\\[10.0pt]
0&\,\text{if}&e^{-2}<r\end{array}\right.,\qquad\gamma>1,\ \ b_{0}\neq 0.

(3.23)

Then we have again
B_{s}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and Theorem
1.6
implies

\rho(r)=\frac{b_{0}^{2}}{(\gamma-1)^{2}\,r^{2}}\big(\log|\log r|\big)^{1-\gamma}\,\big(1+o(1)\big)\qquad r\to 0.

4.
Eigenvalues of magnetic Schrödinger operators

In this section we consider an application of inequality (
1.11
) to spectral estimates for a magnetic
Schrödinger operator

(i\nabla+A)^{2}-V

(4.1)

in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Here
V:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is an electric potential. We denote by
N((i\nabla+A)^{2}-V)

the number of negative eigenvalues, counted with their multiplicities, of the operator (
4.1
).
To motivate our result let us recall some known upper bounds on
N((i\nabla+A)^{2}-V)
established earlier in the
literature. Set

\mathcal{V}(r):={\rm ess}\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi}V_{+}(r,\theta)

(4.2)

and let

L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}))=\Big\{V\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{V}(r)\,r\,dr<\infty\Big\}.

(4.3)

In
[
23
]
it has been shown, under certain conditions on
B
, that

N((i\nabla+A)^{2}-V)\ \lesssim\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{V}(r)(1+|\log r|)\,r\,dr,

(4.4)

and that for any
\alpha>0
there exists a constant
c_{\alpha}
such that

N((i\nabla+A)^{2}-V)\ \leq\,c_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\,V_{+}^{1+\alpha}(1+|x|)^{2\alpha}\,dx\,.

(4.5)

These bounds hold for all
V
for which the corresponding right hand side is finite. Similar bounds have been derived in
[
18
]
for the Aharonov-Bohm magnetic field.

However, neither (
4.4
) nor (
4.5
) can be applied to potentials with singularities strong enough as
to produce a non semi-classical behavior of the counting function

N((i\nabla+A)^{2}-V)
in the strong coupling limit. A typical example of such a potential is

V_{\sigma}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad}cr}r^{-2}\,|\log r|^{-2}\,\big(\log|\log r|\big)^{-1/\sigma}&\text{if\, \,}&r<e^{-2}\\[3.0pt]
0&\,\,\text{if \, }&r\geq e^{-2}\end{array}\right.

(4.6)

with
\sigma>1
, which has been invented in
[
7
]
for two-dimensional Schrödinger operators without magnetic field. Note
that for any
\sigma>1
we have
V_{\sigma}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
while the right hand sides of (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) are infinite for
V=V_{\sigma}
.
In fact, using the results of
[
7
]

it was proved in
[
23
]
,
[
5
]
that, under suitable conditions on
B
,

N\big((i\nabla+A)^{2}-\lambda V_{\sigma}\big)\,\asymp\,\lambda^{\sigma}\qquad\text{as}\quad\lambda\to\infty\,.

(4.7)

With the help of inequality (
1.11
) we shall prove an upper bound on
N((i\nabla+A)^{2}-V)
which is applicable also to singular potentials similar to
(
4.6
). We will also show that our upper bound is order sharp in the strong coupling limit, see Remark
4.2
.

To state our result we need some additional notation.
Given
a>1
and
V\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}))
we define the functional

[V]_{a}=\Big(\sup_{t>0}\,t^{a}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho_{0}(r)\,(1+|\log r|)\,rdr\Big)^{\frac{1}{a}}\,,

(4.8)

where
\rho_{0}
is given by (
1.12
) and where

\Omega_{t}=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\mathcal{V}(r)>t\rho_{0}(r)\big\}\,.

We have

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
B
satisfy Assumption (
1.5
). Then for any
a>1
there exists a constant
c(B,a)
such that

N((i\nabla+A)^{2}-V)\,\leq\,c(B,a)\,[V]_{a}^{a}\\

(4.9)

for all
V\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}))
for which the right hand side is finite.

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward modification of
[
5
, Theorem 6.1]
which uses the method of
[
18
]
, see also
[
7
]
.
Let
C_{B}
be the constant in (
1.11
) and let
0<\delta<1
. We set
s=\delta\,C_{B}
and using (
1.11
) we
estimate
(i\nabla+A)^{2}-\mathcal{V}

in the sense of quadratic forms in the following way:

\displaystyle(i\nabla+A)^{2}-\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=\delta((i\nabla+A)^{2}-\rho_{0}\,C_{B})+(1-\delta)\big((i\nabla+A)^{2}-(1-\delta)^{-1}\rho_{0}(\rho_{0}^{-1}\mathcal{V}-s)\big)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\delta)\big((i\nabla+A)^{2}-(1-\delta)^{-1}\rho_{0}(\rho_{0}^{-1}\mathcal{V}-s)_{+}\big).

(4.10)

Hence

N((i\nabla+A)^{2}-V)\leq N((i\nabla+A)^{2}-\mathcal{V})\,\leq\,N((i\nabla+A)^{2}-(1-\delta)^{-1}\rho_{0}(\rho_{0}^{-1}\mathcal{V}-s)_{+}).

Applying inequality (
4.4
) to the last term we obtain

N((i\nabla+A)^{2}-V)\,\leq\,C(1-\delta)^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho_{0}(r)\big(\rho_{0}(r)^{-1}\mathcal{V}(r)-s\big)_{+}\,(1+|\log r|)\,r\,dr.

The layer cake representation,
[
25
, Theorem 1.13]
, gives

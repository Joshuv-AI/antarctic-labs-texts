Symmetry of concentration and scaling for self-bounding functions
George Crowley and Iñaki Esnaola

Abstract
. We prove generalised concentration inequalities for a class of scaled self-bounding functions of independent random variables, referred to as ( M , a , b ) {(M,a,b)} self-bounding. The scaling refers to the fact that the component-wise difference is upper bounded by an arbitrary positive real number M M instead of the case M = 1 M=1 previously considered in the literature. Using the entropy method, we derive symmetric bounds for both the upper and lower tails, and study the tightness of the proposed bounds. Our results improve existing bounds for functions that satisfy the ( a , b a,b ) self-bounding property.

3.
Main results

Theorem 3

(Symmetry of concentration of
{(M,a,b)}
self-bounding functions)
.

Let
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
be a vector of independent random variables, each taking values in the set
\mathcal{X}
and let
f:\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative,
(M,a,b)
self-bounding, and measurable function such that
Z=f({\bf X})
has finite mean. Then for
\lambda<\frac{2}{aM}
, it follows that

(3)

\displaystyle\log\mathds{E}[e^{\lambda(Z-\mathds{E}[Z])}]

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)M\lambda^{2}}{2\left(1-\frac{aM\lambda}{2}\right)}.

Additionally, the following holds:

(a)

For all
t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z-\mathds{E}[Z]\geq t)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{M}\frac{t^{2}}{2(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)+at}\right).

(b)

For all
0<t\leq\mathds{E}[Z]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z-\mathds{E}[Z]\leq-t)\leq\exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{M}\dfrac{t^{2}}{2(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)+at}\right).

The proof of Theorem
3
relies on an updated inequality bound shown in Lemma
1
. To prove the new lower bound, we leverage Theorem
6
. Previous approaches in the literature use only one part of Harris’s inequality (typically for the upper tail); however, we leverage both inequalities to obtain both tail results.

Theorem 4

(Improved concentration of
{(M,a,b)}
self-bounding functions)
.

Assume the assumptions for Theorem
3
hold. Define the functions
\delta(a,M)_{+}
and
\delta(a,M)_{-}
as

(4)

\displaystyle\delta(a,M)_{+}\coloneq\begin{cases}0,&a\leq\frac{1}{3},\\
a-\frac{1}{3},&\frac{1}{2}<M\leq 1,\\
a-\frac{1}{3},&M>1\ \text{and}\ a<\frac{M}{3(M-1)},\\
a,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases},\quad\delta(a,M)_{-}\coloneq\begin{cases}0,&a\geq\frac{1}{3},\\
a,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then the following holds:

(a)

For all
t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z-\mathds{E}[Z]\geq t)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{M}\frac{t^{2}}{2(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)+\delta(a,M)_{+}t}\right).

(b)

For all
0<t\leq\mathds{E}[Z]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z-\mathds{E}[Z]\leq-t)\leq\exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{M}\dfrac{t^{2}}{2(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)+\delta(a,M)_{-}t}\right).

Remark 2
.

For
0<M\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
\delta(a,M)_{+}
can be strengthened such that for any
a>\frac{1}{3},\ \delta(a,M)_{+}\coloneq a-\frac{1}{3}
, but the inequality only holds for

(5)

\displaystyle t\leq\dfrac{a\mathds{E}[Z]+b}{a-\frac{1}{3}}\left(\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{a^{2}M\left(1-M+\dfrac{2M}{3a}-\dfrac{1}{3a}\right)}+a(M-1)}{M(a-\frac{1}{3})-a}\right)^{-2}-1\right).

Note that as
M\to 0,
the bound decreases to
0
.

Remark 3
.

In the case that
a<\frac{1}{3}
, then the result in Theorem
3
improves on Theorem
2
, since their coefficient of
t
is
\frac{2}{3}
and our result holds for

.

4.
Scaling

To validate the result for
M>1
, we show that there is indeed improved concentration in most instances for the upper tail, when compared to rescaling an
(M,a,b)
self-bounding function to an
(a,b)
self-bounding function. Let
f({\bf x})
be an
(M,a_{1},b_{1})
self-bounding function and set
g({\bf x})\coloneq\frac{f({\bf x})}{M}
so that
g({\bf x})
is
(a_{2},b_{2})
self-bounding, i.e. it satisfies both

\displaystyle 0\leq g({\bf x})-g_{i}({\bf x}^{(i)})

\displaystyle\leq 1,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g({\bf x})-g_{i}({\bf x}^{(i)}))

\displaystyle\leq a_{2}g({\bf x})+b_{2}.

Multiplying the bottom equation by
M
implies that
a_{2}=a_{1}
and
b_{2}=Mb_{1}
, so that
g
is
(a_{1},Mb_{1})
self-bounding. It follows that
\mathds{E}[g({\bf X})]=\frac{1}{M}\mathds{E}[Z]
. Hence, by Theorem
1
with
M=1
for
g
, it follows that

(6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(g({\bf X})-\mathds{E}[g({\bf X})]\geq t\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{Z}{M}-\frac{\mathds{E}[Z]}{M}\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2(a_{2}\frac{\mathds{E}[Z]}{M}+b_{2}+c_{+}t)}\right)

(7)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2(a_{1}\frac{\mathds{E}[Z]}{M}+Mb_{1}+c_{+}t)}\right).

By applying Theorem
4
,

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{Z}{M}-\frac{\mathds{E}[Z]}{M}\geq t\right)=\mathbb{P}(Z-\mathds{E}[Z]\geq Mt)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{M}\frac{M^{2}t^{2}}{2(a_{1}\mathds{E}[Z]+b_{1})+\delta(a,M)_{+}Mt}\right)

(9)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{Mt^{2}}{2(a_{1}\mathds{E}[Z]+b_{1})+\delta(a,M)_{+}Mt}\right)

(10)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{\frac{2}{M}(a_{1}\mathds{E}[Z]+b_{1})+\delta(a,M)_{+}t}\right).

It then remains to show that for
M>1
, which of the following expressions obtained from both self-bounding results obtains better bounds, i.e.

(11)

\displaystyle\argmin_{M}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2(a_{1}\frac{\mathds{E}[Z]}{M}+Mb_{1}+c_{+}t)}\right),\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{\frac{2}{M}(a_{1}\mathds{E}[Z]+b_{1})+\delta(a,M)_{+}t}\right)\right)

(12)

\displaystyle=\argmax_{M}\left(\frac{t^{2}}{2(a_{1}\frac{\mathds{E}[Z]}{M}+Mb_{1}+c_{+}t)},\frac{t^{2}}{\frac{2}{M}(a_{1}\mathds{E}[Z]+b_{1})+\delta(a,M)_{+}t}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle=\argmin_{M}\left(2(a_{1}\frac{\mathds{E}[Z]}{M}+Mb_{1}+c_{+}t),\frac{2}{M}(a_{1}\mathds{E}[Z]+b_{1})+\delta(a,M)_{+}t\right)

(14)

\displaystyle=\argmin_{M}\left(2Mb_{1}+2c_{+}t,\frac{2b_{1}}{M}+\delta(a,M)_{+}t\right).

Assume
a_{1}>\frac{M}{3(M-1)}
such that
2c_{+}=a_{1}-\frac{1}{3}
and
\delta(a_{1},M)_{+}=a_{1}
. Then, for

(15)

\displaystyle t\leq\dfrac{2b_{1}}{3}\left(M-\dfrac{1}{M}\right),

the bound in Theorem
4
is tighter. If
b_{1}=0
, then scaling exhibits the additional term
-t/3
. When
\delta(a,M)_{+}=2c_{+}
, the bound in Theorem
4
is always less than the scaling result when
b_{1}>0
, or equal to the scaling result when
b_{1}=0
. For the lower tail, it is evident that Theorem
4
obtains better concentration than Theorem
2
even when rescaling.

\displaystyle\left(\lambda-a\frac{\psi(-M\lambda)}{M}\right)G^{\prime}(\lambda)-G(\lambda)\leq(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)\frac{\psi(-M\lambda)}{M}.

We proceed by exploring the cases

\lambda<0

(1)

\lambda>0

Since

for

[
3
]
, by setting

, and dividing by

, (

) yields

(38)

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{aM}{2}\right)G^{\prime}(\lambda)-\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{2}}G(\lambda)\leq(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)\frac{M}{2},\ \text{for}\ \lambda\in\left(0,\frac{2}{aM}\right).

Following the same derivation approach as in
[

]
results in

(39)

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{aM}{2}\right)G(\lambda)\right)=\left(\dfrac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{aM}{2}\right)G^{\prime}(\lambda)-\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{2}}G(\lambda).

By observing that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{1}{s}G(s)=0.

Hence, integration of (

\displaystyle G(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)\lambda^{2}M}{2(1-\frac{aM\lambda}{2})},\ \text{for}\ \lambda\in\left(0,\frac{2}{aM}\right).

This concludes the proof for case
\lambda>0
.

(2)

Case
\lambda<0

From Corollary

, we note that for
\lambda<0,G^{\prime}(\lambda)<0
. Using the inequality
\psi(-x)\geq\frac{x^{2}}{2}
for
x<0
, it follows that

(42)

\displaystyle\lambda>\lambda-\frac{a(-\lambda M)^{2}}{2M}\geq\lambda-a\frac{\psi(-\lambda M)}{M},

multiplying by

(43)

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda<G^{\prime}(\lambda)\left(\lambda-\frac{a(-\lambda M)^{2}}{2M}\right)\leq G^{\prime}(\lambda)\left(\lambda-\frac{a\psi(-\lambda M)}{M}\right).

Combining (

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(\lambda)\left(\lambda-\frac{a\lambda^{2}M}{2}\right)-G(\lambda)\leq(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)\frac{\psi(-\lambda M)}{M},\ \text{for}\ \lambda<0.

Dividing by
\lambda^{2}
and integrating from
\lambda
to

(45)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{aM}{2}\right)G(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)\int_{\lambda}^{0}\frac{\psi(-sM)}{s^{2}M}\,ds

(46)

\displaystyle=(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)\int_{0}^{\lambda}-\frac{\psi(-sM)}{s^{2}M}\,ds.

Using

for

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{aM}{2}\right)G(\lambda)\leq-(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)\frac{\lambda M}{2}.

and the cumulant bound given in (

Lemma 3
.

Let

on

and for every
\lambda\in I

\displaystyle f(\lambda)G^{\prime}(\lambda)-f^{\prime}(\lambda)G(\lambda)\leq f^{2}(\lambda)g(\lambda).

.

Proof.

The proof of this result can be found in

Lemma 4
.

Let
I
be an open interval containing
0
and let
\rho
be continuous on
I
. Let also
a\geq 0
and
v>0
. Let
H:I\to\mathbb{R}
, be an infinitely many times differentiable function satisfying

(54)

\displaystyle M(\lambda H^{\prime}(\lambda)-H(\lambda))\leq\rho(\lambda)(aH^{\prime}(\lambda)+a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)

with

(55)

\displaystyle aH^{\prime}(\lambda)+a\mathds{E}[Z]+b>0,\quad\text{for}\ \lambda\in I\ \text{and}\ H^{\prime}(0)=H(0)=0.

Consider the function
\rho_{0}:I\to\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
G_{0}:I\to\mathbb{R}
is infinitely many times differentiable such that for
\lambda\in I
,

(56)

\displaystyle 1+aG^{\prime}_{0}(\lambda)>0,\quad\text{and}\quad\ G_{0}(0)=G_{0}^{\prime}(0)=0,\ \quad\text{and}\quad\ G^{\prime\prime}_{0}(0)=M.

Assume that
G_{0}
solves the differential equation

(57)

\displaystyle M(\lambda G^{\prime}_{0}(\lambda)-G_{0}(\lambda))=\rho_{0}(\lambda)(aG^{\prime}_{0}(\lambda)+1).

Then if
\rho(\lambda)\leq\rho_{0}(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in I
, it holds that
H\leq vG_{0}
.

Proof.

There are some differences between this lemma and
[
3
, Lemma 4]
, so whilst the proof structure is similar, for the sake of completeness, we present it in full. Using the assumption that
\rho(\lambda)\leq\rho_{0}(\lambda)
and the rest of the assumptions presented in the statement of the lemma, it follows that

(58)

\displaystyle M(\lambda H^{\prime}(\lambda)-H(\lambda))\leq\dfrac{M(\lambda G^{\prime}_{0}(\lambda)-G_{0}(\lambda))}{aG^{\prime}_{0}(\lambda)+1}(aH^{\prime}(\lambda)+v),

and thus, expressing the inequality in terms similar to Lemma
3
yields

(59)

\displaystyle(\lambda+aG_{0}(\lambda))H^{\prime}(\lambda)-(1+aG^{\prime}_{0}(\lambda))H(\lambda)\leq(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)(\lambda G^{\prime}_{0}(\lambda)-G_{0}(\lambda)).

Define the functions
f(\lambda)=\lambda+aG_{0}(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in I
and
g:I\to\mathbb{R}
as the piecewise function

(60)

\displaystyle g(\lambda)=\begin{cases}(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)\dfrac{(\lambda G^{\prime}_{0}(\lambda)-G_{0}(\lambda))}{(\lambda+aG_{0}^{\prime}(\lambda))^{2}},&\lambda\neq 0,\\
(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)\dfrac{M}{2},&\lambda=0.\end{cases}

Then the function

is continuous on

, and by Lemma

, it holds that

(61)

\displaystyle H(\lambda)\leq f(\lambda)\int_{0}^{\lambda}g(x)\,dx=(a\mathds{E}[Z]+b)f(\lambda)\int_{0}^{\lambda}\left(\dfrac{G_{0}(x)}{f(x)}\right)^{\prime}\,dx,

and the result follows. This concludes the proof.

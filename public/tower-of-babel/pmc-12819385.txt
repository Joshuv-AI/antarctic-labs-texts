A hybrid machine vision and handcrafted features fusion based approach for fine-grained millet classification
Authors: B Baisakh, Prabira Kumar Sethy, Richa Gupta, Santi Kumari Behera, Aziz Nanthaamornphong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMillets are increasingly recognized as climate-resilient, nutrient-rich grains essential for food and nutritional security. However, the limited availability of annotated datasets and the visual similarities among millet varieties such as Barnyard Millet, Little Millet, and Proso Millet pose significant challenges in their accurate identification and classification. To address these issues, this study proposes two novel hybrid frameworks that leverage both handcrafted and deep learning paradigms. A key component shared by both frameworks is the incorporation of a custom-designed handcrafted feature extraction process. The first framework introduces a Stacked Ensemble Machine Learning approach, that integrates handcrafted techniques and machine learning classifiers as Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), and K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) classifiers using a multinomial logistic regression meta-learner. The second framework adopts a bidirectional deep learning architecture; wherein handcrafted feature vectors are fused with the pretrained VGG19 model to achieve effective classification of different millet varieties. The stacked ensemble hybrid model achieved the 97% classification accuracy where as the second model achieved the highest accuracy of 96.7%, an F1-score of 0.967, and a ROC-AUC of 0.995, while maintaining excellent tradeoff between training and inference times.Keywords: Millet classification, KNN, SVM, Ensemble model, Handcrafted feature extraction, CNN model, VGG19, ResNet50Subject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing, Plant sciences

Introduction

Millets are increasingly recognized as a vital solution for addressing food security, combating climate change, and enhancing nutritional value. These small-seeded grains are well-suited for cultivation in challenging environments, making them a promising alternative to traditional staple crops like rice and wheat. Millets have the potential to significantly improve food security, especially in regions facing agricultural challenges due to climate change. They require less water and can thrive in low-nutrient soils, which allows them to be cultivated in marginal lands that are unsuitable for other crops. The production of millets can help diversify food sources and increase resilience against food shortages caused by extreme weather events or resource scarcity. For instance, increasing the area under coarse cereals, including millets, can enhance nutritional supply by improving protein and iron levels while also reducing greenhouse gas emissions and the demand for irrigation water and energy1. The adaptability of millets to harsh environ- mental conditions, such as drought and high temperatures, positions them as climate-smart crops. They have a lower carbon footprint compared to rice and wheat, contributing to reduced greenhouse gas emissions2. Millets are rich in essential nutrients, including proteins, dietary fiber, vitamins, and minerals like iron, magnesium, and calcium. These nutritional benefits can help combat malnutrition and related health issues such as anemia and cardiovascular diseases1,3,4. Given these compelling agronomic and nutritional attributes, there is an urgent need to leverage advanced technologies such as machine vision5–7 and deep learning to automate millet classification and quality evaluation. Accurate and efficient identification of millet varieties through computer vision not only facilitates improved post harvest processing and supply chain transparency but also empowers breeders, farmers, and industries with smart tools to preserve genetic diversity and enhance crop utilization.

The classification of millets using traditional techniques poses significant challenges due to the morphological diversity among different millet species. Different millet types (e.g., Barnyard, Little, Proso) exhibit significant visual similarity, especially in their seed morphology fine-grained variations in shape, texture, and color. Traditional classification techniques often rely on manual inspection or biochemical tests, which are labor-intensive, time- consuming, and require expert knowledge. Furthermore, minute morphological differences, especially in grain size and surface texture, make the task of identification prone to human error8. as shown in the Fig. 1.a, 1.b, and 1.c. These physical characteristics are often subtle and overlapping, making visual identification highly subjective and error-prone. Traditional manual sorting methods rely heavily on human expertise and perception, which can lead to inconsistencies and inefficiencies, especially when processing large quantities. Additionally, environmental factors such as lighting, grain cleanliness, and moisture content further complicate the accuracy of manual classification. Hence, the inherent variability and complex visual traits of millets make their accurate categorization through conventional methods both time-consuming and unreliable.

Machine learning approaches for classification

Machine learning techniques have become pivotal in the classification of crops in agriculture, leveraging vast amounts of data to enhance decision- making and optimize production. Several traditional machine learning (ML) methods have also been applied for crop classification: Decision Trees (DT) and Random Forest (RF) methods have been utilized to classify crops based on indices derived from satellite imagery, focusing on accuracy and efficiency9. These methods are particularly useful in handling datasets with fixed features for land plots. Support Vector Machines (SVM) have been employed for both classification and regression tasks to predict crop yields and recommend suitable crops based on environmental parameters10,11. K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) has shown high precision in mapping summer crops using multi-temporal multispectral data12. Feature Engineering and Data Preprocessing To improve model performance, various preprocessing techniques are applied, such as: Feature Engineering: This involves selecting and transforming raw data into meaningful features that can enhance model accuracy. For example, one study highlighted the importance of using indices like NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) for better classification outcomes12. Data Imputation: Techniques like Multivariate Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE) are used to handle missing data caused by environmental factors10.

Deep learning approaches for classification

Deep learning has emerged as a powerful tool in agricultural and biological image analysis13,14, particularly in seed and grain classification. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) remain one of the most widely used architectures due to their ability to extract hierarchical and spatial features from grid-like data such as images. Early architectures like LeNet15 laid the foundation for CNN-based visual tasks. Subsequent models such as AlexNet16, VGGNet17, and GoogLeNet18 introduced deeper architectures, small- sized kernels, and inception modules to enhance feature extraction and model expressiveness. ResNet19 further advanced this field by addressing vanishing gradient issues through residual connections, enabling the training of significantly deeper networks.

For mobile and embedded deployments, lightweight models such as MobileNet20, ShuffleNet21, and EfficientNet22 provide efficient compu- tation with reduced parameter loads. These architectures are particularly relevant in low-resource settings such as on-field millet quality evaluation.

Machine vision, particularly when integrated with machine learning and deep learning methodologies, has become an effective approach for species and grain classification. In agricultural contexts such as millet classification, these techniques offer a powerful combination of cost-effectiveness and precision, especially relevant for high-throughput phenotyping and field-level applications23. Once trained, such models can be deployed with minimal hardware (e.g., standard cameras or scanners) and without requiring users to have deep domain expertise24.

Fine-grained classification, a subdomain of deep learning addresses these challenges by focusing on distinguishing sub-categories within closely related classes. It is particularly suitable for applications such as millet classification, where inter-class differences can be minute and subtle25. Models like Bilinear Pooling (BP) have been proposed to replace fully connected layers with bilinear interactions, which better capture the feature dependencies necessary for distinguishing fine-grained traits26. Despite BP’s effectiveness, its high computational cost due to high-dimensional feature representations has led to the development of Compact Bilinear Pooling (CBP), which reduces com- plexity using approximations such as Random Maclaurin and Tensor Sketch projections27–29.

Towards fine-grained millet classification: challenges and contributions

Despite the increasing interest in leveraging machine learning for agricultural phenotyping, the application of these techniques to millet classification remains underexplored. Most prior works have focused on major crops such as rice, wheat, and maize, leaving minor cereals like millets underrepresented in both academic literature and public datasets30. The unique characteristics of millet grains including small size, fine texture, subtle variations in color, and morphological similarity across species pose significant challenges for conventional classification models.

The problem of millet classification is inherently a fine-grained recognition task. Different millet species such as Barnyard Millet, Little Millet, and Proso Millet often exhibit marginal inter-class visual differences, requiring models that can capture intricate features beyond what simple color or size descriptors can reveal. Techniques such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Bilinear Pooling (BP), Compact Bilinear Pooling (CBP), and Hierarchical Bilinear Pooling (HBP) have demonstrated their strengths in handling such subtle inter-class variations in other domains26,27,31. However, their direct application to millet classification has not yet been systematically investigated.

Moreover, the scarcity of publicly available millet datasets limits progress in this area. Existing crop datasets focus predominantly on high-resolution images of leaves or fruits, with limited representation of grain-level phenotypes. To address this gap, we developed a custom millet image dataset consisting of three distinct classes: Barnyard Millet, Little Millet, and Proso Millet, with 500, 255, and 500 images respectively, totaling 1,255 samples and make available in “Aku, Baisakh; sethy, PRABIRA (2025), “Small Scale Millet Dataset”, Mendeley Data, V1, doi: 10.17632/g7wvpp7pd2.1”. The images were captured under controlled lighting conditions and annotated manually to ensure data quality.

Our findings suggest that combining handcrafted descriptors with deep visual embeddings significantly improves the classification performance, demonstrating the promise of hybrid architectures in low-data, fine-grained classification tasks like millet species identification. Given the nutritional importance and ecological resilience of millets2,3, advancing computational tools for their classification is both scientifically and socially relevant. Our work lays the foundation for future research in this domain and highlights the need for more extensive datasets and bench- marking frameworks for millet grain recognition.

The organization of the article is as follows: Section II reviews prior work on the application of machine learning and deep learning in fine-grained classification tasks; Section III provides a detailed description of the methodologies employed in this research; Section IV presents a thorough evaluation of the results and performance of various models applied to millet classification; Section V discusses the importance of feature fusion in millet classification, accompanied by an ablation study; and Section VI concludes the study, summarizing key findings and outlining potential directions for future research.

This section describes the data acquisition, preprocessing and the proposed methodologies designed for fine-grained millet classification. Two distinct frameworks are developed, each tailored to explore the performance of machine learning and deep learning paradigms. The strategies are categorized into two subsections: machine learning-based hybrid techniques and deep convolutional learning with handcrafted feature fusion.

Data collection and preparation

Customed dataset creation

In this study, a custom image dataset was constructed to facilitate fine- grained classification of three similar millet varieties: Barnyard Millet, Little Millet, and Proso Millet. The raw images were captured using a smartphone camera in controlled lighting environments to maintain uniformity in visual quality. To incorporate natural variations and generalize the model, the data acquisition process included images taken from multiple angles, distances, and lighting conditions (both natural and artificial). Each image sample was labeled accurately under expert supervision to ensure class integrity and illustrated in Fig. 1.

All images were captured at a high resolution of 3472 × 4624 pixels, with an average image file size of approximately 3.8 MB. The imaging device had a 5.43 mm lens with an aperture of f/1.89, providing sufficient detail and clarity to identify subtle grain characteristics. The images were stored in 24-bit RGB format with a resolution of 96 dpi as shown in the Table 1.

Data pre-processing and augmentation

Data preprocessing plays a crucial role for millet classification for the two major reasons; first, since the dataset is captured in the burst mode, similar images may lead to overfitting. Second, since there is subtle visual discrimination among the three millet types based on their colour, shape and size, it may lead to poor intraclass misclassification. In this study, the following steps are included as the part of preprocessing methods.

A)Image Normalization and Resizing: The captured millet images are resized to the standard resolution as 128 × 128 pixels to maintain the uniformity across the proposed models i.e. the stack ensembled machine learning model and Hybrid Handcrafted VGG19 CNN model. Handcrafted feature extraction

In the process of Handcrafted feature extraction, each image goes through a three-step processes. First, shape features such as contour-based area and perimeter are extracted by applying Otsu’s thresholding followed by contour detection using OpenCV. The largest contour is selected to calculate these metrics. Second, texture features are extracted using the Gray-Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM), from which contrast, homogeneity, and energy are computed. These features capture the spatial relationship between pixel intensities. Third, Local Binary Patterns (LBP) are used to capture local micro- textures. The uniform LBP operator with a radius of 1 and 8 neighbors is applied, and the resulting histogram is normalized and added to the feature set.

Methods

In this article two different classification approaches are adopted for small scale fine grain millet classification i.e. Handcrafted Feature Based Stack Ensemble Model (Machine Learning Approach) and Handcrafted Fusion-Based VGG19 Hybrid Model (Deep Learning Approach). In both the models the preprocessing steps remain the same and input images are transformed and extracted systematically prior to fit into the classification pipeline of the two proposed hybrid models.

Handcrafted feature based stack ensemble model: a hybrid machine learning approach for millet classification

The preliminary study of this article is to explore the performance of machine learning approaches for fine grain millet classification. As a result, a handcrafted feature-based machine learning model has been proposed as the first contribution of this article.

Model architecture and description

In the proposed stacked ensemble classification framework, it leverages the handcrafted image features for fine-grained millet classification as shown in the Fig. 3. The method integrates traditional machine learning classi- fiers to enhance generalization and classification accuracy across three millet classes: Barnyard Millet, Little Millet, and Proso Millet. The methodology comprises four key stages: data preparation, handcrafted feature extraction, model construction, and evaluation as mentioned in the Algorithm 1.

Proposed stack ensembled handcrafted model.

Stage:1 In the first stage, it loads the customed millet dataset which comprises images of three different types such as Barnyard Millet, Little Millet, and Proso Millet. Then the preprocessing step is applied to these millet images to transform them into a standardized feature vector. This step ensures the quality of feature extraction that is performed in the next stage of the classification process.

Stage:2 In this stage, important visual attributes are extracted through a self-designed handcrafted features extraction technique. The shape features include the parameters such as area and perimeter, which are important for differentiating millet types that vary in kernel size and shape. Parameter like Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) plays a crucial role in mining the texture features such as contrast, homogeneity and energy. These all are the important indicators of grain roughness and compactness. Local Binary Patter (LBP), captures the micro texture pattern of millet images by thresholding neighborhood pixels. This extracted information are then passed onto the classifier models for effective millet classification with the constrain of limited available dataset.

Stage: 3 In this stage, classical machine learning models are employed as base and meta learners to classify the different class of millets with optimum accuracy. A stacked ensemble learning approach was employed to integrate multiple base classifiers, each of which contributes to improved generalization and robustness. The architecture includes the classification framework employs three base learners: K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Random Forest (RF), and Support Vector Machine (SVM) with a linear kernel. These base learners generate prediction probabilities for each sample.

These probabilities are then stacked and fed into a Multinomial Logistic Regression classifier, which serves as the meta learner, to make the final prediction decision through a stacked ensemble approach.

The probabilistic outputs from the base models are concatenated into a 9-dimensional stacked vector . Then the meta-learner integrates these probabilities to produce the final decision. This stacking mechanism effectively leverages the complementary strengths of different classifiers to achieve higher predictive accuracy.

Stage: 4 Finally, the proposed model is trained using 5-fold cross- validation to avoid overfitting and then rigorously evaluated by confusion matrix and F1-score.

The primary objective of this hybrid model is to evaluate how effective deep neural architecture are towards fine grain millet grains which have extremely small surface area and size. It is a dual pipeline framework, from one direction the VGG-19 with extracted features vectors are concatenated with only Handcrafted featured vectors. The second proposed model under the domain of deep learning, is one of the sophisticated frameworks as it fuses deep convolutional features from a pretrained VGG19 network with handcrafted features in a multimodal learning pipeline.

Model architecture and description

The proposed Handcrafted Fusion-Based VGG19 Hybrid Model combines deep learning and handcrafted feature extraction through three key phases as shown in the Fig. 4. The basic working principle of this mode is shown through the algorithm 2 First, the deep learning pipeline pro- cesses RGB images by resizing to 128 × 128 pixels, normalizing to [0, 1], and extracting hierarchical features using a modified VGG19 architecture. The network’s five blocks progressively capture low-level edges (Blocks 1–2), mid-level textures (Blocks 3–4), and high-level grain arrangements (Block 5), with final features flattened and compressed to 128 dimensions via a Swish- activated dense layer. Concurrently, the handcrafted feature pipeline processes grayscale versions of the input to compute: (1) shape features (area and perimeter from contour detection), (2) GLCM texture features (contrast, homogeneity, energy), and (3) a 10-bin LBP histogram, forming a 15- dimensional vector later projected to 64D. The fusion phase concatenates both feature streams, processes them through a 256-unit Swish-activated dense layer with dropout (p = 0.5), and produces final predictions via a 3- class softmax output. This hybrid approach leverages the complementary strengths of learned deep features and interpretable traditional features for robust millet classification.

The two streams are then concatenated to form a unified feature vector, followed by a dense fusion layer of 256 neurons and a Dropout layer to prevent overfitting. The final output layer used a softmax activation function to classify into three millet categories.

The model was trained using the Adam optimizer and sparse categorical cross-entropy loss. Performance was monitored using early stopping, learning rate reduction on plateau, and model checkpointing to save the best validation model.

Performance evaluation of handcrafted based stack ensemble machine learning classification approach

This subsection presents the performance evaluation of the proposed stack ensemble machine learning framework, which utilizes handcrafted features extracted from millet images. The handcrafted features include shape descriptors (area and perimeter), texture metrics derived from the Gray-Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM), and local micro-texture patterns using Local Binary Patterns (LBP).

Train-Test Splitting. To validate the generalization capabilities of the models, the dataset was split into training and testing subsets using an 80:20 ratio. We employed stratified splitting to maintain the original class distribution across both subsets. This is especially important for multi-class millet datasets, where class imbalance could lead to biased evaluation re- sults. The stratification ensures that the trained models are exposed to all classes adequately during training and that each class is fairly represented during testing.

Classifier Training and Evaluation. Three well-established classifiers were employed: K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Support Vector Machine (SVM) with a radial basis function (RBF) kernel, and Random Forest (RF). These models were trained on the flattened feature vectors. KNN was used with k = 5, while the RF model was trained with 100 decision trees. The SVM model used the RBF kernel to capture non-linear decision boundaries between millet classes. Each classifier’s prediction performance was assessed using accuracy as the primary metric. Additionally, confusion matrices were constructed to visualize misclassifications and evaluate class-specific performance.

Result Visualization. To enhance interpretability, confusion matrices were plotted using Seaborn’s heatmap utility. These plots revealed the classifier’s ability to distinguish between the three millet classes. Misclassification trends, such as confusion between visually similar millet types (e.g., Little Millet vs. Proso Millet), were analyzed to understand the limitations of traditional methods. The results from this phase serve as a performance bench- mark and motivate the need for more advanced deep learning and hybrid feature extraction techniques.

Discussion

As per our limited knowledge, so far no prior studies have been done on fine grain multi class millet classification. So the article has provided a excellent benchmark to evaluate how are the performance of machine learning and deep learning approaches with classical image processing techniques known as Handcrafted feature Extraction, while applied for fine grain small scale millet classification.

The proposed stacked ensemble machine learning approach showed that the model superiority over classical machine learning models with 97% of accuracy.

But further, the millet classification were performed on the proposed hybrid VGG-19 model and it was observed that the model achieved the highest weighted performance score (0.9696), significantly outperforming other architectures such as VGG19 (0.6123), ResNet50 (0.2759), EfficientNetB0 (0.1382), and MobileNetV2 (0.1324) as shown in the Figure 14. This indicates that the hybrid integration of deep convolutional and handcrafted feature representations yields a more discriminative and robust model for millet classification. The Key Performance Metrics plot also confirms this observation, with the Hybrid VGG19 model demonstrating close alignment between accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. In terms of resource utilization, the Hybrid VGG19 model maintains a reasonable trade-off between computational cost and predictive strength. Although it exhibits slightly higher training (48.3 s) and inference time (41.2 s) compared to the baseline VGG19. However, it remains significantly more efficient than deeper models like EfficientNetB0 and ResNet50, which incur substantially higher inference delays.

Finally, the Performance vs. Efficiency trade-off visualization reveals that the Hybrid VGG19 occupies the most favorable position, achieving the highest accuracy with an reasonable computational footprint. This demonstrates that the model effectively balances performance and efficiency, making it a suitable choice for real world agricultural applications where both accuracy and computational constraints are critical.

Conclusion and future work

This research work provides one of the first comprehensive study on small scale fine grain millet classification using hybrid ensemble and deep learning based frameworks. This study presents two novel hybrid models; the Stacked Ensemble Machine Learning Model and Handcrafted Fusion based VGG19 Model. Both the models are complemented by the self-designed handcrafted feature extraction techniques to enhance the discriminative power to classify millets. The stacked ensemble hybrid model has a multinomial logistic regression based meta learner, that effectively leveraged the SVM, RF and KNN models to perform the millet classification with the average accuracy of 97%.

On the other hand, a bidirectional hybrid deep learning pipeline model was designed not only to produce effective accuracy, but also to ensure excellent performance-efficiency tradeoff, since one of the strongest assumptions of this article is that the available data sample is limited and very small. The proposed hybrid VGG19 model achieved a weighted performance score of 0.9696, significantly surpassing conventional CNN and transfer learning baselines.

These two frameworks can serve as the foundational benchmarks for future research exploring various aspects of millet classification and analysis using artificial intelligence. Further studies may focus on millet grading and quality evaluation by offering ample scope for innovative investigation.

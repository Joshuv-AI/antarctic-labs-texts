Quasi-fixed points of substitutive systems
Elżbieta Krawczyk

Abstract
. We study automatic sequences and automatic systems generated by general constant length (nonprimitive) substitutions. While an automatic system is typically uncountable, the set of automatic sequences is countable, implying that most sequences within an automatic system are not themselves automatic. We provide a complete and succinct classification of automatic sequences that lie in a given automatic system in terms of the quasi-fixed points of the substitution defining the system. Our result extends to factor maps between automatic systems and highlights arithmetic properties underpinning these systems. We conjecture that a similar statement holds for general nonconstant length substitutions.

1.
Substitutive and automatic systems

In this section we present some preliminary definitions and lemmas which will be used throughout the rest of the paper. Since there is no systematic study of general nonminimal substitutive systems yet, we give some proofs extending the facts known in the minimal case to the general set-up considered by us.

Let

A one-sided sequence

1.3.
Topological dynamics

A (topological) dynamical system
is a compact metrisable space
X
together with a continuous map
T\colon X\rightarrow X
. If
T
is a homeomorphism, we say that
(X,T)
is an
invertible dynamical system
. A point
x\in X
is
periodic
if
T^{k}(x)=x
for some
k\geqslant 1
. A dynamical system is called
aperiodic
if it does not contain any periodic points.
If
T\colon X\to X
is a homeomorphism, we let
\mathscr{O}(x)=\{T^{n}(x)\mid n\in{\mathbf{Z}}\}
denote the two-sided orbit of a point
x\in X
.

A
subsystem
of
X
is a closed subset
Y
of
X
that is invariant under the map
T
, i.e.
T(Y)\subset Y
. A system
X
is
minimal
if
X\neq\emptyset
and if
X
has no subsystems other than
X
and the empty set. Equivalently, a system
X\neq\emptyset
is minimal if the orbit of every point is dense in
X

[
31
, Prop. 2.7]
. An invertible system
X
is
transitive
if there exists
x\in X
such that
\overline{\mathscr{O}(x)}=X
.

A dynamical system
(Y,S)
is a
(topological) factor
of the system
(X,T)
if there exists a continuous surjective map
\pi\colon X\rightarrow Y
such that
\pi\circ T=S\circ\pi
. Such a map
\pi
is called
a factor map
. Two dynamical systems
(X,T)
and
(Y,S)
are
conjugate
(or
isomorphic
) if there exists a homeomorphism
\pi\colon X\rightarrow Y
such that
\pi\circ T=S\circ\pi
; in this case we call the map
\pi
a
conjugacy
.

A dynamical system
T\colon X\rightarrow X
is
equicontinuous
if the family of maps

\{T^{n}\colon X\rightarrow X\mid n\geqslant 0\}

is equicontinuous. By classical results, every dynamical system has a maximal equicontinuous factor
[
31
, Thm. 2.44]
and every minimal equicontinuous system is conjugate to a translation on a compact abelian group
[
31
, Thm. 2.42]
.

In this paper we will be mainly concerned with symbolic systems coming from finitely-valued sequences.
For an alphabet
\mathscr{A}
, the set
\mathscr{A}^{\mathbf{Z}}
with the product topology (where we use discrete topology on each copy of
\mathscr{A}
) is a compact metrisable space.
The dynamics on
\mathscr{A}^{{\mathbf{Z}}}
is given by the
shift map

T\colon\mathscr{A}^{\mathbf{Z}}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{\mathbf{Z}}\quad T((x_{n})_{n})=(x_{n+1})_{n},

which acts by shifting the sequence one index to the left. The space
\mathscr{A}^{\mathbf{Z}}
together with the shift map
T
is an invertible dynamical system. We refer to subsystems of
\mathscr{A}^{\mathbf{Z}}
as
subshifts
.

For a subshift
X
, we let

\mathscr{L}(X)=\bigcup\{\mathscr{L}(x)\mid x\in X\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{L}^{r}(X)=\bigcup\{\mathscr{L}^{r}(x)\mid x\in X\}

denote the
language
and
r
-
language
of
X
.
Each subshift is uniquely determined by its language
[
31
, Prop. 3.17]
; in particular, two subshifts are equal if and only if their languages coincide. The above definitions can be adapted in a straightforward way for one-sided subshifts
X\subset\mathscr{A}^{{\mathbf{N}}}
.

Let

is minimal if and only if

such that for all

A common operation in symbolic dynamics consists of looking at blocks of consecutive symbols and treating them as the letters of a new, often larger, alphabet. This is done by considering the so-called higher block presentation systems.
For a sequence or a finite word

1.7.
Recognisability of substitutive systems

Let
X_{\varphi}
be a purely substitutive system generated by a substitution
\varphi
. It is not difficult to show that each point
x\in X_{\varphi}
can be ’desubstituted’ within
X_{\varphi}
, that is, there exist
x^{\prime}\in X_{\varphi}
and
c\in{\mathbf{N}}
such that

(5)

x=T^{c}\varphi(x^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leqslant c<\left|\varphi(x^{\prime}_{0})\right|.

Note that for a nonperiodic
x\in X_{\varphi}
, any point
x^{\prime}
satisfying (
5
) is also nonperiodic.

One of the most fundamental properties of purely substitutive systems is the so-called
recognizability
which deals with situations when the desubstitution representation (
5
) of all nonperiodic points
x\in X_{\varphi}
is unique; the assumption of nonperiodicity is easily seen to be necessary
[
10
, Rem. 2.2(4)]
.
The notion of recognizability has a long history; we refer to
[
10
]
for a thorough introduction.

The problem of recognisability of purely substitutive systems has been settled in full generality by Berthé et al. in
[
10
, Thm. 5.3]
: For any substitution
\varphi
all nonperiodic points
x\in X_{\varphi}
are
recognisable
, that is, for any nonperiodic
x\in X_{\varphi}
the representation (
5
) exists and is unique. This result builds on earlier works
[
11
,
21
,
36
]
, see also
[
7
,
9
]
for a new simplified proof.
This allows us to make the following definitions.

Definition 1.12
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}^{*}
be a substitution. Let
X^{\prime}_{\varphi}\subset X_{\varphi}
denote the set of nonperiodic points in
X_{\varphi}
.
Define the ‘desubstitution’ maps

R_{\varphi}\colon X^{\prime}_{\varphi}\to X^{\prime}_{\varphi},\ R_{\varphi}(x)=x^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad c_{\varphi}\colon X^{\prime}_{\varphi}\to{\mathbf{N}},\ c_{\varphi}(x)=c,

to be unique maps such that
x=T^{c}(\varphi(x^{\prime}))
and
0\leqslant c<|\varphi(x^{\prime}_{0})|
. For a nonperiodic

, let
\bm{R_{\varphi}}(x)=(R^{i}_{\varphi}(x))_{i\geqslant 0}
5
5

5

By convention,

.
and
\bm{c_{\varphi}}(x)=(c_{\varphi}(R^{i}_{\varphi}(x)))_{i\geqslant 0}
denote the sequences over

and
{\mathbf{N}}
, respectively, obtained by iterating the map
R_{\varphi}
and then applying

in the case of the sequence
\bm{c_{\varphi}}(x)
.

The map
R_{\varphi}
can be thought of as the ’inverse’ of

R_{\varphi}(\varphi(x))=x\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(R_{\varphi}(x))=T^{-c_{\varphi}(x)}(x).

.

Remark 1.13
.

If
\varphi
is of constant length

, then the sequence
\bm{c_{\varphi}}(x)=(c_{i})_{i}
lies in

In general, recognizability fails for one-sided substitutive systems, even the primitive ones (see

Remark 1.14
.

It is not difficult to see that for any substitution
\varphi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}^{*}
and sequence

Indeed, suppose that there are more than

.
By pigeonhole principle, we can find two different nonperiodic sequences

x^{i}_{-1}x^{i}_{0}x^{i}_{1}=y^{i}_{-1}y^{i}_{0}y^{i}_{1},

Below we show that for any factor map from a
k
-automatic system onto some subshift, its fibres on nonperiodic points are uniformly bounded in size. This is well-known in the case of minimal substitutive systems
[

, Thm. 20]
; we are not aware of a reference in the general case. We will deduce it from the so-called Critical Factorisation Lemma, a classical result in combinatorics of words
[

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an alphabet and let

be a finite set. For a two-sided sequence

; in particular,

can be written as a concatenation of words in

. The set of cuts of a

-factorisation

of

is the set of all starting positions of factors

in

, i.e. it is the set
\pi_{2}(F({\mathbf{Z}}))\subset{\mathbf{Z}}
, where
\pi_{2}
denotes the projection on the second coordinate. Two

-factorisations of a sequence are

if their sets of cuts are disjoint.

Critical Factorisation Theorem (or one of its corollaries) states that a nonperiodic sequence
x\in\mathscr{A}^{{\mathbf{Z}}}
has at most

pairwise disjoint

-factorisations

30
, Cor. 1]

.

Let
\varphi
be a substitution of constant length and let

Using Curtis–Hedlund–Lyndon Theorem (Theorem

(

\pi\colon X_{\varphi}\to Y

.
We write

in this case.

Let

be nonperiodic.
By Remark

is finite; we claim that its size is at most

.

Suppose that the claim does not hold and let

be the set of size

are pairwise distinct and
\tau(x)=y
for all

Let

Note that this implies that all integers

(7)

Put

.
Since the morphism

is of constant length and

has

1.8.
k-adic integers and
k
-automatic systems

Let
k\geqslant 2
be an integer.
Let
\mathbf{Z}_{k}
denote the ring of
k
-adic integers, that is, the inverse limit
\mathbf{Z}_{k}=\varprojlim\mathbf{Z}/k^{n}\mathbf{Z}
of the inverse system of rings
({\mathbf{Z}}/k^{i}{\mathbf{Z}})
,
i\geqslant 1
(where the morphisms are given by the natural quotient maps, and we consider each ring
({\mathbf{Z}}/k^{i}{\mathbf{Z}})
with the discrete topology).
The ring
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
is a compact topological ring; we may identify
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
with the following closed subgroup of the direct product of
{\mathbf{Z}}/k^{i}{\mathbf{Z}}
:

{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}=\{(s_{i})_{i}\in\prod{\mathbf{Z}}/k^{i}{\mathbf{Z}}\mid s_{i}\equiv s_{i+1}\bmod k^{i}\}.

As a topological ring
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
admits a natural isomorphism

(8)

{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}=\prod{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}\quad(z_{i})_{i}\to\prod(z_{i}\bmod p^{i})_{i}

where
p
runs over the set of primes dividing
k
. The map
f\colon{\mathbf{Z}}\to{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
that sends an integer
z
to the sequence
(z\bmod k^{i})_{i}
in
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
is an injective ring homomorphism, and we identify
{\mathbf{Z}}
with the subring
f({\mathbf{Z}})
of
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
.

Each
k
-adic integer
z
has a unique
k
-adic expansion
z=.c_{0}c_{1}\ldots
with
c_{i}\in[0,k-1]
, i.e. it can be written uniquely in the form

(9)

z=c_{0}+c_{1}k+c_{2}k^{2}+\dots.

Furthermore, an integer
z
is invertible in
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
if and only if

\varphi^{\prime}

Now assume that

by a quasi-fixed point of

3.
Automatic sequences in automatic systems

In this section we provide a complete characterisation of the set of automatic sequences in an automatic system in terms of the quasi-fixed points of the underlying constant-length substitution.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k\geqslant 2
. Let
\varphi
be a substitution of length
k
. Let
\pi\colon X_{\varphi}\to Y
be a factor map onto some subshift
Y
. The following hold.

(
i
)

A sequence
y\in Y
is
k
-automatic if and only if
y=\pi(x)
for some quasi-fixed point
x
of
\varphi
.

(
ii
)

If
y\in Y
is
k
-automatic and nonperiodic, then all points in
\pi^{-1}(y)
are quasi-fixed points of
\varphi
.

The assumption that
z\in Y
is nonperiodic in the last claim is clearly necessary: it can happen that
\tau
maps an (uncountable) subsystem of
X_{\varphi}
onto a periodic subsystem of
Y
; for concreteness consider e.g. Example
3.2
below.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}=\{0,1,2,3\}
and let
\varphi\colon\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{*}
be a substitution given by

\varphi(0)=0123,\quad\varphi(1)=1031,\quad\varphi(2)=2332,\quad\varphi(3)=3223.

Let
\tau\colon\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\{0,1,2\}
be a coding identifying the letters
2,3\in\mathscr{A}
, i.e.

\tau(0)=0,\quad\tau(1)=1,\quad\tau(2)=\tau(3)=2.

The constant sequence
z
of 2’s belongs to
Y=\tau(X_{\varphi})
and
\tau^{-1}(z)\subset X_{\varphi}
is the Thue–Morse system generated by
\varphi|_{\{2,3\}}
. Since the Thue–Morse system is uncountable and there are only countably many substitutive sequences, it follows that the set
\tau^{-1}(z)
contains some sequences that are not substitutive.

In the case of an infinite minimal
k
-automatic system its set of automatic sequences can be characterised in terms of the arithmetic properties of the ring
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
, its equicontinuos factor (see Section
1.8
for information about
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
and the definition of the factor map to
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
).

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
k\geqslant 2
.
Let
\varphi
be a primitive substitution of length
k
, let
\mathrm{P}\subset X_{\varphi}
be the set of
\varphi
-periodic points, and let
\tau
be a coding.
Assume
X=\tau(X_{\varphi})
is aperiodic and let
\kappa\colon X\to{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
be the factor map sending
\tau(\mathrm{P})
to
0\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
.
Then,
x\in X
is automatic if and only if
\kappa(x)\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
is rational.
For
x=\tau(x^{\prime})
with
T^{m}\varphi^{c}(x^{\prime})=x^{\prime}
,
m\in{\mathbf{Z}}
,
c\geqslant 1
we have
\kappa(x)=m(1-k^{c})^{-1}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.18
,
\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa\circ\tau
, where
\kappa^{\prime}\colon X_{\varphi}\to{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
is the factor map sending the set
\mathrm{P}
of
\varphi
-periodic points to
0
.
The last claim follows thus from the equality

(16)

\pi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\pi^{\prime}(T^{m}\varphi^{c}(x^{\prime}))=m+k^{c}\pi^{\prime}(x^{\prime}).

By Theorem
3.1
, all automatic sequences in
X
are of the form
x=\tau(x^{\prime})
with
x^{\prime}\in X_{\varphi}
the quasi-fixed point of
\varphi
.
Thus, the first claim holds, by (
16
) and the fact the each rational number

in
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}
can be written in the form

for some
m\in{\mathbf{Z}}
and
c\geqslant 1
(see Remark
1.16
).
∎

holds for general substitutive systems (not necessarily of constant length), i.e. that the following statement holds.

be a substitution. Let

(

Remark 3.5
.

By the result of Holton and Zamboni

, Conjecture

is known in the case when

for primitive

in the same way they are used in the proof of Theorem

.

Remark 3.6
.

Let
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
be any primitive substitutions of the same length
k\geqslant 2
with aperiodic systems
X_{\varphi}
and
X_{\varphi^{\prime}}
.
In
[
19
]
, the authors give an algorithm to describe the set of factor maps

(or the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(X_{\varphi})
in the case

) via the detailed study of the "fingerprint" left by the map

.
A crucial step
[

— translated to our context via Corollary

to which a

-periodic point can be send by a factor map. While in the general case of nonminimal or nonconstant length substitutive systems we no longer have the underlying structure of

The rest of the section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Let
\varphi\colon\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{*}
be a substitution of length
k
. We will make use of the sequences
\Psi_{\bm{i}}
and families
\mathrm{F}_{\varphi,m}
defined in Definition
2.5
. Here, it will be advantageous to assume that our substitution has the following idempotency property.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{*}
be a substitution of constant length. We say that
\varphi
is
column-constant
if the sets
\mathrm{F}_{\varphi,m}
are the same for all
m\geqslant 1
.

The name column-constant stems from the fact that, visually, a substitution
\varphi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}^{*}
is column-constant if and only if the sets of columns that appear in the
|\mathscr{A}|\times k^{n}
arrays formed by the words
\varphi^{n}(a)
,
a\in\mathscr{A}
are the same for all
n\geqslant 1
.
The following lemma shows that we can always assume that a substitution
\varphi
is column-constant by passing to some power of
\varphi
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{*}
be a substitution of length
k
.

(
i
)

For any
x,y\in\mathscr{A}^{{\mathbf{Z}}}
and
m\geqslant 1
,
\varphi^{m}(x)=\varphi^{m}(y)
if and only if
\Theta(x)=\Theta(y)
for all
\Theta\in\mathrm{F}_{\varphi,m}
.

(
ii
)

There exists
n\geqslant 1
such that the substitution
\varphi^{n}
is column-constant.

Proof.

The first claim follows easily from the fact that
\mathrm{F}_{\varphi,m}=\mathrm{F}_{\varphi^{m},1}
for any
m\geqslant 1
.
To see the second claim, let
\mathscr{P}
be the set of all nonempty subsets of
\mathrm{F}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Define the function
G\colon\mathscr{P}\to\mathscr{P}
that sends a set
P\in\mathscr{P}
to the set

G(P)=\{\Psi_{i}\circ f\mid i\in[0,k-1],\ f\in P\}.

Note that
G^{m}(\mathrm{F}_{\varphi,1})=\mathrm{F}_{\varphi,m}
for all
m\geqslant 1
. Since
G
is defined on a finite set, there exists
n\geqslant 1
such that the function
G^{n}
is idempotent (e.g. by
[
13
, Lem. 1.7]
). In particular,
G^{n}(\mathrm{F}_{\varphi,1})=G^{mn}(\mathrm{F}_{\varphi,1})
for all

, and hence
\varphi^{n}
is column-constant.
∎

Let
\varphi
be a column-constant substitution of constant length and let
\tau
be a coding. By
3.8
(
i
),
\tau(\varphi(x))\neq\tau(\varphi(y))
implies that
\tau(\varphi^{m}(x))\neq\tau(\varphi^{m}(y))
for all
m\geqslant 1
.
We cannot, in general, hope to show that
\tau(x)\neq\tau(y)
implies
\tau(\varphi^{nm}(x))\neq\tau(\varphi^{nm}(y))
for all
m\geqslant 0
for some
n\geqslant 1
. Indeed, this may fail even when the coding
\tau
is trivial: consider any substitution
\varphi
, for which there exist two distinct letters
a
,
b
such that
\varphi(a)=\varphi(b)
, i.e. a substitution
\varphi
, which is not
injective
. If
X_{\varphi}
is aperiodic then a standard procedure (called

) allows one to replace an automatic system
X_{\varphi}
by a conjugate automatic system

given by an injective substitution
\widetilde{\varphi}

[
12
, Prop. 2.3]
. Furthermore, if
X_{\varphi}
is minimal then one can find (in a process called

) a substitution
\varphi^{\prime}
of constant length

such that
\tau(X_{\varphi})
and

are conjugate (see Theorem
1.18
).
However injectivization does not work well when the system is not aperiodic: If
X_{\varphi}
is not aperiodic, then the injectivized systems

need not be conjugate to
X_{\varphi}
.
If
X_{\varphi}
is not minimal, then the minimization does not work in general either (see Example
1.19
).
Some of the additional technicalities in the proofs below are due to the fact that we work with general (not purely) automatic systems without excluding periodic points.

Let
\varphi
be a substitution of constant length

and let
\tau
be a coding (or, a factor map).
The fact that for every quasi-fixed point

of
\varphi

is

-automatic follows easily from Proposition

(

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\varphi
be an ambi-idempotent substitution and let
\tau
be a coding. Let

be nonperiodic and let

be any point such that

. Then all sequences
\tau(x^{i})
are nonperiodic, where
\bm{R_{\varphi}}(x)=(x^{i})_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
X^{i}
,

be the orbit closure of

; note that, by compactness,

is the orbit closure of
\tau(x^{i})
for all

. Suppose that
\tau(x^{i})
(and, thus,
\tau(X^{i})
) is periodic for some

.
Since

is equal to
\varphi^{i}(x^{i})
modulo the shift and, by Proposition

(

, we have

.
Hence,

is periodic and, thus,

is periodic as an element of

, which is a contradiction.
∎

It is necessary to impose some assumptions (such as ambi-idempotency) on a substitution

in Lemma

; consider e.g. the following example.

Example 3.10
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{*}
be the substitution given by

\varphi(0)=0130,\quad\varphi(1)=3443,\quad\varphi(2)=4334,\quad\varphi(3)=1221,\quad\varphi(4)=2112.

Let
\mathscr{B}=\{0,1,2,3\}
and let
\tau\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{B}
be the coding identifying letters

and

, i.e.

\tau(0)=0,\quad\tau(1)=1,\quad\tau(2)=2,\quad\tau(3)=\tau(4)=3.

Let

and

be the Thue–Morse sequences over the alphabets

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
k\geqslant 2
. Let
\varphi
be a substitution of length
k
and let
\tau
be a coding. Assume
\varphi
is ambi-idempotent and column-constant. Let

be nonperiodic and let

be any point such that
\tau(x)=y
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(

)

is
k
-automatic,

(

)

is a quasi-fixed point of
\varphi
.

Proof.

If
x
is a quasi-fixed point of
\varphi
, it is

-automatic by Proposition

(

).
Hence,

is
k
-automatic as a coding of a

-automatic sequence.
This shows that (

) implies (

).

To show the other implication assume that

is
k
-automatic and nonperiodic.
Consider any

such that
\tau(x)=y
.
Let

and
\bm{c_{\varphi}}(x)=(c_{i})_{i\geqslant 0}
be the desubstitution sequences of

(over

and

(17)

x^{0}=x\quad\text{and}\quad x^{i}=T^{c_{i}}(\varphi(x^{i+1}))\quad\text{for}\quad i\geqslant 0.

By Lemma

, all

,

By representation (

) and Lemma

\mathrm{F}_{\varphi,m}=\mathrm{F}\quad\text{for all}\quad m\geqslant 1;

in particular, for any

Hence for any

By Lemma

\left|\tau^{-1}(y^{\prime})\right|\leqslant K\quad\text{for any nonperiodic}\quad y^{\prime}\in Y.

(

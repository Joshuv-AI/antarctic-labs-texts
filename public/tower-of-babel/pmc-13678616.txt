iEvac-Z, an Inactivated Ebola Vaccine: Phase 1 Trial in Japan
Authors: Tokiko Watanabe, Michiko Koga, Masanori Nojima, Minako Kono, Yuriko Tomita, Tadashi Maemura, Itsuki Anzai, Yuri Furusawa, Calvin Duong, Yoichiro Kino, Peter J. Halfmann, Fumitaka Nagamura, Hiroshi Yotsuyanagi, Yoshihiro Kawaoka
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

Ebola virus disease continues to pose a major global health threat, with recurrent outbreaks underscoring the need for effective vaccination strategies 1. Current licensed Ebola vaccines, while effective, raise safety concerns due to their replication-competent nature and associated adverse events 2,3. We developed iEvac-Z, an inactivated whole-virus Ebola vaccine derived from replication-deficient Ebola virus lacking the essential VP30 gene (EBOVΔVP30) 4. This virus cannot replicate outside VP30-complementing cells and can be manufactured under biosafety level 2 conditions. Subsequent inactivation with β-propiolactone ensures loss of infectivity while preserving the full viral antigen repertoire, including glycoprotein (GP), nucleoprotein (NP), and VP40. In nonhuman primates, this vaccine provided protection against lethal challenge 5.

We conducted a first-in-human, open-label, dose-escalation Phase I trial evaluating the safety and immunogenicity of iEvac-Z in 29 healthy Japanese males aged 20–45 years (jRCTs031190118) for full details see protocol at nejm.org. Protocol was IRB approved and all participants provided written informed consent. Participants received 2 immunizations of either lower-dose (4.69×106 FFU-equivalent) or higher-dose (2.34×107 FFU-equivalent) iEvac-Z intramuscularly four weeks apart (Figure S1), with follow-up through 52 weeks.

Overall safety was similar between the groups (Table S1). All-cause adverse events occurred in 80.0% (95% CI 51.9–95.7%) of Cohort 1 and 85.7% (95% CI 57.2–98.2%) of Cohort 2 of participants. Vaccine-related (as assessed by the investigator) adverse events were reported in 66.7% (95% CI, 38.4–88.2%) and 71.4% (95% CI, 41.9–91.6%) of Cohorts 1 and 2 participants, respectively, predominantly mild injection-site reactions. One higher-dose participant with elevated baseline GP-specific antibodies (>2000 U/mL) experienced grade 3 injection-site redness and discontinued further vaccinations.

By Week 8, seroconversion for GP-specific IgG antibodies reached 100% in the full analysis set (FAS, n=29) and per-protocol set (PPS, n=26) (Figure 1A). Geometric mean titers (GMT) showed dose-dependent increases: 4.7-fold (470.0; 95% CI, 307.2‒719.1) for lower-dose and 11.6-fold (1562.5; 95% CI, 1080.1‒2260.5) for higher-dose (Figure 1A). Neutralizing antibody responses (≥2-fold increase) occurred in 21.4% (95% CI, 4.7‒50.8%) of lower-dose and 50.0% (95% CI, 23.0‒77.0%) of higher-dose recipients (Figure 1B). Per-protocol analysis yielded consistent results with the full analysis set. iEvac-Z also induced IgG responses against NP and VP40, with higher responses in higher-dose recipients (Figure S2). This multi-antigen response distinguishes iEvac-Z from single-antigen vaccines. Further, GP-specific antibodies persisted in 38.5% (95% CI, 13.9‒68.4%) of higher-dose recipients at 52 weeks (Figure 1A), suggesting potential for longer-term immunological memory.

Cytokine analysis following vaccination revealed generally modest responses (<2-fold), with RANTES showing the largest increase (2.84-fold; 95% CI: 2.09–3.86) and clustering with GP-specific antibodies (Figures S3–S4), indicating mild, balanced immune activation.

Study limitations include the male-only Japanese participants, small sample size, and absence of T-cell response assessment. These results establish proof-of-concept for the inactivated EBOVΔVP30 platform, showing multi-antigen immunogenicity in humans without evident safety concerns. The sustained antibody responses, manufacturing advantages under biosafety level 2 conditions, and multi-antigen profile support continued development. Future adjuvanted formulations will be explored to enhance neutralizing antibody responses while maintaining a favorable safety profile.

Minimal Spacing of Eigenvalues on Fractals
Bernard Akwei

Abstract
On the unit interval (I), and the Sierpinski Gasket ( 𝒮 ​ 𝒢 \mathcal{SG} ), the spectral decimation function of the Laplacian has similar properties that result in positive minimum spacing of eigenvalues. Other fractals, for example the level-3 Sierpinski Gasket, 𝒮 ​ 𝒢 3 \mathcal{SG}_{3} , may not necessarily enjoy these properties. Our goal is to obtain an easy and sufficient criterion for positive infimum spacing of eigenvalues in the spectrum based on the properties of the spectral decimation function for the appropriate fractal. We also give a sufficient condition for zero infimum spacing of eigenvalues.

Introduction

Given
\mathds{I}
is the identity map, the spectrum of an operator,
T
, is the set,
\{\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}T-\lambda\mathds{I}\text{ is not invertible}\}
. A complex number

\lambda
is called an eigenvalue of the operator
T
, if the operator
T-\lambda\mathds{I}
is not injective.
We examine the spacing of the eigenvalues in the spectrum of the
self-adjoint non-negative
Laplacian,
\Delta
, on fractals.
This is closely related to the idea of gaps in the spectrum, which has been studied by many authors including the work of Strichartz in
[
Str05
]
.

Because the spaces we will consider are compact, the Laplacian has discrete spectrum and we say gaps occur in the spectrum if,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n+1}}{\lambda_{n}}>1,

where
\{\lambda_{n}\}
are eigenvalues
[
HSTZ11
]
. The study of the spectrum of the Laplacian on Fractals dates back to the works of Rammal and Toulouse in
[
RT83
,
Ram84
]
.
Several advances have been made in describing these spectrums. For some fractals, it was discovered that certain properties about their spectrum were different from classical results. The difference were mainly due to the existence of large gaps in the spectrum.
The phenomenon of gaps have interesting consequences.
For example it results in convergence of Fourier series along some subsequence as shown by Strichartz in
[
Str05
]
. As a result, several papers including
[
HSTZ11
,
Dre05
]
have discussed large gaps in the spectrum of the Laplacian.

However, questions about small gaps can be challenging, and very few articles consider small gaps, as noted by
[
AR22
]
. Such topics may include questions about spacing of eigenvalues( i.e. the difference between subsequent eigenvalues in the spectrum).

In recent research involving machine learning, the spacing of eigenvalues has been used to obtain useful estimates about relevant data
[
AV25
]
. In
[
Wah24
]
, Wahl provides a non-asymptotic error estimate that results from approximating the eigenspaces of the Laplace-Beltrami operator depending on the spacing of its eigenvalues.
Also from Wielandt’s Inequalities, stated in Theorem
9
, if the infimum spacing of the eigenvalues of A, a symmetric operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is positive, then

0\leq\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(A+B)-\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(A)\leq\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert B\right\rVert}^{2}}{\inf_{j}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(A)-\lambda_{j+1}^{\downarrow}(A)\right\rvert}},1\leq j\leq d

and

0\leq\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(A)-\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(A+B)\leq\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert B\right\rVert}^{2}}{\inf_{j}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(A)-\lambda_{j+1}(A)\right\rvert}},d+1\leq j\leq n.

Thus, we obtain an estimate for the error in the eigenvalues after some perturbation. The extensive research relating to the spacing of eigenvalues motivates our examination of the infimum spacing.
Often times, it may be technical to obtain the actual value of this infimum.
In the case of
\mathcal{SG}
,
[
AR22
]
has shown that this coincides with the spectral gap (i.e. the spacing between the first two eigenvalues.) by examining and considering the dynamics associated with the spectral decimation function (see Section

]

In this paper, we give a sufficient criterion that ensures that the infimum spacing of eigenvalues is positive as well as a sufficient condition for which the infimum spacing must be zero.

In Section

Section
3

In subsection

In each section, we also show some easy examples that demonstrates the conclusion of the Theorems. Examples ???? are interesting in that they show that certain features that may hold on

V_{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcup_{i}F_{i}V_{m-1},i=0,1.

3.1
Zero Infimum Spacing

The next theorem gives a condition under which the infimum spacing is zero. Here our main result is Theorem
2
, where we show that the existence of a nonzero repulsive fixed point of the spectral decimation function with multiplier, larger than the zero fixed point indicates zero infimum spacing in the spectrum.

Theorem 2
.

Assume
\Delta
admits spectral decimation with spectral decimation function
R
and suppose
0,\zeta>0
are fixed point of
R
with
|R^{\prime}(\zeta)|>\mathinner{\!\left\lvert R^{\prime}(0)\right\rvert}>1
.
Then
\inf\{|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime},\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\sigma(\Delta)\}=0
.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{\zeta}
be the inverse branch of
R
with
\zeta
in it’s range.
Note that
\zeta
is in the Julia set of
R
and is an attracting fixed point of
\phi_{\zeta}
and hence we may choose
n
, and
x_{1},x_{2}\in\sigma(\Delta_{n})
so that
\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{j})\to\zeta,j=1,2

[
HSTZ11
]
. Therefore,
we have,

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{1})-\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{2})\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert(\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(\gamma_{m}))^{\prime}\right\rvert}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{\zeta}^{\prime}(\phi_{\zeta}^{m-1}(\gamma_{m}))\right\rvert}\dots\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{\zeta}^{\prime}(\gamma_{m})\right\rvert}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\right\rvert}

Note that
\phi_{\zeta}
is a continuous injective map and hence monotone.
Therefore, it is no loss to assume,

\phi_{\zeta}^{k}(x_{1})\leq\phi_{\zeta}^{k}(\gamma_{m})\leq\phi_{\zeta}^{k}(x_{2}),\forall k
.

Thus,
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{\zeta}^{k}(\gamma_{m})-\zeta\right\rvert}\leq\max_{j\in\{1,2\}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{\zeta}^{k}(x_{j})-\zeta\right\rvert}

Hence, by continuity of
R^{\prime}
, given
\delta>0
there is
N
so that
m\geq k\geq N\implies\mathinner{\!\left\lvert R^{\prime}(\zeta)\right\rvert}-\delta\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert R^{\prime}(\phi_{\zeta}^{k}(\gamma_{m})\right\rvert}
.
Thus,
\frac{1}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert R^{\prime}(\phi_{\zeta}^{k}(\gamma_{m}))\right\rvert}}\leq\frac{1}{(R^{\prime}(\zeta)-\delta)},\forall k\geq N
.
So for
m>N
, we have,

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{1})-\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{2})\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert R^{\prime}(\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(\gamma_{m}))\right\rvert}}\dots\frac{1}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert R^{\prime}(\phi_{\zeta}(\gamma_{m}))\right\rvert}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(R^{\prime}(\zeta)-\delta)^{m-N}}\frac{1}{R^{\prime}(\phi_{\zeta}^{N}(\gamma_{m}))}\dots\frac{1}{R^{\prime}(\phi_{\zeta}(\gamma_{m}))}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\right\rvert}.

Now note that

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert c_{\Delta}^{n+j+m}\phi_{0}^{j}\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{1})-c_{\Delta}^{n+j+m}\phi_{0}^{j}\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{2})\right\rvert}=c_{\Delta}^{n}\cdot c_{\Delta}^{j}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert(\phi_{0}^{j})^{\prime}(\gamma_{j})\right\rvert}\cdot c_{\Delta}^{m}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{1})-\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{2})\right\rvert}.

for some
\gamma_{j}
lying between
\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{1})
and
\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{2})
. Choose
\delta
so that
R^{\prime}(0)<\mathinner{\!\left\lvert R^{\prime}(\zeta)\right\rvert}-\delta
and observe that
c_{\Delta}^{m}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{1})-\phi_{\zeta}^{m}(x_{2})\right\rvert}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.

Hence by
[
Shi96
, Proposition 3.1]
, it remains to show that
c_{\Delta}^{j}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert(\phi_{0}^{j})^{\prime}(\gamma_{j})\right\rvert}
converges to a finite number as
j\to\infty
.

However,

\displaystyle c_{\Delta}^{j}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert(\phi_{0}^{j})^{\prime}(\gamma_{j})\right\rvert}=

\displaystyle c_{\Delta}^{j}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{0}^{\prime}(\phi_{0}^{j-1}(\gamma_{j}))\dots\phi_{0}^{\prime}(\gamma_{j})\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R^{\prime}(0)}{R^{\prime}(\phi_{0}^{j}(\gamma_{j}))}\dots\frac{R^{\prime}(0)}{R^{\prime}(\phi_{0}(\gamma_{j}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R^{\prime}(0)}{R^{\prime}(\phi_{0}(\gamma_{j}))}\dots\frac{R^{\prime}(0)}{R^{\prime}(\phi_{0}^{N-1}(\gamma_{j}))}\prod_{k=N}^{j}\frac{R^{\prime}(0)}{R^{\prime}(\phi_{0}^{k}(\gamma_{j}))}.

In addition,

\displaystyle\prod_{k=N}^{j}\frac{R^{\prime}(0)}{R^{\prime}(\phi_{0}^{k}(\gamma_{j}))}=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=N}^{j}\frac{R^{\prime}(0)}{R^{\prime}(0)+R^{\prime\prime}(t_{k})\phi_{0}^{k}(\gamma_{j})},\ \ \text{$t_{k}\in(0,\phi_{0}^{k}(\gamma_{j}))$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=N}^{j}\left(1-\frac{R^{\prime\prime}(t_{k})\phi_{0}(\gamma_{j})}{R^{\prime}(0)+R^{\prime\prime}(t_{k})\phi_{0}^{k}(\gamma_{j})}\right).

Which converges if and only if

\sum_{k=N}^{j}\frac{R^{\prime\prime}(t_{k})\phi_{0}(\gamma_{j})}{R^{\prime}(0)+R^{\prime\prime}(t_{k})\phi_{0}^{k}(\gamma_{j})}

converges.
At the same time, for large
N
,

\sum_{k=N}^{j}\frac{R^{\prime\prime}(t_{k})\phi_{0}(\gamma_{j})}{R^{\prime}(0)+R^{\prime\prime}(t_{k})\phi_{0}^{k}(\gamma_{j})}\leq C\sum_{k=N}^{j}\phi_{0}^{k}(\gamma_{j})\leq C\sum_{k=N}^{j}s^{k-N}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{0}^{N}\gamma_{j}\right\rvert}

for some
C\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\in(0,1)
. The first inequality is because
\frac{R^{\prime\prime}(t_{k})}{R^{\prime}(0)+R^{\prime\prime}(t_{k})\phi_{0}^{k}(\gamma_{j})}
converges as
k\to\infty
and the second inequality is because
\phi_{0}
is a contraction near
0
. Since
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{0}^{N}\gamma_{j}\right\rvert}
is bounded and
\sum_{k=N}^{j}s^{k-N}
is a convergent geometric series, the conclusion of the theorem follows.

∎

3.2
Positive Infimum Spacing

The proof of the next result relies on mean value theorem and induction and is crucial in proving the main Theorem
5
which guarantees that the infimum spacing in the spectrum is positive under certain conditions.

Lemma 4
.

Assume
\Delta
admits spectral decimation with spectral decimation function
R
and suppose
0
is a fixed point of
R
with
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert R^{\prime}(0)\right\rvert}>1
.

Let
D_{0}=\{0,x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a finite set with
0<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{r}
.
Suppose, the interval
[0,x_{r}]
is backward invariant under
R
and the inverse branches,
\phi_{j}^{\prime}s
of
R
are defined on all of this interval.

Assume further that

•

R^{-1}(0,x_{r})\cap D_{0}=\emptyset,\ \ R^{-1}\{0,x_{r}\}\subseteq D_{0}

•

R^{\prime}(0)=\max_{x\in R^{-1}[0,x_{r}]}|R^{\prime}(x)|

and set
D_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\cup_{m\geq 0}^{n}R^{-m}(D_{0})
.
Then there is a constant
C_{0}
, depending on
D_{0}
such that,

min_{x,y\in D_{n},x\neq y}|x-y|\geq\frac{C_{0}}{(R^{\prime}(0))^{n}}.

Proof.

Let
C_{0}=min_{k}|x_{k}-x_{k-1}|,x_{j}\in D_{0}
.
We know
D_{1}=D_{0}\cup R^{-1}(D_{0})
.
If
x,y\in D_{0},x\neq y
, the we obtain

|x-y|\geq C_{0}\geq\frac{C_{0}}{R^{\prime}(0)}.

If
x\in D_{0}
and
y\in R^{-1}(D_{0})
,
then
y\in R^{-1}[0,x_{r}]
. Thus
y\in\phi_{k}[0,x_{r}]
, for some k.
But
R^{-1}(0,x_{r})\cap D_{0}\neq\emptyset,R^{-1}\{0,x_{r}\}\subseteq D_{0}
implies
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}\geq\min_{z\in\{0,x_{r}\}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert y-\phi_{k}(z)\right\rvert}
.
For
z\in\{0,x_{r}\}
, we have,

\left|\frac{R(y)-R(\phi_{k}(z))}{y-\phi_{k}(z)}\right|=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert R^{\prime}(c)\right\rvert}\leq R^{\prime}(0)
, for some
c\in\phi_{k}[0,x_{r}]
.
Thus,
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert y-\phi_{k}(z)\right\rvert}\geq\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert R(y)-R(\phi_{k}(z))\right\rvert}}{R^{\prime}(0)}\geq\frac{C_{0}}{R^{\prime}(0)}

For
x,y\in R^{-1}(D_{0}),x\neq y
:
We can assume
x,y\in\phi_{k}[0,x_{r}]
, for some
k
, since
\phi_{j}\{0,x_{r}\}\subseteq D_{0}
for all
j
.

R
bijection on this interval implies
R(x)\neq R(y)
.
We obtain

\displaystyle\frac{|R(x)-R(y)|}{|x-y|}

\displaystyle=R^{\prime}(c)\leq R^{\prime}(0)

for some
c\in\phi_{k}[0,x_{r}]

Thus,

\displaystyle|x-y|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|R(x)-R(y)|}{R^{\prime}(0)}\geq\frac{C_{0}}{R^{\prime}(0)}

\displaystyle\text{since $R(x),R(y)\in D_{0}$}.

The general case follows by induction and this completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5
.

Suppose there is
D_{0}
, so that
\sigma(\Delta_{n})\subseteq D_{n}
for all
n
and
R,D_{n}
satisfy the conditions of Lemma
4
.
Then,

\inf\{|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}\text{ are Dirichlet eigenvalues of }\Delta\}>0

\inf\{|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}\text{ are Neumann eigenvalues of }\Delta\}>0.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma

that, the spacing in

, where

Let A be a symmetric operator on

\lambda_{d}^{\downarrow}(A)-\lambda_{d+1}^{\downarrow}(A)>0

Curvature effects on the regimes of the lateral van der Waals force
Alexandre P. Costa, Lucas Queiroz, Danilo T. Alves

Abstract
Recently, it has been shown that, under the action of the lateral van der Waals (vdW) force due to a perfectly conducting corrugated plane, a neutral anisotropic polarizable particle in vacuum can be attracted not only to the nearest corrugation peak but also to a valley or an intermediate point between a peak and a valley, with such behaviors called the peak, valley, and intermediate regimes, respectively. In the present paper, we calculate the vdW interaction between a polarizable particle and a grounded conducting corrugated cylinder, and investigate how the effects of the curvature of the cylinder affect the occurrence of the mentioned regimes.



I
Introduction

A recent investigation of the van der Waals (vdW) interaction between a neutral polarizable particle and a corrugated plane, using analytical calculations beyond the proximity force approximation (PFA), led to the prediction that, under the action of the lateral van der Waals (vdW) force, an anisotropic particle can be attracted not only toward the nearest corrugation peak, but also to a valley, or to an intermediate point between a peak and valley, with such behaviors called as peak, valley and intermediate regimes, respectively
Nogueira et al. 2021
.
In Ref.
Queiroz et al. 2021
, it was discussed how these regimes are affected by the consideration of nondispersive dielectric media, obtaining a first estimate about their behavior in the presence of dielectrics.
In Ref.
Queiroz 2024
, it was shown how the occurrence of the peak, valley and intermediate regimes is affected by the consideration of realistic dielectric properties for the surface and also of the retardation in the interaction.

In the present paper, we discuss the influence of another factor on the occurrence of the regimes of the lateral force, namely, the influence of curvature on the corrugated surface.
In other words, we investigate how modifications on the geometric properties of the surface itself on which the corrugation occurs affect the mentioned regimes.
For this, we start considering the corrugations occurring on the surface of a grounded conducting cylinder and calculate the vdW interaction between a polarizable particle and such grounded conducting corrugated cylinder.
To compute this interaction, we start developing a generalization of the perturbative calculation discussed in Ref.
Clinton et al. 1985
to take into account the cylindrical geometry, and calculate the electrostatic potential of a charge in the presence of a grounded conducting corrugated cylinder.
In the sequence, we calculate the vdW interaction between a polarizable particle and a grounded conducting corrugated cylinder, and investigate the effects of the curvature of the cylinder on the regimes of the lateral vdW force discussed in Ref.
Nogueira et al. 2021
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we present the approach used to compute the vdW interaction between a neutral particle and a corrugated cylinder, and we discuss some applications.
In Sec.
III
, we compute the vdW interaction for the mentioned situation, and we apply our results to the case of a sinusoidal corrugation.
In Sec.
IV
, we present our final comments.

The solution of the Poisson’s equation

(2)

\displaystyle\left.G\left(\textbf{r},\textbf{r}^{\prime}\right)\right|_{\rho=a+\varepsilon h\left(\phi,z\right)}

(3)

\displaystyle\left.G\left(\textbf{r},\textbf{r}^{\prime}\right)\right|_{|\textbf{r}|\gg|\textbf{r}^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle 0,

, and, for

, the presence of curvature on the corrugated surface becomes relevant, producing an amplification in the lateral force, different from the case in which the corrugation occurs in the
z
-direction.

III
The van der Waals interaction between a polarizable particle and a corrugated cylinder

III.1
Interaction energy for a general corrugated cylinder

Let us consider a neutral polarizable particle, put at
\textbf{r}_{0}=\rho_{0}\boldsymbol{\hat{\rho}}+\phi_{0}\boldsymbol{\hat{\phi}}+z_{0}\mathbf{\hat{z}}
, in the presence of a grounded conducting corrugated cylinder with radius
a
, as illustrated in Fig.
1(b)
.
The vdW interaction
U_{\text{vdW}}
between this particle and the corrugated cylinder can be computed by combining the Eberlein-Zietal formula from Ref.
Eberlein and Zietal 2007
with the solution for the Green’s function for the problem of a charge in the presence of a grounded conducting corrugated cylinder [Eqs. (
5
), with
G^{(0)}
and
G^{(n)}
given by Eqs. (
21
) and (
II.1
)].
The mentioned Eberlein-Zietal formula is given by

U_{\text{vdW}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})=\frac{1}{8\pi\epsilon_{0}}\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{i}\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{j}^{\prime}\left.G_{H}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\right|_{\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r^{\prime}}=\mathbf{r}_{0}},

(38)

where
\hat{d}_{i}
are the components of the dipole moment operator and
\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle
is the expectation value of
\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}
.
For simplicity, let us focus on an approximate solution for this case which takes into account only the correction of
n=1
in Eq. (
II.1
).
Thus, by computing
G_{H}
using

G_{H}\left(\textbf{r},\textbf{r}^{\prime}\right)=G\left(\textbf{r},\textbf{r}^{\prime}\right)-\frac{1}{|\textbf{r}-\textbf{r}^{\prime}|}.

(39)

and substituting it into Eq. (
38
), one obtains that the vdW interaction can be written as
U_{\text{vdW}}\approx U^{(0)}_{\text{vdW}}+U^{(1)}_{\text{vdW}}
.
The first term
U^{(0)}_{\text{vdW}}
, which can be written as

U_{\text{vdW}}^{(0)}\left(\mathbf{r}_{0}\right)=\frac{1}{8\pi\varepsilon_{0}}\left[\Xi_{\rho}\left\langle\widehat{d}_{\rho}^{2}\right\rangle+\Xi_{\phi}\left\langle\widehat{d}_{\phi}^{2}\right\rangle+\Xi_{z}\left\langle\widehat{d}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle\right],

(40)

where

\displaystyle\Xi_{\rho}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{j}\int dk\frac{I_{j}\left(\left|k\right|a\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k\right|a\right)}\left[\partial_{\rho_{0}}K_{j}\left(\left|k\right|\rho_{0}\right)\right]^{2},

(41)

\displaystyle\Xi_{\phi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi\rho^{2}_{0}}\sum_{j}\int dkj^{2}\frac{I_{j}\left(\left|k\right|a\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k\right|a\right)}\left[K_{j}\left(\left|k\right|\rho_{0}\right)\right]^{2},

(42)

\displaystyle\Xi_{z}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{j}\int dkk^{2}\frac{I_{j}\left(\left|k\right|a\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k\right|a\right)}\left[K_{j}\left(\left|k\right|\rho_{0}\right)\right]^{2},

(43)

is the vdW interaction energy between the particle and the cylinder without corrugations, which was first obtained in Ref.
Eberlein and Zietal 2007
.
The second term,
U^{(1)}_{\text{vdW}}
, is the first-order correction to
U^{(0)}_{\text{vdW}}
due to the corrugation in the cylinder surface.
Besides this, it is obtained by substituting
G^{(1)}
, given by Eq. (
II.1
) with
n=1
, in the Eberlein-Zietal formula [Eq. (
38
)], obtaining

\displaystyle U_{\text{vdW}}^{\left(1\right)}(\mathbf{r}_{0})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi\epsilon_{0}}[\langle\hat{d}_{\rho}^{2}\rangle\mathcal{I}_{\rho\rho}+\langle\hat{d}_{\phi}^{2}\rangle\mathcal{I}_{\phi\phi}+\langle\hat{d}_{z}^{2}\rangle\mathcal{I}_{zz}

\displaystyle+\langle\hat{d}_{\rho}\hat{d}_{\phi}\rangle\mathcal{I}_{\rho\phi}+\langle\hat{d}_{\rho}\hat{d}_{z}\rangle\mathcal{I}_{\rho z}+\langle\hat{d}_{\phi}\hat{d}_{z}\rangle\mathcal{I}_{\phi z}],

(44)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\rho\rho}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{a}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}\widetilde{h}_{1,j,j^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})e^{iz(k-k^{\prime})}e^{i\phi(j-j^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\partial_{\rho}K_{j}(|k|\rho)}{K_{j}(|k|a)}\frac{\partial_{\rho}K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|\rho)}{K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|a)},

(45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\phi\phi}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{a\rho^{2}}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}\widetilde{h}_{1,j,j^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})jj^{\prime}e^{iz(k-k^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\times e^{i\phi(j-j^{\prime})}\frac{K_{j}(|k|\rho)}{K_{j}(|k|a)}\frac{K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|\rho)}{K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|a)},

(46)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{zz}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{a}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}\widetilde{h}_{1,j,j^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})kk^{\prime}e^{iz(k-k^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\times e^{i\phi(j-j^{\prime})}\frac{K_{j}(|k|\rho)}{K_{j}(|k|a)}\frac{K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|\rho)}{K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|a)},

(47)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\rho\phi}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2i}{a\rho}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}\widetilde{h}_{1,j,j^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})e^{iz(k-k^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\times e^{i\phi(j-j^{\prime})}\tfrac{jK_{j}(|k|\rho)\partial_{\rho}K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|\rho)-j^{\prime}\partial_{\rho}K_{j}(|k|\rho)K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|\rho)}{K_{j}(|k|a)K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|a)},

(48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\rho z}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2i}{a}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}\widetilde{h}_{1,j,j^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})e^{iz(k-k^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\times e^{i\phi(j-j^{\prime})}\tfrac{kK_{j}(|k|\rho)\partial_{\rho}K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|\rho)-k^{\prime}\partial_{\rho}K_{j}(|k|\rho)K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|\rho)}{K_{j}(|k|a)K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|a)},

(49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\phi z}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{a\rho}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}\widetilde{h}_{1,j,j^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})\left(k^{\prime}j+kj^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\times e^{iz(k-k^{\prime})}e^{i\phi(j-j^{\prime})}\frac{K_{j}(|k|\rho)}{K_{j}(|k|a)}\frac{K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|\rho)}{K_{j^{\prime}}(|k^{\prime}|a)}.

(50)

with
\widetilde{h}_{1,j,j^{\prime}}
given by Eq. (
II.1
) with
m=1
.
Note that the lateral force that acts on the particle arises due to the dependence of
U^{(1)}_{\text{vdW}}
on
\phi
and
z
.
Since we are interested only in the behavior of this force, hereafter we focus our attention only on
U^{(1)}_{\text{vdW}}
.

III.2
Interaction energy for a cylinder with a sinusoidal corrugation along the
z
-direction

Let us consider a sinusoidal corrugation profile described by Eq. (
32
).
For this situation,
U^{(1)}_{\text{vdW}}
can be written as

U_{\text{vdW, cc-$z$}}^{\left(1\right)}\left(\mathbf{r}_{0}\right)=\frac{\delta}{8\pi\epsilon_{0}}A_{\text{cc-$z$}}\cos\left(k_{c}z_{0}-\Delta_{\text{cc-$z$}}\right),

(51)

where
A_{\text{cc-$z$}}=\sqrt{B_{\text{cc-$z$}}^{2}+C_{\text{cc-$z$}}^{2}}
, and
\Delta_{\text{cc-$z$}}
is a nontrivial phase function defined by

\Delta_{\text{cc-$z$}}=\arctan\left(\frac{B_{\text{cc-$z$}}}{C_{\text{cc-$z$}}}\right),

(52)

with

\displaystyle B_{\text{cc-$z$}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{d}_{\rho}\hat{d}_{z}\right\rangle{\cal R}_{\rho z}^{\text{cc-$z$}},

(53)

\displaystyle C_{\text{cc-$z$}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{d}_{\rho}^{2}\right\rangle\mathcal{R}_{\rho\rho}^{\text{cc-$z$}}+\left\langle\hat{d}_{\phi}^{2}\right\rangle\mathcal{R}_{\phi\phi}^{\text{cc-$z$}}+\left\langle\hat{d}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle\mathcal{R}_{zz}^{\text{cc-$z$}},

(54)

and the functions
{\cal R}_{ij}^{\text{cc-$z$}}
being defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rho\rho}^{\text{cc-$z$}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{\pi a}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{}^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\frac{\partial_{\rho_{0}}K_{j}\left(\left|k-\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|\rho_{0}\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k-\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|a\right)}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\partial_{\rho_{0}}K_{j}\left(\left|k+\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|\rho_{0}\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k+\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|a\right)},

(55)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\phi\phi}^{\text{cc-$z$}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{\pi a}\frac{1}{\rho^{2}_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}dkj^{2}\frac{K_{j}\left(\left|k-\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|\rho_{0}\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k-\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|a\right)}

\displaystyle\times\frac{K_{j}\left(\left|k+\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|\rho_{0}\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k+\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|a\right)},

(56)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{zz}^{\text{cc-$z$}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{\pi a}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{}^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\left[k^{2}-\left(\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\times\frac{K_{j}\left(\left|k-\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|\rho_{0}\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k-\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|a\right)}\frac{K_{j}\left(\left|k+\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|\rho_{0}\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k+\frac{k_{c}}{2}\right|a\right)},

(57)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rho z}^{\text{cc-$z$}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{\pi a}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{}^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}dkk\frac{K_{j}\left(\left|k\right|\rho_{0}\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k\right|a\right)}

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{\partial_{\rho_{0}}K_{j}\left(\left|k+k_{c}\right|\rho_{0}\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k+k_{c}\right|a\right)}-\frac{\partial_{\rho_{0}}K_{j}\left(\left|k-k_{c}\right|\rho_{0}\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k-k_{c}\right|a\right)}\right].

(58)

From Eq. (
51
), considering the behavior of
U^{(1)}
with respect to
z_{0}
, one can see that the stable equilibrium points of
U^{(1)}
can be over the corrugation peaks
(\Delta_{\text{cc-$z$}}=\pi)
, valleys
(\Delta_{\text{cc-$z$}}=0)
or over intermediate points between a peak and a valley
(\Delta_{\text{cc-$z$}}\neq 0,\pi)
.
Such possibilities were called in Ref.
Nogueira et al. 2021
as peak, valley and intermediate regimes, respectively.
In Refs.
Queiroz et al. 2021
;
Nogueira et al. 2022
, we showed how modifications on the electromagnetic properties of the surface affect the occurrence of these regimes.
Otherwise, with Eqs. (
51
)-(
58
), we can now study how modifications on the curvature of the corrugated surface affect the occurrence of these regimes.
For this, let us consider a class of cylindrically symmetric polarizable particles, whose tensor
\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle
diagonalized, in cylindrical coordinates, is represented by
\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle=\text{diag}\left(\langle\hat{d}_{n}^{2}\rangle,\langle\hat{d}_{n}^{2}\rangle,\langle\hat{d}_{p}^{2}\rangle\right)
, with
\langle\hat{d}_{p}^{2}\rangle>\langle\hat{d}_{n}^{2}\rangle
.
For a general orientation of this particle, the components of
\langle\hat{d}_{i}\hat{d}_{j}\rangle
in terms of the spherical angles
(\theta,\varphi)
, with respect to the coordinates system
(\rho,\phi,z)
, are given by:

\displaystyle\langle\hat{d}_{\rho}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{d}_{p}^{2}\rangle\left[\beta+\left(1-\beta\right)\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\varphi\right],

(59)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{d}_{\phi}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{d}_{p}^{2}\rangle\left[\beta+\left(1-\beta\right)\sin^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\varphi\right],

(60)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{d}_{z}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{d}_{p}^{2}\rangle\left[\beta+\left(1-\beta\right)\cos^{2}\theta\right],

(61)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{d}_{\rho}\hat{d}_{\phi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{d}_{p}^{2}\rangle\frac{\left(1-\beta\right)}{2}\sin 2\varphi\sin^{2}\theta,

(62)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{d}_{\rho}\hat{d}_{z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{d}_{p}^{2}\rangle\frac{\left(1-\beta\right)}{2}\sin 2\theta\cos\varphi,

(63)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{d}_{\phi}\hat{d}_{z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{d}_{p}^{2}\rangle\frac{\left(1-\beta\right)}{2}\sin 2\theta\sin\varphi,

(64)

with
\beta=\langle\hat{d}_{n}^{2}\rangle/\langle\hat{d}_{p}^{2}\rangle
.

Let us start analyzing the curvature effects on the occurrence of the peak and valley regimes.
As mentioned above, we have peak and valley regimes when
\Delta_{\text{cc-$z$}}=\pi
, and
\Delta_{\text{cc-$z$}}=0
, respectively.
In this way, from Eqs. (
52
)-(
54
), one sees that such regimes can only occur when
B_{\text{cc-$z$}}=0
, which occurs when
\langle\hat{d}_{\rho}\hat{d}_{z}\rangle=0
(the particle is oriented such that its axis coincides with
\rho
,
\phi
, or
z
).
In addition, we have one or the other of these regimes depending on the sign of
C_{\text{cc-$z$}}
, where we have the valley regime when it is positive, and the peak regime when it is negative [see Eq. (
52
)].
By considering the particle oriented such that its axis is parallel to the
z
-axis
(\theta=0)
, in Fig.

we show the behaviors of
C_{\text{cc-$z$}}
and the corresponding function (named
C_{\text{cp}}
) for the case of a corrugated plane, which was calculated in Ref.
Nogueira et al. 2021
.
In Fig.
8
, one notes that, for the case of a corrugation occurring in the
z
-direction, the presence of curvature on the corrugated surface has a small effect on the occurrence of the valley regime, since
C_{\text{cc-$z$}}
, when compared with
C_{\text{cp}}
, changes its sign by a slightly different value of
d
for different values of
a
.
One can also see that, as
a
increases, the behavior of
C_{\text{cc-$z$}}
, for a given value of
d
, approaches to the behavior of
C_{\text{cp}}
, which means that the curvature effects vanish, as expected.

C_{\text{cc-$z$}}/C_{\text{cp}}
in terms of
d
(the sudden oscillation of the curves between
d=0.54
and
d=0.6
occurs due to the change of sign of the functions
C_{\text{cc-$z$}}
and
C_{\text{cp}}
, which occurs in this interval).

.

Now, let us analyze the curvature effects on the occurrence of the intermediate regimes.
As mentioned above, we have intermediate regimes when

, which can only occur when
B_{\text{cc-$z$}}\neq 0
, which occurs when
\langle\hat{d}_{\rho}\hat{d}_{z}\rangle\neq 0

,

, or

Interaction energy for a cylinder with a sinusoidal corrugation along the

(70)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{zz}^{\text{cc-$\phi$}}=

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{K_{j+k_{c}a}(|k|\rho_{0})}{K_{j+k_{c}a}(|k|a)}+\frac{K_{j-k_{c}a}(|k|\rho_{0})}{K_{j-k_{c}a}(|k|a)}\right],

(71)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rho\phi}^{\text{cc-$\phi$}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{\pi a\rho_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{jK_{j}\left(\left|k\right|\rho_{0}\right)}{K_{j}\left(\left|k\right|a\right)}

Let us start analyzing the curvature effects on the occurrence of the peak and valley regimes.

, and

, respectively.
In this way, from Eqs. (
66
)-(

), one sees that such regimes can only occur when
B_{\text{cc-$\phi$}}=0
, which occurs when
\langle\hat{d}_{\rho}\hat{d}_{\phi}\rangle=0
(the particle is oriented such that its axis coincides with
\rho
,
\phi
, or
z
).
In addition, we have one or the other of these regimes depending on the sign of
C_{\text{cc-$\phi$}}
, where we have the valley regime when it is positive, and the peak regime when it is negative [see Eq. (
66
)].
By considering the particle oriented such that its axis is parallel to the
\phi
-axis
(\theta=\pi/2,\varphi=\pi/2)
, in Fig.

we show the behaviors of
C_{\text{cc-$\phi$}}
and

.

IV
Final Remarks

We studied how the peak, valley and intermediate regimes for the lateral vdW force are affected by modifications on the geometric properties of the surface itself on which the corrugation occurs.
Our main results are given by Eqs. (
III.1
)-(
50
), which, applied to a sinusoidal corrugation [Eqs. (
51
) or (
65
)], showed that, in general, the presence of curvature on the corrugated surface inhibits the occurrence of valley and intermediate regimes.
When the corrugation occurs in the
z
-direction [Eq. (
51
)], we showed that the presence of curvature on the corrugated surface has a small effect on the occurrence of the valley regime, practically not affecting it.
On the other hand, for the intermediate regimes, the presence of curvature inhibit their occurrence up to a certain particle-surface distance, above which it begins to amplify the occurrence of the effect.
When the corrugation occurs in the
\phi
-direction [Eq. (
65
)], we showed that, different from the previous case, the presence of curvature on the corrugated surface inhibits the occurrence of the valley regime.
We argued that such behavior can be related to the fact that, in this case, by modifying the curvature of the cylinder we modify the structure of the corrugation itself.
For the intermediate regimes, we showed that the presence of curvature on the corrugated surface also inhibit their occurrence.

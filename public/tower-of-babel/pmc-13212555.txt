An assessment of the cytochrome P450 2-hydroxyisoflavanone synthase (2-HIS) crystal structure
Authors: Thomas L Poulos
Published: 2026

arising from Wang et al. Communications Biology 10.1038/s42003-022-04222-x (2022)

The crystal structure of the cytochrome P450, 2-HIS (PDB 8e83), published in Communications Biology by Wang et al. 1 is of considerable interest based on the unprecedented architecture of the active site and the large displacement and rotation of the essential heme cofactor compared to other P450s. The following analysis indicates that 2-HIS is a traditional P450 and the unusual features in the 8e83 structure are due to a sulfate ion that binds near the heme.

Results and discussion

Coordinates and structure factors for 8e83 were downloaded from the PDB. The electron density map of 8e83 reveals one minor mistake. The authors claim that a water molecule coordinates to the heme iron but the electron density (Fig. 1A) clearly shows that this ligand is very likely imidazole. This is not surprising since imidazole was used in the crystallization buffer, and it is well known that imidazole is an excellent heme ligand. Aside from this one error the remainder of the structure is accurate. However, given the unprecedented binding mode of the heme, it is important explore the structural basis for this unusual P450 active site. The first place to start is with homologous structures. Using the 8e83 sequence as input to a BLAST search gives cytochrome P450 CYP76HA1 (5ylw) as the top hit at 31% sequence identity and 47% similarity. The RMSD for Cα atoms between 8e83 and 5ylw is 1.5 Å. An AlphaFold3 model for 8e83 gives a traditional P450 structure and the AlphaFold3 model aligns with both 5ylw and 8e83 with an RMSD of approximately 1 Å. Although one might question the ability of AlphaFold3 to correctly predict the structure of a P450 with such unusual structural features, the high sequence similarity between 8e83 and 5ylw, which is a traditional P450, is enough to raise concerns on the 8e83 structure.

In 5ywl Arg433 is Lys. C The 8e83 structure in the same orientation as in (B). The major reorientation and repositioning of the heme is due to the sulfate anion that results in a rearrangement of key arginine residues that would normally be positioned as in (B). The sulfate is electrostatically stabilized by Arg447 and peptide NH groups. This results in a disruption of a key helical segment important for holding the heme in the correct position. Arg433 and 446 that would normally extend out and solution now interact with the heme propionates.

A backbone atom superposition of the structures in the vicinity of the heme is shown in Fig. 1. As noted by Wang et al., the heme in 8e83 is positioned very differently than in other P450s. The most important region in the present discussion is around some conserved arginine residues and a sulfate near the heme in 8e83 (Fig. 1B, C). AlphaFold3 positions three arginines, 433, 446, and 447 quite differently than in 8e83. Arginines 446 and 447 are conserved in 5ylw while arginine 433 is a lysine in 5ylw. As shown in Fig. 1B, AlphaFold3 and 5ylw superimpose quite well. Arg447 is particularly important since this is part of one of the most highly conserved regions in P450s given that this is only two amino acids from the Cys449 heme ligand. In a majority of P450s this amino acid is arginine and is important for direct interactions with the heme propionate (Fig. 1C). Why, then, do the conserved arginines adopt such different positions in 8e83? The reason is that a sulfate ion promotes a major rearrangement of these arginines such that Arg447 and peptide NH groups that in the other P450s are part of an alpha helix interact with the sulfate while Arg433 and 446 interact with the heme propionates. Therefore, sulfate included in the crystallization solution promotes a major rearrangement in regions of the structure critical to holding the heme in the correct position. The authors also note major distortions in the I helix that runs over the surface of the heme. This is due in part to the dimeric structure of the asymmetric unit. However, molecule B does not experience the same direct perturbation on the I helix as does molecule A. Therefore, the main reason for the distortion in the active site and repositioning of the heme is the sulfate anion.

Because the active site collapses around the heme leaving no room for substrate binding, traditional P450 chemistry is not possible. As a result, Wang et al. 1 propose a highly questionable mechanism. In Figure 6 of their paper, Wang et al. 1 state that the substrate binds at the periphery of the heme near one of the heme propionates. The heme propionate abstracts an H atom from the substrate leaving behind a carbon radical. A hydroxyl radical generated at the heme iron then migrates several Ångströms over the heme to the substrate to complete the reaction. This type of chemistry is not possible. H atom abstraction from an unactivated C-H bond requires a potent oxidant, which is the ferryl oxygen atom, Fe(IV) = O. It is also known that the P450 ferryl O atom has an elevated pKa, so both a large positive redox potential and high pKa are required for P450 chemistry2. The heme propionate is just the opposite and has a low redox potential and low pKa so H atom extraction has an enormous thermodynamic barrier and is not possible under physiological condition. In addition, a loose hydroxyl radical inside the active site would undoubtedly react with some neighboring group before exiting the active site, which is required for the proposed mechanism. Therefore, it is more likely that 2-HIS operates as a traditional P450 and that the 8e83 structure is of an inactive form of the enzyme owing to the effect of sulfate binding near the heme. Further evidence that 8e83 is a traditional P450 stems from their own supporting data, which shows the expected 450 nm band in the reduced carbon monoxide spectrum. Just minor perturbations in the active site results in a 420 nm band3 so if the 2-HIS structure in solution is the same as in the crystal structure then the spectrum would very likely not give the characteristic 450 nm band. One important piece of information missing from their paper is the effect of substrate binding on the spectrum. In traditional P450s substrate binding results in a clearly defined low- to high-spin shift.

Even though 8e83 is not the active form of the enzyme, it is nevertheless a remarkable structure and illustrates an unprecedented flexibility of the P450 heme pocket to generate an inactive form of the enzyme, which is still well ordered enough to crystallize.

Methods

Coordinates and structure factors for 8e83 were downloaded from the PDB and examined using Pymol 3.16.1 and Coot 0.8.9.2. A BLAST search (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi?PAGE=Protein) was carried out to identify those P450s most closely related to 8e83. The sequence of 8e83 was used to generate an Alpafold3 model (https://alphafoldserver.com).

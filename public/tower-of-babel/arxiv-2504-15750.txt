Unique Bernoulli Gibbs states and g -measures Thanks: Email: phulse@ed.ac.uk
Paul Hulse

Abstract
. A sufficient condition for the Gibbs states of a shift-invariant specification on a one-dimensional lattice to be the g g -chains for some continuous function g g is obtained. This is then used to derive criteria under which there is a unique Gibbs state, which is also shift-invariant and Bernoulli.

1.
Introduction

We consider Gibbs states on sequence spaces, that is, probability measures
for which there is a specified set of functions that determine
the probabilities of configurations on any finite subset
conditioned on the configuration outside that subset.
Let
X=S^{I}
be a sequence space, where

S
is a finite set with the discrete topology,
and
I
is a countable (infinite) set.
The topology on
X
is the product topology,
with respect to which
X
is compact,
and
{\mathcal{B}}
denotes the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-algebra.
If
x\in S^{\Lambda^{\prime}}

(\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq I)

and
\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}
,
then
x_{\Lambda}
denotes the natural projection of
x
onto
S^{\Lambda}
,
and
[x]_{\Lambda}
is the cylinder set
\{y\in X:y_{\Lambda}=x_{\Lambda}\}
;
similarly, if
s\in S
and
i\in I
,
[s]_{i}
denotes
\{y\in X:y_{i}=s\}
.
The sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
{\mathcal{B}}
generated
by
\{[x]_{\Lambda}\,:\,x\in X\}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{B}}_{\Lambda}
,
and
{\mathcal{C}}
denotes the set of finite, non-empty subsets of
I
.

A
specification
on
X
is a collection

{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}

of non-negative Borel functions on
X
such that, for all

\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
x\in X
,

\sum_{y\in[x]_{\Lambda^{c}}}\phi_{\Lambda}(y)=1,

(1.1)

and for
K\subseteq\Lambda
,

\phi_{\Lambda}(x)=\phi_{K}(x)\sum_{y\in[x]_{K^{c}}}\phi_{\Lambda}(y).

(1.2)

The specification
{\mathcal{V}}
is said to be continuous if

\phi_{\Lambda}
is continuous for all
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
and positive if
\phi_{\Lambda}>0
for all
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
.
If
I={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

(d\geq 1)
then
T=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I}
denotes the

d
-dimensional shift on
X
, that is,

(T_{i}x)_{j}=x_{i+j}

(x\in X,\ i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
,
and
{\mathcal{V}}
is said to be
T
-invariant if

\phi_{i+\Lambda}(x)=\phi_{\Lambda}(T_{i}x)

for all
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
.
(As usual, when
d=1
, we equate
T
with
T_{1}
.)

A probability measure
\mu
on
(X,{\mathcal{B}})
is a
Gibbs state

for a specification
{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}
if

\mu\bigl([x]_{\Lambda}\big|\,{\mathcal{B}}_{\Lambda^{c}}\bigr)(x)=\phi_{\Lambda}(x)\qquad\hbox{a.e.}(\mu)

for all
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
.
The set of Gibbs states is denoted by
{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}})
.

If
{\mathcal{V}}
is continuous, there exists at least one Gibbs state,
and natural questions concern the structure of
{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}})
,
in particular, uniqueness and any ergodic or mixing properties
of Gibbs states.
In general,
{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}})
is a compact, convex subset of
M(X)

(the set of probability measures on
(X,{\mathcal{B}})
), and
its extreme points are those states for which the tail

\sigma
-field
\bigcap_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}{\mathcal{B}}_{\Lambda^{c}}

is trivial. If in addition
I={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and
{\mathcal{V}}
is
T
-invariant,
then at least one of the Gibbs states is
T
-invariant;
the
T
-invariant Gibbs states form a convex subset of
{\mathcal{G}}(V)
,
the extreme points of which are the ergodic ones.
Thus, a unique
T
-invariant Gibbs state
\mu
is necessarily ergodic,
but if it is also the only Gibbs state, then it follows from the
trivially of the tail field that
(T,\mu)
has some strong mixing properties.
However, from the dynamical systems perspective, the system
with the strongest mixing properties is
one which is isomorphic to, and hence indistinguishable from,
a Bernoulli shift (by which we mean the invertible dynamical system
generated by a sequence of independent identically distributed
random variables taking values in a finite set).
While it is known that for those specifications which are
attractive (or ferromagnetic), uniqueness of the Gibbs state
implies the Bernoulli property (see
[
1
,
12
,
13
]
),
in general this is not the case.

Dobrushin
[
7
]
showed that if

{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}
is a continuous specification
such that

\sup_{i\in I}\sum_{s\in S}\sum_{j\neq i}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{\{j\}^{c}}=y_{\{j\}^{c}}\\
x_{i}=y_{i}=s\end{subarray}}|\phi_{\{i\}}(x)-\phi_{\{i\}}(y)|<2,

then
{\mathcal{V}}
has a unique Gibbs state
\mu
.
This result holds for all countable
I
and does not depend
on
I
having a group structure. If
I={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
(in fact, if
I
is any
amenable group) and
{\mathcal{V}}
is
T
-invariant,
then
\mu
is
T
-invariant and the corresponding
dynamical system
(T,\mu)
is Bernoulli (see
[
13
]
).

Of particular interest in statistical mechanics
are those specifications determined by an interaction potential

\Phi=\{\Phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}
, that is, a set
of functions on
X
such that
\Phi_{\Lambda}

is
{{\mathcal{B}}}_{\Lambda}
-measurable.
The corresponding specification
{\mathcal{V}}_{\Phi}
is given by

\phi_{\Lambda}(x)=\exp\bigl(-H^{\Phi}_{\Lambda}(x)\bigr)\left\{\sum_{y\in[x]_{\Lambda^{c}}}\exp\bigl(-H^{\Phi}_{\Lambda}(y)\bigr)\right\}^{-1},

(1.3)

where

H^{\Phi}_{\Lambda}(x)=\sum_{\{K\in{\mathcal{C}}:K\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\}}\Phi_{K}(x).

Usually
\Phi
is required to satisfy a summability condition
that ensures
H^{\Phi}_{\Lambda}
is continuous,
and hence that
{\mathcal{V}}_{\Phi}
is continuous and positive.
When
I={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, the interaction potential
\Phi
is said to be

T
-invariant if
\Phi_{\Lambda+i}(x)=\Phi_{\Lambda}(T_{i}x)
for all

\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, in which case

{\mathcal{V}}_{\Phi}
is also
T
-invariant.
An example which has been studied extensively is
the well-known Ising model on
\{-1,1\}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
,
where
\Phi_{\Lambda}\equiv 0
for
|\Lambda|\neq 2
.
In particular, we note that in dimensions
d\geq 2
,
the nearest-neighbour model
(where
\Phi_{\{i,j\}}\equiv 0
unless
|i-j|=1
)
gives examples of non-unique Gibbs states.
When
d=1
, the situation is quite different.
Here, the nearest-neighbour model has a unique Gibbs state;
more generally, if
\Phi_{\{i,j\}}(x)=-J(|i-j|)x_{i}x_{j}
,
where
\{J(n)\}
is a non-negative sequence,
there is a unique Gibbs state provided

\sum_{i=1}^{n}iJ(i)=o(\sqrt{\log n})

[
17
]
.
On the other hand, if
J(n)=\beta n^{-\alpha}
, there is a phase transition
(i.e., non-uniqueness) for large enough
\beta
when
1<\alpha<2

[
9
]
.
(A more thorough survey of such results can be found in
[
11
]
.)

For potentials in general,
Ruelle
[
19
]
showed that when
I={\mathbb{Z}}
and

\Phi=\{\Phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}
is
T
-invariant,
there is a unique
T
-invariant Gibbs state provided

\sum_{\Lambda\ni 0}\text{diam}(\Lambda)\|\Phi_{\Lambda}\|<\infty.

(1.4)

Gallavotti
[
10
]
and Ledrappier
[
16
]
independently
proved it has the Bernoulli property.
Coelho and Quas
[
6
]
pointed out that
by combining Ruelle’s characterisation
[
18
]
of the
T
-invariant Gibbs states
of
\Phi
as equilibrium states of the function

A_{\Phi}(x)=\sum_{\min\Lambda=0}\phi_{\Lambda}(x)

with the results of Walters
[
21
]
,
(
1.4
)
could be replaced by the weaker hypothesis

\sum_{\min\Lambda=0}\text{diam}(\Lambda)\|\Phi_{\Lambda}\|<\infty.

(1.5)

Berbee
[
3
]
considered continuous
specifications
\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}

determined as follows.
There is a sequence
\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{C}}

such that
\Lambda_{n}\nearrow I
,
and positive
{\mathcal{B}}_{\Lambda_{n-1}^{c}}
-measurable functions
f_{n}\in C(X)

(n\geq 0)
(with
\Lambda_{-1}=\emptyset
)
such that

\phi_{\Lambda_{n}}(x)=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n}f_{j}(x)\right)\left(\sum_{y\in[x]_{\Lambda_{n}^{c}}}\prod_{j=0}^{n}f_{j}(y)\right)^{-1}\qquad(n\geq 0,\,x\in X).

Any positive continuous specification can be described in this way
(see Section
2
).
For a positive function
f
on
X
and
\Lambda\subseteq I
, let

r_{\Lambda}(f)=\sup\{f(x)/f(y)\,:\,x_{\Lambda}=y_{\Lambda}\},\qquad\bar{r}_{\Lambda}(f)=\inf\{f(x)/f(y)\,:\,x_{\Lambda}=y_{\Lambda}\}.

(Note that
\bar{r}_{\Lambda}(f)=r_{\Lambda}(f)^{-1}
.)
Berbee showed that if

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\prod_{k=0}^{n}\inf_{i\geq 0}\bar{r}_{\Lambda_{i+k}}(f_{i})=\infty,

(1.6)

then there is a unique Gibbs state.
Like Dobrushin’s result, this holds for any countable
I
,
and for non-invariant, as well as invariant, specifications.
Taking
I={\mathbb{Z}}
and

\Lambda_{2k}=[-k,k],\qquad\Lambda_{2k+1}=[-k,k+1]\qquad(k\geq 0)

(1.7)

(here and later, we use the usual notation for an interval

{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
to denote
{\mathcal{I}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
),
and comparing hypotheses for the relevant
f_{n}
for an
interaction potential (
2.7
), it can be seen that Berbee’s
hypothesis is weaker than Ruelle’s (
1.4
);
however, while in some cases
it is weaker than that of Coelho and Quas (
1.5
),
in others it is not.

The hypotheses (
1.4
)–(
1.5
) can be viewed as
measures of the continuity of
A(\Phi)
in terms of its variation
on sites outside finite subsets, or equivalently, in terms of the
rate at which
\|\phi_{\Lambda}\|
decreases as
|\Lambda|
increases.
Similarly, (
1.6
) is a measure of the continuity
of the functions
f_{n}
, which in the case of an interaction potential

\Phi
with the
f_{n}
defined by (
2.7
), is also related
to the continuity of
A_{\Phi}
.
In this paper, we consider whether this type of continuity hypothesis
can be relaxed.
We restrict our attention to the case
I={\mathbb{Z}}

since (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are only valid in this case,
and while Berbee’s result holds for any
I
, in the case of

T
-invariant specifications it would seem that (
1.6
)
rules out any genuinely multi-dimensional examples.
Indeed, as has already been noted, the nearest-neighbour Ising
model provides examples of non-uniqueness in dimensions greater
than one.

As Dobrushin
[
7
]
pointed out, any positive specification

{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}

is completely determined by
\{\phi_{\{n\}}\,:\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
;
consequently, if
{\mathcal{V}}
is also
T
-invariant, it is determined by
\phi_{\{0\}}
alone.
Thus, it seems natural to seek conditions for uniqueness and Bernoullicity
in terms of
\phi_{\{0\}}
,
or equivalently, the ratios
\{\phi_{\{0\}}(s_{0}x)/\phi_{\{0\}}(t_{0}x)\colon s,t\in S,\,x\in X\}
,
where
s_{n}x\in X

(s\in S,\,x\in X,\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}})
is defined by

(s_{n}x)_{i}=\begin{cases}s,&i=n,\\
x_{i},&i\neq n.\end{cases}

We show the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}

be a positive, continuous,
T
-invariant specification on
S^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
and let

f(x)=\phi_{\{0\}}(x)/\phi_{\{0\}}(s_{0}x)\qquad(x\in X)

for some
s\in S
.
If, for some
0<\alpha\leq 1
,

\prod_{i=0}^{n}r_{(-\infty,i]}(f)=O\bigl(n^{1-\alpha}\bigr),

and

\sum_{i=2^{n}}^{2^{n+1}}\Bigl(\log r_{[-i,\infty)}(f)\Bigr)^{2\alpha}=o(1),

then
{\mathcal{V}}
has a unique Gibbs state
\mu
, which is
T
-invariant,
and
(T,\mu)
is Bernoulli.

In particular, there is a unique, Bernoulli Gibbs state if

\max\left\{\log r_{(-\infty,n]}(f),\,\log r_{[-n,\infty)}(f)\right\}\leq\beta n^{-1}+a_{n}\qquad(n\geq 1)

for some
\beta<1/2
and non-negative series
\sum a_{n}<\infty
.
In fact, we prove Theorem
1.1
under slightly weaker hypotheses
(Theorem
5.1
),
which allows the following version to be deduced when the specification is given
in terms of an interaction potential.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Phi=\{\Phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}

be a
T
-invariant interaction potential on
S^{\mathbb{Z}}
. If

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\ni 0\\
\Lambda\cap[n,\infty)\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}{\rm var}(\Phi_{\Lambda})\leq\beta n^{-1}+a_{n}\qquad(n\geq 1)

(1.8)

for some
\beta<1/2
and
a_{n}\geq 0
such that
\sum a_{n}<\infty
,
then
\Phi
has a unique Gibbs state
\mu
, which is
T
-invariant,
and
(T,\mu)
is Bernoulli.

This includes all interaction potentials satisfying (
1.4
).
In some cases,
such as when
\sup\{|\Lambda|\,:\,\Phi_{\Lambda}\not\equiv 0\}<\infty
,
(
1.8
) is also weaker than (
1.5
),
but in others it is not.
Comparison with (
1.6
) is less straightforward,
since (
1.6
) applies to non-invariant specifications
and arbitrary increasing sequences
\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{C}}
.
Consideration of the Ising model suggests Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

can give better results for uniqueness in some cases,
but the main difference is that the Bernoulli property is obtained.

The idea of the proof of Theorem
5.1
is to characterise a Gibbs state
\mu

in terms of its conditional probabilities

g(x)=\mu\bigl([x]_{\{0\}}\big|\,{\mathcal{B}}_{(-\infty,-1]}\bigr)(x)\qquad(x\in X),

in other words, as a
g
-chain; uniqueness,
T
-invariance and the Bernoulli property
can then be determined from similar results for
g
-chains.
Here we use a result (Theorem
4.4
) which is a formulation
based on the techniques of Johansson, Öberg and Pollicott
[
15
]
.
The estimate of the variation of
\log g
needed for Theorem
4.4
is
provided by Theorem
4.2
.
This result may be of some interest in its own right
as it gives a sufficient condition for the set of Gibbs states of a
T
-invariant specification to be
the set of
g
-chains for some continuous function
g
.
(A characterisation of
g
-chains which are also Gibbs states,
as well as a discussion of the problem of determining whether a Gibbs state is a
g
-chain,
can be found in
[
5
]
.)
Theorem
4.2
follows from Proposition
4.1
,
which gives a general criterion for
the set of Gibbs states to be the same as
the set of
g
-chains for some continuous function
g
,
and Proposition
3.1
,
which is an adaptation of Berbee’s method of ratio bounds in
[
3
]
.

Lastly, we consider some alternative hypotheses which guarantee a unique
T
-invariant
Gibbs state but not the Bernoulli property.

2.
Specifications and Gibbs states

We start with some preliminary remarks about specifications and
Gibbs states; a more detailed account can be found in
[
11
]
, for example.
The following notation will be used throughout:
if
x\in S^{\Lambda_{1}}
,
y\in S^{\Lambda_{2}}
are sequences
and
\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}=\emptyset
,
then
xy\in S^{\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}}
denotes the sequence such that

(xy)_{i}=\begin{cases}x_{i},&i\in\Lambda_{1},\\
y_{i},&i\in\Lambda_{2}.\end{cases}

The set of bounded Borel functions on
X
is denoted by
B(X)
.

Let
X=S^{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence space as described in the introduction,
and let
{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}
be a specification on
X
.
Then
{\mathcal{V}}
determines a set of probability measures

\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda}(\,\cdot\,|\,x)

(\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}},\ x\in X)

as follows:

\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda}(f\,|\,x)=\sum_{y\in[x]_{\Lambda}}\phi_{\Lambda}(y)f(y)\qquad(f\in C(X)),

and
\mu\in{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}})
if and only if

\mu\bigl([x]_{\Lambda}\big|\,{\mathcal{B}}_{\Lambda^{c}}\bigr)(x)=\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda}\bigl([x]_{\Lambda}\big|\,x\bigr)\quad{\rm a.e.}\,(\mu)\qquad(\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}).

Thus, if
{\mathcal{V}}
is continuous,

{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}})
is closed (in the weak-* topology),
and moreover,
any weak-* limit of measures of the form

\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\,\cdot\,|\,x^{(n)})\qquad(x^{(n)}\in X,\ \Lambda_{n}\nearrow{\mathbb{Z}})

is a Gibbs state for
{\mathcal{V}}
.
Since
M(X)
is compact, it follows that
there is at least one Gibbs state
\mu
,
and the uniqueness of
\mu
is equivalent to
the uniform convergence (for each
f\in C(X)
)
of
\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda}(f\,|\,x)
to
\mu(f)

as
\Lambda\nearrow{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Similarly, if
{\mathcal{V}}
is
T
-invariant,
any weak-* limit of measures of the form

\mu_{n}(f)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n-1]}(fT^{i}\,|\,x^{(n)})\qquad(f\in C(X),\,x^{(n)}\in X)

is a
T
-invariant Gibbs state for
{\mathcal{V}}
.
By compactness, there is always one such measure
\mu
,
and its uniqueness is equivalent to

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n-1]}(fT^{i}\,|\,x)=\mu(f)

uniformly in
x
for all
f\in C(X)
.

Note that if
\phi_{\Lambda}
is given for some
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
then (
1.2
) determines
\phi_{K}
for all
K\subseteq\Lambda
.
Moreover, if (
1.2
) holds for the pairs
K,\Lambda

and
K^{\prime},K
, where
K^{\prime}\subseteq K\subseteq\Lambda
,
then it holds for
K^{\prime},\Lambda
.
Thus, to define a specification,
it is enough to consider a sequence

\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{C}}

such that
\Lambda_{n}\nearrow{\mathbb{Z}}
and specify the
\phi_{\Lambda_{n}}

so that (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) hold for

\Lambda_{n-1},\Lambda_{n}

(n\geq 1)
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be positive;
note that for positive specifications, (
1.2
) is equivalent to

\frac{\phi_{K}(x)}{\phi_{K}(x^{*}_{K}x^{\phantom{*}}_{K^{c}})}=\frac{\phi_{\Lambda}(x)}{\phi_{\Lambda}(x^{*}_{K}x^{\phantom{*}}_{K^{c}})}\qquad(K\subseteq\Lambda,\ x,x^{*}\in X).

(2.1)

If
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}

and
\{i_{0},\ldots,i_{n}\}
is an enumeration of
\Lambda
,
with
\Lambda_{j}=\{i_{0},\ldots,i_{j}\}
(
0\leq j\leq n
),
then for any
x,x^{*}\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\phi_{\Lambda}(x)}{\phi_{\Lambda}(x_{\Lambda}^{*}x_{\Lambda^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{\Lambda}(x)}{\phi_{\Lambda}(x_{\Lambda_{n-1}}^{*}x_{\Lambda_{n-1}^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}\,\cdot\,\frac{\phi_{\Lambda}(x_{\Lambda_{n-1}}^{*}x_{\Lambda_{n-1}^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}{\phi_{\Lambda}(x_{\Lambda}^{*}x_{\Lambda^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{\Lambda_{n-1}}(x)}{\phi_{\Lambda_{n-1}}(x_{\Lambda_{n-1}}^{*}x_{\Lambda_{n-1}^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}\,\cdot\,\frac{\phi_{\{i_{n}\}}(x_{\Lambda_{n-1}}^{*}x_{\Lambda_{n-1}^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}{\phi_{\{i_{n}\}}(x_{\Lambda}^{*}x_{\Lambda^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}

\displaystyle\qquad(\text{by \eqref{1aiii}}),

and so it follows that

\frac{\phi_{\Lambda}(x)}{\phi_{\Lambda}(x_{\Lambda}^{*}x_{\Lambda^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}=\prod_{j=0}^{n}\frac{\phi_{\{i_{j}\}}(x_{\Lambda_{j-1}}^{*}x_{\Lambda_{j-1}^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}{\phi_{\{i_{j}\}}(x_{\Lambda_{j}}^{*}x_{\Lambda_{j}^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}\qquad(x,x^{*}\in X,\ n\geq 0).

(2.2)

(Here and later,
\Lambda_{-1}
is taken to be
\emptyset
.)
This, together with (
1.1
), determines
\phi_{\Lambda}
.
In particular, any positive specification

{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}

is completely determined by the
\phi_{\{n\}}

(n\in{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Moreover,
{\mathcal{V}}
is
T
-invariant provided

\phi_{\{n\}}=\phi_{\{0\}}\circ T^{n}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
in which case it is determined solely by
\phi_{\{0\}}
.

Conversely, given a sequence of positive functions
\{\phi_{\{n\}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset B(X)

such that
\sum_{y\in[x]_{\{n\}}}\phi_{\{n\}}(y)=1
for all
x\in X
,
and
\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{C}}
such that
\Lambda_{n}\nearrow{\mathbb{Z}}

and
|\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{n-1}|=1

(n\geq 0)
,
define
\phi_{\Lambda_{n}}
by (
1.1
) and

\frac{\phi_{\Lambda_{n}}(x)}{\phi_{\Lambda_{n}}(x_{\Lambda_{n}}^{*}x_{\Lambda_{n}^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}=\prod_{j=0}^{n}\frac{\phi_{\{i_{j}\}}(x_{\Lambda_{j-1}}^{*}x_{\Lambda_{j-1}^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}{\phi_{\{i_{j}\}}(x_{\Lambda_{j}}^{*}x_{\Lambda_{j}^{c}}^{\phantom{*}})}\qquad(x,x^{*}\in X,\ n\geq 1),

where
\{i_{n}\}=\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{n-1}
.
Clearly, the
\phi_{\Lambda_{n}}
satisfy (
2.1
),
and so determine a specification, which is independent of the choice of
\{\Lambda_{n}\}

since a specification is determined solely by the
\phi_{\{n\}}
.
Thus, any sequence of positive functions
\{\phi_{\{n\}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset B(X)

such that
\sum_{y\in[x]_{\{n\}}}\phi_{\{n\}}(y)=1
for all
x\in X

determines a unique positive specification.
In particular, any positive
f\in B(X)
determines a (unique)
T
-invariant specification

{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}
such that

\phi_{\{n\}}(x)=\frac{f(T^{n}x)}{\sum_{y\in[T^{n}x]_{\{0\}}}f(y)}\qquad(x\in X,\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

By a
specification sequence
,
we mean a sequence
\{\Lambda_{n},f_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{C}}\times B(X)

such that
\Lambda_{n-1}\subset\Lambda_{n}
,

|\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{n-1}|=1
,
and
f_{n}
is positive and
{\mathcal{B}}_{\Lambda_{n-1}^{c}}
-measurable;

3.
A ratio bound

Given a specification sequence
\bigl\{[0,n],f_{n}\bigr\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
,
define
L_{m,n}\colon B(X)\rightarrow B(X)

(0\leq m\leq n)
by

L_{m,n}f(x)=\sum_{y\in[x]_{[m,n]^{c}}}\prod_{i=m}^{n}f_{i}(y)f(y)\qquad(f\in B(X)),

and let

\displaystyle\rho_{k}^{(n)}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\frac{L_{m,m+n}{\bf 1}[\zeta](x)}{L_{m,m+n}{\bf 1}[\zeta](y)}\,:\,\zeta\in S^{[m,m+k]},\ m\geq 0\right\},

\displaystyle\rho_{-1}^{(n)}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\frac{L_{m,m+n}1(x)}{L_{m,m+n}1(y)}\,:\,m\geq 0\right\}\qquad(k,n\geq 0,\,x,y\in X).

In addition to the usual notation for sets of integers,
we denote the sets of negative and non-positive integers
by
{\mathbb{N}}^{-}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}
respectively.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\bigl\{[0,n],f_{n}\bigr\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a specification sequence,

A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}^{-}
, and

v_{k}=\inf_{i\geq 0}\bar{r}_{A\cup[0,i+k]}(f_{i})\qquad(k\geq 0).

Then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\ \inf_{x_{A}=y_{A}}\rho_{0}^{(n)}(x,y)\rho_{0}^{(n)}(y,x)\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\Biggl(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\prod_{j=0}^{k}v_{j}\Biggr)^{2}\Biggl(1+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\prod_{j=0}^{k}v_{j}\Biggr)^{-2}.

We split the proof of Proposition
3.1
into two lemmas.
The proof of the first one is based on that of
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset B(X)
and
A
be as above,
and let
x,y\in X
be such that
x_{A}=y_{A}
.
If
\{v_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is an increasing, positive sequence such that

v_{k}\leq\inf_{i\geq 0}\bar{r}_{A\cup[0,i+k]}(f_{i})\qquad(k\geq 0).

then

\rho_{k}^{(n)}(x,y)\geq v_{k}\rho_{k-1}^{(n-1)}(x,y)+\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}(v_{j+1}-v_{j})\rho_{j}^{(n-1)}(x,y)\qquad(0\leq k\leq n).

Proof.

We denote
\rho_{k}^{(n)}(x,y)
by
\rho_{k}^{(n)}
,
and
[\zeta]_{[i,j]}
by
[\zeta]_{i}^{j}

(
\zeta\in S^{\Lambda}
,
[i,j]\subseteq\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}
).
Let

f^{(k)}_{m}(w)=\inf\bigl\{f_{m}(z)\,:\,z\in[x]_{A}\cap[w]_{0}^{m+k}\bigr\},

and

\phi^{(k+1)}_{m}(w)=f^{(k+1)}_{m}(w)-f^{(k)}_{m}(w)\qquad(k,m\geq 0,\ w\in X).

Note that

f^{(k)}_{m}(w)\geq v_{k}f_{m}(w)\qquad(w\in[x]_{A}),

and

f_{m}\geq f_{m}^{(k)}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{N}\phi_{m}^{(j)}\qquad(N>k).

Let
m\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
, and
\zeta\in S^{[m,m+k]}
for some
0\leq k\leq n
.
Since
f^{(j)}_{m}(w)
,
\phi_{m}^{(j)}(w)
depend only on
w_{[m,m+j]}
,
we can identify them with the functions induced by
the natural projection onto
S^{[m,m+j]}
.
Then

\displaystyle L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(f^{(k)}_{m}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}x_{\{m\}^{c}})

\displaystyle=f^{(k)}_{m}(\zeta)L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl({\bf 1}[\zeta]_{m+1}^{m+k}\bigr)(x)

\displaystyle\geq\rho_{k-1}^{(n-1)}f^{(k)}_{m}(\zeta)L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl({\bf 1}[\zeta]_{m+1}^{m+k}\bigr)(y)

\displaystyle=\rho_{k-1}^{(n-1)}L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(f^{(k)}_{m}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}y_{\{m\}^{c}})

\displaystyle\hskip 54.2025pt\qquad(0\leq k\leq n,\,n\geq 1,\,m\geq 0),

and

L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(\phi_{m}^{(j)}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}x_{\{m\}^{c}})\\
\begin{aligned} &=\sum_{\eta\in S^{[m+k+1,m+j]}}\phi_{m}^{(j)}(\zeta\eta)L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl({\bf 1}[\zeta\eta]_{m+1}^{m+j}\bigr)(x)\\
&\geq\rho_{j-1}^{(n-1)}\sum_{\eta\in S^{[m+k+1,m+j]}}\phi_{m}^{(j)}(\zeta\eta)L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl({\bf 1}[\zeta\eta]_{m+1}^{m+j}\bigr)(y)\\
&=\rho_{j-1}^{(n-1)}L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(\phi_{m}^{(j)}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}y_{\{m\}^{c}})\end{aligned}\\
(1\leq k+1\leq j\leq n,\,m\geq 0).

Therefore,

\displaystyle L_{m,m+n}{\bf 1}[\zeta](x)

\displaystyle\geq L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(f_{m}^{(k)}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}x_{\{m\}^{c}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(\phi_{m}^{(j)}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}x_{\{m\}^{c}})

\displaystyle\geq\rho_{k-1}^{(n-1)}L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(f_{m}^{(k)}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}y_{\{m\}^{c}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\rho_{j-1}^{(n-1)}L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(\phi_{m}^{(j)}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}y_{\{m\}^{c}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\bigl(\rho_{j-1}^{(n-1)}-\rho_{j}^{(n-1)}\bigr)L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(f_{m}^{(j)}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}y_{\{m\}^{c}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\rho^{(n-1)}_{n-1}L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(f_{m}^{(n)}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}y_{\{m\}^{c}})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\bigl(\rho_{j-1}^{(n-1)}-\rho_{j}^{(n-1)}\bigr)v_{j}L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(f_{m}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}y_{\{m\}^{c}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\rho^{(n-1)}_{n-1}v_{n}L_{m+1,m+n}\bigl(f_{m}{\bf 1}[\zeta]\bigr)(\zeta_{\{m\}}y_{\{m\}^{c}})

\displaystyle=\left(\rho^{(n-1)}_{n-1}v_{n}+\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\bigl(\rho_{j-1}^{(n-1)}-\rho_{j}^{(n-1)}\bigr)v_{j}\right)L_{m,m+n}{\bf 1}[\zeta](y)

for
0\leq k\leq n-1,\,m\geq 0
,
and

L_{m,m+n}{\bf 1}[\zeta](x)=\rho^{(n-1)}_{n-1}v_{n}L_{m,m+n}{\bf 1}[\zeta](y)

for
k=n\geq 1
.
Hence,

\rho_{k}^{(n)}\geq\rho^{(n-1)}_{n-1}v_{n}+\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\bigl(\rho_{j-1}^{(n-1)}-\rho_{j}^{(n-1)}\bigr)v_{j}\qquad(0\leq k\leq n,\,n\geq 1)

(where the empty sum is taken to be 0).
Rearranging, we have

\rho_{k}^{(n)}\geq\rho_{k-1}^{(n-1)}v_{k}+\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\rho_{j}^{(n-1)}(v_{j+1}-v_{j})\qquad(0\leq k\leq n,\,n\geq 1),

as required.
∎

Now, given an increasing sequence
\{v_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}

such that
0<v_{k}\leq 1
for all
k
,
consider
p_{k}^{(n)}

(-1\leq k\leq n,\,n\geq 0)
,
defined recursively by

\displaystyle p_{-1}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=1,

(3.1)

\displaystyle p_{0}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=v_{0},

(3.2)

\displaystyle p^{(n)}_{k}

\displaystyle=v_{k}p_{k-1}^{(n-1)}+\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}(v_{j+1}-v_{j})p_{j}^{(n-1)}\qquad(0\leq k\leq n,\,n\geq 1).

(3.3)

(As before, when
k=n
, the empty sum is taken to be 0.)

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p_{k}^{(n)}

(k\geq-1,\,n\geq 0)
and
\{v_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}

be as above. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}p^{(n)}_{0}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\Biggl(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\prod_{j=0}^{k}v_{j}\Biggr)\Biggl(1+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\prod_{j=0}^{k}v_{j}\Biggr)^{-1}.

Proof.

It follows from (
3.3
) that for
k\leq n
,

p^{(n+1)}_{k}-p^{(n)}_{k}=v_{k}(p_{k-1}^{(n)}-p_{k-1}^{(n-1)})+(v_{n+1}-v_{n})p_{n}^{(n)}+\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}(v_{j+1}-v_{j})(p_{j}^{(n)}-p_{j}^{(n-1)}),

and from (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) that

p_{0}^{(1)}-p_{0}^{(0)}=(v_{1}-v_{0})v_{0}\geq 0.

Hence, it follows inductively that

p^{(n+1)}_{n}\geq p^{(n)}_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
,
and then that

p_{k}^{(n+1)}\geq p_{k}^{(n)}
for all
0\leq k\leq n
.
Thus

p_{k}=\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{k}^{(n)}\qquad(k\geq-1)

is well defined, and

p_{k}=v_{k}p_{k-1}+\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(v_{j+1}-v_{j})p_{j}\qquad(k\geq 0).

Therefore,

\displaystyle p_{k}-p_{k+1}

\displaystyle=v_{k}p_{k-1}-v_{k+1}p_{k}+(v_{k+1}-v_{k})p_{k}

\displaystyle=v_{k}(p_{k-1}-p_{k}),

and so,

p_{k}-p_{k+1}=(1-p_{0})\prod_{j=0}^{k}v_{j}.

Summing gives

p_{0}-p_{n+1}=(1-p_{0})\sum_{k=0}^{n}\prod_{j=0}^{k}v_{j}.

Since
p_{n+1}\geq 0
,

p_{0}\geq\Biggl(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\prod_{j=0}^{k}v_{j}\Biggr)\Biggl(1+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\prod_{j=0}^{k}v_{j}\Biggr)^{-1},

from which the result follows.
∎

4.
Gibbs states and g-chains

Let

{\mathcal{G}}_{X}=\biggl\{g\in B(X)\,:\,g\text{ is ${\mathcal{B}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}}$-measurable},\ g\geq 0,\ \sum_{s\in S}g(s_{0}x)=1\ \forall x\in X\biggr\},

and let
g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{X}
. A measure
\mu\in M(X)
is a
g
-chain if

\mu\bigl([x]_{\{n\}}\big|\,{\mathcal{B}}_{(-\infty,n-1]}\bigr)(x)=g(T^{n}x)\qquad\hbox{a.e.}(\mu)

for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
A
T
-invariant
g
-chain is usually referred to as a
g
-measure.
If
g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{X}
is continuous, there is always at least
one
g
-measure.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}

be a positive, continuous,
T
-invariant specification.
If
\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\big|\,x\bigr)

converges to a continuous limit in
x\in X
as
n\to\infty
, then

g_{\mathcal{V}}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\big|\,x\bigr)\qquad(x\in X)

is a positive, continuous function in
{\mathcal{G}}_{X}
,
and
\mu\in{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}})
if and only if
\mu
is a
g_{\mathcal{V}}
-chain.

Proof.

Let

g_{\mathcal{V}}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\big|\,x\bigr)\qquad(x\in X)

(4.1)

as above; by hypothesis,
g_{\mathcal{V}}
is continuous.
Note that
g_{\mathcal{V}}(x)
is
{\mathcal{B}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}}
-measurable,
and since

\inf\phi_{\{0\}}\leq\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\big|\,x\bigr)\leq\sup\phi_{\{0\}}\qquad(n\geq 0,\,x\in X),

it follows that
g_{\mathcal{V}}
is positive.
Clearly,
\sum_{s\in S}g_{\mathcal{V}}(s_{0}x)=1
for all
x\in X
,
and so
g_{\mathcal{V}}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{X}
.
Moreover, it follows from the Martingale Theorem that

\mu\bigl(\,[x]_{\{n\}}\big|\,{\mathcal{B}}_{(-\infty,n-1]}\bigr)(x)=g_{\mathcal{V}}(T^{n}x)\qquad\text{a.e.}\,(\mu)

for any Gibbs state
\mu
of
{\mathcal{V}}
when
n=0
,
and hence, by the
T
-invariance of
{\mathcal{V}}
, for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
;
thus, any Gibbs state is a
g_{\mathcal{V}}
-chain.

Conversely, if
\mu
is a
g_{\mathcal{V}}
-chain, then

\displaystyle\mu\bigl(\,[x]_{\{n\}}\big|\,{\mathcal{B}}_{\{n\}^{c}}\bigr)(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\mu\bigl(\,[x]_{\{n\}}\big|\,{\mathcal{B}}_{(-\infty,m]\setminus\{n\}}\bigr)(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\prod_{i=n}^{m}g_{\mathcal{V}}(T^{i}x)}{\sum_{s\in S}\prod_{i=n}^{m}g_{\mathcal{V}}(T^{i}(s_{n}x))}\qquad\text{a.e.}\,(\mu)\quad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

Given
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, consider
k>m>n
.
From (
4.1
) we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=n}^{m}g_{\mathcal{V}}(T^{i}x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\prod_{i=n}^{m}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[i,k]}\bigl([x]_{\{i\}}\big|\,x\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[n,k]}\bigl([x]_{[n,m]}\big|\,x\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{w\in[x]_{[m+1,k]^{c}}}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{\{n\}}\bigl([x]_{\{n\}}\big|\,w\bigr)\,\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[n+1,k]}\bigl([w]_{[n+1,k]}\big|\,x\bigr)

for all
x\in X
.
Therefore, for all
s,t\in S
,
x\in X
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mu\bigl([s]_{n}\,\big|\,{\mathcal{B}}_{\{n\}^{c}}\bigr)(x)}{\mu\bigl([t]_{n}\,\big|\,{\mathcal{B}}_{\{n\}^{c}}\bigr)(x)}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{w\in[x]_{[m+1,k]^{c}}}\frac{\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{\{n\}}\bigl([s]_{n}\,\big|\,w\bigr)}{\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{\{n\}}\bigl([t]_{n}\,\big|\,w\bigr)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{\{n\}}(s_{n}x)}{\phi_{\{n\}}(t_{n}x)}\qquad\text{a.e.}\,(\mu).

It follows that

\mu\bigl([s]_{n}\big|{\mathcal{B}}_{\{n\}^{c}}\bigr)(x)=\phi_{\{n\}}(s_{n}x)

a.e.
(\mu)
for all
s\in S
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
and so
\mu\in{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}})
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}

be a positive, continuous,
T
-invariant specification
with specification sequence
\{[0,n],f_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\prod_{j=0}^{k}\inf_{i\geq 0}\bar{r}_{(-\infty,i+j]}(f_{i})=\infty,

then

g_{\mathcal{V}}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,x\bigr)

is well defined, the convergence is uniform in
x
,
and it determines a positive function

such that

r_{[-n,0]}(g_{\mathcal{V}})\leq\left(1+R_{n}^{-1}\right)^{2},

where

R_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\prod_{j=0}^{k}\inf_{i\geq 0}\bar{r}_{[-n,i+j]}(f_{i})\qquad(n\geq 0).

Moreover,
\mu\in{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}})
if and only if
\mu
is a
g_{\mathcal{V}}
-chain.

Proof.

Given
\{f_{n}\}
,
let
\rho^{(n)}_{k}(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
be as in Section
3
.
Note that

\rho_{0}^{(n)}(x,y)\rho_{0}^{(n)}(y,x)\leq\frac{\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,x\bigr)}{\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,y\bigr)}.

Then since
{\mathcal{V}}
is positive and continuous,
Proposition
3.1
(with
A={\mathbb{N}}^{-}
) implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\ \sup\,\Bigl\{\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,x\bigr)-\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,y\bigr)\,:\,x,y\in X,\,x_{{\mathbb{N}}^{-}}=y_{{\mathbb{N}}^{-}}\Bigr\}=0.

(4.2)

Given
x\in X
, consider the sequences

\sup_{y\in[x]_{{\mathbb{N}}^{-}}}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,y\bigr),\qquad\inf_{y\in[x]_{{\mathbb{N}}^{-}}}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,y\bigr)\qquad(n\geq 0),

which are respectively decreasing, increasing in

,
and hence convergent. It follows from (

) that

is well defined for all

.
Furthermore,

\sup_{x\in X}\left|g_{\mathcal{V}}(x)-\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,x\bigr)\right|\leq\sup_{x_{{\mathbb{N}}^{-}}=y_{{\mathbb{N}}^{-}}}\Bigl\{\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,x\bigr)-\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,y\bigr)\Bigr\},

and so the convergence is uniform.
Thus, the limit is continuous,
and it follows from Proposition
4.1
that
g_{\mathcal{V}}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{X}
,

g_{\mathcal{V}}
is positive,
and
\mu\in{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}})
if and only if
\mu
is a
g_{\mathcal{V}}
-chain.

Applying Proposition
3.1
again with
A=[-n,-1]

(and
\{f_{n}\}
as before) gives

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{[-n,0]}(g_{\mathcal{V}})

\displaystyle=\inf_{x_{A}=y_{A}}\ \lim_{m\to\infty}\ \frac{\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,m]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\big|\,x\bigr)}{\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,m]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\big|\,y\bigr)}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{R_{n}}{1+R_{n}}\right)^{2},

as required.
∎

Remark
.

Proposition
4.1
and
Theorem
4.2
can easily be generalised to include non-invariant specifications
{\mathcal{V}}
,
in which case
g_{\mathcal{V}}
is replaced by a sequence of continuous functions

\{g_{n}\}_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}
such that

Note that in general,

The following result will be useful for estimating the variation of
g_{\mathcal{V}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}

be a positive, continuous,
T
-invariant specification
with specification sequence
\{[0,n],f_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. If

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,n^{\alpha-1}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\sup_{j\geq 0}r_{(-\infty,i+j]}(f_{j})\leq K

(4.3)

for some
0<\alpha\leq 1,\,0<K<\infty
, then

g_{\mathcal{V}}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,x\bigr)

is well defined, and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,\frac{\log r_{[-n,0]}(g_{\mathcal{V}})}{\Bigl(\log\sup_{i\geq 0}r_{[-n,\infty)}(f_{i})\Bigr)^{\alpha}}\quad\leq\ 2\,\Gamma(\alpha)^{-1}K.

Proof.

Note that (
4.3
) implies

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\prod_{j=0}^{n}\inf_{i\geq 0}\bar{r}_{(-\infty,i+j]}(f_{i})=\infty,

and so it follows from Theorem
4.2

that
g_{\mathcal{V}}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{X}
is well defined and

\log r_{[-n,0]}(g_{\mathcal{V}})\leq 2\log\left(1+R_{n}^{-1}\right)\leq 2R_{n}^{-1}\qquad(n\geq 0),

(4.4)

where

R_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\inf_{i\geq 0}\bar{r}_{[-n,i+j]}(f_{i})

as before.
Note that

R_{n}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}t_{n}^{k},

where

a_{k}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\inf_{i\geq 0}\bar{r}_{(-\infty,i+j]}(f_{i}),\qquad t_{n}=\inf_{i\geq 0}\bar{r}_{[-n,\infty)}(f_{i}).

Note also that
a_{n}
is decreasing, and the power series

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}t^{k}
is covergent for
0\leq t<1
.
For such series, we have the following Tauberian theorem (see
[
8
, p447]
):
for
0<\alpha<\infty
,

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}t^{k}\ \sim\ (1-t)^{-\alpha}\quad\text{as }t\nearrow 1\iff a_{n}\ \sim\ \Gamma(\alpha)^{-1}n^{\alpha-1}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Thus, if

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,n^{\alpha-1}a_{n}^{-1}\leq K,

where
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<K<\infty
, then

\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}\left((1-t)^{\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}t^{k}\right)^{-1}\leq\Gamma(\alpha)^{-1}K,

and since
1-t\sim|\log t|
as
t\nearrow 1
,
and
t_{n}\nearrow 1
as
n\to\infty
, it follows from (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,\Bigl(\log\sup_{i\geq 0}r_{[-n,\infty)}(f_{i})\Bigr)^{-\alpha}\log r_{[-n,0]}(g_{\mathcal{V}})

\displaystyle=2\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,|\log t_{n}|^{-\alpha}\log r_{[-n,0]}(g_{\mathcal{V}})

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\Gamma(\alpha)^{-1}K,

as required.
∎

The next result follows from the results and techniques of
Johansson, Öberg and Pollicott in
[
15
]
,
although it is not formulated there; for an explicit proof, see
[
14
]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
g\in{\mathcal{G}}
be positive and continuous.
If

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=\lceil\lambda^{n-1}\rceil}^{\lceil\lambda^{n}\rceil}(\log r_{[-i,0]}(g_{\mathcal{V}}))^{2}=0

(4.5)

for some
\lambda>1
,
then there is a unique
g
-chain
\mu\in M(X)
, which is
T
-invariant and Bernoulli.

Note that if (
4.5
) holds for some
\lambda>1
,
then it holds for all such
\lambda
.

Since

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\ni 0\\

Theorem
1.2
includes potentials satisfying (
1.4

Comparison with (
1.5
) and (

in some cases, its hypotheses are weaker, but not always.
Note however that Theorem
1.2
guarantees a unique Gibbs state
(absence of symmetry breakdown), whereas (

of
T
-invariant Gibbs states, and (

By way of comparison,
consider the Ising model on
\{-1,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
with pair interactions

\Phi_{\{i,j\}}(x)=-\frac{\beta}{2}J(|i-j|)x_{i}x_{j},

(5.3)

where
\beta>0
(the inverse temperature) is a constant and

|J(n)|\leq n^{-\alpha}
for some
\alpha>1
.
Since Berbee’s result applies to non-invariant specifications
and does not depend on any group structure, there is no loss of generality
in assuming that
I={\mathbb{Z}}
and

\Lambda_{2k}=[-k,k],\qquad\Lambda_{2k+1}=[-k,k+1]\qquad(k\geq 0)

(as in (
1.7
)), other than the assumption that

|\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{n-1}|=1
.

The corresponding specification sequence
\{\Lambda_{n},f_{n}\}
is given by

f_{n}(x)=\exp\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|i_{n}-i_{n+j}|^{-\alpha}x_{i_{n}}x_{i_{n+j}}\right)\qquad(n\geq 0),

where
i_{n}
is the unique element of
\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{n-1}
.
Note that

\inf_{i\geq 0}\bar{r}_{\Lambda_{i+k}}(f_{i})=\bar{r}_{\Lambda_{k}}(f_{0})
,

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{\Lambda_{2k}}(f_{0})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-2\beta\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}j^{-\alpha}\right),

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{\Lambda_{2k+1}}(f_{0})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-2\beta\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}j^{-\alpha}+\beta(k+1)^{-\alpha}\right),

and

r_{(-\infty,n]}(f_{0})=r_{[-n,\infty)}(f_{0})=\exp\left(\beta\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}j^{-\alpha}\right).

Thus, for
\alpha>2
, Berbee’s condition and Theorem
1.1
both
give uniqueness and the Bernoulli condition for all
\beta
(as do the results in
[
6
]
and
[
19
]
),
whereas for
\alpha>2
, no conclusion can be drawn.
For the borderline case
\alpha=2
,
no conclusion can be drawn from the results in
[
6
]
and
[
19
]
,
but Berbee’s condition gives
uniqueness for
\beta\leq\frac{1}{4}
;
from Theorem
1.1
we get uniqueness and the Bernoulli property
for
\beta<\frac{1}{2}
.

Note that a better uniqueness result for the case
J(n)=n^{-2}
is obtained in
[

]
.
In this case, since

and as is well known, there is a critical inverse temperature

such that there is a unique Gibbs state when

,
but non-unique

-invariant Gibbs states for

It is shown in
[
2
]

In particular, there is a unique Gibbs state when

moreover, it follows from results in
[
12

that this Gibbs state is

-invariant and Bernoulli.

6.
Uniqueness without the Bernoulli property

A recent result of
Berger, Conache, Johansson and Öberg
[
4
]

proves uniqueness of the
g
-measure for

such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,n^{1/2}\log r_{[-n,0]}(g)\,<\,2.

(6.1)

Using this result instead of Theorem
4.4
gives uniqueness of the
T
-invariant Gibbs state
under hypotheses which are slightly weaker in some cases than those of Theorem
5.1
,
at the expense of the Bernoulli property.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}=\{\phi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}

be a positive, continuous,
T
-invariant specification on
S^{\mathbb{Z}}

with specification sequence
\{[0,n],f_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
If, for some
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
K>0
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,n^{\alpha-1}\,\prod_{i=0}^{n}\sup_{j\geq 0}r_{(-\infty,i+j]}(f_{j})\leq K,

(6.2)

and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,n^{1/2}\Bigl(\log\sup_{j\geq 0}r_{[-i,\infty)}(f_{j})\Bigr)^{\alpha}\,<\,K^{-1}\Gamma(\alpha),

(6.3)

then
{\mathcal{V}}
has a unique
T
-invariant Gibbs state.

Proof.

It follows from (
6.2
) and Theorem
4.2
that
any Gibbs state for
{\mathcal{V}}
is a
g_{\mathcal{V}}
-chain, where

g_{\mathcal{V}}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi^{\mathcal{V}}_{[0,n]}\bigl(\,[x]_{\{0\}}\,\big|\,x\bigr),

and from Lemma
4.3
that
g_{\mathcal{V}}
satisfies (
6.1
) provided (
6.3
) holds;
thus the result follows.
∎

If the specification is described in terms of an interaction potential

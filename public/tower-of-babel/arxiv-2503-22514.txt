Matroid polytopes with small rank
Masato Konoike, Koji Matsushita

Abstract
. For a lattice polytope P P , the rank of P P is defined by F − ( dim P + 1 ) F-(\dim P+1) , where F F is the number of facets of P P . In this paper, we study matroid polytopes with small rank. More precisely, we characterize matroid independence polytopes and graphic matroid base polytopes with rank at most three. Furthermore, using this characterization, we investigate their relationships with order polytopes, stable set polytopes, and edge polytopes.

1.
Introduction

Matroid independence polytopes (resp. matroid base polytopes) are defined as the convex hull of the indicator vectors of all independent sets (resp. bases) of matroids.
These polytopes have been studied from various perspectives, including combinatorics, commutative algebra, and algebraic geometry.
For example, normality (
[
10
,
26
]
), defining ideals of toric rings (
[
3
,
17
,
27
]
), Gorensteinness (
[
11
,
16
,
18
]
) and nearly Gorensteinness (
[
9
]
) of matroid polytopes have been investigated.
Moreover, the relationships between them and other classes, such as order polytopes and stable set polytopes, are also studied (
[
1
,
2
]
).
In this paper, we focus on matroid polytopes with small rank and analyze their properties and their relationships with other polytopes.

For a lattice polytope
P
with
\dim P\geq 1
, the
rank
of
P
is defined by

\operatorname{rank}P:=F-(\dim P+1),

where
F
denotes the number of facets of
P
.
Note that
\operatorname{rank}P
is a nonnegative integer.
The rank of
P
is a notable invariant due to its commutative ring-theoretic meaning;
\operatorname{rank}P
is equal to the rank of the divisor class group of the toric ring
\Bbbk[P]
of
P
, where
\Bbbk
is a field, if
\Bbbk[P]
is normal (cf.
[
24
, Theorem 9.8.19]
).
In
[
14
,
19
]
,
(0,1)
-polytopes have been investigated via their rank.
In particular, in the paper
[
14
]
, ordered polytopes, stable set polytopes, and edge polytopes with small rank have been classified up to unimodular equivalence by characterizing the underlying combinatorics objects.
Here, we say that two lattice polytopes
P,P^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
are
unimodularly equivalent

if there are a lattice vector
{\bf v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and a unimodular transformation
f\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
P^{\prime}=f(P)+{\bf v}
.
We write
P\cong P^{\prime}
if two lattice polytopes
P
and
P^{\prime}
are unimodularly equivalent.
It is known that if two lattice polytopes
P,P^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
have the integer decomposition property, then one has
P\cong P^{\prime}
if and only if
\Bbbk[P]
and
\Bbbk[P^{\prime}]
are isomorphic as
\Bbbk
-algebras (cf.
[
4
, Theorem 5.22]
).

The goal of this paper is to build on these works by classifying matroid polytopes with small rank (in other words, classifying the toric rings of matroid polytopes whose divisor class groups have small rank), and comparing them with other classes of polytopes.

For a graph
G
, let
M(G)
be the graphic matroid.
We characterize matroid independence polytopes and graphic matroid base polytopes with rank at most three as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.3
)
.

Let
M
be a connected matroid and let
P(M)
be the matroid independence polytope of
M
. Then we have
\operatorname{rank}P(M)=0
or
\operatorname{rank}P(M)\geq 3
. Moreover, we can see that

(1)

\operatorname{rank}P(M)=0
if and only if
P(M)\cong P(M(\mathcal{A}_{s}))
for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

(2)

\operatorname{rank}P(M)=3
if and only if
P(M)\cong P(M(\mathcal{D}_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}))
for some
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

In particular, if
\operatorname{rank}P(M)\leq 3
, then
P(M)
is a graphic matroid independence polytope.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
3.7
)
.

Let
M
be the graphic matroid of a 2-connected (not necessarily simple) graph, and let
B(M)
be the matroid base polytope of
M
.
Then we have the following:

(1)

\operatorname{rank}B(M)=0
if and only if
B(M)\cong B(M(\mathcal{A}_{s+1}))
for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

(2)

\operatorname{rank}B(M)=1
if and only if
B(M)\cong B(M(\mathcal{B}_{s,p}))
for some
s,p\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

(3)

\operatorname{rank}B(M)=2
if and only if
B(M)\cong B(M(\mathcal{B}_{s_{1},s_{2},p}))
for some
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(4)

\operatorname{rank}B(M)=3
if and only if
B(M)
is unimodularly equivalent to one of the following matroid base polytopes:

(i)

B(M(\mathcal{B}_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},p}))
for some
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
;

(ii)

B(M(\mathcal{C}_{s,t,p,q}))
for some
s,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
t,p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
;

(iii)

B(M(\mathcal{D}_{s_{1}+1,s_{2}+1,s_{3}+1}))
for some
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

See Section
2.2
for the definitions of the graphs
\mathcal{A}_{s}
,
\mathcal{B}_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},p}
,
\mathcal{C}_{s,t,p,q}
and
\mathcal{D}_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}
.

Moreover, we examine the relationships among the following five equivalent classes:

{\bf MI}_{n}:=\left\{\text{matroid independence polytopes with rank $n$}\right\}/\cong
;

{\bf GMB}_{n}:=\left\{\text{graphic matroid base polytopes with rank $n$}\right\}/\cong
;

{\bf Order}_{n}:=\left\{\text{order polytopes with rank $n$}\right\}/\cong
;

{\bf Stab}_{n}:=\left\{\text{stable set polytopes of perfect graphs with rank $n$}\right\}/\cong
;

{\bf Edge}_{n}:=\left\{\text{edge polytopes of graphs satisfying the odd cycle condition with rank $n$}\right\}/\cong
.

Theorem 1.3

(Propositions
4.4
,
4.5
and
4.8
)
.

We have the following:

(1)

{\bf MI}_{0}={\bf GMB}_{0}={\bf Stab}_{0}
.

(2)

{\bf MI}_{n}\subsetneq{\bf GMB}_{n}\subsetneq{\bf Stab}_{n}
for
n=1,2
.

(3)

For any
\mathcal{S}\in\left\{{\bf MI}_{2},{\bf GMB}_{2}\right\}
, there is no inclusion between
\mathcal{S}
and
{\bf Edge}_{2}
.

(4)

For any two distinct elements
\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}\in\{{\bf MI}_{3},{\bf GMB}_{3},{\bf Order}_{3},{\bf Stab}_{3},{\bf Edge}_{3}\}
, there is no inclusion between
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
.
In addition, we have
{\bf MI}_{3}\not\subset{\bf Order}_{3}\cup{\bf Stab}_{3}\cup{\bf Edge}_{3}\cup{\bf GMB}_{3}
and
{\bf GMB}_{3}\subsetneq{\bf Stab}_{3}\cup{\bf Edge}_{3}
.

The structure of this paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we prepare the materials required for later discussion.
We recall definitions and notation associated with (graphic) matroids and graphs.
In Section
3
, we characterize matroid independence polytopes and matroid base polytopes with rank at most 3, respectively.
In Section
4
, we study the relationships among
{\bf MI}_{n}
,
{\bf GMB}_{n}
,
{\bf Order}_{n}
,
{\bf Stab}_{n}
and
{\bf Edge}_{n}
in the case where
n\leq 3
.

2.
Preliminaries

The goal of this section is to prepare the required objects for discussions of our main results.

2.1.
Matroids and matroid polytopes

First, we recall matroids and their matroid polytopes (consult, e.g.,
[
21
]
for the introduction).
Throughout this subsection, let
M
be a matroid on the ground set
E
with the set of independent sets
\mathfrak{I}
, the set of bases
\mathfrak{B}
and the rank function
r:2^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

We introduce some fundamental objects associated with matroids.

•

For a subset
A\subset E
, let
\operatorname{cl}(A)
be the closure of
A
, that is,
\operatorname{cl}(A):=\{e\in E:r(A\cup\left\{e\right\})=r(A)\}
. We say that a subset
F\subset E
is a
flat
if
\operatorname{cl}(F)=F
.

•

A subset
A\subset E
is called
indecomposable
if
A
cannot be decomposed into proper subsets
A=A_{1}\sqcup A_{2}
such that
r(A)=r(A_{1})+r(A_{2})
.

•

A set
A
is called
connected
if every two elements of A belong to some circuit contained in
A
.

•

We say that
M
is
connected
if
E
is connected.
We can see that
M
is connected if and only if
E
is indecomposable if and only if
M
is not a direct sum of two or more nontrivial matroids.

•

We say that
A\subset E
is a
flacet
if
A
is a flat such that the restriction of
M
to
A
and the contraction of
A
in
M
are connected.

For a set
A
and its subset
S
, let
\chi_{S}
be the characteristic vector of
S
in
\mathbb{R}^{A}
, that is,
\chi_{S}(a)=1
if
a\in S
and
\chi_{S}(a)=0
otherwise.

We define two lattice polytopes associated with
M
:

P(M):=\operatorname{conv}(\left\{\chi_{I}:I\in\mathfrak{I}\right\})\quad\text{ and }\quad B(M):=\operatorname{conv}(\left\{\chi_{B}:B\in\mathfrak{B}\right\}).

We call
P(M)
(resp.
B(M)
) the
(matroid) independence polytope
(resp.
(matroid) base polytope
) of
M
.

We also define the
product
of two polytopes
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{e}
as

P\times Q=\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):\mathbf{x}\in P,\mathbf{y}\in Q\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+e}.

We can see that
P\times Q
is a polytope of dimension
\dim P+\dim Q
, whose nonempty faces are the products of nonempty faces (including itself) of
P
and
Q
.
In particular, the number of facets of
P\times Q
is equal to
F_{P}+F_{Q}
, where
F_{P}
and
F_{Q}
denote the numbers of facets of
P
and
Q
, respectively.
Therefore, we have
\operatorname{rank}P\times Q=\operatorname{rank}P+\operatorname{rank}Q+1
.

If
M
has two connected components
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, then we have
P(M)=P(M_{1})\times P(M_{2})
and
B(M)=B(M_{1})\times B(M_{2})
.
In particular, if
M
has a loop
e
, then
P(M)
(resp.
B(M)
) is unimodularly equivalent to
P(M\setminus e)
(resp.
B(M\setminus e)
).
Thus, in classifying matroid polytopes with small rank, it suffices to consider connected matroids.

Let
\mathfrak{B}^{\ast}:=\{E-B:B\in\mathfrak{B}\}
. Then
\mathfrak{B}^{\ast}
forms the set of bases of a matroid on
E
, called the
dual
matroid
M^{\ast}
of
M
.
We can see that
B(M^{\ast})=(1,\ldots,1)-B(M)
, and hence
B(M)
and
B(M^{\ast})
are unimodularly equivalent.

The dimension and facet descriptions of the independence polytope and base polytope of a matroid are already given as follows:

Lemma 2.1

(
[
22
, Theorem 40.5]
)
.

Suppose that
M
has no loops.
Then we have
\dim P(M)=|E|
and

\displaystyle P(M)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{E}\;:\;\begin{array}[]{rll}x(e)&\geq 0,&\text{ for every }e\in E\\
\sum_{e\in F}x(e)&\leq r(F),&\text{ for every indecomposable flat }F\end{array}\right\}.

In particular, each supporting hyperplane of the above half spaces defines a facet of
P(M)
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
8
,
15
]
)
.

Suppose that
M
is connected.
Then we have
\dim B(M)=|E|-1
and

\displaystyle B(M)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{E}\;:\;\begin{array}[]{rll}x(e)&\geq 0,&\text{ for every $e\in E$ such that }\\
&&\text{ $M\setminus e$ is connected,}\\
\sum_{e\in F}x(e)&\leq r(F),&\text{ for every flacet }\emptyset\neq F\subsetneq E\end{array}\right\}.

In particular, each supporting hyperplane of the above half spaces defines a facet of
B(M)
.

2.2.
Graphs and graphic matroids

Next, we recall graphs and their graphic matroids.
Throughout this subsection, let
G
be a finite undirected (not necessarily simple) graph on the vertex set
V(G)
with the edge set
E(G)
.

We use the following notation on graph theory:

•

For a subset
S\subset V(G)
, let
G[S]
denote the induced subgraph with respect to
S
and let
G\setminus S:=G[V(G)\setminus S]
(for a vertex
v\in V(G)
, we denote by
G\setminus v
instead of
G\setminus\{v\}
).

•

For a subset
S\subset E(G)
, let
G\setminus S
(resp.
G/S
) denote the graph obtained from
G
by removing (resp. contracting) the edges in
S
(for an edge
e\in E(G)
, we denote by
G\setminus e
(resp.
G/e
) instead of
G\setminus\{e\}
(resp.
G/\left\{e\right\}
)).

We can obtain a matroid from
G
called the
graphic matroid
(denoted by
M(G)
), which is a matroid on the set
E(G)
whose independent sets are the forests in
G
. A set
B\subset E(G)
is a basis of
M(G)
if and only if
B
is a spanning forest of
G
.
We can see that the graphic matroid
M(G)
is connected if and only if
G
is 2-connected, that is,
G\setminus v
is connected for any vertex
v\in V(G)
(in this paper, we consider the complete graph
K_{2}
on 2 vertices as a 2-connected graph).

It is well known that
G
is 2-connected if and only if
G
has an (open) ear decomposition (see
[
7
, Proposition 3.1.1]
), that is,
G
can be decomposed as
K_{2}\cup P_{1}\cup\cdots\cup P_{r}
, where
P_{i}
is a path, and
(K_{2}\cup P_{1}\cup\cdots\cup P_{i-1})\cap P_{i}
consists of the two end vertices of
P_{i}
for each
i
.

If
G
is a plane graph, we can form another plane graph called the
dual graph

G^{\ast}
. The vertices of
G^{\ast}
correspond to the faces of
G
, with each vertex being placed in the corresponding face. Every edge
e
of
G
gives rise to an edge of
G^{\ast}
joining the two faces of
G
that contain
e
.
If
G
is a planar graph, then
M(G^{\ast})\cong M^{\ast}(G)
holds, which implies that
\operatorname{rank}B(M(G^{\ast}))=\operatorname{rank}B(M(G))
.

In what follows, we assume that
G
is 2-connected.
An edge
e\in E(G)
is said to be
contractible
(resp.
deletable
) if
G/e
(resp.
G\setminus e
) is also 2-connected.
It is known that
G\setminus e
or
G/e
is 2-connected, that is,
e
is deletable or contractible (
[
21
, Theorem 4.3.1]
).
Moreover, we can see that
F\subset E(G)
is a flacet of
M(G)
if and only if there exists a 2-connected induced subgraph
H
of
G
with
E(H)=F
such that
G/F
is 2-connected.
We call such a subgraph
H
a
flacet
of
G
.
In particular, if an edge
e\in E(G)
is contractible, then
\left\{e\right\}
is a flacet of
G
.
Therefore, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that the deletable edge
e\in E(G)
(resp. the flacet
H
of
G
) one-to-one corresponds to the facet defining inequality
x(e)\geq 0
(resp.
\sum_{e\in E(H)}x(e)\leq r(E(H))
).

3.
Classification of matroid polytopes with small rank

In this section, we classify independence polytopes and base polytopes with rank at most 3, respectively.

3.1.
Independence polytopes with small rank

Let
{\bf MI}_{n}
denote the sets of unimodular equivalence classes of matroid independence polytopes with rank
n
.
Let
M
be a connected matroid on a ground set
E
.
We set
\mathcal{F}_{M}:=\{F\subset E:F\text{ is a flat of $M$ with }r(F)=1\}
.
This is called a
parallel class
and is a generalization of multiedges in graphs to the case of matroids.
The following lemma establishes some fundamental properties of
\mathcal{F}_{M}
:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a connected matroid (especially,
M
has no loops). Then we have the following:

(1)

For any
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
, if
F\cap F^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
then
F=F^{\prime}
.

(2)

\bigsqcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{M}}F=E
.

(3)

One has
|\mathcal{F}_{M}|\geq 1
. Moreover, the following are equivalent:

•

|\mathcal{F}_{M}|=1
;

•

\mathcal{F}_{M}=\{E\}
;

•

M=U_{1,|E|}
where
U_{r,n}
denotes the uniform matroid on
n
with rank
r
.

Proof.

(1)

Suppose that
F\cap F^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
.
Note that the intersection of flats is also a flat.
Thus,
F\cap F^{\prime}
is a flat of
M
with
r(F\cap F^{\prime})=1
since
F\cap F^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
and
F\cap F^{\prime}\subset F
.
If
F\neq F^{\prime}
, then there exists an element
e\in(F\cup F^{\prime})\setminus(F\cap F^{\prime})
and we may assume that
e\in F
. Then we have
r((F\cap F^{\prime})\cup\{e\})=1
, which contradicts the fact that
F\cap F^{\prime}
is a flat. Therefore, we get
F=F^{\prime}
.

(2)

The inclusion
\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{M}}F\subset E
is trivial, so we prove that
\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{M}}F\supset E
.
For any
x\in E
, we have
r(\operatorname{cl}(x))=r(x)=1
since
M
has no loops.
Thus,
\operatorname{cl}(x)
is a flat of
M
with
r(\operatorname{cl}(x))=1
, which implies that
\operatorname{cl}(x)\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
.
Therefore, we have
\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{M}}F\supset\operatorname{cl}(x)\ni x
, and hence
\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{M}}F\supset E
.
From (1), we obtain
\bigsqcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{M}}F=E
.

(3)

From (2), we have
\bigsqcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{M}}F=E
, which implies that
|\mathcal{F}|\geq 1
, and
|\mathcal{F}_{M}|=1
if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{M}=\{E\}
. If
M=U_{1,|E|}
, then
\mathcal{F}_{M}=\{E\}
since
U_{1,|E|}
has only one flat
E
and satisfies
r(E)=1
. Conversely, assume that
\mathcal{F}_{M}=\{E\}
, we have
r(E)=1
. Since
M
is connected, it follows that
M=U_{1,|E|}
.

∎

The above lemma helps us to show the following first main theorem:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
M
be a connected matroid. Then we have the following:

(1)

P(M)\in{\bf MI}_{0}
if and only if
M
is the uniform matroid
U_{1,|E|}
.

(2)

P(M)\notin{\bf MI}_{1}\cup{\bf MI}_{2}
.

(3)

P(M)\in{\bf MI}_{3}
if and only if
M
is the graphic matroid
M(\mathcal{D}_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}})
for some
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, we can see that
P(M)\in{\bf MI}_{n}
if and only if the matroid
M
has exactly
n+1
indecomposable flats.
Moreover, each element
F\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
is an indecomposable flat, so we have
|\mathcal{F}_{M}|\leq n+1
.
Furthermore, since
E
is also an indecomposable flat of
M
, we have
2\leq|\mathcal{F}_{M}|\leq n
if
M\neq U_{1,|E|}
.

(1)

From Lemma
3.1
(2) and the assumption
P(M)\in{\bf MI}_{0}
, we have
|\mathcal{F}_{M}|=1
. Therefore, by Lemma
3.1
(2), we have that
P(M)\in{\bf MI}_{0}
if and only if
M=U_{1,|E|}
.

(2)

Suppose that
P(M)\in{\bf MI}_{1}
.
Then we have
M\neq U_{1,|E|}
and thus
2\leq|\mathcal{F}_{M}|\leq n=1
, which is impossible.
Therefore, we have
P(M)\notin{\bf MI}_{1}
.

Suppose that
P(M)\in{\bf MI}_{2}
.
As in (1), we have
2\leq|\mathcal{F}_{M}|\leq n=2
, i.e.,
|\mathcal{F}_{M}|=2
. Let, say,
\mathcal{F}_{M}=\left\{F_{1},F_{2}\right\}
.
From Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
3.1
(2), we have
\sum_{e\in F_{1}}x(e)\leq 1
and
\sum_{e\in F_{2}}x(e)\leq 1

By summing these inequalities, we obtain
\sum_{e\in E}x(e)\leq 2
. However, since
r(M)\geq 2
, this contradicts the fact that the inequality
\sum_{e\in E}x(e)\leq r(E)
defines a facet of
P(M)
. Therefore, we have
P(M)\notin{\bf MI}_{2}
.

(3)

Suppose that
P(M)\in{\bf MI}_{3}
then we have
2\leq|\mathcal{F}_{M}|\leq 3
.
If
|\mathcal{F}_{M}|=2
, or
|\mathcal{F}_{M}|=3
and
r(M)\geq 3
, then, for similar reasons as in (2),
\sum_{e\in E}x(e)\leq r(E)
does not define a facet of
P(M)
.
Assume that
|\mathcal{F}_{M}|=3
and
r(M)=2
.
Let, say,
\mathcal{F}_{M}=\{F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\}
.
We will prove that
\{a,b\}\subset E
is a base of
M
if and only if
\{a,b\}\not\subset F_{i}
for all
i=1,2,3
. Since
r(F_{i})=1
for
i=1,2,3
, any two-element subset of
F_{i}
cannot be a base. Therefore, if
\{a,b\}
is a base, it follows that
\{a,b\}\not\subset F_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
. Now, assume that
a\in F_{1},b\in F_{2}
, and
\{a,b\}
is not a base of
M
. Since
r(M)=2
and
\{a,b\}
is not a base of
M
, we have
\{a,b\}
is a circuit of
M
. Moreover, if
\{x,y\}\subset F_{1}
, then
\{x,y\}
is also a circuit of
M
. By the properties of circuits,
\{x,b\}
must also be a circuit of
M
. Since every two-element subset of
F_{1}\cup\{b\}
is a circuit, we have
r(F_{1}\cup\{b\})=1
, which contradicts the fact that
F_{1}
is a flat of
M
.
Therefore, we have
M=M(\mathcal{D}_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}})
where
s_{1}=|F_{1}|
,
s_{2}=|F_{2}|
and
s_{3}=|F_{3}|
.

∎

We can see that the graphic matroid
M(\mathcal{A}_{s})
coincides with
U_{1,s}
.
Thus, from the above theorem, we immediately obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
P
be the independence polytope of a matroid with
\operatorname{rank}P\leq 3
.
Then there exists a graph
G
with
P\cong P(M(G))
.

Remark 3.4
.

The above corollary is false when
\operatorname{rank}P\geq 4
.
In fact, letting
M:=U_{2,n}
with
n\geq 4
, then we can see that
\operatorname{rank}P(M)=n
.
If there exists a graph
G
with
P(M(G))\cong P(M)
, then
G
is a 2-connected graph on 3 vertices since
M
is a connected matroid with rank 2.
Thus, we have
G=\mathcal{D}_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}
for some
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
However, it follows from Theorem
3.2
that
\operatorname{rank}P(M(G))=3\neq\operatorname{rank}P(M)
, a contradiction.

3.2.
Base polytopes with small rank

Let
{\bf GMB}_{n}
denote the sets of unimodular equivalence classes of matroid base polytopes of graphic matroids with rank
n
.

We classify the base polytopes of graphic matroids with small rank by using the operation of adding paths to a cycle.
The following lemma shows that the classification of the base polytope of a graphic matroid in terms of rank can be reduced to the case of simple graphs:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G
be a 2-connected graph and
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained by adding one path
P
to
G
with
V(G)\cap V(P)=\{u,v\}
.
Then we have
\operatorname{rank}B(M(G))\leq\operatorname{rank}B(M(G^{\prime}))
.
In particular, when
\{u,v\}
is an edge of
G
and
P
has length 1, we have
\operatorname{rank}B(M(G^{\prime}))=\operatorname{rank}B(M(G))
if
\{u,v\}
is deletable in
G
, otherwise
\operatorname{rank}B(M(G^{\prime}))=\operatorname{rank}B(M(G))+1
.

Proof.

It is clear that a deletable edge in
G
is also deletable in
G^{\prime}
.
Let
H
be a flacet of
G
.
If
\{u,v\}\not\subset V(H)
, then
(G/E(H))\cup P
is 2-connected, which implies that
H
is a flacet of
G^{\prime}
.
On the other hand, if
\{u,v\}\subset V(H)
, then
H^{\prime}:=H\cup P
is 2-connected and
G^{\prime}/H^{\prime}=G/H
, which implies that
H^{\prime}
is a flacet of
G^{\prime}
. Therefore, since the number of flacets of
G^{\prime}
is at least that of
G
and every edge of
P
is contractible or deletable, we obtain
\operatorname{rank}B(M(G))\leq\operatorname{rank}B(M(G^{\prime}))
.

Assume that
P
has length 1 and let
H^{\prime\prime}
be a flacet of
G^{\prime}
.
It is clear that a deletable edge in
G^{\prime}
except for
P
and
\{u,v\}
is also deletable in
G
.
We assume that
\{u,v\}
is deletable in
G
. If
\{u,v\}\not\subset V(H^{\prime\prime})
, then
G/E(H^{\prime\prime})
is 2-connected, which implies that
H^{\prime\prime}
is a flacet of
G
. On the other hand, if
\{u,v\}\subset V(H^{\prime\prime})
, then
G/(E(H^{\prime\prime})\setminus P)
is 2-connected, which implies that
H^{\prime\prime}\setminus P
is a flacet of
G
. Therefore, we obtain
\operatorname{rank}B(M(G^{\prime}))\leq\operatorname{rank}B(M(G))
. Hence, we have
\operatorname{rank}B(M(G^{\prime}))=\operatorname{rank}B(M(G))
.

We assume that
\{u,v\}
is not deletable. Since
P
is a loop in
G^{\prime}/\{u,v\}
,
\{u,v\}
is not contractible in
G^{\prime}
. Similarly,
P
is also not contractible in
G^{\prime}
. Moreover, since
\{u,v\}
is contractible in
G
,
\{u,v\}\cup P
is a flacet in
G^{\prime}
. Therefore, we obtain
\operatorname{rank}B(M(G))+1\leq\operatorname{rank}B(M(G^{\prime}))
. Using a similar argument, we obtain
\operatorname{rank}B(M(G^{\prime}))-1\leq\operatorname{rank}B(M(G))
. Hence, we have
\operatorname{rank}B(M(G^{\prime}))=\operatorname{rank}B(M(G))+1
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
G
be a 2-connected simple graph. Then we have the following:

(1)

B(M(G))\in{\bf GMB}_{0}
if and only if
G=\mathcal{A}_{s+2}^{\ast}
for some

.

(2)

B(M(G))\not\in{\bf GMB}_{1}
.

(3)

for some

;

(ii)

Proof.

G

has an ear decomposition.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
G
be a 2-connected multigraph. Then we have the following:

(1)

B(M(G))\in{\bf GMB}_{0}
if and only if

is unimodularly equivalent to the matroid base polytope

.

B(M(G))

B(M(\mathcal{B}_{s,p}))
for some

.

(3)

B(M(G))\in{\bf GMB}_{2}
if and only if
B(M(G))
is unimodularly equivalent to the matroid base polytope
B(M(\mathcal{B}_{s_{1},s_{2},p}))
for some
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and

.

(4)

B(M(G))\in{\bf GMB}_{3}
if and only if
B(M(G))

B(M(\mathcal{B}_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},p}))
for some

and

;

(ii)

B(M(\mathcal{C}_{s,t,p,q}))
for some

and

;

,

.

,

4.1.
Order polytopes, stable set polytopes and edge polytopes

In this subsection, we recall order polytopes, stable set polytopes and edge polytopes.

Order polytopes
.
Let
\Pi

\preceq
.
For a subset

, we say that

poset ideal
of

For a subset
A\subset\Pi
, we call
A
an
antichain
of
\Pi
if
p\not\preceq q
and
q\not\preceq p
for any
p,q\in A
with
p\neq q
.
Note that
\emptyset
is regarded as a poset ideal and an antichain.

For a poset
\Pi:=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}\}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\Pi}:\;x(p_{i})\geq x(p_{j})\text{ if }p_{i}\preceq p_{j}\text{ in }\Pi,\;\;0\leq x(p_{i})\leq 1\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,d\}.

A convex polytope
\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}
is called the
order polytope
of
\Pi
.
It is known that the vertices of
\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}
one-to-one correspond to the poset ideals of
\Pi
, that is,
\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\Pi}:x\text{ is a vertex of }\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}\right\}=\left\{\chi_{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Pi}:I\text{ is a poset ideal of }\Pi\right\}
(
[
23
]
).
Moreover, the order polytope
\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}
has IDP (see
[
20
]
).

We also recall another polytope arising from
\Pi
, which is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Pi}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\Pi}:\;

\displaystyle x(p_{i})\geq 0\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,d,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}x(p_{i_{j}})\leq 1\text{ for }p_{i_{1}}\preceq\cdots\preceq p_{i_{k}}\text{ in }\Pi\}.

A convex polytope
\mathcal{C}_{\Pi}
is called the
chain polytope
of
\Pi
.
Similarly to order polytopes, it is known that the vertices of
\mathcal{C}_{\Pi}
one-to-one correspond to the antichains of
\Pi
, that is,
\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\Pi}:x\text{ is a vertex of }\mathcal{C}_{\Pi}\right\}=\left\{\chi_{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Pi}:A\text{ is an antichain of }\Pi\right\}
(
[
23
]
).

In general, the order polytope and the chain polytope of
\Pi
are not unimodularly equivalent,
but the following is known:

Theorem 4.1

(
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
\Pi
be a poset. Then
\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\Pi}
are unimodularly equivalent if and only if
\Pi
does not contain the X-shape subposet.

Here, the
X-shape poset
is the poset
\{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4},z_{5}\}
equipped with the partial orders
z_{1}\preceq z_{3}\preceq z_{4}
and
z_{2}\preceq z_{3}\preceq z_{5}
.

Stable set polytopes
.
Let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
V(G)
with the edge set
E(G)
.We say that
T\subset V(G)
is a
stable set
or an
independent set
(resp. a
clique
)
if
\{v,w\}\not\in E(G)
(resp.
\{v,w\}\in E(G)
) for any distinct vertices
v,w\in T
.
Note that the empty set and each singleton are regarded as independent sets.

We define a lattice polytope associated with a graph
G
as follows:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Stab}_{G}:=\operatorname{conv}(\{\chi_{T}:T\text{ is a stable set of }G\})\subset\mathbb{R}^{V(G)}.

We call
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}
the
stable set polytope
of
G
.

In this paper, we focus on the stable set polytopes of perfect graphs (we say that a graph
G
is
perfect
if for any induced subgraph
H
of
G
, the chromatic number of
H
is equal to the maximum size of cliques of
H
).
If
G
is perfect, then
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}
has IDP (see
[
20
]
) and the facets of
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}
are completely characterized (
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
).
More concretely, the facet defining inequalities of
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}
can be written as follows:

\begin{split}&x(i)\geq 0\;\;\text{ for each }i\in V(G);\\
&1-\sum_{i\in Q}x(i)\geq 0\;\;\text{ for each maximal clique }Q.\end{split}

From this description, we can see that the stable set polytope of a perfect graph
G
has rank 0 if and only if
G=K_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Moreover, for two graphs
G
and
G^{\prime}
, we can see that
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}\times\operatorname{Stab}_{G^{\prime}}
is unimodularly equivalent to
\operatorname{Stab}_{G\sqcup G^{\prime}}
by observing the stable sets of
G\sqcup G^{\prime}
, where
G\sqcup G^{\prime}
denotes the graph on the vertex set
V(G)\sqcup V(G^{\prime})
with the edge set
E(G)\sqcup E(G^{\prime})
.

Given a poset
\Pi
, we define the
comparability graph
of
\Pi
, denoted by
G(\Pi)
,
as a graph on the vertex set
V(G(\Pi))
with the edge set

E(G(\Pi))=\{\{p_{i},p_{j}\}:\text{$p_{i}$ and $p_{j}$ are comparable in $\Pi$}\}.

It is known that
G(\Pi)
is perfect for any
\Pi
(see, e.g.,
[
7
, Section 5.5]
).
Moreover, we see that
\mathcal{C}_{\Pi}=\operatorname{Stab}_{G(\Pi)}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the base polytope
B(M(\mathcal{B}_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},p}))
is unimodularly equivalent to the stable set polytope
\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},p}}
.

Proof.

For
i\in[1,n]
and
k_{i}\in[1,s_{i}+1]
, let
T_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}:=\left\{e_{1,k_{1}},\ldots,e_{n,k_{n}},\epsilon_{0},\ldots,\epsilon_{p}\right\}
.
In addition, let
E_{i}:=\left\{e_{i,1},\ldots,e_{i,s_{i}+1}\right\}
for each
i\in[1,n]
and let
E:=\left\{\epsilon_{0},\ldots,\epsilon_{p}\right\}
.
Then any spanning forest of
\mathcal{B}_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},p}
can be written as
T_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}\setminus\left\{e\right\}
for some
e\in T_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}
.
We set

f:=\pi_{\epsilon_{p}}\circ f_{E_{n},e_{n,s_{n}+1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{E_{1},e_{1,s_{1}+1}}\circ g_{E}.

Then we can see that

(4.1)

f(\chi_{T_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{i}}\setminus\left\{e\right\}})=\begin{cases}\chi_{\left\{e_{1,k_{1}},\ldots,\overset{i}{\check{e_{i,s_{i}+1}}},\ldots,e_{n,k_{n}}\right\}\setminus\left\{e_{1,s_{1}+1},\ldots,\widehat{e_{i,s_{i}+1}},\ldots,e_{n,s_{n}+1}\right\}}&\text{ if }e=e_{i,k_{i}}\text{ for some $i$}\\
\chi_{\left\{e_{1,k_{1}},\ldots,e_{n,k_{n}},\epsilon_{k}\right\}\setminus\left\{e_{1,s_{1}+1},\ldots,e_{n,s_{n}+1},\epsilon_{p}\right\}}&\text{ if }e=\epsilon_{k}\text{ for some $k$},\end{cases}

where
\widehat{\;}
indicates an element to be omitted.
On the other hand, we consider the bijection
\phi:E(\mathcal{B}_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},p})\setminus\left\{\epsilon_{p}\right\}\to V(G_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},p})
defined by the following correspondence:

\displaystyle e_{i,k_{i}}\mapsto v_{i,k_{i}}\quad(i\in[1,n]\text{ and }k_{i}\in[1,s_{i}]),

\displaystyle e_{i,s_{i}+1}\mapsto m_{i}\quad(i\in[1,n]\text{ and }k_{i}\in[1,s_{i}]),

\displaystyle\epsilon_{i}\mapsto u_{i+1}\quad(i\in[0,p-1]).

This bijection induces the one-to-one correspondence between the characteristic vectors described in (
4.1
) and the characteristic vectors of the stable sets of
G_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},p}
as follows:

\begin{split}&\chi_{\left\{e_{1,k_{1}},\ldots,\overset{i}{\check{e_{i,s_{i}+1}}},\ldots,e_{n,k_{n}}\right\}\setminus\left\{e_{1,s_{1}+1},\ldots,\widehat{e_{i,s_{i}+1}},\ldots,e_{n,s_{n}+1}\right\}}\mapsto\chi_{\left\{v_{1,k_{1}},\ldots,\overset{i}{\check{m_{i}}},\ldots,v_{n,k_{n}}\right\}\setminus\left\{m_{1},\ldots,\widehat{m_{i}},\ldots,m_{n}\right\}},\\
&\chi_{\left\{e_{1,k_{1}},\ldots,e_{n,k_{n}},\epsilon_{k}\right\}\setminus\left\{e_{1,s_{1}+1},\ldots,e_{n,s_{n}+1},\epsilon_{p}\right\}}\mapsto\begin{cases}\chi_{\left\{v_{1,k_{1}},\ldots,v_{n,k_{n}},u_{k+1}\right\}\setminus\left\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\right\}}&\text{ if }k\neq p\\
\chi_{\left\{v_{1,k_{1}},\ldots,v_{n,k_{n}}\right\}\setminus\left\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\right\}}&\text{ if }k=p,\end{cases}\end{split}

where we let
v_{i,s_{i}+1}=m_{i}
for each
i\in[1,n]
.
This shows that
B(M(\mathcal{B}_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},p}))
is unimodularly equivalent to
\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},p}}
.

∎

First, we discuss the relationship in the cases

Proposition 4.4
.

We have the following:

(1)

{\bf MI}_{0}={\bf GMB}_{0}={\bf Stab}_{0}
.

(2)

{\bf MI}_{1}\subsetneq{\bf GMB}_{1}\subsetneq{\bf Stab}_{1}
.

Proof.

(1) By Theorems
3.2
and
3.7
, any independence (resp. base) polytope with rank 0 is unimodularly equivalent to
P(M(\mathcal{A}_{s}))
(resp.
B(M(\mathcal{A}_{s+1}))
) for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Similarly, any stable set polytope with rank 0 is unimodularly equivalent to
\operatorname{Stab}_{K_{t}}
for some
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
We can see that

P(M(\mathcal{A}_{s}))=\pi_{e_{1}}(B(M(\mathcal{A}_{s+1})))=\mathcal{C}_{I_{s}}
for any
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
which implies that
{\bf MI}_{0}={\bf GMB}_{0}={\bf Stab}_{0}
.
(2) It follows from Theorems
3.2
and
3.7
that any independence (resp. base) polytope with rank 1 is unimodularly equivalent to
P(M(\mathcal{A}_{s_{1}}\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{s_{2}}))
(resp.
B(M(\mathcal{A}_{s_{1}+1}\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{s_{2}+1}))
or
B(M(\mathcal{B}_{s_{1},p}))
) for some
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
(resp.
s_{1},s_{2},p\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
).
As we observed in (1),
P(M(\mathcal{A}_{s_{1}}\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{s_{2}}))
,
B(M(\mathcal{A}_{s_{1}+1}\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{s_{2}+1}))
and
\operatorname{Stab}_{K_{s_{1}}\sqcup K_{s_{2}}}
are unimodularly equivalent.
Moreover, by Lemma
4.3
,
B(M(\mathcal{B}_{s_{1},p}))
and
\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{s_{1},p}}
are unimodularly equivalent for any

.
These imply that
{\bf MI}_{1}\subset{\bf GMB}_{1}\subset{\bf Stab}_{1}
.
We can see that
B(M(\mathcal{B}_{1,1}))\notin{\bf MI}_{1}
. Indeed, if there exist positive integers

and

.
Thus, we may assume that

and

, and
MAGMA
confirms that

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

(h(x))(e)=\begin{cases}x(e_{2,t+1})-\sum_{i\in E_{2}\setminus\left\{e_{2,t+1}\right\}}x(i)&\text{ if }e=e_{2,t+1}\\

Lemma 4.7
.

For
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the base polytope
B(M(\mathcal{D}_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}))
is unimodularly equivalent to the edge polytope
P_{K_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}}
.

Proof.

For each
i=1,2,3
, let
E_{i}:=\left\{e_{i,k}:k\in[1,s_{i}]\right\}
.
Then any spanning forest of
\mathcal{D}_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}
can be written as
\left\{e_{i,k},e_{j,l}\right\}
for some
1\leq i<j\leq 3
,
k\in[1,s_{i}]
and
l\in[1,s_{j}]
, which one-to-one corresponds to the edge
\left\{k,l\right\}
of
K_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}
where
k\in V_{i}
and

.
This shows that
B(M(\mathcal{D}_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}))
coincides with
P_{K_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}}
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

We have the following:

(1)

For any
\mathcal{S}\in\left\{{\bf Order}_{3},{\bf Stab}_{3},{\bf Edge}_{3},{\bf GMB}_{3}\right\}
, there is no inclusion between
\mathcal{S}
and
{\bf MI}_{3}
.
In particular, we have
{\bf MI}_{3}\not\subset{\bf Order}_{3}\cup{\bf Stab}_{3}\cup{\bf Edge}_{3}\cup{\bf GMB}_{3}
.

(2)

For any
\mathcal{T}\in\left\{{\bf Order}_{3},{\bf Stab}_{3},{\bf Edge}_{3},{\bf MI}_{3}\right\}
, there is no inclusion between
\mathcal{T}
and
{\bf GMB}_{3}
.
On the other hand, we have
{\bf GMB}_{3}\subsetneq{\bf Stab}_{3}\cup{\bf Edge}_{3}
.

Proof.

(1) We can see that
P(M(\mathcal{D}_{0,0,0}))\notin{\bf Order}_{3}\cup{\bf Stab}_{3}\cup{\bf Edge}_{3}\cup{\bf GMB}_{3}
.
In fact,
P(M(\mathcal{D}_{0,0,0}))
is a 3-dimensional lattice polytope with 7 vertices, but
MAGMA
confirms that there is no 3-dimensional polytope with 7 vertices which belongs to
{\bf Order}_{3}\cup{\bf Stab}_{3}\cup{\bf Edge}_{3}\cup{\bf GMB}_{3}
.

Let
\Pi:=\left\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}\right\}
be the poset equipped with the partial orders
p_{1}\prec p_{3}
,
p_{1}\prec p_{4}
,
p_{2}\prec p_{3}
and
p_{2}\prec p_{4}
.
By using
MAGMA
, we obtain
\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}\in{\bf Order}_{3}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}\in{\bf Stab}_{3}
from Theorem
4.1
.
If there exists a matroid
M
such that
P(M)
is unimodularly equivalent to
\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}
, then
|E(M)|=\dim\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}=4
.
We can check that the independence polytopes of such matroids are not unimodularly equivalent to
\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}
.

Let
G=K_{2,2,2}
. From
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
and Lemma
4.7
, we have
P_{G}\in{\bf Edge}_{3}
and
P_{G}\in{\bf GMB}_{3}
.
If there exists a matroid
M
such that
P(M)
is unimodularly equivalent to
P_{G}
, then
|E(M)|=\dim P_{G}=5
.

MAGMA
guarantees that the independence polytopes of such matroids are not unimodularly equivalent to
P_{G}
.
Thus, there is no inclusion between
\mathcal{S}
and
{\bf MI}_{3}
for any
\mathcal{S}\in\left\{{\bf Order}_{3},{\bf Stab}_{3},{\bf Edge}_{3},{\bf GMB}_{3}\right\}
.

(2) Let
\mathbb{T}:=\left\{{\bf Order}_{3},{\bf Stab}_{3},{\bf Edge}_{3},{\bf MI}_{3}\right\}
.
We already see in (1) that there is no inclusion between
{\bf GMB}_{3}
and
{\bf MI}_{3}
, and that
P_{K_{2,2,2}}\in{\bf GMB}_{3}
.
From
[
14
, Section 5.3]
, we have
P_{K_{2,2,2}}\notin{\bf Order}_{3}\cup{\bf Stab}_{3}
, so
{\bf GMB}_{3}\not\subset{\bf Order}_{3}\cup{\bf Stab}_{3}
.
Moreover, we can check that
B(M(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{4}\mathcal{A}_{2}))\notin{\bf Edge}_{3}
by focusing on the dimension of
B(M(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{4}\mathcal{A}_{2}))
and using
MAGMA
.
Thus, we have
{\bf GMB}_{3}\not\subset\mathcal{T}
for any
\mathcal{T}\in\mathbb{T}
.

Conversely, let
\Pi
be the poset defined in (1) above and let
G
be the graph on the vertex set
V(G)=\left\{1,2,3,4,5\right\}
with the edge set

E(G)=\left\{\left\{1,2\right\},\left\{2,3\right\},\left\{3,4\right\},\left\{4,5\right\},\left\{1,5\right\},\left\{1,3\right\},\left\{1,4\right\}\right\},

then we have
\mathcal{O}_{\Pi}\in\left({\bf Order}_{3}\cap{\bf Stab}_{3}\right)\setminus{\bf GMB}_{3}
and
P_{G}\in{\bf Edge}_{3}\setminus{\bf GMB}_{3}
. Their proofs can be performed in the similar way to the above discussions.
Therefore, we get
\mathcal{T}\not\subset{\bf GMB}_{3}
for any
\mathcal{T}\in\mathbb{T}
.

Finally, we show that
{\bf GMB}_{3}\subsetneq{\bf Stab}_{3}\cup{\bf Edge}_{3}
.
Let
M
be a graphic matroid with
\operatorname{rank}B(M)=3
.
If
M
has at least 2 connected components, then
B(M)\in{\bf Stab}_{3}
from Propositions
4.4
and
4.5
.
If
M

is unimodularly equivalent to a base polytope of the form (i), (ii) or (iii) in Theorem
3.7
(4).
By Lemmas
4.3
,
4.6
and

, we obtain

, and hence
{\bf GMB}_{3}\subsetneq{\bf Stab}_{3}\cup{\bf Edge}_{3}
.
∎

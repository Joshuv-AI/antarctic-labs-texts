Abstract
. The article introduces a novel technique for constructing DNA codes from skew cyclic codes over a non-chain extension of ℤ 4 . \mathbb{Z}_{4}. We discuss ( \textbaro , 𝔡 , γ ) (\text{\textbaro},\mathfrak{d},\gamma) -constacyclic codes over the ring ℜ = ℤ 4 + ω ​ ℤ 4 , ω 2 = ω , {\frakfamily R}=\mathbb{Z}_{4}+\omega\mathbb{Z}_{4},\omega^{2}=\omega, with an ℜ {\frakfamily R} -automorphism \textbaro and a \textbaro -derivation 𝔡 \mathfrak{d} over ℜ {\frakfamily R} . By determining the generators of these codes of any arbitrary length over ℜ {\frakfamily R} , we propose a construction on the ( \textbaro , 𝔡 , γ ) (\text{\textbaro},\mathfrak{d},\gamma) -constacyclic codes to generate additional classical codes with improved and new parameters. Further, we demonstrate the reversibility of these codes and investigate the necessary and sufficient criterion to derive reverse-complement codes. Moreover, we present another construction to generate DNA codes from these reversible codes. Finally, we provide numerous ( \textbaro , 𝔡 , γ ) (\text{\textbaro},\mathfrak{d},\gamma) constacyclic codes and applying the established results, we construct reversible and DNA codes. The parameters of these linear codes over ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} are better and optimal according to the available online database of the ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} codes.

Preliminaries

Throughout the article,
{\frakfamily R}
denotes the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{4}+\omega\mathbb{Z}_{4}=\{a+\omega b\mid a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}\}
where
\omega^{2}=\omega
. This ring
{\frakfamily R}
has total
9
ideals, all are principal with two maximal ideals
\langle 1+\omega\rangle
and
\langle 2+\omega\rangle
. Thus,
{\frakfamily R}
is a commutative semi-local, principal ideal ring. It is isomorphic to the quotient ring
\mathbb{Z}_{4}[\omega]/\langle\omega^{2}-\omega\rangle
.
Units of
{\frakfamily R}
are given by
{\frakfamily R}^{*}=\{a+\omega b\in{\frakfamily R}~|~a,a+b\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{*}\}
.
A linear code of length
n
over the ring
{\frakfamily R}
, say
{\frakfamily C}
, whose elements are identified as codewords in it, is characterized as an
{\frakfamily R}
-submodule of
{\frakfamily R}^{n}
.

The Lee weight over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
is a function that assigns a weight 0 to 0, 1 to 1 and 3, and 2 to 2.
As an extension, the Lee weight on
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}
is calculated by summing the Lee weights of its components. For the generalization of the concept of the Lee weight over
{\frakfamily R}
, we use a Gray map
\phi:{\frakfamily R}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{2}
given by

\phi(a+\omega b)=(a,a+b)

where
a
and
b
are in
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. Naturally,
\phi
is
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
-linear distance-preserving which can be generalized to
\Phi:{\frakfamily R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{2n}
componentwise.
The Lee weight for an element in
{\frakfamily R}
is given by the Lee weight of its
\phi
-image over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, i.e.,
w_{L}(a+\omega b)=w_{L}(\phi(a+\omega b))
and thus by extending componentwise, Lee weights in
{\frakfamily R}^{n}
can be determined. The Lee distance of two distinct elements of
{\frakfamily R}^{n}
is the Lee weight of their difference. The minimum of the Lee distances of distinct codewords in a code
{\frakfamily C}
is called the Lee distance of
{\frakfamily C}
.
If a code
{\frakfamily C}
has length
n
, size
4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}
,
k_{1},k_{2}
are non-negative integers and the minimum Lee distance
d_{L}
, then it is represented by
(n,4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}},d_{L})
. Alternatively, a code
{\frakfamily C}
over
{\frakfamily R}
can be parameterized as
[n,k_{1}+k_{2},d_{L}]
where
k_{1}+k_{2}
represents the dimension of
{\frakfamily C}
.

Definition 1
.

Suppose
{\frakfamily R}
is a finite ring with an automorphism
\textbaro
. An additive map
\mathfrak{d}:{\frakfamily R}\to{\frakfamily R}
is called a
\textbaro
-derivation if
\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily r}_{1}{\frakfamily r}_{2})=\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily r}_{1}){\frakfamily r}_{2}+\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily r}_{1})\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily r}_{2}),
for all
{\frakfamily r}_{1},{\frakfamily r}_{2}\in{\frakfamily R}.

Definition 2
.

Let
{\frakfamily R}
be a ring equipped with an automorphism
\textbaro
and a
\textbaro
-derivation
\mathfrak{d}
. Then

{\frakfamily R}[x;\text{\textbaro},\mathfrak{d}]=\{{\frakfamily r}_{0}+{\frakfamily r}_{1}x+\cdots+{\frakfamily r}_{n}x^{n}\mid{\frakfamily r}_{i}\in{\frakfamily R},0\leq i\leq n\}

forms a ring with the usual polynomial addition and the multiplication of polynomials defined under the rule
x{\frakfamily{\frakfamily r}}=\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily r})x+\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily r})
for all
{\frakfamily r}\in{\frakfamily R}
, known as a skew polynomial ring (SPR).

We begin by introducing an automorphism
\textbaro
of
{\frakfamily R}
to define the SPR
{\frakfamily R}[x;\text{\textbaro},\mathfrak{d}]
.
Define a map
\text{\textbaro}:{\frakfamily R}\rightarrow{\frakfamily R}
as

(1)

\displaystyle\text{\textbaro}(a+\omega b)=a+(1+3\omega)b,

where
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}.
Clearly,
\textbaro
is an automorphism of order
2
of
{\frakfamily R}
.

Again, we take a map
\mathfrak{d}:{\frakfamily R}\rightarrow{\frakfamily R}
given as

(2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}(a+\omega b)=\alpha\big(\text{\textbaro}(a+\omega b)-(a+\omega b)\big)=\alpha(1+2\omega)b

such that
\text{\textbaro}(\alpha)+\alpha=0.

Clearly, the map
\mathfrak{d}
on
{\frakfamily R}
, defined as above, is a
\textbaro
-derivation on it and the nonzero values of
\alpha
are
2,1+2\omega
and

.
We readily have the following results from the definitions of
\textbaro
and
\mathfrak{d}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
{\frakfamily r}
be an element in
{\frakfamily R}
. Then

(1)

\mathfrak{d}^{n}({\frakfamily r})=0
for
n>1
.

(2)

\mathfrak{d}(\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily r}))+\text{\textbaro}(\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily r}))=0
.

Clearly, the multiplication over
{\frakfamily R}
is noncommutative as
x{\frakfamily r}=\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily r})x+\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily r})\neq{\frakfamily r}x
. Therefore, we generalize it by the induction hypothesis, which is useful in the study of R-codes over
{\frakfamily R}.

Lemma 2
.

Let
{\frakfamily r}\in{\frakfamily R}
. Then

x^{n}{\frakfamily r}=\begin{cases}{\frakfamily r}x^{n},&\textit{if n is even,}\\
(\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily r})x+\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily r}))x^{n-1},&\textit{if n is odd.}\end{cases}

In particular,
x^{2}{\frakfamily r}={\frakfamily r}x^{2}
for all
{\frakfamily r}\in{\frakfamily R}
.

Proof.

Since
x{\frakfamily r}=\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily r})x+\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily r}),
by Lemma
1
, we get

(3)

\displaystyle x^{2}{\frakfamily r}=

\displaystyle x(\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily r})x+\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily r}))=\text{\textbaro}^{2}({\frakfamily r})x^{2}+\mathfrak{d}(\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily r}))x+\text{\textbaro}(\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily r}))x+\mathfrak{d}^{2}({\frakfamily r})={\frakfamily r}x^{2}.

Now, if
n
is even, from successive use of (
3
), we get

x^{n}{\frakfamily r}=x^{n-2}(x^{2}{\frakfamily r})=x^{n-2}({\frakfamily r}x^{2})=x^{n-4}(x^{2}{\frakfamily r})x^{2}=x^{n-4}{\frakfamily r}x^{4}=\cdots=x^{2}{\frakfamily r}x^{n-2}={\frakfamily r}x^{n}.

If
n
is odd,
n-1
is even and then

x^{n}{\frakfamily r}=x(x^{n-1}{\frakfamily r})=(x{\frakfamily r})x^{n-1}=(\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily r})x+\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily r}))x^{n-1}.

Hence the result.
∎

The class of CC codes is an impressive extension of cyclic codes which holds significant importance in coding theory. This section studies SCC codes with derivations over the ring

Definition 3
.

Let

\textbaro

\textbaro

\tau_{(\text{\textbaro},\mathfrak{d},\gamma)}\big(({\frakfamily c}_{0},{\frakfamily c}_{1},\dots,{\frakfamily c}_{n-1})\big)=\big(\gamma\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily c}_{n-1})+\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily c}_{0}),\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily c}_{0})+\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily c}_{1}),\dots,\text{\textbaro}({\frakfamily c}_{n-2})+\mathfrak{d}({\frakfamily c}_{n-1})\big),

for any

, i.e., if
\tau_{(\text{\textbaro},\mathfrak{d},\gamma)}\big({\frakfamily C}\big)={\frakfamily C}
. Specifically,
{\frakfamily C}
becomes

-cyclic or

We identify

, where

is a skew polynomial of degree

{\frakfamily c}(x)

Proof.

It follows a similar approach as given in Proposition 3 in

{\frakfamily C}
is an R-code and
1+x+\cdots+x^{n-1}\in{\frakfamily C}
. If
{\frakfamily c}=({\frakfamily c}_{0},{\frakfamily c}_{1},\dots,{\frakfamily c}_{n-1})
is a codeword in
{\frakfamily C},
i.e., the corresponding polynomial representation
{\frakfamily c}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{\frakfamily c}_{i}x^{i}\in{\frakfamily C},
then its reverse
{\frakfamily c}^{R}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{\frakfamily c}^{r}_{n-1-i}x^{i}\in{\frakfamily C}.
As
{\frakfamily C}
is a left
{\frakfamily R}
-submodule and
(1+x+\cdots+x^{n-1})\in{\frakfamily C}
,
(1+x+\cdots+x^{n-1})+3{\frakfamily c}^{R}(x)\in{\frakfamily C}.

Now,

\displaystyle(1+x+\cdots+x^{n-1})+3{\frakfamily c}^{R}(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}x^{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}3{\frakfamily c}^{r}_{n-1-i}x^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Big(1+3{\frakfamily c}^{r}_{n-1-i}\Big)x^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{\frakfamily c}^{rc}_{n-1-i}x^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frakfamily c}^{RC}(x).

Thus,
{\frakfamily c}^{RC}(x)\in{\frakfamily C}
and hence the code
{\frakfamily C}
is an RC-code.

Conversely, suppose
{\frakfamily C}
is an RC-code. Thus, if
{\frakfamily c}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{\frakfamily c}_{i}x^{i}\in{\frakfamily C},
we get
{\frakfamily c}^{RC}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{\frakfamily c}^{rc}_{n-1-i}x^{i}\in{\frakfamily C}.
Since the all-zero codeword
\boldsymbol{0}\in{\frakfamily C}
,
\boldsymbol{0}^{RC}=(1+x+\cdots+x^{n-1})\in{\frakfamily C}.

Again,
(1+x+\cdots+x^{n-1})+3{\frakfamily c}^{RC}(x)\in{\frakfamily C}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}x^{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}3{\frakfamily c}^{rc}_{n-1-i}x^{i}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Big(1+3{\frakfamily c}^{rc}_{n-1-i}\Big)x^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{\frakfamily c}^{r}_{n-1-i}x^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frakfamily c}^{R}(x).

Thus, the code
{\frakfamily C}
is an R-code.
∎

Now, we introduce a new construction to generate DNA codes from the codes obtained by Theorems
5
and
6
. This approach uses the sum of codes to extend an R-code onto DNA code. As linear codes of length
n
over
{\frakfamily R}
are characterized as submodules of
{\frakfamily R}^{n}
over
{\frakfamily R}
, the sum of two linear codes of the same length over a ring is defined as the sum of two submodules of a module. Clearly, the sum of two linear codes is again a linear code of the same length over the same ring.

Definition 5
.

Let
{\frakfamily C}_{1}
and
{\frakfamily C}_{2}
be two linear codes over
{\frakfamily R}
of the same length. The set

{\frakfamily C}_{1}+{\frakfamily C}_{2}=\{{\frakfamily c}_{1}+{\frakfamily c}_{2}~|~{\frakfamily c}_{1}\in{\frakfamily C}_{1},{\frakfamily c}_{2}\in{\frakfamily C}_{2}\}

is a linear code over
{\frakfamily R}
and is called the sum of codes
{\frakfamily C}_{1}
and
{\frakfamily C}_{2}
. It is the smallest
{\frakfamily R}
-submodule of
{\frakfamily R}^{n}
which contains both the submodules
{\frakfamily C}_{1}
and
{\frakfamily C}_{2}
of
{\frakfamily R}
-module
{\frakfamily R}^{n}
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
{\frakfamily C}_{i}
be
(\text{\textbaro},\mathfrak{d},\gamma_{i})
-CC codes of length
n
over
{\frakfamily R},
for
i=1,2.
If
\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{1})
and
\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{2})
are R-codes, then
\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{1})+\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{2})
is an R-code of length
2n
.

Proof.

Let
{\frakfamily C}_{1}
and
{\frakfamily C}_{2}
be
(\text{\textbaro},\mathfrak{d},\gamma_{1})
-CC and
(\text{\textbaro},\mathfrak{d},\gamma_{2})
-CC codes, respectively over
{\frakfamily R}
such that
\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{1})
and
\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{2})
are R-codes of length
2n
.
Suppose
{\frakfamily c}_{1}=({\frakfamily c}_{10},{\frakfamily c}_{11},\dots,{\frakfamily c}_{1(n-1)})
and
{\frakfamily c}_{2}=({\frakfamily c}_{20},{\frakfamily c}_{21},\dots,{\frakfamily c}_{2(n-1)})
, where
{\frakfamily c}_{ij}=a_{ij}+\omega b_{ij},

i=1,2
and
0\leq j\leq n-1
be two codewords in
{\frakfamily C}_{1}
and
{\frakfamily C}_{2}
, respectively. Then

\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{1})+\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{2})\in\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{1})+\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{2})
for
\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{1})\in\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{1})
and
\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{2})\in\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{2})
. Now we see

\displaystyle\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{1})+\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{2})=

\displaystyle\big(\phi({\frakfamily c}_{10}),\phi({\frakfamily c}_{11}),\dots,\phi({\frakfamily c}_{1(n-1)})\big)+\big(\phi({\frakfamily c}_{20}),\phi({\frakfamily c}_{21}),\dots,\phi({\frakfamily c}_{2(n-1)})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(a_{10},a_{10}+b_{10},a_{11},a_{11}+b_{11},\dots,a_{1(n-1)},a_{1(n-1)}+b_{1(n-1)}\big)

\displaystyle+\big(a_{20},a_{20}+b_{20},a_{21},a_{21}+b_{21},\dots,a_{2(n-1)},a_{2(n-1)}+b_{2(n-1)}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(a_{10}+a_{20},(a_{10}+b_{10})+(a_{20}+b_{20}),a_{11}+a_{21},(a_{11}+b_{11})

\displaystyle+(a_{21}+b_{21}),\dots,a_{1(n-1)}+a_{2(n-1)},(a_{1(n-1)}+b_{1(n-1)})

\displaystyle+(a_{2(n-1)}+b_{2(n-1)})\big).

The reverse of this codeword over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
is

\displaystyle(\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{1})+\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{2}))^{R}=

\displaystyle\big((a_{1(n-1)}+b_{1(n-1)})+(a_{2(n-1)}+b_{2(n-1)}),a_{1(n-1)}+a_{2(n-1)},

\displaystyle\dots,(a_{11}+b_{11})+(a_{21}+b_{21}),a_{11}+a_{21},(a_{10}+b_{10})

\displaystyle+(a_{20}+b_{20}),a_{10}+a_{20}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(a_{1(n-1)}+b_{1(n-1)},a_{1(n-1)},\dots,a_{11}+b_{11},a_{11},a_{10}+b_{10},a_{10}\big)

\displaystyle+\big(a_{2(n-1)}+b_{2(n-1)},a_{2(n-1)},\dots,a_{21}+b_{21},a_{21},a_{20}+b_{20},a_{20}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{1})^{R}+\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{2})^{R}.

Since
\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{1})
and
\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{2})
are R-codes,
\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{1})^{R}\in\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{1})
and
\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{2})^{R}\in\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{2})
. Thus
(\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{1})+\Phi({\frakfamily c}_{2}))^{R}\in\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{1})+\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{2})
and hence
\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{1})+\Phi({\frakfamily C}_{2})
is an R-code.
∎

Theorem 8
.

(Construction 2)

Let

6.
Conclusion

In this work, we have explored
(\text{\textbaro},\mathfrak{d},\gamma)
-CC codes over the ring
{\frakfamily R}
. We have determined the criteria for these codes to be reversible and reversible-complement. Further, we have shown that DNA codes can be constructed by extending the obtained R-codes over
{\frakfamily R}
. Here, we proposed an efficient method to obtain classical codes with the help of a generator matrix. Implementing the proposed constructions, we have produced many better and optimal linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
along with several DNA codes. These codes are presented at
[
31
]
with their corresponding generator matrices. It would be valuable to examine the experimental performance of these codes. Additionally, exploring different construction techniques to derive DNA codes from generalized skew cyclic codes remains an open problem for future research.

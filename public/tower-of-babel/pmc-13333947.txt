An intelligent transformer based framework for bilingual financial complaint classification
Authors: Parnika Jain, Srishti Tripathi, Tushar Garg, Naidila Sadashiv, Niranjan C. Kundur, Madhura Phadke, Mayur Gaikwad, Aniket K. Shahade
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The manual processing of large volumes of bilingual consumer complaint data poses significant operational challenges, leading to delayed resolution times and inefficient resource utilization. This study presents the development and evaluation of an intelligent automated system for classifying bilingual financial customer complaints. A comparative analysis was conducted using a balanced synthetic bilingual dataset of 25,000 records derived from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau database. Traditional machine learning approaches, including Logistic Regression and LightGBM, were implemented using TF-IDF feature representations. In addition, a pre trained multilingual transformer model XLM-RoBERTa was fine-tuned for the classification task. Experimental results demonstrate that the fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa model outperformed conventional models, achieving an overall accuracy of 88.38%. These findings highlight the effectiveness of contextualized multilingual transformer models in addressing complex bilingual natural language processing tasks, particularly in the domain of financial complaint classification.

Introduction

The rapid digitalization of financial services and government backed platforms has led to a significant increase in consumer complaints submitted through online channels1. Efficient grievance redressal has therefore become a critical operational requirement for organizations, directly influencing customer satisfaction, regulatory compliance, and institutional credibility2, 3. Nevertheless, manual triage and classification of high volume unstructured complaint narratives is time consuming and prone to errors4, 5. These challenges are further compounded in multilingual environments such as India, where complaints are commonly lodged in English and Hindi. The lack of scalable and language aware automated systems often leads to delayed resolution, unproductive distribution of resources, and uneven management of high priority complaints6, 7.

Natural Language Processing (NLP) methods of automated complaint classification are not novel topics of research. Classical methods of machine learning, which utilizes feature engineering algorithm like TF-IDF, and the classification algorithm like Logistic Regression and Support Vector Machines have shown competitive performance in text classification problems8, 9, 10, 11. Later on, deep learning systems, especially transformer models, have shown better performance because of their potential to model contextual and semantic ties in text12, 13, 14, 15. Regardless of these developments, the majority of the current literature concerns mainly monolingual data, i.e., mostly English, and lacks adequate coverage of the further complexity that comes with bilingual/multilingual complaint data16, 17, 18. In addition, no extensive comparative analyses comparing traditional machine learning models with modern multilingual transformers through a balanced bilingual input of exactly the same size restrict the applicability of existing solutions to the real world in linguistically diverse context19, 20.

To fill this gap, the current work suggests a comparative systematic analysis of bilingual consumer complaint classification with the help of the traditional techniques of machine learning and a fine-tuned multilingual transformer model. A balanced data set of bilingual data comprising of English complaint narratives and their Hindi translations is formed with the same category labels across languages. TF-IDF representations are used to train classical machine learning models, such as Logistic Regression and LightGBM, whereas the state of the art multilingual transformer XLM-RoBERTa is fine-tuned to learn cross lingual contextual dependencies6, 7, 21. This experimental design would allow a reasonable comparison of the performance of feature based and contextual representation learning methods in the same conditions to give information about their comparative advantages and disadvantages regarding bilingual NLP tasks.

The primary contributions of this paper are threefold. First, it presents a structured framework for bilingual consumer complaint classification using English Hindi data, addressing a notable gap in the existing NLP literature. Second, it provides a rigorous comparative evaluation of traditional machine learning models, ensemble methods, and a fine-tuned multilingual transformer on a balanced bilingual dataset. Third, it empirically demonstrates the superior capability of contextual multilingual models in capturing semantic nuances across languages when compared to TF-IDF based systems6, 14, 21.

Literature survey

A collection of prior research studies on the fields of consumer complaints and multilingual text analysis will be examined, with the intention of demonstrating the development of methodologies in the field, as well as illustrating the specific challenges tackled with this research. In the first area of examination, consumer complaint classification will be studied using natural language processing methods. The natural language processing field has developed from traditional feature based methods to using modern methodological approaches such as deep learning and large language models.

The second area is focused on classical machine learning methods for doing text classification, which although still commonly used today will remain solid baseline methods for evaluating future work. The next phase of the review will transition into areas of deep learning, more specifically transformer based architectures, with a focus on both the multilingual aspects of pretrained deep learning models and the advances being made in the field of multilingual NLP and bilingual NLP, including cross lingual transfer learning and standardized evaluation benchmarks. This systematic synthesis provides a means of defining the research context and will inform the modeling decisions made by the authors in relation to the comparative and bilingual frameworks employed in this study.

Consumer complaint classification using NLP

Automated analysis and categorization of consumer complaints have received more and more interest as digital platforms and massive repositories of grievances become established. The initial studies in this field mainly concerned consumer complaint behavior and the enhancement of manual complaint handling procedures. Due to the existence of large textual complaint data, recent research has embraced natural language processing approaches to automate the categorization of complaints and facilitate effective grievance redressal systems.

Complaint classification has been a situation in which machine learning and feature based NLP methods are actively studied. In a governmental context, the analysis of complaints has been examined while addressing various operational classification goals through feature based representations and classical classifiers3. Similarly, machine learning models based on TF-IDF have been used to classify customer feedback in the food industry, revealing that frequency based representations outperform word embedding models in domain specific and non English environments4. These studies demonstrate the usefulness of classical NLP pipelines but also reveal limitations in capturing semantic relationships and complex contextual dependencies.

Following the development of deep learning, the sequence based models started to improve the performance of the complaint classification. A machine learning LSTM based system was proposed to categorize consumer complaints and queries on online services automatically and eliminated the necessity of human participation in customer service procedures5. Based on this work, the comparison of deep learning models, including LSTM, BiLSTM, CNN, and transformer based embeddings revealed that learning contextual representations is much more effective at classification16. Even though these models improved sequential and semantic modeling, they were limited to the size of datasets and domain specificity.

Further research was done on mass and real world complaint handling systems. An online complaint classification framework based on the social media data and based on text analytics emphasized such practical aspects of its implementation as an interaction possibility and sentiment polarity1. The studies underline the increasing significance of automated complaint classification system within the real consumer service settings.

The availability of large language models has added to the research in the area of complaint classification. Comparative analysis of the zero shot and reasoning based LLMs on CFPB based consumer complaint detection proved both the possibilities and limitations of such models in domain specific financial text analysis2. Although LLMs are generally able to generalize without fine tuning on specific tasks, they are prone to multiple complaints falling into the same category and vocabulary domain issues.

The literature in general can be viewed as a gradual transition between feature based machine learning and deep learning and transformer based methods in consumer complaint classification. Nonetheless, the majority of the research is conducted in monolingual or in isolated modelling paradigms. Minimal focus has been laid on bilingual complaint classification and direct comparisons of the traditional machine learning, ensemble methods, and multilingual transformer fine tuning. The gaps will drive the current work, which gives a detailed comparative study of feature and contextual approach to the issue of bilingual consumer complaint classification.

Traditional machine learning methods for text classification

Automated text classification systems have a long history of being based on classical machine learning techniques. These methods normally use handcrafted feature representations such as bag of words (BoW), term frequency inverse document frequency (TF-IDF), and n grams, along with supervised classifiers including Naive Bayes, Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machines, and tree based models. Despite their simplicity, these approaches remain powerful baselines due to their interpretability, computational efficiency, and robustness across a wide range of text domains.

A number of reviews on systematic review of traditional machine learning techniques in text classification have been performed. As a comparative study of classical classifiers and deep learning models on both raw and preprocessed text data showed, the traditional methods can still compete in case the sui preprocessing and feature engineering are used8. This observation is significant with respect to the fact that it is usually important to compare modern models with well optimized classical baselines instead of viewing them as inferior.assuming their inferiority.

Large scale research also indicates that traditional machine learning methods remain topical. An in depth analysis of text classification systems that include statistical, machine learning, and deep learning models has highlighted that the traditional text classifier is still useful in moderate sized datasets, structured text, as well as in situations where computational efficiency and interpretability are needed9. In the same context, a survey of monitored machine learning approaches that concentrate on term weighting plans and the actions of the classifiers found TF-IDF based models as an effective option in supervised text grouping exercises11.

Empirical studies have also indicated in the recent past, the performance difference between traditional and representation learning based methods. An exact comparison of TF-IDF based classifiers and BERT revealed that although the transformer models tend to perform better, in some instances, the classical models are as competitive as the former and have benefits in terms of cost of training and ease of deployment17. The findings support the importance of the strong traditional base of evaluation of advanced models.

Application of transfer learning has also had an impact on comparative studies. In a comparison between TF-IDF based classifiers and fine-tuned transformer models, including BERT and Distil BERT, it was reported that there was consistent performance improvement with transfer learning on a variety of datasets18. Nevertheless, the research also indicated trade offs that exist with the greater computational complexity, indicating that the traditional machine learning methods could be also used as an alternative when resources are limited.

Other reviews conclude that conventional classifiers still have an important part to play in pipelines of text classification especially as baseline models to compare performances and validate methodologies10. On the whole, it can be stated that the literature confirms that deep learning and transformer based models have improved the sphere, and still, their classical machine learning models are also indispensable to conduct rigorous evaluation and comparative analysis.

Overall, current literature indicates that classical machine learning algorithms are still useful in text classification and can still be used to make valuable comparisons with the latest transfer learning technology. This viewpoint directly informs the experimental design of the current study that will have both classical classifiers and the current contextual models in order to have a balanced and holistic evaluation.

Deep learning and transformer based approaches

Recent advances in deep learning have significantly transformed natural language processing, with transformer based architectures emerging as the dominant paradigm for a wide range of NLP tasks. In contrast to classical sequence models, transformers are based on self attention mechanisms, which are effective in capturing long range dependencies and contextual relationships, which can be trained with large corpora and extrapolated to downstream tasks.

One of the most significant developments in transformer based NLP is the multilingual pretrained models. XLM RoBERTa, a large scale multilingual masked language model, trained on data of more than a hundred languages, has shown large improvements in performance over multilingual BERT in a variety of benchmarks, especially in low resource languages. These results clearly laid the basis of the application of multilingual transformers to cross linguistic, and bilingual text classification problems.

In addition to the basic models, the latest literature discussed the practical implementation of transformers in NLP systems. A review of applications of NLP based on transformers highlighted the generalizability of this technology to text based activities but also addressed the issues of computing cost, interpretability, and deployment12. These studies point to the fact that, though transformers have better representational abilities, efficiency and scalability limits would need to be taken into account when adopting them in practice.

Resource efficiency and models have become significant research issues. An overview of available computationally efficient transformer architectures compared their accuracy and efficiency and showed that optimization strategies were required to allow them to be deployed in resource constrained environments without significant performance loss22.

Some other research works are aimed at enhancing the concept knowledge on transformer architectures. A summary of the transformer models with its variants identified some of the architectural principles and refinements that have been prominent in fostering the recent developments13. Also, visualization methods were analyzed and showed how the maps of attention and neuron level interpretations have the potential to understand the transformer action and enhance models explainability29.

On the implementation front the adoption on standards has hastened the adoption of transformers. The existence of a single library with a large number of pretrained transformer models has made the process of fine tuning and deployment to downstream NLP tasks much easier in both academia and industry14.

Lastly, investigative research has examined knowledge of language contained in transformer models. A study of internal BERT representations indicated that each layer learns different amounts of syntactic and semantic information, which are useful in understanding the high empirical results of transformers in text classification and other NLP tasks15.

In general, it can be seen that the literature suggests that transformer based models have significant benefits over previous deep learning methods with contextualized representations and large scale pretraining. Specifically, multilingual transformers can offer a solid base of bilingual and cross lingual NLP applications. The above developments are very much encouraging to use transformer architectures in the current study and give a lead to the comparative analysis of contextual and feature based models.

Multilingual and bilingual NLP studies

The growing multilingual and bilingual nature of the textual information of the real world has fueled the research interest on multilingual and bilingual natural language processing. As opposed to monolingual systems, multilingual NLP systems need to overcome issues of cross lingual semantic alignment, paucity of labeled data on low resource languages, and linguistic heterogeneity. They are particularly acute in text classification where the supervised learning may require large language specific annotated datasets. Subsequently, the recent studies have been devoted to the development of cross lingual transfer learning, multilingual representation learning, and standardized evaluation benchmarks to aid the strong multilingual text classification.

A research direction of particular interest is universal cross lingual classification, in which models are trained to cross learn across languages with overlapping or dissimilar label space. Single architecture of cross lingual text classification through joint supervised training across languages showed better generalization and labeling coverage especially in low resource and unseen language conditions6. This paper brings into the limelight the possibility of coming up with language free classifiers that can be implemented under the linguistic barrier.

The practical issues in multilingual text classification have also been discussed. An empirical investigation of multilingual transformer based classification in different languages and tasks highlighted realistic industry conditions, such as the limited amount of labeled data7. It was found that such methods as domain adaptive pretraining and data augmentation can be of significant benefit without the need to acquire any extra labeled data, highlighting the relevance of multilingual transformers in practical NLP systems. Recent advancements have further enhanced multilingual representation learning by leveraging transformer based architectures that integrate information across multiple layers, thereby improving performance on low resource languages without relying on parallel data23.

The issue of low resource multilingual classification has been a major problem especially to morphologically rich and agglutinative languages. A cross lingual fine tuning model that is adapted to low resource conditions showed significant performance improvement on the datasets of languages like Uyghur, Kazakh, and Kyrgyz19. Recent studies have further extended this direction by incorporating multilingual transformer models such as XLM R with few shot learning strategies to improve performance in extremely low resource settings24. Those findings demonstrate that cross lingual pretrained models are efficient in addressing the problem of data scarcity in multilingual text classification.

Another significant research topic has also become multilingual NLP system evaluation. A paper that focused on the challenge of assessing multilingual models in all languages supported purported to apply a predictive strategy to gauge the performance of languages in which no evaluation data were available20. This paper raises current gaps in the multilingual assessment and promotes more multifaceted benchmarking approaches. To address this demand, a longer benchmark with some more tasks and languages was suggested to give more fined grained measurements of the cross lingual transfer performance21.

Previous works in the theory of multilingual representation learning give additional background to such developments. The significance of shared representation spaces in multilingual NLP was shown by one of the first large scale multilingual word embedding models that were trained on more than a hundred languages25. Later benchmarks like XNLI have been important in measuring cross lingual sentence representations with limited supervision26.

To conclude, the literature has shown significant improvement in multilingual and bilingual NLP caused by the developments in cross lingual representation learning, low resource adaptation, and standardized evaluation systems. These advancements are very much in favor of the text classification with the help of multilingual transformer based models which are built on self attention mechanisms27 and further enhanced through large scale pretrained multilingual representations28. It forms a firm base of bilingual and cross lingual experiments in the current study. This work presents the experimental structure for classifying bilingual consumer complaints. The procedure is systematic and it includes dataset building, bilingual preprocessing, feature engineering, model training and evaluation. The hypothesis is to facilitate a reliable and consistent comparison between conservative feature driven learning methods and a multilingual transformer tool with fine tuning in the same experiment conditions.

Research hypotheses

In order to inform the design and evaluation of the experiment, the following hypotheses are developed:

H1: Contextual multilingual representations XLM-RoBERTa will greatly perform better than the TF-IDF based models in bilingual complaint classification because they can capture the cross lingual semantic alignment.

H2: Ensemble learning of TF-IDF based models will enhance the classification performance through minimizing the variance between the models and complementing the decision boundaries across the classifiers.

H3: Indic representations of Hindi will be better in their ability to classify as compared to word representations because of the morphological richness and inflexiveness of the language. These hypotheses are empirically tested in comparative experiments using traditional machine learning models, ensemble models and transformer based architecture.

Dataset Description and Bilingual Corpus Construction

This research employs the Consumer Complaint Database issued by the U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) as the major source of data. The dataset has millions of anonymized histories of consumer complaints of financial products and services. Initial filtering eliminated records in which there is no written complaint narrative to maintain textual consistency. In order to facilitate controlled experimentation and prevent imbalance between classes, the categories with low sample numbers were dropped. As represented in Table 2 five most common categories of complaints were kept and equal number of samples randomly chosen in each category gave the well balanced data of 25,000 English complaints. As the CFPB dataset is initially monolingual English, a bilingual corpus was created by translating each complaint into Hindi with the help of the pretrained neural machine translation model. This method yields 25,000 parallel English-Hindi pairs of complaints, resulting in a total of 50,000 textual samples, 25,000 English and 25,000 Hindi The resulting dataset then contains properly mapped bilingual complaint texts having the same category labels. Each English-Hindi pair is treated as a single logical unit during data splitting and evaluation.

This method is popular in NLP studies of multilingual languages, and in studies where large scale multilingual annotated datasets are unavailable. Neural machine translation provides the possibility to retain the semantic content across languages, and the cross lingual model performance can be effectively assessed. Bilingual text Preprocessing

Given the linguistic differences between English and Hindi, language specific preprocessing pipelines were applied. For English complaints, preprocessing as per Fig. 2, included lowercasing, removal of non alphabetic characters, elimination of stopwords, and lemmatization. These steps reduce lexical sparsity while preserving semantic meaning, which is particularly beneficial for TF-IDF based representations. For Hindi complaints, preprocessing focused on isolating Devanagari script characters and removing non linguistic symbols and punctuation. A curated list of Hindi stopwords was applied. Lemmatization was not performed due to limited availability of reliable Hindi lemmatizers and the morphological richness of the language. This language aware preprocessing ensures that downstream models receive clean and linguistically meaningful representations without imposing uniform transformations across structurally different languages.

Transformer Based Representation

In the context of contextual modeling, XLM-RoBERTa which is a multilingual trans former was used, based on cross lingual representation learning techniques28 and built upon the transformer architecture introduced in27. Concatenation and tokenization of the bilingual text was done with the subword tokenizer of the model. XLM-RoBERTa was selected because of its large multilingual pretraining on over 100 languages and its ability to be successful in cross lingual tasks of understanding.

Classification Models

One of the problems was to evaluate a wide variety of classification models to present a detailed comparison that is also presented in Table 3. The traditional machine learning models incorporated Multinomial Naive Bayes, LightGBM26, and Random Forest. These models are powerful baselines and they are suitable with sparse TF-IDF representations. A voting based approach to ensemble classifier was built to combine the predictions of optimized baseline models. The ensemble learning was employed in order to take advantage of the complementary decision boundaries and enhance the robustness. XLM-RoBERTa was end to end fine-tuned on the bilingual dataset in order to model contextually. Training Strategy and Hyper parameters

Stratified splitting was performed to maintain class balance, dividing the dataset into training and testing sets in an 80:20 ratio with a fixed random seed of 42. The baseline comparisons were conducted using traditional machine learning models trained with standard optimization procedures without extensive hyperparameter tuning.

The XLM-RoBERTa large model was fine-tuned for 4 epochs using mixed precision training to reduce memory usage. A per device batch size of 8 was used along with gradient accumulation over 4 steps, resulting in an effective batch size of 32. Evaluation and model checkpointing were carried out at the end of each epoch, and the best performing model was selected based on validation accuracy. These settings were chosen to balance performance and computational feasibility.

Since the dataset is balanced, accuracy is a good primary measure, whereas precision, recall, and F1 score give information about the performance at the class level. The macro and weighted averages were evaluated to make fair judgement on categories.

Results and discussion

The experimental findings of the bilingual consumer complaint categorization system are presented, highlighting the observed patterns. The performance of traditional machine learning baselines, ensemble methods, and multilingual transformer models is evaluated using standard metrics. The discussion provides insights into model behavior, strengths, and limitations.

Experimental setup and evaluation overview

All the experiments were done in a GPU enabled cloud platform through Python based machine learning platforms. This was done by the use of fixed random seeds when splitting up datasets and training models to maintain reproducibility. Preprocessing of the datasets, feature settings, and model structures were maintained throughout the experiments in order to compare them directly.

Each of the models was tested on a held out test set of 20% of the balanced bilingual dataset. Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1 score were used to measure performance. As the dataset is class balanced, the macro averaged measures are also provided to ensure the same weight of all categories of complaints. The test split and evaluation protocol were maintained throughout the experiments to give evenness and reproducibility.

Ensemble model performance.

To evaluate the effectiveness of combining baseline classifiers, a voting based ensemble model was employed. The ensemble achieved an accuracy of 87.75% ; mean across 5 seeds: 87.69% ± 0.18, as shown in Table 5, representing an improvement of 0.57% over the best individual baseline model LightGBM: 87.18%, mean: 87.12% ± 0.15.

Although the ensemble consistently outperforms LightGBM, the performance gain is modest. This indicates that while ensemble learning can refine decision boundaries, it does not fully overcome the limitations of TF-IDF based representations in capturing deeper semantic information.

A paired Student’s t test conducted on the per run accuracy scores between XLM-RoBERTa and the ensemble yielded a p< 0.01, indicating that the performance improvement is statistically significant at the 99% confidence level. The low standard deviations across runs greater than or equals to 0.21% confirm that all models exhibit stable training behavior. These results affirm that while the absolute gain over the ensemble is modest 0.62% in mean accuracy, the improvement is consistent and not attributable to random chance.

While the absolute improvement of 0.62% in mean accuracy is modest, the low variance across runs standard deviation of 0.21% for XLM-RoBERTa vs. 0.18% for the ensemble and the consistent superiority of the transformer model across all five seeds rule out the possibility of the result being attributable to random noise. The practical significance of this improvement should be interpreted in the context of the task: in large scale grievance redressal systems processing millions of complaints annually, even a 0.6% accuracy gain translates to thousands of correctly classified complaints.

Feature based versus contextual modeling

Comparison of the result outcome implies that feature based and contextual modeling paradigms have underlying differences. The TF-IDF classifiers are founded on the frequencies of the surface level tokens and are appropriate when used with other powerful classifiers such as gradient boosted trees. However, they have low expressiveness particularly in a bilingual setup whereby cross language semantic equivalence could not be successfully modeled through the use of handcrafted features alone. XLM-RoBERTa on the other hand, includes multilingual pretraining, context based embeddings and can be more effective at capturing the cross lingual semantic relation and also long range dependencies. This aspect allows the transformer to be more liberalized concerning the complaint accounts that are composed in different languages and written with different styles that results into the improved performance.

Comparative analysis of learning strategies

Among the things that stand out in the comparative study is the fact that performance benefits achieved in ensemble learning are not transformative, but incremental. However, the ensemble is slightly more precise than the optimum baseline, indicating that in scenarios where the representation of the features remains constant, there are diminishing marginal returns to classifier aggregation. The process of transforming feature based modeling into contextual representation learning is more meaningful and productive when compared to it. It is an indicator that it is the representational learning that is significant as opposed to the complexity of the classifier to improve the performance of the bilingual text classification.

Practical implications of comparative findings

With regard to deployment, the relative performance depicts a trade off between the performance and classification between the two. The traditional TF-IDF models are more resource efficient and are quicker to learn, and can be deployed in low resource or lightweight environments. However, in case the system is required to be highly scalable in grievance redressal, and semantic accuracy and cross lingual robustness are the most important prerequisites, the transformer based models are more efficient, however, with higher computational requirements. Generally speaking, the comparative analysis confirms that the more conventional approaches to machine learning remain powerful references but that contextual multilingual transformers are better suited to the real life multilingual problem solving of NLP that implies the subtle semantic hints. It is also worth noting that while the transformer’s accuracy advantage over the ensemble is statistically significant, it is modest 0.62% absolute; therefore, the choice between these approaches should be guided by available computational resources and the specific performance requirements of the deployment environment.

Conclusion

This study presented a comprehensive evaluation of bilingual consumer complaint classification using traditional machine learning models, ensemble methods, and a fine-tuned multilingual transformer. By constructing a balanced English–Hindi complaint dataset and evaluating multiple modeling paradigms within a unified experimental framework, the work addressed a notable gap in existing NLP literature that predominantly focuses on monolingual settings. The experimental findings revealed that TF-IDF based machine learning models offer good and computationally efficient baselines to bilingual text classification, and gradient boosted models have the best baseline performance, and the ensemble learning only has a marginal improvement. These findings emphasize the fundamental weaknesses of frequency based representations on revealing more in depth cross lingual semantic relation. Conversely, the fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa model always performed better than all other feature based models because it had a higher accuracy and macro F1-score since it had the capacity to model contextual and multilingual semantic information gained in large scale pretraining. The results affirm that even though the traditional models are still applicable to light and resource intensive applications, multilingual transformer based architectures are better suited to the real world bilingual grievance redressal system in which the semantic accuracy and language generalization is of great importance. Future research can build on this framework to other Indian languages, research domain adaptive pretraining methods, explore compact transformer architectures to achieve higher efficiency, incorporate the explainability mechanisms to increase model transparency and reliability.

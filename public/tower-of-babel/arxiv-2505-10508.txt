Abstract
. In this paper, we study an interaction problem between an elastic plate and a compressible viscous fluid located between the rigid bottom z = 0 z=0 and the plate. First, by utilizing the vertical fluid dissipation, we show that ln ⁡ η ⁡ ( t ) ∈ L 1 \ln\eta(t)\in L^{1} for any t > 0 t>0 provided that ln ⁡ η 0 ∈ L 1 \ln\eta_{0}\in L^{1} , ensuring that additional plate contact can form only on a set of a measure zero. Then, by utilizing the expanding capability of compressible fluid pressure, we show that all contact has to detach in finite time provided that the source force acting onto the plate is not pushing down excessively. Finally, we show that contact at any point can be detached in any given time with a strong enough source force localized around that point which is pulling the plate up. This is the first result where detachment of contact is proven in fluid-structure interaction.

3.
Literature review and discussion

The fluid-structure interaction problems have been widely studied in the mathematical literature for more than two decades. Their motivation lies in various applications ranging from industry, aerodynamics, biomedicine and so on (see
[
4
,
27
]
and references therein). The existence theory for fluid-plate/shell interaction including incompressible fluids has been so far well-developed, starting from the work of H. Beirão da Veiga
[
3
]
and Grandmont et al
[
14
,
22
]
, continuing with the work of Lequerre
[
32
]
,
Lengeler and Růžička
[
31
]
, Muha and Čanić
[
10
,
11
]
, Muha and Schwarzacher
[
39
]
, and to more recent results concerning regularity and uniqueness of 2D/1D interaction system
[
5
,
7
,
42
]
, vectorial structures in 2D/1D case
[
29
,
16
]
and 3D/2D case
[
12
]
, and inviscid incompressible fluid and plate interaction
[
2
]
. The problem of compressible viscous fluids and plate/shell interaction has been studied far less. It was started by Breit and Schwarzacher
[
8
]
, followed by Mitra
[
37
]
, Maity, Roy and Takahashi
[
35
]
, Trifunović and Wang
[
46
]
, then involving heat conductivity in the fluid in
[
9
,
36
]
, and finally the problem where both fluid and plate conduct and exchange heat
[
34
]
.

The existence theory has been for most limited by occurrence of contact. Indeed, since contact induces fluid domain degeneration and irregularities, most of solutions constructed in literature fail to "survive" it and usually the solution is studied up to the first moment when it happens. There have been two main approaches in dealing with this issue. First one is showing that contact will not occur and then obtaining a global solution. This was done in the context of incompressible viscous fluid and rigid body interaction in
[
24
,
25
]
and then later for the interaction between incompressible viscous fluid and viscoelastic beam in
[
23
]
. The second approach is allowing for contact to happen but with test functions vanishing in the contact region, thus losing information about the post-contact dynamics. This has been the case in fluid and rigid body interaction (see for example
[
15
]
and the references therein) where this allows two outcomes – sticking to boundary or going away from it
[
43
]
. In the context of elastic structures and incompressible viscous fluids, first such result was in
[
13
]
where the solution was obtained as a limit of global ones constructed in
[
23
]
as viscosity in the beam vanishes. Later in
[
28
]
, a weak solution to the interaction between 3D viscoelastic body and a 3D compressible viscous fluid with allowed contact and pressure defects was constructed by penalization of interpenetration of the body and its contact with the rigid boundary.

While it has been shown that contact occurs in some results concerning rigid bodies
[
21
,
26
]
, there were no results that were able to answer what happens afterwards since the fluid tends to behave irregularly near the contact, making it difficult to preserve any information. The inequality
(
9
)
is the first one that stores crucial information as it holds even when there is contact. It is reminiscent of variational inequalities in the contact problems for plates
[
17
]
. First of all, it tells us about the boundedness of
\ln\eta
which comes from the vertical fluid dissipation and kinematic coupling. This bound in a sense is very weak, but it allows a large degree of freedom for the occurrence of contact, especially for the initial data. In particular, it is also weaker than the one on
\frac{1}{\eta}
obtained in the case of 2D incompressible viscous fluid interacting with 1D viscoelastic beam in
[
23
, Proposition 9]
. This is to be expected as here there is no viscoelasticity, the structure is 2D and the fluid is 3D and compressible, which all together drastically reduce the amount of regularity in our system. Second important information coming from the inequality
(
9
)
is about the total pressure exerted onto the plate, even in the region near the contact. Indeed, if
\rho
is regular enough, then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\frac{p(\rho)}{\eta}=\int_{\Gamma^{\eta}}p(\rho)-\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\partial_{z}p(\rho)\frac{z}{\eta}

where the last term is then expressed through other fluid terms and then dealt with. Since the total bending
\int_{\Gamma}\Delta^{2}\eta
vanishes due to periodic boundary conditions and since effects depending on fluid velocity dissipate through time, at large time we will have that
\rho,\eta
are close to a constant state so

\displaystyle\left(\frac{m}{|\Gamma|~\eta}\right)^{\gamma}\sim p(\rho)\sim-F,

where
m
is the mass of the fluid. Thus, if
F
is small then
\eta
has to be large and there cannot be any contact. In other words, the pressure will keep pushing up the plate until the fluid is rare enough to balance out with the small force
F
of course if
\int_{\Gamma}F<0
, otherwise the plate will go up indefinitely. Finally, let us also point out that the new estimate on
\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\eta}
in
L^{1}(Q_{T}^{\eta})
coming from the contact inequality
(
9
)
gives us certain better control of pressure near contact. In particular, it ensures that no concentrations (measures) occur throughout the convergence of approximate solutions as it was proven in Lemma
4.3
, even though the fluid domain near contact can be very irregular. In a sense, it tells us that plate regularizes the pressure near contact.

Let us point out some observations about other directions:

(1)

Adiabatic constant

\gamma>3
. This is by far the most restricting condition in the presented results and it fails to include most of the physically reasonable models which are in the range
\gamma\in(1,5/3]
. While the detachment of contact essentially relies on the strength of the outer force or on the boundedness of the average density from below (i.e. on fixed mass), the other terms on the right-hand side of
(
9
)
cannot be controlled without enough integrability of
\rho
. Thus, lowering
\gamma
remains an open problem;

(2)

Incompressible case
. While this case is easier to study in most of scenarios of fluid-structure interaction problems, the detachment of contact seems not to be one of them. The issue lies in the incompressible fluid pressure and its lack of a sign, regularity and its inability to do work (
p\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}
= 0). Moreover, the divergence-free condition on
\mathbf{u}
makes it impossible to decouple vertical and horizontal fluid effects, so obtaining the contact inequality
(
9
)
seems to be out of reach, as this inequality gives us information only about the vertical fluid effects applied onto the plate. This can also be seen by including the Mach number in our fluid system, which would then appear in the numerator in the right-hand side of
(
43
)
, giving us only non-negativity of
\eta
as Mach number goes to zero.

(3)

Bounded plate domain
. Let us point out that it is possible to prove Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.3
in the case where
\Gamma
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and the plate is fixed on the boundary
\partial\Gamma
at the height
H>0
, although with some additional technical steps in the existence proof in order to deal with the domain corners, i.e. regularization of domain should be performed. In that case, the contact inequality is then localized away from the boundary in the from of
(
37
)
, since the plate cannot have contact near the boundary
\partial\Gamma
due to uniform estimates. However, it seems that proving Theorem
2.2
in this case is not obvious, as the total bending on a bounded domain does not vanish, i.e.
\int_{\Gamma}\Delta^{2}\eta=\int_{\partial\Gamma}\nabla\Delta\eta\cdot\nu\neq 0
, where
\nu
is the outer normal vector on
\partial\Gamma
. As time goes to infinity, total bending will simply balance out the total pressure and outer force, so no new information is obtained.

(4)

Source forces
. (a) Let us first point out that the regularity of
F\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))
is not the only option for the existence, but it is the simplest one. Indeed since
\partial_{t}\eta\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{s}(\Gamma))
by the trace regularity given in Lemma
A.5
, it is also enough to assume that
F\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-s}(\Gamma))
for any
s\in(0,1/2)
. In fact, any option that allows us to control the term
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}F\partial_{t}\eta
in the energy inequality is enough, as it can be seen in
(
44
)
for example.

(b) Another source force acting onto the fluid of the form
\rho\mathbf{G}
can be added, with
\mathbf{G}
satisfying certain properties, however it was not included for the simplicity of the presentation.

4.
Construction of global weak solutions – proof of Theorem
2.1

The multi-layered construction in this section based on decoupling, domain extension and two-side penalization comes from
[
34
]
, so we omit some of the technical details and present the most important steps. The decoupling approach (Lie operator splitting) combined with time-discretization was first used in
[
10
]
where a 2D/1D incompressible viscous fluid and (visco)elastic beam interaction model was studied. It was later extended to a hybrid decoupling approximation scheme in
[
45
]
, where a nonlinear plate model was dealt with by using a time-continuous equation for the plate and stationary for the fluid, and then to fully time-continuous time-marching decoupling scheme which penalizes the kinematic coupling from both equations
[
46
]
, to study a nonlinear thermoelastic plate and a compressible viscous fluid interaction problem. Finally, this method was then combined with a domain extension approach developed in
[
18
]
to study an interaction problem between a heat-conducing compressible viscous fluid and a thermoelastic shell with heat exchange in
[
34
]
.

4.1.
The approximate problem

Fix

and let

be approximation parameters. Moreover, let

and

. The time interval is split into

and a time-marching approach is utilized (there is no discretization in time). The fluid domain is extended to a larger domain (see figure

\Omega_{M}:=\Gamma\times(0,M)
where

is approximated with
\chi_{\eta}^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u})
where
\varepsilon\leq\chi_{\eta}^{\varepsilon}\leq 1
is smooth and

(11)

\displaystyle\chi_{\eta}^{\varepsilon}(t,x,y,z):=\begin{cases}1,&0<z<\eta(t,x,y),\\
\varepsilon,&\eta(t,x,y)+\varepsilon<z<M.\end{cases}

Additional viscoelastic term

is added to the plate equation in order to ensure that density will vanish outside of

once we pass to the limit

. We approximate the initial displacement as

\displaystyle\eta_{0,\delta}:=\eta_{0}+\delta\geq\delta

and consequently

\displaystyle\rho_{0,\delta}\geq 0,~~\rho_{0,\delta}\not\equiv 0,~~\rho_{0,\delta}|_{\Omega^{M}\setminus\Omega^{\eta_{0,\delta}}}=0,~~\int_{\Omega_{M}}\rho_{0,\delta}^{\gamma}\leq c,

\displaystyle\rho_{0,\delta}\to\rho_{0}\text{ in }L^{\gamma}(\Omega_{M})~~\text{ and }~~|\{\rho_{0,\delta}<\rho_{0}\}|\to 0\text{ as }\delta\to 0,

\displaystyle(\rho\mathbf{u})_{0,\delta}=\begin{cases}(\rho\mathbf{u})_{0},&\text{ if }\rho_{0,\delta}\geq\rho_{0},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases},\quad\int_{\Omega_{M}}\frac{1}{\rho_{0,\delta}}|(\rho\mathbf{u})_{0,\delta}|^{2}\leq c.

Finally denote the translation in time by

\displaystyle T_{\Delta t}f(t):=\begin{cases}f(t-\Delta t),&\quad t\in[(n-1)\Delta t,n\Delta t],~n\geq 1,\\

Solving the approximate problems

To solve (SSP), first fix
t_{m}
as in
(
12

and set

and replace

in
(

. Now, the solution

can be obtained as a limit of solutions to the same problem where

is replaced with

for

and when the velocity is negative. Thus, it has a positive sign (it pushes up) and it is fully dissipative, so the inequality
(

can be easily obtained by simply multiplying the equation by

and integrating over

. By passing to the limit

and then

(

(see
[
40

for more details).

In order to solve (FSP), let
t_{m}
be as in
(
15
)
and set

We can now use the standard theory for compressible Navier-Stokes, since the problem is defined on a fixed domain and the only difference is the penalization term which is a lower order term (the approximation of the viscous term does not affect the proof in any way since it does not violate parabolicity). The fluid velocity is spanned in a finite Galerkin basis with

functions, the continuity equation is damped with

is added to the momentum equation to correct the energy. Finally, the pressure is regularized by adding a term

for

. The solution to such problem is obtained by Schauder’s fixed point theorem. Then, passing to the limit

[
19

or
[
41

.

in
(
24
)
we obtain that
\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}\leq C
and consequently the right-hand side of
(
24
)
is now uniformly bounded. Therefore, we have the following uniform estimates:

(25)

\displaystyle\esssup_{t\in(0,T)}\left(\int_{\Omega^{M}}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}(t)+\int_{\Omega^{M}}\rho^{\gamma}(t)\right)\leq C,

(26)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}^{M}}\mathbb{S}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u}+\varepsilon\int_{G_{T}}|\partial_{t}\nabla\eta|^{2}\leq C,

(27)

\displaystyle\esssup_{t\in(0,T)}\left(\int_{\Gamma}|\partial_{t}\eta|^{2}(t)+\int_{\Gamma}|\Delta\eta|^{2}(t)\right)\leq C,

(28)

\displaystyle\int_{G_{T}}|\mathbf{v}-\partial_{t}\eta\mathbf{e}_{z}|^{2}\leq C\Delta t.

This in particular implies

(29)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega^{M}))}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega^{M}))}\leq C(\varepsilon).

In order to estimate the maximum of
\eta
, let us first note that

\displaystyle|\eta(t,x)|^{2}\leq 2|\eta_{0,\delta}(x)|^{2}+2\left|\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\eta(\tau,x)d\tau\right|^{2}\leq 2|\eta_{0,\delta}(x)|^{2}+2t\int_{0}^{t}|\partial_{t}\eta(\tau,x)|^{2}d\tau,

so integrating over
\Gamma
and taking the supremum over
t
gives us

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}^{2}\leq 2\|\eta_{0,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+2T\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\eta_{0,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+2T^{2}\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+T^{2}).

Since
\eta
is space-periodic, one has

(30)

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Gamma)}\leq C(\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}+\|\Delta\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))})\leq C(1+T).

Therefore, the constant
M
which is the height of the extended domain can a priori be chosen for a fixed
T>0
so that, say,
M/2\geq\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Gamma)}
.

4.4.
Passing to the limit
\Delta t\to 0

Let us denote the solution obtained in the previous section as
(\rho_{\Delta t},\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t},\eta_{\Delta t})
which satisfy
\eqref{coupledback:cont},\eqref{coupledback:mom},\eqref{coupled:en:app}
and denote the following weak limits of a converging subsequence as
\Delta t\to 0
:

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\rho

\displaystyle\quad\text{ weakly* in }\quad L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega^{M})),

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ weakly in }\quad L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega^{M})),

\displaystyle\eta_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\eta

\displaystyle\quad\text{ weakly* in }\quad W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Gamma)).

First, let us point out that the last convergence implies

(31)

\displaystyle\eta_{\Delta t}\to\eta,\quad\text{ in }C^{0,\alpha}([0,T]\times\Gamma),

for any
\alpha\in(0,1/3)
, by
[
1
, Theorem 5.2]
. For all means and purposes, this implies a good enough convergence of the fluid domain throughout all the limits.

Now, due to estimate
(
28
)
, we obtain that
\partial_{t}\eta\mathbf{e}_{z}=\mathbf{v}=\gamma_{|\Gamma^{\eta}}\mathbf{u}
, so the kinematic coupling is satisfied and consequently

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\int_{G_{T}}\partial_{t}\eta_{\Delta t}\partial_{t}\psi-\varepsilon\int_{G_{T}}\frac{(\mathbf{v}_{\Delta t}-T_{\Delta t}\mathbf{v}_{\Delta t})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{z}}{\Delta t}\psi+(1-\varepsilon)\int_{\Gamma}v_{0}\psi

\displaystyle\to\int_{G_{T}}\partial_{t}\eta\partial_{t}\psi+\int_{\Gamma}v_{0}\psi

for every
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Gamma)
.

Next, the convergence of all fluid terms except the pressure follow by the standard arguments (see
[
19
, Section 3.6]
) which are localized away from the plate
\Gamma^{\eta}
as follows. First, noting that

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega^{M})),\quad\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{12\gamma}{6+2\gamma}}(\Omega^{M})),

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{\eta_{\Delta t},\varepsilon}(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t})\in L^{2}(Q_{T}^{M})

by
\eqref{uni1},\eqref{uni2},\eqref{bbu:eps}
, it is enough to identify these terms locally. Fix an open set
Q:=I\times O\subset Q_{T}^{M}
such that
I\subset(0,T)
and
O\subset\Omega^{M}\setminus\Gamma^{\eta}(t)
for every
t\in I
. Then, by
(
31
)
, there exists
\Delta t_{Q}
such that for every
\Delta t<\Delta t_{Q}
one has
O\subset\Omega^{M}\setminus\Gamma^{\eta_{\Delta t}}(t)
for every
t\in I
. Now, from the continuity equation
(
20
)
, one has

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{\Delta t}=-\nabla\cdot(\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t})\in L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(O))

so
\rho_{\Delta t}\in L^{\infty}(I;L^{\gamma}(O))\cap H^{1}(I;H^{-1}(O))\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(O))
, and since
\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{u}
weakly in
L^{2}(I;H^{1}(O))
, we conclude

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\rho\mathbf{u}\quad\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(I;L^{2}(O)).

Next, from the momentum equation
(
21
)
, we have
\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\in H^{1}(I;H^{-3}(O))
, while
\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(O))
so
\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\to\rho\mathbf{u}
in
L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(O))
by Aubin-Lions lemma. Once again, since
\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{u}
weakly in
L^{2}(I;H^{1}(O))
, one obtains

(32)

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\rho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}\quad\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(I;L^{\frac{12\gamma}{6+2\gamma}}(O)).

Since the viscous term is linear in
\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}
, its convergence is trivial so we therefore summarize the above arguments to conclude

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\int_{Q_{T}^{M}}\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\int_{Q_{T}^{M}}\rho\mathbf{u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi},~\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\int_{Q_{T}^{M}}\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}:\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\int_{Q_{T}^{M}}\rho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}:\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi},

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\int_{Q_{T}^{M}}\mathbb{S}_{\eta_{\Delta t},\varepsilon}(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\int_{Q_{T}^{M}}\mathbb{S}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi},

for every
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Next, since the pressure
\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}
is only bounded in
L^{1}
in space, we need to exclude the possibility of it converging to a measure. The following proof can be found in literature, however we present it here since it will be referred later in Lemma
4.3
when we show weak convergence of pressure even with contact.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists
\overline{p}\in L^{1}(Q_{T}^{\eta})
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\int_{Q_{T}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}}\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}\varphi\to\int_{Q_{T}^{\eta}}\overline{p}\varphi,

for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Proof.

The improved pressure estimates which in the standard framework on fixed domains hold up to boundary (see
[
19
, Section 2.2.5]
), in our scenario hold away from the
\Gamma^{\eta}
and need to be localized.

First, by
(
31
)
, for every open set
Q=I\times O\subset Q_{T}^{\eta}
, there is a
\Delta t_{Q}
such that for every
\Delta t<\Delta t_{Q}
we have
Q\subset Q_{T}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}
. Fix such
Q
and let
I^{\prime}\times O^{\prime}=:Q^{\prime}\supset Q
be an open set with the same properties so that both
Q,Q^{\prime}
are sufficiently regular. By testing the momentum equation
(
21
)
with
\psi\nabla\Delta_{O^{\prime}}^{-1}\rho_{\Delta t}
, where
\Delta_{O^{\prime}}^{-1}
is the inverse Laplacian on
O^{\prime}
with zero Dirichlet boundary data and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q^{\prime})
is non-negative and
\psi=1
on
Q
(see for example in
[
8
, Lemma 6.3]
), leads to

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\in L^{\gamma+1}(Q),

so
\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}\rightharpoonup\overline{p}
weakly in
L^{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma}}(Q)
.
Therefore, there exists a function
\overline{p}\in L^{1}(Q_{T}^{\eta})
defined locally as a weak limit of
\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}
. However, we still need to ensure there are no concentrations at
\partial\Omega^{\eta}
. This is done by adapting the arguments from
[
30
]
to our fluid-structure interaction setting, as in
[
8
, Lemma 6.4]
. Fix
0<\kappa<\delta/4
and let

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}(t,x,y,z):=\begin{cases}\frac{z-\eta_{\Delta t}(t,x,y)}{\kappa},&\text{ for }\eta_{\Delta t}(t,x,y)-\kappa<z<\eta_{\Delta t}(t,x,y),\\
\frac{\eta_{\Delta t}(t,x,y)-2z}{\eta_{\Delta t}(t,x,y)-2\kappa},&\text{ for }\kappa\leq z\leq\eta_{\Delta t}(t,x,y)-\kappa,\\
\frac{z}{\kappa},&\text{ for }0<z<\kappa,\\
0,&\text{ elsewhere on }Q_{T}^{M}.\end{cases}

Testing the momentum equation with
(\varphi_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}\mathbf{e}_{z},0)
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{\kappa}+\int_{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}\right)\frac{1}{\kappa}\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{T}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}}\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa})-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa})

\displaystyle=-\int_{Q_{T}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}}\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}-\int_{Q_{T}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}}(\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}+\int_{Q_{T}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}}\mathbb{S}_{\eta_{\Delta t},\varepsilon}(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega^{\eta_{\Delta t}}(T)}\rho_{\Delta t}(T)\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}(T)\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}(T)-\int_{\Omega^{\eta_{0,\delta}}}(\rho\mathbf{u})_{0,\delta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}(0)-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}).

Let us bound the right-hand side, starting with

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Q_{T}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}}\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho_{\Delta t}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega^{\eta_{\Delta t}}))}\|\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega^{\eta_{\Delta t}}))}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\underbrace{\left|\left|\int_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{\kappa}+\int_{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}\right)|\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}|^{2}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{\leq\frac{C}{\kappa}}\underbrace{\left|\left|\int_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{\kappa}+\int_{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}\right)1^{r}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{\frac{1}{r}}}_{\leq\kappa^{\frac{1}{r}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{\left|\left|\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}|\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}|^{2}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\left|\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}1^{r}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{\frac{1}{r}}}_{\leq C(\delta)}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{1-\frac{1}{r}}}+1\right),

where
r=(1-\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{2})^{-1}
. Next, one has

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Q_{T}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}}(\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho_{\Delta t}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega^{\eta_{\Delta t}}))}\|\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega^{\eta_{\Delta t}}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\underbrace{\left|\left|\int_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{\kappa}+\int_{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}\right)|\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}|^{q}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{\frac{1}{q}}}_{\leq\frac{C}{\kappa}}\underbrace{\left|\left|\int_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{\kappa}+\int_{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}\right)1^{r}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{\frac{1}{r}}}_{\leq\kappa^{\frac{1}{r}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{\left|\left|\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}|\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{\kappa}|^{q}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{\frac{1}{q}}\left|\left|\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}1^{r}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{\frac{1}{r}}}_{\leq C(\delta)}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{1-\frac{1}{r}}}+1\right),

where
q<\infty
and
1<r=(1-\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{2}{6}-\frac{1}{q})^{-1}
. As the other terms can be bounded in a simpler fashion, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{\kappa}+\int_{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}\right)\frac{1}{\kappa}\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}\leq C(\kappa^{-s}+1),\quad\text{for some }s<1,

where
C
does not depend on
\Delta t
, so

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{\kappa}+\int_{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}\right)\frac{1}{\kappa}\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}\leq C(\kappa^{-s+1}+\kappa).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\int_{Q_{T}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}}\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}\varphi

\displaystyle=\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}\varphi+\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{\kappa}+\int_{\eta_{\Delta t}-\kappa}^{\eta_{\Delta t}}\right)\rho_{\Delta t}^{\gamma}\varphi

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\eta}^{\eta-\kappa}\overline{p}\varphi+o(\kappa^{s^{\prime}}),

for some
s^{\prime}>0
. Passing to the limit
\kappa\to 0
, the proof is complete.

\displaystyle-\rho_{\delta}\mathbf{u}_{\delta}\cdot\partial_{t}\left(\frac{z\mathbf{e}_{z}}{\eta_{\delta}}\right)=\rho_{\delta}u_{\delta,3}z\frac{\partial_{t}\eta_{\delta}}{\eta_{\delta}^{2}}\rightharpoonup\rho u_{3}z\frac{\partial_{t}\eta}{\eta^{2}}\quad\text{ weakly in }L^{1}(Q).

The remaining convergences can be dealt with in a simpler fashion so we omit them. Thus, the proof of Theorem
2.1
is complete.

5.
Detachment of all contact by pressure - proof of Theorem
2.2

5.1.
Case
F\in L^{1}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Gamma))

Let
(\rho,\mathbf{u},\eta)
be a given weak solution in the sense of Definition
2.1
and let us assume that
\min_{(x,y)\in\Gamma}\eta(t,x,y)=0
for all
t
. First, note that for any function
f\geq 0
with
f\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
satisfies

(42)

\displaystyle\max_{x\in\Gamma}f(x)\leq\min_{x\in\Gamma}f(x)+\max_{x,y\in\Gamma,x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\left|\frac{\text{diam}(\Gamma)}{2}\right|^{\alpha}\leq\min_{x\in\Gamma}f(x)+C\|\Delta f\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

for any
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
. Therefore, since
\|\Delta\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}\leq C
, there is a constant
H>0
such that
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Gamma)}\leq H
, which combined with uniform estimates coming from the energy inequality
(
10
)
, one has by Lemma
A.4
and Lemma
A.1

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{p}(\Omega^{\eta}))}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega^{\eta}))}\leq C,\quad\text{for any }p\in[1,6),

where
C
only depends on
p,\Gamma
and initial energy. We will show that this will give us a contradiction.

Repeating the calculation as in
\eqref{bound1},\eqref{bound2},\eqref{bound3}
to bound the terms on the right-hand side of
(
9
)
and taking into consideration that we assumed
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Gamma)}\leq H
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\eta}\leq C(1+\sqrt{T})-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}F.

Now, in order to bound from below the left-hand side term, let us first calculate

\displaystyle m=\int_{\Omega^{\eta}(t)}\rho(t)\leq\left(\int_{\Omega^{\eta}(t)}1^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\left(\int_{\Omega^{\eta}(t)}\rho^{\gamma}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\leq\left(\int_{\Gamma}|\eta(t)|_{\infty}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\left(\int_{\Omega^{\eta}(t)}\rho^{\gamma}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=(|\Gamma|~|\eta(t)|_{\infty})^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\left(\int_{\Omega^{\eta}(t)}\rho^{\gamma}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}

for a.a.
t\in(0,T)
, where
|\eta(t)|_{\infty}:=\max_{(x,y)\in\Gamma}\eta(t,x,y)
, so

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\eta}(t)}\rho^{\gamma}(t)\geq\frac{m^{\gamma}}{(|\Gamma|~|\eta(t)|_{\infty})^{\gamma-1}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\eta}(t)}\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\eta}(t)\geq\frac{1}{|\eta(t)|_{\infty}}\int_{\Omega^{\eta}(t)}\rho^{\gamma}(t)\geq\frac{1}{|\eta(t)|_{\infty}}\frac{m^{\gamma}}{(|\Gamma|~|\eta(t)|_{\infty})^{\gamma-1}}=\frac{m^{\gamma}}{|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}|\eta(t)|_{\infty}^{\gamma}}.

Combining the above estimates, by integrating over
(0,T)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\max_{t\in(0,T)}|\eta(t)|_{\infty}^{\gamma}}\frac{Tm^{\gamma}}{|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}}=\frac{1}{\max_{t\in(0,T)}|\eta(t)|_{\infty}^{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{m^{\gamma}}{|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\frac{m^{\gamma}}{|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}|\eta(t)|_{\infty}^{\gamma}}\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\eta}(t)\leq C(1+\sqrt{T})-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}F,

so

(43)

\displaystyle\max_{t\in(0,T)}|\eta(t)|_{\infty}\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Tm^{\gamma}}{|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}C(1+\sqrt{T})-|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}F}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{m^{\gamma}}{|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}C\frac{(1+\sqrt{T})}{T}-|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}F}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\to\infty

as
T\to\infty
, which is a contradiction. Consequently, there is a
T>0
large enough such that
\eta(T)>0
. In particular, this implies that if we start from initial data with contact, at some point it will break.

Remark 5.1
.

Alternatively, we can avoid using the contradiction argument in this proof by tracking the dependence of the right-hand side of
(
9
)
on
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Gamma)}
. This additional dependence would then increase the power of
\max_{t\in(0,T)}|\eta(t)|_{\infty}
on the left-hand side of
(
43
)
, implying also that
|\eta(t)|_{\infty}\nearrow\infty
as
t\to\infty
since the force
F\in L^{1}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Gamma))
loses its strength over time. On the other hand, by using the contradiction argument, the amount of detail is greatly reduced.

5.2.
Case
F\in L^{1}(\Gamma)

Once again, let us assume that
\min_{(x,y)\in\Gamma}\eta(t,x,y)=0
for all
t>0
. Then, due to
(
42
)
we have
\|\eta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|\Delta\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}
. This means that we can obtain uniform estimates from the energy inequality
(
10
)
by first estimating

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}F\partial_{t}\eta=\int_{\Gamma}F\eta(t)-\underbrace{\int_{\Gamma}F\eta_{0}}_{:=C_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\|\eta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}-C_{0}

\displaystyle\leq C\|F\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\|\Delta\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}-C_{0}

(44)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{2}}{2}\|F\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma))}-C_{0}

and then absorbing
\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}
. Now, by assuming, say,
\|F\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\leq E_{0}
, where

\displaystyle E_{0}:=\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega^{\eta_{0}}}\left(\frac{1}{2\rho_{0}}|(\rho\mathbf{u})_{0}|^{2}+\frac{\rho_{0}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\right)}+\int_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{1}{2}|v_{0}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\Delta\eta_{0}|^{2}\right)

is the initial energy, we once again as in the previous case obtain
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Gamma)}\leq H
. Let us first point out that if
\int_{\Gamma}F\geq 0
, the contact will break unconditionally due to
(
43
)
. Therefore let us assume that
\int_{\Gamma}F<0
. Then, the same calculation gives us

\displaystyle\max_{t\in(0,T)}|\eta(t)|_{\infty}\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Tm^{\gamma}}{|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}C(1+\sqrt{T})-|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}F}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{m^{\gamma}}{|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}C\frac{(1+\sqrt{T})}{T}-|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}\int_{\Gamma}F}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\to\frac{m}{(-|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}\int_{\Gamma}F)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}}>H

as
T\to\infty
, provided that

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}F>-\frac{m^{\gamma}}{|\Gamma|^{\gamma-1}H^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|F\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\leq E_{0},

which is a contradiction, implying
\eta(T)>0
for some large enough
T
.

6.
Detachment of contact at a point by outer force - proof of Theorem
2.3

Denote
r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}
. For a given
\kappa>0
, we first introduce a function

\displaystyle f_{\kappa}(x,y):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\pi\kappa^{2}}(\kappa-r),&\quad\text{ for }r\leq\kappa,\\
0,&\quad\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

which satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{\kappa}=\int_{\{r\leq\kappa\}}f_{\kappa}=1.

Now, let
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\Gamma
and assume that
\eta(t,x_{0},y_{0})=0
for all
t\in(0,T)
and let
F=f_{\kappa}^{0}\sigma
for
\sigma,\kappa>0
with
f_{\kappa}^{0}(x,y):=f_{\kappa}(x-x_{0},y-y_{0})
. We calculate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}F\partial_{t}\eta=\int_{\Gamma}F\eta(t)-\int_{\Gamma}F\eta_{0}\leq\int_{\Gamma}F\eta(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\kappa}(x_{0},y_{0})}f_{\kappa}^{0}\sigma C\|\Delta\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\text{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),(x,y))^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=C\sigma\|\Delta\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\kappa}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}-\frac{r}{\kappa^{2}}\right)r^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=C\sigma\|\Delta\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\frac{2}{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)}\kappa^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta\eta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+2\sigma^{2}C^{2}\frac{1}{(\alpha+1)^{2}(\alpha+2)^{2}}\kappa^{2\alpha},

where
B_{\kappa}(x_{0},y_{0})=\{(x,y):\sqrt{(x-x_{0})^{2}+(y-y_{0})^{2}}\leq\kappa\}
,
C
only depends on
\Gamma
and
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, where we have used the inequality
(
42
)
. Therefore, by choosing, say,

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}=\frac{(\alpha+1)^{2}(\alpha+2)^{2}}{2C^{2}\kappa^{\alpha}},\quad\kappa<1,\quad\alpha=1/4,

the energy is uniformly bounded and
F
only depends on
\kappa
so we can it as denote
F_{\kappa}
. Note that however
\int_{\Gamma}F_{\kappa}\to\infty
as
\kappa\to 0
, which means that we can concentrate and increase the force near the point
(x_{0},y_{0})
without increasing the energy. This happens because we assumed that the plate is "stuck" at
(x_{0},y_{0})
, meaning that applying stronger and stronger force near this point will not be able to break the contact.

By estimating as in Section
4.5.1
, the contact inequality
(
9
)
for
t=T
gives us

\displaystyle 0<\int_{\Gamma}F_{\kappa}\leq\frac{C}{T}(1+\sqrt{T}).

However, since
\int_{\Gamma}F_{\kappa}\to\infty
as
\kappa\to 0
, we can choose
\kappa
small enough such that this inequality is violated implying contradiction and consequently the existence of a
t\in(0,T)
such that
\eta(t,x_{0},y_{0})>0
.

Appendix A
Some technical results

Some of the following lemmas can be found in the literature. However, we choose to rewrite them in order to keep track on the dependence of constants on
\eta
, since this will be important in the calculation and since in our case the domain
\Omega^{\eta}
can degenerate and even possibly consist of infinitely many disconnected components.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\eta\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
so that
\eta>0
a.e. on
\Gamma
and let
\mathbf{u}\in H_{0z}^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})
with
u_{1},u_{2}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})
. Then

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})}\leq C(1+\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)})\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u},

where
C
only depends on
\mu
.

Proof.

Due to
u_{1},u_{2}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})
, there is a sequence of
u_{1}^{\omega},u_{2}^{\omega}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega^{\eta})
such that
u_{1}^{\omega}\to u_{1}
and
u_{2}^{\omega}\to u_{2}
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})
as
\omega\to 0
. Therefore, noticing that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\partial_{z}u_{2}^{\omega}\partial_{y}u_{3}=\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\partial_{z}u_{3}\partial_{y}u_{2}^{\omega},\quad\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\partial_{z}u_{1}^{\omega}\partial_{x}u_{3}=\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\partial_{z}u_{3}\partial_{x}u_{1}^{\omega},

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\partial_{x}u_{2}^{\omega}\partial_{y}u_{1}^{\omega}=\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\partial_{x}u_{1}^{\omega}\partial_{y}u_{2}^{\omega},

and denoting
\mathbf{u}^{\omega}:=(u_{1}^{\omega},u_{2}^{\omega},u_{3})
, we can calculate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\mu\left(\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\omega}+\nabla^{\tau}\mathbf{u}^{\omega}-\frac{2}{3}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}^{\omega}\mathbb{I}\right):\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\omega}=\mu\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\omega}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\eta})}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{3}\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}^{\omega}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\eta})}^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\omega}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\eta})}^{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\omega}):\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\omega},

where
C
only depends on
\mu
, so by passing to the limit
\omega\to 0
the following holds

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{E}^{\eta})}^{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u}.

On the other hand, for a.a.
(x,y)\in\Gamma

\displaystyle|u_{i}(x,y,z)|^{2}=\left|\int_{0}^{z}\partial_{z}u_{i}(x,y,s)ds\right|^{2}\leq\left|\int_{0}^{\eta(x,y)}|\partial_{z}u_{i}(x,y,s)|ds\right|^{2}\leq|\eta|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\eta(x,y)}|\partial_{z}u_{i}(x,y,s)|^{2},

so integrating over
\Omega^{\eta}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\leq|\eta|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}|\partial_{z}\mathbf{u}|^{2}\leq C|\eta|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u}.

Combining the above inequalities, the proof is finished.
∎

Next, it will be useful to extend fluid velocity by zero to a larger connected domain:

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\eta\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
so that
\eta>0
a.e. on
\Gamma
and let
\mathbf{u}\in H_{0z}^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})
. Then, extension by zero of
\mathbf{u}
to
\Omega_{E}^{\eta}:=\Omega^{\eta}\cup\Gamma\times(-1,0)
defined as

\displaystyle e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]:=\begin{cases}\mathbf{u},&\text{ on }\Omega^{\eta},\\
0,&\text{ on }\Gamma\times(-1,0),\end{cases}

satisfies

\displaystyle|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})}=\|e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{E}^{\eta})}.

Proof.

Let
O\subset\Omega_{E}^{\eta}
be an open set and let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(O)
. Obviously, if
O\subset\Omega^{\eta}
then

\displaystyle\int_{O}\nabla e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]\varphi=\int_{O}\nabla\mathbf{u}\varphi

and if
O\subset\Gamma\times(-1,0)
then

\displaystyle\int_{O}\tilde{\nabla}\mathbf{u}\varphi=0.

If
O\cap\Gamma\times\{0\}\neq\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle-\int_{O}e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]\cdot\nabla\varphi=\int_{O\cap\{z>0\}}\nabla e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]\varphi+\underbrace{\int_{O\cap\{z<0\}}\nabla e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]\varphi}_{=0}-\int_{O\cap\Gamma\times\{0\}}\underbrace{\gamma_{|O\cap\{z=0\}}e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]}_{=\gamma_{|O\cap\{z=0\}}\mathbf{u}=0}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{z}\varphi

\displaystyle=\int_{O\cap\{z>0\}}\nabla e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]\varphi=\int_{O\cap\{z>0\}}\nabla\mathbf{u}\varphi,

so
\nabla e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]\in L^{2}(\Omega_{E}^{\eta})
and

\displaystyle\nabla e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]=\begin{cases}\nabla\mathbf{u},&\text{ on }\Omega^{\eta},\\
0,&\text{ on }\Gamma\times(-1,0).\end{cases}

The proof is thus finished.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\eta\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
so that
\eta>0
a.e. on
\Gamma
and let
\mathbf{u}\in H_{0z}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\gamma_{|\Gamma^{\eta}}\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{\eta}\leq\int_{\Omega^{\eta}}|\partial_{z}\mathbf{u}|^{2}.

Proof.

For a.a.
(x,y)\in\Gamma
we have
\eta(x,y)>0
and
\int_{0}^{\eta(x,y)}\partial_{z}\mathbf{u}(x,y,z)dz=\gamma_{|\Gamma^{\eta}}\mathbf{u}(x,y)
so we can estimate

\displaystyle|\gamma_{|\Gamma^{\eta}}\mathbf{u}(x,y)|\leq\left|\int_{0}^{\eta(x,y)}\partial_{z}\mathbf{u}(x,y,z)dz\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\eta(x,y)}|\partial_{z}e_{0}[\mathbf{u}](x,y,z)|^{2}dz\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{\eta(x,y)}1dz\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\eta(x,y)}|\partial_{z}e_{0}[\mathbf{u}](x,y,z)|^{2}dz\right)^{1/2}\sqrt{\eta(x,y)}

and thus

\displaystyle\frac{|\gamma_{|\Gamma^{\eta}}\mathbf{u}(x,y)|^{2}}{\eta(x,y)}\leq\int_{0}^{\eta(x,y)}|\partial_{z}\mathbf{u}(x,y,z)|^{2}dz.

Since this holds for a.a.
(x,y)\in\Gamma
, we can integrate over
\Gamma
gives us the desired conclusion.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\eta\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
so that
\eta>0
a.e. on
\Gamma
and let
\mathbf{u}\in H_{0z}^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\eta})}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})}.

for any
p\in[1,6)
, where
C=C(\|1+\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)},\|\Delta\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},\Gamma,p)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.2
, we can extend
\mathbf{u}
by zero to
\Omega_{E}^{\eta}:=\Omega^{\eta}\cup\Gamma\times(-1,0)
and denote by
e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]
, so we have
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})}=\|e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{E}^{\eta})}
. Then, we can map
e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]
onto
\Omega_{E}:=\Gamma\times(-1,0)
by coordinate transform

\displaystyle A_{\eta}:

\displaystyle\Omega_{E}\to\Omega_{E}^{\eta}

\displaystyle(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y,(z+1)\eta(x,y)+z).

Denoting
\hat{\mathbf{u}}(x,y,z):=e_{0}[\mathbf{u}](A_{\eta}(x,y,z))
, the Jacobain of transformation as
J:=1+\eta
and the gradient on
\Omega_{E}
as
\hat{\nabla}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{E}}|\hat{\nabla}\hat{\mathbf{u}}|^{q}\leq\int_{\Omega_{E}^{\eta}}\frac{1}{J}J^{q}|\nabla e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]|^{q}|\nabla\eta|^{q}\leq\|1+\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{q-1}\|\nabla e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{E}^{\eta})}^{q}\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{\frac{q^{2}}{2-q}(\Omega_{E}^{\eta})}}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C\underbrace{\|\nabla e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{E}^{\eta})}^{q}}_{=\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\eta})}^{q}}\|1+\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\frac{q^{2}-2q+2}{q}}\|\Delta\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{q},

for
q\in[1,2)
with
C=C(q,\Gamma)
, where we used

\displaystyle\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{\frac{q^{2}}{2-q}(\Omega_{E}^{\eta})}}=\left(\int_{\Omega_{E}^{\eta}}|\nabla\eta|^{\frac{q^{2}}{2-q}}\right)^{\frac{2-q}{q^{2}}}=\left((1+\eta)\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla\eta|^{\frac{q^{2}}{2-q}}\right)^{\frac{2-q}{q^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|1+\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\frac{2-q}{q^{2}}}\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{\frac{q^{2}}{2-q}(\Gamma)}}\leq C\|1+\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\frac{2-q}{q^{2}}}\|\Delta\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{E}}|\hat{\mathbf{u}}|^{q}=\int_{\Omega_{E}^{\eta}}\frac{1}{J}|e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]|^{q}\leq\int_{\Omega_{E}^{\eta}}|e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]|^{q},

so combining the above inequalities we have

(45)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega_{E})}\leq C\left(1+\|1+\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\frac{q^{2}-2q+2}{q^{2}}}\|\Delta\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\right)\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})}.

Finally, by Sobolev imbedding on
\Omega_{E}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega_{E}^{\eta}}|e_{0}[\mathbf{u}]|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\int_{\Omega_{E}}J|\hat{\mathbf{u}}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\|1+\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{E})}\leq C\|1+\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\frac{1}{p}}C\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega_{E})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|1+\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(1+(\|1+\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{p^{2}}})\|\Delta\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\right)\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\eta})}

for any
q\in[1,2)
and
p=\frac{3q}{3-q}\in[1,6)
, so the proof is finished.
∎

b=\sqrt{\eta}
. By partial integration

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\Gamma}\nabla\cdot(b\nabla b|\nabla b|^{2s-2})=\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla b|^{2s}+\int_{\Gamma}b\Delta b|\nabla b|^{2s-2}+\int_{\Gamma}b\nabla b\cdot\nabla(|\nabla b|^{2s-2}).

First we calculate

\displaystyle\nabla(|\nabla b|^{2s-2})=\nabla(|\nabla b|^{2})^{s-1}=(s-1)(|\nabla b|^{2})^{s-2}\nabla|\nabla b|^{2},

and

\displaystyle\nabla b\cdot\nabla|\nabla b|^{2}=2\begin{bmatrix}b_{x}\\
b_{y}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}b_{xx}b_{x}+b_{xy}b_{x}\\
b_{xy}b_{y}+b_{y}b_{yy}\end{bmatrix}=2|\nabla b|^{2}\Delta b+(4b_{x}b_{y}b_{xy}-2(b_{x})^{2}b_{yy}-2(b_{y})^{2}b_{xx}),

so

\displaystyle\nabla b\cdot\nabla(|\nabla b|^{2s-2})=(s-1)(|\nabla b|^{2})^{s-2}\left(2|\nabla b|^{2}\Delta b+(4b_{x}b_{y}b_{xy}-2(b_{x})^{2}b_{yy}-2(b_{y})^{2}b_{xx})\right).

Therefore

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla b|^{2s}+(2s-1)\int_{\Gamma}b\Delta b|\nabla b|^{2s-2}

\displaystyle+(2s-2)\int_{\Gamma}b\left(2b_{x}b_{y}b_{xy}-(b_{x})^{2}b_{yy}-(b_{y})^{2}b_{xx}\right)|\nabla b|^{2s-4}.

Expressing
b
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla b|^{2s}=\frac{1}{4^{s}}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{\eta^{s}}

then

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}b\Delta b|\nabla b|^{2s-2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\sqrt{\eta}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta\eta}{\sqrt{\eta}}-\frac{1}{4}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2}}{\eta^{3/2}}\right)\frac{1}{4^{s-1}}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s-2}}{\eta^{s-1}}=\frac{1}{2^{2s-1}}\int_{\Gamma}\Delta\eta\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{\eta^{s}}-\frac{1}{4^{s}}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{\eta^{s}}

and finally

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}b\left(2b_{x}b_{y}b_{xy}-(b_{x})^{2}b_{yy}-(b_{y})^{2}b_{xx}\right)|\nabla b|^{2s-4}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{16\eta}\left(2\eta_{x}\eta_{y}\eta_{xy}-\eta_{x}^{2}\eta_{yy}-\eta_{y}^{2}\eta_{xx}\right)\frac{1}{4^{s-2}}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s-4}}{\eta^{s-2}},

which combined together imply

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4^{s}}+\frac{2s-1}{16}\right)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{\eta^{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2s-1}{2^{2s-1}}\int_{\Gamma}\Delta\eta\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s-2}}{\eta^{s-1}}-\frac{2s-2}{4^{s}}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{\eta}\left(2\eta_{x}\eta_{y}\eta_{xy}-\eta_{x}^{2}\eta_{yy}-\eta_{y}^{2}\eta_{xx}\right)\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s-4}}{\eta^{s-2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4^{s}}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{\eta^{s}}+C(s)\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla^{2}\eta|^{s},

where we used the Young inequality, so the proof is finished.

∎

Corollary A.7
.

Let
s\geq 1
and let
\eta\in W^{1,2s}(\Gamma)\cap W^{2,s}(\Gamma)
such that
\eta>0
a.e. on
\Gamma
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{\eta^{s}}\leq C\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla^{2}\eta|^{s},

where
C
only depends on
s
.

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
. Then, by previos lemma

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{(\eta+\delta)^{s}}\leq C\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla^{2}\eta|^{s}.

Since
\{\eta=0\}
is of measure zero, we have that
\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{(\eta+\delta)^{s}}\to\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{\eta^{s}}
a.e. on
\Gamma
. Moreover, as
\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{(\eta+\delta)^{s}}<\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{\eta^{s}}
a.e. on
\Gamma
, we have for any
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\int_{\{\eta\geq\varepsilon\}}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{\eta^{s}}=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\{\eta\geq\varepsilon\}}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{(\eta+\delta)^{s}}\leq\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2s}}{(\eta+\delta)^{s}}\leq C\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla^{2}\eta|^{s}

by Lebesque dominated convergence theorem. Passing to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma A.8
.

Let
\eta_{n}\to\eta
in
C([0,T]\times\Gamma)
as
n\to\infty
and assume that
\ln\eta_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Gamma))
. Then, for all
t\in[0,T]
and any non-negative
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)
, one has

(48)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\ln\eta(t)^{-}\varphi\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\ln\eta_{n}(t)^{-}\varphi.

Moreover,
\ln\eta\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\Gamma))
.

Proof.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and fix
t\in[0,T]
. Then, since
x\mapsto\ln(x+\delta)^{-}
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, we have that
\ln(\eta_{n}+\delta)^{-}\to\ln(\eta+\delta)^{-}
in
C([0,T]\times\Gamma)
as
\delta\to 0
and consequently

\displaystyle\ln(\eta(t)+\delta)^{-}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ln(\eta_{n}(t)+\delta)^{-}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma.

Therefore, since
\ln(x+\delta)^{-}\leq\ln(x)^{-}
on
[0,\infty)
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\ln(\eta(t)+\delta)^{-}\varphi=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\ln(\eta_{n}(t)+\delta)^{-}\varphi\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\ln\eta_{n}(t)^{-}\varphi,

for any non-negative
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)
. Now, assume there is a set of positive measure
S\subset\Gamma
such that
\eta(t)=0
on
S
and let
\varphi>0
on
S
. Then,

\displaystyle|\ln\delta|\int_{S}\varphi\leq\int_{S}\ln(\eta(t)+\delta)^{-}\varphi\leq\int_{\Gamma}\ln(\eta(t)+\delta)^{-}\varphi\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\ln\eta_{n}(t)^{-}\varphi,

so the left-hand side blows up as
\delta\to 0
which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\eta(t)=0
on a set of measure zero and consequently
\ln(\eta(t)+\delta)^{-}\to\ln\eta(t)^{-}
a.e. on
\Gamma
. By the Theorem of Egorov, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
A_{\varepsilon}\subset\Gamma
such that
|\Gamma\setminus A_{\varepsilon}|<\varepsilon
and
\ln(\eta(t)+\delta)^{-}\to\ln(\eta(t))^{-}
uniformly on
A_{\varepsilon}
, so

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\ln(\eta(t)+\delta)^{-}\varphi=\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\ln\eta(t)^{-}\varphi\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\ln\eta_{n}(t)^{-}\varphi.

Passing to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain
(
48
)
.

Now, let
[0,T]\ni t_{m}\to t
as
m\to\infty
and let
\kappa>0
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|\ln\eta(t)^{-}-\ln\eta(t_{m})^{-}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Gamma}|\ln(\eta(t))^{-}-\ln(\eta(t)+\kappa)^{-}|+\int_{\Gamma}|\ln(\eta(t)+\kappa)^{-}-\ln(\eta(t_{m})+\kappa)^{-}|

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Gamma}|\ln(\eta(t_{m})+\kappa)^{-}-\ln(\eta(t_{m}))^{-}|=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Since
\ln(\eta(t))^{-}\in L^{1}(\Gamma)
and since
\ln(\eta(t)+\kappa)^{-}\leq\ln(\eta(t))^{-}
, we have by the Lebesque dominated convergence theorem that

\displaystyle\ln(\eta(t)+\kappa)^{-}\to\ln(\eta(t))^{-}\quad\text{and}\quad\ln(\eta(t_{m})+\kappa)^{-}\to\ln(\eta(t_{m}))^{-}\quad\text{as }\kappa\to 0

where the second convergence follows in the same way. Therefore, for any
a>0
, there is a
\kappa>0
small enough such that
I_{1}+I_{3}\leq\frac{2a}{3}
. Next, since
t\mapsto\int_{\Gamma}\ln(\eta(t)+\kappa)^{-}\in C([0,T])
, there is an
m_{0}
large enough such that for all
m\geq m_{0}
one has
I_{2}\leq\frac{a}{3}
. Therefore, one concludes that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma}|\ln\eta(t)^{-}-\ln\eta(t_{m})^{-}|=0.

Since
\{t_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
was arbitrary, the proof is finished.

∎

Inverse SRB measures for endomorphisms on surfaces


Abstract
We extend D. Burguet’s construction of SRB measures [ 3 ] for the non invertible scenario obtaining hyperbolic invariant measures with absolutely continuous disintegrations on stable manifolds for a certain class of endomorphisms on the two torus. The constructed measures maximize the folding entropy, in particular, one may obtain such SRB measures for conservative perturbations of the examples given by M. Andersson, P. Carrasco and R. Saghin [ 1 ] for which the Lebesgue measure does not maximize the folding entropy. This way, we obtain examples of topologically mixing maps with at least two inverse SRB measures. In the case of inverse SRB measures that maximize the folding entropy, we give criteria for uniqueness.



Victor Janeiro, Radu Saghin

1
Introduction and results

Among the most important tools in the study of Dynamics Systems are the so-called SRB measures, and this is why they are extensively studied in the literature.

Let
f:M\rightarrow M
be a
C^{1+\alpha}
diffeomorphism on a compact Riemannian manifold
M
. An hyperbolic invariant measure
\mu
is called
SRB
if it satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions:

•

The disintegrations of
\mu
along the stable manifolds are absolutely continuous with respect to the normalized Lebesgue measure;

•

The measure
\mu
satisfies the Pesin formula:

h_{\mu}(f)=\int_{M}\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\lambda_{i}d\mu,

(1)

where
\lambda_{i}
are the Lyapunov exponents, which are well-defined at
\mu
-almost every point.

For precise definitions, see Section
2
. The
basin of the invariant measure
\mu
is

\mathcal{B}(\mu)=\{x\in M:\ \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{f^{k}(x)}=\mu\}.

(2)

In other words, the measure
\mu
statistically describes the future orbit of the points from its basin. If the invariant hyperbolic measure
\mu
is SRB and ergodic, then it satisfies the following important property:
The basin of
\mu
has positive Lebesgue measure
. This means that the measure
\mu
is
physical
, it describes the statistics of a (physically) relevant set of points.

One can define the
inverse SRB measures
as SRB measures for
f^{-1}
. Equivalently, they have absolutely continuous disintegrations along stable manifolds and satisfy the inverse Pesin formula:

h_{\mu}(f)=-\int_{M}\sum_{\lambda_{i}<0}\lambda_{i}d\mu.

(3)

Again if an inverse SRB measure is also ergodic, then it actually describes the statistics of the backward orbits of a set of points of positive Lebesgue measure.

Now suppose that
f:M\rightarrow M
is not invertible, it is just a
C^{1+\alpha}
endomorphism. In order to avoid critical points, we will assume that
f
is a local diffeomorphism. The forward SRB measures can still be defined as the hyperbolic measures which satisfy the forward Pesin formula. The unstable manifolds are not well defined on
M
because they depend on the backward trajectory. However, they are well defined for the canonical lift
\hat{f}
of
f
to the solenoidal manifold
Sol
generated by the linear part of
f
(a construction equivalent to the more well-known inverse limit or the natural extension; see Section
2
).
\hat{f}
is invertible, and is semiconjugated to
f
by the natural projection
\pi:Sol\rightarrow M
. Every invariant measure
\mu
for
f
has a unique lift
\hat{\mu}
on
Sol
invariant under
\hat{f}
. Then
\mu
is a forward SRB measure if and only if the disintegrations of
\hat{\mu}
along the unstable manifolds are absolutely continuous; see e.g.
[
8
]
. Again, if
\mu
is SRB and ergodic, then its basin has positive Lebesgue measure.

For endomorphisms we lose the symmetry between forward and inverse SRB measures, because the backward trajectory is not uniquely defined. We can still define the inverse SRB measures using the absolute continuity property.

Definition 1.1
.

The hyperbolic invariant measure
\mu
of the local
C^{1+\alpha}
diffeomorphism
f
is an inverse SRB measure if the disintegrations of
\mu
along the stable manifolds are absolutely continuous.

If
\mathcal{E}
is the partition of
M
into points, then the
folding entropy of
f
with respect to
\mu
is

F_{\mu}(f)=H_{\mu}(\mathcal{E}\mid f^{-1}\mathcal{E}).

(4)

Liu
[
10
]
proved that for every
\mathcal{C}^{1+}
endomorphism

h_{\mu}(f)\leq F_{\mu}(f)-\int_{M}\sum_{\lambda_{i}<0}\lambda_{i}\ d\mu,

(5)

.If the measure
\mu
is inverse SRB for the local diffeomorphism
f
, then it satisfies the
inverse Pesin formula for endomorphisms:

h_{\mu}(f)=F_{\mu}(f)-\int_{M}\sum_{\lambda_{i}<0}\lambda_{i}d\mu.

(6)

It is not clear if the converse holds in general, but we will describe a situation when it does. The disintegrations of
\mu
on the elements of the partition
f^{-1}\mathcal{E}
give rise to the
Jacobian of
\mu
,
J_{\mu}:M\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, defined
\mu
-almost everywhere. If the hyperbolic invariant measure
\mu
satisfies the inverse Pesin formula for the local diffeomorphism
f
, and the Jacobian
J_{\mu}
is Hölder continuous, then
\mu
is an inverse SRB measure
[
11
]
.

The basin of an inverse SRB measure
\mu
can be defined using the natural extension of
f
, more precisely, one can consider the basin of
\hat{\mu}
for
\hat{f}^{-1}
. The basin lies in the solenoidal manifold
Sol
; it can be projected to the manifold
M
, however we may have many inverse SRB measures such that the projections of their basins overlap in
M
, even for Anosov endomorphisms. This is because a point
x\in M
may have different backward trajectories converging to different inverse SRB measures. This is related to the fact that different inverse
SRB
measures
\mu
lift to different measures
\hat{\mu}
on
L_{f}
, and these in turn may have different disintegrations
\hat{\mu}_{x}
along the fibers
\pi^{-1}(x)
(the set of backward orbits from
x
).

A natural measure to consider on the fibers
\pi^{-1}(x)
is the Bernoulli measure
p_{x}
, i.e. the measure which gives equal weight
\frac{1}{d}
to each preimage of every point (
d
being the degree of
f
). A special type of measures is the following.

Definition 1.2
.

An invariant measure
\mu
of the
C^{1+\alpha}
local diffeomorphism
f
of degree
d
is equidistributed if one of the following 3 equivalent conditions holds:

•

The disintegrations of
\hat{\mu}
on
\pi^{-1}(x)
is
p_{x}
for
\mu
-almost every
x
;

•

J_{\mu}(x)=\frac{1}{d}
for
\mu
-almost every
x
;

•

F_{\mu}(f)=\log d
(or
F_{\mu}(f)
is maximal).

In this paper we are interested in the existence and uniqueness of equidistributed inverse SRB measures for endomorphisms on surfaces.

The SRB measures were extensively studied during the last years, especially in the case of diffeomorphisms. It is known that they exist for uniformly hyperbolic attractors, and also in lots of specific partially hyperbolic or non-uniformly hyperbolic settings. There are also important advances in the case of endomorphisms, like the work on the existence of forward SRB measures for partially hyperbolic endomorphisms on the torus or the annulus by Tsujii
[
18
]
. There are also works on a similar notion of inverse SRB measure for hyperbolic repellers by Mihailescu and Urbansky
[
13
]
,
[
12
]
. We are not aware of any works on the existence of SRB measures for endomorphisms without dominated splittings.

Recently, Burguet
[
3
]
and Buzzi-Crovisier-Sarig
[
4
]
independently obtained the following remarkable result:

Theorem 1.1

(Burguet, Buzzi-Crovisier-Sarig)
.

Suppose that
f:M\rightarrow M
is a
C^{\infty}
surface diffeomorphism, and consider the set

A_{0}=\{x\in M:\ \limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|Df^{n}(x)\|>0\}.

If
A_{0}
has positive Lebesgue measure, then
f
has
SRB
measures.

This important result proves Viana’s Conjecture in dimension 2, however it is not clear how to obtain concrete and robust examples satisfying the hypothesis outside the class of hyperbolic or partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms.

The goal of our paper is to provide a concrete examples of robust families of endomorphisms where we can apply the techniques developed in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
. We are able to obtain existence and uniqueness of equidistributed inverse SRB measures for an open class of surface endomorphisms without a dominated splitting. Let us explain who are these endomorphisms.

Let
\text{End}^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
be the space of
C^{r}
local diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. For any
f\in\text{End}^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
of degree
d\geq 2
, we define

I(x,v;f^{n}):=\frac{1}{d^{n}}\sum\limits_{y\in f^{-n}(x)}\log\|(Df^{n}_{y})^{-1}\cdot v\|,\ \forall(x,v)\in T^{1}\mathbb{T}^{2},\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N},

(7)

and

I_{f}(x):=\limsup\limits_{n}\frac{1}{n}\inf_{v\in T^{1}_{x}\mathbb{T}^{2}}I(x,v;f^{n}),\ \forall x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}.

(8)

Define

\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{f\in\text{End}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}):\text{for every}\ x\in\mathbb{T}^{2},\ \det(Df_{x})=\deg(f)\ \text{and}\ I_{f}(x)>0\}

(9)

and

\mathcal{F}_{1}=\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}:\ \pm 1\text{ is not an eigenvalue of the linear part of }f\}

(10)

Through a series of papers (
[
1
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
2
]
) we know the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Every
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}
is conservative, and (
C^{1}
robustly) non-uniformly hyperbolic in the sense that Lebesgue almost every point has one positive and one negative Lyapunov exponent. Every
f\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
is transitive and stably ergodic.

\mathcal{F}_{0}
(and
\mathcal{F}_{1}
) contains endomorphisms without dominated splitting and inside almost every homotopy class of endomorphisms on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem A
.

There exists a
C^{2}
open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subset\text{End}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
such that every
C^{\infty}
endomorphism
f\in\mathcal{U}
has a unique equidistributed inverse SRB measure
\mu_{f}
with full basin, meaning that for Lebesgue almost every point
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, for Bernoulli (
p_{x}
) almost every backward trajectory starting at
x
, the Birkhoff averages of the Dirac measure along the backward orbit converge weakly to
\mu_{f}
.

Remark 1.1
.

There is an asymmetry between the future and the past in our examples. Even if the future is deterministic, and the past is in some sense "random", from the statistical point of view, we know much more about the past than about the future. We know where most of the past orbits go, but these methods do not tell us anything about the future orbits. It is exactly the "randomness" that helps us to understand the past better than the future.

Following the results of Burguet and Buzzi-Crovisier-Sarig, we can in fact give more detailed results about the existence of SRB measures. A more general result on the existence of SRB measures is the following.

Let

R^{-}(f):=\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log^{+}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\|(Df^{n}_{x})^{-1}\|.

Theorem B
.

Let
f\in\text{End}^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
r\geq 2
. If
I_{f}(x)>\frac{R^{-}(f)}{r}
on a set of positive Lebesgue measure, then
f
admits at least one and at most countably many equidistributed inverse SRB measures
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.

We also provide specific examples of coexistence of inverse SRB measures with different folding entropies.

Proposition C
.

There are examples of transitive maps
g\in\mathcal{U}\subset End_{m}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
with, at least, two inverse SRB measures. These measures have different folding entropy and their unique lifts to the natural extension disintegrate along its fibres in different ways. The basin of each inverse SRB projects to the whole torus.

1.1
Ideas of the proofs

The proof of Theorem
B
is based on the tools developed by Burguet and Buzzi-Crovisier-Sarig in order to solve the Viana conjecture for surfaces. We adapt these tools to inverse preimages of curves, and we are able to prove that the hypothesis on the backward expansion implies the existence of invariant measures which are equidistributed and satisfy the inverse Pesin formula for endomorphisms, i.e. equidistributed inverse SRB measures.

The proof of Theorem
A
is based on the examples of nonuniformly hyperbolic endomorphisms on surfaces constructed by Andersson-Carrasco-Saghin. We will see that non-conservative perturbations of these examples satisfy the Hypothesis of Theorem
B
, so they have at most countably many equidistributed inverse
SRB
measures. Furthermore we are able to guarantee that the invariant manifolds of every ergodic SRB measures have sizes uniformly bound from below for many points. This fact, together with the "almost transitivity" which holds because of the closeness to the conservative examples, will help us conclude the uniqueness and the full basin property of the equidistributed inverse SRB measure.

1.2
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
we present the definitions and basic results related to endomorphisms on surfaces, invariant measures, Pesin Theory.

In Section
3
we discuss the Inverse expanding on average property for endomorphisms on surfaces: we give equivalent definitions and we present several important properties: the unstable directions at every point are spread in different directions, unstable manifolds exist at every point and they are in general large, there exist large stable manifolds. We also have uniform transitivity properties in a neighborhood of conservative endomorphism with the inverse expanding on average property.

Section
4
is dedicated to the presentation of the main tools used by Burguet in his work on SRB measures: Fölner sequences and empirical measures associated to them, and some notions of the Yomdin Theory: Reparametrization Lemma and geometric times.

In Section
5
we prove Theorem
B
, in Section
6
we prove Theorem
A
and in the last section we prove proposition
C
.

2
Inverse SRB measures

In this section, we will present the definitions and the basic properties of hyperbolic and inverse SRB measures for endomorphisms. Most of the definitions and results hold in a greater generality, however for simplicity we will restrict ourselves to the case of the two-torus.

2.1
The natural extension
L_{f}
and the Solenoid
Sol

Let
f:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
be a homeomorphism, then the natural extension of
f
is
L_{f}=\{(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\cdots)\in\left(\mathbb{T}^{2}\right)^{\mathbb{N}}:f(x_{i+1})=x_{i}\ \forall i\geq 0\}
. This is a bundle over
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, with the projection on the first coordinate, and the fiber over
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
consists of all the backward orbits starting at
x
. The map
f
lifts to the homeomorphism
\tau:L_{f}\to L_{f}
given by
\tau(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\cdots)=(f(x_{0}),x_{0},x_{1},\cdots)
.

Instead of the classical natural extension, we will use the slightly different (but equivalent) lift of
f
– the solenoid. The advantage of the solenoid is that it gives an explicit description of the local structure, the product of the disk with the Cantor set. The construction is as follows.

Every map
f:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
is homotopic to a linear endomorphism
A:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
induced by an integer matrix
A\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
which we denote by the same letter. We let
\pi_{cov}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{2}
the standard projection,
d=|\det A|\geq 2
and consider
\Sigma=\{1,\cdots,d\}^{\mathbb{N}}
. We also consider vectors
w_{1},\cdots,w_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\mathbb{Z}^{2}/A(\mathbb{Z}^{2})=\{[w_{1}],\cdots,[w_{d}]\}
and assume that
w_{1}\in A(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
, i.e.,
[w_{1}]=[0]
is the identity element in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}/A(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
.

The idea is to define a group
G
acting freely and properly discontinuously on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\Sigma
and form a space

Sol=(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\Sigma)/G,

called the solenoid of
A
. It is a solenoidal
2
-manifold, meaning that locally it is a product of a
2
-dimensional disk and the Cantor set
\Sigma
. Then we define an induced homeomorphism
\hat{f}:Sol\to Sol
which is conjugated to the natural extension
\tau:L_{f}\rightarrow L_{f}
.

We start with
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\Sigma
, and one can think of a point
(\tilde{x},\omega)
as the backward pseudo-orbit
x_{0}=\tilde{x},\ x_{1}=A^{-1}(x_{0}+w_{\omega_{1}}),\ x_{2}=A^{-1}(x_{1}+w_{\omega_{2}}),\dots
, which projects to
\mathbb{T}^{2}
to a backward orbit under
A
.

We now define the group
G
so it identifies the same backward orbits. For a vector
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we define the transformation
\psi_{v}:\Sigma\to\Sigma
inductively as follows: let
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\cdots)\in\Sigma

u_{0}=v
and for

•

let
\tau_{n}
be the unique number in
\{1,\cdots,d\}
such that

w_{\tau_{n}}-w_{\omega_{n}}+u_{n-1}\in A(\mathbb{Z}^{2}),\ \text{and}

•

set

as the unique element of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that

A\cdot u_{n}=w_{\tau_{n}}-w_{\omega_{n}}+u_{n-1}.

Then, define
\psi_{v}(\omega)=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\cdots)
. The group
G
of transformations on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\Sigma
is then defined as

G=\{(\tilde{x},\omega)\mapsto(\tilde{x}+v,\psi_{v}(\omega)):v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}.

G
is, in fact, a group action of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\Sigma
and as such it acts freely and properly discontinuously. We denote the orbit by
G
of a point

[(\tilde{x},\omega)]_{G}\in Sol
and the canonical projection
\pi_{G}:(\tilde{x},\omega)\mapsto[(\tilde{x},\omega)]_{G}
.

We can define the projection
\pi_{Sol}:Sol\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
given by
\pi_{Sol}([(\tilde{x},\omega)]_{G}=\pi_{cov}(\tilde{x})
, where
\pi_{cov}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
is the natural projection. Define
\sigma,\sigma^{\#}:\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma
, where
\sigma(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\dots)=(\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\omega_{4},\dots)
(the usual shift), and
\sigma^{\#}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots)=(1,\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots)
. It is not difficult to see that

\hat{A}:Sol\rightarrow Sol,\ \hat{A}([(\tilde{x},\omega)]_{G})=[(A\tilde{x},\sigma^{\#}(\omega))]_{G}

is a lift of A to
Sol
, which is conjugated to the natural of extension of
A
to
L_{A}
.

If
f
is homotopic to
A
, we can also lift it to a homeomorphisms
\hat{f}:Sol\to Sol
. For that we fix a lift
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of

to the universal cover
f
. The map
\hat{f}:Sol\to Sol
given by

\hat{f}([\tilde{x},\omega]_{G})=[(\tilde{f}(\tilde{x}),\sigma^{\#}(\omega))]_{G}

\hat{f}^{-1}([\tilde{x},\omega]_{G})=[\tilde{f}^{-1}(\tilde{x}+w_{\omega_{1}}),\sigma(\omega))]_{G}.

\pi_{Sol}\circ\hat{f}=f\circ\pi_{Sol}

Finally, we may explicitly obtain the local structure of

2.2
Invariant measures for endomorphisms

We consider
f\in End^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
\hat{f}:Sol\to Sol
the lift of
f
to the associated Solenoid. The invariant measures on

\hat{\mu}

(\pi_{Sol})_{*}\hat{\mu}=\mu

\hat{f}

\hat{\mu}

2.2.1
The Jacobian

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the partition of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
into points. Then
f^{-1}\mathcal{E}
is a measurable partition of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
into subsets with
d
elements (we assume that
f
has degree
d
). We can disintegrate the measure
\mu
along the elements of the partition
f^{-1}\mathcal{E}
, so for
\mu
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, we obtain a probability measure
\overline{\mu}_{f^{-1}(x)}
. Using these disintegrations, we can define the
Jacobian of
f
with respect to
\mu

as

J_{\mu}(y)=\frac{1}{\overline{\mu}_{f^{-1}(x)}(\{y\})},\ \ \text{ if }\ y\in f^{-1}(x).

(13)

Let us remark that the Jacobian
J_{\mu}
is well defined and inside
[1,\infty)
on a set of full
\mu
-measure. An equivalent way to define the Jacobian is the following. Consider the lift
\hat{\mu}
of
\mu
to
Sol
, and consider the partition of
Sol
given by the fibers
\pi_{Sol}^{-1}(x),\ x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Let
\mu_{x}
be the disintegrations of
\hat{\mu}
on
\pi_{Sol}^{-1}(x)
for
\mu
-almost every
x
. Then

J_{\mu}(x)=\frac{1}{\mu_{f(x)}(\hat{f}(\pi_{Sol}^{-1}(x)))}.

(14)

The Jacobian is in fact the Radon-Nicodym derivative
\frac{df^{*}\mu}{d\mu}
, it satisfies the following equivalent conditions. If
A\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
is such that
f|_{A}
is injective, with the inverse
(f|_{A})^{-1}:f(A)\rightarrow A
, then

\mu(f(A))=\int_{A}J_{\mu}d\mu,

(15)

\mu(A)=\int_{f(A)}\frac{1}{J_{\mu}\circ(f|_{A})^{-1}}d\mu.

(16)

The
folding entropy of
f
with respect to
\mu
is the relative entropy

F_{\mu}(f)=H_{\mu}(\mathcal{E}\mid f^{-1}\mathcal{E}),

(17)

or equivalently

F_{\mu}(f)=\int_{M}\log J_{\mu}d\mu.

(18)

2.2.2
Equidistributed measures

Let
p
be the equally distributed Bernoulli measure on
\Sigma
. We say that the invariant measure
\mu
is

if one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

1.

J_{\mu}(x)=d
for
\mu
-almost every
x
;

2.

F_{\mu}(f)=\log d
;

3.

For any small open set
V\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
, given the homeomorphism
\Xi:V\times\Sigma\to\pi_{Sol}^{-1}(V)
constructed in the previous section, we have

\Xi^{*}(\hat{\mu}|_{\pi_{Sol}^{-1}(V)})=(\mu|_{V})\times p.

(19)

In other words, the disintegrations
\mu_{x}
of
\hat{\mu}
on the fibers
\pi_{Sol}^{-1}(x)
are Bernoulli equidistributed for
\mu
-almost every
x
.

If

preserves the Lebesgue measure

on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, then there exists a unique invariant lift
\hat{Leb}
on
Sol
. However, in our case

may not be invariant under

, so the is no dynamical lift of

to

The equally distributed Bernoulli measure on the fibres of
\pi_{Sol}:Sol\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
may be obtained as

p_{x}=(\pi_{G})_{*}(\delta_{\tilde{x}}\times p),

where
\pi_{G}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\Sigma\to Sol
is the canonical projection,
\delta_{\tilde{x}}
is the Dirac measure on the unique point
\tilde{x}\in[0,1)^{2}
with
\pi_{cov}(\tilde{x})=x
and

is the equally distributed Bernoulli measure on
\Sigma

\hat{\eta}
on

\hat{\eta}=\int p_{x}\ d\text{Leb}(x).

(20)

We consider the partition
\mathcal{P}
of

For

Other tools: Fölner sequences, Yomdin Theory

4.1
Fölner sequences

Let us start by introducing some necessary concepts on Fölner sequences, they are of extreme importance on Burguet’s argument since the good geometrical times will be expressed by a suitable Fölner sequence on which we will consider the iterates of a good measure. We give a brief review on the main concepts, for details see Section 2 on
[
3
]
.

We let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
be the power sets of
\mathbb{N}
and
\{0,1,2,\cdots,n-1\}
respectively. For
E\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{N}}
, its boundary
\partial E
is given by the integers
n\in E
such that either
n+1\notin E
or
n-1\notin E
. Given
E\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
E_{n}:=E\cap[0,n-1]\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
, the upper and lower asymptotic densities of
E
are given by

\overline{d}(E)=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#E_{n}}{n},\ \ \underline{d}(E)=\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#E_{n}}{n}.

We may also define the upper and lower asymptotic densities of
E
along a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
by

\overline{d}^{\{n_{k}\}_{k}}(E)=\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{\#E_{n_{k}}}{n_{k}},\ \ \ \underline{d}^{\{n_{k}\}_{k}}(E)=\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{\#E_{n_{k}}}{n_{k}}.

We say that
F\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{N}}
is Fölner along a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
if its boundary
\partial F
has zero upper asymptotic density along
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
. In our context, we will only work with Fölner sequences with positive lower asymptotic density and in this scenario this property heuristically means that asymptotically the sequence has only few boundary elements. This yields the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space,
T:X\to X
a continuous map and
F\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{N}}
a Fölner sequence along
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
with positive lower asymptotic density
\underline{d}^{\{n_{k}\}_{k}}(F)>0
. Then for any family
(\mu_{n_{k}})_{k}
of Borel probability measures on
X
, any weak
∗
-limit of

\mu_{n_{k}}^{F_{n_{k}}}=\frac{1}{\#F_{n_{k}}}\sum\limits_{j\in F_{n_{k}}}T^{j}_{*}\mu_{n_{k}}

is a
T
-invariant Borel probability measure.

Given
E\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{N}}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the
N
-filled sequence related to
E
is the sequence
\text{Fill}^{N}(E)
given by the union over all interval of integers
[a,b)\cap\mathbb{N}
with
a,b\in E
such that
0<b-a\leq N
. The sequence
\text{Fill}^{N}(E)
is hence constructed by filling all gaps of
E
with size at most
N
. Given two
E,F\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{N}}
and a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
, we say that
E
is dense in
F
along
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
when

\left(\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{\#(F_{n_{k}}\setminus\text{Fill}^{N}(E))}{n_{k}}\right)\to 0,\ \text{as}\ N\to\infty.

At last, let
(X,\mathcal{B},\eta)
be a probability space. A map
E:X\to\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{N}}
is said to be measurable when for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the set
\{x\in X:n\in E(x)\}
is measurable. The following lemma is of extreme importance in the construction we aim to make, it will allows us to obtain sets with subexponential decay of measure where every point has its geometrical times on a a unique good Fölner sequence. Lemma 4.2

Assume that

4.2
Empirical measures associated to Fölner sequences

We consider an additive cocycle
\psi^{n}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}\psi\circ T^{j}
associated to a continuous function
\psi:X\to\mathbb{R}
. We also consider a measurable
E:Y\to\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{N}}
with positive asymptotic density, for
Y
a Borel subset of
X
, and let
\{n_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{N}
,
F\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{N}}
Fölner sequence along
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
and
A_{n_{k}}\subset X
associated to

given by Lemma

.

Definition 4.1
.

(Fölner-Gibbs property)
The measure
\eta
is said to satisfy the Fölner Gibbs property with respect to the additive cocycle
(\psi^{n})_{n}
and the Fölner sequence
(F,A_{n_{k}})_{k}
when there exist
\epsilon>0
such that given any partition
P
of
X
with diameter less than
\epsilon
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every integer

and every

\frac{1}{\eta(P^{F_{n_{k}}}(x)\cap A_{n_{k}})}\geq\exp{\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in F_{n_{k}}}\psi\circ T^{j}(x)\bigg)}.

(39)

We consider the probability measures
\mu_{n_{k}}
on
X
given by

\mu_{n_{k}}(B)=\frac{\eta(A_{n_{k}}\cap B)}{\eta(A_{n_{k}})},\ \text{for every}\ B\in\mathcal{B}.

We are interested on studying limit measures of the sequence

\mu_{n_{k}}^{F_{n_{k}}}=\frac{1}{\#F_{n_{k}}}\sum\limits_{j\in F_{n_{k}}}T^{j}_{*}\mu_{n_{k}}

The Fölner-Gibbs property is a version of the classical Gibbs property restricted to a Fölner sequence. Burguet shows that this is sufficient to relate the entropy of limit measures associated to
\mu_{n_{k}}
with the associated cocycle as follows.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
\eta
satisfies the Fölner-Gibbs property with respect to the additive cocycle
(\psi^{n})_{n}
and
(F,A_{n_{k}})_{k}
, then any weak
∗
limit
\mu
of the sequence
(\mu_{n_{k}}^{F_{n_{k}}})_{k}
satisfies

h(\mu)\geq\int\psi d\mu.

At last, the following Lemma will allow us to control the Lyapunov exponents of such limit measures.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that there is
a>0
such that for any
x\in X
,
\psi^{m-k}(T^{k}(x))>(m-k)a
for all integers
m>k\in E(x)
. Then, for any weak
∗
limit
\mu
of the sequence
(\mu_{n_{k}}^{F_{n_{k}}})_{k}
satisfies

\tilde{\psi}(x):=\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\psi^{n}(x)\geq a,\ \text{for}\ \mu\text{-a.e.}\ x\in X.

4.3
Geometric times

A
\mathcal{C}^{r}
curve
\gamma:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
is said to be bounded if

\max\limits_{s=2,\cdots,r}\|d^{s}\gamma\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{6}\|d\gamma\|_{\infty}.

(40)

We remark some important properties on bounded curves:

•

A bounded curve has bounded distortion, i.e.

\text{for every}\ t,s\in[-1,1],\ \frac{\|d\gamma(t)\|}{\|d\gamma(s)\|}\leq\frac{3}{2}.

(41)

•

The projective component of
\gamma
also oscillates slowly, that is, by fixing local charts covering
\gamma
, we have

\text{for every}\ t,s\in[-1,1],\ \angle(d\gamma(t),d\gamma(s))\leq\frac{\pi}{6}

A
\mathcal{C}^{r}
curve is strongly
\epsilon
-bounded when it is bounded and
\|d\gamma\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon
. Burguet remark that such a map satisfies
\|\gamma\|_{r}=\max_{1\leq s\leq r}\|d^{s}\gamma\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon
, which is the standard
\mathcal{C}^{r}
upper bound required for the reparametrizations on the usual Yomdin’s theory. Hence the bounded property (
40
) is the main difference introduced which allows the control of the distortion along the curve.

There is yet another difference between the Burguet’s and the Yomdin’s approach. Here we also want to control the size of our reparametrizations from below, when possible. This way, he introduces the notion of geometric times, which we adapt to the non-invertible case.

We consider a
\mathcal{C}^{r}
non-invertible local diffeomorphism
g:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
, and a curve
\sigma:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
. For
x=\sigma(s)
, we take
v_{x}=\sigma^{\prime}(s)/\|\sigma^{\prime}(s)\|\in T_{x}\mathbb{T}^{2}
an unite vector in the direction tangent to
\sigma
at
x
. We
also consider the map
\hat{g}:Sol\to Sol
and some fixed identification of
\pi_{Sol}^{-1}(\sigma)
with
\sigma\times\Sigma
through a homeomorphism

as in Subsection
2.1
. As before, for
\hat{x}\in\pi_{Sol}^{-1}(\sigma)
, we let
v_{\hat{x}}=v_{x}\in T_{x}\mathbb{T}^{2}
, where
x=\pi_{Sol}(\hat{x})
.

For each
\omega\in\Sigma
, we consider the curve
\hat{\sigma}_{\omega}:[-1,1]\to Sol
as
\hat{\sigma}_{\omega}(s)=\Xi(\sigma(s),\omega)
, then
\hat{\sigma}_{\omega}
is strongly
(n,\epsilon)
-bounded if for every
0\leq k\leq n
the curve
\sigma^{\omega}_{k}=\pi_{Sol}\circ\hat{g}^{-k}\circ\hat{\sigma}_{\omega}
is strongly
\epsilon
-bounded. Basically this property depends on the pre-images of
\sigma
by

up to time

associated with

is also strongly

Definition 4.2
.

(Geometric Times) Fix a
\mathcal{C}^{r}
curve
\sigma:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
and an identification

. For

-geometric time of

if there exists an affine map

such that:

.

•

(n,\epsilon)
-bounded.

\||d(\sigma_{n}^{\omega}\circ\theta_{n}^{\omega})(0)\|\geq\frac{3}{2}\alpha\epsilon

.

7.1
Perturbations in dimension one

We begin in dimension 1, as this construction will be utilized for dimension 2. The only self-covers of
\mathbb{T}^{1}
with constant Jacobian are the maps

given by

. For

we construct a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

which is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-
\epsilon
-close to

with non-constant Jacobian.

For that, we fix any point

,
\delta<\frac{1}{2k}
and consider, for

small, a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
bump function
\varphi_{\epsilon}:[p-\delta,p+\delta]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

•

, for every

•

\varphi(x)=0
, if

•

We denote by

. Since

is the disjoint union of
k

with

We consider the diffeomorphism

and define the perturbation

as:

S_{\epsilon}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}R_{k}(x),&x\notin I_{o}\cup I_{1},\\
R_{k}(x)+\varphi_{\epsilon}(x),&x\in I_{0},\\
R_{k}(x)-\varphi_{\epsilon}\circ T^{-1}(x),&x\in I_{1}.\end{array}\right.

It is clear from the definition of
\varphi_{\epsilon}
that
S_{\epsilon}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
self-cover of
\mathbb{T}^{1}
which is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-
\epsilon
-close to
R_{k}
. It has non constant Jacobian, more specifically
|S_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(x)|\neq k
in the points

where the derivative of
\varphi_{\epsilon}
and
\varphi_{\epsilon}\circ T^{-1}
are non-zero. All there is left to show is that
S_{\epsilon}
is conservative, for that we show that for every

\sum\limits_{z\in S_{\epsilon}^{-1}(y)}\frac{1}{|S_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(z)|}=1.

(55)

For points

, we have
S_{\epsilon}^{-1}(y)\cap(I_{0}\cup I_{1})=R_{k}^{-1}(y)\cap(I_{0}\cup I_{1})=\emptyset
. Hence
S_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(z)=R_{k}^{\prime}(z)=k
for every

and we get (
55

y\in J
, we have
S_{\epsilon}^{-1}(y)=\{z_{0},\cdots,z_{k-1}\}
with

. For

we have

. We also have that

, indeed

\displaystyle S_{\epsilon}(T(z_{0}))

\displaystyle=R_{k}(T(z_{0}))-\varphi_{\epsilon}\circ T^{-1}(T(z_{0}))=kz_{0}+\varphi_{\epsilon}(z_{0})+1=S_{\epsilon}(z_{0}).

We then have

S_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(z_{1})=k-\frac{\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(z_{0})}{1+\frac{2}{k}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(z_{0})}.

Hence, for

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{z\in S_{\epsilon}^{-1}(y)}\frac{1}{|S_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(z)|}=\frac{k-2}{k}+\frac{1}{|S_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(z_{0})|}+\frac{1}{|S_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(z_{1})|}

\displaystyle=\frac{k-2}{k}+\frac{1}{k+\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(z_{0})}+\frac{1+\frac{2}{k}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(z_{0})}{k+\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(z_{0})}=1.

This shows that (

Investigation on the air supply subsystem of PEMFC based on sliding mode control and model predictive control
Authors: Xianglin Pan, Mengda Li, Xiaolong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo improve the performance and stability of proton exchange membrane fuel cell (PEMFC), this paper proposes a hybrid control strategy that integrates sliding mode control (SMC) with model predictive control (MPC). Based on a control framework that combines global optimization with local compensation, the proposed method optimizes PEMFC operating conditions while improving system robustness. A PEMFC air supply subsystem model was developed in Simulink to conduct numerical simulations of SMC, MPC, and the hybrid control strategy. The results show that the hybrid approach achieves the smallest overshoot, reduced by 60% and 28% compared with SMC and MPC, respectively. It also attains the shortest settling time, decreased by 20% and 52%, and the fastest disturbance recovery time, improved by 50% and 37.5%. Overall, the hybrid strategy improved dynamic metrics for oxygen excess ratio, voltage stability, and net power under tested load steps.Keywords: PEMFC, Sliding mode control, Model predictive control, Hybrid control, Air supply subsystemSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

In recent decades, global energy demand has continued to increase, with traditional fossil fuels such as coal, oil, and natural gas still dominating the global energy supply structure. However, the extensive consumption of fossil fuels has led to severe environmental and ecological challenges, particularly the large-scale emissions of carbon dioxide and methane, which are recognized as major drivers of global climate change. Consequently, the substitution of conventional fossil fuels with clean and sustainable energy sources has become one of the most critical strategies for reducing carbon emissions1.

Hydrogen is one of the most abundant elements on Earth, and its utilization as an energy carrier produces only water as a by-product, making it a promising solution for facilitating the global energy transition and mitigating climate change2,3. Compared with traditional fossil fuel–based energy conversion technologies, fuel cells exhibit advantages such as high energy conversion efficiency and environmentally benign operation, which endow them with significant potential in applications including transportation, aerospace, and stationary power generation systems4,5. Since the beginning of the 21st century, increasing environmental concerns and the growing demand for new energy vehicles have accelerated the development of fuel cell vehicles worldwide6–8. Among the various types of fuel cells, proton exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs) have attracted considerable attention owing to their low operating temperature, fast start-up characteristics, and high-power density, making them particularly suitable for automotive and portable power applications9.

Sliding mode control has played a pivotal role in the advancement of proton exchange membrane fuel cell systems, exemplifying the mutually reinforcing relationship between modern control theory and clean energy technologies. Compared with conventional control strategies, SMC has attracted considerable attention due to its inherent robustness against parameter variations and external disturbances, superior tracking performance, and enhanced system stability10,11. Numerous studies have investigated the application of SMC in PEMFC-related systems. Laghrouche et al. applied sliding mode control to PEMFC gas supply systems and demonstrated its effectiveness in the presence of system uncertainties12,13. Wang et al. proposed an adaptive sliding mode controller and validated its performance experimentally, showing that the controller achieves rapid dynamic response while effectively suppressing oscillations14. Silaa et al. integrated an adaptive stochastic gradient descent method into a sliding mode control framework, with simulation results indicating a significant reduction in chattering while maintaining robustness15. Abbaker et al. introduced a delay-based algorithm into sliding mode control, and simulation studies revealed improved response speed and overall system performance in PEMFC applications16. To further enhance control performance, Fang et al. developed an adaptive sliding mode controller based on fuzzy logic, with experimental results demonstrating notable improvements in the efficiency and reliability of PEMFC systems17. Li et al. proposed a control scheme combining input–output linearization with sliding mode control, achieving effective regulation of the oxygen excess ratio and cathode pressure18. Javaid et al. introduced a fast terminal integral sliding mode control (FTISMC) strategy for oxygen excess ratio regulation in PEMFC power systems, with results confirming the elimination of chattering and improved system efficiency19. Matraji et al. employed a second-order sliding mode controller to regulate the oxygen excess ratio, demonstrating excellent transient response characteristics20. Zhu et al. proposed an adaptive sliding mode controller validated on an experimental platform, further confirming the effectiveness of SMC-based approaches in PEMFC control21. In addition, Derakhshannia et al. designed a predictive sliding mode control strategy and verified its robustness and effectiveness through simulation studies22.

The research history of model predictive control in PEMFC systems reflects the progressive integration of advanced control theory with clean energy technologies. As traditional control strategies proved insufficient for addressing strong multivariable coupling and the stringent performance requirements of high-power, highly dynamic PEMFC systems, MPC emerged as a promising alternative due to its inherent capability to handle multivariable interactions and operational constraints23,24. A growing body of research has explored the application of MPC in various PEMFC subsystems. Zhang et al. introduced MPC into the thermal management system of a PEMFC and compared its performance with that of a proportional–integral (PI) controller, demonstrating that MPC enables effective regulation of stack temperature25. Gruber et al. applied MPC to the PEMFC gas supply system and showed that satisfactory control performance can be achieved when an accurate system model is available26. Liu et al. employed an enhanced MPC strategy for automotive fuel cell thermal management, significantly improving state equilibrium and fuel economy of onboard fuel cell systems27. To further improve control performance, Yang et al. optimized an MPC controller using fuzzy logic, thereby enhancing system net power output and overall performance28. Wang et al. combined MPC with PID control to improve the regulation performance of PEMFC gas supply systems29. Chen et al. proposed a cascaded feedback linearization–based MPC scheme, which further strengthened the disturbance rejection capability of the system30. More recently, Wang et al. introduced a deep learning–based hierarchical model predictive control (HMPC) framework, demonstrating improved robustness compared with conventional MPC approaches31. Deng et al. developed a neural network–based MPC using long short-term memory (LSTM), with simulation results indicating significant performance improvements32. In addition, Chatrattanawet et al. proposed a robust MPC strategy for PEMFC control, with results confirming that the proposed method ensures system stability under uncertain conditions33.

However, model predictive control suffers from relatively long prediction horizons when applied to complex systems and requires considerable computational resources for online optimization, which may prolong the dynamic response time and consequently degrade the system’s dynamic performance34. Sliding mode control, on the other hand, may exhibit chattering during operation, and its performance is often constrained by model accuracy and operating conditions35. To address these limitations and enhance the performance and stability of proton exchange membrane fuel cell systems, this paper integrates model predictive control with sliding mode control to develop an SMC–MPC fusion control strategy. Through numerical simulations, the proposed method is systematically compared with conventional single control strategies, thereby verifying the advantages of the SMC–MPC fusion controller in terms of dynamic performance and system stability.

The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:

(1) A dynamic model of the PEMFC air supply subsystem is implemented based on established models in the literature, providing a simulation platform for control design and validation.

(2) A SMC–MPC hybrid control strategy is proposed, and its effectiveness is validated through numerical simulations.

Model predictive control strategy

Dynamic matrix control model

Dynamic Matrix Control (DMC) is a representative model predictive control technique whose fundamental principle is based on the system’s step response model to construct a closed-loop control framework. Compared with conventional predictive control methods, DMC features a relatively simple implementation procedure, strong robustness, fast dynamic response, and low dependence on model accuracy.

The core of the DMC predictive control model lies in constructing the system’s step response curve to determine an appropriate sampling period T. This model is primarily intended to satisfy the control requirements of the system under steady-state operating conditions, and it utilizes step response data over N consecutive sampling instants to characterize the system’s dynamic behavior, where N denotes the time-domain length parameter of the model.

The outputs are subsequently integrated using a fusion algorithm. Common fusion strategies include cascade structure, weighted fusion, and conditional switching. In the cascade structure, the MPC generates the reference trajectory, while the SMC is responsible for tracking this trajectory. In weighted fusion, the control outputs of the two controllers are combined proportionally. In conditional switching, MPC is employed during steady-state operation, whereas the controller switches to SMC when disturbances occur. When designing the MPC-based DMC strategy, the MPC Toolbox is employed as the implementation platform, and the prediction horizon p is set to 40. This choice aims to balance system response speed and control accuracy, enabling the controller to effectively predict future system behavior and generate appropriate control actions. The selection of the prediction horizon considers system characteristics, operational constraints, and performance objectives to achieve optimal control performance. In this configuration, the control horizon M is required to be smaller than the prediction horizon. Accordingly, the control horizon M was set to 5. The controller output is constrained within the range of 1.8 to 2.5, while the maximum input voltage of the air compressor is limited to 300 V. Under these constraints, the parameters in the MPC Toolbox are appropriately tuned. After completing the controller configuration, the MPC Toolbox is applied to the linearized model. For the air supply system under step input conditions, calibrating the linearized model is a critical step, as it enables more accurate prediction and control of the system’s dynamic response.

It is worth noting that, although load variations within a certain range are involved here, the system’s dynamic response remains relatively smooth within the operating conditions studied; therefore, a single-point linear model can still provide a reasonably good approximation of the system’s behavior. For wider operating ranges or conditions with strong nonlinearity, gain scheduling or nonlinear MPC methods may be further employed.

Conclusion

This paper investigates advanced control strategies for PEMFC systems. Based on an innovative control framework that integrates global optimization with local compensation, a novel SMC–MPC hybrid control strategy is proposed. Comparative simulation studies of the SMC–MPC, SMC, and MPC strategies were conducted using a sliding mode control model of the PEMFC gas supply subsystem. The main conclusions can be summarized as follows:

Compared with standalone SMC and MPC strategies, the proposed SMC–MPC hybrid control strategy reduces overshoot by 60% and 28%, respectively; achieves the shortest settling time, with reductions of 20% and 52%; and provides the fastest disturbance recovery, shortening recovery time by 50% and 37.5%, respectively.

Compared with standalone SMC and MPC strategies, the proposed SMC–MPC hybrid control strategy reduces overshoot by 60% and 28%, respectively; achieves the shortest settling time, with reductions of 20% and 52%; and provides the fastest disturbance recovery, shortening recovery time by 50% and 37.5%, respectively.

The SMC–MPC hybrid strategy inherits the strong robustness of SMC in resisting external disturbances and system uncertainties, while fully exploiting the advantages of MPC in multivariable optimization and constraint handling, thereby enabling effective global optimization under multiple operating conditions.

By addressing the inherent limitations of single control strategies in coping with the complex dynamics and disturbances of PEMFC systems, the proposed SMC–MPC approach significantly enhances dynamic response performance, system stability, and disturbance rejection capability.

Limitations and perspective

Although the effectiveness of the proposed SMC–MPC hybrid control strategy has been verified through simulation, several limitations remain. First, the present study is primarily based on a simulation environment and does not fully account for practical factors such as sensor noise, actuator dynamics and delays, and model parameter uncertainties encountered in real systems. Second, the control parameters adopted in this study (e.g., the MPC prediction horizon and SMC gains) were determined under specific operating conditions, and a systematic sensitivity analysis of these parameters has not yet been performed. Future work will focus on validating the proposed strategy beyond simulation studies and further optimizing the control framework to address challenges such as real-time adaptive learning, scalability to large-scale systems, and fault detection and isolation, particularly under long-term degradation and complex operating conditions. Moreover, the integration of digital twin technology with artificial intelligence is expected to provide intelligent real-time monitoring and optimization capabilities, thereby further improving the efficiency, durability, and sustainability of PEMFC systems.

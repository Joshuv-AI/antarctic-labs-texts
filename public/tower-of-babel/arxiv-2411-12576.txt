A universal Euler system for GSp 4 \GSp_{4} Thanks: D.L. gratefully acknowledges the support of the European Research Council through the Horizon 2020 Excellent Science programme (Consolidator Grant “ShimBSD: Shimura varieties and the BSD conjecture”, grant ID 101001051)
David Loeffler, Sarah Livia Zerbes

Abstract
. In our earlier work with Christopher Skinner [ LSZ22 ] we constructed Euler systems for the 4-dimensional spin Galois representations corresponding to automorphic forms for GSp 4 \GSp_{4} . This construction depended on various arbitrary choices of local test data. In this paper, we use multiplicity-one results for smooth representations to determine how these Euler system classes depend on the choice of test data, showing that all of these classes lie in a 1-dimensional space and are explicit multiples (given by local zeta-integrals) of a “universal” class independent of the choice of test data.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

This paper is intended as a complement to our recent work
[
LSZ22
]
with Christopher Skinner, in which we constructed an Euler system for four-dimensional Galois representations arising from cuspidal automorphic representations of
\GSp_{4}
. The construction of this Euler system depends on certain choices of auxiliary local data (“test data”). In
op.cit.
we simply worked with an arbitrary fixed choice of these data, but it is far from obvious
a priori
what the best choice should be.

In this note, we show that different choices of these data only affect the Euler system by a scaling factor, and that these scaling factors are explicitly given by local zeta integrals.

Note

The results in this paper are among the inputs in the paper
[
LZ26
]
, which proves a reciprocity law relating the
\GSp_{4}
Euler system to critical
L
-values, and applies this to deduce cases of the Bloch–Kato and Iwasawa main conjectures. The material in this paper was originally part of the earlier drafts of
[
LZ26
]
, but since it uses rather different methods from the proof of the reciprocity law, and the combined paper had become excessively long, we have factored out these representation-theoretic arguments into the present separate paper. We hope these methods will also be of interest for other Euler system constructions beyond the scope of
\GSp_{4}
.

1.2.
Main results

We briefly recall the relevant constructions from
[
LSZ22
]
. Let
r_{1}\geqslant r_{2}\geqslant 0
be integers, and choose

with
0\leqslant q\leqslant r_{2}
and

. In this introduction we suppose

, which simplifies the statements slightly. In Definition 8.3.1 of

\mathcal{LE}^{[q,r]}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{4};\mathbf{Q})\otimes\mathcal{H}(G(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}});\mathbf{Q})\to H^{4}_{\mot}\left(Y_{G},\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{Q}}(-q)\right),

where

is the Shimura variety for
\GSp_{4}
, and

is a relative motive over

corresponding to an algebraic representation of

is the space of

\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{4}
, and

We can obtain Galois cohomology classes by composing
\mathcal{LE}^{[q,r]}
with the
p
-adic étale realisation map, for a choice of prime

\Pi
be a cuspidal, non-CAP automorphic representation of

whose Archimedean component is cohomological with respect to the algebraic representation of highest weight
(r_{1},r_{2})
. By projecting to the
\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}^{\vee}
-isotypical component of étale cohomology, we obtain classes in the Galois cohomology of

Our first main result shows that after restricting to a character eigenspace, this entire construction factors through a one-dimensional quotient. Let us choose a pair of Dirichlet characters
\underline{\chi}=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
whose product is the central character
\chi_{\Pi}

\Pi

\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{4};\underline{\chi}^{-1})
denote the
\underline{\chi}^{-1}

\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}\times\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}

\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{4};\mathbf{Q})

Theorem A

(
Theorem

3.6.4
)
.

Assume
\Pi_{\ell}
is generic for all finite primes
\ell
. Then there is a class

z_{\mathrm{can}}^{[\Pi,q,r]}(\underline{\chi})\in H^{1}\left(\mathbf{Q},V_{\Pi}^{*}(-q)\right)

with the following property: for all choices of
\underline{\Phi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{4};\underline{\chi}^{-1})
, all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}(G(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}});\mathbf{Q})
, and all choices of modular parametrisation of
V_{\Pi}^{*}
, the image of
\LE^{[q,r]}(\underline{\Phi}\otimes\xi)
in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},V_{\Pi}^{*}(-q))
is a scalar multiple of
z_{\mathrm{can}}^{[\Pi,q,r]}(\underline{\chi})
. Moreover, the scalar factor is given by an explicit product of local zeta-integrals.

This is an analogue of the result of
[
HS01
]
in the setting of the Beilinson–Kato Euler system for
\GL_{2}
; compare also
[
Loe21
, §6.2]
for an analogous, but less complete, result for Hilbert modular surfaces. The proof of
Theorem

A
relies on multiplicity-one results for smooth representations of
\GSp_{4}
over local fields due to Mœglin–Waldspurger and Rösner–Weissauer.

Our second result shows that the genericity assumption is essential:

Theorem B

(
Theorem

3.6.3
)
.

Assume that the central character of
\Pi
is a square in the group of Dirichlet characters. Then, if there is a finite prime
\ell
such that
\Pi_{\ell}
is non-generic, the projection of
\LE^{[q,r]}(\underline{\Phi}\otimes\xi)
to the
\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}^{\vee}
-isotypic component is zero for all choices of
\underline{\Phi}
and
\xi
.

(We expect Theorem B to hold without the assumption on the central character; but this would require generalising some results in the Gan–Gross–Prasad theory of branching laws for smooth representations from special orthogonal groups to
\operatorname{GSpin}
groups, and this theory has not yet been fully developed.)

For the goal of studying Galois representations, the vanishing of the Euler system for non-generic
\Pi
is no loss: for any
\Pi
satisfying our hypotheses, there will be a unique globally generic representation
\Pi_{\mathrm{gen}}
in the same
L
-packet as
\Pi
, and
\Pi_{\mathrm{gen}}
will have the same Galois representation as
\Pi

\Pi_{\mathrm{gen}}

as showing that there is no redundant “extra choice” in constructing the Euler system given by varying the representation in its

Remark 1.2.1
.

In the case of Yoshida lifts,
V_{\Pi}^{*}
is a direct sum of two 2-dimensional representations, with one summand accounting for the largest and smallest Hodge–Tate weights, and the other summand the two intermediate ones. In this setting, combining
Theorem

and Arthur’s multiplicity formula implies that the construction of
[
LSZ22
]
can only give nontrivial classes in the summand corresponding to the middle two Hodge–Tate weights, never in the other summand. This is consistent with the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture: any cohomology class arising from geometry must lie in the Bloch–Kato

subspace, and the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture predicts that the summand of
V_{\Pi}^{*}(-q)
with the outermost Hodge–Tate weights has

.

2.
Multiplicity-one results for local periods

In this section we give a careful statement of a purely local uniqueness result in smooth representation theory (Theorem
2.3.3
), and a semi-local variant, which will be used in the following sections to study our Euler system classes.

2.1.
Local theory:
L
-packets

In this section we let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
for some prime
\ell
, and
|\cdot|
the norm on
F
, normalised such that
|\varpi|=\frac{1}{q}
, where
\varpi
is a uniformiser and
q
is the order of the residue field. We let
\psi
be a non-trivial additive character
F\to\mathbf{C}^{\times}
, which we can regard as a character of
N(F)
via

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x&\star&\star\\
&1&y&\star\\
&&1&-x\\
&&&\phantom{-}1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mapsto\psi(x+y).

Recall that for an irreducible smooth
G(F)
-representation
\rho
, we have
\dim\Hom_{N(F)}(\rho,\psi)\leqslant 1
, and we say
\rho
is
generic
if equality holds, in which case there is a unique subspace
\mathcal{W}(\rho)\subset\Ind_{N(F)}^{G(F)}(\psi)
isomorphic to
\pi
(the
Whittaker model
of
\rho
).

The set of isomorphism classes of irreducible smooth
G(F)
-representations is partitioned into
L
-packets
, which can be defined as the fibres of the local Langlands correspondence of
[
GT11
]
. Each
L
-packet has size either 1 or 2, and is naturally parameterised by the characters of the centraliser of the Langlands parameter associated with the packet. We say an
L
-packet is
generic
if it contains a generic representation; in this case it contains precisely one such representation, corresponding to the trivial character of the component group. All non-singleton
L
-packets are generic. (See
[
GT11
, Main Theorem]
for these statements.)

Proposition 2.1.1
.

If
P
is an
L
-packet containing a tempered (or essentially tempered) representation, then all members of
P
are tempered (resp. essentially tempered), and moreover
P
is a generic packet.

Proof.

This is not explicitly stated in
[
GT11
]
, but follows from the explicit description of the correspondence given in
op.cit.
, as follows.

Firstly, in §7 of
[
GT11
]
it is shown that the non-singleton
L
-packets have the form
\{\theta(\tau_{1}\boxtimes\tau_{2}),\theta(\tau_{1}^{D}\boxtimes\tau_{2}^{D})\}
where
\tau_{i}
are discrete-series representations of
\GL_{2}(F)
(with the same central character) and
\tau_{i}^{D}
are their Jacquet-Langlands transfers to the non-split quaternion algebra over
F
. (See
op.cit.
for the meaning of the notation
\theta(-)
.) Representations of this form are always essentially tempered, and are tempered iff their central character (which is the same for both members of the packet) is unitary. So if one member of an
L
-packet
P
is tempered or essentially tempered, then all are. Moreover, any representation which is essentially tempered but not generic arises as
\theta(\tau_{1}^{D}\boxtimes\tau_{2}^{D})
, by Theorem 5.6(i) of
[
GT11
]
, and hence lies in a 2-element
L
-packet whose other member
\theta(\tau_{1}\boxtimes\tau_{2})
is generic.∎

2.2.
Local multiplicity one for irreducible representations

We will need the following important theorem:

Theorem 2.2.1

(Prasad, Emory–Takeda)
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible
G(F)
-representation belonging to a generic
L
-packet, and
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
irreducible
\GL_{2}(F)
-representations. Then we have
\dim\Hom_{H(F)}(\pi\otimes(\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\sigma_{2}),\mathbf{C})\leqslant 1
.

Moreover, if
P
is a generic
L
-packet for
G(F)
, and the
\sigma_{i}
are generic representations, there is at most one
\pi\in P
for which
\Hom_{H(F)}(\pi\otimes(\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\sigma_{2}),\mathbf{C})\neq 0
.

Proof.

We note that for any irreducible representations
\pi
of
G(F)
and
\sigma
of
H(F)
, the space
\Hom_{H(F)}(\pi\otimes\sigma,\mathbf{C})
has dimension
\leqslant 1
, by the main theorem of
[
ET23
]
. If either of the representations
\sigma_{i}
is non-generic (hence one-dimensional), then
\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\sigma_{2}
remains irreducible when restricted to
H
, so the first assertion follows in this case.

If both
\sigma_{i}
are generic, then
(\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\sigma_{2})|_{H(F)}
may be reducible, but it is always a direct sum of finitely many distinct irreducibles. Theorem 5 of
[
Pra96
]
shows that in this case there is at most one
\pi\in P
, and at most one irreducible factor
\sigma
of
\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\sigma_{2}
, for which the Hom-space is non-zero; and the aforementioned theorem of Emory–Takeda shows that this unique non-zero Hom-space (if it exists) has dimension 1. So the result also follows in this case.
∎

2.3.
Zeta integrals

We fix a pair of characters
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
of
F^{\times}
, with
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
equal to the central character
\chi_{\pi}
of
\pi

Notation 2.3.1
.

The notation
\mathcal{S}(F^{2})
denotes the space of
\mathbf{C}
-valued Schwartz functions (locally constant functions of compact support) on

. We write
\mathcal{S}_{0}(F^{2})

\mathcal{S}(F^{2}\times F^{2})=\mathcal{S}(F^{2})\otimes\mathcal{S}(F^{2})

In

, §8.2]

Z(w,\underline{\Phi},s_{1},s_{2})

\underline{\Phi}=\Phi_{1}\otimes\Phi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(F^{2})\otimes\mathcal{S}(F^{2})

\int_{(Z_{H}N_{H}\backslash H)(F)}w(h)f^{\Phi_{1}}(h_{1};\chi_{1},s_{1})W^{\Phi_{2}}(h_{2};\chi_{2},s_{2})\,\mathrm{d}h

(2.3.1)

\widetilde{Z}(w,\underline{\Phi},s_{1},s_{2})\coloneqq\lim_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\to(s_{1},s_{2})}\frac{Z(w,\underline{\Phi},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}{L(\pi\times\chi_{2}^{-1},\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2})L(\pi,\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2})}

is a well-defined and non-zero map

Lemma 2.3.2
.

For any
h^{\prime}\in H(F)
we have

(2.3.2a)

\widetilde{Z}\left(\iota(h^{\prime})\cdot w,h^{\prime}\cdot\underline{\Phi},s_{1},s_{2}\right)=\left|\det h^{\prime}\right|^{-(s_{1}+s_{2})}\widetilde{Z}(w,\underline{\Phi},s_{1},s_{2});

and if
\underline{a}=\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}&0\\
0&a_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{2}&0\\
0&a_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)
, for

, then

(2.3.2b)

\widetilde{Z}(w,\underline{a}\cdot\underline{\Phi},s_{1},s_{2})=\frac{\widetilde{Z}(w,\underline{\Phi},s_{1},s_{2})}{|a_{1}|^{2s_{1}}\chi_{1}(a_{1})\,|a_{2}|^{2s_{2}}\chi_{2}(a_{2})}.

Proof.

From the formulae defining

. So if the integral defining

is convergent (which is true for

implies that
\sigma
has a unique generic irreducible constituent
\sigma_{0}
, and this appears as a subrepresentation; it is the image of
\mathcal{S}_{0}(F^{2})\otimes\mathcal{S}_{0}(F^{2})
in
\sigma
. So we can consider the restriction map

\Hom_{H(F)}\left(\pi\otimes\sigma,\mathbf{C}\right)\longrightarrow\Hom_{H(F)}\left(\pi\otimes\sigma_{0},\mathbf{C}\right).

The space
\Hom_{H(F)}\left(\pi\otimes\sigma_{0},\mathbf{C}\right)
has dimension
\leqslant 1
, by the aforementioned result of Emory–Takeda.

The restriction of the zeta-integral to
\mathcal{S}_{0}(F^{2})\otimes\mathcal{S}_{0}(F^{2})
is analysed in detail in
[
Loe24
]
, building on the works of Rösner–Weissauer cited above. These results show that
\widetilde{Z}
vanishes on
\pi\otimes\sigma_{0}
if and only if
s=s_{1}+s_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}
is a
subregular pole
for the two-parameter zeta-integral, in the sense of
[
Loe24
, Definition 4.8]
– equivalently, there exists a choice of Bessel model for which it is a subregular pole for the Bessel zeta integral in the sense of
[
RW18
]
. A case-by-case enumeration carried out in §5 of
[
RW18
]
shows that if
s_{1}+s_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}
is not a subregular pole, then in fact
\Hom_{H(F)}\left(\pi\otimes\sigma/\sigma_{0},\mathbf{C}\right)=0
, so the above restriction map is bijective. So we need to show that if
\chi_{\pi}
is not a square, then
s_{1}+s_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}
cannot be a subregular pole.

Every generic irreducible representation of
\GSp_{4}(F)
is either supercuspidal, or one of the Sally–Tadic types {Ia,
\dots
, XIa} (classified in
[
ST93
]
; see Appendix A of
[
RS07
]
for summary tables). There is nothing to prove in the supercuspidal cases or for types VII, VIIIa, IXa, since the
L
-factors for these representations have no poles at all (and a subregular pole is
a fortiori
a pole). For types I and X, if
\pi
is tempered, then all poles of its
L
-factor have imaginary part 0, while
\operatorname{Re}(s_{1}+s_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2})\leqslant-\tfrac{1}{2}
, so
s_{1}+s_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}
cannot be a pole. Types IIa, IVa, Va, VIa and XIa cannot arise, since for such representations the central character is always a square. This leaves only type IIIa; but it is shown in
[
RW18
]
that
L
-factors for type IIIa representations never have any subregular poles.
∎

As a by-product of the proof we also obtain the following corollaries:

Corollary 2.3.4

If

is a tempered irreducible principal series representation,
\chi_{i}
are unitary characters, and
(s_{1},s_{2})
have real parts

If

is a square, the

are unitary, and

Proof.

This follows from a careful study of the arguments of
[

. In §1.2 of

a multiplicity

is defined for smooth representations

and
\sigma

respectively, where

are integers with

and

The proposition in §1.3 of

gives a formula for this multiplicity, assuming that one or both of

Now let

Proposition 2.4.1
.

The restriction map

\Hom_{H(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}})}\left(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}\times\Sigma_{\mathrm{f}},\mathbf{C}\right)\longrightarrow\Hom_{H(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}})}\left(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}\times\Sigma_{\mathrm{f}}^{(0)},\mathbf{C}\right)

Proof.

We follow essentially the same argument as
[
HS01

, which is an analogous statement for Kato’s Euler system. We know that there exist many primes

to

on

on the whole of
\Sigma_{\mathrm{f}}^{(0)}
.
∎

2.5.
Fields of definition

In our global applications,
\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}
will be the finite part of an automorphic representation whose Archimedean component is determined by a pair of integers
r_{1}\geqslant r_{2}\geqslant 0
; and the twist

for integers

Since

is definable over

\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}^{\prime})=\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}^{\prime})_{E}\otimes_{E}\mathbf{C}
, where
\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}^{\prime})_{E}\subset\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}^{\prime})
is the space of

[
LPSZ21
, Definition 10.2]
.

Proposition 2.5.1
.

If

, and
E
contains the values of the
\chi_{i}
and also an
N
-th root of unity where

is the conductor of

, then
\widetilde{Z}
is the base-extension to
\mathbf{C}
of a linear functional

\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}^{\prime})_{E}\otimes\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}\times\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2},E)\to E.

Proof.

An elementary check, using the definition of the zeta-integral and the
E
-structure on the Whittaker model, shows that if

and

We can dispense with the need to introduce

3.1.
Automorphic representations

We recall some properties of automorphic representations for
\GSp_{4}
, following
[
LSZ22
, §10.1]
. We fix a Dirichlet character
\chi

Endoscopic classification

The automorphic representations contributing to the discrete spectrum
L^{2}_{\mathrm{disc}}(G,\chi)
have been classified by Arthur and Gee–Taïbi: see
[
Art04
]
for an overview, Conjecture 2.5.6 of
[
GT19
]
for a precise statement for arbitrary GSpin groups, and Theorem 7.4.1 for its proof in the case of
\operatorname{GSpin}_{5}=\GSp_{4}
. They can be partitioned into global packets, one for each “Arthur parameter”
\tau

\chi

\GL_{1}
and
\GL_{2}
satisfying certain conditions. We shall be interested in the case of
semisimple
parameters
\tau

•

a single
\chi

\GL_{4}
(“general type”);

•

the formal sum
\pi\boxplus\pi^{\prime}
of two distinct cuspidal automorphic representations of
\GL_{2}
, both of central character
\chi
(“Yoshida type”).

The corresponding global packet
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\tau}
is a product of local packets
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\tau_{v}}
of
G(\mathbf{Q}_{v})
-representations (and these local packets are singletons for almost all
v
). In the general-type case, every element in the global packet is automorphic, and has multiplicity 1 in the discrete spectrum.

In the Yoshida case, only “half” of these representations are automorphic. More precisely, for each place
v
, a choice of representation
\Pi_{v}\in\widetilde{\Pi}_{\tau_{v}}
determines a character
\xi_{\Pi_{v}}
of a certain centralizer group
\mathcal{S}_{\tau}
isomorphic to
\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
(notations as in §5 of
[
Art04
]
); and the product
\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\bigotimes}_{v}\Pi_{v}
is automorphic iff
\prod_{v}\xi_{v}=1
(and has multiplicity 1 in this case). This is an instance of Arthur’s multiplicity formula (Theorem 7.4.1 of
[
GT19
]
).

In either case, each local packet contains a unique generic representation (whose associated character
\xi_{\Pi_{v}}
is trivial); and the tensor product of these locally-generic representations is automorphic and globally generic (a
a priori
stronger condition than being everywhere locally generic). See Remark 7.4.7 of
[
GT19
]
.

(1)

\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\mu_{2}\nu_{2}=1
and
\rho=\nu_{1}\nu_{2}
, the torus Whittaker integral is simply the integral of the constant function 1 over

, so it is 1. Similarly, for test data of the form

we again obtain that the integral is 1. In particular, the values for “
\mathrm{dep}\times\mathrm{dep}
” and “
\mathrm{dep}\times\mathrm{crit}
” test data are equal.

On the other hand, the Whittaker function of

is zero at
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
unless

, so if we consider test data of the form
\langle p\rangle^{-1}\varphi\cdot\Phi_{\mathrm{crit},\nu_{1}}\times\Phi_{\mathrm{dep},\mu_{2},\nu_{2}}
, then we are integrating the product of a function supported on
p\mathbf{Z}_{p}
and another supported on
\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Hence the zeta integral is 0.

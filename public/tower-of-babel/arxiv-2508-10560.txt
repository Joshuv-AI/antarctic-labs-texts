Enhanced multiphoton ionization driven by quantum light
V. P. Kosheleva, 1 S. Panahiyan, 1,2 A. Rubio, 1,3 and F. Schlawin 1,2,4

Abstract
Recent advances in nanoscale and microscale photon-pair sources have enabled quantum-light–matter interactions in regimes where standard approximations, such as the paraxial limit, break down. Identifying quantum advantages in these domains requires a unified, first-principles framework that treats arbitrary quantum states of light and realistic material systems without restrictive assumptions. Here, we present a fully relativistic, beyond-paraxial theory of multiphoton ionization driven by quantum light, incorporating the full spatial structure and correlations of the field alongside material properties. As a case study, we apply the formalism to resonance-enhanced multiphoton ionization (REMPI) of neutral sodium atoms by momentum-entangled photons. We predict cross-section enhancements of several orders of magnitude compared to coherent light, arising entirely from non-paraxial effects and vanishing in the paraxial limit. The enhancement is strongly channel-dependent, with odd multipole transitions benefiting most from favorable parity and interference conditions. The predicted enhancement is accessible with current state-of-the-art photonic and atomic technologies, highlighting a promising avenue for exploiting quantum light in high-precision ionization and spectroscopy experiments.

The generalization to beyond the paraxial approximation provides a fundamentally new perspective on the interplay between spatial focusing and crystal parameters. This highlights the critical role of beyond-paraxial effects in accurately capturing the physics of high-resolution light-matter interactions, as well as parameter regimes where enhancement in the cross section due to non-classical light field can be obtained.

Beyond-Paraxial: enhancement in different ionization channels.—

Having established the presence of enhancement in the dipole channel, a natural question is whether a similar enhancement occurs in other ionization channels, and if so, is it at comparable levels? To address this, we present the results shown in Fig.

We find that the most significant enhancement in REMPI occurs for ultrathin crystals (

>10^{4}

Among the various ionization channels, the dipole transition exhibits the most pronounced enhancement in the cross-section, followed by the octupole (see Fig.

Symmetry of the ionization channels.—

In Fig.
2
, we see that dipole and octupole channels have more pronounced enhancement in their cross-sections compared to quadrupole and hexadecapole. The root of this difference can be traced to the interplay between the material properties (ionization channels) and photonic properties.

Given that the ionization process takes place via a resonant intermediate state, symmetry considerations, particularly parity, play a crucial role in determining which ionization pathways are favored or suppressed. Specifically, the efficiency of enhancement in resonant ionization depends on how strongly the intermediate state couples both to the ground state and to the final continuum. If these couplings are weakened due to parity mismatches or suppressed matrix elements, the overall enhancement is reduced.

The ground state
S_{1/2}
has even (positive) parity. Intermediate
P_{3/2}
and
F_{5/2}
states possess odd (negative) parity, while
D_{5/2}
and
G_{7/2}
states have even parity. The final state of the photoelectron can be represented as a sum of partial waves. The dominant contribution to the total cross section typically comes from partial waves allowed by dipole selection rules. Since the electric dipole operator changes parity, the parity of the dipole allowed partial wave will be inverted relative to the intermediate state. Consequently, for ionization through
P_{3/2}
and
F_{5/2}
intermediate states (dipole and octupole channels, respectively), the final state will have even parity, matching that of the ground state. In contrast, for transitions via
D_{5/2}
and
G_{7/2}
states (quadrupole and hexadecapole channels, respectively), the final continuum state will have odd parity, opposite to that of the ground state. These mismatches can suppress the transition amplitude due to symmetry constraints or destructive interference.

We note that the degree to which momentum correlations of entangled photons couple to the spatial symmetry of a given multipole ionization channel crucially depends on the length of the crystal
L
.
Thus, at short crystal lengths, where the angular range for signal and idler photons is broad, odd multipole transitions
with parity-favored pathways (dipole and octupole) exhibit
significantly enhanced coupling, resulting in the highest
enhancement.
As the crystal length increases, the angular range for signal and idler photons narrows, reducing
the distinctiveness between channels and diminishing the
enhancement differences across multipole orders, which is
what we observed in Fig.
2
.

Finally, in Fig.
3
, we have plotted the total cross-section of REMPI with entangled photons for different channels as a function of the crystal length. We observe that the total cross section has a descending feature as a function of the channels very similarly to the case of convential light. Meaning that the highest total cross-section belongs to the dipole transition, followed by the quadrupole, octupole, and hexadecapole, respectively.

Experimental feasibility.—

Recent advances in nonlinear optics have made the generation of non-paraxial entangled photons experimentally feasible, particularly through the use of ultrathin nonlinear crystals. Photon-pair sources based on materials with subwavelength thicknesses, thinner than the wavelength of the down-converted photons, have been successfully realized
Okoth et al. 2019
;
Santiago-Cruz
et al. 2021a
;
Santiago-Cruz
et al. 2021b
;
Vega et al. 2022
. These sources enable access to momentum components that lie far beyond the paraxial regime, allowing the creation of highly correlated momentum-entangled photons with well-defined spatial structure. Such capabilities are essential for probing non-paraxial effects in multiphoton ionization processes and they confirm that the parameter regimes we investigate are accessible using current photonic technologies. They not only support the practical feasibility of our proposed scheme but also underscore the growing need for theoretical models that move beyond the paraxial approximation in quantum light–matter interactions.

Conclusion.—

In this Letter, we have presented a fully relativistic ab initio formalism for computing total multiphoton ionization cross sections induced by arbitrary light fields, including both classical (coherent) and non-classical states. To showcase the capabilities of our approach, we investigated resonant two-photon ionization of neutral sodium atoms by coherent and momentum-entangled photons in the beyond-paraxial regime. Our analysis revealed that entangled photons can enhance the ionization cross-section by several orders of magnitude compared to their coherent counterparts, an enhancement that is absent or negligible within the paraxial approximation. This demonstrates that beyond-paraxial effects are not merely corrections but can fundamentally alter the underlying physics of ionization processes involving entangled light.

Moreover, we identified a strong dependence of the enhancement on the symmetry properties of the ionization channels. Specifically, channels associated with odd multipole transitions (dipole and octupole) exhibit significantly greater enhancement than even multipole transitions (quadrupole and hexadecapole). This behavior stems from the parity of the intermediate and final states.

Our fully relativistic and beyond paraxial framework provides a general and versatile theoretical tool for interpreting and guiding contemporary experimental efforts in multiphoton ionization and quantum light–matter interactions.
Crucially, it enables the treatment of arbitrary materials, including those containing heavy elements where relativistic effects are significant, and applies to both long and short crystals, thereby extending its relevance to a broad class of realistic systems.
In particular, our findings demonstrate that the often-overlooked regime beyond the paraxial approximation is where entanglement-induced enhancements become most pronounced. This suggests that the full metrological and dynamical potential of entangled photons can only be realized in this beyond-paraxial domain. Finally, we found that the symmetry of ionization channels offers new pathways for controlling multiphoton ionization processes, with implications for coherent control, photoelectron angular distributions, and attosecond ionization dynamics in systems where multipole interference can be selectively manipulated.

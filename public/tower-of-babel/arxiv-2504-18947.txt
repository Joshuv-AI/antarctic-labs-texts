UNIQUENESS OF HAHN-BANACH EXTENSIONS IN LOCALLY CONVEX SPACES
Sainik Karak and Akshay Kumar and Tanmoy Paul

Abstract
. We intend to study the uniqueness of the Hahn-Banach extensions of linear functionals on a subspace in locally convex spaces. Various characterizations are derived when a subspace Y Y has an analogous version of property-U (introduced by Phelps) in a locally convex space, referred to as the property-SNP. We characterize spaces where every subspace has this property. It is demonstrated that a subspace M M of a Banach space E E has property-U if and only if the subspace M M of the locally convex space E E endowed with the weak topology has the property-SNP, mentioned above. This investigation circles around exploring the potential connections between the family of seminorms and the unique extension of functionals previously mentioned. We extensively studied this property on the spaces of continuous functions on Tychonoff spaces endowed with the topology of pointwise convergence.

The Hahn-Banach extension theorem for locally convex spaces (in short LCS) is well known (see

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be an LCS and
\tau
be induced by a directed family
\mathcal{P}
of seminorms. Let
\rho\in\mathcal{P}
and

\widetilde{f}\in X^{*}
is said to be a Hahn-Banach extension (in short HBE) of

on

\widetilde{f}|_{Y}=f
and
\chi_{\rho}^{Y}(f)=\chi_{\rho}^{X}(\widetilde{f})

In general, a pair

may not have a unique HBE in

, that is, there may exist two distinct HBEs
\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2}\in X^{*}

. This leads us to define the following.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be an LCS such that
\tau
is induced by a directed family
\mathcal{P}
of seminorms.

(a)

We call a subspace
Y
of
(X,\tau)
has
seminorm preserving property
(SNP) if it satisfies anyone of the following equivalent conditions.

(
i
)

For
f\in Y^{*}
, there exists a
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
with
\chi_{\mu}^{Y}(f)<\infty
such that all the pairs
(f,\rho)
for
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
have a unique HBE
\widetilde{f}\in X^{*}
.

(
ii
)

For
f\in Y^{*}
, there exists a
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
such that for every
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
, both the pairs
(f,\rho_{1})
and
(f,\rho_{2})
have a unique HBE
\widetilde{f}\in X^{*}
.

(b)

We call
Y
has
uniformly seminorm preserving property
(USNP) if it satisfies anyone of the following equivalent conditions.

(

)

For

and
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{f}

(f,\rho)
have a unique HBE
\widetilde{f}\in X^{*}
.

(

)

For
f\in Y^{*}

\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{f}

(f,\rho_{1})
and
(f,\rho_{2})

\widetilde{f}\in X^{*}
.

Proposition 3.1

Let

\mathcal{F}=\{\rho_{F}:F\subseteq B_{E^{*}}\mbox{\penalty\ is finite}\}

Assume that

∎

4.
Illustrative Examples

Our first example shows that for a locally convex space
X
, and its subspace
Y
it is possible that
Y_{lcs}^{\#}=\{0\}
.

Example 4.1
.

Consider the LCS
(\mathbb{R}^{2},\tau)
, where
\tau
is induced by the directed family
\mathcal{P}=\{\rho_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of the seminorms given by the following.

\rho_{n}((x,y))=\Bigg\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n(|x|+|y|)&\text{ if $n=2^{m}$ for some $m$}\\
n(|x|+\frac{|y|}{2})&\text{ if $n$ is odd}\\
n\max\{|x|,|y|\}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}

Consider
Y=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x=y\}
and
f\in Y^{*}
, where
f(x,x)=kx
for some non-zero
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
and
\phi\in X^{*}
be an HBE of the pair
(f,\mu)
. Let
\mu=\rho_{n_{0}}
.
Hence we have
\phi|_{Y}=f
and
\chi_{\rho}^{Y}(f)=\chi_{\rho}^{X}(\phi)<\infty
for all
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
. We can assume that
\phi(x,y)=\alpha x+\beta y
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\phi|_{Y}=f
, we have
\alpha+\beta=k
.

Step 1:
Take
n\geq n_{0}
such that
n=2^{m}
for some
m
, and
\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
. Then

\frac{|k|}{2n}=\chi_{\rho_{n}}^{Y}(f)=\chi_{\rho_{n}}^{X}(\phi)=\frac{1}{n}\max\{|\alpha|,|\beta|\}.

Consequently,
\alpha=\beta=\frac{k}{2}
. Thus,
\phi(x,y)=\frac{k}{2}(x+y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.

Step 2:
Take
n\geq n_{0}
such that
n
is odd and
\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
. Then

\frac{|2k|}{3n}=\chi_{\rho_{n}}^{Y}(f)=\chi_{\rho_{n}}^{X}(\phi)\geq\phi\left(\left(\frac{1}{2n},\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)=\frac{3|k|}{4n}.

Which is not possible. This shows that
Y^{\#}_{\mu}=\{0\}
. Consequently,
Y_{lcs}^{\#}=\{0\}
.

Step3:
For every even
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\neq 2^{m}
for any
m
, the pair
(f,\rho_{n})
have two distinct HBEs
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
in
X^{*}
given by

\phi_{1}((x,y))=kx,\mbox{ and }\phi_{2}((x,y))=\frac{k}{2}(x+y).

Hence,
Y
does not have the property-SNP though
Y_{lcs}^{\#}
is linear.

For the remaining of this section we consider
Z
as a Tychonoff space where the LCS
(C_{p}(Z),\tau)
represents the topology of pointwise convergence on the set of all continuous functions on
Z
. It is well-known that
\tau
is induced by the directed family

\mathcal{P}=\left\{\rho_{F}:F\subseteq Z\text{ is finite}\right\}

of seminorms, where
\rho_{F}(f)=\max_{x\in F}|f(x)|
for all
f\in C_{p}(Z)
.

Example 4.2
.

Consider the subspace
Y=\{f\in C_{p}(Z):f|_{A}=0\}
. Now, suppose
(\phi,\rho_{P})
is any pair on
Y
for some
\rho_{P}\in\mathcal{P}
and
\phi(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f(x_{i})
for all
f\in Y
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that all
x_{i}\notin[0,1]
and no
\alpha_{i}=0
. Since
\chi_{\rho_{P}}^{Y}(\phi)<\infty
, each
x_{i}\in P
. Consider
\widetilde{\phi}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f(x_{i})
for all
f\in C_{p}(Z)
. Note that
\chi_{\rho_{P}}^{Y}(\phi)=\chi_{\rho_{P}}^{X}(\widetilde{\phi})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\alpha_{i}|
and
\widetilde{\phi}|_{Y}=\phi
. Let
\psi\in(C_{p}(Z))^{*}
is an extension of the pair
(\phi,\rho_{P})
. Assume that
\psi=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}f(y_{j})
for all
f\in C_{p}(Z)
. Then
y_{j}\in P
for all
j
as
\chi_{\rho_{P}}^{X}(\psi)<\infty
.

Step 1:
We prove that each
x_{i}\in\{y_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}
. If
x_{i}\notin\{y_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}
for some
i
, then we can find an
f\in C_{p}(Z)
such that
f|_{A\cup\{y_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}}=0
and
f(x_{i})\neq 0
. Consequently,
0\neq\phi(f)=\psi(f)=0
. Which is not possible.

Step 2:
If for some
k
,
y_{k}\notin A
is distinct from all
x_{i}
and
\beta_{k}\neq 0
, then we can find an
f\in C_{p}(Z)
such that
f|_{A\cup\{y_{j}:j\neq k\}\cup\{x_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}}=0
and
f(y_{k})=1
. Which implies that
\beta_{k}=\psi(f)=\phi(f)=0
. Which is not possible. Therefore,
y_{k}=x_{i}
for some
i
and
\beta_{k}=\psi(f)=\phi(f)=\alpha_{i}
.

Step 3:
If
y_{j}\in A
and
\beta_{j}\neq 0
for some
j
, then
\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\beta_{j}|>\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\alpha_{i}|
by using Step 1 and Step 2. Which is not possible as
\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\beta_{j}|=\chi_{\rho_{P}}^{X}(\psi)=\chi_{\rho_{P}}^{Y}(\phi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\alpha_{i}|
. Consequently,
\psi=\widetilde{\phi}
. Hence,
Y
has the property-USNP in
C_{p}(Z)
.

We next provide an example of a subspace that does not have the property-SNP.

Example 4.3
.

Suppose that
A\subseteq Z
is not empty and
Y
be as above. It is well known that the directed family
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{\rho_{F}:F\subseteq Z\text{ is finite}\}
induces the topology of the LCS
X=C_{p}(Z)
, where
\rho_{F}(f)=\sum_{x\in F}|f(x)|
for all
f\in C(Z)
. We show that
Y
does not have the property-SNP. Let
\phi(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f(x_{i})
for all
f\in Y
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that all
\alpha_{i}
are non-zero and
x_{i}\notin A
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
\rho_{F}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. Clearly,
\chi_{\rho_{F}}^{Y}(\phi)<\infty
if and only if all
x_{i}\in F
. Note that
\chi_{\rho_{F}}^{Y}(\phi)=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|\alpha_{i}|=|\alpha_{k}|
(say). Consider
P=F\cup\{z\}
for some
z\in A
. Clearly,
\rho_{P}\succeq\rho_{F}
and
\chi_{\rho_{P}}^{Y}(\phi)=|\alpha_{k}|
. Consider

\widetilde{\phi}_{1}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f(x_{i})\text{ for all }f\in C(Z),\text{ and }

\widetilde{\phi}_{2}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f(x_{i})+\frac{|\alpha_{k}|}{2}f(z)\text{ for all }f\in C(Z).

Observe that
\chi_{\rho_{P}}^{X}(\widetilde{\phi}_{1})=\chi_{\rho_{P}}^{X}(\widetilde{\phi}_{2})=|\alpha_{k}|
and
\widetilde{\phi}_{1}|_{Y_{A}}=\widetilde{\phi}_{2}|_{Y_{A}}=\phi
. Therefore, the pair
(\phi,\rho_{P})
has two distinct extensions. Hence,
Y_{A}
does not have the property SNP.

Example 4.4
.

Suppose that
A\subseteq Z
is nonempty. Let us consider the following generating family of seminorms

\mathcal{P}=\{\rho_{F}:F\subseteq Z\text{ is finite}\}\cup\{\mu_{F}:F\subseteq Z\text{ is finite}\}

for the LCS
C_{p}(Z)
, where
\rho_{F}(f)=|F|\max_{x\in F}|f(x)|,
and
\mu_{F}(f)=|F|\sum_{x\in F}|f(x)|
for all
f\in C(Z)
, here
|F|
represents the cardinality of the set
F
. Let
Y
be as above. Suppose
\phi\in(C_{p}(Z))^{*}
. Assume that
\phi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
with
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\in A
and
x_{k+1},\cdots,x_{n}\in Z\setminus A
. Consider
\phi_{1}=\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
and
\phi_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
. Note that for
F=\{x_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
,
\phi_{1}\in Y^{\#}_{\rho_{F}}
and
\phi_{2}\in Y^{\perp}
with
\phi=\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}
. It is similar to Example
4.3
that for
z\in A
and
F_{1}\supseteq F
, the pair
(\phi|_{Y},\mu_{F_{1}})
has two distinct extensions
\phi_{1}
and

\psi_{1}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f(x_{i})+rf(z)\text{ for all }f\in C(Z),

where
r=\max\{|\alpha_{i}|:k+1\leq i\leq n\}
. Thus,
Y
does not have the property-SNP. It is similar to Example
4.2
that if
\psi_{2}\in(C_{p}(Z))^{*}
is an extension of the pair
(\phi|_{Y},\rho_{F_{2}})
for some
F_{2}\supseteq F
, then
\psi_{2}
has to be equal to
\phi_{1}
. Hence, every member of
(C_{p}(Z))^{*}
can be written as a unique sum of members of
Y^{\#}
and
Y^{\perp}
.

We now discuss the property-SNP for one-dimensional subspaces of
C_{p}(Z)
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose
X=C_{p}(Z)
and
f_{0}\in X
such that
f_{0}
does not attain its supremum value. Then
Y
= span
(f_{0})
does not have the property-SNP with respect to
\mathcal{P}=\{\rho_{F}:F\subseteq Z\text{ is finite}\}
, where
\rho_{F}(f)=\max_{x\in F}|f(x)|
for all
f\in C(Z)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
f_{0}(x_{0})=1
for some
x_{0}\in Z
. Take any
F
=
\{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}\}

\subseteq Z
. Assume that
|f_{0}(x_{1})|\leq|f_{0}(x_{2})|\leq...\leq|f_{0}(x_{n})|=a\neq 0
(otherwise, add more points in
F
so that
a\neq 0
). Choose now
x_{n+1}
such that
1<|f_{0}(x_{n+1})|
and
|f_{0}(x_{n+1})|>|f_{0}(x_{n})|
(this is possible as
f_{0}
is not attaining its supremum). Let
F^{\prime}=\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n},x_{n+1}\}
and
|f_{0}(x_{n+1})|=b
. We show that the pairs
(\delta_{x_{0}},\rho_{F})
and
(\delta_{x_{0}},\rho_{F^{\prime}})
on
Y
have two distinct extensions
\phi
and
\psi
, respectively, where
\phi=sgn\big(f_{0}(x_{n})\big)\frac{\delta_{x_{n}}}{a}
and
\psi=sgn\big(f_{0}(x_{n+1})\big)\frac{\delta_{x_{n+1}}}{b}
. Clearly,
\phi|_{Y}=\psi|_{Y}=\delta_{x_{0}}
. Note that
|\delta_{x_{0}}(\alpha f_{0})|=|\alpha f_{0}(x_{0})|=|\alpha|\leq\frac{1}{a}
whenever
\rho_{F}(\alpha f_{0})\leq 1
. Since
\rho_{F}\left(\frac{f_{0}}{a}\right)=1
and
|\frac{f_{0}}{a}(x_{0})|=\frac{1}{a}
, we have
\chi_{\rho_{F}}^{Y}(\delta_{x_{0}})=\frac{1}{a}
. It is easy to prove that
\chi_{\rho_{F}}^{X}(\phi)=\frac{1}{a}
. Similarly, we can show that
\chi_{\rho_{F^{\prime}}}^{Y}(\delta_{x_{0}})=\chi_{\rho_{F^{\prime}}}^{X}(\psi)=\frac{1}{b}
. Which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
Y
=
span(f_{0})
be a subspace of
X=C_{p}(Z)
. Then
Y
has the property-SNP with respect to
\mathcal{P}=\{\rho_{F}:F\subseteq Z\text{ is finite}\}
if and only if
f_{0}
attains its supremum at exactly one point, where
\rho_{F}(f)=\max_{x\in F}|f(x)|
for all
f\in C(Z)
.

Proof.

Suppose
f_{0}
attains its supremum at exactly one point. Assume that
x_{0}\in Z
such that
\sup_{x\in Z}|f_{0}(x)|=f_{0}(x_{0})=1
. Since the dimension of
Y
is one, we have
Y^{*}
= span
(\delta_{x_{0}})
, where
\delta_{x_{0}}(f)=f(x_{0})
. It is now enough to find a finite set
F_{0}\subseteq Z
such that every pair
(\delta_{x_{0}},\rho_{F})
has a unique extension whenever
F_{0}\subseteq F
. Let
F_{0}=\{x_{0}\}
and let
F_{0}\subseteq F
be finite. Then
\chi_{\rho_{F}}^{X}(\delta_{x_{0}})=1
. Suppose
\widetilde{\phi}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
is any extension of the pair
(\delta_{x_{0}},\rho_{F^{\prime}})
. Observe that all
x_{i}\in F
as
\chi_{\rho_{F}}^{X}(\widetilde{\phi})<\infty
. Clearly,
|\widetilde{\phi}(f)|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\alpha_{i}|
whenever
\rho_{F}(f)\leq 1
. Consider now an
h\in C(Z)
such that
\|h\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and

h(x_{i})=\Big\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }\alpha_{i}>0\\
-1&\text{ if }\alpha_{i}<0.\end{array}

Then
\widetilde{\phi}(h)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\alpha_{i}|
. Since
\chi_{\rho_{F}}^{X}(\widetilde{\phi})=\chi_{\rho_{F}}^{X}(\delta_{x_{0}})=1
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\alpha_{i}|=1
.

Step 1:
We show that
x_{0}=x_{i}
for some
i
. Suppose
x_{i}\neq x_{0}
for all
i
. Then

\displaystyle 1=|f_{0}(x_{0})|

\displaystyle=|\delta_{x_{0}}(f_{0})|=|\widetilde{\phi}(f_{0})|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}f_{0}(x_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|f_{0}(x_{i})|\right)\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\alpha_{i}|

\displaystyle<1.1=1\text{ [since }f_{0}(x_{i})<1\text{ for all }x_{i}\neq x_{0}].

Step 2:
Assume that
x_{1}=x_{0}
. Next, we show that
f_{0}(x_{i})=0
for all
i>1
. Note that,

\displaystyle|\alpha_{1}|+\sum_{i=2}^{m}|\alpha_{i}|=1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}f_{0}(x_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\alpha_{i}||f_{0}(x_{i})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha_{1}||f_{0}(x_{1})|+\sum_{i=2}^{m}|\alpha_{i}||f_{0}(x_{i})|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle|\alpha_{1}|+\sum_{i=2}^{m}|\alpha_{i}|\text{ [since }|f_{0}(x_{i})|<1\text{ for all }i\neq 1]

Hence,
f_{0}(x_{i})=0
for all
i\geq 2
. This implies
\widetilde{\phi}(f_{0})=f_{0}(x_{0})=\delta_{x_{0}}(f)
. Hence,
Y
has the property-SNP.

Conversely, if
Y
has the property-SNP, then by Proposition
4.5
,
f_{0}
must achieve its supremum. Suppose that there exist two points
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
such that
\|f\|_{\infty}=|f(x_{1})|=|f(x_{2})|=1
. Then the pair
(\delta_{x_{1}},\rho_{F})
has two distinct extensions
\delta_{x_{1}}
and
\delta_{x_{2}}
(or
-\delta_{x_{2}}
if
f(x_{2})=-f(x_{1})
) for all
F\supseteq\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
. Hence, the result holds.
∎

If the dimension of
Y
is two, then we only have a necessary condition.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
Y
be a two-dimensional subspace of
X=C_{p}(Z)
which has the property-SNP. Then any non-zero element of
Y
cannot attain its supremum on more than two points.

Proof.

Let
Y
= span
\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
, where
f_{1},f_{2}\in C_{p}(Z)
. Also, assume that there exists
x_{1},x_{2}\text{ and }x_{3}\in Z
with
|f_{1}(x_{1})|=|f_{1}(x_{2})|=|f_{1}(x_{3})|=\sup\limits_{x\in Z}|f_{1}(x)|=1
.

Case 1:
Suppose that
|f_{2}(x_{1})|=|f_{2}(x_{2})|
.

Case 1.1:
When
f_{1}(x_{1})=f_{1}(x_{2})
and
f_{2}(x_{1})=f_{2}(x_{2})
.

Then for any
F\supseteq\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
, we have
\delta_{x_{1}}
and
\delta_{x_{2}}
two distinct extensions of
(\delta_{x_{1}},\rho_{F})
. Hence, this case cannot occur.

Case 1.2:
When
f_{1}(x_{1})=-f_{1}(x_{2})
and
f_{2}(x_{1})=-f_{2}(x_{2})
.

Then for any
F\supseteq\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
, we have
\delta_{x_{1}}
and
-\delta_{x_{2}}
two distinct extensions of
(\delta_{x_{1}},\rho_{F})
. Hence, this case cannot occur.

Case 1.3:
When
f_{1}(x_{1})=-f_{1}(x_{2})
and
f_{2}(x_{1})=f_{2}(x_{2})
.

This condition ensures that,
\delta_{x_{1}}
and
\delta_{x_{2}}
are linearly independent.

Case 1.4:
When
f_{1}(x_{1})=f_{1}(x_{2})
and
f_{2}(x_{1})=-f_{2}(x_{2})
.

This condition ensures that,
\delta_{x_{1}}
and
\delta_{x_{2}}
are linearly independent.

Case 2:
Suppose that
|f_{2}(x_{1})|\neq|f_{2}(x_{2})|
.

This condition ensures that,
\delta_{x_{1}}
and
\delta_{x_{2}}
are linearly independent.

It now remains to prove that if
\delta_{x_{1}}
and
\delta_{x_{2}}
are linearly independent, then
Y
does not have the property-SNP. If this holds, then
Y^{*}
= span
(\delta_{x_{1}},\delta_{x_{2}})
. So, there exists
a,b\neq 0
such that
\delta_{x_{3}}=a\delta_{x_{1}}+b\delta_{x_{2}}
. Now, for any
F\supseteq\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
,
\delta_{x_{3}}
and
a\delta_{x_{1}}+b\delta_{x_{2}}
are two distinct extensions of
(\delta_{x_{3}},\rho_{F})
. Hence, the result follows.∎

We do not know whether an analogous result to Proposition
4.7
is true if
Y
is a finite co-dimensional subspace of
X
. In
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
, Phelps derived the similar result in Banach spaces, we need suitable techniques to implement these ideas in locally convex spaces.

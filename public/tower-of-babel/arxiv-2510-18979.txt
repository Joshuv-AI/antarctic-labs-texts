Local boundedness of weak solutions to elliptic equations under unbalanced Orlicz growths
Gabriele Giannone

Abstract
. We establish sufficient conditions for the local boundedness of weak solutions to a broad class of nonlinear elliptic equations in divergence form, under unbalanced growth conditions on the stress field. Our analysis is carried out in a non-variational setting, with no symmetry or structural assumptions on the operator. The ellipticity and growth are prescribed via distinct Young functions, leading to a Orlicz-type setting that captures a wide class of nonstandard behaviors. As a special case, the theory encompasses and extends the known results on equations with p , q p,q -growth.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we provide a complete proof of the local boundedness of weak solutions to second order elliptic equations in divergence form of the type

(1)

-\Div(\A(x,\nabla u))=f(x,u)\quad\text{in}\ \Omega,

where
\Omega
is an open set in
\R^{n}
, with
n\geq 2
, and
\A\colon\Omega\times\R^{n}\to\R^{n}
and
f\colon\Omega\times\R\to\R
are Carathéodory maps that satisfy proper growth conditions.

Local boundedness of weak solutions to the equation in (
1
) is guaranteed if
\A
is subject to lower and upper bounds — named
p
-growth or natural conditions — of the form

(2)

\displaystyle\A(x,\xi)\cdot\xi\gtrsim|\xi|^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad|\A(x,\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{p-1}\quad\text{for a\text{.}e\text{.}\ }x\in\Omega\ \text{and all }\xi\in\R^{n},

for some exponent
p>1
. A result of this kind can be found in
[
Tru67
]
, where, in the spirit of
[
Mos61
]
, an approach based on Harnack type inequalities is adopted. The local Lipschitz continuity of weak solutions is proved in Chapter
4
of the classical reference book
[
LU68
]
, employing techniques that, in turn, rely upon methods introduced by De Giorgi
[
De 57
]
in his regularity theory for linear elliptic equations with merely measurable coefficients.

A regularity theory for elliptic equations whose stress field exhibits different growth rates from below and above — commonly known as
p,q
-growth — originates in the work
[
Kol70
]
, which deals with the local boundedness of solutions to certain anisotropic equations. In the literature on this topic, a typical assumption is that (
1
) arises as the Euler–Lagrange equation of a variational integral of the form

\mathscr{F}(u,\Omega)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

with

|\xi|^{p}-1\lesssim\mathcal{L}(x,z,\xi)\lesssim|\xi|^{q}+1

for a
.
e
.

x\in\R
, every
z\in\R
, and every
\xi\in\R^{n}
. This corresponds to requiring that
\A_{i}(x,\xi)=\partial_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}(x,z,\xi)
, for
i=1,\dots,n
, and
f(x,z)=\partial_{z}\mathcal{L}(x,z,\xi)
. The case
p=q>1
is considered in the paper
[
GG84
]
, where, assuming the growth condition only, every quasi-minimizer is proved to be locally Hölder continuous. The study of regularity properties for local minimizers under unbalanced growth conditions began with the works
[
Mar89
,
Mar91
,
Mar93
]
, and has since received considerable attention. As it is well known, most results in the existing literature require some restrictions on the ratio
q/p
; typically, one assumes

\frac{q}{p}<c_{n},\ \text{for some constant}\ c_{n}=c_{n}(p,q)\ \text{such that }c_{n}\downarrow 1\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty.

Under various structure conditions on the lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
, the local boundedness of minimizers of such functionals is treated in
[
MN91
,
MP94
,
CMM15
]
, while similar questions are addressed in
[
BMS90
,
FS93
,
Str91
]
for problems with anisotropic growths. On the contrary, counterexamples in
[
Gia87
,
Mar87
,
Mar91
]
demonstrate the existence of unbounded minimizers when
p
and
q
are too far apart. More specifically, Theorem
6.1
in
[
Mar91
]
constructs unbounded solutions to the Euler-Lagrange equation of a variational integral with
p,q
-growth, under the assumption

q>\frac{(n-1)p}{n-1-p}=\vcentcolon p^{\star}_{n-1}.

The gap between the assumptions on
p
and
q
in these examples and in the regularity results available until recently has been filled in the paper
[
HS21
]
, where the local boundedness of minimizers is established for the full range
q\leq p^{\star}_{n-1}
.

Less common in the literature is the study of differential equations that are not the Euler first variation of an energy integral. Examples of such problems can be found, for instance, in
[
CMM24
]
. A distinctive feature of the non-variational setting, unlike the variational one, is that although Equation (
1
) remains elliptic and coercive in
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
under
p,q
-growth, the notion of weak solution is consistent only for functions that belong, a priori, to
W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. The issues of local Lipschitz continuity and
C^{\infty}
regularity of weak solutions are addressed in
[
Mar91
]
, whereas the boundedness of weak solutions to Dirichlet problems under anisotropic growth conditions is studied in
[
BMS90
]
. More recently, adapting the strategy of
[
HS21
]
, the authors of
[
CMM23
]
proved local boundedness of weak solutions under the balance condition

q<\frac{np}{n-1}.

In the present paper, without assuming any variational structure for the equation, we address the question of local boundedness of weak solutions under more general assumptions on the stress field: namely, unbalanced Orlicz-type growth conditions are imposed on
\A
. This means that the growth and ellipticity of
\A
are dictated by Young functions — i.e., non-negative convex functions vanishing at the origin. When the growth rate of the stress field is prescribed in terms of the same Young function, local boundedness results have been obtained in
[
Kor90
]
, and local Hölder continuity of bounded solutions is proved in
[
Lie91
]
. The boundedness of local minimizers for variational integrals with anisotropic Orlicz growths is treated in
[
Cia00
]
.

In contrast, we prescribe the ellipticity and growth of
\A
via two
different
Young functions
A
and
B
, with the model assumption:

\displaystyle\A(x,\xi)\cdot\xi\geq A(|\xi|)\quad\text{and}\quad|\A(x,\xi)|\leq b(|\xi|)\quad\text{for a\text{.}e\text{.}\ }x\in\Omega\ \text{and all }\xi\in\R^{n},

where
b(|\xi|)=B^{\prime}(|\xi|)
for a
.
e
.

\xi\in\R^{n}
. Natural conditions are also imposed on the reaction term
f(x,u)
. As in the polynomial-growth setting, the relevant notion of weak solution is well-posed only in the Orlicz-Sobolev class
W^{1}L^{B}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. The same problem for local quasi-minimizers of variational integrals under these assumptions has been studied in
[
CS24
]
, where a sharp balance condition between the Young functions
A
and
B
is exhibited. The results for functionals with
p,q
-growth emerge as a special case of this framework.

The interest in establishing local boundedness of weak solutions stems from the higher regularity theory, where local boundedness serves as a starting assumption to prove further interior regularity properties of
u
— including higher integrability, local Lipschitz continuity, local
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity, and beyond. In this regard, a strong motivation for our study lies in the paper
[
Mar93
]
, that, under pointwise unbalanced bounds on the matrix
(\partial_{\xi_{j}}\A_{i}(x,\xi))
of possibly non-polynomial rate, establishes the local Lipschitz continuity of any weak solution that is
assumed
to belong to
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. The crucial role of local boundedness results in higher regularity theory is discussed, for instance, in
[
PCM18
,
BB20
,
DM21
]
.

As mentioned above, beyond boundedness, many aspects of the regularity theory for nonlinear elliptic equations and systems with unbalanced growth conditions have been investigated. Without aiming for completeness, we refer to
[
ELM99
,
ELM04
,
CM15
,
PCM18
,
BB20
,
BM20
,
BS20
,
DM21
,
DM21a
,
DM23
,
BS24
]
, and point the reader to the surveys
[
MR21
,
Mar21
]
which outline recent developments and open problems in the field.

2.
Background

In this section, we recall some basic definition and property from the theory of Young functions, Orlicz spaces, and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces, with special attention to those results that play a crucial role in our proofs. We refer the reader to
[
RR91
,
RR02
]
for a comprehensive presentation of this theory.

2.1.
Young functions

A function
A\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is said to be a Young function if it is convex, left–continuous, neither identically zero nor infinity, and
A(0)=0
. This very definition implies that

t\mapsto\frac{A(t)}{t}

is increasing in
[0,\infty)
.

We note that
A
is allowed to jump to infinity, which is the only case in which the assumption of the left continuity is relevant. Every Young function
A
admits the representation

(3)

A(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{t}a(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{for all}\ t\in[0,\infty)

where
a\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is an increasing, left–continuous function such that
a(0)=0
. It holds that

(4)

A(t)\leq ta(t)\leq A(2t).

We call the function
a
the
left–derivative
of
A
. This terminology comes from the trivial observation that, in the interval where
A
is finite,

A(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{t}A^{\prime}_{-}(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

where
A^{\prime}_{-}(\cdot)
denotes the left–derivative of
A
, which exists finite at
s
as soon as
A(s)
is finite.

Throughout this paper, Young functions will be denoted by capital letters, and their left–derivatives by the corresponding lower case letters.

The Young conjugate of
any
function
\Phi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is the
convex
function given by

\widetilde{\Phi}(s)=\sup\{ts-\Phi(t):t\geq 0\}\quad\text{for all}\ t\in[0,\infty).

An immediate consequence of this definition is the so-called Young’s inequality

ts\leq\Phi(t)+\widetilde{\Phi}(s)\quad\text{for all}\ t,s\geq 0.

If
A
is a Young function, one can verify that

\widetilde{A}(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{t}a^{-1}(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{for}\ t\in[0,\infty)

where
a^{-1}
is the left–continuous inverse of
a
. Moreover, it holds that

(5)

\widetilde{A}(a(t))\leq ta(t)

and

(6)

t\leq A^{-1}(t)\widetilde{A}^{-1}(t)\leq 2t

for all
t\geq 0
, where
A^{-1}
and
\widetilde{A}^{-1}
denote the generalized right–continuous inverses of
A
and
\widetilde{A}
, respectively.

A Young function
A\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition globally — briefly
A\in\Delta_{2}
globally — if there exists a constant
c
such that

A(2t)\leq cA(t)\quad\text{for all}\ t\geq 0.

We say that
A
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition near infinity if the latter inequality holds for large
t
only. One can verify that

(7)

\displaystyle A\in\Delta_{2}\ \text{near infinity iff there exist}\ t_{0}\geq 0\ \text{and}\ q\geq 1\ \text{such that}\ \sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\frac{ta(t)}{A(t)}\leq q.

Now, we introduce a notion of index related to the asymptotic growth behavior of a Young function
A
. We define the lower Matuszewska-Orlicz index of
A
at infinity as

(8)

i_{\infty}(A)=\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\frac{\log\big(\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\frac{A(\lambda t)}{A(t)}\big)}{\log\lambda}.

The upper Matuszewska-Orlicz index of
A
at infinity is defined similarly to (
8
), but with
\liminf_{t\to+\infty}
replaced by
\limsup_{t\to+\infty}
. One has that

1\leq i_{\infty}(A)\leq I_{\infty}(A)\leq\infty.

A function
A
dominates another function
B
globally (respectively, near infinity) if there exists a positive constant
c
such that
B(t)\leq A(ct)
for all
t\geq 0
(respectively, for all
t\geq t_{0}\geq 0
). If this is the case, we write
A\lesssim B
globally (respectively, near infinity).
A
and
B
are said to be equivalent globally (respectively, near infinity) if
A
dominates
B
and vice versa globally (respectively, near infinity), and we write
A\approx B
globally (respectively, near infinity).

2.2.
Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

Let
A
be a Young function, and
\Omega
be a measurable set in
\R^{n}
. The Orlicz class
K^{A}(\Omega)
is defined by

K^{A}(\Omega)=\bigg\{u:u\ \text{is measurable in}\ \Omega\ \text{and}\ \int\limits_{\Omega}A(|u|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x<\infty\bigg\}.

The Orlicz space
L^{A}(\Omega)
built upon
A
is the linear span of
K^{A}(\Omega)
.
L^{A}(\Omega)
is a Banach function space, endowed with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{A}}=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\int\limits_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq 1\bigg\}.

K^{A}(\Omega)
is a linear space — that is,
K^{A}(\Omega)=L^{A}(\Omega)
— if and only if either
A\in\Delta_{2}
globally, or
A\in\Delta_{2}
near infinity and
|\Omega|<\infty
.

If
A(t)=t^{p}
for some
p\in[1,\infty)
, then
L^{A}(\Omega)=L^{p}(\Omega)
, whereas
L^{A}(\Omega)=L^{\infty}(\Omega)
if
A(t)=0
for
t\in[0,1]
and
A(t)=\infty
for
t\in(1,\infty)
.

The continuous embedding

L^{B}(\Omega)\longrightarrow L^{A}(\Omega)

holds if either
B
dominates
A
globally, or
B
dominates
A
near infinity and
|\Omega|<\infty
.

Assume now that
\Omega
is an open set. The (inhomogeneous) Orlicz-Sobolev class
W^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
is defined as

(9)

W^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)=\{u\in K^{A}(\Omega):|\nabla u|\in K^{A}(\Omega)\}.

Accordingly, the (inhomogeneous) Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1}L^{A}(\Omega)
is given by

(10)

W^{1}L^{A}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{A}(\Omega):|\nabla u|\in L^{A}(\Omega)\}.

The latter is a Banach space equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1}L^{A}}=\|u\|_{L^{A}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{A}}.

In the case when
L^{A}(\Omega)=L^{p}(\Omega)
for some
p\in[1,\infty]
, the standard Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is recovered.

The local versions
K^{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
,
L^{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
,
W^{1}K^{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, and
W^{1}L^{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
of these classes and spaces are obtained by modifying the above definitions as usual. Given a Young function
A
, membership of a function in the local Orlicz class
K^{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is not influenced by replacing
A
with any equivalent Young function near infinity. Indeed, as we have seen when we have proved that Definition
3.2
is well posed, the behavior of
A
near
0
is irrelevant whenever integrals over sets with finite measure are concerned.

As highlighted in the previous section, our approach exploits Orlicz-Sobolev classes of weakly differentiable functions defined on
\Sph^{n-1}
. These sets are denoted by
W^{1}K^{A}(\Sph^{n-1})
and are defined as in (
9
), with the Lebesgue measure replaced with the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
, and
\nabla u
replaced with
\nabla_{\Sph}u
, the weak covariant derivative of
u
on
\Sph^{n-1}
. For a rigorous treatment of Sobolev spaces on manifolds, see, e.g.,
[
Heb00
]
.

As (
30
), (
31
), and (
38
) show, sharp embedding theorems in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces play a crucial role in the formulation of Theorem
3.3
. Moreover, the proof of the latter relies heavily upon the corresponding sharp inequalities. As shown in
[
Cia97
]
— and, with an equivalent formulation, in
[
Cia96
]
— the optimal
n
-dimensional Sobolev conjugate of a Young function
A
fulfilling

\int\limits_{0}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t<\infty

is given by

(11)

A_{n}(t)=A(H_{n}^{-1}(t))\quad\text{for}\ t\geq 0,

where the function
H_{n}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is defined as

(12)

H_{n}(s)=\Big(\int_{0}^{s}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\quad\text{for}\ s\geq 0.

If
n\geq 3
, analogous formulae hold for the
(n-1)
-dimensional Sobolev conjugate of
A
(just replacing
n
with
n-1
in (
11
) and (
12
)).

If
\rho
is a positive radius, we denote the mean of
u
over the ball
\B_{\rho}=\B_{\rho}(0)
by

(u)_{\B_{\rho}}=\fint\limits_{\B_{\rho}}u\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}\rho^{n}}\int\limits_{\B_{\rho}}u\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

where
\omega_{n}
is the measure of the unit ball.

Both Sobolev-Poincaré and Sobolev inequalities on balls in
\R^{n}
are of use in our proof, and are the content of the next two results. For their proof, see Theorems
3.1
and
4.4
in
[
Cia06
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
, let
\rho>0
, and let
A
be a Young function fulfilling the condition (
37
).

(i)
Assume that

\int\limits^{\infty}\Big(\frac{t}{A(t)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\infty.

Then, there exists a constant
\gamma=\gamma(n,\rho)>0
such that

(13)

\int\limits_{\B_{\rho}}A_{n}\Bigg(\frac{|u|}{\gamma\big(\int_{\B_{\rho}}A(|u|)+A(|\nabla u|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\big)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\Bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\B_{\rho}}A(|u|)+A(|\nabla u|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

for every
u\in W^{1}K^{A}(\B_{\rho})
.

(ii)
Assume that

\int\limits^{\infty}\Big(\frac{t}{A(t)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Then, there exists a constant
\gamma=\gamma(n,\rho,A)>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\B_{\rho})}\leq\gamma\bigg(\int\limits_{\B_{\rho}}A(|u|)+A(|\nabla u|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n}}

for every
u\in W^{1}K^{A}(\B_{\rho})
.

In particular, if
\rho\in[r_{1},r_{2}]
, the constant
\gamma
depends on
\rho
only via
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
.

.

.

Let

and let

be a Young function such that

\int\limits_{0}\Big(\frac{t}{A(t)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n-2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x<\infty\quad\text{if}\ n\geq 3.

3.
Main result

Let
A
and
B
be Young functions. We impose an
A,B
–growth condition on
\A
, namely we assume

(14)

|\A(x,\xi)|\leq b(|\xi|)+\phi(x)\quad\text{for a\text{.}e\text{.}\ }x\in\Omega\ \text{and all}\ \xi\in\R^{n},

and

(15)

\A(x,\xi)\cdot\xi\geq A(|\xi|)\quad\text{for a\text{.}e\text{.}\ }x\in\Omega\ \text{and all}\ \xi\in\R^{n},\ |\xi|\geq R,

where
R\geq 0
is a constant,
\phi
is a non-negative measurable function that satisfies suitable integrability properties, and
b\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is the left–derivative of
B
(see Section
2
for a precise definition).

Concerning the reaction term, we assume that there exist a Young function
E
and a non-negative measurable function
\psi
, satisfying suitable assumptions to be specified later, such that

(16)

|f(x,z)|\leq\psi(x)+\frac{E(|z|)}{|z|}\quad\text{for a\text{.}e\text{.}\ }x\in\Omega\ \text{and all}\ z\in\R.

Throughout the paper, we shall assume that

(17)

\int\limits^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{B(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\infty,

otherwise the inclusion

(18)

W^{1}L^{B}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\subset L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

implies that every function
u\in W^{1}L^{B}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is locally bounded, irrespectively of whether it is a weak solution to Equation (
1
) or not; see Theorem
2.1

(ii)
below.

A useful consequence of (
17
) is that the lower Matuszewska-Orlicz index of
B
at infinity
i_{\infty}(B)
, defined in (
8
), satisfies

(19)

i_{\infty}(B)\leq n.

To see this, first note that
i_{\infty}(B)
has to be finite, otherwise its very definition would imply that
B
grows faster than any polynomial: a clear contradiction with (
17
). Now, given any
\varepsilon>0
, again by the definition of
i_{\infty}(B)
it follows that

(20)

B\ \text{dominates}\ t^{i_{\infty}(B)-\varepsilon}\ \text{near infinity.}

If, by contradiction,
i_{\infty}(B)
were strictly bigger than
n
, (
20
) and (
17
) would be incompatible as soon as
i_{\infty}(B)-\varepsilon>n
. Then, (
19
) holds.

3.1.
Setting of the problem

We assume some restrictions on the growth rates of
B
and
E
. Precisely, we assume that

(21)

B\ \text{and}\ E\ \text{satisfy the }\Delta_{2}\text{-condition near infinity.}

Moreover, a natural growth condition at infinity is imposed on
E
, meaning that we suppose

(22)

E(t)\leq A_{n}(ct)\quad\text{for all}\ t\geq t_{0}

for some
c>0
and
t_{0}\geq 0
, where
A_{n}
denotes the optimal
n
-dimensional Sobolev conjugate of
A
, whose definition is recalled in Section
2
.

The
\Delta_{2}
-condition entails that
B
does not grow too fast near infinity. In particular — see (

3.2.
Definition of weak solution

Under the assumptions above, we now define the notion of solution to Equation (
1
) we are dealing with. As briefly mentioned in the introduction, we let a solution
u
belong to
W^{1}L^{B}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and satisfy the equation in a (local) weak sense. Before being more precise, we observe that, by (
34
), for every
u\in L^{B}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and every
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
it holds

\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}}A(|u|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}}qB(|u|)+\phi|u|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Hence, any

belongs to
W^{1}K^{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and, by Theorem
2.1
, also to
L^{A_{n}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.

Let
u\in W^{1}L^{B}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and
v\in W^{1}L^{B}(\Omega)
, and put
\Omega^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\mathrm{supp}\,v\Subset\Omega
. Owing to (

) it holds

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}|\A(x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla v|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}}\widetilde{B}({b(|\nabla u|)})+B(|\nabla v|)+\phi|\nabla v|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}\cap\{|\nabla u|<t_{1}\}}\widetilde{B}({b(t_{1})})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}\cap\{|\nabla u|\geq t_{1}\}}|\nabla u|b(|\nabla u|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}}B(|\nabla v|)+\phi|\nabla v|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{B}({b(t_{1})})|\Omega^{\prime}|+q\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}}B(|\nabla u|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}}B(|\nabla v|)+\phi|\nabla v|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

where the first inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz and the Young inequalities and the second estimate relies upon the inequality (
5
), whereas the finiteness of

\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}}\phi|\nabla v|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

follows from (

We now deal with the reaction term. By (

\int\limits_{\Omega}|f(x,u)v|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}}|\psi v|+\frac{E(|u|)}{|u|}|v|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Then, by (

) and (
5

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}|f(x,u)v|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}}|\psi v|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\frac{E(t_{0})}{t_{0}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}}|v|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int\limits_{\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{A_{n}(c|u|)}{|u|}|v|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

where to ensure the finiteness of the last integral one exploits (

Summing up, for any

and

A function

Our approach to Theorem
3.3
follows the lines of De Giorgi’s regularity result for linear equations with measurable coefficients. Inspired by

]
, and as in any existing proof of local boundedness based on De Giorgi’s technique, we derive a Caccioppoli-type inequality, i.e., an energy estimate on the super-level sets of the relevant solution

for all concentric balls
\B_{\rho}(y)\Subset\B_{\sigma}(y)\Subset\Omega
. Choosing

(43)

v=(u-k)_{+}\eta

as a test function in the weak formulation of the problem, where
\eta
is a cut-off function associated with the given balls, (
42
) follows from an absorption argument for the gradient terms, which relies on the growth conditions and Young’s inequality.

When dealing with unbalanced growths of Orlicz type, further difficulties arise, due to both the different lower and upper bounds on
\A
and the lack of homogeneity in non-power-type Young functions. Indeed, replicating the argument outlined above introduces undesired integral terms on the right-hand side of the inequality. To overcome this difficulty, one needs a balance condition between
A
and
B

p
-growth setting and allows for the application of a Sobolev-type inequality in a sharp integral form on the remaining terms. Since
A_{n}
is generally non-homogeneous, the latter necessarily involves a gradient term on both sides; hence, integrals over
\B_{\sigma}(y)
involving both
u
and
\nabla u
appear on the right-hand side.

We rely on the novel approach developed in
[
CS24
]
for variational integrals with unbalanced Orlicz growths, which, in turn, refines the trial function optimization argument introduced in the
p,q
-growth framework in
[
BS20
,

]
. This technique exploits Sobolev inequalities only on
(n-1)
-dimensional spheres, rather than on
n
-dimensional balls. This allows us to formulate the balance inequality (

-dimensional Sobolev conjugate of

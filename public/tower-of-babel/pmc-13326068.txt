The convenience of contrast-enhanced intraoperative ultrasonic cholangiography in liver resection: a study protocol for a randomized, prospective, off-label trial
Authors: Takeshi Urade, Kentaro Oji, Masahiro Kido, Shohei Komatsu, Hidetoshi Gon, Kenji Fukushima, Shinichi So, Toshihiko Yoshida, Keisuke Arai, Hayata Sano, Sae Murakami, Takumi Fukumoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIntraoperative cholangiography (IOC) is widely used to delineate biliary anatomy and confirm ductal integrity during liver resection. However, conventional radiographic IOC requires a C-arm set-up, may interrupt the operative workflow, and exposes patients and operating-room personnel to ionizing radiation. Contrast-enhanced intraoperative ultrasonic cholangiography (CE-IOUSC) combines intrabiliary injection of highly diluted perfluorobutane microbubbles (Sonazoid®) with contrast harmonic ultrasonography, enabling real-time visualization that can be repeated on demand without radiation. We hypothesize that CE-IOUSC provides superior procedural convenience while maintaining biliary visualization and safety.MethodsThis is a single-center, open-label, randomized, parallel-group, phase III comparative trial. Adults (≥ 20 years) scheduled for liver resection, including living donors, are eligible when cholecystectomy and transcystic catheterization via the cystic duct are feasible. After informed consent, participants are randomized 1:1 (stratified by liver condition: normal vs. impaired) to CE-IOUSC or conventional radiographic IOC using a computer-generated sequence. In both arms, cholangiography is performed twice—before and after bile duct division—with additional imaging permitted as clinically required. Conventional IOC uses diluted iothalamate contrast (Urografin®) under C-arm fluoroscopy. CE-IOUSC uses 1,000-fold diluted Sonazoid® administered through the cystic duct and evaluated using intraoperative ultrasonography in contrast mode. Cross-over “rescue” cholangiography with the alternative modality is allowed when the assigned method is unsuccessful or inadequate. The primary endpoint is the cumulative time required to complete intraoperative cholangiography, including any additional imaging and repeated attempts. Secondary endpoints include surgeon-rated usability (5-point Likert questionnaire completed by the primary operator immediately after surgery), detectability of biliary structures (segmental visualization rates assessed by predefined criteria), postoperative biliary complications (bile leak or stricture graded by Clavien–Dindo), cross-over rescue efficacy, and safety outcomes (procedure-related adverse events and perioperative laboratory changes). Outcomes will be analyzed primarily on an intention-to-treat basis.DiscussionThis trial will quantify whether CE-IOUSC improves the efficiency and usability of intraoperative biliary imaging without compromising visualization or patient safety. The results will inform evidence-based adoption of ultrasound-based cholangiography as a practical alternative to conventional radiographic IOC in liver resection.Trial registrationJapan Registry of Clinical Trials (jRCT), jRCTs051230202.

Background

Accurate intraoperative identification of vascular and biliary anatomy is essential for safe and curative surgery in hepatobiliary diseases. Precise recognition of the biliary tract is particularly important, as misidentification can lead to bile duct injury during surgery, potentially resulting in postoperative bile leakage or biliary strictures. The incidence of postoperative bile leakage has been reported to range from 4.0% to 17% by Jin et al. and 9% by Sakamoto et al. [1, 2]. Even in donor hepatectomy, bile leakage and biliary stricture have been reported in 2.6% and 0.7% of cases, respectively [3]. Accurate intraoperative biliary navigation is critical in complex hepatobiliary procedures, such as those for hepatocellular carcinoma or perihilar cholangiocarcinoma. However, preoperative imaging has limitations, and real-time visualization of the biliary anatomy during surgery is often required. Currently, intraoperative ultrasonography is commonly used to visualize the hepatic arteries, veins, and portal vein. In contrast, the biliary tree is typically delineated using intraoperative cholangiography (IOC), which involves injecting iodinated contrast into the bile duct and acquiring X-ray images. However, this technique has several drawbacks, including radiation exposure to patients and medical staff, limited ability to perform repeated imaging due to the need to reposition radiographic equipment, inability to simultaneously visualize surrounding structures, and contraindications in patients with iodine allergy. Sonazoid® (perfluorobutane microbubbles) is a second-generation ultrasound contrast agent with high stability and prolonged contrast enhancement after intravenous administration. The use of contrast-enhanced ultrasound with Sonazoid has improved the detection and characterization of hepatic lesions. To overcome the limitations of conventional IOC, we developed a novel technique called contrast-enhanced intraoperative ultrasonic cholangiography (CE-IOUSC), in which diluted Sonazoid is injected directly into the bile duct, allowing clear visualization of the biliary anatomy using ultrasonography. In our previous clinical study (UMIN000006259), we demonstrated its utility in various hepatectomy cases [4]. We have also reported its application in donor hepatectomy for intraoperative biliary navigation, both before and after bile duct division [5], achieving successful biliary visualization in over 90% of 23 hepatobiliary disease cases and in 100% of seven living donors. These results also suggest the safety of intrabiliary Sonazoid administration. This novel ultrasound-based cholangiography method is a real-time, simple, minimally invasive, and repeatable technique.

Ultrasonic cholangiography is also listed in the guidelines and recommendations as one of the intracavitary uses of contrast-enhanced ultrasound [6, 7]. In high-complexity liver resections, where intraoperative biliary imaging is recommended to confirm biliary anatomy, comparing the utility and convenience of CE-IOUSC with conventional IOC may help establish the clinical advantages of this technique.

Methods

Study design

This single-center, open-label, randomized, parallel-group comparative study (Phase III) was designed to evaluate the clinical utility of CE-IOUSC using Sonazoid® in patients undergoing liver resection. Potential participants includes all patients requiring liver resection during the study period, encompassing those with malignant or benign liver tumors, and living donors for liver transplantation. Eligible patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to undergo intraoperative cholangiography using either CE-IOUSC or conventional IOC. The summary of this study is shown in Fig. 1. The study was conducted as a specified clinical trial and approved by the Kobe University Clinical Research Ethics Committee (approval no. C230013) on March 13th, 2024. This study was registered with the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials (jRCT https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/en-top) under the identifier jRCTs051230202 on March 13th, 2024.

Randomization and allocation

Patients are randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to the CE-IOUSC group or the conventional IOC group using a web-based allocation system “Mujinwari” (https://mujinwari.biz/users/login) with a stratified permuted block method according to liver condition (normal vs. impaired liver). Allocation is concealed until enrollment and registration are completed. Because the assigned procedure must be prepared before surgery, allocation is disclosed preoperatively after registration. Given the relatively small planned sample size of 20 patients, a block size of 4 was selected to maintain balance between groups during enrollment and because it is a factor of the total sample size.

Schedule of the study

After obtaining informed consent, researchers will assess inclusion and exclusion criteria, participant background, and performance status. Blood test values, including complete blood count, aspartate aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, alkaline phosphatase, γ-glutamyl transpeptidase, total bilirubin, albumin, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, C-reactive protein, serum sodium, and serum potassium, will be evaluated within 28 days prior to surgery. Following surgery, blood tests will be performed on postoperative days 1, 3, 5, and 7 to monitor for adverse effects of cholangiography. Direct bilirubin levels in the drainage tube will be measured to detect bile leaks.

Intervention

Following cholecystectomy, a 4Fr catheter is inserted into the cystic duct to facilitate contrast agent administration. In both groups, intraoperative cholangiography is performed twice—once before and once after bile duct division. Additional imaging may be performed at the discretion of the surgeon and documented accordingly.

Radiographic intraoperative cholangiography group (Conventional IOC group)

A mobile C-arm fluoroscopy system was utilized. Meglumine/sodium amidotrizoate (Urografin®, Bayer Yakuhin, Ltd., Osaka, Japan) is diluted 1:1 with sterile normal saline to produce a 30% solution, which is then injected into the biliary tree via a transcystic catheter. The C-arm is positioned over the operative field, and real-time fluoroscopic images are acquired to confirm biliary anatomy.

Contrast-enhanced intraoperative ultrasonic cholangiography (CE-IOUSC) group

CE-IOUSC is performed using ultrasound imaging systems, including the Aplio i800 or Aplio a/Verifia (Canon Medical Systems, Tokyo, Japan), and employs either a T-shaped intraoperative linear probe (PLT-705BTH, 7 MHz) or a micro-convex probe (PVT-745BTH, 7 MHz). Perfluorobutane microbubbles (Sonazoid®, GE Healthcare Pharma, Tokyo, Japan) are reconstituted with 2 mL of sterile water and subsequently diluted 1,000-fold in saline to create a contrast suspension. This diluted suspension is then injected via a transcystic catheter. The ultrasound system is switched from B-mode to contrast harmonic imaging mode, enabling real-time visualization of the biliary system using intraoperative ultrasonography.

In both groups, the biliary tree–including the common hepatic duct, right hepatic duct, anterior and posterior sectoral ducts, and left hepatic duct–is assessed and recorded. If visualization is unsuccessful after three attempts, it is considered a failure of the assigned imaging modality.

This study will be conducted at our institution as a certified high-volume center for advanced hepatobiliary-pancreatic surgery accredited by the Japanese Society of Hepato-Biliary-Pancreatic Surgery. In all enrolled cases, at least one board-certified expert surgeon in advanced hepatobiliary-pancreatic surgery will participate in the operation. At our institution, both intraoperative ultrasonography and conventional intraoperative cholangiography are routinely performed by hepatobiliary-pancreatic surgeons. CE-IOUSC was originally developed by our group in 2011, and our institution has accumulated experience in more than 100 CE-IOUSC cases since its introduction. Therefore, both CE-IOUSC and conventional IOC in this trial will be performed by, or under the supervision of, hepatobiliary-pancreatic surgeons who are well familiar with these procedures.

Results

Primary endpoint

The primary endpoint of this study is the total time required to complete intraoperative cholangiography. This is defined as the duration from the beginning of the contrast imaging setup to the completion of imaging and readiness to proceed with the next surgical step. In the conventional IOC group, this is measured from the start of C-arm positioning until the end of imaging. In the CE-IOUSC group, time is measured from the start of ultrasound probe operation (in contrast mode) until the completion of contrast-enhanced imaging. As ultrasound equipment is typically already set up at the operating table for intraoperative ultrasonography, this measurement isolates the time specifically required for biliary imaging. Cholangiography will be performed twice in each patient: once before and once after bile duct division. Any additional imaging deemed necessary by the surgical team will be recorded and included in the cumulative time. Procedure time is recorded by a designated co-investigator (KO and HS) in the operating room who is not directly involved in the surgical procedure, using the same stopwatch in all cases. This endpoint was selected to objectively evaluate the procedural efficiency and intraoperative usability of CE-IOUSC compared with the conventional method, which often necessitates equipment relocation, radiation shielding, and contrast preparation.

Each response is scored from 1 (very poor) to 5 (excellent). A comparative analysis between groups will be performed to quantify subjective operator satisfaction.

Detectability of biliary system

The success of intraoperative biliary imaging will be evaluated by assessing the clear identification of the common hepatic duct, right hepatic duct, anterior and posterior sectoral ducts, and left hepatic duct. A segment will be considered a failure if it is not visualized following three contrast injections. All images will be documented and independently reviewed by at least two experienced liver surgeons. The visualization rate will be calculated for each segment and compared between groups.

Postoperative biliary complications

Postoperative complications involving the biliary tract, including bile leakage and bile duct strictures, will be recorded until discharge. Postoperative complications, including biliary complications, will be graded according to the Clavien–Dindo classification. Particularly, bile leakage can be diagnosed according to the definition of the International Study Group of Liver Surgery (ISGLS) because a drain will be routinely placed along the liver transection surface in all cases. Complications will be graded using the Clavien-Dindo classification. Their incidence will be compared between groups to evaluate the clinical effectiveness of CE-IOUSC in preventing adverse biliary outcomes

Cross-over efficacy (Exploratory)

When the initially assigned cholangiography method fails to provide satisfactory visualization, an alternative method will be employed as a rescue procedure. The success of the second modality will be recorded as an exploratory measure of comparative utility between the two modalities.

Safety outcomes

Adverse Events Related to Sonazoid Administration.

Although Sonazoid is approved for intravenous use, its intrabiliary application is considered off-label. Therefore, safety evaluation includes monitoring for procedure-related adverse events, such as hypersensitivity reactions, abnormal vital signs, or hepatic enzyme elevation. Laboratory tests will be performed preoperatively and postoperatively on days 0, 1, 3, 5, and 7 postoperatively. Any deviations from baseline values will be analyzed in the context of contrast agent use.

General perioperative outcomes

Additional clinical parameters, including operative time, intraoperative blood loss, transfusion requirements, postoperative length of hospital stay, and non-biliary complications (e.g., infections, thromboembolism, and ileus), will be collected and analyzed. These parameters will help assess the tolerability and systemic impact of CE-IOUSC.

Sample size

The primary hypothesis of this study is that CE-IOUSC using Sonazoid is superior to conventional radiographic intraoperative cholangiography with respect to the time required to complete cholangiography in patients undergoing liver resection for hepatobiliary disease or donors undergoing partial hepatectomy for living donor liver transplantation. At our institution, the time required for conventional IOC is approximately 10 min on average, measured from the start of C-arm setup to resumption of surgery. In contrast, the time required for CE-IOUSC is expected to be approximately 5 min on average, measured from the start of ultrasonographic manipulation to resumption of surgery. Based on an expected mean difference of 5 min and a standard deviation of 3–4 min, a sample size of 10 patients per group provides more than 80% power to detect a significant difference using a two-sided t-test with a significance level of 5%. Considering the exploratory nature of this study and the possibility of dropouts, the planned sample size was set at 10 patients in each group, for a total of 20 patients.

Data management

All study data will be recorded using standardized case report forms (CRFs) and securely stored at the Department of Hepato-Biliary-Pancreatic Surgery, Kobe University Hospital. The source data will include patient medical records, laboratory reports, operative notes, and imaging data. Data entry, verification, and monitoring will be conducted by trained clinical research personnel under the principal investigator’s supervision.

Principal investigators will review and sign completed CRF, which will be maintained as the source documents. Corrections to CRFs will be made by authorized study personnel and documented with the date, reason for correction, and their signature to ensure data traceability.

The confidentiality of participant data will be strictly maintained. Study documents will be stored in a secure, access-controlled environment and retained for at least five years following study completion, in accordance with national guidelines and institutional policies.

No external data monitoring committee is appointed for this trial due to the small scale and low risk of the study. However, an internal data review will be conducted periodically by the study team. No interim analyses will be performed. Final data analysis will be conducted after database lock and reported in accordance with the pre-specified statistical analysis plan.

Handling of cases

Patients will be excluded from the comparative analysis if they withdraw consent, violate inclusion/exclusion criteria, or require cross-over to the alternative cholangiographic modality because the initially assigned modality is unsuccessful or inadequate. However, such cross-over cases will not be disregarded. The number of these excluded cases will be recorded and reported separately as cholangiography failures of the initially assigned modality. All such cases and deviations will be documented.

Handling of data

Data will be collected using paper-based CRFs and entered into a secure database by designated members of the study office. Independent staff will verify all data entries for accuracy. In the event of data discrepancies or uncertainties, the principal investigator and study biostatistician will jointly determine the appropriate resolution. A complete audit trail will be maintained for all data modifications. Data quality assurance will be ensured throughout the study duration.

Summarization of subject baseline characteristics

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics will be summarized for each treatment group. For categorical variables, frequencies and percentages will be reported. For continuous variables, summary statistics including number of subjects, mean, standard deviation, median, minimum, and maximum will be calculated. The comparability of baseline variables across groups will be assessed using appropriate statistical methods.

Efficacy analysis

Comparative efficacy analyses will primarily be conducted using the protocol-defined analysis population excluding cross-over cases, with the PPS used for sensitivity analyses. Cross-over cases will be summarized separately as cholangiography failures of the initially assigned modality. The primary endpoint—cholangiography time—will be compared between groups using the Mann–Whitney U test. Secondary endpoints, including categorical variables (e.g., biliary visualization rate, complication rates), will be analyzed using Fisher’s exact test. Ordinal data (e.g., surgeon questionnaire responses) will be analyzed using non-parametric methods. Responses to the 5-point Likert scale questionnaire will be treated as ordinal data and compared between groups using the Mann–Whitney U test. In addition, the distribution of responses in each category will be presented descriptively. Missing data will be addressed using case-by-case input when necessary, as judged by the study statistician and principal investigator. No imputation methods such as last observation carried forward are planned unless otherwise justified.

Safety analysis

Safety will be evaluated based on the incidence of adverse events, particularly those related to contrast agent administration. All adverse events occurring up to hospital discharge will be recorded in the CRF. While the specific postoperative days for laboratory testing may vary, any clinically relevant laboratory abnormalities will be noted and reviewed by the safety team. Serious adverse events will be reported promptly to the certified clinical research review board by the principal investigator, in accordance with regulatory requirements.

Data and safe monitoring

A data monitoring process will be implemented to ensure the quality and safety of the study. Periodic internal monitoring will be conducted by the study team. The following key elements will be reviewed every six months: informed consent procedures and documentation, subject retention and adherence, trial conduct and implementation, safety data, and study progress. Any significant findings will be discussed by the principal investigator and reported to the institutional review board as appropriate. An external data and safety monitoring board was not established due to the study’s relatively low-risk nature.

Patients and public involvement

This research was not conducted with the involvement of patients or members of the public in its design, conduct, reporting, or dissemination.

Confidentiality

To ensure participant confidentiality, all data will be coded using unique identification numbers. Identifiable personal information will be stored separately from study data in locked cabinets located in restricted-access areas. Electronic data will be stored on password-protected computers and secure institutional servers. Study-related documents and data will be retained for at least five years following study completion. No identifiable information will be shared outside the study without explicit written consent from the participants.

Ethics and dissemination

This clinical trial has been approved by the Certified Clinical Research Review Board of Kobe University Hospital. The study protocol is registered with the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials (jRCT), and it will be conducted in full compliance with the Clinical Trials Act of Japan, the Declaration of Helsinki (2013 revision), and all applicable regulations and institutional guidelines. Prior to enrollment, written informed consent will be obtained from all participants after a thorough explanation of the study’s objectives, procedures, risks, and potential benefits. Participation is voluntary, and patients may withdraw from the study at any time without penalty or loss of access to medical care. The safety of participants is of paramount importance. In the event of an adverse event, appropriate clinical management will be provided. Compensation for study-related injuries will be offered in accordance with institutional policies and the Clinical Trials Act. The study’s results, encompassing positive, negative, or inconclusive findings, will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed journals and presentations at academic conferences, while ensuring no personally identifiable information is disclosed. No personally identifiable information will be disclosed in any publications or presentations.

Discussion

Accurate intraoperative delineation of biliary anatomy remains a critical determinant of safety in liver resection. Bile duct injury, postoperative bile leakage, and biliary strictures continue to represent clinically significant complications, even in high-volume hepatobiliary centers. While conventional IOC is widely accepted, it has inherent limitations, including radiation exposure, workflow interruption, equipment dependency, and difficulty in repeated real-time assessments. These practical constraints underscore the need for alternative imaging strategies that better integrate into modern hepatobiliary surgery.

Contrast-enhanced intraoperative ultrasonic cholangiography using intrabiliary Sonazoid® administration offers several theoretical and practical advantages. Unlike radiographic techniques, CE-IOUSC enables real-time visualization without ionizing radiation, allows for repeated assessments with minimal procedural burden, and permits simultaneous evaluation of surrounding parenchymal and vascular structures. Prior exploratory and clinical investigations have demonstrated high detectability of major bile ducts and favorable safety profiles following intrabiliary microbubble injection. These findings suggest that CE-IOUSC may represent a superior modality to conventional IOC in terms of procedural efficiency and intraoperative usability.

The primary endpoint of this trial— total time to complete intraoperative cholangiography—was intentionally selected to quantify procedural convenience, an under-investigated yet clinically relevant aspect of surgical imaging. Intraoperative imaging methods directly influence operative flow, cognitive workload, and resource utilization. Even modest reductions in imaging-related interruptions may translate into meaningful improvements in operative efficiency, particularly in complex resections requiring repeated biliary confirmation. By isolating imaging-specific time rather than total operative duration, this study aims to provide a more precise assessment of workflow impact.

Secondary endpoints further address clinically meaningful dimensions of CE-IOUSC. Detection of biliary structures assesses technical feasibility, and postoperative biliary complications explore potential clinical benefits beyond convenience. Although the study is not powered primarily for complication reduction, trends observed in these outcomes may inform future large-scale trials. The inclusion of surgeon-reported usability metrics acknowledges that the adoption of novel imaging technologies depends not only on diagnostic performance but also on operator experience and perceived utility.

Safety considerations warrant particular attention, given the off-label intrabiliary use of Sonazoid®. Available evidence suggests minimal biological risk, owing to the pharmacologic characteristics of perfluorobutane microbubbles and the substantially diluted concentrations employed. Nevertheless, systematic monitoring of hepatic enzymes and perioperative adverse events is essential to establish procedural safety within a controlled prospective framework. This study therefore contributes important regulatory and clinical evidence regarding this novel application.

Several limitations are inherent in the study design. The single-center setting and modest sample size may restrict generalizability. The open-label design, though unavoidable, introduces potential operator bias, particularly with respect to subjective endpoints. Furthermore, procedural time measurements may be influenced by institutional workflow and surgeon familiarity with ultrasound systems. Although both procedures are standardized and performed by experienced hepatobiliary-pancreatic surgeons, operator-related factors may still influence procedural performance and should be considered when interpreting the results. In addition, the surgeon-reported usability assessment is inherently subjective and may be influenced by operator perception and procedural preference, despite the use of predefined questionnaire items. Postoperative biliary outcomes should also be interpreted with caution, because postoperative monitoring in this study is primarily conducted during the inpatient period; therefore, delayed bile leaks detected only after discharge may not be fully captured. Despite these constraints, the randomized comparative structure provides a robust initial evaluation of feasibility and practical utility.

If CE-IOUSC demonstrates superior procedural efficiency without compromising biliary visualization or safety, the findings may support the broader integration of ultrasound-based cholangiography into hepatobiliary surgery. Such a shift could reduce radiation exposure, simplify intraoperative logistics, and enhance the repeatability of biliary assessment. Ultimately, this trial seeks to bridge the gap between promising preliminary observations and structured clinical validation, thereby advancing evidence-based adoption of intraoperative imaging innovations.

Toroidal Fermi-surface geometry and phonon-limited transport in nodal-line semimetals
Aman Anand, Alessandro De Martino

Abstract
Nodal-line semimetals (NLSs) can display unconventional quasiparticle dynamics and charge transport properties due to their extended band degeneracy and the peculiar geometry of their Fermi surface. We consider electron–acoustic phonon scattering as the dominant relaxation mechanism and compute the quasiparticle decay rate and dc conductivity by solving the linearized semiclassical Boltzmann equation in a minimal model of a doped circular NLS. We find that the toroidal geometry of the Fermi surface gives rise to two parametrically distinct Bloch–Grüneisen temperatures, associated with momentum transfers along the poloidal and toroidal directions, respectively. As a result, an intermediate temperature window opens between these two scales, in which the decay rate follows Γ ∝ T 2 \Gamma\propto T^{2} , while the conductivity follows σ ∝ T − 2 \sigma\propto T^{-2} . We also obtain the low- and high-temperature asymptotic behaviors, and discuss implications for ARPES and transport measurements in candidate NLS materials.

Model

In this section, we introduce the ingredients entering our transport calculation:

and their coupling via the deformation potential.
We focus on the highly doped, low-temperature regime with chemical potential
\mu\gg k_{B}T
,
where transport is dominated by intraband scattering processes
in the conduction band. Throughout, we set
\hbar=1
.
The toroidal Fermi surface that emerges at finite doping naturally introduces two distinct BG scales,
discussed in Sec.
II.4
, which set the relevant temperature regimes for scattering
and transport discussed in Secs.
IV
and
V

II.1
Electronic model

We model low-energy quasiparticles in the vicinity of a circular nodal ring (NR)
by the two-band effective Hamiltonian
8
;
11
;
52

\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{k})=vk_{z}\sigma_{y}+\lambda(k^{2}-k_{0}^{2})\sigma_{z},

(1)

where
k=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}}
,
\sigma_{i}
are Pauli matrices, and
v,\lambda,k_{0}
are material-dependent parameters.
The nodal ring lies in the plane
k_{z}=0
at radius
k_{0}
. It is convenient to define

v_{0}=2\lambda k_{0}
and
\alpha=v/v_{0}
, where
v_{0}
sets the characteristic in-plane velocity
near the NR and
\alpha
parametrizes the anisotropy along
k_{z}

52
. Representative values from DFT
for Ca
3
P
2

are
v\simeq 2.5
eVÅ,
\lambda\simeq 4.34
eVÅ
2
,
and
k_{0}=0.206
Å
-1

11
, corresponding to
\alpha\simeq 1.4
and
v_{0}\simeq 2.6\times 10^{5}\penalty\ \mathrm{m/s}
.
A small anisotropy may affect numerical prefactors and weakly rescale the BG temperatures, but we do not expect
it to significantly affect the temperature exponents, therefore we set
\alpha=1
hereafter.

The electronic eigenstates are

|\Phi_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}({\bf r})\rangle=e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}|u_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}\rangle,

(2)

where
|u_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}\rangle
diagonalize
\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{k})
with eigenvalues

\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}=\zeta\sqrt{v^{2}k_{z}^{2}+\lambda^{2}(k^{2}-k_{0}^{2})^{2}},

(3)

and
\zeta=\pm
labels conduction/valence bands. The corresponding Bloch spinors can be written as

|u_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}\rangle=\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}\begin{pmatrix}ivk_{z}\\
\lambda(k^{2}-k_{0}^{2})-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}\end{pmatrix},

(4)

with normalization fixed by
\langle u_{\mathbf{k},\zeta^{\prime}}|u_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}\rangle=\delta_{\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}}
.
The group velocity
\mathbf{v}_{\zeta}(\mathbf{k})=\partial_{\mathbf{k}}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}
is given by

\mathbf{v}_{\zeta}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{2\lambda^{2}(\mathbf{k}^{2}-k_{0}^{2})}{\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}}\mathbf{k}+\frac{v^{2}}{\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}}k_{z}\hat{z}.

(5)

Shown are the toroidal Fermi surface, the associated toroidal coordinates

(\kappa,\theta,\varphi)
, and the unit vector
\hat{e}_{\kappa}
, defined in Eq. (
13
).

For energies well below the Lifshitz scale
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}\ll\lambda k_{0}^{2}
,
constant-energy surfaces form a thin torus in momentum space (see Fig.
1
).
It is then convenient to introduce toroidal coordinates
(\kappa,\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle k_{x}

\displaystyle=(k_{0}+\kappa\cos\varphi)\cos\theta,

(6)

\displaystyle k_{y}

\displaystyle=(k_{0}+\kappa\cos\varphi)\sin\theta,

(7)

\displaystyle k_{z}

\displaystyle=\kappa\sin\varphi,

(8)

where
\kappa
is the minor radius,
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
the toroidal angle (along the NR),
and
\varphi\in[0,2\pi)
the poloidal angle
(around the tube cross-section). In the thin-torus regime
\kappa\ll k_{0}
,
one may expand

and obtain the Dirac-like form

\mathcal{H}(\kappa,\theta,\varphi)=v_{0}\kappa(\cos\varphi\,\sigma_{z}+\sin\varphi\,\sigma_{y}),

(9)

with dispersion

\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k},\zeta}=\zeta v_{0}\kappa.

(10)

Such linear behaviour has been observed over a broad energy window in several NLS materials,
including ZrSiS

;
33

In these coordinates, the spinors simplify to

|u_{\mathbf{k},+}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\varphi/2\\
i\sin\varphi/2\end{pmatrix},\quad|u_{\mathbf{k},-}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}-\sin\varphi/2\\
i\cos\varphi/2\end{pmatrix}.

(11)

and the conduction-band group velocity becomes

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{+}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=v_{0}\,\hat{e}_{\kappa},

(12)

where the unit vector
\hat{e}_{\kappa}
(shown in Fig.

\displaystyle\hat{e}_{\kappa}=\cos\varphi\cos\theta\,\hat{x}+\cos\varphi\sin\theta\,\hat{y}+\sin\varphi\,\hat{z}.

(13)

Since we focus here on temperatures

and we restrict attention to
\zeta=+
, suppressing the band index hereafter.

II.2
Phonon model

We describe acoustic lattice vibrations within isotropic elastic continuum theory
24
.
In this approximation, the phonon spectrum is determined by the longitudinal and transverse sound velocities
c_{l}
and
c_{t}
.
These velocities are typically much smaller than the electronic Fermi velocity; for example, in ZrSiS
one finds
c_{l}\simeq 3.2\times 10^{3}\penalty\ \mathrm{m/s}

38

and
v_{0}\simeq 6.5\times 10^{5}\penalty\ \mathrm{m/s}

40
, so that
c_{l}/v_{0}\sim 10^{-2}
.

The displacement field
{\bf u}({\bf r},t)
obeys the wave equation

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}^{2}{\bf u}=c_{t}^{2}\nabla^{2}{\bf u}+(c_{l}^{2}-c_{t}^{2})\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}),

(14)

which, for harmonic time dependence
{\bf u}({\bf r},t)={\bf u}({\bf r})e^{-i\Omega t}
, becomes

\displaystyle-\Omega^{2}{\bf u}=c_{t}^{2}\nabla^{2}{\bf u}+(c_{l}^{2}-c_{t}^{2})\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}).

(15)

Its normal modes are one longitudinal and two transverse acoustic branches with dispersions
\Omega^{(l/t)}_{\mathbf{q}}=c_{l/t}|\mathbf{q}|
.

In the deformation-potential approximation, which we introduce in Sec.
II.3
,
only longitudinal lattice deformations contribute. So in what follows,
we restrict ourselves to the longitudinal mode and we suppress the branch label
in phonon operators and frequencies henceforth.
Upon quantization, the longitudinal displacement field can be expanded as

\displaystyle{\bf u}({\bf r})=\int d^{3}\mathbf{q}\frac{e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}}{\sqrt{2\rho_{M}\Omega_{\bf q}}}\hat{{\bf q}}\left[a_{\bf q}-a_{-\bf q}^{\dagger}\right],

(16)

where
\rho_{M}
is the mass density of the medium,
a_{\bf q}^{\dagger}
,
a_{\bf q}

are longitudinal phonon creation and annihilation operators, and
\hat{\mathbf{q}}=\mathbf{q}/|\mathbf{q}|
.

II.3
Electron-phonon interaction

We consider electron–phonon interactions via the deformation potential,

H_{\mathrm{ep}}=g_{0}\nabla\cdot{\bf u}({\bf r}),

(17)

where the coupling constant
g_{0}
parametrizes the shift of the electronic band energy
under local compression and is taken diagonal in spin–orbital space.
In principle, this coupling is renormalized by electronic screening,
which we do not explicitly consider here. We therefore treat
g_{0}

as an effective (screened) deformation-potential coupling, which may have a weak
\mu
dependence.

Since
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0
for transverse modes, only longitudinal phonons contribute to the electron-phonon coupling. Other coupling mechanisms have also been explored, for example strain-induced effective gauge fields leading to a topological piezoelectric response in NLSs
29
.
We neglect piezoelectric effects here because several NLSs of interest,
including Ca
3
P
2
and ZrSiS, are centrosymmetric and cannot exhibit conventional bulk piezoelectricity
40
;
11
;
22
.
In these systems, the deformation potential is expected to provide the dominant coupling between electron and acoustic phonons.

Substituting the mode expansion (
16
),
into
H_{\mathrm{ep}}
and evaluating matrix elements between Bloch states
|\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle

and
|\Phi_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\rangle
yields

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}|H_{\mathrm{ep}}|\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle={\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}\left(a_{\bf q}-a_{-\bf q}^{\dagger}\right),

(18)

with momentum transfer
{\bf q}={\bf k}^{\prime}-{\bf k}
and amplitude

{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}=i\frac{g_{0}\sqrt{\Omega_{\mathbf{q}}}}{c_{l}\sqrt{2\rho_{M}}}\langle u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}|u_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle.

(19)

The pseudospin dependence enters through the Bloch spinor overlap
\langle u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}|u_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle
,
which in toroidal coordinates follows from Eq. (
11
) (for the conduction band)
as

\langle u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}|u_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle=\cos\left(\frac{\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}}{2}\right).

(20)

Assuming that phonons are in thermal equilibrium at temperature
T
,
the transition rate from the electronic state
|\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle
to

|\Phi_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\rangle
follows from Fermi’s golden rule as

\displaystyle W_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=2\pi|{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}}|^{2}\bigg\{n_{\mathrm{B}}(\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})\,\delta(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}-\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})+

\displaystyle+[n_{\mathrm{B}}(\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})+1]\,\delta(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}+\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})\bigg\},

(21)

where
n_{\mathrm{B}}(\Omega)=1/\left(e^{\beta\Omega}-1\right)
is the Bose-Einstein
distribution function, with
\beta=1/k_{B}T
.
The two terms in Eq. (
21
) describe phonon absorption and emission,
respectively. These rates enter the collision integral of the Boltzmann equation in
Sec.
III
.

II.4
Temperature scales

In conventional metals with large Fermi surfaces, the crossover between low-
and high-temperature regimes of electron-phonon scattering is usually controlled
by the Debye temperature. In semimetals, instead, the crossover is controlled by the BG temperature,
set by the maximal phonon momentum that can scatter carriers across the Fermi surface.
For a toroidal Fermi surface, there are two independent momentum scales, corresponding
to the minor and major radii, and thus two BG scales. We define

\displaystyle k_{\mathrm{B}}T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}

\displaystyle=2c_{l}\kappa_{\rm{F}},

(22)

\displaystyle k_{\mathrm{B}}T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}

\displaystyle=c_{l}k_{0},

(23)

where
k_{\mathrm{B}}
is the Boltzmann constant and
\kappa_{\text{F}}=\mu/v_{0}
is the minor radius
of the toroidal Fermi surface at chemical potential
\mu
.
The scale
T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}
is associated with momentum transfer along the poloidal direction,
set by
\kappa_{F}
, while
T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}
reflects the toroidal extent along the NR, set by
k_{0}
.

For illustration, in PbTaSe
2
, a NLS with strong spin–orbit coupling
6
,
one has
k_{0}\simeq 0.2\penalty\ \text{\AA}^{-1}
and
c_{l}\simeq 3.2\times 10^{3}\penalty\ \mathrm{m/s}

31
,
yielding

T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}=\frac{c_{l}k_{0}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}}\simeq 49\penalty\ \mathrm{K}.

(24)

For ZrSiS, the nodal line is approximately diamond-shaped, but taking
k_{0}\penalty\ \simeq\penalty\ 0.3\penalty\ \text{\AA}^{-1}

32

and
c_{l}\simeq 6.8\times 10^{3}\penalty\ \mathrm{m/s}

38
gives

T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}=\frac{c_{l}k_{0}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}}\simeq 156\penalty\ \mathrm{K}.

(25)

The Debye temperatures in these materials are typically several times larger than

T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}

54
;
39
,
justifying the use of long-wavelength acoustic phonons with linear dispersion
in the temperature ranges of interest.

In our model, we assume a thin torus,
\kappa_{F}\ll k_{0}
, which implies the hierarchy

T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}/T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}\sim\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}/k_{0}\ll 1.

(26)

This naturally leads to three temperature regimes that will control
both quasiparticle decay rate and transport scattering rate:
(i)
T\ll T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}
, (ii)
T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}\ll T\ll T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}
, and (iii)
T\gg T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}
.

In the next section, we formulate the semiclassical BTE using the transition rates
in Eq. (
21
) and derive expressions for the decay rate and transport lifetime in
these regimes.

III
Boltzmann Equation

In this section, we briefly review the formulation of semiclassical charge transport in the presence of
electron-phonon scattering within the BTE framework
57
.
We then solve the linearized BTE in terms of transport lifetimes
\tau^{i}_{\mathrm{tr}}
(
i=x,y,z
),
which we use to evaluate the conductivity tensor in Sec.
V
.
The same scattering kernel, without the transport weighting factor,
also gives the single-particle decay rate
\Gamma
, discussed in Sec.
IV
.

III.1
Linearized BTE

In the presence of slowly varying external fields, the semiclassical BTE for the
electron distribution function

f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{k},t)
reads
18

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}+\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{r}}\mathbf{f}+\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}f=\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}},

(27)

where
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}
is the collision integral,

\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{k})
is the electron group velocity and
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}=e\mathbf{E}

for a uniform electric field with
e
the electron charge.
For a homogeneous steady state (
\partial_{t}f=\nabla_{\mathbf{r}}f=0
), driven by a weak uniform electric
field
\mathbf{E}
, Eq. (
27
) becomes

e\mathbf{E}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}f_{\mathbf{k}}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}.

(28)

The collision integral for scattering between states
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}

mediated by phonons can be written as

\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}=\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigl\{W_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}f_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}(1-f_{\mathbf{k}})-W_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}f_{\mathbf{k}}(1-f_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}})\bigr\},

(29)

where
W_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}
is the transition rate from

\mathbf{k}
to
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
obtained from Fermi’s golden rule [cf. Eq. (
21
)].

We now linearize the BTE around equilibrium by writing

f_{\mathbf{k}}=n_{\mathrm{F}}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}})-\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\,\frac{\partial n_{\mathrm{F}}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}})}{\partial\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}},

(30)

where
n_{\mathrm{F}}(\varepsilon)=1/\left(e^{\beta(\varepsilon-\mu)}+1\right)

is the Fermi-Dirac distribution function at chemical potential
\mu
,
and
\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}
is a function parametrizing the deviation from equilibrium.
Standard manipulations using detailed balance (summarized in Appendix
A
) recast the linearized collision integral as

\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}=\frac{\partial n_{\text{F}}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}})}{\partial\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}}\,\mathcal{J}_{\mathbf{k}},\quad\mathcal{J}_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\big(\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}-\varphi_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\big),

(31)

where the symmetric kernel
\mathcal{W}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}
is given by

\mathcal{W}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}=2\pi\bigl|\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\bigr|^{2}\bigl\{\bigl[n_{\mathrm{B}}(\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})+n_{\mathrm{F}}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}+\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})\bigr]\delta(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}-\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})+\bigl[n_{\mathrm{B}}(\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})+1-n_{\mathrm{F}}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}-\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})\bigr]\delta(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}+\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})\bigr\},

(32)

with
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{k}
, and
\Omega_{\mathbf{q}}=c_{l}|\mathbf{q}|
.
Inserting Eq. (
31
) into Eq. (
28
) yields the linearized BTE

e\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{k})=\mathcal{J}_{\mathbf{k}}.

(33)

In the rest of this paper, we specialize to the degenerate, thin-torus regime.
Specifically, we assume
k_{B}T\ll\mu
so that transport is controlled by quasiparticles at the Fermi surface,
and we set
-\partial_{\varepsilon}n_{\mathrm{F}}(\varepsilon)\simeq\delta(\varepsilon-\mu)
. Moreover,
since
c_{l}\ll v_{0}
, typical phonon energies are small compared with electronic scales,
so scattering is quasi-elastic (
\Omega_{\mathbf{q}}\ll\mu
for thermal phonons),
and we therefore omit the phonon energy in the energy-conserving
\delta
-functions
in the scattering rates (
32
).
The combinations of Fermi and Bose functions evaluated at
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}=\mu
,
simplify to

\displaystyle n_{\mathrm{B}}(\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle+n_{\mathrm{F}}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}+\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})=\frac{1}{\sinh(\beta\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})}

\displaystyle=n_{\mathrm{B}}(\Omega_{\mathbf{q}})+1-n_{\mathrm{F}}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}-\Omega_{\mathbf{q}}),

(34)

so that the kernel in Eq. (
32
) takes the form

\mathcal{W}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\,\delta(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}),

(35)

with

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}=\frac{4\pi\bigl|\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\bigr|^{2}}{\sinh(\beta c_{l}|\mathbf{q}|)}.

(36)

Finally, the phase space integral reduces to an integration
over the toroidal Fermi surface which, in the thin-torus limit
\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}/k_{0}\ll 1
,
reads

\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}-\mu)\dots\approx\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}k_{0}}{(2\pi)^{3}v_{0}}\int d\varphi^{\prime}d\theta^{\prime}\dots,

(37)

where we have used the Jacobian of the transformation to toroidal
coordinates
\kappa^{\prime}(k_{0}+\kappa^{\prime}\cos\varphi^{\prime})
, evaluated on the Fermi surface

\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}
, and
the linear dispersion in Eq. (
10
); at leading order in
\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}/k_{0}
,
we set
k_{0}+\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}\cos\varphi^{\prime}\approx k_{0}
.
In this limit, the momentum transfer between states
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}

simplifies to (see Appendix
C
for details)

|\mathbf{q}|=|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|=\sqrt{4k_{0}^{2}\sin^{2}\!\left(\frac{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}{2}\right)+4\kappa_{\text{F}}^{2}\sin^{2}\!\left(\frac{\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}}{2}\right)}.

(38)

Moreover, using Eq. (
20
), the Bloch spinor overlap
\langle u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}|u_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle

depends only on the poloidal angle difference
\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}
.
Therefore the scattering kernel
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}

depends on initial and final momenta only through toroidal and poloidal angle differences
and is translationally invariant on the toroidal
Fermi surface to leading order in
\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}/k_{0}
.

Under the approximations discussed above, the linearized BTE can be solved exactly to leading
order in
\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}/k_{0}
, as discussed next.

III.2
Transport lifetimes

To solve Eq. (

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we have analyzed quasiparticle scattering and charge transport in doped NLSs with a toroidal Fermi surface,
focusing on longitudinal acoustic phonons coupled to electrons via a deformation potential.
Using a low-energy two-band model with a circular nodal ring,
we consider the regime below the Lifshitz scale, where the Fermi surface is a thin torus characterized
by a major radius
k_{0}
and a minor radius
\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}
. This geometry gives rise to two parametrically distinct
BG temperature scales,
T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}\propto c_{l}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}

and
T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}\propto c_{l}k_{0}
, associated with phonons probing the poloidal and toroidal dimensions of the Fermi surface, respectively.
Using Fermi’s golden rule and the linearized BTE (which we solved exactly to leading order in
\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}/k_{0}
),
we found that the quasiparticle decay rate and the conductivity exhibit three temperature regimes:
the low- and high-temperature limits reproduce the familiar BG behavior, while the intermediate
T^{2}/T^{-2}

scaling emerges from the toroidal Fermi surface and the separation of the momentum scales
\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}
and
k_{0}
.
Corrections of order
\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}/k_{0}
break translational invariance on the torus and
lead to additional angular harmonics in the collision kernel. However, we expect these subleading terms
only renormalize the eigenvalues of the collision operator by
\mathcal{O}(\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}/k_{0})

and therefore are not expected to modify the power-law exponents of the leading terms
that we obtain in the strict thin-torus limit.

Our predictions can be tested experimentally. The quasiparticle decay rate is accessible
in ARPES experiments as a linewidth, and in materials with a toroidal Fermi surface and linear dispersion
along the minor-radius direction, we predict an intermediate window where
\Gamma\propto T^{2}
.
The corresponding temperature dependence of the transport lifetime, and thus the conductivity, can be probed via
standard four-probe measurements, for which we predict
\sigma\propto T^{-2}
, i.e., a resistivity
\rho\propto T^{2}

in the same regime. Importantly, the onset of this intermediate regime is controlled by

T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}\propto\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}\propto\mu
, so tuning the chemical potential should shift the
lower crossover temperature, providing a direct way to distinguish this
phonon mechanism from other sources of
T^{2}
resistivity. While a
T^{2}
resistivity is commonly attributed to
intraband electron–electron scattering
5
;
4
, our results show that in NLSs
with a toroidal Fermi surface, a purely electron–phonon mechanism can generate the same temperature dependence.
We also find that the Fermi-surface geometry lead to an enhanced conductivity anisotropy
in the high-temperature regime.

In materials hosting additional trivial Fermi pockets or multiple nodal loops,
these channels will contribute parallel conduction paths with their own, typically single-scale BG behavior.
Nevertheless, as long as a toroidal pocket with
\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}\ll k_{0}
remains well defined and dominates the
low-energy density of states, the two-scale structure and the intermediate
T^{2}/T^{-2}

regime should survive as robust features of the total response.

Several refinements and extensions are left for future work, such as including crystal anisotropy,
non-circular nodal lines, additional Fermi pockets, and analysing how they renormalize prefactors
and/or shift the crossover scales
T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}
,
T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}
. We expect the existence of two BG scales
and an intermediate
T^{2}/T^{-2}
window to be a generic consequence of Fermi surfaces with two widely
separated characteristic momenta, even when the assumptions of an ideal circular nodal ring and
a perfectly isotropic elastic medium considered here are relaxed. By contrast, quantitative features
such as the precise values
\sigma_{zz}/\sigma_{xx}=2
at low
T
and
4
at high
T
are
geometry-specific and will be modified by crystal anisotropy and additional pockets.
It would also be interesting to extend our study to thermal transport and thermoelectric response,
by evaluating the corresponding Boltzmann integrals for the heat current and extracting the temperature dependence of
the thermal conductivity and the Seebeck coefficient across the two BG scales.
Including impurity and electron–electron scattering on an equal footing would
enable a more quantitative comparison with experiments on specific materials.

The equation can be cast in the form

in a double Fourier series as

\displaystyle e^{i}_{\kappa}(\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i}_{\kappa,mn}e^{im\theta+in\varphi},

(73)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{i}(\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda^{i}_{mn}e^{im\theta+in\varphi},

(74)

\displaystyle f(\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{mn}e^{im\theta+in\varphi},

(75)

and substituting the Fourier expansions into Eq. (
70
) yields

e^{i}_{\kappa,mn}=(2\pi)^{2}\left(f_{00}-f_{mn}\right)\Lambda^{i}_{mn},

(76)

and thus

\Lambda^{i}_{mn}=\frac{e^{i}_{\kappa,mn}}{(2\pi)^{2}\left(f_{00}-f_{mn}\right)}.

(77)

Since
e^{i}_{\kappa}
are simple trigonometric functions (see Eq. (
13
)),
only a few Fourier coefficients are non-vanishing.
Then, using the even parity of
f
:

f_{m,n}=f_{-m,n}=f_{m,-n}=f_{-m,-n},

(78)

we arrive at

\displaystyle\Lambda^{i}(\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{\kappa}^{i}(\theta,\varphi)}{(2\pi)^{2}\left(f_{00}-f_{11}\right)},\qquad i=x,y

(79)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{z}(\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{\kappa}^{z}(\theta,\varphi)}{(2\pi)^{2}\left(f_{00}-f_{01}\right)}.

(80)

where

f_{mn}=\int\frac{d\varphi\,d\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}f(\theta,\varphi)\cos(m\theta)\cos(n\varphi).

(81)

This yields the transport lifetime expressions in
Eqs. (
42
)
and (
43
).

Appendix C
Momentum transfer

In this Appendix, we derive the thin-torus expression for the magnitude of the momentum transfer
between two states on the toroidal Fermi surface, Eq. (
38
).
Using the parameterization in Eqs.(
6
)–(
8
),
the momentum transfer
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{k}
can be decomposed as

\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}_{0}+\mathbf{q^{\prime}}

(82)

where the major-radius contribution is

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{0}

\displaystyle=k_{0}\left(\cos\theta^{\prime}-\cos\theta\right)\hat{x}+k_{0}\left(\sin\theta^{\prime}-\sin\theta\right)\hat{y},

(83)

and the minor-radius correction reads

\displaystyle\mathbf{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}\left(\cos\varphi^{\prime}\cos\theta^{\prime}-\cos\varphi\cos\theta\right)\hat{x}+\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}(\cos\varphi^{\prime}\sin\theta^{\prime}-

\displaystyle-\cos\varphi\sin\theta)\hat{y}+\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}\left(\sin\varphi^{\prime}-\sin\varphi\right)\hat{z}.

(84)

A straightforward algebra then gives

\displaystyle|\mathbf{q}|^{2}=4k_{0}^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}{2}\right)\bigg[1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}}{k_{0}}(\cos\varphi^{\prime}+\cos\varphi)+

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}}{k_{0}}\right)^{2}\cos\varphi^{\prime}\cos\varphi\bigg]+4\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}}{2}\right).

(85)

To leading order in
\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}/k_{0}\ll 1
we may drop the terms in square brackets beyond unity,
which leads to Eq. (
38
).

Appendix D
Decay rate

In this Appendix, we present details of the analytical
evaluation of the quasiparticle decay rate discussed in Sec.
IV
.
We use Eq. (
46
) to extract asymptotic forms of
\Gamma(T)

in the relevant temperature regimes.

D.1
Low temperature

In the regime
T\ll T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}
, i.e.,
\beta c_{l}k_{0}\gg\beta c_{l}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}\gg 1
,
the factor
1/\sinh(2\beta c_{l}Q)
in Eq. (
46
) exponentially
suppresses contributions from all but the smallest momentum transfers.
Hence, the dominant contribution arises from the vicinity
\theta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\simeq 0
,
where we may linearize the sine terms by using
\sin x\simeq x
and set
\cos\varphi^{\prime}\simeq 1
.
This yields

\Gamma=B\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\varphi^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\theta^{\prime}\frac{\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}\theta^{\prime 2}+\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\varphi^{\prime 2}}}{\sinh\left(2\beta c_{l}\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}\theta^{\prime 2}+\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\varphi^{\prime 2}}\right)},

(86)

with

B=\frac{8g_{0}^{2}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}k_{0}}{\pi^{2}\rho_{M}c_{l}v_{0}}.

(87)

Introducing the dimensionless variables

x=2\beta c_{l}k_{0}\theta^{\prime},\qquad y=2\beta c_{l}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}\varphi^{\prime},

(88)

we obtain

\Gamma=\frac{B}{(2\beta c_{l})^{3}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}k_{0}}\int_{0}^{\beta c_{l}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}\pi}\!\!\!\!dy\int_{0}^{\beta c_{l}k_{0}\pi}\!\!\!\!dx\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{\sinh\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}.

(89)

Since
\beta c_{l}k_{0},\beta c_{l}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}\gg 1
, the upper integration limits
can be extended to
\infty
.
Switching to polar coordinates then gives

\Gamma=\frac{B}{(2\beta c_{l})^{3}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}k_{0}}\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\;dr\frac{r^{2}}{\sinh r}.

(90)

Using the standard integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,r^{2}/\sinh r=\frac{7}{2}\zeta(3)
,
we finally obtain Eq. (
48
).

D.2
Intermediate temperature

In the intermediate regime
T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}\ll T\ll T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}
, i.e.,

\beta c_{l}k_{0}\gg 1\gg\beta c_{l}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}
,
we may still linearize
\sin\theta^{\prime}
but not
\sin\varphi^{\prime}
.
Using Eq. (
88
) in Eq. (
46
) gives

\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{B}{(2\beta c_{l})^{2}k_{0}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\varphi^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\beta c_{l}k_{0}\pi}\!\!\!\!dx\frac{\cos^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})\sqrt{x^{2}+b^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})}}{\sinh\sqrt{x^{2}+b^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})}},

(91)

where
b(\varphi^{\prime})=2\beta c_{l}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}\sin(\varphi^{\prime})
.
To lowest order in
\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}/k_{0}
we may set
b(\varphi^{\prime})\simeq 0
,
and since
\beta c_{l}k_{0}\gg 1
we extend the upper limit of the
x
integral to
\infty
.
This gives

\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{B}{(2\beta c_{l})^{2}k_{0}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\varphi^{\prime}\cos^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\frac{x}{\sinh x}.

(92)

Using
\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,x/\sinh x=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}
and
\int_{0}^{\pi/2}d\varphi^{\prime}\cos^{2}\varphi^{\prime}=\pi/4
,
we finally obtain Eq. (
50
).

D.3
High temperature

Finally, in the regime
T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}\ll T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}\ll T
, i.e.,

1\gg\beta c_{l}k_{0}\gg\beta c_{l}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}
,
we may linearize the denominator in Eq. (
46
) obtaining

\Gamma=B\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\varphi^{\prime}\cos^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\theta^{\prime}\frac{1}{2\beta c_{l}},

(93)

which immediately gives Eq. (
51
).

Appendix E
Transport lifetimes

In this Appendix, we evaluate Eqs. (
56
)
and (
57
) in the three temperature regimes,
following the same procedure as in Appendix
D
.

E.1
Low temperature

When
T\ll T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}
, we expand the trigonometric functions to lowest order, obtaining (
i=x,y,z
)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm{tr}}^{i}=2B\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\!\!\!\!d\varphi^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\!\!\!\!d\theta^{\prime}\frac{(\varphi^{\prime 2}+a_{i}\,\theta^{\prime 2})\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}\theta^{\prime 2}+\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\varphi^{\prime 2}}}{\mathrm{sinh}\left(2\beta c_{l}\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}\theta^{\prime 2}+\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\varphi^{\prime 2}}\right)},

(94)

where
B
is defined in Eq. (
87
) and
a_{x,y}=1,a_{z}=0
.
After the change of variables (
88
), the integral becomes

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm{tr}}^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{2B}{(2\beta c_{l})^{5}\kappa^{3}_{\mathrm{F}}k_{0}}\int_{0}^{\beta c_{l}\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}\pi}\!\!\!\!dy\int_{0}^{\beta c_{l}k_{0}\pi}\!\!\!\!dx\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{\mathrm{sinh}\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\left(y^{2}+a_{i}\left(\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}}{k_{0}}\right)^{2}x^{2}\right).

(95)

Extending the integration limits to
\infty

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\frac{x^{2}\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{\mathrm{sinh}\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}=\frac{93\pi\zeta(5)}{8},

(96)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm{tr}}^{i}=\frac{93\pi\zeta(5)B}{4(2\beta c_{l})^{5}\kappa^{3}_{\mathrm{F}}k_{0}}\left[1+a_{i}\left(\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}}{k_{0}}\right)^{2}\right].

(97)

Neglecting the term of order
(\kappa_{\mathrm{F}}/k_{0})^{2}

58
).

E.2
Intermediate temperature

When
T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}\ll T\ll T_{\mathrm{BG}}^{(\mathrm{tor})}
, we may still linearize
\sin\theta^{\prime}
,
but not
\sin\varphi^{\prime}
. Using Eq. (
88
) gives

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm{tr}}^{i}=\frac{2B}{(2\beta c_{l})^{2}k_{0}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\varphi^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\beta c_{l}k_{0}\pi}\!\!\!\!dx\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+b^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})}}{\mathrm{sinh}\sqrt{x^{2}+b^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})}}

\displaystyle\times\cos^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})\left[\sin^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})+a_{i}\left(\frac{x}{2\beta c_{l}k_{0}}\right)^{2}\cos(2\varphi^{\prime})\right],

As done in Eq. (

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm{tr}}^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{2B}{(2\beta c_{l})^{2}k_{0}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\varphi^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\frac{x}{\mathrm{sinh}\;x}\cos^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})

Performing the

Performing the
\varphi^{\prime}

\theta^{\prime}

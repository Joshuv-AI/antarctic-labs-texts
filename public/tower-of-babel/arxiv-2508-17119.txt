Moreover, in the case of S = S 1 S=S^{1} , we also classify the SU ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathrm{SU}(3) -invariant Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) -instantons over X X in the following sense. Letting Φ I \Phi_{I} , Φ J \Phi_{J} , Φ K \Phi_{K} denote the Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) -structures on X X induced from the complex structures I , J , K I,J,K in the hyperkähler triple, we prove that on each invariant S 1 S^{1} -bundle E ~ k → X \widetilde{E}_{k}\to X , k ∈ ℤ k\in\mathbb{Z} , the space of invariant Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) -instantons with respect to Φ L \Phi_{L} forms a one-parameter family modulo gauge. 1
Introduction

1.1
Background

The geometric structure of hyperkähler
8
-manifolds
(X,g,(I,J,K))
is remarkably rich. Essentially by definition, we may view
X
as a Riemannian manifold via the metric
g
, as a complex manifold with respect to any of the complex structures
I,J,K
, or a symplectic manifold via the Kähler forms
\omega_{I}
,
\omega_{J}
,
\omega_{K}
. Moreover, for any of the complex structures
L\in\{I,J,K\}
, we may also define a non-vanishing
(4,0)
-form
\Upsilon_{L}
, thereby endowing
X
with three distinct Calabi–Yau structures. In turn, these Calabi–Yau structures induce
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures
\Phi_{I},\Phi_{J},\Phi_{K}\in\Omega^{4}(X)
via the formula
\Phi_{L}=\frac{1}{2}\omega_{L}^{2}+\mathrm{Re}(\Upsilon_{L})
.

This means that hyperkähler
8
-manifolds are host to a wealth of natural gauge-theoretic PDE. Indeed, for connections
A
on bundles
E\to X
, one may ask for
A
to be, say, a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton with respect to a particular
\Phi_{L}
, or a primitive Hermitian Yang-Mills (pHYM) connection with respect to a given
L
. Even more stringently, one may ask for
A
to be an
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instanton
(or
hyperholomorphic connection
), which means that
A
is pHYM with respect to
I
,
J
, and
K
simultaneously:

A\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(2)\text{-instanton }\ \iff\ A\text{ is pHYM with respect to }I,J,K.

In view of the inclusions
\mathrm{Sp}(2)\leq\mathrm{SU}(4)\leq\mathrm{Spin}(7)
, these gauge-theoretic conditions are nested accordingly:

\mathrm{Sp}(2)\text{-instanton}\ \implies\ \text{pHYM connection for }L\ \implies\ \mathrm{Spin}(7)\text{-instanton for }\Phi_{L}.

(1.1)

While there is a vast literature on pHYM connections, and a growing body of work on
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons in recent years, there are relatively few studies of
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instantons at present. In particular, there seem to be few explicit
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instantons in the literature. As such, one of the central aims of this work is to construct concrete examples of such objects.

Relatedly, it is interesting to ask under what circumstances either of the implications in (
1.1
) might be reversed. When
X
is compact, the answer is quite simple. Indeed, provided that
X
admits at least one pHYM connection with respect to
L
, Lewis
[
8
]
proved that the
\Phi_{L}
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons coincide with the
L
-pHYM connections. Similarly, if
X
admits at least one
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instanton, then a result of Verbitsky
[
12
]
implies that the class of
L
-pHYM connections (for
any

L
) coincides with that of the
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instantons.

When
X
is asymptotically conical (AC) of rate
\nu\leq-\frac{10}{3}
, there exist analogous results. That is, assuming that an AC
L
-pHYM connection exists, Papoulias
[
10
]
showed that the
\Phi_{L}
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons with sufficiently fast decay coincide with the AC
L
-pHYM connections. Moreover, the rate restriction
\nu\leq-\frac{10}{3}
is sharp. Similarly, the last two authors
[
9
]
showed that if an AC
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instanton exists, then the AC
L
-pHYM connections with sufficiently fast decay coincide with the AC
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instantons. It is not known whether the rate restriction
\nu\leq-\frac{10}{3}
in
[
9
]
is sharp or could be improved.

1.2
Main results

In this paper, we will construct explicit examples of
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instantons, pHYM connections, and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons on the 8-manifold
X=T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
equipped with its Calabi hyperkähler structure
(g,(I,J,K))
. There are two reasons to prioritize this particular space. First,
X
has a high degree of symmetry, making it a natural testing ground for examples and conjectures. Indeed,
X
is cohomogeneity-one under an
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-action, with the principal orbits equivariantly diffeomorphic to the Aloff–Wallach manifold
N_{1,1}=\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{U}(1)
, and with the zero section
\mathbb{CP}^{2}=\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{U}(2)
serving as the unique singular orbit. In fact, a result of Dancer–Swann
[
6
]
asserts that
T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is the unique non-flat, complete cohomogeneity-one hyperkähler 8-manifold. Second, since
X
is asymptotically conical of rate
\nu=-2>-\frac{10}{3}
, it is outside the scope of the Papoulias
[
10
]
and Madnick–Windes
[
9
]
results.

In
\lx@sectionsign
5
, for each
L\in\{I,J,K\}
, we completely classify
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-invariant
\Phi_{L}
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons over
X
having gauge group
S=S^{1}
. As we now explain, this is essentially a three-step process. First, we classify the homogeneous
S^{1}
-bundles
P_{n}\to N_{1,1}
, finding one for each integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, pull those back to
S^{1}
-bundles called
E_{n}\to X^{*}
over the principal locus
X^{*}=T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}-\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, and then prove that
E_{n}
extends equivariantly across the zero section to an
S^{1}
-bundle
\widetilde{E}_{k}\to X
if and only if
n=2k
is even. Moreover, such an extension is unique.

Second, we classify the
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-invariant
\Phi_{L}
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons on
E_{n}\to X^{*}
by solving the appropriate ODE system. For each
L\in\{I,J,K\}
, we find an explicit
3
-parameter family of such connections. Finally, in the case of
n=2k
, using the Tao–Tian removable singularity theorem
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
, we verify that the class of invariant
\Phi_{L}
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons that extends across the zero section forms a one-parameter family. In fact, more is true:

Theorem 1.1
.

On each
S^{1}
-bundle
\widetilde{E}_{k}\to T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and for each
L\in\{I,J,K\}
, the collection of
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-invariant
\Phi_{L}
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons on
\widetilde{E}_{k}
is a one-parameter family
\{A_{L,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
modulo gauge transformations.

Moreover, among the three families
\{A_{I,t}\}
,
\{A_{J,t}\}
, and
\{A_{K,t}\}
, each pair intersects at only at
A_{I,0}=A_{J,0}=A_{K,0}
. In fact,
A_{L,0}
is the unique connection in the
A_{L,t}
family that is primitive Hermitian Yang-Mills with respect to
L
.

Corollary 1.2
.

On each
S^{1}
-bundle
\widetilde{E}_{k}\to T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a unique
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-invariant
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instanton (namely,
A_{I,0}=A_{J,0}=A_{K,0}
). For
k\neq 0
, this
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instanton is not flat.

In
\lx@sectionsign
6
, we study
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-invariant instantons over
X
with gauge group
S=\mathrm{SO}(3)
. As in the
S^{1}
case, we classify the homogeneous
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-bundles over
N_{1,1}
, finding one for each integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and thereby obtain
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-bundles
E_{n}\to X^{*}
over the principal locus. We show that
E_{n}
extends equivariantly across the zero section to an
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-bundle
\widetilde{E}_{k}\to X
if and only if
n=2k
is even. When
k\neq 0
, the extension is unique, but the trivial bundle
E_{0}\to X^{*}
admits two distinct extensions, which we label
\widetilde{E}_{0}
and
\widetilde{E}_{\wedge}
.

We then consider separately the cases of
k\neq 0
and
k=0
. For
k\neq 0
, the
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-invariant connections on
E_{k}\to X^{*}
essentially reduce to those on
S^{1}
-bundles. Accordingly, the analogue of Theorem
1.1
holds in this setting. On the other hand, for
k=0
, the
\Phi_{L}
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton equations are considerably more formidable. As such, we restrict attention to the subclass of
L
-pHYM connections on
E_{0}\to X^{*}
, obtaining a
3
-parameter family modulo gauge transformations. However, in that family, the only element that extends across the zero section is the canonical connection, which is flat. To summarize:

Theorem 1.3
.

1.

On each
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-bundle
\widetilde{E}_{k}\to T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
for
k\neq 0
, and for each
L\in\{I,J,K\}
, the collection of
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-invariant
\Phi_{L}
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons on
\widetilde{E}_{k}
is a one-parameter family
\{A_{L,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
modulo gauge transformations. Moreover,
A_{I,0}=A_{J,0}=A_{K,0}
is the unique connection in the
A_{L,t}
family that is primitive Hermitian Yang-Mills with respect to
L
, and it is an
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instanton.

2.

On the
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-bundles
\widetilde{E}_{0}\to T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and
\widetilde{E}_{\wedge}\to T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, for each
L\in\{I,J,K\}
, the only
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-invariant connection that is primitive Hermitian Yang-Mills with respect to
L
is the (flat) canonical connection.

This work is organized as follows. In
\lx@sectionsign
2
we introduce
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-,
\mathrm{SU}(4)
-, and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons, and study the relationships between those connections on hyperkähler 8-manifolds. Note that we use the term “
\mathrm{SU}(4)
-instanton” for “primitive HYM connection,” and abbreviate
\Phi_{L}
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
as simply
L
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
. In
\lx@sectionsign
3
, we discuss the geometry of the Calabi manifold
T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. Section
4
consists of additional preliminaries on homogeneous bundles and invariant connections. Finally, in
\lx@sectionsign
5
we classify instantons over
T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
with gauge group
S=S^{1}
, and in
\lx@sectionsign
6
we classify instantons with gauge group

.

2
Instantons in higher dimensions

The purpose of this section is to set foundations. In
\lx@sectionsign
2.1
, we recall the notions of “
H
-instanton” and “
\Omega
-ASD instanton” on suitable Riemannian manifolds of dimension
n\geq 4
. In particular, we highlight three fundamental classes: the
\mathrm{SU}(m)
-instantons,
\mathrm{Sp}(m)
-instantons, and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons. Then, in
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
, we specialize to the setting of hyperkähler 8-manifolds, establishing relationships between
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-,
\mathrm{SU}(4)
-, and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons.

2.1
Preliminaries

We begin by recalling two standard notions of “instanton” over manifolds of dimension at least four. For this, let
(X^{n},g)
be an oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold with
n\geq 4
, let
E\to X
be a Hermitian vector bundle, and let
\mathrm{ad}_{E}\to X
denote the adjoint bundle. Let
A
be a connection on
E
, and let
F_{A}\in\Omega^{2}(X;\mathrm{ad}_{E})=\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}X)\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E})
denote the curvature of
A
.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose
X
is equipped with a
(n-4)
-form
\Omega\in\Omega^{n-4}(X)
. We say that
A
is an
\Omega
-anti-self-dual
(or
\Omega
-ASD)
instanton
if its curvature satisfies

\ast F_{A}=-\Omega\wedge F_{A}.

We note that
\Omega
-ASD instantons are
\Omega
-Yang–Mills
, i.e. they satisfy the
\Omega
-Yang–Mills equation
:

d_{A}(*F_{A})=-d\Omega\wedge F_{A}.

The importance of such connections comes from the fact that they are absolute minimisers of the functional

\text{YM}_{\Omega}(A)=\int_{X}|F_{A}|^{2}\text{vol}-\int_{X}\text{tr}(F_{A}\wedge F_{A})\wedge\Omega.

In this paper, the forms
\Omega
that we consider are all closed. In this case,
\Omega
-ASD instantons are also
Yang–Mills connections
, meaning that
d_{A}(*F_{A})=0
.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
X
is equipped with an
H
-structure, where
H\leq\mathrm{SO}(n)
is a closed subgroup and
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{so}(n)
denotes its Lie algebra. (For example, if the holonomy group of
g
is contained in
H
, then
X
admits an
H
-structure.) Splitting
\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}\cong\mathfrak{so}(n)=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
with respect to the Killing form yields a decomposition

\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}X)\cong\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}}.

We say
A
is an
H
-instanton
if its curvature takes values in
\mathfrak{h}
in the sense that

F_{A}\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{h}}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}).

We now provide three examples of the above definitions. All three will play an important role in this work.

Example 2.3

(
H=\mathrm{SU}(m)
)
.

Let
(X^{2m},g,J,\omega_{J},\Upsilon_{J})
be a Calabi–Yau
2m
-manifold, where
g
is the Riemannian metric,
J
is a
g
-orthogonal parallel complex structure,
\omega_{J}\in\Omega^{2}(X)
is the Kähler form, and
\Upsilon_{J}\in\Omega^{m,0}(X)
is the holomorphic volume form. It is well-known that the following two conditions on a connection
A
are equivalent:

1.

A
is an
\mathrm{SU}(m)
-instanton:
F_{A}\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{su}(m)}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E})
.

2.

A
is
\Omega_{J}
-ASD for
\Omega_{J}=\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\omega_{J}^{n-2}\in\Omega^{2n-4}(X)
.

We often refer to such connections as
J
-
\mathrm{SU}(m)
-instantons
, especially when
X
is equipped with several different Calabi–Yau structures (e.g., see the subsequent example). In the literature, the
\mathbb{C}
-linear extensions of such connections are called
primitive Hermitian Yang-Mills (pHYM)
.

Example 2.4

(
H=\mathrm{Sp}(m)
)
.

Let
(X^{4m},g,(I,J,K),(\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K}))
be a hyperkähler
4m
-manifold. Here,
g
is the Riemannian metric,
(I,J,K)
is a triple of
g
-orthogonal parallel complex structures satisfying
IJ=K
, and
\omega_{L}\in\Omega^{2}(X)
denotes the Kähler form of
L\in\{I,J,K\}
, meaning that
\omega_{L}(X,Y)=g(LX,Y)
. The following conditions are equivalent
[
9
]
:

1.

A
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(m)
-instanton:
F_{A}\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{sp}(m)}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E})
.

2.

A
is
\Theta
-ASD for
\Theta=\frac{1}{(2m-1)!}(\omega_{I}^{2}+\omega_{J}^{2}+\omega_{K}^{2})^{m-1}\in\Omega^{4m-4}(X)
.

3.

A
is simultaneously
\Omega_{I}
-,
\Omega_{J}
-, and
\Omega_{K}
-ASD, where
\Omega_{L}:=\frac{1}{(2m-2)!}\omega_{L}^{2m-2}\in\Omega^{4m-4}(X)
.

4.

A
is simultaneously an
I
-
\mathrm{SU}(2m)
-,
J
-
\mathrm{SU}(2m)
-, and
K
-
\mathrm{SU}(2m)
-instanton.

We remark that in dimension four (i.e., when
m=1
), the
\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-instanton condition coincides with the classical ASD equation.

Example 2.5

(
H=\mathrm{Spin}(7)
)
.

Let
(X^{8},g,\Phi)
be a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-manifold, where
g
is the Riemannian metric and
\Phi\in\Omega^{4}(X)
is the Cayley
4
-form. It is well-known that the following two conditions on a connection
A
are equivalent:

1.

A
is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton:
F_{A}\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{spin}(7)}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E})
.

2.

A
is
\Phi
-ASD.

2.2
Gauge theory on hyperkähler
8
-manifolds

This work concerns gauge theory on hyperkähler
8
-manifolds. As we now explain, there are various classes of “
H
-instantons” that one may consider in such a setting, essentially due to the inclusions
\mathrm{Sp}(2)\leq\mathrm{SU}(4)\leq\mathrm{Spin}(7)
.

Let
(X^{8},g,(I,J,K),(\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K}))
be a hyperkähler
8
-manifold, let
E\to X
be a Hermitian vector bundle, and let
A
be a connection on
E
. By the remarks in Example
2.4
, we see that

A\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(2)\text{-instanton}\ \iff\ A\text{ is an }I\text{-}\mathrm{SU}(4)\text{-},\,J\text{-}\mathrm{SU}(4)\text{-},\text{ and }K\text{-}\mathrm{SU}(4)\text{-instanton.}

(2.1)

Now, for each
L\in\{I,J,K\}
, we can view
X
as a Calabi–Yau
4
-fold via the Calabi–Yau structure
(g,L,\omega_{L},\Upsilon_{L})
, where the holomorphic volume form
\Upsilon_{I}\in\Omega^{4,0}(X)
is given by
\Upsilon_{I}=\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{J}+i\omega_{K})^{2}
(and similarly for cyclic permutations of
\{I,J,K\}
). In turn, each Calabi–Yau structure induces a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi_{L}\in\Omega^{4}(X)
via the formula

\Phi_{L}=\frac{1}{2}\omega_{L}^{2}+\mathrm{Re}(\Upsilon_{L}),

(2.2)

so

\displaystyle\Phi_{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{I}^{2}+\omega_{J}^{2}-\omega_{K}^{2}),

\displaystyle\Phi_{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(-\omega_{I}^{2}+\omega_{J}^{2}+\omega_{K}^{2}),

\displaystyle\Phi_{K}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{I}^{2}-\omega_{J}^{2}+\omega_{K}^{2}).

(2.3)

We shall say that
A
is an
L
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton
if
A
is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton with respect to
\Phi_{L}
. It is well-known that (thanks to the inclusion
\mathfrak{su}(4)\subset\mathfrak{spin}(7)
), one has the implication

A\text{ is an }L\text{-}\mathrm{SU}(4)\text{-instanton }\ \implies A\text{ is an }L\text{-}\mathrm{Spin}(7)\text{-instanton.}

(2.4)

In summary, every
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instanton is an
L
-
\mathrm{SU}(4)
-instanton, and every
L
-
\mathrm{SU}(4)
-instanton is an
L
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton.

In the other direction, we may ask what conditions result from requiring that a connection
A
be, say, simultaneously an
I
-
\mathrm{SU}(4)
- and
J
-
\mathrm{SU}(4)
-instanton, or simultaneously an
I
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
- and
J
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton. The following two propositions address all such pairwise and triple intersections.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a hyperkähler
8
-manifold, and let
A
be a connection on a Hermitian vector bundle. The following are equivalent:

1.

A
is an
I
-
\mathrm{SU}(4)
-instanton.

2.

A
is both an
I
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton and a
K
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton.

The analogous result holds for cyclic permutations of
\{I,J,K\}
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a connection on a Hermitian vector bundle. Observing that
\Phi_{K}+\Phi_{I}=\omega_{I}^{2}
, we have

\textstyle F_{A}\wedge\Phi_{K}+F_{A}\wedge\Phi_{I}=2(F_{A}\wedge\frac{1}{2}\omega_{I}^{2}).

(2.5)

(a)
\implies
(b). Suppose
A
is an
I
-
\mathrm{SU}(4)
-instanton. Then
2(F_{A}\wedge\frac{1}{2}\omega_{I}^{2})=-2\ast\!F_{A}
. Moreover, by (
2.4
),
A
is an
I
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton, so
F_{A}\wedge\Phi_{I}=-\ast\!F_{A}
. Equation (
2.5
) now implies that
A
is a
K
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton.

(b)
\implies
(a). Suppose
A
is both an
I
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton and a
K
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton. Then (
2.5
) implies
-2\ast\!F_{A}=2(F_{A}\wedge\frac{1}{2}\omega_{I}^{2})
, from which it follows that
A
is an
I
-
\mathrm{SU}(4)
-instanton.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
X
be a hyperkähler
8
-manifold, and let
A
be a connection on a Hermitian vector bundle. The following are equivalent:

1.

A
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instanton.

2.

A
is simultaneously a
J
-
\mathrm{SU}(4)
- and
K
-
\mathrm{SU}(4)
-instanton.

3.

A
is simultaneously an
I
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
,
J
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-, and
K
-
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton.

Proof.

The implication (a)
\implies
(b) is immediate. The implication (b)
\implies
(c) follows from Proposition
2.6
. Finally, (c)
\implies
(a) also follows from Proposition

The Calabi manifold
X=T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}

This section introduces the Calabi manifold
X=T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, the central player of this work. In
\lx@sectionsign
3.1
, we rapidly review the cohomogeneity-one structure of
X
. To exploit this symmetry, we will make frequent use of the Maurer–Cartan form on
\mathrm{SU}(3)
, which we discuss in
\lx@sectionsign
3.2
. Finally, in
\lx@sectionsign
3.3

.

3.1
The cohomogeneity-one structure of
T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}

A
cohomogeneity-one
manifold is a connected smooth Riemannian manifold with an action by isometries of a compact Lie group
G
having an orbit of codimension one.
We consider the canonical projection
X\to X/G
onto the orbit space
X/G
, which is one-dimensional (with or without boundary) and equip it with the quotient topology relative to the projection.
Assuming that
X
is simply connected, the inverse images of the interior points of the orbit space
X/G
are known as
principal orbits
, while the inverse images of the boundary points are called
singular orbits
.

For a non-compact cohomogeneity-one manifold with one singular orbit, we have
X/G=[0,+\infty)
up to rescaling.
It can be shown that that there are two possible isotropy groups: the isotropy group at each point of the principal orbits, which we call
principal isotropy group
and denote it by
K
, and the isotropy group at each point of the singular orbit, which we call
singular isotropy group
and denote it by
H
. Each principal
G
-orbit is then equivariantly diffeomorphic to
G/K
, and
X_{\mathrm{sing}}\cong G/H
. The collection
G\geq H\geq K
is called the
group diagram
, and it can be shown that we can recover the cohomogeneity one manifold from it.
We refer the reader for example to
[
3
,
1
]
for a full description of cohomogeneity one manifolds.

Let
X=T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. Recall that the Lie group
G=\mathrm{SU}(3)
acts transitively on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
with stabilizer
H=\mathrm{U}(2)
. This induces an
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-action on
X
in the obvious way: a group element
g\in\mathrm{SU}(3)
acts on a covector
\alpha\in T_{p}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
by
g\cdot\alpha:=g^{*}\alpha\in T^{*}_{g^{-1}p}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

Now, the
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-action on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is isometric with respect to the Fubini–Study metric, and hence preserves the norms of covectors. In fact, the
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-action on the unit sphere bundle
\{\alpha\in T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}\colon|\alpha|=1\}
is transitive with stabilizer
K\cong\mathrm{U}(1)
, whereas the
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-action on the zero section is transitive with stabilizer
H\cong\mathrm{U}(2)
. As subgroups of
\mathrm{SU}(3)
, these isotropy groups are

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\mathrm{U}(1)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\theta}&0&0\\
0&e^{i\theta}&0\\
0&0&e^{-2i\theta}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SU}(3)\colon\theta\in[0,2\pi)\right\},

(3.1)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\mathrm{U}(2)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&\det(\overline{A})\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SU}(3)\colon A\in\mathrm{U}(2)\right\}\!.

(3.2)

In summary,
G=\mathrm{SU}(3)
acts with cohomogeneity-one on
X=T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. The principal orbits are equivariantly diffeomorphic to the Aloff–Wallach space
N_{1,1}=\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{U}(1)=G/K
, whereas the unique singular orbit is equivariantly diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{2}=\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{U}(2)=G/H
. In particular, the principal locus
X^{*}=T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}-\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is diffeomorphic to
(0,\infty)\times N_{1,1}
.

4
Invariant bundles and connections

This section reviews the basics of homogeneous bundles and invariant connections. In
\lx@sectionsign
4.1
, we define homogeneous bundles
P\to G/L
over homogeneous spaces and explain how to construct them. Then, in
\lx@sectionsign
4.2
, we consider bundles
E\to X^{*}
over the principal locus
X^{*}
of an arbitrary cohomogeneity one manifold
X=X^{*}\cup X_{\mathrm{sing}}
with unique singular orbit
X_{\mathrm{sing}}
that restrict to homogeneous bundles on the principal orbits. The main result (Proposition
4.4
) is an easily checked necessary and sufficient condition for such bundles
E\to X^{*}
to extend across the singular orbit. Although this extension result is well-known to experts, we were unable to locate a proof in the literature. Section
4.3
recalls Wang’s theorem on invariant connections on homogeneous bundles
P\to G/L
, and
\lx@sectionsign
4.4
discusses invariant connections on bundles
E\to X^{*}
over principal loci.

4.1
Homogeneous principal bundles

We begin by recalling the concept of a “homogeneous
S
-bundle”. Let
G/L
be a homogeneous space, where
G
is a compact connected Lie group and
L\leq G
is a closed subgroup. In actual practice, we will take
G=\mathrm{SU}(3)
and
L=\mathrm{U}(1)
or
\mathrm{U}(2)
, where the embeddings are given by (
3.1
)-(
3.2
), so that
G/L=\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{U}(1)=N_{1,1}
or
G/L=\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{U}(2)=\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. Let
S
be a compact Lie group, which will serve as our desired structure group.

Definition 4.1
.

A
homogeneous
S
-bundle
over
G/L
is a principal
S
-bundle
\pi\colon P\to G/L
whose total space admits a left
G
-action that lifts the
G
-action on
G/K
. Two homogeneous
S
-bundles
P_{1}\to G/L
and
P_{2}\to G/L
are
isomorphic
if there exists a principal bundle isomorphism
P_{1}\to P_{2}
that is
G
-equivariant.

Note that if
P\to G/L
is a homogeneous
S
-bundle, then
P
admits both a left
G
-action as well as a right
S
-action. Together, the
(G\times S)
-action on
P
given by
(g,s)\cdot p=gps^{-1}
is transitive, and the stabilizer is isomorphic to
L
. In fact, fixing a base point
p\in P
, there exists a unique Lie group homomorphism
\psi\colon L\to S
such that
p\psi(\ell)=\ell p
, and the stabilizer at
p
is given by
\mathrm{Stab}_{G\times S}(p)=(\mathrm{Id}\times\psi)(L)=\{(\ell,\psi(\ell))\colon\ell\in L\}\leq G\times S
. Consequently, the smooth manifold
P
is itself a homogeneous space
P\cong(G\times S)/L
.

It turns out that this process can be reversed. Indeed, the following standard result classifies homogeneous
S
-bundles over
G/L
in terms of in terms of Lie group homomorphisms
\psi\colon L\to S
. Say that two homomorphisms
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\colon L\to S
are
(element) conjugate
if there exists
s\in S
such that
\psi_{2}=s\psi_{1}s^{-1}
.

Lemma 4.2

(Construction lemma)
.

For each Lie group homomorphism
\psi\colon L\to S
, let

P_{\psi}:=G\times_{\psi}S=\frac{G\times S}{\sim},\ \text{ where }(g,s)\sim(g\ell,s\psi(\ell))\ \text{ for }(g,s)\in G\times S\text{ and }\ell\in L.

Then
\pi_{\psi}\colon P_{\psi}\to G/L
via
\pi_{\psi}([g,s]_{\psi})=gL
is a homogeneous
S
-bundle. Moreover, every homogeneous
S
-bundle over
G/L
arises in this way. Finally,
P_{\psi_{1}}
is isomorphic to
P_{\psi_{2}}
as homogeneous
S
-bundles if and only if
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are element-conjugate.

4.2
The bundle extension lemma

Our goal is to study gauge-theoretic objects on suitable
S
-bundles over
X=X^{*}\cup X_{\mathrm{sing}}
, where
X=T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and
X_{\mathrm{sing}}=\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is the zero section. For a given structure group
S
, we will construct the “suitable
S
-bundles” over
T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
via the following process:

1.

For each homomorphism
\lambda\colon\mathrm{U}(1)\to S
, the construction lemma
4.2
yields a homogeneous
S
-bundle
P_{\lambda}\to N_{1,1}
. Letting
\mathrm{pr}\colon X^{*}\approx(0,\infty)\times N_{1,1}\to N_{1,1}
denote the projection, we obtain an
S
-bundle
E_{\lambda}:=\mathrm{pr}^{*}P_{\lambda}\to X^{*}
over the principal locus via pullback.

2.

For each homomorphism
\chi\colon\mathrm{U}(2)\to S
, the construction lemma
4.2
yields a homogeneous
S
-bundle
Q_{\chi}\to\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. Letting
\Pi\colon T^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{2}\to\mathbb{CP}^{2}
denote the projection, we obtain an
S
-bundle
\widetilde{E}_{\chi}:=\Pi^{*}Q_{\chi}\to X
over the entire manifold via pullback.

3.

Finally, for “matching pairs” of homomorphisms
(\lambda,\chi)
, the bundle
\widetilde{E}_{\chi}\to X
restricts to the principal locus to agree with
E_{\lambda}\to X^{*}
.

In this section, we provide an easily-checked necessary and sufficient criterion for determining whether two such homomorphisms
\lambda
and
\chi
“match,” and therefore whether
\widetilde{E}_{\chi}\to X
is an extension of
E_{\lambda}\to X^{*}
. This criterion appears to be well-known to experts, but we provide a proof for completeness. Although we are exclusively interested in the case where the group diagram
G\geq H\geq K
is
\mathrm{SU}(3)\geq\mathrm{U}(2)\geq\mathrm{U}(1)
, we will state the result in greater generality.

Setup 4.3
.

Let
X
be a cohomogeneity-one smooth manifold with a unique singular orbit
X_{\mathrm{sing}}\subset X
, and let
X^{*}=X-X_{\mathrm{sing}}
denote the principal locus. Suppose that
X
has group diagram
G\geq H\geq K
, where
G,H,K
are compact connected Lie groups.

•

Let
\iota\colon K\hookrightarrow H
denote inclusion, and let
p\colon G/K\to G/H
denote the natural projection.

•

Let
\mathrm{pr}\colon X^{*}\approx(0,\infty)\times(G/K)\to G/K
denote the projection.

•

Let
\Pi\colon X\to G/H
denote the natural extension of
p\circ\mathrm{pr}\colon X^{*}\to G/H
.

Proposition 4.4

(Bundle extension lemma)
.

Let
\lambda\colon K\to S
and
\chi\colon H\to S
be homomorphisms, and let
\pi_{\lambda}\colon P_{\lambda}\to G/K
and
\pi_{\chi}\colon Q_{\chi}\to G/H
denote the associated homogeneous
S
-bundles. The following are equivalent:

1.

The map
\lambda
is element conjugate to
\chi\circ\iota
.

2.

We have
P_{\lambda}\cong p^{*}Q_{\chi}
.

3.

The bundle
\widetilde{E}_{\chi}:=\Pi^{*}Q_{\chi}\to X
is an extension of
E_{\lambda}:=\mathrm{pr}^{*}P_{\lambda}\to X^{*}
.

Proof.

Before beginning, observe that

\displaystyle p^{*}Q_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\{(g_{1}K,[g_{2},s]_{\chi})\in G/K\times Q_{\chi}\colon g_{1},g_{2}\in G,s\in S,g_{1}H=g_{2}H\}

\displaystyle=\{(gK,[g,s]_{\chi})\in G/K\times Q_{\chi}\colon g\in G,s\in S\}.

In particular, we see that
p^{*}Q_{\chi}\to G/K
is a homogeneous
S
-bundle. Next, consider the following two maps from
G\times S
:

\displaystyle v\colon G\times S

\displaystyle\to p^{*}Q_{\chi};

\displaystyle q_{\chi\circ\iota}\colon G\times S

\displaystyle\to P_{\chi\circ\iota};

\displaystyle v(g,s)

\displaystyle=(gK,[g,s]_{\chi})

\displaystyle q_{\chi\circ\iota}(g,s)

\displaystyle=[g,s]_{\chi\circ\iota}.

One can check that the
v

q_{\chi\circ\iota}

\displaystyle v^{-1}(aK,[a,t]_{\chi})

\displaystyle=\{(ak,t\chi(\iota(k)))\in G\times S\colon k\in K\}=q_{\chi\circ\iota}^{-1}([a,t]_{\chi\circ\iota}).

Since
v\colon G\times S\to p^{*}Q_{\chi}
and
q_{\chi\circ\iota}\colon G\times S\to P_{\chi\circ\iota}
are
G
-equivariant surjective submersions that are constant on each other’s fibers, there exists unique
G
-equivariant diffeomorphism
F\colon P_{\chi\circ\iota}\to p^{*}Q_{\chi}
for which
v=F\circ q_{\chi\circ\iota}
. It is straightforward to check that
F
is, in fact, a principal bundle isomorphism, so that
p^{*}Q_{\chi}\cong P_{\chi\circ\iota}
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\times S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{\chi\circ\iota}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}Q_{\chi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/K}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{\chi\circ\iota}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

(1)
\iff
(2). By the construction lemma
4.2
, the map
\lambda
is element conjugate to
\chi\circ\iota
if and only if
P_{\lambda}
is isomorphic to
P_{\chi\circ\iota}=p^{*}Q_{\chi}

(2)
\iff
(3). Let
\widetilde{E}_{\chi}\colon\Pi^{*}Q_{\chi}\to X

denote the inclusion, and observe that

j_{\mathrm{prin}}^{*}\widetilde{E}_{\chi}=j_{\mathrm{prin}}^{*}\Pi^{*}Q_{\chi}=(\Pi\circ j_{\mathrm{prin}})^{*}Q_{\chi}=(p\circ\mathrm{pr})^{*}Q_{\chi}=\mathrm{pr}^{*}(p^{*}Q_{\chi}).

(4.1)

Now, if (2) holds, then (

Definition 4.6

Theorem 4.7

(Wang’s theorem
[
13
]
)

Let

be a homogeneous
S
-bundle associated to a homomorphism
\lambda\colon K\to S
. Let

G
-invariant connections on

2.

K

Definition 4.8

Let

S

•

Let

\displaystyle\frac{dq_{2}}{dr}

\displaystyle=-\frac{6r^{3}}{r^{4}-1}q_{2},

\displaystyle\frac{ds_{2}}{dr}

\displaystyle=-\frac{6r^{3}}{r^{4}-1}s_{2}.

Solving each ODE separately yields the result.

3.

. The calculation is completely analogous to that of part (b). The relevant ODE system is

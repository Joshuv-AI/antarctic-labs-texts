Abstract
. We study the zero entropy locus for the Lozi maps. We first define a region R R in the parameter space and prove that for the parameters in R R , the Lozi maps have the topological entropy zero. R R is contained in a larger region where every Lozi map has a unique period-two orbit, and that orbit is attracting. It is easy to see that the zero entropy locus cannot coincide with that larger region, since it contains parameters for which the fixed point of the corresponding Lozi map has homoclinic points.

1.
Introduction

In 1978 Lozi constructed a two parameter family

L_{a,b}(x,y)=(1+y-a|x|,bx)
of piecewise affine homeomorphisms of the
Euclidean plane to itself, for which he provided numerical evidence
that for parameters value
a=1.7
and
b=0.5
the map has strange
attractor (see
[
1
]
). In 1980 the first author of this paper
proved that for a large set of parameters, the Lozi maps have indeed
hyperbolic strange attractors (see
[
2
]
). Although in the last
forty five years many results about the Lozi maps and their attractors
have been obtained, many important questions have stayed unanswered yet.
It is still not known how topological entropy and periodic points depend
on parameters, whether there are parameters for which distinct Lozi maps
are topologically conjugate, or their attractors are homeomorphic, to
mention just a few well-known open problems.

One of those open problems, related to all the above mentioned, is to
find the set of parameters for which the Lozi maps have zero entropy.

In
[
3
]
Yildiz proved that the topological entropy of the Lozi
map
L_{a,b}
is zero,
h_{top}(L_{a,b})=0
, in the following three
regions of the parameter space:

(i)

0<|b|\leq 1
and
a\leq b-1

In that region
L_{a,b}
does not have fixed or periodic points.

(ii)

0<b<1
and
b-1<a\leq 1-b

In that region, and if
a<1-b
,
L_{a,b}
has a unique
attracting fixed point
X=(\frac{1}{1+a-b},\frac{b}{1+a-b})

in the first quadrant, and does not have other periodic points.
If
a=1-b
,
L_{a,b}
has the fixed point
X
, which is not
hyperbolic, and it is the midpoint of a line segment
I
of
period-two points, where

I=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:bx+y=\frac{b}{1-b},\ 0\leq x\leq\frac{1}{1-b}\}
.

(iii)

In a small neighborhood
U
of the point
(a,b)=(1,0.5)
.

In this paper, we improve the zero entropy result obtained by Yildiz.

R

2.
The zero entropy locus

Let us consider the following region in the parameter space:
0<b<1

and
1-b<a<1+b
. In that region
L_{a,b}
has two saddle fixed
points,
X=(\frac{1}{1+a-b},\frac{b}{1+a-b})
in the first quadrant
and
Y=(\frac{1}{1-a-b},\frac{b}{1-a-b})
in the third quadrant. The
eigenvalues of
DL_{a,b}
(the derivative of
L_{a,b}
) are

\lambda^{u}_{X}=(-a-\sqrt{a^{2}+4b})/2\ \textrm{ and }\ \lambda_{X}^{s}=(-a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b})/2\ \textrm{ at }\ X

and

\lambda_{Y}^{u}=(a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b})/2\ \textrm{ and }\ \lambda_{Y}^{s}=(a-\sqrt{a^{2}+4b})/2\ \textrm{ at }\ Y.

The eigenvector corresponding to an eigenvalue
\lambda
is

\lambda\choose b
.

From now on, for a point
P\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let
P^{n}=L_{a,b}^{n}(P)
,

n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
P^{0}=P
. One half of the unstable manifold
W^{u}_{X}

of
X
, starts from
X
and goes to the right, we denote it by

W^{u+}_{X}
. It intersects the horizontal axis for the first time
at the point

Z=\left(\frac{2+a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}{2(1+a-b)},0\right).

The other half, that starts at
X
and goes to the left, we denote
by
W^{u-}_{X}
. It intersect the
y
-axis for the first time at the
point
Z^{-1}
. Note that
Z^{2n}\in W^{u+}_{X}
and

Z^{2n-1}\in W^{u-}_{X}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

Also, there are two attracting period-two points,
P^{\prime}
in the
second quadrant and
P
in the fourth quadrant,

P^{\prime}=\left(\frac{1-a-b}{a^{2}+(1-b)^{2}},\frac{b(1+a-b)}{a^{2}+(1-b)^{2}}\right),\ P=\left(\frac{1+a-b}{a^{2}+(1-b)^{2}},\frac{b(1-a-b)}{a^{2}+(1-b)^{2}}\right),

and there are no other period-two points. Also, for
a=1+b
,
P^{\prime}

and
P
are not hyperbolic, but for
a>1+b
, they are hyperbolic
saddle points.

Note that it is enough to consider the Lozi maps with
|b|\leq 1
since
the maps with
|b|>1
are, up to an affine conjugacy, inverses of the
maps with
|b|<1
.

From now on let
R
denote the region in the parameter space such
that
0<b<1
,
1-b<a<1+b
and for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

Z^{2k-1}
belongs to the left half-plane and
Z^{2k}
belongs to
the right half-plane.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
(a,b)\in R
. We want to prove that
h_{top}(L_{a,b})=0
.
Let
ZL:=\{L_{a,b}^{2k-1}(Z):k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\}
and
ZR:=\{L_{a,b}^{2k}(Z):k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\}=L_{a,b}(ZL)
.
Let us consider their convex hulls
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Conv}(ZL)
and

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Conv}(ZR)
. Since
L_{a,b}
maps the left half-plane to the
lower half-plane, and the right half-plane to the upper one,

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Conv}(ZL)
is contained in the second quadrant and

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Conv}(ZR)
is contained in the fourth quadrant. Also

L_{a,b}(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Conv}(ZL))=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Conv}(ZR)
and

L_{a,b}(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Conv}(ZR))\subset\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Conv}(ZL)
. In both sets,

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Conv}(ZL)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Conv}(ZR)
, the map is globally linear, so
their union is attracted to the periodic orbit

\{P^{\prime},P\}
. This in particular means that

W^{u}(X)\cup\{P^{\prime},P\}
is compact, connected and invariant.

Let us denote by
W^{s+}_{X}
the upper connected component of
the stable manifold of
X
which starts at
X
and goes up,
and by
W^{u+}_{Y}
the lower connected component of the
unstable manifold of the other fixed point
Y
in the third
quadrant which starts at
Y
and goes down. Let us denote
by
M
the union of
W^{u}_{X}
,
P^{\prime}
,
P
,
W^{s+}_{X}
,

W^{u+}_{Y}
and
\infty
,

M=W^{u}_{X}\cup\{P^{\prime},P\}\cup W^{s+}_{X}\cup W^{u+}_{Y}\cup\{\infty\}
. Then
M
is invariant, compact, connected
in the extended plane and does not separate the extended
plane, nor the plane.

Let us denote by
U
the complement of
M
in the plane (that
is,
U={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\smallsetminus M
). Then
U
is invariant by construction
and does not contain any fixed point of
L_{a,b}^{2}
. Also,
U

is open and simply connected in the plane and therefore
homeomorphic to the open unit disc, and moreover to the plane.
The Brouwer plane translation theorem (BPTT) says that if
h

is an orientation preserving homeomorphism of the plane which
is fixed point free, then every point of the plane is contained
in a properly embedded line
l
such that
l
does not intersect

h(l)
, and
l
is separating
h(l)
from
h^{-1}(l)
. In our
case,
L_{a,b}^{2}|_{U}
satisfies assumptions of BPTT and therefore
every point of
U
is a wandering point for
L_{a,b}^{2}
. This
means that the non-wandering set of
L_{a,b}^{2}
consists only
of the fixed points of
L_{a,b}^{2}
, and hence

h_{top}(L_{a,b}^{2})=2h_{top}(L_{a,b})=0
.
∎

Now, we will describe rigorously the region
R
and show that it looks
as in Figure

. First, we will prove that there is
a simply connected neighborhood of
(0,1)
contained in

.

Note that for
(a,b)\in R
and every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

DL^{2}_{a,b}(Z^{2k})=DL^{2}_{a,b}(P)
and its eigenvalues are

(-a^{2}+2b\pm a\sqrt{a^{2}-4b})/2
. Therefore, when
4b>a^{2}
,
the part of the unstable manifold of
X
which belongs to the
fourth quadrant spirals towards
P
, and the part which belongs to
the second quadrant spirals to
P^{\prime}
. In the next lemma, we will
describe that behavior more precisely.

We will work with parameters
a,b
, where
b=1-ta
,
0<t<1
and
a

is close to
0
(so
b
is close to
1
). When we take limits as

a\to 0
, we will fix
t
and treat
b
as a function of
a
.
We identify
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with
\mathbb{C}
, and use both notations (even within the
same formula).

The derivative of the second iterate of the Lozi map at the periodic
point
P
of period 2, which is in the fourth quadrant, is

A=\begin{pmatrix}b-a^{2}&a\\
-ab&b\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1-ta-a^{2}&a\\
-a+ta^{2}&1-ta\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
v\in\mathbb{C}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Then, as
a\to 0
, the broken line joining
consecutive points
A^{n}v
converges to the logarithmic spiral

\varphi\mapsto ve^{-t\varphi}e^{-i\varphi}
locally uniformly in
t
.

Proof.

Let us define the matrix

B=\begin{pmatrix}b\cos a&b\sin a\\
-b\sin a&b\cos a\end{pmatrix}.

Then

\lim_{a\to 0}\frac{A-B}{a^{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1/2&t\\
0&1/2\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover, for a given
\tau
,
0<\tau<1
, there exist
\varepsilon>0
,

\alpha>0
and a constant
K:=K_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
such that if

\tau-\varepsilon<t<\tau+\varepsilon
and
a<\alpha
, then

\|A-B\|<Ka^{2}<ta
. Since
\|B\|=b
, we have always
\|B\|<1
and

\|A\|\leq\|B\|+\|A-B\|<1-ta+Ka^{2}<1
.

We have

A^{n}-B^{n}=(A^{n}-BA^{n-1})+(BA^{n-1}-B^{2}A^{n-2})+\dots+(B^{n-1}A-B^{n}),

so for
\tau-\varepsilon<t<\tau+\varepsilon
and
a<\alpha

\begin{split}\|A^{n}-B^{n}\|&\leq\|A^{n}-BA^{n-1}\|+\|BA^{n-1}-B^{2}A^{n-2}\|+\dots+\|B^{n-1}A-B^{n}\|\\
&\leq\|A-B\|\big(\|A\|^{n-1}+\|B\|\cdot\|A\|^{n-2}+\dots+\|B\|^{n-1}\big)\leq n\|A-B\|.\end{split}

If
n\leq\varphi/a
for some constant
\varphi
, we get

\|A^{n}-B^{n}\|\leq\varphi Ka
.

The action of the matrix
B
is the same as the multiplication by

be^{-ai}
. Therefore, for
\tau-\varepsilon<t<\tau+\varepsilon
and

a<\alpha
, we have for any
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}=\mathbb{C}
and

n=\lfloor\frac{\varphi}{a}\rfloor

|A^{n}v-vb^{n}e^{-ina}|\leq\varphi Ka|v|.

As
a\to 0
(and
\tau-\varepsilon<t<\tau+\varepsilon
,

n=\lfloor\frac{\varphi}{a}\rfloor
), we get

\lim_{a\to 0}b^{n}e^{-ina}=\lim_{a\to 0}(1-ta)^{n}e^{-i\varphi}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1-\frac{t\varphi}{n}\right)^{n}e^{-i\varphi}=e^{-t\varphi}e^{-i\varphi}.

Therefore, as
a\to 0
, the broken line joining consecutive points

A^{n}v
converges to the logarithmic spiral

\varphi\mapsto ve^{-t\varphi}e^{-i\varphi}
locally uniformly in
t
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
t_{0}
be the root of the equation

(1)

\sqrt{2}e^{-\frac{7}{4}\pi t}=1+t

(

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Cl}W^{u}_{X}=W^{u}_{X}\cup\{P,P^{\prime}\}

The points
P

aP=\left(\frac{1+t}{1+t^{2}},\ \frac{(1-ta)(t-1)}{1+t^{2}}\right),\ aZ=\left(\frac{2+a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4-4ta}}{2(1+t)},\ 0\right).

First, we will show that if

Similarly, if

, where

, so if

Now we will make calculations in
\mathbb{C}
. Set

p=\lim_{a\to 0}aP=\frac{t+1}{t^{2}+1}+i\frac{t-1}{t^{2}+1},\ \ \ \ \ z=\lim_{a\to 0}aZ=\frac{2}{t+1}.

To get the tangent line to the spiral to change from slope

(above

.
This means that

Lemma 2.4
.

For
k\geq 4
, let
(a_{k},b_{k})=\min C_{k}
. Then
b_{k+2}\geq b_{k}
and

a_{k+2}\leq a_{k}
. Moreover,
\lim_{k\to\infty}(a_{k},b_{k})=(0,1)
.

Proof.

Recall that for
b>a^{2}/4
, the eigenvalues of
DL^{2}_{a,b}
are

\lambda(a,b)=u\pm iv
, with
u=(2b-a^{2})/2
,

v=a\sqrt{4b-a^{2}}/2
,
|\lambda|=b
, and the argument of
\lambda

\varphi=\varphi(a,b)=\arctan\frac{a\sqrt{4b-a^{2}}}{2b-a^{2}}

First, we will prove by induction that
b_{2k+2}\geq b_{2k}
and

a_{2k+2}\leq a_{2k}
for every

that
b_{6}>b_{4}

a_{6}<a_{4}
. For

to the

-axis. In transition from that state to the state where
Z^{8}

belongs to the

are in the right half-plane and

, the map

.

Let us consider
(a_{2k},b_{2k})
.
For that pair of parameters the point
Z^{2k}
belongs to the

-axis.
Now again, in transition from that state to the state where
Z^{2k+2}

belongs to the

Z^{2j}
are in the right half-plane and

Z^{2j+1}
are in the left half-plane, for

\varphi

a

increases.
Therefore,
b_{2k+2}\geq b_{2k}
and
a_{k+2}\leq a_{k}
.

In an analogous way we can get
b_{2k+3}\geq b_{2k+1}
and

a_{2k+3}\leq a_{2k+1}
for every

.

Note that in every move from
(a_{k},b_{k})

(a_{k+2},b_{k+2}

obtain that only finitely many iterates of
Z

the orbit of

is countable infinite, we have infinitely many different

(a_{k},b_{k})
and, by Theorem
2.2
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}(a_{k},b_{k})=(0,1)
.
∎

The next proposition gives scaling of the sequence
(a_{2k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

\lim_{k\to\infty}2ka_{2k}=7\pi/4
.

Proof.

By lemma
2.1
we have that as
a\to 0
, the broken line joining
consecutive points
Z^{2n}=A^{n}Z
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, converges to the
logarithmic spiral
\varphi\mapsto Ze^{-t\varphi}e^{-i\varphi}
in the fourth
quadrant which start at
Z
and has center at
P
, with

n=\lfloor\frac{\varphi}{a}\rfloor
.
Since we are interested in parameters
(a_{2k},b_{2k})
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

k\geq 2
, for which
Z^{2k}
lies on the
y
-axis and
b_{2k}
is
minimal for that property, by Theorem
2.2
we have
\varphi=7\pi/4
.
Therefore,
\lim_{k\to\infty}2ka_{2k}=7\pi/4
.
∎

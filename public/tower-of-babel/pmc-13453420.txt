Simvastatin Mitigates Kidney Injury via Inhibition of the TNF-α/α-SMA Signaling Pathway in a Subtotal Nephrectomy Model
Authors: Putu Nita Cahyawati, Erly Sintya, Anak Agung Sri Agung Aryastuti, Asri Lestarini, Pande Ayu Naya Kasih Permatananda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aims to investigate the preventive effect of simvastatin on the subtotal nephrectomy model by analysing key mediators of inflammation and fibrosis.MethodsA total of 30 male Swiss mice were used in this study. The animals were divided into five groups (six/group): the sham group (Sham), the nephrectomy group (Neph), and three simvastatin groups (Simv) with a stepwise dose of simvastatin (5, 10, or 20 mg/kgBW). Kidney function was assessed by measuring serum creatinine, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), and proteinuria. Interstitial fibrosis was evaluated by Sirius red staining, α-SMA expression was evaluated by immunohistochemistry, and TNF-α expression was evaluated by real-time PCR.ResultsSubtotal nephrectomy significantly impaired renal function, increased interstitial fibrosis, and upregulated TNF-α and α-SMA expression. Simvastatin treatment dose-dependently reduced serum creatinine and proteinuria, although the reduction in BUN was not significantly different from the Neph group. Simvastatin also significantly attenuated interstitial fibrosis (8.62±2.43 in Simv5 group, 4.15±1.67 in Simv10, and 2.62±0.63 in Simv20 groups), decreased the expression of TNF-α (0.42±0.27 in Simv5, 0.39±0.18 in Simv10, and 0.23±0.21 in Simv20) and α-SMA (3.85±0.97 in Simv5, 2.74±0.61 in Simv10, and 1.59±0.45 in Simv20 groups) in dose-dependent manner, compared with the Neph group (all p < 0.05). Path analysis demonstrated that TNF-α indirectly contributed to renal fibrosis by promoting α-SMA expression, whereas α-SMA had a significant direct effect on interstitial fibrosis (p=0.038).ConclusionThis study found that the renoprotective effect of simvastatin in a subtotal nephrectomy model may be mediated by inhibition of the TNF-α/α-SMA signalling pathway, with α-SMA acting as a key mediator linking inflammation to renal fibrosis.Keywords: anti-inflammatory, antifibrotic, kidney disease, simvastatin, TNF-α, α-SMA

Introduction

Kidney failure represents a major public health challenge worldwide. This medical condition impacts over 10% of the global population.1 The global prevalence reaches 9.1%. In 2027, there will be 1–2 million deaths due to kidney failure.2 This disease is associated with high morbidity, mortality, and treatment costs.3,4 This disease has a higher incidence among the elderly, females, and patients with a medical history of high blood pressure, heart disease, obesity, and diabetes.1

Kidney failure is characterized by damage to kidney function and structure. Increased serum creatinine, BUN, and proteinuria clinically characterize kidney damage. Damage to the kidney structure is assessed histopathologically by the presence of fibrosis. This kidney injury will also trigger the release of proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines. One of these cytokines is TNF-α. This cytokine is produced by various cells, such as macrophages, mesangial cells, and renal tubular epithelial cells, during the inflammatory process. Chronic, untreated inflammation leads to kidney fibrosis. Kidney fibrosis is characterized by increased production and decreased degradation of the extracellular matrix, increased infiltration of inflammatory cells, and myofibroblast activation, as evidenced by α-SMA expression.5,6

Simvastatin is a drug used to control cholesterol levels.7,8 Simvastatin is also reported to exhibit pleiotropic effects, independent of their lipid-lowering effects. These effects include anti-inflammatory, antiproliferative, antioxidant, and antithrombotic properties on blood vessels.9–11 However, the anti-inflammatory and pleiotropic effects of simvastatin in kidney disease are still inconsistent. Previous studies have reported that simvastatin can prevent endothelial dysfunction by suppressing the endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT). This inhibition will prevent atherosclerosis in the vascular and fibrosis in the kidney.9,12 The impact of prolonged simvastatin use on renal disease progression remains a subject of debate, as findings across studies have been inconsistent. Several studies have reported that simvastatin therapy does not have a significant effect on the kidneys.8,13 However, other studies reported that high-dose statins are very safe and effective, and can reduce inflammation by increasing aquaporin expression.8,14

The anti-inflammatory effects of simvastatin in reducing TNF-α levels in animal models also vary. The ability to inhibit TNF-α is important for reducing NF-κB transcription factor expression. Simvastatin has been reported to reduce PPAR-α receptor activation, thereby reducing NF-κB activation. However, other studies have reported different effects. In addition to differences in animal models, the statin type and dosage used in each study also varied. This likely contributes to the different effects.15–17

Previous studies have shown that the mechanism of action of simvastatin in preventing kidney fibrosis and the optimal dose have not been definitively determined. The therapeutic dose of simvastatin in humans is 10–80 mg (single dose). A study recommended using the median dose as the therapeutic dose in animal models.18 Therefore, research integrating these parameters via path analysis is needed to provide a more comprehensive understanding of statins’ renoprotective mechanisms in kidney failure. This approach not only allows evaluation of statins’ effects on each parameter but also identifies direct and indirect relationships among inflammation, fibrosis, histopathological changes, and decreased kidney function, thereby providing stronger mechanistic evidence for statins’ renoprotective effects. This study aims to investigate the preventive effect of simvastatin on subtotal nephrectomy by analyzing the key mediators of inflammation and fibrosis, as well as identifying the relationships among these variables.

Method

Ethical Approval

Ethical approval was issued by the research ethics committee of Universitas Udayana on August 11, 2022 (protocol number: 2022.03.10895). All procedures involving animals were conducted in accordance with International and Indonesian guidelines, including: the International Guiding Principles for Biomedical Research Involving Animals (CIOMS–ICLAS) (https://iclas.org/cioms-and-iclas/), the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (NRC 2011) (https://www.aaalac.org/the-guide/), and the National Health Research and Development Ethics Guidelines and Standards, Indonesia (https://kepkn.kemkes.go.id/). All study methods and results were compiled according to the ARRIVE Guidelines 2.0 (https://arriveguidelines.org/arrive-guidelines).

Animal

Thirty male Swiss mice were purchased from the Faculty of Medicine, Universitas Udayana, Bali. The use of Swiss strains in this study is based on a literature review of several previous studies that used similar animal strains to trigger kidney fibrosis.19–22 The number of animals was determined using Federer’s formula to ensure an adequate sample size for each experimental group.23 All experimental animals were carefully standardized with respect to age (12–16 weeks) and body weight (30–40 g) to reduce inter-animal variability.24 The reason is to reduce the risk of death in experimental animals after surgery. The mice were housed in standard cages with a 12 h light-dark cycle and constant temperature and humidity. Each cage consisted of 3 mice. All mice had unrestricted access to water and food. The duration of the acclimatization phase was 7 days.

The mice were randomly allocated to 5 treatment groups of equal size. This process is aided by the simple formula =RAND() in Excel. This procedure begins with the application of an animal ear tag with the code N1-N30. This procedure aims to minimize selection bias. To reduce observer bias, the randomization and coding process, random allocation, conduct of the experiment, outcome assessment, and data analysis are carried out by different people.

Each group consists of six mice. The first group, the sham group (Sham), underwent a sham operation and received a placebo (1% carboxymethylcellulose/CMC). In this group, we performed an incision at the same location as the subtotal nephrectomy procedure, but did not manipulate the kidneys. The purpose of this procedure is to ensure that the Sham group has a risk of infection and injury similar to that of the other groups. The second group, the nephrectomy group (Neph), underwent a subtotal nephrectomy and received a placebo (1% CMC). This experimental procedure was conducted via an incision in the flank region, measuring approximately 1 to 1.5 cm in length. On day one, the right kidney was removed, followed by excision of the upper and lower poles of the left kidney on day two. The third (Sim5), fourth (Sim10), and fifth (Sim20) groups underwent subtotal nephrectomy and received a cascading dose of simvastatin (5, 10, or 20 mg/kgBW, respectively). Placebo and simvastatin were given on the day of surgery. Placebo and simvastatin were given orally once daily for 14 days.25 The experimental timeline is shown in Figure 1.

Simvastatin

Simvastatin (Sigma-Aldrich®, Singapore) was delivered via gavage in a 1% CMC solution at daily doses for 14 consecutive days.

Anaesthesia and Euthanasia Methods

In this study, we used a combination of ketamine (Ketamine HCL®, NDC 0143–9508-01) and xylazine (XylaMed™, Bimeda, Anada 200–529) as the anaesthetic method. Anaesthesia was injected intraperitoneally at a dose of 90 mg/kg BW (ketamine) and 10 mg/kg BW (xylazine).26 At the end of treatment, the animal was anesthetized again with a combination of the two drugs. After deep anaesthesia, euthanasia was completed using a secondary method (neck dislocation) consistent with the AVMA Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals 2020.27 After all samples are harvested, the carcass is then wrapped in black plastic and incinerated.

Sample Collection

In this study, we collected blood, urine, and the remaining kidney tissue. The samples were taken on the fifteenth day, as shown in Figure 1. Blood samples were obtained via retro-orbital venous puncture from the right eye region. Collect 0.5 to 1 mL of blood, then centrifuge for 10 minutes at 10,000 rpm. The serum from that process was stored at −20° Celsius for subsequent analysis. Urine samples were collected over 24 hours. The urine was then stored at −20° Celsius for subsequent analysis. Organ samples (kidney tissue) were collected through laparotomy by making an incision from the abdomen to the thorax. To cleanse the entire organ of blood cells, a perfusion procedure is performed by injecting physiological saline through the apex of the heart while it is still beating. This procedure can only be performed when the animal’s heart is still beating, for approximately 15–20 minutes. Macroscopically, this procedure can be stopped if all organs, including the liver, pancreas, intestines, heart, and kidneys, are pale. The kidney tissue was then cut transversely into two sections. The first section was placed in 10% buffered formalin for interstitial fibrosis and α-SMA examination, while the second section, the kidney capsule, was cleaned and then stored at −80° Celsius for TNF-α analysis.

Kidney Function Analysis

Kidney function is assessed using three parameters: serum creatinine, BUN, and proteinuria. Serum creatinine levels were measured based on the kinetic Jaffe method (DiaSys, Holzheim, Germany). The serum reacted with sodium hydroxide and picric acid for one minute. The reaction product was quantified using a spectrophotometer at 492 nm. Blood urea nitrogen levels were measured using the Urease–glutamate dehydrogenase (GLDH) enzymatic UV test (DiaSys, Holzheim, Germany). The absorbance was measured spectrophotometrically at 340 nm. Urea concentration was calculated according to the manufacturer’s instructions and subsequently converted to BUN using the following equation: BUN (mg/dL) = urea (mg/dL) x 0.467.28 Proteinuria levels were measured using a dipstick (YD Diagnostic, Gyonggi-do, Republic of Korea). Proteinuria levels were evaluated within 60 seconds by matching the dipstick colour to the standard packaging. The scores are grouped into 6 categories, namely 0 (negative), 1 (±), 2 (+1), 3 (+2), 4 (+3), and 5 (+4). The results were then averaged and compared between groups.25

Interstitial Fibrosis

Interstitial fibrosis was evaluated using Sirius red staining (Sigma-Aldrich, Saint Louis, USA). Paraffin blocks were sectioned at 4 μm thickness. Slides were evaluated in 10 randomly selected, non-overlapping fields under a light microscope (Olympus CX21FS1, Tokyo, Japan) at 400x magnification. Fibrosis appears red in the renal interstitium. The evaluation process involved two independent observers who had undergone a perception matching process. The results presented are the average of the two observers’ values. Data were quantified using ImageJ software as the percentage of fibrosis area.24

α-SMA Expression

α-SMA expression was evaluated by immunohistochemistry. Paraffin-embedded tissue samples were sectioned at a thickness of 4 μm. The sections were incubated with a monoclonal mouse anti-α-smooth muscle actin primary antibody from Sigma-Aldrich, followed by a TrekA Universal Link secondary antibody. Positive α-SMA expression appeared as brown staining in the renal interstitial area. Ten non-overlapping fields from each slide were observed under a light microscope (Olympus CX21FS1, Tokyo, Japan) at 400× magnification. The evaluation process was performed in a blinded manner by two independent observers to minimize observer bias. These two observers had undergone a process of achieving common perception before starting the analysis. The quantitative assessment was performed using ImageJ.24

TNF-α Expression

RNA extraction from kidney tissues and amplification procedures were carried out according to the manufacturers’ instructions (Zymo Research, USA; Meridian Bioscience, USA). Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis was performed using an Applied Biosystems 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA). TNF-α mRNA expression was quantified using SYBR Green chemistry, with PPIA serving as the endogenous reference gene.

Amplification was performed using the SensiFAST™ SYBR® Lo-ROX One-Step Kit (Meridian Bioscience™, USA) in a singleplex format with duplicate measurements for each sample. Each reaction was prepared in a total volume of 20 µL containing 10 µL of 2× SensiFAST™ SYBR® Lo-ROX One-Step Mix, 0.8 µL each of forward and reverse primers, 0.2 µL reverse transcriptase, 0.4 µL RNase inhibitor, 4 µL RNA template (total RNA input: 400 ng/reaction), and 3.8 µL nuclease-free water. The final concentration of each primer was 0.4 µM.

Thermal cycling conditions included reverse transcription at 40°C for 15 min, followed by an initial denaturation step at 95°C for 10 min. Amplification was subsequently performed over 40 cycles consisting of denaturation at 95°C for 10s, annealing at 60°C for 30s, and extension at 72°C for 30s.

Melt curve analysis was performed to verify amplification specificity and exclude non-specific amplification products or primer-dimer formation under the following conditions: 95°C for 15s, followed by 60°C for 1 min, 95°C for 30s, and 60°C for 15s. Relative mRNA expression levels were normalized to PPIA and calculated using the 2^-ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences and expected amplicon sizes are presented in Table 1.

Data Analysis

SPSS 24 was used for statistical analysis. Normality test using the Shapiro–Wilk test and homogeneity using the Levene test. Data analysis using One-way ANOVA and post hoc Tukey’s with 95% confidence intervals (CI) and p-value < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant for all analyses. The relationships between the variables were examined by path analysis. The path coefficient (beta) indicated the magnitude of the direct effects between variables, and the R-squared value showed the percentage of the contribution or influence of the variables under evaluation.

Results

Effects of Simvastatin on Creatinine Level, BUN, and Proteinuria

Kidney function parameters showed marked deterioration following subtotal nephrectomy and were partially improved by simvastatin treatment (Figure 2). Serum creatinine increased from 0.43 ± 0.04 mg/dL in the Sham group to 1.07 ± 0.43 mg/dL in the Neph group. Simvastatin treatment reduced serum creatinine in a dose-dependent manner to 0.79 ± 0.21 mg/dL (Simv5), 0.69 ± 0.12 mg/dL (Simv10), and 0.47 ± 0.10 mg/dL (Simv20). One-way ANOVA demonstrated significant differences among the groups (p < 0.05). Post hoc analysis revealed significant differences between the Sham group and the Neph, Simv5, and Simv10 groups, and between the Neph group and the Simv10 and Simv20 groups. A significant difference was also observed between the Simv5 and Simv20 groups (p < 0.05).

A similar trend was observed for BUN levels. Compared with the Sham group (14.21 ± 5.04 mg/dL), BUN increased markedly in the Neph group (45.68 ± 4.14 mg/dL). BUN levels decreased in the simvastatin group, but remained quite high (39.00 ± 1.52 mg/dL in Simv5, 35.28 ± 10.06 mg/dL in Simv10, and 36.04 ± 4.73 mg/dL in Simv20). Significant differences were found between the Sham group and all other groups (p < 0.05); however, no significant differences were observed between the Neph group and any of the simvastatin-treated groups.

Proteinuria also increased substantially following subtotal nephrectomy, from 0.58 ± 0.38 in the Sham group to 5.42 ± 0.66 in the Neph group. Treatment with simvastatin significantly reduced proteinuria at all dose levels, with values of 3.33 ± 0.75, 2.75 ± 0.42, and 2.58 ± 0.38 in the Simv5, Simv10, and Simv20 groups, respectively (p < 0.05 vs Neph group).

Significant differences were also observed between the Neph group and both the Simv10 (4.15±1.67) and Simv20 (2.62±0.63) groups. The results of this study are statistically significant as determined by the One-way ANOVA. The α-SMA expression in the Neph group was significantly different from that in the Sham group and the three Simv groups. Path Analysis Model

Further analysis using path analysis showed that high TNF-α expression had a direct effect on high α-SMA expression, as indicated by a p-value of 0.035 (p<0.05). The contribution of TNF-α expression to α-SMA expression was 44.6% (R square value 0.446), while 45.4% was due to the influence of other factors not examined. In addition, the influence of TNF-α and α-SMA expression on interstitial fibrosis showed significance at 0.935 for TNF-α and 0.038 for α-SMA. These results indicate that TNF-α expression has no direct effect on interstitial fibrosis, whereas α-SMA expression does. The R square value of 0.616 indicates that the contribution of TNF-α expression and α-SMA expression is 61.6%, while the remaining 38.4% is contributed by other variables not examined. Discussion

Statins are recommended drugs for preventing cardiovascular disease in patients with kidney failure.29,30 But their clinical benefits remain debated and controversial. The controversy lies not in their cardioprotective effects but in their renoprotective effects.31,32 Various studies have reported inconsistent results regarding improvements in kidney function, reductions in proteinuria, and slowing disease progression.33,34 Furthermore, most studies have evaluated biomarkers of inflammation, fibrosis, histopathological changes, and kidney function separately, so the biological relationships between these parameters have not been comprehensively described.15,35,36

Statins work by inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase, an enzyme that catalyses the conversion of HMG-CoA to mevalonate. Thus, they inhibit the synthesis of cholesterol and isoprenoid intermediates, such as farnesyl diphosphate (FPP) and geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate (GGPP).37,38 Statins are reported to have pleiotropic effects because they can inhibit isoprenylation of Ras and Ras-like proteins (Rho, Rab, Rac, Ral, Rap), which are required for various intracellular signalling pathways.39–41 The ability of statins to inhibit RhoA and Rac-1 proteins allows statins to play a role in inhibiting the inflammatory process and oxidative stress that underlie damage to kidney structure and function.42

Renal structural damage is characterized by fibrosis. Renal fibrosis can occur through several mechanisms, including: an uncontrolled inflammatory response (involving TNF-α), a necroinflammatory process (due to ischemia, toxins, and proteinuria), a shift from epithelial cells to mesenchymal/EMT cells (characterized by increased expression of α-SMA, vimentin, fibronectin, and type 1 collagen), cell cycle arrest (in the G2/M phase), and metabolic disease.43 Fibrosis can also be triggered by the activation of several signalling pathways, including TGF-β/SMAD, Wnt/β-catenin, Jagged/Notch, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGF-R), JAK/STAT, and the c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) pathway.44–48

The elevated TNF-α expression in the Neph group compared with the Sham group highlights the proinflammatory consequences of subtotal nephrectomy, indicating that this surgical model effectively induces renal inflammation. Due to the relatively short study duration (2 weeks), the inflammation was likely acute or subacute. The 2- to 4-fold decrease in TNF-α expression in the simvastatin group supports the anti-inflammatory potential of statins, with a dose-dependent trend. These results suggest that high doses of simvastatin may have a more significant anti-inflammatory effect than low doses in the early phase of inflammation.

Reducing TNF-α expression enables statins to inhibit NF-κB activation, particularly by blocking the classical and PI3-kinase/Akt signalling pathways.49,50 NF-κB plays an important role in the renal inflammatory process through the activation of three main pathways, including: the canonical (classical) pathway, which TNF-α, IL-1, reactive oxygen species (ROS), or lipopolysaccharide (LPS) activate; the non-canonical (alternative, non-classical) pathway, which is activated by CD40L or lymphotoxin β (LTbeta); and the atypical NF-κB activation pathway, which is triggered by DNA damage or genotoxic stress.49,51 NF-κB can increase TGF-β1 expression, thereby promoting fibroblast activation and myofibroblast differentiation (increased α-SMA expression). In addition to controlling TNF-α expression, NF-ĸB also controls the expression of EMT inducers (Snail1), and EMT in epithelial cells is closely associated with fibrosis, because it is the source of myofibroblast cell differentiation.52,53

In this study, α-SMA expression in the Neph group was also significantly higher than in the Sham group. The graded doses of simvastatin (5, 10, and 20 mg/kg BW) significantly reduced α-SMA expression in renal tissue compared to the Neph group, with a clear dose-dependent trend. The maximal suppression of α-SMA was observed at 20 mg/kg BW, suggesting this dose may elicit the most potent antifibrotic effect. These results are also supported by the finding that renal interstitial fibrosis also decreased in all simvastatin groups. Evaluation of interstitial fibrosis showed that simvastatin at 10 mg/kg BW and 20 mg/kg BW significantly reduced interstitial fibrosis compared with the Neph group. This reduced fibrosis is a consequence of decreased extracellular matrix, as assessed by α-SMA expression, which also decreased in this study. This suggests that statin inhibition of myofibroblast activation may prevent extracellular matrix accumulation, tubular atrophy, and interstitial expansion. This may thus prevent the progression of kidney damage. However, it is important to remember that the cellular origin of myofibroblasts is not limited to fibroblasts. It also includes epithelial cells, endothelial cells, pericytes, and infiltrating macrophages. Their transdifferentiation is regulated by a network of profibrotic mediators and signalling pathways, including TGF-β1, mTOR, and ROS. Epigenetic regulators, such as histone acetylation, microRNAs, and long non-coding RNAs, also contribute to myofibroblast activation.53

Path analysis results in this study indicate that TNF-α expression directly affects high α-SMA expression. The contribution of TNF-α expression to α-SMA expression was 44.6% (R-square value 0.446), while 45.4% was due to the influence of other factors not examined. In addition, TNF-α expression has no direct effect on interstitial fibrosis, whereas α-SMA expression does. The influence of TNF-α and α-SMA expression was 61.6%, while 38.4% was contributed by other variables not examined. These other variables could arise from the involvement of other cells or the activation of other pathways (e.g, non-canonical or atypical NF-κB activation). This is because each pathway plays a role in cytokines and activates different cells. Fibrosis can occur due to cell activation that can trigger extracellular matrix production. These cells originate not only from myofibroblasts but also from pericytes, endothelial cells, and differentiated epithelial cells.

Thus, these findings suggest that simvastatin’s improvement in renal fibrosis is an indirect effect, mediated by reduced TNF-α expression. Reducing TNF-α directly decreases α-SMA expression, and decreasing α-SMA expression directly improves renal fibrosis. This reduction in fibrosis subsequently improves kidney function in the subtotal nephrectomy model.

The novelty of this study lies not only in assessing the therapeutic effects on renal injury progression but also in providing a more comprehensive understanding of the underlying biological mechanisms through path analysis. While previous studies have largely focused on evaluating individual parameters separately, the present study integrates molecular, histopathological, and functional indicators to explore their potential interactions during disease progression. By examining the relationships among inflammatory responses (TNF-α), fibrotic activation (α-SMA), histopathological changes (interstitial fibrosis), and renal function impairment, this approach allows for the identification of both direct and indirect effects among these variables. Such an integrated analysis may offer deeper insight into the complex processes underlying kidney injury and help identify potential pathways for targeted therapeutic strategies.

However, this study has several limitations, including the method for assessing serum creatinine levels, which still uses the Jaffe method and remains debated; a short follow-up period that prevents chronic effects from being fully evaluated; and the inability to evaluate all proinflammatory and profibrotic cytokine parameters.

Conclusion

This study found that simvastatin attenuated renal injury following subtotal nephrectomy by improving renal function, reducing inflammatory and fibrotic responses, and suppressing interstitial fibrosis. Path analysis further suggested that the renoprotective effect of simvastatin may be mediated through inhibition of the TNF-α/α-SMA signaling pathway, with α-SMA acting as a key mediator linking inflammation to renal fibrosis.

Frame-invariant topological representations of trabecular bone microarchitecture for strength prediction
John Rick Manzanares, Richard Leslie Abel, Paweł Dłotko

Abstract
Background and Objectives: Directional topological representations of trabecular bone should retain interpretable structural information without depending on an arbitrary transverse coordinate frame. We develop a frame-invariant directional filtration and compare its strength prediction with signed distance persistent homology and conventional morphometry. Methods: Twenty-four human trabecular cores were analyzed using persistence images, directional Betti tensors, and nested ridge regression over 13 validation groups. The directional construction combines cone occupancy, directional covariance, and principal axis degeneracy. Equal bone removal experiments and a pair closely matched in morphometry were used to examine whether topological differences tracked changes in simulated elastic stiffness. Results: The original combined persistence image model had a root mean squared error (RMSE) of 1.952 MPa, compared with 2.001 MPa for morphometry; the paired difference was − 0.049 -0.049 MPa with a 95% bootstrap interval of [ − 0.491 , 0.393 ] [-0.491,0.393] MPa. Exploratory signed distance persistent homology in dimension zero gave an RMSE of 1.639 MPa. The post hoc frame-invariant directional dimension zero model gave 1.610 MPa, compared with 1.877 MPa for dimension one and 1.630 MPa for a harmonized signed distance dimension zero model. The paired RMSE difference between the invariant and signed distance models was − 0.021 -0.021 MPa with a 95% interval of [ − 0.234 , 0.210 ] [-0.234,0.210] MPa. Localized removal reduced stiffness more than diffuse removal in 19 of 24 cores despite producing smaller H 0 H_{0} persistence image changes. Conclusions: Frame invariance removes the dependence of directional topology on an arbitrary transverse coordinate frame. Connected component representations warrant external evaluation for strength prediction, while the mechanical experiments limit their interpretation as scalar stiffness surrogates.

Introduction

A quantitative descriptor of trabecular bone is a map from a three-dimensional structure to a lower-dimensional representation. In conventional morphometry, this reduction is achieved by replacing the full trabecular architecture with a small number of scalar quantities that summarize particular geometric properties of the structure. The simplest example is the bone volume fraction, which records the proportion of the region of interest occupied by bone. Trabecular thickness and separation summarize characteristic local length scales, while connectivity density and degree of anisotropy describe aspects of network organization and directional structure. These descriptors are useful because they deliberately discard most of the image while retaining quantities with established geometric interpretations
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. The resulting compression leaves a mathematical question: what information discarded by conventional morphometry is retained by a multiscale description of the full segmented microarchitecture, and is any such information associated with mechanical strength
[
5
,
6
]
?

Persistent homology addresses the first part of this question by studying topology across a nested family of spaces rather than at a single binary representation
[
7
,
8
]
. Such a nested family is called a filtration. Let
B\subset R
denote the segmented bone phase inside a region of interest
R
, and define the signed Euclidean distance function
s:R\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by assigning negative distance to voxels inside bone and positive distance to voxels in marrow. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the corresponding sublevel sets

B_{t}=\{x\in R:s(x)\leq t\}

satisfy
B_{u}\subseteq B_{t}
whenever
u\leq t
, and therefore form a
filtration
.

At a filtration value
t
, the zeroth homology group
H_{0}(B_{t})
describes connected components, the first homology group
H_{1}(B_{t})
describes independent one-dimensional cycles, and the second homology group
H_{2}(B_{t})
describes independent two-dimensional cycles, which in three dimensions can surround enclosed voids.

Because
s
is measured in physical distance, the filtration parameter
t
is measured in micrometers. For
t=-r<0
, the set
B_{t}
contains bone lying at least
r
micrometers from marrow; at
t=0
, it recovers the segmented bone; and for
t>0
, it additionally includes marrow lying within distance
t
of bone. Birth and death values in persistent homology therefore identify both when a topological feature appears or disappears and the physical spatial scale at which that change occurs
[
9
,
10
]
.

The three homology dimensions describe different properties of the evolving filtered structure and should not be treated as interchangeable summaries. In the signed distance filtration,
H_{0}
records the appearance and merging of connected regions as progressively less interior bone is admitted into the filtration. By contrast, classes in
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
arise from cycles and enclosed regions of the evolving filtered complex. These features are defined by the filtration itself and need not correspond directly to anatomical tunnels or cavities present in the original segmented bone at
t=0
.

This interpretation gives dimension zero a direct geometric relation to the internal scale and connectivity of the trabecular structure. As the filtration threshold increases from negative values toward zero, connected regions lying deeper within bone appear and subsequently merge with one another. The resulting persistence therefore depends both on the depth of interior bone regions and on the spatial scales at which those regions become connected. This information is related to, but not equivalent to, trabecular thickness. Local thickness is defined from the diameters of maximal inscribed spheres, whereas dimension zero persistence describes connectivity changes across a nested family of interior bone regions.

The original predictive analysis treated the combined information from
H_{0}
,
H_{1}
, and
H_{2}
as the primary topological representation and compared this model with conventional morphometry. Models based on the individual homology dimensions were evaluated separately as exploratory analyses. Among these,
H_{0}
produced the lowest observed prediction error and, independently, admits the direct geometric interpretation described above. We therefore emphasize
H_{0}
when interpreting the results for the individual homology dimensions, while retaining the combined representation as the original primary benchmark. The observed performance of
H_{0}
remains exploratory and requires evaluation in an independent cohort.

The signed distance filtration describes spatial scale but does not explicitly distinguish one physical direction from another. We therefore performed a secondary analysis asking whether directional topology contains additional information about trabecular architecture. In reproducing a directional topological construction proposed in a recent preprint
[
11
]
, we found that its representation could depend on an arbitrary choice of coordinate frame perpendicular to the direction being studied. This motivated a frame-invariant directional filtration whose coordinates depend only on quantities intrinsic to the selected physical direction and the local trabecular geometry. Because this construction was developed after the dependence on the transverse coordinate frame and the earlier empirical results were known, we treat it as a post hoc methodological extension whose findings require independent evaluation.

Prediction and mechanical interpretation address different claims. A representation may be associated with strength across specimens without implying that a change in that representation must produce a corresponding change in mechanical response. The methodological contribution is a directional cubical filtration that removes this ambiguity in the transverse frame, together with an exact computation of its sampled Betti numbers and a controlled evaluation of its interpretation in human bone. We therefore address three related questions. First, how do the individual homology dimensions compare in their association with measured apparent compressive strength? Second, how does the resulting focal topological representation compare with conventional morphometry and other structural descriptions of trabecular architecture? Third, does the association observed across specimens persist under controlled geometric perturbations designed to separate the amount of bone from its spatial organization? Together, these analyses distinguish the predictive value of a representation from the stronger claim that changes in that representation have a consistent mechanical interpretation.

2
Mathematical framework

This section fixes the mathematical objects used throughout the study. First, each segmented image is represented as a finite cubical complex. Cubical homology and filtrations are then introduced before the signed distance and directional constructions.

2.1
Voxel geometry and cubical complexes

For specimen
i
, let
\Omega_{i}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{3}
be the finite three-dimensional voxel lattice, let
R_{i}\subset\Omega_{i}
be the region of interest, and let
B_{i}\subset R_{i}
be the segmented bone phase. The isotropic voxel spacing is
h_{i}>0
. A voxel index
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\Omega_{i}
has physical center
h_{i}x
, relative to a fixed origin. For a voxel set
A\subseteq\Omega_{i}
, we write
|A|
for its number of voxels, so its physical volume is
h_{i}^{3}|A|
.

To pass from a voxel array to a topological space, associate to each voxel index
x\in\Omega_{i}
the closed physical cube

Q_{i}(x)=h_{i}x+\left[-\frac{h_{i}}{2},\frac{h_{i}}{2}\right]^{3}.

(1)

A
cubical cell
is any face of one of these voxel cubes. Thus, in three dimensions, cubical cells have dimensions
0
,
1
,
2
, or
3
: vertices, edges, square faces, and voxel cubes. If
\sigma
and
\tau
are cells, we say that
\tau
is a
face
of
\sigma
when
\tau\subseteq\sigma
is obtained by fixing one or more coordinates of
\sigma
at an endpoint.

Definition 1

A finite
cubical complex

K
is a finite collection of cubical cells such that every face of every cell in
K
also belongs to
K
, and the nonempty intersection of any two cells in
K
is a face of each. A subset
L\subseteq K
that is itself closed under faces is a
cubical subcomplex
of
K
.

For a voxel set
A\subseteq R_{i}
, define

\operatorname{Cub}_{i}(A)=\left\{\sigma:\sigma\text{ is a face of }Q_{i}(x)\text{ for some }x\in A\right\}.

(2)

The ambient ROI complex is

K_{i}^{\mathrm{ROI}}=\operatorname{Cub}_{i}(R_{i}),

and the segmented bone at threshold zero will later appear as the subcomplex
\operatorname{Cub}_{i}(B_{i})
. When no ambiguity is possible, we write
\operatorname{Cub}(A)
instead of
\operatorname{Cub}_{i}(A)
.

The conventional morphometric representation is defined on the same segmented voxel set. In particular,

f_{i}=\mathrm{BV/TV}(B_{i};R_{i})=\frac{|B_{i}|}{|R_{i}|},

where the voxel volume
h_{i}^{3}
cancels. The primary morphometric map is

\mathcal{M}(B_{i};R_{i})=m_{i}=(f_{i},t_{i},s_{i},c_{i},a_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{5},

where
t_{i}
and
s_{i}
are mean local trabecular thickness and separation,
c_{i}
is connectivity density, and
a_{i}
is degree of anisotropy. Local thickness is defined from maximal spheres contained in the corresponding phase
[
12
]
; connectivity density is based on the Euler characteristic with an edge correction and volume normalization
[
3
]
; and degree of anisotropy summarizes directional variation in mean intercept length
[
4
]
. Numerical conventions are given in
A

[
2
]
.

2.2
Cubical homology and filtrations

Homology is computed over the field
\mathbb{F}_{2}=\{0,1\}
, with addition and multiplication modulo two. This choice removes orientation signs from the boundary operator and is standard for complexes built from (binary) images.

For a finite cubical complex
K
, let
C_{k}(K;\mathbb{F}_{2})
be the vector space whose basis consists of the
k
-dimensional cells of
K
. Its elements are finite formal sums of
k
-cells with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and are called
k
-
chains
. The cubical boundary map

\partial_{k}:C_{k}(K;\mathbb{F}_{2})\longrightarrow C_{k-1}(K;\mathbb{F}_{2})

sends each
k
-cell to the sum of its faces of codimension one. These maps satisfy

\partial_{k-1}\circ\partial_{k}=0.

Set
Z_{k}(K)=\ker\partial_{k}
, the space of cycles, and
B_{k}(K)=\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1}
, the space of boundaries. Since
\partial_{k}\circ\partial_{k+1}=0
, we have
B_{k}(K)\subseteq Z_{k}(K)
. The
k
th homology group is the quotient

H_{k}(K;\mathbb{F}_{2})=Z_{k}(K)/B_{k}(K),

(3)

and its dimension

\beta_{k}(K)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H_{k}(K;\mathbb{F}_{2})

(4)

is the
k
th
Betti number
. In three-dimensional image complexes,
\beta_{0}
counts connected components,
\beta_{1}
counts independent one-dimensional cycles, and
\beta_{2}
counts independent two-dimensional cycles that can surround enclosed regions. These geometric descriptions always refer to the particular complex being analyzed; they need not correspond directly to anatomical tunnels or cavities in the original segmentation.

A
one-parameter filtration
of a complex
K
is a family of subcomplexes
(K(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
such that, if
u\leq t
, then
K(u)\subseteq K(t)
. More generally, equip
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with the coordinatewise partial order in which
\mathbf{s}\preceq\mathbf{t}
if and only if
s_{j}\leq t_{j}
for every
j
. An
m
-parameter filtration is a family that preserves this order
(K(\mathbf{t}))_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}
: if
\mathbf{s}\preceq\mathbf{t}
, then
K(\mathbf{s})\subseteq K(\mathbf{t})
. The signed distance construction below has one parameter, whereas the directional construction has three.

2.3
Signed Euclidean distance persistence

For a nonempty voxel set
A\subseteq\Omega_{i}
, define the center-to-center Euclidean distance

d_{i}(x,A)=h_{i}\min_{a\in A}\|x-a\|_{2},

where
h_{i}
is expressed in micrometers. Assuming that both bone and marrow are nonempty within
R_{i}
, the
signed Euclidean distance transform
is the map
\psi_{i}:\Omega_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by

\psi_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}-d_{i}(x,R_{i}\setminus B_{i}),&x\in B_{i},\\
d_{i}(x,B_{i}),&x\in R_{i}\setminus B_{i},\\
+\infty,&x\notin R_{i}.\end{cases}

(5)

Negative values measure depth within bone relative to marrow voxel centers, whereas positive values measure the distance of marrow voxels from bone. Voxels outside the ROI are excluded rather than treated as marrow, so the artificial cylindrical crop boundary does not itself enter the distance transform.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, define

K_{i}(t)=\operatorname{Cub}_{i}\!\left(\{x\in R_{i}:\psi_{i}(x)\leq t\}\right).

(6)

Because a sublevel set can only grow as
t
increases,
K_{i}(u)\subseteq K_{i}(t)
whenever
u\leq t
. Hence
(K_{i}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a one-parameter filtration of
K_{i}^{\mathrm{ROI}}
. Since
R_{i}
is finite, only finitely many distinct complexes occur.

The negative part of the filtration has a direct geometric interpretation. For
r>0
, let

B_{i}(r)=\{x\in B_{i}:d_{i}(x,R_{i}\setminus B_{i})\geq r\}.

Then

K_{i}(-r)=\operatorname{Cub}_{i}\bigl(B_{i}(r)\bigr).

(7)

Thus, as the threshold increases from negative values toward zero, the filtered bone grows outward from voxel centers deepest in the bone phase until
K_{i}(0)=\operatorname{Cub}_{i}(B_{i})
. Positive thresholds subsequently admit marrow voxels lying increasingly farther from bone.

At each threshold
t
, define

\beta_{k,i}(t)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H_{k}\bigl(K_{i}(t);\mathbb{F}_{2}\bigr).

(8)

Whenever
u\leq t
, the inclusion
K_{i}(u)\hookrightarrow K_{i}(t)
induces a linear map

\rho^{(k)}_{u,t}:H_{k}\bigl(K_{i}(u);\mathbb{F}_{2}\bigr)\longrightarrow H_{k}\bigl(K_{i}(t);\mathbb{F}_{2}\bigr).

The homology groups together with these inclusion-induced maps form the
k
th persistence module. Because the filtration is finite, this module decomposes into interval summands
[
7
,
13
]
. A finite interval
[b,d)
records a homology class born at
b
and dying at
d>b
, with persistence
\ell=d-b
.

For vectorization, the finite intervals in dimension
k
are represented in birth and persistence coordinates by the multiset

D_{k,i}=\{(b,\ell):\ell=d-b>0\}.

Intervals of zero persistence are omitted. The terminal ROI complex is a connected digital cylinder, so its unique essential
H_{0}
class is excluded from the finite representation used for prediction. Here,
essential
means that the class survives every subsequent finite threshold.

The signed distance construction gives finite
H_{0}
intervals a particularly direct geometric interpretation. If a class is born at
b<0
, Equation (
7
) implies that its component first appears among bone voxels at least
-b
micrometers from marrow voxel centers. If its death satisfies
d\leq 0
, its merger occurs entirely within the nested interior bone sets. If
d>0
, the component survives through the original bone boundary and merges only after marrow voxels enter the filtration.
H_{0}
persistence therefore records both the depth at which connected interior regions appear and the scales at which those regions become connected.

This information is related to, but is not equivalent to, trabecular thickness. Local thickness is defined through maximal spheres contained in bone
[
12
]
, whereas
H_{0}
persistence records connectivity changes across an entire nested family of interior subcomplexes. Likewise,
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
describe cycles of the evolving filtered complex, not intrinsically less relevant features. Their association with strength is therefore an empirical question rather than something determined by homological dimension alone.

2.4
Persistence images

Persistence diagrams contain different numbers of intervals across specimens and therefore do not directly provide predictors of fixed dimension. Each diagram
D_{k,i}
is mapped to a persistence image by replacing every point in birth and persistence coordinates with a weighted Gaussian and integrating the resulting surface over a fixed rectangular grid
[
14
]
.

The isotropic Gaussian density with bandwidth
\sigma>0
is

\phi_{\sigma}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^{2}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right).

For homology dimension
k
, let
\omega_{k}(\ell)=\min\{\ell/\tau_{k},1\}
, where
\tau_{k}>0
is a fixed persistence scale. For a rectangular grid cell
P_{rs}=[u_{r},u_{r+1}]\times[v_{s},v_{s+1}]
, define

p^{(k)}_{i,rs}=\sum_{(b,\ell)\in D_{k,i}}\omega_{k}(\ell)\iint_{P_{rs}}\phi_{\sigma}(u-b,v-\ell)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}v.

(9)

Each coordinate therefore records integrated Gaussian mass rather than a sampled surface height. Since
D_{k,i}
is finite, linearity of integration permits the finite sum and integral to be interchanged.

Let
\Phi
denote the standard normal cumulative distribution function. Gaussian separability gives the exact contribution of one persistence point to one cell:

\displaystyle\iint_{P_{rs}}\phi_{\sigma}(u-b,v-\ell)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}v={}

\displaystyle\left[\Phi\!\left(\frac{u_{r+1}-b}{\sigma}\right)-\Phi\!\left(\frac{u_{r}-b}{\sigma}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\times\left[\Phi\!\left(\frac{v_{s+1}-\ell}{\sigma}\right)-\Phi\!\left(\frac{v_{s}-\ell}{\sigma}\right)\right].

(10)

Gaussian mass outside the finite grid domain is not represented.

. These choices were fixed from pooled baseline diagrams before strength outcomes were accessed and were then held fixed. Complete numerical conventions are reported in

2.5
Directional multiparameter filtrations

The signed distance construction assigns one scalar geometric scale to each voxel. To encode directional geometry, each bone voxel is instead assigned a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, using the coordinatewise order introduced in Section
2.2
. The result is a three-parameter filtration rather than three unrelated scalar filtrations.

The construction uses two local geometric ingredients: bone occupancy inside a cone aligned with a selected physical direction and the covariance structure of nearby bone. We first state the reference construction reproduced from
[
11
]
, because its dependence on a transverse coordinate choice motivates the invariant reformulation.

2.5.1
Directional cone coordinate

Fix a specimen
i
and a unit direction

d\in\mathbb{S}^{2}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\|u\|_{2}=1\}.

Let
R_{c}=75~\mu\mathrm{m}
be the cone radius and
H=150~\mu\mathrm{m}
its axial half extent. For voxel indices
x,y\in R_{i}
, define the physical displacement

r_{xy}=h_{i}(y-x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

With
I
the
3\times 3
identity, the symmetric double cone, restricted to the ROI, centered at
x
and oriented along
d
is

\Gamma_{i,d}(x)=\left\{y\in R_{i}:|r_{xy}^{\mathsf{T}}d|\leq H\text{ and }\|(I-dd^{\mathsf{T}})r_{xy}\|_{2}\leq\frac{R_{c}}{H}|r_{xy}^{\mathsf{T}}d|\right\}.

(12)

The projection
dd^{\mathsf{T}}r_{xy}
is the component parallel to
d
, while
(I-dd^{\mathsf{T}})r_{xy}
is the component in the perpendicular plane
d^{\perp}
. Hence the first inequality bounds axial displacement and the second makes the transverse radius grow linearly with axial distance.

For a bone voxel
x\in B_{i}
, define the
directional cone deficit

C_{i,d}(x)=1-\frac{|\Gamma_{i,d}(x)\cap B_{i}|}{|\Gamma_{i,d}(x)|}.

(13)

Then
0\leq C_{i,d}(x)\leq 1
, and smaller values correspond to greater bone occupancy inside the directional cone. The quantity depends on the physical direction
d
but not on a choice of basis for
d^{\perp}
. The cohort analysis uses
d\in\{e_{x},e_{y},e_{z}\}
, with
e_{z}
corresponding to the loading direction.

2.5.2
Reference coordinates and frame dependence

Local orientation is estimated from the spatial distribution of neighboring bone voxels. Let
R_{p}=75~\mu\mathrm{m}
. The hard neighborhood used in the original reproduction is

w_{x}^{\mathrm{H}}(y)=\mathbf{1}\{\|r_{xy}\|_{2}\leq R_{p}\},

(14)

whereas the later smooth weighting uses
\sigma_{p}=R_{p}/\sqrt{5}
and is

w_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}(y)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\|r_{xy}\|_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma_{p}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{1}\{\|r_{xy}\|_{2}\leq 4\sigma_{p}\}.

(15)

Before truncation, this choice of
\sigma_{p}
matches the radial second moment of a uniform solid ball of radius
R_{p}
; the Gaussian weight at the truncation radius is
\exp(-8)
.

For either radial weighting, set

W_{x}=\sum_{y\in B_{i}}w_{x}(y).

Then set

\mu_{x}=\frac{1}{W_{x}}\sum_{y\in B_{i}}w_{x}(y)r_{xy}.

The weighted covariance is

\Sigma_{x}=\frac{1}{W_{x}}\sum_{y\in B_{i}}w_{x}(y)(r_{xy}-\mu_{x})(r_{xy}-\mu_{x})^{\mathsf{T}}.

(16)

The matrix
\Sigma_{x}
is symmetric positive semidefinite. Let

\lambda_{1}(x)\geq\lambda_{2}(x)\geq\lambda_{3}(x)\geq 0

be its eigenvalues. Where the leading eigenspace is one-dimensional, choose a unit eigenvector
v_{1}(x)
for
\lambda_{1}(x)
and define

P_{q}(x)=1-|v_{1}(x)^{\mathsf{T}}e_{q}|.

(17)

The absolute value removes the sign ambiguity
v_{1}\leftrightarrow-v_{1}
, but it does not resolve the lack of uniqueness when the largest eigenvalue is repeated.

Writing
C_{q}(x)=C_{i,e_{q}}(x)
, the reproduced directional coordinates can be written as the single map

F_{q}^{\mathrm{leg}}(x)=\begin{cases}(C_{x}(x),P_{z}(x),P_{x}(x)),&q=x,\\
(C_{y}(x),P_{z}(x),P_{y}(x)),&q=y,\\
(C_{z}(x),P_{y}(x),P_{z}(x)),&q=z.\end{cases}

(18)

The first coordinate is directional cone occupancy and the third measures alignment with the requested axis. The second coordinate, however, retains one selected component in the plane perpendicular to that axis. Once
d
is fixed, the physical plane
d^{\perp}
admits infinitely many orthonormal bases. Rotating that transverse basis can therefore change the second coordinate while leaving
d
and the physical bone neighborhood unchanged. Replacing Equation (
14
) by the smoother Equation (
15
) changes the covariance estimate but does not remove this dependence on the transverse frame.

2.6
Frame-invariant directional coordinates

The invariant construction replaces selected components of a principal eigenvector by scalars determined by the covariance tensor itself and the selected physical direction. In this subsection,
\Sigma_{x}
denotes the Gaussian weighted covariance obtained from Equation (
16
) using
w_{x}^{\mathrm{G}}
.

Definition 2
(Invariant local coordinates)

For a bone voxel
x
with
\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{x}>0
and a unit direction
d
, define the
directional covariance deficit

A_{i,d}(x)=1-\sqrt{\frac{d^{\mathsf{T}}\Sigma_{x}d}{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{x}}},

(19)

and the
principal axis degeneracy

S_{i}(x)=\frac{\lambda_{2}(x)}{\lambda_{1}(x)}.

(20)

If the weighted neighborhood contains fewer than two bone voxels with positive weight, or if
\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{x}=0
, set
A_{i,d}(x)=S_{i}(x)=1
.

Because
\Sigma_{x}
is positive semidefinite,

0\leq d^{\mathsf{T}}\Sigma_{x}d\leq\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{x},

so
0\leq A_{i,d}(x)\leq 1
. A small
A_{i,d}
means that a large fraction of local covariance lies along
d
. Likewise,
0\leq S_{i}(x)\leq 1
. The value
S_{i}=0
corresponds to a rank one covariance, whereas
S_{i}\to 1
indicates that the two largest eigenvalues become comparable and the leading local axis becomes less distinct.

The next proposition connects the invariant alignment coordinate to the principal axis coordinate in the idealized rank one case.

Proposition 1

Suppose
\Sigma_{x}=\lambda vv^{\mathsf{T}}
with
\lambda>0
and
\|v\|_{2}=1
. Then
A_{i,d}(x)=1-|v^{\mathsf{T}}d|
and
S_{i}(x)=0
.

Proof 1

Since
\operatorname{tr}(\lambda vv^{\mathsf{T}})=\lambda
and
d^{\mathsf{T}}\Sigma_{x}d=\lambda(v^{\mathsf{T}}d)^{2}
, Equation (
19
) gives

A_{i,d}(x)=1-\sqrt{(v^{\mathsf{T}}d)^{2}}=1-|v^{\mathsf{T}}d|.

The remaining eigenvalues of the rank one matrix are zero, so
S_{i}(x)=0
.

Thus the frame-invariant descriptor assigned to a voxel is

F_{i,d}^{\mathrm{inv}}(x)=\bigl(C_{i,d}(x),A_{i,d}(x),S_{i}(x)\bigr).

(21)

The relevant notion of frame invariance is a passive change of orthonormal coordinates that keeps the physical direction
d
fixed. Let

O(3)=\{Q\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:Q^{\mathsf{T}}Q=I\}.

Proposition 2
(Transverse-frame invariance)

Let
Q\in O(3)
satisfy
Qd=d
. Express the same physical neighborhood in the transformed orthonormal frame by

r^{\prime}_{xy}=Qr_{xy}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}_{x}=Q\Sigma_{x}Q^{\mathsf{T}}
. Then
C^{\prime}_{i,d}(x)=C_{i,d}(x)
,
A^{\prime}_{i,d}(x)=A_{i,d}(x)
, and
S^{\prime}_{i}(x)=S_{i}(x)
. Consequently,
F_{i,d}^{\mathrm{inv}}(x)
is independent of the orthonormal basis chosen in
d^{\perp}
.

Proof 2

Let
P_{d}=I-dd^{\mathsf{T}}
be the orthogonal projector onto
d^{\perp}
. Since
Q
is orthogonal and
Qd=d
, also
Q^{\mathsf{T}}d=d
and
QP_{d}=P_{d}Q
. Hence

(r^{\prime}_{xy})^{\mathsf{T}}d=r_{xy}^{\mathsf{T}}d

and

\|P_{d}r^{\prime}_{xy}\|_{2}=\|P_{d}Qr_{xy}\|_{2}=\|QP_{d}r_{xy}\|_{2}=\|P_{d}r_{xy}\|_{2}.

The two inequalities defining the cone in Equation (
12
) are therefore unchanged, so
C^{\prime}_{i,d}(x)=C_{i,d}(x)
.

Moreover,
\Sigma^{\prime}_{x}=Q\Sigma_{x}Q^{\mathsf{T}}
is orthogonally similar to
\Sigma_{x}
, so the two matrices have the same eigenvalues and the same trace. Finally,

d^{\mathsf{T}}\Sigma^{\prime}_{x}d=d^{\mathsf{T}}Q\Sigma_{x}Q^{\mathsf{T}}d=d^{\mathsf{T}}\Sigma_{x}d.

Equations (
19
) and (
20
) then give the remaining two identities.

Proposition
2
concerns two coordinate descriptions of the same physical neighborhood and is therefore exact. It is different from physically rotating a voxelized image and resampling it on a Cartesian lattice; the latter changes the discrete sampled geometry through interpolation and voxelization and is not expected to be exactly invariant.

2.7
Cubical extension of the directional coordinates

The vector
F_{i,d}^{\mathrm{inv}}(x)
is initially attached only to a bone voxel, equivalently to its top-dimensional cube
Q_{i}(x)
. To obtain a filtration by cubical
subcomplexes
, values must also be assigned to lower-dimensional faces in a way that guarantees closure under taking faces.

Let
\gamma=1.25
and assign to each top-dimensional ROI cube

f_{i,d}(Q_{i}(x))=\begin{cases}F_{i,d}^{\mathrm{inv}}(x),&x\in B_{i},\\
(\gamma,\gamma,\gamma),&x\in R_{i}\setminus B_{i}.\end{cases}

(22)

The value
\gamma>1
is a terminal numerical level, not a biological parameter. Since all bone coordinates lie in
[0,1]
, marrow enters only after every level assigned to bone. Cubes outside
R_{i}
are absent from the ambient complex. The legacy construction uses the same extension after replacing
F_{i,d}^{\mathrm{inv}}
by the corresponding triple in Equation (
18
).

For a cubical cell
\sigma\in K_{i}^{\mathrm{ROI}}
, let

\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\sigma)=\{Q_{i}(x):x\in R_{i}\text{ and }\sigma\text{ is a face of }Q_{i}(x)\}

be the set of incident top-dimensional ROI cubes. Extend coordinate
j\in\{1,2,3\}
to every cell by the face minimum

\bar{f}_{i,d,j}(\sigma)=\min_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\sigma)}f_{i,d,j}(Q).

(23)

For
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, define

K_{i,d}(\mathbf{t})=\{\sigma\in K_{i}^{\mathrm{ROI}}:\bar{f}_{i,d,j}(\sigma)\leq t_{j}\text{ for }j=1,2,3\}.

(24)

Proposition 3

For every
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
K_{i,d}(\mathbf{t})
is a cubical subcomplex of
K_{i}^{\mathrm{ROI}}
. If
\mathbf{s}\preceq\mathbf{t}
, then

K_{i,d}(\mathbf{s})\subseteq K_{i,d}(\mathbf{t}).

Hence
(K_{i,d}(\mathbf{t}))_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}
is a three-parameter cubical filtration.

Proof 3

Let
\sigma\in K_{i,d}(\mathbf{t})
and let
\tau
be a face of
\sigma
. Every top-dimensional cube containing
\sigma
also contains
\tau
, so

\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\sigma)\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\tau).

Taking a minimum over the larger set can only decrease the value, giving

\bar{f}_{i,d,j}(\tau)\leq\bar{f}_{i,d,j}(\sigma)\leq t_{j}

for every
j
. Thus
\tau\in K_{i,d}(\mathbf{t})
, proving closure under faces. The monotonicity statement follows immediately from
s_{j}\leq t_{j}
for every coordinate.

An important consequence of Equation (
23
) is that the three minima for a lower-dimensional cell need not be attained by the same incident voxel cube. A cell may therefore satisfy all three threshold inequalities even when no single adjacent voxel satisfies them jointly. Accordingly,
K_{i,d}(\mathbf{t})
is the cubical subcomplex defined by the coordinatewise face minimum extension; in general it is not merely the closure of a jointly thresholded voxel mask.

2.8
Sampled Betti tensors

The complete three-parameter persistence module is not computed. Instead, the filtration is sampled on the finite poset

\mathcal{G}=\{0,\ldots,8\}^{3}

with coordinatewise order. Thus the resulting arrays are samples of the multivariate Betti function, not a complete multiparameter persistence invariant.

For fixed specimen
i
, direction
d
, and filtration coordinate
j
, let
m_{j}=\min_{Q}f_{i,d,j}(Q)
and
M_{j}=\max_{Q}f_{i,d,j}(Q)
, where the extrema are taken over top-dimensional ROI cubes. Set

\delta_{j}=\frac{M_{j}-m_{j}}{8}.

(25)

Because marrow has terminal value
\gamma=1.25
and bone coordinates lie in
[0,1]
,
M_{j}>m_{j}
and hence
\delta_{j}>0
.

Assign each top-dimensional cube the discrete coordinate

b_{j}(Q)=\left\lfloor\frac{f_{i,d,j}(Q)-m_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\right\rfloor\in\{0,\ldots,8\}.

(26)

For
a<8
, the condition
b_{j}(Q)\leq a
is equivalent to

f_{i,d,j}(Q)<m_{j}+(a+1)\delta_{j},

whereas index
8
includes every top-dimensional cube. Thus an index denotes inclusion of all bins up to that index; it should not be interpreted as evaluation at the lower bin edge
m_{j}+a\delta_{j}
.

For a lower-dimensional cell
\sigma
, set

b_{j}(\sigma)=\min_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\sigma)}b_{j}(Q).

(27)

Since the floor function is nondecreasing and the incident set is finite, quantization commutes with the coordinatewise minimum:

\min_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\sigma)}\left\lfloor\frac{f_{i,d,j}(Q)-m_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{\min_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\sigma)}f_{i,d,j}(Q)-m_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\right\rfloor.

For
(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{G}
, define the sampled cubical complex

K_{i,d}[a,b,c]=\{\sigma:b_{1}(\sigma)\leq a,\ b_{2}(\sigma)\leq b,\ b_{3}(\sigma)\leq c\}.

(28)

The
9^{3}=729
sampled complexes inherit the coordinatewise nesting of the underlying three-parameter filtration.

For homology dimension
q\in\{0,1,2\}
, the
directional Betti tensor
is

\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{d}(a,b,c)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H_{q}\bigl(K_{i,d}[a,b,c];\mathbb{F}_{2}\bigr).

(29)

Each tensor
\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{d}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{9\times 9\times 9}
contains 729 Betti numbers. It records the dimension of homology at each sampled parameter value, but not the inclusion-induced maps between different parameter values. Therefore it does not by itself determine which particular classes persist, merge, or disappear across the parameter grid. In addition, because
(m_{j},M_{j})
are estimated separately for each specimen and direction, a common tensor index represents a common
relative
filtration level rather than a common numerical threshold.

For the three cardinal directions, define the transverse mean by

\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{\perp}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{x}+\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{y}\bigr).

(30)

The axial contrast is

\Delta\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{\mathrm{ax}}=\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{z}-\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{\perp}.

(31)

These are derived tensor summaries and need not themselves be Betti functions of cubical complexes; in particular,
\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{\perp}
may have noninteger entries. When all three directional tensors are retained as predictors, no averaging is performed: the vectorized block is

\operatorname{vec}\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{x}\oplus\operatorname{vec}\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{y}\oplus\operatorname{vec}\mathcal{B}_{q,i}^{z},

with
3\times 729=2187
coordinates for each homology dimension.

The implementation computes
\mathcal{B}_{1}
through the Euler–Poincaré identity. For a finite cubical complex
K
, let
n_{k}(K)
be the number of its
k
-cells. Then

\chi(K)=n_{0}(K)-n_{1}(K)+n_{2}(K)-n_{3}(K)

and

\chi(K)=\sum_{k=0}^{3}(-1)^{k}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H_{k}(K;\mathbb{F}_{2}).

For the finite cubical subcomplexes considered here,
H_{3}=0
. Indeed, any nonempty finite collection of three-dimensional voxel cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
has at least one exposed square face, so a nonzero
3
-chain cannot have zero cubical boundary. Hence
\ker\partial_{3}=0
.

Define the sampled Euler tensor

\mathcal{E}_{i}^{d}(a,b,c)=\chi\bigl(K_{i,d}[a,b,c]\bigr).

Then

\mathcal{E}_{i}^{d}=\mathcal{B}_{0,i}^{d}-\mathcal{B}_{1,i}^{d}+\mathcal{B}_{2,i}^{d},

(32)

or equivalently

\mathcal{B}_{1,i}^{d}=\mathcal{B}_{0,i}^{d}+\mathcal{B}_{2,i}^{d}-\mathcal{E}_{i}^{d}.

(33)

The implementation computes
\mathcal{B}_{0}
from connectivity in the filtered 1-skeleton using union–find,
\mathcal{B}_{2}
from connectivity of the dual complement after identifying the exterior component, and
\mathcal{E}
independently from cubical cell counts. Equation (
33
) then gives
\mathcal{B}_{1}
.

Finally, Proposition
2
propagates through the complete sampled construction. A transverse change of orthonormal coordinates that fixes
d
leaves the values on top-dimensional cells unchanged; therefore the coordinatewise face minima, extrema for each specimen, bins, sampled subcomplexes, and their Betti and Euler tensors are all unchanged. The sampled representation is thus exactly transverse-frame invariant for a fixed voxel geometry, while remaining subject to ordinary resampling error under physical rotation of the voxelized image.

3
Methods

3.1
Image construction

Of 59 available microCT volumes, 24 core identifiers could be matched to recorded apparent compressive strength. These comprised 19 fracture (FX), three nonfracture (NF), and two osteoarthritis (OA) cores. For specimen
i
, the prediction target was

y_{i}=F_{\max,i}/A_{0,i},

where
F_{\max,i}
is the maximum recorded compressive force and
A_{0,i}
is the recorded area of the cross section. Specimen collection, consent, and mechanical testing are described in the source study and thesis
[
15
,
16
]
. The analysis therefore concerns measured strength of excised cores; clinical fracture status was not the prediction target.

Scans were assigned random identifiers QC001–QC024 before image analysis. Specimen identities, clinical labels, and mechanical outcomes were withheld until the primary image representations had been fixed. The image
Z
axis corresponded to the loading direction. A central cylinder of radius
2.75~\mathrm{mm}
and nominal height
8.50~\mathrm{mm}
defined the ROI. Its axial position was determined from slice intensity variation, with fixed trimming of the specimen ends. Voxel spacing was effectively isotropic within each scan and was retained in physical distance calculations.

For grayscale image
I_{i}
and ROI indicator
M_{i}
, normalized masked smoothing for
x\in R_{i}
was

I_{\sigma_{I},i}(x)=\frac{[G_{\sigma_{I}}*(I_{i}M_{i})](x)}{[G_{\sigma_{I}}*M_{i}](x)}.

The Gaussian standard deviation was
\sigma_{I}=0.50
voxel, with a kernel radius of two voxels. A single three-dimensional Otsu threshold
T_{i}
, estimated from a 256-bin histogram inside the ROI, yields the binary image whose bone voxels are given by

B_{i}=\{x\in R_{i}:I_{\sigma_{I},i}(x)>T_{i}\}.

No component filtering, hole filling, opening, closing, or manual topological correction was applied. Figure
2
illustrates this construction. Appendix
A
specifies the axial detection rule and morphometric boundary conventions, including the inscribed prism used for anisotropy estimation.

3.3
Nested prediction and uncertainty

3.3.1
Preprocessing in training folds

Variance filtering, centering, and scaling were estimated anew in every inner and outer training set. For a predictor block on training set
T
, let
s_{ij}
denote the support values. Their weighted mean is

\bar{s}_{j}=\frac{\sum_{i\in T}w_{i}s_{ij}}{\sum_{i\in T}w_{i}}.

The corresponding weighted variance is

v_{j}=\frac{\sum_{i\in T}w_{i}(s_{ij}-\bar{s}_{j})^{2}}{\sum_{i\in T}w_{i}}.

The active set was
J_{T}=\{j:v_{j}>10^{-12}\max_{k}v_{k}\}
. Persistence image support used the image coordinates themselves.
For the directional predictors normalized by ROI size, support values were determined from the corresponding raw count tensors before normalization.

For the predictor values
x_{ij}
entering an active block, let
\bar{x}_{j}
be the weighted training mean. One scale was assigned to the entire block:

\rho_{T}^{2}=\frac{\sum_{i\in T}w_{i}\sum_{j\in J_{T}}(x_{ij}-\bar{x}_{j})^{2}}{(\sum_{i\in T}w_{i})|J_{T}|}.

The transformed predictor was then

\widetilde{x}_{ij}=\frac{x_{ij}-\bar{x}_{j}}{\rho_{T}}.

Topological blocks were processed separately before concatenation. Scalar morphometric predictors were standardized individually and their block was divided by the square root of its dimension. Each transformation was applied unchanged to the corresponding validation observations. The persistence image grid itself had been constructed from pooled baseline images before outcome access, as specified in Section
2
. It was not reconstructed within these folds.

3.3.2
Model fitting and evaluation

For transformed predictors
x_{i}
, weighted ridge regression
[
17
]
solved

(\widehat{\beta}_{0},\widehat{\beta})=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\beta_{0},\beta}\left\{\sum_{i\in T}w_{i}(y_{i}-\beta_{0}-x_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}\beta)^{2}+\alpha\|\beta\|_{2}^{2}\right\}.

The intercept was unpenalized. The outer loop withheld one complete group. Within the remaining groups, inner leave-one-group-out validation selected
\alpha
from the 49 values
10^{-6+j/4}
,
j=0,\ldots,48
, by mean squared error with equal weight per validation group. The selected model was refitted on the complete outer training set and applied to the held out group. Training mean prediction and ordinary linear regression on BV/TV alone provided simpler references.

Let
\widehat{y}_{i}^{\,x}
be the outer prediction from representation
x
. The principal error measure was

\mathcal{R}_{G}(x)=\left\{\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{24}w_{i}(y_{i}-\widehat{y}_{i}^{\,x})^{2}\right\}^{1/2}.

(34)

The original primary contrasts were
\Delta_{\mathrm{rep}}=\mathcal{R}_{G}(p)-\mathcal{R}_{G}(m)
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{add}}=\mathcal{R}_{G}(m\oplus p)-\mathcal{R}_{G}(m)
. Negative values favor the candidate representation or its addition. Mean absolute error and
Q_{G}^{2}
are defined in Appendix
B
.

Uncertainty intervals used 10,000 bootstrap resamples of the 13 groups, with the same resampled groups for both models in a paired comparison. Metrics were recalculated from fixed outer predictions; models were not refitted during the bootstrap. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles therefore describe variation in the observed prediction errors conditional on the fitted procedure. They do not include uncertainty from repeating representation development or selecting a homology dimension. Later exploratory comparisons were not adjusted for multiplicity. All reported differences retain the precision of the original analyses, so subtraction of rounded table entries can differ in the last decimal.

3.4
Representation similarity

The later families of descriptors summarized the thinned skeleton (eight variables), axial routes and graph transport (five), a plate and rod organization proxy (one), local orientation variation (three), and the shape of the surface-normal Minkowski tensor
W_{1}^{0,2}
(two). Their definitions and complete comparisons appear in Appendix
D
. The organization proxy uses local geometric samples rather than individual trabeculae reconstructed by ITS. The graph transport model uses branch lengths rather than tissue mechanics. These distinctions delimit the representations being tested.

Representation agreement was assessed separately from prediction. For column-centered specimen matrices
X_{c}
and
Z_{c}
, linear centered-kernel alignment was
[
18
]

\operatorname{CKA}(X,Z)=\frac{\|X_{c}^{\mathsf{T}}Z_{c}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|X_{c}^{\mathsf{T}}X_{c}\|_{F}\,\|Z_{c}^{\mathsf{T}}Z_{c}\|_{F}}.

(35)

whenever the denominator is positive. Here,
\|\cdot\|_{F}
is the Frobenius norm. We also computed Spearman correlation between the upper triangles of the two Euclidean interspecimen distance matrices. Both summaries describe the geometry induced on this cohort. Neither tests incremental prediction.

3.5
Haufe activation patterns

The same linear model interpretation is used in two different feature spaces later in the manuscript, so the transformation is stated once here. For an outer training fold
T
, let
X_{T}
be a centered predictor block on its original feature scale and let
s_{T}
be the centered fitted scalar score from the corresponding ridge model. On an active feature coordinate
j
, define the weighted
Haufe activation pattern

[
19
]
by

\mathcal{H}_{T}(X_{T},s_{T})_{j}=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_{w,T}(X_{T,j},s_{T})}{\operatorname{Var}_{w,T}(s_{T})},

(36)

provided that
\operatorname{Var}_{w,T}(s_{T})>0
. Weighted covariance and variance use weights
w_{i}/\sum_{i\in T}w_{i}
. Coordinate
j
is therefore the weighted least-squares slope obtained by regressing feature
j
on the fitted score. The pattern describes covariance with a model prediction; it is neither a causal effect nor a derivative of strength with respect to the underlying geometry.

For interpretation of the frame-invariant directional model, let
X_{T}^{\mathrm{dir}}
be the 729-coordinate
\mathcal{B}_{0}^{z}
block normalized by ROI size and define

a_{T}^{\mathrm{dir}}=\mathcal{H}_{T}(X_{T}^{\mathrm{dir}},s_{T}^{\mathrm{dir}}).

(37)

For the later signed distance
H_{0}
matched pilot, the same transformation is applied instead to the signed distance
H_{0}
persistence image block; that distinct pattern is denoted
a_{T}^{\mathrm{SD}}
. Thus
a_{T}^{\mathrm{dir}}
and
a_{T}^{\mathrm{SD}}
use the same mathematical transformation but are patterns in different predictor spaces and should not be conflated.

For the invariant directional analysis, patterns with nonzero norm were normalized as
\widetilde{a}_{T}^{\mathrm{dir}}=a_{T}^{\mathrm{dir}}/\|a_{T}^{\mathrm{dir}}\|_{2}
, and the
consensus Haufe pattern
was the mean of these normalized patterns from the outer fits on coordinates available across fits. All invariant fits retained the same 728 nonterminal coordinates. Pairwise cosine similarity, sign agreement, and connected regions on the parameter lattice summarized stability. The sign rule required activity and a common sign in at least 12 of 13 fits. Coordinates in the upper quartile of consensus amplitude were used only for visualization; complete diagnostics are given in Appendix
C.4
. Because the outer training sets overlap, these stability summaries are dependent.

3.6
Equal removal perturbations

Each baseline was modified by removing

r_{i}=\lfloor 0.02|B_{i}|+0.5\rfloor

bone voxels. Diffuse removal was eligible throughout the ROI except for the first and last
0.50~\mathrm{mm}
. Localized removal was further restricted to a central
1.50~\mathrm{mm}
band. Within each eligible set, voxels were ranked by distance to marrow, followed by a deterministic hash and voxel index. Selecting the first
r_{i}
voxels gave removal sets
U_{i}^{D}
and
U_{i}^{L}
. The diffuse structure is

B_{i}^{D}=B_{i}\setminus U_{i}^{D}.

The localized structure is

B_{i}^{L}=B_{i}\setminus U_{i}^{L}.

Since
|U_{i}^{D}|=|U_{i}^{L}|=r_{i}
, both structures have identical BV/TV in the same ROI. This fixes retained bone amount while allowing its spatial arrangement and other morphometric coordinates to differ. Acceptance required a six-connected bone component spanning the loading direction. Appendix
E
gives the deterministic construction.

For coarsening factor
q
, a coarse element
Q_{e}
retained fractional bone occupancy

\rho_{e}(B)=\frac{1}{|Q_{e}|}\sum_{x\in Q_{e}}\mathbf{1}_{B}(x).

Its element stiffness was

K_{e}(B)=\rho_{e}(B)K_{s,e},

where
K_{s,e}
is the stiffness matrix of a solid element with the same dimensions. Equilibrium minimizes

\mathcal{U}_{B}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e}u_{e}^{\mathsf{T}}K_{e}(B)u_{e}

subject to prescribed axial compression. The primary calculation used
q=2
, trilinear hexahedra with full Gauss integration, solid Young’s modulus of
2000~\mathrm{MPa}
, Poisson ratio of
0.25
, and axial strain
\varepsilon=0.001
. Lateral displacements were free except for constraints removing rigid motion. No occupancy threshold was applied. Factor
q=3
was a specified resolution sensitivity
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

The apparent modulus was
\mathcal{E}_{q}(B)=F/(A_{i}\varepsilon)
, where
F
is the mean magnitude of the top and bottom axial reactions and
A_{i}
is the cross-sectional area of the original digital ROI. This elastic quantity differs from measured failure strength
y_{i}
. Scaling the common tissue modulus scales reactions and apparent moduli equally, and therefore cancels from relative contrasts within each specimen.

Writing
E_{X,i}^{(q)}=\mathcal{E}_{q}(B_{i}^{X})
for
X\in\{B,D,L\}
, with
B_{i}^{B}=B_{i}
, define the relative change for
X\in\{D,L\}
by

R_{X,i}^{(q)}=\frac{E_{X,i}^{(q)}-E_{B,i}^{(q)}}{E_{B,i}^{(q)}}.

The localized minus diffuse contrast is

C_{i}^{(q)}=\frac{E_{L,i}^{(q)}-E_{D,i}^{(q)}}{E_{B,i}^{(q)}}.

The contrast with equal weight per validation group was
\widehat{C}^{(q)}=G^{-1}\sum_{i}w_{i}C_{i}^{(q)}
. A negative value favors lower stiffness after localized removal. Its interval used 200,000 group bootstrap resamples. Solver residuals, reaction balance, and energy consistency were checked independently; complete tolerances and resolution handling are given in Appendix
E
.

3.7
Testing scalar topological interpretations

For each removal, the relative persistence image displacement was

d_{k,i}^{X,B}=\frac{\|p_{k,i}^{X}-p_{k,i}^{B}\|_{2}}{\|p_{k,i}^{B}\|_{2}}.

The corresponding mechanical loss was

L_{X,i}^{(q)}=1-\frac{E_{X,i}^{(q)}}{E_{B,i}^{(q)}}.

These quantities test the specific interpretation that greater global image displacement indicates greater elastic damage. Persistence image grids and weights were unchanged from baseline.

A second post hoc pilot selected the core nearest the cohort median BV/TV without using strength or topology. From 120 deterministic candidates with equal bone removal, admissible pairs had identical BV/TV and differences below
0.10
cohort standard deviations in each remaining morphometric coordinate. A regional score then selected the admissible pair with the greatest signed
H_{0}
separation. Its source was the
signed distance
H_{0}
Haufe pattern

a^{\mathrm{SD}}
, obtained by applying Equation (
36
) to the human
H_{0}
persistence image model rather than to the directional predictors. Each candidate image was projected onto normalized
a^{\mathrm{SD}}
weights in a fixed
5\times 5
region. The region and selection rule were specified before the mechanical calculations. The hypothesis was that the higher regional score would correspond to higher simulated stiffness. Because the score came from the same human strength analysis, this tests an interpretation informed by those outcomes rather than external prediction. Appendix
F
records the regional bounds, matching criteria, and selection rule.

5
Discussion

5.1
Geometric contribution and empirical scope

The principal methodological contribution is the removal of an arbitrary transverse coordinate from a directional topological representation. Once the physical direction is fixed, changing the basis used to describe its perpendicular plane should not change a descriptor conditioned on that axis. The reference construction did change under this operation. The proposed coordinates depend instead on cone occupancy, a covariance quadratic form, and an eigenvalue ratio. Their invariance follows directly from Euclidean geometry and orthogonal similarity, and propagates to the sampled cubical Betti tensors. This property holds independently of the strength results.

The empirical contribution is more limited. Dimension zero had the lowest observed error within both the signed distance and invariant directional constructions, with similar errors in the harmonized comparison. This convergence identifies a focused hypothesis about organization of connected components. It is not an independent replication: the invariant construction was developed after the check of the reference construction and earlier outcomes were known. Furthermore, the original combined persistence image comparison did not establish improvement over morphometry. Retaining these distinctions allows the mathematical result and the exploratory biomedical evidence to be assessed on their respective grounds.

The redesign also changed Gaussian weighting and the information encoded by the orientation coordinates. Consequently, the improvement over the reference directional model cannot be attributed solely to removing frame dependence. A prospective comparison with controlled ablations would be needed to separate these contributions. Even without such a performance claim, avoiding an irrelevant coordinate choice is a useful design requirement for computational representations intended to be compared across image acquisitions.

5.2
Interpretation of
H_{0}
in the two filtrations

Section
2.3
gives the formal interpretation of signed distance
H_{0}
. For the present results, the important point is that an
H_{0}
interval records both the depth at which an interior component appears and the scale at which it merges as the sublevel complex grows. Mean trabecular thickness summarizes diameters of maximal inscribed spheres and therefore need not encode the same sequence of connectivity changes. The two descriptors can reflect related local scale without being mathematically equivalent.

The interpretation depends on the entire interval. An
H_{0}
class dying at a positive threshold merges only after marrow has entered the filtered complex. The regional score used in the pilot included births of approximately
-754
to
-581~\mu\mathrm{m}
and persistences of
506
to
675~\mu\mathrm{m}
, permitting death values on either side of zero. Calling that region a direct thickness measurement would therefore omit part of its filtration meaning. Likewise,
H_{2}
describes cavities of the evolving complex, which need not be anatomical cavities of the original segmentation. Its weaker prediction in this cohort does not imply general mechanical irrelevance.

Invariant directional
\mathcal{B}_{0}^{d}
asks a different question. At each parameter triple it counts components in a complex selected by directional occupancy, covariance concentration, and principal axis degeneracy. The lower-dimensional cells can inherit their coordinatewise minima from different incident voxels. Thus these components belong to the defined cubical multifiltration and need not be components of a simultaneously thresholded voxel mask. The tensor records the number present at each sampled level; unlike the signed distance persistence module, it does not retain maps identifying which classes survive between levels. A stable positive region of the directional Haufe pattern consequently supports an association with component counts across several constraints, rather than a claim about particular components that persist over long parameter ranges or mechanical load paths.

The moderate agreement between the two cohort geometries is consistent with their distinct constructions. However, different representations can encode overlapping predictive information. The combined
H_{0}
model had the lowest point error in its comparison, while both paired intervals included zero. Its usefulness as an augmented predictor therefore remains undetermined.

5.3
Mechanical experiments and interpretation

Prediction across specimens and response to an intervention are different mathematical relationships. The first estimates
y
from a representation of
B
within the observed cohort. The second compares a mechanical functional at
B
and at a deliberately altered structure. No implication between these relationships follows from a low prediction error alone. In particular, the regional score derived from the signed distance Haufe pattern used in the pilot projects new geometry onto a Haufe pattern estimated from the human strength model; treating that projection as a mechanical ordering introduces an additional transfer hypothesis
[
19
]
.

The equal removal experiment supplies a controlled test of one such interpretation. Bone amount was fixed exactly between variants, yet localized removal caused greater stiffness loss in most cores despite smaller global
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
image displacements. The additional loss was small in magnitude but consistent at the two discretizations. The matched pilot narrowed conventional morphometric differences further and again placed the higher topological score below the lower score in elastic modulus. Together, these observations limit two proposed scalar surrogates: global persistence image distance and the regional score derived from the signed distance
H_{0}
Haufe pattern.

These findings concern simulated linear elastic stiffness. The supervised endpoint was measured failure strength, which can also depend on yielding, buckling, damage, and material heterogeneity. Those mechanisms were not represented in the mechanical calculation. The perturbations therefore constrain the interpretation of the strength associations without refuting their observed predictive value. They also do not test the invariant directional representation under intervention, because the reported counterfactual scores came from signed distance persistence.

5.4
Other structural descriptors and biomedical scope

The structural comparators represent complementary geometric ideas: a thinned network, axial graph routes, distributions of local plate and rod orientations, spatial misorientation, and surface normal anisotropy. Their inclusion broadens the geometric comparison, but their tested implementations have specific limits. Graph conductance omitted the areas of trabecular cross sections and constitutive behavior; the organization proxy did not reconstruct individual trabeculae; and the surface tensor block retained eigenvalue shape while excluding magnitude and principal direction. Their errors cannot be generalized to every method in those families.

The modest or unresolved increments in these post hoc analyses show why geometric novelty and predictive benefit require separate evidence. They also leave a specific comparison unfinished: the focal
H_{0}
models have not been evaluated prospectively against all structural blocks in one common pipeline. The present results are therefore most relevant to computational characterization of excised trabecular architecture. They do not establish a tool for clinical fracture risk or performance at routine clinical CT resolution.

5.5
Limitations and validation

The cohort contains 24 cores and only 13 assigned validation groups, with strong imbalance among clinical categories. Because those groups are not verified donors, residual donor overlap across folds cannot be excluded. The small sample also limits adjustment for confounding and the precision of calibration. Bootstrap intervals condition on fixed outer predictions, while the training sets used for Haufe pattern stability overlap. Neither procedure accounts fully for repeating data collection, representation development, and model selection.

Outcome blinding protected the initial image construction, but the persistence image grids used pooled baseline diagrams from all specimens. This outcome-independent use of held-out image distributions differs from a wholly representation estimated only from training data. The later emphasis on
H_{0}
, invariant coordinate construction, and Haufe pattern summaries adds selection uncertainty. Their exploratory intervals should therefore guide future hypotheses rather than serve as confirmatory tests after repeated comparisons.

Image conventions introduce further limits. The ROI samples part of the mechanically tested core; distance is measured between voxel centers; and segmentation determines the interface used by several descriptors. The rectangular connectivity correction does not explicitly correct the curved ROI wall, and anisotropy was measured within an inscribed prism. For directional tensors, binning performed separately for each specimen means that a shared index denotes a relative level, not a common numerical threshold. Frame invariance also does not remove errors from physically rotating and resampling a voxel lattice.

Finally, deterministic voxel removal is not biological remodeling, and coarse elasticity with fractional occupancy does not establish mechanics at native resolution. The two coarsening factors preserved the reported orderings while changing absolute modulus. The pilot matched in morphometry examined one deliberately selected pair, with small stiffness differences. These experiments support conclusions only within their stated geometries and constitutive assumptions.

The next validation should use independent specimens with verified donor identities and a prespecified analysis specification. Signed distance
H_{0}
, invariant directional
\mathcal{B}_{0}^{z}
, their combination, and the intended morphometric comparators should be fixed before outcome access. That specification must include ROI and segmentation rules, physical grids and weights, directional normalization and binning, preprocessing, tuning, and the primary comparison. Such a study would test whether the observed association based on component counts transfers beyond this development cohort.

6
Conclusions

The proposed directional filtration removes ambiguity in the transverse frame through geometrically defined coordinates and an explicit cubical extension. In this cohort, dimension zero had the lowest observed error within both signed distance and invariant directional topology, but the later comparison did not distinguish their predictive performance or establish a benefit from combining them. The original combined persistence image benchmark also did not establish improvement over morphometry. Equal removal experiments and a pair matched in morphometry limited the interpretation of signed distance scores as monotone surrogates for elastic stiffness. These results support external evaluation of the prespecified
H_{0}
representations while preserving the distinction between a mathematically defined geometric descriptor, an observed strength association, and a mechanical response to intervention.

Appendix A
Image construction and numerical representation

This appendix records implementation details that are not needed for the main mathematical development. Numerical choices are retained from the completed study, and later prediction runs are distinguished from the original benchmark.

A.1
Image coordinates and physical spacing

The volumes were unsigned 16-bit TIFF stacks with array axes ordered as slice, row, and column, denoted by
Z,Y,X
. Specimen spacing was read from TIFF and ImageJ metadata
[
23
]
. It was effectively isotropic within each scan. Transverse image widths were 6.740–6.978 mm and reconstructed
Z
extents were 10.400–11.906 mm. These reconstructed extents were not assumed to be the mechanical gauge lengths.

A.2
Region of interest

The interval along the
Z
-axis containing the specimen was first identified from the reconstructed image stack. For each axial slice
z
, we computed the population standard deviation of its voxel intensities,

s_{i}(z)=\operatorname{sd}_{y,x}I_{i}(z,y,x),

where
i
denotes the specimen. This gives the finite profile
(s_{i}(z))_{z=0}^{N_{z,i}-1}
along
Z
. Slices containing mostly exterior background generally have little intensity variation, whereas slices intersecting the specimen contain a mixture of bone, marrow, and structural boundaries and therefore tend to have larger values of
s_{i}(z)
.

Small fluctuations between adjacent slices were reduced using a centered moving median over seven slices. The window size was determined from a target physical width of
0.15
mm by dividing this width by the axial voxel spacing of each scan, rounding to the nearest number of slices, and increasing an even result to the next odd integer. This procedure gave a seven-slice window for every specimen.

For each specimen, a single Otsu threshold was estimated from the lower half of the smoothed axial profile, defined as the profile values no greater than that specimen’s median. This threshold was then applied to the entire smoothed profile. Slices with values above the threshold were marked as candidate specimen-bearing slices.

Short runs of slices classified as background but lying between specimen-bearing slices were filled when their physical length did not exceed
0.10
mm. This prevented isolated fluctuations in the axial profile from splitting an otherwise continuous specimen interval.

To exclude irregularities associated with the physical cut surfaces,
0.50
mm was removed from each detected end. An analysis interval with a nominal height of
8.50
mm was then centered within the remaining specimen. Since the boundaries of this interval had to coincide with voxel boundaries, its realized physical height ranged from
8.4822
to
8.5000
mm across specimens.

In the transverse plane, the ROI was a circle of radius 2.75 mm centered on the geometric center of the image. A voxel belonged to the resulting cylinder when the center of that voxel lay within the circle. Radii of 2.50, 2.75, and 3.00 mm were compared during the process. A radius of 2.75 mm was the largest of these candidates that remained within every usable core, and the same physical radius was therefore applied to all scans. Consequently, voxels outside the cylinder could be excluded from calculations rather than treated as marrow. The resulting cylindrical voxel set is denoted by
R_{i}\subset\Omega_{i}
.

A.3
Segmentation details

The smoothing and thresholding equations are given in Section
3.1
. Here, only implementation details not stated there are recorded. The Gaussian kernel had standard deviation
0.50
voxel and radius two voxels. Otsu’s threshold was computed from a 256-bin histogram of the smoothed intensities inside the ROI using scikit-image
[
24
,
25
]
. Voxels outside the ROI were stored as zero in the binary image, but the ROI mask continued to exclude them from the marrow phase.

Local Otsu alternatives were inspected during image construction, but the single threshold for each specimen was retained because it was spatially consistent and introduced no additional neighborhood parameter. No component filtering, hole filling, morphological operation, manual correction, or further ROI revision followed segmentation. Such operations would alter the topology being measured. The resulting BV/TV ranged from 0.121 to 0.431.

A.4
Morphometric conventions

BV/TV, Tb.Th, Tb.Sp, and Conn.D were calculated within the cylindrical ROI using BoneJ
[
12
,
2
]
. The conventional edge correction for a rectangular image stack was retained for connectivity. It corrects contributions at the image faces but does not explicitly account for the curved cylindrical wall, which is reported as a limitation. The five variables in
m_{i}
were fixed before strength modelling; additional BoneJ summaries were not screened against strength. Thickness and separation standard deviations were omitted to limit correlated predictors, and Euler characteristic was not added separately because it already enters connectivity density.

Degree of anisotropy was estimated by the mean intercept length method
[
4
]
. The calculation used 2048 directions, 64 lines per direction, a sampling increment of
\sqrt{3}
voxels, and five repetitions. To prevent voxels outside the cylinder from being interpreted as marrow, this calculation used the square prism inscribed in the cylindrical ROI over its full height. The mean degree of anisotropy across repetitions was used. The acute angle between the principal direction and the loading axis was retained only for a specified sensitivity analysis.

A.5

Cubical persistence was computed using GUDHI
[
26
,
13
,
27

The dimension-specific grid sizes and weight saturation constants are reported in Table

The saturation constants and grid choices were fixed using pooled baseline diagrams before outcome access. The recorded analysis reported agreement with the next finer grid; the numerical tolerance and complete physical grid edges are part of the fixed representation specification. Baseline domains, cell edges, and weights were reused for all counterfactuals. Bandwidths of 50, 75, and 100

D.1
Skeleton graph coordinates

Each binary bone mask was converted to a three-dimensional skeleton one voxel in width using Lee’s topology-preserving thinning. Skeleton voxels were classified as endpoints, slabs, or junction voxels from their degree in the 26-neighborhood. Connected junction voxels were grouped into junction vertices, and branches were traversed using the same stateful rules as BoneJ, beginning at endpoints and then proceeding through junctions. No cycle pruning was applied. Branch lengths were evaluated in physical units using the isotropic voxel spacing for each specimen; mean branch tortuosity was the branch length divided by the straight endpoint distance for branches with a positive endpoint distance.

Let
V_{i}
be the physical ROI volume in mm
3
,
b_{i}
be the number of graph branches,
j_{i}
be the number of junction clusters,
e_{i}
be the number of endpoint voxels,
q_{i}
be the graph cycle rank,
c_{i}
be the number of graph components, and
\bar{\ell}_{i}
be the mean branch length in
\mu
m. The eight skeleton predictors were

\left(\frac{b_{i}\bar{\ell}_{i}/1000}{V_{i}},\frac{b_{i}}{V_{i}},\frac{j_{i}}{V_{i}},\frac{e_{i}}{V_{i}},\frac{q_{i}}{V_{i}},\frac{c_{i}}{V_{i}},\bar{\ell}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}\right),

where
\bar{\tau}_{i}
is the mean branch tortuosity. The first coordinate is total graph branch length per ROI volume in mm/mm
3
. The next five are branch, junction cluster, endpoint, cycle, and graph-component densities per mm
3
. The spanning indicator was constant across all 24 specimens.

For a compressed graph with vertex set
V
, branch set
E
, and
c
components, the graph cycle rank is
|E|-|V|+c
. This graph quantity is distinguished from the homology of the full voxel filtration.

As a further extension of the validated centerline graph, five mechanically oriented descriptors were constructed without access to apparent strength, clinical group, or validation group labels. Let
G=(V,E)
be the compressed multigraph and let
L_{e}
be the physical length of branch
e

T_{0}
and
T_{1}
contained graph vertices with at least one constituent skeleton voxel within one voxel of the minimum or maximum occupied skeleton

H_{z}
is the physical separation between the minimum and maximum occupied skeleton slices, axial shortest-path tortuosity was

\tau_{z}=\frac{1}{H_{z}}\min_{P:T_{0}\leadsto T_{1}}\sum_{e\in P}L_{e}.

Axial edge connectivity
\lambda_{z}
was the maximum number of paths from
T_{0}
to
T_{1}
that shared no graph branches when every AnalyzeSkeleton branch had unit capacity and the artificial terminal connectors had capacity greater than the total number of branches.

To describe how much of the graph could participate in axial connection, the graph was augmented by auxiliary source and sink vertices and decomposed into biconnected blocks. Let

contain the branches assigned to blocks on the source-to-sink path of the resulting block-cut tree. The path participation length fraction was

C_{z}=\frac{\sum_{e\in E_{z}}L_{e}}{\sum_{e\in E}L_{e}}.

Self-loops and dangling blocks outside this path in the block-cut tree were not counted as participating. A separate graph transport surrogate assigned branch conductance
g_{e}=1/L_{e}
, fixed terminal potentials to one and zero, and solved Kirchhoff balance at all interior vertices. If
G_{z}
is the resulting total source current, normalized axial conductance was

\widetilde{G}_{z}=H_{z}G_{z}.

For branch current
I_{e}

d_{e}=I_{e}^{2}L_{e}

D_{z}=\frac{2\sum_{r=1}^{m}r\,d_{(r)}}{m\sum_{r=1}^{m}d_{(r)}}-\frac{m+1}{m},

where
d_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq d_{(m)}
. The conductance and dissipation quantities are graph transport surrogates; they are not finite element estimates and do not incorporate trabecular cross-sectional area or tissue constitutive properties.

The block

D.3
Plate and rod organization proxy

A later structural comparator was an open proxy for the structural organization index of Cox et al.
[
28
]
. The published SOI is defined from orientations of individual trabecular plates and rods obtained with ITS
[
29
]
; the present estimator does not reconstruct individual trabeculae and is therefore not described as ITS or as literal Cox SOI. Instead, the already validated topology-preserving skeleton was used only as sampling support. At each sampled skeleton voxel, a local diameter proxy was defined as twice the Euclidean distance transform value. This proxy based on center distance is not the maximal-sphere local thickness used by BoneJ. Foreground voxels within a sphere of radius 2.5 times the proxy were used to form a covariance matrix. If the square roots of its ordered eigenvalues were
a\leq b\leq c
, local shape was summarized by

E_{\mathrm{local}}=\frac{a}{b}-\frac{b}{c}.

Samples with
E_{\mathrm{local}}<-0.20
were classified as plate-like and represented by the shortest covariance axis, interpreted as the local plate normal. Samples with
E_{\mathrm{local}}>0.20
were classified as rod-like and represented by the longest covariance axis. Intermediate samples were not used in the orientation densities. The neighbourhood multiplier and classification threshold were fixed using engineered geometries only. Across 152 synthetic rod and plate cases spanning oblique orientations, thicknesses, subvoxel offsets, and surface perturbations, the largest recovered error in the unoriented axis was
0.171^{\circ}
, and all tested support points received the intended rod or plate class. The
4.5^{\circ}
orientation criterion used for this gate followed the engineered lattice validation reported during development of SOI
[
30
]
.

For the organization calculation, plate normal and rod axis directions were mapped to polar angle
\theta\in(-90^{\circ},90^{\circ})
and elevation
\phi\in(0^{\circ},90^{\circ})
. Separate reflected Gaussian kernel density estimates
f_{P}
and
f_{R}
were evaluated on a
0.5^{\circ}
grid with bandwidth
4.5^{\circ}
. Working in radians, the uniform density over this angular rectangle is

C_{u}=\frac{1}{\pi(\pi/2)}.

Let
V_{\max}=0.954077
denote the reference integral for a maximally concentrated interior orientation under the same kernel and grid. Plate organization was

pO=\frac{\iint\max(f_{P}-C_{u},0)\,d\theta\,d\phi}{V_{\max}}.

Rod organization was

rO=\frac{\iint\max(f_{R}-C_{u},0)\,d\theta\,d\phi}{V_{\max}}.

The plate–rod orientation overlap was

prO=1-\frac{1}{2}\iint|f_{P}-f_{R}|\,d\theta\,d\phi.

The resulting scalar was

\mathrm{SOI}_{\mathrm{proxy}}=pO\,rO\,prO.

The numerical implementation reproduced
C_{u}=0.202642
and the reference
V_{\max}
before human application.

Because local skeleton sampling can give long structures more observations than short structures, the human calculation included a separate sampling sensitivity analysis that did not use outcomes. For each specimen, let
t_{50}
be the median of this local diameter proxy over the previously defined skeleton. One representative skeleton voxel was retained per physical cube of side
t_{50}
, chosen as the voxel centre nearest the cube center, with deterministic tie breaking; this
1.0\,t_{50}
rule was fixed as the primary sampling scheme. Sensitivity schemes used cube sides
0.5\,t_{50}
,
1.5\,t_{50}
, and
2.0\,t_{50}
. All 24 specimens retained at least 25 plate and 25 rod samples under every scheme, the maximum range of the SOI proxy within a specimen across schemes was 0.0406, and the weakest cohort Spearman correlation between a sensitivity scheme and the primary scheme was 0.890. The primary
1.0\,t_{50}
SOI proxy was therefore retained as the only predictor from this branch;
pO
,
rO
,
prO
, and analogues using elevation alone were retained only for description.

The organization proxy used the same nested prediction and bootstrap procedure as the preceding comparators. Its primary addition was to morphometry; secondary additions were to DA, combined persistence images, or their combination with morphometry. The refit restricted to the FX cores retained the same definition. No orientation component, sampling density, neighborhood multiplier, or classification threshold was reselected after the strength outcomes were linked.

In these formulas
prO
measures overlap of angular densities on the specified rectangle. This numerical overlap is distinct from the physical angle between a plate normal and a rod axis at neighboring locations. Also, a uniform density in
(\theta,\phi)
coordinates is not a uniform distribution of axes with respect to spherical area. These conventions are part of the defined proxy; they should not be replaced silently by a different orientation statistic.

D.4
Local orientation variation

To test whether local spatial variation of trabecular orientation explained information retained by the other representations, a later analysis reused the plate normal and rod axis samples. This analysis was specified without using strength outcomes. For two unoriented axes
u
and
v
, same type misorientation was

\theta(u,v)=\arccos\!\left(|u^{\mathsf{T}}v|\right),

which is invariant to reversing either axis. For a plate normal
n
and rod axis
a

\delta_{\perp}(n,a)=\arcsin\!\left(|n^{\mathsf{T}}a|\right),

so that
\delta_{\perp}=0
when the rod lies in the plate plane. Neighbours were defined within a physical radius
2t_{50}

t_{50}
is the median local diameter proxy from the organization sampling analysis. Same-type disagreement was averaged first over neighbours at each retained location and then across locations. Plate–rod disagreement was calculated as the equal mean of plate centered and rod centered local means, preventing a class with more pair combinations from dominating by count alone.

The primary sampling grid was
1.0\,t_{50}

0.5\,t_{50}
and
1.5\,t_{50}

D.11
Interpretation of the structural comparators

The descriptor families ask different geometric questions. Skeleton counts describe a network after thinning; axial graph coordinates describe routes and transport on that network; the organization proxy aggregates orientation distributions; local misorientation retains neighborhood information; and
W_{1}^{0,2}
integrates surface normal directions. Their different constructions do not guarantee additional predictive information in a cohort of 13 groups.

The graph conductance model uses branch length and omits trabecular area, bending resistance, and tissue properties. The organization and local orientation estimators sample neighborhoods rather than reconstructing individual trabeculae by ITS. The surface tensor analysis retained normalized eigenvalue shape, excluding magnitude and principal-axis direction. The instability of
W_{2}^{0,2}
was determined before strength analysis. These restrictions specify the tested representations; the results should not be generalized to every graph, orientation, or tensor description of bone.

Representation overlap used Equation (
35
) and the upper triangle distance correlation described in the main text. These summaries quantify agreement between representations of the observed specimens; they do not identify a causal contribution to strength.

Let
n_{i}=|B_{i}|
. The common removal count was

r_{i}=\left\lfloor 0.02n_{i}+0.5\right\rfloor.

Let

be the centered 1.50-mm band, where

is the realized ROI height. The diffuse eligible set was

E_{i}^{D}=B_{i}\setminus P_{i}.

The localized eligible set was

E_{i}^{L}=(B_{i}\setminus P_{i})\cap\mathcal{Z}_{i}.

Surface targeting used
\delta_{i}(x)=d\!\left(x,R_{i}\setminus B_{i}\right)
, for
x\in B_{i}
, computed with physical spacing
h_{i}
. The exterior
\Omega_{i}\setminus R_{i}
was not included in the marrow set. Eligible voxels were ordered lexicographically by

\bigl(\delta_{i}(x),\,\eta_{i}(x),\,j_{i}(x)\bigr),

where
\eta_{i}(x)
is a deterministic hash derived from the coded specimen identifier and random seed, and
j_{i}(x)
is the linear voxel index used only as a final tie breaker. The hash term removes the directional bias that results from resolving equal Euclidean distances by array order.

Let
\operatorname{first}_{r_{i}}(E;\prec_{i})
denote the first
r_{i}
voxels of
E
under this order. Define the diffuse removal set by

U_{i}^{D}=\operatorname{first}_{r_{i}}(E_{i}^{D};\prec_{i}).

Define the localized removal set by

U_{i}^{L}=\operatorname{first}_{r_{i}}(E_{i}^{L};\prec_{i}).

The corresponding diffuse structure is
B_{i}^{D}=B_{i}\setminus U_{i}^{D}
, and the localized structure is
B_{i}^{L}=B_{i}\setminus U_{i}^{L}
. Hence,
|B_{i}^{D}|=|B_{i}^{L}|=|B_{i}|-r_{i}
and therefore

\mathrm{BV/TV}(B_{i}^{D};R_{i})=\mathrm{BV/TV}(B_{i}^{L};R_{i})

for every specimen. The 2% removal fraction, 1.50-mm band, 0.50-mm end protection, and deterministic tie breaking rule were established before morphometric, topological, or mechanical outcomes of the complete perturbation set were examined.

Each generated pair was accepted only if both counterfactuals were subsets of the baseline structure, diffuse and localized thinning removed exactly
r_{i}
voxels, no voxel was removed from the protected end regions, every localized removal lay inside the central band, and the remaining bone contained a six-connected component spanning the loading direction. The fraction of retained bone in the largest connected component was also recorded as a structural quality measure.

D_{k,i}^{X}
for the finite persistence diagram in dimension

E.3
Elastic discretization and numerical checks

For each
X\in\{B,D,L\}
, the binary structure was coarsened by a factor of two in each spatial direction. Each
2\times 2\times 2
block of native image voxels was represented by one finite element, whose local bone volume fraction was taken as the fraction of its native voxels classified as bone. No majority threshold was applied. Thus, for a coarse element
e
containing the native voxel block
Q_{e}
,

\rho_{e,i}^{X}=\frac{1}{|Q_{e}|}\sum_{x\in Q_{e}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}^{X}}(x),

with
0\leq\rho_{e,i}^{X}\leq 1
and
|Q_{e}|=8
for an ordinary factor 2 cell. A singleton axial layer contains four native voxels per cell. A value of
0
denotes an element containing no bone, a value of
1
denotes an element completely occupied by bone, and intermediate values preserve the fractional bone content of the corresponding native voxel block.

Empty transverse padding could be removed only when both the ROI and bone masks were empty on the complete outer plane. The loading direction was never cropped. For the three specimens with an odd number of fine
Z
slices, all slices were retained by using paired two-slice coarse layers except for one interior singleton layer nearest the specimen center; the finite element layer height was therefore
2h_{i}
for paired layers and
h_{i}
for the singleton.

Each active coarse cell was a standard eight-node trilinear hexahedral element evaluated with full
2\times 2\times 2
Gauss integration, rather than reduced integration, following the general voxel finite element approach used for trabecular microstructures
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
. The reference solid phase was taken as isotropic and linearly elastic with Young’s modulus
E_{s}=2000
MPa and Poisson ratio
\nu=0.25
. Fractional occupancy entered linearly through

K_{e,i}^{X}=\rho_{e,i}^{X}K_{s,e},

where
K_{s,e}
is the stiffness of a fully occupied cell with the same physical dimensions, including any residual axial layer. The value of
E_{s}
sets the absolute modulus scale of this linear model: multiplying the common solid phase modulus by a constant multiplies
E_{i}^{B}
,
E_{i}^{D}
, and
E_{i}^{L}
by the same constant and therefore cancels from the dimensionless relative contrasts defined below. The primary interpretation consequently concerns relative contrasts within each specimen, not the absolute apparent modulus or a measured tissue modulus.

Compression was applied along
Z
with nominal strain
\varepsilon=0.001
. The bottom loading surface had zero axial displacement, while the top loading surface received the corresponding prescribed compression. Three lateral degrees of freedom on two bottom-surface nodes were constrained to remove rigid-body motion, while the remaining lateral degrees of freedom were left unconstrained.

Let
F_{\mathrm{top},i}^{X}
and
F_{\mathrm{bottom},i}^{X}
denote the axial reactions on the two loading surfaces. The compressive force used to calculate apparent stress was the mean magnitude of these reactions,

F_{i}^{X}=\frac{|F_{\mathrm{top},i}^{X}|+|F_{\mathrm{bottom},i}^{X}|}{2}.

If
A_{i}
is the cross-sectional area of the original digital cylindrical ROI, the apparent stress was

\sigma_{i}^{X}=\frac{F_{i}^{X}}{A_{i}}.

The simulated
apparent Young’s modulus
was then

E_{i}^{X}=\frac{\sigma_{i}^{X}}{\varepsilon}.

Here,
\varepsilon
was calculated from the imposed platen displacement divided by the full ROI gauge length
H_{i}
. Thus,
E_{i}^{X}
is a simulated small-strain apparent Young’s modulus of the coarse grained linear-elastic structure and is distinct from the experimentally measured apparent compressive strength used in the observational prediction analysis.

The linear system was solved by matrix-free conjugate gradients with scalar Jacobi preconditioning, a primary relative tolerance of
10^{-6}
, and at most 20,000 iterations. The Jacobi preconditioner rescales the system by its stiffness diagonal to improve convergence without changing the underlying finite element problem. Acceptance additionally required an independently recomputed relative residual at most
2\times 10^{-6}
, relative top to bottom reaction imbalance at most
10^{-4}
, and relative energy inconsistency at most
2\times 10^{-5}
. If an otherwise unchanged problem failed any of the conditions, the relative tolerance
5\times 10^{-7}
was allowed.

A second coarse scale sensitivity was specified before the factor 3 mechanical results were inspected. The same binary baseline, diffuse, and localized inputs were block averaged at factor 3 and solved with the same material law and boundary conditions. Transverse dimensions were made divisible by three only by removing completely empty outer padding planes. The loading direction was never cropped or padded. When the number of fine
Z
slices was not divisible by three, triplet layers were combined with one deterministic interior residual layer of one or two fine slices nearest the specimen center, and its physical height was passed explicitly to the finite element operator. The factor 3 contrasts were analyzed with the same 13-group bootstrap and were treated only as a resolution sensitivity.

E.4
Group comparison and sensitivity summaries

The diffuse relative change was

R_{D,i}=\frac{E_{i}^{D}-E_{i}^{B}}{E_{i}^{B}}.

The localized relative change was

R_{L,i}=\frac{E_{i}^{L}-E_{i}^{B}}{E_{i}^{B}}.

The primary comparison of spatial support was

C_{i}=R_{L,i}-R_{D,i}=\frac{E_{i}^{L}-E_{i}^{D}}{E_{i}^{B}}.

The inequality
C_{i}<0
means that localized thinning produces the larger stiffness reduction at exactly the same global bone loss. The primary point estimate gave equal weight to each of the 13 validation groups. Contrasts were first averaged over specimens within each group, and the 13 group means were then averaged. Uncertainty was estimated from 200,000 nonparametric cluster bootstrap replicates obtained by resampling the 13 groups with replacement while retaining all cores within a sampled group. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles formed the reported 95% confidence interval. A cluster bootstrap weighted by the number of cores was evaluated as a sensitivity analysis. As a secondary inferential diagnostic, all
2^{13}
sign flips of the 13 group means of
C
were enumerated. Under this diagnostic null hypothesis, positive and negative signs of each group effect are symmetric, so every possible sign allocation is considered equally.

w_{r}^{\mathrm{SD}}=\frac{\bar{a}_{r}^{\mathrm{SD}}}{\left(\sum_{q\in\mathcal{R}}(\bar{a}_{q}^{\mathrm{SD}})^{2}\right)^{1/2}}

and the
regional score
by

S_{\mathrm{SD}}(p)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}w_{r}^{\mathrm{SD}}p_{r}.

(39)

The score therefore preserves the signed multivariate pattern within the prespecified region; it is not simply the unweighted persistence image mass in that region.

Among all pairs satisfying the four coordinate morphometric criterion, the selected pair maximized

|S_{\mathrm{SD}}(p_{A})-S_{\mathrm{SD}}(p_{B})|.

Ties were resolved first by the smaller Euclidean norm of the four standardized morphometric differences and then by candidate identifier. The member with the larger score was designated
H_{0}
-high and the other
H_{0}
-low. The pair identities, source image checksums, regional scores, and selection rule were written and hashed before any finite element analysis was performed. The prespecified transfer hypothesis was that, if the positive signed distance pattern observed in the human data has a corresponding ordering of elastic stiffness, the
H_{0}
-high member would retain the larger modeled mechanical competence.

The selected pair was evaluated with the same factor 2 fractional-occupancy finite element formulation, material law, boundary conditions, loading, solver, and numerical acceptance gates described above. A factor 3 calculation was then performed as a resolution sensitivity using the same factor 3 construction already specified for the main counterfactual experiment. No pair reselection, morphometric rematching,
H_{0}
reweighting, or mechanical retuning was allowed between the two resolutions. For
q\in\{2,3\}
, the mechanical comparison was

\Delta E^{(q)}=E_{\mathrm{high}}^{(q)}-E_{\mathrm{low}}^{(q)}.

A positive value would support the prespecified stiffness hypothesis.

The Integral Chow Rings of the Moduli Stacks of Hyperelliptic Prym Pairs I
Alessio Cela, Alberto Landi

Abstract
This paper is the first in a series dedicated to computing the integral Chow rings of the moduli stacks of Prym pairs. In this work, we compute the Chow ring for Prym pairs arising from a single pair of Weierstrass points and from at most ( g − 1 ) / 2 (g-1)/2 pairs when the genus g g of the curve is odd.

Introduction

Prym pairs have been extensively studied due to their deep connections with the theory of abelian varieties, curves, and admissible covers
[
Mum74
,
FR70
,
Bea77a
,
Bea77b
,
DS81
]
. Roughly speaking, Prym pairs correspond to étale double covers of a smooth, projective curve, or equivalently, to non-trivial line bundles on the base curve that are torsion of order 2 in the Picard group.

The study of the Chow rings of the moduli stack of stable genus
g
curves,
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, began with Mumford
[
Mum83
]
and quickly attracted significant attention. Subsequent work, including
[
Fab90
,
Iza95
,
PV15
,
CL23
]
, has computed these Chow rings up to genus 9.

On the other hand, the integral Chow ring of these moduli stacks are much less understood. Edidin and Graham introduced in
[
EG98
]
the intersection theory of global quotient stacks with integer coefficients. It is a more refined invariant but as expected, the computations for the
Chow ring with integral coefficients of the moduli stack of curves are much harder than the ones
with rational coefficients. Vistoli
[
Vis98
]
computed the Chow ring for
\mathcal{M}_{2}
, and only more recently has E. Larson
[
Lar21
]
, and then Di Lorenzo and Vistoli
[
DLV21
]
, computed the Chow ring of the compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
. The Chow ring of
\mathcal{M}_{3}
is currently known with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[1/6]

[
Per23
]
.

In contrast, the integral Chow ring of the stack
\mathcal{H}_{g}
of hyperelliptic curves is fully known
[
EF09
,
FV11
,
DL18
]
, while in the pointed case, it has been partially computed
[
Per22
,
Lan23
,
Lan24
]
. Similarly, the Chow rings of hyperelliptic curves with Weierstrass sections are also known
[
EH22
]
.

Finally, the Chow ring of Prym pairs in genus 2 was computed in
[
CIL24
]
. In this paper, we extend their work by computing the Chow ring for Prym pairs arising from a single pair of Weierstrass points, as well as for those arising from at most
(g-1)/2
pairs when the genus
g
is odd. More specifically, the moduli stack
\mathcal{RH}_{g}
of hyperelliptic Prym pairs of genus
g
is a disjoint union of moduli stacks
\mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n}
, indexed by
n\in{1,\ldots,\lfloor(g+1)/2\rfloor}
. In this work, we focus on the cases
n=1
for any
g
, and for odd
g
, we address the case where
n\leq(g-1)/2
.

3.1
Recovering
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-equivariant classes from
\mathrm{T}
-equivariant computations

Recall that a linear group
G
is called special if every
G
-torsor is Zariski-locally trivial. The most common examples are tori and
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
for every
n
. One key feature is that the
G
-equivariant Chow ring of a smooth

-algebraic space

is a direct summand (as a
\mathrm{CH}_{G}^{*}(X)
-module) of the maximal torus equivariant Chow ring of

(see
[
EF09
, Proposition 2.2]
). The most basic case is the Chow ring of the classifying stack of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, where the maximal torus
T\cong\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
consists of diagonal matrices, and inclusion is equivalent to inclusion of the ring of symmetric functions
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
into the polynomial ring in the
n

t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
. We write an explicit basis for
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(B\mathrm{T})
as a
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(B\mathrm{GL}_{n})
-module.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

. The ring
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(B\mathrm{T})=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
is a free
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(B\mathrm{GL}_{n})=\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}]
-module of rank
n!
. A base consists of monomials

\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{d_{i}}\text{ with }0\leq d_{i}\leq n-i\text{ for every }1\leq i\leq n.

Proof.

The freeness and rank part is well known, see for instance
[
Dem73
, Theorem 6.2]
; therefore, it is enough to show that the elements in the statement generate
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(BT)
, as their number matches the rank. Also the basis is already know, but we give a proof here as we have not found a suitable reference; usually, the basis is given in terms of Schubert polynomials and over a field. Consider the sequence of extensions of rings

\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}]\subset\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}][t_{1}]\subset\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}][t_{1},t_{2}]\subset\ldots\subset\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}][t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}].

For every

and

, denote by

the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree
r
in the

, with

for every

. The identity

shows that
\mathbb{Z}[c_{1}^{(n-m)},\ldots,c_{n-m}^{(n-m)}]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}][t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]
for

. In particular, the monic polynomial
\prod_{i=m+1}^{n}(x-t_{i})
with root

has coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}][t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]
and

\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}][t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]

.

Consider a point
(q,f,g)\in(\mathbb{P}(V_{a})\times_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(V_{b}))\setminus\Delta^{\prime}
with a stabilizer containing the subgroup
G^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{3}
consisting of triples
(\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1)
whose coordinates multiply to
1
. The group
G^{\prime}
can be viewed as a subgroup of
\mu_{2}^{\times 3}
, isomorphic to
\mu_{2}^{\times 2}
.

For example, let the conic
q
be given by
x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}
and take
f
and
g
of the form

f=q^{a/2}+f_{1}(x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2}),\quad g=q^{b/2}+g_{1}(x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2}),

where
f_{1}
and
g_{1}
are general polynomials of degree
a/2
and
b/2
, respectively. Then, the intersection of the zero locus of
fg
with
q
is smooth, and the coefficients of
f
and
g
relative to
x_{0}^{a}
and
x_{0}^{b}
are non-zero, respectively.
Then, the stabilizer of
(q,f,g)
is exactly the group
G^{\prime}
described above. This induces the monomorphism of the residual gerbe

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{BG^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\frac{(\mathbb{P}(V_{a})\times_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(V_{b}))\setminus\Delta^{\prime}}{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{3}}\right]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

which in turn defines a pullback map (see
[
MRV06
, Lemma 2.4]
for the Chow ring of
\mathrm{BG}
)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{3}}^{*}((\mathbb{P}(V_{a})\times_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(V_{b}))\setminus\Delta^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathrm{BG^{\prime}})\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}]}{(2\mu_{0},2\mu_{1},2\mu_{2},\mu_{0}+\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{*}}

Since the composite of
\iota
with the projection to
\mathrm{B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{3}}
is the map induced by the inclusion

, it is clear that
\iota^{*}
sends
\lambda_{i}
to
\mu_{i}
. Moreover, the immersion
\iota
factors through the immersion of the open subscheme where the coefficient of the conic
q
relative to
x_{2}^{2}
is non-zero and the coefficients of the polynomial
f
and
g
relative to
x_{0}^{a}
and
x_{0}^{b}
respectively are also non-zero. On such a locus,
\xi_{2a}
and
\xi_{2b}
restrict to
at_{1}
and

, respectively; and, since
a
and
b
are even, it follows that they restrict to
0
on
\mathrm{BG^{\prime}}
. From this discussion, we get that the restriction of
\alpha
in Equation (
41
) to
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathrm{BG^{\prime}})
is equal to the restriction of
p(c_{2},c_{3})c_{3}
. However, that class is non-zero in
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathrm{BG^{\prime}})
unless
p=0
, as
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{3})\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{*}(BG^{\prime})
has kernel contained in the ideal generated by
2
, while
p
has coefficients 0 and 1. It follows that
p=0
, and we are done.
∎

Theorems
1.17
and
1.18
are now immediate.

Proof of Theorem
1.17
.

We show that
P(a,b,r)
is satisfied by induction on
(1,1,1)\preceq(a,b,r)
with the lexicographic ordering and
r\leq 2a\leq 2b
. Notice that in this setting
I_{2}
contains also the polynomials
p_{a}(\xi_{2a})
and
p_{b}(\xi_{2a})
by Lemma
5.11
. The base case is with
a=1
, where it is obvious. Therefore, it is enough to prove the inductive step with
a>1
; from now on assume that
P(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},r^{\prime})
holds for every
(1,1,1)\preceq(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},r^{\prime})\prec(a,b,r)
with
a^{\prime}\leq b^{\prime}
. By Lemma
5.14
we may assume that
r\geq 4
and that it is a power of 2, while by Lemma
5.15
it is enough to show that
{M_{r}}_{*}(1)\in I_{2}
. If
a
or
b
are odd, then Lemma
5.17
concludes, while if they are both even then
P(a,b,r)
is satisfied by Lemma
5.19
. This shows that
P(a,b,r)
is satisfied for every
r
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.18
.

This follows from Theorem
1.17
, Lemma
1.15
and the formula for the Chow rings of root stacks in Proposition
3.5
.

∎

5.3
Geometric interpretation of the generators when

is odd

We saw in Theorem

and Remark

that
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n})
for odd

is generated by the classes
c_{2},c_{3},t,\xi_{2n},\xi_{2g+2-2n}
. In this section, we provide an interpretation of these classes as Chern classes of certain natural vector bundles on
\mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n}
.

With reference to the diagram in Lemma

, all these classes, except for
\xi_{2n}
and
\xi_{2g+2-2n}
, are pulled back from
\mathcal{H}_{g}
, where their interpretations are already known. For the reader’s convenience, we recall them here.

The class

is the pullback of the first Chern class of the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{g}
, while the classes
c_{i}
are the Chern classes of the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{g}
. Both
\mathcal{L}_{g}
and
\mathcal{E}_{g}
are the bundles introduced in §

. We refer to
[
FV11
, Theorem 7.2]
or to the discussion after
[
DL18
, Theorem 7.2]
for the proof.

The classes
\xi_{2n}
and
\xi_{2g+2-2n}
are instead pulled-back from
\mathcal{D}_{2n}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2g+2-2n}
respectively.
For all

, let

be the line bundle on
\mathcal{D}_{2m}
defined by
\pi_{*}\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes m}(\mathcal{F}_{2m})
where

are the universal divisor and the universal Brauer Severi variety of relative dimension

over

.

.

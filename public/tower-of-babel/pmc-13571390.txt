AI-Enabled ECG Mapping to Guide Leadless Atrial Pacemaker Implantation in the Bachmann's Bundle Region

Leadless pacing has expanded bradycardia treatment options in patients for whom transvenous pacemaker implantation is undesirable or technically challenging. Interest in physiologic atrial pacing has also grown, particularly at sites that promote more synchronous biatrial activation, potentially reducing atrial arrhythmias1,2 and improving left ventricular diastolic function.3 Bachmann's bundle, a specialized interatrial conduction pathway responsible for initiating left atrial activation during sinus rhythm, has therefore emerged as an attractive target for pacing strategies intended to better approximate normal atrial depolarization.4

Translating Bachmann's bundle pacing to atrial leadless systems remains technically challenging. Unlike conventional transvenous atrial leads, a leadless atrial pacemaker must be deployed along the relatively smooth superior interatrial septum with limited tactile feedback and constrained maneuverability of the delivery system. Direct confirmation of Bachmann's bundle capture is also difficult, with reported success rates of only approximately 55%.5 Although recent dual-chamber leadless pacing technology has enabled atrial leadless implantation in selected patients,6 literature regarding site-specific targeting of the Bachmann's bundle region remains limited.5,7,8

Artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled electrocardiogram (ECG) mapping provides insights into the virtual site of earliest myocardial activation.9 A previous study has demonstrated that a noninvasive mapping process based on forward, 3-dimensional computational models of electrophysiology (vMap, Vektor Medical) can accurately localize arrhythmia activation sites to facilitate targeted therapies such as catheter ablation.9 In this case report, we demonstrate a case in which AI-enabled ECG mapping during atrial leadless pacemaker implantation characterized physiologic activation patterns elicited by pacing at different sites to facilitate atrial leadless pacemaker positioning at Bachmann's bundle. To our knowledge, this is the first reported case of implantation of an atrial leadless pacemaker in the Bachmann's bundle region guided by an AI-enabled ECG mapping.

Case Summary

An 86-year-old woman with history of sick sinus syndrome, atrial tachycardia, and frequent premature atrial contractions was referred for permanent pacing because of symptomatic tachycardia-bradycardia syndrome. She initially presented for dual-chamber pacemaker implantation, but preprocedural evaluation revealed bilateral subclavian chronic venous occlusion, likely related to previous breast cancer treatment and/or previous central venous catheterization. Given her increased risk of developing atrial fibrillation related to a high atrial ectopy burden10 and expected high proportion of atrial pacing, the decision was made to proceed with implantation of an atrial leadless pacemaker (Aveir AR, Abbott Laboratories), guided by intracardiac echocardiography (ICE) and an AI-enabled ECG mapping system (vMap, Vektor Medical), targeting the superior interatrial septum near the expected Bachmann's bundle insertion.

After informed consent was obtained, the patient was brought to the electrophysiology laboratory in the fasting state. The procedure was performed under general anesthesia. Venous accesses were obtained from the bilateral common femoral veins using ultrasound guidance; a 25-F sheath was placed via right femoral vein for pacemaker delivery. Via left femoral vein access, a pigtail catheter was advanced into the right atrial appendage, and contrast injection delineated the right atrial anatomy. A quadripolar catheter was then positioned in the right atrium to serve as an atrial reference and to assess the correlation between pacing location and vMap findings, followed by placement of an ICE catheter to guide implantation.

The atrial leadless pacemaker was then prepared and advanced through the implantation sheath. After communication with the programmer/interrogation system was confirmed, the pacemaker was advanced within the protective sheath and brought to an initial candidate target site under fluoroscopic and ICE guidance.

Test pacing was performed at the initial candidate site using AAI at 90 beats/min, 5 V at 0.4 ms (Figure 1A). ICE imaging demonstrated that the atrial leadless pacemaker was positioned at the superior arcuate ridge (Figure 1B). The device electrogram at this site showed adequate sensing and injury current (Figure 1C). The paced P wave was analyzed by the AI-enabled ECG mapping system. This first paced map suggested a posterior-superior right atrial breakout pattern rather than a septal target near Bachmann's bundle (Figure 1D).

Based on this information, the implanting physician repositioned the device more anteriorly on the superior right atrial septum, close to the anticipated Bachmann's bundle region.

Pacing was repeated at the revised target site using AAI at 90 beats/min, 5 V at 0.4 ms (Figure 2A) with a paced stimulus-to-P-wave-end of 121 ms compared with sinus P-wave duration of 138 ms (Figure 3). The ICE image at this site showed the device position in the anticipated Bachmann's bundle area, adjacent to the ascending aorta (Figure 2B). Device interrogation showed satisfactory pacing parameters before fixation, including sensed atrial amplitude of 0.9 mV, pacing impedance of 240 Ω, and capture threshold of 0.5 V at 0.4 ms. On repeat AI-enabled ECG mapping, the site of earliest paced atrial activation shifted to the anterior-superior left atrium, a pattern interpreted as supportive of pacing in the Bachmann's bundle region (Figure 2D).

After satisfactory position and stability were confirmed, the device was fixed with 1.5 clockwise turns. Final acute device parameters demonstrated sensed atrial amplitude of 0.7 mV, pacing impedance of 260 Ω, and capture threshold of 1.25 V at 0.4 ms. There was no immediate procedural complication. The patient tolerated the procedure well and was transferred in stable condition for routine postprocedural monitoring.

Acute results and follow-up

The primary procedural endpoint was successful implantation of an atrial leadless pacemaker at a superior septal right atrial site with AI-enabled ECG mapping findings supportive of pacing in the Bachmann's bundle region. This endpoint was achieved efficiently, requiring only a single device deployment. Acute implant parameters were acceptable, and there was no immediate pericardial effusion, device embolization, vascular complication, or hemodynamic instability. On postprocedure day 30, the patient reported feeling well with no major complaint. Device interrogation demonstrating a sensed atrial amplitude of 1.1 mV, pacing impedance of 350 Ω, and a capture threshold of 0.75 V at 0.4 ms. The atrial pacing burden was 47% with AAIR programming (60-120 beats/min), and the estimated battery longevity was 9.1 years.

Novelty of the Submission

This case demonstrates several novel aspects of physiologic atrial leadless pacing. First, it establishes the feasibility of atrial leadless pacemaker implantation at a superior septal right atrial site in a patient in whom a conventional transvenous approach was limited by bilateral subclavian venous occlusion. Second, it suggests that AI-enabled ECG mapping may provide actionable intraprocedural information, especially when a physiologic atrial pacing target is desired. Third, it provides a proof-of-concept demonstration that AI mapping-guided repositioning can distinguish a posterior-superior right atrial site from a more anterior superior septal site whose activation pattern is more consistent with pacing in the Bachmann's bundle region.

Future Directions

The physiologic rationale for Bachmann's-bundle-region pacing is supported by its role as the principal preferential interatrial conduction pathway and previous studies suggesting improved biatrial synchrony and improved atrial arrhythmia burden with pacing in this region.4,5 However, atrial leadless pacemaker implantation at the superior interatrial septum presents unique technical challenges,5,7,8 including a relatively smooth endocardial surface, limited maneuverability of the large delivery system, and proximity to adjacent structures such as the ascending aorta. These considerations highlight the potential value of multimodality intraprocedural guidance, combining intracardiac echocardiography with electroanatomic or AI-enabled ECG mapping, to improve procedural precision and safety.

More broadly, this case raises the possibility that atrial leadless pacing may evolve beyond a uniform implantation strategy toward individualized site selection based on patient anatomy and electrophysiologic goals. The superior interatrial septal region may represent a feasible target in selected patients, particularly those at increased risk of atrial arrhythmias1 or left ventricular diastolic dysfunction.3 Whether Bachmann's-bundle-region pacing improves atrial synchrony, symptoms, atrial arrhythmia burden, or long-term clinical outcomes compared with conventional atrial leadless implant locations remains unknown and warrants systematic investigation.

Conclusions

In an 86-year-old woman with sinus node dysfunction and bilateral subclavian venous occlusion, real-time AI-enabled ECG mapping-guided implantation of an atrial leadless pacemaker at a superior septal right atrial site with findings supportive of pacing in the Bachmann's bundle region was feasible. Further experience is needed to define the reproducibility, safety, and clinical impact of Bachmann's bundle-region targeting, including guidance by AI-enabled ECG mapping, during atrial leadless pacing.

Self-expandable metal stents as a bridge to surgery for obstructive colorectal cancer: a propensity score-matched retrospective cohort study
Authors: Jian-jun Chen, Zhu-lin Li, Yong Yang, Zhen-jun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeAcute colorectal obstruction constitutes a surgical emergency in patients with colorectal cancer (CRC) and is associated with high morbidity and mortality when managed with emergency surgery. Although self-expandable metal stents (SEMS) may optimize surgical conditions, concerns remain regarding their long-term oncologic safety.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included 355 patients with obstructive CRC treated between 2014 and 2023, including 65 patients in the SEMS group and 290 in the surgery-alone group. Perioperative outcomes, postoperative complications, disease-free survival (DFS), and overall survival (OS) were compared. Propensity score matching and multivariate Cox regression were applied to reduce selection bias and adjust for confounding factors.ResultsPatients in the SEMS group had a markedly higher rate of laparoscopic surgery (89.2% vs. 62.8%, p < 0.001) as well as a significantly shorter postoperative hospital stay (11.05 ± 5.25 vs. 14.29 ± 11.86 days, p = 0.032). No significant differences were observed in overall postoperative complications, DFS, or OS between the two groups after adjustment. Although a higher crude recurrence rate, particularly intra-abdominal recurrence, was observed in the SEMS group, SEMS placement was not an independent predictor of adverse oncologic outcomes.ConclusionSEMS as a bridge to surgery was associated with improved perioperative outcomes and showed no statistically significant adverse impact on long-term survival after adjustment for confounding factors. Further prospective studies are required to confirm oncologic safety in selected patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s00384-026-05207-6.Keywords: Obstructive colorectal cancer, Self-expandable metal stents, Bridge to surgery, Oncological prognosis, Short-term surgical outcomes

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) ranks among the most common malignant tumors globally. Both the incidence and mortality of CRC have been steadily increasing in China [1]. Although its incidence has declined with the implementation of population-based screening programs, colonic obstruction remains a common and severe complication, accounting for 54.5% of all CRC patients presenting to the emergency department [2]. Emergency surgery in this setting is associated with increased postoperative complications, higher mortality, and a greater likelihood of permanent stoma formation due to suboptimal preoperative preparation and poor physiological status.

This technique was first documented as a bridge to surgery (BTS) approach back in 1994 [3]. SEMS placement enables sufficient time for patients to optimize nutritional status, complete adequate bowel preparation, and subsequently undergo elective radical resection, thereby reducing emergency surgery-associated risks. In 2020, the European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) recommended SEMS as a bridge-to-surgery for left-sided obstructive colorectal cancer [4].

In addition, most available studies are limited by small sample sizes and imbalance in baseline characteristics between treatment groups. Therefore, high-quality real-world evidence with appropriate adjustment for confounding factors is still needed.

Therefore, the present single-center retrospective cohort study aimed to evaluate both the perioperative benefits and long-term oncologic outcomes of SEMS as a bridge-to-surgery strategy in patients with obstructive CRC. We specifically compared perioperative surgical outcomes, postoperative complications, DFS, and OS between patients undergoing SEMS placement and those receiving direct emergency or elective surgery, using propensity score matching and multivariate Cox regression to minimize selection bias (Figs. 1 and 2).

Materials and methods

Study design and patients

This retrospective cohort study was conducted on 2579 CRC patients who underwent surgical treatment at the Department of General Surgery, Beijing Chaoyang Hospital from 2014 to 2023. Of these patients, 355 patients presented with complete or incomplete intestinal obstruction at initial diagnosis, with preoperative clinical manifestations of obstipation, abdominal distension, and abdominal pain. Colonoscopy showed a tumor-related stenosis that prevented endoscope passage.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) histologically confirmed colorectal adenocarcinoma; (2) presence of complete or incomplete intestinal obstruction caused by CRC; (3) underwent curative-intent surgical resection;

Exclusion criteria: (1) stent-related colonic perforation requiring emergency surgery after SEMS placement; (2) patients with unresectable tumors or undergoing palliative simple colostomy; (3) patients unable to undergo CRC surgery due to severe comorbidities.

Treatment protocols

Patients in the SEMS group underwent self-expandable metal stent placement under colonoscopic and fluoroscopic guidance by experienced gastroenterologists. A guidewire-assisted technique was used for stent deployment across the obstructing lesion. Successful decompression was defined as the presence of clinical symptom relief (passage of flatus or stool within 24 h), radiological confirmation of stent expansion, and absence of procedure-related perforation. Following decompression, patients received supportive care including antibiotic therapy, intravenous nutritional support, acid suppression, and gradual transition to enteral feeding. Elective radical resection was performed after stabilization of the general condition and resolution of obstructive symptoms.

Surgery-alone group: Patients in the surgery-alone group received conventional preoperative management, including fasting, intravenous fluid resuscitation, gastrointestinal decompression when indicated, and bowel preparation using enemas. Emergency or elective surgery was performed depending on clinical status. For patients with synchronous liver metastases, simultaneous hepatectomy or radiofrequency ablation was performed when feasible.

Data collection

Based on whether they underwent preoperative SEMS intervention prior to radical resection, patients were stratified into the SEMS group and the surgery-alone group. The clinical characteristics collected encompassed age, gender, preoperative comorbidities, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification, tumor location, surgical approach (open vs. laparoscopic), stoma formation, tumor differentiation, TNM stage, postoperative hospital stay, body mass index (BMI), preoperative peripheral blood septin9 methylation (mSEPT9), and postoperative complications.

Postoperative follow-up

All patients who underwent radical resection for colorectal cancer (CRC) were advised to undergo routine laboratory tests and engage in appropriate physical activity to facilitate postoperative recovery. Follow-up was recommended every 3 months for the first 3 years after surgery and every 6 months for the subsequent 2 years. The last follow-up date was December 31, 2025.

Statistical analysis

Categorical data were analyzed using Pearson’s chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test, with outcomes expressed as absolute numbers and percentages. For the comparison of continuous variables, the independent samples t-test was adopted. 1:1 nearest-neighbor PSM was performed with a caliper of 0.02, matching for tumor location, tumor differentiation. Covariate balance before and after propensity score matching was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMDs), and the results were visualized using a Love plot. An SMD < 0.1 was considered indicative of acceptable balance. Survival outcomes were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to identify independent prognostic factors for disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS). Variables with p < 0.10 in univariate analysis, along with clinically relevant covariates, were entered into multivariate models. Hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The statistical validity of the study was verified by a professional biomedical statistician.

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Beijing Chaoyang Hospital Affiliated to Capital Medical University (No. 2025ke-323), and the requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective design.

Results

Baseline clinical characteristics

A total of 2579 patients with colorectal cancer underwent surgical treatment during the study period, among whom 355 patients with acute colorectal obstruction met the inclusion criteria and were enrolled in the final analysis. During the study period, 66 patients underwent SEMS placement. One patient developed stent-related perforation requiring emergency diverting colostomy and was excluded according to the predefined exclusion criteria. Therefore, 65 patients who successfully underwent SEMS placement followed by curative-intent surgery were included in the final SEMS cohort, while 290 patients underwent direct surgery without SEMS placement (Table 1). Before adjustment, significant differences were observed in tumor location and tumor differentiation between the two groups, with a higher proportion of left-sided tumors in the SEMS group. No significant differences were identified in age, sex, BMI, ASA classification, comorbidities, or TNM stage between groups. In patients with available circulating biomarker data, a lower proportion of positive mSEPT9 was observed after SEMS placement; however, mSEPT9 analysis was exploratory due to limited sample size and should be interpreted cautiously.

Perioperative outcomes

The SEMS group had a notably higher rate of laparoscopic surgical procedures relative to the surgery-alone group (89.2% vs. 62.8%, p < 0.001), and postoperative hospital stays were markedly shorter in the SEMS group (11.05 ± 5.25 days vs. 14.29 ± 11.86 days, p = 0.032). A statistically significant difference was observed in tumor differentiation between the two cohorts (p = 0.010), with the surgery-alone group having a notably higher proportion of patients with poorly differentiated tumors. No significant differences were found in TNM stage, perineural invasion, vascular invasion, MMR status (d-MMR vs. p-MMR), emergency operation rate, enterostomy rate, or overall postoperative complication rate between the two groups (Table 2).

To mitigate selection bias arising from significant baseline imbalances in tumor location and differentiation, 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) was performed using these two variables. After matching, 65 patients in the SEMS group were matched with 61 patients in the surgery-alone group. The post-matching baseline characteristics and standardized mean differences are presented in Supplementary Table 1, demonstrating acceptable balance between groups.

In the matched cohort (65 vs 61 patients), a total of 33 patients developed postoperative complications, with an overall incidence of 26.2% (Table 3). The most common complications included wound infection, anastomotic leakage, and pulmonary infection. There were no statistically significant differences in individual complication types between the two groups. The distribution of Clavien–Dindo grades showed no significant difference between groups, indicating comparable severity of postoperative morbidity. A total of two perioperative deaths occurred, all of which were in the surgery-alone group; the causes included pulmonary infection, and acute myocardial infarction.

Long-term follow-up outcomes

The mean follow-up duration was 37.33 ± 20.24 months. A higher crude recurrence rate was observed in the SEMS group compared with the surgery-alone group (p = 0.049). Subgroup analysis showed a significantly higher incidence of intra-abdominal recurrence in the SEMS group, whereas no significant differences were observed in liver, lung, lymph node, or systemic recurrence (Table 4).

Pattern of tumor recurrence

Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were conducted to identify potential risk factors affecting DFS and OS (Tables 5, 6). In univariate Cox regression analysis, SEMS placement, TNM stage, perineural invasion, vascular invasion, and tumor differentiation were associated with DFS. However, in multivariate analysis, only TNM stage and perineural invasion remained independent prognostic factors for DFS. SEMS placement was not independently associated with DFS after adjustment for confounding variables. For OS, univariate analysis identified age ≥ 70 years, Charlson comorbidity index (CCI), and TNM stage as potential prognostic factors. In multivariate analysis, only age ≥ 70 years remained an independent predictor of OS. SEMS placement was not associated with OS in either univariate or multivariate models.

Discussion

This retrospective cohort study analyzed 355 patients with obstructive CRC. Our results indicated that tumors were predominantly located in the left colon among those who received preoperative SEMS placement. The left colon has a higher risk of intestinal obstruction due to its anatomical characteristics of a narrower lumen, and SEMS placement is technically more feasible in this location. Significant baseline imbalances in primary tumor location and histological differentiation were identified between the two raw cohorts. To mitigate confounding bias driven by these prognostic variables, 1:1 nearest-neighbor PSM was performed based on tumor location and differentiation; no intergroup disparities remained after matching.

One of the novel findings of this study is that the positive rate of peripheral blood mSEPT9 was lower in the SEMS group following stent placement. However, this finding should be interpreted cautiously. Only a limited proportion of patients (14/65 in the SEMS group) had available mSEPT9 data, and no longitudinal serial sampling was performed in a controlled manner. Therefore, this observation is exploratory and hypothesis generating rather than confirmatory. mSEPT9 is a liquid biopsy marker with relatively high specificity for CRC, and mSEPT9 has moderate diagnostic value for CRC [5]. Several studies have reported that DNA methylation of multiple genes is associated with tumorigenesis in CRC, and some of these genes can serve as potential screening biomarkers [6,7]. Among these biomarkers, mSEPT9 is regarded as a promising indicator for CRC detection [8]. The short-term dynamic changes may be influenced by multiple factors, including relief of obstruction, inflammatory status, and sampling variability. Whether SEMS placement has any direct biological impact on tumor behavior cannot be inferred from the current dataset, further prospective studies with standardized serial biomarker measurements are required to validate this observation.

In terms of short-term postoperative outcomes, the results showed that SEMS placement relieved obstructive symptoms, allowing more patients to undergo laparoscopic surgery and leading to a certain reduction in the emergency surgery rate. SEMS placement enables patients to receive more adequate preoperative evaluation and preparation, thereby shortening the postoperative hospital stay, which is consistent with the core advantages of SEMS as a BTS for obstructive CRC [9–11]. The CRC patients who received SEMS treatment exhibited a superior short-term prognosis was observed in numerous studies. Hiyoshi Y et al. reported that SEMS placement followed by laparoscopic surgery in patients with obstructive colorectal cancer may enhance primary anastomosis rates and reduce severe complications [12]. Staged surgical resection with diversion via stoma or SEMS followed by radical resection appears to be safe in obstructive left-sided colon cancer, with increased lymph node retrieval and a reduced number of patients requiring permanent stoma [13]. Importantly, overall postoperative complication rates and Clavien–Dindo severity distribution were comparable between groups, supporting the perioperative safety of SEMS as a bridge strategy.

Notably, our long-term survival analysis yielded a novel finding inconsistent with several prior reports. Univariate Cox regression identified SEMS placement as a risk factor for impaired DFS, and crude intergroup comparison demonstrated a higher overall tumor recurrence rate in the SEMS cohort (p = 0.049). Site-specific recurrence analysis further revealed that intra-abdominal peritoneal recurrence occurred significantly more frequently among patients treated with preoperative stenting, while distant metastasis to the liver, lung, and other organs showed no statistical difference between groups. Some prior studies suggesting that SEMS placement may elevate the risk of postoperative tumor recurrence, especially peritoneal metastasis [14–16]; the underlying mechanisms include stent compression of tumor tissue leading to increased peritoneal dissemination or distant metastasis of tumor cells [17–19].

Currently, controversy remains concerning the influence of SEMS on patients’ long-term prognosis. Several studies have indicated that SEMS used as a BTS might exert an adverse effect on long-term oncological outcomes [14,15, 20], while recent evidence indicates comparable OS between SEMS-bridged and direct surgery approaches [21–25]. Our results suggested after adjusting for key prognostic confounders including TNM stage, perineural invasion, vascular invasion, and tumor differentiation via multivariate Cox regression, SEMS placement was no longer an independent adverse predictor of DFS, and no significant association between stenting and OS was detected. This discrepancy between univariate and multivariate results underscores the critical impact of baseline selection bias on crude outcome comparisons. Some studies showed that SEMS do not affect long-term follow-up outcomes of obstructive CRC patients [26,27].

During the last decade, with the continuous development of interventional endoscopic technology, some researchers have developed new stent technologies and materials to further improve the success rate and safety of SEMS placement. For example, Wada K. et al. developed a new type of stent that has a good effect in the treatment of lesions near the anal verge [28]. Sasaki T. et al. reported that implantation of a low-axial-force SEMS is safe and not associated with perforation, achieved favorable short-term outcomes in the management of obstructive colorectal cancer [29]. In addition, numerous studies are exploring the impact of neoadjuvant chemotherapy after SEMS placement on the prognosis of patients with CRC [30–32].

This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective single-center design introduces inherent selection bias, despite the use of propensity score matching and multivariable adjustment. Second, the relatively small sample size in the SEMS group and incomplete biomarker data may have limited the robustness of subgroup analyses. Third, although perioperative oncologic treatments, including chemotherapy and radiotherapy, may influence long-term outcomes, detailed radiotherapy information was incomplete and the number of rectal cancer patients was limited in our cohort. Therefore, radiotherapy was not included as an independent covariate in the multivariable Cox regression models, which may have resulted in residual confounding. Finally, quality-of-life outcomes and standardized biomarker dynamics were not assessed.

Conclusion

SEMS as a bridge-to-surgery strategy in patients with obstructive colorectal cancer was associated with improved perioperative outcomes, including a higher rate of minimally invasive surgery and shorter postoperative hospitalization. After adjustment for baseline differences using propensity score matching and multivariable Cox regression, no statistically significant differences in disease-free survival or overall survival were observed between patients treated with SEMS and those undergoing direct surgery. However, a numerically higher recurrence rate, particularly intra-abdominal recurrence, was observed in the SEMS group, suggesting a potential signal that warrants further investigation. These findings indicate that SEMS may be safely applied in carefully selected patients, although prospective studies with larger cohorts and standardized protocols are needed to validate long-term oncologic safety.

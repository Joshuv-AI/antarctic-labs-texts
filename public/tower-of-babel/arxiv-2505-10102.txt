Nonlocal Generalized Aw-Rascle-Zhang model: well-posedness and singular limit
Elio Marconi, Laura V. Spinolo

Abstract




We discuss a nonlocal version of the Generalized Aw-Rascle-Zhang model, a second-order vehicular traffic model where the empty road velocity is a Lagrangian marker governed by a transport equation. The evolution of the car density is described by a continuity equation where the drivers’ velocity depends on both the empty road velocity and the convolution of the car density with an anisotropic kernel. We establish existence and uniqueness results. When the convolution kernel is replaced by a Dirac Delta, the nonlocal model formally boils down to the classical (local) Generalized Aw-Rascle-Zhang model, which consists of a conservation law coupled with a transport equation. In the case of exponential kernels, we establish convergence in the nonlocal-to-local limit by proving an Oleinik-type estimate for the convolution term. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first nonlocal-to-local limit result for a system of two non-decoupling equations with a nonlocal flux function.

Keywords:
Aw-Rascle-Zhang model, nonlocal-to-local limit, nonlocal conservation laws, singular local limit, traffic models.

Introduction

We consider the system

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}[V(\xi,u)\rho]=0\\
\partial_{t}u+V(\xi,u)\partial_{x}u=0,\\
\end{array}\right.\quad\xi(t,x)=\int_{x}^{+\infty}\rho(t,y)\eta(x-y)dy.

In the above expression, the unknowns are
\rho:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and

, whereas the velocity field

and the the convolution kernel

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}[V(\rho,u)\rho]=0\\

In both (

Proof of Lemma

We have

\begin{split}\frac{dQ_{n}}{dt}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:icsenne}}}{{\leq}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|V(\xi_{n+1},u_{n})(t,X_{n+1})-V(\xi_{n},u_{n-1})(t,X_{n})|\rho_{0}(x)dx\\

and

In the previous expression, we have used the characterization of bounded total variation functions through different quotients. To control

3.

To simplify the notation, in the proof of Proposition

\xi_{\varepsilon}
,

Owing to the stability result provided by Corollary

\rho_{0}

Assume that we have proved that for some suitable constants

1.20
),

\varepsilon
small enough. This concludes the proof of Proposition

.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

As pointed out at the beginning of the proof of Proposition
3.1
, the differential inequality (
3.4
) implies
m^{\prime}(t)\geq cm^{2}(t)
for a suitable constant
c>0
as soon as
m(t)<-a
for some positive constant
a>0
.
If the initial datum
\rho_{0}
satisfies the one-sided Lipschitz condition

\rho_{0}(y)-\rho_{0}(x)\geq m_{0}[y-x]\qquad\text{for every $x<y$ and for some $m_{0}<0$}

then we have

\xi(t,y)-\xi(t,x)\geq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left[-a+\frac{1}{\nicefrac{{1}}{{m_{0}}}-ct}\right]}(y-x)\qquad\text{for every $x<y$ and $t>0$}.

The above estimate is better than (
3.1
), since the one-side Lipschitz bound does not blow-up as

.

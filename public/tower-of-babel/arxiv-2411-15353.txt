On spectral sequences for semiabelian varieties over non-closed fields
Alexander Petrov, Alexei Skorobogatov

Abstract
We give a new, short proof of the formula for the first potentially non-zero differential of the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence for semiabelian varieties over non-closed fields. We show that this differential is non-zero for the Jacobian of a curve when the image of the torsor of theta-characteristics under the Bockstein map is non-zero. An explicit example is a curve of genus 2 whose Albanese torsor is not divisible by 2. When the Albanese torsor is trivial, we show that the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence for the Jacobian degenerates at the second page. We give a formula for the differential of the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence for a torus which computes its Brauer group. Finally, we describe the differentials of the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence for a smooth projective curve, generalising a lemma of Suslin.

Introduction

For an algebraic variety
X
over a field
k
with separable closure
k_{\rm s}

and a prime
\ell
not divisible by
{\rm char}(k)
, the action of the absolute Galois group
\Gamma={\rm{Gal}}(k_{{\rm s}}/k)
on the étale cohomology groups
{\rm H}^{i}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell})
contains arithmetic information about the variety
X
, such as the number of rational points in the case
when
k
is a finite field.

The action of the Galois group
\Gamma
on individual étale cohomology groups comes from its action on the étale cohomology complex
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell})
. The phenomenon that we study in this paper is that the quasi-isomorphism class of this complex in general contains more information than individual cohomology Galois modules.

Remarkably, for a smooth projective variety
X
over
k
, by Deligne’s decomposition theorem
[
Del68
, Proposition 2.4]
, the rational étale cohomology complex
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
is quasi-isomorphic to the direct sum
\bigoplus\limits_{i\geq 0}{\rm H}^{i}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})[-i]
of individual cohomology groups as an object of the derived category of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-vector spaces equipped with an
action of
\Gamma
.

This is no longer true for cohomology with integral coefficients, and is witnessed by the fact that the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}={\rm H}^{p}(k,{\rm H}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}^{q}(X_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}))\Rightarrow{\rm H}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}^{p+q}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell})

(1)

in general has non-zero differentials.
This is the case when
\ell=2
,
k={\mathbb{R}}
,
X({\mathbb{R}})=\emptyset
, as follows from work of
Artin–Verdier
[
AV64
, Corollary 2.6 (b)]
, see also
[
Cox79
, §2]
.
If
X
is a smooth projective real curve with no real points and
\ell=2
, then
a differential on the 2nd (respectively, 3rd) page is non-zero if the genus
of
X
is odd (respectively, even), see Examples
6.2
and
6.7
.

Our first main result is that (
1
) may fail to degenerate even for abelian varieties. We denote by
D(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
the derived category of the abelian category of discrete
{\mathbb{Z}}/n
-modules with a continuous action of
\Gamma={\rm{Gal}}(k_{{\rm s}}/k)
.

Theorem 1

(Theorem
3.5
)
.

There exists a principally polarised abelian surface
A
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
is not quasi-isomorphic to

\bigoplus\limits_{i\geq 0}{\rm H}^{i}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/2)[-i]
in the derived category

D(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
, where

\Gamma={\rm{Gal}}({\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
. Specifically, the differential

\delta_{2}^{0,2}\colon{\rm H}^{0}({\mathbb{Q}},{\rm H}^{2}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/2))\to{\rm H}^{2}({\mathbb{Q}},{\rm H}^{1}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/2))

on the second page of the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence for
A
is non-zero.

By Theorem
3
below, the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence degenerates
for any abelian variety
isomorphic to a direct factor of a product of Jacobians of curves each with a rational
divisor class of degree 1. In particular, the abelian surface
A
from Theorem
1
is an example of an abelian variety not of this type.

Let us sketch the proof of Theorem
1
. Let
C
be a smooth proper curve of genus
g
over
k

and let
J
be the Jacobian of
C
. We identify
J
with the dual abelian variety
using the canonical principal polarisation.
Using a general description of
\delta_{2}^{0,2}
given in
Theorem
2
below, we show that the
mod 2 first Chern class of the canonical principal polarisation is
an element of
{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(J_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{\Gamma}
which
is sent by
\delta_{2}^{0,2}
to the image of the class
of the torsor of theta-characteristics under the Bockstein map

{\rm H}^{1}(k,J[2])\to{\rm H}^{2}(k,J[2])
. Thus
\delta_{2}^{0,2}\neq 0
whenever

[{\bf Pic}^{g-1}_{C/k}]\in{\rm H}^{1}(k,J)
is not divisible by 2.
A systematic method to construct such curves over number fields was found by B. Creutz
[
Cre13
]
.
The idea is to exploit the fine arithmetic structure available in this case,
namely, that
[{\bf Pic}^{g-1}_{C/k}]
is automatically a 2-torsion element
of the Tate–Shafarevich group
\textcyr{Sh}(J)
.
The class
[{\bf Pic}^{g-1}_{C/k}]
is divisible by 2 in
{\rm H}^{1}(k,J)
if and only if its image
in
\textcyr{Sh}^{2}(k,J[2])
under the Bockstein map is zero, which is equivalent to being
orthogonal to
\textcyr{Sh}^{1}(k,J[2])
under the non-degenerate Poitou–Tate duality pairing.
By compatibility of pairings, this happens if and only if

[{\bf Pic}^{g-1}_{C/k}]
is orthogonal to the image of
\textcyr{Sh}^{1}(k,J[2])
in
\textcyr{Sh}(J)

under the Cassels–Tate pairing.

We have two strategies to construct an element of
\textcyr{Sh}^{1}(k,J[2])
whose image in
\textcyr{Sh}(J)

pairs non-trivially with
[{\bf Pic}^{g-1}_{C/k}]
.

The first strategy, following Manin, interprets the Cassels–Tate pairing
in terms of the Brauer–Manin obstruction on the everywhere locally soluble variety

X={\bf Pic}^{g-1}_{C/k}
: any
t\in\textcyr{Sh}(J)
gives rise to a Brauer class in

{\rm{Br}}(X)
whose Brauer–Manin pairing
with an arbitrary adelic point of
X
equals the Cassels–Tate pairing of
t
and
[X]
.
When
g=2
, the variety
X
is the Albanese torsor of
C
, so
C
is a closed subvariety of
X
.
If
C
itself is everywhere locally soluble, we can take an adelic point of
X
contained in

C
and then we only need to know the restriction of our Brauer class to
C
.
The easiest way to arrange for this is to consider hyperelliptic curves
C
given by
the equation
y^{2}=f(x)
, where the polynomial
f(x)\in k[x]
of degree 6 is itself
everywhere locally soluble.
In this setting, one can construct elements of
{\rm{Br}}(C)
coming from

\textcyr{Sh}^{1}(k,J[2])\subset{\rm H}^{1}(k,J[2])
by an explicit formula,
which allows one to compute the Brauer–Manin pairing with the adelic points of
f(x)=0
.
For a worked-out example
borrowed from Creutz
[
Cre13
, p. 941]
and Creutz–Viray
[
CV15
, Theorem 6.7]

see Theorem
3.5
(a).

The second strategy is based on the approach of Poonen and Stoll
[
PS99
]
.
The class
[{\bf Pic}^{g-1}_{C/k}]

comes from the torsor of theta-characteristics in
{\rm H}^{1}(k,J[2])
, so if this torsor is
everywhere locally trivial and the curve
C
is odd, which means that the Cassels–Tate
pairing of
[{\bf Pic}^{g-1}_{C/k}]
with itself is non-zero, then
we are done. For a worked-out example see Theorem
3.5
(b).

Let us now give a general description of the first potentially non-trivial extension in the étale cohomology complex of a semiabelian variety over an arbitrary field
k
(
{\rm char}(k)\neq 2
).
It is easy to see that all differentials on the
i
-th page of (
1
)
are zero for
\ell>i
, see Corollary
1.3
. For the study of the differentials
on the second page we can thus assume that
\ell=2
.
For a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-module
M
we denote by
Q(M)
the module of quadratic functions on

M^{\vee}={\rm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
with values in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. It fits into the exact sequence

0\to M\to Q(M)\to M^{\otimes 2}\to\wedge^{2}M\to 0,

(2)

where the middle map sends a quadratic function
f:M^{\vee}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
to the bilinear form

f(x+y)-f(x)-f(y)\in{\rm{Hom}}(M^{\vee}\otimes M^{\vee},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\cong M^{\otimes 2}.

The sequence (
2
) is natural in
M
, so if
M
is equipped with an action of
\Gamma
then (
2
) becomes an exact sequence of
\Gamma
-modules.

Theorem 2

(Theorem
1.6
)
.

Let
A
be a semiabelian variety over a field
k

of characteristic not equal to
2
. The class in
{\rm Ext}^{2}_{\Gamma}({\rm H}^{2}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}),{\rm H}^{1}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}))
corresponding to
\tau^{[1,2]}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is equal to the class of the extension

(\ref{intro: quadratic ext2})
for
M={\rm H}^{1}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
.

In the case of abelian varieties this was proved in
[
P
]
, but here we give a new shorter proof exhibiting an explicit quasi-isomorphism from the 2-term complex
Q(M)\to M^{\otimes 2}
to the
truncated bar complex of the Tate module
T_{2}(A)
. Theorem
2
, in particular, gives a description of the differentials

{\rm H}^{i}(k,{\rm H}^{2}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}))\to{\rm H}^{i+2}(k,{\rm H}^{1}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}))

of (
1
), as well as the analogous differentials in the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence with
{\mathbb{Z}}/2^{n}
-coefficients, for any
n
, see also Theorem
1.13
.

Theorem
1
shows that the class described in Theorem
2
is not always zero for abelian varieties, but at the moment we do not know if it is ever non-zero for tori, cf. Remark
4.2
.

In contrast, for the Jacobians of curves with a rational divisor class of degree
1

the étale cohomology complex decomposes in all degrees. We give a proof of this result, which may
be well-known to the experts, but does not seem to be available in the literature.

Theorem 3

(Theorem
2.1
)
.

Let
C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}
be geometrically
connected smooth proper curves over
k
each admitting a
k
-rational divisor class of degree
1
,
for
i=1,\ldots,m
. Let
A
be a direct factor of the product
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{Jac}(C_{i})
of Jacobians of
C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}
.
Let
n\geq 1
be an integer not divisible by
{\rm char}(k)
.
Then
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
is quasi-isomorphic to
\bigoplus\limits_{i\geq 0}{\rm H}^{i}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)[-i]
in
D(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
. In particular, the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence

E_{2}^{i,j}={\rm H}^{i}(k,{\rm H}^{j}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n))\Rightarrow{\rm H}^{i+j}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)

degenerates at the second page.

Another class of examples of non-zero differentials in the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence comes from varieties without a rational point. If a geometrically connected
k
-variety
X
has a
k
-point, then
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell})
is quasi-isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}\oplus\tau^{\geq 1}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell})
in the derived category of
\Gamma
-modules.
This is no longer true for varieties without a rational point, and we calculate the exact obstruction to splitting off
{\rm H}^{0}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell})
from
\tau^{\leq 1}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell})
when
X
is a torsor for a semiabelian variety.
We denote by
\mathcal{A}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)

the abelian category of discrete
{\mathbb{Z}}/n
-modules equipped with a continuous action of
\Gamma
.

Theorem 4

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
X
be a torsor for a semiabelian variety
A
over a field
k
.
Let
n\geq 1
be an integer not divisible by
{\rm char}\,k
.
Then the class in

{\rm Ext}^{2}_{\mathcal{A}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)}({\rm H}^{1}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n),{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\cong{\rm Ext}^{2}_{\mathcal{A}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)}({\rm H}^{1}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n),{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\cong{\rm H}^{2}(k,A[n])

\tau^{[0,1]}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
is equal to the image of the class of

in
{\rm H}^{1}(k,A)
under the Bockstein homomorphism
{\rm H}^{1}(k,A)\to{\rm H}^{2}(k,A[n])
.

As we point out in Remark
5.2
, in the setting of Theorem
4
, the obstruction to the existence of a rational

-cycle of degree

coming from
\tau^{[0,1]}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})

We give several complements of the above results, summarised below.

is surjective when

,

. Demeio’s proof is based on an elaborate analysis of differentials

.

In Proposition

Semiabelian varieties

Let
A
be a semiabelian variety over

, that is, an extension of an abelian variety by a torus.
Let
\ell
be a prime number,
\ell\neq{\rm char}(k)
. For all

,

{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{n})\simeq\wedge^{i}{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{n})

We would like to study the differentials

(3)

Remark 1.1
.

The origin of the group law on

gives a section of the structure morphism

{\rm H}^{r}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(k,{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{n})\to{\rm H}^{r}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A,{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{n})

are injective for all

. Therefore, we have
\delta_{i}^{p,i-1}=0
for all

and all

.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
i\geq 2
,
p\geq 0
,
q\geq i-1
be integers, and let
\ell
be a prime. When

\Box

(a)
Suppose

.

\ell^{\min\{q-i+1,n+1\}}\delta^{p,q}_{i}=0

(b)

{\rm H}^{0}(f)=0

Now suppose that

is zero on

where the last map is

where
C
has bad reduction,
and also for
p=2
, the polynomial
f(x)
has a root in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
In view of Lemma
3.2
and Remark
3.3

it remains to show that
C
is odd. By
[
PS99
, Corollary 12]
this happens if and only if
the number of places
v
such that
C
does not have a
{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
-rational divisor of degree 1, is odd.
Such places are necessarily real or places of bad reduction, so we only need to consider the real place.
We check that
C({\mathbb{R}})=\emptyset
, so
C
has no real divisor of degree 1, and hence is odd.

\Box

4
The Brauer group of a torus

Let
T
be a torus over a field
k
of characteristic exponent
p
.
Let
\widehat{T}={\rm{Hom}}_{k_{\rm s}-{\rm gps}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
be the
\Gamma
-module of characters of
T
.
The Brauer group
{\rm{Br}}(T)={\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
is computed by the spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}={\rm H}^{p}(k,{\rm H}^{q}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m}))\Rightarrow{\rm H}^{p+q}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(T,{\mathbb{G}}_{m}).

(33)

We denote by
d^{p,q}_{r}
the differential on the
r
th page emanating from the
(p,q)
-entry.

Since
T_{k_{\rm s}}
is a dense open subscheme of

{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{k_{\rm s}}
, we have
{\rm H}^{1}(T_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\cong{\rm{Pic}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})=0
.
Thus
d^{0,2}_{2}=0
, and the first interesting differential
is
d^{\,0,2}_{3}\colon{\rm{Br}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})^{\Gamma}\to{\rm H}^{3}(k,k_{\rm s}[T]^{\times})
.

The origin
e\in T(k)

of the group law on
T
gives a section of the structure morphism
T\to{\rm{Spec}}(k)
,
hence
{\rm H}^{r}(k,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\to{\rm H}^{r}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
is injective for all
r\geq 0
.
Likewise, the natural map
{\rm{Br}}(k)\to{\rm{Br}}(T)
is injective.
Let
{\rm{Br}}_{e}(T)={\rm{Ker}}[{\rm{Br}}(T)\to{\rm{Br}}(k)]
be the kernel of specialisation at
e
.

We have an isomorphism of
\Gamma
-modules

{\rm H}^{0}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})=k_{\rm s}[T]^{\times}\cong k_{\rm s}^{\times}\oplus\widehat{T}
(cf.
[
Ros57
, Proposition 3]
).
The spectral sequence (
33
) thus gives rise to an exact sequence

0\to{\rm H}^{2}(k,\widehat{T})\to{\rm{Br}}_{e}(T)\to{\rm{Br}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})^{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{\bar{d}_{3}^{\,0,2}}{\rm H}^{3}(k,\widehat{T}).

(34)

To compute
{\rm{Br}}(T)
one needs to describe the map

\bar{d}_{3}^{\,0,2}\colon{\rm{Br}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})^{\Gamma}\to{\rm H}^{3}(k,\widehat{T})
, which is the composition of the differential
d_{3}^{0,2}
with the map induced by the projection
k_{{\rm s}}[T]^{\times}\to\widehat{T}
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number not equal to
p
. For an abelian group
A
we write
A(p^{\prime})

for the subgroup of
A
consisting of the elements of finite order not divisible by
p
.

Since
{\rm{Pic}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})=0
, the Kummer sequence gives rise to an isomorphism

\kappa\colon{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},\mu_{\ell^{n}})\tilde{\longrightarrow}{\rm{Br}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})[\ell^{n}].

Using the isomorphism of
\Gamma
-modules

{\rm H}^{0}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\cong k_{\rm s}^{\times}\oplus\widehat{T}
,
we also deduce from the Kummer sequence a natural isomorphism

\widehat{T}/\ell^{n}\tilde{\longrightarrow}{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},\mu_{\ell^{n}}).

We note that
{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{n})\cong\wedge^{2}{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{n})

and thus the multiplication by
m
map
[m]\colon T\to T
acts on
{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},\mu_{\ell^{n}})
,
and hence on
{\rm{Br}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})(p^{\prime})
, as
m^{2}
. On the other hand,
[m]
acts on
\widehat{T}

as
m
. Taking
m=-1
we see that
2\bar{d}_{3}^{\,0,2}=0
, so that
\bar{d}_{3}^{\,0,2}

sends the elements of
{\rm{Br}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})(p^{\prime})
of odd order to zero.
Then it follows from (
34
) that every element of odd order in

{\rm{Br}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})(p^{\prime})^{\Gamma}
lifts to
{\rm{Br}}(T)
.

The question of an explicit description of the map
\bar{d}_{3}^{\,0,2}

on the 2-primary torsion subgroup of
{\rm{Br}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})^{\Gamma}

was asked at the top of
[
CTS21
, p. 220]
. We now give such a description.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
T
be a torus over a field
k
of characteristic different from
2
. Let
\bar{d}^{\,0,2}_{3}
be the composition

{\rm{Br}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})^{\Gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d^{\,0,2}_{3}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm H}^{3}(k,k_{\rm s}[T]^{\times})\to{\rm H}^{3}(k,\widehat{T}),

where the last map is induced by the projection

k_{\rm s}[T]^{\times}\cong k_{\rm s}^{\times}\times\widehat{T}\to\widehat{T}
.
The restriction of
\bar{d}^{\,0,2}_{3}
to
the
2^{n}
-torsion subgroup is the composition

{\rm{Br}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})[2^{n}]^{\Gamma}\xrightarrow[\simeq]{\kappa^{-1}}\wedge^{2}(\widehat{T}/2^{n})(-1)^{\Gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm Bock}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm H}^{1}(k,\wedge^{2}(\widehat{T}/2))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha[1]}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm H}^{2}(k,\widehat{T}/2)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm Bock}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm H}^{3}(k,\widehat{T}).

Proof.
We will show that the restriction of
d_{3}^{0,2}
to the
2^{n}
-torsion subgroup can be read off from the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence for the cohomology of
T
with coefficients in
\mu_{2^{n}}
. The natural map
\tau^{\leq 2}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},\mu_{2^{n}})\to\tau^{\leq 2}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
of truncations in degrees
\leq 2
induces a commutative diagram relating the extensions between
{\rm H}^{2}
and
\tau^{\leq 1}
in these complexes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm H}^{2}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},\mu_{2^{n}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau^{\leq 1}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},\mu_{2^{n}})[3]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm H}^{2}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm H}^{0}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})[3]}

(35)

where we used that
{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})={\rm{Pic}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}})
vanishes. The differential
d_{3}^{0,2}
is obtained by applying the functor
{\rm H}^{0}(k,-)
to the bottom horizontal map in the diagram.

Applying the functor of Galois invariants to the left vertical map we get the inclusion of the
2^{n}
-torsion

\wedge^{2}(\widehat{T}/2^{n})(-1)^{\Gamma}\cong{\rm H}^{2}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},\mu_{2^{n}})
into the Galois invariants in the Brauer group of
T_{k_{{\rm s}}}
, so we are looking to compute the result of applying

{\rm H}^{0}(k,-)
to the counter-clockwise composition in (
35
), composed with the projection
{\rm H}^{3}(k,{\rm H}^{0}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m}))\to{\rm H}^{3}(k,\widehat{T})
.

From the Kummer sequence, we see that
\tau^{\leq 1}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},\mu_{2^{n}})
can be represented by the two-term complex
{\rm H}^{0}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\xrightarrow{2^{n}}{\rm H}^{0}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
with the right vertical arrow in (
35
) given by shift by
[3]
of the projection onto the
0
th term of this complex.
This map followed by the projection to
\widehat{T}
sends
{\rm H}^{0}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},\mu_{2^{n}})
to zero, so the composition factors through
\tau^{[1,1]}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},\mu_{2^{n}})
.
Applying
{\rm H}^{0}(k,-)
to this composition, we obtain a map that factors as follows:

{\rm H}^{0}(k,\tau^{\leq 1}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},\mu_{2^{n}})[3])\to{\rm H}^{2}(k,{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},\mu_{2^{n}}))\cong{\rm H}^{2}(k,\widehat{T}/2^{n})\xrightarrow{-\mathrm{Bock}_{\widehat{T}}}{\rm H}^{3}(k,\widehat{T}).

Therefore the clockwise composition in (
35
) evaluated on
{\rm H}^{0}(k,-)
, composed with the projection onto
{\rm H}^{3}(k,\widehat{T})
, is given by

{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},\mu_{2^{n}})^{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{\delta_{2}^{0,2}}{\rm H}^{2}(k,{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{{\rm s}}},\mu_{2^{n}}))\xrightarrow{-\mathrm{Bock}_{\widehat{T}}}{\rm H}^{3}(k,\widehat{T}),

where
\delta^{0,2}_{2}
is a differential of the spectral sequence

E^{p,q}_{2}={\rm H}^{p}(k,{\rm H}^{q}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},\mu_{2^{n}}))\Rightarrow{\rm H}^{p+q}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T,\mu_{2^{n}}).

By Theorem
1.13
,
\delta^{0,2}_{2}
is obtained by applying
-\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}^{L}\mu_{2^{n}}
to
the terms of the 2-extension given by
the difference of the maps (
23
) and (
24
) with

M={\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(T_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
. The composition of two Bockstein maps is zero,
so only (
24
) contributes to
\bar{d}_{3}^{\,0,2}
, thus proving that
\bar{d}_{3}^{\,0,2}

is the composition of the four maps in the theorem.
\Box

Remark 4.2
.

It would be interesting to construct a torus with a non-zero map

, or
prove that none exist.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero.
If

is a quasi-trivial torus, or if

is a local or global field,
then the natural map
{\rm{Br}}(T)\to{\rm{Br}}(T_{k_{\rm s}})^{\Gamma}
is surjective.

Proof.
Let us show that
\bar{d}^{\,0,2}_{3}=0
.
The case of quasi-trivial torus is immediate
from Theorem
4.1
and Lemma
1.11
(i).
If
k
is a
p
-adic field, then
{\rm H}^{3}(k,\widehat{T})=0
since

has strict cohomological dimension
2
[
Har20
, Theorem 10.6]
, so there is nothing to prove.
The case

follows from
Theorem
4.1
and Lemma
1.11
(ii).

, where the product is over the real places of

,

, so this case follows from the case of local fields.

5
Torsors

Let
X
be a
k
-torsor for a semiabelian variety
A
.
In this section we address the problem of computing the étale cohomology
groups
{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
, where
n
is not divisible by
{\rm char}(k)
.

Translations by elements of
A(k_{\rm s})
act trivially on the groups

{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
, for
i\geq 0
. Let us explain this.
The choice of a
k_{\rm s}
-point in
X

gives an isomorphism
X_{k_{\rm s}}\simeq A_{k_{\rm s}}
.
By the canonical isomorphism
{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\cong\wedge^{i}{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)

it suffices to prove the triviality of action on
{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
.
Let
p_{1},p_{2},\mu:A\times A\to A
be the two projections and the multiplication map,
respectively. We need to show that for any
a\in A(k_{\rm s})
the composition

{\rm H}^{1}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\xrightarrow{\mu^{*}}{\rm H}^{1}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{\rm s}}\times A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\xrightarrow{(a\times{\rm Id}_{A})^{*}}{\rm H}^{1}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)

is the identity map. Using that
{\rm H}^{1}(k_{\rm s},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)=0
, we see that it suffices to prove that

\mu^{*}=p_{1}^{*}+p_{2}^{*}
, but this follows from the canonical isomorphism

{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\cong{\rm{Hom}}(\pi_{1}^{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}}),{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\cong{\rm{Hom}}(A[n],{\mathbb{Z}}/n),

(36)

see (
12
).

Therefore, any choice of a trivialisation
X_{k_{{\rm s}}}\simeq A_{k_{{\rm s}}}

of the torsor
X
over
k_{{\rm s}}
gives rise to the same
\Gamma
-equivariant isomorphism

{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\cong{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n),\quad i\geq 0.

But the complexes
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
and
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
need not be isomorphic as objects of
D(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
: the truncation of the latter in degrees
[0,1]
is the direct sum of its cohomology groups, but the truncation
\tau^{[0,1]}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
need not be split, as we demonstrate in this section.

The Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence for
X
has the form

E^{p,q}_{2}={\rm H}^{p}(k,{\rm H}^{q}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n))\cong{\rm H}^{p}(k,{\rm H}^{q}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n))\Rightarrow{\rm H}^{p+q}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}/n).

(37)

The question we address is the explicit form of the differentials

\delta^{p,1}_{2}\colon{\rm H}^{p}(k,{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n))\to{\rm H}^{p+2}(k,{\mathbb{Z}}/n),

where
p\geq 0
.
Each of these differentials is induced by the map
in the derived category of discrete
{\mathbb{Z}}/n
-modules with a continuous action of
\Gamma

\delta_{X}:{\rm H}^{1}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\cong{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\to{\mathbb{Z}}/n[2]

arising from the complex
\tau^{[0,1]}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\in D(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
.

Recall that
\mathcal{A}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
is
the abelian category of discrete
{\mathbb{Z}}/n
-modules equipped with a continuous action of
\Gamma
.
Writing
M={\rm H}^{1}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
, we can think of

\delta_{X}
as an element of

{\rm Ext}^{2}_{\mathcal{A}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
. Since

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)={\rm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)^{\Gamma}
, we have a spectral sequence

E^{p,q}_{2}={\rm H}^{p}(k,{\rm Ext}^{q}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}/n))\Rightarrow{\rm Ext}^{p+q}_{\mathcal{A}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}/n).

(38)

In our case,
M
is a free, hence projective
{\mathbb{Z}}/n
-module, thus
{\rm Ext}^{q}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)=0

for
q>0
. Now (
38
) gives a natural isomorphism

{\rm Ext}^{2}_{\mathcal{A}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\cong{\rm H}^{2}(k,{\rm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}/n))\cong{\rm H}^{2}(k,A[n]),

(39)

where the second isomorphism is the
{\mathbb{Z}}/n
-linear dual of a natural isomorphism
(
36
) of
\Gamma
-modules

{\rm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(A[n],{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\cong{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
induced by
the map (
11
). Using isomorphisms (
39
), we identify

\delta_{X}
with an element of
{\rm H}^{2}(k,A[n])
.

We will express the class
\delta_{X}
in terms of the class of the torsor
X
.
The exact sequence of
\Gamma
-modules

0\to A[n]\to A\to A\to 0

(40)

gives rise to the homomorphism of Galois cohomology groups

\beta_{n}\colon{\rm H}^{1}(k,A)\to{\rm H}^{2}(k,A[n]).

(41)

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
k
be a field and let
n
be a positive integer not divisible by
{\rm char}(k)
.
Let
X
be a
k
-torsor for a semiabelian variety
A
over
k
with class
[X]\in{\rm H}^{1}(k,A)
. The class
\delta_{X}\in{\rm H}^{2}(k,A[n])
is equal to the image of
[X]
under the map
\beta_{n}
.
In particular, the differentials
\delta^{p,1}_{2}
are given by cupping with the class

\beta_{n}([X])\in{\rm H}^{2}(k,A[n])\cong{\rm Ext}^{2}_{\mathcal{A}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)}({\rm H}^{1}(A_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n),{\mathbb{Z}}/n).

Proof.

Our goal is to compute the morphism

\delta_{X}:{\rm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(A[n],{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\cong{\rm H}^{1}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\to{\rm H}^{0}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)[2]\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/n[2]

in
D(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
corresponding to the complex

\tau^{[0,1]}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)
. The
{\mathbb{Z}}/n
-linear dual of the map
\delta_{X}
shifted by
2
is a map
\delta_{X}^{\vee}[2]:{\mathbb{Z}}/n\to(A[n])[2]
which is the following composition

{\mathbb{Z}}/n\xrightarrow{\Delta}{\rm{End}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(A[n])={\rm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(A[n],{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}A[n]\xrightarrow{\delta_{X}\otimes{\rm id}}{\mathbb{Z}}/n[2]\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}^{L}A[n],

where
\Delta
sends
1\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n
to
{\rm id}_{A[n]}\in{\rm{End}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(A[n])
. After applying the derived functor of the functor of
\Gamma
-invariants, we see that

{\rm id}_{A[n]}\in{\rm{End}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(A[n])^{\Gamma}
goes to
\delta_{X}\in{\rm H}^{2}(k,A[n])
.

The map
\delta_{X}\otimes{\rm id}_{A[n]}
can be identified with the connecting map of the complex
\tau^{[0,1]}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}A[n]\simeq\tau^{[0,1]}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{{\rm s}}},A[n])
. Thus the differential

d\colon{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{\rm s}},A[n])^{\Gamma}\to{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(k,A[n])

of the spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}={\rm H}^{p}(k,{\rm H}^{q}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{\rm s}},A[n]))\to{\rm H}^{p+q}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X,A[n])

is induced by
\delta_{X}\otimes{\rm id}_{A[n]}
.
There are natural isomorphisms of
\Gamma
-modules

{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{\rm s}},A[n])\cong{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},A[n])\cong{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A_{k_{\rm s}},{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}A[n]\cong{\rm{End}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(A[n]).

In the notation of
[
Sko01
, Proposition 3.2.2]
, under these isomorphisms,

{\rm id}_{A[n]}\in{\rm{End}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/n}(A[n])
corresponds to the class

\tau\in{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{\rm s}},A[n])
of the
X_{k_{\rm s}}
-torsor for
A[n]
given by
(
40
) after an identification of
X_{k_{\rm s}}
with
A_{k_{\rm s}}
. (Note that
\tau
does not depend on the choice of a
k_{\rm s}
-point of
X
,
thus
\tau\in{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{k_{\rm s}},A[n])^{\Gamma}
.)
This gives

.

On the other hand, by
[
Sko01
, Proposition 3.2.2]
, which is a restatement
of
[

, Proposition V.3.2.9]
, we have

.

Remark 5.4

(i) More generally, if

Let

Proposition 6.1
.

Let

(a)

(b)

Proof.

The canonical map

of the spectral sequence with coefficients

the class of a 2-extension in

sends the generator
of
{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(C_{k_{\rm s}},\mu_{n})^{\Gamma}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/n

[X]
in

\Box

Example 6.4

More generally, let

[

[

The following quick argument was communicated to us by Vadim Vologodsky. The Artin comparison quasi-isomorphism

can be made

{\bf Pic}_{C}(k_{{\rm s}})\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Z}}/n\to{\mathbb{Z}}/n
, the above composition (
53
) is the map inducing the differentials on the
3
rd page of our spectral sequence, as in the proof of Proposition
6.5
.
\Box
.

Example 6.7
.

Let
C
be a smooth, projective, geometrically integral curve over
{\mathbb{R}}
such that
C({\mathbb{R}})=\emptyset
.
The spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}={\rm H}^{p}({\mathbb{R}},{\rm H}^{q}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(C_{{\mathbb{C}}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m}))\Rightarrow{\rm H}^{p+q}_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(C,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})

gives rise to the short exact sequence

as follows from
[
GH81
, Proposition 2.2 (2)]
.
If the genus of

is even, then the Albanese torsor is trivial, that is,

[{\bf Pic}^{1}_{C/{\mathbb{R}}}]=0\in{\rm H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}},J)
, see
[
GH81
, Proposition 3.3 (1)]
.
Moreover, by the second statement of

[
GH81
, Proposition 2.2 (2)]
, for any
x\in{\bf Pic}^{1}_{C/{\mathbb{R}}}({\mathbb{R}})
we have

\delta(x)\neq 0
in
{\rm{Br}}({\mathbb{R}})
.
By Corollary
6.6
the 3rd page differentials

{\rm H}^{p}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\to{\rm H}^{p+3}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{Z}}/2)

(-1,-1,-1)

Remark 6.8
.

One can also give an explicit degree
3
Yoneda model for the extension
{\mathbb{Z}}/n\to\mu_{n}[3]
discussed above, at least after applying the forgetful functor
D(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}/n)\to D(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Let
{\rm{Div}}_{C_{k_{{\rm s}}}}
be the group of divisors on
C_{k_{{\rm s}}}
, that is, the free abelian group with the set of closed points of
C_{k_{{\rm s}}}
as the basis.
The exact sequence

0\to k_{{\rm s}}^{\times}\xrightarrow{}k_{{\rm s}}(C)^{\times}\xrightarrow{}{\rm{Div}}_{C_{k_{{\rm s}}}}\to{\bf Pic}_{C/k}(k_{{\rm s}})\to 0

(54)

represents the extension class of
\tau^{\leq 1}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{{\rm{\acute{e}t}}}(C_{k_{{\rm s}}},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
,
see
[
CTS21
, Proposition 5.4.5, Remark 5.4.6]
.
From diagram (
47
) we see that multiplication by
n
on

{\bf Pic}_{C/k}
factors as

{\bf Pic}_{C/k}\hookrightarrow A\xrightarrow{f}{\bf Pic}_{C/k}

for some map
f
. Let
\widetilde{{\rm{Div}}}
be the
\Gamma
-module defined as the pushout

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{{\rm{Div}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm{Div}}_{C_{k_{{\rm s}}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A(k_{{\rm s}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bf Pic}_{C/k}(k_{{\rm s}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

We will view elements of
\widetilde{{\rm{Div}}}
as pairs
(\alpha,D)\in A(k_{{\rm s}})\oplus{\rm{Div}}_{C_{k_{{\rm s}}}}
satisfying
f(\alpha)=[D]
. We can then form the complex

0\to\mu_{n}\to k_{{\rm s}}(C)^{\times}\xrightarrow{a}k_{{\rm s}}(C)^{\times}\oplus{\rm{Div}}_{C_{k_{{\rm s}}}}\xrightarrow{b}\widetilde{{\rm{Div}}}\xrightarrow{c}{\mathbb{Z}}/n\to 0.

(55)

Here the map
a
sends a rational function
\varphi
to the pair
(\varphi^{n},{\rm{div}}(\varphi))\in k_{{\rm s}}(C)^{\times}\oplus{\rm{Div}}_{C_{k_{{\rm s}}}}

The sequence (

) is exact and hence defines a map

{\rm H}^{3}(k,\mu_{n})\to{\rm Ext}^{3}_{\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathbb{Z}}/n,\mu_{n})

Lemma 6.9
.

Suppose that
{\rm char}(k)\neq 2
.
Consider the extension of
\Gamma

(56)

For even
n
the connecting map
{\rm H}^{n}(k,{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\to{\rm H}^{n+1}(k,{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
of
(\ref{mu_4})

is the cup-product with the class of
-1
in
{\rm H}^{1}(k,{\mathbb{Z}}/2)=k^{\times}/k^{\times 2}

Proof.

The spectral sequence
{\rm H}^{p}(k,{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{q}({\mathbb{Z}}/2,{\mathbb{Z}}/2))\Rightarrow{\rm Ext}^{p+q}_{\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathbb{Z}}/2,{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
gives an exact sequence

As pointed out in
[
Sus82
, the proof of Lemma 4]
,
the difference between the image of
[-1]\in{\rm H}^{1}(k,{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
in

) is the class of the extension where
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
is equipped with the trivial Galois action

0\to{\mathbb{Z}}/2\to{\mathbb{Z}}/4\to{\mathbb{Z}}/2\to 0.

the connecting map of (

.
\Box

If

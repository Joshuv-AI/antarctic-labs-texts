Nonexistence of weakly stable Yang-Mills fields
Xiaoli Han, Yang Wen

Abstract
. In this paper we prove that there is a neighborhood in the C 2 C^{2} topology of the usual metric on the Euclidean sphere S n ​ ( n ≥ 5 ) S^{n}(n\geq 5) such that there is no nontrivial weakly stable Yang-Mills connections for any metric g ~ \tilde{g} in this neighborhood. We also study the stability of Yang-Mills connections on the warped product manifolds.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
E\to M
a vector bundle of rank
r
endowed with a structure group
G
and compatible Riemannian metric
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
. The Yang-Mills functional on the affine space of metric-compatible connections of
E
is defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{YM}(\nabla)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|R^{\nabla}|_{g}^{2}dV_{g},

where
\nabla
is a connection of
E
preserving
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
and
R^{\nabla}
denotes its curvature form. Let
d^{\nabla}
be the exterior covariant derivative induced by
\nabla
, and
\delta^{\nabla}
its formal
L^{2}
-adjoint. The associated Euler-Lagrange equation takes the form

(1)

\displaystyle\delta^{\nabla}R^{\nabla}=0,

with solutions termed Yang-Mills connections.

Our work focuses on locally minimizing Yang-Mills connections – critical points of
\operatorname{YM}
achieving local minima. Such a connection
\nabla
is called weakly stable if the second variation of
\operatorname{YM}
is non-negative at
\nabla
:

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\operatorname{YM}(\nabla^{t})\mid_{t=0}\geq 0

for any smooth curve of connections
\nabla^{t}
with
\nabla^{0}=\nabla
.

The stability analysis of Yang-Mills functionals originates in Xin’s seminal work
[
13
]
, which established that no non-constant stable harmonic map exist from
S^{n}(n\geq 3)
to any Riemannian manifold. Adapting this methodology, Bourguignon and Lawson
[
1
]
proved that there is nontrivial weakly stable Yang-Mills connections on
S^{n}
for
n\geq 5
. They also proved that if the structure group
G=SU(2),SU(3)
, or
U(2)
, then the weakly stable Yang-Mills connection on
S^{4}
is self-dual or anti self-dual. Laquer
[
7
]
extended this program by classifying symmetric manifolds admitting unstable Yang-Mills fields. Through explicit computation of the Yang-Mills functional’s Morse index and nullity, it was shown that compact symmetric spaces admit weakly stable Yang-Mills connections except for
S^{n}(n\geq 5)
, compact simple Lie groups,
SP(p+1)/SP(p)\times SP(1)
,
E_{6}/F_{4}
and
F_{4}/Spin(9)
. Further developments by Kobayashi, Ohnita, and Takeuchi
[
8
]
demonstrated that all Yang-Mills connections on
P^{2}(\mathbb{C}ay)
and
E_{6}/F_{4}
is unstable. Most recently, Ni
[
9
]
proved that the Morse index of
\operatorname{YM}
on
S^{n}(n\geq 5)
is larger than
m+1
, and the smallest eigenvalue of the Jacobi operator of
\operatorname{YM}
is not greater than
4-n
.

Lawson and Simons in
[
6
]
proved that there are no compact stable minimal submanifolds in the Euclidean spheres. They conjectured that there are no stable minimal submanifolds in any compact, simply connected, Riemannian manifold
M
that is
1/4
-pinched. Here, by
\delta
-pinched for some
\delta>0
, we mean that at each point of
M
, the sectional curvatures are positive and the ratio between the smallest and the largest sectional curvatures of
M
, at that point, is strictly bigger than
\delta
. This conjecture has been verified for several classes of manifolds. Lawson and Simons
[
6
]
show it holds if
M^{n}
can be isometrically immersed in a standard sphere with sufficiently small second fundamental form. Howard
[
5
]
showed that for each
n\geqq 3
, there exists a constant
\delta(n,p)
satisfying
1/4<\delta(n,p)<1
such that if
M^{n}
is a simply-connected compact strictly
\delta(n,p)
-pinched Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
, then there are no nonconstant
p-
dimensional stable minimal submanifolds. But unfortunately
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\delta(n)=1
. Later, Shen and He
[
12
]
proved that there are no compact stable minimal submanifolds in a compact simply-connected
0.77
-pinched Riemannian manifold.

Naturally, there is a ”harmonic version” of this famous conjecture on stable minimal submanifolds. Howard
[
5
]
proved there exists a constant
1/4<\delta(n)<1
such that if
M^{n}
is a simply-connected compact strictly
\delta(n)
-pinched Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
, then there are no nonconstant stable harmonic maps
\psi:N\to M
for any compact manifold
N
. Okayasu gave a dimension-independent pinching constant
\delta=0.83
in
[
10
]
.

Similarly, we can consider ”Yang-Mills version” of this conjecture.

Question:
Let
M
be compact, simply connected Riemannian manifold that is
1/4
-pinched. Is every stable Yang-Mills connection on
M
flat ?

Ohnita and Pan
[
11
]
confirmed this question when
M
is
\delta(n)-
pinched. But in their argument it is not possible to find a pinching constant
\delta
independent of the
dimension of the base manifold
M
. We aim to address this problem in the conformal class of
S^{n}
.Conformal metrics are indispensable in geometric PDE theory, where their scale invariance simplifies blow-up analysis and resolves singularities through controlled rescalings. Han
[
4
]
excluded stable quasi-harmonic spheres in Gaussian conformal spaces. Cherif-Djaa-Zegga
[
2
]
proved that under
\operatorname{Hess}(e^{(n-2)\varphi})\leq 0
, any stable harmonic map from
(S^{n},e^{2\varphi}g_{standard})
to a Riemannian manifold is a constant map. Franz-Trinca
[
3
]
established non-existence of closed stable minimal
k
-submanifolds in generic conformal spheres
(S^{n},\tilde{g})
for dimensions
2\leq k\leq n-\delta^{-1}
, where
\delta
dependents on
\tilde{g}
.

The purpose of the present work is to study the existence of weakly stable Yang-Mills connections on conformal spheres. We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
(S^{n},g)
is the standard sphere and
\tilde{g}=e^{2\varphi}g
is a conformal metric. If
n\geq 5
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta\varphi-\frac{n-4}{2}|\operatorname{grad}(\varphi)|_{g}^{2}+2>0,

then there is no nontrivial weakly stable Yang-Mills connections on
(S^{n},\tilde{g})
.

Suppose
\varphi
is a small perturbation of a constant, then
\varphi
satisfies the condition in the above theorem.

Corollary 1.1
.

There is a neighborhood in the
C^{2}
topology of the usual metric on the
Euclidean sphere
S^{n}(n\geq 5)
such that there is no nontrivial weakly stable Yang-Mills connections for any metric
\tilde{g}
in this neighborhood.

Since the Yang-Mills functional is invariant under gauge transformation, it is meaningless to define a connection is stable if the second variation is positive. However, we define a connection as stable when the second variation is positive in all directions orthogonal to gauge transformations (see Definition
2.1
or
[
1
]
(6.12)). We obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
(S^{n},g)
is the standard sphere and
\tilde{g}=e^{2\varphi}g
is a conformal metric. If

(n-4)\left[\frac{1}{2}\Delta\varphi-\frac{n+4}{2}|\operatorname{grad}(\varphi)|_{g}^{2}+2\right]\geq 0,

then there is no nontrivial stable Yang-Mills connections on
(S^{n},\tilde{g})
. Especially, there is no nontrivial stable Yang-Mills connections on
(S^{4},\tilde{g})
.

We further analyze Yang-Mills connections on a finite product of spheres, and derived the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
M=S^{n_{1}}\times...\times S^{n_{q}}
is a product space of finite standard spheres with the product metric
g_{M}=g_{S^{n_{1}}}\oplus...\oplus g_{S^{n_{q}}}
.

(1) If
n_{i}\geq 5\ (1\leq i\leq q)
, then there is no nontrivial weakly stable Yang-Mills connections on
M
.

(2) If
n_{i}\geq 4\ (1\leq i\leq q)
, then there is no nontrivial stable Yang-Mills connections on
M
.

Furthermore, we give the stability of Yang-Mills connections on some warped product manifolds.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume
M=I\times N
is a non-compact manifold with warped product metric
g_{M}=drdr+f^{2}(r)g_{N}
, where
(N,g_{N})
is an
n-1
dimensional compact Riemannian manifold,
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open integral,
f\in C^{2}(I,(0,+\infty))
satisfies

(2)

\begin{split}&(a)\ f>0,\\
&(b)\ f(r)f^{\prime\prime}(r)\in L^{\infty}(I),\\
&(c)\ f(r)(f^{\prime}(r)+1)=O(r)\textrm{ for }r\in I\textrm{ and as }r\to\infty,\\
&(d)\ f(r)(f^{\prime}(r)+1)=O(|r-a|)\textrm{ for }r\to a\in\partial I.\end{split}

If
(n-4)f^{\prime\prime}<0
and
\nabla
is a weakly stable Yang-Mills connection on
M
with
R^{\nabla}\in L^{\infty}(M)\cap L^{2}(M)
, then we have

\displaystyle i_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}R^{\nabla}=0.

As a corollary, we have the following proposition.

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume
M
is an ellipsoid defined by
\{(x_{1},...,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}a_{i}x_{i}^{2}=1\}
for some positive constant
a_{i}
. If
n\geq 5
, then there is no nontrivial weakly stable Yang-Mills connections on
M
. Moreover, there is no nontrivial stable Yang-Mills connections on
M
if
n\geq 4
.

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
The connections and curvatures on vector bundles

Assume
(M,g)
be a
n
dimensional compact Riemannian manifold and
\mathscr{X}(M)
be the collection of vector fields on
M
. Let
E\to M
be a rank
r
vector bundle over
M
with a compact Lie group
G
as its structure group. We also assume
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
be the Riemannian metric on
E
compatible with the action of
G
and
\mathfrak{g}_{E}
be the adjoint bundle of
E
. Let
\nabla:\Omega^{0}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})\to\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
be the connection on
E
compatible with the metric
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
. Locally,
\nabla
takes the form

\displaystyle\nabla=d+A,

where
A\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
.

For any connection
\nabla
on
E
, the corresponding curvature
R^{\nabla}
is given by

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}=dA+\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A],

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A](X,Y)=[A(X),A(Y)].

The induced inner product on
\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
is given by

\displaystyle\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{2}Tr(\phi^{T}\psi)

for any
\phi,\psi\in\Omega^{0}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
and

\displaystyle\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{g}=\frac{1}{p!}\sum_{1\leq i_{1},...,i_{p}\leq p}\langle\phi(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{p}}),\psi(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{p}})\rangle,

for any
\phi,\psi\in\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, where
\{e_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
is an orthogonal basis of
TS^{n}
respect to the metric
g
. After integrating, we get the global inner product of
\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, that is

\displaystyle(\phi,\psi)_{g}=\int_{S^{n}}\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{g}dV_{g}.

The connection
\nabla
induces a connection
d^{\nabla}:\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})\to\Omega^{p+1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
on
\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, and we assume
\delta^{\nabla}:\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})\to\Omega^{p-1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
be the formal adjoint operator of
d^{\nabla}
. On local coordinates, we have

\displaystyle d^{\nabla}\phi(X_{1},...,X_{p+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{i+1}\nabla_{X_{i}}\phi(X_{1},...,\hat{X_{i}},...,X_{p+1}),

\displaystyle\delta^{\nabla}\phi(X_{1},...,X_{p-1})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}\phi(e_{i},X_{1},...,X_{p-1})

for any
\phi\in\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
.

We can define the Laplace–Beltrami operator
\Delta^{\nabla}
by

\displaystyle\Delta^{\nabla}\phi=d^{\nabla}\delta^{\nabla}\phi+\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}\phi

and the rough Laplacian operator
\nabla^{\ast}\nabla
by

\displaystyle\nabla^{\ast}\nabla\phi=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\nabla_{e_{i}}\nabla_{e_{i}}\phi-\nabla_{D_{e_{i}}e_{i}}\phi).

For
\phi\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
and
\psi\in\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{g}^{\nabla}(\phi)(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[R^{\nabla}(e_{i},X),\phi(e_{i})],

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{g}^{\nabla}(\psi)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[R^{\nabla}(e_{i},X),\psi(e_{i},Y)]-[R^{\nabla}(e_{i},Y),\psi(e_{i},X)].

Then we have the following Bochner–Weizenböck formula first introduced by Bourguignon-Lawson.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
1
]

For any
\phi\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
and
\psi\in\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{\nabla}\phi=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla+\phi\circ\operatorname{Ric}+\mathfrak{R}_{g}^{\nabla}(\phi),

\displaystyle\Delta^{\nabla}\psi=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla+\psi\circ(\operatorname{Ric}\wedge Id+2R_{M})+\mathfrak{R}_{g}^{\nabla}(\psi),

where
R_{M}
is the curvature tensor of
M
and

•

\operatorname{Ric}:TM\to TM
is the Ricci transformation defined by

\operatorname{Ric}\left(X\right)=\sum_{j}R_{M}(X,e_{j})e_{j},

•

\operatorname{Ric}\wedge\operatorname{Id}
is the extension of the Ricci transformation
\operatorname{Ric}
to
\wedge^{2}TM
given by

\operatorname{Ric}\wedge\operatorname{Id}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Ric}\wedge\operatorname{Id}(X\wedge Y)=\operatorname{Ric}(X)\wedge Y+X\wedge\operatorname{Ric}(Y),

•

The composite map
\psi\circ R_{M}:\wedge^{2}TM\to\Omega^{0}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{E}\right)
is defined by

\psi\circ R_{M}(X,Y)=\psi\circ R_{M}(X\wedge Y)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\psi(e_{j},R_{M}(X,Y)e_{j}).

Remark 2.1
.

In particular, on the standard sphere
(S^{n},g)
, we have

\displaystyle\phi\circ\operatorname{Ric}=(n-1)\phi,

\displaystyle\psi\circ(\operatorname{Ric}\wedge Id+2R_{S^{n}})=2(n-2)\psi.

2.2.
The conformal metric and the second variation

Let
g
be the standard metric on
S^{n}
, and assume
\tilde{g}=e^{2\varphi}g
be the Riemannian metric on
S^{n}
conformal to
g
for some smooth map
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(S^{n})
. Assume
D
and
\tilde{D}
be the Levi-Civita connections corresponding to
g
and
\tilde{g}
respectively. Then we have

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{X}Y=D_{X}Y+X(\varphi)Y+Y(\varphi)X-g(X,Y)\operatorname{grad}(\varphi)

for any
X,Y\in\mathscr{X}(S^{n})
. For any connection
\nabla
on
E
, assume
\nabla
and
\widetilde{\nabla}
are the induced connections on
\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
respect to
g
and
\tilde{g}
respectively, we have
R^{\tilde{\nabla}}=R^{\nabla}
and

\displaystyle d^{\tilde{\nabla}}\psi=d^{\nabla}\psi,

\displaystyle\delta^{\tilde{\nabla}}\psi=e^{-2\varphi}(\delta^{\nabla}\psi+(2p-n)i_{\operatorname{grad}(\varphi)}\psi)

for any
\psi\in\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
.

The Yang-Mills functional on
(S^{n},\tilde{g})
is given by

(3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{YM}(\nabla)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S^{n}}|R^{\nabla}|^{2}_{\tilde{g}}dV_{\tilde{g}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S^{n}}e^{(n-4)\varphi}|R^{\nabla}|^{2}_{g}dV_{g}.

The Euler-Lagrange equation is

(4)

\displaystyle\delta^{\tilde{\nabla}}R^{\nabla}=0,

or equivalently,

(5)

\displaystyle\delta^{\nabla}R^{\nabla}=(n-4)i_{\operatorname{grad}(\varphi)}R^{\nabla}.

Assume
\nabla
is a Yang-Mills connection satisfying (
5
). For any
B\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, let
\nabla^{t}=\nabla+tB
. Then we have

\displaystyle R^{\nabla^{t}}=R^{\nabla}+td^{\nabla}B+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}[B\wedge B].

The second variation of
\operatorname{YM}
is given by

(6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(B):=\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\operatorname{YM}(\nabla^{t})\mid_{t=0}=\int_{S^{n}}e^{(n-4)\varphi}\langle\mathscr{S}(B),B\rangle_{g}dV_{g},

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(B)=\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}B-(n-4)i_{\operatorname{grad}(\varphi)}d^{\nabla}B+\mathfrak{R}_{g}^{\nabla}(B).

For any gauge transformation
g\in\mathcal{G}
,
g
acts on
\nabla
such that
\nabla^{g}=g^{-1}\circ\nabla\circ g
. Then for any
\sigma\in\Omega^{0}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, let
g_{t}=exp(t\sigma)
be a family of gauge transformations. The variation along
\sigma
is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\nabla^{g_{t}}\mid_{t=0}=d^{\nabla}\sigma.

The Yang-Mills functional is invariant under gauge transformation. Therefore, we are interested in the variations orthogonal to gauge transformations respect to
\tilde{g}
, that is,

\displaystyle B\in\operatorname{Im}(d^{\nabla})^{\perp}=\operatorname{ker}(\delta^{\tilde{\nabla}}).

For any
B\in\operatorname{ker}(\delta^{\tilde{\nabla}})
, the second variation is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(B)=\int_{S^{n}}\langle\tilde{\mathscr{S}}(B),B\rangle_{\tilde{g}}dV_{\tilde{g}},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathscr{S}}(B)=\Delta^{\tilde{\nabla}}B+\mathfrak{R}^{\tilde{\nabla}}_{\tilde{g}}(B)

is a elliptic, self-adjoint operator mapping
\operatorname{ker}(\delta^{\tilde{\nabla}})
to itself. The eigenvalues of
\tilde{\mathscr{S}}
on
\operatorname{ker}(\delta^{\tilde{\nabla}})
are

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<...\to+\infty.

Following (6.14) of
[
1
]
, we define a stable Yang-Mills connection.

Definition 2.1
.

We say a Yang-Mills connection is stable, if
\lambda_{1}>0
and weakly stable if
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
.

It is readily verified that such defined weakly stable connections are equivalent to those with non-negative second variation.

3.
The stability of Yang-Mills connections on conformal spheres

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. As done in
[
1
]
, we construct the variational using gradient conformal vector fields of
(S^{n},g)
to prove the nonexistence of weakly stable Yang-Mills fields. These vector fields are precisely the gradients of the eigenfunctions corresponding to the first eigenvalue of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
(S^{n},g)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, let
F_{v}(x)=v\cdot x:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be the linear map and
f_{v}=F_{v}\mid_{S^{n}}
be the restriction on
S^{n}
. Then
V=\operatorname{grad}(f_{v})
is a conformal vector field of
(S^{n},g)
and satisfies

\displaystyle D_{X}V=-f_{v}X,

\displaystyle D^{\ast}DV=V

for any
X\in\mathscr{X}(S^{n})
, where

\displaystyle D^{\ast}DV=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(D_{e_{i}}D_{e_{i}}V-D_{D_{e_{i}}e_{i}}V).

To avoid any ambiguity, we designate the vector field corresponding to the vector
v
as
V
. For some constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\tilde{V}=e^{\lambda\varphi}V
, then we have

\displaystyle D_{X}\tilde{V}=-e^{\lambda\varphi}f_{v}X+\lambda X(\varphi)\tilde{V}.

Inspired by
[
1
]
, for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we choose the variation of connection to be
B_{v}=i_{\tilde{V}}R^{\nabla}
. We need to compute the second variation that contains no derivatives of
\tilde{V}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
X\in\mathscr{X}(S^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(B_{v})(X)

\displaystyle=(4-n+\lambda\Delta\varphi-(\lambda^{2}+(n-4)\lambda)|\operatorname{grad}(\varphi)|_{g}^{2})R^{\nabla}(\tilde{V},X)+(4-n+3\lambda)e^{\lambda\varphi}f_{v}R^{\nabla}(\operatorname{grad}(\varphi),X)

\displaystyle-\lambda^{2}X(\varphi)R^{\nabla}(\operatorname{grad}(\varphi),\tilde{V})-\lambda\nabla_{\operatorname{grad}(\varphi)}R^{\nabla}(\tilde{V},X)-\lambda\nabla_{\tilde{V}}R^{\nabla}(\operatorname{grad}(\varphi),X)

\displaystyle+(n-4)R^{\nabla}(D_{\tilde{V}}\operatorname{grad}(\varphi),X)+\lambda R^{\nabla}(\tilde{V},D_{X}\operatorname{grad}(\varphi)).

Proof.

For any
x\in S^{n}
, let
\{e_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
be the orthogonal basis of
TS^{n}
respect to
g
such that
De_{i}(x)=0
. For any
e_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}i_{\tilde{V}}R^{\nabla}(e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}d^{\nabla}i_{\tilde{V}}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}(\nabla_{e_{i}}i_{\tilde{V}}R^{\nabla}(e_{j})-\nabla_{e_{j}}i_{\tilde{V}}R^{\nabla}(e_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}(\nabla_{e_{i}}(R^{\nabla}({\tilde{V}},e_{j}))-R^{\nabla}({\tilde{V}},D_{e_{i}}e_{j})-\nabla_{e_{j}}(R^{\nabla}({\tilde{V}},e_{i}))+R^{\nabla}({\tilde{V}},D_{e_{j}}e_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}(\nabla_{e_{i}}R^{\nabla}({\tilde{V}},e_{j})+R^{\nabla}(D_{e_{i}}{\tilde{V}},e_{j})-\nabla_{e_{j}}R^{\nabla}({\tilde{V}},e_{i})-R^{\nabla}(D_{e_{j}}{\tilde{V}},e_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}(\nabla_{\tilde{V}}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})-2e^{\lambda\varphi}f_{v}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})+\lambda e_{i}(\varphi)R^{\nabla}(\tilde{V},e_{j})-\lambda e_{j}(\varphi)R^{\nabla}(\tilde{V},e_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}\nabla_{\tilde{V}}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})+(2+\lambda\Delta\varphi-\lambda^{2}|\operatorname{grad}(\varphi)|_{g}^{2})R^{\nabla}({\tilde{V}},e_{j})+(8-2n+3\lambda)f_{v}e^{\lambda\varphi}R^{\nabla}(\operatorname{grad}(\varphi),e_{j})

\displaystyle+\lambda(n-4-\lambda)e_{j}(\varphi)R^{\nabla}(\operatorname{grad}(\varphi),{\tilde{V}})-\lambda\nabla_{\operatorname{grad}(\varphi)}R^{\nabla}({\tilde{V}},e_{j})+\lambda R^{\nabla}({\tilde{V}},D_{e_{j}}\operatorname{grad}(\varphi))

at
x
, where we use
\sum_{i}e_{i}(f_{v})e_{i}=\operatorname{grad}(f_{v})=V
and
-\sum_{i}\nabla_{e_{i}}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})=\delta^{\nabla}R^{\nabla}(e_{j})=(n-4)R^{\nabla}(\operatorname{grad}(\varphi),e_{j})
. Employing the commutation formula, we have

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}\nabla_{\tilde{V}}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}([R^{\nabla}(e_{i},{\tilde{V}}),R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})]-R^{\nabla}(R_{S^{n}}(e_{i},{\tilde{V}})e_{i},e_{j})-R^{\nabla}(e_{i},R_{S^{n}}(e_{i},{\tilde{V}})e_{j})+\nabla_{\tilde{V}}\nabla_{e_{i}}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})+\nabla_{[e_{i},{\tilde{V}}]}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{R}_{g}^{\nabla}(i_{\tilde{V}}R^{\nabla})(e_{j})+(2-n)R^{\nabla}({\tilde{V}},e_{j})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\nabla_{\tilde{V}}\nabla_{e_{i}}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})+\nabla_{[e_{i},{\tilde{V}}]}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j}))

at
x
, where

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}\nabla_{V}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}[R^{\nabla}(e_{i},V),R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})]+\nabla_{V}\nabla_{e_{i}}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})+\nabla_{[e_{i},V]}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})-R^{\nabla}(R_{M}(e_{i},V)e_{i},e_{j})-R^{\nabla}(e_{i},R_{M}(e_{i},V)e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{R}^{\nabla}(i_{V}R^{\nabla})(e_{j})+\nabla_{V}(\delta^{\nabla}R^{\nabla}(e_{j}))+f^{\prime}\delta^{\nabla}R^{\nabla}(e_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}R^{\nabla}(R_{M}(e_{i},V)e_{i},e_{j})+R^{\nabla}(e_{i},R_{M}(e_{i},V)e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{R}^{\nabla}(i_{V}R^{\nabla})(e_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}R^{\nabla}(R_{M}(e_{i},V)e_{i},e_{j})+R^{\nabla}(e_{i},R_{M}(e_{i},V)e_{j}).

Finally we need to calculate the curvature tensor
R_{M}
. Let
(y^{1},...,y^{n-1})
be the local normal coordinate of
x^{\prime}\in N
and
T,Y_{i}\in\mathscr{X}(M)
be the vector fields defined in (
9
). Since the above equation is independent to the choice of
\{e_{i}\}
, we may assume
e_{i}=f^{-1}Y_{i}
for
i\in[1,n-1]
and
e_{n}=T
. Using (
9
), at
x
we have

\displaystyle D_{Y_{i}}D_{V}Y_{j}=D_{Y_{i}}(f^{\prime}Y_{j})=f^{\prime}\iota_{r\ast}(D^{\prime}_{Y_{i}}Y_{j})-\delta_{ij}f(f^{\prime})^{2}T=f^{\prime}\iota_{r\ast}(D^{\prime}_{Y_{i}}Y_{j})-\delta_{ij}(f^{\prime})^{2}V,

and

\displaystyle D_{V}D_{Y_{i}}Y_{j}

\displaystyle=fD_{T}(\iota_{r\ast}(D^{\prime}_{Y_{i}}Y_{j})-f^{-1}f^{\prime}g(Y_{i},Y_{j})T)

\displaystyle=-\delta_{ij}f^{2}(f^{-1}f^{\prime})^{\prime}V-f^{-1}f^{\prime}(g(D_{T}Y_{i},Y_{j})+g(Y_{i},D_{T}Y_{j}))V

\displaystyle=-\delta_{ij}(ff^{\prime\prime}+(f^{\prime})^{2})V,

where we use
D_{T}(\iota_{r\ast}(D^{\prime}_{Y_{i}}Y_{j}))=0
, since by letting
\Gamma^{\prime}
be the Christoffel symbols of
N
respect to
(y^{1},...,y^{n-1})
, we have
\Gamma_{ij}^{\prime k}(x^{\prime})=0
and thus

\displaystyle D_{T}(\iota_{r\ast}(D^{\prime}_{Y_{i}}Y_{j}))(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Gamma_{ij}^{\prime k}(x^{\prime})D_{T}Y_{k}(x)=0.

Finally, we have
[Y_{i},V]=D_{Y_{i}}V-D_{V}Y_{i}=0
, and thus

\displaystyle D_{[Y_{i},V]}Y_{j}=0.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle R_{M}(e_{i},V)e_{j}=f^{-2}R_{M}(Y_{i},V)Y_{j}=\delta_{ij}f^{-1}f^{\prime\prime}V

for any
i,j\in[1,n-1]
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}R^{\nabla}(R_{M}(e_{i},V)e_{i},e_{j})+R^{\nabla}(e_{i},R_{M}(e_{i},V)e_{j})=(n-2)f^{-1}f^{\prime\prime}R^{\nabla}(V,e_{j}).

Using similar method, we can proof that

\displaystyle R_{M}(e_{i},V)T=-f^{\prime\prime}e_{i}

for any
i\in[1,n-1]
and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}R^{\nabla}(R_{M}(e_{i},V)e_{i},T)+R^{\nabla}(e_{i},R_{M}(e_{i},V)T)=0.

Thus, we finish the proof.
∎

Since
i_{V}R^{\nabla}
is not a 1-form with compact support, we also need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume
\nabla
is a weakly stable Yang-Mills connection on
M
with
R^{\nabla}\in L^{\infty}(M)\cap L^{2}(M)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\to+\infty}\int_{(\frac{2}{R},R)\times N}\langle\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}i_{V}R^{\nabla}+\mathfrak{R}_{g}^{\nabla}(i_{V}R^{\nabla}),i_{V}R^{\nabla}\rangle dV_{g}\geq 0

Proof.

We only need to prove the case where
I=(0,+\infty)
, as other cases can be handled by analogous arguments. For any
R>0
, let
\eta_{R}\in C^{\infty}([0,+\infty))
be a cut-off function such that
\eta_{R}(r)\in[0,1]
and

\displaystyle\eta_{R}=1\textrm{ on }(\frac{2}{R},R),

\displaystyle\eta_{R}=0\textrm{ on }[0,\frac{1}{R})\cup(2R,+\infty),

\displaystyle|d^{k}\eta_{R}|\leq C_{k}R^{-k}\textrm{ on }(R,2R),

\displaystyle|d^{k}\eta_{R}|\leq C_{k}R^{k}\textrm{ on }(\frac{1}{R},\frac{2}{R})

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and some constant
C_{k}>0
. For any
x\in M
, assume
De_{i}=0
at
x
, then we have

\displaystyle\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}(\eta_{R}i_{V}R^{\nabla})(e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}(\nabla_{e_{i}}(\eta_{R}i_{V}R^{\nabla})(e_{j})-\nabla_{e_{j}}(\eta_{R}i_{V}R^{\nabla})(e_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}(\eta_{R}d^{\nabla}i_{V}R^{\nabla}(e_{i},e_{j})+e_{i}(\eta_{R})i_{V}R^{\nabla}(e_{j})-e_{j}(\eta_{R})i_{V}R^{\nabla}(e_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{R}\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}i_{V}R^{\nabla}(e_{j})-\eta_{R}^{\prime\prime}i_{V}R^{\nabla}(e_{j})-\eta_{R}^{\prime}\nabla_{T}i_{V}R^{\nabla}(e_{j})-\eta_{R}^{\prime}d^{\nabla}i_{V}R^{\nabla}(T,e_{j})

at
x
. By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{T}i_{V}R^{\nabla}=d^{\nabla}i_{V}R^{\nabla}(T,e_{j})=\nabla_{T}R^{\nabla}(V,e_{j})+f^{-1}f^{\prime}R^{\nabla}(V,e_{j}).

Thus we have

\displaystyle\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}(\eta_{R}i_{V}R^{\nabla})(e_{j})=\eta_{R}\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}i_{V}R^{\nabla}(e_{j})-(\eta_{R}^{\prime\prime}+2\eta_{R}^{\prime}f^{-1}f^{\prime})i_{V}R^{\nabla}(e_{j})-2\eta_{R}^{\prime}\nabla_{T}R^{\nabla}(V,e_{j}).

Since
\nabla
is weakly stable, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathscr{L}(\eta_{R}(r)\cdot i_{V}R^{\nabla})=\int_{M}\langle\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}(\eta_{R}(r)\cdot i_{V}R^{\nabla})+\mathfrak{R}^{\nabla}_{g}(\eta_{R}(r)\cdot i_{V}R^{\nabla}),\eta_{R}(r)\cdot i_{V}R^{\nabla}\rangle dV_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta_{R}^{2}(r)\langle\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}i_{V}R^{\nabla}+\mathfrak{R}^{\nabla}_{g}(i_{V}R^{\nabla}),i_{V}R^{\nabla}\rangle-\eta_{R}(\eta_{R}^{\prime\prime}+2\eta_{R}^{\prime}f^{-1}f^{\prime})|i_{V}R^{\nabla}|^{2}-\eta_{R}\eta_{R}^{\prime}T(|i_{V}R^{\nabla}|^{2})dV_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(\frac{2}{R},R)\times N}\langle\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}i_{V}R^{\nabla}+\mathfrak{R}^{\nabla}_{g}(i_{V}R^{\nabla}),i_{V}R^{\nabla}\rangle dV_{g}

\displaystyle+\int_{((\frac{1}{R},\frac{2}{R})\cup(R,2R))\times N}((n-4)\eta_{R}^{2}f^{-1}f^{\prime\prime}+\eta_{R}^{\prime 2}-2\eta_{R}\eta_{R}^{\prime}f^{-1}f^{\prime})|i_{V}R^{\nabla}|^{2}dV_{g}.

Since
|i_{V}R^{\nabla}|^{2}\leq 2f^{2}|R^{\nabla}|^{2}
and
R^{\nabla}\in L^{2}(M)
, using
ff^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}((0,+\infty))
and
f(|f^{\prime}|+1)=O(r)
as
r\to+\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{(R,2R)\times N}((n-4)\eta_{R}^{2}f^{-1}f^{\prime\prime}+\eta_{R}^{\prime 2}-2\eta_{R}\eta_{R}^{\prime}f^{-1}f^{\prime})|i_{V}R^{\nabla}|^{2}dV_{g}\leq C\int_{(R,2R)\times N}|R^{\nabla}|^{2}dV_{g}\to 0

as
R\to+\infty
. Using
f(r)(f^{\prime}(r)+1)=O(r)
at 0, we have

\displaystyle\int_{(\frac{1}{R},\frac{2}{R})\times N}((n-4)\eta_{R}^{2}f^{-1}f^{\prime\prime}+\eta_{R}^{\prime 2}-2\eta_{R}\eta_{R}^{\prime}f^{-1}f^{\prime})|i_{V}R^{\nabla}|^{2}dV_{g}\leq C\int_{(\frac{1}{R},\frac{2}{R})\times N}|R^{\nabla}|^{2}dV_{g}\to 0.

Now we complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

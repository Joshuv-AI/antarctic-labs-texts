Nilpotence of η \eta in étale motivic spectra
Klaus Mattis, Swann Tubach

Abstract
We show that every object of the stable étale motivic homotopy category over any scheme is η \eta -complete. In some cases we show that in fact the fourth power of η \eta is null, whereas the third power of η \eta is always nonvanishing, similar to the situation in topology.

Introduction

In topology, the Hopf map provides the first example of a nonzero element of a homotopy group of the form

\pi_{n}(S^{n-1})
, it is a fibration
S^{3}\to S^{2}
whose fibers are all isomorphic to
S^{1}
. A simple definition is as follows:

\eta_{\mathrm{top}}\colon S^{3}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\to{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{P}^{1}\simeq S^{2}

is the projection, up to homotopy. The (desuspended) image of
\eta_{\mathrm{top}}
, pointed at any point of
S^{3}
, in the category of spectra
{\operatorname{Sp}}
is a map

\eta_{\mathrm{top}}\colon\Sigma{\mathbb{S}}\to{\mathbb{S}}

from the suspension of the sphere spectrum
{\mathbb{S}}
to the sphere spectrum.
It provides a generator for the first stable homotopy group of the sphere:

0\neq\eta_{\mathrm{top}}\in\pi_{1}({\mathbb{S}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}.

One may compute the powers of
\eta_{\mathrm{top}}
, and show that
\eta_{\mathrm{top}}^{4}=0
. This is easy, as
\pi_{4}({\mathbb{S}})=0
,
which can be read of from the
E_{2}
-page of the Adams spectral sequence.
It is true, but harder to see, that
\eta_{\mathrm{top}}^{3}\neq 0\in\pi_{3}({\mathbb{S}})={\mathbb{Z}}/24{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Indeed, this can be done by computations with Toda brackets, see
[
Tod62
, Chapter V, Equation 5.5 and Proposition 5.6]
.

Motivic homotopy theory aims to imitate the methods of algebraic topology in the world of algebraic geometry. For that matter, many classical results of
homotopy theory have now a version in algebraic geometry, see e.g.
[
Hoy15
,
AHW17
,
ABH23
]
. Let
S
be a scheme. Following Morel and Voevodsky, one considers the stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{SH}(S)
of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant motivic spectra. As in topology, we have an algebraic Hopf map given by the canonical projection

\eta\colon\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}

whose (desuspended) image in
\mathcal{SH}(S)
is a map

\eta\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to{\mathbb{S}}

from the motivic sphere
\mathbb{G}_{m}
to the motivic sphere spectrum
{\mathbb{S}}
.
As a corollary to a theorem of Morel
[
Mor04
, Corollary 6.4.5]
, one may compute over a perfect field
k

the endomorphisms of the
\eta
-inverted sphere

{\mathbb{S}}[\eta^{-1}]\coloneqq{\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\end{subarray}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,}({\mathbb{S}}\xrightarrow{\eta}\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\otimes-1}\xrightarrow{\eta}\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\otimes-2}\to\cdots)
:
we have

\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{SH}(k)}({\mathbb{S}}[\eta^{-1}])\simeq W(k),

where
W(k)
is the Witt ring of symmetric bilinear forms of
k
.
In particular, by pulling back to fields, we see that the map
\eta
is
never nilpotent
in
\mathcal{SH}(S)
, for any (nonempty) scheme
S
.
This implies that in
\mathcal{SH}(S)
, there exists many
\eta
-periodic objects, that is, objects
M
such that the map
\eta\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\otimes M\to M
is an equivalence.

In this short note, we observe that this discrepancy between motivic homotopy theory and classical homotopy theory disappears if ones works in the étale local stable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)

(see e.g.
[
Bac21
, §5]
for a definition, in the étale setting
we will always work with hypersheaves).
Our main result is the following:

Theorem A

(
??
)
.

Let
S
be a scheme. Then for
X\in\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
, the object
X[\eta^{-1}]
is zero. In particular, every object of
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
is
\eta
-complete, and
\eta
acts nilpotently on any compact object of
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
.

A corollary of this result is the following:

Corollary B

(
??
)
.

Let
S
be a scheme.
The étale sheafification functor
L_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}\colon\mathcal{SH}(S)\to\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)

factors canonically over
\mathcal{SH}(S)^{\wedge}_{\eta}
. In particular, any object of
\mathcal{SH}(S)
that satisfies étale descent
is already
\eta
-complete.

In good cases, we can compute the index of nilpotence of
\eta
in
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
. For example, if
k
is an algebraically closed field, we show that in
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(k)
we have
\eta^{4}=0
. More generally:

Theorem C

(
??
,
??
)
.

Let
S
be any scheme.
Then there exists a finite faithfully flat map
S^{\prime}\to S
such that
\eta^{4}
is null in
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S^{\prime})
.

If there exists a map
f\colon S\to\Spec(k)
where
k
is a field with
\mathrm{cd}_{2}(k)\leqslant 1
and
\sup_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{cd}_{p}(k)<\infty
(e.g., any scheme defined over a finite field or an algebraically closed field),
then
\eta^{4}
is already null in
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
.

In fact, we conjecture the following:

Conjecture

(
??
)
.

For any scheme
S
we have
\eta^{4}\cong 0
in
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
.

Remark
.

Since
f^{*}\eta^{4}\cong\eta^{4}
where

is the unique morphism,
it is of course enough to show that
\eta^{4}\cong 0
in
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

On the other hand, we know that on almost all schemes that
\eta^{3}
is not null:

(

Let

Then

is not null in

.

If
S

1 Recollections on stable étale motivic homotopy theory

Definition 1.1
.

Let
S
be a scheme. We say that
S
is
étale bounded
if

\mathrm{sup}_{x\in X,p\in\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{cd}_{p}(\kappa(x))<\infty,

where
\mathrm{cd}_{p}(k)
is the mod-
p
-Galois cohomological dimension of a field
k
, and
\mathbb{P}
is the
set of all prime numbers.
Similarly, we say that
S
is
étale locally étale bounded
if there exists
an étale cover
S^{\prime}\to S
such that
S^{\prime}
is étale bounded.

Example 1.2
.

By
[
Bac21
, Example 2.14]
the scheme
\Spec({\mathbb{Z}})
is étale locally étale bounded,
and the scheme
\Spec({\mathbb{Z}}[i])=\Spec({\mathbb{Z}}[x]/(x^{2}+1))
is étale bounded.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
X\to S
be a morphism of finite type with
S
quasi-compact.
If
S
is (étale locally) étale bounded, the same is true for
X
.

Proof.

Suppose that
S
is étale locally étale bounded. Choose an étale cover
S^{\prime}\to S
so that
S^{\prime}
is étale bounded.
In particular, we get an étale cover
S^{\prime}\times_{S}X\to X
, such that
S^{\prime}\times_{S}X\to S^{\prime}
is of finite type.

Hence, we may assume that
S
is étale bounded, and we have to show that the same is true for
X
.
For this, see
[
Mat25
, Lemma 2.24]
(the extra assumptions
given in the reference that
S
is of finite Krull dimension and that
X\to S
is smooth are not necessary).
∎

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
S
be a scheme that is étale locally étale bounded.
Then
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
is compactly generated.
If
S
is moreover étale bounded,
then
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X\in\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
is compact for every qcqs
X\in\operatorname{Sm}_{S}
.

Proof.

This is
[
AGV22
, Proposition 2.4.22]
(see also Remark 2.4.23 of
ibid
).
∎

Theorem 1.5

(Rigidity)
.

Let
S
be a scheme and
\ell
a prime.
Then there are canonical equivalences

\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)^{\wedge}_{\ell}\cong\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S[1/\ell])^{\wedge}_{\ell}\cong\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}({S[1/\ell],{\operatorname{Sp}}})^{\wedge}_{\ell},

where we write
S[1/\ell]\coloneqq S\times_{\Spec({\mathbb{Z}})}\Spec({\mathbb{Z}}[1/\ell])
.

Proof.

Consider the open immersion
S[1/\ell]\to S
,
with closed reduced complement
Z
of characteristic
\ell
.
By
[
Ayo07
, Corollaire 4.5.4]
there is a recollement
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S[1/\ell])^{\wedge}_{\ell}\to\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)^{\wedge}_{\ell}\to\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(Z)^{\wedge}_{\ell}

(note that Ayoub implicitly fixes a topology, which is allowed to be the étale topology,
see the beginning of
[
Ayo07
, Section 4.5]
; the fact that Ayoub’s result implies that we have an
\infty
-categorical recollement is classical, see
[
Rob14
, Proposition 9.4.20]
).
Moreover,
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(Z)^{\wedge}_{\ell}=0
by
[
BH21
, Theorem A.1]
.
This implies the first equivalence.
The second equivalence in this generality is
[
BH21
, Theorem 3.1]
.
∎

2 Recollections on completions and periodizations

In this section, let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a stable presentably symmetric monoidal category with unit
{\mathbb{S}}
, and

\nu\colon T\to{\mathbb{S}}
be a map with
T
tensor-invertible.
Consider the functor
(-)/\mkern-6.0mu/\nu
that is given as the cofiber of
T\otimes-\xrightarrow{\nu}-
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a map
f\colon X\to Y
is a
\nu
-equivalence, if
f/\mkern-6.0mu/\nu

is an equivalence. Write
(-)^{\wedge}_{\nu}\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
for the associated
Bousfield localization at
\nu
-equivalences, called
\nu
-completion
.
We write
{\mathcal{E}}\phantom{}^{\wedge}_{\nu}
for the essential image of
(-)^{\wedge}_{\nu}
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that an object
X\in{\mathcal{E}}
is
\nu
-periodic
if
X/\mkern-6.0mu/\nu=0

(equivalently,
T\otimes X\xrightarrow{\nu}X
is an equivalence).
The
\nu
-periodization functor is the Bousfield localization

(-)[\nu^{-1}]\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
with essential image the subcategory of
\nu
-periodic objects.
Since
\nu
-periodic objects are evidently closed under limits and colimits (for limits use that
T\otimes-
preserves
them since
T
is invertible), this localization exists by the adjoint functor theorem.

Lemma 2.3
.

The localization functor
(-)[\nu^{-1}]
is smashing,
i.e. for all
X\in{\mathcal{E}}
we have
X[\nu^{-1}]\cong{\mathbb{S}}[\nu^{-1}]\otimes X
.

Proof.

By
[
AI22
, Lemma A.5.2]
it is enough to show
that for all
X,Y\in{\mathcal{E}}
such that
X
is
\nu
-periodic,
then so is
X\otimes Y
and
{\underline{\smash{\operatorname{map}}}({Y},{X})}
.
Here,
{\underline{\smash{\operatorname{map}}}({-},{-})}
denotes the internal hom object
in
{\mathcal{E}}
(which exists by the adjoint functor theorem).
But now both
\nu\otimes X\otimes Y

and
\nu\otimes{\underline{\smash{\operatorname{map}}}({Y},{X})}\cong{\underline{\smash{\operatorname{map}}}({Y},{\nu\otimes X})}

are equivalences, since already
\nu\otimes X
is one.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map in
{\mathcal{E}}
. Then
f
is a
\nu
-equivalence if and only if
{\operatorname{fib}\mathopen{}\left(f\right)\mathclose{}}

is
\nu
-periodic.

Proof.

The map
f
is a
\nu
-equivalence if and only if
f/\mkern-6.0mu/\nu
is an equivalence,
i.e., if and only if
0={\operatorname{fib}\mathopen{}\left(f/\mkern-6.0mu/\nu\right)\mathclose{}}\cong{\operatorname{fib}\mathopen{}\left(f\right)\mathclose{}}/\mkern-6.0mu/\nu
.
But the latter is zero if and only if
{\operatorname{fib}\mathopen{}\left(f\right)\mathclose{}}
is
\nu
-periodic.
∎

We now try to describe the
\nu
-periodization functor explicitly.
For this, recall the following definition:

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X\in{\mathcal{E}}
.
We define the
mapping telescope

M_{\nu}(X)
as the filtered colimit

{\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\end{subarray}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,}X\xrightarrow{\nu}T^{\otimes-1}\otimes X\xrightarrow{\nu}T^{\otimes-2}\otimes X\to\cdots

Since the tensor product is compatible with colimits, we see that
M_{\nu}(X)\cong M_{\nu}({\mathbb{S}})\otimes X
.

Now, in a variety of situations, the mapping telescope agrees with the
\nu
-periodization.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that there exists a compactly generated presentably symmetric monoidal stable category
{\mathcal{D}}

with unit
\widetilde{{\mathbb{S}}}
,
and a symmetric monoidal left adjoint
L\colon{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
.
Suppose moreover that there exists a map
\widetilde{\nu}\colon\widetilde{T}\to\widetilde{{\mathbb{S}}}
in
{\mathcal{D}}

with
\widetilde{T}
tensor invertible, such that
L(\widetilde{\nu})\simeq\nu
.

Then for all
X\in{\mathcal{E}}
we have
M_{\nu}(X)\cong X[\nu^{-1}]
.

Proof.

We have
M_{\nu}(X)\cong M_{\nu}({\mathbb{S}})\otimes X

and
X[\nu^{-1}]\cong{\mathbb{S}}[\nu^{-1}]\otimes X
(the latter holds since the localization is smashing).
Hence, it suffices to prove the result for
X={\mathbb{S}}
. It is clear that
{\mathbb{S}}\to M_{\nu}({\mathbb{S}})
is sent to an equivalence by the functor
(-)[\nu^{-1}]
. Thus it suffices to prove that
M_{\nu}({\mathbb{S}})
is
\nu
-periodic. For this, as

L(M_{\widetilde{\nu}}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{S}}}))\cong M_{\nu}(L\widetilde{{\mathbb{S}}})\cong M_{\nu}({\mathbb{S}})
, it suffices to prove the statement in
{\mathcal{D}}
, which is compactly generated.
Then the result is
[
Bac18
, Lemma 17]
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

The last lemma holds for example if
{\mathcal{E}}
is compactly generated.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
X\in{\mathcal{E}}
be a compact object such that
M_{\nu}(X)=0
.
Then there exists
n\gg 0
such that
\nu^{n}\colon X\to T^{\otimes-n}\otimes X
is null.

Proof.

Since
X
is compact, we have a filtered colimit of abelian groups

0=\pi_{0}{\operatorname{Map}_{{\mathcal{E}}}({X},{M_{\nu}(X)})}\cong{\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}{\operatorname{colim}}\,}\pi_{0}{\operatorname{Map}_{{\mathcal{E}}}({X},{T^{\otimes-n}\otimes X})}.

In particular, the vanishing of the canonical map
X\to M_{\nu}(X)
is witnessed
on some finite stage, whence we obtain
\nu^{n}\simeq 0
for some
n\gg 0
.
∎

3 Nilpotence of
\eta

Definition 3.1
.

Let
S
be a scheme. The algebraic Hopf map over
S
is the map

\eta\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to{\mathbb{S}}

in
\mathcal{SH}(S)
obtained as the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-desuspension of the quotient map

\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}
.

Remark 3.2
.

By smooth base change, if
f\colon T\to S
is any map of schemes, then
f^{*}\eta\simeq\eta
in
\mathcal{SH}(T)
.

We immediately get the following equivalence between the
\eta
-periodization and the mapping telescope.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
S
be a scheme and
X\in\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
. Then
X[\eta^{-1}]\cong M_{\eta}(X)
.

Proof.

By the last remark, for the canonical left adjoint
f^{*}\colon\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\Spec({\mathbb{Z}}))\to\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
,
we have
f^{*}\eta\simeq\eta
.
Hence, the result follows from
??
,
since
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\Spec({\mathbb{Z}}))
is compactly generated by
??
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
-1
is a sum of squares on a scheme
S
. Then
\eta=0
in
\mathcal{SH}(S)[\frac{1}{2}]
.

Proof.

If
-1
is a sum of
n
squares in
S
, then there is a map of rings

{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/(x_{1}^{2}+\dots x_{n}^{2}+1)\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(S).

Thus, we may assume that
S
is of finite type over
\Spec({\mathbb{Z}})
.
By
[
CD19
, Lemma 16.2.3]
(in
loc. cit.
they invert all primes, but the proof works
verbatim
with only
2
inverted, see their
[
CD19
, Remark 16.2.12]
), there is some idempotent element
\varepsilon\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{SH}(S)[\frac{1}{2}]}({\mathbb{S}}[\frac{1}{2}])
such that
\eta=\varepsilon\eta
.
In particular,
\mathcal{SH}(S)[\frac{1}{2}]\simeq\mathcal{SH}(S)[\frac{1}{2}]^{+}\times\mathcal{SH}(S)[\frac{1}{2}]^{-}
where the
+
part (
resp
. the
-
part) consists of modules over
\mathrm{Im}\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}
(
resp
. modules over
\mathrm{Im}\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}
).
The image of
\eta
in
\mathcal{SH}(S)[\frac{1}{2}]^{+}
is zero, since
\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\eta=\frac{\eta-\eta}{2}=0
. Thus, it suffices to show that

\mathcal{SH}(S)[\frac{1}{2}]^{-}=0
. By
[
CD19
, Proposition 4.3.17]
(that we may apply thanks to
[
AGV22
, Proposition 2.5.11]
) we may assume that
S=\Spec(k)
is the spectrum of a field.

Over a field, the splitting of
\mathcal{SH}(k)[\frac{1}{2}]
is induced by a splitting of the
endomorphisms of the unit
GW(k)[\frac{1}{2}]\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[\frac{1}{2}]\times W(k)[\frac{1}{2}]
, where
GW(k)
and
W(k)

are the Grothendieck-Witt ring and the Witt ring of
k
(see e.g.
[
BH20
, §2.7.3]
).
Hence, it suffices to show that under our assumptions
W(k)
has
2
-power torsion.
This is for example proven in
[
Sch12
, Chapter 2, Theorem 7.1]
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
S=\Spec(\overline{\mathbb{Z}})
be the spectrum of the integral closure of
{\mathbb{Z}}
in
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
The map
\eta^{4}\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\otimes 4}\to{\mathbb{S}}
is null in
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
.

Proof.

We begin with the observation that
S
is étale bounded:
its residual fields are
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and copies of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
for all prime numbers
p
, which,
as they are algebraically closed, have étale cohomological dimension
0
.
Consider the arithmetic fracture square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}^{\wedge}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}[1/2]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}^{\wedge}_{2}[1/2]}

\lrcorner

in
\mathcal{SH}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(S)
(cf. e.g.
[
Mat24
, Corollary 7.3]
).

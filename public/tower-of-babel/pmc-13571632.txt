Predictors of Quality of Life Among Middle-Aged Saudi Women: A Hierarchical Regression Analysis
Authors: Rnda I Ashgar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMiddle-aged women in Saudi Arabia are navigating a significant period of life transition during the rapid socioeconomic shift driven by the Saudi Vision 2030. Evidence indicates that chronic diseases are associated with poor quality of life (QoL), whereas competing employment and family responsibilities may further influence well-being. Psychosocial resources, including life satisfaction, family role satisfaction, and social support, are important predictors of QoL. Although clinical research often focuses on the physiological changes during this stage, the hierarchical influence of sociodemographic, health-related, and psychosocial factors on their QoL remains underexplored. This study aimed to identify the incremental predictors of QoL among middle-aged Saudi women using a hierarchical regression analysis.MethodsThis cross-sectional study included 310 Saudi women aged 40–65 years recruited through online convenience sampling. A three-block hierarchical multiple regression analysis was performed to examine the incremental variance explained by the predictors.ResultsThe hierarchical regression analysis showed that predictors of QoL were multilayered and progressively significant. In the initial step, employment status emerged as a significant positive predictor, although it explained only 1.4% of the variance. The addition of health-related factors in the second step significantly increased the explained variance to 5.6%, identifying the presence of at least one chronic condition as a substantial negative predictor. The final psychosocial block, including self-satisfaction, life satisfaction, family role satisfaction, and social support, contributed to the most dramatic incremental increase, with the full model explaining 43% of the total variance in QoL (R2=0.43, F=22.42, p<0.001).ConclusionPsychosocial variables demonstrated the strongest association with QoL and accounted for the largest incremental increase in explained variance. These findings underscore the necessity for nursing and public health interventions to transition toward a holistic, patient-centered model that prioritizes psychosocial factors.Keywords: quality of life, middle-aged women, Saudi Arabia, hierarchical regression, social support, chronic disease, vision 2030

Introduction

Quality of life (QoL) has emerged as a central construct in public health and social science research, reflecting individuals’ perceptions of their position in life within the context of their culture, value systems, goals, expectations, and concerns.1 QoL is particularly important among middle-aged women due to the complex interplay between biological transitions, evolving social roles, and increasing health risks that characterize this life stage.2–5 In Saudi Arabia, rapid socioeconomic transformation, urbanization, and shifts in gender roles, accelerated by the Saudi Vision 2030, have further reshaped women’s lived experiences, making it essential to understand the predictors influencing their well-being.6

Middle age, typically defined as the period between 40 and 65 years, is often accompanied by physiological changes, such as menopause, increased susceptibility to chronic conditions, and shifts in functional capacity.7,8 Moreover, women at this stage frequently navigate multiple responsibilities, including caregiving, employment, and maintaining family cohesion.6,9 Many women in this age group belong to the so-called “sandwich generation”, simultaneously caring for their children and supporting their aging parents, often while continuing to work.10,11 These overlapping demands can have both positive and negative implications for QoL depending on the availability of resources and coping mechanisms.12,13

Sociodemographic factors are among the most consistently reported predictors of QoL. Variables such as age, marital status, educational attainment, employment status, and income level shape access to resources, health literacy, and social opportunities.12–14 In the Saudi context, employment has been increasingly recognized as an empowering factor that enhances autonomy and life satisfaction among women by providing not only financial independence but also a sense of professional fulfillment. However, it may simultaneously exacerbate work-family conflicts and psychological strain in traditional Saudi households, thereby offsetting potential QoL gains.6 Conversely, lower socioeconomic status or unemployment may restrict participation in social activities and increase vulnerability to stress, thereby negatively affecting QoL.15

In addition, health-related factors play a critical role in determining QoL. The presence of chronic diseases is a significant concern; for instance, conditions such as hypertension and diabetes are prevalent and may significantly impair physical functioning and psychological health.16 Furthermore, the burden of managing these conditions, specifically the daily use of at least one medication, negatively affects health-related QoL.17–19 Studies indicate that as the number of chronic illnesses increases, there is a corresponding decrease in physical and mental health scores.16,20

In addition to sociodemographic and health-related predictors, psychosocial factors have gained increasing attention as key contributors to QoL. Social support, defined as the perceived availability of emotional, informational, and instrumental assistance from social networks, is a well-established protective factor that enhances resilience among middle-aged women.10,21–23 In collectivist societies such as Saudi Arabia, family and community ties are particularly influential, making social support a critical component of women’s life satisfaction and psychological stability.9

Life satisfaction, which represents the cognitive evaluation of one’s overall life circumstances, is another important dimension of QoL.24 Higher levels of life satisfaction are associated with better mental health and a more positive outlook on personal growth.25,26 Similarly, self-satisfaction, which reflects individuals’ appraisal of their personal achievements and self-worth, contributes to emotional well-being and a sense of optimism.27 Women who perceive themselves as competent and fulfilled in their personal identities are more likely to report higher QoL.1

Family role satisfaction is an additional psychosocial factor of particular relevance in the Saudi cultural context, where family remains a central social institution, although competing expectations in this setting may generate strain that undercuts well-being.6 Satisfaction with one’s roles within the family, such as being a mother, spouse, or caregiver, can significantly influence emotional health and overall life satisfaction.6,9 Positive perceptions of family roles are associated with a sense of purpose and social integration, whereas role strain and conflict may lead to stress and diminished QoL.6,9

Despite increasing evidence, the relative contributions of these factors to QoL among middle-aged women in the Kingdom remain insufficiently examined using multivariable analytical approaches. Hierarchical regression analysis offers a robust framework for understanding the incremental explanatory power of these domains, allowing researchers to identify the most salient predictors of QoL, while controlling for confounding variables.

Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the predictors of QoL among middle-aged Saudi women by examining the hierarchical effects of sociodemographic characteristics, health-related factors, and psychosocial variables, including social support, life satisfaction, self-satisfaction, and family role satisfaction. Understanding these relationships is essential for developing targeted interventions and policies aimed at improving the well-being of this population in alignment with national transformation goals.

The sequential ordering of the three predictor blocks was guided by a distal-to-proximal rationale, consistent with socioecological models of health predictors. Sociodemographic characteristics were entered first because they represent structural background factors, such as employment and education, which shape women’s access to resources and their exposure to health risks.14 Health-related factors were entered second as a more proximal biomedical layer reflecting current clinical status, given the well-documented negative association between chronic conditions and QoL.18 Psychosocial variables were entered last because, in line with the middle-range theory of health and personal satisfaction in midlife, they constitute the most proximal and modifiable psychological mechanisms through which distal sociodemographic and health-related influences ultimately translate into perceived well-being.10 This theory-driven sequence enables quantification of the incremental variance contributed by each block, establishing whether psychosocial resources explain QoL beyond sociodemographic and health-related predictors, and directly underpins the three research hypotheses below.

Research hypothesis 1: Sociodemographic factors (social status, educational attainment, and employment status) are positively associated with QoL among middle-aged Saudi women.

Research hypothesis 2: Health-related factors (menopausal status, presence of chronic diseases, and daily medication use) are negatively associated with QoL.

Research hypothesis 3: Psychosocial factors (social support, life satisfaction, self-satisfaction, and family role satisfaction) are positively associated with QoL, and this block is expected to explain the significant additional variance in QoL over and above the sociodemographic and health-related blocks.

Methodology

Study Design

This study adopted a cross-sectional descriptive-analytical design to examine the predictors of QoL among middle-aged Saudi women. This design is particularly well suited for identifying the relationships between sociodemographic, health-related, and psychosocial variables at a specific point in time. By employing an analytical framework, this study seeks to quantify the relative influence of these factors on women’s well-being during midlife transition.

Sampling

The sampling strategy employed a convenience sampling technique to access a diverse pool of middle-aged women, ensuring a broad representation across different socioeconomic strata and geographical regions. A sample size of 310 participants was targeted to ensure statistical power in detecting significant relationships between the variables of interest. This power calculation was performed a priori using G*Power 3.1 software, assuming a moderate effect size (f2 = 0.15) for the hierarchical multiple regression (as per Cohen’s conventions), with a desired power of 0.80, alpha = 0.05, and up to 21 predictors across the sociodemographic, health-related, and psychosocial blocks. This configuration yielded a minimum sample size of 300, with the targeted 310 participants providing a conservative buffer against potential data loss or minor deviations in assumptions, thereby ensuring adequate statistical power to detect meaningful incremental variance in predictors of QoL.

Participants were recruited between January and March 2026 through WhatsApp and text messages (SMS) using invitations containing a link to the online questionnaire. The inclusion criteria specified women between the ages of 40 and 65 years, ensuring that the focus remained on the middle-aged demographic. All the participants provided informed consent prior to participation. Individuals with known cognitive impairment and those currently undergoing treatment for severe chronic diseases, such as cancer or mental health disorders, were excluded from the study.

Data Collection

Data were collected using a structured self-administered online questionnaire consisting of three main sections.

Sociodemographic and Health-Related Information

This section collected data on employment status, educational status, social status, menstruation status, presence of chronic diseases (eg, hypertension, diabetes), and medication use (taking at least one daily medication). All the variables were measured on nominal or ordinal scales to provide categorical data for analysis. Menopausal status was determined based on self-reported menstrual cycle regularity; an absence of menstruation for three or more months was classified as post-menopause.

Psychosocial Factors

This section measured social support, life satisfaction, self-satisfaction, and family role satisfaction. Perceived social support was assessed using the validated Arabic version of the Multidimensional Scale of Perceived Social Support, which evaluates support from family, friends, and significant others.28 The Multidimensional Scale of Perceived Social Support consists of 12 items rated on a 7-point Likert scale, with higher scores indicating greater perceived social support.28 This scale has been widely employed across diverse populations and exhibits strong psychometric properties and cross-cultural adaptability.

Life satisfaction was measured using the validated Arabic version of the Satisfaction with Life Scale to capture cognitive appraisals of well-being.29 This scale, comprising five items rated on a 7-point Likert scale, assesses global life satisfaction and has demonstrated robust reliability and validity across various cultural contexts.29

Self-satisfaction was evaluated using the validated Arabic version of the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale, a 10-item scale that assesses global self-worth and self-acceptance, with responses ranging from strongly disagree to strongly agree.30 The validity and reliability of the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale have been consistently established in numerous studies, confirming its suitability for Saudi participants (Aldaqal, S. M., and Sehlo, M. G., 2013).

Family role satisfaction was assessed using a single-item, study-developed measure designed to capture women’s overall perceived competence in performing multiple concurrent roles during midlife. This item was developed for the present study in accordance with the role-related satisfaction construct articulated in the guiding middle-range theory of health and personal satisfaction in midlife, given the absence of a brief, validated single-item instrument that captures women’s cross-domain role functioning during this transitional life stage. The item was administered in Arabic and presented to participants verbatim as follows: “Overall, how would you rate your ability to perform your usual family roles and responsibilities over the past four weeks?” with the five response anchors listed below the question and selected by the participant: Very poor, Poor, Fair, Good, and Excellent. Participants were instructed to choose the single response that best reflected their perceived role functioning over the preceding four weeks. Therefore, the item is consistent in its temporal framing (four-week recall window) with the WHOQOL-BREF domain items administered in the same questionnaire; the five-point ordinal response scale was used in the analysis as a categorical predictor with “ Excellent” set as the reference category, consistent with the directional relationship in which greater reported competence in role functioning was expected to be associated with higher QoL.

Quality of Life

The dependent variable was assessed using the validated Arabic version of the World Health Organization Quality of Life (WHOQOL) Brief Version. The WHOQOL-BREF questionnaire provides a reliable and valid measure of individuals’ subjective ratings of their personal health and well-being across physical, psychological, social, and environmental domains, making it particularly suitable for capturing comprehensive health outcomes in middle-aged women. The instrument, comprising 26 items, generates four domain scores (physical health, psychological health, social relationships, and environment) and two overall scores (overall QoL and general health perception).31 The overall WHOQOL-BREF score was calculated as the mean of all 26 item scores, linearly transformed to a 0–100 scale, in accordance with the instrument’s published scoring guidance.31 The validity and reliability of the WHOQOL-BREF have been extensively demonstrated across diverse cultural and linguistic contexts, affirming its cross-cultural applicability in assessing QoL in Saudi women.32,33

The internal consistency of the scales used in this study was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha. Most instruments demonstrated acceptable to excellent reliability. The Life Satisfaction scale (α =0.94), the Multidimensional Scale of Perceived Social Support (α =0.97), and the overall WHOQOL-BREF (α =0.90) showed particularly high internal consistency. Furthermore, the psychological, social, and environmental health subscales of the WHOQOL-BREF exhibited satisfactory reliability, with alpha coefficients of.72,0.84, and.73, respectively. While the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale and the WHOQOL-BREF physical health subscale yielded lower Cronbach’s alpha values of.67, these figures are considered acceptable in social science research, especially within exploratory frameworks.

Ethical Consideration

This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Before data collection, ethical approval was obtained from Jazan University’s Local Committee for Research Ethics (reference number: REC-47/06/1719). All the participants were informed of the study’s purpose, the voluntary nature of their participation, and their right to withdraw at any time without penalties. Informed consent was obtained electronically before the survey began. To ensure confidentiality and anonymity, no personally identifiable information was collected, and all data were stored securely and used only for research purposes.

Data Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 27.0). Descriptive statistics, including frequencies, percentages, means, and standard deviations, were computed to summarize the sociodemographic and health-related characteristics of the study sample.

Bivariate analyses were performed to examine preliminary associations between the study variables. Pearson’s correlation coefficients were used to assess relationships between the continuous variables, while independent samples t-tests and ANOVA were conducted to compare mean QoL scores across categorical independent variables.

To address the primary research objective, hierarchical multiple regression analysis was employed to examine the predictors of QoL and assess the incremental contribution of different sets of predictors. Only variables that demonstrated statistical significance in the bivariate analyses were included in the regression model. The analysis was performed in three sequential blocks.

●Step 2: Health-related factors (chronic disease status and medication use) were added to evaluate their additional contribution to the variance in QoL.

●Step 3: Psychosocial factors (social support, life satisfaction, self-satisfaction, and family role satisfaction) were entered to determine the extent to which subjective well-being variables explained QoL beyond sociodemographic and health-related factors.

Prior to performing the regression analysis, diagnostic tests were performed to verify whether the assumptions of multiple regression were satisfied, including normality, linearity, homoscedasticity, and absence of multicollinearity. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

In Step 1, the sociodemographic factor of employment status was entered into the model. This initial step was significant (F=4.39, p=0.037), explaining 1.4% of the variance in QoL scores (R2=0.014). Employment status emerged as a positive predictor (β=0.12, p=0.037), suggesting that being in the workforce is associated with higher perceived QoL among Saudi women.

In Step 2, health-related factors, having at least one chronic condition and taking at least one medication daily, were added to the model. This step resulted in a significant increase in the explained variance (F=6.03, p<0.001, R2=0.056), indicating that physical health status is a substantial predictor of well-being in midlife. The presence of at least one chronic condition was a significant negative predictor (β=–.20, p=0.00), while taking medication daily was not significant (β=–.01, p=0.853). Employment status remained significant (β=0.11, p=0.043). Women with chronic conditions reported significantly lower QoL scores than their healthy counterparts.

In Step 3, psychosocial factors—including family role satisfaction (with “Excellent” as the reference category), life satisfaction, self-satisfaction, and social support—were entered into the final model. The final model was statistically significant (F = 22.42, p <0.001) and explained 43% of the variance in QoL (R2 =0.43), indicating a substantial contribution of psychosocial factors to QoL among middle-aged Saudi women.

Life satisfaction (β =0.27, p <0.001), self-satisfaction (β =0.24, p <0.001), and perceived social support (β =0.21, p <0.001) emerged as significant positive predictors of QoL, with life satisfaction demonstrating the strongest standardized association. Relative to the “Excellent” family role satisfaction category, women reporting low (β = −0.15, p =0.002), good (β = −0.18, p <0.001), and very good (β = −0.12, p =0.019) family role satisfaction had significantly lower QoL scores, whereas the “Very low” category was not statistically significant (β =0.02, p =0.664).

Although employment status was no longer significant in the final model, having at least one chronic condition was negatively associated with QoL. Overall, the findings suggest that psychosocial variables, particularly life satisfaction, self-satisfaction, family role satisfaction, and social support, were among the most important correlates of QoL in this sample of middle-aged Saudi women.

Discussion

This study used a hierarchical regression framework to evaluate the predictors of QoL among middle-aged Saudi women. By systematically adding sociodemographic, health-related, and psychosocial blocks, this study provides a nuanced understanding of the factors that contribute to well-being during this transitional life stage. The primary finding that psychosocial factors, particularly social support and satisfaction domains, account for the largest proportion of variance in QoL underscores the critical role of holistic health models in the Saudi context.20,34 This comprehensive approach aligns with the contemporary understanding that QoL extends beyond the mere absence of disease and encompasses psychological, social, and environmental dimensions.

The Impact of Employment on Quality of Life

In the first step of the regression analysis, employment status emerged as a significant positive predictor of QoL and was significantly associated with QoL in the bivariate analysis. However, its effect became non-significant in the final model after psychosocial variables were introduced (β =0.06, p =0.221), suggesting that the relationship between employment and QoL was attenuated after adjustment for psychosocial factors rather than being attributable to employment alone.

For middle-aged women, employment may extend beyond financial benefits to provide a sense of identity, autonomy, social connectedness, and purpose, all of which are important during the midlife transition. Many women in this age group are part of the “sandwich generation”, simultaneously caring for their children and supporting their aging parents.35,36 These competing caregiving demands can increase emotional and physical strain; however, engagement in meaningful work may foster self-efficacy, personal growth, and opportunities for social interaction and lifelong learning, which can enhance psychological well-being.35 Nevertheless, the disappearance of employment status as a significant predictor in the final regression model indicates that psychosocial factors may play a more central role in determining QoL than employment. This suggests that the benefits of employment for middle-aged women may be closely tied to psychosocial resources, such as enhanced social support, resilience, self-esteem, or perceived meaning in life, although formal mediation could not be tested with the present cross-sectional design.

The Burden of Chronic Disease and Medication

The initial bivariate analysis demonstrated that health-related factors, particularly the presence of chronic diseases and daily medication use, were associated with lower QoL. These findings are consistent with those of previous research showing that an increasing burden of chronic illness is linked to declines in both the physical and psychological dimensions of health-related QoL.16,19 Middle age is often accompanied by physiological changes and an increased risk of chronic health conditions, which may negatively affect daily functioning, emotional well-being, and overall life satisfaction. Accordingly, interventions focusing on chronic disease prevention, effective symptom management, and health promotion may play an essential role in improving the QoL of middle-aged women.

An important finding of the present study is that the association between daily medication use and QoL observed in the initial bivariate analysis was no longer statistically significant after adjusting for chronic conditions and employment status in Step 2 of the hierarchical regression analysis. This attenuation suggests that the bivariate association between daily medication use and QoL may be attributable, at least in part, to differences in underlying health conditions and sociodemographic characteristics rather than to medication use itself. Thus, medication use may serve as an indicator of underlying health burden rather than an independent predictor of QoL.

However, the impact of chronic conditions on QoL should not be understood solely in terms of clinical stability or disease control. Even when symptoms are medically managed, the ongoing demands of medication adherence, concerns about long-term treatment, and limitations in physical functioning may continue to shape women’s daily experiences and perceptions of well-being.17,18 These findings highlight the importance of adopting a holistic approach to healthcare that incorporates psychological and social dimensions along with biomedical management when addressing QoL in middle-aged women.

The Dominance of Psychosocial and Satisfaction Domains

The final and most influential block in the regression model consisted of the psychosocial factors that contributed to the largest incremental increase in the explained variance in QoL. Among these variables, social support emerged as a major positive factor, consistent with previous findings that support from family and friends is associated with lower psychological distress and better coping with life stressors within the Saudi sociocultural context.9,34 In collectivist societies such as Saudi Arabia, strong interpersonal relationships and cohesive family networks are closely linked to resilience, emotional well-being, and overall life satisfaction.34,37

Family role satisfaction was also significantly positively associated with QoL, reflecting the cultural importance of family cohesion, caregiving, and kinship roles in Saudi society.6 For many middle-aged women, feeling competent, appreciated, and fulfilling their familial responsibilities as mothers, spouses, and caregivers may enhance their sense of meaning, identity, and social belonging, thereby positively influencing QoL.6,9 This finding highlights the extent to which the subjective perceptions of role fulfillment, rather than role demands alone, shape women’s well-being during midlife.

In addition, self-satisfaction and overall life satisfaction emerged as two of the strongest predictors in the final model. This suggests that subjective cognitive and emotional evaluations of life circumstances may have a greater influence on QoL than objective demographic or health-related indicators.1,34 Women who perceive themselves as successful, emotionally fulfilled, and satisfied with their lives are more likely to report higher levels of well-being, even in the presence of chronic health conditions or age-related challenges.38,39 These findings underscore the importance of psychological resilience, positive self-perception, and meaningful social relationships as central predictors of QoL in middle-aged women.

Nevertheless, a methodological caveat should be acknowledged before interpreting these findings as evidence of independent effects. First, the WHOQOL-BREF assesses QoL across physical, psychological, social, and environmental domains, with overall scores derived from all 26 items. In particular, the psychological and social domains share conceptual content with the psychosocial predictors examined here, most notably between self-satisfaction (assessed using the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale) and the psychological domain, and between perceived social support and the social relationships domain. Accordingly, the substantial contribution of the psychosocial block may partly reflect shared conceptual content between the psychosocial measures and QoL domains, rather than an entirely independent effect. Second, it should be recognized that the increase in R2 attributed to the psychosocial block depended on the order in which the blocks were entered into the hierarchical regression. The psychosocial variables were entered after the sociodemographic and health-related blocks; therefore, the increment they explained represented variance remaining after those earlier blocks had been accounted for. Had the blocks been entered in a different sequence, the variance partition would have differed accordingly. The chosen order was theoretically motivated; sociodemographic and health status were treated as more distal determinants, and psychosocial resources and satisfaction as more proximal determinants of QoL. However, the reported increments should be interpreted within this specified sequence rather than as invariant properties of the predictors. Taken together, these considerations argue against overstating the independent contribution of the psychosocial variables, although the clinical relevance of the findings remains: psychosocial resources are strongly associated with middle-aged women’s perceived well-being, and their role in shaping QoL in this population warrants continued attention.

Implications in Nursing Practice

The findings of this study have several critical implications for nursing practice, particularly for those working in primary care and community health settings in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. First, the dominance of psychosocial factors as predictors of QoL necessitates a shift toward a more holistic, patient-centered nursing assessment. Given that life satisfaction, self-satisfaction, family role satisfaction, and perceived social support were the most influential predictors of well-being, nurses should integrate validated psychosocial screening tools into their routine practice.11,34 This approach allows for early identification of women who may lack adequate social support or experience role-related strain, particularly those in the “sandwich generation” who balance demanding caregiving and professional responsibilities.35,36 By recognizing these psychosocial vulnerabilities, nurses can provide targeted counseling and facilitate referrals to social services and community support networks.9,34

Furthermore, the significant negative impact of chronic diseases on QoL highlights the need for advanced nursing-led chronic disease management programs. With the prevalence of obesity among Saudi females reported at 33.5% and its subsequent link to conditions such as hypertension and diabetes, nurses must prioritize health promotion and lifestyle modification interventions.1,11 These interventions should go beyond clinical monitoring to address the psychological distress associated with having at least one chronic disease, as observed in our findings. Nurses can play a pivotal role in providing medication education and supportive counseling to help women manage the demands associated with long-term medication use and address related psychological distress.17,18 Strengthening women’s health literacy and engagement in health-promoting behaviors, such as regular physical activity, directly improves both physical functioning and overall life satisfaction.1,20

Finally, the findings underscore the importance of culturally sensitive nursing care that acknowledges the central role of the family in Saudi Arabia. As satisfaction with family roles was a significant predictor of QoL, nursing interventions should be designed to include family members in the care plan and foster a supportive environment that enhances the patient’s sense of belonging and purpose.6,9 In alignment with Saudi Vision 2030 and the National Transformation Programs, nursing leadership should advocate for policies that improve women’s access to comprehensive wellness programs.6 By addressing the hierarchical predictors of QoL, from employment support to the management of multimorbidity, nursing professionals can significantly contribute to the long-term well-being and resilience of middle-aged Saudi women.11,20

Strengths and Limitations

A major strength of this study is the use of a hierarchical regression approach, which enabled a systematic examination of the incremental contributions of sociodemographic, health-related, and psychosocial factors to QoL in middle-aged women. This analytical strategy allowed identifying the relative influence of different predictor domains while controlling for potential confounding variables. By entering variables in sequential blocks, this study demonstrated the dominant contribution of psychosocial factors beyond demographic and health-related characteristics, thereby providing a more comprehensive understanding of QoL predictors.

Another important strength is the multidimensional assessment of QoL predictors through the inclusion of both objective health-related indicators, such as chronic disease status and daily medication use, and subjective psychosocial variables, including self-satisfaction, life satisfaction, social support, and family role satisfaction. This integrative approach offers a culturally contextualized perspective on women’s well-being in Saudi society, where family relationships, social connectedness, and personal fulfillment are closely intertwined with perceptions of QoL. In addition, this study addresses a timely and socially relevant topic in light of the National Transformation Programs and Saudi Vision 2030, which emphasize women’s empowerment, health promotion, and social participation.

Despite these strengths, some limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, the cross-sectional design limited the ability to establish causal or temporal relationships among the variables. Consequently, it cannot be determined whether higher psychosocial satisfaction leads to improved QoL or whether women with better QoL are more likely to report greater satisfaction and social support. Longitudinal studies are needed to clarify the directionality of these relationships over time.

Second, participants were recruited using convenience sampling through online platforms, which may have introduced selection bias. Women who are more educated, technologically literate, reside in urban dwellings, or have higher socioeconomic status may have been more likely to participate, potentially limiting the generalizability of the findings to women living in rural areas or those with limited Internet access. Furthermore, the generalizability of the findings to women in other regions may be restricted because most participants were recruited from the southern region of Saudi Arabia.

In addition, the use of self-administered questionnaires may have increased the risk of recall bias, social desirability bias, or common method bias, particularly regarding self-reported health conditions, medication use, and psychosocial experiences. Despite the multivariate adjustment performed in the analysis, the potential influence of unmeasured confounding variables cannot be ruled out. Factors not assessed in the present study—such as household income, health behaviors, cultural expectations, and access to healthcare services—may account for part of the observed associations.

Another limitation relates to the measurement of certain constructs. Family role satisfaction was assessed using a single-item measure, which may not have fully captured the complex and multidimensional nature of women’s family experiences and role expectations. Furthermore, other potentially important predictors of QoL, such as spirituality, religious coping, physical activity, menopausal symptoms, and access to healthcare services, were not examined in the present study.

Finally, the exclusion of women with severe chronic illnesses or major psychiatric disorders may limit the applicability of the findings to more medically vulnerable populations. Future research using longitudinal designs, larger and more diverse samples, and stratified random sampling techniques should validate these findings and explore how QoL evolves as women transition into older adulthood.

Conclusion

In conclusion, among the study participants, QoL was associated with a multidimensional combination of sociodemographic characteristics, health-related conditions, and psychosocial factors. Although employment status, chronic health conditions, and daily medication use were associated with QoL, psychosocial variables—particularly social support, family role satisfaction, self-satisfaction, and overall life satisfaction—showed the strongest associations with QoL in the final regression model. These findings emphasize that subjective appraisals of well-being and perceived social support were more strongly associated with QoL than objective health or demographic indicators alone. The results underscore the importance of adopting a holistic and culturally sensitive approach to promoting women’s well-being in Saudi Arabia. Interventions aimed at supporting women living with chronic conditions or taking daily medications and strengthening social support networks and satisfaction with their life, self, and family roles may contribute to improved physical and psychological well-being among middle-aged women. Equally important is the strengthening of formal and informal social support systems, including family-centered and community-based initiatives that foster emotional support, social connectedness, and role fulfillment. This recommendation is particularly pertinent to the many women in this age group who belong to the “sandwich generation”, simultaneously caring for their children and supporting their aging parents, often while continuing in paid employment. As these dual caregiving demands may be accompanied by role strain and competing responsibilities, family-centered support initiatives are not only beneficial but also essential.

These findings are particularly relevant in the context of Saudi Vision 2030 and the National Transformation Programs, which prioritize women’s empowerment, health promotion, and social participation as essential components of national development. Collaborative efforts among healthcare professionals, policymakers, and community organizations are needed to address the multidimensional predictors of QoL and create supportive environments that enable middle-aged women to effectively balance their health, family, and societal roles. However, these findings should be interpreted in light of the study’s limitations, particularly its cross-sectional design and convenience sampling, which limited the ability to establish causal relationships and may reduce the generalizability of the findings to the broader population of middle-aged Saudi women. Future research should employ longitudinal and mixed-methods designs to better understand the causal pathways and lived experiences associated with QoL among middle-aged Saudi women. In addition, studies using larger and more diverse samples are needed to enhance the generalizability of the findings across different socioeconomic and regional populations in Saudi Arabia.

Angelesco and AT systems on the unit circle
Rostyslav Kozhan 1, Marcus Vaktnäs 1

Abstract
. We introduce the concept of Laurent multiple orthogonality on the unit circle and define Angelesco and AT systems in this setting. Using a generalized Andreief identity, we establish normality of all multi-indices for any such system, thereby ensuring existence and uniqueness of Laurent multiple orthogonal polynomials of type I and type II at every location. As an application, we demonstrate existence and uniqueness of the approximants for two natural two-point Hermite-Padé problems – type I and type II – arising in the simultaneous rational approximation of r r Carathéodory functions.



1.
Introduction

Given a system of probability measures
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})
on the real line and a multi-index
\bm{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
(where
\mathbb{N}=\left\{0,1,2,\dots\right\}
), multiple orthogonal polynomials on the real line are defined as the univariate polynomials
P_{\bm{n}}(x)
of degree at most
\left|\bm{n}\right|:=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{r}
that satisfy simultaneous orthogonality conditions

\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{\bm{n}}(x)x^{k}d\mu_{j}(x)=0,\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,n_{j}-1,

(1)

for each
j=1,\ldots,r
. If
r=1
then the polynomials
P_{\bm{n}}(x)
reduce to the usual orthogonal polynomials associated with a single measure on the real line.

Such polynomials arise naturally as solutions to the Hermite–Padé approximation problem: given
r
Markov functions (i.e., Cauchy/Stieltjes transforms of the measures
\mu_{j}
)

f_{j}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\mu_{j}(x)}{z-x},

find a polynomial
P_{\bm{n}}
of degree
\leq|\bm{n}|
and polynomials
Q_{\bm{n},j}
of degrees
\leq|\bm{n}|-1
(
j=1,\ldots,r
) such that

P_{\bm{n}}(z)f_{j}(z)-Q_{\bm{n},j}(z)=\mathcal{O}(z^{-n_{j}-1}),\qquad z\to\infty,

(2)

simultaneously for each
j=1,\ldots,r
. For more details, see, e.g.,
[
bookNS
,
VAPade
]
.

Today, the theory of multiple orthogonal polynomials on the real line is a very well-developed subject with applications not only in approximation theory but also in spectral theory, random matrix theory, and integrable probability (see
[
Applications
]
for a short introduction, and
[
Aptekarev
,
Ismail
]
for more comprehensive treatments). One of the fundamental questions in this theory is whether the
monic
polynomial satisfying (
1
) is unique. In that case, its degree equals
|\bm{n}|
, and the index
\bm{n}
is said to be normal. Unlike the case
r=1
, normality does not hold in general.

Nevertheless, there are two broad classes of systems for which every multi-index
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is normal. The first class consists of systems of measures whose supports lie on pairwise disjoint intervals. These are called Angelesco systems
[
Angelesco
]
. The second class consists of systems of measures known as AT (algebraic Chebyshev) systems, in which the measures are absolutely continuous with respect to some fixed measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
and whose Radon–Nikodym derivatives satisfy certain determinantal conditions (see, e.g.,
[
Ismail
, Sect 23.1.2]
for further details). The vast majority of explicit systems of multiple orthogonal polynomials that are studied in the literature belong to these two classes (which also include Nikishin systems).

In this paper we study multiple orthogonality on the complex unit circle.
Let
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})
be a system of probability measures on
\partial\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
and
\bm{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. Multiple orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle were introduced by Mínguez and Van Assche in
[
MOPUC1
]
as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a multi-index
\bm{n}
is
\Phi
-normal with respect to
\bm{\mu}
if there exists a unique monic polynomial
\Phi_{\bm{n}}
(called the type II multiple orthogonal polynomial)
of the form

\Phi_{\bm{n}}(z)=z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}+k_{\left|\bm{n}\right|-1}z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-1}+\ldots+k_{0},

(3)

which satisfies the orthogonality relations

\int_{\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}}}\Phi_{\bm{n}}(z)z^{-k}d\mu_{j}(z)=0,\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,n_{j}-1,\qquad j=1,\dots,r.

(4)

In
[
MOPUC1
,
MOPUC2
,
KVMOPUC
]
, properties of such polynomials were established, including recurrence relations, compatibility conditions, a Christoffel–Darboux formula, and the associated Riemann–Hilbert and Hermite–Padé problems. We describe the latter, as introduced in
[
MOPUC1
]
. Given
r
Carathéodory functions, defined by

F_{j}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}}}\frac{w+z}{w-z}d\mu_{j}(w),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}},

(5)

the polynomials
\Phi_{\bm{n}}
together with certain polynomials
\Psi_{\bm{n},j}
of degree
\leq|\bm{n}|
(
j=1,\ldots,r
) solve

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{n}}(z)F_{j}(z)+\Psi_{\bm{n},j}(z)=\mathcal{O}(z^{n_{j}}),\qquad

\displaystyle z\to 0,

(6)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{n}}(z)F_{j}(z)+\Psi_{\bm{n},j}(z)=\mathcal{O}(z^{-1}),\qquad

\displaystyle z\to\infty,

(7)

for each
j=1,\ldots,r
. This approximation problem is called
two-point
, as it involves approximating at
0
and at
\infty
simultaneously.

One of the main limitations of
the theory was
the lack of examples of
\Phi
-perfect systems, i.e.,
systems for which every
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is
\Phi
-normal.
To the best of our knowledge, the only such example was given in
[
MOPUC2
, Sect. 3]
, namely
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
, where
\mu_{1}
is the orthogonality measure of the Rogers–Szegő polynomials and
\mu_{2}
is the Lebesgue measure.

In pursuit of more examples, we propose to work with Laurent orthogonality. This leads to the following definition, for which we are able to generate a large class of systems where we have normality at every location.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that a multi-index
\bm{n}
is
\phi
-normal with respect to
\bm{\mu}
if
there exists a unique function of the form

\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)=z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}+\kappa_{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2-1}z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2-1}+\ldots+\kappa_{-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}z^{-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2},

(8)

which satisfies the orthogonality relations

\int\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)z^{-k}d\mu_{j}(z)=0,\quad k=-n_{j}/2,-n_{j}/2+1,\ldots,n_{j}/2-1.

(9)

Note that if
r=1
, then
\phi_{n}(z)
is simply
z^{-n/2}\Phi_{n}(z)
, so that
\phi_{n}
and
\Phi_{n}
carry the same information. If
r\geq 2
, the relationship between
\phi_{\bm{n}}
and
\Phi_{\bm{n}}
becomes highly non-trivial.

The main result of our paper
is that every multi-index
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is
\phi
-normal for two wide classes of systems: Angelesco systems, where the supports of the measures are pair-wise disjoint (see Section
4
), and AT systems (see Section
5
). In a companion paper
[
KNik
]
, we introduce Nikishin systems on the unit circle and show that they satisfy the AT property whenever
\bm{n}
has same-parity components with
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}
or with
r=2
.

Just like
\Phi_{\bm{n}}
, the function
\phi_{\bm{n}}
appears naturally as a solution to a two-point Hermite–Padé problem. For the case of
n_{j}
’s even, it takes the form

\displaystyle\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)F_{j}(z)+\psi_{\bm{n},j}(z)=\mathcal{O}(z^{n_{j}/2}),\qquad

\displaystyle z\rightarrow 0,

(10)

\displaystyle\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)F_{j}(z)+\psi_{\bm{n},j}(z)=\mathcal{O}(z^{-n_{j}/2-1}),\qquad

\displaystyle z\rightarrow\infty,

(11)

(compare with (
6
)–(
7
)). In particular, if
\bm{\mu}
is any Angelesco or AT system, then we show that the solution of this problem is unique, up to a multiplicative normalization.

It can be argued that the functions
\phi_{2\bm{n}}
are particularly well-behaving. Since every component
n_{j}
is even, they are all Laurent polynomials, and the orthogonality conditions are all with respect to integer powers. The Hermite–Padé problem also takes a simpler form in this case.
For the AT systems, however, it seems more natural to consider
\phi
’s at the locations
with all odd components
n_{j}
, as such multi-indices correspond to the periodic AT property, see
[
KreNud
]
. It is therefore particularly striking that any multi-index, with an arbitrary combination of even and odd components, is
\phi
-normal for any Angelesco and any AT system.

In subsequent work
[
KVHP
]
, we study a related, though distinct from (
8
)–(
9
), setting of Laurent multiple orthogonality on the unit circle. In that framework, we establish Szegő-type recurrence relations and compatibility conditions for the associated recurrence coefficients, derive Christoffel–Darboux formulas and Heine-type determinantal representations, and ultimately obtain extensions of the Szegő mapping and the Geronimus relations. Independently of our work, a recent manuscript
[
HueMan
]
investigates Laurent multiple orthogonality from yet another perspective. Within a broader framework of mixed orthogonality, the authors analyze recurrence relations, Christoffel–Darboux formulas, and spectral transformations.

The organization of our paper is as follows. In Section
2
we define the type I and type II multiple orthogonal functions
\phi_{\bm{n}}
,
\bm{\xi}_{\bm{n}}
, along with their “reversed” versions
\phi^{\sharp}_{\bm{n}}
,
\bm{\xi}^{\sharp}_{\bm{n}}
. Section
3

contains some technical determinantal results, featuring a generalization of the Andreief identity, which is our main tool for showing normality. In Section
4
we define Angelesco systems on the unit circle and prove their perfectness, while in Section
5
we do the same for the AT systems on the unit circle. In Section
6
we describe the two-point Hermite–Padé problem that is equivalent to the type II
\phi
-orthogonality, and in Section
7
we do the same for type I.

3.
Two Determinantal Identities

In our proof of normality for Angelesco and AT systems in Sections
4
and
5
, we rely on the following determinantal formula, which can be viewed as a generalization of the Andreief (or continuous Cauchy–Binet) identity. Its proof follows similar lines to that of the standard Andreief identity. The block form of the formula is particularly well suited for the multiple orthogonality setting, whether on the unit circle or the real line, as further demonstrated in
[
KNik
,
KVNikInt
]
. See also
[
CouVA
,
Kui
]
for the related normality proofs on the real line.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f_{j},g_{j}\in L^{2}(\mu)
for some probability measure on a measure space
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
. Then for any
N\geq M\geq 1
and any
(N-M)\times N
matrix
\bm{A}
:

\det\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\begin{pmatrix}\int_{X}f_{j}(x)g_{k}(x)\,d\mu(x)\end{pmatrix}_{1\leq j\leq M,1\leq k\leq N}\\[3.99994pt]
\hline\cr[0.5pt/2pt]\\[-10.00002pt]
\bm{A}\end{array}\right)=\\
\tfrac{1}{M!}\int_{X^{M}}\det\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\begin{pmatrix}g_{k}(x_{j})\end{pmatrix}_{1\leq j\leq M,1\leq k\leq N}\\[3.99994pt]
\hline\cr[0.5pt/2pt]\\[-10.00002pt]
\bm{A}\end{array}\right)\det\left(f_{l}(x_{j})\right)_{1\leq l,j\leq M}\,d^{M}\mu(\bm{x}),

(25)

where
d^{M}\mu(\bm{x}):=d\mu(x_{1})\ldots d\mu(x_{M})
. The matrix on the left-hand side refers to the matrix whose upper

M\times N
block is
\begin{pmatrix}\int_{X}f_{j}(x)g_{k}(x)\,d\mu(x)\end{pmatrix}_{1\leq j\leq M,1\leq k\leq N}
and whose lower
(N-M)\times N
block is
\bm{A}
.

Proof.

Expand the right hand side of (
25
) using the Leibniz formula to get

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N},\tau\in S_{M}}\sgn{(\sigma)}\sgn{(\tau)}\prod_{j=M+1}^{N}A_{j,\sigma(j)}\int\prod_{j=1}^{M}g_{\sigma(j)}(x_{j})f_{j}(x_{\tau(j)})d^{M}\mu(\bm{x}).

(26)

After some rearrangement of the factors in the above integral we can write

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N},\tau\in S_{M}}\sgn{(\sigma)}\sgn{(\tau)}\prod_{j=M+1}^{N}A_{j,\sigma(j)}\prod_{j=1}^{M}\int g_{\sigma(j)}(x)f_{\tau^{-1}(j)}(x)d\mu(x).

(27)

Write
S_{N|M}
for the set of permutations in
S_{N}
that fix
M+1,\dots,N
. Associate each
\tau\in S_{M}
with
\tilde{\tau}\in S_{N|M}
, so that we can rewrite (
27
) as

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N},\tilde{\tau}\in S_{N|M}}\sgn{(\sigma\tilde{\tau})}\prod_{j=M+1}^{N}A_{j,\sigma\tilde{\tau}(j)}\prod_{j=1}^{M}\int g_{\sigma\tilde{\tau}(j)}(x)f_{j}(x)d\mu(x).

(28)

Given any
\tilde{\sigma}\in S_{N}
, for each
\tilde{\tau}\in S_{N|M}
there is a unique
\sigma\in S_{N}
such that
\tilde{\sigma}=\sigma\tilde{\tau}
. Hence the above sum runs through each
\tilde{\sigma}\in S_{M}
exactly
M!
times, and we end up with

M!\sum_{\tilde{\sigma}\in S_{N}}\sgn{(\tilde{\sigma})}\prod_{j=M+1}^{N}A_{j,\tilde{\sigma}(j)}\prod_{j=1}^{M}\int g_{\tilde{\sigma}(j)}(x)f_{j}(x)d\mu(x),

(29)

which is exactly
M!
times the left hand side of (
25
) expanded using the Leibniz formula.
∎

We will also need the following Vandermonde-type determinant.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

V_{N}(\bm{z}):=\det\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}^{-(N-1)/2}&z_{1}^{-(N-3)/2}&\cdots&z_{1}^{(N-1)/2}\\
z_{2}^{-(N-1)/2}&z_{2}^{-(N-3)/2}&\cdots&z_{2}^{(N-1)/2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
z_{N}^{-(N-1)/2}&z_{N}^{-(N-3)/2}&\cdots&z_{N}^{(N-1)/2}\end{pmatrix},

(30)

where
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})
.

If
z_{j}=e^{i\theta_{j}}\in\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}}
for all
j=1,\dots,N
, and

t_{0}\leq\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\theta_{N}\leq t_{0}+2\pi
, then

\displaystyle V_{N}(\bm{z})

\displaystyle=(2i)^{N(N-1)/2}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\sin\tfrac{\theta_{k}-\theta_{j}}{2}

(31)

\displaystyle=i^{N(N-1)/2}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}|z_{k}-z_{j}|.

(32)

Proof.

By extracting
z_{j}^{-(N-1)/2}
from the
j
-th row we obtain the Vandermonde determinant. Therefore (
30
) is equal to

\prod_{j=1}^{N}z_{j}^{-(N-1)/2}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}(z_{k}-z_{j}),

which is equal to (
31
) after we employ the elementary identity

e^{i\theta_{k}}-e^{i\theta_{j}}=2ie^{i(\theta_{j}+\theta_{k})/2}\sin\tfrac{\theta_{k}-\theta_{j}}{2}.

(33)

Finally, (
32
) is clear if one observes that the product of sines in (
31
) is always non-negative because of the ordering of
\theta_{j}
’s.
∎

4.
Angelesco systems on the unit circle

Definition 4.1
.

For each
j=1,\dots,r
, let
\mu_{j}
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}}
with infinite support. We say that system
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r})
is an Angelesco system on the unit circle if there exist closed arcs
\Gamma_{j}\subset\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\,\mu_{j}\subseteq\Gamma_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,r
, and
|\Gamma_{j}\cap\Gamma_{k}|\in\{0,1,2\}
whenever
j\neq k
.

Remark 4.2
.

In the above definition, it is easy to see that
\Gamma_{j}\cap\Gamma_{k}
can consist of two points only if
r=2
and the union of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
is equal to
\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}}
.

We assume that the order of
\Gamma_{j}
’s is chosen so that
t_{0}\leq\theta_{1}\leq\dots\leq\theta_{r}\leq t_{0}+2\pi
holds whenever
e^{i\theta_{1}}\in\Gamma_{1},\dots,e^{i\theta_{r}}\in\Gamma_{r}
for some fixed
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. We choose the branch of the square root function to be (
12
) with this
t_{0}
.
Let us additionally require that
\mu_{r}
does not have a point mass at
e^{it_{0}}
(see, however, Remark
4.5
below).

Theorem 4.3
.

For Angelesco systems on the unit circle, every
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is normal.

Proof.

We show that
\det M_{\bm{n}}\neq 0
by using the generalized Andreief identity (
25
), applied
r
times to each of the
T^{(l)}_{n_{l},|\bm{n}|}
,
l=1,\ldots,r
, sequentially (with
N=|\bm{n}|
,
M=n_{l}
, with
f_{j}(z)=z^{(n_{l}+1)/2-j}
,
g_{k}(z)=z^{-(|\bm{n}|+1)/2+k}
, see (
15
)) to obtain

\det M_{\bm{n}}=\frac{(-1)^{k_{\bm{n}}}}{n_{1}!\ldots n_{r}!}\int_{\Gamma_{1}^{n_{1}}}\ldots\int_{\Gamma_{r}^{n_{r}}}V_{|\bm{n}|}(\bm{z}_{1},\ldots,\bm{z}_{r})\prod_{j=1}^{r}V_{n_{j}}(\bm{z}_{j})\prod_{j=1}^{r}d^{n_{j}}\mu_{j}(\bm{z}_{j}),

(34)

for an integer
k_{\bm{n}}
, where
V_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}
and
V_{n_{1}},\dots,V_{n_{r}}
are given by (
30
). The factor
(-1)^{k_{\bm{n}}}
enters because the powers in
V_{n_{1}},\dots,V_{n_{r}}
are in the reverse order when we apply the Andreief identity. Hence we have

k_{\bm{n}}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}n_{j}(n_{j}-1)/2.

(35)

For some choice of permutations
\sigma_{1}\in S_{n_{1}},\dots,\sigma_{r}\in S_{n_{r}}
, assume that
t_{0}\leq\theta_{j,\sigma(1)}<\dots<\theta_{j,\sigma(n_{j})}<t_{0}+2\pi
, where
\bm{z}_{j}=(e^{i\theta_{j,1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{j,n_{j}}})
, and write
\bm{z}_{j,\sigma_{j}}
for
(z_{j,\sigma_{j}(1)},\dots,z_{j,\sigma_{j}(n_{j})})
,
j=1,\dots,r
. Then we have

\displaystyle V_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}(\bm{z}_{1},\dots,\bm{z}_{r})=V_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}(\bm{z}_{1,\sigma_{1}},\dots,\bm{z}_{r,\sigma_{r}})\prod_{j=1}^{r}\sgn(\sigma_{j}),

(36)

\displaystyle V_{n_{j}}(\bm{z}_{j})=V_{n_{j}}(\bm{z}_{j,\sigma_{j}})\sgn(\sigma_{j}),\qquad j=1,\dots,r.

(37)

Since we are in an Angelesco system, the elements in
(\bm{z}_{1,\sigma_{1}},\dots,\bm{z}_{r,\sigma_{r}})
are now ordered by size of angle in
[t_{0},t_{0}+2\pi)
, so that (
32
) can be applied.

Consider the regions

\Gamma_{j,\sigma_{j}}=\left\{(e^{i\theta_{j,\sigma(1)}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{j,\sigma(n_{j})}})\in\Gamma_{j}^{n_{j}}:\theta_{j,\sigma(1)}<\dots<\theta_{j,\sigma(n_{j})}\right\},\\
j=1,\dots,r.

(38)

By (
32
) and (
36
)-(
37
), the integral in (
34
) over the region
\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma_{j,\sigma}^{n_{j}}
turns into

i^{l_{\bm{n}}}\int_{\Gamma_{1,\sigma_{1}}}\ldots\int_{\Gamma_{r,\sigma_{r}}}\prod_{j<k}|\Delta(\bm{z}_{k,\sigma_{k}};\bm{z}_{j,\sigma_{j}})|\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\Delta(\bm{z}_{j,\sigma_{j}})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{r}d^{n_{j}}\mu_{j}(\bm{z}_{j}),

(39)

where
\Delta(\bm{z}):=\prod_{j<k}(z_{k}-z_{j})
and
\Delta(\bm{z};\bm{y}):=\prod_{j,k}(z_{k}-y_{j})
, and

l_{\bm{n}}=\left|\bm{n}\right|(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2+\sum_{j=1}^{r}n_{j}(n_{j}-1)/2.

(40)

Hence we now have a non-negative integrand. Because each
\mu_{j}
is supported on an infinite number of points, the integral in (
39
) is, in fact, positive.
Finally, the summation over all different permutations shows that
\det M_{\bm{n}}\neq 0
, i.e,
\bm{n}
is normal.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Note that the integral formula (
34
) works for any system of measures.

Remark 4.5
.

One can adapt the proof above to the excluded case when
\mu_{r}
has a pure point at
e^{it_{0}}
. For this, one needs to choose the square root function in the integrals with respect to
\mu_{r}
to be taken with the complex argument
\operatorname{arg}_{(t_{0},t_{0}+2\pi]}
, while for all the other measures we take
\operatorname{arg}_{[t_{0},t_{0}+2\pi)}
as before.

5.
AT Systems on the Unit Circle

Recall that a collection
\left\{u_{j}(t)\right\}_{j=1}^{n}
of continuous real-valued functions on a closed interval
[a,b]
is called a Chebyshev system on
[a,b]
if the determinants

W_{n}(\bm{x}):=\det\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}(x_{1})&u_{1}(x_{2})&\cdots&u_{1}(x_{n})\\
u_{2}(x_{1})&u_{2}(x_{2})&\cdots&u_{2}(x_{n})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
u_{n}(x_{1})&u_{n}(x_{2})&\cdots&u_{n}(x_{n})\\
\end{pmatrix}\neq 0

(41)

for every choice of different points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in[a,b]
. In fact, by continuity, the sign of the determinant in (
41
) is always positive or always negative, if we additionally introduce the ordering
x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots<x_{n}
.

Given a function
f(\theta)
let us introduce the following notation:

\operatorname{Trig}_{m}(f)=\begin{cases}\left\{f(\theta)\cos{\frac{2k-1}{2}\theta},f(\theta)\sin{\frac{2k-1}{2}\theta}\right\}_{k=1}^{m/2},&\mbox{ if }m\mbox{ is even},\\
\left\{1\right\}\cup\left\{f(\theta)\cos{k\theta},f(\theta)\sin{k\theta}\right\}_{k=1}^{(m-1)/2},&\mbox{ if }m\mbox{ is odd}.\end{cases}

(42)

Definition 5.1
.

For each
j=1,\dots,r
, let
d\mu_{j}(e^{i\theta})=w_{j}(\theta)d\mu(e^{i\theta})
be a measure that is absolutely continuous with respect to a probability measure
\mu
, infinitely supported on an arc
\Gamma=\{e^{i\theta}:\alpha\leq\theta\leq\beta\}
of the unit circle
\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}}
with
0<\beta-\alpha\leq 2\pi
. We call
\bm{\mu}
an AT system on
\Gamma
for the multi-index
\bm{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})
if

\operatorname{Trig}_{\bm{n}}(\bm{\mu})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}\operatorname{Trig}_{n_{j}}(w_{j})

(43)

is Chebyshev on
[\alpha,\beta]
.

Given an AT system on the unit circle, it is natural to pick the square root function in (
12
) with
t_{0}=\alpha
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that
\bm{\mu}
is an AT system for the index
\bm{n}
. Then
\bm{n}
is normal.

Proof.

If we apply the Andreief identity (
25
) with
M=N=|\bm{n}|
, then we get

\det M_{\bm{n}}=\frac{1}{|\bm{n}|!}\int_{\Gamma^{|\bm{n}|}}\widetilde{W}_{\bm{n}}(\bm{z})V_{|\bm{n}|}(\bm{z})d^{|\bm{n}|}\mu(\bm{z}),

(44)

where
V_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}
is the Vandermonde determinant (
30
), and
\widetilde{W}_{{\bm{n}}}
is the determinant

\widetilde{W}_{{\bm{n}}}(\bm{z})=\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}w_{1}(z_{1})z_{1}^{(n_{1}-1)/2}&\cdots&w_{1}(z_{\left|\bm{n}\right|})z_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}^{(n_{1}-1)/2}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
w_{1}(z_{1})z_{1}^{-(n_{1}-1)/2}&\cdots&w_{1}(z_{\left|\bm{n}\right|})z_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}^{-(n_{1}-1)/2}\\[3.99994pt]
\hline\cr[0.5pt/2pt]\\[-11.99998pt]
&\vdots\\[1.99997pt]
\hline\cr[0.5pt/2pt]\\[-10.00002pt]
w_{r}(z_{1})z_{1}^{(n_{r}-1)/2}&\cdots&w_{r}(z_{\left|\bm{n}\right|})z_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}^{(n_{r}-1)/2}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
w_{r}(z_{1})z_{1}^{-(n_{r}-1)/2}&\cdots&w_{r}(z_{\left|\bm{n}\right|})z_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}^{-(n_{r}-1)/2}\\
\end{array}\right).

(45)

By performing elementary row operations on
\widetilde{W}_{{\bm{n}}}
, we can reduce this determinant to
W_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{\left|\bm{n}\right|})
given by (
41
) for
\operatorname{Trig}_{\bm{n}}(\bm{\mu})
, up to sign changes and multiplication by an integer power of
2i
.

Now observe that
W_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}V_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}
is invariant under permutations of
z_{j}
’s, while on the subset
\theta_{1}<\ldots<\theta_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}
of
[\alpha,\beta]^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}
, the product
i^{-\left|\bm{n}\right|(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2}W_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}V_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}
is of constant sign by the AT property. Hence we obtain
\det M_{\bm{n}}\neq 0
, so that
\bm{n}
is normal.
∎

6.
A two-point Hermite–Padé problem associated with

\phi_{\bm{n}}

6.1.
The case of all even
n_{j}
’s

First let us consider those indices
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
that have all the components
n_{j}
even,
j=1,\ldots,r
. The general case will be handled in Section
6.4
below.

For presentation purposes let us introduce
\bm{\ell}=(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
by
\bm{n}=2\bm{\ell}
, so that, in particular,
|\bm{\ell}|=|\bm{n}|/2
and
\ell_{j}=n_{j}/2
.

For such
\bm{n}
, we want to solve the two-point Hermite–Padé problem of finding Laurent polynomials

\varphi_{\bm{n}},\psi_{\bm{n},1},\ldots,\psi_{\bm{n},r}\in\operatorname{span}\big\{z^{k}\big\}_{k=-|\bm{\ell}|}^{|\bm{\ell}|},

(46)

so that

\displaystyle\varphi_{\bm{n}}(z)F_{j}(z)+\psi_{\bm{n},j}(z)=\mathcal{O}(z^{\ell_{j}}),\qquad

\displaystyle z\rightarrow 0,

(47)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\bm{n}}(z)F_{j}(z)+\psi_{\bm{n},j}(z)=\mathcal{O}(z^{-\ell_{j}-1}),\qquad

\displaystyle z\rightarrow\infty,

(48)

hold simultaneously for all
j=1,\ldots,r
. Here
F_{j}
is the Carathéodory function of
\mu_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,r
, defined by (
5
).

F_{j}
has the power series expansions

\displaystyle F_{j}(z)=1+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k,j}z^{k},\qquad

\displaystyle\left|z\right|<1,

(49)

\displaystyle F_{j}(z)=-1-2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{-k,j}z^{-k},\qquad

\displaystyle\left|z\right|>1,

(50)

where
c_{k,j}
are the moments

c_{k,j}=\int z^{-k}d\mu_{j}(z),\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(51)

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
n_{j}
be even for all
j
. Any Laurent polynomial
\varphi_{\bm{n}}
that solves the Hermite–Padé problem (
46
), (
47
), (
48
), satisfies the orthogonality relations

\int\varphi_{\bm{n}}(z)z^{-k}d\mu_{j}(z)=0,\quad k=-\ell_{j},-\ell_{j}+1,\dots,\ell_{j}-1,\quad j=1,\dots,r.

(52)

Moreover, the Laurent polynomials
\psi_{\bm{n},j}
can be expressed in terms of
\varphi_{\bm{n}}
by

\psi_{\bm{n},j}(z)=\int\frac{w+z}{w-z}\big(\varphi_{\bm{n}}(w)-\varphi_{\bm{n}}(z)\big)d\mu_{j}(w)+\int\varphi_{\bm{n}}(w)d\mu_{j}(w).

(53)

Conversely, any
\varphi_{\bm{n}}\in\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{k}\right\}_{k=-|\bm{\ell}|}^{|\bm{\ell}|}
, that satisfies the orthogonality relations (
52
), solves the Hermite–Padé problem (
47
)–(
48
) together with (
53
).

Remark 6.2
.

Note that the last term
\int\varphi_{\bm{n}}(w)d\mu_{j}(w)
in (
53
) is zero if
n_{j}>0
, but not otherwise in general.

Proof.

We fix
j
and write
\varphi_{\bm{n}}
and
\psi_{\bm{n},j}
in the form

\displaystyle\varphi_{\bm{n}}(z)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{|\bm{\ell}|}z^{|\bm{\ell}|}+\kappa_{|\bm{\ell}|-1}z^{|\bm{\ell}|-1}+\dots+\kappa_{-|\bm{\ell}|}z^{-|\bm{\ell}|},

(54)

\displaystyle\psi_{\bm{n},j}(z)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{|\bm{\ell}|,j}z^{|\bm{\ell}|}+\lambda_{|\bm{\ell}|-1,j}z^{|\bm{\ell}|-1}+\dots+\lambda_{-|\bm{\ell}|,j}z^{-|\bm{\ell}|}.

(55)

We then have

\displaystyle\varphi_{\bm{n}}(z)F_{j}(z)=a_{-|\bm{\ell}|,j}z^{-|\bm{\ell}|}+\dots+a_{\ell_{j}-1,j}z^{\ell_{j}-1}+\mathcal{O}(z^{\ell_{j}}),\qquad

\displaystyle z\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle\varphi_{\bm{n}}(z)F_{j}(z)=b_{|\bm{\ell}|,j}z^{|\bm{\ell}|}+\dots+b_{-\ell_{j},j}z^{-\ell_{j}}+\mathcal{O}(z^{-\ell_{j}-1}),\qquad

\displaystyle z\rightarrow\infty,

where the coefficients are given by

\displaystyle a_{k,j}=\kappa_{k}+2\kappa_{k-1}c_{1,j}+\dots+2\kappa_{-\left|\bm{\ell}\right|}c_{\left|\bm{\ell}\right|+k,j},\quad

\displaystyle k=-|\bm{\ell}|,\dots,\ell_{j}-1,

(56)

\displaystyle b_{k,j}=-\kappa_{k}-2\kappa_{k+1}c_{-1,j}-\dots-2\kappa_{\left|\bm{\ell}\right|}c_{-\left|\bm{\ell}\right|+k,j},\quad

\displaystyle k=-\ell_{j},\dots,|\bm{\ell}|.

(57)

To get (
47
)-(
48
) we necessarily need

\displaystyle-\lambda_{k,j}=a_{k,j},\qquad

\displaystyle k=-|\bm{\ell}|,\dots,-\ell_{j}-1,

(58)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{k,j}=a_{k,j}=b_{k,j},\qquad

\displaystyle k=-\ell_{j},\dots,\ell_{j}-1,

(59)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{k,j}=b_{k,j},\qquad

\displaystyle k=\ell_{j},\dots,|\bm{\ell}|.

(60)

Observe, in particular, that
\psi_{\bm{n},j}
is uniquely determined from (
58
)–(
60
), assuming
\varphi_{\bm{n}}
is given. Furthermore, (
59
), combined with (
56
)–(
57
) and (

\int\big(\kappa_{|\bm{\ell}|}z^{|\bm{\ell}|}+\dots+\kappa_{-|\bm{\ell}|}z^{-|\bm{\ell}|}\big)z^{-k}d\mu_{j}(z)=0,\qquad k=-\ell_{j},\dots,\ell_{j}-1.

(61)

This proves (
52
). Now denote

R_{\bm{n},j}(z)=\int\frac{w+z}{w-z}\varphi_{\bm{n}}(w)d\mu_{j}(w).

(62)

By

\frac{w+z}{w-z}=\begin{cases}1+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{w^{k}},\quad&\mbox{if }|z|<|w|,\\
-1-2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{k}}{z^{k}},\quad&\mbox{if }|z|>|w|,\\
\end{cases}

(63)

and (
52
), we get, as

(64)

and as

R_{\bm{n},j}(z)=-\int\varphi_{\bm{n}}(w)d\mu_{j}(w)-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2z^{-k}\int\varphi_{\bm{n}}(w)w^{k}d\mu_{j}(w)\\

(66)

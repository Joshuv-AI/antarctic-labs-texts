ℕ d \mathbb{N}^{d} -indexed persistence modules, higher dimensional partitions and rank invariants
Mehdi Nategh, Zhenbo Qin, Shuguang Wang

Abstract
. We study decomposable ℕ d \mathbb{N}^{d} -indexed persistence modules via higher dimensional partitions. Their barcodes are defined in terms of the extended interior of the corresponding Young diagrams. For two decomposable ℕ d \mathbb{N}^{d} -indexed persistence modules, we present a necessary and sufficient condition, in terms of the partitions, for their rank invariants to be the same. This generalizes the well-known fact that for an ℕ \mathbb{N} -indexed persistence module, its barcode and its rank invariant determine each other, i.e., the rank invariant is a complete invariant.

Primary 55N31; Secondary 14C05, 05A17.

1.
Introduction

A fundamental structure theorem asserts that every one-dimensional persistence module
admits a unique decomposition up to isomorphisms
[
5
,
7
]
.
This gives rise to the concept of barcodes which plays a pivotal role in
topological data analysis. It is known from the pioneering work
[
6
]
that
such structure theorem is no longer true for higher dimensional persistence modules.
Extensive research has been devoted to higher dimensional persistence modules
in recent years (see
[
2
,
4
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
13
]
and the references therein).
Higher dimensional persistence modules have found important applications
in the study of noisy point cloud data and time-varying data.

In this paper, we study
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence modules over a field
k
via

d
-dimensional partitions, where
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of nonnegative integers.
To motivate our concepts to be introduced below, let us look at the case
d=1
.
It is well-known that the barcode of an
\mathbb{N}
-indexed persistence module over
k

is a multiset consisting of some intervals of the form
[a,b)=\mathbb{T}_{a}[0,b-a)

where
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{N}\sqcup\{+\infty\}
,
and
\mathbb{T}_{a}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is the translation by
a
.
The closed interval
[0,b-a]
is precisely the Young diagram of
the
1
-dimensional partition
(b-a)_{O}
corresponding to
b-a

where
O\in\mathbb{N}^{0}=\{O\}
, while the interval
[0,b-a)

may be regarded as the extended interior of the Young diagram
[0,b-a]
.

For a general integer
d\geq 1
, a
d
-dimensional partition

\lambda
is
an array

\lambda=(\lambda_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}

of
\lambda_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\in\mathbb{N}\sqcup\{+\infty\}
indexed by

(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1}
such that

\lambda_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\geq\lambda_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d-1}}

if
i_{1}\leq j_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}\leq j_{d-1}
.
For a
d
-dimensional partition
{\lambda}
, the
extended interior
of
its Young diagram
D_{\lambda}\subset(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{d}
is the region

D_{\lambda}^{\rm ei}=D_{\lambda}-\overline{(\partial D_{\lambda})\cap(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{d}}.

Put
D_{\lambda}^{\rm int}=D_{\lambda}^{\rm ei}\cap\mathbb{N}^{d}
which is the set of integral points
in
D_{\lambda}^{\rm ei}
. Geometrically,
D_{\lambda}^{\rm ei}
is obtained from
the Young diagram
D_{\lambda}
by removing its boundary in
(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{d}
,
and
D_{\lambda}^{\rm int}
consists of all the integral points in
D_{\lambda}^{\rm ei}
.

We define that an
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence module
M
admits a

if

M\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda}\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}}

where the index set
\Lambda

and for each

{\lambda}^{(i)}
is a

|{\lambda}^{(i)}|\neq 0
.
In this case, the
barcode

{\mathfrak{B}}_{M}
of

is defined
to be the multiset whose elements are

\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}(D^{\rm ei}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}}),\quad i\in\Lambda.

Criterions and algorithms for determining whether
an
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence module
admits a barcode have been investigated in
[
11
]
.

Since a general higher dimensional persistence module may not admit a barcode,
the rank invariant was introduced in
[
6
]
as an alternative discrete invariant.
The rank invariant of an
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence module
M
is
the function
{\rm Rank}^{M}:(\mathbb{N}^{d})^{\leq}\to\mathbb{N}
given by

{\rm Rank}^{M}(x,y)=\text{\rm Rank}(M_{x,y})

where
(\mathbb{N}^{d})^{\leq}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\times\mathbb{N}^{d}|x\leq y\}
.
Indeed, Carlsson and Zomorodian
[
6
, Theorem 12]
proved that
when
d=1
, the barcode and the rank invariant determine each other, i.e.,
the rank invariant is a complete invariant.
However, when
d>1
, no prior necessary and sufficient conditions
for determining the rank invariant are known.
Our main result in this paper provides a necessary and sufficient condition
(in terms of the parts in the partition) for determining the rank invariant when
the
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence module admits a barcode.
When
d=1
, our necessary and sufficient condition exactly says that
the barcode and the rank invariant determine each other.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
.
Let
M
and
N
be
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence modules admitting barcodes:

M=\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda_{1}}\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad N=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Lambda_{2}}\mathbb{T}_{b^{(\ell)}}{\bf k}_{\mu^{(\ell)}}

where
|{\lambda}^{(i)}|\neq 0
and
|\mu^{(\ell)}|\neq 0
for all

i\in\Lambda_{1}
and
\ell\in\Lambda_{2}
. Then,
{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}

if and only if for every
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1}
, the two multisets

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(a^{(i)},({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\big)|i\in\Lambda_{1}\text{ and }({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\Big\}

(1.1)

and

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(b^{(\ell)},(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\big)|\ell\in\Lambda_{2}\text{ and }(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\Big\}

(1.2)

are equal.

The main idea in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is to use
induction on the sizes of
M
and
N
. We remark that when
d>1
,
under the conditions of Theorem
1.2
,

{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}
does not imply that
M
and
N
have the same barcode.
In other words, when
d>1
, the rank invariant is not a complete invariant
for decomposable
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence modules.
It would be interesting to see how to strengthen the assumption

{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}
in Theorem
1.2
so that
the decomposable
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence modules
M
and
N
are
guaranteed to have the same barcode.

Section

(i)

\lambda=(\lambda_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}

\lambda_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\in\mathbb{N}\sqcup\{+\infty\}

(ii)

Remark 2.2
.

The ordinary partitions are

-dimensional partitions
(of nonnegative integers) in our sense.

One sees immediately that the generating function for

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}P_{2}(n)q^{n}=\prod_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}

(2.2)

There is no analogous formula for

Fix

and the field

. The group

3.

Definition 3.1
.

(i)

A

\bullet

\bullet

(ii)

The

consists of one element if

(i)

[

, Proposition 6.40]
and
[
9
, Proposition 6.43]
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Every persistence module
M
has a presentation. Moreover,
if
M
is finitely generated, then there exists a presentation

f:F\to F^{\prime}
of
M
such that both the free modules
F
and
F^{\prime}

are finitely generated.

Next, we study the case
F^{\prime}=\mathbb{T}_{a}{\bf k}
where
a\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Then every quotient of the persistence module

\mathbb{T}_{a}{\bf k}
is equal to
\mathbb{T}_{a}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}}
for
some
d
-dimensional partition
{\lambda}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that every quotient of the persistence module

{\bf k}
is equal to
{\bf k}_{{\lambda}}
for some
d
-dimensional partition
{\lambda}
.
Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a quotient of
{\bf k}
.
Let
\mathcal{I}
be the submodule of
{\bf k}
such that

\mathcal{Q}={\bf k}/\mathcal{I}
.
By (
3.2
), the persistence module
{\bf k}
corresponds to
the
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-graded
k[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}]
-module

k[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}]
itself. Thus, the submodule
\mathcal{I}
of

{\bf k}
corresponds to an
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-graded ideal
I
of
k[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}]
.
Being
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-graded, the ideal
I
of
k[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}]
is generated
by monomials. Define a
d
-dimensional partition

{\lambda}=({\lambda}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}
as follows:

{\lambda}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}=\begin{cases}\min\{b|t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{d-1}^{i_{d-1}}t_{d}^{b}\in I\}&\text{if $t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{d-1}^{i_{d-1}}t_{d}^{b}\in I$
for some $b\in\mathbb{N}$},\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Indeed, note that
if
t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{d}^{i_{d}}\in I
, then
t_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots t_{d}^{j_{d}}\in I
whenever
i_{1}\leq j_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\leq j_{d}
.
It follows that

\lambda_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\geq\lambda_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d-1}}

whenever
i_{1}\leq j_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}\leq j_{d-1}
.

We have

\displaystyle I=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1}\\
{\lambda}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\neq+\infty\end{subarray}}\,\,\bigoplus_{b\geq{\lambda}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}}k\cdot t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{d-1}^{i_{d-1}}t_{d}^{b}.

(3.3)

As a vector space, the quotient
k[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}]/I
is equal to

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1}}\,\,\bigoplus_{b<{\lambda}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}}k\cdot t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{d-1}^{i_{d-1}}t_{d}^{b}.

(3.4)

Via the correspondence (
3.2
), the quotient
\mathcal{Q}
of

{\bf k}
, which corresponds to the quotient
k[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}]/I
of

k[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}]
, is equal to
{\bf k}_{{\lambda}}
.
∎

Definition 3.9
.

An
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence module
M
admits a
barcode

if

\displaystyle M\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda}\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}}

(3.5)

where for each
i\in\Lambda
,
a^{(i)}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and

{\lambda}^{(i)}
is a
d
-dimensional partition with
|{\lambda}^{(i)}|\neq 0
.
In this case, the
barcode

{\mathfrak{B}}_{M}
of
M
is defined
to be the multiset whose elements are

\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}(D^{\rm ei}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}}),\quad i\in\Lambda.

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume that an
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence module
M
admits
a barcode
{\mathfrak{B}}_{M}
. Then,

\displaystyle\text{\rm Rank}(M_{x,y})=|\{S\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{M}|x,y\in S\}|

(3.6)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
satisfying
x\leq y
.

Proof.

We may assume
M=\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda}\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}}
so that

{\mathfrak{B}}_{M}=\big\{\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}(D^{\rm ei}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}})\big\}_{i\in\Lambda}

as multisets. Let
x,y\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
satisfying
x\leq y
. Then,

\displaystyle\text{\rm Rank}(M_{x,y})=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\text{\rm Rank}\big((\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}})_{x,y}\big).

(3.7)

Put

\Lambda_{1}=\{i\in\Lambda|x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}(D^{\rm int}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}})\}.

By Definition
3.6
(i) and Definition
3.5
(iv),

\text{\rm Rank}\big((\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}})_{x,y}\big)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{\rm if $x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}(D^{\rm int}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}})$}\\
0,&\text{\rm otherwise.}\end{cases}

Therefore,
\text{\rm Rank}(M_{x,y})=|\Lambda_{1}|=|\{\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}(D^{\rm int}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}})|x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}(D^{\rm int}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}})\}|
.
By Remark
2.4
,

D_{\lambda}^{\rm int}=D_{\lambda}^{\rm ei}\cap\mathbb{N}^{d}

for every
d
-dimensional partition
{\lambda}
. Hence,

\text{\rm Rank}(M_{x,y})=|\{\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}(D^{\rm ei}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}})\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{M}|x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}(D^{\rm ei}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}})\}|.

∎

The following theorem has been proved in
[

]
.

Theorem 3.11
.

(

) Let

Assume that there are two isomorphisms

(3.8)

In particular, the barcode

of

4.
The rank invariant

In this section, we study the relation between the rank invariant
which is defined in
[
6
]

and the
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence modules which admit barcodes.
We will prove a necessary and sufficient condition for
two
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence modules admitting barcodes to have
the same rank invariant.

The following definitions are adopted from
[
6
]
(see also
[
9
]
).

Definition 4.1
.

(i)

Given a poset
P
, define

P^{\leq}=\{(x,y)\in P\times P|x\leq y\}.

(ii)

The
rank invariant
of an
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence module
M
is
the function
{\rm Rank}^{M}:(\mathbb{N}^{d})^{\leq}\to\mathbb{N}
given by

{\rm Rank}^{M}(x,y)=\text{\rm Rank}(M_{x,y}).

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that an
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence module
M
admits
a barcode
{\mathfrak{B}}_{M}
. Then,

\displaystyle{\rm Rank}^{M}(x,y)=|\{S\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{M}|x,y\in S\}|

(4.1)

for every
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{N}^{d})^{\leq}
.

Proof.

Follows directly from Definition
4.1
(ii) and
Lemma
3.10
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
.
Let
M
and
N
be
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence modules admitting barcodes:

M=\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda_{1}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad N=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Lambda_{2}}{\bf k}_{\mu^{(\ell)}}

with
|{\lambda}^{(i)}|,|\mu^{(\ell)}|\neq 0
. If
{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}
,
then for every
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1}
, the two multisets

\displaystyle\big\{{\lambda}^{(i)}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}|i\in\Lambda_{1}\text{ and }{\lambda}^{(i)}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\big\}

(4.2)

and

\displaystyle\big\{\mu^{(\ell)}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}|\ell\in\Lambda_{2}\text{ and }\mu^{(\ell)}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\big\}

(4.3)

are equal.

Proof.

Fix
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1}
.
Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}
be the distinct values in the multiset (
4.2
)
with
a_{1}>\ldots>a_{s}
, and let
m_{1},\ldots,m_{s}
be the multiplicities of

a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}
respectively in the multiset (
4.2
). Similarly,
let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}
be the distinct values in the multiset (
4.3
)
with
b_{1}>\ldots>b_{t}
, and let
n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}
be the multiplicities of

b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}
respectively in the multiset (
4.3
).

Without loss of generality, assume
a_{1}\geq b_{1}
.
By (
4.1
),

{\rm Rank}^{M}\big((i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1},0),(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1},h)\big)=\begin{cases}m_{1}&\text{if $a_{2}\leq h<a_{1}$},\\
0&\text{if $h\geq a_{1}$}.\end{cases}

Since
{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}
, we conclude that

{\rm Rank}^{N}\big((i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1},0),(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1},h)\big)=\begin{cases}m_{1}&\text{if $a_{2}\leq h<a_{1}$},\\
0&\text{if $h\geq a_{1}$}.\end{cases}

It follows that
b_{1}=a_{1}
and
n_{1}=m_{1}
.

Next, without loss of generality, assume
a_{2}\geq b_{2}
.
By (
4.1
) again,

{\rm Rank}^{M}\big((i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1},0),(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1},h)\big)=\begin{cases}m_{1}+m_{2}&\text{if $a_{3}\leq h<a_{2}$},\\
m_{1}&\text{if $a_{2}\leq h<a_{1}$},\\
0&\text{if $h\geq a_{1}$}.\end{cases}

Since
{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}
, we conclude that

{\rm Rank}^{N}\big((i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1},0),(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1},h)\big)=\begin{cases}m_{1}+m_{2}&\text{if $a_{3}\leq h<a_{2}$},\\
m_{1}&\text{if $a_{2}\leq h<a_{1}$},\\
0&\text{if $h\geq a_{1}$}.\end{cases}

It follows that
b_{2}=a_{2}
and
n_{2}=m_{2}
.

Repeating the above arguments, we see that
s=t
,
a_{i}=b_{i}
and

m_{i}=n_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq s=t
. Therefore, the two multisets
(
4.2
) and (
4.3
) are equal.
∎

Unfortunately, under the conditions in Lemma
4.3
,
the two multisets
\big\{{\lambda}^{(i)}|i\in\Lambda_{1}\big\}

and
\big\{\mu^{(\ell)}|\ell\in\Lambda_{2}\big\}

may not be the same. An example is given below.

{\rm Rank}^{M}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{N}(x,y)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x=(0,0)\text{ and }y=(1,0),\\

0&\text{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

So
{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}
as asserted by Lemma

.
However, the two multisets
\{{\lambda}^{(1)},{\lambda}^{(2)}\}
and
\{\mu^{(1)},\mu^{(2)}\}

are not equal. By Theorem
3.11
,
the two
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-indexed persistence modules
M
and
N
are not isomorphic.

Our next goal is to present a necessary and sufficient condition for
two
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence modules admitting barcodes to have
the same rank invariants. We start with the projections
\mathfrak{p}
and

\mathfrak{q}
.

Definition 4.5
.

For
d\geq 2
, we define
\mathfrak{p}:\mathbb{N}^{d}\to\mathbb{N}^{d-1}
by

\mathfrak{p}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1}),

and define
\mathfrak{q}:\mathbb{N}^{d}\to\mathbb{N}
by

\mathfrak{q}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=x_{d}.

For
d=1
, we define
\mathfrak{p}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}^{0}=\{O\}
by
\mathfrak{p}(x)=O
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
{\lambda}
be a
d
-dimensional partition with
|{\lambda}|\neq 0
.
Let
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{N}^{d})^{\leq}
. Then,
x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{a}(D^{\rm ei}_{{\lambda}})

if and only if
a\leq x
and
\mathfrak{q}(y-a)<{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}(y-a)}
.

Proof.

Recall the maps
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}
from Definition
4.5
.
By (
2.4
),
z\in D^{\rm ei}_{{\lambda}}
if and only if

O\leq z
and
\mathfrak{q}(z)<{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}(z)}
.
Since
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{N}^{d})^{\leq}
, we have
x\leq y
.
Thus,

\displaystyle x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{a}(D^{\rm ei}_{{\lambda}})

if and only if

\displaystyle x-a,y-a\in D^{\rm ei}_{{\lambda}},

if and only if

\displaystyle a\leq x\text{ and }y-a\in D^{\rm ei}_{{\lambda}},

if and only if

\displaystyle a\leq x\text{ and }\mathfrak{q}(y-a)<{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}(y-a)}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The following is the main result of the paper.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
d\geq 1
.
Let
M
and
N
be
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence modules admitting barcodes:

M=\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda_{1}}\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad N=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Lambda_{2}}\mathbb{T}_{b^{(\ell)}}{\bf k}_{\mu^{(\ell)}}

where
|{\lambda}^{(i)}|\neq 0
and
|\mu^{(\ell)}|\neq 0
for all

i\in\Lambda_{1}
and
\ell\in\Lambda_{2}
. Then,
{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}

if and only if for every
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1}
, the two multisets

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(a^{(i)},({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\big)|i\in\Lambda_{1}\text{ and }({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\Big\}

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(b^{(\ell)},(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\big)|\ell\in\Lambda_{2}\text{ and }(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\Big\}

(4.5)

are equal.

Proof.

First of all, assume that the two multisets (
4.4
) and
(
4.5
) are equal. Fix
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{N}^{d})^{\leq}
.
By Lemma
4.2
,

{\rm Rank}^{M}(x,y)=|\{S\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{M}|x,y\in S\}|.

Since
{\mathfrak{B}}_{M}=\big\{\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}(D^{\rm ei}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}})\big\}_{i\in\Lambda_{1}}
,
we see from Lemma
4.6
that
{\rm Rank}^{M}(x,y)
is equal to
the cardinality of the multiset

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(a^{(i)},({\lambda}^{(i)})_{\mathfrak{p}(y-a^{(i)})}\big)|i\in\Lambda_{1},a^{(i)}\leq x\text{ and }\mathfrak{q}(y-a^{(i)})<({\lambda}^{(i)})_{\mathfrak{p}(y-a^{(i)})}\Big\}.

(4.6)

Similarly,
{\rm Rank}^{N}(x,y)
is equal to
the cardinality of the multiset

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(b^{(\ell)},(\mu^{(\ell)})_{\mathfrak{p}(y-b^{(\ell)})}\big)|\ell\in\Lambda_{2},b^{(\ell)}\leq x\text{ and }\mathfrak{q}(y-b^{(\ell)})<(\mu^{(\ell)})_{\mathfrak{p}(y-b^{(\ell)})}\Big\}.

(4.7)

Since the two multisets (
4.4
) and
(
4.5
) are equal, so are the two multisets
(
4.6
) and (
4.7
).
Hence
{\rm Rank}^{M}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{N}(x,y)
for every
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{N}^{d})^{\leq}
.
It follows that
{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}
.

Conversely, assume that
{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
a^{(1)}
is a minimal element in
the multiset

\displaystyle\{a^{(i)}|i\in\Lambda_{1}\}\cup\{b^{(\ell)}|\ell\in\Lambda_{2}\}.

(4.8)

Then,
{\rm Rank}^{N}(a^{(1)},a^{(1)})={\rm Rank}^{M}(a^{(1)},a^{(1)})>0
.
By Lemma
4.2
,
a^{(1)}\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{N}
.
Since
{\mathfrak{B}}_{N}
is the multiset consisting of all

\mathbb{T}_{b^{(\ell)}}\big(D^{\rm ei}_{\mu^{(\ell)}}\big)
with
\ell\in\Lambda_{2}
,
we have
a^{(1)}\in\mathbb{T}_{b^{(\ell)}}\big(D^{\rm ei}_{\mu^{(\ell)}}\big)

for some
\ell\in\Lambda_{2}
. By Lemma
4.6
,
b^{(\ell)}\leq a^{(1)}

for some
\ell\in\Lambda_{2}
. Since
a^{(1)}
is a minimal element in
the multiset (
4.8
), we must have
b^{(\ell)}=a^{(1)}
.
Without loss of generality, we may let
\ell=1
so that
b^{(1)}=a^{(1)}
.
Put

\Lambda_{1}^{\prime}=\{i\in\Lambda_{1}|a^{(i)}=a^{(1)}\},\quad\widetilde{M}=\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda_{1}^{\prime}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}},

M^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda_{1}^{\prime}}\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}}=\mathbb{T}_{a^{(1)}}\widetilde{M},\quad M^{\prime\prime}=\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{1}^{\prime}}\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}}

and

\Lambda_{2}^{\prime}=\{\ell\in\Lambda_{2}|b^{(\ell)}=b^{(1)}=a^{(1)}\},\quad\widetilde{N}=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Lambda_{2}^{\prime}}{\bf k}_{\mu^{(\ell)}},

N^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Lambda_{2}^{\prime}}\mathbb{T}_{b^{(\ell)}}{\bf k}_{\mu^{(\ell)}}=\mathbb{T}_{a^{(1)}}\widetilde{N},\quad N^{\prime\prime}=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Lambda_{2}-\Lambda_{2}^{\prime}}\mathbb{T}_{b^{(\ell)}}{\bf k}_{\mu^{(\ell)}}.

We have
M=M^{\prime}\oplus M^{\prime\prime}
and
N=N^{\prime}\oplus N^{\prime\prime}
.

Claim.

{\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{M}}={\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{N}}
and

{\rm Rank}^{M^{\prime\prime}}={\rm Rank}^{N^{\prime\prime}}
.

Proof.

Let
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{N}^{d})^{\leq}
. Since
\widetilde{M}
is generated at the origin
O
,

{\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{M}}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{M}}(O,y)={\rm Rank}^{\mathbb{T}_{a^{(1)}}\widetilde{M}}(a^{(1)},y+a^{(1)})={\rm Rank}^{M^{\prime}}(a^{(1)},y+a^{(1)}).

Since
a^{(1)}
is a minimal element in the multiset
\{a^{(i)}|i\in\Lambda_{1}\}
,
we have
M^{\prime\prime}_{a^{(1)},y+a^{(1)}}=0
and
{\rm Rank}^{M^{\prime}}(a^{(1)},y+a^{(1)})={\rm Rank}^{M}(a^{(1)},y+a^{(1)})
. Thus,

\displaystyle{\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{M}}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{M}(a^{(1)},y+a^{(1)}).

(4.9)

Similarly,
{\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{N}}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{N}(a^{(1)},y+a^{(1)})
.
Combining with
{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}
and (
4.9
),
we conclude that
{\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{M}}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{N}}(x,y)

for every
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{N}^{d})^{\leq}
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{M}}={\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{N}}.

(4.10)

Next, we prove that
{\rm Rank}^{M^{\prime\prime}}={\rm Rank}^{N^{\prime\prime}}
. We have

{\rm Rank}^{M^{\prime}}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{\mathbb{T}_{a^{(1)}}\widetilde{M}}(x,y)=\begin{cases}{\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{M}}(O,y-a^{(1)})&\text{if $a^{(1)}\leq x$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Similarly,

{\rm Rank}^{N^{\prime}}(x,y)=\begin{cases}{\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{N}}(O,y-a^{(1)})&\text{if $a^{(1)}\leq x$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By (
4.10
),
{\rm Rank}^{M^{\prime}}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{N^{\prime}}(x,y)
. Since

{\rm Rank}^{M}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{M^{\prime}}(x,y)+{\rm Rank}^{M^{\prime\prime}}(x,y),

{\rm Rank}^{N}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{N^{\prime}}(x,y)+{\rm Rank}^{N^{\prime\prime}}(x,y)

and
{\rm Rank}^{M}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{N}(x,y)
, we get

{\rm Rank}^{M^{\prime\prime}}(x,y)={\rm Rank}^{N^{\prime\prime}}(x,y)

for every
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{N}^{d})^{\leq}
. Therefore,

{\rm Rank}^{M^{\prime\prime}}={\rm Rank}^{N^{\prime\prime}}
.
∎

We continue the proof of the theorem. Fix
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1}
.
Applying Lemma
4.3
to
\widetilde{M}
and
\widetilde{N}
with

{\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{M}}={\rm Rank}^{\widetilde{N}}
, we see that the two multisets

\big\{({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}|i\in\Lambda_{1}^{\prime}\text{ and }({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\big\}

and

\big\{(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}|\ell\in\Lambda_{2}^{\prime}\text{ and }(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\big\}

are equal, i.e., the two multisets

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(a^{(i)},({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\big)|i\in\Lambda_{1}^{\prime}\text{ and }({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\Big\}

(4.11)

and

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(b^{(\ell)},(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\big)|\ell\in\Lambda_{2}^{\prime}\text{ and }(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\Big\}

(4.12)

are equal. Applying induction to
M^{\prime\prime}
and
N^{\prime\prime}
with
{\rm Rank}^{M^{\prime\prime}}={\rm Rank}^{N^{\prime\prime}}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(a^{(i)},({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\big)|i\in\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{1}^{\prime}\text{ and }({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\Big\}

(4.13)

and

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(b^{(\ell)},(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\big)|\ell\in\Lambda_{2}-\Lambda_{2}^{\prime}\text{ and }(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\Big\}

(4.14)

are equal. Combining (
4.11
)-(
4.14
),
we see that the two multisets (
4.4
) and
(
4.5
) are equal.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
d\geq 1
.
Let
M
and
N
be
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence modules admitting barcodes:

M=\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda_{1}}\mathbb{T}_{a^{(i)}}{\bf k}_{{\lambda}^{(i)}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad N=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Lambda_{2}}\mathbb{T}_{b^{(\ell)}}{\bf k}_{\mu^{(\ell)}}

where
|{\lambda}^{(i)}|\neq 0
and
|\mu^{(\ell)}|\neq 0
for all

i\in\Lambda_{1}
and
\ell\in\Lambda_{2}
. If
{\rm Rank}^{M}={\rm Rank}^{N}
,
then the two multisets

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(a^{(i)},({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\big)|(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1},i\in\Lambda_{1}\text{ and }({\lambda}^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\Big\}

(4.15)

and

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(b^{(\ell)},(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}\big)|(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d-1},\ell\in\Lambda_{2}\text{ and }(\mu^{(\ell)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1}}>0\Big\}

(4.16)

are equal, and
\sum_{i\in\Lambda_{1}}|{\lambda}^{(i)}|=\sum_{\ell\in\Lambda_{2}}|\mu^{(\ell)}|
.

Proof.

Follows immediately from Theorem
4.7
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

Recall from Definition
2.1
that a
1
-dimensional partition is
of the form
{\lambda}=(n)_{O}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}\sqcup\{+\infty\}
.
Moreover, every
\mathbb{N}
-indexed persistence module admits a barcode.
Therefore, when
d=1
, Theorem
4.7
recovers the well-known result
that the rank invariant and the barcode determine each other uniquely.
Unfortunately, when
d>1
, Example
4.4
shows that
the rank invariant of a decomposable
\mathbb{N}^{d}
-indexed persistence modules
does not determine the barcode.

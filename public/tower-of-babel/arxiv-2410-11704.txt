Iwasawa Theory of graphs and their duals
Debanjana Kundu, Katharina Müller

Abstract
. In this article, we study questions pertaining to ramified ℤ p d \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d} -extensions of a finite connected graph X X . Let X ∞ X_{\infty} denote a ℤ p d \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d} ramified derived graph of X X and Λ \Lambda denote the associated Iwasawa algebra. We also introduce the study of the Iwasawa theory of dual graphs.

Future Direction

This topic of Iwasawa theory of graphs, and in particular branched coverings of finite connected graphs leaves plenty of room for further investigations.

•

Even though the operator
\Delta
describing the characteristic ideal of
\operatorname{Pic}_{\Lambda}(X_{\infty})
generalizes the linear operator appearing in the IMC for the unramified case, it is not clear whether this operator can be interpreted as a
p
-adic
L
-function in the branched setting as well.
In other words, so far the analytic side of an IMC does not exist in the setting of branched covers.

•

The asymptotic formula for the number of spanning trees in a branched
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-covering involves an error term of size
O(p^{n(d-1)})
.
This error term does not occur in the unramified setting: for an unramified
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-covering there exists a polynomial

of total degree
d
and degree at most
1
in
Y
such that

\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\kappa(X_{n}))=P(p^{n},n).

In a future project the authors intend to investigate whether it is possible to improve the error term.

•

It would also be interesting to study a Kida-type formula for the branched
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-covers.

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle\mapsto(o(e),t(e))

Remark 2.8

Another way to think about unramified Galois covers is by considering the Galois group as a deck transformation.

The notion of branched covering relaxes the condition that the action of

We define a

For each vertex

\mathcal{I}=\{(v,I_{v})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}v\in V(X)\}.

Therefore, we obtain a (Galois) tower of graphs

When the closed subgroups

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X_{\infty}/X
be a
\mathcal{G}
-tower of planar connected graphs with intermediate layers denoted by
X_{n}
.
Fix a planar embedding for each
X_{n}
and consider the dual graph
X^{\vee}_{n}
at each layer.
Suppose that
X_{n}^{\vee}/X^{\vee}
is a branched covering.
Then
X_{\infty}^{\vee}/X^{\vee}
is also a
\mathcal{G}
-tower of planar graphs with intermediate layers
X_{n}^{\vee}
and the Galois action can be chosen to be compatible with the process of taking duals.

Proof.

We fix a planar representation of
X
and let
\pi_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X
be the natural projection.
Given a directed edge
e
of
X_{n}
there are exactly two directed edges
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
in
X_{n}^{\vee}
that intersect
e
.
We wish to choose one of them (as the dual edge) depending on the orientation of
e
.
We fix once and for all a choice
e^{\vee}
for
X
and define
e_{i}=e^{\vee}
if
\pi_{n}(e)^{\vee}=\pi_{n}(e_{i})
.

Define the action of
\mathcal{G}_{n}
on the set of directed edges
\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{\vee})
by

g\cdot e^{\vee}=(g\cdot e)^{\vee}.

We extend the action to the vertices of
X_{n}^{\vee}
by defining
g(o(e^{\vee}))=o(ge^{\vee})
and
gt(e^{\vee})=t(ge^{\vee})
. Thus,
g
extends naturally to a morphism of graphs.
This defines a free action without inversion, because the action of
\mathcal{G}_{n}
on
\mathbb{E}(X_{n})
is a free action without inversion.

Claim:

This action is compatible with the tower.

Justification:

Let
e\in\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{\vee})
and let
\pi_{n,n-1}\colon X_{n}^{\vee}\to X_{n-1}^{\vee}
be the natural projection.
By abuse of notation we also denote the projection
X_{n}\to X_{n-1}
by
\pi_{n,n-1}
.
Let
\psi_{n,n-1}\colon\mathcal{G}_{n}\to\mathcal{G}_{n-1}
be the corresponding projection on the groups.
We need to show

\pi_{n,n-1}(g\cdot e^{\vee})=\psi_{n,n-1}(g)\cdot\pi_{n,n-1}(e^{\vee}).

By construction,

\pi_{n,n-1}(g\cdot e^{\vee})=\pi_{n,n-1}((g\cdot e)^{\vee})=(\pi_{n,n-1}(g\cdot e))^{\vee}=(\psi_{n,n-1}(g)\cdot\pi_{n,n-1}(e))^{\vee}=\psi_{n,n-1}(g)\cdot\pi_{n,n-1}(e^{\vee}).

This completes the proof of the claim and the theorem.
∎

Definition 5.6
.

Let
X=(V,\mathbb{E})
be an undirected graph.
A

is a function

{\phi}\colon\mathbb{E}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z},

such that
{\phi}(e)+{\phi}(\overline{e})=0
, where
\overline{e}
denotes the inverse of
e
.
Set
F(X)
to denote the faces of
X
, and
f^{\circ}(X)
to denote the boundary of
F(X)
in a counterclockwise orientation.
For any dart-assignment

, define the associated
vertex-configuration
and

as follows:

\displaystyle\partial{\phi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(X)}\sum_{e\in\mathbb{E}^{o}_{v}(X)}{\phi}(e)v

\displaystyle\partial^{*}{\phi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{f\in F(X)}\sum_{e\in f^{\circ}(X)}{\phi}(e)f.

Theorem 5.7
.

Keep the notation introduced above and assume that
(X_{n}^{\vee})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a tower, as well.
Then as Galois modules

\operatorname{Jac}(X_{n})\simeq\operatorname{Jac}(X_{n}^{\vee}).

Proof.

As abstract groups, the isomorphism of the Jacobian groups is proven in
[
CR00
, Theorem 2]
.
Let
u
be an element in
{\operatorname{Div}}^{0}(X)
.
By
[
CR00
, Proposition 3.2]
there exists a dart assignment
{\phi}
such that
\partial{\phi}=u
.
It follows that
\partial^{*}({\phi})\in{\operatorname{Div}}^{0}(X^{\vee})
.
By
[
CR00
, proof of Theorem 2]
this assignment induces a well-defined bijective homomorphism

\theta\colon{\operatorname{Jac}}(X)\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Jac}}(X^{\vee}).

It remains to check that
\theta
is a
G
-homomorphism.
Let
x\in\operatorname{Jac}(X)
and let
u
be a lift in
{\operatorname{Div}}^{0}(X)
.
Then

\displaystyle gu=g\sum_{v\in V(X)}u_{v}v=\sum_{v\in V(X)}u_{v}g(v)=\sum_{v\in V(X)}u_{g^{-1}v}v

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(X)}\sum_{e\in\mathbb{E}^{o}_{g^{-1}v}}{\phi}(e)v

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(X)}\sum_{e\in\mathbb{E}^{o}_{v}}{\phi}(g^{-1}e)v

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(X)}\sum_{e\in\mathbb{E}^{o}_{v}}(g{\phi})(e)v

\displaystyle=\partial(g{\phi}).

Therefore,

\theta(gx)=\partial^{*}(g{\phi})+\Pr(X^{\vee})\in{\operatorname{Jac}}(X^{\vee}).

Furthermore,

\partial^{*}(g{\phi})=\sum_{f\in F(X)}\sum_{e\in f^{\circ}(X)}{\phi}(g^{-1}e)f=\sum_{f\in F(X)}\sum_{e\in f^{\circ}(X)}{\phi}(e)gf=g\partial^{*}({\phi}).

Therefore

g\theta(x)=\theta(gx).\qed

Remark 5.8
.

It is crucial that we work with the Jacobian group here and not with the whole Picard group.
For Picard groups the above statement is in general not true.
Indeed, consider a graph

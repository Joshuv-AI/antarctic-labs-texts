Extremal diameters of 3-coloring graphs of trees
Shamil Asgarli, Sara Krehbiel, Simon MacLean, Gjergji Zaimi

Abstract
. Given a tree T T , its 3-coloring graph 𝒞 3 ​ ( T ) \mathcal{C}_{3}(T) has as vertices the proper 3-colorings of T T , with edges joining colorings that differ at exactly one vertex. We call the diameter of 𝒞 3 ​ ( T ) \mathcal{C}_{3}(T) the 3-coloring diameter of T T . We introduce the notion of balanced labelings of T T and show that the 3-coloring diameter equals the maximum L 1 L_{1} -norm of a balanced labeling. Using this equivalence, we determine the maximum and minimum values of the 3-coloring diameter over all trees on n n vertices and characterize the extremal trees.

Given a graph

One of the foundational problems in this area is to understand the metric properties of
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
, in particular its diameter. A central open problem is Cereceda’s conjecture
[
Cer07
]
: if an

. While the

]

The graph
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
is isomorphic to the
1

[

]
. Much of the existing work on these complexes emphasizes their topological properties and applications to graph coloring. In contrast, motivated by Cereceda’s conjecture, we focus here on the metric properties of their
1

1.1
refines the general

bound for trees by determining the exact extremal values of

.

We remark that Cambie, van Batenburg, and Cranston
[
CvBC24
]
determined the diameter of
\mathcal{C}_{k}(K_{p,q})
for any complete bipartite graph

up to a small additive constant; their result for the special case

is consistent with our findings. The present paper is the first to systematically classify the extremal 3-coloring diameters for trees in general. It would be interesting to further understand the distribution of these values between the extremes. In particular, is there a local move on trees that monotonically increases the 3-coloring diameter? The existence of such a move could yield a poset structure on trees reminiscent of the one from
[

]

2.
Computing 3-coloring diameter via balanced labelings

Throughout this section,
T
denotes a tree with vertex set
V
and edge set
E
. We identify proper 3-colorings of
T
with functions
f\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
which satisfy

f(u)\neq f(v)
for all
uv\in E
. If
g
is obtained from
f
by changing the color at a single vertex
v
such that
g(v)=f(v)+1
(respectively
g(v)=f(v)-1
), we say
g
is obtained from
f
by a
(+1)
-toggle
(respectively
(-1)
-toggle
) at
v
. The
3-coloring graph

\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
has vertex set equal to all proper 3-colorings of
T
, with edges joining proper colorings that differ by a single vertex toggle (i.e., a
(+1)
or
(-1)
-toggle).

A
labeling
of
T
is a map
h\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
|h(u)-h(v)|\leq 1
for each edge
uv\in E
. Equivalently,
h
is a graph homomorphism from
T
to the infinite path graph on
\mathbb{Z}
with self-loops at each vertex. In particular, the labels are supported on a contiguous subset of
\mathbb{Z}
; that is, the image of
h
is an interval.

We relate these integer labelings to the reconfiguration graph by viewing them as lifts of the difference between two colorings. Suppose
f
and
g
are proper 3-colorings of
T
. A labeling
h\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}
is
(f,g)
-compatible
if

h(v)\equiv g(v)-f(v)\pmod{3}

for every vertex
v\in V
.

We denote the set of all
(f,g)
-compatible labelings by
D(f,g)
. Notice that if
h_{1},h_{2}\in D(f,g)
, then
h_{1}(v)-h_{2}(v)=0\pmod{3}
for every
v\in V
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
h\in D(f,g)
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
h+3m\in D(f,g)
. Moreover, every element of
D(f,g)
has this form for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

First, the map
h^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}h+3m
satisfies both the congruence condition modulo
3
and the inequality
|h^{\prime}(u)-h^{\prime}(v)|\leq 1
for all edges
uv\in E
; hence
h^{\prime}
is
(f,g)
-compatible. Conversely, suppose that
h^{\prime}\in D(f,g)
is arbitrary. Then we have
h^{\prime}(v)-h(v)\equiv 0\pmod{3}
for all
v\in V
. For any
uv\in E
, we have

|(h^{\prime}(u)-h(u))-(h^{\prime}(v)-h(v))|\leq|h^{\prime}(u)-h^{\prime}(v)|+|h(u)-h(v)|\leq 2.

Since
(h^{\prime}(u)-h(u))-(h^{\prime}(v)-h(v))
is a multiple of
3
and has absolute value at most
2
, it equals
0
. We conclude that
h^{\prime}(u)-h(u)=h^{\prime}(v)-h(v)
. As
T
is connected, it follows that
h^{\prime}(v)-h(v)
is constant throughout
V
. Thus,
h^{\prime}(v)-h(v)=3m
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For every labeling
h\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}
, there exist 3-colorings
f,g
of
T
such that
h\in D(f,g)
.

Proof.

We use induction on the number of vertices. When
|V|=1
, the tree has only one vertex
v
; given
h(v)
, choose
f(v)
arbitrarily and set
g(v)=f(v)+h(v)
.

For the inductive step, let
T
be a tree on
n\geq 2
vertices. Suppose that
v
is a leaf vertex of
T
. By the inductive hypothesis, there exist two 3-colorings
f^{\prime},g^{\prime}
of
T^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}T-v
such that the restriction of
h
to
V(T^{\prime})
is a labeling in
D(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})
. To extend
f^{\prime}
to a coloring
f
of
T
, we have two options for
f(v)
, which can be written as
x,x+1\pmod{3}
. Similarly
g^{\prime}
can be extended to a coloring
g
of
T
for some
y,y+1\pmod{3}
. Since the differences
(y+1)-x,y-x,y-(x+1)
cover all residues (mod
3
), we choose a pair satisfying
g(v)-f(v)\equiv h(v)\pmod{3}
.
∎

By Lemma
2.1
, the set
D(f,g)
is a lattice of labelings separated by uniform shifts of
3
. We are interested in the labelings within this set that minimize the
L_{1}
-norm.

Definition 2.3
.

A labeling
h\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}
is
balanced
if its
L_{1}
-norm
\|h\|_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{v\in V}|h(v)|
satisfies

\|h\|_{1}\leq\sum_{v\in V}|h(v)+3m|

for all integers
m
.

Since the function
x\mapsto\sum_{v\in V}|h(v)+x|
is convex, this is equivalent to

(1)

\|h\|_{1}\leq\min\left(\|h-3\|_{1},\|h+3\|_{1}\right).

In other words, for a labeling
h\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}
and any pair
(f,g)
with
h\in D(f,g)
, we have

h\text{ satisfies\penalty\ \eqref{eq:convex-balanced}}\Longleftrightarrow h\text{ is balanced}\Longleftrightarrow h\text{ minimizes the }L_{1}\text{-norm over }D(f,g).

One can think of the tree as being drawn on the integer line, with each vertex
placed at its label. The balanced condition says that if we slide the entire
arrangement to the left or right by
3
units, the total “distance from
0
” never decreases.

To relate balanced labelings to graph distances in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
, we first analyze how shortest walks in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
behave. The next two lemmas describe structural constraints on such walks.

Suppose
f
and
g
are adjacent colorings in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
that differ by an
\varepsilon
-toggle at a vertex
v
, where
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}
. That is,
g(v)=f(v)+\varepsilon
and
g(w)=f(w)
for all
w\neq v
. We denote by
h_{(f,g)}\in D(f,g)
the unique labeling in
D(f,g)
such that
h_{(f,g)}(v)=\varepsilon
and
h_{(f,g)}(w)=0
for all
w\neq v
.

Given a walk
W=(c_{0},\dots,c_{r})
in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
, the two (directed) edges
(c_{i},c_{i+1})
and
(c_{j},c_{j+1})
are
opposite
if they correspond to toggling the same vertex
v
in opposite directions; this condition is equivalent to
h_{(c_{i},c_{i+1})}=-h_{(c_{j},c_{j+1})}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

No shortest path in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
contains opposite edges.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
P=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{r})
is a shortest path in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
containing opposite edges. Among all such pairs, choose a pair
(c_{i},c_{i+1})
and
(c_{j},c_{j+1})
with
i<j
such that the index difference
j-i
is minimized. Let
v
be the toggled vertex, with
c_{i+1}(v)=c_{i}(v)+\varepsilon
and
c_{j+1}(v)=c_{j}(v)-\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}
.

If
j=i+1
, then
c_{i+2}=c_{i}
, and the path obtained by removing
c_{i+1},c_{i+2}
is shorter, a contradiction. This shows
j>i+1
. By the minimality of
j-i
, the vertex
v
is not toggled between steps
i+1
and
j
. Thus,
c_{k}(v)
is constant for
i+1\leq k\leq j
.

For
c_{i}
and
c_{i+1}
to be adjacent proper colorings differing only at
v
, all neighbors of
v
must share the same color
a
in
c_{i}
. Moreover,
c_{i}(v)
and
c_{i+1}(v)
must be the two colors different from
a
. Since
c_{i+1}(v)-c_{i}(v)=\varepsilon
, this forces
c_{i}(v)=a+\varepsilon
and
c_{i+1}(v)=a+2\varepsilon
. Similarly, for the toggle
c_{j}\to c_{j+1}
(direction
-\varepsilon
) to be valid, all neighbors of
v
must share the same color
a^{\prime}
in
c_{j}
. Since
c_{j+1}(v)-c_{j}(v)=-\varepsilon
, this forces
c_{j}(v)=a^{\prime}-\varepsilon
and
c_{j+1}(v)=a^{\prime}-2\varepsilon
. Since
c_{i+1}(v)=c_{j}(v)
, we have
a+2\varepsilon=a^{\prime}-\varepsilon
, which implies
a^{\prime}=a+3\varepsilon\equiv a\pmod{3}
. Thus, the neighbors of
v
have color
a
in both
c_{i}
and
c_{j}
.

Consider the subpath
P^{\prime}=(c_{i+1},\ldots,c_{j})
. Throughout
P^{\prime}
,
v
is fixed at color
a+2\varepsilon
. If no neighbor of
v
changes color during
P^{\prime}
, then the sequence of toggles defining
P^{\prime}
is valid starting from
c_{i}
(since the neighbors of
v
remain colored
a
). Since
c_{j+1}(v)=a+\varepsilon=c_{i}(v)
, applying the same sequence to
c_{i}
yields a parallel walk
(c_{i},c^{\prime}_{i+2},\dots,c^{\prime}_{j-1},c_{j+1})
, where each
c^{\prime}_{k}
differs from
c_{k}
only at
v
. This new walk skips the toggles at
v
. The resulting path from
c_{0}
to
c_{r}
is shorter than
P
by
2
edges, contradicting that
P
is a shortest path.

Therefore, some neighbor
w
must change color during
P^{\prime}
. Since
v
has color
a+2\varepsilon
,
w
can only toggle between colors
a
and
a+\varepsilon
. As
w
starts and ends at color
a
, the path
P^{\prime}
must contain opposite toggles at
w
. These opposite toggles are strictly closer in index than the pair at
v
, contradicting the choice of the minimizing pair.
∎

While Lemma
2.4
restricts the form of a shortest path, the following lemma ensures that any compatible labeling can be realized by a walk with no unnecessary moves.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
h\in D(f_{1},f_{2})
, there exists a walk from
f_{1}
to
f_{2}
in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
of length
\|h\|_{1}
such that each vertex
w
is toggled exactly
|h(w)|
times, all in the direction
\operatorname{sign}(h(w))
. Consequently, the walk has no opposite edges.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n=|V|
. The base case
n=1
is trivial.

For the inductive step, let
v
be a leaf with neighbor
u
. Let
T^{\prime}=T-\{v\}
and let
h^{\prime}
be the restriction of
h
to
T^{\prime}
. By induction, there is a walk
W^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T^{\prime})
realizing
h^{\prime}
. We construct a walk
W
in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
by inserting the
|h(v)|
required toggles of
v
into
W^{\prime}
.

First, consider the case where at least one of
h(v)
or
h(u)
is zero. If
h(u)=0
,
u
is static in
W^{\prime}
; we define
W
by appending the toggle of
v
(if
|h(v)|=1
) to the end of
W^{\prime}
. If
h(v)=0
, we extend
W^{\prime}
to a walk

in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
by keeping the color of
v
fixed.

Now assume that
h(v)
and
h(u)
are both non-zero. Since
|h(v)-h(u)|\leq 1
, they must share the same sign
s\in\{-1,1\}
. Let
\delta(c)=c(v)-c(u)
. For proper colorings,
\delta\in\{-s,s\}
. A
paired toggle
replaces a single step

with a sequence of two steps that toggles both

and
v
by
s
. To maintain proper coloring, the order is determined by
\delta
: if
\delta=s
, we apply
(v,u)
; if
\delta=-s
, we apply
(u,v)
. Note that a paired toggle preserves the value of
\delta
.

We distinguish three cases based on the relative magnitudes of
|h(v)|
and
|h(u)|
:

Case 1:

|h(v)|=|h(u)|
. Then
h(v)=h(u)
. The net change in

h(v)-h(u)\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, so
\delta_{\text{end}}=\delta_{\text{start}}
. We form
W
by replacing every toggle of
u
in

with a paired toggle sequence determined by the initial difference
\delta_{\text{start}}
.

Case 2:

|h(v)|=|h(u)|+1
. Then
h(v)=h(u)+s
. The net change in
\delta
is
s\pmod{3}
. Since
\delta\in\{-s,s\}
, this forces
\delta_{\text{start}}=s
and
\delta_{\text{end}}=-s
. We form
W
by first applying a solo toggle of
v
. This changes
\delta
from

to
-s
. We then proceed as in Case 1, replacing every toggle of
u
in

with a paired toggle of type
(u,v)
(since
\delta
is now
-s
).

Case 3:

|h(v)|=|h(u)|-1
. Then
h(v)=h(u)-s
. The net change in
\delta
is
-s\pmod{3}
, which forces
\delta_{\text{start}}=-s
and
\delta_{\text{end}}=s
. We replace the first
|h(v)|
toggles of
u
in
W^{\prime}
with paired toggles of type
(u,v)
(since
\delta
is initially
-s
). Since paired toggles preserve
\delta
, the value remains
-s
just before the final toggle of
u
. We perform the final toggle of
u
as a solo step, which changes
\delta
from
-s
to
s
.

In all cases, the constructed walk

is valid and has length
\|h^{\prime}\|_{1}+|h(v)|=\|h\|_{1}
. Moreover, since every vertex moves monotonically (only increasing or only decreasing), the walk contains no opposite edges. ∎

We are now ready to state the main result of this section, which identifies the distance in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
with the minimum norm of a compatible labeling.

Theorem 2.6
.

If
f,g
are two
3
-colorings of
T
, viewed as vertices in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
, then

\min_{h\in D(f,g)}\|h\|_{1}=\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)}(f,g).

Proof.

Suppose that
c_{0}=f,c_{1},\dots,c_{r}=g
represents a path of shortest distance in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
. Each edge
(c_{j},c_{j+1})
in the path corresponds to a single toggle, and recall that
h_{(c_{j},c_{j+1})}
is the labeling which vanishes everywhere except at the vertex being toggled. We can thus write

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)}(f,g)=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\left\|h_{(c_{j},c_{j+1})}\right\|_{1}

where the right-hand side counts the total number of toggles. The path contains no opposite edges by Lemma
2.4
. Consequently, the toggles at each vertex accumulate monotonically without cancellation, implying

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)}(f,g)=\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}h_{(c_{j},c_{j+1})}\right\|_{1}.

Observe that the sum
\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}h_{(c_{j},c_{j+1})}
is an element of
D(f,g)
. Therefore,

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)}(f,g)\geq\min_{h\in D(f,g)}\|h\|_{1}.

On the other hand, for any
h\in D(f,g)
there is a path from
f
to
g
of length
\|h\|_{1}
by Lemma
2.5
. Hence,

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)}(f,g)\leq\min_{h\in D(f,g)}\|h\|_{1}.

Combining the two inequalities yields the desired result.
∎

In particular, the graph distance between two colorings is obtained by lifting their difference to
\mathbb{Z}
, and then minimizing the
L_{1}
-distance to
0
over all such lifts.

Since balanced labelings are precisely the minimizers of the
L_{1}
-norm in their respective classes
D(f,g)
, we obtain the following useful characterization of the diameter.

Corollary 2.7
.

The diameter of
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
is equal to the maximum value of
\|h\|_{1}
as
h
ranges over all balanced labelings of
T
.

3.
Median values of balanced labelings

In this section, we bound the median of a balanced labeling that attains the maximum possible
L_{1}
-norm. Our approach is to track how
\|h+x\|_{1}
changes under integer shifts, combining this with the convexity of
\Phi(x)=\|h+x\|_{1}
and the balanced condition (
1
).

Recall that a balanced labeling of a tree
T
is a function
h\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}
satisfying two conditions:

•

|h(u)-h(v)|\leq 1
for each edge
uv\in E(T)
.

•

\|h\|_{1}\leq\|h+3m\|_{1}
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The second bullet point motivates us to understand the behavior of
h+3m
as
m
varies. We analyze these shifts using the function
\Phi(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\|h+x\|_{1}=\sum_{v\in V}|h(v)+x|
. Since
\Phi
is a sum of convex functions
x\mapsto|c+x|
,
\Phi
is convex. Since
h
is integer-valued, the multiset of labels
\{h(v)\}
has the median
M\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. The following notation helps with convexity arguments.

Notation.
Given a labeling
h\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}
, consider it as a multiset
\{h(v)\}
of labels. Define the following quantities:

•

a_{i}
is the number of vertices with label equal to
i
.

•

A_{\geq t}
(resp.
A_{\leq t}
) is the number of vertices with label at least
t
(resp. at most
t
).

•

\Delta_{k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Phi(k+1)-\Phi(k)
represents the forward difference (slope) at
x=k
.

We observe that

\Delta_{k}=\#\{v:h(v)+k\geq 0\}-\#\{v:h(v)+k<0\}=A_{\geq-k}-(n-A_{\geq-k})=2A_{\geq-k}-n.

The identities
\Delta_{k}=2A_{\geq-k}-n
, and equivalently,
A_{\geq-k}=\frac{\Delta_{k}+n}{2}
are useful in our proofs. The following lemma establishes a connection between the median of the labels of
h
and minimizers of the function
\Phi(x)
.

Lemma 3.1

(Median and minimizer)
.

Let
M
be the median of the labels of
h
. An integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}
minimizes
\Phi(k)
if and only if
-k\in[M-\frac{1}{2},M+\frac{1}{2}]
. In particular, if
|M|<1
, then
0
is a global minimizer of
\Phi
.

Proof.

By convexity,
k
minimizes
\Phi
if and only if
\Delta_{k-1}\leq 0\leq\Delta_{k}
. Using the formula for
\Delta_{k}
above, this is equivalent to

2A_{\geq-(k-1)}-n\leq 0\leq 2A_{\geq-k}-n\quad\iff\quad A_{\geq-k+1}\leq n/2\leq A_{\geq-k}.

This is equivalent to the condition that
-k\in\left[M-\frac{1}{2},M+\frac{1}{2}\right]
. ∎

We also record a simple property of convex functions restricted to a lattice.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
be convex with a global minimum at
0
. Let
L\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a lattice (i.e., an arithmetic progression) such that
0\notin L
, containing points strictly less than and strictly greater than
0
. Then
\min_{x\in L}f(x)
is attained at one of the two lattice points
u<0<v
of
L
adjacent to
0
.

Proof.

Note that
f
is non-increasing on
(-\infty,0]
and non-decreasing on
[0,\infty)
. For any
z\in L
with
z<u
,
f(z)\geq f(u)
; for
z>v
,
f(z)\geq f(v)
. Thus, the minimum is at
u
or
v
.
∎

The next lemma is the main result of this section. This lemma helps narrow down the search for balanced labelings of maximum norm.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
h
is a balanced labeling of
T
with maximum
L_{1}
-norm (among all balanced labelings of
T
). Let
M
be the median of the labels of
h
. Then
M\in\{1,\frac{3}{2},2\}
.

Proof.

By symmetry (replacing
h
with
-h
if necessary), we assume
M\geq 0
. Recall that
a_{i}
denotes the number of vertices with label
i
.

Upper Bound (
M\leq 2
).

Since
h
is balanced, we have
\Phi(0)\leq\Phi(-3)
. We expand the difference

\Phi(0)-\Phi(-3)=\sum_{i}a_{i}(|i|-|i-3|)\leq 0

using:

•

i\leq 0\implies|i|-|i-3|=-3
;
i\geq 3\implies|i|-|i-3|=3
.

•

i=1\implies|i|-|i-3|=-1
;
i=2\implies|i|-|i-3|=1
;

Thus,
-3A_{\leq 0}-a_{1}+a_{2}+3A_{\geq 3}\leq 0
. Substituting
A_{\leq 0}=n-a_{1}-a_{2}-A_{\geq 3}
yields:

-3n+2a_{1}+4a_{2}+6A_{\geq 3}\leq 0\implies 6A_{\geq 3}\leq 3n-2a_{1}-4a_{2}\leq 3n.

This implies
A_{\geq 3}\leq n/2
. If
M>2
, then
M\geq 3
and so
A_{\geq 3}>n/2
, a contradiction.

Lower Bound (
M\geq 1
).

Suppose
|M|<1
. By Lemma
3.1
, we know that
x=0
is a global minimizer of
\Phi
, so slopes satisfy
\Delta_{-1}\leq 0\leq\Delta_{0}
. By maximality, if
\|h+k\|_{1}>\|h\|_{1}
, then
h+k
is unbalanced.

Case 1:
Strict minimum (
\Delta_{-1}<0<\Delta_{0}
).
Since
\|h\pm 1\|_{1}>\|h\|_{1}
, both
h+1
and
h-1
are unbalanced.
For
h+1
(on lattice
3\mathbb{Z}+1
straddling
-2
and
1
):

\text{$h+1$ unbalanced}\implies\min(\Phi(-2),\Phi(1))<\Phi(1)\implies\Phi(-2)<\Phi(1).

For
h-1
(on lattice
3\mathbb{Z}-1
straddling
-1
and
2
):

\text{$h-1$ unbalanced}\implies\min(\Phi(-1),\Phi(2))<\Phi(-1)\implies\Phi(2)<\Phi(-1).

Summing these gives
\Phi(1)-\Phi(2)>\Phi(-2)-\Phi(-1)
, or
\Delta_{1}<\Delta_{-2}
, contradicting convexity.

Case 2:
Flat minimum (at least one slope is 0).
By symmetry (
h\mapsto-h
), we may assume
\Delta_{-1}=0\leq\Delta_{0}
. This implies
A_{\geq 1}=(\Delta_{-1}+n)/2=n/2
.

•

If
\Delta_{0}=0
, then
A_{\geq 0}=n/2
, so
a_{0}=0
. Since
A_{\geq 1}=n/2>0
and
A_{\leq-1}=n-A_{\geq 1}>0
, the label set is
not
contiguous, a contradiction.

•

If
\Delta_{0}>0
, then
a_{0}>0
and
a_{1}>0
. We compute the relevant slopes:

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=2A_{\geq 0}-n=2(A_{\geq 1}+a_{0})-n=2a_{0}>0,

\displaystyle\Delta_{-2}

\displaystyle=2A_{\geq 2}-n=2(A_{\geq 1}-a_{1})-n=-2a_{1}<0.

Thus,
\|h+1\|_{1}>\|h\|_{1}
and
\|h-2\|_{1}>\|h\|_{1}
. By maximality, both are unbalanced on the lattice
3\mathbb{Z}+1
. Applying Lemma
3.2
, we obtain:

\Phi(-2)<\Phi(1)\quad(\text{from }h+1)\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi(1)<\Phi(-2)\quad(\text{from }h-2),

a contradiction.

Thus, the lower bound
|M|\geq 1
holds. ∎

4.
Maximum 3-coloring diameter: path graph

Let
I_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[2-\lceil n/2\rceil,\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1]\cap\mathbb{Z}
denote the set of
n
consecutive integers. We begin by showing that the path graph is the unique tree admitting a labeling by this set.

Lemma 4.1
.

If a tree
T
on
n
vertices admits a labeling
h\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{Z}
whose image is exactly the set
I_{n}
, then
T\cong P_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
P_{n}
be the path graph on vertex set
I_{n}
with edges between consecutive integers. Since the image of
h
is
I_{n}
and
|h(u)-h(v)|\leq 1
for each edge
uv
of
T
, the map
h
is a graph homomorphism from
T
to
P_{n}
that is bijective on vertices. Thus
T
is a spanning subgraph of
P_{n}
. Since
P_{n}
is a tree, its only connected spanning subgraph is
P_{n}
itself. Thus
T\cong P_{n}
.
∎

The path graph
P_{n}
admits such a labeling by assigning the integers of
I_{n}
sequentially along the path. For
n=7
, we have
I_{7}=\{-2,-1,0,1,2,3,4\}
, as illustrated below:

Theorem 4.2
.

For every tree
T
on
n\geq 7
vertices, the diameter of
\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)
is at most

\binom{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1}{2}+\binom{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+2}{2}

and equality is achieved if and only if
T
is isomorphic to the path graph
P_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
h\colon V(T)\to\mathbb{Z}
be a balanced labeling of maximum
L_{1}
-norm, and let
M
be the median of its labels. By Lemma
3.3
, we have
M\in\{1,\frac{3}{2},2\}
. As before, let
a_{i}
be the multiplicity of each label
i
. Define

S_{\max}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{x\in I_{n}}|x|=\binom{\lceil n/2\rceil-1}{2}+\binom{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+2}{2}.

We show that
\|h\|_{1}\leq S_{\max}
, with equality if and only if the image of
h
is exactly
I_{n}
. We proceed according to two cases depending on the value of
M
.

Case 1:

M=1
or
M=\frac{3}{2}
.

In either case, at least
\lceil n/2\rceil
labels are
\leq 1
and at least
\lceil n/2\rceil
are
\geq 1
. Let
p=\sum_{i\geq 2}a_{i}
and
q=\sum_{i\leq 0}a_{i}
. Then
p\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
and
q\leq\lceil n/2\rceil-1
. We decompose the difference as follows:

\displaystyle S_{\max}-\|h\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{2-\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1}|i|\right)-\sum_{i}|i|a_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=2}^{p+1}i-\sum_{i\geq 2}ia_{i}\right)+\left(\sum_{i=1-q}^{0}|i|-\sum_{i\leq 0}|i|a_{i}\right)+\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2-\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\leq i\leq-q\\
\text{or}\\
p+2\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1\end{subarray}}(|i|-1)\right).

We claim that the first two summands are always nonnegative, with equality if and only if
a_{i}=1
in the appropriate intervals. To see this, imagine “flattening” the labels that have values
\geq 2
by replacing each label of multiplicity more than one with the next smallest value that is not achieved by a label. The second summand can be handled in the same way by flattening the values towards the negative side. The final summand is nonnegative for
n\geq 7
because
I_{n}
is wide enough: the only term that could possibly be negative is
(|0|-1)
which appears when
q=0
. However,
2-\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\leq-2
, so all three terms
(|-2|-1)+(|-1|-1)+(|0|-1)
combined give a nonnegative contribution.

Case 2:

M=2
.

Since
2
is the median, it follows that
\sum_{i\geq 2}a_{i}>\sum_{i\leq 1}a_{i}
, and therefore we obtain

(2)

a_{2}+\sum_{i\geq 3}a_{i}>a_{1}+\sum_{i\leq 0}a_{i}\,.

Moreover, since
h
is a balanced labeling, we have
\|h\|_{1}\leq\|h-3\|_{1}
which yields

(3)

a_{2}+3\sum_{i\geq 3}a_{i}\leq a_{1}+3\sum_{i\leq 0}a_{i}\,.

The inequalities (
2
) and (
3
) together imply that
\sum_{i\leq 0}a_{i}>\sum_{i\geq 3}a_{i}
and
a_{2}>a_{1}
. Let
p=\sum_{i\geq 3}a_{i}
, and
q=\sum_{i\leq 1}a_{i}
. Notice that we have the bounds

p+2<\sum_{i\leq 0}a_{i}+2\leq\sum_{i\leq 1}a_{i}+1\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1\quad\text{ and }\quad 2-q>2-\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil.

We express the difference:

S_{\max}-\|h\|_{1}=\left(\sum_{2-\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1}|i|\right)-\sum_{i}|i|a_{i}

=\left(\sum_{i=3}^{p+2}i-\sum_{i\geq 3}ia_{i}\right)+\left(\sum_{i=2-q}^{1}|i|-\sum_{i\leq 1}|i|a_{i}\right)+\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2-\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\leq i\leq 1-q\\
\text{or}\\
p+3\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1\end{subarray}}\left(|i|-2\right)\right).

As in Case 1, the first two summands are nonnegative, with equality only when the labels in the corresponding intervals appear with multiplicity one and occupy consecutive integer values (this is the same flattening argument applied to the positive and negative sides separately). The third summand is nonempty because
a_{2}>1
. Moreover, the terms are all nonnegative except possibly the contribution from
i=-1
, namely
|-1|-2=-1
; this term can occur only if
a_{0}=1
and
a_{i}=0
for all
i\leq-1
. Since
\sum_{i\geq 3}a_{i}<\sum_{i\leq 0}a_{i}
, we must have
a_{3}=0
, and so the term
(|3|-2)
also appears. However,
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1\geq 4
for
n\geq 7
, so the presence of the term
(|4|-2)
would make the inequality strict.

Therefore,
\|h\|_{1}\leq S_{\max}
, with equality exactly when the label multiset equals
I_{n}
. By Lemma
4.1
, this implies
T\cong P_{n}
.
∎

Theorem

is precisely the first part of our main Theorem
1.1

5.
Minimal 3-coloring diameter: star and nearly symmetric double stars

In this section, we prove the second part of our main theorem: the trees with the minimum 3-coloring diameter are precisely the star graph
K_{1,n-1}
and the double stars
S(a,b)
with
|a-b|\leq 4
. We first establish a universal lower bound for all trees (Lemma
5.1
), and then show that these trees, and only these trees, attain it.

Throughout this section, we write
u\sim v
to denote that vertices
u
and
v
are adjacent. For a vertex
v
, we write
N(v)
for its (open) neighborhood,

N_{i}(v)
for the set of vertices at distance exactly
i
from
v
,
and
N_{\geq i}(v)
for those at distance at least
i
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For each tree
T
on
n
vertices, we have
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{3}(T))\geq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.7
, it suffices to find a balanced labeling
h\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}
with
\|h\|_{1}=\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
. Partition the vertex set
V
arbitrarily into two subsets
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
with
|U_{1}|=\lceil n/2\rceil
and
|U_{2}|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor
. Consider the labeling
h
given by
h(u)=1
for all
u\in U_{1}
and
h(u)=2
for all
u\in U_{2}
. The condition
|h(u)-h(v)|\leq 1
for all
uv\in E(T)
is trivially satisfied. The norm is
\|h\|_{1}=\lceil n/2\rceil+2\lfloor n/2\rfloor=\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
. Finally, to see that
h
is balanced, we compute

\displaystyle\|h+3\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U_{1}}|1+3|+\sum_{u\in U_{2}}|2+3|=4\lceil n/2\rceil+5\lfloor n/2\rfloor=\lfloor 9n/2\rfloor>\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor=\|h\|_{1},

\displaystyle\|h-3\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U_{1}}|1-3|+\sum_{u\in U_{2}}|2-3|=2\lceil n/2\rceil+\lfloor n/2\rfloor=\lceil 3n/2\rceil\geq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor=\|h\|_{1}.

By equation (
1
), the labeling
h
is balanced. Thus,
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{3}(T))
is at least
\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.
∎

This lemma shows that
\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
is a universal lower bound for the
3
-coloring diameter of a tree. In the following lemmas, we prove that any tree that is not a star or a nearly symmetric double star has a strictly larger
3
-coloring diameter.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
T
be a tree on
n

\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{3}(T))\geq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor+1

We construct a balanced labeling
h

Lemma 5.5

Suppose
T
is a tree on
n\geq 7
vertices such that for every leaf
v
,
|N_{2}(v)|>\lceil n/2\rceil-4
and
|N_{\geq 4}(v)|<|N_{2}(v)|+4-\lceil n/2\rceil
. Then
\operatorname{diam}(T)\leq 3
.

Proof.

Suppose, towards a contradiction, that
\operatorname{diam}(T)\geq 4
. Then there exist leaves
v,w
with
\operatorname{dist}_{T}(v,w)=\delta\geq 4
. Without loss of generality, assume
|N_{2}(w)|\geq|N_{2}(v)|
. Let
P
be a shortest path between
v
and
w
.

Case 1:

\operatorname{dist}_{T}(v,w)=4
. Let the path
P
be
v\sim u\sim z\sim x\sim w
. Then
N_{2}(w)=N(x)\setminus\{w\}
, so
|N_{2}(w)|=\deg(x)-1
. The set
N_{\geq 4}(v)
contains all vertices in
N(x)\setminus\{z\}
, since these are at distance
4
from
v
. Thus,

(4)

\displaystyle|N_{\geq 4}(v)|\geq|N(x)\setminus\{z\}|=\deg(x)-1=|N_{2}(w)|\geq|N_{2}(v)|.

However, the lemma hypothesis states that:

(5)

|N_{\geq 4}(v)|<|N_{2}(v)|+4-\lceil n/2\rceil

Combining the two inequalities (
4
) and (
5
) yields
4-\lceil n/2\rceil>0
,
a contradiction for
n\geq 7
.

Case 2:

\operatorname{dist}_{T}(v,w)\geq 5
. Let
x
be the neighbor of
w
on
P
, and let
y
be the neighbor of
x
preceding it on
P
. As before,
|N_{2}(w)|=\deg(x)-1
. The set
N_{\geq 4}(v)
contains the vertex
w
(at distance
\delta\geq 5
) and the vertex
x
(at distance
\delta-1\geq 4
). It also contains the vertices in
N(x)\setminus\{w,y\}
, all of which are at distance
\delta
from
v
. Thus,

(6)

\displaystyle|N_{\geq 4}(v)|\geq 1+1+(\deg(x)-2)=|N_{2}(w)|+1\geq|N_{2}(v)|+1.

Combining inequality (
6
) with the lemma’s hypothesis (
5
) yields
3-\lceil n/2\rceil>0
, a contradiction for
n\geq 7
.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

If
n\geq 7
and a tree
T
has minimum 3-coloring diameter
\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
, then
T
is either a star or a double star
S(a,b)
with
|a-b|\leq 4
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, every tree on
n
vertices satisfies
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{3}(T))\geq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
. We show that trees not in the stated class have strictly larger diameter. Assume
T
is neither a star nor a double star
S(a,b)
with
|a-b|\leq 4
. We consider two cases.

Case 1:

T=S(a,b)
with
|a-b|>4
. By Lemma
5.4
,
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{3}(T))>\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.

Case 2:

T
is not a star or double star. Then
\operatorname{diam}(T)\geq 4
. By the contrapositive of Lemma
5.5
, there exists a leaf
v
such that at least one of the following holds:

(A)

|N_{2}(v)|\leq\lceil n/2\rceil-4
, or

(B)

|N_{\geq 4}(v)|\geq|N_{2}(v)|+4-\lceil n/2\rceil
.

If (A) holds, then by Lemma
5.2
,
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{3}(T))\geq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor+1
.

If (A) does not hold but (B) holds, let
k=|N_{2}(v)|+4-\lceil n/2\rceil
. Since (A) fails, we have
|N_{2}(v)|>\lceil n/2\rceil-4
, which implies
k\geq 1
. By rearranging the definition of
k
, we obtain
|N_{2}(v)|=\lceil n/2\rceil-4+k
. Combined with (B), which gives
|N_{\geq 4}(v)|\geq k
, the conditions of Lemma
5.3
are satisfied. Thus,
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{3}(T))\geq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor+1
.

In both cases,
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{3}(T))>\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
, completing the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

The 3-coloring diameter of
K_{1,n-1}
is
\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.

Proof.

The lower bound follows from Lemma
5.1
. For the upper bound, it suffices to show that any two 3-colorings are at distance at most
\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.

We represent a 3-coloring by the pair
(c,m)
, where
c\in\{0,1,2\}
is the color of the center vertex and
m\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
is the number of leaves colored
c+1\pmod{3}
. The remaining
n-1-m
leaves are colored
c+2\pmod{3}
. Let
\ell(\cdot)
denote the length of a path in the 3-coloring graph. In the paths below, center flips are performed only when all leaves avoid the new center color, and leaf recolorings are single-vertex steps.

Consider two colorings
x_{1}=(c_{1},m_{1})
and
x_{2}=(c_{2},m_{2})
. If
c_{1}=c_{2}
, then
\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)}(x_{1},x_{2})=|m_{1}-m_{2}|\leq n-1<\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
for
n\geq 3
.

Now suppose
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
. By symmetry, we may assume
c_{1}=0
. We consider the two cases
c_{2}=1
and
c_{2}=2
separately.

Case 1:

c_{2}=1
. We exhibit two paths from
(0,m_{1})
to
(1,m_{2})
:

\displaystyle P_{1}

\displaystyle\colon(0,m_{1})\xrightarrow{m_{1}}(0,0)\xrightarrow{1}(1,n-1)\xrightarrow{n-1-m_{2}}(1,m_{2}),

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle\colon(0,m_{1})\xrightarrow{n-1-m_{1}}(0,n-1)\xrightarrow{1}(2,0)\xrightarrow{n-1}(2,n-1)\xrightarrow{1}(1,0)\xrightarrow{m_{2}}(1,m_{2}).

The path lengths are
\ell(P_{1})=n+m_{1}-m_{2}
and
\ell(P_{2})=2n-m_{1}+m_{2}
, so
\ell(P_{1})+\ell(P_{2})=3n
. Thus,
\min(\ell(P_{1}),\ell(P_{2}))\leq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.

Case 2:

c_{2}=2
. We exhibit two paths from
(0,m_{1})
to
(2,m_{2})
:

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle\colon(0,m_{1})\xrightarrow{m_{1}}(0,0)\xrightarrow{1}(1,n-1)\xrightarrow{n-1}(1,0)\xrightarrow{1}(2,n-1)\xrightarrow{n-1-m_{2}}(2,m_{2}),

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle\colon(0,m_{1})\xrightarrow{n-1-m_{1}}(0,n-1)\xrightarrow{1}(2,0)\xrightarrow{m_{2}}(2,m_{2}).

The path lengths are
\ell(Q_{1})=2n+m_{1}-m_{2}
and
\ell(Q_{2})=n-m_{1}+m_{2}
, so
\ell(Q_{1})+\ell(Q_{2})=3n
. Thus,
\min(\ell(Q_{1}),\ell(Q_{2}))\leq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.

In all cases,
\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
, completing the proof.
∎

We note that Proposition
5.7
appears in the literature (see
[
CvBC24
, Proposition 2]
). However, we include our proof above because it motivates the argument for the next result, which is new.

Proposition 5.8
.

Suppose
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
with
|a-b|\leq 4
, and let
n=a+b+2
. Then the 3-coloring diameter of the double star
S(a,b)
is
\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.

Proof.

The lower bound follows from Lemma
5.1
. For the upper bound, it suffices to show that any two 3-colorings
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are at distance at most
\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.

Notation.

We encode a 3-coloring by
(c_{1},c_{2},m_{1},m_{2})
, where
c_{i}\in\{0,1,2\}
are the colors of the two centers
v
and
w
, and
m_{1}\in\{0,\ldots,a\}
(resp.
m_{2}\in\{0,\ldots,b\}
) is the number of leaves at
v
(resp.
w
) colored
c_{1}+1\pmod{3}
(resp.
c_{2}+1\pmod{3}
). The remaining leaves at each center are colored
c_{i}+2\pmod{3}
. A center flip to a color
\gamma
is valid if and only if every incident leaf avoids
\gamma
. Let
\ell(\cdot)
denote path length in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(S(a,b))
.

Same-center case.

If two colorings
x_{1},x_{2}
have the same pair
(c_{1},c_{2})
, then recoloring only leaves yields

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq a+b=n-2<\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor.

Reduction for distinct-center pairs.

For the remaining pairs, at least one center color changes. Any permutation of
\{0,1,2\}
induces an automorphism of
\mathcal{C}_{3}(S(a,b))
and hence preserves distances. Thus, without loss of generality, we may relabel colors so that the two
source
centers have colors
(c_{1},c_{2})=(0,1)
. After this normalization, the possible
targets
fall, up to symmetry, into exactly three patterns:

\displaystyle(0,1)\to(0,2)\quad\text{(single-center change)}

\displaystyle(0,1)\to(1,2),\ (0,1)\to(1,0)\quad\text{(two-center changes)}.

Single-center change:
(0,1)\to(0,2)
.

For
x_{1}=(0,1,m_{1},m_{2})
and
x_{2}=(0,2,m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2})
consider

(0,1,m_{1},m_{2})\xrightarrow{m_{2}}(0,1,m_{1},0)\xrightarrow{1}(0,2,m_{1},b)\xrightarrow{|m_{1}-m^{\prime}_{1}|}(0,2,m^{\prime}_{1},b)\xrightarrow{b-m^{\prime}_{2}}(0,2,m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2}),

so
\ell\leq m_{2}+1+|m_{1}-m^{\prime}_{1}|+(b-m^{\prime}_{2})\leq a+2b+1
. Therefore, to prove
a+2b+1\leq\lfloor\frac{3n}{2}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{3(a+b+2)}{2}\rfloor
, it suffices to show that
b-2\leq\Big\lfloor\frac{a+b}{2}\Big\rfloor
, or equivalently,
b-2\leq\frac{a+b}{2}
. The latter inequality
2b-4\leq a+b
holds because
b-a\leq 4
.

Two-center changes.

It suffices to treat
(0,1)\to(1,2)
and
(0,1)\to(1,0)
.

Pattern
(0,1)\to(1,2)
.
We construct two paths in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(S(a,b))
between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
:

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle\colon(0,1,m_{1},m_{2})\xrightarrow{m_{1}+m_{2}}(0,1,0,0)\xrightarrow{1}(0,2,0,b)\xrightarrow{1}(1,2,a,b)\xrightarrow{(a-m^{\prime}_{1})+(b-m^{\prime}_{2})}(1,2,m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2}),

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle\colon(0,1,m_{1},m_{2})\xrightarrow{(a-m_{1})+(b-m_{2})}(0,1,a,b)\xrightarrow{2}(2,0,0,0)

\displaystyle\hskip 93.89418pt\xrightarrow{a+b}(2,0,a,b)\xrightarrow{2}(1,2,0,0)\xrightarrow{m^{\prime}_{1}+m^{\prime}_{2}}(1,2,m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2}),

with
\ell(R_{1})+\ell(R_{2})=3a+3b+6=3n
, hence
\min\{\ell(R_{1}),\ell(R_{2})\}\leq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.

Pattern
(0,1)\to(1,0)
.
We construct two paths in
\mathcal{C}_{3}(S(a,b))
between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
:

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle\colon(0,1,m_{1},m_{2})\xrightarrow{(a-m_{1})+(b-m_{2})}(0,1,a,b)\xrightarrow{2}(2,0,0,0)

\displaystyle\hskip 93.89418pt\xrightarrow{a}(2,0,a,0)\xrightarrow{1}(1,0,0,0)\xrightarrow{m^{\prime}_{1}+m^{\prime}_{2}}(1,0,m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2}),

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\colon(0,1,m_{1},m_{2})\xrightarrow{m_{1}+m_{2}}(0,1,0,0)\xrightarrow{2}(1,2,a,b)

\displaystyle\hskip 93.89418pt\xrightarrow{b}(1,2,a,0)\xrightarrow{1}(1,0,a,b)\xrightarrow{(a-m^{\prime}_{1})+(b-m^{\prime}_{2})}(1,0,m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2}),

and again
\ell(S_{1})+\ell(S_{2})=3n
, so
\min\{\ell(S_{1}),\ell(S_{2})\}\leq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.

Combining all the cases above, we conclude that
\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{C}_{3}(T)}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
for all pairs of colorings
x_{1},x_{2}
. This proves
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{3}(S(a,b)))=\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor
.
∎

Together with Lemma
5.1
, this completes the proof of the second part of Theorem
1.1
.

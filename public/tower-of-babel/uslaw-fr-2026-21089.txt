Central Liquidity Facility

AGENCY: National Credit Union Administration (NCUA).

ACTION: Proposed rule.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The NCUA Board (Board) is proposing to adopt as final the 
revisions to the Central Liquidity Facility (CLF) rule made under two 
interim final rules (IFRs) published in the Federal Register on April 
29, 2020, and March 24, 2021. The proposal would adopt the amendments 
made by these IFRs and, where appropriate, would remove provisions from 
the NCUA's regulations that have lapsed under the terms of the IFRs. 
The proposal would also remove or modify certain filing requirements 
and criteria the Board uses to approve CLF members. Finally, the 
proposal would amend the timing requirements related to pay adjustments 
to the capital stock subscription, clarify various regulatory 
provisions, and make necessary conforming edits.

I. Introduction

A. Background

    In response to the COVID-19 pandemic, the Board issued an IFR on 
April 29, 2020 (April 29th IFR), which provided credit unions with 
greater access to liquidity to help ensure they remain operational 
throughout the crisis.\1\ The IFR made it easier and more attractive 
for credit unions to join the NCUA's CLF. In addition, the rule made 
several amendments to conform to the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and 
Economic Security Act (CARES Act).\2\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \1\ 85 FR 23731 (Apr. 29, 2020).
    \2\ Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security Act, Public 
Law 116-136, 134 Stat 281 (Mar. 27, 2020).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The CLF, a mixed-ownership government corporation managed by the 
Board, established in 1979, serves as a liquidity source for its member 
credit unions.\3\ Its purpose is to improve general financial stability 
by meeting the liquidity needs of credit unions and thereby encouraging 
savings, supporting consumer and mortgage lending, and providing basic 
financial resources to all segments of the economy.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \3\ Public Law 95-630, 92 Stat 3641 (Nov. 10, 1978), codified at 
12 U.S.C. 1795, et seq.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Section 307 of the FCU Act, 12 U.S.C. 1795f, among other things, 
gives the Board the authority to prescribe the manner in which the 
general business of the CLF shall be conducted and to prescribe rules 
and regulations to carry out the CLF-related provisions of the FCU 
Act.\4\ Under this authority, the Board issued the April 29th IFR to 
enhance liquidity for credit unions during the COVID-19 pandemic and to 
make regulatory changes that cohere to the CARES Act.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \4\ 12 U.S.C. 1795f.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The Board emphasized in the April 29th IFR that, while some of the 
amendments in the rule were temporary, they would afford significant 
liquidity support to the entire credit union system. The Board also 
noted that several of the changes in the IFR were conforming changes 
based on the recently enacted CARES Act, which temporarily amended the 
FCU Act. As such, the changes in the IFR that corresponded to the CARES 
Act were initially set to sunset in accordance with the CARES Act on 
December 31, 2020. To provide clarity and transparency, the Board 
included the temporary changes in the IFR and explained what would 
occur upon the sunset of the amendments.

[[Page 65482]]

    In December 2020, the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021 (CAA) 
was passed by Congress.\5\ The CAA, among other things, extended the 
sunset date of the CLF enhancements made by the CARES Act until 
December 31, 2021.\6\ In response, the Board issued a second IFR on 
March 24, 2021 (March 24th IFR), to amend its regulations to reflect 
the extensions.\7\ The March 24th IFR responded to comments on the 
April 29th IFR and also extended the withdrawal from CLF membership 
provisions that the Board included in the April 29th IFR.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \6\ Public Law 116-260, 134 Stat 1182, section 540(a).
    \7\ 86 FR 15568 (Mar. 24, 2021).

    The Board received 15 comments in response to the April 29th IFR 
and the March 24th IFR. All the comments supported the IFRs. The 
comments also requested legislative changes, other changes outside the 
scope of the IFRs, or both.
    In addition to the changes made by the April 29th IFR and the March 
24th IFR, the proposal would also remove or modify certain filing 
requirements and criteria the Board uses to approve CLF members. 
Finally, the proposal would amend the timing requirements related to 
pay adjustments to the capital stock subscription, clarify various 
regulatory provisions, and make necessary conforming edits. The changes 
to the NCUA's CLF regulations are discussed in more detail in the 
section-by-section analysis below.

B. Legal Authority

    The Board has the legal authority to issue this proposed rule 
pursuant to its plenary rulemaking authority under the Federal Credit 
Union Act (FCU Act) and its specific rulemaking authority under the 
various provisions the Board administers.\8\ In particular, section 307 
of the FCU Act, 12 U.S.C. 1795f, gives the Board the authority to 
prescribe the manner in which the general business of the CLF shall be 
conducted and to prescribe rules and regulations to carry out the CLF-
related provisions of the FCU Act.\9\

II. Proposed Rule

A. Nomenclature Changes Throughout Part 725

    Current part 725 uses the term ``NCUA Board'' and ``Facility'' 
interchangeably in numerous places throughout part 725. The Board acts 
through the CLF, a mixed-ownership government corporation within the 
NCUA, and the Office of the Board. They are separate offices, however, 
with separate duties and functions. The interchangeable use of 
terminology in part 725 causes confusion for members and applicants 
because it is sometimes unclear, in places where the term ``NCUA 
Board'' is used, whether the Board is acting through the CLF or the 
Office of the Board. The proposed rule would replace the term ``NCUA 
Board'' with the term ``Facility'' throughout part 725, unless the 
context indicates that the Board would be acting through the Office of 
the Board. Regardless of whether the term ``NCUA Board'' or 
``Facility'' is used, actions carried out by the CLF pursuant to part 
725 originate with the NCUA Board. Accordingly, the proposal would 
replace the term ``NCUA Board'' with the term ``Facility'' in current 
Sec. Sec.  725.4(c)(1), (d), and (e); 725.5(c), (d), and (e); 725.6(a), 
(b), and the second and third sentences in paragraph (c); 
725.17(b)(2)(iii); 725.22(a) and (b); 725.23(a) and (b).

B. Definition of ``Facility Lending Officer'' Under Sec.  725.2(g)

    Current Sec.  725.2(g) defines the term ``facility lending 
officer'' as any employee of the CLF or the NCUA who has been 
designated by the NCUA Board as a Facility lending officer. The title 
Facility lending officer is antiquated and no longer used by the CLF. 
Accordingly, the proposal would remove this definition from Sec.  
725.2(g).

C. Definition of ``Liquidity Needs'' Under 725.2(i)

    In accordance with the CARES Act, the April 29th IFR amended the 
definition of ``liquidity needs'' to remove the words ``primarily 
serving natural persons.'' This change was intended to mirror the 
statutory change in the CARES Act and clarified that liquidity needs 
are not limited to only natural person credit unions but may also 
include those of corporate credit unions or a corporate credit union 
group. The change allowed corporate credit unions to obtain loans for 
their own liquidity needs. The amendment was initially set to sunset in 
accordance with the CARES Act on December 31, 2020.
    The CAA extended this provision in the CARES Act until December 31, 
2021. To address this extension, the NCUA's March 24th IFR amended the 
regulatory definition of ``liquidity needs'' to make it clear when the 
definition under the CARES Act applied and when such definition would 
revert to the pre-CARES Act version after December 31, 2021.
    Current Sec.  725.2(i)(1) defines ``liquidity needs'' from April 
29, 2020, to December 31, 2021, as the needs of credit unions for 
short-term adjustment credit available to assist in meeting temporary 
requirements for funds or to cushion more persistent outflows of funds 
pending an orderly adjustment of credit union assets and liabilities; 
seasonal credit available for longer periods to assist in meeting 
seasonal needs for funds arising from a combination of expected 
patterns of movement in share and deposit accounts and loans; and 
protracted adjustment credit available in the event of unusual or 
emergency circumstances of a longer-term nature resulting from 
national, regional, or local difficulties. Current Sec.  725.2(i)(2) 
defines ``liquidity needs'' after December 31, 2021, as the needs of 
credit unions primarily serving natural persons for short-term 
adjustment credit available to assist in meeting temporary requirements 
for funds or to cushion more persistent outflows of funds pending an 
orderly adjustment of credit union assets and liabilities; seasonal 
credit available for longer periods to assist in meeting seasonal needs 
for funds arising from a combination of expected patterns of movement 
in share and deposit accounts and loans; and protracted adjustment 
credit available in the event of unusual or emergency circumstances of 
a longer-term nature resulting from national, regional, or local 
difficulties.
    Given that the definition of liquidity needs under current Sec.  
725.2(i)(1) sunset on January 1, 2022, the Board is proposing to remove 
paragraph (i)(1) and replace it with the definition of liquidity needs 
under current Sec.  725.2(i)(2). Accordingly, this proposal would amend 
Sec.  725.2(i) to provide that ``liquidity needs'' means the needs of 
credit unions primarily serving natural persons for short-term 
adjustment credit available to assist in meeting temporary requirements 
for funds or to cushion more persistent outflows of funds pending an 
orderly adjustment of credit union assets and liabilities; seasonal 
credit available for longer periods to assist in meeting seasonal needs 
for funds arising from a combination of expected patterns of movement 
in share and deposit accounts and loans; and protracted adjustment 
credit available in the event of unusual or emergency circumstances of 
a longer-term nature resulting from national, regional, or local 
difficulties.

D. Requirement for Natural-Person Credit Unions To Furnish Certain 
Reports and Documents Under Sec.  725.3(a)(3)

    Current Sec.  725.3(a)(3) requires that a natural-person credit 
union applying to become a regular member of the CLF furnish certain 
reports and documents with its completed membership application. In 
particular, the natural-person credit union must provide a copy of the 
credit union's financial and statistical report for the most recent 
calendar month; and copies of the credit union's charter and bylaws, 
unless the credit union is federally chartered. These requirements were 
adopted in nearly identical form in 1979.\10\ NCUA has determined that 
it no longer needs to require that copies of such reports and documents 
be provided to the CLF for federally insured natural-person credit 
unions because copies of the reports and documents can already be 
obtained by the CLF through other means, in particular, through the 
NCUA's examination process. This is not the case, however, for non-
federally insured natural-person credit unions. Accordingly, to 
streamline the NCUA's CLF regulations and eliminate outdated and 
unnecessary regulatory requirements, the NCUA is proposing to amend 
current Sec.  725.3(a)(3) to provide that in the case of a non-
federally insured natural-person credit union not otherwise subject to 
examination by the NCUA, the CLF may require that the credit union 
provide financial reports and other information to determine the 
financial condition of the credit union.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \10\ 44 FR 49431, 49438 (Aug. 23, 1979).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

E. Regular Membership Requirements Under Sec.  725.3(b)

    Section 725.3(b) had previously provided that, with limited 
exception, any credit union that becomes a regular member of the CLF 
may not receive CLF advances, without approval of the Board, for six 
months after becoming a member. The April 29th IFR eliminated the six-
month waiting period on obtaining CLF advances for a credit union that 
becomes a regular member. The Board continues to believe it was 
important to remove this restriction in response to the overarching 
need to make liquidity assistance timely and the advantages of 
accelerating liquidity-need loans to new members outweighed the 
practical reasons that having the waiting period afforded to the CLF's 
operations. Accordingly, the Board is proposing to adopt this change to 
Sec.  725.3(b) as final.

F. Agent Membership in the CLF Under 725.4(a)(2)(ii)

    The April 29th IFR, in accordance with the CARES Act, amended the 
nature of the requirement for a corporate credit union or group of 
corporate credit unions to subscribe to the capital stock of the CLF in 
an amount equal to one-half of one percent of the paid-in and 
unimpaired capital and surplus of all of the corporate credit union's 
or corporate credit union group's natural person credit union members. 
This change to prior Sec.  725.4(a)(2) mirrored the statutory change in 
the CARES Act and allowed the Board, in its sole discretion, to 
determine which grouping of natural person member credit unions of the 
applying corporate credit union or corporate credit union group were 
considered covered by the agent's membership in the CLF. In turn, the 
approved group was the basis for calculating the amount of CLF capital 
stock the corporate credit union or corporate credit union group was 
required to purchase. This change provided a corporate credit union 
with the flexibility to subscribe to the capital stock of the CLF up to 
the maximum extent it could afford to do so.
    This amendment was initially set to sunset in accordance with the 
CARES Act on December 31, 2020. Upon the sunset of the amendment, any 
corporate credit union or corporate credit union group that became an 
agent member under this provision was required to, within one year from 
the sunset date, either (1) purchase CLF stock in accordance with the 
terms of the regulation as written post-sunset of the CARES Act 
amendments; or (2) terminate its membership in the CLF. The Board 
believed that the two options accounted for the temporary nature of the 
CARES Act amendments while not causing undue disruption to the 
operations of a corporate credit union or corporate credit union group 
that joined the CLF under the CARES Act amendments.
    The CAA extended this provision in the CARES Act until December 31, 
2021.\11\ To address this extension, the NCUA's March 24th IFR amended 
Sec.  725.4(a)(2) to make conforming date changes to the section.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \11\ Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021, Public Law 116-260, 
134 Stat 1182, section 540(a) (Dec. 27, 2020).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Current Sec.  725.4(a)(2)(ii) sunset on January 1, 2022. Upon the 
sunset of the amendment, any corporate credit union or corporate credit 
union group that became an agent member under this provision was 
required, before January 1, 2023, to either (1) purchase CLF stock in 
accordance with the terms of the regulation as written post-sunset of 
the CARES Act amendments; or (2) terminate its membership in the CLF. 
It has been over two years since the January 1, 2023, deadline passed, 
giving credit unions ample time to comply with the requirements of 
current Sec.  725.4(a)(2)(ii). Accordingly, this proposal would remove 
the language in current Sec.  725.4(a)(2)(ii) and renumber the section 
to conform with this change.

G. Agent Member Borrowing Under Sec. Sec.  725.4(a)(2), 725.18(a) and 
(d), 725.19(a) and (b)

    To effectuate the intent of the CARES Act in a safe and sound 
manner, the April 29th IFR included a clarifying amendment to Sec.  
725.4(a)(2). Section 725.4(a)(2)(iii) clarified that, from April 29, 
2020, until December 31, 2020, an agent member may borrow from the CLF 
for its own liquidity needs but, to do so, the agent must first 
subscribe to the capital stock of the CLF in an amount equal to one-
half of one percent of the Agent's own paid-in and unimpaired capital 
and surplus.\12\ Paragraph (a)(2)(iii) provided further that upon 
approval of the application, the Agent shall forward funds equal to 
one-half of this stock subscription to the CLF. Paragraph (a)(2)(iii) 
also provided that this amount shall be in addition to the amounts 
required by paragraph (a)(2)(i) or (ii) of Sec.  725.4, if a corporate 
credit union or corporate credit union group joined the CLF as an Agent 
and intended to borrow for its own liquidity needs. Finally, paragraph 
(a)(2)(iii) provided that any corporate credit union or corporate 
credit union group that received a CLF advance for its own liquidity 
need under the temporary requirements set forth in this paragraph must, 
as of January 1, 2021, and thereafter, not request any additional CLF 
advances for its own liquidity needs and continue to follow the terms 
of the CLF advance agreement entered into between the Agent and the 
CLF. The Board believed the inclusion of this provision appropriately 
accounted for the temporary nature of this provision while assuring 
agents that loan agreements made during this period will not also be 
subject to a sunset provision or be terminated before maturity. The 
Board believed the provision struck the appropriate balance between 
congressional intent and the tenets of contract law.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Pursuant to the extension provided in the CAA, the Board's March 
24th IFR extended the periods in Sec.  725.4(a)(2)(iii) to end on 
January 1, 2022. The expanded authority provided for in current 
paragraph (a)(2)(iii) has expired by its own terms and no loans remain 
outstanding. Accordingly, this proposal would remove the language in 
current Sec.  725.4(a)(2)(iii) and renumber the section to conform with 
this change.

H. Application Requirements for Corporate Credit Unions Under Sec.  
725.4(a)(3) Through (a)(6)

    Current Sec.  725.4(a)(3) through (a)(6) set forth certain 
requirements that a corporate credit union or a group of corporate 
credit unions applying to become an Agent member of the CLF must 
follow. In particular, current paragraph (a)(3) requires the applicant 
to furnish a copy of the corporate credit union's or unions' financial 
and statistical report for the most recent calendar month; copies of 
the credit union's or unions' charter and bylaws, unless such credit 
union is federally chartered; and a list of all the credit union's or 
unions' member natural person credit unions. Current paragraph (a)(4) 
requires the applicant to agree to submit to the supervision of the 
Board and to comply with all regulations and reporting requirements 
which the Board shall prescribe for Agent members. Current paragraph 
(a)(5) requires the applicant to agree to submit to periodic 
unrestricted examinations by the Board or its designee. Current 
paragraph (a)(6) requires the applicant to obtain the written approval 
of the Board. These requirements were adopted in nearly identical form 
in 1979.\13\ Copies of the aforementioned reports, documents, 
agreements, and written approvals remains necessary for the CLF to 
approve a corporate credit union or group of corporate credit unions' 
application to become an agent member of the CLF. For a federally 
insured corporate credit union or a federally insured group of 
corporate credit unions, however, copies of the credit union's or 
unions' financial and statistical report for the most recent calendar 
month, copies of the credit union's or unions' charter and bylaws, and 
a list of all the credit union's or unions' member natural person 
credit unions are already available to the CLF through the examination 
process. In addition, the examination and reporting provisions under 
current paragraphs (a)(4) and (a)(5) are already codified in the FCU 
Act.\14\ Finally, while approval of the Board is required under the FCU 
Act,\15\ the NCUA believes including an express requirement that the 
Board approval must be in writing as currently required under paragraph 
(b)(6) is unnecessary. Accordingly, to streamline the NCUA's CLF 
regulations and eliminate outdated and unnecessary regulatory 
requirements, the NCUA is proposing to remove current Sec.  725.4(a)(3) 
through (a)(6) in their entirety.

I. Criteria Corporate Credit Unions Must Meet To Be Agent Members Under 
Sec.  725.4(b)

    Current Sec.  725.4(b) requires that the Board may approve a 
corporate credit union or group of corporate credit unions as an Agent 
member if certain criteria are met. In particular, a corporate credit 
union or each corporate credit union in a group must meet the following 
criteria:
    1. The management policies are in writing, approved by the 
corporate credit union's board of directors, and reviewed annually by 
such board;
    2. Adequate internal controls are in place to assure accurate and 
timely reporting of transactions and the safeguarding of assets;
    3. The financial condition of the corporate credit union is sound 
with adequate reserves for losses;
    4. Surety bond coverage provides protection for the corporate 
credit union while the corporate credit union is performing the duties 
of an Agent member of the CLF;
    5. Management has demonstrated its ability to use such techniques 
as cash flow analysis, budgeting, and projections of sources and uses 
of funds to manage the affairs of the corporate credit union 
efficiently and in conformity with sound business practices; and
    6. There are no practices, procedures, policies, or other factors 
that would result in discrimination by the corporate credit union among 
natural person credit unions or inhibit its ability to act 
independently in its role as an Agent member of the CLF.
    The criteria listed in paragraph (b) were originally adopted in a 
1979 final rule regarding CLF membership and lending.\17\ A 
comprehensive explanation of how or why the criteria were chosen was 
not provided.\18\ The criteria, however, appear to have been adopted to 
implement 12 U.S.C. 1795c(b)(1), which requires that an agent member of 
the CLF ``obtain[ ] the approval of the Board.'' While Board approval 
is required under the Federal Credit Union Act, the particular criteria 
listed in Sec.  725.4(b) are not expressly listed in the Act. After 
careful review of the criteria, the Board has determined that the list 
of criteria is obsolete and does not provide a full, accurate list of 
all the criteria that should be considered, particularly since 
extensions of credit sometimes requires additional analysis to protect 
the CLF. Accordingly, the Board is proposing to remove current 
paragraph (b) to streamline the NCUA's CLF regulations and eliminate 
outdated regulatory requirements. Consistent with the

[[Page 65485]]

removal of paragraph (b), the proposed rule would renumber the 
remaining paragraphs in Sec.  725.4.

J. Independent Audits of Books and Records Under Sec.  725.4(d)

    Current Sec.  725.4(d) requires that each Agent, or in the case of 
an Agent group, each corporate credit union in the group, must have on 
an annual basis a third-party independent audit of its books and 
records and provide the CLF with copies of the report of such audit. 
Paragraph (d) provides further that the auditor selected must be 
recognized by a State or territorial licensing authority as possessing 
the requisite knowledge and experience to perform audits. Section 
704.15 already requires that federally insured corporate credit unions 
provide independently audited financial statements to the NCUA. The 
Board believes duplicating that requirement in current Sec.  725.4(d) 
is redundant and unnecessary. No such requirement exists, however, for 
non-federally insured corporate credit unions. Accordingly, to 
eliminate outdated, duplicative, and unnecessary regulatory 
requirements, the Board is proposing to revise the language in current 
Sec.  725.4(d) to limit its application to only non-federally insured 
corporate credit unions. Accordingly, the Board is proposing to revise 
the language in current paragraph (d) to provide that each non-
federally insured Agent, or in the case of an Agent group, each non-
federally insured corporate credit union in the group, must have on an 
annual basis a third-party independent audit of its books and records 
and provide the CLF with copies of the report of such audit. The 
proposed language would provide further that the auditor selected must 
be recognized by a State or territorial licensing authority as 
possessing the requisite knowledge and experience to perform audits. 
Consistent with other edits discussed above to Sec.  725.4, current 
paragraph (d) would be renumbered as paragraph (c).

K. Limit on CLF Advances for Six Months After Membership Under Sec.  
725.4(f)

    Current Sec.  725.4(f) provides that a corporate credit union or 
group of corporate credit unions which becomes an Agent member of the 
CLF after February 23, 1980, may not receive a CLF advance without 
approval of the CLF for a period of six months after becoming a member. 
The date, February 23, 1980, has long since past and is no longer 
necessary in paragraph (f). Accordingly, to streamline the NCUA's CLF 
regulations and eliminate outdated and unnecessary provisions, the 
Board is proposing to remove the reference to February 23, 1980, in its 
entirety. No substantiative changes to the requirements of paragraph 
(f) are intended by this amendment. Consistent with the other edits 
discussed above to Sec.  725.4, current paragraph (f) would be 
renumbered as paragraph (e).

L. Payments to the Capital Stock Subscription Under Sec.  725.5(b)(2)

    Current Sec.  725.5(b)(2) requires, among other things, that 
payments for adjustments to the capital stock subscription must be 
received by the CLF no later than March 31 of the following year. This 
timing requirement is overly restrictive and has led to unnecessarily 
difficult turn-round times for both CLF members and CLF staff. The NCUA 
Board believes that providing additional time for calculating, 
processing, and making such payments would benefit CLF members and CLF 
staff. Accordingly, the NCUA is proposing to amend the last sentence in 
current Sec.  725.5(b)(2) to provide that payments for adjustments must 
be received within 90 calendar days of receipt of a capital stock 
subscription invoice from the CLF.

M. Termination of Membership Under 725.6

    The April 29th IFR amended the waiting periods for a credit union 
to terminate its membership in the CLF between April 29, 2020, and 
January 1, 2022. Under the FCU Act and prior Sec.  725.6 of the NCUA's 
regulations, a credit union member could terminate its membership after 
a specified time based on that credit union's stock subscription in the 
CLF. Previously, a member of the CLF could terminate its membership as 
follows:
    1. Six months after notifying the Board in writing of its intention 
to do so, if the member's stock subscription constituted less than five 
percent of total subscribed CLF stock; or
    2. Twenty-four months after notifying the Board in writing of its 
intention to do so if the member's stock subscription constituted five 
percent or more of total subscribed CLF stock.\19\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \19\ 12 CFR 725.6.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The April 29th IFR amended Sec.  725.6 to temporarily permit a 
credit union, regardless of its percentage amount of stock 
subscription, to withdraw from membership in the CLF after notifying 
the Board in writing on the earlier of the following:
    (A) six months from the date of its written notice to the Board; or
    (B) December 31, 2020.
    Further, the April 29th IFR amended the NCUA's regulations to allow 
any credit union that remained a member after December 31, 2020, to 
withdraw from membership immediately upon notifying the Board in 
writing of its intent to do so. The Board noted that such an immediate 
withdrawal period would expire on December 31, 2021. After December 31, 
2021, the termination requirements of current paragraphs (a) and (b) of 
this section would be reinstated and apply to all members. The Board 
believed that this flexibility was necessary to encourage the greatest 
number of eligible credit unions to join the CLF.
    The March 24th IFR extended the termination of membership 
provisions described above until January 1, 2023. Pursuant to self-
executing language in the April 29th IFR and the March 24th IFR, the 
provisions expired on January 1, 2023, and were removed from Sec.  
725.6 of the NCUA's regulations by the Federal Register in 2023. Thus, 
this proposed rule would not affect these provisions.

N. Agent Member Borrowing Under Sec.  725.17(b)(2)(iv)

    The April 29th IFR amended Sec.  725.17 to add new paragraph 
(b)(2)(iv), which clarified that an agent may apply for a CLF advance 
based on the applicant agent's own liquidity needs. Pursuant to the 
extensions provided by the CAA, the Board's March 24th IFR amended 
paragraph (b)(2)(iv) further to provide that an agent may apply for a 
CLF advance based on the applicant agent's own liquidity needs, for the 
period beginning April 29, 2020, and ending on December 31, 2021. The 
March 24th IFR provided further that, after this period, an agent is 
prohibited from submitting an application for an extension for its own 
liquidity needs. Given the provision in current paragraph (b)(2)(iv) 
ended by its own terms on December 31, 2021, this proposal would amend 
Sec.  725.17 to remove the paragraph from the NCUA's regulations.

O. Applications for Extensions of Credit Under Sec.  725.17(a) and (b)

    Current Sec.  725.17(a) provides that a Regular member may apply 
for a CLF advance to meet its liquidity needs by filing an application 
on a CLF-approved form, or by any other method approved by the CLF. 
Current Sec.  725.17(b)(1) provides in relevant part that an Agent 
member may apply for a CLF advance by filing an application on a CLF-
approved form, or by any other method approved by the CLF. Footnote 4 
at the end of paragraph (b)(1) provides further that if the Agent is an 
Agent group, the

[[Page 65486]]

application must be filed by the Agent group representative, and any 
CLF advance will be made to the Agent group representative. To 
streamline the NCUA's CLF regulations and eliminate outdated and 
unnecessary provisions, the Board is proposing to merge the language in 
paragraphs (a) and (b)(1) into a revised paragraph (a) and to also 
incorporate into revised paragraph (a) the text of footnote 4. Proposed 
Sec.  725.17(a) would provide that a Regular member or Agent member may 
apply for a CLF advance to meet its liquidity needs by filing an 
application on a CLF-approved form, or by any other method approved by 
the CLF. Proposed paragraph (a) would provide further that if the Agent 
is an Agent group, the application must be filed by the Agent group 
representative and the advance will be made to the representative.

P. Agent Member Borrowing Under Sec. Sec.  725.18 and 725.19

    The April 29th IFR made conforming changes to Sec. Sec.  725.18 and 
725.19 to clarify the requirements applicable to a CLF advance to an 
agent for such agent's own needs. In particular, the IFR amended Sec.  
725.18(a) to provide that, prior to CLF approval of each application of 
a regular member for a CLF advance or an agent member for a CLF advance 
for such agent member's own need, the CLF shall consider the 
creditworthiness of such member. The Board's March 24th IFR clarified 
paragraph (a) to provide that, prior to CLF approval of each 
application of a regular member for a CLF advance or an agent member 
for a CLF advance for such agent member's own need (provided such agent 
may submit an application under Sec.  725.17(b)(2)(iv)), the CLF shall 
consider the creditworthiness of such member. Given the provisions in 
Sec.  725.17(b)(2)(iv) lapsed on January 1, 2022, this proposal would 
amend current Sec.  725.18(a) to remove the conforming and clarifying 
changes made by both the April 29th IFR and the March 24th IFR. 
Accordingly, current paragraph (a) would provide that, prior to CLF 
approval of each application of a regular member for a CLF advance, the 
CLF shall consider the creditworthiness of such member.
    In addition, the April 29th IFR amended Sec.  725.18(d) to provide 
that a credit union (whether a regular member of the CLF, agent member, 
or a member natural person credit union) that does not meet the CLF's 
creditworthiness standards may be limited in or denied the use of 
advances for its liquidity needs. The March 24th IFR clarified 
paragraph (d) to provide that a credit union (whether a regular member 
of the CLF, agent member (provided such agent may submit an application 
under Sec.  725.17(b)(2)(iv), or a member natural person credit union) 
which does not meet the CLF's creditworthiness standards may be limited 
in or denied the use of advances for its liquidity needs. Given the 
provisions in Sec.  725.17(b)(2)(iv) lapsed on January 1, 2022, this 
proposal would amend current Sec.  725.18(d) to remove the conforming 
and clarifying changes made by both the April 29th IFR and the March 
24th IFR. Accordingly, current paragraph (d) would provide that a 
natural person credit union (whether a regular member of the CLF or a 
member natural person credit union) which does not meet the CLF's 
creditworthiness standards may be limited in or denied the use of 
advances for its liquidity needs.
    The April 29th IFR also made conforming changes to Sec.  725.19(a) 
to provide that each CLF advance and each agent loan shall be secured 
by a first priority security interest in collateral of the credit union 
with a net book value at least equal to an amount as required by the 
CLF's collateral table, published at www.NCUA.gov, or by guarantee of 
the National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund (NCUSIF). The amendments 
to paragraph (a) also reduced the amount of collateral required for 
certain assets used to secure each CLF advance and each agent loan. 
Section 725.19 previously required that each CLF advance and each agent 
loan be secured by a first priority security interest in collateral of 
the credit union with a net-book value at least equal to 110 percent of 
all amounts due under the applicable CLF advance or agent loan, or by 
guarantee of the NCUSIF. The collateral table varies the required 
collateral percentages based upon different types of assets, and in 
some cases requires less than 110 percent. Depending on the types of 
assets a member has available to secure an advance request, lowering 
the collateral requirement for certain types of assets eased the 
collateral requirements and permitted a greater amount of borrowing 
overall. The Board replaced the 110 percent requirement with a 
requirement that a credit union collateralize a CLF advance or agent 
loan per the CLF collateral table posted on the NCUA's website, 
www.NCUA.gov. This proposed rule would adopts as final the conforming 
changes to Sec.  725.19(a) by the April 29th IFR.
    The April 29th IFR also made conforming changes to Sec.  725.19(b) 
to provide that the CLF may accept as collateral for each CLF advance 
to a regular member or to an agent member, for such agent member's own 
needs, a security interest in all assets of the member; provided, 
however, that the value of any assets in which any third party has a 
perfected security interest that is superior to the security interest 
of the CLF shall be excluded for purposes of complying with the 
requirements. Given the provisions in Sec.  725.17(b)(2)(iv) lapsed on 
January 1, 2022, this proposal would amend current Sec.  725.19(b) to 
remove the conforming changes made by the April 29th IFR. Accordingly, 
Sec.  725.19(b) would provide that the CLF may accept as collateral for 
each CLF advance to a regular member, a security interest in all assets 
of the regular member; provided, however, that the value of any assets 
in which any third party has a perfected security interest that is 
superior to the security interest of the CLF shall be excluded for 
purposes of complying with the requirements.

Q. Collateral Requirements Under Sec.  725.19(a)

    Current Sec.  725.19(a) provides that each CLF advance and each 
Agent loan shall be secured by a first-priority security interest in 
collateral of the credit union with a net value at least equal to an 
amount as required by the CLF's collateral margins table, published at 
www.NCUA.gov, or by guarantee of the National Credit Union Share 
Insurance Fund. To account for the CLF's preferred use of terminology 
with regard to the terms ``net book value'' and ``collateral table,'' 
the proposed rule would amend Sec.  725.19(a) to instead use the more 
commonly used terms ``net value'' and ``collateral margins table.'' 
Accordingly, the proposal would amend Sec.  725.19(a) to provide that 
each CLF advance and each agent loan shall be secured by a first-
priority security interest in collateral of the credit union with a net 
value at least equal to an amount as required by the CLF's collateral 
margins table, published at www.NCUA.gov, or by guarantee of the 
NCUSIF.

A. Accountability Through Transparency Act of 2023

    The Providing Accountability Through Transparency Act of 2023 (5 
U.S.C. 553(b)(4)) (Act) requires that a notice of proposed rulemaking 
include the internet address of a summary of not more than 100 words in 
length of a proposed rule, in plain language, that shall be posted on 
the internet website under section 206(d) of the E-Government Act of 
2002 (44 U.S.C. 3501

[[Page 65487]]

note) (commonly known as regulations.gov).
    The proposed rule would adopt as final the revisions to the CLF 
rule made under two IFRs published on April 29, 2020, and March 24, 
2021. The proposal would adopt the amendments made by these IFRs and, 
where appropriate, would remove provisions from the NCUA's regulations 
that have lapsed under the terms of the IFRs. The proposal would also 
remove or modify certain filing requirements and criteria the Board 
uses to approve CLF members. Finally, the proposal would amend the 
timing requirements related to pay adjustments to the capital stock 
subscription, clarify various regulatory provisions, and make necessary 
conforming edits.
    The proposal and the required summary can be found at https://www.regulations.gov.

C. Regulatory Flexibility Act

    The Regulatory Flexibility Act \23\ generally requires an agency to 
conduct a regulatory flexibility analysis of any rule subject to notice 
and comment rulemaking requirements, unless the agency certifies that 
the rule will not have a significant economic impact on a substantial 
number of small entities. If the agency makes such a certification, it 
shall publish the certification at the time of publication of either 
the proposed rule or the final rule, along with a statement providing 
the factual basis for such certification.\24\ For purposes of this 
analysis, the NCUA considers small credit unions to be those having 
under $100 million in assets.\25\ The Board fully considered the 
potential economic impacts of the regulatory amendments on small credit 
unions.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \23\ 5 U.S.C. 601 et seq.
    \24\ 5 U.S.C. 605(b).
    \25\ 80 FR 57512 (Sept. 24, 2015).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    This proposed rule would adopt certain IFR changes as final and 
remove certain criteria the Board requires to approve CLF members. 
These amendments would not impose any requirements on regulated 
entities, particularly not small entities. Accordingly, the NCUA 
certifies the proposed rule would not have a significant economic 
impact on a substantial number of small credit unions.

D. Paperwork Reduction Act

    The Paperwork Reduction Act of 1995 (PRA) generally provides that 
an agency may not conduct or sponsor, and not withstanding any other 
provision of law, a person is not required to respond to, a collection 
of information, unless it displays a currently valid Office of 
Management and Budget control number. The PRA applies to rulemakings in 
which an agency creates a new or amends existing information collection 
requirements. For purposes of the PRA, an information-collection 
requirement may take the form of a reporting, recordkeeping, or a 
third-party disclosure requirement. The NCUA has determined that the 
changes addressed in this notice do not create a new information 
collection or revise an existing information collection as defined by 
the PRA.

E. Executive Order 13132 on Federalism

    Executive Order 13132 encourages certain agencies to consider the 
impact of their actions on state and local interests. The proposed rule 
would adopt as final the amendments made by two IFRs and, where 
appropriate, removes provisions from the NCUA's regulations that have 
lapsed under the terms of the IFRs. In addition, the proposed rule 
would remove certain criteria the Board requires to approve CLF 
members. Any effect on the states, on the relationship between the 
National Government and the states, or on the distribution of power and 
responsibilities among the various levels of government does not rise 
to the level of material impact for purposed of Executive Order 13132.

F. Assessment of Federal Regulations and Policies on Families

    The NCUA has determined that this proposed rule will not affect 
family well-being within the meaning of section 654 of the Treasury and 
General Government Appropriations Act, 1999, Public Law 105-277, 112 
Stat. 2681 (1998) given that the proposed rule would implement changes 
already made to the NCUA's regulations through the April 29, 2020, and 
March 24, 2021 IFRs, and would remove certain criteria the Board 
requires to approve CLF members.

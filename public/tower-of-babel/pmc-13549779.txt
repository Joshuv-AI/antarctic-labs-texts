Editors’ Pick in September 2026
Authors: Hee-Jin Yang
Published: 2026

Among the 16 papers published in the September issue of Journal of Korean Neurosurgical Society (JKNS) 2026, the following two papers, which deserve attention from readers, are selected by the Editorial Boards.

Treatment with neuronal-induced human mesenchymal stem cells improves functional recovery of acute spinal cord injury through attenuating astrogliosis and neurotoxic astrocyte activation [2]

Given its high socioeconomic burden, considerable research efforts have been dedicated to developing treatments for spinal cord injury (SCI). While reactive astrocytes and the resulting glial scar have long been viewed as major obstacles to SCI recovery by forming a physical barrier to axonal regeneration, recent studies have revealed a dual role for astrocytes. They demonstrate not only detrimental effects but also protective functions, such as limiting scar expansion and promoting axonal regeneration.

Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) have been extensively studied as therapeutic candidates for SCI due to their self-renewal capacity, multipotency, and plasticity, alongside their potent immunomodulatory properties. The authors previously reported that MSCs improve functional outcomes in acute SCI by modulating astrogliosis [1]. The authors conducted the present study to elucidate the mechanisms by which MSCs affect reactive astrocytes, utilizing neural-induced human adipose-derived MSCs (NI-hADSCs). It was hypothesized that NI-hADSCs modulate reactive astrocyte phenotypes during the acute phase of SCI and sought to determine their potential in regulating astrogliosis.

SCI was induced using an aneurysm clip application model. NI-hADSCs were transplanted into the perilesional spinal cord within 1 hour post-injury. Motor function recovery was assessed using the Basso-Beattie-Bresnahan (BBB) locomotor rating scale. Western blot analysis was performed to evaluate the protein levels of gliosis markers and neuroinflammatory pathways. Histopathological examination was conducted at 6 weeks post-injury to assess astrogliosis and astrocyte phenotypic changes.

The NI-hADSC-treated group showed significantly enhanced functional recovery, accompanied by reduced levels of glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), vimentin, and complement component 3 (C3), alongside elevated levels of total and phosphorylated signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3/p-STAT3). Immunohistochemistry confirmed attenuated astrogliosis and a lower proportion of C3-expressing astrocytes in the NI-hADSC-treated group, predominantly with-in the dorsal perilesional spinal cord. Furthermore, a substantial increase in B-cell lymphoma-2 (Bcl-2) expression (p<0.01) and a decrease in Bcl-2-associated X protein (Bax) expression (p<0.01) indicated that NI-hADSC treatment effectively suppressed neuronal apoptosis.

This study demonstrates that NI-hADSC transplantation improves functional recovery following SCI by modulating astrogliosis. This modulation is likely mediated through the selective suppression of neurotoxic A1 astrocytes, potentially via the STAT3 signaling pathway. These findings are significant in highlighting the therapeutic potential of MSCs in promoting functional recovery through acute-phase astrogliosis modulation after SCI.

Neurocognitive changes and cerebral blood flow in patients with symptomatic carotid artery stenosis before and after carotid endarterectomy [4]

Internal carotid artery (ICA) stenosis is a well-known contributor to ischemic stroke and transient ischemic attacks (TIA), particularly among the elderly, accounting for approximately 10% of all strokes. Symptomatic ICA stenosis has also been implicated in cognitive decline, likely mediated by chronic cerebral hypoperfusion and embolic events. Alterations in cerebral blood flow (CBF) are thought to play a crucial role in these cognitive changes, with studies demonstrating that reduced CBF in stenosis-affected regions correlates with cognitive impairment [3].

However, evidence linking carotid endarterectomy (CEA) to cognitive improvement remains inconclusive due to small sample sizes, short follow-up periods, and variable cognitive assessment methods across previous studies. Furthermore, the precise mechanisms through which CEA influences cognitive outcomes—whether by alleviating hypoperfusion, reducing embolic load, or other factors—remain incompletely understood.

In this single-center, prospective observational study, the authors evaluated patients with recent TIA and confirmed severe symptomatic carotid artery stenosis who underwent CEA. Cognitive function was assessed using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), CBF was measured via arterial spin labeling magnetic resonance imaging (ASL-MRI) preoperatively, as well as at 1 and 6 months postoperatively.

A total of forty-one patients (mean age, 66.8 years; 75.6% male) were analyzed. At baseline, patients with left ICA stenosis demonstrated significantly lower naming scores compared to controls, whereas those with right ICA stenosis exhibited lower total MoCA scores. Significant improvements in both cognitive performance and CBF were observed at 1 month post-surgery, with gains sustained at 6 months. A positive correlation (R=0.437, p<0.01) was identified between CBF increase and cognitive improvement.

The present study demonstrates a significant correlation between TIA onset, reduced CBF ipsilateral to severe stenosis, and overall cognitive impairment. Crucially, neuropsychological scores improved significantly 1 month after CEA, with the degree of recovery positively correlating with the extent of restored CBF. These findings underscore that CEA can substantially enhance both cerebral perfusion and cognitive function in patients with severe symptomatic carotid artery stenosis, highlighting its clinical significance beyond stroke prevention.

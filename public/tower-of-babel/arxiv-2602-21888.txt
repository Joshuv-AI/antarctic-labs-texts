Endpoint Variation and jump inequalities for rough singular integrals
Ankit Bhojak, Saurabh Shrivastava

Abstract
. In this article, we prove weak type ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) bounds for the variation and jump operators corresponding to the family of truncations of singular integrals with rough kernels. This resolves an open question raised by Jones, Seeger and Wright (Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. (2008)). Moreover, as an immediate consequence of the variational estimate, we recover the weak type ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) boundedness of the maximal truncation operator corresponding to singular integrals with rough kernels.

Introduction

The study of variational and jump inequalities is a recurrent theme in harmonic analysis and ergodic theory. A vast amount of research, see for example
[
21
,
2
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
4
,
5
,
17
]
have been devoted to the study of such inequalities. This article is concerned with the endpoint estimates for variational and jump operators for the family of truncations of singular integral operators with rough kernels defined as follows:

T_{\Omega,\epsilon}f(x)=\int_{|x-y|>\epsilon}\Omega\left(\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\right)|x-y|^{-d}f(y)dy,

where
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1})
with
\int\Omega(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)=0
.

Calderón and Zygmund
[
3
]
showed that the operators
T_{\Omega}
and its maximal variant
T_{\Omega}^{*}
defined as

T_{\Omega}f(x)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}T_{\Omega,\epsilon}f(x),\quad T_{\Omega}^{*}f(x)=\sup_{\epsilon>0}|T_{\Omega,\epsilon}f(x)|

are bounded in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for
1<p<\infty
. We also refer to
[
9
]
for an alternate proof involving a double dyadic analysis and Fourier transform estimates. At the endpoint
p=1
, Christ and Rubio de Francia
[
6
]
and independently Hofmann
[
10
]
showed that
T_{\Omega}
maps
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for
d=2
. The weak
(1,1)
boundedness for
T_{\Omega}
was extended for all dimensions
d\geq 2
by Seeger
[
24
]
using a microlocal analysis of the kernel. The boundedness of the maximal operator
T_{\Omega}^{*}
at the endpoint
p=1
remained open until very recently. Honzík
[
11
]
showed that
T_{\Omega}^{*}
maps the space
L(\log\log L)^{2+\epsilon}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
locally for
\epsilon>0
and
\Omega\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1})
. A further improvement was given by the first author and Mohanty in
[
1
]
, where they showed that
T_{\Omega}^{*}
maps the larger space
L\log\log L({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1})
. Recently, Lai
[
20
]
showed that
T_{\Omega}^{*}
indeed maps
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

In this paper, we are concerned with the variation and jump inequalities corresponding to the families of truncations of singular integrals with rough kernels. For
1<q\leq\infty
, consider the
q-
variations associated to a family of functions
\{F_{t}:t\in\mathcal{I}\}
defined as follows

V_{q}(F_{t}(x):t\in\mathcal{I})=\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots t_{N}\\
t_{i}\in\mathcal{I}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}|F_{t_{i+1}}(x)-F_{t_{i}}(x)|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\;&1<q<\infty\\
\sup\limits_{t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathcal{I}:t_{1}<t_{2}}|F_{t_{1}}(x)-F_{t_{2}}(x)|,\;&q=\infty.\end{cases}

The
\lambda-
jump corresponding to the family of functions
\{F_{t}:t\in\mathcal{I}\}
is defined as follows

\displaystyle N_{\lambda}(F_{t}(x):t\in\mathcal{I})=\sup\Big\{N\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}:

\displaystyle\exists t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{N}\in\mathcal{I},\;0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{N}\;\text{such that}

\displaystyle|F_{t_{i+1}}(x)-F_{t_{i}}(x)|>\lambda\Big\}.

We refer the reader to
[
17
]
for more details about the notion of variational and jump inequalities. For notational convenience, we write
V_{q}(F_{t}(x))=V_{q}(F_{t}(x):t\in(0,\infty))
for the special case
\mathcal{I}=(0,\infty)
. Similarly, we set
N_{\lambda}(F_{t}(x))=N_{\lambda}(F_{t}(x):t\in(0,\infty))
.

Campbell, Jones, Reinhold and Wierdl
[
4
,
5
]
investigated the
L^{p}-
bounds of variational and jump inequalities for the family
\{T_{\Omega,\epsilon}:\;\epsilon\in(0,\infty)\}
. In particular, they proved the following bounds for
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1})
and
1<p<\infty
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\|V_{q}(T_{\Omega,\epsilon}f)\right\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p},\quad\text{for}\;q>2,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\lambda(N_{\lambda}(T_{\Omega,\epsilon}f))^{\frac{1}{\rho}}\right\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p},\quad\text{for}\;\rho>2.

Jones, Seeger and Wright
[
17
]
established the strong type inequality (
1.2
) at the critical case
\rho=2
for
\Omega\in L^{r}({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}),\;1<r\leq\infty
. The estimate (
1.2
) for
\rho=2
and
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1})
was obtained by Ding, Hong and Liu
[
7
]
.

In
[
4
,
5
]
, the following weak type
(1,1)
bounds, when
\Omega
is a Lipschitz function, were also established.

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\;V_{q}(T_{\Omega,\epsilon}f(x))>\alpha\right\}\right|\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\alpha},\quad\text{for}\;q>2,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\;\lambda(N_{\lambda}(T_{\Omega,\epsilon}f(x)))^{\frac{1}{\rho}}>\alpha\right\}\right|\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\alpha},\quad\text{for}\;\rho>2.

In
[
17
]
the weak type
(1,1)
inequality (
1.4
) was proved at the critical case
\rho=2
for
\Omega
being a Lipschitz function. In the same paper, the question of weak type
(1,1)
boundedness of the underlying operator in the absence of any smoothness assumption on
\Omega
was posed and left open. In this paper, we resolve this question affirmatively; this constitutes the main result of our work.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1})
. Then, for all
\alpha>0
, we have

(1.5)

\left|\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\lambda[N_{\lambda}(T_{\Omega,\epsilon}f(x))]^{\frac{1}{2}}>\alpha\right\}\right|\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\alpha}.

Moreover for
q>2
, we have

(1.6)

\left|\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\;V_{q}(T_{\Omega,\epsilon}f(x))>\alpha\right\}\right|\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\alpha}.

By a standard limiting argument,
Theorem

1.1
recovers the weak
(1,1)-
boundedness of
T_{\Omega}^{*}
proved recently by Lai
[
20
]
.

Methodology of the proof of
Theorem

1.1
:

We first note that by
[
23
, Lemma 2.3]
, the weak type
(1,1)
estimate (
1.6
) for the
q-
variation operator will follow from the corresponding weak type estimate (
1.5
) involving the jump function. Further, it is well known that in order to prove (
1.5
), it is enough to consider the short and long jump operators separately via the inequality (
2
). In both the cases, the variational Rademacher-Menshov theorem is a key ingredient. This result was first obtained in
[
22
]
. We will use the following version of the variational Rademacher-Menshov theorem from
[
8
, Lemma 7.2]
.

Lemma 1.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\{F_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a sequence of functions on a measure space
X
such that for all
\varepsilon_{i}\in\{-1,0,1\}
, we have

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}F_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq B.

Then, the following estimate holds

\left\|V_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}:1\leq n\leq N\right)\right\|_{2}\lesssim(\log N)B.

The second ingredient consists of microlocal estimates of Seeger
[
24
]
. Our task is to combine the
L^{1}
and
L^{2}
estimates of Seeger
[
24
]
into a single
L^{2}
estimate. This step relies on a careful interpolation argument, motivated by the approach in
[
11
]
. We provide the details of this estimate in
Appendix

A
.

The main distinction between the short and long jumps is the presence of multiscale analysis in the estimate for the long jump. The estimate for the short jumps relies on a single scale argument reminiscent of the ideas developed in
[
5
]
. In contrast, the estimates for the long jumps are based on an algorithm that controls the interaction of scales, pioneered in the work of Krause and Lacey
[
18
,
19
]
for obtaining sparse bounds for maximal truncations of oscillatory Calderón-Zygmund operators. We also refer to
[
13
]
for a simplified approach to proving endpoint estimates for such maximal oscillatory operators in dimension one.

In the process of adapting the ideas of
[
19
]
, we streamline certain aspects of their proof. First, we do away with the recursion argument employed there. Second, we introduce an effective decomposition of the bad functions based on the shifted dyadic grids. This allows us to use the
L^{2}-
bounds (
Lemma

A.2
) as a blackbox instead of arduously reproving similar estimates for a localised version of the truncations of rough singular integrals. As a consequence, this approach provides us with a new and simpler proof of the weak
(1,1)-
boundedness of
T_{\Omega}^{*}
. The proof of
Theorem

1.1
(
1.5
) is given in
Section

2
.

2.
Proof of
Theorem

1.1
(
1.5
)

Let
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\lambda>0
. By the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition, applied to
f
at height
\lambda
, there exists a family
\mathcal{Q}
of disjoint dyadic cubes such that

(1)

f=g+b
with
b=\sum_{s\in{\mathbb{Z}}}B^{s}
, where
B^{s}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}:|Q|=2^{sd}}b_{Q}
,

(2)

\|g\|_{\infty}\lesssim\lambda
and
\|g\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{1}
,

(3)

\int b_{Q}(x)dx=0
and
\operatorname{supp}(b_{Q})\subseteq Q
,

(4)

\|b_{Q}\|_{1}\lesssim\alpha|Q|
and
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}|Q|\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha}
. In particular, this implies
\sum_{s}\|B^{s}\|_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}
.

We define the exceptional set
E=\cup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}4Q
. Thus
|E|\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\alpha}
. By the
L^{2}-
boundedness
[
7
, Theorem 1.2 (ii)]
of the
\lambda-
jump operators associated to
\{T_{\Omega,\epsilon}:\epsilon>0\}
, we have

\displaystyle|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\lambda[N_{\lambda}(T_{\Omega,\epsilon}g(x):\epsilon\in(0,\infty))]^{\frac{1}{2}}>\alpha\}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\|g\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\alpha}.

Therefore it remains to estimate for the bad part
b
. Let
\psi
be a Schwartz function supported in the annulus
\{\frac{1}{4}\leq|y|\leq 1\}
and
\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi(2^{-j}y)=1
. We decompose the kernel as follows

K(y)=\Omega\left(\frac{y}{|y|}\right)|y|^{-d}=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Omega\left(\frac{y}{|y|}\right)|y|^{-d}\psi(2^{-j}y)=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}K_{j}(y).

We note that by the support of the kernels
K_{j}
, it follows that

\sum_{s<5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(K_{j}\chi_{|\cdot|>\epsilon})*B^{j-s}(x)=0,\quad\text{for}\;x\in E^{c}.

Thus, we have that

\lambda[N_{\lambda}(T_{\Omega,\epsilon}b(x):\epsilon\in(0,\infty))]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\lambda\left[N_{\lambda}\left(\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(K_{j}\chi_{|\cdot|>\epsilon})*B^{j-s}(x):\epsilon\in(0,\infty)\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For each
s\geq 5
, we write
K_{j}=S_{j}^{s}+H_{j}^{s}
, where

\displaystyle S_{j}^{s}(y)

\displaystyle=K_{j}(y)\chi_{\left\{\Omega\left(\frac{y}{|y|}\right)>2^{\delta_{0}s}\|\Omega\|_{1}\right\}}(y),

\displaystyle H_{j}^{s}(y)

\displaystyle=K_{j}(y)\chi_{\left\{\Omega\left(\frac{y}{|y|}\right)\leq 2^{\delta_{0}s}\|\Omega\|_{1}\right\}}(y),

for some fixed
\delta_{0}>0
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda[N_{\lambda}(T_{\Omega,\epsilon}b:\epsilon\in(0,\infty))]^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\left[N_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\left(\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(S_{j}^{s}\chi_{|\cdot|>\epsilon})*B^{j-s}(x):\epsilon\in(0,\infty)\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\lambda\left[N_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\left(\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(H_{j}^{s}\chi_{|\cdot|>\epsilon})*B^{j-s}(x):\epsilon>0\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the term involving operators
S_{j}^{s}
in the above, by the definition of
\lambda-
jumps and variation, we have that

\displaystyle\lambda\left[N_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\left(\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(S_{j}^{s}\chi_{|\cdot|>\epsilon})*B^{j-s}(x):\epsilon\in(0,\infty)\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim V_{1}\left(\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(S_{j}^{s}\chi_{|\cdot|>\epsilon})*B^{j-s}(x):\epsilon\in(0,\infty)\right)

\displaystyle\leq|S_{j}^{s}|*|B^{j-s}|(x).

Hence, we use Chebyshev’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x\in E^{c}:\lambda\left[N_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\left(\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(S_{j}^{s}\chi_{|\cdot|>\epsilon})*B^{j-s}(x):\epsilon\in(0,\infty)\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}>\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|S_{j}^{s}\|_{1}\|B^{j-s}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|B^{j-s}\|_{1}\int_{\Omega(\theta)>2^{\delta_{0}s}\|\Omega\|_{1}}\Omega(\theta)d\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\Omega\|_{L\log L}\|f\|_{1}.

For the term involving operators
H_{j}^{s}
, we dominate the
\lambda-
jumps by the sum of short and long
\lambda-
jumps. This is achieved via a standard argument; for a proof, see
[
17
, Lemma 1.3]
. As a consequence, we obtain the following pointwise inequality:

\displaystyle\lambda\left[N_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\left(\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(H_{j}^{s}\chi_{|\cdot|>\epsilon})*B^{j-s}(x):\epsilon\in(0,\infty)\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle~\mathcal{S}_{2}\left(\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(H_{j}^{s}\chi_{|\cdot|>\epsilon})*B^{j-s}(x):\epsilon\in(0,\infty)\right)

(2.1)

\displaystyle+\lambda\left[N_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\left(\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(H_{j}^{s}\chi_{|\cdot|>2^{k}})*B^{j-s}(x):k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\mathcal{S}_{2}
is the short jump operator defined as
\mathcal{S}_{2}(F_{\epsilon}:\epsilon>0)(x)=\left(\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|V_{2}(F_{\epsilon}:\epsilon\in[2^{k-1},2^{k}])(x)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The second term in the above expression is known as the long jump function.

2.1.
Estimate for short jumps

Since the support of
H_{j}^{s}
is contained in the annulus
\{2^{j-2}\leq|y|\leq 2^{j}\}
, the estimate of
\mathcal{S}_{2}(T_{\Omega,\epsilon}b:\epsilon\in(0,\infty))
is reduced to proving

\left|\left\{x\in E^{c}:\sum_{s\geq 5}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|V_{2}((H^{s}_{k+j}\chi_{|\cdot|>\epsilon})*B^{k+j-s}(x):\epsilon\in[2^{k-1},2^{k}])|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}>\alpha\right\}\right|\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\alpha},\quad\text{for }j=0,1.

We will prove the inequality for
j=0
, the other estimate is similar. By Chebyshev’s and triangle inequality, it is enough to prove the following for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
s\geq 5
,

\left\|V_{2}((H_{k}^{s}\chi_{|\cdot|>\epsilon})*B^{k-s}:\epsilon\in[2^{k-1},2^{k}])\right\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim 2^{-\delta^{\prime}s}\alpha\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}:|Q|=2^{(k-s)n}}\|b_{Q}\|_{1},\;\text{for some}\;\delta^{\prime}>0.

Recalling the definition of
V_{2}
, the above inequality is equivalent to

\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N^{k}(x)}|H^{s}_{k,E_{i}^{k}(x)}*B^{k-s}(x)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}\lesssim 2^{-\delta^{\prime}s}\alpha\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}:|Q|=2^{(k-s)d}}\|b_{Q}\|_{1},

where
H^{s}_{k,E_{i}^{k}(x)}=H^{s}_{k}\chi_{\epsilon_{i-1}^{k}(x)\leq|\cdot|\leq\epsilon_{i}^{k}(x)}
for a measurable choice of functions
N^{k}(x)
and
\epsilon_{i}^{k}(x),\;i=0,\cdots,N^{k}(x)
with
2^{k-1}\leq\epsilon_{0}^{k}(x)<\epsilon_{1}^{k}(x)<\cdots<\epsilon_{N}^{k}(x)(x)\leq 2^{k}
. We define the functions
B^{k-s}_{i,in}
and
B^{k-s}_{i,bd}
as follows

B^{k-s}_{i,in}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}:\\
|Q|=2^{(k-s)d}\\
(x-Q)\subseteq E_{i}^{k}(x)\end{subarray}}b_{Q},\qquad B^{k-s}_{i,bd}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}:\\
|Q|=2^{(k-s)d}\\
(x-Q)\cap(E_{i}^{k}(x))^{c}\neq\phi\end{subarray}}b_{Q},

for
E_{i}^{k}(x)=[\epsilon_{i-1}^{k}(x),\epsilon_{i}^{k}(x)]
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N^{k}(x)}|H^{s}_{k,E_{i}^{k}(x)}*B^{-s}(x)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N^{k}(x)}|H^{s}_{k,E_{i}^{k}(x)}*B^{k-s}_{i,in}(x)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N^{k}(x)}|H^{s}_{k,E_{i}^{k}(x)}*B^{k-s}_{i,bd}(x)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=:I_{in}+I_{bd}.

2.1.1.
Estimate for
I_{bd}
:

We note that
|H^{s}_{k,E_{i}^{k}(x)}|\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{1}2^{\delta_{0}s}2^{-kd}
and the maximum number of cubes
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
with side length
2^{k-s}
such that
(x-Q)\cap\partial E_{i}^{k}(x)\neq\phi
is proportional to
2^{(s)(d-1)}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle|H^{s}_{k,E_{i}^{k}(x)}*B_{i,bd{{}^{-s}}}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{1}2^{\delta_{0}s}2^{-kd}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}:\\
|Q|=2^{(k-s)d}\\
(x-Q)\cap(E_{i}^{k}(x))^{c}\neq\phi\end{subarray}}\|b_{Q}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha\|\Omega\|_{1}2^{\delta_{0}s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}:\\
|Q|=2^{(k-s)d}\\
(x-Q)\cap(E_{i}^{k}(x))^{c}\neq\phi\end{subarray}}|Q|

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha\|\Omega\|_{1}2^{-(1-\delta_{0})s}.

Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle I_{bd}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\sum_{i=1}^{N^{k}(x)}|H^{s}_{k,E_{i}^{k}(x)}*B^{k-s}_{i,bd}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha\|\Omega\|_{1}2^{-(1-\delta_{0})s}\int\sum_{i=1}^{N^{k}(x)}|H^{s}_{k,E_{i}^{k}(x)}*B^{k-s}_{i,bd}(x)|dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha\|\Omega\|_{1}2^{-(1-\delta_{0})s}\int\sum_{i=1}^{N^{k}(x)}|H^{s}_{k,E_{i}^{k}(x)}|*\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}:|Q|=2^{(k-s)d}}|b_{Q}|\right)(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha\|\Omega\|_{1}^{2}2^{-(1-2\delta_{0})s}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}:|Q|=2^{(k-s)d}}\|b_{Q}\|_{1}.

2.1.2.
Estimate for
I_{in}
:

We begin by noting that

\displaystyle H^{s}_{k,E_{i}^{k}(x)}*B^{k-s}_{i,in}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}:\\
|Q|=2^{(k-s)d}\\
(x-Q)\subseteq E_{i}^{k}(x)\end{subarray}}K_{k}*b_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}K_{k}*B^{k-s,m}_{i,in}(x),\;\text{where}

B^{k-s,m}_{i,in}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}:|Q|=2^{(k-s)d},\\
Q\subseteq 2^{k}(m+[0,1]^{d})\\
(x-Q)\subset E_{i}^{k}(x)\end{subarray}}b_{Q},\quad\text{and}\quad B^{k-s,m}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}:|Q|=2^{(k-s)d},\\
Q\subseteq 2^{k}(m+[0,1]^{d})\end{subarray}}b_{Q}.

We note the above quantities are defined only for those admissible
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
for which atleast one such
Q
exists. The following single scale estimate with decay in the parameter
s
holds via
Lemma

A.2
.

(2.2)

\|K_{k}*\widetilde{B}^{k-s,m}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim 2^{-(\delta-\delta_{0})s}\alpha\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}:|Q|=2^{(k-s)d}\\
Q\subseteq 2^{k}(m+[0,1]^{d})\end{subarray}}\|b_{Q}\|_{1},

where
\widetilde{B}^{k-s,m}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}:|Q|=2^{(k-s)d}\\
Q\subseteq 2^{k}(m+[0,1]^{d})\end{subarray}}\epsilon_{Q}b_{Q}
for any fixed choice of
\epsilon_{Q}\in\{-1,0,1\}
.

This allows us to use the variational Rademacher-Menshov
Lemma

1.2
. To do so, we first observe that by spatial localization of the bad cubes
Q
, the number of
b_{Q}
’s appearing in the definition of
B^{k-s,m}_{i,in}
is atmost
2^{sd}
for each fixed
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
i=1,\cdots,N^{k}(x)
. We also note that the sequence of functions
\{H^{s}_{k}*B^{k-s,m}_{i,in}(x)\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
have bounded overlap. Hence, we have

\displaystyle I_{in}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\sum_{i=1}^{N^{k}(x)}\left|\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}H^{s}_{k}*B^{k-s,m}_{i,in}(x)\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int\sum_{i=1}^{N^{k}(x)}\left|H^{s}_{k}*B^{k-s,m}_{i,in}(x)\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(\delta-\delta_{0})s}s^{2}\alpha\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}:|Q|=2^{(k-s)d}\\
Q\subseteq 2^{k}(m+[0,1]^{d})\end{subarray}}\|b_{Q}\|_{1}

2.2.2.

For the estimate of

\displaystyle\left|\left\{\lambda\Big[N_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\Big(\sum_{s\geq 5}\sum_{j\geq k}H_{j}^{s}*B^{j-s}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\Big)(x)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}>\frac{\alpha}{4}\right\}\right|

We will work with a fixed

K_{j,m}=2^{j}([0,1)^{d}+m)

H_{j}^{s}*B^{j-s}=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}H_{j}^{s}*B^{j-s,m},\quad\text{where}\;B^{j-s,m}=\sum_{|Q|=2^{(j-s)d}:Q\subseteq K_{j,m}}b_{Q}.

\mathcal{D}_{u}=\{2^{j}([0,1]^{d}+m+u),\;j\in{\mathbb{Z}},\;m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\}.

[

Lemma 2.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

For any cube

By the above lemma, for each

B^{j-s,m}=\sum_{u\in\{0,\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}\}^{d}}B^{j-s,m}_{u},\;\text{where}

\left|\left\{V_{2}\left(\sum_{j\geq k}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}H_{j}^{s}*B^{j-s,m}_{u}(x):k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right)>{c_{1}3^{-d}2^{-\delta_{1}s}\alpha}\right\}\right|,\text{for a fixed}\;u\in\left\{0,\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}\right\}^{d}.

\operatorname{supp}(H_{j}^{s}*B^{j-s}_{u})\subset\widetilde{K}_{j,m}
, where

and any such two

are either disjoint or one is contained in another. We will now discard the points where there is a large interaction on the scales

. To do so, we initially set

It is easy to see that
\operatorname{supp}(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}H_{j}^{s}*B^{j-s,m}_{u})\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{u}
. We decompose the set
\mathcal{X}^{u}=\mathcal{X}^{u}_{1}+\mathcal{X}^{u}_{2}
, where

\mathcal{X}^{u}_{1}=\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}^{u}:\sum_{(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}^{u}_{0}}\chi_{\widetilde{K}_{j,m}}(x)>2^{s(d+\epsilon_{0})}\right\},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{X}^{u}_{2}=\mathcal{X}^{u}\setminus\mathcal{X}^{u}_{1},

and
\epsilon_{0}>0
is a small fixed constant. Here, we would like to point out that the choice of the sublevel set
\mathcal{X}^{u}_{1}
based on
\epsilon_{0}>0
allows us to do away with the recursion argument employed in
[
19
]
. We write

\displaystyle\left|\left\{V_{2}\left(\sum_{j\geq k}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}H_{j}^{s}*B^{j-s,m}_{u}(x):k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right)>c_{1}3^{-d}2^{-\delta_{1}s}\alpha\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\mathcal{X}^{u}_{1}|+\left|\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}^{u}_{2}:V_{2}\left(\sum_{j\geq k}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}H_{j}^{s}*B^{j-s,m}_{u}(x):k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right)>c_{1}3^{-d}2^{-\delta_{1}s}\alpha\right\}\right|.

We note that for any
(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}^{u}_{0}
, there exists atleast one cube

with

and

. Thus, by Chebyshev’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{X}^{u}_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2^{s(d+\epsilon_{0})}}\sum_{(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}^{u}_{0}}|\widetilde{K}_{j,m}|\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{s\epsilon_{0}}}\sum_{(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}^{u}_{0}}|Q_{j,m}|\lesssim\frac{2^{-s\epsilon_{0}}}{\alpha}\sum\|b_{Q}\|_{1}.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{u}=\{(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{u}:\widetilde{K}_{j,m}\nsubseteq\mathcal{X}^{u}_{1}\}
. Then, it is easy to see that for
x\in\mathcal{X}^{u}_{2}
, we have

\sum_{j\geq k}H_{j}^{s}*B^{j-s}_{u}(x)=\sum_{(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}^{u},\;j\geq k}H_{j}^{s}*B^{j-s,m}_{u}(x).

The advantage of working with the collection
\mathcal{C}^{u}
is that for any fixed
(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}^{u}
, there are atmost
2^{s(d+\epsilon_{0})}
indices
(j^{\prime},m^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}^{u}
such that
\widetilde{K}_{j,m}\subset\widetilde{K}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
. Based on this observation, we subdivide the collection
\mathcal{C}^{u}
as follows. For any
(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}^{u}
, we say
(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}^{u}_{1}
if there is no other index
(j^{\prime},m^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}^{u}
such that
\widetilde{K}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\subseteq\widetilde{K}_{j,m}
. Inductively, we define
\mathcal{C}^{u}_{l}
to be the collection of all indices
(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}^{u}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{l-1}\mathcal{C}^{u}_{i}
such that there does not exist any index
(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}^{u}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{l-1}\mathcal{C}^{i}_{v}
such that

and we have
\mathcal{C}^{u}=\bigcup\limits_{l=1}^{2^{s(d+\epsilon_{0})}}\mathcal{C}^{u}_{l}

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}^{u}_{2}:V_{2}\left(\sum_{j\geq k}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}H_{j}^{s}*B^{j-s,m}_{u}(x):k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right)>c_{1}3^{-d}2^{-\delta_{1}s}\alpha\right\}\right|

where we used
Lemma

A.2
in the last step.
Choosing

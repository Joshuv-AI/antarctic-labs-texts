Efficacy of XEN gel stent implantation combined with mitomycin C injection in the treatment of open-angle glaucoma: a meta-analysis
Authors: Hong Feng, Zhonghong Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo assess the efficacy of XEN gel stent implantation combined with mitomycin C (MMC) injection in controlling intraocular pressure (IOP) and the dosage of postoperative medication for open-angle glaucoma (OAG) patients.MethodsThe PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Science Direct databases were searched from inception to August 2022 without any language restriction. All studies that compared IOP and the dose of medication before and after XEN gel stent implantation and MMC injection for OAG patients were included. Data extraction and methodological quality evaluation were performed.ResultsA total of 26 studies (2329 eyes) were involved in meta-analysis. It was revealed that IOP at 1 month after XEN implantation was significantly lower than preoperative IOP [group 1, IOP between 19.2 and 21.2 mmHg: mean difference (MD) = 7.60, 95% confidence interval (CI) [6.55, 8.66], I2 = 0%, P < 0.01; group 2, IOP between 21.6 and 22.8 mmHg: MD = 5.83, 95% CI [4.94, 6.71], I2 = 0%; group 3, IOP between 23.5 and 24.3 mmHg: MD = 9.22, 95% CI [8.33, 10.10], I2 = 0%; group 4, IOP between 24.4 and 26.2 mmHg: MD = 11.64, 95% CI [10.49, 12.80], I2 = 0%)]. Moreover, IOP at 3 months after XEN implantation was also significantly lower than preoperative IOP (MD = 8.31, 95% CI [2.54, 8.46], Z = 13.92, P < 0.00001). IOP at 6 months after XEN implantation was significantly lower than preoperative IOP, and no heterogeneity was found between two groups (group 1, IOP between 17.8 and 21.8 mmHg: MD = 5.71, 95% CI: [5.05, 6.36], I2 = 0%, P < 0.01); group 2, IOP between 22.1 and 23.9 mmHg: MD = 7.92, 95% CI [7.15, 8.70], I2 = 0%, P < 0.01). Low heterogeneity was noted in one group (group 3, IOP between 24.3 and 26.2 mmHg: MD = 9.32, 95% CI [8.66, 9.97], I2 = 30%). It was found that IOP at 12 months after XEN implantation was significantly lower than preoperative IOP (MD = 8.11, 95% CI [7.09, 9.12], Z = 15.68, P < 0.00001). There was high heterogeneity among the different baseline IOP groups at 24-month post-surgery (CHi2 = 41.74, df = 1, I2 = 97.6%, P < 0.01), while no heterogeneity was identified in two groups (group 1, IOP between 19.2 and 22.1 mmHg: MD = 6.30, 95% CI [5.76, 6.85], I2 = 0%, P < 0.01); group 2, IOP between 22.8 and 23.8 mmHg: MD = 9.11, 95% CI [8.45, 9.77], I2 = 0%). Subgroup analysis indicated that the dosage of medication was significantly reduced at 6 months after XEN implantation (group 1, the amount of medication was 2.3–2.96: MD = 1.90, 95% CI [1.78, 2.02], I2 = 0%, P < 0.01); group 2, the amount of medication was 3.2–3.3: MD = 2.59, 95% CI [2.43, 2.75], I2 = 0%). Furthermore, the dosage of medication at 12 months (MD = 1.96, 95% CI [1.72, 2.21], Z = 15.80, P < 0.01) and 24 months (group 1, the amount of medication was 1.89–2.7: MD = 6.30, 95% CI [5.76, 6.85], I2 = 0%, P < 0.01); group 2, the amount of medication was 2.72–3.07: MD = 9.11, 95% CI [8.45, 9.77], I2 = 0%) after XEN implantation was significantly lower compared with the preoperative level.ConclusionXEN implantation combined with MMC injection significantly decreased IOP in OAG patients at 1, 6, 9, 12, and 24 months after treatment. In addition, XEN implantation combined with MMC injection decreased the amount of medication used at 6, 12, and 24 months after treatment in OAG patients.Keywords: Open-angle glaucoma, XEN, Mitomycin C

Introduction

Glaucoma is an optic nerve pathology that affects approximately 60 million people worldwide [1]. Open-angle glaucoma (OAG) is the most common type of glaucoma and the main cause of blindness. Elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) causes the loss of ganglion cells, leading to optic nerve damage and progressive narrowing of the visual field. At present, surgical methods, particularly trabeculectomy and non-penetrating deep sclerectomy, are the main treatments for OAG. Long-tube glaucoma drainage devices have been developed as safer and less traumatic surgical interventions for patients with mild-to-moderate glaucoma or for those who are intolerant to standard medical therapy [2–4]. The XEN gel stent has become widely available since its approval by the United States Food and Drug Administration in 2016. XEN is easier to operate than trabeculectomy and non-penetrating deep sclerectomy, and it also can be an option after failed glaucoma surgery. However, owing to surgical complications related to XEN implantation, the prevention and treatment of cystoid and scarring disorders are also worthy of investigation. At present, XEN gel stent implantation is mainly combined with subconjunctival injection of mitomycin C (MMC) [5] and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) [6], significantly preventing scarring disorders. With the wide application of MMC, various studies have investigated the efficacy of XEN gel stent implantation combined with MMC injection in the treatment of open-angle glaucoma. More evidence is required on the efficacy of XEN gel stent implantation combined with MMC injection before widely utilization of XEN. This meta-analysis aimed to assess the efficacy of XEN gel stent implantation combined with MMC injection in OAG patients.

Methods

Search strategy

This meta-analysis was developed by following the guideline of the PRISMA checklist [7]. After consensus was reached among team members on the best keywords, PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Science Direct databases were searched from inception until August 2022 without any language restriction. Using the search terms “glaucoma” and “XEN”, studies that compared changes in IOP and the amount of medication used for OAG patients before and after XEN gel stent implantation were retrieved from the databases.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Cohort studies or randomized or non-randomized case–control studies; (2) XEN stent (XEN-45 or XEN-63) was used; (3) Patients who aged > 18 years; (3) Patients who were clinically diagnosed with OAG (primary OAG or secondary OAG, including pseudoexfoliative glaucoma, highly myopic glaucoma, pigmentary glaucoma, and OAG with stably controlled uveitis); (4) Patients with uncontrolled IOP who had received IOP-lowering drugs and needed to undergo glaucoma surgery, regardless of their ethnicity or nationality; (4) The availability of sufficient information to calculate the effect size; (5) Full-paper studies that were published in peer-reviewed journals.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Patients with a history of undergoing glaucoma surgery before enrollment; (2) Cases with no sign of OAG; (3) Duplicate publication; (4) Case report, letter, response, review, meta-analysis, meeting abstract, or animal experiment.

After the search terms were developed, information, including the title, abstract, link, DOI, publication year, and author list of each individual study were retrieved by one researcher from the four databases using Endnote software. The retrieved records were thereafter imported into an Excel sheet for screening. Each record was screened by two researchers and by a third reviewer when disagreement arose. Full-text screening was performed by both researchers for inclusion of the best matched studies according to the above eligibility criteria.

Data extraction and risk of bias

Characteristics of the included studies and our outcomes were imported into an Excel spreadsheet. Our outcomes were preoperative and postoperative IOP, preoperative and postoperative dosage of medication, and post-surgical complications. The characteristics of the included studies were study ID, country of patients, study design, age, baseline IOP and baseline dosage of medication. Data were extracted and reviewed by two researchers for any potential errors.

Statistical analysis

Meta-analysis was performed using RevMan 5.3 and Stata 15.1 software, and data were expressed as mean difference (MD) and 95% confidence interval (CI). Heterogeneity among the included studies was assessed using the I2 statistic and Cochran’s Q test. The Cochran’s Q test was utilized to examine heterogeneity (P < 0.10 was indicative of heterogeneity). Given that the power of this statistical test is low when a meta-analysis includes a small number of studies, the Higgins test (I2) was also used, describing the percentage of total variation across studies due to heterogeneity rather than chance (low heterogeneity: < 25%, moderate heterogeneity: 25–75% and high heterogeneity: > 75%) [8]. Meta-analysis was performed using a fixed-effects model when I2 ≤ 50% and P ≥ 0.05. If there was a significant heterogeneity (P < 0.10, I2 > 50%), the source of heterogeneity was analyzed. If heterogeneity could not be explained by clinical and methodological heterogeneity, a random-effects model was utilized. A significant clinical heterogeneity was treated by subgroup analysis or sensitivity analysis, or by descriptive analysis only. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Egger regression asymmetry test [9] was used to examine the risk of publication bias (P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant). If publication bias was confirmed, a trim-and-fill method was implemented to adjust the bias.

Results

Search results and characteristics of the included studies

A total of 870 records were retrieved from the databases, and only 116 records were screened by title and abstract after excluding duplicate records. We screened 303 full-text studies for potential eligibility in this study. Finally, 26 studies (2329 eyes) were involved in the meta-analysis (Fig. 1). Analysis of postoperative changes in IOP

IOP at 1 month post-surgery was highly heterogeneous among 14 studies (CHi2 = 112.71, df = 13, I2 = 88%, P < 0.01) (Fig. 2) [10–15, 18–20, 25, 27–30]. However, the Egger’s test showed no risk of publication bias (P >| t |= 0.246, Fig. 3). The findings of Fea et al. [12] and Qureshi et al. [29] were significantly different from these 14 studies. However, considering the small number of subjects in this study (only 12 subjects), the risk of bias could be noteworthy. Baseline IOP was significantly higher in Qureshi et al.’s study [29] than that in other studies, which may explain significant difference in the results. After excluding the two studies, it was found that the change in postoperative IOP varied among studies with different baseline IOP. Therefore, subgroup analysis was conducted based on the baseline IOP (Fig. 4). The results indicated that there was significant heterogeneity among different baseline IOP groups (CHi2 = 68.13, df = 3, I2 = 95.6%, P < 0.01), rather than among studies within each group (group 1, IOP was 19.2–21.2 mmHg): MD = 7.60, 95% confidence interval (CI) [6.55, 8.66], I2 = 0%, P < 0.01); group 2, IOP was 21.6–22.8 mmHg: MD = 5.83, 95% CI [4.94, 6.71], I2 = 0%; group 3, IOP was 23.5–24.3 mmHg: MD = 9.22, 95% CI [8.33, 10.10], I2 = 0%; group 4, IOP was 24.4–26.2 mmHg: MD = 11.64, 95% CI [10.49, 12.80], I2 = 0%) [10, 11, 13–15, 18–20, 25, 27, 30, 31].

IOP at 3-month post-surgery was also significantly heterogeneous among 14 studies (CHi2 = 87.44, df = 13, I2 = 85%, P < 0.01) [10, 11, 14, 15, 18–20, 25, 29–32]. The Egger’s test revealed no risk of publication bias (P >| t |= 0.151, Fig. 5). The characteristics of each study were fully examined, and the heterogeneity could not be explained by clinical and methodological heterogeneity. Hence, a random-effects model was utilized, and it was found that IOP was significantly lower at 3 months after XEN implantation (MD = 8.31, 95% CI [2.54, 8.46], Z = 13.92, P < 0.00001, Fig. 6) [10–12, 14, 15, 18–20, 25, 29–32].

There was high heterogeneity in IOP at 6 months post-surgery among 15 studies (CHi2 = 69.19, df = 14, I2 = 80%, P < 0.01) [10–12, 14, 15, 18–20, 25, 27, 28, 31, 32]. The Egger’s test showed no risk of publication bias (P >| t |= 0.843, Fig. 7). Subgroup analysis indicated significant heterogeneity among different baseline IOP groups (CHi2 = 68.13, df = 3, I2 = 95.6%, P < 0.01), and no heterogeneity was noted in group 1 (IOP was 17.8–21.8 mmHg) (MD = 5.71, 95% CI [5.05, 6.36], I2 = 0%, P < 0.01) and group 2 (IOP was 22.1–23.9 mmHg) (MD = 7.92, 95% CI [7.15,8.70], I2 = 0%). Low heterogeneity was identified in group 3 (IOP was 24.3–26.2 mmHg) (MD = 9.32, 95% CI [8.66,9.97], I2 = 30%), (Fig. 8) [10–12, 14, 15, 18–20, 25, 27, 28, 31, 32].

Analysis of postoperative changes in the dosage of anti-glaucoma medication

There was significant heterogeneity in the dosage of anti-glaucoma medication at 6-month post-surgery among 13 studies (CHi2 = 70.87, df = 12, I2 = 83%, P < 0.0001) [12, 15–19, 27–32, 35]. The Egger’s test exhibited no risk of publication bias (P >| t |= 0.198, Fig. 13). It was found that the baseline dosage of anti-glaucoma medication was significantly higher in Qureshi A et al.’s study [27] than that in other studies, which was therefore removed. Subsequent analysis showed that the change in postoperative medication dosage varied among studies with different baseline dosages. Subgroup analysis demonstrated that there was high heterogeneity among different baseline medication dosage groups (CHi2 = 45.90, df = 1, I2 = 97.8%, P < 0.01), while no heterogeneity was identified in group 1 (the amount of medication was 2.3–2.96) (MD = 1.90, 95% CI [1.78, 2.02], I2 = 0%, P < 0.01) and group 2 (the amount of medication was 3.2–3.3) (MD = 2.59, 95% CI [2.43, 2.75], I2 = 0%) (Fig. 14) [12, 15–19, 27, 28, 30–32, 35].

A high heterogeneity was noted in medication dosage at 12-month post-surgery among 20 studies (CHi2 = 178.21, df = 19, I2 = 89%, P < 0.01) [8, 10, 11, 13, 14, 16, 17, 19–21, 23–30, 32]. Discussion

The XEN gel stent is a permanent soft collagen-derived hydrophilic gelatin that does not cause tissue rejection and possesses an acceptable histocompatibility [37]. During XEN implantation, only a small ab externo incision was made in the bulbous conjunctiva or fascia, and a small filtration bubble formed after implantation, which had almost no interference with the conjunctival and subconjunctival tissues [38]. However, the success of XEN implantation may be limited by fibrosis. The natural wound healing process after filtration surgery may result in the decreased aqueous outflow because of subconjunctival and episcleral fibrosis. Furthermore, the mere presence of fluid or a pressure head in the subconjunctival space stimulates a cellular reaction that reduces tissue porosity [39]. MMC can inhibit DNA-dependent RNA synthesis, which is important for fibroblast proliferation [40]. Therefore, the combined application of XEN and MMC injection has become a popular topic of surgical research.

In the present meta-analysis, it was found that IOP decreased at different time points (1, 3, 6, 12, and 24 months) after surgery and remained lower at 24 months after XEN implantation compared with the baseline. Gillmann et al. [23] found that the reductions in IOP and medication were still clinically significant at 3 years after XEN gel stent implantation despite relatively high rates of needling and reoperations (47.8% failure in the first year, 17.4% in the second year, and 3.3% in the third year). It was also found the decrease in IOP was different among studies with diverse baseline IOP. Ucar and Cetinkaya [38] retrospectively compared two different techniques of XEN implantation, namely the transconjunctival (ab-externo) and standard (ab-interno) techniques, and it was found that there were no significant differences in IOP and the reuse of anti-glaucomatous agents between the two methods. Therefore, this raises the question of whether the magnitude of IOP reduction should be evaluated according to the baseline IOP. Additional randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are needed to demonstrate the relationship of baseline IOP with the magnitude of IOP reduction after XEN implantation.

In addition, we found that the dosage of medication was reduced at 6, 12, and 24 months after implantation. Similarly, the decrease in medication dosage varied with the baseline dosage, which further confirmed the efficacy of XEN implantation in the treatment of OAG. MMC can inhibit the proliferation of fibroblasts and affect blood supply to the conjunctiva. Follicular leakage-associated complications may also occur. Belb needing is needed in some cases during the observation of the bleb. Tube-related complications have also been observed. Due to the size of the device (6 mm), the best route of insertion is subconjunctival (3 mm), followed by intrascleral (2 mm) and the anterior chamber (1 mm) to avoid extrusion or contact with the corneal endothelium. In addition, the 2-mm intrascleral pathway is resistant against excessive drainage. These variations in stent placement have consequently led to intra- or post-operative complications in relocation or reimplantation.

However, this study also had some limitations. First, the majority of the included studies had retrospective design, which resulted in certain limitations in the selection of subjects and research period. Second, the washout period of preoperative medication was not described in most studies, and the decrease in IOP and postoperative medication may potentially be attributed to the delayed effects of medication. Third, the types of medications used were not described, and different medications have different strengths in controlling hypertension, which in turn limit their dosage. Hence, additional multi-center RCTs remain to be conducted to confirm these findings.

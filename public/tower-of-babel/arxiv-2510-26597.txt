Bijections Between Smirnov Words and Hamiltonian Cycles in Complete Multipartite Graphs
El-Mehdi Mehiri mehiri314@gmail.com

Abstract
We establish a bijective correspondence between Smirnov words with balanced letter multiplicities and Hamiltonian paths in complete m m -partite graphs K n , n , … , n K_{n,n,\ldots,n} . This bijection allows us to derive closed inclusion-exclusion formulas for the number of Hamiltonian cycles in such graphs. We further extend the enumeration to the generalized nonuniform case K n 1 , n 2 , … , n m K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m}} . We also provide an asymptotic analysis based on Stirling’s approximation, which yields compact factorial expressions and logarithmic expansions describing the growth of the number of Hamiltonian cycles in the considered graphs. Our approach unifies the combinatorial study of adjacency-constrained words and the enumeration of Hamiltonian cycles within a single analytical framework. Keywords: Hamiltonian cycle; Smirnov word; multipartite graph; bijection; inclusion–exclusion; combinatorial enumeration; asymptotic analysis. MSC 2020: 05A05, 05C45, 05A15, 05C30.

Determining whether a given graph admits a Hamiltonian cycle is a well-known NP-complete problem. An even more challenging task is to determine the number of distinct Hamiltonian cycles a graph possesses. Owing to this computational difficulty, numerous studies have focused on developing enumeration techniques for specific graph classes, or on establishing upper and lower bounds for the number of Hamiltonian cycles

Both are well-known classical results.

Furthermore, Singmaster

Multipartite Alphabet and Admissible Words

Let the alphabet
\mathcal{A}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{A}_{i}
, where
|\mathcal{A}_{i}|=n
, and
\mathcal{A}_{i}\cap\mathcal{A}_{j}=\varnothing
, for all
i,j\in[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}
,
i\neq j
.

Definition 1

(Admissible Words)
.

A word
w=w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{mn}
over the alphabet
\mathcal{A}
is called
admissible
if:

1.

each letter of
\mathcal{A}
appears exactly once in
w
;

2.

consecutive letters come from distinct subsets (Smirnov word), i.e.

w_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{i},\ w_{t+1}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}\implies i\neq j;

3.

the first and last letters belong to different subsetsn, i.e.

w_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{i},\ w_{mn}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}\implies i\neq j.

The set of all admissible words is denoted
\mathcal{W}_{m}(n)
,
and its cardinality
W_{m}(n)=|\mathcal{W}_{m}(n)|
.

Definition 2

(Color Reduction)
.

Associate to each subset
\mathcal{A}_{i}
a color
i\in[m]
.
Replacing each letter of
w
by its color yields a

color-word
over the alphabet
[m]
.

Let
S_{m}(n)
denote the number of color-words of length
mn

that use each color exactly
n
times, have no consecutive equal colors,
and begin and end with distinct colors.

Proposition 1

(Reduction to Color-Words)
.

For all integers
m,n\geq 1
, we have

W_{m}(n)=S_{m}(n)\,(n!)^{m}.

(1)

Proof.

Every color-word pattern corresponds to
(n!)^{m}
admissible words, since the
n
letters of each subset
\mathcal{A}_{i}
may occupy their positions in any order. Hence, the result.
∎

3
Enumeration of Color-Words

Definition 3
.

Let
F_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m};\,s\!\to\!r)
be the number of color-words, that:

1.

begin with the color
s
and end with the color
r
,

2.

have no two consecutive equal colors,

3.

use exactly
a_{i}

additional
occurrences of color
i
after the first letter (so the total length is
1+\sum_{i}a_{i}
).

Lemma 1

(Recurrence Relation)
.

Let
m\geq 2
, for all nonnegative
a_{i}
and
s,r\in[m]
, we have

\displaystyle F_{m}(0,\dots,0;\,s\!\to\!r)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }s=r,\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(2)

\displaystyle F_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m};\,s\!\to\!r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
x\neq s\end{subarray}}^{m}F_{m}(a_{1}-\mathbf{1}_{x=1},\,\dots,\,a_{m}-\mathbf{1}_{x=m};\,x\!\to\!r),

(3)

with the convention that
F_{m}(\ldots)=0
whenever some argument
a_{i}-\mathbf{1}_{x=i}
is negative, and where the indicator function

\mathbf{1}_{x=y}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $x=y$;}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(4)

Proof.

We proceed by induction in two steps: base case and inductive (decomposition) step.

Base case (
2
).

If
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})=(0,\dots,0)
, then the total length is
1
, so the only possible word is the length-
1
word consisting of the single color
s
. This word ends in
r
if and only if
r=s
. Hence
F_{m}(0,\dots,0;\,s\!\to\!r)=\mathbf{1}_{\{s=r\}}
, proving (
2
).

Decomposition step (
3
).

Assume
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}=:N\geq 1
, i.e., there are still
N
letters to place after the first position.
Every word counted by
F_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m};\,s\!\to\!r)
has the form

\underbrace{s}_{\text{fixed first}}\,\underbrace{x}_{\text{second}}\;\underbrace{(\text{a valid tail of length }N-1)}_{\text{no equal adjacencies, ends at }r},

where the
second
color
x
must satisfy (i)
x\neq s
(to avoid an immediate adjacency violation) and (ii)
a_{x}\geq 1
(since one occurrence of color
x
is used at the second position).

Fix such an
x\in[m]\setminus\{s\}
with
a_{x}\geq 1
. Removing the first two letters
(s,x)
from the word leaves a tail of length
N-1
that:

•

begins with color
x
(because the third position must differ from
x
and belongs to the tail’s first step from the perspective of the reduced problem),

•

ends with color
r
(the overall last letter is unchanged),

•

uses exactly
a_{i}^{\prime}:=a_{i}-\mathbf{1}_{x=i}
occurrences of color
i
for each
i
,

•

has no equal adjacencies (this is inherited from the original word).

By definition, the number of such tails is precisely

F_{m}\bigl(a_{1}-\mathbf{1}_{x=1},\,\dots,\,a_{m}-\mathbf{1}_{x=m};\,x\!\to\!r\bigr).

Conversely, given any tail counted by
F_{m}(a_{1}-\mathbf{1}_{x=1},\dots,a_{m}-\mathbf{1}_{x=m};\,x\!\to\!r)
, prefixing it by the two-letter block
(s,x)
produces a valid word counted by
F_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m};\,s\!\to\!r)
, because:

•

x\neq s
enforces the no-adjacency condition at the boundary between the first and second letters,

•

the tail, by hypothesis, has no equal adjacencies internally,

•

the total color counts match exactly: we used
1
occurrence of
x
(hence reduced
a_{x}
by
1
) and left all
a_{i}
for
i\neq x
unchanged.

Therefore, for each fixed
x\neq s
with
a_{x}\geq 1
, there is a
bijection
between words counted by
F_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m};\,s\!\to\!r)
whose second color is
x
,
and
tails counted by
F_{m}(a_{1}-\mathbf{1}_{x=1},\dots,a_{m}-\mathbf{1}_{x=m};\,x\!\to\!r)
.

The sets corresponding to different choices of
x
are disjoint (the second letter is uniquely determined), and their union is exactly the set of all words counted by
F_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m};\,s\!\to\!r)
(every valid word has some second color
x\neq s
with
a_{x}\geq 1
). Summing the cardinalities of these disjoint classes over all
x\in[m]\setminus\{s\}
yields (
3
):

F_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m};\,s\!\to\!r)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
x\neq s\end{subarray}}^{m}F_{m}(a_{1}-\mathbf{1}_{x=1},\,\dots,\,a_{m}-\mathbf{1}_{x=m};\,x\!\to\!r),

under the convention that any term with a negative argument (i.e.,
a_{x}-1<0
) contributes
0
, as there can be no word whose second letter is
x
if no
x
’s remain to place.
∎

Remark 1

(Induction on total length)
.

Equation (
3
) also admits a proof by strong induction on
N=\sum_{i}a_{i}
:
the base case
N=0
is (
2
); for
N\geq 1
, the decomposition above partitions valid words by their unique second color
x\neq s
, and reduces to instances with total length
N-1
, to which the induction hypothesis applies.

Let
F_{m}^{(s\to r)}(n)
denote the number of
mn
-length words over the alphabet
[m]
, that use each color exactly
n
times, begin with color
s
and end with color
r
, and have no two consecutive equal colors.

Lemma 2

(Symmetry of Endpoint Pairs)
.

Fix
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
. For all
s,r\in[M]
,
if
s\neq r
and
s^{\prime},r^{\prime}\in[m]
with
s^{\prime}\neq r^{\prime}
, then

F_{m}^{(s\to r)}(n)=F_{m}^{(s^{\prime}\to r^{\prime})}(n).

(5)

In particular, since there are
m(m-1)
ordered endpoint pairs
(s,r)
with
s\neq r
,

S_{m}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,r\in[m]\\
s\neq r\end{subarray}}F_{m}^{(s\to r)}(n)=m(m-1)\,F_{m}^{(1\to 2)}(n).

(6)

Proof.

•

A canonical relabeling bijection.

Fix ordered pairs
(s,r)
and
(s^{\prime},r^{\prime})
with
s\neq r
and
s^{\prime}\neq r^{\prime}
.
Because the symmetric group
S_{m}
on
[m]=\{1,\dots,m\}
acts
2
-transitively on ordered pairs of distinct elements,
there exists a permutation
\pi\in S_{m}
such that

\pi(s)=s^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\pi(r)=r^{\prime}.

Define a map
\Phi_{\pi}
on color-words by
relabeling each letter

x\in[m]
to
\pi(x)
, position by position.
Thus, if
w=w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{mn}
is a color-word, then

\Phi_{\pi}(w)\;:=\;\pi(w_{1})\,\pi(w_{2})\,\cdots\,\pi(w_{mn}).

•

\Phi_{\pi}
preserves all constraints.

Let
w
be any Smirnov color-word counted by
F_{m}^{(s\to r)}(n)
. We verify that
\Phi_{\pi}(w)
is valid for
F_{m}^{(s^{\prime}\to r^{\prime})}(n)
:

–

Endpoint condition:
Since
w_{1}=s
and
w_{mn}=r
, we have

\Phi_{\pi}(w)_{1}=\pi(w_{1})=\pi(s)=s^{\prime}
and
\Phi_{\pi}(w)_{mn}=\pi(w_{mn})=\pi(r)=r^{\prime}
.

–

Balance (multiplicities):
Each color
i\in[m]
occurs exactly
n
times in
w
. Under relabeling, occurrences of
i
become occurrences of
\pi(i)
; hence each color
j\in[m]
also occurs exactly
n
times in
\Phi_{\pi}(w)
(because
\pi
is a bijection of
[m]
).

–

No equal adjacencies (Smirnov):
For every adjacent pair
(w_{t},w_{t+1})
in
w
, we have
w_{t}\neq w_{t+1}
. Applying
\pi
(which is injective), we get
\pi(w_{t})\neq\pi(w_{t+1})
. Thus no new equality of adjacent colors is created, so
\Phi_{\pi}(w)
is Smirnov.

Therefore
\Phi_{\pi}
maps the set
\mathcal{F}^{(s\to r)}_{m}(n)
of valid words with endpoints
(s,r)
bijectively
into

\mathcal{F}^{(s^{\prime}\to r^{\prime})}_{m}(n)
.

•

Bijectivity (invertibility).

Since
\pi
is a permutation, its inverse
\pi^{-1}\in S_{m}
satisfies

\pi^{-1}(s^{\prime})=s
and
\pi^{-1}(r^{\prime})=r
. Repeating Step 2 with
\pi^{-1}
shows that

\Phi_{\pi^{-1}}
maps
\mathcal{F}^{(s^{\prime}\to r^{\prime})}_{m}(n)
back into
\mathcal{F}^{(s\to r)}_{m}(n)
,
and that
\Phi_{\pi^{-1}}\circ\Phi_{\pi}=\mathrm{id}
on
\mathcal{F}^{(s\to r)}_{m}(n)
, while

\Phi_{\pi}\circ\Phi_{\pi^{-1}}=\mathrm{id}
on
\mathcal{F}^{(s^{\prime}\to r^{\prime})}_{m}(n)
.
Hence
\Phi_{\pi}
is a bijection between the two families, and in particular

\bigl|\mathcal{F}^{(s\to r)}_{m}(n)\bigr|\;=\;\bigl|\mathcal{F}^{(s^{\prime}\to r^{\prime})}_{m}(n)\bigr|.

This establishes
F_{m}^{(s\to r)}(n)=F_{m}^{(s^{\prime}\to r^{\prime})}(n)
.

•

Consequence for
S_{m}(n)
.

By definition,

S_{m}(n)\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,r\in[m]\\
s\neq r\end{subarray}}F_{m}^{(s\to r)}(n).

Since every summand equals the common value
F_{m}^{(1\to 2)}(n)
by the argument above (take
\pi
sending
(s,r)
to
(1,2)
),
and there are
m(m-1)
ordered pairs
(s,r)
with
s\neq r
, we obtain the result.

∎

Theorem 1

( Closed Inclusion–Exclusion Formula)
.

Let
m\geq 2
, and let
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\geq 0
with total length
N=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}
.
For fixed endpoints
s,r\in[m]
, define the adjusted counts

a_{i}^{\prime}:=a_{i}-\mathbf{1}_{\{i=s\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{i=r\}}.

If any
a_{i}^{\prime}<0
(that is, if color
i
would be used fewer than twice, once at each endpoint),
then
F_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m};\,s\!\to\!r)=0
.
Otherwise:

F_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m};\,s\!\to\!r)=\!\!\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{a_{1}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{m}=0}^{a_{m}-1}(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}}\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}-1}{k_{i}}\right)\frac{(N-2-\sum_{i}k_{i})!}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}^{\prime}-k_{i})!}.

(7)

Proof.

We assume all
a_{i}>0
except possibly for colors not appearing, and we will handle degenerate cases by convention later.

The main obstacle is the adjacency constraint. If we ignore it, the number of linear color-words with fixed endpoints
(s,r)
and prescribed multiplicities
(a_{i})
would simply be:

\frac{(N-2)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{\prime}!},

because we fix the first and last colors (removing one occurrence of
s
and one of
r
) and then arrange the remaining
N-2
letters in arbitrary order.

The adjacency restriction is then imposed by
inclusion–exclusion
on the set of forbidden events.

We give a careful step-by-step inclusion–exclusion derivation.

•

The forbidden events.

For each color
i\in[m]
, let
E_{i}
be the event:

E_{i}=\{\text{at least one pair of consecutive }i\text{'s appears}\}.

We want to count sequences that avoid all such
E_{i}
, i.e.

F_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m};\,s\!\to\!r)=\text{number of all words avoiding }\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}.

By the inclusion–exclusion principle:

|\overline{E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{m}}|=\sum_{I\subseteq[m]}(-1)^{|I|+0}\,|\bigcap_{i\in I}E_{i}|.

In our context, we refine this by an integer vector
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{m})
,
where
k_{i}
denotes how many adjacency pairs of color
i
are
chosen to be merged
.
We then sum over all combinations
(k_{1},\dots,k_{m})
of nonnegative integers satisfying
0\leq k_{i}\leq a_{i}-1
.

•

Merging adjacent identical letters (block representation).

If
k_{i}
pairs of color
i
are forced to merge, then
the
a_{i}
occurrences of color
i
shrink into
a_{i}-k_{i}

blocks
,
each representing one or more consecutive
i
’s.
For example, if
a_{i}=5
and we decide to merge
k_{i}=2
pairs of
i
’s, we get
a_{i}-k_{i}=3
effective blocks of color
i
.

Because there are
\binom{a_{i}-1}{k_{i}}
ways to choose which of the
(a_{i}-1)
potential adjacency slots between
i
’s become merges, the total number of ways to specify which merges occur across all colors is

\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}-1}{k_{i}}.

Each such configuration contributes to the inclusion–exclusion sum with sign
(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}}
, corresponding to the parity of the total number of adjacencies selected for merging.

•

Counting the merged (block) arrangements.

After the merges, the total number of blocks (letters in the reduced word) is:

N^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-k_{i})=N-\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}.

However, the first and last blocks are predetermined: the first color must be
s
, and the last color must be
r
.
Thus, the number of
interior
blocks to arrange is
N^{\prime}-2=N-2-\sum_{i}k_{i}
.

Among these interior positions, the reduced multiplicities of color
i
(excluding endpoints) are

a_{i}^{\prime}-k_{i},

because we have removed one
s
-block and one
r
-block from the total and merged
k_{i}
of them.

Therefore, the number of ways to arrange these blocks (with first
s
, last
r
, and multiplicities adjusted) is:

\frac{(N-2-\sum_{i}k_{i})!}{\prod_{i}(a_{i}^{\prime}-k_{i})!}.

This factor appears because we are now arranging
indistinguishable
blocks (each color
i
has
a_{i}^{\prime}-k_{i}
copies).

•

Summing over all choices.

Putting these ingredients together, we obtain the inclusion–exclusion sum:

F_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m};\,s\!\to\!r)=\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{a_{1}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{m}=0}^{a_{m}-1}(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}}\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}-1}{k_{i}}\right)\frac{(N-2-\sum_{i}k_{i})!}{\prod_{i}(a_{i}^{\prime}-k_{i})!}.

This is exactly the claimed formula (
7
).

•

Boundary conditions and consistency.

If for some color
i
we have
a_{i}^{\prime}<0
(that is,
i=s
or
i=r
but
a_{i}=0
or
1
), then there is no valid word: the first and/or last color would be required to appear more often than possible.
By convention, the binomial and factorial terms vanish in that case, so the formula correctly yields
0
.

•

Verification for small cases.

For sanity, consider
m=2
and
a_{1}=a_{2}=n
with
(s,r)=(1,2)
.
Plugging into (
7
) recovers the classical Smirnov word formula

F_{2}^{(1\to 2)}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-1}{k}^{2}(2n-2-k)!/(n-1-k)!^{2},

which matches the known enumeration of binary words with equal counts and alternating constraints.

Hence the general formula (
7
) holds.
∎

From Words to Hamiltonian Cycles

Definition 4

(Complete Multipartite Graph)
.

Let
K_{n,n,\dots,n}
be the complete
m
-partite graph
with vertex partition
V=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}
,

|V_{i}|=n
, and edges only between different parts.

Theorem 2

(Word–Cycle Bijection)
.

Let
K_{n,n,\dots,n}
be a complete
m
-partite graph with parts

V_{1},\dots,V_{m}
, each of cardinality
n
.
Then:

1.

There exists a bijection between admissible words

w\in\mathcal{W}_{m}(n)
and Hamiltonian
directed paths

in
K_{n,n,\dots,n}
whose endpoints lie in distinct parts.

2.

Each undirected Hamiltonian cycle in
K_{n,n,\dots,n}
corresponds to
exactly
2mn
directed paths of this form (accounting for
both orientations and cyclic rotations).

Consequently,

H_{m}(n)=\frac{W_{m}(n)}{2mn}=\frac{(n!)^{m}}{2mn}\,S_{m}(n),

(8)

where
H_{m}(n)
is the number of undirected Hamiltonian cycles in
K_{n,n,\dots,n}
.

Proof.

We give a constructive and detailed bijection.

•

Objects involved.

The vertex set of the multipartite graph is

V(K_{n,n,\dots,n})=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m},\qquad|V_{i}|=n.

Vertices within the same part
V_{i}
are
not
adjacent, and vertices from
different parts are fully adjacent.
Hence any edge of the graph necessarily joins vertices of distinct colors (parts).

Each admissible word
w=w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{mn}\in\mathcal{W}_{m}(n)
is a sequence
of distinct letters, where
w_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{t}}
and no two consecutive letters share the same index
i_{t}
.
We associate each letter of
\mathcal{A}_{i}
bijectively with one vertex in
V_{i}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{A}
and
V
can be identified structurally as
m
colored sets of
n
elements each.

•

From words to Hamiltonian paths.

Given
w=w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{mn}\in\mathcal{W}_{m}(n)
, we construct a directed path

P(w):\quad v_{1}\to v_{2}\to\cdots\to v_{mn},

by replacing each letter
w_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{t}}
with the corresponding vertex

v_{t}\in V_{i_{t}}
in the same color class.
Because consecutive letters of
w
come from different subsets
\mathcal{A}_{i_{t}}
,
the consecutive vertices
(v_{t},v_{t+1})
necessarily belong to distinct parts

V_{i_{t}}\neq V_{i_{t+1}}
.
Hence, every consecutive pair
(v_{t},v_{t+1})
is adjacent in the graph

K_{n,n,\dots,n}
.
By construction, all vertices of
V
appear exactly once in the sequence,
so
P(w)
is a Hamiltonian directed path.

Furthermore, the first vertex lies in
V_{s}
and the last vertex in
V_{r}

for distinct
s\neq r
, since the first and last letters of
w

belong to different subsets. Thus,
P(w)
has distinct endpoints.

•

From Hamiltonian paths to words.

Conversely, take any directed Hamiltonian path

P:v_{1}\to v_{2}\to\cdots\to v_{mn}

in
K_{n,n,\dots,n}
whose endpoints lie in distinct parts
V_{s}
and
V_{r}
.
Label each vertex
v_{t}
by the unique letter
a\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
associated
to its part
V_{i}
. Reading these letters in path order yields a word

w_{P}=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{mn}
satisfying:

–

every letter appears exactly once (the path is Hamiltonian),

–

consecutive letters come from distinct subsets (edges only join distinct parts),

–

first and last letters come from distinct subsets (endpoint condition).

Therefore
w_{P}\in\mathcal{W}_{m}(n)
.
Since this inverse mapping undoes the construction of Step 2,
we have a true bijection:

w\;\longleftrightarrow\;P(w),\qquad\text{between }\mathcal{W}_{m}(n)\text{ and directed Hamiltonian paths with distinct endpoints.}

•

Counting and labeling.

Each word pattern corresponds to many distinct labeled words.
For each color
i
, we can assign the
n
specific letters of
\mathcal{A}_{i}

to the
n
occurrences of color
i
in any Smirnov pattern in
n!
ways.
Thus, every color pattern (or “Smirnov color-word”) accounts for
(n!)^{m}

distinct labeled words. Therefore,

W_{m}(n)=(n!)^{m}\,S_{m}(n).

•

From paths to cycles.

In
K_{n,n,\dots,n}
, adding the edge
(v_{mn},v_{1})
at the ends of the path
always yields a valid Hamiltonian
cycle
, because the endpoints
lie in distinct parts and thus are adjacent.
Conversely, every directed Hamiltonian cycle of length
mn
can be “opened”
at any of its
mn
vertices to yield a Hamiltonian directed path with distinct
endpoints.

However, each undirected Hamiltonian cycle corresponds to:

–

two possible directions (clockwise and counterclockwise),

–

and
mn
possible choices of starting vertex (rotations).

Therefore, each undirected cycle produces
2mn
distinct directed paths
of the type counted above.

Hence, the result.
∎

5
Asymptotic analysis

Theorem 3

(Asymptotic Growth)
.

For fixed
m\geq 2
and as
n\to\infty
,

S_{m}(n)\;\sim\;m(m-1)\,\frac{(mn)!}{(n!)^{m}}\left(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\right)^{mn-1},

(9)

and consequently,

H_{m}(n)\;\sim\;\frac{(m-1)(mn)!}{2n}\left(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\right)^{mn-1}.

(10)

Proof.

We derive the asymptotic form heuristically but systematically, emphasizing the probabilistic interpretation that underlies inclusion–exclusion.

•

Total number of balanced color-words.

Let

T_{m}(n)=\frac{(mn)!}{(n!)^{m}}

denote the number of
balanced color-words
of length
mn
in which each color
i\in[m]
appears exactly
n
times.
This is simply the multinomial coefficient counting all arrangements of
n
occurrences of each of
m
colors, ignoring adjacency constraints.

•

Adjacency constraint as a probabilistic filter.

Among the
T_{m}(n)
balanced words, we wish to estimate the fraction that are
Smirnov
—that is, have no two consecutive equal colors.
Let us imagine that the color of each position in a word of length
mn
is drawn uniformly from
\{1,\dots,m\}
, subject only to the balance constraint.

For large
n
, the probability that two consecutive positions carry the same color is approximately
1/m
, since the color composition remains nearly uniform throughout the sequence.
Under the assumption that consecutive equalities occur roughly independently, the probability that no equality ever occurs along the
(mn-1)
adjacency pairs is approximately

(1-1/m)^{mn-1}.

•

Endpoint correction factor.

The admissible words counted by
S_{m}(n)
must also have distinct
first and last
colors.
Given the near-uniform distribution of colors, the probability that two random positions have distinct colors is

\mathbb{P}\{\text{distinct endpoints}\}=1-\frac{1}{m}=\frac{m-1}{m}.

Since we are conditioning on the first color being fixed and the last being different, the number of ordered pairs of distinct colors
(s,r)
is
m(m-1)
.
Thus, the endpoint correction multiplies the previous estimate by a factor of
m(m-1)
.

•

Asymptotic estimate for
S_{m}(n)
.

Multiplying the total number of balanced words by the adjacency-avoidance and endpoint factors gives:

S_{m}(n)\;\approx\;m(m-1)\,T_{m}(n)\,(1-1/m)^{mn-1}.

This captures both the combinatorial structure and the probabilistic constraint in a simple product form.

•

Simplification and re-expression.

We note that
1-1/m=(m-1)/m
, and it is convenient to rewrite

(1-1/m)^{mn-1}=\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{mn-1}=\left(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\right)^{mn-1}\cdot\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{mn-1}\!\left(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\right)^{-mn+1}.

Hence, up to constants that are asymptotically negligible, both forms are equivalent.
We adopt the second (shifted) form because it simplifies later algebraic manipulations involving
m-1
rather than
m
.

•

Stirling’s approximation.

To obtain an explicit asymptotic expression, we expand
T_{m}(n)
using Stirling’s formula:

n!\sim\sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/e)^{n},\quad\text{so}\quad T_{m}(n)=\frac{(mn)!}{(n!)^{m}}\sim\frac{\sqrt{2\pi mn}\,(mn/e)^{mn}}{(2\pi n)^{m/2}\,(n/e)^{mn}}=(2\pi n)^{\frac{1-m}{2}}\,m^{mn+\frac{1}{2}}.

This grows factorially with
n
and exponentially with
m
.

Substituting this into the previous expression for
S_{m}(n)
gives

S_{m}(n)\;\sim\;m(m-1)\,(2\pi n)^{\frac{1-m}{2}}\,m^{mn+\frac{1}{2}}\,\left(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\right)^{mn-1}.

•

From
S_{m}(n)
to
H_{m}(n)
.

Using Theorem
2
and noting that the factorial factor cancels the
(n!)^{-m}
present in
T_{m}(n)
,
we obtain:

H_{m}(n)\;\sim\;\frac{(m-1)(mn)!}{2n}\,\left(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\right)^{mn-1},

which is the claimed asymptotic form (
10
).∎

The argument is asymptotically exact because the local dependency between consecutive colors has a negligible global effect when
n\to\infty
and
m
is fixed. Moreover, the factorial term
(mn)!
reflects the combinatorial explosion in arranging all
mn
vertices or letters, while the exponential correction

\left(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\right)^{mn-1}
encodes the probability of avoiding forbidden adjacencies in an
m
-color setting.
The prefactor
\frac{(m-1)}{2n}
accounts for endpoint distinctness and the normalization of undirected cycles.

Theorem 4

(Logarithmic Asymptotic Expansion)
.

For fixed
m\geq 2
and
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\log H_{m}(n)

\displaystyle=\log\!\Bigl(\frac{m-1}{2n}\Bigr)+\log((mn)!)+(mn-1)\log\!\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\Bigr)+O(1)

(11)

\displaystyle=mn\log(mn)\;-\;mn\;+\;\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi mn)\;-\;\log(2mn)

\displaystyle+\;mn\log\!\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\Bigr)\;+\;\log(m-1)\;+\;O(1).

Equivalently,

\log H_{m}(n)=mn\log n+n\!\left[m\log m+m\log\!\Bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{m-1}\Bigr)-m\right]-\log(2mn)+O(\log n).

(12)

Proof.

Take logarithms of the asymptotic expression (
10
) for
H_{m}(n)
:

\log H_{m}(n)=\log(m-1)-\log(2n)+\log((mn)!)+(mn-1)\log\!\Bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{m-1}\Bigr)+o(1).

Applying Stirling’s approximation,

\log((mn)!)=mn\log(mn)-mn+\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi mn)+O(1/mn),

and substituting yields the first displayed equality.
Writing
\log(mn)=\log n+\log m
and gathering the linear terms in
n

gives (
12
).
∎

Corollary 1

(Compact Logarithmic Form)
.

There exists a constant
C_{m}
depending only on
m
such that

\log H_{m}(n)=mn\log n+n\log(m!)-\log(2mn)-n+C_{m}+o(1),\qquad n\to\infty.

(13)

Thus the leading behavior is

\log H_{m}(n)=mn\log n+\Theta(n)
,
showing factorial growth in
(mn)!
with an exponential correction in
n
.

Proof.

Equation (
12
) has the
n
-dependent part

mn\log n+n\bigl[m\log m+m\log(1-\tfrac{1}{m-1})-m\bigr].

Since
m
is fixed, the bracketed quantity is constant in
n
.
Replacing it by
\log(m!)-1
up to an additive constant
and grouping all sublinear contributions into
C_{m}+o(1)

gives the stated compact form.
∎

The ratios

R_{m}(n)\;:=\;\frac{H_{m}(n+1)}{H_{m}(n)}

illustrate the expected factorial escalation predicted by Theorem
3

and provide a numerical validation of the asymptotic formula.

Theorem 5

(Empirical Ratios and Asymptotic Match)
.

For each fixed
m\geq 2
, the observed ratios
R_{m}(n)
satisfy

R_{m}(n)\;\approx\;\frac{(m(n+1))!}{(mn)!}\cdot\frac{n}{n+1}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\right)^{m}=\Bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{m}(mn+k)\Bigg)\cdot\frac{n}{n+1}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\right)^{m}.

(14)

Hence
R_{m}(n)
increases superlinearly with
n
,
dominated by the factorial factor
\prod_{k=1}^{m}(mn+k)
, in accordance
with the factorial term
(mn)!
of Theorem
3
.

Proof.

Divide the asymptotic form (
10
) for
H_{m}(n+1)
by that for
H_{m}(n)
.
Simplifying gives the displayed approximation. The dominant term originates from
the factorial ratio
(m(n+1))!/(mn)!
, while the remaining factors

n/(n+1)
and
\left(1-\frac{1}{m-1}\right)^{m}

represent the contribution of the normalization
and the adjacency-avoidance probability.
∎

6
Extensions and Generalizations

We outline some natural extensions of our framework, indicating how the
enumeration and bijective correspondences adapt in each case.

6.1
Weighted Smirnov words and generalized complete
m
-partite graph
K_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}

Definition 5

(Weighted Smirnov words)
.

Let
A=\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{A}_{m}
with
|\mathcal{A}_{i}|=n_{i}\geq 1
(not necessarily equal),
and
N=\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}
.
A
weighted Smirnov color-word
is a length-
N
word over
[m]
using
color
i
exactly
n_{i}
times, with no two consecutive equal colors.
We further consider the boundary constraint that the first and last colors are distinct.
Let
S(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})
denote the number of such color-words.

Proposition 2

(Inclusion–exclusion, non-uniform case)
.

Fix distinct endpoints
s\neq r
. Define, for
i\in[m]
,

n_{i}^{\prime}:=n_{i}-\mathbf{1}_{i=s}-\mathbf{1}_{i=r}.

If some
n_{i}^{\prime}<0
then
F(n_{1},\dots,n_{m};s\!\to\!r)=0
.
Otherwise

F(n_{1},\dots,n_{m};s\!\to\!r)=\!\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n_{1}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{m}=0}^{n_{m}-1}(-1)^{\sum_{i}k_{i}}\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n_{i}-1}{k_{i}}\right)\frac{(N-2-\sum_{i}k_{i})!}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(n_{i}^{\prime}-k_{i})!}.

(15)

By color-symmetry of endpoint pairs,

S(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,r\in[m]\\
s\neq r\end{subarray}}F(n_{1},\dots,n_{m};\,s\!\to\!r).

(16)

Proof sketch.

As in the uniform case, we fuse
k_{i}
internal adjacencies of color
i
, reducing
its block count from
n_{i}
to
n_{i}-k_{i}
; alternating signs correct overcounts.
Fixing endpoints
s\neq r
removes two letters and yields
(N-2-\sum_{i}k_{i})!

permutations of the remaining blocks, divided by
\prod_{i}(n_{i}^{\prime}-k_{i})!
for
indistinguishability. Summing over all admissible
(k_{1},\dots,k_{m})
gives
F
.
Summing over endpoint pairs gives
S
.
∎

Theorem 6

(Hamiltonian cycles in
K_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}
)
.

Let
G=K_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}
with
N=\sum_{i}n_{i}
.
Then the number of undirected Hamiltonian cycles is

H(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})\;=\;\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(n_{i}!)}{2N}\;S(n_{1},\dots,n_{m}).

(17)

Proof.

The word–path bijection holds verbatim:
each color
i
carries
n_{i}
letters/vertices, filled independently in
n_{i}!
ways.
A cycle of length
N
has
2N
linear representations (two directions,
N
starts).
∎

Remark 2

(Answer to a problem posed in
The American Mathematical Monthly
)
.

In February 2025, Donald E. Knuth posed the following problem in
[
15
]
:

How many undirected Hamiltonian cycles are there in the complete tripartite graph
K_{p,q,r}
with nonzero part sizes
p
,
q
, and
r
?

We believe that Theorem
6
provides an answer to this question for
n_{1}=p
,
n_{2}=q
, and
n_{3}=r
, through the closed inclusion–exclusion formula stated in Proposition
2
.

Remark 3

(Large-
N
asymptotics with proportions)
.

Let
p_{i}=n_{i}/N
with
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
and
p_{i}>0
fixed as
N\to\infty
.
A heuristic replacement of the adjacency-avoidance factor yields

S(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})\;\approx\;\frac{N!}{\prod_{i}n_{i}!}\;\Bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}^{2}\Bigr)^{N-1},

(18)

so that

\log H(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})\approx\log\Bigl(\frac{N!}{2N}\Bigr)+\sum_{i}\log(n_{i}!)+(N-1)\log\Bigl(1-\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}\Bigr).

(19)

Applying Stirling to
N!
and
n_{i}!
gives a precise version in the spirit of
Theorem
3
.

6.2
Directed multipartite graphs

Definition 6

(Orientation-aware cycles)
.

In the directed setting, a Hamiltonian cycle and its reverse are
distinct
.
Thus a cycle of length
N
has
N
linear representations (rotations only), not
2N
.

Corollary 2

(Directed-cycle counts)
.

For
K_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}
,

H^{\rightarrow}(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})\;=\;\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(n_{i}!)}{N}\;S(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})\;=\;2\,H(n_{1},\dots,n_{m}).

(20)

Proof.

Replace the symmetry divisor
2N
by
N
in Theorem
6
.
∎

6.3
Circular Smirnov words and cycles without endpoint correction

Definition 7

(Circular Smirnov words)
.

A circular Smirnov color-word of type
(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})
is a cyclic word with
no equal adjacent colors
including the wrap-around pair
(last to first).
Let
S^{\circ}(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})
be the number of such color-words up to rotation.

Proposition 3

(Linearization identity)
.

Let
N=\sum_{i}n_{i}
. Then

S^{\circ}(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})\;=\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{s=1}^{m}F(n_{1},\dots,n_{m};\,s\!\to\!s),

(21)

where
F(\cdot;\,s\!\to\!s)
counts linear words with first and last colors both equal to
s

and no equal adjacencies in between.

Proof.

Each circular word corresponds to
N
linear representatives obtained by cutting at
any position; exactly those representatives have matching endpoints,
and each circular word contributes equally. The Smirnov constraint carries through.
∎

Corollary 3

(Direct mapping to cycles)
.

For
K_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}
, there is a direct correspondence between circular Smirnov
color-words (after letter-filling by
\prod_{i}n_{i}!
) and
directed
Hamiltonian cycles
without any endpoint correction, yielding

H^{\rightarrow}(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})\;=\;\prod_{i}(n_{i}!)\;S^{\circ}(n_{1},\dots,n_{m}).

(22)

Dividing by
2
recovers the undirected count.

7
Conclusion

We presented a unified combinatorial framework linking Smirnov words and Hamiltonian cycles in multipartite graphs through explicit bijections and inclusion–exclusion enumeration.
The asymptotic results highlight the factorial nature of growth and the subtle role of adjacency-avoidance factors. Moreover, the extension to nonuniform part sizes
K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}}
provides a direct analytical answer to a problem recently posed by Donald E. Knuth
[
15
]
.

This study illustrates how structural bijections between word combinatorics and graph theory can yield closed enumeration formulas for problems previously addressed only recursively.

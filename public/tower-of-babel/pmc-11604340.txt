Response to “The Value of Non-Clinical Applications of Artificial Intelligence in Radiology Should Be Noted”
Authors: Nicole Kessa Wee, Kim-Ann Git, Wen-Jeng Lee, Gaurang Raval, Aziz Pattokhov, Evelyn Lai Ming Ho, Chamaree Chuapetcharasopon, Noriyuki Tomiyama, Kwan Hoong Ng, Cher Heng Tan
Published: 2024

We are pleased to note that our publication has garnered interest from the journal’s readership. Dr. Ye acknowledged in his letter to the editor the timeliness and value of our paper entitled “Position Statements of the Emerging Trends Committee of the Asian Oceanian Society of Radiology on the Adoption and Implementation of Artificial Intelligence for Radiology” [1]. However, Dr. Ye also opines that our paper “reflects a current bias in the focus of various stakeholders on AI technology in radiology,” while “ignoring the other values that AI can bring to radiology” [2].

In response to his comment, we would like to highlight that the main purpose of our paper was to specifically address the relatively low adoption and integration of clinical artificial intelligence (AI) in real-world settings, despite a myriad of regulatory approved AI solutions. To say that our paper was “ignoring” the utility of AI in radiology more comprehensively would be unfair, given that this was beyond the scope of our paper. We recognize that AI has indeed the potential to play a useful role in operational processes and workflows, professional training and radiology equipment management [3].

In our position statements, we put out recommendations to guide radiologists on current best practice. Our perspectives were guided by fundamental principles in the practice of modern medicine—patient autonomy, non-maleficence, justice, and beneficence—centered on the direct utilization of AI by clinical radiologists. Therefore, much as we would agree with Dr. Ye that AI could bring value in the broader context of healthcare beyond radiologist interpretation, our paper was not meant to downplay or even dismiss the potential value of non-clinical applications.

Dr. Ye concludes by suggesting that research needs to go into proving the value of AI when it is applied to the broader context of a radiology service. We agree on this point and would like to further suggest that being able to demonstrate its tangible economic and societal value is an important step for sustained adoption of AI in clinical (and non-clinical) applications. Consensus on this amongst the radiology community is lacking [4]. Again, this was not within the scope of our position statements. As such, we would much welcome Dr. Ye or other colleagues to pursue this in a more substantiated fashion.

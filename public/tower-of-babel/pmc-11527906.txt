Commentary: treatment indicators and prognostic factors in colorectal neuroendocrine neoplasms and adenocarcinoma with neuroendocrine differentiation
Authors: Dhivya Viswanathan, Rajakumar Govindasamy
Published: 2024

Dear Editor

The study, “Treatment Indicators and Prognostic Factors in Colorectal Neuroendocrine Neoplasms and Adenocarcinoma with Neuroendocrine Differentiation: A Single-Center Retrospective Study,” [1], sheds light on the distinct challenges and prognostic factors associated with colorectal neuroendocrine neoplasms (cNEN) and colorectal adenocarcinoma with neuroendocrine differentiation (cNED). Analyzing a cohort of 435 patients, the research compares survival rates and metastasis between these tumor types, providing crucial insights into the management of these aggressive malignancies.

The cohort included patients with neuroendocrine tumors (NET), neuroendocrine carcinomas (NEC), mixed neuroendocrine-non-neuroendocrine neoplasms (MiNEN), and cNED. One of the most notable findings was the high metastasis rates in MiNEN (58.6%) and NEC (65.4%) compared to NET (8.6%). These emphasizes more aggressive behavior of MiNEN and NEC, requiring more intensive treatment strategies, including radical surgery. Furthermore, NET patients with tumor grades G2/G3, tumors located in the colon, or those with tumors ≥ 1 cm had higher risks of metastasis, highlighting the need for early intervention [2].

The study’s identification of key prognostic factors, such as tumor size, location, and grade, holds significant clinical relevance. NET tumors larger than 1 cm, especially those in the colon, should be prioritized for aggressive treatment, including radical surgery, to improve outcomes. Additionally, CK20 positivity was associated with higher metastatic potential, suggesting that this biomarker could guide treatment decisions for patients with NET. The study also highlights diagnostic challenges, particularly with preoperative biopsies in cNEN, which frequently fail to capture neuroendocrine components in complex tumors like MiNEN [3]. This diagnostic ambiguity underscores the necessity of advanced diagnostic techniques and multidisciplinary consultation to improve preoperative accuracy, allowing for timely and appropriate treatment. Misdiagnoses or delayed diagnoses can lead to suboptimal treatment, further exacerbating patient outcomes.

Survival outcomes varied across tumor types and grades, with NET demonstrating a 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of over 90%, while MiNEN and NEC fared much worse, with OS rates under 50% and 30%, respectively. Within NET, the prognosis varied by grade, with G2 and G3 tumors associated with significantly worse outcomes than G1 [4]. These findings underscore the importance of considering tumor grade in treatment planning. Finally, the study draws attention to the poor prognosis of cNED compared to traditional adenocarcinoma, suggesting a need for more tailored treatment approaches. Current treatment guidelines may not fully account for the unique behavior of cNED, highlighting an area for future research and guideline refinement [5].

In conclusion, this study provides critical insights into the treatment and prognostic factors for cNEN and cNED, underscoring the importance of tumor grade, size, and location in predicting outcomes. The findings emphasize the need for more aggressive treatment strategies for high-risk patients and call for improved diagnostic tools to enhance treatment accuracy. Future research should focus on validating these findings in larger, multicenter studies to improve outcomes for these rare and aggressive tumors.

Legal Basis and Need for Collections

    The Call Report information collections are mandatory: 12 U.S.C. 
161 (national banks), 12 U.S.C. 324 (State member banks), 12 U.S.C. 
1817 (insured

[[Page 67652]]

State nonmember commercial and savings banks), and 12 U.S.C. 1464 
(Federal and State savings associations). At present, except for 
selected data items and text, these information collections are not 
given confidential treatment.
    Banks and savings associations submit Call Report data to the 
agencies each quarter for the agencies' use in monitoring the 
condition, performance, and risk profile of individual institutions and 
the industry as a whole. Call Report data serve a regulatory or public 
policy purpose by assisting the agencies in fulfilling their shared 
missions of ensuring the safety and soundness of financial institutions 
and the financial system and protecting consumer financial rights, as 
well as agency-specific missions affecting Federal and State-chartered 
institutions, such as conducting monetary policy, ensuring financial 
stability, and administering Federal deposit insurance. For many 
institutions, the Call Report is the source of the most current 
statistical data available for identifying areas of focus for on-site 
and off-site examinations. Among other purposes, the agencies use Call 
Report data in evaluating institutions' corporate applications, 
including interstate merger and acquisition applications for which the 
agencies are required by law to determine whether the resulting 
institution would control more than 10 percent of the total amount of 
deposits of insured depository institutions in the United States. Call 
Report data also are used to calculate risk-based assessments for 
insured depository institutions.

II. Current Actions

    Section 29 of the Federal Deposit Insurance Act (FDI Act) restricts 
insured depository institutions that are less than well capitalized 
from accepting brokered deposits. Reciprocal deposits \2\ obtained, 
directly or indirectly, from or through the mediation or assistance of 
a deposit broker are a subset of brokered deposits and generally must 
be reported as brokered deposits unless an exception applies. Section 
29(i) of the FDI Act provides a limited exception for reciprocal 
deposits to allow an insured depository institution that qualifies as 
an ``agent institution'' to exclude from treatment as brokered deposits 
a limited amount of reciprocal deposits. Reciprocal deposits held by an 
institution that does not qualify as an agent institution, must be 
treated as brokered deposits.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \2\ See 12 U.S.C. 1831f(i); 12 CFR 337.6.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Section 902 of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (Housing Act), 
which became law on July 11, 2026, amended the limited exception for 
reciprocal deposits in section 29(i) of the FDI Act in two principal 
respects. First, section 902 revised one of the three methods by which 
an insured depository institution may qualify as an agent institution. 
Previously, the first method required an insured depository institution 
to be well capitalized and to have a composite condition of 
``outstanding'' or ``good,'' corresponding to a CAMELS composite rating 
of ``1'' or ``2.'' Under the revised provision, an institution may 
qualify under this method if, when most recently examined, the 
institution was assigned a composite CAMELS rating of ``1,'' ``2,'' or 
``3'' and is well capitalized. Second, section 902 replaced the 
calculation of the maximum amount of reciprocal deposits that can be 
treated as not being brokered deposits under the limited exception, 
known as the general cap, with a tiered calculation based on the agent 
institution's total liabilities. Under the amended general cap, an 
agent institution may exclude from treatment as brokered deposits an 
amount of reciprocal deposits equal to the sum of:
    1. 50 percent of the portion of the institution's total liabilities 
that is less than or equal to $1 billion;
    2. 40 percent of the portion of total liabilities that is greater 
than $1 billion but less than or equal to $10 billion; and
    3. 30 percent of the portion of total liabilities that is greater 
than $10 billion but less than or equal to $96,333,333,333.
    On August 27, 2026, the FDIC Board of Directors approved an interim 
final rule,\3\ effective September 1, 2026, to implement section 902 of 
the Housing Act. In response to the interim final rule, the agencies, 
under the auspices of the FFIEC, requested and received emergency 
approval on September 28, 2026, from OMB to implement revisions to the 
Call Report that took effect beginning with the September 30, 2026, 
report date. The agencies revised the Call Reports by revising the 
instructions to conform to the statutory and regulatory definitions of 
brokered and reciprocal deposits for the following items and Glossary 
entry:
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \3\ See 91 FR 56022 (Sept. 1, 2026).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

     Schedule RC-E, Memorandum item 1.b., ``Total brokered 
deposits'';
     Schedule RC-E, Memorandum item 1.g., ``Total reciprocal 
deposits'';
     Schedule RC-E, Examples for Calculating the Special Cap;
     Schedule RC-O, item 9, ``Brokered reciprocal deposits''; 
and
     Glossary entry titled ``Brokered Deposits.''
    The agencies also established a new confidentiality exception for 
the amounts reported in Schedule RC-O, items 9, ``Brokered reciprocal 
deposits,'' and 9.a, ``Fully consolidated brokered reciprocal 
deposits,'' respectively.
    The new confidentiality exception is considered necessary because 
the amounts reported in Schedule RC-O, items 9 and 9.a., when compared 
with publicly available total reciprocal deposits reported in Schedule 
RC-E, Memorandum item 1.g., could reveal nonpublic supervisory 
information about an institution. The agencies are requesting comment 
on whether there should be any further changes to the Call Report 
instructions developed by the agencies to implement the revisions for 
which emergency approval was received from OMB. If modifications are 
made to the associated FDIC final rule, the agencies would modify the 
information collection revisions in this proposal to incorporate any 
necessary changes.

III. Timing

    The Call Report revisions made in response to the Housing Act, as 
implemented by the FDIC's interim final rule, were approved by OMB 
through the emergency clearance process, and these revisions have taken 
effect for the September 30, 2026, Call Report.

IV. Request for Comment

    Public comment is requested on all aspects of this joint notice. 
Comment is specifically invited on:
    (a) Whether the revised instructions accurately and clearly 
implement the amendments to the limited reciprocal deposit exception 
made by section 902 of the Housing Act;
    (b) Whether the proposed revisions to the collections of 
information that are the subject of this notice are necessary for the 
proper performance of the agencies' functions, including whether the 
information has practical utility;
    (c) The accuracy of the agencies' estimates of the burden of the 
information collections as they are proposed to be revised, including 
the validity of the methodology and assumptions used;
    (d) Ways to enhance the quality, utility, and clarity of the 
information to be collected;
    (e) Ways to minimize the burden of information collections on 
respondents, including through the use of automated collection 
techniques or other forms of information technology; and
    (f) Estimates of capital or start-up costs and costs of operation, 
maintenance, and purchase of services to provide information.

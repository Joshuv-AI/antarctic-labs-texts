Weakly turbulent saturation of the nonlinear scalar ergoregion instability
Nils Siemonsen

Abstract
We perform time-domain evolutions of the ergoregion instability on a horizonless spinning ultracompact spacetime in scalar theories with potential-type and derivative self-interactions mimicking the nonlinear structure of the Einstein equations. We find that the instability saturates by triggering a weakly turbulent direct cascade, which transfers energy from the most unstable and large-scale modes to small scales. The cascade’s nonlinear timescales of each mode are orders of magnitude shorter than the corresponding linear e-folding times. Through this mechanism, the counter-rotating stable light ring is filled with a spectrum of higher-order azimuthal modes forming a ring-like shape. Thereby we demonstrate that turbulent processes are likely also important during the fully gravitational saturation of the instability, leaving imprints in the gravitational wave emission.



I
Introduction

Ultracompact horizonless asymptotically flat spacetimes are characterized by stable light rings and ergoregions disconnected from any horizon—features that set them apart from vacuum black holes and less compact objects. At the linear level, such ergoregions have been shown to lead to an exponential growth of negative energy states of massless fields
Friedman 1978
;
Vilenkin 1978
;
Moschidis 2018
through the so-called ergoregion instability. Stable light rings, on the other hand, have been conjectured to trigger a nonlinear instability mechanism due to accumulation of interactions between quasi-trapped massless modes
Keir 2016
;
Cardoso et al. 2014a
. In the context of rapidly spinning ultra compact spacetimes, these two phenomena are intrinsically linked as the presence of an ergoregion implies the existence of a stable light ring
Cardoso et al. 2014a
.

In contrast to the nonlinear saturation and endstate of the ergoregion instability, which is practically unknown beyond simplistic expectations, the nonlinear properties of stable light rings has received some attention recently. In particular, using numerical relativity a nonlinear instability was claimed to exist in spinning boson star spacetimes with stable light rings in Ref.
Cunha et al. 2023
; however, thus far these results could not be reproduced
Evstafyeva et al. 2025
;
Siemonsen
(see also Refs.
Marks et al. 2025
;
Siemonsen 2024
). A step away from this nonlinear gravitational setting, the dynamics of a massless scalar testfield with quartic self-interactions trapped in a light ring were studied in Refs.
Benomio et al. 2024
;
Redondo-Yuste and Cárdenas-Avendaño 2025
. This scalar field was found to exhibit wave turbulence for sufficiently large initial data, triggered by the nonlinear interactions between long-lived linear modes. While such turbulent phenomena are ubiquitous in systems described by a nonlinear Schrödinger-type equation
Zakharov et al. 2012
;
Galtier 2022
;
Nazarenko 2011
, the dispersive nature of massless fields acts against accumulation of nonlinear interactions, unless in confining settings.

Gravitational turbulence has been observed in several such settings: the perfectly trapping Anti-de Sitter (AdS) spacetime was found to exhibit weak turbulence, ultimately leading to black hole formation
Bizon and Rostworowski 2011
;
Jalmuzna et al. 2011
;
Buchel et al. 2012
;
Balasubramanian et al. 2014
. Linear massless modes show slow decay also in asymptotically flat and asymptotically AdS black hole spacetimes
Detweiler 1977
;
Hod 2008
;
Yang et al. 2013
;
Cardoso et al. 2014b
;
Holzegel and Smulevici 2014
, and some progress has been made in understanding their nonlinear gravitational interactions
Yang et al. 2015
;
Iuliano et al. 2025
;
Figueras and Rossi 2025
;
Ma et al. 2025
. Turbulent dynamics may also occur in cosmological contexts and higher dimensional settings
Galtier and Nazarenko 2017
;
Galtier and Nazarenko 2021
;
Benomio 2021
;
Bhattacharyya et al. 2008
;
Carrasco et al. 2012
;
Adams et al. 2014
;
Green et al. 2014
;
Krynicki et al. 2025
;
Gay and Galtier 2024
;
Liang et al. 2025
;
Andrade et al. 2021
. If turbulent dynamics emerges during the nonlinear saturation of the ergoregion instability, this would, for instance, have implications for the existence of so-called black hole mimickers
Cardoso and Pani 2019
;
Bambi et al. 2025
;
Carballo-Rubio et al. 2025
as well as gravitational wave searches for them
Barausse et al. 2018
;
Mastrogiovanni et al. 2025
;
Fan and Chen 2018
.

The ergoregion instability mechanism exponentially amplifies negative energy states inside the ergoregion through positive energy fluxes at infinity
Friedman 1978
. Specifically, massless field configurations subject to this instability in axisymmetric spacetimes are non-axisymmetric with azimuthal index
m
and complex frequency
\omega_{m}
. Let
m>0
be the family of unstable modes, then the growth timescales of the energy of these modes was shown to increase as
\tau^{\rm EI}_{m}=[2\ \text{Im}(\omega_{m})]^{-1}\sim e^{am}
, with
a>0
, in the eikonal limit
m\rightarrow\infty

Comins and Schutz
. In this limit, such massless modes approach the dynamics of stably trapped null geodesics in the counter-rotating light ring contained within the ergoregion
Comins and Schutz
;
Siemonsen
; in particular, the unstable mode’s pattern speed
\Sigma=\text{Re}(\omega_{m})/m
approaches those geodesics’ orbital frequency
\omega_{-}
:
\Sigma\rightarrow\omega_{-}
(see e.g., Ref.
Siemonsen
). Intuitively, the decay rates of the longest lived massless modes, trapped in the counter-rotating light ring, turn negative as soon as the spacetime carries sufficient angular momentum to spawn an ergoregion. In particular, the linear ergoregion instability is a natural mechansim to drive perturbations into the nonlinear regime, where nonlinear processes associated with the stable light ring become important.

Given this intrinsic connection, in this work we investigate the nonlinear saturation of the ergoregion instability for the first time. As a model of the full Einstein equations, we focus on a massless scalar testfield with derivative and potential-type self-interactions propagating on a (horizonless) spinning boson star background with ergoregion. We find that the fastest-growing ergoregion unstable state drives rapid amplification of higher-order azimuthal modes leading to a weakly turbulent saturation of the instability. This turbulent mechanism transfers energy from the largest scales (of this most unstable mode) down towards the eikonal limit, i.e., into high-frequency highly trapped modes filling the stable light ring. Initially this process occurs repeatedly. We characterize the nonlinear turbulent transfer timescales and speculate about the late-time dynamics of the system, as well as discuss implications for observational searches for black hole mimickers. We set
G=c=1
throughout.

IV
Discussion

In this work, we studied the nonlinear saturation of the ergoregion instability in a scalar theory with self-interactions mimicking the derivative structure of the Einstein equations. We found that non-trivial couplings between linear modes induced by both potential-type and derivative self-interactions results in a weakly turbulent saturation of the instability. Energy injected through this unstable process, at large scales, cascades down towards small scales into more trapped modes primarily localized on the counter-rotating equatorial stable light ring. While the nonlinear energy transfer times grow with mode number in the case of potential-type self-interactions, derivative interactions (as also present in the Einstein equations) lead to a far more efficient energy transport to small scales. Derivative self-interactions have a wavenumber dependence,
\alpha|\partial\Psi|^{2}\sim\alpha k^{2}|\Psi|^{2}
, which leads to qualitatively similar dynamics to potential-type self-interactions at larger scales,
\alpha k^{2}\sim\alpha M^{-2}\sim\kappa
, but different mode coupling at small scales
k^{2}\gg M^{-2}
; this likely explains the drastically different nonlinear energy transfer times. Moreover, these turbulent timescales are, in general, amplitude-dependent. In the setting considered here the exponential growth dominates the dynamics until these nonlinear timescales match, or are shorter than, the e-folding time of the instability. This naturally leads to a non-decaying and non-equilibrium turbulent state towards late times, where the energy density is concentrated at the counter-rotating stable light ring. There is no turbulence in the radial or polar directions. Lastly, the unstable field’s frequency and growth rate are enhanced (weakened) when
\alpha
is negative (positive) by weakly nonlinear effects prior to triggering the turbulent cascade. With that our findings explicitly demonstrate that turbulent mechanisms present in the stable light ring are crucial to understanding the nonlinear saturation of the ergoregion intability.

Due to the similarity between the nonlinear structure of the theories considered here and the Einstein equations, the resulting mode coupling may trigger parametric driving of higher-order azimuthal perturbations and a turbulent cascade also in the case of the gravitational ergoregion instability. This would have implications for gravitational wave searches targeting smoking-gun signatures believed to be emitted by black hole mimickers
Cardoso and Pani 2019
.

To
speculate
about the energy spectrum of the system at late times, the energy in each mode can be modeled by
\dot{E}_{\ell+1,\ell+1}=E_{\ell\ell}/\tau^{\rm NL}_{\ell}-E_{\ell+1,\ell+1}/\tau^{\rm NL}_{\ell+1}
, for
\ell>1
, in an inertial range up the dissipation scale
\ell_{\max}+1
. Assuming a constant-in-time energy injection rate into the
\ell=m=1
mode (which we found to approximately hold at late times) together with stationarity of the spectrum, i.e.,
\dot{E}_{\ell\ell}=0
, implies
E_{\ell\ell}\sim\tau^{\rm NL}_{\ell}
. Potential-type self-interactions lead to increasing
\tau^{\rm pNL}_{\ell}
(at roughly fixed energy), resulting in the energy spectrum
growing
with mode number; though, this behavior is likely broken up in the eikonal limit, where
\omega_{m=\ell}\rightarrow m\omega_{-}

\tau^{\rm dNL}_{\ell}\sim
const. at decreasing linear energies
-E_{\ell\ell}

\ell

Let us briefly discuss some of the shortcomings of our treatment. We justify considering only couplings between the
\ell=m\geq 0
modes in Appendix
B
. Beyond the treatment there, one may naively expect that the inclusion of all possible couplings either only to lead to additional, but negligible, laminar mixing, or additional pumping of energy into higher-order polar modes, which would render the state “more” turbulent. Lastly, these dynamics are features of the ergoregion instability and stable light ring, rather than specifics to the background spacetime chosen in this work.

Real-world chemotherapy use and survival outcomes in postmenopausal HR+/HER2−breast cancer with low genomic risk (RS ≤ 25): a multicenter cohort study from China
Authors: Chengye Hong, Duanduan Wang, Yongwu Chen, Songping Lin, Yidong Huang, Liangqiang Li, Debo Chen, Weibin Lian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe RxPONDER trial established that adjuvant chemotherapy does not confer survival benefit in postmenopausal women with hormone receptor–positive (HR+)/HER2−negative breast cancer, 1–3 positive lymph nodes, and a low 21-gene Recurrence Score (RS ≤ 25). Whether these findings have been adopted in routine clinical practice in Asian populations remains unclear. We conducted a multicentre retrospective cohort study of postmenopausal Chinese patients with HR+/HER2−breast cancer and limited nodal involvement who underwent surgical treatment and Oncotype DX testing between 2017 and 2023. Among 385 eligible patients, 54.8% had an RS ≤ 25, yet 46.4% of this low-genomic-risk group received adjuvant chemotherapy. Chemotherapy use declined substantially following publication of RxPONDER, indicating a marked shift in treatment patterns. Importantly, no disease-free survival (DFS) benefit was observed with chemotherapy in patients with RS ≤ 25. Multivariable analyses revealed that higher nodal burden, high histologic grade, and low progesterone receptor expression independently influenced chemotherapy decision-making despite low genomic risk. These real-world data provide supportive evidence consistent with the RxPONDER findings in a Chinese population; however, given the relatively short follow-up duration, longer-term outcomes are warranted to further substantiate these observations.Keywords: RxPONDER, Breast cancer, Recurrence scores, Chemotherapy, SurvivalSubject terms: Cancer, Oncology

Introduction

Hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative breast cancer is the most prevalent molecular subtype, accounting for approximately 65% of newly diagnosed breast cancer cases worldwide1. Despite its generally favorable prognosis, this subtype displays considerable heterogeneity in recurrence risk and treatment response, necessitating more individualized treatment strategies. Multigene expression assays such as the 21-gene recurrence score (RS) assay (Oncotype DX, Exact Sciences) have emerged as pivotal tools in evaluating tumor biology and guiding adjuvant therapy decisions in early-stage HR+/HER2−breast cancer2. Oncotype DX was initially developed and validated in node-negative, hormone receptor–positive, HER2-negative early breast cancer cohorts, with its prognostic and predictive value established in the NSABP B-14 and B-20 trials, and subsequently confirmed in the prospective TAILORx randomized trial2–4. The TAILORx study demonstrated that patients with recurrence scores (RS) of 0–25 did not derive a meaningful benefit from adjuvant chemotherapy in the overall population, and exhibited excellent outcomes with endocrine therapy alone, thereby supporting omission of chemotherapy in this subgroup in contemporary clinical practice2. However, subsequent data from the RxPONDER trial expanded these findings to include postmenopausal women with 1–3 positive axillary lymph nodes and RS ≤ 25, suggesting that chemotherapy could also be omitted in this population without compromising outcomes5. Accordingly, both the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and the American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) have incorporated these insights into their clinical guidelines, recommending routine Oncotype DX testing in node-positive postmenopausal patients with HR+/HER2−disease.

Nevertheless, real-world evidence evaluating the clinical utility of Oncotype DX in guiding adjuvant therapy decisions among patients with limited nodal involvement remains limited. Han and colleagues reported in a post-hoc analysis of the RxPONDER trial that more than 80% of women aged ≥ 50 years with estrogen receptor (ER)-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer and ≤ 3 positive lymph nodes could safely omit chemotherapy based on the RS6. A recent study from Ireland demonstrated that, following the dissemination of the RxPONDER results, the use of Oncotype DX testing increased significantly among patients aged ≥ 50 years with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer and N1 disease. Conversely, a decline in testing rates was observed in patients under 50 years of age. Notably, the implementation of this targeted testing approach resulted in 58% of patients avoiding unnecessary chemotherapy, ultimately yielding a cost savings of over 3.3 million euros7. However, real-world evidence addressing this issue in the Chinese population remains limited. This study investigates the impact of the publication of the RxPONDER study results on the decision-making process related to the use of adjuvant chemotherapy in Chinese postmenopausal patients with HR+/HER2−breast cancer with 1–3 positive lymph nodes. In addition, we examine the factors influencing chemotherapy selection among this patient population.

Results

Patients and characteristics

A total of 385 postmenopausal patients with HR+/HER2−breast cancer and 1–3 positive lymph nodes were included in the final analysis. Figure 1 shows the distribution of the 21-gene RS in the study cohort. The analysis revealed that the majority of patients (42.34%) exhibited intermediate-risk scores (RS 16–25). High-risk genomic profiles constituted a substantial segment of the study population, with 21.30% of patients falling within the RS 26–30 category and 23.90% presenting with RS ≥ 31, collectively representing 45.20% of the cohort. In contrast, low-risk RS values were markedly less frequent, with only 7.01% of patients classified as RS 11–15 and 5.45% as RS ≤ 10, accounting for only 12.46% of the total population.

The distribution of the 21-gene recurrence score (RS) in postmenopausal patients with HR+/HER2−breast cancer and 1–3 positive lymph nodes.

Patients were subsequently stratified into two groups based on the 21-gene RS: RS ≤ 25 (n = 211) and RS > 25 (n = 174). As detailed in Table 1, the RS > 25 cohort exhibited significantly more advanced tumor characteristics. A higher percentage of grade III tumors was observed in this group compared to the RS ≤ 25 group (34.78% vs. 9.74%, P < 0.001). Furthermore, patients in the RS > 25 group demonstrated significantly elevated Ki67 expression (≥ 14%), as well as a greater prevalence of Luminal B subtype diseases compared to their counterparts (Ki67: 70.69% vs. 51.66%, P < 0.001; Luminal B: 85.06% vs. 58.77%, P < 0.001). In addition, we observed a significant difference in the proportion of progesterone receptor (PR) expression greater than 20% between the two groups, with 54.02% in the RS > 25 group and 86.26% in the RS ≤ 25 group (P < 0.001). These findings had tangible implications for treatment decisions, as the RS > 25 group was significantly more likely to receive chemotherapy (88.51% vs. 46.45%, P < 0.001). Although not statistically significant, there was a trend towards a higher proportion of patients in the RS > 25 group receiving anthracycline combined with docetaxel. No statistically significant differences were found between the two groups concerning age, histological type, lymphovascular invasion (LVI), tumor size and the number of positive lymph nodes.

Recurrence score ≤ 25 cohort analysis

Within the cohort of postmenopausal patients with HR+/HER2−breast cancer and a RS ≤ 25, clinicopathological characteristics and treatment patterns were compared between those who received adjuvant chemotherapy and those who did not (Table 2). A significant age imbalance was observed between the two groups (P < 0.001): patients receiving chemotherapy were predominantly ≤ 60 years old, whereas those in the non-chemotherapy group were more frequently aged > 70 years. Temporal trends in treatment selection were also evident. Patients diagnosed between 2017 and 2020 were substantially more likely to receive chemotherapy than those diagnosed between 2021 and 2023 (79.59% vs. 20.41%, P < 0.001). Differences in tumor biological characteristics were also apparent between the two groups. The chemotherapy group exhibited a higher proportion of grade III tumors (14.61% vs. 5.66%, P = 0.045) and a greater prevalence of elevated Ki67 expression (63.27% vs. 41.59%, P = 0.002), consistent with a predominance of the Luminal B subtype (72.45%). In contrast, the non-chemotherapy group had significantly higher rates of tumors with progesterone receptor (PR) expression > 20% (92.04% vs. 79.59%, P = 0.009) and a greater proportion of the Luminal A subtype. Distinct patterns of locoregional management were also observed. Mastectomy was performed more frequently in the chemotherapy group than in the non-chemotherapy group (73.47% vs. 57.52%, P = 0.015). Moreover, patients receiving chemotherapy had a higher incidence of involvement of two to three metastatic lymph nodes (39.79% vs. 23.00%, P = 0.011).

During a median follow-up of 41 months, a total of nine DFS events were recorded, including two cases of local recurrence, six cases of distant metastasis, and one breast cancer–related death. Despite the baseline differences in clinicopathological characteristics and treatment patterns, Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated no statistically significant difference in DFS between patients who received adjuvant chemotherapy and those who did not within the RS ≤ 25 cohort (Fig. 2).

Factors influencing chemotherapy decision-making in breast cancer patients with a recurrence score ≤ 25

In the multivariate analysis of postmenopausal breast cancer patients with a RS ≤ 25, we identified several critical determinants that significantly influenced chemotherapy recommendations (Table 3) . Notably, the diagnostic year emerged as a significant factor, with a remarkable 95% reduction in the odds of chemotherapy recommendations for the period from 2021 to 2023 compared to 2017–2020 (OR 0.05, 95% CI 0.02–0.14, P < 0.001). Age constituted another pivotal determinant, as patients aged 61–70 years and those older than 70 years displayed substantially lower odds of receiving chemotherapy (OR 0.16, 95% CI 0.06–0.46, P < 0.001 and OR 0.02, 95% CI0.00–0.10, P < 0.001, respectively). Furthermore, lymph node metastasis had a profound impact on treatment decisions; specifically, patients with 1, 2, or 3 positive lymph nodes demonstrated significantly increased odds for chemotherapy recommendations compared to micrometastatic patients (OR 9.09, 95% CI 2.40–34.42, P = 0.001; OR 25.00, 95% CI 5.04–124.01, P < 0.001; and OR 31.01, 95% CI 4.04–237.86, P < 0.001, respectively). In addition, tumors classified as Grade III exhibited a higher likelihood of chemotherapy recommendation compared to Grade I tumors (OR 14.21, 95% CI 1.42–142.16, P = 0.024). Similarly, patients who underwent mastectomy displayed an increased probability of receiving chemotherapy in contrast to those who underwent breast-conserving surgery (OR 4.17, 95% CI 1.60–10.82, P = 0.003). Lastly, PR expression levels played a crucial role in chemotherapy decision-making; specifically, low PR expression was associated with a higher likelihood of chemotherapy recommendations (OR 5.01, 95% CI 1.37–18.35, P = 0.015).

Discussion

In this retrospective study, we report the real-world application of adjuvant chemotherapy following 21-gene RS assay in Chinese postmenopausal breast cancer patients who are HR+/HER2−and have 1–3 positive lymph nodes. Our findings indicate that 54.8% (211/385) of patients had a 21-gene RS ≤ 25. Given the limited number of distant recurrence events, DFS was used as the primary endpoint. Among these patients, only 46.4% (98 of 211) ultimately received adjuvant chemotherapy, and no survival benefit was observed with the addition of chemotherapy. Further analysis identified several independent predictors significantly influencing chemotherapy recommendations for this cohort. These predictors included younger age (≤ 60 years), a later diagnosis period (2021–2023, post-RxPONDER era), higher tumor grade (Grade III), an increased number of positive lymph nodes (1–3) and low PR expression. Notably, our results suggest that the publication of the RxPONDER trial results was temporally associated with changes in adjuvant chemotherapy decision-making in Chinese breast cancer patients.

The integration of the 21-gene RS assay into clinical practice has led to significant changes in treatment recommendations. A multicenter study in Quebec found that the application of the 21-gene RS assay resulted in a 67.1% reduction in chemotherapy recommendations among physicians8. Similarly, a prospective multicenter study from Canada reported that 52% of treatment decisions changed after knowing the RS, with a net 45% reduction in chemotherapy usage9. Current clinical guidelines advocate for the use of the 21-gene RS in postmenopausal women with HR+/HER2−breast cancer, particularly those with 1–3 positive lymph nodes. The European Commission Initiative on Breast Cancer recommends the use of the 21-RS for lymph node-negative women and suggests its application for high-risk patients with node-positive disease10. Furthermore, the ASCO has updated its guidelines to endorse the use of genomic assays, including the 21-gene RS, to guide adjuvant chemotherapy decisions in this patient population11. According to these guidelines, approximately 54.8% of postmenopausal patients with HR+/HER2−breast cancer and 1–3 positive lymph nodes in our cohort could potentially avoid chemotherapy. These results are similar to data from the United Kingdom, where they found that chemotherapy utilization decreased by 51.5% after obtaining RS results12. Stratifying the data by the timing of diagnosis relative to the release of the RxPONDER trial results demonstrates a notable shift in treatment patterns. Following the dissemination of the trial results (2021–2023), 78.9% of patients (75 out of 95) chose not to receive chemotherapy. In contrast, before the release of these findings, only 32.8% (38 out of 116) of patients opted out of chemotherapy. We further confirmed through multivariate analysis that the release time of the RxPONDER trial results is an independent factor influencing chemotherapy decision-making. This temporal change in chemotherapy utilization may reflect the dissemination of the RxPONDER trial results; however, the comparison between different diagnostic periods should be interpreted cautiously because baseline differences between the groups may introduce potential confounding.

Furthermore, we have confirmed that the number of lymph node metastases is an important factor influencing chemotherapy decisions. The study by Kalinsky et al. indicates that in postmenopausal patients, chemotherapy is generally not recommended for those with RS ≤ 25; however, subgroup analyses suggest that as the number of lymph node metastases increases, some patients may face a heightened risk of recurrence5. A real-world database study from the Danish Breast Cancer Cooperative Group compared long-term survival among different risk groups defined by “monarch E” trial (≥ 4 positive lymph nodes or 1–3 with high-risk tumor characteristics). The results suggest that the number of lymph nodes is an important factor for risk stratification13. Notably, more than half of the patients enrolled in the RxPONDER trial had only one lymph node metastasis, and the proportion of patients with three lymph node metastases was less than 10%. Moreover, there are no additional prospective randomized controlled trials to further confirm that this group of patients can safely be exempted from chemotherapy. In light of these findings, our study underscores the importance of individualized treatment strategies in postmenopausal HR+/HER2−breast cancer patients. Incorporating lymph node metastasis into the evaluation process could enhance clinical decision-making and improve patient outcomes. Future research should further explore how to integrate these factors—RS and lymph node involvement—to optimize treatment strategies, ultimately leading to better survival rates and quality of life for patients. Additionally, cultural and institutional factors may influence treatment intensity preferences. In China, for example, physician caution or patient anxiety may drive overtreatment despite low RS, particularly in the absence of long-term local outcome data. Moreover, factors like low PR expression may be seen as a marker of endocrine resistance, thus influencing chemotherapy recommendations even with low RS14.

To our knowledge, it represents one of the largest cohorts of postmenopausal HR+/HER2−breast cancer patients with 1–3 positive lymph nodes who underwent 21-gene RS testing in the Chinese population. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design may introduce selection bias, particularly because the decision to perform the 21-gene RS assay was at the discretion of treating physicians. Second, the follow-up duration may be insufficient to fully evaluate long-term survival outcomes in HR-positive breast cancer, which is characterized by a relatively indolent clinical course and a persistent risk of late recurrence. Moreover, the number of DFS events observed in our cohort was limited, which may reduce the statistical power of survival analyses. Therefore, longer follow-up and studies with a larger number of outcome events are warranted to further validate the safety of chemotherapy de-escalation in this population.

In conclusion, our real-world data demonstrate that the 21-gene Recurrence Score played a pivotal role in guiding adjuvant chemotherapy decision-making in this Chinese cohort, with more than half of the patients ultimately omitting chemotherapy based on the genomic risk assessment. These findings underscore the clinical utility of the 21-gene assay in facilitating treatment de-escalation in patients with favorable genomic profiles. Nevertheless, given the relatively limited follow-up duration and the small number of survival events observed, the long-term oncologic safety of this chemotherapy-sparing strategy requires further confirmation through studies with extended follow-up and larger cohorts.

Methods

Study population

This study included patients who underwent surgical treatment between January 2017 and December 2023 at Shanghai Ruijin Hospital, the Fifth Hospital of Xiamen, Longyan Second Hospital, and Quanzhou First Hospital. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) histologically confirmed hormone receptor–positive, HER2-negative early breast cancer; (2) postmenopausal status; (3) one to three positive axillary lymph nodes, including micrometastases; and (4) completion of the 21-gene Recurrence Score assay. Exclusion criteria included: (1) male breast cancer; (2) incomplete data regarding chemotherapy treatment; and (3) insufficient demographic or pathological information. A total of 411 patients were initially screened for eligibility. Of these, 26 were excluded due to incomplete follow-up data. The final analytic cohort comprised 385 patients with complete follow-up information, and all analyses were conducted based on this population. The study protocol was approved by the independent ethics committee of all participating hospitals, adhering to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Given the retrospective design of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the Ethics Committee of Quanzhou First Hospital.

21-gene recurrence score assay

The 21-gene RS assay was performed as part of routine clinical management rather than for the specific purpose of this study. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue was sectioned at a thickness of 10 µm for molecular analysis. Prior to RNA extraction, each specimen was reviewed to ensure adequate tumor content and to exclude potential DNA contamination. Total RNA was isolated from two unstained sections of each FFPE sample. Gene expression profiling was performed using quantitative reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using the Omniscript RT Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. qRT-PCR amplification was subsequently conducted on the Applied Biosystems 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Expression levels of cancer-related genes included in the 21-gene panel were quantified in triplicate and normalized to a set of reference housekeeping genes. The final Recurrence Score was calculated based on the standardized expression algorithm incorporating the weighted expression of proliferation-, invasion-, and hormone-related gene groups.

Definition

Postmenopausal status was determined based on clinical criteria and hematologic testing. Patients had not had a menstrual period within the past 12 months at the time of diagnosis and had measured serum hormone levels, including estradiol, follicle-stimulating hormone, and luteinizing hormone, at postmenopausal levels. ER or PR positivity was defined as having at least 1% staining in tumor nuclei. PR was categorized as low expression (< 20%) or high expression (≥ 20%). Ki-67 index was categorized as low expression (< 14%), or high expression (≥ 14%). Micrometastasis was defined according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system as metastatic tumor deposits measuring > 0.2 mm and ≤ 2.0 mm in greatest dimension, or comprising more than 200 tumor cells but not exceeding 2.0 mm. DFS, the primary study endpoint, was defined as the time from surgery to the first occurrence of local recurrence, distant recurrence, or breast cancer-related death. Survival outcomes were analyzed using time-to-event methods.

Statistical analyses

The distribution of demographic and pathological characteristics was compared using the χ2 test. Univariate and multivariable logistic regression models with odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were used to identify factors influencing chemotherapy decision-making. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using R 4.2.3 version.

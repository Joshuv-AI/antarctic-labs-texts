Kondo-Peierls transition with nonsymmorphic zone boundary gap formation
Kazumasa Hattori 1 and Hiroaki Kusunose 2

Abstract
We study nonsymmorphic space group symmetry breakings in correlated electron systems. Under nonsymmorphic symmetry, it is well known that there are degeneracies in the electronic Bloch states at the Brillouin zone boundaries. When the system undergoes a phase transition into an ordered phase with breaking the nonsymmorphic symmetry, the degeneracy is lifted. This happens even when the order parameter is uniform. We point out that this general feature leads to various uniform Peierls transition in nonsymmorphic systems. In particular, we show that such mechanism of the Peierls gap formation can be realized accompanying with uniform anisotropic Kondo singlet formations. This explains the hidden electric order observed in CeCoSi.



Introduction.—
Correlated electrons exhibit various symmetry breaking phases at low temperatures. A prototype of charge density-wave (DW) and bond orders with ordering vector
{\bm{Q}}
is well understood by the Peierls mechanism
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
. The translational symmetry breaking generating two sublattices compactifies the original Brillouin zone (BZ) at
{\bm{Q}}/2
. This leads to a single-particle energy gap formation at the new BZ boundaries. Such charge DWs can be driven not only by electronic correlations
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
and the nested Fermi surface
[
10
,
11
]
but also couplings with other degrees of freedom; typically lattice phonons
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
and dimerizations of ions
[
15
,
16
]
.

Various DW orders are also understood in a similar way by replacing the charge degrees of freedom by the corresponding ones such as spin
[
17
,
18
,
16
,
15
]
, orbital
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
, and various multipoles
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
. When
{\bm{Q}}
is incommensurate, long-pitched modulation of order parameter emerges. For several decades, such incommensurate DW orders have been studied in a context of multiferroics
[
25
]
and also in moiré phenomena in skyrmion lattice
[
26
]
, recently.

When the ordering vector
{\bm{Q}}
is zero, the lattice translation is not broken, i.e. the unit cell is unchanged. One considers that there is no strong driving force which leads to “DW” orders with modulation of the order parameter inside the unit cell. This is because there is no symmetry reasons to form gaps in the single-particle spectra. Thus, whether
{\bm{Q}}=\bf{0}
orders occur or not depends on the detail of the system considered. However, this naive expectation is not the case when the system possesses nonsymmorphic symmetries (nSSs). It is known that there are additional degeneracies at BZ boundaries protected by symmetry operations in nonsymmorphic systems
[
27
]
. This nSS can be broken even by orders with
{\bm{Q}}={\bf 0}
. This leads to partial gap formation at the BZ boundaries where the electronic states are degenerate in the original system with nSSs.
[
28
]

In this Letter, we show that sublattice degrees of freedom in a unit cell can cause
{\bm{Q}}={\bf 0}
“Peierls” transitions in nonsymmorphic Kondo lattice systems. We will introduce an intuitive picture by using simple one-dimensional models.
Then, we will discuss that such a phase transition is
indeed realized in the nonsymmorphic tetragonal compound CeCoSi
[
29
]
, where we use the extended Kondo lattice with nearest-neighbor Kondo exchange interactions. We show the phase transition to anisotropic Kondo singlet phases leads to single-particle gap formation along the BZ boundaries. To the best of our knowledge, CeCoSi is the first real material candidate where the intersite anisotropic Kondo singlet “order”, proposed theoretically by Ghaemi and Senthil
[
30
]
, is realized.

To intuitively understand the nonsymmorphic BZ-boundary gap formation, let us discuss simple models in one-dimensional zigzag chain with two sites in the unit cell along the

These examples tell us a possible mechanism of

These FSs tend to be gapped out with positive energy gains when the nSSs are broken by some electronic orders [Fig.

The key ingredient is that the nSS guarantees the degeneracy at the BZ boundary and the nearly-local nature, and thus large energy gain is achieved in Kondo systems.

Another issue is the pressure

At much higher pressure, it is reported that a structural transition possibly occurs again above

44

, which seems to be a pure structural transition independent of electronic origin as contrary to the lower-pressure phase.

Meanwhile, the resistivity exhibits a hump just below

[
29
]
, which indicates some FSs are gapped out. Such a signature of gap opening is also reported in the optical reflection spectroscopy
[
45
]
. Moreover, inelastic neutron experiments report momentum independent excitations at around

[

]
.
It is also likely that the

, while

46
]
.

The above mysteries can be resolved if the intermediate order below

is associated with the Kondo screening. Since the conduction electrons in CeCoSi mainly consist of Co
d
electrons, the Kondo coupling is of intersite form. Indeed, such couplings are reported by the first-principle calculations
[
47
]
. The nSS breaking can occur by realizing
anisotropic intersite Kondo singlet
with the ordering vector

{\bm{Q}}={\bf 0}
as mentioned in the introduction
[
30
]
. Such orders can break the nSSs in CeCoSi, and the hump in the resistivity is caused by the BZ boundary gap proportional to the order parameter, which becomes prominent only when the order parameter is sufficiently large at high pressure. As for the rapid decrease of
T_{0}
for

GPa, the sudden change in the Ce valence that suppresses the Kondo energy scale is most likely as recently reported
[
44

. In this Letter, we focus on the ordering mechanism at
T_{0}
and do not discuss the valence transition and the trivial Néel order for
T<T_{\rm N}
in order to make our analysis clearer.

Discussions.—
We have demonstrated that the anisotropic Kondo singlet phases such as
\varepsilon_{g}
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{g}
can be realized in nonsymmorphic Kondo systems.
Indeed, the
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{g}
phase is consistent with the situation for
T_{\rm N}<T<T_{0}
reported in CeCoSi.
Since the difference in the
\varepsilon_{g}
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{g}
phases is just the different linear combinations within the two-dimensional irreps., they are energetically close, and it is natural to appear next to each other.
One of the two is favored when the transition from the high-
T
phase is of the second order, which is determined by the fourth-order coefficient in the free energy
[
39
,
34
]
.
It is interesting to note that the
\varepsilon_{g}
type phase is indeed realized under magnetic fields
\parallel
[100].
[
34
]

In a recent Ramann scattering experiment
[
53
]
, the results are interpreted by a direct (spin-dependent) hybridization between
f
electrons at the two Ce sites, and the relation between the excitations
\omega\sim 2.5
meV below
T_{0}
in the neutron experiments. From our point of view, the excitations can be related to the anisotropic Kondo singlet formation accompanying the BZ boundary gap opening, while elaborated analyses are needed for this issue.
Although the suppression mechanism of
T_{0}
is not included in our model and the
T_{c}
’s eventually increases as

Summary.—
We have investigated nonsymmorphic symmetry breaking in the Kondo lattice system with intersite couplings. Anisotropic Kondo singlet orders can account for the enigmatic order observed in CeCoSi such as the increase in the transition temperature under pressure and the signature of gap formation, and the absence of the quadrupole degrees of freedom in the local electronic state in low-energy scale. Our results not only propose a possible microscopic order parameter in CeCoSi but also a unique mechanism of BZ gap formation similar to the Peierls mechanism in Kondo lattice systems with nonsymmorphic degeneracy.

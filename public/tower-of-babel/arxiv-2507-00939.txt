Convergence Rate Analysis for Monotone Accelerated Proximal Gradient Method This work was partially funded by the China Scholarship Council 202208520010, and also benefited from the support of the FMJH Program Gaspard Monge for optimization and operations research and their interactions with data science.
Zepeng Wang, Juan Peypouquet

Abstract
We analyze the convergence rate of the monotone accelerated proximal gradient method, which can be used to solve structured convex composite optimization problems. A linear convergence rate is established when the smooth part of the objective function is strongly convex, without knowledge of the strong convexity parameter. This is the fastest convergence rate known for this algorithm.



keywords
Accelerated proximal gradient method, Strongly convex, Convex optimization

1
Introduction

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space, and consider the convex composite optimization problem:

\min_{x\in H}F(x)=f(x)+g(x),

(1)

where
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and
L
-smooth, and
g:H\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is convex, proper and lower-semicontinuous. We assume that the solution set of (
1
) is nonempty, write
x^{*}\in\argmin(F)
and denote
F^{*}=F(x^{*})
.

To solve (
1
), one can employ the Accelerated Proximal-gradient Method, also referred to as FISTA
Beck and Teboulle 2009
, which was developed based on Nesterov’s acceleration technique
Nesterov 1983
. It takes the form
Beck and Teboulle 2009
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} y_{k+1}&=\prox_{sg}\left(x_{k}-s\nabla f(x_{k})\right),\\
x_{k+1}&=y_{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+\alpha}(y_{k+1}-y_{k}),\end{aligned}\right.

(APM)

for
k\geq 0
, where
\alpha>0
and
0<s\leq\frac{1}{L}
. For
\alpha\geq 3
, a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)
for the function values was shown in
Beck and Teboulle 2009
. But one can actually obtain a faster rate
o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)
with
\alpha>3

Attouch and Peypouquet 2016
. When
f
is convex and satisfies a local error-bound condition, a convergence rate of
o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2\alpha}}\right)
is guaranteed, as long as
\alpha>1
and
0<s<\frac{1}{L}

Liu et al. 2024
. When
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex, a linear convergence rate
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}(1+\rho)^{k}}\right)
, with
\rho=\frac{\mu}{16L}
, holds when
s=\frac{1}{2L}
and
k
is large enough
Li et al. 2024
. This rate was further improved to
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{(1-\rho)^{k}}{k^{2}}\right)
, with
\rho=\frac{\mu}{4L}
, and proved valid for all
k\geq 0

Bao et al. 2023
. This linear convergence rate is achieved without knowing the strong convexity parameter
\mu
, which may be difficult or computationally expensive to estimate in practice. If
\mu
is known, an accelerated linear convergence rate
\mathcal{O}\left(\left(1-\rho\right)^{k}\right)
, with
\rho=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L}}
is obtained by replacing the extrapolation parameter
\frac{k}{k+\alpha}
in (
APM
) by
\frac{1-\sqrt{\mu/L}}{1+\sqrt{\mu/L}}

Nesterov 2004
.

Despite their fast convergence, the function values on the sequences generated by (
APM
) are, in general,
not monotonically
decreasing. This problem can be fixed by using restarting techniques
Giselsson and Boyd 2014
;
O’Donoghue and Candès 2015
;
Alamo et al. 2023
;
Maulén and Peypouquet 2023
;
Aujol et al. 2024
, for example, but we shall not pursue this line of research here. Another solution is to force the monotonicity of the function values by structurally modifying (
APM
). This is achieved by the Monotone Accelerated Proximal-gradient Method
Beck and Teboulle 2009
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} z_{k}&=\prox_{sg}\left(x_{k}-s\nabla f(x_{k})\right),\\
y_{k+1}&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}z_{k},&&\text{if }F(z_{k})\leq F(y_{k}),\\
y_{k},&&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.\\
x_{k+1}&=y_{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+\alpha}(y_{k+1}-y_{k})+\frac{k+\alpha-1}{k+\alpha}(z_{k}-y_{k+1}),\end{aligned}\right.

(M-APM)

where
\alpha\geq 3
and
0<s\leq\frac{1}{L}
. Remarkably, the convergence rate
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)
is preserved when
f
is convex. If
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex, a linear convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}(1+\rho)^{k}}\right)
, with
\rho=\frac{\mu}{16L}
, was proved in
Fu and Shi 2024
for
s=\frac{1}{2L}
and
k
large enough. The purpose of this article is to show that the constant
\rho
can be further improved to
\rho\sim\frac{\mu}{4L}
, as for the nonmonotone counterpart
Bao et al. 2023
.

2
Linear convergence of (
M-APM
)

In this section, we prove the linear convergence of (
M-APM
) for the function values. To facilitate the convergence rate analysis, we define

G_{s}(x):=\frac{x-\prox_{sg}\left(x-s\nabla f(x)\right)}{s}.

As shown in
(
Bao et al. 2023
, Lemma 7)
, if
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex,
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right]
and
x,y\in H
, we have

F\left(x-sG_{s}(x)\right)\leq F(y)+\left\langle G_{s}(x),x-y\right\rangle-\frac{s(2-sL)}{2}\|G_{s}(x)\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

(2)

If
f
is just convex, the inequality is valid with
\mu=0
.

With the notation of
G_{s}(x)
, we have
z_{k}=x_{k}-sG_{s}(x_{k})
, and (
M-APM
) gives:

(k+1)(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})-k(x_{k}-y_{k})+(\alpha-1)(x_{k+1}-x_{k})=-(k+\alpha-1)sG_{s}(x_{k}).

(3)

Our convergence analysis relies on the energy sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
, given by

E_{k}:=\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}+\theta_{k}\left(F(y_{k})-F^{*}\right),

(4)

with
\phi_{k}=k(x_{k}-y_{k})+(\alpha-1)(x_{k}-x^{*})
and
\theta_{k}=k(k+\alpha-1)s
.

2.1
Quantification of the energy decrease

With the notation introduced above, we have the following:

Proposition 1
.

Let
\alpha\geq 3
and
0<s\leq\frac{1}{L}
. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be generated according to (
M-APM
), and consider the sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
defined by (
4
). Then,

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{(1-sL)(k+\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}\|sG_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\mu sk(k+\alpha-1)}{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\mu s(\alpha-1)(k+\alpha-1)}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Proof.

By (
4
), we have

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}\right)+\theta_{k}\left(F(y_{k+1})-F(y_{k})\right)

(5)

\displaystyle+\left(\theta_{k+1}-\theta_{k}\right)\left(F(y_{k+1})-F^{*}\right).

On the other hand, (
3
) implies that

\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}=-(k+\alpha-1)sG_{s}(x_{k}),

so that

\|\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\|^{2}=(k+\alpha-1)^{2}\|sG_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2},

and

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{k},\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle k(x_{k}-y_{k})+(\alpha-1)(x_{k}-x^{*}),-(k+\alpha-1)sG_{s}(x_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle=-k(k+\alpha-1)\langle sG_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle-(\alpha-1)(k+\alpha-1)\langle sG_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle.

Since

\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}=\langle\phi_{k},\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\|^{2},

we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(k+\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}\|sG_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}-k(k+\alpha-1)\langle sG_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(\alpha-1)(k+\alpha-1)\langle sG_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle.

(6)

Noting that
F(y_{k+1})\leq F(z_{k})
, and using (
2
) with
x=x_{k}
and
y=y_{k}
, we obtain

F(y_{k+1})\leq F(y_{k})+\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle-\frac{s(2-sL)}{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}.

Likewise, setting
x=x_{k}
and
y=x^{*}
in (
2
), we obtain

F(y_{k+1})-F^{*}\leq\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle-\frac{s(2-sL)}{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}.

(7)

Using these two inequalities, together with (
2.1
), in (
5
), it follows that

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\left[(\alpha-3)k+(\alpha-3)\alpha+1\right]\langle sG_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(k+1)(k+\alpha)\frac{(2-sL)}{2}\|sG_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(k+\alpha-1)^{2}\|sG_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-k(k+\alpha-1)\frac{\mu s}{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}-(2k+\alpha)\frac{\mu s}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2},

since
\theta_{k}=k(k+\alpha-1)s
and
\theta_{k+1}-\theta_{k}=(2k+\alpha)s
. If
\alpha\geq 3
, then
(\alpha-3)k+(\alpha-3)\alpha+1>0
. Also, inequality (
7
) implies that

\langle sG_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle\geq\frac{(2-sL)}{2}\|sG_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\mu s}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}.

This precisely results in

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{(1-sL)(k+\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}\|sG_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\mu sk(k+\alpha-1)}{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\mu s(\alpha-1)(k+\alpha-1)}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2},

as claimed.
∎

Remark 1
.

In particular, the sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is nonincreasing.

2.2
An upper bound for the energy

It is possible to bound the energy in terms of the same quantities that appear in the energy decrease given by Proposition
1
, namely:

Proposition 2
.

Let
\alpha\geq 3
and
0<s\leq\frac{1}{L}
. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be generated according to (
M-APM
), and consider the sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
defined by (
4
). Then,

\displaystyle E_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k^{2}}{2}\left(1+\omega+\lambda\right)\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\omega}+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(k+\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}+\sigma+\frac{1-\mu s(2-sL)}{\mu s}\right)\|sG_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2},

for every
\omega,\lambda,\sigma>0
.

Proof.

By (
3
), we have

\phi_{k+1}=k(x_{k}-y_{k})+(\alpha-1)(x_{k}-x^{*})-(k+\alpha-1)sG_{s}(x_{k}).

For every
\zeta,\xi\in H
and
m>0
, we have
\langle\zeta,\xi\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2m}\|\zeta\|^{2}+\frac{m}{2}\|\xi\|^{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k^{2}}{2}\left(1+\omega+\lambda\right)\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\omega}+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle+\frac{(k+\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}+\sigma\right)\|sG_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2},

where
\omega,\lambda,\sigma>0
. On the other hand, (
7
) gives

\displaystyle F(y_{k+1})-F^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{s(2-sL)}{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\frac{1-\mu s(2-sL)}{2\mu}\right]\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2},

because
F(y_{k+1})\leq F(z_{k})
. Recalling that

\displaystyle E_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}+s(k+1)(k+\alpha)\big(F(y_{k+1})-F^{*}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}+s(k+\alpha-1)^{2}\big(F(y_{k+1})-F^{*}\big)

for
\alpha\geq 3
, and combining this with (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle E_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k^{2}}{2}\left(1+\omega+\lambda\right)\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\omega}+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(k+\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}+\sigma+\frac{1-\mu s(2-sL)}{\mu s}\right)\|sG_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2},

as claimed.
∎

2.3
Main result

The proof of our main result relies on the following comparison tool, whose proof is elementary:

Lemma 3
.

Let
(a_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
,
(b_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
and
(W_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
be positive, and assume
A,B\in\mathbb{R}
are such that

A\leq-\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}W_{i}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad B\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}W_{i}.

Then,

A+\rho B\leq 0,\qquad\hbox{where}\qquad\rho:=\min_{i=1,\dots,N}\left(\frac{a_{i}}{b_{i}}\right).

Now we are in a position to prove our main result, namely:

Theorem 4
.

Let
F=f+g
, where
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
is
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth for
L\geq\mu>0
, and
g:H\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is convex, proper and lower-semicontinuous. Consider algorithm (
M-APM
) with parameters
\alpha\geq 3
and
0<s\leq\frac{1}{L}
, and initial condition
x_{0}=y_{0}\in H
. For every
k\geq k_{\alpha}:=\lceil\alpha-1\rceil
, we have

F(y_{k})-F^{*}\leq\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2sk(k+\alpha-1)}(1+\rho)^{-k+k_{\alpha}},

where

\rho\geq\min\left\{\frac{\mu s(1-sL)}{1+\mu s(sL+2)},\ \frac{\mu s}{2}\right\}.

Proof.

By comparing the conclusions of Propositions
1
and
2
, under the light of Lemma
3
, we deduce that

(1+\rho)E_{k+1}\leq E_{k},

where

\rho=\max_{\omega,\lambda,\sigma>0}\min\left\{\frac{\mu s(1-sL)}{1+\mu s\left(sL-1+\frac{1}{\lambda}+\sigma\right)},\frac{\mu s(k+\alpha-1)}{k(1+\omega+\lambda)},\frac{\mu s(k+\alpha-1)}{(\alpha-1)\left(1+\frac{1}{\omega}+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)}\right\}.

Setting
\omega=\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
and
\sigma=1
, we obtain

\rho\geq\min\left\{\frac{\mu s(1-sL)}{1+\mu s(sL+2)},\ \frac{\mu s(k+\alpha-1)}{2k},\ \frac{\mu s(k+\alpha-1)}{4(\alpha-1)}\right\},

which, if
k\geq\alpha-1
, reduces to

\rho\geq\min\left\{\frac{\mu s(1-sL)}{1+\mu s(sL+2)},\ \frac{\mu s}{2}\right\}.

As a result, for every
k\geq k_{\alpha}:=\lceil\alpha-1\rceil
, we have

E_{k}\leq E_{k_{\alpha}}(1+\rho)^{-k+k_{\alpha}}\leq E_{0}(1+\rho)^{-k+k_{\alpha}},

in view of Remark
1
. Using
x_{0}=y_{0}
, we have
E_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-1)^{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}
. We conclude by applying
F(y_{k})-F^{*}\leq\frac{E_{k}}{k(k+\alpha-1)s}
.
∎

2.4
A few special cases

When Theorem
4
is applied with the canonical step size
s=\frac{1}{2L}
, we obtain the following:

Corollary 5
.

Let
f
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be generated by (
M-APM
) with parameters
\alpha\geq 3
and
s=\frac{1}{2L}
. Given
x_{0}=y_{0}
, we have, for every
k\geq k_{\alpha}:=\lceil\alpha-1\rceil
,

F(y_{k})-F^{*}\leq\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}L\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{k(k+\alpha-1)}\left(1+\frac{\mu}{4L+5\mu}\right)^{-k+k_{\alpha}}.

The linear convergence is lost using the critical step size
s=\frac{1}{L}
. But a sublinear convergence rate
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)
is still valid. More precisely, in view of Remark
1
and using
F(y_{k})-F^{*}\leq\frac{E_{k}}{k(k+\alpha-1)s}
, we have the following:

Theorem 6
.

Let
f
be convex and
L
-smooth. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be generated by (
M-APM
) with parameters
\alpha\geq 3
and
0<s\leq\frac{1}{L}
. Given
x_{0}=y_{0}
, we have, for every
k\geq 0
,

F(y_{k})-F^{*}\leq\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{2sk(k+\alpha-1)}.

Abstract
We study p p -adic manifolds associated with twisted points of quotient stacks 𝒳 = [ U / G ] \Xc=[U/G] and their quotient spaces π : 𝒳 → X \pi:\Xc\to X . We prove several structural results about the fibres of π \pi and derive in particular a formula expressing p p -adic integrals on X X in terms of the cyclotomic inertia stack of 𝒳 \Xc , generalizing the orbifold formula for Deligne-Mumford stacks. We then apply our formalism to moduli problems associated to hereditary abelian categories with symmetric Euler pairing, and show that their refined BPS-invariants are computed locally on the coarse moduli space by a p p -adic integral. As a consequence we recover the χ \chi -independence of these invariants for 1 1 -dimensional sheaves on del Pezzo surfaces previously proven by Maulik–Shen. Along the way we derive a new formula for the plethystic logarithm on the λ \lambda -ring of functions on k k -linear stacks, which might be of independent interest.

This paper is the third in a series of articles by the authors devoted to

Methods: twisted
p
-adic points & Bruhat-Tits buildings

Let us recall the geometric setting
2.8
of a global quotient stack
\Xc=[U/G]
with a morphism

\pi:\Xc\to X
,
to an algebraic space
X
relative to
\Spec(\Oc_{F})
. We further require the existence of an open dense

such that the base change
\Xc_{W}=\Xc\times_{X}W\to W
is an equivalence, and thus

is reminiscent to the map to a coarse moduli space of semistable objects. In this analogy, the subset

corresponds to a subset of the stable locus.

The proof of Formula (
2
) exploits structural properties of the map
\Xc(\Oc_{F})\to X(\Oc_{F})
. As will be stated in Theorem
1.4
below, this map is almost everywhere a locally measure-preserving topological covering. In order to solve the over-counting problem caused by the finite fibres of this covering, it is necessary to embed
\Xc(\Oc_{F})
inside an even bigger covering of
r
-twisted points,
\Xc(\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r})\to X(\Oc_{F})
. Here,
\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r}
is defined to be as the root stack of
\mathbb{D}_{F}=\Spec\Oc_{F}
, obtained by formally adjoining an
r
-th root to the divisor given by the ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{F}\subset\Oc_{F}
. This is a tame stack, containing
\Spec F
as an open dense subscheme and
B\mu_{r}=[\Spec k/\mu_{r}]
as a closed subscheme.

Under the assumptions above, there is a subset
\Xc(\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r})^{\sharp}\subset\Xc(\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r})
,
r\geq 1
, with the complement being of measure zero, admitting the structure of a
p
-adic manifold. We show the following fundamental properties:

Theorem 1.4

(
3.11
,
3.31
)
.

(a)

The map

\pi_{r}:\Xc(\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r})^{\sharp}\to X(\Oc_{F})^{\sharp},

is étale with finite fibres.

(b)

Assume
\Xc/\Oc_{F}
is
S
-complete and fix a presentation
\Xc=[U/G]
with
G
a general linear group. Then, for any
x\in X(\Oc_{F})^{\sharp}
we can identify
\cup_{r\geq 1}\pi_{r}^{-1}(x)
with the rational points of a bounded convex region inside the Bruhat-Tits building of
G
.

Figure 1:
The product of this graph with
\Rb
is the extended Bruhat-Tits building of
\GL_{2}(\Qb_{3})
.

The second assertion is crucial for solving the aforementioned over-counting problem. As shown by part (b) of the result above, the cardinality of a fibre
N_{r}=|\pi_{r}^{-1}(x)|
agrees with the number of
\Zb[\frac{1}{r}]
-points in a convex polytope
P
in the Bruhat-Tits building of
G/F
. The generating series

E_{P}(T)=\sum_{r\geq 1}N_{r}T^{r}

is known as the Ehrhart series and can be shown to be the Taylor series expansion of a rational function
E_{P}(T)\in\Qb(T)
with a single pole in
T=1
. Furthermore, it is a well-known principle in combinatorics that the limit
\mathsf{lim}_{T\to\infty}E_{P}(T)
agrees with
-1
. Heuristically exchanging limits, analytic continuation and integration, one sees that the volume

can be computed in the following way:

\mu_{orb}\big(X(\Oc_{F})\big)=-\mathsf{lim}_{T\to\infty}Q_{X}(T),

where

is the analytic continuation of
\sum_{r\geq 1}\mu_{orb}(\Xc(\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r}))T^{r}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
combines this strategy with a volume formula for Artin stacks

, to obtain a formula for the volume of
\Xc(\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r})^{\sharp}
for a fixed

.

Relation to previous work

To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
1.1
is the first result of its kind applying to moduli spaces of Artin stacks. We are not aware of any similar formulae in the p-adic or motivic integration literature. The overall shape of the expression, that is, evaluating the analytic continuation of a generating series at infinity to obtain a quantity of interest has appeared before, notably in connection with motivic vanishing cycles (see
[
DL98
,
HL15
]
). It would be interesting to investigate a possible connection between these two, a priori unrelated, formulae.

The

case of Theorem
3.11
, that is the map
\pi_{1}\colon\Xc(\mathbb{D}_{F})\to X(\Oc_{F})
, was first studied in the motivic setting in
[
SU22
]
, where it was interpreted as a measure of the non-separatedness of a stack. It should be pointed out that in this context, unlike (1) of Theorem
3.11
for
p
-adic integration,
\pi_{1}
can have infinite fibres, see Section 10 in
loc. cit
.

In Donaldson-Thomas theory, BPS-invariants and their refinements appear as integral invariants in the presence of strictly semi-stable objects in a given CY3 category
[
JS12
,
KS08
]
. In
[
COW24
]
it was observed that in the case of
1
-dimensional sheaves on local del Pezzo surfaces and meromorphic Higgs bundles on a curve, the alternating sum of Frobenius-traces on BPS-cohomology can be computed as a

-adic integral on the corresponding coarse moduli spaces. The proof in
loc. cit.
is rather indirect, based on a

, and the present article grew out of the desire to provide a more conceptual understanding from the point of view of

above), albeit

-adic integration only allows one to recover a slightly weaker statement.

In this subsection we refer to a group object in the category of

Let

Proposition 2.1

[

Under the assumptions above, the topological quotient

Proof.

It is clear that there can be at most one such structure of an
F
-analytic manifold, by virtue of the Implicit Function Theorem.

Thus, we turn to the proof of existence. Hausdorffness of the quotient follows from properness of the action. Second countability is inherited from

\pi\colon X\to X/G

The last step will be to construct charts on

Proof.

Note that the orbit

To construct a chart as above, we let

Definition 2.3

A stack

Definition 2.4

Let

2.3
Proper and étale maps

Recall the notion of a finite covering map
\pi\colon U\to V
of topological spaces as map for which there exists an open covering
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
V
such that each
\pi^{-1}(V_{i})\xrightarrow{\pi}V_{i}
is isomorphic to the projection map
V_{i}\times F_{i}\to V_{i}
for some finite set
F_{i}
endowed with the discrete topology. The purpose of this subsection is to prove the following auxiliary results:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\pi\colon U\to V
be a proper and étale morphism of
F
-analytic manifolds. Such a
\pi
is a covering map in the neighbourhood of any point
x\in V
where the preimage
\pi^{-1}(x)
is finite.

Proof.

According to the Inverse Function Theorem, every
y\in\pi^{-1}(x)
has an open neighbourhood
W_{y}
such that
\pi(W_{y})\subset V
is open and the restriction

\pi|_{W_{y}}\colon W_{y}\to\pi(W_{y})

is an isomorphism of
F
-analytic manifolds.

Since there are only finitely many elements in
\pi^{-1}(x)
, the intersection

N_{x}=\bigcap_{y\in\pi^{-1}(x)}\pi(W_{y})

is open. By construction, we have
\pi^{-1}(N_{x})\supset\bigsqcup_{y\in\pi^{-1}(x)}W_{y}
, and thus we see that

\pi^{-1}(N_{x})\supset\bigsqcup_{y\in\pi^{-1}(x)}\underbrace{\big(\pi^{-1}(N_{x})\cap W_{y}\big)}_{=L_{y}}.

We refer to
L_{y}
as the
leaf
of
y\in\pi^{-1}(x)
.

We intend to show that possibly after replacing
N_{x}
by a smaller neighbourhood, the above inclusion is in fact an equality, i.e., that
\pi^{-1}(N_{x})
equals the disjoint union of leaves. Let
N_{x}^{(n)}
be a descending sequence of compact-open neighbourhoods of
x
contained in
N_{x}
and forming a neighbourhood basis. We denote
\pi^{-1}(N_{x}^{(n)})\cap L_{y}
by
L_{y}^{(n)}
.

Assume by contradiction that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
z_{n}
in
\pi^{-1}(N_{x}^{(n)})
, which does not lie in
\bigsqcup_{y\in\pi^{-1}(x)}L_{y}^{(n)}
. By properness of the map
f
, this sequences possesses a limit point
z
. By construction, this point
z
lies in
\pi^{-1}(x)
and must belong to a leaf
L_{z}
. Since
L_{z}
is open in
U
, it follows that for some
n
the point
z_{n}
is contained in
L_{z}
and hence in
L_{z}^{(n)}
. This contradiction concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Assume that
\pi\colon U\to V
is a proper and étale morphism of
F
-analytic manifolds with finite fibres. Then, the function

\sep\colon V\to\mathbb{N}

assigning to
x\in V
the cardinality
|\pi^{-1}(x)|
is locally constant.

Proof.

By virtue of definition of a finite covering, every point
x\in V
possesses an open neighbourhood
N_{x}
such that there exists a finite discrete space
F_{x}
and a homeomorphism
\pi^{-1}(N_{x})=N_{x}\times F_{x}
intertwining
\pi
and the projection to the first component. For every
y\in N_{s}
we therefore have
\sep(y)=|F_{x}|
, and thus the function
\sep
is locally constant.
∎

Lemma 3.4

For a quotient stack

By

This construction gives an exact monoidal equivalence between the category of normed finite-dimensional vector spaces over

We first show essential surjectivity: A vector bundle over

The fact that

The equivariant

We start by introducing a natural

\Xc

A point in

and

is defined as in the case of Deligne-Mumford stacks

GWZ20a
, Definition 2.20]

\Xc
at

, we must work with a tangent complex, a two-term complex of
k
-vector spaces
T_{x}\Xc=[V_{-1}\to V_{0}]

Furthermore,

can be realised as a complex of vector spaces endowed with actions of the stabiliser group
\Aut(x)

can be defined as an object of the derived category

of quasi-coherent sheaves on

-action on

For every pair

as above, we therefore obtain a

-action on

by restricting the natural

-action along

Definition 4.1

\displaystyle=\psi_{n}\left(\sum_{y^{\prime}\in\phi^{-1}(x^{\prime})}f(y^{\prime})\right)=\psi_{n}\left(\phi_{!}(f)\right)(x).

This shows that
\phi_{!}
is a
\lambda
-ring homomorphism.

Now, assume
\phi
is as in
(ii)
. Then we have for
f,g\in\mcf(S_{2})
and
y\in S_{1}

\displaystyle\phi^{*}(fg)(y)=fg(\phi(y))=\sum_{x^{\prime}+x^{\prime\prime}=\phi(y)}f(x^{\prime})g(x^{\prime\prime})=\sum_{y^{\prime}+y^{\prime\prime}=y}f(\phi(y^{\prime}))g(\phi(y^{\prime\prime}))=\phi^{*}(f)\phi^{*}(g)(y).

Finally, let
f\in\mcf(S_{2})
,
y\in S_{1}
and
n\geq 1
. As above we argue that if there is no
y^{\prime}\in S_{1}
such that

, then the same holds for
\phi(y)
by assumption, and thus
\phi^{*}(\psi_{n}(f))(y)=0=\psi_{n}(\phi^{*}(f))(y)
. If

we have

\phi^{*}(\psi_{n}(f))(y)=\psi_{n}(f)(\phi(y))=\psi_{n}(f(\phi(y^{\prime})))=\psi_{n}(\phi^{*}(f))(y).

The final statement follows since (plethystic) exponential and logarithm are defined using only
\lambda
-ring operations.
∎

For later use let us record the following explicit formula:

Proposition 5.8
.

For any

and

the following holds:

\Log(1+f)(x)_{n}=\sum_{m,s\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{s-1}\mu(m)}{ms}\sum_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{s})\in S_{nm}^{s}\colon\;x=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\Tr_{nm/n}(y_{i})}f(y_{1})_{nm}\cdots f(y_{s})_{nm}

Proof.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Log(1+f)(x)_{n}&=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\psi_{m}(\log(1+f))(x)_{n}\\
&=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\sum_{y\in\Tr^{-1}(x)}\log(1+f)(y)_{nm}\\

5.2
k
-linear stacks

Following an idea of Artin–Zhang
[
AZ01
]
, moduli stacks for finitely presented objects in an
R
-linear abelian category are studied by Alper–Halpern-Leistner–Heinloth in
[
AHLH23
, Section 7]
. The abelian category is assumed to be co-complete and locally noetherian. No restrictions other than commutativity and unitality are imposed on the base ring
R
(which is denoted
k
in
loc. cit.
). We fix such a co-complete category
\Bc
, and denote the stack of finitely presented objects in
\Bc
by
{\mathbb{M}}_{\Bc}
.

The definition of the stack
{\mathbb{M}}_{\Bc}
hinges on the insight of Artin–Zhang that in the context above, there is a natural (exterior) tensor product

\otimes_{R}\colon\Mod(R)\times\Bc\to\Bc,

which allows one to define the notion of an
S
-family of objects in
\Bc
for every commutative
R
-algebra
S
. We denote by
\Bc_{S}
the
S
-linear abelian category obtained by this base-change procedure from
\Bc
. For an object
X\in\Bc
we write
X_{S}
for
X\otimes_{R}S
. Faithfully flat descent theory is satisfied in this general setting, which implies the statement that
{\mathbb{M}}_{\Bc}
is a stack.

Henceforth, we assume that our base ring
R
is a field
k
with algebraic closure
\bar{k}
.
We also fix a full sub-category
\overline{\mathcal{C}}\subset\Bc^{\rm fp}_{\bar{k}}
, which we assume to be an extension-closed abelian sub-category of the category of finitely presented objects in
\Bc_{\bar{k}}
defined over
\bar{k}
.

We then obtain an abelian subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subset\Bc^{\rm fp}
, which we define to be the full sub-category of objects
X
in
\Bc^{\rm fp}
satisfying
X_{\bar{k}}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}}
.

Furthermore, it gives rise to a sub-stack
{\mathbb{M}}={\mathbb{M}}_{\mathcal{C}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{\Bc}
. Here, an
S
-family of objects
X_{S}
in
\Bc^{\rm fp}_{S}
is assumed to belong to
{\mathbb{M}}_{\mathcal{C}}(S)
if and only if for every
z\colon\Spec\bar{k}\to S
the base change

.

In applications, the objects in
\mathcal{C}
are of principal interest and
\Bc
plays an auxiliary role, as for instance, higher Ext-groups
\Ext^{i}_{\Bc}(X,Y)
for two objects in
\mathcal{C}
are defined with respect to the ambient category
\Bc
. This set-up is necessary to ensure that the higher Ext-groups reflect the geometry of interest.

Example 5.10
.

Let
\Bc
be the category of sheaves of complex vector spaces on a connected topological manifold

. We denote by
\mathcal{C}
the category

of finite-dimensional complex local systems on

, that is, locally constant sheaves of finite-dimensional complex vector spaces on

. The category
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to
\Loc(B\pi_{1}(X))
, that is, the category of local systems on the classifying space of

. In particular, one has
\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{C}}(\underline{\Cb}_{X},\underline{\Cb}_{X})=H^{i}(B\pi_{1}(X),\Cb)
, whereas
\Ext^{i}_{\Bc}(\underline{\Cb}_{X},\underline{\Cb}_{X})=H^{i}(X,\Cb)
.

The higher Ext-groups

satisfy the following flat base-change relation, which we record for later use.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let

be a flat ring homomorphism and
\Bc
be a co-complete and locally noetherian

-linear abelian category. There is a natural isomorphism
\Ext^{i}_{\Bc}(X,Y)\otimes_{R}S\simeq\Ext^{i}_{\Bc_{S}}(X_{S},Y_{S})

Proof.

This is Proposition C3.1(ii) in
[
AZ01
]

∎

Following Mozgovoy

-linear stack. Note that in

In addition to the assumptions fixed above we will work with categories satisfying the following:

\Hom(\mu_{r},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/r{\mathbb{Z}}
. In our parametrization, this amounts to replacing
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{s})
by a cyclic shift
(y_{k},\ldots,y_{s},\sigma(y_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(y_{k-1}))
and the weights
w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}
by the weights
w_{i}+w\pmod{1/m}
for some
w\in({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})[r]
accordingly. This defines an action of
({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})[r]
on
\Delta_{m,s}[r]
and we denote the resulting quotient by
\overline{\Delta_{m,s}[r]}
.

Since
H^{1}(k_{n},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})=0
, the homomorphism
\Aut(x)(k_{n})\to\uAut(x)(k_{n})
is surjective. Hence if two different choices of homomorphism
\tilde{\chi}\colon\mu_{r}\to\Aut(x)
yield isomorphic objects
\chi\in I_{\mu_{r}}B\uAut(x)(k_{n})
, then up to a cyclic shift of the weights
(w_{i})_{i}
as above the homomorphisms
\tilde{\chi}
are already conjugate over
k_{n}
and so come from different choices of isomorphism
x\cong\Tr_{m,s}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{2})
. This shows that the only ambiguity in our parametrization of the isomorphism classes in
I_{\mu_{r}}B\uAut(x)(k_{n})
is described by the above cyclic shifts.

Next we wish to determine the group
\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)
in terms of this parametrisation, since this is exactly the automorphism group of the pair
(x,\chi)\in I_{\mu_{r}}{\mathcal{M}}
. The projection
\pi\colon\Aut(x)\to\uAut(x)
restricts to a homomorphism
\Cent_{\Aut(x)_{\bar{k}}}(\tilde{\chi})\to\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)
. We denote by
\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)^{\circ}\subset\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)
the connected component of the identity and by

\pi_{0}(\chi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)/\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)^{\circ}

the group of connected components of the centralizer of
\chi
.

Lemma 5.15
.

(i)

The group scheme
\Cent_{\Aut(x)_{\bar{k}}}(\tilde{\chi})
is connected and surjects onto
\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)^{\circ}
.

(ii)

The finite group scheme
\pi_{0}(\chi)
is constant over
k_{n}
.

(iii)

|\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)(k_{n})|=|\pi_{0}(\chi)|\prod_{i=1}^{s}|\Aut(y_{i})(k_{nm})|/|{\mathbb{G}}_{m}(k_{n})|

Proof.

(i) A decomposition of
x
into indecomposable elements in
{\mathbb{M}}(\bar{k})
identifies
\Aut(x)_{\bar{k}}
with an extension of a product of copies of the group
\GL_{n,\bar{k}}
(for varying
n
) by a unipotent group. Hence the claim about the centralizer of
\tilde{\chi}
follows from the fact that the centralizer of every homomorphism
\mu_{r}\to\GL_{n}
is smooth and connected.

Hence
\pi
factors through a morphism
\tilde{\pi}\colon\Cent_{\Aut(x)_{\bar{k}}}(\tilde{\chi})\to\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)^{\circ}
. By
[
CGP15
, A.8.10]
the Lie algebra of
\Cent_{\Aut(x)_{\bar{k}}}(\tilde{\chi})
(resp.
\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)^{\circ}
) is equal to the subspace of the Lie algebra of
\Aut(x)_{\bar{k}}
(resp.
\uAut(x)_{\bar{k}}
) fixed by
\mu_{r}
under the natural
\mu_{r}
-action via
\tilde{\chi}
(resp.
\chi
) and the adjoint action. This implies that
\tilde{\chi}
induces a surjective map of Lie algebras. Since both these centralizers are smooth and connected this implies that
\tilde{\pi}
is surjective.

(ii) Every connected component of
\Cent_{\uAut(x)(\chi)}
is a torsor under
\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)^{\circ}
and hence has a
k_{n}
-point by Lang’s theorem.

(iii) We start with the identity

|\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)(k_{n})|=|\pi_{0}(\chi)||\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)^{\circ}(k_{n})|.

Since
\tilde{\chi}^{\sigma}
differs from
\tilde{\chi}
by a central cocharacter, the centralizer
\Cent_{\Aut(x)}(\tilde{\chi})
canonically descends to
k_{n}
. Since we chose our isomorphism
x\cong\Tr_{m,s}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{s})
over
k_{n}
, the surjection
\Cent_{\Aut(x)}(\tilde{\chi})\to\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)
descends to a homomorphism over
k_{n}
with kernel
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
. Using this we find

|\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)^{\circ}(k_{n})|=|{\mathbb{G}}_{m}(k_{n})||\Cent_{\Aut(x)}(\tilde{\chi})(k_{nm})^{\sigma}|=|{\mathbb{G}}_{m}(k_{n})|\prod_{i}|\Aut(y_{i})(k_{nm})|,

which concludes the proof.
∎

It remains to describe
\pi_{0}(\chi)
:

Lemma 5.16
.

(i)

Sending a class
g\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)^{\circ}\in\pi_{0}(\chi)
to the homomorphism
{}^{\tilde{g}}{\tilde{\chi}}-\tilde{\chi}\colon\mu_{r}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
associated to a lift
\tilde{g}\in\Aut(x)(\bar{k})
of
g
gives a well-defined injection

\iota\colon\pi_{0}(\chi)\hookrightarrow\Hom(\mu_{r},{\mathbb{G}}_{m}).

(ii)

A homomorphism
\tau\colon\mu_{r}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
is in the image of
\iota
if and only if
\tilde{\chi}+\tau
is
\Aut(x)(\bar{k})
-conjugate to
\lambda
.

Proof.

(i) A different lift
\tilde{g}
differs from the given one by an element in the center of
\Aut(x)
and so doesn’t change the homomorphism
{}^{\tilde{g}}\tilde{\chi}-\chi
. Since every element of
\Cent_{\uAut(x)}(\chi)^{\circ}\in\pi_{0}(\chi)(\bar{k})
can be lifted to
\Cent_{\Aut(x)_{\bar{k}}}(\tilde{\chi})
by Lemma
5.15
, the choice of
g
also doesn’t affect this homomorphism. Furthermore, by construction the composition of
{}^{\tilde{g}}\tilde{\chi}-\tilde{\chi}
with
\pi\colon\Aut(x)\to\uAut(x)
is the trivial homomorphism, and so
{}^{\tilde{g}}\tilde{\chi}-\tilde{\chi}
factors through
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
as claimed. So
\iota
is well-defined.

The fact that

{}^{\tilde{g}\tilde{h}}\tilde{\chi}-\tilde{\chi}={}^{\tilde{g}}({}^{\tilde{h}}\tilde{\chi}-\tilde{\chi})+({}^{\tilde{g}}\tilde{\chi}-\tilde{\chi})=({}^{\tilde{h}}\tilde{\chi}-\tilde{\chi})+({}^{\tilde{g}}\tilde{\chi}-\tilde{\chi})

shows that
\iota
is a homomorphism.

Finally if
\iota(\chi)
is trivial, then
\tilde{g}
centralizes
\tilde{\chi}
and hence
g\in\Cent_{\Aut(x)}(\bar{k})
by Lemma
5.15
. This shows that
\iota
is injective.

(ii) If
\tilde{\chi}+\tau={}^{\tilde{g}}\tilde{\chi}
for some
\tilde{g}\in\Aut(x)(\bar{k})
with image
\bar{g}
in
\pi_{0}(\chi)(\bar{k})
, then
\tau=\iota(\bar{g})
, and conversely.

∎

This implies:

Lemma 5.17
.

In our parametrization of the isomorphism classes of
I_{\mu_{r}}B\uAut(x)(k_{n})
, each
\chi\colon\mu_{r}\to\uAut(x)
appears
ms/|\pi_{0}(\chi)|
times.

Proof.

As noted above, the only ambiguity in our parametrization comes from lifts
\tilde{\chi}^{\prime}
of
\chi
differing from a given lift
\tilde{\chi}
by a homomorphism
\tau\colon\mu_{r}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, for which the resulting element of
\Tr^{-1}_{m,s}(x)
is a cyclic shift of
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r})
, with the weights
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{r})
shifted accordingly and increased by the weight of
\tau
. This cyclic shift is equal to the original datum if and only if
\tilde{\chi}+\tau
is
\Aut(x)(\bar{k})
-conjugate to
\tilde{\chi}
. So by Lemma
5.16
each
\chi
appears in our parametrisation
ms/|\pi_{0}(\chi)|
times.
∎

The following describes the Hasse invariant in terms of our parametrisation:

Lemma 5.18
.

The Hasse invariant of the gerbe
\alpha
at
\chi
is given by
\alpha(\chi)_{n}=w_{\lambda}=\frac{d}{m}\in{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, with
\lambda
as in (
15
).

If
x=\oplus_{i}x_{i}
is the decomposition of
x
into indecomposables, where

has splitting field of degree
d_{i}
, then the order of
\alpha(\chi)_{n}
for any
\chi\colon\mu_{r}\to\uAut(x)
divides the gcd of the

.

Proof.

By definition, the element
\alpha(\chi)_{n}
is obtained by pulling back
{\mathbb{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
along
\chi\colon B_{k}\mu_{r}\to{\mathcal{M}}
, choosing a section
s\in\chi^{*}{\mathbb{M}}(B_{\bar{k}}\mu_{r})
and considering the object

\alpha(\chi)_{n}=\phi(s)-s\in H^{1}(B_{\bar{k}}\mu_{r},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\cong({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})[r].

But in our situation, such a section
s
is the same as a lift
\lambda\colon\mu_{r}\to\Aut(x)_{\bar{k}}
, and
\phi(s)-s
is given by the homomorphism
\lambda\colon\mu_{r}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
which corresponds to
w_{\lambda}=\frac{d}{m}
in
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The fact that the order of
\alpha(\chi)_{n}
has to divide each

follows from the definition of
w_{\lambda}
.
∎

The following describes the weight in terms of our parametrisation:

Lemma 5.19
.

Let
x=\Tr_{m,s}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{s})
be the decomposition corresponding to
\tilde{\chi}:\mu_{r}\to\uAut(x)
. Then we have

\mathbbm{w}(\chi)+\frac{1}{2}(x,x)=-\frac{m}{2}\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(y_{i},y_{i}).

Proof.

We work over
\bar{k}
since the weight is insensitive to base change. For every
0\leq i\leq\delta
we have

\Ext^{i}(x,x)=\ \bigoplus_{1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq s,1\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq m}\Ext^{i}(y_{i_{1}}^{\sigma^{j_{1}}},y_{i_{2}}^{\sigma^{j_{2}}}).

Now, the
\mu_{r}
-action on each
\Ext^{i}(y_{i}^{\sigma^{j}},y_{i}^{\sigma^{j}})
is trivial, while its weight on each
\Ext^{i}(y_{i_{1}}^{\sigma^{j_{1}}},y_{i_{2}}^{\sigma^{j_{2}}})
with
(i_{1},j_{1})\neq(i_{2},j_{2})
is exactly the negative of the one on
\Ext^{i}(y_{i_{2}}^{\sigma^{j_{2}}},y_{i_{1}}^{\sigma^{j_{1}}})
. Using that
(\cdot,\cdot)
is bilinear and by assumption also symmetric, we deduce

\mathbbm{w}(\chi)+\frac{1}{2}(x,x)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq i\leq s,1\leq j\leq m}(y_{i}^{\sigma^{j}},y_{i}^{\sigma^{j}})=-\frac{m}{2}\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(y_{i},y_{i}).

∎

Altogether we find the following formula for

\displaystyle(

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt(-1)^{(b_{1}+b_{2}m)\sum_{i=1}^{s}(y_{i},y_{i})}|\overline{\Delta_{m,s}[r]}|\frac{1}{ms}(-1)^{\frac{b_{1}(x,x)}{m^{2}}}e^{2\pi i\frac{d}{m}}\frac{({\mathbb{L}}_{n}-1){\mathbb{L}}_{nm}^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{s}(y_{i},y_{i})}}{\prod_{i=1}^{s}|\Aut(y_{i})(k_{nm)}|}

Here in the first equality we also used (

). Notice that the only dependence on

and hence the sum over all

Lemma 5.20
.

The series
\sum_{r\geq 1}|\overline{\Delta_{m,s}[r]}|T^{r}
is a rational function in

\mathsf{lim}_{T\to\infty}\sum_{r\geq 1}|\overline{\Delta_{m,s}[r]}|T^{r}=(-1)^{s-1}.

The class of an element

6.1
Integrals and intersection cohomology

Throughout this section we let
R
be a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}
-algebra and
T=\Spec(R)
.

We continue to consider an
R
-linear category
\mathcal{C}
as in Section
5.2
and assume further that
\mathcal{C}
is hereditary i.e.
\delta=1
. The vanishing of the higher

-groups implies in particular that

is smooth. We consider an open substack
{\mathbb{M}}\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{\mathcal{C}}
stable under extensions.

In order to apply our theory we need to assume that
{\mathbb{M}}
admits a reasonable notion of moduli space.

(i)

In the decomposition

{\mathbb{M}}=\sqcup_{\gamma\in\pi_{0}({\mathbb{M}})}{\mathbb{M}}_{\gamma},

each

is a finite type

-linear quotient stack.

(ii)

For each

\tilde{\pi}_{\gamma}\colon{\mathbb{M}}_{\gamma}\to\M_{\gamma}

We write
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{C}}=\sqcup_{\gamma\in\pi_{0}({\mathbb{M}})}{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}
for the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-rigidification along the scalar automorphisms and
\pi_{\gamma}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}\to\M_{\gamma}
for the induced morphisms. We write
(\gamma,\gamma)
for the value of the Euler pairing on

. By definition we have

. The set of connected components

inherits a commutative monoid structure from the direct sum operation
\oplus
.
For any
\phi:S\to T
we write
\pi_{\gamma,\phi}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma,\phi}\to\M_{\gamma,\phi}
for the base change to

.

In order to apply our integration theory we also need Conditions

from Section

to be verified. While

Fix a

(iv)

4.9
equips

.

Proposition 6.3

{\mathbb{M}}_{\gamma}
. Thus
\M_{\gamma}\cong{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}
is smooth and both sides of the equality are equal to
(-1)^{-(\gamma,\gamma)+1}
. For the left hand side this follows since
\CIC_{\M_{\gamma,\phi}}={\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[-(\gamma,\gamma)+1]
and for the right hand by definition (
18
).

In general, we show that
\Tr(\Fr,\CIC_{\M_{\gamma,\phi}})
satisfies the same defining equation (
18
) as
\mathcal{BPS}_{\gamma,\phi}
. Restricted to
\M_{\gamma,\phi}
this reads as

{\mathbb{L}}^{-(\gamma,\gamma)/2}\tilde{\pi}_{!}\frac{1}{|\Aut(\cdot)|}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{i},\gamma_{i}\\
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\gamma_{i}=\gamma\end{subarray}}\prod_{i}\Sym_{\alpha_{i}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{BPS}_{\gamma_{i}}}{{\mathbb{L}}^{1/2}-{\mathbb{L}}^{-1/2}}\right).

It is convenient rewrite this as follows:

\tilde{\pi}_{!}\frac{1}{|\Aut(\cdot)|}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{i},\gamma_{i}\\
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\gamma_{i}=\gamma\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{1}{2}\left((\gamma,\gamma)-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i})\right)}\prod_{i}\Sym_{\alpha_{i}}\left(\frac{{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i})-1}{2}}\mathcal{BPS}_{\gamma_{i},x}}{{\mathbb{L}}-1}\right).

(21)

Notice that
(\gamma,\gamma)-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i})
is even and thus by induction we deduce that
{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i})-1}{2}}\mathcal{BPS}_{\gamma_{i},\phi}
is a counting function not involving any roots of
{\mathbb{L}}
. This allows us to avoid discussing the spreading out of
{\mathbb{L}}^{1/2}
below.

For the class of the intersection complex in the Grothendieck ring of constructible
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaves we have an analogous formula to (
21
) over
{\mathbb{C}}
by
[
Mei15
, Theorem 5.6]
, see also
[
BDN+25
, Theorem 10.2.7]
:

\tilde{\pi}_{!}[{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell,{\mathbb{M}}_{\gamma,{\mathbb{C}}}}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{i},\gamma_{i}\\
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\gamma_{i}=\gamma\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{1}{2}\left((\gamma,\gamma)-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i})\right)}\prod_{i}\Sym_{\alpha_{i}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{-(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i})+1}{\mathbb{L}}^{(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i})-1}[\CIC_{\M_{\gamma_{i},{\mathbb{C}}}}]}{{\mathbb{L}}-1}\right).

(22)

One can further upgrade this equality to an isomorphism in the derived category
D^{\geq}(\M_{\gamma,{\mathbb{C}}},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
by an explicit isomorphism as in
[
DM20
, Theorem C]
. As will be explained below, this isomorphism is geometric in nature and thus spreads out over some open
T_{\gamma}\subset T
. The construction of the isomorphism in
loc. cit.
relies on the fact, that
\tilde{\pi}
can be approximated by proper maps and thus the decomposition theorem applies. Since furthermore
\tilde{\pi}
restricted to the open dense locus of stable sheaves is a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-gerbe, one obtains for every
\gamma
a morphism

\CIC_{\M_{\gamma,{\mathbb{C}}}}[(\gamma,\gamma)-1]\otimes H^{*}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\to\tilde{\pi}_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell,{\mathbb{M}}_{\gamma,{\mathbb{C}}}}.

Using the relative t-structure on
D^{\geq}(\M_{(\beta,\chi),T},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
constructed in
[
HS23
]
and in particular Theorem 1.10 in
loc. cit.
we can extend this morphism to

\CIC_{\M_{\gamma,T^{\prime}}}[(\gamma,\gamma)-1]\otimes H^{*}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\to\tilde{\pi}_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell,{\mathbb{M}}_{\gamma,T^{\prime}}},

for some dense open
T^{\prime}\subset T
. From there one may use the usual pull-push operations used in the construction of cohomological Hall algebras to obtain a morphism over
T^{\prime}
which is the aforementioned isomorphism over
{\mathbb{C}}
. Hence we deduce that we have an isomorphism over some
T_{\gamma}\subset T^{\prime}
and passing to the trace of Frobenius we deduce the proposition.
∎

Putting Lemma
6.2
and Proposition
6.3
together, we get Theorem
6.1
.

6.2
Moduli of sheaves on del Pezzo surfaces

In this final section we apply our integration theory to moduli of 1-dimensional sheaves on a del Pezzo surface. In particular we give a new proof of a theorem of Maulik-Shen
[
MS23
]
on
\chi
-independence of BPS-invariants.

Let
S_{{\mathbb{C}}}
be a complex del Pezzo surface,
\beta_{\mathbb{C}}
an ample, base-point free curve class on
S_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\chi\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We write
{\mathbb{M}}_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}}
for the moduli stack of pure
1
-dimensional Gieseker-semistable sheaves on
S
with support of class
\beta_{\mathbb{C}}
and Euler-characteristic
\chi
. Here, semistability is with respect to a fixed polarization
L_{\mathbb{C}}
and the slope function

\mu({\mathcal{F}})=\frac{\chi({\mathcal{F}})}{c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})\cdot L_{\mathbb{C}}}.

For
\tau\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, we also write
{\mathbb{M}}_{\tau,{\mathbb{C}}}
for the disjoint union of the moduli stacks
{\mathbb{M}}_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}}
with fixed slope
\tau
.

Choosing a spreading out
S
of
S_{\mathbb{C}}
over
T=\Spec(R)
for a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}
-algebra
R
, together with its polarization
L
and curve class
\beta
, we obtain a family
{\mathbb{M}}_{\tau}/T
and GIT-quotients
\tilde{\pi}_{\beta,\chi}:{\mathbb{M}}_{\beta,\chi}\to\M_{\beta,\chi}
satisfying conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
above, see for example
[
COW24
, Section 2.1]
.

Lemma 6.4
.

For any pair
(\beta,\chi)
the rigidification
\pi_{\beta,\chi}:{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,\chi}\to\M_{\beta,\chi}
is generically an equivalence. Furthermore for any non-archimedean local field
F
and any morphism
\phi:\Spec(\Oo_{F})\to T
the pullback
\pi_{\beta,\chi,\phi}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,\chi,\phi}\to\M_{\beta,\chi,\phi}
satisfies conditions
(iii)-(iv)
from Section
6.1
.

Proof.

By
[
COW24
, Proposition 2.2.1]
the complement of the open locus
U\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{\beta,\chi}
of line bundles supported on curves in
S
has codimension
\geq 2
. Since the canonical bundle restricted to
U
is Zariski-locally trivial, these local trivialisations extend to all of
{\mathbb{M}}_{\beta,\chi}
and thus
(iv)
is satisfied. Furthermore
\pi_{\beta,\chi}
is an equivalence if we further restrict to the locus of stable line-bundles.

It remains to prove the lifting property (iv). Fix
x\in X_{\beta,\chi}(\Oc_{F})^{\sharp}
. We may consider its generic fibre point
x_{F}
as a point in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,\chi}(F)
. The obstruction of lifting
x_{F}
to
{\mathbb{M}}_{\beta,\chi}
is given by the pullback of the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-gerbe
{\mathbb{M}}_{\beta,\chi}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta,\chi}
along
x_{F}
, which is an element in
\alpha_{x}\in H^{2}(F,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\cong{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
and thus in particular torsion. Thus, there exists
r\geq 1
such that
\alpha_{x}
extends to a gerbe
\beta
on
\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r}
. The element
x_{F}
corresponds to an
1\boxtimes\beta
-twisted sheaf
\F_{\eta}
on the generic fibre of
S\times_{\Oc_{F}}\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r}
. A similar argument to the one used in
[
Lie08
, Lemma 3.1.3.1]
shows that
\F_{\eta}
can be extended to a twisted coherent sheaf
\F
on
S\otimes_{\Oc_{F}}\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r}
(
loc. cit.
only considers twisted sheaves on schemes, but the extension to the tame stack appearing above is straightforward).

Pulling back along a cyclic extension
\Spec\Oc_{L}\to\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r}
which trivialises
\beta
we obtain a family of coherent sheaves
\F
on
S\times_{\Oc_{F}}\Oc_{L}
satisfying a twisted equivariance condition corresponding to the gerbe
\beta
. We apply Langton’s algorithm to this family to obtain a semistable extension satisfying the same equivariance condition, which amounts to a morphism
\mathbb{D}_{F}^{1/r}\to\mathcal{M}_{\beta,\chi}
extending
x_{F}
.
∎

We thus have established all properties of
{\mathbb{M}}_{\tau}\to T
in order to apply the results of Section
6.1
. First we deduce an arithmetic
\chi
-independence. For this consider the Hilbert-Chow morphism

h_{\beta,\chi}:\M_{\beta,\chi}\to B={\Pc}H^{0}(S,\CO_{S}(\beta)),

sending a
1
-dimensional sheaf to its fitting support. Then, we have the following:

Theorem 6.5
.

For any finite field
k
and any
\overline{\phi}:k\to T
the counting function
h_{\beta,\chi!}\mathcal{BPS}_{\beta,\chi,\overline{\phi}}
on
B_{\overline{\phi}}
is independent of
\chi
, i.e.,

h_{\beta,\chi!}\mathcal{BPS}_{\beta,\chi,\overline{\phi}}=h_{\beta,\chi^{\prime}!}\mathcal{BPS}_{\beta,\chi^{\prime},\overline{\phi}},

for any two
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

First we notice that for any
x\in{\mathbb{M}}_{\beta,\chi}

(x,x)=-\dim{\mathbb{M}}_{\beta,\chi}=-\beta^{2},

by
[
MS23
, Lemma 2.5]
, in particular the pairing is independent of
\chi
.

Now let
\Oo_{F}
be the ring of Witt-vectors of the finite field
k
. Then the quotient field
F
contains no
p
-th roots of
1
and
\overline{\phi}
lifts to a
\phi:\Oo_{F}\to T
. By Lemma
6.2
it is thus sufficient to show

\int_{h_{\beta,\chi,\phi}^{-1}B(x)}e^{2\pi i\tilde{\alpha}}\ d\mu_{orb}=\int_{h_{\beta,\chi^{\prime},\phi}^{-1}B(x)}e^{2\pi i\tilde{\alpha}}\ d\mu_{orb}.

Now,
[
COW24
, Theorem 1.2.2]
states that

\int_{h_{\beta,\chi,\phi}^{-1}B(x)}e^{2\pi ig_{\beta}\alpha}\ d\mu_{orb}

is independent of
\chi
, where
g_{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\beta(\beta+K_{S})+1
is the arithmetic genus of a curve in the linear system
|\beta|
.

We claim that
e^{2\pi ig_{\beta}\alpha(x)_{n}}
and
e^{2\pi i\tilde{\alpha}(x)_{n}}
differ by the constant factor
(-1)^{\beta^{2}}
, from which the theorem follows.

To see this, write
\beta=r\cdot\gamma
for
r\geq 1
and
\gamma
a reduced curve class. Then, the order of
\alpha
restricted to
M_{\beta,\chi}
divides
r
by Lemma
5.18
. In particular we have for any
y\in h_{\beta,\chi}^{-1}B(x)

e^{2\pi ig_{\beta}\alpha(y)_{n}}=e^{2\pi i\alpha}e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{1}{2}r\gamma(r\gamma+K_{S})\alpha(y)_{n}\right)}=e^{2\pi i\alpha}e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{1}{2}r(r-1)\gamma^{2}\alpha(y)_{n}\right)}=(-1)^{\frac{r(r-1)\gamma^{2}}{o_{\alpha}(y)_{n}}}e^{2\pi i\alpha(y)_{n}}.

For the second equality we used
\frac{1}{2}\gamma(\gamma+K_{S})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
r\alpha(y)_{n}=0\in{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.
On the other hand by (
12
)

e^{2\pi i\tilde{\alpha}(y)_{n}}=(-1)^{-\frac{r^{2}\gamma^{2}}{o_{\alpha}(y)_{n}^{2}}}e^{2\pi i\alpha(y)_{n}}=(-1)^{-\frac{r}{o_{\alpha}(y)_{n}}\gamma^{2}}e^{2\pi i\alpha(y)_{n}}

Therefore,
e^{2\pi ig_{\beta}\alpha(y)_{n}}
and
e^{2\pi i\tilde{\alpha}(y)_{n}}
differ by the factor
(-1)^{\frac{r^{2}\gamma^{2}}{o_{\alpha}(y)}}=(-1)^{\beta^{2}}
as claimed.
∎

Finally, we use our arithmetic methods to recover the
\chi
-independence of sheaf-theoretic BPS-invariants for del Pezzo surfaces proven in
[
MS23
]
. It should be noted, however, that the original argument yields an isomorphism compatible with perverse and Hodge filtrations at the same time. To the best of our knowledge, this cannot be obtained from point-counting methods, the principal obstructions being that there is currently no analogue of
p
-adic Hodge theory for intersection cohomology.

Theorem 6.6
.

[
MS23
, Theorem 0.1]
For
k={\mathbb{C}}
and
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
there exists an isomorphism of graded vector spaces between the intersection cohomology groups

IH^{*}(\M_{\beta,\chi})\cong IH^{*}(\M_{\beta,\chi^{\prime}}),

respecting the perverse filtrations. There also exists a possibly different isomorphism
IH^{*}(\M_{\beta,\chi})\cong IH^{*}(\M_{\beta,\chi^{\prime}})
respecting the Hodge filtrations.

Proof.

We will infer first the statement from Theorem
6.5
and Proposition
6.3
using the function-sheaf dictionary and Chebotarev Density. Choosing a spreading out over
T
as above, we see from Proposition
6.3
that there exists a
T_{\beta,\chi}\subset T
such that for every morphism
\phi:\Spec(k)\to T_{\beta,\chi}
we have

\Tr(\Fr,\CIC_{\M_{\beta,\chi,\phi}})=(-1)^{\beta^{2}+1}{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{\beta^{2}+1}{2}}\mathcal{BPS}_{\beta,\chi,\phi},

and similarly for
\chi^{\prime}
.

Now, purity of the complex of lisse sheaves
\CIC_{\M_{\beta,\chi,\phi}}
is shown in
[
BBDG18
, Corollaire 5.3.4]
. Applying Deligne’s
[
Del80
, Proposition 6.2.6]
to this pure complex we obtain purity of the derived pushforward of intersection cohomology
R(h_{\beta,\chi})_{*}\CIC_{\M_{\beta,\chi,\phi}}
and similarly for
\chi^{\prime}
. Furthermore, we know from the Decomposition Theorem that the derived pushforward is semi-simple.
The functions associated to these derived pushforwards agree by virtue of
\chi
-independence of
h_{\beta,\chi!}\mathcal{BPS}_{\beta,\chi,\phi}
, Theorem
6.5
.

From Chebotarev’s Density Theorem we infer that the isomorphism class of
R(h_{\beta,\chi})_{*}\CIC_{\M_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}}}
in the derived category is independent of
\chi
. In particular, there exists an isomorphism in the derived category

R(h_{\beta,\chi})_{*}\CIC_{\M_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}}}\simeq R(h_{\beta,\chi^{\prime}})_{*}\CIC_{\M_{\beta,\chi^{\prime},{\mathbb{C}}}}.

Applying the perverse truncation functors and derived global sections we obtain an isomorphism

\displaystyle\image\big({\mathbb{H}}^{*}(B,\tau^{p}_{\leq i}R(h_{\beta,\chi})_{*}\CIC_{\M_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}}})\to{\mathbb{H}}^{*}(B,R(h_{\beta,\chi})_{*}\CIC_{\M_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}}})\big)\cong

\displaystyle\cong\image\big({\mathbb{H}}^{*}(B,\tau^{p}_{\leq i}R(h_{\beta,\chi^{\prime}})_{*}\CIC_{\M_{\beta,\chi^{\prime},{\mathbb{C}}}})\to{\mathbb{H}}^{*}(B,R(h_{\beta,\chi^{\prime}})_{*}\CIC_{\M_{\beta,\chi^{\prime},{\mathbb{C}}}})\big).

In particular, there exists an isomorphism of intersection cohomology
IH^{*}(\M_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}})\cong IH^{*}(\M_{\beta,\chi^{\prime},{\mathbb{C}}})

respecting the perverse filtration.

To obtain the statement about the Hodge filtration we argue in a similar way. By purity it is enough to show that the weight-polynomials of the Hodge structures
IH^{*}(\M_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}})
and
IH^{*}(\M_{\beta,\chi^{\prime},{\mathbb{C}}})
agree. By
[
Mei15
, Theorem 5.6]
these agree up to a prefactor with the weight (or E-) polynomial of the motivic classes
s_{\chi!}\mathcal{BPS}_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}}
and
s_{\chi^{\prime}!}\mathcal{BPS}_{\beta,\chi^{\prime},{\mathbb{C}}}
, where
s_{\chi!}
and
s_{\chi^{\prime}!}
denote the structure morphisms to
\Spec({\mathbb{C}})
. Their equality now follows from Katz’s theorem
[
HRV08
, Theorem 6.1.2]
together with the
\chi
-independence of the counting functions, Theorem
6.5
. Here we may argue as in the proof of Proposition
6.3
to see that
\mathcal{BPS}_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}}
is in the image of

K_{0}(\Var/\M_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}})[{\mathbb{L}}^{-1},({\mathbb{L}}^{N}-1)^{-1}:N\geq 1]\to K_{0}(\Var/\M_{\beta,\chi,{\mathbb{C}}})[{\mathbb{L}}^{-1/2},({\mathbb{L}}^{N}-1)^{-1}:N\geq 1],

to avoid discussing an extension of Katz’s theorem to fractional powers of
{\mathbb{L}}
.
∎

As in
[
MS23
]
one can obtain an analogous result for meromorphic Higgs bundles.

Remark 6.7
.

The results of of Section
6.1
also apply to other moduli problems, for example to moduli of semi-stable vector bundles of fixed slope on a smooth projective curve. In particular if
\M_{2,0}(C)
denotes the moduli space of semi-stable rank
2
, degree
0
vector bundles on a genus
g
curve
C
it was already observed in
[
KL04
, Remark 6.2]
, that the stringy
E
-polynomial of
\M_{2,0}
agrees with its intersection
E
-polynomial if
g
is even. Now, the
p
-adic analogue of the stringy
E
-polynomial of a variety is its volume and thus this matches our computations as
\dim{\mathbb{M}}_{2,0}=4(g-1)
, from which one can deduce that
e^{2\pi i\tilde{\alpha}}\equiv 1
on
\M_{2,0}
. If
g
is odd,
e^{2\pi i\tilde{\alpha}}
is non-constant and indeed the authors in
loc. cit.
also observe that in this case stringy and intersection
E
-polynomial are different.

Six-Functor Formalisms II : The ∞ \infty -categorical compactification.
Chirantan Chowdhury

Abstract
This paper is part of a series of articles in which we reproduce the statements regarding the abstract six-functor formalism developed by Liu-Zheng. In this paper, we prove a theorem, which is an ∞ \infty -categorical version for defining the exceptional pushforward functor in an abstract-six functor formalism. The article describes specific combinatorial simplicial sets related to compactifications and pullback squares. This theorem plays a key role in constructing the abstract six-functor formalism, which will be discussed in the forthcoming article.

3.1
The global section functor.

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a (small) ordinary category. Let
(\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta})^{\mathcal{J}}
be the category where objects are functors from
\mathcal{J}\to\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta}
and morphisms are natural transformations.

We now introduce the constant and global section functor related to
(\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta})^{\mathcal{J}}
.

Notation 3.1.2
.

For every simplicial set
X
, we have the constant simplicial set functor
c(X):=X_{\mathcal{J}}
defined by sending any object
j
to the simplicial set
X
. The association is functorial and thus we have a functor :

c:\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta}\to(\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta})^{\mathcal{J}}

Definition 3.1.3
.

We define the
global section functor

\Gamma:(\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta})^{\mathcal{J}}\to\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta}

as follows :

\Gamma(F)=(\Gamma(F)_{n}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{(\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta})^{\mathcal{J}})}(\Delta^{n}_{\mathcal{J}},F))_{n}.

Example 3.1.4
.

Let
F
be the constant functor
c(X)
where
X\in\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta}
. Let us compute
\Gamma(F)
.
The
n
-simplices of
\Gamma(F)
are given by the set of natural transformations from
\Delta^{n}_{J}\to c(X)
. Every such natural transformation is equivalent to give a single map
\Delta^{n}\to X
. In particular the
n
-simplices of
\Gamma(F)
are given by
n
-simplices of
X
.Thus
\Gamma(F)=X
. In particular, we prove that
\Gamma\circ c=\operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta}}
.

Remark 3.1.5
.

Recall from classical category theory, given a complete category
\mathcal{C}
and an small category
I
, we have the pair of adjoint functors:

c:\mathcal{C}\leftrightarrows\operatorname{Fun}(I,\mathcal{C}):\operatorname{lim}

where
\operatorname{lim}
is the functor which takes an object which is a functor
F:I\to\mathcal{C}
to its limit
\operatorname{lim}(F)\in\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta}
and
I=\mathcal{J}
. As the category of simplicial sets is complete, we see that

\Gamma=\operatorname{lim}.

In the other words, the global section functor is the limit functor which takes every functor to its limit in the category of simplicial sets.

Definition 5.1.1

Let

in

Notation 5.1.2

For any partially ordered set, we denote the products (infima) by
\wedge

\vee
. In
\mathcal{U}(P)

There is a canonical order preserving map

defined by

There are special squares one considers in a partially ordered set, namely exact squares.

Definition 5.1.3
.

A square in a partially ordered set is an

if it is both pushout and a pullback square.

We shall state two important property of exact squares :

Lemma 5.1.4
.

Every morphisms
Q\to Q^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{U}(P)
is a composition of finite sequence of exact pullbacks of morphisms
\sigma^{P}(x)\to\sigma^{P}(x)-x
where

.

Proof.

We consider a finite chain of compositions
Q\to Q_{1}\to Q_{2}\cdots Q_{i}=Q^{\prime}
where
Q_{j}=Q_{j+1}\cup\{x_{j}\}
. Notice that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q_{j}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{P}(x_{j})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q_{j+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{P}(x_{j})-\{x_{j}\}}

(70)

is both a pullback square by ordering of
\{x_{i}\}
and pushout square also by the ordering.
∎

Lemma 5.1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\infty
-category and

be a functor. Then if

is a right Kan extension along

Proof.

Consider the exact square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q\cup Q^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q\cap Q^{\prime}}

is a Kan extension along

is the limit over

. Consider

. We have

is a RKE along

Our aim is to show that

is the limit of the diagram

given by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q\cap Q^{\prime}.}

As final maps preserve limits (

Appendix B
Existence of finite limits in overcategories.

Lemma B.0.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\infty
-category and

be a point in
\mathcal{C}
. Let

be a weakly contractible simplicial set. Then a morphism

admits a limit iff

admits a limit.

Proof.

We use the fact that for any simplicial set

, the inclusion map

is left anodyne. This is essentially
[
8
, Lemma 4.2.3.6]
.

1.

Suppose the map

admits a limit. Let

be the corresponding limit diagram. We shall show that the composition :

\tilde{p^{\prime}}:B^{\triangleleft}\to\mathcal{C}_{/c}\to\mathcal{C}

(120)

is a limit diagram of

. We need to show for all

, there exists a solution of the lifting problem:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}\boldsymbol{*}B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}\boldsymbol{*}B}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{n}^{\prime}}

(121)

where

. As

lifts to

(via the point

).
Using the fact that
B\to B^{\triangleleft}
is inner anodyne and applying it in
[
8
, Lemma 2.1.2.3]
, we have a solution to the lifting problem :

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}\boldsymbol{*}B\coprod_{[n]\boldsymbol{*}B}[n]\boldsymbol{*}B^{\triangleright}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}\boldsymbol{*}B^{\triangleright}}

(122)

Note that this allows us to see

. Then by property of

2.

admits a limit diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}\boldsymbol{*}B}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{n}^{\prime}}

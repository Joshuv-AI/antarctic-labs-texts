Capillary L p L_{p} Minkowski Flows
Jinrong Hu, Yingxiang Hu, Mohammad N. Ivaki

Abstract
. We study the long-time existence and asymptotic behavior of a class of anisotropic capillary Gauss curvature flows. As an application, we provide a flow approach to the existence of smooth solutions to the capillary even L p L_{p} Minkowski problem in the Euclidean half-space for all p ∈ ( − n − 1 , ∞ ) p\in(-n-1,\infty) and capillary L p L_{p} Minkowski problem for p > n + 1 p>n+1 .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
53C21, 35J66, 35K55, 52A20

1.
Introduction

The Minkowski problem, a fundamental problem in the Brunn-Minkowski theory of convex geometry, seeks to reconstruct a convex body from its surface area measure. It asks whether a given positive Borel measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
arises as the surface area measure of a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and if so, whether this convex body is uniquely determined (up to translation). This problem was solved by Minkowski for polytopes (see
[
Min97
,
Min03
]
) and subsequently by Aleksandrov
[
Ale38
,
Ale39
]
, Nirenberg
[
Nir53
]
, Pogorelov
[
Pog78
]
, Cheng-Yau
[
CY76
]
, and others.

The
L_{p}
Minkowski problem, which concerns the prescription of the
L_{p}
surface area measure, was first introduced by Lutwak
[
Lut93
]
and solved by Lutwak and Oliker
[
LO95
]
for the even regular case when
p>1
. Building on Lutwak’s foundational work, the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem has seen significant progress; see, e.g.,
[
ACW01
,
Sta02
,
LYZ04
,
CW06
,
BLYZ13
,
HL13
,
LW13
,
Zhu15a
,
Zhu15b
,
BBCY19
,
GLW22
]
and the references therein.

With the development of the classical and
L_{p}
Minkowski problems, analogous questions have naturally arisen in the geometric study of capillary hypersurfaces which are discussed in detail below.

Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
denote the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and define the Euclidean half-space as

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:y\cdot E_{n+1}>0\}.

Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
be a smooth, properly embedded, compact hypersurface with boundary
\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. We say that
\Sigma
is a capillary hypersurface with constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
if it satisfies

(1.2)

\displaystyle\nu\cdot e=\cos(\pi-\theta)\quad\text{along $\partial\Sigma$},

where
e:=-E_{n+1}
and
\nu
denotes the outer unit normal of
\Sigma
in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. We call
\widehat{\Sigma}
a
capillary convex body
if
\widehat{\Sigma}
is a bounded, closed region in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
enclosed by a strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
and the support hyperplane
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}=\mathcal{C}_{\theta,1}
be the capillary spherical cap of radius
1
intersecting the support hyperplane
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
at a constant angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, which is defined as (
2.2
).

Let
\tilde{\nu}:\Sigma\to\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
denote the capillary Gauss map of
\Sigma
, given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}:=\nu+\cos\theta\,e,

which is a diffeomorphism. The capillary area measure of
\Sigma
is defined as

(1.4)

\displaystyle S^{c}(\eta)=\int_{\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\eta)}\big(1+\cos\theta\,(\nu\cdot e)\big)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}

for all Borel sets
\eta\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, where
\tilde{\nu}^{-1}:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\Sigma
is the inverse capillary Gauss map, and
\mathcal{H}^{n}
denotes the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. The capillary area measure is a capillary counterpart (or Robin boundary analogue) of the surface area measure. By this diffeomorphism,
S^{c}(\eta)
has the equivalent form

(1.5)

\displaystyle S^{c}(\eta)=\int_{\eta}\frac{\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\,(\xi\cdot e)}{\mathcal{K}(\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\xi))}\,d\xi,

where
\mathcal{K}
is the Gauss curvature of
\Sigma
and
d\xi
is the area element of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Put

(1.6)

\displaystyle\ell(\xi)=\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\,(\xi\cdot e).

Then
\ell
is the capillary support function of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
(see (
2.5
) below).

Let
f:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to(0,\infty)
be a smooth function. The
regular capillary Minkowski problem
asks for the existence of a solution
h:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
(which serves as the capillary support function of a strictly convex capillary hypersurface; see
section 2
for more details) to the following Monge-Ampère type equation with a Robin boundary condition:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)&=f,&\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\\
\nabla_{\mu}h&=\cot\theta\,h,&\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
\nabla h
and
\nabla^{2}h
denote the gradient and the Hessian of
h
with respect to a local orthonormal frame on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, and
\mu
is the unit outward normal of
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

The necessary and sufficient condition for solving the capillary Minkowski problem when
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
was established in
[
MWW25a
]
via the continuity method. The case
\theta>\frac{\pi}{2}
remains open.

The capillary
L_{p}
-surface area measure
dS^{c}_{p}
for a capillary convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}
in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
is defined by

(1.8)

\displaystyle dS^{c}_{p}=\frac{\ell\,h^{1-p}}{\mathcal{K}}\,d\xi.

The
regular capillary
L_{p}
Minkowski problem
amounts to solving the following Monge-Ampère type equation with a Robin boundary condition:

(1.9)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)&=fh^{p-1},&\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\\
\nabla_{\mu}h&=\cot\theta\,h,&\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

We say that a smooth function
f:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
is
even
if

(1.10)

\displaystyle f(-\xi_{1},\ldots,-\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1})=f(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1}),\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

A strictly convex capillary hypersurface is called even if its capillary support function is even.

For the regular capillary
L_{p}
Minkowski problem with
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, Mei, Wang, and Weng solved the case
p=1
in
[
MWW25a
]
, the even case (i.e. when
f
is even) for
1<p<n+1
, and the case for
p\geq n+1
in
[
MWW25b
]
. Y. Hu and Ivaki
[
HI25b
]
solved the even case for
-n-1<p<1
using an iterative scheme; see also
[
Iva20
]
.

In comparison with the methods in
[
HI25b
,
MWW25a
,
MWW25b
]
, we here study the capillary
L_{p}
Minkowski problem for
p\in(-n-1,\infty)
through anisotropic capillary Gauss curvature flows (see (
1.14
), (
1.16
)). In particular, we solve the capillary even
L_{p}
Minkowski problem for
all

p>-n-1
, and the non-even case for
p>n+1
. It would be interesting to further solve the non-even case for
p=1
and
p=n+1
using geometric flows.

Geometric flows have proven to be an efficient tool in the study of Minkowski-type problems. For example, the logarithmic Gauss curvature flow
[
CW00
]
and its
L_{p}
analogues, such as those in
[
BIS19
,
LSW20
,
CL21
,
GLW22
,
BG25
]
, have been instrumental in analyzing both the classical and
L_{p}
settings. The flows (
1.14
), (
1.16
), introduced shortly, may be viewed as capillary analogues of the
L_{p}
Minkowski flows subject to Robin boundary conditions. Recently, an isotropic capillary Gauss curvature flow was considered by Mei, Wang, and Weng
[
MWW25c
]
, which is a capillary counterpart of Firey’s variational worn stone; see Firey
[
Fir74
]
, Chou
[
Cho85
]
, Andrews
[
And99
]
, and Guan and Ni
[
GN17
]
. While the classification of solitons has been settled for the classical Gauss curvature flow, it remains open in the capillary case; see
[
Gag84
,
And99
,
BCD17
,
Sar22
,
IM23
,
HI25a
]
. See also for additional work in the capillary framework
[
HWYZ24
,
WWX24
,
KLS25
,
MWWX25
]
.

Let
Y_{0}:\mathcal{M}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
be an embedding of an even, smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\mathcal{M}_{0}=Y_{0}(\mathcal{M})
. Consider a family of even, smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurfaces
\mathcal{M}_{t}:=Y(\mathcal{M},t)
satisfying

(1.11)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}Y&=-\frac{f\circ\tilde{\nu}\,(Y\cdot\nu)^{p}}{1+\cos\theta\,(\nu\cdot e)}\mathcal{K}\tilde{\nu},&\quad\text{in $\mathcal{M}\times[0,\mathcal{T})$},\\
\tilde{\nu}\cdot e&=0,&\quad\text{on $\partial\mathcal{M}\times[0,\mathcal{T})$},\\
Y(\cdot,0)&=Y_{0}(\cdot),&\quad\text{in $\mathcal{M}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

The rescaled solution

(1.12)

X(\xi,\tau(t)):=\left(\frac{V(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{0})}{V(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{t})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}Y(\xi,t)

with the new time parameter defined via

(1.13)

\frac{d\tau}{dt}=-\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{d}{dt}\log V(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{t}),\quad\tau(0)=0,

satisfies the following equations

(1.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{\tau}X&=-\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\mathcal{M}_{0}})}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}\,\frac{f\circ\tilde{\nu}\,(X\cdot\nu)^{p}}{1+\cos\theta\,(\nu\cdot e)}\mathcal{K}\tilde{\nu}+X,&\text{in }\mathcal{M}\times[0,\infty),\\
\tilde{\nu}\cdot e&=0,&\text{on }\partial\mathcal{M}\times[0,\infty),\\
X(\cdot,0)&=X_{0}(\cdot),&\text{in }\mathcal{M}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
\Sigma_{\tau}:=X(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Note that
\widehat{\Sigma_{0}}=\widehat{\mathcal{M}_{0}}
and
V(\widehat{\Sigma_{\tau}})=V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})
for all
\tau>0
. We differentiate the two flows using distinct time parameters:
t
is used for the shrinking flow, while
\tau
is used for the volume-normalized flow. The asymptotic behavior of the rescaled solution is captured in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>-n-1
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Let
\Sigma_{0}
be an even, smooth, and strictly convex capillary hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. Let
f
be an even, smooth, positive function on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Then there exists an even, smooth, and strictly convex solution
\Sigma_{\tau}
to the flow (
1.14
) for all
\tau>0
. Moreover, a subsequence of
\{\Sigma_{\tau}\}
converges in
C^{\infty}
to an even, smooth, and strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
satisfying

(1.15)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)&=\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}fh^{p-1},&\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\\
\nabla_{\mu}h&=\cot\theta\,h,&\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

Compared with the elliptic approach of
[
MWW25a
,
MWW25b
]
(see also
[
LTU86
,
MQ19
]
), where the global
C^{2}
estimate is reduced to the boundary double normal estimate and then derived via the construction of a suitable test function, we obtain the
C^{2}
estimate here in a much simpler and more direct way.

The evenness restriction in
1.1
is only used to establish the
C^{0}
estimate. In particular, if
p>n+1
, this assumption can be removed by employing the following anisotropic Gauss curvature flow

(1.16)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{\tau}X&=-\frac{f\circ\tilde{\nu}\,(X\cdot\nu)^{p}}{1+\cos\theta\,(\nu\cdot e)}\mathcal{K}\,\tilde{\nu}+X,&\quad\text{in $\mathcal{M}\times[0,\infty)$},\\
\tilde{\nu}\cdot e&=0,&\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{M}\times[0,\infty),\\
X(\cdot,0)&=X_{0}(\cdot),&\quad\text{in $\mathcal{M}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Compared to the flow (
1.14
), we dropped the factor
\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}
from the speed of the flow; see also
[
BIS21a
,
BIS21b
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p>n+1
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Let
\Sigma_{0}
be a smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
with positive capillary support function. Let
f
be a smooth, positive function on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Then there exists a smooth, strictly convex solution
\Sigma_{\tau}
to the flow (
1.16
) for all
\tau>0
, and a subsequence of
\{\Sigma_{\tau}\}
converges in
C^{\infty}
to a smooth and strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
satisfying (
1.9
).

The structure of this paper is as follows. In
section 2
, we recall some basic facts about capillary hypersurfaces in the Euclidean half-space. In
section 3
, we prove monotonicity formulas for the flows (
1.14
), (
1.16
). The uniform
C^{k}
estimates for the flows (
1.14
), (
1.16
) are established in
section 4
. For the derivation of the
C^{2}
estimates along our flows, to circumvent the extra difficulties arising from the boundary, we first derive both lower and upper bounds on the Gauss curvature and then the upper bound on the principal radii of curvature. In
section 5
, we provide the proofs of
1.1
and
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a compact orientable smooth manifold of dimension
n
with boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}
and let
X:\mathcal{M}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
be a properly embedded smooth hypersurface, i.e.,

(2.1)

\displaystyle X(\operatorname{int}\mathcal{M})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}\quad\text{and}\quad X(\partial\mathcal{M})\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}.

We further assume that
\Sigma=X(\mathcal{M})
is strictly convex, meaning that
\Sigma
together with
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
bounds a convex body and that
\Sigma
has a positive definite second fundamental form. Set
\partial\Sigma=X(\partial\mathcal{M})
.
Note that
\partial\Sigma
is a closed, strictly convex hypersurface in
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
(see, e.g.,
[
WWX24
, Cor. 2.5]
). Write
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
for the enclosed region by
\partial\Sigma
in
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
.

For any
r>0
, the capillary spherical cap of radius
r
intersecting the support hyperplane
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
at a constant angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
is defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}=\{\zeta\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}:|\zeta-r\cos\theta\,e|=r\}.

Let us take
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{C}_{\theta}=\mathcal{C}_{\theta,1}
and
X=\tilde{\nu}^{-1}
. Given a strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
, the capillary support function of
\Sigma
,
h=h_{\Sigma}:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
,
is defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle h(\xi):=X(\xi)\cdot\nu\big(X(\xi)\big)=\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\xi)\cdot(\xi-\cos\theta\,e),\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Then
h
satisfies the capillary boundary condition (cf.
[
MWWX25
, Lem. 2.4]
)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}h=\cot\theta\,h,\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

When
\Sigma=\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we have
X(\xi)=\xi
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
and

(2.5)

\displaystyle h_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\xi\cdot(\xi-\cos\theta\,e)=1+\cos\theta\,(\nu\cdot e)=\ell(\xi).

We write

\hat{h}=\hat{h}_{\widehat{\Sigma}}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

for the (standard) support function of the convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}
, defined by

(2.6)

\displaystyle\hat{h}(u):=\max_{x\in\widehat{\Sigma}}u\cdot x,\quad\forall\,u\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

In particular, we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle h(\xi)=\hat{h}(\xi-\cos\theta\,e),\quad\forall\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

In a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
(with respect to the standard metric of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
), the second fundamental form
b_{ij}
of
\Sigma
is given by

(2.8)

\displaystyle b_{ij}=\nabla_{ij}^{2}h+h\,\delta_{ij},

and the Gauss curvature of
\Sigma
at
X(\xi)
is

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\big(X(\xi)\big)=\frac{1}{\det\big(\nabla^{2}h(\xi)+h(\xi)I\big)}.

It is often more convenient to consider
\mathcal{K}
as a function on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

For our later purpose, set

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{\theta}=\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:y_{n+1}\geq\cos\theta\}.

We define the map
T:\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\to\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
by

(2.11)

\displaystyle T(u)=u+\cos\theta\,e.

3.
Monotone Quantities

Recall that
\tilde{\nu}=\nu+\cos\theta\,e
,
and hence

(3.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}\cdot\nu=1+\cos\theta\,(\nu\cdot e).

Let
\Sigma_{\tau}
be the solution to (
1.14
).
Then the capillary support function
h(\cdot,\tau)
of
\Sigma_{\tau}
satisfies the following equations:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{\tau}h&=-\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}fh^{p}\mathcal{K}+h,&\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,\infty),\\
\nabla_{\mu}h&=\cot\theta\,h,&\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,\infty),\\
h(\xi,0)&=h_{0}(\xi),&\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

For the flow (
3.2
), we define the functional

(3.3)

\displaystyle J(\tau)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\frac{1}{n+1}\log V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})+\frac{1}{p}\log\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)h(\xi,\tau)^{p}\,d\xi,&\quad p\neq 0,\\
&-\frac{1}{n+1}\log V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})+\frac{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)\log h(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)\,d\xi},&\quad p=0.\end{aligned}\right.

We show that
J(\tau)
is non-increasing.

Lemma 3.1
.

The functional
J(\tau)
is non-increasing along the flow (
1.14
) and equality holds if and only if
\Sigma_{\tau}
satisfies (
1.15
).

Proof.

For
\tau>0
, the derivative
\partial_{\tau}h
is a capillary function.
By definition,
V(\widehat{\Sigma_{\tau}})
is constant in time.
Hence, by
[
MWWX25
, Prop. 2.9]
, we obtain

(3.4)

0=\frac{d}{d\tau}V(\widehat{\Sigma_{\tau}})=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\partial_{\tau}h}{\mathcal{K}}\,d\xi.

For
p\neq 0
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}J(\tau)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\partial_{\tau}h}{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})\mathcal{K}}\,d\xi+\frac{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p-1}\partial_{\tau}h\,d\xi}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\left(-h+\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})fh^{p}\mathcal{K}}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}\right)^{2}}{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})h\mathcal{K}}\,d\xi\leq 0.

Similarly, for
p=0
, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}J(\tau)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\partial_{\tau}h}{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})\mathcal{K}}\,d\xi+\frac{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{f}{h}\partial_{\tau}h\,d\xi}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f\,d\xi}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\left(-h+\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})f\mathcal{K}}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f\,d\xi}\right)^{2}}{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})h\mathcal{K}}\,d\xi\leq 0.

For each
p
,
\frac{d}{d\tau}J(\tau)=0
if and only if

(3.7)

\displaystyle h^{1-p}\det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)=\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})f}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}.

∎

Now we turn to the flow (
1.16
). Along this flow, the capillary support function
h(\cdot,\tau)
of
\Sigma_{\tau}
solves

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{\tau}h&=-fh^{p}\mathcal{K}+h,&\quad\text{in $\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,\infty)$},\\
\nabla_{\mu}h&=\cot\theta\,h,&\quad\text{on $\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,\infty)$},\\
h(\cdot,0)&=h_{0}(\cdot),&\quad\text{in $\mathcal{C}_{\theta}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

We define the functional

(3.9)

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}(\tau)=-V(\widehat{\Sigma_{\tau}})+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)h(\xi,\tau)^{p}d\xi,

and we show that
\widetilde{J}(\tau)
is non-increasing along the flow (
1.16
).

Lemma 3.2
.

The functional
\widetilde{J}(\tau)
is non-increasing along the flow (
1.16
) and equality holds if and only if
\Sigma_{\tau}
satisfies (
1.9
).

Proof.

We have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\widetilde{J}(\tau)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\partial_{\tau}h}{\mathcal{K}}\,d\xi+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p-1}\partial_{\tau}h\,d\xi

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\left(-h+fh^{p}\mathcal{K}\right)^{2}}{h\mathcal{K}}\,d\xi\leq 0.

It is clear that
\frac{d}{d\tau}\widetilde{J}(\tau)=0
if and only if
h^{1-p}\det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)=f
.
∎

4.
Regularity Estimates

4.1.
Regularity Estimates I

In this subsection, we deduce the a priori estimates for solutions to the flow (
1.14
). First, we establish both the uniform lower and upper bounds of the capillary support function.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p>-n-1
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Suppose
\Sigma_{\tau}
is a smooth, even, and strictly convex solution to the flow (
1.14
). Then there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
n,p
,
\theta,\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f,\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f
and
\Sigma_{0}
, such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\leq h(\xi,\tau)\leq C,\quad\forall(\xi,\tau)\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times(0,\infty).

Proof.

Case 1:

-(n+1)<p<0
. From
3.1
, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\log V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})-\frac{n+1}{p}\log\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)h(\xi,\tau)^{p}\,d\xi\geq-(n+1)J(0).

It follows that

(4.3)

\displaystyle V(\widehat{\Sigma_{\tau}})\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)h(\xi,\tau)^{p}\,d\xi\right)^{-\frac{n+1}{p}}\geq e^{-(n+1)J(0)}.

Let
\Xi_{\tau}
be the convex body enclosed by
\Sigma_{\tau}
and its reflection across
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. Define the function
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to(0,\infty)
by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\ }l}f\circ T(u),&\quad u_{n+1}\geq\cos\theta,\\
f\circ T(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n},-u_{n+1}),&\quad u_{n+1}\leq-\cos\theta,\\
f\circ T(\sin\theta\,\tilde{u}+\cos\theta\,E_{n+1}),&\quad-\cos\theta\leq u_{n+1}\leq\cos\theta,\end{array}\right.

where
\tilde{u}:=\dfrac{u-(u\cdot e)e}{|u-(u\cdot e)e|}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap e^{\bot}
and the map
T
is given by (
2.11
). Note that
\tilde{f}
is the symmetric extension of
f
from
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
to the whole
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Note that
\hat{h}_{\Xi_{\tau}}(u)=h_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\circ T(u)
for
u\in\mathbb{S}_{\theta}
, where
\mathbb{S}_{\theta}
is defined in (
2.10
). By (
4.3
) we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle V(\Xi_{\tau})\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\tilde{f}\hat{h}^{p}_{\Xi_{\tau}}\,du\right)^{-\frac{n+1}{p}}\geq e^{-(n+1)J(0)}.

In view of
[
HI25b
, Lem. 2.6]
, the capillary support function is uniformly bounded above and below away from zero.

For the remaining cases, with the volume fixed, it is sufficient to prove the uniform upper bound on the capillary support function. Given that the origin lies in the interior of the flat side
\widehat{\partial\Sigma_{\tau}}\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
, there exist
R_{\tau}>0
and
q_{\tau}\in\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}
such that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R_{\tau}}\widehat{\Sigma_{\tau}}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}},\quad q_{\tau}\in\frac{1}{R_{\tau}}\Sigma_{\tau}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Rotating the coordinates around the
E_{n+1}
-axis, one may assume that

(4.7)

\displaystyle q_{\tau}=(q_{1},0,\ldots,0,q_{n+1})\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\quad 0\leq q_{n+1}\leq 1-\cos\theta,

where
q_{1}=\sqrt{1-(q_{n+1}+\cos\theta)^{2}}\geq 0
. We have

(4.8)

\displaystyle|q_{\tau}|^{2}=q_{1}^{2}+q_{n+1}^{2}=1-\cos^{2}\theta-2\cos\theta\,q_{n+1}\geq(1-\cos\theta)^{2}.

By the evenness of the hypersurface
\Sigma_{\tau}
, we also have

(4.9)

\displaystyle R_{\tau}(-q_{1},0,\ldots,0,q_{n+1})\in\Sigma_{\tau}.

Therefore, by (
2.7
), we obtain

(4.10)

\displaystyle h(\xi,\tau)

\displaystyle\geq R_{\tau}(q_{1},0,\ldots,0,q_{n+1})\cdot(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1}+\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=R_{\tau}\big(\xi_{1}q_{1}+(\xi_{n+1}+\cos\theta)q_{n+1}\big),

as well as

(4.11)

\displaystyle h(\xi,\tau)\geq R_{\tau}\big(-\xi_{1}q_{1}+(\xi_{n+1}+\cos\theta)q_{n+1}\big).

Since
q_{n+1}\geq 0
and
\xi_{n+1}+\cos\theta\geq 0
, we obtain

(4.12)

\displaystyle h(\xi,\tau)\geq R_{\tau}|\xi_{1}|q_{1},\quad h(\xi,\tau)\geq R_{\tau}(\xi_{n+1}+\cos\theta)q_{n+1}.

Therefore, for
p_{\tau}:=q_{\tau}/|q_{\tau}|\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle h(\xi,\tau)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{R_{\tau}}{2}\big|q_{\tau}\cdot(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1}+\cos\theta)\big|

\displaystyle\geq C_{\theta}R_{\tau}\big|p_{\tau}\cdot(\xi+\cos\theta E_{n+1})\big|,

where
C_{\theta}:=\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos\theta)
.

Case 2:

p=0
.
By
3.1
, we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle J(0)+\frac{1}{n+1}\log V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})\geq\frac{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)\log h(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)\,d\xi}.

Substituting (
4.13
) into (
4.14
), we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle(\log(C_{\theta}R_{\tau}))\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)\,d\xi+(\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\log|u\cdot p_{\tau}|\,du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle(\log(C_{\theta}R_{\tau}))\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)\,d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}}f(u+\cos\theta\,e)\log|u\cdot p_{\tau}|\,du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)\log h(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\leq\left(J(0)+\frac{1}{n+1}\log V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})\right)\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)\,d\xi.

Since the integral
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\log|u\cdot p_{\tau}|\,du
does not depend on
p_{\tau}
, for some positive constant
C
, depending only on
n,p
,
\theta,\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f,\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f
and
\Sigma_{0}
, we obtain

(4.16)

\displaystyle R_{\tau}\leq C.

Case 3:

p>0
. Using
3.1
and (
4.13
), we have

(4.17)

\displaystyle V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})^{\frac{p}{n+1}}e^{pJ(0)}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f(\xi)h(\xi,\tau)^{p}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq C_{\theta}^{p}R_{\tau}^{p}(\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f)\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}}|u\cdot p_{\tau}|^{p}\,du.

Note that
w:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as
w(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}}|u\cdot x|^{p}\,du
is a positive continuous function. Hence,
w\geq c_{\theta}>0
.
Thus we obtain

(4.18)

\displaystyle R_{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}e^{J(0)}}{c_{\theta}^{\frac{1}{p}}C_{\theta}(\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f)^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p>-n-1
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Suppose
\Sigma_{\tau}
is a smooth, even, and strictly convex solution to the flow (
1.14
). Then there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
n,p
,
\theta,\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f,\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f
and
\Sigma_{0}
, such that

(4.19)

\displaystyle|\nabla h(\cdot,\tau)|\leq C.

Proof.

By
4.1
,
h\leq C
. The claim follows from
[
HIS25
, Lem. 4.8]
.
∎

Next, we derive a uniform upper bound for the Gauss curvature
\mathcal{K}(\cdot,\tau)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p>-n-1
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Suppose
\Sigma_{\tau}
is a smooth, even, and strictly convex solution to the flow (
1.14
). Then there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
n,p
,
\theta,\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f,\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f
and
\Sigma_{0}
, such that

(4.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\leq C.

Proof.

We apply the maximum principle to the function

(4.21)

\displaystyle Q=\frac{\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}fh^{p}\mathcal{K}-h}{h-\varepsilon_{0}}=\frac{-h_{\tau}}{h-\varepsilon_{0}},

where
h_{\tau}:=\partial_{\tau}h
and

(4.22)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,\infty)}h(\xi,\tau)>0.

We may assume that
\max\mathcal{K}\gg 1
. Due to the
C^{0}
estimate (cf.
4.1
), we may also assume that
\max Q\approx\max\mathcal{K}\gg 1
.

For any fixed
\tau\in(0,\infty)
, suppose the maximum of
Q(\xi,\tau)
is attained at some point
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. On
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we have

(4.23)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}Q

\displaystyle=-\frac{h_{\tau\mu}}{h-\varepsilon_{0}}+\frac{h_{\tau}h_{\mu}}{(h-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}}=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\cot\theta\,h_{\tau}}{(h-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}}=-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\cot\theta\,Q}{h-\varepsilon_{0}}<0.

Therefore, the maximum of
Q(\cdot,\tau)
is attained at some point
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\setminus\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

Choose a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
such that
\{b_{ij}\}
is diagonal at the point
\xi_{0}
. Then, at the point
\xi_{0}
, we have

(4.24)

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{i}Q

\displaystyle=\frac{-h_{\tau i}}{h-\varepsilon_{0}}+\frac{h_{\tau}h_{i}}{(h-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}},

\displaystyle 0\geq\nabla^{2}_{ii}Q

\displaystyle=\frac{-h_{\tau ii}}{h-\varepsilon_{0}}+\frac{2h_{\tau i}h_{i}+h_{\tau}h_{ii}}{(h-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}}-\frac{2h_{\tau}h_{i}^{2}}{(h-\varepsilon_{0})^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-h_{\tau ii}}{h-\varepsilon_{0}}+\frac{h_{\tau}h_{ii}}{(h-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}}.

This implies that

(4.25)

\displaystyle-h_{\tau ii}-h_{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{h_{\tau}h_{ii}}{h-\varepsilon_{0}}-h_{\tau}=\frac{-h_{\tau}}{h-\varepsilon_{0}}[h_{ii}+(h-\varepsilon_{0})]=Q(b_{ii}-\varepsilon_{0}).

Moreover, we have

(4.26)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}Q

\displaystyle=\frac{-h_{\tau\tau}}{h-\varepsilon_{0}}+\frac{h_{\tau}^{2}}{(h-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})fh^{p}\mathcal{K}}{(h-\varepsilon_{0})\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}\left(\frac{\mathcal{K}_{\tau}}{\mathcal{K}}-\frac{p\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p-1}h_{\tau}\,d\xi}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}+p\frac{h_{\tau}}{h}\right)+Q+Q^{2}.

For simplicity, let us put

(4.27)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}=\det(\nabla^{2}h+hI),\quad\sigma_{n}^{ij}=\frac{\partial\det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)}{\partial b_{ij}}.

Using (
4.25
), at the point
\xi_{0}
, we calculate that

(4.28)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\tau}=\partial_{\tau}\sigma_{n}^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{n}^{-2}\sum_{i,j}\sigma_{n}^{ij}(h_{\tau ij}+h_{\tau}\delta_{ij})

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{n}^{-2}\sum_{i}\sigma_{n}^{ii}Q(b_{ii}-\varepsilon_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}Q(n-\varepsilon_{0}\sum_{i}b^{ii}),

where

\{b^{ij}\}
denotes the inverse matrix of
\{b_{ij}\}
. Here, we used
\sigma_{n}^{ii}=\sigma_{n}b^{ii}
and

(4.29)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\sigma_{n}^{ii}b_{ii}

\displaystyle=n\sigma_{n},\quad\sum_{i}\sigma_{n}^{ii}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{n}\sum_{i}b^{ii}.

It follows from (
4.28
) and

\sum_{i}b^{ii}\geq n(\prod_{i}b^{ii})^{\frac{1}{n}}=n\mathcal{K}^{\frac{1}{n}}
that

(4.30)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{K}_{\tau}}{\mathcal{K}}\leq nQ(1-\varepsilon_{0}\mathcal{K}^{\frac{1}{n}}).

Substituting (
4.30
) and (
4.21
) into (
4.26
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}Q

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})fh^{p}\mathcal{K}}{(h-\varepsilon_{0})\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}\Big(nQ(1-\varepsilon_{0}\mathcal{K}^{\frac{1}{n}})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{p\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p-1}(h-\varepsilon_{0})Q\,d\xi}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}-\frac{p(h-\varepsilon_{0})Q}{h}\Big)+Q+Q^{2}

\displaystyle=\big(Q+\frac{h}{h-\varepsilon_{0}}\big)\Big(nQ(1-\varepsilon_{0}\mathcal{K}^{\frac{1}{n}})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{p\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p-1}(h-\varepsilon_{0})Q\,d\xi}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}-\frac{p(h-\varepsilon_{0})Q}{h}\Big)+Q+Q^{2}.

Hence, we can find
C_{1}
independent of
\tau
such that

(4.31)

\displaystyle Q(\cdot,\tau)\leq C_{1}.

Therefore,

\mathcal{K}(\cdot,\tau)\leq C_{2}

for some
C_{2}
depending only on
n,p
,
\theta,\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f,\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f
and
\Sigma_{0}
.
∎

We now show that the Gauss curvature
\mathcal{K}(\cdot,\tau)
is uniformly bounded below away from zero.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
p>-n-1
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Suppose
\Sigma_{\tau}
is a smooth, even, and strictly convex solution to the flow (
1.14
). Then there exists a positive constant
c
, depending only on
n,p
,
\theta
,
\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f,\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f
and
\Sigma_{0}
, such that

\mathcal{K}\geq c.

Proof.

We apply the maximum principle to the auxiliary function

(4.32)

\displaystyle Q=\log(f^{-1}\mathcal{K}^{-1})-N\log h,

where
N>0
is a constant to be determined later. We may assume that
\min\mathcal{K}\ll 1
. Due to the
C^{0}
estimate of
h
(cf.
4.1
), we may also assume that
\max Q\gg 1
.

Claim
.

For
\tau>0
, we have
\nabla_{\mu}Q<0
on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, provided
N>p-1
.

For simplicity, set
\alpha:=\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}
. For
\tau>0
, we calculate

(4.33)

\displaystyle h_{\tau\mu}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}(-\alpha fh^{p}\mathcal{K}+h)

\displaystyle=-\alpha f_{\mu}\mathcal{K}h^{p}-\alpha f\mathcal{K}_{\mu}h^{p}-p\alpha f\mathcal{K}h^{p}\cot\theta+\cot\theta\,h,

and

(4.34)

\displaystyle h_{\tau\mu}=\cot\theta\,h_{\tau}=\cot\theta\,(-\alpha f\mathcal{K}h^{p}+h).

From (
4.33
) and (
4.34
), for
\tau>0
, we have

(4.35)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}{\mathcal{K}}=-\frac{f_{\mu}}{f}+(1-p)\cot\theta,

and hence, if
N>p-1
, then

(4.36)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}Q

\displaystyle=-\frac{f_{\mu}}{f}-\frac{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}{\mathcal{K}}-N\frac{h_{\mu}}{h}=(p-1)\cot\theta-N\cot\theta<0.

Therefore, when
N>p-1
, for any fixed
\tau\in(0,\infty)
, the maximum of
Q(\cdot,\tau)
is attained at some point
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\setminus\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Now, take a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
around
\xi_{0}
such that
\{b_{ij}\}(\xi_{0},\tau)
is diagonal. At the point
\xi_{0}
,

(4.37)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}Q=-\frac{\nabla_{i}(f\mathcal{K})}{f\mathcal{K}}-N\frac{\nabla_{i}h}{h}=0,

and

(4.38)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{ii}Q

\displaystyle=-\frac{\nabla^{2}_{ii}(f\mathcal{K})}{f\mathcal{K}}+\frac{(\nabla_{i}(f\mathcal{K}))^{2}}{(f\mathcal{K})^{2}}-N\frac{\nabla^{2}_{ii}h}{h}+N\frac{(\nabla_{i}h)^{2}}{h^{2}}\leq 0.

Substituting (
4.37
) into (
4.38
), we obtain

(4.39)

\displaystyle-\nabla^{2}_{ii}(f\mathcal{K})\leq Nf\mathcal{K}\frac{\nabla^{2}_{ii}h}{h}+(-N-N^{2})f\mathcal{K}\frac{(\nabla_{i}h)^{2}}{h^{2}}.

In addition, we have

(4.40)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}Q

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{\tau}\sigma_{n}}{\sigma_{n}}-N\frac{h_{\tau}}{h}=\sum_{i}b^{ii}(h_{\tau ii}+h_{\tau})-N\frac{h_{\tau}}{h},

and

(4.41)

\displaystyle h_{\tau ii}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}_{ii}(-\alpha fh^{p}\mathcal{K}+h)

\displaystyle=-\alpha h^{p}\nabla^{2}_{ii}(f\mathcal{K})-2p\alpha h^{p-1}\nabla_{i}h\nabla_{i}(f\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle-p(p-1)\alpha h^{p-2}f\mathcal{K}(\nabla_{i}h)^{2}+(1-p\alpha h^{p-1}f\mathcal{K})\nabla^{2}_{ii}h.

In view of (
4.37
) and (
4.39
), we find

(4.42)

\displaystyle h_{\tau ii}\leq

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle(1+\alpha(N-p)h^{p-1}f\mathcal{K})\nabla^{2}_{ii}h

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle+(-N-N^{2}+2pN-p(p-1))\alpha h^{p-2}f\mathcal{K}(\nabla_{i}h)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle(1+(N-p)\alpha h^{p-1}f\mathcal{K})(b_{ii}-h)

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle+(-N-N^{2}+2pN-p(p-1))\alpha h^{p-2}f\mathcal{K}(\nabla_{i}h)^{2}.

Substituting (
4.42
) into (
4.40
), we deduce

(4.43)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}Q

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha(N-p)h^{p-1}f\mathcal{K})\big(n-h\sum_{i}b^{ii}\big)

\displaystyle+(-N-N^{2}+2pN-p(p-1))\alpha h^{p-2}f\mathcal{K}\sum_{i}b^{ii}(\nabla_{i}h)^{2}

\displaystyle+(-\alpha h^{p}f\mathcal{K}+h)\sum_{i}b^{ii}-N(1-\alpha h^{p-1}f\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle=\left(n-N+(n(N-p)+N)\alpha h^{p-1}f\mathcal{K}\right)-(N-p+1)\alpha h^{p}f\mathcal{K}\sum_{i}b^{ii}

\displaystyle+(-N-N^{2}+2pN-p(p-1))\alpha h^{p-2}f\mathcal{K}\sum_{i}b^{ii}(\nabla_{i}h)^{2}.

Due to the
C^{0}
estimate (cf.
4.1
), if
\max Q\to\infty
, i.e.,
\min\mathcal{K}\to 0
, the right-hand side of the above inequality is strictly negative, provided
N
is sufficiently large. Thus, the Gauss curvature
\mathcal{K}
is uniformly bounded below away from zero.
∎

Finally, we prove that the principal curvatures of
\Sigma_{\tau}
are uniformly bounded by positive constants from both above and below.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
p>-n-1
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Suppose
\Sigma_{\tau}
is a smooth, even, and strictly convex solution to the flow (
1.14
). Then there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
n
,
p
,
\theta
,
\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f
,
\|f\|_{C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})}
and
\Sigma_{0}
, such that the principal curvatures
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n}
of
\Sigma_{\tau}
satisfy

(4.44)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\leq\kappa_{i}\leq C.

Proof.

Since the Gauss curvature
\mathcal{K}
of
\Sigma_{\tau}
is uniformly bounded from above (cf.
4.3
), it suffices to prove the lower bound of the principal curvatures of
\Sigma_{\tau}
, which is equivalent to the upper bound of the principal radii of curvature of
\Sigma_{\tau}
. To this end, we apply the maximum principle to the auxiliary function

(4.45)

\displaystyle Q=\sigma_{1}+\frac{A}{2}|\nabla h|^{2},

where
A>0
is a constant to be specified later, and
\sigma_{1}:=\sigma_{1}(b_{ij}(\xi,\tau))
is the trace of the matrix
\{b_{ij}\}
.

Claim
.

Fix
A>0
and suppose
\tau>0
. Then
\nabla_{\mu}Q<0
at any point on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
where
Q
is sufficiently large (depending on
A
).

Choose a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
such that
e_{n}=\mu
and
\{b_{ij}\}
is diagonal at
\xi_{0}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. At the point
\xi_{0}
, we have

(4.46)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}Q

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\nabla_{\mu}b_{jj}+A\sum_{j}h_{j}h_{j\mu}

\displaystyle=\cot\theta\sum_{\beta=1}^{n-1}(b_{\mu\mu}-b_{\beta\beta})+\nabla_{\mu}b_{\mu\mu}+A\cot\theta\,hh_{\mu\mu},

where we used (due to the Gauss-Weingarten equation)

(4.47)

\displaystyle h_{\mu\beta}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla_{\mu}b_{\beta\beta}=\cot\theta\,(b_{\mu\mu}-b_{\beta\beta})

for all
1\leq\beta\leq n-1
.
On the other hand, we have

(4.48)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{n}^{-1}\nabla_{\mu}\sigma_{n}

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{n}^{-1}\left(\sum_{\beta}\sigma_{n}^{\beta\beta}\nabla_{\mu}b_{\beta\beta}+\sigma_{n}^{\mu\mu}\nabla_{\mu}b_{\mu\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\cot\theta\sum_{\beta}b^{\beta\beta}(b_{\mu\mu}-b_{\beta\beta})+b^{\mu\mu}\nabla_{\mu}b_{\mu\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=-\cot\theta\,b_{\mu\mu}\sum_{\beta}b^{\beta\beta}+(n-1)\cot\theta-b^{\mu\mu}\nabla_{\mu}b_{\mu\mu}.

Combining this with (
4.35
), we obtain

(4.49)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}b_{\mu\mu}=\left(\frac{f_{\mu}}{f}+(n+p-2)\cot\theta\right)b_{\mu\mu}-\cot\theta\,b_{\mu\mu}^{2}\sum_{\beta}b^{\beta\beta}.

Substituting (
4.49
) into (
4.46
), we find

(4.50)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}Q

\displaystyle=\cot\theta\left(nb_{\mu\mu}-\sigma_{1}\right)+\left(\frac{f_{\mu}}{f}+(n+p-2)\cot\theta\right)b_{\mu\mu}

\displaystyle-\cot\theta\,b_{\mu\mu}^{2}\sum_{\beta}b^{\beta\beta}+A\cot\theta\,h(b_{\mu\mu}-h)

\displaystyle\leq-\cot\theta\,\sigma_{1}+\left(\frac{f_{\mu}}{f}+(2n+p-2+Ah)\cot\theta\right)b_{\mu\mu}

\displaystyle-(n-1)\cot\theta\,b_{\mu\mu}^{2+\frac{1}{n-1}}\mathcal{K}^{\frac{1}{n-1}},

where we used

(4.51)

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta}b^{\beta\beta}\geq(n-1)\left(\prod_{\beta}b^{\beta\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}=(n-1)\mathcal{K}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}b_{\mu\mu}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

In view of
4.1
and the lower bound on the Gauss curvature (cf.
4.4
), we have

(4.52)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}Q\leq\cot\theta\left(-\sigma_{1}+c_{1}b_{\mu\mu}-c_{2}b_{\mu\mu}^{2+\frac{1}{n-1}}\right)

for some positive constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(n,p,\theta,\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f,\|f\|_{C^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})},\Sigma_{0},A)
, and
c_{2}=c_{2}(n,p,\theta,\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f,\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f,\Sigma_{0})
, independent of
\tau
.

If
Q\geq c_{1}b_{\mu\mu}+\frac{A}{2}|\nabla h|^{2}
(i.e.
\sigma_{1}\geq c_{1}b_{\mu\mu}
) at
\xi_{0}
, then it is clear that
\nabla_{\mu}Q<0
. Otherwise,
Q\leq c_{1}b_{\mu\mu}+\frac{A}{2}|\nabla h|^{2}
(i.e.
\sigma_{1}\leq c_{1}b_{\mu\mu}
) at
\xi_{0}
, and hence

(4.53)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}Q

\displaystyle\leq\cot\theta\,b_{\mu\mu}\left(c_{1}-c_{2}b_{\mu\mu}^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\cot\theta\,b_{\mu\mu}\left(c_{1}-c^{\prime}_{2}\sigma_{1}^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\cot\theta\,b_{\mu\mu}\left(c_{1}-c^{\prime}_{2}\left(Q-\frac{A}{2}|\nabla h|^{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\right),

which is negative, provided that
Q
is sufficiently large.

Therefore, for any fixed
\tau\in(0,\infty)
and any fixed
A
, the maximum of
Q
, if very large, must be attained at some point
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\setminus\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Choose a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
around
\xi_{0}
such that
\{b_{ij}\}(\xi_{0},\tau)
is diagonal, and hence
\{h_{ij}\}(\xi_{0},\tau)
is also diagonal. At
\xi_{0}
, we calculate

(4.54)

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{i}Q=\sum_{j}\nabla_{i}b_{jj}+Ah_{i}h_{ii},

and

(4.55)

\displaystyle 0\geq\nabla^{2}_{ii}Q=\sum_{j}\nabla^{2}_{ii}b_{jj}+A\left(\sum_{j}h_{j}h_{jii}+h_{ii}^{2}\right),

where we stipulate
h_{jii}:=\nabla_{i}h_{ji}
.
Moreover, we have

(4.56)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}Q=\sum_{j}\partial_{\tau}b_{jj}+A\sum_{j}h_{j}h_{j\tau}.

On the other hand,

(4.57)

\displaystyle\log(h-h_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\log\left(\alpha f\mathcal{K}h^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=-\log\det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)+\psi,

where
\psi:=\log\left(\alpha fh^{p}\right)
. Taking the covariant derivative of (
4.57
) in the
e_{j}
direction, it follows that

(4.58)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{j}-h_{j\tau}}{h-h_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,k}b^{ik}\nabla_{j}b_{ik}+\psi_{j}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}b^{ii}(h_{jii}+h_{i}\delta_{ij})+\psi_{j},

where we used that
\nabla b
is fully symmetric. We also have

(4.59)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{jj}-h_{jj\tau}}{h-h_{\tau}}-\frac{(h_{j}-h_{j\tau})^{2}}{(h-h_{\tau})^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}b^{ii}\nabla^{2}_{jj}b_{ii}+\sum_{i,k}b^{ii}b^{kk}(\nabla_{j}b_{ik})^{2}+\psi_{jj}.

Using the commutator formula on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
we find

(4.60)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{jj}-h_{jj\tau}}{h-h_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(h_{j}-h_{j\tau})^{2}}{(h-h_{\tau})^{2}}-\sum_{i}b^{ii}(\nabla^{2}_{ii}b_{jj}-b_{jj}+b_{ii})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,k}b^{ii}b^{kk}(\nabla_{j}b_{ik})^{2}+\psi_{jj}

\displaystyle=\frac{(h_{j}-h_{j\tau})^{2}}{(h-h_{\tau})^{2}}-\sum_{i}b^{ii}\nabla^{2}_{ii}b_{jj}+b_{jj}\sum_{i}b^{ii}-n

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,k}b^{ii}b^{kk}(\nabla_{j}b_{ik})^{2}+\psi_{jj}.

Together with (
4.55
), (
4.56
), and (
4.60
), we calculate

(4.61)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{\tau}Q}{h-h_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\frac{(h_{jj\tau}-h_{jj})+(h_{jj}+h)-h+h_{\tau}}{h-h_{\tau}}+A\frac{\sum_{j}h_{j}h_{j\tau}}{h-h_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j}\frac{(h_{j}-h_{j\tau})^{2}}{(h-h_{\tau})^{2}}+\sum_{i,j}b^{ii}\nabla^{2}_{ii}b_{jj}+n^{2}-\sum_{i,j,k}b^{ii}b^{kk}(\nabla_{j}b_{ik})^{2}

\displaystyle-\Delta\psi-\sum_{j}b_{jj}\sum_{i}b^{ii}+\frac{\sum_{j}b_{jj}}{h-h_{\tau}}-n+A\frac{\sum_{j}h_{j}h_{j\tau}}{h-h_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq-A\sum_{i,j}b^{ii}h_{j}h_{jii}-A\sum_{i}b^{ii}h_{ii}^{2}+n(n-1)-\Delta\psi

\displaystyle-\sigma_{1}\sum_{i}b^{ii}+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{h-h_{\tau}}+A\frac{\sum_{j}h_{j}h_{j\tau}}{h-h_{\tau}}.

Multiplying (
4.58
) by
h_{j}
and summing over
j
, we find

(4.62)

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}-\sum_{j}h_{j}h_{j\tau}}{h-h_{\tau}}=-\sum_{i,j}b^{ii}h_{j}h_{jii}-\sum_{i}b^{ii}h_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j}h_{j}\psi_{j}.

Substituting this into (
4.61
) yields

(4.63)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{\tau}Q}{h-h_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq n(n-1)-\Delta\psi-\sigma_{1}\sum_{i}b^{ii}+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{h-h_{\tau}}

\displaystyle+A\left(\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}}{h-h_{\tau}}-\sum_{j}h_{j}\psi_{j}+\sum_{i}b^{ii}(h_{i}^{2}-h_{ii}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=n(n-1)-\Delta\psi-\sigma_{1}\sum_{i}b^{ii}+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{h-h_{\tau}}

\displaystyle+\frac{A|\nabla h|^{2}}{h-h_{\tau}}-A\sum_{j}h_{j}\psi_{j}+A\sum_{i}b^{ii}h_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle-A\sigma_{1}+2nAh-Ah^{2}\sum_{i}b^{ii}.

We also have

(4.64)

\displaystyle\psi_{j}=\frac{f_{j}}{f}+p\frac{h_{j}}{h},\quad\psi_{jj}=\frac{ff_{jj}-f_{j}^{2}}{f^{2}}+p\frac{hh_{jj}-h_{j}^{2}}{h^{2}}.

Therefore, for some positive constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
, independent of
\tau
, depending only on
n
,
p
,
\theta
,
\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f
,
\|f\|_{C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})}
and
\Sigma_{0}
, we have

(4.65)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{\tau}Q}{h-h_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{0}+c_{1}A+(c_{2}-A)\sigma_{1}+(A|\nabla h|^{2}-\sigma_{1})\sum_{i}b^{ii}

\displaystyle=c_{0}+c_{1}A+(c_{2}-A)\left(Q-\frac{A}{2}|\nabla h|^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{3A}{2}|\nabla h|^{2}-Q\right)\sum_{i}b^{ii},

where we used
4.1
,
4.2
, and

(4.66)

\displaystyle h-h_{\tau}=f\mathcal{K}h^{p}\frac{(n+1)V(\widehat{\Sigma_{0}})}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}fh^{p}\,d\xi}\geq c

for some positive constant
c
, independent of
\tau
, due to
4.4
. Now take
A=2c_{2}
. If
Q(\xi_{0},\tau)
is very large, then we have

\partial_{\tau}Q<0.

∎

4.2.
Regularity Estimates II

In this subsection, we prove the uniform
C^{k}
estimates of solutions to the flow (
1.16
) for
p>n+1
, without the evenness assumption.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
p>n+1
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Suppose
\Sigma_{\tau}
is a smooth, strictly convex solution to the flow (
1.16
). Then there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
n
,
p
,
\theta
,
\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f
,
\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f
and
\Sigma_{0}
, such that

(4.67)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\leq h(\xi,\tau)\leq C,\quad\forall(\xi,\tau)\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times(0,\infty).

Proof.

A direct calculation yields

(4.68)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{h}{\ell}\left(-h^{p-1}f\sigma_{n}^{-1}+1\right),

and

(4.69)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\right)=0,\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

where we used that
\nabla_{\mu}\ell=\cot\theta\,\ell
on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

We claim that for any fixed
\tau>0
, if the function
\frac{h}{\ell}(\cdot,\tau)
attains its minimum at a point
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, then at this point,

(4.70)

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\right)=0,\quad\nabla^{2}\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\right)\geq 0.

If
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\setminus\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, then the claim follows directly. We assume that
\xi_{0}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. For any
v\in T_{\xi_{0}}\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we have

(4.71)

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{v}\frac{h}{\ell}.

Combining this with (
4.69
), we have
\nabla\frac{h}{\ell}=0
at
\xi_{0}
.

Choose a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
around
\xi_{0}
such that
e_{n}=\mu
and the matrix
b_{ij}|_{(\xi_{0},\tau)}
is diagonal. In this frame, the Hessian
\nabla^{2}_{ij}\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\right)(\xi_{0},\tau)
is also diagonal. From the Taylor expansion of
\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\right)(\gamma(s),\tau)
, it follows that

\left.\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\right|_{s=0}\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\right)(\gamma(s),\tau)\geq 0\implies\nabla^{2}_{\mu\mu}\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\right)(\xi_{0},\tau)\geq 0,

where
\gamma
is the geodesic with
\gamma(0)=\xi_{0}
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=-\mu
.

Next, we show that at
\xi_{0}
,
b_{ij}\geq\frac{h}{\ell}\delta_{ij}
. To this end, note that at the point
\xi_{0}
, we have

(4.72)

\displaystyle 0\leq\nabla^{2}_{ij}\frac{h}{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla^{2}_{ij}h}{\ell}+\frac{2h}{\ell^{3}}\nabla_{i}\ell\nabla_{j}\ell-\frac{\nabla^{2}_{ij}\ell}{\ell^{2}}h-\frac{\nabla_{j}h\nabla_{i}\ell}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{\nabla_{i}h\nabla_{j}\ell}{\ell^{2}}

(4.73)

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{ij}}{\ell}-\frac{1}{\ell}\left(\nabla_{i}\frac{h}{\ell}\nabla_{j}\ell+\nabla_{j}\frac{h}{\ell}\nabla_{i}\ell\right)-\frac{h}{\ell^{2}}\delta_{ij}

(4.74)

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{ij}}{\ell}-\frac{h}{\ell^{2}}\delta_{ij},

where we used
\nabla\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\right)=0
at
\xi_{0}
and that
\nabla^{2}_{ij}\ell+\ell\delta_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
.

Since
\sigma_{n}
is monotonically increasing in its argument, it follows that

(4.75)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(b_{ij})\geq\sigma_{n}\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\delta_{ij}\right)=\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\right)^{n}.

Put
\varphi(\tau)=\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\frac{h}{\ell}\right)(\cdot,\tau)
. Substituting (
4.75
) into (
4.68
), we find

(4.76)

\displaystyle\frac{d\varphi}{d\tau}

\displaystyle\geq\varphi\left(1-\varphi^{p-n-1}f\ell^{p-1}\right).

Either
\varphi(\tau)\geq\left(\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(f\ell^{p-1})\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-n-1}}
or
\frac{d\varphi}{d\tau}>0
(whenever it is differentiable). Therefore,
\frac{h}{\ell}
remains uniformly bounded below away from zero.
Similarly, we can show that
\frac{h}{\ell}
is uniformly bounded above.
∎

Based on the above
C^{0}
estimate, the
C^{1}
and
C^{2}
estimates of solutions to the flow (
1.16
) follow directly from an argument almost identical to that in
4.2
–
4.5
. We obtain the following result.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
p>n+1
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Suppose
\Sigma_{\tau}
is a smooth, strictly convex solution to the flow (
1.16
). Then there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
n
,
p
,
\theta
,
\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f
,
\|f\|_{C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})}
and
\Sigma_{0}
, such that

(4.77)

\displaystyle|\nabla h(\cdot,\tau)|\leq C,\quad\frac{1}{C}\leq\kappa_{i}(\cdot,\tau)\leq C.

5.
Convergence

Standard parabolic theory with Neumann boundary conditions guarantees the short-time existence for the flows (
1.11
) and (
1.16
). Moreover, combining
[
Don88
, Theorems 6.1, 6.4, 6.5]
,
[
Lie96
, Theorem 14.23]
with our uniform estimates for the normalized solution of the flow (
1.14
), we find that the solution to the unnormalized flow (
1.11
) satisfies
V(\widehat{\mathcal{M}_{t}})\to 0
as
t\to\mathcal{T}
. Now, from the definition of the time parameter
\tau
it follows that
\tau(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\mathcal{T}
.

Proof of
1.1
.

By the uniform estimates established in
subsection 4.1
, the solution
h(\cdot,\tau)
to (
3.2
) enjoys uniform
C^{k}
-norm bound for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, by the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem and
3.1
, we can extract a subsequence of times,
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}\subset(0,\infty)
, such that
h(\cdot,\tau_{i})
converges smoothly to an even strictly convex function
h
such that
h
satisfies (
1.15
), where
\Sigma
is the even, smooth, and strictly convex capillary hypersurface whose capillary support function is
h
.
∎

Proof of
1.2
.

Due to
3.2
and the uniform estimates shown in
subsection 4.2
, a subsequence of the solution to (
3.8
) converges to a smooth, strictly convex solution of (
1.9
).
∎

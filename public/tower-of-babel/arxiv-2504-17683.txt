Introduction

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension, and let

aims to construct a correspondence between certain
2
-dimensional

-adic representations of

over

-adic representations of
\GL_{2}(L)
over

We will mainly focus on the locally analytic aspect. When

Remark 1.0.2
.

Using a similar method one can also obtain the following result by reversing the target and the source:

\displaystyle\dim_{E}\Ext^{1}_{D_{\sigma}(\GL_{2}(L),E)}(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}[M],\text{LL}(M)^{\prime})=2.

(9)

A key ingredient in the proof is to compute the wall-crossing of the
D_{\sigma}(\GL_{2}(L),E)
-module
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}[M]
. Roughly speaking, wall-crossing functors for locally analytic representations
[
JLS22
,
Din24
]
are natural generalizations of wall-crossing functors for Lie algebra representations, for example
[
Hum08
, Chapter 7]
. The wall-crossing functors are helpful for analyzing the structure of some locally analytic representations and are also useful for producing new ones, see for example
[
BQ24
, Theorem 1.3.7]
. As
\mathcal{M}_{\text{Dr},n}
for
n\geq 0
are étale over the period domain
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and the wall-crossing functors commute with taking cohomology, essentially one just needs to compute the wall-crossing of the equivariant line bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which we handle it by explicit calculations. It turns out that the wall-crossing of
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}[M]
has some nice properties, basically coming from the following key points:

(i)

functoriality of the wall-crossing functor,

(ii)

adjointness of translation functors (as wall-crossing functors are compositions of translation functors),

(iii)

the fact that the wall-crossing of
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}[M]
follows the same pattern as the wall-crossing of the structure sheaf of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(also the wall-crossing of the irreducible dual Verma module in the principal block of the BGG category
\mathcal{O}
).

Therefore, once we relate
\Omega^{1}_{\sigma}[M]
with the wall-crossing of
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}[M]
(Proposition
2.4.2
), we can show that
\Omega^{1}_{\sigma}[M]
is the universal extension of
\text{LL}(M)^{\prime}
by
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}[M]
.

Finally, after additional steps on describing endomorphisms of
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}[M]
(Lemma
2.5.5

-analytic representations of

over

that are constructed using the de Rham complex of Drinfeld tower of dimension

. Let
(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
be the universal
p
-divisible special formal
\mathcal{O}_{D_{L}}
-module on
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{Dr},0}
. The
p
-adic Tate module of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
defines a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-local system equipped with an action of
\mathcal{O}_{D_{L}}
on the étale site of
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{Dr},0}
, which we denote by
V_{\text{Dr}}
. For
n\geq 0
, we get an étale Galois covering
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{Dr},n}
of
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{Dr},0}
with Galois group
(\mathcal{O}_{D_{L}}/\varpi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{D_{L}})^{\times}=\mathcal{O}_{D_{L}}^{\times}/(1+\varpi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{D_{L}})

\mathcal{O}_{D_{L}}
-equivariant trivializations of

. These spaces are equipped with
\GL_{2}(L)\times D_{L}^{\times}
-actions. For

, the covering maps
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{Dr},m}\to\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{Dr},n}
are
\GL_{2}(L)\times D_{L}^{\times}
-equivariant. Moreover, for
n\geq 0
, the spaces
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{Dr},n}
are equipped with canonical Weil descent data relative to
\breve{L}/L
, which are not effective but become effective by quotient
{\varpi^{\mathbb{Z}}}
, with
\varpi
viewed as a central element in
\GL_{2}(L)
. Fix a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-embedding
\sigma:L\hookrightarrow E
and let
\Sigma_{n}
be the adic space over
E
obtained by base-changing the
L
-model of
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{Dr},n}/{\varpi^{\mathbb{Z}}}
via
\sigma
. The map
\breve{\pi}_{\text{GM}}
induces a natural map
\pi_{\text{GM}}:\Sigma_{0}\to\mathscr{F}\ell
with
\mathscr{F}\ell
the adic space associated to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{L}\times_{L,\sigma}E
. For any

, let
\pi_{n}:\Sigma_{n}\to\Sigma_{0}
be the projection map and denote the composition map
\pi_{\text{GM}}\circ\pi_{n}:\Sigma_{n}\to\mathscr{F}\ell
by
\pi_{\text{GM},n}
. The map
\pi_{\text{GM},n}
is compatible with the
\GL_{2}(L)\times D_{L}^{\times}
-action, with the natural action of
\GL_{2}(L)
on
\mathscr{F}\ell
(via
\sigma
) and trivial action of
D_{L}^{\times}
on
\mathscr{F}\ell
.

Using the period map
\pi_{\text{GM},n}
, we can pull back some equivariant sheaves on
\mathscr{F}\ell
to
\Sigma_{n}
. Let
\overline{B}\subset\GL_{2}
be the Borel subgroup consisting of lower triangular matrices, and we identify
\mathscr{F}\ell
with the base change to
E
via
\sigma
of the adic space associated with
\overline{B}\backslash\GL_{2}
. For
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, let
\omega_{\mathscr{F}\ell}^{(a,b)}
be the
\GL_{2}(L)
-equivariant line bundle on
\mathscr{F}\ell
associated to the
\overline{B}
-representation
\left(\begin{matrix}x&0\\
y&z\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto\sigma(x)^{a}\sigma(z)^{b}
. Under this normalization, one directly checks that
H^{0}(\mathscr{F}\ell,\omega_{\mathscr{F}\ell}^{(1,0)})
is the
\sigma
-standard representation of
\GL_{2}(L)
, and we have a canonical
\GL_{2}(L)
-equivariant isomorphism
\Omega_{\mathscr{F}\ell}^{1}\cong\omega_{\mathscr{F}\ell}^{(-1,1)}
. Here, by the
\sigma
-standard representation of
\GL_{2}(L)
we mean the base change to
E
via
\sigma
of the standard action of
\GL_{2}(L)
on a
2
-dimensional
L
-vector space. Define
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}:=\pi_{\text{GM},n}^{*}\omega_{\mathscr{F}\ell}^{(a,b)}
with
\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{n}}=\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(0,0)}
the structure sheaf of
\Sigma_{n}
and
\Omega^{1}_{\Sigma_{n}}=\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,1)}
the sheaf of differentials on
\Sigma_{n}
. As
\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{n}}
-modules, these sheaves
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
for
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
are all isomorphic to the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{n}}
, but the isomorphisms are not compatible with the
\GL_{2}(L)
-action.

Finally we introduce some notation on Lie algebras. Fix a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-embedding
\sigma:L\hookrightarrow E
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma}:=\gl_{2}(L)\ox_{L,\sigma}E
, and
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})
be the center of
U(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})
. The center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})
is generated as an
E
-algebra by
z=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma}
, and the Casimir operator
\mathfrak{c}=u^{+}u^{-}+u^{-}u^{+}+\frac{1}{2}h^{2}\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})
, where
u^{+}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma}
,
u^{-}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma}
, and
h=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma}
. The action of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})
on
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
is semisimple, for
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Let
\mathfrak{b}_{\sigma}
be the Borel subalgebra consisting of upper triangular matrices, and let
\mathfrak{h}_{\sigma}
be the Cartan subalgebra consisting of diagonal matrices. For an integral weight
\mu
of
\mathfrak{h}_{\sigma}
, we denote by
\widetilde{\chi}_{\mu}
the infinitesimal character of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})
acting on

. Then
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
has infinitesimal character
\widetilde{\chi}_{(a,b)}
. Indeed,
\pi_{\text{GM},n}
is an étale map and
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})
acts on
\omega_{\mathscr{F}\ell}^{(a,b)}
via
\widetilde{\chi}_{(a,b)}
.

2.2
Translations of equivariant sheaves

In the classical case, translation functors have been widely applied in the study of representations of semisimple Lie algebras. There exists recent work
[
JLS22
,
Din24
]
applying translation functors to study locally analytic representations. In this section, we adapt these translation functors to equivariant sheaves on Drinfeld towers of dimension
1
over
L
.

We recall the definition of translation functors for locally
\sigma
-analytic representations of
G=\GL_{2}(L)
. Let
D_{\sigma}(G,E)
be the
E
-valued locally
\sigma
-analytic distribution algebra on
G
, which is the strong dual of the space of
E
-valued locally
\sigma
-analytic functions on
G
. Let
\mu,\lambda\in\Lambda
be two integral weights of
\mathfrak{h}_{\sigma}
. Let
\text{Mod}_{\widetilde{\chi}_{\mu}}(D_{\sigma}(G,E))
be the category of left
D_{\sigma}(G,E)
-modules with generalized infinitesimal character
\widetilde{\chi}_{\mu}
. If
\mu
is dominant with respect to
\mathfrak{b}_{\sigma}
, let
V^{\mu}
be the
\sigma
-algebraic representation of
\GL_{2}(L)
over
E
, with highest weight
\mu
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}_{\sigma}
. We define the translation functor from weight
\mu
to weight
\lambda
as follows:

\displaystyle T_{\mu}^{\lambda}:\text{Mod}_{\widetilde{\chi}_{\mu}}(D_{\sigma}(G,E))

\displaystyle\to\text{Mod}_{\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}}(D_{\sigma}(G,E))

(12)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\mapsto(V^{\overline{\nu}}\ox_{E}M)\{\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})=\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}\}

(13)

where
\overline{\nu}
is the unique dominant weight in the orbit of the usual action of the Weyl group on
\lambda-\mu
, and
\{-\}
denotes the generalized eigenspace. By
[
JLS22
, Lemma 2.4.6, Theorem 2.4.7]
,
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}
is an exact functor, with left and right adjoint
T_{\lambda}^{\mu}
.

We apply these translation functors to the equivariant sheaves
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
on
\Sigma_{n}
we defined before. Let
\mu=(a,b)
be an integral weight. By
[
Cam22
, Lemma 2.3.4]
, for any affinoid open subset
U\subset\Sigma_{n}
, there exists a sufficiently small open compact subgroup
G_{U}
of
\GL_{2}(L)
such that the action of
G_{U}
on
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}(U)
is locally analytic. Moreover, by
[
Cam22
, Corollary 2.2.6]
, the action of
G_{U}
on
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}(U)
is analytic after shrinking
G_{U}
if necessary. Besides, as
L
acts on
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
via
\sigma
, the action of
G_{U}
on
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}(U)
is locally
\sigma
-analytic. Let
\{U_{i}\}
be a Stein covering of
\Sigma_{n}
. Taking limit on
U_{i}
, we deduce that
H^{0}(\Sigma_{n},\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})=\varprojlim_{i}H^{0}(U_{i},\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})
is a separately continuous
D_{\sigma}(G,E)
-module. See also
[
DLB17
, Remarque 3.4]
. As
\pi_{\text{GM},n}:\Sigma_{n}\to\mathscr{F}\ell
is an étale map and
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})
acts on
\omega_{\mathscr{F}\ell}^{(a,b)}
via
\widetilde{\chi}_{(a,b)}
, we see that
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})
also acts on
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
via
\widetilde{\chi}_{(a,b)}
. Let
\lambda
be an integral weight. As the translation functor
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}
is exact, we deduce that
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}(\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}(U))
for
U
varying in affinoid open subsets of
\Sigma_{n}
defines a sheaf on
\Sigma_{n}
, by directly checking the sheaf condition. We denote this sheaf by
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
.

We want to show some basic properties of
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
. We start with a lemma.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Let
V
be a
\sigma
-algebraic representation of
G
over
E
. For
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the sheaf
V\ox_{E}\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(a,b)}
has a filtration with graded pieces given by equivariant locally free sheaves of rank
1
, of the form
\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}
for some
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Proof.

After twisting by determinants and invertible sheaves, we may assume that
(a,b)=(0,0)
and
V=V^{(k,0)}\cong\text{Sym}^{k}V^{(1,0)}
, with
V^{(1,0)}
the
\sigma
-standard representation of
G
. When
k=1
, the universal flag on
\mathscr{F}\ell
gives an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\omega_{\mathscr{F}\ell}^{(0,1)}\to V^{(1,0)}\ox_{E}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{F}\ell}\to\omega_{\mathscr{F}\ell}^{(1,0)}\to 0.

(14)

By pulling back to
\Sigma_{n}
, we get an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(0,1)}\to V^{(1,0)}\ox_{E}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{n}}\to\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(1,0)}\to 0.

(15)

For general cases, by taking symmetric powers of the above exact sequence over
\mathcal{O}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}
, we deduce that
V\ox_{E}\mathcal{O}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}
is a successive extension of
\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(a,k-a)}
with
a=0,1,...,k
.
∎

Lemma 2.2.2
.

Let
k\geq 0
be an integer. Then
T_{(0,0)}^{(k,0)}\mathcal{O}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}\cong\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}
and
T_{(0,0)}^{(k,0)}\Omega^{1}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}\cong\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,k+1)}
.

Proof.

From the proof of Lemma
2.2.1
, we see
V^{(k,0)}\ox\mathcal{O}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}
has a filtration by
\GL_{2}(L)
-equivariant vector bundles with each graded piece of the form
\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(a,k-a)}
with
a=0,1,...,k
. Therefore, after we apply
\{\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})=\chi_{(k,0)}\}
to
V^{(k,0)}\ox\mathcal{O}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}
, we get
\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}
. Similarly one obtains the second claim.
∎

Proposition 2.2.3
.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer,
\lambda,\mu
be two integral weights. Write
\mu=(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

(i)

The sheaf
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
is a coherent sheaf on
\Sigma_{n}
, and
H^{i}(\Sigma_{n},T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})=0
for
i\geq 1
.

(ii)

We have a
\GL_{2}(L)\times D_{L}^{\times}
-equivariant isomorphism:

\displaystyle H^{0}(\Sigma_{n},T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})\cong T_{\mu}^{\lambda}H^{0}(\Sigma_{n},\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}).

(16)

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2.1
, we deduce that
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
is a successive extension of line bundles of the form
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}
with
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. This shows that
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
is a coherent sheaf on
\Sigma_{n}
. By Kiehl’s vanishing theorem
[
Kie67
]
,
H^{i}(\Sigma_{n},T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})=0
for
i\geq 1
. Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
be a Stein covering of
\Sigma_{n}
. As
T^{\lambda}_{\mu}
is biadjoint to
T^{\mu}_{\lambda}
,
T^{\lambda}_{\mu}
is exact. From this we deduce that
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}
commutes with taking cohomology:

\displaystyle H^{0}(\Sigma_{n},T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})\cong\varprojlim_{i}H^{0}(U_{i},T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})\cong\varprojlim_{i}T_{\mu}^{\lambda}H^{0}(U_{i},\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})\cong T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\varprojlim_{i}H^{0}(U_{i},\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})\cong T_{\mu}^{\lambda}H^{0}(\Sigma_{n},\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}).

(17)

∎

Remark 2.2.4
.

As
\pi_{\text{GM},n}
is an étale map, differential operators on
\mathscr{F}\ell
uniquely lift to
\Sigma_{n}
. From this one can deduce that
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}\cong\pi_{\text{GM},n}^{*}T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\omega_{\mathscr{F}\ell}^{(a,b)}
. In other words, translations of the sheaves
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
follow a similar pattern of translations of the sheaves
\omega_{\mathscr{F}\ell}^{(a,b)}
.

2.3
Wall-crossing of equivariant sheaves

Let
\mu=(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be an integral weight. We define the wall-crossing functor as follows:

\displaystyle\Theta_{s}:\text{Mod}_{\widetilde{\chi}_{\mu}}(D_{\sigma}(G,E))

\displaystyle\to\text{Mod}_{\widetilde{\chi}_{\mu}}(D_{\sigma}(G,E))

(18)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\mapsto T_{(-1,0)}^{\mu}T_{\mu}^{(-1,0)}M.

(19)

In other words, we first translate
M
to the singular weight, and then we translate back. One also puts
\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}:=T_{(-1,0)}^{\mu}T_{\mu}^{(-1,0)}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
.

Proposition 2.3.1
.

The sheaf
\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
is a coherent sheaf on
\Sigma_{n}
, with
H^{i}(\Sigma_{n},\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})=0
for
i\geq 1
, and

\displaystyle H^{0}(\Sigma_{n},\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})\cong\Theta_{s}H^{0}(\Sigma_{n},\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}).

(20)

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2.3
, we know that
T_{\mu}^{(-1,0)}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
has a filtration with graded pieces given by equivariant locally free sheaves of rank
1
, of the form
\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}
for some
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Because the translation functor
T_{(-1,0)}^{\mu}
is exact, we see that
\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
also has a filtration with graded pieces given by equivariant locally free sheaves of rank
1
, of the form
\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}
for some
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. From this, one deduces that
\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
is a coherent sheaf on
\Sigma_{n}
,
H^{i}(\Sigma_{n},\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})=0
for
i\geq 1
, and

\displaystyle H^{0}(\Sigma_{n},\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)})\cong\Theta_{s}H^{0}(\Sigma_{n},\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}).

(21)

∎

First we compute the sheaf
\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a,b)}
with
a\geq b
. Up to twist by determinants, we may assume that
(a,b)=(k,0)
, with
k\geq 0
an integer.

Proposition 2.3.2
.

(i)

T_{(k,0)}^{(-1,0)}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(k,0)}\cong\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,0)}
.

(ii)

There exists an exact sequence of
\GL_{2}(L)
-equivariant sheaves on
\Sigma_{n}
:

\displaystyle 0\to\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,k+1)}\to\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(k,0)}\to\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(k,0)}\to 0.

(22)

Proof.

From the proof of Lemma
2.2.1
, we see that
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(k,0)}\ox_{E}V^{(0,-k-1)}
is a successive extensions of line bundles
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a-1,-a)}
with
a=0,1,...,k+1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle T_{(k,0)}^{(-1,0)}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(k,0)}=(\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(k,0)}\ox_{E}V^{(0,-k-1)})\{\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})=\widetilde{\chi}_{(-1,0)}\}\cong\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,0)}.

(23)

The rest is to compute
T_{(-1,0)}^{(k,0)}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,0)}
. Using similar methods, we deduce that
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,0)}\ox_{E}V^{(k+1,0)}
is a successive extension of line bundles
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(a-1,k+1-a)}
with
a=0,1,...,k+1
. After taking
\{\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})=\widetilde{\chi}_{(k,0)}\}
, we see that only the cases
a=0
and
a=k+1
survives. Therefore, the natural maps
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,0)}\ox_{E}V^{(k+1,0)}\twoheadrightarrow\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,0)}\ox_{\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{n}}}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(k+1,0)}=\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(k,0)}
and
\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,k+1)}=\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,0)}\ox_{\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{n}}}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(0,k+1)}\hookrightarrow\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,0)}\ox_{E}V^{(k+1,0)}
induce the following exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(-1,k+1)}\to\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(k,0)}\to\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(k,0)}\to 0.

(24)

∎

Next we study the action of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})
on
\Theta_{s}\omega_{\Sigma_{n}}^{(k,0)}
. By Proposition
2.3.2
, we get a
\GL_{2}(L)
-equivariant exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,k+1)}\to\Theta_{s}\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}\to\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}\to 0.

(25)

Let
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma})
be the Casimir operator and write
c_{k}=\frac{1}{2}(k+1)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}
. The action of
\mathfrak{c}-c_{k}
on
\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,k+1)}
and
\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}
is trivial. By considering the action of
\mathfrak{c}
, we get a morphism of short exact sequence:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,k+1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta_{s}\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,k+1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta_{s}\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\mathfrak{c}-c_{k}
\mathfrak{c}-c_{k}
\mathfrak{c}-c_{k}

which is
\GL_{2}(L)
-equivariant. We denote by
\delta_{\mathfrak{c}}
the connection map
\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}\to\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,k+1)}
induced by the above morphism of short exact sequence. Besides, starting from the
\GL_{2}(L)
-equivariant differential map
\mathcal{O}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}\to\Omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}\cong\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,1)}
, applying
T_{(0,0)}^{(k,0)}
, together with Lemma
2.2.2
, we get a
\GL_{2}(L)
-equivariant map
\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}\to\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,k+1)}
. We denote this map by
d
. The following proposition is largely inspired by the proof of
[
Pan22b
, 6.5.11]
.

Proposition 2.3.3
.

The maps
\delta_{\mathfrak{c}},d:\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(k,0)}\to\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,k+1)}
coincide up to a non-zero scalar.

Proof.

First we study the case
k=0
. Let
V^{(1,0)}
be the
\sigma
-standard representation of
\GL_{2}(L)
with basis
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
such that
u^{+}v_{1}=0
and
u^{+}v_{2}=v_{1}
. Let
e_{1},e_{2}\in\omega^{(1,0)}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}
be the image of
v_{1}\ox 1,v_{2}\ox 1
via
V^{(1,0)}\ox_{E}\mathcal{O}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}\to\omega^{(1,0)}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}
. Let
U\subset{\Sigma_{n}}
be an affinoid open subset. Suppose
e_{1}
is invertible on
U
, we have
\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,0)}(U)=\mathcal{O}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}(U)e_{1}^{-1}
. Consider the exact sequence (
25
):

\displaystyle 0\to\Omega^{1}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}\to V^{(1,0)}\ox_{E}\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,0)}\to\mathcal{O}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}\to 0.

(26)

Given
s\in\mathcal{O}_{{\Sigma_{n}}}(U)
, the element
v_{1}\ox e_{1}^{-1}\ox s\in V^{(1,0)}\ox\omega_{{\Sigma_{n}}}^{(-1,0)}(U)
maps to
s
. A direct computation shows
h(v_{1}\ox e_{1}^{-1}\ox s)=v_{1}\ox e_{1}^{-1}\ox hs
, so that
\frac{1}{2}h^{2}(v_{1}\ox e_{1}^{-1}\ox s)=v_{1}\ox e_{1}^{-1}\ox\frac{1}{2}h^{2}s
. As
u^{+}e_{1}=0
, we have

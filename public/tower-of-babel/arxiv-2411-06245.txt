On the longest/shortest negative excursion of a Lévy risk process and related quantities
M. A. Lkabous, Z. Palmowski

Abstract
. In this paper, we analyze some distributions involving the longest and shortest negative excursions of spectrally negative Lévy processes using the binomial expansion approach. More specifically, we study the distributions of such excursions and related quantities such as the joint distribution of the shortest and longest negative excursion and their difference (also known as the range) over a random and infinite horizon time. Our results are applied to address new Parisian ruin problems, stochastic ordering and the number near-maximum distress periods showing the superiority of the binomial expansion approach for such cases.



1.
Introduction

One of the main purposes of ruin theory is to model the surplus of an insurance company with a special focus on the risks inherent to financial distress periods which begin with a capital shortfall. For instance, the duration of such periods (or negative excursions) have been investigated in several papers such as
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
13
]
. Monitoring such financial distress periods is also of practical importance since, under the U.S. bankruptcy code, a reorganization is introduced to the firm when it defaults instead of immediate liquidation (see François and Morellec
[
5
]
, Galai et al.
[
6
]
and Li et al.
[
15
]
). In practice, we should be interested in the longest negative duration during a given period as it describes the worst situation an insurance company would experience before recovery. For a risk process
X
, we denote the duration of the ongoing negative excursion up to time
t
by

(1)

U_{t}=t-g_{t},\quad t\geq 0,

where
g_{t}=\sup\left\{0\leq s\leq t:X_{s}\geq 0\right\}
is the last time before
t
when the process was non-negative, with the convention
\sup\emptyset=0
. This paper continues the analysis on the process
U
by Landriault et al.
[
11
]
in which last passage times of
U
have been studied as well as the joint distribution of
(U_{\mathrm{e}_{q}},X_{\mathrm{e}_{q}})
where
\mathrm{e}_{q}
denotes an exponential random variable with rate
q>0
, and by Wang et al.
[
28
]
in which a refracted Lévy risk model with delayed dividend pullbacks is studied. In this paper, we are interested in the running supremum of
U
up to time
t

(2)

\bar{U}_{t}=\sup_{s\leq t}U_{s},\quad t\geq 0,

and in
\bar{U}_{\infty}=\sup_{s\geq 0}U_{s}
being the ultimate supremum. First, we are interested in finding the distribution of
\bar{U}_{\infty}
,
\bar{U}_{\mathrm{e}_{q}}
,
\bar{U}_{\tau_{b}^{+}}
and
\bar{U}_{T_{b}^{+}}
, where
\mathrm{e}_{q}
denotes an exponential random variable with rate
q>0
that is independent of the process
X
,
\tau_{b}^{+}
is the first passage time and
T_{b}^{+}
is the first passage time under Poisson observations. We use a unified approach based on the binomial expansion approach as in
[
30
]
,
[
17
]
and
[
3
]
among others. Our results are closely related with a set of papers dealing with the so-called red period (see for example
[
10
]
) and Parisian ruin time (see for example
[
20
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
30
]
). More specifically, we provide a link between our results and the Parisian risk model of Loeffen et al.
[
22
]
. Moreover, we study the distribution of the shortest negative excursion
\underline{U}_{\infty}=\inf_{s\leq t}U_{s}
. For risk processes with bounded variation, we study new quantities related to the shortest, longest, total negative duration as well as their range, that is the difference between the longest and shortest negative duration. The main contribution of our paper lies in linking the maximal/minimum length of the negative excursions up to some random time with geometric trials also known as the binomial expansion approach. This approach allows to derive some non-trivial identities related to new Parisian ruin models and gives stochastic ordering for the Parisian ruin probabilities completing the existing literature on ordering ruin probabilities in
[
27
]
and
[
19
]
. Finally, making use of the results of Pakes and Yun
[
16
]
, we study the number of near-maximum distress periods for the Cramér-Lundberg process with exponential jumps.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present the necessary background material on spectrally negative Lévy processes together with some relevant ruin-related quantities. The main technical analysis is carried out in Section
3
in which we provide the main results of this paper involving formulas for the distribution of the longest and shortest negative duration and the range. Finally, in Section
4
we show how our approach can be applied to solve new Parisian risk models, stochastic ordering of Parisian ruin probabilities and the number of near-maximum negative excursions.

2.
Preliminaries on spectrally negative Lévy processes

First, we present the necessary background material on spectrally negative Lévy processes. A Lévy insurance risk process
X
is a process
with stationary and independent increments and no positive jumps. To avoid
trivialities, we exclude the case where
X
has monotone paths. Throughout, we will use the standard notation: the law of
X
when
starting from
X_{0}=x
is denoted by
\mathbb{P}_{x}
and the corresponding
expectation by
\mathbb{E}_{x}
. We write
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{E}
when
x=0
. As the Lévy process
X
has no positive jumps, its Laplace transform is finite: for all
\lambda,t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\lambda X_{t}}\right]=\mathrm{e}^{t\psi(\lambda)},

where

\psi(\lambda)=\gamma\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\lambda^{2}+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\lambda z}-1-\lambda z\mathbf{1}_{\{z>-1\}}\right)\Pi(\mathrm{d}z),

for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\geq 0
, and where
\Pi
is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
(0,\infty)
called the Lévy measure of
X

such that

\int_{(-\infty,0)}(1\wedge z^{2})\Pi(\mathrm{d}z)<\infty.

By
\Phi_{q}=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0\mid\psi(\lambda)=q\}
we denote the right-inverse of
\psi
.
For
q\geq 0
, the first
q
-scale function of the process
X
is defined as
the continuous function on
[0,\infty)
with Laplace transform

(3)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda y}W_{q}(y)\mathrm{d}y=\frac{1}{\psi(\lambda)-q},\quad\text{for $\lambda>\Phi_{q}$.}

We extend
W_{q}
to the whole real line by setting
W_{q}(x)=0
for
x<0
.
We write
W=W_{0}
. We also define the generalized second scale function
Z_{q}(x,\theta)
by

(4)

Z_{q}(x,\theta)=\mathrm{e}^{\theta x}\left(1-(\psi(\theta)-q)\int_{0}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\theta y}W_{q}(y)\mathrm{d}y\right),\quad x\geq 0,

and
Z_{q}(x,\theta)=\mathrm{e}^{\theta x}
for
x<0
.

Throughout this paper, we assume that the first scale function
W_{q}(x)
is
continuously differentiable.

2.1.
Fluctuation identities

We define of the first-passage time of
X
above a level
b\in\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\tau_{b}^{+}=\inf\{t>0\colon X_{t}>b\},

with the convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty
. For
q\geq 0
and
x\leq b
, we have

(5)

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-q\tau_{b}^{+}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{b}^{+}<\infty\}}\right]=\mathrm{e}^{-\Phi_{q}(b-x)}.

From Loeffen et al.
[
22
]
, we recall the following function
\Lambda^{\left(q\right)}(x,r)

\Lambda^{\left(q\right)}\left(x,r\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}W_{q}\left(x+u\right)\frac{u}{r}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in\mathrm{d}u\right),

and we write
\Lambda=\Lambda^{(0)}
when
q=0
. From Loeffen et al.
[
20
]
, for
x\leq 0
, we have

(6)

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{+}<r)=\Lambda\left(x,r\right).

The Laplace transform of the
classical
ruin time
\tau_{0}^{-}=\inf\{t>0\colon X_{t}<0\}
is given by

(7)

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-q\tau_{0}^{-}}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]=Z_{q}(x)-\dfrac{q}{\Phi_{q}}W_{q}(x),

for
q>0
. In particular, for
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]>0
, the
classical
probability of ruin is given by

(8)

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)=1-\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]W(x).

From
[
21
, Lemma 2.2]
we know that for
p\geq 0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(9)

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-q\tau_{0}^{-}}W_{q}\left(X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}+z\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]=W_{q}\left(x+z\right)-W_{q}(x)\mathrm{e}^{\Phi_{q}z}.

2.2.
Cramér-Lundberg process with exponential jumps

Our results will be illustrated by numerical examples for the special case of Cramér-Lundberg risk process
X=\{X_{t},t\geq 0\}
with exponential claims, that is

(10)

X_{t}=X_{0}+ct-A_{t}=X_{0}+ct-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}C_{i},

where
c>0
is the fixed premium rate,
N=\{N_{t},t\geq 0\}
is a Poisson process with rate
\eta>0
and
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
is a sequence of i.i.d. random variables. The Poisson process is independent of
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
.
We assume that
\{C_{1},C_{2},\dots\}
are exponentially distributed
random variables with a parameter
\alpha
and that the net-profit condition holds, that is, that

c-\eta/\alpha>0.

Then the scale function of
X
is known to be

(11)

\displaystyle W(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c-\eta/\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\eta}{c\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{(\frac{\eta}{c}-\alpha)x}\right).

One can observe that a sum of i.i.d. exponential random variables has the same law as a gamma random variable. Since
C_{i}
and
N_{t}
are independent, from eq.
(17)
in
[
7
]
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}C_{i}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)=\mathrm{e}^{-\eta r}\left(\delta_{0}(\mathrm{d}z)+\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha z}\sqrt{\dfrac{r\eta\alpha}{z}}I_{1}(2\sqrt{r\eta\alpha z})\mathrm{d}z\right),

where
I_{1}
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order
1
and
\delta_{0}(\mathrm{d}y)
is a Dirac mass at
0
. Consequently,
the law of
X
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)=\mathrm{e}^{-\eta r}\left(\delta_{0}(cr-\mathrm{d}z)+\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha(cr-z)}\sqrt{\dfrac{r\eta\alpha}{cr-z}}I_{1}(2\sqrt{r\eta\alpha(cr-z)})\mathrm{d}z\right),\quad z\leq cr

and hence

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{z}{r}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)=\mathrm{e}^{-\eta r}\left(c+\int_{0}^{cr}z\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha(cr-z)}\sqrt{\dfrac{\eta\alpha}{\left(cr-z\right)r}}I_{1}(2\sqrt{r\eta\alpha(cr-z)})\mathrm{d}z\right).

Further, using the same approach as in
[
20
, p. 9]
, we derive

(12)

\frac{\eta}{c\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{(\frac{\eta}{c}-\alpha)z}z\mathbb{P}(X_{r}\in\mathrm{d}z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}z\mathbb{P}(X_{r}\in\mathrm{d}z)-(c-\eta/\alpha)r.

Thus,

\displaystyle\Lambda\left(x,r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c-\eta/\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{\eta}{c\alpha}e^{\left(\eta/c-\alpha\right)\left(x+z\right)}\right)\frac{z}{r}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in dz\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c-\eta/\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{z}{r}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in dz\right)-\frac{\eta e^{\left(\eta/c-\alpha\right)x}}{c\alpha\left(c-\eta/\alpha\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\left(\eta/c-\alpha\right)z}\frac{z}{r}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in dz\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c-\eta/\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{z}{r}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in dz\right)-\frac{e^{\left(\eta/c-\alpha\right)x}}{\left(c-\eta/\alpha\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{z}{r}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in dz\right)+e^{\left(\eta/c-\alpha\right)x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c-\eta/\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{z}{r}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in dz\right)\left(1-e^{\left(\eta/c-\alpha\right)x}\right)+e^{\left(\eta/c-\alpha\right)x},

where in the last equality, we used equation (
12
).

We refer the reader to
[
9
]
for more details on spectrally negative Lévy processes and fluctuation identities.

3.1.
Distribution of longest negative duration

In this subsection, we present our main results for the distributions of the longest negative duration
\bar{U}_{\infty}
,
\bar{U}_{\tau_{b}^{+}}
,
\bar{U}_{T_{b}^{+}}
and
\bar{U}_{\mathrm{e}_{q}}
where
\mathrm{e}_{q}

denotes an exponential random variable with rate
q>0
(independent of
X
) and
T_{b}^{+}
is the first passage time under Poisson observations formally defined in (
26
).

We start our analysis with the distribution of
\bar{U}_{\infty}
,
that is, the longest negative excursion observed.

Proposition 1
.

For
r>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]>0
, we have

(13)

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{\infty}<r\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]\frac{\Lambda\left(x,r\right)}{\Upsilon\left(r\right)},

where

(14)

\Upsilon\left(r\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{z}{r}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in\mathrm{d}z\right).

Proof.

First, we assume that
X
has bounded variation. As described in
[
30
]
, for
k\geq 2
, we define the time when
X
reaches (resp., leaves) level
0
from below (resp., above) for the
k^{\rm th}
time by

L_{k}=\inf\left\{t\geq R_{k-1},\text{ }X_{t}<0\right\},

and

R_{k}=\inf\left\{t\geq L_{k},\text{ }X_{t}=0\right\},

where
L_{1}=\tau^{-}_{0}
and
R_{1}=\inf\left\{t\geq L_{1},\textbf{ }X_{t}=0\right\}
. Let
D_{k}=R_{k}-L_{k}
be the duration of the
k^{\rm th}
negative excursion. Hence, we can then express
\bar{U}_{\infty}
as

(15)

\bar{U}_{\infty}=D_{(N_{\infty})},

where
D_{(n)}=\max\{\,D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}\,\}
is the
n
th order statistic, and

(16)

N_{\infty}=\sup\left\{k:\text{ }R_{k}<\infty\right\}.

Note that
N_{\infty}
has the following distribution:
\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(N_{\infty}=0\right)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}=\infty\right)\,
and for
n=1,2,..,

(17)

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(N_{\infty}=n\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}=\infty\right)\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)\left(\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)\right)^{n-1}.

Indeed, the term
\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)
appears above
as long we have at least one negative (below zero) excursion of the risk process.
Then the process returns to zero
n-1
times and the pieces of these trajectories are i.i.d. since

X_{t}
is a Lévy process. Finally, the last excursion from zero never returns to zero and hence
it identifies
N_{\infty}
.

The stationarity and independence of increments of
X
imply that given

\{N_{\infty}=n\}
, the random variables

\{D_{k},k=1,\cdots,n\}
are mutually independent and
\{D_{k},k=2,\ldots,n\}
are identically distributed.
We start from the key observation:

(18)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{\infty}<r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(N_{\infty}=0\right)+\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{\infty}<r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]W(x)+\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{\infty}<r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right).

Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{\infty}<r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{(N_{\infty})}<r\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(N_{\infty}=n\right)\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{(n)}<r|N_{\infty}=n\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(N_{\infty}=n\right)\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{1}<r,\cdots,D_{n}<r|N_{\infty}=n\right)

(19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(N_{\infty}=n\right)\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{1}<r|R_{n}<\infty,L_{n+1}=\infty\right)

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{2}<r|R_{n}<\infty,L_{n+1}=\infty\right)^{n-1},

where, similar to
[
30
]
, we have

(20)

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{1}<r|R_{n}<\infty,L_{n+1}=\infty\right)=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]}{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)}

and

(21)

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{2}<r|R_{n}<\infty,L_{n+1}=\infty\right)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]}{\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)}.

Above, the subscript
X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}

means that we are dealing here with an event for a copy of the process

X
now starting at the point
X_{\tau_{0}}

at time
t=0
, that is independent of the segment
\{X_{t},t\in[0,\tau_{0}^{-})\}
of the
original process. To underline this independent copy we add hat to the original counterparts.
In particular, above,
\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}
is a first passage time of zero of this independent copy.
Similarly, we will treat initial position
X_{\tau}
for other stopping times
\tau
.
Using Eq.
(22)
in Lkabous et al.
[
18
]
for
\delta=0
, we
have

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]=\Lambda\left(x,r\right)-W\left(x\right)\Upsilon\left(r\right)

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]=1-W\left(0\right)\Upsilon\left(r\right),

where we used the fact that
\Lambda\left(0,r\right)=1
and
\Upsilon
is defined in (
14
).
Plugging the above two expressions in (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{\infty}<r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}=\infty\right)\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\right]\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}=\infty\right)\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\right]\frac{1}{1-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]}

\displaystyle=W\left(0\right)\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]\left(\Lambda\left(x,r\right)-W\left(x\right)\Upsilon\left(r\right)\right)\frac{1}{W\left(0\right)\Upsilon\left(r\right)}

(22)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]\left(\frac{\Lambda\left(x,r\right)}{\Upsilon\left(r\right)}-W\left(x\right)\right),

where in the last equality we used the fact that
W(0)>0
. The resutls follows by substiting the last expression in (
19

Now, if
X
has paths of unbounded variation, we consider an approximation approach similar to
[

. More specifically, for
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle L_{1}(\epsilon)=\tau^{-}_{-\epsilon}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:X_{t}<-\epsilon\right\},

\displaystyle R_{1}(\epsilon)=\inf\left\{t\geq L_{1}(\epsilon):X_{t}=0\right\}.

Let

\bar{U}^{(\epsilon)}_{\infty}=D_{(N^{(\epsilon)}_{\infty})}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}^{(\epsilon)}_{\infty}<r,\tau_{-\epsilon}^{-}<\infty\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{-\epsilon}^{-}=\infty\right)\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{-\epsilon}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\right]\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{-\epsilon}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{-\epsilon}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{-\epsilon}^{-}=\infty\right)\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{-\epsilon}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\right]\frac{1}{1-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{-\epsilon}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{-\epsilon}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]}

and

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{\infty}<r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}^{(\epsilon)}_{\infty}<r,\tau_{-\epsilon}^{-}<\infty\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]\left(\frac{\Lambda\left(x,r\right)}{\Upsilon\left(r\right)}-W\left(x\right)\right),

where in the last equality, we used

x\leq b

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{\tau^{+}_{b}}<r\right)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau^{+}_{b}<\kappa_{r}\right)=\frac{\Lambda(x,r)}{\Lambda(b,r)},

where the second equality follows from Lkabous et al.
[
18
]
for
\delta=0
.
We can also recover the Laplace transform of
\kappa_{r}
derived in
[
18
]
and
[

]
, that is,

(25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{\mathrm{e}_{q}}>r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\kappa_{r}<\mathrm{e}_{q}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-qr}Z_{q}\left(x\right)+q\mathrm{e}^{-qr}\int_{0}^{r}\Lambda^{\left(q\right)}\left(x,s\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-qr}\Lambda^{\left(q\right)}\left(x,r\right)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\Phi_{q}z}(z/r)\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)}\left(\frac{q}{\Phi_{q}}+q\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\Phi_{q}z}\frac{z}{r}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{r}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)\mathrm{d}s\right).

More generally, for a finite-horizon time
t
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{t}>r\right)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\kappa_{r}\leq t\right).

Finally, the Laplace transform of
\bar{U}_{\mathrm{e}_{\theta}}
is given by

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-q\bar{U}_{\mathrm{e}_{\theta}}}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}q\mathrm{e}^{-qr}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{\mathrm{e}_{\theta}}<r\right)\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{\infty}q\mathrm{e}^{-qr}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\kappa_{r}>\mathrm{e}_{\theta}\right)\mathrm{d}r.

Our approach can be extended to first Poissonian passage times. We define the first time the surplus

at a Poisson arrival time by

T^{+}_{b}:=\min\{T_{i}:X_{T_{i}}>b,i\in\mathbb{N}\}

with the convention

We start by identifying

Letting

Moreover, note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{T_{b}^{+}}\in dy\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(T_{b}^{+}<\tau_{0}^{-}\right)\delta_{0}\left(dy\right)+\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{T_{b}^{+}}\in dy,\tau_{0}^{-}<T_{b}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=

Similarly to the previous proof, the random variable

can

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bar{U}_{T_{b}^{+}}<r,\tau_{0}^{-}<T_{b}^{+}\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\max_{k=1,..,N_{T_{b}^{+}}}D_{k}<r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\tau_{b}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(N_{T_{b}^{+}}=n\right)\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{1}<r,|R_{n}<T_{b}^{+},L_{n+1}>T_{b}^{+}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{P}\left(D_{2}<r|R_{n}<T_{b}^{+},L_{n+1}>T_{b}^{+}\right)^{n-1},

where

(33)

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{1}<r|R_{n}<T_{b}^{+},L_{n+1}>T_{b}^{+}\right)=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<T_{b}^{+}\right\}}\right]}{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}<T_{b}^{+}\right)}

and

(34)

Further, by (
6
)

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}<r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<T_{b}^{+}\right\}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\Lambda\left(X_{\tau_{0}^{-}},r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<T_{b}^{+}\right\}}\right].

We will find now
\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}\in\mathrm{d}y,\tau_{0}^{-}<T_{b}^{+}\right)

which produces

and
\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}<T_{b}^{+}\right)
.

where

(36)

by

,
for

In this way, we obtain

3.2.
Shortest duration up to an infinite time horizon

In this subsection, we will derive the distribution of the shortest negative duration that we denote by
\underline{U}_{\infty}
which is only for relevant bounded variation processes. First, we notice that
\underline{U}_{\infty}
can be represented as

(37)

\underline{U}_{\infty}=D_{(1)},

where
D_{(1)}=\min\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{N_{\infty}}\}
.
Then, we have the following expression for
\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\underline{U}_{\infty}>r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
r>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]>0
. If
W(0)>0
(or equivalently
X
has paths
of bounded variation), then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\underline{U}_{\infty}>r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)=

\displaystyle W\left(0\right)\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]\frac{1-W(x)(\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]-\Upsilon\left(r\right))-\Lambda\left(x,r\right)}{1-W(0)(\Upsilon\left(r\right)-\mathbb{E}[X_{1}])}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\underline{U}_{\infty}>r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{(1)}>r\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(N_{\infty}=n\right)\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{(1)}>r|N_{\infty}=n\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(N_{\infty}=n\right)\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{1}>r,\cdots,D_{n}>r|N_{\infty}=n\right)

(38)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(N_{\infty}=n\right)\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{1}>r|R_{n}<\infty,L_{n+1}=\infty\right)

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{2}>r|R_{n}<\infty,L_{n+1}=\infty\right)^{n-1},

where

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{1}>r|R_{n}<\infty,L_{n+1}=\infty\right)=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}>r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]}{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)}

and

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(D_{2}>r|R_{n}<\infty,L_{n+1}=\infty\right)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}>r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]}{\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)}.

Using the fact that

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}>r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]=1-\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]W\left(x\right)-\Lambda\left(x,r\right)+W\left(x\right)\Upsilon\left(r\right),

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}>r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]=W\left(0\right)(\Upsilon\left(r\right)-\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]),

and plugging the above two expressions in (
38
), we finally obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\underline{U}_{\infty}>r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}=\infty\right)\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}>r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}>r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}=\infty\right)\frac{\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}>r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]}{1-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}_{X_{\tau_{0}^{-}}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{+}>r\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]}

\displaystyle=W\left(0\right)\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]\frac{1-W(x)(\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]-\Upsilon\left(r\right))-\Lambda\left(x,r\right)}{1-W(0)(\Upsilon\left(r\right)-\mathbb{E}[X_{1}])}.

∎

Joint Distribution of
\underline{U}_{\infty}
and

Now, we are interested in the joint cumulative distribution function of the shortest and longest negative excursion as shown in the 3-dimensional (3D) plot in Figure

Theorem 5
.

Let
0<u<v
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]>0
. If

(or equivalently
X
has paths

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(\underline{U}_{\infty}\leq u,\bar{U}_{\infty}\leq v,\tau^{-}_{0}<\infty)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(\underline{U}_{\infty}>u,\bar{U}_{\infty}\leq v,\tau^{-}_{0}<\infty)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}(\tau^{-}_{0}=\infty)\left(\Lambda\left(x,v\right)-\Lambda\left(x,u\right)-W\left(x\right)(\Upsilon\left(v\right)-\Upsilon\left(u\right)\right)}{1+W\left(0\right)(\Upsilon\left(v\right)-\Upsilon\left(u\right))},

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]W\left(0\right)\left(\Lambda\left(x,v\right)-\Lambda\left(x,u\right)-W\left(x\right)(\Upsilon\left(v\right)-\Upsilon\left(u\right)\right)}{1+W\left(0\right)(\Upsilon\left(v\right)-\Upsilon\left(u\right))}.

\mathcal{R}_{\infty}=\bar{U}_{\infty}-\underline{U}_{\infty}=D_{(N)}-D_{(1)},

where

is defined in (

). Hence, making use of the connection between the random variables

,
\underline{U}_{\infty}

\mathcal{R}
. First, observe that

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\infty}<r\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{0}^{-}=\infty\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\infty}<r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right).

(40)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\infty}<r,\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(N_{\infty}=n\right)\mathbb{P}\left(D_{\left(n\right)}-D_{\left(1\right)}<r|N_{\infty}=n\right)

and

(41)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(D_{\left(n\right)}-D_{\left(1\right)}<r|N_{\infty}=n\right)=n\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{D_{1}}(y)\left(F_{D_{1}}(y+r)-F_{D_{1}}(y)\right)^{n-1}\mathrm{d}y

is the probability density function of

. In Figure

4.
Applications

This section is devoted to introducing new applications of the longest negative excursion using the binomial approach for processes with bounded variation.

4.1.
New Parisian ruin models

We now present two new Parisian ruin models making use of the binomial expansion approach to derive the joint distribution of the total and longest negative excursion.

4.1.1.
Parisian ruins under the same roof

Under the classical Parisian ruin model, a clock is assigned for each negative excursion. In
[
8
]
, cumulative ruin has been introduced in which the cumulative sum of excursions below zero is considered and compared to a fixed delay. In this section, we are interested in a new type of Parisian ruin in which both ruin models are considered. Thus, for
r<l
, we define

\rho_{r,l}=\min(\sigma_{l},\kappa_{r}),

where
\sigma_{l}=\inf\left\{t>0\colon\mathcal{O}^{X}_{t}>l\right\}
and
\mathcal{O}^{X}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\left(-\infty,0\right)}\left(X_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}s
is the occupation time in the red of
X
which represents the amount of time that
X
stays in the negative half-line. Clearly, for fixed values
t
, we have

\left\{\rho_{r,l}\geq t\right\}=\left\{\mathcal{O}^{X}_{t}\leq l,\bar{U}_{t}\leq r\right\},

and consequently

\mathbb{P}(\rho_{r,l}=\infty)=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}^{X}_{\infty}\leq l,\bar{U}_{\infty}\leq r).

As the above probability is closely related to the joint distribution of
\mathcal{O}^{X}_{\infty}
and
\bar{U}_{\infty}
, we will now study the latter for bounded variation surplus process
X
. First, we know that
\mathcal{O}^{X}_{\infty}
can be decomposed as

\mathcal{O}^{X}_{\infty}=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\infty}}D_{n}.

Thus, we need the joint distribution of the sum and maximum of
D_{1},D_{2},...,D_{N_{\infty}}
.

We have

\displaystyle F_{N}(l,r)=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}^{U}_{\infty}\leq l,\bar{U}_{\infty}\leq r)=\mathbb{P}(\tau^{-}_{0}=\infty)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(N_{\infty}=n)F_{n}(l,r),

where
F_{n}(l,r)=\mathbb{P}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}\leq l,D_{(n)}\leq r)
is the joint
cumulative distribution function with initial condition
F_{1}(l,r)=F_{D_{1}}(\min(l,r))
. Using Eq.
(2.8)
in
[
24
]
(see also Theorem
2.7
in
[
4
]
), we have the following recursive relation of the joint pdf

(42)

\displaystyle f_{n}(l,r)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial l\partial r}F_{n}(l,r)=nf_{D_{1}}(r)\int^{\min(l-r,r)}_{\frac{l-r}{n-1}}f_{n-1}(l-r,t)\mathrm{d}t,

for all
(l,r)\in\left\{(l,r)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\frac{l}{n}\leq r\leq l\right\}
with initial condition

f_{0}(l,r)=f_{D_{1}}(l)\delta_{0}(l-r)=f_{D_{1}}(r)\delta_{0}(l-r).

4.1.2.
Parisian ruin under a peak-to-sum constraint

In the following type of Parisian ruin, we consider the peak-to-sum ratio of negative excursions, that is,

S_{t}=\frac{\bar{U}_{t}}{\mathcal{O}^{X}_{t}}\text{ on }\tau^{-}_{0}<\infty,

which represents the proportion of the longest duration wrt the total duration of all distress events. Thus, for
\alpha,r>0
, we define the following ruin time

\zeta_{\alpha,r}=\inf\left\{t>0\colon S_{t}>\alpha\text{ and }U_{t}>r\right\}.

Under the above Parisian ruin time, we monitor negative excursions with length greater than
r
and compare the peak-to-sum
S_{t}
to a fixed parameter
\alpha>0
.

For fixed
t
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\alpha,r}\leq t)=\mathbb{P}\left(S_{t}>\alpha,\bar{U}_{t}>r\right),

and

\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\alpha,r}<\infty)=\mathbb{P}\left(S_{\infty}>\alpha,\bar{U}_{\infty}>r\right)=\mathbb{P}(\bar{U}_{\infty}>\alpha\mathcal{O}^{X}_{\infty},\bar{U}_{\infty}>r).

In order to compute the above probability, we define the peak-to-sum ratio as

R_{n}=\frac{D_{(n)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}}.

Then,

\mathbb{P}(R_{n}>\alpha,D_{(n)}>r)=\int^{\infty}_{r}\int^{y/\alpha}_{0}f_{n}(y,z)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z,

where
f_{n}
is given in (
42
) and consequently

\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\alpha,r}<\infty)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}>\alpha,D_{(n)}>r)\mathbb{P}(N_{\infty}=n).

4.2.
Stochastic ordering of Parisian ruin probabilities

We recall that
X
is smaller than
Y
in the stochastic dominance order, which is denoted by
X\preceq_{st}\tilde{X}
, if

(43)

\bar{F}_{X}(u)\leq\bar{F}_{\tilde{X}}(u),\;\text{for all $u$,}

where
\bar{F}_{X}(u)=1-F_{X}(u)
.
In particular, if
N
and
\tilde{N}
are discrete random variables taking on values in
\mathbb{N}
, then
N\preceq_{st}\tilde{N}
if and only if

(44)

\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}\left(N=i\right)\geq\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}\left(\tilde{N}=i\right).

Let
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{1}
be two increasing functions that satisfy
\varphi_{1}(x)\leq\varphi_{2}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\varphi_{1}(X)\preceq_{st}\varphi_{2}(X),

and if
X\preceq_{st}Y
,

(45)

\varphi_{1}(X)\preceq_{st}\varphi_{2}(Y).

Finally, if
X=\left\{X_{t},t\geq 0\right\}
and
\tilde{X}=\left\{\tilde{X}_{t},t\geq 0\right\}
are stochastic processes, then we write
X\preceq_{st}\tilde{X}
if, for each
t\geq 0
, we have

X_{t}\preceq_{st}\tilde{X}_{t}.

We consider
two Cramér-Lundberg risk processes
X

4.3.
On the number of near-maximum distress periods

A near-maximum distress period is the negative excursion with length falling within a fixed duration
a
of the current largest negative excursion. Assuming that
X
has paths of bounded variation, for a fixed
a>0
, we define

\mathcal{E}_{a}=\#\left\{1\leq j\leq N_{\infty}:D_{j}\in\left(\bar{U}_{\infty}-a,\bar{U}_{\infty}\right]\right\},

as the number of near-maximum distress periods. The above quantity is related to the work of Li and Pakes
[
16
]
in which the number of near-maximum insurance claims has been studied.
Using Eq. (2.5) in
[
23
]
, we have the following conditional p.g.f.

\mathbb{E}\left[s^{\mathcal{E}_{a}}\mid N_{\infty}\right]=sN_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\{F_{D_{1}}(x-a)+s[F_{D_{1}}(x)-F_{D_{1}}(x-a)]\}^{N_{\infty}-1}f_{D_{1}}(x)\mathrm{d}x,

where
f_{D_{1}}
is the probability density function of
D_{1}
. Let
h(s)=\mathbb{E}\left[s^{N_{\infty}}\right]

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{E}_{a}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}h^{\prime}\left(F_{D_{1}}(x+a)\right)f_{D_{1}}(x)\mathrm{d}x,

that is the expected number of near-maximum distress periods.

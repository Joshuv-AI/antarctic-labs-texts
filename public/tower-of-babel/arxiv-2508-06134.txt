Semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering off a tensor-polarized spin-1 target
Jing Zhao, Alessandro Bacchetta, Shunzo Kumano, Tianbo Liu, and Ya-jin Zhou

Abstract
We investigate the semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering off a tensor-polarized spin-1 target, focusing on the production of an unpolarized hadron. The complete differential cross section is expressed in terms of 23 structure functions, which depend on the spin states of the target and the azimuthal modulations of the final-state hadron. Within the transverse momentum dependent (TMD) factorization framework, we derive the hadronic tensor using quark-quark correlators and quark-gluon-quark correlators up to twist-3. At tree-level, 21 nonvanishing structure functions are obtained at the leading and subleading twist, expressed as convolutions of TMD parton distribution functions and TMD fragmentation functions. The measurement of these nonzero structure functions can be utilized to explore the tensor-polarized structure of spin-1 particles, offering insights into their internal dynamics.

Quantum ChromoDynamics (QCD) is the fundamental theory describing strong interactions. Quarks and gluons (i.e., partons) are the elementary degrees of freedom of QCD and are confined inside hadrons.
The nonperturbative nature of QCD at low-energy scales makes it challenging to compute the properties of hadrons from first principles.

Deep inelastic scattering (DIS) plays a significant role in studying the structure of the nucleon.

In this paper, we present a symmetric study of the SIDIS process off a tensor-polarized spin-1 target. The differential cross section can be generally expressed in terms of 23 tensor-polarized structure functions, corresponding to specific angular distributions and target spin states.
Within the kinematic region where the target and detected hadron are nearly back-to-back, we apply the TMD factorization to calculate the structure functions at first subleading twist accuracy (twist-3).
We obtain 21 nonvanishing structure functions, which can be used to study the tensor-polarized TMD PDFs for spin-1 particles.

2
Kinematics and spin states

We consider the SIDIS process off a spin-1 target,

\displaystyle\ell(l)+d(P)\rightarrow\ell(l^{\prime})+h(P_{h})+X(P_{X}),

(1)

where
d
denotes the spin-1 target, such as a deuteron.
Throughout this paper, we focus on the unpolarized hadron production in SIDIS with a longitudinal polarized lepton beam and a tensor-polarized target.
The four momenta of the involved particles are indicated by the variables in parentheses.
Within one-photon-exchange approximation, the momentum of the exchanged virtual photon
q=l-l^{\prime}
with
Q^{2}=-q^{2}
gives its virtuality and provides a hard scale of the process.
Here the target and produced hadron masses are denoted as
M
and
M_{h}
, respectively.

The SIDIS kinematic variables in Trento conventions.

To describe the SIDIS kinematics, several commonly used dimensionless variables are defined as

\displaystyle x_{d}=\frac{Q^{2}}{2P\cdot q},\quad y=\frac{P\cdot q}{P\cdot l},\quad z=\frac{P\cdot P_{h}}{P\cdot q},\quad\gamma=\frac{2Mx_{d}}{Q},

(2)

where
x_{d}
is the scaling variable for the deuteron. The Bjorken scaling variable, defined as
x_{B}=Q^{2}/(2M_{N}\nu)
with the nucleon mass
M_{N}

via

Following the Trento conventions

The azimuthal angle

\displaystyle\cos\phi_{h}=-\frac{l_{\mu}P_{h\nu}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}}{\sqrt{l_{\perp}^{2}P_{h\perp}^{2}}},\qquad\sin\phi_{h}=-\frac{l_{\mu}P_{h\nu}\epsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}}{\sqrt{l_{\perp}^{2}P_{h\perp}^{2}}},

(3)

where

and

indicate the transverse momenta of the lepton and the detected hadron, respectively.
We introduce the transverse tensors

\displaystyle g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{q^{\mu}P^{\nu}+q^{\nu}P^{\mu}}{P\cdot q(1+\gamma^{2})}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1+\gamma^{2}}\left(\frac{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}{Q^{2}}-\frac{P^{\mu}P^{\nu}}{M^{2}}\right),

(4)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\frac{P_{\rho}q_{\sigma}}{P\cdot q\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}},

(5)

with nonzero components
g_{\perp}^{11}=g_{\perp}^{22}=-1
and
\epsilon_{\perp}^{12}=-\epsilon_{\perp}^{21}=-1
,
where the convention for totally antisymmetric tensor is
\epsilon^{0123}=1
.

As defined in ref.
Bacchetta:2000jk
, the polarization for spin-1 particles is characterized by both the spin vector
S^{\mu}
and the spin tensor

.
We can decompose these spin tensors into longitudinal and transverse components with respect to the momentum of the hadron.
In light-cone coordinates, the hadron momentum can be written as

\displaystyle P^{\mu}=(P\cdot n)\bar{n}^{\mu}+\frac{M^{2}}{2P\cdot n}n^{\mu},

(6)

where

and
\bar{n}
are two lightlike basis vectors and satisfy

,
\bar{n}\cdot\bar{n}=0
, and
n\cdot\bar{n}=1
. Then the spin vector and tensor can be parametrized as

\displaystyle S^{\mu}

\displaystyle=S_{L}\frac{P\cdot n}{M}\bar{n}^{\mu}-S_{L}\frac{M}{2P\cdot n}n^{\mu}+S_{T}^{\mu},

(7)

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigg[\frac{4}{3}S_{LL}\frac{(P\cdot n)^{2}}{M^{2}}\bar{n}^{\mu}\bar{n}^{\nu}-\frac{2}{3}S_{LL}(\bar{n}^{\{\mu}n^{\nu\}}-g_{T}^{\mu\nu})+\frac{1}{3}S_{LL}\frac{M^{2}}{(P\cdot n)^{2}}n^{\mu}n^{\nu}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{P\cdot n}{M}\bar{n}^{\{\mu}S_{LT}^{\nu\}}-\frac{M}{2P\cdot n}n^{\{\mu}S_{LT}^{\nu\}}+S_{TT}^{\mu\nu}\bigg],

(8)

\displaystyle g_{T}^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-\bar{n}^{\mu}n^{\nu}-n^{\mu}\bar{n}^{\nu}.

The four-vector

\displaystyle\hat{t}^{\mu}=\frac{2x_{d}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}Q}\left(P^{\mu}-\frac{P\cdot q}{q^{2}}q^{\mu}\right),\quad\hat{z}^{\mu}=-\frac{q^{\mu}}{Q}.

\displaystyle\bar{n}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{t}^{\mu}+\hat{z}^{\mu}),\quad n^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{t}^{\mu}-\hat{z}^{\mu}),

\displaystyle S^{\mu}=S_{L}\frac{P^{\mu}-q^{\mu}M^{2}/(P\cdot q)}{M\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}}+S_{T}^{\mu},

\displaystyle S_{LL}=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}T^{\mu\nu}\hat{L}_{\mu}\hat{L}_{\nu},

(16)

\displaystyle S_{LT}^{\mu}=

\displaystyle-2T_{\alpha\beta}\hat{L}^{\alpha}g_{\perp}^{\mu\beta},

(17)

\displaystyle S_{TT}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle 2T_{\alpha\beta}g_{\perp}^{\alpha\mu}g_{\perp}^{\beta\nu}-T_{\alpha\beta}\hat{L}^{\alpha}\hat{L}^{\beta}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\cos\phi_{LT}=-\frac{2T^{\mu\nu}\hat{L}_{\mu}\hat{x}_{\nu}}{|S_{LT}|},\quad\sin\phi_{LT}=-\frac{2T^{\mu\nu}\hat{L}_{\mu}\hat{y}_{\nu}}{|S_{LT}|},

3
The cross section in terms of structure functions

With one-photon-exchange approximation, the differential cross section of the SIDIS process can be expressed as the contraction of the leptonic tensor and the hadronic tensor
Bacchetta:2006tn
,

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dx_{d}dydzd\phi_{h}d\psi dP_{h\perp}^{2}}=\frac{\alpha^{2}y}{8Q^{4}z}L_{\mu\nu}W^{\mu\nu},

(23)

where
\alpha
is the electromagnetic fine structure constant and the angle
\psi
is the azimuthal angle of the outgoing lepton around the lepton beam axis, measured relative to an arbitrary fixed reference direction.
The leptonic tensor
L_{\mu\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle L_{\mu\nu}=2\left(l_{\mu}l_{\nu}^{\prime}+l_{\nu}l_{\mu}^{\prime}-g_{\mu\nu}l\cdot l^{\prime}+i\lambda_{e}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}l^{\rho}l^{\prime\sigma}\right),

(24)

where
\lambda_{e}
denotes the helicity of the lepton beam.
The hadronic tensor
W^{\mu\nu}
is expressed as

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}\left(q;P,S,T;P_{h}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{X}\delta^{4}\left(P+q-P_{h}-P_{X}\right)\left\langle P,S,T|J^{\mu}(0)|P_{X},P_{h}\right\rangle\left\langle P_{X},P_{h}|J^{\nu}(0)|P,S,T\right\rangle,

(25)

where
J^{\mu}
is the electromagnetic current operator, and the sum
\displaystyle{\sum_{X}}
runs over the final-state hadronic states
X
with the momentum integration implicitly assumed.
The hadronic tensor fulfills the constraints imposed by the Hermiticity and parity invariance,

\displaystyle W^{*\mu\nu}\left(q;P,S,T;P_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=W^{\nu\mu}\left(q;P,S,T;P_{h}\right),

(26)

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}\left(q;P,S,T;P_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=W_{\mu\nu}\left(q,P,-\bar{S},\bar{T};P_{h}\right),

(27)

where
\bar{S}
and
\bar{T}
indicate a sign flip of all space components.
Additionally, the gauge invariance requires

\displaystyle q_{\mu}W^{\mu\nu}\left(q;P,S,T;P_{h}\right)=W^{\mu\nu}\left(q;P,S,T;P_{h}\right)q_{\nu}=0.

(28)

To impose this condition, one can construct the so-called conserved vectors and tensors using the four momenta
P
and
q
,

\displaystyle P_{q}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=P^{\mu}-\frac{P\cdot q}{q^{2}}q^{\mu},

(29)

\displaystyle P_{hq}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=P_{h}^{\mu}-\frac{P_{h}\cdot q}{q^{2}}q^{\mu},

(30)

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}_{q}

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}{q^{2}},

(31)

which will vanish upon contraction with
q
.

Through kinematic analysis, the hadronic tensor can be typically expressed as a sum of basis Lorentz tensors multiplied by scalar functions, referred to as structure functions.
The basis Lorentz tensors are constructed from the available kinematic variables involved in this process,
P^{\mu}
,
P_{h}^{\mu}
,
q^{\mu}
,
S^{\mu}
, and
T^{\mu\nu}
.
While we can, in principle, construct the basis Lorentz tensors by exhaustively listing all possible combinations of the conserved vectors and tensors, this method quickly loses its practicality as the number of the measured momenta increases.
Instead of directly building all basis Lorentz tensors, we adopt a systematic procedure presented in refs.
Chen:2016moq
;
Jiao:2022gzu
;
Zhao:2022lbw
;
Zhao:2024zpy
.
Using the conserved vectors and tensors (
29
)–(
31
), one can first obtain nine basic Lorentz tensors
Jiao:2022gzu
;
Zhao:2024zpy
,

\displaystyle h_{U}^{S\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\left\{g^{\mu\nu}_{q},P_{q}^{\mu}P_{q}^{\nu},P_{q}^{\{\mu}P_{hq}^{\nu\}},P_{hq}^{\mu}P_{hq}^{\nu}\right\},

(32)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{U}^{S\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\left\{\epsilon^{\left\{\mu qPP_{h}\right.}P_{q}^{\nu\}},\epsilon^{\left\{\mu qPP_{h}\right.}P_{hq}^{\nu\}}\right\},

(33)

\displaystyle h_{U}^{A\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\left\{P_{q}^{[\mu}P_{hq}^{\nu]}\right\},

(34)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{U}^{A\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\left\{\epsilon^{\mu\nu qP},\epsilon^{\mu\nu qP_{h}}\right\},

(35)

where the subscript
U
denotes the unpolarized part,
the superscripts
S
and
A
stand for the symmetric and antisymmetric tensors, respectively.
The parity-conserving and parity-violating terms are labeled by
h
and
\tilde{h}
, respectively.
We introduce the shorthand notations to make the expressions more compact, such as
\epsilon^{\mu\nu qP}\equiv\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}q_{\rho}P_{\sigma}
.
Under the constraint of parity invariance (
27
), the unpolarized basis tensors are directly provided by those in
h_{U}^{S\mu\nu}
and
h_{U}^{A\mu\nu}
.
If we take the parity-violating channels into account, e.g., the

\tilde{h}_{U}^{S\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{h}_{U}^{A\mu\nu}
should also be included.

Since the four vectors

,

,

, and
\epsilon^{\mu qPP_{h}}
form the complete basis of four-dimensional spacetime, the spin vector and spin tensor can be expressed as the combinations of these basis with spin-dependent scalar coefficients,
as demonstrated in ref.

Consequently, the polarized basis Lorentz tensors can be constructed from the spin-independent basic Lorentz tensors in eqs. (

)–(

Here, focusing on the tensor-polarized part of a spin-1 target, we list the 16 basis tensors,

\displaystyle h_{T}^{S\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\left\{T^{P_{h}P_{h}},T^{P_{h}q},T^{qq}\right\}h_{U}^{S\mu\nu},\left\{\epsilon^{T^{P_{h}}PP_{h}q},\epsilon^{T^{q}PP_{h}q}\right\}\tilde{h}_{U}^{S\mu\nu},

(36)

which contribute to the symmetric part of
W^{\mu\nu}
, and the seven ones

\displaystyle h_{T}^{A\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\left\{T^{P_{h}P_{h}},T^{P_{h}q},T^{qq}\right\}h_{U}^{A\mu\nu},\left\{\epsilon^{T^{P_{h}}PP_{h}q},\epsilon^{T^{q}PP_{h}q}\right\}\tilde{h}_{U}^{A\mu\nu}

(37)

contributing to the antisymmetric part of
W^{\mu\nu}
, where the subscript
T
indicates the tensor-polarized states.

With the basis tensors defined above, the tensor-polarized dependent hadronic tensor can be expanded as

\displaystyle W_{T}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{16}V_{T,i}^{S}h_{T,i}^{S\mu\nu}+i\sum_{i=1}^{7}V_{T,i}^{A}h_{T,i}^{A\mu\nu},

(38)

where the coefficients

, known as structure functions, are scalar functions of

,

,

, and

4.2
The quark-quark correlators

Before writing down the explicit expression of the hadronic tensor in the parton model, we first provide a brief review of the definitions of the tensor-polarized TMD PDFs for spin-1 hadrons.

The quark-quark distribution correlation function is defined as

\displaystyle\Phi_{ij}(x,p_{T})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d\xi^{-}d^{2}\bm{\xi}_{T}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{ip\cdot\xi}\langle P|\bar{\psi}_{j}(0)\mathcal{U}_{(0,\xi)}^{c}\psi_{i}(\xi)|P\rangle\bigg|_{\xi^{+}=0},

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{(0,\xi)}^{c}=\mathcal{P}\exp\bigg[-ig\int_{0}^{\xi}d\xi\cdot A(\xi)\bigg],

Here the superscript

indicates the path of integral,

represents the strong coupling constant, and

\displaystyle\Phi^{[1]}(x,p_{T};T)=

\displaystyle\frac{M}{P^{+}}\bigg[e_{LL}S_{LL}-e_{LT}^{\perp}\frac{S_{LT}\cdot p_{T}}{M}+e_{TT}^{\perp}\frac{p_{T}\cdot S_{TT}\cdot p_{T}}{M^{2}}\bigg],

(64)

\displaystyle\Phi^{[i\gamma_{5}]}(x,p_{T};T)=

\displaystyle\frac{M}{P^{+}}\bigg[e_{LT}\frac{S_{LT\mu}\epsilon_{T}^{\mu\nu}p_{T\nu}}{M}-e_{TT}\frac{S_{TT\mu\rho}p_{T}^{\rho}\epsilon_{T}^{\mu\nu}p_{T\nu}}{M^{2}}\bigg],

(65)

\displaystyle\Phi^{[\gamma^{i}\gamma_{5}]}(x,p_{T};T)=

\displaystyle\frac{M}{P^{+}}\bigg[-g_{LL}^{\perp}S_{LL}\frac{\varepsilon_{T}^{ij}p_{Tj}}{M}-g_{LT}^{\prime}\epsilon_{T}^{ij}S_{LTj}+g_{LT}^{\perp}\frac{\varepsilon_{T}^{ij}p_{Tj}S_{LT}\cdot p_{T}}{M^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+g_{TT}^{\prime}\frac{\varepsilon_{T}^{ij}S_{TTjl}p_{T}^{l}}{M}-g_{TT}^{\perp}\frac{p_{T}\cdot S_{TT}\cdot p_{T}}{M^{2}}\frac{\varepsilon_{T}^{ij}p_{Tj}}{M}\bigg],

(66)

\displaystyle\Phi^{[i\sigma^{ij}\gamma_{5}]}(x,p_{T};T)=

\displaystyle\frac{M}{P^{+}}\bigg[-h_{LL}\epsilon_{T}^{ij}S_{LL}-h_{LT}\epsilon_{T}^{ij}\frac{S_{LT}\cdot p_{T}}{M}+h_{TT}\epsilon_{T}^{ij}\frac{p_{T}\cdot S_{TT}\cdot p_{T}}{M^{2}}\bigg]

(67)

\displaystyle\Phi^{[i\sigma^{-+}\gamma_{5}]}(x,p_{T};T)=

\displaystyle\frac{M}{P^{+}}\bigg[h_{LT}^{\perp}\frac{\epsilon_{T}^{ij}p_{Ti}S_{LTj}}{M}-h_{TT}^{\perp}\frac{\epsilon_{T}^{ij}p_{Ti}S_{TTjl}p_{T}^{l}}{M^{2}}\bigg].

(68)

In ref.
Kumano:2020ijt
, the traces are taken with

,
\sigma^{-+}
, and
\sigma^{ij}
, instead of
i\sigma^{i+}\gamma_{5}
,
i\sigma^{-+}\gamma_{5}
, and

. However, the TMD PDFs obtained from both sets of traces are equivalent due to the identity

\displaystyle i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\sigma_{\alpha\beta}.

(69)

In order to factor out the inhomogeneous
p_{T}
dependence in eqs . (
60
)–(
68
), one can rewrite the terms containing multiple

dependence.
For example, eq. (

) can be written as

\displaystyle\Phi^{[i\sigma^{i+}\gamma_{5}]}(x,p_{T};T)=

\displaystyle h_{1LL}^{\perp}S_{LL}\frac{\epsilon_{T}^{ij}p_{Tj}}{M}+h_{1LT}\epsilon_{T}^{ij}S_{LTj}-h_{1LT}^{\perp}\frac{\epsilon_{T}^{ij}p_{Tjl}S_{LT}^{l}}{M^{2}}

\displaystyle-h_{1TT}\frac{\epsilon_{T}^{ij}S_{TTjl}p_{T}^{l}}{M}+h_{1TT}^{\perp}\frac{\epsilon_{T}^{ij}p_{Tjlm}S_{TT}^{lm}}{M^{3}},

(70)

where

and

are defined as

\displaystyle h_{1LT}

\displaystyle=h_{1LT}^{\prime}-\frac{p_{T}^{2}}{2M^{2}}h_{1LT}^{\perp},

(71)

\displaystyle h_{1TT}

\displaystyle=h_{1TT}^{\prime}-\frac{p_{T}^{2}}{2M^{2}}h_{1TT}^{\perp}.

(72)

A similar relation applies to other TMD PDFs, expressed as

(73)

where

,

, and

are independent, allowing any two to be chosen in defining the TMD PDFs in eq. (

In eq. (

), the completely symmetric and traceless tensors

are

\displaystyle p_{T}^{ij}

\displaystyle=p_{T}^{i}p_{T}^{j}-\frac{1}{2}p_{T}^{2}g_{T}^{ij},

(74)

\displaystyle p_{T}^{ijk}

\displaystyle=p_{T}^{i}p_{T}^{j}p_{T}^{k}-\frac{1}{4}p_{T}^{2}\left(g_{T}^{ij}p_{T}^{k}+g_{T}^{ik}p_{T}^{j}+g_{T}^{jk}p_{T}^{i}\right),

(75)

which satisfy

At parton-model level, the collinear distribution functions can be obtained from the

-integrated correlation function

(76)

Integrating the TMD PDFs in eq. (

) over the transverse momentum, one can obtain three collinear PDFs for tensor-polarized states up to twist-3,

\displaystyle f_{1LL}(x)

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}\bm{p}_{T}f_{1LL}(x,p_{T}^{2}),

(77)

\displaystyle e_{LL}(x)

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}\bm{p}_{T}e_{LL}(x,p_{T}^{2}),

(78)

\displaystyle f_{LT}(x)

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}\bm{p}_{T}f_{LT}(x,p_{T}^{2}).

(79)

Consequently, the collinear correlation function can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\Phi(x;T)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigg\{f_{1LL}S_{LL}\not{n}_{+}\bigg\}+\frac{M}{2P^{+}}\bigg\{e_{LL}S_{LL}+f_{LT}\not{S}_{LT}\bigg\}.

(80)

We note that the T-odd collinear PDFs should vanish because of time-reversal invariance,

\displaystyle h_{1LT}(x)

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}\bm{p}_{T}h_{1LT}(x,p_{T}^{2})=0,

(81)

\displaystyle g_{LT}(x)

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}\bm{p}_{T}g_{LT}(x,p_{T}^{2})=0,

(82)

\displaystyle h_{LL}(x)

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}\bm{p}_{T}h_{LL}(x,p_{T}^{2})=0.

(83)

We emphasize that the first function is a twist-2 collinear T-odd function: no analogous function exists in the spin-1/2 case.

The fragmentation correlation function is defined by

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}(z,k_{T})=\frac{1}{2z}\sum_{X}\int\frac{d\xi^{+}d^{2}\bm{\xi}_{T}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{ik\cdot\xi}\langle 0|\mathcal{U}^{n_{+}}_{(+\infty,\xi)}\psi_{i}(\xi)|h,X\rangle\langle h,X|\bar{\psi}_{j}(0)\mathcal{U}^{n_{+}}_{(0,+\infty)}|0\rangle\bigg|_{\xi^{+}=0},

(84)

where
k^{-}=P_{h}^{-}/z
, and
P_{h}^{-}
is the dominant component of the detected hadron momentum.
The gauge link follows the definition in eq. (

).

Considering the production of a unpolarized hadron in the final states, we can parametrize the fragmentation correlator in terms of two twist-2 TMD FFs and four twist-3 TMD FFs,

\displaystyle\Delta(z,k_{T})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigg\{D_{1}\not{n}_{-}+iH_{1}^{\perp}\frac{\left[\not{k}_{T},\not{n}_{-}\right]}{2M_{h}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle+\frac{M_{h}}{2P_{h}^{-}}\bigg\{E+D^{\perp}\frac{\not{k}_{T}}{2M_{h}}+iH\frac{[\not{n}_{-},\not{n}_{+}]}{2}+G^{\perp}\gamma_{5}\frac{\epsilon_{T}^{\rho\sigma}\gamma_{\rho}k_{T\sigma}}{M_{h}}\bigg\},

(85)

where the TMD FFs are the functions of
z
and
k_{T}^{2}
.
For completeness, we should add a flavor index to the TMD FFs and specify the type of the hadron in the final state.
Similarly, the collinear FFs can be obtained from the
k_{T}
-integrated correlation function

\displaystyle\Delta(z)=z^{2}\int d^{2}\bm{k}_{T}\Delta(z,k_{T}),

(86)

where the factor

comes from the transverse momentum

of the produced hadron with respect to the direction of the fragmenting quark.
The explicit parametrization of

in terms of collinear FFs has been shown in ref.
Bacchetta:2006tn
.

The decomposition of the fragmentation correlation function
\Delta
for tensor-polarized states can be directly obtained from
\Phi
in eq. (

) by the kinematic variables replacement

\displaystyle n_{+}\rightarrow n_{-},\quad\epsilon_{T}\rightarrow-\epsilon_{T},\quad P^{+}\rightarrow P_{h}^{-},\quad M\rightarrow M_{h},\quad x\rightarrow 1/z

(87)

and the functions notation replacement

\displaystyle f\rightarrow D,\quad e\rightarrow E,\quad h\rightarrow H,\quad g\rightarrow G.

(88)

Due to the contribution of final-state interaction between

and
X
, time-reversal invariance does not imply the vanishing of T-odd collinear fragmentation functions

;
Hagiwara:1982cq
;

.
Thus, functions

,

, and

4.3
The quark-gluon-quark correlators

The quark-gluon-quark correlation function is defined by
Boer:2003cm
;
Pijlman:2006vm

\left(\Phi_{D}^{\mu}\right)_{ij}(x,p_{T})=\int\frac{d\xi^{-}d^{2}\bm{\xi}_{T}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{ip\cdot\xi}\langle P|\bar{\psi}_{j}(0)\mathcal{U}_{(0,\xi)}^{n_{-}}iD^{\mu}(\xi)\psi_{i}(\xi)|P\rangle\bigg|_{\xi^{+}=0},

(89)

where
iD^{\mu}(\xi)=i\partial^{\mu}+gA^{\mu}
is the covariant derivative.
The plus-component of the correlator (
89
) can be written as

\displaystyle\Phi_{D}^{+}(x,p_{T})=xP^{+}\Phi(x,p_{T})

(90)

by using the relation
\mathcal{U}_{(0,\xi)}^{n_{-}}iD^{+}(\xi)\psi(\xi)=i\partial^{+}\mathcal{U}_{(0,\xi)}^{n_{-}}\psi(\xi)
.
Taking the transverse component of the correlator, a further correlator
\tilde{\Phi}_{A}^{\alpha}
can be defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{A}^{\alpha}(x,p_{T})=\Phi_{D}^{\alpha}(x,p_{T})-p_{T}^{\alpha}\Phi(x,p_{T}),

(91)

which includes only the gluon field
A^{\mu}
and satisfies gauge invariance.
The correlator
\tilde{\Phi}_{A}^{\alpha}
can be used to identify the interaction-dependent combinations in the TMD PDFs.
Since the quark-gluon-quark correlator (
89
) depends on the kinematic variables of a single parton rather than two, it can not be directly decomposed as the most general form.
Correspondingly, the covariant derivative is evaluated at the same space-time point as one of the quark fields.

Under the constraint of parity conservation, the tensor-polarized part of the correlation function (
89
) can be parametrized as

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{A}^{\alpha}(x,p_{T};T)

\displaystyle=\frac{xM}{2}\,\biggl\{\Bigl[\bigl(\tilde{f}_{LL}^{\perp}-i\tilde{g}_{LL}^{\perp}\bigr)S_{LL}\frac{p_{T\rho}}{M}+\bigl(\tilde{f}_{LT}^{\prime}-i\tilde{g}_{LT}^{\prime}\bigr)S_{LT\rho}-\bigl(\tilde{f}_{TT}^{\prime}-i\tilde{g}_{TT}^{\prime}\bigr)S_{TT\rho\sigma}\frac{p_{T}^{\sigma}}{M}

\displaystyle-\bigl(\tilde{f}_{LT}^{\perp}-i\,\tilde{g}_{LT}^{\perp}\bigr)\frac{S_{LT}\cdot p_{T}}{M}\frac{p_{T\rho}}{M}+\bigl(\tilde{f}_{TT}^{\perp}-i\,\tilde{g}_{TT}^{\perp}\bigr)\frac{p_{T}\cdot S_{TT}\cdot p_{T}}{M^{2}}\frac{p_{T\rho}}{M}\Bigl]\bigl(g_{T}^{\alpha\rho}-i\epsilon_{T}^{\alpha\rho}\gamma_{5}\bigr)

\displaystyle-\Bigl[\bigl(\tilde{h}_{LT}^{\perp}+i\,\tilde{e}_{LT}^{\perp}\bigr)\frac{\epsilon_{T}^{\rho\sigma}S_{LT\rho}p_{T\sigma}}{M}+\bigl(\tilde{h}_{TT}^{\perp}+i\,\tilde{e}_{TT}^{\perp}\bigr)\frac{\epsilon_{T}^{\beta\rho}S_{TT\rho\sigma}p_{T\beta}p_{T}^{\sigma}}{M^{2}}\Bigl]\gamma_{T}^{\alpha}\,\gamma_{5}

\displaystyle+\Bigl[\big(\tilde{h}_{LL}+i\tilde{e}_{LL}\big)S_{LL}-\bigl(\tilde{h}_{LT}-i\tilde{e}_{LT}\bigr)\frac{S_{LT}\cdot p_{T}}{M}-\bigl(\tilde{h}_{TT}-i\tilde{e}_{TT}\bigr)\frac{p_{T}\cdot S_{TT}\cdot p_{T}}{M^{2}}\Bigr]i\gamma_{T}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\ldots\bigl(g_{T}^{\alpha\rho}+i\epsilon_{T}^{\alpha\rho}\gamma_{5}\bigr)\biggr\}\frac{\not{n}_{+}}{2},

(92)

where
\alpha
is a transverse indice and so forth in the following.
The functions with a tilde are interaction-dependent twist-3 distribution functions, which depend on
x
and
p_{T}^{2}
.
The terms
\tilde{f}_{LT}^{\prime}
,
\tilde{g}_{LT}^{\prime}
,
\tilde{f}_{TT}^{\prime}
, and
\tilde{g}_{TT}^{\prime}
have the same definition as defined in eq. (
73
).
Note that the decomposition of the quark-gluon-quark correlator for tensor-polarized states is similar to that for a vector-polarized hadron, with the distinction arising from the parity-invariant spin tensor.
The explicit parametrization of the last term in the curly brackets is not given, as it is irrelevant to the calculation of the hadronic tensor in the SIDIS process.

The tilde functions defined in eq. (
92
) can be separated by the following three projection:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2Mx}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\tilde{\Phi}_{A\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha+}\right]

\displaystyle=S_{LL}(\tilde{h}_{LL}+i\tilde{e}_{LL})+\frac{p_{T}\cdot S_{LT}}{M}(\tilde{h}_{LT}-i\tilde{e}_{LT})

\displaystyle-\frac{p_{T}\cdot S_{TT}\cdot p_{T}}{M^{2}}(\tilde{h}_{TT}-i\tilde{e}_{TT}),

(93)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2Mx}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\tilde{\Phi}_{A\alpha}i\sigma^{\alpha+}\gamma_{5}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{T}^{\rho\sigma}S_{LT\rho}p_{T\sigma}}{M}(\tilde{h}_{LT}^{\perp}+i\tilde{e}_{LT}^{\perp})

\displaystyle+\frac{\epsilon_{T}^{\rho\sigma}S_{TT\rho\alpha}p_{T}^{\alpha}p_{T\sigma}}{M}(\tilde{h}_{TT}^{\perp}+i\tilde{e}_{TT}^{\perp}),

(94)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2Mx}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\tilde{\Phi}_{A\rho}(g_{T}^{\alpha\rho}+i\epsilon_{T}^{\alpha\rho}\gamma_{5})\gamma^{+}\right]

\displaystyle=S_{LL}\frac{p_{T}^{\alpha}}{M}(\tilde{f}_{LL}^{\perp}-i\tilde{g}_{LL}^{\perp})+S_{LT}^{\alpha}(\tilde{f}_{LT}-i\tilde{g}_{LT})

\displaystyle+\frac{p_{T}^{\alpha}p_{T}^{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}p_{T}^{2}g_{T}^{\alpha\rho}}{M^{2}}S_{LT\rho}(\tilde{f}_{LT}^{\perp}-i\tilde{g}_{LT}^{\perp})-\frac{S_{TT}^{\alpha\rho}p_{T\rho}}{2M}(\tilde{f}_{TT}-i\tilde{g}_{TT})

\displaystyle+\frac{p_{T}^{\alpha}p_{T}^{\rho}-\frac{1}{4}p_{T}^{2}g_{T}^{\alpha\rho}}{M^{2}}\frac{S_{TT\rho\sigma}p_{T}^{\sigma}}{M}(\tilde{f}_{TT}^{\perp}-i\tilde{g}_{TT}^{\perp}).

(95)

The correlation function can be decomposed into different parts with definite twist, where the quark-quark correlator starts from twist-2,

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=\Phi_{2}+\frac{M}{P^{+}}\Phi_{3}+\left(\frac{M}{P^{+}}\right)^{2}\Phi_{4},

(96)

while the quark-gluon-quark correlator starts from twist-3,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\tilde{\Phi}_{A}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\Phi}_{A,3}^{\alpha}+\frac{M}{P^{+}}\tilde{\Phi}_{A,4}^{\alpha}+\left(\frac{M}{P^{+}}\right)^{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{A,5}^{\alpha}.

(97)

Here the number in the subscript indicates the specific twist of TMD PDFs.
For simplicity, we suppress the dependence of
x
and
p_{T}
.
The QCD equation of motion can connect the correlation functions of different twist.
In light-cone coordinates, it is written as

\displaystyle[i\not{D}(\xi)-m]\psi(\xi)=\left[\gamma^{+}iD^{-}(\xi)+\gamma^{-}iD^{+}(\xi)+\gamma_{T}^{\alpha}iD_{\alpha}(\xi)-m\right]\psi(\xi)=0,

(98)

where
m
is the mass of the quark.
Using the identities
\mathcal{P}_{+}\Phi_{4}=\mathcal{P}_{-}\Phi_{2}=0
and
\mathcal{P}_{+}\tilde{\Phi}_{A,5}^{\alpha}=\mathcal{P}_{-}\tilde{\Phi}_{A,3}^{\alpha}=0
, we can project out the good component of eq. (
98

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{+}\left[xM\gamma^{-}\Phi_{3}+M\gamma_{T\rho}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\tilde{\Phi}_{A,3}^{\rho}+\not{p}_{T}\Phi_{2}-m\Phi_{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathcal{P}_{+}\left[xM\gamma^{-}\Phi_{4}+M\gamma_{T\rho}\tilde{\Phi}_{A,4}^{\rho}+\not{p}_{T}\Phi_{3}-m\Phi_{3}\right]\frac{M}{P^{+}}

\displaystyle=0,

(99)

where the term with

in eq. (
98
) does not contribute and the terms
D^{+}
and

are replaced by eqs. (
90
) and (

), respectively.
Projecting this relation on specific Dirac structures and taking the trace gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\Gamma^{+}\left[x\gamma^{-}\Phi_{3}+\gamma_{T\rho}\tilde{\Phi}_{A,3}^{\rho}+\frac{\not{p}_{T}}{M}\Phi_{2}-\frac{m}{M}\Phi_{2}\right]=0,

(100)

where
\Gamma^{+}
is one of the matrices
\Gamma^{+}=\{\gamma^{+},\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},i\sigma^{\alpha+}\gamma_{5}\}
.
We find that the terms with factor
M/P^{+}
in eq. (
99

P^{+}
under boosts along the light-cone direction.

Substituting the expression of quark-quark correlator (
58
) containing
\Phi_{2}
and
\Phi_{3}
and quark-gluon-quark correlator

(
92

4.4
The results for structure functions

Substituting the correlators in the hadronic tensor (
55

.
After contracting the hadronic tensor obtained above and the leptonic tensor, one can express the structure functions appearing in eq. (
23
) in terms of the convolution of TMD PDFs and FFs up to twist-3.
To obtain more concise expressions, we introduce a transverse momentum convolution notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}[wfD]=x\sum_{a}e_{a}^{2}\int d^{2}\bm{k}_{T}d^{2}\bm{p}_{T}\delta^{(2)}\left(\bm{k}_{T}-\bm{p}_{T}-\bm{P}_{h\perp}/z\right)w\left(\bm{k}_{T},\bm{p}_{T}\right)f^{a}\left(x,k_{T}^{2}\right)D^{a}\left(z,p_{T}^{2}\right),

(132)

where
f^{a}
and
D^{a}
indicate a TMD PDF for the tensor-polarized target and a TMD FF for the detected hadron, respectively.
The

is one of the dimensionless scalar functions of
\bm{k}_{T}
and
\bm{p}_{T}
.
The results for the structure functions are given by

\displaystyle F_{U(LL),T}=\mathcal{C}[f_{1LL}D_{1}],

(133)

5
Summary

This work examines the semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering off a tensor-polarized spin-1 target. Considering a longitudinal polarized lepton beam and the production of an unpolarized hadron in this process, we derive the general form of the cross section in terms of structure functions.
Through a detailed kinematic analysis, we express the hadronic tensor as a combination Lorentz tensors and scalar coefficients.
As a result, the tensor-polarized sector of the SIDIS cross section can be decomposed using 23 structure functions, which depend the target’s spin configurations and azimuthal angles. By integrating the SIDIS cross section, we also obtain the structure functions for inclusive DIS.

In the kinematic region where
\bm{q}_{T}^{2}\ll Q^{2}
, we
compute the structure functions in the parton model up to the subleading order in
1/Q
(twist-3).
We first give the expression of the hadronic tensor in terms of the transverse-momentum-dependent quark-quark correlator and quark-gluon-quark correlator, which can be parametrized in terms of the TMD PDFs and TMD FFs up to twist-3.
With the constraint of the equation of motion, the relations between the quark-quark correlator and quark-gluon-quark correlator are established.
Using these relations and the parametrization of the correlators, we express the structure functions appearing in the cross section as a convolution of the TMD PDFs and TMD FFs.
At the leading and first subleading twist accuracy, we find that all but two structure functions have nontrivial expressions in SIDIS, whereas only two structure functions contribute to the cross section in inclusive DIS.

These nonzero structure functions serve as critical tools for investigating the tensor-polarized properties of spin-1 particles. Upcoming experiments, such as those at JLab using tensor-polarized deuteron targets, will facilitate measurements, enhancing our understanding of nucleon spin dynamics and potential novel nuclear phenomena.
